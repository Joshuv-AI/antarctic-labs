Spherical Twists for Gorenstein Orders and G G -Hilb
Marina Godinho

Abstract
. This paper constructs derived autoequivalences of Gorenstein orders as twists around spherical functors. More precisely, given a Gorenstein order A A and a quotient p : A → B p\colon A\to B , then we specify natural conditions on B B under which the twist around the corresponding derived restriction of scalars functor is a derived autoequivalence of A A . In the process, we show that the associated cotwist is a shift of the Nakayama functor of B B . These results, together with local-to-global technology, are then used construct new derived autoequivalences for skew group algebras and G G -Hilbert schemes, and we apply this theory to explicit examples.

In this paper, we will construct autoequivalences of derived categories as spherical twists and cotwists. Twists around spherical objects were introduced by Seidel and Thomas

1.2.
Skew-group algebras

Consider a finite abelian group
G\subset\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})
acting on the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
R=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
. Then, this group action allows us to define the

, the construction of which is recalled in §
4.1
. These algebras exhibit a rich representation-theoretic structure. For example, they can be presented, up to a Morita equivalence, by the McKay quiver of
G
. This correspondence is often referred to as the
special McKay correspondence
, which is also briefly recalled in §
4.1
.

In section §
4.2
we develop local-to-global technology in order to lift
E
to the setting of
R\#G
. As a consequence, we obtain the following result.

Theorem F

(
4.21
,
4.18
)
.

Let
p\colon R\#G\to B
be a surjection, where
B
is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-algebra which either is self-injective or has finite global dimension. Suppose that
\ker p=(\ker p)^{2}
. Then, the functor

F=-\otimes^{{\rm\bf L}{}\kern-4.09723pt}_{B}B\colon\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu B)\to\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu S\#G)

is spherical. Hence, the twist

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}={\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{R\#G}}(\ker p,-)\colon\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu R\#G)\to\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu R\#G)

is an autoequivalence.

1.3.
G
-Hilbert schemes

On the geometric side, the action of a finite abelian group
G\subset\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})
on
R=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
allows us to construct the invariant ring

which defines the quotient singularity
\mathbb{C}^{3}/G:=\spec R^{G}
.

This quotient singularity admits a crepant resolution known as

-
\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3})

[
Nak01
]
. Moreover,
[
BKR01
]
prove that there is a derived equivalence

\Phi_{G}\colon\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu R\#G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\coh G\mathrm{-Hilb}(\mathbb{C}^{3}))

thereby establishing a bridge between skew-group algebras and

-Hilbert schemes. Thus, autoequivalences

induce autoequivalences of

. As a corollary of the equivalence established by

BKR01
]
and of Theorem

we obtain the following result in §

.

Theorem G

(

)
.

Let
p\colon R\#G\to B
be a surjection, where

is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-algebra which either is self-injective or has finite global dimension. Suppose that

. Then, the functor

F_{G}=\Phi_{G}\cdot-\otimes^{{\rm\bf L}{}\kern-4.09723pt}_{B}B\colon\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu B)\to\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\coh G\text{-}\Hilb(\mathbb{C}^{3}))

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{G}=\Phi_{G}\cdot{\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{R\#G}(\ker p,-)}\cdot\Phi_{G}^{-1}\colon\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\coh G\text{-}\Hilb(\mathbb{C}^{3}))\to\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\coh G\text{-}\Hilb(\mathbb{C}^{3})).

1.4.
Examples

Let
n>3
be an odd number,
\upvarepsilon_{n}
a primitive
n
th root of unity and consider the group

G=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}\upvarepsilon_{n}&0&0\\
0&\upvarepsilon_{n}&0\\
0&0&\upvarepsilon_{n}^{n-2}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle

acting on

as

. Then, in §

have finite global dimension and, thus, induce derived autoequivalences of

. We conclude that section by explicitly computing all idempotent quotients which induce derived autoequivalences of

for the case

1.5.
Further connections to the literature

Let
f\colon X\to\spec R
be a crepant contraction (see e.g.
[
DW19a
, 2.1]
for the precise technical definition of contraction) with fibres of dimension at most one. Assume further that

is a local isolated hypersurface singularity. Then, there is an

-module

and derived equivalence

[

. In

]

The authors show that it is an equivalence by proving that it is a composition of flopping equivalences.

E
recovers this result in the following way. To the contraction

, Donovan and Wemyss
[

, 2.9]
associate an invariant called the

, which encodes much of the information associated to the contraction.

Let

be idempotent in

. Then, the contraction algebra is the idempotent quotient

Hence, in this setting,

The Ext-vanishing condition required to construct the spherical twist may seem difficult to compute in general. However, when

Lemma 3.12
.

Under
3.3
, suppose that
\dim\mathcal{R}=2,3
and
(\ker p)^{2}\cong\ker p
. Then, for all

,

. If moreover,

is self-injective, then the converse is true.

Proof.

Consider the case
\dim\mathcal{R}=2
. Since

[

, 2.16]

\pdim_{A}B\leqslant\pdim_{A}B+\depth_{\mathcal{R}}B=2.

Whence,

\Ext^{1}_{A}(B,B)
vanishes.

Consider next the case
\dim\mathcal{R}=3
. Then, as before, we may apply the Auslander-Buchsbaum
[
IW14
, 2.16]

\pdim_{A}B\leqslant\pdim_{A}B+\depth_{\mathcal{R}}B=3

and so
\Ext^{k}_{A}(B,B)=0
for

and

. Moreover, by Serre duality (

), there is a vector space isomorphism
\Ext^{2}_{A}(B,B)\cong D\Ext^{1}_{A}(B,B)

vanishes.

Well, applying the functor
\Hom_{A}(-,B)
to the exact sequence

(3.C)

\displaystyle 0\to\ker p\to A\to A/\ker p\ (\cong B)\to 0

induces that long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\Hom_{A}(B,B)\to\Hom_{A}(A,B)\to\Hom_{A}(\ker p,B)\to\Ext^{1}_{A}(B,B)\to 0.

However,
\Hom_{A}(\ker p,B)=0
. To see this, consider a morphism

. Since
\ker p=(\ker p)^{2}
, then any
x\in\ker p
is such that

for

. Thus, necessarily

. Since

, it must be that

. Thus,

is the zero map and we may conclude that
\Hom_{A}(\ker p,B)=0
, which implies that
\Ext^{1}_{A}(B,B)=0
.

Suppose next that
B
is self-injective and that
\Ext^{1}_{A}(B,B)=0
. We will prove the statement for the case

, but the proof for the two-dimensional case is very similar.

Since

by

, there are vector space isomorphisms

\Tor_{1}^{A}(B,B)\cong\Tor_{1}^{A}(B,DB)\cong\Ext^{2}_{A}(B,B)\cong D\Ext^{1}_{A}(B,B)=0

It is a classical result that
\Tor_{1}^{A}(B,B)=\Tor_{1}^{A}(A/\ker p,A/\ker p)\cong\ker p/(\ker p)^{2}

) yields the exact sequence

It is easy to check that

and, thus,
\Tor_{1}^{A}(B,B)\cong\ker p\otimes_{A}A/\ker p
. Moreover, applying the functor
-\otimes\ker p
to (
3.C
) induces an exact sequence

\ker p\otimes_{A}\ker p\xrightarrow{g}A\otimes_{A}\ker p\to A/\ker p\otimes_{A}\ker p\to 0.

It is straightforward that

. We may conclude that

\Tor_{1}^{A}(B,B)\cong A/\ker p\otimes_{A}\ker p\cong\ker p/(\ker p)^{2}

and thus the statement follows.
∎

4.2.2.

Under

can be readily specified as a corollary of

Corollary 4.18
.

Under

, the twist and cotwist around

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}={\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{R\#G}}(\ker p,-)\colon\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu R\#G)\to\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu R\#G),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}={\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{B}}(C(s\cdot\upbeta),-)\colon\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu B)\to\Dcat^{\mathrm{b}}(\modu B).

This is an immediate consequence of

(as well as its right and left adjoints) preserve bounded complexes of finitely generated modules.

In what follows, we will show that

Proposition 4.19
.

Consider a field
\mathbb{K}
, and let
\mathcal{R}
be a commutative noetherian
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. Suppose that
\Upgamma
is a module finite
\mathcal{R}
-algebra, and let
M
be a biperfect complex of finitely generated
\Upgamma
-bimodules. If

is tilting for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\maxspec\mathcal{R}

is tilting.

Proof.

We will show that

is tilting because it satisfies (a)-(c) of
[
Miy03
, 1.8]
. Namely,

(a)

The bimodule complex
M
is biperfect.

(b)

The left multiplication map
\uplambda\colon\Upgamma\to{\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma}}(M,M)
is an isomorphism in
\Dcat(\Mod\Upgamma\otimes_{k}\Upgamma^{\mathrm{op}})

(c)

\uprho\colon\Upgamma\to{\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma^{\mathrm{op}}}}(M,M)
is an isomorphism in
\Dcat(\Mod\Upgamma\otimes_{k}\Upgamma^{\mathrm{op}})
.

Observe that (a) is true by assumption. To prove (b), we aim to show that the natural left multiplication map
\uplambda\colon\Upgamma\to{\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma}}(M,M)
is a bimodule isomorphism in the
\Dcat(\Mod\Upgamma\otimes_{k}\Upgamma^{\mathrm{op}})
. First, since
M_{\mathfrak{m}}
is tilting for all ideals
\mathfrak{m}\in\maxspec R
, then the natural left multiplication map
\Upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\to{\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}},M_{\mathfrak{m}})
is a quasi-isomorphism. That is,
{\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}},M_{\mathfrak{m}})
is concentrated in degree zero. By
[
DW16
, 2.15]
, and because localisation is exact, for
i\neq 0
,

0=\mathrm{H}^{i}\left({\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}},M_{\mathfrak{m}})\right)\cong\mathrm{H}^{i}\left({\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma}}(M,M)\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{m}}.

Since the module
\mathrm{H}^{i}\left({\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma}}(M,M)\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{m}}
vanishes for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}\in\maxspec R
, necessarily
\mathrm{H}^{i}\left({\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma}}(M,M)\right)=0
for

. Therefore, it suffices to show that

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\uplambda)\colon\Upgamma\to\mathrm{H}^{0}\left({\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma}}(M,M)\right)\cong\Hom_{\Dcat(\Upgamma)}(M,M)

is an isomorphism.

Well, since
M_{\mathfrak{m}}
is tilting, the natural multiplication map

\upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\colon\Upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\to\mathrm{H}^{0}\left({\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}},M_{\mathfrak{m}})\right)\cong\Hom_{\Dcat(\Upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}})}(M_{\mathfrak{m}},M_{\mathfrak{m}})

is an isomorphism. Moreover, it straightforward to check that, under the identification,

\Hom_{\Dcat(\Upgamma)}(M,M)\otimes\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\mathrm{H}^{0}\left({\rm\bf R}{\Hom_{\Upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}},M_{\mathfrak{m}})\right)\cong\Hom_{\Dcat(\Upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}})}(M_{\mathfrak{m}},M_{\mathfrak{m}})

then
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\uplambda)\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{m}}=\upgamma_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Hence,
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\uplambda)\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is an isomorphism for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\maxspec\mathcal{R}
. Thus,
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\uplambda)
must be an isomorphism, as required.

The dual argument works to show that (c) holds. That is, that the natural left multiplication map is an isomorphism.
∎
