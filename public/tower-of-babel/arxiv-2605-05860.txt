A closer target setting approach to boundary problems with the Russell graph measure
Atsushi Hori Kazuyuki Sekitani Nagoya Institute of Technology Seikei University

Abstract
A Russell graph measure (RGM) is one of the standard DEA models, but its efficiency measure is not well-defined—or has unacceptable properties—at the boundary of the non-negative orthant. This is known as a boundary problem. Existing studies have tackled this issue; however, their models may fail to identify an efficient target or fail to satisfy some desirable properties of efficiency measures. In this paper, we incorporate a closer target setting approach into the RGM model with production trade-offs to overcome such issues. We demonstrate that the efficiency measure of the proposed model overcomes the boundary problem and has stronger properties than existing models. We also demonstrate that the efficiency scores of the proposed model can be computed by solving a series of LPs. We conduct a numerical experiment with a real-world dataset to illustrate how targets provided by our model are realistic compared with the existing model, which also suggests the validity of our model in applications.



Keywords—
DEA, closest target,
Russell graph measure, monotonicity, production trade-offs

Introduction

Data envelopment analysis (DEA)
[
3
,
6
]

is an effective nonparametric method for estimating the set of feasible combinations of inputs and outputs, also called the production possibility set (PPS), and
for evaluating the efficiency of decision making units (DMUs) relative to the estimated set.
DEA models are formulated as mathematical programming problems of maximization or minimization, which are flexibly defined by
the choice of PPS and objective function depending on DEA applications.
One of the practical advantages of DEA is to provide an efficiency score and
target for DMUs, which are often computed via linear programming (LP).

Efficiency scoring of an input–output vector of the PPS is a map from
the PPS to the range
[0,1]
, which is referred to as an efficiency measure.
Our research is situated in the field of axiomatic analysis in the literature
on efficiency measurement.
Färe and Lovell
[
10
]
provided three fundamental axioms of
the input-oriented efficiency measure as follows:

•

input-indication (the measure is equal to one if and only if the input vector is technically efficient in the sense of
[
12
]
);

•

input-homogeneity (e.g., doubling all input quantities while holding all outputs constant cuts the measure in half);

•

strong monotonicity in inputs (increasing one input quantity while holding all other inputs and all outputs constant lowers the measure).

By virtue of Russell
[
18
]
, Blackorby and Russell
[
4
]
, and Russell and Schworm
[
19
]
,
the initial axioms
[
10
]
were clarified and extended to axioms of an efficiency measure defined on the full space of positive inputs and positive outputs.
Specifically, Russell and Schworm
[
19
]
showed that no inefficiency measure can satisfy both indication and continuity.
This incompatibility shows that any efficiency measure of DEA models
on the positive full space is classified into two groups:
Those that are continuous in the PPS and those that satisfy indication since any efficiency measure is equivalently reduced to an inefficiency measure.
In fact, the latter group includes representative efficiency measures of popular DEA models such as the Russell graph measure (RGM,
[
9
]
), the slacks-based measure
(SBM,
[
22
]
) which is equivalent to the
enhanced RGM
[
14
]
and the Range adjusted measure
[
7
]
; see also Sueyoshi and Sekitani
[
21
]
.

The RGM
[
9
]

simultaneously accounts for the inefficiency in both inputs and outputs.
The RGM satisfies unit invariance, but it fails to satisfy both
indication and strong monotonicity in the full space of non-negative inputs and non-negative outputs.
Levkoff et al.
[
13
]
pointed out that the failure in the non-negative full space
is caused by the adjustment work for zero output data, which is called the boundary problem.
To address the problem, they slightly modified the objective function of the RGM DEA model.
Their efficiency measure satisfies indication and weak monotonicity,
which is a relaxation of strong monotonicity, but the modified RGM model may have no optimal solution, thereby failing to provide any target for the assessed DMU.

This study aims to overcome the boundary problem by incorporating a closer target setting approach into the RGM model with production trade-offs.
The closer target setting approach assumes that closest targets are very similar to the assessed DMU, and the closest targets also lead to efficiency projections that may be reached with less effort than other alternatives.
Recent developments of the closer target setting DEA models are summarized in
[
1
,
2
,
16
,
17
,
20
]
.
To enhance the reliability of the RGM and the practicality of the targets,
we show that the proposed RGM satisfies strong monotonicity and its target achieves
the least input-distance or the least output-distance.
Moreover, the proposed RGM has a computational advantage such that the efficiency measurement is implemented by solving a series of LPs.

As a relevant study,
Sekitani and Zhao
[
20
]
also developed the closer target setting approach to the RGM model under an empirical production possibility without a trade-offs axiom.
Furthermore, they restricted the assessed DMUs to those with positive input and output data.
Hence, the closer target setting approach to RGM by
[
20
]
cannot overcome
the boundary problem, while our approach can.

The paper unfolds as follows:
Section
2
introduces the notation and describes the
general assumptions on the production possibility set and
desirable properties of efficiency measures.
Section
3
discusses existing RGM-type efficiency measures on trade-offs axioms

Section

Section

discusses the implementation of checking frontier assumptions

which produces outputs with zero input.
Section
6
illustrates the

on Olympic Games performance. Section
7
concludes
this paper.

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle f(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}):=\frac{1}{m+s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\phi_{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}{x}_{ij}+\sum_{t=1}^{K}\pi_{t}r^{-}_{it}\leq\theta_{i}{x_{i}},\ i=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}{y}_{rj}+\sum_{t=1}^{K}\pi_{t}{r}^{+}_{rt}\geq\phi_{r}{y}_{r},\ r=1,\ldots,s,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}=1,

(34)

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\ j=1,\ldots,n,\ \pi_{t}\geq 0,\ t=1,\ldots,K,

\displaystyle 0\leq\theta_{i}\leq 1,\ i=1,\ldots,m,\ 1\leq\phi_{r},\ r=1,\ldots,s.

Denote by
H(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})
the optimal value of the minimization problem (

Let
\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x}

denote

and

(\phi_{1}y_{1},\ldots,\phi_{s}y_{s})
, respectively.
Then, the minimization problem (

)–(

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle f(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})

(37)

s.t.

\displaystyle(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}},~\eqref{P5}

(38)

Let
(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})

\displaystyle(\bm{\theta}^{*}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}^{*}\otimes\bm{y})\in\partial^{s}(T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})

since

)–(

\displaystyle\min\left\{\,f(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\left|\,(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})\in\partial^{s}(T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}),\eqref{P5}\right\}.\right.

(40)

As stated by
[
13
,
20
]
, the minimization problem (

)–(

) has
boundary problems that occur in observed output data containing zero
y_{r}=0
and target input data with zero
\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}=0
.
When
y_{r}=0
for some
r\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
,
Levkoff et al.
[
13
]
showed that the minimization problem (
31
)–(
36
) has no optimal solution, and hence

H(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})
is not well-defined.
Moreover, Levkoff et al.
[
13
, Theorem 1]
showed that the modification of
H(\cdot,\cdot;T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})
for
y_{r}=0
fails to satisfy (I) and (WM).
When
\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}=0
and
x_{i}>0
, Sekitani and Zhao
[
20
]
showed that there exists
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
that fails to satisfy (SM) of
H(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})
.
Moreover, Sekitani and Zhao
[
20
]
reported from
experiments with real-world DEA applications that the minimization
problem (
31
)–(
36
) often provides the optimal input vector

\bm{\theta}^{*}\otimes\bm{x}=\bm{0}
even if

(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
, which is referred to as the

free lunch issue
.
Hence, the optimal input–output vector
(\bm{\theta}^{*}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}^{*}\otimes\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}
with
\bm{\theta}^{*}\otimes\bm{x}=\bm{0}
and
\bm{\phi}^{*}\otimes\bm{y}\in\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{s}_{+}

contradicts the no free lunch production axiom.
The minimization problem (
31
)–(
36
) may provide an unrealistic target for
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
.
To avoid the free lunch issue, Sekitani and Zhao
[
20
]
modified the minimization problem (
40
) into the following maximization problem:
For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
,

\displaystyle\max\left\{\,f(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\left|\,(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})\in\partial^{s}(T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}),\eqref{P5}\right\}.\right.

(41)

However, since they assumed positive inputs and positive outputs,
they did not overcome the boundary problem.

Let

\displaystyle T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}:=\left\{\left(\bm{x},\bm{y}\right)\begin{array}[]{|l}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\leq\bm{x},\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\bm{y}_{j}\geq\bm{y},\\
\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\ j=1,\ldots,n\\
\end{array}\right\}\cap\left(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}_{+}\right).

Färe and Lovell
[
10
]
proposed an input-oriented efficiency measure:
For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny{CRS}}}
,

\displaystyle E_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}):=

\displaystyle\min\left\{\left.\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta(x_{i})\theta_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\delta(y_{r})\cdot 1/\phi_{r}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta(x_{i})+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\delta(y_{r})}\right|(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}},\eqref{P5}\right\},

(45)

where

\delta(z):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $z=0$};\\
1&\text{if $z>0$}.\end{cases}

However,
E_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}})
fails to satisfy both (I) and (WM) on the boundary of
T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}
with
y_{r}=0
.

Then, Levkoff et al.
[
13
]
modified the objective function of the Färe–Lovell efficiency measure

E_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}})
as follows:
For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}
,

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}})

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\left.\bar{f}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\right|(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}},\eqref{P5}\right\},

(46)

where

\displaystyle\bar{f}(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}):=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta(x_{i})\theta_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\psi_{r}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}})\cdot 1/\phi_{r}}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta(x_{i})+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\psi_{r}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}})},

(47)

and

\psi_{r}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if }y_{r}=0\mbox{ and }(\bm{x},\bm{y}+\epsilon\bm{e}_{r})\not\in T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}\mbox{ for any }\epsilon>0;\\
1&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

The modified version
\bar{E}_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\cdot,\cdot;T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}})
satisfies (I) and (WM).

From the definitions of (
45
) and (
46
),
for each
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}\cap\left(\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}_{++}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}})=E_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}),

(48)

and for each
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}_{++}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}_{++}\right)
,

\displaystyle H(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}})=\bar{E}_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}})=E_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny CRS}}).

(49)

The modified version
\bar{E}_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\cdot,\cdot;T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})
also satisfies
the properties (I) and (WM) if
(\bm{0},\bm{y})\not\in T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}
for any
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}_{++}
.

Although the modified efficiency measure by Levkoff et al.
[
13
]
overcomes the boundary problem,
their model fails to provide a target.
For an inefficient DMU, there may exist no feasible solution to (
46
)
that attains the minimum, or efficiency score, and hence
no target representing an improvement from the current state for the inefficient DMU can be identified.

In this study, to enhance
\bar{E}_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\cdot,\cdot;T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})
and
E_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}(\cdot,\cdot;T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})
,
we adopt the maximization model (
41
) by
[
20
]
and overcome the boundary problem.
We show the following properties:

•

The maximization problem (
41
) has an optimal solution for any boundary point
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}
;

•

An efficiency measure defined by the optimal value of (
41
) satisfies (I) and (SM);

•

The optimal value of (
41
) can be provided by solving
(m+s)
linear programming problems.

4
Extended Max Russell Graph Measure

In this section, we demonstrate that the efficiency measure
induced from (
41
) has desired properties (I) and (SM)
under
\partial^{s}(P)=\partial^{w}(P)
.
We also show that the computation of the efficiency score reduces to
solving a series of LPs.

We extend the domain
T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
of
the corresponding efficiency measure for (
41
) into

T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{+}
,
which is indeed identical to
T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}
.
For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}
, we define

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}):=\mbox{ the optimal value of~\eqref{maxRM} },

(50)

and we refer to
\hat{F}
as an
extended
max Russell graph measure
(max RGM).

The following lemma establishes the well-definedness of the proposed efficiency
measure
\hat{F}
by showing that there exists an optimal solution to (
41
)
over
T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For a
PPS

T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}}\in{\cal T}
,
assume that
\partial^{s}(P)=\partial^{w}(P)
.
Then, for any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}
,
the extended max RGM
\hat{F}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})

is well-defined, i.e., the optimal value of (
41
) exists.
Moreover, the following strict inequality holds:

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})>1-\frac{1}{m+s};

(51)

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}I^{+}(\bm{x}):=\left\{\,i\mid x_{i}>0,\ i=1,\dots,m\right\}\text{ and }I^{+}(\bm{y}):=\left\{\,r\mid y_{r}>0,\ r=1,\dots,s\right\}.\end{gathered}

By the assumption
\partial^{s}(P)=\partial^{w}(P)
, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

(\bm{v}^{l},\bm{u}^{l})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
for all
l=1,\ldots,L
.
Let
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}}
and

\phi^{\natural}_{r}:=\min_{l=1,\ldots,L}\frac{\bm{v}^{l}\bm{x}-\sum_{q\not=r}u^{l}_{q}y_{q}-\sigma^{l}}{u^{l}_{r}y_{r}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all $r\in I^{+}(\bm{y})$}.

Since
\bm{v}^{l}\bm{x}-\sum_{q\neq r}u^{l}_{q}y_{q}-\sigma^{l}\geq u^{l}_{r}y_{r}

for all
l=1,\dots,L
, we have
\phi^{\natural}_{r}\geq 1
.
By the definition of
\phi^{\natural}_{r}
for all
r\in I^{+}(\bm{y})
,

\displaystyle\phi^{\natural}_{r}\leq\frac{\bm{v}^{l}\bm{x}-\sum_{q\not=r}u^{l}_{q}y_{q}-\sigma^{l}}{u^{l}_{r}y_{r}}\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{ for all }l=1,\dots,L,

and then a simple calculation leads to

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{l}\bm{x}-\sum_{q\not=r}u^{l}_{q}y_{q}-\sigma^{l}\geq\phi^{\natural}_{r}u^{l}_{r}y_{r}\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{ for all }l=1,\dots,L,

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{l}(\bm{1}_{m}\otimes\bm{x})-\bm{u}^{l}\left(((\phi^{\natural}_{r}-1)\bm{e}_{r}+\bm{1}_{s})\otimes\bm{y}\right)-\sigma^{l}\geq 0\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{ for all }l=1,\dots,L.

Let
\bm{e}_{p}
be a unit vector whose
p
th component is
1
. By the definition of
\phi^{\natural}_{r}
, there exists
\bar{l}\in\{1,\dots,L\}

such that

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{\bar{l}}(\bm{1}_{m}\otimes\bm{x})-\bm{u}^{\bar{l}}\left(((\phi^{\natural}_{r}-1)\bm{e}_{r}+\bm{1}_{s})\otimes\bm{y}\right)-\sigma^{\bar{l}}=0.

(53)

It follows from (
53
) and Lemma
2.2
that

(\bm{1}_{m}\otimes\bm{x},((\phi^{\natural}_{r}-1)\bm{e}_{r}+\bm{1}_{s})\otimes\bm{y})\in\partial^{s}(T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}})
, and

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{m+s}<

\displaystyle\frac{m+s-1+1/\phi_{r}^{\natural}}{m+s}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}1+\sum_{q\not=r}1+1/\phi_{r}^{\natural}}{m+s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\bm{1}_{m},(\phi^{\natural}_{r}-1)\bm{e}_{r}+\bm{1}_{s})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\,f(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\left|\,(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})\in\partial^{s}(T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}),\eqref{P5}\right\}.\right.

(54)

Suppose that

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}\leq\bm{1}_{m},\ \bm{\phi}\geq\bm{1}_{s}\mbox{ and }(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})\in\partial^{s}(T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}}).

Then, for each
l=1,\dots,L
, we have

\bm{v}^{l}(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x})-\bm{u}^{l}(\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})-\sigma^{l}\geq 0
, and hence it follows from
\bm{\theta}\leq\bm{1}_{m}

and
\bm{\phi}\geq\bm{1}_{s}
that

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{l}(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x})-\bm{u}^{l}(\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})-\sigma^{l}\leq\bm{v}^{l}\bm{x}-\sum_{q\neq r}u^{l}_{q}y_{q}-u^{l}_{r}\phi_{r}y_{r}-\sigma^{l}.

Thus, for all
r\in I^{+}(\bm{y})
,

\displaystyle\phi_{r}\leq\frac{\bm{v}^{l}\bm{x}-\sum_{q\neq r}u^{l}_{q}y_{q}-\sigma^{l}}{u^{l}_{r}y_{r}}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for each $l=1,\dots,L$}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\phi_{r}\leq\min_{l=1,\dots,L}\frac{\bm{v}^{l}\bm{x}-\sum_{q\neq r}u^{l}_{q}y_{q}-\sigma^{l}}{u^{l}_{r}y_{r}}=\phi^{\natural}_{r},

which implies that

1\leq\phi_{r}\leq\phi_{r}^{\natural}
for all
r\in I^{+}(\bm{y})
.
Therefore,
the problem (
54
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\,f(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\left|\,\begin{array}[]{l}(\bm{\theta}\otimes\bm{x},\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{y})\in\partial^{s}(T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}),\\
\theta_{i}=1\ (i\notin I^{+}(\bm{x})),\ 0\leq\theta_{i}\leq 1(i\in I^{+}(\bm{x})),\\
\phi_{r}=1\ (r\notin I^{+}(\bm{y})),\ 1\leq\phi_{r}\leq\phi^{\natural}_{r}\ (r\in I^{+}(\bm{y}))\end{array}\right\}\right..

This is because
\theta_{i}x_{i}=0
holds for all
i\not\in I^{+}(\bm{x})
and any
\theta_{i}\in[0,1]
,
and similarly,
\phi_{r}y_{r}=0
holds for all
r\not\in I^{+}(\bm{y})
and any
\phi_{r}\geq 1
.
The feasible region of (
4
) is nonempty and compact, and
the objective function
f
is continuous over the feasible region.
Hence, the problem (
4
) has an optimal solution;
thus the maximum value of (
41
) exists.

The latter assertion is readily shown due to the inequalities (
54
)
and the well-definedness of
\hat{F}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}})
.
∎

As the following theorem indicates, the extended max RGM
\hat{F}
avoids an

allows free lunch, that is,
(\bm{0},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}}

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\partial^{s}(P)=\partial^{w}(P)
.
For
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}
, let

(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
be an optimal solution to (

).
Then,

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{++}\mbox{ and }\theta^{*}_{i}x_{i}>0\mbox{ for each }i\in I^{+}(\bm{x}).

(58)

Proof.

For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}}
, it follows from (
51
) of Lemma
4.1
that

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{x},\bm{y};T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}})=\frac{1}{m+s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta^{*}_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\phi^{*}_{r}}\right)>1-\frac{1}{m+s}=\frac{m+s-1}{m+s}

\displaystyle\iff\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta^{*}_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\phi^{*}_{r}}>m+s-1

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \iff\theta^{*}_{i}&>\left(m-\sum_{p\neq i}\theta^{*}_{p}\right)+\left(s-\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\phi^{*}_{r}}\right)-1\\
&\geq(m-(m-1))+(s-s)-1=0\mbox{\ for all }i=1,\ldots,m,\end{aligned}

which means that
\bm{\theta}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{++}
.
Therefore,
\theta^{*}_{i}x_{i}>0
for each
i\in I^{+}(\bm{x}).

∎

\hat{F}

(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})

).

\hat{F}

\bm{\theta}^{*}\leq\bm{1}_{m}

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{l}(\bm{\theta}^{*}\otimes\bm{x})-\bm{u}^{l}(\bm{\phi}^{*}\otimes\bm{y})-\sigma^{l}\geq 0,\ l=1,\ldots,L,

(59)

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{\bar{l}}\left(\bm{\theta}^{*}\otimes\bm{x}^{\prime}\right)-\bm{u}^{\bar{l}}\left(\bm{\phi}^{*}\otimes\bm{y}^{\prime}\right)<\sigma^{\bar{l}}<\bm{v}^{\bar{l}}\left(\bm{1}_{m}\otimes\bm{x}^{\prime}\right)-\bm{u}^{\bar{l}}\left(\bm{1}_{s}\otimes\bm{y}^{\prime}\right),

means free lunch.
Thus, the modified RGM
E_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}

exposes free lunch of
T_{\mbox{\tiny VRS-TO}}
by the input target of the assessed DMU.

.

.

\bar{E}_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}

4.2

\hat{F}(\bm{x}_{j},\bm{y}_{j};T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}})>\hat{F}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{y}^{\prime};T_{\mbox{\tiny{VRS-TO}}})

.

Conclusions

In this paper, to tackle the boundary problem with the RGM,
we employed the closer target setting approach to the extended PPS.
This approach allows its efficiency measure to satisfy some desirable
properties such as indication and strong monotonicity.
The desirable properties of efficiency measures are valid for the extended PPS under various types of returns to scale.

This approach provides an efficient and no free lunch target.
Moreover, the target achieves the input-oriented or output-oriented least distance to the strong efficiency frontier,
and it requires only a single input or output to be improved.
Hence, the target may be reached from the assessed DMU with less effort.

The computation of the efficiency measure only requires
solving a series of LPs while existing modifications for the RGM require
solving nonlinear programming problems.
Through a numerical experiment with a real-world dataset,
we illustrated how to choose
(R^{-},R^{+})
and
also verified the validity of the targets compared with
E_{\mbox{\tiny FGL}}
.
For given trade-offs representation matrices
(R^{-},R^{+})
,
we demonstrated that it is easy to verify the assumption

\partial^{s}(P)=\partial^{w}(P)
by solving LPs.

The proposed efficiency measure has a lower bound
1-{1}/{(m+s)}
from Lemma
4.1
;
that is, the range of efficiency scores becomes narrower as the number
of inputs and outputs (
m+s
) increases.
We can avoid the narrow range of efficiency scores by changing the objective function

\frac{1}{m+s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}{1}/{\phi_{r}}\right)
to

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}{1}/{\phi_{r}}-(m+s-1)
, whose lower bound is
0
.

As future work, the proposed approach for the RGM can be extended
to other representative non-radial DEA models such as the SBM DEA model
[
8
,
22
]
.
The SBM is a ratio form of input efficiency and output efficiency
[
11
]
while the objective function of the RGM is an additive form.
Comparative studies of empirical benchmarking using non-radial DEA models, e.g., RGM, the proposed efficiency measure, and SBM, are attractive.
