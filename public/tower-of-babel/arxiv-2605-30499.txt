Exact asymptotics of the ruin probability in the Sparre Andersen model for non-life insurance with investments in a Lévy process
Platon Promyslov

Abstract
We establish the exact power-law asymptotics of the ruin probability, as a function of the initial capital, in the Sparre Andersen model for non-life insurance with investments in an arbitrary Lévy process. The main advance over previous work, where only the two-sided order was obtained, is a proof of the existence of an exact limiting equality with a positive finite constant. The method combines a reduction to discrete time, the one-dimensional Kesten–Goldie theorem for the stationary measure of the associated affine stochastic recursion, and Goldie’s result on the asymptotics of the supremum of a perpetuity. The limiting constant is bounded below by the integral Goldie constant of the stationary measure. The exponent coincides with the positive Cramér root of the Laplace exponent of the Lévy process given by the logarithm of the risky-asset price; the distribution of the inter-jump times of the business process affects only the constant, not the exponent. In the final section the constant is compared with an explicit formula in terms of double confluent Heun functions, obtained for the Cramér–Lundberg submodel with proportional investment in a geometric Brownian motion; the agreement of the two approaches is illustrated numerically.

Introduction

The study of the ruin probability of an insurance company in the presence of risky investments goes back to the work of Paulsen
Paulsen1993
, where the dramatic change in the nature of the asymptotics of the ruin probability
\Psi(u)
was first observed—from exponential, in models without investments, to power-law, in models with investments in a risky asset. In parallel, Kalashnikov and Norberg

obtained power-law asymptotics in the Cramér–Lundberg model with risky investments by applying the Kesten–Goldie theory to the associated affine stochastic equation. The theory has developed along two lines: the analytic approach, via integro-differential equations
Paulsen2002
;
Grandits2004
;
GaierGrandits2004
;
AK2026
;
P2601
;
P2604
;
PRYu2026
, and the probabilistic approach, via distributional equations and implicit renewal theory
BDM2016
;
EKS2022
;
Goldie1991
;
KLP2026
;
KP2023
. A modern exposition of the latter is contained in the monograph
BDM2016
, and the general theory of ruin in
AsmussenAlbrecher2010
.

Two-sided estimates for the order of the ruin probability in the Cramér–Lundberg model with investments in a geometric Brownian motion were obtained by Pergamenshchikov and Zeitouni
PergamenshchikovZeitouni2006
. Eberlein, Kabanov and Schmidt
EKS2022
considered the non-life insurance case in the Sparre Andersen model with investments in an arbitrary Lévy process and established the two-sided estimate for the ruin probability

0<\liminf_{u\to\infty}u^{\beta}\Psi(u)\leq\limsup_{u\to\infty}u^{\beta}\Psi(u)<\infty,

(1)

where
\beta>0
is the unique positive root of the cumulant
H(q):=\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{-qV_{T_{1}}}]
, and
V
is the logarithm of the risky-asset price. In

. The existence of an exact limit between the two extremes in (
1

In parallel, in
P2601

\Psi(u)\sim Cu^{-\beta}
with an explicit computable constant
C
was obtained for a particular case—the Cramér–Lundberg model for the life insurance case with proportional investment in a geometric Brownian motion and a constant fraction

-smooth solution to the integro-differential equation for an analogous model, but for the non-life insurance case, was established under the moment condition

The main result of the work is the following: in the Sparre Andersen model for non-life insurance with investments in a general Lévy process, the two-sided estimate (
1

\lim_{u\to\infty}u^{\beta}\Psi(u)=C^{*}\in(0,\infty),

The method rests on three facts. First, the reduction to a discrete-time model from

of the business process, which reduces the problem to the analysis of the tail of the supremum of the discrete chain

\mathbb{P}(\sup_{n}Y_{n}>u)\sim C^{*}u^{-\beta}

\mathbb{P}(\sup_{n}Y_{n}>u)

Conceptually, the exponent

The transfer of the result to the annuity and mixed models is a natural direction for further research and will be the subject of separate papers of the series. In the annuity model the trajectories of

2
The Model and Assumptions

All random elements are defined on a stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
satisfying the usual conditions.

Let
R=(R_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a Lévy process with triplet
(a,\sigma^{2},\Pi)
,
\Pi((-\infty,-1])=0
. The stochastic exponential
\mathcal{E}(R)=e^{V}
is interpreted as the price of the risky asset. We introduce the log-price

V_{t}=at-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}t+\sigma W_{t}+h*(\mu-\nu)_{t}+\bigl(\ln(1+x)-h\bigr)*\mu_{t},

where
h(x)=x\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}
,
\mu
is the jump measure of
R
, and
\nu
is its compensator. Then
V
is again a Lévy process. By
\psi_{V}(q):=\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{-qV_{1}}]
we denote its Laplace exponent.

The business process
P
is independent of
R
and has the form
P_{t}=ct+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}\xi_{i}
, where
N=(N_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a renewal counting process with jump times
0<T_{1}<T_{2}<\dots
; the inter-jump times
U_{i}:=T_{i}-T_{i-1}
,
i\geq 1
, form a sequence of independent identically distributed random variables with law
F
, where
F(\{0\})=0
, and the jumps
\xi_{i}:=\Delta P_{T_{i}}
are independent identically distributed random variables with law
F_{\xi}
,
F_{\xi}(\{0\})=0
. The sequences
(U_{i})
and
(\xi_{i})
are mutually independent. We set
T_{0}:=0
.

In the present work we consider the non-life insurance case:
c>0
and
\xi_{i}<0
almost surely. The process is observed starting from the moment
T_{0}:=0
, which corresponds to the standard setting of
EKS2022
.

The capital process
X^{u}
satisfies the linear stochastic equation

X_{t}^{u}=u+\int_{0}^{t}X_{s-}^{u}\,dR_{s}+P_{t},\qquad X_{0}^{u}=u>0.

We define the ruin time
\tau^{u}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}^{u}\leq 0\}
and the ruin probability
\Psi(u):=\mathbb{P}(\tau^{u}<\infty)
.

By the stochastic Cauchy formula
(
KP2023
, Corollary 3.2)
,

X_{t}^{u}=e^{V_{t}}(u-Y_{t}),\qquad Y_{t}:=-\int_{(0,t]}e^{-V_{s-}}\,dP_{s}.

The strict positivity of
e^{V_{t}}
yields
\tau^{u}=\inf\{t\geq 0:Y_{t}\geq u\}
.

Lemma 1

In the non-life insurance case, the trajectories of
Y
decrease continuously on each interval
(T_{n-1},T_{n})
and have positive jumps at the moments
T_{n}
. Consequently,

\Psi(u)=\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sup_{n\geq 0}Y_{T_{n}}\geq u\Bigr).

(3)

Proof

Between jumps
dP_{s}=c\,ds
, hence
dY_{s}=-e^{-V_{s-}}c\,ds<0
. At the moment
T_{n}
the jump equals
\Delta Y_{T_{n}}=-e^{-V_{T_{n}-}}\xi_{n}>0
because
\xi_{n}<0
. Since
Y
decreases between jumps, the supremum over
[T_{n},T_{n+1})
is attained at
T_{n}
. Hence
\sup_{t\geq 0}Y_{t}=\sup_{n\geq 0}Y_{T_{n}}
, and identity (
3
) follows from
\tau^{u}=\inf\{t:Y_{t}\geq u\}
.

Lemma
1
is a particular case of the reduction to a discrete-time model stated explicitly in
EKS2022
: in the non-life insurance model it suffices to track the reserve process along the sequence of jump times
T_{n}
. Set
Y_{n}:=Y_{T_{n}}
and
M^{*}:=\sup_{n\geq 0}Y_{n}
. By (
3

\Psi(u)=\mathbb{P}(M^{*}\geq u)

Put

M_{m}:=e^{-(V_{T_{m}}-V_{T_{m-1}})},\qquad Q_{m}:=-\int_{T_{m-1}}^{T_{m}}e^{-(V_{s-}-V_{T_{m-1}})}\,dP_{s},\qquad m\geq 1.

By the independence of the increments of the Lévy process
V
, the independence of

and
V
, and the independence and identical distribution of the inter-jump times

, the pairs
(M_{m},Q_{m})_{m\geq 1}

Y_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}M_{i}\Bigr)Q_{k},\qquad Y_{0}=0.

(4)

We emphasize that (

) is not the affine recursion

. The latter holds for the backward (time-reversed) sequence

\widetilde{Y}_{0}:=0,\qquad\widetilde{Y}_{n}:=M_{n}\widetilde{Y}_{n-1}+Q_{n},\quad n\geq 1,

(5)

for which, for each fixed

\widetilde{Y}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Y_{n}
. Indeed,
\widetilde{Y}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\prod_{i=k+1}^{n}M_{i})Q_{k}
is obtained from (

, and since these pairs are independent and identically distributed, reversing the order does not change the joint law. Associated with the recursion (

Y_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}M_{1}\,Y_{\infty}+Q_{1}\qquad(Y_{\infty}\text{ independent of }(M_{1},Q_{1})),

(A1)

The process

Lemma 2

If
\sigma^{2}>0

holds.

Proof

If

Lemma 3

If the Lévy measure

Remark 3

Condition (A2),

Lemma 5

Under assumptions
(A1)–(A3)
the following hold:

(i)

\mathbb{E}[M_{1}^{\beta}]=1
;

(ii)

\mathbb{E}[M_{1}^{\beta}\,|\log M_{1}|]<\infty
;

(iii)

m:=\mathbb{E}[M_{1}^{\beta}\log M_{1}]=H^{\prime}(\beta)>0
;

(iv)

\mathbb{E}[|Q_{1}|^{\beta}]<\infty

Proof

(i) is a reformulation of the definition of

\mathbb{E}[M_{1}^{\beta}]=\mathbb{E}[e^{-\beta V_{T_{1}}}]=e^{H(\beta)}=1.

(ii) follows from

(assumption (A2)): there exists

such that
\beta\pm\delta\in\mathrm{dom}\,H
. Then the elementary inequality

|x|\,e^{-\beta x}\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\bigl(e^{-(\beta-\delta)x}+e^{-(\beta+\delta)x}\bigr),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

) to

. Applying (

and taking expectations,

\mathbb{E}[|V_{T_{1}}|\,e^{-\beta V_{T_{1}}}]\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[e^{-(\beta-\delta)V_{T_{1}}}]+\mathbb{E}[e^{-(\beta+\delta)V_{T_{1}}}]\bigr)=\\
=\frac{1}{\delta}\bigl(e^{H(\beta-\delta)}+e^{H(\beta+\delta)}\bigr)<\infty.

Since

and

, this gives
\mathbb{E}[M_{1}^{\beta}\,|\log M_{1}|]<\infty
.

(iii) is a direct computation (differentiation under the integral sign is justified by (ii)):

m=\mathbb{E}[M_{1}^{\beta}\log M_{1}]=\mathbb{E}[e^{-\beta V_{T_{1}}}\cdot(-V_{T_{1}})]=-\mathbb{E}[V_{T_{1}}\,e^{-\beta V_{T_{1}}}]=H^{\prime}(\beta).

For the positivity

, ensured by (A1)), and

4
Connection with exact analytic solutions in the Cramér–Lundberg model with investments

We compare the established result with the exact analytic solution for the classical submodel—the Cramér–Lundberg model with proportional investment in a geometric Brownian motion, studied in
AK2026
;
PRYu2026
. In this submodel the inter-jump times are exponentially distributed with intensity
\lambda>0
, the price of the risky asset is a geometric Brownian motion, and a fixed fraction
\kappa\in(0,1]
of the capital is invested in the risky asset, the rest in a riskless account with rate
\tilde{r}\geq 0
. The effective log-value of the portfolio is

V_{t}=\bigl(\tilde{a}_{\kappa}-\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{\kappa}^{2}\bigr)t+\tilde{\sigma}_{\kappa}W_{t},\qquad\tilde{a}_{\kappa}:=\tilde{r}+\kappa(\tilde{a}-\tilde{r}),\;\;\tilde{\sigma}_{\kappa}:=\kappa\tilde{\sigma},

a purely diffusive Lévy process; the jumps
\xi_{i}
are strictly negative when
c>0
. The Laplace exponent of
V
is

\psi_{V}(q)=-\bigl(\tilde{a}_{\kappa}-\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{\kappa}^{2}\bigr)q+\tfrac{\tilde{\sigma}_{\kappa}^{2}}{2}q^{2}.

The equation
\psi_{V}(\beta)=0
with
\beta>0
gives

\beta=\frac{2\tilde{a}_{\kappa}}{\tilde{\sigma}_{\kappa}^{2}}-1=\frac{2(\tilde{r}+\kappa(\tilde{a}-\tilde{r}))}{(\kappa\tilde{\sigma})^{2}}-1=\gamma-1,

(10)

where
\gamma:=2\tilde{a}_{\kappa}/\tilde{\sigma}_{\kappa}^{2}
is the standard notation of
AK2026
;
PRYu2026
. By Theorem
3.1
,
\Psi(u)\sim C^{*}u^{-\beta}=C^{*}u^{-\gamma+1}
, which agrees with the known result of
AK2026
; there this asymptotics is established by analytic methods through reduction to a Volterra integral equation, and the constant is obtained explicitly via the solution of a special integral equation. In the more general Sparre Andersen model with an arbitrary distribution of the inter-jump times, the analytic approach via integro-differential equations loses its applicability, since there is no closed second-order equation for
\Psi
, whereas the method considered here, based on Goldie’s theorem and the asymptotics of the supremum of the perpetuity, works without changes.

Model parameters and the exact representation.

For definiteness we fix the control values from
PRYu2026
:
\lambda=1
,
\mu=1
(the parameter of the exponential claim distribution),
\tilde{a}=0.15
,
\tilde{\sigma}=0.4
,
c=0.5
,
\tilde{r}=0.05
,
\kappa=0.9
. From (
10
),

\beta=\frac{2\cdot 0.14}{0.1296}-1\approx 1.1605.

By the exact-solution theorem of
PRYu2026
, the survival probability
\Phi(u)=1-\Psi(u)
for this model is expressed through an integral of the regular branch of the double confluent Heun function
\mathrm{HeunD}

slavyanov
with parameters determined by the set
(\tilde{a},\tilde{\sigma},c,\kappa,\tilde{r},\lambda,\mu)
.

Agreement of the asymptotics.

From the properties of the analytic continuation of the Heun function near the irregular singular point
\zeta=\infty
, studied in
(
PRYu2026

, it follows that the ruin probability has the power-law asymptotics

\Psi(u)\sim C_{\infty}\,u^{-\gamma+1}\quad\text{as }u\to\infty,

\gamma=2((\tilde{a}-\tilde{r})\kappa+\tilde{r})/(\kappa\tilde{\sigma})^{2}

The coincidence of the two methodologies—Goldie’s implicit renewal theory and the analysis of special functions—on their common intersection serves as a nontrivial cross-check of both methods. We note that the work does not recompute the constant itself by the second method: the exponent and the internal consistency of the tail are confirmed numerically, while the equality
C_{\infty}=C^{*}
is established analytically.

5
Discussion

The established result—the exact equality
\lim u^{\beta}\Psi(u)=C^{*}
—gives a complete description of the leading term of the asymptotics of the ruin probability in the Sparre Andersen model for non-life insurance with investments in an arbitrary Lévy process. The exponent
\beta
is the Cramér root of the Laplace exponent
\psi_{V}
of the Lévy process
V
, independent of the distribution of the inter-jump times. The distribution
F
affects only the value of the constant, not the exponent. At the same time, no explicit formula for
C^{*}
itself is available in the general model (see below); only the lower bound
C^{*}\geq C_{+}^{Y}
is known, where
C_{+}^{Y}
has the integral representation (
9
). Compared with the two-sided estimate (
1
) of
EKS2022
;
KLP2026
, the new content is precisely the existence of an exact limit rather than of the order of the asymptotics.

The natural directions for continuing the research are the following.

Annuity models.
For annuity payments (
c<0
,
\xi_{i}>0
a.s.) the trajectories of
Y
have a positive continuous drift between jumps and negative jumps at the moments
T_{n}
, so that
\sup_{t\geq 0}Y_{t}\neq\sup_{n\geq 0}Y_{T_{n}}
in general. The discrete reduction of Lemma
1
ceases to work directly: ruin may occur continuously between jumps, and for the Markov property one has to consider the pair
(X_{t},D_{t})
with the age process
D_{t}
. An analytic study of the annuity model via an integro-differential equation was carried out in
P2601
; the transfer of the result of the present work (by the probabilistic method) to the annuity case requires a substantial adaptation of the technique and is the content of a separate paper.

The mixed model.
When
F_{\xi}
charges both half-axes,
Y_{\infty}
takes values of both signs, and the question of the two-sided tail of
\mathcal{L}(Y_{\infty})
arises: the left tail
\mathbb{P}(Y_{\infty}<-u)
has the same power-law form
\sim C^{-}u^{-\beta}
under appropriate conditions on the support of
\mathcal{L}(Y_{\infty})
(a two-sided version of Proposition 2.5.4 of
BDM2016
). This topic is the content of a separate paper.

Quantitative rate-of-convergence estimates.
The limiting equality obtained does not provide a quantitative estimate of the rate of convergence
|u^{\beta}\Psi(u)-C^{*}|\leq Ku^{-\delta}
. Such estimates in perpetuity asymptotics are known
(
Goldie1991
, Theorem 3.2)
under additional conditions on the moment characteristics of the pair
(M_{1},Q_{1})
. Their transfer to
\Psi(u)
through the links we have built requires separate work.

An explicit formula for
C^{*}
.
Obtaining an explicit (computable) formula for the constant
C^{*}
in the general model is an open question. In the Cramér–Lundberg model with geometric Brownian motion an exact formula is established by other methods (see Section
4
); the agreement of the two approaches on their common intersection gives a nontrivial cross-check of both programs.

Another meaningful direction is the relaxation of the moment condition on the claim sizes
\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{\beta}]<\infty
, which excludes heavy-tailed claims with regularly varying tails. This direction already has some results. In
HultLindskog2011
a model with investments in general semimartingales and heavy-tailed claims is considered. In
GaierGrandits2004
concrete asymptotic estimates are obtained for a model with investments at a constant interest rate under regularly varying claim tails. The theory of regularly varying functions and their probabilistic applications is presented in
BinghamGoldieTeugels1989
, and the classical theory of extremal events in insurance and finance in
EKM1997
. The transfer of the apparatus of the present work to the heavy-tailed case is the content of a separate study. When the condition
\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{\beta}]<\infty
is dropped, Goldie’s theorem ceases to give a purely power-law asymptotics with exponent
\beta
, and the asymptotic behavior is determined by the competition between the claim tail and the Cramér tail of the perpetuity. When the tail of
Q_{1}
varies regularly with exponent less than
\beta
, the tail of
Y_{\infty}
inherits the same regular variation. This result was first established by Grincevicius
Grincevicius1975
. An improved proof and the converse direction were given by Grey
Grey1994
.
