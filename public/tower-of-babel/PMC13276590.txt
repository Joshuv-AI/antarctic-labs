Spontaneous coronary artery dissection with associated takotsubo syndrome: Possible pathogenetic underpinnings
Authors: John E Madias
Published: 2026

Letter to the Editor:

Chi et al. [1] reported on a subgroup of 44 patients from the total of 259 patients of the International Spontaneous Coronary Artery Dissection registry (iSCAD), who have suffered SCAD and a possible concomitant takotsubo syndrome (TTS), termed in the article “a TTS-like angiographic pattern (TVP)”, since follow-up imaging confirming the resolution of the wall motion abnormalities for a definite diagnosis of TTS was not available. The study is important because in addition to the very careful characterization of the patients, the coronary angiography and ventriculography data have been adjudicated by a core laboratory, a crucial prerequisite for a correct diagnosis of SCAD, and its differentiation from coronary pathology, usually accosiated with acute coronary artery syndromes. The authors did not detect any difference in a large number of examined variables between the patients with isolated SCAD and the ones with SCAD/TVP combination, except that coronary revascularization, higher incidence of in-hospital events, heart failure during the hospitalization, and ventricular and supraventricular arrhythmias, were more frequent in the former [1].

The pathophysiology of an established TTS or a TVP in the setting of SCAD is not fully elucidated, and the authors have observed in their patients with SCAD/TVP a “classic apical ballooning or wall motion abnormalities discordant with the dissected coronary territory” [1], and referred to the relevant literature [2], [3], [4], [5], to the exclusion of the reference with the original description of the concept which was reported in 2015, and reads as follows: “The pathogenesis of SCAD is still elusive, but one wonders whether the excessive vigorous contraction of the LV base in conjunction with the adjacent akinetic/dyskinetic systolic ‘ballooning’ of the LV midmyocardium and apex could form a prerequisite anatomic/functional substrate for the causation of SCAD in a predisposed individual” [6]. Accordingly, as this author has repeatedly emphasized in the literature, there is an amphidromic relationship between SCAD and TTS; thus, TTS could be at the roots of SCAD, as its precipitant, and conversely, SCAD could be the physical/emotional trigger for the emergence of TTS. Regarding this “chicken or the egg” causality dilemma, of TTS leading to SCAD or SCAD precipitating TTS, it is conceivable that scrutiny of coronary angiograms, and echocardiograms, along with the time of their performance and the time of the clinical onset of the illness could probably provide some insight as to which of the two pathologies preceded the other. Finally, it may be beneficial to carefully review the coronary angiograms of the iSCAD registry [1], in reference to the site(s) of SCAD and the “hinge” point(s)/plane(s) between the hyperkinetic and abutting hypokinetic/dyskinetic cardiac segments, which will provide support or refutation of the hypothesis that TTS and SCAD are pathogenetically linked [6].
