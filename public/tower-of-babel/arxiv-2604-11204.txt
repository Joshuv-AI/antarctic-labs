Semantic Rate–Distortion Theory: Deductive Compression and Closure Fidelity
Jianfeng Xu

Abstract
Shannon’s rate–distortion theory treats source alphabets as unstructured sets. When the source is a knowledge base equipped with a proof system, a natural fidelity criterion is closure fidelity : a reconstruction is acceptable if it preserves the deductive closure of the original. This paper develops a rate–distortion theory under this criterion. The irredundant core Atom ​ ( S O ) \text{Atom}(S_{O}) —a canonical irredundant generating set, extracted by a fixed-order deletion procedure, from which the full deductive closure can be re-derived—plays a central role. We prove that the zero-distortion semantic rate equals P A ​ H ​ ( π A ) P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}) , where P A P_{A} is the source probability of the core and π A \pi_{A} the conditional core distribution; this is strictly below the classical H ⁡ ( P O ) H(P_{O}) whenever the knowledge base contains redundant states. More generally, the full semantic rate–distortion function decomposes into a contribution from the core alone: redundant states are invisible to both rate and distortion. We derive a semantic source–channel separation theorem exhibiting a semantic leverage phenomenon: under closure fidelity the required source rate is reduced, with an asymptotic i.i.d. leverage factor Λ ∞ = log ⁡ | S O | / ( P A ​ H ​ ( π A ) ) > 1 \Lambda_{\infty}=\log|S_{O}|/(P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}))>1 , enabling the same knowledge base to be communicated with proportionally fewer channel uses—not by violating the Shannon capacity C ⁡ ( W ) C(W) , which remains an upper bound, but because closure-based fidelity renders redundant states free. We also prove a strengthened Fano inequality exploiting core structure. For heterogeneous multi-agent communication, an overlap decomposition yields necessary and sufficient conditions for closure-reliable transmission and identifies a semantic bottleneck in broadcast settings that persists even over noiseless carriers. All results are verified on Datalog instances with up to 24 000 base facts.



I
Introduction

Shannon’s mathematical theory of
communication
[
29
]
deliberately sets aside
the
meaning
of messages, treating source and channel alphabets
as unstructured label sets.
This abstraction has yielded the fundamental limits of data
compression, channel coding, and network information
theory
[
8
,
9
]
, and remains
the foundation of modern digital communications.
Yet already in the companion essay by
Weaver
[
32
]
, a three-level hierarchy was
envisaged: accurate symbol transmission (Level A), conveying intended
meaning (Level B), and achieving the desired effect (Level C).
Shannon’s theory addresses Level A with unmatched precision; the
present paper develops a rigorous
rate–distortion theory
for
Level B that is mathematically compatible with—and strictly
generalizes—Level A.
Crucially, the “meaning” captured here is not a generic similarity
measure or a black-box task metric, but the
deductive content

induced by a shared proof system: the fidelity criterion is
preservation of the deductive closure, and the compression gain arises
from the receiver’s ability to re-derive redundant knowledge—a
mechanism that is structurally distinct from both classical
symbol-level coding and the task/effect-oriented metrics studied at
Level C.

Motivation

The need for such a theory has intensified with the rise of
knowledge-intensive communication systems.
In multi-agent coordination, retrieval-augmented generation pipelines,
and federated knowledge-base synchronization, communicating parties
exchange not raw symbols but structured
semantic states
—ground
facts, rules, or queries drawn from a shared or partially overlapping
logical vocabulary.
A symbol-level error that leaves the deductive content unchanged is
harmless, while a symbol-level “success” that corrupts a single
irredundant axiom may destroy an entire branch of derivable knowledge.
Classical information theory cannot make this distinction because it
treats every source symbol as equally “expensive” to protect.

The key observation motivating this work is that a fixed proof system
shared by communicating agents induces a natural partition of the
source alphabet into an
irredundant core

\text{Atom}(S_{O})
—a canonical irredundant generating set, extracted
via a deterministic deletion procedure under a fixed canonical order,
from which the full deductive closure can be
re-derived—and a collection of
stored shortcuts

J=S_{O}\setminus\text{Atom}(S_{O})
that are deductively redundant.
Note that
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
is irredundant (no element can be removed
without losing closure equivalence) but need not have minimum
cardinality among all generating subsets; the
order-dependent extraction procedure of
Definition
2.1
determines a unique canonical
representative.
Under a
closure-based fidelity criterion
(which deems a
reconstruction acceptable if it preserves the deductive closure), only
the
|\text{Atom}(S_{O})|
core elements need to be transmitted reliably;
the remaining
|J|
states can be recovered by the receiver’s
inference engine at zero additional channel cost.
This suggests that the
effective source entropy
for
closure-reliable communication should be strictly less than the
Shannon entropy
H(P_{O})
—a qualitative prediction that we make
precise and tight in this paper.

Semantic information theory.

Gap.

The works reviewed above illuminate complementary facets of semantic
communication, yet a unified framework that derives

tight rate–distortion bounds
from the proof-system structure
of the communicated knowledge base—and that handles the vocabulary
heterogeneity arising in multi-agent settings—has not been
established.
The present paper addresses this gap.

Approach and Key Insight

Our approach originates from an ontological perspective on
information
[
37
,
16
,
36
,
27
]
:
we model a source as a finite knowledge base
S_{O}
equipped with a
deductive closure operator
\text{Cn}(\cdot)
induced by a fixed proof
system
\mathsf{PS}
, and define
closure distortion

d_{\text{Cn}}
—a Jaccard-distance-based measure under which replacing a
source state by any deductively equivalent substitute incurs zero
penalty.
Under this distortion, redundant states (those in

\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})
) are “free”: they can be mapped to
any output in the closure without cost.
Only the irredundant core elements carry genuine rate, because they
contribute unique deductive content that cannot be recovered from the
remaining knowledge base.

The central quantitative consequence is a
tight
zero-distortion
semantic rate:

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O})\;=\;P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}),

(1.1)

where
P_{A}=P_{O}(\text{Atom}(S_{O}))
and

\pi_{A}(a)=P_{O}(a)/P_{A}
is the conditional core distribution
(Theorem
4.1
).
This is strictly below the classical
R(0;d_{H})=H(P_{O})
whenever

|J|>0
, and the ratio

P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})/H(P_{O})
quantifies the
deductive compression
gain
.
Under a uniform source, the ratio reduces to

k\log k/(|S_{O}|\log|S_{O}|)

where
k=|\text{Atom}(S_{O})|
.

II-A

Proof System and Deductive Closure

We fix a finite set
\mathbb{S}_{O}
of potential semantic states
(the
ambient semantic universe
) equipped with an injective
encoding
\text{enc}_{O}:\mathbb{S}_{O}\to\{0,1\}^{*}
and a fixed canonical
order.
Each element of
\mathbb{S}_{O}
is identified with a ground atom
of an inference fragment

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kb}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}

(typically Datalog or a Horn fragment; see
Appendix
A-A
for the full logical language).
Additional closure properties of
\mathbb{S}_{O}
(closure under
definable recodings, effective representability) are stated in
Assumption
A.3
of
Appendix
A
.

Assumption 2.1

(Fixed effective proof system)
.

We fix an effective proof system
\mathsf{PS}
over the syntax
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kb}}
such that proof checking is
decidable.
For any finite

\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kb}}
and any

\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kb}}
, write

\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm{kb}}\varphi
for derivability
in
\mathsf{PS}
, and define the
deductive closure
operator

\text{Cn}(\Gamma)\;:=\;\bigl\{\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kb}}:\ \Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm{kb}}\varphi\bigr\}.

The operator
Cn
satisfies three standing properties used
throughout without further comment:

(Cn1)

Reflexivity:

\Gamma\subseteq\text{Cn}(\Gamma)
.

(Cn2)

Monotonicity:

\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\text{Cn}(\Gamma)\subseteq\text{Cn}(\Gamma^{\prime})
.

(Cn3)

Idempotence:

\text{Cn}\bigl(\text{Cn}(\Gamma)\bigr)=\text{Cn}(\Gamma)
.

When elements of a state set
S_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
serve as
premises for
Cn
, each state is identified with the corresponding
ground atom of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{kb}}
.

Assumption 2.2

(Finite and effectively listable knowledge bases)
.

The knowledge bases
S_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
considered in this
paper are finite and effectively listable under the fixed canonical
order.
Throughout,
\mathbb{S}_{O}
denotes the
active-domain

semantic universe: for each problem instance (logical structure,
domain, rule set),
\mathbb{S}_{O}
is the finite set of all
ground atoms over the active domain—typically the Herbrand base
restricted to the constants appearing in the
instance
[
1
]
.
The finiteness of
\mathbb{S}_{O}
is thus a per-instance property,
not a restriction on the logical language itself.

Assumption 2.3

(Effective redundancy test)
.

For the knowledge bases considered, the predicate

s\in\text{Cn}(\Gamma)
is decidable whenever

\Gamma\subseteq S_{O}
is finite and
s\in S_{O}
.
This holds in Datalog/Horn settings and bounded-domain
theories
[
1
,
10
]
.

II-B

Irredundant Core and Derivation Depth

Definition 2.1

(Irredundant core)
.

Let
S_{O}
be a finite knowledge base.
Define
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
by the following deterministic procedure:
initialize
A\leftarrow S_{O}
; scan elements of
S_{O}
in canonical
order; for each
s
, if
s\in\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})
, set

A\leftarrow A\setminus\{s\}
; output
A
.

Proposition 2.1

(Core correctness)
.

Under
Assumptions
2.2
–
2.3
,
the set
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
satisfies:

(i)

\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
;

(ii)
for every
a\in A
,

a\notin\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{a\})

(irredundancy)
;

(iii)

\text{Atom}(S_{O})
is uniquely determined by
S_{O}
and
the canonical order;

(iv)

S_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(\text{Atom}(S_{O}))
.

Each removal preserves the closure: if
s\in\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})
,
then
A\subseteq\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})
, so

\text{Cn}(A)\subseteq\text{Cn}(\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\}))=\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})

by (Cn2) and (Cn3).
By induction over the scan,
\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
,
giving (i).
Canonicality (iii) is immediate from the
determinism of the procedure and the fixed canonical order.
Part (iv) follows from (i):
reflexivity (Cn1) gives

S_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})=\text{Cn}(A)
.
Irredundancy (ii): let
A_{\mathrm{scan}}
denote
the current set at the moment
a
is scanned, and let

A_{\mathrm{final}}
denote the output.
Since
a
was retained,

a\notin\text{Cn}(A_{\mathrm{scan}}\setminus\{a\})
.
Subsequent removals only shrink the set, so

A_{\mathrm{final}}\subseteq A_{\mathrm{scan}}
and hence

A_{\mathrm{final}}\setminus\{a\}\subseteq A_{\mathrm{scan}}\setminus\{a\}
.
By monotonicity (Cn2),

\text{Cn}(A_{\mathrm{final}}\setminus\{a\})\subseteq\text{Cn}(A_{\mathrm{scan}}\setminus\{a\})
, so

a\notin\text{Cn}(A_{\mathrm{final}}\setminus\{a\})
.
∎

Definition 2.2

(Core and shortcuts)
.

For a knowledge base
S_{O}
, write

A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
(core premises) and

J:=S_{O}\setminus A
(stored shortcuts).

Remark 2.1

(Proof-system dependence of the core)
.

The irredundant core
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
depends on two choices: the proof
system
\mathsf{PS}

(
which determines the closure
operator
Cn
)
and the canonical order on
S_{O}

(
which resolves ties in the deletion procedure of
Definition
2.1
)
.
Changing either may alter
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
while preserving the closure
equivalence
\text{Cn}(\text{Atom}(S_{O}))=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
All information-theoretic quantities in this paper—the semantic rate

P_{A}H(\pi_{A})
, the leverage factor
\Lambda
, the compression
ratio—are therefore
proof-system-relative
structural
invariants.
This is a feature, not a limitation: it captures the fact that the
“value” of a semantic state for communication depends on the
inference capabilities shared by sender and receiver.

Derivation depth via the immediate consequence
operator.

Definition 2.3

(Immediate consequence operator)
.

Write
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm{kb}}^{1}s
for single-step
derivability.
Define

T_{\mathsf{PS}}(\Gamma):=\Gamma\cup\{s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}:\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm{kb}}^{1}s\}
,
with iteration

T^{0}_{\mathsf{PS}}(\Gamma):=\Gamma
and

T^{n+1}_{\mathsf{PS}}(\Gamma):=T_{\mathsf{PS}}(T^{n}_{\mathsf{PS}}(\Gamma))
.

Axiom 2.1

(Properties of
T_{\mathsf{PS}}
)
.

The operator satisfies:

(IC1)
monotonicity;

(IC2)
computability (finite output for finite input);

(IC3)
closure characterization:

\text{Cn}(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}T^{n}_{\mathsf{PS}}(\Gamma)
;

(IC4)
finite stabilization.
These hold in Datalog and Horn-clause settings over finite
domains
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.4

(Derivation depth)
.

For finite
B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
and

s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
,

\text{Dd}(s\mid B)\;:=\;\min\bigl\{n\geq 0:s\in T^{n}_{\mathsf{PS}}(B)\bigr\},

with
\text{Dd}(s\mid B):=\infty
if
s\notin\text{Cn}(B)
.

Lemma 2.1

(Properties of derivation depth)
.

Under Axiom
2.1
, for finite

B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
:

(i)

\text{Dd}(s\mid B)
is a unique, finite, computable
non-negative integer for every
s\in\text{Cn}(B)
;

(ii)

\text{Dd}(s\mid B)=0
iff
s\in B
;

(iii)
if
B\subseteq B^{\prime}
and
s\in\text{Cn}(B)
, then

\text{Dd}(s\mid B^{\prime})\leq\text{Dd}(s\mid B)
.

Part (i): by (IC4), the chain stabilizes at

\text{Cn}(B)
in finitely many steps, so

\{n:s\in T^{n}(B)\}\neq\varnothing
and its minimum is finite;
computability follows from (IC2).
Part (ii):
T^{0}(B)=B
.
Part (iii): monotonicity (IC1) gives

T^{n}(B)\subseteq T^{n}(B^{\prime})
by induction, so

\min\{n:s\in T^{n}(B^{\prime})\}\leq\min\{n:s\in T^{n}(B)\}
.
∎

(Intrinsic and operational depths)
.

For

n_{\mathrm{int}}(q):=\text{Dd}(q\mid A)
and

(Semantic atomicity)
.

The

\mathsf{A}(\mathcal{I}):=|\text{Atom}(S_{O})|

(Maximum intrinsic derivation depth)
.

The

\mathsf{D_{d}}(\mathcal{I})\;:=\;\max_{q\,\in\,S_{O}}\;\text{Dd}\bigl(q\mid\text{Atom}(S_{O})\bigr),

each
\text{Dd}(q\mid A)
is finite and computable by
Lemma
2.1
(i); the maximum over the finite
set
S_{O}
is computable by enumeration.
Part (ii):
A\subseteq S_{O}
gives

\text{Dd}(q\mid S_{O})\leq\text{Dd}(q\mid A)
by
Lemma
2.1
(iii); the bound by

\mathsf{D_{d}}
is immediate from the definition.
Part (iii): if
A=S_{O}
, every

q\in S_{O}=T^{0}(A)
has depth
0
; conversely, depth
0
for
all
q
implies
S_{O}\subseteq A
by
Lemma
2.1
(ii), and
A\subseteq S_{O}
by
construction.
∎

II-C

Noisy Information and Closure Fidelity

When the sender’s knowledge base
S_{O}
and the receiver’s
reconstructed space
\hat{S}_{O}
differ, the discrepancy is
captured by a
noise pair
.

Definition 2.8

(Noisy semantic base)
.

A
noisy semantic base
of
S_{O}
is any set

\tilde{S}_{O}:=(S_{O}\setminus S_{O}^{-})\cup S_{O}^{+}
, where

S_{O}^{-}\subseteq S_{O}
(lost states) and

S_{O}^{+}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}\setminus S_{O}
(spurious states).
The pair
(S_{O}^{-},S_{O}^{+})
is the
noise pair
; it is

trivial
when both sets are empty.

Definition 2.9

(Closure fidelity)
.

For finite
S,\hat{S}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
,

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S,\hat{S})\;:=\;\frac{|\text{Cn}(S)\cap\text{Cn}(\hat{S})|}{|\text{Cn}(S)\cup\text{Cn}(\hat{S})|},

with
0/0:=1
.
We have
\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}=1
iff
\text{Cn}(S)=\text{Cn}(\hat{S})
.

Definition 2.10

(Core preservation ratio)
.

For
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and any

\hat{S}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
,

\rho_{\text{Atom}}(S_{O},\hat{S}):=|A\cap\hat{S}|/|A|

(with
0/0:=1
).

Proposition 2.2

(Noise pair, core preservation, and closure fidelity)
.

Let
\tilde{S}_{O}=(S_{O}\setminus S_{O}^{-})\cup S_{O}^{+}
and

A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.

(i)

\rho_{\text{Atom}}(S_{O},\tilde{S}_{O})=1
iff

A\cap S_{O}^{-}=\varnothing
.

(ii)

If
A\cap S_{O}^{-}=\varnothing
, then

\text{Cn}(S_{O})\subseteq\text{Cn}(\tilde{S}_{O})
.

(iii)

If
A\cap S_{O}^{-}=\varnothing
and

S_{O}^{+}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, then

\text{Cn}(S_{O})=\text{Cn}(\tilde{S}_{O})
and

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S_{O},\tilde{S}_{O})=1
.

(iv)

Trivial noise implies
\rho_{\text{Atom}}=1
and

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}=1
.

(i):
Since
A\subseteq S_{O}
and

S_{O}^{+}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}\setminus S_{O}
, we have

A\cap S_{O}^{+}=\varnothing
, hence

A\cap\tilde{S}_{O}=A\setminus S_{O}^{-}
and

\rho_{\text{Atom}}=|A\setminus S_{O}^{-}|/|A|=1
iff

A\cap S_{O}^{-}=\varnothing
.

(ii):
If
A\cap S_{O}^{-}=\varnothing
, then

A\subseteq S_{O}\setminus S_{O}^{-}\subseteq\tilde{S}_{O}
.
By (Cn2),

\text{Cn}(A)\subseteq\text{Cn}(\tilde{S}_{O})
, and

\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
by
Proposition
2.1
(i).

(iii):
By (ii),
\text{Cn}(S_{O})\subseteq\text{Cn}(\tilde{S}_{O})
.
For the reverse:

S_{O}\setminus S_{O}^{-}\subseteq S_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})

by (Cn1), and
S_{O}^{+}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
by hypothesis,
so
\tilde{S}_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
By (Cn2) and (Cn3),

\text{Cn}(\tilde{S}_{O})\subseteq\text{Cn}(\text{Cn}(S_{O}))=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.

(iv): Immediate from
\tilde{S}_{O}=S_{O}
.
∎

Remark 2.2

(Zero-distortion property of redundant states)
.

Proposition
2.2
(iii)
has a
per-state counterpart crucial for the rate–distortion
analysis: if
j\in J=S_{O}\setminus\text{Atom}(S_{O})
, then

j\in\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{j\})
, so replacing
j
by any

\hat{s}\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})
preserves the deductive closure.
Errors on redundant states incur zero closure distortion—a
property absent from any classical distortion measure and the
source of the deductive compression gain formalized in
Section
IV
.

III
Semantic Channel

This section erects the probabilistic layer on the structural
framework of Section
II
.
The central object is the
semantic channel
: a composition of
Markov kernels—encoding, carrier transmission, and decoding—each
constrained by the enabling structure of an underlying information
model (Definition
A.3
).

Notation.

All state spaces are finite.
A
probability distribution
on a nonempty finite set
S
is a
function
P:S\to[0,1]
with
\sum_{s}P(s)=1
; write

\Delta(S)
for the probability simplex and

\supp(P):=\{s:P(s)>0\}
.
A
Markov kernel

\kappa:X\rightsquigarrow Y
is a function

\kappa:X\times Y\to[0,1]
with

\kappa(\cdot\mid x)\in\Delta(Y)
for each
x
;
kernels compose by

(\kappa_{2}\circ\kappa_{1})(z\mid x):=\sum_{y}\kappa_{1}(y\mid x)\,\kappa_{2}(z\mid y)
.
A kernel is
deterministic
if

|\supp(\kappa(\cdot\mid x))|=1
for all
x
.
Shannon entropy, conditional entropy, and mutual information are
denoted
H(\cdot)
,
H(\cdot\mid\cdot)
, and

I(\cdot\,;\cdot)
(base 2,
bits)
[
8
]
.
Random variables are in sans-serif
(
\mathsf{S}_{o},\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o}
); expectations are
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
.

III-A

Enabling Kernels and the Semantic Channel

Definition 3.1

(Semantic source)
.

A
semantic source
is a pair
(S_{O},P_{O})
with

P_{O}\in\Delta(S_{O})
.
It is
full-support
if
\supp(P_{O})=S_{O}
and

uniform
if
P_{O}\equiv 1/|S_{O}|
.

Definition 3.2

(Enabling kernel)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an information model with enabling map

\mathcal{E}:S_{O}\Rightarrow S_{C}

(Axiom
A.3
).
An
enabling kernel
for
\mathcal{I}
is a Markov kernel

\kappa:S_{O}\rightsquigarrow S_{C}
satisfying

\supp\bigl(\kappa(\cdot\mid s_{o})\bigr)\;\subseteq\;\mathcal{E}(s_{o}),\qquad\forall\,s_{o}\in S_{O}.

(3.1)

Write
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I})
for the set of all enabling kernels
for
\mathcal{I}
.
By Axiom
A.3
(E3), the deterministic kernel

\kappa_{e}(s_{c}\mid s_{o}):=\mathbf{1}[s_{c}=e(s_{o})]
belongs to

\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I})
, so
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I})\neq\varnothing
.

Proposition 3.1

(Enabling kernels compose)
.

If
(\mathcal{I}_{1},\mathcal{I}_{2})
is composable

(Definition
A.4
)
and

\kappa_{i}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I}_{i})
for
i=1,2
, then

\kappa_{2}\circ\kappa_{1}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I}_{2}\circ\mathcal{I}_{1})
.

If
(\kappa_{2}\circ\kappa_{1})(s^{\prime}\mid s_{o})>0
, there exists

s_{c}
with
\kappa_{1}(s_{c}\mid s_{o})>0
and

\kappa_{2}(s^{\prime}\mid s_{c})>0
.
The enabling constraints give

s_{c}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(s_{o})
and

s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{2}(s_{c})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{2\circ 1}(s_{o})
.
∎

We now define the semantic channel as a three-stage composition.

Definition 3.3

(Semantic channel)
.

A
semantic channel
is a tuple

\mathfrak{C}=(\mathcal{I},\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{ch}},\,\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dec}},\,\kappa_{\text{enc}},\,W,\,D)
,
where:

(i)

\mathcal{I}
is the sender’s information model
(Definition
A.3
) with encoding kernel

\kappa_{\text{enc}}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I})
,
mapping
S_{O}\rightsquigarrow S_{C}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{ch}}
is a carrier channel model
with
S_{O}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{ch}})=S_{C}
,
carrier state set
\hat{S}_{C}
, and carrier channel
kernel
W\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{ch}})
,
mapping
S_{C}\rightsquigarrow\hat{S}_{C}
;

(iii)

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dec}}
is a decoding model with

S_{O}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dec}})=\hat{S}_{C}
,
reconstructed space

\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
, and decoding kernel

D\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dec}})
,
mapping
\hat{S}_{C}\rightsquigarrow\hat{S}_{O}
.

The
end-to-end kernel
is

\kappa_{\mathrm{sem}}:=D\circ W\circ\kappa_{\text{enc}}:S_{O}\rightsquigarrow\hat{S}_{O}.

(3.2)

By Proposition
3.1
(applied twice),

\kappa_{\mathrm{sem}}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{sem}})

where

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{sem}}:=\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dec}}\circ\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{ch}}\circ\mathcal{I}

is the composite information model

(Definition
A.4
,
Remark
A.1
)
.
Since
\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
, the proof system

(\mathsf{PS},T_{\mathsf{PS}},\text{Cn})
acts on
\hat{S}_{O}
,
making
\text{Cn}(\hat{S}_{O})
,
\text{Atom}(\hat{S}_{O})
, and

\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid\text{Atom}(\hat{S}_{O}))
well-defined.

Definition 3.4

(End-to-end noise pair)
.

Setting
\tilde{S}_{O}:=\hat{S}_{O}
, the reconstructed space is
a noisy semantic base of
S_{O}

(Definition
2.8
)
with

S_{O}^{-}:=S_{O}\setminus\hat{S}_{O}
and

S_{O}^{+}:=\hat{S}_{O}\setminus S_{O}
.
By Proposition
2.2
, the core preservation
and closure fidelity properties hold with this noise pair.

Definition 3.5

(Ideal semantic channel)
.

A semantic channel is
ideal
if all three constituent
models are ideal (Definition
A.7
) and the
kernels are the deterministic bijections induced by the
synonymy witnesses.
In this case
\kappa_{\mathrm{sem}}
is a deterministic
bijection and
S_{O}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}\tilde{S}_{O}

(Proposition
A.2
)
.
When additionally
\tilde{S}_{O}=S_{O}
and

\tau_{\mathrm{e2e}}=\mathrm{id}_{S_{O}}
, the noise pair is
trivial.

III-B

Semantic Distortion

Definition 3.6

(Distortion function)
.

A
distortion function
is any

d:S_{O}\times\hat{S}_{O}\to[0,\infty)
with

d(s,s)=0
for
s\in S_{O}\cap\hat{S}_{O}
.
It is
normalized
if
d\leq 1
.

Definition 3.7

(Hamming distortion)
.

d_{H}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}):=\mathbf{1}[s_{o}\neq\hat{s}_{o}]
.

Definition 3.8

(Closure distortion)
.

For a reference base
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}

(typically
\Gamma=S_{O}
),
write
\Gamma_{-s}:=\Gamma\setminus\{s\}
,

C_{s}:=\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{s\})
, and

C_{\hat{s}}:=\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\})
.
The
closure distortion
is the Jaccard distance

d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid\Gamma):=1-\frac{|C_{s}\cap C_{\hat{s}}|}{|C_{s}\cup C_{\hat{s}}|},

(3.3)

with
0/0:=0
.
We abbreviate

d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}):=d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})
.

Remark 3.1

(Zero distortion on redundant states)
.

Two properties follow from the
Cn
-axiomatics:

(a)
if
s_{o}\in\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}})
(redundant), then
any
\hat{s}_{o}\in\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}})
yields

d_{\text{Cn}}=0
;

(b)
if
s_{o}\in\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and
\Gamma=S_{O}
,
then
s_{o}\notin\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}})
, so the replacement
genuinely matters.

Definition 3.9

(Depth distortion)
.

Let
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and

d_{\max}:=\mathsf{D_{d}}(\mathcal{I})

(Definition
2.7
)
.
Define

d_{\text{Dd}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}):=\begin{cases}\min\!\bigl(\frac{|\text{Dd}(s_{o}\mid A)-\text{Dd}(\hat{s}_{o}\mid A)|}{\max(d_{\max},1)},\,1\bigr)&\text{if }\hat{s}_{o}\in\text{Cn}(A),\\
1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Definition 3.10

(Composite semantic distortion)
.

For weights
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
with

\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1
,

d_{\mathrm{sem}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}):=\alpha\,d_{H}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o})+\beta\,d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o})+\gamma\,d_{\text{Dd}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}).

(3.4)

Setting
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(1,0,0)
recovers Hamming distortion;

(0,1,0)
yields a purely deductive-content measure.

Definition 3.11

(Expected distortion)
.

For a semantic source
(S_{O},P_{O})
and distortion
d
,

\bar{d}(\mathfrak{C},P_{O}):=\sum_{s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}}P_{O}(s_{o})\,\kappa_{\mathrm{sem}}(\hat{s}_{o}\mid s_{o})\,d(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}).

(3.5)

Definition 3.12

(Per-input expected distortion)
.

For any
s_{o}\in S_{O}
and distortion
d
,

\bar{d}(s_{o}\mid\mathfrak{C})\;:=\;\sum_{\hat{s}_{o}}\kappa_{\mathrm{sem}}(\hat{s}_{o}\mid s_{o})\,d(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}).

When
d=d_{\text{Cn}}
, we write

\bar{d}_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o}\mid\mathfrak{C})
.

Proposition 3.2

(Noise-pair bounds on closure distortion)
.

Let
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.
If
A\cap S_{O}^{-}=\varnothing
and

S_{O}^{+}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, then for every

s_{o}\in S_{O}\setminus A
and

\hat{s}_{o}\in\tilde{S}_{O}
,

d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0
.
Consequently,

\bar{d}_{\text{Cn}}(\mathfrak{C},P_{O})\leq P_{O}(A)\cdot\max_{a\in A}\bar{d}_{\text{Cn}}(a\mid\mathfrak{C})
.

For
s_{o}\in S_{O}\setminus A
,

\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\})=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
By Proposition
2.2
(iii),

\text{Cn}(\tilde{S}_{O})=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
Since

S_{O}\setminus S_{O}^{-}\subseteq S_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})

(by (Cn1)) and

S_{O}^{+}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
by hypothesis,

\tilde{S}_{O}=(S_{O}\setminus S_{O}^{-})\cup S_{O}^{+}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
,
so in particular

\hat{s}_{o}\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
Since
s_{o}\in J
, we have

\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\})=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
Because

\hat{s}_{o}\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\})
,
the set

(S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\})\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\})
,
so by monotonicity (Cn2) and
idempotence (Cn3),

\text{Cn}((S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\})\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\})=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
The reverse inclusion follows from

S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\}\subseteq(S_{O}\setminus\{s_{o}\})\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\}

and monotonicity.
Hence
C_{s_{o}}=C_{\hat{s}_{o}}=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
and

d_{\text{Cn}}=0
.
Non-core states contribute zero to the expected distortion;
the bound follows.
∎

III-C

Semantic Channel Invariants

Definition 3.13

(Semantic mutual information and capacity)
.

For a semantic source
(S_{O},P_{O})
and semantic
channel
\mathfrak{C}
, the
semantic mutual information

is

I_{\mathrm{sem}}(P_{O},\mathfrak{C}):=I(\mathsf{S}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o})

under the joint

P_{O}(s_{o})\,\kappa_{\mathrm{sem}}(\hat{s}_{o}\mid s_{o})
.
The
Shannon capacity
of the carrier channel is

C(W):=\max_{P_{C}}I(\mathsf{S}_{c};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c})
.
The
semantic channel capacity
is

C_{\mathrm{sem}}(W):=\max_{P_{O},\,\kappa_{\text{enc}}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I}),\,D\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dec}})}I_{\mathrm{sem}}(P_{O},\mathfrak{C}).

(3.6)

Remark 3.2

(Source of “semantic” in
I_{\mathrm{sem}}
)
.

The semantic mutual information
I_{\mathrm{sem}}
is defined as
the standard Shannon mutual information and satisfies the same
algebraic properties.
Its “semantic” qualifier refers not to the functional form but to
the
context
in which it is evaluated: the encoding and
decoding kernels are constrained by enabling maps

(Definition
3.2
)
, and the performance
criteria are closure-based distortion measures

(Section
III-B
)
rather than symbol-level
metrics.
The semantic novelty thus enters through the feasible set and the
fidelity criterion, not through a redefinition of mutual information
itself.

Theorem 3.1

(Data processing bound)
.

(i)

C_{\mathrm{sem}}(W)\leq C(W)
.

(ii)

C_{\mathrm{sem}}(W)\leq\log|S_{O}|
.

(iii)

If the enabling maps are full

(
\mathcal{E}(s_{o})=S_{C}
,

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\hat{s}_{c})=\hat{S}_{O}

for all inputs
)
and the alphabet sizes satisfy

|S_{O}|\geq|S_{C}|
and
|\hat{S}_{O}|\geq|\hat{S}_{C}|

(
so that a deterministic surjection

f:S_{O}\to S_{C}
and a deterministic injection

g:\hat{S}_{C}\to\hat{S}_{O}
exist
)
, then

C_{\mathrm{sem}}(W)=C(W)
.

(i): The Markov chain

\mathsf{S}_{o}\to\mathsf{S}_{c}\to\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c}\to\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o}
and the data processing
inequality
[
8
]
give

I(\mathsf{S}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o})\leq I(\mathsf{S}_{c};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c})\leq C(W)
.
(ii):
I_{\mathrm{sem}}\leq H(\mathsf{S}_{o})\leq\log|S_{O}|
.
(iii): Let
P_{C}^{*}
achieve
C(W)
.
Choose a deterministic surjection

f:S_{O}\to S_{C}
, set
P_{O}
so that

P_{C}^{*}=f_{\#}P_{O}
, and take a deterministic injection

g:\hat{S}_{C}\to\hat{S}_{O}
.
Then

I(\mathsf{S}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o})=I(\mathsf{S}_{o};g(\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c}))=I(\mathsf{S}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c})=I(\mathsf{S}_{c};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c})=C(W)
,
where the second equality uses the invertibility of
g
,
and the third uses the Markov chain

\mathsf{S}_{o}\to\mathsf{S}_{c}\to\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c}

(since the encoding is deterministic,

H(\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c}\mid\mathsf{S}_{o})=H(\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c}\mid\mathsf{S}_{c})
,
giving

I(\mathsf{S}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c})=I(\mathsf{S}_{c};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c})
).
∎

Remark 3.3

(Role of the size condition in
Theorem
3.1
(iii))
.

The condition
|S_{O}|\geq|S_{C}|
ensures the existence of
a surjection
f:S_{O}\to S_{C}
used to push forward the
capacity-achieving input distribution; it is a sufficient
condition for the single-letter equality

C_{\mathrm{sem}}=C(W)
.
All block-coding results in this paper

(Theorems
3.4
–
3.5

and Section
V
)
use
C(W)
directly
via
S_{C}^{n}
and do not require this condition.

Proposition 3.3

(Enabling-constrained capacity bound)
.

If the encoding enabling map satisfies

|\mathcal{E}(s_{o})|\leq k_{\mathrm{enc}}
for all
s_{o}\in S_{O}
,
then

C_{\mathrm{sem}}(W)\;\leq\;\log\!\Bigl|\bigcup_{s_{o}\in S_{O}}\mathcal{E}(s_{o})\Bigr|\;\leq\;\log\bigl(|S_{O}|\cdot k_{\mathrm{enc}}\bigr).

(3.7)

In particular, when the enabling map is singleton-valued

(
k_{\mathrm{enc}}=1
)
, as in an ideal
channel
(Definition
3.5
)
,

C_{\mathrm{sem}}(W)\leq\log|S_{O}|
, reproducing the source
entropy bound of
Theorem
3.1
(ii)
.

By the enabling support constraint (
3.1
),

\supp(P_{C})\subseteq\bigcup_{s_{o}}\mathcal{E}(s_{o})
, so

H(\mathsf{S}_{c})\leq\log|\bigcup_{s_{o}}\mathcal{E}(s_{o})|
.
Data processing gives

I(\mathsf{S}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o})\leq I(\mathsf{S}_{c};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{c})\leq H(\mathsf{S}_{c})
.
The second inequality uses

|\bigcup_{s_{o}}\mathcal{E}(s_{o})|\leq|S_{O}|\cdot k_{\mathrm{enc}}
.
∎

Definition 3.14

(Structural quality indices)
.

The
semantic fidelity index
and
depth expansion
index
of
\mathfrak{C}
are

\displaystyle\mathsf{F}(\mathfrak{C})

\displaystyle:=1-\max_{s_{o}\in S_{O}}\sum_{\hat{s}_{o}}\kappa_{\mathrm{sem}}(\hat{s}_{o}\mid s_{o})\,d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O}),

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}(\mathfrak{C})

\displaystyle:=\max_{s_{o}\in S_{O}}\sum_{\hat{s}_{o}}\kappa_{\mathrm{sem}}(\hat{s}_{o}\mid s_{o})\,d_{\text{Dd}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}).

(3.9)

Both lie in
[0,1]
.
For any
P_{O}
,

\bar{d}_{\text{Cn}}\leq 1-\mathsf{F}
and

\bar{d}_{\text{Dd}}\leq\mathsf{E}
.

Corollary 3.1

(Fidelity concentration on core)
.

Under the conditions of
Proposition
3.2
,

\mathsf{F}(\mathfrak{C})=1-\max_{a\in\text{Atom}(S_{O})}\bar{d}_{\text{Cn}}(a\mid\mathfrak{C})
:
the worst-case closure distortion is attained at a core element.

Definition 3.15

(Noise-pair indices)
.

Let
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and

\tilde{S}_{O}^{\cap}:=S_{O}\cap\tilde{S}_{O}=S_{O}\setminus S_{O}^{-}

denote the preserved region.
The
probabilistic core preservation index

Remark 3.4

(General case: confusability structure and

Remark 3.5

(Computation and source–channel separation)
.

The semantic rate–distortion function

can be computed to arbitrary
precision via a variant of the Blahut–Arimoto
algorithm
[
8
]
: the standard alternating
minimization applies because the feasible set is a compact
subset of a finite-dimensional probability simplex and the
objective
I(\mathsf{S}_{o};\hat{\mathsf{S}}_{o})
is convex
in the conditional distribution for fixed
P_{O}
.
Han et al.
[
14
]
have recently developed an
extended Blahut–Arimoto algorithm for computing semantic
rate–distortion functions under synonymous mappings.
Their alternating-minimization framework is conceptually related
to ours and can potentially be adapted to the closure-distortion
setting; a formal verification that the feasibility structure
of
d_{\text{Cn}}
satisfies the regularity conditions of their
convergence proof is an interesting algorithmic direction.
The classical source–channel separation
theorem
[
29
,
8
]

carries over verbatim to the semantic framework: semantic
source coding at rate
R_{\mathrm{sem}}(D)
followed by
channel coding at rate
C(W)
is optimal.
The semantic novelty is that

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(D;d_{\text{Cn}})<R(D;d_{H})
for

near zero whenever
|J|>0
, yielding a strict
improvement in the achievable distortion–capacity operating
point.

d_{\text{Cn}}(\cdot,\cdot\mid S_{O})
with

\hat{S}_{O}\supseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})\cap\mathbb{S}_{O}
and
Assumption
3.1
:

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O})\;=\;P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}).

(4.1)

Under the uniform source:

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0)=\frac{k}{|S_{O}|}\log k
.
The semantic compression gain relative to Hamming fidelity is

\frac{R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\,d_{\text{Cn}})}{R(0;\,d_{H})}\;=\;\frac{P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})}{H(P_{O})}\;<\;1\quad\text{whenever }k<|S_{O}|.

(4.2)

We establish the lower and upper bounds separately.

Lower bound.

Let
P_{\hat{S}\mid S}
be any conditional distribution achieving

\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}(S_{o},\hat{S}_{o}\mid S_{O})]=0
.
Define the auxiliary random variable

T:\,S_{O}\to A\cup\{*\}
by

T(s_{o}):=s_{o}
if
s_{o}\in A
and

T(s_{o}):=*
if
s_{o}\in J
.
Since
T
is a deterministic function of
S_{o}
, the data
processing inequality gives

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})\;\geq\;I(T;\hat{S}_{o}).

(4.3)

We now lower-bound the right-hand side.

Under Assumption
3.1
, the zero-distortion
constraint forces

\supp(P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a))\subseteq R_{\text{Cn}}(a)

for each
a\in A
, where

R_{\text{Cn}}(a):=\{\hat{s}_{o}\in\hat{S}_{O}:d_{\text{Cn}}(a,\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0\}

(Assumption
3.1
),
with the sets
\{R_{\text{Cn}}(a)\}_{a\in A}
pairwise disjoint.
For redundant states
j\in J
,

P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid j)
is unconstrained on

\text{Cn}(S_{O})\cap\hat{S}_{O}

(Remark
3.1
(a)).

Let
Q_{j}:=P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid j)
for each
j\in J
,
and define the average redundant output distribution

Q:=\sum_{j\in J}[P_{O}(j)/P_{J}]\,Q_{j}
.
Write

\bar{\pi}:=\sum_{a\in A}\pi_{A}(a)\,P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a)

for the average core output distribution.
Since the supports
R_{\text{Cn}}(a)
are pairwise disjoint,
the entropy of the mixture
\bar{\pi}
decomposes by the
standard disjoint-support identity

[
8
, Theorem 2.7.3]
:

H(\bar{\pi})\;=\;H(\pi_{A})+\sum_{a\in A}\pi_{A}(a)\,H\bigl(P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a)\bigr).

(4.4)

The conditional entropy of
\hat{S}_{o}
given
T
is

H(\hat{S}_{o}\mid T)=P_{A}\sum_{a}\pi_{A}(a)\,H(P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a))+P_{J}\,H(Q),

and the output marginal is

P_{\hat{S}}=P_{A}\,\bar{\pi}+P_{J}\,Q
.
By the concavity of Shannon
entropy
[
8
, Theorem 2.7.3]
,

H(P_{A}\,\bar{\pi}+P_{J}\,Q)\;\geq\;P_{A}\,H(\bar{\pi})+P_{J}\,H(Q).

(4.5)

Therefore, using (
4.4
):

\displaystyle I(T;\hat{S}_{o})

\displaystyle=H(P_{A}\,\bar{\pi}+P_{J}\,Q)-H(\hat{S}_{o}\mid T)

\displaystyle\geq P_{A}\,H(\bar{\pi})+P_{J}\,H(Q)-P_{A}\!\sum_{a}\pi_{A}(a)\,H(P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a))-P_{J}\,H(Q)

\displaystyle=P_{A}\bigl[H(\bar{\pi})-\!\sum_{a}\pi_{A}(a)\,H(P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a))\bigr]

\displaystyle=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}).

(4.6)

Since (
4.6
) holds for every feasible

P_{\hat{S}\mid S}
(the distribution
Q
is determined by the
conditional, not chosen by us), combining
with (
4.3
) gives

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0)\geq P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
.

Upper bound (achievability).

We exhibit a feasible conditional achieving

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
at zero distortion.
Define

P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\hat{s}_{o}\mid s_{o}):=\begin{cases}\mathbf{1}[\hat{s}_{o}=s_{o}]&\text{if }s_{o}\in A,\\
\pi_{A}(\hat{s}_{o})&\text{if }s_{o}\in J,\end{cases}

where
\pi_{A}
is supported on
A
.

Distortion check.

For
s_{o}=a\in A
:
\hat{s}_{o}=a
, giving

d_{\text{Cn}}(a,a\mid S_{O})=0
.
For
s_{o}=j\in J
:
\hat{s}_{o}
is drawn from
\pi_{A}
on
A
,
so
\hat{s}_{o}\in A\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
; since
j
is redundant,

d_{\text{Cn}}(j,\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0

(Remark
3.1
(a)).

Rate computation.

Under this conditional,
Q=\pi_{A}
and the output marginal is

P_{\hat{S}}=P_{A}\pi_{A}+P_{J}\pi_{A}=\pi_{A}
.
All redundant inputs produce the same conditional output
distribution
\pi_{A}
, so

\hat{S}_{o}\perp\!\!\!\perp S_{o}\mid T
, giving

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid T)=0
.
Hence

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})=I(T;\hat{S}_{o})=H(\pi_{A})-P_{J}\,H(\pi_{A})=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}).\qed

Remark 4.1

(Relaxed acceptable sets)
.

The lower bound
P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
in the proof of
Theorem
4.1
holds for
arbitrary acceptable-set sizes
|R_{\text{Cn}}(a)|\geq 1
,
provided the pairwise-disjoint support condition of
Assumption
3.1
holds;
the key step is the entropy splitting
identity (
4.4
).
The achievability construction of the upper bound applies
unchanged.

Remark 4.2

(Role of the reconstruction alphabet assumption)
.

Theorem
4.1
assumes

\hat{S}_{O}\supseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})\cap\mathbb{S}_{O}
, ensuring that
the decoder can output any state in the sender’s closure.
This is an idealized “full reconstruction alphabet” setting
that maximizes the deductive compression gain; it corresponds
to a receiver whose vocabulary contains at least all closure
elements expressible in the ambient universe.
When the reconstruction alphabet is restricted to

\hat{S}_{O}\subsetneq\text{Cn}(S_{O})\cap\mathbb{S}_{O}
—as in the
heterogeneous setting of
Section
V
, where

\hat{S}_{O}=S_{O}^{(j)}
may exclude some closure
elements—the zero-distortion rate can only increase:

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\hat{S}_{O})\geq R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;\text{Cn}(S_{O})\cap\mathbb{S}_{O})=P_{A}H(\pi_{A})
.
The heterogeneous achievability results of
Theorem
5.1
show that
the rate
P_{A}H(\pi_{A})
remains achievable as long as the
core coverage condition
A^{(i)}\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}
holds;
when it fails,
Corollary
5.3

provides the impossibility characterization.

Remark 4.3

(Necessity of the proof-system structure)
.

The result (
4.1
) depends on three concepts absent
from classical rate–distortion theory:
the irredundant core
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
, the deductive
closure
Cn
, and the core/redundant partition of source
symbols.
Replacing
d_{\text{Cn}}
by any distortion that assigns positive cost
to all symbol errors eliminates the zero-distortion property of
redundant states and recovers
R(0)=H(P_{O})
.

IV-B

Rate–Distortion Core Decomposition

Theorem
4.1
extends to all distortion
levels: the full semantic rate–distortion function decomposes
into a contribution from the core alone.

Theorem 4.2

(Core decomposition of
R_{\mathrm{sem}}
)
.

Under the hypotheses of
Theorem
4.1
,

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(D;\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,P_{O})\;=\;P_{A}\cdot R^{(A)}\!\Bigl(\frac{D}{P_{A}};\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,\pi_{A}\Bigr),\qquad D\geq 0,

(4.7)

where
R^{(A)}(D^{\prime};\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,\pi_{A})
is the rate–distortion
function of the core sub-source
(A,\pi_{A})
with distortion

d_{\text{Cn}}(\cdot,\cdot\mid S_{O})
restricted to core inputs.

Consequences:

(i)
At
D=0
,

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0)=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
, recovering
Theorem
4.1
.

(ii)

R_{\mathrm{sem}}
is convex, non-increasing, and
computable.

(iii)
Redundant states are invisible: the
function (
4.7
) depends on
S_{O}
only through

A
,
P_{A}
,
\pi_{A}
, and the closure-distortion structure
on
A
.

Distortion decomposition.

By Remark
3.1
(a),

d_{\text{Cn}}(j,\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0
for all
j\in J
and

\hat{s}_{o}\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
The expected distortion therefore decomposes as

\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}]=\sum_{a\in A}P_{O}(a)\,\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}(a,\hat{S}_{o}\mid S_{O})\mid S_{o}=a]=P_{A}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{A}}[\bar{d}_{A}],

where
\bar{d}_{A}(a):=\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}(a,\hat{S}_{o}\mid S_{O})\mid S_{o}=a]
.
The constraint
\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}]\leq D
is thus equivalent to

\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{A}}[\bar{d}_{A}]\leq D/P_{A}
.

Rate decomposition.

Using the auxiliary variable
T
from the proof of
Theorem
4.1
:

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})=I(T;\hat{S}_{o})+I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid T)\geq I(T;\hat{S}_{o}).

The residual
I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid T)
is minimized to zero by
choosing identical conditionals for all
j\in J
(as in the
achievability of Theorem
4.1
).
Hence the minimum of
I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})
subject to

\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{A}}[\bar{d}_{A}]\leq D/P_{A}
equals the minimum of

I(T;\hat{S}_{o})
subject to the same constraint.

The distribution of
T
concentrates probability
P_{A}
on

A
(with conditional distribution
\pi_{A}
) and
probability
P_{J}
on the singleton
\{*\}
.
The problem

\min_{P_{\hat{S}\mid T}}\{I(T;\hat{S}_{o}):\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{A}}[\bar{d}_{A}]\leq D/P_{A}\}

is a rate–distortion problem whose source is the mixture
of
\pi_{A}
(with weight
P_{A}
) and a point mass (with
weight
P_{J}
).
The point-mass component contributes zero mutual information
and zero distortion, so the minimum equals

P_{A}\cdot R^{(A)}(D/P_{A};\,d_{\text{Cn}},\,\pi_{A})

by the standard rate–distortion scaling for mixtures with a
“free” component
[
8
, Problem 10.8]
.
∎

IV-C

Semantic Source–Channel Separation and Semantic Leverage

Theorem 4.3

(Semantic source–channel separation)
.

Let
W:S_{C}\rightsquigarrow\hat{S}_{C}
with
C(W)>0
, and let

(S_{O},P_{O})
be a semantic source with core
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.

Part A
(
Single-shot / message-set regime
)
.

Consider an
(n,|S_{O}|)
semantic block code

(Definition
3.17
)
with message
set
\mathcal{M}=S_{O}
.

(A1)

Converse.

Closure-reliable communication
(
P_{e,\text{Cn}}^{(n)}\to 0
) requires blocklength

n\geq(1-o(1))\log|A|/C(W)

(Theorem
3.4
(ii))
.

(A2)

Achievability.

The two-layer code

(Theorem
3.5
(ii))
achieves

P_{e,\text{Cn}}^{(n)}\to 0
whenever

\log|A|/n<C(W)
.

(A3)

Single-shot leverage.

The minimum blocklength for closure reliability is

n^{*}_{\text{Cn}}\approx\log|A|/C(W)

(Corollary
3.3
)
.
Define the
single-shot semantic leverage factor

\Lambda_{1}(S_{O})\;:=\;\frac{\log|S_{O}|}{\log|\text{Atom}(S_{O})|}\;>\;1\quad\text{whenever }k<|S_{O}|.

(4.8)

Since identifying one of
|S_{O}|
knowledge-base states
requires
\log|S_{O}|
bits, closure reliability conveys
this identification in

n^{*}_{\text{Cn}}\approx\log|A|/C(W)
channel uses,
yielding an
effective task-identification rate

R_{\mathrm{eff}}\;:=\;\frac{\log|S_{O}|}{n^{*}_{\text{Cn}}}\;\approx\;C(W)\cdot\Lambda_{1}(S_{O}).

(4.9)

The quantity
R_{\mathrm{eff}}
is a

task-throughput index
that counts
knowledge-base index bits conveyed per channel
use under closure fidelity; it does
not

represent a Shannon mutual-information rate,
and its exceeding
C(W)
is
consistent with the data processing bound

C_{\mathrm{sem}}\leq C(W)

(Theorem
3.1
(i))
, because
the closure-based fidelity criterion treats
|J|

redundant states as free.

Part B
(
Asymptotic i.i.d. source-coding
regime
)
.

Consider a length-
m
i.i.d. source block from

(S_{O},P_{O})
mapped to
n
channel uses of
W^{\otimes n}
,
with
m\to\infty
.
The fidelity criterion is zero expected closure distortion
(
D=0
in (
3.13
)).
We work under the same hypotheses as
Theorem
4.1
:

\hat{S}_{O}\supseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})\cap\mathbb{S}_{O}
and
Assumption
3.1
.
The relevant source rate is therefore

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;d_{\text{Cn}})=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
.

(B1)

Necessary condition.

Achieving zero expected closure distortion requires

P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})\;\leq\;C(W).

(4.10)

(B2)

Sufficient condition.

If the strict inequality holds
in (
4.10
), then zero expected
closure distortion is achievable: by the classical
rate–distortion coding
theorem
[
8
, Theorem 10.2.1]
,
for every
R>R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0)=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})

there exist length-
m
block source codes achieving

\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}]=0
at rate
R
;
cascading with a capacity-achieving channel code
completes the separation argument.

(B3)

Asymptotic leverage.

Encoding
m
source symbols at distortion
D{=}0

requires at least
m\,P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
bits, and
each channel use carries at most
C(W)
bits, so

\liminf_{m\to\infty}n/m\geq P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})/C(W)
, yielding the

asymptotic semantic leverage factor

\Lambda_{\infty}(S_{O},P_{O})\;:=\;\frac{\log|S_{O}|}{P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})}\;\geq\;\Lambda_{1}(S_{O}),

(4.11)

with equality when
\pi_{A}
is uniform on
A
and

P_{A}=1
.
Like
\Lambda_{1}
, the factor
\Lambda_{\infty}

uses
task-identification normalization
: the
numerator
\log|S_{O}|
counts the index bits needed
to specify one knowledge-base state, not the source
entropy
H(P_{O})
.
The entropy-based compression gain is the distinct
ratio
P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})/H(P_{O})<1

of (
4.2
).
Under the uniform source:

\Lambda_{1}=\log|S_{O}|/\log k
and

\Lambda_{\infty}=|S_{O}|\log|S_{O}|/(k\log k)
.

Part A.

(A1) is Theorem
3.4
(ii).
(A2) is Theorem
3.5
(ii).
(A3) follows by dividing
\log|S_{O}|
by

n^{*}_{\text{Cn}}\approx\log|A|/C(W)
.

Part B.

(B1): the closure distortion
d_{\text{Cn}}\geq 0
is a bounded
single-letter distortion on finite alphabets, so the
classical source–channel separation theorem

[
29
,
8
]
applies:
achieving expected distortion
D=0
over the i.i.d. source requires source rate

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0;d_{\text{Cn}})=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})\leq C(W)
, giving (
4.10
).
(B2): by the rate–distortion coding theorem for finite
alphabets
[
8
, Theorem 10.2.1]
, for
every
\epsilon>0
there exists a block source code of
rate
P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})+\epsilon
achieving

\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}]=0
; a capacity-achieving channel code
transmits the compressed index reliably whenever

P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})+\epsilon<C(W)
.
(B3): by (B1), every achievable pair
(m,n)
satisfies

m\,P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})\leq nC(W)
, hence

\liminf_{m\to\infty}n/m\geq P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})/C(W)
.
The leverage ordering

\Lambda_{\infty}\geq\Lambda_{1}\geq 1
follows from

P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})\leq\log|A|\leq\log|S_{O}|
.

Remark 4.4

(Comparison with the synonymous-mapping
semantic leverage)
.

The leverage factors

\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{\infty}
are the closure-fidelity
counterparts of the semantic throughput gain
C_{s}\geq C
of
Niu and Zhang
[
25
]
.
The two mechanisms are complementary:

[
25
]
achieves its gain through

IV-D

Strengthened Semantic Fano Inequality

The classical Fano inequality bounds
H(X\mid\hat{X})
in terms
of
\Pr[X\neq\hat{X}]
and the full alphabet size
|X|
.
The core/redundant decomposition enables a tighter bound whose
penalty term involves
\log|A|
rather than
\log|S_{O}|
.

Theorem 4.4

(Semantic Fano inequality)
.

Let
(S_{O},P_{O})
be a full-support semantic source,

\mathfrak{C}
a semantic channel, and

\epsilon_{A}:=\Pr[\hat{S}_{o}\neq S_{o},\;S_{o}\in A]

the core error probability.
Then

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})\;\geq\;P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})-h_{b}(\epsilon_{A})-\epsilon_{A}\log(|A|-1),

(4.12)

where
h_{b}
is the binary entropy.
When
|J|=0
,
P_{A}=1
,
\pi_{A}=P_{O}
, and
(
4.12
) reduces to the classical Fano bound
with alphabet
|A|=|S_{O}|
.
When
|J|>0
, the penalty involves
\log|A|
rather than

\log|S_{O}|
; additionally, the reference level is the
semantic rate
P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
rather than the full source
entropy
H(P_{O})
, which is a tightening by

H(P_{O})-P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})>0
bits.

Define
B:=\mathbf{1}[S_{o}\in A]
.
Since
B
is a deterministic function of
S_{o}
,

H(B\mid S_{o})=0
and hence
I(S_{o};B)=H(B)=h_{b}(P_{A})
.
The chain rule for mutual information gives

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})+I(S_{o};B\mid\hat{S}_{o})\;=\;I(S_{o};B)+I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid B).

Since
B=f(S_{o})
, we have

I(S_{o};B\mid\hat{S}_{o})=H(B\mid\hat{S}_{o})-H(B\mid S_{o},\hat{S}_{o})=H(B\mid\hat{S}_{o})\geq 0
.
Hence

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})\;=\;h_{b}(P_{A})-H(B\mid\hat{S}_{o})+I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid B)\;\geq\;I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid B),

(4.13)

where the inequality uses

H(B\mid\hat{S}_{o})\leq H(B)=h_{b}(P_{A})
.
Expanding the right-hand side:

\displaystyle I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid B)

\displaystyle=P_{A}\,I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid B{=}1)+P_{J}\,I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid B{=}0)

\displaystyle\geq P_{A}\,I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid B{=}1),

(4.14)

since mutual information is non-negative.

Core term.

Conditionally on
B=1
, the source
S_{o}
takes values in
A

with distribution
\pi_{A}
, so

H(S_{o}\mid B{=}1)=H(\pi_{A})
.
Let

p_{e}^{A}:=\Pr[\hat{S}_{o}\neq S_{o}\mid S_{o}\in A]=\epsilon_{A}/P_{A}
.
By the standard Fano inequality applied to the

|A|
-valued variable
S_{o}
given
\hat{S}_{o}

conditioned on
B=1
:

H(S_{o}\mid\hat{S}_{o},\,B{=}1)\;\leq\;h_{b}(p_{e}^{A})+p_{e}^{A}\log(|A|-1),

where
|A|-1
is the standard Fano penalty for the

|A|
-valued source
S_{o}|_{B=1}
; this form is valid
regardless of the range of the reconstruction
\hat{S}_{o}
,
since the penalty is determined by the source alphabet
size
[
8
, Theorem 2.10.1]
.
Hence

\displaystyle I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o}\mid B{=}1)

\displaystyle=H(\pi_{A})-H(S_{o}\mid\hat{S}_{o},\,B{=}1)

\displaystyle\geq H(\pi_{A})-h_{b}(p_{e}^{A})-p_{e}^{A}\log(|A|-1).

Substituting into (
4.14
) and
(
4.13
):

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})\geq P_{A}\bigl[H(\pi_{A})-h_{b}(\epsilon_{A}/P_{A})-(\epsilon_{A}/P_{A})\log(|A|-1)\bigr].

This equals

P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})-P_{A}\,h_{b}(\epsilon_{A}/P_{A})-\epsilon_{A}\log(|A|-1)
.
It remains to bound the first penalty term.
Define the random variable
Z
that takes value

\epsilon_{A}/P_{A}
with probability
P_{A}
and
0
with
probability
P_{J}
.
Then
\mathbb{E}[Z]=\epsilon_{A}
and

\mathbb{E}[h_{b}(Z)]=P_{A}\,h_{b}(\epsilon_{A}/P_{A})
.
By Jensen’s inequality (concavity of
h_{b}
):

P_{A}\,h_{b}(\epsilon_{A}/P_{A})=\mathbb{E}[h_{b}(Z)]\leq h_{b}(\mathbb{E}[Z])=h_{b}(\epsilon_{A}).\qed

Remark 4.5

(Operational significance)
.

Bound (
4.12
) provides a converse for
semantic source coding: any encoder–decoder pair with low core
error
\epsilon_{A}
must transmit mutual information at least

P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})-h_{b}(\epsilon_{A})-\epsilon_{A}\log(|A|-1)
.
Compared with the classical Fano bound

I\geq H(P_{O})-h_{b}(\epsilon)-\epsilon\log(|S_{O}|-1)
,
the semantic improvement is twofold:

(a)
the reference level drops from

H(P_{O})
to the semantic rate
P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
, absorbing
all redundant-state entropy;

(b)
the Fano penalty
involves
\log|A|
instead of
\log|S_{O}|
.
Moreover, under the optimal semantic code (which maps all
redundant inputs to core elements), the total Hamming error
is
\epsilon=\epsilon_{A}+P_{J}
, substantially larger
than
\epsilon_{A}
; the classical bound applied with this
inflated
\epsilon
is much looser than the semantic
bound (
4.12
) applied with
\epsilon_{A}

alone.
Combined with Theorem
4.3
, this
characterizes the operational regime where semantic compression
strictly outperforms symbol-level compression.

Rate–Delay–Distortion Tradeoff and Semantic
Sampling Theorem

The rate–distortion results of
Sections
IV-A
–
IV-B

treat the receiver’s inference engine as unconstrained: it may
iterate
T_{\mathsf{PS}}
arbitrarily many times to
reconstruct the full closure.
In practice, communication is subject to
delay
constraints
: the receiver has a bounded computation budget

\delta\geq 0
(measured in
T_{\mathsf{PS}}
-iterations)
within which it must reconstruct the deductive closure.
This subsection shows that the interaction between derivation
depth and delay budget gives rise to a
rate–delay–distortion
surface
that smoothly interpolates between the classical
zero-distortion rate
H(P_{O})
(no inference,
\delta=0
)
and the semantic rate
P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
(full inference,

\delta=\mathsf{D_{d}}
), and yields a semantic analogue of the
Nyquist sampling theorem.

The key structural ingredient is the

derivation-depth stratification
of
Section
II-B
: the partition of
S_{O}

into strata
L_{d}:=\{s\in S_{O}:\text{Dd}(s\mid A)=d\}
for

d=0,1,\ldots,\mathsf{D_{d}}
.
When the receiver’s derivation budget is
\delta
, states at
depth greater than
\delta
from the transmitted base cannot be
reconstructed within the budget; conversely, states within depth

\delta
are “free” and need not be transmitted.
This
depth-for-rate exchange
is formalized below.

Preview of key objects and main result.

The subsection introduces three new objects:
(a) the
\delta
-constrained closure distortion

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}

(Definition
4.2
), a

\{0,1\}
-valued fidelity measure that requires the receiver to
reconstruct every stored state within
\delta
derivation steps;
(b) the
\delta
-irredundant core

A_{\delta}:=\text{Atom}_{\delta}(S_{O})

(Definition
4.3
), the subset of
S_{O}

whose elements
cannot
be rederived within
\delta
steps from
the remaining knowledge base;
(c) the
rate–delay function

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,\delta)=P_{\delta}\,H(\pi_{\delta})

(Theorem
4.5
), the minimum communication
rate when the receiver has a derivation budget of
\delta
steps.
The central finding is that
A_{\delta}
shrinks monotonically as

\delta
increases
(Proposition
4.1
): at
\delta=0
no
state is free and the rate equals the classical
H(P_{O})
; at

\delta=\mathsf{D_{d}}
all redundant states become free and the
rate drops to the semantic rate
P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
.

(Derivation-path completeness; used only
for the computable bound in

)
.

The knowledge base

contains all intermediate

This holds whenever

includes all materialized IDB facts up to

\mathsf{D_{d}}

When this assumption fails, the auxiliary bounds of
Proposition

(iv)
require

by

T^{m}_{\mathsf{PS}}(A)
; the tight rate–delay
result (
4.17
) and the rate–delay–distortion

) remain valid, as their proofs

\delta
-redundancy definition.

(Depth-stratified stored base)

For
m\in\{0,1,\ldots,\mathsf{D_{d}}\}
, define the

m
-deep stored base

(

Definition 4.2

(
\delta
-constrained closure distortion)
.

For a reference base
\Gamma=S_{O}
,

\Gamma_{-s}:=S_{O}\setminus\{s\}

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})\;:=\;\begin{cases}0&\text{if }S_{O}\subseteq T^{\delta}_{\mathsf{PS}}\bigl(\Gamma_{-s_{o}}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\}\bigr),\\[4.0pt]

(4.15)

Remark 4.6

(Properties of
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}
)
.

In operational terms:

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0
means that
after replacing the single stored state
s_{o}

by
\hat{s}_{o}
, the receiver can re-derive every element
of
S_{O}
within
\delta
immediate-consequence iterations.

The distortion
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}
takes values in

\{0,1\}
.
When
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0
and

\hat{s}_{o}\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})
—a condition satisfied by every constructive coding
scheme in Sections
III
–
IV
,
whose decoders output elements of
S_{O}

(
or subsets such as
A
,

A_{\delta}
, or
B_{\delta}
, all contained
in
\text{Cn}(S_{O})
)
,
as well as by the heterogeneous two-layer code of
Theorem
5.1

(
with
S_{O}:=S_{O}^{(i)}
as reference
base
)
,
whose decoder outputs elements of

A^{(i)}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})
—the chain

S_{O}\subseteq T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})\subseteq\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})

gives

\text{Cn}(S_{O})\subseteq\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})

by
(Cn2)
, while

\Gamma_{-s_{o}}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})

gives the reverse inclusion
by
(Cn2)
and
(Cn3)
;
hence the closures coincide and

d_{\text{Cn}}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0
.
Without the condition
\hat{s}_{o}\in\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, neither
direction of implication between
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}=0

and
d_{\text{Cn}}=0
holds in general.
The following auxiliary property is used
solely
to verify the
boundary case
(ii)
of
Theorem
4.5
and does not assert a
universal equivalence between
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}
and
d_{\text{Cn}}
:
for
Cn
-redundant
states
s_{o}\in J
and

\hat{s}_{o}\in S_{O}
, if
\delta\geq\mathsf{D_{d}}
, then

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0
, since

A\subseteq\Gamma_{-s_{o}}
and monotonicity give

T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})\supseteq T^{\mathsf{D_{d}}}(A)\supseteq S_{O}
.
For general
(
non-redundant
)
states and

\delta<\mathsf{D_{d}}
, the two distortion measures

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}
and
d_{\text{Cn}}
need not coincide.
The core mechanism of the rate–delay theory is the

\delta
-redundancy–based free substitution of
Lemma
4.1
, which holds for
arbitrary
\delta\geq 0
without reference to
d_{\text{Cn}}
.
Operationally, the condition

S_{O}\subseteq T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s_{o}}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})

requires every
explicitly stored
state in
S_{O}

to be re-derivable within
\delta

T_{\mathsf{PS}}
-iterations
from the modified base—a stronger requirement than
merely preserving the closure
\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, reflecting the
constraint that the receiver must reconstruct each element of
the sender’s knowledge base within
\delta

T_{\mathsf{PS}}
-iterations.

Definition 4.3

(
\delta
-redundancy and
\delta
-irredundant core)
.

A state

is
\delta
-redundant
if

s\in T^{\delta}_{\mathsf{PS}}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})
.
The
\delta
-irredundant core
is

\text{Atom}_{\delta}(S_{O})\;:=\;\bigl\{s\in S_{O}:s\notin T^{\delta}_{\mathsf{PS}}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})\bigr\}.

Lemma 4.1

(
\delta
-redundant free substitution)
.

If
s\in S_{O}
is
\delta
-redundant

(Definition
4.3
)
, i.e.,

s\in T^{\delta}_{\mathsf{PS}}(S_{O}\setminus\{s\})
, then for
every

\hat{s}\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
:

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s,\hat{s}\mid S_{O})\;=\;0.

Write
\Gamma_{-s}:=S_{O}\setminus\{s\}
.
We must show

S_{O}\subseteq T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\})
.
Since

\Gamma_{-s}\subseteq\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\}
,
monotonicity (IC1) gives

T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s})\subseteq T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\})
.
The
\delta
-redundancy hypothesis

s\in T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s})
then yields

s\in T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\})
.
Furthermore,

\Gamma_{-s}\subseteq T^{0}(\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\})\subseteq T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\})
.
Hence

S_{O}=\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{s\}\subseteq T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\})
.
∎

Remark 4.7

(Output insensitivity for
\delta
-redundant states)
.

Lemma
4.1
gives

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s,\hat{s}\mid S_{O})=0
for

when

is
\delta
-redundant.
This universality is by design:

tests whether the receiver can
reconstruct all of

within

\delta

-iterations from

\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\}
, not whether
\hat{s}

itself is meaningful.
Since
\delta
-redundancy gives

S_{O}\subseteq T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s})
,
monotonicity yields

S_{O}\subseteq T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-s}\cup\{\hat{s}\})

regardless of
\hat{s}
.
Operationally, every constructive coding scheme in
Sections
III
–
IV

outputs elements of
S_{O}

(
or subsets such as
A
,
A_{\delta}
,

B_{\delta}
, all contained in
\text{Cn}(S_{O})
)
;
the universality over
\mathbb{S}_{O}
is needed only in the

of Theorem
4.5
,
where it ensures that
\delta
-redundant states contribute
zero mutual information under
any
feasible conditional.

Assumption 4.2

(Deductive independence of

\delta
-irredundant core)
.

For each
\delta\in\{0,1,\ldots,\mathsf{D_{d}}\}
and

for

Under the condition

,

3.1
implies
the present assumption for every
\delta

(Remark

)
.
When

Remark 4.8

(Proof that core-disjointness implies

\delta
-core-disjointness)
.

Suppose
\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
and let

\hat{s}_{o}\in R_{\delta}(a)
, so

S_{O}\subseteq T^{\delta}(\Gamma_{-a}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})\subseteq\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-a}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})
.
By monotonicity
(Cn2)
,

\text{Cn}(S_{O})\subseteq\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-a}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})
.
For the reverse: since

\hat{s}_{o}\in\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
and

\Gamma_{-a}\subseteq S_{O}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
,
we have

\Gamma_{-a}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})
,
giving

\text{Cn}(\Gamma_{-a}\cup\{\hat{s}_{o}\})\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O})

(Cn2)
and
(Cn3)
.
Hence

,

and

\hat{s}_{o}\in R_{\text{Cn}}(a)
.
Thus
R_{\delta}(a)\subseteq R_{\text{Cn}}(a)
, and the
pairwise disjointness of
Assumption
3.1

transfers to the
\delta
-constrained sets.
At
\delta=\mathsf{D_{d}}
and under the same condition

,

(Proposition
4.1
(ii))
and
the present assumption coincides with
Assumption
3.1
.

.

,

We now state the main result of this subsection: the
zero-distortion rate under a delay constraint.

Theorem 4.5

(Tight zero-distortion rate–delay function)
.

Let
(S_{O},P_{O})
be a semantic source with core

A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and maximum derivation depth

\mathsf{D_{d}}
.
For
\delta\in\{0,1,\ldots,\mathsf{D_{d}}\}
, write

A_{\delta}:=\text{Atom}_{\delta}(S_{O})
,

P_{\delta}:=P_{O}(A_{\delta})
, and

\pi_{\delta}(s):=P_{O}(s)/P_{\delta}
for
s\in A_{\delta}
.
Under the
\{0,1\}
-valued
\delta
-constrained closure
distortion
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}

(Definition
4.2
)
,
with reconstruction alphabet

\hat{S}_{O}
satisfying

A_{\delta}\subseteq\hat{S}_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
,
and under
Assumption
4.2

(
Assumption
4.1

enters only in part
(iv);
the tight
rate (
4.17
) and
parts
(i)–(iii)
are independent of
it
)
:

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,\delta;\,d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta},\,P_{O})\;=\;P_{\delta}\,H(\pi_{\delta}).

(4.17)

Boundary values and monotonicity:

(i)

No inference
(
\delta=0
):

A_{0}=S_{O}
,
P_{0}=1
,
\pi_{0}=P_{O}
, so

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,0)=H(P_{O})
, recovering the
classical zero-distortion rate.

(ii)

Full inference
(
\delta=\mathsf{D_{d}}
):

A_{\mathsf{D_{d}}}=A
,
P_{\mathsf{D_{d}}}=P_{A}
,

\pi_{\mathsf{D_{d}}}=\pi_{A}
, so

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,\mathsf{D_{d}})=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
,
recovering Theorem
4.1
.

(iii)

Monotonicity:

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,\delta)
is non-increasing in

\delta
:

H(P_{O})=R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,0)\geq R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,1)\geq\cdots\geq R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,\mathsf{D_{d}})=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}).

(4.18)

(iv)

Depth-stratified upper bound:

If additionally

\hat{S}_{O}\supseteq B_{\delta}:=B^{(\leq\mathsf{D_{d}}-\delta)}
, then

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,\delta)\leq P_{B_{\delta}}\,H(\pi_{B_{\delta}})
,
where

P_{B_{\delta}}:=P_{O}(B_{\delta})
and

\pi_{B_{\delta}}
is the conditional distribution
on
B_{\delta}
.
This bound is computable directly from the
derivation-depth stratification without extracting

\text{Atom}_{\delta}(S_{O})
.

(
Since

A_{\delta}\subseteq B_{\delta}

by Proposition
4.1
(iv)
,
the condition

\hat{S}_{O}\supseteq B_{\delta}
strengthens the
standing hypothesis

\hat{S}_{O}\supseteq A_{\delta}
.
)

The proof parallels Theorem
4.1

with the substitutions
A\to A_{\delta}
,

\pi_{A}\to\pi_{\delta}
,
P_{A}\to P_{\delta}
,
and
Cn
-redundancy replaced by
\delta
-redundancy.
Write
P_{J_{\delta}}:=1-P_{\delta}
for the probability of
the
\delta
-redundant set
S_{O}\setminus A_{\delta}
.

Lower bound.

Let
P_{\hat{S}\mid S}
be any conditional distribution
achieving
\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(S_{o},\hat{S}_{o}\mid S_{O})]=0
.
Define the auxiliary random variable

T_{\delta}:S_{O}\to A_{\delta}\cup\{*\}
by

T_{\delta}(s):=s
if
s\in A_{\delta}
and

T_{\delta}(s):=*
if
s\in S_{O}\setminus A_{\delta}
.
Since
T_{\delta}
is a deterministic function of
S_{o}
,
data processing gives

I(S_{o};\hat{S}_{o})\;\geq\;I(T_{\delta};\hat{S}_{o}).

(4.19)

Under Assumption
4.2
, the

\delta
-constrained zero-distortion sets

R_{\delta}(a):=\{\hat{s}_{o}\in\hat{S}_{O}:d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(a,\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0\}

for distinct
a\in A_{\delta}
are pairwise disjoint.
Since
d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}\in\{0,1\}
, the constraint

\mathbb{E}[d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(S_{o},\hat{S}_{o}\mid S_{O})]=0
is
equivalent to

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(s_{o},\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0
holding

P_{O}(s_{o})\,P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\hat{s}_{o}\mid s_{o})
-almost
surely; in particular,

\supp(P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a))\subseteq R_{\delta}(a)
for each
a\in A_{\delta}
.
For
\delta
-redundant states

j\in S_{O}\setminus A_{\delta}
,
Lemma
4.1
gives

d_{\text{Cn}}^{\delta}(j,\hat{s}_{o}\mid S_{O})=0
for every

\hat{s}_{o}\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
, so

P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid j)
is unconstrained.

Let
Q_{j}:=P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid j)
for each

j\in S_{O}\setminus A_{\delta}
, and define the average

\delta
-redundant output distribution

Q:=\sum_{j\in S_{O}\setminus A_{\delta}}[P_{O}(j)/P_{J_{\delta}}]\,Q_{j}
.
Write

\bar{\pi}_{\delta}:=\sum_{a\in A_{\delta}}\pi_{\delta}(a)\,P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a)

for the average core output distribution.
Since the supports
R_{\delta}(a)
are pairwise disjoint,
the standard disjoint-support entropy splitting
identity
[
8
, Theorem 2.7.3]
gives

H(\bar{\pi}_{\delta})\;=\;H(\pi_{\delta})+\sum_{a\in A_{\delta}}\pi_{\delta}(a)\,H\bigl(P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a)\bigr).

(4.20)

The conditional entropy of
\hat{S}_{o}
given
T_{\delta}
is

Therefore, using (

\displaystyle I(T_{\delta};\hat{S}_{o})

\displaystyle=H(P_{\hat{S}})-H(\hat{S}_{o}\mid T_{\delta})

\displaystyle\geq P_{\delta}\,H(\bar{\pi}_{\delta})+P_{J_{\delta}}\,H(Q)-P_{\delta}\!\sum_{a}\pi_{\delta}(a)\,H(P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a))-P_{J_{\delta}}\,H(Q)

\displaystyle=P_{\delta}\bigl[H(\bar{\pi}_{\delta})-\!\sum_{a}\pi_{\delta}(a)\,H(P_{\hat{S}\mid S}(\cdot\mid a))\bigr]

(4.21)

Combining with (

Upper bound

Define

Remark 4.10

(Communication–computation exchange)

V
Application: Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Semantic Communication

The theoretical framework developed in Sections
II

and
III
is fully general: the semantic state
space
S_{O}
, the reconstructed space
\hat{S}_{O}
, and the
enabling structures that constrain encoding and decoding are
left as abstract parameters.
This section instantiates the framework in a concrete and
practically motivated setting—
heterogeneous multi-agent
semantic communication
—and derives new results that illustrate the framework’s
applicability in a setting where classical channel coding
theory does not capture the deductive structure of the
communicated content.

The distinguishing feature of the heterogeneous setting is that
the sender and receiver maintain
different
knowledge
bases: the sender’s semantic space is
S_{O}
while the
receiver’s reconstructed space
\hat{S}_{O}
may differ
from
S_{O}
both in vocabulary (the set of expressible states)
and in inferential structure (the irredundant core and
derivation-depth stratification).
In the terminology of Section
II-C
, the
end-to-end noise pair
(S_{O}^{-},S_{O}^{+})
is generically

non-trivial
:
S_{O}^{-}\neq\varnothing
captures
sender concepts absent from the receiver’s vocabulary
(
vocabulary loss
), and
S_{O}^{+}\neq\varnothing

captures receiver concepts absent from the sender’s intent
(
vocabulary surplus
).
Classical Shannon theory, which treats sender and receiver
alphabets as abstract label sets, cannot distinguish vocabulary
loss from vocabulary surplus, nor can it exploit shared
deductive structure to reduce communication cost.
The semantic channel invariants of Section
III-C

are precisely the tools needed to make these distinctions precise
and quantitative.

The section is organized as follows.
Section
V-A
describes the multi-agent
communication scenario and identifies the key design questions.
Section
V-B
formalizes the scenario
within the information model framework and states the standing
assumptions specific to this application.
Section
V-C
instantiates the semantic channel
machinery and derives closed-form relationships between
knowledge-base overlap structure and semantic channel invariants.
Section
V-D
presents the main analytical
results: conditions for closure-reliable heterogeneous
communication, a heterogeneous deductive compression theorem,
and a broadcast extension to one-sender–multi-receiver
scenarios.
Section
V-E
verifies all results on an
explicit Datalog knowledge-base instance with full numerical
computation of every invariant.

V-A

Problem Description: Heterogeneous Agent Communication

Consider a network of
K+1
autonomous agents—indexed by

i\in\{0,1,\ldots,K\}
—that must coordinate by exchanging semantic
states over noisy physical links.
Each agent
i
maintains a finite knowledge base

S_{O}^{(i)}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
, where
\mathbb{S}_{O}
is the
common ambient semantic universe introduced in
Section
II-B
.
All agents share the same proof system

(\mathsf{PS},\,T_{\mathsf{PS}},\,\text{Cn})
and the same semantic
sublanguage
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
; they differ, however, in
the
sets of semantic states they store and operate on
.
Agent
i
can derive consequences within
\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})
and
possesses the irredundant core
A^{(i)}:=\text{Atom}(S_{O}^{(i)})

together with the associated derivation-depth stratification
(Definitions
2.1
and
2.4
).

This knowledge-base heterogeneity is the defining feature of the
scenario and the source of all phenomena that distinguish it from
classical Shannon-theoretic
communication
[
29
,
8
]
.
Recent work on semantic
communication
[
20
,
30
,
4
]

has highlighted the need for frameworks that go beyond
symbol-level fidelity, but a rigorous logical-information-theoretic
treatment of heterogeneous knowledge bases has been lacking.
When agent
i
(the
sender
) transmits a semantic
state
s_{o}\in S_{O}^{(i)}
to agent
j
(the
receiver
),
the receiver reconstructs a state

\hat{s}_{o}\in S_{O}^{(j)}
—not necessarily in

S_{O}^{(i)}
—because agent
j
can only produce outputs
expressible in its own vocabulary.
Unless
S_{O}^{(i)}=S_{O}^{(j)}
, the end-to-end noise pair

(S_{O}^{-},S_{O}^{+})

(Definition
3.4
) is generically nontrivial:
states in
S_{O}^{(i)}\setminus S_{O}^{(j)}
have no direct
counterpart in the receiver’s vocabulary (
vocabulary loss
),
while states in
S_{O}^{(j)}\setminus S_{O}^{(i)}
can appear in the
receiver’s output without having been intended by the sender
(
vocabulary surplus
).
Classical channel coding theory, which treats the source and
reconstruction alphabets as unstructured label sets, is blind to
this distinction: it can detect that a symbol error has occurred, but
cannot determine whether the error represents a genuine loss of
semantic content or a harmless reformulation within the receiver’s
richer (or merely different) vocabulary.

Communication Sub-Scenarios

Three sub-scenarios of increasing structural complexity arise
naturally in the multi-agent setting; they are listed below in
decreasing order of analytical depth in this paper.

Definition 5.1

(Pairwise unicast scenario)
.

Fix a sender–receiver pair
(i,j)
with
i\neq j
.
Agent
i
wishes to communicate its full knowledge base

S_{O}^{(i)}
to agent
j
over a noisy carrier channel

W_{ij}:S_{C}\rightsquigarrow\hat{S}_{C}

(cf. Definition
3.3
(ii))
.
Agent
j
reconstructs a state in its own vocabulary

\hat{S}_{O}:=S_{O}^{(j)}
, using a decoding kernel

D\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dec}}^{(j)})
.
The end-to-end semantic channel is

\mathfrak{C}^{ij}\;=\;\bigl(\,\mathcal{I}^{(i)},\;\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{ch}}^{ij},\;\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dec}}^{(j)},\;\kappa_{\text{enc}},\;W_{ij},\;D\,\bigr),

with semantic source space
S_{O}^{(i)}
, reconstructed
space
S_{O}^{(j)}
, and noise pair

S_{O}^{-}=S_{O}^{(i)}\setminus S_{O}^{(j)},\qquad S_{O}^{+}=S_{O}^{(j)}\setminus S_{O}^{(i)}.

(5.1)

The constituent information models

\mathcal{I}^{(i)}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{ch}}^{ij}
, and

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{dec}}^{(j)}
are formalized in
Section
V-B
.

The pairwise unicast scenario is the primary focus of
Sections
V-B
–
V-D
.
All new theorems are stated and proved for this case first;
generalizations to the broadcast setting are given as corollaries.

Definition 5.2

(Broadcast scenario)
.

A designated sender (agent
0
) communicates its knowledge base

S_{O}^{(0)}
simultaneously to
K
receivers
(agents
1,\ldots,K
) over a common carrier channel
W
.
Each receiver
j
maintains a distinct vocabulary

\hat{S}_{O}^{(j)}:=S_{O}^{(j)}
and observes a
(possibly receiver-specific) noise pair

(S_{O}^{-,j},\;S_{O}^{+,j})\;=\;\bigl(S_{O}^{(0)}\setminus S_{O}^{(j)},\;S_{O}^{(j)}\setminus S_{O}^{(0)}\bigr).

All
K
receivers observe the
same
channel
output
\hat{S}_{C}
; the receiver-specific noise pairs arise
solely from vocabulary mismatch, not from different physical
channel realizations.
The broadcast semantic channel is a family

\{\mathfrak{C}^{0j}\}_{j=1}^{K}
of pairwise channels sharing the
same sender, the same encoding kernel, and the same carrier channel,
but differing in decoding model and noise pair.

The broadcast scenario reveals a phenomenon absent from classical broadcast channel
theory
[
8
]
: even over a
noiseless
carrier
(
W=\mathrm{id}_{S_{C}}
), the achievable fidelity at each receiver is
constrained by its vocabulary overlap with the sender—a purely
semantic bottleneck (see Proposition
5.9

in Section
V-D
).

Remark 5.1

(Relay scenario (future work))
.

A third sub-scenario arises when an intermediate agent
k
acts as
a relay: agent
i
transmits to agent
k
, which performs
inference within
\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(k)})
and then re-encodes and forwards
the result to agent
j
.
This is naturally modeled as a composition of two pairwise channels,

\mathfrak{C}^{ij}_{\mathrm{relay}}=\mathfrak{C}^{kj}\circ\mathfrak{C}^{ik}
,
using the information-model composition machinery of
Definition
A.4
and
Remark

.
The relay setting raises the question of whether intermediate inference can

change
the effective capacity of the end-to-end
link—a possibility that has no direct classical analogue,
since in classical relay channel
theory
[
8
]
the relay cannot exploit
logical structure to reduce the message set.
A complete treatment requires multi-letter extensions and is deferred
to future work; the pairwise and broadcast results of this section
provide the necessary building blocks.

Key Questions

The heterogeneous multi-agent setting gives rise to four design
and analysis questions that the classical framework cannot address.

Q1 (Closure reliability from vocabulary overlap).

Under what conditions on the overlap between
S_{O}^{(i)}
and

S_{O}^{(j)}
can agent
j
reconstruct the
deductive closure

of agent
i
’s knowledge base, i.e., achieve

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S_{O}^{(i)},S_{O}^{(j)})=1
?
Proposition
2.2
provides abstract conditions;
the task is to translate them into explicit predicates on the
knowledge-base pair.

Q2 (Heterogeneous deductive compression).

How many channel uses are needed to communicate

S_{O}^{(i)}
to agent
j
under closure reliability, and does the
deductive compression ratio

\log|\text{Atom}(S_{O})|/\log|S_{O}|
of the homogeneous setting
(Corollary
3.3
) persist under
vocabulary heterogeneity?

Q3 (Invariant diagnosis).

How do the six families of semantic channel invariants
(Theorem
3.3
) depend on the
knowledge-base overlap, and which invariants can be evaluated

a priori
from knowledge-base metadata alone?

Q4 (Broadcast bottleneck).

In the broadcast scenario, which receiver determines the minimum
blocklength?
Does a purely
semantic
axis of receiver weakness, distinct
from physical channel degradation, arise?

Preview of Main Results

The answers to Q1–Q4 are developed in full in
Sections
V-C
–
V-D
.
In brief:
for Q1, closure fidelity

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S_{O}^{(i)},S_{O}^{(j)})=1
holds if and only if
every sender core element is derivable from the receiver’s knowledge
base and every receiver surplus state is derivable from the sender
(Proposition
5.3
);
the operational two-layer code requires the stronger literal
containment
A^{(i)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(j)}

(Theorem
5.1
).
For Q2, the deductive compression ratio is
invariant
under
vocabulary heterogeneity: the minimum blocklength remains

n^{*}\approx\log|\text{Atom}(S_{O}^{(i)})|/C(W_{ij})
, identical to the
homogeneous case
(Theorem
5.2
);
when core coverage fails, no code of any blocklength achieves

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}=1

(Corollary
5.3
).
For Q3, each invariant family is expressed as a function of the
overlap decomposition
(Propositions
5.2
–
5.6
);
set-level invariants depend on the knowledge-base pair alone, while
probabilistic indices are additionally constrained by the channel
kernel.
For Q4, the broadcast blocklength depends only on the sender’s core
and is independent of the number of receivers, provided every
receiver covers the sender’s core
(Theorem
5.3
);
a receiver violating this condition becomes a
semantic
bottleneck
whose fidelity limitation persists even over a noiseless
carrier
(Proposition
5.9
).

Remark 5.2

(Classical recovery as a special case)
.

When all agents share the same knowledge base
(
S_{O}^{(i)}=S_{O}^{(j)}
for all
i,j
), the noise pair is trivial,
the two-condition criterion is vacuously satisfied, and all results of
this section reduce to the homogeneous theory of
Section
III-D
(and, in the irredundant case, to
classical Shannon theory via
Corollary
3.2
).
The heterogeneous analysis thus strictly generalizes rather than
replaces the earlier results.

Formal Model and Standing Assumptions

Definition 5.4

(Pairwise overlap decomposition)
.

For a fixed sender–receiver pair
(i,j)
with
i\neq j

.

Three-way partition of

\displaystyle S_{\cap}^{ij}

\displaystyle\;:=\;S_{O}^{(i)}\cap S_{O}^{(j)}

\displaystyle\text{(common states)},

(5.2)

\displaystyle S_{-}^{ij}

\displaystyle\;:=\;S_{O}^{(i)}\setminus S_{O}^{(j)}

\displaystyle\text{(lost states)},

(5.3)

\displaystyle S_{+}^{ij}

\displaystyle\;:=\;S_{O}^{(j)}\setminus S_{O}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\text{(surplus states)}.

(5.4)

\displaystyle A_{\cap}^{ij}

\displaystyle\;:=\;A^{(i)}\cap S_{O}^{(j)}

\displaystyle\text{(preserved core)},

(5.5)

\displaystyle A_{-}^{ij}

\displaystyle\;:=\;A^{(i)}\setminus S_{O}^{(j)}

\displaystyle\text{(lost core)}.

(5.6)

Surplus stratification:

\displaystyle S_{+,d}^{ij}

\displaystyle\;:=\;S_{+}^{ij}\cap\text{Cn}\bigl(S_{O}^{(i)}\bigr)

\displaystyle\text{(derivable surplus)},

(5.7)

\displaystyle S_{+,n}^{ij}

(5.8)

Proposition 5.1

(Overlap partition properties)
.

The overlap decomposition of
Definition
5.4
satisfies:

(i)

Three-way disjoint union:

S_{O}^{(i)}\cup S_{O}^{(j)}=S_{-}^{ij}\;\dot{\cup}\;S_{\cap}^{ij}\;\dot{\cup}\;S_{+}^{ij}
.

(ii)

Sender decomposition:

S_{O}^{(i)}=S_{\cap}^{ij}\;\dot{\cup}\;S_{-}^{ij}
.

(iii)

Receiver decomposition:

S_{O}^{(j)}=S_{\cap}^{ij}\;\dot{\cup}\;S_{+}^{ij}
.

(iv)

Core partition:

A^{(i)}=A_{\cap}^{ij}\;\dot{\cup}\;A_{-}^{ij}
.

(v)

Surplus partition:

S_{+}^{ij}=S_{+,d}^{ij}\;\dot{\cup}\;S_{+,n}^{ij}
.

(vi)

Noise-pair consistency:

S_{-}^{ij}=S_{O}^{-}
and
S_{+}^{ij}=S_{O}^{+}
,
where
(S_{O}^{-},S_{O}^{+})
is the end-to-end noise
pair of
Definition
5.1
.

(vii)

Core loss refines state loss:

A_{-}^{ij}\subseteq S_{-}^{ij}
.

(viii)

Computability:

All seven sets and their cardinalities are computable
from the finite knowledge bases

S_{O}^{(i)},S_{O}^{(j)}
under
Axiom
2.1
.

Parts (i)–(iii) are standard set partition
identities:
S_{\cap}^{ij}
,
S_{-}^{ij}
,
S_{+}^{ij}

are pairwise disjoint by construction, and their union equals

S_{O}^{(i)}\cup S_{O}^{(j)}
; restricting to
S_{O}^{(i)}

yields (ii), and to
S_{O}^{(j)}
yields (iii).

Part (iv): since
A^{(i)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(i)}
,

A^{(i)}=(A^{(i)}\cap S_{O}^{(j)})\;\dot{\cup}\;(A^{(i)}\setminus S_{O}^{(j)})=A_{\cap}^{ij}\;\dot{\cup}\;A_{-}^{ij}
.

Part (v): immediate from the definition of

S_{+,d}^{ij}
and
S_{+,n}^{ij}
as complementary
subsets of
S_{+}^{ij}
.

Part (vi): comparing
(
5.3
)–(
5.4
) with
(
5.1
) gives the identification
directly.

Part (vii):
A_{-}^{ij}=A^{(i)}\setminus S_{O}^{(j)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(i)}\setminus S_{O}^{(j)}=S_{-}^{ij}
, since
A^{(i)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(i)}
.

Part (viii): since
S_{O}^{(i)}
and
S_{O}^{(j)}

are finite and effectively listable
(Assumption
2.2
), membership is
decidable by exhaustive comparison.
The sets
S_{\cap}^{ij}
,
S_{-}^{ij}
,
S_{+}^{ij}
,

A_{\cap}^{ij}
,
A_{-}^{ij}
are then computable by
enumeration.
The surplus stratification requires testing

s\in\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})
for each
s\in S_{+}^{ij}
, which
is decidable by iterating
T_{\mathsf{PS}}
from

S_{O}^{(i)}
until stabilization
(Axiom
2.1
(IC2)–(IC4)).
∎

Remark 5.3

(Core preservation is tested against

S_{O}^{(j)}
, not against
A^{(j)}
)
.

The preserved core

A_{\cap}^{ij}=A^{(i)}\cap S_{O}^{(j)}
tests whether each
sender core element is
present in the receiver’s
vocabulary
—that is, an element of
S_{O}^{(j)}
—not
whether it belongs to the receiver’s irredundant core

A^{(j)}
.
A sender core element
a\in A^{(i)}
may appear in

S_{O}^{(j)}
as a
redundant
stored shortcut
(i.e.,
a\in J^{(j)}
); it is still counted as preserved,
because the receiver can produce it as a decoding output
regardless of its redundancy status in
S_{O}^{(j)}
.

Remark 5.4

(Key scalar summaries of the overlap)
.

Although the overlap decomposition produces seven subsets, the

set-level
conditions governing closure fidelity

(Proposition
5.3
)
reduce to two
binary tests:

|A_{-}^{ij}|=0
(no core loss) and

|S_{+,n}^{ij}|=0
(no non-derivable surplus).
The
coding-theoretic
results of
Section
V-D
(blocklength, compression
ratio) depend additionally on
|A^{(i)}|
and
C(W_{ij})
,
while the
probabilistic
indices
(
\Phi_{\text{Atom}}
,
\Psi_{+}
,
\mathsf{F}
,

\mathsf{E}
) depend further on the channel kernel

\kappa_{\mathrm{sem}}^{ij}
.
The remaining overlap cardinalities are related by simple
accounting:

|A_{\cap}^{ij}|=|A^{(i)}|-|A_{-}^{ij}|
,

|S_{+}^{ij}|=|S_{+,d}^{ij}|+|S_{+,n}^{ij}|
;
the quantities
|S_{-}^{ij}|
,
|S_{\cap}^{ij}|
, and

|S_{O}^{(j)}|
are mutually determined once any one of them
is known, via

|S_{\cap}^{ij}|=|S_{O}^{(i)}|-|S_{-}^{ij}|
and

|S_{O}^{(j)}|=|S_{\cap}^{ij}|+|S_{+}^{ij}|
.

Proposition 5.2

(Set-level invariants from overlap)
.

For the heterogeneous semantic channel
\mathfrak{C}^{ij}

with noise pair

(S_{O}^{-},S_{O}^{+})=(S_{-}^{ij},S_{+}^{ij})

(Proposition
5.1
(vi))
:

(i)

Preserved region:

\tilde{S}_{O}^{\cap}=S_{O}^{(i)}\cap S_{O}^{(j)}=S_{\cap}^{ij}
.

(ii)

Core preservation ratio:

\rho_{\text{Atom}}\bigl(S_{O}^{(i)},S_{O}^{(j)}\bigr)\;=\;\frac{|A_{\cap}^{ij}|}{|A^{(i)}|}\;=\;1-\frac{|A_{-}^{ij}|}{|A^{(i)}|}.

(5.10)

In particular,
\rho_{\text{Atom}}=1
if and only if

A_{-}^{ij}=\varnothing
.

(iii)

Spurious derivability:

S_{O}^{+}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})
if and only if

S_{+,n}^{ij}=\varnothing
.

(i):
S_{O}^{-}=S_{-}^{ij}

(Proposition

(vi)), so

\tilde{S}_{O}^{\cap}=S_{O}^{(i)}\setminus S_{O}^{-}=S_{O}^{(i)}\setminus(S_{O}^{(i)}\setminus S_{O}^{(j)})=S_{O}^{(i)}\cap S_{O}^{(j)}=S_{\cap}^{ij}
.

(ii): By Definition

,

\rho_{\text{Atom}}=|A^{(i)}\cap S_{O}^{(j)}|/|A^{(i)}|=|A_{\cap}^{ij}|/|A^{(i)}|
.
By Proposition

|A_{\cap}^{ij}|=|A^{(i)}|-|A_{-}^{ij}|
.

(iii):
S_{O}^{+}=S_{+}^{ij}
and

S_{+}^{ij}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})

iff

S_{+}^{ij}\setminus\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})=\varnothing

iff
S_{+,n}^{ij}=\varnothing
.
∎

Proposition 5.3

(Closure fidelity: necessary and sufficient
conditions)
.

For the sender–receiver pair
(i,j)
,

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}\bigl(S_{O}^{(i)},\,S_{O}^{(j)}\bigr)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{Cn}\bigl(S_{O}^{(i)}\bigr)=\text{Cn}\bigl(S_{O}^{(j)}\bigr),

and this holds if and only if both of the following conditions
are satisfied:

(F1)

Sender core derivable from receiver:

A^{(i)}\subseteq\text{Cn}\bigl(S_{O}^{(j)}\bigr)
.

(F2)

No non-derivable surplus:

S_{+,n}^{ij}=\varnothing

(
equivalently,

S_{+}^{ij}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})
)
.

The first equivalence is the definition of

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}

(Definition
2.9
):

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}=1
iff the Jaccard index of the two
closures equals
1
, i.e., the closures coincide.
It remains to show

\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
iff
(F1)+(F2).

Sufficiency.

From (F1):

A^{(i)}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
.
By monotonicity (Cn2),

\text{Cn}(A^{(i)})\subseteq\text{Cn}(\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)}))=\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})

(idempotence (Cn3)).
Since

\text{Cn}(A^{(i)})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})

(Proposition
2.1
(i)),

\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
.

From (F2):

S_{+}^{ij}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})
.
Since

S_{\cap}^{ij}\subseteq S_{O}^{(i)}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})

(reflexivity (Cn1)),

S_{O}^{(j)}=S_{\cap}^{ij}\cup S_{+}^{ij}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})
.
By monotonicity and idempotence,

\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})
.

Combining the two inclusions yields

\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
.

Necessity.

Suppose
\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
.

For (F1):

A^{(i)}\subseteq\text{Cn}(A^{(i)})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
.

For (F2):

S_{+}^{ij}\subseteq S_{O}^{(j)}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})
,
so
S_{+,n}^{ij}=S_{+}^{ij}\setminus\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})=\varnothing
.
∎

Remark 5.9

(Strong vs. weak core coverage)
.

(F1)
requires that each sender core element
be
derivable from
the receiver’s knowledge base; it
does
not
require the element to be literally present
in

.
A strictly stronger condition is

(F1
′
)

A^{(i)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(j)}
.

Condition
(F1
′
)
implies
(F1)
(since

S_{O}^{(j)}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
) but not conversely:
a core element
a\in A_{-}^{ij}
may satisfy

a\in\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
even though
a\notin S_{O}^{(j)}
.

For
set-level
closure fidelity, the weak
condition
(F1)
is both necessary and sufficient

(Proposition

)
.
For

(F1
′
)
so that

.
When only
(F1)
holds with

, a more sophisticated decoding
strategy is needed; this is addressed in
Section
V-D
.

Proposition 5.4

(Noise-pair probabilistic indices from

Let

(5.11)

(ii)

(5.12)

(iii)

If

(Weak core coverage and alternative decoding)
.

Theorem
5.1
uses the strong core
coverage condition

(H1)
:
A^{(i)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(j)}
.
As noted in Remark
5.9
, set-level
closure fidelity
\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}=1
requires only the
weaker condition
(F1)
:

A^{(i)}\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
.
When
(F1)
holds but
(H1)
fails
(i.e., some core element
a\in A_{-}^{ij}
is derivable from

S_{O}^{(j)}
but not literally present), the two-layer code
cannot directly output
a
.
A modified decoder could instead output a

proxy element

\hat{a}\in S_{O}^{(j)}
satisfying

d_{\text{Cn}}(a,\hat{a}\mid S_{O}^{(i)})=0
—i.e., a state in
the receiver’s vocabulary whose substitution for
a
preserves
the sender’s deductive closure.
Such a proxy exists whenever

a\in\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})
, but identifying it requires
knowledge of the sender’s closure structure at the decoder,
making the code design more involved.
A complete treatment of proxy-based decoding is deferred to
future work; the results of this section focus on the
operationally simpler setting where
(H1)
holds.
The proxy-based decoding strategy shares conceptual
affinity with the inverse contextual reasoning of
Seo et al.
[
28
]
, who address the problem
of inferring a sender’s communication context from noisy
observations using Bayesian methods.

Theorem 5.2

(Heterogeneous deductive compression)
.

Under the hypotheses of
Theorem
5.1
, the
minimum blocklength for closure-reliable communication
of the full knowledge base
S_{O}^{(i)}
to agent
j

satisfies, for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
:

(i)

Closure blocklength:

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\epsilon)\log|A^{(i)}|-1}{C(W_{ij})}\;\leq\;

\displaystyle n^{*}\bigl(S_{O}^{(i)},W_{ij},P_{e,\text{Cn}},\epsilon\bigr)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;

\displaystyle\left\lceil\frac{\log|A^{(i)}|}{C(W_{ij})-\delta(\epsilon)}\right\rceil,

(5.20)

where
\delta(\epsilon)\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

(ii)

Hamming baseline:

Under the additional hypothesis

S_{O}^{(i)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(j)}

(
i.e.,
S_{-}^{ij}=\varnothing
,
which strengthens
(H1)
to full vocabulary
containment
)
,

n^{*}\bigl(S_{O}^{(i)},W_{ij},P_{e},\epsilon\bigr)\;\geq\;\frac{(1-\epsilon)\log|S_{O}^{(i)}|-1}{C(W_{ij})}.

(5.21)

(iii)

Deductive compression ratio:

When both bounds apply,

\frac{n^{*}(P_{e,\text{Cn}})}{n^{*}(P_{e})}\;\approx\;\frac{\log|A^{(i)}|}{\log|S_{O}^{(i)}|}\,,

(5.22)

identical to the homogeneous ratio of
Corollary
3.3
.

Part (i) combines
Theorem
5.1
(ii)
(upper bound) and (iii) (lower bound).
Part (ii) is
Theorem
3.4
(i) applied with

M=|S_{O}^{(i)}|
; the condition

S_{O}^{(i)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(j)}
ensures that the
Hamming criterion is meaningful (each sent state has a valid
identity reconstruction in the receiver’s vocabulary).
Part (iii) follows by dividing the bounds.
∎

Remark 5.15

(Heterogeneity does not degrade the compression
ratio)
.

The deductive compression
ratio (
5.22
) depends only on
the sender’s knowledge-base structure
(
|A^{(i)}|
vs.
|S_{O}^{(i)}|
) and not on the
receiver’s vocabulary
S_{O}^{(j)}
, provided the core
coverage condition
(H1)
holds.
This invariance is a consequence of the two-layer code
structure: the core sub-code operates identically regardless
of the receiver’s surplus states, and the redundant extension
incurs zero closure distortion by the algebraic properties
of
Cn
.

The Hamming baseline (
5.21
)
does, however, depend on the receiver’s vocabulary: it
requires
S_{-}^{ij}=\varnothing
(full vocabulary
containment), a strictly stronger condition than
(H1)
.
When
S_{-}^{ij}\neq\varnothing
, perfect Hamming
reconstruction is impossible (some sent states have no
counterpart in the receiver’s vocabulary), while closure
reliability may still be achievable under
(H1)
.
This gap illustrates the advantage of semantic fidelity
criteria over symbol-level criteria in heterogeneous settings.

Corollary 5.3

(Impossibility under core loss)
.

Let
(i,j)
be a sender–receiver pair.

(i)

Set-level impossibility:

If condition
(F1)
of
Proposition
5.3
fails,
i.e.,

A^{(i)}\not\subseteq\text{Cn}\bigl(S_{O}^{(j)}\bigr)
,
then

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S_{O}^{(i)},S_{O}^{(j)})<1
.
This is a property of the knowledge-base pair,
independent of the channel, the blocklength, and the
coding strategy.

(ii)

Quantitative bound:

Under the hypothesis of
(i)
, the closure
fidelity satisfies

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}\bigl(S_{O}^{(i)},S_{O}^{(j)}\bigr)\;=\;\frac{|\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})\cap\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})|}{|\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})\cup\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j)})|}\;<\;1.

(5.23)

(iii)

Core preservation ratio:

If
A_{-}^{ij}\neq\varnothing
, then

\rho_{\text{Atom}}(S_{O}^{(i)},S_{O}^{(j)})=1-|A_{-}^{ij}|/|A^{(i)}|<1

(Proposition
5.2
(ii))
.

Similarly, if condition
(F2)
fails

(
S_{+,n}^{ij}\neq\varnothing
)
, then

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}<1
regardless of any coding strategy.

Part (i) is the contrapositive of
Proposition
5.3
.
Part (ii) is the definition of

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}

(Definition
2.9
); the strict
inequality follows from (i).
Part (iii) is
Proposition
5.2
(ii).
The final claim follows from
Proposition
5.3
(necessity
of (F2)).
∎

Proposition 5.8

(Minimum receiver vocabulary for
closure-reliable communication)
.

Given a sender knowledge base
S_{O}^{(i)}
, the
minimum-cardinality subset

V\subseteq S_{O}^{(i)}
serving as a receiver vocabulary
(
\hat{S}_{O}=V
) that simultaneously achieves:

(a)

the two-layer code of
Theorem
5.1
(ii)

achieves
P_{e,\text{Cn}}^{(n)}\to 0
, and

(b)

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S_{O}^{(i)},V)=1
,

is
V^{*}=\text{Atom}(S_{O}^{(i)})=A^{(i)}
, with

|V^{*}|=\mathsf{A}(\mathcal{I}^{(i)})
.

Sufficiency.

Set
V=A^{(i)}
.
Since
A^{(i)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(i)}
, the overlap
with sender
i
and “receiver”
V
gives

S_{+}^{ij}=V\setminus S_{O}^{(i)}=\varnothing
and

A_{-}^{ij}=A^{(i)}\setminus V=\varnothing

(condition (H1)).
Condition (H2) holds trivially since

S_{+}^{ij}=\varnothing
.
Closure fidelity:

\text{Cn}(A^{(i)})=\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(i)})

(Proposition
2.1
(i)),
giving
\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S_{O}^{(i)},A^{(i)})=1
.
The two-layer code of
Theorem
5.1
(ii) applies
with
\hat{S}_{O}=V=A^{(i)}
.

Minimality.

Let
V\subseteq S_{O}^{(i)}
satisfy both
conditions (a) and (b).
Condition (a) requires the two-layer code
of Theorem
5.1
(ii) to
succeed.
That code’s decoder outputs elements of
A^{(i)}
,
so the output alphabet
\hat{S}_{O}=V
must contain
every core element:
A^{(i)}\subseteq V
.
Hence
|V|\geq|A^{(i)}|
.
Since
V=A^{(i)}
achieves this bound, it is
minimal.
∎

Remark 5.16

(Vocabulary design rule)
.

Proposition
5.8
yields a principled
vocabulary-selection rule for receiver design:

the receiver need store only the sender’s irredundant
core
.
All remaining semantic states (the sender’s stored
shortcuts
J^{(i)}
) can be reconstructed by the receiver’s
inference engine via
\text{Cn}(A^{(i)})
.
The channel-use cost of this strategy is

n^{*}\approx\log|A^{(i)}|/C(W_{ij})
, the minimum
achievable under closure reliability.

When the receiver already maintains a richer vocabulary

S_{O}^{(j)}\supsetneq A^{(i)}
, the additional states are
harmless provided
S_{+,n}^{ij}=\varnothing

(condition
(H2)
); they do not increase the
blocklength.
When some surplus states are non-derivable
(
S_{+,n}^{ij}\neq\varnothing
), set-level closure fidelity
drops below
1

(Corollary
5.3
), but the
coding-theoretic closure reliability may still hold if the
two-layer code is used (since it ignores the surplus entirely;
see Remark
5.13
).

Part 2: Broadcast Extension

We now extend the pairwise results to the broadcast scenario
of Definition
5.2
.
Agent
0
(the sender) communicates its knowledge base

S_{O}^{(0)}
to
K

with

.

Theorem 5.3

(Broadcast deductive compression)
.

Suppose that for every receiver

j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, the overlap conditions hold:

(BH1)

A_{-}^{0j}=\varnothing

(
the sender’s core is contained in every
receiver’s vocabulary:

A^{(0)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(j)}
)
;

(BH2)

(

the sender:

)
.

Then:

(i)

Simultaneous closure fidelity:

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S_{O}^{(0)},S_{O}^{(j)})=1
for
every
j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
.

(ii)

Broadcast achievability:

There exists a
single
sequence of

(n,|S_{O}^{(0)}|)
semantic block codes
(with a common encoding function
f_{n}
) such that

P_{e,\text{Cn}}^{(n,j)}\to 0
simultaneously for all

j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, provided

\frac{\log|A^{(0)}|}{n}\;<\;C(W).

(5.24)

(iii)

Blocklength independence from
K
:

The minimum blocklength for broadcast closure
reliability is

n^{*}_{\mathrm{bc}}\;\approx\;\frac{\log|A^{(0)}|}{C(W)},

(5.25)

independent of the number of receivers
K
.

(iv)

Broadcast converse:

Under
Assumption
3.1
for

S_{O}^{(0)}
with output space
S_{O}^{(j)}
for
each
j
, any code achieving

\max_{j}P_{e,\text{Cn}}^{(n,j)}\leq\epsilon
satisfies

\log|A^{(0)}|\leq(nC(W)+1)/(1-\epsilon)
.

(i): For each
j
, conditions (BH1)–(BH2)
instantiate (H1)–(H2) of

5.1
, giving

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S_{O}^{(0)},S_{O}^{(j)})=1
by

.

(ii): Construct a single two-layer code as in the proof of

5.1
(ii), with the

common

(f_{n}^{A},g_{n}^{A})
for message
set
A^{(0)}
.
All
K

\hat{S}_{C}^{n}
and each independently applies the
same core decoder

.
Since
A^{(0)}\subseteq S_{O}^{(j)}
for every

(by (BH1)), the decoded core element

.

(iii): The blocklength is determined by the core code,

, independent of

.

(iv): Fix any receiver

Proposition 5.9

(Broadcast semantic bottleneck)
.

In the broadcast scenario of
Definition
5.2
, suppose
there exists a receiver
j^{*}\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
such that
condition
(F1)
of
Proposition
5.3
fails for
the pair
(0,j^{*})
:

A^{(0)}\not\subseteq\text{Cn}\bigl(S_{O}^{(j^{*})}\bigr).

Then:

(i)

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}(S_{O}^{(0)},S_{O}^{(j^{*})})<1
,
regardless of the carrier channel
W
, the
blocklength
n
, and the encoding/decoding strategy.

(ii)

Even if the carrier channel is noiseless
(
W=\mathrm{id}_{S_{C}}
), the closure fidelity at
receiver
j^{*}
is bounded by

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}\bigl(S_{O}^{(0)},S_{O}^{(j^{*})}\bigr)\;=\;\frac{|\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(0)})\cap\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j^{*})})\!|}{|\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(0)})\cup\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j^{*})})\!|}\;<\;1.

(5.26)

(iii)

Receiver
j^{*}
is a
semantic bottleneck
:
its performance limitation arises from vocabulary
mismatch, not from the physical channel.
The other receivers
j\neq j^{*}
satisfying

(BH1)
–
(BH2)
achieve

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}=1
and closure reliability
simultaneously, unaffected by
j^{*}
.

Parts (i) and (ii) follow from
Corollary
5.3
(i)–(ii)
applied to the pair
(0,j^{*})
.
Part (iii): the common encoding and core code are
shared by all receivers; the failure at
j^{*}
is due
solely to the mismatch

, which is
independent of the channel.

(BH1)–(BH2) are handled by

5.3
.
∎

Remark 5.17

(Semantic bottleneck vs. classical channel
degradation)
.

In classical broadcast channel theory

[
8
,
9
]
, the weakest
receiver is the one with the noisiest channel, and the rate
region depends only on channel transition probabilities.
Proposition
5.9
reveals a second,
purely
structural
axis of weakness invisible to
classical theory: even over a noiseless carrier, a receiver
with
A^{(0)}\not\subseteq\text{Cn}(S_{O}^{(j^{*})})
cannot achieve

\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}=1
.
This semantic bottleneck is irreducible by coding: resolving
it requires a
design-time
vocabulary augmentation
(e.g., pre-loading
A^{(0)}
into
S_{O}^{(j^{*})}

per Proposition
5.8
), fundamentally
different from the encoder/decoder optimization that
suffices in classical settings.

Remark 5.18

(Classical recovery)
.

When
S_{O}^{(j)}=S_{O}^{(0)}
for all
j
,

(BH1)
–
(BH2)
hold vacuously and
Theorem
5.3
reduces to

Theorem
3.5
(ii).
If additionally
A^{(0)}=S_{O}^{(0)}

(irredundant source)
, the broadcast blocklength
becomes
n^{*}\approx\log|S_{O}^{(0)}|/C(W)
, recovering the
classical channel coding theorem
[
8
]
.

VI
Conclusion

This paper has developed a rate–distortion theory for semantic
communication grounded in formal proof systems.
The framework rests on three pillars: an axiomatic

information model
with computable enabling maps
(Section
II
); a
semantic channel
built as a
composition of enabling kernels
(Section
III
); and an
overlap-based

heterogeneous multi-agent theory
(Section
V
).

The central quantitative finding is the
deductive
compression gain
.
Under a closure-based fidelity criterion that accepts any
reconstruction preserving the deductive closure, the minimum
blocklength drops from

n_{H}^{*}\approx\log|S_{O}|/C(W)
to

n_{\text{Cn}}^{*}\approx\log|\text{Atom}(S_{O})|/C(W)
, yielding a
compression ratio
\log|\text{Atom}(S_{O})|/\log|S_{O}|<1
that is
invariant under receiver vocabulary heterogeneity.
This gain arises because the receiver’s inference engine
reconstructs all redundant states from the irredundant core at
zero additional channel cost.

The tight zero-distortion semantic rate

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0)=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})

(Theorem
4.1
) and the full
rate–distortion decomposition
(Theorem
4.2
) show that redundant states
are invisible to both rate and distortion under closure fidelity.
The semantic source–channel separation theorem
(Theorem
4.3
) exhibits a
semantic
leverage
phenomenon with leverage factor

\Lambda_{\infty}=\log|S_{O}|/(P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A}))>1
: under closure
fidelity the required source rate drops from
H(P_{O})
to

P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
, enabling the same knowledge base to be
communicated with proportionally fewer channel uses—not by
violating the Shannon capacity (the data processing bound

C_{\mathrm{sem}}\leq C(W)
remains in force) but because
closure-based fidelity renders redundant states “free.”

The rate–delay–distortion surface
(Theorem
4.6
) reveals a fundamental

depth-for-rate exchange
that has no classical counterpart:
each additional derivation step
\delta
at the receiver
renders a new stratum of states redundant, reducing the effective
source entropy along the filtration

R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,0)=H(P_{O})\geq\cdots\geq R_{\mathrm{sem}}(0,\mathsf{D_{d}})=P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
.
The critical delay
\delta^{*}
below which closure-reliable
communication is impossible
(Corollary
4.1
) is a semantic analogue of
the Nyquist sampling period, and the marginal rate of delay
quantifies the “value” of one derivation step in bits of
channel capacity.
The hard-budget staircase can be relaxed to an
expected-budget model via time-sharing among transmitted
bases (Remark
4.10
),
yielding a convex rate–computation tradeoff whose Lagrangian
multiplier prices one unit of receiver inference in bits of
communication rate—an exchange rate absent from classical
information theory, where the decoder’s computation is treated
as a free resource.

The strengthened semantic Fano inequality
(Theorem
4.4
) provides a
converse bound in which both the reference level and the
penalty term are improved: the former drops from
H(P_{O})
to

P_{A}\,H(\pi_{A})
, absorbing all redundant-state entropy, and
the latter involves
\log|A|
rather than
\log|S_{O}|
.
Combined with the semantic source–channel separation theorem,
this characterizes the operational regime where semantic
compression strictly outperforms symbol-level compression.
Six families of computable semantic channel invariants
(Theorem
3.3
) provide a multi-scale
fingerprint of channel quality: from set-level fidelity metrics
that depend only on the knowledge-base pair, through noise-pair
probabilistic indices that capture core preservation and
hallucination probabilities, to information-theoretic quantities
that bound achievable throughput.

In the heterogeneous multi-agent setting, the overlap
decomposition (Definition
5.4
)
translates knowledge-base structure into two binary feasibility
tests—no core loss (
A_{-}^{ij}=\varnothing
) and no
non-derivable surplus (
S_{+,n}^{ij}=\varnothing
)—that
fully determine whether perfect closure fidelity is achievable
(Proposition
5.3
).
The broadcast extension reveals a
semantic bottleneck
: a
receiver whose vocabulary does not cover the sender’s irredundant
core cannot achieve
\mathsf{F}_{\text{Cn}}=1
regardless of carrier
channel quality, blocklength, or coding strategy
(Proposition
5.9
).
Resolving this bottleneck requires a design-time vocabulary
augmentation, fundamentally different from the encoder/decoder
optimization that suffices classically.

Relation to Prior Work

The framework complements several existing lines of research.
The synonymous-mapping theory of Niu and
Zhang
[
25
,
38
]
achieves a
semantic leverage effect through source-side equivalence-class
collapsing (their “
C_{s}\geq C
” result quantifies semantic
throughput gains under a synonymous-mapping fidelity criterion;
it does not violate the Shannon capacity upper bound

C_{\mathrm{sem}}\leq C(W)
, which remains valid by data
processing);
our framework achieves a complementary gain through receiver-side
deductive reconstruction.
A unified theory combining both mechanisms—synonymous
collapsing of the irredundant core followed by deductive
expansion at the receiver—could potentially compound the
two gains; establishing the precise interaction is an open
problem.
The semantic channel coding theorem of Ma
et al.
[
21
]
and the companion tools
of
[
14
,
19
]
operate within the
synonymous-mapping paradigm; our two-layer code mechanism and
closure-based fidelity criterion handle the structured
knowledge-base setting and extend naturally to heterogeneous
vocabularies.
On the multi-agent front, the overlap decomposition and semantic
bottleneck phenomenon provide a coding-theoretic complement to
the Bayesian approach of Seo
et al.
[
28
]
and the modal-logic framework of
Alshammari and Bennis
[
3
]
: where those
works quantify inference cost and resilience conditions, our
framework quantifies the minimum number of channel uses needed to
overcome vocabulary mismatch.
The irredundant core can also be viewed as a form of query-aware
source compression related to database-theoretic notions of view
materialization
[
22
,
2
]
.

Limitations and Future Directions

The common proof system assumption
(Assumption
5.1
) could be relaxed to

heterogeneous
proof systems where each agent uses a
sub-system
\mathsf{PS}^{(i)}\subseteq\mathsf{PS}
, yielding
richer compression/fidelity trade-offs.
The current single-letter coding theorems invite a

multi-letter
extension for temporally correlated
knowledge-base streams, connecting the framework to ergodic
source theory.
The relay scenario (Remark
5.1
), where
intermediate inference may change the effective end-to-end
capacity, requires multi-hop coding theorems built on the
composition machinery of
Definition
A.4
.
Scaling core extraction and closure computation to knowledge
graphs with millions of entities, and the formal connection
between derivation depth
Dd
and Bennett’s logical
depth
[
5
]
, are further promising avenues.

From a structural standpoint, the receiver’s inference engine
plays a role analogous to
decoder side information
in
Wyner–Ziv coding
[
34
]
: the shared proof system

\mathsf{PS}
provides the decoder with a “structured
codebook” (the closure operator
Cn
) that reduces the
effective source rate without requiring explicit side-information
transmission.
Formalizing this analogy—e.g., by deriving a semantic
Wyner–Ziv theorem in which the side information is the proof
system itself—would connect the present framework to the
established body of source coding with side information and may
yield tighter bounds when the proof system is only partially
shared.

Appendix A
Axiomatic Foundations

This appendix provides the full axiomatic development of the
information model framework summarized in
Section
II
.

A-A

Logical Language and Expressible State Sets

Throughout we fix a many-sorted logical language

\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{FO(LFP)}[\Sigma]

[
15
]

with sorts including
\mathsf{Obj}
,
\mathsf{Time}
, and

\mathsf{Carrier}
.

Assumption A.1

(Finite ordered structures)
.

We restrict attention to
finite

\mathcal{L}
-structures.
When descriptive-complexity claims are invoked, we work over

ordered
finite structures

[
24
,
31
]
.

Assumption A.2

(Semantic sublanguage)
.

Fix a partition

\Sigma=\Sigma_{\mathrm{sem}}\,\dot{\cup}\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{rep}}
,
where
\Sigma_{\mathrm{rep}}
contains auxiliary symbols (including
any built-in order).
The designated semantic sublanguage is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}:=\mathcal{L}\!\upharpoonright_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{sem}}}
.
All notions of semantic equivalence are relative
to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
.

Definition A.1

(Expressible state sets)
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a finite
\mathcal{L}
-structure and let

(X,T)
be definable subdomains of the object and time sorts.
An
(object–time) state domain
over
(X,T)
is an

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable binary relation
S(x,t)

with

\mathfrak{R}\models\forall x\,\forall t\,[S(x,t)\to(\delta_{X}(x)\wedge\delta_{T}(t))]
.
A set
A\subseteq X\times T
is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-
expressible
over
S
if
there exists an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-formula

\varphi(x,t)
with

(x,t)\in A\Leftrightarrow\mathfrak{R}\models S(x,t)\wedge\varphi(x,t)
.
We write
s\in S
as shorthand for a pair
(x,t)
with

S(x,t)
, and set
\mathsf{time}(s):=t
.

A-B

Information Model and Enabling Mechanisms

Definition A.2

(State spaces)
.

Information is modeled using object/carrier domains
O,C
with
time domains
T_{O},T_{C}
and state sets

S_{O}\subseteq O\times T_{O}
,

S_{C}\subseteq C\times T_{C}
.
We write
s_{o}=(o,\tau)\in S_{O}
and
s_{c}=(c,\theta)\in S_{C}
.

Axiom A.1

(Time domains and precedence)
.

Fix a finite
\mathcal{L}
-structure
\mathfrak{R}
.
The state sets
S_{O},S_{C}
are

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable.
There exist
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable linear orders

\prec_{O}
on
T_{O}
,
\prec_{C}
on
T_{C}
, and a
cross-domain precedence
\prec_{OC}\subseteq T_{O}\times T_{C}

that is monotone with respect to
\prec_{O}
and
\prec_{C}
,
and mildly total (every
\tau\in T_{O}
has some

\theta\in T_{C}
with
\tau\prec_{OC}\theta
).

Axiom A.2

(State representation)
.

There exist injective encodings

\text{enc}_{O}:S_{O}\to\{0,1\}^{*}
and
\text{enc}_{C}:S_{C}\to\{0,1\}^{*}
such
that semantic-time, carrier-time, and cross-domain precedence
predicates, as well as membership, are decidable from the codes.

Axiom A.3

(Enabling mapping)
.

There exists a relation

R_{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq O\times T_{O}\times C\times T_{C}

inducing a set-valued map

\mathcal{E}:S_{O}\Rightarrow S_{C}
satisfying:

(E1)
totality (
\mathcal{E}(s_{o})\neq\varnothing
for
all
s_{o}
);

(E2)
coverage
(
\bigcup_{s_{o}}\mathcal{E}(s_{o})=S_{C}
);

(E3)
existence of a computable enabling selector;

(E4)
precedence compatibility
(
s_{c}\in\mathcal{E}(s_{o})
implies

\mathsf{time}_{O}(s_{o})\prec_{OC}\mathsf{time}_{C}(s_{c})
).

Definition A.3

(Information model)
.

An
information model
is

\mathcal{I}=\langle O,T_{O},S_{O},C,T_{C},S_{C},R_{\mathcal{E}}\rangle

together with the temporal structure of
Axiom
A.1
.

Definition A.4

(Composition of information models)
.

Two models
\mathcal{I}_{1},\mathcal{I}_{2}
are
composable
if

S_{C}^{(1)}=S_{O}^{(2)}
.
The composite enabling map is

\mathcal{E}_{2\circ 1}(s_{o}):=\bigcup_{s_{c}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(s_{o})}\mathcal{E}_{2}(s_{c})
.

Proposition A.1

(Composition preserves enabling axioms)
.

If
(\mathcal{I}_{1},\mathcal{I}_{2})
is composable and each satisfies
Axiom
A.3
, then

\mathcal{E}_{2\circ 1}
satisfies

(E1)
–
(E3)
.

(E1): totality of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
yields

s_{c}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(s_{o})
; totality of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
gives

\mathcal{E}_{2}(s_{c})\neq\varnothing
, so

\mathcal{E}_{2\circ 1}(s_{o})\supseteq\mathcal{E}_{2}(s_{c})\neq\varnothing
.
(E2): for any
s^{\prime}\in S_{C}^{(2)}
, coverage
of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
gives
s_{c}
with
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{2}(s_{c})
;
coverage of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
gives
s_{o}
with

s_{c}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}(s_{o})
, whence

s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{2\circ 1}(s_{o})
.
(E3): if
e_{1},e_{2}
are selectors,

e_{2\circ 1}:=e_{2}\circ e_{1}
is computable and satisfies

e_{2\circ 1}(s_{o})\in\mathcal{E}_{2}(e_{1}(s_{o}))\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{2\circ 1}(s_{o})
.
∎

Remark A.1

(Associativity)
.

Composition of enabling maps is associative: for a composable triple,

\mathcal{E}_{3\circ(2\circ 1)}=\mathcal{E}_{(3\circ 2)\circ 1}
,
so iterated compositions are unambiguous.

Synonymous State Sets and Ideal Information

Definition A.5

(
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable coding
isomorphism)
.

A relation
G_{12}\subseteq S_{1}\times S_{2}
is an

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable coding isomorphism graph if
it is definable by an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-formula and
induces a bijection between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}

(i.e., for every
s_{1}\in S_{1}
there is a unique

s_{2}\in S_{2}
with
G_{12}(s_{1},s_{2})
, and vice versa).

Definition A.6

(Synonymous state sets)
.

State sets
S_{1},S_{2}
are
synonymous
, written

S_{1}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}S_{2}
, if there exists an

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable coding isomorphism graph

G_{12}
inducing a bijection
\tau_{12}:S_{1}\to S_{2}
that
preserves and reflects the induced time precedence:

s\prec_{S_{1}}s^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow\tau_{12}(s)\prec_{S_{2}}\tau_{12}(s^{\prime})
.

Proposition A.2

(
\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}
is an equivalence relation)
.

The relation
\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}
on

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable state sets is reflexive,
symmetric, and transitive.

Reflexivity is witnessed by the identity graph

G(s,s^{\prime}):=\mathbf{1}[s=s^{\prime}]
, which is

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable.
Symmetry: if
G_{12}
witnesses
S_{1}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}S_{2}
, then

G_{21}(s_{2},s_{1}):=G_{12}(s_{1},s_{2})
witnesses
S_{2}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}S_{1}
.
Transitivity: given witnesses
G_{12}\subseteq S_{1}\times S_{2}
and

G_{23}\subseteq S_{2}\times S_{3}
, define

G_{13}(s_{1},s_{3}):=\exists\,s_{2}\,[G_{12}(s_{1},s_{2})\wedge G_{23}(s_{2},s_{3})]
.
This is
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable (closed under
quantification over its own sorts), and the uniqueness clauses of each
component ensure
G_{13}
is a bijection.
The induced map
\tau_{13}=\tau_{23}\circ\tau_{12}
preserves and
reflects precedence by composition.
∎

Definition A.7

(Ideal information)
.

An information model
\mathcal{I}
is
ideal
if

S_{O}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}S_{C}
, witnessed by a coding isomorphism graph

G_{OC}
with induced bijection
\tau_{OC}
, and

\mathcal{E}(s_{o})=\{\tau_{OC}(s_{o})\}
for every

s_{o}\in S_{O}
.

Assumption A.3

(Common semantic universe)
.

The ambient set

\mathbb{S}_{O}\supseteq S_{O}

(
introduced in Section
II-A
)

is closed under
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable recodings:
if
S\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
and
S^{\prime}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}S
with
S^{\prime}
over the
same sorts, then
S^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
.
Moreover,
\mathbb{S}_{O}
is effectively representable

(
the encoding
\text{enc}_{O}
and membership predicate extend to
all of
\mathbb{S}_{O}
)
.

Assumption A.4

(Carrier representability)
.

There exists an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable

S^{\prime}_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
with
S^{\prime}_{O}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}S_{C}
.

Definition A.8

(Noisy information)
.

Given
\mathcal{I}
satisfying
Assumption
A.4
and an

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}
-definable

\tilde{S}_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
with
\tilde{S}_{O}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}S_{C}

(
witnessed by
G
with bijection

\tau_{OC}:\tilde{S}_{O}\to S_{C}
)
,
the
noisy information
is

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}=(\tilde{S}_{O},S_{C},G,\tau_{OC},\tau_{CO})
with noise pair

S_{O}^{-}:=S_{O}\setminus\tilde{S}_{O}
and

S_{O}^{+}:=\tilde{S}_{O}\setminus S_{O}
.

Proposition A.3

(Existence of noisy information)
.

Under Assumption
A.4
, for every
information model
\mathcal{I}
there exists a noisy
information
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}
with

\tilde{S}_{O}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}S_{C}
.
If
\mathcal{I}
is ideal, the perturbation is trivial.

Set
\tilde{S}_{O}:=S^{\prime}_{O}
from
Assumption
A.4
.
If
\mathcal{I}
is ideal,
S_{O}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}}S_{C}
, so choosing

S^{\prime}_{O}:=S_{O}
gives
S_{O}^{-}=S_{O}^{+}=\varnothing
.
∎

Remark A.2

(Computability of semantic invariants for noisy bases)

Since
\tilde{S}_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}

with the finite set
S_{C}

\mathsf{A}(\tilde{\mathcal{I}})=|\text{Atom}(\tilde{S}_{O})|

Theorem
2.1
(i)
.
