A Successful Outcome of Upadacitinib Therapy in a Patient with Genital Erosive Fixed Drug Eruption: A Case Report
Authors: Changxia Li, Xiaobing Wang, Xinze Li, Meiqin Xu, Shuling Kong, Xiaohua Tao, Weijun Liu, Haiping Wan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFixed drug eruption (FDE) is a common cutaneous adverse drug reaction with limited effective treatments for severe genital erosive cases. This first reported case demonstrates the successful use of the selective Janus kinase 1 (JAK1) inhibitor upadacitinib as monotherapy for a severe, erosive FDE involving the genitalia. A 50-year-old male presented with rapidly progressing, painful erythema, erosions, and exudation on the scrotum and glans penis following drug intake, confirmed by histopathology. Treated solely with upadacitinib, the lesions achieved near-complete re-epithelialization by day 18, with no recurrence or adverse events during follow-up. This case highlights upadacitinib as a rapidly effective and well-tolerated novel therapeutic option for severe FDE in sensitive areas, addressing an unmet need and expanding the potential application of targeted Janus kinase (JAK) inhibition in cutaneous adverse drug reactions.

Introduction

FDE is a distinctive cutaneous adverse drug reaction characterized by the activation of skin-resident memory CD8+ T cells1 and cytokine-mediated keratinocyte damage, driven by key mediators such as Interferon-γ (IFN-γ) and Tumor Necrosis Factor-α(TNF-α).2,3 Studie have reported that up to 90% of male patients with FDE have genital involvement.4 Severe blistering and erosions in the genital region often cause significant pain and psychosocial burden, creating an urgent need for rapid, effective and safe therapeutic options. While systemic corticosteroids remain a mainstay of treatment, their use may be limited by potential adverse effects, including hypertension, hyperglycemia, osteoporosis, gastrointestinal injury, increased infection risk, and rebound flare after dose reduction.5 In recent years, JAK inhibitors have demonstrated significant efficacy in the treatment of severe cutaneous adverse drug reactions such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis (SJS/TEN),6,7 Notably, fixed drug eruption may share overlapping pathophysiological mechanisms with SJS/TEN. However, there are currently no published reports on the use of JAK inhibitors, particularly the highly selective JAK1 inhibitor upadacitinib, for managing FDE. This article presents the first documented case of severe genital erosive FDE successfully treated with upadacitinib monotherapy. It aims to propose a novel, rapid, and well-tolerated potential targeted treatment option for such refractory cases and to provide preliminary evidence for further exploration of this therapeutic strategy.

Case Presentation

A 50-year-old male patient presented with a 10-day history of erythema and erosions on the scrotum and glans penis, accompanied by pruritus and severe pain. Four days prior to the onset of skin lesions, he had taken compound amantadine hydrochloride capsules and spiramycin tablets for a “cold and fever”. He had no prior history of exposure to either medication. Four days after initiating these medications, he developed erythema on the scrotum with pruritus. Over the subsequent 10 days, the lesions progressively worsened, evolving into erosions with exudation and severe pain. Treatment at a local clinic with intravenous ceftriaxone sodium, topical Kangfuxin liquid, and human epidermal growth factor failed to halt the progression, and the lesions continued to expand. Upon admission, the patient’s vital signs were stable Dermatological examination revealed involvement of the entire scrotum and glans penis, characterized by erythema, epidermal denudation, erosions, and exudation (Figure 1), associated with severe pain (VAS pain score: 9). Laboratory investigations, including complete blood count, biochemistry, serology for anti-dsg1, anti-dsg3, and anti-bp180 antibodies, T-SPOT.TB, as well as HSV-1/2 and bacterial cultures from the lesion site, were all negative. A skin biopsy from the scrotal lesion revealed epidermal hyperkeratosis, parakeratosis, neutrophilic aggregation within the stratum corneum, mild squamous hyperplasia with elongation of rete ridges, focal liquefactive degeneration of basal cells, and infiltration of neutrophils and lymphocytes in the superficial dermis and perivascular areas (Figure 2). After the patient was admitted, wet compresses with Kangfuxin Liquid and dressing changes with mupirocin cream were applied to the wound site twice daily. After three and five days, the patient’s skin lesions showed improvement compared to before (Figure 3a and b). Given the particular location of the lesions, the severity of symptoms, and the significant physical and psychological burden on the patient, there was a strong imperative for rapid and effective intervention. After excluding contraindications including malignancy, tuberculosis, and hepatic or renal impairment, we initiated treatment with upadacitinib extended-release tablets 15 mg once daily on day 6, without the concomitant use of corticosteroids or other immunosuppressants. Over the 18-day treatment course, the patient’s skin lesions gradually improved and healed, and his pain progressively subsided; he was discharged on day 23 of hospitalization (Figure 4a–c). Discussion

Upadacitinib is a selective Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor approved by regulatory authorities worldwide, including the US Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency, for the treatment of various chronic inflammatory diseases such as rheumatic, dermatologic, and gastrointestinal conditions.8 Upadacitinib inhibits JAK, thereby blocking the phosphorylation of downstream effector proteins and suppressing cytokine signaling in key inflammatory disease pathway,9,10 targets a pathway central to the pathogenesis of FDE. The drug’s high selectivity for JAK1 enables it to effectively block the signaling of critical cytokines like IFN-γ, IL-6, and IL-15, and to inhibit CD8+ T cell activity.11 This mechanism directly counteracts the core pathogenic event in FDE, which involves the activation and epidermal migration of resident CD8+ T cells and their production of cytokines such as IFN-γ and TNF-α.12

Currently, clinical reports on the use of upadacitinib for FDE remain scarce. However, emerging evidence underscores the potential of JAK inhibitors in severe cutaneous adverse reactions. The Nordmann team first identified the pivotal role of the JAK/STAT pathway in SJS/TEN, which was subsequently validated in preclinical models. Thereafter, the team administered abrocitinib 200 mg/d or tofacitinib 10 mg/d in combination with methylprednisolone to 7 patients with SJS/TEN; all 7 patients with severe drug eruptions treated with a JAK inhibitor in their study showed favorable responses.13 Meanwhile, there have also been successful reports of treatment with upadacitinib 45 mg/day in combination with methylprednisolone, and no adverse drug reactions were detected.14 In the report by Zhou et al, a patient diagnosed with SJS/TEN achieved rapid disease control following combination therapy with methylprednisolone (40 mg daily, patient weight 49 kg) and upadacitinib (15 mg daily for 2 consecutive weeks).15 Importantly, FDE shares core pathogenic mechanisms with SJS/TEN, as both are driven by the activation of CD8+ T cells and the release of cytokines such as interferons, leading to overlapping clinical and histopathological features.12 This mechanistic overlap provides a strong rationale for exploring JAK inhibition, specifically with upadacitinib, in the management of FDE.

The safety profile of upadacitinib should also be considered. Upadacitinib has been shown to have a well-established safety profile across multiple indications. Multiple studies have indicated that, in addition to potential elevations in transaminases, lipids, and creatine kinase, upadacitinib may also cause serious adverse events such as infections and malignancies.16,17 In our patient, no clinical or laboratory adverse events were observed during the three-month follow-up period, and there were no signs of recurrence or withdrawal effects after treatment discontinuation. However, given the lack of established protocols for JAK inhibitor use in fixed drug eruption, careful patient selection, baseline screening for infections (particularly tuberculosis and viral hepatitis), and close monitoring during treatment remain essential. Long-term safety data still warrant further investigation.

The strength of this case report lies in its first demonstration of the remarkable efficacy of the highly selective JAK1 inhibitor upadacitinib as monotherapy for severe genital erosive fixed drug eruption. Without concomitant systemic corticosteroids or other immunosuppressants, the patient achieved near-complete re-epithelialization by day 18, with no recurrence or adverse events observed during the three-month follow-up period. This outcome offers a novel therapeutic avenue for refractory FDE, particularly for cases involving sensitive areas where rapid intervention is crucial. However, as a single-case observation, this report has inherent limitations, including the inability to exclude the influence of individual variability, the lack of comparative controls, and the need for validation of long-term efficacy and safety through larger studies with extended follow-up duration.

Conclusion

We report the successful treatment of severe genital erosive fixed drug eruption with upadacitinib monotherapy. The rapid re-epithelialization and complete resolution observed, coupled with the absence of recurrence or adverse events, suggest that upadacitinib offers a promising targeted therapeutic avenue for refractory fixed drug eruption, particularly in sensitive anatomical areas. Given the mechanistic parallels with other severe cutaneous adverse reactions, this approach warrants further validation in larger studies to confirm its efficacy and safety.
