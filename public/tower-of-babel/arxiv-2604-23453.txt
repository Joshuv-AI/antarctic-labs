A robust a posteriori error estimator for the Oseen problem
Muhammad Afzal, Naveed Ahmed, Volker John

Abstract
A residual-based a posteriori error estimator is proposed for the incompressible Oseen problem in the convection-dominated regime. The SUPG/PSPG/grad-div stabilized finite element method is used as discretization. The error estimator estimates the global error in a norm that is used in the a priori error analysis of the method. Based on several hypotheses concerning the error and interpolation errors, the robustness of the estimator in the convection-dominated regime is proved. Numerical studies support the analytic results. Finally, the extension of the a posteriori error estimator to the steady-state Navier–Stokes equations is discussed.

Introduction

This paper considers the stationary incompressible Oseen problem, already with dimensionless quantities,
for the velocity
\boldsymbol{u}\ :\ \Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the
pressure
p\ :\ \Omega\to\mathbb{R}
that is of the form

\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\nu\,\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+(\boldsymbol{b}\!\cdot\!\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}+\sigma\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p&=&\boldsymbol{f}&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}&=&0&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{u}&=&\boldsymbol{0}&\text{on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}},\\
(\nu\nabla\boldsymbol{u}-p\mathbb{I})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}&=&\boldsymbol{g}&\mbox{on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}},\end{array}

(1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, is a polygonal (polyhedral) domain with Lipschitz boundary

\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\cap\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}=\emptyset
. The unit outer normal vector at

\partial\Omega
is denoted by
\boldsymbol{n}
and
\mathbb{I}
is the unit tensor.
The Dirichlet part
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
has positive

(d-1)
-dimensional measure and contains the inflow boundary

\partial\Omega^{-}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\partial\Omega\ :\ \ \boldsymbol{b}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}(\boldsymbol{x})<0\right\}\subset\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}.

(2)

Given data comprise the (kinematic) viscosity
0<\nu\ll 1
, the convection field

\boldsymbol{b}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}={\boldsymbol{W}}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, which is assumed to be divergence-free
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{b}=0
a.e. in
\Omega
,
a scalar reaction field

\sigma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}\geq 0
,
\sigma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, a.e. in
\Omega
, acting componentwise on
\boldsymbol{u}
, a body force

\boldsymbol{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}=\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, and boundary data

\boldsymbol{g}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}})^{d}=\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}})
. The consideration of homogeneous data at the Dirichlet boundary is just
for the sake of simplifying the presentation of the numerical analysis. Inhomogeneous
Dirichlet boundary data could be incorporated with a standard lifting argument.

Oseen problems are standard model problems in numerical analysis. They can be considered as
a linearized incompressible Navier–Stokes problem. In practice, Oseen-type problems have to be solved in each
step of certain iterative methods in each time step for time-dependent Navier–Stokes problems, where
\sigma
is
proportional to the inverse of the length of the time step.

A situation of much interest in practice is the case that
\nu\ll\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, the
so-called convection-dominated case. In this case, where the convective transport dominates the viscous one,
the solution might possess very small scales, e.g.,
layers, that cannot be resolved on affordable grids.
Thus, from the numerical point of view, one
faces a multiscale problem. It is well known that the numerical simulation of multiscale problems
requires to use special numerical methods to perform stable simulations. There are various proposals
of stabilized finite element methods for Oseen problems, which include the streamline-upwind Petrov–Galerkin (SUPG)/pressure-stabilization Petrov–Galerkin (PSPG)
method, e.g., see
[
15
,
24
,
12
]
for some of the early applications of this method to incompressible flow problems,
the local projection stabilization (LPS) method, e.g. see
[
7
]
for its application to the Oseen problem, or the continuous interior
penalty (CIP) method, studied for the Oseen problem in
[
8
]
. The most popular one is probably the first method. Another
stabilization, which is popular for finite element methods applied to all kinds of incompressible flow problems,
is the grad-div stabilization from
[
13
]
. Originally, this stabilization was proposed to improve
the local mass conservation of finite element solutions. But it has been found that it also stabilizes
dominant convection, e.g., see
[
11
,
14
]
.

This paper considers SUPG/PSPG/grad-div stabilized finite element methods for the Oseen problem. With
this method, both inf-sup stable pairs of velocity-pressure finite element spaces and equal order
pairs can be used. The a priori error analysis of this method, see
[
25
]
, aims to prove a
so-called convection-robust (or semi-robust) estimate. That is, the constant in the estimate does not
blow up if
\nu\to 0
. To achieve such an estimate, one has to use an appropriate norm, which contains
contributions from the stabilization, compare (
9
) and (
10
) below.

The goals of an a posteriori error analysis are twofold. First, a derived error estimator should
provide information about the global error in some norm. Second, the local contributions of the estimator
should control the construction of adaptively refined grids. There are several types of a posteriori
error estimators, e.g., residual-based estimators, see
[
28
]
, or estimators that are based on the
solution of local problems, see
[
3
]
. In particular, estimators for the Oseen or the steady-state
Navier–Stokes equations can be found, e.g., in
[
2
,
6
,
9
,
4
,
5
]
. As discussed in
[
16
]
,
none of these estimators leads to convection-robust estimates. More recently, in
[
22
]
, a robust
residual-based estimator for a
\boldsymbol{H}(\mathrm{div})
-conforming discontinuous Galerkin (DG) finite element method
for the Oseen equations is presented and analyzed. This method contains only the
standard stabilization term of DG methods.
The robust error bound is derived with respect to a norm that has as one part
a dual seminorm, which can be only approximated, but not computed.

This paper can be considered as an extension of
[
19
]
, where a robust residual-based error estimator
for convection-dominated scalar equations was proposed. Like in
[
19
]
, one of the goals is that the estimator
should be for the same norm for which the a priori finite element error analysis of the method is performed.
And again like in
[
19
]
, the derivation of the estimator is based on some hypotheses concerning the relation
of interpolation errors and the error of the finite element solution in the stabilized norm. Some of the hypotheses
are analogs to those from
[
19
]
, but some new hypotheses are needed, in particular coming from the fact
that the second component of the solution is the pressure field. With this approach, a residual-based
a posteriori error estimator is derived and an estimate for the global error, in a norm that
is used in the a priori error analysis, is proved. Numerical studies support the robustness of the proposed
estimator. In addition, the satisfaction of the hypotheses is studied. Finally, an extension of the
proposed estimator to the steady-state incompressible Navier–Stokes problem is presented and illustrated with a
brief numerical study.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div stabilized
finite element method for the Oseen problem, together with all necessary notations. The residual-based
a posteriori error estimator is derived and discussed in Section
3
. Section
4

contains the numerical studies for the Oseen problem. The discussion of an extension of the error
estimator to the steady-state Navier–Stokes problem is presented in Section
5
.

\boldsymbol{P}_{k}

and the space

consists of piecewise polynomials of degree

,
continuous or discontinuous, so that

on simplicial grids and

Q_{h}\in\{Q_{l},Q_{l}^{\mathrm{disc}}\}

\tilde{Q}=\left\{q\in Q\ :\ \left.q\right|_{K}\in H^{1}(K)\mbox{ for all }K\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\right\}.

Let
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
be two mesh cells
with a common facet

\left[\!\!\;\left|v\right|\!\!\;\right]_{F}=\lim_{\boldsymbol{y}\to\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in K_{1}}v(\boldsymbol{y})-\lim_{\boldsymbol{y}\to\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in K_{2}}v(\boldsymbol{y}),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in F,

\mathcal{T}_{h}
of

\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}=\bigcup F
. Given
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)

\boldsymbol{g}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}})^{d}

\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}\right)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
such that

A_{\mathrm{spg}}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}\right),\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}\right)\right)=L_{\mathrm{spg}}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}\right)\right)\quad\forall\;\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}\right)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h},

(6)

where the bilinear form
A_{\mathrm{spg}}\ :\ (\boldsymbol{V}\times\tilde{Q})\times(\boldsymbol{V}\times\tilde{Q})\to\mathbb{R}

is defined by

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{spg}}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{u},p\right),\left(\boldsymbol{v},q\right)\right)

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\left(\nabla\boldsymbol{u},\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\right)+\left(\left(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla\right)\boldsymbol{u}+\sigma\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right)-\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v},p\right)+\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u},q\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mu_{K}\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\gamma_{F}\left(\left[\!\!\;\left|p\right|\!\!\;\right]_{F},\left[\!\!\;\left|q\right|\!\!\;\right]_{F}\right)_{F}

L_{\mathrm{spg}}\ :\ (\boldsymbol{V}\times\tilde{Q})\to\mathbb{R}

L_{\mathrm{spg}}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{v},q\right)\right)=\left(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}\right)+\sum_{F\in\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{F}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\boldsymbol{f},\delta_{K}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla\right)\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla q\right)\right)_{K}.

(8)

The parameters of method (
6

8
) are the grad-div parameter

\mu_{K}\geq 0
, the jump parameter
\gamma_{F}>0
, and the SUPG parameter
\delta_{K}>0
. Notice that the jump
term disappears if
Q_{h}
consists of continuous functions. It can be also observed that the SUPG term leads
to a coupling of pressure ansatz and test functions. This kind of coupling is called PSPG method. Consequently,
the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div method does not lead to a problem of saddle point type.

The goal of using stabilized methods consists of computing numerical solutions that show an improved physical
consistency. For the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div stabilized finite element method of the Oseen equations, spurious
oscillations should be reduced by the SUPG stabilization and the conservation of mass should be improved
by the grad-div stabilization. The reduction of spurious oscillations is usually supported in the numerical
analysis with so-called robust error estimates
(sometimes called semi-robust). These are estimates where the constant does not blow up for
\nu\to 0
and
all other data fixed. That is, if the dependency on
\nu
is tracked in the analysis, robust error bounds do not contain
inverse powers of
\nu
. It is well known that robust estimates for convection-dominated problems can be
achieved only for special norms. Such norms contain usually terms of the actually used stabilization.
For the analysis of the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div method, appropriate (mesh-dependent) norms are

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},q\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\nu\left\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\right\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\sigma^{1/2}\boldsymbol{v}\right\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mu_{K}\left\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\gamma_{F}\left\|\left[\!\!\;\left|q\right|\!\!\;\right]_{F}\right\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{K}\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla\right)\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla q\right\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(K)}^{2}\Bigg)^{1/2}

and

\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},q\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg},p}=\left(\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},q\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}+\omega_{\mathrm{pres}}^{-2}\left\|q\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(10)

with

\omega_{\mathrm{pres}}=\max\left\{1,\nu^{-1/2},\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\right\}.

The numerical analysis of the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div method, usually presented for the situation

is well established, showing, under appropriate assumptions on some stabilization parameters, existence and
uniqueness of a solution and a robust estimate for the errors

Remark 1

(Optimal asymptotic choice of the stabilization parameters)
.

To achieve optimal order of convergence in these norms, one has to choose
the stabilization parameters appropriately, e.g., see the discussion in
[

has to distinguish two situations that are of main interest.

If one uses a pair of finite element spaces

k\geq 2

\delta_{K}\sim h_{K}^{2},\quad\mu_{K}\sim 1,\quad\gamma_{F}\sim h_{K}.

(11)

These choices lead to the convergence of order
k
. If in the following ’inf-sup stable pairs of spaces’ are
mentioned, then always pairs with polynomials of order
k
for the velocity and

In the case of using equal order spaces, i.e.,
k=l\geq 1

then the recommended asymptotic choices are

\delta_{K}\sim\begin{cases}h_{K}&\mbox{if }\nu<h_{K},\\
h_{K}^{2}&\mbox{if }\nu\geq h_{K},\end{cases}\quad\mu_{K}\sim\begin{cases}h_{K}&\mbox{if }\nu<h_{K},\\

h_{K}&\mbox{if }\nu\geq h_{K}.\end{cases}

(12)

The order of error reduction in the convection-dominated regime is

and in the diffusion-dominated

3
The a Posteriori Estimator

The derivation of the a posteriori error indicator follows
[
19
]
. It is based on some
hypotheses concerning the relation of some norms of interpolation errors and the error in the
norm
\left\|(\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}
. In particular, hypotheses on pressure interpolation
errors are needed. The difficulty is that the norm
\left\|(\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}
does not
contain a separate pressure term. Hence it is hard to find good reasonings for the stated assumptions.
In fact, we cannot provide such a good reasoning for the term that contains the jump of the
finite element pressure. For this reason, only pairs of finite element spaces will be considered
with continuous finite element pressure, so that the jump term does not appear. In practice, the
use of continuous finite element pressures seems to be much more popular when applying the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div method,
since it simplifies the implementation of this method considerably.

First, some properties of the discrete problem are shown. It can be checked with a straightforward
calculation that the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div method is consistent, i.e.,

A_{\mathrm{spg}}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{u},p\right),\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}\right)\right)=L_{\mathrm{spg}}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}\right)\right)\quad\forall\;\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}\right)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h},

for a sufficiently smooth solution
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\boldsymbol{V}\times Q
of (
)–(
4
),
e.g., compare
[
21
, Lemma 5.31]
.
From consistency the so-called Galerkin orthogonality follows

A_{\mathrm{spg}}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right),\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}\right)\right)=0\quad\forall\;\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}\right)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}.

(15)

∎

As next step, local residuals are defined in the usual way, namely the mesh cell residual

\boldsymbol{r}_{K}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}\right)=\left.\left(\boldsymbol{f}+\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\left(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla\right)\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\sigma\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\nabla p_{h}\right)\right|_{K}\quad\forall\;K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

and the residual at the facets

\boldsymbol{r}_{F}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}\right)=\begin{cases}\left[\!\!\;\left|\left(-\nu\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+p_{h}\mathbb{I}\right)\right|\!\!\;\right]_{F}\boldsymbol{n}_{F},&\mbox{if }F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,\mathrm{in}},\\
\boldsymbol{g}-\left(\nu\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-p_{h}\mathbb{I}\right)\boldsymbol{n}_{F},&\mbox{if }F\in\mathcal{F}_{h},\ F\subset\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}},\\
\boldsymbol{0},&\mbox{if }F\in\mathcal{F}_{h},\ F\subset\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}.\end{cases}

(18)

Remark 3

(Polynomial approximations of coefficients)
.

Strictly speaking, instead of the coefficients
\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{b},\sigma
one
would need to use polynomial approximations of these coefficients, so that the arising integrals and norms can be
computed exactly. Then, the errors committed by these approximations enter the error bound. We decided to neglect
this technical issue to keep the notations simple and to concentrate in the error bounds below on the
essential terms.

With the notation of the residuals, one obtains, by utilizing the Galerkin orthogonality (
15
), the weak form
(
)–(
4
) of the Oseen problem, and integration by parts

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{spg}}\left((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v},q)\right)

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{spg}}\left((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}),\left(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{v},q-I_{h}^{Q}q\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\boldsymbol{r}_{K}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}\right),\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{K}-\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h},q-I_{h}^{Q}q\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\boldsymbol{r}_{F}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}\right),\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{F}

\displaystyle+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\boldsymbol{r}_{K}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h}\right),\delta_{K}\left((\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla)\left(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{v}\right)+\nabla\left(q-I_{h}^{Q}q\right)\right)\right)_{K}

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mu_{K}\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\nabla\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{v}\right)\right)_{K},

for all
(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\boldsymbol{V}\times Q
.

In the same spirit as in
[
19
]
, some hypotheses on the relation of certain norms of the interpolation
errors and the error in the norm
\left\|(\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}
are formulated now. The rationale behind these
hypotheses is that the (unknown) interpolation
(\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u},I_{h}^{Q}p)
is usually considered to be a very good approximation
of the solution
(\boldsymbol{u},p)
of (
)–(
4
) and consequently that interpolation errors are usually (much) smaller than
errors committed by the solution
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})
of the discrete problem (
6
)–(
8
).

Hypothesis 4

(Relations of the interpolation error and the error in the norm
\left\|(\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg},p}
)
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{u},p)
the solution of (
)–(
4
) and
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})
be the solution of the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div problem (
6
)–(
8
).
Assume that
(\boldsymbol{u},p)
is sufficiently smooth, at least
\boldsymbol{u}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(\Omega)
and
p\in H^{k}(\Omega)
.

First, it will be assumed that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{K}^{-1}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq 2\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}.

(20)

The discussion of this hypothesis and all the followings is based on the special case of a family of uniform triangulations.
Consider on the one hand the optimal choices of
\delta_{K}
and the corresponding orders of convergence
discussed in Remark
1
and, on the other hand, the interpolation estimate (
13
)
for
s=0
and smooth functions. Then one finds that both sides of (
20
) are of the same order.
Thus, the actual hypothesis is the factor
2
on the right-hand side.

An assumption of a similar spirit as (
20
) is

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{K}^{\alpha}\left\|\nabla\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq 2\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}

(21)

with

\alpha=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }\nu<h\mbox{ and equal order spaces are used,}\\
0&\mbox{else.}\end{cases}

(22)

Comparing here the asymptotics of both sides, then one finds again that the orders are the same.

The third assumption reads as follows

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(F)}^{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\leq 2\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}.

(23)

With the trace inequality (
14
) and the convergence orders discussed in Remark
1
, it
can be shown that term on the left-hand side of (
23
) is of order
2k+1
, whereas the
term on the right-hand side is of order
2k
or
2k+1
, depending on the concrete situation, compare Remark
1
.
Concerning this assumption, notice that the corresponding hypothesis in
[
19
]
contains the factor

\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(F)}
on the left-hand side. However, since the convection field appears quadratically on
the right-hand side, we think it more appropriate from the scaling point of view that it appears quadratically
on the left-hand side, too.

Next, it will be assumed that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{K}\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla\right)\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u}\right)+\nabla\left(p-I_{h}^{Q}p\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq 2\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}.

(24)

In the case of inf-sup stable pairs of finite element spaces with order
k
for the velocity and
k-1
for the pressure,
both sides of (
24
) are of the same order
2k
if the optimal asymptotic choice (
11
) of
\delta_{K}

is used. Notice that both interpolation operators can be chosen independently, so that one can consider the order
of the interpolation errors for both terms on the left-hand side separately. Performing the same considerations for
equal order pairs of spaces and the corresponding optimal asymptotic choice (
12
) of
\delta_{K}
,
one can see that both sides of (
24
) are also of the same order, provided that
p\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
.

Additionally, an assumption for the interpolation error of the pressure is needed. Since the pressure error does not
appear as a separate term in the error measured in the norm
\left\|(\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}
, the pressure interpolation
cannot be related directly to the error in this norm. Instead, the pressure interpolation will be related to the velocity
interpolation. Consider first the case of equal order finite element spaces and assume that
p\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
.
In this case, both interpolation operators map to a finite element space with the same polynomials. On the one hand,
the interpolation operators are not identical, since
\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}
has to obey Dirichlet boundary conditions and
if
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
then
I_{h}^{Q}
has to map to a function with vanishing integral mean value. But on the other hand,
it can be expected that generally the velocity interpolation error is not much worse than the pressure interpolation
error. Thus, it will be assumed that

\left\|p-I_{h}^{Q}p\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 2\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(25)

Of course, there are special situations, e.g., if
\boldsymbol{u}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h},p\not\in Q_{h}
, where this assumption does not hold.
Inserting (
25
) in (
20
) leads to

\left\|p-I_{h}^{Q}p\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 4\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{\delta_{K}\}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{K}^{-1}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq 8\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{\delta_{K}\}\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}.

(26)

In the case of inf-sup stable pairs of spaces, the pressure interpolator maps to a finite element space with polynomials
of one degree less than contained in the velocity finite element space. Taking this issue into account and assuming
again that the velocity interpolation error is not much worse than the pressure interpolation error leads to the
hypotheses

\left\|p-I_{h}^{Q}p\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 2h^{-1}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(27)

which gives with (
20
)

\left\|p-I_{h}^{Q}p\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{8}{h^{2}}\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{\delta_{K}\}\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}.

(28)

The hypotheses (
25
) and (
27
) are based on the assumption that the,
usually unknown, velocity and pressure components of the solution are of a similar complexity. If it is known
a priori that this is not the case, then the constants on the right-hand sides of (
25
) and (
27
)
could be adapted, one direction or the other.

Theorem 5

Proof.

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{10d\mu_{K}^{2}}{\delta_{K}^{\alpha}}\left\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\frac{1}{40}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{K}^{\alpha}\left\|\nabla\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{10d\mu_{K}^{2}}{\delta_{K}^{\alpha}}\left\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\frac{1}{20}\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}.

Alternatively, one can use (
30
), the estimate of the divergence by the gradient and
the interpolation property (
13
) to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mu_{K}\left(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\nabla\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{u}\right)\right)_{K}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mu_{K}\left\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\nabla\cdot\left((\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h})-\boldsymbol{I}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{V}}\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}Cd^{1/2}\mu_{K}\left\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{Cd\mu_{K}^{2}}{\nu}\left\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\frac{1}{20}\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}^{2}.

Inserting the derived bounds for the terms on the right-hand side of (
31
) in this estimate gives
the statement of the theorem.
∎

Remark 6

(On the last two terms on the right-hand side of (
29
))
.

The last two terms on the right-hand side of (
29
) are of the same form as in the corresponding error bound for the
scalar convection-diffusion-reaction problem presented in
[
19
]
. These terms are discussed in

[
19
, Remark 3]
. Here, this discussion shall not be repeated and it is just notices that these terms can be expected
to be small in the convection-dominated regime, which comes essentially from the factor
\nu^{2}
,
compare also Figure
5
below. For the
diffusion-dominated regime, one can apply other techniques than in the present paper to derive global a posteriori
error bounds that do not lead to terms of this form.

Remark 7

(On adaptive mesh refinement)
.

As already mentioned in the introduction, a second goal of utilizing a posteriori error estimators,
besides providing information about global errors, consists of controlling an adaptive mesh refinement.
To support the use of an a posteriori error estimator from the point of view of numerical analysis, local
estimates are derived so that the local error is bounded by a local contribution of the error estimator.
In the case of residual-based estimators, one applies cut-off functions, so-called bubble functions,
to derive the local estimate, see
[
26
]
. The derivation is usually quite technical and very
lengthy, compare
[
19
, Section 4]
for the case of convection-dominated convection-diffusion-reaction
problems. Since the main goal of the current paper consists of proposing a robust estimator for the
error
\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}
, as well as for the sake of brevity, we decided not to
present a local error analysis. The numerical studies in Section
4.2
will show that
with the proposed estimator properly refined grids are obtained.

4.3
Summary of the Numerical Studies

This section studied two examples with different properties and setup:

\bullet

Smooth solution with
\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}
independent of
\nu
vs.

\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p-p_{h}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}

5
Some Considerations on the Navier–Stokes Equations

This section presents a numerical study that investigates the behavior of a modified a posteriori error
estimator applied to the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div method of the incompressible Navier–Stokes problem.

The Oseen problem (
)–(
4
) is a popular model problem for the numerical
analysis of convection-dominated incompressible flow problems since it is a linear problem. The actual
model for steady-state incompressible flows is the set of the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations
given, already in weak form, by:
Find
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\boldsymbol{V}\times Q
such that

\displaystyle\nu(\nabla\boldsymbol{u},\nabla\boldsymbol{v})+((\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})-(p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v})+(\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{v})_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}\quad\forall\;\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{V},

(36)

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u},q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\forall\;q\in Q.

(37)

An a priori error analysis of a stabilized SUPG/PSPG/grad-div finite element method for
problem (
36
)–(
37
) is presented in
[
25
]
. This analysis does not
require that the velocity and pressure finite element spaces satisfy a discrete inf-sup condition.
It is proposed to choose the stabilization parameters for all pairs of spaces to be

\delta_{K}\sim h_{K}^{2}
and
\mu_{K}\sim 1
. While these are the standard asymptotic choices for
inf-sup stable pairs of finite element spaces for the Oseen problem, compare (
11
), they correspond
for equal order pairs to a recommendation in the situation where the problem is not strongly
convection-dominated, see (
12
).
In
[
25
]
, the existence and uniqueness of a solution to the
discrete problem is proved for small data (or large viscosity coefficients). Then, an a priori
error analysis is performed for the norm

\left(\left\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\right\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|q\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{K}\left\|\nabla q\right\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(38)

where here the case of continuous pressure finite element spaces is considered.
Comparing this norm with
\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},q\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}
defined in (
9
), one can see
first that (
38
) does not contain the viscosity coefficient. The motivation in
[
25
]
is that the
case
\nu\to 0
is not of interest for simulations of the steady-state Navier–Stokes equations, since the
solution is not unique for small viscosity coefficients. Second, the grad-div term is missing
in (
38
). And last, the convective term of the SUPG stabilization is missing in
(
38
), since including this term with
\boldsymbol{b}
replaced by
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
would not define a norm.
The a priori error analysis presented in
[
25
]
leads for smooth solutions and

P_{k}/P_{k-1}
,
k\geq 2
, and
P_{k}/P_{k}
,
k\geq 1
,
to the order of convergence
k
in the norm (
38
).

Having an a posteriori error estimator for the SUPG/PSPG/grad-div method for the Oseen problem at hand,
a natural question is how it performs, after appropriate modifications, for the same discretization
of the Navier–Stokes problem (
36
)–(
37
). These modifications comprise:

\bullet

to define the mesh cell residual by

\boldsymbol{r}_{K}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})=\left.(\boldsymbol{f}+\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\nabla p_{h})\right|_{K}\quad\forall\;K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

\bullet

to take
\sigma_{0}=0
in
\eta_{K,\mathrm{res}}
and
\eta_{F}
, so that the corresponding terms
can be removed from the minimum,

\bullet

to replace
\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(F)}
in
\eta_{F}
by
\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(F)}
.

Since the error estimate with this estimator will potentially depend on the value of the
viscosity coefficient and on the contribution from the grad-div term, we think that (
38
)
is not an appropriate norm to compare the a posteriori estimates with. The definition of such a norm
should resemble the norms
\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},q\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg}}
or
\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},q\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg},p}
as much as possible
and we think that an appropriate norm is

\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{v},q\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{spg,nse}}=\left(\nu\left\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\right\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\nu\left\|q\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mu_{K}\left\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\delta_{K}\left\|\nabla q\right\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The numerical studies considered the Navier–Stokes problem with the right-hand side and the Dirichlet
boundary condition prescribed such that (
34
)–(
35
) is the solution of (
36
)–(
37
).

As can be seen in (
12
), there is not a unique optimal asymptotic choice of the grad-div
stabilization parameter for equal order pairs of spaces if the problem is not strongly dominated by
convection. We observed in our numerical studies that with
\mu=0.5h_{K}
one obtains notably more
accurate solutions than with
\mu=0.5
, while both choices fit into (
12
). For this
reason and the for sake of brevity, here only the former results will be presented. To be concrete,
the SUPG stabilization parameter was chosen in all simulations to be
\delta_{K}=0.5h_{K}^{2}
and the grad-div
parameter to be
\mu=0.5
for inf-sup stable pairs of spaces and
\mu=0.5h_{K}
for equal order
pairs.

The SUPG/PSPG/grad-div method for the Navier–Stokes equations (
36
)–(
37
) has in principle the
form (
6
)–(
8
) with the obvious modification of the convective term. For the velocity
field in the SUPG test function, always the current iterate of a Picard iteration to solve the
nonlinear problem was taken.
