An interpretable attention-based TabTransformer framework with feature fusion for green architecture classification
Authors: Zhao Yiyuan, Li Chang, Li Xintong, Xu Xiaochen, Shen Na
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, we propose a deep learning-based transformer framework for the automated classification of green vs non-green architecture to analyze environmental effects. The environmental significance of this research work lies in enhancing sustainability by enabling scalable, data-driven assessments of building projects which minimize the reliance on costly and manual certification methods. To address the limitations of existing approaches, we introduce EcoArch- TabFusionNet, a Tab transformer based Fusion Network that fuses grouped architectural, energy, and contextual attributes through a specialized attention-based TabTransformer architecture. The model integrates key feature domains based on Energy, Eco-Tech, Design, and Context while employing dimensionality reduction using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to minimize redundancy and enhance generalization. The framework is further integrated with explainable AI (XAI) components, including attention heatmaps, LIME, and SHAP, to ensure transparency in feature contributions and decision logic. Proposed model performs late fusion classification across refined feature groups and achieves superior results accuracy of 97.5%, compared to four tabular transformer baselines.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Sustainable environment, Deep learning, Data acquisition, Green design, Expert systemsSubject terms: Engineering, Environmental sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The growing global emphasis on environmental sustainability has placed architecture and urban development under greater scrutiny. As climate change accelerates and natural resources dwindle, the need for green architectural design has become more urgent than ever1. Green buildings, characterized by their efficient use of energy, materials, water, and their reduced ecological footprint, are considered vital in meeting global carbon neutrality goals2. However, identifying whether a building adheres to green principles is not straightforward. Traditional certification systems like LEED or BREEAM involve manual audits, which are resource-intensive, time-consuming, and often limited in scalability3. Therefore, a more efficient and automated way to classify architectural designs as green or non-green based on their feature learning with co-occurrence, is necessary to support large-scale sustainability efforts4.

In response to these challenges, artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a promising tool in the field of environmental design assessment5. Particularly, machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) methods have shown substantial potential in modeling building performance, optimizing design, and forecasting energy consumption6. Prior works have applied various ML techniques, including Random Forests, Support Vector Machines, and Gradient Boosting, to predict energy efficiency, ventilation needs, or carbon emissions. More recently, deep learning models, especially transformer-based architecture, have been adopted for their ability to learn complex feature interactions. Despite this, very few studies have focused on directly classifying architectural projects into green and non-green categories using a diverse set of design, environmental, and contextual features in a unified model.

To bridge this gap, this study introduces a novel model called EcoArch-TabFusionNet, which integrates spatial attention mechanisms with grouped feature learning to improve the classification of green versus non-green architecture. The model is designed to capture the complex relationships between key feature categories through targeted attention heads with fusion networks and deep layered learning. The architecture is evaluated against multiple deep learning baselines for tabular data. By integrating domain-specific groupings and interpretability mechanisms, the proposed model not only achieves high classification performance but also enhances the explainability of its decisions.

Data description

The data sourced from online platform Kaggle which gathered from multiple verified architectural and sustainability information sources such as municipal energy reports, green certification audits, and building performance registries. It contains 40,000 examples of buildings, which are categorized to be Green or Non-Green and are described in 20 features that contain the energy usage, environmentally friendly installations, architectural design features, and the surrounding environment. Preprocessing the dataset is performed before normalization, categorical encoding, elimination of outliers and imputation of missing values are done to ensure the consistency of the modeling. Table 1 provides the nature of features that will be used in this research. There are 20 variables in the dataset comprising 18 input variables, one identifier (Building ID) and one target variable (Environmental Design). The target variable Environmental Design is directly presented in the dataset, and it indicates the degree of sustainability of every building. It is the label of domain-specific characteristics that will be aggregated under the Energy, Eco-Tech, Design, and Context and such characteristics as energy efficiency, existence of eco-technologies (e.g., solar systems, green roofs), material usage, and environmental context. Buildings that meet sustainability-directed requirements in each of these feature groups are termed as being green whereas others are classified as being non-green.

Data preprocessing

The preprocessing pipeline was to provide consistency of data, compatibility of models and strong learning. First, all the categorical attributes were standardized into uniform string formats to eliminate formatting errors. Numerical characteristics such as energy usage, carbon emissions, date constructed, structural ratios were confirmed and converted to the right numeric type. The non-numerical variables were processed with the help of a mean imputation strategy, whereas the categorical variables were addressed with the help of a mode imputation strategy, which did not leave any blank spaces in the dataset and did not produce bias.

After that categorical variables were turned with the help of One-Hot Encoding which allowed the transformer-based architecture and the target variable Environmental Design was turned to binary label encoding (Green = 1, non-Green = 0). To make the model training stable, the features like window-to-wall ratio, the surrounding green space, energy consumption per square foot, and carbon emissions were normalized through the Min–Max scaling to the [0, 1] interval. Interquartile Range (IQR) was used to determine the outliers of the numerical features, and outliers were filtered or capped to reduce their effect on the model learning process.

After preprocessing, the dimensionality reduction of PCA followed the features grouping and fusion, reducing the redundancy but not the significant variation of the data. Also, to counter slight imbalance of classes, weights on classes were automatically calculated and applied to the loss term, which resulted in equitable learning on both green and non-green groups. Such an organized preprocessing pipeline also makes the data quality sound, filters the noise, and prepares the data adequately to the proposed TabFusionNet framework.

Feature selection

Analysis of the input variables, which were used in the determination of the most significant predictors of building sustainability, using Information Gain (IG) analysis of all the input variables used. It is a statistical method which quantifies the degree of entropy decrease of every characteristic with respect to the desired category to enable a person to select the features with the highest discriminative strength in an information-driven way. The proposed model was modelled via a systematic feature-selection strategy that ensured that no more than most informative and sustainability-relevant contributions were incorporated into the proposed model33. The data had all its variables categorized into four semantically consistent categories to denote the various dimensions of the green building performance. Energy characteristics were the efficiency of operations, whereas Eco-Tech characteristics were the high-technology sustainability solutions, e.g. smart systems and eco-materials34. The thermal and resource performance are influenced by encoded design features of the spatial distribution, the type of facade as well as material settings. The environment surroundings were the context characteristics that involved the climate conditions and socio-behavioral features that were linked with the occupant’s well-being. Using IG, we were able to determine the contribution of each feature to the classification between green and non-green buildings.

IG-based feature selection strategy was used to guarantee feature relevance as well as dimensionality reduction. First, IG values of all input features were calculated against the target variable with the high IG values reflecting more contribution to entropy reduction and class discrimination. Following these scores, the features were ordered by their rank. A selection criterion was then used by keeping those features whose IG values were more than the mean IG value of all features thereby making sure that only the most informative features were retained whilst the rest were eliminated.

To eliminate leakage of data and provide a strong and unbiased assessment, the complete preprocessing and feature selection process was limited to the training sample in every cross-validation fold. Precisely, only the training part of each fold was used to compute IG scores and the feature subset corresponding to the subset was always used on the validation/test split. Moreover, all the preprocessing actions such as normalization and PCA were only fitted on the training data and then applied to the test data. An audit on data leakage was also done to ensure that the information on the validation/test sets was not utilized at any point in the feature selection or preprocessing. This stringent process will make sure that the model assessment is fair, reliable and information leakage free.

Consequently, the features that have been retained are more or less major domain features like Energy efficiency indicators, Eco-technology adoption, and critical Design and Context, whereas the redundant or informative characteristics were discarded. This ethical choice procedure optimizes the model generalization, minimizes overfitting and demands the interpretability of the ultimate forecasting structure.

Deep learning models

In this study, four state of the art deep learning networks on tabular data were used as baseline models. SAINT builds on row column attention and contrastive pretraining to learn intricate inter-feature interactions, which makes it effective to structured data47. TabNet uses sequential systems of attention with the help of which the model learns sparse and hierarchical representations, focusing on the most informative features in each step of a decision35. FT-Transformer adapts classic transformer block to tabular inputs with the help of feature tokenization and positional embeddings, which enables it to capture long-range interactions in the case of heterogeneous attributes. TabTransformer, however, uses self-attention specifically on categorical variables, which allows rich, context-sensitive embedding without destroying the underlying tabular formatting36. All these models give a solid comparative basis on gauging the effectiveness of proposed architecture.

To be able to make a fair and architecture-sensitive comparison, each of the baseline models was tuned based on model-specific hyperparameters instead of imposing homogeneous settings. In FT-Transformer and TabTransformer (Simple), a standard transformer-based setup was used with an embedding dimension of 64, 4 attention heads, and 2 transformer layers which were trained with the AdamW optimizer using a learning rate of 1e-4, dropout rate of 0.3, GELU activation, and cosine annealed learning rate scheduling. Equally, the SAINT and TabNet model was set with similar transformer settings and similar parameters of 64-dimensional embeddings, 4 attention heads, and 2 layers with AdamW to optimize and proper regularization via dropout and weight decay. All the models were trained through the same evaluation framework with early stopping and the number of training epochs varied with convergence behavior (27 epochs in case of transformer-based models with early stopping). The architecture-specific tuning will guarantee that every baseline runs in the most appropriate conditions so that we can make an adequate and trustworthy comparison of the proposed TabFusionNet model.

All these models received the same settings to offer a fair comparison with the proposed model that builds upon TabTransformer with the addition of the multi-branch feature encoding, attention-based fusion, and context-adaptive recalibration to all four feature groups. It is a cohesive architecture that has the capability of sharing energy, eco-tech, design, and contextual features to model sustainability patterns in a more effective way, as working flow shown in Fig. 4. The originality of model is that there is a feature-conscious selective attention architecture, explicitly representing the heterogeneity of sustainability indicators37. PCA is applied as a group-wise dimensionality reduction step prior to token embedding, as a pre-attention projection mechanism. This step ensures that the subsequent TabTransformer blocks receive low-dimensional, de-correlated, and informative representations, which is especially important given the heterogeneity of environmental design features38. As opposed to classical tabular transformers, which treat all features equally, the model discussed organizes predictors into groups that are interpretable, then apply specific transformer encoders to every group, and fuses them through selective attention fusion. Such a mechanism enhances sustainability cues of high impact and dampens noise due to low-relevance characteristics49. Moreover, proposed model incorporates a reliability-based calibration component, which makes it possible to obtain more credible probability approximations to make real-life decisions in green architecture evaluation.

As opposed to the default TabTransformer that mainly involves the use of a basic transformer encoder on tabular tokens, the suggested model is characterized by sustainability-conscious fusion attention and contextual token embedding that are directly relevant to the environmental setting. Through the addition of grouped tokenization, depending on sustainability feature categories, localized learning is possible in semantically related feature spaces. PCA is independently applied to a feature group based on the presence of numerical features in a group. The principal components are determined by preserving 95 percent of the variance in each group hence different component variables per group. PCA is only fitted on the training set and the transformation learned applied to the validation and test sets to avoid the leakage of data. The encoded feature vectors48 are then mapped into a 64-dimensional embedding space which is fixed with a fully connected layer. Every group-level embedding is viewed as a token, and a sequence of four tokens is generated in response to the four groups of features, and then the TabTransformer encoder is applied to them. Moreover, the model combines the dropout mechanism, dense fusion layers and attention aggregation elements at the decision step that the original TabTransformer does not have and together they improve generalization, interpretability, and convergence. This is what makes the suggested framework capture domain-specific patterns that are important in terms of differentiating hence making it a solid and environmentally contextual deep learning model.

The choice of hyperparameters and setup used in the proposed model were informed by the empirical tuning and hyperparameter guides recommended by the best practice in deep learning on tabular classification and shown in Table 11. To maintain the stability but efficient convergence, a learning rate of 0.0001 with AdamW optimizer was selected and since a moderate batch size of 64 was selected to balance between the stability of gradient estimates and memory efficiency. Regularization strategies that were used to reduce overfitting were weight decay (0.01) and a dropout rate of 0.3. An early patient stopping of 5 epochs was incorporated to terminate training at a point in which no progress was made in validation loss. The choice training epochs was based on initial convergence tests, and the validation performance was found to stabilize after the 25th epoch in most models, and additional marginal gains were obtained afterwards. To avoid overfitting and to have an efficient training process, we added early stopping at a patience of 5 epochs- this is so because it allows the model to terminate training once there was no improvement in the validation process in 5 consecutive epochs. At epoch 27 we achieved maximum results. This system was useful in balancing exploration and regularization and maintaining convergence behavior. Furthermore, the Cosine Annealing Learning Rate Scheduler was also useful in converging the model more stably in the later stages of fine-tuning because it decreased the learning rate progressively, allowing it to fine tune the objective at the eventual stage. The regularization mechanisms used are the weight decay (0.01) and dropout rate (0.3), which were used to protect longer training. The choice of a binary cross-entropy loss function was achieved because the classification problem was binary (green vs. non-green). All the experiments were run using the constant random seed (42) to ensure reproducibility and to avoid data leakage, all the transformations were applied to the training data split only. The dataset was partitioned using an 80–20 holdout strategy, where 80% of the data was used for training and 20% for testing. The training set was further divided into training and validation subsets to support hyperparameter tuning and early stopping. This environment, combined with the validation tracking, proved that the leakage of data has been avoided with particular attention by applying preprocessing transformations to the training set only.

The model has always given attention to ecologically significant features that have contributed to high confidence in applicability of the model on sustainability conscious architectural evaluation, planning and decision making. Compared to this kind of performance, they highlight the importance of organized multi-feature fusion, which is the integration of energy indicators, eco-technology features, architectural design hints, and contextual environmental influences, to extract the rich semantics of green and non-green architecture.

Although the suggested TabFusionNet model proves to be very useful in terms of performance and interpretation of the results in terms of sustainability-based classification, several limitations can be noted. To begin with, the model is based on the predetermined feature grouping that implies some dependability of the domains and possibly needs professional knowledge to be adapted to other datasets or domains. Second, the incorporation of transformer-based elements and attention mechanisms adds complexity to computations and raises the training cost, which does not make the model resource-constrained. Third, even though the PCA enhances alignment of representation, it can result in the loss of information unless it is configured cautiously especially when dealing with features whose difference is slight yet significant. Also, the model has been tested on a definite dataset, and its ability to be generalized on a wide range of real-world building datasets needs to be re-tested. Lastly, SHAP and LIME are useful in the sense that they give insights into interpretability but are used post hoc and may not be able to identify all the internal decision dynamics of the model. The next round of work will aim at minimizing the computational load, automated feature grouping, and testing the framework on bigger and more varied data sets to make it more robust and generalized.

In the comparative study, which is carried out in Table 13, the excellent performance of the proposed model is established against some of the newest methods in various methodological paradigms. Even though other models such as XGBoost + SVR14 and Extreme Gradient Boosting7, 8 had higher levels of precision up to 91%, they depended either on engineered features or restricted representations limiting generalization. Similarly, deep models, such as DNN + MOPSO and Faster R-CNN + SVM, were at 85.88 percent accuracy, and they did not introduce any additional advanced feature attention or interpretability layers. On the other hand, the four engineered property combinations, dimensionality reduction using PCA method, and sustainability-aware fusion mechanisms in the proposed model led to the highest accuracy of 97.52 improving.

Conclusion

This paper presented an interpretable deep learning architecture called EcoArch-TabFusionNet, which is a deep learning model that is trained to distinguish between Green and Non-Green buildings by the systematic fusion of Energy, Eco-Tech, Design, and Context features. The experimental findings also indicate that the proposed model performs well in the tested dataset, with an accuracy of 97.5% and ROC-AUC of 99.8, which is better than FT-Transformer, SAINT, TabNet, and standard TabTransformer baselines. Moreover, the K-fold cross-validation analysis is used to ensure that the model is stable and robust, and the results show the same performance across the folds and low variance, which means that the model is applicable to the whole dataset. The ablation analysis also suggests that all the feature groupings are useful in the prediction task, though complete feature fusion has the most consistent outcome. The use of explainable AI models, such as SHAP and LIME, also gives new insight into the behavior of a model, as the attributes related to sustainability, including energy efficiency, carbon emissions, and eco-technological features, are highlighted. These results indicate that the model can be trained to acquire meaningful patterns that are consistent with the indicators of sustainability in the analyzed data. It should be noted though that the assessment is done on one dataset under controlled experimental conditions. Thus, although the findings are well moderate, the applicability of the suggested framework to different real-life contexts is to be validated. Further research will be aimed at testing the model on a variety of datasets, with multimodal data sources (satellite imagery, BIM models, etc.) and testing the model in different regional and environmental conditions. Such extensions will be necessary in order to determine the strength and practical relevance of the framework in more general sustainability assessment situations.
