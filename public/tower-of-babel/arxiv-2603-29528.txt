Abstract
. We study the model theory of deeply ramified fields of positive characteristic. Generalizing the perfect case treated in work by Jahnke and Kartas on the model theory of perfectoid fields, we obtain Ax–Kochen/Ershov principles for certain deeply ramified fields of positive characteristic and fixed degree of imperfection. Our results apply in particular to all deeply ramified henselian valued fields of rank 1 1 .

2.
Deeply ramified and almost separably tame fields

At the core of the main theorem of this paper are two properties of valued
fields: being deeply ramified and almost separably tame. In this section, we will
exhibit an elementary class of fields with these properties and argue that this
is a reasonable class to work in.

2.1.
Separably tame valued fields

The theory of (separably) tame valued fields is a key recent development in
the model theory of valued fields. Pioneered by Kuhlmann
[
17
]
and
Kuhlmann and Pal
[
15
]
, the class of (separably) tame valued fields is one
where a wide variety of AKE principles hold for (separably) tame valued fields
of finite degree of imperfection. Recently, Anscombe expanded their work to
include the case of infinite degree of imperfection
[
3
]
. We give a brief
explanation of the model-theoretic results for separably tame valued fields as
we will need them later on.

Definition 2.1.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field of residue characteristic
p>0

and
(L,w)/(K,v)
a finite field extension. This extension is called tame
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

p\nmid(wL:vK)
;

(2)

Lw/Kv
is separable;

(3)

the extension is defectless.

A valued field is then called separably tame if all separable finite field
extensions are tame. Trivially valued fields are always separably tame. For
non-trivially valued fields, the property of being separably tame is
equivalent to the following properties:

(1)

vK
is
p
-divisible;

(2)

Kv
is perfect;

(3)

(K,v)
is separable-algebraically maximal.

The main result in the theory of separably tame valued fields are their AKE
principles
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
. The infinite degree of
imperfection case was proven in
[
3
, Theorem 4.22(II) and Theorem 4.24]
.

Theorem 2.2
(Kuhlmann for
e=0
, Kuhlmann–Pal for
e<\infty
, Anscombe for
e=\infty
)
.

Let
(L,w),(L^{\prime},w^{\prime})
be two separably tame valued fields of positive
characteristic such that
L,L^{\prime}
have the same degree of imperfection
e\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

(L,w)\equiv(L^{\prime},w^{\prime})
;

(2)

wL\equiv w^{\prime}L^{\prime}
and
Lw\equiv L^{\prime}w^{\prime}
.

Moreover, assume that
(L,w)
and
(L^{\prime},w^{\prime})
share a common separably
tame subfield
(K,v)
with
L/K
and
L^{\prime}/K
both separable extensions
such that
wL/vK
is torsion-free and, if
e=\infty
, assume further that
the relative imperfection degrees of
L/K
coincides with that of
L^{\prime}/K
.
Then we get equivalences between the following statements:

(1)

(L,w)\equiv_{(K,v)}(L^{\prime},w^{\prime})
;

(2)

wL\equiv_{vK}w^{\prime}L^{\prime}
and
Lw\equiv_{Kv}L^{\prime}w^{\prime}

2.2.
Deeply ramified valued fields

Deeply ramified valued fields have been extensively studied in the context of
almost mathematics
[
12

and more recently, the model theory of valued fields
[
16
,
5

13

7

Definition 2.3.

Let
(K,v)
be a valued field of characteristic
p
. It is called deeply
ramified if one of the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

\Omega_{\mathcal{O}_{v^{\text{sep}}}/\mathcal{O}_{v}}=0
;

(2)

the completion
\hat{K}
is perfect;

(3)

K
is dense in its perfect hull;

(4)

for all
x\in K
with
0<vx
, the ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(x)
is
semi-perfect.

Proof.

The equivalence of the first three statements is standard and can be
found in
[
16
]
.

We prove (iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv).

For the left to right implication, there is an isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{v}/(x)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\hat{v}}/(x)
. Therefore if the Frobenius map is
surjective on
\hat{K}
(and hence
\mathcal{O}_{\hat{v}}
), then it is also
surjective on
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(x)
.

For the right to left implication, consider a basic closed ball around

\sqrt[p^{r}]{a}
for
a\in K
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
of radius

\varepsilon
:

B=\{b\in K|v(x-\sqrt[p^{r}]{a})\geq\varepsilon\}.

Take
x\in K
with
v(x)\geq p^{r}\cdot\varepsilon
. Then

\mathcal{O}_{v}/(x)
is semi-perfect so there is a
b\in K
with

v(b^{p}-a)\geq v(x)
. A simple verification shows that
b\in B
:

\displaystyle v(b-\sqrt[p^{r}]{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{r}}v\left(b^{p^{r}}-a\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p^{r}\cdot\varepsilon}{p^{r}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon.

∎

2.3.
Unramified extensions

In the literature, there are multiple different definitions of unramified
extensions. Here, unramified extensions are defectless and must induce a
separable extension of the residue field.

Definition 2.4.

A finite extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
of henselian valued fields is
unramified if
f(v^{\prime}/v)=[K^{\prime}:K]
and
K^{\prime}v^{\prime}/Kv
is separable.

There are a variety of equivalent definitions of unramified extensions. The ones
we will use are expressed using the different: a quantity associated to an
element of an algebraic field extension similar to the discriminant.

Definition 2.5.

Let
K
be a field and
a\in\overline{K}
an element of the algebraic
closure. Write
m_{a}(x)
for its minimal polynomial over
K
. We write

\delta(a)=m_{a}^{\prime}(a)
for the different of
a
. Factoring
m_{a}(x)

as

m_{a}(x)=\prod_{i}(x-a_{i})

in
\overline{K}
with
a_{0}=a
, using the product rule we can also write the different as

\delta(a)=\prod_{i>0}(a_{i}-a).

Lemma 2.6.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field and
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
a finite
extension. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
is unramified;

(2)

there exists an

such that
\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{v}[a]

and
\delta(a)\in\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
;

(3)

there exists an
a\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}
such that
K^{\prime}=K(a)
and

\delta(a)\in\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
;

(4)

there exists an
f\in K[X]
such that

\discr(f)\in\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
and
K^{\prime}=K[X]/(f)
;

(5)

\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/\mathcal{O}_{v}
is étale.

Proof.

See
[
13
, Fact 2.4.7]
.

2.4.

The concept of almost separably tame valuations is one of the main tools we
use to demonstrate the AKE principle.
These allow us to split up valuations into different
relatively nice parts that we can understand separately. Under certain

Let

tame valuation and therefore separably tame itself.

By
[

, the valued field

\mathbb{Z}
or

(Kv_{t^{+}},\overline{v_{t^{-}}})

must be

.

This means that
(Kv_{t^{+}},\overline{v_{t^{-}}})
has

p

∎

If we restrict to

p
case of

[

]
. As pointed out before, there pointed valued fields are studied
which are also deeply ramified and henselian and almost tame, but the involved

We now give an example of a valued field which is not separably tame, but is
contained in
\mathcal{C}
.

Example 2.13
(Pointed out to us by Philip Dittmann)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. The pointed valued field

({\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}},v,t)
is in
\mathcal{C}
. It is deeply ramified
and rank
1
, so the coarsening
\mathcal{O}_{v_{t^{+}}}
is the trivial
valuation ring which is defectless. The field itself however is not
separably tame.

We claim that separable extension

\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)(\sqrt{t})\left(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}\right)
is
linearly disjoint from
{\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}}
. Then the composite extension

\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)(\sqrt{t})\left(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}\right).{\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}}/{\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}}

must have defect: both the value group and residue field of

have no degree
p
extension. The value

\frac{1}{p^{\infty}}\mathbb{Z}
which is
p
-divisible, and the
residue field
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{\text{AS}}
has no degree
p
-extensions either.

Now to show linear independence we use some basic Galois theory. In this
case, it is sufficient to show that

\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)(\sqrt{t})\left(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}\right)\cap{\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}}

is
\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
. Therefore, we assume that the intersection is a
non-trivial intermediate extension as in the diagram below.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)(\sqrt{t})\left(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}\right).{\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)(\sqrt{t})\left(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}

The field extension
L/\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
must have degree
p
:

{\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
is a tower of degree

p
extensions and
\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)(\sqrt{t})(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}})/\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)

has degree
2p
. This means that
L/\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
has degree

p
. It is also a Galois extension because it is the intersection of two
Galois extensions.

The Galois group of this extension is non-commutative. Consider
generators of the Galois group which act in the following manner:

\displaystyle\sigma:\sqrt{t}

\displaystyle\mapsto-\sqrt{t},

\displaystyle\tau:\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}+1.

We know that
\sigma(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}})=-\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}

because
\sigma(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}})
must be an Artin-Schreier root of

\sigma(\sqrt{t})=-\sqrt{t}
and the sum of Artin-Schreier roots of
a,b

is an Artin-Schreier root of
a+b
. Therefore

\sigma(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}})+\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}
is an Artin-Schreier
root of
0
and hence

\sigma(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}})=-\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}+n
for some

. A simple computation then gives

\displaystyle\sigma(\tau(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}))

\displaystyle=\sigma(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}+1)

\displaystyle=-\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}+n+1

\displaystyle\tau(\sigma(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}))

\displaystyle=\tau(-\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}})

\displaystyle=-\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}-(n+1).

The Galois group

\Gal\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)(\sqrt{t})(\rho_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}})/\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)\right)

must then be the dihedral group

of order
2p
, as it is the only
non-commutative group of order
2p
.
This group has no normal subgroup of
order
2
, so by the fundamental theorem of Galois theory the non-trivial
intermediate field

cannot exist.

This demonstrates that
{\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}}
with the

t
-adic valuation is not separably defectless.

Example 2.14.

There also exist fields which are not almost separably tame. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a
non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and

an odd prime. We claim that

on
\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)

It suffices to show that for any

Now, let

Because

the degree

Something even stronger is true for the above valued field: no elementary
extension is almost separably tame.

Example 2.15.

No elementary extension of

is
almost separably tame.

We may assume that the elementary extensions are ultrapowers: by the
Keisler-Shelah theorem, for any elementarily equivalent valued field

(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
we can find an ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
on some set such that

(K,v)^{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}\cong(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})^{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}}
. Then, if
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
did contain a parameter

t^{\prime}
such that
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
, by elementarity, the ultraproduct
would be in
\mathcal{C}_{p}
as well.

Take
(x_{i})_{i}\in\mathfrak{M}_{v^{\prime}}
. Then for all

x_{i}\in\mathfrak{M}_{v}
, by the previous example we can find a
separable extension of degree
p
which has defect with respect to the
coarsening
v_{x_{i}}^{+}
. For a fixed (or bounded) degree, this is an
elementary property and by Los’ theorem, the element

must
have the same property.

The class
\mathcal{C}_{p}
is surprisingly large since being deeply ramified passes down
from (non-trivial) coarsenings in positive characteristic, so
many
fields are automatically deeply ramified.

Proposition 2.16.

Let
(K,v)
be a non-trivially valued field of characteristic
p>0
with
a non-trivial coarsening
v^{+}
which is separably tame. Then
K
is
deeply ramified.

Proof.

As separably tame valued fields are deeply ramified
[

, Theorem

, so is
\mathcal{O}_{v^{+}}
. By
[

, Theorem 2.3.4]
,
both
\mathcal{O}_{v}
and its coarsening induce the same topology on

and hence
(K,v)
is also dense in its perfect hull.
∎

It is important to note that it is very important that
\mathcal{O}_{v_{t^{+}}}
is not
trivially valued in the above proposition. If
(K,v)
had rank
1
then

v_{t^{+}}
for any pseudo-uniformizer

would satisfy all other requirements
of
2.16
. However, not all rank
1
valued fields are
deeply ramified.

Fortunately, if the coarsening is trivially valued there are still
straightforward ways to determine whether the field is deeply ramified.

Lemma 2.17.

Let
(K,v)
be a valued field. Let
t
be a pseudo-uniformizer such that

vt
is not minimal positive,
v(t^{n})
is cofinal in
vK
and

\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t)
is semi-perfect. Then
(K,v)
is deeply ramified.

Proof.

Because
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t)
is semi-perfect,
conclude that
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t^{n})
is semi-perfect for all

:
note that the coarsest coarsening
w_{1}
of
v
with
w_{1}(t)>0
and
the coarsest coarsening
w_{n}
of
v
with
w_{n}(t)>0
coincide, so

Kw_{1}=Kw_{n}
. Since
[
13
, Lemma 7.2.19]
shows that
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t^{n})
is semi-perfect if and only if

is perfect, the semiperfectness of
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t)

implies that
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t^{n})
is semiperfect.
As

is cofinal, for any

with

there is an

such that

, and we get a surjection

\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t^{n})\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{v}/(x)

\mathcal{O}_{v}/(x)
is also semi-perfect.
∎

Elementary substructures

As an immediate consequence of
3.1
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 3.2.

If
(K,v,t)\subseteq(L,w,t)
are both valued fields in
\mathcal{C}_{p,e}
such
that

is separable. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(K,v)\preceq(L,w)
,

(2)

and

and

are regularly dense, the value group

As mentioned in the introduction, the corollary above now allows us to draw
conclusions about embeddings of certain rank
1
deeply ramified fields.

Corollary 3.3.

Let
K/\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h}
be an algebraic extension of valued fields with
ramification such that the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
of
K
is
semi-perfect mod
t
. Then we get an elementary embedding of valued fields

K\preceq K.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right).

Proof.

We claim the following:
The inclusion of valued fields induces an isomorphism of value group and
valuation rings mod
t
. Furthermore, the extension is separated. Next
we show both fields are deeply ramified and
\mathcal{O}_{v_{t^{+}}}
is trivially
valued for both fields. Both fields are therefore contained in the same
class
\mathcal{C}_{p,e}
and all the requirements of
3.2
are met,
and the embedding is elementary.

Now we show all these claims.

We know that
\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
is an
immediate extension. Therefore, the extension of value groups of

K.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)/K
is trivial as well.

Next we show that the ring extension
\mathcal{O}_{K.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}/(t)/\mathcal{O}_{K}/(t)
is trivial.

We demonstrate that
\mathcal{O}_{K}/(t)=\mathcal{O}_{K.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}/(t)
first for finite
simple extensions
K/\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h}
and show we can repeat this
process for arbitrary algebraic extension.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h}(a)
be a simple algebraic extension. We claim that
for all
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)(a)
, there is an

\widetilde{f}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h}(a)
such that
v(f-f^{\prime})\geq t
. The
element
f
is a finite linear combination of powers of
a
and
elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
:
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}f^{i}
for

x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
. Polynomials are continuous, and
therefore there is some
\delta
such that if

v(\widetilde{x_{i}}-x_{i})\geq\delta
and

v(\widetilde{f}-f)\geq\delta
then

v\left(\sum_{i}x_{i}f^{i}-\sum_{i}\widetilde{x_{i}}f^{i}\right)\geq v(t).

Because
\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h}
is dense in
\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
, we may
take all
\widetilde{x_{i}}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h}
. This gives that

\mathcal{O}_{K}/(t)\to\mathcal{O}_{K.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}/(t)
is a surjective map. It is
injective by construction, so it is an isomorphism as well.

More generally: if we want to show that some residue
class of
\mathcal{O}_{K.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}/(t)
is in the image of the
inclusion, it suffices to consider a finite simple extension generated
by an element of this residue class.

Now we show is that the extension is separated. We make a case distinction depending on whether

K
is perfect. If
K
is perfect, then
K.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)/K
is separable by the definition of perfect fields.
If
K
is non-perfect, then
\sqrt[p^{n}]{t}
is a
p
-basis for some

n\geq 0
and also in
K.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)
.
In either case, the extension
K.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)/K
is
separated.

By assumption
v(t)
is not minimal positive in
vK
. Thus by

2.16

(K,v)
is deeply ramified.

The coarsening
\mathcal{O}_{v_{t^{+}}}
is trivially valued because
v

is a rank
1
valuation.

In conclusion, we have shown all necessary requirements to apply

3.2
, and hence the embedding is elementary.
∎

Corollary 3.4.

The natural embedding

{(\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h})}^{\text{AS}}\subseteq{(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)))}^{\text{AS}}
is
elementary.

Proof.

We show that

{(\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h})}^{\text{AS}}.\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)={\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)}^{\text{AS}}

and use
3.3
. To show the equality, we use Krasner’s lemma

[

, Theorem 4.1.7]
.

Clearly the former is contained in the latter. We show the first has all
roots of Artin-Schreier polynomials. By the same argument as in

3.3
, the inclusion

{(\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h})}^{\text{AS}}\subseteq{(\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h})}^{\text{AS}}.\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h}

has dense image. Now take any

f\in{(\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h})}^{\text{AS}}.\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h}
. By Krasner’s lemma it
is sufficient to show that
X^{p}-X-\tilde{f}
has a root for

\tilde{f}
sufficiently close to
f
. We can get such an

\tilde{f}
from
{(\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{h})}^{\text{AS}}
.
∎

3.2
also applies naturally in valued fields of higher rank:

Corollary 3.5.

The natural embedding

{(\mathbb{F}_{p}\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right))}^{\text{AS}}\subseteq{(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\left(\!\left(t\right)\!\right)))}^{\text{AS}}\left(\!\left(\mathbb{Q}\right)\!\right)

and the induced embedding of value groups

.

Elementary equivalence

As separably tame valued fields of positive characteristic even satisfy an

Theorem 3.6.

Fix a prime

e\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, and let

(K,v,t),(L,w,s)
be pointed valued fields in
\mathcal{C}_{p,e}
. If

(vK,v(t))\equiv(wL,w(s))
and
\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t)\equiv\mathcal{O}_{w}/(s)
then

(K,v)\equiv(L,w)
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to that of
3.1
where we take the
common substructure to be
(\mathbb{F}_{p},v_{\text{triv}})
with the trivial
valuation. Each step is similar to the corresponding one in the proof of

3.1
. At the end, we get an isomorphism of valued fields over

\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

The original intermediate isomorphism

(\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}/(t))_{\text{red}}\cong_{(\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t))\text{red}}(\mathcal{O}_{w}/(t))_{\text{red}}

now becomes an isomorphism over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
instead of

(\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t))_{\text{red}}
. We can just as well apply

[
3
, Theorem 4.22]
to this as well. Note that this works for

\mathbb{F}_{p}
in particular because it is perfect and trivially valued
(hence tame), and therefore any extension is separable and

vK/(v_{\text{triv}}\mathbb{F}_{p})=vK
is torsion free.
∎

Note that in the special case of
3.6
in case that
K
and
L

are both perfect (i.e.,
[
13
, Theorem 5.1.7]
), the conclusion can be
strengthened to
(K,v,t)\equiv(L,w,s)
(and hence it is possible to rephrase

[
13
, Theorem 5.1.7]
as an equivalence): in case
e=0
, the field

(\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)^{\textrm{h}})^{\mathrm{perf}}\cong(\mathbb{F}_{p}(s)^{\textrm{h}})^{\mathrm{perf}}
is a common subfield of both

(K,v,t)
and
(L,w,s)
which is also in
\mathcal{C}_{p,0}
, and so the elementary
equivalence can be strengthened to include a symbol for the constants
t
or

s
respectively. Whether this works in greater generality remains open:

Question 3.7.

Can
3.6
be strengthened to an equivalence in general? More precisely:
Given
p
prime
and
e\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,
e>0
, and two pointed valued fields

(K,v,t)
and
(L,w,t)
in
\mathcal{C}_{p,e}
. Does the equivalence

((vK,v(t))\equiv(wL,w(s))\textrm{ and }\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t)\equiv\mathcal{O}_{w}/(s))\Longleftrightarrow(K,v,t)\equiv(L,w,s)

hold?

Existential closedness

Last but not least, we obtain an AKE principle transferring existential
closedness.

Theorem 3.8.

Let
(K,v)\subseteq(L,w)
be a separable inclusion of deeply ramified
valued fields in a fixed class
\mathcal{C}_{p,e}
. Then the following statements
are equivalent:

(1)

(K,v)\preceq_{\exists}(L,w)
,

(2)

vK\preceq_{\exists}wL
and

\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t)\preceq_{\exists}\mathcal{O}_{w}/(t)
.

Proof.

This proof is similar to that of

3.1
. The implication (1)
\implies
(2) is clear. We now prove (2)

\implies
(1). By
2.11
, we may assume that
L
is saturated of size

|{K}^{+}|
.

Then can find a sufficiently saturated ultrapower
(K^{*},v^{*})
of

(K,v)
such that we have a (non-elementary) embedding
\mathcal{O}_{w}/(t)\to\mathcal{O}_{v^{*}}/(t)
and
wL\to v^{*}K^{*}
over
(K,v)
. Then the reduced
ring
(\mathcal{O}_{w}/(t))_{\text{red}}
embeds into

(\mathcal{O}_{v^{*}}/(t))_{\text{red}}
over
(\mathcal{O}_{v}/(t))_{\text{red}}
as
well. This is exactly an embedding of valued fields

(Lw_{t^{-}},\overline{w})\to(K^{*}v^{*}_{t^{-}},\overline{v^{*}})
over

(K,v)
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Kv_{t^{-}},\overline{v})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Lw_{t^{-}},\overline{w})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(K^{*}v^{*}_{t^{-}},\overline{v^{*}})}

As before, the valued field
(Lv_{t^{+}},\overline{v_{t^{-}}})
is tame by
saturation so the composition of valuations
(L,v_{t^{-}})
is separably
tame.

Now, we can use the separable relative embedding property
[
3
, Theorem 4.19]
to embed

(L,w_{t^{-}})
into
(K^{*},v^{*}_{t^{-}})
over
(K,v_{t^{-}})
. To see
this note that by assumption
L/K
is separable and the residue fields of
all non-trivial coarsenings are perfect because the valued fields are
deeply ramified. The value group extension is torsion free because we
assumed that
vK\preceq_{\exists}vL
.

We then obtain a commutative diagram using the separable relative
embedding property

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(K,v_{t^{-}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(L,w_{t^{-}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(K^{*},v^{*}_{t^{-}})}

The map
K\to K^{*}
is induced by the map
O_{w}/(t)\to\mathcal{O}_{v^{*}}/(t)
,
hence we get that this is an embedding with respect to the total
valuations
v,w,v^{*}
. Now because
(K,v)\to(K^{*},v^{*})
is
elementary by construction, we can conclude
(K,v)\preceq_{\exists}(L,w)
.
∎
