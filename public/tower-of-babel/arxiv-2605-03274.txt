Formalizing Singer Sidon Constructions and Sidon Set Infrastructure in Lean 4
David B. Hulak, Arthur F. Ramos, Ruy J. G. B. de Queiroz

Abstract
Erdős Problem 30 asks for sharp asymptotics of the Sidon extremal function h ⁡ ( N ) h(N) , and Singer’s construction is the classical source of lower-bound examples matching the main term. We present a Lean 4 formalization of Singer’s Sidon set construction, together with reusable Sidon-set infrastructure for additive combinatorics. For every prime power q = p k q=p^{k} , we prove the existence of a Sidon set modulo q 2 + q + 1 q^{2}+q+1 of cardinality q + 1 q+1 ; the prime-field case q = p q=p is the base presentation. The proof proceeds through a non-trivial algebraic chain: construction of the base field and its degree-three extension, analysis of the trace kernel as a 2-dimensional subspace over the base field, a geometric argument via subspace intersections establishing the multiplicative Sidon property in the quotient group, and a transfer from quotient multiplication to modular integer addition. Around this central result, we develop a reusable Sidon set library for additive combinatorics. It comprises interval Sidon sets, modular Sidon sets, the extremal function h ⁡ ( N ) h(N) , Lindström’s cross-difference inequality, a Johnson-route shift-incidence upper bound of the form h ⁡ ( N ) ≤ N + N 1 / 4 + O ⁡ ( 1 ) h(N)\leq\sqrt{N}+N^{1/4}+O(1) , exact representation-function identities, and unconditional two-sided h ⁡ ( N ) = Θ ⁡ ( N ) h(N)=\Theta(\sqrt{N}) bounds with exact floor-rounded finite statements for N ≥ 5 N\geq 5 . We further formalize a conditional reduction: subpolynomial prime gaps together with a full subpolynomial upper-error hypothesis for h ⁡ ( N ) h(N) imply the Erdős Problem 30 estimate h ⁡ ( N ) = N + O ε ​ ( N ε ) h(N)=\sqrt{N}+O_{\varepsilon}(N^{\varepsilon}) for every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . The Singer/Sidon modules and transfer lemmas comprise 7 541 lines of Lean 4 with zero active uses of sorry . We describe the mathematical lessons learned, focusing on how formalization clarifies the precise scope of classical arguments and forces explicit treatment of the passage from the field-theoretic construction to integer Sidon predicates.

1.2
Why formalize?

Formalization of classical combinatorial number theory serves three purposes
beyond mere verification:

1.

Scope discipline
: the type system forces the formalizer to
confront exactly which hypotheses each theorem requires. For Singer’s
construction, the development separates the prime-field presentation from
the prime-power theorem, where the base field is
GaloisField p k

and the degree-three extension is supplied by Mathlib’s finite-field
extension API.

2.

Field-to-integer transfer
: the passage from algebraic structures
(quotient groups, field extensions) to combinatorial predicates (modular
Sidon, interval containment) requires explicit definitions and transfer
lemmas that informal proofs often elide.

3.

Conditional dependencies
: formal verification makes the logical
dependencies between open conjectures (prime gaps, upper bounds) and the
target statement (Erdős 30) machine-checkable.

Scope and limitations

We separate deliberate modeling choices from practical limitations.

Modeling choices

stated results):

•

Singer’s original theorem
[

applies to all prime

q

Mathematical Background

2.1
Sidon sets

A set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is
Sidon
if whenever
a+b=c+d
with

a,b,c,d\in A
and
a\leq b
,
c\leq d
, then
\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}
.
Equivalently, one may omit the ordering convention and allow the two possible
ordered identifications; the Lean predicate uses this unordered collision form.
For a positive integer
M
, we say
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is
Sidon modulo
M
if

M\mid(a+b)-(c+d)
with
a,b,c,d\in A
implies

\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}
as unordered pairs of integers. Modular Sidon immediately implies
ordinary Sidon: if
a+b=c+d
as integers, then

M\mid(a+b)-(c+d)=0
, so the modular condition gives

\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}
. The converse requires a bounded embedding:

Lemma 2.1

(No-wraparound,
IsSidon.isSidonMod_of_interval
)
.

If
A
is Sidon with
A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
and
M\geq 2N-1
, then

A
is Sidon modulo
M
.

The bound ensures all pairwise-sum differences lie in

\{-(2N-2),\dots,2N-2\}\subset(-M,M)
, so
M
-divisibility forces the
difference to be zero, reducing to an ordinary collision.

2.2
Singer’s construction

A
(v,k,\lambda)
-difference set
is a subset
D
of

\mathbb{Z}/v\mathbb{Z}
with
|D|=k
such that every nonzero residue is
representable as exactly
\lambda
ordered differences
d_{i}-d_{j}
; a
(v,k,1)
-set
is called
perfect
. The
\lambda=1
condition implies the Sidon
property in the cyclic group (at most one representation per difference);
for Singer parameters, where
k(k-1)=v-1
, the converse also holds.

Singer
[
19
]
proved that for every prime power
q
, the quotient

\mathrm{GF}(q^{3})^{\times}/\mathrm{GF}(q)^{\times}
is cyclic of order

q^{2}+q+1
, and the image of the nonzero trace-kernel elements

\ker(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{GF}(q^{3})/\mathrm{GF}(q)})\setminus\{0\}

under the quotient map forms a
(q^{2}+q+1,q+1,1)

perfect difference set of size
q+1
in
\mathbb{Z}/(q^{2}+q+1)\mathbb{Z}
.
Indeed, the trace kernel has
q^{2}
elements, so its
q^{2}-1
nonzero
elements fall into
(q^{2}-1)/(q-1)=q+1
scalar orbits.
The formalization now covers this prime-power scope: the prime presentation uses

\mathrm{GF}(p^{3})
over
\mathrm{GF}(p)
, while the prime-power theorem uses

GaloisField p k
as the base field and a degree-three finite-field
extension over that base.

The Sidon property rests on a geometric fact. Let

V=\ker(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{GF}(q^{3})/\mathrm{GF}(q)})
, a 2-dimensional

\mathrm{GF}(q)
-subspace (by rank-nullity applied to the surjective trace).
For an element
\alpha
of the extension not lying in the embedded base field:

1.

The scaled subspace
\alpha V
differs from
V
: if
\alpha V=V
,
then
V
would be a proper
\alpha
-invariant submodule of

\mathrm{GF}(q^{3})
, contradicting the no-invariant-subspace theorem.

2.

Two distinct 2-dimensional subspaces of a 3-dimensional space intersect
in dimension 1 (Grassmann formula).

3.

Therefore
V\cap\alpha^{-1}V
is 1-dimensional for such

\alpha\in\mathrm{GF}(q^{3})\setminus\mathrm{GF}(q)
.

This forces any “product collision” in the quotient (i.e.,
[u]\cdot[v]=[w]\cdot[x]
) to degenerate into a pair equality, establishing the
multiplicative Sidon property that is then transferred to additive modular
arithmetic via a cyclic quotient group isomorphism (the abstract
discrete-logarithm map).

2.3
Known bounds on
h(N)

The pair-difference counting argument gives
h(N)\leq\sqrt{2N}+1
.
Lindström’s cross-difference inequality
[
15
]
sharpens this to

h(N)\leq\sqrt{N}+N^{1/4}+1
. Balogh–Füredi–Roy
[
2
]

and Carter–Hunter–O’Bryant
[
8
]
subsequently
improved the constant in the
N^{1/4}
term, the latter giving

h(N)\leq N^{1/2}+0.98183N^{1/4}+O(1)
; these refinements do not change the
exponent-level reduction formalized here. For a broader annotated guide to
the Sidon-sequence literature, see O’Bryant
[
18
]
. For the lower
bound, Singer sets transferred to intervals via prime-finding results give

h(N)\geq\sqrt{N}-O(N^{\theta})
where
\theta
depends on the available
prime gap theorem (Bertrand gives
\theta=1/2
;
Baker–Harman–Pintz
[
3
]
gives
\theta=0.2625
).
Bose–Chowla
[
6
]
is another classical algebraic source of
finite Sidon sets; in the present development it appears at the
parameter-wrapper and transfer-lemma level, with Singer’s theorem supplying
the construction theorem used for the formal lower-bound derivation.

3.2
Quotient-group Sidon property

We define the canonical group homomorphism

\mathtt{singerMk}:\mathrm{GF}(p^{3})^{\times}\to\mathrm{GF}(p^{3})^{\times}/\mathrm{GF}(p)^{\times}

and write
[u]

u\cdot\mathrm{GF}(p)^{\times}

Multiplication below denotes field multiplication in
\mathrm{GF}(p^{3})

We prove:

Theorem 3.4

(Singer quotient Sidon)
.

Let
u,v,w,x\in\ker(\mathrm{Tr})\setminus\{0\}

\alpha\in\mathrm{GF}(p)^{\times}

[v]=[w]

Here
\mathrm{GF}(p)^{\times}

\mathrm{GF}(p^{3})^{\times}

The proof splits on whether

\mathrm{GF}(p)

•

Base case
(

[v]=[x]

•

Non-base case
(
\beta\notin\mathrm{GF}(p)

Now

3.3
From quotient multiplication to modular sums

The final step connects the quotient-group Sidon property to the combinatorial

IsSidonMod
predicate. This transfer has four components:

1.

Projective representatives
: we construct
p+1
nonzero
elements of
\ker(\mathrm{Tr})
representing the projective lines of the
2-dimensional kernel, parametrized by
\mathrm{Option}(\mathrm{GF}(p))

(the
p
affine lines plus one line at infinity).

2.

Injectivity
: these representatives map to distinct cosets in
the quotient, proved by showing that proportionality of representatives
forces equality of the parametrizing indices.

3.

Cyclic isomorphism
: we exhibit a group isomorphism

\mathbb{Z}/(p^{2}+p+1)\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathrm{GF}(p^{3})^{\times}/\mathrm{GF}(p)^{\times}
by computing the index

[\mathrm{GF}(p^{3})^{\times}:\mathrm{GF}(p)^{\times}]=(p^{3}-1)/(p-1)=p^{2}+p+1
and invoking cyclicity of the quotient
(since
\mathrm{GF}(p^{3})^{\times}
is cyclic as the multiplicative group of
a finite field, its quotient by any subgroup is cyclic).

4.

Additive transfer
: let
g
be a generator of the cyclic
quotient of order
M=p^{2}+p+1
, and let

i:\mathrm{GF}(p^{3})^{\times}/\mathrm{GF}(p)^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z}/(p^{2}+p+1)\mathbb{Z}
be the group isomorphism sending

[x]\mapsto\log_{g}[x]
. No canonical generator is used; any chosen
cyclic generator yields such an isomorphism. Since
i
is a homomorphism,
a multiplicative collision
[u][v]=[w][x]
gives

i([u])+i([v])\equiv i([w])+i([x])\pmod{M}
,
so the multiplicative Sidon property yields modular-additive Sidon:

\displaystyle M\mid(i([u]){+}i([v]))-(i([w]){+}i([x]))

\displaystyle\quad\implies\{[u],[v]\}=\{[w],[x]\}

\displaystyle\quad\implies\{i([u]),i([v])\}=\{i([w]),i([x])\}.

This is formalized via

ZMod.intCast_zmod_eq_zero_iff_dvd
.

Concretely, after choosing the isomorphism

S
is formed
by taking the canonical integer representative in
\{0,\dots,M-1\}
of each
residue

u
ranges over the chosen projective representatives
of the trace kernel. Injectivity of the projective representatives and of the
cyclic isomorphism gives
|S|=p+1
. The passage from equality in

\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}
to the divisibility predicate in

IsSidonMod

\overline{x}=\overline{y}\iff M\mid x-y
; unordered-pair equality is then
transported back through the injective representative map. The quotient-group
construction is therefore realized as a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}
with
integer representatives.

Sidon Set Infrastructure

4.1
Predicates

We define three levels of Sidon property, each building on the previous:

•

\mathtt{IsSidon}(A)
: the set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is Sidon.

•

\mathtt{IsSidonMod}(M,A)
:
A
is Sidon modulo
M
, i.e.,

M\mid(a+b)-(c+d)
with
a,b,c,d\in A
implies
\{a,b\}=\{c,d\}
.

•

\mathtt{IsIntervalSidon}(N,A)
:
A
is Sidon and

A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
.

The modular predicate mediates between algebraic constructions (which produce
Sidon sets in cyclic groups) and combinatorial targets (which require Sidon
sets in integer intervals).

4.2
Cardinality identities for Sidon sets

For finite integer Sidon sets, the library also proves exact cardinality
consequences that are independent of the Singer construction. The theorem

IsSidon.card_add
gives the sharp sumset formula

|A+A|=|A|(|A|+1)/2
, and

isSidon_iff_card_add
proves the converse characterization:
maximal sumset cardinality forces the Sidon property. For nonempty Sidon
sets,
IsSidon.card_sub
gives

|A-A|=|A|^{2}-|A|+1
. Separately,
IsSidon.addEnergy_eq
gives
additive energy
2|A|^{2}-|A|
without a nonemptiness hypothesis. Combining
the exact sumset and difference-set formulas,
IsSidon.card_sub_ge_card_add

proves
|A-A|\geq|A+A|
for nonempty Sidon sets (strictly for
|A|\geq 3
via

IsSidon.card_sub_gt_card_add
).

4.3
Representation functions

The representation-function modules connect the Sidon predicates to Mathlib’s
additive convolution API.

Definition 4.1

(Representation function)
.

For a finite integer set
A
, write

r_{A}(n)=|\{(a,b)\in A^{2}:a+b=n\}|.

The theorem
IsSidon.repr_le_two
proves that, if
A
is Sidon, then

r_{A}(n)\leq 2
for every integer
n
. The library also proves the exact
identities

\sum_{n\in A+A}r_{A}(n)=|A|^{2},\qquad\sum_{n\in A+A}r_{A}(n)^{2}=2|A|^{2}-|A|,

and the deficiency formula

\sum_{n\in A+A}(2-r_{A}(n))=|A|.

These results are independent of Singer’s construction and provide a reusable
link between Sidon-set combinatorics, additive energy, and analytic
upper-bound arguments.

4.4
The extremal function

Definition 4.2
.

\mathtt{sidonMaximum}(N)=\max\{|A|:\mathtt{IsIntervalSidon}(N,A)\}
.

We write
h(N)
for
\mathtt{sidonMaximum}(N)
, matching the standard
notation of
\lx@sectionsign
1
.

Existence of the maximum is proved via finiteness of the powerset of

\{1,\dots,N\}
and non-emptiness (the singleton
\{1\}
is Sidon for

N\geq 1
). The Lean definition then uses
Classical.choose
to select
the unique maximum witness, so
sidonMaximum
is noncomputable even
though the underlying finite maximum exists. We also prove basic properties:
positivity (
h(N)\geq 1

for
N\geq 1
) and monotonicity (
N\leq M\implies h(N)\leq h(M)
).

4.5
Transfer machinery

The modular-to-interval transfer proceeds via cyclic window relabeling.
Given a modular Sidon set
S
with
\mathtt{IsSidonMod}\ M\ S
, a cyclic
window of length
N
starting at offset
u
restricts
S
to elements

x
with
(x-u)\bmod M<N
and relabels them to

\{1,\dots,N\}
. The key theorem (
IsSidonMod.windowSidon
):

Lemma 4.3

(Cyclic window transfer)
.

If
\mathtt{IsSidonMod}\ M\ S
and
0<N\leq M
, then for any offset
u
,
the relabeled window restriction is an interval Sidon set in
\{1,\dots,N\}
.

The cardinality of the window image depends on how many elements of
S
fall
in the chosen window. The first averaging lemma counts membership directly:
for each fixed
s\in S
, exactly
N
of the
M
offsets satisfy

(s-u)\bmod M<N
. Hence the sum, over all offsets
u
, of the window
cardinalities is
|S|N
, so some offset captures at least

\lceil|S|N/M\rceil
elements
(
IsSidonMod.exists_large_intervalSidon
).

The full-size transfer uses the stronger cyclic-gap structure forced by the
modular Sidon property. Sort the residues of
S
in
\{0,\dots,M-1\}
and let
the cyclic gaps be the successive forward differences. These gaps are
positive and sum to
M
. They are also pairwise distinct: if two distinct
cyclic gaps were equal, the two associated ordered difference representations
would contradict
the modular Sidon condition. If
G
is the largest gap and
s=|S|
, then
the
s
distinct positive gaps are all at most
G
, so

M\leq G+(G-1)+\cdots+(G-s+1)=sG-\frac{s(s-1)}{2}.

Thus
G\geq(M+s(s-1)/2)/s
, and Lean uses the corresponding
natural-number floor threshold

\lfloor(M+|S|(|S|-1)/2)/|S|\rfloor.

Deleting a largest gap leaves a complementary cyclic window that contains all
elements of
S
, giving a full-size interval Sidon set whenever

N\geq M-\lfloor(M+|S|(|S|-1)/2)/|S|\rfloor+1

(formalized by the quantitative-gap theorem in
SidonGap.lean
and its
Singer-parameter specialization in
PrimePowerTransfer.lean
).
For Singer parameters (
|S|=p+1
,
M=p^{2}+p+1
), Lean’s natural-number
division simplifies the threshold to

N\geq p^{2}+p+2-\lfloor 3p/2\rfloor
, yielding an interval Sidon set of
size exactly
p+1>p
.

5
Unconditional Bounds

5.1
Upper bound

Theorem 5.1

(Pair-difference upper bound)
.

For
N\geq 1
,
h(N)\leq\sqrt{2N}+1
.

This follows from the pair-difference counting bound: if
A
is a Sidon
subset of
\{1,\dots,N\}
with
|A|=m
, then the
m(m-1)/2
positive
pairwise differences are all distinct elements of
\{1,\dots,N-1\}
.
Thus
m(m-1)/2\leq N-1
. Since

(m-1)^{2}\leq m(m-1)\leq 2(N-1)<2N,

we obtain
m-1<\sqrt{2N}
, hence
m\leq\sqrt{2N}+1
.

We also formalize Lindström’s cross-difference inequality as reusable
infrastructure:

Theorem 5.2

(Lindström cross-difference inequality)
.

For a Sidon set
\{a_{1}<\cdots<a_{m}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
and any

1\leq k\leq m
, the cross-differences
a_{j}-a_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq k<j\leq m
are
distinct elements of
\{1,\dots,N-1\}
, giving
(m-k)\cdot k\leq N-1
.

The proof proceeds by splitting the sorted set into bottom-
k
and top-
(m-k)

elements, establishing the injectivity of the cross-difference map via the Sidon
property, and bounding the image within
\{1,\dots,N-1\}
.

Theorem 5.3

(Johnson-route shift-incidence upper bound)
.

For
N\geq 16
,

h(N)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+\lfloor\sqrt{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\rfloor+2.

This matches Lindström’s classical
\sqrt{N}+N^{1/4}+O(1)
leading order.

The proof follows the Johnson-bound route
[
14
]
: the
same shift-incidence counting argument originally used for codes applies
mutatis mutandis to Sidon sets, as in the classical discussion of

B_{2}
-sequences
[
12
, §C9]
. For a Sidon set
A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}

with
|A|=m
, consider the
r
shifted copies

A,A+1,\dots,A+(r-1)
, all contained in
\{1,\dots,N+r-1\}
.
Distinct shifts intersect in at most one point
(
IsSidon.shift_inter_le_one
). If
f(x)
counts how many shifted
copies contain
x
, then

\sum_{x}f(x)=mr
and

\sum_{x}\binom{f(x)}{2}\leq\binom{r}{2}
. Hence

\sum_{x}f(x)^{2}\leq mr+r(r-1)=r(m+r-1)
. Cauchy–Schwarz gives

|A|^{2}r\leq(N+r-1)(r+|A|-1).

Choosing

r=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor\cdot\lfloor\sqrt{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\rfloor
and carrying out the integer
rounding in Lean yields Theorem
5.3
; writing

m=\sqrt{N}+t
, the continuous relaxation suggests taking

r\approx N^{3/4}
, which balances the error terms
r^{2}
and
N^{3/2}

and gives
t=O(N^{1/4})
. The displayed additive constant
2
is the one
obtained by the exact natural-number floor argument; the preceding continuous
calculation motivates the scale of the error term.
Asymptotically this has the same leading order as Lindström’s classical

\sqrt{N}+O(N^{1/4})
estimate; the displayed finite bound records the exact
integer-rounded form proved in Lean.

Corollary 5.4

(Upper-bound hypothesis for
\varepsilon\geq 1/4
)
.

For every
\varepsilon\geq 1/4
, there are constants
C,N_{0}
such that

h(N)\leq\sqrt{N}+CN^{\varepsilon}\qquad(N\geq N_{0}).

This is formalized as
sidonUpperBound_quarter
, with

C=2
and
N_{0}=16
. It proves the upper-bound half of the conditional
Erdős 30 upper-bound condition in the range
\varepsilon\geq 1/4
; it does not by
itself improve the unconditional two-sided Erdős 30 range, whose lower
side still comes from the Singer–Bertrand transfer below.

5.2
Lower bound

Theorem 5.5

(Singer-via-Bertrand lower bound)
.

For
N\geq 5
,

h(N)>\left\lfloor\frac{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1}{2}\right\rfloor.

The proof combines Singer’s theorem with Bertrand’s
postulate
[
4
]
. Given
N\geq 9
, set

m=\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
and
n=\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor
. Bertrand’s
postulate gives a prime
p
with
n<p\leq 2n
. Since
2n+1\leq m
, the
containment condition is satisfied by the chain

p^{2}+p+2-\lfloor 3p/2\rfloor\leq p^{2}+1\leq 4n^{2}+1\leq(2n+1)^{2}\leq m^{2}\leq N.

The cyclic window transfer then yields an interval Sidon set of size
p+1
,
and
p>n
gives

\lfloor(\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1)/2\rfloor<p+1
. The cases

5\leq N<9
are discharged by exhibiting an explicit small Sidon set, for
instance
\{1,2\}

Combined bound and partial Erdős 30

Theorem 5.6

(Two-sided bound)

For
N\geq 5

\left\lfloor\frac{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1}{2}\right\rfloor<h(N)\leq\lfloor\sqrt{2N}\rfloor+1\leq\sqrt{2N}+1.

Corollary 5.7

(Partial Erdős 30 for
\varepsilon\geq 1/2

For every

The proof shows

\lfloor\sqrt{2N}\rfloor+1\leq 2\sqrt{N}

The stronger upper side from Corollary

The formal statement of Erdős Problem 30 is:

\forall\varepsilon>0,\ \exists C\geq 0,\ \exists N_{0},\ \forall N\geq N_{0}:\ h(N)\geq\sqrt{N}-C\cdot N^{\varepsilon}

The subpolynomial prime gap hypothesis
(

given
\varepsilon>0

h(N)\leq\sqrt{N}+C_{1}N^{\varepsilon}
and the lower bound gives
h(N)\geq\sqrt{N}-C_{2}N^{\varepsilon}
for
N\geq N_{0}
; taking
C=\max(C_{1},C_{2})
and the

yields

For the lower bound, we prove a conditional transfer (whose Lean signature
additionally carries

for modularity, now

7
Mathematical Lessons from Formalization

7.1
The projective-line representation was forced by formalization

In the informal Singer argument, one says “the
q+1
lines through the origin
in the 2-dimensional trace kernel give
q+1
cosets.” Formalizing this
required constructing
explicit representatives
: a function

\mathrm{Option}(\mathrm{GF}(p))\to\ker(\mathrm{Tr})\setminus\{0\}

mapping each projective coordinate to a specific nonzero kernel element.

The choice of basis is arbitrary but must be
fixed
: the entire injectivity
argument (showing distinct indices map to distinct cosets) depends on the explicit
form of the representatives in terms of a chosen kernel basis. This is invisible
in informal proofs but becomes a nontrivial formal proof component.

7.2
The modular Sidon predicate carries the field-to-integer transfer

A recurring design tension in additive combinatorics formalization is whether to
work with sets in
\mathbb{Z}
or in
\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}
. Our experience
strongly favors
IsSidonMod M A
(with
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
) over
direct
\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}
sets, because:

•

The Singer quotient-group argument naturally produces an

\mathbb{Z}/(p^{2}+p+1)\mathbb{Z}
-indexed result, expressible via divisibility.

•

The interval Sidon target uses subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
.

•

The modular predicate connects both without changing the carrier type.

The direction
\texttt{IsSidonMod}\implies\texttt{IsSidon}
is trivially
one-line (every literal collision
a+b=c+d
also satisfies
M\mid 0
).
The non-trivial direction is the converse: the no-wraparound lemma shows
that an interval Sidon set in
\{1,\dots,N\}
with
M\geq 2N-1
is also
Sidon modulo
M
, since pairwise-sum differences lie in
\{-(2N-2),\dots,2N-2\}\subset(-M,M)
, making
M
-divisibility equivalent to zero.
This factoring into “construction
\to
modular
\to
interval”
is cleaner than any single-step argument we attempted.

7.3
The non-invariance argument requires the full irreducibility theory

Theorem
3.2
(no proper invariant subspace) might appear to be
a simple linear algebra fact, but its formalization reveals a substantive
mathematical dependency: one needs that the minimal polynomial of

\alpha\notin\mathrm{GF}(p)
has degree 3 (irreducibility over the base
field), which in turn requires the intermediate field characterization of
algebraic elements in Mathlib.

The alternative proof strategy—directly showing
V\neq\alpha V
by trace
computations—turned out to be
longer
in Lean than the general
invariant-subspace argument, because trace manipulation requires unfolding the
Galois field representation.

7.4
The extremal function is defined non-constructively

The definition
h(N)=\max\{|A|:A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}\text{ is Sidon}\}

requires an existence argument for the maximum.
We define
sidonMaximum
via
Classical.choose
, making it

noncomputable
in Lean. This forces a clean separation: properties
of
h(N)
are proved via bounds and transfer theorems, never by evaluation.
This mirrors the mathematical practice: known asymptotic results about
h(N)

use existence arguments (Singer, Bose–Chowla
[
6
]
) and
counting bounds, with no evaluation of optimal sets for general
N
.

7.5
The conditional theorem factors into three hypotheses

By factoring Erdős 30 into

SidonUpperBound

+

SidonLowerBound
, and further
decomposing the lower bound into
SingerFamily

+

SubpolynomialPrimeGap
, the formal development achieves a
clear separation:

•

Singer family: pure algebra (now discharged).

•

Prime gap transfer: analytic number theory (open hypothesis).

•

Upper bound: formalized unconditionally for
\varepsilon\geq 1/4
;
still a hypothesis for the full range
\varepsilon>0
.

•

Assembly: the triangle inequality.

The formal development records this decomposition as named hypotheses and
theorem statements for the three components.

7.6
Floor-rounding carries mathematical content

The Singer-via-Bertrand lower bound (Theorem
5.5
)
is stated in the integer-rounded form

h(N)>\lfloor(\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+1)/2\rfloor
; the informal

h(N)\geq\sqrt{N}/2
serves as asymptotic shorthand. The distinction is
mathematical: the difference between
\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor
and
\sqrt{N}

interacts with the floor-form transfer threshold

p^{2}+p+2-\lfloor 3p/2\rfloor
and with the integrality of Sidon-set
cardinalities, and the integer-rounded statement is what one actually proves.
Likewise, the shift-incidence upper bound (Theorem
5.3
)
records the exact expression

\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor+\lfloor\sqrt{\lfloor\sqrt{N}\rfloor}\rfloor+2
;
the asymptotic
\sqrt{N}+N^{1/4}+1
describes its leading scale.
Formalization makes these floor steps unavoidable, and the finite Lean
statements record exactly which mathematical inequality is being proved at each
stage. The accompanying type transitions
(
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
and the cyclic-group/finite-set
type transitions) are then carried by routine compatibility lemmas.

contribution occupies a complementary niche: it develops foundational
infrastructure in additive combinatorics and makes explicit the links between
algebraic constructions and combinatorial interval statements.

9
Conclusion

We have presented a Lean 4 formalization of Singer’s Sidon set construction
through prime powers, together with a Sidon set library and conditional
Erdős 30 reduction. The development demonstrates that:

1.

Classical algebraic constructions require explicit handling of
projective representatives, modular-to-interval transfers, and type
transitions that informal proofs routinely elide.

2.

Formalization makes the scope explicit: the discharged Singer-family
input, the still-open gap and upper-bound hypotheses for Erdős 30, and
the exact logical structure of the conditional reduction are all made
explicit.

3.

The modular Sidon predicate is a natural formalization-friendly
intermediate between algebraic and combinatorial viewpoints.

Thus the submission contributes checked Singer constructions through prime powers,
modular-to-interval Sidon transfer, formal
h(N)
bounds, and a reusable
theorem-indexed Lean artifact for future additive-combinatorics formalization.
