Gauss law constraint in 𝒜 {\cal A} -theory branes
Machiko Hatsuda, Ondřej Hulík, William D. Linch, Di Wang, Yu-Ping Wang

Abstract
𝒜 {\cal A} -theory realizes U-duality symmetry by extending the string worldsheet to a higher dimensional brane worldvolume, in which the worldvolume and the spacetime belong to different representations of the exceptional group. The closure of the brane Virasoro algebra requires the Gauss law constraint. The Gauss law constraint promotes spacetime coordinates to gauge fields and extends the string worldsheet into the brane worldvolume. While the Virasoro constraint is used to reduce the spacetime coordinate, the Gauss law constraint is used to reduce both the worldvolume and the spacetime coordinates. As in conventional gauge theories, the treatment of the Gauss law constraint is a technically important aspect of the quantization of 𝒜 {\cal A} -theory. We show that the string solution is only consistent solution of the Gauss law dimensional reduction condition for D=3 and 4 cases. This result implies that the physical symmetry of the theory is two-dimensional conformal symmetry, suggesting that the theory admits a string-like quantization. We further construct a string solution that is covariant under the exceptional group symmetry. The relation between this solution and the constant charge parameter appearing in the exceptional σ \sigma -model is also discussed.

String duality plays a central role in superstring theory. It not only relates the six superstring theories including M-theory, but also provides the foundation for constructing a unified framework. In superstring theories, the gravitational field and gauge fields can be understood as coset parameters of the duality symmetry group, indicating that duality symmetry determines the structure of spacetime geometry itself.

6
Discussion

The Gauss law constraint in
{\cal A}
-theory branes promotes spacetime coordinates to gauge fields and enlarges the string worldsheet into the brane worldvolume.
We have shown that the consistent solution of the
Gauss law constraint is only string solution for D=3 and 4 cases.
We also proposed the covariantized stirng solution
\partial^{m}=q^{m}\partial_{\sigma}
in such a way that it solves the Gauss law constraint,
{\cal U}_{\cal M}={\mathbbm{h}}_{m{\cal M}}^{M}\partial^{m}\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleright$}_{M}={\mathbbm{h}}_{m{\cal M}}^{M}q^{m}\partial_{\sigma}\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleright$}_{M}=0
.
In this gauge the brane Virasoro algebra reduces to the standard Virasoro algebra showing the worldsheet
conformal symmetry.
Once the physical worldvolume is chosen, the quantization is performed analogously to the standard string theory.
We leave the quantization of
{\cal A}
-theory branes for future work.

The two-dimensional conformal symmetry implies that the scattering amplitudes are described by the dual resonance model.
Since the Gauss law constraint reduce massive brane modes to the two-dimensional massive string modes while leaving massless modes intact, one could have an educated guess of the structure of
{\cal{A}}
-theory.
Schematically
{\cal{A}}
-theory amplitude can be written as
A_{n}=\langle\,V_{1},\cdots V_{n}\,\rangle
.
V
is the vertex operators, which can be written as
V=E(\epsilon,\partial X)e^{ik\cdot X}
.
E(\epsilon,\partial X)
is some functions between the polarization vector
\epsilon
and
\partial X
.

When one evaluates
A_{n}
contractions between
\partial X
and
e^{ik\cdot X}
, and contractions among
\partial X
themselves gives the kinetic factor of the amplitudes. While the contractions between
e^{ik_{i}\cdot X},e^{ik_{j}\cdot X}
gives the dynamic factors (where the massive poles are). Since after solving the Gauss law constraint the
XX
correlators are the same as string theory. We shall expect that the dynamical factors are identical to the string theory ones.

As an example, for D=3 case, 4-point
{\cal{A}}
-theory amplitudes is written as
[
37
]

A_{4}=K(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3},\epsilon_{4})\frac{\Gamma(-s)\Gamma(-t)\Gamma(-u)}{\Gamma(1+s)\Gamma(1+t)\Gamma(1+u)},

where
K(\epsilon_{1},\cdots\epsilon_{4})
is the kinematic factor which is the function of polarization vectors and momentum
k_{i}
. By using its subgroup
H
=Sp(4) covariance, gauge invariance plus the section condition, one could completely fix the kinematic factor
K
, which is given in
[
37
]
. We hope to more systematically bootstrap the amplitude using this method in the future.
