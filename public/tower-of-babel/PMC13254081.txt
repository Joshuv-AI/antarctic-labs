Human macrophage adhesion is significantly higher on SIBS based glaucoma microshunts than on common intraocular lens materials in vitro
Authors: Bauer Dirk, Kasper Maren, Schell Melanie, Heinz Carsten, Heiligenhaus Arnd
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This in vitro study investigated the adhesion of human human monocytes / macrophages (MΦ) to poly(styrene-block-isobutylene-block-styrene) (SIBS)-based PreserFlo® MicroShunts, as macrophage interactions can significantly influence microimplant success. For this, purified human MΦ were cultured for 35 days on SIBS-based PreserFlo® MicroShunts and compared against control intraocular lens (IOL) materials (PMMA and hydrophilic acrylate). We found significantly higher MΦ adhesion on the PreserFlo® MicroShunts than on PMMA or hydrophilic acrylic IOLs (p < 0.05). These results indicate that the surface properties of the SIBS-based MicroShunt promote greater MΦ adhesion in vitro compared to commercial IOLs under the conditions tested. This differential adhesion could have implications for the long-term performance of the implant, particularly in uveitic eyes. While increased MΦ adhesion is a known component of the foreign body response, further functional analysis of the macrophage secretome and polarization state (M1 vs. M2) is required to confirm whether this leads to clinically significant fibrosis. Furthermore, in vivo studies are required to elucidate the specific impact of these interactions on long-term clinical performance in uveitic eyes.

Introduction

Uveitis, an inflammatory condition affecting various parts of vascular layer within the eye, represents a significant cause of visual impairment and blindness globally. This diverse group of uveitic diseases can stem from numerous etiologies, including autoimmune disorders, infections, and idiopathic causes, making its diagnosis and management particularly complex1. A major and often challenging complication of uveitis is the development of elevated intraocular pressure, which can lead to glaucoma characterized by an irreversible optic nerve damage and vision loss2. Ocular hypertension or secondary glaucoma develops in up to 20% of all uveitis cases during the course of the disease. The pathogenesis of this ocular hypertension in uveitis is multifactorial, involving mechanisms such as inflammation-induced changes to the eye’s drainage system and the side effects of corticosteroid therapy, a common treatment for uveitis2,3.

Glaucoma treatment aims to manage intraocular pressure (IOP), prevent optic nerve damage and preserve vision. Initial treatment options in uveitis include topical glaucoma medication as carboanhydrase inhibitors, beta-blockers, alpha-2 agonists, and prostaglandin analogues4,5. If conservative treatment fails or optic nerve damage progresses, surgical interventions are indicated to prevent further functional loss. In uveitis patients mainly bleb forming techniques as standard trabeculectomy and implants as XEN®, PreserFlo® or classical glaucoma drainage devices such as Baerveldt or Ahmed implant are used. However, outcome after surgery may be limited by secondary scar formation in Tenon layer obstructing outflow. Thus, activated fibroblasts induced by surgery and their transformation into myofibroblasts might ultimately lead to fibrosis and postoperative failure. In addition, it is known that macrophages can also transform directly into myofibroblasts, which is induced by TGF-β16. In uveitic eyes, the intraocular inflammatory environment significantly amplifies surgical failure due to an exaggerated inflammatory response and enhanced fibrosis7, both heavily influenced by macrophages8.

Compared with the traditional filtration procedure of trabeculectomy, PreserFlo® Microshunt implantation is less traumatic, but has shown quite similar hypotensive efficacy to trabeculectomy. However, its tube made of combined polystyrene, isobutylene and styrene (SIBS) is intended to remain within the anterior chamber of the eye. Although SIBS is associated with less inflammation and fibroses as compared with other implants, macrophage (MΦ) interaction with the implant material might limit its use in the presence of intraocular inflammation9. In this study, we analyzed the in vitro adhesion of human MΦ to commercially available PreserFlo® MicroShunts (made of Poly(styrene-block-isobutylene-block-styrene) [SIBS]), comparing it to well-established intraocular lens (IOL) materials known for their excellent biocompatibility also in uveitis eyes. This comparison aims to elucidate the potential role of MΦ adhesion in the context of PreserFlo® use in uveitic eyes and the potential differences in success rates compared to non uveitic eyes.

Results

Purity of isolated human monocytes

The isolation of human monocytes commenced with fresh peripheral blood samples. Following density gradient centrifugation to obtain PBMCs and subsequent positive magnetic bead selection using Miltenyi Biotec reagents, flow cytometric analysis confirmed the purity of the isolated monocyte population. Prior to magnetic bead selection, monocytes (identified as CD45 + CD15-CD14 + cells) constituted an average of 32.92% of the total PBMCs. Post-selection, the purity of isolated human monocytes reached 85.37% (Fig. 1). The viability of the final isolated monocyte population was determined to be 97.06%. Furthermore, a discernible disparity in adhesion was evident between the two IOL types. PMMA IOLs showed a substantially elevated level of MΦ adhesion as compared to hydrophilic acrylate IOLs (p < 0.05). This finding suggests that IOL surface properties significantly influence cellular adhesion. Representative microscopic images captured at 35 days visually support these quantitative findings, illustrating the distinct cell densities on each biomaterial surface (Fig. 2B, C, 3).

Discussion

The present study investigated the adhesion of human MΦ to PreserFlo® MicroShunts and compared it to established IOLs. Understanding these cell-biomaterial interactions is crucial, as macrophages play a pivotal role in mediating the foreign body response, inflammation, and subsequent fibrotic encapsulation. These processes are essential for the success of bleb forming glaucoma surgery with implants10. Our 35-day long-term culture was employed to provide a continuous in vitro representation aiming to better approximate certain aspects of the in vivo environment, offering insights into implant performance, which would be unobtainable from short-term studies.

The materials investigated included SIBS, a hydrophobic and highly biocompatible polymer used in PreserFlo® due to its inertness and biostability; PMMA, a rigid, hydrophobic, and optically clear material, historically the first IOL polymer; and hydrophilic acrylate IOLs, a flexible, water-containing variant designed for enhanced biocompatibility11. While IOL materials are established as an initial screen in our in vitro model due to their long history of intraocular safety, it must be acknowledged that their clinical environment differs from that of a glaucoma shunt. IOLs are typically sequestered within the capsular bag with limited chronic exposure to circulating inflammatory components. In contrast, a glaucoma shunt is continuously bathed in aqueous humor and bridges the anterior chamber to the subconjunctival space, where it is exposed to a more dynamic inflammatory environment. We utilized IOL materials here as a reference point to establish a baseline for macrophage adherence on highly biocompatible polymers under standardized in vitro conditions.

The findings of the present study suggest higher MΦ adhesion on the PreserFlo® MicroShunt in comparison to both PMMA or hydrophilic acrylic IOLs. This enhanced adhesion on the PreserFlo® could potentially be relevant for its clinical use. While an increased number of MΦ at glaucoma drainage sites has been associated with chronic inflammation and fibrotic encapsulation12, whether these in vitro observations translate to a higher rate of surgical failure in uveitic patients remains to be elucidated in clinical trials.

Therefore, the substantial adhesion we observed on the device indicates that PreserFlo®’s inherent surface properties might promote MΦ recruitment or at least retention, potentially compromising its long-term drainage efficacy in eyes with secondary glaucoma associated to uveitis.

The surgical management of glaucoma secondary to uveitis presents significant challenges, with varying outcomes depending on the underlying uveitis subtype. Recent studies have explored the efficacy of minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries (MIGS) in this context, revealing differential success rates. Specifically, the XEN-45 Gel Stent has demonstrated favorable outcomes for glaucoma associated with Fuchs Uveitis Syndrome (FUS) and Intermediate Uveitis (IU), while showing limited effectiveness for Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA)-related uveitis13. Similarly, the PreserFlo® MicroShunt has proven more success in viral uveitic glaucoma (UG) than in JIA-related UG, highlighting the critical need for subtype-specific treatment approaches to optimize patient outcomes14.

In contrast, while IOLs generally exhibited lower MΦ adhesion than PreserFlo®, we noted a tendency for slightly higher adhesion on hydrophobic PMMA surfaces compared to hydrophilic acrylics. This finding is consistent with previous research that established a correlation between surface hydrophilicity/hydrophobicity and protein adsorption, which subsequently affects cell adhesion15.

The phenomenon of differential adhesion is likely the result of a multifaceted interaction between the biomaterial’s surface topography, chemical composition, and host protein adsorption. The adsorbed proteins form a conditioning film that enables subsequent cellular interactions. The observed differences in MΦ adhesion may be attributed to the distinct physicochemical properties of the materials. SIBS is characterized by a high degree of hydrophobicity and a specific surface topography that may facilitate protein adsorption—a prerequisite for macrophage binding. In contrast, hydrophilic acrylate surfaces tend to bind water molecules, creating a hydration layer that inhibits non-specific protein adsorption and subsequent cell attachment15. While our study did not measure contact angles directly, our biological findings align with the established surface chemistry of these polymers. While increased MΦ adhesion is a known component of the foreign body response, further functional analysis of the macrophage secretome (e.g., TGF-β, IL-6) and polarization state (M1 vs. M2) is required to confirm whether this leads to clinically significant fibrosis16.

A salient limitation pertains to the in vitro nature of this study. While in vitro models offer controlled insights into cell-material interactions, they are not capable of fully replicating the dynamic in vivo ocular microenvironment, with its complex fluid flow, diverse cell populations, and intricate immunological responses. Consequently, the direct application of these findings to clinical outcomes necessitates a cautious approach.

Furthermore, the static nature of our in-vitro model does not account for the protective effect of continuous aqueous humor flow. In a clinical setting, shear stress and the constant turnover of cytokines might modulate cell-biomaterial interactions. Our results should therefore be viewed as a baseline assessment of the material’s intrinsic ‘stickiness’ to inflammatory cells under idealized conditions.

Furthermore, while this study focused on SIBS, future research comparing SIBS directly with other glaucoma drainage materials—such as medical-grade silicone or other MIGS devices—as well as in vivo studies, will be crucial to fully understand the clinical impact of these adhesion patterns on bleb survival and long-term IOP management.

In this study, a classical viability assay—in the sense of a photometric measurement—proved unfeasible with our setup. The solvent utilized in the assay demonstrated adverse interactions with the IOL lenses, thereby hindering precise measurement of absorbances. Additionally, the PreserFlo® devices exhibited a deep blue staining that persisted over an extended period, thereby precluding the possibility of meaningful photometric analysis. To ensure data accuracy, we therefore relied on manual enumeration of the stained cells.

In summary, this study underscores the heterogeneity in macrophage adhesion to the diverse biomaterials used in ophthalmology. The significant adhesion to the PreserFlo® MicroShunt observed here may represent a possible contributing factor to its fibrotic complications, potentially indicating limitations in its use within an inflammatory context, and suggesting a possible need for individual adjustment of anti-inflammatory/anti-fibrotic therapies.

Future research should extend these in vitro observations to in vivo models and clinical studies to fully assess the impact of macrophage-biomaterial interactions on the long-term success of ophthalmic implants.

Materials and methods

PreserFlo® MicroShunt and intraocular lenses

The following ophthalmic implants were used: PreserFlo® MicroShunt (Santen), hydrophobic PMMA IOL (Bausch + Lomb, Model L122UV), and hydrophilic acrylate IOL (Bausch + Lomb, Model Akreos ADAPT).

Isolation of Human MΦ from peripheral blood samples

Human monocytes were isolated from peripheral blood of healthy volunteers. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Westphalia-Lippe Medical Association and the University of Münster (reference number: 2014-418-f-S). All participants provided prior written informed consent for the collection and use of their blood samples for scientific research. All procedures were conducted in strict accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were initially separated from the blood by density gradient centrifugation. In summary, the blood was initially diluted at a ratio of 1:1 with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and subsequently meticulously layered onto PBMC Spin Medium (pluriSelect Life Science UG & Co.KG, Leipzig, Germany). Centrifugation was performed at 800 × g for 15 min at room temperature without brake. The PBMC layer was subsequently collected with meticulous care, undergoing a thorough washing process with PBS twice (centrifugation at 500 × g for 10 min without brake). Thereafter, the cell pellet was resuspended in MACS buffer (PBS containing 0.5% bovine serum albumin [BSA] and 2 mM EDTA). Thereafter, human MΦ were isolated from the PBMC suspension using CD14 MicroBeads (Miltenyi Biotec, Bergisch Gladbach, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

The magnetically labeled MΦ were subsequently positively isolated by passing the cell suspension through an LS Column placed in a MACS Separator (Miltenyi Biotec). The viability and purity of isolated MΦ was assessed by flow cytometry using propidium iodide (PI), CD14, CD15, and CD45 (BioLegend Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) as markers (Fig. 1). Subsequently, the cells were subjected to freezing and stored at a temperature of − 80 °C until the assay (freezing medium: RPMI-1640 medium, 20% FCS, 20% DMSO).

Cocultivation of isolated MΦ on PreserFlo® MicroShunts or IOLs

Isolated MΦ were thawed and seeded in 24-well tissue culture test plates (TPP Techno Plastic Products AG, Sigma-Aldrich, Germany) directly onto the PreserFlo® or IOLs. PreserFlo® devices were secured to the well plate using a surgical needle to prevent floating during culture. IOLs were placed at the bottom of the well. The cells were cultivated in 24-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells/2 mL in X-Vivo medium™ 15 (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Germany) with the following supplements: 20% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco), 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco), and 50 ng/mL human recombinant macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF) (PeproTech, London, UK). The cells were then incubated without medium exchange at a temperature of 37 °C and 5% CO2. Following a period of 35 days of cultivation, the cells exhibited the characteristic morphology of MΦ, characterized by their flattened adherence and irregular shape.

MΦ quantification on IOLs and PreserFlo® via MTT assay

The viability and adhesion of cells on PreserFlo® devices or IOLs were evaluated using the MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) assay. MTT reagent was added to the cell cultures at a final concentration of 0.5 mg/mL to visualize and quantify all metabolically active, viable cells. Following the incubation period, viable cells were stained blue, and the presence of visible cells on the IOLs or PreserFlo® devices was documented through photographic evidence. This photographic documentation was subsequently subjected to manual enumeration. For PreserFlo® MicroShunts, cell adhesion was quantified by counting MΦ in one field of view (0.4 mm2) on the shunt’s surface and in four additional fields on the adjacent well plate bottom. For IOLs, two fields of view (each 0.4 mm2) were analyzed on the IOL surface near the edge, and two fields were counted on the surrounding well plate bottom.

Statistics

Statistical analysis was performed using MedCalc Statistical Software (Version 19.3.1).

Normality of the cell adhesion ratios was assessed via the Shapiro–Wilk Test. Since the ratio data were non-normally distributed (p < 0.05), differences among the three independent implant groups (PreserFlo®, PMMA, and acrylic IOL) were analyzed using the Kruskal–Wallis H Test.

Where a significant overall difference was detected (p < 0.05), specific pairwise comparisons were executed using Conover’s Test. Resulting p-values were adjusted via the Holm correction to control the family-wise error rate (FWER) at α = 0.05. Descriptive results are reported as Median and Interquartile Range (IQR).
