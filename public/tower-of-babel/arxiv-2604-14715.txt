Uniform volume estimates and maximal functions on generalized Heisenberg-type groups
Cheng Bi, Hong-Quan Li

Abstract
. On generalized Heisenberg-type groups 𝔾 ⁡ ( 2 ​ n , m , 𝕌 , 𝕎 ) \mathbb{G}(2n,m,\mathbb{U},\mathbb{W}) , we give uniform volume estimates for the ball defined by a large class of Carnot-Carathéodory distances, and establish weak (1, 1) O ⁡ ( C m ​ n ) O(C^{m}\,n) -estimates for associated centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal functions, extending the results in [ 3 ] . As a by-product, we establish uniformly volume doubling property on Heisenberg groups for a class of left-invariant Riemannian metrics.

1.
Introduction

Recently, the phenomenon of uniformly volume doubling was studied by N. Eldredge, M. Gordina and L. Saloff-Coste
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
. More precisely, let
\mathbb{X}
be a finite-dimensional connected real Lie group. For a left-invariant Riemannian metric
\mathbf{g}
on
\mathbb{X}
, denote by
d_{\mathbf{g}}
the induced Riemannian distance, by
\nu_{\mathbf{g}}
the corresponding Riemannian measure and by
B_{\mathbf{g}}(z,r)
the open ball centered at
z\in\mathbb{X}
with radius
r>0
. The doubling constant
D_{\mathbf{g}}
is defined by

D_{\mathbf{g}}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{X},\,r>0}\frac{\nu_{\mathbf{g}}(B_{\mathbf{g}}(z,2r))}{\nu_{\mathbf{g}}(B_{\mathbf{g}}(z,r))}.

We say
\mathbb{X}
is
uniformly doubling
if

D(\mathbb{X}):=\sup_{\mathbf{g}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{X}}}D_{\mathbf{g}}<\infty,
where
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{X}}
is the set of all left-invariant Riemannian metrics on
\mathbb{X}
.
As noted in
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
, this property is particularly significant, as it implies a series of analytical consequences such as a uniform Poincaré inequality
[
5
, Theorem 8.2]
and uniform two-sided heat kernel estimates
[
5
, Theorem 8.10]
.

As noted in
[
5
, p. 1324]
, the Euclidean spaces and the tori are uniformly doubling. Besides, the authors of
[
5
]
conjectured that every connected real compact Lie group is uniformly doubling. They proved this conjecture for the three-dimensional special unitary group SU(2)
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
as well as the quotient groups of SU(2)
\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(including non-compact ones)
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, there
are
no other nontrivial examples for which the conjecture holds.

A natural question is whether the famous Heisenberg groups are uniformly doubling. These groups are non-compact and do not admit uniform lower and upper bounds on the Ricci curvatures (see e.g.
[
14
]
) even within the class of the left-invariant Riemmannian metrics considered in this work. Therefore, the celebrated Bishop-Gromov volume comparison theorem is not applicable. In this paper, we provide a partial answer to this question by establishing the uniform doubling property for very natural left-invariant Riemannian metrics. Indeed, in the broader setting of generalized H-type groups (see Section
2.2
), we derive explicit formulas describing the volume growth of balls defined by a large family of Carnot-Carathéodory distances; see Theorem
2.2
below.

With volume estimates in hand, we proceed to investigate an extension of Stein-Strömberg’s
O(n)
bound
[
16
]
on the centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal functions. Our results are Theorems
2.7
and
4.8
, which also extend the corresponding results in
[
3
]
. For other related works, we refer to
[
9
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
15
]
.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some basic facts and state our main results. In Section
3
, we derive uniform volume estimates. As an application, we study the weak (1, 1) estimates for the associated maximal functions in Section
4
.

2.2.
Generalized H-type groups

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}=\{1,2,\cdots\}
and
\mathbb{U}=\{U^{(1)},\cdots,U^{(m)}\}
be an
m
-tuple of linearly independent
(2n)\times(2n)
skew-symmetric real matrices. Let
\mathbb{W}
be a positive definite real matrix such that

U(\lambda)\,U(\lambda^{\prime})+U(\lambda^{\prime})\,U(\lambda)=2\lambda\cdot\lambda^{\prime}\,\mathbb{W}^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},

where
U(\lambda)=\mathrm{i}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\lambda_{l}\,U^{(l)}
for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}).

The generalized H-type groups
{\mathbb{G}}=\mathbb{G}(2n,m,\mathbb{U},\mathbb{W})
can be identical to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
endowed with the group law

\displaystyle(x,t)\cdot(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}):=\left(x+x^{\prime},\ t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathbb{U}x,x^{\prime}\rangle_{\star}\right),\ x,x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n},\ t,t^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},

\displaystyle\mbox{with}\ \langle\mathbb{U}x,x^{\prime}\rangle_{\star}=\left(\langle{U}^{(1)}x,x^{\prime}\rangle,\,\cdots,\,\langle{U}^{(m)}x,x^{\prime}\rangle\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}.

Note that the classical H-type group
\mathbb{H}(2n,m)
is the special case where
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{I}_{2n}
, and the generalized Heisenberg group corresponds to the case
m=1
.

Set
U^{(l)}=\left(U^{(l)}_{\iota,q}\right)_{1\leq\iota,q\leq 2n}
for
1\leq l\leq m
. The corresponding left invariant vector fields (namely the canonical basis) are defined by

\mathrm{X}_{q}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{q}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{\iota=1}^{2n}U^{(l)}_{q,\iota}x_{\iota}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{l}}\right),\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq q\leq 2n.

The canonical sub-Laplacian is
\Delta:=\sum_{q=1}^{2n}\mathrm{X}_{q}^{2}
.
We shall also consider the following general sub-Laplacian

\Delta_{\mathbb{B}}=\Delta_{\mathbb{G}(2n,m,\mathbb{U},\mathbb{W}),\mathbb{B}}:=\Delta+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathbb{B}_{i,l}\mathrm{T}_{l}\right)^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{T}_{l}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{l}}\ (1\leq l\leq m),

where
\mathbb{B}=(\mathbb{B}_{i,l})
is an
m\times m
real matrix. Note that the cases
\mathbb{B}=0
and
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{I}_{m}
correspond to the canonical sub-Laplacian and the full Laplacian, respectively.

Let
g=(x,t)\in{\mathbb{G}}
and
o=(0,0)
be the identity element of
{\mathbb{G}}
. The Haar measure
dg
on
{\mathbb{G}}
coincides with the
(2n+m)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Denote by
d_{\mathbb{B}}=d_{\mathbb{W},\mathbb{B}}
the left-invariant Carnot-Carathéodory distance associated to
\Delta_{\mathbb{B}}
, cf. e.g.
[
17
, III 4]
. By the left-translation invariance, we set in the sequel
d_{\mathbb{B}}(g):=d_{\mathbb{B}}(g,o)
.

2.3.
Explicit expression of
d_{\mathbb{B}}(g)

Let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}.
Up to an orthogonal transformation w.r.t
x
, we may assume

(2.1)

\mathbb{W}=\mbox{diag}\left\{a_{1}\mathbb{I}_{2k_{1}},\,\cdots,\,a_{\ell}\,\mathbb{I}_{2k_{\ell}}\right\},\ \mbox{with}\ 0<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{\ell},\ \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}=n,\ k_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}.

The case of
\ell=1
corresponds to the classical H-type group, the result for which can be found in
[
3
]
. In the rest of this paper, we always assume
\ell\geq 2
.
It follows from the Radon-Hurwitz number that:

(2.2)

m\leq 2\min_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\log_{2}(4k_{j}),\hskip 20.00003ptm+1\leq\min_{1\leq j\leq\ell}2k_{j},

which will be used repeatedly. Similarly, we may suppose in addition:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}:=\mathbb{B}^{T}\,\mathbb{B}=\mbox{diag}\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{m}\}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with all $b_{j}\geq 0$.}

In view of (
2.1
), we write in the sequel

x=\left(x_{(1)},\cdots,x_{(\ell)}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002ptx_{(j)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2k_{j}},\hskip 10.00002ptj=1,\cdots,\ell.

As in
[
1
]
and
[
4
, chapter 10]
, the convolution kernel of
e^{h\Delta_{\mathbb{B}}}
(
h>0
) is given by:

(2.4)

p_{h}(g)=\frac{h^{-n-m}}{(2\pi)^{m}(4\pi)^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\mathbf{V}(\lambda)\exp\left(-\frac{\Phi(g;\lambda)}{4h}\right)\,d\lambda,

where

\mathbf{V}(\lambda)=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{j}|\lambda|}{\sinh(a_{j}|\lambda|)}\right)^{k_{j}},\ \Phi(g;\lambda)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}|x_{(j)}|^{2}a_{j}|\lambda|\coth(a_{j}|\lambda|)-4\mathrm{i}t\cdot\lambda+4\lambda^{T}\mathbb{A}\lambda.

Set for
-\pi<r<\pi
(cf. e.g.
[
11
, § 3]
)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mu(r):=-\frac{d}{dr}(r\,\cot{r})=\frac{2r-\sin(2r)}{2\sin^{2}r}=2\,r\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\Big((j\,\pi)^{2}-r^{2}\Big)^{-2},\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{\mu}(r):=\frac{\mu(r)}{4r}.

The former is a strictly increasing diffeomorphism from
(-\pi,\pi)
onto
{\mathbb{R}}
.

The following result can be readily deduced from
[
11
]
:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

(1) Let
\Omega_{*}:=B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\left(0,\pi/a_{\ell}\right)
. Then we have:

(2.6)

d_{\mathbb{B}}(g)^{2}=\sup_{\tau\in\Omega_{*}}\phi(g;\tau),\hskip 10.00002pt\phi(g;\lambda):=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(a_{j}|\lambda|)\cot{(a_{j}|\lambda|)}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}+4t\cdot\lambda-4\lambda^{T}{\mathbb{A}}\lambda.

(2) Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with
x_{(\ell)}\neq 0
. Then the following map
\mathbf{F}_{x}
is a
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphism from
\Omega_{*}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,

(2.7)

t_{l}=\theta_{l}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,\widetilde{\mu}(a_{j}|\theta|)\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}+2b_{l}\right),\hskip 10.00002ptl=1,\cdots,m.

Furthermore, it holds for any
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{m})\in\Omega_{*}
that

(2.8)

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{B}}(x,\mathbf{F}_{x}(\theta))^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{j}\,|\theta|}{\sin{(a_{j}\,|\theta|)}}\right)^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}+4\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}\theta_{k}^{2}.

In what follows, we may assume that
x_{(\ell)}\neq 0
.

We introduce the following constants which will be used throughout this paper.

(2.9)

K:=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,k_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{Tr}(\mathbb{W}^{2})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}:=\frac{K}{n}\,(<a_{\ell}^{2}),

\mathbb{W}^{2}

for all

Theorem 2.2
.

We have uniformly in
r>0
that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|B_{\mathbb{B}}(o,r)|\sim|B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+m}}(0,r)|\ \det\left(\frac{r^{2}\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}}{12}\,\mathbb{I}_{m}+\mathbb{B}^{T}\mathbb{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Consequently,

(2.11)

\sup_{g\in{\mathbb{G}},\,r>0,\,\mathbb{B}}\frac{|B_{\mathbb{B}}(g,2r)|}{|B_{\mathbb{B}}(g,r)|}\lesssim 2^{2(n+m)}.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that

is exactly the dimension at infinity of
\mathbb{G}
.

As a direct consequence, we obtain:

Corollary 2.4
.

In the setting of Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}(2n,1)
, the uniformly volume doubling property is valid for the following natural Laplacians:

\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\widetilde{\mathrm{X}}_{j}^{2}+c^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with $c>0$ and $\{\widetilde{\mathrm{X}}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2n}$ a basis of $\mathrm{span}\{\mathrm{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{X}_{2n}\}$.}

Remark 2.5
.

We will show in a future work that all step-two Carnot groups of corank
1
admit the uniformly doubling property. In particular, the extra condition “natural Laplacians” in Corollary
2.4
can be removed.

inequality for the centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal function

\displaystyle M_{\mathbb{B}}f(z)=\sup_{r>0}\,\fint_{B_{\mathbb{B}}(z,r)}|f(g)|\,dg,\hskip 10.00002ptz\in\mathbb{G},

If Assumption

C>0

\|M_{\mathbb{B}}\|_{L^{1}\longrightarrow L^{1,\infty}}\leq C\left(\frac{3\,a_{\ell}^{2}}{2\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}n.

In the special case of H-type groups, where

It follows from (

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

The goal of this section is to establish volume estimates (
2.10
), whose strategy follows the spirit of
[
3
, Lemma 5.3]
. In our setting, however, some significant modifications are necessary due to the lack of symmetry w.r.t.
x
. Specifically, in computing the main contribution, we are faced with a more complicated integral estimate after making use of the diffeomorphism defined by (
2.7
). A new inequality (Lemma
3.2

3.1.
Auxiliary estimates

We present several elementary inequalities based on the Stirling’s formula (see (
3.3
) below).

Let
B_{R}:=B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}(0,R)
for
R>0
, and
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
denote the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
. Recall that its area is
|\mathbb{S}^{k-1}|=k\,|B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}(0,1)|=2\pi^{\frac{k}{2}}\,{\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)}^{-1}
, where
\Gamma
denotes the usual gamma function.

Lemma 3.1
.

(i) We have, uniformly in
1\leq j\leq\ell
and
0\leq u\leq 100\min_{1\leq j\leq\ell}k_{j}^{1/2}
, that

\fint_{B_{1}}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2u}\,dx\sim\left(\frac{k_{j}}{n}\right)^{u}.

(ii)
Let
(n,m)
be as in (
2.1
)-(
2.2
), then
it holds, uniformly in
0\leq v\leq 10m
, that

(3.1)

\int_{(1+\frac{2m}{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}\,(1-r^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}r^{2n+v-1}\,dr\sim\int_{0}^{1}\,(1-r^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\,r^{2n+v-1}\,dr\sim\frac{\Gamma(\frac{m}{2}+1)}{n^{\frac{m}{2}+1}}.

Proof.

For (i), using polar coordinates in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}=\mathbb{R}^{2k_{j}}\times\mathbb{R}^{2(n-k_{j})}
, we get

\fint_{B_{1}}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2u}\,dx=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{2k_{j}-1}|\cdot|\mathbb{S}^{2(n-k_{j})-1}|}{|B_{1}|}\int_{\mathop{r^{2}+s^{2}<1}\limits_{r>0,s>0}}r^{2(u+k_{j})-1}s^{2(n-k_{j})-1}\,dr\,ds.

Notice that for
\alpha>-1
and
\beta>-1
(cf. e.g.
[
7
, § 4.635]
),

(3.2)

\int_{\mathop{r^{2}+s^{2}<1}\limits_{r>0,s>0}}r^{\alpha}s^{\beta}\,drds=\frac{1}{\beta+1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha}\,(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta+1}{2}}\,dr=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha+1}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{\beta+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+2)}.

Then we have

\fint_{B_{1}}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2u}\,dx=\frac{\Gamma(u+k_{j})}{\Gamma(k_{j})}\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(u+n+1)}.

Now we use the Stirling’s formula (see e.g.
[
7
, § 8.327.1]
)

(3.3)

\Gamma(r+1)\sim\left(\frac{r}{e}\right)^{r+\frac{1}{2}},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,r\geq 1,

as well as
\Gamma(r+1)=r\,\Gamma(r)
whenever
r>0
to get that

\frac{\Gamma(u+k_{j})}{\Gamma(k_{j})}\sim\frac{\Gamma(u+k_{j}+1)}{\Gamma(k_{j}+1)}\sim e^{-u}\frac{(u+k_{j})^{u+k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}}}{k_{j}^{k_{j}+\frac{1}{2}}}=k_{j}^{u}\,e^{(u+k_{j}+\frac{1}{2})\ln{(1+\frac{u}{k_{j}})}-u}\sim k_{j}^{u},

which holds uniformly in
1\leq j\leq\ell
and
0\leq u\leq 100\min_{1\leq j\leq\ell}k_{j}^{1/2}
.
Similarly, we have
\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(u+n+1)}\sim n^{-u}
. This finishes the proof of (i).

Now we prove (ii). Let us begin with the case where
n\lesssim 1
. Then it follows from (
2.2
) that
m\lesssim 1
, and (
3.1
) is trivial.

In the opposite case where
n
is large enough, so
n>2(1+m)
. In this case, the second “
\sim
” is due to (
3.2
) as well as the Stirling’s formula (
3.3
).

Aiming now at the first “
\sim
” in (
3.1
). “
\leq
” is trivial. For “
\gtrsim
”, using the change of variables
s=1-r^{2}
, we obtain

I_{m,n}:=\int_{(1+\frac{2m}{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}\,(1-r^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}r^{2n+v-1}\,dr=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{2m}{n+2m}}(1-s)^{n+\frac{v}{2}-1}\,s^{\frac{m}{2}}\,ds.

By (
2.2
) and Taylor’s formula
\log(1-s)=-s+O(s^{2})
at
0
, it holds that

(1-s)^{n+\frac{v}{2}-1}=e^{(n+\frac{v}{2}-1)\log(1-s)}\sim e^{-(n+\frac{v}{2}-1)s},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,0<s<\frac{2m}{n+2m}.

Thus,

I_{m,n}\sim\int_{0}^{\frac{2m}{n+2m}}e^{-(n+\frac{v}{2}-1)s}s^{\frac{m}{2}}\,ds=(n+\tfrac{v}{2}+1)^{-\frac{m}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{m\,\frac{2n+v-2}{n+2m}}e^{-\mathrm{r}}\,\mathrm{r}^{\frac{m}{2}}\,d\mathrm{r}.

Note that
(n+\frac{v}{2}+1)^{-\frac{m}{2}-1}\sim n^{-\frac{m}{2}-1}
by (
2.2
). And
n>2(1+m)
gives
\frac{2n+v-2}{n+2m}>1
.
In view of the second “
\sim
” in (
3.1
), it remains to show that:

(3.4)

\int_{0}^{m}s^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-s}\,ds\sim\Gamma(\tfrac{m}{2}+1),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,m\geq 1.

Indeed it is trivial for
m\lesssim 1
. For
m
large enough, the standard trick of

implies that

\int_{m}^{\infty}s^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-s}\,ds\leq e^{-\frac{m}{2}}\int_{m}^{\infty}s^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\,ds=2\left(\frac{2}{e}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}\int_{\frac{m}{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{r}^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\mathrm{r}}\,d\mathrm{r}<\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{r}^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\mathrm{r}}\,d\mathrm{r}.

Then we also get (
3.4
).
∎

3.2.
A useful Lemma

For
R>0
and
\nu\geq 0
, we set

(3.5)

\mathbf{D}_{\nu}:=\fint_{B_{1}}\,\left|\mathbb{W}x\right|^{\nu}dx=\fint_{B_{1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\nu}{2}}dx.

\mathbf{D}_{\nu}

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell},\nu)

Lemma 3.2
.

With the dimension restriction
(n,m)
in (
2.1
)-(
2.2
), and the constant
\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}
defined by (
2.9
),
we have uniformly in
1\leq\nu\leq 10m

\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\,\sim\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{\,\frac{\nu}{2}}.

Proof.

First of all, from the symmetry, we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{1}}x_{q}^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{2n}\fint_{B_{1}}|x|^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{2n+2}\ (q=1,\ldots,2n),\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{so}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{D}_{2}=\frac{n}{n+1}\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}.

Next we prove
\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\gtrsim\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{\,\frac{\nu}{2}}
. If
10m\geq\nu\geq 2
, Hölder’s inequality gives
\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\geq\mathbf{D}_{2}^{\,\frac{\nu}{2}}\sim\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{\frac{\nu}{2}}
in view of the dimensional restriction (
2.2
) and the Stirling’s formula.
In the opposite case where
1\leq\nu<2
, reverse Minkowski inequality implies that:

|B_{1}|\,\mathbf{D}_{\nu}=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\nu}{2}}(B_{1})}^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\geq\ \left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,\left\||x_{(j)}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\nu}{2}}(B_{1})}\right)^{\frac{\nu}{2}}.

Then we apply Lemma
3.1
(i) to get the desired estimate.

Aim now at the upper bound
\mathbf{D}_{\nu}\lesssim\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{\,\frac{\nu}{2}}
. It follows from Hölder’s inequality that
\mathbf{D}_{\nu}^{2}\leq\mathbf{D}_{2\nu}
. Then it suffices to show

\mathbf{D}_{2\nu}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbb{W}^{2}\right)^{-\nu}=\fint_{B_{1}}\,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{a_{j}^{2}}{\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbb{W}^{2}\right)}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\right)^{\nu}dx\,\lesssim\,(2n)^{-\nu}.

Consider the following function
\mathfrak{F}
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{\ell}=[0,\infty)^{\ell}
,

w=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{\ell})\,\longmapsto\mathfrak{F}(w)=\fint_{B_{1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}w_{j}|x_{(j)}|^{2}\right)^{\nu}dx.

It is convex because
\nu\geq 1
. We have

\mathbf{D}_{2\nu}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbb{W}^{2}\right)^{-\nu}\leq\max_{w\in\mathscr{K}}\mathfrak{F}(w)\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \mathscr{K}=\left\{q\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{\ell}:\ \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}2k_{j}q_{j}=1\right\}.

The maximum is attained at some extreme point of
\mathscr{K}
, in other words,

\mathbf{D}_{2\nu}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbb{W}^{2}\right)^{-\nu}\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\ \frac{1}{(2k_{j})^{\nu}}\fint_{B_{1}}|x_{(j)}|^{2\nu}dx\,\sim\,(2n)^{-\nu}

by means of Lemma
3.1
(i), which finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

We have uniformly in
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{m})\in[0,\infty)^{m}
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1
that

\int_{B_{1}}\,\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}+\beta_{l}\right)^{\alpha}\,dx\sim|B_{1}|\,\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+\beta_{l}\right)^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
d_{l}=d_{l}(\boldsymbol{\beta},m)\geq 0\,(1\leq l\leq m)
be determined by

(3.7)

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(u^{2}+\beta_{l}\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{m}d_{l}\,u^{2l},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,u\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Combining this with Hölder inequality, we obtain that:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}+\beta_{l}\right)^{\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq|B_{1}|^{1-\alpha}\left(\int_{B_{1}}\prod_{l=1}^{m}(|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}+\beta_{l})\,dx\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=|B_{1}|^{1-\alpha}\left(\int_{B_{1}}\sum_{l=0}^{m}d_{l}\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2l}\,dx\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim|B_{1}|\,\left(\sum_{l=0}^{m}d_{l}\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{l}\right)^{\alpha}=|B_{1}|\,\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+\beta_{l}\right)^{\alpha},

where we have used Lemma
3.2
in “
\lesssim
”.

Similarly, together (
3.7
) with reverse Minkowski inequality, we have that:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}+\beta_{l}\right)^{\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sum_{l=0}^{m}d_{l}\,\||\mathbb{W}x|^{2l}\|_{L^{\alpha}(B_{1})}\right)^{\alpha}\gtrsim|B_{1}|\,\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+\beta_{l}\right)^{\alpha},

which completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
The first step of proof of Theorem
2.2

Inspired by
[
3
, Lemma 5.3]
and Theorem
2.1
, in
(x,\theta)
-coordinates, the main contribution of the volume comes from the region
|x|\approx R
and
|\theta|\ll 1
. We will introduce a family of ellipsoidal surfaces which enables us to appropriately partition the region. Moreover,
the condition (
2.2
) and Stirling’s formula will be used repeatedly without mention.

Recall that
a_{\ell}=1
and
K
is defined by (
2.9
). For
R>0
and
\delta\in(0,\pi)
, we define

(3.8)

S_{\delta,R}:=\left\{x\in B_{R}\,:\,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}|x_{(j)}|^{2}\left(\frac{a_{j}\delta}{\sin{(a_{j}\delta})}\right)^{2}=R^{2}\right\}.

We have the following observations. First,

S_{\delta_{1},R}\cap S_{\delta_{2},R}=\emptyset
provided
\delta_{1}\neq\delta_{2}
. Second,
x\in S_{\delta,R}
if and only if
x/R\in S_{\delta,1}
. Third,

B_{R}\backslash\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}:x_{(\ell)}=0\right\}\subset\bigcup_{0<\delta<\pi}S_{\delta,R}\subset B_{R}.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{x,R}:=\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}:d_{\mathbb{B}}(x,t)<R\}
, and then we can write

(3.9)

\displaystyle|B_{\mathbb{B}}(o,R)|=\int_{\Sigma_{1}(R)}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{x,R}}\,dt\,dx+\int_{\Sigma_{2}(R)}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{x,R}}\,dt\,dx=:\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{1}}+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{2}},

where we set

(3.10)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}(R):=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{0<\delta<\sqrt{\varrho}}S_{\delta,R},\hskip 20.00003pt\Sigma_{2}(R):=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{\sqrt{\varrho}<\delta<\pi}S_{\delta,R},

while we can see from the following proof that it is enough to take

(3.11)

\varrho:=\min\{\varepsilon,k_{\ell}^{-\frac{3}{4}}\},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with an \ }\varepsilon>0\ \mbox{sufficiently small.}

3.4.
Bounds of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{1}}

Our goal is to show that
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{1}}
admits the desired estimates, i.e.

(3.12)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{1}}\sim|B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+m}}(0,R)|\ \det\left(\frac{R^{2}\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}}{12}\,\mathbb{I}_{m}+\mathbb{B}^{T}\mathbb{B}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To do this, by slightly modifying the arguments in
[
3
, p. 3788]
, the problem can be reduced to establishing the estimate (
3.17
) below.
In brief,
due to
the fact that
(\frac{r}{\sin r})^{2}=1+\frac{r^{2}}{3}+O(r^{4})
for
r
near
0
, there exist constants
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
such that

(3.13)

\int_{0<R^{2}-|x|^{2}<c_{1}\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}}|\mathcal{B}_{x,R}|\,dx\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{1}}\leq\int_{0<R^{2}-|x|^{2}<c_{2}\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}}|\mathcal{B}_{x,R}|\,dx.

For
R^{2}-|x|^{2}<c_{2}\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}
, we estimate the volume of
\mathcal{B}_{x,R}
by means of the diffeomorphism
\mathbf{F}_{x}
defined by (
2.7
). The key point is that, in our setting, it follows from
[
2
, Theorem VI.7.1]
that the Jacobian determinant of
\mathbf{F}_{x}
satisfies

(3.14)

\displaystyle\det\big(D\mathbf{F}_{x}(\theta)\big)\sim\det\big(D\mathbf{F}_{x}(0)\big)=6^{-m}\,\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\left|\mathbb{W}x\right|^{2}+12b_{j}\right).

And a direct calculation yields that

(3.15)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{x,R}|\sim C_{m}\,(R^{2}-|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\left|\mathbb{W}x\right|^{2}+12b_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ C_{m}=\left(\frac{1}{12}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{m-1}|}{m}.

By (
3.13
), a scaling gives

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{1}}\sim C_{m}\,R^{2n+2m}\ \mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{B}},

with (for simplicity, we can omit the constant
c_{i}
)

(3.16)

\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{B}}=\int_{1-|x|^{2}<\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}+\frac{12b_{j}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dx.

Therefore, to prove (
3.12
), it remains to show

(3.17)

\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{B}}\sim\frac{\Gamma(\frac{m}{2}+1)}{n^{\frac{m}{2}+1}}\,|\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}|\prod_{j=0}^{m}\left(\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+\frac{12b_{j}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=:E_{m,n}\prod_{j=0}^{m}\left(\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+\frac{12b_{j}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for which we need a lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\varrho
be as in (
3.11
). We have, uniformly in
j\in\{0,1,\cdots,m\}
, that

(3.18)

\mathbf{M}_{0,j}:=\int_{1-|x|^{2}<\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{j}\,dx\sim E_{m,n}\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{\frac{j}{2}},

where the implicit constant may depend on
\varepsilon
in (
3.11
).

Proof.

Let
d\sigma
be the standard surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}
. Using polar coordinates, we obtain

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbf{D}_{j}=\frac{1}{(2n+j)\,|B_{1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}}|\mathbb{W}w|^{j}\,d\sigma(w),\quad\mbox{and}\\
&\mathbf{M}_{0,j}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}}|\mathbb{W}w|^{j}\left(\int_{(1+\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}w|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}\,(1-r^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}r^{2n+j-1}\,dr\right)d\sigma(w).\end{split}

Thus, Lemma
3.2
gives

(3.20)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}}|\mathbb{W}w|^{j}\,d\sigma(w)\sim\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{\frac{j}{2}}\,(2n+j)\,|B_{1}|\sim\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{\frac{j}{2}}\,|\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}|.

Combining this with (
3.1
), we can easily get the desired upper bound in (
3.18
).

For the lower bound, notice that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{M}_{0,j}\geq&\int_{\mathcal{E}}|\mathbb{W}w|^{j}\left(\int_{(1+\kappa_{0})^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}\,(1-r^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}r^{2n+j-1}\,dr\right)d\sigma(w),\\
&\mbox{where}\quad\mathcal{E}=\{w\in\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}:\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}w|^{2}\geq\kappa_{0}\},\end{split}

for some parameter
\kappa_{0}>0
to be chosen momentarily. We consider two cases
k_{\ell}\gg 1
and
k_{\ell}\lesssim 1
.

Case I.
k_{\ell}\gg 1
.
In such case, we have
\varrho=k_{\ell}^{-\frac{3}{4}}\ll 1
. Choosing
\kappa_{0}=2\,m\,n^{-1}
such that
\mathcal{E}\neq\emptyset
.

Let us begin with the case where
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. It follows from (
3.1
) that:

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{0,j}\gtrsim\frac{\Gamma(\frac{m}{2}+1)}{n^{\frac{m}{2}+1}}\int_{\mathcal{E}}\,|\mathbb{W}w|^{j}\,d\sigma(w)\sim\frac{\Gamma(\frac{m}{2}+1)}{n^{\frac{m}{2}+1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}}\,|\mathbb{W}w|^{j}\,d\sigma(w)\sim E_{m,n}\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{\frac{j}{2}},

where the first “
\sim
” is due to the following inequality

(3.23)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}\backslash\mathcal{E}}\,|\mathbb{W}w|^{j}\,d\sigma(w)\leq\left(\varrho^{-1}\kappa_{0}\right)^{\frac{j}{2}}|\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}\backslash\mathcal{E}|\ll\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}}\,|\mathbb{W}w|^{j}\,d\sigma(w),

while “
\ll
” in (
3.23
)
comes from the fact that
\varrho^{-1}\kappa_{0}\ll\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}
by (
2.2
).

For the opposite case
j=0
.
It suffices to show

II:=\int_{1-|x|^{2}\,\geq\,\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\,dx\ll E_{m,n}.

In fact, Hölder’s inequality implies that:

II\leq\left(\int_{B_{1}}(1-|x|^{2})^{m}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{1-|x|^{2}\,\geq\,\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By polar coordinates and applying the second “
\sim
” in (
3.1
) with
2m
instead of
m
, the first term in the right hand side has order
E_{2m,n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\left(\frac{\Gamma(m+1)}{n^{m}}\,|B_{1}|\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For the second one, a simple calculation shows that it equals

(3.24)

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}\log(1+a_{j}^{2}\,\varrho)\right\}\,|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{4}\varrho\,k_{\ell}}\,|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies immediately the desired lower bound for
k_{\ell}\gg 1
.

Case II.
k_{\ell}\lesssim 1
.
In such case, we have
\varrho=\varepsilon\ll 1
and take
\kappa_{0}=\varepsilon^{2}n^{-1}
.
Since
m\lesssim k_{\ell}\lesssim 1
, estimates of type (
3.1
) (with
2mn^{-1}
replaced by
\varepsilon^{2}n^{-1}
) still holds, with implicit constants depending on
\varepsilon
. The proof in
Case I
involving (
3.22
)-(
3.23
) can be applied here. Indeed, if
j\geq 1
, we use the fact
\varrho^{-1}\kappa_{0}\ll\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}
to get (
3.23
); if
j=0
, the proof can be simplified, for which we obtain (
3.23
) via polar coordinates.
∎

Now we are in a position to prove (
3.17
).
Note that if
\mathbb{B}=0
, we have
\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{B}}=\mathbf{M}_{0,m}
and Lemma
3.4
applies.
Thus, we are left to prove the case
\mathbb{B}\neq 0
.

Let us begin with the lower bound. Similar to (
3.7
). Let
d_{j}:=d_{j}(R,{\mathbb{A}})\geq 0
(
0\leq j\leq m
) denote the coefficients determined by

(3.25)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(u^{2}+\frac{12b_{j}}{R^{2}}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}d_{j}\,u^{2j},\hskip 20.00003ptu\in{\mathbb{R}}.

By the reverse Minkowski inequality, we get that

(3.26)

\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{B}}^{\,2}\,\geq\,\sum_{j=0}^{m}d_{j}\left(\int_{1-|x|^{2}<\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{j}\,dx\right)^{2},

which, together with Lemma
3.4
, implies that

\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{B}}\gtrsim E_{m,n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}d_{j}\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}^{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=E_{m,n}\prod_{j=0}^{m}\left(\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+\frac{12b_{j}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We turn to the upper bound of
\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{B}}
. In fact, using Lemma
3.4
with
j=0
and Hölder’s inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{B}}^{\,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim E_{m,n}\int_{1-|x|^{2}<\varrho\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}+\frac{12b_{j}}{R^{2}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim E_{m,n}^{2}\,\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+\frac{12b_{j}}{R^{2}}\right),

by repeating the above argument using (
3.25
). These estimates imply (
3.17
), which concludes the proof of (
3.12
).

3.5.
Estimations for
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{2}}

Let
b_{0}=0
. For any
0\leq l\leq m
and
x\in\mathop{\cup}\limits_{0<\delta<\pi}S_{\delta,R}
. There exists a unique

Using the inequality
\frac{r}{\sin r}\geq 1+\frac{r^{2}}{6}
for

(see
[
3

), we have

\widetilde{\theta}_{l}\leq\frac{1}{2}(R^{2}-|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{12}|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}+b_{l}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\hskip 20.00003pt0\leq l\leq m.

Let
g=(x,t)
with
x\in\mathop{\cup}\limits_{0<\delta<\pi}S_{\delta,R}
satisfying
d_{\mathbb{B}}(g)<R
and
x_{(\ell)}\neq 0
, and
\theta=\theta(g)
be as in (
2.7
).
Then by the expression of

(see (
2.8
)) and the monotonicity of the function
\frac{v}{\sin v}
on
(0,\pi)
, we know
|\theta|\leq\widetilde{\theta}_{0}
and
|\theta_{l}|\leq\widetilde{\theta}_{l}
(
1\leq l\leq m
).
Since
\widetilde{\mu}
is increasing on
(0,\ \pi)
(cf. (
2.5
)), we obtain that:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{x,R}|\leq\prod_{l=1}^{m}\widetilde{\theta}_{l}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\,\widetilde{\mu}(a_{j}\widetilde{\theta}_{0})+2b_{l}\right)\leq c^{m}\,(R^{2}-|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\,\mathcal{K}(x;\widetilde{\theta}_{0}(x)),

where

(3.28)

\mathcal{K}(x;r)=\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\,\widetilde{\mu}(a_{j}r)+b_{l}\right)\left(|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}+b_{l}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\hskip 20.00003ptr>0.

Plugging this estimate into
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{2}}
and using the scaling

, we deduce that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c^{m}R^{2n+m}\int_{\Sigma_{2}(1)}(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\,\mathcal{K}\left(Rx;\widetilde{\theta}_{0}(Rx)\right)\,dx=:c^{m}R^{2n+m}\mathbf{K}.

Next, in view of (
2.2
) and by considering separately the cases where
m\lesssim 1
and
m\gg 1
, we can choose
\gamma\in(1,2)
such that

(3.29)

2k_{\ell}>\gamma\,m+\frac{1}{2}.

For instance, we can take
\gamma=\frac{201}{200}
.
Then Hölder’s inequality gives

\mathbf{K}\leq\left(\int_{B_{1}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{m\gamma^{\prime}}{2}}\,dx\right)^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{2}(1)}\mathcal{K}\big(Rx;\widetilde{\theta}_{0}(Rx)\big)^{\gamma}\,dx\right)^{1/\gamma}=:{\mathbf{K}}_{1}\,{\mathbf{K}}_{2}.

First, by polar coordinates, we get

(3.30)

{\mathbf{K}}_{1}\lesssim c^{m}\,\Gamma(\tfrac{m}{2}+1)\,n^{-\frac{m}{2}}|B_{1}|^{1/\gamma^{\prime}}.

To estimate
{\mathbf{K}}_{2}
, we decompose the domain into annuli. More precisely,
let
\delta_{0}=\varrho^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\delta_{k}=\pi-2^{-k}
(
k\geq 1
), and then

{\mathbf{K}}_{2}^{\gamma}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\bigcup_{\delta_{k}<\delta<\delta_{k+1}}S_{\delta,1}}\mathcal{K}\big(Rx;\widetilde{\theta}_{0}(Rx)\big)^{\gamma}\,dx=:\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}J_{k}.

Since
\mathcal{K}(x;\cdot)>0
is increasing on
(0,\pi)
and
\widetilde{\theta}_{0}(Rx)=\delta
for
x\in S_{\delta,1}
, it follows that

\displaystyle J_{k}\leq\int_{\bigcup_{\delta>\delta_{k}}S_{\delta,1}}\mathcal{K}(Rx;\delta_{k+1})^{\gamma}\,dx=\int_{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}|x_{(j)}|^{2}\left(\frac{a_{j}\delta_{k}}{\sin{(a_{j}\delta_{k}})}\right)^{2}<1}\mathcal{K}(Rx;\delta_{k+1})^{\gamma}\,dx.

From (
2.5
) and the elementary equality

(3.31)

\displaystyle\frac{\sin{r}}{r}=\prod_{j=1}^{+\infty}\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{j^{2}\pi^{2}}\right),\hskip 20.00003ptr\in{\mathbb{R}},

we have uniformly in
j,k\geq 1
that:

(3.32)

\frac{\sin{(a_{j}\delta_{k})}}{a_{j}\delta_{k}}\geq\frac{\sin\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\left(\frac{\sin{(a_{j}\delta_{k})}}{a_{j}\delta_{k}}\right)^{2}\widetilde{\mu}(a_{j}\delta_{k+1})\lesssim 1.

Then

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}(R^{2}\,|\mathbb{W}x|^{2}+b_{l})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(\frac{\sin\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\right)^{-1}\left[R^{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\,\left(\frac{\delta_{k}}{\sin{\delta_{k}}}\right)^{2}+b_{l}\left(\frac{\delta_{k}}{\sin{\delta_{k}}}\right)^{2}\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\sin\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\right)^{-m}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(R^{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\,\left(\frac{a_{j}\delta_{k}}{\sin{(a_{j}\delta_{k})}}\right)^{2}+b_{l}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

and

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left[R^{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\,\widetilde{\mu}(a_{j}\delta_{k+1})+b_{l}\right]\leq C^{m}\,\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left[R^{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\,\left(\frac{a_{j}\delta_{k}}{\sin{(a_{j}\delta_{k})}}\right)^{2}+b_{l}\right].

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(Rx;\delta_{k+1})\leq C^{m}\,\left(\frac{\sin\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\right)^{-m}\,\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(R^{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\,\left(\frac{a_{j}\delta_{k}}{\sin{(a_{j}\delta_{k})}}\right)^{2}+b_{l}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now perform linear transform
\left\{y_{(j)}=\frac{a_{j}\delta_{k}}{\sin{(a_{j}\delta_{k})}}x_{(j)}\right\}_{j=1}^{\ell}
. This yields

J_{k}\leq c^{m}\left(\frac{\sin\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\left(\frac{\sin\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\right)^{2k_{\ell}-m\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{B_{1}}\,\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(R^{2}\,|\mathbb{W}y|^{2}+b_{l}\right)^{\gamma/2}\,dy.

By Corollary
3.3
, we have

\int_{B_{1}}\,\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(R^{2}\,|\mathbb{W}y|^{2}+b_{l}\right)^{\gamma/2}\,dy\sim|B_{1}|\,\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(R^{2}\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+b_{l}\right)^{\gamma/2}.

Moreover, by (
3.29
) and the elementary inequality
\frac{\sin r}{r}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{120}r^{2}}
whenever
r\in(0,\pi)
, we know

\displaystyle J_{k}

\displaystyle\leq C^{m}e^{-\frac{1}{120}(2k_{\ell}-m\gamma-\frac{1}{2})\varrho}|B_{1}|\left(\frac{\sin\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{L=1}^{m}\left(R^{2}\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+b_{L}\right)^{\gamma/2}.

Since
\frac{\sin\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\sim 2^{-k}
, summing in
k
gives

(3.33)

\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\gamma}\leq C^{m}e^{-\frac{1}{120}(2k_{\ell}-m\gamma-\frac{1}{2})\varrho}|B_{1}|\prod_{L=1}^{m}\left(R^{2}\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}+b_{L}\right)^{\gamma/2}.

Finally, for any constant
c_{0}>0
,
by considering separately the cases where
m\lesssim 1
and
m\gg 1
, one gets that there exists a constant
D(c_{0})
only depending on
c_{0}
(in particular, independent of
m
) such that
c_{0}^{m}e^{-\frac{1}{120}(2k_{\ell}-m\gamma-\frac{1}{2})\varrho}\lesssim D(c_{0}).

Combining (
3.30
) and (
3.33
), we conclude
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{2}}\lesssim\boldsymbol{\mathbf{O}_{1}}
. The proof of Theorem
2.2
is then finished.

4.
Maximal Function on
{\mathbb{G}}

As in
[
9
]
, it is enough to establish the following uniform estimates:

(4.1)

\big|B_{\mathbb{B}}(o,r)\big|^{-1}\chi_{B_{\mathbb{B}}(o,r)}(g)\lesssim n\left(\frac{3}{2\,\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}}}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}\fint_{0}^{\frac{r}{\sqrt{n}}}{P}_{h}(g)\,dh,\hskip 10.00002pta.e.\ g,\ \forall\,r>0,

where
P_{h}\ (h>0)
denotes the convolution kernel of
e^{-h\sqrt{-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}}}}
. With the uniform volume estimates in hand, it suffices to establish uniform asymptotic behaviour of
P_{h}
, see Proposition
4.7
below. To do this, we adapt the method used in
[
3
]
.

4.1.
Poisson Kernel

Set

(4.2)

\displaystyle\ N:=n+m+\frac{1}{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathfrak{c}_{j}:=\frac{k_{j}}{N},\hskip 10.00002ptj=1,\cdots,\ell-1,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}:=1-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\mathfrak{c}_{j}.

Using the subordinate formula, the Poisson kernel
P_{h}\ (h>0)
is given by:

(4.3)

P_{h}(g)=\frac{2^{m}\Gamma(N)}{\pi^{N}}h\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}Q_{h}(g;\lambda)\,d\lambda,

where

\displaystyle Q_{h}(g;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{V}(\lambda)\left(\Phi(g;\lambda)+h^{2}\right)^{-N}=\left(\frac{\sinh|\lambda|}{|\lambda|}\right)^{m+\frac{1}{2}}S_{h}(g;\lambda)^{-N},

\displaystyle S_{h}(g;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\left(\Phi(g;\lambda)+h^{2}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\sinh(a_{j}|\lambda|)}{a_{j}|\lambda|}\right)^{c_{j}},\hskip 10.00002pth>0.

We first introduce some notation which looks a little complicated due to the lack of symmetry.
For
0\leq r<\pi
, set in the sequel (cf. (
3.31
))

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda(r):=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\sin(a_{j}r)}{a_{j}r}\right)^{\mathfrak{c}_{j}},\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi(r):=\frac{d}{dr}\ln{\Lambda(r)}=-r\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{2a_{j}^{2}\mathfrak{c}_{j}}{(k\pi)^{2}-(a_{j}r)^{2}}.

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(r):=1+r\,\varphi(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathfrak{c}_{j}\,(a_{j}r)\,\cot{(a_{j}r)},\hskip 10.00002ptu(r):=-\frac{\varphi(r)}{r\,\mathcal{G}(r)}.

Moreover, for given
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
with
x_{(\ell)}\neq 0
and
h>0
, set for
0\leq r<\pi
and
\lambda\in B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}(0,\pi)
:

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}(r):=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(u(r)\left(\frac{a_{j}r}{\sin{(a_{j}r)}}\right)^{2}+\frac{a_{j}\mu(a_{j}r)}{r}\right)\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}+u(r)\,h^{2},\\
&\mathfrak{W}_{x,h}(\lambda):=\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}(|\lambda|)+4u(|\lambda|)\,\lambda^{T}\mathbb{A}\lambda,\quad\mathfrak{V}_{x,h}(\lambda):=\frac{\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}^{\prime}(|\lambda|)+4u^{\prime}(|\lambda|)\,\lambda^{T}\mathbb{A}\lambda}{|\lambda|}.\end{split}

Inspired by Theorem
2.1
, let us begin with the maximizer of the following crucial function defined on
B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}(0,\pi)
for given
g
and
h>0
:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{S}(\lambda)&\equiv\mathrm{S}_{\mathbb{A}}(g,h;\lambda):=S_{h}(g;\mathrm{i}\lambda)=\Lambda(|\lambda|)\left(\phi(g;\lambda)+h^{2}\right),\end{split}

Notice that
\mathrm{S}
has the form
f_{1}(|\lambda|)(f_{2}(\lambda)+4t\cdot\lambda)
, the following simple observation will be used:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}(B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}(0,\pi))
and
F_{t}(\lambda):=f_{1}(\lambda)\left(f_{2}(\lambda)+4t\cdot\lambda\right).
If
\lambda_{0}
is a critical point of
F_{t}
such that
f_{1}(\lambda_{0})\neq 0
and
f_{1}(\lambda_{0})+\lambda_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{1}(\lambda_{0})\neq 0
, then

(4.8)

4t=-\nabla f_{2}(\lambda_{0})-\nabla f_{1}(\lambda_{0})\,\frac{f_{2}(\lambda_{0})-\lambda_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{2}(\lambda_{0})}{f_{1}(\lambda_{0})+\lambda_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{1}(\lambda_{0})}.

Consequently,

(4.9)

F_{t}(\lambda_{0})=f_{1}(\lambda_{0})^{2}\,\frac{f_{2}(\lambda_{0})-\lambda_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{2}(\lambda_{0})}{f_{1}(\lambda_{0})+\lambda_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{1}(\lambda_{0})}.

Proof.

The equation
\nabla F_{t}(\lambda_{0})=0
gives

(4.10)

\nabla f_{1}(\lambda_{0})\left(f_{2}(\lambda_{0})+4t\cdot\lambda_{0}\right)+f_{1}(\lambda_{0})\left(\nabla f_{2}(\lambda_{0})+4t\right)=0.

By taking the inner product with
\lambda_{0}
on both sides of the last equality, we yield

(4.11)

\displaystyle\Big(f_{1}(\lambda_{0})+\lambda_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{1}(\lambda_{0})\Big)\,4t\cdot\lambda_{0}+\Big(\lambda_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{1}(\lambda_{0})\,f_{2}(\lambda_{0})+\lambda_{0}\cdot\nabla f_{2}(\lambda_{0})\,f_{1}(\lambda_{0})\Big)=0,

which allows us to determine
t\cdot\lambda_{0}
. Substituting it in (
4.10
), we get the desired results.
∎

4.2.
The Maximizer of
\mathrm{S}

The following claim can be easily proved in a more general setting that
(\mathbb{G},\Delta)
admit GM-property, by means of the operator convexity. However, we will provide an elementary proof, where we determine the maximizer by means of a diffeomorphism
\mathscr{C}_{x,h}
(see (
4.12
) below). This map is also useful in estimating the Poisson kernel; see Section
4.3
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The function
\mathrm{S}
possesses a unique maximizer in
B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}(0,\pi)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
with
x_{(\ell)}\neq 0
and
h>0
. We have

(1)
The unique zero
r_{*}
of
\mathcal{G}
in
(0,\ \pi)
satisfies
\frac{\pi}{2}\leq r_{*}<\pi
.
Moreover, the smooth functions
u
and
\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}
are positive and strictly increasing on
[0,\ r_{*})
.

(2) The following smooth map is a
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphism from
B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}(0,r_{*})
onto
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{x,h}(\lambda):=4^{-1}\,\Big(\mathfrak{W}_{x,h}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{I}_{m}+8\mathbb{A}\Big)\lambda.

Proof.

The claim
\frac{\pi}{2}\leq r_{*}<\pi
is clear by the fact that
\mathcal{G}
is strictly decreasing on
(0,\pi)
(cf. (
4.4
) and (
4.5
)) together with
\mathcal{G}(\tfrac{\pi}{2})\geq 0
and
\mathcal{G}(\pi^{-})=-\infty
. Moreover, (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) also imply that
u
is positive and strictly increasing on
[0,\ r_{*})
. The corresponding assertion for
\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}
follows from (
3.31
) and (
2.5
).

Aim now at the second assertion.
A direct calculation implies that the Jacobian matrix of
\mathscr{C}_{x,h}
equals
\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{J}(\lambda)
, where

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}(\lambda):=\mathbb{D}_{1}(\lambda)+\lambda\lambda^{T}\,\mathbb{D}_{2}(\lambda),

with

(4.14)

\mathbb{D}_{1}(\lambda)=\mathfrak{W}_{x,h}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{I}_{m}+8\mathbb{A},\ \ \mathbb{D}_{2}(\lambda)=\mathfrak{V}_{x,h}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{I}_{m}+8u(|\lambda|)\mathbb{A}.

Notice that
\mathbb{D}_{1}
and
\mathbb{D}_{2}
are real, diagonal matrices. Moreover
\mathbb{D}_{2}(\lambda)
is positive semi-definite, and the positive definite matrix
\mathbb{D}_{1}(\lambda)\geq\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}(0)\,\mathbb{I}_{m}>0
.
From Schur’s Lemma, we get that

\det\mathbb{J}(\lambda)=\big(1+\lambda^{T}\mathbb{D}_{2}(\lambda)\mathbb{D}_{1}(\lambda)^{-1}\lambda\big)\det\mathbb{D}_{1}(\lambda)>0.

By applying Hadamard’s theorem (cf. e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 6.2.8]
), it remains to show that
\mathscr{C}_{x,h}
is proper, which is clear by the fact that
\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}(r)\rightarrow+\infty
as
r\rightarrow r_{*}^{-}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

The existence of the maximizer is due to the facts that

\mathrm{S}(0)=|x|^{2}+h^{2}>0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{\ |\lambda|\rightarrow\pi^{-}}\mathrm{S}(\lambda)=-\infty.

Moreover,

\mathop{\mbox{lim}}\limits_{\ r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{d}{dr}\mathrm{S}(r\hat{t})=4|t|>0,

so
0
cannot be a maximizer.

Let
\tau
be a maximizer, and
\rho:=|\tau|\in(0,\pi)
. We first claim that
1+\rho\frac{\Lambda^{\prime}(\rho)}{\Lambda(\rho)}\neq 0
. Suppose the contrary.
Applying (
4.11
) with

f_{1}(\lambda)=\Lambda(\lambda),\hskip 10.00002ptf_{2}(\lambda)=\phi(g;\lambda)-4t\cdot\lambda+h^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{0}=\tau,

it follows from the elementary equality
r\cot{r}-r\frac{d}{dr}(r\cot{r})=(\frac{r}{\sin{r}})^{2}
that

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}|x_{(j)}|^{2}\left(\frac{a_{j}\rho}{\sin{(a_{j}\rho)}}\right)^{2}+4\tau^{T}\mathbb{A}\tau+h^{2}=0,

which leads to a contradiction.

Moreover it follows from (
4.9
) that

(4.15)

\displaystyle(0<)\,\mathrm{S}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda(\rho)}{1+\rho\,\varphi(\rho)}\left(h^{2}+4\tau^{T}\mathbb{A}\tau+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{j}\rho}{\sin{(a_{j}\rho)}}\right)^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\right).

Hence
1+\rho\,\varphi(\rho)>0
. Thus by (
4.8
), a direct computation shows that
t=\mathscr{C}_{x,h}(\tau)
. The desired conclusion then follows from Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Let
h>0
. In what follows, we always assume that

(4.16)

\displaystyle g\in\mathfrak{G}:=\left\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{G}}:x_{\iota},t_{l}\neq 0,\ \forall\,\iota,l\right\}.

Let
\tau_{h}=\tau_{h}(g)
denote the unique maximizer of
\mathrm{S}
in
B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}(0,\pi)
. Then we have

(4.17)

\displaystyle t=\mathscr{C}_{x,h}(\tau_{h})=4^{-1}\,\Big(\mathfrak{W}_{x,h}(|\tau_{h}|)\,\mathbb{I}_{m}+8\mathbb{A}\Big)\tau_{h},\hskip 10.00002pt0<\rho_{h}:=|\tau_{h}|<r_{*},

(4.18)

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})=\frac{\Lambda(\rho_{h})}{1+\rho_{h}\,\varphi(\rho_{h})}\left(h^{2}+4\tau_{h}^{T}\mathbb{A}\tau_{h}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{j}\rho_{h}}{\sin{(a_{j}\rho_{h})}}\right)^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\right).

We further provide some elementary properties of
\tau_{h}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The following statements hold uniformly:

\ \ \ (1)\ \phi(g;r\hat{\tau_{h}})\ \mbox{is increasing in}\ r\in[0,\rho_{h}];\ \ \ \,\;\,(2)\ d_{\mathbb{B}}(g)^{2}\lesssim\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})\leq\left(d_{\mathbb{B}}(g)^{2}+h^{2}\right)\Lambda(\rho_{h});

(3)\,(\pi-\rho_{h})\sim 1\mbox{\, if Assumption \ref{assumption} holds;}\hskip 10.00002pt\ (4)\ t_{l}^{2}\lesssim(b_{l}+\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h}))\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h}),\ 1\leq l\leq m.\,

Proof.

We first prove (1).
Notice that

\phi(g;r\hat{\tau_{h}}\,)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(a_{j}r)\cot{(a_{j}r)}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}+\frac{4r}{\rho_{h}}t\cdot\tau_{h}-\frac{4r^{2}}{\rho_{h}^{2}}\tau_{h}^{T}\mathbb{A}\tau_{h}.

From the definition of
\mu
in (
2.5
), it holds that
\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}\phi(g;r\tau_{h})\leq 0
. Hence we only need to show
\frac{d}{dr}\phi(g;r\hat{\tau_{h}})|_{r=\rho_{h}}\geq 0
, which can be checked directly by (
4.17
) and (
4.6
).

For (2), the upper bound comes from (
2.6
) and (
4.7
). For the lower bound, since
\tau_{h}
is the maximizer, for
\theta
as in (
2.7
) and
r_{0}>0
sufficiently small, we have

\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})\geq\mathrm{S}(r_{0}\theta)\gtrsim\theta^{T}\mathbb{A}\theta+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}|\theta|\Big(r_{0}\cot(a_{j}r_{0}|\theta|)+\mu(a_{j}|\theta|)\Big)|x_{(j)}|^{2}\sim d_{\mathbb{B}}(g)^{2},

by the facts that
r\cot r\sim 1+r\mu(r)\sim(\frac{r}{\sin r})^{2}\sim 1
near
r=0
, and
1+r\mu(r)\sim(\frac{r}{\sin r})^{2}
for
0<r<\pi
.

To prove (3), we show
(\pi-r_{*})\,\sim\,1
, where
r_{*}
is as in Lemma
4.3
. Since Assumption
2.6
implies
\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}\sim 1
, it suffices to notice that

\,r_{*}\cot r_{*}=-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\mathfrak{c}_{j}\,(a_{j}r_{*})\cot(a_{j}r_{*})\,\geq-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\mathfrak{c}_{j}=\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}-1}{\mathfrak{c}_{\ell}}.

The proof of (4) is similar to that of
[
3
, Lemma 5.4 (c)]
and is omitted here.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Lemma
4.4
(3) says that
\tau_{h}
is bounded away from
\partial B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}(0,\pi)
. This property is crucial for establishing uniform asymptotic behavior of Poisson kernel; see Proposition
4.7
. Recall that in the classical H-type groups, we have stronger result
|\tau_{h}|<\frac{\pi}{2}
; see
[
3
, Lemma 5.4 (
a
)]
. Moreover, Assumption
2.6
is in fact necessary. For example, take
\ell=2
,
0<a_{1}<\frac{1}{2}<1=a_{2}
.
Then
r_{*}\rightarrow\pi^{-}
as
\mathfrak{c}_{2}\rightarrow 0^{+}
, since
\mathcal{G}(r_{*})=0
implies

-\frac{\cot r_{*}}{\cot\left(a_{1}\,r_{*}\right)}=a_{1}\,\mathfrak{c}_{1}\,\mathfrak{c}_{2}^{-1}\rightarrow+\infty.

By (
4.17
), we know
\rho_{h}\rightarrow r_{*}^{-}
as
|t|\rightarrow+\infty
. Thus
\rho_{h}\rightarrow\pi^{-}
as
|t|\rightarrow+\infty
and
\mathfrak{c}_{2}\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

4.3.
The Hessian of
\mathrm{S}

Recall
\mathrm{S}(\cdot)=\mathrm{S}_{\mathbb{A}}(g,h\,;\cdot)
in (
4.7
). We compute the Hessian matrix of
\mathrm{S}
at
\tau_{h}
. By Lemma
4.3
, it holds that:

\nabla_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{S}_{\mathbb{A}}(x,\mathscr{C}_{x,h}(\lambda),h\,;\lambda)=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\lambda\in B_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}(0,r_{*}).

Let
\frac{\partial^{2}\mathrm{S}}{\partial\lambda\partial t}
be the mixed Hessian and
\mathbb{J}
be as in (
4.13
). The chain rule then gives that

\displaystyle\begin{split}-&\mbox{Hess}_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{S}_{\mathbb{A}}(x,\mathscr{C}_{x,h}(\lambda),h\,;\lambda)=4^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathrm{S}_{\mathbb{A}}}{\partial\lambda\partial t}(x,\mathscr{C}_{x,h}(\lambda),h\,;\lambda)\,\mathbb{J}(\lambda)\\
&=\left(\Lambda(|\lambda|)\,\mathbb{I}_{m}+\lambda\lambda^{T}\frac{\Lambda^{\prime}(|\lambda|)}{|\lambda|}\right)\mathbb{J}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{1}(\lambda)\,\mathbb{I}_{m}+8\Lambda(|\lambda|)\mathbb{A}+\mathcal{F}_{2}(\lambda)\lambda\lambda^{T},\end{split}

where in the last equality we use
u(r)=-\frac{\varphi(r)}{r\,(1+r\varphi(r))}
(cf. (
4.5
)), and write

(4.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(\lambda)=\Lambda(|\lambda|)\,\mathfrak{W}_{x,h}(|\lambda|),\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{F}_{2}(\lambda)=\Lambda(|\lambda|)\,\mathcal{G}(|\lambda|)\,\mathfrak{V}_{x,h}(\lambda)+\frac{\Lambda^{\prime}(|\lambda|)}{|\lambda|}\,\mathfrak{W}_{x,h}(\lambda).

The following proposition is the counterpart of
[
3
, eq. (5.18)-(5.19)]
:

Proposition 4.6
.

Under Assumption
2.6
, we have uniformly in
0<h\leq n^{-\frac{1}{2}}d_{\mathbb{B}}(g)
,

-\mathrm{Hess}_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})\sim\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})\,\mathbb{I}_{m}+\mathbb{A}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\det\left(-\mathrm{Hess}_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})\right)\lesssim\prod_{l=1}^{m}\big(d_{\mathbb{B}}(g)^{2}+8b_{l}\big).

Proof.

By

[
2
, Theorem VI.7.1]
and Lemma
4.4
(2), it suffices to show that:

(1)\ \mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})\lesssim\mathcal{F}_{1}(\tau_{h})\leq\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h});\hskip 20.00003pt(2)\ 0\leq\mathcal{F}_{2}(\tau_{h})|\tau_{h}|^{2}\lesssim\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h}).

For convenience, we may abbreviate
f_{1}(\rho_{h})=f_{1}
for any function
f_{1}
.

We prove (1) first. From (
4.18
), it follows directly that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(\tau_{h})=\Lambda\,\mathfrak{W}_{x,h}=\Lambda\,\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}+4u\Lambda\tau_{h}^{T}\mathbb{A}\tau_{h}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varphi}{\rho_{h}}\,\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})+\frac{\Lambda}{\rho_{h}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\mu(a_{j}\rho_{h})\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\varphi}{\rho_{h}}\,\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})\gtrsim\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h}),

by the second equality in (
4.4
).
Moreover, the inequality
\frac{\mu(r)}{r}\left(\frac{\sin r}{r}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{2}{3}
(cf.
[
3
, eq. (5.7)]
) gives
\mathcal{F}_{1}(\tau_{h})\leq(-\frac{\varphi}{\rho_{h}}+\frac{2}{3}\,\mathcal{G})\,\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h})
.
It then suffices to prove

-\frac{\varphi(r)}{r}+\frac{2}{3}\,\mathcal{G}(r)\leq 1
whenever
r\in(0,r_{*}).

Since
\varphi(r)=r^{-1}(\mathcal{G}(r)-1)
, it is equivalent to show
(\frac{2}{3}r^{2}-1)\,\mathcal{G}(r)\leq r^{2}-1
. The case where
r\in[1,r_{*})
can be easily derived by the fact
0\leq\mathcal{G}(r)\leq 1
.
For the case
r\in(0,1)
, we use the inequality

\mathcal{G}(r)\geq r\cot r=\tfrac{r}{\sin r}\cos r\geq(1+\tfrac{r^{2}}{6})(1-\tfrac{r^{2}}{2})\geq(1-r^{2})(1-\tfrac{2}{3}r^{2})^{-1},

where in the second “
\geq
” we
use
[
3
, eq. (5.7), (3.5)]
.

To prove (2), by the fact that
\Lambda(r)\sim 1
provided
0\leq r\leq r_{*}
, we need to show

0\leq\rho_{h}\left(\varphi\left(\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}+4u\tau_{h}^{T}\mathbb{A}\tau_{h}\right)+(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)\left(\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}^{\prime}+4\tau_{h}^{T}\mathbb{A}\tau_{h}u^{\prime}\right)\right)\lesssim\mathrm{S}(\tau_{h}).

By (
4.18
), it remains to establish:

(4.20)

\displaystyle 0\leq u\varphi+(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)u^{\prime}\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho_{h}(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)},

(4.21)

\displaystyle 0\leq\varphi\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}+(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}^{\prime}\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho_{h}(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)}\left(h^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{j}\rho_{h}}{\sin{(a_{j}\rho_{h})}}\right)^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\right).

First, (
4.20
) is a consequence of the identity

u\varphi+(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)u^{\prime}=\frac{-\rho_{h}\varphi^{\prime}+\varphi(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)}{\rho_{h}^{2}(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)}

as well as the inequality

(4.22)

0\leq-\rho_{h}\varphi^{\prime}+\varphi(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)\lesssim\rho_{h}.

To verify (
4.22
), by (
4.4
) we know
-\frac{\varphi(r)}{r}
is increasing on
(0,r_{*}]
. Thus

0\leq\rho_{h}^{2}\left(-\frac{\varphi(r)}{r}\right)^{\prime}\Big|_{r=\rho_{h}}=-\rho_{h}\varphi^{\prime}+\varphi\leq-\rho_{h}\varphi^{\prime}+\varphi(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)\leq-\rho_{h}\varphi^{\prime}\lesssim\rho_{h},

as desired.
To prove (
4.21
), a direct calculation and (
4.6
) yield

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\rho_{h}\,\Big(\varphi\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}+(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}^{\prime}\Big)=(1+\rho_{h}\varphi)(\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}+\rho_{h}\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}^{\prime})-\mathfrak{T}_{x,h}\\
&=\left(\frac{\varphi^{2}-\varphi^{\prime}}{1+\rho_{h}\varphi}-u\right)\left(h^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{a_{j}\rho_{h}}{\sin{(a_{j}\rho_{h})}}\right)^{2}\,|x_{(j)}|^{2}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}^{2}|x_{(j)}|^{2}\left(\mu^{\prime}(a_{j}\rho_{h})-\frac{\mu(a_{j}\rho_{h})}{a_{j}\rho_{h}}\right).\end{split}

By (
4.22
) and the fact
u\geq 0
, we see that
0\leq\frac{\varphi^{2}-\varphi^{\prime}}{1+\rho_{h}\varphi}-u\leq\frac{\varphi^{2}-\varphi^{\prime}}{1+\rho_{h}\varphi}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+\rho_{h}\varphi}.
Moreover, note that
\frac{1}{1+\rho_{h}\varphi}\geq 1
, and by (
2.5
),

\mu^{\prime}(r)-\frac{\mu(r)}{r}=r\left(\frac{\mu(r)}{r}\right)^{\prime}\geq 0,\hskip 20.00003pt\left(\frac{\mu(r)}{r}\right)^{\prime}\Big|_{r=\rho_{h}}\lesssim 1.

The proof is then finished.
∎

4.4.
Asymptotics of the Poisson Kernel

Recall from (
4.3
) that

Q_{h}(g;\lambda)=\mathbf{V}(\lambda)\left(\Phi(g;\lambda)+h^{2}\right)^{-N}.

By Lemma
4.4
(1) and arguing as in
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
, we obtain:

(4.23)
