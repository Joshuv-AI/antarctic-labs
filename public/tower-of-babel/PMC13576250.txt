Corneal endothelial morphology in vitiligo: a cross-sectional study with subgroup analysis of periocular involvement
Authors: Özkan Kocamış, Hacı Sütiçen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate corneal endothelial morphology and anterior segment parameters in patients with vitiligo and to investigate the potential impact of periocular involvement on endothelial characteristics.MethodsIn this cross-sectional observational study, 50 patients with vitiligo and 50 age- and sex-matched healthy controls were enrolled. Vitiligo patients were subdivided according to the presence or absence of periocular involvement. All participants underwent comprehensive ophthalmologic examination including autokeratorefractometry, Goldmann applanation tonometry, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, axial length measurement, and specular microscopy. Corneal endothelial parameters including endothelial cell density (CD), hexagonality (HEX), coefficient of variation (CV), and total cell count (N) were analyzed. Statistical comparisons were performed using appropriate parametric and non-parametric tests.ResultsAge and sex distribution were comparable between groups (p>0.05). No significant differences were observed between vitiligo and control groups in spherical equivalent, axial length, central corneal thickness, or endothelial parameters (CD, HEX, CV, N) (p>0.05 for all). Subgroup analysis revealed significantly higher endothelial cell density in the periocular vitiligo group compared with the non-periocular subgroup (p=0.008), while other endothelial indices remained similar.ConclusionCorneal endothelial morphology appears preserved in patients with vitiligo. The disease does not seem to confer clinically significant reduction in endothelial reserve or increased surgical risk related to endothelial compromise. Periocular involvement may represent a biologically distinct subset; however, no evidence of endothelial vulnerability was observed. Longitudinal studies incorporating multimodal imaging are warranted.Keywords: Vitiligo, Corneal endothelium, Specular microscopy, Endothelial cell density, Periocular involvement

Introduction

Vitiligo is an acquired depigmenting disorder characterized by selective destruction of melanocytes in the skin and mucous membranes. Increasing evidence suggests that melanocyte loss may not be confined to cutaneous tissues but may also involve melanocyte-containing ocular structures, including the uveal tract and retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) [1–3]. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses have demonstrated a higher prevalence of ocular abnormalities in patients with vitiligo compared with healthy controls, encompassing retinal pigmentary alterations, uveitis, glaucoma, and tear film dysfunction [1, 3].

Several clinical investigations have further delineated the ocular spectrum of vitiligo. Case–control and cross-sectional studies have reported abnormalities involving both anterior and posterior segments, including dry eye disease, meibomian gland dysfunction, and retinal vascular alterations detected by optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) [4–8]. In addition, large cohort studies have suggested associations between vitiligo and retinal detachment as well as broader ocular and systemic comorbidities, highlighting the need for interdisciplinary awareness [9, 10].

Despite accumulating data on retinal and ocular surface involvement, corneal structural changes in vitiligo remain insufficiently characterized. Dry eye–related surface alterations have been described [11, 12]; however, data regarding corneal endothelial morphology are limited and heterogeneous. Specular microscopy provides a noninvasive and reproducible method for evaluating endothelial cell density (CD), hexagonality (HEX), and coefficient of variation (CV), parameters known to be sensitive to systemic stress and intraocular procedures [13]. Whether vitiligo is associated with subclinical endothelial alterations or reduced endothelial reserve remains unclear.

Therefore, the present study aimed to evaluate corneal endothelial morphology and anterior segment parameters in patients with vitiligo, with particular attention to periocular involvement, and to compare these findings with age- and sex-matched healthy controls.

Materials and methods

This cross-sectional observational study included 50 right eyes of 50 patients with a dermatologist-confirmed diagnosis of vitiligo and 50 right eyes of 50 age- and sex-matched healthy controls. Vitiligo patients were further stratified according to the presence of periocular involvement. Periocular vitiligo was defined as the presence of clinically visible depigmented lesions affecting the upper and/or lower eyelids or the adjacent periorbital skin extending to the eyebrow region. Patients were classified as having periocular involvement when depigmentation was present in at least one of these anatomical areas, based on dermatological examination. Those without lesions in the periocular region were categorized as the non-periocular vitiligo group. All participants underwent a comprehensive ophthalmologic examination in both eyes; however, to eliminate potential bias arising from inter-eye correlation, only the right eye of each participant was included in the statistical analyses.

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Kırşehir Ahi Evran University, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Participants with corneal pathologies (including corneal scars, dystrophies, or ectasia), a history of uveitis or ocular inflammation, history of ocular trauma, contact lens wear, or chronic topical ocular medication use, previous refractive surgery, cataract or glaucoma surgery, as well as those with systemic diseases such as diabetes mellitus or hypertension, were excluded from the study.

All subjects underwent a comprehensive ophthalmological examination, including autokeratorefractometry, Goldmann applanation tonometry, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, and dilated fundus examination. Axial length measurements were obtained using the Lenstar LS 900 device (Haag-Streit, Switzerland). Corneal endothelial parameters were evaluated by specular microscopy using the Topcon SP-1P system (Topcon Corporation, Tokyo, Japan).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, median (minimum–maximum), frequency, and percentage values where appropriate. The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Shapiro–Wilk tests. For normally distributed quantitative variables, independent samples t-test and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey post hoc analysis were applied. Non-normally distributed quantitative variables were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test and Kruskal–Wallis test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and a p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. A post-hoc power analysis indicated that the study had sufficient power (> 80%) to detect clinically meaningful differences in endothelial cell density between groups.

Endothelial parameters, including HEX, CV, CD, and N values, were also similar among the three groups (p > 0.05; Table 2). However, the corneal endothelial cell density (CD) was significantly higher in the periocular vitiligo group compared with the non-periocular vitiligo group (p = 0.008; Table 2).

Discussion

Vitiligo is characterized by immune-mediated melanocyte destruction; however, melanocytes are not intrinsic components of the corneal endothelium. Although melanocyte loss is central in vitiligo pathogenesis, our findings indicate that corneal endothelial integrity appears largely preserved. This observation aligns with previous systematic reviews demonstrating that ocular involvement in vitiligo predominantly affects melanocyte-rich tissues such as the RPE and uveal tract rather than the corneal endothelium [1–3].

Multiple clinical and population-based studies have reported retinal pigmentary abnormalities, retinal vascular alterations, and increased risk of retinal detachment in patients with vitiligo [4, 5, 9]. OCTA-based investigations have revealed subclinical microvascular changes in the posterior segment [6]. Furthermore, large retrospective analyses have emphasized broader ocular and systemic associations [10]. In contrast, anterior segment studies have primarily described tear film instability and meibomian gland dysfunction rather than endothelial compromise [11, 12]. Our findings are consistent with this pattern, suggesting that the autoimmune mechanisms underlying vitiligo do not exert clinically meaningful structural stress on the corneal endothelium.

From a surgical standpoint, preservation of endothelial morphology has important implications. Corneal endothelial reserve is a critical determinant of postoperative outcomes following cataract surgery or laser procedures. Energy-dependent endothelial changes after Nd: YAG capsulotomy have been demonstrated even in otherwise healthy corneas [13]. The absence of baseline endothelial compromise in our cohort suggests that no evidence of reduced endothelial reserve was detected in patients with vitiligo. However, larger longitudinal studies are required before definitive conclusions regarding surgical risk can be drawn.

Interestingly, endothelial cell density was higher in the periocular vitiligo subgroup compared with non-periocular cases. Given the relatively small sample size and the absence of disease severity assessment, this finding may represent random variation rather than a true biological difference. Further studies are required before any mechanistic conclusions can be drawn. However, all endothelial parameters, including endothelial cell density, hexagonality, and coefficient of variation, remained within established physiological limits for healthy adult corneas as reported in previous specular microscopy studies [14]. Therefore, the clinical significance of this finding remains uncertain. Although periocular vitiligo has been reported in association with corneal pathologies such as keratoconus [15], our data do not demonstrate clinically relevant endothelial vulnerability in this subgroup.

Recent large-scale cohort studies have further emphasized systemic comorbidities associated with vitiligo [16]. However, the corneal endothelium appears structurally stable, reinforcing the tissue-specific nature of melanocyte-targeted autoimmunity.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the cross-sectional design precludes assessment of causality and longitudinal endothelial changes. Second, the sample size was relatively modest, particularly for subgroup analyses. Third, information regarding disease severity, disease activity, clinical subtype, treatment history, and smoking status was unavailable; therefore, their potential influence on ocular findings could not be assessed. Finally, multiple comparisons were performed without statistical correction, which may have increased the likelihood of type I error.

In conclusion, although vitiligo is associated with various ocular abnormalities involving melanocyte-rich tissues, corneal endothelial morphology appeared preserved in this cohort. No evidence of endothelial compromise was detected; however, these findings should be interpreted in light of the cross-sectional design, relatively small sample size, and the absence of standardized disease severity assessment. From a clinical perspective, no evidence was found to suggest increased susceptibility to corneal endothelial compromise among patients with vitiligo. Nevertheless, definitive conclusions cannot be drawn from a single cross-sectional study, and larger prospective longitudinal studies incorporating validated disease severity scoring systems are warranted to confirm these observations.
