Universal quantum state purification with energy-preserving operations
Xing-Chen Guo, Benchi Zhao, Xin Wang

Abstract
Quantum state purification, which operates not by identifying and correcting specific errors but by repeatedly projecting multiple noisy copies onto special subspaces, provides a syndrome-free alternative to quantum error correction. Existing purification protocols, however, generally assume unconstrained operations and thus overlook the energetic restrictions inherent in realistic quantum devices. Here, we establish a general framework for universal state purification under energy-conservation constraints for depolarizing noise. We derive a necessary and sufficient condition for the nonexistence of universal energy-preserving purification and, whenever such purification is feasible, analytically determine the optimal performance and the corresponding protocols. We further show how the optimal protocols can be systematically implemented using only energy-preserving operations. Numerical results confirm the effectiveness of the proposed scheme. Our framework recovers the standard purification setting as a special case and naturally extends to scenarios assisted by external energy resources. These results identify fundamental physical limits on state distillation and provide an energy-efficient route to quantum error mitigation.



I
Introduction

Quantum computers are believed to offer substantial speedups for solving many computational problems and are expected to have a broad impact across areas such as cryptography
Shor 1999
;
Rivest et al. 1978
, machine learning
Biamonte et al. 2017
;
Lloyd et al. 2014
, and quantum chemistry
Feynman 2018
;
Cao et al. 2019
. However, because quantum devices are inherently imperfect, quantum systems inevitably interact with their environment, causing deviations from the ideal setting of pure states and unitary dynamics. The presence of noise degrades computation and limits achievable accuracy.

To address this challenge and realize practical quantum utility in the presence of unavoidable noise, quantum state purification
Barenco et al. 1997
;
Fiurášek 2004
;
Childs et al. 2025
;
Yao et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2025a
;
Li et al. 2024
provides a systematic framework for mitigating quantum noise. It does so by transforming multiple copies of an unknown noisy quantum state into a single output state with higher fidelity, typically approaching the principal eigenvector of the input ensemble. Originally introduced in
Barenco et al. 1997
, quantum state purification via symmetrization, which can be viewed as a kind of quantum voting implemented by projecting onto the symmetric subspace, serves as a passive, syndrome-free alternative to active quantum error correction (QEC). Rather than identifying and correcting specific errors, it works by repeatedly projecting the computation onto the exponentially small symmetric subspace of redundant copies. In this way, error components are effectively suppressed through the quantum watchdog effect, without the need for classical measurement feedback or complex syndrome-extraction circuits. This process is essential for preserving the coherence required for quantum information processing.

Considerable effort has been devoted to the study of quantum state purification in different scenarios. When only specific target states are considered, such as Bell states or magic states, the problem reduces to resource distillation
Bennett et al. 1996
;
Devetak and Winter 2005
;
Fang et al. 2019
;
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Liu et al. 2025b
;
Itogawa et al. 2025
;
Bravyi and Haah 2012
;
Sales Rodriguez et al. 2025
;
Briegel et al. 1998
;
Dür et al. 1999
;
Azuma et al. 2015
;
Chen et al. 2024
. In a more general setting, universal purification protocols have been proposed. Rather than focusing on a single target state, universal purification aims to increase the fidelity, on average, for arbitrary pure states, and significant progress has been made in this direction
Fiurášek 2004
;
Yao et al. 2025
;
Childs et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2025a
.
However, researchers have found that some universal purification protocols
Fiurášek 2004
;
Yao et al. 2025
;
Childs et al. 2025
are difficult to realize under practical physical constraints. Consequently, attention has shifted to purification under restricted operational settings, where only certain classes of operations are allowed, such as local operations and classical communication
Zhao et al. 2025
and classically simulatable operations
He et al. 2025
.

Existing studies have overlooked one fundamental physical constraint—energy. As one of the most important resources in physics, energy underpins modern technologies
Vallina-Rodriguez and Crowcroft 2012
;
Hinton et al. 2011
;
Muralidhar et al. 2022
. While standard purification protocols typically assume access to arbitrary unitary gates, real-world quantum hardware is governed by a fixed Hamiltonian
H
, and the cost of operations is often measured in terms of energy exchange. If a purification protocol requires an external energy source to remove entropy from a system, then it is no longer a closed-system operation and instead becomes a heat-engine problem. By focusing on energy-preserving operations, one can identify the ultimate physical limits of state distillation and determine whether the gain in purity arises from genuinely clever information processing or merely from energetic overhead. This perspective is especially important in quantum thermodynamics and low-temperature physics, where preserving the energetic integrity of a state is as important as maintaining its coherence.

This work aims to develop a framework for universal energy-preserving purification under zero-energy-cost constraints. First, we show that energy-preserving purification does not always exist and provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the non-existence of universal energy-preserving purification. Second, we analytically derive the maximum average purification fidelity and calculate the maximum average purification success probability. Moreover, the Choi operators of the corresponding optimal purification protocols can be obtained explicitly. We then present the complete procedure for constructing a physical implementation directly from the Choi operators of the energy-preserving operations, with every step being energy-preserving. Finally, we conduct numerical experiments to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed energy-preserving purification protocols.
We emphasize that the standard purification framework without energy constraints
Fiurášek 2004
arises as a special case of our energy-preserving purification framework. Moreover, our framework can be further extended to energy-constrained purification assisted by an externally engineered energy resource.

II
Energy-preserving operations

We start from the definition of energy-preserving operation. A detailed study of which can be found in Ref.
Chiribella and Yang 2017

Definition 1

(Energy-preserving operations)

For Hamiltonian
H\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, a channel
\Lambda\in\text{CPTP}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
is termed energy-preserving for
H
if

\Lambda^{\dagger}(H^{n})=H^{n},\quad\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1)

An instrument
\{\Lambda_{x}\}_{x}
, with
\Lambda_{x}\in\text{CPTN}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
and
\sum_{x}\Lambda_{x}\in\text{CPTP}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))

H
if
\sum_{x}\Lambda_{x}
is an energy-preserving channel for
H

\Lambda\in\text{CPTN}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
is energy-preserving for
H
if it belongs to some energy-preserving instrument for
H

Physically, a channel

Note that energy-preserving operations can indeed be physically implemented in an energy-preserving manner: system-environment interactions that conserve the total energy without drawing energy from the environment, followed by projective measurements that do not disturb the energy of the system or the environment. This fact is formalized as follows.

Let

There exists a set of Kraus operators

for

3.

The Choi operator

of

The requirement of zero energy cost imposes thermodynamic neutrality, implying that the restoration of purity arises solely from the collective information processing of the noisy copies rather than through the injection of external work. Furthermore, the stipulations regarding success probability and fidelity ensure that the protocol is operationally viable and provides a genuine advantage over raw noisy states, distinguishing effective purification from trivial operations that fail to mitigate quantum noise.

Specifically, it demonstrates that for particular noise and Hamiltonian settings, the average fidelity cannot be enhanced beyond its initial value by any protocol performed at zero energy cost. Therefore, Theorem

IV
Optimal universal energy-preserving purification

Recently, Yao
et al.

Yao et al. 2025
revealed that the trade-off between average purification success probability and average purification fidelity exhibits a saturation behavior: sacrificing probability enhances fidelity only up to a critical threshold, beyond which the fidelity stabilizes at its global maximum. This saturation constitutes the
golden point
of purification, representing the ideal balance where maximum fidelity is achieved with the highest possible yield. Capturing this physical insight, we define the
optimal universal energy-preserving purification protocol
as the one that attains this golden point under zero energy cost constraints. This dictates a strict hierarchy of optimization: the protocol must yield the maximum average success probability conditioned on achieving the globally maximum average purification fidelity allowed by energy-preserving operations. This definition ensures the protocol delivers the optimal purification performance while retaining the highest possible efficiency.

Formally, we implement this optimization hierarchy via a two-stage maximization. First, we determine the maximum average purification fidelity across the entire space of universal energy-preserving purification protocols,
\text{UEPP}(\mathcal{N},H,n)
:

F_{\text{max}}(\mathcal{N},H,n)\coloneqq\max_{\mathcal{E}\in\text{UEPP}(\mathcal{N},H,n)}\bar{F}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E},n).

(8)

The protocols achieving this maximum constitute the optimal fidelity set
\text{UEPP}^{*}(\mathcal{N},H,n)\subseteq\text{UEPP}(\mathcal{N},H,n)
:

\text{UEPP}^{*}(\mathcal{N},H,n)\coloneqq\{{\cal E}\in\text{UEPP}({\cal N},H,n)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{F}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E},n)=F_{\text{max}}(\mathcal{N},H,n)\}.

(9)

Crucially, this set is not a singleton; it contains protocols that yield the identical global maximum fidelity but possess varying average purification success probabilities. Consequently, the second step identifies the optimal protocol
\mathcal{E}^{*}
by maximizing the average success probability within this subset:

p_{\text{succ}}^{\text{max}}(\mathcal{N},H,n)\coloneqq\max_{\mathcal{E}\in\text{UEPP}^{*}(\mathcal{N},H,n)}\bar{p}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{E},n).

(10)

With the optimization framework established, the main goal is to determine the optimal protocol
{\cal E}^{*}
and access its performance,
F_{\text{max}}(\mathcal{N},H,n)
and
p_{\text{succ}}^{\text{max}}(\mathcal{N},H,n)

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we established a comprehensive framework for quantum state purification under the constraint of zero energy cost, characterized by energy-preserving operations. We provide the necessary and sufficient conditions for the non-existence of a universal energy-preserving purification protocol. Besides, the optimal universal energy-preserving purification protocol is studied, and the corresponding physically implementable operations are constructed. The numerical experiments are also conducted to demonstrate the effectiveness of the optimal universal energy-preserving purification protocol. This work can be understood as an extension of the conventional purification protocol, which does not take the energy constraint into consideration. The results shown in this work collapse to the results in Ref.
Fiurášek 2004
when the Hamiltonian is identity.

Moreover, the energy-preserving purification framework can also extend to the non‑zero energy cost scenario, where an external battery serves as the sole energy resource for purification. In this setting, the purification protocol on the
n
-copy noisy systems is implemented by first running a joint operation of the battery and the
n
‑copy system and then discarding the battery. Using only that battery as energy resource demands that the joint operation to be energy‑preserving. Provided that the battery is engineered in a full‑rank state, the optimal purification protocol can be obtained analogously Appendix
C
for details).

In summary, this work provides both foundational insights and a practical toolkit for improving the purity of quantum states within energy limitations. It thus lays a theoretical groundwork for the development of energy-efficient quantum devices and protocols, particularly relevant for the resource constrained quantum technologies.
