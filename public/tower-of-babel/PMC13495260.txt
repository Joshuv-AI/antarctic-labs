Risk factors for massive intraoperative hemorrhage during cesarean section in patients with complete placenta previa and placenta accreta spectrum, without prior cesarean delivery: a retrospective case-control study
Authors: Pin Cao, Peng Liu, Lu Ji, Chong Qiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundResearch on placenta accreta spectrum (PAS) primarily focuses on patients with complete placenta previa (cPP) and a prior cesarean delivery. Studies on patients without a prior cesarean delivery are relatively scarce. Background

Placenta Accreta Spectrum (PAS) is characterized by abnormal adhesion or invasion of trophoblast cells into the myometrium. Based on the depth of infiltration, PAS is classified into placenta accreta, placenta increta, and placenta percreta [1]. With the increasing rate of cesarean sections, the incidence of PAS has risen annually [2]. PAS surgery is often complicated by massive hemorrhage, with a median blood loss of approximately 2000 ml and reported cases reaching up to 10,000 ml in extreme instances [3, 4]. Such substantial bleeding frequently leads to adverse maternal outcomes, including renal insufficiency, sepsis, hysterectomy, and even death [5]. Placenta previa is the primary risk factor for PAS [6]. When PAS occurs in conjunction with complete placenta previa (cPP), surgical complexity is further heightened, leading to a substantial increase in intraoperative blood loss.

The Placenta Accreta Spectrum Ultrasound Scoring System (PASUSS) is a preoperative ultrasound scoring system developed by Peking University Third Hospital. Although several predictive models have been derived from it, PASUSS remains the most widely recognized and utilized method for the preoperative diagnosis of PAS in China. The system consists of nine scoring items, each assigned a score of 0, 1, or 2 points. The total score reflects the severity of PAS and predicts intraoperative blood loss [7, 8]. Current research on PAS primarily focuses on patients with cPP and a history of cesarean delivery, whereas studies involving populations without a prior cesarean section remain relatively limited. In clinical practice, it has been observed that with advancing maternal age and a growing number of infertility patients, the incidence of PAS coexisting with cPP has increased—even in cases without a prior cesarean section. Furthermore, some patients may require hysterectomy due to uncontrollable massive hemorrhage. Compared to patients with a history of cesarean section, the baseline characteristics of those without a prior cesarean section show significant differences. In clinical practice, it has been observed that relying solely on the preoperative PASUSS score may underestimate the actual severity of PAS, potentially leading to insufficient attention to the patient’s condition before surgery and, ultimately, adverse outcomes.

Therefore, this study focuses on patients with cPP and PAS without a history of cesarean section. Based on patients’ baseline characteristics, preoperative laboratory tests, and ultrasound indicators, we aim to identify high-risk groups for massive intraoperative hemorrhage (MIH) during cesarean delivery. This will facilitate adequate preoperative and intraoperative preparation and improve patient outcomes.

Method

Study subjects

A retrospective study was conducted on patients with cPP and PAS who had no prior history of cesarean section at Shengjing Hospital of China Medical University between January 2020 and December 2024. Inclusion criteria were: age 18–45 years, cPP with PAS, gestational age at delivery ≥ 28 weeks, singleton pregnancy with cesarean delivery, and live birth. Exclusion criteria included: history of cesarean section or myomectomy, twin or multiple gestation, intrauterine fetal death, fetal malformations, cesarean delivery performed at other hospitals, pregnancy complicated by hematological disorders, and absence of PASUSS scores or incomplete data.

Research indicators

The medical center’s electronic records system was employed to gather patients’ demographic and clinical data. Maternal baseline characteristics included age, gravidity, parity, number of curettages and abortions, history of antepartum hemorrhage, hysteroscopic adhesiolysis, mode of conception, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, and diabetes mellitus. Laboratory and ultrasound parameters—comprising hemoglobin levels, placental location, and the PASUSS score assessed two weeks before surgery—were recorded based on the most recent preoperative evaluation. Delivery characteristics and maternal outcomes consisted of neonatal birth weight, intraoperative blood loss, packed red blood cell transfusion, plasma transfusion, hysterectomy during cesarean delivery, operation duration, ICU admission, and duration of postoperative hospitalization.

Ultrasound examination

Because the participants in this study were diagnosed with PAS and cPP, patients often fear that transvaginal examination could increase the risk of iatrogenic bleeding. Transabdominal ultrasound enables a comprehensive evaluation of the entire uterus, placenta, and adjacent organs such as the bladder. This is crucial for assessing the extent of placental invasion and for planning subsequent cesarean delivery and hemorrhage prevention strategies—key objectives of preoperative evaluation in PAS. Although transvaginal ultrasound offers superior resolution for detailed placental assessment, transabdominal ultrasound better meets the overall preoperative evaluation needs of our study population. Therefore, considering both the comprehensiveness of the assessment and patient compliance, transabdominal ultrasound was selected as the preferred preoperative ultrasound approach in this study. If transabdominal imaging yields suboptimal results or if the patient cannot tolerate a full bladder, transvaginal ultrasound should be considered as an alternative. All ultrasound examinations and PASUSS score assessments in our study were uniformly completed preoperatively prior to cesarean delivery. All ultrasonic indicators were evaluated at about two weeks before surgery and recorded based on the latest preoperative ultrasound findings. The anatomical survey ultrasound was performed as part of routine prenatal ultrasonography, and all ultrasonic indicators used to calculate the PASUSS score were collected at this preoperative time point. No additional PASUSS scoring was performed intraoperatively or postoperatively. The PASUSS scoring criteria are detailed in Table 1.

Perioperative management

A multidisciplinary team, led by senior obstetricians, formulates surgical plans, and performs the operations. The surgeons were experienced in perioperative PAS management and capable of efficiently managing intraoperative emergencies. Patients diagnosed with PAS in our center were primarily managed with fertility-sparing conservative surgery. Efforts were made to preserve the uterus; however, in cases of uncontrollable hemorrhage, a hysterectomy was performed without delay. To accurately quantify blood loss during cesarean section, new suction devices were utilized intraoperatively. Intraoperative blood loss was measured volumetrically, with adjustments made for the volume of amniotic fluid and irrigation solution used.

Related definitions

According to WHO guidelines, preoperative anemia is defined as a hemoglobin level below 110 g/L prior to surgery [9]. The diagnosis of PAS was established based on the FIGO clinical and histological criteria [10]. In this study, Grade 1 was classified as adhesive PAS, whereas Grades 2 and 3 were collectively defined as invasive PAS. Previous studies have defined massive intraoperative hemorrhage as blood loss of 2000 mL or more [7]. And the average blood loss in PAS surgeries has been reported to be approximately 2000 mL [3]. Therefore, in this study, MIH was defined as blood loss of ≥ 2000 mL during cesarean section, and patients were categorized into an MIH group and a non-MIH group.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2). Continuous variables are presented as mean±standard deviation or median (interquartile range), while categorical data are shown as percentages. Categorical variables were compared using chi-square tests, while continuous variables were analyzed with t-tests or wilcoxon rank-sum tests. Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify risk factors for MIH during cesarean section in patients with cPP and PAS who had no prior cesarean history. A P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 109 patients were included in the study, comprising 71 cases of adhesive PAS and 38 cases of invasive PAS. Patients without prior cesarean delivery tend to have extensive placental adhesion and invasion into the uterine wall throughout the uterine corpus. Therefore, intraoperative hemostatic measures mainly consisted of local suturing, intrauterine balloon tamponade and uterine artery ligation, whereas focal lesion resection was only conducted in a small number of cases. In this study, 7 patients were definitively diagnosed via postoperative pathological examination of surgical specimens. The remaining patients were diagnosed in strict accordance with clinical criteria, which were determined intraoperatively by experienced senior obstetric surgeons based on direct visual inspection, placental adherence, abnormal placental invasion and intraoperative clinical manifestations. Among these, 22 patients experienced MIH during cesarean delivery, corresponding to an incidence of 20.18%. The maximum recorded blood loss was 5,000 mL. No hysterectomy was performed during the procedure. The operation time, ICU admission, postoperative hospitalization days, red blood cell and plasma transfusion were significantly higher in the MIH groups compared to the non-MIH groups (Table 2).

Impact of MIH

A comparison of baseline characteristics revealed that the MIH group had a significantly higher proportion of individuals aged ≥ 35 years, a higher prevalence of diabetes mellitus, and a greater history of hysteroscopic adhesiolysis compared to the non-MIH group. In contrast, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of conception method, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, gravidity, parity, number of curettages or abortions, antepartum hemorrhage, or preoperative anemia. Regarding ultrasound characteristics, the PASUSS score was significantly higher in the MIH group, but no statistically significant difference in placental location was observed between the two groups (Table 3).

Discussion

In this study, PAS was predominantly diagnosed based on clinical criteria. And the incidence of MIH during cesarean delivery among patients with cPP and PAS, but without a prior cesarean section, was approximately 20.18%. Maternal age, intrauterine adhesiolysis, and the PASUSS score were identified as independent risk factors for MIH during cesarean section. In clinical practice, it is essential not only to prioritize patients with a preoperative PASUSS score, but also to consider patient’s baseline characteristics, in order to accurately identify high-risk groups for MIH before surgery.

As maternal age increases, the risk of cPP and PAS rises even in women without a history of cesarean section or myomectomy [11]. While most cases present as adhesive PAS, some patients may develop invasive PAS. In clinical practice, insufficient attention to these conditions often leads to inadequate preoperative preparation, excessive intraoperative bleeding, and severe outcomes—including hysterectomy. Unlike patients with a history of cesarean section, in whom placenta accreta or increta typically occurs locally at the previous cesarean scar in the lower uterine segment, those without prior cesarean delivery tend to have extensive placental adhesion and invasion into the uterine wall throughout the uterine corpus. Consequently, the routine management for patients with previous cesarean delivery, namely resecting the invaded uterine tissue to reduce hemorrhage, is impractical for most patients without such history given the diffuse placental invasion. The relationship between age and intraoperative blood loss has been extensively studied [12]. In clinical practice, advanced maternal age is often associated with an increased number of gravidity, parity, and intrauterine procedures. These procedures are closely linked to intrauterine adhesions, and adhesion lysis can cause extensive endometrial injury, leading to excessive trophoblast invasion. Multiple studies have indicated that hysteroscopic adhesiolysis is a high-risk factor for PAS. Moreover, as the number of adhesiolysis procedures increases, both the incidence of PAS and the amount of intraoperative blood loss during cesarean delivery rise significantly [13, 14].

The PASUSS was introduced in 2016 by Peking University Third Hospital as a tool for the preoperative diagnosis of PAS [15]. Studies have consistently demonstrated that higher PASUSS scores are associated with an increased likelihood of PAS, massive hemorrhage, and hysterectomy. However, the predictive capacity of individual components of the PASUSS score for massive hemorrhage remains inconsistent [16].

In addition, previous studies have explored the associations of preoperative anemia, antepartum hemorrhage, and diabetes mellitus with blood loss [17, 18], though the findings remain inconsistent. In the present study, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups. This may be attributed to differences in the selection of the study population and the criteria used for defining blood loss.

Strengths and limitations

A notable strength of this study is that, while the incidence of PAS with cPP in patients without a prior cesarean section is relatively low, most patients from Northeast China are referred to our tertiary hospital for delivery. However, we acknowledge several limitations in our research. First, as a retrospective study, the diagnosis of PAS was mainly based on intraoperative findings, which may introduce bias due to human factors, and these results should be interpreted with caution. Second, this study collected data only from the past five years; due to the relatively limited sample size, this study did not specifically analyze the correlation between the individual components of the PASUSS score and MIH during cesarean delivery, future studies should extend the follow-up period and increase the sample size to enhance reliability. Lastly, this study is that most of the ultrasound examinations were performed abdominally. Abdominal ultrasound may be affected by factors such as maternal abdominal fat thickness, fetal position, and amniotic fluid volume, which may reduce the accuracy of detecting placenta previa and low-lying placenta.

Conclusions

In patients with cPP and PAS diagnosed mainly through clinical criteria and no prior cesarean delivery, the incidence of MIH was approximately 20.18%. Maternal age, intrauterine adhesiolysis, and the preoperative PASUSS score were significantly associated with the occurrence of MIH. In clinical practice, it is important not only to consider the preoperative PASUSS score but also to account for patients’baseline characteristics. This approach can help accurately identify high-risk groups for MIH prior to surgery and improve patient outcomes.
