RetinexDualV2: Physically-Grounded Dual Retinex for Generalized UHD Image Restoration
Mohab KishawyJun Chen {kishawym, chenjun}@mcmaster.ca McMaster University

Abstract
We propose RetinexDualV2, a unified, physically grounded dual-branch framework for diverse Ultra-High-Definition (UHD) image restoration. Unlike generic models, our method employs a Task-Specific Physical Grounding Module (TS-PGM) to extract degradation-aware priors (e.g., rain masks and dark channels). These explicitly guide a Retinex decomposition network via a novel Physical-conditioned Multi-head Self attention (PC-MSA) mechanism, enabling robust reflection and illumination correction. This physical conditioning allows a single architecture to handle various complex degradations seamlessly, without task-specific structural modifications. RetinexDualV2 demonstrates exceptional generalizability, securing 4 th place in the NTIRE 2026 Day and Night Raindrop Removal Challenge and 5 th place in the Joint Noise Low-light Enhancement (JNLLIE) Challenge. Extensive experiments confirm the state-of-the-art performance and efficiency of our physically motivated approach.

Introduction

Although imaging sensors and displays have advanced significantly to deliver highly detailed and sophisticated Ultra-High-Definition (UHD) images, restoring captured information distorted or degraded by light exposure, haze, shadow, or weather conditions has grown increasingly difficult. This causes a bottleneck for more advanced tasks, such as object detection, semantic segmentation, and visual reasoning. Accordingly, images restoration (IR) became more crucial not only for the plausibility of the image but also for the improvement of computer vision in general.

Numerous learning-based methods have been surfaced to tackle image restoration challenges
17
;
28
;
2
, substantially improving the removal of various degradation artifacts. Nevertheless, these techniques struggle to process UHD images effectively, as the enormous data volume and resolution demand excessive computational resources. This constraint has become a major obstacle in achieving high-quality UHD image restoration.

To advance UHD image restoration (IR), several dedicated research efforts have been developed
18
;
14
;
30
;
33
.
Some approaches rely on a Downsampling-Enhancement-Upsampling paradigm
18
;
14
, which lowers computational demands by processing features in a reduced-resolution space before reconstructing full resolution. However, this strategy inevitably discards important high-frequency details because the resampling steps (downsampling and upsampling) are decoupled from the core enhancement process
29
.
Other methods instead emphasize frequency-domain processing, decomposing the image across multiple frequency bands to exploit global contextual information and simplify the modeling of certain degradation patterns that become more apparent in the frequency domain
34
;
33
. While effective in many scenarios, this frequency-based strategy lacks universal applicability; in cases dominated by localized degradations, the spatial domain often provides a more direct and accurate depiction of the correspondence between degraded and clean image regions
11
.

Alternatively, Retinexformer
2
introduced a Retinex-based model for low-light enhancement, inspiring several follow-up works
1
;
15
. Although these methods achieved top performance in low-light restoration, they generally treat reflectance and illumination as homogeneous components and do not extend Retinex theory into a more general framework for broader image restoration tasks beyond illumination/color correction. RetinexDual
11
addressed this problem by proposing an approach that recongizes the duel nature of retinex decomposition. However, it faces challenges in certain complex scenarios as there is no physical-grounding for the learning process.

Another promising direction lies in incorporating physics-informed strategies that embed domain-specific physical models or priors directly into the deep learning framework, such as the dark channel prior in dehazing
6
, the illumination invariant prior in low-light enhancement
21
, or the region of interest in degradation mechanisms
8
. This approach leverages established knowledge of image formation processes to regularize training, guide feature learning, and constrain outputs toward physically plausible solutions.

Based on this, we propose RetinexDualV2, a physically grounded evolution of RetinexDual that preserves its effective dual-branch handling of reflectance and illumination while infusing task-specific physical priors to guide restoration.
Specifically, the degraded input is fed into two parallel extractors: a Retinex decomposer and a task-specific physical grounding module. The resulting reflectance and illumination components are then processed through Physical-Grounded Scale Attentive Mamba (PG-SAMBA) for spatially adaptive, detail-preserving reflectance correction and Physical-Grounded Frequency Illumination Adaptor (PG-FIA) for physically consistent illumination adjustment. Within each layer, the physical features are incorporated using Physical-conditioned Multi-head Self attention (PC-MSA).
The main contributions are summerized as follows:

•

We introduce
RetinexDualV2
, a physically grounded extension of RetinexDual that assesses the restoration of retinex components inside the specialized sub-networks.

•

We implement a task-specific physical grounding module that extracts physical characteristics from the input as dark channel prior in dehazing, rain drop ROI in deraining, and illumination-invariant edge detection in low light, and shadow-invariant prior for shadow removal.

•

In PC-MSA, we inject the physical map into the restoration process using modulated self attention mechanism to regularize outputs and ensure physical plausibility.

•

Extensive evaluations across multiple NTIRE 2026 challenges demonstrate our model’s robust generalization and competitive performance. Specifically, RetinexDualV2 secured the 4
th
place in the Day and Night Raindrop Removal Challenge and the 5
th
place in the Joint Noise Low-light Enhancement Challenge, while maintaining an efficient parameter footprint of 4.8M.

Related Work

Ultra-High-Definition Image Restoration.
With the growing demand for high-fidelity visual content, extensive research has been dedicated to Ultra-High-Definition (UHD) image restoration
18
;
33
;
34
;
20
. For instance, LLFormer
20
relies on fusion cross-attention to merge features across different stages, alongside axis-based multi-head self-attention to manage multi-scale representations. While highly effective at retaining UHD details, this architecture demands massive computational resources, making it impractical for standard hardware. Alternatively, methods like Wave-Mamba
34
tackle downsampling-related information loss by processing distinct frequency bands in parallel through specialized Mamba blocks. Similarly, ERR
33
introduces a three-stage pipeline specifically designed to handle zero-, low-, and high-frequency components separately. Even though these frequency-domain techniques successfully mitigate computational bottlenecks and maintain fine details, they are not a universal solution. Specifically, localized spatial degradations are typically more effectively addressed directly within the spatial domain, as this approach provides a more precise representation of the mapping between corrupted and pristine image regions
11
.

Retinex Theory in Image Restoration.
Low-Light Image Enhancement (LLIE) has recently shifted focus toward Retinex theory, leveraging the idea that image noise is primarily concentrated in the illumination layer
2
;
1
;
15
. Retinexformer
2
successfully applied this concept via an illumination-guided transformer, inspiring a wave of state-of-the-art extensions including RetinexMamba
1
, Retinexformer+
15
, ERetinex
9
, and MambaLLIE
24
. Despite their strong performance, these models exhibit structural shortcomings: they either modify only one Retinex component or process both using the exact same architectural design, and they largely ignore Retinex principles for broader Image Restoration (IR) applications. RetinexDual
11
tackled this issue directly by formulating an architecture tailored to the dual nature of Retinex components. However, this method encounters difficulties in specialized cases, demonstrating that relying solely on Retinex theory is inadequate for universal restoration. To overcome these limitations and effectively process highly complex scenes, incorporating explicit physical grounding is essential.

Physical Grounding in Image Restoration.
Physical priors derived from the optical properties of image formation serve as foundational regularizers for ill-posed restoration tasks. By explicitly modeling the underlying degradation mechanisms ,such as leveraging the dark channel prior for atmospheric scattering
6
or utilizing log-chromaticity and shadow-resistant representations for illumination invariance
21
. These approaches effectively isolate the clean signal from complex perturbations. Furthermore, incorporating explicit structural priors, such as structure-guided designs in scale-aware low-light enhancement
7
, explicitly utilizes physical geometry to constrain the restoration space. Integrating these explicit physical and structural constraints into modern frameworks enables models to maintain strict radiometric consistency and robust edge preservation across a broad spectrum of adverse conditions, from extreme low-light environments to weather-induced artifacts. Consequently, this guides the learning process toward physically plausible image recovery, proving highly resilient where purely data-driven statistical mappings often fail.

In each of the branches, Physical-Grounded Scale Adaptive Mamba Block (PG-SAMB) and Physical-Grounded Fourier Correction Block (PG-FCB) are responsible for restoring the reflectance and illumination components, respectively, while relying on the physical conditioning provided by (TS-PGM).

3.1
Task-Specific Physical Grounding Module (TS-PGM)

The TS-PGM module extracts task-dependent physical priors as shown in Fig.
2
, guiding the restoration process by modulating feature interactions through PC-MSA. Unlike general-purpose feature extraction, TS-PGM explicitly encodes degradation-specific physics to condition both reflection and illumination branches. Across all tasks, the physical prior is computed by fusing gradient and intensity-based representations:

\mathbf{P}_{m}=\text{Fusion}(\text{Conv}_{\text{grad}}(I),\text{Conv}_{\text{int/struct}}(\text{Prior})),

(1)

where
\text{Conv}_{\text{grad}}(I)
captures image gradients to preserve high-frequency details, and the intensity or structure prior emphasizes task-specific degradation characteristics.

Residual Rain Intensity Mask for Deraining.
Rain degradation manifests as localized artifacts with high spatial variance. We compute the residual rain mask by learning pixel-wise differences between rain-corrupted and rain-free regions. A shallow UNet
\Phi_{\text{rain}}
trained on paired drops and blur images produces the rain intensity mask:

\mathbf{M}_{\text{rain}}=\Phi_{\text{rain}}(I),

(2)

where
\mathbf{M}_{\text{rain}}
emphasizes regions with rain artifacts. The mask is generated offline by computing the boosted grayscale difference:

\mathbf{M}_{\text{rain}}^{\text{gt}}=\alpha\cdot\text{Gray}(|I_{\text{drop}}-I_{\text{blur}}|),

(3)

with boost factor
\alpha
normalizing intensity variations.

Structure-Aware Illumination Prior for Low-Light Enhancement.
Low-light degradation couples high noise with insufficient illumination, requiring joint recovery of structural clarity and brightness. We extract a structure-aware illumination prior inspired by
7
via color-invariant convolutions that remain robust to illumination changes. Specifically, we compute the Weberized invariant
W_{\text{inv}}
through Gaussian color model transformations:

\displaystyle=\mathbf{G}_{\text{cm}}\cdot I,

(4)

\displaystyle W_{\text{inv}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\{0,1,2\}}\left(\frac{\nabla^{(k)}E_{l}}{\nabla^{(k)}E+\epsilon}\right)^{2},

where
\mathbf{G}_{\text{cm}}
is the Gaussian color model,
\nabla^{(k)}
denotes
k
-th order derivatives via Gaussian filtering at learnable scales, and
\epsilon
prevents numerical instability. This color-invariant representation captures local structure independent of absolute intensity, providing stable guidance under extreme underexposure.

Dark Channel Prior for Nighttime Dehazing.
Haze and atmospheric scattering in nighttime scenes compound illumination deficiency, requiring joint dehazing and contrast enhancement. The dark channel prior, a fundamental haze prior from classical image restoration, captures pixels where at least one color channel exhibits low intensity due to scattering. We compute the dark channel as:

\mathbf{D}_{c}(I)=\min_{c\in\{R,G,B\}}I_{c},

(5)

where
\mathbf{D}_{c}
represents the minimum intensity across color channels at each pixel. This prior effectively identifies haze-dominated regions with low contrast.

Log-Chromaticity Shadow-Resistant Prior for Shadow Removal.
Shadow regions exhibit localized illumination variations that distort reflectance estimates. We adopt a log-chromaticity shadow-resistant representation that isolates shadow cues independent of absolute brightness. Given an image
I
normalized to
[0,1]
, we compute:

\displaystyle\mu_{g}

\displaystyle=(I_{R}\cdot I_{G}\cdot I_{B})^{1/3},

(6)

\displaystyle\rho_{R}

\displaystyle=\log(I_{R}/\mu_{g}),\quad\rho_{B}=\log(I_{B}/\mu_{g}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{inv}

\displaystyle=\rho_{R}\cos(\theta)+\rho_{B}\sin(\theta),

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{inv}^{\text{norm}}

\displaystyle=\text{clip}\left(\frac{\mathbf{S}_{inv}-p_{2}}{p_{98}-p_{2}},0,1\right),

where
\mu_{g}
is the geometric mean across color channels,
\rho_{R},\rho_{B}
are log-chromaticity features,
\theta
is a learnable projection angle, and
p_{\alpha}
denotes the
\alpha
-th percentile for robust normalization. This shadow-resistant invariant enables the illumination branch to recover shadow-free lighting cues while guiding the reflection branch to reconstruct shadow-occluded details.

3.2
RetinexDualV2 Framework:

Inspired from RetinexDual
11
, RetinexDualV2 follows Retinex theory, which decomposes a distorted image
I\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}
as:

I=(R_{eff}+\hat{R})\odot(L_{eff}+\hat{L})

(7)

where
R_{eff}
,
L_{eff}
are the effective reflection and illumination components, and
\hat{R}
,
\hat{L}
represent distortion artifacts. Our Retinex decomposer
\psi_{d}
extracts these components, and the restoration is formulated as:

\displaystyle R_{eff},L_{eff}=

\displaystyle\psi_{d}(I),

(8)

\displaystyle\hat{R}=-\mathcal{S}(R_{eff},P_{m}),

\displaystyle\hat{L}=-\mathcal{F}(L_{eff},P_{m}),

\displaystyle I_{out}=(R_{eff}-\hat{R})\odot

\displaystyle(L_{eff}-\hat{L})

where
\mathcal{S}
(PG-SAMBA) and
\mathcal{F}
(PG-FCB) are complementary sub-networks designed to correct reflection and illumination distortions respectively.

Physical-conditioned Multi-head Self attention (PC-MSA)

The core component enabling physical grounding across both branches is the Physical-conditioned Multi-head Self attention (PC-MSA). PC-MSA extends standard multi-head self-attention by modulating feature interactions with task-specific physical guidance extracted from TS-PGM.

Given input features
\mathbf{F}_{\text{in}}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}
, we first reshape to
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{HW\times C}
. The Query, Key, and Value representations are obtained through linear projections:

\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_{Q}^{\top},\quad\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_{K}^{\top},\quad\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_{V}^{\top},

(9)

where
\mathbf{W}_{Q},\mathbf{W}_{K},\mathbf{W}_{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times C}
are learnable projection matrices. Crucially, PC-MSA incorporates physical guidance
\mathbf{P}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{HW\times C}
extracted from TS-PGM to modulate the value vectors, ensuring feature refinement respects physical constraints of the degradation task:

\mathbf{V}^{\prime}=\mathbf{V}\odot\mathbf{P}_{m},

(10)

where
\odot
denotes element-wise multiplication. The physical-conditioned attention is then computed as:

\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V}^{\prime})=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{K}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}}{\sigma}\right)\mathbf{V}^{\prime},

(11)

where
\sigma
is a learnable per-head rescale parameter. Finally, positional embeddings via grouped convolutions capture spatial context, grounding self-attention in both physical and spatial domains.

Physical-Grounded Scale Adaptive Mamba Block (PG-SAMB)

PG-SAMB is a compact, coarse-to-fine block that injects TS-PGM priors into Mamba-style feature aggregation via PC-MSA. Instead of reworking the core scanning mechanism, we condition scale-wise attention and local convolutions on the physical prior so that the block emphasizes degradation-relevant regions across three scales and fuses these conditioned corrections into the residual path. A lightweight group state-space component supplies non-sequential semantic context to improve long-range consistency.

Physical-Grounded Fourier Correction Block (PG-FCB)

PG-FCB is a small, frequency-domain illumination corrector that applies PC-MSA conditioning before compact Fourier-domain processing. Guided by the physical prior, it modulates amplitude/phase via lightweight 1×1 convolutions in the spectral domain and returns an adaptive residual correction in image space. This design captures global contrast and color biases efficiently, keeping the illumination branch parameter-light ( 0.2M).

3.3
Multi-level Training Objectives

Following a deep supervision strategy, we employ a multi-level training with outputs at three resolutions:
\hat{I}_{1}
,
\hat{I}_{2}
, and
\hat{I}_{3}
as shown in Fig.
3
. The total loss combines Charbonnier loss
\mathcal{L}_{cb}

13
, SSIM loss
\mathcal{L}_{ssim}

23
, FFT loss
\mathcal{L}_{fft}
, and perceptual loss
\mathcal{L}_{p}

32
using AlexNet features
12
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{total}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}w_{i}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{cb}(I_{i},\hat{I}_{i})+\lambda_{ssim}(1-S_{i})

(12)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{fft}\mathcal{L}_{fft}(I_{i},\hat{I}_{i})+\lambda_{p}\mathcal{L}_{p}(I_{i},\hat{I}_{i})\Big),

where
w=[0.25,0.5,1.0]
for
i=1,2,3
scales,
S_{i}=\text{SSIM}(I_{i},\hat{I}_{i})
, and
\lambda_{ssim}=0.5
,
\lambda_{fft}=0.0001
,
\lambda_{p}=0.5
are the loss weights. This multi-scale formulation progressively refines predictions while maintaining retinex decomposition constraints across all resolution levels.

Ablation Study.

To dissect the effectiveness of our physical-guided structural designs and the efficacy of the illumination-reflection decomposition, we conducted an ablation study on the UHD-LL dataset
14
. Specifically, we focus on three core aspects: the dual-branch Retinex decomposition architecture, the extraction of task-specific physical priors via the Task-Specific Physical Grounding Module (TS-PGM), and their targeted injection through the Physical-conditioned Multi-head Self attention (PC-MSA). As reported in Table
5
, utilizing a single-branch architecture (PG-SAMBA) without decoupling illumination and reflection (“w/o Dual-Branch”) yields the lowest performance at 26.67 dB PSNR and 0.919 SSIM. Introducing the dual-branch design but ignoring the synthesized physical prior entirely (“w/o Physical Prior”) provides a significant improvement up to 28.79 dB PSNR and 0.920 SSIM. If the network extracts the physical prior but relies on standard feature interactions by concatnating the prior instead of selectively conditioning via PC-MSA (“w/o PC-MSA”), it achieves 28.43 dB PSNR and 0.927 SSIM. Finally, our full RetinexDualV2 model achieves the optimal performance of 28.91 dB PSNR and 0.935 SSIM, demonstrating that dynamically synthesizing task-specific physical priors and explicitly steering the dual-branch feature representation via PC-MSA are strictly necessary for maximizing restoration capabilities.

5
Conclusion

We presented RetinexDualV2, a unified and parameter-efficient framework for diverse UHD image restoration tasks. By integrating a Task-Specific Physical Grounding Module (TS-PGM) and Physical-conditioned Multi-head Self attention (PC-MSA), our model explicitly leverages degradation-aware physical priors to guide a dual-branch Retinex decomposition network. This design gracefully overcomes the generalizability limits of purely data-driven black-box architectures, adapting seamlessly to various degradations without task-specific modifications. RetinexDualV2’s robustness is evidenced by its competitive performance, notably securing 4
th
place in the NTIRE 2026 Day and Night Raindrop Removal Challenge and 6
th
place in the Joint Noise Low-light Enhancement Challenge. Our extensive evaluations underscore the value of integrating physical priors, highlighting meaningful progress toward interpretable foundation models for adverse weather image restoration.
