Severe microvascular disease incidence over a 34-year period among Chinese with newly diagnosed diabetes and impaired glucose tolerance: the Da Qing diabetes outcome study 1986–2020
Authors: Qier An, Jinping Wang, Zhihai Huang, Xinxin Feng, Xin Qian, Shuhan Zhou, Siyao He, Hui Li, Guangwei Li, Yanyan Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsThe aim of this study was to investigate the incidence of severe microvascular disease in adults with newly diagnosed diabetes (NDD), impaired glucose tolerance (IGT) and normal glucose tolerance (NGT) over a 34-year follow-up in China.MethodsThis cohort study enrolled 1631 individuals, including 598 NDD, 540 IGT and 493 NGT initially identified in 1986, who were followed until death, loss to follow-up, or December 31, 2020. Severe microvascular disease was defined as a composite outcome of severe retinopathy, nephropathy, and neuropathy.ResultsDuring the 34-year follow-up, 85.18% (460/540) of the IGT population progressed to diabetes, while 34.89% (172/493) of the NGT subjects developed diabetes. By 2020, severe microvascular disease occurred in 65.03% (95% CI: 58.90%-70.48%) of individuals with NDD, 32.90% (95% CI: 28.19%-37.81%) with IGT, and 16.18% (95% CI: 12.64%-20.11%) with NGT. A comparable trend was observed for the cumulative incidence of each individual microvascular complication component across the three groups. The incidence rate per 1000 person-years was 21.7 in the NDD group, which was higher than that in the IGT (9.7) and NGT (4.4) groups. After adjustment for confounders, the risk of severe microvascular disease was 6.3-fold higher in the NDD group (95% CI: 4.65–8.38, P < 0.0001), and 2.2-fold higher in the IGT group (95% CI: 1.58–2.95, P < 0.001) compared with the NGT group.ConclusionsNDD and IGT in Chinese adults were associated with markedly elevated long-term risk of severe microvascular complications. These findings underscore the importance of achieving optimal glycemic control early in the course of diabetes and implementing intensive lifestyle intervention in IGT to slow or prevent progression to diabetes.Keywords: Diabetes, Microvascular disease, Impaired glucose tolerance, Retinopathy, Nephropathy, Neuropathy

Introduction

Diabetes is a significant global public health issue, with projections estimating that the age-standardized prevalence rate of type 2 diabetes will rise to 9.5% by 2050 [1]. As the incidence of diabetes increases, so too does the prevalence of microvascular diseases. Microvascular disease is a common and specific complication of diabetes, primarily encompassing retinopathy, nephropathy, and neuropathy, which are leading causes of adult blindness, end-stage renal disease, and non-traumatic amputations. The crude prevalence of microvascular disease among patients with type 2 diabetes worldwide is approximately 18.8%, with a median duration of 4.1 years before the onset of microvascular complications [2]. A large-scale epidemiological survey in the United States indicated that nearly 30% of adult diabetes patients experienced diabetic retinopathy [3]. Furthermore, the incidence of diabetic peripheral neuropathy can exceed 50% as the duration of diabetes increases [4]. Additionally, around 50% of diabetes patients may simultaneously suffer from nephropathy and neuropathy, imposing substantial health and economic burdens on individuals, families, and public health systems [5, 6]. Our previous study demonstrated that following a 6-year lifestyle intervention trial in individuals with impaired glucose tolerance (IGT), 252 participants progressed to diabetes, 114 maintained IGT status, and 174 reverted to normal glucose tolerance (NGT). The 24-year cumulative incidence of microvascular disease was 36.8%, 21.7%, and 16.5%, respectively [7]. This indicates that transitions from IGT to various glycemic states significantly affect the long-term incidence of microvascular disease. However, there is a lack of large-scale and over 30 years of follow-up cohorts concerning microvascular disease in populations with diabetes and IGT. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the incidence and the risk of severe microvascular disease in adults with newly diagnosed diabetes (NDD) and IGT over a 34-year period.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

The details of the design and participants of the Da Qing IGT and Diabetes Study, along with its subsequent 20-, 23-, and 30-year follow-up studies, have been previously described [8–11]. Briefly, in 1986, 110,660 residents aged 25–74 years were screened in 33 primary care clinics in Da Qing, China, through the measurement of 2-h plasma glucose levels after a standard breakfast. Among them, 3,956 participants with 2-h plasma glucose levels ≥ 6.67 mmol/l after the standard breakfast were administered a 75-g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT). Diabetes, IGT, and NGT were defined according to the World Health Organization (WHO) 1985 criteria based on 75-g OGTT results [12]. Diabetes was defined as fasting plasma glucose ≥ 7.8 mmol/l or 2 h post-load plasma glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/l, IGT as fasting plasma glucose < 7.8 mmol/l and 2 h post-load plasma glucose ≥ 7.8 and < 11.1 mmol/l, and NGT as fasting plasma glucose < 5.8 mmol/l and 2 h post-load plasma glucose < 7.8 mmol/l. Among the screening population, 630 participants were diagnosed with NDD and 576 were identified as having IGT. In addition, 519 individuals with normal glucose tolerance matched for age and sex to the IGT group were included as controls. All participants underwent a baseline examination and received routine medical care from their usual providers. Those with diabetes were informed of the diagnosis and referred to their local clinics for continuing medical care, during which most were treated with oral hypoglycemic agents or insulin. During the 34-year follow-up, 32 participants with NDD, 36 with IGT, and 26 with NGT were lost to follow-up. Therefore, 1631 participants, including 598 with NDD, 540 with IGT, and 493 with NGT, were included in the present study. Among them, 460 patients (85.18%) in the IGT group progressed to diabetes and 80 patients (14.81%) maintained IGT status during 34 years of follow-up; In the NGT group, 172 people (34.89%) developed diabetes and 321 people (65.11%) were in non-diabetes state (Fig. 1). The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board and Ethics Committee of WHO and the Fuwai Hospital. All surviving study participants and the proxies who served as informants for deceased participants gave written informed consent. The investigations were conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki as revised in 2008.

Data collection and outcomes

In 2020, we conducted a follow-up study to determine the incidence rate of severe microvascular disease in the NDD, IGT, and NGT groups. Severe microvascular disease was defined as a composite outcome including severe retinopathy (defined as a history of photocoagulation, blindness from retinal disease or proliferative retinopathy), severe nephropathy (defined as a history of end-stage renal disease, renal dialysis, renal transplantation or death from chronic kidney disease), and severe neuropathy (defined as a history of leg, ankle or foot ulceration, gangrene or amputation). For the 34-year follow-up analysis, we assessed the vital status of participants as of December 31, 2020. For deceased participants, a verbal autopsy was conducted by interviewing an informant to ascertain the date, place, and circumstances of death. Additionally, the dates and causes of death were obtained from death certificates, medical records, and proxy interviews with close relatives. For living participants, data were collected through interviews, clinical examinations, and medical record reviews conducted by trained staff and physicians at Da Qing First Hospital (Da Qing, China). Two physicians, blinded to the participants’ glucose status, independently adjudicated outcomes, and any disagreements were resolved through discussion with a third senior physician. Diagnostic information regarding severe microvascular disease was sourced from medical or death records and proxy interviews.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were employed to compare baseline characteristics. Continuous variables were presented as means (± standard deviation [SD]), while categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Variance analysis and multiple comparison tests were utilized for continuous variables comparisons, and the chi-squared tests were applied for categorical variables. Multiple comparison tests were also conducted for proportions. To ascertain the incidence of severe microvascular disease events, data were censored at the date of the first report of a severe microvascular disease event, death, loss to follow-up, or 31 December 2020, whichever occurred first. The incidence of severe microvascular disease was calculated as the number of cases per 1,000 person-years at risk. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate the cumulative incidence of severe microvascular disease over the 34-year follow-up period, and cumulative incidence curves were generated. Log-rank tests were performed to compare differences between each group. Cox proportional hazards analyses were employed to estimate hazard ratios for differences in severe microvascular disease rates among different glycemic status groups. Model 1 was unadjusted, while Model 2 was adjusted for age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking, and blood pressure. Sensitivity analysis estimated the HRs between the glucose status groups after adjusting for risk factors using the Cox model. Considering that IGT and NGT may progress to diabetes during follow-up, the Cox proportional hazards analysis with diabetes as a time-dependent variable was conducted in the NGT group, IGT group and the combined group of NGT and IGT. Two-tailed p-values were reported, with significance set at < 0.05. All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS, Cary, NC, USA).

Characteristics of changes in incidence among different glycemic status groups at the end of the follow-up

Table 3 showed the incidence of severe microvascular disease per 1,000 person-years and the cumulative incidence during 34 years follow-up according to glycemic status in 2020. In the IGT group, 85.18% (460/540) of the participants progressed to diabetes, while 14.81% (80/540) remained in the IGT state. In the NGT group, 34.89% (172/493) of the subjects developed diabetes, and 65.11% (321/493) were still in non- diabetes state by 2020. In both the IGT group and the NGT group, individuals who progressed to diabetes had a higher incidence of all types of severe microvascular diseases compared to those who remained nondiabetic. Regardless of the glycemic status, the incidence rate of severe retinopathy was higher than that of severe nephropathy, with severe neuropathy having the lowest incidence rate.

Hazard ratios for severe microvascular disease risk among different glycemic status groups

As shown in Table 4, in Model 1, without adjustment for any risk factors, the NDD group and the IGT group had a higher risk of severe composite microvascular disease, severe retinopathy, severe nephropathy and severe neuropathy than the NGT group (all P < 0.05). In Model 2, after adjusting for age, sex, BMI, smoking and systolic blood pressure, the risk of severe composite microvascular disease, severe retinopathy, severe nephropathy and severe neuropathy in the IGT group and the NDD group was still higher than that in the NGT group. Compared with the NGT group, the risk of composite severe microvascular disease was 6.25 (95% CI 4.65–8.38, P < 0.0001) and 2.16 (95% CI 1.58–2.95, P < 0.0001) times higher in the NDD and IGT groups, respectively. In Table 5, after adjusting for the relevant risk factors, for severe composite microvascular disease and severe retinopathy, the risk in the NDD group was higher than that in the IGT-Diabetes group, and the risk in the IGT-Diabetes group was higher than that in the NGT-Diabetes group.

Discussion

The present study provided robust evidence regarding the long-term risk of severe microvascular disease among Chinese adults with NDD and IGT. Over a 34-year follow-up, more than one-third of individuals with NDD developed at least one severe microvascular disease, while nearly a quarter of those with IGT were also affected. After adjusting for potential confounders, the risk of severe microvascular disease was 6-fold higher in the NDD group and 2-fold higher in the IGT group, compared to participants with NGT. During follow-up, in the NGT and IGT population, the risk of severe microvascular disease in patients who progressed to diabetes was about three times that of those in non-diabetes status. Among the individual components of microvascular complications, diabetic retinopathy exhibited the highest cumulative incidence across all glycemic categories. Notably, in the NDD group, the incidence of retinopathy alone surpassed the combined occurrence of nephropathy and peripheral neuropathy. Our findings underscore that both diabetes and IGT are associated with substantial long-term risks of severe microvascular diseases, indicating that dysglycemia-even at the prediabetic stage—is far from benign. Preventing the progression of NGT and IGT to diabetes is crucial to reduce the occurrence and long-term risk of severe microvascular diseases. Moreover, the predominance of severe retinopathy observed in the Da Qing cohort emphasizes the necessity for early prevention and routine screening to mitigate the burden of vision-threatening outcomes.

Microvascular disease poses significant health challenges. In our study, the 34-year cumulative incidence of the severe composite microvascular disease in the NDD, IGT, and NGT groups was 65.03% (95% CI 58.90%-70.48%), 32.96% (95% CI 28.19%-37.81%), and 16.18% (95% CI 12.64%-20.11%), respectively. The DISCOVER study revealed that 19.0% of participants had microvascular disease at enrollment. Over the 3-year follow-up, 31.5% of patients developed at least one form of microvascular disease [13]. Additionally, the 3B study indicated that the incidence of microvascular disease among type 2 diabetes mellitus patients with a duration of less than one year approached 20%, while the incidence in patients with a duration exceeding ten years approached 50% [14]. Our study, with follow-up of up to 34 years, provides new evidence regarding the long-term incidence of microvascular disease in the Chinese population, thereby addressing a gap in previous research. Notably, the cumulative incidence of microvascular disease in our study was significantly lower than that reported in the DISCOVER and 3B studies as the duration of diabetes increased. This difference may stem from different definitions of microvascular diseases. The DISCOVER and 3B studies defined microvascular disease as retinopathy, neuropathy (peripheral, autonomic), erectile dysfunction, and nephropathy (albuminuria, chronic kidney disease), while our study required cases to meet the criteria for severe retinopathy, nephropathy, or neuropathy. The restriction of severe microvascular disease makes the definition more precise and rigorous, but it may also underestimate the long-term incidence of microvascular disease in practice. In summary, our study reports the long-term incidence of severe events for the first time. We also investigated the incidence trends of severe microvascular disease over a span of 34 years across three groups. Notably, the onset of severe microvascular disease in the IGT group was delayed by approximately 5 years compared to the NDD group, likely due to the typical progression timeline of prediabetes to diabetes.

Among individual outcomes, retinopathy exhibits the highest cumulative incidence and risk elevation. This reinforces the importance of routine retinopathy surveillance and may be related to the high metabolic demands of retinal tissue and the fragility of capillaries. In addition, diabetic retinopathy is not only a microvascular disease of diabetes, but also a neuropathy. As a part of the peripheral nervous system, the optic nerve is more sensitive to hypoxia, ischemia and metabolic disorders. Diabetes not only damages blood vessels, but also leads to retinal nerve cell dysfunction and apoptosis. A meta-analysis encompassing thirty-one studies with a total of 10,539 prediabetes cases revealed that the prevalence of retinopathy ranged from 0.3% to 20.9%, with a median of 8.1% and an interquartile range of 4.2–11% [15]. The study conducted by White NH et al. included 747 subjects with IGT. After 16 years of follow-up, 127 individuals were diagnosed with retinopathy, resulting in a prevalence of 15% [16]. The Shanghai Diabetic Complications Study (SHDCS) recruited 3,259 Chinese subjects from urban communities in Shanghai, of which 865 were diagnosed with IGT based on ADA criteria. The findings indicated that 22 individuals within the IGT population had DR, yielding a prevalence rate of 2.5%. Among these, 21 individuals (2.4%) were diagnosed with clinically significant macular edema [17]. The variability in the prevalence of retinopathy within the IGT population can be attributed to differences in screening methods, grading techniques for retinopathy, and the characteristics of study populations. Furthermore, these differences in prevalence rates may diminish when accounting for the follow-up period.

Our findings also showed that the risk of severe microvascular disease in the NDD group was 6.25 (95% CI 4.65–8.38, P < 0.0001) times higher than that in the NGT group, while the risk of severe microvascular disease in the IGT group was 2.16 (95% CI 1.58–2.95, P < 0.0001) times higher than that in the NGT group. The results illustrated that optimal control of blood glucose in individuals with diabetes, as well as the prevention of progression to diabetes in the IGT population, can reduce the long-term risk of severe microvascular disease. This view is supported by several studies. In the Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) study [18] and the Diabetes Prevention Program Outcomes Study (DPPOS), the researchers evaluated the prevalence of aggregate microvascular disease at DPPOS year 11 in people who regressed to NGT at least once (vs. never) during the DPP study. The findings revealed that regression to NGT was associated with a lower prevalence of aggregate microvascular disease (OR 0.78, 95% CI 0.65–0.78, P = 0.011). For participants who achieved normal glucose regulation through effective interventions in prediabetes, the overall prevalence of microvascular disease was reduced by 22% to 30% after 10 years compared to those who never regressed. These results further suggest that early interventions aimed at controlling blood glucose levels are beneficial for the prevention of microvascular disease. The 24-year follow-up of the United Kingdom Prospective Diabetes Study (UKPDS) [19] showed that the relative risk of microvascular disease was reduced by 26% (95% CI 14–36, P < 0.0001) in the early intensive glucose control group compared with the conventional glucose control group, and the absolute risk was reduced by 3.5%. The UKPDS study confirmed that early intensive glucose control in NDD patients significantly reduced the risk of microvascular disease, and that the legacy effects of early intensive glucose control showed no sign of waning at the 24-year follow-up. Furthermore, we analyzed the effect of diabetes status as a time-dependent variable on the risks of microvascular disease, adjusting for age, sex, BMI, blood pressure, and smoking. The findings demonstrate that transitions to diabetes substantially increase long-term complication risk, even when dysglycemia is present only transiently or emerges later during follow-up. When IGT and NGT are analyzed together, the diabetes progression is associated with a 3-fold increase. A similar pattern is observed within the IGT subgroup alone, where the hazard ratio for composite microvascular disease reaches about 5-fold higher compared with non-diabetic status. These results clearly indicated that the microvasculature is highly vulnerable to glycemic deterioration, so it is crucial to regard early intervention as a critical prevention strategy.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the incidence of severe microvascular disease in adults with NDD, IGT and NGT over a 34-year period in a Chinese population. This study provides evidence that the risk of severe retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy, and microvascular disease in the NDD and IGT populations is significantly higher than that in the NGT population. It emphasizes that preventing the onset of diabetes or delaying its development during the prediabetes period is crucial not only for curbing the increasing incidence of diabetes but also for effectively reducing the incidence of serious long-term severe microvascular diseases and alleviating the burden on the healthcare system. The study has several strengths. First, it employed a population-based longitudinal design, recruiting individuals with IGT through screening a well-defined population using 75 g OGTTs at baseline. Second, the Da Qing Diabetes Prevention Study is the longest follow-up study in the field of diabetes prevention, with a 34-year follow-up period, providing a sufficient number of severe microvascular disease cases. Furthermore, minimal migration from the city has occurred since the original trial began, which has kept loss to follow-up to a minimum and enabled high completion rates even many years later. Lastly, all participants received standard care from primary care clinics in Daqing, ensuring that most remained within the same healthcare system and hospital throughout the study, facilitating access to their medical records.

The study also has some limitations. The most significant limitation is that the sample size of this study is relatively small, and future cohort studies should involve larger sample sizes. Secondly, in the Cox proportional hazards model, the long-term medication use was not measured, which may underestimate the risk of microvascular disease in the NDD and IGT populations. Thirdly, we did not conduct systematic examinations at regular intervals for participants throughout the follow-up, which limited the outcomes assessed to those identifiable and reliably determined from medical history and other records.

Conclusions

The present study demonstrated that Chinese adults with NDD and IGT faced a significantly increased risk of severe microvascular disease, including severe retinopathy, severe nephropathy, and severe neuropathy over a 34-year period. The results underscore the importance of early blood glucose management and structured lifestyle interventions, especially the combination of exercise and diet intervention to prevent diabetes progression. Proactive diabetes care is essential in reducing the burden of severe microvascular disease, highlighting the long-term benefits of early intervention.
