Charging profiles for unmanaged charging vs. vehicles responding to day-ahead energy prices
 Energy prices were computed using the unmanaged profile as the EV load forecast (zero foresight of price-responsiveness)

 large load spikes

 Source: Lavin and Hale (2026)

• Price-responsive mechanisms like TOU and RTP schedule charging with full information at the individual
 vehicle level, but the responses are not dynamically coordinated with supply.
• The resulting price-chasing behavior can create timer peaks and difficult to serve load ramps.

 Relative Value of Different Dispatch
 Mechanisms with Perfect Foresight
• At low levels of participation, TOU and RTP can
  provide more value per vehicle than DLC because
  they have access to individual vehicles’ full
  flexibility
• At higher levels of participation, and especially for
  within-week flexibility, DLC provides more value by
  directly coordinating charging with electricity supply
• TOU is value-competitive with DLC, especially
  for within-session flexibility
• Note 1: Ramp penalties were applied during
  vehicle-level dispatch to flatten TOU and RTP
  aggregate responses
• Note 2: More realistic plug-in behavior would likely
  move the within-session results in the direction of
  what we see for within-week scheduling
 Source: Lavin and Hale (2026)

Impact is a function of per-
  vehicle value and scale
• Per-vehicle incremental and average value
  declines as more vehicles participate
• Price-responsive mechanisms eventually
  yield negative incremental value
• By directly coordinating with supply, DLC
  incremental value remains positive,
  thereby enabling scale
• In our study, TOU incremental value also
  remains positive for up to 45% of LDVs
  participating within-session
• Positive incremental value adds to bulk
  system savings. In our study, 45% of LDVs
  participating could save 5-10% of
  production costs, but enablement costs
  would have to be very low.                                            National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   15
