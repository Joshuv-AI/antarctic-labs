Exceptional-point-constrained locking of boundary-sensitive topological transitions in non-Hermitian lattices Publication type: Paper
Huimin Wang 1, Yanxin Liu 2, Zhihao Xu 1,∗ and Zhijian Li 1,∗

Abstract
Point-gap topology under periodic boundary conditions and line-gap topology under open boundary conditions are generally inequivalent in non-Hermitian systems. We show that, in chiral non-Hermitian lattices, these two boundary-sensitive topological transitions become locked when the parameter sweep is confined to an exceptional-point (EP)-constrained manifold, such that the Bloch spectrum remains pinned to a zero-energy degeneracy throughout the evolution. In an extended non-Hermitian Su-Schrieffer-Heeger chain, this locking can be established analytically in a tractable limit, where the EP-constrained manifolds and the corresponding PBC and OBC transition boundaries are obtained in closed form, and it persists away from this limit when the generalized Brillouin zone is determined numerically. Outside the EP-constrained manifold, the two transitions generally decouple, even in the presence of isolated EPs or Hermitian degeneracies. We further show that the same mechanism survives in a four-band spinful extension with branch-resolved generalized Brillouin zones, including branch-imbalanced regimes. These results identify EP-constrained band evolution as a simple organizing principle for boundary-sensitive topology in chiral non-Hermitian systems and suggest a useful route for diagnosing non-Bloch topological transitions from periodic-boundary spectral evolution when such spectral information can be accessed in photonic, circuit, and cold-atom platforms.

2.1
Extended non-Hermitian SSH chain and chiral symmetry

We consider a one-dimensional extended non-Hermitian SSH chain with nonreciprocal intracell hopping and staggered reciprocal intercell hopping, as illustrated in Fig.
1
(a). This model provides a minimal setting in which point-gap topology, line-gap topology, and the NHSE can be studied within a chiral two-band structure. Each unit cell contains two sublattices, labeled
A
and
B
, and is indexed by
n
. The real-space Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H=\sum_{n}\Big[

\displaystyle t_{1}\,b_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}+t_{2}\,a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}+\left(t_{3}+\delta\right)\left(b_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n+1}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left(t_{3}-\delta\right)\left(a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n+1}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)\Big].

(1)

Here
a_{n}^{\dagger}
(
a_{n}
) and
b_{n}^{\dagger}
(
b_{n}
) create (annihilate) a particle on sublattices
A
and
B
in the
n
th unit cell, respectively. The inequality
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
introduces nonreciprocal intracell hopping and renders the Hamiltonian non-Hermitian, whereas the intercell couplings remain reciprocal. The parameter
\delta
controls the alternation between the two inequivalent intercell hoppings,
t_{3}+\delta
and
t_{3}-\delta
. Throughout this work, all hopping amplitudes are taken to be real.

Under PBCs, Fourier transformation of Eq. (
1
) yields the Bloch Hamiltonian in the basis
(a_{k},b_{k})^{T}
,

H(k)=\begin{bmatrix}0&h_{2}(k)\\
h_{1}(k)&0\end{bmatrix},

(2)

with

\displaystyle h_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=t_{1}+2\bigl(t_{3}\cos k+i\delta\sin k\bigr),

\displaystyle h_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=t_{2}+2\bigl(t_{3}\cos k-i\delta\sin k\bigr).

(3)

The corresponding band energies are

E_{\pm}(k)=\pm\sqrt{h_{1}(k)h_{2}(k)}.

(4)

Because Eq. (
2
) is purely off diagonal, the model has chiral symmetry,

\sigma_{z}H(k)\sigma_{z}^{-1}=-H(k),

(5)

which enforces the spectral pairing
E_{+}(k)=-E_{-}(k)
and pins Bloch band degeneracies to zero energy. Zero energy therefore plays a distinguished role in the present model and naturally serves as the reference point for the degeneracy structure introduced below.

2.2
Zero-energy degeneracies and EP-constrained manifolds

We now examine the zero-energy degeneracy structure implied by the chiral two-band form of Eq. (
2
). In the non-Hermitian regime, such degeneracies are generically exceptional.

For the two-band Hamiltonian in Eq. (
2
), a zero-energy degeneracy occurs at momentum
k_{0}
when

\det[H(k_{0})]=h_{1}(k_{0})h_{2}(k_{0})=0.

(6)

If only one of the two off-diagonal elements vanishes, namely
h_{1}(k_{0})=0
or
h_{2}(k_{0})=0
, then
H(k_{0})
is defective and hosts a zero-energy exceptional point (EP). By contrast, a nondefective zero-energy degeneracy requires the fine-tuned condition
|h_{1}(k_{0})|=|h_{2}(k_{0})|=0
. Thus, away from special Hermitian or symmetry-enhanced limits, zero-energy band touchings are generically exceptional in the present model.

This observation motivates the notion of an EP-constrained manifold. We define it as a parameter submanifold
\mathcal{M}
in control-parameter space—for example, a line, a surface, or a higher-dimensional region—such that, for every parameter point
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}
, there exists at least one momentum
k_{0}
satisfying

\det[H(k_{0};\lambda)]=0.

(7)

Equivalently, along
\mathcal{M}
the Bloch spectrum remains pinned to a zero-energy degeneracy somewhere in the Brillouin zone throughout the parameter evolution. At generic non-Hermitian points on
\mathcal{M}
, this degeneracy is exceptional, whereas at intersections with Hermitian limits it continuously reduces to an ordinary nondefective band touching. A one-parameter path confined to
\mathcal{M}
will be referred to as an EP-constrained sweep. As we show below, such constrained spectral evolution provides the condition under which the PBC point-gap transition and the OBC real-line-gap transition become locked.

2.3
PBC point-gap winding and OBC non-Bloch topological characterization

We next summarize the boundary-sensitive topological quantities. In non-Hermitian band theory, the complex nature of the energy spectrum gives rise to two distinct notions of spectral gaps, namely point gaps and line gaps
[
2
,
23
]
. As illustrated schematically in Figs.
1
(b) and
1
(c), a point gap with respect to a reference energy
E_{\rm b}
is open when the complex-energy spectrum does not cross the reference point
E_{\rm b}
[Fig.
1
(b)], whereas a line gap is open when the spectrum does not cross a reference line in the complex-energy plane [Fig.
1
(c)]. In the latter case, the bands can be continuously separated on the two sides of the reference line. The distinction between point gaps and line gaps is directly reflected in the corresponding topological phase transitions. A point-gap transition occurs when the complex spectrum crosses the reference point
E_{\rm b}
, so that the winding around that point becomes ill defined and can change across the transition. By contrast, a line-gap transition occurs when the spectrum touches the reference line, so that the separation of bands across that line breaks down and the associated line-gap invariant can change. Since these two gap-closing conditions are generally inequivalent, point-gap and line-gap transitions need not coincide in non-Hermitian systems. One of the central questions of the present work is therefore to identify the condition under which a PBC point-gap transition and an OBC real-line-gap transition become locked to each other.

Under PBCs, the relevant quantity is the point-gap winding number defined from the Bloch spectrum. For a chosen reference energy
E_{\rm b}
, a point gap is open when
\det[H(k)-E_{\rm b}]\neq 0
for all
k
. The associated point-gap winding number is

\nu_{\rm PBC}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dk\,\partial_{k}\ln\det\left[H(k)-E_{\rm b}\right],

(8)

which measures the spectral winding of the Bloch Hamiltonian around the reference energy
E_{\rm b}
as
k
traverses the Brillouin zone. In the two-band model considered here, a nonzero
\nu_{\rm PBC}
signals the presence of the NHSE, and its sign determines the skin localization direction:
\nu_{\rm PBC}=-1
(
+1
) corresponds to left- (right-) directed skin accumulation, whereas
\nu_{\rm PBC}=0
indicates the absence of a unipolar skin effect. In the numerical results below,
E_{\rm b}
is chosen within the relevant open point-gap region and kept fixed along each parameter sweep unless stated otherwise.

Under OBCs, translational invariance is lost and bulk eigenstates generally acquire exponential spatial envelopes. To characterize the OBC bulk topology in the presence of the NHSE, one must replace the Bloch description by a non-Bloch formulation on the generalized Brillouin zone (GBZ). In the thermodynamic limit, this is achieved by replacing
e^{ik}
with a complex variable
\beta
, which leads to the non-Bloch Hamiltonian

H(\beta)=\begin{bmatrix}0&h_{2}(\beta)\\
h_{1}(\beta)&0\end{bmatrix},

(9)

with

\displaystyle h_{1}(\beta)

\displaystyle=t_{1}+(t_{3}+\delta)\beta+(t_{3}-\delta)\beta^{-1},

\displaystyle h_{2}(\beta)

\displaystyle=t_{2}+(t_{3}+\delta)\beta^{-1}+(t_{3}-\delta)\beta.

(10)

The chiral symmetry persists in the non-Bloch description,

\sigma_{z}H(\beta)\sigma_{z}^{-1}=-H(\beta).

(11)

The admissible values of
\beta
are determined by the GBZ condition in the thermodynamic limit, which restores the correct OBC bulk spectrum in the presence of the skin effect.

In this work, the relevant OBC line gap is the real line gap with respect to
\mathrm{Re}(E)=0
. When the non-Bloch spectrum does not intersect the line
\mathrm{Re}(E)=0
, the OBC bulk spectrum is real-line gapped. For the present two-band chiral model, the corresponding OBC topology is conveniently characterized by a non-Bloch biorthogonal Berry phase accumulated along the GBZ. Parameterizing the GBZ by
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi)
through
\beta=\beta(\theta)
, we define
[
19
]

\nu_{\rm OBC}=\frac{\varphi_{B}}{2\pi},\qquad\varphi_{B}=\varphi_{Z+}+\varphi_{Z-},

(12)

with

\varphi_{Z\pm}=-\oint d\theta\,\frac{\langle\psi^{(L)}_{\pm}(\theta)|\,i\,\partial_{\theta}\,|\psi^{(R)}_{\pm}(\theta)\rangle}{\langle\psi^{(L)}_{\pm}(\theta)|\psi^{(R)}_{\pm}(\theta)\rangle}.

(13)

Here the subscripts
\pm
label the two non-Bloch bands with energies
E_{\pm}(\theta)
, which remain paired by chiral symmetry as
E_{+}(\theta)=-E_{-}(\theta)
. The states
|\psi^{(R)}_{\pm}(\theta)\rangle
and
|\psi^{(L)}_{\pm}(\theta)\rangle
are the corresponding right and left bulk eigenvectors of
H(\beta)
, biorthogonally normalized. The invariant
\nu_{\rm OBC}
is well defined only when the real line gap is open, and it can change only when the non-Bloch bulk continuum touches
\mathrm{Re}(E)=0
. In the present two-band setting, it determines the appearance or disappearance of zero-energy boundary modes under OBCs.

3
Locking of PBC point-gap and OBC line-gap transitions in the two-band model

We now turn to the central result of this work: the locking between the PBC point-gap transition and the OBC real-line-gap transition under EP-constrained parameter sweeps. In general, these two transitions are not intrinsically linked, because they are defined from different spectra and controlled by different gap-closing conditions. The former is a point-gap transition of the Bloch spectrum under PBCs, whereas the latter is a real-line-gap transition of the non-Bloch bulk spectrum under OBCs. However, they become synchronized once the parameter evolution is restricted to an EP-constrained manifold. We first establish this mechanism in the analytically tractable limit
\delta=t_{3}
, then show that it persists away from that limit, and finally contrast it with parameter paths outside the EP-constrained manifold, for which the locking is absent.

3.1
Analytically tractable limit:
\delta=t_{3}

We begin with the limit
\delta=t_{3}
, where one of the two intercell channels vanishes and the non-Bloch problem becomes analytically tractable. In this limit, the EP-constrained manifolds, the PBC point-gap transition boundary, and the OBC real-line-gap transition boundary can all be obtained, making the locking mechanism transparent.

3.2
Persistence of locking away from the solvable limit

We next move beyond the analytically tractable limit and consider the generic case
\delta\neq t_{3}
, where both intercell channels remain finite and the GBZ is no longer a simple circle. Although the analytic simplifications are lost, the underlying mechanism remains unchanged: as long as the parameter sweep stays on an EP-constrained manifold, the OBC real-line-gap transition remains locked to the corresponding change in PBC point-gap topology.

The locking disappears once the parameter sweep leaves the EP-constrained manifold. This demonstrates that the synchronization established above is neither generic nor guaranteed by the mere existence of isolated exceptional points or isolated Hermitian band touchings. Taken together, Figs.

The locking between the OBC real-line-gap transition and the PBC point-gap transition is not a generic feature, nor is it implied by the presence of isolated EPs or isolated Hermitian degeneracies. It requires that the entire parameter sweep remain on an EP-constrained manifold, so that the zero-energy degeneracy is maintained continuously along the path. We next show that the same principle persists in a four-band extension.

We now examine whether the locking mechanism established in the minimal two-band model survives in a multiband setting. To this end, we consider a four-band spinful extension in which the non-Bloch problem contains two chiral spectral branches and two branch-resolved generalized Brillouin zones. This setting provides a nontrivial test of whether EP-constrained evolution continues to synchronize the PBC point-gap topology with the OBC real-line-gap transition.

4.1
Four-band model and branch-resolved GBZs

To test whether the locking mechanism established in the minimal two-band model persists in a multiband setting, we consider a four-band spinful extension. As shown schematically in Fig.
5
(a), this model consists of two spin-resolved copies of the extended SSH chain coupled by spin-flip hopping terms. A key difference from the two-band case is that the non-Bloch problem now contains two chiral spectral branches, each associated with its own GBZ.

The real-space Hamiltonian is
H=H_{0}+H_{\mathrm{s}}
, where

\displaystyle H_{0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,\sigma}\Big[t_{1}\,b_{n,\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{n,\sigma}+t_{2}\,a_{n,\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{n,\sigma}

\displaystyle+(t_{3}+\delta)\bigl(b_{n,\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{n+1,\sigma}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr)

\displaystyle+(t_{3}-\delta)\bigl(a_{n,\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{n+1,\sigma}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr)\Big],

(20)

and

H_{\mathrm{s}}=\sum_{n,\sigma}\Big[u\bigl(a_{n,\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{n,\bar{\sigma}}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr)+v\bigl(a_{n+1,\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{n,\bar{\sigma}}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr)\Big].

(21)

Here
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
,
\bar{\sigma}
denotes the opposite spin, and
u
and
v
are the intra- and intercell spin-flip hopping amplitudes, respectively.

Under PBC, the Bloch Hamiltonian in the basis
(a_{\uparrow},b_{\uparrow},a_{\downarrow},b_{\downarrow})^{T}
takes the form

H(k)=\sigma_{0}\otimes H_{0}(k)+\sigma_{x}\otimes H_{\mathrm{s}}(k),

(22)

where
\sigma_{0}
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix and
\sigma_{x}
is the Pauli matrix. The two
2\times 2
blocks are

H_{0}(k)=\begin{bmatrix}0&h_{2}(k)\\
h_{1}(k)&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad H_{\mathrm{s}}(k)=\begin{bmatrix}0&h_{\mathrm{s}}(-k)\\
h_{\mathrm{s}}(k)&0\end{bmatrix},

(23)

where
h_{1}(k)
and
h_{2}(k)
are defined in Eq. (
3
), and
h_{\mathrm{s}}(k)=u+ve^{ik}
. The Hamiltonian preserves chiral symmetry,

\Gamma H(k)\Gamma^{-1}=-H(k),\qquad\Gamma=\sigma_{0}\otimes\sigma_{z},

(24)

and the spectrum separates into two chiral branches,

\displaystyle E_{\pm}^{(1)}(k)

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{\bigl[h_{1}(k)-h_{\mathrm{s}}(k)\bigr]\bigl[h_{2}(k)-h_{\mathrm{s}}(-k)\bigr]},

\displaystyle E_{\pm}^{(2)}(k)

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{\bigl[h_{1}(k)+h_{\mathrm{s}}(k)\bigr]\bigl[h_{2}(k)+h_{\mathrm{s}}(-k)\bigr]}.

(25)

Under OBCs, replacing
e^{ik}
with a complex parameter
\beta
yields the non-Bloch Hamiltonian

H(\beta)=\sigma_{0}\otimes H_{0}(\beta)+\sigma_{x}\otimes H_{\mathrm{s}}(\beta),

(26)

where
h_{1}(\beta)
and
h_{2}(\beta)
are given in Eq. (
10
), and
h_{\mathrm{s}}(\beta)=u+v\beta
.
For a fixed branch energy
E^{(\eta)}
, each branch
\eta=\{1,2\}
gives a quartic equation for
\beta
,

\displaystyle{E^{(1)}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl[h_{1}(\beta)-h_{\mathrm{s}}(\beta)\bigr]\bigl[h_{2}(\beta)-h_{\mathrm{s}}(\beta^{-1})\bigr],

\displaystyle{E^{(2)}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl[h_{1}(\beta)+h_{\mathrm{s}}(\beta)\bigr]\bigl[h_{2}(\beta)+h_{\mathrm{s}}(\beta^{-1})\bigr].

(27)

For each branch, let the four roots be ordered as
|\beta_{1}^{(\eta)}|\leq|\beta_{2}^{(\eta)}|\leq|\beta_{3}^{(\eta)}|\leq|\beta_{4}^{(\eta)}|
. Following the Yokomizo construction
[
29
,
30
]
, the GBZ for branch
\eta
is determined by the condition
|\beta_{2}^{(\eta)}(E)|=|\beta_{3}^{(\eta)}(E)|
. The four-band model therefore supports two branch-resolved GBZ loops, denoted by
{\rm GBZ}^{(1)}
and
{\rm GBZ}^{(2)}
.

When the OBC real line gap is open, the corresponding topology is characterized by the multiband generalization of Eq. (
13
). Concretely, the total real-line-gap invariant is obtained by summing the branch-resolved contributions,

\nu_{\rm OBC}=\sum_{\eta}\frac{\varphi_{B}^{(\eta)}}{2\pi},\qquad\varphi_{B}^{(\eta)}=\varphi_{Z+}^{(\eta)}+\varphi_{Z-}^{(\eta)},

(28)

with

\varphi_{Z\pm}^{(\eta)}=-\oint d\theta\,\frac{\langle\psi^{(\eta,L)}_{\pm}(\theta)|\,i\,\partial_{\theta}\,|\psi^{(\eta,R)}_{\pm}(\theta)\rangle}{\langle\psi^{(\eta,L)}_{\pm}(\theta)|\psi^{(\eta,R)}_{\pm}(\theta)\rangle}.

(29)

Here each
\varphi_{B}^{(\eta)}
is the biorthogonal Berry phase evaluated along
{\rm GBZ}^{(\eta)}
using the left and right bulk eigenstates associated with the branch
E_{\pm}^{(\eta)}
. The presence of two chiral branches and two distinct GBZs thus provides a natural multiband setting for testing the robustness of the locking mechanism beyond the minimal two-band case.

4.2
Locked transitions under EP-constrained sweeps

We first consider a representative EP-constrained sweep in the generic four-band regime, where both chiral branches exhibit appreciable non-Bloch deformation. The main question is whether the locking mechanism identified in the two-band model survives in this multiband setting, despite the branch-dependent GBZ geometry and skin localization. As shown below, changes in the PBC point-gap topology remain locked to the OBC real-line-gap transitions.

A representative example is shown in Fig.
5
for
t_{2}=3.4t_{3}
,
\delta=-0.5t_{3}
,
u=2t_{3}
, and
v=0.6t_{3}

t_{1}/t_{3}
varied along the sweep. For this parameter choice,
h_{2}(\pi)=t_{2}-2t_{3}=1.4t_{3}
and

, so that
E^{(1)}(\pi)=0
throughout the entire sweep. The sweep therefore remains on a zero-energy degeneracy manifold and, away from Hermitian points, is constrained by EPs.

The branch-resolved GBZs at
t_{1}=0.4t_{3}
are shown in Fig.
5
(b).
{\rm GBZ}^{(1)}
lies predominantly outside the unit circle, whereas
{\rm GBZ}^{(2)}
lies predominantly inside it, indicating opposite skin-localization tendencies for the two branches. The resulting spectral evolution is displayed in Figs.

5

t_{1}/t_{3}
. Under PBCs, as in the two-band case, one branch remains pinned to a zero-energy degeneracy at

throughout the sweep, so the PBC spectrum touches

This evolution is captured by the OBC invariant
\nu_{\rm OBC}
shown in Fig.

(e). In the interval

. Once the line gap opens,

Crucially, the PBC point-gap winding number

4.3
Branch-imbalanced non-Bloch response

We next consider a branch-imbalanced regime, in which one spectral branch exhibits pronounced non-Bloch deformation while the other remains close to the conventional Brillouin zone. This provides a more stringent test of the locking mechanism, since the non-Bloch response is concentrated predominantly in a single branch rather than being distributed comparably between the two. As shown below, the synchronization between the PBC point-gap topology and the OBC real-line-gap transition nevertheless remains intact under an EP-constrained sweep.

A representative example is shown in Fig.
6

t_{2}=3t_{3}
,
\delta=-0.5t_{3}

E^{(1)}(\pi)=0
throughout the entire sweep. The evolution is therefore again pinned to a zero-energy degeneracy manifold and, away from Hermitian points, remains EP constrained. As in the previous example, the PBC spectrum passes through zero energy throughout the sweep, so the relevant changes are encoded in the spectral winding around the origin rather than in the opening of a conventional zero-energy point gap.

The branch-resolved non-Bloch geometry is shown in Fig.

Despite this pronounced asymmetry, the topological transitions remain locked. As shown in Figs.

t_{1}/t_{3}=1

|\psi|

5
Conclusion

In this work, we identified a simple condition under which periodic-boundary point-gap topology and open-boundary real-line-gap topology become locked in chiral non-Hermitian systems: the parameter sweep must remain confined to an EP-constrained manifold, so that the Bloch spectrum stays pinned to a zero-energy degeneracy throughout the evolution. Under this condition, changes in PBC spectral winding occur in direct correspondence with OBC real-line-gap transitions and the associated boundary zero modes.

We demonstrated this mechanism in the extended non-Hermitian SSH model. In the analytically tractable limit
\delta=t_{3}
, the EP-constrained manifolds and the corresponding PBC and OBC transition boundaries can be obtained in closed form, making the locking especially transparent. The same correspondence persists away from this solvable limit, where the non-Bloch deformation becomes substantial and the GBZ must be determined numerically. By contrast, once the sweep leaves the EP-constrained manifold, the PBC point-gap transition and the OBC real-line-gap transition generally decouple, even if isolated exceptional points or Hermitian degeneracies are encountered along the path. We further showed that this locking survives in a four-band spinful extension with two branch-resolved GBZ loops. Even when the two branches contribute differently to the skin effect, or when the non-Bloch response is concentrated in a single branch, an EP-constrained sweep still synchronizes the change in PBC point-gap winding with the OBC real-line-gap transition.

Beyond establishing a boundary-sensitive topological correspondence, our results provide an operationally useful diagnostic principle for non-Hermitian quantum systems: under an EP-constrained sweep, changes in PBC spectral winding can be used to infer the onset, disappearance, and topological character of OBC real-line-gap phases and boundary zero modes. Because direct extraction of OBC non-Bloch invariants is often experimentally demanding, this correspondence offers an alternative route for identifying boundary topological transitions from periodic-boundary spectral evolution when such spectral information can be reconstructed or tracked experimentally. The mechanism should be relevant to experimentally accessible non-Hermitian platforms, including photonic lattices, topolectrical circuits, and synthetic quantum simulators with controllable gain, loss, or nonreciprocal couplings
[
42
,
43
,
44
,
62
,
8
]
. More broadly, our work identifies EP-constrained band evolution as a natural organizing principle for boundary-sensitive topology in chiral non-Hermitian systems and suggests a simple route toward diagnosing non-Bloch topological transitions in multiband quantum platforms
[
48
,
49
,
50
]
.
