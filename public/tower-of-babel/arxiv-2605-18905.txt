Stability and Discretization Error of State Space Model Neural Operators
Abderrahim Bendahi, Adrien Fradin, Johan Peralez, Julie Digne, Madiha Nadri

Abstract
Neural operators have emerged as a powerful, discretization-invariant framework for solving partial differential equations (PDEs). Although established approaches like the Deep Operator Network (DeepONet) have successfully achieved universal approximation for operators, and architectures such as Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) have shown algebraic convergence rates, a precise theoretical connection between the continuous theory and its discrete numerical implementation remains a challenge. Specifically, the relationship between the continuous formulation and the discrete numerical stability has yet to be fully explored. In this paper, we address this gap by establishing theoretical guarantees for the discretization error and stability of neural operator approximation schemes. We prove analytical bounds that link solution regularity to input discretization, providing a formal quantification of neural operator accuracy under real-world numerical constraints. We derive these bounds to the specific cases of State Space Model-based Neural Operators (SS-NOs) and FNOs, thus providing a new discretization error theorem for these models. Additionally, through an input-to-state stability (ISS) analysis, we formally assess the impact of discretization on the stability of SS-NOs results obtained in the continuous domain. Our empirical experiments on 1D and 2D benchmarks validate our theoretical bounds and show the robustness of SS-NOs under varying resolutions.



1
Introduction

Scientific machine learning increasingly uses neural networks to solve partial differential equations
Brunton and Kutz 2023
. Since solving PDEs amounts to learning solution operators, neural operators (NOs)
Kovachki et al. 2023
have emerged as models between infinite-dimensional function spaces that can generalize across grid resolutions. Architectures such as DeepONet
Lu et al. 2021
, Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs)
Li et al. 2021
, and State Space Neural Operators (SS-NOs) are defined in the continuous domain, but their implementation requires discretizing the convolution kernel. Most analyses focus on the continuous setting
Kovachki et al. 2021
, leaving open how discretization affects accuracy and stability. While discretization error has recently been studied for FNOs
Lanthaler et al. 2025
, analogous guarantees for SS-NOs are still missing.

In this work, we bridge this gap through three contributions:

•

Continuous stability.
We prove Lipschitz stability of deep SS-NO architectures in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.

•

Discretization error.
We derive explicit continuous-to-discrete error bounds that account for the kernel and activation regularity. Compared with prior FNO results
Lanthaler et al. 2025
, our analysis covers fractional Sobolev regularity, more general convolution kernels, and non-smooth activations such as
ReLU
and
LeakyReLU
.

•

Discrete stability.
We prove a global stability bound for the discretized model, including both discretization error and input perturbations.

Related Work

Neural operators aim to approximate mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces
Boullé and Townsend 2024
;
Lanthaler et al. 2025
;
Kovachki et al. 2024
. Unlike classical neural networks designed for finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces
Boullé and Townsend 2024
;
Kovachki et al. 2023
;
Li et al. 2021
, neural operators are discretization-invariant, allowing them to share model parameters across different grid resolutions and providing significant speedups over traditional numerical solvers
Boullé and Townsend 2024
;
Li et al. 2023a
;
Li et al. 2021
.

Foundational Approaches in Neural Operator Learning

Deep Operator Networks (DeepONets) are a seminal neural-operator architecture based on the universal approximation theorem for operators, using a branch net for input functions and a trunk net for output locations
Lu et al. 2021
. They achieve high-order error convergence and reduced generalization error compared to fully-connected networks on dynamic systems and PDEs
Lu et al. 2021
. Theory has mainly studied universal approximation for neural operators composed of linear integral operators and nonlinear activations
Kovachki et al. 2023
;
Boullé and Townsend 2024
. Subsequent architectures use graph kernels
Li et al. 2020
;
Anandkumar et al. 2020
, learned physical-space kernels
Gin et al. 2020
, physics priors
Li et al. 2024
, or geometry-aware designs
Li et al. 2023b
. We focus on two theoretically relevant neural-operator families below.

Fourier Neural Operators

The Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) introduced by
Li et al. 2021
parameterize the integral kernel in Fourier space, enabling the modeling of turbulent flows with high efficiency. To handle complex geometries beyond uniform grids,
Li et al. 2023a
proposed Geo-FNO, which uses a learnable deformation to map irregular domains into a canonical latent space. On the theoretical side,
Lanthaler et al. 2025
established convergence rates for FNOs, quantifying the discretization error between the continuous operator and its numerical implementation. We extend their results to SS-NOs, and beyond discretization error, we study the discrete stability of SS-NOs.

State Space Models and SS-NO

In parallel,
Gu et al. 2022
introduced S4D, a simplified diagonal state space model (SSM) that captures long-range dependencies with minimal computational overhead.
Building on this, the State Space Neural Operator (SS-NO)
Koren and Lanthaler 2025
extends SSMs to joint spatiotemporal modeling.
This architecture introduces adaptive damping and learnable frequency modulation, proving a universality theorem for full field-of-view convolutional architectures while maintaining high parameter efficiency on benchmarks like Navier-Stokes
Koren and Lanthaler 2025
.
Related efforts in computational efficiency also include generative frameworks like LD3
Tong et al. 2025
, which optimize time discretization to improve sampling efficiency in diffusion probabilistic models without retraining.
However, a rigorous theoretical quantification of the discretization error and numerical stability for SS-NO architectures is currently missing. We address this gap by establishing explicit error bounds linked to the kernel parameters.

4.2
Global ISS under Discretization Errors

Based on the previous approximation error analysis, we now turn to a global stability assessment. Specifically, we examine the operator’s behavior across different latent states, through an Input-to-State Stability (ISS) analysis. To do so, we establish the stability properties of the discretized SS-NO layers, viewed as nonlinear operators acting on grid functions over the discrete torus
\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d}

T

Theorem 4.4

(Global Stability of Discretized SS-NO)

Let

T

\|\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(T)}(v)-\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(T)}(w)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})}\leq\mathbf{C}_{N,T}\|v-w\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})}
, where

\mathbf{C}_{N,T}:=\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}L_{\sigma_{t}}\left(\|W_{t}\|+\|\mathcal{K}_{N,t}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})}\right).

(10)

Let

T

6
Conclusion

This work develops a rigorous continuous-to-discrete theory for convolution-based neural operators, with a focus on SS-NOs. We derive explicit discretization-error bounds that yield an algebraic convergence rate in the spatial resolution under standard Sobolev regularity assumptions and mild spectral conditions on the kernels (e.g., frequency cutoff or decay). In parallel, we establish stability estimates both in the continuous model and for its discretized implementation, that quantify how input perturbations and numerical errors propagate across layers. Numerical experiments in 1D and 2D corroborate our theory and highlight the practical role of regularity assumptions. Overall, these results provide a rigorous pathway to reason about resolution generalization and stability of neural operators. They also suggest principled guidelines for choosing kernels, activations, and depth, to control discretization effects in operator learning.

Appendix A
Fourier Analysis on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}

In this section we briefly recall the basic notions of Fourier analysis on the flat torus and on its discrete approximation. The
d
–dimensional flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is defined as the quotient space

\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\,\mathbb{Z}^{d},

that is,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with points differing by an element of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
are identified. It is a compact
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
manifold without boundary and can be canonically identified with the fundamental domain

[0,1)^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Throughout, we identify functions
v\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{H}
with
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
–periodic functions
v\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{H}
. We also recall the definition of the uniform grid
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
used to approximate integrals over
\mathbb{T}^{d}
:

\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\simeq\frac{1}{N}[N]^{d}:=\left\{\left(\frac{i_{1}}{N},\ldots,\frac{i_{d}}{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\,:\,\text{for all $j\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}$, $0\leq i_{j}<N$}\right\}.

A.1
Fourier Transform and Inversion Formula

Definition A.1

(Fourier Transform on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
)
.

For
f\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
, the continuous Fourier transform on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is defined by:

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(x)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Then the inversion formula is

f(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot x},\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Definition A.2

(Discrete Fourier Transform (on
(\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{N\mathbb{Z}}}^{d}
))
.

For
u\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
, the discrete Fourier transform on
(\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{N\mathbb{Z}}})^{d}
is defined by:

\widehat{u}_{N}(\xi):=\dfrac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}u(x)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot x},\quad\xi\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}^{d}.

Then the inversion formula is

u(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\widehat{u}_{N}(\xi)e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot x},\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}.

A.2
Convolution Operator and Some Properties

We state below some properties of the Fourier transform and its discrete counterpart.

Lemma A.1

(Fourier Transform and Convolution)
.

For any kernel
\mathcal{K}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{m\times n})
and any function
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have:

\widehat{(\mathcal{K}*v)}(\xi)=\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(\xi)\widehat{v}(\xi),\quad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}$}.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{(\mathcal{K}*v)}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mathcal{K}*v)(x)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)v(y)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle{=}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)v(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot(x-y)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)v(y)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(\xi)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}v(y)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(\xi)\widehat{v}(\xi),

as claimed. The permutation of integrals in (
4.1
) follows from the Fubini–Tonelli theorem and the fact that on the compact
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq\sqrt{\left\lvert\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\right\rvert}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

which follows by Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Fourier Transform and Product)
.

For any kernel
\mathcal{K}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{m\times n})
and any function
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have:

\widehat{(\mathcal{K}\times v)}(\xi)=\left(\widehat{\mathcal{K}}*\widehat{v}\right)(\xi)=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{v}(\eta),\quad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}$}.

Proof.

We have:

\displaystyle\widehat{(\mathcal{K}\times v)}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{K}(x)v(x)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{K}(x)\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{v}(\eta)e^{2i\pi\eta\cdot x}e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{K}(x)e^{-2i\pi(\xi-\eta)\cdot x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\widehat{v}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{v}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\left(\widehat{\mathcal{K}}*\widehat{v}\right)(\xi),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have

\sum_{k\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}^{d}}e^{\frac{2i\pi}{N}\,\xi\cdot k}=\begin{cases}N^{d},&\text{if $\xi\equiv 0\,[N]$;}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let us fix
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}^{d}}e^{\frac{2i\pi}{N}\,\xi\cdot k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\prod_{r=1}^{d}e^{\frac{2i\pi}{N}\,\xi_{r}k_{r}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{r=1}^{d}\sum_{k_{r}=0}^{N-1}e^{\frac{2i\pi}{N}\,\xi_{r}k_{r}},

and, for
r\in\left\{1,2,\ldots,d\right\}
fixed, we have

\sum_{k_{r}=0}^{N-1}e^{\frac{2i\pi}{N}\,\xi_{r}k_{r}}=\begin{cases}N,&\text{ if }\xi_{r}\equiv 0\,[N];\\
0,&\text{ otherwise;}\\
\end{cases}

hence, this gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}^{d}}e^{\frac{2i\pi}{N}\,\xi\cdot k}

\displaystyle=\prod_{r=1}^{N}N\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\xi_{r}\equiv 0\,[N]\right\}}

\displaystyle=N^{d}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\xi\equiv 0\,[N]\right\}},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

For any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we have

\dfrac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}f(y)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(mN),

and

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\widehat{f}(0).

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, then, we have

f(y)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot y},

and it follows that:

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}f(y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot y}

(12)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\left[\dfrac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{k\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}e^{\frac{2i\pi}{N}\,\xi\cdot k}\right]

(13)

\displaystyle{=}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\xi\equiv 0\ [N]\right\}}

(14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(mN).

(15)

Moreover, notice that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot y}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\underbrace{\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)}_{=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $\xi\neq 0$;}\\
1,&\text{if $\xi=0$;}\end{cases}}=\widehat{f}(0),

after switching the sum of the integral. This achieves the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark A.1
.

Notably, the above lemma shows that the evaluation of a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
on the grid can be rewritten as

\dfrac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}f(y)-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{f}(mN).

Lemma A.5

(Continuous Parseval’s Identity on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with Fourier coefficients

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Then Parseval’s identity holds:

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left\lvert\widehat{f}(\xi)\right\rvert^{2}.

Lemma A.6

(Discrete Parseval’s Identity on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
)
.

For any function
f\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
, we have

\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{2}:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert^{2}=N^{d}\sum_{\xi\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\left\lvert\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi+mN)\right\rvert^{2}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left\langle f(y),\overline{f(y)}\right\rangle

(16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left\langle\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot x},\overline{\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\eta)e^{2i\pi\eta\cdot x}}\right\rangle

(17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot x}\overline{\widehat{f}(\eta)}e^{-2i\pi\eta\cdot x}

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}(\eta)}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}e^{2i\pi(\xi-\eta)\cdot x}

(19)

\displaystyle{=}N^{d}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}(\eta)}\mathbbm{1}_{\xi\equiv\eta\,[N]}

(20)

\displaystyle=N^{d}\sum_{r\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\sum_{\xi,\eta\in(r+N\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\widehat{f}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}(\eta)}

(21)

\displaystyle=N^{d}\sum_{r\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\left\lvert\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(r+mN)\right\rvert^{2},

(22)

as claimed.
∎

Function Spaces and Embeddings

This appendix collects notation, definitions, and standard results on function spaces used throughout the paper.

B.1
Notation and Preliminaries

B.1.1
Multi-Index Notation

Following standard notations, we let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
be a
multi-index
, and we define its
length
as
\smash{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}}
. Moreover, given
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the monomial of degree
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert
as

x^{\alpha}:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}.

For a sufficiently regular function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{K}
or
f\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{K}
with
\mathbb{K}
being
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, the partial derivative of order
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert
corresponding to the multi-index
\alpha
is denoted by

\partial^{\alpha}f:=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}f}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{d}}^{\alpha_{d}}f=\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}f.

and, when convenient, we write
D^{\alpha}f
for
\partial^{\alpha}f
.

This notation is used in the definition of the functions spaces manipulated in this work.

B.2
Function Spaces

Since we work with functions defined either on the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
(for instance, the input functions of the neural operator) or on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(such as activation functions, including
ReLU
,
GELU
and others), we formulate the function spaces below in both settings, for clarity and self-containedness. For simplicity, we assume all functions to be
real-valued
.

Before introducing the various function spaces, we recall the notion of the support of a function, which will be used repeatedly throughout this subsection.

Definition B.1

(Support of a Function)
.

Let
E
denote either
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, the support of a function
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\mathrm{supp}\,(f):=\overline{\left\{x\in E\,:\,f(x)\neq 0\right\}},

which is the
closure
(in
E
, with respect to its canonical topology) of points
x\in E
where
f
does not vanish.

B.2.1
Smooth Compactly Supported Functions, Schwartz Space and Tempered Distributions

Case of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Definition B.2

(Smooth Functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
.

We define the space
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of
real-valued
and
smooth
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,:\,D^{\alpha}f\text{ exists and is \emph{continuous} on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ for every multi-index }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}\right\}.

Equivalently,
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if all partial derivatives of every order exist (pointwise) and are continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We endow
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the Fréchet topology generated by the family of seminorms

\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,n}:=\sup_{\left\|x\right\|\leq n}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}f(x)\right\rvert,

where
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition B.3

(Smooth Compactly Supported Functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
.

The space
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of real-valued,
smooth
and
compactly supported
functions is

\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,:\,\mathrm{supp}\,(f)\text{ is compact in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}.

For notational convenience, when needed, we write
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of smooth, real-valued functions
f
whose support lies in the compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It follows that

\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigcup_{K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ compact}}\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Definition B.4

(Schwartz Space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(
Bahouri et al. 2011a
, Definition 1.18)
)
.

The Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of smooth functions
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have

\left\|f\right\|_{k,\mathcal{S}}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq k\end{subarray}}\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1+\left\|x\right\|_{2}\right)^{k}\left\lvert D^{\alpha}f(x)\right\rvert<+\infty.

Definition B.5

(Tempered Distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(
Bahouri et al. 2011a
, Definition 1.20)
)
.

The set
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of tempered distributions consists of all continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. More precisely,
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if, and only if there exists a constant
C>0
, and an integer
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\left\lvert\left\langle u,\phi\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq C\left\|\phi\right\|_{k,\mathcal{S}},

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Following
Bahouri et al. 2011a
, a sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of tempered distributions is said to converge to
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if, and only if

\left\langle u_{n},\phi\right\rangle\xrightarrow[n\to+\infty]{}\left\langle u,\phi\right\rangle,

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Case of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

In the case of the
d
-dimensional torus, which is compact, any real-valued smooth function
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is automatically compactly supported, i.e.,
Definitions

B.2
and
B.3
are equivalent. Consequently, the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
(which in the Euclidean setting lies strictly between
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) coincides in this case with both
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. That is,

\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})=\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{T}^{d})=\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

As a result, in the case of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, the space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of tempered distributions coincides with the space of distributions

\left(\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\right)^{\prime},

namely the topological dual of
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

hence by taking the square and integrating over
\mathbb{T}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}v-\mathcal{L}w\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\sigma}\left\|W(v-w)+\mathcal{K}*(v-w)\right\|_{L^{2}}

(25)

\displaystyle\leq L_{\sigma}(\left\|W(v-w)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathcal{K}*(v-w)\right\|_{L^{2}}).

(26)

The first term of the sum is bounded by
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|W\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\left\|v-w\right\|_{L^{2}}
. For the second term, we have for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{K}*(v-w)(x)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)(v-w)(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right\|

(27)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left\|\mathcal{K}(x-y)(v-w)(y)\right\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}(x-y)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\left\|(v-w)(y)\right\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

(29)

\displaystyle=({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}*\left\|(v-w)(\cdot)\right\|)(x),

(30)

where the convolution product between two functions
f
and
g
is defined by
(f*g)(x)={\displaystyle\int f(x-y)g(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{K}*(v-w)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left\|\mathcal{K}*(v-w)(x)\right\|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

(31)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}*\left\|(v-w)(\cdot)\right\|)(x)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

(32)

\displaystyle=\left\|{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}*\left\|(v-w)(\cdot)\right\|\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(33)

and using
Young’s convolution inequality
(see
Lemma

G.4
), it follows that,

\displaystyle\left\|{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}*\left\|(v-w)(\cdot)\right\|\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\right\|_{L^{1}}\left\|\left\|(v-w)(\cdot)\right\|\right\|_{L^{2}}

(34)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}(z)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\right)\left\|v-w\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(35)

This implies that

\left\|\mathcal{K}*(v-w)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}(z)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\right)\left\|v-w\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining the previous inequalities yields

\left\|\mathcal{L}v-\mathcal{L}w\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq L_{\sigma}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|W\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}(z)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\right)\left\|v-w\right\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

Theorem
4.4

(Global Stability of Discretized SS-NO)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(T)}=\mathcal{L}_{N,T-1}\circ\dots\circ\mathcal{L}_{N,0}
be the operator representing the composition of
T
discretized SS-NO layers. For any latent states
v,w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{H})
, we have:

\|\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(T)}(v)-\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(T)}(w)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})}\leq\mathbf{C}_{N,T}\|v-w\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})},

(44)

where the global discrete Lipschitz constant
\mathbf{C}_{N,T}
is given by:

\mathbf{C}_{N,T}:=\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}L_{\sigma_{t}}\left(\|W_{t}\|+\|\mathcal{K}_{N,t}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})}\right).

(45)

Proof of
Theorem

4.4
.

To establish the global stability of the deep architecture, we first characterize the Lipschitz property of a single layer. Let
v,w\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{H}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we have by the
L_{\sigma}
-Lipschitz continuity of
\sigma
:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}_{N}v-\mathcal{L}_{N}w\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\sigma}\left\|W(v-w)+\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\mathcal{K}(\cdot-y)(v-w)(y)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\sigma}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|W\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\left\|v-w\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}+\left\|\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\mathcal{K}(\cdot-y)(v-w)(y)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}L_{\sigma}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|W\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\left\|v-w\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\left\|v-w\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{N,\sigma}\left\|v-w\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})},

with
C_{N,\sigma}:=L_{\sigma}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|W\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\right)
. Inequality (
4.1
) follows from the
discrete Young’s convolution inequality
(see
Lemma

G.4
) on the finite
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
.

Now, we extend this result to the full sequence of
T
layers by induction. Let
\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(T)}=\mathcal{L}_{N,T-1}\circ\dots\circ\mathcal{L}_{N,0}
. For any
t\in\{0,\dots,T-1\}
, each layer
\mathcal{L}_{N,t}
satisfy:

\|\mathcal{L}_{N,t}(v)-\mathcal{L}_{N,t}(w)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})}\leq C_{N,\mathcal{L}}^{(t)}\|v-w\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})},

where
C_{N,\mathcal{L}}^{(t)}=L_{\sigma_{t}}(\|W_{t}\|+\|\mathcal{K}_{N,t}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})})
. By the property of composition of Lipschitz maps, the Lipschitz constant of the sequence is the product of the individual layer constants:

\|\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(T)}(v)-\mathcal{L}_{N}^{(T)}(w)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})}\leq\left(\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}C_{N,\mathcal{L}}^{(t)}\right)\|v-w\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{N}^{d})},

yielding the global constant
\mathbf{C}_{N,T}
.
∎

Appendix D

D.1
Fourier Transform of FNO and SS-NO Kernels

Lemma
4.1

(Fourier Transform of FNO Kernel)
.

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have:

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}^{\textnormal{FNO}}(\xi)=\sum_{k\in\left\{-K,\ldots,K\right\}^{d}}P_{t}^{(k)}\delta_{k}(\xi)=P_{t}^{(\xi)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\xi=k\right\}},

(46)

where we let
P_{t}^{(\xi)}=0
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{-K,\ldots,K\right\}^{d}
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

By linearity of the Fourier transform, we have, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}^{\textnormal{FNO}}(\xi)=\sum_{k\in\left\{-K,\ldots,K\right\}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}^{(k)}(\xi),

and, by definition of
\mathcal{K}_{t}^{(k)}
in (
3
), for any
k\in\left\{-K,\ldots,K\right\}^{d}
we have

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}^{(k)}(\xi)=P_{t}^{(k)}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{2i\pi k\cdot z}e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot z}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.=P_{t}^{(k)}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{2i\pi(k-\xi)\cdot z}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=P_{t}^{(k)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\xi=k\right\}}.

Therefore,

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}^{\textnormal{FNO}}(\xi)=\sum_{k\in\left\{-K,\ldots,K\right\}^{d}}P_{t}^{(k)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\xi=k\right\}}=P_{t}^{(\xi)}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\xi=k\right\}},

(47)

as claimed.
∎

Lemma
4.2

(Fourier Transform of SS-NO Kernel)
.

For any
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}^{\textnormal{SS-NO}}(\xi)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{t,k,i}\left[F_{+,k,i}(\xi_{i})A_{k,+}+F_{-,k,i}(\xi_{i})A_{k,-}\right],

where
A_{k,\varepsilon}:=C_{t,\varepsilon}^{(k)}\left(B_{t,\varepsilon}^{(k)}\right)^{\top}
for
\varepsilon\in\left\{+,-\right\}
, and

\displaystyle F_{+,k,i}(\xi_{i})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1-e^{-(\rho_{t,k,i}-i(\omega_{t,k,i}-2\pi\xi_{i}))/2}}{\rho_{t,k,i}-i(\omega_{t,k,i}-2\pi\xi_{i})}

(48)

\displaystyle F_{-,k,i}(\xi_{i})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1-e^{-(\rho_{t,k,i}+i(\omega_{t,k,i}-2\pi\xi_{i}))/2}}{\rho_{t,k,i}+i(\omega_{t,k,i}-2\pi\xi_{i})}.

(49)

Proof of
Lemma

4.2
.

Let’s compute the exact Fourier transform of the SS-NOs kernel (defined in (
4
).

In
1
D, the kernel is given by:

\mathcal{K}_{t}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{t,k}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{z\geq 0}e^{-\rho_{t,k}\left\lvert z\right\rvert}e^{i\omega_{t,k}z}C_{t,+}^{(k)}(B_{t,+}^{(k)})^{\top}+\mathbbm{1}_{z\leq 0}e^{-\rho_{t,k}\left\lvert z\right\rvert}e^{i\omega_{t,k}z}C_{t,-}^{(k)}(B_{t,-}^{(k)})^{\top}\right],

periodized on
\mathbb{T}^{1}=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
. For integer Fourier mode
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the coefficient is:

\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(n)=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{K}_{t}(z)e^{-2\pi inz}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{t,k}\left[F_{+,k}(n)C_{t,+}^{(k)}(B_{t,+}^{(k)})^{\top}+F_{-,k}(n)C_{t,-}^{(k)}(B_{t,-}^{(k)})^{\top},\right]

(50)

where the scalar factors are:

\displaystyle F_{+,k}(n)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-(\rho_{t,k}-i\omega_{t,k})z}e^{-2i\pi nz}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1-e^{-(\rho_{t,k}-i(\omega_{t,k}-2\pi n))/2}}{\rho_{t,k}-i(\omega_{t,k}-2\pi n)}

(51)

\displaystyle F_{-,k}(n)

\displaystyle:=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{0}e^{(\rho_{t,k}+i\omega_{t,k})z}e^{-2i\pi nz}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1-e^{-(\rho_{t,k}+i(\omega_{t,k}-2\pi n))/2}}{\rho_{t,k}+i(\omega_{t,k}-2\pi n)}

(52)

Notice that, when
\left\lvert n\right\rvert\to\infty
, we have:

F_{+,k}(n)=\dfrac{1}{-2i\pi n}(1+O(\left\lvert n\right\rvert^{-1}))

and:

F_{-,k}(n)=\dfrac{1}{-2i\pi n}(1+O(\left\lvert n\right\rvert^{-1}))

so
\left\lvert\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(n)\right\rvert\sim\Theta\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)
and hence
\left\lvert\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}\right\rvert
is not
L^{1}
(unless we truncate).

Now for the
d
-dimensional version, with a multi-index
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{t,k,i}\left[F_{+,k,i}(\xi_{i})C_{t,+}^{(k)}(B_{t,+}^{(k)})^{\top}+F_{-,k,i}(\xi_{i})C_{t,-}^{(k)}(B_{t,-}^{(k)})^{\top}\right],

(54)

where

\displaystyle F_{+,k,i}(\xi_{i})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1-e^{-(\rho_{t,k,i}-i(\omega_{t,k,i}-2\pi\xi_{i}))/2}}{\rho_{t,k,i}-i(\omega_{t,k,i}-2\pi\xi_{i})}

(55)

\displaystyle F_{-,k,i}(\xi_{i})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1-e^{-(\rho_{t,k,i}+i(\omega_{t,k,i}-2\pi\xi_{i}))/2}}{\rho_{t,k,i}+i(\omega_{t,k,i}-2\pi\xi_{i})}.

(56)

∎

.

We have:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}_{t}^{\textnormal{SS-NO}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}_{t}^{\textnormal{SS-NO}}(x)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}Kd\sum_{i\in[d]}\sum_{k\in[K]}\left\lvert c_{t,k,i}\right\rvert^{2}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|C_{t,+}^{(k)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|B_{t,+}^{(k)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|C_{t,-}^{(k)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|B_{t,-}^{(k)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(Kd)^{2}N^{d}\sup_{i\in[d],k\in[K]}\left\lvert c_{t,k,i}\right\rvert^{2}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|C_{t,+}^{(k)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|B_{t,+}^{(k)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|C_{t,-}^{(k)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|B_{t,-}^{(k)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\right)^{2},

as desired

∎

Appendix E
Proof of the Discretizations Error

E.1
Proof Strategy: Error Decomposition

We start by decomposing the error at each SS-NO layer, let
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(0)}\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{t}}
define by

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(0)}(x):=v_{t}^{N}(x)-v_{t}(x),

for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
. We let
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{t+1}}
be the function defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}(x):=\frac{1}{N^{d}}

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\mathcal{K}_{t}(x-y)v_{t}(y)-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{K}_{t}(x-y)v_{t}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

(60)

for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
. Additionally, we consider
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(2)}\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{t+1}}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(2)}(x):=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\mathcal{K}_{t}(x-y)\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(0)}(y),

for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
. The term
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(2)}
corresponds to the error in the inputs at the entry of layer
t
, after the discrete Fourier transform is applied. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}(x)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(2)}(x)=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\mathcal{K}_{t}(x-y)v_{t}^{N}(y)-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{K}_{t}(x-y)v_{t}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

(62)

which is the discretization error related to the convolution. Also, note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t+1}^{(0)}(x):\!

\displaystyle=v_{t+1}^{N}(x)-v_{t+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{t}\left(W_{t}v_{t}(x)+(\mathcal{K}_{t}*v_{t})(x)+b_{t}+W_{t}\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(0)}(x)+\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}(x)+\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(2)}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sigma_{t}\left(W_{t}v_{t}(x)+(\mathcal{K}_{t}*v_{t})(x)+b_{t}\right).

(63)

In the following parts, we bound each component
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}
,
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(2)}
and
\mathcal{E}_{t+1}^{(0)}
defined in (
60
), (
E.1
) and (
63
).

E.2
Bound on
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}

This next lemma provides a first general upper bound on the error
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}
(in the
t^{\textnormal{th}}
layer), and then we will specify for every special case.

Lemma E.1

(A Bound with Sobolev Norm)
.

Let
s>\frac{d}{2}
be a real number, and take
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
then, for any integer
N>0
and any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, if
m^{*}(r)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
denote the unique vector such that
r+mN\in\left\{-\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}
, we have

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left\|\widehat{v}(r+mN)\right\|\leq C_{d,s}N^{-s}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}},

where
C_{d,s}
is a universal constant depending only on the Sobolev exponent
s
and the dimension
d
.

Proof of
Lemma

E.1
.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and any integer
N>0
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{v}(r+mN)\right\|

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|\right)^{-s}\left(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|\right)^{s}\left\|\widehat{v}(r+mN)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|\right)^{-2s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|\right)^{2s}\left\|\widehat{v}(r+mN)\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|\right)^{-2s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|^{2}\right)^{s}\left\|\widehat{v}(r+mN)\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(64)

\displaystyle{\leq}\sqrt{2}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|\right)^{-2s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(65)

where in (
4.1
) we use the well-known inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
, for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
(and the fact that
s>0
). In (
4.1
) we bound second sum in (
64
), which run over points on the grid
r+N\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, by the sum over all
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, resulting in the Sobolev norm of
v
. We now need to bound the remaining sum from (
65
). First, observe by the choice of
m^{*}(r)
that for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}
we have

\left\|r+mN\right\|\geq N,\,\,\text{ i.e., }\,\,\left\|\frac{r}{N}+m\right\|\geq 1,

then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|\right)^{-2s}

\displaystyle{\leq}2\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\frac{1}{1+\left\|r+mN\right\|^{2s}}

\displaystyle=2N^{-2s}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\frac{1}{N^{-2s}+\left\|\frac{r}{N}+m\right\|^{2s}}

\displaystyle\leq 2N^{-2s}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left\|\frac{r}{N}+m\right\|^{-2s}

\displaystyle{=}2N^{-2s}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\left\|\frac{r_{0}}{N}+m\right\|^{-2s}

\displaystyle\leq 2N^{-2s}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\left\|\frac{r_{0}}{N}+m\right\|_{\infty}^{-2s},

(66)

where in (
4.1
) we define
r_{0}
as the unique vector of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
r_{0}\in\left\{-\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}
and
r_{0}\equiv r\,[N]
and, therefore,
m^{*}(r)=r_{0}
and
m^{*}(r_{0})=0
. Then, using the triangle inequality, for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
we have
\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}\geq 1
and

2\left\|\frac{r_{0}}{N}+m\right\|_{\infty}\geq 2\left(\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}-\left\|\frac{r_{0}}{N}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\text{(a)}}{\geq}2\left(\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\geq\left\|m\right\|_{\infty},

where in (
4.1
) we use the fact that
\frac{r_{0}}{N}\in\mathopen{[}-\frac{1}{2}\mathclose{}\mathpunct{},\frac{1}{2}\mathclose{]}
. Injecting (
E.2
) in (
66
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|\right)^{-2s}

\displaystyle{\leq}2^{2s+1}N^{-2s}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\frac{1}{\left(2\left\|\frac{r_{0}}{N}+m\right\|_{\infty}\right)^{2s}}

\displaystyle{\leq}2\left(\dfrac{N}{2}\right)^{-2s}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}^{-2s}

\displaystyle{\leq}2\left(\dfrac{N}{2\sqrt{d}}\right)^{-2s}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\left\|m\right\|^{-2s}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{2\cdot(4d)^{s}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\left\|m\right\|^{-2s}}_{:=c_{d,s}<\infty}\,N^{-2s}

\displaystyle\leq c_{d,s}N^{-2s},

(68)

where in (
4.1
) and (
4.1
) we use the fact that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{d}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
(in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
). The bound (
68
) follows from
s>\frac{d}{2}
. This gives

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left\|\widehat{v}(r+mN)\right\|\leq C_{d,s}N^{-s}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}},

as desired, with constant
C_{d,s}:=\sqrt{2c_{d,s}}<+\infty
.
∎

Lemma E.2

(A First General Result on
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}
)
.

We have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{2}=N^{d}\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\left\|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-(r+mN))\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m\}}\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)\right\|^{2}.

(69)

Proof of
Lemma

E.2
.

Let us define for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, the periodic function

f_{x}(y):=\mathcal{K}(x-y)v_{t}(y),\quad y\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

such that the quantity
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}(x)
(with
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
) becomes

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}(x)=\dfrac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}f_{x}(y)-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f_{x}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

and, using
Remark

A.1
, it follows

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{f_{x}}(mN).

Now, if we denote
g_{x}(y):=\mathcal{K}(x-y)
we have

\displaystyle g_{x}(y)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(\xi)e^{2i\pi\xi\cdot(x-y)},

(71)

and so

\displaystyle\widehat{g_{x}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{x}(y)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

(72)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(\xi)e^{2i\pi\eta\cdot(x-y)}\right)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

(73)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(\eta)e^{2i\pi\eta\cdot x}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi(\eta+\xi)\cdot y}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

(74)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(-\xi)e^{-2i\pi\xi\cdot x},

(75)

then, using the fact that the Fourier transform of a product of two functions is the convolution of their individual Fourier transforms (see
Lemma

A.2
), it follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{f_{x}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{g_{x}}(\eta)\widehat{v_{t}}(\xi-\eta)

(76)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi\eta\cdot x}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-\eta)\widehat{v_{t}}(\xi-\eta),

(77)

hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}(x)

\displaystyle{=}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{f_{x}}(mN)

(78)

\displaystyle{=}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi\eta\cdot x}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-\eta)\widehat{v_{t}}(mN-\eta)

(79)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi\eta\cdot x}\underbrace{\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-\eta)\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{v_{t}}(mN-\eta)}_{:=c_{\eta}}

(80)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi\eta\cdot x}c_{\eta},

(81)

where
c_{\eta}
is a vector in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Therefore, the
\ell^{2}
–norm of
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}
on the grid
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}(x)\right\|^{2}

(82)

\displaystyle{=}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left\|\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi\eta\cdot x}c_{\eta}\right\|^{2},

(83)

and, decomposing each
\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
uniquely as
\eta=r+mN
with
r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the inner sum in (
83
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi\eta\cdot x}c_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi(r+mN)\cdot x}c_{r+mN}

(84)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi r\cdot x}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}c_{r+mN}\right)}_{:=a_{r}}

(85)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi r\cdot x}a_{r}.

(86)

Now, using the fact that for
v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
we have
\left\|v\right\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left\lvert v_{i}\right\rvert^{2}=\left\langle v,\overline{v}\right\rangle
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle{=}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}\left\langle\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi r\cdot x}a_{r},\overline{\sum_{s\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi s\cdot x}a_{s}}\right\rangle

(87)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}a_{r}\overline{a_{s}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N}}e^{-2\pi i(r-s)\cdot x}

(88)

\displaystyle{=}N^{d}\sum_{r,s\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}a_{r}\overline{a_{s}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{r\equiv s\,[N]\right\}}

(89)

\displaystyle=N^{d}\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\left\|a_{r}\right\|^{2}

(90)

\displaystyle{=}N^{d}\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\left\|\sum_{m}c_{r+mN}\right\|^{2}

(91)

\displaystyle{=}N^{d}\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\left\|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-(r+mN))\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{v_{t}}(m^{\prime}N-(r+mN))\right\|^{2}

(92)

\displaystyle=N^{d}\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\left\|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-(r+mN))\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m\}}\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)\right\|^{2},

(93)

which is the claimed result.
∎

Lemma E.3

(Polynomial Decay of Fourier Coefficients
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(\xi)
)
.

Let
d\geq 1
be a integer and
s>\frac{d}{2}
a real number. Assume there exists some real number
\alpha>d
and a finite real constant
C_{d,\alpha}\geq 0
such that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(\xi)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq C_{d,\alpha}(1+\left\|\xi\right\|)^{-\alpha},

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then, there exists a finite real constant
C_{d,s,\alpha}\geq 0
such that the inequality

\left\|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}\leq C_{d,s,\alpha}N^{\max\left\{\frac{d}{2}-s,d-\alpha\right\}}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{H^{s}},

holds.

Proof of
Lemma

E.3
.

By
Lemma

E.2
, we have

\left\|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(1)}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{2}\leq N^{d}\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\left\|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-(r+mN))\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m\}}\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)\right\|^{2}.

Now, for any
r\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}^{d}
, let
m^{*}(r)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the unique vector such that
r+m^{*}(r)N\in\left\{-\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}
.
Then, the inner expression in (
E.2
) reads

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-(r+mN))\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m\}}\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(-(r+m^{*}(r)N))\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\widehat{v}_{t}(qN-r)}_{:=\,A}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\underbrace{\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-(r+mN))\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m\}}\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)}_{:=\,B}

\displaystyle=A+B,

(96)

and, using the well-known inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
we obtain

\left\|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-(r+mN))\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m\}}\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)\right\|^{2}\overset{{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\eqref{277aeb6d-7ab5-4951-94d8-523d924c32d9}}}}{=}\left\|A+B\right\|^{2}\leq 2(\left\|A\right\|^{2}+\left\|B\right\|^{2}),

and we need to bound both
A
and
B
. For
A
we have

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle{=}\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(-(r+m^{*}(r)N))\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\widehat{v}_{t}(qN-r)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(-(r+m^{*}(r)N))\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\left\|\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\widehat{v}_{t}(qN-r)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(-(r+m^{*}(r)N))\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\left(\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\left\|\widehat{v}_{t}(qN-r)\right\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle{\leq}C_{d,s}^{2}N^{-2s}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(-(r+m^{*}(r)N))\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2},

(97)

and, for the term
B
, we rely on the decay property of the Fourier coefficients
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(\cdot)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\|B\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-(r+mN))\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m\}}\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(-(r+mN))\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m\}}\left\|\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)\right\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle{\leq}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}C_{d,\alpha}(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|)^{-\alpha}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m\}}\left\|\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)\right\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=C_{d,\alpha}^{2}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|)^{-\alpha}\left\|\widehat{v_{t}}(-(r+m^{*}(r)N)\right\|\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left.\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|)^{-\alpha}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m,-m^{*}(r)\}}\left\|\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)\right\|\right)^{2},

then, by
Lemma

E.1
we have

\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-m,-m^{*}(r)\}}\left\|\widehat{v_{t}}(qN-r)\right\|\leq C_{d,s}N^{-s}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{H^{s}},

and
\left\|\widehat{v_{t}}(-(r+m^{*}(r)N)\right\|\leq\left\|\widehat{v}_{t}\right\|_{\infty}
therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|B\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,\alpha}^{2}\left(\left\|\widehat{v}_{t}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+C_{d,s}^{2}N^{-2s}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\left\{m^{*}(r)\right\}}(1+\left\|r+mN\right\|)^{-\alpha}\right)^{2}.

(98)

Now, since
\alpha>d
and

for all

Lemma

(and more precisely (

hence

Next, from (

97

98

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+C_{d,\alpha}^{2}D_{d,\alpha}^{2}N^{-2\alpha}\left(\left\|\widehat{v}_{t}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+C_{d,s}^{2}N^{-2s}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right)\right),

from where

\displaystyle 2N^{d}

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in\left\{0,\dots,N-1\right\}^{d}}\!\left(C_{d,s}^{2}N^{-2s}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(-(r+m^{*}(r)N))\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\right.

(Truncating Frequency for Fourier Coefficients

.

Proof of
Lemma

E.4
.

Note that, since
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(\xi)=0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

then

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}(\xi)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\infty}(2K_{\textnormal{cutoff}}\sqrt{d})^{\frac{d}{2}+s}\left(1+\left\|\xi\right\|\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}-s},

E.2
) holds, or

Proof of

.

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(2)}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(2)}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2d}}\left\|{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}_{t}(\cdot)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}*\left\|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(0)}(\cdot)\right\|\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}^{2}

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathcal{K}_{t}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{N})}=\Theta(N^{\frac{d}{2}})

), we have:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{K}_{t}*v_{t}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+\left\|\xi\right\|^{2})^{s}\left\|\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}(\xi)\widehat{v_{t}}(\xi)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+\left\|\xi\right\|^{2})^{s}\left\|\widehat{v_{t}}(\xi)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{t}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

and hence
\mathcal{K}_{t}*v_{t}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
Furthermore,
W_{t}v_{t}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
since
W_{t}
is a constant matrix. Using
Corollary

4.2
, we conclude that
v_{t+1}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
∎

.

Appendix G
Useful Identities and Inequalities

Lemma G.1

(Jensen’s Inequality)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function then

1.

(Probabilistic Form) for any random vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[f(X)\right]\geq f\left(\mathbb{E}\left[X\right]\right).

2.

(Deterministic Form) for any vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and scalars
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f(v_{i})\geq f\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}v_{i}\right),

provided
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in[n]
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1
.

Lemma G.2
.

For any vectors
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any scalar
\alpha\geq 1
we have

\left\|a+b\right\|^{\alpha}\leq 2^{\alpha-1}\left(\left\|a\right\|^{\alpha}+\left\|b\right\|^{\alpha}\right).

Proof.

Note that the function
x\mapsto\left\|x\right\|^{\alpha}
is convex over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
since
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is convex (as a norm) and
t\mapsto t^{\alpha}
is convex since
\alpha\geq 1
. Hence, by the Jensen’s inequality (
Lemma

G.1
), we have

\left\|\frac{a+b}{2}\right\|^{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|a\right\|^{\alpha}+\left\|b\right\|^{\alpha}\right),

i.e.,

\left\|a+b\right\|^{\alpha}\leq 2^{\alpha-1}\left(\left\|a\right\|^{\alpha}+\left\|b\right\|^{\alpha}\right),

as claimed.
∎

Lemma G.3
.

For any real numbers
x\geq 0
and
\alpha\geq 0
, we have

(1+x)^{\alpha}\geq C_{\alpha}\left(1+x^{\alpha}\right),

where
C_{\alpha}=\min\left\{2^{\alpha-1},1\right\}\geq\frac{1}{2}>0
.

Proof.

Let us define the function
\phi_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\phi_{\alpha}\colon x\mapsto\dfrac{(1+x)^{\alpha}}{1+x^{\alpha}},

. The function
\phi_{\alpha}
is continuously differentiable over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and its derivative is

\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(x)=\dfrac{\alpha(1+x)^{\alpha-1}(1-x^{\alpha-1})}{(1+x^{\alpha})^{2}}.

From (
G
), if
\alpha\geq 1
then
\phi_{\alpha}
is increasing on
[0,1]
and decreasing on
[1,\infty)
, and
\phi_{\alpha}(0)=\mathop{\mathrm{lim}}\limits_{x\to\infty}\phi_{\alpha}(x)=1
, hence
\phi_{\alpha}\geq 1
. Likewise, if
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, the minimum is attained at
1
, with
\phi_{\alpha}(1)=2^{\alpha-1}
, hence
\phi_{\alpha}\geq 2^{\alpha-1}
. Hence the result.
∎

Lemma G.4

(Young’s Inequality (General Case;
Bahouri et al. 2011a
)
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a locally compact, topological group endowed with a left-invariant Haar measure
\mu
. If
\mu
satisfies
\mu(A^{-1})=\mu(A)
for any Borel set
A\subseteq G
then, for all real numbers
(p,q,r)\in\mathopen{[}1\mathclose{}\mathpunct{},+\infty\mathclose{]}^{3}
such that

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1+\frac{1}{r},

and any functions
f\in L^{p}(G,\mathbb{R},\mu)
and
g\in L^{q}(G,\mathbb{R},\mu)
, the convolution

(formally) defined by

f*g\colon x\mapsto\int_{G}f(y)g(y^{-1}\cdot x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(y),

is well-defined, and
f*g\in L^{r}(G,\mathbb{R},\mu)
. Moreover, we have

\left\|f*g\right\|_{L^{r}(G,\mathbb{R},\mu)}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(G,\mathbb{R},\mu)}\,\left\|g\right\|_{L^{q}(G,\mathbb{R},\mu)}

Lemma G.5

(An Inequality Between Sobolev Norm and Sup-Norm)
.

For any integer
s>0
and any
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
we have

\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}}\geq\left\|v\right\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

By definition of the Sobolev norm, we have

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}:\!

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(1+\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}\right)\left\|\widehat{v}(\xi)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left[\left(1+\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}\right)\left\|\widehat{v}(\xi)\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left\|\widehat{v}(\xi)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|v\right\|_{\infty}^{2},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma G.6
.

For any matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times H}
of the form
A=UV^{\top}
with
U
and
V
two column vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{H}
, the following holds:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|A\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\left\|U\right\|_{2}\left\|V\right\|_{2}.

Proof of
Lemma

G.6
.

If

V=0

and

\alpha>0

\alpha
enhances the suppression of high-frequency modes, yielding smoother samples, as shown in

5
and
6

Appendix L
Additional Stress Test: Under-Resolved Oscillatory Inputs

Beyond the empirical well-behaved discretization analysis of the main text, we include here a deliberately
under-resolved
stress test designed to exhibit a regime where discretization error becomes large in a way consistent with our theory. This experiment addresses the question of whether the continuous-to-discrete analysis is only descriptive of well-behaved settings, or whether it also predicts degradation when the operator is evaluated outside its numerically resolvable regime.

L.1
Experimental design

We consider a fixed 1D SS-NO with
GELU
activation and output resolution
8192
, with randomly initialized parameters kept frozen throughout the experiment. No training is involved: the goal is to isolate the numerical implementation gap of the operator itself.

For each frequency

k\in\{4,8,16,32,64,128\},

we construct oscillatory inputs of the form

u_{k}(x)=\sin(2\pi kx+\phi),

where
\phi
is a random phase. For each
k
, we evaluate the same SS-NO on a very fine grid, which serves as a reference discretization, and then re-evaluate it on coarser grids of sizes

L\in\{16,32,64,128,256,512,1024\}.

The discretization error is measured as the relative
\ell^{2}
difference between the coarse-grid evaluation and the fine-grid reference. We average over multiple random phases.

This setup is intentionally chosen to probe a simple failure mode predicted by the theory: as the input frequency
k
increases, the relevant oscillations become harder to resolve on a coarse grid. Hence one should expect the coarse-grid implementation to depart more strongly from the fine-grid one when
L
is too small relative to
k
. In each subplot we indicate the rough Nyquist threshold
L\approx 2k
, which marks the minimal sampling scale required to begin resolving a sinusoid of frequency
k
.

L.3
Interpretation

This experiment provides a concrete example of a discretization failure regime that is qualitatively predicted by our theory. The point is not that the SS-NO becomes unstable in an optimization sense, but rather that its
