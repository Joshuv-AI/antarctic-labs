A Grid-Rate Condition for Valid Uniform Inference
Emmanuel Selorm Tsyawo

Abstract
Estimating a continuous functional F : 𝒳 → ℝ F:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R} involves specifying L n d L_{n}^{d} nodes on 𝒳 ⊂ ℝ d \mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} for estimation and uniform inference. While asymptotically valid inference requires L n L_{n} to increase with n n , existing fixed- L L rules of thumb and heuristic data-driven approaches lack formal justification. This paper shows that, for functions within a Donsker class, the simple grid-growth condition L n = ω ⁡ ( r n 1 / 4 ) L_{n}=\omega(r_{n}^{1/4}) is sufficient for valid inference for twice continuously differentiable functions estimable at the r n 1 / 2 r_{n}^{1/2} rate. This condition ensures that the approximation error is asymptotically negligible relative to the stochastic variation of the empirical process. Keywords: grid density, multilinear interpolation, weak convergence, empirical process JEL classification: C01, C55

For

2.2
Multilinear interpolation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

A tractable interpolation device in the multivariate setting is multilinear interpolation, with linear interpolation in the preceding subsection as the case
d=1
. Multilinear interpolation extends linear, bilinear, and trilinear interpolation to
d
-dimensional axis-aligned hyper-rectangles:

\mathcal{X}:=\prod_{k=1}^{d}[\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\,\bar{\mathrm{x}}_{k}],

i.e., the Cartesian product of coordinate-wise intervals in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For multilinear interpolation on hyper-rectangles,
F
is evaluated at the
2^{d}
vertices of a cell

\mathfrak{C}_{\bm{\ell}}=\prod_{k=1}^{d}[\mathrm{x}_{\ell_{k},k},\,\mathrm{x}_{\ell_{k}+1,k}],

indexed by
\bm{\ell}=(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d})\in\{1,\ldots,L-1\}^{d}
. On each axis
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, there are
L
equally spaced nodes with mesh

\varepsilon_{k}=\frac{\bar{\mathrm{x}}_{k}-\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{k}}{L-1},\qquad k=1,\dots,d,

where
x_{1,k}
and
x_{L,k}
are set to the endpoints
\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{k}
and
\bar{\mathrm{x}}_{k}
, respectively, for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Then observe that the index set
\mathcal{X}
is the union of all cells:

\mathcal{X}=\bigcup_{\bm{\ell}\in\{1,\ldots,L-1\}^{d}}\mathfrak{C}_{\bm{\ell}}=\prod_{k=1}^{d}[\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\,\bar{\mathrm{x}}_{k}].

For
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathcal{X}
, let

\bm{\ell}_{L}(\bm{x})=\big(\ell_{L,1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\ell_{L,d}(x_{d})\big)

denote the cell index of
\bm{x}
, where

\ell_{L,k}(x_{k}):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}\ell\,\mathbf{1}\{x_{k}\in[\mathrm{x}_{\ell,k},\mathrm{x}_{\ell+1,k})\},

with the convention
\ell_{L,k}(\mathrm{x}_{L,k})=L-1
. Define the local interpolation coordinates

\delta_{L,k}(\bm{x}):=\frac{x_{k}-\mathrm{x}_{\ell_{L,k}(x_{k}),k}}{\varepsilon_{k}},\qquad\varepsilon_{k}=\mathrm{x}_{\ell_{L,k}(x_{k})+1,k}-\mathrm{x}_{\ell_{L,k}(x_{k}),k}.

The
d
-linear interpolant is then given by

\displaystyle F_{L}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}F\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big)\prod_{k=1}^{d}\big(1-\delta_{L,k}(\bm{x})\big)^{1-\iota_{k}}\delta_{L,k}(\bm{x})^{\iota_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}F\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big)\underbrace{\prod_{k=1}^{d}\Big((1-\iota_{k})+(2\iota_{k}-1)\delta_{L,k}(\bm{x})\Big)}_{w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})F\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big),

where

\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})=\Big(\mathrm{x}_{\ell_{L,1}(x_{1})+\iota_{1},1},\dots,\mathrm{x}_{\ell_{L,d}(x_{d})+\iota_{d},d}\Big),\qquad\bm{\iota}=(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{d})\in\{0,1\}^{d}

This enumerates the
2^{d}
vertices of the cell containing
\bm{x}
; see, e.g.,
11

F_{L}(\bm{x})
is a convex combination of the vertex values

F\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big)
,
since the weights

w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})
,

\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}
, are non-negative and satisfy

\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})=1.

Moreover, for any
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}
,
F_{L}\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big)=F\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big)\quad\text{for all }\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}
.

2.3
Interpolation error

For the multivariate interpolant defined above, set the resulting interpolation error as

R_{n}(\bm{x}):=r_{n}^{1/2}\big(F_{L}-F\big)(\bm{x}),\qquad\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}.

The following conditions on
\mathcal{X}
and
F
are useful in characterising this deterministic interpolation error.

Condition 2.1
.

\mathcal{X}=\prod_{k=1}^{d}[\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\bar{\mathrm{x}}_{k}]
is a separable compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\displaystyle\sqrt{C}\geq\max_{1\leq k\leq d}(\bar{\mathrm{x}}_{k}-\underline{\mathrm{x}}_{k})
.

Condition 2.2
.

F
is twice continuously differentiable, and
\displaystyle C\geq\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{1\leq k\leq d}\left|\partial^{2}F(\bm{x})/\partial x_{k}^{2}\right|.

The following result characterises the interpolation error and the growth rate of
L
in
n
needed to make it asymptotically negligible.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under
2.2
and
2.1
,

\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}|R_{n}(\bm{x})|\leq\frac{r_{n}^{1/2}dC^{2}}{8(L-1)^{2}}.

Consequently,
\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}|R_{n}(\bm{x})|=o(1)
whenever
L_{n}=\omega(r_{n}^{1/4})
.

Remark 2.1
.

For functions that are not twice continuously differentiable, such as Lipschitz or Hölder-continuous functions, the interpolation error is controlled by the modulus of continuity. For any continuous function
F
on
\mathcal{X}
,

\|F_{L}-F\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathcal{X}\\
\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta_{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}|F(\bm{x})-F(\bm{y})|,\qquad\delta_{\varepsilon}:=\max_{1\leq k\leq d}\varepsilon_{k};

see

If

is Hölder-continuous with exponent
\alpha\in(0,1]
(with Lipschitz continuity as the special case

\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\big|R_{n}(\bm{x})\big|\leq r_{n}^{1/2}\widetilde{C}\,\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\leq\frac{r_{n}^{1/2}\,\widetilde{C}C^{\alpha/2}}{(L-1)^{\alpha}}

for some constant

L_{n}=\omega(r_{n}^{1/(2\alpha)})
ensures
\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}|R_{n}(\bm{x})|=o(1)
in this setting. The main results are stated under
2.2
because twice continuous differentiability gives the clean benchmark rate
L_{n}=\omega(r_{n}^{1/4})
; the preceding display indicates how the grid-rate condition changes under weaker Hölder smoothness.

3
Inference

Having isolated the deterministic interpolation error
R_{n}(\bm{x})=r_{n}^{1/2}\{F_{L}(\bm{x})-F(\bm{x})\}
, this section studies its effect on inference for the interpolated estimator
\widehat{F}_{L}
. The underlying centred empirical process is
Z_{n}:=r_{n}^{1/2}(\widehat{F}-F)
. The section first shows that interpolation preserves the weak limit of this process under the grid-growth condition, then records the computational algorithm, and finally proves that the resulting interpolated confidence bands have the same asymptotic coverage as the corresponding continuous-index benchmark.

Condition 3.1

3.2
Computation algorithm

The algorithm follows standard sup-
t
procedures for uniform confidence bands, e.g.,
3
and
9
, but makes the interpolation step (as a final step) explicit. This records the operation that is otherwise implicit when estimates computed on a finite grid are plotted as continuous curves.

Algorithm 1

Sup-
t
confidence bands for an interpolated functional
F_{L}

1.

Estimate
F
on the grid.

Fix a tensor-product grid
\mathcal{X}^{L}\subset\mathcal{X}
and compute

\{\widehat{F}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}\}
.

2.

Generate draws and scale estimates on the grid.

Construct draws

\{\widehat{F}^{*(b)}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}\}_{b=1}^{B}

from a conditionally weakly consistent estimator of the law of

r_{n}^{1/2}(\widehat{F}-F)
on
\mathcal{X}^{L}
. For each
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}
, compute

\widehat{\sigma}^{2}(\mathbf{x})
and set

\widehat{\sigma}(\mathbf{x}):=\sqrt{\widehat{\sigma}^{2}(\mathbf{x})}
.

3.

Approximate the grid-point sampling law of the sup-
t
statistic.

For each
b=1,\ldots,B
, compute

T_{n}^{L,(b)}:=\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}}\frac{|Z_{n}^{*(b)}(\mathbf{x})|}{\widehat{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})},\qquad Z_{n}^{*(b)}(\mathbf{x}):=r_{n}^{1/2}\big(\widehat{F}^{*(b)}-\widehat{F}\big)(\mathbf{x}).

Let
t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}
be the empirical approximation to the conditional
(1-\alpha)
quantile of the grid-bootstrap statistic, computed from

\{T_{n}^{L,(b)}\}_{b=1}^{B}
.

4.

Construct grid-point confidence intervals.

For each
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}
, compute

\mathcal{C}_{n,1-\alpha}(\mathbf{x})=\left[\widehat{F}(\mathbf{x})-\widehat{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})\frac{t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}}{r_{n}^{1/2}},\ \widehat{F}(\mathbf{x})+\widehat{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})\frac{t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}}{r_{n}^{1/2}}\right]=:\big[\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x}),\widehat{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbf{x})\big].

5.

Interpolate the centre and confidence bands.

For each
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, return

\widehat{F}_{L}(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\,\widehat{F}\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big),

and

\mathcal{C}_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}(\bm{x})=\big[\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{L}(\bm{x}),\widehat{\mathcal{U}}_{L}(\bm{x})\big],

where

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{L}(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\,\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big),\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{U}}_{L}(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\,\widehat{\mathcal{U}}\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big).

Steps 2 and 3 can be implemented using weighted-bootstrap draws. Let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
be non-negative bootstrap weights with mean one and variance one, independent of the data. For example, a grid-point scale estimate à la
3
is

\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{Q_{F}^{*}(0.75;\mathbf{x})-Q_{F}^{*}(0.25;\mathbf{x})}{\Phi^{-1}(0.75)-\Phi^{-1}(0.25)},\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}

where
Q_{F}^{*}(u;\mathbf{x})
denotes the
u
-th quantile of

\{r_{n}^{1/2}(\widehat{F}^{*(b)}-\widehat{F})(\mathbf{x}):1\leq b\leq B\}
and
\Phi^{-1}(\cdot)
denotes the quantile function of the standard normal. The output of
Algorithm

1
is therefore a function-valued estimate and a function-valued band, both continuous on
\mathcal{X}
, obtained by interpolating the grid-point estimate and the grid-point bands.

Since
\widehat{F}_{L}
is the estimator of

\sup

in-sample. This subsection focuses on the multivariate case, which generalises the univariate case. Define the infeasible confidence band by

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{n,1-\alpha}(\bm{x})=\left[\widehat{F}(\bm{x})\ -\ \widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})\,\frac{t_{n,1-\alpha}}{r_{n}^{1/2}},\ \widehat{F}(\bm{x})\ +\ \widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})\,\frac{t_{n,1-\alpha}}{r_{n}^{1/2}}\right],\quad\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}

\widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})
is the robust bootstrap standard-error function and

Following
Algorithm

1
, first construct the confidence bands simultaneously on the grid:

\mathcal{C}_{n,1-\alpha}(\mathbf{x})=\left[\widehat{F}(\mathbf{x})-\widehat{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})\frac{t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}}{r_{n}^{1/2}},\ \widehat{F}(\mathbf{x})+\widehat{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})\frac{t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}}{r_{n}^{1/2}}\right]=:\big[\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x}),\widehat{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbf{x})\big],\qquad\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}.

Next, for
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, define the interpolated lower and upper endpoints by

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{L}(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\,\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big),\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{U}}_{L}(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\,\widehat{\mathcal{U}}\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big).

The feasible interpolated confidence band is then

\mathcal{C}_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}(\bm{x})=\big[\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{L}(\bm{x}),\widehat{\mathcal{U}}_{L}(\bm{x})\big],\quad\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}.

Equivalently, since the same critical value is used at every grid point, define the interpolant of the grid-point standard error by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}_{L}(\bm{x}):=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\,\widehat{\sigma}\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big).

Then

\mathcal{C}_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}(\bm{x})=\left[\widehat{F}_{L}(\bm{x})\ -\ \widehat{\sigma}_{L}(\bm{x})\,\frac{t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}}{r_{n}^{1/2}},\ \widehat{F}_{L}(\bm{x})\ +\ \widehat{\sigma}_{L}(\bm{x})\,\frac{t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}}{r_{n}^{1/2}}\right],\quad\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}.

In order to examine the asymptotic validity of the interpolated confidence bands
\mathcal{C}_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}
, the following condition imposes a weighted-bootstrap approximation and robust studentisation used to compute the sup-
t
critical value.

Condition 3.2

(Bootstrap validity and robust studentisation)
.

Let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
be bootstrap weights, independent of the data, satisfying
\xi_{i}\geq 0
,
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{i}]=1
,
\mathbb{V}[\xi_{i}]=1

\mathbb{E}|\xi_{i}-1|^{2+\delta}<\infty

where
\mathcal{W}_{i}
denotes the observed data. The weighted-bootstrap process admits the expansion

Z_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})=\mathbb{G}_{n}^{*}\psi_{\bm{x}}+o_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}(1)\qquad\text{in }\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}),

in probability, where
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
denotes probability under the conditional bootstrap law and
\Psi=\{\psi_{\bm{x}}:\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}\}
is the
P
-Donsker class in
3.1
. The Gaussian limit
Z
in
3.1
has continuous sample paths on
\mathcal{X}
, and its standard-error function

is continuous on
\mathcal{X}
and satisfies

\displaystyle 0<C^{-1}\leq\inf_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\sigma(\bm{x})\leq\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\sigma(\bm{x})\leq C<\infty.

Since
\Psi
is

, the conditional multiplier central limit theorem yields

\mathbb{G}_{n}^{*}\psi_{\cdot}\rightsquigarrow_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}Z\qquad\text{in }\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}),

see, e.g.,

Z_{n}^{*}\rightsquigarrow_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}Z\qquad\text{in }\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}).

By
3

\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\big|\widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})-\sigma(\bm{x})\big|=o_{p}(1),\qquad W_{n}^{*}:=Z_{n}^{*}/\widehat{\sigma}\rightsquigarrow_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}W:=Z/\sigma\quad\text{in }\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}).

be the

The following result shows that the in-sample continuous uniform bands based on the interpolated process, which emanate from the usual practice of displaying bands computed on a finite tensor grid

.

If
2.1
,
2.2
,
3.1

3.4
Hadamard differentiable transformations

Although the preceding results apply directly to
F
, they extend to transformations of the form

\phi\circ F
. Specifically, suppose
\phi
is Hadamard differentiable at
F
, tangentially to

\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
, with derivative
\dot{\phi}_{F}
. Then, by standard functional
delta-method arguments, e.g.,
10
,
\phi(F_{L})-\phi(F)=\dot{\phi}_{F}(F_{L}-F)+o\!\big(\lVert F_{L}-F\rVert_{\infty}\big).

Consequently, the present analysis extends directly to such transformations of
F
.

As a leading example, let
\phi
denote the quantile map, and let
F_{X}
be a strictly
increasing and continuously differentiable distribution function with derivative

f_{X}
such that
\displaystyle\inf_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}f_{X}(x)>0
. Fix
\tau\in(0,1)
and write
x:=F_{X}^{-1}(\tau)
. Then under the rate
condition
L_{n}=\omega(r_{n}^{1/4})
,

\displaystyle r_{n}^{1/2}\big(\widehat{F}_{X,L}^{-1}-F_{X}^{-1}\big)(\tau)=

\displaystyle r_{n}^{1/2}\big(\phi(\widehat{F}_{X,L})-\phi(F_{X})\big)(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{F_{X}}\!\left(r_{n}^{1/2}\big(\widehat{F}_{X,L}-F_{X}\big)\right)(\tau)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{f_{X}(x)}\,\widehat{Z}_{n}^{L}(x)+o_{p}(1),

where
\widehat{Z}_{n}^{L}(x):=r_{n}^{1/2}\big(\widehat{F}_{X,L}-F_{X}\big)(x)
. It follows under the conditions of
Theorem

3.1
and the continuous mapping theorem that

r_{n}^{1/2}\big(\widehat{F}_{X,L}^{-1}-F_{X}^{-1}\big)(\tau)\ \rightsquigarrow\ \frac{1}{f_{X}(x)}\,Z(x)\qquad\text{in }\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}).

3.5
Monte Carlo evidence

Appendix

S.2
in the appendix reports Monte Carlo evidence for the interpolated confidence band construction in
Algorithm

1
. The simulations use two-period difference-in-differences designs with univariate and bivariate continuously distributed outcomes. In each design, the procedure is applied to two target parameters: the Distribution Treatment Effect on the Treated,
F^{\mathrm{DTT}}
, and the (counterfactual) distribution function of the untreated potential outcomes for the treated group,
F^{\mathrm{CF}}
. The DGP sets the treatment effect to zero, so
F^{\mathrm{DTT}}\equiv 0
, while
F^{\mathrm{CF}}
is non-zero and known from the simulation design.

For each target
m\in\{\mathrm{DTT},\mathrm{CF}\}
, the estimator is computed on a finite grid
\mathcal{X}_{m}^{L}
, the sup-
t
critical value is computed on that grid, and the resulting estimator and confidence band are interpolated over the evaluation region
\mathcal{X}_{m}
. The object-specific grid size follows
L_{m,n}=\lceil a\,(\bar{y}_{m,1}-\underline{y}_{m,1})n^{b}\rceil\vee 2
, with
(a,b)\in\{1,2,4\}\times\{0.30,0.35,0.40\}
, allowing the simulations to examine both sample-size behaviour and sensitivity to grid density.

The results are reassuring for the proposed implementation. Across the univariate and bivariate designs, the continuous
\mathcal{L}_{2}
error of the interpolated estimator declines steadily with
n
for both
F^{\mathrm{DTT}}
and
F^{\mathrm{CF}}
. Uniform coverage is close to nominal overall, with mild conservativeness in parts of the bivariate DTT design and only limited sensitivity to the precise grid rule once the grid is moderately dense. In particular, choices such as
L_{m,n}\approx n^{0.3}
, which lie above the
n^{1/4}
threshold, perform well in the simulations while remaining computationally less taxing than substantially finer tensor grids. The dominant improvement comes from increasing
n
, rather than from fine-tuning
(a,b)
, supporting the practical use of the interpolated bands described above.

4
Conclusion

This paper provides a theoretical basis for choosing the grid density over the index set
\mathcal{X}
when conducting uniform inference on a continuous target functional
F
. The starting point is the practical observation that feasible simultaneous confidence bands are computed on a finite tensor grid but are typically reported as continuous curves by linearly interpolating between grid points. Treating this interpolation step as part of the statistical procedure, rather than as a purely graphical convention, makes the approximation error explicit.

Under twice continuous differentiability and weak convergence of the underlying empirical process, the paper shows that the interpolation error is asymptotically negligible whenever
L_{n}=\omega(r_{n}^{1/4})
. This rate condition is sufficient for the interpolated estimator to inherit the weak limit of the grid-point process and for the resulting interpolated confidence bands to attain their nominal uniform coverage. For estimators with the usual
r_{n}=n
rate, the rule becomes
L_{n}=\omega(n^{1/4})
, giving a simple and theoretically grounded guide for balancing computational cost against asymptotic validity.

T_{n}=\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}|Z_{n}(\bm{x})|/\widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})
converges weakly to
T=\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}|Z(\bm{x})|/\sigma(\bm{x})
. Since
t_{n,1-\alpha}
consistently estimates the
(1-\alpha)
-quantile of
T
, and since
3.2
implies continuity of the distribution of
T
at that quantile,

\mathbb{P}\big(|T_{n}-t_{n,1-\alpha}|\leq\eta_{n}\big)\to 0.

Therefore,

\mathbbm{1}\Big\{(\widehat{T}_{n}^{L}-T_{n})\wedge 0\,\leq\,(t_{n,1-\alpha}-T_{n})\,<\,(\widehat{T}_{n}^{L}-T_{n})\vee 0\Big\}=o_{p}(1).

Part (b):
Consider the term:
\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}\Big\{(t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha})\wedge 0\,<\,(\widehat{T}_{n}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha})\,\leq\,(t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha})\vee 0\Big\}
. Under
3.2
, the grid-based critical value is controlled by the interpolation error of the studentised bootstrap process.

Consider the decomposition

\displaystyle t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha}=\widehat{Q}_{1-\alpha}\Big(\max_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}}\frac{|Z_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})|}{\widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})}\Big)-\widehat{Q}_{1-\alpha}\Big(\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{|Z_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})|}{\widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})}\Big).

For a generic bootstrap draw, define the studentised bootstrap process

W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x}):=\frac{Z_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})}{\widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})},\qquad Z_{n}^{*}(\bm{x}):=r_{n}^{1/2}\big(\widehat{F}^{*}-\widehat{F}\big)(\bm{x}),

and let

T_{n}^{L}=\max_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}}|W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})|.

The corresponding infeasible continuous-space statistic is

T_{n,\infty}:=\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}|W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})|.

Now define the
d
-linear interpolant of the studentised bootstrap process:

W_{n,L}^{*}(\bm{x}):=\sum_{\bm{\iota}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}W_{n}^{*}\big(\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})\big)w_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota}).

Since
W_{n,L}^{*}(\bm{x})
is multilinear on each interpolation cell and is a convex combination of the corresponding vertex values, its extrema over each cell are attained at the vertices. Consequently,

\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}|W_{n,L}^{*}(\bm{x})|=\max_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{L}}|W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})|=T_{n}^{L}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\big|T_{n}^{L}-T_{n,\infty}\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}|W_{n,L}^{*}(\bm{x})|-\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}|W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left|W_{n,L}^{*}(\bm{x})-W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})\right|,

since the
\sup
operator is
1
-Lipschitz. Indeed, since the interpolation weights sum to one and each vertex
\mathbf{x}_{L}(\bm{x};\bm{\iota})
lies in the same cell as
\bm{x}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left|W_{n,L}^{*}(\bm{x})-W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\left|W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})-W_{n}^{*}(\bm{y})\right|.

Under
3.2
,
W_{n}^{*}\rightsquigarrow_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}W
in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
. Conditional weak convergence in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
implies conditional stochastic equicontinuity in probability. Since
\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\to 0
, it follows that

\sup_{\lVert\bm{x}-\bm{y}\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\left|W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})-W_{n}^{*}(\bm{y})\right|=o_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}(1)\quad\text{in probability}.

Thus,

\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left|W_{n,L}^{*}(\bm{x})-W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})\right|=o_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}(1)\quad\text{in probability}.

Recall
t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}
and
t_{n,1-\alpha}
denote respectively the conditional
(1-\alpha)
-quantiles of
T_{n}^{L}
and
T_{n,\infty}
. Since

T_{n}^{L}\leq T_{n,\infty}+\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left|W_{n,L}^{*}(\bm{x})-W_{n}^{*}(\bm{x})\right|,

and similarly with the roles reversed, the discrepancy between the feasible grid-based critical value and its infeasible continuous-space counterpart is controlled by the uniform interpolation error of the studentised bootstrap process. Thus,

\big|T_{n}^{L}-T_{n,\infty}\big|=o_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}(1)\quad\text{in probability}.

This implies that the conditional distribution functions of
T_{n}^{L}
and
T_{n,\infty}
are asymptotically equivalent at continuity points of their common limiting distribution. Since
T_{n,\infty}\rightsquigarrow_{\widehat{\mathbb{P}}}T
, and since the distribution of
T
is continuous at its
(1-\alpha)
-quantile, the corresponding conditional quantiles satisfy

t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha}=o_{p}(1)

under the rate condition
L_{n}=\omega(r_{n}^{1/4})
. Together with
\widehat{T}_{n}^{L}-T_{n}=o_{p}(1)
from Part (a), the displayed indicator is bounded above by

\mathbbm{1}\Big\{|T_{n}-t_{n,1-\alpha}|\leq|\widehat{T}_{n}^{L}-T_{n}|+|t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha}|\Big\}.

The right-hand side has an
o_{p}(1)
random radius. Hence, there exists a deterministic sequence
\eta_{n}\downarrow 0
such that

\mathbb{P}\big(|\widehat{T}_{n}^{L}-T_{n}|+|t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha}|>\eta_{n}\big)\to 0.

As in Part (a), continuity of the limiting distribution of
T
at its
(1-\alpha)
-quantile, together with consistency of
t_{n,1-\alpha}
, implies

\mathbb{P}\big(|T_{n}-t_{n,1-\alpha}|\leq\eta_{n}\big)\to 0.

Therefore,

\mathbbm{1}\Big\{(t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha})\wedge 0\,<\,(\widehat{T}_{n}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha})\,\leq\,(t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}-t_{n,1-\alpha})\vee 0\Big\}=o_{p}(1)

under the rate condition
L_{n}=\omega(r_{n}^{1/4})
.

It follows from the above, thanks to the linearity of the expectation operator, the expectation inequality, and the rate condition
L_{n}=\omega(r_{n}^{1/4})
that

(S.12)

\begin{split}\Big|\mathbb{P}\Big[F(\bm{x})\in\mathcal{C}_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}(\bm{x})\ &\forall\ \bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}\Big]-\mathbb{P}\Big[F(\bm{x})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{n,1-\alpha}(\bm{x})\ \forall\ \bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}\Big]\Big|\\
&=\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}\Big\{\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{|\widehat{Z}_{n}^{L}(\bm{x})|}{\widehat{\sigma}_{L}(\bm{x})}\leq t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}\Big\}-\mathbbm{1}\Big\{\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{|Z_{n}(\bm{x})|}{\widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})}\leq t_{n,1-\alpha}\Big\}\Big]\Big|\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\mathbbm{1}\Big\{\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{|\widehat{Z}_{n}^{L}(\bm{x})|}{\widehat{\sigma}_{L}(\bm{x})}\leq t_{n,1-\alpha}^{L}\Big\}-\mathbbm{1}\Big\{\sup_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{|Z_{n}(\bm{x})|}{\widehat{\sigma}(\bm{x})}\leq t_{n,1-\alpha}\Big\}\Big|\Big]\\
&=o(1).\end{split}

Thanks to
3.2
, the conclusion follows from
3
.

Appendix S.2
Monte Carlo Simulations

This section considers two Monte Carlo designs based on a two-period difference-in-differences data-generating process following
7
;
4
, with continuously distributed outcomes and the identity link function. The simulations study the finite-sample behaviour of the interpolated estimator and confidence band construction in
Algorithm

1
. For each sample size
n
, treatment status is generated as
D_{i}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)
and the time indicator is set to
t_{i}\in\{0,1\}
, with half of the sample assigned to each period.

In the univariate design, outcomes are generated from

Y_{i}=\alpha+\beta D_{i}+\gamma t_{i}+\delta D_{i}t_{i}+\mathcal{E}_{i},

where
\mathcal{E}_{i}\sim N(0,1)
independently across observations. In the bivariate design, outcomes are generated as

Y_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}Y_{i1}\\
Y_{i2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha+\beta D_{i}+\gamma t_{i}+\delta D_{i}t_{i}\\
\alpha+\beta D_{i}+\gamma t_{i}+\delta D_{i}t_{i}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{E}_{i},

where
\mathcal{E}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}_{2}(0,\Sigma)
independently across observations, with

\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.5\\
0.5&1\end{pmatrix}.

Throughout the Monte Carlo study,
\alpha=0.1
,
\beta=0.2
,
\gamma=-0.1
, and
\delta=0
. Each design uses
R=1000
Monte Carlo replications and
B=499
non-parametric bootstrap draws. Hence, in both the univariate and bivariate settings, the null hypothesis of no Distribution Treatment Effect on the Treated (DTT) holds. Thus, the true DTT object,
\mathrm{DTT}(\bm{x})=F_{Y(1)\mid D=1}(\bm{x})-F_{Y(0)\mid D=1}(\bm{x}),\,\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, is identically zero over its support.

The estimands are treated as functionals of the form
F:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, as in the main text. For each
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, write

F^{\mathrm{DTT}}(\bm{x}):=F_{Y(1)\mid D=1}(\bm{x})-F_{Y(0)\mid D=1}(\bm{x}),\qquad F^{\mathrm{CF}}(\bm{x}):=F_{Y(0)\mid D=1}(\bm{x}),

where
F^{\mathrm{CF}}
denotes the untreated counterfactual CDF for the treated group. For notational compactness, write

F^{m}:=\begin{cases}F^{\mathrm{DTT}},&m=\mathrm{DTT},\\
F^{\mathrm{CF}},&m=\mathrm{CF}.\end{cases}

Under the identity-link difference-in-differences specification, the estimators are

\widehat{F}^{\mathrm{CF}}(\bm{x})=\widehat{F}_{10}(\bm{x})+\widehat{F}_{01}(\bm{x})-\widehat{F}_{00}(\bm{x}),\qquad\widehat{F}^{\mathrm{DTT}}(\bm{x})=\widehat{F}_{11}(\bm{x})-\widehat{F}^{\mathrm{CF}}(\bm{x}),

where
\widehat{F}_{dt}
denotes the empirical CDF in treatment group
D=d\in\{0,1\}
and period
t\in\{0,1\}
. Since
\delta=0
,
F^{\mathrm{DTT}}\equiv 0
. The counterfactual target
F^{\mathrm{CF}}
is non-zero and known under the simulation design, so coverage and continuous error are evaluated against this population target.

For each Monte Carlo replication and each object
m\in\{\mathrm{DTT},\mathrm{CF}\}
, estimation is performed on an equally spaced grid
\mathcal{X}_{m}^{L}
, followed by interpolation to obtain
\widehat{F}_{m,L}
and
\mathcal{C}_{m,n,1-\alpha}^{L}
on the corresponding evaluation region

\mathcal{X}_{m}=\prod_{k=1}^{d}[\underline{y}_{m,k},\bar{y}_{m,k}].

The object-specific grid size is chosen following the rule

L_{m,n}:=L_{m}(a,b)=\left\lceil a\,(\bar{y}_{m,1}-\underline{y}_{m,1})\,n^{b}\right\rceil\vee 2,\qquad m\in\{\mathrm{DTT},\mathrm{CF}\},

for
(a,b)\in\{1,2,4\}\times\{0.30,0.35,0.40\}
. In the univariate design, the DTT grid is constructed over the interval between the empirical
\tau_{\mathrm{lb}}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{ub}}
quantiles of the realised outcome sample, with
\tau_{\mathrm{lb}}=0.05
and
\tau_{\mathrm{ub}}=0.95
, so that

\underline{y}_{\mathrm{DTT},1}=\widehat{Q}_{Y}(\tau_{\mathrm{lb}}),\qquad\bar{y}_{\mathrm{DTT},1}=\widehat{Q}_{Y}(\tau_{\mathrm{ub}}).

The counterfactual CDF uses an object-specific central region: in the univariate design, its endpoints are the
5\%
and
95\%
quantiles of the known population counterfactual distribution. In the bivariate design, the evaluation grid for object
m
is a tensor product with
L_{m,n}^{2}
nodes. The evaluation regions are chosen with common coordinate-wise widths, so the first-coordinate width
\bar{y}_{m,1}-\underline{y}_{m,1}
is the width used along each margin. For DTT, the lower and upper bounds are chosen symmetrically as
\underline{\bm{y}}_{\mathrm{DTT}}=(-c,-c)
and
\bar{\bm{y}}_{\mathrm{DTT}}=(c,c)
, where
c>0
is determined under the equally weighted mixture of the four group-time outcome distributions so that

F(\bar{\bm{y}}_{\mathrm{DTT}})-F(\underline{\bm{y}}_{\mathrm{DTT}})=0.9.

For the counterfactual CDF in the bivariate design, the lower and upper diagonal endpoints
\underline{\bm{y}}_{\mathrm{CF}}
and
\bar{\bm{y}}_{\mathrm{CF}}
are chosen so that the known counterfactual lower-orthant probabilities equal
0.05
and
0.95
, respectively. The resulting DTT and counterfactual CDF grids may therefore have different realised values of
L_{m,n}
, as reported separately in
Tables

S.1
and
S.2
.

Bootstrap uniform confidence bands are constructed object by object using the non-parametric bootstrap, following
3
in the univariate case and
9
in the multivariate case. This implementation is a weighted bootstrap based on multinomial frequency weights. As in
Algorithm

1
, the bootstrap draws are used to compute the grid-point sup-
t
critical value
t_{m,n,1-\alpha}^{L}
, and the grid-point endpoints are then interpolated to form

\mathcal{C}_{m,n,1-\alpha}^{L}(\bm{x})=\big[\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{m,L}(\bm{x}),\widehat{\mathcal{U}}_{m,L}(\bm{x})\big],\qquad\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{m}.

This is the in-sample continuous band whose validity is studied in
Theorem

3.2
.

Two performance measures are reported. First,
uniform coverage
is the Monte Carlo frequency with which the interpolated band contains the target function over the whole evaluation region:

\mathbbm{1}\Big\{F^{m}(\bm{x})\in\mathcal{C}_{m,n,1-\alpha}^{L}(\bm{x})\quad\forall\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{m}\Big\},\qquad m\in\{\mathrm{DTT},\mathrm{CF}\}.

For the DTT object this reduces to checking whether the zero function is contained in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{DTT},n,1-\alpha}^{L}
. For the counterfactual CDF, coverage is evaluated against the known population counterfactual
F^{\mathrm{CF}}
. Equivalently, the DTT rejection indicator is

\varphi_{\mathrm{DTT},n}^{L}=\mathbbm{1}\Big\{\exists\,\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{DTT}}\text{ such that }0\notin\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{DTT},n,1-\alpha}^{L}(\bm{x})\Big\}.

Secondly, approximation accuracy is measured by a normalised continuous
\mathcal{L}_{2}
error of the interpolated estimator relative to its target:

\mathcal{L}_{2,m}=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}_{m}|}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{m}}\big(\widehat{F}_{m,L}(\bm{x})-F^{m}(\bm{x})\big)^{2}\,d\bm{x}\right)^{1/2},

where
|\mathcal{X}_{m}|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of the evaluation region, i.e., the length of the interval when
d=1
and the area of the rectangle when
d=2
. This criterion is computed on the interpolated function, not merely on the grid-point estimates.
