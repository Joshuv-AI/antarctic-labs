Abstract
. In this paper, we establish in Theorem 1.2 the reciprocity of wavefront sets for irreducible admissible representations π \pi of classical groups G G over any local field F F of characteristic zero if π \pi has a generic local L L -parameter. Over archimedean local fields, based on the progress made in [ 56 ] , we prove in Theorem 1.5 that for an irreducible Casselman-Wallach representation π \pi with a generic local L L -parameter, the Wavefront Set Conjecture [ 56 , Conjecture 1.2] and its refinement (Conjecture 1.1 ) hold for the arithmetic wavefront set WF ari ​ ( π ) \mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi) as defined by the associated enhanced local L L -parameter of π \pi and the wavefront set WF tr ​ ( π ) \mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi) defined by the Harish-Chandra distribution character Θ π \Theta_{\pi} of π \pi . Hence the microlocal structure of Θ π \Theta_{\pi} is completely determined by the arithmetic information carried by the enhanced local L L -parameter of π \pi . The relations with the algebraic wavefront set WF wm ​ ( π ) \mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi) defined by the degenerate Whittaker models are extensively discussed by means of the composition law (Theorem 3.4 ) over all local fields of characteristic zero. Under Conjecture 1.3 , the Wavefront Set Conjecture is fully established over archimedean local fields. As a consequence, we prove a refinement of Vogan’s maximal-orbit principle (Theorem 1.7 ).

1.
Introduction

As a sequel of our previous work (
[
56
]
),
we prove in this paper a large and essential part of the wavefront set conjecture (
[
56
, Conjecture 1.2]
) for real classical groups. This brings substantial progress for a large family of irreducible admissible representations of real classical groups in the investigation of long-standing open problems in the representation theory of real reductive Lie groups on relations among the wavefront set of an irreducible admissible representation and other relevant invariants. The approach we took is based on the theory of local descents for irreducible admissible representations, which has been well developed for representations with generic local Langlands parameters, and puts for the first time the study of the long-standing open problems on wavefront sets and related problems within the framework of the local Langlands program.

Around 1970, L. Hörmander introduced an influential notion of wavefront sets for distributions over smooth manifolds in the theory of Fourier integral operators (
[
47
,
48
]
). For any unitary representation
\pi
of a real Lie group
G
, R. Howe introduced around 1980 the notion of the wavefront set of
\pi
(
[
50
]
). He first proved that the wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}(\pi)
of the Harish-Chandra distribution character
\Theta_{\pi}
(
[
39
,
40
]
) is
G
-invariant under the adjoint action and is determined by its image under the projection
G\times{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, where

{\mathfrak{g}}
is the Lie algebra of
G
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is the dual space of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Hence the wavefront set of
\pi
as defined by R. Howe is a subset of the nilpotent cone of
{\mathfrak{g}}
if
G
is reductive and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is identified with
{\mathfrak{g}}
via the Killing form.

As expected in
[
50
,
12
]
, for a real reductive group
G
, the microlocal structure of the wavefront set of an irreducible admissible representation
\pi
of
G
should be detected by the germ expression near the identity of the distribution character
\Theta_{\pi}
. Meanwhile,
D. Vogan introduced the notion of associated variety
{\mathrm{AV}}(\pi)
of a Harish-Chandra module of a reductive Lie group and conjectured that
{\mathrm{AV}}(\pi)=\mathrm{WF}(\pi)
, which provides an algebraic approach to understand the microlocal structure of the distribution character
\Theta_{\pi}
. It remains a difficult problem in theory to explicitly determine the structure of either the associated varieties
{\mathrm{AV}}(\pi)
or the wavefront sets
\mathrm{WF}(\pi)
.

The expectation that an irreducible representation should have a well-defined maximal nilpotent support goes back to the Kostant-Kirillov orbit method philosophy and to the early work on primitive ideals. Duflo’s work on primitive ideals
[
29
]
provided one of the algebraic foundations for this viewpoint. For complex semisimple Lie groups, the relationship between primitive ideals, orbital integrals, local character expansions, and nilpotent orbits was developed in the work of Barbasch–Vogan and Joseph; see, for example,
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
59
]
. In this setting the maximal nilpotent support is governed by the closure of a single nilpotent orbit. We shall refer to this principle as Vogan’s maximal-orbit principle. This viewpoint is also consistent with the Kirillov–Kostant orbit method for the unitary dual of real reductive groups, as explained for instance in
[
89
,
92
,
93
,
6

.

1.1.
Wavefront set and Langlands reciprocity

In our previous paper (
[
56
]
), we assume that
F
is a local field of characteristic zero.
In order to discover a practical way to determine the
F
-rational structure of the wavefront sets, based on our previous work (
[
57
]
), we developed the theory of local descents for enhanced local
L
-parameters and proposed in
[
56
]
to utilize the local Langlands correspondence and the local Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture as a bridge to transfer the problem of explicit computation of wavefront sets for a representation
\pi
to that on the Langlands dual side and consider the structure of the arithmetic wavefront set associated to the enhanced local
L
-parameter of
\pi
, which seems quite productive and fruitful.

In
[
56
]
, for any classical group
{\mathbb{G}}
defined over
F
and
G={\mathbb{G}}(F)
, we introduced the notion of
arithmetic wavefront set

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)

(
[
56
, Theorem 4.4]
) for any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(G)
with generic local
L
-parameter (see Section
2.3
) and made the following
Wavefront Set Conjecture
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Wavefront Set)
.

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic zero and
G
be a classical group over
F
. For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(G)
with a generic local
L
-parameter, the following identities hold:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi)^{s-\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)^{s-\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)^{s-\mathrm{max}},

where
\mathrm{WF}_{\square}(\pi)^{s-\mathrm{max}}
denotes the subset of all maximal members in the wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}_{\square}(\pi)
under the
F
-stable topological order over
{\mathcal{N}}_{F}({\mathfrak{g}})_{\circ}

given in Definition
2.2
. Moreover, the following (stronger) identities also hold:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}},

where
\mathrm{WF}_{\square}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}
denotes the subset of all maximal members in the wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}_{\square}(\pi)
under the
F
-rational topological order over
{\mathcal{N}}_{F}({\mathfrak{g}})_{\circ}
.

To illustrate the spirit of the local Langlands reciprocity in the theory of local descents for both representations
\pi
of
G(F)
and their associated enhanced local
L
-parameters we established (joint with C. Chen) in
[
19
, Theorem 1.4]
the coincidence of the first occurrence indices of the local descents for the representations and their enhanced local
L
-parameters when the local
L
-parameters are generic. In order to understand Conjecture
1.1
, we introduce in this paper a new notion of the wavefront set of
\pi
when a
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(G)
has a generic local
L
-parameter by the local descents. It is called the
descent wavefront set
of
\pi
and is denoted by
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{des}}(\pi)
. We refer to Section
2.3
for details. The spirit of the local Langlands reciprocity for the wavefront sets is illustrated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Reciprocity of Wavefront Set)
.

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic zero and
G
be an
F
-quasisplit classical group or its pure inner form.
For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(G)
with a generic enhanced
L
-parameter
(\varphi,\chi)
, the wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{des}}(\pi)

defined by the consecutive descents of
\pi
and the wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)
defined by the consecutive descents of
(\varphi,\chi)
are equal, i.e.

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{des}}(\pi)=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi).

This theorem will be restated as Theorem
2.5
and proved in Section
2.3
. By the composition law of descents as established in Theorem
3.4
, we obtain in Corollary
3.6
that

(1.6)

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{des}}(\pi)\subset\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi),\quad{\rm for\ any}\ \pi\in\Pi_{F}(G),

with a generic
L
-parameter and over all local fields
F
of characteristic zero.
Conjecture
1.1
naturally leads to the following assertion.

Conjecture 1.3

(Descent and Generalized Whittaker)
.

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic zero and
G
be a classical group over
F
. For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(G)
with a generic local
L
-parameter, the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{des}}(\pi)^{s-\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)^{s-\mathrm{max}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{des}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}},

where the notations take the same meaning as in Conjecture
1.1
.

It is worthwhile to point out that Conjecture
1.3
is of representation-theoretic nature and is expected to be proved through an approach based on representation theory and combinatorics. It is even more mysterious if one considers the relations between
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{des}}(\pi)
and
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)
for the boundary nilpotent orbits of the maximal ones.

In Section
1.2
, we explain the indispensable roles played by this reciprocity of wavefront sets (Theorem
1.2
) in our approach to the Wavefront Set Conjecture (Conjecture
1.1
) in the archimedean case.

1.2.
Main results over
{\mathbb{R}}

As indicated in our previous work (
[

]
), the arithmetic wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)
for

\pi\in\Pi_{F}(G)
with a generic local
L
-parameter is defined by means of the consecutive descents of the enhanced local Langlands datum

associated with

, based on the

-rational structure of the local Langlands correspondence and the local Gan-Gross-Prasad conjecture. The

-rational structure of
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)
is expected to be completely determined and explicitly computed by the arithmetic information of

. This expectation was completely realized in

for all classical groups over the archimedean local fields. For later use, we record the following consequence of

Theorem 1.4

(Arithmetic Wavefront Set over
{\mathbb{R}}
)
.

Let

be an archimedean local field and let

be an enhanced local

(1)

If the component group

(2)

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}=\{{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)\}

It is important to mention that over a non-archimedean local field

,

[

for some relevant discussions.

The objective of this paper is to prove a large part of Conjecture
1.1
(the Wavefront Set Conjecture) for all classical groups over archimedean local fields. When
F={\mathbb{C}}
, the field of complex numbers, Conjecture
1.1
holds trivially, and hence for the archimedean case considered in this paper we assume that

, the field of real numbers. Here is the main result of this paper (Theorem

).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a classical group defined over
{\mathbb{R}}
, and denote by
G
the group
{\mathbb{G}}({\mathbb{R}})

{\mathbb{R}}

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}\subset\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)^{s-\mathrm{max}}

With Theorem

Let

Theorem 1.7

(An arithmetic refinement of Vogan’s maximal-orbit principle)
.

Let

be a classical group over
{\mathbb{R}}
as in Theorem

and
(\varphi,\chi)
be an enhanced local

with

associated with
(\varphi,\chi)
. More precisely, if the component group

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}
and
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}

{\mathbb{R}}

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}
consists of a unique

{\mathbb{R}}
-rational
{\mathbb{R}}
-distinguished nilpotent orbit

and can be explicitly and effectively determined by
(\varphi,\chi)
via the
L
-parameter descent method.

Let us also point out that this maximal-orbit principle is a feature of the real-group setting and should not be interpreted as a literal statement over all local fields. In the
p
-adic case, Tsai has shown that the geometric wavefront set need not be a singleton
[
84
]
. This does not contradict the orbit method philosophy; rather, it indicates that the
p
-adic form of the philosophy is more genuinely arithmetic. The leading asymptotic behavior of the Harish-Chandra distribution character
\Theta_{\pi}
for some
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(G)
is still governed by nilpotent orbital data, as in the local character expansion and in the relation with generalized Whittaker models, but the passage from rational nilpotent orbits to geometric nilpotent orbits need not collapse to a single maximal orbit. Thus the correct
p
-adic analogue of the orbit method should allow a finite set of maximal orbits, reflecting the
p
-adic nature of the rational structures associated with representations
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(G)
.
From this viewpoint, Tsai’s examples refine rather than refute the orbit method picture: they show that over non-archimedean fields the leading nilpotent support is orbital but not necessarily geometrically irreducible.

The idea of the proof of Theorem
1.5
can be briefly outlined as follows. As mentioned earlier, the general comparison between
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)
and
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi)
in the archimedean case is a long-standing open problem. The work of W. Schmid and K. Vilonen (
[
81
]
) compares wavefront cycles with associated cycles, and thereby relates
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi)
to the associated variety

{\rm AV}(\pi)
of

in the sense described above. Moreover, the genericity of the parameter of
\sigma
and the inductive continuation of the descent are exactly the conditions required for the consecutive descent on the parameter side to proceed.
By the induction hypothesis applied to
\sigma
(which has generic
L
-parameter
\phi_{1}
), the subsequent terms in the orbit decompositions for the descent wavefront set and the arithmetic wavefront set must also coincide.

Therefore, an
F
-rational nilpotent orbit belongs to
\mathrm{WF}_{{\mathrm{des}}}(\pi)
(i.e., it arises from an iterative chain of nonzero descents through the quotients with generic
L
-parameters, with
p_{1}\geq p_{2}
at each step) if and only if it is realized by a decreasing admissible tableau in
{\mathcal{Y}}_{a}(\varphi,\chi)
via the corresponding
L
-parameter descents.
This is precisely the defining criterion for membership in
\mathrm{WF}_{{\mathrm{ari}}}(\pi)
and proves that
\mathrm{WF}_{{\mathrm{des}}}(\pi)=\mathrm{WF}_{{\mathrm{ari}}}(\pi)
.
∎

3.
Composition Law for Generalized Whittaker Models

The results in this section hold for all local fields
F
of characteristic zero. We establish a root exchange lemma (Lemma
3.2
) for general affine algebraic groups over
F
, and as an application we prove a composition law (Theorem
3.4
) for generalized Whittaker models of classical groups.

3.1.
Root exchange lemma

In this section, we formulate a version of the so-called root exchange lemma (
[

, Lemma 2.2]
,
[
34
, Lemma 7.1]
), which works for all local fields of characteristic zero following the arguments in
[
36
]
, and then prove a generalization of it that will be a key technical ingredient in our proof of Theorem

and will be useful in future applications.

For a group

Lemma 3.1

(Root Exchange)
.

Let

and

defined by

3.2.
Composition law

In this section, by using the root exchange lemma (Lemma
3.2
), we prove a composition law for generalized Whittaker models of classical groups over any local field of characteristic zero.
We mention that a special case of its global analog can be found in
[
33
]
. Our proof is Lie algebra theoretical, which avoids matrix computations and should be more conceptual.

Assume that

{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{F}({\mathfrak{g}})_{\circ}
corresponds to a sesquilinear Young tableau of the form

([p_{1}^{m_{1}},\ldots,p_{r}^{m_{r}},1^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime\prime}}],\{V^{\prime}_{p_{i}},V^{\prime}_{1}\}),

where

under the action of an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{h^{\prime},e^{\prime},f^{\prime}\}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
with

{\mathfrak{n}}={\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}+{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime\prime}
, where
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}=|\underline{p}|:=\sum^{r}_{i=1}m_{i}p_{i}

Put

V^{\prime}:=\bigoplus^{r}_{i=1}S_{p_{i}}\otimes V^{\prime}_{p_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad V^{\prime\prime}:=S_{1}\otimes V^{\prime}_{1}.

There is an involution
\theta
of

such that

{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\theta}\cong V^{\prime}\otimes V^{\prime\prime}\text{ as $(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\prime\prime})$-modules}.

p_{r}\geq q_{1}>1.

.

For a subspace

{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then condition (

Lemma 3.5
.

For
1\leq i\leq p_{1}-2
, the map
\mathrm{ad}\,f:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
restricts to an isomorphism

\mathrm{ad}\,f|_{{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}}:{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}\to{}^{c}{\mathfrak{y}}_{i},\quad x\mapsto[f,x].

Proof.

It is straightforward to show that
\mathrm{ad}\,f|_{{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}}
is injective.
Indeed, take any elements
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{j}^{\prime}
and
u\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq j+1}
with
j\geq 1
and
x\neq 0
. Then
[f^{\prime},x]\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{j-2}^{\prime}
and is nonzero because

x
is not a lowest weight vector under the action of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{h^{\prime},e^{\prime},f^{\prime}\}
. From
[f^{\prime},u]\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq j-1}
and

[f^{\prime\prime},x+u]\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq j}
, we conclude that

[f,x+u]=[f^{\prime},x]+[f^{\prime},u]+[f^{\prime\prime},x+u]\neq 0.

This proves the injectivity of
\mathrm{ad}f|_{{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}}
.

Consider the decompositions

{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}=({\mathfrak{x}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 3})\oplus({\mathfrak{x}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathfrak{y}}_{i}=({\mathfrak{y}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\leq-1})\oplus({\mathfrak{y}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{0}).

We first prove that
\mathrm{ad}\,f
restricts to an isomorphism

(3.5)

\mathrm{ad}\,f:{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 3}\to{}^{c}({\mathfrak{y}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\leq-1}).

The map (
3.5
) is well-defined, noting that

[f,{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 3}]=[f,{\mathfrak{g}}_{2-i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 3}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\theta}]\subset{\mathfrak{g}}_{-i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 1}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\theta}={}^{c}({\mathfrak{y}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\leq-1}).

By a similar argument as above, the map

\mathrm{ad}\,e:{\mathfrak{g}}_{-i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 1}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\leq p_{r}-2}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\theta}\to{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 3}

is also injective. Due to the assumption
p_{r}\geq q_{1}
in (
3.4
), we have

{\mathfrak{g}}_{-i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 1}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\theta}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\leq p_{r}-2}\quad\text{for}\quad i\geq 1.

It follows that the map

\mathrm{ad}\,e:{}^{c}({\mathfrak{y}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\leq-1})={\mathfrak{g}}_{-i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 1}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\theta}\to{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 3}

is injective. Hence (
3.5
) is an isomorphism. Moreover, the map

(3.6)

\mathrm{ad}\,f:{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{2}\to{}^{c}({\mathfrak{y}}_{i}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{0})

is also an isomorphism.

Combining the isomorphisms (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) proves the lemma.
∎

It is easy to see that

{\mathfrak{c}}\oplus\bigoplus^{p_{1}-2}_{i=1}{\mathfrak{x}}_{i}={\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{\geq 1.5}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime\prime}_{\geq 1.5}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathfrak{c}}\oplus\bigoplus^{p_{1}-2}_{i=1}{\mathfrak{y}}_{i}={\mathfrak{l}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\geq 2}=:{\mathfrak{g}}_{\geq 1.5}

for a Lagrangian subspace

.
Applying the root exchange Lemma

, we obtain that

\mathrm{Wh}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime\prime}}({\mathcal{J}}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(\pi))\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{U^{\prime}_{1.5}U^{\prime\prime}_{1.5}}(\pi,\psi_{f^{\prime}}\psi_{f^{\prime\prime}})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{U_{1.5}}(\pi,\psi_{f})\cong\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi),

where

and
U^{\prime\prime}_{1.5}:=\exp({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime\prime}_{\geq 1.5})
. This proves Theorem
3.4
.

By induction, we have the following consequence of Theorem

.

5.4.
Associated variety

Let
\theta
be the Cartan involution of
G
, which induces the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
. Let
K=G^{\theta}
and
{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{G}}^{\theta}
be the corresponding subgroups of
G
and
{\mathcal{G}}
respectively. For a finite length
(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},K)
-module
X
, its associated variety
{\rm AV}(X)
is a finite union of closures of nilpotent
K
-orbits on
\mathfrak{p}
, which is an important invariant of

X
(see e.g.
[
6
]
).
More precisely, given a
K
-stable good filtration on
X
, the associated graded
{\rm gr}(X)
is an
S(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
-module, where
S(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
is the symmetric algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. By definition, the associated variety
{\rm AV}(X)
of
X
is the support of
{\rm gr}(X)
, viewed as a subvariety of
(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}/\mathfrak{k})^{*}\cong\mathfrak{p}
.
For an irreducible Casselman-Wallach representation
\pi
of
G
, define the associated variety of
\pi
to be:
\AV(\pi):=\AV(\pi_{\rm HC})
, where
\pi_{\rm HC}
is the Harish-Chandra module of
\pi
.

Now we proceed to compute the associated variety of a discrete series representation.
Since the discrete series representation
\pi_{x}(\lambda)
is realized as a cohomologically induced representation, we can use the following theorem to compute its associated variety. This theorem is well known to experts (see, for example, the introduction of
[
83
]
) but is difficult to find in a proper reference. The following very general version is from
[
76
, Proposition 8.2]
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a
\theta
-stable parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
with Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
. Let
\overline{\mathfrak{q}}
denote the parabolic subalgebra opposite to
\mathfrak{q}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}
be the nilpotent radical of
\overline{\mathfrak{q}}
.
For any one-dimensional character
\lambda
of
\mathfrak{l}
, suppose that
X
is an irreducible submodule of
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
. Then the associated variety of
X
is given by

\AV(X)={\mathcal{K}}(\mathfrak{u}^{*}\cap\mathfrak{p}^{*})={\mathcal{K}}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\cap\mathfrak{p}).

Remark 5.6
.

It is important to point out that there is a subtle typo in
[
83
]
, which claims that the associated variety should be
{\mathcal{K}}(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{p})
instead of
{\mathcal{K}}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\cap\mathfrak{p})
.
Meanwhile, Trapa’s combinatorial formula in fact computes
{\mathcal{K}}(\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\cap\mathfrak{p})
.

This can be seen from the example of
\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{sl}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
be the complexified Lie algebra and
x=\mathrm{diag}({\rm i},-{\rm i})
be the diagonal matrix. Then
x^{2}=-1=(-1)^{2-1}
.
Let
\mathfrak{q}
be the upper triangular parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
with Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
, where
\mathfrak{l}
is the subalgebra of diagonal matrices, so that
\overline{\mathfrak{q}}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{u}}
is the lower triangular parabolic subalgebra.
Then
\mathfrak{l}
is the Lie algebra of the maximal compact torus
T={\mathrm{U}}(1)
of
{\mathrm{SU}}(1,1)
.
The representations of
T
are parameterized by
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Let
{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}
denote the one-dimensional
U(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})
-module on which
\mathfrak{l}
acts by the character
\lambda
and
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}
acts trivially.
The Harish-Chandra module of a holomorphic discrete series representation is given by
\pi=U(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\otimes_{U(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})}{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}
.
Note that
\pi
has
T
-type given by
\lambda,\lambda+2,\lambda+4,\cdots
(compare the definition of

in
[
95
]
).

denote the associated graded of

has a natural grading, and as an

-module

Here

denotes the symmetric algebra and

denotes the polynomial algebra on a vector space.
Therefore, we see that

under the identification of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

6.
Arithmetic Wavefront Set and Associated Variety

Following Theorem
1.4
(
[
56
, Theorem 9.2]
), when the Vogan packet
\Pi_{\varphi}[G^{*}]
is not a singleton, associated to each
\pi=\pi(\varphi,\chi)\in\Pi_{\varphi}[G^{*}]
, there is a real distinguished nilpotent orbit

{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)={\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)\subset{\mathfrak{g}},

which is the unique maximal element of the arithmetic wavefront set
{\rm WF_{ari}}(\pi)
defined in
loc. cit.
under the real topological order. The case of discrete series representations has been recalled in Section
4.2
.

In terms of the Kostant-Sekiguchi correspondence (see
[
6
, Section 2]
for instance) given by

(6.1)

\displaystyle\Set{\text{nilpotent $G$-orbits on $\mathfrak{g}$}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle\Set{\text{nilpotent ${\mathcal{K}}$-orbits on $\mathfrak{p}$}},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}},

we prove the following result.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
\pi=\pi(\varphi,\chi)
is an irreducible Casselman-Wallach representation of
G
with a generic
L
-parameter
\varphi
and the Vogan packet
\Pi_{\varphi}[G^{*}]
being not a singleton. Then the associated variety
{\rm AV}(\pi)
is the closure of

{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)_{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)_{\mathfrak{p}},

the image of
{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)={\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)
under the Kostant-Sekiguchi correspondence.

By the relation between the associated cycle and the wavefront cycle established in
[
81
]
, we have the following immediate consequence of Theorem
6.1
.
Note that if
\Pi_{\varphi}[G^{*}]
is a singleton, then
G
is quasi-split and

\pi
is generic with respect to every Whittaker datum of
G
, in which case
{\rm AV}(\pi)
is the whole nilpotent cone in
\mathfrak{p}
.

Corollary 6.2
.

Assume that
\pi=\pi(\varphi,\chi)
is an irreducible Casselman-Wallach representation of
G
with a generic
L
-parameter
\varphi
. Then Conjecture
1.1
holds for both
{\rm WF_{tr}}(\pi)
and
{\rm WF_{\rm ari}}(\pi)
:

{\rm WF_{tr}}(\pi)^{r-\max}={\rm WF_{\rm ari}}(\pi)^{r-\max}.

6.1.
Reduction

We first reduce the proof of Theorem
6.1
to the case where
\pi
is a discrete series representation.
Recall that
\varphi
is given by (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
). First assume that
G
is linear. Since
\varphi
is generic, it follows from

[
82
]
that
\pi(\varphi,\chi)
is an irreducible normalized parabolic induction

(6.2)

\pi(\varphi,\chi)\cong|\cdot|_{E}^{s_{1}}\pi_{\tau_{1}}\times\cdots\times|\cdot|_{E}^{s_{l}}\pi_{\tau_{l}}\times\pi(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi),

where
\pi_{\tau_{j}}
is the (limit of) discrete series representation of
{\rm GL}_{1}(E)
or
{\rm GL}_{2}(E)
with
L
-parameter
\tau_{j}
, and
\pi(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi)
is a representation of a classical group
G^{\prime}
in the Witt tower of
G
, with the
L
-parameter
\varphi_{\rm gp}
as in (
4.4
). A slight modification of (
6.2
) also holds when
G={\mathrm{Mp}}_{2n}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Consider a discrete
L
-parameter
\varphi^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
of the form

\varphi^{\prime}=\rho_{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\rho_{\alpha_{m}^{\prime}}\oplus\varphi_{\rm quad},

where
m=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<\alpha^{\prime}_{m}
, with
\varphi_{\rm quad}
as in (
4.4
). There is a natural embedding of centralizers

C_{G^{\prime\vee}}({\rm im}\,\varphi^{\prime})\hookrightarrow C_{G^{\prime\vee}}({\rm im}\,\varphi_{\rm gp}),

which in turn induces a surjective homomorphism of component groups

p:{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi^{\prime}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi_{\rm gp}}.

Let
\chi^{\prime}=\chi\circ p\in\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi^{\prime}}}
. Then
\pi(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi)
and
\pi(\varphi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
lie in the same coherent family (see
[
90
, Chapter 7]
), and by the translation principle (
[
88
, Lemma 2.7]
) it holds that

\AV(\pi(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi))=\AV(\pi(\varphi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})).

Assume that Theorem
6.1
holds for the discrete series representation
\pi(\varphi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
, so that
\AV(\pi(\varphi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}))
is the closure of

{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})_{\mathfrak{p}}
. By the algorithm in
[
56
]
, we have

{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi)={\mathcal{O}}(\varphi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
, hence
\AV(\pi(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi))
is the closure of
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi)_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Write

{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi)=((p_{1},\epsilon_{1}),(p_{2},\epsilon_{2}),\ldots,(p_{k},\epsilon_{k})).

Put
t=\dim\tau
. The algorithm also gives that

{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)=((p_{1}+2t,\epsilon_{1}),(p_{2},\epsilon_{2}),\ldots,(p_{k},\epsilon_{k})).

By
[
56
, Proposition 7.13]
,
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi)
is distinguished. In other words, it holds that if
p_{i}=p_{j}
then
\epsilon_{i}=\epsilon_{j}
.
It follows that the Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction (
[
68
]
) of the regular nilpotent orbit in
\mathfrak{gl}_{t}(E)
and the nilpotent orbit
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi_{\rm gp},\chi)
in
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is the nilpotent orbit
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then by (
6.2
) and
[
11
]
,
\AV(\pi(\varphi,\chi))
is the closure of
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)_{\mathfrak{p}}
. This proves that Theorem
6.1
holds for

\pi(\varphi,\chi)
.

6.2.
Proof for discrete series

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
6.1
for discrete series representations. Let
\pi=\pi(\varphi,\chi)
be a discrete series representation of
G
. We need to show that
\AV(\pi)
is the closure of
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

The proof is case-by-case. We first outline the common strategy.

(1)

Translate the algorithm in Section
5.4
into a recursive algorithm parallel to the one in
Section
4.2
.
Concretely, this produces the first row of the signed Young tableau
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
together with a new Harish-Chandra parameter
\delta^{\prime}
, which then generates the remaining rows of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
. We call
\delta^{\prime}
an
HC-descent
of
\delta
.

(2)

Show that the first rows of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
and
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)
produced by the two algorithms agree.

(3)

Show that the HC-descent
\delta^{\prime}
of
\delta
and the
L
-descent
\chi^{\prime}
of
\chi
match as in Section
5.4
.

Then by induction, the signed Young tableaux
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
and
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)
generated by both algorithms are the same.

We present the details for each family of real classical groups.

The unitary groups:

(i)
Recursive algorithm
.
We first divide the sequence
\delta
into consecutive subsequences, where the signs in each subsequence are the same:

\delta=(m_{1}\delta_{k_{1}},m_{2}\delta_{k_{2}},\dots,m_{\ell}\delta_{k_{\ell}}).

For example, if
\delta=(+1,+1,-1,-1,-1,+1)
, then
(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})=(2,3,1)
and

(\delta_{k_{1}},\delta_{k_{2}},\delta_{k_{3}})=(\delta_{1},\delta_{3},\delta_{6})=(+1,-1,+1)
.
By the associativity of the operation
\oplus
, we can rewrite
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
as

{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)=c_{m_{1}\delta_{k_{1}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus c_{m_{\ell}\delta_{k_{\ell}}},

where
c_{m_{i}\delta_{k_{i}}}
denotes the column with
m_{i}
signs
\delta_{k_{i}}
.
Then the first row of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
is given by picking one sign from each
m_{i}\delta_{k_{i}}
, and the remaining rows of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
are generated by the same algorithm for the HC-descent

(6.3)

\delta^{\prime}:=((m_{1}-1)\delta_{k_{1}},(m_{2}-1)\delta_{k_{2}},\ldots,(m_{\ell}-1)\delta_{k_{\ell}}).

This indeed gives a recursive algorithm for the associated variety.

(ii)
Matching the first rows.

We now show that the first rows of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
and
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)
are the same. It suffices to check that their lengths and last signs are respectively the same. Recall from Section
5.4
that
\delta
and
\chi
are related by

\delta_{i}=(-1)^{n-i}\chi_{i},\quad i=1,2,\dots,n.

It follows that

(6.4)

{\mathrm{sgn}}(\chi)=\set{1\leq i<n}{\delta_{i}\delta_{i+1}=1}.

Hence the length of the first row of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
is

\ell=1+\#{\mathrm{sgn}}(\delta)=1+(n-1)-\#{\mathrm{sgn}}(\chi)=n-s_{\chi}=p_{1}.

The last sign of the first row of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
is clearly
\delta_{n}=\chi_{n}=\epsilon_{1}
. This proves that
the first rows of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
and
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)
are the same.

(iii)
Matching HC-descent and
L
-descent.
Finally, we show that
\delta^{\prime}
and
\chi^{\prime}
are related as in Section
5.4
.
From (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) it is clear that

\delta^{\prime}=(\delta_{j}:j\in{\rm sgn}(\chi)).

Recall from Section
4.2
that

\chi^{\prime}=((-1)^{n-j+1}\chi_{n}:j\in{\rm sgn}(\chi)).

Take
j\in{\rm sgn}(\chi)
, and suppose that it is the
k
-th largest element in
{\mathrm{sgn}}(\chi)
.
It suffices to verify that

(6.5)

\delta_{j}=(-1)^{k-1}\cdot(-1)^{n-j+1}\chi_{n}=(-1)^{n-j-k}\chi_{n}.

In fact, one has

n-j-k=\#\set{j\leq i<n}{i\notin{\mathrm{sgn}}(\chi)}=\#\set{j\leq i<n}{i\in{\mathrm{sgn}}(\delta)}.

Therefore,
\delta_{j}
and
\delta_{n}=\chi_{n}
differ by
(-1)^{n-j-k}
, which shows that
(
6.5
) holds.

The even special orthogonal groups:

(i)
Recursive algorithm.

We first write
\delta=(m_{1}\delta_{k_{1}},m_{2}\delta_{k_{2}},\dots,m_{\ell}\delta_{k_{\ell}})
as in the unitary case.
By the associativity of the operation
\oplus
, we can rewrite
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
as

{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)=c_{m_{\ell}\delta_{k_{\ell}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus c_{m_{2}\delta_{k_{2}}}\oplus c_{2m_{1}\delta_{k_{1}}}\oplus c_{m_{2}\delta_{k_{2}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus c_{m_{\ell}\delta_{k_{\ell}}},

Then the first row of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
is given by picking one sign from each
m_{i}\delta_{k_{i}},i\geq 2
and
2m_{1}\delta_{k_{1}}
, and the remaining rows of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)

\delta^{\prime}:=((m_{1}-1)\delta_{k_{1}},(m_{2}-1)\delta_{k_{2}},\ldots,(m_{\ell}-1)\delta_{k_{\ell}}),\quad\delta_{0}^{\prime}=\delta_{1}.

This indeed gives a recursive algorithm for the associated variety.

(ii)
Matching the first rows
.
We now show that the first rows of
{\mathcal{O}}(\delta)
and
{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)
are the same. It suffices to check that their lengths and last signs are respectively the same. Recall from Section
5.4

\delta

Hence the length of the first row of

The last sign of the first row of

the first rows of

(iii)
Matching HC-descent and
L
-descent
. Finally, we show that the auxiliary signs are also matched, i.e.,

, and
\delta^{\prime}

For the auxiliary sign, note that

From (

Recall from Section

.
It suffices to verify that

(6.8)

Therefore,

7.
Proof of the Main Theorem

Combining the results in previous sections, we
prove the following main result of this paper.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume that

and
\pi=\pi(\varphi,\chi)\in\Pi_{\mathbb{R}}(G)
has a generic local
L
-parameter
\varphi
.
If the component group
{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}
is trivial, Conjecture

Proof.

Suppose that
\pi=\pi(\varphi,\chi)
under the local Langlands correspondence as in Section
4.1

(\varphi,\chi)
denotes the enhanced
L

\pi

{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}
is trivial, then
G
is quasi-split and all the three sets

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}
,
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}
and
\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}
are equal, consisting of all the regular nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus the assertion is clear in this case.

Assume that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}
is non-trivial. By Corollary
6.2
,

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)^{r-\mathrm{max}}=\{{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)\}

is a singleton. By Theorem
2.5

\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{des}}(\pi)
. Hence from Corollary
3.6

{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)\in\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{ari}}(\pi)=\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{des}}(\pi)\subset\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi).

Recall that by
[
81
]
we have

{\rm AV}(\pi)=\overline{{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)_{\mathfrak{p}}}.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Ann}\,\pi)\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
be the associated variety of the annihilator ideal
{\rm Ann}\,\pi
of
\pi

(which is a two-sided ideal of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
). By
[
70
, Corollary 4]
, if
{\mathcal{O}}\in\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)
then

{\mathcal{O}}\subset{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Ann}\,\pi).

By
[
91

{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Ann}\,\pi)
is the Zariski closure of a single stable nilpotent
orbit
{\mathcal{O}}^{\rm st}(\pi)
, and by
[
91

we have

{\mathrm{AV}}(\pi)\subset{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Ann}\,\pi)\cap\mathfrak{p},\qquad{\mathcal{O}}(\varphi,\chi)_{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathcal{O}}^{\rm st}(\pi)\cap\mathfrak{p}.

In particular, if

{\mathcal{O}}\in\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{wm}}(\pi)^{s-\mathrm{max}}

and also the last statement of the theorem. The proof of the theorem is finished.
