Authors: María Lourdes del Río-Solá, Noelia Cenizo-Revuelta, Laura Saiz Viloria, Miguel Martín Pedrosa, José Antonio González-Fajardo
Published: 2026

We thank Dr Keleş and Dr Özbakkaloğlu for their interest in our article and for their thoughtful comments regarding the association between chronic venous disease (CVD) and cardiovascular risk.

We appreciate the opportunity to further clarify several aspects of our analysis.

First, regarding the pooled effect estimate, we acknowledge that the presentation of both logarithmic and exponentiated effect measures may have generated some ambiguity in the interpretation of the meta-analytic results. The statistical analysis consistently demonstrated a significant association between CVD and cardiovascular events. As illustrated in the forest plot, the pooled effect corresponded to an odds ratio of 2.50 (95% confidence interval, 1.15-5.44; P = .021). Therefore, the overall interpretation of the findings and the conclusions of the study remain unchanged. We thank the authors for highlighting this point and allowing us to provide additional clarification.

Second, we fully agree that substantial heterogeneity was present across the included studies. However, this was anticipated given the broad spectrum of populations, study designs, outcome definitions, and adjustment strategies represented in the available literature. For this reason, a random-effects model using restricted maximum likelihood estimation was selected a priori. Furthermore, heterogeneity was explored through complementary analyses, including metaregression, Galbraith plots, L’Abbé plots, and sensitivity analyses. Although effect magnitudes varied among studies, the direction of association was largely consistent, supporting the validity of the observed relationship between CVD and cardiovascular outcomes.

With regard to risk-of-bias assessment, we agree that formal evaluation of methodological quality represents an important component of evidence synthesis. Studies were selected according to predefined eligibility criteria and underwent independent review by two investigators. We acknowledge that the inclusion of a dedicated risk-of-bias tool could provide additional methodological transparency and may be considered in future updates of this work.

Importantly, we are pleased that the authors recognize the clinical relevance of investigating the relationship between CVD and cardiovascular disease. Beyond shared cardiovascular risk factors, accumulating evidence suggests common pathophysiological mechanisms involving systemic inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, vascular remodeling, and adverse cardiovascular outcomes. In our view, the totality of current evidence supports the concept that CVD should not be regarded solely as a localized venous disorder but rather as a potential marker of systemic vascular dysfunction.

We appreciate the constructive comments provided by Dr Keleş and Dr Özbakkaloğlu and welcome further discussion on this important topic.
