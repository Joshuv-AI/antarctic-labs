On the global dynamics and blow-up dichotomy for inhomogeneous coupled nonlinear Schrödinger systems
Mykael Araujo Cardoso, Lázaro Santos Gil

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the dynamics of an inhomogeneous coupled nonlinear Schrödinger system with quadratic-type interactions. Such systems arise naturally in nonlinear dynamics and mathematical physics, particularly in nonlinear optics, plasma physics, and wave propagation in inhomogeneous dispersive media. We establish a sharp criterion characterizing the dichotomy between global existence and finite-time blow-up of solutions to the associated initial value problem. This criterion is formulated in terms of conserved quantities, namely mass and energy, measured relative to the ground state solutions of the corresponding elliptic system. The analysis combines variational methods, conservation laws, and sharp Gagliardo–Nirenberg-type inequalities to obtain local and global well-posedness results in both subcritical and intercritical regimes. Our results extend and unify previous studies on single and multi-component nonlinear Schrödinger equations, providing a general analytical framework applicable to a broad class of coupled systems with spatially inhomogeneous nonlinearities and quadratic growth. Keywords: Schrödinger system, Well-posedness, Global solution, Blow-up

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 contains preliminary results derived from assumptions (H1)–(H8). Section 3 is devoted to the study of the local and global theories of system (
1
) in the spaces
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
. Our approach follows the standard techniques for Schrödinger-type equations: the Strichartz estimates, Hölder’s inequality and, Sobolev embedding combined with the contraction mapping principle are sufficient to prove the local well-posedness. On the other hand, the global results are proved in view of an a priori bound of the local solution in the spaces of interest. In particular, solutions are global for any initial data in the subcritical regimes; global existence holds provided that certain conditions on the charge and energy of the initial data are satisfied. In Section 4, we investigate the existence of ground state solutions for the associated elliptic system. This step is necessary since we want to obtain a sharp Gagliardo–Nirenberg-type inequality, whose best constant depends on such solutions. The existence of ground states is established by minimizing the so-called Weinstein functional over an appropriate set. Finally, in Section 5 we are interested in the dichotomy between global well-posedness and finite-time blow-up. In the critical case, we establish a sharp criterion for global existence, depending on the parameters of the system. In the supercritical case, we show that solutions remain global provided there is a suitable balance between the charge and the energy of the initial data, measured relative to those of the ground states.

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{u})(t)\;=\;U_{k}(t)u_{k0}+i\int_{0}^{t}U_{k}(t-t^{\prime})\dfrac{|x|^{-b}}{\alpha_{k}}f_{k}(\mathbf{u})\,dt^{\prime}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;k=1,\ldots,l.

is a contraction on the complete metric space

B(T,a)\;=\;\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{X}(I):\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}:=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\|u_{j}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\leq a\},

with the metric
d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}
.

Indeed, using Strichartz estimates Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
, we get

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{u})(t)\|_{S(L^{2},I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U_{k}(t)u_{k0}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}U_{k}(t-t^{\prime})\dfrac{|x|^{-b}}{\alpha_{k}}f_{k}(\mathbf{u})\,dt^{\prime}\right\|_{S(L^{2},I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{k0}\|_{L^{2}}+CT^{\frac{4-n-2b}{4}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}^{2}.

Summing the above inequality over
k
, since
\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}<r
and
\mathbf{u}\in B(T,a)
, we have:

\|\Gamma(\mathbf{u})\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}\;\leq\;Cr+CT^{\frac{4-n-2b}{4}}a^{2}.

Let us choose
a=2Cr
and take
T
such that
CT^{\frac{4-n-2b}{4}}a<\dfrac{1}{4l}
. We obtain

\|\Gamma(\mathbf{u})\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}\;\leq\;\dfrac{a}{2}+\dfrac{a}{4l}<a.

Therefore
\Gamma=(\Phi_{1},\ldots.,\Phi_{l}):B(T,a)\to B(T,a)
is well defined.

Now, we show that
\Gamma
is a contraction. Again using Strichartz estimates Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{u})(t)-\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{\prime})(t)\|_{S(L^{2},I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}U_{k}(t-t^{\prime})\dfrac{|x|^{-b}}{\alpha_{k}}[f_{k}(\mathbf{u})-f_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{\prime})]\,dt^{\prime}\right\|_{S(L^{2},I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT^{\frac{4-n-2b}{4}}\left(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}+\|\mathbf{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}\right)\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2CT^{\frac{4-n-2b}{4}}a\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2l}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}.

Therefore, summing again, over
k

\|\Gamma(\mathbf{u})-\Gamma(\mathbf{u}^{{}^{\prime}})\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}\;\leq\;\dfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}.

Finally, we will check the continuous dependence. Again by Strichartz estimates Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
, we get

\displaystyle\|u_{k}(t)-u_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)\|_{S(L^{2},I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|U_{k}(t)(u_{k0}-u_{k0}^{{}^{\prime}})\|_{S(L^{2},I)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}U_{k}(t-t^{\prime})\dfrac{|x|^{-b}}{\alpha_{k}}[f_{k}(\mathbf{u})-f_{k}(\mathbf{u}^{\prime})]\,dt^{\prime}\right\|_{S(L^{2},I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{k0}-u_{k0}^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}}+2CT^{\frac{4-n-2b}{4}}a\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}

Since
T
is small, and summming over
k
, we get

\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}\leq\widetilde{K}\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}\|_{\mathbf{X}(I)}

and the result follows.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Note that the time
T>0
obtained in Theorem
1.3
depends solely on the
\mathbf{L}^{2}
norm of the initial data. In fact, the existence time of the solution depends only on the
\mathbf{L}^{2}
norm of the initial data, not its position in space, that is, if

Proposition 3.6
.

(Sobolev embedding)
Let

.

(i)

If

is continuosly embedded in

, where

(ii)

for all

Proof.

See Bergh-Löfström

(see also Linares-Ponce

and Demenguel-Demenguel

As in the
L^{2}
case, using Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding, we obtain the following result

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
n+2b<6
and
0<b<\min\left\{2,\dfrac{n}{2}\right\}
, then

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}fg\|_{S^{{}^{\prime}}(H^{1},I)}\leq CT^{\theta(n)}\|f\|_{S(H^{1},I)}\|g\|_{S(H^{1},I)},

where

\theta(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\dfrac{4-n-2b}{4},\;\;\;\,\textrm{ if }\;n+2b<4\\
\dfrac{6-n-2b}{4},\;\;\;\,\textrm{ if }\;4\leq n+2b<6\end{array}\right..

Proof.

Let
R>0
be chosen later and denote by
B_{R}
the open ball of radius
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We consider the sets
B_{+}^{R}=B_{R}
and
B_{-}^{R}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}
. Let
0<\eta<1
. We will divide the proof into three cases:

(i)

4\leq n+2b<6

Let
\eta<\dfrac{6-n-2b}{6}
and
0<\epsilon<\min\left\{\dfrac{6-n-2b}{6}-\eta,\;\eta+\dfrac{n+2b-4}{2}\right\}
.

Define

r=\dfrac{2n}{n-2+2\eta}\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;r_{\pm}=\dfrac{4n}{n-2b+4-2\eta\mp 2\epsilon}.

From the choice of
\eta
and
\epsilon
, together with
4\leq n+2b<6
and
0<b<\min\left\{2,\dfrac{n}{2}\right\}
, one gets that
2<r<\dfrac{2n}{n-2}
and
2<r_{\pm}<3\leq n
. Moreover, the following relation holds:

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{r^{\prime}}=\dfrac{b+1\pm\epsilon}{n}+2\left(\dfrac{1}{r_{\pm}}-\dfrac{1}{n}\right)=\dfrac{b\pm\epsilon}{n}+\left(\dfrac{1}{r_{\pm}}-\dfrac{1}{n}\right)+\dfrac{1}{r_{\pm}}

Therefore, from the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev embedding (Theorem
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla(|x|^{-b}fg)\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{b\pm\epsilon}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}+\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{b+1\pm\epsilon}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}\right)\|\nabla f\|_{L^{r_{\pm}}}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{r_{\pm}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{\pm\epsilon}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{r_{\pm}}}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{r_{\pm}}},

and

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}fg\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}\leq CR^{\pm\epsilon}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{r_{\pm}}}\|g\|_{L^{r_{\pm}}}.

Now, let
q
,
q_{\pm}
be such that
(q,r)
,
(q_{+},r_{+})
and
(q_{-},r_{-})
are
L^{2}
- admissible pair. We can easily check that
\theta_{\pm}=\dfrac{6-n-2b}{4}\mp\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}
, and the following condition is satisfied:

\dfrac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\theta_{\pm}+\dfrac{2}{q_{\pm}}

then by the Hölder’s inequality in the time variable, we have

\||x|^{-b}fg\|_{S^{{}^{\prime}}(H^{1},I)}\leq C(T^{\theta_{+}}R^{\epsilon}+T^{\theta_{-}}R^{-\epsilon})\|f\|_{S(H^{1},I)}\,\|g\|_{S(H^{1},I)}.

Finally, taking
R=T^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have the desired estimate.

(ii)

n=3
and
0<b<\dfrac{1}{2}
.

Let
\eta<\dfrac{4-n-2b}{2}=\dfrac{1-2b}{2}
and
0<\epsilon<\dfrac{1-2b}{2}-\eta
. Define

r=\dfrac{6}{1+2\eta}=\dfrac{2n}{n-2+2\eta}\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;r_{\pm}=\dfrac{3}{1-b-\eta\mp\epsilon}=\dfrac{n}{1-b-\eta\mp\epsilon}.

From the choice of
\eta
and
\epsilon
, together with
0<b<\dfrac{1}{2}
, one gets that
2<r<6=\dfrac{2n}{n-2}
and
2<r_{\pm}<6=\dfrac{2n}{n-2}
. Moreover, the following relation holds:

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{r^{\prime}}=\dfrac{b+1\pm\epsilon}{n}+\dfrac{1}{r_{\pm}}+\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{n}\right)=\dfrac{b\pm\epsilon}{n}+\dfrac{1}{r_{\pm}}+\dfrac{1}{2}

Therefore, from the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev embedding, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla(|x|^{-b}fg)\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{b\pm\epsilon}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}+\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{b+1\pm\epsilon}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}\right)\|f\|_{W^{1,r_{\pm}}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{\pm\epsilon}\|f\|_{W^{1,r_{\pm}}}\|\nabla g\|_{H^{1}},

and

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}fg\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(B_{\pm}^{R})}\leq CR^{\pm\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{r_{\pm}}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

Now, let
q
,
q_{\pm}
be such that
(q,r)
,
(q_{+},r_{+})
and
(q_{-},r_{-})
are
L^{2}
-admissible pair. One can easily check that
\theta_{\pm}=\dfrac{4-n-2b}{4}\mp\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}
, and the following condition is satisfied

\dfrac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\theta_{\pm}+\dfrac{1}{q_{\pm}}.

Then by the Hölder’s inequality in the time variable, we have

\||x|^{-b}fg\|_{S^{{}^{\prime}}(H^{1},I)}\leq C(T^{\theta_{+}}R^{\epsilon}+T^{\theta_{-}}R^{-\epsilon})\|f\|_{S(H^{1},I)}\,\|g\|_{S(H^{1},I)}.

Finally, taking

, we have the desired estimate.

(iii)

n=2
and
0<b<1
.

Consider
B_{+}^{1}=B_{1}
and
B_{-}^{1}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}

B_{+}^{1}

0<\epsilon<\dfrac{1-b}{3}
. Define

r=\dfrac{6}{1-b},\;\;\;r_{+}=\dfrac{6}{1-b-3\epsilon},\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;p=\dfrac{6}{4-b}.

From the choice of
\epsilon

\dfrac{1}{r^{\prime}}=\dfrac{b+\epsilon}{2}+\dfrac{1}{r_{+}}+\dfrac{1}{p}.

Hölder’s inequality, Hardy’s inequality, and the Sobolev embedding give

\displaystyle\|\nabla(|x|^{-b}fg)\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(B_{+}^{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{b+\epsilon}}(B_{+}^{1})}\left\|\dfrac{f}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{+}^{1})}\|g\|_{L^{r_{+}}(B_{+}^{1})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{b+\epsilon}}(B_{+}^{1})}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(B_{+}^{1})}\|g\|_{L^{r_{+}}(B_{+}^{1})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{b+\epsilon}}(B_{+}^{1})}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(B_{+}^{1})}\|f\|_{L^{r_{+}}(B_{+}^{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\dfrac{p}{2-p}\right)\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{+}^{1})}\|g\|_{L^{r_{+}}(B_{+}^{1})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(B_{+}^{1})}\|g\|_{L^{r_{+}}(B_{+}^{1})}+C\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(B_{+}^{1})}\|f\|_{L^{r_{+}}(B_{+}^{1})}

\displaystyle C\left(\dfrac{4-b}{1-b}\right)\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\frac{r}{2}}}\|g\|_{H^{1}}+C\|\nabla g\|_{L^{\frac{r}{2}}}\|f\|_{H^{1}},

and

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}fg\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(B_{+}^{1})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{r}{2}}}.

This holds since

, where

Now, let

We now estimate on

. Consider, without loss of generality,

. Take

\displaystyle C\||x|^{-b-1}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{b-\epsilon}}(B_{-}^{1})}\|f\|_{L^{r_{-}}}\|g\|_{L^{r}}

3.3
Global existence of
L^{2}
-solutions

The key to obtaining global solutions is to establish an a priori estimate in
L^{2}
for the local solution, which follows from conservation of the charge.

The following derivation is formal, but the process can be made rigorous by approximating with sufficiently regular solutions and passing to the limit, or by using the strategy in
31
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Under assumptions
(H3)
and
(H4)
, the charge of the system (
1
), defined as

\displaystyle Q(\mathbf{u}(t))\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{l}{\alpha_{k}\sigma_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx

is conserved.

Proof.

Multiplying (
1
) by
\overline{u_{k}}
, we obtain

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}Q(\mathbf{u}(t))\;=\;2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}\mbox{Im}\;\sum_{k=1}^{l}\sigma_{k}f_{k}(\mathbf{u})\overline{u}_{k}=0,

where the last equality follows from Remark
1.1
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of the previous Lemma, we have the following

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
1\leq n\leq 3
,
0<b<\min\left\{2,\dfrac{n}{2}\right\}
with
n+2b<4
. Assume that
(H1)-(H4)
hold. Then, for any
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}
, system (
1
) has a unique global solution
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{X}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,

Q(\mathbf{u}(t))\;=\;Q(\mathbf{u}_{0}),\;\;\;\;\;\;\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Similary to Corollary
3.10
, define

T^{\ast}=\sup\{T;\;\;\mbox{ there exists a solution of }\eqref{INLS}\mbox{ in }[0,T]\}.

From Theorem
1.3
, there exists a unique solution
\mathbf{u}
of (
1
) corresponding to the initial data
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}
such that
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T^{\ast}),\mathbf{L}^{2})\cap\mathbf{X}([0,T^{\ast}))
. Now, if
T^{\ast}<\infty
, assume by contradiction that there exists a sequence
t_{k}\uparrow T^{\ast}
. Since
Q(\cdot)
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}
are equivalent norms on
\mathbf{L}^{2}
and by Lemma
3.11
, we have

\|\mathbf{u}(t_{k})\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}}\leq CQ(\mathbf{u}_{0})
for some positive constant

3.5

\mathbf{u}^{k}(0)=\mathbf{u}(t_{k})
there exists

-solutions

We begin by recalling an important tool for minimizing the Weinstein functional, which is fundamental in the study of INLS: a Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality, proved by Farah

Theorem 3.13

Let

The conservation of energy for system (

Lemma 3.15
.

If condition

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{u}(t))=\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{k=1}^{l}\beta_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{k}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx-2\emph{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}F(\textbf{u}(t))\,dx

\displaystyle\mbox{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\left[\dfrac{\partial F(\mathbf{u})}{\partial u_{k}}\partial_{t}u_{k}+\dfrac{\partial F(\mathbf{u})}{\partial\overline{u}_{k}}\partial_{t}\overline{u}_{k}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\mbox{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\left[\overline{\dfrac{\partial F(\mathbf{u})}{\partial u_{k}}}\partial_{t}\overline{u}_{k}+\dfrac{\partial F(\mathbf{u})}{\partial_{t}\overline{u}_{k}}\partial_{t}\overline{u}_{k}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\mbox{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\left[\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial\overline{u}_{k}}(\mathbf{u})+\overline{\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial u_{k}}}(\mathbf{u})\right]\partial_{t}\overline{u}_{k}\,dx

\displaystyle\mbox{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}\sum_{k=1}^{l}f_{k}(\mathbf{u})\partial_{t}\overline{u}_{k}\,dx.

The last equality is from

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}E(\mathbf{u}(t))

Remark 3.17

Theorem 3.18
.

Let

,

(i)

(ii)

If

\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}
satisfying

\textbf{u}\in\mathbf{Y}(\mathbb{R})
.

(iii)

If

Proof.

From Remark
3.17
, it suffices to obtain an a

. For
(i)
, using (

) and Young’s inequality, we can write for any

\alpha\beta\leq\epsilon\alpha^{p}+\dfrac{1}{q(\epsilon p)^{\frac{q}{p}}}\beta^{q}
, where

In our case

p=\dfrac{4}{n+2b}>1\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;q=\dfrac{4}{4-n-2b}

2|P(\textbf{u})|\leq\epsilon K(\textbf{u})+C(\epsilon)Q(\textbf{u})^{\frac{6-n-2b}{4-n-2b}}.

\displaystyle K(\textbf{u})=E(\textbf{u}_{0})-L(\textbf{u})+2P(\textbf{u})\leq E(\textbf{u}_{0})+2|P(\textbf{u})|\leq E(\textbf{u}_{0})+\epsilon K(\textbf{u})+C(\epsilon)Q(\textbf{u})^{\frac{6-n-2b}{4-n-2b}}.

2|P(\textbf{u})|\leq K(\textbf{u})\cdot\dfrac{2}{\xi_{0}}Q(\textbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly to the previous argument, we have

Therefore,

This bound is valid since

Finally, for case

Applying Lemma

is equivalent to (

is equivalent to (

4
Existence of ground state solutions

Building on the Theorem

, we will establish a sharp criterion for the dichotomy of global existence versus finite-time blow-up. This criterion, formulated in terms of the ground state of the corresponding elliptic system, for this reason, this section is devoted to proving the existence of ground state solutions as stated in Theorem
1.9
.
From now on, we will assume that conditions (H1)-(H8) hold.
First, let us introduce
the
Weinstein functional
, for the elliptic system. Given
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}
define

J(\boldsymbol{\psi}):=\dfrac{\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{\psi})^{\frac{6-n-2b}{4}}K(\boldsymbol{\psi})^{\frac{n+2b}{4}}}{P(\boldsymbol{\psi})};\;\;\;\;\;\;P(\boldsymbol{\psi})\neq 0,

(36)

where

K(\boldsymbol{\psi}):=\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}\|\nabla\psi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\;\;\;\;\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{\psi}):=\sum_{k=1}^{l}b_{k}\|\psi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;P(\boldsymbol{\psi}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}F(\boldsymbol{\psi}).

Remark 4.1
.

Note that we can rewrite the action functional
I
, defined in (
10
), as

I(\boldsymbol{\psi})=\dfrac{1}{2}[K(\boldsymbol{\psi})+\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{\psi})]-P(\boldsymbol{\psi}).

(37)

Remark 4.2
.

If
b_{k}=\alpha_{k}\sigma_{k}
i.e.
\omega=1
and
\beta_{k}=0
, for all
k
, we have the functions
Q
and
\mathcal{Q}
are iquals.

Remark 4.3
.

Similary to Propostion
3.14
, the following inequality holds

|P(\mathbf{u})|\leq C_{0}\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{u})^{\frac{6-n-2b}{2}}K(\mathbf{u})^{\frac{n+2b}{2}}.

(38)

We start showing that these functionals are indeed Fréchet differentiable. Here, the primes denote their Fréchet derivatives.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}
. Then

K^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\psi})(\mathbf{g})=2\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\psi_{k}\nabla g_{k}\,dx,\;\;\;\;\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\psi})(\mathbf{g})=2\sum_{k=1}^{l}b_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi_{k}g_{k}\,dx,

and

P^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\psi})(\mathbf{g})=\sum_{k=1}^{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}f_{k}(\boldsymbol{\psi})g_{k}\,dx.

Proof.

In view of our assumptions, the proof is quite standard, and we therefore omit the details.
∎

Lemma
4.4
implies that
I
is Fréchet differentiable and that its critical points (in the sense of Definition
1.6
) are precisely the solutions of (
9
).

In the following lemma we obtain Pohozaev-type identities which are satisfied by any solution of (

).

Lemma 4.5
.

(Pohozaev-type identities)

Let
\boldsymbol{\psi}
be a solution of (

). Then

\displaystyle P(\boldsymbol{\psi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2I(\boldsymbol{\psi}),

(39)

\displaystyle K(\boldsymbol{\psi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+2b)I(\boldsymbol{\psi}),

(40)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{\psi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(6-n-2b)I(\boldsymbol{\psi}).

(41)

Proof.

We first note that by letting
g_{k}=\psi_{k}
, for
k=1,\ldots,l
in (
1.6
) we obtain

\gamma_{k}\|\nabla\psi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b_{k}\|\psi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}f_{k}(\boldsymbol{\psi})\psi_{k}\,dx.

Summing over

and using Lemma
2.4
,
we have

K(\boldsymbol{\psi})+\mathcal{Q}(\boldsymbol{\psi})=3P(\boldsymbol{\psi}).

(42)

Therefore,

To prove (

), define
(\delta_{\lambda}f)(x)=f\left(\dfrac{x}{\lambda}\right)

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k}|^{2}\Delta^{2}\varphi\,dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}\mbox{Re}F(\mathbf{u})\Delta\varphi\,dx-4b\mbox{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}F(\mathbf{u})\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}\,dx,

where
\nabla(|x|^{-b})\nabla\varphi=-b|x|^{-b}\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}
.

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}(|x|^{-b})=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}((|x|^{2})^{-\frac{b}{2}})=-\dfrac{b}{2}(|x|^{2})^{-\frac{b}{2}-1}2x_{j}=-b|x|^{-b-2}x_{j}

and

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}(|x|^{-b})\dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}=-b|x|^{-b-2}x_{j}\varphi^{\prime}\dfrac{x_{j}}{r}\;\;\;\;\mbox{thus}\;\;\;\;\nabla(|x|^{-b})\nabla\varphi\;=\;-b|x|^{-b}\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}.

Using (
58
), we can re-write
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\mathcal{T}_{n}(u)\;+\;4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}-2\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}{r^{2}}-\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{3}}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|x\cdot\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx\;-\;\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k}|^{2}\Delta^{2}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\,Re\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\Delta\varphi-2(n+2b)+2b\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}\right]\,|x|^{-b}F(\mathbf{u})\,dx

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle 8\mathcal{T}_{n}(u)+\mathcal{R}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{R}_{2}(t)+\mathcal{R}_{3}(t),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}-2\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx\;+\;4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}{r^{2}}-\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{3}}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|x\cdot\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx;

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k}|^{2}\Delta^{2}\varphi\,dx;

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\,\mbox{Re}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\Delta\varphi-2(n+2b)+2b\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}\right]\,|x|^{-b}F(\mathbf{u})\,dx.

Let
\varphi(x)
as in Lemma
5.3
, then

0\leq\varphi\leq|x|^{2}
,
\partial_{r}^{2}\varphi(x)\leq 2
and
\partial_{r}^{4}\varphi(x)\leq\dfrac{4}{R^{2}}
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here,
R
is seen as a parameter that be chosen later.

Step
1
:
We will show
\mathcal{R}_{1}\leq 0
.

Let be the set
\Omega\;=\;\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;\;\;\dfrac{\varphi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}{r^{2}}-\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{3}}\leq 0,\quad\mbox{where}\;r=|x|\right\}
.

Since
\varphi^{\prime\prime}\leq 2
then
\varphi^{\prime}\leq 2r
,
\forall r\in\mathbb{R}
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}-2\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx\;+\;4\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}{r^{2}}-\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{3}}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|x\cdot\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}-2\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}{r^{2}}-\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r^{3}}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|x\cdot\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}-2\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx\;+\;4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\left(\varphi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}-2\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0.

Step
2
:
Bound
\mathcal{R}_{2}
.

Using that
|\Delta^{2}\varphi|\leq\dfrac{C}{R^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k}|^{2}\Delta^{2}\varphi\,dx\;\leq\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta^{2}\varphi|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}|u_{k}|^{2}\right)\,dx

(67)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{C}{R^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{k}|^{2}\,dx\;\leq\;\dfrac{C}{R^{2}}Q(\mathbf{u}(t))\;=\;\dfrac{C}{R^{2}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0}).

Step
3
:
Estimate
\mathcal{R}_{3}
.
Note that if
0\leq|x|\leq R
, then
\Delta\varphi=2n
and
\varphi^{\prime}=2r
. Thus,

\Delta\varphi-2(n+2b)+2b\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}}{r}=0\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{in}\;\;0\leq|x|\leq R.

Also, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\mathcal{R}_{3}(t)\;\leq\;C\,\mbox{Re}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}|F(\mathbf{u})|\,dx\;\leq\;C\sum_{k=1}^{l}\int_{|x|>R}|x|^{-b}|u_{k}|^{3}\;dx.

In the last inequality, we use Lemma
2.4
.

Recall that for any radial function
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see, for instance,
(
30
, equation (3.7))
)

\int_{|x|\geq R}|f|^{3}\,dx\leq CR^{-\frac{(n-1)}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{\frac{5}{2}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, from Young’s inequality we can write, for any
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{l}\int_{|x|>R}|x|^{-b}|u_{k}|^{3}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R^{-b}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\int_{|x|>R}|u_{k}|^{3}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{-\frac{(n+2b-1)}{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{\frac{5}{2}}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{l}C(\epsilon)R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{\frac{10}{3}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{l}2(n+2b-4)\epsilon\gamma_{k}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}}+2(n+2b-4)\epsilon K(\mathbf{u}(t)),

where
C_{\epsilon}
is a positive constant depending on
\epsilon
,
\alpha_{k}
,
\gamma_{k}
and
\sigma_{k}
.

Gathering together the estimates for
\mathcal{R}_{1}
,
\mathcal{R}_{2}
and
\mathcal{R}_{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathbf{u}(t))+CR^{-2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})+2(n+2b-4)\epsilon K(\mathbf{u}(t))+C_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8(1-\epsilon)\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathbf{u}(t))+8\epsilon\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathbf{u}(t))+CR^{-2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})+2(n+2b-4)\epsilon K(\mathbf{u}(t))

\displaystyle+C_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}}.

Since
\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathbf{u}(t))\leq-\delta
and

\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathbf{u}(t))\;+\>\dfrac{(n+2b-4)}{4}K(\mathbf{u}(t))\;\leq\;\dfrac{n+2b}{4}|E(\mathbf{u}_{0})|,

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(t)\;\leq\;-8(1-\epsilon)\delta+2(n+2b)\epsilon|E(\mathbf{u}_{0})|+CR^{-2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})+C_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}}.

(68)

Choosing
\epsilon
and
R
such that,

0<\epsilon<\min\left\{\dfrac{1}{8},\;\dfrac{\delta}{2(n+2b)|E(\mathbf{u}_{0})|}\right\}
and
\dfrac{1}{R^{2}}<\min\left\{\dfrac{\delta}{CQ(\mathbf{u}_{0})},\;\left[\dfrac{\delta}{C_{\epsilon}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}}}\right]^{\frac{3}{n+2b-1}}\right\}
, we have

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(t)\leq-2\delta.

(69)

Integrating (
69
) from
[0,t)
, we have

\mathcal{R}(t)-\mathcal{R}(0)\;=\;\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(s)\,ds\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{t}-2\delta\,ds\;=\;-2\delta\,t,

and therefore,

\mathcal{R}(t)\leq-2\delta t+\mathcal{R}(0).

On the other hand, using (
53
)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}(t)|=\left|2\,\mbox{Im}\,\sum_{k=1}^{l}\alpha_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u_{k}\,\overline{u}_{k}\,dx\right|\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{l}\alpha_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\varphi|\,|\nabla u_{k}|\,|u_{k}|\,dx.

Observe that
|\nabla\varphi|\leq CR
, from Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR\sum_{k=1}^{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u_{k}|\,|u_{k}|\,dx\leq CR\sum_{k=1}^{l}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CRQ(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}K(\mathbf{u}(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, we may choose
T_{0}
sufficiently large such that
\dfrac{\mathcal{R}(0)}{\delta}\;<\;T_{0}
, and thus,

\mathcal{R}(t)\leq-2\delta\,t+\mathcal{R}(0)\;=\;-\delta\,t+(\mathcal{R}(0)-\delta\,t)<-\delta\,t<0,\;\;\;t\geq T_{0},

and therefore,

0<\delta\,t\leq-\mathcal{R}(t)=|\mathcal{R}(t)|\leq CRQ(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}K(\mathbf{u}(t))^{\frac{1}{2}},\;\;\;t\geq T_{0}.

Equivalently,

\delta^{2}t^{2}\leq C^{2}R^{2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})K(\mathbf{u}(t)),\;\;\;t\geq T_{0}.

Thus

\displaystyle K(\mathbf{u}(t))\geq C_{0}t^{2},\;\;\;t\geq T_{0},

(70)

for some positive constant
C_{0}
.

Moreover, taking into account that
\epsilon
is sufficiently small (less than
1/2
is enough) by (
68
) and (
59
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathbf{u}(t))+CR^{-2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})+2(n+2b-4)\epsilon K(\mathbf{u}(t))+C_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\left(\dfrac{n+2b}{4}E(\mathbf{u}_{0})-\dfrac{(n+2b-4)}{4}K(\mathbf{u}(t))-\dfrac{n+2b}{4}L(\mathbf{u}(t))\right)

\displaystyle+CR^{-2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})+2(n+2b-4)\epsilon K(\mathbf{u}(t))+C_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(n+2b)E(\mathbf{u}_{0})-2(n+2b-4)(1-\epsilon)K(\mathbf{u}(t))-2(n+2b)L(\mathbf{u}(t))

\displaystyle+CR^{-2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})+C_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(n+2b-4)K(\mathbf{u}(t))+2(n+2b)E(\mathbf{u}_{0})+CR^{-2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})+C_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}}.

In the inequality above, we have used the conservation of the energy and the fact that
L(\mathbf{u}(t))\geq 0
. Note that the last three terms of the above equation do not depend on
t
. Then, we may take
T_{1}>T_{0}
such that

C_{0}\dfrac{(n+2b-4)}{2}T_{1}^{2}\geq 2(n+2b)E(\mathbf{u}_{0})+CR^{-2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})+C_{\epsilon}R^{-\frac{2(n+2b-1)}{3}}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{5}{3}},

where
C_{0}
is the constant appearing in (
70
). Thus,

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(t)\leq-(n+2b-4)K(\mathbf{u}(t))+C_{0}\dfrac{(n+2b-4)}{2}T_{1}^{2}.

Since
K(\mathbf{u}(t))\geq C_{0}t^{2}\geq C_{0}T_{1}^{2}\geq T_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(n+2b-4)K(\mathbf{u}(t))+\dfrac{(n+2b-4)}{2}K(\mathbf{u}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{(n+2b-4)}{2}K(\mathbf{u}(t)),\;\;\;\;t>T_{1}.

Now, integrating the last inequality on
[T_{1},t)
gives us

\mathcal{R}(t)-\mathcal{R}(T_{1})\;=\;\int_{T_{1}}^{t}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(s)\,ds\;\leq\;-\dfrac{(n+2b-4)}{2}\int_{T_{1}}^{t}K(\mathbf{u}(s))\,ds.

Since
\mathcal{R}(t)<0
, for
t\geq T_{0}
and
T_{1}>T_{0}
, and then,

\mathcal{R}(t)\;\leq\;-\dfrac{(n+2b-4)}{2}\int_{T_{1}}^{t}K(\mathbf{u}(s))\,ds.

Therefore,

\dfrac{(n+2b-4)}{2}\int_{T_{1}}^{t}K(\mathbf{u}(s))\,ds\leq-\mathcal{R}(t)=|\mathcal{R}(t)|\leq CRQ(\mathbf{u}_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}K(\mathbf{u}(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, define

\eta(t):=\int_{T_{1}}^{t}K(\mathbf{u}(s))\,ds\;\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;\;\;A:=\dfrac{(n+2b-4)^{2}}{4C^{2}R^{2}Q(\mathbf{u}_{0})}.

Since
K(\mathbf{u}(t))\geq C_{0}t^{2}
, we have
\displaystyle\eta(t)\geq\int_{T_{1}}^{t}C_{0}\,s^{2}\,ds>0
, for
t>T_{1}
.

Thus
A\leq\dfrac{\eta^{{}^{\prime}}(t)}{\eta(t)^{2}}
, for
t>T_{1}
. Finally, taking
T_{1}<T^{{}^{\prime}}
and integrating on
[T^{{}^{\prime}},t)
, we obtain

A(t-T^{{}^{\prime}})\;=\;\int_{T^{{}^{\prime}}}^{t}A\,ds\leq\int_{T^{{}^{\prime}}}^{t}\dfrac{\eta^{{}^{\prime}}(s)}{\eta(s)^{2}}\,ds\;=\;\left.-\dfrac{1}{\eta(s)}\right|_{s=T^{{}^{\prime}}}^{s=t}=\dfrac{1}{\eta(T^{{}^{\prime}})}-\dfrac{1}{\eta(t)}\leq\dfrac{1}{\eta(T^{{}^{\prime}})}.

In the last inequality we have used
\eta(t)>0
when
t>T_{1}
. The above inequality gives

0<\eta(T^{{}^{\prime}})\leq\dfrac{1}{A(t-T^{{}^{\prime}})},

taking
t\to+\infty
, we obtain
0<\eta(T^{{}^{\prime}})\leq 0
, which is a contradiction. Hence the proof of Theorem
1.11
is completed.
