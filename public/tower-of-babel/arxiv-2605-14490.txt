Geometric construction of superintegrable Poisson projection chains via Poisson centralizers


Abstract
. We introduce a geometric framework for constructing superintegrable systems from Poisson centralizers (commutants) in the Lie-Poisson algebra S ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) S(\mathfrak{g}) of a complex semisimple Lie algebra. Starting from a chain of reductive subgroups, we study the corresponding invariant Poisson subalgebras and their Poisson centers, and formulate superintegrability in terms of a Poisson projection chain of affine Poisson varieties. For a maximal torus T ⊂ G T\subset G , we prove that the inclusions S ​ ( 𝔤 ) G ⊂ S ​ ( 𝔤 ) T ⊂ S ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\subset S(\mathfrak{g}) determine a superintegrable chain and identify the associated quotient maps 𝔤 → χ T 𝔤 / / T → 𝜌 𝔤 / / G \mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\chi_{T}}\mathfrak{g}//T\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathfrak{g}//G . The rank (transcendence degree) computations yield the expected dimension split between commuting Hamiltonians and first integrals, and we describe the corresponding symplectic leaves in the intermediate space. Several examples illustrate how the centralizer generators organize into explicit superintegrable Poisson chains.

1.
Introduction

Integrable and superintegrable Hamiltonian systems provide a unifying language for the appearance of large families of conserved quantities in mathematical physics and symplectic geometry
30
;
37
;
20
;
33
;
25
;
19
;
42
;
34
. For a
2n
-dimensional symplectic manifold, Liouville integrability requires
n
functionally independent first integrals that Poisson-commute, leading (on regular level sets) to
n
-dimensional invariant Lagrangian tori. Superintegrability strengthens this by allowing more than
n
independent integrals, so that the generic invariant tori have smaller dimension
k<n
. In the extreme case of maximal superintegrability, one has
2n-1
integrals and all trajectories are closed
37
;
25
;
24
. The perspective adopted in this paper is that such over complete families of integrals can be organized algebraically via Poisson centralizers in the symmetry algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})
and geometrically via Poisson projection chains between the associated affine Poisson quotients.

The modern notion of superintegrability, as formalized by Nekhoroshev
33
, extends the Liouville-Arnold theorem to the noncommutative case and provides geometric descriptions of how superintegrabilities are realized in symplectic manifolds. A Liouville integrable system consists of
n
functionally independent conserved quantities on a
2n
-dimensional symplectic manifold and yields action-angle coordinates
33
;
1
. The further development on constructing superintegrable systems can be understood from the following two perspective:

On the algebraic side, a series of examples related to Lie groups was given by Mishchenko and Fomenko in
31
. In their paper, they introduced the argument shift method in the study of Euler equations on Lie algebras, constructing large Poisson-commutative algebras of integrals from invariant polynomials. Independently, Thimm developed a complementary method based on chains of subalgebras and proved that invariant geodesic flows in certain homogeneous spaces are completely integrable
40
.

On the geometric side, Guillemin and Sternberg
21
constructed the Gelfand-Cetlin system on coadjoint orbits of
U(n)
(in particular, on flag manifolds): using the chain
U(1)\subset\cdots\subset U(n)
and the corresponding moment maps, one obtains the Gelfand-Cetlin map whose components form an explicit collection of commuting Hamiltonians and yield a completely integrable system on a dense open subset of the orbit.

These first integrals in these constructions are expressed as polynomials in a basis of the Lie algebra. It is not required that they all Poisson-commute, provided that they generate a commutative subalgebra of sufficiently large dimension. This generalized integrability framework has been employed in a number of contexts. For instance, noncommutative integrability of certain magnetic geodesic flows on homogeneous symplectic manifolds was established by Efimov
18
. A construction based on Poisson projection chains was formalized by Reshetikhin
37
, and equivalence relations between superintegrable systems were derived. More recently, new superintegrable systems have been constructed on moduli spaces and in other Lie-theoretic settings
3
.

Although the theoretical framework of integrable and superintegrable systems is relatively mature, significant research directions remain in the following directions:

(1) Geometric realization of superintegrability: While Nekhoroshev’s theory provides a general framework for noncommutative integrability, the explicit construction of families of conserved quantities satisfying the noncommutative integrability conditions on concrete homogeneous or symmetric spaces remains an open problem.

(2) Classification and hierarchical structure of superintegrable systems: There exist various intermediate cases between minimally superintegrable (
n+1
integrals) and maximally superintegrable (
2n-1
integrals). How can these different levels of superintegrability be characterized geometrically? What is their correspondence with the foliation structure of the phase space?

(3) Unification of algebraic and geometric methods: The argument shift method of Mishchenko-Fomenko and the subalgebra chain method of Thimm construct integrable systems from algebraic and geometric perspectives, respectively. However, the intrinsic relationship between these two methods, as well as their generalization to superintegrable cases, has not been fully explained.

(4) Construction of superintegrable systems in new contexts: Recent progress has been made in constructing novel superintegrable systems on moduli spaces and in other Lie-theoretic contexts
3
. Nevertheless, the explicit expressions of their first integrals, their relationship with classical superintegrable systems, and their behaviour under quantization remain to be thoroughly investigated.

These questions suggest that, beyond individual examples, one needs a structural mechanism that simultaneously produces large families of first integrals, controls the induced foliation of the phase space, and clarifies how algebraic constructions in
S(\mathfrak{g})
manifest geometrically on Poisson quotients. A natural setting where these topic meet is provided by
reductive chains of Lie algebras and the associated chains of Poisson subalgebras. Recently, the construction of superintegrable systems based on Lie algebra reductive chains has been studied in
9
;
27
;
10
. The main novelties of the present paper are:

•

We make explicit the geometric construction from Poisson subalgebra chains in
S(\mathfrak{g})
to canonical sequences of Poisson maps between affine Poisson quotients, including the dimension identities required for superintegrability.

•

From suitable Poisson subalgebras of
S(\mathfrak{g})
, we construct an explicit
Poisson projection chain
(Definition
2.1
), and prove that it satisfies the superintegrability dimension conditions, recovering known special cases and producing new families of superintegrable systems.

•

We explain how algebraic invariant theory controls the geometry of the induced foliations and symplectic leaves along the projection chain.

•

We provide a unified Lie-theoretic framework that connects reductive (subalgebra) chain constructions with Mishchenko-Fomenko noncommutative integrability.

To help the reader navigate the paper, we summarize the main constructions and theorems below. The precise results are as follows.

(I) Theorem
3.18
is the basic example that motivates the rest of the paper: taking
A=T
produces a canonical Poisson inclusion chain
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
and hence a Poisson projection chain
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}//T\to\mathfrak{g}//G

S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
, which Poisson-commutes with all
T
-invariants, and the dimension identity is governed by the rank
r=\dim T
.

Main Theorem A
.

(Theorem
3.18
) Let
G
be a connected complex semisimple Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
T\subset G
be a maximal torus of rank
r
. With the Lie-Poisson bracket on
S(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
,
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
is a Poisson subalgebra chain inducing the superintegrable affine Poisson chain

(1.1)

\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\chi_{T}}\mathfrak{g}//T\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathfrak{g}//G

in the sense of Definition
3.16
. Moreover,

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset\mathcal{Z}\bigl(S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\bigr),\quad\mathrm{trdeg}_{\mathbb{C}}S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=r,\quad\mathrm{trdeg}_{\mathbb{C}}S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-r,

and

\chi_{T}
and

(II) Our second main result replaces the maximal torus with an arbitrary Abelian reductive and connected subgroup
A\subset G
. In this setting, there is, in general, no canonical analogue of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
inside the Poisson center of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}

\mu_{A}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{a}^{*}
induced by an

-invariant bilinear form and show that it provides the required base algebra for superintegrability.

Main Theorem B
.

(Theorem
3.25
)
Let
G
be a connected complex semisimple Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
A\subset G
be connected Abelian reductive with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
,
\dim\mathfrak{a}=s
. Fix a nondegenerate
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant symmetric bilinear form

, define

\displaystyle\mu_{A}(X)(H):=B(X,H),\qquad\mathcal{B}_{A}:=\mu_{A}^{*}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}^{*}]\subset S(\mathfrak{g}),

where
\mu_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{a}^{*}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}\subset\mathcal{Z}\bigl(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\bigr),\qquad\mathrm{trdeg}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{B}_{A}=s,\qquad\mathrm{trdeg}_{\mathbb{C}}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-s.

Hence

is superintegrable in the sense of Definition

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\chi_{A}}\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\overline{\mu}_{A}}\mathfrak{a}^{*},\qquad\mu_{A}=\overline{\mu}_{A}\circ\chi_{A},

where
\chi_{A}
is the affine quotient morphism.

(III) The first two theorems are formulated purely in terms of Poisson algebras on
\mathfrak{g}
and their affine quotients. The next step is to connect this Lie theoretic construction with the symplectic geometry of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
and the magnetic geodesic flows studied in our previous work
23
;
9
. In those papers, the basic integrals were obtained from the momentum map
P\colon T^{*}M\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
together with invariant polynomials on
\mathfrak{g}
, and the resulting level sets were analysed via reduction.

Here we package the same families of first integrals into a functorial statement: we construct an intermediate Poisson space
Y:=\mathfrak{g}\times_{\mathfrak{g}//G}(\mathfrak{m}-\varepsilon W)//T
and a Poisson projection chain
T^{*}(G/T)\to Y\to\mathfrak{g}//G
whose spectrum is identified with the torus quotient chain
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}//T\to\mathfrak{g}//G
. This produces a precise spectral equivalence between the dynamical system on
T^{*}M
and the algebraic superintegrable system on
\mathfrak{g}

Main Theorem C
.

(Theorem
3.19
)
Let
M=G/T
, write
T^{*}M
as pairs
(g,u)
with
u\in\mathfrak{m}-\varepsilon W
, and put
P(g,u):=\mathrm{Ad}(g)u\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{g}
. For basic generators
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
, set
\chi=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{r})
and

\displaystyle Y:=\mathfrak{g}\times_{\mathfrak{g}//G}(\mathfrak{m}-\varepsilon W)//T=\bigl\{(X,[u]_{T}):\chi(X)=\chi(u)\bigr\}.

With
\pi_{1}(g,u):=(P(g,u),[u]_{T})

\pi_{2}(X,[u]_{T}):=\chi(X)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\chi_{T}}\mathfrak{g}//T\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathfrak{g}//G.

\displaystyle\chi_{T}\circ\phi=\phi_{1}\circ\pi_{1},\qquad\rho\circ\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}\circ\pi_{2}=\pi_{2}.

(IV) Since we identified the two projection chains at the level of their spectral data, the next natural question is how the associated Poisson geometry is reflected on each side. In particular, the intermediate quotient
\mathfrak{g}//T

The following theorem makes this description explicit and relates it to the familiar Marsden-Weinstein reduction on coadjoint orbits.

Main Theorem D
.

(Theorem
3.39

\displaystyle J=(\rho,\overline{\mu}_{T}):(\mathfrak{g}//T)_{\mathrm{reg}}\to(\mathfrak{g}//G)\times\mathfrak{t}^{*},\qquad J([u]_{T})=\bigl((C_{1}(u),\ldots,C_{r}(u)),u|_{\mathfrak{t}}\bigr).

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}=\bigl\{[u]_{T}\in\mathfrak{g}//T:C_{i}(u)=c_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq r),\ u|_{\mathfrak{t}}=\alpha\bigr\}_{\varpi}.

Equivalently, if

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}\cong Z_{c,\alpha}/T=\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{c}\cap\mu_{T}^{-1}(\alpha)\bigr)/T.

In this Section

Let
(M,\omega)
be a
2n
-dimensional symplectic manifold. The symplectic form induces the Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\omega}
defined by

\begin{split}\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\omega}:C^{\infty}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)&\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(M)\\
(f,g)&\longmapsto\{f,g\}_{\omega}:=\omega(X_{f},X_{g}),\end{split}

for
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
, where
X_{f}
is the Hamiltonian vector field characterized by
\iota_{X_{f}}\omega=df
.

A function
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
is called a
first integral
(also: integral of motion, conserved quantity) of
f
if
\{f,g\}_{\omega}=0
. Equivalently,
g
is constant along the trajectories of
X_{f}
since

\displaystyle X_{f}(g)=\{f,g\}_{\omega}=0.

In this sense, first integrals encode symmetries of the Hamiltonian flow generated by
f
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional real Lie algebra with Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
. There is a classical Lie-Poisson bracket on the dual of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. For
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, the Lie-Poisson bracket is

(2.1)

\displaystyle\{f,g\}(\xi)=\langle\xi,[d_{\xi}f,d_{\xi}g]\rangle.

where
d_{\xi}f,d_{\xi}g\in T_{\xi}^{*}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong\left(\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)^{*}\cong\mathfrak{g}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the dual pair between
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{g}
. We refer the reader to
(
26
, Chapter 7)
for details.

For a symplectic manifold
M
, we assume that the Poisson algebra
C^{\infty}(M)
has a Poisson subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\subset C^{\infty}(M)
of rank (transcendence degree)
2n-k
for some integer
k
with
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
and the Poisson center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
is of rank
k
. Smooth functions in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
are called Hamiltonian, which are smooth functions Poisson-commuting with first integrals.

We recall the geometric description of the superintegrability of a Hamiltonian dynamical system. The reader can refer to
37
for more details and example.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a
2n
-dimensional connected symplectic manifold. A
superintegrable system
on
(M,\omega)
is a sequence consisting of a triple
(M,\mathcal{P}_{2n-k},\mathcal{B}_{k})
of Poisson manifolds with two Poisson maps
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle M\xrightarrow{\;\pi_{1}\;}\mathcal{P}_{2n-k}\xrightarrow{\;\pi_{2}\;}\mathcal{B}_{k}

such that the following holds:

(i) The Poisson maps
\pi_{i}
are Poisson submersions.

(ii)
\dim\mathcal{P}_{2n-k}+\dim\mathcal{B}_{k}=2n=\dim M
.

Remark 2.2
.

From the chain in Definition
2.1
, we obtain two natural Poisson subalgebras of
C^{\infty}(M)
by pullback:

\displaystyle J=\pi_{1}^{*}\bigl(C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{2n-k})\bigr),\qquad I=(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1})^{*}\bigl(C^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{k})\bigr).

Intuitively, elements of
J
only depend on the
\mathcal{P}_{2n-k}
-coordinate , whereas elements of
I
only depend on the
\mathcal{B}_{k}
-coordinate.

For a Poisson subalgebra
A\subset C^{\infty}(M)
, write

\displaystyle C_{A}(M):=\{f\in C^{\infty}(M)\ :\{f,A\}=0\}

for its Poisson commutant. With this notation, we set
C_{I}(M):=C_{I}(M)
and
C_{J}(M):=C_{J}(M)
.
Because
\pi_{1}
is Poisson and the functions coming from
\mathcal{B}_{k}
are Casimirs on
\mathcal{P}_{2n-k}
(via
\pi_{2}
), the two pullback algebras Poisson-commute. Equivalently,
J\subset C_{I}(M)
and
I\subset C_{J}(M)
. Concretely, for

and
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{k})
,

\displaystyle\{\pi_{1}^{*}h,(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1})^{*}g\}_{M}=\pi_{1}^{*}\bigl(\{h,\pi_{2}^{*}g\}_{\mathcal{P}_{2n-k}}\bigr)=0.

\displaystyle\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}C_{jk}^{i}x_{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}=0\ \ \text{for each }j=1,\dots,s;

X\in\mathfrak{g}
, let

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}_{X}:S(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbb{C},\qquad f\longmapsto f(X)

be the evaluation homomorphism. Similar to (
3.16
), we define the affine (GIT) quotient of
\mathfrak{g}
by
A
to be
\mathfrak{g}//A:=\mathrm{Spec}\bigl(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\bigr)
. Let
\mathsf{i}_{A}:S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})
denote the inclusion of invariant polynomials. By functoriality of
\mathrm{Spec}
, this inclusion induces a morphism

(3.18)

\displaystyle\chi_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//A,\qquad X\longmapsto\mathrm{ev}_{X}|_{S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}},

whose pullback map on functions is precisely

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi}_{A}=\mathsf{i}_{A}:S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g}).

In particular, for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\chi_{A}(X)=\chi_{A}(Y)\Longleftrightarrow f(X)=f(Y)\text{ for all }f\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}.

For
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by

(3.19)

\displaystyle[X]_{A}:=\chi_{A}^{-1}\bigl(\chi_{A}(X)\bigr)=\{Y\in\mathfrak{g}:f(Y)=f(X)\text{ for all }f\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\}

the fiber of the quotient morphism through
X
. Equivalently,
[X]_{A}
is the invariant-theoretic equivalence class of
X
(two points are equivalent if they cannot be separated by invariant polynomials). In general, this fiber may contain several distinct
A
-orbits, so it need not coincide with the single orbit
A\cdot X
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\zeta}
be homogeneous generators of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. By Proposition
3.9
, we deduce

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\simeq\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{\zeta}]/I,\qquad\mathfrak{g}//A\simeq V(I)\subset\mathbb{A}^{\zeta}.

Under this identification, the quotient morphism is the map

\displaystyle\chi_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V(I)\subset\mathbb{A}^{\zeta},\qquad X\longmapsto\bigl(f_{1}(X),\ldots,f_{\zeta}(X)\bigr).

Therefore, for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\chi_{A}(X)=\chi_{A}(Y)\Longleftrightarrow f_{i}(X)=f_{i}(Y)\text{ for every }1\leq i\leq\zeta\Longleftrightarrow f(X)=f(Y)\text{ for every }f\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}//A\simeq\mathbb{A}^{\zeta}\Longleftrightarrow I=(0)\Longleftrightarrow f_{1},\ldots,f_{\zeta}\text{ are lineally independent}.

Since
A
is reductive (in particular, semisimple), the invariant ring
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
is finitely generated and the morphism
\chi_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}//A=\mathrm{Spec}\bigl(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\bigr)
is the affine GIT quotient (hence a good quotient). Consequently, every fiber of
\chi_{A}
contains a unique closed
A
-orbit. Equivalently,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\chi_{A}(X)=\chi_{A}(Y)\Longleftrightarrow\overline{A\cdot X}\cap\overline{A\cdot Y}\neq\varnothing.

In particular, the closed
A
-orbits in
\mathfrak{g}
are in bijection with the closed points of
\mathfrak{g}//A
. If
A\cdot X
and
A\cdot Y
are both closed, then this relation (
3.20
) reduces to
\chi_{A}(X)=\chi_{A}(Y)
if and only if
A\cdot X=A\cdot Y
.

We now introduce the regular locus, which enable us to define smooth morphism on the GIT quotient.

Definition 3.14
.

(
22
, Chapter 1, Section 5)
,
39

The smooth value locus of the quotient morphism
\chi_{A}
is the maximal Zariski open subset of
\mathfrak{g}//A
over which
\chi_{A}
is smooth:

(3.21)

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{g}//A)_{\mathrm{reg}}:=\bigcup\left\{\mathcal{U}\subset\mathfrak{g}//A:\mathcal{U}\text{ is Zariski open and }\chi_{A}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})\to\mathcal{U}\text{ is smooth}\right\}.

Equivalently,
y\in(\mathfrak{g}//A)_{\mathrm{reg}}
if and only if there exists a Zariski open neighbourhood
y\in\mathcal{U}\subset\mathfrak{g}//A
such that

\displaystyle\chi_{A}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}

is a smooth morphism.

Proposition 3.15
.

(
7
, Proposition 1.26)

The affine quotient
\mathfrak{g}//A
carries a unique Poisson structure for which
\chi_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}//A
is a Poisson morphism. Moreover,
(\mathfrak{g}//A)_{\mathrm{reg}}
is a non-empty Zariski open dense subset of
\mathfrak{g}//A
, and the restricted morphism

\displaystyle\chi_{A}:\chi_{A}^{-1}\bigl((\mathfrak{g}//A)_{\mathrm{reg}}\bigr)\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{g}//A)_{\mathrm{reg}}

is a smooth Poisson morphism. In particular, after passing to the associated complex manifolds, this restriction is a Poisson submersion.

Proof.

Since the adjoint action of
A
preserves the Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
, it also preserves the Lie-Poisson bracket on
S(\mathfrak{g})
. Hence, for
f,h\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
and
a\in A
, one has

\displaystyle a\cdot\{f,h\}=\{a\cdot f,a\cdot h\}=\{f,h\}.

Thus,
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
is a Poisson subalgebra of
S(\mathfrak{g})
. This gives a Poisson structure on

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}//A]=S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}.

Since
\widehat{\chi}_{A}:S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})
is the inclusion,
\chi_{A}
is a Poisson morphism. The uniqueness of the Poisson structure follows from the injectivity of
\widehat{\chi}_{A}
, because the bracket on
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}//A]
is forced to satisfy

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi}_{A}\{u,v\}_{\mathfrak{g}//A}=\{\widehat{\chi}_{A}u,\widehat{\chi}_{A}v\}_{\mathfrak{g}},\qquad u,v\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}.

It remains to prove the smoothness statement. The variety
\mathfrak{g}
is smooth and irreducible, and the morphism
\chi_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}//A
is dominant because
\widehat{\chi}_{A}
is injective. Since the ground field has characteristic zero, by Definition
3.14
, regularity on
\mathfrak{g}//A
implies that there exists a non-empty Zariski open subset
U\subset\mathfrak{g}//A
such that

\displaystyle\chi_{A}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}

is smooth. By definition of the regular value locus,
U\subset(\mathfrak{g}//A)_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Therefore,
(\mathfrak{g}//A)_{\mathrm{reg}}
is non-empty, Zariski open, and dense. Since
\chi_{A}
is Poisson on the whole quotient, the restricted morphism remains Poisson. Since
\chi_{A}
is Poisson on the whole quotient, the restricted morphism remains Poisson. Finally, a smooth morphism of complex Lie varieties induces a submersion on the associated complex manifolds, and hence the restricted morphism is a Poisson submersion.
∎

We now provide a geometric interpretation of the definition of a superintegrable system in
S(\mathfrak{g})
, which can be seen as the combination of both Definition
3.12
and Definition
2.1
.

Definition 3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an affine Poisson variety with coordinate algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X})
. The inclusion chain of finitely generated Poisson subalgebras

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X})

defines a superintegrable system on
\mathcal{X}
if

(a)
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
. That is,
\mathcal{B}_{k}
is in the Poisson center of
\mathcal{A}
;  (b)
\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{A}+\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{B}=\dim\mathcal{X}
.

Equivalently, the induced chain of affine Poisson morphisms

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\ \rho\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}

has
\dim\mathcal{X}=\dim\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{A}+\dim\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}
.

Remark 3.17
.

Definition
3.16
illustrates an affine-algebraic expression: the chain
X\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\ \rho\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}
is required to be a chain of Poisson morphisms (i.e., the pullbacks
\pi^{*},\rho^{*}
are Poisson algebra morphisms), together with the dimension identity

\displaystyle\dim X=\dim\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{A}+\dim\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}\ \Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{A}+\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{B}=\dim X.

In particular,
\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{A}
and
\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}
are
not
assumed to be smooth.

We can recapture a Poisson submersion chain defined in Definition
2.1
by restricting to a Zariski open dense
regular locus
on which the relevant Jacobian ranks are maximal. See Definition
3.14
. Concretely, for a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
with
X
smooth, let
Y_{\mathrm{reg}}(f)\subset Y
denote the smooth value locus of
f
in the sense of Definition
3.14
, and define

\displaystyle X_{\mathrm{reg}}(f):=f^{-1}\bigl(Y_{\mathrm{reg}}(f)\bigr)\subset X.

Equivalently (since
X
is smooth),
X_{\mathrm{reg}}(f)
is the Zariski open locus on which the differential
df_{x}
has maximal rank. If
Y
is singular, the phrase
f
is a (Poisson) submersion
is therefore to be understood after restricting to
X_{\mathrm{reg}}(f)\to Y_{\mathrm{reg}}(f)
, where the target is smooth and the restriction is a smooth morphism (hence a submersion on associated complex manifolds).

On such regular loci, the induced maps in the corresponding chains become Poisson submersions between smooth Poisson manifolds, such that the geometric hypotheses of Definition
2.1
apply without modifying the algebraic superintegrability of Definition
3.16
as restricting to a Zariski open dense subset does not change the function-field
level relations.

Let
\chi_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}//A
be the categorical quotient map defined above. Since
\chi_{A}
collapses each
A
-orbit, we have the fundamental identity
\dim\mathfrak{g}//A=\dim\mathfrak{g}-d_{A}
. By Definition
3.16
, to construct a superintegrable system, we seek a chain of affine Poisson varieties

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})\,\xrightarrow{\,\chi_{A}\,}\,\mathfrak{g}//A=\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\,\xrightarrow{\,\rho\,}\,\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B},

where
\mathcal{B}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
is a finitely-generated Poisson-commutative polynomial ring of transcendence degree
m
, such that
\dim\mathfrak{g}=\dim\mathfrak{g}//A+m
. In what follows, we will concentrate on a specific case, letting
A=T
.

Let
T\subset G
be a maximal torus with
\dim T=r
. Then
d_{T}=r
, and Chevalley’s restriction theorem produces

(3.23)

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\cong S(\mathfrak{t})^{W}\cong\mathbb{C}[F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}],

where
F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}
are algebraically independent homogeneous generators. Hence
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
is a polynomial ring and therefore
\mathfrak{g}//G\cong\mathbb{A}^{r}
. The Kostant theorem identifies
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
with the Poisson center
\mathcal{Z}\bigl(S(\mathfrak{g})\bigr)
, and since
T\subset G
, we must have
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
. We then claim that the chain

(3.24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})\,\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{T}\ }\,\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\,\xrightarrow{\ \rho\ }\,\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}

is superintegrable.

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
G
be a semisimple and connected Lie group over a field
\mathbb{C}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
T\subset G
be a maximal rank torus
r=\dim T
. Then the inclusion of Poisson algebras

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})

makes the chain of affine Poisson varieties

(3.25)

\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\,\chi_{T}\,}\mathfrak{g}//T\xrightarrow{\,\rho\,}\mathfrak{g}//G

a superintegrable system. More precisely:

(i)

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset\mathcal{Z}\big(S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\big),

(ii)

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=r,\qquad\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-r,

(iii)

\displaystyle\dim\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})=\bigl(\dim\mathfrak{g}-r\bigr)+r.

Proof.

We start with (i). From the discussion above, we know
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
. Now, for any
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
, we have
f\in\mathcal{Z}(S(\mathfrak{g}))
. As
S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
, for any
g\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
, we must have
\{f,g\}=0
. Hence (i) is claimed. Moreover, using (
3.23
), we have
\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=r
, proving the first identity in (ii). By the definition of the spectrum, we identify
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})
.

To conclude that (
3.25
) is superintegrable, we only need to check the dimension. Consider the
T
-action on
\mathfrak{g}
by the coadjoint representation, which has a generic orbit dimension
r
. Indeed,
\mathfrak{g}
is decomposed into
T
-weight spaces

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\qquad\left.\mathrm{Ad}(t)\right|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}}=\alpha(t)\cdot\mathrm{id}.

Here,
\Phi
is the set of roots for
\mathfrak{g}
. Similar decomposition holds for
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The stabiliser in
T
is then finite, so
\dim T\cdot X=r
with
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. By the dimension formula for the GIT quotients in (
3.17
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-\dim(\text{generic }T\text{-orbit})=\dim\mathfrak{g}-r.

Hence, (ii) holds. Since
\dim\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})=\dim\mathfrak{g}
, together with (ii), gives

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{g}=\bigl(\dim\mathfrak{g}-r\bigr)+r=\dim\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}+\dim\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}.

As
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
are Poisson subalgebras and
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
lies in the Poisson center of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
, the induced maps in (
3.25
) are Poisson morphisms, and the pair

(3.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset\mathcal{A}=S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}

defines a superintegrable system in the sense of Definition
3.16
. This proves (iii) and the claim.
∎

Let
\chi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}//G
be the canonical quotient map and let

\displaystyle\chi_{T}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//T,\qquad\rho:\mathfrak{g}//T\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//G

be induced by the inclusions
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})
, so that
\chi=\rho\circ\chi_{T}
.

In what follows, let
M=G/A
. Recall that in
23
, we constructed the superintegrable Poisson chain on the cotangent bundle of a homogeneous space with the magnetic symplectic form. We will use the map

\displaystyle P:T^{*}M\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g},\qquad P(g,X):=\mathrm{Ad}(g)(X),

which is the moment map in our trivialisation of
T^{*}M
. We then consider the regular superintegrable system chain. That is, consider the case with
A=T
.

The vector space
\mathfrak{m}=T_{eA}(G/T)
is
T
-invariant. Define

\displaystyle\overline{\chi}_{T}:\mathfrak{m}//T\ \longrightarrow\ \mathfrak{g}//G,\qquad[u]_{T}\longmapsto\chi(u),

where
[u]_{T}
denotes the
T
-orbit class of
u\in\mathfrak{m}
. The corresponding fiber product is

\displaystyle Y:=\mathfrak{g}\times_{\mathfrak{g}//G}\mathfrak{m}//T=\{(X,[u]_{T})\,:\,\chi(X)=\chi(u)\}.

We equip
Y
with the unique Poisson structure for which the projections
\mathrm{pr}_{1}:Y\to\mathfrak{g}
and
\pi_{2}:Y\to\mathfrak{g}//G
are Poisson.

Theorem 3.19
.

Consider the superintegrable system chain

\displaystyle T^{*}M\xrightarrow{\ \pi_{1}\ }Y\xrightarrow{\ \pi_{2}\ }\mathfrak{g}//G,\qquad\begin{matrix}\pi_{1}(g,X):=\left(P(g,X),[u]_{T}\right),\\
\pi_{2}\left(P(g,X),[u]_{T}\right):=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}),\end{matrix}

where

are the basic invariant polynomials (i.e., coordinate functions on
\mathfrak{g}//G
) evaluated at the
\mathfrak{g}
-component.

Together with the chain
\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{T}\ }\mathfrak{g}//T\xrightarrow{\ \rho\ }\mathfrak{g}//G
, these two systems are spectrally equivalent in the sense of Definition
2.3
. In particular, we have the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T^{*}M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{g}\times_{\mathfrak{g}//G}(\mathfrak{m}-\varepsilon W)//T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{g}//G}}

This equivalence is implemented by the Poisson maps

(3.27)

\displaystyle\phi:=P:T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*},\qquad\phi_{1}:=\chi_{T}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}:Y\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}//T,\qquad\phi_{2}:=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}//G}.

They satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle\chi_{T}\circ\phi=\phi_{1}\circ\pi_{1},\qquad\rho\circ\phi_{1}=\pi_{2}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.18
, these two chains are superintegrable. From the assumption above, we have

\phi=P
,
\phi_{1}:=\chi_{T}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}
, and
\phi_{2}:=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}//G}
. It is clear that all the morphisms defined in (

\phi
is a moment map,
\phi_{1}
as a composition of Poisson maps, and

\displaystyle\rho\circ\phi_{1}=\rho\circ\chi_{T}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}=\chi\circ\mathrm{pr}_{1}=\pi_{2},

3.3.
The superintegrabilities chains for the case
A\neq T

In Theorem
3.18
, we take
A=T
, and form the superintegrability. In particular, we showed that the base algebra is chosen to be the Poisson center
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
. For a general closed reductive subgroup
A\subset G
, the base algebra is neither unique nor canonically equal to
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
. In Subsection
3.3
, we aim to determine possible finitely generated base algebras that give rise to a superintegrable Poisson inclusion chain with the fixed intermediate Poisson algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{Lie}(A)
. Recall that

\displaystyle d_{A}:=\max_{X\in g}\dim\bigl(\mathrm{Ad}(A)\cdot X\bigr).

By (
3.17
), we find
\mathrm{trdeg}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-d_{A}
. Since
Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})
is the Poisson center of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
, every subalgebra of
Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})
is automatically a Poisson commutative subalgebra.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
A\subset G
be a reductive subgroup of
G
, and let
\rho_{B}:\mathfrak{g}//A=\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}
be the morphism induced by the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Then the chain of affine Poisson varieties

(3.28)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{A}\ }\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{B}\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}

is superintegrable in the sense of Definition
3.16
if and only if
\mathrm{trdeg}\ \mathcal{B}=d_{A}
. Consequently, the set of all possible base algebras for superintegrable chains with the fixed intermediate Poisson algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}:=\left\{B\subset Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\right)\ :\ \mathcal{B}\text{ is a finitely generated Poisson subalgebra and }\mathrm{trdeg}\ \mathcal{B}=d_{A}\right\}.

Proof.

We first assume that the chain
\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{A}\ }\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{B}\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}
is superintegrable. By Definition
3.16
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\subset Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})\quad\text{ and }\quad\dim\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}+\mathrm{trdeg}\ \mathcal{B}.

Using (
3.17
), we then have
\dim\mathfrak{g}-d_{A}+\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{B}=\dim\mathfrak{g}
. Hence,
\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{B}=d_{A}
.

On the other hand, assume that
\mathcal{B}\subset Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})
is a finitely generated Poisson commutative algebra satisfying
\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{B}=d_{A}
. Since
\mathcal{B}
lies in the Poisson center
Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})
, the condition (a) in Definition
3.16
is automatically satisfied. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{A}\ }\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{B}\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}

is superintegrable, and the description of
\mathcal{B}_{A}
follows immediately.
∎

Remark 3.21
.

(i) Suppose that there exist algebraically independent generators
b_{1},\ldots,b_{d_{A}}
. Then base algebras are polynomial algebras of the form
\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{C}[b_{1},\ldots,b_{d_{A}}]
, and
\mathrm{trdeg}\ \mathcal{B}=d_{A}
. By Proposition
3.20
,
\mathcal{B}
is the base algebra of superintegrable chain (
3.28
).

(ii) Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

there exists a superintegrable chain of affine Poisson varieties
\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\chi_{A}}\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\rho_{B}}\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}
;

(2)

there exists a finitely generated Poisson subalgebra
\mathcal{B}\subset Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})
such that
\mathrm{trdeg}\ \mathcal{B}=d_{A}
;

(3)

we have
\mathrm{trdeg}\,Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})\geq d_{A}
.

If these conditions hold, then one may choose algebraically independent elements
b_{1},\dots,b_{d_{A}}\in Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})
and take

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{C}[b_{1},\dots,b_{d_{A}}].

In particular, the base algebra can always be chosen to be a polynomial Poisson-commutative algebra.

Corollary 3.22
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{trdeg}\,Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})\leq d_{A}
. Then, with the fixed intermediate algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
, there does not exist a superintegrable chain in (
3.28
).

Remark 3.23
.

Theorem
3.18
shows that the torus case is special: when
A=T
, we may take

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\right),

and then
\mathrm{trdeg}\ \mathcal{B}=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}=d_{T}
. For a general subgroup
A
, however, no such canonical choice is available unless we first prove that
Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})
has a transcendence degree of at least
d_{A}
.

Now, let us consider another special case of
A
. In this case, we choose
A
to be the normaliser of
T
. In the following proposition, we will show that the base space in this case is still the Poisson center.

Proposition 3.24
.

Let
T\subset G
be a fixed maximal torus, and let

\displaystyle N_{G}(T):=\{g\in G\mid gTg^{-1}=T\}

denote its normalizer. Set
A:=N_{G}(T)
and let
W:=N_{G}(T)/T
be the corresponding Weyl group. Then

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\right)^{W}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-\dim\mathfrak{t},\qquad S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\right).

Hence, the chain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\chi_{A}}\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\rho_{A}}\mathfrak{g}//G

is superintegrable.

Proof.

Since
A=N_{G}(T)
and the Weyl group
W=N_{G}(T)/T
, we have the connected component
A^{\circ}=T
and
A/A^{\circ}\cong W
. Thus Remark
3.6
(i) gives

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\bigl(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A^{\circ}}\bigr)^{A/A^{\circ}}=\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\right)^{W}.

As
W
is finite, the extension

\displaystyle\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\right)^{W}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}

is integral. Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}.

On the other hand, by Theorem
3.18
, we deduce
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})\right)
, whence
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\right)
. Checking the dimension leads to

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}+\mathrm{trdeg}S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=\mathrm{trdeg}S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}+\mathrm{trdeg}S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=\dim\mathfrak{g}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\chi_{A}}\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\rho_{A}}\mathfrak{g}//G

is superintegrable in the sense of Definition
3.16
.
∎

Finally, to conclude this subsection, consider
A
to be an Abelian reductive connected subgroup of
G
. Under this assumption, with the intermediate Poisson algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
, we will consider the superintegrable system chain.

Theorem 3.25
.

Let
G
be a connected complex semisimple Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
A\subset G
be a connected Abelian reductive subgroup, and let its Lie algebra be
\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{Lie}(A)
with
s:=\dim\mathfrak{a}
. For every
H\in\mathfrak{a}
and
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, define the linear polynomial
\mu_{H}\in S(\mathfrak{g})
by
\mu_{H}(X):=B(X,H)
. Let

\displaystyle\mu_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{a}^{*}

be the linear map
\mu_{A}(X)(H):=B(X,H)
. For a basis
H_{1},\dots,H_{s}
of
\mathfrak{a}
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}:=\mu_{A}^{*}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}^{*}]=\mathbb{C}[\mu_{H_{1}},\dots,\mu_{H_{s}}]\subset S(\mathfrak{g}).

Then
\mathcal{B}_{A}\subset Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\right)
, where
Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})
denotes the Poisson center of the Poisson algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Consequently, the inclusion chain of Poisson algebras

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})

defines a superintegrable system in the sense of Definition
3.12
. Equivalently, the induced chain of affine Poisson varieties

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{A}\ }\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \overline{\mu}_{A}\ }\mathfrak{a}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{a}

is superintegrable, where
\chi_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//A:=\mathrm{Spec}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
is the affine quotient morphism, and
\overline{\mu}_{A}
is the unique morphism satisfying
\mu_{A}=\overline{\mu}_{A}\circ\chi_{A}
. In coordinates, for a basis
H_{1},\dots,H_{s}
of
\mathfrak{a}
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}_{A}([X]_{A})=\bigl(B(X,H_{1}),\dots,B(X,H_{s})\bigr).

Proof.

Since
A
is reductive, the invariant algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
is finitely generated. Hence,
\mathfrak{g}//A:=\mathrm{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
is an affine variety, and the inclusion
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})
induces the quotient morphism
\chi_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//A
.

We first prove that
\mu_{H}
is
A
-invariant for every
H\in\mathfrak{a}
. Let
a\in A
,
H\in\mathfrak{a}
, and
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since
A
is Abelian, the adjoint action of
A
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
is trivial, and therefore
\mathrm{Ad}(a)H=H
. Using the
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariance of
B
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{H}(\mathrm{Ad}(a)X)=B(\mathrm{Ad}(a)X,H)=B(X,\mathrm{Ad}(a^{-1})H)=B(X,H)=\mu_{H}(X).

Thus,
\mu_{H}\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
for every
H\in\mathfrak{a}
, and hence
\mathcal{B}_{A}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.

We next prove that
\mathcal{B}_{A}
lies in the Poisson center of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. For
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})
and
V\in\mathfrak{g}
, let
\nabla f(X)\in\mathfrak{g}
be defined by
df_{X}(V)=B(\nabla f(X),V)
. Since
\nabla\mu_{H}=H
, under the identification
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, by the definition of the Lie-Poisson bracket, we have

(3.29)

\displaystyle\{\mu_{H},f\}(X)=B\left(X,[H,\nabla f(X)]\right).

By the
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariance and symmetry of
B
,

\displaystyle B\left(X,[H,\nabla f(X)]\right)=B([X,H],\nabla f(X))=-B(\nabla f(X),[H,X])=-df_{X}([H,X]).

Now assume
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Then
f
is
A
-invariant, hence also invariant under the identity component
A^{\circ}
of
A
. Since
H\in\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{Lie}(A^{\circ})
, the one-parameter subgroup
\exp(tH)\subset A^{\circ}
acts trivially on
f
. Hence,

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{Ad}(\exp(tH))X)=f(X)

for all
t
, and differentiating at
t=0
gives
df_{X}([H,X])=0
. Therefore, back to (
3.29
), we have
\{\mu_{H},f\}(X)=0
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
was arbitrary, we obtain
\{\mu_{H},f\}=0
for all
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Thus

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\in Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\right)

for every
H\in\mathfrak{a}
. Since
\mathcal{B}_{A}
is generated by the functions
\mu_{H_{1}},\dots,\mu_{H_{s}}
, it follows that
\mathcal{B}_{A}\subset Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\right)
.

We now compute the transcendence degrees. The map
\mu_{A}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{a}^{*}
is surjective. Indeed, the dual map

\displaystyle\mu_{A}^{*}:\mathfrak{a}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*},\qquad H\longmapsto B(\cdot,H),

is injective because
B
is nondegenerate. Therefore the linear functions

\displaystyle\mu_{H_{1}},\dots,\mu_{H_{s}}

are algebraically independent, and hence
\mathcal{B}_{A}\simeq\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}^{*}]
is a polynomial algebra in
s
variables. Consequently,
\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{B}_{A}=s
.

It remains to compute
\mathrm{trdeg\,}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Let
A^{\circ}
be the identity component of
A
and set
\Gamma=A/A^{\circ}
. Then
\Gamma
is finite. Let
R:=S(\mathfrak{g})^{A^{\circ}}
. The quotient group
\Gamma
acts on
R
by algebra automorphisms and we have

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A^{\circ}}\right)^{A/A^{\circ}}=R^{\Gamma}.

We claim that the inclusion
R^{\Gamma}\subset R
is integral (See, for instance,
(
4
, Section 5, Proposition 5.1)
). Let
f\in R
. Define

\displaystyle P_{f}(T):=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\bigl(T-\gamma\cdot f\bigr)\in R[T].

Then
P_{f}(T)
is monic and clearly
P_{f}(f)=0
. We now show that
P_{f}(T)\in R^{\Gamma}[T]
. Indeed, for any
\delta\in\Gamma
we have

\displaystyle\delta\cdot P_{f}(T)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\bigl(T-\delta\cdot(\gamma\cdot f)\bigr)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\bigl(T-(\delta\gamma)\cdot f\bigr)=P_{f}(T),

since
\gamma\mapsto\delta\gamma
is a permutation of the finite set
\Gamma
. Hence all coefficients of
P_{f}(T)
are
\Gamma
-invariant, i.e.,
P_{f}(T)\in R^{\Gamma}[T]=S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}[T]
. Therefore
f
is integral over
R^{\Gamma}
, and consequently the extension

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A^{\circ}}\right)^{A/A^{\circ}}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A^{\circ}}

is integral. Hence, taking invariants under the finite group
A/A^{\circ}
does not change the transcendence degree, and therefore

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\mathrm{trdeg}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A^{\circ}}.

To find the transcendence degree of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A^{\circ}}
, by the dimension formula of an affine quotient, we now compute the dimension of
A^{\circ}
-orbit, which is
\dim A^{\circ}-\dim{A^{\circ}}_{X}
. In other words, we now show the stabilizer
{A^{\circ}}_{X}
is finite. Since the torus
A^{\circ}
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
, so there is a weight-space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
, where
\Lambda\subset\mathfrak{a}^{*}
is the finite set of weights. The infinitesimal kernel of the
A^{\circ}
-action on
\mathfrak{g}
is

\displaystyle\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\ker\lambda.

If
H\in\mathfrak{a}
lies in this intersection, then
[H,Y]=0
for all
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Hence,
H\in Z(\mathfrak{g})
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple,
Z(\mathfrak{g})=0
. Therefore, the infinitesimal
A^{\circ}
-action on
\mathfrak{g}
is faithful.

Choose
X\in\mathfrak{g}
such that

(3.30)

\displaystyle X=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda},\qquad X_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda},

we have
X_{\lambda}\neq 0
for every nonzero weight
\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}
. Such elements form a nonempty Zariski open subset of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
H\in\mathfrak{a}
stabilizes such an
X
, then
[H,X]=0
. Using the expression (
3.30
), we find
\lambda(H)X_{\lambda}=0
for every nonzero weight
\lambda
. Since
X_{\lambda}\neq 0
, it follows that
\lambda(H)=0
. Since the nonzero weights span
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, we conclude that
H=0
. Thus, the generic stabilizer of the
A^{\circ}
-action is finite, and the generic
A
-orbit has dimension
d_{A}=s
. By the dimension formula for affine quotients (
3.16
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-d_{A}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-s.

Combining the two transcendence-degree identities gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}+\mathrm{trdeg}\ \mathcal{B}_{A}=(\dim\mathfrak{g}-s)+s=\dim\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g}).

Together with
\mathcal{B}_{A}\subset Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\right)
, this proves that the inclusion relations

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})

is a superintegrable inclusion chain in the sense of Definition
3.16
. Equivalently, the induced chain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{A}\ }\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \overline{\mu}_{A}\ }\mathfrak{a}^{*}

is a superintegrable chain of affine Poisson varieties. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.26
.

When
A=T
is a maximal torus, Theorem
3.25
gives the central base algebra

\displaystyle B_{T}=\mathbb{C}[\mu_{H_{1}},\dots,\mu_{H_{r}}]\simeq\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{t}^{*}],\qquad r=\dim T=\mathrm{rank}\mathfrak{g}.

Thus, we obtain a superintegrable chain
\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//T\longrightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. Moreover, in this case, we may also consider the chain from Theorem
3.18
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//T\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//G,

whose base algebra is
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
. This alternative is still compatible with the dimension characteristic as

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}=\dim T.

For a proper Abelian reductive subgroup
A\subsetneq T
, however, the chain
\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//A\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//G
is generally not superintegrable. Indeed,
\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}
. By the dimension characteristic, with the intermediate algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
, the base algebra needs to have a transcendence degree of
d_{A}=\dim A=\dim\mathfrak{a}
. Thus, for a general Abelian reductive subgroup
A
, the natural base algebra is not usually
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
. Instead, we may take

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{A}=\mu_{A}^{*}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{a}^{*}]=\mathbb{C}[\mu_{H_{1}},\dots,\mu_{H_{s}}]\subset Z\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\right),

which has the required transcendence degree
\mathrm{trdeg}\ \mathcal{B}_{A}=s=d_{A}
.

3.4.
Mishchenko-Fomenko subalgebras inside
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}

Throughout this Subsection
3.4
, let
G
be connected and semisimple over
\mathbb{C}
, let
A\subset G
be a closed reductive subgroup, and let
B(\cdot,\cdot)
be the fixed
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form used to identify
\mathfrak{g}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. In this subsection, we will consider the relations between the Mischenko-Fomenko algebra and the Poisson center of the centraliser
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. We would like to know under what condition the Mischenko-Fomenko algebra can be used as the commutative subalgebra satisfying the superintegrable Poisson projection chain. We define the Lie algebra centraliser by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{A}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}:\mathrm{Ad}(a)X=X\text{ for all }a\in A\},

We denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}
the set of regular elements of
\mathfrak{g}
. Here

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}:=\left\{X\in\mathfrak{g}:\dim\mathfrak{g}_{X}=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}\right\},\quad\text{with }\mathfrak{g}_{X}=\left\{y\in\mathfrak{g}:[x,y]=0\right\}

Moreover, we write
Z_{P}(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A})
for the Poisson center of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{A}=S(\mathfrak{g})^{A},\qquad d_{A}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{C}_{A}.

Recall that a base algebra for the superintegrable chain through
\mathcal{C}_{A}
is a finitely generated subalgebra
\mathcal{B}\subset Z_{P}(\mathcal{C}_{A})
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{B}=d_{A}.

We now recall a basic fact about the Mishchenko-Fomenko subalgebra defined in
32
. For a regular shift
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, the
argument shift
construction produces a Poisson-commutative algebra of maximal possible size, and in fact, it is freely generated by the expected number of elements. Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{\ell}
be homogeneous, algebraically independent generators of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
, where
\ell=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}
. For
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})
, define

\displaystyle D_{\mu}^{j}(f)(X):=\left.\dfrac{d^{j}}{dt^{j}}\right|_{t=0}f(X+t\mu).

The corresponding Mishchenko-Fomenko subalgebra is

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mu}:=\mathbb{C}\big[D_{\mu}^{j}(P_{i}):1\leq i\leq\ell,\ 0\leq j\leq\deg P_{i}-1\big].

Equivalently,
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is generated by the coefficients of all polynomials
P(X+t\mu)
with
P\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
.

Theorem 3.27
.

32

If
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is a polynomial Poisson-commutative algebra on

\displaystyle b(\mathfrak{g}):=\frac{1}{2}\big(\dim\mathfrak{g}+\mathrm{rank}\mathfrak{g}\big)

generators. Equivalently, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\cong\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{b(\mathfrak{g})}].

Proposition 3.28
.

For
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}
if and only
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}
. Let
P\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
. For every
a\in A
and every
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle P(\mathrm{Ad}(a)X+t\mu)=P(\mathrm{Ad}(a)X+t\mathrm{Ad}(a)\mu)=P(\mathrm{Ad}(a)(X+t\mu))=P(X+t\mu).

Hence, every coefficient of the polynomial
P(X+t\mu)
is
A
-invariant. Since
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is generated by all such coefficients, it follows that
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.

Assume conversely that
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Consider the quadratic invariant

\displaystyle Q(X):=\dfrac{1}{2}B(X,X)

defined in
41
.
Since
\kappa
is
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant, we find
Q\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
. The coefficient of
t
in
Q(X+t\mu)
is

\displaystyle D_{\mu}^{1}(Q)(X)=B(X,\mu).

By definition of
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
, this function belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
, hence to
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Therefore, for every
a\in A
and every
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, we deduce
B(\mathrm{Ad}(a)X,\mu)=B(X,\mu)
. Using
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariance of
\kappa
, this is equivalent to

\displaystyle B(X,\mathrm{Ad}(a^{-1})\mu)=B(X,\mu).

Since
B
is nondegenerate, we obtain
\mathrm{Ad}(a^{-1})\mu=\mu
. Hence,
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}
.
∎

Corollary 3.29
.

There exists a regular element
\mu
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
if and only if
\mathfrak{g}^{A}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}\neq\varnothing
. In particular, if
A=T
is a maximal torus, then every
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}
satisfies
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}

. If

Corollary 3.30
.

Let
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}
be a regular semisimple. Then, no base algebra
\mathcal{B}\subset Z_{P}(\mathcal{C}_{A})
such that
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
.

Proof.

Assume, for contradiction, that
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is a commutative base algebra, we deduce
\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{B}=d_{A}
. Since
\mu
is regular semisimple, the Mishchenko-Fomenko algebra has maximal possible transcendence degree, hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}(\dim\mathfrak{g}+\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}).

Therefore,

(3.31)

\displaystyle d_{A}=\frac{1}{2}(\dim\mathfrak{g}+\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}).

By the definition of
d_{A}
, this implies

(3.32)

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{C}_{A}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-d_{A}=\frac{1}{2}(\dim\mathfrak{g}-\mathrm{rank}\mathfrak{g}).

On the other hand,
\mathcal{B}\subset Z_{P}(\mathcal{C}_{A})
implies
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{C}_{A}
, and therefore

(3.33)

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mu}=\mathrm{trdeg}\ \mathcal{B}\leq\mathrm{trdeg}\,\mathcal{C}_{A}.

Substituting the identities (
3.31
) and (
3.32
) into (

) yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\dim\mathfrak{g}+\mathrm{rank}\mathfrak{g})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\dim\mathfrak{g}-\mathrm{rank}\mathfrak{g}),

which is impossible for nonzero semisimple
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

Remark 3.31
.

A regular semisimple Mishchenko-Fomenko subalgebra can never be a base algebra for the superintegrable chain through
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.

Theorem 3.32
.

Assume that
\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, and let
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

The chain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{A}\ }\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{B}\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}

is superintegrable in the sense of Definition
3.16
.

(2)

We have
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.

(3)

The inclusion of Poisson algebras
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
induces a Poisson morphism

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu,A}:\mathfrak{g}//A\longrightarrow\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mu}.

Therefore, whenever
\mathfrak{g}//A
occurs as the intermediate Poisson variety of a superintegrable chain, every regular element

\displaystyle\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}

produces a distinguished Poisson-commutative subalgebra
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
inside the intermediate algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.

Proof.

We first show part (1). Since
\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, Proposition
3.20
applies and yields that the sequence of morphisms

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{A}\ }\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{B}\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{B}

forms a superintegrable chain in the sense of Definition
3.16
.

We now show the second part. As
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}
, Proposition
3.28
implies that
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.

Finally, by Theorem
3.27
,
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is Poisson-commutative in
S(\mathfrak{g})
, i.e., the Poisson bracket on
S(\mathfrak{g})
restricts to a Poisson bracket on
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
.
By part (2),
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is a Poisson subalgebra of the Poisson algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. In particular, the inclusion

\displaystyle\iota:\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}

preserves both the commutative algebra structure and the Poisson bracket. Hence, it is a morphism of Poisson algebras. Applying
\mathrm{Spec}(\cdot)
to
\iota
yields a morphism of affine varieties

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu,A}:\mathfrak{g}//A=\mathrm{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mu}.

Moreover, by construction, the pullback
\psi_{\mu,A}^{*}:\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\to S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
equals
\iota
and is Poisson. Therefore,
\psi_{\mu,A}
is a Poisson morphism. This proves all the claims.
∎

Remark 3.33
.

The algebra
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
should not be identified with the base algebra
\mathcal{B}
from Definition
3.16
. The base algebra is required to satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\subset Z(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}),

whereas
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is only a Poisson-commutative subalgebra of

. Thus, in the superintegrable chain,
\mathcal{B}
remains the base algebra, while

is an additional Poisson-commutative subalgebra inside the intermediate algebra.

Corollary 3.34
.

Let
d_{A}=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Then

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\subset S(\mathfrak{g}),

and the chain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{A}\ }\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \rho\ }\mathfrak{g}//G

is superintegrable, where
\rho
is induced by the inclusion
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.

Proof.

We first verify that
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
is a base algebra for the chain through
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.
Since
A\subset G
, invariance under
G
implies invariance under
A
, and hence

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}.

The Poisson bracket on
S(\mathfrak{g})
restricts to a Poisson bracket on the subalgebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Moreover,
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
is the Poisson center of
S(\mathfrak{g})
. Thus, for every
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
and every
h\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
,
\{f,h\}=0
. Equivalently,

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset Z_{P}\!\left(S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\right),

where
Z_{P}(\cdot)
denotes the Poisson center.

As
\mathfrak{g}
is complex semisimple, Chevalley’s theorem identifies
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
as a polynomial algebra on
\ell=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}
homogeneous generators. In particular,

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathfrak{g}=d_{A}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{B}_{0}:=S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
is a base algebra in the sense of Definition
3.16
. Let

\displaystyle\rho:=\operatorname{Spec}(\iota):\mathfrak{g}//A=\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=\mathfrak{g}//G

be the morphism induced by the inclusion of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras
\iota:S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
. Proposition
3.20
applied to
\mathcal{B}_{0}
then implies that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{A}\ }\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \rho\ }\mathfrak{g}//G

is a superintegrable chain.

Next, we show the inclusion with
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
. By definition,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mu}=\mathbb{C}\big[D_{\mu}^{j}(P_{i}):1\leq i\leq\ell,\ 0\leq j\leq\deg P_{i}-1\big].

Taking
j=0
shows
P_{i}=D_{\mu}^{0}(P_{i})\in\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
for all
i
, hence
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
. Finally, since
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}
, Proposition
3.28
yields
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
.
∎

Remark 3.35
.

More generally, given a superintegrable chain
M\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}}\mathcal{P}\xrightarrow{\pi_{2}}\mathcal{B}
, we call it
refined
if we choose an intermediate Poisson-commutative subalgebra

\displaystyle C^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})\subset\mathcal{F}\subset C^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}),

such that the projection
\pi_{2}
factors through the associated affine Poisson variety
\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{F}
. In other words,
\pi_{2}
is the composite

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}.

See more detailed definition on the refined superintegrable system in
2
.

In the situation of Corollary
3.34
, the Mishchenko-Fomenko subalgebra
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
provides a refinement of the base
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
. Hence, we obtain the
refined superintegrable system

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\chi_{A}}\mathfrak{g}//A\xrightarrow{\ \psi_{\mu,A}\ }\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{\mu}\ }\mathfrak{g}//G,

where
\psi_{\mu,A}
is induced by the inclusion
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
and
\rho_{\mu}
is induced by
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
such that
\rho=\rho_{\mu}\circ\psi_{\mu,A}
.

Corollary 3.36
.

Assume that
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Then we have
\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\cong\mathbb{A}^{b(\mathfrak{g})}
, where
b(\mathfrak{g})=\frac{1}{2}\big(\dim\mathfrak{g}+\mathrm{rank}\mathfrak{g}\big)
. In particular, if
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}
, then
\psi_{\mu,A}:\mathfrak{g}//A\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{b(\mathfrak{g})}
is a Poisson morphism.

Proof.

Assume
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{reg}}
. By Theorem
3.27
, the Mishchenko-Fomenko algebra
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is a polynomial algebra on
b(\mathfrak{g})
algebraically independent generators. That is, there exists a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra isomorphism
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\cong\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{b(\mathfrak{g})}]
. Applying the contravariant functor
\operatorname{Spec}
yields an isomorphism of affine varieties

\displaystyle\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{b(\mathfrak{g})}]=\mathbb{A}^{b(\mathfrak{g})}.

For the final statement, assume in addition that
\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{A}
. Theorem
3.32
constructs a Poisson algebra morphism
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}
; hence, by taking spectra, a Poisson morphism

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu,A}:\mathfrak{g}//A=\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{A}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}.

Composing
\psi_{\mu,A}
with the identification
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}\cong\mathbb{A}^{b(\mathfrak{g})}
,
\psi_{\mu,A}:\mathfrak{g}//A\to\mathbb{A}^{b(\mathfrak{g})}
is a Poisson morphism.
∎

3.5.
Symplectic leaves in
\mathfrak{g}//T

Let
\left(\mathfrak{g}//T\right)_{\mathrm{reg}}
be the regular subset of
\mathfrak{g}//T
. In this Section
3.5
, we will examine the symplectic leaves in
\left(\mathfrak{g}//T\right)_{\mathrm{reg}}
by studying the pull back of the regular element in
\mathfrak{g}//G
via
\rho^{-1}
. Let
G
be a semisimple and connected Lie group with a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Fix an
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
B(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{g}
. For any
H\in\mathfrak{t}
and
u\in\mathfrak{g}
, define the linear function:

\displaystyle\mu_{H}(u):=B(u,H).

Note that
\mu_{H}
can be considered linear generators for the Cartan commutant
S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
. A similar argument can be found in
(
9
, Proposition 2.5)
.

Lemma 3.37
.

For every
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, we have
\mu_{H}\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
and
\{\mu_{H},f\}=0
for all
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
.

Proof.

See the first part of the proof Theorem
3.25
. ∎

Let
\{C_{1},\dots,C_{r}\}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
be a set of homogeneous algebraically independent generators. By Theorem
3.18
,

\displaystyle\chi_{T}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//T,\qquad\rho:\mathfrak{g}//T\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//G,\qquad\chi:=\rho\circ\chi_{T}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}//G,

such that
\rho
is the morphism induced by the inclusion
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
. Then, for any
c\in(\mathfrak{g}//G)_{\mathrm{reg}}
and the corresponding coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{c}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho^{-1}(c)=\chi_{T}(\mathcal{O}_{c})\subset\mathfrak{g}//T,\qquad\chi_{T}^{-1}\big(\rho^{-1}(c)\big)=\chi^{-1}(c)=\{u\in\mathfrak{g}:C_{i}(u)=c_{i}\}.

Thus, the fiber of
\rho
is the image of a single coadjoint orbit, and its pullback to
\mathfrak{g}
is the usual Kostant-Chevalley level set.

By Remark
2.2
, we see that
\rho^{-1}(c)
is usually a union of symplectic leaves. To isolate a single leaf inside
\rho^{-1}(c)
, we must fix the
T
moment map component. Consider the
T
-equivariant linear map

(3.34)

\displaystyle\mu:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{t},\qquad\mu(u)=u|_{\mathfrak{t}}.

Since
\mu
is
T
-invariant, it induces a regular map

(3.35)

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}:\mathfrak{g}//T\longrightarrow\mathfrak{t},\qquad\bar{\mu}([u]_{T})=u|_{\mathfrak{t}}.

We then define

\displaystyle J:=(\rho,\bar{\mu}):\mathfrak{g}//T\longrightarrow\left(\mathfrak{g}//G\right)\times\mathfrak{t}

by joining
\rho
and
\bar{\mu}
in (
3.35
) such that each component in the fiber of
\rho
are controlled.

Proposition 3.38
.

Each component of
J
is a Casimir in the center of
\mathfrak{g}//T
. Equivalently, the pullbacks
\rho^{*}(C_{i})
and the coordinate functions of
\bar{\mu}
all lie in
\mathcal{Z}\bigl(S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\bigr)
.

Proof.

The components of
\bar{\mu}
are
\mu_{H}
with
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, which are central in
S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
by Lemma
3.37
.
∎

By Proposition
3.38
, each component of
J:\mathfrak{g}//T\rightarrow(\mathfrak{g}//G)\times\mathfrak{t}
is a Casimir. Hence, all Hamiltonian vector fields on
\mathfrak{g}//T
are tangent to the fibers of
J
. Therefore, the characteristic distribution of the Poisson structure is contained in
\ker dJ
, and every fiber
J^{-1}(c,\alpha)
is a union of symplectic leaves. On
(\mathfrak{g}//T)_{\mathrm{reg}}
, the rank of
dJ
is constant and equal to
2r
, and it is sufficient to show that for every regular pair
(c,\alpha)
, there exists a connected symplectic submanifold whose image is equal to
J^{-1}(c,\alpha)
. Then each connected component of that fiber is a symplectic leaf, and the Poisson rank is constant. At any regular point, the symplectic leaf has dimension

\displaystyle\dim(\mathfrak{g}//T)_{\mathrm{reg}}-\mathrm{rank}(dJ).

Thus, each connected component of a regular fiber is a single symplectic leaf.

We now provide a formal construction of the symplectic leaf in
\mathfrak{g}//T
. Let
\chi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}//G
be the Chevalley map given by
\chi(u)=(C_{1}(u),\dots,C_{r}(u))
. For a point
c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{r})\in\mathfrak{g}//G
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{c}:=\chi^{-1}(c),

which is a union of
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-orbits. In particular, on the regular locus
\left(\mathfrak{g}//G\right)_{\mathrm{reg}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{c}
, there is a single coadjoint orbit. For each
\alpha
, we define the restricted moment map

\mu_{T}:=\left.\mu\right|_{\mathcal{O}_{c}}:\mathcal{O}_{c}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \iota\ }\mathfrak{t}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{t}

such that
\iota_{X_{H}}\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c}=dB(\mu_{T},H)
with
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, given by
\mu_{T}^{c}(\eta)=\eta|_{\mathfrak{t}}
. For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, define

\displaystyle J^{-1}(c,\alpha)=\bigl\{[u]_{T}\in\mathfrak{g}//T:C_{i}(u)=c_{i},\text{ with }i=1,\dots,r,\ u|_{\mathfrak{t}}=\alpha\bigr\}.

In the rest of this subsection, we will work on the regular locus
(\mathfrak{g}//T)_{\mathrm{reg}}
, where we assume that the differentials
\{\,d(\rho^{*}C_{i}),\,d(\bar{\mu}_{1}),\dots,d(\bar{\mu}_{r})\,\}
have maximal rank
2r
, and the
T
-action on
\mu_{T,c}^{-1}(\alpha):=\mu^{-1}(\alpha)\cap\mathcal{O}_{c}
is globally free on the regular locus. Then in the following theorem, we illustrate the symplectic leaf in
\mathfrak{g}//T
. Then, on the regular locus,

\displaystyle\rho^{-1}(c)=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\mu_{T}(\mathcal{O}_{c})}J^{-1}(c,\alpha).

In the following theorem, we provide the identification of the symplectic leaf
J^{-1}(c,\alpha)
by identifying these fibers with reduced adjoint orbits. For the Marsden-Weinstein reduction on a submanifold, see, for instance,
14
.

Theorem 3.39
.

Let
c\in\left(\mathfrak{g}//T\right)_{\mathrm{reg}}
, and let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is the regular value of
\mu_{T}:\mathcal{O}_{c}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. Let
\bigl(J^{-1}(c,\alpha)\bigr)_{\varpi}
be the smooth and connected component of
J^{-1}(c,\alpha)
. On
(\mathfrak{g}//T)_{\mathrm{reg}}
, the symplectic leaves are precisely the connected components of the common level sets of
J
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}=\bigl(J^{-1}(c,\alpha)\bigr)_{\varpi}=\bigl\{[u]_{T}\in\mathfrak{g}//T:\ C_{i}(u)=c_{i},\ u|_{\mathfrak{t}}=\alpha\bigr\}_{\varpi}.

Equivalently, for any
c\in(\mathfrak{g}//G)_{\mathrm{reg}}
, suppose that
\mu_{T}:\mathcal{O}_{c}\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is the
T
-moment map on any coadjoint orbit
(\mathcal{O}_{c},\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c})
. Let

(3.36)

\displaystyle Z_{c,\alpha}:=\mu_{T,c}^{-1}(\alpha)=\mathcal{O}_{c}\cap\mu^{-1}(\alpha).

Suppose that
T
-action on
Z_{c,\alpha}
is free. The Marsden-Weinstein reduction is then given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}\cong\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{c}\cap\mu^{-1}(\alpha)\bigr)\big/T.

Proof.

Fix
c\in(\mathfrak{g}//G)_{\mathrm{reg}}
and let
(\mathcal{O}_{c},\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c})
be the coadjoint orbit. For
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, the fundamental vector field generated by
H
at
\eta\in\mathcal{O}_{c}
is
X_{H}(\eta)=[H,\eta]
. For any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
, the KKS form at
c
is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c}(\eta)\big([X,\eta],[Y,\eta]\big)=B(\eta,[X,Y]).

We proof this theorem by showing the following parts: we first show that
\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T:=\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{c}\cap\mu^{-1}(\alpha)\bigr)\big/T
is symplectic by providing its symplectic structure. We then show that
\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T
to
\mathfrak{g}//T
is a Poisson immersion. Finally, we explain why
\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T
can be identified by the level set of
J
.

Recall that the
T
-action on
\mathcal{O}_{c}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{g}
is Hamiltonian, with the moment map
\mu_{T}:\mathcal{O}_{c}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}
given by restriction to
\mathfrak{t}
. For a fixed regular value
\alpha\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{t}
such that the
T
-action on

\displaystyle Z_{c,\alpha}=\mu_{T,c}^{-1}(\alpha)=\mathcal{O}_{c}\cap\mu^{-1}(\alpha)

is free and proper. Let
\zeta:Z_{c,\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{c}
be the inclusion, and let

\rho:Z_{c,\alpha}\rightarrow Z_{c,\alpha}/T
be the principal
T
-bundle. Then the pullback of the KKS form to
Z_{c,\alpha}
gives

\displaystyle\iota_{X_{H}}\,\zeta^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c}=\zeta^{*}\big(\iota_{X_{H}}\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c}\big)=\zeta^{*}\big(d\langle\mu_{T},H\rangle\big)=d\big(\langle\mu_{T},H\rangle\circ\zeta\big)=0,

since
\langle\mu_{T},H\rangle
is constant on the level set
Z_{c,\alpha}
. Moreover,
\zeta^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c}
is
T
-invariant as
\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c}
is
G
-invariant. Hence,
\zeta^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c}
is a basic
2
-form for the principal
T
-bundle
\rho
. Since
T
acts freely and properly on
Z_{c,\alpha}
, by Marsden-Weinstein reduction, this action produces a smooth symplectic manifold

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T=Z_{c,\alpha}/T

with a symplectic structure
\omega_{\mathrm{red}}
induced by

\displaystyle\zeta^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c}=\rho^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{red}}.

Let
J_{\alpha}:\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}//T
. To show that
\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T
is a symplectic leaf, it is sufficient to show that
J_{\alpha}
is a Poisson immersion. Consider the GIT quotient
\chi_{T}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}//T
. It is a Poisson submersion. The pullback on the coordinate algebra induces the following injection
\chi_{T}^{*}:\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{g}//T)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]\cong S(\mathfrak{g})
. The composition
Z_{c,\alpha}\xrightarrow{\ \zeta\ }\mathcal{O}_{c}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{T}|_{\mathcal{O}_{c}}=\chi_{T}\ }\mathfrak{g}//T
is
T
-invariant, and therefore it factors through a continuous map
J_{\alpha}:\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}//T
. In this way, we form a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z_{c,\alpha}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}_{c}\subset\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T=Z_{c,\alpha}/T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{g}//T}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\zeta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{T}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\circlearrowright}

with
J_{\alpha}\circ\rho=\chi_{T}\circ\zeta
. We first show that
dJ_{\alpha}:T\left(\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T\right)\rightarrow T\left(\mathfrak{g}//T\right)
is injective. By the definition of the orbit space, the fiber of
\rho
through
u
is exactly the orbit. That is,
\rho^{-1}([u]_{T})=T\cdot u
. Fix
u\in Z_{c,\alpha}
and let
[u]_{T}:=\rho(u)\in\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T
. Since
\rho
is a principal
T
-bundle, each fiber

is an embedded submanifold, and

\displaystyle\ker(d\rho)_{u}=T_{u}(T\cdot u).

Here

is the
T
-orbit through
u
, and
T_{u}(T\cdot u)
denotes its tangent space. Consequently, by the first isomorphism theorem on the Poisson projection
d\rho
, there is a canonical identification

(3.37)

\displaystyle T_{[u]_{T}}(\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T)\cong T_{u}Z_{c,\alpha}\big/T_{u}(T\cdot u).

We now provide the differentiation of
J_{\alpha}
. Since
\zeta
is an inclusion, for every
v\in T_{u}Z_{c,\alpha}
,
(d\zeta)_{u}:T_{u}Z_{c,\alpha}\to T_{u}\mathcal{O}_{c}
is the inclusion of tangent spaces. That is,
(d\zeta)_{u}(v)=v
. Then the differential of the commutative relations above is as follows:

(3.38)

\displaystyle(dJ_{\alpha})_{[u]_{T}}\big((d\rho)_{u}(v)\big)=(d\chi_{T})_{u}(v).

To conclude that

at
[u]_{T}
is injective, it is sufficient to show that
\ker(d\chi_{T})_{u}\subset\ker(d\rho)_{u}
. As
\chi_{T}
is
T
-invariant,
(d\chi_{T})_{u}
vanishes on
T_{u}(T\cdot u)
, the right hand side of (

) depends only on the class of
v
modulo
T_{u}(T\cdot u)
. Thus, (
3.38
) is compatible with (
3.37
). Suppose
(dJ_{\alpha})_{[u]_{T}}(\varsigma)=0
for some

. Let
V=(\mathfrak{g}//T)_{\mathrm{reg}}
. By Proposition
3.15
, the restriction
\chi_{T}:\chi_{T}^{-1}(V)\to V
is smooth. Hence,
(d\chi_{T})_{u}
is surjective for all

. Choose
v\in T_{u}Z_{c,\alpha}
with
\varsigma=(d\rho)_{u}(v)
. Since
\chi_{T}
is
T
-invariant, the identity (
3.38
) gives
(d\chi_{T})_{u}(v)=0
. Hence,
v\in\ker(d\chi_{T})_{u}
, and

T_{u}(T\cdot u)\subseteq\ker(d\chi_{T})_{u}.

Assume now that
J_{\alpha}([u]_{T})\in V
. Then
u\in\chi_{T}^{-1}(V)
. In particular,
\chi_{T}
is a submersion at
u
. Therefore, the fiber
F:=\chi_{T}^{-1}(\chi_{T}(u))
is a smooth embedded submanifold of
\mathfrak{g}
, and the constant rank theorem yields

(3.39)

\displaystyle T_{u}F=\ker(d\chi_{T})_{u}.

Since
T
is compact, invariant polynomials separate
T
-orbits. Hence, the fiber
F
coincides with the
T
-orbit through
u
. That is,
F=T\cdot u
. Hence, (
3.39
) immediately gives

(3.40)

\displaystyle\ker(d\chi_{T})_{u}=T_{u}(T\cdot u).

Finally, since

is a principal
T
-bundle, we have
\ker(d\rho)_{u}=T_{u}(T\cdot u)
. Thus, if

, as claimed.
Therefore,
\varsigma=(d\rho)_{u}(v)=0
implies that
(dJ_{\alpha})_{[u]_{T}}
is injective. Since

was arbitrary,
J_{\alpha}
is an immersion.

Define the pullback of
J_{\alpha}
by

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}^{*}(f)\circ\rho=\left.(\chi_{T}^{*}f)\right|_{Z_{c,\alpha}}

for any

\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\sharp}
be the Poisson structure on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathfrak{g}//T\right)
. Using the fact that
\chi_{T}
is Poisson, we compute

(3.41)

\displaystyle\rho^{*}\big(J_{\alpha}^{*}\{f,g\}_{\sharp}\big)

\rho^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{red}}=\zeta^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{KKS},c}

\displaystyle\rho^{*}\big(J_{\alpha}^{*}\{f,g\}_{\sharp}\big)=\rho^{*}\big\{J_{\alpha}^{*}F,J_{\alpha}^{*}G\big\}_{\mathrm{red}}.

Here

is the Poisson bracket induced by the symplectic structure

.
Since
\rho
is a surjective submersion (on algebras), and
\rho^{*}
is injective (on functions), the equality holds before applying

as follows:

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}^{*}\{f,g\}_{\sharp}=\{J_{\alpha}^{*}F,J_{\alpha}^{*}G\}_{\mathrm{red}}.

Let
\pi_{\mathrm{red}}
be the Poisson tensor on
\mathcal{O}_{c}//_{\alpha}T
, and let
\pi_{\sharp}
be the Poisson tensor on
\mathfrak{g}//T
. Since

{(J_{\alpha})}_{*}\pi_{\mathrm{red}}=\left.\pi_{\sharp}\right|_{\mathrm{Im}\,J_{\alpha}}.

Hence,

is a symplectic submanifold of

. This can be seen as follows: for any

\displaystyle J(J_{\alpha}([u]_{\mathrm{red}}))=(c(u),u|_{\mathfrak{t}})=(c,\alpha).

. Thus, we have

, and each connected component of this fiber is a symplectic leaf in

.

Remark 3.40
.

If either
T
-action is not free or

is a stratified symplectic space in the sense of

.

3.18
, we have

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{g}//T=\dim\mathfrak{g}-r.

Fixing the

central coordinates
(C_{1},\dots,C_{r},\bar{\mu}_{1},\dots,\bar{\mu}_{r})
yields the following:

.

Assume that
c
and
\alpha
are regular, and the
T
-action is free in

. Then
\dim\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}=\dim\mathfrak{g}-3r
.

For a regular

,
\mathcal{O}_{c}
has a dimension

as the stabiliser is a Cartan of dimension

. Moreover, if

is a regular value of

, and the

.

Remark 3.42
.

(i) In irregular strata (e.g., non-maximum rank of
dJ
), the foliation stratifies further, and the dimensions of the leaves may decrease.

(ii) By Definition
2.1
, together with Theorem
3.39
, we immediately conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\mathfrak{g}//T\right)_{\mathrm{reg}}=\bigsqcup_{(c,\alpha)\in J\left(\left(\mathfrak{g}//T\right)_{\mathrm{reg}}\right)}\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}.

For a fixed
(c,\alpha)\in\left(\mathfrak{g}//G\right)\times\mathfrak{t}
with
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})
. Taking
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
to be the basis elements of
\mathfrak{t}
. Define the Poisson ideal

(3.42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{c,\alpha}:=\left\langle\rho^{*}(C_{1})-c_{1},\ldots,\rho^{*}(C_{r})-c_{r},\ \mu(H_{1})-\alpha(H_{1}),\ldots,\mu(H_{r})-\alpha(H_{r})\right\rangle

in
S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
. It is clear that
\mathcal{I}_{c,\alpha}
is a Poisson ideal, as it is generated by fixing the Casimir elements. Then by Theorem
3.39
, we could define the following fiber algebra
\mathcal{P}_{c,\alpha}=S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\big/\mathcal{I}_{c,\alpha}
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{P}_{c,\alpha}=\left(J^{-1}(c,\alpha)\right)_{\varpi}=\mathcal{L}_{c,\alpha}.

4.
Examples

In this Section
4
, we will present an example to describe the superintegrable system chain presented above. Consider that

(4.1)

\displaystyle G=SL_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad T=\left\{\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})\right\}.

Let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}=\mathrm{Lie}(T),\qquad H_{i}=E_{ii}-E_{i+1,i+1},\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1,

and the linear coordinate functions on
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted by
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1}
, where
E_{ij}
(
i\neq j
) is the elementary matrix. The symmetric algebra is then given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{C}\left[h_{1},\dots,h_{n-1},e_{ij}\mid i\neq j\right].

We now consider the torus case, where
A=T
. This case is mainly considered, and its algebraic construction is provided in
10
. Here, we will provide an alternative proof. For pairwise distinct indices
i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}
with
1\leq d\leq n
, define the cycle monomial

\displaystyle p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{d}}:=e_{i_{1}i_{2}}e_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots e_{i_{d-1}i_{d}}e_{i_{d}i_{1}}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
be the
T
-invariant polynomial Poisson subalgebra of
S(\mathfrak{g})
. Then

\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}\cong\mathbb{C}[h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1},p_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}:2\leq d\leq n]\big/\mathcal{I},

where
\mathcal{I}
is generated by the following relation

\displaystyle\prod_{u=1}^{k-1}p_{i_{u},i_{u+1}}\,p_{i_{k},i_{1}}

\displaystyle=p_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}\,p_{i_{1},i_{k},i_{k-1},\ldots,i_{2}},

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{i,j}

\displaystyle=p_{1,2,\ldots,n}\,p_{1,n-1,\ldots,2}\,\prod_{m=1}^{n-2}\ \prod_{s=m+2}^{n}p_{m,s},

\displaystyle\prod_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}\neq\cdots\neq i_{k}}p_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{r<s}p_{r,s}\right)^{\varphi(k)},\qquad\varphi(k)=\prod_{s=2}^{k-1}(n-s).

Proof.

Since
T
is connected, Proposition
3.5
implies that
S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}=S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{t}}
. Since
\mathfrak{t}
is the Cartan subalgebra of diagonal traceless matrices. Write

\displaystyle H=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in t,\qquad\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}=0,

such that the scalars
\lambda_{i}
are the diagonal entries of
H
. Recall that, for an elementary matrices
e_{ij}
(
i\neq j
), we have
[H,e_{ij}]=(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})e_{ij}
. Hence, by the PBW theorem, the monomial
p=\prod_{i\neq j}e_{ij}^{m_{ij}}\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
, then using the Leibniz rule, we have

\displaystyle\{H,p\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}m_{ij}\,\{H,e_{ij}\}\,e_{ij}^{m_{ij}-1}\prod_{(a,b)\neq(i,j)}e_{ab}^{m_{ab}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i\neq j}m_{ij}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\right)p=0.

It is convenient to regroup the coefficient of
M
as

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}m_{ij}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\Bigl(\underbrace{\sum_{j\neq k}m_{kj}}_{\text{out}(k)}-\underbrace{\sum_{i\neq k}m_{ik}}_{\text{in}(k)}\Bigr).

Since
H
varies over all diagonal traceless matrices, the scalars
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
are arbitrary, subject only to
\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}=0
. Therefore,
p\in S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
if and only if

(4.3)

\sum_{j\neq k}m_{kj}=\sum_{i\neq k}m_{ik}

for every
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Define the root lattices by
R=\bigoplus_{i\neq j}\mathbb{Z}\,\varepsilon_{ij}
. Define also a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map

\displaystyle\delta:R\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n},\quad\delta(\varepsilon_{ij})=e_{i}-e_{j}.

It is clear that
\delta
is a group homomorphism. Then

\displaystyle\delta\Bigl(\sum_{i\neq j}m_{ij}\varepsilon_{ij}\Bigr)=0

if and only if (
4.3
) holds. For pairwise distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{d}
(
d\geq 2
) define the cycle element by

\displaystyle c_{i_{1},\dots,i_{d}}:=\varepsilon_{i_{1}i_{2}}+\varepsilon_{i_{2}i_{3}}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{i_{d}i_{1}}\in R.

A direct telescoping computation gives
\delta(c_{i_{1},\dots,i_{d}})=0
, so
c_{i_{1},\dots,i_{d}}\in\ker(\delta)
.

We claim that any vector

m:=\sum_{i\neq j}m_{ij}\varepsilon_{ij}\in\ker(\delta)

with
m_{ij}\geq 0
is a sum of such cycle elements by induction on
|m|:=\sum_{i\neq j}m_{ij}
. If
|m|=0
, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, choose
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
with
m_{i_{1}i_{2}}>0
. Suppose
i_{r}
has been chosen. The
i_{r}
-th balance equation implies that the total outgoing multiplicity from
i_{r}
equals the total incoming multiplicity. Hence, in particular, there exists some
i_{r+1}\neq i_{r}
with
m_{i_{r}i_{r+1}}>0
. Continuing in this way produces a sequence
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},\dots
. Since
\{1,\dots,n\}
is finite, some index repeats:
i_{s}=i_{t}
with
s<t
. If the repeated segment contains further repetitions, delete subloops to obtain a subsequence with pairwise distinct indices. In either case, we obtain a cycle
(j_{1},\dots,j_{d})
with pairwise distinct entries such that each edge
(j_{r}\to j_{r+1})
(with
j_{d+1}=j_{1}
) occurs with positive multiplicity in
m
. Therefore,
m-c_{j_{1},\dots,j_{d}}
still has nonnegative coefficients and still lies in
\ker(\delta)
(since
\delta(c_{j_{1},\dots,j_{d}})=0
). By induction,
m-c_{j_{1},\dots,j_{d}}
is a sum of cycle elements, hence so is
m
.

Translating back, subtracting
c_{j_{1},\dots,j_{d}}
corresponds to factoring
M
by the cycle monomial
p_{j_{1},\dots,j_{d}}
. Thus, any
T
-invariant monomial
M
is a product of cycle monomials
p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{d}}
with pairwise distinct indices.

Since the action of
T
on
t
is trivial, every polynomial in
h_{1},\dots,h_{n-1}
is
T
-invariant. It follows that

S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
is generated by
h_{1},\dots,h_{n-1}
together with the cycle monomials.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We interpret the generators
p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{d}}=e_{i_{1}i_{2}}e_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots e_{i_{d-1}i_{d}}e_{i_{d}i_{1}}
as
loop
(or
cycle
) monomials: the ordered list
(i_{1}\,i_{2}\,\dots\,i_{d})
encodes the directed cycle
i_{1}\to i_{2}\to\cdots\to i_{d}\to i_{1}
.

A
subloop
of such a loop is any smaller directed cycle obtained from a proper subset of the vertices, i.e., a cycle
(j_{1}\,\dots\,j_{m})
with
2\leq m<d
and
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}\}\subset\{i_{1},\dots,i_{d}\}
for which the corresponding monomial
p_{j_{1},\dots,j_{m}}
is formed by the edges among these vertices. Equivalently, the presence of a subloop means that (after reordering commuting factors) the associated monomial is
decomposable
, i.e., it factorizes as a product of loop monomials of strictly smaller lengths.

The
T
-invariant subalgebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}
is then generated by the Cartan variables
h_{1},\dots,h_{n-1}
together with these loop monomials, modulo the algebraic relations among them, collected in the ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

We now have a direct application of Theorem
3.18
. Let

\displaystyle c_{k}(X):=\mathrm{tr}(X^{k}),\qquad 2\leq k\leq n.

Then
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=\mathbb{C}[c_{2},\dots,c_{n}]
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}=n-1,\qquad\mathrm{trdeg}\,S(\mathfrak{g})^{T}=(n^{2}-1)-(n-1)=n(n-1).

Hence, the chain
\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ \chi_{T}\ }\mathfrak{g}//T\xrightarrow{\ \rho\ }\mathfrak{g}//G
is superintegrable.

5.
Conclusion

In this work, we developed a Poisson-algebraic framework for constructing
A
-invariant functions in
S(\mathfrak{g})
and for describing the associated Poisson projection chain. The central point is that the relevant families of first integrals are organized by morphisms of affine Poisson varieties, so that superintegrability can be checked by intrinsic dimension identities along the chain rather than by model dependent computations. Concretely, we introduced the notion of a superintegrable Poisson chain, constructed Poisson subalgebras arising from reductive data, and made explicit the corresponding Poisson projections between quotients. This yields a uniform geometric explanation for why reductive chains produce superintegrable systems and recovers the previously known special cases within a single conceptual picture.

On the geometric side, the projection-chain viewpoint clarifies how invariant theory controls the induced foliations: the base algebra governs the Casimir directions, while the intermediate quotients encode the additional commuting or noncommuting integrals that generate the superintegrable distribution. In particular, the resulting description of symplectic leaves and their fibers provides a convenient starting point for comparing with classical constructions such as Thimm-type subalgebra chains and Mishchenko-Fomenko noncommutative integrability, and for extracting explicit generators (as illustrated in the
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
examples).

Several directions remain open. First, beyond the cases
A=T
and
A
Abelian, it would be interesting to identify reductive subgroups
A\subset G
(or more general
A
-stable Poisson subalgebras) for which the superintegrability dimension conditions fail and to characterize the failure in terms of the algebraic relations, the singular locus of the quotients, and the degeneration of generic fibers. Second, one can ask for global refinements: a description of the connected components of generic fibers, the completeness of the Hamiltonian flows produced by the chain, and the behaviour under restriction to real forms and compact forms.

Third, a natural continuation of the present work is quantization. One expects a parallel construction at the level of
U(\mathfrak{g})
(or of quantized coordinate rings) producing large commutative and/or Gelfand-Tsetlin type subalgebras, together with a precise comparison between their semiclassical limits and the Poisson projection chains constructed here. Finally, it would be interesting to extend the projection-chain formalism to other Lie-theoretic Poisson manifolds (e.g., coadjoint orbits, multiplicative Poisson-Lie settings, or moduli-type spaces) where similar reductive data should produce new superintegrable families. We plan to address these problems in future work.
