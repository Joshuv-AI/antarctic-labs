Constraints on millicharged particles from thunderstorms on the Solar system planets Preprint: INR-TH-2026-003
Ekaterina Dmitrieva, Petr Satunin

Abstract
We investigate the production of millicharged particles (mCPs) by the Schwinger mechanism in thunderstorms in the atmospheres of different planets in the Solar system. We study both fermion and scalar mCPs; for scalar mCPs, we take into account the effect of Bose enhancement. We use the observational data of planetary atmospheres obtained by satellite missions to establish constraints on the charge and mass of mCP particles. The best constraints came from the observation of thunderstorms in Saturn’s atmosphere: q > 10 − 11 q>10^{-11} for fermionic mCP and q > 10 − 24 q>10^{-24} for bosonic mCPs. These constraints are, to the best of our knowledge, the best in the literature.

III
Bose enhancement effect for scalar millicharged particles

For mCPs with Bose statistics, an additional enhancement factor may appear if the cloud structure consists of layers of clouds with opposite electric charges, which form several electrostatic potential wells (see Fig.
2
). Bosonic mCPs are captured in these wells, forming a Bose-Einstein condensate. The stimulated production of mCPs in the presence of the condensate grows exponentially with time
Klein and Rafelski 1978
.

The concentration of mCPs with a fixed wavenumber, or momentum
p
in the potential well — the mCP phase density — is governed by the Boltzmann equation,

\dot{f}_{p}=\Gamma^{\prime}_{p}\left(1+f_{p}\right),

(7)

which has an exponentially growing solution,

f_{p}=e^{\Gamma^{\prime}_{p}t}-1.

(8)

Here
\Gamma^{\prime}_{p}
is the rate for the collection of mCP produced by the Schwinger process in the potential well.

The momentum spectrum for scalar Schwinger production reads
Nikishov 1970
;
Cohen and McGady 2008
:

P=\frac{V}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int dp_{3}\int d^{2}p_{\perp}\log\left(1+e^{-\frac{\pi(m^{2}+p_{\perp}^{2})}{qE}}\right).

(9)

Here, the integration reads:

\int dp_{3}\to qET,\qquad\int d^{2}p_{\perp}e^{-\frac{\pi p_{\perp}^{2}}{qE}n}={\frac{qE}{\pi n}}.

(10)

The mCP collected to the potential well from the distance
L
so we can consider
T\sim L
. Finally, the rate
\Gamma^{\prime}_{p}
does not depend on
p
if
p<\sqrt{qE}
,

\Gamma^{\prime}_{p}=L^{-1}.

(11)

Since the considered volume is a cube
L^{3}
, all momenta should be larger than
L^{-1}
. As we see in (
9
), the minimal momenta play the role of mass in the exponent if
m<L^{-1}
. Thus, the Schwinger process is not suppressed if

q>\frac{\pi\,\mbox{max}(m^{2},L^{-2})}{E}.

(12)

The overall charge density of the mCP condensate accumulated in the potential well is as follows:

\rho_{\rm mc}\sim q\times\int d^{3}p\;f_{p}\sim q\,(qE)^{2}\left(e^{L^{-1}\;t}-1\right).

(13)

The exponential growth stops when the overall Coulomb charge of the mCP condensate becomes an essential part (
\sim 0.5
) of the charge of the thundercloud. A rough estimation for the mCP condensate charge reads
Q_{\rm mc}=\rho_{\rm mc}\times L^{3}
, while the charge in the thundercloud is
J^{\rm amp}_{\rm lightning}\times t_{\rm lightning}
. The discharge due to mCP condensate production should be faster than the pause between lightnings
t_{pause}
. Taking
t_{\rm pause}\sim 1
sec and
L\sim 1
km, in (
13

e^{3\times 10^{5}}
. Thus, if the necessary condition for the Schwinger effect (
12
) is satisfied and the potential well in the cloud structure really exists, the bosonic mCP is produced in a resonantly exponentially growing way.

Thus, the necessary condition (
12
) in the case of Bose enhancement is also sufficient. So, we establish the constraints on mCP parameters from the observation of thunderstorms on different planets from this equation.

V
Conclusion

In this letter, we set the constraints on the parameters of millicharged particles, both scalar or fermion, by the absence of supercritical Schwinger pair production of mCPs in thunderstorms in the atmospheres of Solar system planets. Although the thunderstorms on Earth are closer to us and have been studied much more, the thunderstorms on gas giants are significantly more intense and extensive, so the corresponding constraints have improved by several orders of magnitude.

The best constraints came from the thunderstorm in Saturn’s atmosphere,

\displaystyle q_{S}>10^{-11},\qquad\text{Fermi}

(23)

\displaystyle q_{S}>10^{-24},\qquad\text{Bose}.

(24)

These constraints are the best in the literature.

The constraints on bosonic mCPs are more than
10
orders of magnitude better than those on fermion mCPs. The reason is the Bose enhancement. It can be realized only in the case of a ladder structure of clouds that form an electrostatic well for mCPs which seems to really exist in Saturn atmosphere
Fischer et al. 2008
. We hope that a proper analysis of thunderstorms on Jupiter can further improve the constraints (
23
). The constraints from Titan, ice giants, and Venus are worse than those for gas giants, as expected.

The phenomenon investigated in this letter can be considered a providing example of how to use the physics of Solar system planets to probe some “new physics” hypotheses. For example, an investigation of the influence of axion-like-particles and dark photons may be an interesting task.
