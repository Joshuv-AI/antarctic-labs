Abstract
. A classical question in quantitative topology is to bound the mapping degree deg ⁡ ( f ) \operatorname{deg}(f) in terms of its Lipchitz constant Lip ⁡ ( f ) \operatorname{Lip}(f) . For a closed, orientable, Riemannian manifold M M , the flexible exponent α ⁡ ( M ) \alpha(M) is the infimum of α ⩾ 0 \alpha\geqslant 0 such that | deg ⁡ ( f ) | ⩽ C ⋅ ( Lip ⁡ ( f ) ) α |\deg(f)|\leqslant C\cdot(\operatorname{Lip}(f))^{\alpha} holds for any Lipschitz map f : M → M f:M\rightarrow M . For a geometric 3-manifold M M in the sense of Thurston, α ⁡ ( M ) \alpha(M) is determined in [ DLWWW ] . In this paper, we determine α ⁡ ( M ) \alpha(M) for non-geometric 3-manifolds.

1.
Introduction

We assume all manifolds in this paper are connected, compact, and orientable, unless otherwisely stated.

For a closed, orientable manifold
M
, we use
D(M)
to denote the set of degrees of self-maps of
M
. Following Gromov, a closed, orientable manifold
M
is called inflexible (or flexible) if and only if
D(M)
is finite (or infinite).
Inspired by a recent work of Berdnikov-Guth-Manin
[
BGM
]
, the flexible exponent
\alpha(M)
was defined in
[
DLWWW
]
, which is a numerical invariant of
M
. Roughly speaking, the flexible exponent measures how effectively a self-map wraps
M
to itself.

Let
M
and
N
be compact Riemannian manifolds.
For a map
f:M\to N
, we define the Lipschitz constant of
f
as

\operatorname{Lip}(f):=\sup_{u,v\in M}\frac{d_{N}(f(u),f(v))}{d_{M}(u,v)}\in[0,+\infty].

We say that
f
is an
L
-Lipschitz map for some positive number
L
if
\operatorname{Lip}(f)\leqslant L<+\infty
.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose
M
is a closed, orientable Riemannian
n
-manifold. The flexible exponent of
M
is defined as

\alpha(M):=\inf\{\alpha\geq 0\mid\exists C>0\text{ s.t. }|\operatorname{deg}(f)|\leq C\cdot(\operatorname{Lip}(f))^{\alpha}\text{ for all }f:M\to M\}.

It is known that
\alpha(M)\in[0,\dim M]
always holds, and
\alpha(M)>0
if and only if
M
is flexible, see
[
DLWWW
]
. For a simply-connected closed manifold
M
, it is known by
[
BGM
, Theorem A, Theorem D]
that
\alpha(M)=\dim M
if and only if
M
is formal.

For any closed orientable geometric 3-manifold
M
,
\alpha(M)
is determined in
[
DLWWW
]
, and a summary of results in
[
DLWWW
]
can be found below.
We will determine
\alpha(M)
for non-geometric 3-manifolds
M
in this paper.

By Kneser–Milnor’s prime decomposition theorem and Papakyriakopoulos’s sphere theorem, any closed orientable 3-manifold
M
other than
S^{3}
has a prime decomposition (unique up to orders and homeomorphisms)

M=(\#_{i=1}^{m}M_{i})\#(\#_{j=1}^{n}N_{j})\#(\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{1}),

where each
M_{i}
is aspherical, and each
N_{j}
has finite fundamental group.

There are eight homogeneous simply-connected complete Riemannian 3-manifolds:

\mathbb{H}^{3},\widetilde{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{E}^{1},Sol,Nil,\mathbb{E}^{3},S^{3},S^{2}\times\mathbb{E}^{1}.

We say that a closed orientable 3-manifold
M
is geometric, in the sense of Thurston, if it supports one of above eight geometries, and otherwise
M
is non-geometric.

In Thurston’s geometrization picture (confirmed by Perelman),
it is proved that a closed orientable 3-manifold
M
has infinite
D(M)
if and only if
M
belongs to one of the following topological families (see
[
Wang
, Corollary 4.3]
).

(i)

M
is covered by a torus bundle over the circle, or

(ii)

M
is covered by
\Sigma\times S^{1}
for some closed orientable surface
\Sigma
with genus
>1
, or

(iii)

each prime factor of
M
is covered by
S^{3}
or
S^{2}\times S^{1}
.

Therefore we have the following criterion.
A closed orientable 3-manifold
M
has positive flexible exponent if and only if
M
belongs to one of the following two geometric families.

•

M
admits either
S^{3},\ \mathbb{E}^{3},\ S^{2}\times\mathbb{E}^{1},\ \mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{E}^{1}
, Nil, or Sol geometry, or

•

M
is non-geometric, and each prime factor of
M
admits either
S^{3}
or
S^{2}\times\mathbb{E}^{1}
geometry.

For closed orientable geometric 3-manifolds, the value of
\alpha(M)
was computed in
[
DLWWW
]
:

Geometry of
M

S^{3},\ \mathbb{E}^{3},\ S^{2}\times\mathbb{E}^{1}

Nil

Sol

\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{E}^{1}

\mathbb{H}^{3},\widetilde{SL_{2}}

In this paper, we compute
\alpha(M)
for all closed, orientable, non-geometric 3-manifolds.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
M
is a closed, orientable, non-geometric 3-manifold.
Then

(1)

\alpha(M)=2
if
M
is a nontrivial connected sum, and each prime factor admits the geometry of

S^{3}
or
S^{2}\times\mathbb{E}^{1}
,

(2)

\alpha(M)=0
otherwise.

We only need to prove Theorem
1.2
(1), since the
3
-manifolds in (2) has finite
D(M)
, by
[
Wang
, Corollary 4.3]
.

In Section
2
, we review and prove some basic properties of the flexible exponent. In Section
3
, we prove that
\alpha(M)\leq 2
for
M
as in Theorem
1.2
(1). In Section
4
, we prove that
\alpha(M)\geq 2
for
M
as in Theorem
1.2
(1) based on Theorem
4.1
, which is a major technical result that constructs non-zero degree self-maps of
\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{1}
with controlled Lipschitz constants. Theorem
4.1
will be proved in Section
5
.
Certain delicate designs of graph packings in
\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{1}

make the construction of maps with controlled Lipschitz constants possible.

Up to some well-known facts, this paper is self-contained.

2.
The flexible exponent

We start with some basic properties of Lipschitz maps.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
,
Y
,
Z
be Riemannian manifolds.

(1)

If
f:X\rightarrow Y
and
g:Y\rightarrow Z
are Lipschitz maps, then

\operatorname{Lip}(g\circ f)\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\operatorname{Lip}(g).

(2)

For a differentiable map
f:X\rightarrow Y
, we use
df:TX\rightarrow TY
to denote the tangent map of
f
. We consider the operator norm

\|df\|:=\sup_{p\in X}\|df_{p}\|,\quad\text{where\hskip 10.22217pt}\|df_{p}\|:=\sup_{v\in T_{p}X,\ \|v\|=1}\|df_{p}(v)\|_{T_{f(p)}Y}.

Then we have

\operatorname{Lip}(f)=\|df\|.

(3)

If a map
f:X\to Y
is differentiable away from an embedded codimension-
1
subcomplex
Z\subset X
, then

\operatorname{Lip}(f)=\sup_{p\in X\setminus Z}\|df_{p}\|.

We will call a map
f:X\to Y
as in Proposition
2.1
(3) a piecewise smooth map.

The 3-manifold
M
that we consider in this paper do not have naturally associated geometries, since they are non-geometric.
To estimate
\alpha(M)
, we must put a Riemannian metric on
M
. The next lemma shows that
\alpha(M)
is independent of
the choice of Riemannian metrics.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose two closed connected orientable Riemannian
n
-manifolds
M_{1},\ M_{2}
are homotopy equivalent to each other, then
\alpha(M_{1})=\alpha(M_{2})
.

Another description of
\alpha(M)
makes the proof of Lemma
2.2
more directly.
For a closed orientable Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
and number
L>0
, define

P_{M}(L):=\sup\{|\deg(f)|\mid f:M\rightarrow M\text{ is a Lipschitz map with }\operatorname{Lip}(f)\leqslant L\}.

It is clear that
P_{M}
is a non-negative function and is also non-decreasing, and

\alpha(M)=\inf\{\alpha\geqslant 0\mid P_{M}(L)\leqslant C\cdot L^{\alpha}\text{ for some $C>0$}\}.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Let
f_{1}:M_{1}\to M_{2}
and
f_{2}:M_{2}\to M_{1}
be maps realizing the homotopy equivalence.
By smooth approximation, we can assume that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are smooth maps of degree
\pm 1
.
Since
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
are compact, we have

\operatorname{Lip}(f_{1}),\operatorname{Lip}(f_{2})\leq C

for some constant
C>0
.

Claim:

P_{M_{1}}(L)\leq P_{M_{2}}(C^{2}L)
.

Proof: By definition, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a map
f:M_{1}\to M_{1}
such that

\operatorname{Lip}(f)\leq L,\,\,|\deg(f)|>P_{M_{1}}(L)-\epsilon.

Let
f^{\prime}=f_{1}\circ f\circ f_{2}:M_{2}\to M_{2}
. Then it is easy to see that

\operatorname{Lip}(f^{\prime})\leqslant\operatorname{Lip}(f_{1})\cdot\operatorname{Lip}(f)\cdot\operatorname{Lip}(f_{2})\leq C^{2}L,\quad|\deg(f)|=|\deg(f^{\prime})|.

Then the Claim follows by

P_{M_{1}}(L)-\epsilon<|\deg(f)|=|\deg(f^{\prime})|\leq P_{M_{2}}(\operatorname{Lip}(f^{\prime}))\leq P_{M_{2}}(C^{2}L).

The Claim implies that
\alpha(M_{1})\leq\alpha(M_{2})
.
By symmetry,
\alpha(M_{1})\geq\alpha(M_{2})
also holds. So we have
\alpha(M_{1})=\alpha(M_{2})
.
∎

Now we state several facts as lemmas below. The first two will be used to prove
\alpha(M)\leq 2
, and the third will be used to
prove
\alpha(M)\geq 2
, for
M
in Theorem
1.2
(1).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p:\tilde{M}\rightarrow M
be a finite cover between closed orientable manifolds. Suppose that any non-zero degree self-map
f:M\rightarrow M
can be lifted to
\tilde{f}:\tilde{M}\rightarrow\tilde{M}
, then
\alpha(M)\leqslant\alpha(\tilde{M})
.

Proof.

Fix a Riemannian metric for
M
and let
\tilde{g}
be the pull-back Riemannian metric on
\tilde{M}
by the covering map. Then for any non-zero degree self-map
f:M\rightarrow M
and its lift
\tilde{f}:\tilde{M}\rightarrow\tilde{M}
we have

\operatorname{Lip}(f)=\operatorname{Lip}(\tilde{f}),\quad\deg(f)=\deg(\tilde{f}).

Then it follows from the definition that
\alpha(M)\leqslant\alpha(\tilde{M})
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
M
is a closed, orientable, differentiable
n
-manifold, and we fix a Riemannian metric on
M
. Then there exists a positive constant
C>0
, such that for any non-zero degree differentiable self-map
f:M\rightarrow M
, the following statements hold.

(1)

If the
k
-th betti number
\beta_{k}:=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H_{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
is positive for some integer
k
and let
f_{*}:H_{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H_{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
be the induced linear map, then we have

|\det(f_{*})|\leqslant C\cdot(\operatorname{Lip}(f))^{k\cdot\beta_{k}},

where
\det(f_{*})
is the determinant of
f_{*}
.

(2)

Moreover, if
f
induces an isomorphism on
H_{k}(M;\mathbb{Z})/\operatorname{Tor}
, then we have

|\deg(f)|\leqslant C\cdot(\operatorname{Lip}(f))^{n-k}.

In particular,
\alpha(M)\leqslant n-k
holds.

Proof.

We fix a fundamental classes
[M]\in H_{n}(M;\mathbb{Z})
. The cup product induces a non-degenerate bilinear pairing

x(u,v):=\langle u\smile v,[M]\rangle,\quad u\in H^{k}(M;\mathbb{Q}),\ v\in H^{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Q})

on the rational cohomology groups. By Poincaré duality and the universal coefficient theorem, the vector spaces
H^{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
have the same
\mathbb{Q}
-dimension, which equals
\beta_{k}
. We choose a rational basis
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\beta_{k}}
for
H^{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
and let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{\beta_{k}}
be the dual basis for
H^{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
, such that

x(\eta_{i},\omega_{j})=\delta_{ij},\quad 1\leqslant i,j\leqslant\beta_{k}.

In the following, we use the same notation to denote a de Rham cohomology class and a closed differentiable form representative.

By Thom’s realization theorem
[
Thom
]
, for any
j
, there exists a closed, oriented, immersed
k
-dimensional submanifold
X_{j}
(possibly disconnected) of
M
and an integer
n_{j}
, such that
\frac{1}{n_{j}}[X_{j}]
is Poincaré dual to
\omega_{j}
. (We need
\omega_{j}\in H^{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
to guarantee the existence of
X_{j}
.) Then pairings with
\omega_{j}
can be thought of as integrations on these submanifolds, i.e. for any cohomology class
u\in H^{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
, we have

x(u,\omega_{j})=\langle u\smile\omega_{j},[M]\rangle=\frac{1}{n_{j}}\int_{X_{j}}u.

Under the bases
\{\eta_{i}\}
and
\{\omega_{j}\}
,
let
A_{1}:H^{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
and
A_{2}:H^{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{n-k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
be the matrices of the linear maps induced by
f
. Then there is an upper bound for the entries of
A_{1}
:

|(A_{1})_{i,j}|=|x(f^{*}\eta_{i},\omega_{j})|=\Big|\frac{1}{n_{j}}\int_{X_{j}}f^{*}\eta_{i}\Big|\leqslant(\operatorname{Lip}(f))^{k}\cdot\frac{1}{n_{j}}\int_{X_{j}}\|\eta_{i}\|\ d\text{vol}_{k}\leq C\cdot(\text{Lip}(f))^{k}.

Here
\|\eta\|
denotes the norm of a differential form induced by the Riemannian metric on
M
, and
d\text{vol}_{k}
donotes the
k
-dimensional volume form on
X_{j}
induced by the Riemannian metric. The constant
C>0
is the maximum of

\{\frac{1}{n_{j}}\int_{X_{j}}\|\eta_{i}\|d\text{vol}_{k}\ |\ i,j=1,\cdots,\beta_{k}\},

, which depends on the Riemannian metric, the forms
\eta_{i}
, the
k
-dimensional submanifolds
X_{j}
, and the integers
n_{j}
. It is important to note that
C
is independent of the map
f
.

By the universal coefficient theorem, the matrix representing
f_{*}:H_{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})\to H_{k}(M;\mathbb{Q})
is the transpose of
A_{1}
and hence we have

|\det(f_{*})|=|\det(A_{1})|\leqslant\beta!\cdot C^{\beta_{k}}\cdot(\operatorname{Lip}(f))^{k\cdot\beta_{k}}.

Up to replacing
\beta_{k}!\cdot C^{\beta_{k}}
by
C
, this proves the first statement.

For the second statement, the same proof also applies to
A_{2}
, so we have
|\det(A_{2})|\leqslant\beta!\cdot C^{\beta_{k}}\cdot(\operatorname{Lip}(f))^{{(n-k)}\cdot\beta_{k}}
(for a possibly different constant
C
independent of
f
). If
f_{*}
induces an isomorphism on
H_{k}(M;\mathbb{Z})/\operatorname{Tor}
, then
\det(A_{1})=\pm 1
.
It is clear that
x(f^{*}(u),f^{*}(v))=\pm\deg(f)\cdot x(u,v)
, which implies that
A_{1}^{t}\cdot A_{2}=\pm\deg(f)\cdot I
and

\det(A_{1})\cdot\det(A_{2})=\pm(\deg(f))^{\beta_{k}}.

So we have

|\deg(f)|=|\det(A_{2})|^{1/\beta_{k}}\leqslant(\beta!)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}}\cdot C\cdot(\operatorname{Lip}(f))^{n-k}.

Up to changing the constant, we have
|\deg(f)|\leq C\cdot(\text{Lip}(f))^{n-k}
.

By a result attributed to Shoen and Uhlenbeck (see the introductions of
[
Be
,
Ha
]
, with
W^{1,p}=W^{1,\infty}
), any Lipschitz map between Riemannian manifolds can be approximated by differentiable maps, with arbitrarily close Lipschitz constants. So the above argument for differentiable maps implies that
\alpha(M)\leq n-k
holds.
∎

3.
An upper bound of flexible exponents of non-geometric 3-manifolds

By the discussion in the introduction, Theorem
1.2
(2) follows from
[
Wang
, Corollary 4.3]
. So it remains to prove Theorem
1.2
(1), which is restated as the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
M
is a closed, orientable, non-geometric 3-manifold and

M=(\#^{m}S^{2}\times S^{1})\#(\#_{i=1}^{l}P_{i}),

where each
P_{i}
is covered by
S^{3}
and
|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|>1
.
Then
\alpha(M)=2
.

We will prove that
\alpha(M)\leq 2
in this section, and will prove
\alpha(M)\geq 2
in the next two sections. We first give a more explicit description of the
3
-manifold
M
in Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Any closed, orientable, non-geometric
3
-manifold

M=(\#^{m}S^{2}\times S^{1})\#(\#_{i=1}^{l}P_{i})

in Theorem
3.1
belongs to one of the following two families:

(1)

M=\#^{m}S^{2}\times S^{1}
with
m\geq 2
,

(2)

M=(\#^{m}S^{2}\times S^{1})\#(\#_{i=1}^{l}P_{i})
such that
l\geq 1
,
m+l\geq 2
, and
M\neq\mathbb{R}P^{3}\#\mathbb{R}P^{3}
.

Proof.

If
M
is a connected sum of
m
copies of
S^{2}\times S^{1}
, since
\#^{0}S^{2}\times S^{1}=S^{3}
and
\#^{1}S^{2}\times S^{1}=S^{2}\times S^{1}
are geometric, we must have
m\geq 2
. So
M
belongs to case (1).

For an
M
not in case (1), we have
l\geq 1
. If
l=1
, then we must have
m\geq 1
, otherwise
M=P_{1}
is geometric. So we have
m+l\geq 2
. Moreover,
\mathbb{R}P^{3}\#\mathbb{R}P^{3}
is geometric since it is doubly covered by
S^{2}\times S^{1}
. So any
M
in Theorem
3.1
and not in case (1) belongs to case (2).
∎

For any
3
-manifold
M
in Lemma
3.2
(2), we want to construct a finite cover that is homeomorphic to
\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{1}
with
k\geq 2
. This follows from the fact that
M
is a non-geometric
3
-manifold, but we give a more direct proof here.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
M
is a 3-manifold and

M=(\#^{m}S^{2}\times S^{1})\#(\#_{i=1}^{l}P_{i}).

where each
P_{i}
is covered by
S^{3}
and
|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|>1
. We also assume that
l\geq 1
,
m+l\geq 2
, and
M\neq\mathbb{R}P^{3}\#\mathbb{R}P^{3}
. Then
M
has a finite regular cover
M^{\prime}
that is homeomorphic to
\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{1}
with
k\geq 2
.

Proof.

We have

\pi_{1}(M)=(*^{m}\mathbb{Z})*(*_{i=1}^{l}\pi_{1}(P_{i})),

and we consider the group homomorphism

\Phi:\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\pi_{1}(P_{1})\times\cdots\times\pi_{1}(P_{l})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(P_{1})\times\cdots\times\pi_{1}(P_{l}),

where
\Phi
is the composition of two obvious homomorphisms. Note that the restriction of
\Phi
sends
\pi_{1}(P_{i})<\pi_{1}(M)
to
\pi_{1}(P_{i})<\pi_{1}(P_{1})\times\cdots\times\pi_{1}(P_{l})
by identity.

The kernel
H:=\ker\Phi
is a finite index normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
. By the Kurosh Theorem in group theory (
[
ScW
]
), we have

H\cong(*^{k}\mathbb{Z})*(*_{j=1}^{a}C_{j}),

where each
C_{j}
conjugates to some
C<\pi_{1}(P_{i})<\pi_{1}(M)
for some
i
. Since
C<\ker\Phi
and
\Phi|:\pi_{1}(P_{i})\to\pi_{1}(P_{i})
is the identity,
C
must be the trivial group and so is
C_{j}
. We conclude that
H
is a free group of rank
k<\infty
, and we want to check
k\geq 2
.

Let
\pi:M^{\prime}\to M
be the finite regular cover of
M
corresponding to
H<\pi_{1}(M)
, then
M^{\prime}
is homeomorphic to
\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{1}
. Let
D=\text{deg}(\pi)=\Pi_{i=1}^{l}|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|
. Since
l\geq 1
and
|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|\geq 2
,
D\geq 2
holds. We consider
M
as a union of
m
copies of
S^{2}\times S^{1}\setminus\text{int}(B^{3})
, one
P_{i}\setminus\text{int}(B^{3})
for each
i=1,\cdots,l
, and one
S^{3}\setminus\bigcup^{m+l}\text{int}(B^{3})
, pasting along
(m+l)
copies of
S^{2}
.

In
M^{\prime}
, each
S^{2}\times S^{1}\setminus\text{int}(B^{3})
lifts to
D
copies of
S^{2}\times S^{1}\setminus\text{int}(B^{3})
, each
P_{i}\setminus\text{int}(B^{3})
lifts to
\frac{D}{|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|}
copies of
S^{3}\setminus\bigcup^{|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|}\text{int}(B^{3})
, the
S^{3}\setminus\bigcup^{m+n}\text{int}(B^{3})
lifts to
D
copies of
S^{3}\setminus\bigcup^{m+n}\text{int}(B^{3})
, pasting along
D(m+l)
copies of
S^{2}
. The corresponding dual graph of
M^{\prime}
has

(m+1)D+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{D}{|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|}

vertices and
D(m+l)
edges, with
Dm
vertices correspond to punctured
S^{2}\times S^{1}
and remaining vertices correspond to punctured
S^{3}
.

The Euler characteristic of this graph is
(1-l)D+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{D}{|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|}
, so we have

(1)

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=Dm+\Big[1-(1-l)D-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{D}{|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|}\Big]=1+D(m-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}D(1-\frac{1}{|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|}).

If
m\geq 1
, since
l\geq 1
,
D\geq 2
, and
|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|\geq 2
, we have

k\geq 1+\sum_{i=1}^{l}D(1-\frac{1}{|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|})\geq 1+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{1}{2}D=1+\frac{D}{2}l\geq 2.

If
m=0
, then
l\geq 2
, and we have

k\geq 1-D+\sum_{i=1}^{l}D(1-\frac{1}{|\pi_{1}(P_{i})|})\geq 1-D+\frac{D}{2}l\geq 1.

The equality holds if and only if
l=2
and
|\pi_{1}(P_{1})|=|\pi_{1}(P_{2})|=2
, which correspond to the case of
M=\mathbb{R}P^{3}\#\mathbb{R}P^{3}
. Since
\mathbb{R}P^{3}\#\mathbb{R}P^{3}
is excluded in this lemma and
k
is an integer,
k\geq 2
still holds. The proof of this lemma is done.

∎

.

where each

Then
\alpha(M)\leq 2

Proof.

must induce an isomorphism on the fundamental group, hence an isomorphism on the first homology. By Lemma

h_{1}:(M_{k},H_{k},H_{k,n}(G))\to(M_{k},H_{k},H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}))
.

(2)

By Lemma
5.5
, for any
i,j\in[-n,n]\cap\mathbb{Z}
, we have a
C_{2}
-bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism
h_{2,i,j}:(M_{k},H_{k},H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}))\to(M_{k},H_{k},H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j}))
.

(3)

By Lemma
5.6
, we have a
(C_{3}n)
-Lipschitz homeomorphism
h_{3}:(M_{k},H_{k},H_{k,n}(G))\to(M_{k},H_{k},H_{k}(2))
such that
h_{3}^{-1}
is
C_{3}
-Lipschitz.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Roughly speaking, the map
F_{n}
will restricts to the identity on the complement of
\cup_{i,j=-n}^{n}H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})
, and it maps each
H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})
to its complement via a piecewise smooth orientation-reversing homeomorphism, with controlled Lipschitz constant. Since we have
(2n+1)^{2}
terms in the union
\cup_{i,j=-n}^{n}H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})
, we have
\text{deg}(F_{n})=1-(2n+1)^{2}
.

Now we give details of the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
For
M_{k}=\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{1}
, its standard genus-
k
Heegaard decomposition
M_{k}=H\cup H^{\prime}
is obtained by taking two copies of the genus-
k
handlebody and using the identity map to paste their boundaries. We take a Riemannian metric
g_{0}
on
M
such that its restriction to
H
is isometric to
H_{k}
. By Lemma
5.1
, we only need to prove Theorem
4.1
for
g_{0}
.

Once we identify
H\subset M_{k}
with the Riemannian genus-
k
handlebody
H_{k}
, we get a submanifold
H_{k}(2)\subset H_{k}\subset M
. Since
H_{k}\setminus\text{int}(H_{k}(2))
is homeomorphic to
\Sigma_{k}\times I
,
M_{k}=H_{k}(2)\cup(M_{k}\setminus\text{int}(H_{k}(2)))
is also a standard genus-
k
Heegaard decomposition of
M_{k}
. So there exists an orientation-reversing homeomorphism
h_{4}:M_{k}\to M_{k}
that swaps the two components of
M_{k}\setminus\partial(H_{k}(2))
and restricts to the identity on
\partial H_{k}(2)
. We can make
h_{4}
to be a pieceswise smooth bi-Lipschitz homeomophism, with bi-Lipschitz constant
C_{4}>1
, which only depends on the Riemannian metric on
M_{k}
.

For any positive integer
n
, we define the desired map
F_{n}:M_{k}\to M_{k}
by

\displaystyle F_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if\ }x\in M_{k}\setminus\cup_{i,j=-n}^{n}\text{int}(H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j}))\\
h_{2,i,j}\circ h_{1}\circ h_{3}^{-1}\circ h_{4}\circ h_{3}\circ h_{1}^{-1}\circ h_{2,i,j}^{-1}(x)&\text{if\ }x\in H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})\text{\ for\ some\ }i,j\in[-n,n]\cap\mathbb{Z}.\end{cases}

In the second case of the formula of
F_{n}(x)
, the composition works as the sequence below.

\displaystyle H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})\xrightarrow{h_{2,i,j}^{-1}}H_{k,n}(G^{\prime})\xrightarrow{h_{1}^{-1}}H_{k,n}(G)\xrightarrow{h_{3}}H_{k}(2)\xrightarrow{h_{4}}M_{k}\setminus\text{int}(H_{k}(2))\xrightarrow{h_{3}^{-1}}

\displaystyle M_{k}\setminus\text{int}(H_{k,n}(G))\xrightarrow{h_{1}}M_{k}\setminus\text{int}(H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}))\xrightarrow{h_{2,i,j}}M_{k}\setminus\text{int}(H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})).

This composition is an orientation-reversing homeomorphism
H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})\to M_{k}\setminus\text{int}(H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j}))
and we denote it by
F_{n,i,j}
.

Here we need the fact that
H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})
’s are disjoint from each other to define
F_{n}
. Since
h_{4}
restricts to the identity on
\partial H_{k}(2)
, the restriction of
F_{n,i,j}
to
\partial H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})
is the identity. So
F_{n}
is well-defined.

To compute
\text{deg}(F_{n})
, we take any
x_{0}\in M_{k}\setminus\cup_{i,j=-n}^{n}H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j})
. Then
F_{n}^{-1}(x_{0})=\{x_{0}\}\cup\{F_{n,i,j}^{-1}(x_{0})\ |\ i,j\in[-n,n]\cap\mathbb{Z}\}
. For
F_{n}
, the local degree at
x_{0}
is
1
, and the local degree at any
F_{n,i,j}^{-1}(x_{0})
is
-1
, since
F_{n,i,j}
is an orientation reversing homeomorphism. Since
|[-n,n]\cap\mathbb{Z}|=2n+1
, we have
\text{deg}(F_{n})=1-(2n+1)^{2}=-4n^{2}-4n
, thus item (1) of Theorem
4.1
holds.

By our estimates of bi-Lipschitz constants and the definition of
F_{n}
,
F_{n}
is a Lipschitz map with Lipschitz constant
C_{2}\cdot C_{1}\cdot(C_{3}n)\cdot C_{4}\cdot C_{3}\cdot C_{1}\cdot C_{2}=C_{1}^{2}C_{2}^{2}C_{3}^{2}C_{4}n
. We take
C=C_{1}^{2}C_{2}^{2}C_{3}^{2}C_{4}
, then item (2) of Theorem
4.1
holds.

Since
H^{\prime}\subset M_{k}\setminus\cup_{i,j=-n}^{n}\text{int}(H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j}))
and
F_{n}
restricts to the identity on
M_{k}\setminus\cup_{i,j=-n}^{n}\text{int}(H_{k,n}(G^{\prime}_{i,j}))
, we have
F_{n}|_{H^{\prime}}=id_{H^{\prime}}
. So item (3) of Theorem
4.1
holds.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
is done.

∎
