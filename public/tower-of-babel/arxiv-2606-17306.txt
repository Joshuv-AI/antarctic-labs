Robust Beamforming Design for Secure Uplink NOMA-ISAC
Azadeh Tabeshnezhad, Milad Tatar Mamaghani, A. Lee Swindlehurst, Tommy Svensson, Erik Ström

Abstract
Integrated sensing and communication is an important technology for sixth-generation (6G) mobile networks, enabling the joint use of communication and radar sensing within a unified system. While offering significant benefits in terms of spectral efficiency, ISAC introduces new security challenges. In particular, the joint use of resources for sensing and communication can increase vulnerability to eavesdropping and information leakage. In this paper, we study an uplink Non-Orthogonal Multiple Access (NOMA) system where the base station (BS) simultaneously receives user data and senses a potential eavesdropper (Eve) with uncertain location. To enhance the physical-layer security, a robust sensing signal is designed to both sense and jam Eve. We formulate a joint optimization problem that aims to maximize the users’ sum rate and the BS sensing performance while maintaining security against Eve. Since the resulting optimization problem is non-convex, we develop an iterative alternating optimization (AO) algorithm that decomposes it into two tractable subproblems. In the first subproblem, the receive combining vectors are optimized in closed form using generalized eigenvalue decomposition. In the second subproblem, the transmit beamforming matrices and sensing power are jointly optimized via semidefinite relaxation (SDR) and successive convex approximation (SCA). Simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness of our solution in terms of fast convergence and resource allocation.

Introduction

The next generation of mobile communication networks is expected to support a diverse range of mission-critical applications, including autonomous systems, industrial automation, and connected smart societal infrastructure. These emerging services demand not only ultra-reliable and low-latency communications but also high-precision environmental awareness and stringent digital security.
Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)
has emerged as a promising technology for

However, ISAC also introduces new design challenges, particularly in managing the interference between communication and sensing signals, and safeguarding such systems against malicious actors.

Concurrently,

, distinguishing multiple users by their signal power and thus enabling them to simultaneously occupy the same time, frequency, and spatial resources. NOMA also opens new opportunities for interference-resilient and resource-constrained ISAC system design by providing new degrees of freedom for designing robust and secure waveforms against eavesdroppers in shared-spectrum environments.

I-B

Our Contributions

We consider a novel scenario in which an ISAC system employing uplink
PD-NOMA
must also provide security against an eavesdropping target with an imprecisely known location. Unlike prior work that considers imperfect CSI or sensing-parameter estimation, we incorporate knowledge of the estimation-theoretic uncertainty into the beamforming design. In particular, we introduce a CRB-informed design framework to capture the impact of parameter estimation errors on communication, sensing, and secrecy performance. Our detailed contributions are as follows:

•

We develop an uncertainty-aware model for an uplink NOMA-ISAC system with an eavesdropping target, and characterize how sensing-estimation errors for the target’s angle and delay parameters propagate and affect the sensing and system security. We then derive tractable SINR approximations for the communication, sensing, and eavesdropping performance metrics.

•

We formulate a joint beamforming and sensing-power optimization problem that maximizes a weighted communication–sensing utility while enforcing security and power budget constraints. To tackle the problem, we propose an alternating optimization algorithm that admits a computationally efficient iterative solution, and we provide a convergence and complexity analysis.

•

We demonstrate via simulations that the proposed robust-secure design keeps the eavesdropper SINR below a prescribed threshold under location uncertainty while maintaining adequate sensing and communication performance relative to ideal and non-secure baselines.

\Delta\tau_{u_{k}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\tau})
and
\Delta{\theta_{u_{k}}}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\theta})
.

In the following, we obtain a fundamental lower bound for the variances
\sigma^{2}_{\tau}
and
\sigma^{2}_{\theta}
by means of the
CRB
, which is used to evaluate the received SINR performance at Eve under such realistic channels.

III
Uncertainty-aware Performance Analysis

To capture the impact of uncertainty in the sensing parameters on system performance, this section develops a unified framework that models the radar parameter estimation bounds and analyzes the impact on the assumed communication channels and sensing signal
{\bf x}
. This uncertainty is rooted in the imperfect knowledge of Eve’s location, specifically in her angle of departure
\theta_{b}
and delay
\tau_{b}
.

III-A

Estimation Performance via CRB

In the sensing subsystem, we are interested in characterizing the CRB for estimating these parameters:

\boldsymbol{\zeta}=[\theta_{b},\tau_{b},\beta_{r},\beta_{i}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 1},

where
\beta_{0}=\beta_{r}+j\beta_{i}
denotes the complex

of target Eve. To derive the CRB for estimating

\displaystyle{\bf Y}_{\text{b,s}}=\beta_{0}{\bf A}(\theta_{b}){\bf X}+{\bf H}_{\text{\tiny SI}}\widetilde{\bf X}+{\bf N}_{\text{b}},

We vectorize the sensing signal

\displaystyle{\bf y}_{\text{b,s}}

(38)

The derivatives of

III-B

Received SINR at Eve

To evaluate the SINR at Eve, we consider the following cases from the SIC perspective:

•

Case 1 (No SIC): When signals from both UEs are either strong or weak, Eve cannot exploit SIC to efficiently decode the messages. In this case, Eve will try to decode each UE signal in the presence of interference from the other UE signal. Thus, the SINR at Eve to decode the
k
th UE is given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{e,k}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{k}}^{H}\tilde{\bf w}_{k}|^{2}\}}{\sum_{m\neq k}\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{m}}^{H}\tilde{\bf w}_{m}|^{2}\}+\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{b}^{H}{\bf x}|^{2}\}+\sigma^{2}_{e}}.

(42)

•

Case 2 (With SIC): If one signal is significantly stronger than the other, Eve applies SIC to decode the dominant signal first, denoted by UE
k
. Then, the SINR for decoding the weaker signal UE
m
after canceling the dominant signal is given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{e,m}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{m}}^{H}\tilde{\bf w}_{m}|^{2}\}}{\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{b}^{H}{\bf x}|^{2}\}+\sigma^{2}_{e}},\qquad m,k\in\{1,2\},\;m\neq k.

(43)

For the Eve decoding analysis, with no loss of generality, we assume that the received signal power of UE
2
at Eve is stronger than that of UE
1
, such that
|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{2}}^{H}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{2}|^{2}\geq\lambda_{\text{\tiny SIC}}|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{1}}^{H}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{1}|^{2}
holds, where
\lambda_{\text{\tiny SIC}}\geq 1
is a predefined power-ordering factor for SIC at Eve. This assumption reflects Case 2, where Eve is capable of potentially decoding the dominant signal from UE
2
first, then attempting to recover UE
1
’s message via SIC operation.
To limit the information leakage to Eve at any stage of decoding, we consider the maximum effective SINR across Eve’s SIC decoding stages, given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{e}

\displaystyle=\max\Bigg\{\frac{\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{2}}^{H}\tilde{\bf w}_{2}|^{2}\}}{\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{1}}^{H}\tilde{\bf w}_{1}|^{2}\}+\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{b}^{H}{\bf x}|^{2}\}+\sigma^{2}_{e}},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{1}}^{H}\tilde{\bf w}_{1}|^{2}\}}{\mathbb{E}\{|\tilde{{\bf g}}_{b}^{H}{\bf x}|^{2}\}+\sigma^{2}_{e}}\Bigg\}.

(44)

A closed-form analytical expression for
\gamma_{e}
is presented in the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Accounting for the uncertainty in her location, the received SINR at Eve is approximately

\displaystyle\boxed{\gamma_{e}\approx\max\left\{\frac{\tilde{\bf w}_{2}^{H}{\bf G}_{u_{2}}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{2}}{\tilde{\bf w}_{1}^{H}{\bf G}_{u_{1}}\tilde{\bf w}_{1}+p_{s}q_{b}+\sigma^{2}_{e}},\frac{\tilde{\bf w}_{1}^{H}{\bf G}_{u_{1}}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{1}}{p_{s}q_{b}+\sigma^{2}_{e}}\right\},}

(45)

where

\displaystyle q_{b}

\displaystyle=\big(\alpha_{1}(\hat{\tau}_{b})^{2}\|{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{b})\|^{4}+\sigma^{2}_{\tau}\alpha_{\tau_{b}}^{\prime 2}\|{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{b})\|^{4}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma_{(\tau,\theta)}\alpha_{\tau_{b}}^{\prime}\alpha_{1}(\hat{\tau}_{b})^{H}{\bf a}_{\theta_{b}}^{\prime H}{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{b})\|{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{b})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma_{(\theta,\tau)}\alpha_{\tau_{b}}^{\prime H}\alpha_{1}(\hat{\tau}_{b}){\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{b})^{H}{\bf a}_{\theta_{b}}^{\prime}\|{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{b})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma^{2}_{\theta}\alpha_{1}(\hat{\tau}_{b})^{2}|{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{b})^{H}{\bf a}^{\prime}_{\theta_{b}}|^{2}\big)

(46)

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{u_{k}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\!\left\{\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{k}}\tilde{{\bf g}}_{u_{k}}^{H}\right\}

\displaystyle\approx\alpha_{1}(\hat{\tau}_{u_{k}})^{2}{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{u_{k}}){\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{u_{k}})^{H}+\sigma^{2}_{\theta}\alpha_{1}(\hat{\tau}_{u_{k}})^{2}{\bf a}^{\prime}_{\theta_{u}}{\bf a}^{\prime H}_{\theta_{u}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma_{(\tau,\theta)}\alpha_{1}(\hat{\tau}_{u_{k}})\alpha^{\prime}_{\tau_{u}}{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{u_{k}}){\bf a}^{\prime H}_{\theta_{u}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma_{(\tau,\theta)}\alpha^{\prime}_{\tau_{u}}\alpha_{1}(\hat{\tau}_{u_{k}}){\bf a}^{\prime}_{\theta_{u}}{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{u_{k}})^{H}

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma^{2}_{\tau}\alpha^{\prime 2}_{\tau_{u}}{\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{u_{k}}){\bf a}(\hat{\theta}_{u_{k}})^{H}

(47)

IV
Joint Optimization Framework for Secure ISAC-NOMA

In this section, we design a secure uplink transmission strategy for the considered ISAC-NOMA system by jointly optimizing beamforming and power allocation. Our goal is to maximize the total achievable communication rate and sensing performance while safeguarding the uplink transmissions against eavesdropping. To this end, we first formulate the resource management challenge as a non-convex optimization problem to improve communication and sensing performance, given transmit power budget and information leakage constraints. We then propose an efficient iterative algorithm to tackle it.

IV-A

Problem Formulation

We consider the following utility function

\displaystyle U(\gamma_{k},\gamma_{s};a_{k},a_{s})=\sum_{k=1}^{2}a_{k}\log_{2}(1\!+\!\gamma_{k})+a_{s}\log_{2}(1\!+\!\gamma_{s})

(48)

where the coefficients
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are scaling factors that determine the importance of the individual UE data rates (i.e., the uplink communication performance), and
a_{s}\geq 0
weights the importance of the sensing criteria. If
a_{s}\gg a_{k}
, then the sensing performance becomes our priority; otherwise, when
a_{s}=0
, only communication performance is considered regardless of information leakage.
We formulate the joint
optimization problem (OP)
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0}:\penalty\ \max_{\{\tilde{\bf w}_{k},{\bf v}_{k},{\bf v}_{s},p_{s}\}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2}a_{k}\log_{2}(1\!+\!\gamma_{k})+a_{s}\log_{2}(1\!+\!\gamma_{s})

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbf{C1}:

\displaystyle\quad\|\tilde{\bf w}_{k}\|^{2}\leq P^{\mathrm{max}}_{u},\quad k=1,2

(49a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C2}:

\displaystyle\quad p_{s}\geq 0,

(49b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C3}:

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\{\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\}\leq P^{\mathrm{max}}_{s},

(49c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C4}:

\displaystyle\quad\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{th}},

(49d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C5}:

\displaystyle\quad\tilde{\bf w}_{2}^{H}{\bf G}_{u_{2}}\tilde{\bf w}_{2}\geq\lambda_{\text{\tiny SIC}}\,\tilde{\bf w}_{1}^{H}{\bf G}_{u_{1}}\tilde{\bf w}_{1},

(49e)

where constraint
C1
ensures that the UEs’ transmit power does not exceed the maximum power budget
P^{\mathrm{max}}_{u}
,
C2
guarantees non-negative sensing power,
C3
imposes an upper bound
P^{\mathrm{max}}_{s}
on the total transmit power of the radar signal,
C4
limits information leakage under the predefined secrecy threshold
\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}
, and
C5
enforces the received-power ordering required for SIC feasibility at Eve.

Problem
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is non-convex and challenging to solve. The non-convexity of the maximization problem arises from the non-concave objective function and non-convex constraints (
49c
)–(
49e
) with respect to (w.r.t.) the optimization variables
\tilde{\bf w}_{k},{\bf v}_{k},{\bf v}_{s},\text{ and }p_{s}
. In the following, we present an algorithm to efficiently tackle the
OP
.

IV-B

Proposed Solution

To tackle the non-convex
OP
, we apply an
alternating optimization (AO)
, where we split
\mathcal{P}_{0}
into two subproblems:

•

Subproblem 1
: Optimize
{\bf v}_{k}
and
{\bf v}_{s}
while keeping the other variables fixed.

•

Subproblem 2
: Jointly optimize the beamformer vectors
\tilde{\bf w}_{k},\penalty\ \forall k
, and the sensing power
p_{s}
.

These two subproblems are then solved iteratively, alternating between them until convergence, starting from an initial feasible point.

IV-B
1
Solution to Subproblem 1

In problem
\mathcal{P}_{0}
, the precoding vectors
{\bf v}_{k}
and
{\bf v}_{s}
appear exclusively in the objective function via the SINR terms in the form of a sum of logarithm expressions. Since the logarithms in the objective are strictly increasing, maximizing the objective w.r.t. these variables is equivalent to directly maximizing their respective SINRs.

The SINRs (
14
), (
15
), and (
22
) can be rewritten as Rayleigh quotients, i.e., ratios of quadratic forms:

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bf v}_{1}^{H}\tilde{{\bf A}}{\bf v}_{1}}{{\bf v}_{1}^{H}\tilde{{\bf B}}{\bf v}_{1}},

(50a)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bf v}_{2}^{H}\tilde{{\bf C}}{\bf v}_{2}}{{\bf v}_{2}^{H}\tilde{{\bf D}}{\bf v}_{2}},

(50b)

\displaystyle\gamma_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bf v}_{s}^{H}\tilde{{\bf E}}{\bf v}_{s}}{{\bf v}_{s}^{H}\tilde{{\bf F}}{\bf v}_{s}},

(50c)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf A}}

\displaystyle={\bf H}_{u_{1}}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{1}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{1}^{H}{\bf H}_{u_{1}}^{H},

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf B}}

\displaystyle={\bf H}_{u_{2}}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{2}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{2}^{H}{\bf H}_{u_{2}}^{H}+p_{s}{\bf Q}_{\text{\tiny SI}_{\text{u}}}+\sigma_{b}^{2}{\bf I},

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf C}}

\displaystyle={\bf H}_{u_{2}}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{2}\tilde{{\bf w}}_{2}^{H}{\bf H}_{u_{2}}^{H},\quad\tilde{{\bf D}}=p_{s}{\bf Q}_{\text{\tiny SI}_{\text{u}}}+\sigma_{b}^{2}{\bf I},

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf E}}

\displaystyle=p_{s}|\beta_{0}|^{2}L^{2}{\bf Q}_{s},\quad\tilde{{\bf F}}=p_{s}|c(\ell^{\prime}(\tau_{b}))|^{2}{\bf Q}_{\text{SI}}+\sigma_{b}^{2}{\bf I}.

(51)

Since all of the above matrices are Hermitian and the matrices in the denominator are positive definite, the optimal SINRs can be expressed as

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\tilde{{\bf B}}^{-1}\tilde{{\bf A}}\bigr),\quad\|{\bf v}_{1}\|^{2}=1,

(52a)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\tilde{{\bf D}}^{-1}\tilde{{\bf C}}\bigr),\quad\|{\bf v}_{2}\|^{2}=1,

(52b)

\displaystyle\gamma_{s}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\tilde{{\bf F}}^{-1}\tilde{{\bf E}}\bigr),\quad\|{\bf v}_{s}\|^{2}=1.

(52c)

where

denotes the largest eigenvalue of its matrix argument. Since the precoders are not involved in any constraints, they can be updated in closed form at each iteration, given fixed values of
{\bf w}_{k}
and
p_{s}
. Therefore, the optimal precoding vectors for this subproblem are the normalized generalized eigenvectors corresponding to the largest generalized eigenvalue:

\displaystyle{\bf v}_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{eigvec}_{\max}(\tilde{{\bf A}},\tilde{{\bf B}}),

(53a)

\displaystyle{\bf v}_{2}^{*}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{eigvec}_{\max}(\tilde{{\bf C}},\tilde{{\bf D}}),

(53b)

\displaystyle{\bf v}_{s}^{*}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{eigvec}_{\max}(\tilde{{\bf E}},\tilde{{\bf F}}).

(53c)

2

\displaystyle\eqref{cst1}\text{--}\eqref{cst4}.

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle y_{2}

VI
Conclusion

We investigated a secure uplink PD-NOMA ISAC system in the presence of an eavesdropping target with uncertain location. To account for the sensing uncertainty, a CRB-informed framework was developed to characterize how angle and delay estimation errors propagate into the assumed communication, sensing, and eavesdropping channel models. Based on this uncertainty-aware model, a joint beamforming and sensing-power optimization problem was formulated to maximize a weighted communication–sensing utility subject to secrecy constraints, and an AO-SCA algorithm was proposed to obtain an efficient solution.
Simulation results confirm that the proposed robust-secure design reliably maintains Eve’s SINR below the prescribed threshold under location uncertainty, while maintaining strong sensing performance and competitive communication rates. These results demonstrate that CRB-aware robust design is an effective and practically relevant approach for secure uplink NOMA-ISAC, and that ignoring sensing-induced uncertainty can lead to overly optimistic and potentially insecure system designs. A natural direction for future work is to extend the framework to mobile eavesdroppers and time-varying uncertainty models.
