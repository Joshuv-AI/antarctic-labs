 SUMMARY

Melni Technologies LLC has developed a line splice technology that can be 3D printed and promises
market improvements in the form of lower cost and superior mechanical properties. The new 35 kV line
splice design is working its way through go/no-go testing to validate the design and hit project milestones.
This report provides an initial high potential (HIPOT) testing result for a go/no-go design approval
decision. The test resulted in a “go” decision.

 Melni Line Splice Report
 1. Project Summary

Melni Technologies LLC is developing a novel new 35kV Direct Burial In-Line Splice technology that
has sufficient efficiency gain potential to be considered a grid enhancing technology that can help reduce
the cost of grid projects and make future transition less burdensome to utilities. If successful, the
deployment of this splice technology will greatly enhance the performance and ease of grid maintenance
and modifications. Primary anticipated improvements of splice include a 3x reduced installation time,
>40% reduction in workmanship errors and 1.5x service life longevity improvement. The cost savings
production quality and potential service life improvements resulting from this design warrant additional
INL support pending available resources.

The line splice design and resulting prototype have passed a series go/no-go test gates already. For this
test INL testing facilities were made available to perform a high potential (HIPOT) test from the IEEE-
404 standard. This test requires a voltage ramp followed by a 9 kV plateau for one minute. While the
design is rated for a 35 kV line, the milestone requirement for the prototype corresponds to a 2.5 kV
production joint. This go/no-go was designed to identify any critical design failures at a lower voltage.

Following a successful IEEE-404 test, the INL team ramped the voltage to the point of failure.
Unfortunately, the test configuration caused a fault at 30kV. The splice itself had no apparent failures and
no indication of failure upon a posttest inspection.

Having passed the test gate, the Melni engineering team will perform a HIPOT test to failure on the
prototype. An INL engineer has been invited and will include those results in an update of this report
pending available resources.

 2. Test Plan
A 3D printed prototype design line splice was to be tested following the IEEE-404 standard for AC
Voltage testing (Section 7.4.1) for cable joints rated up to 2.5 kV. The testing required a voltage ramp
raise rate of 5kV/s +- 3kV/s up to a 9kV level, which was then to be maintained for one min with no
failures. Following the one min AC voltage test, the voltage was to be increased to failure.

  Acquire a Line Splice 3D printed prototype and perform a HIPOT test using INLs test equipment
  Test/demonstrate the 3D printed Line Splicer on INLs HIPOT at for the IEEE-404 standard using
 section 7.4.1 and 5.2 Table 1 for the 2.5kV rated joints.

The results of the initial IEEE-404 test demonstrated no failure during the ramp or maintenance of the
9kV for one minute. The leakage current was just under 1 mA without the guard cable connected and
unreadable ~0mA with the guard cable connected. The results constitute a clear “go” for the prototype
design. An image of the HIPOT tester after 1 minute at 9kV is shown in Figure 4.

 3.2. Test to Failure
Following the IEEE-404 test the voltage was ramped up to 20kV at which point the HIPOT tester tripped.
Examination of the footage demonstrated the failure was not the splice joint, but instead the terminations
that had been set up for a 9kV test. A still image of the fault is shown in Figure 5. After the trip at 20kV
the grounds on the cable were peeled back (shown in Figure 6) to create a larger distance between them
and the conductor at the center of the cable. The test was performed again with a failure generated at
30kV. This failure was again the result of the configuration and not the splice joint.

 4. Conclusion
The Melni Line splice passed the IEEE-404 9 kV one minute go/no-go test requirement. The splice was
not tested to failure due to deficiencies in the test configuration, however the Melni design did perform up
to the 30kV threshold which was the test configurations limit. After testing, the splice joint was examined
and no clear indication of failure was visible, while the location of the failure was visible on the test stand
and cable termination. Figure 7 shows the splice posttest.

 5. Next Steps

 A test configuration capable of tests above 150kV need to be devised to establish the limit of this
splice joint. Additional tests from the IEEE 404 standard will need to be performed for partial discharge,
AC voltage, and impulse testing.
