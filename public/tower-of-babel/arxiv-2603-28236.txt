1.
Introduction

Auslander-Reiten theory is fundamental in the study of representation theory of finite dimensional algebras from the perspective of homological algebra.
A higher version of Auslander-Reiten theory was introduced in
[
Iya07a
,
Iya07b
]
which has connections to algebraic geometry
[
IW11
,
IW13
,
IW14
,
HIMO23
]
, combinatorics
[
OT12
]
and symplectic geometry
[
DJL21
]
. It is also a crucial ingredient to prove the Donovan-Weymss conjecture
[
JKM22
]
.
In higher Auslander-Reiten theory, the object of study is some category
\mathcal{A}
,
usually the module category of a finite dimensional algebra or its bounded derived category,
equipped with a
d
-cluster tilting subcategory
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
,
possibly with some additional properties.
Depending on different settings,
d
-cluster tilting subcategories give rise to higher notions in homological algebra.
For example, if
\mathcal{A}
is abelian and
\mathcal{M}
is
d
-cluster tilting,
then
\mathcal{M}
is a
d
-abelian category in the sense of Jasso
[
Jas16
]
.
The notion of
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting property was introduced by Iyama and Jasso
[
IJ17
]
.
In the setting when
\mathcal{A}
is triangulated,

\mathcal{M}
is a
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
precisely means
\mathcal{M}
is
d
-cluster tilting and closed under the
d
-fold suspension.
Consequently such
\mathcal{M}
gives rise to a
(d+2)
-angulated category in the sense of Geiss-Keller-Oppermann
[
GKO13
]
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional algebra and denote by
{\rm{mod}}A
the category of finitely generated right
A
-modules.
A
is called
d
-representation finite if
{\rm{gldim}}A=d
and there exists a
d
-cluster tilting subcategory
\mathcal{M}
in
{\rm{mod}}A
. In this case,
\mathcal{M}
is unique and canonically induces a
d
-cluster tilting subcategory
\mathcal{U}
in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
. More precisely,

\mathcal{U}={\rm{add}}\{M[di]\mid M\in\mathcal{M}\text{ and }i\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

This case was intensively studied by many authors, see for example
[
Iya11
,
IO11
,
IO13
,
HI11a
,
HI11b
]
.
For
A
with
{\rm{gldim}}A>d
,
much fewer results are known.
Certain families of such algebras which are not self-injective were found by Vaso
[
Vas19
,
Vas20
,
Vas21
,
Vas23
]
.
The self-injective case was studied in
[
DI20
]
.
Other notable families of such algebras include Nakayama algebras studied in
[
Vas19
,
DI20
,
HKV25
]
and higher Nakayama algebras
[

]
.

As the
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting property is significantly stronger than
d
-cluster tilting,
algebras with a
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory are even rare.
However,
as shown by Kvamme
[
Kva21
]
,
a
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory in the module category canonically induces a
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory in its singularity category.
Further, the Auslander-Iyama correspondence introduced by Jasso-Muro
[
JKM22
]
established the one-to-one correspondence between a triangulated category with a
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting object and twisted periodic self-injective algebras up to equivalence.

Motivated by these results, this paper aims to give a construction called the sink-source gluing to obtain more algebras with
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories.
And as an application, we give a classification of
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories of a non-self-injective
d
-Nakayama algebra.

Such gluings were considered in
[
IPTZ87
,
Lév08
,
BCW15
,
Vas20
]
.
We show that the algebra obtained from sink-source gluing of algebras with a
d
-cluster tilting subcategory (respectively
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory) has a canonical
d
-cluster tilting subcategory (respectively
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory) in their module category,
which is obtained by gluing the corresponding
d
-cluster tilting subcategories (respectively
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories) of each piece.

Our classification result for higher Nakayama algebras generalizes the classification of
n\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories for Nakayama algebras
[
HKV25
]
.
For a
d
-Nakayama algebra
A
with
{\rm{rad~}}^{2}A=0
,
we may view it as a classical Nakayama algebra and apply the classification reault obtained in
[
HKV25
]
.
More generally, a characterization of
d
-cluster tilting subcategories and
d\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories for radical square zero algebras given by quivers with relations was given in
[
Vas23
]
.
Otherwise, for each non self-injective
d
-Nakayama algebra, we give a classification of
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories for
n>1
.
In this case, such an integer
n
is unique and the
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory is also unique.
We subdivide the classification result into three cases – depending on the Kupisch series – namely,
acyclic homogeneous,
acyclic non-homogeneous and cyclic non-homogeneous.

The classification for acyclic homogeneous
d
-Nakayama algebras is given in Theorem
3.11
.
In this case, all such algebras with
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories are
2
-subhomogeneous (see
[
Xin25
, Definition 3.1]
).
Equivalently, the indecomposable objects in such an
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory consist of projective or injective objects.
The classification result for acyclic non-homogeneous
d
-Nakayama algebras is given in Theorem
3.15
.
All such algebras are obtained by
sink-source gluing of acyclic homogeneous ones with
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories.
The classification result for cyclic non-homogeneous
d
-Nakayama algebras is given in Theorem
3.23
.
Such algebras are obtained by self-gluing acyclic non-homogeneous ones with
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories.

For a self-injective
d
-Nakayama algebra, such a classification result seems to be infeasible.
So instead we construct an explicit example for each such algebra with extra conditions.

\tau_{nd}M=\tau_{d}\Omega^{(n-1)d}M\in\mathcal{C}_{I}
.
Moreover
\Omega^{(n-1)d}M
is indecomposable and non-projective by Definition
2.6
(d).
So
\Omega^{(n-1)d}M\in\mathcal{M}_{P}
as
M\in\mathcal{M}
and
\Omega^{d}\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
.
Hence
\tau_{d}\Omega^{(n-1)d}M\in\mathcal{M}_{I}
.
Thus we have
\tau_{nd}M\in\mathcal{C}_{I}\cap\mathcal{M}_{I}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{M}}_{I}
.

Arguing similarly we have
\tau_{nd}^{-1}\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{M}}_{I}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{M}}_{P}
.

So the inverse bijections

\textstyle{\mathcal{C}_{P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau_{nd}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{C}_{I}.\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau_{nd}^{-1}}

restricts to inverse bijections

\textstyle{\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{M}}_{P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau_{nd}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{M}}_{I}.\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau_{nd}^{-1}}

∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
A
be an algebra.
Assume
\mathcal{M}\subseteq{\rm{mod}}A
is a

-cluster tilting subcategory and

-cluster tilting subcategory.

(a)

,

\Omega^{-d}\tau_{d}M\in\mathcal{C}
.

(b)

If

2.3.
The sink-source gluing

In this section, we introduce the gluing of algebras given by quivers with relations along a sink and a source, which will be called a sink-source gluing.
Such gluings or pullbacks were considered in
[
IPTZ87
,
Lév08
,
BCW15
]
.
In
[
Vas20
]
, the gluing of representation-directed algebras along left and right abutments of height
h
for a positive integer
h
was introduced.
The sink-source gluing is a specialization of gluing along left and right abutments of height
0
.
The difference here is that we don’t restrict to representation-directed algebras.
To establish notation as well as make the article self-contained,
we give full proof of some results which may be already considered in
[
IPTZ87
,
Lév08
,
BCW15
,
Vas20
]
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let

Remark 2.13
.

Denote by
S_{a}\in{\rm{mod}}A
the simple injective
A
-module at vertex
a
and
S_{b}\in{\rm{mod}}B
the simple projective
B
-module at vertex
b
.
Let
S
be the simple
\Lambda
-module at vertex
a
, or equivalently
b
.
We identify both
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}S_{a}
and
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}S_{b}
with
S
along the isomorphisms

S\cong{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}S_{a}\cong{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}S_{b}.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\Lambda=A\Delta_{S}B
.
Then the following statements hold true.

(a)

The functors

{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}^{\ast}
and
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}^{\ast}
preserve projective objects.
More precisely,

{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}^{\ast}(e_{i}\Lambda)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}e_{i}A&i\in(Q_{A})_{0}\\
0&\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.\text{ and~~~~ }{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}^{\ast}(e_{i}\Lambda)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}e_{i}B&i\in(Q_{B})_{0}\\
0&\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

(b)

We have
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}(e_{i}A)\cong e_{i}\Lambda
and

{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}(e_{i}B)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}e_{i}\Lambda&i\neq b\\
S&i=b\end{array}\right.

(a’)

The functors
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}^{!}
and
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}^{!}
preserve injective objects.
More recisely,

{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}^{!}(D\Lambda e_{i})\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}DAe_{i}&i\in(Q_{A})_{0}\\
0&\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.\text{ and~~~~ }{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}^{!}(D\Lambda e_{i})\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}DBe_{i}&i\in(Q_{B})_{0}\\
0&\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

(b’)

We have
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}(DBe_{i})\cong D\Lambda e_{i}
and

{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}(DAe_{i})\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}D\Lambda e_{i}&i\neq a\\
S&i=a\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Part (a) follows from the properties of the recollement.
We prove (b).

{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}(e_{i}A)\cong(e_{i}\Lambda)/(e_{i}\Lambda(1-e_{A})\Lambda)\cong e_{i}\Lambda

as
e_{i}\Lambda(1-e_{A})=0
for

by definition.
Similarly,

{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}(e_{i}B)\cong(e_{i}\Lambda)/(e_{i}\Lambda(1-e_{B})\Lambda)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}e_{i}\Lambda&i\neq b\\
S&i=b.\end{array}\right.

Dually, statement (a’) and (b’) can be proved similarly.
∎

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
\Lambda=A\Delta_{S}B
. We have

\nu_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}(e_{i}A))\cong{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}\nu_{A}(e_{i}A),i\in(Q_{A})_{0}\backslash\{a\},

and

\nu_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}(e_{i}B))\cong{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}\nu_{B}(e_{i}B),i\in(Q_{B})_{0}\backslash\{b\}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}\nu_{A}(e_{a}A)

\displaystyle\cong S,

\displaystyle\nu_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}(e_{b}B))

\displaystyle\cong\nu_{\Lambda}(S),

\displaystyle\nu_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}(e_{a}A))

\displaystyle\cong{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}\nu_{B}(e_{b}B).

Proof.

Since the Nakayama functor maps the indecomposable projective module at each vertex to the corresponding indecomposable injective module,
the statement follows by applying Proposition
2.14
.
∎

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\Lambda=A\Delta_{S}B
.
Let

and

.
The following statements hold true.

(a)

We have

{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M)\cong{\rm{Hom}}_{B}(N,S)\otimes_{\Bbbk}{\rm{Hom}}_{A}(S,M).

{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi_{\ast}N,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M)=0
if
S\notin{\rm{add}}M
or
S\notin{\rm{add}}N
.

(b)

Similarly,

{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N)\cong{\rm{Hom}}_{A}(M,S)\otimes_{\Bbbk}{\rm{Hom}}_{B}(S,N).

{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N)\cong\Bbbk^{\ell_{M}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}{\rm{Hom}}_{B}(S,N)\cong{\rm{Hom}}_{A}(M,S)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\Bbbk^{r_{N}}

where

is the minimal left
{\rm{add}}S
-approximation of
M
and

is the minimal right
{\rm{add}}S
-approximation of
N
.
In particular,

{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N)=0

f_{M}=0
or
g_{N}=0
.

Proof.

As
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}({\rm{mod}}A)\cap{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}({\rm{mod}}B)={\rm{add}}S
,
all morphisms from
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N
to
{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M
factor through
S
.
So we have

{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N,S)\otimes_{\Bbbk}{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(S,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M)\twoheadrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M).

Since

\displaystyle{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M)

{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M)=0

or

.

On the other hand,

{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}({\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{A}\pi}}_{\ast}M,{\rm{{}^{\Lambda}_{B}\pi}}_{\ast}N)=0

2.4.
The orbit construction

We recall background on orbit categories following
[
DI20
]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a locally bounded
\Bbbk
-linear Krull-Schmidt category and let
G
be a group.
A
\Bbbk
-linear
G
-action on the category
\mathcal{A}
is an assignment
g\mapsto F_{g}
of a
\Bbbk
-linear automorphism
F_{g}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
such that
F_{g}\circ F_{h}=F_{gh}
for all
g,h\in G
.
To simplify notations, we write
g(x):=F_{g}(x)
for
g\in G
and
x\in\mathcal{A}
.
A
G
-action is called admissible if
g(x)\ncong x
for all
x\in{\rm{ind}}{\mathcal{A}}
and
g\in G\backslash\{1\}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a locally bounded
\Bbbk
-linear Krull-Schmidt category and
G
be a group acting admissibly on
\mathcal{A}
.
The orbit category
\mathcal{A}/G
is also locally bounded
\Bbbk
-linear Krull-Schmidt which is given by the following data.

\bullet

The objects of
\mathcal{A}/G
are the objects of
\mathcal{A}
.

\bullet

For
x,y\in\mathcal{A}/G
,

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}/G}(x,y)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(x,g(y)).

\bullet

For
(a_{g})_{g\in G}\in{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}/G}(x,y)
and
(b_{g})_{g\in G}\in{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}/G}(y,z)
, set

(ba)_{g}=\sum_{h\in G}h(b_{h^{-1}g})a_{h}:x\rightarrow g(z),

so
ba\in{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}/G}(x,z)
.

The natural functor
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}/G
induces an exact functor

F^{\ast}:{\rm{Mod}}\mathcal{A}/G\rightarrow{\rm{Mod}}\mathcal{A}

called pull-up.
This functor has a left adjoint

F_{\ast}:{\rm{Mod}}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow{\rm{Mod}}\mathcal{A}/G

called the push-down, which is also exact.
Moreover,
F_{\ast}
induces a functor
F_{\ast}:{\rm{mod}}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow{\rm{mod}}\mathcal{A}/G
.

Notice that a
G
-action on
\mathcal{A}
induces a
G
-action on
{\rm{mod}}\mathcal{A}
defined by
g(M)=M\circ g
for
M\in{\rm{mod}}\mathcal{A}
and
g\in G
.
A subcategory
\mathcal{U}\subseteq{\rm{mod}}\mathcal{A}
is called
G
-equivariant if
g(\mathcal{U})\subseteq\mathcal{U}
for all
g\in G
.

We recall one of the main results in
[
DI20
]
.

Theorem 2.28
.

[
DI20
, Corollary 5.2]

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a locally bounded
\Bbbk
-linear Krull-Schmidt category and
G
a free abelian group of finite rank acting admissibly on
\mathcal{A}
.
If
\mathcal{U}\subseteq{\rm{mod}}\mathcal{A}
is a locally bounded

-equivariant

-cluster tilting subcategory,
then

is a locally bounded
d
-cluster tilting subcategory.

3.
Classification

In this section, we study the existence and classification of
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories for each
d
-Nakayama algebra
A=A_{\underline{\ell}}^{d}
and
n>1
.
In Section
3.1
, we obtain certain necessary conditions on
\underline{\ell}
such that there exists a partial
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory for some
n>1
.
In the case when
A
is non-self-injective, we obtain a classification of its
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategories in Section
3.2
.
The classification is completely determined by the arithmetic properties of
\underline{\ell}
.
In fact, except for the case where
{\rm{rad~}}^{2}A=0
, there exists at most one
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory for a suitable integer
n
.
In the case when
A=\widetilde{A}_{m-1,\ell}^{d}
is self-injective,
we show that an
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory is only possible if
n|m
and
n|(\ell-2)
.
In case
n=\ell-2
, we show that such subcategory does exist by constructing an explicit example.

3.1.

Let
A=A_{\underline{\ell}}^{d}
be a non-self-injective
d
-Nakayama algebra.
In this section, we show that
{\rm{mod}}A
admits a partial
nd\mathbb{Z}
-cluster tilting subcategory only if
\underline{\ell}
is piecewise homogeneous, i.e.
\underline{\ell}=\underline{\ell_{1}}\Delta\cdots\Delta\underline{\ell_{t}}
where each
\underline{\ell_{i}}

, or
\underline{\ell}
is the self-glue of a piecewise homogenous Kupisch series.

We show by the following Lemma that if

Lemma 3.26
.

Let

be an

-cluster tilting subcategory.

if and only if

.

Proof.

First note that
\mathcal{C}
is closed under

For

For

For

The case
