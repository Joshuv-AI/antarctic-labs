Pruritus in leprosy: an overlooked symptom
Authors: Shraddha Uprety, Pratichya Thapa, Prajwal Pudasaini, Prakriti Lamichhane, Aditi Joshi, Sunil Jaiswal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHansen disease (leprosy) is a chronic granulomatous disease caused by Mycobacterium leprae, which usually affects the skin and peripheral nerves. Patients usually present with hypoaesthetic or anaesthetic skin lesions. Patients with leprosy rarely complain of pruritus and if present, it can be confused with various pruritic dermatological conditions, leading to diagnostic dilemma. We hereby report a case of leprosy in a 57-year-old woman presenting with pruritic skin lesion, highlighting the importance of recognizing pruritus as a possible manifestation of leprosy, particularly in endemic areas, to facilitate early diagnosis and prevent complications.

Dear Editor, A 57-year-old woman presented with a severely pruritic erythematous plaque on the forehead of 6 months’ duration (Figure 1). The visual analogue scale score for pruritus was 7. Examination revealed decreased hair over the lesion; however, loss of sensation and peripheral nerve thickening were absent. The forehead location, with overlapping trigeminal nerve supply, might cause subtle sensory deficits that are difficult to appreciate clinically. Punch biopsy was advised due to the chronicity of the lesion. Haematoxylin and eosin-stained section showed dense lymphocytic aggregate and epithelioid histiocytes in periadnexal, perivascular and perineural regions (Figure 2). Ziehl-Neelsen stain and Wade–Fite stain for acid-fast bacilli were negative. The patient was diagnosed with tuberculoid leprosy based on clinicohistopathological correlation, and she was advised to start multibacillary multidrug therapy for 6 months.

Erythematous indurated plaque on the forehead involving the frontal hair line with decreased hair.

(a) Haematoxylin and eosin-stained histopathological image (low magnification). (b, c) Histopathology (high magnification) shows well-defined granulomas in the periadnexal, perivascular and perineural regions.

Our observation highlights the presence of pruritus in a patient with Hansen disease. The proposed mechanism of pruritus in leprosy is attributed to neuropathy.1,2 The peripheral nerve fibres are damaged in leprosy due to host’s immune-mediated response against Mycobaterium leprae bacilli.3 The neuronal injury induces the release of various proinflammatory mediators such as histamine, canonical itch mediator, lysophosphatidic acid, interleukin-31 and interleukin-33. These mediators stimulate the free nerve endings, which induces the sensation of itch.4 Similarly, damage to autonomic sympathetic fibres impairs sweat gland function leading to anhidrosis or hypohidrosis in affected dermatomes, producing dry skin that is often pruritic.2 Pruritus in Hansen disease can mimic factitious dermatitis, prurigo nodularis and lichenoid eruption.1,2,5,6
