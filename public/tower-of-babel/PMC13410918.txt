Impact on survival of severe immuno-related adverse events in deficient mismatch repair/microsatellite instable-high digestive cancers treated with immunotherapy
Authors: David Tougeron, Charles Kauffmann, Margherita Ambrosini, Olayide Boussari, Sara Lonardi, Frank A Sinicrope, Marwan Fakih, Michael J Overman, Elena Elez, Anthony Turpin, Marie Decraecker, Antoine Hollebecque, Simon Pernot, Thibault Mazard, Marie Dutherage, Romain Cohen, Francesco Sclafani, Mohamed Ben Abdelghani, Thomas Aparicio, Clélia Coutzac, Vincent Hautefeuille, Marie Muller, Chiara Cremolini, Rosine Guimbaud, Julien Taieb, Filippo Pietrantonio, Raphael Olivier, Emily Alouani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDeficient DNA mismatch repair/microsatellite instability-high (dMMR/MSI-H) cancers are very sensitive to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), yet their use is frequently complicated by immune-related adverse events (irAEs). Our study analyzed the impact of grade ≥3 irAEs (immune-related severe adverse events (irSAEs)) on survival in patients with dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers treated with ICIs.MethodsWe conducted an international, multicenter, ambispective study involving 1,175 patients from 34 centers. The primary endpoint was the correlation between the occurrence of irSAEs and progression-free survival (PFS). Secondary endpoints included factors associated with irSAEs, correlation between irSAEs and overall response rate (ORR) and overall survival (OS). Prespecified landmark and time-dependent survival models were used to account for the time to irSAE occurrence.ResultsAmong 1,175 patients treated with ICIs for digestive cancers, 49.1% were female, median age was 66.9 years (IQR 53.7–77.3) and 82.9% had colorectal cancer. Overall, 382 patients (32.5%) had an irAE, including 117 (10.0%) irSAEs. Median time to irSAEs occurrence was 3.78 months (IQR 1.97–7.62). The most frequent irSAEs were gastrointestinal (3.4%), hepatic (1.8%) and dermatologic (1.0%). The only factor associated with irSAEs was the use of an ICI combination versus monotherapy (20.0% vs 11.4%, p=0.03). Among patients with irSAEs, 71.5% received oral and 29.8% intravenous corticosteroids, and 15.7% immunosuppressive agents. Overall, in 26.9% of patients, ICIs were resumed following irSAEs, with an irAE recurrence rate of 57.1%. irSAE was associated with a better ORR (48.6% vs 34.6%, p<0.0001). The multivariable Cox model revealed that irSAE as a time-dependent variable was not associated with better PFS (HR=1.280 (95% CI 0.898 to 1.824), p=0.172) or OS (HR=1.043 (95% CI 0.712 to 1.528), p=0.828). Likewise, landmark analysis at 3 months and 6 months also showed that irSAEs were not associated with better PFS or OS.ConclusionsThe occurrence of irSAE was not independently associated with better survival in patients with dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers treated with ICIs.Keywords: Immune related adverse event - irAE, Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor, Microsatellite, Mismatch repair - MMR, Gastrointestinal Cancer

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting programmed death 1 (PD-1) or programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA-4) have revolutionized the prognosis of many cancers. About 15% of early-stage colorectal cancers (CRC) have a deficient DNA mismatch repair/microsatellite instability-high (dMMR/MSI-H) phenotype, while the prevalence of this phenotype in non-colorectal digestive cancers varies from 30% in small bowel adenocarcinomas to 1% in pancreatic adenocarcinomas.1–7 dMMR/MSI-H tumors could occur either in a hereditary context (Lynch syndrome) or more frequently as sporadic cases. Determination of MMR/MSI tumor status has important prognostic and therapeutic implications and is recommended at diagnosis for all CRC, gastric and small bowel adenocarcinomas. In the metastatic setting, even though dMMR/MSI-H digestive tumors have historically been associated with a poor prognosis and chemoresistance,8–12 they are associated with frameshift mutations, which generate immunogenic neoantigens, making them highly sensitive to ICIs.13 Three randomized trials, the KEYNOTE-177, CHECKMATE 8HW and SAMCO-PRODIGE 54 have shown the superiority of ICIs over chemotherapy in metastatic dMMR/MSI-H CRC.14–16 In metastatic dMMR/MSI-H, data for non-colorectal tumors are more limited, but non-randomized studies have demonstrated the efficacy of ICIs versus chemotherapy.17 18 A recent meta-analysis comparing ICIs alone or in combination with chemotherapy versus standard of care in patients with advanced MSI-H gastric cancer suggested that ICI use procured an improvement in overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS).19

ICIs stimulate the immune system against the tumor, but can also cause immune-related adverse events (irAEs).20 21 Severe grade 3–5 irAEs occur in 10–20% of patients with ICI monotherapy and in 20–40% with ICI combinations, leading to approximately 10% of treatment discontinuations and 3% of immune-related toxic deaths.22–24 Rates of irAEs associated with ICIs are well-described, but factors associated with these irAEs are poorly studied. It is challenging to compare the frequency of irAEs according to the type of ICI or primary tumor, and predictive factors for severe irAEs and their correlations with outcomes are largely unknown.25 26 In addition, data concerning irAEs in dMMR/MSI-H gastrointestinal tumors are limited due to the rarity of these tumors, in particular the factors associated with severe grade ≥3 irAEs (immune-related severe adverse events (irSAEs)). Moreover, the impact of these irAEs on prognosis is not well-known. Several studies have shown that survival in patients with irAEs from ICIs is longer than that in patients with no irAEs, even following ICI discontinuation, especially in melanoma and non-small-cell lung cancer.27–30 By contrast, some studies found no association between irAEs and treatment efficacy.31–33 To our knowledge, there are no data concerning the association between irAEs and treatment efficacy in dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers.

Herein, we report an international multicenter ambispective study to describe irAEs in dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers treated with ICIs and analyze the correlation between the occurrence of irSAEs and ICI efficacy.

Materials and methods

Population

This is an international, multicenter, ambispective study involving 34 centers in France, Italy, the USA and Belgium. All consecutive patients with locally advanced or metastatic unresectable dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancer treated with immunotherapy (ICI monotherapy or in combination) regardless of the treatment line between January 2014 and December 2023 were included. The cut-off date was June 30, 2025.

Inclusion criteria were age ≥18 years, MSI-H (by molecular biology using PCR and/or next-generation sequencing as per standard institutional practices) and/or dMMR (by immunohistochemistry of the four MMR proteins) digestive cancer, and treatment with ICI (monotherapy or in combination). Exclusion criteria were ICIs combined with chemotherapy or targeted therapy and discordance between MSI and MMR status (ie, proficient mismatch repair (pMMR)/MSI-H or dMMR/microsatellite stable (MSS) tumors).

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the French regulatory requirements (Commission Nationale de l’Informatique et des Libertés, CNIL, reference 2222018 v0) and local ethics committees.

Patient and tumor characteristics

Data on patients (age, sex and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS)) and tumors (primary tumor site, tumor node metastasis stage, tumor grade, locally advanced vs metastatic disease, sites of metastases at ICI start) were collected. BRAF and RAS mutational status were also collected for CRC.

The treatment line and type of ICI (anti-PD-(L)1 monotherapy vs anti-CTLA-4-based combination), the duration of treatment and the reason for ICI discontinuation were collected.

Adverse events

irAEs were defined as AEs occurring after ICI start and considered by treating physicians to result from immunological dysfunction. irAEs were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events V.4.0. All AEs, principally endocrine, gastrointestinal, hepatic, dermatological, pulmonary, rheumatological and nephrological, were collected, but the AEs of interest were grade ≥3 irAEs (irSAEs), which were used to classify patients with and without irSAEs.

Information on the management and outcome of irSAEs was collected, including the use of oral or intravenous corticosteroids and immunosuppressive agents (according to the American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) guidelines). Data on treatment decisions, including ICI discontinuation and possible reoccurrence of irAEs after treatment re-introduction, were also collected.

Outcomes and objectives

Patients were divided into two groups: with irSAE (grade ≥3 irAEs) or without irSAE (no irAE or irAE grade 1 or 2). The main objective of the study was to determine whether irSAEs were associated with PFS. The secondary objectives concerned the correlation between irSAEs and overall response rate (ORR) and OS. The predictive factors for irSAEs were also determined in univariable and multivariable analyses.

The ORR was defined as the percentage of patients with a complete or partial response according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors V.1.1. The disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the proportion of patients with a complete/partial response or stable disease as the best response. PFS was defined as the time from the start of ICI to the first documented disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Patients alive and without progression, whether included retrospectively or prospectively, were censored at the end of the follow-up (June 30, 2025). OS was defined as the time from the start of the ICI to the date of death from any cause. Patients alive were censored at the end of the follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Age (continuous variable) was described according to its median and IQR. Categorical variables (ie, sex, ECOG, primary tumor site, grade, metastatic sites) were described as frequencies and percentages. Factors associated with the occurrence of irSAEs were evaluated using the Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney test for age and using Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. A p value of <0.05 denotes a significant association. PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and described using medians or percentages, with their 95% CIs, at specific time points. Median follow-up was estimated using the reverse Kaplan-Meier method. Associations between patients’ and tumor characteristics on the one hand and PFS and OS on the other were examined using a Cox regression model, and variables with a p value ≤0.1 were entered into a multivariable Cox regression model with HR and 95% CI. To consider the time to irSAE occurrence and immortal time bias, prespecified landmark analyses were done at different time points depending on the median time to irSAE occurrence. Also, an additional prespecified analysis using toxicity as a time-dependent variable in the multivariable Cox model was carried out.

A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical tests were two-sided. All analyses were done using R V.4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). This observational study follows the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.

Results

Characteristics of patients and tumors

Overall, 1,175 patients with dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers treated with ICIs were included.

The median age was 66.9 years (IQR 53.7–77.3) and 49.1% of patients were women (table 1). The most frequent primary tumor was CRC (82.9%), followed by esophagogastric cancer (7.7%). Most tumors were metastatic at ICI initiation (93.8%). Most patients (70.5%) had previously undergone resection of the primary tumor. Most patients (85.2%) had a good performance status (ECOG PS 0 or 1) at baseline. Among colorectal adenocarcinomas, 30.2% harbored the RAS mutation (n=268/887) and 36.6% had the BRAFV600E mutation (n=327/894).

Nearly half of the patients received ICIs in the first-line setting (48.0%) and one-third (32.1%) in the second-line setting. The most common ICI used was pembrolizumab (67.6%) and most patients (87.6%) received ICI monotherapy. The median duration of ICI treatment was 9.7 (IQR 2.7–22.4) months and treatment was ongoing in 207 patients (17.6%) at the end of the follow-up. Overall, 82.4% patients permanently discontinued ICI treatment (n=931/1130), mainly due to disease progression (n=413/931, 44.4%), completion of treatment (n=249/931, 26.7%) or irAEs (n=102/931, 11.0%).

Immune-related adverse events

Overall, 382 patients (32.5%) had at least one irAEs, including 117 (10.0%) with at least one irSAEs. Median time to irSAE occurrence was 3.78 months (IQR 1.97–7.62) (figure 1).

Time to serious immune-related adverse events.

The most frequent irAEs of any grade were cutaneous (11.5%), dysthyroidism (10.3%) and gastrointestinal events (9.9%) (table 2). Other irAEs occurred less frequently (5% or less). irSAEs were mainly digestive, hepatic and cutaneous AEs (3.4%, 1.8% and 1.0%, respectively). Other AEs accounted for a minority (<1%) of irSAEs. Five grade 5 AEs were observed, corresponding to 4.3% of patients with irSAEs and 0.4% of the overall population. Fatal irAEs included one hepatocellular failure (related to anti-PD-1), one colitis (related to anti-PD-1 plus anti-CTLA-4), one autoimmune lung disease (related to anti-PD-1), one myocarditis (related to anti-PD-1) and one nephrotoxicity (related to anti-PD-1 plus anti-CTLA-4).

Immune-related adverse events

irAE, immune-related adverse events.

The only factor significantly associated with the occurrence of irSAEs was the use of an ICI combination (20.0% vs 11.4% with anti-PD-(L)1 monotherapy, p=0.03) (table 1). The primary tumor site, ECOG PS, age and line of treatment were not significantly associated with the occurrence of irSAEs.

Management of immune-related adverse events

A total of 121 irSAEs were recorded in 117 patients, including 4 patients with two events. Regarding the management of irSAEs, 71.5% of patients received oral corticosteroids (n=60/84), 29.8% intravenous corticosteroids (n=25/84) and 15.7% immunosuppressive therapy, including antitumor necrosis factor (n=13/83). The irSAEs resolved (grade ≤1) in most cases (86.2%). Among the 12 patients whose irSAE did not resolve, 5 died, 3 were still being treated for their irSAE, 3 had their irSAE controlled by other treatments (1 diabetes mellitus treated with insulin therapy and 2 hypophysitis managed with hydrocortisone) and 1 was still receiving ICI (grade 3 lipase increase). After the first irSAEs, 21 patients (26.9%, n=21/78) resumed ICIs and 12 patients (57.1%, n=12/21) experienced recurrence of the irAE.

Outcomes according to immune-related adverse events

In the overall population, median follow-up was 34.1 months (IQR 18.91–55.29). The ORR and DCR rates were 36.1% (n=357/987) and 78.5% (n=775/987), respectively.

ORR was significantly higher in patients with irSAEs (48.6% for patients with irSAE vs 34.6% for patients without irSAE (p=0.005)) as was DCR (88.0% vs 77.3%, p=0.014) (table 3). When irSAEs were treated as a time-fixed covariate, PFS was significantly longer in patients with irSAEs than in those without irSAE (median PFS: not reached vs 43.3 months, 95% CI 34.2 to 54.1 and HR=0.594, 95% CI 0.424 to 0.834, p=0.003) (online supplemental figure 1 and table 1). PFS at 3 years was 66.1% in patients with irSAE versus 52.6% in patients without irSAE. However, when irSAEs were modeled as a time-dependent variable in multivariable Cox analysis, no association with improved PFS was observed (HR=1.280, 95% CI 0.898 to 1.824, p=0.172) (table 4). Landmark analyses were done at 3 and 6 months since the median time to irSAE onset was 3.78 months. Similarly, landmark analysis at 3 months (HR=0.896, 95% CI 0.456 to 1.759, p=0.750) and 6 months (HR=1.054, 95% CI 0.585 to 1.899, p=0.861) did not show an association between irSAE and improved PFS (table 5, online supplemental table 2 and figure 2,3). In multivariable analysis, the presence of peritoneal carcinomatosis, liver metastasis, lung metastasis, a later line of ICI treatment and worse ECOG PS at ICI initiation were independently associated with worse PFS (table 4).

Discussion

Our study did not identify grade ≥3 irAE as a predictor of better survival outcomes in patients with dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers treated with ICIs. To our knowledge, this is the first study to specifically evaluate the relationship between irSAEs and outcomes in dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers. In addition, our series represents the largest real-world cohort to date, including 1,175 patients with dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers treated with ICIs. Patients’ and tumor characteristics from our series are consistent with those reported in previously published trials and cohorts of dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers.14–19 It is worth noting that 48.0% of patients received ICI in a first-line setting, and that the majority (87.6%) were treated with ICI monotherapy.

Overall, 32.5% of patients experienced at least one irAE, including 10.0% of irSAEs. Five patients (0.4%) experienced a grade 5 adverse event. Only 11.0% of patients permanently discontinued ICI treatment due to irAEs. These rates are consistent with the literature, according to which the expected rate of grade ≥3 irAEs in ICI monotherapy is 10–20%, rising to 20–40% with ICI combination therapy, with about 10% of permanent discontinuation and 3% of immune-related toxic deaths in other tumor types.22–24 The rates of AEs in our study are comparable to those in the KEYNOTE-177 trial (pembrolizumab vs chemotherapy), with 31% of irAEs and 9% of grade ≥3 irAEs (no death) with anti-PD-1 monotherapy.34 Similarly, these results align with those of the CHECKMATE-8HW trial, which reported 14% of grade ≥3 irAEs with nivolumab (one death) versus 22% with nivolumab plus ipilimumab combination (two deaths).15

In our study, irSAEs were mainly gastrointestinal, hepatic and cutaneous AEs (3.4%, 1.8% and 1.0%, respectively). Approximately 90% of patients recovered from irSAE with adequate management. This toxicity profile is consistent with that reported in pivotal trials. In the KEYNOTE-177 trial, the most common grade ≥3 irAEs were colitis (3%) and hepatitis (3%).34 In the CHECKMATE-8HW study, the most common grade ≥3 irAEs in the nivolumab plus ipilimumab and nivolumab groups were diarrhea or colitis (3% and 2%, respectively), hypophysitis (3% and 1%, respectively), and adrenal insufficiency (3% and <1%, respectively).15

To our knowledge, few studies have investigated predictors of irSAEs, and none have focused on patients with dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers treated with ICIs. IrSAEs were reported to occur more frequently with ICI doublet therapy than with monotherapy.15 In our cohort, the only factor significantly associated with irSAEs was ICI combination therapy compared with monotherapy (20.0% vs 11.4%, respectively). A major risk factor for irAEs is a personal history of chronic inflammatory disease, but unfortunately, this information was not collected in our study. Recent studies, mainly in lung cancer, identified a younger age, brain metastases, a history of smoking, high PD-L1 expression and first-line treatment as factors associated with a higher incidence of irSAEs.35–37 These discrepancies likely reflect tumor-specific differences in immune toxicity profiles, as previously suggested.25 26

The median time to irSAE onset was approximately 3.5 months in our study. While KEYNOTE 177 and CHECKMATE-8HW did not report time to irAEs onset, our results are consistent with other randomized trials and real-world cohorts across tumor types, where median onset typically ranges from 2 to 3 months, depending on the type of toxicity.38–40 Skin toxicities usually occur first (3–6 weeks), followed by colitis, whereas hepatitis and endocrinopathies tend to appear later (6–24 weeks).

When irSAEs were analyzed as a time-fixed covariate, an approach known to induce bias, irSAEs appeared to be associated with improved outcomes, including higher ORR (48.6% vs 34.6%), higher DCR (88.0% vs 77.3%), longer PFS (3-year PFS: 66.1% vs 52.6%), and a trend toward improved OS (3-year OS: 70.6% vs 63.0%). However, irSAEs are by nature time-dependent events that occur during treatment. The higher ORR and DCR observed in patients with irSAEs should be interpreted with caution, as these endpoints are susceptible to guarantee-time bias. Patients who develop irSAEs must remain on treatment long enough to both respond to treatment and develop toxicity, while those with early progression may not. When accounting for this using time-dependent Cox models and landmark analyses, irSAEs were no longer associated with improved PFS or OS. This suggests a classic immortal time bias; patients with favorable prognostic factors survive longer and therefore have more time to develop irSAEs, whereas patients with a poor prognosis may progress or die before having the opportunity to experience ICI toxicity. Indeed, we found that patients who progressed or died within 3 months were more likely to have peritoneal carcinomatosis, liver metastasis, lung metastasis, to be treated with ICIs in later lines and to have a worse ECOG PS, all of which are associated with shorter PFS and OS. Landmark analyses could be subject to selection bias by excluding patients with early progression or death. The approach in the time-dependent Cox analyses is a well-known alternative that avoids not only the problem of selecting a landmark time (which excludes patients with early events) but also any misclassification errors regarding irSAE status.

Several studies in other tumor types, including melanoma, renal cell carcinoma, non-small-cell lung cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and non-MSI-H gastric cancer, have reported associations between irAEs and improved PFS, OS, or response to treatment.27–30 33 In more than one thousand patients with resected melanoma treated with adjuvant pembrolizumab, irAEs were associated with better recurrence-free survival (HR, 0.61; 95% CI 0.39 to 0.95; p=0.03).27 Similar associations have been reported in metastatic renal cell carcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma.28 30 41 However, results remain inconsistent, with several studies, particularly in melanoma, failing to identify irSAEs as predictors of improved outcome, and many not accounting for the time-dependent nature of irSAEs.40 42–44 Moreover, some data suggest that only specific toxicities, such as endocrine or cutaneous irAEs, may be associated with better outcomes.35 44

We also studied the management of irSAEs. Most patients received corticosteroids (70%) and about 15% required immunosuppressive agents. In about 90%, irSAE resolved with appropriate management, with no detrimental impact on survival, even when ICIs were discontinued. Previous studies in other tumor types also demonstrated that ICI discontinuation after irAE did not negatively affect survival, as was the case with the use of corticosteroids.29 32 40 43 In our study, only one-third of patients resumed immunotherapy after the occurrence of an irSAE, and about half of these patients experienced a recurrence of the same irAE. This rate is in line with prior studies, which reported irAE recurrence rates ranging from 30 to 50%.20 35 45 A recent meta-analysis identified several factors associated with irAE recurrence, including the type of irAE, the type of cancer, a short time to re-introduction (less than 10 weeks), gastrointestinal irAEs and ICI combination therapy.20 These findings may help to guide the management of dMMR/MSI-H patients with irSAEs. Given that most patients achieve disease control and that the risk of irAE recurrence is substantial, ICI reintroduction should be considered only in patients with disease progression after ICI discontinuation and discussed on a case-by-case basis within a multidisciplinary team.

The main limitations of our study include its ambispective design and the potential under-reporting of low-grade irAEs. It is for this last reason that we decided to look only at the links between grade ≥3 irAEs and outcomes. However, major strengths include the large sample size: the largest existing cohort of dMMR/MSI-H digestive tumors, supported by multiple international centers. In addition, data concerning tumor and patients’ characteristics, treatment response and survival are reliable with little missing information. Importantly, we applied appropriate time-dependent analytical methods, including landmark analyses, to account for the delayed occurrence of irSAEs.

Conclusion

Together, these findings indicate that while irAEs may reflect immune activation, irSAEs should not be interpreted as reliable surrogate markers of treatment efficacy in dMMR/MSI-H digestive cancers since when appropriate time-dependent methods were applied there was no association with survival. Among patients who experienced irSAEs, most achieved durable disease control, despite permanent ICI discontinuation and the use of corticosteroids and immunosuppressive agents. These findings suggest that a watch-and-wait strategy, without systematic reintroduction of ICIs after irSAEs, may be appropriate in patients who maintain disease control.
