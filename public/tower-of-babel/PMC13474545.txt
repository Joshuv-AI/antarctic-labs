Impact of implant-supported fixed prostheses on quality of life in patients with severe jaw atrophy reconstructed with anterior iliac crest grafts: a retrospective study
Authors: Hacer Eberlikose, Caner Ozturk, Hakan Alpay Karasu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFixed prostheses in edentulous patients offer enhanced stability, functionality, and aesthetics compared to removable options, significantly improving chewing efficiency and comfort. However, severe alveolar ridge atrophy poses significant challenges for implant-based rehabilitation, necessitating advanced bone augmentation techniques to ensure optimal functional and aesthetic outcomes. Anterior iliac crest bone grafting is widely used for restoring bone volume in such cases. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of implant-supported fixed prostheses (ISFPs) on oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) and general health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in patients with severely atrophied maxillae reconstructed using anterior iliac crest grafts.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted on 28 patients who underwent iliac crest bone grafting on the maxilla followed by ISFP rehabilitation. Patient-reported outcomes were assessed using the Oral Health Impact Profile-14 (OHIP-14) and Short Form-36 (SF-36) questionnaires before and after treatment. Statistical analyses were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Mann Whitney U and Kruskal Wallis test, with a significance level set at p < 0.05.ResultsAnalysis revealed significant improvements in posttreatment OHRQoL scores, particularly among female patients (p < 0.05), who showed greater sensitivity to aesthetic and functional changes. Male participants reported higher scores for physical role function and general health perception (p < 0.05). Age was found to influence general health scores but had no significant impact on OHRQoL.ConclusionsThese findings emphasize the effectiveness of anterior iliac crest bone grafting in restoring oral function and aesthetics while underscoring the importance of tailoring treatment approaches based on patient demographics and preferences. In the context of patient-centered care, incorporating patients’ subjective experiences has become essential to evidence-based clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-026-08730-8.

Introduction

The rehabilitation of edentulism with implant-supported fixed prostheses (ISFPs) is widely recognized as the preferred treatment modality [1]. ISFPs offer superior stability, masticatory function, phonetics, and aesthetics compared to removable prostheses, leading to increased patient satisfaction and quality of life (QoL). Removable prostheses, however, are generally considered easier to clean and maintain, particularly for older individuals or those with reduced manual dexterity [1, 2].

Rehabilitating patients with severe alveolar ridge resorption presents significant challenges. Insufficient bone volume compromises primary implant stability and osseointegration, increasing the risk of implant failure and complicating prosthetic planning. To overcome these challenges and enhance long-term outcomes, advanced bone augmentation techniques are frequently required [3, 4].Among augmentation options, the anterior iliac crest is a reliable donor site due to its accessibility and availability of both cortical and cancellous bone, which promote graft integration and implant stability. Despite its efficacy, iliac crest harvesting may cause donor-site morbidity such as pain and gait disturbances [5–8]. A two-stage surgical protocol is commonly employed, allowing grafts to heal before implant placement. This approach improves vascularization and reduces risks like graft resorption and implant exposure [9–11].

Despite the clinical effectiveness of iliac crest grafting, limited data exist on its long-term impact on oral health–related quality of life (OHRQoL) and general health–related quality of life (HRQoL) when combined with ISFP rehabilitation.Although prior research has focused chiefly on implant survival rates and graft integration, there is a paucity of publications examining the impact of iliac crest bone grafting in conjunction with ISFPs on long-term patient satisfaction, functional rehabilitation, and psychosocial well-being [12–14].

Understanding the long-term effects of iliac crest bone grafting with ISFPs on patient quality of life will help clinicians to improve treatment procedures, align outcomes with patient expectations, and reduce post-surgical problems. To evaluate these effects comprehensively, validated patient-reported outcome measures are essential. The Oral Health Impact Profile-14 (OHIP-14) is a widely used instrument for evaluating oral health conditions’ functional, psychological, and social effects on daily life [15]. The Short Form-36 (SF-36), a general health survey, assesses physical and mental well-being on a variety of dimensions [16]. Combining OHIP-14 and SF-36 in oral rehabilitation research provides a more comprehensive evaluation of patient outcomes, including disease-specific and HRQoL. This multifaceted approach allows for a more nuanced assessment of implant-supported fixed prostheses’ functional and psychosocial benefits after iliac crest bone grafting [17].

This study aims to evaluate whether anterior iliac crest bone grafting followed ISFP rehabilitation improves oral and general health-related quality of life in patients with severely resorbed maxillae. Patient satisfaction and postoperative well-being were assessed using validated tools (OHIP-14 and SF-36). The null hypothesis states that this treatment approach does not significantly affect OHRQoL.

Materials and methods

A total of 36 patients were initially screened for eligibility. Of these, 8 were excluded due to incomplete clinical or follow-up records (n = 3), the use of non-standard grafting techniques (n = 2), or the presence of systemic conditions such as uncontrolled diabetes or bisphosphonate use (n = 3). Ultimately, 28 patients with reconstructed maxillae, who completed both preoperative and postoperative assessments, were included in the final analysis. These patients received a total of 179 implants supporting screw-retained fixed prostheses.

Patients were referred to the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery at Ankara Medipol University, Turkey, between December 2022 and January 2023, presenting with complaints of pain, mobility, and loss of retention associated with their removable dentures. Following clinical and radiographic evaluation, a standardized treatment protocol was planned for all cases, including anterior iliac crest bone grafting followed by screw-retained fixed prosthetic rehabilitation to address maxillary structural deficiencies.

The inclusion criteria were as follows; age between 18 and 65 years; insufficient residual bone volume to allow implant placement without augmentation; and receipt of anterior iliac crest onlay bone grafting. Exclusion criteria comprised uncontrolled systemic diseases (e.g., diabetes mellitus, osteoporosis), active periodontal disease, prior radiotherapy to the head and neck region, or medications affecting bone metabolism (e.g., bisphosphonates). All patients were required to have completed the entire treatment protocol, including grafting, implant placement, successful osseointegration (according to the criteria of Buser et al. [14]), and final prosthetic restoration.

All surgical procedures were performed under general anesthesia. Corticocancellous bone blocks were harvested from the anterior iliac crest and secured to the maxilla using titanium screws. A healing period of four months was allowed prior to implant placement. All implants were inserted using a submerged approach and later restored with screw-retained prostheses.

To minimize potential sources of bias, all procedures were performed by the same surgical and prosthetic team using a consistent protocol. As this was a retrospective cohort study, only patients who completed the entire treatment and follow-up period were included in the analysis. Consequently, no patients were lost to follow-up and no imputation was necessary.

This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines to ensure transparent and comprehensive reporting. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Ankara Medipol University (Approval No. 158), and written informed consent was secured from all participants in line with the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants were informed of the study objectives and voluntarily agreed to complete the associated questionnaires (see Fig. 1).

Oral Health-Related Quality of Life (OHRQoL): Slade initially created and validated this instrument in 1997 [18]. The Oral Health Impact Profile-14 (OHIP-14), a validated tool adapted to Turkish [19, 20], was used to assess patients’ OHRQoL. The OHIP-14 consists of 14 questions across seven domains, with responses recorded on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from “never” (0) to “always” (4). The questionnaire was administered twice: preoperatively, during the final examination before anterior iliac crest grafting, and at the six-month follow-up appointment. The patient was regularly scheduled for routine follow-up appointments, and the implants were deemed successful according to Buser’s criteria.

General Health and Quality of Life: The general health survey was utilized to assess the overall HRQoL of participants [21]. Kocyigit et al. validated the Turkish version of the SF-36 [22]. The questionnaire evaluates physical and mental well-being across various aspects, including physical functioning, physiological pain, overall health perception, energy levels, social functioning, emotional well-being, and role limitations stemming from physical and emotional health issues. The Short Form-36 (SF-36) questionnaire was employed to assess the effects of anterior iliac crest bone grafting on patients’ mental and physical well-being. The SF-36 was administered once, two weeks postoperatively after anterior iliac crest bone transplantation.

Both questionnaires were administered in Turkish, using versions previously validated through cross-cultural adaptation procedures described by Beaton et al., including forward and backward translation, expert panel review, and pretesting on representative populations [23, 24].

To sum up, the primary outcome variables of this study were OHRQoL and HRQoL, which were assessed using the validated Turkish versions of the OHIP-14 and the SF-36 questionnaires, respectively. These instruments were chosen due to their widespread application in oral health research and well-established psychometric properties. The OHIP-14 was administered twice—preoperatively and at the six-month follow-up—while the SF-36 was completed two weeks after anterior iliac crest bone grafting. All questionnaires were self-administered under standardized conditions during routine follow-up visits. The Turkish versions had been cross-culturally adapted following internationally accepted guidelines, ensuring both linguistic accuracy and conceptual equivalence. As all participants followed the same treatment protocol and completed identical instruments at fixed intervals, the assessment methods were fully standardized and comparable across the entire cohort. All participants completed the OHIP-14 and SF-36 questionnaires in full, and no missing data were encountered for the variables included in the analysis.

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics software (version 26, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). To compare categorical variables between groups, the Chi-square test was used. Differences between two independent groups were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Pre- and postoperative OHIP-14 scores were compared using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Subgroup comparisons based on gender, follow-up duration, and number of implants were conducted using the Mann–Whitney U test, while age group differences were assessed using the Kruskal–Wallis test followed by pairwise Mann–Whitney U tests for post hoc comparisons, when appropriate. In the SF-36 group, the Kruskal–Wallis test was used to compare age groups, and the Mann–Whitney U test was used for comparisons between binary variables such as gender, follow-up duration, and implant number.

Quantitative variables were categorized for subgroup analysis as follows: age (18–35, 36–55, ≥ 56 years), number of implants (≤ 6 vs. >6), and follow-up duration (≤ 6 vs. >6 months). These thresholds were selected based on clinical relevance and to ensure balanced group sizes. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Given the retrospective nature of the study, no a priori sample size calculation was performed. Instead, the final study population consisted of all eligible patients who met the inclusion criteria and completed the full treatment and follow-up protocol within the defined study period (December 2022 to January 2023). To assess the adequacy of the sample size, a post hoc power analysis was conducted based on the observed differences in OHIP-14 scores before and after treatment. This analysis was intended to provide supportive evidence for the robustness of the findings, although it does not substitute for a prospective power calculation.

Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Discussion

This study evaluated the effects of anterior iliac crest bone grafting followed by implant-supported fixed prosthesis rehabilitation on OHRQoL and HRQoLin patients with severe maxillary atrophy. The statistically significant differences in pre- and postoperative OHIP-14 and SF-36 scores indicate that this treatment modality positively influences both oral function and overall well-being. These findings support the rejection of the null hypothesis and confirm the clinical efficacy of combining extensive bone augmentation with fixed prosthetic rehabilitation in this patient group.

In the present study, the significant reduction in OHIP-14 scores following ISFP rehabilitation is consistent with the findings of Tanır et al., who reported improved OHRQoL in patients treated with anterior iliac crest grafts and fixed prostheses [4]. Similar trends were also observed in a longitudinal study by Bramanti et al., where implant therapy led to sustained improvements in functional limitation and psychological discomfort domains of OHIP over two years [2].

Another significant improvement in postoperative OHIP-14 scores was observed in female patients (p = 0.03), suggesting gender-specific differences in quality of life improvements. Psychosocial variables may contribute to this disparity, as women tend to prioritize body image, social relationships, and self-esteem, all of which can be profoundly influenced by dental health and aesthetic rehabilitation [25]. Previous studies support this, noting that women are often more sensitive to health changes and demonstrate distinct health-seeking behaviors [26–28]. These observations highlight the importance of considering gender-specific expectations during treatment planning and follow-up care to optimize patient-centered outcomes. So, future studies could explore tailored treatment strategies that address gender-specific needs in oral rehabilitation.

The SF-36 is widely used for evaluating self-reported health and functional abilities. The questionnaire includes subscales assessing various health domains, while the physical and mental component summaries reflect overall health dimensions [12]. Sönmez et al. found that OHIP-14 scores were closely linked to the Turkish population’s physical and mental dimensions of SF-36 [29]. Despite this study’s relatively high general health perception scores (females: 70.00 ± 13.28; males: 79.5 ± 14.99), Reissmann et al. noted that using the iliac crest as a donor site can reduce HRQoL. Therefore, clinicians and patients should consider the potential impact on HRQoL when choosing extra-oral donor sites for bone grafting [30].

In contrast to the OHIP-14 results, the SF-36 showed significant improvements in physical functioning and role limitations. Physical role function scores were higher in males (98.00 ± 4.83) than females (83.00 ± 12.67), suggesting gender-related differences in perceived recovery. These findings highlight the importance of evaluating both physical and mental health domains after iliac crest bone grafting and the need for multidisciplinary care, including psychological and functional support.The decrease in general health perception among older patients is consistent with Lee et al., who reported age-related declines in self-rated health in individuals with oral functional limitations [26]. Our gender-related findings also align with Lee et al. (2022), who emphasized the influence of gender on health perception and quality of life [27].However, due to the small subgroup sizes, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Limited statistical power may affect their generalizability, and future studies with larger cohorts are needed to confirm these trends.

Anterior iliac crest bone grafting has some potential drawbacks, including discomfort, gait issues, and a longer recovery period at the donor site, despite the fact that it can enhance OHRQoL and HRQoL [7–9]. When proper protocols are followed, these risks are rare, but they do necessitate a customized risk-benefit analysis and careful patient selection. In terms of implant longevity, biological issues, and OHRQoL, less invasive alternatives such as zygomatic and short implants have produced comparable outcomes. For instance, Laventure et al. discovered no significant distinctions between autogenous grafts and zygomatic implants [31]. With minimal bone loss and few complications, short implants (≤ 8 mm) also demonstrated positive outcomes [32]. However, because they are less stable and have higher crown-to-implant ratios, they might not be the best option for patients with severe anterior atrophy [33]. The patient’s anatomy, preferences, and long-term functional goals should ultimately guide the treatment decision.

This study provides evidence supporting the use of anterior iliac crest bone grafting in combination with implant-supported fixed prostheses as an effective approach for rehabilitating patients with severe jaw atrophy. The findings indicate that this treatment protocol contributes to improvements in oral function and patient-reported quality of life.

Despite positive results, the relatively short follow-up period in this study represents a key limitation, as it may not fully capture the long-term stability of grafted bone, implant survival, or sustained improvements in quality of life. Autogenous iliac crest grafts, while offering excellent initial osteogenic potential and implant stability, are known to undergo progressive resorption over time, potentially compromising long-term outcomes [34, 35]. An additional limitation is that the SF-36 was administered only postoperatively, which precludes a direct comparison with baseline HRQoL levels. As such, the findings reflect early postoperative perceptions rather than longitudinal changes in general health status. Furthermore, comparisons with other augmentation materials—such as intraoral autografts, xenografts, and synthetic substitutes—are needed to better define the relative advantages and limitations of iliac crest grafting. Finally, potential confounding factors, including systemic health conditions and socioeconomic variables, should be accounted for in future research to enhance the generalizability of findings.

Conclusion

This study underscores the significant improvements in OHRQoL and HRQoL following iliac crest bone grafting and ISFP rehabilitation. The observed gender- and age-related differences highlight the need for personalized treatment planning and postoperative care strategies. Future research should focus on larger cohorts, extended follow-up periods, and additional psychosocial and functional factors to optimize patient-centered approaches in maxillofacial rehabilitation.
