Introduction and main results

To introduce our questions, let us begin with the NLS with a pure-power nonlinearity set on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For any fixed
\lambda>0
, it is well known that the following equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u,\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(1.1)

has a unique positive solution
u_{\lambda}
(up to translations) whenever
2<p<2^{*}:=2N/(N-2)^{+}
, see
[
14
]
. Moreover, by using Pohozaev identity, see e.g.
[
26
, Appendix B]
, it can be checked that (
1.1
) admits no solution when
\lambda\leq 0
. Due to scaling invariance, direct computation yields

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-2}}u_{1}(\sqrt{\lambda}x)\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{\lambda}|^{2}dx=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-2}-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{1}|^{2}dx.

This indicates that whenever
p\in(2,2^{*})
and
p\neq 2+4/N
, for any
\mu>0
, there exists a
unique
positive function
u_{\mu}
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{\mu}|^{2}dx=\mu
, with a parameter
\lambda_{\mu}
, solving (
1.1
) with
\lambda=\lambda_{\mu}
. Define the energy functional

\displaystyle E(u,\mathbb{R}^{N})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx.

In the
mass subcritical
case, that is

2<p<p_{c}:=2+4/N,

we see that
E(u_{\mu},\mathbb{R}^{N})\to 0
and
\lambda_{\mu}\to 0^{+}
as
\mu\to 0^{+}
while
E(u_{\mu},\mathbb{R}^{N})\to-\infty
and
\lambda_{\mu}\to+\infty
as
\mu\to+\infty
. On the contrary, in the
mass supercritical
case, that is,
p_{c}<p<2^{*}
, it holds that
E(u_{\mu},\mathbb{R}^{N})\to+\infty
and
\lambda_{\mu}\to+\infty
as
\mu\to 0^{+}
while
E(u_{\mu},\mathbb{R}^{N})\to 0
and
\lambda_{\mu}\to 0^{+}
as
\mu\to+\infty
.

The situation on a
bounded domain
is quite different. We focus on the case of the unitary ball. For any fixed
\lambda>-\lambda_{1}(B_{1})
where

is the unitary ball centralized at

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary condition, as well known, the following equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }B_{1},\quad u\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{1})

(1.2)

has a unique positive solution
u_{\lambda}
whenever

, see
[
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
,

. Moreover, (

[

. Though scaling invariance is lost in this case, the authors in
[

showed that when

, (

-norm
\mu

\mu>0
; when

, there exists

-norm

and no positive solution with

Thus, for small

-norm

and
\lambda_{\mu,2}\to+\infty
, where

\displaystyle E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{B_{1}}|u|^{p}dx.

Inspired by the above results regarding positive solutions, we guess that for small
\mu>0

for (

\lim_{\mu\to 0^{+}}E(u_{\text{sc},\mu})=0,\quad\lim_{\mu\to 0^{+}}\Big(-\lambda_{\text{sc},\mu}\Big)=\text{some Dirichlet eigenvalue of}-\Delta;

\lim_{\mu\to 0^{+}}E(\tilde{u}_{\text{sc},\mu})=+\infty,\quad\lim_{\mu\to 0^{+}}\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{sc},\mu}=+\infty.

In this paper, we are concerned with the exact number of positive solutions and existence of sign-changing solutions with prescribed mass for NLS on
bounded domains
. More precisely, we consider the following equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u=|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\quad\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx=\mu.

(1.3)

Here

\mu>0

\displaystyle E(u,\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx.

Note that

Secondly, we focus on the mass-supercritical case. When

In the Sobolev critical case (

), a similar result to Theorem

by introducing a new kind of linking. In this paper we use a different approach based on genus theory to address all supercritical, critical and subcritical cases. We note that in

whose Morse index is no more than

is sign-changing or not in general, unless in the regime of

such that no positive solution exists. Compared to the results in
[

]
, the current Theorem

has improvements in three aspects:

•

The solutions that we obtain are sign-changing.

•

Only one solution was found in
[
20
]
while we establish the multiplicity of solutions. Particularly, in the mass subcritical and critical cases, we find infinitely many sign-changing solutions. It seems that this is the first result on the existence of infinitely many sign-changing solutions with prescribed mass for NLS set on bounded domains.

•

The asymptotic behaviors of the energy and of Lagrange multiplier with respect to the parameter
\mu
are provided.

As we know, searching for a sign-changing solution is much more difficult than just finding weak solutions without any sign information. It is worth mentioning that there are many papers and results on free sign-changing solutions (without the mass constraint), but few results of (
1.3
) and of other equations with
L^{2}
constraint. Recently the authors of
[
7
]
established the existence of one sign-changing solution with prescribed mass by developing an action approach. In addition, descending flow technique was developed on a manifold setting to search for multiple sign-changing solutions, see
[
17
,
25
]
. Our work will further enrich the methods and results in this direction.

The Morse index information will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. As a foresight, the boundedness of the Morse index is useful for studying the asymptotic behaviors of the energy and of the Lagrange multiplier via blow-up analyses. In this respect, the
C^{1}
regularity assumption on
\partial\Omega
simplifies the treatment of possible concentration phenomena towards the boundary. In
[
20
]
, the estimate of the Morse index is a direct consequence of the genus (see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
22
]
). The situation is much more complex when we need a sign-changing information. To overcome these difficulties, we provide an abstract tool to obtain Palais-Smale sequences with
location
information and with
approximating Morse index
, and the location information ensures that the solution obtained is sign-changing. Such an abstract tool and framework have their own interest and we believe they will have more applications.

Finally, the following result give an affirmative answer to (Q3).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p_{c}<p<2^{*}
and
\Omega=B_{1}
. There exists
\mu^{**}_{p}>0

\lim_{\mu\to 0^{+}}E(u_{\text{sc},\mu})=+\infty,\quad\lim_{\mu\to 0^{+}}\lambda_{\text{sc},\mu}=+\infty.

In order to prove Theorem
1.4
, we use a variational method and study the sign-changing critical points for the energy functional constrained to the
L^{2}
-sphere

S_{\mu}(\Omega):=\bigl\{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx=\mu\bigr\}.

When
\Omega=B_{1}
, we use notation
S_{\mu}
for simplicity. A key idea is to construct the min-max level by generalizing the mountain pass structure via a saddle-point theorem. To address the bounded issue of Palais-Smale sequences, inspired by
[
3
]
and
[
16
]
, we consider approximating problems firstly and then come back to the origin problem. More precisely, we aim to find two odd functions
\gamma_{i,k}:\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\to S_{\mu}(\Omega)
where
i=0,1
, such that the set

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}:=\big\{\gamma\in C([0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{k-1},S_{\mu}(\Omega)):\forall t\in[0,1],\gamma(t,\cdot)\text{ is odd},\gamma(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0,k},\text{ and }\gamma(1,\cdot)=\gamma_{1,k}\big\}

is not empty and is independent of
\tau
, and

\displaystyle c_{\tau}^{k}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}}\sup_{(t,s)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}E_{\tau}(\gamma(t,s),\Omega)>\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}\max\big\{E_{\tau}(\gamma_{0,k}(s),\Omega),E_{\tau}(\gamma_{1,k}(s),\Omega)\big\},

where

\displaystyle E_{\tau}(u,\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\tau}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx,\quad\tau\in[\frac{1}{2},1].

When
k=1
,

is exactly the mountain pass level. This min-max structure was pointed out in
[
20
, Remark 4.5]
, and later in
[
5
]
, it was used to search for infinitely many solutions with prescribed mass of

-supercritical NLS equations on noncompact metric graphs with localized nonlinearities.

For nonlinear elliptic equations or Schrödinger equations set on bounded domains or on compact metric graphs, it is unknown how to use this min-max structure to find a solution different from the one obtained by mountain pass geometry. In fact, if
c_{1}^{1}<c_{1}^{2}
, we obtain a new solution immediately but we do not know whether

holds true or not. Observing that the solution at mountain pass level can be taken as a positive function, our strategy is to search for a sign-changing one: Using
[

, Theorem 1.12]
, which is stated as Theorem

in this paper, we can prove that for almost every
\tau\in[1/2,1]
,

is indeed a critical value and there is a constrained critical point

whose Morse index is no more than

. Moreover, when

, then

is positive and its Morse index is no more than

. Using some new arguments, we prove that if

, then

is sign-changing and its Morse index is no more than

. When

\displaystyle D^{*}_{\delta}=\overline{\mathcal{P}_{\delta}}\cup\overline{(-\mathcal{P})_{\delta}},\quad S^{*}(\delta)=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\backslash D^{*}_{\delta},

Remark 1.5
.

All the techniques to prove Theorem

Therefore, we keep it as a future research topic.

Preliminary

Firstly, we recall the celebrated Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. For any

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{p,N}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\gamma_{p}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1-\gamma_{p}},

\in(2,2^{*})&\text{if }p_{c}<p<2^{*}.\end{cases}

For any

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p,N}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\gamma_{p}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1-\gamma_{p}}.

(2.2)

Next, we recall the uniqueness result for positive solution of (

.

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u=\tau|u|^{p-1}u\quad\text{in }B_{1},\quad u>0\quad\text{in }B_{1},\quad u\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}),

(2.3)

In the following we make some preparations in search for sign-changing solutions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

\displaystyle 0<\delta<\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}{2}\mu}.

(2.4)

Then
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{\delta}}\cap\overline{(-\mathcal{P})_{\delta}}\cap S_{\mu}(\Omega)=\emptyset
.

If

Next, we introduce the definition of Morse index.

Definition 2.3
.

For any solution
U\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of

\begin{cases}-\Delta U+\lambda U=\tau|U|^{p-2}U&\text{in }\Omega,\\[4.5pt]
\displaystyle U(x)=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.5)

with
\lambda,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider

Q(\varphi;U,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\bigl(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+(\lambda-(p-1)\tau|U|^{p-2})\varphi^{2}\bigr)dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

The Morse index of
U
, denoted by
m(U)
, is the maximal dimension of a subspace
W\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
Q(\varphi;U,\Omega)<0
for all
\varphi\in W\backslash\{0\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\phi
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. For any
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, we
define the continuous bilinear map:

D^{2}\phi(u):=\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(u)[u]}{|u|^{2}}(\cdot,\cdot).

Remark 2.5
.

If
u
is a critical point of
\phi
restricted to
the sphere
S_{\mu}(\Omega)
, then
D^{2}\phi(u)
, seen as a bilinear form on

T_{u}S_{\mu}(\Omega)
, is the second derivative of
\phi|_{S_{\mu}(\Omega)}
at
u
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\phi
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. For any
u\in S_{\mu}(\Omega)
and

\theta>0
, we define the
approximate Morse index
by

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{\theta}(u)=\sup\bigl\{\dim L:L\text{ is a subspace of }T_{u}S_{\mu}(\Omega)\text{ such that }D^{2}\phi(u)[\varphi,\varphi]<-\theta\|\varphi\|^{2},\ \forall\varphi\in L\setminus\{0\}\bigr\}.

If
u
is a critical point for the constrained functional

\phi|_{S_{\mu}(\Omega)}
and
\theta=0
, we say that this is the
Morse
index of
u
as constrained critical point
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}(\Omega)
be a Palais-Smale sequence of
E(\cdot,\Omega)
constrained to
S_{\mu}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Assume that there exists
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
such that
\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq d
for some positive integer
d
. Then
\tilde{m}_{0}(u)\leq d
. Moreover, for some
\lambda_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
,
u
is a solution of equation (
1.3
) with
\lambda=\lambda_{u}
and
m(u)\leq d+1
.

Proof.

Let us first show that the Morse index of
u
, as a constrained critical point, satisfies
\tilde{m}_{0}(u)\leq d
. Assume by contradiction that this is not the case. Then, in view of Definition
2.6
, we may assume that there exists a
W_{0}\subset T_{u}S_{\mu}(\Omega)
with
\dim W_{0}=d+1
such that

D^{2}E(u,\Omega)[w,w]<0\quad\text{for all }w\in W_{0}\setminus\{0\}.

Since
W_{0}
is of finite dimension, its unit sphere is
compact and there exists
\theta>0
such that

D^{2}E(u,\Omega)[w,w]<-\theta\|w\|^{2}\quad\text{for all }w\in W_{0}\setminus\{0\}.

Now, observing that
E(\cdot,\Omega)\in C^{2,\alpha}
where
\alpha\in(0,1]
depending on
p
, it follows that there exists
\delta>0
small enough such that, for any
v\in S_{\mu}(\Omega)
satisfying
\|v-u\|\leq\delta
,

D^{2}E(v,\Omega)[w,w]<-\frac{\theta}{2}\|w\|^{2}\quad\text{for all }w\in W_{0}\setminus\{0\}.

In particular, for
n
large enough,
\|u_{n}-u\|\leq\delta
and
\zeta_{n}<\theta/2
(as
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
),
so the previous inequality implies

D^{2}E(u_{n},\Omega)[w,w]<-\frac{\theta}{2}\|w\|^{2}<-\zeta_{n}\|w\|^{2}\quad\text{for all }w\in W_{0}\setminus\{0\}.

Remembering that
\dim W_{0}>d
and observing that the fact that
\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq d
directly implies that if there
exists a subspace
W_{n}\subset T_{u_{n}}S_{\mu}(\Omega)
such that

D^{2}E(u_{n},\Omega)[w,w]<-\zeta_{n}\|w\|^{2},\quad\text{for all }w\in W_{n}\setminus\{0\},

then necessarily
\dim W_{n}\leq d
, we have reached a contradiction. Note that

\displaystyle\lambda_{u}=-\frac{1}{\mu}E^{\prime}(u,\Omega)[u]=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mu}E^{\prime}(u_{n},\Omega)[u_{n}].

Then, recalling that
S_{\mu}(\Omega)
is of codimension
1
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and observing that, for any
w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle D^{2}E(u,\Omega)[w,w]

\displaystyle=E^{\prime\prime}(u,\Omega)[w,w]+\lambda_{u}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}+\left(\lambda_{u}-(p-1)|u|^{p-2}\right)|w|^{2}\right)dx,

we obtain that
m(u)\leq d+1
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

where

is close, convex in

\displaystyle kw\in\tilde{B},\quad\forall k\in(0,1),w\in\tilde{B}.

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow 0}s^{-1}\text{dist}(u-sV(u),B_{\mu})=0.

\displaystyle|u_{s}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(u-sV(u),u-sV(u))

3
Lagrange multipliers and energies of solutions with bounded Morse index

Throughout this section we will deal with a sequence
\{u_{n},\lambda_{n},\tau_{n}\}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}\times[1/2,1]
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{n}+\lambda_{n}u_{n}=\tau_{n}|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}&\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u_{n}=0&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.1)

We always assume that there exists a positive integer
\bar{k}
such that
m(u_{n})\leq\bar{k}
for all
n
and that
\tau_{n}\to\bar{\tau}
for
\bar{\tau}\in[1/2,1]
as
n\to\infty
in this section. The core study is the relationship between the parameter
\lambda_{n}
and the energy
E_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)
. Roughly speaking, when assuming that the Morse index of
u_{n}
is uniformly bounded, we will prove that the boundedness of
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is equivalent to the boundedness of
\{E_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)\}
if
p\neq p_{c}
. Main tools in the proof are some results obtained by the Morse index information and by blow-up analyses.

Observe that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded from below since the Morse index of
u_{n}
is uniformly bounded, that is

Lemma 3.1
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}>-\infty.

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, up to a subsequence we assume that
\lambda_{n}\to-\infty
. Let
W\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfy
\dim W=\bar{k}+1
. For any
\varphi\in W\backslash\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle Q(\varphi;u_{n},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bigl(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+(\lambda_{n}-(p-1)\tau|u_{n}|^{p-2})\varphi^{2}\bigr)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx+\lambda_{n}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{2}dx<0

for sufficiently large
n
independent of the choice of
\varphi
. This shows that
m(u_{n})\geq\bar{k}+1
if
n
is large enough, contradicting that
m(u_{n})\leq\bar{k}
for all
n
.
∎

In the following lemma we will show that the boundedness of
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is equivalent to the boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}
in
L^{\infty}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded if and only if
\{u_{n}\}
is a
L^{\infty}
-bounded sequence.

Proof.

Sufficiency:

It is plain that
u_{n}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
by regularity. Without loss of generality, we take
x_{n}\in\Omega
such that
u_{n}(x_{n})=\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}>0
and so
u_{n}(x_{n})=\max_{x\in\Omega}u_{n}(x)
. Then, by (
3.1
) we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}u_{n}(x_{n})\leq\tau_{n}|u_{n}(x_{n})|^{p-2}u_{n}(x_{n}),

yielding that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tau_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-2}=\bar{\tau}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-2}.

In view of Lemma
3.1
, we get that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded if
\{u_{n}\}
is a
L^{\infty}
-bounded sequence.

Necessity:
Assuming that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded and
\{u_{n}\}
is unbounded in
L^{\infty}
, we aim to find a contradiction by a classical blow-up argument as
[
2
]
. In fact, up to a subsequence we assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-2}}=0
. We then consider

, which was stated for

Proposition 3.7
.

Let

. The following hold:

(1)

If

, we have that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded if and only if
\{E_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)\}
is bounded. Moreover,

–

when

, up to a subsequence,
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty

–

when

\lambda_{n}\to+\infty

\{\lambda_{n}\}
is not bounded, and we have that

(2)

If

Proof.

Necessity:
Let
2<p<2^{*}
. Assuming that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded and
\{E_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)\}
is not bounded, we aim to find a contradiction. Up to a subsequence, we can assume that
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx\to+\infty
as

. Since

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+\lambda_{n}\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{2}dx=\tau_{n}\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{p}dx,

one of the following holds

(a)

\lambda_{n}\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{2}dx\to-\infty
,

(b)

\tau_{n}\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{p}dx\to+\infty
.

Observe that both
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{\tau_{n}\}
are bounded. If the case (a) occurs, then
\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{2}dx\to+\infty
and so
\{u_{n}\}
is unbounded in

, contradicting Lemma
3.2
. If (b) holds, we can find a similar contradiction.

Sufficiency:
Let
p\neq p_{c}
and
\{E_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)\}
be bounded. We claim that there exists
\bar{\lambda}<+\infty
such that
\lambda_{n}\leq\bar{\lambda}
for all
n
. By negation, we assume that
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
up to a subsequence. By Proposition
3.5
we have

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{p}{p-2}}E_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)\to\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla V_{i}|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V_{i}|^{p}dx.

(3.10)

On the one hand, since
\{\tau_{n}\}\subset[1/2,1]
and
N/2-p/(p-2)<0
, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{p}{p-2}}E_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)\to 0.

On the other hand,
V_{i}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies the following Pohozaev identity

\displaystyle\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla V_{i}|^{2}dx+\frac{N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V_{i}|^{2}dx=\frac{N}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V_{i}|^{p}dx,\quad i=1,\cdots,k,

see e.g.
[

, Theorem B.3]
, which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla V_{i}|^{2}dx=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V_{i}|^{p}dx,\quad i=1,\cdots,k,

and so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla V_{i}|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V_{i}|^{p}dx=\left(\frac{p-2}{4p}N-\frac{1}{p}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|V_{i}|^{p}dx\neq 0,

(3.11)

since

. Thus we find a contradiction and prove the claim. In view of Lemma
3.1
we conclude that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded.

Moreover, if
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is not bounded, by Lemma
3.1
, up to a subsequence we have
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
. Then using (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) we can complete the proof.
∎

4
Exact number of positive solutions

This section is devoted to prove Theorem
1.2
. In the following we will give two useful lemmas and use the reductio ad absurdum to complete the proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p_{c}<p<2^{*}
and
u_{\tau,\lambda}
be the unique solution of (
2.3
), given by Proposition
2.1
; let
M_{\tau}(\lambda)=\int_{B_{1}}|u_{\tau,\lambda}|^{2}dx
. Then, there exists
\Lambda_{1}^{*},\Lambda_{2}^{*}\in(-\lambda_{1}(B_{1}),\infty)
independent of
\tau\in[1/2,1]
, such that
M_{\tau}(\lambda)
is strictly increasing in
\lambda\in(-\lambda_{1}(\Omega),\Lambda_{1}^{*})
and is strictly decreasing in
\lambda>\Lambda_{2}^{*}
.

Proof.

Note that the map
\lambda\in(-\lambda_{1}(B_{1}),\infty)\mapsto u_{\tau,\lambda}\in H_{0}^{1}
is of class
C^{1}
(see
[
23
, Corollary 2.4]
for the proof). Thus
M_{\tau}^{\prime}(\lambda)
exists in
\lambda\in(-\lambda_{1}(B_{1}),\infty)
, and
M_{\tau}^{\prime}(\lambda)=2\int_{B_{1}}u_{\tau,\lambda}v_{\tau,\lambda}dx
, where
v_{\tau,\lambda}=\partial_{\lambda}u_{\tau,\lambda}
.

Firstly, we prove that there exists
\Lambda_{2}^{*}\in(-\lambda_{1}(B_{1}),\infty)
independent of
\tau\in[1/2,1]
such that
M_{\tau}^{\prime}(\lambda)<0
in
\lambda>\Lambda_{2}^{*}
. We see that
v_{\tau,\lambda}\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{1})
is also radial and satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta v+\lambda v-\tau(p-1)|u_{\tau,\lambda}|^{p-2}v=-u_{\tau,\lambda},&\quad\text{in }B_{1},\\
v(x)=0,&\quad\text{on }\partial B_{1}.\end{cases}

For
\lambda>0
and
\tau\in[1/2,1]
we rescale the solutions as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{\tau,\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p-2}}\tau^{\frac{1}{p-2}}u_{\tau,\lambda}(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}x),\quad\tilde{v}_{\tau,\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{-\frac{3-p}{p-2}}\tau^{\frac{1}{p-2}}v_{\tau,\lambda}(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}x).

Observe that
\tilde{u}_{\tau,\lambda}(x)\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{\sqrt{\lambda}})
satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta\tilde{u}_{\tau,\lambda}+\tilde{u}_{\tau,\lambda}=|\tilde{u}_{\tau,\lambda}|^{p-2}\tilde{u}_{\tau,\lambda}\quad\text{in }B_{\sqrt{\lambda}},

and that
\tilde{v}_{\tau,\lambda}(x)\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{\sqrt{\lambda}})
satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta\tilde{v}_{\tau,\lambda}+\tilde{v}_{\tau,\lambda}-(p-1)|\tilde{u}_{\tau,\lambda}|^{p-2}\tilde{v}_{\tau,\lambda}=-\tilde{u}_{\tau,\lambda}\quad\text{in }B_{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

For any
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{\tau_{n}\}\subset[1/2,1]
satisfying
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
, after a small modification of the proof for
[
10
, Proposition 11]
, we get that as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}\to Q_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{v}_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}\to\frac{1}{p-2}Q_{1}+\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla Q_{1},\quad\text{strongly in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
Q_{1}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the unique, positive, radial solution of (
1.1
) with
\lambda=1
. Moreover, since
p>p_{c}
, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle M_{\tau_{n}}(\lambda_{n})=\int_{B_{1}}|u_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}|^{2}dx=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-2}-\frac{N}{2}}\tau_{n}^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}\int_{B_{\sqrt{\lambda}}}|\tilde{u}_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}|^{2}dx\to 0.

Now arguing by contradiction, we suppose that for any
\Lambda>0
, there exist
\lambda>\Lambda
and
\tau=\tau(\lambda)
such that
M_{\tau}^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
. Then we find
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{\tau_{n}\}\subset[1/2,1]
such that
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
and
M_{\tau_{n}}^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})=0
, and get the following self-contradictory inequality

\displaystyle 0=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{4-p}{p-2}}\tau_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-2}}M_{\tau_{n}}^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{4-p}{p-2}}\tau_{n}^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\int_{B_{1}}u_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}v_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\sqrt{\lambda}}}\tilde{u}_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}\tilde{v}_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}dx

\displaystyle\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q_{1}\left(\frac{1}{p-2}Q_{1}+\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\nabla Q_{1}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{p-2}-\frac{N}{4}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|Q_{1}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle<0.

Next, we prove that there exists
\Lambda_{1}^{*}\in(-\lambda_{1}(B_{1}),\infty)
independent of
\tau\in[1/2,1]
such that
M_{\tau}^{\prime}(\lambda)>0
in
\lambda\in(-\lambda_{1}(B_{1}),\Lambda_{1}^{*})
. For any
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{\tau_{n}\}\subset[1/2,1]
satisfying
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
, after a small modification of the proof for
[
10

, we get that as

\displaystyle\frac{u_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}}{\|u_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}}\to\phi_{1},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{v_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}}{\|v_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}}\to\phi_{1}\quad\text{strongly in }H_{0}^{1}(B_{1}).

\displaystyle 0\geq\frac{1}{2\|u_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}}M_{\tau_{n}}^{\prime}(\lambda_{n})=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{u_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}}{\|u_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}}\frac{v_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}}{\|v_{\tau_{n},\lambda_{n}}\|_{L^{2}}}dx\to\int_{B_{1}}|\phi_{1}|^{2}dx=1.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u_{n}\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{1})
solve

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{n}+\lambda_{n}u_{n}=\tau_{n}|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}\quad\text{in }B_{1},\quad u_{n}>0\quad\text{in }B_{1},\quad\int_{B_{1}}|u_{n}|^{2}dx=\mu_{n},

(4.1)

with
\{\tau_{n}\}\subset[1/2,1]
and
\mu_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Up to a subsequence, we have

\displaystyle\text{either}\quad\lambda_{n}\to+\infty\quad\text{or}\quad\lambda_{n}\to-\lambda_{1}(B_{1}).

Proof.

As mentioned in the introduction, it is necessary that
\lambda_{n}>-\lambda_{1}(B_{1})
, see e.g.
[
26
, Theorem 1.19]
. Passing to a subsequence, we suppose that
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda_{n}\to\bar{\lambda}\geq-\lambda_{1}(B_{1})
,
\tau_{n}\to\bar{\tau}\in[1/2,1]
, and we aim to prove
\bar{\lambda}=-\lambda_{1}(B_{1})
, which will complete our proof.

Since
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded, Remark
3.3
shows that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}
. Thus
\{u_{n}\}
is a

-bounded sequence. By (

), it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx+\lambda_{n}\mu_{n}=\tau_{n}\int_{B_{1}}|u_{n}|^{p}dx,

yielding the boundedness of

. Moreover, by (

), the boundedness of

in

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx=\tau_{n}\int_{B_{1}}|u_{n}|^{p}dx-\lambda_{n}\mu_{n}\to 0.

This means that

is a bifurcation point, and by classical bifurcation theory,

is an eigenvalue of

with Dirichlet condition. Recalling that

5
Descending flow techniques and flow invariance

In this section we will construct a descending flow with a pseudogradient operator. Introducing the following bounded subsets of the mass constraint for
\rho>0
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}=\bigl\{u\in S_{\mu}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx<\rho\bigr\},\quad\mathcal{U}_{\rho}^{\mu}=\bigl\{u\in S_{\mu}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\rho\bigr\},

we give a result preparing the introduction a pseudogradient operator.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\tau\in[1/2,1]
and
2<p<2^{*}
. Given
u\in S_{\mu}(\Omega)
, for each
\bar{\lambda}>-\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
the linear equation with a constraint

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta w+\bar{\lambda}w=\tau|u|^{p-2}u+\omega u,\quad w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\
&\int_{\Omega}wudx=\mu,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

has a unique solution where
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
is an unknown Lagrange multiplier. Moreover, if
\mu,\rho>0
and
\bar{\lambda}>-\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
satisfy

\displaystyle C_{p,N}^{p}\rho^{\frac{(p-1)\gamma_{p}}{2}}\mu^{\frac{p-(p-1)\gamma_{p}-2}{2}}\leq\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)+\min\{0,\bar{\lambda}\}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}}\right)^{\gamma_{p}}\left(\lambda_{1}(\Omega)+\bar{\lambda}\right)^{1-\gamma_{p}},

(5.2)

then
\omega\geq 0
for all
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
.

Proof.

Existence:
Let
\bar{\lambda}>-\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
. We consider the following minimization problem

\displaystyle m:=\big\{\inf\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}+\bar{\lambda}w^{2}-\tau|u|^{p-2}uw\right)dx:w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}wudx=\mu\big\}.

(5.3)

By Hölder inequality and Sobolev inequality we get

\displaystyle\tau\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}uwdx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|w|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\mathcal{S}_{p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and thus

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}+\bar{\lambda}w^{2}-\tau|u|^{p-2}uw\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)+\min\{0,\bar{\lambda}\}}{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}dx-\mathcal{S}_{p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(5.4)

which indicates that
m
, defined in (
5.3
), is bounded from below. Let
\{w_{n}\}
be a minimizing sequence for
m
. By (
5
) we obtain that the sequence
\{w_{n}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Up to a subsequence, we assume that
w_{n}
converges to
w
weakly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
L^{p}(\Omega)
for some
w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. It is clear that
\int_{\Omega}wudx=\mu
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}uw_{n}dx\to\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}uwdx
. Then we have

\displaystyle m\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla w|^{2}+\bar{\lambda}w^{2}-\tau|u|^{p-2}uw\right)dx\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}+\bar{\lambda}w_{n}^{2}-\tau|u|^{p-2}uw_{n}\right)dx=m,

which shows that
m
is attained by
w
. By Lagrange multiplier principle,
w
solves (
5.1
) for some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
.

Uniqueness:
If
w_{1},w_{2}
are two solutions of (
5.1
) with
\omega=\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
respectively, by direct computation we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla(w_{2}-w_{1})|^{2}+\bar{\lambda}(w_{2}-w_{1})^{2}\right)dx=\int_{\Omega}\omega_{2}u(w_{2}-w_{1})dx-\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}u(w_{2}-w_{1})dx=0,

(5.5)

implying
w_{2}\equiv w_{1}
, and so
\omega_{2}=\omega_{1}
. Thus (
5.1
) has a unique solution.

Sign of
\omega
:
Given
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
, let
\chi=(-\Delta+\bar{\lambda})^{-1}u\neq 0
. It holds that

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}(\Omega)+\bar{\lambda})\|\chi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}\|\chi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{2}}+\bar{\lambda}\|\chi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\chi|^{2}+\bar{\lambda}\chi^{2}\right)dx=\int_{\Omega}u\chi dx\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\chi\|_{L^{2}},

by which it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{1}(\Omega)+\bar{\lambda}\right)\|\chi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)+\min\{0,\bar{\lambda}\}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}}\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

Then, by Hölder inequality, Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.2
), and (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\tau\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\chi dx

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\|\chi\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,N}^{p}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{(p-1)\gamma_{p}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{(p-1)(1-\gamma_{p})}\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{2}}^{\gamma_{p}}\|\chi\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\gamma_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,N}^{p}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}}{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)+\min\{0,\bar{\lambda}\}}\right)^{\gamma_{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)+\bar{\lambda}}\right)^{1-\gamma_{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{(p-1)\gamma_{p}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{p-(p-1)\gamma_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu.

Multiplying (
5.1
) by
\chi
and integrating by parts yields that

\displaystyle\mu=\int_{\Omega}uwdx=\tau\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\chi dx+\omega\int_{\Omega}u\chi dx\leq\mu+\omega\int_{\Omega}u\chi dx.

Thus we conclude that
\omega\geq 0
and complete the proof.
∎

For
\mu,\rho>0

satisfying (

\displaystyle G_{\tau}:S_{\mu}(\Omega)\to H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\quad u\mapsto G_{\tau}(u)=w,

Lemma 5.2
.

Given

, we have

.

Proof.

The result is clear by noticing

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(u-G_{\tau}(u))udx=\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}wudx=\mu-\mu=0,

where

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

The operator
G_{\tau}\in C^{1}(S_{\mu}(\Omega),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
.

Proof.

It suffices to apply the Implicit Theorem to the
C^{1}
map

\displaystyle\Psi:S_{\mu}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}\to H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R},\quad\text{where}

\displaystyle\Psi(u,w,\omega)=\left(w-(-\Delta+\bar{\lambda})^{-1}(\tau|u|^{p-2}u+\omega u),\int_{\Omega}wudx-\mu\right).

Note that (
5.1
) holds if and only if
\Psi(u,w,\omega)=0
. By computing the derivative of
\Psi
with respect to
w,\omega
at the point
(u,w,\omega)
in the direction
(\bar{w},\bar{\omega})
, we obtain a map
\Phi:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}\to H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle\Phi(\bar{w},\bar{\omega})

\displaystyle:=D_{w,\omega}\Psi(u,w,\omega)(\bar{w},\bar{\omega})

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{w}-\bar{\omega}(-\Delta+\bar{\lambda})^{-1}u,\int_{\Omega}\bar{w}udx\right)

If
\Phi(\bar{w},\bar{\omega})=(0,0)
, then we multiply the equation

\displaystyle-\Delta\bar{w}+\bar{\lambda}\bar{w}=\bar{\omega}u

by
\bar{w}
and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\bar{w}|^{2}+\bar{\lambda}\bar{w}^{2}\right)dx=\bar{\omega}\int_{\Omega}\bar{w}udx=0.

By
\bar{\lambda}>-\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
we have
\bar{w}\equiv 0
and so
\bar{\omega}=0
. Hence,
\Phi
is injective.

On the other hand, for any
(g,\nu)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}
, let

\kappa=\frac{1}{\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta+\bar{\lambda})^{-1}uudx}\left(\nu-\int_{\Omega}gudx\right).

Computing directly we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(g+\kappa(-\Delta+\bar{\lambda})^{-1}u,\kappa)=(g,\nu).

Hence
\Phi
is surjective, that is,

is a bijective map. This completes the proof.
∎

Sign-changing solutions obtained by genus theory

In this section, we are devoted to search for sign-changing solutions by using genus theory and provide the proof of Theorem

Definition 7.1
.

Let

be a closed set, symmetric with respect to the origin (i.e.,

). We define the genus
\gamma
of

\displaystyle\gamma(A):=\sup\bigl\{m:\exists h\in C(\mathbb{S}^{m-1},A),h(-s)=-h(s)\text{ for any }s\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\bigr\}.

Furthermore, we define

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mu}=\bigl\{A\subset S_{\mu}(\Omega):A\text{ is closed and }-A=A\bigr\},\quad\quad\Sigma_{\mu}^{(k)}=\bigl\{A\in\Sigma_{\mu}:\gamma(A)\geq k\bigr\};

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rho,\mu}=\bigl\{A\subset\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}}:A\text{ is closed and }-A=A\bigr\},\quad\quad\Sigma_{\rho,\mu}^{(k)}=\bigl\{A\in\Sigma_{\rho,\mu}:\gamma(A)\geq k\bigr\}.

Lemma 7.2
.

If
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\mu\leq\rho

\Sigma_{\rho,\mu}^{(k)}
is not empty. Particularly,

Clearly,

is closed and there exists a function

\displaystyle h(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{i},

A\cap\text{span}\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{k-1}\}^{\bot}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Reasoning as mentioned at the end of the proof of Lemma

, we just prove the case of

Note that

(7.4)

7.2
and Lemma

\displaystyle\nu_{\mu,k}:=\inf_{A\in\Sigma_{\mu}^{(k)}}\sup_{u\in A}E(u,\Omega)

is well-defined, finite and independent of

5.12

. Otherwise, (

, there exists

, there exists
\eta:C([0,\infty)\times S_{\mu}(\Omega),S_{\mu}(\Omega))

(ii)

\forall u\in S

(8.1)

Suppose that, for every
\tau\in I
,
\Phi_{\tau}|_{S_{\mu}}
is even, and moreover that
\Phi^{\prime}_{\tau}
and
\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\tau}
are

\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets in the sense of
Definition
6.1
for some

\alpha\in(0,1]
. Finally, suppose that there exists an integer
k\geq 1
and two odd functions

\gamma_{i,k}:\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\to S_{\mu}
where
i=0,1
, such
that the set

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}:=\big\{\gamma\in C([0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{k-1},S_{\mu}):\forall t\in[0,1],\gamma(t,\cdot)\text{ is odd},\gamma(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0,k},\text{ and }\gamma(1,\cdot)=\gamma_{1,k}\big\}

(8.2)

is not empty and is independent of
\tau
, and

\displaystyle c_{\tau}^{k}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}}\sup_{(t,s)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}\Phi_{\tau}(\gamma(t,s))>\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}\max\bigl\{\Phi_{\tau}(\gamma_{0,k}(s)),\Phi_{\tau}(\gamma_{1,k}(s))\bigr\}.

(8.3)

Then, for almost every
\tau\in I
, there exist sequences

\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}
and
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
such that, as

,

(i)

\Phi_{\tau}(u_{n})\to c_{\tau}^{k}
;

(ii)

\bigl\|\nabla\Phi_{\tau}|_{S_{\mu}}(u_{n})\|\to 0
;

(iii)

\{u_{n}\}

E
;

(iv)

\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq k+1
; moreover,
\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq 1

.

Remark 8.2
.

For
\tau\in I
such that
(c_{\tau}^{k})^{\prime}
, the derivative of
c_{\tau}^{k}
with respect to
\tau
, exists, the results in Theorem

]
.

In the following, we study a special situation when

. In this case, with an additional condition (

), we can obtain the location information of the bounded Palais-Smale sequence that is established in Theorem

. This observation is useful to search for sign-changing critical points.

Proposition 8.3
.

Assume that the assumptions in Theorem
8.1
hold with both
k=1
and
k=2
. Let
F^{*}\subset S_{\mu}
be a closed set satisfying

\displaystyle F^{*}\cap(-F^{*})\cap S_{\mu}=\emptyset,\quad\text{where }-F^{*}=\bigl\{-u:u\in F^{*}\bigr\}.

(8.4)

For almost every
\tau\in I
, if
c=c_{\tau}^{1}=c_{\tau}^{2}
, there exist sequences

\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}\backslash(-F^{*}\cup F^{*})
and
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
such that, as

n\to+\infty
,

(i)

\Phi_{\tau}(u_{n})\to c
;

(ii)

\bigl\|\nabla\Phi_{\tau}|_{S_{\mu}}(u_{n})\|\to 0
;

(iii)

\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
E
;

(iv)

\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq 1
.

We present two topological lemmas preparing the proof of Proposition

.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
A\subset(0,1)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a closed set and let the map
P
be defined as

\displaystyle P:[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1},\quad(t,s)\mapsto s.

Then
[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\backslash A
is not path-connected is equivalent to that
A
contains a continuous closed curve
\mathcal{C}
such that the degree
\deg(P|_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{C},\mathbb{S}^{1})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Sufficiency:
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a continuous closed curve contained in
(0,1)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that
\deg(P|_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{C},\mathbb{S}^{1})\neq 0
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\deg(P|_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{C},\mathbb{S}^{1})=1
. After a continuous deformation, we assume that
\mathcal{C}=\{\frac{1}{2}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\backslash\mathcal{C}
is not path-connected. Since
\mathcal{C}\subset A
, we get that
[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\backslash A
is not path-connected.

Necessity:

Let
A
be an union of single points, then
[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\backslash A
is path-connected. Moreover, if any path-connected branch of
A
can continuously contract into a single point, then
[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\backslash A
is path-connected. Let
A_{1}
, not being a single point, be a path-connected branch of
A
. Next we prove that
A_{1}
can continuously contract into a single point provided that any continuous closed curve
\mathcal{C}\subset A_{1}
satisfies
\deg(P|_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{C},\mathbb{S}^{1})=0
. Let us take
p\in A_{1}
. For any other point
\bar{p}\in A_{1}
, since
A_{1}
is path-connected, we can find a continuous closed curve
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\subset A_{1}
such that
p,\bar{p}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
. By
\deg(P|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}},\widetilde{\mathcal{C}},\mathbb{S}^{1})=0
it follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
can continuously contract into the single point
p_{1}
. This shows that
A_{1}
can continuously contract into a single point, completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a continuous closed curve contained in
(0,1)\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
satisfying
\deg(P|_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{C},\mathbb{S}^{1})\neq 0
. There exist
t\in(0,1)
and
s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that
(t,s)\in\mathcal{C}
and
(t,-s)\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be parameterized by
\epsilon\in[-1,1]
, that is
\mathcal{C}=\{(t(\epsilon),s(\epsilon)):\epsilon\in[-1,1]\}
for some
(t(\epsilon),s(\epsilon))\in C([-1,1],(0,1)\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
satisfying
(t(1),s(1))=(t(-1),s(-1))
. Allowing
(t(\epsilon_{1}),s(\epsilon_{1}))=(t(\epsilon_{2}),s(\epsilon_{2}))
for
\epsilon_{1}\neq\epsilon_{2}
, we can assume that
s(\epsilon-1)=-s(\epsilon)
for
\epsilon\in[0,1]
since
\deg(P|_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{C},\mathbb{S}^{1})\neq 0
. We consider the function

\displaystyle g(\epsilon)=t(\epsilon)-t(\epsilon-1),\quad\epsilon\in[0,1].

Without loss of generality, assuming
g(0)\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle g(1)=t(1)-t(0)=-(t(0)-t(-1))=-g(0)\geq 0.

By continuity, there exists
\epsilon_{0}\in[0,1]
such that
g(\epsilon_{0})=0
, that is
t(\epsilon_{0})=t(\epsilon_{0}-1)
. Recalling
s(\epsilon_{0}-1)=-s(\epsilon_{0})
, the pair of points
(t(\epsilon_{0}),s(\epsilon_{0}))
and
(t(\epsilon_{0}-1),s(\epsilon_{0}-1))
satisfies our requirement. The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Proposition
8.3
.

By Remark

we assume that
(c_{\tau}^{2})^{\prime}
exists. Let
\{\tau_{n}\}\subset I
be a monotone increasing sequence converging
\tau

[

]

, we obtain a fixed

(ii)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau}(\gamma_{n}(t,s))\geq c-\varepsilon_{n},\quad(t,s)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}.

In view of (

(a)

\displaystyle\max_{(t,s)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{0}}\Phi_{\tau}(\hat{\gamma}_{n}(t,s))\leq c+\varepsilon_{n}

(b)

\hat{\gamma}_{n}(t,s)\in B(0,R-1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau}(\hat{\gamma}_{n}(t,s))\geq c-\varepsilon_{n},\quad(t,s)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{0},

9
Saddle-point type sign-changing solutions

9.1
Approximating problems with a parameter in the nonlinear term

As already anticipated in the introduction, we can construct minimax structures by generalizing the mountain pass structure and obtain Palais-Smale sequences. However, in the present setting the existence of a bounded Palais-Smale sequence at the minimax level, is not straightforward. To overcome this issue, we follow the framework in
[
3
]
and introduce the family of functionals

\displaystyle E_{\tau}(u,\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\tau}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx,

depending on the parameter
\tau\in[1/2,1]
. The main idea is to apply Theorem
8.1
. In the following lemma we show that there exists the uniformly minimax geometry.

Lemma 9.1
.

Suppose
p_{c}<p<2^{*}
. Let
k
be a positive integer and let

\displaystyle\mu\in\bigl\{\mu>0:\exists\rho>0\quad\text{such that}\quad\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\mu+\frac{2}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}<\rho\bigr\}.

(9.1)

Then there exist two odd functions
\gamma_{i,k}:\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\to S_{\mu}(\Omega)
where
i=0,1
, such that the set

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}:=\big\{\gamma\in C([0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{k-1},S_{\mu}(\Omega)):\forall t\in[0,1],\gamma(t,\cdot)\text{ is odd},\gamma(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0,k},\text{ and }\gamma(1,\cdot)=\gamma_{1,k}\big\}

(9.2)

is not empty and is independent of
\tau
, and

\displaystyle c_{\tau}^{k}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}}\sup_{(t,s)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}E_{\tau}(\gamma(t,s),\Omega)>\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}\max\big\{E_{\tau}(\gamma_{0,k}(s),\Omega),E_{\tau}(\gamma_{1,k}(s),\Omega)\big\}.

(9.3)

Further, we have
c^{k}_{\tau}\to c^{k}_{1}
as
\tau\to 1^{-}
.

Proof.

Let us define

\displaystyle\gamma_{0,k}:\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\to S_{\mu}(\Omega),\quad(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{i}.

Note that
\gamma_{0,k}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
is exactly the set
\mathbb{M}_{k}
given in (
7.1
). If
\rho>\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\mu
we have
\gamma_{0,k}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
. Let
\rho
satisfy the inequality in (
9.1
). By Hölder inequality, the assumption (
9.1
) and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.2
), for all
\tau\in[1/2,1]
, we get

\displaystyle\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}E_{\tau}(\gamma_{0,k}(s),\Omega)\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\mu<\frac{1}{2}\rho-\frac{1}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}\leq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\rho}^{\mu}}E_{1}(u,\Omega)\leq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\rho}^{\mu}}E_{\tau}(u,\Omega).

(9.4)

Let us take
k
non-zero
H_{0}^{1}
functions
v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{k}
such that
\text{supp}\ v_{i}\cap\text{supp}\ v_{j}=\emptyset
and so
v_{i}
is orthogonal to
v_{j}
both in
H_{0}^{1}
and
L^{2}
for
i\neq k
. Denoting
v_{i,t}(x):=t^{N/2}v_{i}(tx)
with
t>1
. For any
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
, we can check that
v_{i,t}\in S_{\mu}(\Omega)
, and that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{i,t}|^{2}dx=t^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}dx.

So
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{i,t}|^{2}dx>\rho
and
v_{i,t}\notin\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}}
for
t
sufficiently large (independent of
\tau
). Moreover, for
u=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}v_{i}
with
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
, we have
u_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}v_{i,t}
where
u_{t}=t^{N/2}u(tx)
and it holds that
u_{t}\in S_{\mu}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle E_{\tau}(u_{t},\Omega)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\tau t^{\frac{(p-2)N}{2}}}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}dx\leq\max_{i}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{p}\int_{\Omega}|v_{i}|^{p}dx\geq k^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\min_{i}\int_{\Omega}|v_{i}|^{p}dx.

Then we get

\displaystyle E_{\tau}(u_{t},\Omega)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\max_{i}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{i}|^{2}dx-\frac{t^{\frac{(p-2)N}{2}}}{2p}k^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\min_{i}\int_{\Omega}|v_{i}|^{p}dx,

and since
p>p_{c}
,

\displaystyle E_{\tau}(u_{t},\Omega)<\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}E_{1}(\gamma_{0,k}(s),\Omega)\leq\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}E_{\tau}(\gamma_{0,k}(s),\Omega),\quad\tau\in[\frac{1}{2},1],

for
t
sufficiently large independent of
\tau
and of
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
. Now we take
w_{i}=v_{i,t}
with
t
sufficiently large and independent of
\tau
. Without loss of generality, we also assume that
w_{i}
is non-negative in
\Omega
. Define

\displaystyle\gamma_{1,k}:\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\to S_{\mu}(\Omega),\quad(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}w_{i}.

Then any
u\in\gamma_{1,k}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
does not belong to
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
and

\displaystyle\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}E_{\tau}(\gamma_{1,k}(s),\Omega)<\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}E_{\tau}(\gamma_{0,k}(s),\Omega).

(9.5)

We define the subspace
V_{k}=\text{span}\{\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{1},\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{2},\cdots,\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{k};w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{k}\}
. Note that
d=\dim(V_{k})\leq 2k
. Let
R
be an operator (in
L^{2}(\Omega)
) such that
R=I
on
V_{k}^{\perp}
,
R(\sqrt{\mu}\phi_{i})=w_{i}
,
i=1,2,\cdots,k
. Possibly after permutations, we can choose
R
such that
R\big|_{V_{k}}\in SO(d)
(actually, there are infinitely many different choices of
R
). Now, since
SO(d)
is connected, there is a continuous path
\tilde{\gamma}:\,[0,1]\rightarrow SO(d)
such that
\tilde{\gamma}(0)=I
,
\tilde{\gamma}(1)=R\big|_{V_{k}}
. Let us define the map

\displaystyle\gamma:[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\to S_{\mu}(\Omega),\quad(t,a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\tilde{\gamma}(t).

It is clear that
\gamma
is continuous,
\gamma(t,\cdot)
is odd for all
t\in[0,1]
, and
\gamma(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0,k}(\cdot)
,
\gamma(1,\cdot)=\gamma_{1,k}(\cdot)
. Hence,
\Gamma_{k}
is not empty.

For any
s\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
, we have
\gamma_{0,k}(s)\in\mathcal{B}^{\mu}_{\rho}
and
\gamma_{1,k}(s)\notin\mathcal{B}^{\mu}_{\rho}
. By continuity, for any
\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}
, there exists
(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
such that
\gamma(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{U}_{\rho}^{\mu}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\max_{(t,s)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}E_{\tau}(\gamma(t,s),\Omega)\geq E_{\tau}(\gamma(t^{\prime},s^{\prime}),\Omega)\geq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\rho}^{\mu}}E_{\tau}(u,\Omega),

and so, combining (
9.4
) and (
9.5
) we conclude

\displaystyle c_{\tau}^{k}\geq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\rho}^{\mu}}E_{\tau}(u,\Omega)>\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}E_{\tau}(\gamma_{0,k}(s),\Omega)>\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}E_{\tau}(\gamma_{1,k}(s),\Omega).

Finally, we prove
c^{k}_{\tau}\to c^{k}_{1}
as
\tau\to 1^{-}
. On the one hand, for any
u\in S_{\mu}(\Omega)
and
\tau\in[1/2,1]
, we have
E_{\tau}(u,\Omega)\geq E_{1}(u,\Omega)
. This shows that
\liminf_{\tau\to 1^{-}}c^{k}_{\tau}\geq c^{k}_{1}
. On the other hand, it is well known that
\limsup_{\tau\to 1^{-}}c^{k}_{\tau}\leq c^{k}_{1}
see, e.g.
[
16
]
. Hence, we have
c^{k}_{\tau}\to c^{k}_{1}
as
\tau\to 1^{-}

and complete the proof.
∎

Remark 9.2
.

It seems that the choice of
\gamma_{1,k}
in the proof of Lemma
9.1
, and so the set
\Gamma_{k}
, depends on
\rho
. However, for
\mu
satisfying (

), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\mu<\rho<\left(\frac{p}{2C_{p,N}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p\gamma_{p}-2}}\mu^{-\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{p\gamma_{p}-2}}

since

. Thus, we can take
\gamma_{1,k}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
outside
\mathcal{B}_{g(\mu)}^{\mu}
where

g(\mu)=\left(\frac{p}{2C_{p,N}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p\gamma_{p}-2}}\mu^{-\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{p\gamma_{p}-2}},

independent of the choice of
\rho
. Moreover, we provide a lower bound of
c_{\tau}^{k}
independent of
\rho
. We define

\displaystyle\Theta(\mu):=\bigl\{\rho>0:\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\mu+\frac{2}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}<\rho\bigr\}.

We observe that the assumption (
9.1
) holds if and only if
\Theta(\mu)\neq\emptyset
. Using (
9.4
) in the proof of Lemma

, for all
\tau\in[1/2,1]
we have

\displaystyle c_{\tau}^{k}\geq\sup_{\rho\in\Theta(\mu)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho-\frac{1}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}\right).

(9.6)

This estimate is useful to show that
c_{\tau}^{k}\to+\infty
as
\mu\to 0^{+}
.

Now we can apply Theorem
8.1
and Proposition
8.3
on the approximating problems.

Proposition 9.3
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
9.1
, for almost every
\tau\in[1/2,1]
, there exists a critical point
u_{\tau}^{k}
of
E_{\tau}(\cdot,\Omega)
constrained to
S_{\mu}(\Omega)
at level
c_{\tau}^{k}
, which solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\tau}^{k}+\lambda_{\tau}^{k}u_{\tau}^{k}=\tau|u_{\tau}^{k}|^{p-2}u_{\tau}^{k}&\text{in }\Omega,\\[4.5pt]
\displaystyle u_{\tau}^{k}(x)=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(9.7)

for some
\lambda_{\tau}^{k}\geq-C_{k}
where
C_{k}>0
is a constant depending on
k
and independent of
\tau
. The Morse index
m(u_{\tau}^{k})\leq k+2
. Moreover,
u_{\tau}^{1}
is positive in
\Omega
and
m(u_{\tau}^{1})\leq 2
; and if
c_{\tau}^{1}=c_{\tau}^{2}
,
u_{\tau}^{2}
can be chosen as a sign-changing function with
m(u_{\tau}^{2})\leq 2
. As
\mu\to 0^{+}
, we have
c_{\tau}^{k}\to+\infty
uniformly with respect to
\tau
.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
8.1
to the family of functionals
E_{\tau}(\cdot,\Omega)
, with
E=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
H=L^{2}(\Omega)
, and
\Gamma_{k}
defined in Lemma
9.1
. Setting

\displaystyle A(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\quad\text{and}\quad B(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx,

assumption (
8.1
) holds, since we have

\displaystyle u\in S_{\mu}(\Omega),\|u\|\to+\infty\quad\Rightarrow\quad A(u)\to+\infty.

Moreover, the unconstrained first and second derivatives of
E_{\tau}
are Hölder continuous on bounded sets of
S_{\mu}(\Omega)
. In this way, together with Lemma
9.1
, for almost every
\tau\in[1/2,1]
there exists a bounded Palais-Smale sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}(\Omega)
for the constrained functional
E_{\tau}(\cdot,\Omega)|_{S_{\mu}(\Omega)}
at level
c_{\tau}^{k}
, and
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
, such that
\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq k+1
. By Lemma
5.8
, we have
u_{n}\to u_{\tau}^{k}
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}
, and
u_{\tau}^{k}
is a constrained critical point, thus a solution to (
9.7
) for some Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{\tau}^{k}
. By Lemma
2.7
we have
m(u_{\tau}^{k})\leq k+2
. The existence of
C_{k}>0
depending on
k
and independent of
\tau\in[1/2,1]
such that
\lambda_{\tau}^{k}\geq-C_{k}
can be deduced immediately from the uniform boundedness of Morse index of
u_{\tau}^{k}
, by using arguments in the proof of Lemma
3.1
. Particularly, when
k=1
, the geometry presented in Lemma
9.1
degenerates to the mountain pass geometry and the positivity of
u_{\tau}^{1}
and
m(u_{\tau}^{1})\leq 2
can be proved using arguments in
[
6
]
. If
c_{\tau}^{1}=c_{\tau}^{2}
, we use Proposition
8.3
with
F^{*}=\overline{\mathcal{P}_{\delta}}
, where
\delta>0
satisfies (
2.4
). In view of Lemma
2.2
, we know (
8.4
) holds true. Thus we obtian a bounded Palais-Smale sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}(\Omega)\backslash(\overline{\mathcal{P}_{\delta}}\cup(\overline{-\mathcal{P}_{\delta}}))
for the constrained functional
E_{\tau}(\cdot,\Omega)|_{S_{\mu}(\Omega)}
at level
c_{\tau}^{2}
, and
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
, such that
\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq 1
. Then we can get a constrained critical point
u_{\tau}^{2}\in S_{\mu}(\Omega)\backslash(\mathcal{P}_{\delta}\cup(-\mathcal{P}_{\delta}))
, yielding that
u_{\tau}^{2}
is sign-changing, with
m(u_{\tau}^{2})\leq 2
.

Finally, we study the asymptotic behavior of
c_{\tau}^{k}
as
\mu\to 0^{+}
. Let

\displaystyle f(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\rho-\frac{1}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}},\quad\rho>0.

Computing directly we get that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}-1},

and

\displaystyle f(\rho_{\mu})=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p\gamma_{p}}\right)\rho_{\mu}=\max_{\rho>0}f(\rho),

where

\displaystyle\rho_{\mu}=\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{p}C_{p,N}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p\gamma_{p}-2}}\mu^{-\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{p\gamma_{p}-2}}.

Since
\gamma_{p}<1
and
p\gamma_{p}>2
, for
\mu>0
sufficiently small we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\mu<\left(1-\frac{2}{p\gamma_{p}}\right)\rho_{\mu},

showing that
\Theta(\mu)\neq\emptyset
, where
\Theta(\mu)
is introduced in Remark
9.2
. By (
9.6
), we obtain that, as
\mu\to 0^{+}
,

\displaystyle c_{\tau}^{k}\geq\sup_{\rho\in\Theta(\mu)}f(\rho)=f(\rho_{\mu})=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p\gamma_{p}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{p}C_{p,N}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p\gamma_{p}-2}}\mu^{-\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{p\gamma_{p}-2}}\to+\infty.

The proof is complete.
∎

9.2
Proof of Theorem
1.4

As a consequence of Proposition
3.7
, we have the following result which is prepared to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 9.4
.

Let
p_{c}<p<2^{*}
and
\{u_{n}\}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be a sequence of solutions to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{n}+\lambda_{n}u_{n}=\tau_{n}u_{n}^{p-1}&\text{in }\Omega,\\[4.5pt]
u(x)=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(9.8)

where
\tau_{n}\to 1
and
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{2}dx=\mu,\quad m(u_{n})\leq\bar{k},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

for some
\mu>0
and
\bar{k}\in\mathbb{N}
, and that

\displaystyle\text{the sequence of the energy levels }\{E_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n},\Omega)\}\text{ is bounded}.

(9.9)

Then the sequences
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\{u_{n}\}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
must be bounded. In addition,
\{u_{n}\}
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
E_{1}(\cdot,\Omega)
constrained on
S_{\mu}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.7
, the sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is bounded. Then using Lemma
3.2
we know
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}
and so in
L^{p}
. By (
9.8
) we get that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}
immediately and complete the proof.
∎

Finally we complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Recall that

\displaystyle\Theta(\mu):=\bigl\{\rho>0:\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\mu+\frac{2}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}<\rho\bigr\}

is given in Remark
9.2
and we take
k=2
here. Firstly, we prove that
\Theta(\mu)\neq\emptyset
is equivalent to
0<\mu<\mu_{2}
where

\displaystyle\mu_{2}:=\sup\bigl\{\mu>0:\exists\rho>0\quad\text{such that}\quad\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\mu+\frac{2}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}<\rho\bigr\}.

(9.10)

From
p\gamma_{p}>2
we see that
\mu_{2}<+\infty
. Moreover, for any
\rho>0
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\mu_{2}+\frac{2}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu_{2}^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}\geq\rho

by using the definition of
\mu_{2}
. Thus
\Theta(\mu_{2})=\emptyset
. Next it suffices to show that for
\mu\in(0,\mu_{2})
it holds that
\Theta(\mu)\neq\emptyset
. By the definition of
\mu_{2}
, there exist
\omega_{n}<\mu_{2}
and
\rho_{n}\in\Theta(\omega_{n})
such that
\omega_{n}\to\mu_{2}
. Since
p\gamma_{p}>2
we get that
\rho_{n}
is bounded, and up to a subsequence, we assume that
\rho_{n}\to\rho_{0}
for some
\rho_{0}>0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\mu_{2}+\frac{2}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu_{2}^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho_{0}^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}=\rho_{0}.

Then, for
\mu<\mu_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\mu+\frac{2}{p}C_{p,N}^{p}\mu^{\frac{p(1-\gamma_{p})}{2}}\rho_{0}^{\frac{p\gamma_{p}}{2}}<\rho_{0},

yielding that
\Theta(\mu)\neq\emptyset
.

Let
0<\mu<\mu_{2}
. By Proposition
9.3
, for almost every
\tau\in[1/2,1]
, there exist critical points
u_{\tau}^{1}
and
u_{\tau}^{2}
of
E_{\tau}(\cdot,\Omega)
constrained to
S_{\mu}
at levels
c_{\tau}^{1}
and
c_{\tau}^{2}
respectively, which solves (
1.4
) for some
\lambda=\lambda_{\tau}^{k}\geq-C_{k}
,
k=1,2
. The Morse index
m(u_{\tau}^{1})\leq 2
and
m(u_{\tau}^{2})\leq 4
. Moreover,
u_{\tau}^{1}
is positive; and if
c_{\tau}^{1}=c_{\tau}^{2}
,
u_{\tau}^{2}
can be chosen as a sign-changing function with
m(u_{\tau}^{2})\leq 2
. From now on we emphasize that
\Omega=B_{1}
in this proof. As
\mu\to 0^{+}
, we have
c_{\tau}^{1},c_{\tau}^{2}\to+\infty
uniformly with respect to
\tau
. If
c_{\tau}^{1}<c_{\tau}^{2}
, we have
u_{\tau}^{1}\neq u_{\tau}^{2}
. By Theorem
1.2
, there exists
\mu^{**}_{p}\in(0,\min\{\mu_{2},\mu^{*}_{p,0}\})
such that for
0<\mu<\mu^{**}_{p}
, the function
u_{\tau}^{2}
is sign-changing. From above discussion we always assume that
0<\mu<\mu^{**}_{p}
and
u_{\tau}^{2}
is sign-changing with
m(u_{\tau}^{2})\leq 4
. Observer that
c_{1}^{2}\leq E_{\tau}(u_{\tau}^{2})=c_{\tau}^{2}\leq c_{1/2}^{2}
. Hence, we can take a sequence
\tau_{n}\to 1^{-}
, with a corresponding sequence
\{u_{n}\}=\{u_{\tau_{n}}\}
whose energy is uniformly bounded with respect to
n
. By Proposition
9.4
,
\{u_{n}\}
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence of
E_{1}
constrained to
S_{\mu}
. Up to a subsequence, there exists
u_{\text{sc},\mu}\in S_{\mu}
such that
u_{n}\to u_{\text{sc},\mu}
strongly in
H_{0}^{1}(B_{1})
, and thus
u_{\text{sc},\mu}
is a constrained critical point of
E_{1}
constrained to
S_{\mu}
. By Lagrange multiplier principle,
u_{\text{sc},\mu}
solves (
1.3
) for a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda=\lambda_{\text{sc},\mu}
. It is relatively standard to see that
m(u_{\text{sc},\mu})\leq 4
.

Now we prove that
u_{\text{sc},\mu}
is indeed sign-changing. Take
\rho>0
such that
\{u_{n}\}=\{u_{\tau_{n}}\}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\mu}
. Let
V_{\tau}
be defined in (
5.11
). It is clear that
V_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n})=0
. By Lemma
5.7
, there exists
\delta>0
depending on
\rho
and on
\mu
but independent of
n
such that
u_{n}\notin D^{*}_{\delta}
, that is
u_{n}\in S^{*}(\delta)
. Thus
u_{\text{sc},\mu}\in\overline{S^{*}(\delta)}
, showing that
u_{\text{sc},\mu}
is sign-changing.

Recall that

\displaystyle E(u_{\text{sc},\mu})=c_{1}^{2}\to+\infty\quad\text{as }\mu\to 0^{+}.

(9.11)

To complete the proof, it needs to prove that
\lambda_{\text{sc},\mu}\to+\infty
as
\mu\to 0^{+}
. Observe that
m(u_{\text{sc},\mu})\leq 4
, and, by Lemma
3.1
and by Proposition
3.7
we complete the proof.
