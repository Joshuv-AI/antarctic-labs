1. Overview
Chalk-River Unidentified Deposit (CRUD) forms on the outer surface of fuel cladding
during Light Water Reactor (LWR) operation when coolant chemistry additives and
corrosion products released from structural materials deposit on fuel rods. CRUD leads
to two important phenomena that cause operational concern: CRUD-induced Localized
Corrosion (CILC), and CRUD-Induced Power Shift (CIPS).[1] New thermochemistry
and boiling models have been developed in recent years to better represent CRUD
growth, thermal transport and dryout behavior.[2, 3] These models have been
implemented in the LANL MAMBA3D code[4, 5]and provide a more physical
description of two-phase flow and heat transport in porous CRUD deposits.
To support stakeholder adoption of these improved modeling capabilities, this effort
focuses on the implementing the CRUD two-phase flow model, referred to here as
CTPM, into VERA-MAMBA. This milestone also received technical support from
ORNL (under a separate milestone) for code review, benchmarking and MultiApp
integration. This report documents the initial implementation and testing of the CTPM
in VERA-MAMBA. Additional validation, benchmarking against MAMBA3D and
experiments, and code cleanup are needed before broad production use.

2. Introduction
CRUD forms on fuel rods when chemical additives in the coolant and corrosion
products released from reactor structural materials deposit on the outer surface of the
cladding during Light Water Reactor (LWR) operation [1]. CRUD can affect reactor
operation and fuel performance through two primary mechanisms: (i) CRUD-Induced
Localized Corrosion (CILC) and (ii) CRUD-Induced Power Shift (CIPS), also
associated with axial offset anomaly behavior. CIPS is also associated with axial offset
anomaly behavior and can occur when boron concentrates within CRUD deposits,
altering the local neutron absorption distribution and shifting the axial power profile.
MAMBA3D, the MPO Advanced Model for Boron Analysis, was developed under the
Consortium for Advanced Simulation of Light Water Reactors (CASL) to model
CRUD growth, chemistry, and transport behavior. Recent development efforts at

 1

LANL have implemented new thermochemistry and heat-transport capabilities in
MAMBA3D, including an enhanced two-phase flow model for CRUD boiling and
dryout. These capabilities enable more physical prediction of CRUD composition,
porosity, and dryout behavior.
VERA-MAMBA is widely used by researchers in nuclear industry to perform CRUD-
related analyses,[6] including: Prediction of CRUD buildup on fuel rods during reactor
operation; Assessment of CIPS and axial offset anomaly risk due to boron concentration
in CRUD; Optimization of coolant chemistry, including pH, lithium, hydrogen, and
boric acid concentrations; Evaluation of CRUD effects on fuel performance such as
CILC and CIPS.
The existing VERA-MAMBA still uses the wick boiling model, which assumes that
steam vapor leaves CRUD through chimneys (pores with a larger radius in CRUD)
while liquid is supplied through wick region (pores with smaller radius in CRUD). The
velocity of Darcy flow is determined through the application of single-phase energy
conservation equation.[7, 8] Although this model has been useful for CRUD analysis,
it has several limitations. First, the model does not account for variations in CRUD
properties, such as the radius distribution of the chimneys and the wick pores. In
addition, this model lacks the ability to predict the dryout of CRUD. Dryout has been
proven to be important, particularly in regions near the cladding surface when the fuel
rod is operated at a high heat flux.[8-10] Therefore, it limits its applicability in high
heat-flux regions where CRUD may partially dryout and where local changes in
saturation can strongly affect boron concentration, thermal resistance, and corrosion
risk.
The new CTPM model improves the physical representation of two-phase flow and heat
transfer in porous CRUD.[2, 3] It accounts for capillary-pressure-driven liquid-vapor
distribution, Darcy flow of liquid and vapor phases, and boiling heat transfer with phase
change. Key features of the CTPM include: capillary pressure-saturation relationships
based on the pore size distribution model; Darcy flow treatment for both liquid and
vapor phases; liquid saturation tracking over the range 0≤s≤1; continuous transition
among liquid-saturated, two-phase, and vapor-dominated zones based on local thermal-
hydraulic conditions; coupling of species diffusion and advection to local saturation;
and prediction of CRUD dryout regions under high-heat-flux conditions.
To support stakeholder use of the improved boiling and dryout model, the objective of
this work is to implement the model into VERA-MAMBA. This report documents the
technical basis and implementation for the initial deployment of the two-phase flow
model.
3. Description of code changes
This section summarizes the code changes made to implement CTPM in VERA-
MAMBA. The implementation adds pore-size-distribution functions, additional nodal
state variables, a radial CRUD class for two-phase heat and species transport, pressure-
and temperature-dependent borated water properties, and a model-selection option.

3.1 Pore size distribution functions
The functions required to evaluate the CRUD pore-size distribution are implemented in
CRUD_Properties.f90. These functions provide the local relationships among capillary
pressure, liquid saturation, relative permeability, effective thermal conductivity, and
other porous-media properties required by the CTPM. In particular, the pore size
distribution model is used to compute quantities such as:
Capillary pressure, pc; Liquid saturation, s; Liquid and vapor relative permeabilities;
Effective thermal conductivity; Liquid/vapor velocities; and other properties required
for CRUD property calculation. These properties are used directly by the radial
temperature solver described in Section 3.3.

3.2 New Two-Phase Flow variables in CRUD_Node.f90
Additional state variables were added to the CRUDNode type in CRUD_Node.f90 to
store the local CTPM solution at each radial node. These variables describe the capillary
pressure, saturation, phase pressures, and Darcy flow velocities of the liquid and vapor
phases.
TYPE :: CRUDNode
  ! ... existing variables ...
  ! CTPM state variables
  REAL(SRK) :: pc = 0.0_SRK ! Capillary pressure, MPa
  REAL(SRK) :: sat = 1.0_SRK ! Liquid saturation, -
  REAL(SRK) :: ul = 0.0_SRK ! Liquid velocity, cm/s
  REAL(SRK) :: uv = 0.0_SRK ! Vapor velocity, cm/s
  REAL(SRK) :: pl = 0.0_SRK ! Liquid pressure, MPa
  REAL(SRK) :: pv = 0.0_SRK ! Vapor pressure, MPa
END TYPE

The default state corresponds to fully liquid-saturated CRUD, with zero capillary
pressure and zero phase velocities.
3.3 CRUDRadial_CTPM Class in CRUD_Radial_CTPM.f90
A new file, CRUD_Radial_CTPM.f90, was added to implement the CTPM radial
solution. This file defines a new class, CRUDRadial_CTPM, which extends the existing
CRUDRadial class in VERA-MAMBA.
The new class overrides the standard radial temperature and species transport
calculations so that heat transfer, boiling, dryout, and species transport are coupled to
local liquid saturation and two-phase flow behavior. The inheritance structure is:
TYPE, EXTENDS(CRUDRadial) :: CRUDRadial_CTPM
CONTAINS
  PROCEDURE :: calcRadTemperature => calcRadTemperature_CRUDRadial_CTPM
  PROCEDURE :: calcSpeciesTransport => calcSpeciesTransport_CRUDRadial_CTPM
END TYPE CRUDRadial_CTPM

3.3.1 Adaptive radial temperature solver
The CTPM temperature solver marches adaptively from the coolant/CRUD interface
toward the cladding surface. During the radial march, the CRUD layer is divided into
three possible regions: Liquid-Saturated Zone (LSZ) where the pores are fully filled
with liquid, and the saturation is s=1; Two-Phase Zone (TPZ) where the liquid and
vapor coexist in the pores. The saturation varies between 0 and 1, and the local
temperature follows the borated-water saturation temperature; Vapor-Saturated Zone
(VSZ) where the CRUD is dry or nearly dry, with s≈0.
The overall logic of the adaptive solver is:
SUBROUTINE calcRadTemperature_CRUDRadial_CTPM(this)
  ! Initialize radial integration at the coolant/CRUD interface
  xg(0) = 0.0_SRK ! Radial position, cm
  pcg(0) = CTPM_pcLL ! Initial capillary pressure, MPa
  Tg(0) = T_surf ! Boundary temperature, K
  sg(0) = 1.0_SRK ! Initial liquid saturation
  zone = 1 ! Start in the liquid-saturated zone
  n = 0
  DO WHILE (xg(n) < L_cm) ! March inward
 SELECT CASE(zone)
 CASE(1) ! Liquid-saturated zone
 CALL lsz_step(...)
        IF (Tg(n+1) >= Tsat_loc) zone = 2
 CASE(2) ! Two-phase zone
 CALL tpz_step(...)
        IF (pcg(n+1) >= CTPM_pcUL .OR. sg(n+1) <= TOL_S) zone = 3
 CASE(3) ! Vapor-saturated zone
 CALL vsz_step(...)
 END SELECT
 n = n + 1
  ENDDO
  ! Project adaptive grid solution onto CRUD radial nodes
END SUBROUTINE

After the adaptive solution is completed, the temperature, capillary pressure, saturation, phase pressures,
and phase velocities are projected onto the fixed radial nodes used by VERA-MAMBA.

3.3.2 Liquid Saturated Zone (LSZ)
In the liquid-saturated zone, the capillary pressure is held at the lower limit, the
saturation is set to 1, and the temperature is updated using the effective thermal
conductivity of saturated CRUD.
CASE(1) ! LSZ
  dx_sp = MIN(DX_LSZ, L_cm - xg(n))
  xg(n+1) = xg(n) + dx_sp
  pcg(n+1) = CTPM_pcLL

 4

  sg(n+1)   = 1.0_SRK
  keff = keff_from_pc(CTPM_pcLL, ks, kl, kv, L_cm, eps)
  Tg(n+1) = Tg(n) + qw * dx_sp / keff ! Temperature update
  Tsat_loc = Tsat_borated(pvg(n+1), CB)
  IF (Tg(n+1) >= Tsat_loc) THEN ! Check for onset of boiling
 zone = 2
  ENDIF

The solver transitions from the LSZ to the TPZ when the local temperature reaches the
borated-water saturation temperature.
3.3.3 Two-Phase Zone (TPZ)
In the two-phase zone, the solution is advanced in capillary pressure rather than by a
fixed spatial step. The spatial step is determined from the local energy balance,
including heat conduction and latent heat effects.
CASE(2) ! TPZ
  ! Select capillary pressure step size
  IF ((pcg(n) - CTPM_pcLL) < 0.5_SRK * 0.01_SRK) THEN
    dpc = 0.000122_SRK
  ELSE
    dpc = 0.00272_SRK
  ENDIF
  pcg(n+1) = pcg(n) + dpc
  ! Compute properties at the current state
  CALL crud_psd(pcg(n), L_cm, eps, Tg(n), plg(n), pvg(n), &
 sg(n), K_per, krl, krv, keffpc, keffs, &
 keffl, keffv, mulv, lamg(n))
  pl_pred = plg(n) - (1.0_SRK - lamg(n)) * dpc
  pv_pred = pl_pred + pcg(n+1)
  ! Compute properties at the next state
  CALL crud_psd(pcg(n+1), L_cm, eps, Tg(n), pl_pred, pv_pred, &
                  sg(n+1), K_per1, krl1, krv1, keffpc1, keffs1, &
 keffl1, keffv1, mulv1, lamg(n+1))
  plg(n+1) = plg(n) - 0.5_SRK * &
               ((1.0_SRK - lamg(n)) + (1.0_SRK - lamg(n+1))) * dpc
  pvg(n+1) = plg(n+1) + pcg(n+1)
  Tg(n+1) = Tsat_borated(pvg(n+1), CB)

The properties of borated water are evaluated as functions of local vapor pressure and
boron concentration. The spatial step is then computed from the energy balance:
  dx = dpc / (2.0_SRK * qw) *( lamg(n+1) * keffpc1 * eta1 &
         + 1.0E7_SRK * hfg1 * K_per1 / mulv1 * lamg(n+1) * (1.0_SRK -
lamg(n+1)) + lamg(n) * keffpc * eta &
         + 1.0E7_SRK * hfg * K_per / mulv * lamg(n) * (1.0_SRK - lamg(n)) )
xg(n+1) = MIN(xg(n) + dx, L_cm)

 5

The liquid and vapor Darcy velocities are computed from the capillary pressure
gradient:
  dpcdx = dpc / dx
  ulg(n) =    1.0E7_SRK * K_per * lamg(n) * (1.0_SRK - lamg(n)) * dpcdx / &
 (mulv * rhow(Tg(n)))
  uvg(n) = -1.0E7_SRK * K_per * lamg(n) * (1.0_SRK - lamg(n)) * dpcdx / &
              (mulv * rhos(Tg(n), pvg(n)))

The solver transitions from the TPZ to the VSZ when the upper capillary pressure limit
is reached or when the liquid saturation becomes sufficiently small:
   IF (pcg(n+1) >= CTPM_pcUL - 1.0E-12_SRK .OR. sg(n+1) <= TOL_S) THEN
 zone = 3
  ENDIF

3.3.3 Vapor-Saturated Zone (VPZ)
In the vapor zone, the liquid saturation is set to zero, the capillary pressure is held at
the upper capillary pressure limit, and the temperature is updated using the effective
thermal conductivity of dry CRUD.
CASE(3) ! VSZ
  dx_sp = MIN(DX_VSZ, L_cm - xg(n))
  xg(n+1) = xg(n) + dx_sp
  pcg(n+1) = CTPM_pcUL
  sg(n+1)     = 0.0_SRK
  lamg(n+1) = 0.0_SRK
  keff = keff_from_pc(CTPM_pcUL, ks, kl, kv, L_cm, eps)
  Tg(n+1) = Tg(n) + qw * dx_sp / keff

3.4 Species Transport
The CTPM implementation modifies the species transport calculation so that diffusion
and advection depend on the local liquid saturation and phase velocity predicted by
CTPM. The modified species transport routine is implemented in
CRUD_Radial_CTPM.f90:
SUBROUTINE calcSpeciesTransport_CRUDRadial_CTPM(this, id, D0, Ccool)
  DO i = 1, this%Nr-1
 sat2 = this%radial(i)%myNode%sat
 eps2 = this%radial(i)%myNode%xc(id_Por)
 ! Determine whether the node is dry
 dry2 = (sat2 <= sat_species_min) .OR. &
 (sat2 * eps2 <= liq_species_min)
 ! Effective diffusivity in the liquid phase
 IF (dry2) THEN
       D2 = 0.0_SRK

 6

 ELSE
 tort2 = this%radial(i)%myNode%getTortuosity()
 Dmol2 = D_mult * Diff_StokesEinstein(T, D0)
        D2 = sat2 * Dmol2 * (eps2 / tort2)
 ENDIF
 ! Liquid velocity at the cell face
 IF (dry2 .OR. dry3) THEN
        ul_face = 0.0_SRK
 ELSE
        ul_face = 0.5_SRK * (ul(i) + ul(i+1))
 ENDIF
 ! Advective flux through the face
 jflow2 = ul_face * A2
 ! Assemble finite-volume coefficients
  ENDDO
END SUBROUTINE

The key difference from the standard species transport model is that liquid-phase
diffusion is scaled by saturation: Deff=sDmolε/τ where: Deff is the effective diffusivity, s
is the liquid saturation, Dmol is the molecular diffusivity, ε is the CRUD porosity, and τ
is the tortuosity.
If a node or face is dry, liquid-phase diffusion and advection are set to zero. This allows
the model to capture the strong reduction in species transport through partially dried or
fully dried CRUD regions.
3.5 Borated water properties in Water_Properties.f90
Pressure- and temperature-dependent borated water property functions were added to
Water_Properties.f90. These functions are used by the CTPM temperature solver to
evaluate local boiling and latent heat effects. The added or updated properties include:
borated-water saturation temperature, Tsat_borated; saturated borated-water viscosity
or related transport property, etasat_borated; latent heat of vaporization, hfg_borated;
and density functions for liquid water and steam as functions of local temperature and
pressure.
  eta = etasat_borated(pvg(n), CB)
  hfg = hfg_borated(pvg(n), CB)
  eta1 = etasat_borated(pvg(n+1), CB)
  hfg1 = hfg_borated(pvg(n+1), CB)

These property functions allow the two-phase model to account for local pressure,
boron concentration, and temperature effects during boiling and dryout.
3.6 Model selection
The CTPM can be activated through the use_ctpm option in pymamba. The Python and
Cython interfaces, pymamba.py and cython.pyx, were updated to pass this option to
Fortran code. It can enable the CTPM using: pin.set_use_ctpm(True). If use_ctpm is

 7

set to False, or if it is not specified, VERA-MAMBA uses the original standard CRUD
model. During CRUD_Pin initialization, the radial CRUD grid is allocated using either
the standard CRUDRadial class or the new CRUDRadial_CTPM class:
IF (use_ctpm) THEN
  ALLOCATE(CRUDRadial_CTPM :: this%myGrid(nth, nz))
ELSE
  ALLOCATE(CRUDRadial :: this%myGrid(nth, nz))
ENDIF

This design preserves backward compatibility with the original VERA-MAMBA
implementation while allowing users to activate the new CTPM capability when
needed.
4. Initial code testing
The initial implementation tests were performed using the thermal hydraulic boundary
conditions and coolant chemistry data on a pin-resolved basis, including as heat flux,
TKE, boiling heat flux, and species concentrations, for several full reactor fuel cycles
provided by Robert at ORNL and Collins at Veracity.[11] The results for a
representative fuel rod, labeled as C5, are shown in this section.
Figure 1 shows the predicted CRUD property distribution for rod C5 using the original
VERA-MAMBA model. In this baseline calculation, only NiFe2O4 and Li2B4O7 (LTB)
deposition were enabled. It is seen that the CRUD primarily forms on the upper portion
of the rod, where local thermal-hydraulic conditions are more favorable for the
deposition and growth of CRUD. It is also seen that LTB is formed near the cladding
side of CRUD layer. As LTB deposits in this region, it fills the pore and decreases the
porosity to 0.

C5, predicted using the original VERA-MAMBA model with only NiFe2O4 and
Li2B4O7 (LTB) deposition enabled. Species concentrations are given in moles/cm3, and
temperature is in K. The color bars below each plot indicate the corresponding value
scales.

Figure 2 illustrates the CRUD property distributions predicted by the updated VERA-
MAMBA code with the CTPM model enabled using use_ctpm=True, for different heat

fluxes on cladding surface. Similar to the baseline calculation, CRUD is mainly forms
on the upper portion of the rod, and LTB deposition occurs near the cladding side of
the CRUD layer. In addition, because the chemical conversion from NiFe2O4 to
Ni2FeBO5 is implemented in the updated version, Ni2FeBO5 is also predicted to be
formed. [12, 13] The simulations show that the spatial extend of LTB region increases
with increasing cladding heat flux, suggesting that the dryout region expands as the heat
flux increases.

However, the LTB deposition region predicted by the updated VERA-MAMBA code
is larger than what is expected using MAMBA3D simulations. This behavior is traced
back to the rapid increase of the soluble species concentrations within the CRUD layer.
As shown in Figure 3, the boric acid and soluble lithium concentrations increase rapidly
within approximately 10 μm of the coolant side of the CRUD layer. This rapid increase
promotes LTB precipitation, which fills the local pore space and drives the porosity
toward zero. The steep concentration gradients indicate that the coupling between the
CTPM model and species transport strongly effects the predicted local chemistry.
Further investigation of this coupling of the properties calculated from CTPM with
species transport in VERA-MAMBA is needed to determine whether the enhanced
LTB precipitation is physically reasonable.
5. Future work
In this effort, the CTPM model was implemented in VERA-MAMBA to enable
stakeholders to use the model for CRUD modeling, including dryout and CILC-related
analyses within the VERA package. The initial implementation has been tested under
selected conditions; however, additional validation, benchmarking, and code cleanup
are needed before broader application.
Future work will focus on the following areas:
(i) Validation and benchmarking. The thermochemistry and heat-transport models will
be validated and benchmarked against both MAMBA3D results and available
experimental data. [14, 15] Although these models have been tested in MAMBA3D,
the transfer to VERA-MAMBA requires careful evaluation since the two codes differ
in several important ways. In particular, MAMBA3D uses time-dependent solvers for
species transport, whereas VERA-MAMBA uses time-independent analytical
equations for species transport. These differences may lead to different predictions of

 10

local species concentration, precipitation, CRUD composition, and boron
accumulation. Additional benchmarking is therefore needed to compare: CRUD
thickness predictions; CRUD composition and porosity; boron and lithium
concentration distributions; dryout onset and dryout-region size; and cladding surface
temperature.
(ii) Parameter reconciliation between MAMBA3D and VERA-MAMBA. The default
parameters used in MAMBA3D and VERA-MAMBA differ significantly. These
include parameters related to: species transport; CRUD material properties; surface
deposition rates; surface erosion rate; internal reaction kinetics; and Arrhenius
parameters for chemical reactions. A detailed parameter comparison will be performed
to identify differences between the two codes and determine which parameters should
be updated, calibrated, or preserved for VERA-MAMBA applications. Experimental
CRUD-growth data from various plant cycles including Callaway Cycle 9, Callaway
Cycle 10, and Seabrook Cycle 5 will be used to support this calibration. [14, 15]
(iii) Initial testing showed large dryout regions and significant increases in boric acid
concentration within CRUD. These results indicate that the coupling between Darcy
flow from CTPM and species transport formulation in VERA-MAMBA requires
further investigation. Future work will evaluate whether the current treatment of
diffusion and advection that strongly affects species transport, boric acid
concentration; and lithium tetraborate precipitation.
(iv) Code structure and cleanup. The code structure will be reviewed and improved to
better align with the object-oriented design of VERA-MAMBA. The CTPM
implementation will be organized so that model-specific functionality is clearly
separated from the standard CRUD model while preserving backward compatibility.
Future improvements should include: clearer class organization; improved separation
between standard and CTPM-specific methods; consistent naming conventions;
reduced code duplication; improved error handling; adding testing functions; and
clearer interfaces between thermal, species transport, and thermochemistry
calculations. In addition, the current implementation includes debug-related output and
temporary code used during model development and testing. Future work will remove
unnecessary debug output and produce a clean, maintainable version of the code.

6. Summary
This report documents the initial implementation of the CRUD Two-Phase Model, or
CTPM, in VERA-MAMBA. The implementation enhances the heat-transport
capability of VERA-MAMBA by allowing the code to represent liquid saturation,
capillary-pressure-driven liquid-vapor flow, and CRUD dryout. Initial testing
demonstrated that CRUD behavior is strongly dependent on local thermal-hydraulic
conditions. Rods with higher boiling heat flux showed increased CRUD thickness and
thermal resistance, particularly in upper axial regions. Large dryout regions were also
observed, accompanied by increased boric acid concentration within the CRUD layer.
These results suggest that the coupling of CTPM with species transport model in

 11

VERA-MAMBA should be further evaluated. Future work should focus on
benchmarking the implementation against MAMBA3D and experimental CRUD-
growth data, reconciling model parameters between the two codes, improving the
species transport treatment, and cleaning and restructuring the code to support long-
term maintenance and stakeholder use.
