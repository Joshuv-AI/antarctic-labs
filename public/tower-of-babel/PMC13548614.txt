Development and external validation of a prediction model for major adverse kidney events within 30 days in sepsis associated acute kidney injury: a multi-center retrospective clinical study
Authors: Qinyue Su, Yi Zhou, Jianxiao Chen, Qihua Ling, Yong Jiang, Wenjie Chen, Enqiang Mao, Hongping Qu, Ruilan Wang, Duming Zhu, Gang Zhao, Sheng Wang, Qian Wang, Changqing Zhu, Yuan Gao, Erzhen Chen, Ying Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMajor adverse kidney events within 30 days (MAKE30) are associated with poor outcomes in patients with sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI). This study aimed to develop and validate a nomogram-based prediction model for MAKE30 in SA-AKI patients.MethodsClinical and laboratory data were collected from SA-AKI patients admitted to eight tertiary Grade-A hospitals in Shanghai between January 2021 and October 2022, forming the development cohort. External validation was performed using data from SA-AKI patients treated at Ruijin Hospital between January 2017 and December 2019. A predictive nomogram was constructed using LASSO regression followed by multivariate logistic regression. Model performance was assessed using area under the curve (AUC), calibration plots, decision curve analysis (DCA), and clinical impact curves (CIC). The model was subsequently validated in the external validation cohort.ResultsA total of 531 SA-AKI patients were included, with 372 in the development cohort and 159 in the validation cohort. The incidence of MAKE30 was 55.6% in the development cohort and 62.9% in the external validation cohort. Seven independent predictors of MAKE30 were identified: AKI for 7 days, AKI stage, combination antimicrobial therapy, nutritional risk, maximum heart rate (HRmax), cumulative fluid balance D3, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score. The nomogram achieved an AUC of 0.829 (95% CI 0.787–0.870) in the development cohort and 0.776 (95% CI 0.698–0.855) in the validation cohort. Calibration plots, DCA, and CIC demonstrated favorable clinical applicability of the model.ConclusionsA prediction model incorporating seven readily available risk indicators can effectively predict the risk of MAKE30 in SA-AKI patients, facilitating early risk stratification and potential intervention.Trial registrationNone (Retrospective cohort study).Keywords: Sepsis, Acute kidney injury, Major adverse kidney event, Prediction model, Nomogram

Background

Sepsis is defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection [1]. Among its complications, sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI) is particularly common and associated with high mortality, prolonged kidney dysfunction, and considerable healthcare resource utilization [2–4]. To standardize clinically meaningful endpoints in AKI research, the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Disease workgroup proposed a composite outcome known as Major Adverse Kidney Events (MAKE) [5, 6]. MAKE encompasses death, dialysis initiation, and persistent renal dysfunction (PRD). This composite endpoint provides a more comprehensive reflection of kidney-related outcomes than any single complication alone. Across intensive care unit (ICU) populations, the reported incidence of Major adverse kidney events within 30 days (MAKE30) ranges from 13.6% to 38.9% [7–11]. However, existing prediction models for MAKE30 are either static [7, 12] or rely on complex deep learning architectures that are not easily deployable at the bedside [11]. Furthermore, a model tailored to the Chinese population remains lacking. To address this gap, we developed a nomogram-based predictive tool for MAKE30 in SA-AKI patients, aiming to enable timely, individualized risk stratification and ultimately improve clinical outcomes.

Methods

Study design and population

This retrospective study employed a multicenter design involving eight Grade-A tertiary hospitals in Shanghai, China. This study enrolled emergency department (ED) or ICU patients who were diagnosed with SA-AKI between January 2021 and October 2022. Sepsis was diagnosed according to the Sepsis-3.0 criteria (suspected or confirmed infection plus an acute increase in Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score of ≥ 2 points) [1]. SA-AKI was defined as AKI developing within 7 days after sepsis diagnosis. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pregnant or lactating women, or individuals under 18 years of age; (2) hospital length of stay shorter than 72 h; (3) pre-existing chronic kidney disease (stage 4–5), renal transplantation, or ongoing hemodialysis; (4) more than 20% of data missing. Both the derivation and external validation cohorts applied the identical set of inclusion and exclusion criteria.

The derivation cohort comprised 372 SA-AKI patients admitted to the ED or ICU of the eight participating hospitals between January 2021 and October 2022. These hospitals included: Ruijin Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (n = 152), Renji Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (n = 64), Shanghai Tenth People’s Hospital Affiliated to Tongji University School of Medicine (n = 40), Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (n = 36), Shuguang Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (n = 35), Zhongshan Hospital Affiliated to Fudan University (n = 24), and Longhua Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (n = 12). Shanghai First People’s Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (n = 9). The external validation cohort was derived from an entirely independent dataset, consisting of 159 SA-AKI patients admitted to the ED or ICU of Ruijin Hospital between January 2017 and December 2019. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by ethics committee of Ruijin Hospital ([2021] Research Approval No. 59). The requirement for informed consent was waived owing to retrospective study.

Definitions

AKI was defined as an increase in serum creatinine (Scr) of ≥ 26.4 µmol/L within 48 h, or a rise to at least 1.5 times the baseline value within 7 days [13]. Among the 531 patients, 416 (78.3%) had a Scr measurement within the prior year (derivation cohort: 298/372, 80.1%; validation cohort: 118/159, 74.2%). For the remaining 115 patients, baseline creatinine was estimated using the sex- and age-based MDRD formula [14] : Creatinine(𝜇mol/l) = 88.4 × (0.74 − 0.2 + 0.003×age)in females, and Creatinine(µmol/l) = 88.4 × (0.74 + 0.003×age)in males. The primary outcome was MAKE30, a composite of all-cause death, new renal replacement therapy, or PRD, defined as final inpatient Scr ≥ 200% of baseline [15, 16]. Outcomes were assessed within 30 days or at hospital discharge, whichever came first. Based on onset timing, AKI was classified as early (≤ 48 h after sepsis) or late (> 48 h) [17, 18]. Persistent AKI was defined as lack of recovery within 3 days or by the time of death. Recovery was defined as a ≥ 50% decrease in Scr and/or return to baseline without renal replacement therapy [19, 20]. AKI severity followed KDIGO stages: 1 (mild), 2 (moderate), and 3 (severe). Nutritional risk was assessed on admission using the NRS2002 tool, which evaluates nutritional status, disease severity, and age (≥ 70 years). Scores range from 0 to 7, with ≥ 3 indicating nutritional risk.

Data collection

The following baseline characteristics were collected: age, sex, body mass index (BMI), chronic medical history, source of septic infection, type of bacterial culture, nutritional risk on admission, critical illness-related scores on admission, AKI status within 7 days, persistent AKI, AKI stage, early AKI, fluid balance on days 1–3, cumulative fluid balance on days 2 and 3, and the presence or absence of septic shock. Vital signs and laboratory data were recorded using the worst value within the first 24 h of admission, including blood cell counts, liver and kidney function tests, serum electrolytes, coagulation parameters, cardiac enzyme profiles, and other relevant measures. Additionally, data on combination antimicrobial therapy within 7 days and other therapeutic interventions during the initial 3 days after admission were collected.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables following a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed variables were reported as median with interquartile range (IQR). Group comparisons for continuous variables were performed using the t-test or its non-parametric equivalent as appropriate. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages) or absolute counts, with differences assessed using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. To identify independent predictors of MAKE30 from a large set of candidate variables, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 10-fold cross-validation was first applied, followed by multivariate logistic regression. A nomogram was then constructed based on the results of the multivariate logistic regression analysis. The discriminatory performance of the nomogram for predicting MAKE30 was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and calibration curves were generated to assess the agreement between observed outcomes and predicted probabilities. Decision curve analysis (DCA) and clinical impact curves (CIC) were performed to evaluate the clinical net benefit of the predictive model. The accuracy of the nomogram was further validated using an external validation cohort. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 26.0 and R software (version 4.3.2). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the development and validation cohorts

The patient enrollment process is illustrated in the flow chart (Fig. 1). A total of 372 patients with SA-AKI were included in the development cohort, and 159 in the validation cohort. Baseline characteristics of both cohorts are summarized in Table 1. Due to the distinct data sources, the two cohorts were not fully balanced with respect to baseline features. In the development cohort, 207 of 372 patients (55.6%) experienced MAKE30 during hospitalization. Regarding the individual components, mortality occurred in 130 patients (34.9%), new renal replacement therapy (RRT) was initiated in 104 (28.0%), and PRD was observed in 105 (28.2%). In the validation cohort, 100 of 159 patients (62.9%) reached the composite endpoint. The incidence of mortality was 72 (45.3%), new RRT was 46 (28.9%), and PRD was 69 (43.4%). Detailed component distributions are presented in Table 2, and the overlap among MAKE30 components is visualized using a Venn diagram (Fig. 2).

Baseline characteristics of the development cohort by MAKE30 status

In the development cohort, baseline characteristics were compared between patients who developed MAKE30 and those who did not, as summarized in Table 3. The overall median age of participants was 71 years (interquartile range, 61 to 79), and 68.3% were male. Compared with the non-MAKE30 group, the MAKE30 group had significantly higher values across multiple clinical parameters, including body temperature, maximum heart rate (HRmax), SOFA score, APACHE II score, GCS score, albumin, admission serum creatinine, PT, APTT, CK-MB, MB, as well as fluid intake volumes on days 1, 2, and 3, fluid balance on days 1–3, and cumulative fluid balance on days 1–3 (all p < 0.05). In addition, the MAKE30 group exhibited a higher incidence of the following: MAP < 65 mmHg, nutritional risk, gram-positive bacterial infection, combination antimicrobial therapy, vasopressor administration, mechanical ventilation, persistent AKI, AKI for 7 days, and advanced AKI stage (all p < 0.05).

Impact of fluid balance on MAKE30

Figure 3 illustrates the daily fluid intake (enteral or parenteral) and output (urinary output and other fluid losses, including extracorporeal fluid elimination and drainage) during the first three days after admission, stratified by MAKE30 status. In the MAKE30 cohort, fluid balance was significantly higher on each of the first three days compared with the non-MAKE30 cohort. The median fluid balances on days 1, 2, and 3 were 12.70 (0.25, 28.25), 10.20 (− 0.45, 20.70), and 5.30 (− 6.15, 15.40) mL/kg, respectively. Cumulative fluid balance in the MAKE30 cohort increased progressively from a median of 12.70 (0.25, 28.25) mL/kg on day 1 to 21.00 (2.90, 46.65) mL/kg on day 2, and further to 25.80 (3.50, 54.20) mL/kg on day 3. All these values were consistently higher than those observed in the non-MAKE30 cohort.

Screening for predictive factors using LASSO and multivariate logistic regression

LASSO regression was applied to screen the 66 candidate predictors. Eleven variables with non-zero coefficients were retained based on the log λ (1-standard error) criterion (Fig. 4). These selected potential predictors included: HRmax, GCS score, NE%, fluid balance D3, cumulative fluid balance D3, nutritional risk, gram-positive bacteria, combination antimicrobial therapy, persistent AKI, AKI for 7days. Subsequently, multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent predictors of MAKE30. As shown in Table 4, the following factors remained independently associated with MAKE30 in patients with SA-AKI: AKI for 7 days (OR: 3.140, 95% CI: 1.632–6.042, p = 0.001), AKI stage (OR: 2.112, 95% CI: 1.087–4.103, p = 0.027), combination antimicrobial therapy (OR: 1.899, 95% CI: 1.027–3.509, p = 0.041), nutritional risk (OR: 1.842, 95% CI: 1.101–3.081, p = 0.020), HRmax (OR: 1.020, 95% CI: 1.006–1.033, p = 0.004), cumulative fluid balance D3 (OR: 1.014, 95% CI: 1.005–1.022, p = 0.002), GCS score (OR: 0.899, 95% CI: 0.845–0.956, p = 0.001).

Construction and validation of a predictive nomogram for MAKE30

Based on the seven independent predictors identified by multivariate logistic regression, a nomogram was developed to predict the probability of MAKE30 (Fig. 5). In this nomogram, each predictor was assigned a corresponding point value on a scaled point system. The total points accumulated from all predictors were mapped to the predicted risk of MAKE30, allowing individualized risk estimation. The discriminative performance of the nomogram was evaluated using ROC curves (Fig. 6). In the development cohort, the AUC was 0.829 (95% CI: 0.787–0.870), while in the external validation cohort, the AUC was 0.776 (95% CI: 0.698–0.855). For comparison, the AUC values of the SOFA score and APACHE II score in the development cohort were 0.699 (95% CI: 0.646–0.751) and 0.674 (95% CI: 0.619–0.728), respectively, indicating superior predictive performance of the nomogram.

Evaluation of predictive accuracy of the nomogram

The calibration curve demonstrated good agreement between the predicted probabilities and observed outcomes in the development cohort, indicating satisfactory model calibration (Fig. 7). DCA showed that the nomogram provided a positive net benefit across threshold probability ranges of 0.15–0.90 and 0.93–1.00 (Fig. 8A), supporting its potential clinical utility. CIC were used to predict the risk stratification of MAKE30 at a sample size of 1,000, visually confirming that the nomogram achieved a favorable net benefit over a relatively wide range of threshold probabilities (Fig. 8B). Taken together, these results suggest that the nomogram offers substantial clinical net benefit across multiple threshold probability ranges and represents a reliable tool for predicting MAKE30 in clinical practice.

Discussion

The current study developed a nomogram for predicting MAKE30 in SA-AKI patients using seven readily accessible clinical variables. While most existing prediction models have focused on predicting the onset of AKI itself, our tool addresses the subsequent step: identifying among patients with AKI those at higher risk of adverse outcomes. The nomogram achieved AUCs of 0.829 in the derivation cohort and 0.776 in an external validation cohort, with satisfactory calibration and net clinical benefit. To date, there remains a paucity of studies specifically focused on predicting MAKE30. Recently, ORAKLE demonstrated high accuracy in predicting MAKE30 using deep learning and dynamic time-series data [11]. However, its clinical adoption may be limited by substantial computational requirements and continuous data streaming. In contrast, our nomogram offers a complementary approach based on seven accessible and interpretable clinical variables, enabling early risk stratification at the bedside. Furthermore, our study provides the first validation of a MAKE30 prediction tool in Chinese cohort of patients with SA-AKI, addressing a gap in geographic representation.

Recent studies [21–24] have focused on the relationship between AKI duration, renal function recovery and prognosis. Evidence indicates that SA-AKI patients who do not recover renal function prior to discharge have a higher risk of mortality and end-stage renal disease (ESRD) than those who recover before discharge. Similarly, another study [25] found that patients with unresolved renal dysfunction had higher in-hospital mortality than those with renal recovery within 7 days. Consistent with studies on AKI-related prognostic factors, our findings refine the understanding of AKI duration and its association with MAKE30. Specifically, an AKI duration of 7 days was significantly associated with MAKE30, whereas persistent AKI (defined as non-recovery of renal function within 3 days) was not. This distinction highlights the importance of defining AKI duration thresholds in prognostic assessments. Regarding AKI severity, Kula et al. [26] and Wonsuk et al. [11] identified a significant correlation between AKI stage and MAKE30 in ICU. Our study aligned with previous studies [27], confirming that higher AKI stages are associated with increased MAKE30 incidence. This consistency reinforces the clinical relevance of AKI staging as a reliable prognostic indicator, critical for guiding targeted interventions to improve outcomes. Higher AKI stages and prolonged duration of AKI cause more severe renal damage, which may progress to multiple organ dysfunction and adverse outcomes [28]. These mechanisms emphasize the need for timely interventions to mitigate renal injury and prevent progression.

Sepsis requires immediate antimicrobial therapy, which in most cases involves broad-spectrum antibiotics or their combinations. However, the nephrotoxic potential of certain antibiotics may complicate clinical management [29, 30] To explore the association between initial antimicrobial therapy and SA-AKI prognosis, we analyzed 11 antibiotics administered at admission, covering multiple classes: penicillins, carbapenems, cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones, tetracyclines, oxazolidinones, colistin, vancomycin, macrolides, nitazoles, and antifungals. Our analysis revealed no significant direct association between initial single-agent antibiotic administration and SA-AKI prognosis, suggesting that initial antimicrobial selection is not the primary determinant of renal outcomes. In contrast, post-admission combination antibiotic therapy was significantly associated with MAKE30, emphasizing the necessity of renal function monitoring during combination therapy.

Previous studies have indicated that increased nutritional risk correlated with a heightened inflammatory response [31, 32]. Malnutrition not only impairs immune defense mechanisms but also may increase susceptibility to infection [33]. Conversely, infection may further exacerbate malnutrition [34]. A potential mechanism underlying this bidirectional relationship is an imbalance between energy intake and consumption associated with high nutritional risk, which promotes infection susceptibility and facilitates pathogen invasion of T cells. Subsequent T-cell activation and differentiation trigger excessive cytokine release, amplifying inflammatory cascades and potentially inducing a cytokine storm and severe inflammation [35, 36]. Our study further demonstrated that nutritional risk at admission was associated with poor prognosis in patients with SA-AKI. Therefore, timely and appropriate nutritional support may improve immune function, shorten the disease course, and reduce mortality in this population.

Fluid administration in critically ill patients is a key concern, as fluid overload is associated with multiorgan dysfunction and poor outcomes [37]. Excessive fluid therapy—an independent risk factor for AKI—impairs renal function through increased venous/interstitial pressures, elevated renal vascular resistance, and extrinsic renal compression [38]. Prior studies confirm the prognostic role of fluid balance in sepsis and SA-AKI. Using a multicenter sepsis database, Zhang et al. [39] linked cumulative fluid balance on days 1–3 to adverse outcomes. Wang et al. identified higher cumulative fluid balance as an independent predictor of 28-day mortality [40]. Neyra et al. demonstrated that 72-hour post-ICU cumulative fluid balance independently correlated with hospital mortality in SA-AKI patients [41]. Consistent with these findings, our study associated 3-day post-ICU cumulative fluid balance with MAKE30 in SA-AKI patients. Collectively, aggressive fluid resuscitation and positive fluid balance are not universally beneficial and may be detrimental in critically ill patients.

Admission HRmax indicates patients’ initial physiological stress response, reflecting sepsis severity and its impact on renal perfusion. Our study found that admission tachycardia was associated with a higher risk of MAKE30. GCS is widely employed to evaluate the severity of illness and predict prognosis in critically ill patients [42, 43]. In sepsis, a lower GCS score denotes a vulnerable physiological state, characterized by reduced organ reserve and compromised systemic resilience. We found that SA-AKI patients with lower GCS scores were more likely to experience MAKE30. Thus, our findings highlight the need for the timely assessment and management of tachycardia and nervous system damage in SA-AKI patients on hospital admission.

Limitations

However, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the sample size was relatively small for a multicenter cohort, leading to a limited number of positive cases for some baseline variables. Second, discrepancies in baseline characteristics existed between the development and validation cohorts, as they were derived from different data sources; this may have contributed to the relatively low predictive performance of the validation cohort. Third, approximately 22% of patients lacked historical baseline Scr measurements, which were instead calculated by MDRD equation, potentially introducing bias. Standardized assessment of pre-sepsis baseline Scr parameters could help minimize diagnostic ambiguity. In the future, prospective study designs and external validation across multiple centers with larger databases will be crucial to validate and corroborate our findings.

Conclusion

In summary, we developed a MAKE30 prediction model for SA-AKI patients, which performed well in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical application. Additionally, we further explored the association between fluid accumulation and MAKE30, highlighting the critical role of rational fluid management in improving renal prognosis, findings that provide valuable guidance for clinicians to make timely, personalized clinical decisions.
