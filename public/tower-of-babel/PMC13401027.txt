Author's Reply Regarding “Dynamic Femur Fracture Brace vs Hip Spica Cast for Pediatric Femoral Shaft Fractures: A Retrospective Comparative Cohort Study
Authors: Virginia Casey, Steven Frick
Published: 2026

We want to thank the Dr. Hannon et al. for their thoughtful correspondence and for sharing their institutional experience with the DF2 brace. It is incredibly rewarding to see another high-volume, academic Level 1 trauma center successfully implementing this technology and reproducing the positive clinical outcomes we observed in our study.

The authors’ ability to expand indications to include pathologic fractures and postsurgical patients mirrors our own evolving practice. We are also experiencing logistical challenges with widespread implementation, as navigating practitioner education, interdisciplinary responsibilities, and inventory management is a significant undertaking for any large hospital system.

Regarding their specific clinical observations, the decision to institute a one-week postapplication skin check is an excellent modification. Since our initial study, we have also encountered one patient with significant skin breakdown, reinforcing the reality that early, proactive vigilance is critical. The majority of our surgeons have also converted to seeing patients back at the one-week mark rather than at 2 weeks. As with many emerging technologies and new treatment methods, experience is an excellent teacher, we learn from complications and try to devise ways to avoid them in the future. Specific instructions for parents to seek early return to the clinic for pain in the extremity distal to the fracture as a possible sign of skin ischemia or other problems, and a skin check at one week are responses to our issues with skin complications. It can also be helpful for the family to see the surgeon change the sock liner at the one week time point, as many families are afraid of moving the injured extremity early after a femur fracture- showing them that it can be done comfortably with the support of the injured limb to remove it from the brace and replace the sock helps them do it when they get home.

We look forward to more surgeons sharing their experiences with the DF2 brace so that we can learn from one another and improve patient experiences and outcomes by avoiding complications. We also look forward to future contributions by Hannon et al. on optimal strategies for pain management, improved family satisfaction, and reimbursements for this treatment method.

Sincerely,

Virginia Casey, MD and Steven Frick, MD.
