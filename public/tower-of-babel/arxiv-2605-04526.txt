1.
Notation before the introduction

The physical space is
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Cylindrical coordinates are denoted by

(r,\theta,z),\qquad r=(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})^{1/2}.

An axisymmetric velocity field with swirl is written as

u(r,z,t)=u^{r}(r,z,t)e_{r}+u^{\theta}(r,z,t)e_{\theta}+u^{z}(r,z,t)e_{z}.

The incompressible Euler equations are

(1)

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=0,\qquad\nabla\cdot u=0.

The material derivative in the meridional variables is

D_{t}=\partial_{t}+u^{r}\partial_{r}+u^{z}\partial_{z}.

The swirl variable and lifted azimuthal-vorticity variable are

(2)

\Gamma=ru^{\theta},\qquad G=\frac{\omega^{\theta}}{r},\qquad\omega=\nabla\times u.

For smooth axisymmetric Euler flow with swirl,

(3)

D_{t}\Gamma=0,\qquad D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2}).

We use the local variables

(4)

x=r-r_{*}(t),\qquad y=z,

where
r_{*}(t)>0
is a tracked interior radial center. The tracked center is defined by

(5)

r_{*}^{\prime}(t)=u^{r}(r_{*}(t),0,t),\qquad r_{*}(0)=r_{0}>0.

The moving-frame meridional velocity is

(6)

U(x,y,t)=u^{r}(r_{*}(t)+x,y,t)-r_{*}^{\prime}(t),\qquad V(x,y,t)=u^{z}(r_{*}(t)+x,y,t).

The hyperbolic strain rate is

(7)

\sigma(t)=-\partial_{y}V(0,0,t)=-\partial_{z}u^{z}(r_{*}(t),0,t).

The local strain form to be propagated is

(8)

U=\sigma x+\mathcal{R}^{r},\qquad V=-\sigma y+\mathcal{R}^{z},

with small normalized remainder.

The main quadrupole kernel and positive weight are

(9)

K_{Q}(x,y)=\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}},\qquad W_{Q}(x,y)=\frac{|xy|}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}.

For scale
\lambda>0
, the full four-quadrant packet is

(10)

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(t)=\{|x|<\lambda,\ |y|<\lambda\}.

The full quadrupole score at a tracked packet is

(11)

Q(t)=\mathcal{H}^{\rm full}_{\lambda}[G](t):=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(t)}K_{Q}(x,y)G(x,y,t)\,dx\,dy.

When a supremum over admissible scales is needed, we write

(12)

\mathfrak{Q}(t)=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{A}(t)}\mathcal{H}^{\rm full}_{\lambda}[G](t).

For the final explicit construction we use the tracked score
Q(t)
, which avoids nonlocal re-selection ambiguities.

A narrow diagonal subpacket is also used:

(13)

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}(t)=\left\{|x|<\lambda,\ |y|<\lambda,\left|\frac{|y|}{|x|}-1\right|<\delta_{c}\right\}.

The diagonal subscore is

(14)

\mathcal{H}^{\rm diag}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}[G](t)=\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}(t)}K_{Q}(x,y)G(x,y,t)\,dx\,dy.

The diagonal score is not the active score; it is a coercive subscore.

The tracked packet scale is fixed as part of the moving frame by

(15)

\lambda^{\prime}(t)=-\sigma(t)\lambda(t),\qquad\lambda(0)=\lambda_{0}.

This convention is used in every moving-packet score and projection below.

The main swirl-jet coefficient is

(16)

b(t)=\partial_{x}\partial_{y}^{2}\Gamma(0,0,t).

The normalized source amplitude is

(17)

C(t)=\lambda(t)^{2}b(t).

The source-shape smallness, radial-localization ratio, and master error are

(18)

\mu(t)=\frac{b(t)\lambda(t)^{3}}{\Gamma_{*}(t)},\qquad\rho(t)=\frac{\lambda(t)}{r_{*}(t)},

(19)

\mathbf{E}(t)=\delta_{\rm jet}(t)+\mu(t)+\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}(t)+\rho(t)+\varepsilon_{\rm strain}(t).

Here
\delta_{\rm jet}
measures scale-weighted deviation of the Taylor jet of
\Gamma
from the active mode
xy^{2}
,
\varepsilon_{\rm strain}
is the normalized strain error in (
8
), and
\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}
is defined in
Section

4.1

2.
Introduction

The question whether smooth solutions of the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations can form singularities in finite time is a central problem in mathematical fluid mechanics. The Beale–Kato–Majda continuation criterion shows that smooth continuation is controlled by the time-integrability of the maximum vorticity norm
[
3
]
. The geometric viewpoint developed by Constantin–Fefferman–Majda emphasizes how vortex stretching may be depleted by alignment and coherence mechanisms
[
4
]
. For the axisymmetric class with swirl, the interaction between meridional hyperbolic compression, swirl transport, and the source term in the equation for
G=\omega^{\theta}/r
gives a particularly sharp testing ground for singularity formation.

The boundary scenario of Luo and Hou supplied strong numerical evidence for finite-time blow-up in a periodic cylinder with solid boundary
[
6
,
7
]
. Chen and Hou subsequently gave a computer-assisted proof of finite-time singularity formation for smooth Euler data in a domain with boundary
[
9
]
. Other major developments include Elgindi’s finite-time singularities for
C^{1,\alpha}
solutions
[
10
]
, and related stability and model problems
[
11
,
12
,
8
]
. The whole-space smooth Euler problem is different from the boundary problem: there is no wall reflection and no one-sided Dirichlet kernel sign.

2.1.
Relation with the companion Navier–Stokes manuscripts.

This paper is the second part of a two-paper Euler series on inviscid packet amplification; the first part treats boundary blow-up in a periodic cylinder. The Euler series is also meant to be read alongside two separate Navier–Stokes manuscripts of the author, currently under review, in which a five-dimensional lift and direct first-threshold method are developed for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes global-existence problem with smooth data
[
1
,
2
]
. Those Navier–Stokes papers are cited here only for conceptual context. No theorem, lemma, or estimate in the present Euler series depends on them, and the Euler conclusions would stand or fail independently of the Navier–Stokes manuscripts.

The connection is structural rather than logical. The same packet vocabulary is used to compare two opposite mechanisms. In the Navier–Stokes papers, the five-dimensional lift, full-Dirichlet visibility, viscous coercivity, and first-threshold continuation mechanism are used to suppress concentration packets. In the present inviscid whole-space paper, the diffusion and Dirichlet dissipation are absent; an interior quadrupole packet instead creates a positive hyperbolic feedback loop. Thus the two series are complementary rather than contradictory: the Navier–Stokes manuscripts describe how viscosity prevents first-threshold packet amplification, while the Euler series studies inviscid geometries in which packet amplification can persist and drive singularity formation.

This manuscript develops a whole-space replacement for the boundary hyperbolic packet. The replacement is an interior quadrupole packet. In coordinates centered at a moving radial point,

x=r-r_{*}(t),\qquad y=z,

the active profiles are

(20)

G(x,y,t)\approx a(t)xy,\qquad\Gamma(x,y,t)\approx\Gamma_{*}(t)+\frac{1}{2}b(t)xy^{2},\qquad\Gamma_{*}(t)>0.

These two profiles are compatible with the two dangerous mechanisms of axisymmetric Euler. First, the quadrupole
G
-packet pairs positively with the interior Biot–Savart strain kernel and produces a hyperbolic strain

U\approx\sigma x,\qquad V\approx-\sigma y,\qquad\sigma>0.

Second, the transported swirl variable obeys

D_{t}\Gamma=0,

and the source for
G
is

D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2}).

For the profile
\Gamma=\Gamma_{*}+\frac{1}{2}bxy^{2}
, one has

\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})=2\Gamma_{*}bxy+\text{controlled errors},

so the source regenerates the same quadrupole sign as
G
.

The resulting feedback is

G\sim axy\Longrightarrow\sigma\gtrsim Q,

\Gamma\sim\Gamma_{*}+\frac{1}{2}bxy^{2}\Longrightarrow Q^{\prime}\gtrsim C,

U\sim\sigma x,\quad V\sim-\sigma y\Longrightarrow C^{\prime}\gtrsim QC.

With source dominance
C\geq\kappa Q^{2}
, this gives

Q^{\prime}\geq c\kappa Q^{2},

which blows up in finite comparison time.

3.
Axisymmetric Euler, swirl variables, and 5D recovery

The axisymmetric divergence-free condition is

(21)

\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(ru^{r})+\partial_{z}u^{z}=0.

The azimuthal vorticity is

(22)

\omega^{\theta}=\partial_{z}u^{r}-\partial_{r}u^{z}.

The variables
\Gamma=ru^{\theta}
and
G=\omega^{\theta}/r
satisfy (
3
). The first equation is exact transport of swirl. The second is the source equation for the lifted azimuthal vorticity.

The meridional velocity can be recovered from a scalar potential. Introduce
\phi
by

(23)

-\Delta_{5}\phi=G,\qquad\Delta_{5}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{3}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2}.

Then

(24)

u^{r}=-r\partial_{z}\phi,\qquad u^{z}=2\phi+r\partial_{r}\phi.

Indeed, (
24
) gives (
21
), and direct differentiation gives
-\Delta_{5}\phi=\omega^{\theta}/r
.

The 5D interpretation is the following. Let
(Y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}
,
r=|Y|
. For functions depending only on
(r,z)
, the five-dimensional Laplacian is

\Delta_{Y,z}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{3}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2}=\Delta_{5}.

Thus the elliptic recovery is a five-dimensional Newtonian potential restricted to the axisymmetric quotient of
\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}
. This is the origin of the lifted kernel appearing in the interior quadrupole sign calculation.

Lemma 4.1

(Full score to diagonal coercive subscore)
.

Let
0<\delta_{c}<1/10
. There is

(26)

\mathcal{H}^{\rm diag}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}[G]\geq c_{\delta_{c}}\mathcal{H}^{\rm full}_{\lambda}[G]-CD_{{\rm ang},\lambda}[G],

where

(27)

D_{{\rm ang},\lambda}[G]=\inf_{a\geq 0}\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}W_{Q}(x,y)|G(x,y)-axy|\,dx\,dy.

Proof.

For the model

K_{Q}G=a\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\theta.

The full angular integral is positive. The diagonal subscore restricts to four intervals around
\theta=\pi/4,3\pi/4,5\pi/4,7\pi/4

\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\theta

G
and
axy
is bounded by the weighted angular defect.

5.
Weighted projected amplitudes and moving-packet calculus

This section replaces pointwise Taylor coefficients by weighted moving-packet
projections. The pointwise coefficients are convenient for intuition, but a
referee-level propagation proof must control the commutators coming from the
moving center, shrinking scale, and cutoff.

Fix an even cutoff
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-2,2))
,
0\leq\psi\leq 1
,

\psi=1
on
[-1,1]
, and define

\chi_{\lambda}(x,y)=\psi(x/\lambda)\psi(y/\lambda),\qquad w_{\lambda}=\chi_{\lambda}^{2}.

For a scalar
F
, set

\langle F,H\rangle_{\lambda}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,y)H(x,y)w_{\lambda}(x,y)\,dx\,dy.

The projected quadrupole amplitude is

(31)

a_{\lambda}(t):=\frac{\langle G,xy\rangle_{\lambda}}{\langle xy,xy\rangle_{\lambda}}.

The projected swirl-jet amplitude is

(32)

b_{\lambda}(t):=2\frac{\langle\Gamma-\Gamma_{*},xy^{2}\rangle_{\lambda}}{\langle xy^{2},xy^{2}\rangle_{\lambda}},\qquad\Gamma_{*}(t):=\frac{\int\Gamma w_{\lambda}}{\int w_{\lambda}}.

The corresponding normalized quantities are

(33)

Q_{\lambda}(t):=c_{Q}a_{\lambda}(t)\lambda(t)^{2},\qquad C_{\lambda}(t):=\lambda(t)^{2}b_{\lambda}(t).

On packets with
\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}+\delta_{\rm jet}+\mu+\rho\ll 1
, these are comparable
with the tracked score
Q
and the pointwise coefficient
b
:

(34)

Q_{\lambda}=Q(1+O(\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}+\rho)),\qquad C_{\lambda}=C(1+O(\delta_{\rm jet}+\mu+\rho)).

Lemma 5.1

(Moving projection commutator)
.

Let
F
be smooth on the moving packet and let
M_{F}(t)=\langle F,\Phi\rangle_{\lambda}
, where
\Phi
is a fixed polynomial of degree at most three. Then

\frac{d}{dt}M_{F}=\langle D_{t}F,\Phi\rangle_{\lambda}+\langle F,\mathcal{L}_{\rm hyp}\Phi\rangle_{\lambda}+\operatorname{Comm}_{\lambda,r_{*}}[F,\Phi],

where
\mathcal{L}_{\rm hyp}=\sigma(x\partial_{x}-y\partial_{y})
is the frozen
hyperbolic transport operator. Moreover

(35)

|\operatorname{Comm}_{\lambda,r_{*}}[F,\Phi]|\leq C\sigma(t)\Bigl(\varepsilon_{\rm strain}(t)+\rho(t)\Bigr)\int_{\mathcal{P}_{2\lambda}}|F|\,|\Phi|\,dx\,dy.

Proof.

Differentiate the integral in the moving variables
x=r-r_{*}(t)
,

y=z
. The choice
r_{*}^{\prime}=u^{r}(r_{*},0,t)
removes the center drift, and

\lambda^{\prime}=-\sigma\lambda
cancels the leading dilation of the cutoff. The
remaining transport field is

(U,V)-(\sigma x,-\sigma y),

whose gradient is bounded by
\varepsilon_{\rm strain}\sigma
on the packet.
Curvature of the cylindrical coefficients contributes
O(\rho\sigma)
. The
cutoff derivatives are
O(\lambda^{-1})
, while the remainder velocity is

O((\varepsilon_{\rm strain}+\rho)\sigma\lambda)
. This gives
(
35
).
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Projected amplitude evolution)
.

Inside the quadrupole regime, the projected amplitudes satisfy

(36)

\displaystyle D^{+}Q_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle\geq cC_{\lambda}(t)-C\mathbf{E}(t)C_{\lambda}(t),

(37)

\displaystyle D^{+}C_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle\geq cQ_{\lambda}(t)C_{\lambda}(t)-C\mathbf{E}(t)Q_{\lambda}(t)C_{\lambda}(t).

Proof.

For (
36
), use
D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})
in the
moving projection (
31
). The leading source

2r_{*}^{-4}\Gamma_{*}b_{\lambda}xy
has exactly the test profile
xy
and
therefore contributes
c\lambda^{2}b_{\lambda}=cC_{\lambda}
. The nonlinear
source, radial curvature, profile defect, and moving-cutoff commutators are
bounded by
C\mathbf{E}C_{\lambda}
by
Section

5
.

For (
37
), apply
Section

5
to

\Gamma
with test function
xy^{2}
. In the exact hyperbolic field,

xy^{2}
has growth rate
\sigma
, hence
b_{\lambda}^{\prime}\geq(1-C\mathbf{E})\sigma b_{\lambda}
. The kernel lower bound
\sigma\geq cQ_{\lambda}-C\mathbf{E}Q_{\lambda}

then gives (
37
).
∎

Lemma 5.3

(Weighted profile-defect differential inequality)
.

Let
D_{{\rm prof},\lambda}
be defined by (
29
), with the
best-fit amplitude chosen by the weighted projection (
31
). Then
inside the quadrupole regime,

D^{+}\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}\leq-c\frac{C_{\lambda}}{Q_{\lambda}}\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}+C(\delta_{\rm jet}+\mu+\rho+\varepsilon_{\rm strain})\frac{C_{\lambda}}{Q_{\lambda}}+C\sigma\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}^{2}.

Proof.

The leading source is exactly proportional to

6.
Kernel sign, source compatibility, and strain control

Lemma 6.1

(Interior quadrupole Biot–Savart sign expansion)
.

Let
0<\lambda\ll r_{*}(t)
. The local kernel for

\sigma(t)=-\partial_{z}u^{z}(r_{*}(t),0,t)

has the expansion

(38)

K_{\rm int}(x,y)=C_{0}\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}+K_{\rm rem}(x,y),\qquad C_{0}>0,

with

(39)

|K_{\rm rem}(x,y)|\leq C\frac{\lambda}{r_{*}}\frac{|xy|}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}+K_{\rm smooth}(x,y).

Consequently, on a sign-coherent full quadrupole packet,

(40)

\sigma(t)\geq cQ(t)-C\left(\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}(t)+\frac{\lambda(t)}{r_{*}(t)}\right)Q(t).

In particular, if the master defect is sufficiently small, then

(41)

\sigma(t)\geq cQ(t).

Proof.

In the lifted variables
(Y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}
, the fundamental solution for
-\Delta_{5}
is a multiple of
|(Y,z)|^{-3}
. Near an interior point
|Y|=r_{*}(t)>0
, write local coordinates
(x,y,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
\eta
is tangent to the local
S^{3}
-orbit. The mixed derivative has the leading form

\partial_{x}\partial_{y}|(x,y,\eta)|^{-3}=C\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{7/2}}+\text{terms with the same lower-order symmetry}.

Integrating in
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
gives
C_{0}xy/(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}
. The dependence of the true axisymmetric coefficients on
r=r_{*}+x
produces the relative error
O(\lambda/r_{*})
. Pairing the leading kernel with a sign-coherent packet gives the lower bound. The difference between
G
and its best-fit profile is controlled by
\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}Q
.
∎

Lemma 6.2

(Quadrupole source compatibility)
.

Assume

(42)

\Gamma(x,y,t)=\Gamma_{*}(t)+\frac{1}{2}b(t)xy^{2}+R_{\Gamma}(x,y,t),\qquad\Gamma_{*}(t)>0,

with

(43)

|R_{\Gamma}|\leq\delta_{\rm jet}b\lambda^{3},\qquad|\partial_{y}R_{\Gamma}|\leq\delta_{\rm jet}b\lambda^{2}.

Then

(44)

r^{-4}\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})=2r_{*}^{-4}\Gamma_{*}bxy+\operatorname{Err}_{\rm src},

where

(45)

|\operatorname{Err}_{\rm src}|\leq C\left(\delta_{\rm jet}+\frac{\lambda}{r_{*}}+\frac{b\lambda^{3}}{\Gamma_{*}}\right)r_{*}^{-4}\Gamma_{*}b|xy|.

Proof.

Since
\partial_{y}\Gamma=bxy+\partial_{y}R_{\Gamma}
,

\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})=2\Gamma\Gamma_{y}=2\Gamma_{*}bxy+\text{terms controlled by }R_{\Gamma},\partial_{y}R_{\Gamma},b^{2}x^{2}y^{3}.

The nonlinear term is bounded by

b^{2}|x|^{2}|y|^{3}\leq b|xy|\,b\lambda^{3}=\frac{b\lambda^{3}}{\Gamma_{*}}\Gamma_{*}b|xy|.

Finally,
(r_{*}+x)^{-4}=r_{*}^{-4}(1+O(\lambda/r_{*}))
.
∎

Lemma 6.3

(Swirl-jet amplification)
.

Assume (
8
) and

\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{R}^{r}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})}+\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{R}^{z}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda})}\leq\varepsilon_{\rm strain}\sigma.

Then

(46)

b^{\prime}(t)\geq\left(1-C\left[\delta_{\rm jet}+\varepsilon_{\rm strain}+\lambda/r_{*}\right]\right)\sigma(t)b(t).

In particular, inside the master bootstrap,
b^{\prime}\geq c\sigma b
.

Proof.

In moving coordinates,
D_{t}\Gamma=0
becomes

\Gamma_{t}+U\Gamma_{x}+V\Gamma_{y}=0.

For the exact linear field
U=\sigma x
,
V=-\sigma y
, the term
\frac{1}{2}bxy^{2}
gives

\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}bxy^{2}\right)+\sigma x\partial_{x}\left(\frac{1}{2}bxy^{2}\right)-\sigma y\partial_{y}\left(\frac{1}{2}bxy^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}(b^{\prime}-\sigma b)xy^{2}.

Hence
b^{\prime}=\sigma b
in the flat model. The remainders are controlled by the stated strain, jet, and radial-reflection errors.
∎

Lemma 6.4

(Strain control)
.

Inside the master bootstrap,

(47)

\varepsilon_{\rm strain}(t)\leq C\left(\mathcal{R}_{\rm prof}(t)+\delta_{\rm jet}(t)+\frac{\lambda(t)}{r_{*}(t)}\right).

Proof.

The exact quadrupole profile gives a diagonal hyperbolic strain matrix. The off-diagonal strain kernels have parity that cancels against
axy
. The angular-profile defect measures the failure of this cancellation;
\lambda/r_{*}
measures the failure of exact local radial reflection; and
\delta_{\rm jet}
measures the source-side deviation from the active quadrupole profile. The local Calderon–Zygmund bounds for the lifted kernel give (
47
).
∎

7.
Dini bounds and profile-defect persistence

Lemma 7.1

(Two-sided Dini bounds for the tracked full score)
.

Inside the master bootstrap,

(48)

c_{0}C(t)\leq Q^{\prime}(t)\leq C_{0}C(t).

Proof.

Differentiate (
11
) along the tracked packet. The packet motion is chosen by
r_{*}^{\prime}=u^{r}(r_{*},0)
and
\lambda^{\prime}=-\sigma\lambda
, so the leading transport flux cancels with the hyperbolic scaling. The source contribution is

\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}K_{Q}\,2r_{*}^{-4}\Gamma_{*}bxy\,dxdy\sim\lambda^{2}b=C(t).

All source errors, cutoff errors, radial-reflection errors, and angular-profile errors are bounded by
C\mathbf{E}C(t)
. This gives the lower bound for
\mathbf{E}\ll 1
. Estimating the absolute value of the same differentiated identity gives the upper bound.
∎

Corollary 7.2

(Source dominance persists)
.

If
C(0)\geq\kappa Q(0)^{2}
and
0<\kappa<c/(4C_{0})
, then

(49)

C(t)\geq\kappa Q(t)^{2}

throughout the master bootstrap.

Proof.

Let
S=C-\kappa Q^{2}
. Using
C^{\prime}\geq cQC
and
Q^{\prime}\leq C_{0}C
,

S^{\prime}\geq cQC-2\kappa C_{0}QC>0

for the chosen
\kappa
. Thus
S
cannot cross from nonnegative to negative.
∎

Lemma 7.3

(Angular-profile defect production)

13.
Detailed scale bookkeeping and constants

This section records the scale relations used by the construction. The local packet is two-dimensional in the meridional variables, while the elliptic recovery is inherited from the five-dimensional lift. The full score has the scaling

Q\sim a\lambda^{2},

because the integrand
x^{2}y^{2}/(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}
is homogeneous of degree zero and the packet area is
\lambda^{2}
. The active swirl coefficient has the normalization

C=\lambda^{2}b.

The source term generated by the active swirl jet is

r^{-4}\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})\big|_{\rm main}=2r_{*}^{-4}\Gamma_{*}bxy.

Pairing this with
K_{Q}=xy/(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}
over
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
gives

\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}K_{Q}\,2r_{*}^{-4}\Gamma_{*}bxy\,dxdy=2r_{*}^{-4}\Gamma_{*}b\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}\frac{x^{2}y^{2}}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}\,dxdy\sim b\lambda^{2}=C.

Here the fixed factor
2r_{*}^{-4}\Gamma_{*}
is absorbed into the constants after choosing the interior radius and lower bound for
\Gamma_{*}
. If desired one may keep this factor explicitly; doing so does not change the ODE structure.

The source-dominance condition is

C\geq\kappa Q^{2}.

At time zero, the explicit datum gives

Q(0)\sim a_{0}\lambda_{0}^{2},\qquad C(0)=b_{0}\lambda_{0}^{2}.

Thus it suffices to impose

b_{0}\geq\kappa_{1}a_{0}^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}.

The nonlinear source-shape smallness is

\mu(0)=\frac{b_{0}\lambda_{0}^{3}}{\Gamma_{*,0}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

The simultaneous choice

b_{0}=A_{b}a_{0}^{2}\lambda_{0}^{2},\qquad A_{b}\gg\kappa_{1},

and

A_{b}a_{0}^{2}\lambda_{0}^{5}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\Gamma_{*,0}

therefore enforces both source dominance and source-shape smallness.

13.1.
The Riccati comparison

The propagated inequalities are

Q^{\prime}\geq cC,\qquad C^{\prime}\geq cQC,\qquad C\geq\kappa Q^{2}.

Combining the first and third inequalities gives

Q^{\prime}\geq c\kappa Q^{2}.

For
Q(0)>0
, comparison with
Y^{\prime}=c\kappa Y^{2}
yields

Q(t)\geq\frac{Q(0)}{1-c\kappa Q(0)t}.

Hence the comparison blow-up time is bounded by

gives

14.
Detailed 5D kernel calculation

The five-dimensional Newtonian kernel satisfies

(-\Delta_{5})^{-1}f(X)=c_{5}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\frac{f(X^{\prime})}{|X-X^{\prime}|^{3}}\,dX^{\prime}.

Near an interior packet centered at
r_{*}>0
, write

X=(x,y,\eta),\qquad x=r-r_{*},\quad y=z,\qquad\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

The mixed derivative of the Newtonian kernel is

\partial_{x}\partial_{y}|X|^{-3}=15\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{7/2}}.

The reduction to the meridional kernel requires integrating the tangential variable
\eta
. Let
R^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{d\eta}{(R^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{7/2}}=4\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2}\,ds}{(R^{2}+s^{2})^{7/2}}.

With
s=R\tau
,

4\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2}\,ds}{(R^{2}+s^{2})^{7/2}}=4\pi R^{-4}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau^{2}\,d\tau}{(1+\tau^{2})^{7/2}}=CR^{-4}.

Hence the leading two-variable kernel is

C_{0}\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}.

The sign is fixed by the positive constant
C_{0}
. All terms caused by the curvature of the orbit
|Y|=r_{*}+x
enter through Taylor expansions of the coefficients in powers of
x/r_{*}
. On
|x|\leq\lambda
, these errors have relative size
O(\lambda/r_{*})
.
