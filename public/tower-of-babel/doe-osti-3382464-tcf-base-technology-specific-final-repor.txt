 PROJECT SCOPE AND OBJECTIVE
 We will use negative cracked film lithography (CFL) to form passivated emitter rear contact
 (PERC) CdTe solar cells. CFL allows the scalable patterning of metal contacts and openings down
 to 10 micrometers, where such fine features are needed to adapt silicon PERC architectures to the
 more resistive CdTe absorbers. This project will advance the photovoltaic conversion efficiency
 ceiling of CdTe solar cells and commercialize CFL in the process.

 BENEFIT TO DOE MISSION
 The National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) worked with the world’s leading thin-film
 photovoltaic manufacturer—First Solar, an American company—to develop electrical
 contacting methods that improve the power conversion efficiency of First Solar’s cells by 0.5%
 absolute. We met this milestone but contrary to our hypothesis, these performance gains are
 not passivation (PERC)-based. Instead, we identified another mechanism by which our
 micropatterned Al2O3 contacts improve efficiency. Using the results of this project, we will
 adapt NREL’s cracked film lithography intellectual property (IP) toward the application we
 identified, which is a new route to commercialization.

 COMMERCIALIZATION ADVANCEMENTS

 MARKET ADVANCEMENTS
 We developed processes that increase device efficiency by 0.5% (absolute), relative to
 baseline back contact formation at NLR on First Solar’s samples. However, we also discovered
 how to adapt NLR PERC IP to focus on the newly discovered application. We will target that
 research in a new TCF proposal.

 COMMERCIALIZATION ACHIEVEMENTS AND SUCCESS STORIES
 Obtained solar cell efficiency results and an understanding of how micropatterned
 Al2O3 improves the CdTe back contact that can pave a new path to commercialization.

 CONCLUSION

 PATH FORWARD
 We will refine our micropatterned hybrid contacts for the new application we
 identified. Specifically, we will adapt our current results on laboratory CdTe/Au to
 industry CdTe/ZnTe/metal back contacts, which should benefit from the same
 efficiency enhancement mechanism.

 LESSONS LEARNED
 We learned that while improving CdTe power conversion efficiency through back
 interface passivation (PERC) may still be possible, passivation is unlikely to be the
 cause of the efficiency gains we report on this project.
