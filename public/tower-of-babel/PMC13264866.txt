Use of clips to prevent delayed post-polypectomy bleeding in non-pedunculated colorectal lesions: protocol for a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Yaping Wei, Shi Zhang, Hawa Suleiman Mabenga, Brenda John Ngowi, Zheng Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDelayed post-polypectomy bleeding (DPPB) remains a significant complication of endoscopic resection, contributing to morbidity and increased healthcare costs. Although prophylactic clipping is widely practised to mitigate this risk, evidence from recent randomised controlled trials (RCTs) regarding its efficacy is inconsistent. This protocol outlines a systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the effectiveness of prophylactic clips following thermal resection.Methods and analysisWe will conduct a comprehensive search of MEDLINE, EMBASE and the Cochrane Library from inception to 10 February 2026, to identify RCTs comparing prophylactic clips vs no clips in patients undergoing thermal endoscopic resection of non-pedunculated polyps. The primary outcome is DPPB within 30 days, defined as overt bleeding requiring medical intervention or a haemoglobin decrease ≥2 g/dL. Secondary outcomes include DPPB in proximal large (≥20 mm) lesions, perforation, post-polypectomy syndrome and procedure time. Data synthesis will use a random-effects model. Methodological quality will be assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool. Publication bias will be visualised using funnel plots. We will quantify the effect of potential effect modifiers by meta-regression if appropriate. The quality of evidence will be evaluated according to the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework.Ethics and disseminationThis study will not use primary data, and therefore formal ethical approval is not required. The findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals and committee conferences.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251246840.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. The endoscopic resection of precancerous lesions plays a pivotal role in interrupting the adenoma-carcinoma sequence, significantly reducing incidence and mortality.1 While generally safe, thermal endoscopic resection techniques—specifically endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) and endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD)—are associated with adverse events. Among these, delayed post-polypectomy bleeding (DPPB) is the most common major complication, with a reported incidence of 4–7% in clinical practice.2 DPPB often necessitates hospitalisation, repeat endoscopy and blood transfusion, posing a substantial clinical and economic burden.

Several risk factors for DPPB have been identified, including polyp size, proximal location and the use of antithrombotic agents.3 To reduce this risk, prophylactic clip placement to close the mucosal defect has been widely adopted. However, the efficacy of this practice remains a subject of intense debate. While the European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) guidelines recommend clips for large (≥ 20 mm) non-pedunculated colorectal polyps (LNPCPs) in the right colon,4 recent evidence from large-scale randomised controlled trials (RCTs), such as the CLIPPER trial, has questioned the generalisability of this benefit.5

Existing systematic reviews and meta-analyses (SRMAs) have yielded conflicting results, potentially due to the inclusion of heterogeneous populations (mixing pedunculated and non-pedunculated polyps) or insufficient statistical power to detect differences in subgroups.610 To address these gaps, we developed this protocol for a SRMA. By focusing specifically on non-pedunculated polyps resected via thermal techniques, this study aims to provide robust, conclusive evidence to guide risk-stratified clinical practice.

Methods

This SRMA adheres to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines11 (online supplemental additional file 1). The protocol is registered in the Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) prior to the systematic literature search (registration number CRD420251246840). The literature search will be performed on 10 February 2026, and data analysis is expected to be completed by July 2026.

Criteria for considering studies for this review

Eligibility criteria are established in terms of the Population-Intervention-Comparison-Outcome-Study design framework. Studies will be selected according to the following criteria:

Participants

Patients undergoing thermal endoscopic resection for non-pedunculated colorectal polyps. In this SRMA, the definition of thermal resection will include any resection method that involves the use of electrocautery or electrosurgery. This encompasses techniques such as hot snare polypectomy, underwater EMR and hybrid ESD, in addition to traditional EMR and ESD. Non-pedunculated colorectal polyps will be defined according to the Paris endoscopic classification12 as lesions without a stalk, including sessile (0-Is) and superficial lesions (0-IIa, 0-IIb and 0-IIc, including mixed superficial types such as 0-IIa+IIc). Pedunculated (0-Ip) and subpedunculated (0-Isp) lesions are excluded.

Intervention

Prophylactic placement of clips following polyp resection.

Comparison

No prophylactic clip placement.

Outcomes

The primary outcome is DPPB, defined as hematochezia or melena requiring medical intervention (hospitalisation, transfusion, repeat endoscopy, surgery) or a haemoglobin drop ≥2 g/dL within 30 days. Secondary outcomes include DPPB in proximal LNPCPs, procedure time and adverse events (perforation, post-polypectomy syndrome, post-procedural pain). Proximal polyps will be defined as those located at or proximal to the mid-transverse colon.

Study design

RCTs will be included. Studies will be excluded if they meet at least one of the following criteria: (1) observational studies including case-control and cohort studies; (2) single arm studies; (3) case reports, reviews, editorials and letters to editor; and (4) duplicate studies, in vitro studies or animal studies.

Search methods for identification of studies

Electronic searches

Two investigators (YW and SZ) will independently search MEDLINE, EMBASE and the Cochrane Library from inception to 10 February 2026. The search strategy will use a combination of MeSH terms and free-text keywords, including ‘clipping’, ‘polypectomy’, ‘bleeding’, ‘endoscopic resection’ and ‘hemoclip’. No language restrictions will be applied. The search strategies (online supplemental additional file 2) will be decided on after a discussion among all reviewers. We will assess the eligibility of the retrieved articles by title and abstract using predetermined inclusion criteria. If this information is insufficient for eligibility assessment, we will review the full article. If any new evidence is published during the review period, we will evaluate eligibility and consider including it in the analysis. Reference lists of included studies and relevant reviews will be manually screened to identify additional eligible trials. Conference abstracts reporting RCTs will be considered for inclusion if they provide sufficient details on study design, population, intervention, comparator and outcomes to allow eligibility assessment and data extraction. If critical information is missing, we will attempt to contact the authors for clarification. If no response is received, the study will be excluded from the primary analysis but may be retained in a supplementary description.

Data collection and analysis

Selection of studies

Decisions about study inclusion and exclusion will be made independently by two investigators (ZJ and YW). Disagreements will be resolved by consultation with a third investigator (HSM). The details of the study selection procedure are shown in a PRISMA flow diagram. (Figure 1)

Data extraction and management

Two investigators (YW and HSM) will independently extract the appropriate data onto standardised extraction forms, with disagreements resolved by consultation with a third investigator (ZJ). Data will be extracted for the intention-to-treat population whenever available. If intention-to-treat data are not reported, per-protocol data will be extracted. The following data will be extracted from included trials: study characteristics (country, year, design), patient demographics (age, sex, antithrombotic use), lesion characteristics (size, location, morphology, histology), procedural details (resection technique, clip type) and outcome data. In addition, the definition of prophylactic clipping (eg, complete vs partial closure, clip type and number) and the definition of DPPB will be extracted from each included trial. For trials enrolling mixed polyp types, data specific to the non-pedunculated subgroup will be extracted where possible. When necessary data are not included in the published papers, the first or corresponding authors will be contacted for additional information. If there is no reply, we will analyse only the available data.

Assessment of risk of bias in included studies

Two investigators (HSM and BJN) will independently assess the methodological quality of included trials using the Cochrane Risk of Bias version 2 (RoB 2) tool.13 Disagreements will be resolved by consultation with a third investigator (ZJ). Five domains will be evaluated: randomisation process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome and selection of the reported result. Overall risk of bias will be categorised as ‘low’, ‘some concerns', or ‘high’. Discrepancies in risk of bias judgements will be resolved through iterative discussion; unresolved disagreements will be referred to a third senior reviewer (ZJ) for definitive resolution.

Data synthesis

Dichotomous outcome measures will be analysed by calculating pooled risk ratios (RR) with corresponding 95% CIs. Continuous outcomes will be expressed as mean differences (MD) with 95% CIs. Owing to expected clinical and methodological diversity across trials, we will use a random-effects model for all analyses. Heterogeneity among studies will be assessed by calculating the I2 statistics, with the following classification: 0%–40% (low), 30%–60% (moderate), 50%–90% (substantial) and 75%–100% (considerable) heterogeneity;14 if at least 10 studies are included in the primary-outcome meta-analysis, we will construct funnel plots to evaluate publication bias;15 and Egger test for low sample size with a significance level of 0.05 and Begg and Mazumdar test for high sample volume at a significance level of 0.1.16 All statistical analyses will be performed using Review Manager 5.4.1 (The Cochrane Collaboration, The Nordic Cochrane Centre, Copenhagen, Denmark).

Meta-regression analysis

A random-effects meta-regression analysis will be conducted to elucidate the impact of prespecified covariates (eg, number of participants, mean age, percentage of males, percentage of proximal polyps, percentage of large polyps, completeness of closure) on treatment effects, provided that a sufficient number of studies are available to support reliable modelling.

Subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses

In the case of possible strong heterogeneity, we will explore the possible sources using subgroup analyses. Prespecified subgroup analyses will be performed based on polyp size (≥ 20 mm vs <20 mm), location (proximal vs distal), histology (adenoma vs serrated), antithrombotic therapy (receiving therapy vs not receiving therapy), resection technique (hot snare polypectomy vs EMR vs ESD) and lesion morphology (sessile vs superficial). For those subgroups with only one study included, subgroup analyses will not be performed. Concurrently, sensitivity analyses will evaluate the stability of estimates by systematically excluding studies with high risk of bias or major methodological limitations.

Summary of evidence

The certainty of evidence for each outcome will be graded using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation framework, assessing risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias.17 The quality of evidence will be rated as high, moderate, low or very low.

Discussion

The clinical debate over prophylactic clip placement in colorectal polypectomy persists, primarily driven by the discordant findings of recent high-profile randomised trials.51820 While certain data advocate for routine clipping in proximal LNPCPs, multicentre studies like the CLIPPER trial have challenged the universal utility of this intervention.5 This SRMA is designed to address these inconsistencies by isolating non-pedunculated lesions resected via thermal techniques. By excluding pedunculated polyps, we aim to eliminate the confounding influence of stalk-related bleeding, thereby providing a clearer assessment of clipping efficacy in managing broad-based mucosal defects. This SRMA will provide a rigorous, comprehensive synthesis of the current evidence base. The results are expected to refine professional guidelines and promote a more evidence-based allocation of endoscopic resources.

Limitation

This SRMA has several anticipated limitations related to both the available evidence and the review process: (1) The evidence base will consist solely of RCTs, which minimise confounding and selection bias; however, all included trials are open-label due to the nature of the intervention, and performance or detection bias may therefore be present, so the overall certainty of evidence for the primary outcome will need to be interpreted accordingly. (2) Definitions of DPPB are likely to differ across studies, which may introduce substantial clinical and methodological heterogeneity. (3) Published RCTs in this field primarily define DPPB within a 30-day window, and few have reported long-term outcomes such as recurrent bleeding beyond 30 days or polyp recurrence. Consequently, our review will be unable to address these endpoints. (4) Cost-effectiveness analyses are rarely incorporated into endoscopic trial designs, and therefore an economic evaluation will not be feasible.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval and patient written informed consent will not be required because all analyses in the present study will be performed based on data from published studies. We will disseminate the findings of our work through conference presentations and a peer-reviewed publication.
