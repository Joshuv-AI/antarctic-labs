 Truchas Overview
 Zechariah Jibben, zjibben@lanl.gov

Truchas and Truchas-PBF are two sister codes for part-scale multi-physics modeling of
manufacturing processes. Both programs are open source and made publicly available.
They’re designed for eCicient use of HPC resources and can be programmatically driven from
Python packages. This enables automatic execution and analysis of ensembles of
simulations, in some cases allowing 1000s of simulations to be evaluated in a day on HPC.
Beyond just giving engineers a window into the concealed internal state of a system, the goal
of Truchas is to provide a framework for developing novel manufacturing processes by
understanding how the entire space of engineering inputs a9ects thermal state. It often is
used to explore combinations of capabilities uncommon in commercial software, or to scale
up analyses beyond the capabilities of commercial software.

 Truchas is the ﬂagship code for complex geometry simulations, involving a broader
array of physics capabilities: heat transfer, phase change, diCusion, multiphase
incompressible ﬂuid ﬂow, view-factor radiation, Joule & dielectric electromagnetic heating,
and outputs for microstructure models. It has been used by MST, PT, and Sigma for vacuum
induction melt casting, E-division for glass additive manufacturing, and ORNL for
solidiﬁcation research.

 The current main thrust for Truchas work is microwave cast modeling to support Y-12
and Sigma. We recently developed a new solver to add this capability and are working on
understanding the fundamentals of arcing and carbide diCusion to answer essential
production challenges. Iterative parameter sweeps are improving our understanding of the
electromagnetic and thermal states of the Y-12 furnace, while future studies of basic
geometries in a Sigma furnace aim to provide foundational data for materials studies.

 Truchas-PBF is an optimized variant for modeling the melt pool in additive
manufacturing and welding. It uses a block-structured AMR mesh to support heat transfer,
phase change, and ﬂuid ﬂow, and is often 20-100x faster than Truchas on these problems by
exploiting simple geometry. It has been used by ORNL for AM modeling and has recently
been used by Sigma for AM and welding.

 Current Truchas-PBF work is focused on using it as an eCicient testbed for
understanding how the inputs to any physics software must change when small material
composition changes cause large welding behavior diCerences. We are adding keyholing
capability to broaden the scope of applicability to more production environments.
