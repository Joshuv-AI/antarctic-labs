Trends in the Use of Anamorelin Hydrochloride for Cancer Cachexia: Examining the Timing of Anamorelin Initiation
Authors: Matoka Okamoto, Koji Karasawa, Masahiro Chiba
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCancer cachexia is a clinically important condition associated with worsening nutritional status and difficulty in continuing anticancer treatment. Although multimodal approaches combining exercise, nutritional support, and pharmacological treatment may improve symptoms and delay disease progression, the optimal timing of pharmacological intervention remains unclear. Conventional diagnostic criteria have largely been based on Western populations and may not adequately identify patients at an earlier stage in Asian populations. In 2023, the Asian Working Group for Cachexia (AWGC) proposed diagnostic criteria more suitable for Asian populations; however, it remains unclear to what extent this change has been reflected in real-world prescribing practices for anamorelin. In this study, we examined the anamorelin prescribing trends before and after the introduction of the AWGC criteria and evaluated the associations of cachexia stage and baseline performance status (PS) with treatment continuation.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective chart review of patients who received anamorelin between April 2021 and March 2025 at Showa Medical University Hospital and Showa Medical University Northern Yokohama Hospital. The primary outcome was the duration of treatment continuation. Secondary evaluation focused on chronological changes in the PS. Prescribing patterns before and after the introduction of the AWGC criteria were compared, and subgroup analyses were performed according to Fearon’s classification and baseline PS.ResultsOf 130 patients prescribed anamorelin during the study period, data from 98 were included in the final analysis after exclusions. Overall, 68 and 30 patients were classified into the pre-AWGC and post-AWGC groups, respectively, with no major differences in key background characteristics or clear changes in the prescribing patterns between the groups. According to Fearon’s classification, 90 and 8 patients had cachexia and pre-cachexia, respectively. The pre-cachexia group exhibited a significantly longer continuation duration than did the cachexia group (median: 211.5 vs. 34.5 days, log-rank p = 0.013). Patients with baseline PS of 0–1 demonstrated a significantly longer continuation duration than did those with a PS of 2–4 (median 71 vs. 25 days, log-rank p < 0.001). No marked deterioration in the PS over time was observed.ConclusionThe prescribing patterns of anamorelin did not substantially change after the introduction of the AWGC criteria. Conversely, longer treatment continuation was observed in patients at an earlier stage of cachexia and in those with preserved baseline PS. Thus, in addition to updated diagnostic criteria, earlier intervention and initiation at a stage with better general condition may be important when optimizing the timing of anamorelin treatment.

Introduction

Complete recovery in cancer cachexia cannot be achieved through conventional nutritional support alone, leading to progressive functional impairment. Cancer cachexia is defined as a multifactorial syndrome characterized by an ongoing loss of skeletal muscle mass, with or without loss of fat mass [1]. The incidence of cancer cachexia has been reported to be 50–80%, accounting for up to 20% of cancer-related deaths [2, 3]. Furthermore, in addition to survival rate, weight loss is associated with decreased treatment tolerability to chemotherapy (increased adverse events) and decreased treatment continuity (treatment interruption) [4, 5]. Therefore, active therapeutic intervention is warranted for this condition.

In 2008, Evans et al. [6] proposed the diagnostic criteria for cachexia as “a state in the presence of an underlying disease, with weight loss of more than 5% within 12 months as the principal condition, plus at least 3 of the following: decreased muscle strength, fatigue, anorexia, low fat-free mass, biochemical abnormalities (elevated C-reactive protein [CRP] or interleukin-6), anemia, and hypoalbuminemia”.

In 2011, the international consensus by Fearon et al. [1] classified cancer cachexia into three stages: pre-cachexia, cachexia, and refractory cachexia. Pre-cachexia was defined as a condition with weight loss ≤5% over the past 6 months, with clinical features such as anorexia and metabolic abnormalities. Cachexia was defined as a condition accompanied by reduced oral intake or systemic inflammation, meeting any of the following: (1) weight loss ≥5% over the past 6 months, (2) body mass index (BMI) <20 kg/m2 and weight loss >2%, or (3) sarcopenia and weight loss >2%. Refractory cachexia was defined as a stage presenting with clinical findings including catabolic progression and resistance to anticancer treatment, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 3–4, and predicted survival <3 months, in addition to the pathological condition of cachexia [1].

Treatment for cancer cachexia has consisted of non-pharmacological therapy centered on nutritional therapy and exercise therapy/rehabilitation. In practice, multidimensional intervention is recommended, including ensuring energy and protein intake based on nutritional assessment, using oral nutritional supplements as needed, and combining physical activity with resistance training according to the patient’s condition [7]. From a nutritional aspect, recommendations advocate for nutritional intake strategies that are feasible for patients and their families through counseling and other means [8]. However, these interventions alone are often insufficient in reducing body weight and skeletal muscle mass in cancer cachexia associated with persistent inflammation and metabolic abnormalities [5], and the need for a treatment strategy combining pharmacological therapy while using non-pharmacological therapy as the foundation has been pointed out.

In Japan, anamorelin hydrochloride (anamorelin), a ghrelin-like agent, became available in 2021 as a therapeutic drug for the treatment of cancer cachexia. The drug was clinically introduced to support nutritional intake in patients with cancer cachexia by addressing the loss of appetite, a primary symptom of cancer cachexia. The indications and administration criteria in the package insert are set in a form consistent with the framework of the European Palliative Care Research Collaborative (EPCRC) [9, 10]. However, applying the criteria established primarily in Europe without modification may result in more advanced disease at the time of treatment initiation in Asian populations, which include a higher proportion of lean individuals. Additionally, data from clinical practice have indicated that the PS and nutritional/inflammatory indices at the time of initiation may be associated with early discontinuation of anamorelin and duration of treatment continuation [11, 12].

In 2023, the Asian Working Group for Cachexia (AWGC) published diagnostic criteria for cachexia that take into account the body composition of Asian populations. According to the AWGC criteria, cachexia is diagnosed when, in addition to the presence of an etiology (e.g., cancer), the patient meets the criterion of “weight loss >2% within 3–6 months or BMI <21 kg/m2,” and at least one of the following criteria: anorexia, decreased handgrip strength, or elevated CRP (>0.5 mg/dL) [13]. Under these criteria, some cases that had previously been classified as pre-cachexia may also be recategorized as cachexia, thereby enabling earlier intervention. In an Asian population with gastric cancer, the AWGC criteria identified more cases of cachexia than the Fearon’s criteria did, with a reported association with prognosis and postoperative outcomes [14].

While diagnostic criteria are being updated to enable earlier intervention, indications for and decisions to initiate pharmacological treatment are not being optimized at the same speed and, in clinical practice, variability may arise in the timing of initiation and patient selection. However, changes in the prescribing trends and treatment continuation status of anamorelin before and after the introduction of the AWGC criteria have not been sufficiently examined. Furthermore, although prospective studies have evaluated interventions initiated at the pre-cachexia stage and examined their impact on clinical outcomes, current evidence remains limited because of the restricted range in cancer types, intervention contents, and outcome evaluations [15].

In this study, we investigated the trends of anamorelin use in clinical practice and, by comparing the prescribing patterns before and after the introduction of the AWGC criteria, examined the impact of changes in diagnostic criteria on treatment initiation. Furthermore, we examined differences in patient background between pre-cachexia and cachexia groups and assessed the association between baseline PS and treatment continuation to explore the clinical implications of anamorelin initiation timing in Asian patients with cancer.

Methods

Study Design

A retrospective chart review was conducted for patients who received anamorelin hydrochloride between April 2021 and March 2025 at Showa Medical University Hospital and Showa Medical University Northern Yokohama Hospital.

Study Participants and Exclusion Criteria

Among patients prescribed anamorelin at the two institutions during the target period, the following were excluded: (1) cases in which the initial prescription was issued at institutions other than the study sites, (2) cases in which treatment was discontinued within 7 days after initiation and evaluation of efficacy was difficult, (3) cases that did not meet any of the Evans, EPCRC, or AWGC criteria based on the medical record information at the time of treatment initiation (changes in body weight, BMI, laboratory values as inflammatory and nutritional indicators, etc.), and (4) duplicate cases due to reinitiation in the same patient, etc.

In routine clinical practice, anamorelin initiation was determined by the treating physician based on the indications and prescribing criteria described in the Japanese package insert, as well as the patient’s general condition, appetite loss and weight loss, disease progression, concomitant treatment, and institutional or departmental practice. Based on the international consensus by Fearon et al. [1], disease stage classification of cachexia was performed using information at the time of treatment initiation. Cachexia was determined by “weight loss >5% over the past 6 months,” “BMI <20 kg/m2 with weight loss >2%,” or “(when assessable) sarcopenia with weight loss >2%.” Pre-cachexia was defined as a stage in which weight loss was mild (generally ≤5%) and accompanied by early findings such as anorexia and metabolic abnormalities. Weight loss rate was calculated from the body weight recorded in the medical record 3–6 months before treatment initiation. Anorexia was defined based on documented decreased appetite or anorexia in the medical record (physician consultation record or nurse interview form). Metabolic abnormalities were defined based on whether inflammatory and nutrition-related laboratory values at the time of treatment initiation (e.g., CRP, albumin, hemoglobin) deviated from the reference ranges at each institution. For comparison before and after the introduction of the AWGC criteria, cases with an initial anamorelin prescription date on or before August 31, 2023, were defined as the pre-AWGC group, whereas cases with an initial prescription date on or after September 1, 2023, were defined as the post-AWGC group.

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome measure was the duration of continued anamorelin administration (time until discontinuation). The starting date was defined as the initial prescription date, and the discontinuation date was defined as the end date of the prescription, regardless of outpatient or inpatient setting, unless a clear discontinuation date was documented. Cases continuing treatment at the end of the observation period were censored.

As a secondary outcome measure, chronological changes in the PS were evaluated. The package insert states that “if weight gain or improvement in appetite is not observed, treatment should in principle be discontinued approximately 3 weeks after treatment initiation”; therefore, the evaluation time points were set at the start of treatment and every 3 weeks thereafter. However, because the actual intervals between visits were not uniform across cases, the data were organized by assessment point, rather than by fixed weeks. Given that the PS is an ordinal scale, the PS distribution at each assessment point was descriptively visualized using 100% stacked bar graphs stratified by the initial PS 0–1 group and the initial PS 2–4 group, with the number of evaluable cases at each assessment point also indicated in the figure. Patient background and clinical information (sex, age, body weight, BMI, laboratory values, prescription period, etc.) were collected from the medical records.

Statistical Analysis

JMP Student Edition 18 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) was used for statistical analysis. For categorical variables, Pearson’s chi-square test was applied, and Fisher’s exact test was used when expected frequencies were small. For continuous variables, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used. Duration of continued treatment was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were evaluated using the log-rank test. The significance level was set at p < 0.05 (two-sided).

For chronological changes in the PS, the distribution at each assessment point was descriptively evaluated using 100% stacked bar graphs. As a reference analysis, ordinal logistic regression was performed with the PS as the dependent variable and cachexia group/pre-cachexia group, assessment point, and the group × assessment point interaction as explanatory variables. No statistical testing was performed for the PS distribution stratified by the PS 0–1 group and the PS 2–4 group at the time of initiation, given that this stratification was based on baseline PS; as such, distribution at each assessment point was shown descriptively.

This study is reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology statement [16]. The completed STROBE checklist is provided as online supplementary material (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000553255).

Ethical Considerations

This study was conducted with the approval of the Ethics Committee for Research Involving Human Subjects at Showa Medical University (approval number: 2024-244-B). As this was a retrospective chart review, consent for participation was implemented by opt-out. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and the data were anonymized with consideration for the protection of personal information.

Results

Study Participants

During the study period, 130 patients were prescribed anamorelin. The following patients were then excluded according to the exclusion criteria: 2 patients whose initial prescription was made at institutions other than the study institutions, 18 who discontinued administration within 7 days after treatment initiation and were difficult to evaluate, 11 who did not meet any of the Evans, EPCRC, or AWGC criteria, and 1 duplicate case (e.g., reinitiation in the same patient). Thus, data from 98 patients were included in the analysis (Fig. 1).

Flow diagram of patient selection. During the study period, 130 patients received anamorelin. After exclusion of 2 patients whose initial prescription was issued outside the two study institutions, 18 patients who discontinued treatment within 7 days and were not evaluable, 11 patients who did not meet any of the Evans, European Palliative Care Research Collaborative, or Asian Working Group for Cachexia criteria, and 1 duplicate case (reintroduction in the same patient), 98 patients were included in the final analysis.

We further reviewed the medical records of the 18 patients who discontinued anamorelin within 7 days after initiation or whose actual administration was absent or very limited despite prescription. The reasons were as follows: clinical deterioration, hospitalization or transfer, difficulty continuing oral medications, or very limited actual exposure in 8 patients; gastrointestinal obstruction in 3 patients; gastrointestinal symptoms, such as nausea or diarrhea, in 3 patients; refusal or difficulty taking medication in 1 patient; concern about worsening of diabetes mellitus due to excessive appetite improvement in 1 patient; and insufficient documentation or unclear details in 2 patients. When multiple factors were present, each patient was classified according to the primary reason judged to be most directly related to early discontinuation or limited administration.

Anamorelin Continuation Rate according to Fearon’s Classification (Cachexia versus Pre-Cachexia)

The anamorelin administration continuation rate was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method (Fig. 2). The pre-cachexia group demonstrated a significantly higher continuation rate than the cachexia group did (log-rank test, p = 0.013). The median continuation period (95% confidence interval) was 34.5 (25–47) days in the cachexia group and 211.5 (14–298) days in the pre-cachexia group. The follow-up period began from the date of initiation and lasted until the end of administration or the final confirmation date. Patients were classified according to the PS at initiation into the PS 0–1 (n = 54) and PS 2–4 (n = 44) groups, and the continuation rate of anamorelin administration was analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method (Fig. 3). Discussion

Cancer cachexia is a multifactorial syndrome characterized by inflammation and metabolic abnormalities. Cancer cachexia is known to progress from pre-cachexia to cachexia and refractory cachexia thereafter, and considering intervention at an earlier stage is essential [1]. Supportive care alone, such as nutritional therapy and exercise therapy, is insufficient to adequately improve the loss of body weight and skeletal muscle mass in cancer cachexia. Furthermore, as weight loss and cancer cachexia are associated with poor prognosis and may impair chemotherapy tolerability and treatment continuity, intervention combining pharmacotherapy, as needed, while using supportive care as the foundation, is clinically important [4, 5]. In particular, changes such as weight and appetite loss at the pre-cachexia stage are relatively mild, and there may still be room for intervention combining supportive care and pharmacotherapy.

Anamorelin is a drug introduced to support nutritional intake through appetite improvement in patients with cancer cachexia, and it is positioned as a therapeutic option that complements supportive care [9, 10]. Weight loss in patients with cancer includes an aspect associated with increased catabolism due to tumor progression and systemic inflammation and an aspect mediated through appetite loss and reduced intake [4, 5]. In advanced cachexia, the former has a greater influence. Weight loss is thought to strongly reflect systemic metabolic abnormalities associated with the progression of the underlying disease. In contrast, at earlier stages, including pre-cachexia, appetite loss and insufficient dietary intake may play a more prominent role. Anamorelin may contribute to the maintenance and improvement of body weight and lean body mass through improvement of appetite and intake during early stages of weight loss, suggesting greater benefit when introduced before the disease state has fully progressed, that is, at an earlier stage, including pre-cachexia [10]. Weight loss is not merely a nutritional indicator; in addition to appetite loss and insufficient intake, it reflects clinical findings such as systemic inflammation, increased catabolism, reduced skeletal muscle mass, and reduced systemic reserve. Weight loss can therefore lead to deterioration of the PS, reduced tolerability to chemotherapy, and difficulty continuing treatment. Thus, although maintenance or improvement of body weight does not directly indicate prolongation of survival, it may serve as an indicator of relative suppression of the pathological condition progression associated with poor prognosis. However, optimal anamorelin initiation timing and patient selection remain unclear. In clinical practice, prescribing decisions may be influenced not only by diagnostic criteria but also by factors such as the PS at initiation, inflammatory and nutritional status, and degree of disease progression.

In this context, the introduction of new diagnostic criteria by the AWGC can be regarded as an important update of the framework for identifying cachexia and considering intervention at an earlier stage in Asian populations [13, 14]. However, even if diagnostic criteria are updated, the decision to introduce pharmacotherapy will not necessarily immediately reflect that change. Additionally, whether early introduction of anamorelin directly leads to prolonged survival is not yet fully elucidated. Notably, the duration of anamorelin treatment is reportedly associated with prognosis [12], and thus, examining the relationship between the timing of initiation and clinical outcomes is important. Therefore, we examined whether changes in the actual prescribing patterns of anamorelin were observed before and after the proposal of the AWGC criteria. We also examined treatment continuation in patients who were treated at the pre-cachexia stage and in patients with preserved baseline PS. The Japanese package insert criteria for anamorelin are generally consistent with the EPCRC/Fearon framework, whereas the AWGC criteria were proposed later to better reflect the body composition of Asian patients. Therefore, the diagnostic framework used to identify cachexia and the criteria used to prescribe anamorelin may not have been fully aligned. In routine clinical practice in Japan, prescribing decisions are generally made in accordance with the indications and prescribing criteria described in the package insert, as well as institutional and departmental practice. Therefore, even after the proposal of the AWGC criteria, the updated diagnostic criteria may not have been immediately reflected in the prescribing criteria or decisions for anamorelin. This may partly explain why no clear change was observed in the prescribing patterns after the proposal of the AWGC criteria. In addition, as this was a retrospective chart review, we could not determine whether each prescribing physician was explicitly aware of the AWGC criteria at the time of prescription or whether the prescribing decision was directly based on these criteria. As a result, although no major changes were observed in prescribing patterns before and after the proposal of the AWGC criteria, the pre-cachexia group, and the group with preserved baseline PS showed a tendency toward longer treatment continuation. These results suggest that, in addition to updating diagnostic criteria, intervention at an earlier stage and at a stage in which the general condition is preserved may be important.

In this study, no differences were observed in the demographics of patients treated before and after the proposal of the AWGC criteria. We initially expected, due to the update in diagnostic criteria, the number of patients who are treated to increase in cases exhibiting a milder stage of weight loss, namely, pre-cachexia. However, an increase in the pre-cachexia group was not evident, with no major change in the total number of prescriptions itself. These findings suggest that, even if diagnostic criteria are updated, decisions regarding the introduction of anamorelin in actual clinical practice may not have immediately led to changes in prescribing behavior, as such decisions are governed not only by the AWGC criteria but also by the indications and prescribing criteria in the anamorelin package insert. Additionally, treatment initiation decisions are influenced by multiple factors, including the PS, degree of appetite loss and weight loss, progression status of the underlying disease, concomitant treatment, and operational practices by the department; therefore, patient selection may not have changed immediately following the update in diagnostic criteria. In the future, observing the trends over a longer period of time and at more institutions is warranted.

The tendency toward a higher continuation rate in the pre-cachexia group represents an important clinical implication of this study. However, the limited number of cases in the pre-cachexia group warrants caution in interpreting the estimates, particularly the confidence intervals. As pre-cachexia is milder than cachexia, and general condition and nutritional status may be relatively preserved, this may have contributed to treatment continuation. In clinical practice, the PS and inflammatory/nutritional indices at the time of introduction are reportedly associated with early discontinuation. In cases with poor inflammatory and nutritional indices, poor general condition, including progression of the underlying disease, was present in addition to cachexia progression, and this may have affected anamorelin continuation [11, 17, 18]. The results of this study are consistent with these reports. Furthermore, studies suggest a link between prognosis and the duration of anamorelin treatment [12]. Collectively, the results of this study support the possibility that earlier intervention, including the stage before progression to cachexia, may similarly have clinical significance from the perspective of treatment continuation. Based on the above, optimizing intervention, including timing of initiation, is crucial.

In the analysis stratified by the PS at initiation, the baseline PS 0–1 group showed a tendency toward a higher continuation rate, consistent with the clinical observation that treatment is easier to continue when introduced at a stage where PS is preserved. PS is also an indicator of the reserve capacity to continue treatment [19] and may be influenced not only by the effect of the drug itself but also by factors such as the implementation of supportive care, concomitant treatment, disease status, and patient preference. Therefore, caution is warranted in directly concluding that the relationship between the PS and continuation reflects the effect of anamorelin itself. Additionally, in a prospective randomized trial targeting the pre-cachexia stage and using high-protein oral nutritional supplementation, PS, and quality of life (QOL) were reported to remain generally stable throughout the observation period [15]. Although the intervention and evaluation scales differ, such findings are consistent with the results of this study, in which the PS did not markedly worsen over time.

Anamorelin is a ghrelin receptor agonist that acts on appetite and body weight. In the international phase III trials (ROMANA 1/2), improvements in body weight and lean body mass were observed with anamorelin, whereas no significant improvement was shown in handgrip strength (muscle strength) [20]. Similar results were reported in the extension study (ROMANA 3) [21]. Subsequently, trials and studies of lung cancer and gastrointestinal cancer have also reported improvements in body weight, appetite, and nutritional status [17, 22–24]. Additionally, even in a domestic single-arm study targeting gastrointestinal cancer, safety information, including hyperglycemia, glucose metabolism-related events, and electrocardiographic changes, has been reported [25]. Thus, in clinical practice, decisions regarding anamorelin initiation should be made with consideration of comorbidities and monitoring plans. Considerations regarding the approval conditions and dosage/administration of anamorelin in Japan are presented in Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) review-related documents [26]. In contrast, approval was not granted in Europe, where the clinical utility of anamorelin, including the primary endpoints of ROMANA (e.g., function, muscle strength), remained a point of debate [27]. Additionally, the American Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines state a lack of sufficient evidence to recommend pharmacotherapy as standard treatment for cancer cachexia [8].

Based on these points, a gap remains between the outcomes emphasized in clinical trials (maintenance of muscle strength) and those more readily perceived by patients in daily clinical practice (appetite, dietary intake, body weight, ease of daily living). Furthermore, Asian populations generally have a smaller body size, and application of the conventional Europe-centered framework without modification may result in more advanced disease progression at the time of introduction [13, 14]. Therefore, further examination of the alignment between diagnostic criteria (AWGC) and treatment initiation/evaluation indicators is warranted.

The limitations of the present study are as follows. First, this was a retrospective observational study, and confounding factors could not be sufficiently adjusted for. Second, no comparison was made with patients who had cachexia but did not use anamorelin. Therefore, it is not possible to clearly distinguish whether the outcomes observed in this study were due to the administration of anamorelin itself or reflected the natural course, including patient selection and progression of the underlying disease. Third, this was a survey of only two institutions, and the influence of differences between institutions cannot be ruled out. Fourth, Fearon’s classification and AWGC items were based on information documented in the medical records, and errors may have arisen due to missing data and variability in evaluation timing. In addition, diabetes mellitus and changes in glycemic status were not systematically collected as predefined variables in this retrospective study. Therefore, the assessment of diabetes mellitus and hyperglycemia was limited to concomitant antidiabetic medication use at the time of anamorelin initiation and available medical record documentation. Fifth, the decision to continue treatment is influenced not only by adverse effects but also by disease progression, hospitalization, changes in concomitant treatment, and patient preference, which could not be sufficiently analyzed. Furthermore, appetite, dietary intake, nutritional status, QOL, and physical function were not systematically assessed using standardized measures. Therefore, this study cannot determine whether longer continuation of anamorelin directly resulted in improvements in these outcomes. Future studies should conduct analyses using multicenter, large-scale data with adjustment for confounding factors, and evaluate more precisely the relationship between the timing of anamorelin initiation and clinical outcomes, including patient-reported outcomes (appetite, QOL) and physical function indicators.

In this study, longer treatment continuation was observed in the pre-cachexia group and in patients with preserved baseline PS, whereas no clear differences in patient characteristics were observed before and after the proposal of the AWGC criteria. However, treatment continuation should be interpreted as an indicator of treatment feasibility rather than direct evidence of clinical benefit. Future studies using multicenter data, including information on the implementation of AWGC criteria and analyses adjusted for confounding factors, are needed to more precisely evaluate the relationship between the timing of anamorelin initiation and clinical outcomes, including appetite, nutritional status, QOL, physical function, and prognosis.

Conclusion

In this retrospective two-center study, prescribing patterns of anamorelin did not substantially change after the proposal of the AWGC criteria. Conversely, longer treatment continuation was observed in patients with pre-cachexia and in those with preserved baseline PS. Therefore, in addition to updated diagnostic criteria, earlier initiation at a stage with better general condition may be important when optimizing the timing of anamorelin treatment in clinical practice. Further multicenter studies adjusted for confounding factors are warranted to validate these findings.
