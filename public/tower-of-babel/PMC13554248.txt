Swin-SHARP: a novel approach to wheat disease classification using boosted MAML and weighted ensembling with deep learning classifiers
Authors: Waqar Khalid, Yazeed Alkhrijah, Shehzad Khalid, Syed Muhammad Usman, Sulieman S Alshuhri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe global food security is severely threatened by various bacterial and fungal diseases that significantly degrade the quality, yield and productivity of wheat crop. This increases the need for an accurate and efficient system to improve wheat yield and mitigate these losses by enabling early intervention. The dataset used in this research comprises of 10,000 images from brown rust, yellow rust, powdery mildew, loose smut diseases and healthy wheat plants. The existing neural networks, ensembling and transformer-based models used for classifying wheat diseases are limited by high computational resource requirements that leads to inefficient feature extraction. These challenges are addressed by proposing a customized, lightweight and optimized Swin-Streamlined High Accuracy and Reduced Parameters (Swin-SHARP) transformer, a lightweight and optimized transformer model that enhances feature extraction while significantly reducing computational overhead. In particular, Swin-SHARP results in 82.5% reduction (48.9M to 8.5M parameters), making it an attractive solution for resource-constrained environments. The extracted features are further optimized by integrating the Swin-SHARP transformer with boosted Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) and a weighted ensembling strategy to enhance generalization and classification accuracy. Our proposed model achieves a remarkable 98.1% accuracy, significantly outperforming existing CNN-based solutions, ensemble approaches, transformer, and deep learning models. We also cross-validated our proposed model on an unseen wheat plant diseases dataset, achieving 95.57% accuracy. Our proposed model is also compared against prominent models such as Inception-v3, ResNet-18, and VGG-16, which outperforms them by 1.6%, 1.7%, and 2.6%, respectively. The comparison with existing state-of-the-art models, including Sequential CNN, SGDR-S, Inception-v3, Cereal Conv, Darknet-53 CNN, EfficientNet B3, GLNet, CNN & SVM, Customized CNN, CaiT-YOLOv9 and MSFNet revealed that our method outperforms them by 0.6%, 5.8%, 5.3%, 0.75%, 2.8%, 2.68%, 1.42%, 1.3%, 3.31%, 3.29%, and 2.4% respectively. These results demonstrate the effectiveness and practicality of the Swin-SHARP transformer for wheat disease classification, particularly for real-time agricultural applications on mobile and embedded systems aimed at early disease detection and crop management.Subject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Plant sciences

Introduction

Wheat is one of the main staple foods and crops in the world, with a global consumption of 783 million metric tons in 2020–20211. It is essential for food security and forms the backbone of the economies of all nations, particularly those in the developing world. Pakistan produces around 26.8 million metric tons of wheat each year and ranks eighth in the world2. The agricultural sector contributes towards 25% to the GDP of Pakistan. The crop report published in 2025 states that Pakistan produces 31 million metric tons of wheat; Punjab accounts for 76.4%, Sindh for 14%, Balochistan for 5.1% and KPK for 4.5% of the total share3, as illustrated in Fig. 1.

Province wise wheat production in Pakistan3,5.

The fungal, viral and bacterial infections rapidly spread through the wheat crops that directly impact its productivity, qualtiy and quantity, resulting in lower yields. Stripe rust is the commonly occuring and devastating fungal disease that accounts for 5.5 million tons of global wheat lost each year4. Accurate and timely identification and classification of wheat diseases are crucial for effective disease management and mitigation of these threats. Traditional methods are time-consuming and often require expert opinions, thus highlighting the need for advanced and automated wheat disease classification system.

The current approaches for wheat disease classification is hindered by the subtle distinctions between disease symptoms, emergence of new strains, intricate complexity and vast diversity of diseases, environmental factors promoting disease development and progression, scarcity of reliable data and absence of standardized procedures for accurate and timely classification of wheat diseases. A number of advanced and automated wheat disease classification techniques have been discussed with improved accuracy, efficiency, and reliability.

A number of supervised and unsupervised machine, and deep learning techniques have been proposed in literature to accurately classify wheat diseases. Usha et al. propose a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based model that integrates a modified ResNet50 architecture to leverage the spatial feature identification in wheat leaves6. This method is further enhanced by integrating multi-scale feature fusion methods7, MSCPNET8, and attention-based networks9 to specifically concentrate on disease impacted regions. Ensemble transfer learning methods and gravitational search are employed to achieve meta-heuristic optimization that improves performance and accuracy but at the cost of substantial computational resource requirements10–12.

Qushtom et al. developed explainable CNN frameworks to provide transparency into their wheat disease classification model decision-making13. A StripeRust-Pocket model is proposed by Liu et al. with the goal to deploy an affordable and portable solution on mobile devices, however, it trade off efficiency for performance14. The existing neural networks and ensembling models are parameter-intensive and require significant resources that limit their scalability on resource-constrained edge devices.

Recently, transformers have been frequently used in literature for precise classification and identification of plant diseases. The ability to capture long-range dependencies of Class attention image transformer (CaiT), MobilePlant Vision Transformer (ViT) and Multi-source fusion network (MSFNet) are leveraged to improve classification accuracy15–17. The local and global patterns of dpalnt diseases are effectively captured by integrating CNN feature extractors with vision transformers or hierarchical attention modules. A number of hardware-specific solutions such as Jetson GPU pipelines18 or augmentation-based approaches19 have been proposed to address real-world constraints, while maintaining good performance and efficiency.

The classification of plant diseases have been revolutionized by introducing Swin transformers with shifted window-based self-attention mechanism. Swin transformer has been employed to accurately identify wheat diseases, however, this high accuracy and performance is achieved at the cost of 45 million parameters20. Yu et al. proposed Swin Transformer with Convolutional Feature Interactions (ST-CFI) to improve the classification performance on wheat datasets with complex backgrounds21. These models leverage attention mechanisms to prioritize accuracy over efficiency on large architectures and extensive training periods.

The scholars published various methods to prevent significant crop losses and classify plant diseases, (i) ResNet50 and InceptionV4, Xception, DenseNet121 and VGG1322,23, (ii) Pre-trained CNN and vision transformer24,25 and (iii) EfficientNetv1 and EfficientNetv226.

The gaps identified in the literature are addressed by proposing an optimized variant of Swin transformer to improve efficieny while maintaining accuracy. Our research proposes a novel Swin-SHARP transformer that achieves a remarkable 40.4 million reduction in paramters that significantly reduces computational complexity while maintaining high accuracy. The current neural networks, ensembling and transformer-based methods require high GPU-memory, whereas our proposed Swin-SHARP model offers a lightweight design.

Methods

A novel approach is adapted to develop a robust, efficient, and automated architecture for the early detection and identification of wheat diseases using deep learning techniques. The proposed framework is shown in Fig. 2. The initial step is data acquisition, which contains wheat images from five different classes, such as healthy, brown rust, yellow rust, powdery mildew, and loose smut. In pre-processing, the data acquired is processed using classical and deep learning techniques to enhance the volume of the dataset. In methodology, the features from the pre-processed images are extracted using basic Swin transformer types and several Swin-Small transformer variants to find the most accurate and computationally efficient method. The output of the Swin-SHARP is fed to a homogeneous ensemble method that involves three deep learning models, such as Softmax, Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), in combination with Adaboost and MAML techniques. Lastly, a weighted ensembling model, a heterogeneous ensembling technique, is used to find the most optimized, efficient, and accurate architecture.

Pre-processing

The publicly accessible wheat disease dataset only contains a limited number of images with class imbalance that causes poor performance due to either over-fitting or under-fitting. This issue is resolved by processing the obtained images through a variety of classical and deep learning augmentation techniques that also increase the capacity and heterogeneity of the present wheat disease dataset.

Classical data augmentation techniques employed in this study involve manual manipulation of all images in the wheat disease dataset through tasks such as cropping, flipping, and rotating, as shown in Fig. 4. The areas of interest of the wheat healthy and diseased photos were manually cropped27. Every image in the dataset was rotated at a random angle between 0 and 360 degrees, creating a new image with a different orientation27. A mirrored replica of the original image is created by flipping it horizontally, which strengthens the resilience of the suggested model.

Cropping, flipping, and rotation data augmentation techniques on wheat disease dataset.

Deep learning data augmentation techniques are used to further improve the quantity and magnitude of the image dataset for wheat disease. This technique produces new unseen image data that closely mimic the original data distribution by creating a significantly larger dataset, which improves the robustness and applicability of the wheat disease classification model.

DDPM algorithm

A Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM)28 algorithm is used to create new images that closely approximated the actual data distribution. A technique that iteratively applies random Gaussian noise to images simulating the natural unpredictability and resulting in a huge dataset with genuine variations in texture, color and contrast.

The DDPM consists of a forward and reverse process. In the forward process, the Markov chain principle is used to create noisy samples , ,....., and by gradually applying small quantities of random Gaussian noise to original image in T steps. This process is also known as diffusion or noising process. The reverse process also uses the Markov chain principle to gradually denoise the image produced by the first process to create , ,...., as shown in Fig. 5. This process is also known as the denoising process. A stochastic gradient descent training algorithm, as mentioned in Algorithm 1, iteratively samples initial states, time steps, and noise vectors to minimize the difference between transformed and original noise vectors to converge to optimal model parameters. A sampling procedure for DDPM, as mentioned in Algorithm 2, refines a noise vector through iterative transformations using a learned function and noise schedules to produce a final sample from the model distribution.

Feature extraction

A transformer is a neural network design that utilizes context learning and sequence element interactions to translate an input sequence into an output sequence. A transformer is composed of two layers: one for encrypting and the other for decoding. The encryption layer consists of the self-attention sub-layer and the position-wise feedforward layer. The decoding is composed of the self-attention layer, the encoder-decoder attention layer, and the position-wise feedforward layer.

SWIN transformer

The Swin Transformer29 is a hierarchical vision transformer model that processes images using self-attention strategies. The Swin transformer builds a multilayered representation by first segmenting images into small patches and then iteratively combining adjacent patches to produce higher-level features, as shown in Fig. 6. A fixed number of computations and partitioning images are used, with each image divided into non-overlapping windows; this method achieves linear computational complexity.

Hierarchical structure of Swin transformer.

The Swin transformer creates a set of visual elements with a specified form (H/4, W/4, 16 color channels) by employing a patch partition to divide an input image into multiple distinct and contiguous parts. The new set of visual elements with a modified shape (H/4, W/4, variable color channels) is created by first passing these pieces through a linear transformation and then processing them subsequently through two interconnected Swin transformer blocks. In the second, third, and fourth stages, patch merging and Swin transformer blocks are used to create elements that can be visually depicted with smaller spatial dimensions and more color channels (H/8, W/8 - double color channels, H/16, W/16 - quadruple color channels, and H/32, W/32 - octuple color channels). The input data is processed by the Swin transformer architecture using customized windows, as demonstrated in Fig. 7.

Architecture diagram of Swin transformer.

Boosted MAML algorithm

Boosted MAML algorithm is a robust and adaptive learning model that integrates the Adaboost ensemble with the MAML algorithm. Adaboost combines multiple base models to improve prediction accuracy, while a meta-learning algorithm, MAML, learns to fine-tune models for new tasks. In the boosted MAML algorithm, the base models in Adaboost can be meta-trained using MAML, allowing them to adapt to new tasks and datasets more effectively. This integration leads to improved performance and generalization in various applications. The MAML algorithm employs a task division strategy, dividing the main classification task into 150 smaller sets. These sets are further split into training and testing tasks, with 100 sets dedicated to training and 50 sets reserved for testing to evaluate model performance. Each training set consists of 4 different classes, and is further subdivided into 10 support images for training and 15 query images for testing per class, resulting in more manageable sub-tasks that enable more efficient and effective disease identification. With 25 images per class, resulting in each set has 100 images. So in total, the 50 sets reserved for testing have 5000 images, whereas the 100 sets for training contain 10,000 images.

Heterogeneous ensembling algorithm

Heterogeneous ensembling is a technique used to improve the prediction accuracy by combining classification models with different architectures. In this study, we explore the application of weighted ensembling35 and boosted MAML with various deep learning classifiers for wheat disease identification. Weighted Ensembling Algorithm, as mentioned in Algorithm 4, implements a weighted ensembling technique for deep learning models by training multiple models on a dataset and computing their accuracy on a validation set alongside iteratively calculating weights for each model based on their accuracy, with higher accuracy resulting in higher weights. Finally, the combined prediction of all models using the calculated weights to produce a final ensemble output, which is evaluated on a test set to improve the overall accuracy by leveraging the strengths of multiple models.

Effect of Swin-SHARP transformer on wheat disease classification

Swin-S transformer accurately classifies wheat diseases with 97.7% accuracy and high computational resource requirements. In this research, we propose and evaluate eight variants of Swin-S transformer with different number of channels, layers and heads as outlined in the Table 7 to find the most optimized and accurate variant. A datailed ablation studies is conducted by inputing these varaints with 256x256 image, 32 batch size, 0.003 learning rate and 50 training epochs and evaluating them on the basis of training time, epoch time, inference time, GPU utilization and FLOPs. Sv-III is the least computationally complex model, requiring 8.6 GB of GPU memory, 0.69 GMac FLOPs to achieve an inference time of 26 ms per image and 95.9% accuracy. Although Sv-IV and Sv-VI variants exhibit slightly better performance due to their larger parameter sets, they consume significantly more GPU resources and have longer inference times compared to the SHARP transformer. This increased resource demand is attributed to their layer configuration of 2, 18, 2, in contrast to the SHARP transformer with 2, 2, 18 layer structure. The Sv-I variant is more lightweight in terms of GPU usage, consuming only 7.5 GB, 2.09 GMac FLOPs and 29 ms per image inference time but incurs higher parameters and lower accuracy of 96.8%. Our optimized SHARP transformer model achieves significant 82.5% parameter reduction by streamlining its architecture reducing number of channels by 50, removing the final stage of Swin-S transformer, modifying the number of layers from 2,2,18,2 to 2,2,18 and number of heads from 3,6,12,24 to 3,6,12. This optimized model operates with 9.9 GB of GPU, 2.13 GMac FLOPs, and a 30 ms per image inference time, incurring only a marginal 0.2% accuracy loss compared to the Swin-S transformer. Meanwhile, Sv-II and Sv-V have over 33 million parameters, far exceeding the SHARP transformer 8,534,399 parameters. They also incur significantly higher FLOPs, longer inference times as compared to optimized SHARP transformer. Conclusion

In this research, we addressed the knowledge gap, where current approaches often prioritize accuracy at the cost of computational expense and resource intensity. A novel efficient solution is proposed that leverages the strengths of our customized Swin-SHARP transformer, which is a streamlined variant of the Swin-S architecture, by integrating it with deep learning classifiers, Boosted Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML), and weighted ensembling for accurate wheat disease identification. The Swin-SHARP transformer achieves a substantial 82.5% reduction (48.9M to 8.5M parameters) while still maintaining a high accuracy of 98.1% as compared to traditional Swin-S transformer. Our model also outperforms deep learning, neural networks, and ensembling methods in the literature to classify wheat diseases by offering faster training and lower GPU memory requirements.

The scarcity of publicly available wheat disease datasets necessitated the use of data augmentation to generate a balanced dataset comprising 10,000 images. Although our wheat disease detection model is trained exclusively on a wheat disease dataset, it demonstrates strong performance. We validated the effectiveness of our proposed model by testing it on an unseen wheat plant diseases dataset and achieve an exemplary 95.57% accuracy. This rigorous test validates the capabilities of our proposed model, showcasing its suitability for real-world deployment.
