Abstract
. We study the wave operators for a Jacobi matrix J J whose canonical spectral measure ρ \rho satisfies the Szegő condition. In particular, for γ s \gamma_{s} the Verblunsky coefficients of the measure associated to ρ \rho by the Szegő mapping, we prove existence and completeness of wave operators under the condition lim n → ∞ log ⁡ ( n ) ​ ∑ s = n 2 ​ n | γ s | 2 = 0 \lim_{n\to\infty}\log(n)\sum_{s=n}^{2n}|\gamma_{s}|^{2}=0 . 1.
Introduction

We consider semi-infinite Jacobi matrices

\displaystyle J=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&b_{1}&0&&&&\\
b_{1}&v_{2}&b_{2}&0&&&\\
0&b_{2}&v_{3}&b_{3}&0&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}

in the bounded and self-adjoint setting, where the Jacobi coefficients
\{b_{n}\}
and
\{v_{n}\}
satisfy
b_{n}>0,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\{b_{n}\},\{v_{n}\}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
.
Under these conditions,
J
defines a bounded self-adjoint operator
J:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
. Through the special case
b_{n}=1/2
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, one recovers discrete Schrödinger operators, which serve as Hamiltonians for lattice models in quantum mechanics. Jacobi matrices arise as a natural generalization of these operators, particularly from the perspective of inverse spectral theory discussed below. Our main interest in this work is the dynamics generated by
J
through the discrete time-dependent Schrödinger equation:

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}\psi=J\psi,\quad\psi(0)=f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}),

whose solution may be written in terms of the one-parameter unitary group as
\psi(t)=e^{itJ}f
.

By Favard’s theorem of classical analysis, there is a bijective correspondence between bounded semi-infinite Jacobi matrices
J
and compactly and infinitely supported (in the sense of cardinality) probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
. In the forward direction of this correspondence, one maps
J
to its canonical spectral measure,
\rho
. In this work, we consider Jacobi matrices whose corresponding measures
\rho
are supported on
[-1,1]
, and are in the Szegő class: given the decomposition of
\rho
with respect to the equilibrium measure
\omega
for the interval
[-1,1]
,

\displaystyle d\rho=\rho^{\prime}d\omega+\rho_{s},\,\,d\omega(x)=\pi^{-1}(1-x^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{1}_{[-1,1]}(x)dx,

we assume that
\rho
satisfies the Szegő condition:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\log\rho^{\prime}d\omega>-\infty\,.

Despite the inverse direction of this correspondence passing through the orthogonal polynomials on the real line (OPRL), much of our work here makes use of orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle (OPUC). In particular, exploiting a well-known relationship between OPRL with respect to measures supported on the interval
[-1,1]
, and OPUC with respect to a related measure, we may work on the circle and apply harmonic analysis techniques. Thus, our first result is most naturally stated in the OPUC terminology, and we introduce it now. We refer the reader to
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
for further background on the brief description that follows.

Let
\sigma
be an infinitely supported probability measure on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. Denote the orthonormal polynomials by
\phi_{n}(z)
and the monic orthogonal polynomials by
\Phi_{n}(z)
.
Clearly,
\phi_{n}=\Phi_{n}/\|\Phi_{n}\|_{2,\sigma}
. We will use notation
f^{*}(z)=z^{n}\overline{f(\bar{z}^{-1})}
for a polynomial
f
of degree
n
. The polynomials
\{\Phi_{n}\}
satisfy recurrence
[
23
, Theorem 1.5.2, p. 57]

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi_{n+1}(z)=z\Phi_{n}(z)-\bar{\gamma}_{n}\Phi_{n}^{*}(z),&\Phi_{0}(z)=1,\\
\Phi_{n+1}^{*}(z)=\Phi_{n}^{*}(z)-z\gamma_{n}\Phi_{n}(z),&\Phi_{0}^{*}(z)=1\,,\end{array}\right.

where
\gamma_{n}
are the Verblunsky coefficients corresponding to the measure
\sigma
. The map
\sigma\mapsto\{\gamma_{n}\}|_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
is a bijection, cf.
[
23
, p. 2]
.

Measures
\rho
supported on
[-1,1]
may be related to even measures
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
through the Szegő mapping
Sz
. We will write
\sigma=Sz^{-1}(\rho)
for the measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}
that satisfies
d\sigma(z)=d\sigma(1/z)
and

(1.3)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}f(z)d\sigma(z)=\int\limits_{[-1,1]}f(e^{i\arccos(x)})d\rho(x)

for any
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
with
f(z)=f(1/z),z\in\mathbb{T}

[
25
, p. 31, eq. (1.9.5)]
. Throughout the text,
\sigma
will refer to the measure related to
\rho
in this way. For such measures, all
\gamma_{n}
are real, and they may be related to the Jacobi coefficients by means of the direct Geronimus relations
[
25
, p. 33]

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}b_{k+1}&=\frac{1}{2}\left((1-\gamma_{2k-1})(1-\gamma_{2k}^{2})(1+\gamma_{2k+1})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
v_{k+1}&=-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{2k-2}(1+\gamma_{2k-1})-\gamma_{2k}(1-\gamma_{2k-1}))\,,\end{split}

with the convention
\gamma_{-1}:=-1
. If we denote the polynomials orthonormal with respect to
\rho

, then
[
25
, p. 32, eq. (1.9.12)]

(1.5)

\displaystyle p_{n}((z+1/z)/2)=(2(1-{\gamma}_{2n-1}))^{-1/2}z^{-n}(\phi_{2n}(z)+\phi_{2n}^{*}(z)),\,z\in\mathbb{T}\,.

Szegő’s theorem, cf.
[
25
, Theorem 1.8.6]
, is a cornerstone of the theory of OPUC; for the decomposition of
\sigma
with respect to the normalized Lebesgue measure
dm(z)
on
\mathbb{T}
,
d\sigma(z)=\sigma^{\prime}(z)dm(z)+\sigma_{s}(z)
, it states

\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-|\gamma_{n}|^{2})=\exp\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log(\sigma^{\prime}(z))dm(z)\right)\,,

which in turn implies

\displaystyle\{\gamma_{n}\}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})\iff\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log(\sigma^{\prime}(z))dm(z)>-\infty.

With this in hand, the identity (
1.3
) implies that the Szegő condition (
1.1
) on
J
is equivalent to the corresponding Verblunsky parameters satisfying
\{\gamma_{n}\}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
. Denoting by

the free operator with Jacobi coefficients
b_{n}=\frac{1}{2},v_{n}=0
for all

, we may combine this observation with the Geronimus relations to find that the condition (
1.1
) implies that
J-J_{0}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator. Thus, in addition to being a qualitative condition on the size and distribution of the absolutely continuous part of the measure
\rho
, this condition allows for
J
to be viewed as a perturbation of the free operator.

Both of these perspectives offer a first motivation for the study of the evolution generated by
J
, particularly in comparison to the free evolution
J_{0}
. Motivated by classical results of
[
2
,
20
]
and already given the condition (
1.1
), one may expect the typical state to spread out over
\mathbb{N}
, behaving as in the free evolution in a qualitative sense, meanwhile the perturbative perspective suggests a stronger and more direct comparison may hold between the two evolutions. Such a comparison is often encoded through the existence and completeness of wave operators
\Omega_{\pm}
, a by now well-studied and classical topic. We refer the reader to the foundational scattering theory papers
[
1
,
5
,
11
]
, as well as the textbook treatments of the subject found in
[
14
,
17
,
22
,
27
]
.

When they exist, wave operators are defined as the strong
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
-limits

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\pm\infty}e^{-iTJ}e^{iTJ_{0}}=:\Omega_{\pm}.

We denote by
\ell^{2}_{\rm ac}(\mathbb{N})
the absolutely continuous subspace for

[

\displaystyle\ell^{2}_{\rm ac}(\mathbb{N}):=\{\psi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}):\mu_{\psi}\text{ is a.c.}\},

where

is spectral measure of
\psi

.

When
\Omega_{\pm}
exist, the inclusion
\text{Ran}(\Omega_{\pm})\subseteq\ell^{2}_{\rm ac}(\mathbb{N})
always holds. Completeness is defined as the equality
\text{Ran}(\Omega_{\pm})=\ell^{2}_{\rm ac}(\mathbb{N})
, so that
\Omega_{\pm}
are unitary maps from
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
onto
\ell^{2}_{\rm ac}(\mathbb{N})
. This equality yields the comparison between the two evolutions alluded to above; indeed, once this is established, one has that every state in
\ell^{2}_{\rm ac}(\mathbb{N})
behaves asymptotically freely. Establishing these results under (
1.1
), along with a mild quantitative condition on the tail of the series for
\{\gamma_{n}\}
, will be the main goal of our work here. For the reader more familiar with the OPUC literature, we note that this is a significantly more delicate task than existence and completeness of the CMV wave operators, where the free dynamics are given by translation, cf.
[
24
, Theorem 10.7.9]
.

Working under the following additional assumption on the
\gamma_{n}
related to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\log n\sum_{s=n}^{2n}|\gamma_{s}|^{2}=0,

As further context for the above theorem, we note that in the case when

For more closely related and motivating work, we turn to previous results in the setting of Dirac operators. There, in analogy to the OPUC/CMV setting mentioned above, the free dynamics are again translation. We refer the reader to

2.
Preliminary OPUC results

In this section,
\kappa
will be a large integer and we assume that the sequence of Szegő recursion parameters satisfies
\|\{\gamma_{n}\}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
. Recall that
s_{j}=\phi_{2j}z^{-j}=\overline{\phi_{2j}^{*}}z^{j},z\in\mathbb{T}
and
\{s_{j}\}
is an orthonormal system in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
. We also have

(2.1)

s_{j}\perp_{\sigma}\{z^{j-1},\ldots,z^{-(j-1)}\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
|f_{j,\kappa}|\lesssim 1,|\alpha_{j,u}|\lesssim\kappa^{-1}
for all
0\leqslant j\leqslant\kappa-1,|u|\leqslant\kappa-1
. Consider

(2.2)

A:=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{|u|\leqslant\kappa-1}f_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u}\alpha_{j,u}

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle B=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}f_{j,\kappa}\overline{\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}}\sum_{|u|\leqslant\kappa-1}z^{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u}\alpha_{j,u}=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}f_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa}\sum_{|u|\leq\kappa-1}\alpha_{j,u}z^{u}.

Then,

(2.4)

\|A-B\|_{2,\sigma}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\quad\kappa\to\infty\,.

Proof.

We can write

A=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}B_{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}C_{j}\,,

where

B_{j}=f_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa}\sum_{|u|\leq\kappa-1}\alpha_{j,u}z^{u}

and

C_{j}:=\sum_{|u|\leqslant\kappa-1}f_{j,\kappa}\Bigl(\overline{\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u)}}-\overline{\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}}\Bigr)z^{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u}\alpha_{j,u}\,.

To finish the proof of the lemma, we only need to show that

\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}C_{j}\right\|_{2,\sigma}\to 0,\quad\kappa\to\infty\,.

To this end, we write

C_{j}=\sum_{|u|\leqslant\kappa-1}f_{j,\kappa}(s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u}-s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa}z^{u})\alpha_{j,u}\,.

Notice that
C_{j}\perp_{\sigma}C_{\ell}
for
|j-\ell|\geqslant 1
by orthogonality property (
2.1
) and by checking the degrees of polynomials involved.
So,

\|\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}C_{j}\|_{2,\sigma}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\left\|C_{j}\right\|_{2,\sigma}^{2}\,.

Recall the OPUC recurrence (
1.2
) and the connection between the monic orthogonal and orthonormal polynomials
[
23
, eq. (1.5.1), p. 55]

(2.5)

\phi_{n}=\nu_{n}\Phi_{n},\quad\nu_{n}:=\prod_{0\leqslant s\leqslant n-1}\rho^{-1}_{s},\quad\rho_{s}:=(1-|\gamma_{s}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\nu_{\infty}:=\prod_{0\leqslant s\leqslant\infty}\rho^{-1}_{s}\,.

We write

\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u)}-\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}=\nu_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u)}\Phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u)}-\nu_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}\Phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}

=(\nu_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u)}-\nu_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)})\Phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u)}+\nu_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}(\Phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u)}-\Phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)})\,.

So,

\displaystyle\|C_{j}\|_{2,\sigma}\lesssim|f_{j,\kappa}|\max_{u}|\alpha_{j,u}|\sum_{0\leqslant u\leqslant\kappa-1}\left(\left\|\sum_{s=2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}^{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u)-1}\gamma_{s}\Phi_{s}\right\|_{2,\sigma}+\sum_{s=2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa-\kappa)}^{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+\kappa)}|\gamma_{s}|^{2}\right)+

\displaystyle|f_{j,\kappa}|\max_{u}|\alpha_{j,u}|\sum_{-(\kappa-1)\leqslant u<0}\left(\left\|\sum_{s=2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u)}^{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)-1}\gamma_{s}\Phi_{s}\right\|_{2,\sigma}+\sum_{s=2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa-\kappa)}^{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+\kappa)}|\gamma_{s}|^{2}\right)\,.

Given our assumptions of
f_{j,\kappa},\alpha_{j,u}
and the
orthonormality of
\{\phi_{s}\}
, we get

\|C_{j}\|_{2,\sigma}\lesssim\left(\sum_{s=2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa-\kappa)}^{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+\kappa)}|\gamma_{s}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Hence,

\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\|C_{j}\|_{2,\sigma}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{s>\kappa^{2}}|\gamma_{s}|^{2}\to 0

as
\kappa\to\infty
and the proof is finished.

Lemma 2.2

Let

is
2
-localized to

Proof.

We again write the sum

-orthogonal. Then,

To this end, we write

is smooth and compactly supported on
\mathbb{R}
so
\widehat{\omega}_{0}
is not compactly supported, and we cannot use Lemma
2.1
or Lemma
2.2
directly. Instead, we will use an approximation argument. Take an even function
\mu(\xi)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mu=1
on
[-1,1]
and
\mu=0
for
|\xi|>2
and let
h_{m}(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((\mathcal{F}h)(\xi)\mu(\xi/m))

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
is a large parameter to be chosen later. Clearly,
(\mathcal{F}h_{m})(\xi)=(\mathcal{F}h)(\xi)
for
|\xi|<m
,
(\mathcal{F}h_{m})(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|>2m
, and
(\mathcal{F}h_{m}(\kappa x))(\xi)=\kappa^{-1}(\mathcal{F}h_{m}(x))(\xi/\kappa)
. Next, we let

\tau_{0}(e^{ix})=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}h_{m}((x-2\pi p)\kappa),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\,.

This is a smooth function on
\mathbb{T}
. By the Poisson summation formula,
(\widehat{\tau}_{0})_{n}=(\mathcal{F}h_{m}(x\kappa))(n)=\kappa^{-1}(\mathcal{F}h_{m}(x))(n/\kappa)=\kappa^{-1}(\mathcal{F}h)(n/\kappa)\mu(n/(\kappa m))
. Hence,

(A) we have
(\widehat{\tau}_{0})_{n}=(\widehat{\omega}_{0})_{n}
for
|n|<\kappa m
and
(\widehat{\tau}_{0})_{n}=0
for
|n|>2\kappa m
,

(B) the function
\tau_{0}(z)
is
\infty
-localized to the arc
I_{0}
uniformly in
m
.

(C) the bound

(4.7)

|(\widehat{\tau}_{0})_{n}-(\widehat{\omega}_{0})_{n}|\leqslant_{\ell}m^{-\ell}(\kappa(1+|n/\kappa|^{\ell}))^{-1}

holds for any
n\in\mathbb{Z},\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Now, we introduce

\tau_{j}(z):=\tau_{0}(z/z_{j}),\quad\delta_{j}(z):=\omega_{j}(z)-\tau_{j}(z)

and write

\tau_{j}(z)=:\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta_{j,u}z^{u},\quad\delta_{j}(z)=:\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{j,u}z^{u}\,.

Notice that both
\tau_{j}
and
\delta_{j}
are
\infty
-localized at
I_{j}
and

(4.8)

\|\delta_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{com-1}}}{{\leqslant_{\ell}}}m^{-\ell}.

Then,

A^{(0)}=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{|u|\leqslant\kappa^{2}}f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u}\alpha_{j,u}=I_{1}+I_{2}\,,

where

I_{1}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{|u|\leqslant\kappa^{2}}f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u}\eta_{j,u},\,I_{2}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{|u|\leqslant\kappa^{2}}f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u}\beta_{j,u}\,.

Similarly,

B^{(0)}=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{j,u}z^{u}=J_{1}+J_{2}\,,

where

J_{1}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta_{j,u}z^{u},\quad J_{2}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{j,u}z^{u}=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa}\delta_{j}\,.

Taking
m=\lfloor P/4\rfloor
, we get
\|I_{1}-J_{1}\|_{2,\sigma}\to 0
as
\kappa\to\infty
by adjusting the proof of (
2.4
).
We are going to show that

(4.9)

\|I_{2}\|_{2,\sigma}\leqslant_{\ell}P^{-\ell},\quad\|J_{2}\|_{2,\sigma}\leqslant_{\ell}P^{-\ell/4}\,.

Partitioning
\mathbb{T}=\cup_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}I_{s}
, we may separate diagonal and off-diagonal terms and write

\displaystyle\|J_{2}\|^{2}_{2,\sigma}

\displaystyle{\leq}_{\ell}\,\mathcal{D}+\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{j_{1}\neq j_{2}}|f^{(0)}_{j_{1},\kappa}||f^{(0)}_{j_{2},\kappa}|\frac{\left(\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j_{1}\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j_{2}\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\right)^{1/2}}{(|j_{1}-s|^{\ell}+1)(|j_{2}-s|^{\ell}+1)}

\displaystyle\leq_{\ell}\mathcal{D}+\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{j_{1}\neq j_{2}}|f^{(0)}_{j_{1},\kappa}||f^{(0)}_{j_{2},\kappa}|\frac{\left(\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j_{1}\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j_{2}\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\right)^{1/2}}{|j_{1}-j_{2}|^{\ell/2}(|j_{1}-s|^{\ell/2}+1)(|j_{2}-s|^{\ell/2}+1)}

\displaystyle\leq_{\ell}\mathcal{D}+P^{-\ell/2}\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{j_{1}\neq j_{2}}\frac{\left(\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j_{1}\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j_{2}\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\right)^{1/2}}{(|j_{1}-s|^{\ell/2}+1)(|j_{2}-s|^{\ell/2}+1)}

by
\infty
-localization of
\delta_{j}
, and where
\mathcal{D}
collects the diagonal terms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}^{(0)}|^{2}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}^{*}|^{2}|\delta_{j}|^{2}d\sigma(z).

We estimate the off-diagonal terms first. For these terms, after applying the inequality
2xy\leqslant x^{2}+y^{2}
in the numerator and summing out the
j_{1(2)}
factor (assuming
\ell\geqslant 4
), it suffices to show

(4.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\frac{1}{|j-s|^{\ell/2}+1}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)=O(1)\,.

We now make use of Lemma
4.1
, set
m_{j}=2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)
and define

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 0}(n+1)^{-\ell}\sum_{|r-m_{j}|<\kappa(n+1)}|\gamma_{r}|^{2}=:E_{j}

so (
4.1
) gives

\displaystyle\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\leq E_{j}+O(1/\kappa)\,.

Taking
\ell\geqslant 4
, we may sum out the
s
-index and find

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\frac{1}{|j-s|^{\ell/2}+1}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)

\displaystyle\leq_{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}E_{j}+O(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}(n+1)^{-\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{|r-m_{j}|<\kappa(n+1)}|\gamma_{r}|^{2}+O(1)\,.

We note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{|r-m_{j}|<\kappa(n+1)}|\gamma_{r}|^{2}\lesssim(n+1)\sum_{r\geq 0}|\gamma_{r}|^{2},

so that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}E_{j}\lesssim\sum_{n\geq 0}(n+1)^{-\ell+1},

establishing (
4.10
). We estimate
\mathcal{D}
by separating the terms within a
P
-width of the diagonal as

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\leqslant_{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}|\delta_{j}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\hskip 199.16928pt

\displaystyle{\leqslant_{\ell}}\sum_{|s-j|<P}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}|\delta_{j}|^{2}d\sigma(z)+\sum_{|s-j|\geqslant P}\frac{1}{|j-s|^{2\ell}+1}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\hskip 28.45274pt

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pes5}}}{{\leqslant_{\ell}}}\sum_{|s-j|<P}P^{-2\ell}\frac{|j-s|^{\ell}+1}{|j-s|^{\ell}+1}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)+\sum_{|s-j|\geqslant P}\frac{1}{|j-s|^{2\ell}+1}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)

\displaystyle\leqslant_{\ell}\sum_{|s-j|<P}\frac{P^{-\ell}}{|j-s|^{\ell}+1}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)+\sum_{|s-j|\geqslant P}\frac{P^{-\ell}}{|j-s|^{\ell}+1}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{last-09}}}{{\leqslant_{\ell}}}P^{-\ell}\,.

Thus, the second bound in (
4.9
) is established.

Next, we write

I_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{|u|\leqslant\kappa^{2}}f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}s_{\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u}\beta_{j,u}=\sum_{n=0}^{5\kappa^{2}}s_{n}c_{n},\quad c_{n}=\sum_{j,u:\,\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa+u=n}f_{j,\kappa}^{(0)}\beta_{j,u}

and (

) gives
|c_{n}|\leqslant_{\ell}P^{-\ell}\kappa^{-1}
. The orthogonality of the system
\{s_{n}\}
yields

\|I_{2}\|_{2,\sigma}\leqslant_{\ell}P^{-\ell}

and we have (
4.5
) for
\ell\geqslant 8
.

To prove (
4.6
), we argue similarly by substituting
\omega_{j}=\tau_{j}+\delta_{j}
and using the triangle inequality. First, one can claim

(4.12)

\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}\tau_{j}\|^{2}_{2,\sigma}-\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}|^{2}|\tau_{j}(z)|^{2}dm=o(1),\quad\kappa\to\infty

by adjusting the proof of (
2.6
). Secondly, after decomposing the torus
\mathbb{T}=\cup_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}I_{s}
again, the
\infty
-localization of
\delta_{j}
around
I_{j}
and (
4.8
) give

(4.13)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}|^{2}|\delta_{j}(z)|^{2}dm\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pes5}}}{{\leqslant_{\ell}}}\kappa^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}\min\left(P^{-2\ell},(|s-j|^{2\ell}+1)^{-1}\right)\leqslant_{\ell}P^{-\ell}\,.

Finally, since
|f_{j,\kappa}|\lesssim 1
, we may argue exactly as in (
4.11
) and find

(4.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f^{(0)}_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}\delta_{j}\|^{2}_{2,\sigma}\leqslant_{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\sum_{s=0}^{\kappa-1}\int\limits_{I_{s}}|\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}|^{2}|\delta_{j}|^{2}d\sigma(z)\leq_{\ell}P^{-\ell}.

Now, (
4.6
) follows from (
4.12
), (
4.13
), and (
4.14
) after we take large
\ell
and apply the triangle inequality in the space
\{h_{j}(x)\}|_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}
with the norm

\|h\|_{1}:=(\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|h_{j}(x)|^{2}d\sigma)^{\frac{1}{2}}
for the first term and with the norm
\|h\|_{2}:=(\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|h_{j}(x)|^{2}dm)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for the second as follows. Take
X:=\{f_{j,\kappa}^{(0)}\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}\tau_{j}\}|_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}
,
Y:=\{f_{j,\kappa}^{(0)}\phi^{*}_{2(\kappa^{2}+2j\kappa)}\delta_{j}\}|_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}
and
U:=\{f_{j,\kappa}^{(0)}\tau_{j}\}|_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}
,
W:=\{f_{j,\kappa}^{(0)}\delta_{j}\}|_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}
. One has

(4.15)

\|X+Y\|_{1}^{2}-\|U+W\|_{2}^{2}=(\|X+Y\|_{1}-\|U+W\|_{2})(\|X+Y\|_{1}+\|U+W\|_{2})\,.

Then,

\|X+Y\|_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:remainder}}}{{=}}\|X\|_{1}+O(P^{-\ell/2})

by the triangle inequality and, similarly,

\|U+W\|_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pol-55}}}{{=}}\|U\|_{2}+O(P^{-\ell/2})\,.

We also have

\|X\|_{1}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{pol-54}}}{{=}}\|U\|_{2}^{2}+o(1)\,,

where
\|U\|_{2}\lesssim 1
by the
\infty

. Hence,

and, substitution into (

).

Given (
4.5
), we apply the triangle inequality to write

\|A-B\|_{2,\sigma}\leqslant\sum_{d=0}^{P-1}\|A^{(d)}-B^{(d)}\|_{2,\sigma}

4.2.
The pointwise convergence assumption

As a motivation for the condition (
1.7
) above, we introduce the following definition.

Definition.
Consider a measure
\sigma
in the Szegő class. We say that the pointwise convergence assumption (PCA) holds if
\{\phi_{n}^{*}(z)\}
converges for a.e.
z\in\mathbb{T}
.

Recall that, under the Szegő condition,
\phi^{*}_{n}\to\Pi
in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
where
\Pi=D^{-1}\cdot\mathbb{1}_{E_{{\rm ac},\sigma}}
. Then, the PCA implies that
\{\phi_{n}^{*}(z)\}\to\Pi
Lebesgue a.e.. By the Menshov-Rademacher theorem (see
[
9
]
), the condition
\gamma_{n}\log(n+1)\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
implies the PCA, but it is not known if the PCA holds for all Szegő measures. Clearly, assumption (
1.7
) is weaker than
\gamma_{n}\log(n+1)\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
.

More directly related to our methods, we prove that the input to the proof of Theorem
1.1
that relies on (
1.7
) is also implied by the PCA.

Lemma 4.2
.

The PCA implies (
2.13
).

Proof.

By Egorov’s theorem, we know that for each
\epsilon>0
, there is a set
E_{\epsilon}\subset\mathbb{T}
such that
|E_{\epsilon}^{c}|\leqslant\epsilon
and
\phi_{n}^{*}-\Pi\to 0
uniformly for
z\in E_{\epsilon}
.
In estimating
\left\|\Sigma_{\kappa}-\Pi\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}f_{j,\kappa}\omega_{j}\right\|_{2,\sigma}
, we use
\omega_{j_{1}}\omega_{j_{2}}=0
for
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{T}}(z_{j_{1}},z_{j_{2}})>2\pi/\kappa
to get

\left\|\Sigma_{\kappa}-\Pi\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}f_{j,\kappa}\omega_{j}\right\|_{2,\sigma}^{2}\lesssim\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|\phi^{*}_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}-\Pi|^{2}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\omega_{j}^{2}d\sigma\,.

From (
2.12
) and Remark
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{E_{\epsilon}^{c}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}|\phi_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}(z)|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}\sigma^{\prime}dm+\int\limits_{E_{\epsilon}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}|\phi_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}(z)|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}\sigma^{\prime}dm

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}|\phi_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}(z)|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}d\sigma_{s}-\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}dm=o(1),\quad\kappa\to\infty\,.

Since

|\Pi|^{2}\sigma^{\prime}dm=dm
, we have, by uniform convergence

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\int\limits_{E_{\epsilon}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}|\phi_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}\sigma^{\prime}(z)dm(z)-\int\limits_{E_{\epsilon}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}dm(z)=0\,.

One then has

\displaystyle\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}

\displaystyle\left(\int\limits_{E_{\epsilon}^{c}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}|\phi_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}(z)|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}\sigma^{\prime}dm+\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}|\phi_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}(z)|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}d\sigma_{s}\right)

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leqslant\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}\int\limits_{E_{\epsilon}^{c}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,k}|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}dm(z)\leqslant\epsilon\,,

because
|f_{j,\kappa}|\lesssim 1
and
\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}\omega_{j}=1
. Since
\epsilon
was arbitrary, we get

(4.17)

\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}|\phi_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}(z)|^{2}\omega_{j}(z)^{2}d\sigma_{s}=0\,.

We continue by writing

\displaystyle\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|\phi^{*}_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}-\Pi|^{2}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\omega_{j}^{2}\sigma^{\prime}dm\leqslant

\displaystyle\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}\int\limits_{E_{\epsilon}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|\phi^{*}_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}-\Pi|^{2}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\omega_{j}^{2}\sigma^{\prime}dm+\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}\int\limits_{E^{c}_{\epsilon}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|\phi^{*}_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}-\Pi|^{2}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\omega_{j}^{2}\sigma^{\prime}dm\,.

The first term is zero by the uniform convergence. For the second one, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}\int\limits_{E^{c}_{\epsilon}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}|\phi^{*}_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}-\Pi|^{2}|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\omega_{j}^{2}\sigma^{\prime}dm\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}\int\limits_{E^{c}_{\epsilon}}\sum_{j=0}^{\kappa-1}(|\phi^{*}_{\kappa^{2}+j\kappa}|^{2}+|\Pi|^{2})|f_{j,\kappa}|^{2}\omega_{j}^{2}\sigma^{\prime}dm\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{loi1}}}{{\lesssim}}\epsilon\,.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we get our result.
∎
