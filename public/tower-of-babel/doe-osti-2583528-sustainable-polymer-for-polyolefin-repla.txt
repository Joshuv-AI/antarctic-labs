Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

Sustainable Chemicals, LLC (Participant, Sust-Chem) develops renewable, biodegradable
bioplastics for the replacement of large-volume petrochemical plastics such as polyethylene (PE)
and polypropylene (PP). Participant’s patented bioplastics will find use in everyday items such as
plastic bags, flexible containers, packaging, and toys and will address customers' ask for goods
that are sustainable but don't cost a premium. Participants’ plastic comes from plant biomass and
at the end of its lifecycle, is compostable or chemically recyclable. During the CRADA,
Participant and Contractor will jointly refine the polymerization process used to make our
bioplastic. Participant and Contractor will focus on technologies that are economically and
industrially feasible, complete with full polymer characterization.

Summary of Research Results:

The intent of this project was to increase the technological readiness of a patented bio-derivable
polymer by innovating on the polymerization strategy to realize economic and industrial
feasibility, complete with prototype demonstrations and material characterization. Included
within this report is a summary of each task and the corresponding accomplishments to complete
the above feat.

Task 1: Polymerization optimization and characterization

The polymerization of Sust-Chem’s novel bio-derivable polymer was first published by Luebben
and Raebiger.1 To increase the technological readiness level of this polymer, more robust and
industrially practical polymerization methods were investigated. A successful task 1 outcome
was defined as the synthesis of >10 grams of polymer with a Mw > 30kDa (as determined by gel
permeation chromatography).

NREL’s contribution included a study of the stability and reactivity of the monomer, 2,5-
dihydroxy-1,4-dioxane (DHDO). Sust-Chem delivered ~5g of DHDO (Sigma-Aldrich) for this
study. After screening multiple solvents, it was found that DHDO exhibited acceptable solubility
and stability in tetrahydrofuran and dimethyl sulfoxide while exhibiting unsatisfactory solubility
and instability in acetonitrile. As shown in Figure 1A, hydrogen nuclear magnetic resonance
spectroscopy (HNMR) spectra showed a defined and correlated DHDO structure in dimethyl
sulfoxide. This contrasted with acetonitrile which resulted in several unidentifiable peaks
suggesting the monomer reacts and/or decomposes in this solvent (Figure 1B).

 2

  Figure 1: (A) HNMR spectra of DHDO in deuterated dimethyl sulfoxide showing the predicted
  structure and no impurities. (B) HNMR spectra of DHDO in deuterated acetonitrile showing no
 known structure.

The thermal stability of the monomer was also investigated with differential scanning
calorimetry (DSC) and thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA). A DSC thermogram is given in
Figure 2 that shows DHDO will start melting at 60°C and remain molten till 115°C where it
begins to decompose and vaporize, exemplifying the melt polymerization window for
further experimentation. The degradation is corroborated with TGA that shows the monomer
begins vaporizing at 110°C (data not shown).

 Figure 2: DSC thermogram of DHDO from -50 °C to 200 °C at a ramp rate of 10 °C/min.

Simultaneous to monomer characterization, thermal and molecular weight analysis were jointly
investigated on polymer samples. Method development and optimization of sample preparation
afforded procedures capable of analyzing novel polymer products.

Before further work on task 1 was performed, a re-alignment of priorities within Sust-Chem
prompted the mutual termination of the CRADA.

Task 2: Processing and prototype fabrication

Run in conjunction with Task 1, synthesized polymers would be processed into useful form
factors, namely thin films, for mechanical testing and prototype demonstrations for investors and
meetings. Successful completion of this task would be >0.5g of finished prototype material, such
as a thin film sheet.

The CRADA was terminated before this task could be started (or completed).

Task 3: Chemical recycling

Generated prototype films or related materials would be deconstructed or depolymerized by
solvent mediated processes (hydrolysis, solvolysis) or solid-state process (UV radiation,
pyrolysis). Successful completion of this task would be a >1/2 reduction of molecular weight (as
measured by GPC and/or NMR) of polymer after the deconstruction technique.

The CRADA was terminated before this task could be started (or completed)

Task 4*: (Optional Reach Task) Polymer production scale-up (based upon available time
and funding)

Time and budget remaining, the optimized polymerization identified in Task 1 would be scaled
to 1kg of produced polymer. Successful completion of this task would be the production of >1kg
of purified polymer with >30kDa Mw (as determined by GPC).

The CRADA was terminated before this task could be started (or completed).

Task 5: Additional Tasks

Any additional tasks, consistent with the approved scope, would be performed by NREL.

The CRADA was terminated before this task could be started (or completed).
