The Gaussian Gabor system at the critical density is a weighted lower semi frame
Elias Zikkos

Abstract
We show that the Gaussian Gabor system G ⁡ ( φ , ℤ 2 ) G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2}) at the critical density is a weighted lower semi frame for L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) , thus resolving a question left open in [ 1 ] . This complements recent results showing that the system is neither a weighted frame nor admits a reproducing partner. In fact, after removing any atom from G ⁡ ( φ , ℤ 2 ) G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2}) , suitable weights produce a complete Riesz–Fischer sequence and lower semi frame.



Keywords:
Gaussian Gabor system; critical density; weighted lower semi frame;
Riesz–Fischer sequence; Bessel sequence; biorthogonal families.

2020 MSC: 42C15 (primary); 46C05, 47A05 (secondary).

1
Introduction and Results

Let
G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
be the Gaussian Gabor system
G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
at the critical density, that is

G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})=\{e^{2\pi int}\cdot e^{-\pi(t-m)^{2}}:\,(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}.

It is well known that
G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
has excess equal to one in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, that is,
it is complete and remains complete if any one element is removed but not if two.
It is also a Bessel sequence (upper frame) but not a frame. If any element is removed, then the remaining complete system is not a Schauder basis.

Remark 1.1
.

These classical facts can be found in Christensen
[
4
, Corollary 11.4.3 and Theorem 11.6.1]

and Heil
[
5
, Example 11.34]
.

It was recently proved in
[
1
, Section 6]
that
G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
is
not
a weighted
frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, relying on the fact, established in
[
6
, Corollaries 2.6 and 4.3]
,

that
G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
does not have a reproducing partner.
The question whether it could be a
weighted\,\,lower\,\,semi\,\,frame

for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
was left open. We answer this in the affirmative.
In fact, if we delete any atom from the system, the following is true.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every fixed pair
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, consider the Gabor system

\{e^{2\pi int}\cdot e^{-\pi(t-m)^{2}}:\,(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(a,b)\}\}

which is complete and minimal in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\{\gamma_{m,n,a,b}:\,(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(a,b)\}\}
be the unique biorthogonal family to this Gabor system in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
there exist positive constants
c_{m,n}
, so that the weighted Gabor system

\{c_{m,n}\cdot e^{2\pi int}\cdot e^{-\pi(t-m)^{2}}:\,(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(a,b)\}\}

is a complete Riesz-Fischer sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and a lower semi frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
In particular this holds if we choose the constants
c_{m,n}
so that

\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(a,b)\}}\frac{||\gamma_{m,n,a,b}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}}{c_{m,n}^{2}}<\infty.

Corollary 1.1
.

The Gaussian Gabor system
G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
is a weighted lower semi frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Since
G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
has excess equal to one, deleting any atom yields a complete and minimal system in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
By Theorem
1.1
, suitable weights make this subsystem a complete Riesz–Fischer sequence and lower semi frame. Hence the full Gaussian Gabor system is a weighted lower semi frame.
∎

We point out that Theorem
1.1
follows immediately
from the following general result, whose proof is given in Section 3.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a complete and minimal family in a separable Hilbert space
\cal{H}
, thus having a unique biorthogonal family
\{r_{n}\}
.
If positive constants
\{c_{n}\}
satisfy
\sum||r_{n}||^{2}/c_{n}^{2}<\infty
, then the family
\{r_{n}/c_{n}\}
is a Bessel sequence in
\cal H

and the family
\{c_{n}f_{n}\}
is a complete Riesz-Fischer sequence in
\cal H
, hence a lower semi frame for
\cal{H}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.2
was also proved in
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]

but under the stronger condition that the sequence
\{||r_{n}||/c_{n}\}
belongs to the space
\ell^{1}
.
The present formulation requires only that
\{||r_{n}||/c_{n}\}
belongs to the space
\ell^{2}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Any family in
\cal H
that contains a subfamily which is complete and minimal in
H
is a weighted lower semi frame.

Remark 1.3
.

As proved recently in
[
2
]
, complete Riesz-Fischer sequences are equivalent to minimal lower frame sequences.

Theorem
1.2
answers partially the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
1
, Conjecture 3.1]

If a family
\cal{F}
in a separable Hilbert space
\cal{H}
is complete, then
\cal{F}
is a weighted lower semi frame
for
\cal{H}
.

The conjecture is known to hold if
\cal{F}
has a reproducing partner for
\cal{H}
(
[
1
, Proposition 3.3]
).
A special case is when
\cal{F}
contains a Schauder basis for
\cal{H}
or a frame for
\cal{H}

(
[
1
, Corollary 3.5]
). Corollary
1.2
adds a further
case: any complete family containing a complete and minimal subfamily.

Remark 1.4
.

The case when F is complete but every complete subfamily has infinite
excess remains open.

A natural example is the Müntz family
\{t^{\lambda_{n}}\}
, where
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{N}
and
\sum\lambda_{n}^{-1}=\infty
.
By the Müntz-Szász theorem the family is complete in
L^{2}(0,1)
if and only if
\sum\lambda_{n}^{-1}=\infty
.
Thus, every complete subfamily of
\{t^{\lambda_{n}}\}
again has an infinite excess in
L^{2}(0,1)
. Whether such families are weighted lower semi frames for
L^{2}(0,1)
is unknown.

To this end, we note that since
G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
does not have a reproducing partner,

[
1
, Proposition 3.3]
cannot be applied to deduce whether
G(\varphi,\mathbb{Z}^{2})

is a weighted lower semi frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Theorem
1.1
yields Corollary
1.1
, which settles this question for the Gaussian Gabor system.

Another example of the same principle is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
8
, Theorem 1.4]

For any fixed
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
, the system
\{t^{\alpha}\cdot e^{2\pi int}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
has a finite excess in
L^{2}(0,1)
,
it does not have a reproducing partner for
L^{2}(0,1)
, yet it is a weighted lower semi frame for
L^{2}(0,1)
.

2
Background

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space endowed with an inner product
\langle.\,_{,}\,.\rangle
and a
norm
||\,.\,||
. Let
F:=\{f_{n}\}
be a countable family of vectors in
\mathcal{H}
.

F
is
complete
in
\cal H
if its closed span in
\mathcal{H}
is equal to
\mathcal{H}
.
F

is
minimal
if each vector
f_{n}
lies outside the closed span of the remaining elements of
F
in
\mathcal{H}
.

Remark 2.1
.

We say that
F
is exact if it is both complete and minimal.

The minimality of
F
is equivalent to the existence of a
biorthogonal
family,
that is, there is a sequence
\{g_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{H}
, so that
\langle f_{n},g_{m}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}
. If
F
is exact then

\{g_{n}\}
is unique but it does not have to be exact.

A family
F=\{f_{n}\}
is a
frame
if there exist two positive numbers,
C
and
D
, so that

C\cdot||f||^{2}\leq\sum|\langle f,f_{n}\rangle|^{2}\leq D\cdot||f||^{2},\qquad\forall\,\,f\in\cal{H}.

If only the left inequality holds then
F
is a
lower semi frame
and
if only the right inequality holds, then
F
is a
Bessel
sequence.

Remark 2.2
.

A family
F=\{f_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{H}
is called a
\bf weighted
lower semi frame if there exist a positive number
C

and real positive numbers
c_{n}
, so that

C\cdot||f||^{2}\leq\sum|\langle f,c_{n}f_{n}\rangle|^{2},\qquad\qquad\forall\,\,f\in\cal{H}.

A result attributed to Bari
[
7
, Chapter 4, Section 2, Theorem 3]
characterizes Bessel sequences:

F
is Bessel if and only if there exists a positive number
C
so that for any finite scalar sequence

\{\gamma_{n}\}
we have

\left|\left|\sum\gamma_{n}f_{n}\right|\right|^{2}\leq C\sum|\gamma_{n}|^{2}.

(2.1)

A family
F=\{f_{n}\}
is called a
Riesz-Fischer
sequence (see
[
7
, Chapter 4, Section 2]
)
if for every sequence
\{a_{n}\}
in the space
\ell^{2}
the moment problem
\langle f,f_{n}\rangle=a_{n}

has a solution
f\in\cal{H}
. We also have the following characterization
(
[
7
, Chapter 4, Section 2, Theorem 3]
):

F
is a Riesz-Fischer sequence in
\cal H
if and only if
there exists a positive number
C
so that for any finite scalar sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}
we have

C\sum|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\left|\left|\sum\gamma_{n}f_{n}\right|\right|^{2}.

This inequality implies that a Riesz-Fischer sequence is also a minimal sequence.

Finally, we collect results that connect Bessel sequences, Riesz-Fischer sequences and lower semi frames.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
3
, Proposition 2.3, (ii)]

The Riesz-Fischer sequences in
\cal{H}
are precisely the families for which a biorthogonal Bessel sequence exists.

Complete Riesz-Fischer sequences are equivalent to minimal lower frame sequences: indeed
this follows from the next two results.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.2]

Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a complete Riesz-Fischer sequence in a Hilbert space
\cal{H}
.
Then
\{f_{n}\}
is a minimal sequence and also a lower semi frame for
\cal{H}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
2
, Corollary 6.20]

Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a minimal sequence and also a lower semi frame for a Hilbert space
\cal{H}
. Then

\{f_{n}\}
is a complete Riesz-Fischer sequence in
\cal{H}
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Let
\{r_{n}\}
denote the unique biorthogonal family to the complete minimal system
\{f_{n}\}
in
\cal H
.
Then for any positive constants
\{c_{n}\}
, the families
\{r_{n}/c_{n}\}
and
\{c_{n}f_{n}\}
are biorthogonal.
Completeness of
\{f_{n}\}
in
\cal H
passes to the family
\{c_{n}f_{n}\}
.

Now, choose positive numbers
\{c_{n}\}
so that

\sum\frac{||r_{n}||^{2}}{c^{2}_{n}}<\infty.

This condition implies that the family
\{r_{n}/c_{n}\}
is a Bessel sequence in
\cal H
hence by
Theorem
2.1
its biorthogonal family
\{c_{n}f_{n}\}
is a Riesz-Fischer sequence in
\cal H
. Being also complete in
\cal H
,
Theorem
2.2
gives that
\{c_{n}f_{n}\}
is a lower semi frame for
\cal H
.

||\gamma_{m,n,0,0}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\asymp\log(e+\sqrt{m^{2}+n^{2}}).

Thus if we choose positive constants
c_{m,n}
so that

\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}\frac{\log(e+\sqrt{m^{2}+n^{2}})}{c_{m,n}^{2}}<\infty,

then the weighted Gabor system

\{c_{m,n}\cdot e^{2\pi int}\cdot e^{-\pi(t-m)^{2}}:\,(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\}

is a complete Riesz-Fischer sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and a lower semi frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
