On the Strong Quasiconvexity of Norms and Distance Functions
Vo Si Trong Long a,b and Nguyen Mau Nam c Dedicated to Prof. Phan Quoc Khanh on his 80th birthday

Abstract
This paper studies the strong quasiconvexity of norm and distance functions in finite-dimensional normed spaces. Although the Euclidean norm is known to be strongly quasiconvex on bounded convex sets, a complete characterization of this property for general norms remains open. We establish a necessary condition for the strong quasiconvexity of an arbitrary norm function on a convex set. For the ℓ p \ell_{p} -norm with 1 < p ≤ 2 1<p\leq 2 , this condition becomes also sufficient. We then initiate the study of the strong quasiconvexity of distance functions. Our results provide new insights into the geometric properties of norm and distance functions and extend several existing results in the literature.

Introduction and Preliminaries

Convex analysis plays a fundamental role in optimization theory and has been instrumental in a wide range of applications across economics, engineering, operations research, machine learning, data science, signal processing, control theory, and mathematical finance. For comprehensive treatments and further details on convex analysis and its applications, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
17
,
19
,
22
]
. Motivated by the need to study optimization problems beyond the classical convex framework, a variety of generalized convexity notions have been introduced and extensively investigated in the literature. Among these notions, the concepts of quasiconvexity and strong quasiconvexity have attracted considerable attention due to their ability to preserve certain important properties of convex functions while allowing for greater flexibility in modeling and applications.

The notion of strong quasiconvexity was originally introduced by Boris T. Polyak in
[
18
]
in the context of optimization, with the aim of ensuring stability and convergence properties of minimizing sequences. In comparison with quasiconvexity, strong quasiconvexity imposes additional structural conditions that often guarantee the existence and uniqueness of a global optimal solution in many important settings.
Extensive studies on the properties and characterizations of this class of functions have led to a broad range of applications in both theoretical analysis and numerical methods, including proximal point algorithms, variational inequalities, and equilibrium problems; see
[
3
,
4
,
9
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
21
]
and the references therein.

Due to the fundamental role played by norm functions and distance functions in convex analysis and related areas, an important and intriguing question is to investigate the strong quasiconvexity properties of these classes of functions. A landmark result was established by Jovanović in
[
9
]
, showing that the norm function is strongly quasiconvex on every bounded convex subset of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recently, this result was extended by Nam and Sharkansky
[
16
]
to infinite-dimensional spaces, where various conditions ensuring the preservation of strong quasiconvexity were studied.
Despite these advances, several important questions remain open. In particular, does the strong quasiconvexity of a norm function necessarily imply the boundedness of the underlying convex set? Moreover, under what conditions do distance functions possess strong quasiconvexity properties?

Inspired by the aforementioned works, the main objective of this paper is twofold.
First, we show that the strong quasiconvexity of an arbitrary norm function on a convex set implies the boundedness of the underlying set. For the
\ell_{p}
-norm with
1<p\leq 2
, we obtain a complete characterization by showing that this property is equivalent to boundedness.
Second, we establish sufficient conditions for the strong quasiconvexity of distance functions, with particular emphasis on their behavior on strongly convex sets in the sense of Vial
[
20
]
. Through these investigations, we further explore the relationship between the geometric properties of sets and the analytical properties of the associated distance functions.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
establishes a characterization of the strong quasiconvexity of norm functions. In Section
3
we study the strong quasiconvexity of distance functions relative to balls and strongly convex sets. Several illustrative examples are provided to clarify the theoretical developments. Finally, Section
4
concludes the paper with remarks and possible directions for future research.

\mathbb{R}^{n}

\|\cdot\|_{p}
. For

Given an arbitrary norm
\|\cdot\|

\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a nonempty set

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the

\Omega

d_{\Omega}(x)=\inf\{\|x-y\|\mid y\in\Omega\}\ \;\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We now recall the definitions of quasiconvexity and strong quasiconvexity used in what follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
C
be a nonempty convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
\sigma\geq 0
be a constant. A function
f\colon C\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}=[-\infty,\infty]
is said to be
\sigma
-
quasiconvex
on
C
if

f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\max\{f(x),f(y)\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}

for all
x,y\in C
and all
\lambda\in(0,1)
. In the case where
\sigma>0
, we say that
f
is
\sigma
-
strongly quasiconvex
on
C
.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a nonempty closed convex subset
C
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a point
x\in C
, the
horizon cone
of
C
at
x
is defined by

C_{\infty}(x)=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid x+tv\in C\text{ for all }t\geq 0\}.

It is well known that
C_{\infty}(x)
is the same for every
x\in C
; see, e.g.,
[
14
, Proposition 6.2]
. Thus, it can be simply denoted by
C_{\infty}
.

We conclude this section by recalling a standard result from convexity; see, e.g.,
[
8
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
h\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that

h\!\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)\;\leq\;\frac{h(s)+h(t)}{2}\ \;\mbox{\rm for all }s,t\in[a,b].

(1)

Then
h
is convex on
[a,b]
, that is,

h\bigl((1-\lambda)s+\lambda t\bigr)\;\leq\;(1-\lambda)h(s)+\lambda h(t)\ \;\mbox{\rm for all }s,t\in[a,b],\;\lambda\in[0,1].

Note that a function
h
satisfying (
1
) is called
midpoint convex
.

2
The
\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity of norm functions

In this section, we study necessary conditions for the
\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity of an arbitrary norm function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We then obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for the
\ell_{p}
-norm when
1<p\leq 2
.
We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function.
Assume that there exists a constant
\alpha\geq 0
such that

f\!\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)\leq\frac{f(s)+f(t)}{2}-\alpha(t-s)^{2}\;\ \mbox{\rm for all}\ \;s,t\in[0,1].

(2)

Then we have

f(\lambda)\leq(1-\lambda)f(0)+\lambda f(1)-4\alpha\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\;\ \mbox{\rm for all }\lambda\in[0,1].

Proof.

Given
\lambda\in[0,1],
define

g(\lambda)=4\alpha\lambda^{2}\ \;\text{and}\ \;h(\lambda)=f(\lambda)-g(\lambda).

We claim that
h
is midpoint convex.
Indeed, fix any
s,t\in[0,1]
and get

\frac{g(s)+g(t)}{2}-g\!\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)=4\alpha\Big(\frac{s^{2}+t^{2}}{2}-\frac{(s+t)^{2}}{4}\Big)=\alpha(t-s)^{2}.

Combining this with (
2
), we obtain

h\!\left(\frac{s+t}{2}\right)\leq\frac{h(s)+h(t)}{2}\text{\; for all }s,t\in[0,1].

Since
h
is continuous, it follows from Theorem
1.3
that
h
is convex on
[0,1]
.
Therefore,

h(\lambda)\leq(1-\lambda)h(0)+\lambda h(1)\text{\; for all }\lambda\in[0,1].

Substituting
h=f-g
gives us

\displaystyle f(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)f(0)+\lambda f(1)-4\alpha\lambda+4\alpha\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)f(0)+\lambda f(1)-4\alpha\lambda(1-\lambda),

which completes the proof.
∎

The next proposition provides a connection between the strongly midpoint convexity of a function and its strong quasiconvexity.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty convex set.
Consider a continuous function
f\colon C\to\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose that there exists a constant
\mu\geq 0
such that

f\!\left(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2}\right)\leq\frac{f(x_{1})+f(x_{2})}{2}-\mu\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\;\ \mbox{\rm for all }x_{1},x_{2}\in C,

(3)

Then
f
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
C
, where
\sigma=8\mu
.

Proof.

Fix any
x_{1},x_{2}\in C
with
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
.
For every
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we define

\gamma(\lambda)=\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2}\ \;\text{and}\ \;g(\lambda)=f(\gamma(\lambda)).

(4)

Then
g
is continuous on
[0,1]
. For any
s,t\in[0,1]
, we have

\gamma\Big(\frac{s+t}{2}\Big)=\frac{\gamma(s)+\gamma(t)}{2}.

Applying (
3
) with
x_{1}=\gamma(s)
and
x_{2}=\gamma(t)
gives us

g\Big(\frac{s+t}{2}\Big)\leq\frac{g(s)+g(t)}{2}-\mu\,\|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)\|_{2}^{2}.

Since
\|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)\|_{2}=|t-s|\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2},

we obtain

g\Big(\frac{s+t}{2}\Big)\leq\frac{g(s)+g(t)}{2}-\mu\,(t-s)^{2}\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus,
g
satisfies inequality (
2
) in Lemma
2.1
with parameter

\alpha=\mu\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

g(\lambda)\leq(1-\lambda)g(0)+\lambda g(1)-4\alpha\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\ \;\text{for all }\lambda\in[0,1].

(5)

Since
g(0)=f(x_{2})
and
g(1)=f(x_{1})
, it follows from (
4
) and (
5
) that

f(\gamma(\lambda))\leq(1-\lambda)f(x_{2})+\lambda f(x_{1})-4\mu\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

Setting
\sigma=8\mu
and utilizing the inequality

(1-\lambda)f(x_{2})+\lambda f(x_{1})\leq\max\{f(x_{1}),f(x_{2})\},

we arrive at

f(\gamma(\lambda))\leq\max\{f(x_{1}),f(x_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

This justifies the
\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity of
f
on
C
.
∎

Next, we establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the strong quasiconvexity of norm functions on a convex set.
To prove the main result of this section, we rely on two auxiliary lemmas.
The first lemma may be viewed as a converse to the implication discussed
in
[
9
, Example 2]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\|\cdot\|
be an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x_{0},v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|v\|=1
, and let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a nonempty interval. Define the function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\varphi(t)=\|x_{0}+tv\|\ \;\text{for }t\in\mathbb{R}.

If there exists a constant
\sigma>0
such that
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
I
, then the interval
I
is necessarily bounded.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists
\sigma>0
such that
\varphi
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
I
. Suppose on the contrary that
I
is unbounded.

Step 1: Reduction to the case
0\in I
and
I
unbounded above.

Fix an element
t_{0}\in I
. Define interval
I^{\prime}=I-t_{0}
. Obviously,
0=t_{0}-t_{0}\in I^{\prime}
, and
I^{\prime}
is also unbounded. We first consider the case where
I^{\prime}
is unbounded from above.
Define
\psi\colon I^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\psi(s)=\varphi(t_{0}+s)\;\text{ for }s\in I^{\prime}.

(6)

We will show that
\psi
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
I^{\prime}
.
Indeed, let
s_{1},s_{2}\in I^{\prime}
, and let
\lambda\in(0,1)
be arbitrary.
Set

t_{1}=t_{0}+s_{1}\ \;\text{and}\ \;t_{2}=t_{0}+s_{2}.

(7)

By the
\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity of
\varphi
on
I
, we have

\varphi\bigl(\lambda t_{1}+(1-\lambda)t_{2}\bigr)\leq\max\{\varphi(t_{1}),\varphi(t_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{2}.

Using (
6
) and (
7
), the inequality above can be rewritten as

\psi\bigl(\lambda s_{1}+(1-\lambda)s_{2}\bigr)\leq\max\{\psi(s_{1}),\psi(s_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,|s_{1}-s_{2}|^{2}.

Hence,
\psi
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
I^{\prime}
. Now, we consider the case where

I^{\prime}
is unbounded from below. Consider the interval

\widehat{I}=-I^{\prime}=\{-s\mid s\in I^{\prime}\}

and define

\widehat{\psi}(t)=\varphi(t_{0}-t)\;\text{ for }t\in\widehat{I}.

A direct verification shows that
\widehat{\psi}
is also
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
\widehat{I}
, where the interval
\widehat{I}
is unbounded from above and contains
0
.
In both cases, after replacing
I
and
\varphi
with one of the pairs
(I^{\prime},\psi)
or
(\widehat{I},\widehat{\psi})
, we may assume without loss of generality that
0\in I
and
I
is unbounded from above.

Step 2: Contradiction from strong quasiconvexity.

Using the
\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity of
\varphi
with
t_{1}=0
,

t_{2}=R\in I
and
\lambda=1/2
gives us

\varphi\Big(\tfrac{R}{2}\Big)\leq\max\{\varphi(0),\varphi(R)\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\,R^{2}=\max\{\varphi(0),\varphi(R)\}-\frac{\sigma}{8}R^{2}.

(8)

Since
\|v\|=1
, we see that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi\Big(\tfrac{R}{2}\Big)&=\Big\|x_{0}+\tfrac{R}{2}v\Big\|\\[7.22743pt]
&\geq\tfrac{R}{2}\|v\|-\|x_{0}\|\\[7.22743pt]
&=\tfrac{R}{2}-\|x_{0}\|.\end{array}

(9)

For
R
sufficiently large, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\{\varphi(0),\varphi(R)\}&=\max\{\|x_{0}\|,\|x_{0}+Rv\|\}\\[7.22743pt]
&=\|x_{0}+Rv\|\\[7.22743pt]
&\leq R+\|x_{0}\|.\end{array}

This together with (
8
) and (
9
) implies that

\frac{R}{2}-\|x_{0}\|\leq\varphi\Big(\tfrac{R}{2}\Big)\leq R+\|x_{0}\|-\frac{\sigma}{8}R^{2}.

Hence

0\leq\frac{1}{2}R+2\|x_{0}\|-\frac{\sigma}{8}R^{2},

which leads to a contradiction by letting
R\to\infty
. Therefore,
I
is bounded.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty closed convex set and let
x_{0}\in C
.
Let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonzero vector. Suppose there exist
sequences
(v_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(t_{k})\subset(0,\infty)
such that
v_{k}\to v
,
t_{k}\to\infty
and

x_{k}=x_{0}+t_{k}v_{k}\in C\ \;\text{for all }k\geq 1.

Then the entire ray

\mathcal{R}=\{x_{0}+tv\mid t\geq 0\}

is a subset of
C
.

Proof.

Choose
x_{k}\in C
such that
x_{0}+t_{k}v_{k}=x_{k}
and let
\gamma_{k}=1/t_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\gamma_{k}\to 0
and
\gamma_{k}(x_{k}-x_{0})=v_{k}\to v
. By Proposition 6.4 in
[
14
]
, we have

v\in(C-x_{0})_{\infty},

which implies by definition that
\mathcal{R}\subset C
. ∎

We are now ready to establish the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 2.5
.

Consider an arbitrary norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty convex set. If there exists a constant
\sigma>0
such that the norm function
f(x)=\|x\|
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
C
, then the set
C
is necessarily bounded.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
C
is unbounded.
Since
C
is convex, its closure
\overline{C}
is also convex
and unbounded. By the continuity of
f
, the
\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity of
f
on
C

extends to
\overline{C}
.
Since
\overline{C}
is unbounded, there exist
x_{0}\in\overline{C}

and a sequence
(x_{k})\subset\overline{C}
such that

\|x_{k}\|\to\infty\ \;\text{as }k\to\infty.

Setting

t_{k}=\|x_{k}-x_{0}\|\ \;\text{and}\ \;v_{k}=\frac{x_{k}-x_{0}}{\|x_{k}-x_{0}\|},

we see that
t_{k}\to\infty
and

x_{k}=x_{0}+t_{k}v_{k}\in\overline{C}\;\mbox{\rm for }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Using a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that

v_{k}\to v
for some
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|v\|=1
.
Applying Lemma
2.4
to the closed convex set

\overline{C}
, we conclude that

\overline{C}
contains the entire ray

\mathcal{R}=\{x_{0}+tv\mid t\geq 0\}.

Define the function
\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\varphi(t)=\|x_{0}+tv\|\;\text{ for }t\in[0,\infty).

For
t>s\geq 0
, set

x=x_{0}+sv\ \;\text{and}\ \;y=x_{0}+tv.

Then
x,y\in\mathcal{R}\subset\overline{C}
. By the

\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity of
f
on
\overline{C}
, we have

f\big((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\big)\leq\max\{f(x),f(y)\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}\;\ \text{for any }\lambda\in(0,1).

Rewriting the above inequality in terms of
\varphi
, we obtain

\varphi\big((1-\lambda)s+\lambda t\big)\leq\max\{\varphi(s),\varphi(t)\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|v\|_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{2}.

(10)

Since
\|v\|=1
, we have
\|v\|_{2}>0
. Setting

\widetilde{\sigma}=\sigma\|v\|_{2}^{2}>0,

inequality (
10
) becomes

\varphi\big((1-\lambda)s+\lambda t\big)\leq\max\{\varphi(s),\varphi(t)\}-\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)(t-s)^{2}.

This shows that
\varphi
is
\widetilde{\sigma}
-strongly quasiconvex on the
interval
[0,\infty)
. However, Lemma
2.3
asserts that

\varphi
cannot be
\widetilde{\sigma}
-strongly quasiconvex on any unbounded
interval. This contradiction shows that
C
must be bounded, which completes the proof of the theorem. ∎

We end this section with a useful corollary.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
and equip
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the
\ell_{p}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.
Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty convex set. Then the norm function

f(x)=\|x\|_{p}
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
C
for some
\sigma>0

if and only if
C
is bounded.

Proof.

The necessity follows directly from Theorem
2.5
. The sufficiency follows
from
[
16
, Example 3.8]
.
∎

3
The
\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity of distance functions

In this section, we establish sufficient conditions under which the distance function to a given set is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex. Unless otherwise stated, the distance function is always understood with respect to the norm of the ambient space. To motivate the discussion, we begin with the following illustrative example.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the set

\Omega=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid u\leq 0\}.

Take the point
x_{0}=(1,0)\notin\Omega.

In this setting, the Euclidean distance function to
\Omega
is given by

d_{\Omega}(u,v)=\max\{0,\,u\}.

Consider a neighborhood of
x_{0}
defined by

U=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid\tfrac{1}{2}<u\leq 2\text{ and }-2\leq v\leq 2\}.

Obviously,
U\cap\Omega=\emptyset
and
d_{\Omega}(u,v)=u
for
u\in U
. Choose two points
x_{1}=(1,0)
and
x_{2}=(1,1)

in
U
.
Then we have

d_{\Omega}(x_{1})=d_{\Omega}(x_{2})=1.

Setting

x_{m}=\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2}=\big(1,\tfrac{1}{2}\big),

we obtain
d_{\Omega}(x_{m})=1
.
Suppose on the contrary that there exists a constant
\sigma>0
such that
d_{\Omega}
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
U
.
Then we have

d_{\Omega}(x_{m})\leq\max\{d_{\Omega}(x_{1}),d_{\Omega}(x_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma}{8}\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

Since
\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}=1
, the above inequality yields

0\leq-\frac{\sigma}{8}
,
which is a contradiction since
\sigma>0
.
Therefore,

d_{\Omega}
cannot be
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
U
, where
U
is a bounded neighborhood of
x_{0}
such that
U\cap\Omega=\emptyset
.

From Example
3.1
, we observe that although the distance function may fail to be
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex in a neighborhood of a point outside a closed set, it still exhibits a uniform
\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity property along chords joining points on sufficiently large spheres centered at a Euclidean ball. The following proposition formalizes this observation.

Proposition 3.2
.

Consider the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{2}_{R}
be the closed ball centered at the origin with radius
R>0
. Then for every
r>R
, there exists a constant
\sigma_{0}>0
such that for all

x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r}
, one has

d_{\Omega}\big((1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}\big)\leq\max\{d_{\Omega}(x_{1}),d_{\Omega}(x_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

(11)

for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
If in addition the segment
[x_{1},x_{2}]
satisfies

[x_{1},x_{2}]\cap\mathbb{B}^{2}_{R}=\emptyset,

then there exists a constant
\sigma_{1}>0
such that the distance function
d_{\Omega}
is
\sigma_{1}
-strongly quasiconvex on
[x_{1},x_{2}]
.

Proof.

Fix any
r>R
and take arbitrary
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r}
.
Since
\|x_{1}\|_{2}=\|x_{2}\|_{2}=r
, we have

d_{\Omega}(x_{1})=d_{\Omega}(x_{2})=r-R.

Given
\lambda\in(0,1)
, define

x_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}.

Observe that

d_{\Omega}(x_{\lambda})=\max\{\|x_{\lambda}\|_{2}-R,0\}.

Therefore,

d_{\Omega}(x_{1})-d_{\Omega}(x_{\lambda})=\begin{cases}r-\|x_{\lambda}\|_{2}&\text{if }\|x_{\lambda}\|_{2}\geq R,\\[2.84526pt]
r-R&\text{if }\|x_{\lambda}\|_{2}<R.\end{cases}

(12)

Let
\theta\in[0,\pi]
be the angle between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. Then

\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}=\|x_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}-2\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle=2r^{2}(1-\cos\theta).

(13)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|x_{\lambda}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda)^{2}\|x_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+2\lambda(1-\lambda)\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=r^{2}\bigl((1-\lambda)^{2}+\lambda^{2}+2\lambda(1-\lambda)\cos\theta\bigr)

\displaystyle=r^{2}\Big(1-2\lambda+2\lambda^{2}+2\lambda(1-\lambda)\cos\theta\Big).

Setting

A=1-2\lambda+2\lambda^{2}+2\lambda(1-\lambda)\cos\theta,

(14)

we get

\|x_{\lambda}\|_{2}=r\sqrt{A}.

(15)

Since
\cos\theta\in[-1,1]
, we have

(1-2\lambda)^{2}\leq A\leq 1-2\lambda+2\lambda^{2}+2\lambda(1-\lambda)=1,

and hence
A\in[0,1]
.
It follows from the concavity of the square root function on
[0,1]
that

1-\sqrt{A}\;\geq\;\frac{1-A}{2}.

(16)

Combining (
13
) through (
16
) gives us

\begin{array}[]{ll}r-\|x_{\lambda}\|_{2}&=r(1-\sqrt{A})\\[5.69054pt]
&\geq\frac{r}{2}\,(1-A)\\[5.69054pt]
&\geq r\lambda(1-\lambda)(1-\cos\theta)\\[5.69054pt]
&=\frac{1}{2r}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.\end{array}

(17)

Since
0<\lambda(1-\lambda)\leq\tfrac{1}{4}
and

\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}\leq 2r
, we obtain

\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq r^{2},

and hence

r-R\;\geq\;\frac{r-R}{r^{2}}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

(18)

Using (
12
), (
17
) and (
18
), we get the estimate

d_{\Omega}(x_{1})-d_{\Omega}(x_{\lambda})\;\geq\;\min\!\left\{\frac{1}{2r},\,\frac{r-R}{r^{2}}\right\}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

Choosing

\sigma_{0}=2\min\!\left\{\frac{1}{2r},\,\frac{r-R}{r^{2}}\right\},

we arrive at

d_{\Omega}(x_{1})-d_{\Omega}(x_{\lambda})\geq\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

Since
d_{\Omega}(x_{1})=d_{\Omega}(x_{2})
, this inequality directly yields (
11
).

Under the additional assumption imposed, let us show next that
d_{\Omega}
is
\sigma_{1}
-strongly quasiconvex on
[x_{1},x_{2}]
.
By Corollary
2.6
, applied to the Euclidean norm on the bounded convex
set
C=[x_{1},x_{2}]
, there exists
\sigma_{1}>0
such that the Euclidean norm
function
y\mapsto\|y\|_{2}
is
\sigma_{1}
-strongly quasiconvex on
[x_{1},x_{2}]
.
Then it follows from Definition
1.1
that

\|(1-\lambda)y_{1}+\lambda y_{2}\|_{2}\leq\max\{\|y_{1}\|_{2},\|y_{2}\|_{2}\}-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{2}^{2},

for all

y_{1},y_{2}\in[x_{1},x_{2}]
and all
\lambda\in(0,1).

Since
d_{\Omega}(y)=\|y\|_{2}-R
for all
y\in[x_{1},x_{2}]
, subtracting
R
from both sides gives us

d_{\Omega}\big((1-\lambda)y_{1}+\lambda y_{2}\big)\leq\max\{d_{\Omega}(y_{1}),d_{\Omega}(y_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in[x_{1},x_{2}]
and all
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
which therefore completes the proof
of the proposition.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Inequality (
11
) in Proposition
3.2
relies on the Euclidean structure of the norm.
Indeed, the conclusion may fail for a non-Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as illustrated by the following example.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the maximum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Let
R=1/2
and
r=2
. Then the closed ball and the sphere centered
0
with radius
R
and
r
are given by

\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{\infty}_{R}=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid\|(u,v)\|_{\infty}=\max\{|u|,|v|\}\leq 1/2\}=[-1/2,1/2]^{2}

and

\mathbb{B}^{\infty}_{r}=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid\max\{|u|,|v|\}\leq 2\}=[-2,2]^{2},

respectively.
Take
x_{1}=(2,0)
and
x_{2}=(2,1)
, which belong to
\mathbb{S}^{\infty}_{r}
. Then we have

\|x_{1}\|_{\infty}=\|x_{2}\|_{\infty}=2.

For any
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have

\|x_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}=\|(1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}\|_{\infty}=\max\{2,\lambda\}=2.

The distances from all these three points to
\Omega
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
are computed by

d_{\Omega}(x_{1})=d_{\Omega}(x_{2})=d_{\Omega}(x_{\lambda})=r-R=2-1/2=3/2\ \;\text{for all }\lambda\in(0,1).

Suppose on the contrary that there exists a constant
\sigma>0
satisfying

d_{\Omega}(x_{\lambda})\leq\max\{d_{\Omega}(x_{1}),d_{\Omega}(x_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\;\ \mbox{\rm for all }\lambda\in(0,1).

Since
\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}=\|(0,-1)\|_{2}=1
, this inequality yields

\tfrac{3}{2}\leq\tfrac{3}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda),

or equivalently,

0\leq-\,\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda).

This is a contradiction because
\sigma>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
Therefore, inequality (
11
) fails for the maximum norm.

Remark 3.5
.

Inequality (
11
) does not hold for all pairs
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Indeed, take any two distinct points
x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{2}_{R}
.
Since
\Omega
is convex, the whole segment

[x_{1},x_{2}]=\{(1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}\mid\lambda\in[0,1]\}

is contained in
\Omega
. Thus,

d_{\Omega}(x_{1})=d_{\Omega}(x_{2})=0\ \;\text{and}\ \;d_{\Omega}\big((1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}\big)=0\ \;\text{for all }\lambda\in(0,1).

If there exists
\sigma>0
such that (
11
) holds for this pair
x_{1},x_{2}
,
then we obtain

\displaystyle 0=d_{\Omega}\big((1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\max\{d_{\Omega}(x_{1}),d_{\Omega}(x_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Since
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
and
\lambda(1-\lambda)>0
, the
right-hand side is strictly negative, which is not the case.

The next result shows that the distance function
d_{\Omega}
also possesses a

local

\sigma
-strong quasiconvexity around every point lying outside

\Omega
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Consider the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the
\ell_{p}
-norm

\|\cdot\|_{p}
, where
1<p\leq 2
. Let

\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{R}^{p}

be the closed ball centered at the origin with radius
R>0
. Then, for every
point
x_{0}\notin\Omega
, there exist a bounded neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
with

U\cap\Omega=\emptyset
and a constant
\sigma>0
such that the distance
function
d_{\Omega}
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
U
.

Proof.

Since
\Omega
is closed and
x_{0}\notin\Omega
, we have

\delta=d_{\Omega}(x_{0})=\|x_{0}\|_{p}-R>0.

Choose
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
and define

U=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\|x-x_{0}\|_{p}<\varepsilon\}.

Then
U
is a bounded convex neighborhood of
x_{0}
. Moreover, for every

x\in U
, we have

\|x\|_{p}\geq\|x_{0}\|_{p}-\|x-x_{0}\|_{p}>\|x_{0}\|_{p}-\varepsilon>R.

Hence
U\cap\Omega=\emptyset
.
Since
U
is a nonempty bounded convex subset of
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{p})

with
1<p\leq 2
, Corollary
2.6
guarantees the existence of a constant

\sigma>0
such that the norm function
x\mapsto\|x\|_{p}
is

\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
U
, i.e.,

\|(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\|_{p}\leq\max\{\|x\|_{p},\|y\|_{p}\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}

for all
x,y\in U
and all
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Since

d_{\Omega}(x)=\|x\|_{p}-R\ \;\text{for all }x\in U,

we obtain, by subtracting
R
from both sides, that

\displaystyle d_{\Omega}\big((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\big)

\displaystyle=\|(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\|_{p}-R

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\|x\|_{p},\|y\|_{p}\}-R-\frac{\sigma}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\max\{\|x\|_{p}-R,\|y\|_{p}-R\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\max\{d_{\Omega}(x),d_{\Omega}(y)\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}.

This shows that
d_{\Omega}
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
U
, and the
proof is complete.
∎

Theorem
3.6
involves the
\ell_{p}
-norm with
1<p\leq 2
and does not
apply to an arbitrary norm, as shown in the following example.

Example 3.7
.

Consider the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
equipped with the
\ell_{1}
-norm. Let
R=1

and consider the
\ell_{1}
-ball

\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{R}^{1}=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid\|(u,v)\|_{1}=|u|+|v|\leq 1\}.

Take the point
x_{0}=(2,0)
, which lies outside
\Omega
. The
\ell_{1}
-distance
to
\Omega
is given by

d_{\Omega}(u,v)=\max\{|u|+|v|-1,0\}.

Suppose that
U
is a bounded neighborhood of
x_{0}=(2,0)
such that

U\cap\Omega=\emptyset
. Then there exists
\rho>0
such that

\mathbb{B}^{1}[(2,0);\rho]\subset U.

Choose
\varepsilon\in(0,\rho)
and take

x_{1}=(2,0)\ \;\text{and}\ \;x_{2}=\left(2-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right).

Then
x_{1},x_{2}\in U
. Moreover,

d_{\Omega}(x_{1})=d_{\Omega}(x_{2})=1.

Their midpoint is

x_{m}=\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2}=\left(2-\frac{\varepsilon}{4},\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right),

and hence

d_{\Omega}(x_{m})=1.

Suppose on the contrary that there exists a constant
\sigma>0
such that

d_{\Omega}
is
\sigma
-strongly quasiconvex on
U
. Then

d_{\Omega}(x_{m})\leq\max\{d_{\Omega}(x_{1}),d_{\Omega}(x_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

Since

\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2},

the above inequality yields
0\leq-\frac{\sigma\varepsilon^{2}}{16},

which is impossible since
\sigma>0
and
\varepsilon>0
. This shows that Theorem
3.6
fails for the

\ell_{1}
-norm.

Next, we consider the concept of a
strongly convex set
. This notion was proposed in
[
20
]
and further studied in
[
13
,
15
,
21
]
.

Given
R>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\|x-y\|_{2}\leq 2R
, define the set

D_{R}(x,y)=\bigcap\{B\in\mathcal{B}_{R}\mid x,y\in B\},

where
\mathcal{B}_{R}=\{\mathbb{B}^{2}[c,R]\mid c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
is the collection of all closed balls of radius
R
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\Omega
be a nonempty bounded subset of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We say that
\Omega
is
strongly convex with respect to
a positive real number
R
if

D_{R}(x,y)\subset\Omega\ \;\text{for all }x,y\in\Omega.

The next theorem extends the result of Proposition
3.2
to the case of strongly convex sets.

Theorem 3.9
.

Consider the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty compact set which is
strongly convex with respect to
R>0
.
Let
r>0
be such that

\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r}\cap\Omega=\emptyset.

Suppose that there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{2}\;\geq\;c_{0}\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}\;\ \mbox{\rm for all
}x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r},

(19)

where
p_{i}
denotes the Euclidean projection
of
x_{i}
onto
\Omega
,
i=1,2
.
Then there exists
\sigma>0
(one may take
\sigma=c^{2}/R
for some

0<c\leq c_{0}
) such that for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r}
, one has

d_{\Omega}\big((1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}\big)\leq\max\{d_{\Omega}(x_{1}),\,d_{\Omega}(x_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

(20)

for all

\lambda\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Since
\Omega
and
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r}
are disjoint compact sets, the distance between them is attained and strictly positive. Define

\delta=\min\{\|y-x\|_{2}\mid y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r},\ x\in\Omega\}>0.

(21)

Observe that if (
19
) holds for some
c_{0}>0
,
then it also holds for every constant
c
with
0<c\leq c_{0}
.
Choose

c=\min\Bigl\{c_{0},\sqrt{\frac{2R\delta}{r^{2}}}\Bigr\}>0,

(22)

and define

\sigma=\frac{c^{2}}{R}>0.

(23)

Then (
19
) holds with this
c
and we have

\frac{\sigma}{2}\,r^{2}=\frac{c^{2}}{2R}\,r^{2}\;\leq\;\delta.

(24)

Now fix any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r}
. Since
\Omega
is strongly convex with respect to
R>0
, it is also
convex. Hence, the Euclidean projection
P_{\Omega}
is single-valued
(see, e.g.,
[
14
, Corollary 1.67]
).
For
i=1,2
, let
p_{i}=P_{\Omega}(x_{i})

be the Euclidean projection of
x_{i}
onto
\Omega
.
For each
\lambda\in(0,1)
, set

x_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}\ \;\text{and}\ \;p_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)p_{1}+\lambda p_{2}.

Since
\Omega
is strongly convex with respect to
R
, it follows from the equivalence between (i) and (ii)
in
[
20
, Theorem 1]
that

\mathbb{B}^{2}\big[p_{\lambda};r_{\lambda}\big]\subset\Omega\;\;\text{for any }\lambda\in(0,1),

where

r_{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}{2R}.

Thus,

d_{\Omega}(x_{\lambda})\;\leq\;d_{\mathbb{B}^{2}[p_{\lambda};r_{\lambda}]}(x_{\lambda})=\max\bigl\{\|x_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}\|_{2}-r_{\lambda},\,0\bigr\}.

(25)

By the triangle inequality, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|x_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}\|_{2}&\leq(1-\lambda)\|x_{1}-p_{1}\|_{2}+\lambda\|x_{2}-p_{2}\|_{2}\\[7.22743pt]
&=(1-\lambda)d_{\Omega}(x_{1})+\lambda d_{\Omega}(x_{2}).\end{array}

(26)

Furthermore, by (
19
) and the choice of
c
in
(
22
), we have

\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{2}\geq c\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}.

This together with (
23
) implies

\begin{array}[]{ll}r_{\lambda}&=\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}{2R}\\[7.22743pt]
&\geq\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)c^{2}}{2R}\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\\[7.22743pt]
&=\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.\end{array}

(27)

Next, we consider two cases:
x_{\lambda}\notin\Omega
or
x_{\lambda}\in\Omega
.
In the case where
x_{\lambda}\notin\Omega
, we have

d_{\mathbb{B}^{2}[p_{\lambda};r_{\lambda}]}(x_{\lambda})=\|x_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}\|_{2}-r_{\lambda}.

Using this equality with (
25
), (
26
) and
(
27
), we obtain

\displaystyle d_{\Omega}(x_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{\lambda}-p_{\lambda}\|_{2}-r_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)d_{\Omega}(x_{1})+\lambda d_{\Omega}(x_{2})-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{d_{\Omega}(x_{1}),d_{\Omega}(x_{2})\}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}.

This justifies (
20
).
If
x_{\lambda}\in\Omega
, then
d_{\Omega}(x_{\lambda})=0
, and
it follows from (
21
) that

\delta\leq\max\{d_{\Omega}(x_{1}),d_{\Omega}(x_{2})\}.

(28)

Since
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r}
, we have

\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}\leq 2r\ \;\text{and}\ \;0<\lambda(1-\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{4},

which implies that

\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}\,r^{2}.

Combining this with (
24
) and (
28
), we obtain

\frac{\sigma}{2}\,\lambda(1-\lambda)\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{\sigma}{2}\,r^{2}\;\leq\;\delta\;\leq\;\max\{d_{\Omega}(x_{1}),d_{\Omega}(x_{2})\},

which shows that (
20
) also holds in this case.
Combining the two cases, we conclude that for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r}
,
inequality (
20
) holds for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
. This completes
the proof.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Theorem
3.9
may be viewed as an extension of
Proposition
3.2
from a Euclidean ball to an arbitrary
strongly convex set. Indeed, let

\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{2}[0;R]\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

be the Euclidean ball of radius
R>0
. Fix
r>R
and take arbitrary
points
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{r}
. For
i=1,2
, let
p_{i}
denote the
Euclidean projection of
x_{i}
onto
\Omega
. Since the projection onto a
Euclidean ball is radial, we have

p_{i}=\frac{R}{r}x_{i},\qquad i=1,2.

Consequently,

\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{2}=\frac{R}{r}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}.

Thus, condition (
19
) holds with
c_{0}=R/r
.

The following example shows that condition (
19
) cannot
be extended to arbitrary neighborhoods of points lying outside a ball.

Example 3.11
.

Consider the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{2}[0;1]

be the closed unit ball. Fix any point
x_{0}\notin\Omega
and let

\delta=d_{\Omega}(x_{0})=\|x_{0}\|_{2}-1>0.

Let
U
be any neighborhood of
x_{0}
satisfying

U\cap\Omega=\emptyset.

Then there exists
\rho>0
such that

\mathbb{B}^{2}[x_{0};\rho]\subset U.

Choose
\varepsilon\in(0,\min\{\rho,\delta\})
. Set
u=\frac{x_{0}}{\|x_{0}\|_{2}}

and define

x_{1}=x_{0}-\varepsilon u=(\|x_{0}\|_{2}-\varepsilon)u,\qquad x_{2}=x_{0}+\varepsilon u=(\|x_{0}\|_{2}+\varepsilon)u.

Then

x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{B}^{2}[x_{0};\rho]\subset U.

Since
\varepsilon<\delta=\|x_{0}\|_{2}-1
, we see that both
x_{1},x_{2}\notin\Omega
. Note that their Euclidean projections onto
\Omega
coincide with

P_{\Omega}(x_{1})=P_{\Omega}(x_{2})=u.

Consequently,

\|P_{\Omega}(x_{1})-P_{\Omega}(x_{2})\|_{2}=0.

On the other hand,

\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}=2\varepsilon>0.

Thus, for any constant
c>0
, one has

\|P_{\Omega}(x_{1})-P_{\Omega}(x_{2})\|_{2}=0<c\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}.

This shows that an estimate of the form (
19
) cannot
hold uniformly on arbitrary neighborhoods of points outside
\Omega
.

4
Conclusions

This paper provides further insights into the strong quasiconvexity of norm functions while initiating the study of the strong quasiconvexity of distance functions in finite-dimensional spaces. These results open several promising directions for future research. These include sharpening convergence rates for projection-type algorithms, investigating generalized distance functions in variational analysis and applications to economics and data science, and extending the present theory to infinite-dimensional settings.
