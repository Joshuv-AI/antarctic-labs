Quad-port mm-wave MIMO antenna with a unified ground plane for 5G and beyond applications
Authors: Amit Abhishek, Ankur Utsav, Sachin Kumar, Gunjan Srivastava, Hala Mostafa, Kang Wook Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this paper, a four-element dual-band mm-wave multiple-input-multiple-output (MIMO) antenna is proposed for the n258 and n260 5G bands. The antenna is made up of simple ring-shaped radiators, with a cross-shaped structure loaded into the ground surface to improve isolation and connect the ground planes. A common ground in the presented MIMO antenna ensures uniform current flow across the entire ground surface while preventing unwanted resonances or parasitic modes caused by disconnected grounds. A unified ground also ensures that all resonating elements and associated circuitry use the same reference voltage, which is vital for RF front-end performance. Three MIMO configurations of the proposed antenna are presented, each with a different orientation of the antenna element. The best isolation is obtained by arranging antenna elements orthogonally to one another. The presented antenna offers good matching in both bands, with impedance bandwidths of 23.8 to 25.4 GHz and 37 to 40.5 GHz. The antenna offers simulated peak gain values of 7.7 dBi and 9 dBi, while the corresponding measured gain values are 7.3 dBi and 8.5 dBi at 24 GHz and 39 GHz, respectively. In addition, the suggested antenna exhibits a high radiation efficiency ranging from approximately 94% to 99%, with an average efficiency around 97.5% across the operating band. The proposed mm-wave MIMO antenna with a shared ground offers a more mechanically robust antenna design, which could be useful for multilayer PCBs or mobile/wearable 5G and beyond applications.

Introduction

High data rate antennas are crucial to fulfil the growing demand for fast and reliable wireless communication in 5G and beyond applications. These antennas are intended to accommodate large bandwidths, numerous frequency bands, and advanced techniques such as beamforming and spatial multiplexing. Multiple-input-multiple-output (MIMO) technology can enhance data stream transmission and reception by improving signal quality and reception probability. In a MIMO system, the transmitter sends data through multiple propagation paths, and the receiver mixes the information signals received along these paths. In order to achieve high throughput, antennas must exhibit high efficiency, low mutual coupling, and stable radiation patterns across a wide frequency range.

Recent research work has focused on the design and development of MIMO antenna systems that include spatial and polarization diversity1. Modern wireless systems support MIMO antennas with high isolation, better diversity, multiband functionality, and small size2,3. Decoupling shapes with metallic structures positioned between antenna elements have been reported in recent years to decrease correlation between antenna elements4–7. In8, multi-element MIMO antenna isolation was improved by using fractal-shaped radiators with metamaterial. A large bandwidth is also required to enable low latency. The performance of radiating patches was investigated, and they were converted into MIMO antennas with multiple elements9,10. A T-shaped parasitic element was employed to increase isolation among nearby antennas while preserving a broad bandwidth. A MIMO antenna configuration with two elements set apart 4 mm was proposed for 5G movable systems such as laptops and smartphones, and it demonstrated reasonable efficiency, gain, and separation11–13. Other methods for reducing coupling amongst radiators include introducing metallic stubs on the ground surface and incorporating split-ring resonators14–16. Furthermore, metasurfaces have demonstrated efficacy in realizing wideband operation and enhanced separation in MIMO antennas17. A dual-port MIMO antenna used a common ground plane and a rectangular patch with an extended arm to achieve wideband impedance matching over a 4 GHz bandwidth. In mm-wave applications, a 1 × 2 array antenna serves as a single radiating element in a quad-configuration MIMO system. Feed lines with varying impedance values were used to ensure proper impedance matching between array elements18,19. A compact MIMO antenna designed for 5G communication, as well as a comparative specific absorption rate analysis under various orientations, proved to be extremely useful in real-time applications. Implementing substrate integrated waveguide technology in a quad-mode MIMO configuration improved isolation between antenna elements20,21. In some cases, reducing the antenna size has an adverse impact on performance, thus, metamaterials were used to improve antenna characteristics without incurring significant losses. Also, the defected ground structure (DGS) technique was widely applied to reduce mutual coupling in mm-wave antennas22,23. However, isolation remains a challenge in most of the above-mentioned antenna configurations as isolation must be high, particularly when developing small antennas for 5G and beyond applications. Also, the above-mentioned antenna designs have splitted ground planes, which could result in unstable phase and amplitude characteristics, compromising beamforming and spatial diversity in MIMO systems. The separate ground surfaces of the radiators complicate the manufacture and assembly of PCBs with integrated mm-wave MIMO antennas.

This paper presents a quad-port mm-wave MIMO antenna with a unified ground surface for the n258 and n260 5G bands. The antenna is made up of four ring-shaped elements and a ground surface loaded with a cross-shaped structure. The cross-shaped element decouples the radiators while also connecting their ground planes, which is a critical requirement in 5G mm-wave MIMO antennas. The orthogonal placement of the antenna elements, as well as the use of X-shaped antennas, improves isolation. A connected ground can be helpful to balance current distribution, eliminating unwanted mutual coupling effects between closely spaced resonating elements. Due to its small size and high resolution, the proposed mm-wave dual-band antenna could be useful for 5G and beyond systems, particularly short-range vehicle radar, industrial sensing, and motion detection. The antenna operating bands have enormous potential for delivering low-latency, high-capacity communication, which is critical for upcoming wireless applications.

During the transition from the circular patch to the circular ring geometry, the effective surface current distribution and current path length varied significantly, which altered the input impedance and shifted the resonant frequency toward the 39 GHz band. Development of the quad-port MIMO antenna

In this section, the antenna configuration is progressively extended from a single radiator to a quad-element MIMO structure, followed by a detailed investigation of different element orientations to obtain better isolation and overall MIMO characteristics.

Orientation 1

In this section, the developed single antenna element is transformed into a quad-element MIMO antenna measuring 24 mm × 24 mm × 0.8 mm. The antenna elements are positioned in different orientations, with orientation 1 showing A1 and A2 elements opposite to each other and A3 and A4 elements opposite each other, as shown in Fig. 4a. Figure 4b depicts the current distribution plot with port 1 excited and the quantity of current distributed over the adjacent elements. When element 1 is excited, the current leaks to the adjacent radiators because of the small spacing between the antenna elements 2, 3, and 4. Inter-element spacing is crucial in MIMO antenna systems for overall performance, especially in terms of signal correlation, mutual coupling, and diversity gain. Ideally, the separation between antenna elements should be at least half the wavelength at the operating frequency to reduce correlation and guarantee that the signals received/transmitted by each element are independent. Compact devices with limited space may reduce spacing to less than half the wavelength, however this can lead to greater mutual coupling and decreased efficiency. The optimal spacing must be chosen by balancing the antenna’s physical size constraints with the desired performance.

Figure 4c show the mutual coupling between radiators and the reflection coefficient response of the quad-element antenna in orientation 1. Since all antenna elements are the same, the reflection coefficients at each port are similar. The isolation between antennas 1 and 2 shows a lower value, which is due to current coupling in adjacent elements when port 1 is excited, as illustrated in Fig. 4b.

Fig. 4Quad-element MIMO antenna (orientation 1): (a) Layout, (b) Surface current distribution, (c) Simulated S-parameters.

Quad-element MIMO antenna (orientation 1): (a) Layout, (b) Surface current distribution, (c) Simulated S-parameters.

Orientation 2

The isolation between antenna elements 1 and 2 has a minimum value in orientation 1, therefore another approach (orientation 2) with antenna element A2 positioned at 90˚ rotated from antenna A1 and antenna A4 also rotated 90˚ from antenna A3 as shown in Fig. 5a. In this configuration, antennas A2 and A3 are positioned serially, while A1 and A4 are opposite each other.

The current distribution plot with port 1 excited and the current scattered over the adjacent elements (2, 3, and 4) is displayed in Fig. 5b. It can be observed that there is a small current coupling between element 1 and elements 2 and 3, and a large coupling of current between elements 1 and 4. Figure 5c shows the mutual coupling plots, as well as the reflection coefficient response for orientation 2. In this case, the isolation between antennas 1 and 3 is minimal.

Quad-element MIMO antenna (orientation 2): (a) Layout, (b) Surface current distribution, (c) Simulated S-parameters.

Orientation 3

In order to improve or maintain isolation > 20 dB between antenna elements, each radiating element is placed at 90˚ phase shifts with each other in orientation 3. Figure 6a depicts the pictorial view of orientation 3. MIMO/diversity systems can take use of polarization diversity by positioning antenna elements with orthogonal polarizations, one vertically polarized and another horizontally polarized, which minimizes the correlation between signals received/transmitted by different elements. This leads to improved reliability and capacity of the radio communication link, particularly in multipath situations.

Fig. 6Quad-element MIMO antenna (orientation 3): (a) Layout, (b) Surface current distribution, (c) Simulated S-parameters.

Quad-element MIMO antenna (orientation 3): (a) Layout, (b) Surface current distribution, (c) Simulated S-parameters.

Figure 6b depicts the current distribution and Fig. 6c shows the mutual coupling and reflection coefficient response of antenna elements. The orthogonal orientation improves element isolation, minimizing interference and increasing total system efficiency. The orientation 3 is especially useful in miniature devices, where space constraints make it difficult to achieve large spatial separation. Hence, orthogonal placement of the antenna elements improves the spatial and polarization dimensions of the wireless channel, resulting in increased throughput and robustness in MIMO antenna systems. Tables 2 and 3 summarize the values for the reflection coefficient and mutual coupling in all orientations (1, 2, and 3). It can be noticed that orientations 1 and 2 exhibit comparatively higher surface current leakage and stronger electromagnetic interaction between adjacent elements, resulting in increased mutual coupling. In contrast, the orthogonal orientation 3 offers better isolation characteristics with lower coupling levels at both operating frequencies.

Quad-element MIMO antenna with connected ground planes

The previous section examined multiple configurations of quad-element MIMO antennas based on different antenna elements orientation. Maximum isolation is achieved when each antenna element is rotated 90˚ with respect to the other. Figure 7a, b depict the top and bottom views of the proposed antenna, respectively. In the suggested MIMO antenna, a cross-shaped decoupling element is introduced into the ground surface, which not only results in better impedance matching and isolation but also joins the ground planes of the MIMO antenna elements. Figure 7c, d show photos of the top and bottom views of the antenna prototype, respectively.

Fig. 7Proposed quad-element MIMO antenna: (a) Top view, (b) Bottom view, (c) Photograph of the top view, (d) Photograph of the bottom view.

Proposed quad-element MIMO antenna: (a) Top view, (b) Bottom view, (c) Photograph of the top view, (d) Photograph of the bottom view.

The X-shaped interconnected structure in the ground surface of the antenna improves isolation and impedance matching simultaneously without introducing additional complex decoupling mechanisms. The suggested MIMO configuration therefore offers a simple, compact, and structurally efficient approach for achieving improved diversity performance while maintaining a unified ground architecture.

Simulated and measured results

Figure 8a shows the measured and simulated reflection coefficients of the antenna. The antenna demonstrates good matching in both bands, with impedance bandwidths of 23.8 to 25.4 GHz and 37 to 40.5 GHz. Figure 8b depicts the measured and simulated isolation of the suggested MIMO antenna. It is noticeable that there is more than 20 dB isolation between each radiating element. The cross-shaped structure joins the ground planes while helping in isolating the radiators, preserving the low correlation required for effective MIMO antenna functioning. It also guarantees uniform current distribution and impedance matching, resulting in more steady radiation performance. Also, a connected ground plane simplifies design and fabrication process, while also improving the structural integrity of the MIMO antenna. In compact devices/systems with closely spaced antennas, the existence of a linked ground plane is critical for preserving performance while reducing interference between ports.

The cross-shaped structure acts as a conductive current redistribution path between the antenna elements and the shared ground surface. The antenna elements are arranged in a way that provides a uniform current distribution on the ground plane while eliminating surface current coupling. Consequently, improving diversity performance without adding a complicated decoupling structure to the MIMO antenna.

Fig. 8Measured and simulated response of antenna: (a) Reflection coefficients, (b) Mutual coupling.

Measured and simulated response of antenna: (a) Reflection coefficients, (b) Mutual coupling.

Figure 9a, b show the surface current vector plots of the suggested MIMO antenna for both cases: antenna structure without cross-shaped element and antenna structure with cross-shaped element. From the vector plots, it can be observed that the surface current density is maximum around radiator 1 in both cases, as it is considered the excited reference element, while the remaining three elements behave as neighbouring antennas. In the structure without the cross-shaped decoupling element, stronger current propagation toward adjacent radiators is observed, resulting in higher electromagnetic coupling. However, after introducing the cross-shaped structure, a portion of the induced surface current is redirected and distributed through the ground plane region, thereby suppressing direct current leakage toward neighbouring elements. This redistribution mechanism effectively improves isolation and also contributes to enhanced impedance matching, particularly in the second operating band.

Figure 10a, b show the surface current distribution across the conductive surface of the antenna at 24.2 GHz and 39 GHz, respectively. It is noticeable that when element 1 is excited at both frequencies, there is almost no leakage current flowing through elements 2, 3, and 4, which denotes good isolation and little coupling between antenna elements. Low leakage current guarantees that each antenna element functions independently, preventing undesired interference from nearby elements and preserving the integrity of transmitted/received signals. Also, less leakage current contributes to increased radiation efficiency, better impedance matching, and more predictable radiation patterns. These properties are especially desired in compact antennas, where element coupling can be a significant design difficulty.

Furthermore, the suggested antenna demonstrates high radiation efficiency ranging from around 94% to 99%, with an average efficiency of around 97.5% across the operational band. Figure 12 depicts the radiation patterns of the antenna in both bands. The suggested antenna has a nearly omnidirectional pattern in the H-plane and a bidirectional pattern in the E-plane. The radiation patterns of the proposed MIMO antenna were measured inside an anechoic chamber using a standard far-field setup. During measurement, antenna element 1 was excited while the remaining ports were terminated with 50 Ω matched loads. A calibrated horn antenna was used as the transmitting antenna, and the suggested MIMO antenna was rotated to obtain the E-plane and H-plane radiation characteristics at the desired operating frequencies. Minor deviations between simulation and measurement arise due to practical fabrication tolerances, connector losses, soldering effects, and substrate parameter variations at mm-wave frequencies.

Conclusion

This article presents a quad-element MIMO antenna with a cross-shaped decoupling structure that provides good impedance matching at dual-band operating frequencies of 23.8 to 25.4 GHz and 37 to 40.5 GHz. In the designed MIMO antenna, positioning the antenna elements at orthogonal orientations helps in achieving low mutual coupling and strong isolation between them, even when placed in close proximity. An X-shaped structure is used in the proposed MIMO antenna to provide a uniform current distribution between the antenna elements and to unite their ground plane. The antenna measures 24 mm × 24 mm × 0.8 mm. The isolation between each antenna element is 35 dB, the ECC of the antenna is 0.0138, and the DG is equal to 10. The antenna has a peak gain of 9 dBi and an efficiency of 97.5%. The connected ground plane in the proposed antenna improves isolation and radiation performance, making it suitable for high-frequency, high-capacity 5G applications.
