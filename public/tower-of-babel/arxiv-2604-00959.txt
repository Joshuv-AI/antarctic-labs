Baryonic form factors of light pseudoscalar mesons Journal: Physics Letters B
A.S. Miramontes, J.M. Morgado, J. Papavassiliou

Abstract
Employing the Bethe-Salpeter formalism, we present a computation of the space-like baryonic form factor for the pion and kaon. In the exact isospin-symmetric limit this observable is forbidden by G G -parity, so that any nonzero signal constitutes a direct probe of the quark mass difference m d − m u m_{d}-m_{u} . The form factors are evaluated in the impulse approximation using fully dressed quark propagators, meson Bethe-Salpeter amplitudes, and a dressed baryon-current vertex constrained by the vector Ward-Takahashi identity. The baryonic radius computed with this method for the pion is given by ⟨ r B 2 ⟩ π + 1 / 2 = 0.043 ​ ( 2 ) \langle r_{\!B}^{2}\rangle_{\pi^{+}}^{1/2}=0.043(2) fm, and is consistent with the available dispersive benchmarks. Our predictions for the kaons, namely ⟨ r B 2 ⟩ K + 1 / 2 = 0.265 ​ ( 7 ) \langle r_{\!B}^{2}\rangle_{K^{+}}^{1/2}=0.265(7) fm and ⟨ r B 2 ⟩ K 0 1 / 2 = 0.262 ​ ( 7 ) \langle r_{\!B}^{2}\rangle_{K^{0}}^{1/2}=0.262(7) fm, indicate a larger spatial extent than in the pion case; these results have no dispersive counterparts, and are compatible with chiral QCD models.

Introduction

Electromagnetic form factors provide a direct window on the internal structure of hadrons, encoding the electric charge distribution as a function of momentum transfer.
Less familiar, but conceptually analogous, are the form factors associated with other conserved vector charges of QCD, which probe complementary aspects of the hadron structure.
Among them, the form factor linked to baryon number
is a particularly clean observable in the light-meson sector.
In particular,
for a
q\bar{q}
state, the total baryon number is zero; therefore, in the limit of exact isospin symmetry,
the corresponding pion form factor is forced to
vanish identically for all momenta.
Consequently, any nonzero signal is driven entirely by
isospin breaking, induced by the quark mass difference

m_{d}-m_{u}
, as well as by QED effects. Thus, the "baryonic" form factor (BFF)
serves as a sensitive indicator of how the quark and antiquark contributions fail to cancel each other inside a meson.

The first quantitative study of this observable was carried out in
[
61
]
for the charged pion; there, a constituent quark model estimate was complemented by a data-driven extraction from high-statistics
(e^{+}e^{-})
data collected by BaBar
[
9

and KLOE
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
5
]
, analytically continued to the space-like regime via dispersive relations. That analysis yielded a small but definite baryonic radius, and provided a phenomenological determination of the form factor as a function of the momentum transfer. The kaon case was subsequently explored in
[
16

, employing chiral quark models.

The BFFs are constructed from the baryon-number current coupled to the underlying

2
General framework

In this section, we summarize the formalism employed for the computation of the BFFs of the pion and kaons.

In order to fix the ideas, we recall that in QCD with exact isospin symmetry,
m_{u}=m_{d}
, and neglecting QED, the
G
-parity,
G=C\,e^{i\pi I_{2}}
, is an exact symmetry of the theory.
The baryon-number current,
j_{\!B}^{\mu}
, is odd under charge conjugation, whereas the pion is a
G
-parity eigenstate. As a consequence, the corresponding matrix element vanishes, and,
in that limit,
F_{\!B}^{\pi^{+}}(q^{2})=0
,
for all momentum transfers
[
37
,
59
]
.
Once isospin symmetry is broken, this cancellation no longer holds, and a nonzero BFF is induced. In the following, we focus on the strong isospin-breaking contribution associated with
m_{d}-m_{u}
, leaving additional QED effects for a future, more
detailed study.

From the practical point of view, the computation of a BFF for pseudoscalar mesons proceeds exactly as in the standard evaluation of electromagnetic form factors within the same SDE-BSE framework: one couples an external conserved vector current to the quark line through a fully dressed vertex, which obeys
its own SDE. In particular,
in the case of the electromagnetic current, one has

j_{\rm em}^{\mu}=\sum_{f}Q_{f}\,\bar{q}_{f}\gamma^{\mu}q_{f}\,,

(1)

with
Q_{u}=+2/3
,
Q_{d}=-1/3
, etc. The baryon-number current is instead flavour blind,

j_{\!B}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{f}\bar{q}_{f}\gamma^{\mu}q_{f}\,,

(2)

so that the Dirac and momentum structure of the vertex SDE are unchanged, and only the flavour weights associated with the external current are modified.

The central ingredients for the calculation of the form factors are the dressed quark propagator, the meson BSE, and the SDE for the baryon-current vertex, which is formally identical to the quark-photon vertex equation computed in
[
43
]
.

2.1
Baryonic form factor

The BFF of a pseudoscalar meson is defined from the matrix element of the baryon-number current between on-shell states. Denoting by
j_{\!B}^{\mu}(x)
the conserved baryon current, and by
\ket{\textbf{s}(p)}
the meson under consideration, with mass
M_{\textbf{s}}
, the hadronic matrix element may be written as

\bra{\textbf{s}(p_{\mathrm{out}})}j_{\!B}^{\mu}(0)\ket{\textbf{s}(p_{\mathrm{in}})}=(p_{\mathrm{out}}+p_{\mathrm{in}})^{\mu}\,F_{\!B}^{\textbf{s}}(q^{2})\,,

(3)

where
q=p_{\mathrm{out}}-p_{\mathrm{in}}
is the momentum of the current,
p_{\mathrm{in}}
and
p_{\mathrm{out}}
denote the initial and final meson momentum, and
F_{\!B}^{\textbf{s}}(q^{2})
is the baryonic form factor

[
61
]
. It is important to remark that in the isospin-symmetric limit, where
m_{u}=m_{d}
and the electromagnetic effects are neglected, the total baryon number of the pion is exactly zero, and the corresponding form factor
F_{\!B}(q^{2})
vanishes.
A non-zero baryonic form factor of the pion thus arises from isospin breaking effects, such as the quark mass difference
m_{d}-m_{u}
.
Within the present framework, the matrix element of the baryonic current is evaluated in the impulse approximation, by coupling the current to the constituent quarks inside the meson via a fully dressed baryon-current, see
Figure

1
. Specifically,

\displaystyle J_{\!B}^{\mu}(p_{\text{av}},q)=

\displaystyle\int_{k}\,\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{ff^{\prime}}(k_{\mathrm{out}},p_{\mathrm{out}})\,S_{\!f}(\ell_{+})\,\Gamma_{B,f}^{\mu}(k_{+},q)\,S_{\!f}(\ell_{-})\,

(4)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{A}_{ff^{\prime}}(k_{\mathrm{in}},p_{\mathrm{in}})\,S_{\!f^{\prime}}(k_{-})+(f\leftrightarrow f^{\prime})\,,

where
\mathcal{A}
is the meson BS amplitude,
\bar{\mathcal{A}}
is the charge conjugated meson amplitude, and
S_{\!f}
is the quark propagator of flavour
f
.
In addition,
\Gamma_{\!B}^{\mu}
denotes the
vertex that describes the coupling of the
current to a quark-antiquark pair; for brevity we will refer to it as the "
B
-vertex".

The integration measure and kinematic variables are defined as follows

\int_{k}:=(2\pi)^{-4}\int d^{4}k\,,\\

(5)

\displaystyle k_{\pm}=k\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\,p_{\text{av}},\qquad\ell_{\pm}=k_{+}\pm\tfrac{q}{2}\,\qquad s_{\pm}=k_{-}\pm\tfrac{q}{2}\,

(6)

Once the current has been computed, the baryonic form factor can be extracted from the contraction,

F_{\!B}^{\textbf{s}}(q^{2})=\frac{J_{\!B}^{\mu}(p_{\text{av}},q)\,p_{\text{av}}^{\mu}}{2\,p_{\text{av}}^{2}}\,,

(7)

evaluated for on-shell external momenta,

p_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}=p_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}=-M_{\textbf{s}}^{2}
, with
p_{\text{av}}
parametrized as
p_{\text{av}}=\big(0,0,0,i\sqrt{M^{2}_{\textbf{s}}+q^{2}/4}\big)
.
Finally, the baryonic mean squared radius of the meson is defined from the slope at the origin

\langle r_{\!B}^{2}\rangle_{\textbf{s}}=-6\,\frac{dF_{\!B}^{\textbf{s}}(q^{2})}{dq^{2}}\bigg|_{q^{2}=0}

(8)

This quantity provides a quantitative measure of the baryonic spatial distribution induced inside the meson by isospin breaking.

2.2
Dynamical equations

All building blocks entering the baryon-number current in Eq. (
4
) are obtained from their own dynamical equations, evaluated with the same flavour-dependent effective interaction used in Ref.
[
27
]
. In the present work we employ an explicitly isospin-breaking setup, and therefore solve separate quark gap equations for the
u
,
d
and
s
quarks, yielding distinct propagators
S_{u}
,
S_{d}
and
S_{s}
. Consequently, the pion BS amplitude is computed as a
genuine

u\bar{d}
bound state (and analogously the kaons as
u\bar{s}
and
d\bar{s}
), rather than in the isospin-symmetric basis.

The three dynamical equations
relevant for the present analysis
are (
i
) the quark gap equation,
(
ii
) the meson BSE, and (
iii
)
the SDE for the
B
-vertex, see
Figure

2
.

Following
[
43
]
, the
key ingredient common to these three
equations is
the flavour-dependent
effective interaction,
\mathcal{I}_{\!ff^{\prime}}
(k^{2})
, defined as,

\mathcal{I}_{\!ff^{\prime}}(k^{2})=\tilde{\alpha}_{T}(k^{2})\,A_{f}(k^{2})\,A_{f^{\prime}}(k^{2})\,,

(9)

where
\tilde{\alpha}_{T}(k^{2})
is the modified Taylor coupling introduced
in
[
27
]
. The crucial
difference between
\tilde{\alpha}_{T}(k^{2})
and the
standard Taylor coupling
\alpha_{T}(k^{2})

[
66
,
14
,
67
,
13
,
73
]
is that the former
includes additional process-independent contributions,
extracted from the two
quark-gluon vertices attached to the
gluon propagator (blue circles in Eq. (
2
)).
Convoluting these contributions with the lattice data for

\alpha_{T}(k^{2})

[
73
]
, one
finally obtains the
\tilde{\alpha}_{T}(k^{2})
shown in
the left panel of
Figure

3
. There,
the red dotted curve represents a convenient
parametrization of
\tilde{\alpha}_{T}(k^{2})
introduced
in
[
27
]
, particularly suitable for the ensuing numerical
treatment. On the same plot,
the red band corresponds to variations of
\tilde{\alpha}_{T}(k^{2})

obtained by shifting the fit parameters by one standard
deviation. These variations, in turn, propagate into
the quantities computed (quark propagator, pion BS amplitude,
and BBFs), and can serve as error estimates of this approach.

In the Landau gauge, this construction defines the effective gluon-exchange kernel

D_{\mu\nu}^{ff^{\prime}}(k)=4\pi\,\Delta_{\mu\nu}^{0}(k)\,\mathcal{I}_{ff^{\prime}}(k^{2})\,,\qquad\Delta_{\mu\nu}^{0}(k)=\left(\delta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{k_{\mu}k_{\nu}}{k^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{k^{2}}\,,

(10)

where the flavour dependence of
\mathcal{I}_{\!ff^{\prime}}(k^{2})
in Eq. (
9
)
enters through the quark dressing function
A_{f}(q^{2})
.
With the kernel of Eq. (
10
), the dressed quark propagator
for each flavour
f=u,d,s
is obtained from the gap equation

S_{\!f}^{-1}(p)=Z_{2}\,(i\not{p}+m_{f}^{R})+\underbrace{C_{\!{\scriptscriptstyle F}}\!\!\int_{k}\,\gamma_{\alpha}S_{\!f}(k+p)\gamma_{\beta}\,D_{\!ff}^{\alpha\beta}(k)}_{\Sigma_{f}(p^{2})}\,,

(11)

where
C_{F}=4/3
is the Casimir eigenvalue of the fundamental representation,
and
\Sigma_{f}(p^{2})
denotes the quark self-energy. In addition,
m_{f}^{R}
denotes the renormalized current mass, while
Z_{2}
is the quark renormalization constant, which will be determined via the momentum subtraction renormalization scheme (MOM)
[
17
,
30
,
15
,
8
]
. The inverse quark propagator is decomposed as

S_{\!f}^{-1}(p)=i\not{p}A_{f}(p^{2})+B_{f}(p^{2})\,,

(12)

and the renormalization-group invariant quark mass function is defined as
\mathcal{M}(p^{2})=B(p^{2})/A(p^{2})
.

Furthermore, quark-antiquark bound states are described by the homogeneous BSE

{\cal A}_{ff^{\prime}}(p_{1},p_{2})=-\int_{k}\gamma_{\mu}\,S_{\!f}(k_{1})\,{\cal A}_{ff^{\prime}}(k_{1},k_{2})\,S_{\!f^{\prime}}(k_{2})\,\gamma_{\nu}\,D_{ff^{\prime}}^{\mu\nu}(k)\,,

(13)

with
k_{i}=k+p_{i}
for
i=1,2
. It is convenient to introduce the total and relative momenta
P=p_{1}-p_{2}
and
p=(p_{1}+p_{2})/2
, in terms of which one writes
{\cal A}_{ff^{\prime}}(p_{1},p_{2})\equiv{\cal A}_{ff^{\prime}}(p,P)
. For a pseudoscalar meson, we employ the Dirac decomposition

{\cal A}_{ff^{\prime}}(p,P)=\left(\chi_{1}^{ff^{\prime}}+i\,\chi_{2}^{ff^{\prime}}\!\not{P}+i\,\chi_{3}^{ff^{\prime}}\!\not{p}\,(p\!\cdot\!P)+\chi_{4}^{ff^{\prime}}\![\not{p},\not{P}]\right)\gamma_{5}\,,

(14)

where the dressing functions
\chi_{i}^{ff^{\prime}}(p^{2},p\!\cdot\!P,P^{2})

encode the momentum dependence of the amplitude.

Finally, the coupling of the external baryonic current to a dressed quark is described by the
B-
vertex,
\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2})
, which satisfies an SDE of the form,

\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2})=\gamma^{\mu}-C_{F}\!\!\!\int_{k}\gamma_{\alpha}\,S_{f}(k_{1})\,\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2})\,S_{f}(k_{2})\,\gamma_{\beta}\,D_{\alpha\beta}^{ff}(k)\,.

(15)

The
U(1)_{B}
quark-number symmetry entails that the
B-
vertex satisfies the corresponding vector WTI,

q_{\mu}\,\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2})=i\,S_{f}^{-1}(p_{2})-i\,S_{f}^{-1}(p_{1})\,.

(16)

We emphasize that the
present truncation guarantees the validity
of the WTI in Eq. (
16
), essentially due to
the fact that
the defining equations for
S
and

\Gamma_{B,f}^{\mu}
(i and (iii) in
Fig.
2
), share a common interaction kernel
(blue band).

As was done in the electromagnetic case
[
43
]
, the current-vertex can be decomposed as

\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu}(p_{1},p_{2})=\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu,\text{BC}}(p_{1},p_{2})+\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu,\text{T}}(p_{1},p_{2})\,,

(17)

where the so-called "Ball-Chiu" part,

\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu,\text{BC}}
saturates the
WTI of Eq. (
16
)
[
10
]
, while the remainder,

\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu,\text{T}}
,
vanishes when contracted by
q_{\mu}
.
Then,
\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu,\text{BC}}
may be
expressed solely in terms of the quark dressing functions
A_{f}
and
B_{f}
, while the transverse components encode dynamical information beyond the WTI constraints.
In particular,
\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu,\text{T}}
develops the vector-meson poles generated by the quark–antiquark scattering kernel, namely, the
\rho
in isovector channels and the
\omega
in the isoscalar
[
48
,
47
,
70
]
; these contributions are crucial
for the behaviour of the form factor and its charge radius.
Within the present SDE-BSE framework, these poles emerge dynamically from the same scattering kernel that describes the bound-state spectrum, rather than being introduced through a vector-meson-dominance Ansatz
[
55
]
.
For the pion BFF, this becomes relevant once isospin breaking (
m_{u}\neq m_{d}
) is included.
Indeed, at that point,
the matrix element becomes sensitive to the isoscalar vector strength encoded in
\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu,\text{T}}
, most notably that associated with the
\omega
resonance.

The tensor basis of the vertex
\Gamma_{B,f}^{\mu}

employed in this work
is identical to the one used in
[
43
]

for the quark-photon vertex [see Eqs. (4.4) and (4.7) therein].

In this section, we present the numerical results for the computed quark propagators, meson masses and baryonic form factors. For a detailed description of the numerical techniques we refer the reader to
[
63

.

(
{\it i}
)
Numerical inputs.
The quark gap equations are renormalized in the MOM scheme at
\mu=4.3~\text{GeV}
. The current quark masses are fixed to

m_{u}=3.7~\text{MeV}\,,\quad m_{d}=6.2~\text{MeV}\,,

(18)

which reproduce the physical charged pion mass and its decay constant.
In addition, the strange-quark mass is chosen as

m_{s}=95~\text{MeV}\,,

(19)

such that the charged kaon mass is correctly described within the same flavour-dependent interaction framework. The effective interaction
\mathcal{I}_{ff^{\prime}}(k^{2})
and its parametrization are taken from Ref.
[
27
]
without further adjustment. Thus, no additional parameters are introduced in the present study beyond the standard light-quark masses.

(
{\it ii}
)
Quark propagators.
For each flavour
f=u,d,s
we first solve the corresponding quark gap equation, obtaining the dressing functions
A_{f}(p^{2})
,
B_{f}(p^{2})
and the mass function
\mathcal{M}_{f}(p^{2})
. In Euclidean space-time, the total momentum of an on-shell bound state of mass
M_{\textbf{s}}
can be chosen as

P=(0,0,0,iM_{\textbf{s}})\,,\qquad P^{2}=-M_{\textbf{s}}^{2}\,.

(20)

With this parametrization, the squared momenta flowing through the quark and
antiquark lines are

k_{1,2}^{2}=k^{2}+\frac{P^{2}}{4}\pm k\!\cdot\!P\,.

(21)

As the integration momentum
k
is varied, the quantities
k_{1,2}^{2}

describe parabolic domains in the complex
k_{1,2}^{2}
-plane, which implies that the
quark dressing functions entering
S(k_{1,2})
must be known in the complex region.

The required analytic continuation of the quark propagator to the complex plane is implemented employing the Cauchy interpolation method described in
[
63
,
26
,
38
]
.

The resulting mass functions
\mathcal{M}(p^{2})=B(p^{2})/A(p^{2})
are displayed in the right panel of
Figure

3
for
f=u,d,s
. In all cases,
\mathcal{M}(p^{2})
shows the expected infrared enhancement from dynamical chiral symmetry breaking, and evolves smoothly towards the current masses at large momentum. The corresponding central values at the origin are
\mathcal{M}_{u}(0)=0.328
GeV,
\mathcal{M}_{d}(0)=0.341
GeV and
\mathcal{M}_{s}(0)=0.591
GeV. The band around the central lines corresponds to the error propagation from the variation of the
\tilde{\alpha}_{T}(k^{2})
, displayed in the left panel of
Figure

3
.

(
{\it iii}
)
Masses and decay constants.
The quark propagators obtained from the gap equations are then inserted into the
homogeneous BSEs for the pion and kaon. For a given value of the total momentum

P^{2}
, the BSE defines an integral equation for the BS amplitude, which can be expressed as an eigenvalue problem. In this formulation, one determines an eigenvalue
\lambda(P^{2})
associated with the BSE kernel, and physical bound states are identified at those points
P^{2}=-M^{2}
where the leading eigenvalue fulfills
\lambda(P^{2})=1
. The computed BS amplitudes are subsequently employed in the impulse-approximation evaluation of the baryonic current, and the extraction of the BFF.

Using the inputs for the quark masses in Eq. (
18
) and Eq. (
19
), we obtain the following mass and decay constant for the pion,

m_{\pi}=0.139\text{GeV},\qquad f_{\pi}=0.130\text{GeV}

(22)

while for the kaons we have,

\displaystyle m_{K^{\pm}}=0.493\text{GeV},\qquad f_{K^{\pm}}=0.157\text{GeV}

(23)

\displaystyle m_{K^{0}}=0.499\text{GeV},\qquad f_{K^{0}}=0.157\text{GeV}

(24)

In comparison, the corresponding experimental values are
m_{\pi}=0.139
GeV and
f_{\pi}=0.130
GeV for the pion, and
m_{K}^{\pm}=0.493
GeV,
f_{K}^{\pm}=0.155
GeV,
m_{K}^{0}=0.497
GeV,
f_{K}^{0}=0.155
GeV for the kaons
[
53
]
.
The choice of current-quark masses is guided by the requirement that, within the present approach, the charged pseudoscalar sector be reproduced. Within this approach, the resulting
K^{\pm}
-
K^{0}
splitting reflects explicitly the strong isospin-breaking effect driven by
m_{d}-m_{u}
. The small deviation of

from its physical value, of around
0.5\%
, is not unexpected, because the physical

-
K^{0}
mass difference receives a repulsive
electromagnetic contribution to

that is absent here
[
7
]
.
It is expected that
the inclusion of electromagnetic isospin breaking at the level of the SDE-BSE system would shift the neutral-kaon mass toward its physical value,
yielding the full splitting
[
45
]
.

(
{\it iv}
)
Baryonic form factor.

With the quark propagators and meson BS amplitudes computed, the baryonic form
factor can be obtained from the impulse-approximation expression of Eq. (
4
). The remaining ingredient in this current matrix element is the dressed
B-
vertex,

\Gamma_{\!B,f}^{\mu}
, which we determine by solving the SDE
of Eq. (
15
)
in the space-like region, for each flavour
f=u,d,s
. We compute the full vertex in its most general form, retaining all 12 dressing functions, and solve the vertex equation for the set of
q^{2}
values at which the form factor is evaluated. After projection onto the 12-dimensional Dirac basis and discretization of the momentum variables, the SDE reduces to a linear system for the vertex dressing functions. This system is solved by standard matrix-inversion techniques (LU decomposition
[
56
]
); the form factors obtained coincide with those
shown in Figs. 6 and 7 of

[
43
]
, and will not be repeated here.
The resulting vertex, together with the propagators and BS amplitudes, is then inserted into Eq. (
4
) to compute the baryonic current and extract
F^{\textbf{s}}_{\!B}(q^{2})
from Eq. (
7
).

flavour contributions with different external-current weights. For the neutral
kaon,
K^{0}=d\bar{s}
, the electromagnetic current couples with

Q_{d}=-1/3
and
Q_{\bar{s}}=+1/3

F_{\rm EM}^{K^{0}}(q^{2})=Q_{d}\,F_{d}(q^{2})+Q_{\bar{s}}\,F_{\bar{s}}(q^{2})=\frac{1}{3}\Big[F_{\bar{s}}(q^{2})-F_{d}(q^{2})\Big]\,,

(27)

and therefore

-\,F_{\rm EM}^{K^{0}}(q^{2})=\frac{1}{3}\Big[F_{d}(q^{2})-F_{\bar{s}}(q^{2})\Big]\,.

(28)

In contrast, in the baryon-number current,
j_{B}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{f}\bar{q}_{f}\gamma^{\mu}q_{f}
,
the quark (
d
) and antiquark (
\bar{s}
) enter
with opposite baryon number,
B_{d}=+1/3
and
B_{\bar{s}}=-1/3
. Consequently one may write, in the same schematic notation,

F_{B}^{K^{0}}(q^{2})=B_{d}\,F_{d}(q^{2})+B_{\bar{s}}\,F_{\bar{s}}(q^{2})=\frac{1}{3}\Big[F_{d}(q^{2})-F_{\bar{s}}(q^{2})\Big]\,,

(29)

which makes the relation
F_{B}^{K^{0}}(q^{2})=-F_{\rm EM}^{K^{0}}(q^{2})
manifest.

4
Conclusions

We have computed the baryonic form factors of
\pi^{+}
,
K^{+}
and
K^{0}
in an explicitly strong isospin-breaking scenario, and extracted the corresponding radii from the low-
q^{2}
behaviour.
Our central results are

\langle r_{B}^{2}\rangle_{\pi^{+}}=(0.043(2)\,\mathrm{fm})^{2}
,

\langle r_{\!B}^{2}\rangle_{K^{+}}=(0.265(7)\,\mathrm{fm})^{2}
, and

\langle r_{\!B}^{2}\rangle_{K^{0}}=(0.262(7)\,\mathrm{fm})^{2}
.
The pion value is consistent with the available dispersive benchmark, while the kaon radii are significantly larger and compare with the corresponding charge radius of the electromagnetic counterpart.
The low-momentum behaviour of these form factors is controlled by the interplay between
symmetry-imposed constraints and the dynamical vector-meson content encoded in the "B-vertex",
which is generated from its own SDE.

may be obtained from
the present approach
by adding photonic loops in the
dynamical equations of

, and carrying out
an appropriate re-tuning
of
m_{u}
,
m_{d}
and
m_{s}
, see e.g.,
[
45
]
.
