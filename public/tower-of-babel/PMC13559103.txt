Development and validation of an early warning score for refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia in pediatric patients
Authors: Dani Qin, Mengke Cheng, Xujiao Pu, Lingyun Xia, Xueping Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) is a common respiratory infection in children. Refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (RMPP) can lead to severe complications if not identified early. This study aimed to develop and test an early warning scoring system for RMPP in children, serving as a clinical instrument for early detection of high-risk RMPP patients.MethodsA retrospective study was performed using clinical data of children with MPP admitted during the period of January 2023 to December 2024. The patients were categorized into the general MPP (GMPP) group (56 cases) and RMPP group (34 cases) depending on the occurrence of progression to RMPP. The two groups were compared with regard to clinical features, laboratory findings, radiographic appearances, and features of the pathogen. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent risk factors, and an RMPP early warning scoring system (RMPP-EWS) was developed. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to assess model performance. To mitigate overfitting and quantify the optimism in predictive performance, an internal bootstrap validation with 1,000 resamples was conducted. Furthermore, a sensitivity analysis was performed to assess the impact of excluding fever duration from the prediction model, and a comparative evaluation was carried out between an equal-weighted scoring system and a coefficient-based weighted scoring system.ResultsThe RMPP group had a significantly increased pre-admission fever duration, C-reactive protein (CRP), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), D-dimer level, and more atelectasis and A2063G mutation than the GMPP group (P<0.05). Multivariate analysis showed that duration of fever ≥7 days [odds ratio (OR) =6.40, 95% confidence interval (CI): 2.30–17.82], CRP ≥20 mg/L (OR =3.43), LDH ≥400 U/L (OR =4.29), D-dimer ≥500 µg/L (OR =3.07), atelectasis (OR =5.35), and A2063G mutation (OR =3.84) were predictors of RMPP. The RMPP-EWS (0–12 points) was developed based on these indicators. The analysis of the ROC curve showed an area under the curve (AUC) value of 0.892 (95% CI: 0.823–0.961), and the optimal cutoff value was ≥6 points, with a sensitivity of 85.3% and a specificity of 82.1%. The optimism-corrected AUC from bootstrap validation was 0.871 (95% CI: 0.802–0.940), with a shrinkage factor of 0.92. The five-variable model (excluding fever duration) demonstrated an AUC of 0.854 (95% CI: 0.771–0.937), and the coefficient-based weighted score showed an AUC of 0.896 (95% CI: 0.828–0.964), which was not significantly different from the equal-weighted score (DeLong test, P=0.68). The risk stratification included the following: low risk (0–4 points, RMPP incidence 10.7%), moderate risk (5–8 points, 36.8%), and high risk (9–12 points, 70.8%).ConclusionsThe RMPP-EWS shows a predictive value and has the capacity to help in the early detection of high-risk RMPP patients to inform individual treatment decisions. This score may assist clinicians in early risk stratification. External validation in a larger multicenter cohort is warranted before clinical implementation.Keywords: Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP), refractory, early warning, scoring system, children

Highlight box

Key findings

• A 6-item early warning score [refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia early warning scoring system (RMPP-EWS); 0–12 points] was developed and internally validated for predicting RMPP in children.

• The score achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.892 (optimism-corrected AUC 0.871), outperforming any single predictor.

• Risk stratification (low/moderate/high) showed stepwise RMPP incidence (10.7%, 36.8%, 70.8%) and effectively guided clinical intervention.

What is known and what is new?

• RMPP affects 10–30% of children with Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia and can lead to severe complications. Early identification of high-risk patients is crucial for timely intervention and improved outcomes. Existing warning indicators are largely univariate and lack systematic integration.

• We developed and validated the RMPP-EWS, a simple 6-item scoring system (0–12 points) incorporating fever duration, C-reactive protein (CRP), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), D-dimer, atelectasis, and A2063G mutation. The scoring system achieved an AUC of 0.892 with optimism-corrected AUC of 0.871, outperforming any single predictor. Risk stratification (low/moderate/high) effectively guided clinical decision-making and intervention intensity.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• RMPP-EWS provides clinicians with an objective, bedside tool for early risk stratification. The scoring system facilitates timely intensification of treatment in high-risk patients, potentially reducing complications and improving prognosis. External validation in multicenter cohorts is needed before widespread clinical implementation.

Introduction

Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MP) is the most common bacterial pathogen of community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in children, and it causes 10% to 40% of cases of childhood CAP worldwide (1,2). Although the majority of children with Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) responds to treatment with the macrolide antibiotics, approximately 10–30% develop refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (RMPP), a different clinical phenotype characterized by periodic or persistent fever, progressive disease, and radiographic progression despite 7 days or more of appropriate therapy with the drug (3,4). RMPP imparts significantly higher risks of severe pulmonary complications, which comprise atelectasis, plastic bronchitis, and necrotizing pneumonia. In addition, a subset of infected children acquires an irreversible pulmonary sequelae, bronchiectasis and chronic atelectasis, which underscores the possibility of respiratory morbidity in the long term (4-6).

The RMPP pathogenesis is complex and multidimensional, comprising macrolide resistance, overreactive inflammatory immune response, airway mucus plugging, mixed infection, and hypercoagulable state (7-9). These factors occur in interaction and increase with each other, creating a vicious cycle. The key connection is that the resistance to macrolides allows the continuous multiplication of the MP and release of toxins, leading to an inappropriate reaction of the immune system. Inflammation damages the airway mucosa on the one hand, which contributes to the formation of mucus plugs and bronchial obstruction; and on the other hand, mixed infections enhance the effect of the inflammatory cascade, and hypercoagulability causes pulmonary microthrombosis and ischemic necrosis, which ultimately cause irreversible lung injury (10,11). RMPP may quickly degenerate into respiratory failure, dysfunction of multiple organs, and other severe disorders without timely treatment, making the treatment process much more difficult and this condition more prone to aftereffects (12). Thus, early diagnosis of children with high risks of RMPP and timely intensification of treatment (including corticosteroids, bronchoscopic lavage, and switching antibiotics) are essential in preventing the progression of the disease and improving prognosis (13).

The widely used RMPP warning indicators in clinical practice are largely univariate in nature, and have not been systematically integrated, with cutoff values used inconsistently across studies, and therefore, have restricted clinical utility (14). In the meantime, other signals like cytokines and MP-selective antibodies are complicated to measure and expensive, so these can hardly be introduced into primary healthcare promptly. Thus, the research proposes a retrospective analysis of clinical data on children with MPP, combining multidimensional information such as the disease course, inflammatory markers, coagulation function, imaging results, and pathogen characteristics, to create a simple, practical, and accessible early warning scoring system on RMPP (RMPP-EWS). The tool is projected to be a dependable bedside evaluation tool for early detection of high-risk children and provision of stratified treatment. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2026-0444/rc).

Methods

Study subjects

A retrospective collection of clinical data of children with MPP hospitalized in the period between January 2023 and December 2024 was conducted. No significant missing data existed; patients with incomplete key data were excluded. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Affiliated Yixing Hospital of Jiangsu University (approval No. 2022-Research-166) and individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived due to the use of de-identified data.

Inclusion criteria: (I) age 3–14 years; (II) compliant with the diagnostic criteria of MP as presented in the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Mycoplasma pneumoniae Pneumonia in Children (15); (III) positive nucleic acid test MP within 48 hours of admission; (IV) availability of complete clinical data, including epidemiological history, clinical manifestations, laboratory findings, imaging results, and treatment process.

Exclusion criteria: (I) pre-admission use of immunosuppressants, immunoglobulins, or other medications that could alter immune function; (II) coexisting severe underlying diseases, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, hereditary metabolic disorders, malignant tumors, chronic hepatic or renal diseases, neuromuscular disorders or immunodeficiency syndromes; (III) voluntary discharge or transfer to a different hospital in 48 hours of admission, which would result in the incomplete medical records; (IV) absence of key clinical information, rendering grouping or efficacy evaluation unfeasible.

GMPP group (satisfying all the requirements): (I) fulfillment of the diagnostic criteria of MPP; (II) body temperature normalization after 72 hours of standard macrolide antibiotic treatment; (III) clinical syndrome improvement; (IV) pulmonary improvement or stabilization on radiography; (V) no extrapulmonary complications; (VI) no need for bronchoscopic treatment.

RMPP group (satisfying any criterion): (I) persistence of fever 7 days or more despite conventional macrolide antibiotic treatment; (II) progressive deterioration of the clinical picture; (III) radiological deterioration of pulmonary involvement, i.e., expanding consolidation, pleural effusion or cast removal of bronchial casts; (IV) onset of extrapulmonary complications, i.e., neurological, hematological, cardiovascular, or mucocutaneous involvement; (V) requirement for at least one bronchoscopic intervention, such as bronchoalveolar lavage or bronchial cast removal; (VI) diagnosis of plastic bronchitis; (VII) progression to severe or critically severe MPP.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were done using SPSS version 26.0. Continuous variables are reported in the form of median (interquartile range), and intergroup comparison was made through the use of the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented in the form of frequency (percentage), and group comparisons were conducted with the help of the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. The multivariate logistic regression model was developed by including variables with a P value less than 0.1 during univariate analysis. Forward selection was applied in the selection of independent risk factors. A RMPP-EWS was created according to the values of regression coefficients β. To assess the predictive performance, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used, with area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity having been obtained. Any P value below 0.05 was deemed to be statistically significant. To mitigate overfitting and quantify optimism in predictive performance, an internal bootstrap validation was performed with 1,000 resamples. In each iteration, a bootstrap sample of 90 patients—drawn with replacement from the original cohort—was used to refit the multivariate logistic regression model using the identical forward stepwise selection procedure. Optimism-corrected performance estimates were calculated as the difference between the apparent performance and the mean optimism (averaged across all resamples); 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated using the percentile method. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% CIs were calculated for each predictor.

Internal validation and overfitting assessment

According to the TRIPOD guidelines and the methodological recommendations by Riley et al. (16) for developing clinical prediction models, a minimum of 10 events per candidate predictor variable (EPV) is recommended to mitigate the risk of overfitting (17,18). With 34 events and 6 predictors (EPV =5.7), we conducted three validation procedures to address overfitting: (I) least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) to shrink coefficients; (II) bootstrap (1,000 resamples) to estimate optimism-corrected performance, each resample was refitted using the same forward stepwise method, and the corrected AUC was calculated as apparent AUC minus mean optimism; (III) shrinkage factor to adjust coefficients.

Determination of cut-off values for continuous variables

To assess whether dichotomization reduced predictive accuracy, we compared the binary model with a continuous model using raw values via the DeLong test (19).

Sensitivity analysis for incorporation bias

To address potential circularity, given that prolonged fever duration is both a defining diagnostic criterion of RMPP and a predictor in the model, we excluded this variable and refitted the multivariate logistic regression model using the remaining five predictors: CRP, LDH, D-dimer, atelectasis, and the A2063G mutation. We then compared the discriminatory performance (AUC) of the resulting five-variable model with that of the original six-variable model using the DeLong test (19).

Comparison of equal-weighted and coefficient-based weighted scoring systems

To justify the equal weighting approach (2 points per predictor), we developed a coefficient-based weighted score using the original logistic regression coefficients. The performance of the weighted score was compared with the equal-weighted score using the DeLong test (19) and decision curve analysis (20).

Results

Comparison of general characteristics between the two groups

In this study, a total of 136 children with mycoplasma pneumonia were hospitalized during the period from January 2023 to December 2024. According to the screening criteria, 90 children with mycoplasma pneumonia (including 56 cases of common mycoplasma pneumonia and 34 cases of severe mycoplasma pneumonia) were finally included (Figure 1). There were no significant differences between the two groups in relation to the gender balance (55.4% vs. 55.9%, P=0.96) or age balance (6.5 vs. 7.0 years, P=0.37). The refractory group had a longer time interval between symptom onset and hospital admission (7.0 vs. 5.0 days, P=0.002) and a much longer period of fever before hospital admission (9.0 vs. 5.0 days, P<0.001). The refractory group was also found to have very much higher rates of antibiotic use (88.2% vs. 67.9%, P=0.03) and glucocorticoid use (35.3% vs. 8.9%, P=0.002) before hospitalization. Moreover, the rate of co-infections with viruses (41.2% vs. 21.4%, P=0.040) and bacteria (35.3% vs. 7.1%, P<0.001) was significantly greater in the RMPP than in the GMPP (Table 1).

From January 2023 to December 2024, a total of 136 children with MPP were screened. Among them, 46 were excluded due to incomplete clinical data or previous antibiotic treatment. Ultimately, 90 participants (56 of the common type MPP and 34 of the severe type MPP) participated in the development of the multivariate logistic regression model and the internal validation based on the bootstrap method (1,000 iterations). The final MPP-EWS model achieved an AUC of 0.892 (95% CI: 0.823–0.961), with a sensitivity of 85.3% and a specificity of 82.1%, and was able to classify the risk into three categories: low (0–4 points, n=28), medium (5–8 points), and high (9–12 points, n=24).

Comparison of laboratory parameters between the two groups

CBC indicated that the RMPP group had much higher WBCs (10.8×109/L vs. 8.5×109/L, P=0.02) and NE% (72.5% vs. 65.0%, P=0.01). In terms of acute-phase proteins, the control group showed significantly lower levels of CRP (18.5 vs. 6.5 mg/L, P<0.001), HBP (58.5 vs. 25.0 ng/mL, P=0.001), and SAA (125.0 vs. 45.0 mg/L, P<0.001). Cytokine profiling indicated markedly increased concentrations of IL-6 (15.5 vs. 4.5 pg/mL, P<0.001), TNF-α (32.5 vs. 20.0 pg/mL, P=0.003), and IFN-γ (35.0 vs. 22.0 pg/mL, P=0.005) in the RMPP group. Coagulation function tests showed significantly higher levels of D-dimer (520 vs. 280 µg/L, P<0.001) and fibrinogen degradation products (2.10 vs. 1.20 µg/mL, P=0.001) in RMPP patients. Moreover, LDH was also high in the RMPP group (420.0 vs. 310.0 U/L, P=0.003). There were no statistically significant differences in immunoglobulin parameters among the two groups (Table 2).

Comparison of imaging and etiological characteristics between the two groups

Radiological results showed that atelectasis was much more common in the RMPP group than in the general Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (GMPP) group (70.6% vs. 17.9%, P=0.001), and was much more prevalent in multilobar atelectasis (25.0% vs. 0%, P=0.01). Statistically significant higher prevalence rates of pulmonary consolidation (88.2% vs. 62.5%, P=0.01), pleural effusion (23.5% vs. 5.4%, P=0.01), and bronchial wall thickening (82.4% vs. 57.1%, P=0.02) coupled with a higher multilobar involvement in consolidation (60.0% vs. 28.6%, P=0.03) were also demonstrated by the RMBP group Microbiologically, the positive rate of MP nucleic acid was similar between the two groups in throat swabs, however, in the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid, the positive rate in the RMPP was significantly higher (82.4% vs. 21.4%, P<0.001). Moreover, the A2063G drug-resistant mutation rate (58.8% vs. 21.4%, P<0.001) and the rate of the co-infection with other bacteria (35.3% vs. 7.1%, P<0.001) were significantly higher in the RMPP population (Table 3).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis of independent risk factors for RMPP

The univariate analysis P value <0.1 variables were incorporated into the multivariate logistic regression analysis. The results revealed that the following factors were independent risk factors for RMPP: fever duration ≥7 days before admission (OR =6.40, 95% CI: 2.30–17.82, P<0.001), CRP ≥20 mg/L (OR =3.43, 95% CI: 1.40–8.41, P=0.007), LDH ≥400 U/L (OR =4.29, 95% CI: 1.57–11.72, P=0.004), D-dimer ≥500 µg/L (OR =3.07, 95% CI: 1.20–7.85, P=0.01), atelectasis (OR =5.35, 95% CI: 1.88–15.23, P=0.002), and A2063G mutation (OR =3.84, 95% CI: 1.47–10.03, P=0.006). The model proved to fit well (Hosmer-Lemeshow test P=0.62) (Table 4).

Development and validation of the RMPP early warning scoring system

This study developed an early warning system of RMPP based on six independent risk factors, and each variable is allocated 2 points, leading to a range of 0–12 points on the scores. The risk stratification criteria were established as follows: low risk (0–4 points, equaling to the lack of risk of RMPP), moderate risk (5–8 points, equaling to 30–50% risk of RMPP) and high risk (9–12 points, equaling to greater than 70% risk of RMPP) (Tables 5,6). The ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC of this scoring system in predicting RMPP was 0.892 (95% CI: 0.823–0.961). With a cutoff score of ≥5 points, the system had a sensitivity of 85.3%, specificity of 82.1%, positive predictive value (PPV) of 72.5%, and a negative predictive value (NPV) of 91.4%. It performed much better than any single indicator in predicting (all P<0.05) (Table 7).

Determination of cut-off values for continuous variables

The cut-off values for CRP (≥20 mg/L), LDH (≥400 U/L), and D-dimer (≥500 µg/L) were determined based on three considerations: (I) clinical relevance—these thresholds represent established clinical reference values for severe MPP in The Affiliated Yixing Hospital of Jiangsu University; (II) literature support—CRP ≥20 mg/L and LDH ≥400 U/L have been consistently reported as independent predictors of RMPP in pediatric studies (21,22); (III) empirical optimization—ROC curve analysis in our derivation cohort yielded similar values (CRP: 18.5 mg/L, LDH: 385 U/L, D-dimer: 485 µg/L), which were rounded to clinically practical thresholds. To evaluate whether dichotomization compromised predictive performance, we additionally developed a continuous model using raw CRP, LDH, and D-dimer values in logistic regression and compared its performance with the binary model using the DeLong test (19). The continuous model showed an AUC of 0.901, slightly higher than the binary model (0.892), but the difference was not statistically significant (P=0.31). Given the binary model’s greater clinical simplicity and ease of use at the bedside, we retained the dichotomized approach. Detailed cut-off determination and model comparison are provided in Table S2.

Sensitivity analysis: five-variable model excluding fever duration

To address the incorporation bias concern, we performed a sensitivity analysis by excluding fever duration from the prediction model and re-fitting the model with the remaining five predictors (CRP, LDH, D-dimer, atelectasis, and A2063G mutation). The resulting five-variable model demonstrated an AUC of 0.854 (95% CI: 0.771–0.937), which was slightly lower than the original six-variable model (AUC 0.892) but still indicated good discriminative performance. The calibration remained acceptable (Hosmer-Lemeshow P=0.41). The difference between the two models was not statistically significant (DeLong test, P=0.15). Detailed performance metrics are provided in Table S3.

Comparison of binary and continuous models

To further evaluate whether dichotomization of continuous variables compromised predictive performance, we compared the binary RMPP-EWS model with a continuous model using raw CRP, LDH, and D-dimer values in logistic regression. The continuous model showed an AUC of 0.901, slightly higher than the binary model (0.892), but the difference was not statistically significant (DeLong test, P=0.31). Given the binary model’s greater clinical simplicity and ease of use at the bedside, we retained the dichotomized approach. Detailed performance metrics are provided in Table S4.

Comparison of equal-weighted and coefficient-based weighted scores

To justify the equal weighting approach, we developed a coefficient-based weighted score using the original logistic regression coefficients. The weighted score yielded an AUC of 0.896 (95% CI: 0.828–0.964), which was not significantly different from the equal-weighted score’s AUC of 0.892 (DeLong test, P=0.68). The calibration and decision curve analysis also showed comparable results. These findings support that the simplicity of equal weighting was justified without meaningful loss of accuracy. Detailed performance metrics are provided in Table S5.

Comparison of clinical outcomes among different risk stratifications

Among the 90 enrolled subjects, the RMPP early warning scoring system stratified patients into three distinct risk strata: low-risk group (n=28, 31.1%), intermediate-risk group (n=38, 42.2%), and high-risk group (n=24, 26.7%). This stratification demonstrated a statistically significant positive correlation with the actual incidence of RMPP (P<0.001). The actual incidence of RMPP exhibited a stepwise escalation with increasing risk level: 10.7% in the low-risk group, 36.8% in the intermediate-risk group, and 70.8% in the high-risk group (trend test P<0.001). In the high-risk subgroup (n=24), the implementation rate of clinical interventions was significantly improved, with glucocorticoid utilization at 100%, bronchoscopic intervention required in 91.7% of cases, escalation of antibiotic therapy in 58.3%, and plastic bronchitis incidence at 33.3%. Concurrently, the median length of hospital stay was significantly prolonged in the high-risk group (14 days) compared with both the low-risk group (7 days) and the intermediate-risk group (10 days) (P<0.05 for both comparisons). The risk-outcome heat map further substantiated this graded association, indicating that all evaluated clinical outcome metrics increased significantly with ascending risk levels (P<0.05 for all metrics) (Table 8).

Discussion

Pathogenesis and early identification of RMPP

RMPP has a complicated pathogenesis (21). One of the primary causes of increased cases of RMPP is the prevalence of the macrolide-resistant Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MRMP) (22). The mutation rate of A2063G in the RMPP group was recorded as high as 58.8% compared to 21.4% in the GMPP group (P<0.001). This mutation rearranges the structure of the 23S rRNA so that the macrolide antibiotics are not able to bind their target sites effectively, resulting in a failure in treatment (23). Moreover, MP infection is capable of inducing an inadequate immune reaction in the host in the form of a cytokine storm (24). Another pathogenic factor of RMPP is co-infection (25). The prevalence of bacterial co-infection in the RMPP group was 35.3%, which was significantly more prevalent than in the GMPP (7.1, P<0.001). Bacterial infection intensifies airway inflammatory response, facilitates biofilm development and complicates therapy.

The timely detection of RMPP is important to enhance patient prognosis. Conventional single-indicator methods lack sensitivity; duration of fever 7 days and CRP 20mg/L, had only 76.5% and 70.6% sensitivities, respectively, which are not sensitive enough to provide early warning. In this study, the RMPP-EWS is a multidimensional combination of information, such as disease course, inflammation, tissue injury, coagulation, imaging, and pathogen data, with a sensitivity of 85.3% and a NPV of 91.4%. The system is very effective in identifying indeed low-risk pediatric patients to prevent overtreatment, and screening high-risk cases for early and aggressive treatment. Two indicators that are easily missed but are critically important are especially highlighted in the scoring system: the D-dimer as a manifestation of dysfunction of coagulation and the A2063G mutation as evidence of drug resistance. These two indicators together improve the predictive performance to the AUC of 0.892, which is a competent basis for early clinical decision-making.

Clinical value of the RMPP-EWS

The scoring system of the RMPP-EWS that was created during this study combines clinical data in six dimensions, including disease progression, inflammatory response, tissue injury, coagulation function, imaging findings, and pathogen characteristics. This system attained the AUC of 0.892 to predict RMPP, which was significantly higher than any single parameter (all P<0.05). The scoring system has a number of clinical benefits: (I) simple Implementation: all six indicators are routinely measured in clinical practice, and a result is obtained within 24–48 hours of admission and does not require any special instruments or techniques; (II) early applicability: the score can be calculated early in the course of the disease, as the results are available within 24–48 hours of admission and do not require any special instruments or methods; (III) stratified therapy guidance: low-risk patients can undergo standard azithromycin therapy under close follow-up, moderate-risk patients should be prescribed early treatment with methylprednisolone (2–12 mg/kg/day) and assessed with regard to bronchoscopic intervention, high-risk patients are advised to receive an intensive multidisciplinary approach that includes bronchoalveolar lavage, substitution with tuberculosis antibiotics (tetracycline or quinolones), and high doses. This scoring system offers clinicians an objective, quantitative decision-making instrument, which will enable them to manage RMPP individually and more precisely.

Clinical significance of each risk factor

RMPP (OR =6.40, 95% CI: 2.30–17.82) is best predicted by a fever length of ≥7 days before hospitalization, indicating the possibility of failure in macrolide treatment and sustained inflammatory processes in the body, and thus the need to be careful of disease progression and to promptly change treatment plans. The presence of both a CRP value ≥20 mg/L and an LDH value ≥400 U/L, which are important laboratory parameters for assessing disease severity, indicates active systemic inflammation and significant tissue destruction of the lungs. D-dimer ≥500 µg/L is indicative of aggravated hypercoagulability and an increased risk of microthrombus formation, suggesting the possibility of pulmonary vascular inflammation and thrombosis, and complications related to coagulation dysfunction should be given attention. Airway obstruction with radiographic evidence of atelectasis (OR =5.35, 95% CI: 1.88–15.23) due to inflammatory exudates or plastic secretions is a significant sign of bronchoscopic intervention. Early diagnosis makes it easier to have immediate bronchoalveolar lavage and clearance of plastic secretions. Major molecular mechanism MP has the A2063G mutation (OR =3.84, 95% CI: 1.47–10.03) as the primary molecular mechanism that leads to macrolide resistance. Identification of this mutation gives direct recommendations on the modification of clinical antibiotic regimens, which implies the necessity of switching to tetracyclines or quinolones.

Limitations and prospects

This paper has a number of limitations. To begin with, the results can be biased due to selection bias and recall bias, since the research was a single-center retrospective investigation with a relatively small sample size. With an EPV of 5.7 (34 events across 6 candidate predictor variables), the model is at increased risk of instability and overfitting; although internal validation strategies, including LASSO variable selection, bootstrap resampling, and shrinkage factor calibration, were employed, such methods cannot substitute for external validation in independent, representative cohorts. The conclusions, therefore, need to be validated in multicenter and large-scale prospective studies. Second, the cutoff values and stratification rates of the RMPP-EWS scoring system were defined on the basis of the data obtained in this research, and their relevance and stability in other areas and populations need validation by other researchers. Third, fever duration as both a diagnostic criterion and predictor may introduce incorporation bias; however, the five-variable model (AUC 0.854) demonstrated acceptable performance without this variable. Fourth, dichotomization of continuous variables was justified by comparable performance of the continuous model (AUC 0.901 vs. 0.892, P=0.31). Fifth, equal weighting was supported by the non-inferior performance of the coefficient-based score (AUC 0.896 vs. 0.892, P=0.68). Finally, dynamic cytokine profiles and MP load are potential predictive indicators that were not included in the analysis and might have contributed to the exclusion of potentially useful biomarkers.

Future research directions are: (I) multicenter prospective cohort research, larger samples, to validate and refine the RMPP-EWS scoring system; (II) dynamic monitoring of cytokines like IL-6 and IFN-γ, and alterations in MP DNA load to further optimize the risk prediction model; (III) clinical, radiological, laboratory, and genomic data combined with machine learning algorithms to create a more accurate and intelligent prediction of early warning of RMPP; and (IV) conducting randomized controlled trials according to the RMPP-EWS scoring system in the assessment of the efficacy and safety of stratified treatment approaches, thus offering a higher level of evidence to clinical decision-making.

Conclusions

This research has effectively created an RMPP-EWS using six indicators, such as the duration of fever at admission, CRP, LDH, D-dimer, atelectasis, and the A2063G resistance mutation. This scoring system provides clinicians with an objective and quantitative decision-making tool that is favorable to the enhancement of the prognosis of children with RMPP and the decrease in the number of complications. However, given the limited sample size (EPV =5.7), potential incorporation bias from including fever duration as both a defining criterion and predictor, and the lack of external validation, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Multicenter prospective validation is required to confirm clinical utility, with future models integrating novel biomarkers and artificial intelligence to improve predictive performance.
