Frequency of complex-to-manage, difficult-to-manage and treatment-refractory psoriatic arthritis: applying the 2025 GRAPPA and EULAR definitions in a real-world longitudinal PsA cohort
Authors: Gonul Hazal Koc, Marc R Kok, Selinde V J Snoeck Henkemans, Jolanda J Luime, Ilja Tchetverikov, Lindy A Korswagen, Natasja H A M Denissen, Yvonne P M Goekoop-Ruiterman, Paul Baudoin, Petra Kok, Reinhard Bos, Radboud J E M Dolhain, Fabian Proft, Marijn Vis, CICERO
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo determine the frequency and baseline characteristic differences of complex-to-manage (C2M), difficult-to-manage (D2M) and treatment-refractory (TR) psoriatic arthritis (PsA) in a real-world longitudinal cohort, using the recently proposed 2025 Group for Research and Assessment of Psoriasis and Psoriatic Arthritis (GRAPPA) and the European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) definitions.MethodsWe analysed data from newly diagnosed, disease-modifying antirheumatic drug-naïve patients with PsA enrolled in the Dutch Southwest Early Psoriatic Arthritis cohort between 2013 and 2023. Patients were classified using the 2025 GRAPPA (C2M, TR) and EULAR (D2M, TR) PsA definitions. Frequencies were calculated for each definition. Time-to-event analyses using Kaplan-Meier methods assessed the timing of definition fulfilment and individual components. Longitudinal differences in patient-reported outcomes (PROs) were assessed using linear mixed-effects models.ResultsAmong 885 newly diagnosed patients with PsA, 20% (179/885) fulfilled the GRAPPA-C2M definition, 4.2% (37/885) fulfilled the EULAR-D2M definition, 3.8% (34/885) met the EULAR-TR and 1.2% (11/885) fulfilled the GRAPPA-TR criteria definition during follow-up. Female sex, higher body mass index (BMI), longer symptom duration, presence of enthesitis and greater skin involvement differed with meeting definitions at baseline. Patients classified as GRAPPA-C2M, GRAPPA-TR, EULAR-D2M and EULAR-TR consistently reported worse long-term PROs (Health Assessment Questionnaire-Disability Index scores in C2M (β=0.17, 95% CI 0.10 to 0.24), GRAPPA-TR (β=0.37, 95% CI 0.22 to 0.51), EULAR-D2M (β=0.36, 95% CI 0.20 to 0.48) and EULAR-TR (β=0.40, 95% CI 0.25 to 0.55)).ConclusionsApproximately 20% of patients with early PsA fulfilled the GRAPPA-C2M criteria, representing the earliest and broadest subgroup, while 4% met EULAR-D2M/EULAR-TR and 1.2% GRAPPA-TR definitions. These latter subgroups were more likely to be female, had higher BMI, greater skin involvement and enthesitis and experienced persistently worse PROs, underscoring the need for tailored management strategies.Keywords: Psoriatic Arthritis; Treatment; Outcome Assessment, Health Care; Patient Reported Outcome Measures

Introduction

Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) is a chronic inflammatory disease with a highly heterogeneous phenotype, including musculoskeletal, skin and systemic manifestations, often accompanied by comorbidities that complicate its clinical management.1 Despite the development of multiple biologic and targeted synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (b/tsDMARDs), a considerable proportion of patients fail to achieve sustained low disease activity or remission across all disease domains.2 3 In real-world cohorts, treatment persistence remains suboptimal, and patients often cycle through multiple therapies with diminishing clinical response rates.4 5

These therapeutic challenges have led to growing interest in a distinct subset of patients referred to as ‘difficult-to-treat’ (D2T) PsA.6 7 While the concept has long been established in rheumatoid arthritis, only recently have formal definitions been proposed for axial spondyloarthritis. To address this unmet need in PsA, both the Group for Research and Assessment of Psoriasis and Psoriatic Arthritis (GRAPPA) and the European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) independently introduced definitions for patients with persistent disease burden despite advanced therapies.8 9

In current literature, emerging studies using proxy definitions suggest that 16%–34% of patients with PsA may meet criteria for D2T disease, typically defined by failure of ≥1 conventional synthetic (cs)DMARD and ≥2 b/tsDMARDs with ongoing active symptoms.10,12 These patients are more often female, have higher body mass index (BMI) or body surface area (BSA) and frequently present with comorbidities such as fibromyalgia, depression or axial disease. However, these estimates are based on retrospective or registry-based data and rely on non-standardised definitions.

The availability of formal GRAPPA and EULAR D2T definitions now enables a more systematic and standardised assessment of this complex patient subgroup. However, these definitions have not yet been applied in longitudinal real-world PsA cohorts, and key questions identified by the EULAR task force remain unanswered, including the prevalence of difficult-to-manage (D2M) and treatment-refractory (TR) PsA, the outcome measures that best capture these states and their temporal evolution over time.9

In this study, we applied the newly proposed 2025 GRAPPA-complex-to-manage (C2M), GRAPPA-TR, EULAR-D2M and EULAR-TR definitions to a cohort of newly diagnosed patients with PsA to determine their frequency, baseline characteristics, temporal evolution and long-term disease burden. This approach provides a valuable opportunity to evaluate the applicability and real-world frequency of these definitions and to characterise baseline differences and longitudinal disease trajectories in comparison with the overall PsA population. These insights may support earlier recognition and more tailored management in PsA.

Methods

Patient selection

For this study, we included all patients with PsA consecutively enrolled in the Dutch Southwest Early Psoriatic Arthritis (DEPAR) cohort between July 2013 and March 2023, irrespective of follow-up duration, provided they had at least one follow-up visit after baseline, excluding those participating in Strategy Treatments Aiming at Minimal Disease Activity in PsA (STAMP) clinical trials. Follow-up time was variable due to the ongoing cohort. DEPAR is a multicentre, prospective observational cohort comprising newly diagnosed, DMARD-naïve patients with PsA from 14 centres (one academic hospital, 12 general hospitals and one specialised treatment centre).3 13 PsA was diagnosed by the treating rheumatologist based on clinical judgement, without requiring fulfilment of the CASPAR criteria.

Although patients were routinely engaged in DEPAR research projects, they were not involved in the design or reporting of this particular study.

Data collection

Patients were assessed every 3 months during the first year, every 6 months in the second year and annually thereafter. At each visit, research nurses conducted a physical examination and collected data on medication use, laboratory parameters and patient-reported outcomes (PROs).

Clinical assessments included tender and swollen joint counts (tender joint count-68 (TJC68), swollen joint count-66 (SJC66)), presence and extent of psoriasis (BSA: 0%–100%),14 enthesitis (Leeds Enthesitis Index (LEI): 0–6) and dactylitis.15 Laboratory measurements included C reactive protein (CRP).

PROs included functional ability (Health Assessment Questionnaire-Disability Index (HAQ-DI)),16 patient and physician global health and pain assessments (both on 0–100 mm visual analogue scale (VAS)),17 fatigue (numeric rating scale, 0–10)18 and Psoriatic Arthritis Impact of Disease 12-item questionnaire.19

Composite disease activity measures were used to comprehensively assess PsA activity across multiple domains. These included the Disease Activity index for PSoriatic Arthritis,20 the Psoriatic Arthritis Disease Activity Score21 and Minimal Disease Activity (MDA).22

Treatment

Treatment initiation and modifications were made through shared decision-making between the patient and the treating rheumatologist, based on national and international guidelines. There was no predefined treatment protocol.23 24

Outcomes

Patients were classified according to the 2025 GRAPPA definitions of C2M PsA and TR PsA and the 2025 EULAR definitions of D2M PsA and TR PsA.

All definitions are based on domains and proxy criteria listed in figure 1, table 1. Proxy measures for the domains were derived from published GRAPPA8 and EULAR9 definitions and operationalised using available clinical outcomes and PROs, with final selection refined by research team consensus. As these proxies were designed to reflect the original definitions, some overlap between domains (eg, persistent disease activity and objective inflammation) is inherent to the definitions, with certain proxies (eg, dactylitis, enthesitis, SJC ≥1 and Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI)/BSA; see table 1) contributing to both domains.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to report the frequency of GRAPPA-C2M and GRAPPA-TR PsA and EULAR-D2M and EULAR-TR PsA. Frequency was calculated as the number of patients fulfilling each definition divided by the total study population. Baseline characteristics were compared between patients across these subgroups using appropriate summary measures: mean and SD for normally distributed continuous variables, median and IQR for non-normally distributed variables and frequencies and percentages for categorical variables.

Time-to-event analyses were performed to evaluate the cumulative incidence and timing of fulfilling the GRAPPA and EULAR definitions. Kaplan-Meier methods were used to estimate the probability of fulfilling each definition during follow-up, with time calculated from baseline to the first visit at which the respective criteria or individual components were met. Patients who did not fulfil a definition were censored at their last available visit. Median times and IQRs were reported, and Kaplan-Meier curves were used to visually compare temporal patterns across definitions.

Longitudinal differences in disease burden between groups were evaluated using linear mixed-effects models to assess changes in HAQ-DI, pain, fatigue and patient global health VAS over time. The models included random intercepts and random slopes to account for within-patient variability across repeated visits. All models were adjusted for time, age and sex, as well as CRP, SJC and TJC, which were incorporated as time-varying covariates to account for disease activity parameters (CRP, SJC, TJC) at each visit.

Variables used to define GRAPPA-C2M and GRAPPA-TR PsA and EULAR-D2M and EULAR-TR PsA status were based entirely on observed data. No imputation of missing data was performed. All statistical analyses were conducted using Stata V.18.0 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas, USA).

Results

Patients

The DEPAR cohort comprised 934 patients at baseline. After excluding five patients due to missing medication data and 44 patients who participated in the STAMP, a nested randomised controlled trial within the DEPAR cohort, 885 patients with all PsA clinical phenotypes were included in the current analysis (online supplemental figure 1).

In the total eligible DEPAR population (n=885), the mean follow-up duration was 40.4 months (SD 27.2), with a median of 36 months (IQR 18–72). Data were censored at March 2023 and treatment outcomes were assessed within the available follow-up time for each patient (table 2, online supplemental figure 2). The mean age was 49.7 years (SD 13.8) and 51.0% were female (451/885). The median symptom duration at baseline was 10 months (IQR 4–32) and the mean BMI was 28.6 kg/m² (SD 9.7). Enthesitis was present in 8.0% (70/885) and dactylitis in 8.0% (68/885). The median TJC was 3 (IQR 1–7) and the SJC was 2 (IQR 1–5). Mean BSA affected by psoriasis was 3.5% (SD 5.2) and a history of psoriasis was recorded in 90.0% (794/885) of patients. Mean CRP was 8.8 mg/L (SD 15.8). PROs indicated moderate disease burden, with a mean HAQ-DI of 0.8 (SD 0.5) (table 2).

Based on 7-year follow-up data on medication use, patients were categorised into three main treatment groups: 134 patients (15.1%) were treated with NSAIDs only, 431 patients (48.7%) received only csDMARDs, 320 patients (36.2%) were treated with at least one b/tsDMARD. EULAR-D2M and EULAR-TR PsA

EULAR criteria were applied to the same initial group of 133 patients treated with ≥2 b/tsDMARDs. Of these, 49 were excluded because both therapies had the same mechanism of action and 22 due to lack of follow-up data, leaving 62 patients eligible for EULAR-D2M and EULAR-TR assessment. Among these, 37 patients (60%) fulfilled the EULAR-D2M definition, corresponding to 4.2% of the total study population. The stricter EULAR-TR definition was met by 34 patients (55% of those eligible), after excluding six whose treatment failure was not classified as primary or secondary, corresponding to 3.8% of the total population (figure 2, online supplemental figure 1).

GRAPPA-TR PsA

Among the 133 patients treated with ≥2 b/tsDMARDs, 49 were excluded because both therapies had the same mechanism of action. This resulted in 84 patients eligible for GRAPPA-TR assessment based on treatment failure. Of these, 28 were excluded due to lack of follow-up data and after excluding six whose treatment failure was not classified as primary or secondary, leaving 50 patients for full evaluation. In total, 11 patients (20%) fulfilled the GRAPPA-TR definition at consecutive visits, corresponding to 1.2% of the total study population (11/885) (figure 2, online supplemental figure 1).

Within the domain-specific components, TJC ≥3 was present in 81% of GRAPPA-C2M, 56% of EULAR-D2M, 57% of EULAR-TR and 91% of GRAPPA-TR patients who reached criterion (3). Other objective inflammatory manifestations, including enthesitis, dactylitis, skin involvement (PASI >1 or BSA >3%) and swollen joints (SJC ≥1), were also frequently present across groups. Comparison of baseline characteristics

At baseline, female sex was more common in all four groups, with 60% in GRAPPA-C2M, 70% in EULAR-D2M, 71% in EULAR-TR and 73% in GRAPPA-TR, compared with 51% in the total population. Patients fulfilling these definitions also had a higher BMI (≥30), which was more prevalent in stricter groups: 37% in GRAPPA-C2M, 49% in EULAR-D2M, 53% in EULAR-TR and 64% in GRAPPA-TR versus 33% in the overall cohort. Symptom duration was also longer, with median durations of 10 months in GRAPPA-C2M, 15 months in EULAR-D2M, 23 months in EULAR-TR and 16 months in GRAPPA-TR, compared with 10 months in the total population (table 2).

Skin severity (BSA) was also greater in these groups, with mean values of 4.8% (SD 8.3) in GRAPPA-C2M, 5.4% (8.9) in EULAR-D2M, 5.5% (9.2) in EULAR-TR and 4.9% (9.3) in GRAPPA-TR, compared with 3.5% (6.2) in the total population.

The prevalence of enthesitis (LEI >1) was higher, occurring in 22% of GRAPPA-C2M, 31% of EULAR-D2M, 24% of EULAR-TR and 46% of GRAPPA-TR patients, compared with 19% overall. Median LEI scores followed the same pattern: 3 (IQR 2–4) in GRAPPA-C2M, 3 (2–4) in EULAR-D2M, 3 (1–4) in EULAR-TR and 4 (3–4) in GRAPPA-TR, versus 3 (2–4) in the overall cohort.

Discussion

In this large, prospective real-world PsA cohort, we systematically applied the 2025 GRAPPA and EULAR definitions to identify C2M, D2M and TR PsA and to characterise their frequency, clinical features and longitudinal disease burden. This study represents the first longitudinal application of these definitions in a real-world dataset. Our findings demonstrate that, although the strictest TR categories are relatively uncommon, patients fulfilling any D2M definition experience a persistently higher disease burden over time.

Using the definitions,8 9 20% of patients fulfilled the GRAPPA-C2M criteria, while smaller proportions fulfilled the stricter criteria: 4.2% for EULAR-D2M, 3.8% for EULAR-TR and 1.2% for GRAPPA-TR. These estimates are lower than previously reported rates of D2T PsA, which range from 16% to 34% in registry-based studies using proxy definitions.10,1225 This discrepancy likely reflects important methodological differences. Prior studies relied largely on retrospective data and simplified treatment-failure thresholds, whereas the present analysis applied multicomponent consensus definitions incorporating disease perception, persistent symptoms and, for TR disease, objective evidence of ongoing inflammation.

A key finding of this study is the distinct temporal pattern with which the GRAPPA and EULAR definitions were fulfilled. GRAPPA-C2M was characterised by early treatment failure, typically occurring within the first year after baseline, with completion of all criteria by approximately 18 months. In contrast, EULAR-D2M and EULAR-TR, as well as GRAPPA-TR, were fulfilled later, with treatment failure occurring around 2 years and subsequent criteria extending to 3–4 years of follow-up. These findings indicate that GRAPPA-C2M captures disease complexity at an earlier stage, whereas the EULAR-TR and GRAPPA-TR definitions identify smaller subsets of patients with more prolonged and persistent disease trajectories.

This temporal gradient closely reflects the relative stringency of the definitions. In particular, the GRAPPA-TR definition was the most restrictive, largely driven by the requirement that both patient-reported and physician-reported disease perception exceed predefined thresholds. This resulted in substantial attrition at the disease-perception step and highlights how subjective assessments can precede and, in practice, outweigh objective measures of disease activity in determining case identification.

Across all definitions, failure to achieve MDA was the dominant indicator of persistent disease classification. This finding reinforces the central role of MDA as a clinically meaningful composite outcome that captures multidomain disease burden in PsA.2 Domain-specific contributors, particularly tender joint burden, enthesitis and skin involvement, were highly prevalent and often overlapped with objective inflammatory criteria. By contrast, elevated CRP was observed in only a minority of patients compared with other criteria, even in the strictest groups, occurring in fewer than 50% of patients, emphasising that persistent disease burden in PsA is frequently not reflected by systemic inflammatory markers alone.26

Patients fulfilling the definitions shared a consistent baseline profile characterised by female predominance, higher BMI, longer symptom duration and greater skin and entheseal involvement. These findings align with earlier reports linking female sex, obesity and enthesitis to poorer treatment response and higher disease impact in PsA.510,12 25 Notably, these adverse characteristics were already present at diagnosis, suggesting that difficult disease trajectories will be identifiable early in the disease course rather than being solely a consequence of treatment exposure.

Longitudinal analyses demonstrated that patients meeting any of the D2M definitions experienced a persistently higher burden of disability, pain, fatigue and impaired global health throughout follow-up, even after adjustment for time-varying disease activity indicators. These findings indicate these definitions are not merely administrative or treatment-based labels, but represent clinically meaningful states associated with sustained impairment from the patient’s perspective.

Our study has several strengths, including its large sample size, long follow-up and detailed, standardised data collection within a real-world PsA cohort. Moreover, this study also has several limitations. First, we applied the 2025 GRAPPA and EULAR definitions retrospectively; therefore, the classification of C2M, D2M and TR PsA relied on observational data rather than a protocol-driven approach, which may have introduced variability in treatment decisions and outcome reporting. In particular, key components such as the ‘perceived problematic disease state’ had to be approximated using proxy measures, which may not fully capture the intended multidimensional and subjective aspects of disease perception. We were also unable to apply the fifth criterion of the GRAPPA-TR and EULAR-TR definition as originally specified, the exclusion of alternative explanations for systemic inflammation. This limitation particularly affects the EULAR-TR definition, as it may have reduced its intended stringency and resulted in greater apparent overlap between the EULAR-D2M and EULAR-TR groups. The annual scheduling of follow-up visits after the second year may have limited the capture of treatment changes or switches occurring between visits. Despite long-term follow-up, not all patients had complete longitudinal data, particularly for PROs, which could bias our results. We addressed this issue through mixed-effects modelling, but we cannot fully exclude residual bias. Generalisability may be limited, as all patients were enrolled from Dutch clinical centres; treatment patterns and access to b/tsDMARDs may differ in other healthcare settings. Finally, although we adjusted for disease activity over time, we could not fully disentangle inflammatory and non-inflammatory contributors (eg, fibromyalgia, structural damage or mood disorders), which may have influenced classification according to these definitions.

In this longitudinal real-world cohort, the GRAPPA-C2M definition identified a larger and earlier subgroup of patients. In contrast, the EULAR-D2M and EULAR-TR definitions appeared to delineate more advanced disease states, in which patient-reported and physician-reported perceptions were more closely aligned with objective inflammatory activity, reflecting a more balanced integration of subjective and objective disease domains, consistent with truly D2M or refractory PsA. The GRAPPA-TR definition was the most restrictive, largely driven by the dominance of disease-perception criteria, which may limit its ability to fully capture biological treatment refractoriness in some patients.

Although these definitions provide a structured framework to characterise D2M PsA, their application in routine practice remains challenging due to the need for detailed and concurrently available clinical, patient-reported and treatment data, reflecting the heterogeneous and multidomain nature of PsA. Further refinement and simplification of these criteria may improve the feasibility and consistency of identification in real-world settings, thereby supporting earlier recognition and more tailored management of patients at risk of complex or TR disease.
