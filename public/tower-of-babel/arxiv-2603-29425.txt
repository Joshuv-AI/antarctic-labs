Poincaré duality spaces related to the Joker Thanks: Thanks to Diarmuid Crowley and Csaba Nagy for helpful discussions. Don Davis has also recently drawn attention to the homogeneous space discussed in Section 5 .
Andrew Baker

Abstract
. The well known Joker 𝒜 ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathcal{A}(1) -module of Adams & Priddy is known to be realisable as the cohomology of a 1 1 -connected space. By attaching an extra cell we obtain an 8 8 -dimensional Poincaré duality space whose mod 2 2 cohomology realising is an unstable 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -algebra. We use obstruction theory to show that this admits a PL \mathrm{PL} -structure. Although we are unable to show it is smoothable, it turns out that the cohomology can be realised as that of a homogeneous space.

Introduction

In this note we investigate an
8
-dimensional
Poincaré duality space whose mod
2
cohomology
as a Steenrod module algebra is related to the
Joker module of Adams & Priddy
[
1
]
.
It turns out that such a PD space always admits
a
\mathrm{PL}
-structure and its cohomology can be
realised as that of a homogeneous space although
it is unclear if this has the same homotopy type.

Some history:
The author was led
to this example by serendipity. While reading
about Poincaré duality in Larry Smith’s
book
[
12
]
, he
looked for familiar examples and the
natural symmetry of the Joker module
suggested it might be part of one and
this fitted well with earlier work. The
difficulty of passing from a
\mathrm{PL}
to a
smooth structure was overcome when Csaba
Nagy pointed out some old calculations
of Borel and Hirzebruch. We have decided
to make this work available since it
seems to provide an interesting example
of surgery theory in action building on
some known homotopy theory.

Notation, etc:
We will denote
the unreduced cohomology of a space
X

by
H^{*}(X)
, and also write
H^{*}(Y)

for the cohomology of a spectrum. These
notations are of course inconsistent:
for a space with disjoint basepoint

X_{+}
,

H^{*}(X)\cong H^{*}(\Sigma^{\infty}(X_{+})),

however the context is usually sufficient
to make clear what is meant in practise.

1.
Poincaré duality algebras over
the Steenrod algebra

This section is based on work of Adams and
Smith
[
2
,
13
]
.

In this section we give some algebraic
results on Poincaré duality algebras
over the mod
2
Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}
.
We will refer to such an algebra as a

Steenrod Poincaré duality algebra

(SPDA). Although we work exclusively in
characteristic
2
, of course similar
results hold for other primes.

We recall that for a left
\mathcal{A}
-module

M=M^{*}
its dual
M_{*}
(with grading
defined by
M_{n}=\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(M^{n},\mathbb{F}_{2})

which we view as cohomological degree

-n
) becomes a right
\mathcal{A}
-module
so that for
x\in M
,

(f\cdot\Sq^{r})(x)=f(\Sq^{r}x).

This means that the duality pairing

M_{*}\otimes M^{*}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
factors through

M_{*}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}M^{*}
. Using the antipode

\chi
of
\mathcal{A}
can also view
M_{*}
as
a left
\mathcal{A}
-module by setting

\Sq^{r}\cdot f=f\cdot(\chi\Sq^{r}).

If
P^{*}
is an algebra then there is
a pairing

\cap\colon P_{*}\otimes P^{*}\to P_{*};\quad f\cap a=f(a(-))

making
P_{*}
a right
P^{*}
-module and
also a left
P^{*}
-module since
P^{*}
is
commutative. Taking the diagonal left

\mathcal{A}
-module structure this is an

\mathcal{A}
-module homomorphism.

One way to handle the interaction of
the
P
and
\mathcal{A}
module structures
is by introducing the cross product
algebra
P\sharp\mathcal{A}
which is the
vector space
P\otimes\mathcal{A}
equipped
with the multiplication that is given
on basic tensors by

(a\otimes\alpha)(b\otimes\beta)=\sum_{i}a(\alpha^{\prime}_{i}b)\otimes\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{i}\beta,

where the coproduct on
\alpha
is

\sum_{i}\alpha^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{i}
.
We usually denote the element

a\otimes\alpha
by
a\alpha
so the
above formula becomes

(1.1)

(a\alpha)(b\beta)=\sum_{i}a(\alpha^{\prime}_{i}b)\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{i}\beta,

It is also useful to record the easily
proved formula

(1.2)

a\alpha=\sum_{i}(1\alpha^{\prime}_{i})(\chi\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{i}a).

Then
P^{*}
and
P_{*}
are left
P\sharp\mathcal{A}
-modules
where for
x\in P^{*}
and
f\in P_{*}
,

(a\alpha)\cdot x=a\alpha(x),\quad(a\alpha)\cdot f=a\cdot(\alpha\cdot f).

Definition 1.1
.

A
Steenrod Poincaré duality algebra
of degree
d
is a finite type graded
connected unstable commutative
\mathcal{A}
-algebra

P=P^{*}
with the following properties.

•

P
is a Poincaré duality algebra of
degree
d
: there is an element
[P]\in P_{d}

such that
[P]\cap\colon P^{*}\to P_{d-*}
is
an isomorphism of
P^{*}
-modules.

•

[P]\cap
is an isomorphism of left

P\sharp\mathcal{A}
-modules.

Since
[P]\cap
is an isomorphism of

P^{*}
-modules, it follows that
P_{*}
is
a free
P^{*}
-module with basis
[P]
.

Given such an SPDA
P
we can define
some useful sequences of elements.

Definition 1.2
.

The following sequences in
P^{*}
are
defined.

•

The
Wu classes
:
v_{k}\in P^{k}
is
the unique element for which

(\chi\Sq^{k})\cdot[P]=[P]\cdot\Sq^{k}=[P]\cap v_{k}.

•

The
Stiefel-Whitney classes
:

w_{k}\in P^{k}
is the unique element for
which

w_{k}=\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}\Sq^{i}v_{k-i}.

•

The
dual Stiefel-Whitney classes
:

\overline{w}_{k}\in P^{k}
is the unique element
for which

\Sq^{k}\cdot[P]=[P]\cdot\chi\Sq^{k}=[P]\cap\overline{w}_{k}.

We will refer to these elements as the

characteristic classes
of the
SPDA.

Clearly we have
v_{0}=w_{0}=\overline{w}_{0}=1
.

Proposition 1.3
.

The characteristic classes of the SPDA

P^{*}
have the following properties.

(a)

If
k>\lfloor d/2\rfloor
then
v_{k}=0
.

(b)

If
d
is even then
w_{d}=v_{d/2}^{2}
.

(c)

For
1\leqslant k\leqslant d
the Stiefel-Whitney
classes satisfy

\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}w_{i}\overline{w}_{k-i}=0.

Proof.

(a) This follows from the unstable algebra
condition.

(b) By definition and the unstable algebra
condition,

w_{d}=\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d/2}\Sq^{d-i}v_{i}=\Sq^{d/2}v_{d/2}=v_{d/2}^{2}.

(c) For
1\leqslant k\leqslant d
,

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}w_{i}\overline{w}_{k-i}\cdot[P]

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant k}w_{i}\Sq^{k-i}\cdot[P]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant i\leqslant k\\
0\leqslant j\leqslant i\end{subarray}}(\Sq^{j}v_{i-j})\Sq^{k-i}\cdot[P]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant r\leqslant k\\
0\leqslant j\leqslant k-r\end{subarray}}(\Sq^{j}v_{r})\Sq^{k-r-j}\cdot[P]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant r\leqslant k\\
0\leqslant j\leqslant k-r\end{subarray}}(\Sq^{j}v_{r})\Sq^{k-r-j}\cdot[P]

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant r\leqslant k}\Sq^{k-r}\cdot(v_{r}\cdot[P])

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant r\leqslant k}\Sq^{k-r}\cdot(\chi\Sq^{r}\cdot[P])

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{0\leqslant r\leqslant k}\Sq^{k-r}\chi\Sq^{r}\bigr)\cdot[P]

\displaystyle=0.\qed

Remark 1.4
.

In general, calculation of the universal
Wu classes for a manifold in terms of the
universal Stiefel-Whitney classes can be
non-trivial because of the involvement
of the Steenrod algebra antipode. An
alternative approach due to Atiyah &
Hirzebruch
[
3
]

involves the Todd polynomials; see Hirzebruch
et al
[
9
]
*section 8.1.

We record another useful result.

Proposition 1.5
.

Suppose that
P,Q
are two Poincaré duality
algebras of degree
d
and that
h\colon P\to Q

is an algebra homomorphism restricting to
an isomorphism
h\colon P^{d}\xrightarrow{\cong}Q^{d}
.
Then
h
is injective.

Proof.

The adjoint map
h_{*}\colon Q_{*}\to P_{*}
gives an
isomorphism
h_{*}\colon Q_{d}\to P_{d}
so we might
as well assume that
h_{*}[Q]=[P]
where

h_{*}[Q]=[Q]\circ h
. If
h\in P
satisfies

h(x)=0
then

[P]\cap x=[Q]\circ h(x)=0.

By freeness of the
P
-module
P_{*}
, this
gives
x=0
.
∎
