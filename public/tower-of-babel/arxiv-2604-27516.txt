Barriers, Barenblatt solutions and regularity of soda can domains for the heat equation and nonlinear p p\mspace{1mu} -parabolic equations
Anders Björn

Abstract




Abstract
.
In this paper we study when the
origin
(0,0)
is a regular (or
irregular) boundary point for the
so-called soda can domains of the type

\Theta_{l,\theta}:=\{(x,t)\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}:0<-t<\theta|x|^{l}<\theta\},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{with $l,\theta>0$,}

for the
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic equation

\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{p}u=0
, where
1<p<\infty
.
For
p<2n/{(n+1)}
and for the heat equation (i.e.
p=2
) we completely
determine when the origin is regular for soda can domains.
The domains
\Theta_{l,\theta}
have
nonconvex
time sections
with power dependence on time.
For domains
with rotationally symmetric convex time sections with power dependence on time,
the regularity of the origin as the last point was
characterized by Petrovskiĭ (in 1935) for the heat equation,
and almost completely in the nonlinear case (
p\neq 2
)
in our earlier paper (joint with Gianazza,

Math. Ann.

368
(2017), 885–904).

Key words and phrases
:
barrier,
boundary regularity,
heat equation,
Perron method,

p\mspace{1mu}
-Laplacian,

p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic equation,
soda can domain.

Soda cans

In 1935, Petrovskiĭ
[
31
]
proved the following result
about regularity of the last point.

Petrovskiĭ’s criterion
.

The origin
(0,0)
is regular for the heat equation

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=0

with respect to the domain

\{(x,t)\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\times(-1,0):|x|^{2}<-Kt\log|{\log(-t)}|\}

if and only if
0<K\leq 4
.

A boundary point
\xi_{0}
is
regular

if for every continuous boundary data, the corresponding
solution to the Dirichlet problem
attains the boundary data as a limit at
\xi_{0}
,
see Definition
3.1
.

In particular, Petrovskiĭ’s criterion implies that
(0,0)
is regular for the domain

\{(x,t)\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\times(-1,0):-t>\theta|x|^{l}\},\quad\text{with $l,\theta>0$,}

(1.1)

if and only if
0<l\leq 2
.

In this paper we consider the

-parabolic equation

\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{p}u:=\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\Div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\quad p>1,

(1.2)

.
It is a nonlinear generalization of the usual heat equation.
The heat equation (i.e.
p=2
) is also included in our study.

For the
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic equation a natural counterpart of
the Petrovskiĭ criterion is to study regularity of the origin for
the rotationally symmetric domains (
1.1
).
Regularity in this case
was almost completely (except for the case
1<l=p<2
)
characterized
in our paper
[

(joint with Gianazza).
Lindqvist
[
28
]

and Björn–Björn–Gianazza–Parviainen
[
9

contain
some earlier results in this direction.
Incidentally,
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
also shows that a
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic
analogue of Effros–Kazdan’s tusk condition
[
15
]

is not sufficient for regularity when
p>2
.
Moreover, unlike for
p=2
, regularity
for the origin
in (
1.1
) holds if and only if

0<l<p
when
p>2
.

In this paper we instead consider the complementary
domains of the form

\Theta_{l,\theta}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}:0<-t<\theta|x|^{l}<\theta\},\quad\text{with $l,\theta>0$.}

(1.3)

As in
[
9
]
, we call such domains
soda cans
as they (for

)
resemble the concave inside of the bottom
of a soda (or beer) can.
Just like the Petrovskiĭ set (
1.1
),
the soda can domains (
1.3
)
are rotationally symmetric domains of power type
(but with nonconvex time sections, namely annuli with shrinking
inner radii so that they converge to a punctured ball).
Despite this, they
do not seem to have been studied much.

In contrast to Petrovskiĭ’s criterion for the heat equation,
it was shown in
Björn–Björn–Gianazza
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]

that boundary regularity of the origin is independent of
radial scaling in the
x
-direction
(or equivalently in the

-direction) when
p\neq 2
.
In particular, the regularity
for
\Theta_{l,\theta}

(and for the Petrovskiĭ set (
1.1
))
is independent of
\theta

when
p\neq 2
, see Corollary
3.7
.

Note that since regularity is independent of the future
(see Theorem
3.4
),
it is natural to consider regularity
of the origin with respect to domains (or open sets) in
the lower half-space.
Since regularity is also a local property (see Section
3
),
the lower bound
t>-\theta

(or the upper bound
|x|<1
)
is inessential.

In the positive direction, we obtained
the following result
in

]
when

Neither the construction in

nor its modification in
[

, Proposition 4.1]

gives a barrier family
when the exterior ball touches the boundary point from the past

For

, it follows from the tusk condition
of Effros–Kazdan

that
the north pole exterior ball condition implies regularity.

The soda can domains, considered in this paper, thus

For the heat equation (

In particular, any power-type cusp from the past is sufficient

is sufficient for regularity when

.

In the negative direction, the following result shows

.

-parabolic equation,

.

For

We will use the notation and several results from
Björn–Björn–Gianazza–Parviainen

Here we will be brief and only introduce and discuss what we really need,

From now on we will always assume that

\Theta\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
is a nonempty bounded open set
and
1<p<\infty
.

Let
U\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set.
The
parabolic Sobolev space

L^{p}(t_{1},t_{2};W^{1,p}(U))
,
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
, is the space of functions
u(x,t)
such that the mapping
x\mapsto u(x,t)
belongs to
W^{1,p}(U)
for almost every
t_{1}<t<t_{2}
and the norm

\biggl(\int_{t_{1}\kern 0.0pt\kern 4.09723pt}^{t_{2}\kern-6.92368pt}\kern-5.0pt\int_{U}(|u(x,t)|^{p}+|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p})\,dx\,dt\biggr)^{1/p}

is finite.
Analogously, by the space
C([t_{1},t_{2}];L^{p}(U))
,
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
, we mean the space of functions
u(x,t)
, such that the mapping

t\mapsto\int_{U}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx
is continuous in the time interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
.
(The gradient
\nabla
is always taken with
respect to the
x
-variables in this paper.)

Definition 2.1

Definition 3.1

Definition 3.2

Let

-superparabolic function

A

Theorem 3.3
.

(
[

A boundary point

(

A boundary point

if and only if either

is regular with respect to

Remark 3.5
.

In classical potential theory,
a

is a superharmonic (when dealing with the

(when dealing with the heat equation)

satisfying (

Existence of a single

The same is true for

-harmonic functions, i.e. solutions of

-Laplace equation

In fact, in these cases,

[

On the other hand, for the

-parabolic equation with

Lemma 4.1

Assume that
u,v\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
and
let
(
by abuse of notation
)

u(x)=u(|x|)
and
v(x)=v(|x|)
for
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then, for
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,

\nabla u(x)=u^{\prime}(|x|)\frac{x}{|x|}\quad\text{and}\quad\Div\biggl(v(x)\frac{x}{|x|}\biggr)=v^{\prime}(|x|)+v(x)\frac{n-1}{|x|}.

(4.4)

If moreover,
u\in C^{2}((0,\infty))
and
u^{\prime}\geq 0
, then

\Delta_{p}(u(x))=u^{\prime}(|x|)^{p-2}\biggl((p-1)u^{\prime\prime}(|x|)+u^{\prime}(|x|)\frac{n-1}{|x|}\biggr).

(4.5)

The formulas (
4.4
) are clear by direct calculation.
Thus, for (
4.5
), we have with
v(x)=u^{\prime}(|x|)^{p-1}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}(u(x))

\displaystyle=\Div\biggl(u^{\prime}(|x|)^{p-1}\frac{x}{|x|}\biggr)=(p-1)u^{\prime}(|x|)^{p-2}u^{\prime\prime}(|x|)+u^{\prime}(|x|)^{p-1}\frac{n-1}{|x|}

\displaystyle=u^{\prime}(|x|)^{p-2}\biggl((p-1)u^{\prime\prime}(|x|)+u^{\prime}(|x|)\frac{n-1}{|x|}\biggr).\qed

Note that

\Theta_{l,\theta}\subset\widetilde{\Theta}:=\{x:0<|x|<1\}\times(-\theta,0)=:G\times(-\theta,0),

where
\widetilde{\Theta}
is a (punctured) cylinder and
G
is a punctured ball.
Therefore, by the comments after Theorem
3.3
,
it suffices to show that the origin is regular for
\widetilde{\Theta}
.
We shall construct a barrier family
\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
\widetilde{\Theta}

at
\xi_{0}=(0,0)
.
Fix a positive integer
j
.

First, we aim to find a
radially symmetric function
u_{j}\in C^{2}((0,\infty))

such that, with
u_{j}(x)=u_{j}(|x|)
(by abuse of notation),

\Delta_{p}u_{j}=-j\text{ in }G,\quad u_{j}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad u_{j}(1)=a_{j},

where
a_{j}\geq j
will be chosen later.
By Lemma
4.1
and assuming that
u_{j}^{\prime}\geq 0
,

\Delta_{p}u_{j}=(u_{j}^{\prime})^{p-2}\biggl((p-1)u_{j}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{n-1}{r}u_{j}^{\prime}\biggr),

where the radial derivatives are with respect to
r=|x|
.
Letting
v_{j}=u_{j}^{\prime}
, we therefore want to solve the equation

v_{j}^{p-2}\Bigl((p-1)v_{j}^{\prime}+\frac{n-1}{r}v_{j}\Bigr)=-j.

(4.6)

With
z_{j}:=r^{(n-1)/(p-1)}v_{j}
, the left-hand side can be rewritten as

(p-1)(r^{(n-1)/(p-1)}v_{j})^{p-2}\frac{(r^{(n-1)/(p-1)}v_{j})^{\prime}}{r^{n-1}}=\frac{(z_{j}^{p-1})^{\prime}}{r^{n-1}}

and so (
4.6
) becomes
(z_{j}^{p-1})^{\prime}=-jr^{n-1}
.
We are interested in
z_{j}\geq 0
. Hence

z_{j}=\biggl(C_{j}-\frac{jr^{n}}{n}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)},\quad\text{where}\quad C_{j}\geq\frac{j}{n}.

It follows that

u_{j}^{\prime}=v_{j}=r^{(1-n)/(p-1)}z_{j}=\biggl(C_{j}r^{1-n}-\frac{jr}{n}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}.

Since we require
u_{j}(0)=0
and
u_{j}(1)>0
,
this yields a general radially symmetric solution

u_{j}(x)=u_{j}(|x|)=\int_{0}^{|x|}\biggl(C_{j}\rho^{1-n}-\frac{j\rho}{n}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,d\rho,

where
C_{j}\geq j/n

should be chosen so that it gives the correct value
u_{j}(1)=a_{j}
.
This is possible provided that

a_{j}\geq a_{j}^{\prime}:=\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{j\rho^{1-n}}{n}-\frac{j\rho}{n}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\,d\rho.

Note that
a_{j}^{\prime}<\infty
since
p>n
.

We let
a_{j}=\max\{j,a_{j}^{\prime}\}
and choose
C_{j}
accordingly.
Then
u_{j}
is a
p\mspace{1mu}
-supersolution in
G
(i.e.
\Delta_{p}u_{j}\leq 0
), while

\varphi_{j}(x):=j|x|

is a
p\mspace{1mu}
-subsolution in
G
by Lemma
2.6

or
4.1
.
Note that
u_{j}(0)=\varphi_{j}(0)=0

and
u_{j}(1)=a_{j}\geq\varphi_{j}(1)
.
When
n\geq 2
, it follows from the comparison Lemma 3.18 in
Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
18
]
(and continuity)
that
u_{j}\geq\varphi_{j}
in
G
.
The case
n=1
is not considered in
[
18
]
,
but then

u_{j}
is concave on
(0,1)
while
\varphi_{j}
is convex,
so
u_{j}\geq\varphi_{j}
in
G
also in this case.
Next define

w_{j}(x,t)=u_{j}(x)-jt\geq 0\quad\text{in }\widetilde{\Theta}.

Then

\Delta_{p}w_{j}=-j=\partial_{t}w_{j}\quad\text{in }\widetilde{\Theta},

and
w_{j}
is therefore
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic in
\widetilde{\Theta}
.
It follows that
\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

is a barrier family, and thus
\xi_{0}
is regular with respect to
\widetilde{\Theta}
,
by Theorem
3.3
.
∎

5
The heat equation and Proposition
1.5

In order to prove Proposition
1.5
,
we will use the
Wiener criterion from
Lanconelli
[
24
, Corollario 1.2, with
\lambda=\tfrac{1}{2}
]
and
Evans–Gariepy
[
16
, Theorem 1, (1.11) with
\lambda=2
]
.
It shows that
the origin is regular for the heat equation in

\Theta
, with
(0,0)\in\partial\Theta
,
if and only if

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\capp\bigl({\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{A_{k}\setminus A_{k+1}}}}\setminus\Theta\bigr)=\infty,

(5.1)

where
\capp
denotes the thermal (or parabolic) capacity
(see (
5.2
) below),

A_{k}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,0):F(x,-t)\geq 2^{k}\},

and

F(x,t)=\biggl(\frac{1}{4\pi t}\biggr)^{n/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/4t},\quad t>0,

is the fundamental solution for the heat equation.
Lanconelli
[
24
]
showed the necessity of the Wiener criterion
(i.e. that a point is irregular if the
sum in (
5.1
) converges).
The sufficiency was later shown by Evans and Gariepy
[
16
]
.

The thermal capacity
\capp

is defined by means of parabolic potentials.
Namely, for a compact set
K
,

\capp(K)=\sup\mu(K),

(5.2)

where the supremum is taken over all measures
\mu
supported on
K

with

P^{\mu}(x_{0},t_{0}):=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,t_{0})}\frac{e^{-|x-x_{0}|^{2}/4(t_{0}-t)}}{(4\pi(t_{0}-t))^{n/2}}\,d\mu(x,t)\leq 1

(5.3)

for all
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
.

The following estimates for capacities of cylinders will
be crucial when applying the Wiener criterion.
Below, we write
B(0,r)=\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:|x|<r\}
.
We also write
a\lesssim b
and
b\gtrsim a

if there is an implicit comparison constant
C>0
such that
a\leq Cb
,
and
a\simeq b
if
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Consider the parabolic cylinder

C_{r,h}={\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle B(0,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(0,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle B(0,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(0,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle B(0,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(0,r)}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle B(0,r)}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{B(0,r)}}}\times[0,h]
.
Then

\displaystyle\capp(C_{r,h})

\displaystyle\simeq r^{n},

\displaystyle\text{if }0\leq h\leq r^{2},

(5.4)

\displaystyle\capp(C_{r,h})

\displaystyle\simeq hr^{n-2},

\displaystyle\text{if }h\geq r^{2}\text{ and }n\geq 3,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\frac{h}{1+\log(h/r^{2})}\lesssim\capp(C_{r,h})

\displaystyle\lesssim h,

\displaystyle\text{if }h\geq r^{2}\text{ and }n=2.

(5.6)

The formula (
5.4
) was shown by
Lanconelli
[
24
, Proposizione 5.1]
.
Note that the upper bound in (
5.5
) is better
than the upper bound in
[
24
, Proposizione 6.1]
when
n\geq 3
.
In Avelin–Kuusi–Parviainen
[
3
, Theorem 4.9 and
Corollary 2]
,
the formulas (
5.4
)–(
5.5
)
were obtained
for the corresponding nonlinear
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic capacity when
2\leq p<n
, including
the case
p=2
.
Rather than letting the reader study this sophisticated nonlinear paper, we give a
more elementary proof only covering
p=2
, which is enough for our purposes.

It is well known and easy to see
(cf.
[
24
, Proposizione 4.1(ii)]
)
that

\capp(C_{r,r^{2}})\simeq r^{n}.

On the other hand,
by
[
24
, Proposizione 5.1]
(or Watson
[
34
, Theorem 9]
or

[
35
, Theorem 7.55]
),

\capp(C_{r,0})\simeq r^{n}.

Since
\capp
is a monotone set function, this shows (
5.4
).
(It follows from
[
34
, Lemma 4]
or

[
35
, Lemmas 7.37 and 7.54]
that the thermal
capacity in
[
34
]
and
[
35
]
(with
E=\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
)
coincides with the one defined in (
5.2
).)

Next, the upper bounds in (
5.5
)
and (
5.6
) follow
easily from the subadditivity of
\capp

(
[
24
, Proposizione 1.1(ii)]
or
[
35
, Theorem 7.44]
) as follows,

\capp(C_{r,h})\lesssim\frac{h}{r^{2}}\capp(C_{r,r^{2}})\simeq\frac{h}{r^{2}}r^{n}=hr^{n-2}\quad\text{when }h\geq r^{2}.

For the lower bounds,
assume that
h\geq r^{2}
and

n\geq 2
.
Let
m_{c}
be the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure restricted to
K_{c}:=B(0,r)\times\{c\}
.
By
[
24
, Proposizione 5.1]
(or
[
34
, Theorem 9]
or

[
35
, Theorem 7.55]
with
(a,b)=\mathbf{R}
),

is the thermal capacitary distribution for
K_{c}
and thus

P^{m_{c}}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq 1\quad\text{for all }(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}\times(c,\infty)

and
P^{m_{c}}(x_{0},t_{0})=0
for

.
Moreover, for
t_{0}\in[c+kr^{2},c+(k+1)r^{2}]
with

and all

P^{m_{c}}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq P^{m_{c}}(0,t_{0})\leq(4\pi kr^{2})^{-n/2}\int_{B(0,r)}e^{-|x|^{2}/4(k+1)r^{2}}\,dx.

The last integral is easily estimated using polar coordinates
and the elementary inequality
e^{x}\geq 1+x
as

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r)}e^{-|x|^{2}/4(k+1)r^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{n-2}\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\rho^{2}/4(k+1)r^{2}}\,\rho\,d\rho

\displaystyle=2(k+1)r^{n}(1-e^{-1/(k+1)})\lesssim r^{n}.

It thus follows that for the measure

\mu:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq jr^{2}\leq h\\
j\in\mathbf{Z}\end{subarray}}m_{jr^{2}},

obtained by placing copies

n

in

,

where

.

For

, the sum has an upper bound
independent of

and hence a multiple of

.
It follows that

\capp(C_{r,h})\gtrsim\mu(C_{r,h})\simeq kr^{n}\simeq hr^{n-2},

which proves (

).

Finally, for

which proves (

Lemma 6.1
.

If

-parabolic barrier for

at

Lemma
2.6

is
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic in

For

Barriers from the Barenblatt solution

The so-called

[
5
]

of the

-parabolic equation (

is for

and
\lambda:=n(p-2)+p

defined by

\displaystyle B_{p}(x,t)=B_{p,C}(x,t)

\displaystyle=t^{-n/\lambda}\biggl[C-\frac{p-2}{p\lambda^{1/(p-1)}}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{t^{1/\lambda}}\biggr)^{p/(p-1)}\biggr]_{+}^{(p-1)/(p-2)}

\displaystyle=\biggl[Ct^{\frac{n(2-p)}{\lambda(p-1)}}-\frac{p-2}{p\lambda^{1/(p-1)}}\frac{|x|^{p/(p-1)}}{t^{1/(p-1)}}\biggr]_{+}^{(p-1)/(p-2)},

The Barenblatt solution

(with

\widetilde{w}_{\varepsilon}
for
\Theta_{\theta,l}

),
we have from (
7.6
) that

\frac{|x|}{t_{\varepsilon}^{1/\lambda}}\leq\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{\bigl(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{1/\alpha\lambda}\bigr)^{l-\frac{p}{p-1}}}=:\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}\leq\biggl(\frac{(p-1)\delta_{0}}{M(1-\varepsilon)(p-2)}\biggr)^{1-1/p},

(7.14)

because by (
7.5
) and the assumption
l<\lambda
,

\gamma:=1-\frac{l-p/(p-1)}{\alpha\lambda}=\frac{\lambda-p/(p-1)-l+p/(p-1)}{\alpha\lambda}=\frac{\lambda-l}{\alpha\lambda}>0.

Since also (
7.7
) holds, we conclude that
(
7.12
) and thus
(
7.13
) hold, provided that

\delta_{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small.

If we also choose
\delta_{0}
small enough, then by
(
7.14
),

\biggl(\frac{|x|}{t_{\varepsilon}^{1/\lambda}}\biggr)^{p/(p-1)}\leq\frac{1}{M(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}

and thus (
7.13
) holds without taking
the positive part in the last line, i.e.

B_{p}(\xi)\leq t_{\varepsilon}^{-n/\lambda}\biggl(1-M(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{t_{\varepsilon}^{1/\lambda}}\biggr)^{p/(p-1)}\biggr).

(7.15)

Next, the function

w_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=t_{\varepsilon}^{-n/\lambda}-B_{p}(x,t)=B_{p}(0,t_{\varepsilon})-B_{p}(x,t)

is
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic in
\Theta_{\varepsilon}

and thus
p\mspace{1mu}
-superparabolic therein.
By the continuity of
B_{p}
, we have

\lim_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}\ni\zeta\to(0,t_{\varepsilon})}w_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)=w_{\varepsilon}(0,t_{\varepsilon})=0,

(7.16)

so the first requirement in the definition (
3.1
) of
a barrier holds.
On the other hand, from (
7.15
)
we conclude that
for all
\xi=(x,t)\in\overline{\Theta}_{\varepsilon}
with
x\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}\ni\zeta\to\xi}w_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=w_{\varepsilon}(\xi)=t_{\varepsilon}^{-n/\lambda}-B_{p}(\xi)

\displaystyle\geq t_{\varepsilon}^{-n/\lambda}\biggl(1-\biggl(1-M(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{t_{\varepsilon}^{1/\lambda}}\biggr)^{p/{p-1)}}\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=M(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{|x|^{p/(p-1)}}{t_{\varepsilon}^{(\lambda+n)/\lambda(p-1)}}>0.

(7.17)

This proves (
7.3
) and
concludes the construction of the barrier
w_{\varepsilon}
.

Finally, for
|x|=\delta_{\varepsilon}
and
t_{\varepsilon}=\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(l-p/(p-1))/\alpha}
,
it follows from
(
7.17
) that

w_{\varepsilon}(x,t_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{M(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{p/(p-1)}}{\bigl(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(l-p/(p-1))/\alpha}\bigr)^{(\lambda+n)/\lambda(p-1)}}=M(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\tau},

where, by (
7.5
),

\tau:=\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{(l-p/(p-1))(\lambda+n)}{\alpha\lambda(p-1)}=\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{l-p/(p-1)}{p-2}=\frac{p-l}{p-2}.\qed

The following corollary and its proof show how Lemma
7.1
can be used
to obtain a barrier family for
\Theta_{l,\theta}
when
l>p>2
.
This gives a different proof for a part of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 7.2
.

If
l>p>2
then
(0,0)
is regular with respect to
\Theta_{l,\theta}

for all
\theta>0
.

It suffices to consider
l<\lambda
,
since regularity with respect to a larger domain implies regularity
with respect to a smaller one,
see the comments after Theorem
3.3
.
Let
\theta
,
M
,
w_{\varepsilon}
and
\delta_{\varepsilon}
be as in Lemma
7.1

and let
\tau=(p-l)/(p-2)
.
The pasting Lemma
2.4
, together with the
estimate (
7.4
), then implies that the function,
defined for
(x,t)\in\Theta_{l,\theta}
by

\widetilde{w}_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\begin{cases}\min\{w_{\varepsilon}(x,t+t_{\varepsilon}),M(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\tau}\},&\text{if }|x|<\delta_{\varepsilon},\\
M(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\tau},&\text{if }|x|\geq\delta_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

(7.18)

is
p\mspace{1mu}
-superparabolic in
\Theta_{l,\theta}
.
We will now fix
0<\varepsilon<1
in Lemma
7.1
,
but will vary
\delta_{\varepsilon}
.
Since
\tau<0
in (
7.18
) and
l(p-1)-p>0
, the choice

\delta_{\varepsilon}=\delta_{\varepsilon,j}=1/j\quad\text{and}\quad t_{\varepsilon}=t_{\varepsilon,j}=(1/j)^{\frac{\lambda(l(p-1)-p)}{(p-2)(\lambda+n)}}

in Lemma
7.1

gives, for sufficiently large
j
, a barrier family at
(0,0)

for
\Theta_{l,\theta}
with this particular choice of
\theta
.
Lemma
3.6
then guarantees a barrier family
for all
\theta>0
.
Hence
(0,0)
is regular with respect to
\Theta_{l,\theta}
for all
\theta>0
,
by Theorem
3.3
.
∎

We are now ready to give our second proof of
Proposition
1.6
.

If
l>p>2
, then the full regularity follows
directly from Corollary
7.2
,
whose proof also provides a Barenblatt type barrier family.
Assume therefore that
p/(p-1)\leq l\leq p
.

Let

\theta>0
and
0<\varepsilon<\delta\leq 1
be arbitrary and set

\theta_{0}=\frac{\lambda^{(p-2)/(p-1)}}{np}.

Let
\delta_{\varepsilon}>0
,
t_{\varepsilon}=\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\lambda(l(p-1)-p)}{(p-2)(\lambda+n)}}
,

M=\frac{(p-2)}{p\lambda^{1/(p-1)}}\quad\text{and}\quad w_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=t_{\varepsilon}^{-n/\lambda}-B_{p}(x,t)

be as in Lemma
7.1
.
Then
w_{\varepsilon}
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic in the set

\{(x,t):t_{\varepsilon}-\theta_{0}|x|^{l}<t<t_{\varepsilon}\text{ and }|x|<\delta_{\varepsilon}\},

by Lemma
7.1
.
For

a=\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{\delta},\quad b=\frac{\theta_{0}}{\theta}a^{l}\quad\text{and}\quad A=\biggl(\frac{b}{a^{p}}\biggr)^{1/(p-2)}=\biggl(\frac{\theta_{0}}{\theta}\biggl(\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{\delta}\biggr)^{l-p}\biggr)^{1/(p-2)},

let

u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=Aw_{\varepsilon}(ax,bt+t_{\varepsilon}).

(7.19)

Then by the scaling Lemma
3.6
,

u_{\delta}
is
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic in
the restricted soda can domain
\{(x,t)\in\Theta_{l,\theta}:|x|<\delta\}
.
Moreover, by (
7.1
), (
7.3
) and noting that

\frac{l-p}{p-2}+\frac{p}{p-1}=\frac{l-p/(p-1)}{p-2},

we have
for
(x,t)\in\overline{\Theta}_{l,\theta}
with
|x|\leq\delta
,

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)

\displaystyle\geq\biggl(\frac{\theta_{0}}{\theta}\biggl(\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{\delta}\biggr)^{l-p}\biggr)^{1/(p-2)}\frac{M(1-\varepsilon)^{2}(a|x|)^{p/(p-1)}}{\Bigl(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\lambda(l(p-1)-p)}{(p-2)(\lambda+n)}}\Bigr)^{(\lambda+n)/\lambda(p-1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\biggl(\frac{\lambda^{(p-2)/(p-1)}}{np\theta}\biggr)^{1/(p-2)}\frac{(p-2)(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}{p\lambda^{1/(p-1)}}\biggl(\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}}{\delta}\biggr)^{\frac{l-p}{p-2}+\frac{p}{p-1}}}{\delta_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{l-p/(p-1)}{p-2}}}|x|^{p/(p-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-2)(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}{p}\biggl(\frac{\delta^{\frac{p}{p-1}-l}}{np\theta}\biggr)^{1/(p-2)}|x|^{p/(p-1)},

(7.20)

which, up to the factor
(1-\varepsilon)^{2}
,
gives the same coefficient in front of
|x|^{p/(p-1)}

as in (
1.4
).
In particular, with

M_{\theta}=\frac{p-2}{p}\biggl(\frac{1}{np\theta}\biggr)^{1/(p-2)}

as in (
1.6
) and

\tau=(p-l)/(p-2)\geq 0
, we get as in (
6.2
) that

u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\geq M_{\theta}(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta^{\tau},\quad\text{when }|x|=\delta.

The pasting Lemma
2.4
then shows that the function

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}(x,t):=\begin{cases}\min\{u_{\varepsilon}(x,t),M_{\theta}(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta^{\tau}\},&\text{if }(x,t)\in\Theta_{l,\theta}\text{ and }|x|<\delta,\\
M_{\theta}(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\delta^{\tau},&\text{if }(x,t)\in\Theta_{l,\theta}\text{ and }|x|\geq\delta,\end{cases}

(7.21)

is
p\mspace{1mu}
-superparabolic in
\Theta_{l,\theta}
and is thus a barrier.

To conclude the proof of (
1.5
), let
f
be as in (
1.4
).
We can assume that
f(0,0)=0
.
Then, (
7.20
) and (
7.21
) imply that
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle|f|

\displaystyle\leq M_{\theta}\bigl((1-\varepsilon)^{2}+(2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{2})\bigr)\delta^{(\frac{p}{p-1}-l)/(p-2)}\min\{|x|,\delta\}^{p/(p-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}+(2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{2})M_{\theta}\delta^{\tau}\quad\text{on }\partial\Theta_{l,\theta},

and hence, by the definition of Perron solutions,

{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}}_{\Theta_{l,\theta}}f(x,t)\leq\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}+(2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{2})M_{\theta}\delta^{\tau}\quad\text{in }\Theta_{l,\theta}.

Using (
7.16
) with (

)
and then letting

shows that

\limsup_{\Theta_{l,\theta}\ni\xi\to(0,0)}{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}}_{\Theta_{l,\theta}}f(\xi)\leq 0.

Applying this also to
-f
, and using (
2.2
), yields

0\leq\lim_{\Theta_{l,\theta}\ni\xi\to(0,0)}{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\displaystyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\textstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\underline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}}_{\Theta_{l,\theta}}f(\xi)\leq\lim_{\Theta_{l,\theta}\ni\xi\to(0,0)}{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle H}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{H}}}_{\Theta_{l,\theta}}f(\xi)\leq 0,

i.e. (

∎

8
Real-world interpretations

We end the paper with two naive situations
of how the Dirichlet problem in soda cans and the regularity of the origin
can be interpreted in real-world problems.

Example 8.1
.

Nonlinear diffusion in a medium.

Typically,
n=3
.
At each time
t\in(-t_{0},0)
,
let the ball
B^{t}=B(0,r_{t})
consist of a substance that
is being released to (or dissolving in) the surrounding medium, where it diffuses
according to the
p\mspace{1mu}
-parabolic equation.
The solution
u(x,t)
of this equation in the complement of
\bigcup_{t\in(-t_{0},0)}B^{t}

describes the concentration
of the substance in position
x\notin B^{t}
at time
t\in(-t_{0},0)
.

The initial concentration at
t=-t_{0}
is
u(x,-t_{0})=0
for
x\notin B^{-t_{0}}
.
On the boundary
\partial B^{t}
, the concentration is
a known function
f
(continuous in space and time),
e.g. a fixed constant.

As the substance releases into the medium, the ball
B^{t}
containing it
shrinks to eventually become a point and disappear at the final time
t=0
.
In the case of soda can domains
\Theta_{l,\theta}
, studied in this paper, the speed of
shrinking is given by

r_{t}=\biggl(\frac{-t}{\theta}\biggr)^{1/l},\quad-t_{0}<t<0.

Regularity of the final point
(0,0)
with respect to the soda can
\Theta_{l,\theta}

then means that just before all the substance
has been released, its concentration near

x=0
is close to
f(0,0)
.
Irregularity means a possible jump or discontinuity of the concentration
for some continuous data
f
.

For example, the
p\mspace{1mu}
-superparabolic function
u
in the proof of Proposition
1.4

shows that immediately after the last substance has been released,
its concentration is zero at every
x\neq 0
.
Indeed,
p<2
in this example means fast diffusion.

(The soda cans
\Theta_{l,\theta}
also impose
boundary conditions at
|x|=1
, which we ignore here.
Indeed, a simple comparison between Perron solutions shows that
regularity at
(0,0)
holds
equivalently also for discontinuous bounded boundary data
as long as they are continuous at
(0,0)
,
cf. the proof of Lemma 9.6 in Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
18
]
.)

Example 8.2
.

Heat equation with a partially immersed heater/cooler.

Typically,
n=2=p
.
In this interpretation, a hot or cool body
passes through a shallow layer of a medium, represented by
the 2-dimensional time slices of
\Theta_{l,\theta}
,
until it completely leaves the medium at time
t=0
.
For example, a fading stream of magma is passing through
an underground layer of water in a geological slab.
At each time
t\in(-t_{0},0)
, the body intersects the shallow layer of the medium
in an essentially
2
-dimensional ball
B^{t}=B(0,r_{t})
.
The parameters
l
and
\theta
in
\Theta_{l,\theta}
describe
how fast this cross-section changes in time.

The body has certain temperature (continuous in space and time),
which provides the
boundary data
u(x,t)
on
\partial B^{t}
at time
t
.
The heat transfer in the medium exterior to the body follows the
heat equation with
p=2
.

Proposition
1.5
1
shows that for all

l,\theta>0
, the final temperature of the heater/cooler is
reflected in the temperature of the medium
close to the separation of the body from the medium,
but this need not be the case if the heater/cooler is an infinitesimally
thin fibre.
