1.
Introduction

An initial data set for a spacetime consists of a triple
(M^{3},g,\mathbf{k})
, where
M
is a three-dimensional manifold,
g
is a Riemannian metric, and
\mathbf{k}
is a symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor. The associated energy density function
\mu
and momentum density covector
J
are defined by

\displaystyle(8\pi)\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(R+(\mathrm{tr}_{g}\mathbf{k})^{2}-\|\mathbf{k}\|^{2}_{g}\right),

\displaystyle(8\pi)J

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}_{g}\left(\mathbf{k}-(\mathrm{tr}_{g}\mathbf{k})g\right),

where
R
denotes the scalar curvature of
g
. The initial data set is said to satisfy the dominant energy condition if

\displaystyle\mu\geq|J|_{g}.

Following
[
SY81
]
, an initial data set
(M,g,\mathbf{k})
is called asymptotically flat if, outside a compact set,
M
decomposes into finitely many ends
M_{1},\ldots,M_{p}
, each diffeomorphic to the complement of a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and under such diffeomorphisms, the tensors
g
and
\mathbf{k}
satisfy the decay conditions

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}|+|x|\cdot|\partial g_{ij}|+|x|^{2}\cdot|\partial^{2}g_{ij}|\leq C|x|^{-1},\\
&|R|+|x|\cdot|\partial R|\leq C|x|^{-4},\\
&|\mathbf{k}_{ij}|+|x|\cdot|\partial\mathbf{k}_{ij}|+|x|\cdot\left|\textstyle\sum_{i}\mathbf{k}_{ii}\right|\leq C|x|^{-2},\end{split}

as
|x|\to\infty
, where norms and derivatives are taken with respect to the Euclidean metric.

With each end
M_{k}
we associate the ADM mass
m_{k}
defined by the flux integral

\displaystyle m_{k}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{S_{r}}\sum_{i,j}\left(g_{ij,j}-g_{jj,i}\right)n^{i}dA,

where
S_{r}=\{x:|x|=r\}
is the Euclidean
2
-sphere at infinity.

For convenience, we modify the topology of
M
by compactifying all of the ends of
M
except for one chosen end. We will use
m=m(g)
to denote the mass of the chosen end.

Following
[
BK11
]
, let
\mathcal{S}
denote the collection of surfaces which are
C^{2}
-smooth compact boundaries of open sets
U
in
M
, where
U
is bounded in the chosen end and contains all other ends. A
C^{2}
-smooth surface
\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}
is called a generalized apparent horizon if its mean curvature satisfies

\displaystyle H_{\Sigma}=|\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}\mathbf{k}|,

and a generalized trapped surface if

\displaystyle H_{\Sigma}\leq|\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}\mathbf{k}|.

It was proved by Eichmair
[
Eic10
]
that any generalized trapped surface
\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}
is enclosed by a unique outermost generalized apparent horizon
\tilde{\Sigma}
, which is
C^{2,\alpha}
-smooth and strictly outer area minimizing.

The generalized Penrose conjecture was formulated by Bray-Khuri as follows.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
BK11
, Conjecture 4]
)
.

Suppose that
(M^{3},g,\mathbf{k})
is a complete connected asymptotically flat initial data set satisfying the dominant energy condition. For a chosen end with mass
m
, if
\Sigma\in\mathcal{S}
is a generalized trapped surface, then

(2)

\displaystyle m\geq\sqrt{\frac{A}{16\pi}},

where
A
is the area of the outermost generalized apparent horizon
\tilde{\Sigma}
enclosing
\Sigma
.

When
\mathbf{k}=0
, the conjecture reduces to the Riemannian Penrose inequality, which was proved by Huisken-Ilmanen
[
HI01
]
in the case of a connected horizon and by Bray
[
Bra01
]
in full generality. Beyond the time-symmetric setting, several special cases are also known. In particular, the spherically symmetric case has been established, see
[
Hay96
,
IMM96
,
BK10
]
. In contrast, in the fully general setting, a counterexample was constructed by Carrasco-Mars
[
CM10
]
.

If one replaces generalized apparent horizons by apparent horizons, that is, surfaces satisfying
H_{\Sigma}=\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}\mathbf{k}
or
H_{\Sigma}=-\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}\mathbf{k}
, one obtains the standard Penrose conjecture, which remains widely open. See
[
Mar09
]
for an excellent survey of this conjecture and the references therein.

An important consequence of the Penrose conjecture is the (generalized) positive mass theorem for initial data sets, which asserts the weaker conclusion
m\geq 0
. This result was established by Schoen-Yau
[
SY81
]
and independently by Witten
[
Wit81
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the special case in which
\mathbf{k}
is proportional to
g
, i.e. for a smooth function
\tau
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{k}=\frac{\tau}{3}g.

In this setting, the energy density and the momentum density become

\displaystyle(8\pi)\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(R+\tau^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\tau^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}R+\frac{1}{3}\tau^{2},

\displaystyle(8\pi)J

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\tau}{3}g-\tau g\right)=-\frac{2}{3}d\tau,

and the dominant energy condition becomes

(3)

\displaystyle|\nabla\tau|_{g}\leq\frac{3}{4}R+\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2}.

Define

\displaystyle h=\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}\mathbf{k}=\frac{2}{3}\tau,

which is independent of
\Sigma
.
Then the generalized apparent horizons correspond to surfaces of prescribed mean curvature
|h|
.
The dominant energy condition (
3
) is equivalent to

(4)

\displaystyle R+\frac{3}{2}h^{2}-2|\nabla h|\geq 0.

Remark 1.1
.

This condition coincides with the positivity condition arising in the second variation of
\mu
-bubbles with weight
|h|
. As an application, we can provide an alternative proof of the generalized positive mass theorem; see Section
2
for further details. This observation also motivates the flow introduced below, which can be considered as a parabolic version of
\mu
-bubbles.

We call
(M,g,h)
an asymptotically flat triple if (
1
) is satisfied with
\mathbf{k}=\frac{h}{2}g
. Actually, we only need the following weaker asymptotic decay of
h
:

(5)

\displaystyle|h|+|x|\cdot|\partial h|\leq C|x|^{-2}\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.

The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
(M^{3},g,h)
is a complete connected asymptotically flat triple for a smooth function
h
satisfying the dominant energy condition (
4
). Assume that
M
has one end with mass
m
, and the boundary of
M
is compact and consists of
C^{2,\alpha}
-smooth outermost generalized apparent horizons. Then

(6)

\displaystyle m\geq\sqrt{\frac{|\Sigma|}{16\pi}},

where
|\Sigma|
is the area of any connected component of
\partial M
. Equality holds if and only if
h\equiv 0
and
M
is isometric to the spatial Schwarzschild manifold.

In particular, this establishes the generalized Penrose conjecture for each connected component of the outermost generalized apparent horizon in the case where
\mathbf{k}
is proportional to
g
.

When
h\equiv 0
, the theorem reduces to the time-symmetric case proved by Huisken-Ilmanen
[
HI01
]
, where the authors developed a theory of weak solutions to the inverse mean curvature flow, established Geroch’s monotonicity formula, and derived the Riemannian Penrose inequality as a consequence.

The special non-time-symmetric setting considered here contains many genuinely new cases of the Penrose conjecture beyond both the time-symmetric theory and the known spherically symmetric results. Indeed, one can construct many nonspherically symmetric asymptotically flat triples satisfying the dominant energy condition for which the generalized apparent horizon strictly encloses the classical minimal horizon. In such situations, Theorem
1.2
yields a strictly stronger lower bound for the ADM mass than the classical Riemannian Penrose inequality. See Example
2.1
below for a concrete construction.

The main contribution of the present work is the introduction of a new geometric evolution, which we call the
\sigma
-inverse mean curvature flow, together with a corresponding monotonicity formula for an associated generalized Hawking mass. To the best of our knowledge, both the flow and the monotonicity formula are new. Once these structures are identified, the construction of weak solutions and the extension of the monotonicity formula to the weak setting can, modulo several technical difficulties, be carried out by adapting the strategy of
[
HI01
]
. We now describe this construction.

Let
\sigma
be a Lipschitz nonnegative symmetric
2
-tensor. We introduce the
\sigma
-inverse mean curvature flow (or
\sigma
-IMCF):

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(x,t)=\frac{\nu}{H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}}(x,t),\quad x\in N,\ 0\leq t\leq T,

where
F:N^{n-1}\times[0,T]\to M^{n}
is a family of hypersurfaces
N_{t}:=F(N,t)
,
H
is the mean curvature of
N_{t}
, and
\nu
is the outward unit normal. Here,
H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}=H-\sigma(\nu,\nu)
is assumed positive for classical solutions, and
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}
denotes the normal velocity along the hypersurface
N_{t}
.

In the special case when
\sigma=|h|\cdot g
, (
7
) reduces to

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(x,t)=\frac{\nu}{H-|h|}(x,t),\quad x\in N,\ 0\leq t\leq T.

A key structural feature of this flow is the existence of natural monotonicity formulas for several associated geometric quantities. From now on we specialize to
n=3
.

Define

(9)

\displaystyle A(t):=e^{-t}|N_{t}|,\quad B(t):=e^{\frac{t}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{N_{t}}(H-|h|)^{2}\right),

and introduce the generalized Hawking mass

(10)

\displaystyle m_{h}(N_{t}):=\sqrt{\frac{A(t)}{16\pi}}\cdot B(t).

Then one can show that along classical solutions of the
(|h|g)
-IMCF (
8
), the quantities
A(t)
,
B(t)
, and consequently
m_{h}(N_{t})
are all monotone nondecreasing under the dominant energy condition (
4
), provided the evolving surfaces
N_{t}
remain connected; see Section
5.4
. Furthermore, it can be proved that
\lim_{t\to\infty}m_{h}(N_{t})
is bounded above by the ADM mass
m
(cf. Lemma
7.2
). Therefore, the generalized Penrose inequality would follow if one could construct a classical solution of the flow emanating from the generalized apparent horizon.

The central task is therefore to construct a weak solution to (
8
) and to establish the associated monotonicity formula in this weak setting. To develop such a weak formulation, it is necessary to work with the more general
\sigma
-IMCF (
7
). There have been extensive studies of weak solutions for several related formulations of generalized inverse mean curvature flows. In particular, Moore
[
Moo14
]
considered the flow
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=\frac{\nu}{H+\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}\mathbf{k}}
under the assumption
\mathrm{tr}_{g}\mathbf{k}\geq 0
, while Huisken–Wolff
[
HW25
]
studied
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{H^{2}-(\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}\mathbf{k})^{2}}}
in the maximal case
\mathrm{tr}_{g}\mathbf{k}=0
. By contrast, the
\sigma
-IMCF studied here possesses a fundamentally different structure and cannot be reduced to either of these flows, even when
\mathbf{k}
is proportional to
g
. Nevertheless, by adapting techniques developed in
[
HI01
,
Moo14
,
HW25
]
together with several necessary modifications, we establish a weak theory for
\sigma
-IMCF; see Theorem
4.9
and Theorem
5.1
. After constructing weak solutions, the monotonicity formula can be justified through arguments analogous to those in
[
HI01
]
. One important difference, however, is that uniqueness is not known a priori in our setting, requiring a more delicate analysis. This ultimately yields a more general growth formula of
B(t)
, stated in Theorem
6.5
, which requires only the dominant energy condition rather than nonnegative scalar curvature.

Outline of the paper.
In Section

. In Section

, we establish the monotonicity formula for the weak
(|h|g)
-IMCF. In Section
7
, we finish the proof of the main theorem.

2.
Dominant energy condition and the generalized PMT

In this section, we make some observations about the dominant energy condition, which can be viewed as a generalization of the nonnegative scalar curvature condition, and as motivation for the
\sigma
-IMCF that we will study later.

Given a complete connected asymptotically flat manifold
(M,g)
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}(M,g)

\displaystyle:=\left\{h:R+\frac{3}{2}h^{2}-2|\nabla h|\geq 0,(M,g,h)\text{ is an asymptotically flat triple}\right\}.

Notice that

\displaystyle 0\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(M,g)\iff R\geq 0.

The following proposition shows that the condition
\mathcal{H}_{0}(M,g)\neq\emptyset
reflects an underlying nonnegative scalar curvature phenomenon.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
R(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in M
, then either
\mathcal{H}_{0}(M,g)=\emptyset
or
R\equiv 0
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}(M,g)=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Assume that
R\leq 0
and
h\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(M,g)
. Set
\tau=\frac{3}{2}h
. Choose one base point and a unit-length geodesic
\gamma(t),t\in[0,\infty),
from the base point to one end. Consider
\tau(t)=\tau(\gamma(t))
. Then

\displaystyle|\tau^{\prime}(t)|\leq|\nabla\tau|(\gamma(t))\leq\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2}.

As
t\to\infty
, we know

\displaystyle|\tau(t)|\leq C(1+t^{2})^{-1}\ll t^{-1}.

Fix
\beta>\frac{1}{4}
. For large enough
t_{0}\gg 1
, we have
\beta|\tau(t)|\leq(1+t)^{-1}
. Let’s assume that
t_{1}
is the outermost
t
so that
\beta|\tau(t)|\leq(1+t)^{-1}
. Assume that
t_{1}>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\beta|\tau(t)|\leq(1+t)^{-1},\ \forall t\geq t_{1},\ \beta|\tau(t_{1})|=(1+t_{1})^{-1}>0.

Consider the inverval
(t_{1},t_{2})
where
|\tau(t)|>0
. Such
t_{2}>t_{1}
always exist. For any
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}\log\frac{|\tau(t)|}{|\tau(t_{0})|}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\tau^{\prime}(t)|}{|\tau(t)|}\leq\frac{1}{2}|\tau(t)|\leq\frac{1}{2\beta}(1+t)^{-1}.

So

\displaystyle|\tau(t)|\geq|\tau(t_{1})|\cdot\left(\frac{1+t}{1+t_{1}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2\beta}}>0.

This also shows that
|\tau(t)|>0
and above estimate holds for all
t\geq t_{1}
.

As
t\to\infty
, we have the estimate that

\displaystyle t^{-\frac{1}{2\beta}}\leq C\cdot t^{-2},

a contradiction with our choice of
\beta
that
2-\frac{1}{2\beta}>0
. Thus
t_{1}=0
, and we have

\displaystyle|\tau(t)|\leq\frac{1}{\beta(1+t)},\ \ \forall\beta>\frac{1}{4}.

Taking
\beta\to\infty
implies that
|\tau|\equiv 0
, i.e.
\mathcal{H}_{0}(M,g)=\{0\}
. Together with the assumption, we also have
R\equiv 0
.
∎

On the other hand, when
(M,g)
possesses positive scalar curvature, one has
\mathcal{H}_{0}(M,g)\neq\emptyset
. More precisely, assume
(M,g)
satisfies
R\geq 0
everywhere and
R>0
on some open subset. Then, by choosing a smooth function
h
supported in the region where
R>0
and with sufficiently small
C^{1}
-norm, one obtains
h\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(M,g)
.

The following example constructs an asymptotically flat triple satisfying the dominant energy condition and shows that Theorem
1.2
applies to new cases of the Penrose inequality beyond the classical Riemannian setting and the previously known spherically symmetric settings.

Example 2.1
.

Start with the doubled Schwarzschild metric

\displaystyle(M,g_{m})=(\mathbb{R}\times S^{2},ds^{2}+r(s)^{2}g_{S^{2}}),

where

\displaystyle r(s)\geq 2m,\ r(0)=2m,\ r^{\prime}(0)=0,

and

\displaystyle(r^{\prime}(s))^{2}=1-\frac{2m}{r(s)}.

The scalar curvature is
R\equiv 0
and the mean curvature of
s
-slice is
H(s)=\frac{2r^{\prime}(s)}{r(s)}
.

Choose a smooth positive even function
h
such that

h(s)=\frac{\delta}{(1+|s|)^{3}}

for sufficiently large
|s|
, where
\delta>0
is a small constant. Note that
h
need not be rotationally symmetric.
Define

F:=2|\nabla h|_{g_{m}}-\frac{3}{2}h^{2}.

As
|s|\to\infty
, the leading term of
F
is
\frac{6\delta}{(1+|s|)^{4}}>0
, hence
F
is positive for all sufficiently large
|s|
.

Let
F_{+}=\max(F,0)
,
F_{-}=\max(-F,0)
, and solve

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g_{m}}v=F_{+},\quad v\to 0\text{ as }|s|\to\infty.

By the maximum principle,
v>0
. Define

u=1+v,\quad g_{u}=u^{4}g_{m}.

Then
g_{u}
is asymptotically flat, and the conformal scalar curvature formula together with
u>1
yields

\displaystyle R_{g_{u}}+\frac{3}{2}h^{2}-2u^{-2}|\nabla h|_{g_{m}}\geq 8u^{-5}F_{+}+\frac{3}{2}h^{2}-2|\nabla h|_{g_{m}}=(8u^{-5}-1)F_{+}+F_{-}.

Moreover, by taking
\delta
sufficiently small, the solution
v
becomes uniformly small, so that
u
is sufficiently close to
1
. In particular,
8u^{-5}-1>0
, and hence

\displaystyle R_{g_{u}}+\frac{3}{2}h^{2}-2|\nabla h|_{g_{u}}\geq 0.

This shows that
(M,g_{u},\frac{h}{2}g_{u})
defines an asymptotically flat initial data set satisfying the dominant energy condition.

Since
F_{+}
is even,
u
is symmetric with respect to the middle sphere
\Sigma_{0}=\{s=0\}
. Consequently,
\Sigma_{0}
remains minimal with respect to
g_{u}
. Since
h>0
on
\Sigma_{0}
, the surface
\Sigma_{0}
is generalized trapped. Therefore, by Eichmair’s theorem, there exists a unique outermost generalized apparent horizon
\tilde{\Sigma}
, satisfying
H=h>0
. In particular,
\tilde{\Sigma}
strictly encloses any minimal surface.

Finally, one may further perturb
g_{m}
so that it’s no longer spherically symmetric and obtain constructions with similar properties. For such examples, Theorem
1.2
gives a strictly stronger conclusion.

Now we scratch an alternative proof of the positive mass theorem, using
\mu
-bubbles and the dominant energy condition.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that
(M^{3},g,\mathbf{k})
is a complete asymptotically flat Cauchy data with
\mathbf{k}=\frac{\tau}{3}g
for some smooth function
\tau
. Assume that
\mathbf{k}
satisfies the dominant energy condition.
Let
m
be the ADM mass.
Then

\displaystyle m\geq 0.

Proof.

By the dominant energy condition, for
h=\frac{2}{3}\tau
, we have

\displaystyle R+\frac{3}{2}|h|^{2}-2|\nabla|h||

\displaystyle\geq 0.

We follow the same argument of
[
Sch06
, Theorem 4.2]
, and only indicate the necessary modifications. The proof is done by contradiction and assuming
m<0
. Instead of minimal surface, one can consider
\mu
-bubbles, which by definition are minimizers of

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\Omega)=|\partial^{*}\Omega|-\int_{\Omega}|h|,

with appropriate obstacles. See also definition of the outward optimizing hull (
26
).

Let
\Sigma_{\sigma}=\partial^{*}\Omega
be the minimizer with boundary in a large cylinder
C_{\sigma}
with radius
\sigma
. By the first variation, we have

\displaystyle H_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}=|h|,

with respect to outer normal
\nu
. By the second variation, for any compactly supported function
f
on
\Sigma_{\sigma}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}|\nabla^{\Sigma}f|^{2}-\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}\left(|\mathrm{II}|^{2}+\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu))+\langle\nabla|h|,\nu\rangle\right)f^{2}\geq 0.

Using the Gauss equation

\displaystyle|\mathrm{II}|^{2}+\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\left(R+|\mathrm{II}|^{2}+H^{2}-2K\right),

we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}\left(|\nabla^{\Sigma}f|^{2}+Kf^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma}}\left(R+\frac{3}{2}h^{2}-2|\nabla|h||\right)f^{2}>0,

unless
R+\frac{3}{2}h^{2}-2|\nabla|h||\equiv 0
and
\mathrm{II}=\mathrm{const}\cdot\mathrm{Id}
, which is impossible on
C_{\sigma}
. Taking
\sigma\to\infty
, we obtain a contradiction with the Gauss-Bonnet theorem.

∎

3.
Classical solution of the
\sigma
-IMCF

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold with
n\geq 3
, and
\sigma
be a smooth nonnegative symmetric
2
-tensor. Let
F:N^{n-1}\times[0,T]\to M^{n}
be a family of hypersurfaces
N_{t}:=F(N,t)
. We say
F
is a classical solution of the
\sigma
-inverse mean curvature flow
(or
\sigma
-IMCF) if the following parabolic evolution equation holds in the classical sense:

(*)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(x,t)=\frac{\nu}{H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}}(x,t),\quad x\in N,\ 0\leq t\leq T,

where
H
is the mean curvature of
N_{t}
at the point
F(x,t)
,
\nu
is the outward unit normal,
|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}=\sigma_{ij}\nu^{i}\nu^{j}\geq 0
,

is assumed to be positive,
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}
denotes the normal velocity along the surface
N_{t}
, and all derivatives and geometric quantities are well defined.

.

Classical solutions of

(

)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta^{N}(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})}{H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}}-\frac{2|\nabla^{N}(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})|^{2}}{(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})^{2}}-\left(\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+|\mathrm{II}|^{2}\right)

4.5.

We give a PDE interpretation of our weak solutions in terms of weak mean curvature. Let

\displaystyle\int_{E_{k}}|\nabla u|\left(1+u-k\right)\leq\int_{E_{k}}(k-u)|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}\leq C(\|\sigma\|_{C^{0}})\|k-u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(E_{k})}\cdot|E_{k}|^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Choosing
k
small enough so that
1+u-k\geq\frac{1}{2}
on
E_{k}
, together with the Sobolev inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{E_{k}}|\nabla u|\left(1+u-k\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}C(n)^{-1}\|k-u\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(E_{k})},

which together with above inequality implies that
1\leq C\cdot|E_{k}|^{\frac{1}{n}}
, a contradiction for small enough
k-\inf_{E}u
.

Using (i), (ii) follows exactly as in
[
HI01
, Lemma 4.2]
.

5.4.
Monotonicity formulas for classical solutions

In this section, we compute the monotonicity formulas for classical solutions.
Suppose that a classical solution
(N_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}\subset M
of the
(|h|g)
-IMCF (
23
) exists. For

\displaystyle A(t)=e^{-t}|N_{t}|,

by Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-t}\left(\frac{d}{dt}|N_{t}|-|N_{t}|\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-t}\left(\int_{N_{t}}\frac{H}{H-|h|}-|N_{t}|\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-t}\int_{N_{t}}\frac{|h|}{H-|h|}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

which implies that
A(t)
is monotone increasing.

For

\displaystyle B(t)=e^{\frac{t}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{N_{t}}(H-|h|)^{2}\right),

using (
13
) and the fact that
\nabla_{\nu}(|h|g)=(\nabla_{\nu}|h|)g
and
g(\nu,\nabla^{N}(H-|h|))=0
a.e., we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}B^{\prime}(t)&=\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{t}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{N_{t}}(H-|h|)^{2}\right)\\
&\ \ +\frac{e^{\frac{t}{2}}}{16\pi}\int_{N_{t}}2\left(\frac{|\nabla^{N}(H-|h|)|^{2}}{(H-|h|)^{2}}+\left(\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+|\mathrm{II}|^{2}\right)\right)\\
&\ \ +\frac{e^{\frac{t}{2}}}{16\pi}\int_{N_{t}}\left(2(\nabla_{\nu}|h|)-H(H-|h|)\right)\\
&=\frac{e^{\frac{t}{2}}}{16\pi}\int_{N_{t}}2\left(\frac{|\nabla^{N}(H-|h|)|^{2}}{(H-|h|)^{2}}+\left(\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+|\mathrm{II}|^{2}\right)\right)\\
&\ \ +\frac{e^{\frac{t}{2}}}{16\pi}\left(8\pi+\int_{N_{t}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}(H-|h|)^{2}+2(\nabla_{\nu}|h|)-H(H-|h|)\right)\right).\end{split}

By the Gauss equation

\displaystyle R+|\mathrm{II}|^{2}+H^{2}=R_{N_{t}}+2\left(\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+|\mathrm{II}|^{2}\right),

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}16\pi e^{-\frac{t}{2}}B^{\prime}(t)&=\int_{N_{t}}\frac{2|\nabla^{N}(H-|h|)|^{2}}{(H-|h|)^{2}}+\int_{N_{t}}\left(R+|\mathrm{II}|^{2}+H^{2}-R_{N_{t}}\right)\\
&\ \ +8\pi+\int_{N_{t}}\left(2(\nabla_{\nu}|h|)-\frac{1}{2}(3H-|h|)(H-|h|)\right).\end{split}

Suppose that
n=3
,
N_{t}
remains connected, and
h
satisfies the dominant energy condition (
4
).
Using the fact that
H-|h|>0
,
|\mathrm{II}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}H^{2}
, and the Gauss-Bonnet formula
\int_{N_{t}}R_{N_{t}}=4\pi\chi(N_{t})\leq 8\pi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}16\pi&e^{-\frac{t}{2}}B^{\prime}(t)\\
&=\int_{N_{t}}\frac{2|\nabla^{N}(H-|h|)|^{2}}{(H-|h|)^{2}}+8\pi-4\pi\chi(N_{t})+\int_{N_{t}}\left(|\mathrm{II}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}H^{2}\right)\\
&\ \ +\int_{N_{t}}\left(R+\frac{3}{2}H^{2}+2\nabla_{\nu}|h|-\frac{1}{2}(3H-|h|)(H-|h|)\right)\\
&\geq\int_{N_{t}}\left(R+\frac{3}{2}H^{2}-2|\nabla h|-\frac{1}{2}(3H-|h|)(H-|h|)\right)\\
&=\int_{N_{t}}\left(R+\frac{3}{2}|h|^{2}-2|\nabla h|+2|h|(H-|h|)\right)\\
&\geq 0,\end{split}

which shows that
B(t)
is also monotone increasing. Thus, in dimension
3
,

m_{h}(N_{t})=\sqrt{\frac{A(t)}{16\pi}}B(t)

is monotone increasing along the classical
(|h|g)
-IMCF, under the connectedness assumption and the dominant energy condition.

(|h|g)
-IMCF

In this section, we establish the monotonicity formula for the weak

-IMCF. The strategy is to first derive an
\epsilon

(|h|g)
-IMCF with initial condition

Let’s recall that, as in the proof of Theorem

, there are subsequences

\sigma^{i}
-IMCF on

locally uniformly,
\tilde{\nu}_{i}=\frac{\tilde{\nabla}U_{i}}{|\tilde{\nabla}U_{i}|}\to\tilde{\nu}

(28)

\displaystyle\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}\to\tilde{N}_{t}=N_{t}\times\mathbb{R}\text{ locally in }C^{1,\alpha},\quad\text{a.e. }t\geq 0,

where

,
\tilde{N}_{t}=\partial\tilde{E}_{t}
,
E_{t}=\{u<t\}
and
\tilde{E}_{t}=\{U<t\}
for

.

Take a cutoff function
\phi\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\phi\geq 0,\mathrm{supp}\phi\subset[1,5],

\phi=1
on
[2,4]
,
|\phi^{\prime}|\leq C\phi
, and
\fint\phi(z)dz=1
. Fix an arbitrary
T>0
. By Lemma
4.1
, there exists
R(T)>0
such that for all large
i
, depending on
T
,

\displaystyle\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}\cap(M\times\mathrm{supp}\phi)\subset K(T):=(\bar{G}_{R(T)}\setminus E_{0})\times[1,5],\ 0\leq t\leq T,

a fixed compact set, where
G_{r}:=\{x\in M\setminus E_{0}:d(E_{0},x)<r\}
. Since
\partial\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}=\partial E_{0}\times\{-\frac{t}{\epsilon_{i}}\}\cup\partial\Omega_{i}\times\{\frac{L_{i}-2-t}{\epsilon_{i}}\}
, for any
0\leq t\leq T
,
\partial\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}\cap(M\times\mathrm{supp}\phi)=\emptyset
for all large
i
.

Applying (
13
) to the smooth
\sigma^{i}
-IMCF
\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}
, we obtain

(29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})^{2}\\
&=\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\phi-2\langle\tilde{\nabla}^{N}\phi,\frac{\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})}{\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}}\rangle\\
&\ \ -2\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi\left(\frac{|\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})|^{2}}{(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})^{2}}+\left(\tilde{\mathrm{Ric}}(\tilde{\nu}_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i})+|\tilde{\mathrm{II}}|^{2}\right)\right)\\
&\ \ -2\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi(\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\sigma^{i})(\tilde{\nu}_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i})-4\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi\frac{\sigma^{i}(\tilde{\nu}_{i},\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}))}{\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}}+\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi\tilde{H}_{i}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}).\end{split}

For each

, the integrating form is

(30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{s}}\phi(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})^{2}-\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{r}}\phi(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})^{2}\\
&=\int_{r}^{s}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\phi-2\langle\tilde{\nabla}^{N}\phi,\frac{\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})}{\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}}\rangle\\

&-2\int_{r}^{s}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi(\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\sigma^{i})(\tilde{\nu}_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i})-4\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi\frac{\sigma^{i}(\tilde{\nu}_{i},\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}))}{\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}}+\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi\tilde{H}_{i}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})\\

We wish to pass this identity to limits.

Since the sublevelsets

, for all large

where we have used

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\frac{\phi\tilde{H}_{i}}{\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}}\geq\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi,

i

In particular, for a.e.

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}

\displaystyle|(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\phi|+2\phi|\tilde{\mathrm{Ric}}(\tilde{\nu}_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i})|+2\phi|(\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\sigma^{i})(\tilde{\nu}_{i},\tilde{\nu}_{i})|

\displaystyle\left|2\langle\tilde{\nabla}^{N}\phi,\frac{\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})}{\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}}\rangle\right|

,

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi\left(\frac{|\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})|^{2}}{(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})^{2}}+2|\tilde{\mathrm{II}}|^{2}\right)+C(T),

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})^{2}\text{ exists, }\quad\text{a.e. }t\geq 0.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}\cap(M\times[2,4])}\frac{|\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})|^{2}}{(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})^{2}}+|\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})|^{2}+|\tilde{\mathrm{II}}|^{2}\leq C(T).

Applying Fatou’s Lemma, we obtain

(37)

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}\cap(M\times[2,4])}\frac{|\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})|^{2}}{(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})^{2}}+|\tilde{\nabla}^{N}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})|^{2}+|\tilde{\mathrm{II}}|^{2}<\infty,\text{ a.e. }t\geq 0.

Henceforth we shrink

As in the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{r}^{s}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi\tilde{H}_{i}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})&\to\int_{r}^{s}\int_{\tilde{N}_{t}}\phi H_{\tilde{N}_{t}}(H_{\tilde{N}_{t}}-|h|).\end{split}

Next we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}})|\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{\nu}_{i}}\phi|

Next we claim that for each

Next we apply the trick of

to estimate the term

\displaystyle g_{i}(t):=-\epsilon_{i}\int_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}\phi(z)\langle\tilde{\nabla}^{N}\log(\tilde{H}_{i}-|\tilde{\nu}_{i}|^{2}_{\sigma^{i}}),e_{z}\rangle dA_{\tilde{N}^{i}_{t}}(x,z).

Thus

on
[0,\infty)
in the sense of measures, which implies that for each

,

7.2.
Multiple horizons and the selection of jump times

In this subsection, we describe the selection of jump times in the presence of multiple horizons, using the outward optimizing hulls. The main result is the monotonicity formula for multiple boundary components.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
(M^{\prime},g,h)
be a complete, connected asymptotically flat triple so that
M^{\prime}
is an exterior region with
C^{2,\alpha}
-outermost generalized apparent horizon. Assume that
h
satisfies the dominant energy condition. For each connected component
N
of
\partial M^{\prime}
, there exists a weak flow of compact
C^{1,\alpha}
-surfaces
(N_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, such that
N_{0}=N
, the generalized Hawking mass
m_{h}(N_{t})
is monotone increasing for all time, and for sufficiently large
t
,
N_{t}
satisfies the weak
(|h|g)
-IMCF.

Proof.

The idea is the same as in
[
HI01
, Section 6]
. We will flow a single boundary component while treating the others as inessential occlusions to be slid across, without incurring any loss of the generalized Hawking mass.

Denote by
N,N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}
the boundary components of
M^{\prime}
. Fill in the boundary by
3
-balls
E_{0},W_{1},\ldots,W_{k}
to obtain a smooth, complete, connected asymptotically flat triple
(M,g,h)
without boundary. Write
W:=\cup_{1\leq i\leq k}W_{k}
. By Lemma
5.7
and Lemma
5.8
,
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
E_{0}\cup W
is a strictly outward optimizing hull in
M
. The case when
W
is empty is covered in Theorem
6.6
, so we assume it’s nonempty in the following.

By Theorem
6.6
, there exists a weak
(|h|g)
-IMCF
(E_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with initial condition
E_{0}
so that
\partial E_{t}
remains connected, and
m_{h}(\partial E_{t})
is monotone increasing whenever the dominant energy condition is satisfied, i.e.
\partial E_{t}\subset M^{\prime}
. Define
s_{1}>0
to be the supremum of the times when
E_{t}
is disjoint from
W
. Then
E_{s_{1}}\cap W=\emptyset
and
E_{s_{1}}^{+}\cap W\neq\emptyset
. Define
t_{1}:=s_{1}
if
\bar{E}_{s_{1}}\cap\bar{W}=\emptyset
, otherwise let
t_{1}
be slightly less than
s_{1}
. Notice that
s_{1}
is necessarily a jump time when
t_{1}=s_{1}
.

Now we construct the jump. Let
F
be the connected component of the strictly outward optimizing hull
(E_{t_{1}}\cup W)^{\prime}
that contains
E_{t_{1}}
. Notice that
F
contains at least one component of
W
, so after relabelling we can write
F=(E_{t_{1}}\cup W_{1}\cup\cdots\cup W_{j})^{\prime}
. Since
\partial W
is
C^{2,\alpha}
-generalized apparent horizon,
\partial F
is disjoint from
\partial W
by the strong maximum principle. By Lemma
5.5
,
E_{t_{1}}
is outward optimizing, so

\displaystyle|\partial E_{t_{1}}|\leq|\partial F|-\int_{F\setminus E_{t_{1}}}|h|\leq|\partial F|.

By a smoothing argument (cf. Remark
6.1
), we can choose a sequence of smooth sets
E_{i}
such that
\partial E_{i}\to\partial E_{t_{1}}
in
C^{1}\cap W^{2,2}
and

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E_{i}}(H-|h|)^{2}\to\int_{\partial E_{t_{1}}}(H-|h|)^{2}.

Using (
27
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E_{i}^{\prime}}(H-|h|)^{2}\leq\int_{\partial E_{i}}(H-|h|)^{2}.

Notice that
\partial F\in C^{1,1}
and
\partial E_{i}^{\prime}\to\partial F
in
C^{1}
. By the lower semicontinuity, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial F}(H-|h|)^{2}\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{\partial E_{i}^{\prime}}(H-|h|)^{2}\leq\int_{\partial E_{t_{1}}}(H-|h|)^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle m_{h}(\partial E_{t_{1}})\leq m_{h}(\partial F).

Applying
[
HI01
, Lemma 6.2]
to
J^{|h|}
-functional, one can approximate
\partial F
in
C^{1}
by smooth surfaces of uniformly bounded mean curvature. In view of the gradient estimates in Lemma
4.1
and the Compactness Lemma
4.8
, one can construct a weak
(|h|g)
-IMCF
(F_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with initial condition
F_{0}=F
as in Theorem
6.6
so that
m_{h}(F_{t})
is monotone increasing as long as
\partial F_{t}\subset M^{\prime}
. Then replace
E_{t}
by
F_{t}
for

. We may continue this procedure inductively to obtain a nested family of sets

(E_{t})_{t\geq 0}
so that
E_{t}
is a weak
(|h|g)
-IMCF except at a finite number of times, and
m_{h}(\partial E_{t})
is monotone increasing. This completes the proof.
∎

7.3.
Proof of main theorem

Let
(M,g,h)
be an exterior region satisfying the asymptotically flat condition (
48
) and the dominant energy condition (
49
), with (possibly empty) outermost generalized apparent horizon.

1. Assume
M
has no boundary. For any
x\in M
and each

5.1
, there exists a weak solution

of the
(|h|g)
-IMCF with initial condition

. Notice that
H-|h|\to\infty
on
\partial B_{\delta}(x)
as
\delta\to 0
, and
|h|
becomes negligible compared with
H
. Similarly as in
[
HI01
, Lemma 8.1]
, using the techniques of Lemma
7.1
, one can show that there exists a subsequence
\delta_{i}\to 0
, a sequence
c_{i}\to\infty
and a function

defined on
M\setminus\{x\}
such that
u^{\delta_{i}}-c_{i}\to u
locally uniformly,

in
B_{1}(x)\setminus\{x\}
, and

is nonempty and compact for all
t
, with
N_{t}
nearly equal to
\partial B_{e^{t/2}}
as

. Using the monotone increasing property and the upper semicontinuity of
m_{h}(N^{i}_{t})
, together with Lemma
7.2
and the fact that
m_{h}(\partial B_{\delta_{i}}(x))\to 0
, we obtain
0\leq m_{h}(N_{t})\leq m_{\mathrm{ADM}}(M)
for all

.

2. If
M
has a boundary, let
N
be any connected component of
\partial M
. By Theorem
7.3
, there exists a flow
(N_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
N_{0}=N
and
m_{h}(N_{t})
is monotone increasing. Together with Lemma
7.2
this proves the Penrose inequality that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{|N|}{16\pi}}=m_{h}(N_{0})\leq m_{\mathrm{ADM}}(M).

3. To complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
, it remains to consider the equality case. Whether
M
has a boundary or not, since
N_{t}
solves the weak
(|h|g)
-IMCF except for a finite number of times, in view of (
46

N_{t}
for a.e.

|h|=0
on
N_{t}
and
\int_{N_{t}}|\nabla^{N}(H-|h|)|^{2}=0
for a.e.
t
. By the continuity of
|h|
and lower semicontinuity of

, we have

\displaystyle|h|=0\text{ on }N_{t},\quad\int_{N_{t}}|\nabla^{N}(H-|h|)|^{2}=0\text{ for all }t.

\displaystyle H_{N_{t}}-|h|=H_{N_{t}}=H(t),\text{ a.e. }x\in N_{t},\text{ for all }t,

i.e.

A.1.
Evolution equations and interior estimates of classical
\sigma
-IMCF

Let
\sigma
be a smooth symmetric
2
-tensor on a smooth Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with dimension
n\geq 3
. Let
F:N^{n-1}\times[0,T]\to M^{n}
be a classical solution of the
\sigma
-IMCF, i.e. the following evolution equation is satisfied in the classical sense:

(58)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(x,t)=\frac{\nu}{H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}}(x,t),\quad x\in N,\ 0\leq t\leq T.

In this section, we will first compute the evolution equations, which proves Lemma
3.1
, and then prove the local estimate of mean curvature in Lemma
3.2
.

For any fixed
(x,t)\in N\times(0,T)
, let
\{x^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}
be coordinate functions of
N
in a coordinate chart of
x
and
\{y^{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be coordinate functions of
M
in a neighborhood of
F(x,t)
. Set
f:=\frac{1}{H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}}
,
F^{i}:=y^{i}\circ F
, and
\partial_{\alpha}:=F_{\sharp}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha}})
. We abuse the notation and denote
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}
by
\partial_{t}
. Then the evolution equation is
\partial_{t}=f\nu
, the induced metric on
N_{t}
is
g_{\alpha\beta}=g(\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta})
, and the area element is
dA=\sqrt{\mathrm{det}(g_{\alpha\beta})}dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n-1}
. We calculate

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{\alpha},\partial_{\beta}\rangle+\langle\partial_{\alpha},\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\partial_{t},\partial_{\beta}\rangle+\langle\partial_{\alpha},\nabla_{\partial_{\beta}}\partial_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=2f\mathrm{II}_{\alpha\beta},

where
\mathrm{II}_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\nu,\partial_{\beta}\rangle
is the second fundamental form of
N_{t}
in
M
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}dA

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{t}g_{\alpha\beta}dA=fHdA.

This proves that

(59)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|N_{t}|

\displaystyle=\int_{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}dA=\int_{N}fHdA=\int_{N}\frac{H}{H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}}dA.

We calculate

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu,\partial_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\nu,\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{\alpha}\rangle=-\langle\nu,\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\partial_{t}\rangle=-\langle\nu,\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}(f\nu)\rangle=-\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}f.

Since
\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu,\nu\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nu|^{2}=0
, we have

(60)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu=-\nabla^{N}f=-\nabla^{N}\frac{1}{H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}}=\frac{\nabla^{N}(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})}{(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})^{2}}.

So

(61)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}&=2\sigma(\nu,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu)+(\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\sigma)(\nu,\nu)\\
&=-2\sigma\left(\nu,\nabla^{N}f\right)+f(\nabla_{\nu}\sigma)(\nu,\nu)\\
&=\frac{2\sigma(\nu,\nabla^{N}(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}))}{(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})^{2}}+\frac{(\nabla_{\nu}\sigma)(\nu,\nu)}{H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}}.\end{split}

We calculate

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathrm{II}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\nu,\partial_{\beta}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\nu,\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\nu,\partial_{\beta}\rangle+\langle R(\partial_{t},\partial_{\alpha})\nu,\partial_{\beta}\rangle+f\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\nu,\nabla_{\partial_{\beta}}\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\nabla^{N}f,\partial_{\beta}\rangle-f\langle R(\partial_{\alpha},\nu)\nu,\partial_{\beta}\rangle+fg^{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}\mathrm{II}_{\alpha\gamma}\mathrm{II}_{\gamma^{\prime}\beta}.

So

(62)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H&=g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathrm{II}_{\alpha\beta}+(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g^{\alpha\beta})\mathrm{II}_{\alpha\beta}\\
&=-\Delta^{N}f-f\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)-f|\mathrm{II}|^{2}\\
&=\frac{\Delta^{N}(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})}{(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})^{2}}-\frac{2|\nabla^{N}(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})|^{2}}{(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})^{3}}-\frac{\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+|\mathrm{II}|^{2}}{H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma}}.\end{split}

Thus

(63)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}&\frac{(H-|\nu|^{2}_{\sigma})^{2}}{2}\\

E_{t}^{+}\subset(E_{t})_{\Omega}^{\prime}
up to a set of measure zero.

∎

A.5.
Upper bound by smooth solution

In this section, we prove

Lemma A.12
.

Let
E_{0}
be a precompact open set in
M
such that
\partial E_{0}
is
C^{2}
with
H-|h|>0
and
E_{0}=E_{0}^{\prime}
. Then any weak solution
(E_{t})_{0<t<\infty}
of the
(|h|g)
-IMCF with initial condition
E_{0}
is bounded from above by the
C^{2,\alpha}
-classical solution for a short time, provided that
E_{t}
remains precompact for a short time.

Proof.

Let
\bar{u}
be the
C^{2,\alpha}
-classical solution of the equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{\nabla\bar{u}}{|\nabla\bar{u}|}\right)=|\nabla\bar{u}|+|h|\text{ on }\{0\leq\bar{u}<t_{0}\},

and let
u
be a weak solution in the sense that
u
minimizes
J^{|h|}_{u}
among competitors on
M\setminus E_{0}
. Assume that
|\nabla\bar{u}|\geq c_{0}>0
.

Set
W=\{0\leq\bar{u}<t_{0}\}
. Since
E_{0}=E_{0}^{\prime}
, by Lemma
5.6
,
E_{0}=E_{0}^{+}
. Since
E_{t}
is precompact,
E_{t}
converges to
E_{0}^{+}
in Hausdorff distance as
t\searrow 0
, which shows that
E_{t}\subset\subset W
for all small
0\leq t\leq t_{1}
.

We modify the proof of
[
HI01
, Theorem 2.2]
to show that
u\leq\bar{u}
in a small neighborhood of
E_{0}
. Observe that for
u^{t}=\min(u,t)
, we have
\{u^{t}<s\}
minimizes
J^{|h|}_{u^{t}}
for all
s
, thus
u^{t}
minimizes
J^{|h|}_{u^{t}}
. For a fixed small
t_{1}<t_{0}
, let
\Omega:=\{\bar{u}<t_{1}\}\setminus\bar{E}_{0}
. For each
\delta>0
, we have
\{u^{t_{1}}>\bar{u}+\delta\}\subset\subset\Omega
. It’s enough to show that
u^{t_{1}}\leq\bar{u}+\delta
on
\Omega
.

Set
u_{2}=u^{t_{1}}
and
u_{1}=\bar{u}+\delta
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, consider
u_{1}^{\epsilon}:=(1-\epsilon)^{-1}u_{1}
. Then
\{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}\}\subset\subset\Omega
. For any Lipschitz function
w\geq u_{1}^{\epsilon}
with
\{w\neq u_{1}^{\epsilon}\}\subset\subset\Omega
, we have
J^{|h|}_{u_{1}}(u_{1})\leq J^{|h|}_{u_{1}}((1-\epsilon)w)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\int|\nabla u_{1}|+u_{1}\left(|\nabla u_{1}|+|h|\right)\leq(1-\epsilon)\int|\nabla w|+w\left(|\nabla u_{1}|+|h|\right),

which is equivalent to

(79)

\displaystyle\int|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+u_{1}^{\epsilon}\left(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+|h|\right)+\epsilon\int\left(w-u_{1}^{\epsilon}\right)|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|\leq\int|\nabla w|+w\left(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+|h|\right).

Replace
w
by
u_{1}^{\epsilon}+(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})_{+}
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+u_{1}^{\epsilon}\left(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+|h|\right)+\epsilon\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}\left(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon}\right)|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla u_{2}|+u_{2}\left(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+|h|\right).

Using
u_{2}-(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})_{+}
as a competitor of
u_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla u_{2}|+u_{2}\left(|\nabla u_{2}|+|h|\right)\leq\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+u_{1}^{\epsilon}\left(|\nabla u_{2}|+|h|\right).

Adding these, we get

(80)

\displaystyle\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})\left(|\nabla u_{2}|-|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|\right)+\epsilon\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}\left(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon}\right)|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|\leq 0.

Using
u_{1}^{\epsilon}+(u_{2}-s-u_{1}^{\epsilon})_{+},s\geq 0
as a competitor in (
79
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{u_{2}-s>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+u_{1}^{\epsilon}\left(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+|h|\right)\leq\int_{u_{2}-s>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla u_{2}|+(u_{2}-s)\left(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+|h|\right),

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{u_{2}-s>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}\left(1+u_{1}^{\epsilon}-u_{2}+s\right)|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|\leq\int_{u_{2}-s>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}|\nabla u_{2}|+(u_{2}-s-u_{1}^{\epsilon})|h|.

Integrating over
s
and switching the order of integration, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{\{u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon}>s\}}(1+u_{1}^{\epsilon}-u_{2}+s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}|\nabla u_{2}|\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{\{u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon}>s\}}+\int_{M}|h|\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{\{u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon}>s\}}(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon}-s)ds,

which yields

\displaystyle\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}\left((u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})-\frac{1}{2}(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})^{2}\right)|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|\leq\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})|\nabla u_{2}|+\frac{1}{2}|h|(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})^{2},

i.e.

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})^{2}\left(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+|h|\right)\leq\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})\left(|\nabla u_{2}|-|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|\right).

Inserting this into (
80
) gives

(81)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})^{2}\left(|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|+|h|\right)+\epsilon\int_{u_{2}>u_{1}^{\epsilon}}\left(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon}\right)|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|\leq 0.

Since
|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|\geq c_{0}>0
, if
u_{2}<u_{1}^{\epsilon}+\epsilon^{2}
for a small enough
\epsilon
, we have
\frac{1}{2}(u_{2}-u_{1}^{\epsilon})|h|\leq C\epsilon^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}c_{0}\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{4}\epsilon|\nabla u_{1}^{\epsilon}|
, which yields a contradiction. We conclude that
u_{2}\leq u_{1}+\epsilon^{2}
implies
u_{2}\leq u_{1}
. For general
u_{2}
, by subtracting a constant we can arrange that
0<\sup(u_{2}-u_{1})\leq\epsilon^{2}
, contradicting what has just been proved. This proves that
u_{2}\leq u_{1}
on
\Omega
for any
\delta>0
. Taking
\delta\to 0
, we have
u\leq\bar{u}
on
\Omega
.

∎

In this subsection, we provide details of the proof of several lemmas concerning the asymptotic behavior of weak solutions.
