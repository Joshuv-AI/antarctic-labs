Delineating neutral and charged mesons in magnetic fields Preprint: KEK-TH-2822
Toru Kojo, Sakura Itatani

Abstract
We investigate the properties of neutral and charged mesons in magnetic fields, from weak-field to strong-field regimes. To develop analytic insights, we employ a non-relativistic quark model with a confining potential of the harmonic oscillator type. Short-range correlations, such as Coulomb and color-magnetic interactions, are treated as perturbations. In particular, we focus on the magnetic field dependence of the relative and the center-of-mass motions. The qualitative trends differ significantly between neutral and charged mesons: for neutral mesons, the transverse momenta are conserved and continuous, while charged mesons exhibit quantized transverse dynamics. The Zeeman effects, arising from intrinsic spins and orbital angular momenta, are carefully examined. In particular, for charged mesons with spins s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 , we discuss how the zero-point energy in the internal quark motion cancels the Zeeman energy from the orbital angular momentum, ensuring the energetic stability of mesons with high spins. The effectively reduced dimensionality of these mesons in the strong-field limit is also discussed.

II
Preparation: Kinetic motions of charged particles

n_{+}

In this section we summarize our setup,

This assumption is supported by the lattice results for the chiral condensate at

,

where the overall factor

II.1
Single charged particle

First we consider a single charged particle.
We consider a non-relativistic Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\rm single}=m+\frac{\,\bm{\Pi}^{2}\,}{\,2m\,}-\bm{\mu}\cdot\bm{B}\,,

(1)

where
B
is constant and applied in the
z
-direction.
The kinetic momentum and magnetic moment are

\displaystyle\bm{\Pi}={\bm{p}}-e\bm{A}\,,~~~~~\bm{\mu}=\frac{\,e\,}{\,2m\,}\bm{\sigma}\,,~~~~~\bm{B}={\bm{\nabla}}\times\bm{A}\,,

(2)

respectively.
(We take Landé g-factor to be
g=2
, treating the constituent quark as an elementary Dirac fermion.)

We define a pseudomomentum
Alford and Strickland 2013
;
Kojo 2021

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}\equiv\bm{\Pi}+e\bm{B}\times\bm{r}\equiv e\bm{B}\times\bm{X}\,.

(3)

This can be related to the
guiding center
coordinate

\displaystyle\bm{X}=-\frac{\,\bm{B}\times\bm{\mathcal{K}}\,}{\,eB^{2}\,}\,,~~~~~B=|\bm{B}|\,,

(4)

which characterizes the center of the cyclotron motion.

The commutation rules among
\Pi
and
\mathcal{K}
are

\displaystyle\big[\Pi^{x},\Pi^{y}\big]=\mathrm{i}eB_{z}=-\big[\mathcal{K}^{x},\mathcal{K}^{y}\big]\,,

(5)

and all the other commutators are zero;
in particular,
\big[\Pi^{\alpha},\mathcal{K}^{\beta}\big]=0=\big[\Pi^{\alpha},X^{\beta}\big]
for any sets of
(\alpha,\beta)
.

We note that
\Pi^{x}
and
\Pi^{y}
do not commute with
\bm{\Pi}^{2}

and hence cannot be used as conserved quantities;
indeed, the Lorentz force keeps changing the direction of
kinetic momentum
\bm{\Pi}\sim m\bm{v}
.
Meanwhile,
\bm{\mathcal{K}}
,
or the corresponding guiding center
\bm{X}
, commutes with
\bm{\Pi}^{2}
,
as the location of the center does not affect the energy spectrum.

For explicit computations it is convenient to choose a specific gauge.
Here we take the symmetric gauge

\displaystyle\bm{A}=\frac{\,\bm{B}\times{\bm{r}}\,}{2}\,,

(6)

with which

\displaystyle\bm{\Pi}={\bm{p}}-\frac{\,e\bm{B}\times{\bm{r}}\,}{2}\,,~~~~~~~\bm{\mathcal{K}}={\bm{p}}+\frac{\,e\bm{B}\times{\bm{r}}\,}{2}\,.

(7)

Writing
\bm{\Pi}^{2}=p_{z}^{2}+\bm{\Pi}_{\perp}^{2}
, we note

\displaystyle\bm{\Pi}_{\perp}^{2}={\bm{p}}_{\perp}^{2}+\frac{\,(eB)^{2}\,}{4}{\bm{r}}_{\perp}^{2}-eBL_{z}\,.

(8)

The first two terms correspond to a usual harmonic oscillator in two dimensions.
We first define the creation and annihilation operators for the
x
component through

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptx=\frac{1}{\,\sqrt{2|eB|}\,}\big(a_{x}+a_{x}^{\dagger}\big)\,,~~p_{x}=-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\frac{\,|eB|\,}{2}}\big(a_{x}-a_{x}^{\dagger}\big)\,,

(9)

and also for the
y
component in the same way.
Then we construct the creation and annihilation operators for

+
and
-
circular modes

\displaystyle a_{\pm}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\,\sqrt{2}\,}\big(a_{x}^{\dagger}\pm\mathrm{i}a_{y}^{\dagger}\big)\,,

(10)

with which
the angular momentum can be written as

\displaystyle L_{z}=a_{+}^{\dagger}a_{+}-a_{-}^{\dagger}a_{-}\,.

(11)

In our definition, the orbital magnetic moment points to the
z
direction for the
+
mode, see Fig.
1
.
Now, for later purposes we generalize the Hamiltonian for the transverse dynamics as

\displaystyle H_{\perp}=\frac{\,{\bm{p}}_{\perp}^{2}\,}{\,2m\,}+\frac{\,m\omega_{0}^{2}\,}{2}{\bm{r}}_{\perp}^{2}-\Omega_{0}L_{z}\,.

(12)

For charged particles free from potentials,

\omega_{0}=|eB|/2m
and
\Omega_{0}=eB/2m
.
The spectrum for
H_{\perp}
is

\displaystyle E^{\perp}_{n_{r},\ell_{z}}=\omega_{0}\big(2n_{r}+1\big)+\omega_{0}\bigg(|\ell_{z}|-\frac{\,\Omega_{0}\,}{\omega_{0}}\ell_{z}\bigg)\,,

(13)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle E^{\perp}_{n_{+},n_{-}}=\big(\omega_{0}-\Omega_{0}\big)n_{+}+\big(\omega_{0}+\Omega_{0}\big)n_{-}+\omega_{0}\,,

(14)

where
2n_{\pm}=2n_{r}+|\ell_{z}|\pm\ell_{z}

with
n_{r},n_{\pm}
being non-negative integers and
\ell_{z}
an integer.
The Zeeman energy associated with the orbital angular motion
reduces the energy when its direction is aligned with that of magnetic fields,
while it costs more energy when the direction is opposite.
In the former case, the increase of
\ell_{z}
costs more energy in the kinetic motion,
but the Zeeman coupling tends to cancel such energy cost.

In particular, for charged particles in the absence of external potential,
we find
\omega_{0}=\Omega_{0}
,
so that such cancellations are exact;
this degeneracy for
\ell_{z}\geq 0

is nothing but the usual Landau degeneracy
for a given Landau level.
For
\omega_{0}=\Omega_{0}
,
another way to understand this degeneracy is
to interpret
n_{+}
as a quantum number for the guiding center coordinates;
they should not affect the spectra because of the translational invariance.
When a charged particle is within an external potential or
interacts with another particle,
this degeneracy is lifted, as we discuss in the following sections.

II.2
Two charged particles

For two-body systems, the structure of our Hamiltonian is (
i,j
are used as a particle label)

\displaystyle H=\sum_{j=1,2}\bigg[m_{j}+\frac{\,\bm{\Pi}^{2}_{j}\,}{\,2m_{j}\,}-\bm{\mu}_{j}\cdot\bm{B}\bigg]+V(r_{12})\,.

(15)

Here we included a potential which depends on the distance
r_{12}=|{\bm{r}}_{1}-{\bm{r}}_{2}|
.

Unlike the single particle case, the potential commutes with neither
\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
nor
\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{2}
,
but commutes with the total pseudomomentum
\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{1}+\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\equiv\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{R}
.
But in general we can label eigenstates of the Hamiltonian only by either
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x}
or
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{y}
,
since
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x}
and
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{y}
do not commute in general,

\displaystyle\big[\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x},\mathcal{K}_{R}^{y}\big]=-\mathrm{i}e_{R}B_{z}\,,~~~~(e_{R}\equiv e_{1}+e_{2})\,.

(16)

Clearly, the exception is the charge neutral case,
e_{R}=0
,
for which
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x}
and
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{y}
can be used simultaneously
to label the eigenstates of the Hamiltonian.

III
Long- and short-range interactions

For two-body forces we separately treat
the long-range and short-range interactions.
Following the standard approach,
we treat the long-range confining potential at leading order
and the short-range part as perturbations.
In vacuum, it is essential to have the confining potential in our unperturbed Hamiltonian;
without it quarks would fly away and the perturbative framework never works.

III.1
Confining potential

For a confining potential, we use the harmonic oscillator
which is standard in non-relativistic quark models,

\displaystyle V_{\rm conf}(r)=\lambda{\bm{r}}^{2}\,,~~~~~\lambda\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{3}\,.

(17)

Our motivation for using the harmonic oscillator comes simply from
its tractability in analytic computations.
More realistically, the confining potential should be linear at long distance
which is necessary to derive the Regge trajectories.
But in this work we restrict ourselves to low-lying states
so that the impact of this simplification should not be significant.

III.2
Short-range potential

Next we turn to short-range interactions.
As in usual quark models, we focus on the color-electric (Coulomb)
and color-magnetic interaction.

III.2.1
Color-electric interaction

The color-electric interaction is

\displaystyle V_{E}(r)=-\frac{\,4\,}{\,3\,}\frac{\,\alpha_{s}\,}{\,r\,}\,.

(18)

At strong magnetic fields,
eB\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}
,
actually the details of short-range interactions
become more important than the long-range interactions.
In fact, the model dependence largely appears from
the treatment of the short-range correlations.

As we discuss later,
we evaluate the expectation values
of the following type,

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\langle 1/r\rangle\sim\Lambda_{z}\mathcal{B}\int\frac{\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{r}}_{\perp}\,}{\,\sqrt{z^{2}+({\bm{r}}_{\perp}-{\bm{r}}_{\perp}^{0})^{2}}\,}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,\Lambda_{z}^{2}\,}{2}z^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\mathcal{B}}{2}{\bm{r}}_{\perp}^{2}}\,,

(19)

where we assume
\Lambda_{z}\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
and
\mathcal{B}^{2}\sim B^{2}+\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{4}
.
The factor
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\mathcal{B}
and the Gaussian functions for
z
and
r_{\perp}
come from the
wavefunctions.
At large
B
, the range of
r_{\perp}
is strongly limited to
\sim\mathcal{B}^{-1/2}
.
Here we consider the case
{\bm{r}}_{\perp}^{0}=0
.
If we neglect the small
r_{\perp}
in the denominator,
we would encounter the integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\int_{0}^{\sim\Lambda_{z}^{-1}}\mathrm{d}z\int_{0}^{\sim\mathcal{B}^{-1/2}}\!\!\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{r}}_{\perp}\,}{\,z\,}\sim\int_{0}^{\sim\Lambda_{z}^{-1}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}z\,}{\,z\,}\,,

(20)

which diverges logarithmically because of the short distance singularity around
z\sim 0
.
For such small
z
, the small
1/\mathcal{B}
term in
r
must be kept to cut off the UV contributions, leaving

\displaystyle\big\langle 1/r\big\rangle\sim\Lambda_{z}\int_{\sim\mathcal{B}^{-1/2}}^{\sim\Lambda_{z}^{-1}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}z\,}{\,z\,}\sim\Lambda_{z}\ln\frac{\,\mathcal{B}\,}{\,\Lambda_{z}^{2}\,}\,.

(21)

This perturbation grows logarithmically with
\mathcal{B}
;
at very large
\mathcal{B}
it would induce too much attraction between a quark and an antiquark,
driving the meson unstable.
Such instability, however, has not been found in lattice simulations.

Although this observation is based on a perturbative evaluation,
it is unlikely that non-perturbative treatment of
1/r
forces cures the inconsistency with the lattice results;
with such improvement the wavefunction should have more concentration
for smaller
r_{\perp}
and the disagreement would become even worse.
Thus, a remedy for the instability, if it exists,
should be found already at the evaluation in perturbative treatments.

A plausible scenario is that the running of coupling constants tempers
the growth of the attraction
Simonov 2017
;
Simonov and Trusov 2015
.
At short distance of
\sim B^{-1/2}
,
the typical momentum transfer is large, and the corresponding value of
\alpha_{s}
is small.
At large
B
, we use the one-loop running coupling (
N_{\rm f}=3
)

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}(Q^{2})=\frac{\,4\pi\,}{\,\beta_{0}\ln\frac{\,Q^{2}\,}{\,\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}\,}\,}\,,~~~~\beta_{0}=11-2N_{\rm f}/3\,,

(22)

taking
Q^{2}\sim\mathcal{B}
.
The logarithm in the denominator behaves as
\sim\ln(\mathcal{B}/\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2})
,
which largely cancels the logarithm appearing in
\langle 1/r\rangle
.
Accordingly the
\langle 1/r\rangle
does not grow indefinitely for
\mathcal{B}\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}
.

In this paper we parametrize the effective momentum transfer as (
\Lambda_{z}^{2}=8\mu\lambda
)

\displaystyle Q_{B}^{2}\equiv m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}+\Lambda_{z}^{2}+c_{B}eB\,,

(23)

and use the coupling
\alpha_{s}(Q_{B}^{2})
to evaluate the short-range interactions.
We try to cover the following situations:
(i) for larger quark masses, the wavefunction tends to be more compact so that
Q^{2}
should be larger;
(ii) for
B\rightarrow 0
and small
m_{1,2}
, the
Q^{2}
should be characterized by the confining scale
\Lambda_{z}
,
(iii) for
B\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}
, the momentum transfer should be large,
Q^{2}\sim B
.

Here, the parameter
c_{B}
is introduced because
our results for neutral mesons, which can have very compact structures in the transverse direction,
are sensitive to which
Q^{2}
are used.
In our numerical estimates we vary
c_{B}
and show how the results are affected.

III.2.2
Color-magnetic interaction

The traditional form of the color-magnetic interaction takes the form

\displaystyle V_{M}^{\rm trad}(r)=\frac{\,32\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,9m_{1}m_{2}\,}\,\tilde{\delta}({\bm{r}})\,\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2}\,,

(24)

where the
\tilde{\delta}(\bm{r})
can be the usual
\delta
-function
or a strongly localized function such as
\sim\mathrm{e}^{-\Lambda_{{\rm UV}}^{2}r^{2}}
.
In momentum space, its Fourier transform leads to
\sim\mathrm{e}^{-q^{2}/\Lambda_{{\rm UV}}^{2}}
, cutting off the momenta beyond
\Lambda_{\rm UV}
.

Since the expression (
24
) is arranged for the low energy regime with momentum transfer up to
\sim\Lambda_{\rm UV}
,
it is not clear whether the above parametrized form is reasonable also for the regime of
B\gg\Lambda_{\rm UV}
;
here the momentum transfer in the transverse direction can be as large as
B
, exceeding the cutoff scale.
Also there is a significant asymmetry between the
z
- and transverse directions.

For this reason we examine the original relativistic expression which eventually leads to Eq. (
24
) in a certain limit.
We start from the spin dependent term in momentum space,

\displaystyle V_{M}^{\rm rela}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,16\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,3E_{1}({\bm{p}}_{1})E_{2}({\bm{p}}_{2})\,}\mathcal{P}_{s}({\bm{q}})

(25)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{s}({\bm{q}})\equiv\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2}-\big(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{1}\big)\big(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2}\big)/{\bm{q}}^{2}\,.

(26)

Here the first factor arises from the vertices for a particle 1 and 2,
\bm{q}
the momentum transfer, and
\bm{p}_{1,2}
the momenta of the particle 1 and 2.
If
\bm{p}_{1,2}
are small, one can make a replacement,
E_{1,2}\rightarrow m_{1,2}
, and take the spatial average
q_{i}q_{j}\rightarrow\delta_{ij}{\bm{q}}^{2}/3
,
with which a constant in momentum space yields the
\delta
-function in coordinate space.
Meanwhile, in the presence of large
B
, the momentum transfer and typical particle momenta are large,
\bm{p}_{1,2}^{2}\sim\bm{q}^{2}\gg m_{1,2}^{2}
.

In order to capture the trend of large momentum transfer while keeping the expression as simple as possible,
we consider the following compromised form

\displaystyle V_{M}({\bm{q}})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\,16\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,3\,}\mathcal{F}_{s}(m_{1},m_{2})\mathcal{P}_{s}({\bm{q}})

(27)

where we introduce a form factor (
\mu_{\rm IR}^{-2}\equiv m_{1}^{-2}+m_{2}^{-2}
)

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt\mathcal{F}_{s}(m_{1},m_{2})\equiv C_{M}\bigg[\frac{\,\mathrm{e}^{-{\bm{q}}^{2}/\mu_{\rm IR}^{2}}\,}{\,m_{1}m_{2}\,}+\frac{\,1-\mathrm{e}^{-{\bm{q}}^{2}/\mu_{\rm IR}^{2}}\,}{\,{\bm{q}}^{2}+c_{m}m_{1}m_{2}\,}\bigg]\,.

(28)

The factor
C_{M}
and
c_{m}
are some factors of
O(1)
.
This form factor behaves as
\mathcal{F}_{s}\rightarrow 1/m_{1}m_{2}
for
{\bm{q}}\rightarrow 0
and

\mathcal{F}_{s}\rightarrow 1/{\bm{q}}^{2}
for
{\bm{q}}\rightarrow\infty
, as requested.
The parameter
c_{m}
is introduced
just for the second term to vanish for
{\bm{q}}\rightarrow 0
.

In practice, we need to compute the following expectation value

\displaystyle\langle V_{M}(r)\rangle=\int_{\bm{r}}|\psi({\bm{r}})|^{2}\int_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}{\bm{q}}\cdot({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{\perp}^{0})}V_{M}({\bm{q}})\,.

(29)

For the ground state, the wavefunction is
|\psi({\bm{r}})|^{2}\sim\Lambda_{z}\mathcal{B}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\Lambda_{z}^{2}}{2}z^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\mathcal{B}}{2}{\bm{r}}_{\perp}^{2}}
,
and we find

\displaystyle\langle V_{M}(r)\rangle

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\bm{q}}V_{M}({\bm{q}})~\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,q_{z}^{2}\,}{\,2\Lambda_{z}^{2}\,}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,{\bm{q}}_{\perp}^{2}\,}{\,2\mathcal{B}\,}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}\rangle\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2}+\langle V^{s}_{\rm II}\rangle\sigma^{z}_{1}\sigma^{z}_{2}\,,

(30)

where (
J={\rm I,II}
)

\displaystyle\langle V_{J}^{s}\rangle=\frac{\,16\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,3\,}\int_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,q_{z}^{2}\,}{\,2\Lambda_{z}^{2}\,}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,{\bm{q}}_{\perp}^{2}\,}{\,2\mathcal{B}\,}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\mathcal{F}_{s}\mathcal{P}_{J}\,,

(31)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\rm I}=\frac{1}{\,2\,}\bigg(1+\frac{\,q_{z}^{2}\,}{\,{\bm{q}}^{2}\,}\bigg)\,,~~~~\mathcal{P}_{\rm II}=\frac{1}{\,2\,}\bigg(1-\frac{\,3q_{z}^{2}\,}{\,{\bm{q}}^{2}\,}\bigg)\,.

(32)

The spin terms were split because the spatial wavefunctions are anisotropic.
At
B=0
and
{\bm{r}}_{\perp}^{0}=0
, one recovers the usual expression
with
\mathcal{P}_{\rm I}\rightarrow 2/3
and
\mathcal{P}_{\rm II}\rightarrow 0
.

The major modification in the present treatment
is the behavior at large
B

which are improved by taking into account the scaling
E_{1,2}\sim|{\bm{q}}|
at large
{\bm{q}}
.
The integration over
q_{z}
yields a factor
\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
.
The integration for
{\bm{q}}_{\perp}
is divided to
|{\bm{q}}_{\perp}|<m_{1,2}

and
m_{1,2}<|{\bm{q}}_{\perp}|<\sqrt{B}
.
They yield

\displaystyle\langle V_{J}^{s}\rangle\sim c_{1}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}+c_{2}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\ln\frac{\,B\,}{\,m_{1,2}\,}\,,

(33)

where
c_{1,2}\sim O(1)
.
As in the color-electric case,
we find the logarithmic enhancement for a large
B
,
which is largely cancelled by using the running coupling
\alpha_{s}(B)
.
Thanks to this, the perturbative corrections remain
\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
.

IV
Neutral mesons

In this section we discuss the properties of neutral mesons,
setting
q\equiv-e_{1}=e_{2}
.
As mentioned, the total pseudomomentum is conserved.
Moreover
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x}
and
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{y}
commute with each other.
It is instructive to express
\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{R}
in terms of the guiding centers,

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{R}=\sum_{j=1,2}e_{j}\bm{B}\times\bm{X}_{j}=-\bm{B}_{q}\times\big(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2}\big)\,,

(34)

where we introduced a notation
\bm{B}_{q}=q\bm{B}
.
This means that the distance between two guiding centers,
\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2}
, is conserved,
so that we expect the impact of interactions to appear to be

\langle V\rangle\sim V(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})
.

Below we choose coordinates as

\displaystyle\bm{R}=\frac{\,\sum_{i}m_{i}\bm{r}_{i}\,}{M}\,,~~~~~\bm{\rho}={\bm{r}}_{2}-{\bm{r}}_{1}\,,

(35)

with
M=m_{1}+m_{2}
.
The corresponding momenta are

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{R}=\sum_{i}\bm{p}_{i}\,,~~~~~~~\bm{p}_{\rho}=-\frac{m_{2}}{\,M\,}\bm{p}_{1}+\frac{m_{1}}{\,M\,}\bm{p}_{2}\,.

(36)

We decompose our leading order Hamiltonian into the following pieces

\displaystyle H_{0}=M+H_{z}+H_{\perp}+V_{E}+H_{\rm spin}\,,

(37)

where

\displaystyle H_{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,P_{R_{z}}^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}+\frac{\,(p_{\rho}^{z})^{2}\,}{\,2\mu\,}+\lambda\rho_{z}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle H_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,2}\frac{\,\big(\bm{\Pi}_{j}^{\perp}\big)^{2}\,}{\,2m_{j}\,}+\lambda\rho_{\perp}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle V_{E}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\,4\,}{\,3\,}\frac{\,\alpha_{s}\,}{\,\sqrt{\rho_{z}^{2}+\rho_{\perp}^{2}}\,}\,,

\displaystyle H_{\rm spin}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,B_{q}\,}{\,2\,}\bigg(\frac{\,\sigma_{1}^{z}\,}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{\,\sigma_{2}^{z}\,}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)+V_{M}\,.

(38)

The most nontrivial part is the treatment of
H_{\perp}
,
and we will rewrite it shortly.
After deriving the eigenvalues of
H_{0}
, we construct perturbation theories
for short-range interactions.

IV.1
Transverse motions

It is convenient to label eigenstates of the Hamiltonian by
\bm{K}_{\perp}
such that

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{R}\Phi_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}=\bm{K}_{\perp}\Phi_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}\,.

(39)

Then, in the symmetric gauge

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{R}=\bm{P}_{R}^{\perp}+\frac{\,\bm{B}_{q}\,}{2}\times\bm{\rho}\,.

(40)

The solution of the eigenvalue equation for
\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{R}
is

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}(\bm{R}_{\perp},\bm{\rho})

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg[\,\mathrm{i}\bm{R}_{\perp}\cdot\bigg(\bm{K}_{\perp}-\frac{\,\bm{B}_{q}\,}{2}\times\bm{\rho}\bigg)\,\bigg]\varphi_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}(\bm{\rho})

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Theta_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}(\bm{R}_{\perp},\,\bm{\rho})}\varphi_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}(\bm{\rho})\,,

(41)

where the
R_{\perp}
-dependence is factorized.
Using this form, our eigenvalue equation for the Hamiltonian
Alford and Strickland 2013

\displaystyle H_{\perp}\Phi_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}(\bm{R}_{\perp},\bm{\rho})=E_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}\Phi_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}(\bm{R}_{\perp},\bm{\rho})\,,

(42)

can be transformed into

\displaystyle H_{\perp}^{\rm eff,\,temp}\varphi_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}(\bm{\rho})=E_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}\varphi_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}(\bm{\rho})\,,

(43)

where
H_{\perp}^{\rm eff,\,temp}\equiv\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Theta_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}}H_{\perp}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Theta_{\bm{K}_{\perp}}}
.
The explicit form is
(reduced mass:
\mu^{-1}=m_{1}^{-1}+m_{2}^{-1}
)

\displaystyle H_{\perp}^{\rm eff,\,temp}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\big(\bm{p}^{\perp}_{\rho}\big)^{2}\,}{\,2\mu\,}+\bigg(\frac{\,B_{q}^{2}\,}{\,8\mu\,}+\lambda\bigg)\rho_{\perp}^{2}+\frac{\,K_{\perp}^{2}\,}{\,2\mu\,}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt+\frac{\,B_{q}\,}{\,2\,}\bigg(\frac{1}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{1}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)L_{\rho}^{z}-\frac{\,\bm{B}_{q}\,}{M}\cdot(\bm{\rho}_{\perp}\times{\bm{K}}_{\perp})\,.

(44)

Here we have attached “temp” (temporal) since we will rewrite it shortly
for shifted variables.

This expression can be misleading,
since it would give impressions that
the spectra depend on
\bm{K}_{\perp}
even in the case of
non-interacting limit,
\lambda\rightarrow 0
;
for
\lambda=0
, there should be only two independent
charged particles which yield only quantized spectra.
The present confusing expression comes from
the failure to eliminate all linear terms in dynamical variables.

In eliminating the linear terms,
shifting
\bm{\rho}
by a constant is insufficient, since the attempt to eliminate
the
\bm{\rho}\times\bm{K}_{\perp}
term
yields another linear term in
{\bm{p}}_{\rho}^{\perp}
from
L_{\rho}^{z}
.
We need to shift
\bm{\rho}_{\perp}
and
\bm{p}_{\rho}^{\perp}
at the same time,

\bm{\rho}_{\perp}\rightarrow\bm{\rho}_{\perp}+\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{0}

and
\bm{p}_{\rho}^{\perp}\rightarrow\bm{p}_{\rho}^{\perp}+\bm{p}_{0}^{\perp}
.
This shift with constant terms does not change the commutation relations for
\bm{p}_{\rho}^{\perp}
and
\bm{\rho}_{\perp}
.
Explicitly, the proper shifts turn out to be

\displaystyle\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,{\bm{K}}\times\bm{B}_{q}\,}{\,B_{q}^{2}+2M\lambda\,}\,,

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{0}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,m_{1}-m_{2}\,}{\,2M\,}\frac{\,B_{q}^{2}{\bm{K}}_{\perp}\,}{\,B_{q}^{2}+2M\lambda\,}\,,

(45)

which generate terms that cancel the
K_{\perp}^{2}/2M
term.
One should also notice that these shift also affect the coordinates in

V_{E}
and
V_{M}
;
at large
K_{\perp}
, the
\rho_{\perp}^{0}
serves as a cutoff for short-range interactions.

Now, with new variables the previous
H_{\perp}^{\rm eff,temp}
is replaced with

\displaystyle H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\lambda K_{\perp}^{2}\,}{\,B_{q}^{2}+2M\lambda\,}+\frac{\,(\bm{p}_{\rho}^{\perp})^{2}\,}{\,2\mu\,}+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}^{2}\,}{\,8\mu\,}\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{2}

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{\,\bm{B}_{q}\cdot\bm{L}_{\rho}\,}{\,2\,}\bigg(\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)\,,

(46)

where we introduced a notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{q}^{2}=B_{q}^{2}+8\mu\lambda\,.

(47)

In the present expression, it is clear that

K_{\perp}^{2}
term vanishes for
\lambda=0
;
in this case, only discrete spectra appear,
as they should.

The qualitative meaning of the
K_{\perp}^{2}
term becomes
clearer if we replace
K_{\perp}^{2}
with the guiding center coordinates,

\displaystyle\frac{\,\lambda K_{\perp}^{2}\,}{\,B_{q}^{2}+2M\lambda\,}=\frac{\,\lambda\big(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2}\big)^{2}\,}{\,1+2M\lambda/B_{q}^{2}\,}\,.

(48)

At large
B
, this is nothing but the energy from confining potential
whose separation is specified by the conserved distance
between the guiding centers for two particles.
Note also that the limit
B_{q}\rightarrow 0
reproduces the standard
K_{\perp}^{2}/2M
term.

The spectra take the form

\displaystyle E^{\perp}_{n_{r},\ell_{z}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\lambda K_{\perp}^{2}\,}{\,B_{q}^{2}+2M\lambda\,}+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}{\,2\mu\,}\big(2n_{r}+1\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}{\,2\,}\bigg[\frac{\,|\ell_{z}|\,}{\,\mu\,}+\ell_{z}\frac{\,B_{q}\,}{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}\bigg(\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)\bigg]\,,

(49)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle E_{{\bm{K}}_{\perp}}^{n_{+},n_{-}}\!

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\lambda K_{\perp}^{2}\,}{\,B_{q}^{2}+2M\lambda\,}+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}{\,2\mu\,}

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{\,1\,}{\,2\,}\bigg[\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}{\,\mu\,}+B_{q}\bigg(\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)\bigg]n_{+}

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{\,1\,}{\,2\,}\bigg[\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}{\,\mu\,}+B_{q}\bigg(\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{2}\,}-\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{1}\,}\bigg)\bigg]n_{-}\,.

(50)

From this expression, it is clear that
\lambda\rightarrow 0
yields
the spectra of two independent charged particles.
Note that the coefficients of
n_{+}
and
n_{-}
are manifestly positive definite,
and any internal excitations cost the energy of
\sim B
.
Finally we note that there is a zero-point energy
\mathcal{B}_{q}/2\mu

which, in the ground state, is largely cancelled by the Zeeman energy from
the intrinsic spins.

IV.2
Perturbative corrections and spin dependence

Using the eigenfunctions for
H_{z}+H_{\perp}

with the quantum numbers
\bm{n}=(n_{z},n_{r},\ell_{z})
,
we evaluate the short-range interactions
as the first order of perturbation,

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\langle V_{E}\rangle_{\bm{n}}\,,~~~~\langle V_{M}\rangle_{\bm{n}}=\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}\rangle_{\bm{n}}(\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2})+\langle V^{s}_{\rm II}\rangle_{\bm{n}}\sigma^{z}_{1}\sigma^{z}_{2}\,.

(51)

These expectation values are evaluated numerically.
The electric potential may be simply added to the eigen-energy from
H_{z}
and
H_{\perp}
.
The magnetic part appears in the spin dependent part of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt\langle H_{\rm spin}\rangle_{\bm{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,B_{q}\,}{\,2\,}\bigg(\frac{\,\sigma_{1}^{z}\,}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{\,\sigma_{2}^{z}\,}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)

\displaystyle~~~+\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}\rangle_{\bm{n}}(\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2})+\langle V^{s}_{\rm II}\rangle_{\bm{n}}\sigma^{z}_{1}\sigma^{z}_{2}\,.

(52)

Here
(\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2})
can be diagonalized by a state with the total spin
S
,
and the corresponding eigenvalue is
2\big[S(S+1)-3/2\big]
.
Meanwhile, the Zeeman energy is diagonal only for
|S=1,S_{z}=\pm 1\rangle
;
the Zeeman term converts
|S=0,S_{z}=0\rangle
into
|S=1,S_{z}=0\rangle
and vice versa.

For
|S=1,S_{z}=\pm 1\rangle
, the spectra are

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\big(E_{S=1}^{S_{z}=\pm 1}\big)_{\bm{n}}=\pm\frac{\,B_{q}\,}{\,2\,}\bigg(\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{\,1\,}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)+\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}+V^{s}_{\rm II}\rangle_{\bm{n}}\,.

(53)

For
S_{z}=0
states, we diagonalize the matrix

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}~-3\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}\rangle_{\bm{n}}-\langle V^{s}_{\rm II}\rangle_{\bm{n}}~&~\frac{\,B_{q}\,}{\,2\mu\,}~\\
~\frac{\,B_{q}\,}{\,2\mu\,}~&~\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}\rangle_{\bm{n}}-\langle V^{s}_{\rm II}\rangle_{\bm{n}}~\end{bmatrix}

(54)

and find the spectra (
\langle V^{s}_{\rm I+II}\rangle=\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}+V^{s}_{\rm II}\rangle
)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\big(E_{S=\pm}^{S_{z}=0}\big)_{\bm{n}}=-\langle V^{s}_{\rm I+II}\rangle_{\bm{n}}\pm\sqrt{4\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}\rangle_{\bm{n}}^{2}+\bigg(\frac{\,B_{q}\,}{\,2\mu\,}\bigg)^{2}\,}\,.

(55)

Among all these spectra, the
S_{z}=0
state with the energy
\big(E_{-}^{S_{z}=0}\big)_{\bm{n}}

leads to the smallest energy.

IV.3
Spatial wave functions

Short-range interactions are numerically evaluated
using the wavefunctions of the unperturbed Hamiltonian.
The spatial wavefunctions take the forms

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bm{n}}^{\rm nt}\big(z,\bm{\rho}_{\perp}\big)=\psi_{n_{z}}(z)\phi_{\bm{n}_{\perp}}^{\rm nt}(\bm{\rho}_{\perp})\,,

(56)

where each wavefunction is normalized to one.
The wavefunction in the
z
-direction is

\displaystyle\psi_{n_{z}}(z)=\mathcal{N}_{n_{z}}H_{n_{z}}(z_{\Lambda})\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,\Lambda^{2}\,}{4}z^{2}}\,,

(57)

with
z_{\Lambda}=(2\mu\lambda)^{1/4}z\equiv\Lambda z/\sqrt{2}
, a normalization constant

\mathcal{N}_{n_{z}}=\big(\Lambda/2^{n_{z}}n_{z}!\sqrt{2\pi}\big)^{1/2}
,
and
H_{n_{z}}
being the Hermite polynomials.
For the relative motion in the transverse direction,
the unperturbed wavefunctions for neutral mesons
take the form

\displaystyle\phi_{\bm{n}_{\perp}}^{\rm nt}(\bm{\rho}_{\perp})=\mathcal{N}^{{\rm nt}}_{\bm{n}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\ell_{z}\theta}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{|\ell_{z}|}L_{n_{\perp}}^{|\ell_{z}|}\big(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\big)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}{4}\rho_{\perp}^{2}}\,,

(58)

where
L_{n_{\perp}}^{|\ell_{z}|}
is the associated Laguerre polynomials
and the normalization constant is

\mathcal{N}^{{\rm nt}}_{\bm{n}_{\perp}}=[\,\mathcal{B}_{q}n_{\perp}!/2\pi(n_{\perp}+|\ell_{z}|)!\,]^{1/2}
.
The effective transverse coordinate is
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}=\rho_{\perp}(\mathcal{B}_{q}/2)^{1/2}

with the parameter
\mathcal{B}_{q}
which was given in Eq. (
47
).

We are interested in the modes with
n_{z}=n_{\perp}=l_{z}=0

for which
H_{n_{z}=0}=1
and
L_{n_{\perp}=0}^{|\ell_{z}|}=1
.
We use the following wavefunction

\displaystyle\Phi_{\rm gs}^{\rm nt}\big(z,\bm{\rho}_{\perp}\big)=\mathcal{N}_{n_{z}}\mathcal{N}^{{\rm nt}}_{\bm{n}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,\Lambda^{2}\,}{4}z^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}{4}\rho_{\perp}^{2}}\,,

(59)

to evaluate the color-electric and magnetic interactions.
The analytic structure was examined in Sec.
III.2
.

Now we summarize the integral to be evaluated.
The expressions suitable for numerical evaluations are summarized in Appendix.
B
.
Including the shift
\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{0}\sim O(K_{\perp}/B)
in Eq. (
45
),
the color-electric energy is (
n_{z}=n_{\perp}=0
)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\langle V_{E}\rangle=-\frac{\,4\alpha_{s}\,}{3}|\mathcal{N}_{n_{z}}|^{2}|\mathcal{N}^{{\rm nt}}_{\bm{n}_{\perp}}|^{2}\int_{{\bm{r}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\,\Lambda^{2}\,}{2}z^{2}-\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}{2}\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{2}}\,}{\sqrt{\rho_{z}^{2}+(\bm{\rho}_{\perp}+\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{0})^{2}}}\,,

(60)

whose qualitative trend was discussed in Sec.
III.2.1
.

The color-magnetic energy is

\displaystyle\langle V_{M}\rangle=\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}\rangle\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2}+\langle V_{\rm II}^{s}\rangle\sigma^{z}_{1}\sigma^{z}_{2}\,,

(61)

with (
J={\rm I,II}
)

\displaystyle\langle V_{J}^{s}\rangle=\frac{\,16\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,3\,}\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{q_{z}^{2}}{\,2\Lambda^{2}\,}-\frac{\,{\bm{q}}_{\perp}^{2}\,}{\,2\mathcal{B}_{q}\,}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\mathcal{F}_{s}\mathcal{P}_{J}\,,

(62)

where
\mathcal{F}_{s}
and
\mathcal{P}_{J}
are given in Eqs. (
28
) and (
32
), respectively.

The numerical evaluation is shown shortly.

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptE_{\rm gs}^{B=0}(0)=M+\langle V_{E}\rangle^{B=0}+\frac{\,3\,}{\,2\,}\sqrt{\frac{\,2\lambda\,}{\,\mu\,}}-3\langle V_{\rm I}^{s}\rangle^{B=0}\,.

(65)

At

,

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptE_{\rm gs}^{B\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}}(0)\simeq M+\langle V_{E}\rangle+\frac{\,1\,}{\,2\,}\sqrt{\frac{\,2\lambda\,}{\,\mu\,}}-\langle V_{\rm I+II}^{s}\rangle\,,

(66)

where
O(1/B)

The ground state spectra of
u\bar{u}
,

, and

.

,

To understand this behavior, it is instructive to switch off the short-range interactions by setting
\alpha_{s}\rightarrow 0
(Fig.

, dashed lines).
With increasing

, the zero-point energy of the transverse motion and the Zeeman energy largely cancel,
so the energy at large
B
reduces as

\displaystyle\frac{\,3\,}{\,2\,}\sqrt{\frac{\,2\lambda\,}{\,\mu\,}}~\rightarrow~\frac{\,1\,}{\,2\,}\sqrt{\frac{\,2\lambda\,}{\,\mu\,}}+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{q}-B_{q}\,}{\,2\mu\,}\,,

(67)

where
\mathcal{B}_{q}-B_{q}\sim\mu\lambda/B_{q}
is negligible at large
B
.
Hence the reduction at
\alpha_{s}=0
may be understood
as the effective disappearance of the transverse kinetic energy.
For instance, the energy reduction in the large
B
limit is

\sqrt{2\lambda/\mu}\simeq 0.272
(0.215) GeV for the
u\bar{u}
(
s\bar{s}
) meson.

Now we switch on the short-range interactions, see
Figs.

and
3

for
u\bar{u}
,
d\bar{s}
, and
s\bar{s}
spectra.
Their impact sensitively depend on our choice of
Q^{2}
in the running coupling
\alpha_{s}(Q^{2})
,
as we have emphasized in Sec.
III.2
.
If we neglect the running of
\alpha_{s}
at a small transverse distance of
\sim B^{-1/2}
,
the energy reduction caused by the short-range interactions becomes too large (see the dotted lines in Fig.
3
);
the spectrum in the
u\bar{u}
channel becomes even unstable.
This too large reduction in the mass is tempered by using
\alpha_{s}
with
Q^{2}\sim B
.
While it is sensible to set
Q^{2}\sim B
, we do not know precisely the numerical coefficient in front of
B
.
For this reason we vary the parameter
c_{B}
for
Q_{B}^{2}
in Eq. (
23
) for the range to be compatible with the lattice results.
In Fig.
3
, the band for the range
c_{B}\in[0.5,2.0]
is shown to display the impact of our choice on
Q_{B}
.
Lighter mesons are more sensitive to
Q_{B}
.

Finally we take a look at the ground-state energy for the
u\bar{u}
meson at finite transverse momentum
{\bm{K}}_{\perp}

which is related to the distance between the two guiding centers as
|\bm{X}|\sim|{\bm{K}}_{\perp}|/B_{q}
.
The energy shift at finite
\bm{\rho}_{\perp}
originates from two competing factors:
(i) a reduction in the transverse center-of-mass kinetic energy [Eq. (
48
)],
and (ii) an energy penalty arising from the weakened short-range attraction at finite
\bm{\rho}_{\perp}
.
The results are shown in Fig.
4
.
As the magnetic field
B
increases,
the energy dependence on
K_{\perp}
becomes less pronounced.
In the limit of
B\rightarrow\infty
, the energy becomes independent of
K_{\perp}
,
reflecting the effective dimensional reduction in the energy spectra.

V
Charged mesons

For charged mesons, the analyses of the spectra are much more involved.

\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{R}
is conserved as in the neutral meson case,
but the
x
- and
y
-components do not commute,
[\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x},\mathcal{K}_{R}^{y}]\neq 0
.
Below we choose the eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x}
to label the eigenstates of the Hamiltonian.
We look for the eigenstates of
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{x}_{R}\Phi_{K_{x}}=K_{x}\Phi_{K_{x}}\,.

(68)

We expect a charged meson to circulate in a closed orbit
with which the spectra should be quantized.
Meanwhile, the eigenvalue equation for
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x}
seems to yield a continuous eigenvalue
K_{x}
.
Combining these observations,
we expect that the spectra become independent of
K_{x}
, making the spectra discrete.
We confirm this expectation shortly.

V.1
Transverse motions

For charged systems in coordinates
\bm{R}
and
{\bm{r}}
, the pseudomomentum takes the form
(in the symmetric gauge)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{R}=\bm{P}_{R}+\frac{\,\bm{B}\,}{2}\times\big(e_{R}\bm{R}+e_{\rho}\bm{\rho}\big)\,,

(69)

where we write

\displaystyle e_{\rho}=\frac{\,e_{2}m_{1}-e_{1}m_{2}\,}{M}\,.

(70)

We look for the eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{K}_{R}^{x}
.
The equation to be solved is

\displaystyle\bigg[-\mathrm{i}\frac{\partial}{\,\partial R_{x}\,}-\frac{B}{2}\big(e_{R}R_{y}+e_{\rho}\rho_{y}\big)\bigg]\Phi_{K_{x}}=K_{x}\Phi_{K_{x}}\,.

(71)

The solution takes the form

\displaystyle\Phi_{K_{x}}(\bm{R},\bm{\rho})

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg[\mathrm{i}R_{x}\bigg(K_{x}+\frac{B}{2}\big(e_{R}R_{y}+e_{\rho}\rho_{y}\big)\bigg)\bigg]\varphi_{K_{x}}(R_{y},\bm{\rho})

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Theta_{c}}\varphi_{K_{x}}(R_{y},\bm{\rho})\,.

(72)

As we have done for the neutral meson case, we convert

\displaystyle H_{c\perp}\Phi_{K_{x}}(R_{y},\bm{\rho})=E_{K_{x}}\Phi_{K_{x}}(R_{y},\bm{\rho})\,,

(73)

into

\displaystyle H_{c\perp}^{\rm eff,\,temp}\varphi_{K_{x}}(\bm{\rho})=E_{K_{x}}\varphi_{K_{x}}(\bm{\rho})\,,

(74)

where
H_{c\perp}^{\rm eff,\,temp}\equiv\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Theta_{K_{x}}}H_{c\perp}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Theta_{K_{x}}}
.

In the Hamiltonian, the
K_{x}
comes out when we act
P_{R}^{x}
on the phase
\Theta_{c}
.
We look at
\Pi_{j}^{x}
(
j=1,2
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Theta_{c}}\Pi_{j}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Theta_{c}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,m_{j}\,}{M}\bigg[K_{x}+Be_{R}R_{y}+P_{R}^{x}\bigg]+\frac{B}{2}e_{\rho}\rho_{y}

\displaystyle-\eta_{j}\bigg[p_{\rho}^{x}+\frac{B}{2}e_{\rho}R_{y}+\frac{B}{2}\frac{\,\mu\,}{\,M\,}e_{R}\rho_{y}\bigg]\,,

(75)

where
\eta_{1}=-\eta_{2}=1
.
Now we consider a constant shift of the operators
R_{y}
and
p_{\rho}^{x}
as

\displaystyle R_{y}\rightarrow R_{y}-\frac{K_{x}}{\,Be_{R}\,}\,,~~~~p_{\rho}^{x}\rightarrow p_{\rho}^{x}+\frac{\,e_{\rho}\,}{\,2e_{R}\,}K_{x}\,,

(76)

with which the
K_{x}
term disappears

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Theta_{c}}\Pi_{j}^{x}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Theta_{c}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\frac{\,m_{j}\,}{M}\bigg[Be_{R}R_{y}+P_{R}^{x}\bigg]+\frac{B}{2}e_{\rho}\rho_{y}

\displaystyle~~~-\eta_{j}\bigg[p_{\rho}^{x}+\frac{B}{2}e_{\rho}R_{y}+\frac{B}{2}\frac{\,\mu\,}{\,M\,}e_{R}\rho_{y}\bigg]\,.

(77)

The other terms in the Hamiltonian do not contain
K_{x}
terms
and are unaffected by the above constant shifts.
Hence we verified that the spectra are independent of the value of
K_{x}
.
For this reason, below we no longer attach the subscript
K_{x}

to the energy and states.

After some calculations including the above-mentioned shifts, we arrive at

\displaystyle H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,(P_{R}^{y})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}+\bigg[\frac{\,(Be_{R})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}+\frac{\,(Be_{\rho})^{2}\,}{\,8\mu\,}\bigg]R_{y}^{2}+\frac{\,({\bm{p}}_{\rho}^{\perp})^{2}\,}{\,2\mu\,}+\bigg[\frac{\,\mu(Be_{R})^{2}\,}{\,8M^{2}\,}+\lambda+\frac{(Be_{\rho})^{2}}{\,8\mu\,}-\frac{\,\mu B^{2}e_{\rho}e_{R}\,}{\,4M\,}\bigg(\frac{1}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{1}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)\bigg]\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{2}

\displaystyle~~~+\bigg[-\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}+\frac{\,Be_{\rho}\,}{\,2\,}\bigg(\frac{1}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{1}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)\bigg]L_{\rho}^{z}

\displaystyle~~~-\frac{\,3(Be_{\rho})^{2}\,}{\,8M\,}\rho_{x}^{2}+\bigg[\frac{\,5B^{2}e_{R}e_{\rho}\,}{\,4M\,}-\frac{\,(Be_{\rho})^{2}\,}{\,4\,}\bigg(\frac{1}{\,m_{1}\,}-\frac{1}{\,m_{2}\,}\bigg)\bigg]\rho_{y}R_{y}+\frac{\,Be_{\rho}\,}{\,2\mu\,}R_{y}p_{\rho}^{x}-\frac{\,Be_{\rho}\,}{\,2M\,}\rho_{x}P_{R}^{y}\,.

(78)

The terms in the first two lines are axially symmetric and can be analytically diagonalized.
The complication comes from the third line which is not axially symmetric.
The full Hamiltonian contains anisotropic terms and the coupling between
the center-of-mass and internal motion.
In principle one can diagonalize analytically but the expression is too complicated.
We first consider the
e_{\rho}=0
case for which we can derive the spectra analytically.
Then, we derive the full spectra numerically.

V.2
Analytic insights:
e_{\rho}=0
case

The
e_{\rho}=0
case corresponds to
m_{1}=m_{2}=M/2
and
e_{1}=e_{2}=e_{R}/2
,
which are not realized in physical mesonic systems.
(For diquarks made of identical quarks, this condition can be satisfied
Cao and Wu 2025
.)
But it is still useful to study this case as a baseline to diagonose a more general
e_{\rho}\neq 0
case.

The energy spectra take the form

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544ptE_{n_{R},\bm{n}_{c}}^{S,S_{z}}(K_{z})=M+E_{K_{z}}^{n_{z}}+E_{n_{R}}^{\bm{n}_{\perp}}+\langle V_{E}\rangle_{\bm{n}_{c}}+\big(E_{S}^{S_{z}}\big)_{\bm{n}_{c}}\,.

(79)

The short-range potentials are evaluated
using the eigenfunctions of
H_{0}^{\perp}
.
The Hamiltonian at
e_{\rho}=0
is

\displaystyle H^{\perp}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,(P_{R}^{y})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}+\frac{\,(Be_{R})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}R_{y}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\,2({\bm{p}}_{\rho}^{\perp})^{2}\,}{\,M\,}+\bigg[\frac{\,(Be_{R})^{2}\,}{\,32M\,}+\lambda\,\bigg]\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{2}-\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}L_{\rho}^{z}\,,

(80)

which yields the following spectrum

\displaystyle\big(E_{n_{R}}^{\bm{n}_{\perp}}\big)_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}\big(2n_{R}+1\big)

\displaystyle~+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}(2n_{r}+|\ell_{z}|+1)-\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}\ell_{z}\,,

(81)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\big(E_{n_{R}}^{\bm{n}_{\perp}}\big)_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}\big(2n_{R}+1\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}(n_{+}+n_{-}+1)-\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}(n_{+}-n_{-})\,,

(82)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{R}=\sqrt{(Be_{R})^{2}+32M\lambda\,}\,.

(83)

The angular momentum in a meson
tends to cancel the zero-point energy from the kinetic motion.
At large
B
, states with
\ell_{z}\geq 0
are almost degenerate
with a small splitting due to
\mathcal{B}_{R}-Be_{R}\sim\lambda M/B
.
Thus, at large
B
, there are many low energy excitations with
\ell_{z}\geq 0
.

There are also contributions from the spin-dependent term
\big(E_{S}^{S_{z}}\big)_{\bm{n}_{c}}
.
The spin term is diagonalized by
(S,S_{z})
,

\displaystyle\big(E_{S}^{S_{z}}\big)_{\bm{n}_{c},0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,M\,}S_{z}+\langle V^{s}_{\rm I}\rangle_{\bm{n}_{c}}\big[\,2S(S+1)-3\,\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+4\langle V^{s}_{\rm II}\rangle_{\bm{n}_{c}}S^{z}_{1}S^{z}_{2}\,.

(84)

At large
B
, the ground state is given by the
S=S_{z}=1
state
in which the Zeeman energy
exactly cancels the zero-point energy from the center-of-mass motion
and almost completely cancels that from the relative motion.

The low-lying spectra which are insensitive to
B
are given
by
n_{R}=n_{r}=0
modes with
\ell_{z}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptE_{\ell_{z}\geq 0}^{n_{R}=n_{r}=0}

\displaystyle=M+E_{K_{z}=0}^{n_{z}=0}+\big\langle V_{E}+V_{\rm I+II}^{s}\big\rangle_{n_{r}=0,\ell_{z}}

\displaystyle~~+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}-Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}\big(1+|\ell_{z}|\big)\,.

(85)

The main findings from this special setup for large
B
are:
(i) for
\ell_{z}>0
, the orbital kinetic energy and Zeeman energy tends to
cancel, leaving small excitation energies of
\sim\lambda/B

which vanish for non-interacting limit,
\lambda\rightarrow 0
;
(ii) the zero-point energy of the center-of-mass motion
cancels exactly with the half of the Zeeman energy;
(iii) the zero-point energy of the relative-motion
largely cancels with the half of the Zeeman energy;
(iv) in the limit of
\lambda\rightarrow 0
,
the energy levels of
\ell_{z}\geq 0
are all degenerate.

V.3
Perturbative expansion with respect to
e_{\rho}

For general cases of
e_{1}\neq e_{2}
and
m_{1}\neq m_{2}
,
the expressions become complicated.
Still, if
\delta e=e_{1}-e_{2}
and
\delta m=m_{1}-m_{2}

can be regarded as small,
one can analytically compute corrections to the
e_{\rho}=0
results.
The
e_{\rho}
may be expressed as

\displaystyle e_{\rho}=\frac{\,e_{R}\delta m\,}{\,2M\,}-\frac{\,\delta e\,}{\,2\,}\,,

(86)

which is the sum of
\delta e
and
\delta m
corrections.
In the light quark sector, the former is a good approximation,
and the latter may be regarded as the
1/N_{\rm c}
corrections,
since
e_{u,d}=(\pm 1+1/N_{\rm c})/2
;
for charged combinations (
u,\bar{d}
) and (
d,\bar{u}
),
the charge difference is
\delta e=e/N_{\rm c}
.

Computing to the second order of these perturbations,
we find the correction to the ground state energy at large
B
, with the quanta
n_{R}=n_{r}=\ell_{z}=0
and
S=S_{z}=1
, is
(for details, see Appendix.
A
)

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\delta E_{\ell_{z}=0}^{n_{R}=n_{r}=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\,(Be_{\rho})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}\bigg[\frac{\,\big(\frac{\,\xi\,}{2}-\frac{\,7\,}{\,2\xi\,}\big)^{2}\,}{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}+Be_{R}\,}+\frac{\,\big(\frac{\,\xi\,}{2}+\frac{\,3\,}{\,2\xi\,}\big)^{2}\,}{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}+3Be_{R}\,}\bigg]

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{\,Be_{\rho}^{2}\,}{\,4Me_{R}\,}\frac{\,\xi^{2}+10\,}{\,\xi^{2}\,}+\frac{\,8\delta m^{2}\lambda\,}{\,M^{2}Be_{R}\xi^{2}\,}\,,

(87)

where
\xi=\big[1+32M\lambda/(Be_{R})^{2}\big]^{1/4}
.
In the limit of
B\rightarrow\infty
, the scaling of parameters is

\mathcal{B}_{R}\rightarrow Be_{R}
,
\xi\rightarrow 1
.
In this limit the terms of
O(B)
cancel, leaving

\displaystyle\delta E_{\ell_{z}=0}^{n_{R}=n_{r}=0}~\sim~O(\lambda/B)~~~~~~(B\rightarrow\infty)\,.

(88)

Hence the insensitivity of the ground-state energy to
B
, as seen for the
e_{\rho}=0
case,
remains valid to
O(\delta e^{2},\delta m^{2})
.
This conclusion holds also for
\ell_{z}>0
with
n_{R}=n_{r}=0
.
This result indicates that
for general cases with
e_{\rho}\neq 0
,
the overall trend of the spectra remains similar as the
e_{\rho}=0
cases.

V.4
General spectra for
e_{\rho}\neq 0

The full Hamiltonian for the transverse kinetic term takes a complicated form
and we derive the spectra numerically.
What we need is the eigenfrequencies and the corresponding wavefunctions which
are utilized to compute the expectation values of the short-range interactions.

V.4.1
Eigenfrequencies

First we define

X=(\rho_{x},\rho_{y},R_{y},p_{\rho}^{x},p_{\rho}^{y},P_{R}^{y})

and write the Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{\,2\,}X_{i}A_{ij}X_{j}\,,~~~~~A_{ij}=A_{ji}\,.

(89)

The commutation relations for
X
’s are

\displaystyle[X_{i},X_{j}]=\mathrm{i}J_{ij}\,,~~~~~J=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{\bf 3}~\\
~-I_{\bf 3}&0\end{bmatrix}\,,

(90)

where
J
is the fundamental symplectic matrix.
Our goal is to convert the Hamiltonian into the form

\displaystyle H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\omega_{\alpha}\bigg(b^{\dagger}_{\alpha}b_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\,2\,}\bigg)\,,

(91)

with the constraint
[b_{\alpha},b_{\beta}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{\alpha\beta}
,
and find the relation between
X
and
(b,b^{\dagger})
,

\displaystyle X_{i}=T_{i\alpha}\tilde{b}_{\alpha}\,,~~~~~~~\tilde{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{1}^{\dagger},b_{2}^{\dagger},b_{3}^{\dagger})\,.

(92)

We can also express the commutation relation as
[\tilde{b}_{\alpha},\tilde{b}_{\beta}]=J_{\alpha\beta}
.
This imposes the condition
J=TJT^{T}

which is satisfied in our ordering of operators in
\tilde{b}
.
The rest is the normalization of overall coefficients.

To find the spectra, we examine the Heisenberg equation

\displaystyle\dot{X}_{k}=\mathrm{i}\big[H_{\perp}^{\rm eff},X_{k}\big]=(JA)_{kj}X_{j}\,.

(93)

We consider a unitary transformation

X_{k}=U_{k\alpha}\tilde{X}_{\alpha}
with
U^{-1}(JA)U={\rm diag}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{6})
,

\displaystyle\dot{\tilde{X}}_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}\tilde{X}_{\alpha}~\rightarrow~\tilde{X}_{\alpha}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{\alpha}t}\tilde{X}_{\alpha}(0)\,.

(94)

We note that, if some component has the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\alpha}
, there is also a component having the eigenvalue
-\lambda_{\alpha}
.
We further note that
\tilde{X}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}
has the eigenvalue
\lambda^{*}_{\alpha}
.
These conditions are fulfilled for
\lambda_{\alpha}=\mathrm{i}\omega_{\alpha}
so that
\lambda_{\alpha}^{*}=-\lambda_{\alpha}
.
Now we can set

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{1,2,3}=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\,,~~~~~~\tilde{X}_{4,5,6}=(b_{1}^{\dagger},b_{2}^{\dagger},b_{3}^{\dagger})

(95)

where
\tilde{X}_{1-3}
and
\tilde{X}_{4-6}
have the eigenvalues
-\mathrm{i}\omega_{1-3}
and
\mathrm{i}\omega_{1-3}
, respectively,
and the unitary matrix
U
turns out to be the matrix
T
in Eq. (
92
).
These frequencies are nothing but those in Eq. (
91
).
Now Eq. (
91
) satisfies the time evolution,

\displaystyle b_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega_{\alpha}t}b_{\alpha}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}t}b_{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}t}\,,

\displaystyle b_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega_{\alpha}t}b_{\alpha}^{\dagger}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}t}b_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}t}\,,

(96)

as requested in Eq. (
94
).

V.4.2
Wavefunctions

To find the ground state wavefunctions for the transverse Hamiltonian, we look for the state such that

\displaystyle b_{\alpha}|0_{\perp}\rangle=T^{-1}_{\alpha i}X_{i}|0_{\perp}\rangle=0\,,~~~~~~(\alpha=1,2,3)\,.

(97)

Writing
x_{\alpha}=(\bm{\rho}_{\perp},R_{y})
and
p_{\alpha}=(\bm{p}_{\rho},P_{R}^{y})
,
this takes the form

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=1}^{3}\big(f_{\alpha\beta}x_{\beta}+g_{\alpha\beta}\,p_{\beta}\big)|0_{\perp}\rangle=0\,,

(98)

where
f_{\alpha\beta}=T^{-1}_{\alpha\beta}
and
g_{\alpha\beta}=T^{-1}_{\alpha,\beta+3}

are 3
\times
3 matrices.
The form of the solution is

\displaystyle\langle\bm{{x}}|0_{\perp}\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{\perp}\exp\bigg[-\frac{\,1\,}{\,2\,}x_{\alpha}M_{\alpha\beta}x_{\beta}\bigg]\,,

(99)

where the matrix
M
can be computed as

\displaystyle M_{\alpha\beta}=\mathrm{i}\big[g^{-1}f\big]_{\alpha\beta}\,,

(100)

with the normalization constant is
\mathcal{N}_{\perp}=\sqrt{\det M/\pi^{3}}
.
The wavefunctions for excited states can be created by applying creation operators
b_{\alpha}^{\dagger}
.

V.4.3
Matrix elements

In our computations of the matrix elements,
we evaluate the expectation values of functions independent of
R_{y}
.
The electric potential is (
\mathcal{N}_{z}^{\perp}=\mathcal{N}_{n_{z}=0}\mathcal{N}_{\perp}
)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\langle V_{E}\rangle_{\rm gs}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{N}_{z}^{\perp}|^{2}|\mathcal{N}_{R}|^{2}\int_{R_{y},{\bm{r}}}V_{E}(r)\,\mathrm{e}^{-x_{\alpha}M_{\alpha\beta}x_{\beta}-\frac{\,\Lambda^{2}}{\,2\,}z^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\,4\alpha_{s}\,}{3}|\mathcal{N}_{z}^{\perp}|^{2}\int_{{\bm{r}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{e}^{-\rho^{\perp}_{a}\Sigma_{ab}\rho^{\perp}_{b}-\frac{\,\Lambda^{2}}{\,2\,}z^{2}}\,}{r}\,,

(101)

where integrating
R_{y}
renormalizes the matrix for
\bm{\rho}_{\perp}
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{ab}=M_{ab}-\frac{\,M_{Ra}M_{Rb}\,}{\,M_{RR}\,}\,,~~~~~~~(a,b=x,y)

(102)

For the magnetic potential, we find (for
J={\rm I,II}
)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\langle V_{J}^{s}\rangle_{\rm gs}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bm{q}}}V_{J}^{s}({\bm{q}})\,\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{\,4\,}q^{\perp}_{a}(\Sigma^{-1})_{ab}q^{\perp}_{b}-\frac{\,q_{z}^{2}\,}{\,2\Lambda^{2}\,}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,16\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,3\,}\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-q^{\perp}_{a}\tilde{\Sigma}_{ab}q^{\perp}_{b}}\mathcal{F}_{s}\mathcal{P}_{J}\,,

(103)

where
\mathcal{F}_{s}
and
\mathcal{P}_{J}
are given in Eqs. (
28
) and (
32
), respectively,
and
\tilde{\Sigma}\equiv\Sigma^{-1}/4+I_{zz}/2\Lambda^{2}
.
Useful formulae to reduce the three dimensional integral over
(z,\rho_{x},\rho_{y})

to one-dimensional integral is given in Appendix.
B
.

V.4.4
Spectra

Now we examine numerical results for the ground state spectra for
u\bar{d}
and
u\bar{s}
mesons.

We begin with the eigenfrequencies
\omega_{\alpha}
(
\alpha=1,2,3
) of the transverse Hamiltonian
H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}
,
see Eq. (
91
) and Fig.
5
.
In the limit of
e_{\rho}=0
(see Sec.
V.2
),
we have already seen the center-of-mass motion with the energy cost
\sim|B|/M
,
and two orbital angular motions with the magnetic moments parallel and anti-parallel to the magnetic field,
with the energy cost of
\sim|B|/M
and
\sim\lambda/|B|
, respectively.
The small energy in the latter is the consequence of the orbital Zeeman energy
that tends to cancel the zero-point energy of the orbital motion.

At
e_{\rho}\neq 0
, all these modes can mix,
but the large
B
behaviors are similar to the trend found in the
e_{\rho}=0
case;
we have two energetic modes (
\omega_{2}
and
\omega_{3}
modes) with the linear dependence on
B
,
and one low energy mode (
\omega_{1}
mode) which decreases with
B
.
Extrapolating the assignment of quantum numbers for the
e_{\rho}=0
case,
one can regard the
\omega_{1}
mode to be dominated by the
\ell_{z}=+1
excitation.
Meanwhile, for small
B
, the lowest energy mode is dominated by the center-of-mass motion
but it is eventually dominated by one of the orbital modes.
When these modes (
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
) approach,
the level repulsion occurs, see
\omega_{1}
around
|eB|\sim 0.12\,{\rm GeV}^{2}
.

Shown in Fig.
6

is the magnetic field dependence of the ground state energies for
u\bar{d}
and
u\bar{s}
mesons,
with and without short-range interactions (
\alpha_{s}(Q_{B})\neq 0
and
\alpha_{s}=0
).
For
Q_{B}
, we chose
c_{B}=1.0
.
For
\alpha_{s}=0
, as in the neutral meson cases,
the mass reduction occurs due to the effective disappearance of the transverse zero-point energy.
With the short-range interactions added in,
this trend does not change much.

In Fig.
7
, we show
the ground state energies for
u\bar{d}
and
u\bar{s}
mesons calculated with the running
\alpha_{s}(Q_{B})
.
The colored bands represent the uncertainty originating from the scale parameter

c_{B}\in[0.5,2.0]
, where the solid lines denote the results for the intermediate value,
c_{B}=1.0
.
The results for a constant
\alpha_{s}
are also shown as dotted lines.
For comparison with lattice results, we plot the vacuum masses multiplied
by the ratio
C_{\rm ratio}^{\rm lat}=m_{\rho_{+}}(B)/m_{\rho}(0)
measured on the lattice for
m_{\pi}=0.415
GeV
Bali et al. 2018
.
Since previous lattice analyses have shown that the pion mass dependence is mild between

m_{\pi}\simeq 0.42
GeV and
0.81
GeV,
we applied the same ratio to the vacuum masses of
m_{\rho}
and
m_{K*}

Compared to the lattice data, our model calculations seem to underestimate the mass reduction.
Increasing the value of
\alpha_{s}
, which affects both the color-electric (attractive) and magnetic (repulsive) interactions,
does not resolve the discrepancy.
One possible resolution is to assume that the color-magnetic interaction is weaker for
\rho
and
K_{*+}

if we switch off the color-magnetic interaction (by setting

),
the loss of the repulsive term leads to a significant mass reduction, see Fig.

At present, however,
we have not yet identified a definitive physical mechanism to explain the significant mass reduction observed on the lattice.
Further studies are called for.

VI
Summary

We studied the properties of neutral and charged mesons
within a simple constituent quark model.
We first investigated how the confining potential
affects the dynamics of charged particles in magnetic fields,
and then delineated the impact of the short-range interactions.
The qualitative trends in the mass reduction of neutral and charged mesons, as found in the lattice simulations, are reproduced.
The agreement with the lattice results can be improved to a semi-quantitative level
if a proper scale for the running coupling is chosen for the short-range interactions.

In addition to the ground-state energies,
we also argue that the transverse kinetic terms of neutral mesons have nontrivial behavior at finite
B

which are substantially different from those obtained by extrapolating from a hadronic effective Lagrangian at
B=0

Fukushima and Hidaka 2013
;
Hattori et al. 2016
;
Kojo 2021
.
The impact of transverse momenta is suppressed by a factor of
\sim 1/B
,
and hence at large
B
there are many low-energy states.
This may be interpreted as an effective dimensional reduction of the meson dynamics.
Similar trends can be found for charged mesons with excitations of
\ell_{z}>0
.
In a hadron resonance gas with these low-lying mesons,
thermal contributions at low temperature are significantly enhanced,
and this trend is consistent with the findings on the lattice
Bali et al. 2014
.

In this study we found that the details of the running of
\alpha_{s}

are crucial for quantitative description of the hadronic spectra within the present framework,
especially for states with the spatially compact wavefunctions.
Unfortunately our choice of
Q^{2}
remains largely heuristic and should be improved by more rigorous arguments
Deur et al. 2016
.
Also, quantitatively, our description of charged mesons are not satisfactory.
Considering the sensitivity of hadronic spectra to the choice of
\alpha_{s}
,
the
B
-dependence of the hadronic spectra
should allow us to gain more insights on the running of
\alpha_{s}
at the hadronic scale.

As mentioned in the Introduction,
this work is aimed to establish a reasonable (qualitative) baseline
to study multi-quark systems and hadronic matter in magnetic fields.
In subsequent papers, we discuss hadronic scattering at large
B

and how such scattering processes can be used to describe multi-quark systems.
These results provide a useful framework for understanding
hadronic dynamics in strong magnetic fields.

Perturbative evaluation

To compute the corrections to the analytic expression in the
e_{\rho}=0
(see Sec.
V.3
),
we split the Hamiltonian by classifying terms by the order of
e_{\rho}
,

\displaystyle H_{\perp}^{\rm eff}=H^{\perp}_{0}+H^{\perp}_{1}+H^{\perp}_{2}+\cdots\,.

(104)

Below the reduced mass is expanded as

\mu^{-1}=4M^{-1}+4\delta m^{2}M^{-3}+\cdots
,
and
m_{1}^{-1}-m_{2}^{-1}=-4\delta m/M^{2}+\cdots
.
Explicitly,

\displaystyle H^{\perp}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,(P_{R}^{y})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}+\frac{\,(Be_{R})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}R_{y}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\,2({\bm{p}}_{\rho}^{\perp})^{2}\,}{\,M\,}+\bigg[\frac{\,(Be_{R})^{2}\,}{\,32M\,}+\lambda\,\bigg]\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{2}-\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}L_{\rho}^{z}\,,

(105)

is
O(e_{\rho}^{0})
, and

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91684ptH^{\perp}_{1}=\frac{\,2Be_{\rho}\,}{\,M\,}R_{y}p_{\rho}^{x}+\frac{\,5B^{2}e_{R}e_{\rho}\,}{\,4M\,}\rho_{y}R_{y}-\frac{\,Be_{\rho}\,}{\,2M\,}\rho_{x}p_{R}^{y}\,,

(106)

is
O(e_{\rho}^{1})
, and

\displaystyle H^{\perp}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,(Be_{\rho})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}R_{y}^{2}+\frac{\,2\delta m^{2}\,}{\,M^{3}\,}({\bm{p}}_{\rho}^{\perp})^{2}

\displaystyle+\bigg[\frac{(Be_{\rho})^{2}}{\,2M\,}+\frac{\,B^{2}e_{R}e_{\rho}\delta m\,}{\,4M^{2}\,}-\frac{\,(Be_{R})^{2}\delta m^{2}\,}{\,32M^{3}\,}\bigg]\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\,3(Be_{\rho})^{2}\,}{\,8M\,}\rho_{x}^{2}-\frac{\,2Be_{\rho}\delta m\,}{\,M^{2}\,}L_{\rho}^{z}\,.

(107)

is regarded as
O(e_{\rho}^{2})
.
Finally, we also split the Zeeman term as

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptH_{\rm Zm}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\,e_{1}B\,}{\,2m_{1}\,}\sigma_{1}^{z}-\frac{\,e_{2}B\,}{\,2m_{2}\,}\sigma_{2}^{z}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}\big(\sigma_{1}^{z}+\sigma_{2}^{z}\big)-\frac{\,Be_{\rho}\,}{\,M\,}\big(\sigma_{1}^{z}-\sigma_{2}^{z}\big)

\displaystyle~~~-\frac{\,Be_{\rho}\delta m\,}{\,M^{2}\,}\big(\sigma_{1}^{z}+\sigma_{2}^{z}\big)+O(\delta m^{4})\,.

(108)

where the first, second, and third terms correspond to

O(e_{\rho}^{0})
,
O(e_{\rho}^{1})
, and
O(e_{\rho}^{2})
, respectively.

For the transverse Hamiltonian of
O(e_{\rho})
,
the first order perturbation is

\displaystyle\langle H_{1}^{\perp}\rangle=0\,,

(109)

so that the corrections start from
O(e_{\rho}^{2})
.
The
O(e_{\rho}^{2})
correction to the
n
-th level is as

\displaystyle\big(E_{n_{R}}^{\bm{n}_{\perp}}\big)_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{n}^{\prime}\neq\bm{n}}\frac{\,\langle\bm{n}|H_{1}^{\perp}|\bm{n}^{\prime}\rangle\langle\bm{n}^{\prime}|H_{1}^{\perp}|\bm{n}\rangle\,}{\,\big(E_{n_{R}}^{\bm{n}_{\perp}}\big)_{0}-\big(E_{n_{R}^{\prime}}^{\bm{n}^{\prime}_{\perp}}\big)_{0}\,}+\langle\bm{n}|H_{2}^{\perp}|\bm{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{1}E_{n_{R}}^{\bm{n}_{\perp}}+\delta_{2}E_{n_{R}}^{\bm{n}_{\perp}}+\delta_{2,{\rm Zm}}E_{n_{R}}^{\bm{n}_{\perp}}\,.

(110)

To evaluate
\rho_{x}P_{R}^{y}
,
R_{y}p_{\rho}^{x}
, and
\rho_{y}R_{y}
in
H_{1}^{\perp}
,
it is convenient to use the creation and annihilation operators

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182ptR_{y}=\frac{\,1\,}{\,\sqrt{2Be_{R}}\,}\big(b+b^{\dagger}\big)\,,~~P_{R}^{y}=-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,2\,}}\big(b-b^{\dagger}\big)\,,

(111)

and

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\rho_{i}=\frac{\,1\,}{\,\sqrt{2\kappa}\,}\big(a_{i}+a_{i}^{\dagger}\big)\,,~~~p_{\rho}^{i}=-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\frac{\,\kappa\,}{\,2\,}\,}\big(a_{i}-a_{i}^{\dagger}\big)\,,

(112)

where
i=x,y
and
\kappa=Be_{R}\mu/2M
.

For perturbation treatments for
(\bm{\rho}_{\perp},\bm{p}_{\rho}^{\perp}
),
it is useful to label the unperturbed states by
(n_{+},n_{-})

which is related to
(n_{r},\ell_{z})
by
n_{\pm}=n_{r}+(|\ell_{z}|\pm\ell_{z})/2
.
The first order Hamiltonian is (
\eta_{1}=-\eta_{2}=1
)

\displaystyle H_{1}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\frac{\,Be_{\rho}\,}{\,2M\,}\sum_{j=\pm}\bigg[\frac{\,\xi\,}{2}-\frac{\,5\eta_{j}\,}{\,2\xi\,}-\frac{1}{\,\xi\,}\bigg]a_{j}^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\frac{\,Be_{\rho}\,}{\,2M\,}\sum_{j=\pm}\bigg[\frac{\,\xi\,}{2}-\frac{\,5\eta_{j}\,}{\,2\xi\,}+\frac{1}{\,\xi\,}\bigg]a_{j}b^{\dagger}+({\rm h.c.})\,,

(113)

where
\xi=\big[1+32M\lambda/(Be_{R})^{2}\big]^{1/4}
.

For the ground state, only
a_{j}^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}
type terms yield
nonzero contributions.
The
a_{+}^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}
increases the quanta
(n_{+},n_{-},n_{R})\rightarrow(n_{+}+1,n_{-},n_{R}+1)

with the excitation energy

\displaystyle E^{\Delta n_{+}=1}_{\Delta n_{R}=1}=\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,M\,}+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}-Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}\,,

(114)

while the
a_{-}^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}
increases the quanta
(n_{+},n_{-},n_{R})\rightarrow(n_{+},n_{-}+1,n_{R}+1)

with the energy

\displaystyle E^{\Delta n_{-}=1}_{\Delta n_{R}=1}=\frac{\,Be_{R}\,}{\,M\,}+\frac{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}+Be_{R}\,}{\,2M\,}\,,

(115)

Hence the energy shift
\delta_{1}E
for the ground state is

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\delta_{1}E_{\rm gs}=-\frac{\,(Be_{\rho})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}\bigg[\frac{\,\big(\frac{\,\xi\,}{2}-\frac{\,7\,}{\,2\xi\,}\big)^{2}\,}{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}+Be_{R}\,}+\frac{\,\big(\frac{\,\xi\,}{2}+\frac{\,3\,}{\,2\xi\,}\big)^{2}\,}{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}+3Be_{R}\,}\bigg]\,.

(116)

Next we evaluate
\delta_{2}E
.
The straightforward calculations yield

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{n}_{\perp}|\bm{\rho}_{\perp}^{2}|\bm{n}_{\perp}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\,8\,}{\,Be_{R}\xi^{2}\,}\,\big(n_{+}+n_{-}+1\big)\,,

\displaystyle\big\langle\bm{n}_{\perp}|\big(\bm{p}_{\rho}^{\perp}\big)^{2}|\bm{n}_{\perp}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\,Be_{R}\xi^{2}\,}{\,8\,}\,\big(n_{+}+n_{-}+1\big)\,,

\displaystyle\big\langle n_{R}|R_{y}^{2}|n_{R}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\,1\,}{\,2Be_{R}\,}\big(2n_{R}+1\big)\,.

(117)

The corresponding energy shift is

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\delta_{2}E_{\rm gs}=\frac{\,Be_{\rho}^{2}\,}{\,4Me_{R}\,}\frac{\,\xi^{2}+10\,}{\,\xi^{2}\,}+\frac{\,2Be_{\rho}\delta m\,}{\,M^{2}\xi^{2}\,}+\frac{\,8\delta m^{2}\lambda\,}{\,M^{2}Be_{R}\xi^{2}\,}\,,

(118)

where we used
\xi^{4}-1=32M\lambda/(Be_{R})^{2}
.
Including the corrections to the Zeeman energy, the results are summarized as

\displaystyle\delta_{1}E_{\rm gs}=-\frac{\,(Be_{\rho})^{2}\,}{\,2M\,}\bigg[\frac{\,\big(\frac{\,\xi\,}{2}-\frac{\,7\,}{\,2\xi\,}\big)^{2}\,}{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}+Be_{R}\,}+\frac{\,\big(\frac{\,\xi\,}{2}+\frac{\,3\,}{\,2\xi\,}\big)^{2}\,}{\,\mathcal{B}_{R}+3Be_{R}\,}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle\delta_{2}E_{\rm gs}=\frac{\,Be_{\rho}^{2}\,}{\,4Me_{R}\,}\frac{\,\xi^{2}+10\,}{\,\xi^{2}\,}+\frac{\,2Be_{\rho}\delta m\,}{\,M^{2}\xi^{2}\,}+\frac{\,8\delta m^{2}\lambda\,}{\,M^{2}Be_{R}\xi^{2}\,}\,,

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm 2,Zm}E_{\rm gs}=-\frac{\,2Be_{\rho}\delta m\,}{\,M^{2}\xi^{2}\,}\,,

(119)

where
\xi=\big[1+32M\lambda/(Be_{R})^{2}\big]^{1/4}

and we have used
\xi^{4}-1=32M\lambda/(Be_{R})^{2}

in computations of
\delta_{2}E_{\rm gs}
.

In the limit of
B\rightarrow\infty
, the scaling of parameters is

\mathcal{B}_{R}\rightarrow Be_{R}
,
\xi\rightarrow 1
.
The perturbative corrections of
O(Be_{\rho}^{2})

are assembled to cancel,

\displaystyle\delta_{1}E_{\rm gs}

\displaystyle~\rightarrow~-\frac{\,11Be_{\rho}^{2}\,}{\,4Me_{R}\,}\,,

\displaystyle\delta_{2}E_{\rm gs}

\displaystyle~\rightarrow~\frac{\,11Be_{\rho}^{2}\,}{\,4Me_{R}\,}+\frac{\,2Be_{\rho}\delta m\,}{\,M^{2}\,}\,,

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm 2,Zm}E_{\rm gs}

\displaystyle~\rightarrow~-\frac{\,2Be_{\rho}\delta m\,}{\,M^{2}\,}

(120)

and hence the
B
-dependence appears only through
O(M\lambda/B^{2})
.

Appendix B
Formulae for matrix elements

For perturbative evaluation of short-range potentials for neutral and charged mesons,
here we summarize some useful formulae.
We begin with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\,\sqrt{\xi^{2}+a^{2}}\,}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{\,\mathrm{e}^{-s(\xi^{2}+a^{2})}\,}{\,\sqrt{\pi s}\,}\,.

(121)

In computations of
V_{E}
, the three-dimensional integral is reduced to the one-dimensional integral,

\displaystyle I_{E}[\Sigma]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bm{r}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{e}^{-\bm{x}^{T}\Sigma\bm{x}}\,}{|\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{0}|}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s\,}{\,\sqrt{\pi s}\,}\int_{{\bm{r}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\bm{x}^{T}\Sigma\bm{x}-s(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s~\frac{\pi\mathrm{e}^{s^{2}\bm{x}_{0}^{T}(\Sigma+sI)^{-1}\bm{x}_{0}-s\bm{x}_{0}^{2}}}{\,\sqrt{s\det\big(\Sigma+sI\big)}\,}\,.

(122)

For the magnetic potential, we encounter the following types of integrals

\displaystyle I^{M}_{0}[\tilde{\Sigma}]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\bm{q}\tilde{\Sigma}\bm{q}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}=\frac{1}{\,8\pi^{3/2}\,}\frac{w(\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0})}{\,\sqrt{\det\tilde{\Sigma}}\,}\,,

(123)

\displaystyle\tilde{I}^{M}_{0}[A]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bm{q}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{e}^{-q_{a}\tilde{\Sigma}_{ab}q_{b}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\,}{\,\bm{q}^{2}+m^{2}\,}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\,8\pi^{3/2}\,}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-sm^{2}}w(\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0})}{\,\sqrt{\det\big(\tilde{\Sigma}+sI\big)}\,}\,.

(124)

where

\displaystyle w(\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0})=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{\,4\,}(\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0})^{T}\tilde{\Sigma}^{-1}\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\,.

(125)

We also compute the integral

\displaystyle I^{M}_{j}[\tilde{\Sigma}]=\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\bm{q}^{T}\tilde{\Sigma}\bm{q}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\,\frac{\,q_{j}^{2}\,}{\,\bm{q}^{2}\,}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\,\partial\tilde{\Sigma}_{jj}\,}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\,\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\bm{q}^{T}\tilde{\Sigma}\bm{q}-s\bm{q}^{2}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\,\partial\tilde{\Sigma}_{jj}\,}\frac{1}{\,8\pi^{3/2}\,}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{\,w(\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0})\,}{\,\sqrt{\det\big(\tilde{\Sigma}+sI\big)}\,}\,,

(126)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{I}^{M}_{j}[\tilde{\Sigma}]=\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\bm{q}^{T}\tilde{\Sigma}\bm{q}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\,\frac{\,1\,}{\,\bm{q}^{2}+m^{2}\,}\frac{\,q_{j}^{2}\,}{\,\bm{q}^{2}\,}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\,\partial A_{jj}\,}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}u\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}t\,\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\bm{q}^{T}\tilde{\Sigma}\bm{q}-t(\bm{q}^{2}+m^{2})-u\bm{q}^{2}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\,\partial A_{jj}\,}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\int_{0}^{s}\mathrm{d}t\,\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\bm{q}^{T}[\tilde{\Sigma}+sI]\bm{q}-tm^{2}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\,\partial A_{jj}\,}\frac{1}{\,8\pi^{3/2}\,}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{(1-\mathrm{e}^{-sm^{2}})w(\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0})\,}{\,m^{2}\sqrt{\det\big(\tilde{\Sigma}+sI\big)}\,}\,.

(127)

In particular, when
A
is diagonal,

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046ptI^{M}_{j}[\tilde{\Sigma}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\,w(\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0})\,}{\,16\pi^{3/2}\,}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\frac{\,\mathrm{d}s\,}{\,\tilde{\Sigma}_{jj}+s\,}\prod_{k}\frac{1}{\,\sqrt{\tilde{\Sigma}_{kk}+s}\,}\,,

(128)

\displaystyle\tilde{I}^{M}_{j}[\tilde{\Sigma}]

\displaystyle=\frac{w(\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0})}{\,16\pi^{3/2}\,}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{\,1-\mathrm{e}^{-sm^{2}}\,}{\,m^{2}\big(\tilde{\Sigma}_{jj}+s\big)\,}\prod_{k}\frac{1}{\,\sqrt{\tilde{\Sigma}_{kk}+s}\,}\,.

With these formulae and setting
\tilde{\Sigma}^{\prime}\equiv\tilde{\Sigma}+\mu_{\rm IR}^{-2}I
,
we compute the integrals appearing in the magnetic energy as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-{\bm{q}}^{T}\tilde{\Sigma}{\bm{q}}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\mathcal{F}_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,I^{M}_{0}[\tilde{\Sigma}^{\prime}]\,}{\,m_{1}m_{2}\,}+\tilde{I}^{M}_{0}[\tilde{\Sigma}]-\tilde{I}^{M}_{0}[\tilde{\Sigma}^{\prime}]\,,

(130)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-{\bm{q}}^{T}\tilde{\Sigma}{\bm{q}}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\mathcal{F}_{s}\frac{\,q_{j}^{2}\,}{\,\bm{q}^{2}\,}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,I^{M}_{j}[\tilde{\Sigma}^{\prime}]\,}{\,m_{1}m_{2}\,}+\tilde{I}^{M}_{j}[\tilde{\Sigma}]-\tilde{I}^{M}_{j}[\tilde{\Sigma}^{\prime}]\,.

(131)

The magnetic energy can be written as

\displaystyle\langle V_{\rm I}^{s}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\,16\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,3\,}\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-{\bm{q}}^{T}\tilde{\Sigma}{\bm{q}}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\frac{\,\mathcal{F}_{s}\,}{\,2\,}\bigg(1+\frac{\,q_{z}^{2}\,}{\,{\bm{q}}^{2}\,}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{\,8\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,3\,}\big(\mathcal{G}_{0}+\mathcal{G}_{z}\big)\,,

(132)

\displaystyle\langle V_{\rm II}^{s}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\,16\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,3\,}\int_{{\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-{\bm{q}}^{T}\tilde{\Sigma}{\bm{q}}-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{r}_{\perp}^{0}}\frac{\,\mathcal{F}_{s}\,}{\,2\,}\bigg(1-\frac{\,3q_{z}^{2}\,}{\,{\bm{q}}^{2}\,}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{\,8\pi\alpha_{s}\,}{\,3\,}\big(\mathcal{G}_{0}-3\mathcal{G}_{z}\big)\,.

(133)
