Abstract
. We compute the stable homology of complex braid groups of types B ⁡ ( e , e , n ) B(e,e,n) and B ⁡ ( 2 ​ e , e , n ) B(2e,e,n) for fixed e ≥ 2 e\geq 2 and increasing n n . This accounts for the stable homology of all infinite families of complex braid groups. We achieve this by explicitly computing a quillenization of their stable classifying spaces. In particular, we provide a proof of an identification of the stable homology of Artin groups of type D D claimed by Fuchs in the ’70s.



1.
Introduction

This article is first concerned with the homology of Artin groups of type
D
. The subject is classical; for instance, Goryunov
[
Gor78
,
Gor82
]
has computed the entire homology of the group
D_{n}
with integer coefficients for any
n
. Homological stability holds for the sequence of Artin groups of type
D
, and it is meaningful to ask for a concrete description of the quillenization (or plus construction)
\mathbf{B}D_{\infty}^{+}
of the classifying space of the colimit group
D_{\infty}:=\mathrm{colim}_{n\to\infty}D_{n}
.
In
[
Fuk74
, §2.7, p. 34]
, Fuchs claims the following:

The quillenization of the space
K(B_{C},1)
is
(\Omega^{2}S^{2})_{0}\times\Omega S^{2}
,
and
the quillenization of the space
K(B_{D},1)
is the product of the space
(\Omega^{2}S^{2})_{0}
by the homotopy fiber of the map
S^{3}\to S^{3}
of degree
2
.
Both facts are proved on the basis of the theorem of Segal.

Vershinin has written a detailed proof of the claim for the case
C
in
[
Ver99
, Thm. 11.1, p. 315]
. For the case
D
, Fuchs claims the following equivalence of spaces:

(1)

\mathbf{B}D_{\infty}^{+}\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{3}\times\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\xrightarrow{2}S^{3}).

However, a direct and transparent derivation did not seem to be readily available to the authors.
Therefore, an aim of the present paper is to provide a detailed proof of (
1
). More importantly, we place this result in a broader framework by computing the quillenization of a bigger family of configuration spaces closely related to the complex braid groups. This leads to a uniform description of the stabilized
homotopy types associated with the complex braid groups of type
B(e,e,n)
and
B(2e,e,n)
, extending the picture suggested by Fuchs beyond the cases of type
B
and
D
.

Theorem A
.

For
e\geq 1
and
d\geq 2
we have the following equivalences of spaces:

\mathbf{B}{B(e,e,\infty)}^{+}\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{3}\times\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\xrightarrow{e}S^{3});\quad\quad\mathbf{B}{B(de,e,\infty)}^{+}\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{3}\times\Omega S^{2}.

Theorem
A
follows directly from Theorem
B
and Theorem
C
. These will be phrased in terms of group completions of monoids and localizations of modules.

Notation 1.1
.

For an
E_{1}
-algebra in spaces
A
and a right
E_{1}
-module
M
over
A
we denote by

the group completion of
A
, and by

the localization of

G(A)\simeq\Omega\mathbf{B}A,

where we abbreviate by

with respect to the terminal left and right

where

We shall denote by

the braid monoid, which we shall only regard as an

-monoid; both

Theorem B
.

Let

. Then there is a homotopy equivalence of right
E_{1}
-modules over
G(\mathcal{A})\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{2}

L_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}(e))\simeq G(\mathcal{A})\times\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\xrightarrow{e}S^{3}),

in which the action of

As an immediate application, we identify for any

Remark 1.2
.

We recall (see, for example
[
BL00
, Section 1.1]
) that we can compute the homology of
\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{\to}}S^{3})
via the Serre spectral sequence associated with the homotopy fiber sequence
\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{\to}}S^{3})\to S^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{\to}}S^{3}
: for
n\geq 1
we get that the
n
th
homology of
\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{\to}}S^{3})
is isomorphic
to the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/e
if
n
is even, and is trivial if
n
is odd. The homology of
\mathbf{B}A_{\infty}
has been computed explicitly in
[
Fuk70
,
Vai78
,
CLM76
]
.

As a side result, we also extend the statement of Fuchs about the quillenization of the space
K(B_{C},1)
to the general case of families of complex braid groups of type
B(2e,e,n)
for fixed
e
. It was already known (
[
CM14
, Prop. 4.24]
) that the homology of
B(2e,e,\infty)
is equivalent to the homology of

. Our second main result is the following;

Theorem C
.

There is a homotopy equivalence of right
E_{1}
-modules over
G(\mathcal{A})\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{2}

L_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e))\simeq G(\mathcal{A})\times\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>,

in which the action of
G(\mathcal{A})
on the right hand side is only on the first factor, and
\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>
is the connected
e
-fold covering of the space
\Omega S^{2}
.

Remark 1.3
.

We note that
\Omega S^{2}
is homotopy equivalent to the product
S^{1}\times\Omega S^{3}
. In fact, we can associate with the Hopf fibration the following homotopy fiber sequence:

\Omega S^{3}\to\Omega S^{2}\to S^{1}

and the canonical map
S^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega\Sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega S^{2}
gives a homotopy section. Using the structure of group-like

-algebra on

and
\Omega S^{2}

As a consequence, all connected finite-degree coverings of
\Omega S^{2}

\Omega S^{2}
.

2.
Preliminaries and background

2.1.
Complex braid groups and configuration spaces

Complex braid groups are the generalizations of braid groups associated to arbitrary (finite) complex reflection groups. By a finite complex reflection group
W
, we mean a finite subgroup of some
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
generated by pseudo-reflections; that is, finite-order endomorphisms of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
which leave invariant some hyperplane in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

The classification of irreducible finite complex reflection groups was first given by Shephard
and Todd
[
ST54
]
: there is an infinite family
G(de,e,n)
, where
d,e,n
are positive integers, and 34 exceptional groups, denoted by
G_{4},\dots,G_{37}
. The infinite family
G(de,e,n)
includes the four infinite families
A_{n-1}
,
C_{n}
,
D_{n}
and
I_{2}(e)
of finite Coxeter groups, given by
G(1,1,n)
,
G(2,1,n)
,
G(2,2,n)
and
G(e,e,2)
, respectively.

We can associate to
W
a braid group
B(W)
. If we denote by
\mathcal{H}
the corresponding collection of hyperplanes defined by
W
, the group
W
acts freely on the hyperplane complement
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{H}
(see
[
Ste64
]
). The complex braid group
B(W)
is defined as the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X/W)
(
[
BMR98
]
). It turns out that the complement
X
is aspherical, and hence so is
X/W
. This result is due to many authors, in particular it is due to Fadell and Neuwirth
[
FN62
]
for type
A_{n}
, to Brieskorn
[
Bri73
]
for type
C_{n}
and
D_{n}
, to Nakamura
[
Nak83
]
for the infinite series of complex reflection groups. As a consequence the quotient
X/W
is a classifying space for the complex braid group
B(W)
.

In the follow-up, we shall denote by
B(de,e,n)
the braid group associated with
G(de,e,n)
.

While the groups
G(de,e,n)
for distinct
d,e,n
are pairwise non-isomorphic, for
d>1
we have
B(2e,e,n)\simeq B(de,e,n)
(see
[
BMR98
, Prop.3.8]
).

For completeness, we briefly recall the definition of the group
G(de,e,n)
. Let
d,e,n
be positive integers and let
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
be in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Following
[
BMR98
]
, we denote by
G(de,e,n)
the subgroup of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
consisting of the elements

[\underline{a},\sigma]\colon z_{j}\mapsto a_{j}z_{\sigma(j)},

where
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
is a permutation, and
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
, with
a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
j
, satisfying
a_{j}^{de}=1
and
(a_{1}\cdots a_{r})^{e}=1
.

For
d=1
, the complex braid group
B(e,e,n)
has various presentations
[
BMR98
,
CP11
]
, but we shall not use them.

We next introduce configuration spaces corresponding to the infinite family of the aforementioned complex reflection groups.

2.2.
Localization of monoids

Recall that for a monoid
A
the group completion
G(A)
also inherits the structure of a monoid, which is well-defined up to a contractible space of choices.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A
be a monoid in spaces and let
M
be a right
A
-module. Then there is a chain of equivalences of spaces

L_{A}(M)\simeq\mathbf{B}(M,A,G(A))\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbf{B}(M,A,*)\to\mathbf{B}A).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{Mod}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A}
be the full subcategory of
\mathbf{Mod}_{A}
spanned by local objects. Note first that we have an equivalence
\mathbf{Mod}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A}\simeq\mathbf{Mod}_{G(A)}
, by definition of
G(A)
. Moreover, the full subcategory inclusion can be identified with the restriction functor
\mathbf{Mod}_{G(A)}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{A}
. Passing to left adjoints, the localization functor
L_{A}\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{A}\to\mathbf{Mod}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A}
can be identified with the relative tensor product
-\otimes_{A}G(A)\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{A}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{G(A)}
, which can be concretely modelled by the two-sided bar construction
\mathbf{B}(-,A,G(A))
(see e.g.
[
Lur17
, Proposition 4.6.2.17]
or
[
Fra08
, Lemma 2.2.6]
). The first equivalence follows.

For the second equivalence, we first recall that
May proves that for a group-like
G
and a
G
-module
M
, we have an equivalence
M\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbf{B}(M,G,*)\to\mathbf{B}G)
as
G
-modules (see
[
May75
, Theorem 7.6 and Proposition 7.9]
). Applying this to
M=\mathbf{B}(M,A,G(A))
and
G=G(A)
we get

\mathbf{B}(M,A,G(A))\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{B}(M,A,G(A)),G(A),*)\to\mathbf{B}G(A)).

Now, associativity of the bar construction yields

\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{B}(M,A,G(A)),G(A),*)\simeq\mathbf{B}(M,A,\mathbf{B}(G(A),G(A),*))\simeq\mathbf{B}(M,A,*),

and the equivalence
\mathbf{B}G(A)\simeq\mathbf{B}A
allows us to write

\mathbf{B}(M,A,G(A))\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbf{B}(M,A,*)\to\mathbf{B}A),

concluding the chain of equivalences in the statement.
∎

To connect
L_{A}(M)\simeq\mathbf{B}(M,A,G(A))
with the mapping telescope we are actually interested in – in that it contains information about the stable homology of components of
M
– we will also use the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A
be a monoid with
\pi_{0}(A)\cong\mathbb{N}
, and let
a
be a point in the component
A_{1}
of
A
corresponding to
1\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that the group-completion Theorem
[
MS76
]
applies to
A
, and in particular that
G(A)
is equivalent, as a space, to the quillenization of the mapping telescope

\mathrm{colim}(A\xrightarrow{\_\cdot a}A\xrightarrow{\_\cdot a}A\xrightarrow{\_\cdot a}\dots).

Then the canonical map

\mathrm{colim}(M\xrightarrow{\_\cdot a}M\xrightarrow{\_\cdot a}M\xrightarrow{\_\cdot a}\dots)\longrightarrow L_{A}(M)

induces an equivalence under quillenization.

Proof.

Denoting by
\mathrm{Tel}_{a}(-)
the mapping telescope of a right
A
-module by the iterated action of
a
, and using the first equivalence in Lemma
2.2
, we have a chain of equivalences

\begin{split}L_{A}(M)^{+}&\simeq\mathbf{B}(M,A,G(A))^{+}\simeq\mathbf{B}(M,A,\mathrm{Tel}_{a}(A)^{+})^{+}\overset{\star}{\simeq}\mathbf{B}(M,A,\mathrm{Tel}_{a}(A))^{+}\\
&\overset{\star\star}{\simeq}\mathrm{Tel}_{a}(\mathbf{B}(M,A,A))^{+}\simeq\mathrm{Tel}_{a}(M)^{+}.\end{split}

In particular, the equivalence
\star
follows from the fact that quillenization preserves homotopy colimits and finite products, so that both sides agree with the colimit for
[p]\in\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
of
M^{+}\times(A^{+})^{p}\times\mathrm{Tel}_{a}(A)^{+}
. The equivalence
\star\star
follows from exchanging homotopy colimits over
\mathbb{N}_{\leq}
and
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A
be a monoid in spaces and let
M
be a right
A
-module. Assume that there exists a map
M\to A
of right
A
-modules. Then there is an equivalence
L_{A}(M)\simeq G(A)\times\mathbf{B}(M,A,*)
of right
G(A)
-modules.

Proof.

The hypothesis implies that the map
M\to*
of right
A
-modules factors as a composite
M\to A\to*
. We may therefore compute a chain of equivalences of right
G(A)
-modules as follows:

\begin{split}L_{A}(M)&\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbf{B}(M,A,*)\to\mathbf{B}(*,A,*))\\
&\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbf{B}(M,A,*)\to\mathbf{B}(A,A,*)\to\mathbf{B}(*,A,*))\\
&\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbf{B}(M,A,*)\to*\to\mathbf{B}(*,A,*))\\
&\simeq\Omega\mathbf{B}A\times\mathbf{B}(M,A,*)\simeq G(A)\times\mathbf{B}(M,A,*),\end{split}

concluding the proof.
∎

3.
Setting the module structures

In this section we show that
\mathcal{A}:=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Conf}_{n}
and
\mathcal{D}^{*}(e):=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Conf}_{n}^{*}(e)
have the structure of monoids, that
\mathcal{D}(e):=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e)
is both an
\mathcal{A}
-module and a
\mathcal{D}^{*}(e)
-module, and that these two module structures are compatible.

Notation 3.1
.

In this section we write
\mathbf{z}
for a generic configuration
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}\in\mathrm{Conf}_{n}
.

Notation 3.2
.

For a subspace
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, we denote by
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X)
the subspace of
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}
comprising configurations
\mathbf{z}
such that
z_{i}\in X
for all
i
.

Definition 3.3
.

Given
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we define the continuous injective map
\tau_{t}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
:

\tau_{t}(z):=z\frac{|z|+t}{|z|}.

For every cone
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{*}
, i.e. every subset closed under multiplication by
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the map
\tau_{t}
induces a self map on
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X)
given by the formula

\mathbf{z}\mapsto\tau_{t}(\mathbf{z})=\left\{z_{1}\frac{|z_{1}|+t}{|z_{1}|},\dots,z_{n}\frac{|z_{n}|+t}{|z_{n}|}\right\}.

Definition 3.4
.

We denote by

Y_{e}\colon\mathrm{Conf}_{m}(\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{C}

the unique continuous function
satisfying
Y_{e}(\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\})^{e}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}
and attaining positive real values on
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
.

We prove the following preliminary proposition.

Proposition 3.5
.

There is a monoid structure on
\mathcal{A}
and a right
\mathcal{A}
-module structure on
\mathcal{D}(e)
.

Proof.

We replace
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}
and
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e)
by their “Moore” versions

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Conf}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(\mathbf{z},t)\in\mathrm{Conf}_{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\ |\ \forall i\ \Re(z_{i})>0,|z_{i}|<t\right\}\simeq\mathrm{Conf}_{n};

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Conf}_{n}(e)

\displaystyle:=\left\{(\mathbf{z},y,t)\in\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e)\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\ \left|\begin{array}[]{l}\forall i\ |z_{i}|<t\end{array}\right.\right\}\simeq\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e).

We may now regard
\mathfrak{A}:=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{Conf}_{n}
as a strictly associative and strictly unital topological monoid. We do this by using the multiplication operation which is described as follows:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Conf}_{n}\times\mathfrak{Conf}_{m}

\displaystyle\to\ \mathfrak{Conf}_{n+m},

\displaystyle(\mathbf{z},t),(\mathbf{z}^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mapsto\ \left(\mathbf{z}\cup\tau_{t}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime}),t+t^{\prime}\right).

We may similarly turn
\mathfrak{D}(e):=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{Conf}_{n}(e)
into a strictly associative right module over
\mathfrak{A}
. The formula for multiplication is similar and uses the function
Y_{e}
from Definition

Proof of Theorem
C
.

The composite map of
\mathcal{A}
-modules
\mathcal{D}^{*}(e)\to\mathcal{D}^{*}(1)\to\mathcal{D}(1)\to\mathcal{A}
, together with Lemma
2.4
, allows us to write an equivalence of
\mathcal{A}
-modules
L_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e))\simeq G(\mathcal{A})\times\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e),\mathcal{A},*)
.

For
e=1
the space
\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(1),\mathcal{A},*)
is equivalent to
\Omega S^{2}
, as observed in Remark
4.3
. For generic
e
, the pullback square of simplicial spaces on the left in the following gives rise to the pullback square of spaces on the right, again using that the bottom right simplicial space is constant:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{*}(e)\times\mathcal{A}^{\times\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{*}(1)\times\mathcal{A}^{\times\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega S^{2};}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e)\mathcal{A},*)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(1),\mathcal{A},*)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega S^{2}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

Using that the bottom map is an equivalence of spaces, we conclude that the top map is an equivalence of spaces too.
∎

Proof of Theorem
B
.

By Proposition
3.10
there is a homotopy pushout square of spaces as follows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e)/\mathbb{Z},\mathcal{A},*)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e)/\mathbb{Z}/e,\mathcal{A},*)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e),\mathcal{A},*)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}(e),\mathcal{A},*).}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

In fact the spaces involved in the previous pushout square have a residual
\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>
-action by Proposition
3.8
, and the previous is a pushout of
\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>
-modules.
Using that bar constructions commute with homotopy quotients we
may rewrite the previous as follows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>)/\mathbb{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>)/\mathbb{Z}/e}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B(\mathcal{D}(e),\mathcal{A},*).}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

Using that
\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>
is a grouplike monoid, we may convert each term in the previous pushout square of
\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>
-modules into the fiber of a map towards
\mathbf{B}(\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>)
. All in all we obtain an equivalence

\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}(e),\mathcal{A},*)\simeq\mathrm{fib}(\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}/e\sqcup_{\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}}*\longrightarrow\mathbf{B}(\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>)).

It is left to identify the map
\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}/e\sqcup_{\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}}*\longrightarrow\mathbf{B}(\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>)
, or at least its fiber. We first observe that the given map must induce an equivalence on
\pi_{2}
: indeed both the source and the target have
\pi_{2}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
, and the map must be a surjection (and hence a bijection) because we know that
\pi_{1}
of the fiber, that is
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}(e),\mathcal{A},*))
, vanishes. This can for instance be deduced from the fact that the stable
H_{1}(\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e))
is equal to
\mathbb{Z}
and is not larger than that.

We may pass to 2-connected covers and write an equivalence

\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}(e),\mathcal{A},*)\simeq\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\to S^{3})

for a suitable self map of
S^{3}
. Here we use that the 2-connected cover of
\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}/e\sqcup_{\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}}*
has the form
S^{1}\sqcup_{S^{1}\times S^{1}}S^{1}
and must therefore be
S^{3}
if it is 2-connected. On the other hand the 2-connected cover of
\mathbf{B}(\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>)
is the classifying space of the universal cover of
\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>
, which is the classifying space of
\Omega S^{3}
, which is
S^{3}
.

To conclude that the degree of the self map of
S^{3}
must be precisely
e
it suffices to compute
H_{2}(\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}(e),\mathcal{A},*))\cong\pi_{2}(\mathbf{B}(\mathcal{D}(e),\mathcal{A},*))
to be equal to
\mathbb{Z}/e
. This can for instance be done by computing the stable integral homology group
H_{2}(\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e))=\mathbb{Z}/e\times\mathbb{Z}/2
, as computed in
[
CM14
, Thm. 6.4]
.
∎

As a corollary of Theorems
B
and
C
, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

The spaces
L_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}(e))
and
L_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e))
agree with their own quillenizations.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
B
that
\pi_{1}(L_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}(e)))
is abelian, whence the claim follows. Similarly, we get the statement for
L_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e))
from Theorem
C
.
∎

5.
A conjectural scanning map

In this section we introduce an explicit scanning map
s:\mathcal{D}(e)\to\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{\rightarrow}}S^{2})
, that conjecturally is homotopic to the composition of maps of spaces

\mathcal{D}(e)\to L_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}(e))\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{2}\times\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\xrightarrow{e}S^{3})\xrightarrow{\text{project}}\mathrm{fib}(S^{3}\xrightarrow{e}S^{3}),

where the middle equivalence is provided by
Theorem
B
.
We observe that the analogous composite involving the projection onto the first factor
\Omega^{2}S^{2}
is simply the composition of the forgetful map

\mathcal{D}(e)\to\mathcal{D}(1)=\mathcal{A}
followed by the Segal scanning map

\mathcal{A}\to\Omega^{2}S^{2}
described in
[
Seg73
]
.

We model
S^{3}
by the space
\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, and model the map
S^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{\to}}S^{3}
by the map
q\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
given by
(w_{1},w_{2})\mapsto(w_{1}^{e},w_{2})
. For fixed
n\geq 0
, consider the map of spaces

p_{n}\colon\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e)\to\mathbb{C}^{2}-0,\quad p(\mathbf{z},y)=\left(y,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j\neq i}z_{j}\right)=\left(y,\frac{d}{dz}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z+z_{i})\Big|_{z=0}\right)

We observe that the above formula indeed gives a point in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, using that the points
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
are pairwise distinct.

The composite
q\circ p_{n}\colon\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e)\to\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
is given by the formula

q\circ p_{n}(\mathbf{z},y)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j\neq i}z_{j}\right),

and the map

h_{n}\colon\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e)\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\},\quad(\mathbf{z},y,r)\mapsto\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-r+rz_{i}),\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j\neq i}(1-r+rz_{j})\right)

provides a homotopy between
q\circ p_{n}
and the constant map
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e)\to\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
with value
(1,n)
.

The combination of
p_{n}
and the nullhomotopy of
q\circ p_{n}
provides the map
s
on the subspace
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e)\subset\mathcal{D}(e)
.

We may use the above formulas also to describe a conjectural scanning map
s^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{D}^{*}(e)\to\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>
. Following Remark
1.3
, we may identify
\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>
with the homotopy fiber of the (non-canonically nullhomotopic) map

q^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\},\quad(w_{1},w_{2})\mapsto(w_{1}^{e},w_{2}),

i.e. the restriction of
q
on
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}
.
The definition of
\mathrm{Conf}^{*}_{n}(e)
as a subspace of
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(e)
implies that
p_{n}
restricts to a map
p^{\prime}_{n}\colon\mathrm{Conf}^{*}_{n}(e)\to\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}
. The restriction
h^{\prime}_{n}
of
h_{n}
on
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}^{*}(e)\times[0,1]
provides a homotopy between
q^{\prime}\circ p^{\prime}_{n}
and the constant map
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}^{*}(e)\to\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
at
(1,n)
. We define the map of
s^{\prime}
on
\mathrm{Conf}^{*}_{n}(e)
as the map induced by
p^{\prime}_{n}
and
h^{\prime}_{n}
, and conjecture that
s^{\prime}
is homotopic to the following composition, whose middle equivalence is provided by Theorem
C
.

\mathcal{D}^{*}(e)\to L_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}(e))\simeq\Omega^{2}S^{2}\times\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>\xrightarrow{\text{project}}\Omega S^{2}\left<e\right>.
