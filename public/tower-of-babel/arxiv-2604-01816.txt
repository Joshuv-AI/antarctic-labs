(Even hole, triangle)-free graphs revisited
Beatriz Martins and Nicolas Trotignon ENS de Lyon, CNRS, Université Lyon 1, LIP UMR 5668 69342 Lyon Cedex 07, France

Abstract
We revisit a classical paper about (even hole, triangle)-free graphs [Conforti, Cornuéjols, Kapoor and Vušković, Triangle-free graphs that are signable without even holes, Journal of Graph Theory , 34(3), 204–220, 2000]. In fact, the previous study describes a more general class, the so called triangle-free odd signable graphs, and we further generalise the class to the (theta, triangle, wac)-free graphs (not worth defining in an abstract). We exhibit a stronger structure theorem, by precisely describing basic classes and separators. We prove that the separators preserve the treewidth and several properties. Some consequences are a recognition algorithm with running time O ⁡ ( | V ⁡ ( G ) | 4 ​ | E ⁡ ( G ) | ) O(|V(G)|^{4}|E(G)|) , a proof that the treewidth of graphs in the class is at most 4 (improving a previous bound of 5), and a simple criterion to decide if a graph in the class is planar.



Keywords:
even-hole-free graphs, triangle-free graphs, treewidth, algorithms, structure.

Introduction

We consider simple graphs (finite, undirected, with neither loops nor multiple edges). A graph
G
is
H
-free if
G
does not contain an induced subgraph isomorphic to the graph
H
. When
L
is a list of graphs,
L
-free means
H
-free for all
H
in
L
. A path in a graph from a vertex
a
to a vertex
b
is an
ab
-path
. The
length
of a path or a cycle refers to its number of edges. A
triangle
is a cycle of length 3. A
hole
in a graph is a chordless cycle of length at least 4.

A
theta
is a graph made of three chordless
ab
-paths of length at least 2, vertex-disjoint apart from
a
and
b
, and such that the only edges are the edges of the paths (note that since the paths are chordless,
a
and
b
are not adjacent).
Note that the union of any two paths of a theta forms a hole.
A
wheel

W=(H,c)
is a graph made of a hole
H
called the

rim
and a vertex
c
called the
centre
with at least
three neighbours in the rim, see Figure
1
.
A graph induced by a hole
H
together with two adjacent vertices that both have at least three neighbours in
H
is called a
wac
for short (this stands for
Wheel with Adjacent Centres
), see Figure
2
where planar and none planar wacs are represented.

Our main result is a structure theorem for (theta, triangle, wac)-free graphs.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
G
is a (theta, triangle, wac)-free graph, then
G
is basic or
G
has a clique separator, a proper 2-separator or a proper
P_{3}
-separator.

We postpone the precise definitions of the basic graphs to Section
2
and of the separators to Section
3
. Let us just briefly explain that basic graphs include
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
(where
K_{\ell}
denotes the complete graph on
\ell
vertices), the cube (represented in Figure
4
) and special graphs that we call
daisies
, formed of one hole with paths that we call “petals” added around it, see Figure
3
.
The proper 2-separator is a separator made of two non-adjacent vertices, with the additional condition that removing it yields two components that are substantial in some sense (not reduced to a path).
The proper
P_{3}
-separator is a separator equal to a path on three vertices, with a similar non-degeneracy property (plus other technical conditions).

We insist that Theorem
1.1
is not only a decomposition theorem but a structure theorem for our class as we explain in Section
7.7
.
An evidence of its strength is the following corollary that is obtained by showing that our separators preserve the treewidth (see Section
7.4
), which is of independent interest since they may serve to decompose other classes.

Theorem 1.2
.

Every (theta, triangle, wac)-free graph has treewidth at most 4 (and some have treewidth exactly 4).

We also describe an algorithm that decides in time
O(|V(G)|^{4}|E(G)|)
whether an input graph
G
is (theta, triangle, wac)-free, and if so, computes a tree decomposition of width
\tw(G)
, see Theorem
7.26
.

Let us now motivate Theorem
1.1
by explaining its relation with several well studied classes of graphs.

Even-hole and odd-hole-free graphs

A hole is
even
or
odd
according to the parity of its length. Even-hole-free graphs attracted a lot of attention, see
[
28
]
for a survey.
Despite decades of work resulting in decomposition theorems
[
10
,
14
]
and polynomial time recognition algorithms
[
11
,
20
]
, no structure theorem is known for them.
Also several questions are still open, such as the existence of a polynomial time algorithm to colour them or to find a maximum independent set.

Still, a complete structural description is known for some subclasses of even-hole-free graphs, such as the classical
chordal graphs
(that are the hole-free graphs) or the graphs where all holes have length
2k+1
for some fixed integer
k\geq 3
, see
[
12
]
.

Here, we are interested in the (even hole, triangle)-free graphs. A slightly more general class was studied by Conforti, Cornuéjols, Kapoor and Vušković in
[
8
]
that is the main inspiration of this work.
Let us explain this.
A wheel
(H,c)
is
even
if
c
has an even number of neighbours in
H
. A
prism
is a graph made of three vertex-disjoint chordless paths

P_{1}=a_{1}\dots b_{1}
,
P_{2}=a_{2}\dots b_{2}
,
P_{3}=a_{3}\dots b_{3}
of length at least 1, such that
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}
and
b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}
are triangles and no edges exist between the paths except those of the two
triangles.
A graph is
odd-signable
if it contains no theta, no prism and no even wheel. Observe that since a prism contains a triangle, a triangle-free graph is odd-signable if and only if it contains no theta and no even wheel.
This is precisely the class studied in
[
8
]
: triangle-free odd-signable graphs, or equivalently (theta, triangle, even wheel)-free graphs (from now on, we use this last name because it is more consistent with the others classes that we consider).

It turns out that all thetas, prisms and even wheels contain even holes, and triangle-free wacs contain either an even wheel or a theta (and therefore even holes), see Section
7.1
for more explanations.
So, the class of (theta, triangle, wac)-free graphs is a super-class of (theta, triangle, even wheel)-free graphs, that is in turn a super-class of (even hole, triangle)-free graphs.
These inclusions are strict, as shown by an even hole and an even wheel. Our work is therefore a generalisation of
[
8
]
.

Theorem
1.1
specialises well to the subclasses that we consider as explained in Section
7.1
: we just have to restrict the basic class. The technicalities in our proofs are very similar to the ones in
[
8
]
, but our results differ in several ways.
They apply to a more general class and exhibit basic classes and separators not described in
[
8
]
.
Still,
[
8
]
contains a nice characterisation of the classes under consideration by the so-called
good ear addition
, and we show in Section
7.2
that it can be viewed as a corollary of Theorem
1.1
, see Theorem
7.6
.

Odd-hole-free graphs also attracted some attention, so one may wonder if our theorems implies something for them. It turns out that (triangle, odd hole)-free graphs exactly form the class of bipartite graphs, and this is why we consider bipartite (theta, wac)-free graphs in what follows.

Theta-free graphs

Since every theta contains an even hole, theta-free graphs are a generalisation of even-hole-free graphs.
Little is known about the structure of theta-free graphs.
A structural description is available for some subclasses, such as claw-free graphs
[
7
]
(where the
claw
is the graph on four vertices, made of one vertex adjacent to three pairwise non-adjacent vertices) and (theta, wheel)-free graphs
[
15
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.
Many open problems remain, such as the existence of a polynomial time algorithm for the maximum independent set problem
[
17
]
or induced linkages
[
19
]

(the reference for each problem refers to a paper where the question is solved for claw-free graphs). Also, theta-free graphs might have a quadratic
\chi
-binding function, see
[
5
]
.

So one more time, we believe that the triangle-free case is of interest.
In
[
25
]
, it is proved that every (theta, triangle)-free graph with no clique separator and distinct from the cube has a vertex of degree at most 2 (implying that every (theta, triangle)-free graph is 3-colourable). In
[
1
]
, it is proved that (theta, triangle)-free graphs have logarithmic treewidth.
This suggests that (theta, triangle)-free graphs might have a simple structure, but in
[
27
]
, it is shown that there are complex enough to contain the so-called (theta, triangle)-free layered wheel (and in particular arbitrarily large complete graphs as a minor).

We hope that the present work is a step toward a full understanding of the structure of (theta, triangle)-free graphs, see Section
7.8
.

Planarity and widths

Our structure theorem allows understanding several properties of the classes we consider. For instance, in Section
7.3
, we give a simple criterion for a (theta, triangle, wac)-free graph to be planar (see Theorem
7.8
), and we deduce that bipartite (theta, wac)-free graphs are all planar, which is maybe not obvious from their definition.

In Section
7.4
, we prove several consequences of our structure theorem for some width parameters.
There have been a recent interest in understanding the interplay between excluding induced subgraph and bounding the treewidth (definitions are postponed to Section
7.4
), see for instance
[
1
]
.
A full understanding of this interplay is maybe hopeless as indicated in
[
2
]
, but in our particular class, we explicitly exhibit certificates for all possible values of the treewidth, see Lemma
7.16
.
In particular, as already stated in Theorem
1.2
, we prove that the treewidth of any (theta, triangle, wac)-free graph is at most 4, which improves and generalises the bound of 5 proved in
[
6
]
for (theta, triangle, even wheel)-free graphs (and therefore (even hole, triangle)-free graphs).
Moreover, our method is potentially applicable to other classes of graphs, because we prove that the separators that we use preserve the treewidth in quite a strong sense, see Lemma
7.13
.

It was observed in
[
6
]
that bounding the treewidth of (theta, triangle, even wheel)-free graphs by
t
immediately yields bounds for other parameters and other classes.
More precisely, an upper bound of
3\times 2^{t}
for the cliquewidth of (theta, triangle, even wheel)-free graphs is obtained. A consequence is that for any (theta, cap,
C_{4}
, even wheel)-free graph, the cliquewidth is also at most
3\times 2^{t}
and the treewidth at most
(t+1)\omega(G)-1
, where
C_{4}
denotes the hole of length 4, a
cap
is a cycle of length at least 5 with with exactly one chord creating a triangle with the cycle and
\omega(G)
denotes the maximum number of pairwise adjacent vertices in
G
.
Hence our result allows to bring the cliquewidth down from 48 to 24 and the treewidth from
6\omega(G)-1
to
5\omega(G)-1
for the relevant classes, see Section
7.4
.

Note that in contrast, (theta, triangle)-free graphs and (
K_{4}
, even hole)-free graphs have arbitrarily large treewidth and cliquewidth as proved in
[
27
]
.

Algorithms

Since the treewidth of (theta, triangle, wac)-free graphs is bounded, many problems can be solved in polynomial time for them. We still describe in Section
7.6
a self-contained decomposition algorithm for an input graph
G
, running in time
O(|V(G)|^{4}|E(G)|)
not relying on the treewidth, and with two applications: recognition and actually building a tree representation of width
\tw(G)
provided that the graph is (theta, triangle, wac)-free, see Theorem
7.26
.

Note that for the recognition algorithm, an obvious approach would be to test separately for the presence of a theta, a triangle, and a wac in the input graph. This is tempting because detecting a theta can be performed in polynomial time, see
[
20
]
for the fastest known algorithm. But this approach fails since we prove in Section
7.5
that testing for a wac is an NP-complete problem, see Theorem
7.17
.

Structure theorem

As explained in Section
7.7
, Theorem
1.1
is not just a decomposition theorem, but rather a complete structural description of (theta, triangle, wac)-free graphs (that specialises to (theta, triangle, even wheel)-free, (even hole, triangle)-free and bipartite (theta, wac)-free graphs), see Theorem
7.27
.

Separators

We here define all the separators that we need in our structure
theorem. We call
separator
of
G
a set
X
of vertices of a graph

such that

has at least two connected components. In what follows

of two non-adjacent vertices, or a set inducing a path

Since we are interested in triangle-free graphs, the clique separator is on at most two vertices, and the possible separators are precisely the induced subgraphs of some path of length 2. There might therefore be a more unified way to view all of them, but we believe this would lead to unnecessary abstraction and complicated explanations, so we prefer viewing them one by one.

Clique separator

A

in a graph

K

has at least two connected components.

Note that basic graphs, thetas, triangle, wacs, even holes, prisms, even wheels and odd holes have no clique separators.

When

, …,

Lemma 3.1
.

When
G

theta-free (resp. triangle-free, wac-free, turtle-free, c-wac-free, even-hole-free, prism-free, even-wheel-free, bipartite) if and only if all its blocks of decomposition with respect to

are theta-free (resp. triangle-free, wac-free, turtle-free, c-wac-free, even-hole-free, prism-free, even-wheel-free, bipartite).

Proof.

If
G
is theta-free (resp. triangle-free, wac-free, turtle-free, c-wac-free, even-hole-free, prism-free, even-wheel-free, bipartite) then its block of decomposition are also theta-free (resp. triangle-free, wac-free, turtle-free, c-wac-free, even-hole-free, prism-free, even-wheel-free, bipartite) since they are induced subgraphs of
G

Let us now consider all the converse claims. We view “bipartite” as “(triangle, odd hole)-free”, so each claim is about excluding some structures. Each time, we assume that some structure
H

has no clique separator as we already observed, so if it is present in

∎

Lemma 7.4

Suppose that

It is easy to obtain variants of Theorem

Of course, when restricted to (theta, triangle, wac)-free graphs, the problem of testing for good

7.3
Planarity

We now show that our description allows to understand the planar graphs in our class. By the Jordan curve theorem, any hole
C
in a planar embedding of some graph is a simple closed curve that partitions its complement into a bounded open set and an unbounded open set. They are respectively the
interior
and the
exterior
of
C
.

Theorem 7.8
.

A (theta, triangle, wac)-free graph is planar if and only if it contains no full odd daisy.

Proof.

A full odd daisy is obviously non planar, so a planar graph cannot contain it. The proof of the converse is by induction on the number of vertices of a (theta, triangle, wac)-free graph

that contains no full odd daisy. By Theorem

, it is sufficient to consider the following cases.

Case 1:

is basic (this is the base case of the induction).
The graphs

and the cube are planar.
When

is a daisy, we consider the following planar embedding of

: draw the base hole

as a circle.
Then draw every petal alternatively in the interior and in the exterior of

.
Since the daisy is not full and odd, this yields a planar embedding.

From here on, we consider blocks of decomposition of

, that are proper induced subgraphs of

, and are therefore planar by the induction hypothesis.

Case 2:

G
has a clique separator.
Since

is triangle-free, the clique separator is on at most 2 vertices and a planar embedding of

From here on, we consider blocks of decomposition

-separator

We consider planar embeddings

Case 4:

Indeed, the paths

7.4
Treewidth and cliquewidth

A
tree representation
of a graph
G
is a pair
(T,\mathcal{X})
where
T
is a tree and
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{i}\subseteq V(G):i\in V(T)\}
is a family of subsets of
V(G)
(called
bags
) such that:

1.

For every vertex
v\in V(G)
, there exists at least one bag
X_{i}
such that
v\in X_{i}
.

2.

For every edge
uv\in E(G)
, there exists at least one bag
X_{i}
such that
u,v\in X_{i}
.

3.

For every vertex
v\in V(G)
, the set of nodes
\{i\in V(T):v\in X_{i}\}
induces a connected graph (so a subtree of
T
).

The
width
of a tree representation
(T,\mathcal{X})
is defined as
\max_{i\in V(T)}|X_{i}|-1
.
The
treewidth
of a graph
G
is the minimum width over all possible tree representations of
G
.

Lemma 7.10
.

If
(T,\mathcal{X})
is a tree representation of some graph
G
and
H
is a connected subgraph of
G
, then
\{i\in V(T):X_{i}\cap H\neq\emptyset\}
induces a connected subgraph of
T
(so a subtree of
T
).

Proof.

Clear.
∎

The following lemma is often referred to as the Helly property for subtrees of a tree.

Lemma 7.11

(see
[
18
]
)
.

If
T_{1}
, …,
T_{k}
are subtrees of some tree

such that for all

we have

, then
V(T_{1})\cap\dots\cap V(T_{k})\neq\emptyset
.

When
G
and
H
are graphs, a
model for an
H
-minor in a graph

is a family
(X_{i})_{i\in V(H)}
of disjoint connected nonempty subsets of

such that for all distinct

, if

, then there exists

and

such that

. We also say that

contains

as a minor
. The following is well known (and a direct consequence of Lemma

Lemma 7.12
.

If

and

are graphs such that

contains

as a minor, then
\tw(G)\geq\tw(H)
.

Lemma 7.13
.

Let

-separator and let

, …,

.

Proof.

We consider the separators one by one.

Proper 2-separator
.
Let
(X,\{a,b\},Y)

.

There exists an algorithm that decides in time

7.7

We insist that Theorem

A (theta, triangle, wac)-free graph can be obtained from basic graphs by the operations because of Theorem

Open questions

A natural question is whether Theorem

We wonder whether it is possible to describe the most general (theta, triangle)-free graph with no separator as defined in the present work. We would need to combine in the description at least the (theta, triangle)-free layered wheels from
