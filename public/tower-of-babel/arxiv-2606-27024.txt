Cohen, Levitzki, Hilbert Basis, and Lasker-Noether Theorems for Nil- S S -Noetherian Rings
Aman Pandey, Ajim Uddin Ansari, Tushar Singh

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a new class of rings called Nil- S S -Noetherian rings, which generalizes both S S -Noetherian rings and N ​ i ​ l ∗ Nil_{*} -Noetherian rings. We investigate several properties of this new class and establish generalized versions of some classical results, including Cohen’s theorem, Levitzki’s theorem, and Hilbert’s basis theorem. Furthermore, we prove S S -version of classical Lasker-Noether theorem for Nil- S S -Noetherian rings.

Theorem 2.5

Let

1.

(1)

I

(3)

, and it is well known that an ideal generated by nilpotent elements is a nil ideal. Thus
\varphi(I)R^{\prime}
is a nil ideal of
R^{\prime}
. Since
R^{\prime}
is Nil-
\varphi(S)
-Noetherian, there exist
s^{\prime}\in\varphi(S)
and a finitely generated ideal
J^{\prime}\subseteq R^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}\varphi(I)R^{\prime}\subseteq J^{\prime}\subseteq\varphi(I)R^{\prime}
. Choose
s\in S

\varphi(s)=s^{\prime}
. Applying
\rho
to the chain gives
\rho(s^{\prime}\varphi(I)R^{\prime})\subseteq\rho(J^{\prime})\subseteq\rho(\varphi(I)R^{\prime})
. We claim that
\rho(s^{\prime}\varphi(I)R^{\prime})=sI
.
Indeed, if
x^{\prime}\in s^{\prime}\varphi(I)R^{\prime}
, then
x^{\prime}=s^{\prime}\sum_{j}\varphi(i_{j})r^{\prime}_{j}
, so
\rho(x^{\prime})=\sum_{j}\rho(s^{\prime})\,\rho(\varphi(i_{j}))\,\rho(r^{\prime}_{j})=\sum_{j}si_{j}\rho(r^{\prime}_{j})\in sI
. Hence
\rho(s^{\prime}\varphi(I)R^{\prime})\subseteq sI
. Conversely, every element of
sI
has the form
\sum_{k}(si_{k})r_{k}
with

, and

\sum_{k}(si_{k})r_{k}=\rho\!\left(\sum_{k}\varphi(s)\varphi(i_{k})\varphi(r_{k})\right)\in\rho(s^{\prime}\varphi(I)R^{\prime})
. Thus

\rho(\varphi(I)R^{\prime})\subseteq I
. Therefore,
sI=\rho(s^{\prime}\varphi(I)R^{\prime})\subseteq\rho(J^{\prime})\subseteq\rho(\varphi(I)R^{\prime})\subseteq I
. Since
J^{\prime}
is finitely generated ideal in

, its image

is finitely generated in ideal in

. Thus there exists
s\in S
with
sI\subseteq\rho(J^{\prime})\subseteq I
, showing that

is
S
-finite. Hence
R
is Nil-
S
-Noetherian.

∎

Theorem 2.13
.

(Cohen theorem for Nil-
S
-Noetherian rings).

Let
R
be a ring. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

R
is Nil-
S
-Noetherian.

2.

Every prime ideal

with

is
S
-finite.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: The result is immediate from the definition
2.1
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)

is
S
-finite. Let

be a nil ideal of

is nonempty since

. Ordered by inclusion, every chain in

. We claim that

is a prime ideal. Clearly,

and

In noncommutative ring theory, one of the classical results is

Levitzki’s theorem
, which asserts that every nil one-sided
ideal of a right Noetherian ring is necessarily nilpotent. Motivated by the importance of the
S
-version, Baeck et al.
[
4
]
demonstrated that a nil one-sided ideal of a right
S
-Noetherian ring need not be nilpotent (see
[
4
, Example 2.5]
). We extend this observation to the commutative setting by proving that the same phenomenon occurs for commutative
S
-Noetherian rings when
S\cap Z(R)=\emptyset
, where
Z(R)
denotes the set of all zero divisors of
R
. To illustrate this, we first present an example of a commutative non-Noetherian ring that admits a nil ideal which is not nilpotent.

Example 2.14
.

Let
K
be a field and consider the non-Noetherian ring
R=\dfrac{K[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots]}{\langle x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2},x_{3}^{2},\dots\rangle}
. In
R
, each variable satisfies
x_{i}^{2}=0
, so every
x_{i}
is nilpotent.
Let
I=\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots\rangle\subseteq R
. Then
I
is a nil ideal since every element of
I
is nilpotent. However,
I
is not nilpotent: for each
n\geq 1
we have
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\in I^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\neq 0\text{ in }R
. Thus
I^{n}\neq 0
for all
n
, showing that
I
is a nil ideal but not nilpotent.

To establish Levitzki’s theorem in the context of
S
-Noetherian rings and Nil-
S
-Noetherian rings, we first require some preliminary
lemmas, which we state below.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
R
be a ring,
S\subseteq R
be a multiplicatively closed subset with
S\cap Z(R)=\emptyset
. If
I
and
J
are ideals of
R
, then there exists
s\in S
such that
s(I\cap J)=sI\cap sJ
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in s(I\cap J)
. Then
\alpha=s\beta
, where
\beta\in I\cap J
. This implies that
s\beta\in sI
and
s\beta\in sJ
,
\alpha=s\beta\in sI\cap sJ
. Thus
s(I\cap J)\subseteq sI\cap sJ
. On the other hand, let
\alpha\in sI\cap sJ
. Then
\alpha=s\beta_{1}=s\beta_{2}
for some
\beta_{1}\in I
,
\beta_{2}\in J
. Consequently,
s(\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})=0
,
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
since
S\cap Z(R)=\emptyset
. Therefore
\alpha\in s(I\cap J)
, and hence
s(I\cap J)=sI\cap sJ
.
∎

In Lemma
2.15
, the assumption
S\cap Z(R)=\emptyset
is essential. We illustrate this by providing a counterexample.

Example 2.16
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
S=\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,0)\}
. Consider the ideals
I=\langle(1,0)\rangle
, and
J=\langle(1,1)\rangle
, and take
s=(2,0)\in S\cap Z(R)
. Then we have
s(I\cap J)=\langle(0,0)\rangle
and
sI\cap sJ=\langle(2,0)\rangle
. Hence
s(I\cap J)\neq sI\cap sJ
, as desired.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
R
be an
S
-Noetherian ring and
I
be an ideal disjoint with
S
. Then there exist
s\in S
and an integer
m
such that
s(rad(I))^{m}\subseteq I
, where
rad(I)=\{x\in R|\ x^{n}\in I\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is the radical ideal of
I
.

Proof.

Since
rad(I)
is
S
-finite,
t(rad(I))\subseteq J\subseteq rad(I)
for some
t\in S
and a finitely generated ideal
J=\langle x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{l}\rangle
of
R
. Suppose
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
x_{i}^{n_{i}}\in I
. Take
m=\sum_{i=1}^{l}(n_{i}-1)+1
. Then
J^{m}\subseteq I
. Consequently,
s(rad(I))^{m}\subseteq J^{m}\subseteq I
, where
s=t^{m}
.
∎

Theorem 2.18
.

(
S
-version of Levitzki’s theorem for
S
-Noetherian Rings)

Let
R
be a ring,
S\subseteq R
be a multiplicative set with
S\cap Z(R)=\emptyset
. If
R
is an
S
-Noetherian ring, then every nil ideal
I
with
I\cap S=\emptyset
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Since
R
is
S
-Noetherian, it follows from
[
13
, Theorem 2.9]
that the zero ideal
(0)
admits a minimal
S
-primary decomposition
(0)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}Q_{i}
, where each
Q_{i}
is a
P_{i}
-
S
-primary ideal and
P_{i}=rad(Q_{i})
is an
S
-prime ideal. Consequently,
Nil(R)=rad(0)=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}
.
By Lemma
2.17
, for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, there exist an integer
k_{i}\geq 1
and an element
s_{i}\in S
such that
s_{i}P_{i}^{\,k_{i}}\subseteq Q_{i}
. Let
k=\max\{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\}
and
s=\prod_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}
. Then, by Lemma

,
s\,(Nil(R))^{k}=s\,\Big(\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}\Big)^{k}\subseteq\;s\Big(\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}^{\,k_{i}}\Big)=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}sP^{k_{i}}_{i}
. Consequently,
s(Nil(R))^{k}=sP^{k_{1}}_{1}\cap\cdots\;\cap\;sP^{k_{n}}_{n}\subseteq s_{1}P^{k_{1}}_{1}\cap\cdots\;\cap\;s_{n}P^{k_{n}}_{n}\subseteq Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{n}=(0)
, by Lemma
2.17
. Hence the nilradical
Nil(R)
is
S
-nilpotent. Since
I\subseteq Nil(R)
, it follows that
sI^{k}\;\subseteq\;s(Nil(R))^{k}\;=\;(0)
,
sI^{k}=0
. Since
S\cap Z(R)=\emptyset
, so

. Thus

is a nilpotent ideal of

.
∎

Next, we extend the above result to the setting of Nil-

-Noetherian
rings, where no additional condition on

is required.

Theorem 2.19
.

(

-version of Levitzki’s theorem for Nil-

-Noetherian Rings)

Let

be a commutative ring and let

be a multiplicative subset. If

is Nil-

-Noetherian, then every nil ideal

(disjoint from

) is
S
-nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
I
be a nil ideal of

. By the Nil-

-Noetherian hypothesis,

is

-finite; hence there exist

and a finitely generated ideal

with
sI\subseteq J\subseteq I
. Write
J=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\rangle
, where
a_{i}\in R
for all

. Since

and

is nil, each generator

is nilpotent. Thus for each

there exists
n_{i}\geq 1
with
a_{i}^{n_{i}}=0
. Set

∎

Recall
[
2
]
that a multiplicatively closed subset
S
of a ring

is said to be

if for each

,
\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}s^{n}R\right)\cap S\neq\emptyset
.

Theorem 2.20
.

(Hilbert basis theorem for Nil-
S
-Noetherian rings)
Let

be a ring and

an anti-Archimedean multiplicatively closed subset. If

is Nil-

-Noetherian, then

is Nil-

-Noetherian.

Proof.

Let

be a nil prime ideal of

such that

. For each

, define

. Since a polynomial is nilpotent if and only if each of its coefficients is nilpotent, it follows that each
P_{n}
is a nil ideal of
R
. Suppose
r
is the smallest integer such that
P_{r}\neq 0
. Then
P_{r}\subseteq P_{r+1}\subseteq\cdots
is an ascending chain of ideals of
R
. Also
P\cap S\neq\emptyset
and
0\notin S
, so
P_{i}\cap S=\emptyset
since
P_{i}\subseteq Nil(R)
and
Nil(R)\cap S=\emptyset
. Let
I=\bigcup_{n\geq r}P_{n}
. Then
I
is a nil ideal of
R
as each
P_{n}
is nil. Since
R
is nil-
S
-Noetherian,
I
is
S
-finite. Hence there exists
t\in S
such that
tI\subseteq\langle b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}\rangle
for some
b_{i}\in R
. Take
f_{i}\in P
such that
f_{i}(x)=b_{i}x^{d_{i}}+\text{lower terms}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
. Put
d=\max\{d_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. Since each
P_{k}
is a nil ideal of
R
and
R
is nil-
S
-Noetherian, there exists
t_{k}\in S
such that
t_{k}P_{k}\subseteq\langle a_{k1},a_{k2},\ldots,a_{kn_{k}}\rangle\subseteq P_{k}
. For each
a_{ki}
, take
f_{ki}\in P
such that
f_{ki}(x)=a_{ki}x^{k}
for all

. Let

. If
\nu>d
, then

. This implies that
tp_{\nu}=r_{1}b_{1}+r_{2}b_{2}+\cdots+r_{m}b_{m}
for some

. Hence
tf-tp_{\nu}x^{\nu}=tf-\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}b_{i}x^{\nu}=tf-\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}f_{i}x^{\nu-d_{i}}\in P
is a polynomial of degree at most

. By repeating this process, we obtain a polynomial

with

. Since

, we have

. Write

for some

. Then

is a polynomial of degree at most

. Repeating this process, we obtain
t^{\nu-l}\prod_{k=r}^{l}t_{k}f=g_{1}+g_{2}
for some

with degree at most

. But

was chosen to be the least integer such that

, hence
g_{2}=0
. Since

is anti-Archimedean,
(\bigcap_{n\geq 1}t^{n}R)\cap S\neq\emptyset
. Let
w\in(\bigcap_{n\geq 1}t^{n}R)\cap S
. Then
w\prod_{k=r}^{l}t_{k}f\in\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},f_{r1},\ldots,f_{rn_{r}},f_{(r+1)1},\ldots,f_{(r+1)n_{r+1}},\ldots,f_{d1},\ldots,f_{dn_{d}}\rangle

. Therefore
P
is
S
-finite. Hence by Theorem
2.13
,
R[x]
is a Nil-
S
-Noetherian ring.

∎

Theorem 2.21
.

Let
R
be a ring and
S\subseteq R
be an anti-Archimedean multiplicative set consisting of non-zero divisors. If
R
is Nil-
S
-Noetherian, then the power series ring
R[[x]]
is also Nil-
S
-Noetherian.

Proof.

Let
P
be a nil prime ideal of
R[[x]]
such that
P\cap S\neq\emptyset
. Let

P(0)=\{f(0)\mid f(x)\in P\}
.
Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}x^{i}\in P
. Then
f
is nilpotent as
P
is nil ideal. This implies that each coefficient
a_{n}
is nilpotent, in particular, its constant term
a_{0}=f(0)
is nilpotent.
Consequently,
P(0)
is a nil ideal of
R
. This implies that
P(0)
is
S
-finite since
R
is Nil-
S
-Noetherian. Thus, there exists
s\in S
and a finite set of polynomials
\{p_{1}(x),p_{2}(x),\dots,p_{n}(x)\}\subseteq P
such that

sP(0)\subseteq\langle p_{1}(0),p_{2}(0),\dots,p_{n}(0)\rangle\subseteq P(0)
.
If
x\in P
, then we can write
P=P(0)+xR[[x]]
. It follows that

sP\subseteq\langle p_{1}(0),p_{2}(0),\dots,p_{n}(0),x\rangle\subseteq P
.
Hence
P
is
S
-finite.
Now suppose
x\notin P
.
Let
f\in P
. Then
f(0)\in P(0)
. Write

sf(0)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{0i}p_{i}(0)\quad\text{for some }r_{0i}\in R
.
This implies that

sf-sf(0)=sf-\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{0i}p_{i}(0)=xf_{1}

for some
f_{1}\in R[[x]]
. Since
xf_{1}\in P
and
x\notin P
(as

is a prime ideal), it must be that
f_{1}\in P
. By repeating this iterative process, we can similarly write

sf_{1}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{1i}p_{i}(0)=xf_{2}
,
where

. Continuing this construction, we obtain the representation

.
Note that the coefficients
\frac{r_{ji}}{s^{j+1}}
belong to the localization

. Since

is given to be an anti-Archimedean set, we have

\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{\infty}s^{i}R\right)\cap S\neq\emptyset
.
Let

. Then for each

,

for some

.
Multiplying the expression for

by

, we get

.
This implies that

tP\subseteq\langle p_{1}(0),p_{2}(0),\dots,p_{n}(0)\rangle\subseteq P
.
Thus
P
is
S
-finite. Hence by Theorem
2.13
,

is a Nil-

-Noetherian ring.

It is well known that the Lasker-Noether theorem holds for Noetherian rings; that is, every ideal of a Noetherian ring can be expressed as a finite intersection of primary ideals. Singh et al.
[

-primary decomposition in

Definition 2.22

[

]

An ideal

) of the ring

,

.

,

. Since

, therefore

is not an irreducible ideal of

. Now, take

. Then

. Thus

-irreducible ideal of

Recall
[

-primary if there exists an

such that for all

, then either

or

, let
