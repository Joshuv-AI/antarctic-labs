Inverse boundary value problems for certain doubly nonlinear parabolic and elliptic equations
Cătălin I. Cârstea, Tuhin Ghosh

Abstract
We consider an inverse boundary value problem for the doubly nonlinear parabolic equation ϵ ( x ) ∂ t u m − ∇ ⋅ ( γ ( x ) | ∇ u | p − 2 ∇ u ) = 0 in ( 0 , T ) × Ω , \epsilon(x)\partial_{t}u^{m}-\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma(x)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\bigr)=0\quad\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega, where p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) ∖ { 2 } p\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\} , m > 0 m>0 , and the coefficients ϵ \epsilon and γ \gamma are positive. Our first main result shows that when m > p − 1 m>p-1 , the lateral Cauchy data determine both coefficients. The proof proceeds by reducing the parabolic inverse problem to an inverse problem for the nonlinear elliptic equation − ∇ ⋅ ( γ | ∇ w | p − 2 ∇ w ) + V w m = 0 in Ω . -\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w\bigr)+Vw^{m}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega. Our second main result establishes uniqueness for the pair ( γ , V ) (\gamma,V) from the nonlinear Dirichlet-to-Neumann map of this elliptic equation. The argument has two steps. First, asymptotic expansions of the elliptic Dirichlet-to-Neumann map recover the weighted p p -Laplacian Dirichlet-to-Neumann map, and and from it the coefficient γ \gamma . Second, once γ \gamma is known, linearization at a noncritical background solution yields recovery of V V . In dimension two we work under a simply connectedness assumption on the domain, while in dimensions n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 we assume that the conductivity is invariant in one known direction.

Our first main result is the following uniqueness theorem for the parabolic lateral Cauchy data set.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m>p-1
, and let
\epsilon,\gamma,\tilde{\epsilon},\tilde{\gamma}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
be strictly positive coefficients satisfying (
2
). Assume that

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{lat}}_{\epsilon,\gamma}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{lat}}_{\tilde{\epsilon},\tilde{\gamma}}.

Then the following assertions hold.

1.

If

\epsilon=\tilde{\epsilon},\qquad\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}\qquad\text{in }\Omega.

such that

\xi\cdot\nabla\gamma=\xi\cdot\nabla\tilde{\gamma}=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

\epsilon=\tilde{\epsilon},\qquad\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}\qquad\text{in }\Omega.

\Lambda_{\gamma,V}=\Lambda_{\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{V}}

Proof.

Let
u_{1}=t^{\alpha}w
be the separated solution and let
u_{2}
be any other weak solution with the same lateral data. Since
g>0
, the maximum principle for the elliptic equation implies that
w
is strictly positive in
\Omega
, hence
u_{1}
is bounded away from zero on every strip
[t_{1},t_{2}]\times\Omega
with
0<t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
. If
m>1
, then
u_{1}
is also bounded above on such strips. Applying Corollary
2.6
on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
and letting
t_{1}\to 0^{+}
gives
u_{1}=u_{2}
. Therefore the forward parabolic problem is well posed for the boundary trace
\phi_{g}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume that
m>p-1
and that

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{lat}}_{\epsilon,\gamma}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{lat}}_{\tilde{\epsilon},\tilde{\gamma}}.

Set

V=\frac{m}{m-p+1}\epsilon,\qquad\tilde{V}=\frac{m}{m-p+1}\tilde{\epsilon}.

Then the nonlinear Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps associated with (
7
) satisfy

\Lambda_{\gamma,V}=\Lambda_{\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{V}}.

Proof.

For each strictly positive
g\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, Proposition
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
show that the parabolic solution with boundary trace
\phi_{g}
is uniquely determined by the elliptic solution of (
7
). Moreover, if
u(t,x)=t^{\alpha}w(x)
, then the associated lateral flux is

\gamma|\nabla u|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u=t^{\alpha(p-1)}\gamma|\nabla w|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}w\qquad\text{on }S_{T}.

Since
t^{\alpha(p-1)}
is a fixed nonvanishing factor on
(0,T)
, equality of the lateral Cauchy data sets implies equality of the elliptic boundary fluxes for every strictly positive boundary value
g
, and hence equality of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps.
∎

4
The nonlinear elliptic problem and its Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

We now consider the nonlinear elliptic boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w\bigr)+Vw^{m}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w&=g&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(21)

where
V\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
is strictly positive.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{m+1}(\Omega)
be nonnegative. A function

w\in g+W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\qquad w\geq 0,

is called a weak solution of (
21
) if

\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}Vw^{m}\varphi\,dx=0

(22)

for every
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{m+1}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{m+1}(\Omega)
be nonnegative. Then (
21
) admits a unique weak solution

w\in g+W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\qquad w\geq 0.

Moreover, the following assertions hold.

1.

The solution
w
is the unique minimizer of the energy functional

\mathcal{E}[v]=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{p}\gamma|\nabla v|^{p}+\frac{1}{m+1}Vv^{m+1}\right)\,dx

(23)

over the affine space
g+W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

2.

There exists a constant
C
, depending only on
\Omega
,
p
,
m
, and upper and lower bounds for
\gamma
and
V
, such that

\|w\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{m+1}(\Omega)}\leq C\bigl(1+\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{m+1}(\Omega)}\bigr).

(24)

3.

If
g\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, then

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}.

(25)

4.

If
g\in C^{1,\beta}(\partial\Omega)
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
, then
w\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
and

\|w\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\bigl(1+\|g\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\partial\Omega)}\bigr).

(26)

Proof.

Existence follows by the direct method of the calculus of variations. Indeed, the functional (
23
) is coercive and weakly lower semicontinuous on
g+W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, so it has a minimizer. Since the integrand is strictly convex in
\nabla v
and
v^{m+1}
is strictly convex on
[0,\infty)
, the minimizer is unique. The Euler–Lagrange equation is precisely (
22
), so the minimizer is the unique weak solution.

The bound (
24
) follows by comparing
\mathcal{E}[w]
with
\mathcal{E}[g]
and using the positivity of
\gamma
and
V
. The maximum principle (
25
) follows from
[
36
, Theorem 2.1]
. Finally, the global
C^{1,\beta}
estimate (
26
) follows from
[
37
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

Let
g\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
be strictly positive, and let
w_{g}
denote the corresponding weak solution of (
21
). The nonlinear Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

\Lambda_{\gamma,V}:C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\partial\Omega)

is defined in weak form by

\langle\Lambda_{\gamma,V}(g),h\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla w_{g}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{g}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{h}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}Vw_{g}^{m}\widetilde{h}\,dx,

(27)

for every
h\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
and every extension
\widetilde{h}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying
\widetilde{h}|_{\partial\Omega}=h
.

Remark 4.4
.

The right-hand side of (
27
) is independent of the choice of extension
\widetilde{h}
. Indeed, if
\widetilde{h}_{1}-\widetilde{h}_{2}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, then (
22
) with
\varphi=\widetilde{h}_{1}-\widetilde{h}_{2}
shows that the two expressions agree. When
w_{g}
is smooth up to the boundary, (
27
) coincides with the classical boundary flux

\Lambda_{\gamma,V}(g)=\gamma|\nabla w_{g}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}w_{g}\big|_{\partial\Omega}.

5
Asymptotics of the elliptic Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

We write

\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\bigr)=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

(28)

for the weighted
p
-Laplace equation. In this section we distinguish the two cases
m>p-1
, and
m<p-1
. In the first two cases we assume that
v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is a solution of (
28
) with no critical points in
\overline{\Omega}
. The smoothness assumption is consistent with the nonvanishing of
\nabla v
, by elliptic regularity theory.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
define

J_{j}(\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,n.

Taylor’s formula gives

J_{j}(\zeta)=J_{j}(\xi)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\zeta_{k}-\xi_{k})\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\xi_{k}}J_{j}\bigl(\xi+t(\zeta-\xi)\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}t,

(29)

where

\partial_{\xi_{k}}J_{j}(\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}\left(\delta_{jk}+(p-2)\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right).

(30)

For a noncritical
v
, we write

A[v]=\bigl(A[v]_{jk}\bigr)_{j,k=1}^{n},\qquad A[v]_{jk}=\gamma|\nabla v|^{p-2}\left(\delta_{jk}+(p-2)\frac{\partial_{j}v\,\partial_{k}v}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\right).

(31)

5.1
The case
m>p-1

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and let
w_{\lambda}
be the solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{\lambda}\bigr)+Vw_{\lambda}^{m}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w_{\lambda}&=\lambda v&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(32)

We make the Ansatz

w_{\lambda}=\lambda\bigl(v+\lambda^{m-p+1}R_{\lambda}\bigr).

(33)

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
m>p-1
. Then the family
R_{\lambda}
is bounded in
C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
, and there exists
R\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
R_{\lambda}\to R
in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
. Moreover,
R
solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} \nabla\cdot\bigl(A[v]\nabla R\bigr)&=Vv^{m}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
R&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(34)

and for every
\omega\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
,

\langle\omega,\Lambda_{\gamma,V}(\lambda v|_{\partial\Omega})\rangle=\lambda^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\omega\,\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\lambda^{m}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\nabla\omega\cdot A[v]\nabla R+V\omega v^{m}\Bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}x+o(\lambda^{m}).

(35)

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
, the family

v+\lambda^{m-p+1}R_{\lambda}=\lambda^{-1}w_{\lambda}

is bounded in
C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
uniformly in
\lambda\in(0,1)
. By the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, after passing to a subsequence there exists
\tilde{v}\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\tilde{v}|_{\partial\Omega}=v|_{\partial\Omega}
and

v+\lambda^{m-p+1}R_{\lambda}\to\tilde{v}\qquad\text{in }C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})

as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
.

We next identify the limit. Since
w_{\lambda}
solves (
32
), we have

\lambda^{1-p}\Bigl(-\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{\lambda}\bigr)+Vw_{\lambda}^{m}\Bigr)=0

in the sense of distributions. Using (
33
), the convergence above, and the fact that
m>p-1
, we obtain

\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\text{-}\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda^{1-p}Vw_{\lambda}^{m}=0

and

\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)\text{-}\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda^{1-p}\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{\lambda}\bigr)=\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{v}\bigr).

Therefore
\tilde{v}
satisfies the same weighted
p
-Laplace equation (
28
) as
v
, with the same Dirichlet data. By uniqueness for the weighted
p
-Laplace equation with boundary value
v|_{\partial\Omega}
, we conclude that
\tilde{v}=v
. Hence

\lambda^{m-p+1}R_{\lambda}\to 0\qquad\text{in }C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})

as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
. Since the limit is independent of the subsequence chosen, the convergence holds for the full family.

Applying Taylor’s formula (
29
) with

\xi=\nabla v,\qquad\zeta=\nabla v+\lambda^{m-p+1}\nabla R_{\lambda},

we obtain

\gamma|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{\lambda}=\lambda^{p-1}\gamma|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v+\lambda^{m}A_{\lambda}\nabla R_{\lambda},

where

(A_{\lambda})_{jk}=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma\,\partial_{\xi_{k}}J_{j}\bigl(\nabla v+t\lambda^{m-p+1}\nabla R_{\lambda}\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since
v
solves (
28
), it follows that

\nabla\cdot(A_{\lambda}\nabla R_{\lambda})=V\bigl(v+\lambda^{m-p+1}R_{\lambda}\bigr)^{m}\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

with
R_{\lambda}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. In particular, because
\lambda^{m-p+1}R_{\lambda}\to 0
in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\nabla v
has no zeros in
\overline{\Omega}
, the coefficient matrices
A_{\lambda}
are uniformly elliptic for all sufficiently small
\lambda
, with ellipticity constants independent of
\lambda
. Moreover,

V\bigl(v+\lambda^{m-p+1}R_{\lambda}\bigr)^{m}\to Vv^{m}\qquad\text{in }C(\overline{\Omega}).

Schauder estimates therefore imply that
R_{\lambda}
is bounded in
C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
uniformly in
\lambda
. Passing again to a subsequence, we obtain
R\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
R_{\lambda}\to R
in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, and the limit solves (
34
). By uniqueness for (
34
), the whole family converges to
R
.

Finally, by the weak definition of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map,

\langle\omega,\Lambda_{\gamma,V}(\lambda v|_{\partial\Omega})\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\omega\,\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}V\omega w_{\lambda}^{m}\,\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using the expansion above for the flux term, together with the convergences
A_{\lambda}\to A[v]
and
R_{\lambda}\to R
in
C(\overline{\Omega})
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\omega\,\,\mathrm{d}x=\lambda^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\omega\,\,\mathrm{d}x\\[5.0pt]
+\lambda^{m}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega\cdot A[v]\nabla R\,\,\mathrm{d}x+o(\lambda^{m}).

Also,

w_{\lambda}^{m}=\lambda^{m}\bigl(v+\lambda^{m-p+1}R_{\lambda}\bigr)^{m}=\lambda^{m}v^{m}+o(\lambda^{m})\qquad\text{uniformly in }\overline{\Omega}.

Combining these two expansions yields (
35
).
∎

5.2
The case
m<p-1

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and let
w_{\lambda}
be the solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{\lambda}\bigr)+Vw_{\lambda}^{m}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w_{\lambda}&=\lambda^{-1}v&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(36)

We make the Ansatz

w_{\lambda}=\lambda^{-1}\bigl(v+\lambda^{p-1-m}R_{\lambda}\bigr).

(37)

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
m<p-1
. Then the family
R_{\lambda}
is bounded in
C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
, and there exists
R\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
R_{\lambda}\to R
in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
. The limit
R
solves (
34
), and for every
\omega\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
,

\langle\omega,\Lambda_{\gamma,V}(\lambda^{-1}v|_{\partial\Omega})\rangle=\lambda^{1-p}\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\omega\,\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\lambda^{-m}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\nabla\omega\cdot A[v]\nabla R+V\omega v^{m}\Bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}x+o(\lambda^{-m}).

(38)

Proof.

Note that

\lambda w_{\lambda}=v+\lambda^{p-1-m}R_{\lambda}

solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla(\lambda w_{\lambda})|^{p-2}\nabla(\lambda w_{\lambda})\bigr)+\lambda^{p-1-m}V(\lambda w_{\lambda})^{m}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\lambda w_{\lambda}&=v&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(39)

Since the family of potentials
\lambda^{p-1-m}V
is uniformly bounded in
\lambda
, Proposition
4.2
implies that the family

v+\lambda^{p-1-m}R_{\lambda}=\lambda w_{\lambda}

is bounded in
C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
uniformly in
\lambda
. By Arzelà–Ascoli, after passing to a subsequence there exists
\tilde{v}\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\tilde{v}|_{\partial\Omega}=v|_{\partial\Omega}
and

v+\lambda^{p-1-m}R_{\lambda}\to\tilde{v}\qquad\text{in }C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})

as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
.

As in the previous case, passing to the limit in (
39
) in the sense of distributions gives

\nabla\cdot\bigl(\gamma|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{p-2}\nabla\tilde{v}\bigr)=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega.

Since
\tilde{v}
has the same boundary values as
v
, uniqueness for the weighted
p
-Laplace equation yields
\tilde{v}=v
. Therefore

\lambda^{p-1-m}R_{\lambda}\to 0\qquad\text{in }C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}),

and again the limit is independent of the subsequence.

Applying Taylor’s formula (
29
) as above, we find that

\gamma|\nabla w_{\lambda}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{\lambda}=\lambda^{1-p}\gamma|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v+\lambda^{-m}\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}\nabla R_{\lambda},

where

(\widetilde{A}_{\lambda})_{jk}=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma\,\partial_{\xi_{k}}J_{j}\bigl(\nabla v+t\lambda^{p-1-m}\nabla R_{\lambda}\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

It follows that

\nabla\cdot(\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}\nabla R_{\lambda})=V\bigl(v+\lambda^{p-1-m}R_{\lambda}\bigr)^{m}\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

with
R_{\lambda}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. Since
\lambda^{p-1-m}R_{\lambda}\to 0
in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\nabla v
has no zeros, the coefficient matrices
\widetilde{A}_{\lambda}
are uniformly elliptic independently of
\lambda
for all sufficiently small
\lambda
. Consequently
R_{\lambda}
is bounded in
C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
uniformly in
\lambda
, and passing to the limit yields
R\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
with
R_{\lambda}\to R
in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and

\nabla\cdot\bigl(A[v]\nabla R\bigr)=Vv^{m}\qquad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad R|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

By uniqueness, the full family converges.

Finally, using the weak definition of
\Lambda_{\gamma,V}
together with the flux expansion above and the uniform convergence

w_{\lambda}^{m}=\lambda^{-m}\bigl(v+\lambda^{p-1-m}R_{\lambda}\bigr)^{m}=\lambda^{-m}v^{m}+o(\lambda^{-m}),

we obtain

\langle\omega,\Lambda_{\gamma,V}(\lambda^{-1}v|_{\partial\Omega})\rangle=\lambda^{1-p}\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\omega\,\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\lambda^{-m}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\nabla\omega\cdot A[v]\nabla R+V\omega v^{m}\Bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}x+o(\lambda^{-m}),

which is exactly (
38
).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The borderline case
m=p-1
is excluded from the present argument. In that regime the two terms in the equation scale in the same way, so the asymptotic expansions above no longer separate the conductivity from the lower-order term. Different methods would be required.

6.2
Linearization at a noncritical solution

Assume now that
w_{0}
is a noncritical solution of (

). In the present setting such solutions exist, since the families constructed in Section

are noncritical for sufficiently small or large parameter. See also Remark
6.3
. Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
and let
\tau\in(-\tau_{0},\tau_{0})

the solution of

(40)

We make the Ansatz

w_{\tau}=w_{0}+\tau R_{\tau}.

Proposition 6.4
.

The family
R_{\tau}
converges in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
to the unique solution

of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla\cdot\bigl(A[w_{0}]\nabla\dot{w}\bigr)+mVw_{0}^{m-1}\dot{w}&=0&&\text{in }\Omega,\\

(41)

Moreover, for every

\langle\omega,\Lambda_{\gamma,V}(w_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}+\tau f)\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla w_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{0}\cdot\nabla\omega\,\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}V\omega w_{0}^{m}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\tau\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\nabla\omega\cdot A[w_{0}]\nabla\dot{w}+mVw_{0}^{m-1}\omega\dot{w}\Bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}x+o(\tau).

(42)

Hence
\Lambda_{\gamma,V}
determines the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map of the linearized equation (

).

Proof.

Since
w_{\tau}=w_{0}+\tau f
on
\partial\Omega
, the family
\tau R_{\tau}
has uniformly bounded boundary values. By Proposition
4.2
applied to (

), the family
\tau R_{\tau}=w_{\tau}-w_{0}

. Reasoning as before we can quickly obtain that

(A_{\tau})_{jk}=\gamma\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\xi_{k}}J_{j}\bigl(\nabla w_{0}+t\tau\nabla R_{\tau}\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\mathcal{V}_{\tau}=mV\int_{0}^{1}(w_{0}+t\tau R_{\tau})^{m-1}\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

in

.

Finally, by the weak definition of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map and the same Taylor expansion used above,

\displaystyle\langle\omega,\Lambda_{\gamma,V}(w_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}+\tau f)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\gamma J(\nabla w_{\tau})\cdot\nabla\omega\,\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}V\omega w_{\tau}^{m}\,\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\gamma|\nabla w_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{0}\cdot\nabla\omega\,\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}V\omega w_{0}^{m}\,\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\tau\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\nabla\omega\cdot A[w_{0}]\nabla\dot{w}+mVw_{0}^{m-1}\omega\dot{w}\Bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}x+o(\tau),

which is exactly (
42
). The coefficient of
\tau
is the weak Dirichlet-to-Neumann map of (
41
).
∎

6.3
Recovery of
V
in the plane

We now assume that
n=2
and that
\Omega
is simply connected. By Proposition
6.2
, we may suppose that
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}
. We choose a noncritical solution
w_{0}
for the common conductivity, and denote by
\tilde{w}_{0}
the corresponding solution for the coefficient
\tilde{V}
with the same boundary values.

Proposition 6.5
.

Assume that
n=2
, that
\Omega
is simply connected, and that

\gamma=\tilde{\gamma},\qquad\Lambda_{\gamma,V}=\Lambda_{\gamma,\tilde{V}}.

Then
V=\tilde{V}
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.4
, the nonlinear map
\Lambda_{\gamma,V}
determines the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map of the linearized equation

-\nabla\cdot(A[w_{0}]\nabla\dot{w})+mVw_{0}^{m-1}\dot{w}=0.

Since

\det A[w_{0}]=(p-1)\gamma^{2}|\nabla w_{0}|^{2(p-2)},

we introduce the Riemannian metric

g[w_{0}]=(\det A[w_{0}])^{1/2}A[w_{0}]^{-1}=\sqrt{p-1}\left(I+\frac{2-p}{p-1}\frac{\nabla w_{0}\otimes\nabla w_{0}}{|\nabla w_{0}|^{2}}\right).

If we now set

v=(\det A[w_{0}])^{-1/4}\dot{w},

then
v
solves the Schrödinger equation

-\Delta_{g[w_{0}]}v+Q[w_{0},V]v=0

(43)

on the surface
(\Omega,g[w_{0}])
, where

Q[w_{0},V]=(\det A[w_{0}])^{-1/4}\Bigl[\Delta_{g[w_{0}]}(\det A[w_{0}])^{1/4}+mVw_{0}^{m-1}\Bigr].

The same construction applied to
\tilde{V}
gives an analogous equation involving
\tilde{w}_{0}
,
g[\tilde{w}_{0}]
, and
Q[\tilde{w}_{0},\tilde{V}]
. Since
w_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}=\tilde{w}_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}
and
\nabla w_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}=\nabla\tilde{w}_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}
are known from the nonlinear boundary data, Proposition
6.4
shows that the equality of nonlinear Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps yields equality of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps for the two Schrödinger equations. Therefore
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
gives a boundary-fixing diffeomorphism
\Psi:\Omega\to\Omega
such that

g[\tilde{w}_{0}]=\Psi^{*}g[w_{0}]

(44)

and

Q[\tilde{w}_{0},\tilde{V}]=Q[w_{0},V]\circ\Psi.

Taking determinants in (
44
), we obtain
\det D\Psi=1
. Both
g[w_{0}]^{-1}
and
g[\tilde{w}_{0}]^{-1}
are orthogonally equivalent to the constant matrix

\frac{1}{\sqrt{p-1}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&p-1\end{pmatrix}.

It follows that
D\Psi
maps one orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to another, and hence
D\Psi
is orthogonal. Since
\Psi
fixes the boundary pointwise, we conclude that
\Psi=\mathrm{Id}
. Thus

g[w_{0}]=g[\tilde{w}_{0}]\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad\frac{\nabla\tilde{w}_{0}}{|\nabla\tilde{w}_{0}|}=\frac{\nabla w_{0}}{|\nabla w_{0}|}.

Because
\Omega
is simply connected, no level set of
w_{0}
can be a closed curve contained strictly inside
\Omega
: such a curve would bound a topological disc, and the normalized gradient field
\nabla w_{0}/|\nabla w_{0}|
would then have to vanish somewhere inside by the Brouwer fixed point theorem, contradicting the noncriticality of
w_{0}
. Hence every level set of
w_{0}
meets the boundary. By rotating the normalized gradient field by an angle of
\pi/2
, we obtain a smooth tangent field for the level sets, and the identity above shows that
w_{0}
and
\tilde{w}_{0}
have the same level curves. Since these level curves start on the boundary, where
w_{0}
and
\tilde{w}_{0}
agree, it follows that
w_{0}=\tilde{w}_{0}
in
\Omega
. Returning to (
7
), we conclude that
V=\tilde{V}
.
∎

6.4
Recovery of
V
in higher dimensions

Assume now that
n\geq 3
and that
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}
is invariant in one direction. After an orthogonal change of variables, we may suppose that

\partial_{n}\gamma=0.

(45)

Proposition 6.6
.

Assume that
n\geq 3
, that (
45
) holds, and that

\gamma=\tilde{\gamma},\qquad\Lambda_{\gamma,V}=\Lambda_{\gamma,\tilde{V}}.

Then
V=\tilde{V}
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.2
, we may indeed assume that the conductivity is common. Therefore, in the asymptotic expansions of Section
5
, we may take the same noncritical weighted
p
-harmonic function
v
for both coefficients. Let
R
and
\tilde{R}
be the corresponding correction terms and set

S=R-\tilde{R},\qquad q=V-\tilde{V}.

Then subtracting the two equations for
R
and
\tilde{R}
gives

\left\{\begin{aligned} \nabla\cdot\bigl(A[v]\nabla S\bigr)&=qv^{m}&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
S&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(46)

Moreover, comparing the second terms in (
35
) or (
38
) yields

\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\nabla S\cdot A[v]\nabla\omega+q\omega v^{m}\Bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}x=0

(47)

for every smooth
\omega
.

We now perturb the background solution
v
. Let
v_{0}
be a noncritical weighted
p
-harmonic function, let
f\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, and let

v_{\tau}=v_{0}+\tau\dot{v}+o(\tau)

be the linearization of the weighted
p
-Laplace equation, as in
[
14
]
. Choosing
v=v_{\tau}
in (
47
), differentiating at
\tau=0
, and integrating by parts in the term involving
\dot{S}
gives

0=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\dot{S}\cdot A[v_{0}]\nabla\omega+\nabla S\cdot\dot{A}[v_{0},\dot{v}]\nabla\omega+mqv_{0}^{m-1}\omega\dot{v}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=\int_{\Omega}mqv_{0}^{m-1}\omega\dot{v}\,\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}S\,\nabla\cdot\bigl(\dot{A}[v_{0},\dot{v}]\nabla\omega\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}x,

(48)

where
\dot{S}=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\right|_{\tau=0}S
and

\dot{A}[v_{0},\dot{v}]=(p-2)\gamma|\nabla v_{0}|^{p-4}\Big[(\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nabla\dot{v})I\hskip 70.0001pt\\
+(p-4)(\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nabla\dot{v})\frac{\nabla v_{0}\otimes\nabla v_{0}}{|\nabla v_{0}|^{2}}+\nabla v_{0}\otimes\nabla\dot{v}+\nabla\dot{v}\otimes\nabla v_{0}\Big].

We now choose

v_{0}(x)=x_{n}.

Because of (
45
), this function is weighted
p
-harmonic. We then have

A[v_{0}]=\gamma\bigl(I+(p-2)e_{n}\otimes e_{n}\bigr),

which is the isotropic conductivity
\gamma I
stretched in the direction
e_{n}
. This allows us to use the classical Sylvester–Uhlmann construction
[
48
]
to produce complex geometrical optics solutions for the operator
\nabla\cdot(A[v_{0}]\nabla\cdot)
. More precisely, if
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfies

\zeta\cdot\bigl(I+(p-2)e_{n}\otimes e_{n}\bigr)\zeta=0,

(49)

and
|\zeta|
is large enough, then there exists a solution of the form

\omega_{\zeta}=\gamma^{-1/2}e^{\zeta\cdot x}(1+r_{\zeta}),\qquad\|r_{\zeta}\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{|\zeta|},\quad s>\frac{n}{2}.

Let
\xi,\eta,\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\eta\perp\xi,\mu,e_{n}
,
|\eta|=|\mu|=1
, and
\xi
,
\mu
, and
e_{n}
are coplanar. Define

\zeta_{\pm}=\pm s\mu+i(\xi\pm t\eta).

A direct computation gives

\zeta_{\pm}\cdot\bigl(I+(p-2)e_{n}\otimes e_{n}\bigr)\zeta_{\pm}=s^{2}-|\xi|^{2}-t^{2}\pm 2is\mu\cdot\xi\\
+(p-2)\Big[s^{2}(\mu\cdot e_{n})^{2}-(\xi\cdot e_{n})^{2}\pm 2is(\mu\cdot e_{n})(\xi\cdot e_{n})\Big].

Hence (
49
) is satisfied provided that

\mu\cdot\xi+(p-2)(\mu\cdot e_{n})(\xi\cdot e_{n})=0

(50)

and

s^{2}\Bigl[1+(p-2)(\mu\cdot e_{n})^{2}\Bigr]=t^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\Bigl[1+(p-2)\frac{(\xi\cdot e_{n})^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Bigr].

(51)

The identity (
50
) is an orthogonality condition with respect to the inner product defined by
I+(p-2)e_{n}\otimes e_{n}
. Given any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}e_{n}
, we may choose
\mu\in\mathrm{span}\{\xi,e_{n}\}
so that (
50
) holds, and then choose
\eta
orthogonal to the plane of
\xi
,
\mu
, and
e_{n}
. Finally, (
51
) is satisfied by taking

s=\Bigl[1+(p-2)(\mu\cdot e_{n})^{2}\Bigr]^{-1/2}\Bigl[t^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+(p-2)(\xi\cdot e_{n})^{2}\Bigr]^{1/2},

with
t
as a large parameter.

We now use in (
48
) the choices
\omega=\omega_{\zeta_{+}}
and
\dot{v}=\omega_{\zeta_{-}}
. Keeping only the highest-order terms in
t
, we compute

\nabla\cdot\Bigl(\dot{A}[x_{n},\omega_{\zeta_{-}}]\nabla\omega_{\zeta_{+}}\Bigr)=e^{2i\xi\cdot x}\,2i(p-2)\xi\cdot\Bigl[-s(e_{n}\cdot\mu)(s\mu+it\eta)\hskip 70.0001pt\\
-(p-4)s^{2}(e_{n}\cdot\mu)^{2}e_{n}-(s^{2}-t^{2})e_{n}-s(e_{n}\cdot\mu)(s\mu+it\eta)\Bigr]+\mathscr{O}(t)\\
=-e^{2i\xi\cdot x}\,2i(p-2)\Bigl[2s^{2}(e_{n}\cdot\mu)(\xi\cdot\mu)+(e_{n}\cdot\xi)\bigl(s^{2}(1+(p-4)(e_{n}\cdot\mu)^{2})-t^{2}\bigr)\Bigr]+\mathscr{O}(t)\\
=-e^{2i\xi\cdot x}\,2i(p-2)(e_{n}\cdot\xi)\Bigl[s^{2}\bigl(1-p(e_{n}\cdot\mu)^{2}\bigr)-t^{2}\Bigr]+\mathscr{O}(t)\\
=e^{2i\xi\cdot x}\frac{4i(p-2)(p-1)(e_{n}\cdot\mu)^{2}}{1+(p-2)(e_{n}\cdot\mu)^{2}}t^{2}+\mathscr{O}(t),

where we used the identity
\xi\cdot\mu=-(p-2)(e_{n}\cdot\mu)(e_{n}\cdot\xi)
coming from (
50
). On the other hand,

\int_{\Omega}mqx_{n}^{m-1}\omega_{\zeta_{-}}\omega_{\zeta_{+}}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathscr{O}(1)\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty.

Therefore (
48
) implies that
\widehat{S}(2\xi)=0
for an open set of
\xi
. Since
S
is compactly supported in
\Omega
, analytic continuation yields
S\equiv 0
. Returning to (
46
), we obtain
q=0
, that is,
V=\tilde{V}
.
∎

We may now complete the proof of of our result in the elliptic case.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Proposition
6.2
, the nonlinear Dirichlet-to-Neumann map determines the conductivity
\gamma
under the assumptions of the theorem. Once
\gamma
is known, Proposition
6.5
yields recovery of
V
in the planar case, and Proposition
6.6
yields recovery of
V
in dimensions
n\geq 3
under the one-directional invariance assumption.
∎

7
The parabolic inverse problem

We now deduce the parabolic uniqueness theorem from the elliptic one.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assume that

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{lat}}_{\epsilon,\gamma}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{lat}}_{\tilde{\epsilon},\tilde{\gamma}}

and let

V=\frac{m}{m-p+1}\epsilon,\qquad\tilde{V}=\frac{m}{m-p+1}\tilde{\epsilon}.

Since
m>p-1
, Corollary
3.3
shows that

\Lambda_{\gamma,V}=\Lambda_{\tilde{\gamma},\tilde{V}}.

We now apply Theorem
1.2
.

If
n=2
and
\Omega
is simply connected, then Theorem
1.2
yields

\gamma=\tilde{\gamma},\qquad V=\tilde{V}\qquad\text{in }\Omega.

Using (
5
), we obtain

\epsilon=\tilde{\epsilon}\qquad\text{in }\Omega.

If
n\geq 3
and there exists a nonzero vector
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\xi\cdot\nabla\gamma=\xi\cdot\nabla\tilde{\gamma}=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

then Theorem
1.2
again gives

\gamma=\tilde{\gamma},\qquad V=\tilde{V}\qquad\text{in }\Omega,

and therefore (
5
) implies

\epsilon=\tilde{\epsilon}\qquad\text{in }\Omega.

This proves both assertions of Theorem
1.1
.
∎
