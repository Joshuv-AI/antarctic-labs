Flow Regimes for Gas Migration in Liquid Stemming Columns

Mohmad Mohsin Thalkur, Erin Heilman, Wenfeng Li
Project ID: t26_hoss_stemming_26

Scientific and Programmatic Impact
Some underground engineering projects require the design of an effective stemming column to
confine pressurized gases within boreholes. While solid materials such as grout plugs are
commonly used, their exothermic curing can damage diagnostic tools, particularly under harsh in-
situ conditions involving elevated pressure and temperature.
A promising alternative is the use of fluids as stemming materials; however, this approach requires
thorough validation before deployment in field conditions. In this work, we model gas migration
through various fluid stemming materials in shale and dolomite under confining pressure and
elevated temperature using the state-of-the-art multiphysics code, Hybrid Optimization Software
Suite (HOSS), at LANL.
The modeling results will be validated through experiments employing X-ray radiography and X-
ray tomography at the Advanced Photon Source at Argonne National Laboratory. Preliminary
HOSS simulations show encouraging agreement with experimental observations. Moreover,
modeling has been essential for interpreting the experimental results, as the experimental data
alone did not provide sufficient clarity.

Publications
Our experimental work, currently used to validate HOSS modeling, was featured in the Nuclear
Test Detection Regular Update in December 2025. By integrating HOSS modeling with HPC
capabilities at LANL, we aim to deliver stronger results and make a more impactful contribution
to the RVDC program.

Financial impact
Our team successfully secured a user proposal at the Advanced Photon Source (APS) at Argonne
National Laboratory, a state-of-the-art facility. Although access is provided at no cost to
approved users, the selection process is highly competitive, and equivalent access for industry
can incur costs of thousands of dollars. A second proposal, submitted in March 2026, is currently
under review. The inclusion of high-performance computing (HPC) capabilities, particularly
HOSS modeling, was a key factor in strengthening these proposals and supporting their success.
