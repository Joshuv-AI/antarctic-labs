A laddered training model for breast surgery specialists: development and validation of a CBME-based precision education framework
Authors: Kailun Xu, Lingting Jiang, Yun Zhang, Yushen Du, Yong Shen, Yiding Chen, Yingkuan Shao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to develop a stepwise training model for breast surgery residents and evaluate its efficacy in improving operative skills, clinical decision-making, and patient management competencies within a Competency-Based Medical Education (CBME) framework.MethodsThe study was conducted in the Breast Surgery Department of the Second Affiliated Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine. Twenty surgical residents in their second or third year of standardized training were enrolled and randomly allocated to an experimental group (n = 10, receiving stepwise training) or a control group (n = 10, receiving conventional training). The stepwise training program consisted of three progressive levels: basic skills (L1), minor procedures (L2), and advanced procedures (L3), spanning a 6-month period. Outcome measures included the pathological concordance rate of mass puncture biopsies (L1), catheterization success rate of venous access port implantation (L2), and operative time for breast-conserving surgery performed under supervision (L3). Statistical analyses were performed to compare outcomes between the two groups.ResultsThe pathological concordance rate for mass puncture biopsies was 98.0% in the experimental group, significantly higher than 86.0% in the control group (P = 0.009). The catheterization success rate was 96% in the experimental group compared to 80% in the control group (P = 0.012). Operative time for supervised breast-conserving surgery was significantly shorter in the experimental group (120 ± 15 min) than in the control group (180 ± 20 min, P < 0.001).ConclusionThe stepwise training model significantly enhanced clinical competencies, surgical performance, and training outcomes among breast surgery residents through its structured, competency-based approach. This model offers a scalable and effective pathway for specialized training in breast surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09688-5.Keywords: Breast surgery, Stepwise teaching, CBME, Surgical skills

Background

Breast cancer remains the most commonly diagnosed malignancy among women worldwide, with an age-standardized incidence rate of approximately 5% (1 in 20) and an estimated case-fatality ratio of 1.4% (1 in 70), according to population-based cancer registry data [1]. In 2022, China reported 427,000 new breast cancer cases with a 5-year survival rate of 83.2%, still lagging behind developed nations. Despite tertiary hospitals performing most surgeries, a significant urban-rural resource disparity exists. Retrospective studies from centers in Shanghai and Beijing show the national breast-conserving rate was only 35–55%, far below Western standards [2]. Primary care hospitals (typically Grade I institutions serving rural populations) exhibit significantly lower rates of breast-conserving surgery (BCS) compared to tertiary medical centers [3]. Currently, much of surgical training in China continues to emphasize basic technical skills that should ideally be mastered during undergraduate medical education, such as surgical hand antisepsis, incision and suturing, and urinary catheterization [4]. In contrast, training curricula for breast oncology specialists in the United States require comprehensive proficiency across a broad spectrum of competencies, ranging from radiographic imaging and oncoplastic breast surgery to systemic therapies and genetic counseling [5, 6]. Competency-Based Medical Education (CBME) is designed to prepare healthcare professionals capable of meeting the demands of real-world clinical practice. In contrast to traditional educational models, which prioritize knowledge acquisition, CBME places clinical competence at the core of training, organizing learning around clearly defined outcomes that must be achieved by the end of the training program. Consequently, CBME guides the design of both curriculum content and assessment methods to ensure that trainees are equipped to effectively manage patient needs in clinical settings [7, 8]. Building on the authors’ clinical training experience and the established practices of the Breast Surgery Training Base at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine, this study introduces a stepwise CBME-based teaching model. With ultrasound serving as a bridging technology [9], the model implements a phased and tiered training structure aimed at strengthening the core clinical competencies of breast surgery specialists. This approach emphasizes a systematic, surgery-oriented curriculum that aligns with international trends in breast surgical education. Through the implementation of a scientifically structured, competency-graded training program, it seeks to enhance the acquisition of surgical skills, improve clinical decision-making, and ultimately elevate the quality of care for breast cancer patients.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

A total of 20 surgical residents in their 2nd-3rd year of standardized training at the Breast Surgery Department of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine were enrolled in this study. During the enrollment period (January 2024 to June 2024), 28 residents completed their foundational training and were eligible for the breast surgery rotation. Of these, 24 met the inclusion criteria (completion of 1–2 years of foundational training, minimum 6-month rotation availability, and passing both the breast ultrasound anatomy knowledge test and basic suturing skills assessment). From this eligible pool, 20 residents were randomly selected using stratified random sampling and allocated to either the intervention group (n = 10) or the control group (n = 10), representing 83% of the eligible population. The sample size was calculated using G*Power 3.1 (α = 0.05, β = 0.2, effect size = 1.2), which indicated that a total of 18 participants (9 per group) would be sufficient to detect significant differences in primary outcomes. To account for potential dropouts, we enrolled 20 participants, exceeding the minimum required sample size.

Instructional model design

This study was reported in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines. In this study, both groups were taught by the same pool of attending breast surgeons in the department.

Conventional Training (Control Group): Residents in the control arm followed the standard institutional training model. This conventional approach was time-based and rotation-oriented, with trainees spending fixed periods in the breast surgery department under routine supervision. Skill progression was not structured by predefined milestones; rather, learning was gained opportunistically through case exposure and attending faculty guidance during routine clinical duties. Residents generally followed the routine workflow of their assigned supervising surgeons, so their procedural exposure depended largely on daily case availability and the supervisor’s schedule. Consequently, a resident might encounter advanced procedures before systematically completing basic preparatory training. No formal stepwise curriculum or competency-based allocation of clinical learning opportunities was provided to the control group.

CBME-based Training Content: The program adopted a three-tiered competency progression (Fig. 1). Residents in the stepwise CBME group were assigned to clinical teaching opportunities according to their current competency level. New residents were preferentially matched with L1-level procedures, and progression to L2- or L3-level procedures required completion of the predefined objectives and competency requirements of the preceding stage. Thus, the model reorganized case-driven clinical exposure into sequential, competency-based practice without extending the 6-month rotation period.

L1 fundamental skills course content and assessment

Trainees master ultrasound-guided breast mass biopsy, with the primary outcome being concordance between biopsy and surgical excision pathology. Key objectives include accurate assessment of mass characteristics (size, texture, mobility), interpretation of ultrasound images, and prediction of benign vs. malignant status. Performance standards require: ≥90% accuracy in size measurement, margin evaluation, and Breast Imaging-Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) classification compared to radiology reports; ≥90% accuracy in ultrasound interpretation; and ≥ 85% accuracy in benign/malignant prediction. Biopsy performance is assessed based on mass localization, puncture site selection, technical proficiency, and patient-reported pain. Trainees must achieve accurate localization, appropriate site selection, skilled execution, and ≥ 90% pathological concordance. Clinical training begins with masses > 2 cm, advancing to 1–2 cm after initial success, and then to 0.5–1 cm masses once proficiency is confirmed. A dedicated biopsy room is equipped with 12-18G needles; 12-14G biopsy guns include a freeze-mode function to reduce bleeding risk (Fig. 2A). All procedures are documented using a standardized form (Fig. 2B) to support pathological correlation and procedural review.

L2 minor procedure course contents and assessments

The primary objective of this stage is to achieve independent performance of venous access port implantation. Training in ultrasound-guided biopsy during the L1 stage not only builds fundamental technical skills but also establishes a stable and precise operational foundation essential for port implantation. Through biopsy practice, trainees learn to accurately locate masses and adjacent structures under real-time ultrasound guidance, while mastering probe handling and image optimization to maintain clear visualization throughout the procedure. This experience in high-precision operation facilitates rapid adaptation to sonographic technical standards and promotes stable hand-eye coordination. Port implantation similarly depends on ultrasound for evaluating vascular anatomy, implantation depth, and tissue layers. Previous biopsy training enhances trainees ’acumen in image interpretation and builds substantial real-time operative experience, enabling them to adjust techniques dynamically under ultrasound guidance. As a result, trainees are better prepared to perform vessel localization, depth estimation, and catheter placement during port implantation with greater confidence, ensuring procedural continuity and operational steadiness. Furthermore, the rigorous assessment and immediate feedback embedded in the biopsy process foster more accurate evaluation of intraoperative parameters, contributing to higher overall surgical success and improved patient comfort. Assessment Components: Accuracy of vascular localization, precision of depth estimation, procedural fluency and speed, catheterization success rate, and patient comfort evaluation. Assessment Criteria: ≥95% accuracy in vascular localization; ≤2 cm error in depth estimation; ≥90/100 procedural fluency score; ≥95% success rate for catheter placement; ≥85/100 patient comfort rating. L3 advanced surgical procedure curriculum and evaluations

The L3 advanced surgery phase is a three-month training period focused on the systematic deconstruction of key procedural components in breast-conserving radical surgery. This stage is designed to progressively build mastery of core techniques and full procedural workflows, ultimately enabling physicians to perform the surgery independently.

In the initial phase, trainees develop foundational skills through ultrasound-guided puncture procedures. This training enhances their proficiency in ultrasonographic imaging and real-time lesion localization, while also improving hand-eye coordination and operational stability - essential competencies for subsequent advanced operations.

During the intermediate phase, which includes minimally invasive procedures and port implantation, trainees further apply and refine their ultrasound interpretation and precision operation skills acquired earlier. This strengthens their ability to discern fine anatomical structures and increases operational efficiency, contributing to safer and more fluid surgical execution.

In the final phase, trainees undertake breast-conserving radical surgery - a complex procedure requiring proficiency in tumor resection, lymph node management, and other critical elements. The rigorous, structured training in the preceding stages ensures that surgeons can perform precise and complete excisions while maintaining postoperative aesthetic outcomes.

The overarching instructional approach follows a stepwise progression, with skills consolidated at each level as procedural complexity increases. This method establishes a solid foundation for performing high-quality surgeries independently.

Assessment design

Assessments for both groups were conducted at comparable time points throughout the 6-month study period to ensure fair comparison, despite differences in their training structures. For the experimental group, assessments were performed at the completion of each predefined training stage: at the end of Month 1 (L1 stage), Month 3 (L2 stage), and Month 6 (L3 stage). All L1 and L2 assessments were completed within one week following each stage. For the control group, who received conventional training without staged progression, assessments were conducted at equivalent calendar time points -specifically, at the end of Month 1, Month 3, and Month 6 -using the same outcome measures as the experimental group. This ensured that both groups were evaluated on the same skills after identical durations of training, despite differences in how their training was structured. All L3 stage assessments for both groups were conducted during the last four weeks of Month 6, with each participant’s operative performance evaluated on their scheduled surgical cases.

All competency assessments were conducted by two independent, board-certified breast surgeons with over 5 years of clinical teaching experience. The evaluators were not involved in providing the training to either group. They were trained on standardized assessment criteria for each skill level. Importantly, evaluators were blinded to group assignment: assessment forms did not indicate whether a trainee belonged to the CBME or control group. This blinding helped mitigate observer bias. Both evaluators independently scored each trainee’s performance; discrepancies were resolved by consensus.

Statistical analysis

To ensure transparency, we hereby specify the unit of analysis for all outcome measures. Each resident in both groups performed five independent procedures for each assessment type (L1: ultrasound-guided biopsy; L2: port implantation; L3: breast-conserving surgery). Consequently, for each categorical outcome—including pathological concordance rate, puncture site selection rate, catheterization success rate, and rate of excellent/good cosmetic outcome-the denominator was 50 (10 residents×5 procedures per resident). Continuous variables, such as operative time and pain scores, were similarly analyzed per procedure and presented as mean ± standard deviation.

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS Statistics 27.0. Continuous variables were presented as mean±standard deviation (SD). Comparisons between two groups of continuous variables were conducted using the two-tailed Student’s t-test, while categorical variables were compared using the Chi-square test. P-value of 0.05 was set as the threshold for statistical significance.

L2 stage

The experimental group achieved a significantly higher catheterization success rate (96.0% [48/50]) compared to the control group (80.0% [40/50], P = 0.012). Significant improvements were also observed in all secondary outcomes, including vascular localization accuracy, procedural fluency, patient comfort, and operative time (all P < 0.05, Table 5).

L3 stage

Under supervised instruction, the experimental group outperformed the control group in breast-conserving surgery, with significantly shorter operative time (120 ± 15 min vs. 180 ± 20 min, P < 0.001), lower intraoperative blood loss (35.2 ± 8.7 mL vs. 58.4 ± 12.3 mL, P < 0.001), and better cosmetic outcomes (92% vs. 78%, P = 0.043).

Discussion

Competency-Based Medical Education (CBME) has become the dominant paradigm in contemporary medical training [10]. Characterized by focused competency domains and flexible training timelines, CBME is particularly well-suited for personalized clinical education, enabling learners to select tailored training activities and advance at an individualized pace to achieve defined competencies [11]. This study presents the first development and validation of a novel competency-based, progressive training model for surgical residents specializing in breast surgery in China. The model categorizes training into three sequential competency levels: basic skills (L1), minor procedures (L2), and advanced surgery/management (L3), emphasizing structured competency progression [12]. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional “one-size-fits-all” methodologies by ensuring systematic skill acquisition at each stage [13]. In breast surgical training, a stepwise instructional strategy is essential. The initial phase focuses on ultrasound-guided biopsy, developing the ability to accurately locate lesions under real-time imaging while improving hand-eye coordination and operational stability - foundational skills for subsequent complex procedures. In the intermediate phase, comprising minimally invasive surgery and port placement, trainees build upon previously acquired ultrasound interpretation and precision operation skills to refine their judgment of fine anatomical structures and enhance operational efficiency, contributing to safer and more fluid surgical execution. The final phase involves breast-conserving surgery, a comprehensive procedure requiring high proficiency in tumor resection and lymph node management. Only through rigorous training in the preceding stages can surgeons achieve precise and complete resection while preserving postoperative aesthetic outcomes. This progressive model ensures the consolidation and application of skills at each phase as procedural complexity increases, establishing a robust foundation for independent performance of high-quality surgery.

Beyond policy statements, empirical evidence confirms that structured breast surgery training and dedicated fellowship programs lead to measurable gains in technical performance and operative efficiency. For instance, a prospective comparative study on intraoperative ultrasound (IOUS)-guided breast-conserving surgery reported that general surgery residents demonstrated a clear learning curve and progressively higher rates of negative margins under specialist supervision - highlighting the value of integrated subspecialty mentoring within breast services [14]. Similarly, a multi-surgeon clinical series found that trainee involvement in breast cancer procedures was associated with increased operative duration compared to fellows and attending surgeons - an expected outcome that underscores the importance of dedicated subspecialty rotations and graduated autonomy to safely accelerate progression toward independent practice [15]. Further supporting these observations, studies across complex oncologic procedures have shown that the extent and structure of fellowship training can influence long-term patient outcomes, reinforcing the need for coherent, outcome-driven subspecialty curricula [16]. Evidence also points to specific pedagogical strategies applicable to breast surgery. A randomized trial of simulation-based ultrasound education demonstrated sustained transfer of scanning and needle-guidance skills to clinical practice, even after subsequent clinical immersion - offering a relevant precedent for ultrasound-integrated stepwise training in breast surgery [17]. Beyond simulation, a systematic review and meta-analysis of video-based coaching in surgical residents revealed significant improvements in objective technical skill, suggesting a scalable and faculty-efficient approach to enhancing operative performance in subspecialty contexts [18].

Recent research on resident performance trajectories further indicates that competence can be modeled using longitudinal assessment data, aligning with competency-based frameworks that emphasize explicit benchmarks and entrustment thresholds - key elements of modern breast subspecialty training [19]. Finally, contemporary analyses of breast surgery training ecosystems describe substantial evolution in oncoplastic exposure and skill integration within national programs, documenting broader operative repertoires among newly qualified breast surgeons. These findings provide real-world validation that structured, evidence-informed training models can enhance case complexity management and reconstructive decision-making early in independent practice [20]. Collectively, these studies substantiate the core premise of this work: a competency-based, stepwise training model - particularly one that integrates ultrasound across diagnostic and procedural milestones - effectively addresses existing educational gaps and holds strong potential to accelerate safe, independent practice in breast surgery.

Current surgical residency training in China continues to prioritize theoretical knowledge over practical skill development, lacking a well-defined progression pathway for trainees to advance to the role of primary surgeon. The prevailing competency assessment system remains confined to basic technical skills - such as suturing and knot-tying - that should ideally be mastered during undergraduate medical education [21]. Systemic deficiencies in the surgical training framework have limited evaluation to these foundational elements, while the advanced training necessary to cultivate competent independent surgeons remains underdeveloped. Breast surgery offers unique educational opportunities that must be balanced with the profound impact of breast cancer on patients’ lives. From an educational standpoint, however, the relatively accessible anatomy and clear surgical planes provide a suitable environment for graded trainee participation. This anatomical suitability -combined with the prevalence of breast cancer and the need for specialized surgeons -makes breast surgery a practical training ground, provided that patient safety remains paramount. Our stepwise model honors this balance: trainees gain progressive experience under close supervision, with competency verified at each level before advancement, ensuring educational goals are met without compromising patient outcomes. Our stepwise model also aligns with European standards for breast surgical education and quality care. The BRESO curriculum mandates structured progression from basic diagnostics (Tier 1) to advanced oncologic procedures (Tier 2), including ultrasound interpretation and sentinel node biopsy [22]. Our L1–L3 framework directly operationalizes this: L1 (biopsy/imaging) mirrors Tier 1 diagnostics, while L2–L3 (port implantation to breast-conserving surgery) correspond to Tier 2 surgical domains. This contrasts with China’s time-based residency training, where structured skill progression remains limited [23–25]. This study represents an important application of CBME principles in Chinese breast surgery education, demonstrating a shift from passive observation toward structured competency-attainment milestones. The model further supports EUSOMA quality indicators for breast cancer care, which emphasize preoperative diagnosis and multidisciplinary monitoring [26–28]. The 98.0% pathological concordance achieved in our L1 stage exceeds the EUSOMA target of ≥ 90% for preoperative diagnosis, demonstrating how competency-based training can directly contribute to meeting international quality benchmarks.

Our study addresses these educational gaps through an innovative competency-based progressive model, adapting international breast surgery training principles to the Chinese residency context while incorporating multidisciplinary collaboration. This represents the first implementation of a structured CBME-aligned training system in Chinese breast surgery education. Results indicate substantial improvements in surgical quality and patient outcomes compared to conventional training, accompanied by significantly shortened learning curves.

Because the stepwise CBME model was implemented within a fixed 6-month breast surgery rotation, training time was redistributed rather than expanded. In the conventional model, residents’ procedural exposure was largely case-driven and depended on the daily workflow of their supervising surgeons. As a result, residents might encounter advanced procedures before systematically completing basic preparatory training, which could create a steep learning curve. In contrast, the stepwise CBME model matched residents’ current competency level with appropriate clinical teaching opportunities and required completion of L1-stage objectives before progression to L2-level procedures, and subsequently to L3-level procedures. Thus, the main change was not the omission of core curricular content, but the reorganization of opportunistic clinical exposure into sequential, competency-based practice. Future curriculum iterations should maintain adequate exposure to diverse real-world cases while preserving this structured progression.

Study limitations include a small sample size (n = 20) from a single tertiary hospital, which may affect generalizability; a 6-month training period inadequate for longitudinal competency assessment; and the absence of individualized adaptation strategies for residents with varying baseline abilities. As breast cancer incidence rises and specialized training standards evolve, this study offers empirical support for national reform in surgical residency training. Future efforts should focus on extending this model to primary hospitals, incorporating tele-mentoring platforms, and establishing systematic quality improvement programs to advance breast surgical training across China - thereby bridging the persistent gap between theoretical training and practical skill development in the current residency system.
