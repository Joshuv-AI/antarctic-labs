On u u -Multiple Zeta Values in Positive Characteristic Thanks: The author was supported by the National Science and Technology Council grant no. 113-2628-M-007-004.
Hung-Chun Tsui

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the concepts of the u u -bracket, finite multiple harmonic u u -series, and u u -multiple zeta values via the Carlitz module. These objects serve as function field counterparts to the classical theory of q q -analogs. We prove that the “limits” of finite multiple harmonic u u -series at Carlitz torsion points yield Thakur’s multiple zeta values and finite multiple zeta values over 𝔽 r ​ ( θ ) \mathbb{F}_{r}(\theta) from analytic and algebraic perspectives, respectively. This can be regarded as a positive characteristic analog of the results by Bachmann, Takeyama, and Tasaka [ 3 ] . Furthermore, we investigate the properties of u u -multiple zeta values and their expansions, obtaining a family of explicit relations among Thakur’s multiple zeta values at both positive and non-positive indices.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Classical
q
-Analog Theory

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of positive integers and
\mathbb{Z}
denote the set of integers. We begin by introducing the classical
q
-analog theory. In various mathematical frameworks,
q
-analogs serve as deformations of classical formulas or theorems, such that the original theory is retrieved in the limit as
q\to 1
. The concept of
q
-analogs has important applications in various mathematical fields, especially in number theory, partition theory, and special functions (see
[
16
,
1
]
for more details).

As basic examples, we define the
q
-analogs of integers and factorials. For any non-negative integer
n
, the
q
-bracket
is defined as

[n]_{q}:=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q},

which satisfies
\lim_{q\to 1}[n]_{q}=n
. Based on this, the
q
-factorial
is defined as

[n]_{q}!=[1]_{q}[2]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q}

for
n\geq 1
, with
[0]_{q}!=1
. The
q
-factorial has many properties similar to the classical factorial. For example, the
q
-factorial admits a factorization into cyclotomic polynomials
\Phi_{k}(q)
given by

[n]_{q}!=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}\\
k\neq 1\end{subarray}}\Phi_{k}(q)^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}.

As
q\to 1
, this identity recovers Legendre’s formula

n!=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}p^{\sum_{e\geq 1}\lfloor n/p^{e}\rfloor},

since
[n]_{q}!\to n!
and the value
\Phi_{k}(1)
is
p
if
k=p^{e}
and
1
otherwise.

1.2.
Classical Theory of Multiple Zeta Values

We now turn our attention to the theory of multiple zeta values. We denote by

\mathbf{I}=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{N}^{m}\cup\{\varnothing\}

the set of indices and by

\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}:s_{1}\geq 2\}\cup\{\varnothing\}

the set of
admissible
indices. For a non-empty index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
, we define its
depth
and
weight
of
\mathfrak{s}
by

\operatorname{dep}(\mathfrak{s})=m\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{wt}(\mathfrak{s})=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m},

respectively.
For the empty index
\varnothing
, we put
\operatorname{dep}(\varnothing)=0=\operatorname{wt}(\varnothing)
. Furthermore, throughout this paper, an empty sum is defined to be zero and an empty product to be one.

We first introduce the theory of
finite multiple harmonic
q
-series
, following the framework in
[
3
]
. The classical multiple zeta value is defined by

\zeta(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}\geq 1}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{m}^{s_{m}}}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\text{ad}}.

We put
\zeta(\varnothing)=1
and denote the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by all multiple zeta values by
\mathfrak{Z}=\langle\zeta(\mathfrak{s}):\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\text{ad}}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Besides this, we also consider two other variants called the
finite multiple zeta values
and
symmetric multiple zeta values
in the following.

Consider the ring

\mathcal{A}=\left(\prod_{p}\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)/\left(\bigoplus_{p}\mathbb{F}_{p}\right),

where the product and direct sum are taken over all primes
p
. This ring was introduced by Kontsevich
[
23
]
, and it admits a natural embedding
\mathbb{Q}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}
via the diagonal map. The finite multiple zeta value, first introduced by Kaneko and Zagier
[
24
]
, is defined by

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathfrak{s})=\left(\sum_{p>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{m}\geq 1}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{m}^{s_{m}}}\mod{p}\right)_{p}\in\mathcal{A},\quad\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}.

We put
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\varnothing)=(1)_{p}
and denote the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by all finite multiple zeta values by
\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathcal{A}}=\langle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathfrak{s}):\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

On the other hand, following the framework of Ihara, Kaneko and Zagier
[
21
, Proposition 1]
, we have the notion of
stuffle-regularized multiple zeta values

Z^{\ast}(\mathfrak{s};T)\in\mathbb{R}[T],\quad\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}

satisfying

Z^{\ast}(\mathfrak{s};T)=\zeta(\mathfrak{s})\quad\text{for }\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ad}},\quad\text{and}\quad Z^{\ast}(1;T)=T

where
T
denotes a formal variable.
Then the symmetric multiple zeta value, also introduced by Kaneko and Zagier
[
24
, (87), (88)]
, is defined by

\zeta_{S}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{j}}Z^{\ast}(s_{j},\ldots,s_{1};T)Z^{\ast}(s_{j+1},\ldots,s_{m};T)\pmod{\zeta(2)}

where
\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
. We put
\zeta_{S}(\varnothing)=1\pmod{\zeta(2)}
. Here, we mention that by
[
24
, Theorem 3]
(see also
[
22
]
), the symmetric multiple zeta value does not depend on

T
and lies in
\mathfrak{Z}
.

We recall the famous Kaneko–Zagier conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(Kaneko–Zagier Conjecture)
.

There is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra isomorphism

\varphi_{\textrm{KZ}}:\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathfrak{Z}/\zeta(2)\mathfrak{Z}

such that
\varphi_{\textrm{KZ}}(\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathfrak{s}))=\zeta_{S}(\mathfrak{s})

for all
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
.

In
[
3
]
, Bachmann, Takeyama, and Tasaka provide evidence for the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture by considering the finite multiple harmonic
q
-series

Z_{<n}(\mathfrak{s};q)=\sum_{n>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{m}>0}\frac{q^{(s_{1}-1)n_{1}+\cdots+(s_{m}-1)n_{m}}}{[n_{1}]_{q}^{s_{1}}\cdots[n_{m}]_{q}^{s_{m}}},

where
\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
. Here,
q\in\mathbb{C}
is assumed to satisfy
q^{k}\neq 1
for all
0<k<n
. We also set
Z_{<n}(\mathfrak{s};q)=0
if
\operatorname{dep}(\mathfrak{s})\geq n
and
Z_{<n}(\varnothing;q)=1
. This model of
q
-series is often called the Bradley–Zhao model (see
[
5
,
39
]
). The authors in
[
3
]
showed that the “limits” of
Z_{<n}(\mathfrak{s};q)
evaluated at the
n
-th root of unity
\zeta_{n}:=\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}/n)
relate to both finite and symmetric multiple zeta values. Precisely, they proved the following:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

For any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
, we have

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathfrak{s})=\left(Z_{<p}(\mathfrak{s};\zeta_{p})\mod(1-\zeta_{p})\right)_{p}\in\mathcal{A}.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

For any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
, we have

\zeta_{S}(\mathfrak{s})\equiv\Re\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}Z_{<n}(\mathfrak{s};\zeta_{n})\right)\pmod{\zeta(2)\mathfrak{Z}}

where
\Re(\cdot)
denotes the real part of a complex number.

Since
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathfrak{s})
and
\zeta_{S}(\mathfrak{s})
arise from "limits" of the same object, it is expected that they satisfy the same algebraic relations, providing evidence for the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture. Furthermore, Bachmann, Takeyama and Tasaka used star-version of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
to re-establish duality formulas for both
finite multiple zeta star values
and
symmetric multiple zeta star values
(see
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
). We also mention that similar phenomena for variants of finite and symmetric multiple zeta values have also been investigated in
[
4
]
and
[
32
]
.

On the other hand, for any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ad}}
, the limit of
Z_{<n}(\mathfrak{s};q)
as
n\to\infty
, denoted by
\zeta_{q}(\mathfrak{s})
, is called the
q
-multiple zeta value
. Note that this limit is well-defined for
|q|<1
, and as
q\to 1^{-}
, we have
\zeta_{q}(\mathfrak{s})\to\zeta(\mathfrak{s})
for any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ad}}
. This model of
q
-multiple zeta values inherits many relations from classical multiple zeta values (see
[
5
,
26
,
39
]
). In particular, it admits a modified stuffle relation arising from the identity

\frac{q^{(s-1)n}}{[n]_{q}^{s}}\frac{q^{(r-1)n}}{[n]_{q}^{r}}=(1-q)\frac{q^{(s+r-2)n}}{[n]_{q}^{s+r-1}}+\frac{q^{(s+r-1)n}}{[n]_{q}^{s+r}},\quad n,s,r\in\mathbb{N}

(see
[
5
, (2.2)]
). We also remark that there are various other models of
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
29
,
40
,
25
,
2
]
).

We first recall the well-known Carlitz theory (see
[
20
]
and
[
33
]
for details). For any commutative ring
R
with unity, let
R
-alg
and
R
-mod
denote the category of commutative associative
R

R
-modules, respectively. Then the
Carlitz module
is defined as the functor

\mathbf{C}:A\textbf{-alg}\to A\textbf{-mod}

which sends each
A
-algebra
B
to the
A
-module
\mathbf{C}(B)=B
where the
A
-module structure is given by the unique
\mathbb{F}_{r}
-linear homomorphism

\mathbf{C}:A\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}(\mathbb{G}_{a}(B)),\quad a\mapsto\mathbf{C}_{a}(\tau):=\sum_{i=0}^{\deg a}[a,i]\tau^{i},

such that
\mathbf{C}_{\theta}(\tau)=\theta\operatorname{id}+\tau
. Here,
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{r}}(\mathbb{G}_{a}(B))
denotes the ring of all
\mathbb{F}_{r}
-linear endomorphisms of the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}
over
B
, and
\tau:u\mapsto u^{r}
denotes the
r
-th power Frobenius endomorphism on
B
. Furthermore, for any
a\in A
, we let

\mathbf{C}_{a}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\deg a}[a,i]X^{r^{i}}\in A[X]

be the associated
\mathbb{F}_{r}
-linear polynomial.

The Carlitz module
\mathbf{C}
serves as the function field counterpart of the multiplicative group

\mathbb{G}_{m}:\mathbb{Z}\textbf{-alg}\to\mathbb{Z}\textbf{-mod}.

They share many similar properties. For example, we have the
Carlitz exponential
defined by

\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{X^{r^{i}}}{D_{i}},\quad D_{i}=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}(\theta^{r^{i}}-\theta^{r^{j}}),

which satisfies the function equation

\mathbf{C}_{a}(\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(X))=\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(aX),\quad a\in A,

and plays the role of the classical exponential
\exp(z)
. This induces the following short exact sequence of
A
-modules:

0\longrightarrow\tilde{\pi}A\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{C})(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\xrightarrow{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}}\mathbf{C}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0,

which is analogous to the short exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules:

0\longrightarrow 2\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbb{G}_{m})(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\exp}\mathbb{G}_{m}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\longrightarrow 1.

Here,
\tilde{\pi}
is the
Carlitz period
given by

{\tilde{\pi}}=(-\theta)^{r/(r-1)}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\theta^{1-r^{i}}\right)^{-1}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty},

where a choice of an
(r-1)
-st root of
-\theta
is fixed (thus,
\tilde{\pi}
is well-defined up to a factor in
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times}
). We remark that
\tilde{\pi}
is transcendental (see
[
37
]
) and naturally serves as the function field counterpart of
2\pi\sqrt{-1}
.

On the other hand, for
\mathfrak{n}\in A
, the
A
-module of
Carlitz
\mathfrak{n}
-torsion points
, defined by

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}:=\{u\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}:\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}(u)=0\},

is a cyclic
A
-module generated by the element
\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}:=\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}/\mathfrak{n})
. This torsion submodule
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}
of
\mathbf{C}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
is completely analogous to the classical group of
n
-th roots of unity in
\mathbb{G}_{m}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

Next, we introduce the theory of multiple zeta values in this setting. We denote by

\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}:=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{Z}^{m}\cup\{\varnothing\}

the set of extended indices. An index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
is called
positive
if
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
. Otherwise, it is called
non-positive
. Then
Thakur’s power sum
is defined by

S_{d}(s)=\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{1}{a^{s}},\quad d,s\in\mathbb{Z}.

By the empty sum convention, we note that
S_{<d}(s)=0
for
d<0
. Now, for any non-empty index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
, we define the
Thakur’s multiple power sum

S_{d}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{d=d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0}S_{d_{1}}(s_{1})\cdots S_{d_{m}}(s_{m}),\quad d\in\mathbb{Z},

and

S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0}S_{d_{1}}(s_{1})\cdots S_{d_{m}}(s_{m}),\quad d\in\mathbb{Z}.

We conventionally set
S_{d}(\varnothing)=\delta_{d,0}
and
S_{<d}(\varnothing)=\sum_{d>d^{\prime}\geq 0}S_{d^{\prime}}(\varnothing)
for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}
where
\delta_{i,j}
denotes the Kronecker delta.
With these notions,
Thakur’s multiple zeta value
is defined by

\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})=\lim_{d\to\infty}S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})\in K_{\infty}

for any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
. We mention that for any non-empty positive
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
, we have

\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in A_{+}\\
\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots a_{r}^{s_{r}}}\in K_{\infty}.

On the other hand, for any non-positive
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
, note that the power sum
S_{d}(s)
at non-positive integer
s\leq 0
vanishes for sufficiently large
d
(see
[
33
, Theorem 5.1.2]
). Thus,
S_{d}(s)\to 0
as
d\to\infty
for any
s\in\mathbb{Z}
, which guarantees the convergence of
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
. We denote by
\mathfrak{Z}_{\infty}=\langle\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s}):\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}\rangle_{K}=\langle\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s}):\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle_{K}
the
K
-algebra generated by all Thakur’s multiple zeta values.

In 2010, Thakur
[
35
]
proved that the product of two of Thakur’s multiple zeta values at positive indices can be expressed as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear combination of values of the same weight, known as the
r
-shuffle relations
. The first concrete example was due to Chen
[
9
]
, who proved the following explicit formula:
For any
r_{1},s_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
,

\zeta_{A}(r_{1})\zeta_{A}(s_{1})=\zeta_{A}(r_{1},s_{1})+\zeta_{A}(s_{1},r_{1})+\zeta_{A}(r_{1}+s_{1})+\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r_{1},s_{1}}\zeta_{A}(i,j),

where

(1.1)

\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{r_{1}-1}\binom{j-1}{r_{1}-1}+(-1)^{s_{1}-1}\binom{j-1}{s_{1}-1},&\text{if }(r-1)\mid j\text{ and }j>0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and
\binom{m}{n}
denotes the usual binomial coefficient modulo
p
. Based on Chen’s formula, Yamamoto observed a (conjectural) inductive formula for the
r
-shuffle relations (
1.2
). The following
r
-shuffle algebra
(\mathcal{R},\ast)
was formulated in
[
30
]
:

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the free monoid generated by the set
\{x_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and let
\mathcal{R}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space generated by
\mathcal{M}
. For any non-empty index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
and the empty index
\varnothing
, we define

\mathfrak{s}^{-}=(s_{2},\ldots,s_{m})
and
\varnothing^{-}=\varnothing
. Moreover, we denote the corresponding words by

x_{\mathfrak{s}}=x_{s_{1}}\cdots x_{s_{m}}
and
x_{\varnothing}=1.

We define the
r
-shuffle product

\ast
on
\mathcal{R}
inductively on the sum of depths as follows:

(1)

For any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
, define

1\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}}=x_{\mathfrak{s}}\ast 1=x_{\mathfrak{s}}.

(2)

For non-empty indices
\mathfrak{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}),\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in\mathbf{I}
, define

(1.2)

\displaystyle x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}}=x_{r_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})+x_{s_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})+x_{r_{1}+s_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})+\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\Delta^{i,j}_{r_{1},s_{1}}x_{i}((x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})\ast x_{j}).

(3)

Extend the product
\ast
to the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
\mathcal{R}
by the distributive law.

Shi showed in her PhD thesis
[
30
]
that the
r
-shuffle product
\ast
on
\mathcal{R}
indeed encodes the
r
-shuffle relations of Thakur’s multiple zeta values. Precisely, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 1.5

(
[
30
, Theorem 3.1.4]
)
.

For
d\geq 0
, let
\widehat{S_{<d}},\widehat{\zeta_{A}}:\mathcal{R}\to K_{\infty}
be the unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear maps satisfying

\widehat{S_{<d}}(1):=1,\quad\widehat{S_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}}):=S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s}),

and

\widehat{\zeta_{A}}(1):=1,\quad\widehat{\zeta_{A}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}}):=\lim_{d\to\infty}\widehat{S_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s}).

Then
\widehat{S_{<d}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism.
Consequently,
\widehat{\zeta_{A}}
is also an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism, i.e.,

\widehat{\zeta_{A}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{r})\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s}).

Next, we consider the
finite multiple zeta values over
K
. We begin with the ring

\mathcal{A}_{K}=\left(\prod_{v}\mathbb{F}_{v}\right)\Bigg/\left(\bigoplus_{v}\mathbb{F}_{v}\right),

where
v
ranges over all monic irreducible polynomials in

\mathbb{F}_{v}=A/(v)
. Notice that we have a natural embedding

. The
K
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{K}
is a natural analog of Kontsevich’s ring

and was studied in
[
11
,
30
]
. The finite multiple zeta value over
K
is defined by

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\mathfrak{s})=\left(S_{<\deg v}(\mathfrak{s})\mod{v}\right)_{v}\in\mathcal{A}_{K},\quad\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}.

We set
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\varnothing)=(1)_{v}
and denote the
K
-algebra generated by all finite multiple zeta values over
K
by
\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}=\langle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\mathfrak{s}):\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}\rangle_{K}=\langle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\mathfrak{s}):\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle_{K}
. It is immediate from the definitions and Theorem
1.5
that the finite multiple zeta values over
K
at positive indices also satisfy the same
r
-shuffle relations as Thakur’s multiple zeta values.

Main Results and Organization of the Paper

Theorem 1.6

(restated as Theorem

Let
\mathfrak{n}\in A_{+}
. For any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
, we have

\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})=\lim_{\deg\mathfrak{n}\to\infty}H_{<\deg\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}).

Theorem 1.7

(restated as Theorem
3.19
)
.

For any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
, we have

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\mathfrak{s})=\left(H_{<\deg v}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{v})\mod{\lambda_{v}}\right)_{v}\in\mathcal{A}_{K}.

Roughly speaking, taking the “analytic limit” of the finite multiple harmonic
u
-series at Carlitz torsion points leads to Thakur’s multiple zeta values, while taking the “algebraic limit” provides the finite multiple zeta values over
K
(cf. Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
).

Furthermore, we compute the formal power series expansion of
u
-multiple zeta values and show that their coefficients are in fact
K
-linear combinations of Thakur’s multiple zeta values:

Theorem 1.8

(restated as Propositions
4.6
and
4.7
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
. Then

\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})u^{N(r-1)}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!],

where

\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{n_{1}(r-1)}\cdots\sum_{i_{m}=0}^{n_{m}(r-1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}c_{n_{j},i_{j}}^{(s_{j})}\right)\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-i_{1},\dots,s_{m}-i_{m}),

for some explicit constants
c_{n,i}^{(s)}\in K
.

We remark that the above theorems hold not only for positive indices but also for non-positive ones.

In addition, following the approach of
[
30
]
(see also
[
35
]
), we show that the finite multiple harmonic
u
-series
H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)
and the
u
-multiple zeta values
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})
satisfy the same
r
-shuffle relations as Thakur’s multiple zeta values (see Corollaries
3.12
,
3.13
,
4.10
and
4.11
). As an application, we find a Hasse-Schmidt derivation
(\mathcal{D}_{N})_{N\geq 0}
over the realization map
\widehat{\zeta_{A}}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathfrak{Z}_{\infty}
of Thakur’s multiple zeta values. More precisely, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 1.9

(restated as Theorem
4.18
)
.

For
N\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{D}_{N}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathfrak{Z}_{\infty}
be the unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map such that

\mathcal{D}_{N}(x_{\varnothing})=1

and

\mathcal{D}_{N}(x_{\mathfrak{s}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{-s_{j}}{n_{j}}\right)\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-n_{1}(r-1),\dots,s_{m}-n_{m}(r-1))

for non-empty index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
. Then
for any indices
\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
, we have
\mathcal{D}_{0}=\widehat{\zeta_{A}}
and

\mathcal{D}_{N}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\mathcal{D}_{k}(x_{\mathfrak{r}})\mathcal{D}_{N-k}(x_{\mathfrak{s}}).

In effect, Theorem
1.9
provides a family of explicit relations between Thakur’s multiple zeta values at both positive and non-positive indices (cf. (
4.9
), (
4.10
)).

2.
u
-Bracket and
u
-Carlitz Factorial

2.1.
u
-Bracket and
u
-Carlitz Factorial

Definition 2.1
.

For each
a\in A
, we define the
u
-bracket
as the polynomial in the formal variable
u
given by

[a]_{u}:=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)}{u}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[u]\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!].

When
u
is specialized to a value in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, this recovers the evaluation

[a]_{u}=\begin{cases}\frac{\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)}{u}&\text{if }u\neq 0,\\
a&\text{if }u=0.\end{cases}

One sees that
[a]_{u}\to a
as
u\to 0
, analogous to the classical
q
-bracket
[n]_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}\to n
as
q\to 1
. Furthermore, we have the following immediate properties:

Lemma 2.2
.

For
a,b\in A
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{r}
, we have

(1)

[a+b]_{u}=[a]_{u}+[b]_{u}
.

(2)

[\varepsilon a]_{u}=\varepsilon[a]_{u}
.

(3)

[a]_{u}[b]_{\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)}=[a]_{\mathbf{C}_{b}(u)}[b]_{u}=[ab]_{u}
.

Proof.

(1), (2) follows directly from the
\mathbb{F}_{r}
-linearity of Carlitz action. (3) follows from the fact that

[a]_{u}[b]_{\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)}=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)}{u}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{C}_{b}(\mathbf{C}_{a}(u))}{\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)}=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{ab}(u)}{u}=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{a}(\mathbf{C}_{b}(u))}{\mathbf{C}_{b}(u)}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{C}_{b}(u)}{u}=[a]_{\mathbf{C}_{b}(u)}[b]_{u}.

∎

We now use
u
-brackets to define a
u
-analog of Carlitz factorials.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
n=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}n_{d}r^{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
where
0\leq n_{d}\leq r-1
. We define the
n
-th
u
-Carlitz factorial
by

\Gamma_{u,n+1}=\prod_{d=0}^{\infty}D_{u,d}^{n_{d}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[u]

where for each
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

D_{u,d}:=\prod_{a\in A_{+,d}}[a]_{u}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[u].

Recall that the
n
-th Carlitz factorial is defined by

\Gamma_{n+1}:=\prod_{d=0}^{\infty}D_{d}^{n_{d}}

where

D_{d}=\prod_{a\in A_{+,d}}a\in A
.
A celebrated result by W. Sinnott states that (see
[
20
, Theorem 9.1.1]
)

(2.1)

\Gamma_{n+1}=\prod_{v\text{ monic irreducible}}v^{\sum_{e\geq 1}\lfloor n/|v^{e}|_{\infty}\rfloor},

which serves as the function field analog of Legendre’s formula for the classical factorial

n!=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}p^{\sum_{e\geq 1}\lfloor n/|p^{e}|\rfloor}.

We briefly recall the notion of
Carlitz cyclotomic polynomials
(see
[
27
, Definition 7.1.4]
). For
a\in A_{+}
, the
a
-th Carlitz cyclotomic polynomial is defined as

\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(X):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\deg b<\deg a\\
\gcd(a,b)=1\end{subarray}}(X-\mathbf{C}_{b}(\lambda_{a}))\in K[X],

which serves as the function field analog of the classical cyclotomic polynomial
\Phi_{k}(X)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
. Then the following proposition provides a
u
-analog of Sinnott’s identity.

Proposition 2.4
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\Gamma_{u,n+1}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A_{+}\\
a\neq 1\end{subarray}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(u)^{\lfloor n/|a|_{\infty}\rfloor}

where
\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(x)
denotes the
a
-th Carlitz cyclotomic polynomial.

Proof.

First, notice that for
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

D_{u,d}=u^{-r^{d}}\prod_{b\in A_{+,d}}\mathbf{C}_{b}(u)=u^{-r^{d}}\prod_{b\in A_{+,d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A_{+}\\
a\mid b\end{subarray}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(u)=u^{-r^{d}}\prod_{a\in A_{+,\leq d}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(u)^{r^{d-\deg a}}.

For the last equality, we observe that each
a\in A_{+,\leq d}
has exactly
r^{d-\deg a}
monic multiples of degree
d
in
A
.
Now, write
n=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}n_{d}r^{d}
,
0\leq n_{d}\leq r-1
.
Then

(2.2)

\Gamma_{u,n+1}=u^{-\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}n_{d}r^{d}}\prod_{d=0}^{\infty}\prod_{a\in A_{+,\leq d}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(u)^{n_{d}r^{d-\deg a}}.

For each
a\in A_{+}
, observe that the total exponent of
\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(u)
in (
2.2
) is

\sum_{d\geq\deg a}n_{d}r^{d-\deg a}=\lfloor n/r^{\deg a}\rfloor=\lfloor n/|a|_{\infty}\rfloor.

Finally, note that
\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{1}(u)=u
.
We conclude

\Gamma_{u,n+1}=u^{-n}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{1}(u)^{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A_{+}\\
a\neq 1\end{subarray}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(u)^{\lfloor n/|a|_{\infty}\rfloor}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A_{+}\\
a\neq 1\end{subarray}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(u)^{\lfloor n/|a|_{\infty}\rfloor}.

∎

To see that Proposition
2.4
serves as a
u
-analog of (
2.1
), we require the following lemma:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
a\in A_{+}
with
a\neq 1
. Then the
a
-th Carlitz cyclotomic polynomial
\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(u)
evaluated at
u=0
satisfies

\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(0)=\begin{cases}v,&\text{if }a=v^{e}\text{ for some monic irreducible polynomial }v\text{ and }e\geq 1,\\
1,&\text{if }a\text{ has at least two distinct monic irreducible factors.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that

[a]_{u}=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)}{u}=u^{-1}\prod_{b\mid a}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(u)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\mid a\\
b\neq 1\end{subarray}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{b}(u).

Evaluating both sides at
u=0
, we obtain

a=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\mid a\\
b\neq 1\end{subarray}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{b}(0)=\prod_{b\mid a}f(b),

where
f:A_{+}\to A
is defined by
f(1)=1
and
f(b)=\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{b}(0)
for
b\neq 1
.

By the Möbius inversion formula on the divisibility poset of
A_{+}
(see
[
31
, Section 3.7]
), we can express
f(a)
as

f(a)=\prod_{b\mid a}b^{\mu(a/b)},

where
\mu
is the Möbius function on
A_{+}
defined by
\mu(1)=1
,
\mu(a)=(-1)^{k}
if
a
is a product of
k
distinct monic irreducible polynomials, and
\mu(a)=0
otherwise.

We now determine the values of
f(a)
by considering the following cases.

Case 1:
a=v^{e}
for some monic irreducible polynomial
v\in A_{+}
.

Any divisors of
a
are of the form
v^{k}
for
0\leq k\leq e
and
\mu(v^{e}/v^{k})=\mu(v^{e-k})
is non-zero only when
e-k=0
or
1
. Thus, we obtain

f(v^{e})=(v^{e})^{\mu(1)}\cdot(v^{e-1})^{\mu(v)}=(v^{e})^{1}\cdot(v^{e-1})^{-1}=v.

Hence,
\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{v^{e}}(0)=v
.

Case 2:
a
has at least two distinct monic irreducible factors.

We proceed by induction on
\deg a
. The base case is clear by Case 1. Suppose
\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{b}(0)=1
for all
b\in A_{+}
with
\deg b<\deg a
. Let
a=v_{1}^{e_{1}}v_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots v_{k}^{e_{k}}\in A_{+}
where
v_{i}
are distinct monic irreducible polynomials,
e_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, and
k\geq 2
. Then we have

a=\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(0)\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{e_{i}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{v_{i}^{j}}(0)\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\mid a,\ b\neq a\\
b\text{ has }\geq 2\text{ irreducible factors}\end{subarray}}\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{b}(0)\right).

By Case 1 and induction hypothesis, we have

a=\Phi^{\mathbf{C}}_{a}(0)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{e_{i}}v_{i}\cdot 1=\Phi_{a}^{\mathbf{C}}(0)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}^{e_{i}}=\Phi_{a}^{\mathbf{C}}(0)\cdot a.

This forces
\Phi_{a}^{\mathbf{C}}(0)=1
, which completes the proof.
∎

Hence, as
u\to 0
, Lemma
2.5
recovers Sinnott’s identity (
2.1
).

2.2.
Estimates for the
u
-Bracket

In this subsection, we characterize the domain of
u
for which
[a]_{u}
diverges as
\deg a\to\infty
. These estimates will be essential for the subsequent analysis.

Definition 2.6
.

Define
\mathfrak{D}
to be the subset

\mathfrak{D}=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\{u\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}:|u|_{\infty}=r^{k+1/(r-1)},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}\}.

Remark 2.7
.

We remark that
\mathfrak{D}
contains the following regions:

(1)

u=0
.

(2)

\{u\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}:|u|>r^{1/(r-1)}\}
.

Moreover,
\mathfrak{D}
avoids all non-zero Carlitz torsion points, as the absolute value of any such point is of the form
r^{k+\frac{1}{r-1}}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
(see
[
28
, Proposition 12.13]
).

We shall prove that for
u\in\mathfrak{D}
,
|[a]_{u}|_{\infty}\to\infty
as
\deg a\to\infty
by the following estimates on the terms
[a,i]u^{r^{i}}
of Carlitz polynomials
\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)
.

Lemma 2.8
.

For
0\neq u\in\mathfrak{D}
and
a\in A_{d}
, there exists a unique
0\leq i_{0}:=i_{0}(d)\leq d
such that

|[a,i]u^{r^{i}}|_{\infty}<|[a,i_{0}]u^{r^{i_{0}}}|_{\infty}

for all
0\leq i\leq d
with
i\neq i_{0}
. Moreover, we have the following properties:

(1)

i_{0}
depends only on
d
for any fixed
u
.

(2)

i_{0}(d)\to\infty
as
d\to\infty
.

(3)

d-i_{0}(d)=\kappa_{u}
for all
d
large enough, where
\kappa_{u}
is a non-negative integer depending only on
u
.

Proof.

Since
u\neq 0
, we may write
|u|_{\infty}=r^{\eta}
for some
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
. For
a\in A
with
\deg a=d
and each
0\leq i\leq d
, we have (see
[
28
, Proposition 12.11]
)

|[a,i]|_{\infty}=r^{r^{i}(d-i)}.

Hence,

|[a,i]u^{r^{i}}|_{\infty}=r^{r^{i}(d-i)}r^{r^{i}\eta}=r^{r^{i}(d-i+\eta)}.

Consider the real-valued function

R_{d}(x)=r^{x}(d-x+\eta),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Standard calculus shows that
R_{d}
attains its maximum at

x_{*}=d+\eta-\frac{1}{\ln r},

and that
R_{d}(x)
is strictly increasing on
[0,x_{*}]
and strictly decreasing on
[x_{*},\infty)
.

Let
n=\lfloor x_{*}\rfloor
. Then the maximum among
\{R_{d}(i)\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
is determined as follows:

Case 1:
n>d-1
.
The maximum is attained at
i=d
, so

\max_{0\leq i\leq d}R_{d}(i)=R_{d}(d),

and the maximum is unique.

Case 2:
n<0
.
The maximum is attained at
i=0
, so

\max_{0\leq i\leq d}R_{d}(i)=R_{d}(0),

and the maximum is unique.

Case 3:
0\leq n\leq d-1
.
The maximum is

\max_{0\leq i\leq d}R_{d}(i)=\max\{R_{d}(n),R_{d}(n+1)\}.

We now determine when
R_{d}(n)=R_{d}(n+1)
.
We compute that

\displaystyle R_{d}(n+1)-R_{d}(n)=0

\displaystyle\iff r^{n}\bigl((r-1)(d-n+\eta)-r\bigr)=0

\displaystyle\iff d-n+\eta=\frac{r}{r-1}.

Since
n\leq d-1
,
d-n
is a positive integer. Thus, this equality fails whenever

\eta\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}+\frac{1}{r-1}
.
Consequently, under the assumption that
u\in\mathfrak{D}
, the set
\{R_{d}(i)\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
has a unique maximum.

We now analyze this more precisely by considering the value of
\eta
:

Case A:
\eta>\frac{1}{r-1}
.
Note that

0<\frac{1}{\ln r}-\frac{1}{r-1}<1.

One verifies that

n=\lfloor d+\eta-\tfrac{1}{\ln r}\rfloor\geq d-1.

If
n=d-1
, then
d-n+\eta=1+\eta>1+\frac{1}{r-1}=\frac{r}{r-1}
, so

R_{d}(n+1)>R_{d}(n)
and the unique maximum is
R_{d}(d)
.
If
n>d-1
, the maximum is precisely
R_{d}(d)
as shown in Case 1.
Therefore,

\max_{0\leq i\leq d}R_{d}(i)=R_{d}(d),

and hence
i_{0}(d)=d
in this case.

Case B:
\eta<\frac{1}{r-1}
and
\eta\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}+\frac{1}{r-1}
.
One has

n=\lfloor d+\eta-\tfrac{1}{\ln r}\rfloor\leq d-1.

We remark that for
d
large enough,
n\geq 0
. By Case 3, the unique maximum lies in
\{R_{d}(n),R_{d}(n+1)\}
. Note that

\displaystyle R_{d}(n+1)-R_{d}(n)>0

\displaystyle\iff d-n+\eta>\frac{r}{r-1}.

We compute

d-n+\eta=d-\lfloor d+\eta-\tfrac{1}{\ln r}\rfloor+\eta=\eta-\lfloor\eta-\tfrac{1}{\ln r}\rfloor,

so the choice between
n
and
n+1
depends only on
\eta
.
Consequently, either

i_{0}(d)=\lfloor d+\eta-\tfrac{1}{\ln r}\rfloor=d+\lfloor\eta-\tfrac{1}{\ln r}\rfloor,

or

i_{0}(d)=\lfloor d+\eta-\tfrac{1}{\ln r}\rfloor+1=d+1+\lfloor\eta-\tfrac{1}{\ln r}\rfloor.

The three desired properties follow immediately from the above analysis, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

For
u\in\mathfrak{D}
, we have

|[a]_{u}|_{\infty}\to\infty

as
\deg a\to\infty
.

Proof.

For
u=0
, the result is clear. Suppose
u\neq 0
. Write
|u|_{\infty}=r^{\eta}
for some
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
a\in A
with
d=\deg a\geq 1
. By Lemma
2.8
and the strong triangle inequality, we have

|\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)|_{\infty}=\left|\sum_{i=0}^{d}[a,i]u^{r^{i}}\right|_{\infty}=r^{R_{d}(i_{0})}\geq r^{R_{d}(1)}=r^{\,r(d-1+\eta)}.

Hence,

|[a]_{u}|_{\infty}=\left|\frac{\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)}{u}\right|_{\infty}\geq r^{\,r(d-1+\eta)-\eta}\to\infty

as
d\to\infty
.
∎

3.
Finite Multiple Harmonic
u
-Series

In this section, we study the finite multiple harmonic
u
-series, which is viewed as a
u
-analog of the truncation of Thakur’s multiple zeta values. Specifically, we will first show that they satisfy the
r
-shuffle relations and possess an Euler-Carlitz-type formula. Furthermore, we examine both the “algebraic” and “analytic” limits of the finite multiple harmonic
u
-series evaluated at Carlitz torsion points, in view of the work of Bachmann, Takeyama, and Tasaka
[
3
]
.

3.1.
Finite Multiple Harmonic
u
-Series

First, we recall the notations

\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{Z}^{m}\cup\{\varnothing\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{I}=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{N}^{m}\cup\{\varnothing\}.

Also, by convention, an empty sum is defined to be zero and an empty product to be one.

We now introduce
u
-analogs of Thakur’s power sums and finite multiple harmonic series.

Definition 3.1
.

For
s,d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the

u
-power sum
by

H_{d}(s;u):=\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{1}{[a]_{u}^{s}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!].

For any non-empty index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the

u
-multiple power sum
by

\displaystyle H_{d}(\mathfrak{s};u)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{d=d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0}H_{d_{1}}(s_{1};u)\cdots H_{d_{m}}(s_{m};u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d=\deg a_{1}>\ldots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[a_{1}]_{u}^{s_{1}}\cdots[a_{m}]_{u}^{s_{m}}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!].

We conventionally put
H_{d}(\varnothing;u)=\delta_{d,0}
where
\delta_{i,j}
denotes the Kronecker delta. Evaluating
H_{d}(\mathfrak{s};u)
at any
u\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
such that
\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)\neq 0
for all
a\in A_{+,\leq d}
yields a well-defined element in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
.

Definition 3.2
.

For any non-empty index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the
finite multiple harmonic
u
-series
by

\displaystyle H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0}H_{d_{1}}(s_{1};u)\cdots H_{d_{m}}(s_{m};u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[a_{1}]_{u}^{s_{1}}\cdots[a_{m}]_{u}^{s_{m}}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!].

We conventionally put
H_{<d}(\varnothing;u)=1
. Evaluating
H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)
at any
u\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
such that
\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)\neq 0
for all
a\in A_{+,<d}
yields a well-defined element in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
.

Note that whenever
\operatorname{dep}(\mathfrak{s})>d+1
,
H_{d}(\mathfrak{s};u)
is an empty sum and thus equals to zero. Similarly,
H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)=0
whenever
\operatorname{dep}(\mathfrak{s})>d
.

Remark 3.3
.

One observes that as
u\to 0
, the
u
-multiple power sums and finite multiple harmonic
u
-series reduce to Thakur’s multiple power sums and finite multiple harmonic series, respectively. That is, for any index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
,

\lim_{u\to 0}H_{d}(\mathfrak{s};u)=S_{d}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d=\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots a_{m}^{s_{m}}}

and

\lim_{u\to 0}H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)=S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots a_{m}^{s_{m}}}.

Hence, we have

\lim_{d\to\infty}\lim_{u\to 0}H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)=\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s}).

Parallel to the classical theory, we introduce a
u
-analog of Thakur’s multiple zeta values.

Definition 3.4
.

For any non-empty index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
and
u\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
with
\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)\neq 0
for all
a\in A_{+}
, we define the
u
-multiple zeta value
by

\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s}):=\lim_{d\to\infty}H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)

whenever the series converges in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
.

Remark 3.5
.

Corollary
2.9
implies that for
u\in\mathfrak{D}
, the series
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})
converges for any positive index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
. For non-positive indices, note that for each fixed
s\leq 0
, the
u
-power sum
H_{d}(s;u)
vanishes for all sufficiently large
d
(see
[
33
, Theorem 5.1.2]
). Then Corollary
2.9
together with this observation guarantee the convergence of
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})
.

Remark 3.6
.

In general,
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})
may not converge to
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
as
u\to 0
. However, one can check by using similar arguments in Theorem
3.18
that there exists a sequence
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
tending to
0
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{u_{n}}(\mathfrak{s})=\zeta_{A}(s)
for any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
.

3.2.
r
-Shuffle Relations

In this subsection, we aim to show that both finite multiple harmonic
u
-series and
u
-multiple zeta values at positive indices satisfy the
r
-shuffle relations. To this end, we first establish the result in a more formal and general framework by considering the following setting:

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an integral domain containing
\mathbb{F}_{r}
and fix
d_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
a\in A_{<d_{0}}
, we assign an element
[a]\in\mathbb{K}
.
We assume the bracket satisfying the following properties:

(1)

For all
a\in A_{+,<d_{0}}
,
[a]\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
.

(2)

For all
a,b\in A_{<d_{0}}
,

[a+b]=[a]+[b].

(3)

For all
a\in A_{<d_{0}}
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{r}
,

[\varepsilon a]=\varepsilon[a].

In particular, one sees that for
u\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
with
\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)\neq 0
for all
a\in A_{+,<d_{0}}
, the
u
-bracket
[a]_{u}
satisfies all the properties.

Next, for any index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
, we consider the formal version of multiple power sum

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{d}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d=\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[a_{1}]^{s_{1}}\cdots[a_{m}]^{s_{m}}}\in\mathbb{K},\quad 0\leq d<d_{0},

and the corresponding finite multiple harmonic series

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[a_{1}]^{s_{1}}\cdots[a_{m}]^{s_{m}}}\in\mathbb{K},\quad 1\leq d\leq d_{0}.

By convention, we put
\mathbf{H}_{<0}(\mathfrak{s})=0
for all non-empty
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{H}_{<d}(\varnothing)=1
for all
0\leq d\leq d_{0}
.
We let

\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{K}

be the unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map such that

1=x_{\varnothing}\mapsto 1,\quad x_{\mathfrak{s}}\mapsto\mathbf{H}_{<d}(\mathfrak{s}),

and recall the notation
\mathfrak{s}^{-}\in\mathbf{I}
, denoting the index obtained by removing the first entry of
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
.
Then we show that
\mathbf{H}_{<d_{0}}(\mathfrak{s})
satisfies the
r
-shuffle relations.
For this purpose, we establish several preliminary lemmas:

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
0\leq d<d_{0}
and
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
be a non-empty index. Then we have

\mathbf{H}_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(\mathfrak{s}).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
0\leq d<d_{0}
and
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
be a non-empty index. Then we have

\mathbf{H}_{d}(\mathfrak{s})=\mathbf{H}_{d}(s_{1})\mathbf{H}_{<d}(\mathfrak{s}^{-}).

In the following proofs of Lemma
3.9
and Theorem
3.10
, we closely follow the arguments of Shi
[
30
, Theorem 3.1.4]
for
S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})
(see also
[
35
]
).

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
0\leq d<d_{0}
and
r_{1},s_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have

\mathbf{H}_{d}(r_{1})\mathbf{H}_{d}(s_{1})=\mathbf{H}_{d}(r_{1}+s_{1})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}\end{subarray}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\mathbf{H}_{d}(i,j)

where

\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}
is defined in (
1.1
).

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{d}(r_{1})\mathbf{H}_{d}(s_{1})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{1}{[a]^{r_{1}}}\right)\left(\sum_{b\in A_{+,d}}\frac{1}{[b]^{s_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{1}{[a]^{r_{1}+s_{1}}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in A_{+,d}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[a]^{r_{1}}[b]^{s_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}_{d}(r_{1}+s_{1})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in A_{+,d}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[a]^{r_{1}}[b]^{s_{1}}}.

Next, recall the partial fraction decomposition: For elements
x\neq y
in an integral domain, we have

\frac{1}{x^{r_{1}}y^{s_{1}}}=\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\frac{1}{(x-y)^{j}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{s_{1}}\binom{j-1}{s_{1}-1}}{x^{i}}+\frac{(-1)^{j-r_{1}}\binom{j-1}{r_{1}-1}}{y^{i}}\right).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in A_{+,d}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[a]^{r_{1}}[b]^{s_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in A_{+,d}\\
a\neq b\end{subarray}}\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\frac{1}{\left([a]-[b]\right)^{j}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{s_{1}}\binom{j-1}{s_{1}-1}}{[a]^{i}}+\frac{(-1)^{j-r_{1}}\binom{j-1}{r_{1}-1}}{[b]^{i}}\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A_{+,d}\\
0\neq f\in A_{<d}\end{subarray}}\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\frac{1}{[f]^{j}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{s_{1}}\binom{j-1}{s_{1}-1}}{[a]^{i}}+\frac{(-1)^{j-r_{1}}\binom{j-1}{r_{1}-1}}{[a-f]^{i}}\right).

Note that the first summand in (
3.3
) equals

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A_{+},0\neq f\in A\\
d=\deg a>\deg f\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{s_{1}}\binom{j-1}{s_{1}-1}}{[a]^{i}[f]^{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\left(\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{j}}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,f\in A_{+}\\
d=\deg a>\deg f\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{s_{1}}\binom{j-1}{s_{1}-1}}{[a]^{i}[f]^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}\\
(r-1)\mid j\end{subarray}}(-1)^{s_{1}+1}\binom{j-1}{s_{1}-1}\mathbf{H}_{d}(i,j).

Similarly, the second summand in (
3.3
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a-f)\in A_{+},0\neq f\in A\\
d=\deg(a-f)>\deg f\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{j-r_{1}}\binom{j-1}{r_{1}-1}}{[a-f]^{i}[f]^{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}\\
(r-1)\mid j\end{subarray}}(-1)^{j-r_{1}+1}\binom{j-1}{r_{1}-1}\mathbf{H}_{d}(i,j).

Note that
(-1)^{s_{1}+1}=(-1)^{s_{1}-1}
and
(-1)^{j-r_{1}+1}=(-1)^{r_{1}-1}
since
(r-1)\mid j
. It follows that

\mathbf{H}_{d}(r_{1})\mathbf{H}_{d}(s_{1})=\mathbf{H}_{d}(r_{1}+s_{1})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}\end{subarray}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\mathbf{H}_{d}(i,j).

∎

With the necessary lemmas in hand, we prove that
\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{0}}}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{K}
forms an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism. That is,
{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{0}}}(\mathfrak{s})
satisfies the
r
-shuffle relations.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
1\leq d\leq d_{0}
. Then
\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism. That is,

\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}})\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\mathbf{H}_{<d}(\mathfrak{r})\mathbf{H}_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})

for any indices
\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the total depth
k=\operatorname{dep}(\mathfrak{r})+\operatorname{dep}(\mathfrak{s})
. When
\mathfrak{r}
or
\mathfrak{s}
is empty, the results is clear. In particular, the base case
k=1
holds. Suppose the result holds for all
k<\ell
. We may assume both
\mathfrak{r}
and
\mathfrak{s}
are non-empty. Let

\mathfrak{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}),\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
be non-empty indices
with
m+n=\ell
.
Then by Lemma
3.7
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}})\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(\mathfrak{r})\right)\left(\sum_{0\leq d_{2}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{2}}(\mathfrak{s})\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\sum_{0\leq d_{2}<d_{1}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(\mathfrak{r})\mathbf{H}_{d_{2}}(\mathfrak{s})+\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d_{2}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(\mathfrak{r})\mathbf{H}_{d_{2}}(\mathfrak{s})+\sum_{0\leq d_{2}=d_{1}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(\mathfrak{r})\mathbf{H}_{d_{2}}(\mathfrak{s})\\
=\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(\mathfrak{r})\left(\sum_{0\leq d_{2}<d_{1}}\mathbf{H}_{d_{2}}(\mathfrak{s})\right)+\sum_{0\leq d_{2}<d}\left(\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d_{2}}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(\mathfrak{r})\right)\mathbf{H}_{d_{2}}(\mathfrak{s})\\
+\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(\mathfrak{r})\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(\mathfrak{s})\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(\mathfrak{r})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{1}}(\mathfrak{s})+\sum_{0\leq d_{2}<d}\mathbf{H}_{<d_{2}}(\mathfrak{r})\mathbf{H}_{d_{2}}(\mathfrak{s})+\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(\mathfrak{r})\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(\mathfrak{s})

For the third equality, note that empty sums are taken to be zero.
Hence, by Lemma
3.8
, we obtain

(3.4)

\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}})\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(r_{1})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{1}}(\mathfrak{r}^{-})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{1}}(\mathfrak{s})\\
+\sum_{0\leq d_{2}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{2}}(s_{1})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{2}}(\mathfrak{s}^{-})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{2}}(\mathfrak{r})\\
+\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(s_{1})\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(r_{1})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}(\mathfrak{r}^{-})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}(\mathfrak{s}^{-}).

By induction hypothesis and Lemma
3.8
, the first summand in (
3.4
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}({r_{1}})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{1}}({\mathfrak{r}^{-}})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{1}}(\mathfrak{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}(r_{1})\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{1}}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq d_{1}<d}\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{d_{1}}}(x_{r_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}}))

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{r_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})).

The second equality can be checked immediately by writing
x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}}=\sum_{i}c_{i}x_{\mathfrak{t}_{i}}
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and using the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linearity. Similarly, the second summand in (
3.4
) equals to

\sum_{0\leq d_{2}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{2}}(s_{1})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{2}}(\mathfrak{s}^{-})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{2}}(\mathfrak{r})=\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{s_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})).

Finally, by Lemmas
3.7
,
3.8
,
3.9
and induction hypothesis, the third summand in (
3.4
) is

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(s_{1})\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(r_{1})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}(\mathfrak{r}^{-})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}(\mathfrak{s}^{-})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(s_{1})\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(r_{1})\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\left(\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(r_{1}+s_{1})+\sum_{{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(i,j)\right)\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(r_{1}+s_{1})\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})+\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\sum_{{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(i,j)\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{r_{1}+s_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}}))+\sum_{{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(i)\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}(j)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{r_{1}+s_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}}))+\sum_{{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(i)\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}}(x_{j})

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{r_{1}+s_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}}))+\sum_{{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\sum_{0\leq d_{3}<d}\mathbf{H}_{d_{3}}(i)\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d_{3}}}((x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})\ast x_{j})

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{r_{1}+s_{1}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}}))+\sum_{{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\widehat{\mathbf{H}_{<d}}(x_{i}((x_{\mathfrak{r}^{-}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}^{-}})\ast x_{j})).

∎

3.3.
Euler–Carlitz-type Formula for
H_{<d}
at Carlitz Torsion Points

Recall that for each
\mathfrak{n}\in A_{+}
,
\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}=\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\frac{\tilde{\pi}}{\mathfrak{n}})
is the generator of
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}
, the
A
-module of Carlitz
\mathfrak{n}
-torsion points. In this subsection, we establish an Euler–Carlitz-type formula for
H_{<{\deg\mathfrak{n}}}(s;\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})
, which we call the
finite Euler–Carlitz formula
.

We first define the degenerate Bernoulli–Carlitz numbers.

Definition 3.14
.

For any
a\in A
, we define the
degenerate Bernoulli-Carlitz number

\mathrm{dBC}_{n}(a)\in K
by the identity

\frac{X}{\mathbf{C}_{a}\left(\frac{X}{a}\right)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{dBC}_{n}(a)}{\Gamma_{n+1}}X^{n}.

This can be viewed as the function field analog of the
degenerate Bernoulli number

\beta_{n}(k^{-1})
defined by Carlitz
[
7
]
.
Recall that the Bernoulli–Carlitz number
\mathrm{BC}_{n}\in K
is defined by the identity

\frac{X}{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(X)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{BC}_{n}}{\Gamma_{n+1}}X^{n}.

One checks that as
\deg a\to\infty
,
\mathrm{dBC}_{n}(a)\to\mathrm{BC}_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
since
\mathbf{C}_{a}(\frac{X}{a})\to\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(X)
.

Then the finite Euler–Carlitz formula is given as follows:

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
be a
r
-even integer, i.e.,
(r-1)\mid s
. Let
\mathfrak{n}\in A_{+}
. Then we have

\frac{H_{<\deg\mathfrak{n}}(s;\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})}{(\mathfrak{n}\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})^{s}}=\frac{\mathrm{dBC}_{s}(\mathfrak{n})}{\Gamma_{s+1}}.

In particular,
H_{<d}(s;\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\in K\cdot\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}^{s}
.

Proof.

Let
d=\deg\mathfrak{n}
. Note that

\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}(X)=\prod_{a\in A_{<d}}\left(X-\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}(X)^{\prime}=\sum_{a\in A_{<d}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in A_{<d}\\
b\neq a\end{subarray}}\left(X-\mathbf{C}_{b}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\right).

Thus, the logarithmic derivative of
\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}(X)
is

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}(X)^{\prime}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}(X)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X}+\sum_{0\neq a\in A_{<d}}\frac{1}{X-\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X}+\sum_{0\neq a\in A_{<d}}\frac{1}{X+\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X}+\sum_{0\neq a\in A_{<d}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})}\cdot\frac{1}{\displaystyle 1+\frac{X}{\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X}+\sum_{0\neq a\in A_{<d}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})^{s}}X^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X}+\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{0\neq a\in A_{<d}}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})^{s+1}}X^{s}.

By change of variables, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{X\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\frac{X}{\mathfrak{n}})^{\prime}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}\left(\frac{X}{\mathfrak{n}}\right)}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\sum_{0\neq a\in A_{<d}}\frac{(-1)^{s-1}}{\left(\mathfrak{n}\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\right)^{s}}X^{s}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{\times}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{s}}\right)\left(\sum_{a\in A_{+,<d}}\frac{(-1)^{s-1}}{\left(\mathfrak{n}\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\right)^{s}}\right)X^{s}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s=1\\
(r-1)\mid s\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\sum_{a\in A_{+,<d}}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{\left(\mathfrak{n}\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\right)^{s}}X^{s}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s=1\\
(r-1)\mid s\end{subarray}}^{\infty}(-1)^{s}\frac{H_{<d}(s;\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})}{(\mathfrak{n}\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})^{s}}X^{s}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s=1\\
(r-1)\mid s\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{H_{<d}(s;\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})}{(\mathfrak{n}\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})^{s}}X^{s}.

For the last equality, we notice that
(-1)^{s}=1
either when
p=2
or when
p
is odd with
(r-1)\mid s
. Finally, observe that

\frac{X\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\frac{X}{\mathfrak{n}})^{\prime}}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}\left(\frac{X}{\mathfrak{n}}\right)}=\frac{X}{\mathbf{C}_{\mathfrak{n}}\left(\frac{X}{\mathfrak{n}}\right)}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{dBC}_{s}(\mathfrak{n})}{\Gamma_{s+1}}X^{s},

so the result follows by comparing the coefficients.
∎

3.4.
“Limits” of
H_{<d}
at Carlitz Torsion Points

We then investigate both “analytic” and “algebraic” limits of
H_{<\deg\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})
as
\deg\mathfrak{n}\to\infty
in view of
[
3
]
. First, we require the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.16
.

For
m\geq 1
and elements
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
,
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}
in a commutative ring, one has

\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Bigl(\prod_{i<k}Y_{i}\Bigr)\bigl(X_{k}-Y_{k}\bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i>k}X_{i}\Bigr).

Proof.

We first recall the convention that an empty product equals one. We define for
1\leq k\leq m+1

Q_{k}:=\Bigl(\prod_{i<k}Y_{i}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i\geq k}X_{i}\Bigr).

Then

\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}=Q_{1}-Q_{m+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(Q_{k}-Q_{k+1}).

On the other hand, notice that

Q_{k}=\Bigl(\prod_{i<k}Y_{i}\Bigr)X_{k}\Bigl(\prod_{i>k}X_{i}\Bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{k+1}=\Bigl(\prod_{i<k}Y_{i}\Bigr)Y_{k}\Bigl(\prod_{i>k}X_{i}\Bigr).

Hence,

Q_{k}-Q_{k+1}=\Bigl(\prod_{i<k}Y_{i}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i>k}X_{i}\Bigr)(X_{k}-Y_{k}),

and summing over
k=1,\dots,m
yields the claimed identity.
∎

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}\in A_{+}
. Then for all
a\in A_{<{\deg\mathfrak{n}}}
,

|[a]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}-a|_{\infty}<|a|_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad|[a]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}|_{\infty}=|a|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Let
d=\deg\mathfrak{n}
. Recall that we have

|\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}|_{\infty}=r^{-d+1+\frac{1}{r-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|[a,i]\right|_{\infty}=r^{r^{i}(\deg a-i)}

for
a\in A_{+,<d}
,
0\leq i\leq\deg a
(see
[
28
, Propositions 12.11 and 12.13]
). Thus,

\left|[a,i]\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}^{r^{i}-1}\right|_{\infty}=r^{r^{i}(\deg a-i)}r^{(r^{i}-1)(-d+1+\frac{1}{r-1})}=r^{r^{i}(\deg a-d-i+1+\frac{1}{r-1})+d-1-\frac{1}{r-1}}

attains its unique maximum at
i=0
. Therefore, we obtain

|[a]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}-a|_{\infty}=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\deg a}[a,i]\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}^{r^{i}-1}\right|_{\infty}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq\deg a}\left|[a,i]\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}^{r^{i}-1}\right|_{\infty}<|a|_{\infty}.

In particular,

\left|[a]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}\right|_{\infty}=\left|([a]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}-a)+a\right|_{\infty}=|a|_{\infty}.

∎

Then we can prove that as
\mathfrak{n}\to\infty
, the “analytic limit” of
H_{<\deg\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})
is exactly the corresponding Thakur’s multiple zeta value
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
. Precisely, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}\in A_{+}
. Then for any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
, we have

H_{<\deg\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\to\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})

as
\deg\mathfrak{n}\to\infty
.

Proof.

For the empty index, the result is clear. Let
d=\deg\mathfrak{n}
. We first assume
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
is positive and non-empty.
Let
\epsilon_{a}=[a]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}-a=\sum_{i=1}^{\deg a}[a,i]\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}^{r^{i}-1}
. By Lemma
3.17
, we have

\left|[a]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}\right|_{\infty}=|a|_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\epsilon_{a}\right|_{\infty}<|a|_{\infty}.

Thus, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|[a]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{-s}-a^{-s}\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left|a^{-s}\left(\left(1+\frac{\epsilon_{a}}{a}\right)^{-s}-1\right)\right|_{\infty}.

Note that

\left(1+\frac{\epsilon_{a}}{a}\right)^{-s}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{a}}{a}\right)^{k},

where the right-hand side converges since
|\epsilon_{a}|_{\infty}<|a|_{\infty}
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|[a]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{-s}-a^{-s}\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=|a|_{\infty}^{-s}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{a}}{a}\right)^{k}\right|_{\infty}\leq|a|_{\infty}^{-s}\left|\frac{\epsilon_{a}}{a}\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|a|_{\infty}^{-s-1}\max_{1\leq j\leq\deg a}\left|[a,j]\right|_{\infty}\left|\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}\right|^{r^{j}-1}_{\infty}.

Here, note that
\left|\binom{-s}{k}\right|_{\infty}\leq 1
and
\left|\epsilon_{a}/a\right|_{\infty}<1
.
By Lemma
3.16
and the discussion above, for any fixed
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}
with
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{i=1}^{m}[a_{i}]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{-s_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{-s_{i}}\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{i<k}a_{i}^{-s_{i}}\right)\left(\prod_{i>k}[a_{i}]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{-s_{i}}\right)\left([a_{k}]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{-s_{k}}-a_{k}^{-s_{k}}\right)\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq m}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{m}\left|a_{i}\right|_{\infty}^{-s_{i}}|a_{k}|_{\infty}^{-s_{k}-1}\max_{1\leq j\leq\deg a_{k}}\left|[a_{k},j]\right|_{\infty}\left|\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}\right|^{r^{j}-1}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq m}r^{-2\deg a_{k}}\max_{1\leq j\leq\deg a_{k}}r^{r^{j}(\deg a_{k}-d-j+1+\frac{1}{r-1})+d-1-\frac{1}{r-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq\deg a_{1}}r^{r^{j}(\deg a_{1}-d-j+1+\frac{1}{r-1})+d-1-\frac{1}{r-1}-2\deg a_{1}}.

To justify the last two steps, we note that in the penultimate inequality,
|a_{i}|_{\infty}^{-s_{i}}\leq 1
for all
i
and
-s_{k}-1\leq-2
as
s_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
. For the last inequality, we observe that for any fixed
j\geq 1
, the exponent is a monotonically increasing function of
\deg a_{k}
, so the maximum is attained at
k=1
.

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})-S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{[a_{1}]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{s_{1}}\cdots[a_{m}]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{s_{m}}}-\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots a_{m}^{s_{m}}}\right)\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\left|\prod_{i=1}^{m}[a_{i}]_{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}}^{-s_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{-s_{i}}\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\max_{1\leq j\leq\deg a_{1}}r^{r^{j}(\deg a_{1}-d-j+1+\frac{1}{r-1})+d-1-\frac{1}{r-1}-2\deg a_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq d-1}r^{r^{j}(-j+\frac{1}{r-1})-d+1-\frac{1}{r-1}}=r^{r(-1+\frac{1}{r-1})-d+1-\frac{1}{r-1}}\to 0

as
d\to\infty
, which implies that

H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\to\lim_{d\to\infty}S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})=\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s}).

It remains to prove the result for non-positive indices
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
. Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
be the smallest integer such that
s_{i}\leq 0
. By
[
33
, Theorem 5.1.2]
, there exists an integer
N\geq 0
such that
S_{d}(s_{i})=H_{d}(s_{i};u)=0
for all
d\geq N
. Now, for
d>N
, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0}H_{d_{1}}(s_{1};u)\cdots H_{d_{m}}(s_{m};u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d_{i}=0}^{N-1}H_{d_{i}}(s_{i},\ldots,s_{m};u)\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{i-1}>d_{i}}H_{d_{1}}(s_{1};u)\cdots H_{d_{i-1}}(s_{i-1};u),

and similarly,

\displaystyle S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0}S_{d_{1}}(s_{1})\cdots S_{d_{m}}(s_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d_{i}=0}^{N-1}S_{d_{i}}(s_{i},\ldots,s_{m})\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{i-1}>d_{i}}S_{d_{1}}(s_{1})\cdots S_{d_{i-1}}(s_{i-1}).

Since
i
is the smallest integer such that
s_{i}\leq 0
, the preceding entries
s_{1},\ldots,s_{i-1}
are all positive. Thus, by applying almost the same argument as above, we deduce that for each fixed
0\leq d_{i}\leq N-1
,

\left|\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{i-1}>d_{i}}H_{d_{1}}(s_{1};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\cdots H_{d_{i-1}}(s_{i-1};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})-\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{i-1}>d_{i}}S_{d_{1}}(s_{1})\cdots S_{d_{i-1}}(s_{i-1})\right|_{\infty}\to 0

as
d=\deg\mathfrak{n}\to\infty
. In addition, it follows immediately from definitions that

\lim_{d\to\infty}H_{d_{i}}(s_{i},\ldots,s_{m};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})=S_{d_{i}}(s_{i},\ldots,s_{m}).

Therefore, we obtain

|H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})-S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})|_{\infty}\to 0

as
d\to\infty
, which implies that

H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})\to\lim_{d\to\infty}S_{<d}(\mathfrak{s})=\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s}).

∎

Next, we consider the “algebraic limit” of
H_{<\deg\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})
. We begin by recalling the
K
-algebra

\mathcal{A}_{K}=\left(\prod_{v}\mathbb{F}_{v}\right)\Bigg/\left(\bigoplus_{v}\mathbb{F}_{v}\right),

where
v
ranges over all monic irreducible polynomials in
A
and
\mathbb{F}_{v}=A/(v)
. In addition, we recall the definition of finite multiple zeta values over
K
, which, for any index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
, are given by

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\mathfrak{s})=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg v>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots a_{m}^{s_{m}}}\mod{v}\right)_{v}\in\mathcal{A}_{K}.

Then we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.19
.

For any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
, we have

(3.5)

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\mathfrak{s})=\left(H_{<\deg v}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{v})\mod{\lambda_{v}}\right)_{v}\in\mathcal{A}_{K}.

Proof.

First, note that

H_{<d_{v}}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{v})\in K(\Lambda_{v}),
whose ring of integers is
A[\lambda_{v}]
(see
[
28
, Proposition 12.9]
). Furthermore, for all
a\in A_{+,<d_{v}}
, observe that
\gcd(a,v)=1
, and hence

[a]_{\lambda_{v}}=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{v})}{\lambda_{v}}

is a unit in
A[\lambda_{v}]
(see
[
28
, Proposition 12.6]
), which implies that
H_{<d_{v}}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{v})\in A[\lambda_{v}]
. Next, recall that

vA[\lambda_{v}]=(\lambda_{v})^{\Phi(v)}

is totally ramified where
\Phi(v)=\#(A/(v))^{\times}=[K(\Lambda_{v}):K]
(see
[
28
, Proposition 12.7]
). Thus, the inertia degree of
(\lambda_{v})
over
v
is
f(\lambda_{v}/v)=1
, so
\mathbb{F}_{v}\cong A[\lambda_{v}]/(\lambda_{v})
. Therefore, the right-hand side of (
3.5
) makes sense. Now the result follows immediately from the fact that

[a]_{\lambda_{v}}=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{a}(\lambda_{v})}{\lambda_{v}}\equiv a\pmod{\lambda_{v}}

for all
a\in A_{+}
.
∎

Consequently, the “analytic limit” of
H_{<\deg\mathfrak{n}}(\mathfrak{s};\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})
becomes the corresponding Thakur’s multiple zeta value, whereas its “algebraic limit” yields the corresponding finite multiple zeta value over
K
. Recalling the finite Euler–Carlitz formula (Theorem
3.15
), we obtain the following two corollaries:

Corollary 3.20

(Euler-Carlitz formula)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
be a
r
-even integer, i.e.,
(r-1)\mid s
. Then

\frac{\zeta_{A}(s)}{\tilde{\pi}^{s}}=\frac{\mathrm{BC}_{s}}{\Gamma_{s+1}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.18
, we have

(3.6)

\frac{H_{<\deg\mathfrak{n}}(s;\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})}{(\mathfrak{n}\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}})^{s}}=\frac{\mathrm{dBC}_{s}(\mathfrak{n})}{\Gamma_{s+1}}.

Observe that

\mathfrak{n}\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}=\mathfrak{n}\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}/\mathfrak{n})=\tilde{\pi}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{\pi}^{r^{i}}}{D_{i}\,\mathfrak{n}^{\,r^{i}-1}}\to\tilde{\pi},

and
\mathrm{dBC}_{s}(\mathfrak{n})\to\mathrm{BC}_{s}
as
d=\deg\mathfrak{n}\to\infty
. By Theorem
3.18
, the identity (
3.6
) converges, as
d\to\infty
, to the Euler–Carlitz formula.
∎

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
be a
r
-even integer, i.e.,
(r-1)\mid s
. Then

\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(s)=(0)_{v}\in\mathcal{A}_{K}.

Proof.

The result follows immediately from Theorems
3.15
and
3.19
.
∎

Remark 3.22
.

We mention that Corollary
3.21
was previously established in
[
30
]
via a different approach, where the result was derived from an explicit computation of
sign-free truncated multiple zeta values
.

Recall that in the classical theory, finite multiple harmonic
q
-series
Z_{<n}(\mathfrak{s};\zeta_{n})
evaluated at roots of unity relate to symmetric multiple zeta values in the analytic limit and to finite multiple zeta values in the algebraic limit. This connection is viewed as evidence for the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture.

Hence, it is natural to consider a function field analog of the Kaneko–Zagier conjecture.
We first recall the notions of fixed and binary relations introduced in
[
36
]
.

Definition 3.23
.

For
\bullet\in\{\mathcal{A}_{K},A\}
, a
K
-linear relation

\sum_{i}c_{i}\zeta_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{s}_{i})=0,\quad c_{i}\in K,\mathfrak{s}_{i}\in\mathbf{I}

is called
binary
if there exist
a_{i},b_{i}\in K
such that
a_{i}+b_{i}=c_{i}
for each
i
and

\sum_{i}(a_{i}S_{d}(\mathfrak{s}_{i})+b_{i}S_{d+1}(\mathfrak{s}_{i}))=0

for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, it is called
fixed
if
b_{i}=0
for all
i
.

It is obvious that
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\mathfrak{s})
satisfy all fixed relations. Motivated by a similar spirit of
[
3
]
and Shi’s computation
[
30
]
, we consider the following question:

Question 3.24

(cf.
[
30
, Conjecture 4.6.5]
)
.

Does there exist a well-defined surjective
K
-algebra homomorphism

\varphi:\mathfrak{Z}_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{\infty}/\zeta_{A}(r-1)\mathfrak{Z}_{\infty}

satisfying

\varphi\bigl(\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\mathfrak{s})\bigr)\equiv\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})\pmod{\zeta_{A}(r-1)}

for any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
? If so, is
\operatorname{ker}\varphi
the ideal generated by all binary relations among
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(\mathfrak{s})
that are not fixed?

Remark 3.25
.

In fact, one may further ask whether
\operatorname{ker}\varphi
is generated by Thakur’s fundamental relation
[
34
, Theorem 5]
. That is, whether the equality

\operatorname{ker}\varphi=\left\langle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(r)+(\theta^{r}-\theta)\zeta_{\mathcal{A}_{K}}(1,r-1)\right\rangle

holds.

4.
u
-Multiple Zeta Values

4.1.
u
-Multiple Zeta Values on Drinfeld Upper-Half Plane

We begin by recalling the
Drinfeld upper-half plane

\Omega:=\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\mathbb{P}^{1}(K_{\infty})=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus K_{\infty},

which admits a natural rigid analytic structure (see
[
14
, Proposition 6.1]
). For
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, we let

|z|_{i}=\inf\{|z-x|_{\infty}:x\in K_{\infty}\}

be the
imaginary part
of
z
. Then

\Omega=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega_{n}\quad\text{where}\quad\Omega_{n}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\mid r^{-n}\leq|z|_{i}\leq|z|_{\infty}\leq r^{n}\}

is an admissible cover of
\Omega
. Furthermore, a function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is called
rigid analytic
if its restriction to each
\Omega_{n}
is a uniform limit of rational functions without poles on
\Omega_{n}
(see
[
18
]
and
[
15
]
). We denote by
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
the algebra of rigid analytic functions on
\Omega
.

For any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
, we can view the
u
-multiple zeta values as functions on
\Omega
by defining

\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z):=\zeta_{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z)}(\mathfrak{s}),\quad z\in\Omega.

Then
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)
is well-defined and is a rigid analytic function on
\Omega
:

Lemma 4.1
.

For any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
,
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)
is a rigid analytic function on
\Omega
.

Proof.

For the empty index, the result is clear. First, suppose that
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
is a non-empty positive index. Then

\displaystyle\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)=\zeta_{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z)}(\mathfrak{s})

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z)^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}}}{\mathbf{C}_{a_{1}}(\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))^{s_{1}}\cdots\mathbf{C}_{a_{m}}(\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))^{s_{m}}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z)^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}}}{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}a_{1}z)^{s_{1}}\cdots\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}a_{m}z)^{s_{m}}}.

Now, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\Omega_{n}
. Note that for
a\in A_{+}
with
\deg a>n+1
, we have
|az|_{\infty}\geq r>1
. By
[
17
, Lemma 5.5]
,

\log_{r}|\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(az)|_{\infty}\geq|az|_{i}=|a|_{\infty}|z|_{i}\to\infty

as
\deg a\to\infty
. Thus,
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)
is a uniform limit of rational functions without poles on
\Omega_{n}
, and hence it is a rigid analytic function on
\Omega
.

Next, let
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
be a non-positive index, and let
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
be the smallest integer such that
s_{i}\leq 0
. By
[
33
, Theorem 5.1.2]
, there exists an integer
N\geq 0
such that
H_{d}(s_{i};\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))=0
for all
d\geq N
. Thus, for
d>N
, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0}H_{d_{1}}(s_{1};\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))\cdots H_{d_{m}}(s_{m};\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{d_{i}=0}^{N-1}H_{d_{i}}(s_{i},\ldots,s_{m};\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{i-1}>d_{i}}H_{d_{1}}(s_{1};\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))\cdots H_{d_{i-1}}(s_{i-1};\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))

\displaystyle=\sum_{d_{i}=0}^{N-1}c_{d_{i}}(z)\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{i-1}>d_{i}\end{subarray}}\frac{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z)^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i-1}}}{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}a_{1}z)^{s_{1}}\cdots\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}a_{i-1}z)^{s_{i-1}}}

where
c_{d_{i}}(z)=H_{d_{i}}(s_{i},\ldots,s_{m};\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z))
is clearly rigid analytic.
It follows by a similar estimate that
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)
is a rigid analytic function on
\Omega
.
∎

By Corollary
3.13
, we have the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

The unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map

\zeta_{u}(\bullet;z):\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{O}(\Omega)

sending
x_{\mathfrak{s}}
to
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)
,
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
, is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism.

Next, recall that for a rigid analytic function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
that is
A
-periodic (i.e.,
f(z+a)=f(z)
for all
a\in A
),
f(z)
admits a unique
t
-expansion

f(z)=\sum_{N\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{N}t^{N},\quad a_{N}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}

which converges for
|z|_{i}
sufficiently large (see
[
18
]
). Here, we let the local parameter at infinity be

t=t(z):=\frac{1}{\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z)}.

One checks easily that
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)
is
A
-periodic. We now compute the
t
-expansion of
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)
at positive index.

Theorem 4.3
.

For any non-empty index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
, the rigid analytic function
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)
admits a
t
-expansion with coefficients in
A
:

\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}c_{N}t^{N}\in A[[t]]

where
c_{0}=1
if
m=1
, and
c_{0}=0
if
m\geq 2
.

Proof.

Evaluating at
u=\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{\pi}z)=1/t
, we have

[a]_{1/t}=t\mathbf{C}_{a}(1/t)=t\sum_{i=0}^{d}[a,i]t^{-r^{i}}=t^{1-r^{d}}\left(1+\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}[a,i]t^{r^{d}-r^{i}}\right).

Note that
1+\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}[a,i]t^{r^{d}-r^{i}}
is invertible in
A[[t]]
. Therefore, for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{1}{[a]_{1/t}^{s}}\in t^{s(r^{d}-1)}A[[t]].

Now, we substitute this into the definition of
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)
:

\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};z)=\sum_{d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg a_{j}=d_{j}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{[a_{j}]_{1/t}^{s_{j}}}.

For any fixed sequence
d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0
, the inner sum lies in
t^{M}A[[t]]
, where
M=\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}(r^{d_{j}}-1)
. When
m=1
, the only term that gives
M=0
is
d_{1}=0
, which contributes exactly
1
to the sum, and hence
c_{0}=1
. When
m\geq 2
, we necessarily have
d_{1}\geq 1
, which implies
M\geq s_{1}(r-1)\geq 1
. Hence, there is no constant term. Finally, since
M\to\infty
as
d_{1}\to\infty
, each coefficient
c_{N}
involves only finitely many term and therefore lies in
A
.
∎

4.2.
u
-Multiple Zeta Values as Formal Power Series

We now discuss the expansion of
u
-multiple zeta values as formal power series in
u
. Recall the
u
-multiple zeta values is defined by

\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})=\lim_{d\to\infty}H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)=\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{d>d_{1}>\cdots>d_{m}\geq 0}H_{d_{1}}(s_{1};u)\cdots H_{d_{m}}(s_{m};u)

for any index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
.
We will show that this defines a formal power series with respect to the product topology on
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!]
.

First, for each integer
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the polynomial
P_{i}(X)\in K[X]
by

P_{i}(X):=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{X^{r^{j}-1}}{D_{j}L_{i-j}^{r^{j}}}

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the polynomial
W_{n}^{(s)}(X)\in K[X]
explicitly by

(4.1)

W_{n}^{(s)}(X):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{-s}{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\geq 1\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{r^{i_{\ell}}-1}{r-1}=n\end{subarray}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}P_{i_{\ell}}(X).

We put
W_{0}^{(s)}(X)=1
by convention. Moreover, we write

(4.2)

W_{n}^{(s)}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n(r-1)}c_{n,i}^{(s)}X^{i},\quad c_{n,i}^{(s)}\in K.

Here, note that since
\deg P_{j}(X)=r^{j}-1
, the degree of each product in (
4.1
) is given by

\deg\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}P_{i_{\ell}}(X)\right)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(r^{i_{\ell}}-1)=(r-1)\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{r^{i_{\ell}}-1}{r-1}=n(r-1),

where the indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}
satisfy the condition in (
4.1
). Consequently, we have

\deg W_{n}^{(s)}(X)\leq n(r-1).

We first consider the lemmas below:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

H^{W}_{d,n}(s)\to 0
as
d\to\infty

where

H^{W}_{d,n}(s):=\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{W_{n}^{(s)}(a)}{a^{s}}.

Proof.

By (
4.2
), we have

\frac{W_{n}^{(s)}(X)}{X^{s}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n(r-1)}c_{n,i}^{(s)}X^{i-s},

and hence

\displaystyle H^{W}_{d,n}(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{n(r-1)}c_{n,i}^{(s)}\left(\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}a^{i-s}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n(r-1)}c_{n,i}^{(s)}S_{d}(i-s).

The result follows immediately from the fact that for any
s\in\mathbb{Z}
,
S_{d}(s)\to 0
as
d\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
a\in A
. Then we have

\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)=\sum_{i=0}^{\deg a}[a,i]u^{r^{i}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{a^{r^{j}}}{D_{j}L_{i-j}^{r^{j}}}\right)u^{r^{i}}

where
L_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}(\theta-\theta^{q^{j}})
.

Proof.

We first recall the formal Carlitz exponential and logarithm (see
[
20
]
)

\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(u)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{r^{i}}}{D_{i}},\quad\log_{\mathbf{C}}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{r^{j}}}{L_{j}}.

Then we have the following identity in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!]
:

\mathbf{C}_{a}(u)=\exp_{\mathbf{C}}(a\log_{\mathbf{C}}(u))=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{D_{i}}\left(a\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{r^{j}}}{L_{j}}\right)^{r^{i}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{a^{r^{i}}}{D_{i}L_{k-i}^{r^{i}}}\right)u^{r^{k}}.

∎

With these lemmas in hand, we obtain the following expansion of
u
-multiple zeta values in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!]
:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
. Then

\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})u^{N(r-1)}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!]

where the explicit coefficient
\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})
is given by

(4.3)

\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\sum_{d_{1}>\dots>d_{m}\geq 0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}H^{W}_{d_{j},n_{j}}(s_{j}).

Proof.

Let
a\in A_{+}
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have

[a]_{u}=\frac{1}{u}\left(au+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}[a,i]u^{r^{i}}\right)=a\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}P_{i}(a)u^{r^{i}-1}\right).

We first expand the formal series for
[a]_{u}^{-s}
using the generalized binomial theorem:

[a]_{u}^{-s}=\frac{1}{a^{s}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}P_{i}(a)u^{r^{i}-1}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{a^{s}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s}{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}P_{i}(a)(u^{r-1})^{\frac{r^{i}-1}{r-1}}\right)^{k}.

It follows immediately from the definition in (
4.1
) that

(4.4)

[a]_{u}^{-s}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{W_{n}^{(s)}(a)}{a^{s}}u^{n(r-1)}\in K[\![{u}]\!].

Now, for any fixed degree
d\geq 0
, we have

H_{d}(s;u)=\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{W_{n}^{(s)}(a)}{a^{s}}u^{n(r-1)}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H^{W}_{d,n}(s)u^{n(r-1)}.

For any
d_{1}>\dots>d_{m}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}H_{d_{j}}(s_{j};u)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}H^{W}_{d_{j},n_{j}}(s_{j})u^{n_{j}(r-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}H^{W}_{d_{j},n_{j}}(s_{j})\right)u^{N(r-1)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}H_{<d}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{d>d_{1}>\dots>d_{m}\geq 0}\left(\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}H^{W}_{d_{j},n_{j}}(s_{j})\right)u^{N(r-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\sum_{d>d_{1}>\dots>d_{m}\geq 0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}H^{W}_{d_{j},n_{j}}(s_{j})\right)u^{N(r-1)}

By Lemma
4.4
, we see that the series converges with respect to the product topology and this exactly yields
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})u^{N(r-1)}
, completing the proof.
∎

We mention that the constant in the formal expansion of
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})
is precisely
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
.

As observed in the proof of Lemma
4.4
, the coefficients
\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})
can be, in fact, expressed as finite
K
-linear combinations of Thakur’s multiple zeta values.

Proposition 4.7
.

For any
N\geq 0
and non-empty
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
, the coefficient
\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})
can be written as
K
-linear combination of Thakur’s multiple zeta values

\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{n_{1}(r-1)}\cdots\sum_{i_{m}=0}^{n_{m}(r-1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}c_{n_{j},i_{j}}^{(s_{j})}\right)\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-i_{1},\dots,s_{m}-i_{m}).

Proof.

By (
4.2
), we have

H^{W}_{d,n}(s)=\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{W_{n}^{(s)}(a)}{a^{s}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n(r-1)}c_{n,i}^{(s)}\left(\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{1}{a^{s-i}}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n(r-1)}c_{n,i}^{(s)}S_{d}(s-i).

Substituting this expansion into the formula for
\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\sum_{d_{1}>\dots>d_{m}\geq 0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{i_{j}=0}^{n_{j}(r-1)}c_{n_{j},i_{j}}^{(s_{j})}S_{d_{j}}(s_{j}-i_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{n_{1}(r-1)}\cdots\sum_{i_{m}=0}^{n_{m}(r-1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}c_{n_{j},i_{j}}^{(s_{j})}\right)\sum_{d_{1}>\dots>d_{m}\geq 0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}S_{d_{j}}(s_{j}-i_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{n_{1}(r-1)}\cdots\sum_{i_{m}=0}^{n_{m}(r-1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}c_{n_{j},i_{j}}^{(s_{j})}\right)\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-i_{1},\dots,s_{m}-i_{m}).

∎

We compute the coefficient
\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})
for
N=1
and
\operatorname{dep}(\mathfrak{s})\leq 2
in the following two examples.

Example 4.8
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. We have

\zeta_{u}(s)=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{N}(s)u^{N(r-1)},

where

\gamma_{N}(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{N(r-1)}c_{N,i}^{(s)}\zeta_{A}(s-i).

We compute the first two terms explicitly:

(1)

For
N=0
, we have
W_{0}^{(s)}(X)=1
, which means
c_{0,0}^{(s)}=1
. Thus, the constant term is exactly the Thakur’s zeta value:

\gamma_{0}(s)=\zeta_{A}(s).

(2)

For
N=1
, the condition
\frac{r^{i_{1}}-1}{r-1}=1
forces
k=1
and
i_{1}=1
, so we obtain

W_{1}^{(s)}(X)=\binom{-s}{1}P_{1}(X)=-s\sum_{j=0}^{1}\frac{X^{r^{j}-1}}{D_{j}L_{1-j}^{r^{j}}}=-s\left(\frac{1}{L_{1}}+\frac{X^{r-1}}{D_{1}}\right).

This gives the coefficients
c_{1,0}^{(s)}=\frac{-s}{L_{1}}
and
c_{1,r-1}^{(s)}=\frac{-s}{D_{1}}
. Consequently, the coefficient of
u^{r-1}
is given by

\gamma_{1}(s)=-s\left(\frac{1}{L_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s)+\frac{1}{D_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s-r+1)\right).

Example 4.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathrm{ext}}
. We have

\zeta_{u}(s_{1},s_{2})=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{N}(s_{1},s_{2})u^{N(r-1)}.

By Proposition
4.7
, the coefficient
\gamma_{N}(s_{1},s_{2})
is given by

\gamma_{N}(s_{1},s_{2})=\sum_{n_{1}+n_{2}=N}\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{n_{1}(r-1)}\sum_{i_{2}=0}^{n_{2}(r-1)}c_{n_{1},i_{1}}^{(s_{1})}c_{n_{2},i_{2}}^{(s_{2})}\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-i_{1},s_{2}-i_{2}).

We compute the first two terms:

(1)

For
N=0
, since
c_{0,0}^{(s)}=1
for any
s
, we obtain

\gamma_{0}(s_{1},s_{2})=\zeta_{A}(s_{1},s_{2}).

(2)

For
N=1
, there are two partitions for
n_{1}+n_{2}=1
, namely
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
.

(a)

When
(n_{1},n_{2})=(1,0)
, we have
i_{2}=0
and
c_{0,0}^{(s_{2})}=1
. The index
i_{1}
can be
0
or
r-1
. Using the coefficients
c_{1,0}^{(s_{1})}
and
c_{1,r-1}^{(s_{1})}
computed in Example
4.8
, this part contributes:

-\frac{s_{1}}{L_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s_{1},s_{2})-\frac{s_{1}}{D_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-r+1,s_{2}).

(b)

Similarly, when
(n_{1},n_{2})=(0,1)
, we have
i_{1}=0
and
c_{0,0}^{(s_{1})}=1
. The index
i_{2}
can be
0
or
r-1
, which contributes:

-\frac{s_{2}}{L_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s_{1},s_{2})-\frac{s_{2}}{D_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s_{1},s_{2}-r+1).

Summing these two contributions, the coefficient of
u^{r-1}
is given by

\gamma_{1}(s_{1},s_{2})=-\frac{s_{1}+s_{2}}{L_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s_{1},s_{2})-\frac{s_{1}}{D_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-r+1,s_{2})-\frac{s_{2}}{D_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s_{1},s_{2}-r+1).

Next, we consider the
r
-shuffle relations. Recall in the proof of Proposition
4.6
, we have

H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\sum_{d>d_{1}>\dots>d_{m}\geq 0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}H^{W}_{d_{j},n_{j}}(s_{j})\right)u^{N(r-1)}

for any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
. Furthermore, by Lemma
4.4
, this sequence converges to
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})
coefficient-wise as
d\to\infty
. Therefore, we can apply the theory in §
3.2
and obtain the following corollaries:

Corollary 4.10
.

For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the map

\widehat{H_{<d}}(\bullet;u):\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!]

is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism. That is,

\widehat{H_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}};u)=\widehat{H_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}};u)\widehat{H_{<d}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}};u)=H_{<d}(\mathfrak{r};u)H_{<d}(\mathfrak{s};u)

for any indices
\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
.

Corollary 4.11
.

The map

\widehat{\zeta_{u}}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{u}]\!]

is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism. That is,

\widehat{\zeta_{u}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}};u)=\widehat{\zeta_{u}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}};u)\widehat{\zeta_{u}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}};u)=\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{r};u)\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};u)

for any indices
\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
.

By exploiting the
r
-shuffle relations of the formal power series
\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})
, we can extract explicit relations among the coefficients
\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})
. To formulate this conveniently, we let
\widehat{\gamma_{N}}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map defined by
\widehat{\gamma_{N}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\gamma_{N}(\mathfrak{s})
for any index
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
.

Theorem 4.12
.

For any indices
\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
and any integer
N\geq 0
, we have

\widehat{\gamma_{N}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\widehat{\gamma_{k}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}})\widehat{\gamma_{N-k}}(x_{\mathfrak{s}}).

Proof.

By Corollary
4.11
, we have
\widehat{\zeta_{u}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{r})\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s})
. We expand both sides as formal power series in
u
. For the left-hand side, we have

\widehat{\zeta_{u}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}};u)=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\gamma_{N}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})u^{N(r-1)}.

For the right-hand side, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{r};u)\zeta_{u}(\mathfrak{s};u)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}(\mathfrak{r})u^{k(r-1)}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}(\mathfrak{s})u^{\ell(r-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N}\gamma_{k}(\mathfrak{r})\gamma_{N-k}(\mathfrak{s})\right)u^{N(r-1)}.

Comparing the coefficients of
u^{N(r-1)}
on both sides yields the desired equality.
∎

Theorem
4.12
, together with Proposition
4.7
, provides an specific method to produce relations for Thakur’s multiple zeta values, including values at non-positive indices.

Example 4.13
.

To see how Theorem
4.12
explicitly produces relations involving multiple zeta values at non-positive indices, let us consider the
r
-shuffle of two depth-1 words
x_{r_{1}}
and
x_{s_{1}}
for some
r_{1},s_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
.

By Theorem
4.12
, we extract the relation for
N=1
, which gives the equation

(4.5)

\widehat{\gamma_{1}}(x_{r_{1}}\ast x_{s_{1}})=\gamma_{1}(r_{1})\gamma_{0}(s_{1})+\gamma_{0}(r_{1})\gamma_{1}(s_{1}).

We now express both sides of (
4.5
) completely in terms of multiple zeta values using Examples
4.8
and
4.9
.

First, recall from Example
4.8
that
\gamma_{0}(s)=\zeta_{A}(s)
and

\gamma_{1}(s)=-s\left(\frac{1}{L_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s)+\frac{1}{D_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s-r+1)\right).

Substituting these into the right-hand side of (
4.5
) yields

\displaystyle\left[-r_{1}\left(\frac{1}{L_{1}}\zeta_{A}(r_{1})+\frac{1}{D_{1}}\zeta_{A}(r_{1}-r+1)\right)\right]\zeta_{A}(s_{1})+\zeta_{A}(r_{1})\left[-s_{1}\left(\frac{1}{L_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s_{1})+\frac{1}{D_{1}}\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-r+1)\right)\right]

(4.6)

\displaystyle=-\frac{r_{1}+s_{1}}{L_{1}}\zeta_{A}(r_{1})\zeta_{A}(s_{1})-\frac{1}{D_{1}}\Big(r_{1}\zeta_{A}(r_{1}-r+1)\zeta_{A}(s_{1})+s_{1}\zeta_{A}(r_{1})\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-r+1)\Big).

Next, note that

(4.7)

\widehat{\gamma_{1}}(x_{r_{1}}\ast x_{s_{1}})=\gamma_{1}(r_{1},s_{1})+\gamma_{1}(s_{1},r_{1})+\gamma_{1}(r_{1}+s_{1})+\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\gamma_{1}(i,j).

We apply the explicit formulas from Example
4.8
and
4.9
to each term. Then (
4.7
) becomes

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{1}+s_{1}}{L_{1}}

\displaystyle\zeta_{A}(r_{1})\zeta_{A}(s_{1})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{D_{1}}\Big(r_{1}\zeta_{A}(r_{1}-r+1,s_{1})+s_{1}\zeta_{A}(r_{1},s_{1}-r+1)\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{D_{1}}\Big(s_{1}\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-r+1,r_{1})+r_{1}\zeta_{A}(s_{1},r_{1}-r+1)\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{1}+s_{1}}{D_{1}}\zeta_{A}(r_{1}+s_{1}-r+1)

(4.8)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{D_{1}}\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\Big(i\zeta_{A}(i-r+1,j)+j\zeta_{A}(i,j-r+1)\Big).

Finally, equating the (
4.6
) and (
4.8
), we obtain the identity:

\displaystyle-r_{1}\zeta_{A}(r_{1}-r+1)\zeta_{A}(s_{1})-s_{1}\zeta_{A}(r_{1})\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-r+1)

\displaystyle=-r_{1}\zeta_{A}(r_{1}-r+1,s_{1})-s_{1}\zeta_{A}(r_{1},s_{1}-r+1)-s_{1}\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-r+1,r_{1})-r_{1}\zeta_{A}(s_{1},r_{1}-r+1)

(4.9)

\displaystyle\quad-(r_{1}+s_{1})\zeta_{A}(r_{1}+s_{1}-r+1)+\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\Big((-i)\zeta_{A}(i-r+1,j)+(-j)\zeta_{A}(i,j-r+1)\Big).

For
r_{1}\leq r-1
or
s_{1}\leq r-1
, (
4.9
) yields an algebraic relation among Thakur’s multiple zeta values that involves arguments at non-positive indices.

Example 4.14
.

Let
r_{1},s_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
. By extracting the coefficient of
N=2
from the shuffle relation
x_{r_{1}}\ast x_{s_{1}}
in Theorem

, one can obtain the identity (cf. (

)):

\displaystyle r_{1}s_{1}\zeta_{A}(r_{1}-r+1)\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-r+1)+\binom{-r_{1}}{2}\zeta_{A}(r_{1}-2r+2)\zeta_{A}(s_{1})+\binom{-s_{1}}{2}\zeta_{A}(r_{1})\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-2r+2)

\displaystyle=r_{1}s_{1}\Big(\zeta_{A}(r_{1}-r+1,s_{1}-r+1)+\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-r+1,r_{1}-r+1)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\binom{-r_{1}}{2}\Big(\zeta_{A}(r_{1}-2r+2,s_{1})+\zeta_{A}(s_{1},r_{1}-2r+2)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\binom{-s_{1}}{2}\Big(\zeta_{A}(r_{1},s_{1}-2r+2)+\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-2r+2,r_{1})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\binom{-(r_{1}+s_{1})}{2}\zeta_{A}(r_{1}+s_{1}-2r+2)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i+j=r_{1}+s_{1}}\Delta_{r_{1},s_{1}}^{i,j}\left(ij\zeta_{A}(i-r+1,j-r+1)+\binom{-i}{2}\zeta_{A}(i-2r+2,j)+\binom{-j}{2}\zeta_{A}(i,j-2r+2)\right).

4.3.
Derivations on
r
-shuffle Relations

Inspired by (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we explicitly define a new family of operators
\mathcal{D}_{N}
. We will prove that they form a Hasse-Schmidt derivation over
\widehat{\zeta_{A}}
by constructing a new bracket compatible with our abstract framework (see Corollary
3.11
).

Let us first recall the general notion of a Hasse-Schmidt derivation (see
[
38
]
):

Definition 4.15
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field, and let
A,B
be
\mathbb{K}
-algebras. Given a
\mathbb{K}
-algebra homomorphism
f:A\to B
, a sequence of
\mathbb{K}
-linear maps
(D_{N})_{N\geq 0}
from
A
to
B
is called a
Hasse-Schmidt derivation over
f
if

(1)

D_{0}=f
, and

(2)

D_{N}(xy)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}D_{k}(x)D_{N-k}(y)
for all
x,y\in A
and
N\geq 0
.

Recall that
\mathfrak{Z}_{\infty}
is the
K
-algebra generated by all
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
for
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
. Then the operators
\mathcal{D}_{N}
are defined as follows:

Definition 4.16
.

For
N\geq 0
, we define
\mathcal{D}_{N}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathfrak{Z}_{\infty}
to be the unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map such that

\mathcal{D}_{N}(x_{\varnothing})=1,

and

\mathcal{D}_{N}(x_{\mathfrak{s}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{-s_{j}}{n_{j}}\right)\zeta_{A}(s_{1}-n_{1}(r-1),\dots,s_{m}-n_{m}(r-1))

for non-empty index
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
.

Here, we remark that every Thakur multiple zeta value at a non-positive index is a
K
-linear combination of Thakur multiple zeta values at positive indices, so
\mathcal{D}_{N}
are well-defined.

We now consider the formal variable
X
and the new bracket defined by

[a]_{X}:=a+a^{r}X=a(1+a^{r-1}X)\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{X}]\!].

Then one checks the following:

(1)

For all
a\in A_{+}
,
[a]_{X}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{X}]\!]^{\times}
.

(2)

For all
a,b\in A
,

[a+b]_{X}=(a+b)+(a+b)^{r}X=(a+a^{r}X)+(b+b^{r}X)=[a]_{X}+[b]_{X}.

(3)

For all
a\in A
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{r}
,

[\varepsilon a]_{X}=\varepsilon a+(\varepsilon a)^{r}X=\varepsilon a+\varepsilon a^{r}X=\varepsilon[a]_{X}.

Furthermore, let
\mathbf{H}_{d}^{X}(\mathfrak{s})
and
\mathbf{H}_{<d}^{X}(\mathfrak{s})
be defined as (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) with respect to this bracket.

Lemma 4.17
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
and non-empty
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{<d}^{X}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{-s_{j}}{n_{j}}\right)S_{<d}(s_{1}-n_{1}(r-1),\ldots,s_{m}-n_{m}(r-1))X^{N}.

Therefore, as
d\to\infty
, it converges to

\zeta_{X}(\mathfrak{s}):=\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathbf{H}_{<d}^{X}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{N}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})X^{N}.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbf{I}
. For each
a_{j}\in A_{+}
, we expand
[a_{j}]_{X}^{-s_{j}}
in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{X}]\!]
:

[a_{j}]_{X}^{-s_{j}}=a_{j}^{-s_{j}}(1+a_{j}^{r-1}X)^{-s_{j}}=\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s_{j}}{n_{j}}a_{j}^{-s_{j}+n_{j}(r-1)}X^{n_{j}}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{[a_{1}]_{X}^{s_{1}}\cdots[a_{m}]_{X}^{s_{m}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\binom{-s_{j}}{n_{j}}a_{j}^{-s_{j}+n_{j}(r-1)}X^{n_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}X^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{-s_{j}}{n_{j}}\right)\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}-n_{1}(r-1)}\cdots a_{m}^{s_{m}-n_{m}(r-1)}}.

Substituting this expansion into the definition of the finite multiple harmonic series
\mathbf{H}_{<d}^{X}(\mathfrak{s})
evaluated with the
X
-bracket, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{<d}^{X}(\mathfrak{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[a_{1}]_{X}^{s_{1}}\cdots[a_{m}]_{X}^{s_{m}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}X^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{-s_{j}}{n_{j}}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in A_{+}\\
d>\deg a_{1}>\cdots>\deg a_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}-n_{1}(r-1)}\cdots a_{m}^{s_{m}-n_{m}(r-1)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+\dots+n_{m}=N\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\binom{-s_{j}}{n_{j}}\right)S_{<d}(s_{1}-n_{1}(r-1),\ldots,s_{m}-n_{m}(r-1))X^{N}.

Thus,
\mathbf{H}_{<d}^{X}(\mathfrak{s})
converges coefficient-wise to

\zeta_{X}(\mathfrak{s})=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{N}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})X^{N}

in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{X}]\!]
as
d\to\infty
.
∎

Therefore, we can apply Corollary
3.11
to the formal series

\zeta_{X}(\mathfrak{s}):=\lim_{d\to\infty}\mathbf{H}_{<d}^{X}(\mathfrak{s})

and obtain the following theorem:

Theorem 4.18
.

The sequence of operators
(\mathcal{D}_{N})_{N\geq 0}
forms a Hasse-Schmidt derivation over the realization map
\widehat{\zeta_{A}}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathfrak{Z}_{\infty}
. That is, for any indices
\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbf{I}
and any integer
N\geq 0
, we have

\mathcal{D}_{0}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
and

\mathcal{D}_{N}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\mathcal{D}_{k}(x_{\mathfrak{r}})\mathcal{D}_{N-k}(x_{\mathfrak{s}}).

Proof.

It is clear from the definitions that
\mathcal{D}_{0}=\widehat{\zeta_{A}}
. It suffices to check that
(\mathcal{D}_{N})_{N\geq 0}
satisfies the higher Lebiniz rule.
By Lemma
4.17
and Corollary
3.11
, the map
\widehat{\zeta_{X}}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![{X}]\!]
sending
x_{\mathfrak{s}}\mapsto\zeta_{X}(\mathfrak{s})
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism. Therefore, we have

\widehat{\zeta_{X}}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})=\zeta_{X}(\mathfrak{r})\zeta_{X}(\mathfrak{s}).

Comparing the expansions of both sides, we obtain

\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{N}(x_{\mathfrak{r}}\ast x_{\mathfrak{s}})X^{N}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{k}(x_{\mathfrak{r}})X^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})X^{\ell}\right)=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N}\mathcal{D}_{k}(x_{\mathfrak{r}})\mathcal{D}_{N-k}(x_{\mathfrak{s}})\right)X^{N},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.19
.

Equations (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) serve as special cases of Theorem
4.18
for
\operatorname{dep}(\mathfrak{r})=\operatorname{dep}(\mathfrak{s})=1
and
N=1,2
. This theorem shows that by applying the derivations
\mathcal{D}_{N}
to the
r
