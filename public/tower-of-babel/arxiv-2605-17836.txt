Non-admissibility of some universal supersingular representations
Zachary Feng, Heejong Lee, Ray Li, Vaughan McDonald, Nischay Reddy

Abstract
. Let K / 𝐐 p K/\mathbf{Q}_{p} be an unramified extension of degree f f with residue field k k . Let σ \sigma be an irreducible representation of GL n ​ ( k ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(k) over 𝐅 ¯ p \overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p} . For n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , we prove that the universal supersingular representation of weight σ \sigma is non-admissible and of infinite length when σ \sigma is sufficiently generic and satisfies certain technical conditions. 1.1.
Background

The mod

\sigma

1.2.
Result and method

From now on, we assume that
K/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is unramified of degree
f\geq 1
with residue field
k
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

The universal supersingular representation
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
is non-admissible and of infinite length for
n\geq 3
and sufficiently deep
\sigma
whose
p
-restricted highest weight is contained in a special
p
-restricted
p
-alcove.

This generalizes the previous non-admissibility results for
n=2
. The assumption on
K
is required to apply the main result in
[
23
]
. Our deepness assumption on
\sigma
requires
p
to be sufficiently large. See §
2.3
for the definition of special
p
-restricted
p
-alcoves. The proportion of special ones among all
p
-restricted
p
-alcoves is
1-\left(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\right)^{f}
. See Remark
5.2
for a discussion why the given problem is more difficult without this condition.

The methods in previous works are restricted to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.
[
3
]
extensively used the theory of diagrams (first introduced in
[
30
]
) which is currently not available for groups other than
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.
[
32
,
16
]
proved the non-admissibility of
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
by explicitly computing the pro-
p
Iwahori invariant subspace of
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
(for
K/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
unramified, this was first observed by Breuil; see
[
4
, Remarque 4.2.6]
).
[
33
,
35
]
used a similar argument and a result of Hu
[
20
]
relying on diagrams.
[
13
,
26
]
, which studied different problems, also used the theory of diagrams to prove the result for
n=2
and extended it to general
n
using parabolic induction. This is why the residue field is assumed to be a non-trivial extension of
\mathbf{F}_{p}
in
loc. cit.

Our method is distinct from the previous ones in the sense that:

(1)

we employ a weight cycling argument which applies to any split reductive group over any finite extension
K/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, and

(2)

we make use of the moduli space of mod
p
local Galois representations (the reduced part of the Emerton–Gee stack
[
9
]
) and the connection between mod
p
local Galois representations and mod
p
representations of
p
-adic groups given by globalization and the space of mod
p
algebraic automorphic forms.

Even though the proof of Theorem
5.1
works only when
n\geq 3
, a similar argument can prove the non-admissibility and the infinite length property of
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
for
n=2
and
f\geq 2
. This is explained in Remark
5.3
, which gives a concise illustration of our method. We view our method as a showcase for how Galois representations can guide the mod
p
representation theory of
p
-adic groups.

We explain our method in more detail. The weight cycling argument was first discovered by Buzzard for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
. We use its most general form due to
[
7
]
. In fact, we reinterpret their result in the following form (Proposition
2.7
): for
\sigma
an irreducible representation of
\mathrm{KZ}
and
1\leq i\leq n-1
, there exists a finite length smooth representation
C_{i}(\sigma)
of
\mathrm{KZ}
, which is defined as a parabolic induction of an irreducible
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-representation of a Levi subgroup in the finite group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
, with a surjective morphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{cyc}_{i}:\cind_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{{\mathrm{G}}}C_{i}(\sigma)\twoheadrightarrow\cind_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma/(T_{i}).

In particular,
\cind_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{{\mathrm{G}}}C_{i}(\sigma)
surjects onto
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
. Let
\sigma^{\prime}
be a Jordan–Hölder factor for
C_{i}(\sigma)
. We want to show that the subquotient of
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
given by
\cind_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{cyc}_{i}
is non-admissible and of infinite length. Then this implies the same properties of
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
. There are two difficulties. Firstly, we do not have a complete description of the set of Jordan–Hölder factors
\mathrm{JH}(C_{i}(\sigma))
. Secondly, even if we know that some explicit
\sigma^{\prime}
is in
\mathrm{JH}(C_{i}(\sigma))
, understanding its image under
\mathrm{cyc}_{i}
seems very hard. Due to these difficulties, applications of the weight cycling argument have been limited to
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for
n\leq 3
.

This is where Galois representations come in. Let
G_{K}:=\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
and
\overline{\rho}:G_{K}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
be a continuous representation. Using a globalization of
\overline{\rho}
, we can construct an admissible smooth
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-representation
\pi
of
{\mathrm{G}}
attached to
\overline{\rho}
, and
\overline{\rho}\mapsto\pi
is a candidate for the mod
p
local Langlands correspondence. By carefully choosing
\overline{\rho}
, we can assume that
\sigma
is a modular weight of
\overline{\rho}
. Furthermore, using the main result of
[
27
]
, which reinterprets Hecke operators for
\sigma
in terms of Galois representations, we can take
\overline{\rho}
to be
supersingular with respect to
\sigma
(Definition
3.25
). By the mod
p
local-global compatibility result in
[
27
]
, we obtain a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cind_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{{\mathrm{G}}}C_{i}(\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cind_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi.}

Here, the right diagonal arrow exists because
\sigma
is a modular weight of
\overline{\rho}
. It factors through
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
and induces the vertical dashed arrow by the supersingularity of
\overline{\rho}
with respect to
\sigma
, which in turn induces the diagonal dashed arrow. In order to understand (part of) the left horizontal arrow, we study the left diagonal arrow. By Frobenius reciprocity, we get a non-zero morphism
C_{i}(\sigma)\rightarrow\pi|_{\mathrm{KZ}}
. In particular, there is at least one
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathrm{JH}(C_{i}(\sigma))
that appears in the
\mathrm{KZ}
-socle of
\pi|_{\mathrm{KZ}}
. This is why this method is called weight cycling.

In general, it is difficult to determine which
\sigma^{\prime}
should appear in the
\mathrm{KZ}
-socle of
\pi
. We resolve this by choosing
\overline{\rho}
such that there is a unique
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathrm{JH}(C_{i}(\sigma))
modular for
\overline{\rho}
. Then this induces a non-zero morphism from
\cind_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}
to
\pi
.
Let
\pi^{\prime}
be the subquotient of
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
given by
\cind_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}
. In addition to above conditions on
\overline{\rho}
, we require
\overline{\rho}
to be non-supersingular with respect to
\sigma^{\prime}
. In fact, we choose
\overline{\rho}
ordinary with respect to
\sigma^{\prime}
, i.e. extension of characters whose restriction to the inertia subgroup is explicitly determined by
\sigma^{\prime}
. This implies that the image of
\cind_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}
in
\pi
, which factors through
\pi^{\prime}
, is given by a principal series representation. By twisting the diagonal characters of
\overline{\rho}
by infinitely many unramified characters, we get infinitely many non-isomorphic principal series of fixed weight
\sigma^{\prime}
as quotients of
\pi^{\prime}
.
This implies the desired non-admissibility and infinite length of (a subquotient of)
\mathrm{SS}(\sigma)
(see Lemma
2.11
).

What remains is to find (a family of)
\overline{\rho}
satisfying the above conditions. Let
{\mathcal{X}}_{n,{\mathrm{red}}}
be the reduced Emerton–Gee stack constructed in
[
9
]
. It can be viewed as the moduli space of
n
-dimensional mod
p
representations of
G_{K}
. It is known that there is a bijection between irreducible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
and irreducible components in
{\mathcal{X}}_{n,{\mathrm{red}}}
.
[
23
]
studied explicit local charts of certain subspaces in
{\mathcal{X}}_{n,{\mathrm{red}}}
. The regular colength one local charts in
loc. cit.
have two irreducible components corresponding to
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
. Then
\overline{\rho}
in the intersection of the two components has
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
as its modular weights (Proposition
4.4
). For the ordinarity of
\overline{\rho}
with respect to
\sigma^{\prime}
, we further intersect with the ordinary locus in the component corresponding to
\sigma^{\prime}
. Showing the non-emptiness of this triple intersection is the most technical part of our work (Proposition
3.14
). Using local model theory developed in
[
22
]
, we reduce this to computing intersections of certain translated affine Schubert cells. Then the specialness of the
p
-alcove ensures that our
\overline{\rho}
is supersingular with respect to
\sigma
. Our result also shows that the supersingular locus in the irreducible component for
\sigma
is of codimension one when the (
p
-restricted) highest weight of
\sigma
belongs to a special
p
-alcove (Corollary
3.28
). This exhibits a striking contrast compared to the lowest alcove case.

-bi-equivariant functions

{\mathrm{K}}t{\mathrm{K}}

\displaystyle\sigma\twoheadrightarrow\sigma_{N_{\mu}(k)}\simeq\sigma^{N_{-\mu}(k)}\hookrightarrow\sigma.

By

Recall that for

and maps

to

]
)

\displaystyle\varphi([1,v])=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathrm{K}}\backslash{\mathrm{G}}}\gamma^{-1}[1,\varphi(\gamma)(v)].

Definition 2.6

For a Serre weight

Proposition 2.7

(Weight cycling)
.

Let
\sigma
be a Serre weight and
\mu
be an antidominant fundamental cocharacter. Then there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cind_{\mathrm{K}}^{\mathrm{G}}\Ind_{P_{\mu}(k)}^{G(k)}\sigma^{N_{-\mu}(k)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cind_{\mathrm{K}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma}

The following argument is essentially due to Emerton–Gee–Herzig. Let
\pi

{\mathrm{G}}
-representation over
\mathbf{F}
with a non-zero morphism

-representations with a non-zero map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ind_{P_{\mu}(k)}^{G(k)}\sigma^{N_{-\mu}(k)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi|_{\mathrm{K}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\theta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{\mu}(f)}

and

Let

. The right diagonal arrow

is induced by

in the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{N_{\mu}(k)}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Suppose that

Suppose that

\displaystyle Z_{-\alpha}=(m_{-\alpha}-\langle{\mathbf{a}}_{\tau},-w^{-1}(\alpha)^{\vee}\rangle)c_{-\alpha}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{-\alpha}\\
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{(k_{0}-i+1)i_{0}}\end{subarray}}(m_{\beta_{2}}+\kappa_{\beta_{2}}-\langle{\mathbf{a}}_{\tau},w^{-1}(\beta_{2})^{\vee}\rangle)c_{\beta_{2}}\sum_{(\beta^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\beta^{\prime}_{s})\in I_{\beta_{1}}}(-1)^{i-1-s}c_{\beta^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots c_{\beta^{\prime}_{s}}.

where

Suppose that

. This means that

(2) below, we have

\displaystyle(m_{-\alpha}-\langle{\mathbf{a}}_{\tau},-w^{-1}(\alpha)^{\vee}\rangle)^{-1}Z_{-\alpha}=a_{-\alpha}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{-\alpha}\\

Lemma 3.23
.

(1)

Suppose that

. Then for

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{k_{0}-i_{0}-1}\delta_{w^{-1}(\beta^{\prime}_{t})>0}>\delta_{w^{-1}(\alpha_{(k_{0}-1)i_{0}})>0}.

(2)

Suppose that

. For
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{-\alpha}
and
(\beta^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\beta^{\prime}_{s})\in I_{\beta_{1}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\delta_{w^{-1}(\beta^{\prime}_{i})>0}=\delta_{w^{-1}(\beta_{1})>0}.

Proof.

For item (1),
m_{w^{\diamond},\alpha}\geq 2
. Note that
m_{w^{\diamond},-\beta}=0
for
-\beta\in\Delta
because
w^{\diamond}
is restricted. In particular,

\displaystyle\langle\nu_{w},-\beta^{\vee}\rangle=\delta_{w^{-1}(-\beta)<0}=\delta_{w^{-1}(\beta)>0}.

By the optimal superadditivity (Remark

), we have

\displaystyle m_{w^{\diamond},-\alpha_{(k_{0}-1)i_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nu_{w},-\alpha_{(k_{0}-1)i_{0}}\rangle-\delta_{w^{-1}(-\alpha_{(k_{0}-1)i_{0}})<0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{k_{0}-i_{0}-1}\langle\nu_{w},-\beta_{t}^{\prime\vee}\rangle-\delta_{w^{-1}(\alpha_{(k_{0}-1)i_{0}})>0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{k_{0}-i_{0}-1}\delta_{w^{-1}(\beta^{\prime}_{t})>0}-\delta_{w^{-1}(\alpha_{(k_{0}-1)i_{0}})>0}>0.

For item (2), note that

4.
Global methods

4.1.
Global setup

In this subsection, we briefly explain a candidate for the mod
p
local Langlands correspondence given by a global method. We refer
[
24
, §10.3]
for more detail.

Let
\overline{\rho}_{0}:G_{K}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{F})
be a continuous representation. We assume that
p\nmid 2n
and
\overline{\rho}_{0}
admits a potentially diagonalizable potentially crystalline lift of regular weight. By
[
8
, Corollary A.7]
, we can find a
suitable globalization
of
\overline{\rho}_{0}
which is a triple
(F,F^{+},\overline{r})
of a CM field
F
, its maximal totally real subfield
F^{+}
, and a continuous representation
\overline{r}:G_{F^{+}}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}(\mathbf{F})
. Here,
\widetilde{G}
is the Clozel–Harris–Taylor group scheme which is a certain semi-direct product of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{1}
and
\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
. We refer
[
5
, §2.1]
for the precise definition. The important point for us is that each place
v\mid p
in
F^{+}
splits in
F
and
F^{+}_{v}\simeq K
and after choosing such isomorphisms,
\overline{r}|_{G_{F^{+}_{v}}}
maps into
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{1}
and is isomorphic to
\overline{\rho}_{0}\times\mathrm{det}(\overline{\rho}_{0})
. Moreover,
\overline{r}
is potentially diagonalizably automorphic and unramified at places
v\nmid p
.

By solvable base change, we can and do assume that
[F^{+}:\mathbf{Q}]
is even. We let
{\mathcal{G}}_{/F^{+}}
be a reductive group which is an outer form of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
splitting over
F
such that
{\mathcal{G}}(F_{v}^{+})\simeq U_{n}(\mathbf{R})
for all
v\mid\infty
. By abuse of notation, we also denote the reductive model of
{\mathcal{G}}
over
{\mathcal{O}}_{F^{+}}[1/N]
for some
N\in\mathbf{Z}
coprime to
p
again by
{\mathcal{G}}
.

From now on, we fix a place
\widetilde{v}\mid p
in
F
and write
v:=\widetilde{v}|_{F^{+}}
. Let
S(U,\mathbf{F})
be the space of algebraic automorphic forms on
{\mathcal{G}}
of level
U=U^{v}U_{v}
and coefficients
\mathbf{F}
. We have a set
{\mathcal{P}}_{U}
of finite places
\widetilde{w}
of
F
such that
\widetilde{w}|_{F^{+}}
splits in
F
,

, and
U
is unramified at
\widetilde{w}|_{F^{+}}
. For a subset
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathcal{P}}_{U}
of finite complement, let
\mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{P}}
be the abstract Hecke algebra generated by the usual double coset operators at places in

with a maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\overline{r}}
determined by
\overline{r}
. Then fixing a tame level

\displaystyle\pi(\overline{r}):=\varinjlim_{U_{v}\leq{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}^{+}})}S(U^{v}U_{v},\mathbf{F})[{\mathfrak{m}}_{\overline{r}}].

It is a major open conjecture that

is

, i.e. that it depends only on
\overline{\rho}_{0}
.

Remark 4.1
.

We follow Setup
3.12
. For application, we apply the above formalism to

We keep the notation from the previous subsection.

We define a set of Serre weights of

\displaystyle W_{v}(\overline{r}):=\{\sigma\mid\Hom_{{\mathrm{K}}}(\sigma,\pi(\overline{r})|_{{\mathrm{K}}})\neq 0\}.

[

[

[

for unramified groups. Using the expected properties of Breuil–Mézard cycles (
[
22
, Remark 8.1.2]
), we have the following weak version of Serre weight conjectures.

Conjecture 4.3

Let
W^{g}(\overline{\rho}_{0})
be the set of Serre weights
\sigma
of

such that
\overline{\rho}_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{F})
. Then

\displaystyle W^{g}(\overline{\rho}_{0})\subset W_{v}(\overline{r}).

Proposition 4.4

We follow Setup
3.12
. In addition, we assume that
\sigma
is
3n
-generic. Suppose that

\displaystyle W^{g}(\overline{\rho}_{0})\cap\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))=W_{v}(\overline{r})\cap\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau)).

(which is irreducible),
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\prime})
is also non-zero. In other words,
\sigma\in W_{v}(\overline{r})
. This also shows that
W_{v}(\overline{r})\cap\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau^{\prime\prime}))=\{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\}
, which implies
\{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\}\subset W_{v}(\overline{r})\cap\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
.

The claimed equality is not immediate because
\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))\cap W^{?}(\overline{\rho}^{\prime})
strictly contains
\{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\}
. Still,
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\tau^{\prime\prime}))
and
M_{\infty}(\sigma^{\circ}(\tau))
are generically free of rank one over abstractly isomorphic base rings
R^{\eta,\tau^{\prime\prime}}_{\overline{\rho}_{0}}
and
R^{\eta,\tau}_{\overline{\rho}_{0}}
, respectively. By
[
8
, Lemma 2.2.10]
, the cycles attached to
M_{\infty}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau^{\prime\prime}))
and
M_{\infty}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
are given by the cycle attached to the special fiber of their base rings. In particular, they can be identified under the isomorphism between base rings. (In fact, the special fiber of the two base rings are
identical
as quotients of
R_{\overline{\rho}_{0}}^{\square}
. We do not need this here.) This proves that
\{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\}=W_{v}(\overline{r})\cap\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
. Finally,
\sigma^{\prime}
is a Jordan–Hölder factor of
\overline{\sigma}(\tau)
with multiplicity one because
R^{\eta,\tau}_{\overline{\rho}_{0}}/\varpi
is reduced and thus its cycle is multiplicity free.
∎

.

We keep the notation and assumptions in the previous Proposition. We assume that

in Setup

4.4

The second claim follows from the mod

local-global compatibility result for

(and

)
[

5.
The main result

Let
\sigma
be a Serre weight of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
. For
t\in\mathbf{F}^{\times}
, we define the universal supersingular representation (with fixed central character determined by
t
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{SS}_{t}(\sigma):=\cind_{\mathrm{K}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma/(T_{1},\dots,T_{n-1},T_{n}-t).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\sigma
be a Serre weight with a
3n
-deep lowest alcove presentation
(w^{\diamond},\omega)
. Suppose that
w^{\diamond}
is special. Then
\mathrm{SS}_{t}(\sigma)
is non-admissible and of infinite length for any
t\in\mathbf{F}^{\times}
.

Proof.

We follow Setup
3.12
and choose
\overline{\rho}_{0}
in the triple intersection in Proposition
3.14
. Choose a suitable globalization
(F,F^{+},\overline{r})
as in §
4.1
.

Fix
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Then we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cind_{{\mathrm{K}}}^{{\mathrm{G}}}C_{i}(\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SS}_{t}(\sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi(\overline{r}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cyc}_{i}}

where the horizontal surjection is induced by Proposition
2.7
and the (non-zero) vertical arrow is induced by Corollary
4.5
. The diagonal arrow induces a morphism

\displaystyle f:C_{i}(\sigma)\rightarrow\pi(\overline{r})|_{{\mathrm{K}}}

which is non-zero because
\mathrm{cyc}_{i}
is surjective. Recall from Lemma
2.9
that
\sigma\notin\mathrm{JH}(C_{i}(\sigma))
and
\mathrm{JH}(C_{i}(\sigma))\subset\mathrm{JH}(\overline{\sigma}(\tau))
. Then Proposition
4.4
implies that the socle of the image of
f
isomorphic to
\sigma^{\prime}
. This in particular implies that
\sigma^{\prime}
is a Jordan–Hölder factor of
C_{i}(\sigma)
with multiplicity one.

Let
V\subset C_{i}(\sigma)
be the smallest subrepresentation such that
\sigma^{\prime}\subset C_{i}(\sigma)/V
. Let
\pi
be the image of
\cind_{\mathrm{K}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{SS}_{t}(\sigma)/\mathrm{cyc}_{i}(\cind_{{\mathrm{K}}}^{{\mathrm{G}}}V)
. Note that
\pi
is a subquotient of
\mathrm{SS}_{t}(\sigma)
independent of the choice of
\overline{\rho}_{0}
and
\overline{r}
, and the map
\cind_{\mathrm{K}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}\rightarrow\pi(\overline{r})
factors through
\pi
. We claim that
\pi
is non-admissible. This proves the theorem by Lemma
2.10
.

By Corollary
4.5
, the map
\cind_{{\mathrm{K}}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}\rightarrow\pi(\overline{r})
induced by
f
factors through the irreducible quotient
\cind_{{\mathrm{K}}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathcal{H}}(\sigma^{\prime})}\chi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\overline{\rho}_{0})
. Thus,
\pi
admits
\cind_{{\mathrm{K}}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathcal{H}}(\sigma^{\prime})}\chi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\overline{\rho}_{0})
as a quotient. Since there are infinitely many choices of
\overline{\rho}_{0}
with fixed determinant and distinct
\chi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\overline{\rho}_{0})
as in Remark
3.29
,
\cind_{{\mathrm{K}}}^{\mathrm{G}}\sigma^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathcal{H}}(\sigma^{\prime})}\chi_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\overline{\rho}_{0})
gives rise to infinitely many non-isomorphic principal series (see Remark
4.6
).
This shows that
\pi
is of infinite length and non-admissible by Lemma
2.11
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

We explain why Theorem
5.1
for non-special
w^{\diamond}
is more difficult. Let
n=3
and
f=1
. In this case, we have two
p
-restricted alcoves, denoted by
C_{0}
(the base
p
-alcove) and
C_{1}
, and only
C_{1}
is special.

Let
\sigma=F(\lambda)
with
\lambda=(a,b,c)\in C_{0}
sufficiently deep. In this case, we have an explicit description of
C_{i}(\sigma)
. For
i=1
,
C_{i}(\sigma)
is a non-split extension of two Serre weights with socle
\sigma^{\prime}:=F(a,c,b-p+1)
and cosocle
\sigma^{\prime\prime}:=F(c+p-1,a,b)
. For
i=2
, it has the same form with socle
\sigma^{\prime}:=F(b+p-1,a,c)
and cosocle
\sigma^{\prime\prime}:=F(b,c,a-p+1)
. In each case, we can check that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}
is empty, and although
{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
is non-empty, the intersection
{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}^{{\mathrm{ss}}}\cap({\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\backslash{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ss}})
is empty. This is an obstruction to applying our weight cycling argument to the non-admissibility of
\mathrm{SS}_{t}(\sigma)
.

Roughly speaking, it is expected that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{ss}}
is more complicated for higher (with respect to
\uparrow
-ordering)
\sigma
. When the highest weight of
\sigma
belongs to the base
p
-alcove,
{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{ss}}
is too simple to contain suitable Galois representations for our weight cycling argument. If the highest weight of
\sigma
does not belong to the base
p
-alcove, we expect that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{ss}}
always contains Galois representations suitable for our argument, but finding such representations would require studying more complicated neighborhoods of potentially crystalline stacks or finding a better description of
C_{i}(\sigma)
.
