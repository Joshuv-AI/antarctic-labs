Dietary rosmarinic acid alleviates PM2.5-triggered pulmonary injury in broilers: insights into inflammatory signaling, pulmonary microbiota and metabolic modulation
Authors: Ying Zhou, Bin Xu, Dong Wang, Shaoyu Li, Wen Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) has become a severe environmental hazard in intensive poultry farming, which commonly triggers respiratory impairment and further deteriorates the growth performance and health status of broilers. Developing feasible dietary additives to relieve PM2.5-caused toxic damage is urgently required for the modern poultry industry. Rosmarinic acid (RA) is a naturally occurring polyphenolic compound with validated anti-inflammatory and antioxidative capacities; nevertheless, whether dietary RA can ameliorate pulmonary damage induced by PM2.5 pollution in broilers remains poorly understood. The present study aimed to explore the protective potential and molecular mechanisms of RA against PM2.5-mediated lung injury in broiler models. A total of 144 male Arbor Acres (AA) broilers at 21 days of age were randomly assigned to three groups: control group (CON), PM2.5 exposure group (PM), and PM2.5 exposure supplemented with 200 mg/kg RA group (RA). The experimental feeding period lasted for 21 days. The results showed that RA supplementation effectively mitigated pulmonary inflammatory lesions caused by PM2.5 exposure, which was accompanied by improved lung histological morphology and decreased secretion of inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α, and IFN-γ). Molecular detection confirmed that RA intervention effectively inhibited the overactivation of the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB inflammatory cascade. Moreover, dietary RA reshaped the pulmonary microbial community structure by elevating bacterial richness and evenness. The relative abundances of beneficial bacterial genera (Turicibacter, Hydrogenophilus, and Actinoplanes) were enriched, whereas taxa including Ammoniibacillus, Thermobacillus, and Hyphomicrobium were depleted. Untargeted metabolomic analysis screened a total of 66 differential metabolites, which were mainly annotated to tryptophan metabolism, tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, arginine biosynthesis, and glycerophospholipid metabolism pathways. Collectively, these results demonstrate that 200 mg/kg dietary RA protects broilers from PM2.5-triggered pulmonary injury via inhibiting the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB inflammatory pathway, optimizing lung microbial composition, and regulating metabolic patterns. This research offers empirical evidence to support the application of RA as a promising feed additive for maintaining respiratory health of broilers exposed to PM2.5 contamination.

Introduction

Indoor particulate matter (PM) pollution is a prominent issue in intensive broiler production, with airborne particle concentrations inside poultry houses frequently exceeding official safety thresholds. According to previous investigations, the PM2.5 fraction (particles with an aerodynamic diameter below 2.5 μm) in commercial broiler sheds fluctuates between 40 and 2530 μg/m³, which is considerably higher than the recorded levels in pig and cattle breeding facilities (Guo et al., 2022). Excessive PM contamination acts as a critical hazard to farmed animals. High particle exposure not only induces respiratory disorders and growth depression in poultry, but also imposes potential health risks on breeding personnel and endangers the surrounding ecological environment (Shen et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2020). With fine particle size, PM2.5 can penetrate deep into the lung parenchyma, making the lung its primary target organ and imposing severe respiratory hazards (Liu et al., 2022; Paterson et al., 2021). In rodent models, PM2.5 exposure triggers pulmonary inflammation, microbiota dysbiosis, metabolic disorders and cellular damage (Han et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2020; Li et al., 2020). However, current toxicological research specifically focusing on broiler PM2.5 exposure is still insufficient. Prior poultry studies mainly concentrated on total suspended particulates rather than PM2.5, demonstrating that particulate pollutants trigger pulmonary damage, inflammatory activation, and dysregulated microbe-metabolite interactions in broilers (Shen et al., 2022). At present, relevant mechanistic explorations heavily rely on rodent models. The unique pulmonary pathological and physiological responses of broilers following PM2.5 exposure have not been clearly elucidated. In particular, the interactive association between lung microbiota and host metabolic homeostasis remains to be further explored.

Existing toxicological data from human and rodent studies have proven that PM2.5 exposure breaks the stability of the pulmonary microenvironment and triggers microbial dysbiosis in lung tissues. Such microbial imbalance is tightly correlated with immune malfunction and disease deterioration (Wang et al., 2019; Li et al., 2017, 2020b). Additionally, airborne fine particles can alter metabolic profiles both in the lung and peripheral circulation (Ran et al., 2021; Li et al., 2020a). Metabolic shifts have been widely recognized as a vital intermediate mechanism that bridges external environmental stress and tissue pathological damage (Chen et al., 2019; Kilk et al., 2018). In this context, the correlation between pulmonary microorganisms and metabolic characteristics serves as a reliable theoretical framework to clarify the molecular mechanism of PM2.5-mediated lung damage.

As a water-soluble polyphenolic compound predominantly extracted from rosemary and other Labiatae plants, rosmarinic acid (RA) has emerged as a promising plant-based feed additive. It possesses powerful anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and microbiota-regulating capacities, thereby gaining extensive attention in animal nutrition research. Previous biological studies have validated that RA can alleviate systemic and pulmonary inflammatory responses via the suppression of classic inflammatory cascades, including the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway (Ma et al., 2020; Chu et al., 2012). Notably, RA has been shown to alleviate airway inflammation in allergic asthma (Liang, 2017) and to attenuate nasal mucosal damage in a PM2.5-induced allergic rhinitis model (Zhou et al., 2022), indicating its potential efficacy against particulate matter-induced respiratory injury. Moreover, RA has been documented to stabilize intestinal microbial composition under environmental stress (Guo et al., 2018), indicating its great potential in regulating host-microbe crosstalk. Although RA exhibits favorable biological functions, there is a lack of evidence regarding its protective efficacy and molecular mechanisms against PM2.5-triggered pulmonary damage in broilers. Notably, whether RA targets the lung microbiota-metabolism axis to alleviate lung lesions has not been investigated. Accordingly, this study hypothesized that dietary RA supplementation could mitigate PM2.5-mediated pulmonary inflammation in broilers. The underlying mechanism may be attributed to the modulation of lung microbial community structure, the optimization of host metabolic profiles, and the inhibition of the TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory signaling cascade.

Based on the above research background and scientific hypothesis, the present study set three primary research objectives. First, this trial intended to verify the protective capability of dietary RA against PM2.5-induced pulmonary injury in broilers. Second, the regulatory mechanism of RA was explored with an emphasis on lung microbial reconstruction and metabolic modification. Third, this work aimed to clarify the mediating role of the TLR4/NF-κB cascade during the protective process. In this study, a whole-body PM2.5 exposure model was established, and 16S rRNA sequencing combined with untargeted metabolomics was performed. This integrated approach was adopted to clarify the intrinsic correlation between RA-modulated lung microenvironment and inflammatory damage amelioration. The findings are expected to provide novel mechanistic insights and contribute to the development of RA as a promising functional feed additive for enhancing respiratory resilience in poultry under environmental stress.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics

All experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Animal Husbandry and Veterinary Science Institute, Henan Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Number of permit: IACUC-20250915002), and were performed in strict accordance with the Institute's Animal Care guidelines (Zhengzhou, China).

Materials preparation

The PM2.5 sample used in this study covers a particle size range of 0.2 µm to 2.5 µm, which was acquired from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST, SRM 1649b).

Rosmarinic acid was purchased from Hunan Yike Biological Technology Co., Ltd (Changsha, Hunan, China). The product mainly consists of rosmarinic acid and other rosemary components, with a rosmarinic acid content of 10%.

Animal experiment

In this experiment, 200 one-day-old male Arbor Acres (AA) broilers were purchased. All birds were initially fed a corn-soybean basal diet, which was formulated in line with the nutrient requirements for broilers as outlined by the National Research Council (NRC, 1994). Separate nutritional formulations were prepared for birds aged 1–21 days and 22–42 days. On day 14 of the feeding trial, 144 broilers with uniform body weight were screened and randomly allocated into three treatment groups: the control group (CON), PM2.5 exposure group (PM), and PM2.5 plus rosmarinic acid supplementation group (RA). Each treatment contained six replicates, with eight broilers in each replicate. Prior to the formal trial, all broilers were adaptively raised in an artificial climate chamber for one week. The formal experimental period commenced when the broilers reached 21 days of age. Subsequently, chickens assigned to the exposure treatments were transferred into three structurally identical climate-controlled chambers. Throughout the whole feeding period, the indoor temperature and relative humidity were strictly controlled following standard broiler rearing protocols to ensure a suitable living environment for chickens. Specifically, the brooder temperature was kept at 33°C∼35 °C during the brooding stage and adjusted to 22°C∼26 °C in the later feeding period, with relative humidity consistently maintained at 55%∼65%. Broilers in the PM and RA groups were subjected to daily PM2.5 exposure. The particulate matter was continuously aerosolized at a concentration of 1000 µg/m³ for two hours (08:00–10:00) using a liquid aerosol generator (NSF-6A, TOW, Shanghai, China). Birds in the CON group were treated with sterile saline under the same environmental conditions. Real-time monitoring of PM2.5 concentration was conducted throughout the exposure duration. Both the CON and PM groups were offered the basal diet without additional supplements, whereas the RA group was fed the basal diet supplemented with 200 mg/kg RA. Sample collection and preparation

At the end of the 21-day exposure period (day 42 of the experiment), all broilers were weighed individually. One chicken exhibiting body weight closest to the average value of each replicate was selected for subsequent sample collection. Blood was drawn from the wing vein and centrifuged at 3000 r/min to obtain serum, which was then stored at −20 °C. The selected bird was subsequently euthanized by cervical dislocation, and lung tissues were promptly harvested.The left lobe was collected, and approximately 0.1 g lung tissue per bird was taken for subsequent detection. A small section of the tissue was fixed in paraformaldehyde, while the remainder was immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and preserved at −80 °C for later analysis.

Histopathological examination (HE)

Isolated lung tissues were immersed in 4% paraformaldehyde for a 24-h fixation procedure. Thereafter, the tissue samples were dehydrated with gradient ethanol solutions (75%, 85%, 90%, and 95%), transparentized using xylene, and finally embedded in paraffin. The paraffin-embedded tissue blocks were cut into continuous sections with a thickness of 4–5 µm using a microtome. The obtained slices were dewaxed in xylene, rehydrated via a graded ethanol series, and subjected to hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining. Following further dehydration and transparency treatment, the stained sections were sealed with neutral balsam. Morphological observations were subsequently performed under an optical microscope (Olympus BX53, Tokyo, Japan).

The assayed targets encompassed pro-inflammatory cytokines (Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β), Interleukin-6 (IL-6), Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-α), Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ)), the anti-inflammatory cytokine such as Interleukin-10 (IL-10), key elements of the TLR4 signaling pathway (Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), Myeloid differentiation primary response 88 (MyD88), and Nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB)).

Reverse transcription was performed with the All-in-One First Strand Synthesis Master Mix (Kemix, China). 16S rRNA sequencing and bioinformatics analysis of lung microbiota

Pulmonary microbial composition was analyzed by amplifying the V3–V4 hypervariable regions of bacterial 16S rRNA, followed by high-throughput sequencing on an Illumina MiSeq platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). The raw paired-end sequences were subjected to standardized bioinformatic analyses, including quality screening, adapter trimming, and sequence merging. Valid sequences were subsequently grouped into operational taxonomic units (OTUs) with a 97% similarity cutoff. The SILVA 138 database was utilized for taxonomic annotation of the acquired OTUs. For microbial community analysis, alpha-diversity indicators (ACE, Chao1, Simpson, and Shannon indices) were determined to evaluate bacterial richness and diversity. Additionally, beta-diversity was assessed based on Bray–Curtis distances and visualized via principal coordinate analysis (PCoA).

UHPLC-MS/MS analysis

Fifty milligrams of lung tissue were accurately weighed and placed into a 2 mL centrifuge tube along with a single 6- mm grinding bead. Metabolite extraction was performed by adding 400 μL of extraction solution (methanol:water = 4:1,v/v) containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine as internal standard. The mixture was homogenized using a cryogenic tissue grinder for 6 min under controlled conditions (−10 °C, 50 Hz), followed by low-temperature ultrasonication for 30 min (5 °C, 40 kHz). Subsequently, the sample was stored at −20 °C for 30 min and then centrifuged at 13,000×g for 15 min (4 °C). The resulting supernatant was carefully transferred into an insert-equipped sample vial for subsequent instrumental analysis. LC-MS/MS analysis was carried out on a Thermo Fisher Scientific UHPLC system coupled to a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer equipped with Fourier transform detection.

Differential metabolites were preliminarily screened following rigorous screening criteria: metabolites with a variable importance in projection (VIP) value greater than 1 in the OPLS-DA model and a p-value below 0.05 determined by Student’s t-test were regarded as significantly altered metabolites. The screened differential metabolites were further annotated and mapped against the KEGG database to explore relevant metabolic signaling pathways. Pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using Fisher’s exact test via the Python-based scipy.stats package, which was applied to screen the key biological metabolic pathways that were markedly altered under experimental treatment conditions.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Serum levels of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α, Cat. No. H052-1-2), interleukin-1β (IL-1β, Cat. No. H002-1-2), interleukin-6 (IL-6, Cat. No. H007-1-2), interleukin-10 (IL-10, Cat. No. H009-1-2), interferon-γ (IFN-γ, Cat. No. H025-1-2), and superoxide dismutase (SOD, Cat. No. A001-3-2) were quantified using commercial ELISA kits. All detection procedures were strictly implemented in accordance with the standard protocols provided by the manufacturer (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Before performing one-way ANOVA, the normality and homogeneity of variance of the data were confirmed. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed to compare the expression levels of TLR4, MyD88, NF-κB, IL-10, IL-1β, IFN-γ, IL-6, TNF-α, and SOD across different experimental groups. Tukey’s honest significant difference (HSD) post hoc test was adopted for multiple pairwise comparisons. All experimental data were expressed as the mean ± standard error (SE), and a p-value less than 0.05 was defined as the threshold for statistical significance. Data visualization was completed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

Accordingly, RA treatment was found to significantly reduce the levels of IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α, and IFN-γ (P < 0.05; Fig. 2C), suggesting that it alleviates PM2.5-induced inflammation by down regulating these key mediators.

Then We further analyzed the TLR4 signaling pathway and found that RA significantly decreased the expression of TLR4 and its key downstream molecules, MyD88 and NF-κB (P < 0.05; Fig. 2D). This indicates that RA suppresses PM2.5-induced lung inflammation by inhibiting the activation of the TLR4 pathway.

β diversity

Analysis of β-diversity using PCoA demonstrated distinct clustering among the groups (R = 0.2442, P = 0.028; Fig. 3E), indicating that RA significantly reshaped the overall structure of the lung microbiota in response to PM2.5.

Pulmonary microbiota composition at the phylum and genus level

Further analysis of microbial composition revealed the dominant taxa in the lung microbiota. Actinobacteriota, Firmicutes, Proteobacteria, and Bacteroidota were the most abundant phyla (Fig. 3F). At the genus level, the community was dominated by Rhodococcus, Staphylococcus, Achromobacter, Pseudomonas, Lactobacillus, Romboutsia, Faecalibacterium, Blautia, Ruminococcus_torques_group, Parabacteroides, and Intestinimonas (Fig. 3G).

Discussion

This study demonstrates that dietary supplementation with RA effectively alleviates PM2.5-induced lung injury in broiler chickens. Dietary RA suppresses pulmonary inflammation via the TLR4 signaling pathway, modulates the lung microbiota, and ameliorates metabolic dysregulation. Combined multi-omics analysis enabled this study to suggest the existence of a "microbiota–metabolism–inflammation" regulatory axis in broiler lungs. The present findings also clarify that RA acts as an effective functional additive to optimize respiratory conditions and stress resistance, which is closely dependent on its modulation of host-microbe interactions.

A growing body of evidence indicates that lung inflammation is closely associated with PM2.5 exposure (Ndong et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2019; 2018; Yang et al., 2016; Janssen et al., 2015). Consistent with earlier findings, our study observed that PM2.5 exposure increased the expression of IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α, and IFN-γ in lung tissues and induced pulmonary inflammation. Dietary RA supplementation markedly reduced the abundance of these pro-inflammatory mediators, which directly validated its potent anti-inflammatory property. Consistent with the above molecular changes, histological observation showed slighter inflammatory cell infiltration and negligible alveolar structural destruction in the lungs of PM2.5-challenged broilers after RA intervention.

The anti-inflammatory capacity of RA is closely associated with the regulation of core signaling cascades. Previous research has demonstrated that PM2.5 exposure initiates pulmonary inflammation by activating the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling axis (Wang et al., 2022), and RA has been proven to suppress TLR4-related signaling transduction (Ma et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2018). Similarly, the present study also confirmed the inhibitory effect of RA on the TLR4 signaling pathway. This finding elucidates the upstream molecular mechanism responsible for the protective impacts of RA.

Apart from its immunoregulatory function, RA can also confer pulmonary protection by optimizing lung microbial composition. Unlike the well-studied intestinal microbiota, the lung microbiota has recently been identified as a vital regulator of animal respiratory immunity (Zhou et al., 2024; Yuksel et al., 2023). In intensive poultry farming, environmental stress can destabilize lung microbial homeostasis (Zhang et al., 2026; Niu et al., 2025), thereby worsening respiratory inflammatory injuries. Numerous studies have verified the regulatory ability of RA on intestinal microorganisms (Lai et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023). Nevertheless, whether RA could modify lung microflora—an important tissue directly attacked by airborne PM2.5 pollutants—remains unclarified in broilers. The current trial filled this research gap and evidenced that RA supplementation effectively remodeled the dysregulated pulmonary microbiota induced by PM2.5. In detail, RA treatment elevated the relative abundance of Turicibacter, Hydrogenophilus, and Actinoplanes, while reducing the population of Ammoniibacillus, Thermobacillus, and Hyphomicrobium. Such microbial variation contributed to a healthier lung microecological structure. Among these differential bacteria, Turicibacter is capable of synthesizing butyrate (Maki et al., 2022; Zhong et al., 2015). As a crucial bioactive metabolite, butyrate not only provides energy for host cells but also exerts prominent anti-inflammatory bioactivity (Hamada et al., 2023; Lynch et al., 2023). Moreover, Turicibacter has also been shown to significantly increase the production of nutrients and metabolites such as α-ketoglutarate and lactate (Lin et al., 2023). The generation of α-ketoglutarate may enhance tissue antioxidant capacity (Gupta et al., 2024), support immune homeostasis by modulating the Th17/Treg balance (Zhang et al., 2025). Furthermore, Spearman correlation analysis revealed that Turicibacter was significantly negatively correlated with the inflammatory factors IL-6, TLR4, and TNF-α. These findings suggest that RA-induced enrichment of Turicibacter may contribute to the alleviation of lung inflammation, potentially through the production of beneficial metabolites such as butyrate and the subsequent inhibition of inflammatory pathways like TLR4. Thus, remodeling of the lung microbiota toward a more favorable structure represents a novel nutritional-physiological mechanism through which RA may improve lung health in broilers.

This study further elucidates the mechanism by which RA alleviates PM2.5-induced lung injury from a metabolic perspective. After particulate matter enters the lungs through the respiratory tract, it may also cause health issues such as inflammatory responses by interfering with metabolic processes (Fan et al., 2023). In the present study, RA ameliorated PM2.5-triggered metabolic disorders mainly through the modulation of critical metabolic pathways, including tryptophan metabolism, tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, arginine biosynthesis, and glycerophospholipid metabolism. The metabolic alterations induced by RA exert substantial effects on the nutritional physiological functions of poultry. The intermediates of the TCA cycle (e.g., citric acid, isocitric acid, and aconitic acid) were distinctly modulated by RA, which helped sustain energy metabolic homeostasis (Arnold et al., 2023; Faubert et al., 2017) in pulmonary cells. Broilers have high metabolic requirements; hence, the maintenance of stable TCA cycle flux under PM2.5 stress is crucial for ensuring adequate energy provision to respiratory epithelial cells. This process further preserves the pulmonary barrier structure and mucociliary clearance capacity. Additionally, RA stabilized glycerophospholipid metabolism and regulated its metabolites (e.g., lysophosphatidylethanolamine, LysoPE). Glycerophospholipids serve as essential membrane components and exert regulatory effects on synaptic function (Ziegler et al., 2019). Existing research has confirmed that glycerophospholipid metabolism mediates the modulation of the gut microbiota on the Microbiota-Gut-Brain axis in depressive rodent and non-human primate models (Xue et al., 2020; Zheng et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2018). Thus, it is conducive to retaining cell membrane structural integrity and normal signal transduction. Given that intact cell membranes are essential for alveolar gas exchange and cellular steady-state, RA could effectively sustain pulmonary physiological function during PM2.5 exposure. The regulatory effects of RA on tryptophan and arginine metabolism also reflect its immunometabolic modulation capacity (Mondanelli et al., 2019). These two metabolic pathways facilitate the differentiation and biological functions of regulatory immune cells (e.g., Treg cells) and promote the synthesis of immune mediators such as nitric oxide, ultimately balancing the pulmonary inflammatory response (Zhang et al., 2024). Taken together, RA exerts multi-dimensional metabolic regulation to restore intracellular energy balance, stabilize membrane lipid metabolism, and optimize immunometabolic processes. The systematic improvement of pulmonary nutritional and metabolic microenvironments explains the mechanistic basis of RA-mediated lung protection in PM2.5-challenged broilers.

To further elucidate the interactions among pulmonary inflammation, microbiota, and metabolism following RA intervention under PM2.5 exposure, we performed integrated correlation analyses, which revealed significant associations across these dimensions. To bridge these mechanistic insights with practical poultry production, we propose a holistic "Nutritional Intervention–Host Response" model. In this framework, dietary RA is absorbed and exerts local and systemic effects. It directly or indirectly modulates the lung microbiota, enriching beneficial genera such as Turicibacter. This microbial restructuring drives changes in metabolite profiles—including increased butyrate production—which subsequently regulate host lung metabolism, particularly enhancing energy homeostasis (via TCA cycle intermediates) and stabilizing glycerophospholipid metabolism. These metabolic adaptations further contribute to suppressing the overactivated TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby attenuating pulmonary inflammation and oxidative damage. The mitigation of inflammatory stress is anticipated to support respiratory health, which may in turn enhance overall disease resilience and productive performance in broilers. Collectively, this model suggests that dietary RA supplementation could serve as a preventive nutritional strategy to strengthen the respiratory system’s resilience against ubiquitous environmental PM2.5 challenges. By maintaining pulmonary homeostasis, RA may help stabilize health status and potentially safeguard feed efficiency and growth performance in poultry under environmental stress. While further validation of the precise causal sequences within this model is warranted, this study provides the first systematic evidence supporting its plausibility and offers a mechanistic rationale for considering RA as a natural feed additive to mitigate airborne pollutant-induced health impairments in livestock.

This study provides initial insights into the potential and mechanisms through which RA alleviates PM2.5-induced lung injury in broilers, yet several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the dose and duration of RA intervention require further refinement, and its dose–response relationship as well as long-term feeding effects need to be systematically evaluated. Future studies should assess the direct impact of RA supplementation on broiler growth performance—such as daily weight gain and feed conversion ratio—under commercial rearing conditions, in order to determine its optimal practical application. Additionally, investigating potential synergistic effects between RA and other feed additives, such as probiotics or antioxidants, could support the development of integrated nutritional strategies for mitigating environmental stress. Finally, the precise causal sequence and molecular underpinnings of the proposed "microbiota–metabolism–immune" interaction network remain to be fully elucidated. Further validation through time-course experiments or interventional approaches such as microbiota transplantation would help clarify the directional relationships within this triad.

Conclusion

In summary, this study suggests that dietary supplementation with 200 mg/kg RA effectively alleviates PM2.5-induced pulmonary inflammatory injury in broilers. Its protective effects are closely associated with inhibition of the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway, remodeling of the lung microbial community (e.g., increasing the abundance of Turicibacter, which is linked to butyrate production), and systemic regulation of the lung tissue metabolome (involving key pathways such as the TCA cycle and glycerophospholipid metabolism). These findings preliminarily construct an integrative "nutritional intervention–microbiota–metabolism–immune" framework. Although the precise causal relationships and production benefits require further validation, this study provides important theoretical and experimental evidence for considering RA as a potential natural functional feed ingredient to improve lung health in poultry under intensive farming conditions.
