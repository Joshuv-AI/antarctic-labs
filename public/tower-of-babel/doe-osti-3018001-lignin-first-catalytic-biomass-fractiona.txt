 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 The existing program that ExxonMobil is carrying out with Clariant and Genomatica is consistent
 with all pioneer cellulosic fuels plants inasmuch as biomass enters the facility and is processed, in
 its entirety, through at least the hydrolysis step of biochemical conversion. This approach creates
 significant operating challenges as biomass can vary widely in composition and mechanical
 properties across and within a given biomass type. The proposed program involves a first step
 fractionation using solvolysis and reductive catalysis to generate a lignin oil containing well-
 defined monomers, dimers, and oligomers and a delignified cellulose/ hemicellulose pulp. The
 amount of lignin oil vs pulp and the type of monomers produced are expected to vary with
 biomass type due to differences in lignin composition and chemistry. The goal of the program is
 to determine a) if the pulp generated across biomass types is more consistent from a
 processibility standpoint (vs conventional steam explosion) and b) if a higher value disposition of
 lignin (vs power generation) can be developed. If successful, the program will:

 1. Create a new biomass pretreatment scheme that avoids operability issues seen in
 thermomechanical systems through the use of chemical treatment while avoiding some of
 the unwanted side effects associated with existing chemical treatment approaches (i.e.
 organosolv).
 2. Demonstrate the ability to convert recovered lignin into a form that will allow it to be
 hydroprocessed to fuels range molecules or higher value chemicals. This would replace
 the conventional approach of taking lignin and burning it in a lower efficiency power
 generation step.
 3. Define potential continuous (vs batch) process embodiments for lignin solvolysis,
 concentrating, and downstream upgrading and develop a new continuous lignin-first
 processing experimental capability using a twin-screw extruder system that can
 support/test continuous processing schemes.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Task 1: Evaluation of reductive catalytic fractionation (RCF) of a broad set of biomass
 samples

 The goal of this task was to carry out bench scale evaluation of the performance of a range of
 RCF systems (solvents, process conditions). Performance was evaluated based on lignin
 recovery (total lignin removal and monomer yield).

 Task 2: Characterization of biomass and produced lignin fractions and resulting
 polysaccharide pulp

 The goal of this task was to fully characterize incoming biomass and resulting lignin fractions
 and product pulp. This included characterization of monomers and oligomers in the liquid phase,
 the chemical and structural properties of produced polysaccharide pulps, which as linked back to
 analysis of incoming biomass.

 Task 3: Enzymatic hydrolysis of produced pulps

 This task focused on carrying out a standard (to be defined) bench scale hydrolysis of pulp that
 result from the RCF process above including with commercial cellulase and hemicellulase
 enzyme cocktails as well as cellulosome cocktails (the latter of which have been shown to be
 superior to standard cellulases for delignified biomass, Resch et al. ACS SusChemEng 2014).
 The goal was to determine the overall fit of the produced pulps into a downstream process,
 determined by overall sugar yields and measurement of non-standard contaminant that might
 result in to hydrolysate.

 Task 4: Commission and test a continuous RCF solovolysis process using a twin-screw
 extruder and a downstream catalysis process

 Prior to the project, NLR built a customized extrusion system and conducted tests with poplar
 wood on a bench-scale system. This will enable truly continuous lignin-first processing for the
 first time. This task focused on further deployment and process testing of this process design.
 Scoping of the potential process embodiments was carried out to determine rough energy needs
 as well as to identify potential integration with downstream processing steps.
