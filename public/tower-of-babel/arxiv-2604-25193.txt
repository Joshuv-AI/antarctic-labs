Adaptive Sensing beyond Non-Adaptive Information Limits: End-to-End Co-Design of Geometry, Policy, and Inference
Arvin Keshvari, William Tuxbury, Zin Lin

Abstract
Inverse design has made vast physical parameter spaces a substrate for emergent behavior. In sensing, the stakes of this principle are sharpest at the analog-to-digital boundary, where any information the hardware fails to capture is information no downstream algorithm can recover; hardware optimization alone is therefore not enough, and the geometry must be co-designed with a rule for what to measure next. We formulate this co-design as joint dynamic programming (joint-DP): a single optimization over the continuous hardware geometry and a Bellman-optimal adaptive measurement policy. The outer hardware gradient is computed by differentiable dynamic programming with a sharp Bellman maximum, which the envelope theorem makes exact and bias-free, and a relaxation hierarchy carries the common framework from small discrete POMDPs to 10 5 10^{5} -pixel photonic topologies. Across three case studies, joint-DP beats the natural baseline of its community by a large factor: on a radar beam-search POMDP, classical information-bound-guided geometry selection loses 2.8 × 2.8\times in attainable adaptive value; on a superconducting-qubit flux sensor, joint-DP reduces deployed mean-squared error by 11.3 × 11.3\times over the joint Bayesian Cramér–Rao baseline, with both geometries numerically co-optimized under their respective objectives; and on a 90,000 90{,}000 -pixel photonic metasensor, joint-DP via the Bayesian Fisher-information-matrix surrogate reduces deployed mean-squared error by 123 × 123\times relative to a randomized baseline. For any sensor whose hardware is designed once but whose policy runs for the device’s lifetime, joint optimization of hardware and policy is the minimum principled procedure.

In this sense,

This question sharpens dramatically in sensing and metrology, at the analog-to-digital boundary where a physical signal becomes a digital record and any information lost there bounds everything any downstream algorithm can recover. The conventional blueprint of the last two decades pairs a generic, task-agnostic transducer front-end with powerful digital reconstruction, and so treats the physical hardware as a passive conduit and asks the digital stages to rescue whatever structure the geometry happens to impose. Problem setup and the scalar-reward family

2.1
Sensor model

A
sensor
in this paper is a tuple
(\bm{c},\pi)
where
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{c}}
is a continuous
physical geometry
fixed at manufacturing time (the flux-bias and readout-line mutual inductances of a SQUID loop, the permittivity distribution of an inverse-designed metasurface, the elements of a phased-array antenna), and
\pi:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{S}
is a
measurement policy
that maps the current belief
\bm{b}\in\mathcal{B}
over the unknown state
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
to an action
\bm{s}\in\mathcal{S}
. At each step
k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
the policy emits an action
\bm{s}_{k}=\pi(\bm{b}_{k})
, whose execution produces a
measurement

\bm{y}_{k}\in\mathcal{Y}
drawn from the likelihood

\bm{y}_{k}\;\sim\;p(\bm{y}_{k}\mid\bm{x},\bm{s}_{k};\,\bm{c}),

(1)

where
\mathcal{Y}
is the measurement space specified by the physical readout (discrete detector counts in a photon-starved regime, photon clicks in a Ramsey sequence, real-valued voltages from a homodyne detector, etc.) and
\bm{c}
parametrizes the likelihood through its physical forward model (Maxwell’s equations, circuit Hamiltonian dynamics, Schrödinger evolution with pulse shaping, etc.). For non-adaptive (“fixed-schedule”) policies
\pi
is a constant function of the belief; for adaptive policies
\pi
depends on the history
\mathcal{F}_{k-1}=\{\bm{s}_{1:k-1},\bm{y}_{1:k-1}\}
through the posterior
\bm{b}_{k}=p(\bm{x}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1};\,\bm{c})
. We write
K
for the

(the total number of measurement steps the operator is budgeted before an estimate of
\bm{x}
is reported, which may be limited by coherence time in a quantum sensor, by integration-time budget in a radar search, or by throughput requirements in an imaging pipeline),
p_{0}(\bm{x})
for the prior, and reserve
\pi^{\star}(\bm{c})
for the Bellman-optimal policy at fixed
\bm{c}
. Inner expectations are over observations
\bm{y}
with
\bm{x},\bm{c}
held fixed; outer expectations are over the prior
p_{0}
.

2.2
Joint optimization statement

A
joint co-design objective
is any scalar functional of the hardware-policy pair,

V(\bm{c},\pi)\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim p_{0}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}_{1:K}\mid\bm{x},\pi,\bm{c}}\!\bigl[\,\Phi(\bm{x},\bm{s}_{1:K}^{\pi},\bm{c})\,\bigr],

(2)

\Phi:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{S}^{K}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{R}

\Phi

\bm{x}

\bm{s}_{1:K}

(3)

\Phi

(13)

\displaystyle V_{\text{adaptive}}(\bm{c})

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}\mid\bm{x},\pi,\bm{c}}\!\bigl[\Phi(\bm{x},\bm{s}_{1:K}^{\pi},\bm{c})\bigr],

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{IG}](\bm{c})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}[V_{\text{oracle}}(\bm{x},\bm{c})]-V_{\text{fixed}}(\bm{c}).

(15)

V_{\text{oracle}}(\bm{x},\bm{c})
is the value attained by a
clairvoyant
sensor that knows the true
\bm{x}
and picks the best fixed schedule for it.
V_{\text{fixed}}(\bm{c})
is the best non-adaptive schedule,
V_{\text{adaptive}}(\bm{c})
is the best adaptive policy (the quantity the user ultimately deploys), and
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{IG}](\bm{c})
, the
expected ignorance gap
or
expected value of perfect information
(EVPI)
[
45
,
89
]
, measures how much adaptive slack exists at that geometry.

The three values are ordered at every
\bm{c}
,

V_{\text{fixed}}(\bm{c})\;\leq\;V_{\text{adaptive}}(\bm{c})\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}[V_{\text{oracle}}(\bm{x},\bm{c})],

(16)

because fixed schedules are a subset of adaptive policies (left) and no policy can beat the clairvoyant oracle (right). Subtracting
V_{\text{fixed}}
from Equation (
16
) bounds the
adaptive gain

V_{\text{adaptive}}(\bm{c})-V_{\text{fixed}}(\bm{c})
above by the EVPI:

0\;\leq\;V_{\text{adaptive}}(\bm{c})-V_{\text{fixed}}(\bm{c})\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{IG}](\bm{c}).

(17)

If this upper bound were tight, the EVPI-maximizing geometry would coincide with the adaptive-gain-maximizing one, the hope that motivates the EVPI as a candidate design criterion. §
3.3
shows the hope does not survive scrutiny.

3.2
Why joint hardware–policy co-design is necessary

The quantity the user deploys is
V_{\text{adaptive}}(\bm{c})
, so the question is which hardware maximizes that deployed value. Two facts make it a function of
\bm{c}
that cannot be maximized piecewise.

Fact 1: adaptation extracts real value at most geometries.

A fixed schedule commits all
K
measurements in advance, before any data has arrived. An adaptive policy sees each outcome as it happens and re-routes subsequent measurements accordingly: effort that would have been wasted confirming something the data has already settled gets redirected toward parts of the state space that remain ambiguous. In an imaging problem this is the difference between a fixed raster scan and one that zooms in on the suspected region; in a qubit-flux sensor it is the difference between a fixed Ramsey delay ladder and one that shortens
\tau
when the posterior is broad (to avoid phase-wrapping ambiguity) and lengthens
\tau
once the flux is roughly localized. The adaptive gain of Equation (
17
) is therefore typically strictly positive and often sizable.

Fact 2: the adaptive gain depends nontrivially on
\bm{c}
.

A hardware configuration that maximizes
V_{\text{fixed}}
is not in general the hardware that maximizes
V_{\text{adaptive}}
: a geometry good for blind schedules is good because its expected measurements are individually informative on average, and that property tends to leave little room for outcome-dependent re-routing. Conversely, a geometry with very large oracle value (the right-hand side of Equation (
16
)) may put so much of that value out of reach of any policy that only sees past data that the realizable adaptive value is actually small. The geometry that deploys well under an adaptive policy is an
interior
geometry, one that neither is over-tuned to blind schedules nor relies on clairvoyance; this optimum is not located by examining
V_{\text{fixed}}
or
V_{\text{oracle}}
separately.

Consequence: the two layers cannot be solved in sequence.

One cannot pick
\bm{c}
first (by any single-functional criterion) and then pick
\pi
, because the criterion for a good
\bm{c}
depends on which
\pi
will follow. Joint optimization of
(\bm{c},\pi)
is necessary; the rest of the paper is about making it tractable. Section
4
shows how differentiable dynamic programming (with the envelope theorem providing the sharp-max exactness certificate) decouples the
gradient computation
from the inner-policy solve even when the two quantities themselves do not decouple, so that an outer-loop optimizer can climb
V_{\text{adaptive}}(\bm{c})
with a single Bellman solve per iteration.

3.3
Why EVPI is not a shortcut

It is worth asking whether the EVPI can stand in for
V_{\text{adaptive}}
as a design criterion. Since
V_{\text{oracle}}
and
V_{\text{fixed}}
are computable by direct schedule enumeration (no Bellman recursion, no belief tree),
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{IG}](\bm{c})
is a cheap scalar functional of
\bm{c}
, unlike
V_{\text{adaptive}}(\bm{c})
which requires the full backward-in-time value iteration.

The shortcut fails: the upper bound in Equation (
17
) is slack, and its slackness pattern is non-monotone in
\bm{c}
, so

\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\bm{c}}\,\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{IG}](\bm{c})\;\neq\;\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\bm{c}}V_{\text{adaptive}}(\bm{c}).

(18)

Extreme geometries (a very narrow beam, a near-isotropic beam) produce high EVPI because the oracle’s advantage over any blind fixed schedule is enormous, but the adaptive policy, which does not know
\bm{x}
, cannot replicate the oracle’s strategy and captures only a small fraction of the nominal headroom. Interior geometries have a modest EVPI ceiling but let the adaptive policy capture a larger fraction of it, which is what ultimately matters. On the radar benchmark of Section
6
, the EVPI-maximizing geometry loses a factor of
2.8\times
in attainable
V_{\text{adaptive}}
to the joint-DP optimum; the joint solve is unavoidable.

EVPI retains useful roles as a workflow tool rather than a design target: a cheap pre-screen when schedule enumeration is tractable but the full joint DP is not; a post-hoc gap-to-oracle diagnostic at
\bm{c}^{\star}
; and an interpretive lens on the shape of the
\bm{c}
-landscape. §
9
develops these.

4
Differentiable dynamic programming for the outer gradient

4.1
Differentiable DP in context

The outer joint-DP optimization (Equation (
7
)) requires a gradient of the inner Bellman value
V^{\star}(\bm{c})=V_{0}(p_{0};\,\bm{c})
with respect to the continuous hardware
\bm{c}
. The inner Bellman recursion (Equations (
4
)–(
5
)) contains a
\max
over actions at every layer, and
\max
is non-smooth. Differentiating a nested
\max
-over-expectations recursion is therefore the load-bearing operation, and it is the subject of a now-active ML literature under the name
differentiable dynamic programming

[
70
,
2
,
34
,
10
]
. Two strategies for handling the
\max
dominate the landscape:

Smooth-max (the dominant approach).

Replace
\max
with a smooth relaxation (softmax, Tsallis entropy-regularization, Boltzmann averaging) so the recursion becomes everywhere-differentiable and reverse-mode automatic differentiation handles the rest. This is the path taken by Mensch & Blondel
[
70
]
, differentiable-MPC work
[
2
]
, and a broader family of implicit-differentiation methods
[
34
,
10
]
. The price is a
regularization bias
: the smoothed recursion no longer evaluates
V^{\star}(\bm{c})
exactly, and the gradient is biased by an amount controlled by the smoothing temperature. The bias can be driven to zero by annealing the temperature, but the limit requires a schedule that is itself a hyperparameter.

Sharp-max via envelope (the approach of this paper).

Keep the
\max
operator sharp in the recursion and use the envelope theorem
[
72
,
31
,
7
,
12
]
to certify that, at the Bellman-optimal argmax, the indirect contribution of argmax sensitivity to
\partial V^{\star}/\partial\bm{c}
vanishes. The recursion keeps its exact Bellman value, and reverse-mode AD through an
argmax-frozen
version of the recursion returns an exact, unbiased hardware gradient with no smoothing bias and no temperature schedule.

The two strategies trade smoothness for exactness. Smooth-max gives everywhere-differentiable code and a biased gradient; sharp-max gives exact gradients and requires one additional structural step (freezing the argmax) to take advantage of the envelope identity. For the physical-hardware gradients this paper is concerned with, where biases in
\partial V^{\star}/\partial\bm{c}
could steer the outer optimization to the wrong
\bm{c}^{\star}
and the inner Bellman DP is already exact on its own terms, the sharp-max path is the natural choice. Two further distinctions from the existing differentiable-DP literature are worth noting: we work on belief-state POMDPs rather than fully-observed MDPs or structured-prediction trellises, and the hardware parameter
\bm{c}
enters both the observation likelihood and the Bayesian belief update
T
, rather than parametrizing transition costs or trellis scores directly. The remainder of this section develops the sharp-max path in that setting: the envelope identity (§
4.2
), its operational form unfolded through the Bellman recursion (§
4.3
), and the consequences and outer policy-iteration algorithm that consumes the gradient (§
4.4
).

4.2
The envelope identity

Let
\pi^{\star}(\bm{c})=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi\in\Pi}V(\bm{c},\pi)
be the optimal policy at hardware
\bm{c}
. The joint-optimal geometry solves

\bm{c}^{\star}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\bm{c}}V^{\star}(\bm{c}),\qquad V^{\star}(\bm{c})\coloneqq V(\bm{c},\pi^{\star}(\bm{c})).

(19)

A naive outer gradient requires differentiating through the policy argmax:

\frac{dV^{\star}}{d\bm{c}}\;=\;\frac{\partial V}{\partial\bm{c}}\bigg|_{\pi=\pi^{\star}(\bm{c})}\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{\partial V}{\partial\pi}\bigg|_{\pi=\pi^{\star}(\bm{c})}}_{=0\text{ at optimum}}\cdot\frac{d\pi^{\star}}{d\bm{c}}.

(20)

The second term vanishes at the optimum by the first-order optimality condition (or, in discrete-action settings, by the envelope/Danskin theorem
[
31
,
72
,
12
]
). Consequently the outer gradient is simply

\boxed{\;\frac{dV^{\star}}{d\bm{c}}\;=\;\frac{\partial V(\bm{c},\pi^{\star}(\bm{c}))}{\partial\bm{c}}\bigg|_{\pi^{\star}\text{ held fixed}}.\;}

(21)

The policy Jacobian
d\pi^{\star}/d\bm{c}
does not appear.

4.3
Unfolding the envelope gradient through the Bellman recursion

Equation (
21
) gives the envelope gradient in its abstract form. Making it operational requires unfolding through the Bellman recursion of Equations (
4
)–(
5
) to identify the concrete paths by which
\bm{c}
enters
V^{\star}
. The result is a backward-recursion formula for
\partial V_{k}/\partial\bm{c}
that mirrors the Bellman recursion for
V_{k}
, and that can be computed by reverse-mode automatic differentiation with a single structural step: freeze the argmax.

Define the
Q
-function

Q_{k}(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k};\,\bm{c})\;\coloneqq\;\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}_{k}\mid\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k},\bm{c}}\!\bigl[V_{k+1}\bigl(T(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k},\bm{y}_{k};\,\bm{c});\,\bm{c}\bigr)\bigr],

(22)

so that the Bellman recursion reads
V_{k}(\bm{b};\,\bm{c})=\max_{\bm{s}_{k}}Q_{k}(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k};\,\bm{c})
with
\bm{s}_{k}^{\star}(\bm{b};\,\bm{c})\coloneqq\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\bm{s}_{k}}Q_{k}
. The total derivative of
V_{k}
with respect to
\bm{c}
splits into an indirect piece (via
\bm{s}_{k}^{\star}
) and a direct piece:

\frac{\partial V_{k}}{\partial\bm{c}}(\bm{b};\,\bm{c})\;=\;\underbrace{\frac{\partial Q_{k}}{\partial\bm{s}_{k}}(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k}^{\star};\,\bm{c})\cdot\frac{\partial\bm{s}_{k}^{\star}}{\partial\bm{c}}(\bm{b};\,\bm{c})}_{=0\text{ at the argmax}}\;+\;\frac{\partial Q_{k}}{\partial\bm{c}}(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k}^{\star};\,\bm{c}).

(23)

The first term vanishes at an interior argmax by first-order optimality (
\partial Q_{k}/\partial\bm{s}_{k}=0
at
\bm{s}_{k}^{\star}
). What survives is the partial derivative of
Q_{k}
holding
\bm{s}_{k}^{\star}
fixed, the per-step envelope identity

\frac{\partial V_{k}}{\partial\bm{c}}(\bm{b};\,\bm{c})\;=\;\frac{\partial Q_{k}}{\partial\bm{c}}(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k}^{\star};\,\bm{c}).

(24)

Unfolding
\partial Q_{k}/\partial\bm{c}
using the product and chain rules, three contributions enter:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V_{k}}{\partial\bm{c}}(\bm{b};\,\bm{c})\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}_{k}\mid\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k}^{\star},\bm{c}}\!\Biggl[

\displaystyle\;\underbrace{\frac{\partial\log p(\bm{y}_{k}\mid\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k}^{\star},\bm{c})}{\partial\bm{c}}\,V_{k+1}\bigl(T(\cdots);\,\bm{c}\bigr)}_{\text{(i) score / outcome-likelihood term}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{\partial V_{k+1}}{\partial\bm{b}}\bigl(T;\,\bm{c}\bigr)\cdot\frac{\partial T(\bm{b},\bm{s}_{k}^{\star},\bm{y}_{k};\,\bm{c})}{\partial\bm{c}}}_{\text{(ii) belief-update term}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{\partial V_{k+1}}{\partial\bm{c}}\bigl(T;\,\bm{c}\bigr)}_{\text{(iii) recursive term}}\;\Biggr],

(25)

with terminal condition
\partial V_{K}/\partial\bm{c}=\partial\Phi_{0}(\bm{x},\bm{b}_{K};\,\bm{c})/\partial\bm{c}
at the terminal belief. The three terms pick up the three ways
\bm{c}
enters the Bellman backup once the argmax is frozen: through the outcome distribution (i), through the Bayesian belief update
T
(ii), and through the recursive dependence of
V_{k+1}
itself (iii).

Equation (
25
) mirrors Equation (
5
): same DP graph, same backward sweep order, same memoization structure. If
V_{k}
fits in memory (whether as an exact hash table, a count-tuple dictionary, or a Gaussian-moment parametrization), so does
\partial V_{k}/\partial\bm{c}
.

The operational recipe: argmax-freeze, then reverse-mode AD.

One does not implement Equation (
25
) by hand. The workflow is:

1.

Forward pass.
Run backward induction from
k=K
down to
k=0
, populating
V_{k}
and recording
\bm{s}_{k}^{\star}
at every reachable belief summary. No AD tape is active.

2.

Gradient pass.
Re-evaluate
V_{0}(p_{0};\,\bm{c})
by substituting the recorded
\bm{s}_{k}^{\star}
in place of
\max_{\bm{s}_{k}}
. The resulting expression is argmax-free and, by Equation (
21
), identically equal to
V^{\star}(\bm{c})
.

3.

Reverse-mode AD.
Apply an AD system to the gradient-pass code. Because the expression is composed of closed-form primitives in
\bm{c}
(the likelihood, the belief update, the terminal reward) and
\bm{c}
-free combinators (sums, products, argmax lookups), AD returns
\partial V_{0}/\partial\bm{c}
exactly with no custom rule for the Bellman
\max
.

Freezing the argmax turns the non-smooth max-containing recursion into an ordinary differentiable expression: that is the operational content of the envelope theorem. Without it, AD would try to differentiate through the
\max
and produce subgradient terms that Equation (
21
) tells us are already zero at the optimum.

Concrete realizations across case studies.

In Case Study A (radar, discrete belief), the per-step envelope gradient reduces to a finite sum over reachable beliefs;
\partial\Pr/\partial\bm{c}
and
\partial T/\partial\bm{c}
have closed forms in the beam-pattern parameter. In Case Study B (qubit, count-tuple state), the only
\bm{c}
-carrying primitive in the entire DP is the Ramsey likelihood of Equation (
28
); all of the count-tuple combinatorics, action grid, and memoization are
\bm{c}
-free, so the reverse-mode AD graph consists of one closed-form function composed through the DP tree via the chain rule. In Case Study C (photonic, Gaussian-posterior surrogate), the envelope theorem specializes to the implicit-function-theorem adjoint over the inner sensor-parameter argmax, and the per-step
\partial Q_{k}/\partial\bm{c}
becomes a closed-form expression in the FDFD
S
-matrix derivatives; see §
8
.

4.4
Consequences and the outer policy-iteration algorithm

Equations (
21
) and (
25
) are the workhorses of the paper; together they give the sharp-max differentiable-DP outer gradient. Four consequences:

1.

The outer gradient requires solving the inner problem once (to obtain
\pi^{\star}(\bm{c})
) and then differentiating
V
through the likelihood with the policy held fixed. This decouples the computational cost of the Bellman DP from the cost of the outer gradient.

2.

When
\pi^{\star}(\bm{c})
is computed only approximately (by deep RL, by an approximate value iteration, by a myopic one-step-lookahead), the envelope identity still says that at the
approximate
optimum the approximate policy Jacobian’s contribution to the outer gradient is first-order small. The framework accommodates approximate inner solvers without bias.

3.

The outer gradient is differentiable in
\bm{c}
whenever the likelihood is. For photonic problems this is automatic via differentiable electromagnetic simulation
[
47
,
74
]
; for quantum-circuit problems, via differentiable Hamiltonian dynamics
[
58
]
.

4.

A trajectory-sampling Monte-Carlo realization is available: sample trajectories under
\pi^{\star}
, differentiate the trajectory log-likelihood times the terminal reward under
\bm{c}
, average. This is the pathwise REINFORCE estimator
[
116
,
94
]
specialized to the fixed-policy case.

The natural outer-loop algorithm alternates Bellman solves with these gradient steps:

Joint
(\bm{c},\pi)
policy iteration:

1.

Initialize
\bm{c}^{(0)}
.

2.

For
t=1,2,\ldots
until convergence:

•

Inner: solve
\pi^{\star}(\bm{c}^{(t-1)})
(exact DP, deep RL, myopic, etc.).

•

Outer:
\bm{c}^{(t)}=\bm{c}^{(t-1)}+\eta\cdot\frac{\partial V(\bm{c}^{(t-1)},\pi^{\star}(\bm{c}^{(t-1)}))}{\partial\bm{c}}
with step size
\eta
.

•

Optionally: box-project
\bm{c}^{(t)}
onto the feasible set.

Convergence to a stationary point of
V^{\star}
follows from standard policy-iteration theory
[
88
,
9
]
when the inner solver is exact; the envelope theorem extends this to approximate inner solvers provided the approximation error is
o(\eta)
.

4.5
A note on continuous actions

Nothing in the differentiable-DP construction presupposes discrete actions. For continuous
\bm{s}
, the Bellman backup’s inner maximization is a continuous optimization:

V_{k}(\bm{b})=\max_{\bm{s}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}[V_{k+1}(T(\bm{b},\bm{s},\bm{y}))],

(26)

with the terminal condition
V_{K}
given by the inner expression of
\Phi
at the terminal belief (Table
1
). The
k=0
value under the optimal policy coincides with
V(\bm{c},\pi^{\star})
of Equation (
2
).
Standard tools, differential dynamic programming
[
49
,
104
]
, sample-based tree search
[
100
,
27
]
, local gradient inner optimization, HJB-PDE solutions
[
81
]
, apply. The one caveat specific to our setting is that the standard count-statistic belief memoization used by discrete-action Bellman DP (Section
6
) fails for continuous actions because distinct inner-optimization candidates produce distinct child beliefs. We handle this in Case Study C by using the Gaussian-posterior surrogate, in which the belief state is finite-dimensional and the Bellman backup admits a one-step-lookahead closed form; and in Case Study B by log-spaced discretization of the continuous Ramsey-delay dimension at
J=10
grid points.

5
A hierarchy of relaxations

The joint
(\bm{c},\pi)
optimization of Section
2
is exactly solvable only for small discrete POMDPs. When exact Bellman DP is intractable, two distinct philosophies emerge for handling the inner optimization.

Dichotomy at the exact-DP branch point.

The dominant approach in modern reinforcement learning is to parametrize the policy
\pi_{\theta}
(or the action-value
Q_{\theta}
) with a neural network and optimize it by policy gradient,
Q
-learning, or actor–critic
[
39
,
64
]
.
We do not pursue this branch in the present paper, though we do not rule it out for future work.
The reason is a well-documented pathology: an expressive NN policy can absorb the training signal and compensate for a poor
\bm{c}
by investing in policy complexity, leaving
\bm{c}
near its initialization. The outer loop converges to a
hardware-light
fixed point that looks good in simulation but relies on a near-trivial
\bm{c}
hard to justify as a physical design. Known countermeasures partially mitigate the risk but do not yet admit the clean sharp-max differentiable-DP gradient our framework exploits: capacity control on
\pi_{\theta}
(bottleneck width, weight decay, dropout); two-timescale updates with
\bm{c}
stepped on a faster clock than
\theta
; warm-starts from one of the prescribed inner rules below (rungs 3–4); and auxiliary regularizers penalizing hardware-null geometries. We flag the combination of these remedies as a plausible path to extending joint-
(\bm{c},\pi)
to expressive NN policies in future work, and correspondingly exclude NN-parametrized policies from the hierarchy below on a
provisional
rather than principled basis.

The plausible-policy cascade (our path).

The alternative, which we pursue, is to
prescribe

\pi
in closed form as a
plausibly Bellman-optimal
rule at the affordable level of fidelity, and to focus the optimization machinery on
\bm{c}
. Each rule is a closed-form
\operatorname*{arg\,max}
over sensor parameters given a belief summary, chosen as a principled heuristic that approximates the Bellman optimum at its feasibility ceiling; the outer loop then moves
\bm{c}
. The hierarchy is a
cascade
: each rung is triggered by the previous becoming intractable.

Because the policy at every rung is prescribed as an explicit function of the current posterior (or one of its coarsenings), this philosophy
requires
an explicit Bayesian filter as the inference primitive: the filter supplies the sufficient statistic the prescribed rule consumes. Explicit inference is therefore a
consequence
of prescribing a plausible policy, not an independent design choice. This is what the cascade shares with exact DP (rung 1) and what sharply distinguishes our branch from the NN branch above, where a learned encoder over the raw observation history replaces the Bayesian filter entirely. What descends the cascade is (i) the policy’s proximity to the Bellman optimum (Bellman-optimal at rung 1, clairvoyant-CE at rung 2, myopic one-step at rungs 3–4), and correspondingly (ii) the coarseness of the belief summary the policy consumes (and the filter maintains): exact posterior, point estimate, full distribution, Gaussian moments. At no rung is the inference primitive replaced by a learned representation; the Bayesian filter is a first-class object throughout, because the prescribed policy requires it to be. The differentiable-DP gradient of Section
4
relies on this: it applies whenever the inner rule is a prescribed
\operatorname*{arg\,max}
over a belief-summary sufficient statistic, regardless of how coarsely that summary approximates the true posterior.

Rung 1: exact Bellman DP.

\pi^{\star}_{k}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\bm{s}_{k}}\mathcal{Q}^{\star}_{k}(\bm{b}_{k},\bm{s}_{k})
, the true optimum. Inference: exact belief
\bm{b}_{k}
(tabulated for discrete state; on a
K_{\Phi}

Rung 2: certainty-equivalent control, full horizon.

At step
k

\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}=\mathbb{E}[\bm{x}\mid\bm{b}_{k}]
or posterior mode / MAP) and play the schedule the clairvoyant oracle would choose against
\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}

\bm{x}

\hat{\bm{x}}_{k}
is wrong. Feasibility ceiling: one oracle solve per decision step (itself an
N

Rung 3: myopic one-step information criterion.

Collapse the look-ahead to one step:
\bm{s}_{k}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\bm{s}_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}_{k}\mid\bm{b}_{k},\bm{s}_{k}}\!\bigl[\Phi(\bm{x},\bm{s}_{k},\bm{c})\bigr]
, i.e., maximize the single-step expected information (or reward) gain conditional on the full current belief. Inference: full belief
\bm{b}_{k}
maintained by exact Bayesian update. Near-optimal by submodularity bounds for information-theoretic rewards
[
35
]
; failure mode: myopia proper, i.e., no credit for information that pays off two or more steps out. Feasibility ceiling: one
\operatorname*{arg\,max}
over
\bm{s}_{k}
plus a full-belief expectation per step. When the full-belief expectation is itself too expensive (high-dimensional continuous
\bm{x}
, for example), move to rung 4.

Rung 4: Fisher-trace surrogate with EKF.

Replace the information criterion by the Fisher-information trace evaluated at the posterior mean, and replace the exact Bayesian update by an extended Kalman filter:
\bm{s}_{k}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\bm{s}_{k}}\operatorname{tr}J(\bar{\bm{x}}_{k},\bm{s}_{k},\bm{c})
with
\bm{b}_{k}\approx\mathcal{N}(\bar{\bm{x}}_{k},\Sigma_{k})
propagated by EKF. Inference: Gaussian moments
(\bar{\bm{x}}_{k},\Sigma_{k})
. This is the cheapest rung still compatible with continuous
\bm{c}
, continuous
\bm{x}
, and arbitrary state dimension. Failure modes: Gaussian approximation breaks near prior boundaries or under multimodal posteriors; EKF linearization fails when the forward model is strongly non-linear in
\bm{x}
over the posterior support.

The case studies that follow operate at rungs 1, 1, and 4 respectively: Case Study A (radar) at rung 1 with discrete state; Case Study B (scqubit) at rung 1 with a grid-discretized continuous state; Case Study C (photonic metasensor) at rung 4 with
\sim 10^{5}
continuous hardware parameters. No single rung uniformly dominates. Each names its feasibility ceiling and failure mode explicitly so that a practitioner can descend the cascade deliberately, stopping at the first rung that is tractable for the problem in front of them.

An invitation to a broader research program.

Once exact Bellman DP is relaxed, the inner policy becomes an approximation, and the full zoo of reinforcement-learning and prescribed-policy frameworks opens up (policy gradient, actor–critic, off-policy
Q
-learning, POMCP, information-directed sampling, distributional RL, Thompson sampling, model-based planning with learned dynamics, and many more), each with its own strengths, failure modes, and regimes of applicability. To our knowledge, this paper is the first to embed the optimization of continuous physical hardware
\bm{c}
into this zoo, via sharp-max differentiable DP (the envelope-theorem-certified outer gradient) plus a prescribed, plausibly-Bellman-optimal inner rule. Correspondingly, each existing RL or prescribed-policy framework invites a
\bm{c}
-aware revision (the “what does this framework look like when the hardware is a co-equal decision variable?” question) whose answer will generally require new theory (ensuring the
\bm{c}
-gradient is well-defined and free of the hardware-light pathology), new algorithms (coordinating
\bm{c}
-updates with the inner policy on compatible timescales, integrating regularizers that penalize hardware-null geometries), and new numerics (quantifying how much each framework gains or loses when
\bm{c}
is co-optimized rather than fixed). We view this as a new research area opened by the present formulation rather than exhausted by it; the four rungs above are the first systematic traversal, and many more remain.

A deeper stake: intelligence migrating from digital to physical.

Information loss at the analog-to-digital boundary upper-bounds any downstream reconstruction; joint
(\bm{c},\pi)
optimization responds by shaping the physical geometry to participate in the inference rather than merely transduce for it. The relaxation cascade above carries this principle from the pedagogical small to the physically large. A co-optimized
\bm{c}
is no longer a lens, an antenna, or a SQUID loop that merely transduces; it is a purpose-built information-preserving structure that filters, concentrates, and encodes the incoming signal in the geometry’s own complex degrees of freedom, shaped by the task and by the policy that will consume its output. The broader research program we invite is, in this view, a piece of the larger migration of intelligence from digital to physical: in place of a generic measurement whose deficit is rescued by deep networks downstream, the analog structure carries its task-aware share, and the juncture of information loss is relocated, narrowed, and in some regimes transcended. What exact Bellman DP proves possible in the pedagogical small, the relaxed rungs make feasible in the physically large; and the physical structure, once co-optimized, is no longer a conduit for intelligence but a participant in it.

7.6
Why
\phi_{\max}=0.1
: aliasing, horizon, and PCRB validity

The prior width
\phi_{\max}
interacts with the tractable horizon
K=4
through Ramsey aliasing. The likelihood (
28
) is periodic in
\Delta\omega(\bm{x})\,\tau

\tau

\log_{2}(N_{\text{fringes}})
distinct delays are required. At
\phi_{\max}\to\Phi_{0}/2
(uninformative prior) the support spans many fringes and four epochs are too few for either a fixed or an adaptive schedule to resolve them; both joint-DP and joint-PCRB converge to comparable aliasing-floor MSE and the adaptive margin shrinks. At
\phi_{\max}
well below this the prior is narrow enough that aliasing is essentially absent, both methods operate far below the floor, and the joint-DP margin grows rapidly. Separately, the PCRB is a local/asymptotic bound that elides posterior multimodality, so at large
\phi_{\max}
it is mis-specified as a baseline: the comparison would be doubly uninformative (joint-DP
K
-limited, joint-PCRB outside its regime of validity). Choosing
\phi_{\max}=0.1
sits just inside the regime where joint-DP’s adaptive finesse becomes observable at
K=4
while keeping the PCRB baseline a valid reference, a representative rather than extremal test of co-design quality.

9
Discussion

9.1
The common shape of the adaptive advantage

Across the three case studies the adaptive policy’s margin over the best fixed-schedule baseline comes from one move: redirecting the next measurement based on whichever partition of the state space the current data has already ruled out. In Case A, a detection at cell 1 halves the ring’s support before step 2 targets cell 13. In Case B, the first epoch at
\tau\approx 22
ns collapses the multimodal posterior before later epochs switch to
\tau=320
ns to sharpen within a single fringe; the PCRB fixed schedule commits to
\tau=320
ns from step 1 and cannot undo the aliasing. In Case C, each new pair of probe phases is re-aligned with the largest remaining eigenvector of the EKF posterior covariance, so that successive measurements shrink the least-constrained direction of the state. Prior-averaged information criteria, whether mutual information, Fisher information, or EVPI, marginalize over the data before committing an action, so the geometry they recommend is tuned to whichever schedule is best on average rather than best conditionally. The joint-DP margin is therefore not a numerical artifact of any particular problem: it is the signature of a hardware operating point chosen in anticipation of a conditional policy, and hardware chosen without that anticipation cannot, in general, recover it by attaching an adaptive policy after the fact.

9.2
Why the PCRB baseline loses even when it co-optimizes
\bm{c}

The
11.3\times
Case B margin sharpens this general statement. Both
\bm{c}^{\star}_{1}
and
\bm{c}^{\star}_{2}
are genuine numerical optima of their respective objectives inside the same physical box; nothing prevents the PCRB optimizer from landing on
\bm{c}^{\star}_{1}
if that geometry gave it more Fisher information. It lands on a different point
\bm{c}^{\star}_{2}
and still produces MSE an order of magnitude worse, because the two objectives reward different physical behavior. The Fisher information at long Ramsey delay is high because one long measurement gives sharp local phase resolution, but at prior width
\phi_{\max}=0.1\,\Phi_{0}
the prior support spans many Ramsey fringes and the single-level Fisher criterion has no machinery for disambiguating which fringe the true
\Phi_{\mathrm{ext}}
lies on. The Bellman-optimal policy does have that machinery: a short-
\tau
first epoch reduces the multimodal posterior to a unimodal one, and the remaining epochs exploit long-
\tau
resolution within the selected fringe. The PCRB-optimal
\bm{c}
is therefore tuned to a schedule the adaptive policy would never deploy; and because the coherence and drive parameters that favor long-
\tau
fringe visibility differ from those that favor short-
\tau
aliasing-breaking, the two optimizers converge to different geometries entirely. The belief-conditional action selection is not a refinement one can bolt on afterwards, which is what makes co-design, rather than serial “pick
\bm{c}
then pick
\pi
” optimization, the minimum principled procedure.

9.3
What the EVPI
is
still good for

We advocate demoting the EVPI from a design target to a workflow tool. Three concrete roles survive:

1.

A pre-screen when the full joint DP is computationally prohibitive but schedule enumeration remains tractable.
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{IG}](\bm{c})
requires only
V_{\text{oracle}}
and
V_{\text{fixed}}
, both computable by direct enumeration of candidate schedules, bypassing the backward-in-time value iteration that
V_{\text{adaptive}}
demands; the cheapness of EVPI is inherited from the cheapness of those enumerations (small discrete action sets or low-dimensional continuous actions on a reasonable grid), and evaporates when the schedule space is large enough that enumeration itself becomes the bottleneck.

2.

A post-hoc diagnostic showing how much adaptive headroom is available at the joint-optimum geometry. Small
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{IG}](\bm{c}^{\star})
means the joint-optimum is close to the oracle ceiling; large values indicate remaining value from longer horizons, richer action sets, or tighter priors.

3.

An interpretive lens on the shape of the
\bm{c}
-landscape. The non-coincidence of
\operatorname*{arg\,max}\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{IG}]
and
\operatorname*{arg\,max}V_{\text{adaptive}}
encodes physics: extreme geometries have large ceilings that no policy can reach; interior geometries have smaller ceilings that adaptive policies can consume a larger fraction of.

9.4
Limitations

•

Horizon.
Case Studies A and B are at small horizon (
K=4
) where exact DP is tractable. At larger horizons the Bellman memoization table blows up, forcing descent down rungs 2–4 of the hierarchy. The Gaussian-posterior EKF surrogate (rung 4) extrapolates to arbitrary
K
; its accuracy relative to exact DP at moderate
K
is an open question.

•

Parametric
\bm{c}
in Case B.
Case Study B’s 7-dimensional geometry is the closed-form-parametrized subset of the transmon circuit, not the full layout design space. Extending
\bm{c}
to include SQUID-loop mutual inductances via analytically differentiable Biot–Savart
[
69
]
is architecturally supported and expected to give joint-DP further advantage; this is ongoing work.

•

Finite sample MSE bound.
The Bayesian CRB
1/J_{P}
is an asymptotic lower bound. For biased Bayesian estimators on bounded priors at small
K
the bound is loose by orders of magnitude (Case Study B:
\sim 10^{5}
–
10^{6}\times
gap between the CRB and the deployed MSE of either design). Tighter finite-sample bounds such as the Weiss–Weinstein
[
115
]
, Bobrovsky–Zakai, and Ziv–Zakai
[
107
]
bounds would be more informative anchors; integrating them into the framework is a natural next step.

•

Continuous-action Bellman.
The count-statistic memoization exploited in Case Study B breaks for continuous actions. Practical paths include fine discretization (as we did), Gaussian belief approximations (rung 4), or particle-filter DP. A full continuous-action Bellman for the scqubit problem at
K\geq 4
would require one of these paths; we presented the discretization path as the cheapest.

9.5
Open directions: dynamical state

All three case studies treat the unknown
\bm{x}
as static over the
K
-step measurement horizon. The natural next frontier is
dynamical

\bm{x}
: targets that move, circuit parameters that drift, fields that fluctuate across the sensor integration window. Three things change when the state evolves. First, the belief filter becomes a recursive predict-correct step rather than a single-shot posterior update, and for non-Gaussian posteriors the inference primitive has to move from grids and EKFs to particle filters. Second, the Bellman backup has to anticipate state transitions over the
K
steps, not just measurement accumulation, so the dynamics time scale interacts with the horizon and with each rung’s coarsening of the belief summary. Third, when the action space is high-dimensional or continuous (beam angles, integration times, waveform parameters) the prescribed closed-form
\operatorname*{arg\,max}
of §
5
becomes unwieldy and a neural-network policy becomes the natural inner solver, which re-introduces the hardware-light pathology flagged at the branch point of the cascade. The sharp-max differentiable-DP outer gradient survives this transition unchanged; what does not survive is the exactness of the inner solve, and the clean cascade of rungs 1–4 is replaced by a spectrum of training regimes whose geometry gradients depend on policy capacity, dynamics time scale, and filter fidelity together.

We pursue this extension in a companion line of work on real-time tracking of a nonlinear dynamical system with a jointly-designed physical sensor and a learned measurement policy; full treatment is deferred to a future paper. The present work establishes the static-state foundation on which that extension stands.

10
Conclusion

For any sensor whose physical hardware is designed once but whose measurement policy runs for the lifetime of the device, programmable metasurfaces, cognitive radars, frequency-tunable quantum sensors, reconfigurable photonic-integrated meshes, joint optimization of geometry and policy is the minimum principled procedure. Sharp-max differentiable dynamic programming, with the envelope theorem as the exactness certificate, makes the joint optimization tractable; a principled relaxation hierarchy makes it portable across scales; a family of compatible scalar rewards makes it applicable across communities. The Expected Value of Perfect Information, classical and instructive, serves as a diagnostic: its argmax disagrees with the joint-DP argmax by factors of
\sim\!3\times
in attainable adaptive value. The deployment-aligned comparison of joint-DP and Bayesian-CRB co-design on a superconducting-qubit flux sensor gives the operational headline: an
11.3\times
reduction in Bayesian MSE at
z=+132\sigma
, with both hardware geometries numerically co-optimized under their respective objectives.
