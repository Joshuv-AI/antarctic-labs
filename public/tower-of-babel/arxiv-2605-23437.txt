A superlinear improvement on line-free sets in 𝔽 p 3 \mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
Benedek Kovács

Abstract
Building on an earlier result of the author together with Elsholtz, Führer, Füredi, Pach, Simon and Velich [ 3 ] , we present an improved construction for a line-free set in 𝔽 p 3 \mathbb{F}_{p}^{3} , showing that r p ​ ( 𝔽 p 3 ) ≥ ( p − 1 ) 3 + 1 8 ​ p 3 / 2 − O ⁡ ( p ) r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3})\geq(p-1)^{3}+\frac{1}{8}p^{3/2}-O(p) as p → ∞ p\to\infty . This results in the first superlinear-term improvement over the standard hypercube construction { 0 , 1 , … , p − 2 } 3 \{0,1,\ldots,p-2\}^{3} . By taking the complement of our set, we also get a new upper bound of 3 ​ p 2 − 1 8 ​ p 3 / 2 + O ⁡ ( p ) 3p^{2}-\frac{1}{8}p^{3/2}+O(p) on the smallest size of a 2 2 -blocking set in the affine geometry AG ⁡ ( 3 , p ) \mathrm{AG}(3,p) .

Introduction

In this manuscript, we investigate the following extremal combinatorial problem: what is the maximum possible size of a subset
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
which does not contain a full line? Here
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
is the
3
-dimensional vector space over the
p
-element field for some prime
p
, corresponding to the points of the affine geometry
\mathrm{AG}(3,p)
.

More generally, we denote by
r_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})
the largest size of a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
that does not contain any nontrivial
k
-term arithmetic progression (that is, one with nonzero difference); note that for
k=p
this condition is equivalent to saying that
S
does not contain any full line.

The famous
cap set problem
investigates the value of
r_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n})
; note that three distinct points
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
form a line if and only if
\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{0}
. By the result of Ellenberg and Gijswijt
[
5
]
,
r_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n})=o(2.756^{n})
. They achieved this upper bound using a polynomial technique introduced by Croot, Lev and Pach
[
2
]
, which was later reformulated by Tao and Sawin
[
11
,
12
]
using the
slice rank
of tensors. By a more detailed analysis of the argument, Jiang
[
8
]
later improved this upper bound by a factor of
\sqrt{n}
, yielding the best known improvement so far. The best known lower bound is
\Omega(2.2202^{n})
by Romera-Paredes et al.
[
9
]
.

In the general case when
k=p
, the hypercube construction
\{0,1,\ldots,p-2\}^{n}
gives a lower bound of
r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})\geq(p-1)^{n}
. For
n=1
this is obviously sharp, and for
n=2
its sharpness was proven by Jamison
[
7
]
and Brouwer and Schrijver
[
1
]
using the polynomial method. However, for
n=3
, better constructions exist. The author together with Elsholtz, Führer, Füredi, Pach, Simon and Velich
[
3
]
proved the following lower bound by applying a slight modification to the hypercube construction involving
\Theta(p)
points:

Theorem 1.1

(Elsholtz, Führer, Füredi, Kovács, Pach, Simon, Velich
[
3
]
)
.

For all primes
p\geq 5
, we have

r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3})\geq(p-1)^{3}+p-2\sqrt{p}=p^{3}-3p^{2}+4p-2\sqrt{p}-1.

Moreover, we showed a further slight improvement in the case
p\equiv 7\pmod{24}
, as well as a lower bound for general
n
, and refined bounds for specific small values of
p
.

Regarding the upper bound, we also showed an improvement over a previous bound of
r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3})\leq p^{3}-2p^{2}+1
by Sziklai
[
10
]
, using a double counting argument for point pairs on planes:

Theorem 1.2

(Elsholtz, Führer, Füredi, Kovács, Pach, Simon, Velich
[
3
]
)
.

For all primes
p\geq 3
, we have

r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3})\leq p^{3}-2p^{2}-(\sqrt{2}-1)p+2.

To the author’s knowledge, there have been no further improvements from either side, so the precise coefficient of the
p^{2}
term in
r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3})
remains an open question. For further recent work on the value of
r_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})
, see
[
4
,
6
]
.

In this paper, we refine the earlier line-free construction in
[
3
]
, this time modifying the hypercube
[0,p-2]^{3}
in
\Theta(p^{3/2})
points. We improve on Theorem
1.1
and exhibit the first superlinear improvement in the lower bound on
r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3})
over the hypercube construction:

Theorem 1.3
.

For all primes
p\geq 3
, we have

r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3})\geq p^{3}-3p^{2}+\frac{1}{8}p^{3/2}-\frac{7}{16}p.

As a consequence, we have

r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3})\geq(p-1)^{3}+\frac{1}{8}p^{3/2}-O(p)

as the prime
p
tends to infinity.

In finite geometry, a
d
-blocking set

B
of an affine or projective space
T
is a set of points
B\subseteq T
that intersects every subspace of
T
of codimension
d
in at least one point. Note that
B
is a
d
-blocking set if and only if its complement
T\setminus B
does not contain any full subspace of codimension
d
. For
d=n-1
, this means that
T\setminus B
is line-free. So our main result, Theorem
1.3
, can be stated in terms of
2
-blocking sets in the affine space
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
:

Definition 1.4
.

For integers
n,d,q
such that
n\geq 2
,
\penalty\ 1\leq d\leq n-1
and
q
is a prime power, let
a_{d}(n,q)
denote the smallest size of a
d
-blocking set in the affine geometry
\mathrm{AG}(n,q)
.

Corollary 1.5
.

For primes
p
,

a_{2}(3,p)\leq 3p^{2}-\frac{1}{8}p^{3/2}+O(p)

holds as
p\to\infty
.

2
Constructing our line-free set

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.3
. Throughout the section,
p\geq 3
is a fixed prime, and we identify
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with the set
\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
as usual, inheriting the natural ordering from
\mathbb{Z}
. In
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, a
line
always refers to an affine line, that is, a subset of the form
\{\mathbf{a}+\lambda\mathbf{v}:\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
for some
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
with
\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{0}
. For
m\leq n
, the notation
[m,n]
denotes the set
\{m,m+1,\ldots,n\}
, where
m
and
n
are integers or elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we improve upon the construction in
[
3
]
by the author together with Elsholtz, Führer, Füredi, Pach, Simon and Velich (see Theorem
1.1
). Similarly to that construction, the layers of our
3
-dimensional line-free set will be constructed using complements of planar blocking sets. Namely, we will make use of the following observation:

Observation 2.1
.

Let
L
be any line in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
. If
B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
is a set that contains
L
and meets every line parallel to
L
in at least one point, then
B
is a
1
-blocking set in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
.

Proof.

For any line
L^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
, if
L^{\prime}\cap L\neq\emptyset
then
L^{\prime}
meets
B
as well. Otherwise,
L^{\prime}
is parallel to
L
, hence contains at least one point of
B
by our assumption.
∎

Observe that by the result of Jamison
[
7
]
, and Brouwer and Schrijver
[
1
]
,
r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2})=(p-1)^{2}
, or equivalently,
a_{1}(2,p)=2p-1
. Therefore, a set
B
in Observation
2.1
that meets every line parallel to
L
in exactly one point, which hence has
2p-1
points in total, is actually a minimum-size
1
-blocking set in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
. One specific example is the set
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}\setminus[0,p-2]^{2}
, where we call
[0,p-2]^{2}
the
standard square
, a special case of the hypercube construction.

We use our observation to build the following specific family of planar blocking sets, which will be used to define the special layers of our
3
-dimensional construction:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r=\left\lfloor\sqrt{p}\right\rfloor
and
s=\left\lfloor\frac{p-2}{r}\right\rfloor
. Let
0\leq t\leq p-r
be any integer. Then in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
, the union of the main diagonal
\{(x,x):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
and the grid
[t,t+r-1]\times\{0,r,2r,\ldots,sr,p-1\}
is a
1
-blocking set.

Proof.

Let
B
be the described set. We will apply Observation
2.1
, where
L
is taken to be the main diagonal. It suffices to show that
B
contains at least one point from each line of the form
x-y=c
, where
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
.

Let us track the value of
x-y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
as
(x,y)
ranges over the points of the subgrid
[t,t+r-1]\times\{0,r,2r,\ldots,sr\}\subseteq B
. Consider the points of the grid in order from bottom to top, and within each row, from right to left. The initial point
(t+r-1,0)
has
x-y=t+r-1
. Every time we move to the next point in the same row,
x-y
decreases by one, and every time we move from the leftmost point of a row to the rightmost point of the next row,
x
increases by
r-1
while
y
increases by
r
, making
x-y
decrease by one as well. The final point
(t,sr)
has
x-y=t-sr
.

Altogether, we have covered a range of
(r-1)-(-sr)+1=(s+1)r
consecutive values in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and
(s+1)r=\left(\left\lfloor\frac{p-2}{r}\right\rfloor+1\right)r>\frac{p-2}{r}\cdot r=p-2
, meaning that
(s+1)r\geq p-1
. If we only have
p-1
values, then the point
(t+r-1,p-1)\in B
gives the missing value
x-y=t+r
.
∎

Note that this type of blocking set already appeared in the construction in
[
3
]
, where in the complement of the line-free set, layer
p-2
was essentially an instance of the set in Lemma
2.2
. In our new result, we will use this construction in
\left\lfloor\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{p}\right\rfloor
layers, varying the value of
t
so that the intervals
[t,t+r-1]
, and hence the corresponding grid points, are pairwise disjoint.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We may assume that
p\geq 17
, as otherwise one may check that
(p-1)^{3}\geq p^{3}-3p^{2}+\frac{1}{8}p^{3/2}-\frac{7}{16}p
holds, and the hypercube construction already proves the claimed bound.

Fix
p
, and set
r=\left\lfloor\sqrt{p}\right\rfloor
,
s=\left\lfloor\frac{p-2}{r}\right\rfloor
, and
\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{p}\right\rfloor
. Note that the integers
r,s,\ell
satisfy
r\geq 4
and
s,\ell\geq 1
.

For a set
T\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
, and a value
i\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we define the
i
-th layer
of
T
as

T_{i}:=\{(y,z)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}:(i,y,z)\in T\}.

Therefore,
T\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
can be identified with the ordered
p
-tuple
(T_{0},T_{1},\ldots,T_{p-1})
, where
T_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
for all
i
.

We first provide an initial construction
S^{*}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
which does contain full lines, which we will later slightly modify to obtain our line-free set
S
. The layers of the set
S^{*}
are defined as follows:

•

For each
i\in[0,p-\ell-2]
, let the
i
-th layer of
S^{*}
be a standard square, that is,
S^{*}_{i}:=[0,p-2]^{2}
.

•

For each
i\in[p-\ell-1,p-2]
, let
S^{*}_{i}:=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}\setminus A_{i}
, where

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle:=\{(y,y):y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}

\displaystyle\cup\left(\left([t,t+r-1]\cup\{p-1\}\right)\times\{0,r,2r,\ldots,sr\}\right)

\displaystyle\cup\left([0,\ell r-1]\times\{p-1\}\right),

where we set
t=(i-(p-\ell-1))r
.

Observe that
A_{i}
contains an instance of the set from Lemma
2.2
, and hence is a
1
-blocking set in
(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3})_{i}=\{(i,y,z):y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
, meaning that there are no lines within
S^{*}_{i}
.

•

Finally, let
S^{*}_{p-1}:=[0,\ell r-1]\times\{0,r,2r,\ldots,sr\}
.

Now we prove that all lines contained within
S^{*}
belong to a specific family, and we demonstrate a set of points whose removal from
S^{*}
yields a line-free set.

•

Observe that there are no full lines within a layer. For
i\in[0,p-\ell-2]
,
S^{*}_{i}
is the standard square, which is a line-free set. We already saw that
S^{*}_{i}
contains no lines for
i\in[p-\ell-1,p-2]
either; finally,
S^{*}_{p-1}
is a subset of the standard square because
\ell r-1=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{p}\right\rfloor\cdot\left\lfloor\sqrt{p}\right\rfloor-1\leq\frac{1}{4}p-1<p-1
and
sr=\left\lfloor\frac{p-2}{r}\right\rfloor\cdot r\leq p-2<p-1
.

•

There are no full lines of the form
L_{j,k}=\{(x,j,k):x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
in
S^{*}
. If there were such a line, then we would have
(j,k)\in S^{*}_{p-1}
for some
j=ar+b
and
k=cr
, where
a\in[0,\ell-1]
,
b\in[0,r-1]
and
c\in[0,s]
. However, in this case, the point
(j,k)
also appears in the set
A_{i}
for
i=(p-\ell-1)+a\in[p-\ell-1,p-2]
, meaning that
(i,j,k)\not\in S^{*}
.

•

There are also no full lines in
S^{*}
where the
y
-coordinate is constant (
y=j
) but the
x
- and
z
-coordinates vary. Suppose to the contrary that there is such a line
L
with
(p-1,j,k)\in L
. Then
j\in[0,\ell r-1]
and
k\leq sr<p-1
. Since the
z
-coordinate must take every value once, there exists
i\in[0,p-2]
such that
(i,j,p-1)\in L
. However there cannot actually be any such
i
, since
S^{*}_{i}
is a standard square for all
0\leq i\leq p-\ell-2
, and hence does not contain any point with
z
-coordinate
p-1
, and for
i\in[p-\ell-1,p-2]
we have included all points
(j,p-1)
with
j\in[0,\ell r-1]
in
A_{i}
and hence excluded them from
S^{*}_{i}
.

•

Similarly, there are no full lines in
S^{*}
whose
z
-coordinate is constant (
z=k
) but the
x
- and
y
-coordinates vary, because layers
S^{*}_{i}
for
i\in[0,p-\ell-2]\cup\{p-1\}
do not contain any points
(y,z)
with
y=p-1
, and for all
i\in[p-\ell-1,p-2]
we have included all points
(p-1,k)
with
k\in\{0,r,2r,\ldots,sr\}
in
A_{i}
and hence excluded them from
S^{*}_{i}
.

•

The last case is that we have a full line
L\subseteq S^{*}
in which all three coordinates vary. Let the unique point of
L
in layer
p-1
be
P_{0}=(p-1,j,k)
, where
j\in[0,\ell r-1]
and
k\in\{0,r,2r,\ldots,sr\}
. Since
j,k\neq p-1
, the line
L
must contain a point with
y
-coordinate
p-1
in some layer
i_{1}\neq p-1
, and a point with
z
-coordinate
p-1
in some layer
i_{2}\neq p-1
. Note that these two layers cannot coincide, as the point
(p-1,p-1)
is excluded from every layer. Also, as layers
0
to
p-\ell-2
are standard squares, we have
i_{1},i_{2}\in[p-\ell-1,p-2]
.

Let
P_{1}=(i_{1},p-1,k_{1})
and
P_{2}=(i_{2},j_{2},p-1)
be these two points on
L
. By collinearity of
P_{0}
,
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, the
x
- and
z
-coordinates of these three points satisfy the following equation in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
:

\frac{i_{1}-i_{2}}{(p-1)-i_{2}}=\frac{k_{1}-(p-1)}{k-(p-1)}.

This means that we can express
k_{1}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
as follows:

k_{1}=(p-1)+\frac{i_{1}-i_{2}}{(p-1)-i_{2}}\cdot(k-(p-1)).

For every value of
i_{1}\in[p-\ell-1,p-2]
, the value of
i_{2}
can take at most
\ell-1
possible values (
i_{2}\in[p-\ell-1,p-2]\setminus\{i_{1}\}
), and
k
can take at most
s+1
possible values (
k\in\{0,r,2r,\ldots,sr\}
). Hence for each fixed
i_{1}
, the number of possible values for
k_{1}
is at most
(\ell-1)(s+1)
. If we remove all corresponding points
P_{1}=(i_{1},p-1,k_{1})
from
S^{*}
, we hence get a line-free set. As there are
\ell
possible values of
i_{1}
, this requires deleting at most
\ell(\ell-1)(s+1)
points.

Let
S
be the line-free set obtained from

by deleting these points; then
|S|\geq|S^{*}|-\ell(\ell-1)(s+1)
. Figure

of their points (asymptotically

. Note that as

and

. To make the construction easier to visualize, the ratio

.

We now calculate the size of
S^{*}
, counting its points layer by layer.

•

For
i\in[0,p-\ell-2]
,
S^{*}_{i}
is a standard square, containing
(p-1)^{2}
points.

•

For
i\in[p-\ell-1,p-2]
, the set
A_{i}
contains
p

(s+1)(r+1)

\ell r
points with second coordinate

. A point
(j,j)
on the main diagonal appears in the grid as well if we have
r\mid j
and

. (Note that we cannot have

, since

, so
|A_{i}|=p+(s+1)(r+1)+\ell r-1
and
|S^{*}_{i}|=p^{2}-p-(s+1)(r+1)-\ell r+1
.

•

In layer

, we have
|S^{*}_{p-1}|=(s+1)\cdot\ell r
.

Altogether,

|S^{*}|=(p-\ell-1)(p-1)^{2}+\ell(p^{2}-p-(s+1)(r+1)-\ell r+1)+(s+1)\ell r,

therefore we have

|S|\geq|S^{*}|-\ell(\ell-1)(s+1)\geq(p-\ell-1)(p-1)^{2}+\ell(p^{2}-p-(s+1)(r+\ell)-\ell r+1)+(s+1)\ell r.

Using
r=\left\lfloor\sqrt{p}\right\rfloor
,
s=\left\lfloor\frac{p-2}{r}\right\rfloor
and
\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{p}\right\rfloor
, we now put a lower bound on this size. For
p\geq 5
, the parameters can be bounded by

\sqrt{p}-1\leq r\leq\sqrt{p},

\sqrt{p}-2\leq\frac{p-2}{\sqrt{p}}-1\leq\left\lfloor\frac{p-2}{r}\right\rfloor=s\leq\frac{p-2}{r}\leq\frac{p}{\sqrt{p}-1}\leq\sqrt{p}+2,

\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{p}-1\leq\ell\leq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{p}.

So we have

\begin{split}|S|&\geq(p-\ell-1)(p^{2}-2p)+\ell\left(p^{2}-p-(\sqrt{p}+3)\cdot\frac{5}{4}\sqrt{p}-\frac{1}{4}p\right)+(\sqrt{p}-1)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{p}-1\right)\\
&\geq p^{3}-2p^{2}-\ell p^{2}+2\ell p-p^{2}+2p+\ell\left(p^{2}-\frac{5}{2}p-\frac{15}{4}\sqrt{p}\right)+\frac{1}{4}p^{3/2}-\frac{3}{2}p\\
&=p^{3}-3p^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\ell p-\frac{15}{4}\ell\sqrt{p}+\frac{1}{4}p^{3/2}+\frac{1}{2}p\\
&\geq p^{3}-3p^{2}-\frac{1}{8}p^{3/2}-\frac{15}{16}p+\frac{1}{4}p^{3/2}+\frac{1}{2}p=p^{3}-3p^{2}+\frac{1}{8}p^{3/2}-\frac{7}{16}p=(p-1)^{3}+\frac{1}{8}p^{3/2}-O(p),\end{split}

that is, our line-free set
S
indeed has at least the claimed size.
∎
