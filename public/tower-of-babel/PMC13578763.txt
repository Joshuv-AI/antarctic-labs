Experimental study on dynamic mechanical properties of concrete for hydraulic dams with PVA fiber
Authors: Hongchun Feng, Lin Hu, Kedong Yue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To investigate the improvement effect of PVA fibers on the dynamic mechanical properties of concrete in water conservancy dams under dry-wet cycles and impact loads, C40 plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete were selected as the research objects. 0 to 120 dry-wet cycle tests and SHPB dynamic compression tests under different air pressures of 0.2 to 0.5 MPa were carried out. The evolution laws of damage degree, stress-strain curves, peak stress, toughness, energy dissipation and fracture fragmentation morphology of the specimens were systematically analyzed to reveal the strengthening and toughening mechanism of PVA fibers.The results show that the increase in the number of dry-wet cycles and the increase in impact pressure significantly affect the mechanical properties of concrete. The damage degree of both types of concrete increases with the increase in the number of cycles, and the peak stress and toughness continuously decrease.Under the same conditions, the damage degree of PVA fiber concrete is significantly lower than that of plain concrete. Its peak stress, toughness and dissipation energy are all higher than those of plain concrete, and the rate of performance degradation is slower.As the number of dry-wet cycles increases, the internal cracks in the plain concrete rapidly expand and penetrate, showing obvious brittle disintegration characteristics. Meanwhile, the PVA fibers form a three-dimensional network structure in the matrix. Through bridging crack resistance, stress dispersion and energy absorption and toughening effects, they inhibit crack initiation and propagation, delay damage accumulation, and transform the failure mode from brittle fracture to ductile progressive failure.Research has confirmed that PVA fibers can effectively enhance the dry-wet cycle resistance, dynamic bearing capacity and impact toughness of hydraulic concrete, significantly improving its durability and structural safety in complex service environments. This provides experimental basis and theoretical support for the application of high-performance fiber concrete in water conservancy projects.

Introduction

Concrete cracks are one of the most common and widespread diseases in hydraulic concrete structures. Most concrete buildings will experience cracking and damage.The degree of damage caused by cracks to hydraulic concrete structures varies. Severe cracks not only endanger the integrity and stability of the structure, but also lead to the occurrence and development of other diseases, such as leakage and dissolution, erosion by environmental water, expansion of freeze-thaw damage, carbonation of concrete and rusting of reinforcing bars, etc1–3. Especially with the construction of large-scale water conservancy and hydropower projects in our country, there are more and more high dams and large reservoirs. The stress conditions of the dam bodies have become increasingly complex, and the requirements for the crack resistance performance of the concrete of the dams have also become higher and higher.Therefore, the requirements for the concrete materials used in dam construction are also very high. Not only does it demand that the concrete has good workability and ease of construction, but also it must ensure that the various properties of the hardened concrete meet the design requirements, and at the same time, improve the crack resistance performance and increase the crack resistance reserve capacity of the dam concrete4–6.

The concrete of the dam will be continuously affected by water. Under the repeated erosion of water flow, the concrete material is more prone to suffer damage and deterioration. Currently, there have been abundant research results on the evolution of the performance of concrete materials under the action of dry-wet cycles. Yin conducted a study on the mechanical properties of pavement cement concrete under dry-wet cycles7. The results showed that the dry-wet cycles had a significant deteriorating effect on the concrete. As the number of cycles increased, the peak strength, elastic modulus of the specimens decreased, and the ultimate strain increased. Fu conducted a study on the mechanical properties of polypropylene fiber concrete under the action of dry-wet cycles, and found that the elastic modulus, compressive strength and flexural strength of the specimens gradually decreased as the number of dry-wet cycles increased8. Zhao found that with the increase in the number of dry-wet cycles, the compressive strength of fly ash concrete generally showed a decreasing trend9. Li conducted research on the tensile strength of aerated concrete under dry-wet cycles and found that the tensile strength of the specimens decreased as a result of the dry-wet cycles10. Therefore, in order to further enhance the durability of the dam concrete and improve the thermal, mechanical and deformation properties of the cement-based materials, fibers are often added to the dam concrete11–17. Among them, polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) fibers are widely used due to their excellent properties and the ability to improve the durability properties such as impermeability and freeze resistance of the concrete. Some achievements have been made in the research field of water conservancy concrete materials with PVA fibers added. Sun conducted experiments based on the construction of Xiluodu Project to analyze the influence of PVA fibers on the performance of concrete18. They found that PVA fibers could enhance the early compressive strength, splitting tensile strength and ultimate tensile value of concrete, and reduce the drying shrinkage rate and self-generated volume shrinkage deformation of concrete. Liu found that after adding fibers, the strength of the concrete decreased, the dry shrinkage value slightly increased, and the self-generated volume deformation shrinkage decreased19. Liu conducted a study and found that when the volume fraction of PVA was 0.3%, it achieved the best effect in reducing fiber aggregation and enhancing the strength of concrete20. Ji found that PVA has a very good effect in improving the anti-abrasion property of concrete21. Liu concluded that after incorporating PVA fibers, the peeling problem on the surface of the concrete caused by the impact of high-speed water flow was alleviated, effectively enhancing the concrete’s anti-abrasion ability22. Therefore, PVA fibers are typically used in areas with high requirements for anti-corrosion and wear resistance, such as the spillway surfaces of dams, and the results are very satisfactory. However, the existing research results rarely cover the study of the mechanical properties of PVA fiber concrete under impact loads. And during the use of dam concrete, it often suffers from impacts from water flow, ships, etc. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the mechanical properties of PVA fiber concrete under dynamic loads.

In summary, most of the existing research focuses on investigating the static mechanical properties and durability of ordinary concrete and conventional fiber-reinforced concrete under dry-wet cycling conditions. Regarding the existing research on PVA fiber concrete, it is mainly limited to static mechanical properties, drying shrinkage deformation, and anti-abrasion performance indicators. There are few studies on the performance under the coupling effect of dry-wet cycles and dynamic impact loads, and the performance differences caused by the strain rate of the load have not been considered. Therefore, it is difficult to precisely match the actual complex working conditions of water conservancy dams. The concrete of the dam is constantly exposed to alternating dry and wet conditions, and is also continuously subjected to dynamic impact loads such as water flow and floating objects. The existing research results are unable to provide adequate support for the engineering application of PVA fiber concrete under such conditions. Based on this, this paper takes plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete under dry-wet cycling as the research objects, and explores the damage evolution laws and dynamic mechanical responses of concrete under different dry-wet cycling times and different strain rates. This research has filled the gap in related studies and improved the research system for the performance of fiber concrete under complex hydraulic conditions. It can provide experimental basis and theoretical reference for its engineering application.

Materials and methods

Materials and mix ratio

The concrete strength design grade is C40. The materials include Haoluo brand P·O 42.5 grade silicate cement, fine aggregate is well-graded medium sand, coarse aggregate is basalt crushed stone with particle sizes ranging from 5 to 15 mm, water is laboratory tap water, and the water reducer is polycarboxylate water reducer with a dosage of 1% of the cement mass. Among various types of fibers, PVA fibers have good workability with the concrete materials, and they also have excellent water resistance and durability. Therefore, for this experiment, the TY-PVA type fibers were selected, as shown in Fig. 1. The quality ratio of each material for pouring concrete is cement: sand: stone: water = 1:1.25:2.3:0.4. The dosage of PVA fibers in the concrete is controlled at 0.9 kg/m3 to ensure the mixing effect of fiber concrete23,24.Fig. 1PVA fiber.

PVA fiber.

In order to study the subsequent dynamic mechanical properties of plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete, cylindrical test specimens with a diameter of 74 mm and a height of 37 mm were cast using molds. First, mix the fine aggregate and coarse aggregate in the mixer until they are thoroughly combined. Then, add the cementitious materials and fibers and continue to stir until they are evenly mixed. Finally, add water. After the materials are evenly distributed, place them in the mold and perform thorough oscillation to form the shape. After removing the mold, place the specimens in the standard curing environment for 28 days of curing. Use a sonic velocity meter to select specimens with similar longitudinal wave velocities for subsequent experiments.

Test plan

For this experiment, two types of concrete were used: ordinary concrete and PVA fiber concrete. Based on the actual usage conditions of the hydraulic dam structure, dry-wet cycle tests were conducted on both types of concrete for 0, 30, 60, 90, and 120 times. The "wet-dry" cycle test was carried out using the method of natural water infiltration at room temperature and subsequent oven drying. The specimens were placed in a water-filled container to freely absorb water for 24 hours. After wiping off the surface water with a wet towel, they were then placed in a dehumidification drying oven and dried at 105 ℃ for 48 hours. This constitutes one cycle of drying and wetting. For each group of dry-wet cycles, the test specimen must first be placed in the initial drying oven for 12 hours of drying. To ensure complete water absorption of the specimen, stainless steel grids were placed at the interface between the rock sample and the container, as well as at the interface of the rock layers. The water surface was kept 2 cm above the surface of the rock sample. The subsequent tests were conducted when the number of dry-wet cycles reached the designed value25–28. The longitudinal wave velocity was measured using the NN-4B non-metallic ultrasonic testing analyzer. A group of concrete specimens were selected and their longitudinal wave velocities were measured after undergoing different dry-wet cycle tests. The dynamic compression test used the Φ74 mm variable-section separated Hopkinson pressure bar test device from the impact force laboratory. Before the test, a test impact was conducted. Finally, the impact pressures of 0.2 MPa, 0.3 MPa, 0.4 MPa, and 0.5 MPa were selected to carry out impact compression tests on the two types of concrete specimens after the effect of dry-wet cycles (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Drying oven device.

Results and discussion

Degree of injury

The evolution law of damage degree of plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete specimens after the dry-wet cycle action is shown in Fig. 5.Fig. 5Evolution law of test piece damage degree.

Evolution law of test piece damage degree.

As shown in Fig. 5, the damage degree of both types of concrete specimens increased non-linearly with the increase in the number of impacts. Moreover, the damage growth rate of plain concrete was consistently significantly higher than that of PVA fiber concrete. From the perspective of the damage development pattern, as the number of impacts gradually increases from 0 to 120, the damage degree of plain concrete rapidly rises from 0 to approximately 0.55, while the damage degree of PVA fiber concrete only increases to approximately 0.32. The damage gap between the two continues to widen as the number of impacts increases. Specifically, after the 30 impacts, the damage degree of plain concrete was approximately 0.08, while that of PVA fiber concrete was only 0.04. The former was approximately 2.0 times that of the latter; after 60 impacts, the damage degree of plain concrete reached 0.17, while that of PVA fiber concrete was 0.10, which was approximately 1.7 times that of the former; after 90 impacts, the damage degree of plain concrete reached 0.37, and that of PVA fiber concrete was 0.19, which was approximately 1.95 times that of the latter; after 120 impacts, the damage degree of plain concrete exceeded 0.55, and that of PVA fiber concrete was 0.32, which was approximately 1.72 times that of the latter.

From the analysis of the damage mechanism, the continuous action of the impact load will cause repeated stress concentration within the concrete. On one hand, this will lead to the failure of the bonding between the aggregates and the paste interface. On the other hand, it will cause the original cracks to continuously expand and new cracks to continuously form. The internal porosity of the specimen gradually increases, and ultimately, the cumulative damage degree is manifested. The incorporation of PVA fibers fundamentally improved the impact resistance of the concrete:Firstly, the PVA fibers are randomly distributed within the concrete, forming a three-dimensional spatial mesh-like support structure. This effectively disperses the stress generated by impact loads, delaying the initiation and expansion of cracks. Secondly, PVA fibers have excellent tensile strength and ductility on their own. During the impact deformation process, they can provide continuous bridging and crack-resisting forces, thereby preventing the specimen from cracking and deforming. Thirdly, the excellent adhesion of PVA fibers to the cement matrix enhances the interface bonding strength between the aggregates and the paste, improves the overall impact toughness of the concrete, and thereby significantly reduces the rate of damage accumulation under impact conditions.

By comparing the damage characteristics of the two types of concrete, it can be seen that the addition of PVA fibers can effectively reduce the damage degree of concrete under impact loads, and the crack resistance and toughness enhancement effect is more prominent under high impact frequencies. Reinforced concrete experiences rapid damage under impact and has poor resistance to impact. The PVA fiber concrete, due to the crack-resisting and toughening effect of the fibers, significantly slows down the rate of damage accumulation and significantly improves the impact resistance and durability performance. It is more suitable for engineering scenarios that are subjected to repeated impact loads.

Stress-strain curve

The stress-strain curves of the plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete specimens after the dry-wet cycling process are shown in Fig. 6.Fig. 6Stress-Strain Curve (a) Plain concrete (b) PVC fiber concrete.

Stress-Strain Curve (a) Plain concrete (b) PVC fiber concrete.

As can be seen from Fig. 6, under the influence of different dry-wet cycle times (0, 30, 60, 90, and 120 times), the stress-strain curves of plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete have a basically consistent overall development trend. Both exhibit the typical "elastic rise segment, peak segment, and softening decline segment" variation pattern characteristic of ordinary concrete materials. As the number of dry-wet cycles increases, the mechanical properties of both concrete specimens show a significant decline trend. The peak stress continues to decrease, the peak strain decreases somewhat, and the steepness of the curve’s descending section gradually becomes more pronounced, indicating that the dry-wet cycles have caused continuous and cumulative internal damage to the concrete. During the drying process, the free water and capillary water within the concrete continuously evaporate, causing the matrix to shrink and deform. This leads to tensile stress at the interface between the paste and the aggregates, initiating the expansion of initial microcracks. During the humidification process, water once again penetrates the pores, creating pore water pressure, which further promotes the extension and connection of cracks. This repeated process of dry and wet alternation continuously aggravates the deterioration of the internal structure of the concrete, causing the test piece’s ability to resist external loads to continuously decline. Due to the absence of fiber reinforcement and constraint, the internal damage of plain concrete develops more rapidly under dry-wet cycling, and cracks are more likely to penetrate. Therefore, the peak stress drop is greater, the brittle characteristics of the curve are more obvious, and the stress rapidly drops after reaching the peak, showing significant brittle failure characteristics. The overall bearing capacity and deformation capacity significantly decrease with the increase in the number of cycles.

Under the same conditions of dry-wet cycles, the mechanical indicators of PVA fiber concrete are significantly superior to those of plain concrete. Under the same number of cycles, its peak stress is higher, its peak strain is greater, and the curve decline section is more gentle, demonstrating better ductility and toughness. As the number of dry-wet cycles increased from 0 to 120, the peak stress of PVA fiber concrete decreased, but the decrease was significantly smaller than that of plain concrete. Moreover, the strength difference between the two materials continued to widen as the number of cycles increased. This is because the PVA fibers are distributed in a three-dimensional disordered manner within the concrete, forming a spatial network structure. This can effectively constrain the dry and wet deformation of the matrix, alleviate the tensile stress caused by dry-wet alternation, and inhibit the early initiation and expansion of microcracks. Meanwhile, PVA fibers possess excellent tensile strength and interfacial bonding properties. When cracks occur, the fibers can transfer stress through bridging, slowing down the opening and expansion speed of the cracks, preventing sudden brittle failure of the concrete. This enables the specimen to maintain a certain load-bearing capacity after reaching the peak stress, demonstrating superior energy dissipation ability and durability performance. From this, it can be seen that the incorporation of PVA fibers can significantly improve the mechanical performance decay pattern of concrete under dry-wet cycling conditions, effectively reduce internal structural damage, enhance the material’s resistance to dry-wet cycling durability, enable concrete to maintain relatively stable mechanical properties under long-term harsh environmental conditions, fully demonstrating the important role of fibers in strengthening, toughening, and crack resistance, and providing important material support for improving the service life and safety of concrete structures under dry-wet alternating environments.

Peak Stress (a) Plain concrete (b) PVC fiber concrete.

Based on the curve data of the dynamic peak stress of the two groups changing with the number of cycles and the impact pressure shown in Fig. 7, the evolution laws of the dynamic mechanical properties of plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete can be systematically summarized. From the overall trend of change, the dynamic peak stress of both types of concrete shows a continuous decline as the number of dry-wet cycles increases, and a significant increase as the impact pressure increases. Moreover, the performance differences between the two materials under different conditions are very obvious. Under the same impact pressure, as the number of cycles increases from 0 to 120, the dynamic peak stress of plain concrete decreases significantly, and the structure shows a rapid deterioration trend; while the attenuation of PVA fiber concrete is significantly smaller, and the peak stress remains at a relatively high level, demonstrating excellent cyclic fatigue resistance performance. On the other hand, under the same number of cycles, both plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete showed a significant increase in dynamic peak stress as the impact pressure (0.2 MPa, 0.3 MPa, 0.4 MPa, 0.5 MPa) increased. Higher pressure corresponded to higher peak strength, demonstrating the rate-sensitive characteristic of concrete materials to dynamic loads. It is worth noting that under the same pressure and number of cycles, the dynamic peak stress of PVA fiber concrete is significantly higher than that of plain concrete. Moreover, this advantage becomes more pronounced as the number of cycles increases and the impact pressure rises, indicating that the fibers have an extremely prominent enhancing effect on the concrete’s resistance to cyclic fatigue and dynamic loading. Especially in the service environment of large-scale water conservancy dams with high impact and high cycles, PVA fiber concrete demonstrates stronger dynamic stability and durability.

From the perspective of material mechanism, the improvement effect of PVA fibers on the dynamic mechanical properties of concrete in water conservancy dams is multi-dimensional and multi-level. Firstly, the PVA fibers are randomly distributed in a three-dimensional manner within the concrete, forming a continuous and uniform spatial network framework. This structure can effectively disperse the alternating tensile stress caused by the expansion and contraction of water during the dry-wet cycle, slowing down the initiation and propagation of cracks at the interface, and significantly inhibiting the development speed and extent of micro-cracks. The presence of fibers replaces the simple load-bearing mode solely relying on the matrix strength in plain concrete. Through the combined force-bearing of the fibers and the matrix, the structural integrity of concrete under dry-wet alternating conditions is significantly enhanced, the rate of damage accumulation is slowed down, and the peak stress attenuation amplitude is greatly reduced, resulting in more stable long-term service performance. Secondly, under the dynamic load with high impact pressure, PVA fibers, thanks to their high tensile strength and excellent bonding properties, can exhibit a significant “crack bridging” effect. When cracks occur, the fibers can bridge the crack surface, transfer the remaining stress, prevent the concrete from suddenly undergoing brittle fracture, and transform the failure mode from brittle to ductile failure. After the peak, they can still maintain a stable residual bearing capacity, significantly enhancing the toughness and ductility of the dam concrete under impact loads. In addition, PVA fibers have excellent water resistance, durability and anti-seepage properties on their own. When added, they can effectively fill the internal pores of the concrete, reducing the material’s water permeability and minimizing the erosion depth of moisture during the dry-wet cycle. This further inhibits deterioration mechanisms such as thermal expansion and contraction, and pore water pressure, allowing the internal structure of the concrete to remain more compact and stable under long-term water environment conditions. Overall, PVA fibers not only optimize the crack resistance of concrete at the microstructural level, but also enhance the dynamic strength and toughness of the material at the macroscopic mechanical level. This provides an important material enhancement path for the safe and stable operation of water conservancy dam concrete under complex water environments, high impact loads, and long-term cyclic actions. It demonstrates the significant application value of fiber concrete in water conservancy structure engineering.

As shown in Fig. 8, the toughness J of both types of concrete shows a continuous decline trend with the increase in the number of dry-wet cycles (0, 30, 60, 90, 120 times), and shows a significant upward trend with the increase in impact pressure (0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 MPa). There are fundamental differences in the toughness performance of the two types of materials. For plain concrete, its inherent toughness level is relatively low. At 0 cycles, the initial toughness at 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, and 0.5 MPa is only approximately 0.65, 0.76, 0.94, and 1.09 respectively. And as the number of cycles increases, the decay rate is extremely rapid. After 120 cycles, the toughness at 0.2 MPa condition has dropped to 0.35, a decrease of over 46%. Under high pressure conditions, the toughness also significantly declines, with a steep decline in the curve, indicating that plain concrete, under the combined action of dry-wet cycles and dynamic loading, internal cracks rapidly penetrate, the overall integrity of the structure deteriorates rapidly, the anti-deformation and energy dissipation capabilities continuously deteriorate, and the brittle failure characteristics become increasingly prominent. The toughness of PVA fiber concrete remains at a higher level throughout all working conditions. At 0 cycles, the initial toughness under various pressures is 1.08, 1.21, 1.32, and 1.44, which is an increase of over 30% compared to plain concrete. After 120 cycles, it still maintains toughness values of 0.79, 0.88, 1.01, and 1.13, with a much smaller attenuation rate than plain concrete. Moreover, the curve’s descending section is gentle, demonstrating excellent resistance to cyclic damage and energy dissipation under dynamic loads.

From the perspective of the mechanism of action, the enhancement of the toughness of concrete by PVA fibers is the result of a multi-dimensional collaborative effect. On one hand, the PVA fibers are randomly distributed in three dimensions within the concrete, forming a continuous spatial mesh-like supporting structure, which effectively disperses the repeated expansion and contraction stresses caused by dry-wet cycles, inhibits the initiation and expansion of microcracks, delays the deterioration of the internal structure, and enables the concrete to maintain good deformation coordination ability under long-term cyclic action, with the rate of toughness degradation significantly reduced. On the other hand, under the impact dynamic load, the fibers, thanks to their excellent tensile strength and interface bonding performance, exert a significant crack bridging effect. When cracks occur, the fibers can bridge the two sides of the cracks and transfer the stress, avoiding sudden brittle fracture of the specimen, allowing the concrete to continuously absorb impact energy during the failure process, significantly enhancing its energy dissipation capacity and ductility, thereby achieving a significant improvement in toughness. Meanwhile, the excellent water resistance and impermeability of PVA fibers can reduce the erosion of water on the matrix, further alleviating the structural damage caused by the dry-wet cycle. This enables the concrete to maintain a stable toughness level under the combined conditions of dry-wet cycles and dynamic loads in the service environment of the hydraulic dam, effectively enhancing the structural’s resistance to impact and fatigue, and providing an important guarantee for the long-term safe operation of hydraulic concrete.

From Fig. 9, the energy dissipation changes under the combined action of dry-wet cycles and dynamic loads for the two types of concrete can be systematically analyzed. From the overall trend, the energy dissipation density of both plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete shows a continuous decline as the number of dry-wet cycles (0, 30, 60, 90, 120 times) increases, and it shows a significant increase as the impact pressure (0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 MPa) rises. The degree of performance attenuation and the energy release mode of the two are fundamentally different.

For plain concrete, its baseline level of energy dissipation is low, and its inherent ability to resist damage and absorb energy is insufficient. At the 0th cycle, the initial energy dissipation densities under impact pressures of 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, and 0.5 MPa were approximately 0.50, 0.45, 0.34, and 0.30 respectively. These values decreased rapidly as the number of cycles increased. By the 120th cycle, the dissipated energy at each pressure level decreased to approximately 0.22, 0.18, 0.09, and 0.06 respectively. The reduction rates exceeded 56%, 60%, 73%, and 80% respectively. The decline in the curve was steep, and the constant terms and coefficients in the fitting formula all showed a rapid deteriorating trend. This indicates that under the action of dry-wet cycles, the internal moisture of the plain concrete undergoes repeated migration, causing the matrix to undergo dry shrinkage and wet expansion. At the interface between the paste and the aggregate, alternating tensile stress is generated. The original microcracks continuously emerge, expand and penetrate. When subjected to the impact dynamic load input energy, the crack instantly loses stability and completely penetrates. The impact energy is released instantaneously in the form of acoustic energy, kinetic energy, etc. There is a lack of effective energy dissipation carriers, ultimately resulting in a sharp decline in the energy dissipation density and the brittle nature of the failure. It is impossible to achieve effective energy conversion and dissipation during the failure process, and the structural stability rapidly deteriorates.

In contrast, the energy dissipation density of PVA fiber concrete remains at a relatively high level throughout all working conditions, and its decay rate is much slower than that of plain concrete. During the 0th cycle, the initial dissipation energy at each pressure level was approximately 0.60, 0.54, 0.34, and 0.38 respectively, which was significantly higher than that of plain concrete under the same conditions. After 120 cycles, the dissipation energy still remained at approximately 0.39, 0.32, 0.19, and 0.11 respectively, significantly reducing the attenuation amplitude. This stable dissipative property stems from the multi-scale reinforcing mechanism of PVA fibers: The fibers form a three-dimensional spatial network structure within the concrete, effectively dispersing the expansion and contraction stresses of the matrix during the dry-wet cycle, inhibiting the early initiation and propagation of microcracks, and delaying the deterioration process of structural integrity. During the impact loading stage, the fibers play a crucial role in bridging the cracks. Once cracks occur, the fibers span across the crack surface, providing continuous tensile resistance, forcing the cracks to bend and branch, converting the impact energy into processes such as fiber pull-out, detachment, and fracture, achieving a slow release and dissipation of energy, rather than the instantaneous disintegration of ordinary concrete. This “ductile dissipation” mechanism enables PVA fiber concrete to maintain a high energy absorption and dissipation capacity even under high-cycle and high dynamic loads in hydraulic engineering environments, significantly enhancing the impact resistance, fatigue resistance and safe service performance of the dam concrete.

Under an impact pressure of 0.3 MPa and with different numbers of dry-wet cycles (0, 30, 60, 90, 120 times), the fracture and fragmentation patterns of the two types of concrete show significant differences. Their failure mechanisms are closely related to the material’s own structural characteristics and the accumulation pattern of damage. The failure of plain concrete exhibits the typical brittle cumulative disintegration characteristic. During the 0th cycle, the specimen experienced an overall fracture along the main crack after being impacted. The fracture surface remained relatively intact, with only a small amount of large fragments formed. This indicates that in the initial state, the matrix structure was dense and the cracks rapidly propagated along a single path. As the number of dry-wet cycles increases, the degree of specimen fragmentation continues to intensify:After 30 to 60 cycles, the number of fragments increased while the particle size decreased. A large amount of angular block-like debris appeared, indicating that the repeated expansion and contraction stress caused by the dry-wet cycles had led to deterioration of the interface between the aggregate and the paste. Internal microcracks continuously emerged, expanded, and interconnected, gradually causing the loss of the integrity of the matrix. After 90 to 120 cycles, the specimen completely broke into a mixture of unevenly sized chunks and powders, exhibiting a sudden fragmentation and disintegration, with no obvious overall structural integrity. The core mechanism lies in the fact that the plain concrete has no fiber reinforcement to provide constraints. The internal damage caused by dry-wet cycles accumulates continuously, resulting in an increase in the matrix porosity and a decline in the bonding strength. Under the impact load, the cracks instantly lose stability and penetrate, concentrating and releasing energy, ultimately leading to a typical brittle failure. Its impact resistance and fatigue resistance are extremely poor.

The failure of PVA fiber reinforced concrete exhibits a ductile dissipative progressive failure characteristic, and its fragmentation form is much better than that of plain concrete. During the 0th cycle, although the specimen did break, the fragments were in a large block form and the fracture surface was relatively intact. There was no powdering phenomenon. The bridging effect of the fibers effectively inhibited the instantaneous opening of the cracks. After 30 to 60 cycles, the specimen broke into a blocky aggregate. The fragments were still connected by fibers, and did not completely disintegrate. This indicates that the fibers effectively dispersed the alternating stresses generated by the dry-wet cycles, thereby delaying the accumulation of damage. After 90 to 120 cycles, the specimen still maintained a relatively intact cluster structure. The fragment size was uniform, the edges were rough, and there was no large amount of powder-like debris, demonstrating excellent resistance to fragmentation. The mechanism of its enhancement lies in that the PVA fibers form a three-dimensional spatial network framework within the concrete. On one hand, this framework restrains the dry-wet expansion and contraction deformation of the matrix, inhibits the initiation and propagation of microcracks, and reduces the rate of damage accumulation. On the other hand, under the action of impact loads, the fibers transfer stress through the crack bridging effect, prevent crack贯通, force the cracks to bend and branch, convert the impact energy into the energy-consuming process of fiber extraction and detachment, avoid the instantaneous release of energy, and transform the failure mode from brittle disintegration to ductile progressive failure. Meanwhile, the excellent water resistance and adhesion properties of PVA fibers further enhance the impermeability and structural stability of the matrix, effectively alleviating the erosion of concrete caused by dry-wet cycles. This enables the specimens to maintain high structural integrity under long-term cyclic and dynamic loading coupling, significantly improving the impact durability performance of hydraulic concrete.

Conclusion

The combined effect of dry-wet cycling and dynamic impact loads can cause significant cumulative deterioration damage to concrete. As the number of dry-wet cycles increased to 120 times, the damage degree of plain concrete and PVA fiber concrete continued to increase, and the peak stress, toughness, and energy dissipation performance all showed a decaying trend. An increase in the impact strain rate can significantly enhance the dynamic mechanical properties and energy absorption capacity of concrete. Plain concrete has a relatively weak ability to resist multi-field coupling deterioration. After 120 dry-wet cycles, the maximum reduction in its energy dissipation density can reach 80%, and the toughness decreases by more than 46% under low-pressure conditions. Under high damage conditions, the brittle failure characteristics are prominent, and the overall mechanical stability and impact resistance performance significantly deteriorate.PVA fibers can effectively inhibit the development of concrete damage through the synergistic effects of three-dimensional mesh support, crack bridging and ductile energy dissipation. Under the same coupling conditions, the PVA fiber concrete has a significant improvement in performance compared to plain concrete. After 120 dry-wet cycles, the damage degree decreased by 41.8%, the initial toughness increased by more than 30%, the toughness attenuation amplitude decreased by more than 50%, and the energy absorption capacity in the extreme condition increased by nearly twice. This can effectively improve the brittle disintegration problem of concrete under dynamic loading, changing the failure mode to a stable and gradual ductile failure, significantly enhancing the impact resistance and durability of concrete in the coupled environment of dry-wet and dynamic loading.PVA fiber concrete is highly compatible with the complex service environment of hydraulic structures, and has remarkable engineering application value. PVA fibers can effectively restrain the dry and wet deformation of the matrix, inhibit the propagation of microcracks, and alleviate the stress concentration caused by dynamic loads. As a result, under the combined action of long-term dry-wet alternation and repeated impacts, concrete can maintain a lower damage rate and better mechanical and energy dissipation stability. This research can provide reliable experimental basis and theoretical support for the selection of high-performance concrete for dry-wet and dynamic-load-intensive water conservancy projects, as well as for the optimization of structural durability.
