Technically Natural Suppression of Fifth Force
Kensuke Homma, Taishi Katsuragawa, Shinya Matsuzaki

Abstract
Light scalars generically mediate a fifth force incompatible with local tests of gravity unless their couplings are parametrically suppressed or screening mechanisms are introduced. We demonstrate that such suppression can arise from symmetry. We propose a Z 2 Z_{2} -symmetric mirror extension of the Standard Model within a bi-conformal gravity construction, where spontaneous breaking of scale invariance produces a light scalaron as a pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson. This scalaron couples to the difference of trace anomalies between the Standard Model and mirror sectors. We find a parameter-independent correlation between the fifth-force strength α \alpha and the scalaron mass m σ m_{\sigma} , with the proportionality set by QCD observables and the electroweak scale. The Standard Model predicts α ∼ 10 − 4 \alpha\sim 10^{-4} at meter scales for m σ ∼ 10 − 7 m_{\sigma}\sim 10^{-7} eV, which is directly in the target window of next-generation experiments. In contrast to environmental screening mechanisms, this suppression mechanism follows directly from symmetry rather than nonlinear scalar dynamics.



I
Introduction

Light scalar fields coupled to matter arise in many contexts beyond the Standard Model (SM) and general relativity.
They appear as dark energy candidates (dilatons, scalars in modified gravity),
light dark matter (dilatons, axionlike particles), and moduli in string theory. A generic prediction of such fields is a long-range “fifth force” mediated by the scalar, which has experimentally been constrained to be extremely weak
2
.

The pioneering work of Fujii
10
;
11
proposed a light scalaron, and its mass was shown to be generated radiatively via the conformal anomaly. However, in the original model, the scalaron is universally coupled to the trace of the energy-momentum tensor in the SM, which inevitably leads to a fifth force at orders of magnitude above experimental bounds.

Phenomenological screening mechanisms (chameleon, symmetron, Vainshtein) offer possible escapes, which typically rely on environmental dependencies or nonlinear dynamics rather than on fundamental particle physics principles.
Moreover, these mechanisms often require specific forms of the scalar potential or fine-tuned parameters.

In this Letter, we present a fundamentally different approach: a new screening mechanism rooted in symmetry, anomaly structure, and vacuum alignment, which is technically natural
17
;
16
. We extend Fujii’s original framework by introducing a
Z_{2}
-symmetric mirror QCD sector embedded in a bi-conformal gravity construction. The scalaron
\sigma
couples to the
difference
of the trace anomalies of the QCD and mirror QCD sectors. In the
Z_{2}
-symmetric limit,
\sigma
is an exactly massless Nambu-Goldstone boson at the vacuum
\sigma=0
. In this limit, the
Z_{2}
symmetry forces the nucleon to couple equally to both sectors, canceling the dominant linear fifth force. When
Z_{2}
breaking is included predominantly from the quark mass differences between the two sectors, QCD and mirror QCD confinement stabilize the scalaron
\sigma
at a nontrivial vacuum, where
\sigma
acquires the mass
m_{\sigma}\sim 10^{-7}
eV via a finite two-loop mechanism akin to Fujii’s original framework.
The residual force is thus determined by the well-measured QCD sigma terms. For the SM benchmark, the mechanism predicts a force strength
\alpha\sim 10^{-4}
at meter scales, precisely in the narrow window between current constraints and next-generation experimental sensitivity.

II
Bi-conformal Mirror model

We extend the SM by introducing two real scalar fields
\phi
and
\phi_{D}
and a mirror (“dark”) QCD sector with gauge group
SU(3)_{D}
and three light quark flavors
u_{D},d_{D},s_{D}
. The theory respects a
Z_{2}
symmetry under which
\phi\leftrightarrow\phi_{D}
, SM quarks
q_{L,R}\leftrightarrow
mirror quarks
q_{DL,R}
, and QCD and mirror gluons
G_{\mu}^{a}\leftrightarrow G_{D\mu}^{a}
. The SM Higgs field
\Phi
is
Z_{2}
-even.

The
Z_{2}
symmetry ensures that at high energies, the SM and mirror QCD sectors are identical, with equal gauge couplings
g_{s}(\mu)=g_{sD}(\mu)
at some ultraviolet (UV) scale. The sole explicit breaking of the
Z_{2}
symmetry originates from the electroweak Higgs-induced masses of the SM quarks. The mirror quarks, on the other hand, are assumed to remain (almost) massless, which can keep the mirror pion masses well below those of SM pions (see Appendix
E
).

The choice of a minimal
SU(3)_{D}
sector with three light flavors (
u_{D},d_{D},s_{D}
) is the most predictive construction. Three flavors provide the necessary non-perturbative structures—a trace anomaly and chiral symmetry breaking—while mirroring QCD’s successful low-energy dynamics. Fewer flavors would lack the strange quark contribution essential for the quantitative prediction of the
Z_{2}
breaking; more (including a full mirror electroweak sector) introduce heavy states that decouple from the low-energy alignment mechanism without altering our core prediction (see Appendix
F
).

We embed the SM and mirror sectors in separate Jordan frames with the metrics
g
and
g_{D}

by a bi-conformal construction.
Based on the scale invariance and the
Z_{2}
symmetry defined above, the model Lagrangian terms thus take the form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{J}^{\text{SM,$\phi$}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-g}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\xi\phi^{2}R(g)+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}

\displaystyle+\xi_{h}|\Phi|^{2}R(g)+|D_{\mu}\Phi|^{2}-\lambda_{h}|\Phi|^{4}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}_{\text{SM rest}}\Big],

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{J}^{\text{Mirror,$\phi_{D}$}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-g_{D}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\xi\phi_{D}^{2}R_{D}(g_{D})+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{D})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{L}_{\text{Mirror QCD}}\Big],

(1)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SM rest}}
includes the SM sector terms other than the Higgs sector, and
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Mirror QCD}}
contains the
SU(3)_{D}
gauge sector with three light quark flavors. The scale-invariant nonminimal couplings to Ricci scalars
R
and
R_{D}
have been introduced, including the term for the SM Higgs, just for completeness, the physics of which is presently not what we are concerned with.

The scale invariance is spontaneously broken by the
Z_{2}
-symmetric vacuum expectation values (VEVs)

\langle\phi\rangle=\langle\phi_{D}\rangle=M_{P}/\sqrt{\xi}
, generating the Planck scale.
These VEVs generate the Planck mass via the non-minimal couplings.
The
Z_{2}
symmetry permits a Higgs portal coupling
\lambda_{\text{mix}}|\Phi|^{2}(\phi^{2}+\phi_{D}^{2})
. We set
\lambda_{\text{mix}}=0
in what follows. This choice is technically natural: setting
\lambda_{\text{mix}}=0
enhances the symmetry of the classical action, and the
Z_{2}
symmetry forbids radiative generation of this coupling. Even if a small
\lambda_{\text{mix}}
were present, its effect on the scalaron mass is negligible. A four-loop estimate yields
m_{\phi,\phi_{D}}^{2}|^{\text{loop}}\sim(10^{-9}\text{ eV})^{2}
, far below our target scale
m_{\sigma}\sim 10^{-7}
eV. The origin of the Higgs VEV
v_{\text{EW}}=246
GeV is orthogonal to our mechanism; we simply take it as an input.

The two Jordan frames are mapped to a common Einstein frame metric
g_{\mu\nu}^{E}
via separate Weyl transformations. Ignoring the
\xi_{h}
term for the SM Higgs, for simplicity, the transformations go like

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}^{E}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu},\quad\Omega^{2}=\frac{\xi\phi^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}},

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}^{E}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{D}^{2}g_{D\mu\nu},\quad\Omega_{D}^{2}=\frac{\xi\phi_{D}^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}}.

(2)

The bi-conformal constraint
\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu}=\Omega_{D}^{2}g_{D\mu\nu}
relates the Jordan-frame metrics, which ensures that both sectors describe the same physical spacetime.
Consequently,
g_{D\mu\nu}
is not an independent gravitational degree of freedom, but is defined via the bi-conformal constraint. This constraint ensures that both sectors share a common Einstein-frame metric
g_{\mu\nu}^{E}
while preserving independent Weyl scalings,
which reduces the physical metric degrees of freedom to the standard two (Einstein-frame metric and the lightest scalaron fluctuation, dubbed
\sigma_{-}
, where the heavier one is decoupled at the Planck scale, as clarified right below).

The
\phi
and
\phi_{D}
fields are decomposed into
Z_{2}
-even and odd eigenstates
\sigma_{+}=(\phi+\phi_{D})/\sqrt{2}
and
\sigma_{-}=(\phi-\phi_{D})/\sqrt{2}
. The
\sigma_{-}
then yields null VEV,
\langle\sigma_{-}\rangle=0
, protected by the
Z_{2}
symmetry and scale invariance, hence acts as a Nambu-Goldstone boson field as a consequence of the bi-conformal construction, while
\sigma_{+}
is allowed to develop the VEV
\langle\sigma_{+}\rangle=\sqrt{2}M_{P}/\sqrt{\xi}
.
Generating and stabilizing this VEV requires a potential — or equivalent UV mechanism — . The minimal assumption is a mass term
V(\sigma_{+})=\frac{1}{2}m_{+}^{2}\sigma_{+}^{2}
with
m_{+}\sim M_{P}
, which is technically natural (no symmetry protects
m_{+}
).

In contrast,
\sigma_{-}
remains massless at the classical level. The
Z_{2}
symmetry and the bi-conformal construction force the potential for
\sigma
to be exactly flat. Radiative corrections will generate a potential, but these arise only at two-loop order due to the
\epsilon
factor in the anomaly coupling (see Sec.
B
). The resulting
\sigma
mass is therefore determined entirely by a finite two-loop mechanism described in Sec.
IV
, not by unknown UV physics.

Thus, low-energy physics is governed by the
Z_{2}
-odd counterpart
\sigma_{-}
, which we shall rename
\sigma\equiv\sigma_{-}
.
Crucially, the low-energy physics of
\sigma
, including the fifth-force prediction, is completely insensitive to
m_{+}
and the detailed form of
V(\sigma_{+})
, as these degrees of freedom decouple below
M_{P}
.

III
Particle Physics Screening via Vacuum Alignment

The bi-conformal geometry leads to a universal coupling of
\sigma
to matter. Under a local scale transformation with parameter
\alpha(x)
, the
Z_{2}
symmetry dictates opposite transformations for the two sectors: the SM Jordan frame metric
g
and fields transform with
\alpha(x)
, while the mirror sector transforms with
-\alpha(x)
. After transforming to the Einstein frame, this yields the scale anomaly induced couplings to matter (see Appendix
A
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\sigma T_{\mu}^{\mu}}=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2}f_{\Sigma}}\left[T^{\mu}_{\mu}(\text{SM})-T^{\mu}_{\mu}(D)\right]\,,

(3)

where
f_{\Sigma}=\sqrt{6+1/\xi}~M_{P}
is the scalaron decay constant, which is determined by performing the canonical normalization of
\sigma
(See the Appendix
A
).
This is an operator relation valid at all scales, independent of any dynamics.
Thus,
\sigma
couples to the
difference
of two traces of energy momentum tensors,
T_{\mu}^{\mu}({\rm SM})
and
T_{\mu}^{\mu}(D)
.

The operator
T^{\mu}_{\mu}
in Eq. (
3
) generically receives contributions from both perturbative and non-perturbative physics. The perturbative part, including the Higgs condensate contribution
\sim m_{h}^{2}v_{\text{EW}}^{2}
and short-distance gauge boson loops, is subtracted in the definition of the physical low-energy vacuum. This is the standard procedure in QCD sum rules and the operator product expansion: the physical condensates are defined as the difference between the full non-perturbative expectation value and the perturbative one. Consequently, only the non-perturbative parts arising from confinement survive in the low-energy effective theory relevant to the present-day
\sigma
.

Thus, the relevant quantities for the
\sigma
potential are the non-perturbative condensates:

\displaystyle\langle T^{\mu}_{\mu}(\text{QCD})\rangle_{\text{NP}}\sim\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^{4},\quad\langle T^{\mu}_{\mu}(D)\rangle_{\text{NP}}\sim\Lambda_{D}^{4}.

(4)

Under a constant shift
\sigma\to\sigma+c
, the confinement scales transform oppositely due to the signs in Eq. (
3
):
\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}\to\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}e^{-c/(\sqrt{2}f_{\Sigma})}
,
\Lambda_{D}\to\Lambda_{D}e^{+c/(\sqrt{2}f_{\Sigma})}
. Since the non-perturbative vacuum energy density of each sector scales as the fourth power of its confinement scale, as in Eq.(
4
), the total effective potential generated by confinement thus goes like

\displaystyle V_{\text{align}}(\sigma)=\frac{\kappa}{4}\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^{4}e^{-2\sqrt{2}\sigma/f_{\Sigma}}+\frac{\kappa}{4}\Lambda_{D}^{4}e^{+2\sqrt{2}\sigma/f_{\Sigma}},

(5)

where
\kappa
is a coefficient determined by non-perturbative QCD dynamics with a dilatonic background. (When we simply refer to the vacuum energy scale of QCD
\sim(1\,{\rm GeV})^{4}
together with
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\sim 0.3
GeV, the size of
\kappa
is expected to be of
{\cal O}(10)
.)
This potential, violating the scale invariance, deforms the original flat direction to induce
vacuum alignment
.
There, the scalaron VEV
\langle\sigma\rangle
develops at the nontrivial vacuum generated by the
Z_{2}
breaking difference of
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
and
\Lambda_{D}
as

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\sigma\rangle}{\sqrt{2}f_{\Sigma}}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}}{\Lambda_{D}}\right).

(6)

The
Z_{2}
symmetry is approximately realized when the two QCD sectors have identical light quark features. In particular, in the massless quark limit, we have
\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}=\Lambda_{D}
and
\langle\sigma\rangle=0
.
In this limit, the nucleon mass
m_{N}(\sigma)
is an even function of
\sigma
because the theory, hence the Hamiltonian, reflects the
Z_{2}
invariance. Consequently, its derivative vanishes at the minimum:

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial m_{N}(\sigma)}{\partial(\sigma/\sqrt{2}f_{\Sigma})}\right|_{\sigma=0}=0.

(7)

By the Feynman-Hellmann theorem, this derivative is related to
the matrix element of the difference of
T_{\mu}^{\mu}
sandwiched by the nucleon state
|N\rangle
(see the Appendix
C
), so we have

\displaystyle\langle N|T^{\mu}_{\mu}(\text{QCD})-T^{\mu}_{\mu}(D)|N\rangle=0.

(8)

Thus, the linear coupling of
\sigma
to nucleons, which is proportional to this difference, vanishes identically. This is the origin of the fifth-force cancellation.

When
Z_{2}
breaking from the quark masses is included, the confinement scales differ (
\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}\neq\Lambda_{D}
) and the scalaron acquires a nonzero VEV given by Eq. (
6
). We then see that using Eq. (
6
), the physical confinement scales in both sectors become equal to the geometric mean,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}e^{-\langle\sigma\rangle/(\sqrt{2}f_{\Sigma})}=\Lambda_{D}e^{+\langle\sigma\rangle/(\sqrt{2}f_{\Sigma})}=\sqrt{\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}\Lambda_{D}}.

(9)

Noting that the nucleon mass dominantly arises from gluonic contributions,
m_{N}\approx m_{G}
(see also the Appendix
C
), which almost saturates the vacuum energy as above, we find that the nucleon mass scales as
m_{N}\propto\sqrt{\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}\Lambda_{D}}
. The
Z_{2}
breaking from the quark masses then generates a small difference between the matrix elements, proportional to the QCD sigma terms
\sigma_{\pi N}+\sigma_{s}
, which determines the residual fifth-force strength as derived in the Appendix
C
.
The standard description of nucleon properties by SM QCD remains intact; only the
response
to the QCD vacuum energy
is modified by the mirror sector through the alignment mechanism, hence no modification of hadron wavefunctions or deviation in hadron experiments occurs.

IV
The scalaron mass generation

After the vacuum alignment described in Sec.
III
, the field
\sigma
sits at its minimum
\langle\sigma\rangle
determined by Eq. (
6
). In this true vacuum, the SM particles have their usual masses from electroweak symmetry breaking, and the mirror QCD sector has its confinement scale
\Lambda_{D}
. When expanded around the true vacuum, any particle that acquires mass through scale symmetry breaking generically contributes to
m_{\sigma}^{2}
. As is clarified in detail in the Appendix
B
, the dominant contributions arise at the two-loop level analogously to the Fujii original proposal
10
;
11
, which is finite and takes the following universal form:

\displaystyle m_{\sigma}^{2}=\frac{{\cal C}}{(16\pi^{2})^{2}}\frac{{\cal M}^{4}}{f_{\Sigma}^{2}}.

(10)

{\cal M}
is the characteristic mass scale of particles in the loop, and
\mathcal{C}
aggregates all group theory factors and coupling constants. The factor
(16\pi^{2})^{-2}
is the characteristic two-loop suppression.

In the SM, the dominant contributions come from the electroweak scale, where the top quark provides the largest effect due to its large Yukawa and QCD couplings, which yield
{\cal M}=m_{t}
and
{\cal C}\approx 160
(see Appendix
B
for a parametric estimate). The relevant two-loop contributions thus feed the physical scalaron mass as

\displaystyle m_{\sigma}|_{\rm SM}\approx\frac{9.9\times 10^{-7}\text{ eV}}{\sqrt{6+1/\xi}}.

(11)

V
Fifth-Force Suppression

The
Z_{2}
symmetry between the SM and mirror sectors is broken by quark masses. In the SM,

,
d
, and

m_{u}\sim 2.5
MeV,

\displaystyle\langle N|T^{\mu}_{\mu}(\text{QCD})-T^{\mu}_{\mu}(D)|N\rangle=\sigma_{\pi N}+\sigma_{s}.

(12)

The mirror quarks are taken to be sufficiently light (

\sigma_{\pi N}
and
\sigma_{s}

From Eq. (
3

\sigma

\displaystyle g_{\sigma NN}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}f_{\Sigma}}\left(\langle N|T^{\mu}_{\mu}(\text{QCD})|N\rangle-\langle N|T^{\mu}_{\mu}(D)|N\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{\pi N}+\sigma_{s}}{\sqrt{2}f_{\Sigma}}.

(13)

The fifth-force strength
\alpha

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{g_{\sigma NN}^{2}}{4\pi G_{N}m_{N}^{2}}=\frac{(\sigma_{\pi N}+\sigma_{s})^{2}}{m_{N}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{6+1/\xi},

(14)

where we have used
G_{N}=1/(8\pi M_{P}^{2})

f_{\Sigma}=\sqrt{6+1/\xi}\,M_{P}

With
\sigma_{\pi N}+\sigma_{s}=65-95

\displaystyle\alpha\approx(4.8-10.2)\times 10^{-3}\times\left(\frac{1}{6+1/\xi}\right).

(15)

A key prediction of the framework is a parameter-independent correlation between the scalaron mass and the fifth-force strength. From Eqs. (

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{(\sigma_{\pi N}+\sigma_{s})^{2}}{m_{N}^{2}}\cdot\frac{(16\pi^{2})^{2}}{\mathcal{C}}\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{{\cal M}^{4}}\,m_{\sigma}^{2}.

(16)

This correlation is independent of the non-minimal coupling
\xi

In the SM benchmark with
M_{\text{heavy}}=m_{t}\approx 173

\mathcal{C}\approx 160

we have

\displaystyle\alpha|_{\rm SM}\approx(4.8-10.2)\times 10^{-3}\times\left(\frac{m_{\sigma}|_{\rm SM}}{9.9\times 10^{-7}\text{ eV}}\right)^{2},

VII
Conclusion and Outlook

We have presented a novel, technically natural screening mechanism for fifth forces mediated by light scalars. The proposed mechanism demonstrates that fundamental principles of particle physics, such as symmetry, anomaly structure, and vacuum alignment, can provide natural solutions to the fifth-force problem without resorting to environmental screening or fine-tuning. This establishes a new ballpark for connecting particle physics to gravitational phenomenology, with testable predictions that will be probed by upcoming experiments across multiple frontiers. The scaling formula
\alpha\times(m_{\sigma}{\cal M}/M^{2}_{P})^{2}\sim\text{constant}
predicts
m_{\sigma}\sim 10^{-7}
eV and
\alpha\sim 10^{-4}
for the SM. This places the prediction directly in the target range of next-generation fifth-force and stimulated photon-photon collider experiments.

The present work also opens up several new directions. The screening mechanism applies to any light scalar with Planck-suppressed couplings to matter. It provides a natural solution to the fifth-force problem for light dark-matter candidates (axion-like particles) and for moduli fields in string theory compactifications. The mechanism can be generalized to other gauge groups and matter content.

While the proposed model offers a natural solution to the fifth-force problem, the ultimate origin of the Planck-scale VEVs and the full cosmological history of the scalaron are subjects for future work. Crucially, these UV and cosmological details do not affect the robust, testable low-energy prediction for fifth-force strength.

Appendix A
Derivation of the Anomaly Coupling

We provide a derivation of the scalaron coupling to the difference of QCD nonperturbative trace anomalies using the geometric properties of the bi-conformal construction and the exact quantum definition of the energy-momentum tensor.

We begin with the Jordan-frame actions for the relevant parts in SM and mirror sectors:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{J}^{\text{SM}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}\xi\phi^{2}R(g)+\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{SM matter}}(g,\psi)\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{J}^{\text{Mirror}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-g_{D}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\xi\phi_{D}^{2}R_{D}(g_{D})+\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi_{D})^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{Mirror}}(g_{D},\psi_{D})\right].

(21)

The two Jordan-frame metrics are related by the bi-conformal constraint
\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu}=\Omega_{D}^{2}g_{D\mu\nu}
, where the Weyl factors are defined as

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}=\frac{\xi\phi^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}},\qquad\Omega_{D}^{2}=\frac{\xi\phi_{D}^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}}.

(22)

We transform both sectors to a common Einstein-frame metric
g_{\mu\nu}^{E}
via:

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}^{E}=\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu}=\Omega_{D}^{2}g_{D\mu\nu}.

(23)

For any matter action
S_{\text{matter}}[g,\psi]
coupled to a metric

We stress that the bound on
\dot{\mu}/\mu

Appendix E
Mirror Pions

The mirror quark masses
m_{q_{D}}
are assumed to be sufficiently small (
m_{q_{D}}\ll\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
) that their sigma terms, such as mirror counter parts of
\sigma_{\pi N}
and
\sigma_{s}
are negligible. This is consistent with the scale-invariant UV structure we assume. Here we may have one possible scenario to provide the origin of the mass
m_{q_{D}}
: It has been suggested that asymptotically safe quantum gravity with a Gaussian IR fixed point can generate small fermion masses via chiral symmetry breaking
9
;
7
;
8
. If such a mechanism operates in the mirror sector, it would naturally produce
m_{q_{D}}\ll\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
while preserving the classical scale invariance at high energies. However, our fifth-force prediction does not rely on this specific UV completion; any mechanism that keeps mirror quarks light suffices.

At any rate, the mirror pions
\pi_{D}
are the pseudo Nambu-Goldstone bosons of spontaneously broken mirror chiral symmetry, forming the mirror isospin
SU(2)
triplet. Their mass follows from the Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation:

\displaystyle m_{\pi_{D}}^{2}=\frac{(m_{u_{D}}+m_{d_{D}})\langle\bar{q}_{D}q_{D}\rangle}{f_{\pi_{D}}^{2}},

(60)

where
\langle\bar{q}_{D}q_{D}\rangle\sim-\Lambda_{D}^{3}
is the mirror chiral condensate and
f_{\pi_{D}}\sim\Lambda_{D}
is the mirror pion decay constant. Thus, The mirror pion mass
m_{\pi_{D}}^{2}\sim m_{q_{D}}\Lambda_{D}
. For
m_{q_{D}}\ll\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
, they are light. They are not thermally coupled to the SM and can evade cosmological constraints if the mirror sector is never reheated. A detailed analysis is beyond the scope of this Letter.

Since the mirror QCD sector is completely SM-singlet and parity-invariant in the present setup, the mirror pions cannot decay into anything. They are not thermally coupled to the SM plasma due to the extreme suppression of the
\sigma
-mediated interaction (
\theta_{\sigma-G}\sim 1\,{\rm GeV}^{2}/M_{P}^{2}\sim 10^{-36}
), so they evade direct thermal constraints from Big Bang Nucleosynthesis or the cosmic microwave background. Their cosmological abundance depends on the initial conditions set by inflation and reheating. A minimal scenario is that the mirror sector is never reheated to significant temperatures, leaving it with negligible energy density today. Having a tiny coupling, hence a tiny cross section to the SM sector as above, mirror pions would yield a negligibly small contribution to the effective number of neutrinos,
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
, as well.

Alternatively, if mirror pions are sufficiently heavy (
\gtrsim\text{MeV}
), they could constitute a subdominant cold dark matter component. A detailed cosmological analysis of the mirror sector’s thermal history is beyond the scope of this Letter, but the parameter space exists where all observational constraints are satisfied. Notably, the fifth-force prediction in Eq. (
53
) is independent of these details, relying only on the condition that mirror quark masses are sufficiently small to render their sigma terms (such as a mirror version of
\sigma_{\pi N}
and
\sigma_{s}
) negligible.

Appendix F
Generality of the Mirror Sector Construction

The choice of a minimal
SU(3)_{D}
sector with three light flavors (
u_{D},d_{D},s_{D}
) is the most predictive construction. Three flavors provide the necessary non-perturbative structures, namely the trace anomaly and chiral symmetry breaking, while mirroring the successful low-energy dynamics of QCD. A two-flavor mirror sector would lack the strange quark contribution
\sigma_{s}
. The resulting fifth-force prediction would then depend on the difference in gluon condensates between
N_{f}=3
and
N_{f}=2
QCD, a quantity that is not independently known with comparable precision, thereby reducing the predictive power of the mechanism.

Even if one were to extend the model to include a full mirror SM with heavy quarks, as long as other mirror particles are heavier than the scale of
\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
, the present fifth-force suppression works as it is. Such extensions merely introduce additional free parameters and are not required for the screening mechanism.

The minimal three-flavor mirror sector is therefore the most predictive and natural implementation, with the
Z_{2}
breaking dominated by the quark mass differences relevant to the nucleon matrix element, while maintaining the vacuum alignment.
