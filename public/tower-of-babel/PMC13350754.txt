Non-invasive estimation of hemoglobin using machine learning algorithms from i-PPG and PPG signals
Authors: A S Kaviya Dharshini, J B Jeeva
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSignificant research works have been carried out in the area of non-invasive hemoglobin estimation in recent times. The current study compares the estimation of hemoglobin from two kinds of photoplethysmogram (PPG) signals with the results obtained from the clinical laboratory tests. They are t-PPG and i-PPG signals. The former is collected using a device that utilizes the MAX30102 sensor, and the latter is collected using a smartphone. For this purpose, twenty-one volunteers aged between 20 and 54 years participated in this study. The data was collected twice on the same day with 2 hours interval. Among the volunteers five of them provided their data once. The PPG signals collected were filtered and processed to remove the noise. The features based on optical attenuation were extracted from the signals. These along with the demographic information were fed into the machine learning algorithms. The Hb values were estimated using decision trees, boosted trees, bootstrap, NTanH and XGBoost. The XGBoost model outperformed the others. The results were obtained with the R-squared values of 0.9862, 0.9812 and RMSE of 0.0489 g/dL, 0.0329 g/dL for t-PPG signals and i-PPG, respectively.Keywords: PPG, I-PPG, Hemoglobin, ML models, XGBoost, Bland altman analysisSubject terms: Biomarkers, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Health care, Medical research

Introduction

Hemoglobin (Hb) is an iron-rich globular protein present in red blood cells. The main function of Hb is to carry oxygen from the lungs to cells. Hb consists of four globin molecules, making it a tetramer. Each of those protein chains has the iron component called the heme group, which binds oxygen and is also responsible for the blood’s red color. Since it is a tetramer, each Hb molecule can carry four oxygen molecules. Along with oxygen, nitric oxide is also released to the cells. Other than that, it transports carbon dioxide from the cells to the lungs and during the process, the blood appears dark red in color1. The hemoglobin is measured in grams per deciliter, and the standard Hb values for different genders2 are shown in the Table 1.

The condition of having low hemoglobin is called anemia. It happens due to malnutrition, chronic diseases and blood loss. The persons with Hb values slightly lower than the standards are called mildly anemic, and they experience fatigue, pale skin, cold hands and feet. Anemic adults may also experience reduced working capacity. It severely affects the infants, children, adolescents, and pregnant women. The anemic conditions in infancy cause impairment in cognitive and motor development, affecting the ability to concentrate, learn and move. The females in the reproductive age are prone to anemia due to blood loss during menstruation and childbirth, it also affects their physical and mental health. Severe anemic conditions may even lead to mortality3,4.

According to the survey primarily conducted by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) of India between 2015-2016 (NFHS-4) and between 2019-2021(NFHS-5), more than of the women and children were anemic5. The prevalence of anemia is higher in rural areas compared to urban areas. Since anemia is a significant problem in India, the government introduced a program called “Anemia Mukt Bharat”(AMB) in 2018. This program conducts T3 camps in government institutions that concentrate on three aspects: test, treat and talk (spreading awareness) to reduce anemia among the people. For the testing aspect, digital hemoglobin meters were used6.

The standard test for hemoglobin is invasive1. The invasive and minimally invasive methods are more accurate but they are not user-friendly for frequent testing, and a trained professional is needed to acquire the samples. The need of equipment, reagents, and skilled personnel directly affects the cost of Hb test, making it costlier, especially in the rural settings. Several non-invasive methods are also available to calculate Hb, which are presently at the research level. They typically involve a sensor to acquire a signal, either in one or two dimensions, such as photoplethysmogram (PPG) signals7–11, Palpebral Conjunctiva12,13, palm pallor14,15, nail pallor16 images respectively. Whenever the peripheral tissue is illuminated by an optical radiation, some part of it will be absorbed and the remaining will be scattered. The technique of measuring the attenuated light in the form of voltage is called photoplethysmography. The PPG signals are acquired at different body sites based on the applications, such as fingers, forehead, and wrists. Then the features are extracted from the signal and fed into an analysis system that consists of a mathematical17 or a machine learning/deep learning model to obtain the results. The extracted features are based on the statistical18, demographic, time-domain19, velocity, frequency and optical attenuation10,20 of the signal. Few commercially marketed equipment are available. Though they are not invasive, they are either expensive, have portability issues or are complex in working21. Some of the significant methods proposed by the researchers for non-invasive Hb estimation are discussed in this section.

A dual LED approach with DCM03 was developed by Kavsaoğlu et al to obtain PPG signals from 33 people for the non-invasive estimation of hemoglobin in 2015. They extracted 40 features from the PPG, including the first and second derivatives and four more features (age, height, weight, gender). They employed different ML algorithms. They achieved a Mean Square Error (MSE) value of −0.0027 and R squared value of 0.9664 for the hybrid model using RELIEFF Feature Selection (RFS) and support vector regression (SVR) and also they recommend their device for clinical use19.

Later in 2020, a multi-wavelength spectroscopic device was used by Soumyadipta Acharya et al. to predict Hb values non-invasively using PPG signals acquired at four wavelengths from 1583 women of childbearing age. The extracted features were fed into a two-layer stack regressor where the first layer consists of 4 ML algorithms that individually predicts the value and those predictions were fed into a SVR. They obtained a Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC) of 0.81. This study demonstrated the feasibility of using PPG signals and machine learning for maternal anemia detection20. Five different wavelengths were used by Caje Pinto et al. They developed a finger probe and acquired PPG signals from eight subjects to calculate Hb and obtained an R squared value of 0.96422.

In 2023, another four wavelength approach was used by Zhencheng Chen et al. They developed a data acquisition system using a DCM08 sensor and an NRF52840 chip. They collected PPG signals from 58 fingertips, extracted 106 features and analysed the performance with top 10, 20 and 30 features. They observed R squared value of 0.997, RMSE of 0.762 g/L, and MAE of 0.325 g/L for 30 features set with XGBoost model23.

8 LED setup was developed by Jianming Zhu collected PPG signals from 56 participants. The features were extracted using sliding window’s method and fed into the AdaBoost and random forest models. They achieved a correlation coefficient of 0.9124. Another 8 LED approach was done by Fulai Peng et al. in 2024. They extracted 56 features. The features were reduced using recursive feature elimination algorithms and fed into ensembled independent extreme learning machine regression models. For 249 data points, they achieved RMSE of 1.72g/dL and PCC of 0.76 with reference values25.

The text below provides information about the literatures that reports smartphone based system which with or without external light sources. In 2016, Edward Jay Wang et al. presented a smartphone application called HemaAPP to estimate Hb concentration using a smartphone camera with various light sources where videos of the finger tip are recorded and converted into PPG signals. These are called image-based PPG signals or i-PPG signals. On comparing with the reference value, the HemaApp got a precision of and a sensitivity of . They also obtained FDA approval for their product. An external device was connected to the smartphone, which eliminates the need of large analyzers26. In 2017, his team presented another technique by utilizing the flashlight of the smartphone as the light source. This method achieved a PCC of 0.062 and RMSE value of 1.27 g/dL18.

In 2018, Md Kamrul Hasan et al. predicted Hb values using a 10-second fingertip video recorded from 75 adults with the help of a smartphone. The red, blue, green frames in the recorded videos were divided into 100 blocks, and they reduced the feature space by feeding only the selected blocks into the Artificial Neural Network (ANN) and observed a correlation value of 0.93 with the standard method. Another approach by the same author involves the RGB finger tip videos which were converted into different colour spaces, including HSV and LAB. This work achieved a correlation value of 0.95 using the partial least squares algorithm27.

Md. Rezwanul Haque et al. proposed a novel technique to measure hemoglobin, glucose and creatinine from PPG signals by using deep neural networks (DNN). The study involved the acquisition of PPG signals from 93 subjects with the help of a smartphone camera under an external light source setup made with 6 NIR LEDs operating at 850nm and a white LED at the centre. The 15-second i-PPG signals were fed into a DNN to predict the blood components. This approach obtained an R square value of 0.922 for Hb28.

In 2021, Aziz et al. developed a custom light source which included two white and six NIR LEDs. They acquired i-PPG signals of human finger illuminated by developed source and smartphone’s flashlight, from 167 subjects. They observed a MAPE value of 29.

Abhishek Kesarwani et al. developed a system that uses smartphone, external light source and a cuff to detect Hb values. They collected left hand videos of 131 subjects and observed the pallor with cuff being inflated and deflated. The images of pallor in different timestamps were analyzed. The time and demographic based features were extracted and fed into regression and multi-layer perceptron (MLP) models. They achieved a sensitivity of , MSE of 0.701 and RMSE of 0.69814.

In our earlier work, we developed a simple hardware setup with a photo diode and two LEDs to acquire fingertip PPG signals from 10 healthy volunteers. The Hb values were calculated with the help of an equation based on a mathematical model using the optical absorbance and extinction coefficients30. The discussed literature so far has made use of PPG signals acquired from sensors (t-PPG) or videos (i-PPG) to estimate Hb non-invasively. They either use complex features or complex models or external light sources with a smartphone for Hb estimation. This study is the first of its kind to evaluate and validate the performance of i-PPG signals acquired via a smartphone against traditional sensor-based photoplethysmography (t-PPG) signals in the estimation of non-invasive hemoglobin using machine learning algorithms. The research focused on two primary objectives: first, to decrease the complexity of the features and the ML models used; and second, to remove the necessity for an external light source in the smartphone-based methodology. The proposed system operates without an external light source and is insensitive to variations in skin color.

Materials and methods

The experimental setup and protocol used to acquire the t-PPG and i-PPG signals are explained in the following sections, along with data preprocessing, feature extraction, and the machine learning algorithms employed to estimate the hemoglobin values. The section ends with metrics used to evaluate the performance of the analysis.

Experimental setup

To acquire t-PPG signals, a hardware setup consisting of a MAX30102 sensor was developed. The sensor has two LEDs in the wavelengths of 660nm [Red], 880nm [Infrared], and a photo-detector whose response range is between 600nm and 900nm. It was connected to a microcontroller, and the whole unit measures about 5 cm x 6 cm x 2 cm. The hardware is programmed in a way to get 100 samples per second for each channel. To acquire i-PPG, a Samsung smartphone with quad camera setup was used. Videos of the index finger of the subjects were recorded by using the main rear camera with the resolution of 64MP (Mega Pixels). The smartphone camera operates at a rate of 30 frames per second. The recorded videos were converted into i-PPG signals by taking the average of each frame for each channel, resulting in three i-PPG signals per video for RGB channels31. Both the signals were recorded in the ambient light environment, and the Hb estimation system is independent of the external light sources.

Data collection

The ethical clearance for data collection was granted by the IECH (Institutional Ethical Committee for studies on Human subjects) of our institution. Twenty-one healthy subjects were involved in this study with 27.66 ± 8.45 years of age. The data collection commenced after obtaining a written consent from them. Readings were taken twice with two hours interval. Out of the twenty-one, sixteen of them gave the second set of readings. In total, thirty-seven sets of readings were taken.

Demographic features such as height, weight, age, gender, and vitals such as heart rate, blood pressure, temperature, and oxygen saturation values were noted using clinical instruments. Then, the blood samples were collected by a lab technician at the VIT health centre, and the Hb values of each subject were estimated. This test report is considered as the standard for hemoglobin estimation. After that, the subjects were told to place their right index finger on the sensing area of the hardware to acquire the t-PPG signals for three minutes. Finally, i-PPG signals were recorded for the same period of time during which the subjects placed their right index finger over the rear camera of the Samsung smartphone with the flash light switched on.

The authors confirm that all research procedures were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines/regulations obtained from the IECH and the Declaration of Helsinki. The informed consent forms were obtained from the subjects and the information such as subjects’name and other HIPAA identifiers were not used in any part of the manuscript.

Signal quality analysis

The quality of the PPG signals was analyzed with a functionality available in the open-source Python library neurokit2, specifically the PPG quality function35. This method correlates each pulse wave of a signal with its average pulse shape. It provides a score, where a value of 1 corresponds to the highest correlation and 0 corresponds to no correlation. As a result, the pulse waves of signals with less than correlation were omitted.

Feature extraction and machine learning algorithms

According to Beer Lambert’s Law, the absorbance of the light by a non-scattering clear medium is proportional to the concentration, molar extinction coefficient and the path length. But the biological systems are complex. The PPG Signals are pulsatile in nature and have two components, DC and AC due to pulsatile flow of blood. The former component is a steady component and the latter is not. Whenever the index finger is illuminated by a light source, a part of light gets absorbed, and a part gets scattered. The absorbance of light by the tissues and bones of the finger remains the same with time (a steady state), and can be calculated as the mean of the PPG signal. In contrast to that, the light absorbance of the blood changes from time to time, and can be calculated as standard deviation of the PPG signal. Due to their pulsatile nature, modified Beer-Lambert’s law is used. It states that, the change in absorbance of the light or optical attenuation is proportional to the change in concentration of the components (here it is hemoglobin), molar coefficients, effective path length and a constant related to the geometry and the scattering. Hb can be estimated once all the other terms are known. The change in optical attenuation is calculated as the ratio of AC and DC component of the signal. The complexity of finding the other terms can be reduced when two or more wavelengths of light source is used. When the log to the natural logarithm of the ratio of AC and DC of two wavelengths is available, a mathematical system can be developed to solve for the Hb concentration. Instead of that, the ratio of the optical attenuation of two different wavelengths can be calculated, and it is proportional to the concentration of the Hb and it is called the ratio of ratios. These features are obtained from the denoised augmented signals, and were be fed to ML algorithms to estimate Hb values. Decision trees

Decision trees are one of the fundamental machine learning algorithms. Here, the data is split into two or more smaller homogeneous nodes based on each feature. The process is repeated till a large tree-like structure is formed. Based on the ability of the model to predict the response variable from the validation dataset, the structure is pruned. Once a new node is formed, the total sum of squares is calculated by taking the summation of the square of the difference between the response value and the mean predicted value of that particular node. Then, the difference between the total sum of squares of the parent node and the child node is obtained. The node with the lesser difference value is retained, and the other nodes are pruned set by minimizing the variance within the resulting groups. This is achieved by removing the splits that reduce the model’s accuracy, thereby developing a generalized model.

Boosted tree

Boosted tree is an algorithm built on the core of decision trees; it uses the boosting technique. At first, small trees were formed with shallow depth, then a sequence of additive nodes that reduce the error of the previous tree was formed by calculating residuals between them. So, the model is improved sequentially. Here, the trees were scaled by a learning rate, and the growth is stopped when there is no improvement in the performance. The final response from the model is the summation of all trees from the initial stage till the last.

Bootstrap

Bootstrap aggregating is also built on decision trees. Unlike the sequential addition of the boosted tree, bootstrap creates multiple trees based on a sample dataset taken from the training data with replacement. Those trees are different and trained independently. It is a parallel process. The result of this model is the average value obtained from all the nodes.

Multi layer perceptron neural network – MLP

The core of MLP is penalized maximum likelihood, which means the algorithm finds the best model parameters for maximizing the probability of observing the data. It also provides penalty scores to the log-likelihood of the observed data. Thus, it maximizes the probability of the likelihood and also reduces the over-fitting. Here, N TanH function is used.

XGBoost

XGBoost is also a sequential ensemble technique with a decision tree at the core. As the name indicates, it is an advanced form of the boosted tree algorithm. And it stands out from other boosting models in reducing over-fitting, since it has built-in Lasso and ridge regularization.

Conclusion

In this study, non invasive estimation of Hb using smartphone-based i-PPG signals and t-PPG signals is reported. For this purpose data were collected from 37 volunteers and their Hb values determined from standard Laboratory test served as reference values. The augmented data were denoised and assessed for signal quality. Then the features were extracted based on modified Beer-Lambert’s Law and are referred to as optical attenuation features. All those features, along with the demography, were fed into five ML algorithms. The hyperparameters of those ML algorithms are set to default in the JMP platform, so that the structural compatibility of the network can be found. The evaluation metrics are used to find the best algorithm that suits the t-PPG and i-PPG signals.

It is evident that XGBoost performs well as per the evaluation metrics. The performance of the validation set is slightly lower than the training set and the performance of the test set is slightly lower than the validation set in most cases. This shows that both the validation and test sets contain unseen data. The proposed method with the XGBoost predictor have a deviation of −0.003 g/dL from the true Hb value, with limits of agreement of −0.168 g/dL and 0.163 g/dL when predicted from t-PPG signals. In case of i-PPG signals the proposed method with the same XGBoost would have a deviation of −0.01 g/dL and the limits of agreement −0.187 g/dL and 0.167 g/dL. The obtained results has better performance in terms of RMSE, R-Squared19,20,22,28, PCC18,24,27 and BAA values with lesser complexity compared to the recent studies23–25,38, and is comparable with the results obtained by song et al39. The Welch’s T-test between the accuracy of t-PPG AND i-PPG signals clearly shows there’s significantly no difference between them. The shapley values reveal that the features gender and age have the most impact for both the PPG signals. It is also observed that the features such as standard deviation of the IR (for t-PPG), blue (for i-PPG), red (both PPG signals) channels have more impact than the others. The inference time of the proposed XGBoost algorithm that consists of 30 trees (T) and depth (D) = 6 is less than 5 microseconds per row and the total dataset latency is less than 2 milliseconds for an intel i5 system for both the PPG signals. In conclusion, the results obtained were in the clinically accepTable level of confidence of g/dL40. Further, the prediction of Hb values will be more accurate by including more diverse data and the performance of all those ML models should be estimated to assess robustness. It can be used in the clinical settings for frequent estimation of Hb values in the user’s comfort zone like homes and even in the low resource settings in the form of smartphone application.
