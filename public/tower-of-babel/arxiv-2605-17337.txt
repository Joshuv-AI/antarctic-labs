Minimal dimension equivariant embeddings of real and complex flag manifolds into Euclidean spaces
Zhongzi Wang, and Hang Yin

Abstract
. We determine the minimal equivariant embedding dimension of orthgonal groups acting on real flag manifolds and unitary groups acting on complex flag manifolds. The minimal embedding dimension is achieved at isospectral model.



1.
Introduction

A famous theorem in differential topology is any smooth
n
-dimensional manifold can be smoothly embedded into
\mathbb{R}^{2n}

[
11
]
. There are many results concerning the embedding of manifolds see e.g. Hirsch
[
2
]
proved every orientable
3
-manifold smoothly embedded into
\mathbb{R}^{5}
, and actually the dimension
5
is the best possible for general 3-manifolds. The determination for the minimal dimension of smooth embedding of a specific manifold is in general a very hard problem, and there are many results on the estimate of embedding dimensions.

We first recall the definition of equivariant embedding:

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose
M
is a smooth
n
-dimensional manifold and
G
be a Lie group acting smoothly on
M
. A
G
-equivariant embedding
e
is a smooth embedding
e:M\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
for some positive integer
N
together with a Lie group representation
\rho:G\to{\rm O}(N)
, such that for any
g\in G
,
\rho(g)\circ e=e\circ g
.

Suppose
M
is a smooth compact manifold with a Lie group
G
-action. Mostow
[
4
]
, also Palais
[
5
]
, proved
M
has an
G
-equivariant embedding into
\mathbb{R}^{N}
for some positive integer
N
.
We next recall the definition of a homogeneous manifold:

Definition 1.2
.

Suppose
M
is a smooth compact
n
-dimensional manifold and
G
be a compact Lie group acting smoothly on
M
. We say
M
is a homogeneous manifold if the
G
-action on
M
is transitive.

The general problem is

Question 1.3
.

Suppose
M
is a smooth compact
n
-dimensional manifold and
G
be a compact Lie group acting smoothly on
M
. If
M
is a homogeneous manifold, let
\text{ed}_{G}(M)
be the minimal
N
such that there exists a
G
-equivariant embedding from
M
to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Determine the value of
\text{ed}_{G}(M)
.

There are results concerning the upper bound for the dimension of equivariant embedding of manifolds see e.g.
[
10
]
. When
G
is a finite group, see also
[
9
]
.

There has been studies on minimal dimension of equivariant embedding of finite group action on surfaces, see
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
. Recently,
[
3
]
determined the minimal dimension of equivariant embedding real flag manifolds with orthogonal group action except finitely many cases. In this paper, we determined the minimal dimension for equivariant embedding for arbitrary real flag manifolds and complex flag manifolds.

The core method for studying
\text{ed}_{G}(M)
will be the use of the representation theory of Lie groups. Take a basepoint
x_{0}\in M
. Then the point stabilizer
{\mathrm{Stab}}_{x_{0}}(G)=\{g\in G\mid g\cdot x_{0}=x_{0}\}
is a closed subgroup of
G
, and we have an diffeomorphism
M\cong G/{\mathrm{Stab}}_{x_{0}}(G)
.

The problem of equivariant embedding is equivalent to the problem of finding a representation space of
G
that contains a point with stabilizer equals to
{\mathrm{Stab}}_{x_{0}}(G)
. For representations of Lie groups, see
[
1
]
.

For an
n\times n
-matrix
T
, denote
T^{t}
the trasnpose of
T
.
If
T\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, denote
T^{*}
the conjugate transpose of
T
.

1.1.
Isospectral model over
\mathbb{R}

Let
\text{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
denote the space of symmetric
n\times n
-matrices over
\mathbb{R}
. Let
\text{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
denote the special orthogonal group. Note that
\text{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
acts on
\text{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
by

A\cdot M=AMA^{t}
. So
{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is a representation space of the special orthogonal group
\text{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. The irreducible decomposition of this representation space is

\text{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{R})=\text{Sym}^{tr=0}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathbb{R}

where
\text{Sym}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R})=\{T\in\text{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{R}):trT=0\}.

We say the representation space
\text{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is the isospectral model and the representation space
\text{Sym}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R})
is the traceless isospectral model.

1.2.
Isospectral model over
\mathbb{C}

Let
\text{Her}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
denote the space of Hermitian
n\times n
matrices, i. e.
\text{Her}_{n}(\mathbb{R})=\{T\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}):T=T^{*}\}
. Let
\text{SU}_{n}
be the special unitary group.
Note that
\text{SU}_{n}
acts on
\text{Her}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
by

A\cdot M=AMA^{*}
, where
A^{*}=\overline{A^{t}}
. So
\text{Her}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is a representation space of the special unitary group
\text{SU}_{n}
. The irreducible decomposition of this representation space is

\text{Her}_{n}(\mathbb{R})=\text{Her}^{tr=0}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathbb{R}

where
\text{Her}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R})=\{T\in\text{Her}_{n}(\mathbb{R}):trT=0\}.

We say the representation space
\text{Her}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is the isospectral model and the representation space
\text{Her}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R})
is the traceless isospectral model.

1.3.
Real and complex flag manifolds

The real flag manifold
\mathrm{Flag}(n_{1},...,n_{r},\mathbb{C}^{n})
is a smooth manifold
with a transitive
\text{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-action.
There is an
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant embedding from
\mathrm{Flag}(n_{1},...,n_{r})
into the traceless isospectral model
{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R}).

Let
A
be the block diagonal matrix of diagonal entries
a_{1}I_{k_{1}},...,a_{r}I_{k_{r}}
where
a_{1},...,a_{r}
are distinct real numbers with
\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}k_{i}=0
. The image of the
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant embedding of the flag manifold
\mathrm{Flag}(k_{1},...,k_{r};\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}
-orbit of
A
in
{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R}).

The complex flag manifold
\mathrm{Flag}(n_{1},...,n_{r},\mathbb{C}^{n})
is a smooth complex manifold
with a transitive
{\mathrm{SU}}_{n}
-action.
There is an
{\mathrm{SU}}_{n}
-equivariant embedding from
\mathrm{Flag}(n_{1},...,n_{r})
into the traceless isospectral model
\text{Her}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R}).
Let
A
be the same matrix as in the real case.
The image of the
{\mathrm{SU}}_{n}
-equivariant embedding of the flag manifold
\mathrm{CFlag}(k_{1},...,k_{r};\mathbb{C}^{n})
is the
SU_{n}
-orbit of
A
in
\text{Her}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R}).

The equivariant embedding for the real flag manifold is due to Lim-Ye.

1.4.
Basic of flag variety

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field. Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{K}
. Let
0<k_{1}<...<k_{r}<n
.

(1)

A flag of type
(k_{1},...,k_{r})
in
V
is a filtration of linear subspaces
W_{1}\subset...\subset W_{r}\subset V
such that
\text{dim}W_{i}=k_{i}.

(2)

When
k_{i}=i
, the flag is said to be a
full flag
.

(3)

Given
0<k_{1}<...<k_{r}<n
, the set of all flags of type
(k_{1},...,k_{r})
in
V
can be equipped with the structure of a smooth projective algebraic variety over
\mathbb{K}
. It is called the flag variety of type
(k_{1},...,k_{r})
of
V
, and is denoted as
\text{Flag}(k_{1},...,k_{r},V)
.

(4)

When
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, we also call it a flag manifold of type
(k_{1},...,k_{r})
.

Now we assume
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. The flag manifold
\mathrm{Flag}(k_{1},...,k_{r},V)
has dimension

\frac{n(n-1)}{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}\frac{l_{i}(l_{i}-1)}{2}

where
l_{1}=k_{1},l_{i}=k_{i}-k_{i-1}
for
2\leq i\leq r
and
l_{r+1}=n-k_{r}
.
When
r=1
and
k_{1}=k
,
\mathrm{Flag}(k_{1},...,k_{r})
is the real Grassmann manifold
Gr_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The dimension of
Gr_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
k(n-k)
.

It is known that for any inner product on
V
,
{\mathrm{SO}}(V)
acts transitively on
\mathrm{Flag}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r},V)
, and the stabilizer in
O(V)
of a flag
W_{1}\subset\cdots\subset W_{r}
in
\mathrm{Flag}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r},V)
is
{\mathrm{O}}(U_{1})\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{O}}(U_{r+1})
. Here
U_{i}
is the orthogonal complement of
W_{i-1}
in
W_{i}
, and we identify
W_{0}=0
,
W_{r+1}=V
. If we take stabilizer in
{\mathrm{SO}}(V)
, then we get
{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{O}}(U_{1})\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{O}}(U_{r+1}))
. Here the outer
S
means the total determinant is
1
.

Therefore we have
\text{Flag}(k_{1},...,k_{r})\cong\text{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/H
where
H=\text{S}(\text{O}_{l_{1}}(\mathbb{R})\times\text{O}_{l_{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times...\times\text{O}_{l_{r+1}}(\mathbb{R}))
.

1.5.
Main theorem and preliminary discussions

We are considering equivariant embedding of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})/{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{O}}_{n_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\times\cdots{\mathrm{O}}_{n_{r}}({\mathbb{R}}))
,
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
,
n_{i}\geqslant 1
,
n\geqslant 2
. This amounts to a finite dimensional real representation
V
of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
with a vector
v\in V
whose stabilizer is
{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{O}}_{n_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\times\cdots{\mathrm{O}}_{n_{r}}({\mathbb{R}}))
.

The isospectral model
\{A\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}):A\text{ symmetric and }{\mathrm{tr}}\ A=0\}
is an equivariant embedding whose dimension is
(n+2)(n-1)/2
. Our first main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

The equivariant embedding of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})/{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{O}}_{n_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\times\cdots{\mathrm{O}}_{n_{r}}({\mathbb{R}}))
,
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
,
n_{i}\geqslant 1
,
n\geqslant 2
with minimal dimension is isospectral model.

Especially, the minimal dimension for
\text{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant embedding of the real Grassmann manifold
\text{Gr}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
(n+2)(n-1)/2
and is independent of
k
.

Lim-Ye
[
3
]
proved the case
n\geq 17
of Theorem 1.7.

The definition of real flag manifold can be natuarally extended to the complex case, that is, replacing all the real vector spaces by the complex one. In this section, we always assume that
V
is a complex vector space and denote the corresponding complex flag manifold of type
(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})
by
\mathrm{CFlag}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r},V)

It is known that for any Hermitian inner product on
V
,
{\mathrm{SU}}(V)
acts transitively on
\mathrm{CFlag}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r},V)
, and the stabilizer in
U(V)
of a flag
W_{1}\subset\cdots\subset W_{r}
in
\mathrm{CFlag}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r},V)
is
{\mathrm{U}}(U_{1})\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{U}}(U_{r+1})
. Here
U_{i}
is the orthogonal complement of
W_{i-1}
in
W_{i}
, and we identify
W_{0}=0
,
W_{r+1}=V
. If we take stabilizer in
{\mathrm{SU}}(V)
, then we get
{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{U}}(U_{1})\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{U}}(U_{r+1}))
. Here the outer
S
means the total determinant is
1
.

Our second main theorem is:

Theorem 1.6
.

The equivariant embedding of
{\mathrm{SU}}_{n}/{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{U}}_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{U}}_{n_{r}})
,
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
,
n_{i}\geqslant 1
,
n\geqslant 2
with minimal dimension is isospectral model.

To sum up, we proved that

Theorem 1.7
.

The minimal dimension equivariant embedding of the flag manifold
\mathrm{Flag}(k_{1},...,k_{r},\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-action (resp.
\mathrm{CFlag}(k_{1},...,k_{r},\mathbb{C}^{n}))
with
{\mathrm{SU}}_{n}
-action) is the given by the equivariant embedding to the traceless isospectral model
{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R})
which has dimension
(n-2)(n+1)/2
(resp.
{\rm Her}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R})
which has dimension
n^{2}-1
).

1.6.
Method to prove main theorems

(1) The key to prove Theorem
1.5
is Proposition
2.3
, which states that the diagonal subgroup
H=\text{S}(\text{O}_{1}(\mathbb{R})\times...\times\text{O}_{1}(\mathbb{R}))
acts without nontrivial fixed points on any representation space
V
of the special orthogonal group
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
with
\dim V\leq\dim{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R})
and
V\neq{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}^{tr=0}(\mathbb{R})
.
Here the trivial fixed point in a representation means the origin of that representation space.

(2) The key to prove Theorem
1.6
is Proposition
3.2
, which states that the diagonal subgroup
H={\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{U}}_{1}\times...\times{\mathrm{U}}_{1})
acts without nontrivial fixed points on the representation spaces
V
of the special orthogonal group
{\mathrm{SU}}_{n}
with
\dim V\leq\dim\mathrm{Her}_{n}^{tr=0}({\mathbb{R}})
and
V\neq\mathrm{Her}_{n}^{tr=0}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Both Proposition
2.3
and
3.2
concerns nontrivial fixed points of a subgroup in a representation space.

2.
Equivariant embedding of real flag manifolds

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.5

Theorem
1.5
.

The equivariant embedding of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})/{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{O}}_{n_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\times\cdots{\mathrm{O}}_{n_{r}}({\mathbb{R}}))
,
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
,
n_{i}\geqslant 1
,
n\geqslant 2
with minimal dimension is isospectral model.

Lemma 2.1
.

Theorem
1.5
is true when
n=2
.

Proof.

We are considering the case
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})/{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{O}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})\times{\mathrm{O}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}))\cong S^{1}/\pm 1
, where
S^{1}
is identified with norm
1
element in
{\mathbb{C}}
. The isospectral model corresponds to the representation
S^{1}\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})
given by
z\mapsto z^{2}
.

We know
S^{1}
representation
V
is direct sum of complex one-dimensional representations. To reach the minimal dimension,
V
should be complex one-dimensional, hence is given by
S^{1}\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}({\mathbb{C}})
,
z\mapsto z^{m}
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The correct one must be
m=\pm 2
. Note that the complex conjugation on
{\mathbb{C}}
transforms the representation
z\mapsto z^{-2}
to
z\mapsto z^{2}
, hence
V
is isomorphic to isospectral model.
∎

When
n\geqslant 3
,
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
is a semi-simple Lie group. It is simple when
n\neq 4
.

Denote
H\subseteq{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
the subgroup of diagonal matrices, which should have diagonal entries
\pm 1
. It is clear that for any partition
(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
of
n
,
H\subseteq{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{O}}_{n_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\times\cdots{\mathrm{O}}_{n_{r}}({\mathbb{R}}))
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Consider the set
\Omega
of all real representations of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
with stabilizer
{\mathrm{SO}}({\mathrm{O}}_{n_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{O}}_{n_{r}}({\mathbb{R}}))
. If
V\in\Omega
satisfies
\dim V=\min\{\dim W\mid W\in\Omega\}
, then
V
does not contain trivial sub-representations and each irreducible sub-representation contains a non-zero vector fixed by
H
.

Proof.

Suppose
V
contains a trivial sub-representation, say
{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq V
. Then there is a complement sub-representation
W
such that
V={\mathbb{R}}\oplus W
. Then
W\in\Omega
with smaller dimension, a contradiction.

Let
W
be an irreducible sub-representation of
V
and suppose
H
does not fix non-zero vector in
W
. Let
W^{\prime}
be a complement such that
V=W\oplus W^{\prime}
. Suppose
v\in V
has stabilizer
{\mathrm{SO}}({\mathrm{O}}_{n_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{O}}_{n_{r}}({\mathbb{R}}))
. Write
v=w+w^{\prime}
with
w\in W
and
w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
. Then
H
fixes
v
implies
H
fixes
w
, hence
w=0
. Thus
v=w^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime}\in\Omega
with smaller dimension, a contradiction.
∎

The above Lemma emphasizes the importance of real irreducible representations with non-zero
H
-fixed vector. We shall prove the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume
n\geqslant 3
and
n\neq 4
. Let
V
be an irreducible real representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
. If
\dim V
is no greater than isospectral model and
H
fixes some non-zero vector in
V
, then
V
is either trivial representation or isospectral model.

Remark 2.4
.

The above Proposition is wrong for
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}({\mathbb{R}})
. For example, the highest weight representation
V(2,2)
has smaller dimension but
H
fixes non-zero vector.

Let us prove main theorem when
n\neq 4
assuming Proposition
2.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
for
n\neq 4
assuming Proposition
2.3
.

By Lemma
2.1
, we may assume
n\geqslant 3
. Assume
V
is an equivariant embedding of smallest dimension. By Lemma
2.2
,
V
does not contain trivial sub-representations and each irreducible sub-representation, say
W
, has non-zero
H
-fixed vector. Now
\dim W
is no greater than isospectral model, hence Proposition
2.3
implies
W
should be isospectral model. Considering dimension, we have
V=W
is isospectral model.
∎

The remaining case
{\mathrm{SO}}_{4}({\mathbb{R}})
is proved by hand, see Corollary
2.15
.

The basic ideal is to compute dimension of
V
. However, we can rule out some cases at the begining.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
V={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be the standard representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then
H
does not fix non-zero vector in
V
.

Proof.

This is trivial.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume
n\geqslant 3
. Let
V=\mathfrak{so}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
be the ajoint representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then
H
does not fix non-zero vector in
V
.

Proof.

Suppose
0\neq X\in\mathfrak{so}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
is fixed by
H
. Suppose the
(i,j)
-entry
X_{ij}
of
X
is non-zero,
i<j
. Choose an index
k
different from
i,j
(possible since
n\geqslant 3
). Consider diagonal matrix
A\in H
whose
(i,i)
-entry and
(k,k)
-entry are
-1
and other diagonal entries are
1
. Then the
(i,j)
-entry of
AXA^{-1}
is
-X_{ij}
, hence
X_{ij}=0
, a contradiction.
∎

To classify complex irreducible representations of
SO_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, we first need to consider the type of the Lie group
SO_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.

The Lie algebra of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
belongs to different types depending on parity of
n
. Thus we distinguish these two cases.

2.1.
Case of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}
(
n\geqslant 3
)

This is type
D
Lie group.

Each real representation
V
of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{R}})
gives rise to complex representation
V_{\mathbb{C}}
of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
. We shall first compute dimension of irreducible complex representations of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Denote
I_{n}
the
(n\times n)
-identity matrix,

S=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&-iI_{n}\\
I_{n}&iI_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

For matrix
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
I_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}
, denote

{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n,J}({\mathbb{C}})=\{A\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}}):A^{t}JA=J,\det A=1\}.

It is easy to verify that

(2.1)

f:{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n,J}({\mathbb{C}}),\quad A\mapsto SAS^{-1}

is an isomorphism of algebraic groups.

The maximal torus
T
of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n,J}({\mathbb{C}})
can be taken as
\{{\mathrm{diag}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{1}^{-1},\ldots,a_{n}^{-1}):a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\}
. The charaters of
T
are identified with
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. We shall denote such a character by
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})
,
\mu_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then

\mu({\mathrm{diag}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{1}^{-1},\ldots,a_{n}^{-1}))=\prod_{i}a_{i}^{\mu_{i}}.

Denote
e_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
with only non-zero value
1
at coordinate
i
. Choose positive roots to be
e_{i}\pm e_{j}
,
(i<j)
. It turns out that highest weight complex representations of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n,J}({\mathbb{C}})
correspond to dominant weights
\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
with
\mu_{1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\mu_{n-1}\geqslant|\mu_{n}|
. Denote by
V(\mu)
the highest weight
\mu
representation.

It is easy to compute the dimension by Weyl character formula,

(2.2)

\dim V(\mu)=\prod_{i<j}\frac{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}+j-i}{j-i}\prod_{i<j}\frac{\mu_{i}+\mu_{j}+2n-i-j}{2n-i-j}.

Denote
E(\mu)
the enumerator in the formula (
2.2
) of
\dim V(\mu)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For a dominant weight
\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, debite
\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n-1},-\mu_{n})
. Then
\dim V(\mu)=\dim V(\mu^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By the dimension formula, only need to compare the terms in enumerator involving index
n
. In the formula of
\dim V(\mu)
, the terms are

\prod_{i<n}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{n}+n-i)\prod_{i<n}(\mu_{i}+\mu_{n}+n-i).

This expression is unchanged when
\mu_{n}
becomes
-\mu_{n}
.
∎

In computing dimension, we can only consider dominant weights with non-negative coordinates.

Lemma 2.8
.

When
k\geqslant 3
and
n\geqslant 4
, we have

\dim V(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k},0,\ldots,0)\geqslant\dim V(2,0,\ldots,0).

The equality holds only at
\dim V(1,1,1,1)=\dim V(2,0,0,0)
.

Proof.

Denote
\mu=(2,0,\ldots,0)
and
\lambda_{k}=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k},0,\ldots,0)

If

∎

3.
Equivariant embedding of complex flag manifold

The definition
1.4

V

\mathrm{CFlag}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r},V)

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix a Hermitian inner product on

of a flag

in

If we take stabilizer in

{\mathrm{S}}({\mathrm{U}}(U_{1})\times\cdots\times{\mathrm{U}}(U_{r+1}))
. Here the outer
S
means the total determinant is
1
.

We shall prove the following

Theorem
1.6

The equivariant embedding of

,
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
,
n_{i}\geqslant 1
,
n\geqslant 2
with minimal dimension is isospectral model.

3.1.
Computation of dimension

It is known that
{\mathrm{SU}}_{n}\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
is the complexification, hence every complex representation of
{\mathrm{SU}}_{n}
extends to a complex representation of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Take maximal torus
T
of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
to be diagonal matrices. Denote
e_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
standard basis vector with
1
at
i
-th coordinate. We view
e_{i}
as character on diagonal matrices that is only non-trivial at
(i,i)
-entry. The characters of
T
then lie in the subspace
E=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mid\sum x_{i}=0\}
. The roots are
\Phi=\{e_{i}-e_{j}\mid i\neq j\}
. Choose positive roots to be
\Phi^{+}=\{e_{i}-e_{j}\mid i<j\}
. For each
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-1}
, the vector

\Big(\mu_{1}-\frac{\sum\mu_{i}}{n},\ldots,\mu_{n-1}-\frac{\sum\mu_{i}}{n},-\frac{\sum\mu_{i}}{n}\Big)

is a weight, and it is dominant if and only if
\mu_{1}\geqslant\mu_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\mu_{n-1}\geqslant 0
. Conversely, all weights are of above form. We shall write
\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-1}
to mean a weight identified as above. It is clear
\mu
acts on
T
via

\mu({\mathrm{diag}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}))=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}^{\mu_{i}}.

By Weyl character formula, denote
V(\mu)
,
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n-1})
the irreducible highest weight
\lambda
representation, then

\dim V(\mu)=\frac{\prod_{i<j<n}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}+j-i)\prod_{i<n}(\mu_{i}+n-i)}{\prod_{i<j}(j-i)}

The isospectral model corresponds to
\mu=(2,1,\ldots,1)
, which has dimension
(n+1)(n-1)
.

Recall
H=T\cap{\mathrm{SU}}_{n}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
V(\mu)
contains a non-zero
H
-fixed vector, then
n\mid\sum\mu_{i}
.

Proof.

If
V(\mu)
contains a non-zero
H
-fixed vector, then it contains a weight
\lambda
that is trivial on
H
. Thus
\lambda=0
. Since each root
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1})
satisfies
n\mid\sum\alpha_{i}
, and
\mu-\lambda
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear combintations of roots, the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
\mu=(\underbrace{a,\ldots,a}_{u},\underbrace{b,\ldots,b}_{v},\mu_{u+v+1},\ldots,\mu_{n-1})
with
u,v\geqslant 1
,
u+v\leqslant n-2
,
a>b>\mu_{u+v+1}
. Then

\dim V(\mu)>\dim V(2,1,\ldots,1).

Proof.

It is clear
n\geqslant 4
. Consider item in dimension formula involving indices in
\{1,\ldots,u+v\}
. We see

\displaystyle\dim V(\mu)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leqslant u\\
u<j\leqslant u+v\end{subarray}}\frac{1+j-i}{j-i}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leqslant u\\
u+v<j\leqslant n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{2+j-i}{j-i}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<i\leqslant u+v\\
u+v<j\leqslant n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1+j-i}{j-i}\prod_{i\leqslant u}\frac{2+n-i}{n-i}\prod_{u<i\leqslant u+v}\frac{1+n-i}{n-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v+1}{u!(u+v+1)!}\cdot\big((n+1)\cdots(n-u+2)\big)\cdot\big(n\cdots(n-u-v+1)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)n\cdot\frac{n\cdots(n-u+2)}{u!}\cdot\frac{(n-1)\cdots(n-u-v+1)}{(u+v+1)!}\cdot(v+1)

Here in the last line,
n\cdots(n-u+2)
means
1
if
u=1
.

It is clear
n\cdots(n-u+2)\geqslant u!
. Since
u+v+1\leqslant n-1
, we see

(n-1)\cdots(n-u-v+1)\geqslant\frac{(u+v+1)!}{v+1}.

Thus

\dim V(\mu)\geqslant(n+1)n>(n+1)(n-1)=\dim V(2,1,\ldots,1).

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
\mu=(\underbrace{a,\ldots,a}_{u},b,\ldots,b)
with
1\leqslant u\leqslant n-2
,
a>b\geqslant 1
. Then

\dim V(\mu)\geqslant\dim V(2,1,\ldots,1).

Equaility holds only when
\mu=(2,1,\ldots,1)
.

Proof.

It is clear
n\geqslant 3
. We have

\displaystyle\dim V(\mu)=

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leqslant u\\
u<j\leqslant n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{a-b+j-i}{j-i}\prod_{i\leqslant u}\frac{a+n-i}{n-i}\prod_{u<i\leqslant n-1}\frac{b+n-i}{n-i}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leqslant u\\
u<j\leqslant n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1+j-i}{j-i}\prod_{i\leqslant u}\frac{2+n-i}{n-i}\prod_{u<i\leqslant n-1}\frac{1+n-i}{n-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n+1)\cdots(n-u+2)}{u!}\cdot(n-u)

Denote

A=\frac{(n+1)\cdots(n-u+2)}{u!}\cdot(n-u).

Case
u=1
. We see
A=(n+1)(n-1)\dim V(2,1,\ldots,1)
. However, if
a>2
or
b>1
, then above inequalities become strict, hence equality holds here only when
a=2
,
b=1
.

Case
u=2
. Since
n\geqslant 4
, we have

A=\frac{(n+1)n(n-2)}{2}>(n+1)(n-1)=\dim V(2,1,\ldots,1).

Case
u\geqslant 3
. Then

A=(n+1)n\cdot\frac{(n-1)\cdots(n-u+2)}{u!}\cdot(n-u).

Since
u\leqslant n-2
,

\big((n-1)\cdots(n-u+2)\big)\cdot(n-u)\geqslant(n-2)\cdots(n-u)\geqslant u!.

Thus

A\geqslant(n+1)n>(n+1)(n-1)=\dim V(2,1,\ldots,1).

∎

Lemma 3.6

Suppose
\mu=(\underbrace{a,\ldots,a}_{u},0,\ldots,0)

u<j\leqslant n\end{subarray}}\frac{2+j-i}{j-i}=\frac{(n+1)\cdots(n-u+2)}{(u+1)!}\cdot\frac{n\cdots(n-u+1)}{u!}.

Denote

A=\frac{(n+1)\cdots(n-u+2)}{(u+1)!}\cdot\frac{n\cdots(n-u+1)}{u!}

Case
n=4

Case

∎

Lemma 3.7
