Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g
,
k(X)
the function field of
X
,
D
a very ample divisor on
X
, and
\phi_{D}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
the embedding induced by
D
. If
W\in\mathbb{G}(N-2,N)
, we denote by
\pi_{W}:\mathbb{P}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
the projection away from
W
.

Definition 0.1
.

We say that
\phi_{D}
is a
Galois embedding
if there exists a subspace
W\in\mathbb{G}(N-2,N)
, such that
f_{W}=\pi_{W}\circ\phi_{D}
is a Galois cover; that is, the field extension
f_{W}^{*}k(\mathbb{P}^{1})\subseteq k(X)
is Galois. In this case, we say that
W
is a
Galois subspace
for
D
. Moreover, if
W
is disjoint from
\phi_{D}(X)
, then we say that
W
is a
disjoint Galois subspace
.

The general concept of Galois embeddings and Galois subspaces was introduced by Yoshihara in
13
. The particular case when
W
is a point was introduced by Fukasawa in
8
. In recent years, multiple authors have expanded on the idea of Galois subspaces,
e.g.
,
1
,
9
,
7
,
10
, and
3
.
We build on the work of Auffarth and Rahausen
1
, in which the authors study the following set

G_{X,D}\coloneqq\{W\in\mathbb{G}(N-2,N);\hskip 3.0pt\pi_{W}\circ\phi_{D}\text{ is Galois}\}.

They prove (see
(
1
, Theorem 1.1)
) that if
g\geq 2
, then

(1)

G_{X,D}\cong\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\leq\text{Aut}(X)\\
X/H\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{X}_{D,\pi_{H}},

where
\pi_{H}
denotes the canonical projection
X\rightarrow X/H
, and
\mathcal{X}_{D,\pi_{H}}
is isomorphic to a projective space of dimension
\ell(D-D_{\pi_{H}})-1
, where
\ell(D-D_{\pi_{H}})\coloneqq\text{dim}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D-D_{\pi_{H}}))
. In particular, the disjoint Galois subspaces are in the
0
-dimensional components of the decomposition of Equation (
1
) (see
(
1
, Remark 3.1)
).

The set
G_{X,D}
has been studied when
X
has genus
0
or
1
in
1
, and
2
, respectively. In this article, we will study the set
G_{X,D}
when
g=2
, using the decomposition of Equation (
1
). Because
\mathcal{X}_{D,\pi_{H}}
is a projective space of dimension
\ell(D-D_{\pi_{H}})-1
and
\text{Aut}(X)
is finite, to study the components of the decomposition of
G_{X,D}
, we only need to focus on calculating the dimension of each projective space. This is possible because the isomorphism class of
\text{Aut}(X)
is known for genus
2
(see
5
and
4
).

We mainly focus on disjoint Galois subspaces, which lie in the
0
-dimensional components of the decomposition of Equation (
1
). Moreover,
G_{X,D}
has a
0
-dimensional component if and only if there exists a subgroup
H\leq\text{Aut}(X)
with
X/H\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
D\sim D_{H}
, where
D_{H}\coloneqq D_{H,p}\coloneqq\sum_{h\in H}h[p]
for some

(see Lemma
2.1
). Note that, because

, the definition of

is independent of the choice of

, up to linear equivalence (see Lemma
1.1
).

The main result of this article describes the components of the decomposition of
G_{X,D}
in Equation (
1
) when
D
is induced by a subgroup of
\text{Aut}(X)
, to guarantee the existence of a
0
-dimensional component.

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
2
and let
H\leq G=\text{Aut}(X)
. The following assertions hold:

(1)

Assume
G\not\cong C_{10}
. Two very ample divisors induced by subgroups of
G
differ by a multiple of the canonical divisor.

(2)

In
G_{X,D_{H}}
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between disjoint Galois subspaces and subgroups of order
|H|
.

The structure of the article is as follows:

In Section 1, we prove some general results on compact Riemann surfaces of genus
g\geq 2
. We prove Theorem
0.2
in Section 2. Lastly, in Section
3
, we present a table describing the dimension of the components of
G_{X,D_{H}}
.

Remark 1
.

Even though the techniques used to prove Theorem
0.2
hold for arbitrary genus, that is, Lemma
1.2
and Lemma
1.4
, we are unable to prove an analog of Theorem
0.2
for genus greater than
2
. The main reason is that for a Riemann surface
X
of genus
2
, there exist many subgroups
H\leq\mathrm{Aut}(X)
such that
X/H\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. This is not the case when the genus is greater than
2
. Although the structure of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
is well-known for low genus, it is not clear whether the techniques used in the proof of Theorem
0.2
can be used to give a similar result for higher genus.

Finally, we note that Yoshihara and Fukasawa have a list of current open problems of Galois subspaces
12
, in which they also summarize what is currently known regarding each problem.

1.
Linear equivalence of divisors given by a subgroup

In this section, we will prove the lemmas necessary to prove Theorem
0.2
. For this section, unless stated otherwise,
X
will be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 2
, and
G=\text{Aut}(X)
.

Recall that given a subgroup
H\leq G
with
X/H\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we denote by
D_{H}\coloneqq D_{H,p}\coloneqq\sum_{h\in H}h[p]
the divisor induced by
H
, for some
p\in X
. This is independent of the choice of
p
, up to linear equivalence, by the following lemma.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
H\leq G
be such that
X/H\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then, for any
p,q\in X
we have that
D_{H,p}\sim D_{H,q}
. That is,
D_{H,p}
is independent of the choice of the point
p
, up to linear equivalence.

Proof.

Let
p,q\in X
, and
\pi:X\rightarrow X/H
be the natural projection. Because
X/H\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we have that
\pi(p)\sim\pi(q)
and therefore
\pi^{-1}(\pi(p))\sim\pi^{-1}(\pi(q))
, which follows from
(
11
, Chapter V Lemma 2.3)
and
(
11
, Chapter V Corollary 2.6)
. Noting that
\pi^{-1}(\pi(p))=D_{H,p}
and
\pi^{-1}(\pi(q))=D_{H,q}
, we can conclude that
D_{H,p}\sim D_{H,q}
.
∎

By the previous lemma, we can denote
D_{H}
without specifying the point
p
. Now, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1.2
.

If
H,N,L\leq G
with
L\subseteq N\cap H
,
X/L\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
|H|-|N|=|L|
, then
D_{H}-D_{N}\sim D_{L}
.

Proof.

Because
L\subseteq H
, we can consider the set of right cosets

1

Note that we are not assuming that
L
is a normal subgroup of
H
or
N
. Nonetheless, we can still consider the right cosets of a subgroup
of
L
in
H
. So, by fixing a set of representatives of the right cosets
H_{L}
, we have that

D_{H,p}=\sum_{h\in H}h[p]=\sum_{h\in H_{L}}(Lh)[p]=\sum_{h\in H_{L}}L(h[p])=\sum_{h\in H_{L}}D_{L,h[p]}.

Doing the same process with
D_{N,p}
, and denoting by

a set of representatives of right cosets of

in
N
, we get that

D_{H,p}-D_{N,p}-D_{L,p}=\sum_{h\in H_{L}}D_{L,h[p]}-\sum_{n\in N_{L}}D_{L,n[p]}-D_{L,p},

Because
X/L\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
D_{L,p}=\pi^{-1}(\pi(p))
, where
\pi:X\rightarrow X/L
denotes the natural projection, as in the proof of Lemma
1.1
, we have that
D_{L,q}\sim D_{L,p}
for all

. Furthermore, because
|H|-|N|=|L|
we have that
|H_{L}|-|N_{L}|=1
. Therefore, rearranging Equation (

D_{H,p}-D_{N,p}\sim D_{L,p}
.
∎

Because every compact Riemann surface
X
of genus
2
is hyperelliptic, we have that there exists a unique hyperelliptic involution
\sigma
such that
X/\left<\sigma\right>\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
\left<\sigma\right>\leq Z(G)
, and
D_{\left<\sigma\right>}\sim K
, where
K
is a canonical divisor. So, when
X
has genus
2
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus 2 and
\sigma
its hyperelliptic involution. If
H,N\leq G
with
\left<\sigma\right>\subseteq N\cap H
, and
|H|-|N|=2
, then
D_{H}-D_{N}\sim K
. Moreover,
\ell(D_{H}-D_{N})=2
.

Proof.

We can use the previous lemma, with
L=\left<\sigma\right>
, and the fact that if
D_{H}-D_{N}\sim K
, then

\ell(D_{H}-D_{N})=\ell(K)=g=2

to deduce the corollary.
∎

Returning to the general case, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 2
with
G=\text{Aut}(X)
. If
H,N,L\leq G
with
L\subseteq N\cap H
,
X/L\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
|H|=|N|
, then
D_{H}\sim D_{N}
.

Proof.

This can be proven in the same way as in Lemma
1.2
.
∎

Now, if
X
has genus
2
, then we have an equivalent characterization of a very ample divisor.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
X
be of genus
2
and
D
a divisor on
X
. We have that
D
is a very ample divisor if and only if
\deg(D)\geq 5
.

Proof.

The “if” part of the lemma follows from the Riemann-Roch formula and the fact that
\deg(D)>\deg(K)+2
(see
(
11
, Chapter VII Proposition 1.2)
). The “only if” part of the lemma is a consequence of the fact that there is no immersion from
X
into
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with
n<3
; the case
n=1
follows from Hurwitz’s Formula and the case
n=2
contradicts the Genus-Degree Formula.
∎

Finally, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1.6
.

If
X
has genus
2
and
D_{1},D_{2}
are two divisors on
X
with
\deg(D_{1})-\deg(D_{2})>2
, then
\ell(D_{1}-D_{2})=\deg(D_{1})-\deg(D_{2})-1
.

Proof.

This can be proven using the Riemann-Roch formula and the fact that
\deg(D_{1})-\deg(D_{2})>\deg(K)
.
∎

Proof of main result

We will use the notation of the previous section. As mentioned in the introduction, to study the structure of the
0

G_{X,D}
, we only need to consider the case where
D
is a very ample divisor induced by a subgroup

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 2
and
G=\text{Aut}(X)
. Then,
G_{X,D}
has a
0

D\sim D_{H}
for some
H\leq G
with
X/H\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}

Proof.

Assume that
G_{X,D}
has a
0

1

H\leq G
with
X/H\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}

D\sim D_{H}

H\leq G
with

such that

, then
\ell(D-D_{H})=\ell(D_{H}-D_{H})=1

has a
0

∎

So, if we want to study the disjoint Galois subspaces we can assume that

\text{deg}(D_{H})=|H|
