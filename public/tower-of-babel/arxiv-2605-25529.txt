1.
Introduction

1.1.
Discrete spherical averages

For a function
f:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, with
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
, the discrete spherical average is defined by

M_{\lambda}f(x)=\frac{1}{r_{n}(\lambda)}\sum_{|y|^{2}=\lambda}f(x-y),

where
y
is restricted to range over integer lattice points. Here
r_{n}(\lambda)
is the number of integers
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that
|m|^{2}=\lambda
. Notice that
r_{n}(\lambda)=N_{n}(\lambda)
, where
N_{n}(\lambda)
is the standard function that counts the number of ways to represent
\lambda
as a sum of
n
squares. For
n\geq 5
, we have
c_{n}^{1}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\leq r_{n}(\lambda)\leq c_{n}^{2}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}
for some constants
0<c_{n}^{1}<c_{n}^{2}<\infty
.
In
2002
, Magyar-Stein-Wainger
[
13
]
proved that the full discrete spherical maximal function

M_{*}f(x)=\sup_{\lambda\geq 1}|M_{\lambda}f(x)|

is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
whenever
p>\frac{n}{n-2}
and
n\geq 5
. Later, Ionescu
[
7
]
established the restricted weak-type estimate for the operator
M_{*}
at the endpoint
\frac{n}{n-2},\;n\geq 5
. Note that the ranges of
p
and
n
are best possible in the above results. We call a sequence
\{\lambda_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}=\mathbb{L}
lacunary if for all
l\geq 1
we have
\frac{\lambda_{l+1}}{\lambda_{l}}>c
, where
c
is a real number strictly greater than
1
. The lacunary discrete spherical maximal function associated to any lacunary sequence
\{\lambda_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
is defined as

M_{lac}f(x)=\sup\limits_{l\geq 1}|M_{\lambda_{l}}f(x)|,

We remark that, in contrast to the continuous case, the discrete lacunary spherical maximal operator
M_{lac}
does not admit boundedness on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for values of
p
below
\frac{n}{n-1}
. An explicit construction of the counterexample is given in
[
3
]
*section 7. Kesler-Lacey-Mena
[
9
]
proved that the operator
M_{lac}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
for the range
p>\frac{n-2}{n-3}
and
n\geq 5
. Nevertheless, the question concerning the
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
-boundedness of the operator
M_{lac}
remains unresolved for values of
p
between
\frac{n}{n-1}
and
\frac{n-2}{n-3}
.

1.2.
Averages associated to simplices in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

We call a finite set
R
Ramsey if for every number of colors
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there is a dimension
n=n(R,r)
, for which every
r
-coloring of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
contains a monochromatic congruent copy of
R
. The fundamental question in geometric Ramsey theory, originated in the work of Erdős et al.
[
5
]
, is to characterize the finite sets
R
that are Ramsey. In particular, it is conjectured that a finite set
R
is Ramsey if and only if
R
is a subset of a sphere.
From now on, for any
1\leq k\leq n
, we call a configuration
S=\{0,s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
as a non-degenerate
k
-simplex if the vectors
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
are linearly independent. Non-degenerate simplices constitute one of the canonical examples of Ramsey sets
[
6
]
. For a non-degenerate
k
-simplex
S
and
\lambda\in\sqrt{{\mathbb{N}}}
, we say that a simplex
\bar{S}=\{0,m_{1},\dots,m_{k}\}
is isometric to
S
, denoted
\bar{S}\simeq\lambda S
, if
|s_{i}-s_{j}|=\lambda|m_{i}-m_{j}|
for all
0\leq i,j\leq k.
Now, we define

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}:=\{(m_{1},\dots,m_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{nk}:\bar{S}=\{0,m_{1},\dots,m_{k}\}\simeq\lambda S\}.

Let
|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}|
denote the number of isometric copies of
\lambda S
. It is well-known from
[
12
,
14
,
4
]
that, for
n\geq 2k+3
, the quantity
|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}|
satisfies the bounds
\mathfrak{c}_{S}^{1}\lambda^{nk-k(k+1)}\leq|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}|\leq\mathfrak{c}_{S}^{2}\lambda^{nk-k(k+1)}
for some constants
0<\mathfrak{c}_{S}^{1}<\mathfrak{c}_{S}^{2}<\infty
; i.e.,
|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}|\approx\lambda^{nk-k(k+1)}
. Next, observe that for
k=1
and
s_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)
, the set
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}
is precisely the set of integer lattices over the surface of the Euclidean sphere of radius
\lambda
. Since discrete spherical averages of functions have been extensively studied, it is interesting to investigate the behaviour of the functions when sampled along isometric copies of a non-degenerate
k
-simplex. Thus, it is
natural to ask about the
\ell^{p}
-bounds for the maximal function associated to the discrete simplical averages defined as follows.
For a function
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{nk}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the discrete linear simplical average as

A_{\lambda S}f(x)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}|}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}}f(x-y).

Lyall, Magyar, Newman, and Woolfitt
[
12
]
prove that the maximal function associated to the discrete simplical averages

A_{*}f(x):=\sup_{\lambda\geq 1}|A_{\lambda S}f(x)|

is bounded on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{nk})
for all
k\geq 1,\;n\geq 2k+3
, and a non-degenerate
k
-simplex
S\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. However, the question regarding the
\ell^{p}
-boundedness of the maximal function
A_{*}
for values of
p
other than
2
remains open.

1.3.
The
r
-variation for the family of discrete averages

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, and
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a function in
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
r\in[1,\infty]
and
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{\lambda}:\lambda\in U\}
be a family of operators acting on the function
f
. The
r
-variation corresponding to the family
\mathcal{T}f=\{T_{\lambda}f:\lambda\in U\}
is defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle V_{r}(\mathcal{T}f)(x):=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{L\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sup\limits_{\lambda_{1}<\dots<\lambda_{L}}\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{L-1}|T_{\lambda_{l+1}}f(x)-T_{\lambda_{l}}f(x)|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},&0<r<\infty,\\
\sup\limits_{\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}}\left|T_{\lambda_{1}}f(x)-T_{\lambda_{0}}f(x)\right|,&r=\infty,\end{cases}

where the supremum is taken over all finite increasing sequences
\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{L}\}
lying in
U
. Since the beginning of the study of
r
-variations by Lépingle
[
10
]
, intensive research has been devoted to the study of
r
-variation across various classes of discrete and continuous averages. Contributions by numerous mathematicians include, for instance,
[
2
,
16
,
15
,
17
,
8
]
among others; see also the references therein for related developments. In particular, Jones, Seeger, and Wright
[
8
]
studied
L^{p}-
estimates of the
r
-variation for continuous spherical averages. In the article
[
1
]
, we initiate the study of variational estimates for the class of discrete averages over certain hyper-surfaces, which includes the sphere. Note that, we define
V_{r}(\mathcal{T}f)
as the long
r
-variation corresponding to the family
\mathcal{T}f
, obtained by restricting the supremum in (
1.1
) to a lacunary sequence. As working with any lacunary sequence is equivalent to working with the sequence
\{2^{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
, now onwards we will stick to the lacunary sequence
\mathbb{L}=\{2^{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
.
In this article, we study
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
-boundedness of
r
-variation of the family of discrete simplical averages
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}=\{A_{\lambda S}:\;\lambda\in\mathbb{L}\}
.
In this article, we study
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
-boundedness of the long
r
-variations of the family of discrete averages associated to simplices. Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}=\{A_{\lambda S}:\;\lambda\in\mathbb{L}\}
be the family of discrete simplical averages over lacunary sequence
\mathbb{L}
. Then, the long
r-
variations associated to the family
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}
is defined as follows:

V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f)(x):=V_{r}(\{A_{\lambda S}f,\;\lambda\in\mathbb{L}\})(x).

We note that

|A_{lac}^{S}f(x)|\leq|A_{\lambda_{0}S}f(x)|+2V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f)(x),

holds for any
\lambda_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq r<\infty
. Thus, the
\ell^{p}-
bounds for the long
r-
variations imply the corresponding bounds for the lacunary maximal operator
A_{lac}^{S}
. Also observe that, the
\ell^{p}-
boundedness of
V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f)
immediately implies that the term in (
1.1
) corresponding to the family
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f
is finite for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{nk}
, which in turn implies almost everywhere convergence of the sequence
\{A_{\lambda S}f\}
as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. Our main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S=\{s_{0}=0,s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
be a non-degenerate
k
-simplex and
\mathbb{L}=\{2^{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
, then for
r>2
,
k\geq 1
, and
n\geq 2k+3
we have

(1.2)

\|V_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f)\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn})}.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
relies on the jump inequalities corresponding to the family
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}
. Thus, we now define the
\lambda
-jump corresponding to the family
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}
, as the supremum of all integers
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
for which there is an increasing sequence
0<u_{1}<v_{1}\leq u_{2}<v_{2}\leq\cdots\leq u_{M}<v_{M}
with
u_{i},v_{i}\in\mathbb{L}
, such that

|A_{u_{l}S}f(x)-A_{v_{l}S}f(x)|>\lambda

holds for each
l=1,\dots,M
. We denote the
\lambda
-jump corresponding to the family
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}
by
\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S})
. We suggest the article
[
8
]
to the interested readers for further details involving
\lambda-
jumps. The following lemma is the discrete analogue of
[
8
]
*Lemma 2.1, which plays a crucial role in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. Since its proof is identical to that of
[
8
]
*Lemma 2.1, we omit the details.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
8
]
*Lemma 2.1
Let
\mathcal{T}
be a family of linear operators from
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn})
to the set of measurable functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}
. Suppose

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{T}f))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}

holds for all
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn})
. Then, for all
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn})
and

we have,

\displaystyle\|V_{r}(\mathcal{T}f)\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

It thus suffices to establish the next result, from which
Theorem

1.1
will follow.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn})
, then we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

To prove
Theorem

1.2
, we employ a key decomposition of the function via the discrete analogue of the Littlewood-Paley theory for
\ell^{2}
-functions along with the appropriate square function estimates of each term. In addition, the argument requires the
\ell^{2}
boundedness of the local maximal function for the discrete simplical averages from
[
12
]
. We will discuss these ingredients in detail in the subsequent sections.

2.2
while proving the inequality (
2.2
).

To see this, let us observe that

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f_{2})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{J_{l}-3}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f)(\xi)\left[\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(\Psi_{l,j+1}^{s})(\xi)-\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(\Psi_{l,j}^{s})(\xi)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f)(\xi)\sum_{j=0}^{J_{l}-3}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}\left[\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{kn}}(\psi)\left(\frac{(2|s|)^{l+1-j}}{t_{j}}(x+t_{j}\xi)\right)-\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{kn}}(\psi)\left(\frac{(2|s|)^{l-j}}{t_{j}}(x+t_{j}\xi)\right)\right].

Since the quantity in the square bracket is nonzero if
\frac{1}{2}(2|s|)^{(j-1)-l}<\left|\frac{x}{t_{j}}+\xi\right|\leq(2|s|)^{j-l}
; for each
\xi
, we have
\operatorname{supp}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f_{2})(\xi)\subset(\Omega^{s}_{l,j-2})^{c}
, for some
j\in[0,J_{l}]
. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f_{3})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f)(\xi)\left(1-\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(\Psi_{l,J_{l}-2}^{s})(\xi)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f)(\xi)\left(1-\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{kn}}(\psi)\left((2|s|)^{l-J_{l}+2}\left(\frac{x}{t_{J_{l}-2}}+\xi\right)\right)\right).

Thus,
\operatorname{supp}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f_{3})\subset(\Omega^{s}_{l,J_{l}-3})^{c}
. Therefore, we have

\operatorname{supp}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f_{2}),\;\operatorname{supp}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f_{3})\subset(\Omega^{s}_{l,j})^{c}
, for some
j\in[0,J_{l}-2]
.

2.1.
Proof of (
2.2
) for
i=2
.

Notice that
\operatorname{supp}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f_{2})\subset\Omega^{c}_{j,l}
. Now, to prove this estimate first we use
eq.

2.3
to dominate the
\lambda
-jump by the corresponding square function, then to sum in
l
we use the decay of
\ell^{2}
bound of single average via
Proposition

2.2
,

\displaystyle\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f_{2}))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}|A_{2^{l}S}f_{2}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{J_{l}-1}A_{2^{l}S}(f*\triangle\Psi_{l,j}^{s})\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(\sum_{l\geq 2^{j}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}|A_{2^{l}S}(f*\triangle\Psi_{l,j}^{s})(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We obtain the last inequality by using Minkowski’s inequality and finally we invoke
Proposition

2.1
to obtain the desired estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f_{2}))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(\sum_{l\geq 2^{j}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}|A_{2^{l}S}(f*\triangle\Psi_{l,j}^{s})(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/2}j^{-1}\left(\sum_{l\geq 2^{j}}\|f*\triangle\Psi_{l,j}^{s}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/2}j^{-1}\left(\sum_{l\geq 2^{j}}\sup_{\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{kn}}|\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(\triangle\Psi_{l,j}^{s})(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

2.2.
Proof of (
2.2
) for
i=3
.

Since
\operatorname{supp}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(f_{3})\subset\Omega^{c}_{l,J_{l}-3}
, invoking
Proposition

2.2
for
j=J_{l}-3=(\log_{2}l)-3
we get that

\displaystyle\|\lambda(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f_{3}))^{1/2}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}|A_{2^{l}S}(f-f*\Psi_{l,J_{l}}^{s})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left\|A_{2^{l}S}(f-f*\Psi_{l,J_{l}}^{s})\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}2^{-\log_{2}l}(\log_{2}l)^{-2}\left(1+\|\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}(\Psi_{l,J_{l}}^{s})\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{kn})}\right)^{2}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}},

where we use the estimate (
2.1
) to obtain the final step. At this point, it remains to establish the estimate (
2.2
) for
i=1
, which is the major part of the proof. We will use the following decomposition of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}
: For each non-negative integer
l
, we write

{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}=\bigcup\limits_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{l},

where
\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{l}
is the set of all lattice points lying in the dyadic cube
(2|s|)^{l}(t+[0,1)^{kn})
. Observe that, the collection
\{\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{l}:\;t\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}\}
forms a partition of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}
such that for
l_{1}\leq l_{2}
we have either
\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{l_{1}}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{l_{2}}
or
\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{l_{1}}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{l_{2}}=\emptyset
. Furthermore, for each
\mathcal{Q}_{t_{1}}^{l_{1}}
with
l_{1}<l_{2}
, there exists a unique
t_{2}
satisfying
\mathcal{Q}_{t_{1}}^{l_{1}}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{t_{2}}^{l_{2}}
.

2.3.
Proof of (
2.2
) for
i=1
.

We first decompose the average as follows:

\displaystyle A_{2^{l}S}f_{1}

\displaystyle=(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*f-E_{l}f+E_{l}f,

where
E_{l}f=|\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{l}|^{-1}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{l}}f(y)
, and
w_{\lambda S}(x)=|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}|^{-1}1_{\mathcal{S}_{\lambda S}}(x)
. Now onwards, we use the notation
\mathbb{E}f
and
\mathcal{L}f
to denote the sets
\{E_{l}f:l\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and
\left\{(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*f-E_{l}f:l\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
respectively. Note the following subadditivity property of
\lambda
-jump:

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{S}f)\leq\mathcal{J}_{\lambda/2}(\mathcal{L}f)+\mathcal{J}_{\lambda/2}(\mathbb{E}f),

which will be used in our proof. Let us recall the following
\ell^{p}-
estimate from
[
8
]
:

\left\|\lambda\left(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{E}f)\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})},~\;1<p<\infty.

Based on the above classical
\ell^{p}-
estimate for the jump corresponding to the family
\mathbb{E}f
along with the estimate (
2.3
), the proof of (
2.2
) for
i=1
is reduced to showing the
\ell^{2}-
boundedness of the square function corresponding to the family
\mathcal{L}f
; i.e., it suffices to show that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*f-E_{l}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

To prove (
2.4
), we rely on the following lemma, which is the discrete analogue of
[
8
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Define
D_{m}f(x)=E_{m}f(x)-E_{m-1}f(x)
, then for any
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn})
and
\delta>0
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*D_{m}f-E_{l}(D_{m}f)\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\delta|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

We assume
Lemma

2.1
for the moment. Write
D_{m}f(x)=E_{m}f(x)-E_{m-1}f(x)
and note that we have
f=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}D_{m}f
. Next, using this we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*f-E_{l}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*f-E_{l}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*D_{m}f-E_{l}(D_{m}f)\|_{\ell^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-\delta|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-\delta|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

This concludes the proof of (
2.2
) for
i=1
. ∎

Proof of
Lemma

2.1
.

To establish this lemma we devide the whole analysis into two parts. More precisely, in first part we will discuss the case when
l\geq m
, and in the second part we will discuss the case when
l<m
.

Case 1.

Suppose
l\geq m
. Then we can easily see that the fact
E_{l}(E_{m}f)=E_{l}f
implies that
E_{l}(D_{m}f)=E_{l}(E_{m}f-E_{m-1}f)=0.
Hence
\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m}}D_{m}f=0
. Now, using this observation we can write

\displaystyle(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*D_{m}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m}}\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S}(x-y)D_{m}f(y)

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m}}\left[\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S}(x-y)-\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S}(x-q_{t}^{m})\right]D_{m}f(y),

where
\mathfrak{q}_{t}^{m}
is the center of the cube
\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m}
. Further, by using the mean-value theorem, we obtain the following estimate for the term
|\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S}(x-y)-\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S}(x-q_{t}^{m})|
:

\displaystyle|\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S}(x-y)-\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S}(x-\mathfrak{q}_{t}^{m})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(2|s|)^{-lkn}}{2^{l(nk-k(k+1))}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}}\left|\psi\left(\frac{x-y-z}{(2|s|)^{l}}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{x-\mathfrak{q}_{t}^{m}-z}{(2|s|)^{l}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(2|s|)^{-lkn}}{2^{l(nk-k(k+1))}}\frac{|y-\mathfrak{q}_{t}^{m}|}{(2|s|)^{l}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}}\left|\tilde{\psi}\left(\frac{x-y-z}{(2|s|)^{l}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(l-m)}\left|\tilde{\psi}_{(2|s|)^{l}}(x-y-z_{0})\right|,

where
\tilde{\psi}
is a Schwartz function. Hence the above observation along with (
2.5
) implies that

\displaystyle|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*D_{m}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-(l-m)}\mathcal{M}(D_{m}f)(x-z_{0}),

here in the above expression
\mathcal{M}
is the discrete analogue of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function which is known to be bounded on
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}),\;\text{for}\;1<p\leq\infty
. Consequently, in the case
l\geq m
, the required estimate

\displaystyle\|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*D_{m}f-E_{l}(D_{m}f)\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}

holds.

Case 2.

Let us consider
l<m.
Note that, for
l<m
, we have
E_{l}(E_{m}f)=E_{m}f
.
It follows, therefore, that
E_{l}(D_{m}f)=D_{m}f
. We employ the Poisson summation formula to deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}|}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}}\frac{1}{(2|s|)^{lkn}}\psi\left(\frac{x-y}{(2|s|)^{l}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}|}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}}\mathscr{F}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{kn}}(\psi)\left((2|s|)^{l}x\right)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot y}=1.

Accordingly, one can concludes the subsequent pointwise equality:

\displaystyle(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})*D_{m}f(x)-E_{l}(D_{m}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}[D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)](\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\geq 0}T_{d}(x),

where
T_{d}(x)=\sum\limits_{y\in E_{l,d}}[D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)](\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l},S})(y)
and the set
E_{l,d}
is defined as follows:

E_{l,d}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}:(2|s|)^{l+d-1}\leq|y|\leq(2|s|)^{l+d}\right\}&\text{ if }d>0,\\
\\
\left\{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}:|y|\leq(2|s|)^{l}\right\}&\text{ if }d=0.\end{array}\right.

Now, we turn our attention to the
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn})
estimate of
T_{d}(x)
for
d
greater than
\epsilon|l-m|
, where
\epsilon
is a suitably chosen positive real number. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{d}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}\left|\sum\limits_{y\in E_{l,d}}[D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)](\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})(y)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\in E_{l,d}}\left(\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}}|D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)|^{2}|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})(y)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{y\in E_{l,d}}|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l}S})(y)|.

Note that for
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}
, we have
|z_{i}-z_{j}|=2^{l}|s_{i}-s_{j}|
for all
0\leq i,j\leq k
, with the convention that
z_{0}=0
. If we assume
j=0
in the above equality, we get
|z_{i}|=2^{l}|s_{i}|
for all
0\leq i\leq k
, thereby implying that
|z|=2^{l}|s|
for all
z\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}
. Next, for
d>0
and
y\in E_{l,d}
, we have that
(2|s|)^{l+d-1}\leq(2|s|)^{l}+|y|-|z|
for
z\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}
. Therefore, for any large number
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
we get that

\displaystyle|(\Psi_{l,0}^{s}*w_{2^{l},S})(y)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{l(kn-k(k+1))}}\left|\sum_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S}}\psi_{1,(2|s|)^{l}}(y-z)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(2|s|)^{-lkn}}{2^{l(kn-k(k+1))}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S},\\
y-z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}\end{subarray}}\left|\psi\left(\frac{y-z}{(2|s|)^{l}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(2|s|)^{-lkn}}{2^{l(kn-k(k+1))}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{S}_{2^{l}S},\\
y-z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}\end{subarray}}\frac{(2|s|)^{lL}}{((2|s|)^{l}+|y|-|z|)^{L}}

\displaystyle\leq(2|s|)^{-L(d-1)}(2|s|)^{-lkn}.

Hence, for
d\geq\epsilon|l-m|
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{d\geq\epsilon|l-m|}\|T_{d}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{d\geq\epsilon|l-m|}\sum_{y\in E_{l,d}}(2|s|)^{-L(d-1)}(2|s|)^{-lkn}

\displaystyle=\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{d\geq\epsilon|l-m|}\frac{(2|s|)^{-l(kn-1)}}{(2|s|)^{(L-1)(d-1)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}\frac{2^{-(L-1)|l-m|\epsilon^{\prime}}}{2^{l(kn-1)}}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{2^{-|l-m|\epsilon^{\prime}}}{2^{l(kn-1)}}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Next, we address the remaining part of the sum, i.e., for
d\leq\epsilon|l-m|
. Observe that integers
x
and
x-y
lie in
\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}
whenever
x\in\mathbb{A}:=\{z\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}:\text{dist}(z,{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1})\geq(2|s|)^{d+m+(l-m)}\}
and
|y|\leq(2|s|)^{d+l}
. Thus, for
x\in\mathbb{A}
and
|y|\leq(2|s|)^{d+l}
, we have
D_{m}f(x-y)-D_{m}f(x)=0
. Therefore, to estimate the
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{kn})-
norm of
T_{d}(x)
, we only require to consider those
x
which belongs to the set
U
, defined as:

U:=\{z\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}:\text{dist}(z,{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1})\leq(2|s|)^{d+m+(l-m)}\}.

We note the following bound of the cardinality of
U
, which is needed in the subsequent analysis:

|U|\leq(2|s|)^{d\eta-\eta|l-m|}|\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}|\lesssim_{s}2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}|\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}|,

for some
\eta\in(0,1)
. Using the decomposing of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{kn}
into the cubes
\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}
, we get that

\displaystyle\|T_{d}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}}|T_{d}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t}\sum_{x\in U}|T_{d}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t}\sup\limits_{x\in U}|T_{d}(x)|^{2}|U|

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}\sum_{t}|\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}|\sup_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}}|T_{d}(x)|^{2}.

Observe that, whenever
x\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}
and
|y|\leq(2|s|)^{d+l}
, the integer
x-y
belongs to the ball
B\left(\mathfrak{q}_{t}^{m-1},C(2|s|)^{m}\right)\supseteq\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}
for some constant
C>0
. By employing this, we obtain that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{m-1}}|T_{d}(x)|^{2}\lesssim\sup\limits_{x\in B\left(\mathfrak{q}_{t}^{m-1},C(2|s|)^{m}\right)}|D_{m}f(x)|^{2}.

Define the set
V_{t,m}=\{h:\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{m-1}\cap B\left(\mathfrak{q}_{t}^{m-1},C(2|s|)^{m}\right)\neq\phi\}
. Next, using the fact that, the number of integer lattice points in a ball of radius
C(2|s|)^{m}
is of the order
(2|s|)^{mkn}
one can clearly see that the cardinality of the set
V_{t,m}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
t
. Moreover, for each
h
, the number of
t
such that
h\in V_{t,m}
is uniformly bounded. Finally, we invoke (
2.7
) in (
2.6
) and incorporate the above observations to get the following:

\displaystyle\|T_{d}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}\sum_{t}\sum_{h\in V_{t,m}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{m-1}}|D_{m}f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}\sum_{h}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_{h}^{m-1}}|D_{m}f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, to complete the proof of
Lemma

2.1
, it suffices to sum in
d
for
d\leq\epsilon|l-m|
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq d\leq\epsilon|l-m|}\|T_{d}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}\sum_{0\leq d\leq\epsilon|l-m|}2^{-\eta^{\prime}|l-m|}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\bar{\eta}|l-m|}\|D_{m}f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

This concludes the proof.
∎
