From Topological Order to Mixed-State Phases: A Ground-State Probe of Fractionalized Excitations
Yunlong Zang, Yu-Bin Li, Shenghan Jiang

Abstract
How do we detect topological phases from a single ground state? Entanglement entropy and spectrum have long been the standard tools — but the reduced density matrix (RDM) itself contains far more information. We show that the RDM of a 2D topologically ordered system, expressed at the entanglement cut, realizes a 1D mixed-state phase. For the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} toric code phase, it is a 1D ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SW-SSB) phase, where deconfinement of anyons manifests as the short-range correlation of both ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} charge and ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} domain-wall in the RDM. The bulk e e - m m duality translates into a Kramers–Wannier self-duality of the SW-SSB phase. Extending the framework to gapped ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} spin liquids, the global spin-rotation symmetry manifests as an additional weak symmetry for the 1D RDM. Spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} spinons result in a cusp on the disorder parameter of spin-rotation at θ = π \theta=\pi , providing a direct, ground-state signature of symmetry fractionalization. We verify this prediction analytically using the matrix product density operator formalism and numerically for the kagome-lattice resonating valence bond state. The proposed observable requires only a single ground-state wavefunction, making it amenable to quantum simulation platforms.

Summary and discussion.

The central message of this work is that the RDM of a 2D topological phase, evaluated at an entanglement cut, realizes a 1D nontrivial mixed-state phase, whose symmetry and correlation structure directly encodes universal topological data.
For the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
toric code, the RDM at the entanglement cut realizes a 1D
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SW-SSB phase, whose self-duality under the Kramers–Wannier transformation reflects the electromagnetic duality of the bulk topological order.
By extending this framework to the gapped
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
spin liquids, we propose a direct ground-state diagnostic of spin-rotation symmetry fractionalization, where the corresponding disorder parameter develops non-analytic cusp at
\theta=\pi
.
Analytical derivations based on MPDO and numerical results for the kagome-lattice RVB state are provided to corroborate this prediction.

Looking forward, our work suggests several promising directions.
The linear correlators definition of SW-SSB phase readily generalizes to
d
-dimensional mixed states with a strong
n
-form symmetry
Zhang et al. 2025
.
In this case, both the order parameter
\langle O_{c}\rangle
(supported on a closed
n
-dimensional manifold
M_{c}
) and the disorder parameter
\langle O_{d}\rangle
(supported on a
(d-n)
-dimensional manifold with boundary
M_{d}
) obey “volume-law” scaling:

-\ln\left\lvert\langle O_{c}\rangle\right\rvert\propto V^{c}_{n+1}\penalty\ ,\penalty\ -\ln\left\lvert\langle O_{d}\rangle\right\rvert\propto V^{d}_{d-n}\penalty\ ,

(29)

where
V^{c}_{n+1}
is the minimal volume of manifolds bounded by
M_{c}
, and
V^{d}_{d-n}
is the volume of
M_{d}
.

The natural extension of the pure–mixed correspondence to topological phases with non-invertible (categorical) symmetries
Ji and Wen 2020
;
Kong et al. 2020
is particularly compelling, as the framework may shed new light on detection of such exotic orders.
Extending these ideas to chiral topological phases remains an important open problem.

The most immediate implication of this work is experimental: the disorder parameter
\langle U_{A}(\theta)\rangle
is a concrete, measurable observable that can be accessed on quantum simulation platforms.
It requires only a single ground-state wavefunction, making it a practical route to detecting signatures of fractionalized excitations in engineered quantum systems.

Comparing different definitions of SW-SSB phases

In this section, we compare two complementary definitions of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SW-SSB phase: the Wightman/fidelity correlator of charged operators, and our proposed definition based on the disorder parameter (domain-wall correlator). We highlight the hidden assumptions underlying their equivalence and provide counterexamples where they disagree.

SW-SSB from the canonical purification.

It has been conjectured that mixed-state phases of
\rho
are closely related to pure-state phases of its canonical purification
\lvert\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\!\rangle

For a mixed state
\rho
with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
strong symmetry generated by
\prod_{j}X_{j}
, the purification
\lvert\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\!\rangle
possesses a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, acting on the ket and bra degrees of freedom, respectively.
In this framework, the SW-SSB phase of
\rho
corresponds to spontaneous breaking of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
down to the diagonal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
subgroup in the purified state.
This diagonal SSB phase of
\lvert\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\!\rangle
is characterized by long-range correlations of operators charged under
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
but neutral under the diagonal subgroup, while diagonal-charged operators remain short-range correlated.
Concretely,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\sqrt{\rho}Z_{i}Z_{j}\sqrt{\rho}Z_{i}Z_{j})

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle\sqrt{\rho}\rvert(Z_{i}\otimes Z_{i})\cdot(Z_{j}\otimes Z_{j})\lvert\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\!\rangle\sim O(1)\penalty\ ,

(S29)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\rho Z_{i}Z_{j})

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle\sqrt{\rho}\rvert(Z_{i}\otimes\hat{1})\cdot(Z_{j}\otimes\hat{1})\lvert\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\!\rangle\sim\exp(-\left\lvert i-j\right\rvert/\xi)

The first line is the Wightman correlator

;
Weinstein 2025
, proposed as a diagnostic for spontaneous breaking of the strong
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry. It has been shown to be equivalent to the original fidelity-based definition of SW-SSB
Lessa et al. 2025
.

\rho

When do the two definitions agree?

The equivalence between the Wightman correlator (Eq. (
S29
), first line) and the volume-law disorder parameter (Eq. (
S30
)) rests on two implicit assumptions:

1.

\lvert\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\!\rangle
shares the qualitative properties of a ground state of a local Hamiltonian, so that it is meaningful to speak of its quantum phase.

2.

In such a local-Hamiltonian ground state, long-range order of a charged operator is a sufficient and necessary condition for volume-law scaling of the corresponding disorder parameter.

Both assumptions can fail, as we now illustrate. Below we present two examples where the Wightman-based and disorder-parameter-based definitions of SW-SSB are inequivalent.

Example 1: violation of locality (Assumption
1
).

Consider the mixed state from Ref.
Lessa et al. 2025
,

\rho=\frac{1}{2}\ket{++\cdots}\bra{++\cdots}+\frac{1}{2^{L+1}}(\hat{1}+\prod_{j=1}^{L}X_{j}).

(S31)

Here the charge correlator vanishes,
\langle Z_{i}Z_{j}\rangle=0
, while the Wightman correlator is long-range ordered:
\langle\!\langle\sqrt{\rho}\rvert(Z_{i}\otimes{Z}_{i})\cdot(Z_{j}\otimes{Z}_{j})\lvert\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\!\rangle\sim O(1)
.
The Wightman correlator thus diagnoses SW-SSB.
However, the disorder parameter saturates at a constant,
\langle X_{A}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}
, violating volume-law scaling.
The discrepancy arises because
\lvert\rho\rangle\!\rangle
is not a ground state of any local Hamiltonian, so Assumption
1
does not hold.

Example 2: violation of the order–disorder connection (Assumption
2
).

The decohered Ising model provides a counterexample to Assumption
2

Sahay et al. 2025
.
Starting from the fully polarized state
\ket{+++\dots}
and applying a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
strong symmetric quantum channel
\mathcal{E}=\prod_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathcal{E}_{ij}
with

\mathcal{E}_{ij}(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+pZ_{i}Z_{j}\cdot\rho\cdot Z_{i}Z_{j},

(S32)

the Wightman correlator exhibits a transition
Lessa et al. 2025
:

\mathrm{Tr}(\sqrt{\rho}Z_{i}Z_{j}\sqrt{\rho}Z_{i}Z_{j})\sim\begin{cases}\exp(-\left\lvert i-j\right\rvert/\xi),&p<p_{c}\\
O(1),&p>p_{c}.\end{cases}

(S33)

To compute the disorder parameter, we rewrite
\rho
in the bond-variable representation,

\rho=[p(1-p)]^{N_{b}/2}\cdot\sum_{\{\mu_{b}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\beta\sum_{b}\mu_{b}}\ket{\{x_{v}=\prod_{b\in v}\mu_{b}\}}\bra{\{x_{v}\}},

(S34)

where
\mu_{ij}=-1
indicates that bond
b=\langle ij\rangle
has been acted on by
Z_{i}Z_{j}
, and
\mathrm{e}^{2\beta}=\frac{1-p}{p}
(equivalently
\tanh\beta=1-2p
). The disorder operator is diagonal in this basis, giving

\displaystyle\langle X_{A}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{0}}\sum_{\{\mu_{b}\}}\left(\prod_{b^{\prime}\in\partial A}\mu_{b^{\prime}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\beta\sum_{b}\mu_{b}}

(S35)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{0}}\left(\prod_{b\in\partial A}\sum_{\mu_{b}=\pm 1}\mu_{b}\,\mathrm{e}^{\beta\mu_{b}}\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{b\notin\partial A}\sum_{\mu_{b}=\pm 1}\mathrm{e}^{\beta\mu_{b}}\right)

\displaystyle=(\tanh\beta)^{S_{\partial A}}=(1-2p)^{S_{\partial A}},

with
Z_{0}=\sum_{\{\mu_{b}\}}\mathrm{e}^{\beta\sum_{b}\mu_{b}}=(2\cosh{\beta})^{N_{b}}
. The disorder parameter obeys area-law scaling for all
p

p>p_{c}

These examples demonstrate that the Wightman/fidelity definition and the disorder-parameter definition of SW-SSB are not universally equivalent.

The toric code possesses a electromagnetic (

In the main text, we identified the 1D mixed state

In this section, we show that it descends to a Kramers-Wannier (KW) self-duality of

We provide the explicit mapping between the two representations of the boundary algebra, discuss its physical interpretation, and show how it acts on the phase diagram.

Boundary algebra and the 1D representation.

We begin by recalling the Hamiltonian decomposition from the main text. The toric code Hamiltonian on a bipartitioned system is

H=-\sum_{p}\prod_{i\in p}Z_{i}-\sum_{v}\prod_{i\in v}X_{i}\equiv H_{A}+H_{\bar{A}}+H_{\partial A\cup\partial\bar{A}}

The
\tau

A 1D spin chain provides a faithful representation of the boundary algebra:

V
Correlators and virtual leg symmetries in MPDO

The RDM at the entanglement cut of a 2D topologically ordered phase realizes a 1D SW-SSB phase, which is diagnosed by the long-range behavior of the charged-operator correlator and the domain-wall correlator.
In this section, we show how these correlators are controlled by virtual-leg symmetries of the underlying tensor network representation.
We first review the pure-state MPS formalism for the 1D
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Ising chain, where the key concepts — virtual leg symmetries and transfer matrix degeneracy — are most transparent.
We then generalize to the MPDO formalism and show that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SW-SSB phase is characterized by an analogous virtual-leg symmetry.

V.1
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Ising chain and MPS

We consider the transverse field Ising model in 1+1D, where

H=\sum_{j=1}^{L}-JZ_{j}Z_{j+1}-hX_{j}

(S58)

with periodic boundary condition imposed.
The system exhibits a global
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{1,g\}
symmetry, generated by
\prod_{j}X_{j}
.
For
J>h
, the system is in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SSB phase, characterized by a two-fold degeneracy of ground states in the thermodynamic limit, which are two cat states with distinct
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
quantum number
\pm 1
.
In these two cat states, while
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-charged operators exhibit vanishing expectation values, they are long-range correlated in the thermodynamic limit
L\to\infty
:

\langle Z_{1}\rangle=0\penalty\ ,\quad\lim_{l\to\infty}\langle Z_{1}Z_{l}\rangle\to C\neq 0

(S59)

We now represent the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
even cat state using MPS,

\ket{\psi_{e}}=\sum_{\{j\}}\mathrm{Tr}[\cdots M_{j_{l}}\cdot M_{j_{l+1}}\cdots]\ket{\dots j_{l}j_{l+1}\dots}

(S60)

where
M_{j,\alpha\beta}
is a three leg local tensor.
Imposing
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry on the MPS requires
M
satisfies the symmetry condition:

\sum_{j^{\prime}}X_{jj^{\prime}}\cdot M_{j^{\prime}}=W(g)\cdot M_{j}\cdot W^{\dagger}(g)

(S61)

where the gauge transformation
W(g)
satisfies
[W(g)]^{2}=\hat{1}
.

Physical observables, such as those in Eq. (
S59
), are calculated via the transfer matrix
T_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime},\beta\beta^{\prime}}\equiv\sum_{j}M_{j;\alpha\beta}\cdot M^{*}_{j;\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}
, which inherits the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry generated by
W(g)\otimes W^{*}(g)
.
Assuming
T
possesses a unique dominant eigenvalue (normalized to
1
), with corresponding left and right eigenvectors
\left(v_{l}\right|
and
\left|v_{r}\right)
, Eq. (
S59
) are then expressed as

\langle Z_{1}\rangle=\left(v_{l}\right|T(Z)\left|v_{r}\right)\penalty\ ,\quad\lim_{l\to\infty}\langle Z_{1}Z_{l}\rangle=[\left(v_{l}\right|T(Z)\left|v_{r}\right)]^{2}

(S62)

where
[T(Z)]_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime},\beta\beta^{\prime}}=\sum_{jj^{\prime}}M_{j,\alpha\beta}\cdot Z_{jj^{\prime}}M_{j^{\prime};\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}
.
Both terms vanish, since
T(Z)
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
odd while
\left(v_{l}\right|
and
\left|v_{r}\right)
share the same
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
quantum number.

To recover the long-range correlation of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-charged operators, a two-fold degeneracy in the dominant eigenvectors of
T
with opposite
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
quantum numbers is required.
Denoting these (right) eigenstates as
\left|v^{e}_{r}\right)
(
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
even) and
\left|v^{o}_{r}\right)
(
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
odd), Eq. (
S59
) become

\displaystyle\langle Z_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\left(v^{e}_{l}\right|T(Z)\left|v^{e}_{r}\right)+\left(v^{o}_{l}\right|T(Z)\left|v^{o}_{r}\right)\Big]=0

(S63)

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\langle Z_{1}Z_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathrm{Tr}\Big[(\left|v^{e}_{r}\right)\left(v^{e}_{l}\right|+\left|v^{o}_{r}\right)\left(v^{o}_{l}\right|)\cdot T(Z)\cdot(\left|v^{e}_{r}\right)\left(v^{e}_{l}\right|+\left|v^{o}_{r}\right)\left(v^{o}_{l}\right|)\cdot T(Z)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\left(v^{e}_{l}\right|T(Z)\left|v^{o}_{r}\right)\left(v^{o}_{l}\right|T(Z)\left|v^{e}_{r}\right)+\left(v^{o}_{l}\right|T(Z)\left|v^{e}_{r}\right)\left(v^{e}_{l}\right|T(Z)\left|v^{o}_{r}\right)\Big]\sim O(1)

where the factor
\frac{1}{2}
comes from wavefunction normalization
\mathrm{Tr}(T^{L})=\left(v_{l}^{e}\middle|v_{r}^{e}\right)+\left(v_{l}^{o}\middle|v_{r}^{o}\right)
.

This degeneracy are most naturally enforced by introducing an additional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry acting exclusively on virtual legs, satisfying

M_{j}=J\cdot M_{j}\cdot J^{\dagger}\penalty\ ,\quad\text{where}\penalty\ \penalty\ J^{2}=\hat{1}\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \{W(g),J\}=0

(S64)

Physically,
J
can be viewed as the “renormalized”
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
charge.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
virtual symmetry imposes additional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry on transfer matrix
T
, generated by
J\otimes\hat{1}
and
\hat{1}\otimes J^{*}
, both anti-commuting with
W(g)\otimes W^{*}(g)
.
Therefore, we have
\left|v_{r}^{o}\right)=(J\otimes\hat{1})\left|v_{r}^{e}\right)
.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
odd ground state of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SSB phase can then be constructed by inserting
J

\ket{\psi_{o}}=\sum_{\{j\}}\mathrm{Tr}[\cdots M_{j_{l}}\cdot J\cdot M_{j_{l+1}}\cdots]\ket{\dots j_{l}j_{l+1}\dots}

(S65)

As an example, when
h=0
in Eq. (
S58
), the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
even ground state is
\ket{\psi_{e}}=\ket{\dots\uparrow\uparrow\dots}+\ket{\dots\downarrow\downarrow\dots}
, represented by an MPS with local tensor
M=\ket{\uparrow}\left|\uparrow\right)\left(\uparrow\right|+\ket{\downarrow}\left|\downarrow\right)\left(\downarrow\right|
.
Here,
W(g)=X
and
J=Z
.

\ket{\psi_{o}}=\ket{\dots\uparrow\uparrow\dots}-\ket{\dots\downarrow\downarrow\dots}
is obtained by Eq. (
S65
).

We now calculate the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
domain wall correlator
\langle\prod_{j=1}^{l}X_{j}\rangle
using transfer matrix:

\langle\prod_{j=1}^{l}X_{j}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[(W(g)\otimes\hat{1})\cdot T^{l}\cdot(W^{\dagger}(g)\otimes\hat{1})\cdot T^{L-l}\right]\Big/\mathrm{Tr}(T^{L})

(S66)

For the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetric phase (
h>J
), the transfer matrix has a unique dominant eigenvector, and Eq. (

) reduces in the thermodynamic limit

, giving a non-zero constant — the disorder parameter is long-range ordered.
For the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SSB phase, the disorder parameter evaluates as

\displaystyle\langle\prod_{j=1}^{l}X_{j}\rangle=

\displaystyle\Bigg[\sum_{\alpha,\beta=e,o}\left(v_{l}^{\alpha}\right|W(g)\otimes\hat{1}\left|v_{r}^{\beta}\right)(v_{l}^{\beta}|W^{\dagger}(g)\otimes\hat{1}\left|v_{r}^{\alpha}\right)

(S67)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=e,o}\left(v_{l}^{\alpha}\right|{W(g)\otimes\hat{1}}\cdot\left(\sum_{a}\lambda_{a}^{l-1}\left|w_{a,r}\right)\left(w_{a,l}\right|\right)\cdot{W^{\dagger}(g)\otimes\hat{1}}\left|v_{r}^{\alpha}\right)\Bigg]\Bigg/\mathrm{Tr}(T^{L})

where

are right/left eigenstates of

with eigenvalue
\lambda_{a}
satisfying

Crucially, symmetry

commutes with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

, ensuring
\left|v_{r}^{e}\right)
and
\left|v_{r}^{o}\right)
share the same quantum number under
J\otimes J^{*}
.
However, as
W(g)\otimes\hat{1}
is odd under

, the first term in Eq. (
S67
) vanishes.
We then conclude that for MPS with virtual leg symmetry

\langle\prod_{j=1}^{l}X_{j}\rangle\sim\exp(-l/\xi)
, with
\xi=-(\ln\lambda_{1})^{-1}
(assuming
\left|w_{1,r}\right)
and
\left|v_{r}^{e/o}\right)
carry opposite quantum numbers under
J\otimes J^{*}
).
