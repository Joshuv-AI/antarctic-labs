Microbes facilitate crude oil production in plateau oilfields
Authors: Peiyao Sun, Shasha Ma, Wei Ma, Chunchun Han, Linxu Chen, Xianke Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Microbial-enhanced oil recovery has been applied in plateau oilfields for six years.•Microbes play a crucial role in crude oil production in plateau oilfields.•Microbes exhibit remarkable applications in unconventional oil reservoirs.•Bacillus is the core microbe for enhancing oil recovery in plateau oilfields.Keywords: Microbial-enhanced oil recovery, Bacillus, Microbial-oil displacement agent, Unconventional reservoirs, Plateau oilfields

In 2025, domestic crude oil production reached 216 million tons, an increase of 3 million tons compared with that in 2024. This is supported by the strategic development of tertiary oil recovery programs in petroleum engineering, such as the implementation of tertiary oil recovery techniques in mature oilfields and unconventional reservoirs [1,2]. Tertiary oil recovery techniques are generally categorized into microbial, chemical, thermal, and gas flooding [3,4]. Since the introduction of the concept of microbial-enhanced oil recovery (MEOR), this technology has been under development for a century [5]. MEOR has attracted widespread attention owing to its competitive application costs and environmental friendliness compared with chemical, gas, and thermal flooding [6,7]. Over 30 years of MEOR technology field tests have been conducted in China, achieving remarkable results [6]. However, high-salinity reservoir environments, large temperature differences, and unconventional reservoirs have limited MEOR field applications [8,9].

Although China's energy sector has entered a stage of high-quality development, oil demand will remain high for a long time, and the situation regarding supply security remains severe [10]. Amid the transition of industrial energy toward green and low-carbon development, the Qinghai oilfield adhered to innovation-driven development by adopting MEOR as a strategic alternative technology. Breakthroughs have been achieved in increasing and stabilizing oil production (Fig. 1), reducing costs, and achieving sustainable development [1,[11], [12], [13]]. Specifically, a series of oil displacement agents and their technologies for field application have been developed in plateau oilfields to produce surfactants, degrade hydrocarbons, and adjust the oil reservoir microbial community structure using salt–alkali-tolerant, mesophilic, and psychrotrophic Bacillus velezensis and Bacillus siamensis as core strains. These technologies cover multiple key aspects of oilfield production, such as maintenance, stimulation, and hydrogen sulfide (H2S) control in oil exploitation.

Annual cumulative increment in oil production and annual number of operating oil wells with microbial-enhanced oil recovery.

The consortium QZ-10 was obtained by enriching an oil–water sample from the high saline–alkali reservoir environments of the Qinghai oilfield, which exhibited emulsifying, viscosity-reducing, and paraffin-resistance ability, as well as degradation of heavy hydrocarbons. In 2019–2020, after the injection of the microbial consortium QZ-10 fluid, paraffin wax was removed from 80.95% of the tested oil wells after 140 days in the Qinghai oilfield. The average well-washing cycle was extended by 30 days, with a cumulative oil increment of 1100 tons [12]. The oil reservoirs screening criteria (SY/T 6888–2012) recommends applying MEOR technologies to sandstone oil reservoirs with a permeability ≥ 50 mD, pH of 6–8, salinity ≤ 150,000 mg/L, and crude oil viscosity (at 50 °C) ≤ 3000 mPa·s. In 2020–2021, halotolerant Bacillus velezensis BSA1 and its surfactant were used in low-permeability oil reservoirs (permeability of 5–50 mD, pH of 6–9, salinity of 104,627–256,752 mg/L) in the Huatugou oilfield using the huff-puff technique. After reducing oil–water interfacial tension and altering the wettability of rock surfaces through biosurfactants, this study showed that B. velezensis BSA1 could sustainably enhance oil recovery in low-permeability reservoirs for approximately one year, achieving a cumulative oil production of over 500 m3 [11]. Through field trials of microbial flooding with different microbial concentrations and addition times, an alternative steady-state theory of microbial communities in oil reservoirs was revealed, and the mechanism of enhancing oil recovery by microbial community status was elucidated, providing a theoretical basis for subsequent MEOR field applications [14].

In 2023, owing to its functions of emulsifying, viscosity-reducing, and reducing heavy oil components, B. velezensis B6 was used in the Yingdong, Yuejin, and Huatugou oilfields through the huff-puff operation and paraffin wax removal operation. This resulted in an average delay of 47 days in the well washing cycle and a cumulative oil increase of 1460.36 tons [13]. In addition, in 2024, an oil displacement agent consisting of B. velezensis B6 (spore content of over 70%), its metabolites, and nutrients was tested in low- to medium-permeability and high-salinity reservoirs, which resulted in a cumulative oil production increase of 4573.82 m3 in 270 days and an overall input–output ratio of 1:6.6 [1]. These studies and application cases demonstrate that field tests conducted in oil reservoirs beyond the recommended parameters yielded positive results.

Using the core MEOR mechanisms of petroleum degradation, emulsification, and viscosity reduction, in 2025, microbial wax removal and prevention were carried out in 1353 oil wells, with a cumulative operation exceeding 22,000 wells and a coverage rate of 40%. This resulted in a reduction of >3700 hot-washing operations, directly saving over CNY 11 million in hot-washing and related maintenance expenses. More importantly, the production efficiency of the oil wells was effectively improved, with an average annual increase in production time of >10 days for each well, resulting in an increase of over 15,000 tons of oil. A total of 167 huff-puff operations were conducted using oil displacement agents consisting of B. velezensis and B. siamensis, their‌ metabolites, and nutrients, with an efficiency rate exceeding 70%, resulting in an average daily increase of 0.72 tons of oil per well and a cumulative increase of over 8000 tons of oil. Based on the alternative steady states theory of microbial communities, a pilot test was conducted on the treatment of H₂S in single wells and gathering systems, successfully reducing the H₂S content in six high-sulfur oil wells to zero, with an effective duration of over 140 days. In addition, microbial treatment was implemented in the Huatugou and Qigequan gathering systems, where the H₂S concentration was stably maintained within the safe range of 10 ppm in oil production, providing a “microbial solution” for safe and green production in oilfields. Economic benefit accounting shows that the series of MEOR technologies implemented in the Qinghai oilfield in 2025 increased oil production by 23,000 tons (Fig. 1), generating direct economic benefits of nearly CNY 60 million.

The MEOR practice in the Qinghai oilfield is a typical case of microbes driving the high-quality development of plateau oilfields, an important technical means for maintaining long-term stable operation of oil wells and supporting stable oil production in oilfields. It is also a significant measure for ensuring energy security and building a new type of green and low-carbon energy system. By 2026, with the goal of establishing a demonstration base for MEOR applications in PetroChina, we plan to continue enhancing this practice. The coverage rate of microbial wax removal and prevention is planned to be continuously expanded to 60%, with microbial huff-puff implemented in 200 wells and microbial flooding in 10 well groups annually.

In conclusion, MEOR in the Qinghai oilfield highlights the significance of microbes in crude oil production in plateau oilfields and provides a powerful technical foundation and theoretical basis for the further promotion of MEOR. Although these MEOR results are encouraging, particularly for applications in unconventional oil reservoirs with high-salt content and low permeability, several challenges merit further investigation. Current achievements depend entirely on native microorganisms, and studies on genetic engineering and enzyme-enhanced MEOR efficiency are currently limited. With the development of synthetic biology, genetically engineered microorganisms that combine the traits of different genes or strains to enhance oil recovery technologies is a better choice.
