Hawking–Page Universality, Thermodynamic Dipoles and Categorical Defects
Emilio Torrente-Lujan

Abstract
We reconsider the Hawking–Page transition using the common thermodynamic vector field whose zeros include the Davies and Hawking–Page points. In the elementary AdS branch their winding numbers are w D = − 1 w_{\rm D}=-1 and w HP = + 1 w_{\rm HP}=+1 , so the pair has zero total charge but a non-zero signed first moment. After normalization by the Davies scales this moment gives the familiar universal ratios C S C_{S} and C T C_{T} ; in four dimensions C S = 2 C_{S}=2 and C T = 2 / 3 − 1 C_{T}=2/\sqrt{3}-1 . We check the construction for Schwarzschild–AdS, grand-canonical Reissner–Nordström–AdS, charged non-rotating black holes in arbitrary dimension, and Kerr–AdS at fixed angular velocity. The same reduced geometry gives a barrier B = 1 / 3 B=1/3 in four dimensions and B ⁡ ( d ) = 1 / [ ( d − 1 ) ​ ( d − 3 ) ] B(d)=1/[(d-1)(d-3)] . Finally we propose a formulation involving a defect-resolved version for categorical or non-invertible symmetry sectors.



1
Introduction

Black-hole thermodynamics bridges gravity and statistical mechanics. The four laws of black-hole mechanics
(
Bardeen et al. 1973
)
, the entropy formula of Bekenstein
(
Bekenstein 1973
)
, Hawking radiation
(
Hawking 1975
)
, and the Hawking–Page transition in anti-de Sitter space
(
Hawking and Page 1983
)
are standard pieces of this bridge. In holography the Hawking–Page transition is read as a confinement/deconfinement transition of the boundary gauge theory on a compact space
(
Witten 1998
)
. Charged or rotating AdS black holes add the usual thermodynamic complications: ensemble dependence, metastable branches, swallow tails and extended phase diagrams
(
Chamblin et al. 1999a
;
Chamblin et al. 1999b
;
Caldarelli et al. 2000
;
Kubiznak and Mann 2012
;
Kubiznak et al. 2017
)
.

There is, however, a rather simple numerical structure hidden in these phase diagrams. Let
(S_{\mathrm{D}},T_{\mathrm{D}})
denote the point at which the temperature is minimal along the black-hole branch. In the examples used below this is also the elementary Davies point, where the corresponding heat capacity diverges
(
Davies 1977
)
. Let
(S_{\mathrm{HP}},T_{\mathrm{HP}})
denote the Hawking–Page point. Then the ratios

\displaystyle C_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S_{\mathrm{HP}}-S_{\mathrm{D}}}{S_{\mathrm{D}}},

(1)

\displaystyle C_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\mathrm{HP}}-T_{\mathrm{D}}}{T_{\mathrm{D}}}

were first isolated in Ref,
Belhaj et al. 2020
. For four-dimensional non-rotating AdS black holes one obtains
C_{S}=2
and
C_{T}=2/\sqrt{3}-1
. Related universal structures, including a dual relation between minimum temperatures and Hawking–Page temperatures in neighbouring dimensions, were discussed in Ref.
Wei et al. 2020
.

A second, more recent, line of work has described black-hole thermodynamics in topological terms. In the off-shell free-energy landscape, black-hole branches can be treated as thermodynamic defects carrying integer winding numbers
(
Wei et al. 2022
)
. This idea has been used for Hawking–Page transitions, critical points, Born–Infeld and Rényi examples, and for broader topological classifications of black-hole families
(
Yerra et al. 2022
;
Yerra et al. 2023
;
Barzi et al. 2024
;
Fang et al. 2023
;
Wei et al. 2024
;
Wu et al. 2025
;
Zhu et al. 2025
;
Chen et al. 2025
)
. Especially useful for our purpose is the common vector field of
Hazarika et al. 2025
. Its zeros include both Davies-type points and Hawking–Page points. In the elementary diagram the Davies point has
w_{\rm D}=-1
, whereas the Hawking–Page point has
w_{\rm HP}=+1
.

In this work we ask how the older universal ratios look in this topological language. The answer is almost forced by the signs. The two charges form a neutral pair,

\displaystyle w_{\rm D}+w_{\rm HP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(2)

but they do not sit at the same thermodynamic point. Their first signed moments are

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{HP}}S_{\mathrm{HP}}+w_{\mathrm{D}}S_{\mathrm{D}}

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{HP}}-S_{\mathrm{D}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{HP}}T_{\mathrm{HP}}+w_{\mathrm{D}}T_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{HP}}-T_{\mathrm{D}}.

Dividing by the Davies scales gives back Eq.(
1
). Thus the constants
C_{S}
and
C_{T}
can be read as the entropy and temperature components of a thermodynamic topological dipole.

The winding numbers are genuine topological charges, whereas the components in Eq.(
3
) depend on the thermodynamic coordinates used to locate the zeros. The point of the construction is therefore
not to claim neccesarily a new homotopy invariant, but
to keep in one notation the integer orientation of the defects and the scale-free thermodynamic separations. In that sense the Hawking–Page constants become part of the same bookkeeping as the winding numbers.

The structure of the paper is the following. In Sec.
2
we review the common vector field and give the local sign rule for Hawking–Page and Davies zeros. In Sec.
3
we define the signed moments and show how
C_{S}
,
C_{T}
and the normalized barrier depend only on the reduced shape of the free-energy curve. We then compute the moments in four dimensions, in arbitrary spacetime dimension for charged non-rotating black holes, and perturbatively for Kerr–AdS at fixed angular velocity. The barrier constant is

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}(S_{\mathrm{D}})}{T_{\mathrm{D}}S_{\mathrm{D}}},

(4)

with
B=1/3
in four dimensions and
B=1/[(d-1)(d-3)]
in the higher-dimensional charged family. Finally, we discuss two extensions: a multipole description for diagrams with more than one Davies/Hawking–Page pair, and a tentative categorical refinement based on defect-resolved partition functions.

Finally we propose another direction of study.
Generalized global symmetries are implemented by topological operators
(
Gaiotto et al. 2015
)
; non-invertible symmetries replace group-like multiplication by categorical fusion rules
(
Bhardwaj et al. 2023
;
Shao 2023
)
; and several holographic or brane realizations are now known
(
Apruzzi et al. 2023
;
Gutperle et al. 2024
;
Antinucci et al. 2025
)
. Since the Hawking–Page transition is sensitive to the topology of the Euclidean thermal circle, it is natural to conjecture whether inserting such defects shifts or splits the transition temperature. In a first approximation, we define quantities that can be calculated in models where the relevant defect sectors and both Euclidean saddles are under analytic control.

2
Davies and Hawking–Page zeros

Let us work in units in which
G=\hbar=c=k_{B}=1
. In four-dimensional formulae it will often be convenient to use a reduced entropy
S=r_{+}^{2}
and a reduced temperature differing from the physical Hawking temperature by an overall factor of
\pi
. Such constant rescalings do not affect the ratios
C_{S}
,
C_{T}
, or the winding signs.

Let us consider a one-parameter black-hole branch at fixed ensemble variables
Y_{i}
, such as fixed pressure, fixed electric potential, or fixed angular velocity. Let
\mathcal{E}(S;Y_{i})
be the appropriate Legendre-transformed energy whose derivative gives the ensemble temperature,

\displaystyle T(S;Y_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial S}\right)_{Y_{i}}.

(5)

For example,
\mathcal{E}=M
in a canonical neutral ensemble, while
\mathcal{E}=M-\Phi Q
or
\mathcal{E}=M-\Omega J
in the corresponding grand-canonical ensembles. The on-shell free energy is

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(S;Y_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(S;Y_{i})-T(S;Y_{i})S.

(6)

Along the black-hole branch,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial S}\right)_{Y_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-S\left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial S}\right)_{Y_{i}}.

(7)

The Davies-type point considered in this work is the minimum-temperature point,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial S}\right)_{Y_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(8)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}T}{\partial S^{2}}\right)_{Y_{i}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

where the heat capacity
C_{Y}=T(\partial S/\partial T)_{Y}
diverges. We denote it by
(S_{\mathrm{D}},T_{\mathrm{D}})
. The Hawking–Page point is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(S_{\mathrm{HP}};Y_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(9)

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{HP}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(S_{\mathrm{HP}};Y_{i}),

where the black-hole saddle and thermal AdS have equal free energy.

The basic picture is shown in Fig.(
1
).
The same point that minimizes
T(S)
is the maximum of the on-shell free energy, and the Hawking–Page transition occurs where that free energy crosses zero.

Following the common-field construction of

)

(S,\theta)
by

\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}(S,\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\varphi^{S},\varphi^{\theta}\right)

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{S}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}^{2}}{\partial S},-\cot\theta\,\csc\theta\right).

Equivalently, if the thermodynamic functions are more naturally expressed in terms of the horizon radius
r_{+}
, one may use

\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}(r_{+},\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{r_{+}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}^{2}}{\partial r_{+}},-\cot\theta\,\csc\theta\right).

(11)

The second component vanishes only at
\theta=\pi/2
. The first component satisfies

\displaystyle\varphi^{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathcal{F}}{S}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial S}=-2\mathcal{F}\frac{\partial T}{\partial S}=-2\mathcal{F}\frac{T}{C_{Y}},

(12)

where Eq.(
7
) was used. Thus the isolated zeros of Eq.(
10
) are precisely the points at which

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{1}{C_{Y}}=0,

(13)

namely Hawking–Page or Davies-type points.

A consequence of Eq.(
7
) is worth isolating, since it underlies the barrier of Sec.
4.3
and the sign rule below. Because
\mathcal{F}_{S}=-S\,T_{S}
, the stationary points of the on-shell free energy coincide with those of the temperature: every Davies point is an extremum of
\mathcal{F}
. Differentiating once more gives
\mathcal{F}_{SS}=-(T_{S}+S\,T_{SS})
, so at a Davies point (
T_{S}=0
) one has
\mathcal{F}_{SS}=-S\,T_{SS}
. A
minimum
of the temperature is therefore a
maximum
of the free energy. In the standard portrait of Fig.(
1
) the Davies point is exactly the top of the free-energy barrier separating thermal AdS from the black hole.

The winding number of a zero
z_{i}
is

\displaystyle w_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\Gamma_{i}}\mathrm{d}\Omega,

(14)

where

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arctan\frac{n^{2}}{n^{1}},

\displaystyle n^{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi^{a}}{\sqrt{\varphi^{b}\varphi^{b}}},

and where
\Gamma_{i}
is a small positively oriented contour around the zero. For a simple zero this reduces to the sign of the Jacobian,

\displaystyle w_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{sgn}\left[\det\left(\frac{\partial\varphi^{a}}{\partial x^{b}}\right)_{z_{i}}\right],

(15)

\displaystyle x^{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(S,\theta).

For the elementary branch the local charge assignment is simple. Assume a single locally unstable small-black-hole branch followed by a stable large-black-hole branch. If the Davies point is a local minimum of
T(S)
with
\mathcal{F}(S_{\mathrm{D}})>0
, and the Hawking–Page point lies on the stable branch with
(\partial T/\partial S)_{S_{\mathrm{HP}}}>0
, then

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1,

(16)

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{HP}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+1.

The check is completely local. Near
\theta=\pi/2+\delta\theta
, one has

-\cot\theta\csc\theta=\delta\theta+O(\delta\theta^{3}),

so the angular Jacobian contributes a positive sign.
At the Davies point,
T_{S}=0
and
T_{SS}>0
. Differentiating Eq.(
12

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial\varphi^{S}}{\partial S}\right|_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\mathcal{F}(S_{\mathrm{D}})T_{SS}(S_{\mathrm{D}})<0.

(17)

Thus
w_{\mathrm{D}}=-1
. At the Hawking–Page point,
\mathcal{F}=0
and
T_{S}>0
. Using
\mathcal{F}_{S}=-ST_{S}
,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial\varphi^{S}}{\partial S}\right|_{\mathrm{HP}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\mathcal{F}_{S}(S_{\mathrm{HP}})T_{S}(S_{\mathrm{HP}})

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2S_{\mathrm{HP}}\left[T_{S}(S_{\mathrm{HP}})\right]^{2}>0,

and therefore
w_{\mathrm{HP}}=+1
. The same computation gives the winding number of any simple transition zero in terms of purely local data, which is what one needs for phase diagrams with several Davies and Hawking–Page points.

It is useful to record the same calculation as a local sign rule. At a simple zero of the common field Eq.(
10
),
we have

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{HP}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+1,

(19)

for a simple Hawking–Page zero with
T_{S}\neq 0
,
and

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\operatorname{sgn}\mathcal{F}(S_{\mathrm{D}})\,\operatorname{sgn}T_{SS}(S_{\mathrm{D}}),

(20)

for a simple Davies zero.

The angular factor is the same positive factor as above. At a Hawking–Page zero
\mathcal{F}=0
, so

\partial_{S}\varphi^{S}=-2\mathcal{F}_{S}T_{S}=2S\,T_{S}^{2}>0

whenever
T_{S}\neq 0
. At a Davies zero
T_{S}=0
, so
\partial_{S}\varphi^{S}=-2\mathcal{F}\,T_{SS}
, whose sign
is

-\operatorname{sgn}\mathcal{F}\,\operatorname{sgn}T_{SS}.

Thus a Davies point carries the usual charge
-1
only when it is a temperature minimum on the high-free-energy side (
\mathcal{F}>0
), and it flips to
+1
when it sits in the black-hole-dominated region (
\mathcal{F}<0
) or becomes a temperature maximum, as happens along reentrant or multi-branch curves. The charge configuration of an arbitrary phase diagram can therefore be read directly from the signs of
\mathcal{F}
and
T_{SS}
at its transition points, with no Jacobian evaluation. A non-simple zero, at which a Davies and a Hawking–Page point merge (
\mathcal{F}=0
and
T_{S}=0
simultaneously), is precisely where two charges annihilate and the dipole degenerates.

For Schwarzschild–AdS the horizon-radius version of the field gives

\displaystyle\varphi^{r_{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\left(3r_{+}^{4}-4r_{+}^{2}+1\right),

(21)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\cot\theta\,\csc\theta,

with zeros at
(r_{+},\theta)=(1/\sqrt{3},\pi/2)
and
(1,\pi/2)
. The normalized vector portrait is shown in Fig.(

). The first zero is the Davies point, with charge
-1
, and the second is the Hawking–Page point, with charge
+1
.

3
Universal ratios as signed moments

Let us now connect the topological charges to the universal ratios. Let
z_{i}
denote isolated Hawking–Page or Davies zeros of the common field, and let
w_{i}
be their winding numbers. For any thermodynamic coordinate
X
evaluated at the zeros, define the signed first moment

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}w_{i}X_{i}.

(22)

For a single Davies/Hawking–Page pair,

\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{tot}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{D}}+w_{\mathrm{HP}}

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

so the pair is neutral.
Nevertheless its first moments are non-zero:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{HP}}-S_{\mathrm{D}},

(24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{HP}}-T_{\mathrm{D}}.

For later reference we define the normalized dipole ratios of a single Hawking–Page/Davies pair, with charges
w_{\mathrm{HP}}=+1
and
w_{\mathrm{D}}=-1
, by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{P}_{S}}{S_{\mathrm{D}}},

(25)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{P}_{T}}{T_{\mathrm{D}}}.

With this notation,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{S},\qquad\mathfrak{C}_{T}=C_{T}.

(26)

In this precise, coordinate-level sense, the constants
C_{S}
and
C_{T}
are the two components of the normalized Davies/Hawking–Page dipole. The individual ratios and the winding numbers are known from earlier work. They are two components of a single moment hierarchy.

Note that the distinction between topological charge and thermodynamic moment is essential. The integers
w_{i}
are invariant under smooth deformations that do not create, annihilate, or merge zeros, whereas the coordinates
S_{i}
and
T_{i}
are ordinary thermodynamic data. The ratios Eq.(
25
) are therefore mixed objects: topological in orientation and thermodynamic in magnitude. Their universality in the examples below comes from the cancellation of ensemble scales, not from homotopy alone.

The Fig.(
3
) shows the same statement on the Schwarzschild–AdS curves. The sign of the winding number fixes the orientation of the separation from
D
to HP.

It is useful to introduce also the ratio variables

\displaystyle R_{S}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{S_{\mathrm{HP}}}{S_{\mathrm{D}}}=1+C_{S},

\displaystyle R_{T}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\mathrm{HP}}}{T_{\mathrm{D}}}=1+C_{T}.

The four-dimensional non-rotating result
C_{S}=2
and
C_{T}=2/\sqrt{3}-1
is equivalently

\displaystyle R_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3,

(27)

\displaystyle R_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}.

Let us explore structural reason for the universality seen in the examples below. Suppose the reduced temperature of a black-hole family has the scaling form

\displaystyle T(S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\,t\!\left(\frac{S}{S_{0}}\right),

(28)

for some amplitude
\tau
, scale
S_{0}
, and dimensionless shape function
t(x)
. Then
T_{S}\propto t^{\prime}(S/S_{0})
, so the Davies point sits at
S_{\mathrm{D}}=x_{\mathrm{D}}S_{0}
with
x_{\mathrm{D}}
a root of
t^{\prime}
. Integrating
\mathcal{F}_{S}=-S\,T_{S}
gives
\mathcal{F}(S)=\tau S_{0}\,f(S/S_{0})
with

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}t(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}-x\,t(x),

(29)

so the Hawking–Page point sits at
S_{\mathrm{HP}}=x_{\mathrm{HP}}S_{0}
with
x_{\mathrm{HP}}
a root of
f
.

Both
x_{\mathrm{D}}
and
x_{\mathrm{HP}}
are pure numbers fixed by the shape
t
alone, and hence

\displaystyle C_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x_{\mathrm{HP}}}{x_{\mathrm{D}}}-1,

(30)

\displaystyle C_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t(x_{\mathrm{HP}})}{t(x_{\mathrm{D}})}-1,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f(x_{\mathrm{D}})}{x_{\mathrm{D}}\,t(x_{\mathrm{D}})},

are independent of
\tau
and
S_{0}
, and the last of these is the nucleation-barrier ratio used in Sec.
4.3
.

This gives the working criterion used below: if two black-hole branches have reduced temperatures of the form Eq.(
28
) with the same shape function
t
, then they have identical
C_{S}
,
C_{T}
, and
B
, regardless of their amplitudes and scales.

The four-dimensional non-rotating family realizes a single shape,
t(x)=(1+3x)/(4\sqrt{x})
: for Reissner–Nordström–AdS the choice
S_{0}=\sigma
in Eq.(
40
) returns exactly this
t
, which is why the constants do not depend on the electric potential. In this language the ratios are invariants of a “corresponding-states” shape, and universality is broken precisely when a deformation changes the shape itself. Rotation does precisely this, and produces the corrections computed in Sec.
6
.

4.3
The Hawking–Page nucleation barrier

By the local sign rule the Davies point is the maximum of the on-shell free energy, i.e. the top of the barrier separating the thermal-AdS phase (
\mathcal{F}\to 0
as
S\to 0
) from the stable black hole. Its height
\mathcal{F}(S_{\mathrm{D}})
is the quantity that controls the rate of the Hawking–Page transition in the free-energy-landscape treatment of the kinetics
(
Wei et al. 2022
;
Li and Wang 2020
)
. It is natural to measure this height against the energy scale set by the same defect,
T_{\mathrm{D}}S_{\mathrm{D}}
, and to define the dimensionless barrier

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}(S_{\mathrm{D}})}{T_{\mathrm{D}}\,S_{\mathrm{D}}}.

(45)

For four-dimensional Schwarzschild–AdS, Eq.(
31
)–Eq.(
32
) give
\mathcal{F}(S_{\mathrm{D}})=1/(6\sqrt{3})
and
T_{\mathrm{D}}S_{\mathrm{D}}=1/(2\sqrt{3})
, so

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}.

(46)

Kerr–AdS at fixed angular velocity

Rotation introduces an additional scale, and one should not expect exact parameter independence to survive. Following the grand-canonical thermodynamic treatment of Kerr–AdS black holes

7
Multipoles for richer phase diagrams

The previous sections dealt a simple case: one Davies zero and one Hawking–Page zero. Many black-hole phase diagrams are not that simple. Reentrant transitions, higher-curvature terms, cavities, Born–Infeld electrodynamics, Rényi entropy, and Lovelock or Gauss–Bonnet examples may contain several heat-capacity divergences and several free-energy crossings
(
Altamirano et al. 2013
;
Frassino et al. 2014
;
Su and Li 2022
;
Yerra et al. 2023
;
Hu et al. 2024
;
Barzi et al. 2024
)
. In such cases a single pair of numbers like
C_{S}
and
C_{T}
cannot be the whole story.

Let

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{z_{i}=(X_{i},w_{i})\,|\,\bm{\varphi}(z_{i})=0\}

be the set of transition zeros in some chosen thermodynamic coordinate
X
. The coordinate might be entropy, temperature, pressure, electric potential, or another variable adapted to the ensemble. The total charge is

\displaystyle Q_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{z_{i}\in\mathscr{Z}}w_{i}.

(68)

When
Q_{0}=0
, the first signed moment

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z_{i}\in\mathscr{Z}}w_{i}X_{i}

(69)

is independent of a shift of the origin
X\to X+X_{0}
. A convenient scale is the centroid of the negative charges,

\displaystyle X_{-}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{w_{i}<0}|w_{i}|X_{i}}{\sum_{w_{i}<0}|w_{i}|},

(70)

which gives the normalized first moment

\displaystyle C^{(1)}_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{P}_{X}}{X_{-}}.

(71)

For a single Davies/Hawking–Page pair, this reduces to the ratios already discussed. For several pairs, it measures the net oriented displacement between negative- and positive-winding transition points.

One may also record

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{X}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z_{i}\in\mathscr{Z}}w_{i}\left(\frac{X_{i}}{X_{-}}\right)^{n},

(72)

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,2,\ldots.

The terminology is borrowed from electrostatics, but only as an analogy: the “charges” are winding numbers and their positions are thermodynamic coordinates. The use of this language is practical. A total winding number can be too coarse. Even a first moment can miss real differences between diagrams.

For an explicit black-hole solution the procedure is straightforward. One constructs the common field Eq.(

) or Eq.(

8
A categorical approach

The topology used above is a topology of thermodynamic state space, whose defects are zeros of a vector field in an auxiliary
(S,\theta)
plane. Categorical and non-invertible symmetries are different objects: their defects live in spacetime, or in the associated symmetry topological field theory. Still, there is a natural place where the two languages may meet. The Hawking–Page transition compares two Euclidean saddles with different thermal-circle topology, and line or surface defects can be sensitive to that difference.

In the boundary gauge theory the Hawking–Page transition is the large-
N
deconfinement transition
(
Witten 1998
)
. The Polyakov loop is a standard diagnostic. In thermal AdS the Euclidean thermal circle is non-contractible, while in the Euclidean black-hole saddle it caps off at the horizon. Thus a defect wrapping or linking the thermal circle can distinguish the two saddles. This is the basic reason why it is worth asking how topological defect insertions affect the crossing temperature.

Let
D_{a}
denote topological defect operators labelled by simple objects
a
in a fusion category, or in a higher-categorical symmetry structure. The fusion law has the schematic form

\displaystyle D_{a}D_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{c}N^{c}_{ab}D_{c},

(73)

where the coefficients
N^{c}_{ab}
need not come from a group multiplication table and the individual
D_{a}
need not be invertible. A defect-resolved thermal trace is then

\displaystyle Z_{a}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\left(D_{a}e^{-\beta H}\right),

(74)

\displaystyle F_{a}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T\log Z_{a}(1/T).

For non-invertible defects this should not automatically be read as an ordinary positive thermal partition function. It is safer to call
F_{a}
a defect, or twisted, free energy. In a semiclassical regime one may nevertheless organize it by saddles,

\displaystyle Z_{a}(\beta)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle e^{-I_{\rm AdS,a}(\beta)}+e^{-I_{\rm BH,a}(\beta)}+\cdots.

(75)

This motivates the definition of a defect-resolved Hawking–Page temperature,

\displaystyle F_{\rm BH,a}\!\left(T^{(a)}_{\rm HP}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{\rm AdS,a}\!\left(T^{(a)}_{\rm HP}\right),

(76)

and, when the relevant branch is well defined, a corresponding defect-resolved Davies temperature.

A small deformation makes the effect transparent. Write

\displaystyle\Delta F_{a}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{\rm BH,a}(T)-F_{\rm AdS,a}(T)

(77)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta F_{0}(T)+\delta\Delta F_{a}(T),

where
a=0
is the identity sector. To first order, the shift of the Hawking–Page temperature is

\displaystyle\delta T^{(a)}_{\rm HP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta\Delta F_{a}(T_{\mathrm{HP}})}{\partial_{T}\Delta F_{0}(T)|_{T=T_{\mathrm{HP}}}}.

(78)

The induced shifts of the normalized ratios are

\displaystyle\delta C^{(a)}_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta T^{(a)}_{\rm HP}-R_{T}\,\delta T^{(a)}_{\rm D}}{T_{\mathrm{D}}},

(79)

\displaystyle\delta C^{(a)}_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S^{(a)}_{\rm HP}-R_{S}\,\delta S^{(a)}_{\rm D}}{S_{\mathrm{D}}},

with
R_{T}=T_{\mathrm{HP}}/T_{\mathrm{D}}
and
R_{S}=S_{\mathrm{HP}}/S_{\mathrm{D}}
. The ratios are therefore protected only in the special case where the defect moves the Hawking–Page and Davies data by the same relative scale. If the defect weights the two saddles differently, the topological dipole splits into defect sectors.

Fusion gives an additional constraint. From Eq.(
73
),

\displaystyle Z_{D_{a}D_{b}}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(D_{a}D_{b}e^{-\beta H}\right)

(80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{c}N^{c}_{ab}Z_{c}(\beta).

This is a linear statement for partition functions. The corresponding free energies obey a log-sum-exp relation, and reduce to a dominant-sector relation only after a saddle approximation has been made. This distinction is important: the fusion algebra constrains the family of defect-resolved Hawking–Page temperatures, but it does not imply a linear relation among the free energies themselves.

One concrete testing ground is a thermal orbifold or discrete gauging. In such examples the low-temperature saddle may acquire a Casimir or twisted-sector contribution, while the black-hole saddle can respond differently because the thermal circle is contractible. Holographic examples with non-invertible symmetries in orbifold CFTs and brane constructions provide natural backgrounds in which to check this idea
(
Apruzzi et al. 2023
;
Gutperle et al. 2024
;
Antinucci et al. 2025
)
. Matrix-model descriptions of deconfinement, where the transition is controlled by the Polyakov-loop eigenvalue distribution, offer another useful language
(
Copetti et al. 2022
)
.

A compact dictionary can be stated in words. The Hawking–Page temperature is the zero of
\mathcal{F}
with
w_{\rm HP}=+1
, and in a defect sector it is replaced by the equality of the defect-resolved saddle free energies. The Davies temperature is the heat-capacity divergence with
w_{\rm D}=-1
, and may become a sector-dependent stability threshold. The ratios
C_{T}
and
C_{S}
are normalized first moments of the pair, while in a sector
a
they become
C_{T}^{(a)}
and
C_{S}^{(a)}
if the two saddles are shifted differently. Finally, thermal AdS and the AdS black hole differ in the contractibility of the thermal circle: a defect can remain as a non-contractible twist in one saddle and be absorbed, condensed, or reorganized in the other. This is the point where fusion data can enter.

We can identify three working hypotheses: first, if a defect contributes the same phase-independent factor to both saddles, the ratios should remain unchanged even if individual scales move. Second, if the defect distinguishes the saddles, the Hawking–Page temperature may split into sector-dependent values constrained by Eq.(
80
). Third, in phase diagrams with several transition points, the moment hierarchy of Sec.
7
may itself carry a representation or module structure under the relevant categorical symmetry. Establishing any of these statements is beyond the scope of this work, it would require a top-down model in which both the black-hole saddles and the defect sectors are under analytic control.

9
Discussion, conclusions and outlook

In summary, the universal Hawking–Page ratios
C_{S}
and
C_{T}
can be written in a compact topological form. In the common vector-field construction the Davies point and the Hawking–Page point carry winding numbers
-1
and
+1
. The total charge of the pair is therefore zero, but the pair has a non-zero signed first moment. In entropy and temperature variables,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{HP}}-S_{\mathrm{D}},

(81)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{HP}}-T_{\mathrm{D}},

and normalizing these by the Davies-point scales gives

\displaystyle C_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S_{\mathrm{HP}}-S_{\mathrm{D}}}{S_{\mathrm{D}}},

(82)

\displaystyle C_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{T_{\mathrm{HP}}-T_{\mathrm{D}}}{T_{\mathrm{D}}}.

This is the sense in which the Hawking–Page/Davies pair behaves as a thermodynamic topological dipole.

For four-dimensional Schwarzschild–AdS and grand-canonical Reissner–Nordström–AdS black holes the familiar values are recovered,

\displaystyle C_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2,

(83)

\displaystyle C_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}-1.

For charged non-rotating AdS black holes in
d
spacetime dimensions one obtains

\displaystyle C_{S}(d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d-1}{d-3}\right)^{(d-2)/2}-1,

(84)

\displaystyle C_{T}(d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d-2}{\sqrt{(d-1)(d-3)}}-1.

At fixed angular velocity in four-dimensional Kerr–AdS the winding numbers remain unchanged, while the normalized separations acquire rotational corrections beginning at order
\Omega^{4}
.

The same geometry of the free-energy curve also identifies the Davies point as the top of the Hawking–Page barrier. This gives the dimensionless barrier

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}(S_{\mathrm{D}})}{T_{\mathrm{D}}S_{\mathrm{D}}},

(85)

with
B=1/3
in four dimensions and
B(d)=1/[(d-1)(d-3)]
in the higher-dimensional charged non-rotating family. Together with the shape-function argument, this explains why pressure, electric potential, and other simple scales can drop out of the ratios: they rescale the curve without changing its reduced shape.

The dipole picture does not replace the ordinary thermodynamic calculation of
S_{\mathrm{D}}
,
S_{\mathrm{HP}}
,
T_{\mathrm{D}}
, and
T_{\mathrm{HP}}
,
the moments mimick topological invariants in a more strict mathematical sense. It does, however, put the universal constants and the winding numbers into one language. That language becomes useful once the phase diagram contains several transition points, because the same construction extends to a moment hierarchy. Higher moments can distinguish diagrams that have the same total winding number and even the same first moment.

We have proposed a categorical approach which could be of interest for future work. If topological defects associated with generalized or non-invertible symmetries can be inserted into the thermal trace, then the Hawking–Page temperature can be resolved by defect sector. Fusion rules then constrain the allowed pattern of sector-dependent temperatures and, consequently, the sector-dependent dipole ratios. A concrete test would be to compute these quantities in a holographic orbifold, brane realization, or matrix-model description where both the thermal AdS and black-hole saddle contributions are known. Such a calculation would decide whether the dipole ratios are protected, shifted, or split by categorical data.
