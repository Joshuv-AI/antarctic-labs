A machine learning-derived intratumoral heterogeneity-related signature predicts the prognosis for and therapeutic response in patients with skin cutaneous melanoma
Authors: Feng Ding, Wei Tian, Sarina Bai, Hongmei Jia, Ziying Zhang, Yuchen Jia, Fangxin Zhao, Xingxia Hao, Bing Yi, Lili Niu, Shaojie Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundReliable biomarkers for predicting prognosis and therapeutic response in skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM) remain limited. This study aimed to develop an intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH)-related prognostic signature for SKCM using integrative machine learning.MethodsRNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data from 472 SKCM patients in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and 214 patients in the GSE65904 cohort were analyzed. ITH scores were calculated using the DEPTH2 algorithm. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between high- and low-ITH groups [|log2fold change (FC)| ≥1, false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05]. Based on 38 prognostic DEGs identified by univariate Cox regression, we employed an integrative framework of 101 machine learning algorithm combinations to construct prognostic models in the TCGA training cohort. The model with the highest average concordance index (C-index) was validated in the GSE65904 cohort and selected as the prognostic ITH-related signature (PIRS). Associations of the PIRS risk score with tumor mutational burden (TMB), immune cell infiltration, immune checkpoint gene expression, and drug sensitivity were systematically evaluated. Model performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and Cox regression analyses.ResultsA 38-gene PIRS was constructed using the plsRcox algorithm. Patients with high PIRS risk scores exhibited significantly poorer overall survival (OS) in both the TCGA and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) cohorts. The PIRS was identified as an independent prognostic factor, with area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.779, 0.734, and 0.756 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival, respectively. High-risk samples displayed significantly lower TMB (P<0.05), reduced immune and stromal cell infiltration (P<0.001), downregulated immune function, and decreased expression of immune checkpoint genes. Additionally, high- and low-PIRS risk score groups exhibited distinct sensitivity patterns to different classes of targeted agents.ConclusionsThe machine learning-derived PIRS robustly predicts prognosis in SKCM patients. Its clinical application is promising for optimizing patient risk stratification and treatment decisions, though further prospective validation is warranted.Keywords: Skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM), intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH), prognostic signature, machine learning, tumor microenvironment

Highlight box

Key findings

• A 38-gene prognostic intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH)-related signature (PIRS) was developed for skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM) using an integrative machine-learning framework of 101 algorithm combinations. The PIRS independently predicts overall survival (1-, 3-, and 5-year area under the curve: 0.779, 0.734, and 0.756) and stratifies patients into immune-hot (low-risk) and immune-cold (high-risk) subgroups with distinct tumor mutational burden, immune cell infiltration, immune checkpoint expression, and sensitivity to targeted agents.

What is known and what is new?

• ITH is a well-recognized driver of tumor progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance in SKCM. Previous prognostic signatures for SKCM have largely focused on limited gene sets or tumor-intrinsic features and rarely provide direct guidance for treatment selection.

• In this study, we systematically integrated ITH assessment with a robust machine-learning framework of 101 algorithm combinations to construct the PIRS. For the first time, we directly linked the PIRS risk score to both immune landscape and drug sensitivity, enabling the differentiation between patients likely to benefit from immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy versus those who may respond better to targeted therapies.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The PIRS provides a clinically actionable tool: low-risk patients may benefit more from ICI therapy, whereas high-risk patients may be better suited for MAPK inhibitors, EGFR inhibitors, or cell-cycle inhibitors. Prospective clinical studies are needed to validate the PIRS for guiding treatment decisions. The 38-gene signature could be simplified into a clinically feasible panel (e.g., reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction or NanoString assay) for routine pathology laboratories. Clinicians should consider ITH-based risk stratification when designing personalized treatment plans for melanoma patients.

Introduction

Background

Skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM) is among the most aggressive skin cancers. It was estimated that there would be 104,960 new cases and 8,430 deaths in the United States in 2025 (1). Currently, only a few reliable biomarkers are available for predicting the prognosis for and therapeutic response in SKCM patients. These challenges highlight the urgent need to develop biomarkers that are capable of predicting treatment outcomes and prognosis for patients with SKCM.

Although early-stage melanoma can be effectively treated via surgical resection, advanced and metastatic melanoma remains challenging to manage, thus leading to high mortality rates. Intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH) is among the major contributing factors (2). Owing to genomic instability and morphological/epigenetic plasticity, tumor cells exhibit significant heterogeneity in genetic sequences and nearly all phenotype characteristics (3-6). Furthermore, the development of tumors is influenced by strong evolutionary pressures exerted by factors such as nutrition, metabolism, immunity, and treatment. The relative impact of these factors varies both spatially and temporally, ultimately favoring the formation of a tumor microenvironment (TME) that displays substantial genetic, phenotypic, and behavioral ITH (4). Concurrently, ITH is a key mechanism underlying tumor drug resistance and drives aggressive disease progression in the context of treatment failure (7,8).

Rationale and knowledge gap

ITH signatures have been demonstrated to predict prognosis for and treatment response in patients with colon adenocarcinoma, cholangiocarcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer, breast cancer, and gastric cancer (9-13). Although several prognostic signatures have been proposed for SKCM, a comprehensive model specifically capturing the ITH dimension and systematically linked to therapeutic response prediction using a robust machine learning framework is still lacking. Therefore, it is necessary to develop tools for patients with SKCM that can reflect ITH and to demonstrate the role of these tools in predicting disease prognosis and treatment outcomes.

Objective

Utilizing data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), we identified genes that mediate ITH in patients with SKCM and constructed a prognostic ITH-related signature (PIRS). Furthermore, in this study, the relationships between the PIRS, immune cell infiltration, and drug sensitivity were analyzed. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0801/rc).

Methods

Data acquisition

Bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data from 472 SKCM tumor tissues were obtained from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) as the training cohort (downloaded in July 2025). RNA-seq data obtained from 214 samples in the GSE65904 series were downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) as the validation cohort. Corresponding clinical data and mutation data for SKCM patients were also downloaded from the TCGA database. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Calculation of the ITH score

The DEPTH2 algorithm was applied to estimate the ITH level for each SKCM sample. Briefly, this mRNA-based algorithm calculates an ITH score for each bulk tumor based on the standard deviation of absolute z-scored expression values across genes, without requiring matched normal samples. A higher DEPTH2 score indicates greater ITH. Among available ITH algorithms, DEPTH2 was selected because it has been systematically benchmarked against five other methods (MATH, PhyloWGS, ABSOLUTE, DEPTH, and tITH) across 33 TCGA cancer types, demonstrating the strongest association with unfavorable clinical outcomes and competitive performance in reflecting genomic instability and immune suppression (14). We applied the DEPTH2 algorithm to calculate the ITH score of patients with SKCM and used the R package “survminer” to determine the optimal cutoff point for grouping based on the ITH score, wherein patients were divided into high- and low-ITH-score groups. The relationships between patient clinical characteristics and the ITH score were compared.

Screening for ITH-related prognostic biomarkers

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the high- and low-ITH-score groups were identified by using the R package “limma” [screening criteria: |log2fold change (FC)| ≥1 and P<0.05). Among these DEGs, univariate Cox regression analysis was performed to screen for potential prognostic biomarkers.

Integration of machine learning algorithms to construct the PIRS model

We performed an integrated machine learning analysis to construct PIRSs from potential prognostic biomarkers. Using the TCGA dataset, this study employed a total of 101 machine learning algorithms to fit prognostic models based on the potential prognostic biomarker genes. All algorithm combinations were validated in the GEO cohort, and the PIRS with the best predictive performance was selected.

We calculated the risk score for each sample in the TCGA cohort by using the selected PIRS. The samples were divided into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score as the threshold. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and concordance index (C-index) curves were plotted to evaluate the accuracy of the PIRS in predicting patient survival. Univariate and multivariate Cox analyses were performed to explore risk factors affecting SKCM prognosis. Prognostic C-index curves for clinical signatures and the PIRS were generated. Using the R package “rms”, a nomogram was constructed based on the PIRS risk score and other clinical features to visualize the predictive model.

Correlation of the PIRS with the tumor mutational burden (TMB)

TMB scores were calculated for each sample based on the gene mutation data downloaded from the TCGA database.

Correlations of the PIRS with immune functions

The ESTIMATE algorithm was used to calculate the immune score and ESTIMATE score for each patient. Immune cell infiltration data generated by the seven algorithms were downloaded from the TIMER2.0 website (http://timer.cistrome.org/infiltration_estimation_for_tcga.csv.gz) to assess the correlations between the PIRS and the abundances of various immune cell types in the TME. Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was used to analyze the correlations between the PIRS risk score and immune-related functions.

PIRS risk score predicts the drug sensitivity of targeted therapy in SKCM patients

The R package “OncoPredict” was used to calculate the drug sensitivity value [e.g., half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50)] for patients with SKCM, and the relationship between the risk score and drug sensitivity was analyzed. A higher drug sensitivity value indicates a lower sensitivity to the drug and a greater risk of treatment failure.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and graphing were performed by using R software (version 4.5.0). For comparisons of continuous variables, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (for non-normally distributed data) was used. Comparisons among multiple groups were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis test, as appropriate. Overall survival (OS) rates were visualized using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were compared using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was applied to evaluate the prognostic value of genes or scores in tumors. Time-dependent ROC curves were generated using the “survivalROC” package to assess the predictive accuracy of the PIRS and the nomogram for 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated with 95% confidence intervals using 1,000 bootstrap resamples. The R package “regplot” was used to plot the nomogram. The figures that are not specifically mentioned were generated by using the R package “ggplot2”.

Results

ITH scores of SKCM patients

We calculated the DEPTH2 scores for patients with SKCM (Table S1). We observed that patients with high DEPTH2 scores had a significantly lower OS rate (Figure 1A, P=0.007). There were 413 ITH-related DEGs detected between the high- and low-DEPTH2-score groups (Figure S1). Among these 413 DEGs, 38 DEGs (CRABP2, EVPL, ASPRV1, HEYL, GBP4, NECAB2, S100P, LY6G6C, ADCYAP1R1, WNT11, SLC29A4, CNTFR, ALOXE3, EFNA3, HSD11B2, JPH2, DDN, MDFI, CLDN4, RBM24, ANO2, AQP5, NXPH3, KLK14, PROM2, GAL, SDR9C7, RDH5, DLX3, SLPI, TMEM132D, FERMT1, IDO1, LYPD6B, CXCL9, CAMK2B, TMEM45B, and ARHGEF4) were associated with prognosis and were identified as potential ITH-related prognostic biomarkers (Figure 1B). DEPTH2 scores did not significantly differ across patients of different ages, genders, TNM stages, or overall stages (Figure 1C-1H).

Construction of the PIRS by using machine learning algorithms

Based on the expression data of the aforementioned 38 genes, different machine learning algorithms or combinations were used to construct the PIRS. The 101 prognostic models (Table S2) generated by the integrated machine learning analysis and their C-index values in the training and testing cohorts are shown in Figure 2A. The results demonstrated that the PIRS developed by using the plsRcox algorithm achieved the highest average C-index score (0.649). Therefore, the PIRS model constructed via plsRcox was used for all subsequent analyses in this study. This model incorporated all 38 potential ITH-related prognostic biomarkers (Table S3). Using the risk score model (PIRS), the risk score for each sample was calculated. The samples were divided into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score. The results revealed that patients with high-risk scores exhibited significantly poorer OS both in TCGA and GEO cohort (Figure 2B,2C). To further validate the robustness of this stratification across clinically relevant molecular subtypes, we performed subgroup survival analysis based on BRAF mutation status. As shown in Figure S2, the PIRS significantly distinguished high- from low-risk patients with distinct OS outcomes in both BRAF-mutant and BRAF-wild-type subgroups (log-rank P<0.001 for both), indicating that the prognostic value of the PIRS is independent of BRAF mutational status.

The risk score is an independent predictor of survival in patients with SKCM

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses confirmed that the PIRS is an independent risk factor for clinical outcomes in patients with SKCM (Figure 2D,2E; both P values <0.001). The PIRS demonstrated high accuracy in predicting 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival (AUC =0.779, 0.734, and 0.756, respectively) (Figure 2F). The AUC and C-index values for the PIRS were greater than those for other clinical features, including age, gender, and clinical stage (Figure 2G).

A nomogram incorporating the risk score and clinical data was constructed to predict clinical outcomes in patients with SKCM (Figure 2H). The calibration curves and ROC curves revealed good consistency between the nomogram-predicted 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates and the actual observed rates in the TCGA cohort (AUC =0.860, 0.851, 0.807 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year, respectively, Figure 2I). These results indicate that the nomogram can stably and accurately predict clinical outcomes for patients with SKCM. To further evaluate whether incorporating additional immune-related features could enhance the predictive power of the nomogram, we constructed a combined model integrating PIRS, TMB, and immune score (Figure S3). The 1-, 3-, and 5-year AUC values of this combined model were 0.861, 0.852, and 0.805, respectively, which were nearly identical to those of the original PIRS-only nomogram. This suggests that TMB and immune score contribute minimal incremental prognostic information beyond the PIRS, indicating that the PIRS comprehensively reflects the immune and mutational landscape relevant to prognosis and immunotherapy response.

The risk score is correlated with TMB

TMB is a biomarker for predicting benefits from immunotherapy, with higher scores indicating more potential neoantigens and being associated with better treatment outcomes. In the TCGA cohort, high-risk samples had significantly lower TMB scores (Figure 3A).

The PIRS is associated with the tumor immune microenvironment

As shown in Figure 3B, high-risk patients had significantly lower levels of stromal cell and immune cell infiltration (P<0.001). The PIRS was significantly negatively correlated with the infiltration levels of a wide range of immune cells (Figure 3C). The subset-specific correlation patterns, including those for CD8+ T-cell subsets, CD4+ memory T cells, and NK cell subsets, are further detailed in the Discussion section. Moreover, ssGSEA revealed that all immune functions were downregulated in high-risk patients (Figure 3D).

The PIRS predicts the response to targeted therapy in patients with SKCM

With the exception of the immune-suppressive genes CD276 and VTCN1, the expression levels of 47 immune checkpoint-related genes were downregulated in the high-risk group (Figure 3E). A high PIRS risk score may represent an “immune-cold” tumor microenvironment, in which patients are less likely to benefit from immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy.

We compared the sensitivities of patients in the low- and high-risk groups to those of 198 targeted therapeutic agents. After drugs unrelated to melanoma (such as NVP-ADW742) were excluded, as shown in Figure S4, patients in the low-risk group were more sensitive to DNA damage repair-targeting drugs (KU-55933 and NU7441), PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway-targeting drugs (AMG-319, AZD6482, AZD8055, and PF-4708671), transcription inhibitors (JQ1), and mitotic inhibitors (ribociclib and ZM447439). In contrast, high-risk patients were more sensitive to homologous recombination repair inhibitors (Mirin), MAPK signaling pathway inhibitors (ERK_2440, ERK_6604, selumetinib, ulixertinib, VX-11e, and sorafenib), EGFR inhibitors (afatinib, gefitinib, and lapatinib), a TGF-β signaling pathway inhibitor (LY2109761), cell cycle inhibitors (Wee1 inhibitors, docetaxel, vincristine, BI-2536, and Eg5_9814), and an angiogenesis inhibitor (cediranib). All differences in drug sensitivity between the high- and low-risk groups were statistically significant (P<0.001, Wilcoxon rank-sum test; detailed IC50 values and P values are presented in Figure S4).

Discussion

Key findings

In this study, a PIRS model for SKCM was constructed by integrating 101 machine learning techniques. The predictive model that was built by using the plsRcox method was identified as the optimal PIRS risk model. This model exhibited excellent performance in predicting the OS of SKCM patients and served as an independent risk factor for their clinical outcomes. Further analysis revealed that a low PIRS risk score was significantly associated with a high TMB score, increased expression of immune checkpoint-related genes, increased TMB, and a favorable prognosis. Therefore, the PIRS can serve as an indicator for predicting the benefits of immunotherapy in patients with SKCM.

Strengths and limitations

As a novel ITH-derived biomarker, the PIRS accurately predicts SKCM prognosis, immunotherapy response, and drug sensitivity. Its clinical application is promising for optimizing patient risk stratification and treatment decisions. However, the PIRS alone is insufficient for precisely understanding the spatial and temporal ITH patterns in the TME that confer a response or resistance to therapy, although a multigene expression prognostic model can partially address this limitation. Besides, as this study is inherently retrospective, relying solely on public database information, the findings are considered preliminary and necessitate further validation through prospective clinical trials and more comprehensive experimental exploration. Furthermore, our study assessed ITH solely using the DEPTH2 algorithm. Although DEPTH2 has been benchmarked favorably against multiple alternative ITH metrics, we acknowledge that different algorithms may capture complementary aspects of ITH. Future studies integrating multi-algorithm approaches or incorporating spatial transcriptomic and multi-region sequencing data would provide a more comprehensive view of ITH in SKCM.

Comparison with similar research

Previous studies have confirmed that ITH signatures can indeed predict responses to immune therapy (9-13). In summary, the PIRS can serve as a predictive indicator for immunotherapy benefits in patients with SKCM; specifically, patients with low PIRS scores tend to derive greater benefits from immunotherapy. This study demonstrated that the PIRS strongly predicts OS in patients with SKCM and is an independent risk factor for their clinical outcomes. ITH signatures are also applicable for predicting clinical prognosis of other cancer types, such as gastric cancer (13).

Explanations of findings

The PIRS model integrates 38 key genes, namely, CRABP2, EVPL, ASPRV1, HEYL, GBP4, NECAB2, S100P, LY6G6C, ADCYAP1R1, WNT11, SLC29A4, CNTFR, ALOXE3, EFNA3, HSD11B2, JPH2, DDN, MDFI, CLDN4, RBM24, ANO2, AQP5, NXPH3, KLK14, PROM2, GAL, SDR9C7, RDH5, DLX3, SLPI, TMEM132D, FERMT1, IDO1, LYPD6B, CXCL9, CAMK2B, TMEM45B, and ARHGEF4. Existing research has indicated that WNT11 can promote CD8+ T-cell exclusion and suppression, which is correlated with poor prognosis after liver metastasis (15); moreover, it plays an important role in regulating local invasion and distant metastasis formation in melanoma cells (16). Additionally, Mikael J. Pittet’s research group used the expression ratio of CXCL9 to SPP1 to define macrophage polarity in the TME and demonstrated that this ratio is significantly correlated with disease prognosis (17); moreover, CXCL9 has been shown to increase the efficacy of Braf inhibitors in melanoma (18). CRABP2 may be a biomarker for SKCM patients who are treated with PD-1 inhibitors (19). Furthermore, EVPL can inhibit melanoma progression via the RAS/ERK signaling pathway, alter the inflammatory TME of melanoma, and suppress macrophage recruitment (20). Furthermore, S100P has been implicated in tumor progression across multiple cancer types, including lung, pancreatic, colorectal, and breast cancers, though its role in melanoma remains less characterized (21). EFNA3 can promote the growth and migration of choroidal melanoma cells by activating the Stat3/Akt signaling pathway (22). Moreover, the overexpression of PROM2 is closely associated with increased metastatic potential in melanoma cells (23). Additionally, melanoma cells can utilize IDO1 to significantly impair the cytotoxic capacity of iNKT cells, and the targeting of Ido1 can enhance CD8+ T-cell immune responses against melanoma cells both in vivo and in vitro (24,25). The roles of other key genes in the pathogenesis and progression of cutaneous melanoma or other cancers, as well as their therapeutic applications, require further investigation.

In addition to the gene-level insights, we further explored the immune landscape associated with the PIRS. A high PIRS may indicate a greater risk of failure for targeted therapies. This study revealed that high-risk samples had a lower TMB, potentially leading to reduced immune cell infiltration because fewer antigens are recognizable by immune cells. We also observed that the PIRS was negatively correlated with CD8+ T-cell infiltration (with correlation coefficients ranging from −0.29 to −0.48), thus suggesting that high-risk patients may not benefit from ICI therapy. The number of helper T cells (CD4+ T cells) also demonstrated consistent negative correlations, particularly for memory T cells (CD4+ memory XCELL: cor =−0.514, P=1.58e−31), thus indicating severe defects in the initiation and maintenance of antitumor immune responses. Moreover, in addition to “resting” NK cells, activated NK cells (in the effector state) were negatively correlated with the risk score (NK cell activated_CIBERSORT: −0.171), and the overall NK cell count calculated via the MCPCOUNTER algorithm also demonstrated a strong negative correlation (R=−0.483), suggesting the inhibition of innate immune killing function. We also observed the downregulation of nearly all immune functions and immune checkpoint gene expression in high-risk samples, thus decreasing their likelihood of receiving benefits from targeted therapy.

Furthermore, our combined analysis demonstrated that incorporating TMB and immune score into the nomogram did not substantially improve predictive performance over the PIRS alone, suggesting that the PIRS effectively captures the prognostic information encoded by these immune-related features. This supports the utility of PIRS as a streamlined and robust biomarker for identifying patients who may benefit from immunotherapy (Figure S3).

Implications and actions needed

Future research requires prospective clinical trials to validate the clinical utility of the PIRS and to further explore the molecular mechanisms through which PIRS-related genes mediate tumor development, thereby advancing precision medicine for treating SKCM.

Conclusions

In this study, we developed a 38-gene PIRS for SKCM using an integrative machine-learning framework of 101 algorithm combinations. The PIRS independently predicts OS, with AUC values of 0.779, 0.734, and 0.756 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival, respectively. Patients with high PIRS risk scores exhibit significantly poorer OS in both the TCGA and GEO validation cohorts. Mechanistically, high-risk patients display an “immune-cold” phenotype characterized by lower TMB, reduced immune and stromal cell infiltration, downregulated immune functions, and decreased expression of most immune checkpoint genes, suggesting limited benefit from ICI therapy. In contrast, low-risk patients show an “immune-hot” phenotype and are more sensitive to DNA damage repair inhibitors and PI3K/mTOR inhibitors, whereas high-risk patients demonstrate greater sensitivity to MAPK pathway inhibitors, EGFR inhibitors, and cell-cycle inhibitors. Collectively, the PIRS provides a clinically actionable tool to refine risk stratification and guide personalized therapeutic selection in SKCM. Prospective clinical trials are warranted to validate its clinical utility before routine application.
