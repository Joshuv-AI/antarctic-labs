Robust Markov Decision Processes on Continuous State Spaces
Mengmeng Li, Yifan Hu, Daniel Kuhn, Yan Li

Abstract
We study infinite-horizon robust Markov decision processes (MDPs) on continuous state spaces with structured rectangular ambiguity set. The proposed ambiguity set falls within the convex hull of unknown generating kernels. We utilize the dynamic formulation of the corresponding robust MDPs, and subsequently introduce a stochastic first-order method for robust policy evaluation. We establish its high probability convergence to the robust value function, which in turn leads to an 𝒪 ~ ​ ( 1 / ϵ 2 ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\epsilon^{2}) sample complexity. This high probability accuracy certificate is then used in an approximate policy iteration method that finds an ϵ \epsilon -optimal policy with 𝒪 ~ ​ ( 1 / ϵ 2 ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\epsilon^{2}) samples. The obtained sample complexities for both robust policy evaluation and optimization appear to be new for robust MDPs with continuous state spaces. Of independent interest, the proposed method is also directly applicable to zero-sum Markov games, which seems to strictly improve the existing sample complexities for continuous state spaces.

Definition 1

\penalty\ \mathcal{P}_{s}=\left\{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{w}_{k}P_{k}(\cdot|s,\cdot)\mid\mathrm{w}\in W\right\}.

(7)

We refer to

Definition

For instance, in Example

\xi_{t}
denotes the corresponding wind speed, while
\zeta_{t}
corresponds to the additive Gaussian noise.
Conditioning on a realization

\mathcal{B}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}.

Taking the expectation over

the effective one-step transition kernel becomes the averaged kernel
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\rho_{t}}[P_{\xi}(\cdot\mid s,a)]

from an ambiguity set
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{s}^{0}=\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\rho}\bigl[P_{\xi}(\cdot\mid s,a)\bigr]:\rho\in\mathcal{D}\Bigr\},

which is a mixture ambiguity set generated by the family
\{P_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}

Remark 1
.

If the generating kernel family
\{P_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is
indexed by a potentially continuous

the mixture ambiguity set (

Nevertheless we can proceed as follows.
Let

be i.i.d. samples following distribution

and consider

\mathcal{P}_{s}=\biggl\{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}w_{k}\,P_{\xi^{(k)}}(\cdot\mid s,\cdot):\mathsf{D}_{\phi}(w,\widehat{\rho}_{K})\leq\tau,\ w\in\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}\biggr\},

where

is the empirical distribution over

Under Lipschitz continuity of the generating kernels

with respect to the state and by sample average approximation results for distributionally robust stochastic programming with

solving the robust MDP (

\mathcal{P}^{0}=\prod_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\mathcal{P}_{s}^{0}

up to an error of order

In particular, the required number of generating kernels

depends polynomially on

and is

of the state space.

The mixture ambiguity set (
7
) offers several modeling advantages for robust MDPs.
A first advantage is its interpretability: the size and shape of the ambiguity set are directly determined by the dispersion among the generating kernels.
When the generating kernels are similar to one another, the resulting ambiguity set is tight and the worst-case policy is only mildly conservative; when they are more distinct, the ambiguity set expands accordingly and the robust policy becomes more conservative.
This interpretation is natural in applications such as multi-echelon inventory control
[
17
]
(
cf.
Example
1
), where uncertainty can be represented by a small number of demand regimes, such as different demand intensity levels or seasonal patterns.
Each regime
k
specifies a demand model for the exogenous demand process
d
, which in turn induces a transition kernel
P_{k}(\cdot\mid s,a)
.
If these demand regimes generate similar kernels, then the decision maker faces limited model uncertainty; if they generate markedly different kernels, then the ambiguity set appropriately reflects a higher level of uncertainty.
This contrasts with commonly used approaches for constructing ambiguity sets in robust MDPs, which consider a probability ball centered at some nominal kernel
\widehat{P}
, with a radius that depends on whether uncertainty arises from estimation error or from deployment-time model misspecification.

If
\widehat{P}
is estimated from transaction logs and deployment matches the logging environment, the radius can be calibrated to statistical error – for example, of order
\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/N})
to obtain out-of-sample guarantees from
N
observed transitions at confidence level
1-\delta

[
71
]
.
If deployment involves regime shifts (
e.g.
, season changes, promotions, supplier disruptions, or sim-to-real mismatch
[
61
,
49
]
), then the radius must represent structural misspecification rather than sampling noise, and it is not identified by
N
alone.
For instance, even if one knows an upper bound on the difference in the Poisson rate of exogenous demand between simulation and true demand distribution, it is unclear how to translate this knowledge into an appropriate radius of the corresponding Kullback-Leibler divergence ball centered at the data-generating kernel. This is because the same absolute difference in Poisson parameter can result in an orders-of-magnitude difference in Kullback-Leibler divergence.

Second, it is commonly acknowledged that a major modeling benefit of
\mathrm{s}
-rectangular sets is that they allow one to capture dependence among the action-conditional transition kernels
\{P(\cdot\mid s,a)\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
through a proper construction of the marginal ambiguity set
\mathcal{P}_{s}
, defined in (
7
).
However, while such flexibility is desirable, it is often unclear how this cross-action coupling should be specified in a principled manner unless substantial prior knowledge of the underlying dynamics is available.
In particular, one should take caution not to impose artificially hard-coded coupling across actions when such dependence is not directly justified by the data-generating mechanism.
In applications such as multi-echelon inventory control, the coupling among
\{P(\cdot\mid s,a)\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
should be induced by the common latent demand mechanism rather than introduced manually.
A prior approach toward obtaining such coupling is to construct the marginal ambiguity set
\mathcal{P}_{s}
using statistical procedures, for example through super-level sets of the maximum likelihood estimator
[
71
]
.
Unfortunately, this approach requires the transition kernel to be sufficiently simple so that the analytical form of
P(\cdot\mid s,a)
is available.
Recent approaches instead enforce coupling through aggregated constraints such as
\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathsf{D}(P(\cdot\mid s,a),\widehat{P}(\cdot\mid s,a))\leq\tau

[
35
]
, yet the induced cross-action coupling depends on the particular choice of aggregation and is not directly implied by the underlying demand mechanism.
In contrast, the mixture ambiguity set couples actions through the generating kernels
\{P_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
, so the coupling is inherited from the modeled demand regimes rather than manually hard-coded, and it remains applicable even when the kernels have no analytical form available.

We proceed to introduce some structural properties of nature’s cost-minimizing MDP, which will prove useful in our ensuing algorithmic development.
These properties arise naturally from the structure of the proposed mixture ambiguity set.

Definition 2
.

For any joint policy
(\pi,\omega)\in\Pi\times\Omega
, define the joint action-value function through

\displaystyle Q^{\omega}_{\pi}(s,a,k)=r(s,a)+\gamma\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}V^{\omega}_{\pi}(s^{\prime})P_{k}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\quad\forall(s,a,k)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{K}.

Nature’s action-value function associated with its policy
\omega\in\Omega

\displaystyle{H^{\omega}_{\pi}(s,k)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)Q^{\omega}_{\pi}(s,a,k)\quad\forall(s,k)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathcal{K}.}

Let

induced by

P^{\omega}

S_{0}=s_{0}
(Ionescu Tulcea Theorem

Definition 3
.

We define the discounted state visitation measure induced by joint policy

\displaystyle d^{\omega}_{\pi}(\mathcal{B}|s_{0})=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\mathbb{P}^{\omega}_{\pi}\left(S_{t}\in\mathcal{B}|S_{0}=s_{0}\right),

for any Borel set
\mathcal{B}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}

The following lemma then characterizes the difference of nature’s value functions for any pair of its policies.

It reduces the global value gap to the discounted expectation of local one-step Bellman update differences under the comparison occupancy measure.

For any nature’s policies

\displaystyle V^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi}(s)-V^{\omega}_{\pi}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\!\left[\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}(\omega^{\prime}(k|s^{\prime})-\omega(k|s^{\prime}))H^{\omega}_{\pi}(s^{\prime},k)\right]d^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s).

(12)

\displaystyle\psi_{\pi}^{\omega}(s,\mathrm{w})=\gamma\int{V^{\omega}_{\pi}(s^{\prime})(P^{\mathrm{w}}_{\pi}-P^{\omega(s)}_{\pi})(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s)}\quad\forall\mathrm{w}\in W.

\displaystyle V^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi}(s)-V^{\omega}_{\pi}(s)=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\psi^{\omega}_{\pi}(s^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))d^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s).

(14)

Proof.

The proof of (

applied to the nature’s cost minimizing MDP and Definition

In addition, (

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds.
Denote
\mu=\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)(1-\gamma)
and
L=\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)(1+\gamma)

where
\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)
and
\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)
are the smallest and largest eigenvalues of the matrix
\Sigma
, respectively.

Then, we have

(i)

\|F(\theta)\|_{2}\leq L\|\theta-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}
for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(ii)

\langle F(\theta),\theta-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle\geq\mu\|\theta-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}
for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

To facilitate our discussion, let us define
\mathcal{K}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
through

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(z)=\phi(z)\left[\phi(z)^{\top}-\gamma\int_{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\pi(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})\omega(k^{\prime}|s^{\prime})\phi(s^{\prime},a^{\prime},k^{\prime})^{\top}P_{k}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\right]\quad\forall z\in\mathcal{Z}.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

For Assertion
(i)
, observe first that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{K}(z)\,\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}z)&=\Sigma-\gamma\int_{\mathcal{Z}\times{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}}\pi(a^{\prime}|s^{\prime})\omega(k^{\prime}|s^{\prime})\phi(z)\phi(z^{\prime})^{\top}P_{k}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}z)\\
&=\Sigma-\gamma\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\phi(z^{\prime})\phi(z^{\prime})^{\!\top}\,\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})=(1-\gamma)\Sigma,\end{split}

(29)

where the first and last equalities follow from the definition of
\Sigma
, and the second equality holds because
\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}
is the steady-state distribution.
We then have

F(\theta)=F(\theta)-F(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi})=\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{K}(z)\,\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}z)(\theta-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi})=(1-\gamma)\Sigma(\theta-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}),

(30)

where the first equality holds because
F(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi})=0
,
the second equality follows from the linearity of
F
in
\theta
,
and the third equality uses (
29
).
Assertion
(i)
then follows by the definition of
L
.
For Assertion
(ii)
, we have

\begin{split}\langle F(\theta),\theta-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle=(\theta-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi})^{\top}(1-\gamma)\Sigma(\theta-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi})\geq\mu\|\theta-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2},\end{split}

where the first equality follows from (
30
), and the inequality follows by the definition of
\mu
.
∎

\displaystyle\zeta_{t}=\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},\xi_{t}^{\tau})-F(\theta_{t}).

(31)

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds. We then have

\Big\|F(\theta_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}\big[\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},\xi_{t}^{\tau})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\big]\Big\|_{2}\leq(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}\quad\mathbb{P}^{\omega}_{\pi}\text{-a.s.}.

Proof.

Denote by
\nu_{\tau|t}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z})
the conditional distribution of
Z_{t+\tau}
given
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
, that is, for every Borel set
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathbb{P}^{\omega}_{\pi}[Z_{t+\tau}\in\mathcal{B}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\nu_{\tau|t}(\mathrm{d}z)
.
We then have

\begin{split}\left\|F(\theta_{t})-\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}\big[\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},\xi_{t}^{\tau})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\big]\right\|_{2}&=\left\|\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\!\mathcal{K}(z)\,(\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}z)-\nu_{\tau|t}(\mathrm{d}z))(\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi})\right\|_{2}\\
&\leq\left\|\int_{\mathcal{Z}}\mathcal{K}(z)\,(\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}z)-\nu_{\tau|t}(\mathrm{d}z))\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}\\
&\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\mathcal{K}(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,\|\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}-\nu_{\tau|t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\,\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}\\
&\leq\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\phi(z)\|_{2}(\|\phi(z)\|_{2}+\gamma\|\phi(z^{\prime})\|_{2})\,\|\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}-\nu_{\tau|t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\,\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}\\
&\leq(1+\gamma)\|\nu^{\omega}_{\pi}-\nu_{\tau|t}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\,\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}\leq(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2},\end{split}

where the first and the third inequalities follow from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the second inequality uses Hölder’s inequality, and the fourth inequality holds because
\|\phi(z)\|_{2}\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
. Finally, the last inequality uses Assumption
1
. Thus, the claim follows.
∎

, Lemma

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds, set
\tau>(\log(\mu)-\log(C(1+\gamma)))/\log(\rho)
and define
\widetilde{\mu}=\mu-(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}
.
If
\eta\leq\widetilde{\mu}/8
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\eta^{2}U^{2}+2\eta\left\langle\zeta_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\right\rangle

(32)

and

\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\|\theta_{0}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{U^{2}\widetilde{\mu}^{2}}{64}.

Proof.

Observe first that by the construction of
\widehat{F}
, we have

\|\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},\xi_{t}^{\tau})-\widehat{F}(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi},\xi_{t}^{\tau})\|_{2}=\|\phi(S_{t+\tau})-\gamma\phi(S_{t+\tau+1})\|_{2}\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}\phi(S_{t+\tau})\|_{2}\leq 2\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2},

(33)

and

\|\widehat{F}(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi},\xi_{t}^{\tau})\|_{2}=\|(\langle\phi(S_{t+\tau})-\gamma\phi(S_{t+\tau+1}),\theta_{t}\rangle-r(S_{t+\tau},A_{t+\tau}))\phi(S_{t+\tau})\|_{2}\leq 2\|\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}+1\leq U.

(34)

We then have

\begin{split}\langle\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},\xi_{t}^{\tau}),\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\rangle&=\langle\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},\xi_{t}^{\tau})-\widehat{F}(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi},\xi_{t}^{\tau})+\widehat{F}(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi},\xi_{t}^{\tau}),\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\rangle\\
&\geq-2\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\|_{2}-U\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\|_{2},\end{split}

(35)

where the inequality follows from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the previous observations (
33
) and (
34
).
We then obtain

\begin{split}\eta\langle\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},Z_{t}^{\tau}),\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\|_{2}^{2}&\geq\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\|_{2}\left(-\eta(2\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}+U)+\frac{1}{2}\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\|_{2}\right)\\
&\geq-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}(2\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}+U)^{2}\geq-\eta^{2}(4\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}+U^{2}),\end{split}

(36)

where the first inequality follows from (

), the second inequality holds

since

,

and the third inequality uses
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}.

Note that the update rule in Line
4

\eta\langle\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},Z_{t}^{\tau}),\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle+\eta\langle\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},Z_{t}^{\tau}),\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}-\theta_{t}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}-\theta_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}.

Lower bounding the left-hand side of the above expression using (

\eta\langle\widehat{F}(\theta_{t},\xi_{t}^{\tau}),\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle-4\eta^{2}\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}-U^{2}\eta^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}-\theta_{t}\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}-\theta_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}.

Rearranging terms in the above expression further shows

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}-\theta_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}&\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+4\eta^{2}\right)\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}+U^{2}\eta^{2}-\eta\langle\zeta_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle-\eta\langle F(\theta_{t}),\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\eta\mu+4\eta^{2}\right)\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}+U^{2}\eta^{2}-\eta\langle\zeta_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle,\end{split}

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
3.5
(ii)
.
The first claim then follows by noting that
\eta\leq\widetilde{\mu}/8
.
Taking expectation and multiplying by
2
of the above expression yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\|\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}-\theta_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-2\eta\mu+8\eta^{2}\right)\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}+2U^{2}\eta^{2}-2\eta\langle\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\zeta_{t}],\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-2\eta\widetilde{\mu}+8\eta^{2}\right)\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}+2U^{2}\eta^{2},

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
3.6
.
Thus, the second claim also follows.

Clearly, in view of Lemma

.
To this end,

we proceed to establish the following high probability boundedness guarantee on the iterate
\theta_{t}
generated by Algorithm

.
It should be noted that as the norm of the stochastic error
\zeta_{t}
(defined in (

)) itself depends on the norm of the iterate

,

In the following proposition, we construct an approximate martingale sequence that in turn will help us bound the accumulated stochastic error in stochastic approximation algorithms with high probability,
which might be of independent interest.

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds.
Fix the total number of iterations
T>0
and set
\theta_{0}=0
. For any
\delta\in(0,1)
, set

\tau>\max\{-\log(\sqrt{T}),\log(\mu)-\log(C(1+\gamma))\}/\log(\rho)

and
\eta=\nu/\sqrt{T}

with

\displaystyle\nu=\min\left\{\frac{\widetilde{\mu}}{L^{2}+8},\frac{1}{2U},\frac{1}{4G}\right\},\ G=\max\left\{2(1+\gamma)C(R^{2}+1),4\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{2T}{\delta}\right)}\left[(L+2)(R^{2}+1)+U(R+1)\right]\right\}.

Then with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have

\left\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq R^{2}+1,\quad\forall t\leq T.

Proof.

Define random sequences
\{X_{t}=\langle\zeta_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle\},\{\widetilde{X}_{t}=\langle\zeta_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{t}}\}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{t}=\left\{Y_{t}\leq G\sqrt{t}\right\}
, and

Y_{0}=\widetilde{Y}_{0}=0,\ Y_{t}=Y_{t-1}+X_{t-1},\ \widetilde{Y}_{t}=\widetilde{Y}_{t-1}+\widetilde{X}_{t-1}.

Conditioning on the event
\mathcal{G}_{t}
and defining
M_{(\nu,G)}=R^{2}+2\nu^{2}U^{2}+2\nu G
, recursively applying Lemma
3.8
and using the parameter choice of
\eta
yields

\displaystyle\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}\leq R^{2}+2t\eta^{2}U^{2}+2\eta G\sqrt{t}=R^{2}+2\nu^{2}U^{2}+2\nu G=M_{(\nu,G)},

(37)

We proceed to show that the above bound happens with probability at least
1-\delta
given a proper choice of
(\nu,G)
.
First, note that

\displaystyle\langle\zeta_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{F}_{t}(\theta_{t},\xi^{\tau}_{t})-\widehat{F}_{t}(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi},\xi^{\tau}_{t}),\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle-\langle F(\theta_{t}),\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle+\langle\widehat{F}_{t}(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi},\xi^{\tau}_{t}),\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{F}_{t}(\theta_{t},\xi^{\tau}_{t})-\widehat{F}_{t}(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi},\xi^{\tau}_{t})\|_{2}\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}+\|F(\theta_{t})\|_{2}\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}+\|\widehat{F}_{t}(\theta^{\omega}_{\pi},\xi^{\tau}_{t})\|_{2}\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(L+2)\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}+U\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2},

where the first inequality follows from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and
the second inequality uses the bounds (
33
), Lemma
3.5
(i)
, and (
34
).
We then have

\big|\widetilde{X}_{t}\big|=\left|\left\langle\zeta_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\right\rangle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{t}}\right|\leq(L+2)M_{(\nu,G)}+U\sqrt{M_{(\nu,G)}}.

In addition, observe that

\big|\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\widetilde{X}_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\big|=|\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\langle\zeta_{t},\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\rangle]\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{t}}|\leq(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}\left\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\right\|_{2}^{2}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{t}}\leq(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}M_{(\nu,G)},

(38)

where the equality holds because
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{t}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable, the first inequality follows from Lemma
3.6
, and the second inequality uses (
37
).
Let
b=(L+2)M_{(\nu,G)}+U\sqrt{M_{(\nu,G)}}
. Chernoff bound then implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{Y}_{t}\geq x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\lambda>0}\exp(-\lambda x)\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\lambda\left(\widetilde{Y}_{i+1}-\widetilde{Y}_{i}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\min_{\lambda>0}\exp(-\lambda x)\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{t-2}\lambda\left(\widetilde{Y}_{i+1}-\widetilde{Y}_{i}\right)\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\left(\widetilde{Y}_{t}-\widetilde{Y}_{t-1}\right)\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\lambda>0}\exp(-\lambda x)\cdot\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{t-2}\lambda\left(\widetilde{Y}_{i+1}-\widetilde{Y}_{i}\right)\right)\right]\cdot\exp\left(\lambda(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}M_{(\nu,G)}+\frac{b^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\lambda>0}\exp(-\lambda x)\cdot\exp\left(t\lambda(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}M_{(\nu,G)}+\frac{tb^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\min_{\lambda>0}-(x-t(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}M_{(\nu,G)})\lambda+\frac{tb^{2}\lambda^{2}}{2}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{(x-t(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}M_{(\nu,G)})^{2}}{2tb^{2}}\right),

where the second inequality follows from Hoeffding’s lemma and (
38
), and the third inequality holds by recursive application of the second inequality. Thus, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, by applying the union bound over
1\leq t\leq T
in the above inequality, we have

\widetilde{Y}_{t}\leq t(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}M_{(\nu,G)}+2\left((L+2)M_{(\nu,G)}+U\sqrt{M_{(\nu,G)}}\right)\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{2T}{\delta}\right)t},\quad\forall t\in[T],

with probability at least
1-\delta
. By construction of
\tau
,
G
, and
\nu,
we have
t(1+\gamma)C\rho^{\tau}M_{(\nu,G)}\leq G\sqrt{t}/2
,
2(L+2)M_{(\nu,G)}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{2T}{\delta}\right)t}\leq G\sqrt{t}/4
, together with
2U\sqrt{M_{(\nu,G)}}\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{2T}{\delta}\right)t}\leq G\sqrt{t}/4
. Thus,

\widetilde{Y}_{t}\leq G\sqrt{t}
for all
t\in[T]
with probability
1-\delta
.
Denote by
\mathcal{G}=\{\widetilde{Y}_{t}\leq G\sqrt{t}\ \forall t\in[T]\}
.
We now use induction to show that
Y_{t}=\widetilde{Y}_{t}
over
\mathcal{G}
for all
t\leq T.
Note that the claim holds trivially at
t=0.

Suppose that the claim holds at iteration
t\geq 0
, then for any
\omega\in\mathcal{G}
, we have

\displaystyle Y_{t+1}(\omega)=Y_{t}(\omega)+X_{t}(\omega)

\displaystyle=Y_{t}(\omega)+X_{t}(\omega)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\widetilde{Y}_{t}\leq G\sqrt{t}\right\}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=Y_{t}(\omega)+X_{t}(\omega)\mathds{1}_{\left\{Y_{t}\leq G\sqrt{t}\right\}}(\omega)=Y_{t}(\omega)+\widetilde{X}_{t}(\omega)=\widetilde{Y}_{t+1}(\omega),

where the second equality follows from the definition of
\mathcal{G},
the third equality applies the induction hypothesis, whereas the fourth and fifth equalities follow from the definitions of
\widetilde{X}_{t}
and
\widetilde{Y}_{t+1},
respectively. The induction is then complete. We may now deduce that

\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}^{2}\leq M_{(\nu,G)}=R^{2}+2\nu^{2}U^{2}+2\nu G\leq R^{2}+1\quad\forall t\leq T,

where the first inequality follows from the induction claim, the equality holds by definition of
M_{(\nu,G)}
, and the second inequality uses the choice of
\nu
that
\nu\leq\min\{1/(2U),1/(4G)\}.
Hence, the claim follows.
∎

We are now ready to show that with a proper specification of parameters
(\eta,T)
, Algorithm

indeed produces a solution that certifies Condition
1
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Under the same setup of Proposition
3.9
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, the following hold for all
t\leq T
.

(i)

\|\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[Q_{\theta_{t}}]-Q^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq(1-\eta\widetilde{\mu})^{t/2}R+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}
;

(ii)

\|Q_{\theta_{t}}\|_{\infty}\leq R+1+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\|Q_{\theta_{t}}-Q^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{\infty}^{2}]\leq R^{2}+\frac{U^{2}\widetilde{\mu}^{2}}{64}+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}^{2}
.

Proof.

For Assertion
(i)
, we have

\begin{split}\|\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[Q_{\theta_{t}}]-Q^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{\infty}&\leq\|\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[Q_{\theta_{t}}]-Q_{\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}}\|_{\infty}+\|Q_{\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}}-Q^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\phi(z)^{\top}\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\theta_{t}]-\phi(z)^{\top}\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}\\
&\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\phi(z)\|_{2}\|\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\theta_{t}]-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}\leq(1-\eta\widetilde{\mu})^{t/2}R+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}},\end{split}

where the second inequality holds by the definition of
\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}
, and the last inequality follows from the assumption that
\|\phi(z)\|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
and Lemma
3.7
.
For Assertion
(ii)
, we have

\begin{split}\|Q_{\theta_{t}}\|_{\infty}&\leq\|Q_{\theta_{t}}-Q_{\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}}\|_{\infty}+\|Q_{\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}}-Q^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\phi(z)^{\top}\theta_{t}-\phi(z)^{\top}\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}\\
&\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\phi(z)\|\|\theta_{t}-\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{2}+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}\leq\sqrt{R^{2}+1}+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}\leq R+1+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}},\end{split}

where the fourth inequality follows from Lemma
3.9
.
For Assertion
(iii)
, observe that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\|Q_{\theta_{t}}-Q^{\omega}_{\pi}\|_{\infty}^{2}]\leq 2(\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{\pi}[\|\theta^{\omega}_{\pi}-\theta_{t}\|_{2}^{2}]+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}^{2})\leq R^{2}+\frac{U^{2}\widetilde{\mu}^{2}}{64}+\varepsilon_{\rm{approx}}^{2},\end{split}

where the second inequality uses Lemma

Hence, the claim follows.
∎

Suppose that Assumption

Remark 3
.

It is interesting to note here that for the purpose of the convergence characterization for Algorithm
4
, one has to control the estimation error
\|\widehat{Q}_{\pi_{n}}-Q_{\pi_{n}}\|_{\infty}
either in expectation, or with high probability as stated in Line
3

Despite the abundant development of non-robust policy evaluation
22
,
58
, it is unclear to us how existing development could be readily adapted for controlling
\|\widehat{Q}_{\pi_{n}}-Q_{\pi_{n}}\|_{\infty}
in expectation.

Instead, in this manuscript we proceed
with Theorem
3.11
that certifies high probability control.
It can also be readily seen that Lemma
4.1
and the ensuing Lemma
4.3
no longer hold for approximate policy iteration if one can only control the bias term
\mathbb{E}[{\widehat{Q}_{\pi_{n}}-Q_{\pi_{n}}}]

We proceed by characterizing the difference of value functions between any pair of joint policies.
This could be understood as the performance difference lemma

in the dynamic game setting.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any joint policies
(\pi,\omega),(\pi^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\in\Pi\times\Omega
and any
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, we have

\displaystyle V^{\omega}_{\pi}(s)-V^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\!\left[(\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega(\cdot|s^{\prime})-(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\prime}(\cdot|s^{\prime})\right]d^{\omega}_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle V^{\omega}_{\pi}(s)-V^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)

\displaystyle=(\pi(\cdot|s))^{\!\top}Q^{\omega}_{\pi}(s)\,\omega(\cdot|s)-(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s))^{\!\top}Q^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)\,\omega^{\prime}(\cdot|s)

\displaystyle=(\pi(\cdot|s))^{\top}\left(Q^{\omega}_{\pi}(s)-Q^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)\right)\omega(\cdot|s)+(\pi(\cdot|s))^{\!\top}Q^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)\,\omega(\cdot|s)-(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s))^{\!\top}Q^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)\,\omega^{\prime}(\cdot|s)

\displaystyle=\gamma\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}[V^{\omega}_{\pi}(s^{\prime})-V^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})]\pi(a|s)\omega(k|s)P_{k}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)

\displaystyle\quad\penalty\ +(\pi(\cdot|s))^{\!\top}Q^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)\,\omega(\cdot|s)-(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s))^{\!\top}Q^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s)\,\omega^{\prime}(\cdot|s),

where the third equality follows from Definition
2
.
The claim then follows by iteratively expanding
V^{\omega}_{\pi}(s^{\prime})-V^{\omega^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})
and using Definition
3
.
∎

We are now ready to establish the following convergence characterization for each update of Algorithm
4
.

In particular, Lemma
4.3
below establishes that the optimality gap of the policy generated by Algorithm

contracts linearly up to an additive error proportional to the robust policy evaluation error
\varepsilon
, and the primal-dual gap of the approximate solution
(\pi_{n+1},\omega_{n+1})
for the matrix game (

Lemma 4.3
.

Fix total iteration number
N>0
in Algorithm
4
and set
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Let
\rho\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{S}})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)={\mathcal{S}}
, and
let
D=\sup_{(\pi,\omega)\in\Pi\times\Omega}\!\|\!\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}d^{\omega}_{\pi}(\cdot|s)\rho(\mathrm{d}s)/\rho(\cdot)\|_{\infty}
denote the distribution mismatch coefficient.

Then, with probability at least
1-\delta/N
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n+1}}(s)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle\!\leq\!\left(1-\frac{1-\gamma}{D}\right)\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n}}(s)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)+\frac{2D(\Delta+2\varepsilon)}{1-\gamma}.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.2
, we have with probability
1-\delta/(2N)
that

\displaystyle V_{\pi_{n+1}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n}}(s)+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)

(40)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\Big[(\pi_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-(\pi_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime})+\Delta+2\varepsilon\Big]d^{\omega^{\star}_{n+1}}_{\pi_{n+1}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d^{\omega^{\star}_{n+1}}_{\pi_{n+1}}(\{s\}|s)}{1-\gamma}\Big[(\pi_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-(\pi_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime})+\Delta+2\varepsilon\Big]

\displaystyle\geq(\pi_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-(\pi_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime})+\Delta+2\varepsilon\geq 0,

where the first inequality applies Lemma
4.1
, and the second inequality follows from the fact that
d^{\omega^{\star}_{n+1}}_{\pi_{n+1}}(\{s\}|s)

\geq 1-\gamma
.
Consequently, integrating both sides of the above inequality with respect to
d^{\omega_{n+1}}_{\pi^{\star}}(\cdot|s)
yields

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})-V_{\pi_{n+1}}(s^{\prime})\right]d^{\omega_{n+1}}_{\pi^{\star}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s)\\
&\leq\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[(\pi_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-(\pi_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})\right]d^{\omega_{n+1}}_{\pi^{\star}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s)+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon).\end{split}

(41)

On the other hand, we have

\begin{split}(1-\gamma)(V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n}}(s))&\leq(1-\gamma)(V^{\omega_{n+1}}_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n}}(s))\\
&=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[(\pi^{\star}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-(\pi_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n}(\cdot|s^{\prime})\right]d^{\omega_{n+1}}_{\pi^{\star}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s),\end{split}

(42)

where the inequality holds from the definition of
V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)
, and the equality follows from Lemma
4.2
.
Summing up (
41
) and (
42
) and rearranging terms, we obtain with probability
1-\delta/N
that

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})-V_{\pi_{n+1}}(s^{\prime})\right]d^{\omega_{n+1}}_{\pi^{\star}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s)+(1-\gamma)(V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n}}(s))\\
&\leq\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\big[(\pi^{\star}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})-(\pi_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime}))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})\,\omega^{\star}_{n+1}(\cdot|s^{\prime})\big]d^{\omega_{n+1}}_{\pi^{\star}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s)+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)\\
&\leq\Delta+2\varepsilon+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon),\end{split}

(43)

where the second inequality follows from the observation that

\displaystyle(\pi^{\star}(\cdot|s))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s)\,\omega_{n+1}(\cdot|s)-(\pi_{n+1}(\cdot|s))^{\!\top}Q_{\pi_{n}}(s)\,\omega^{\star}_{n+1}(\cdot|s)\leq\Delta+2\varepsilon\quad\forall s\in{\mathcal{S}},

which in turn follows from Lemma
4.1
(ii)
applied to
(\pi,\omega)=(\pi^{\star},\omega^{\star}_{n+1})
.
Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle D\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi_{n}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n+1}}(s)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)+(1-\gamma)\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n}}(s)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle=D\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi_{n}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n+1}}(s)-\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)+\frac{D}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)+(1-\gamma)\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n}}(s)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})-V_{\pi_{n+1}}(s^{\prime})-\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)\right]d^{\omega_{n+1}}_{\pi^{\star}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s)\rho(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{D}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)+(1-\gamma)\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n}}(s)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi_{n}}(s^{\prime})-V_{\pi_{n+1}}(s^{\prime})\right]d^{\omega_{n+1}}_{\pi^{\star}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s)+(1-\gamma)(V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{n}}(s))\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)+\frac{D}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[\Delta+2\varepsilon+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)-\frac{1}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)+\frac{D}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta+2\varepsilon+\frac{D}{1-\gamma}(\Delta+2\varepsilon),

where the first inequality follows from
(
40

by construction, the second inequality holds because of (

The claim thus follows after dividing both sides in the above expression by

and rearranging terms.

Theorem 4.4

Let

. Suppose

, and define

\!\|\!\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}d^{\omega}_{\pi}(\cdot|s)\rho(\mathrm{d}s)/\rho(\cdot)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)\rho(\mathrm{d}s)-\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}V_{\pi_{N}}(s)\rho(\mathrm{d}s)\leq\epsilon.

In addition, the total number of samples required by Algorithm

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\left(\frac{\bar{B}^{2}\log(|\mathcal{K}|)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\bar{B}^{2}}{\delta(1-\gamma)^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\right)+J\delta\right)\frac{1}{\mu^{2}\nu^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\right).

We have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{N}}(s)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1-\gamma}{D}\right)^{\!N}\int_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left[V_{\pi^{\star}}(s)-V_{\pi_{0}}(s)\right]\rho(\mathrm{d}s)+\frac{2D^{2}(\Delta+2\varepsilon)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1-\gamma}{D}\right)^{\!N}\right)

where the first inequality follows by a recursive application of Lemma

,
and the third inequality uses the choice of

and the choices of
\varepsilon
and

and the choice of
\varepsilon
, which states that each iteration of Algorithm

requires at most

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\left(\frac{\bar{B}^{2}\log(|\mathcal{K}|)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\bar{B}^{2}}{\delta(1-\gamma)^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)+J\delta\right)\frac{1}{\mu^{2}\nu^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\right)

74
and model-free methods
1
,
7
.
On the other hand, it is unclear how the same sample complexity bound could be obtained for continuous-state MDPs, as existing optimal methods do not admit direct generalization to continuous state spaces.
There also exists a fruitful line of research on developing model-free methods for solving zero-sum Markov games with continuous state spaces.
Yet it appears that the current development either obtains suboptimal sample complexity
78
except for finite horizon problems
19
,
16
, or requires additional assumptions on the structure of the model
72
,
8
.
In this sense, the proposed method in this manuscript seems to be the first algorithm for general infinite-horizon zero-sum Markov games with order-wise optimal sample complexity of
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\epsilon^{2})
for continuous state spaces.

5
Concluding Remarks

In this manuscript, we study robust MDPs on continuous state spaces with a structured
\mathrm{s}
-rectangular ambiguity set.
The proposed ambiguity set lies within the convex hull of unknown generating kernels,
which are only accessible via samples.
We propose a stochastic first-order method for robust policy evaluation, and establish its high-probability convergence to the robust value function, from which we establish an
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\epsilon^{2})
sample complexity.
With the high-probability accuracy certificate of the robust policy evaluation, we introduce an approximate policy iteration method that finds an
\epsilon
-optimal policy for the robust policy optimization problem with
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\epsilon^{2})
sample complexity.
Notably the proposed methods operate independently of the size of the state space, and thus can be implemented efficiently for continuous state spaces.
The obtained sample complexities also appear to be new for solving robust MDPs with continuous state spaces.

We discuss a few directions that might be worth future investigation.
First, it is interesting to consider ambiguity sets that are, in some sense, generated nonlinearly from the generating kernels,
e.g.
, when the generation process involves a nonlinear parameterized function that is convex in its parameters.
It would also be interesting to study whether similar methods could be developed for nonparametric ambiguity sets, for instance those generated by
\phi
-divergence or Wasserstein distance.
Lastly, instead of the approximate policy iteration considered in this manuscript, it is also highly rewarding to directly design primal-dual-based methods for robust MDPs in the stochastic setting.
