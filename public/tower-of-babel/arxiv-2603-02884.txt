Spectrality of the Dirac Operator with Complex-Valued Periodic Coefficients
O. A. Veliev

Abstract
In this paper, we study the spectrality of the non-self-adjoint Dirac operator L ⁡ ( Q ) L(Q) with a complex-valued periodic 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrix potential Q . Q. We establish a condition on the off-diagonal elements of the matrix Q Q under which L ⁡ ( Q ) L(Q) is an asymptotically spectral operator. Moreover, we derive a condition on Q Q that ensures the spectrality of this operator. Finally, we consider the spectral expansion in these cases. Key Words: Dirac operator, Spectrality, Spectral expansion. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 34L05, 34L20.

\in L_{2}^{m}(-\infty,\infty)
the
following equality holds

f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{(-1,1]}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1,2;\text{ }\left|k\right|\leq N}}a_{k,j}(t)\Psi_{k,j,t}(x)dt+\frac{1}{2}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1,2;\text{ }\left|k\right|>N}}\int\limits_{(-1,1]}a_{k,j}(t)\Psi_{k,j,t}(x)dt,

where
a_{k,j}(t)=(f,X_{k,j,t}),

\Psi_{k,j,t}
is the normalized
eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{k,j}(t),
and
\left\{X_{k,j,t}:k\in\mathbb{Z},\text{ }j=1,2\right\}
is the biortogonal system to

\left\{\Psi_{k,j,t}:k\in\mathbb{Z},\text{ }j=1,2\right\}.
The series in
this formula converges in the norm of
L_{2}^{2}(a,b)
for every

a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Now, we determine a condition on the potential
Q
under which
L_{t}(Q)
is a
spectral operator. To this end, we look for a condition on
Q
that guarantees
the simplicity of the eigenvalues of
L_{t}(Q)
for all
t\in(-1,1].
For this
purpose, we consider the operator
L_{t}(Q)
as a perturbation of the operator

L_{t}(Q_{b})
by
Q-Q_{b}.
The eigenvalues of
L_{t}(Q_{b})
are simple (see
Remark 1) and lie on the horizontal lines

H(\pm b)=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y=\operatorname{Re}b\right\}\text{
and }H(\pm b)=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y=-\operatorname{Re}b\right\}.

(25)

The distance between neighboring eigenvalues of
L_{t}(Q_{b})
is either
2

(if they lie on the same line) or not less than
2\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|
(if they lie on the different line). Therefore, if the norm of

Q-Q_{b}
is sufficiently small, then the eigenvalues of
L_{t}(Q)
remain
simple for all
t\in(-1,1].
To determine this bound, we use the following
equality

(\lambda-(2n+\left(-1\right)^{j}(t+ib))(\Psi_{\lambda},\varphi_{n,j,t})=(\Psi_{\lambda},(Q-Q_{b})\varphi_{n,j,t}),

(26)

where
\Psi_{\lambda}
is the normalized eigenfunction of
L_{t}(Q)

corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
and
\varphi_{n,j,t}
is defined in
Remark 1. Equality (26) follows from the relation
L_{t}(Q)\Psi_{\lambda}=

\lambda\Psi_{\lambda},
by taking the inner product with
\varphi_{n,j,t}

and using

\left(L_{t}(Q_{b})\right)^{\ast}\varphi_{n,j,t}=(2n+\left(-1\right)^{j}(t-i\overline{b}))\varphi_{n,j,t}.

The following lemmas play a critical role in establishing the simplicity of
the eigenvalues of
L_{t}(Q)
for all
t\in(-1,1].

Lemma 1

If
\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|\geq 1
and

\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\left|a_{1}(x)\pm\left(a_{2}(x)+b\right)i\right|^{2}+\left|a_{3}(x)-b\pm\imath a_{4}(x)\right|^{2}dx\leq\frac{4}{\pi},

(27)

then the circles

C(1,2k\pm t\pm ib)=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\lambda-(2k\pm t\pm ib)\right|=1\right\}

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
lie in the resolvent set of
L_{t}(Q)
.

Proof.
Assume that there exists
\lambda\in C(1,2k+t+ib)
which is an eigenvalue of

L_{t}(Q)
. Then, by (26), we have

\left|\left(\Psi_{\lambda},\varphi_{n,j,t}\right)\right|^{2}\leq\frac{\left\|(Q-Q_{b})\varphi_{n,j,t}\right\|^{2}}{\left|\lambda-(2n+\left(-1\right)^{j}(t+ib))\right|^{2}}.

(28)

Using the definitions of
Q,

Q_{b}
and
\varphi_{n,j,t}
together with
condition (27), we obtain

\left\|(Q-Q_{b})\varphi_{n,j,t}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}},

for all
n,j
and
t.
Therefore, if

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|\lambda-(2n+t+ib)\right|^{2}}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|\lambda-(2n-t-ib)\right|^{2}}<\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2},

(29)

then we obtain the following contradiction:

1=\left\|\Psi_{\lambda}\right\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1,2;n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\left(\Psi_{\lambda},\varphi_{n,j,t}\right)\right|^{2}<1,

(30)

since
\left\{\varphi_{n,j,t}:j=1,2;\text{ }n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
is an
orthonormal basis.

Thus, it remains to prove (29). First, consider the first summation in (29).
By assumption
\lambda=2k+t+ib+x+iy,
where
x\in[-1,1]
and
y=\pm\sqrt{1-x^{2}}.
Then

\left|\lambda-(2n+t+ib)\right|^{2}=\left(2s+x\right)^{2}+1-x^{2}=4s^{2}+4sx+1,

(31)

where
s=k-n
. Therefore, we have

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|\lambda-(2n+t+ib)\right|^{2}}=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{4s^{2}+4sx+1}=1+

\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{4s^{2}+4sx+1}+\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{4s^{2}-4sx+1}=1+\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{2(4s^{2}+1)}{(4s^{2}+1)^{2}-16s^{2}x^{2}}.

Since the function
(4s^{2}+1)^{2}-16s^{2}x^{2}
on the interval
[-1,1]

attains its minimum value at the points
x=1
or
x=-1,
in the both cases,
using the well-known equality

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n-1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{8}\pi^{2},

we obtain

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|\lambda-(2n+t+ib)\right|^{2}}\leq 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{2}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n-1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{4}\pi^{2}.

(32)

Now, consider the second summation in (29). Since

\lambda-\left(2n-t-ib\right)=2k+t+ib+x+iy-\left(2n-t-ib\right)=2(k-n)+2t+x+2ib+iy,

where
b=\alpha+i\beta,
there exist
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
v\in(-1,1)
such
that

\lambda-\left(2n-t-ib\right)=2m+v+\imath(2\alpha+y).

Here,
\left|2\alpha+y\right|\geq\sqrt{1-v^{2}},
since
\left|\alpha\right|\geq 1.
Therefore,

\left|\lambda-(2n-t-ib)\right|^{2}=\left(2m+v\right)^{2}+1-v^{2}=4m^{2}+4mv+1.

(33)

Now, instead of (31), we use (33), where
s
and
x\in[-1,1]
are
replaced by
m
and
v\in(-1,1).
Repeating the proof of (32), we obtain

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|\lambda-(2n-t-ib)\right|^{2}}<\frac{1}{4}\pi^{2}.

(34)

Thus, (29) follows from(32) and (34). Hence, if the point
\lambda
from the
circle
C(1,2k+t+ib)
is an eigenvalue of
L_{t}(Q),
then we arrive at the
contradiction (30). This means that the circle lies in the resolvent set of

L_{t}(Q).
In the same way we prove that the circle
C(1,2k-t-ib)
also lies
in the resolvent set of
L_{t}(Q).
The lemma is proved.

The following lemma, which similar to Lemma 1, can be proved in a similar way
for the case
\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|<1
.

Lemma 2

If
0<\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|<1
and

\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\left|a_{1}(x)\pm\left(a_{2}(x)+b\right)i\right|^{2}+\left|a_{3}(x)-b\pm\imath a_{4}(x)\right|^{2}\leq 2\pi\left(\frac{2}{\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}\pi^{2}\right)^{-1},

(35)

then the circles

C(\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|,2k\pm t\pm ib)=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\lambda-(2k\pm t\pm ib)\right|=\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|\right\}

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
lie in the resolvent set of
L_{t}(Q)
.

Proof.
It follows from the argument used in Lemma 1 that it is enough to show that if

\lambda\in C(\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|,2k\pm t\pm ib),
then

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|\lambda-(2n+\left(-1\right)^{j}(t+ib)\right|^{2}}<\frac{1}{\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}}+\frac{1}{6}\pi^{2}.

(36)

for
j=1,2.
First consider the case
\lambda\in C(\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|,2k+t+ib)
and
j=2.
In this case, we have

\lambda=2k+t+ib+x+iy
and
\lambda-(2n+(t+ib)=2s+x+iy
, where

s=k-n,\text{ }x\in[-\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|,\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|],\text{ }y=\pm\sqrt{\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}-x^{2}}.

Therefore, we have

\left|\lambda-(2n+t+ib)\right|^{2}=\left(2s+x\right)^{2}+\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}-x^{2}=4s^{2}+4sx+\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}

(37)

and

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|\lambda-(2n+t+ib)\right|^{2}}=\frac{1}{\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}}+\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{4s^{2}+4sx+\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}}+

\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{4s^{2}-4sx+\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}}=\frac{1}{\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}}+\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{2(4s^{2}+\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2})}{(4s^{2}+\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2})^{2}-16s^{2}x^{2}}.

Since the function
(4s^{2}+\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2})^{2}-16s^{2}x^{2}
on the interval
[-\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|,\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|]
attains its minimum
value at the points
x=\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|
or

x=-\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|,
in the both cases, we obtain

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|\lambda-(2n+t+ib)\right|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}}+\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left(2s+\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|\right)^{2}}+\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left(2s-\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|\right)^{2}}.

(38)

Now, using the obvious equalities

\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left(2s+\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|\right)^{2}}<\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2s)^{2}}=\frac{1}{24}\pi^{2}

and

\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left(2s-\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|\right)^{2}}<\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2s-1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{8}\pi^{2},

we obtain

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\left|\lambda-(2n+(t+ib)\right|^{2}}<\frac{1}{\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}}+\frac{1}{6}\pi^{2}.

Thus, (36) for the case
j=2
is proved. In the same way, using the arguments
from the proof of (34), we prove (36) for the case
j=1.
Now, instead of (27)
and (29) using (35) and (36) and repeating the proof of Lemma 1, we obtain the
proof of this lemma.

Now, we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4

Suppose that either the conditions of Lemma 1 or the conditions of Lemma 2
hold. Then:

(a)
All eigenvalues of the operators
L_{t}(Q)
for all
t\in(-1,1]
are simple.

(b)

L(Q)
is a spectral operator.

(c)
For each
f

\in L_{2}^{m}(-\infty,\infty),
the following equality
holds:

f=\frac{1}{2}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1,2;\text{ }k\in\mathbb{Z}}}\int\limits_{(-1,1]}a_{k,j}(t)\Psi_{k,j,t}dt,

where the series converges in the
L_{2}^{2}(a,b)
-norm for every

a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

(a)
Consider the family of operators
L_{t}(Q_{b}+\varepsilon(Q-Q_{b}))
for

\varepsilon\in[0,1].
Instead of (26), using

(\lambda(\varepsilon)-(2n+\left(-1\right)^{j}(t+ib))(\Psi_{\lambda(\varepsilon)},\varphi_{n,j,t})=(\Psi_{\lambda(\varepsilon)},\varepsilon(Q-Q_{b})\varphi_{n,j,t}),

where
\Psi_{\lambda(\varepsilon)}
is the normalized eigenfunction of the
operator
L_{t}(Q_{b}+\varepsilon(Q-Q_{b})),
and repeating the proof of Lemma
1, we obtain that if
\left|\operatorname{Re}b\right|\geq 1
and (27)
holds, then the circles
C(1,2k\pm t\pm ib),
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z},
lie in
the resolvent set of
L_{t}(Q_{b}+\varepsilon(Q-Q_{b}))
for all

\varepsilon\in[0,1].
Since
L_{t}(Q_{b}+\varepsilon(Q-Q_{b}))

L_{t,\varepsilon}
forms a holomorphic family with respect to
\varepsilon,

and the operator
L_{t}(Q_{b})
has exactly one eigenvalue inside each of this
circles, the operator
L_{t}(Q)
must also have exactly one eigenvalue
(counting multiplicity) in this circle. This implies that all eigenvalues of

L_{t}(Q)
are simple. In the same way, we prove that all eigenvalues of

L_{t}(Q)
are simple if the conditions of Lemma 2 hold.

(b)
Let
\gamma(t)
be a closed contour lying in the resolvent set

\rho(L_{t}(Q)).
Then, there exist pairs
(k_{1},j_{1}),(k_{2},j_{2}),\cdot\cdot\cdot,(k_{s},j_{s})
from
\left\{(k,j)\in\mathbb{Z}:\left|k\right|<N,j=1,2\right\}
and a subset
\mathbb{D}(t)
of the set

\{(n,j):n\in\mathbb{Z},\mid n\mid\geq N,\text{ }j=1,2\}

such that
\gamma(t)
encloses the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k_{1},j_{1}}(t),\lambda_{k_{2},j_{2}}(t),\cdot\cdot\cdot,\lambda_{k_{s},j_{s}}(t)
and

\lambda_{n,j}(t)
for
\left(n,j\right)\in\mathbb{D}(t)
, where
N
is
defined in Theorem 1
(a)
and does not depend on
t
. Then, we have

e(\gamma(t))f={\textstyle\sum\limits_{i=1,2,...,s}}\frac{1}{\alpha_{k_{i}j_{i}}(t)}(f,\Psi_{k_{i}j_{i},t}^{\ast})\Psi_{k_{i}j_{i},t}+{\textstyle\sum\limits_{\left(n,j\right)\in\mathbb{D}(t)}}\frac{1}{\alpha_{n,j}(t)}(f,\Psi_{n,j,t}^{\ast})\Psi_{n,j,t}.

(39)

It is clear that

\left\|{\textstyle\sum\limits_{i=1,2,...,s}}\frac{1}{\alpha_{k_{i}j_{i}}(t)}(f,\Psi_{k_{i}j_{i},t}^{\ast})\Psi_{k_{i}j_{i},t}\right\|\leq{\textstyle\sum\limits_{i=1,2,...,s}}\frac{\left\|f\right\|}{\left|\alpha_{k_{i}j_{i}}(t)\right|}.

(40)

Moreover,
\left|\alpha_{k_{i}j_{i}}(t)\right|
depends continuously
on
t
and
\alpha_{k_{i}j_{i}}(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in[-1,1].

Therefore, it follows from (21), (24), (39), and (40) that

\left\|e(\gamma(t))\right\|<m_{6}

for all
t\in(-1,1]
and
\gamma(t)\subset\rho\left(L_{t}\right).
Thus,
the proof of
(b)
follows from Definition 1.

(c)
Since all eigenvalues are simple, the expression
a_{k,j}(t)\Psi_{k,j,t}
is integrable for all
j=1,2
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}.
Therefore, the
proof of
(c)
follows from Theorem 3.

Remark 2

Since the eigenvalues of
L_{t}(Q+aI),
where
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
I
is
the identity matrix, are obtained by shifting the eigenvalues of
L_{t}(Q),

and the eigenfunctions remain the same, Theorem 4 continues to hold if (27)
and (35) are replaced, respectively, by

\inf_{a}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\left|a_{1}(x)+a\pm\left(a_{2}(x)+b\right)i\right|^{2}+\left|a_{3}(x)-b\pm\imath(a_{4}(x)+a)\right|^{2}dx\leq\frac{4}{\pi},

and

\inf_{a}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\left|a_{1}(x)+a\pm\left(a_{2}(x)+b\right)i\right|^{2}+\left|a_{3}(x)-b\pm\imath(a_{4}(x)+a)\right|^{2}\leq 2\pi\left(\frac{2}{\left(\operatorname{Re}b\right)^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}\pi^{2}\right)^{-1}.
