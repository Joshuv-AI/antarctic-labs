Heavy Quark Transport is Non-Gaussian Beyond Leading Log Preprint: MIT-CTP/6020
Jean F. Du Plessis, Bruno Scheihing-Hitschfeld

Abstract
We find that heavy quark transport beyond leading logarithm at weak coupling is intrinsically non-Gaussian: the longitudinal momentum transfer distribution has asymmetric exponential tails that are crucial for equilibration dynamics. We show this by computing the leading-order momentum transfer kernel for relativistic heavy quarks in weakly coupled non-Abelian plasmas, matching perturbative momentum transfer on the thermal scale to hard-thermal-loop-resummed soft physics. This is the same structure previously found in strongly coupled holographic plasmas, showing that it is not peculiar to weak or strong coupling, conformality, or supersymmetry. We therefore expect that this is a robust feature that physical quark-gluon plasma should also exhibit.

This raises a natural question: is the same non-Gaussian structure present for heavy quarks in weakly coupled gauge theory plasmas?

In this Letter we answer that question in the affirmative. We find that heavy quark transport at weak coupling beyond leading logarithm exhibits manifest non-Gaussian structure: the longitudinal momentum transfer distribution has a Gaussian core and asymmetric exponential tails. The tails are essential for equilibration in light of the violation of the Einstein relation
42

To establish this result, we consistently match the ultraviolet perturbative contribution to the infrared HTL sector, isolate the strict fixed-order kernel, and determine the longitudinal momentum transfer distribution at all momenta via a Legendre transform. The full matched kernel reproduces the known leading-log results for the drag and momentum diffusion coefficients, as well as the known leading-order diffusion coefficient at
{\bf v}={\bf 0}

Outlook.

We have shown that the longitudinal momentum transfer distribution of a relativistic heavy quark in generic weakly coupled gauge theory plasmas has a Gaussian core and exponential tails, and that, in particular, weakly coupled QCD exhibits this behavior. This is the same qualitative structure previously found in strongly coupled
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theory
41
. This indicates that these features might be generic across many gauge theory plasmas, regardless of their coupling strength or their matter content. In particular, neither conformality nor supersymmetry seem to play a significant role.

Isolating the strict fixed-order kernel was essential to making this structure calculable at weak coupling.
Dissecting the two types of contributions to our result — distinguished by whether there is a logarithmic
\ln(1/g)
enhancement — we find that only the Gaussian characteristics of the momentum transfer probability contain logarithmically enhanced terms. Accordingly, the log-enhanced terms satisfy the Einstein relation, as had long been known
37
. At finite velocity, however, the remaining fixed-order contributions control the shape of the distribution and invalidate a purely Gaussian truncation, as the universal equilibration condition
42
is only satisfied through a detailed balance of all of the cumulants.
A Gaussian description is therefore not consistent with first-principles calculations for generic relativistic heavy-quark transport.

These results have direct implications for heavy-flavor phenomenology. Since non-Gaussian momentum transfer can modify heavy-flavor observables and the transport coefficients extracted from them
24
;
44
;
6
, phenomenological descriptions based purely on Gaussian or Langevin truncations are not microscopically justified by default. Our calculation therefore motivates revisiting heavy-quark transport in the QGP with non-Gaussian kernels if one aims to extract transport coefficients that can be meaningfully compared to lattice determinations. A more realistic,
non-Gaussian
description may be organized in terms of
\eta_{D}(v)
,
\kappa_{L}(v)
, and a single additional parameter
R(v)

), we obtain

\displaystyle\kappa_{\rm hard}=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi T^{2}}\Bigg[-\frac{N_{c}}{3D}\,T^{3}+\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\Bigg\{

\displaystyle\frac{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}}{D}\Bigg[\frac{pT}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\coth\frac{p}{4T}-1\right)-\frac{2T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\ln\left(1-e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)+\frac{4T^{3}}{\pi^{2}p}\Li\left(2,e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{D}\Bigg[\frac{4T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\ln\left(1+e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)-\frac{8T^{3}}{\pi^{2}p}\Li\left(2,-e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)\Bigg]\Bigg\}\Bigg].

(S27)

The remaining quadratic terms in Eq. (
10
) come from the second
line and the explicit matching subtraction in the third line.
These must be combined with the infrared-sensitive part of
Eq. (
S27
) before the final
p
integral is carried out.
The correct combination is

\displaystyle\kappa-\kappa_{\log}=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi T^{2}}\Bigg[I_{\rm elem}+T^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp}{p}\Bigg\{

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}}{D}\Li\left(2,e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)-\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{D}\Li\left(2,-e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)

\displaystyle\;+\frac{2}{3}\,\theta(T-p)\left(\frac{p^{4}}{(p^{2}+m_{D}^{2})^{2}}-1\right)\Bigg\}\Bigg],

(S28)

where the elementary contribution is now

\displaystyle I_{\rm elem}=-\frac{N_{c}}{3D}\,T^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}}{D}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,\frac{pT}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\coth\frac{p}{4T}-1\right)-\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,\frac{2T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\ln\left(1-e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{D}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,\frac{4T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\ln\left(1+e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right).

(S29)

These integrals are simply:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,\frac{pT}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\coth\frac{p}{4T}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{3}}{3},

(S30)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,\frac{2T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\ln\left(1-e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2T^{3}}{3},

(S31)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,\frac{4T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\ln\left(1+e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2T^{3}}{3},

(S32)

and therefore

I_{\rm elem}=T^{3}\left[\frac{2}{3}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{6D}\right].

(S33)

The remaining one-dimensional integral is most conveniently handled by a Mellin
transform.
Since extending the support of the last term in
Eq. (
S28
) from
p<T
to all
p
changes the result only
at
\mathcal{O}(g^{6})
, we may equivalently write

\displaystyle\kappa-\kappa_{\log}=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi T^{2}}\Bigg[I_{\rm elem}+T^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp}{p}\Bigg\{

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}}{D}\Li\left(2,e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)-\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{D}\Li\left(2,-e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)

\displaystyle\;+\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{p^{4}}{(p^{2}+m_{D}^{2})^{2}}-1\right)\Bigg\}\Bigg].

(S34)

Introducing a regulator
(p/2T)^{s}
and using

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x^{s-1}\Li\left(2,e^{-x}\right)

\displaystyle=\Gamma(s)\zeta(s+2),

(S35)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x^{s-1}\Li\left(2,-e^{-x}\right)

\displaystyle=-\Gamma(s)\eta(s+2),

(S36)

together with

\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,p^{s-1}\left(\frac{p^{4}}{(p^{2}+m_{D}^{2})^{2}}-1\right)=-\frac{m_{D}^{s}}{4}\,\pi(s+2)\csc\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right),

(S37)

and

\eta(s+2)=\bigl(1-2^{-s-1}\bigr)\zeta(s+2),

(S38)

we obtain, after expanding around
s=0
,

\displaystyle T^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp}{p}\Bigg\{\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}}{D}\Li\left(2,e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)-\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{D}\Li\left(2,-e^{-\frac{p}{2T}}\right)+\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{p^{4}}{(p^{2}+m_{D}^{2})^{2}}-1\right)\Bigg\}=

\displaystyle-\frac{T^{3}}{3}\Bigg[1+2\gamma_{E}-2\ln\left(\frac{2T}{m_{D}}\right)-2\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{D}\ln 2-2\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(2)}{\zeta(2)}\Bigg].

(S39)

Collecting Eqs. (
S19
),
(
S33
), and
(
S39
), we finally obtain

\kappa=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}T}{6\pi}\Bigg[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{4\left(N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}\right)}-\gamma_{E}+\ln\left(\frac{2T}{m_{D}}\right)+\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}}\ln 2+\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(2)}{\zeta(2)}\Bigg].

(S40)

For QCD,
N_{X}=0
.
Our
\kappa
is the diffusion coefficient for a single Cartesian component.
To compare with the convention of
Refs.
37
;
15
, where the correlator is summed over
the three spatial directions, one multiplies Eq. (
S40
) by

3
.

Appendix S3
v\to 0
limit of higher moments

In this appendix we derive the higher projected longitudinal moments at zero velocity.
Since there is no preferred direction for
{\bf v}={\bf 0}
, we again specialize to

L_{\perp}=0
and write
L\equiv L_{\parallel}
.
We also use the longitudinal kernel

K(x,0)=S_{\rm FO}({\bf L}=-ix\hat{\bf e};{\bf v}={\bf 0})=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{M}_{n}(0)}{n!}\,x^{n}.

(S41)

Therefore

\mathfrak{M}_{n}(0)=-\left.\frac{d^{n}K(x,0)}{dx^{n}}\right|_{x=0}.

(S42)

For
n\geq 4
, only the hard contribution is needed.
Indeed, the first two lines of Eq. (
10
) are manifestly quadratic in
L
,
and the explicit matching subtraction in the last line is also quadratic in
L
.
Hence they do not contribute to
\mathfrak{M}_{n}(0)
for
n\geq 4
.
Only the hard part of the last line contributes.

Taking the
v\to 0
limit of the hard integrand, and introducing

k\equiv\frac{2|k_{0}|}{p}\geq 1,

(S43)

exactly as in the previous appendix, one finds

\displaystyle K_{\rm hard}(x,0)=-\frac{g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi T^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\int_{-1}^{1}d\mu\int_{1}^{\infty}dk\Bigg\{

\displaystyle\frac{3}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}}{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}}\,\frac{\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{p\mu x}{2}\right)}{\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{pk}{4T}\right)}\left(\frac{4}{k^{2}}-4\right)

\displaystyle\;-\frac{3}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}}\,\frac{\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{p\mu x}{2}\right)}{\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{pk}{4T}\right)}

\displaystyle\;+k^{2}\Bigg[\frac{3}{16\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}}{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}}\,\frac{\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{p\mu x}{2}\right)}{\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{pk}{4T}\right)}

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21504pt+\frac{3}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}}\,\frac{\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{p\mu x}{2}\right)}{\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{pk}{4T}\right)}\Bigg]\Bigg\}.

(S44)

Because
\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{p\mu x}{2}\right)
is an even function of both
x
and
\mu
,
all odd moments vanish:

\mathfrak{M}_{2\ell+1}(0)=0.

(S45)

For even
n
,

\left.\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{p\mu x}{2}\right)\right|_{x=0}=\frac{1}{2}(p\mu)^{n},

(S46)

so for even
n\geq 4
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{n}(0)=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi T^{3}}\Bigg\{

\displaystyle\frac{3}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}}{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}}\,4\Big(\mathcal{J}^{(\sinh)}_{n,-2}(T)-\mathcal{J}^{(\sinh)}_{n,0}(T)\Big)

\displaystyle\;+\frac{3}{16\pi^{2}}\frac{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}}{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}}\,\mathcal{J}^{(\sinh)}_{n,2}(T)

\displaystyle\;+\frac{3}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{T_{F}N_{f}}{N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}}\,\Big(\mathcal{J}^{(\cosh)}_{n,2}(T)-\mathcal{J}^{(\cosh)}_{n,0}(T)\Big)\Bigg\},

(S47)

where we have introduced the handy integrals

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(\sinh)}_{n,m}(T)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\int_{-1}^{1}d\mu\int_{1}^{\infty}dk\;\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\,(p\mu)^{n}\,k^{m}}{\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{pk}{4T}\right)},

(S48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(\cosh)}_{n,m}(T)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\int_{-1}^{1}d\mu\int_{1}^{\infty}dk\;\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\,(p\mu)^{n}\,k^{m}}{\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{pk}{4T}\right)}.

(S49)

For odd
n
they vanish by parity.
For even
n
the
\mu
-integral gives

\int_{-1}^{1}d\mu\,\frac{1}{2}\mu^{n}=\frac{1}{n+1},

(S50)

so

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(\sinh)}_{n,m}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,p^{n}\int_{1}^{\infty}dk\;\frac{k^{m}}{\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{pk}{4T}\right)},

(S51)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(\cosh)}_{n,m}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp\,p^{n}\int_{1}^{\infty}dk\;\frac{k^{m}}{\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{pk}{4T}\right)}.

(S52)

Now set

z\equiv\frac{pk}{4T},

(S53)

which gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(\sinh)}_{n,m}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{(4T)^{n+1}}{n+1}\int_{1}^{\infty}dk\,k^{m-n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\frac{z^{n}}{\sinh^{2}z},

(S54)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(\cosh)}_{n,m}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{(4T)^{n+1}}{n+1}\int_{1}^{\infty}dk\,k^{m-n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\frac{z^{n}}{\cosh^{2}z}.

(S55)

Using

\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}z}=4\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}re^{-2rz},\qquad\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}z}=4\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{r+1}re^{-2rz},

(S56)

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\frac{z^{n}}{\sinh^{2}z}

\displaystyle=2^{1-n}n!\,\zeta(n),

(S57)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\frac{z^{n}}{\cosh^{2}z}

\displaystyle=2^{1-n}n!\,(1-2^{1-n})\,\zeta(n).

(S58)

The remaining
k
-integral is

\int_{1}^{\infty}dk\,k^{m-n-1}=\frac{1}{n-m},

(S59)

valid for
m<n
, which is the case for the three values
m=-2,0,2
needed here.
Therefore, for even
n\geq 4
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(\sinh)}_{n,m}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{4(2T)^{n+1}n!\,\zeta(n)}{(n+1)(n-m)},

(S60)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(\cosh)}_{n,m}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{4(2T)^{n+1}n!\,(1-2^{1-n})\,\zeta(n)}{(n+1)(n-m)}.

(S61)

Substituting Eqs. (
S60
) and (
S61
)
into Eq. (
S47
), we obtain the closed-form result

\boxed{\mathfrak{M}_{n}(0)=\frac{3\,g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}\,2^{\,n-1}T^{\,n-2}(n-1)!\,\zeta(n)}{\pi^{3}(n+1)\left(N_{c}+\frac{N_{c}N_{X}}{2}+T_{F}N_{f}\right)}\left[\frac{N_{c}\left(N_{X}n(n+2)+2(n^{2}-2n+8)\right)}{8(n-2)(n+2)}+\frac{(1-2^{1-n})\,T_{F}N_{f}}{n-2}\right]}

(S62)

for all even
n\geq 4
, while
\mathfrak{M}_{n}(0)=0
for odd
n
.

As expected, this formula does not apply to
n=2
: the factor
(n-2)^{-1}
signals the infrared sensitivity of the diffusion coefficient, which is precisely why the
n=2
moment had to be treated separately in the previous appendix.

Appendix S4
Matching-scale invariance

Choosing an arbitrary matching scale
\Lambda\sim g^{0}T
leads to Eq. (
10
) changing to

\displaystyle S_{\rm FO}({\bf L};{\bf v})=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi}\ln\left(\frac{m_{D}}{\Lambda}\right)\Bigg[\frac{(v^{2}-1)\arctanh(v)+v}{2v^{3}}\,iL_{\parallel}\left(iL_{\parallel}+\frac{v}{T}\right)+\frac{3v^{3}+(1-v^{2})^{2}\arctanh(v)-v}{4v^{3}}(iL_{\perp})^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{1}d\mu\Bigg[\mu^{2}\,iL_{\parallel}\left(iL_{\parallel}+\frac{v}{T}\right)+\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}(iL_{\perp})^{2}\Bigg]\Bigg[\Phi\left(a_{E},b_{E}\right)+\frac{v^{2}(1-\mu^{2})}{2(1-v^{2}\mu^{2})}\Phi\left(a_{T},b_{T}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{T}\int_{\bf p}\left\{g^{2}\frac{e^{i{\bf p}\cdot{\bf L}}-1}{1-e^{-{\bf p}\cdot{\bf v}/T}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\rm Hard}-\theta(\Lambda-|{\bf p}|)\,({\bf p}\cdot i{\bf L})\left[{\bf p}\cdot\left(i{\bf L}+\frac{{\bf v}}{T}\right)\right]\frac{T}{2\,{\bf p}\cdot{\bf v}}\,\mathcal{I}_{\rm Match}\right\}.

(S63)

Differentiating with respect to
\Lambda
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}S_{\rm FO}({\bf L};{\bf v})=

\displaystyle-\frac{g^{2}C_{F}m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\Lambda}\Bigg[\frac{(v^{2}-1)\arctanh(v)+v}{2v^{3}}\,iL_{\parallel}\left(iL_{\parallel}+\frac{v}{T}\right)+\frac{3v^{3}+(1-v^{2})^{2}\arctanh(v)-v}{4v^{3}}(iL_{\perp})^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+g^{2}C_{F}\int_{\bf p}\delta(\Lambda-|{\bf p}|)\,({\bf p}\cdot i{\bf L})\left[{\bf p}\cdot\left(i{\bf L}+\frac{{\bf v}}{T}\right)\right]\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\rm Match}}{2\,{\bf p}\cdot{\bf v}}.

(S64)

Using

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\rm Match}}{2\,{\bf p}\cdot{\bf v}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2pv\mu}\left[\frac{2\Gamma_{E}(\mu v)}{p^{4}}+\frac{2v^{2}(1-\mu^{2})\Gamma_{T}(\mu v)}{p^{4}(1-v^{2}\mu^{2})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi m_{D}^{2}}{2p^{5}}\left[1+\frac{v^{2}(1-\mu^{2})}{2(1-v^{2}\mu^{2})}\right],

(S65)

together with

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\,({\bf p}\cdot i{\bf L})\,\left[{\bf p}\cdot\left(i{\bf L}+\frac{{\bf v}}{T}\right)\right]=p^{2}\left[\mu^{2}\,iL_{\parallel}\left(iL_{\parallel}+\frac{v}{T}\right)+\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}(iL_{\perp})^{2}\right],

(S66)

we find

\displaystyle\int_{\bf p}\delta(\Lambda-|{\bf p}|)\,({\bf p}\cdot i{\bf L})\left[{\bf p}\cdot\left(i{\bf L}+\frac{{\bf v}}{T}\right)\right]\frac{\mathcal{I}_{\rm Match}}{2\,{\bf p}\cdot{\bf v}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi\,\Lambda}\int_{0}^{1}d\mu\,\left[\mu^{2}\,iL_{\parallel}\left(iL_{\parallel}+\frac{v}{T}\right)+\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{2}(iL_{\perp})^{2}\right]\left[1+\frac{v^{2}(1-\mu^{2})}{2(1-v^{2}\mu^{2})}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{m_{D}^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\Lambda}\Bigg[\frac{(v^{2}-1)\arctanh(v)+v}{2v^{3}}\,iL_{\parallel}\left(iL_{\parallel}+\frac{v}{T}\right)+\frac{3v^{3}+(1-v^{2})^{2}\arctanh(v)-v}{4v^{3}}(iL_{\perp})^{2}\Bigg].

(S67)

Therefore

\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}S_{\rm FO}({\bf L};{\bf v})=0,

(S68)

proving matching-scale independence.
