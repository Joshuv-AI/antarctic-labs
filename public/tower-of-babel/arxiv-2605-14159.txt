Boundary dynamics, triple transitivity, and mixed identities in weakly hyperbolic groups.
Ekaterina Rybak

Abstract
† † MSC Primary: 20F65, 20F69. Secondary: 20F67, 46L05 We study the interplay between the algebraic and dynamical properties of groups that admit a general type action on a δ \delta -hyperbolic space such that the induced action on the limit set of the Gromov boundary is faithful. We divide the class of such groups into two subclasses based on a dynamical criterion: groups whose induced action on the limit set is topologically free, and those whose action is not. We prove that satisfying the criterion is equivalent to a purely algebraic property of being mixed identity free, generalizing results of P. Fima, F. Le Maître, S. Moon, and Y. Stalder from groups acting on trees to groups acting on arbitrary hyperbolic spaces. As one of the corollaries, we obtain that the reduced C ∗ C^{*} -algebra of such groups is selfless, using the results of N. Ozawa. For the subclass of groups whose action on the limit set is not topologically free, we give the rigidity result for all 3 3 -transitive faithful actions and bound the transitivity degree by 3 3 , generalizing the result of A. Le Boudec and N. Matte Bon. As an application, we show that non-affine simple Kac-Moody groups over finite fields are MIF, answering the question of J. Belk, F. Fournier-Facio, J. Hyde, and M. Zaremsky.

Introduction

1.1
Weakly hyperbolic groups

One of the generalizations of the class of non-elementary hyperbolic groups is the class of acylindrically hyperbolic groups. Recently, the studies of acylindrically hyperbolic groups attracted a lot of attention. A group is
acylindrically hyperbolic
if it admits an acylindrical general type action on a hyperbolic space. The acylindricity condition is the generalization of the properness of the action. We refer the reader to
[
Osi16
]
for the definitions. The class of acylindrically hyperbolic groups is large; in particular, it includes all non-elementary hyperbolic and relatively hyperbolic groups, mapping class groups of punctured closed surfaces except for surfaces with genus
g=0
and less than
3
punctures,
Out(F_{n})
for
n\geq 2
, directly indecomposable right-angled Artin groups, and 1-relator groups with at least 3 generators. Despite being such a broad class, the requirement of the acylindricity of the action is restrictive enough to obtain nontrivial results about the algebraic properties of acylindrically hyperbolic groups. For example, acylindrically hyperbolic groups are closed under taking infinite normal subgroups
[
Osi16
]
, are SQ-universal, and have finite amenable radical
[
DGO
]
. Finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic groups have cut points in all of their asymptotic cones
[
Sis
]
and have exponential conjugacy growth
[
HO13
]
.

We want to enlarge the class of acylindrically hyperbolic groups even further and study what properties hold when the acylindricity condition is removed. Following the terminology from
[
MT
]
, we say

Definition 1.1
.

A group
G
is
weakly hyperbolic
if it admits a general type action on a geodesic hyperbolic space. An isometric action on a hyperbolic space is
of general type
if orbits are unbounded and
G
contains two loxodromic elements with disjoint fixed points on the Gromov boundary.

3

denotes the projective special linear group over
\mathbb{C}
.

We refer the reader to Section
2.1
for details on Gromov hyperbolic spaces and the classification of group actions on them.
Examples of weakly hyperbolic groups that are not necessarily acylindrically hyperbolic come from groups acting on trees and, more generally, groups of isometries of hyperbolic spaces.

Many properties of particular examples of weakly hyperbolic groups are well-represented in the literature. Various results on random walks in weakly hyperbolic groups were obtained in
[
MT
]
,
[
Sun
]
,
[
BMSS
]
,
[
AMS
]
,
[
AS
]
,
[
GTT22
]
,
[
Cho
]
, and
[
Hor
]
. However, not many group structural results are known about the whole class. Using a simple ping-pong argument, one can prove that any weakly hyperbolic group contains a nonabelian free subgroup (see
[
Gro87
, 8.2.F]
or
[
Ham
, Thm. 2.7]
). In
[
Osi17
]
, it is proved that for every invariant random subgroup
H
of a countable weakly hyperbolic group
G
, when
G
acts on some hyperbolic space via a general type action, the induced action of
H
either has the same limit set as
G
or all orbits of
H
are bounded. In particular, every normal subgroup of a weakly hyperbolic group satisfies this dichotomy. In
[
GOR
, Thm 1.2]
it is proved that the Frattini subgroup of a countable weakly hyperbolic group acts on a hyperbolic space with bounded orbits, and thus has infinite index.

A natural question to ask is what other interesting algebraic properties are satisfied by every weakly hyperbolic group. Finding such properties is challenging, as having just the general type action without any properness condition is not very limiting. Therefore, we want to impose an additional condition on the group action.

Recall that the limit set
\Lambda_{S}(G)
in the Gromov boundary of a hyperbolic space
S
is the closure of the set of accumulation points of orbits under the
G
-action.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a group
G
is
faithful weakly hyperbolic
if it admits a general type action on a hyperbolic space
S
such that the induced
G
-action on
\Lambda_{S}(G)
is faithful.

Note that not every weakly hyperbolic group is faithful weakly hyperbolic. A source of examples of weakly hyperbolic groups that are not faithful weakly hyperbolic groups is given in Proposition
3.32
. However, by Proposition
A
, any weakly hyperbolic group has a faithful weakly hyperbolic quotient. Here,
E_{S}(G)
is the
elliptic radical
with respect to the action of
G
on
S
. It is the set of all elements of
G
that act on
\Lambda_{S}(G)
trivially. We refer the reader to Section
2.3
for the basic properties of
E_{S}(G)
.

Proposition A
.

Suppose that a group
G
admits a general type action on a hyperbolic space
S
. Then
G/E_{S}(G)
is faithful weakly hyperbolic.

Let
X
be a topological space. Recall that an action of a group
G
on
X
by homeomorphisms is
topologically free
if the set of fixed points
\Fix_{g}(X)
of every non-trivial element
g\in G
has an empty interior.

Definition 1.3
.

A weakly hyperbolic group
G
is called
lim-free
if it admits a general type action on a hyperbolic space
S
such that the induced action on
\Lambda_{S}(G)
is topologically free.

We have an obvious inclusion: Lim-free weakly hyperbolic groups are faithful weakly hyperbolic groups. While the property of being weakly hyperbolic is not closed under taking non-trivial normal subgroups, both properties of being faithful weakly hyperbolic and of being lim-free are closed under taking non-trivial normal subgroups by Lemma
2.26
. We will see that lim-free weakly hyperbolic groups and faithful weakly hyperbolic groups that are not lim-free are two distinct classes. Groups from the former class include (see Lemma
3.12
) and behave similarly to acylindrically hyperbolic groups without non-trivial finite normal subgroups, while groups from the latter class have very different algebraic properties.

1.2
MIF weakly hyperbolic groups

Let
F_{n}
denote a free group of rank
n
. Recall that a group
G
satisfies a
mixed identity

w=1
for some
w\in G*F_{n}
if every homomorphism
G*F_{n}\rightarrow G
that is the identity on
G
sends
w
to
1
. A mixed identity
w=1
is
non-trivial
if
w
is a non-trivial element of
G*F_{n}
, and a group
G
is
mixed identity free
(or MIF for brevity) if it does not satisfy any non-trivial mixed identity.
The property of being MIF imposes strong restrictions on the algebraic structure of
G
. If
G
is MIF, then
G
has infinite conjugacy classes, it is directly indecomposable and has infinite girth (see
[
HO16
, Prop. 5.4]
). A countable group
G
is MIF if and only if
G
and
G*F_{n}
are universally equivalent as
G
-groups for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see
[
HO16
, Prop. 5.3]
). A surprising connection between MIF groups and the Boone-Higman conjecture was recently discovered in
[
BFFHZ
]
.

In our first main theorem, part (b) reveals a rigidity phenomenon: properties of one general type action impose strong constraints on all general type actions. Furthermore, part (c) shows that the geometrically defined class of lim-free groups can be characterized within the class of faithful weakly hyperbolic groups by a purely algebraic condition.

Theorem B
.

For any faithful weakly hyperbolic group
G
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

G
is lim-free;

(b)

for any general type action of G on a hyperbolic space with trivial elliptic radical, the induced action on the limit set is topologically free;

(c)

G
is MIF.

Considering mixed identities in weakly hyperbolic groups was inspired by the results about groups acting on simplicial trees. An action of a group
G
on a tree
T
is called
minimal
if there are no nontrivial invariant subtrees. Combining their results with
[
BM
, Prop. 3.7]
, P. Fima, F. Le Maître, S. Moon, and Y. Stalder prove that if the action of a group
G
on a simplicial tree
T
is faithful, minimal, and of general type, then
G
is MIF if and only if the induced action on
\partial T
is topologically free
[
FMMS
, Theorem B]
. Their result can be obtained as a corollary of Theorem
B
(See Cor
3.14
).

One of the applications of Theorem
B
, not covered by
[
FMMS
, Theorem B]
, is the proof that infinite simple Kac-Moody groups over finite fields are MIF (see Example
3.43
). This partially answers Question 3.6 from
[
BFFHZ
]
.

Let
G
be a group and
w(t)\in G*\langle t\rangle
be a mixed identity. The solution set
\{g\in G\mid w(g)=1\}
is called
the primitive solution set corresponding to
w(t)
.
The Zariski topology
on
G
is defined by taking the collection of primitive solution sets to be a sub-basis for the closed sets of the topology. A Zariski-closed subgroup (or more generally, a subset) of
G
is called
algebraic
. The natural question to ask is what proper subgroups of a group
G
can be algebraic. If
G
is torsion-free non-elementary hyperbolic, then all such subgroups are cyclic
[
KM
]
. In
[
Jac
]
, structural conditions for non-elementary algebraic subgroups of acylindrically hyperbolic groups were obtained. By a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
B
, we get

Corollary C
.

Let
G
be a lim-free weakly hyperbolic group. Then
G
does not contain any non-trivial proper normal Zariski-closed subgroups.

Non-lim-free faithful weakly hyperbolic groups also have interesting common purely algebraic properties. For example, they contain infinite direct products of infinite non-abelian subgroups (see Corollary
2.36
. Recall that the
monolith

\Mon(G)
of a group
G
is the intersection of all non-trivial normal subgroups of
G
, and
G
is said to be
monolithic
if
\Mon(G)\neq\{1\}
. As a corollary of
[
GR
, Corollary 1.7]
, we obtain the following.

Proposition D
.

Let
G
be a faithful weakly hyperbolic group. If
G
is not lim-free, then it is monolithic with an infinite simple monolith.

In this way, non-lim-free faithful weakly hyperbolic groups are very different from acylindrically hyperbolic groups, whose monolith is always trivial (see Proposition
3.11
). This allows us to obtain the result of M. Hull and D. Osin
[
HO16
, Corollary 5.10]
that acylindrically hyperbolic groups without nontrivial normal finite subgroups are MIF as a corollary of Theorem
B
and Proposition
D
(see Corollary
3.12
).

Another corollary of Proposition
D
gives us a large class of lim-free weakly hyperbolic groups.

Corollary E
.

Every residually finite faithful weakly hyperbolic group is lim-free. In particular, every finitely generated linear faithful weakly hyperbolic group is lim-free.

The famous Tits Alternative states that a finitely generated linear group is either virtually solvable or contains a nonabelian free subgroup. As another corollary of Theorem
B
, we obtain an analogous statement for all subgroups of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

Corollary F
.

Every subgroup of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is either virtually solvable or MIF.

We should note that in
[
GGS
]
, it is proved that any center-free unbounded and non-virtually solvable countable subgroup of
\SL_{2}(k)
is highly transitive, where

is a local field, which implies being MIF by the result of
[
HO16
]
. And in
[
Tom

, Tomanov gives necessary and sufficient conditions for Zariski-dense subgroups of linear groups to be MIF. Corollary

doesn’t require any assumptions on subgroups of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and is not an immediate consequence of Corollary
E
,
[
Tom
]
, or
[
GGS
]
.

A property called
selflessness
was recently introduced for a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra by L. Robert. It implies many important regularity properties such as simplicity, (in the tracial setting) stable rank one, and strict comparison.
We refer the reader to
[
Rob
]
for the definitions. In
[
AGKEP
]
, Tattwamasi Amrutam, David Gao, Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli, and Gregory Patchell proved that acylindrically hyperbolic groups without finite normal subgroups have selfless reduced
C^{*}
-algebras. In their proof, they used a stronger quantitative version of the property of being MIF and the rapid decay property. It attracted attention to the quantitative version of being MIF, which resulted in a series of works:
[
BS
]
,
[
AG
]
,
[
Vid
]
. Later, in his remarkable paper, Ozawa obtained a dynamical condition for the group to have a selfless reduced
C^{*}
-algebra. Using the results of
[
Oza
]
, we obtain

Corollary G
.

If a weakly hyperbolic group
G
is lim-free, its reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{\lambda}^{*}(G)
is selfless.

Corollary
G
gives us the first example of finitely presented simple groups with selfless reduced
C^{*}
-algebras.

Proposition H
.

Non-affine simple finitely presented Kac-Moody groups over finite fields constructed in
[
CR
]
are MIF, and their reduced
C^{*}
-algebras are selfless.

1.3
Rigidity of
3
-transitive actions

Recall that a
G
-action on some set
\Omega
is
k
-transitive
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
if for any two
k
-tuples of distinct points
(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k})\in\Omega^{k}
and
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in\Omega^{k}
, there exists
g\in G
such that
g\omega_{i}=v_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,k
. As discussed earlier, Theorem
B
gives a rigidity phenomenon for general type actions of lim-free weakly hyperbolic groups. If a group is faithful weakly hyperbolic, but not lim-free, we obtain a rigidity phenomenon for all
3
-transitive actions on any set, as our second main theorem shows.

Definition 1.4
.

Two actions of a group
G
on sets
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
are
conjugate
if there exists a bijective
G
-equivariant map
\Omega\rightarrow\Omega^{\prime}
.

Theorem I
.

Suppose that the action of a group
G
on a hyperbolic space
S
is of general type and that the induced action of
G
on
\Lambda_{S}(G)
is faithful and not topologically free.
Assume that
G
acts faithfully and 3-transitively on a set
\Omega
. Then there exists a
G
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\Lambda_{S}(G)
such that the actions of
G
on
\Omega
and on
\mathcal{O}
are conjugate.

This theorem is the analogue of
[
BM
, Theorem 1.4]
for group actions on simplicial trees. We generalize their arguments to groups acting on arbitrary hyperbolic spaces and obtain their result as a corollary (see Corollary
3.29
).

The
transitivity degree
of a group
G
is the maximum
k
such that
G
admits a
k
-transitive faithful action on some set. Among infinite groups, examples of groups with transitivity degree
1
,
2
,
3
, and
\infty
are known. Whether there exists an infinite group with transitivity degree
4
is an open question. The next corollary shows that if such an example exists, it cannot be a faithful weakly hyperbolic group that is not lim-free.

Corollary J
.

Let
G
be a faithful weakly hyperbolic group that is not lim-free. Then the transitivity degree of
G
is at most
3
.

Recall that an action of a group
G
on a topological space
X
is
topologically transitive
if, for every pair of nonempty open subsets
U
and
V
of
X
, there is an element
g\in G
such that
g(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
. It is
topologically
k
-transitive
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, if the induced action of
G
on the
k
-fold Cartesian product
X^{k}
is topologically transitive.

Since Theorem
I
tells us that the limit set “sees” all
3
-transitive actions of a faithful weakly hyperbolic group
G
that is not lim-free, it is sufficient just to look at the
G
-action on the limit set to tell if the transitivity degree of
G
is
3
or less, as the following corollary shows.

Corollary K
.

Let
G
be a faithful weakly hyperbolic group that is not lim-free. If the transitivity degree of
G
is
3
, then for any general type action of
G
on any hyperbolic space
S
, if the induced action on
\Lambda_{S}(G)
is faithful, it is topologically
3
-transitive.

2.1
Group actions on hyperbolic spaces

We refer the reader to
[
DSU
, Chapter 3]
and
[
BS07
]
for more details on the material discussed in this subsection.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
S
be a geodesic metric space. Recall that the
Gromov product
of two points
x,y\in S
with respect to a point
o\in S
is defined by

\langle x|y\rangle_{o}:=\frac{1}{2}({\rm d}_{S}(x,o)+{\rm d}_{S}(y,o)-{\rm d}_{S}(x,y)).

Definition 2.2

(Gromov hyperbolicity)
.

A geodesic metric space
S
is
\delta
-hyperbolic
, if for any points
x,y,z,o\in S
, we have

\langle x|y\rangle_{o}\geq\min\left\{\langle x|z\rangle_{o},\langle z|y\rangle_{o}\right\}-\delta.

If
Y\subseteq S
is a subspace, then for any constant
K\geq 0
, we denote the closed
K
-neighborhood of
Y
in
S
by
\mathcal{N}_{K}(Y)=\left\{x\in S\mid{\rm d}_{S}(x,Y)\leq K\right\}
.

Definition 2.3

(Rips hyperbolicity)
.

Fix
\delta\geq 0
. A geodesic metric space
S
is
\delta
-hyperbolic if given any
x,y,z\in S
and any geodesics
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
between them, we have
\alpha\cup\beta\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\gamma)
.

A geodesic metric space is Rips hyperbolic if and only if it is Gromov hyperbolic (see, for example,
[
KS
, Lemma 1.46]
). In this work, we choose
\delta
in a way that
S
satisfies both Definitions
2.1
and Definition
2.3
simultaneously.

A sequence
\left(x_{i}\right)
of elements of
S

converges at infinity
if
\langle x_{i}|x_{j}\rangle_{o}\rightarrow\infty
as
i,j\rightarrow\infty
for some, or equivalently for any, point
o\in S
. Two such sequences
\left(x_{i}\right)
and
\left(y_{i}\right)
are
equivalent
if
\langle x_{i}|y_{j}\rangle_{o}\rightarrow\infty
as
i,j\rightarrow\infty
. The
Gromov boundary
of
S
, denoted by
\partial S
, is defined as the set of equivalence classes of sequences converging at infinity. If
a
is the equivalence class of
\left(x_{i}\right)
, we say that the sequence
(x_{i})
converges to
a
. There is a natural topology on
\widehat{S}=S\cup\partial S
extending the topology on
S
such that
S
is dense in
\widehat{S}
, which will be discussed later. The definition of
\partial S
is independent of the choice of the basepoint, and any isometric group action on
S
extends to a continuous action on
\widehat{S}
. The Gromov product can be extended to
\widehat{S}
in the following way.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
a,b\in\partial S
and
o,x\in S
.

1.

The
Gromov product
of
a
and
b
with respect to
o
is defined by

\langle a|b\rangle_{o}=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i,j\rightarrow+\infty}\langle x_{i}|y_{j}\rangle_{o}\;\bigm|\;(x_{i})\rightarrow a,(y_{j})\rightarrow b\right\}.

2.

The
Gromov product
of
a
and
x
with respect to
o
is defined by

\langle a|x\rangle_{o}=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\langle x_{i}|x\rangle_{o}\;\bigm|\;(x_{i})\rightarrow a\right\}.

The next lemma shows that taking the
\liminf_{i\to\infty}
in the definition of the Gromov product of the boundary points is the same as taking
\limsup_{i\to\infty}
up to a constant and that the boundary points also satisfy the four-point condition.

Lemma 2.5

(Lemma 2.2.2
[
BS07
]
)
.

Let
S
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic space. Let
o\in S
, and let
a,b,c\in\partial S
.

1.

For arbitrary sequences
\left(x_{i}\right)\rightarrow a,\left(y_{i}\right)\rightarrow b
, we have

\langle a|b\rangle_{o}\leq\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\langle x_{i}|y_{i}\rangle_{o}\leq\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}\langle x_{i}|y_{i}\rangle_{o}\leq\langle a|b\rangle_{o}+2\delta.

2.

\langle a|b\rangle_{o}\geq\min\left\{\langle a|c\rangle_{o},\langle c|b\rangle_{o}\right\}-\delta.

The Gromov product of distinct points of the boundary is always finite, i.e., if
a,b\in\partial S
, then
\langle a|b\rangle_{o}=\infty
if and only if
a=b
. It is also preserved by isometries of
S
, i.e, if
g
is an isometry of
S
,
a,b\in\widehat{S}
then
\langle ga|gb\rangle_{go}=\langle a|b\rangle_{o}
. (see
[
DSU
, Section 3.4.1]
).

For any point
a\in\partial S
,
o\in S
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}
denote by

\mathcal{U}_{o}(a,r)=\left\{s\in\widehat{S}\mid\langle a|s\rangle_{o}>r\right\}.

The subset
\mathcal{U}_{o}(a,r)
is open in the topology on
\widehat{S}
, and
\{\mathcal{U}_{o}(a,r)\mid r\geq 0\}
form a neighborhood base for the topology on
\widehat{S}
at
a
. (see
[
DSU
, Remark 3.4.15 (II)]
).

The
limit set

\Lambda_{S}(G)
of
G
is defined as the intersection of the closure of
Gs
in
\widehat{S}
with
\partial S
. This definition is independent of the choice of
s\in S
.

An element
g\in G
is said to be
loxodromic
if the group
\langle g\rangle
has unbounded orbits and fixes exactly
2
points on
\partial S
(equivalently,
|\Lambda_{S}(\langle g\rangle)|=2
). By
\mathcal{L}_{S}(G)
we denote the set of all loxodromic elements of

.

The following lemma establishes the standard notation for fixed points of loxodromic elements, motivated by the so-called north-south dynamics (see
[
Gro87

[
DSU

Lemma 2.6
.

The fixed points of
g\in\mathcal{L}_{S}(G)
can be denoted by

The next lemma shows that the property of being faithful weakly hyperbolic and the property of being lim-free pass to normal subgroups; thus, all the results in this work pass to normal subgroups.

Lemma 2.26

2.4

Definition 2.28
.

Suppose that a group

Definition 2.29

\rist_{G}(U):=\{g\in G\mid gx=x\quad\forall x\in X\setminus U\}.

Definition 2.30

An action of a group

. If the action of

\rist_{G}(U)\neq 1
for every nonempty open subset

.

The following fact is standard and known to experts (for example, see the proof of
[

, Proposition H]

Lemma 2.31

Let

is non-abelian for every nonempty open subset

Corollary 2.36
.

Let
G
be a faithful weakly hyperbolic group that is not lim-free. Then

contains infinite direct sums of infinite non-abelian subgroups.

Proof.

Let
S
be a hyperbolic space such that the action of
G
on
S
is of general type, and the induced action on
\Lambda_{S}(G)
is faithful and not topologically free. Take an infinite set of pairwise disjoint non-empty open subsets

. By Lemma

, rigid stabilizers
\rist_{G}(V_{i})
are nonempty, and by Lemma

. Elements in

We already know that if the

-action on a Hausdorff topological space

is micro-supported, then the rigid stabilizer of a nonempty open subset

is non-abelian. If, in addition,

is

-compressible, we can say more.

Lemma 2.37
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of the homeomorphism group of a Hausdorff topological space
X
, and

be a nonempty open
G
-compressible subset. Either
\rist_{G}(U)
is torsion-free, or there exists a finite
k\geq 2
, such that

\rist_{G}(U)\wr\mathbb{Z}_{k}<\rist_{G}(U)
.

Proof.

Suppose that a non-identity element
g\in\rist_{G}(U)
has finite order. Take
x\in U
such that the orbit of
x
under the action of
\langle g\rangle
has the maximum size
k
. Since the action of
G
on
X
is faithful,
k\geq 2
and divides the order of
g
. Consider the distinct points
gx,\dots,g^{k-1}x
. Since
X
is Hausdorff, there exist disjoint open subsets
V_{0},\dots V_{k-1}
, such that
g^{i}x\in V_{i}
for

. Consider the subset

It is open and nonempty since

\langle g\rangle
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
acts on

by permutations, and

are pairwise disjoint.

Since

is
G
-compressible, there exists

such that

. Therefore,

g^{i}f\rist_{G}(U)(g^{i}f)^{-1}\leq\rist_{G}(g^{i}V)\leq\rist_{G}(U),\;\;i\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}.

\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}g^{i}f\rist_{G}(U)(g^{i}f)^{-1}

\rist_{G}(U)\wr\mathbb{Z}_{k}<\rist_{G}(U)
.
∎

3.1
The cross-ratio and topological transitivity of actions on the limit set.

In this subsection, we develop a technical tool that is later used in the proof of Proposition
3.16
. We also use it to prove that the induced action on the Gromov boundary of a general-type group is never topologically
4
-transitive (see Proposition

). A similar notion in the case of proper hyperbolic spaces was considered in
[

Definition 3.1
.

Let
a,b,c,d
be four distinct points of
\partial S
and let

. Then the
cross-ratio

[a,b,c,d]_{o}
is the element of
\mathbb{R}
defined by

[a,b,c,d]_{o}=\langle a|b\rangle_{o}+\langle c|d\rangle_{o}-\langle a|d\rangle_{o}-\langle b|c\rangle_{o}.

(7)

Since the Gromov product of distinct points of the boundary is finite and is preserved by isometries of
S
, we can see that the cross-ratio of distinct points of
\partial S
is also finite, and is also preserved by isometries of
S
, i.e., if
g
is an isometry of

, then

[ga,gb,gc,gd]_{go}=[a,b,c,d]_{o}.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R},\varepsilon\geq 0
we say that

if
\left|x-y\right|\leq\varepsilon
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that for arbitrary bounded sequences
(p_{i}),(q_{i})\in\mathbb{R}
, if

\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}p_{i}\approx_{\varepsilon}\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}p_{i}\text{ and }\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}q_{i}\approx_{\varepsilon}\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}q_{i},\text{ then }

\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}p_{i}+\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}q_{i}\approx_{2\varepsilon}\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\left(p_{i}+q_{i}\right).

The next lemma shows that the cross-ratio does not depend much on the choice of the base point
o\in S
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
a,b,c,d
be distinct points of
\partial S
and let

. Then

[a,b,c,d]_{o}\approx_{48\delta}[a,b,c,d]_{o^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Let
(x_{i}),(y_{i}),(z_{i})
, and

be arbitrary sequences converging to

, and

(1), we get

\displaystyle[a,b,c,d]_{o}\approx_{8\delta}\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\langle x_{i}|y_{i}\rangle_{o}+\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\langle z_{i}|t_{i}\rangle_{o}-\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\langle x_{i}|t_{i}\rangle_{o}-\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\langle y_{i}|z_{i}\rangle_{o}.

(8)

Also, since points

are distinct,
\langle a|b\rangle_{o},\langle c|d\rangle_{o},\langle a|d\rangle_{o}

The goal of this subsection is to prove Theorem

Proposition 3.16

Suppose that the action of a group

Lemma 3.17

Suppose that an action of a group

\Omega

Since the action of

\Omega

is transitive on
\Omega\setminus\{\omega\}

\gamma\in G_{\omega}

\gamma\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}
. Since
G
acts transitively on
\Omega

such that

. Hence,
\omega_{2}=\gamma g\omega

Note that the subgroup

in
S
. This action is of general type, and the induced action on
\partial S
is topologically free. However, it is not topologically free (and not even faithful) on
\Lambda_{S}(G)=\partial T
, since elements
(1,n)\in F_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}
,
n\neq 0
fix
\partial T
. Obviously,
G
satisfies a nontrivial mixed identity
[f^{x},n]=1
, where
f\in F_{2}
,
x
is a free variable, and
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

Example 3.40
.

Monolithic lim-free groups
. This example shows that the converse of Proposition
D
is not true, i.e., there exist lim-free weakly hyperbolic (hence MIF) groups with infinite simple monolith.

Consider
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, or any
\PSL_{2}(F)
, where
F
is a field of
char=0
with cardinality less or equal to continuum. By the proof of Corollary
F
, they are lim-free, and it is well known that they are simple (see
[
Dix
]
), so coincide with their monolith.

Example 3.41
.

Non-faithful weakly hyperbolic MIF groups.

Proposition 3.42
.

Every countable group can be embedded into a finitely generated MIF weakly hyperbolic group, which is not faithful.

Proof.

Let
K
be an arbitrary countable group. By the Higmann-Neumann-Neumann Embedding Theorem
[
HNN
]
,
K
can be embedded in a finitely generated group
G
. By the construction in
[
HNN
]
,
G
is a non-ascending HNN-extension; therefore, it is weakly hyperbolic. Then by
[
HL
, Thm 1.1]
, there exists a finitely generated group
H
, such that:

1.

G\leq H\leq\operatorname{Sym}(G\times\mathbb{Z})
;

2.

There is a short exact sequence:

1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow(G\times\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow 1,

where
N
is locally finite;

3.

H
is MIF.

The group
H
maps surjectively on
G\times\mathbb{Z}
, and
G\times\mathbb{Z}
is weakly hyperbolic, since
G
is weakly hyperbolic. Therefore,
H
also admits a general type action on the same hyperbolic space as
G
. Note that the subgroup
N
is non-trivial; otherwise
H
would be isomorphic to
G\times\mathbb{Z}
, which is impossible since
H
is MIF and
G\times\mathbb{Z}
has a non-trivial center. Since
N
is locally finite, it consists of torsion elements of
H
. Since
N
is normal in
H
, it cannot be a parabolic subgroup with respect to any action on any hyperbolic space, so it is always elliptic, and, by Proposition
2.24
, it should be in an elliptic radical of
H
with respect to any general type action. Therefore, any elliptic radical of
H
is non-trivial, and
H
is not faithful weakly hyperbolic.
∎

Example 3.43
.

Finitely presented simple MIF groups.

Kac-Moody groups.
We partially answer Question 3.6 from
[
BFFHZ
]
.

Proof of Proposition
H
.

Let
G(F)
be any infinite simple finitely presented Kac-Moody group over a finite field. We want to show that
G(F)
is lim-free weakly hyperbolic. First, we will show that
G(F)
is weakly hyperbolic.

The group
G(F)
is a lattice in a product of two buildings. Since
F
is a field, by the proof of
[
CF
, Corollary 1.3 (page 19, line 1 of the proof)]
,
G(F)
acts Weyl-transitively on each of the two buildings. By
[
CF
, Proposition 5.3]
, the action of
G(F)
on one of these buildings
X
(which is
\CAT(0)
) contains two rank one elements
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
with disjoint limit points in the boundary of
X
.

By
[
PSZ
, Theorem B]
, there exists some
\delta
such the curtain model
X_{D}
assosiated to
X
is
\delta
-hyperbolic and
\Isom(X)\leq\Isom(X_{D})
. By
[
PSZ
, Theorem C]
, two rank one elements
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
of the action of
G(F)
on
X
become two loxodromic elements of the action of
G(F)
on
X_{D}
. Finally, by
[
PSZ
, Theorem L]
, the boundary
\partial X_{D}
embeds as an
\Isom
-invariant subset of
\partial X
, thus the four limit points of rank one elements
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
in
\partial X
, must also be four distinct points when looking at the corresponding limit points on
\partial X_{D}
. Therefore, the action of
G(F)
on
X_{D}
is of general type. Since
G(F)
is simple,
E_{X_{D}}(G(F))=\{1\}
, and the action of
G(F)
on
\Lambda_{X_{D}}(G(F))
is faithful.

Now, assume that the action of
G(F)
on
\Lambda_{X_{D}}(G)
is not topologically free. Then, by Corollary
2.36
, the rigid stabilizers of nonempty open subsets of
\Lambda_{X_{D}}(G(F))
are infinite and contain infinite direct sums of other rigid stabilizers. Since
F
is finite, the two buildings associated to
G(F)
are locally finite, and
G(F)
acts properly cocompactly by isometries on the product of them, which is a
\CAT(0)
space, by
[
BH99
, Chapter III.
\Gamma
Theorem 1.1]
,
G(F)
contains only finitely many conjugacy classes of finite groups. Hence,
G(F)
cannot have locally finite infinite subgroups. Therefore, the rigid stabilizers of open subsets in the limit set
\Lambda_{X_{D}}(G(F))
are not locally finite. Thus,
G
contains an infinite direct sum of infinite not locally finite subgroups, and, therefore, an infinite direct sum
P
of finitely generated infinite subgroups. By
[
BD
, Theorem 64]
, the asymptotic dimension of
G(F)
is greater than the supremum of the asymptotic dimensions of its finitely generated subgroups. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a subgroup
P
contains a direct sum
P_{n}
of
n
infinite finitely generated factors. By
[
BD
, Proposition 22]
, the asymptotic dimension is monotone under quasi-isometric embeddings, and
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
quasi-isometrically embeds into
P_{n}
. Since the asymptotic dimension of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
n
, we obtain that the asymptotic dimension of
G(F)
is infinite. This yields a contradiction with the fact that the asymptotic dimension of
G(F)
is finite. Indeed, by the Milnor-Svarc lemma and the quasi-isometry invariance of the asymptotic dimension, the asymptotic dimension of
G(F)
is bounded by the asymptotic dimension of the product of two buildings it acts on, which is finite by
[
DS
]
. Therefore, the action of
G(F)
on
\Lambda_{X_{D}}(G(F))
is topologically free and, by Theorem
B
,
G(F)
is MIF. By Corollary
G
, the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra of
G(F)
is selfless.
∎

Burger-Mozes group.
In
[
BM97
]
, the finitely presented simple Burger-Mozes group
G
was constructed. It acts by isometries on a locally finite tree
T
such that the induced action on
\partial T
is
2
-transitive (
[
BM97
, Remark 5]
). Therefore, the action of
G
on
T
is of general type. By construction,
G
acts properly, cocompactly by isometries on the product of two locally finite trees, thus has finite asymptotic dimension. So, the argument of Proposition
H
can be applied to
G
to conclude that
G
is MIF, and the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra of
G
is selfless.
The fact that
G
is MIF was first observed in
[
BFFHZ
, Remark 3.5]
.

Amir-Lazarovich groups.

Very recently, in
[
AL
]
M. Amir and N. Lazarovich constructed simple finitely presented groups acting on products of two Davis complexes: a
c
-regular tree with
c\geq 6
and the right-angled Davis complex of an Odd graph. By
[
AL
, Theorem 5.2]
, the action of such groups on a tree is vertex-transitive, therefore, is of general type. Thus, the rest of the argument of Proposition
H
also applies, and such groups are MIF and have selfless reduced
C^{*}
-algebras.

Remark 3.44
.

In
[
TMW
]
the family of lattices acting on two-dimensional Euclidean buildings was introduced, and recently it was proved that they also produce a family of infinite simple groups
[
LW
]
. Unfortunately, the reasoning from Proposition
H
does not apply to this family, and we don’t know whether they are mixed identity free or not.
