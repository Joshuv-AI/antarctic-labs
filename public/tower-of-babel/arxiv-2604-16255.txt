Multisymmetric functions on eventually constant cyclic graphs
Radford Green, Cornell Holmes, Mee Seong Im

Abstract
. The study of spanning trees and related structures is central in graph theory, closely connected to understanding functions between finite sets. This paper generalizes the established relationship between rooted trees and eventually constant endomorphisms to a wider context including k k -tuples of functions among k k disjoint vertex sets. We derive a weighted count of eventually constant k k -tuples, which are characterized by their stabilization to constancy upon iterated composition. This construction is the set-theoretic analogue of the nilpotent cone and offers new insight into the combinatorial structure of cyclic digraphs. By identifying these k k -tuples with their induced digraphs, we construct explicit formulas for their generating polynomials and analyze the cardinality of the set of eventually constant k k -tuples. These polynomials are multisymmetric in k k sets of variables and can be re-expressed as the character of a representation of the product of general linear groups. We extend the ideas to the more general structures of eventually N N -cyclic and λ \lambda -cyclic k k -tuples, which we define and provide similar theorems for their generating functions and cardinality.

Background

2.1.
Introduction to graphs

We recall the necessary graph-theoretic terminologies.

We consider finite directed graphs (digraphs) that allow for loops but not multiple edges between vertices. A
digraph
on a vertex set
U
is a pair
D:=(U,E_{D})
, where
E_{D}
is a
(directed) edge set
, a subset of ordered vertex pairs
(u,v)\in U\times U
. The edge
(u,v)
is directed from
u
towards
v
. We also denote the vertex set of a digraph
D
by
V(D):=U
.

Consider digraphs
D
and
G
. Then,
D
is a
subgraph
of
G
if
V(D)\subseteq V(G)
and
E_{D}\subseteq E_{G}
, and
D
is a
spanning subgraph
of
G
if
D
is a subgraph and
V(D)=V(G)
.

The
union
of digraphs
D
and
G
is the digraph
(V(D)\cup V(G),E_{D}\cup E_{G})
. When the edge sets
E_{D}
and
E_{G}
are disjoint, we denote the union graph by
D\oplus G
. Also, for any subset
U\subseteq V(D)
, let
D_{U}
denote the spanning digraph formed by removing all outgoing edges from
U
.

A
trail
in
D
from a vertex
s_{1}
to a vertex
s_{t}
is a sequence of distinct adjacent edges in
D
leading from
s_{1}
to
s_{t}
:

(s_{1},s_{2}),\,(s_{2},s_{3}),\,\ldots,\,(s_{t-1},s_{t}).

A
cycle
is a nonempty trail in
D
along vertices
s_{1},\ldots,s_{t},s_{1}
in which
s_{1},\ldots,s_{t}
are distinct, and
D
is
acyclic
if it does not contain any cycles. A digraph
C
whose edge set forms a cycle along its vertices is called a
(directed) cycle graph
.

The
out-degree
of a vertex
u\in V(D)
is the number of outgoing edges
(u,v)
in
D
;
in-degree
is defined analogously. The digraph
D
is
functional
if every vertex has out-degree
1
, which holds precisely if
D
corresponds to the standard set-theoretic graph of an endomorphism
f_{D}:V(D)\to V(D)
on
V(D)
. Note that vertices in cycle graphs have in and out-degree
1
, thus are functional. In fact, a nonempty graph
C
is a cycle graph precisely if it corresponds to a
|V(C)|
-cycle,
f_{C}:V(C)\to V(C)
.

For each vertex set
U
, we work in the vertex-indexed polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[x_{u}]_{u\in U}
and define the
weight

w(D)
of any digraph
D
of
U
via in-degrees:

(12)

w(D):=\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{D}}x_{v}.

Note that if
D
is functional, then each vertex
u\in U
has the unique target
f_{D}(u)
, so the weight becomes the following product over
U
:

w(D)=\prod_{u\in U}x_{f_{D}(u)}.

We also extend
w
additively over any subset
\mathcal{S}
of digraphs on
U
:

w(\mathcal{S}):=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{S}}w(D).

A digraph
D
is an
in-forest
rooted at
R\subseteq V(D)
if there is a unique (possibly empty) trail from each vertex
u\in V(D)
to a vertex
v\in R
. If
D
is an in-forest rooted at
R
, outgoing edges from all vertices correspond to distinct nonempty trails leading to
R
, so vertices in
V(D)\setminus R
have out-degree
1
and vertices in
R
have out-degree
0
. Also, observe that all trails terminate in
R
in at most
|V(D)\setminus R|
steps, and that
D
is acyclic.

We decompose functional digraphs into cyclic and forest parts, following
[
9
]
. Let
D
be such a digraph with corresponding endomorphism
f_{D}:V(D)\to V(D)
. For each vertex
u\in V(D)
, the unique trail from
u
follows the iterated values of
f_{D}
:

u,\,f_{D}(u),\,f_{D}(f_{D}(u)),\,\ldots,\,f^{m}_{D}(u),\,\ldots

As
D
is finite, this sequence must begin to repeat. Thus, either
u
belongs to a cycle if
u
is repeated, or
u
leads to a cycle following a trail of vertices that are not repeated.

Let
C(D)
denote the
cyclic part
of
D
, the subgraph induced on the vertices
u
that belong to a cycle. The subgraph formed by removing all cycle edges,
D_{V(C(D))}
, is an in-forest rooted at
V(C(D))
. Since the cycle and non-cycle edges partition
E_{D}
, we have the disjoint decomposition and weight factorization:

D=C(D)\oplus D_{V(C(D))},\quad\text{and}\quad w(D)=w(C(D))w(D_{V(C(D))}),

respectively.

Note that the iterated images
f^{m}(V(D))
stabilize to the set of cyclic vertices
V(C(D))
, and that
f_{D}
acts as a permutation on this set. We also call the restricted permutation
f_{D}:V(C(D))\to V(C(D))
the
cyclic part
of the endomorphism
f_{D}
.

Since each vertex of
D
has a single outgoing edge, distinct cycles must have disjoint vertex sets, and the unique trail from every vertex leads to exactly one cycle.

Cyclic digraphs

We now turn to a specific vertex set, the disjoint union of components,

A functional subgraph

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}),\quad f_{i}:V(D)_{i}\to V(D)_{i+1},\quad f_{i}(u):=f_{D}(u).

and thus corresponds to a functional subgraph of

The following structural Lemma
2.1
characterizes cycles on
\Gamma
. In particular, we see that cycles distribute evenly across each component

. To study the vertex sets of cycles, we define a

as a vertex subset

that contains exactly one element from each component

, denoted

. More generally, for any positive integer

Lemma 2.1

Let

is an

-choice set.

Proof.

Fix any vertex

.

If
C
is a cycle graph, then
f_{C}
is a bijective

Conversely, suppose each map

contains the orbit under

∎

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{U}
to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U}\times\mathcal{F}_{U}
, and:

(13)

w(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{U})=|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U}|\left(\prod_{u\in U}x_{u}\right)w(\mathcal{F}_{U}).

Proof.

By the cyclic and forest decomposition in the previous section,
D\mapsto(C(D),D_{U})
is a well defined map from
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{U}
to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U}\times\mathcal{F}_{U}
, and
D=C(D)\oplus D_{U}
.

Conversely, fix any
(C,F)\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U}\times\mathcal{F}_{U}
. The disjoint union
C\oplus F
is a subgraph of
\Gamma
since
C
and
F
are, and it is functional since vertices in
U
have out-degree
1
from
C
and vertices in
X\setminus U
have out-degree
1
from
F
. Next,
C(C\oplus F)=C
, since the forest
F
contributes no cycles, and removing the edges outgoing from
U
leaves
(C\oplus F)_{U}=F
. Since
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U}
, we have
C\oplus F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{U}
, and we then see
(C,F)\mapsto C\oplus F
yields the inverse, since
(C(C\oplus F),(F\oplus C)_{U})=(C,F).

Since weight factors over disjoint edge unions, we have:

w(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{U})=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{U}}w(C\oplus F)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{U}}w(C)w(F)=w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U})w(\mathcal{F}_{U}).

Finally, every vertex in a cyclic graph
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U}
belongs to a unique disjoint cycle, thus has in-degree
1
. Therefore,
w(C)=\prod_{u\in U}x_{u}
. So
w(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{U})=|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|\left(\prod_{u\in U}x_{u}\right)
, and (
13
) follows.
∎

2.3.
Matrix-tree calculation

The
directed Laplacian matrix
of
\Gamma
is a square matrix indexed by the vertices of
X
with entries given by:

(14)

\ell_{u,v}=\begin{cases}e_{1}(X_{i+1})&\text{if }u=v\in X_{i},\\
\quad-x_{v}&\text{if }u\in X_{i}\text{ and }v\in X_{i+1},\\
\qquad 0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For each vertex subset
U\subseteq X
, let
\mathcal{L}(U)
denote the principal submatrix obtained by deleting the rows and columns corresponding to vertices in
U
.

We need the following well-known directed matrix-tree theorem
[
3
]
:

Theorem 2.3
.

For any vertex subset
U\subseteq X
, the total weight of the spanning in-forests of
\Gamma
rooted at
U
is given by:

w(\mathcal{F}_{U})={\sf det}(\mathcal{L}(U)).

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
N
-choice set
U
, the weight of the rooted in-forests
\mathcal{F}_{U}
is given by:

(15)

w(\mathcal{F}_{U})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-(N+1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus U_{j})\right).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
, we have
w(\mathcal{F}_{U})={\sf det}(\mathcal{L}(U))
, so define
H:=\mathcal{L}(U)
. To compute
{\sf det}(H)
, express
H
as a block matrix, decompose it, and then apply the matrix determinant lemma.

Order each reduced component
X_{i}\setminus U_{i}
and let
\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}:=(x_{v})_{v\in X_{i}\setminus U_{i}}
denote the ordered column vector of variables in
X_{i}\setminus U_{i}
. Let
p_{i}:=|X_{i}\setminus U_{i}|
, and let
\mathbf{1}_{p_{i}}
denote the column vector of length
p_{i}
with one in each coordinate. Then order
X\setminus U
by sequentially concatenating
X_{1}\setminus U_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\setminus U_{k}
.

Divide
H
into block submatrices
H_{i,j}
of dimension
p_{i}\times p_{j}
. Observe each block
H_{i,j}
is indexed by
X_{i}\setminus U_{i}\times X_{j}\setminus U_{j}
, with entries

(H_{i,j})_{u,v}=\ell_{u,v}
, where
u\in X_{i}\setminus U_{i},
and
v\in X_{j}\setminus U_{j}
.
There are three families of blocks. The entries of the diagonal blocks
H_{i,i}
are given by:

(H_{i,i})_{u,v}=\begin{cases}e_{1}(X_{i+1})&\text{if }u=v,\\
\hskip 19.91692pt0&\text{if }u\neq v,\end{cases}

so
H_{i,i}=e_{1}(X_{i+1})\mathsf{I}_{p_{i}}
, where
\mathsf{I}_{p_{i}}
is
p_{i}\times p_{i}
identity matrix. The entries of the cyclic super-diagonal blocks
H_{i,i+1}
are given by

(H_{i,i+1})_{u,v}=-x_{v}
,
so
H_{i,i+1}=-\mathbf{1}_{p_{i}}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{i+1}^{T}
. The remaining blocks
H_{i,j}
are
0
. This yields the explicit block form:

(16)

H=\begin{pmatrix}e_{1}(X_{2})\mathsf{I}_{p_{1}}&-\mathbf{1}_{p_{1}}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{2}^{T}&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&e_{1}(X_{3})\mathsf{I}_{p_{2}}&-\mathbf{1}_{p_{2}}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{3}^{T}&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&e_{1}(X_{k-1})\mathsf{I}_{p_{k-2}}&-\mathbf{1}_{p_{k-2}}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}^{T}&0\\
0&0&0&\ldots&e_{1}(X_{k})\mathsf{I}_{p_{k-1}}&-\mathbf{1}_{p_{k-1}}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{T}\\
-\mathbf{1}_{p_{k}}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{T}&0&0&\ldots&0&e_{1}(X_{1})\mathsf{I}_{p_{k}}\end{pmatrix}.

We isolate the diagonal blocks of
H
into a matrix

D:=\mathsf{diag}(e_{1}(X_{2})\mathsf{I}_{p_{1}},\ldots,e_{1}(X_{1})\mathsf{I}_{p_{k}})
,
and observe that
H=D-ACB^{T}
, where
C
is the
k\times k
cyclic permutation matrix:

C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\\
1&0&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix},

and

A=\mathsf{diag}(\mathbf{1}_{p_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{1}_{p_{k}})
and
B=\mathsf{diag}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{k})
are block-diagonal matrices.
Applying the matrix determinant lemma (also known as Sylvester’s determinant identity), see e.g,
[
14
,
13
,
10
]
, gives:

(17)

{\sf det}(H)={\sf det}(D-ACB^{T})={\sf det}(D){\sf det}(\mathsf{I}_{k}-B^{T}D^{-1}AC),

where
\mathsf{I}_{k}
is the
k\times k
identity matrix.

Next, we calculate the determinants of matrices
D
and
M=\mathsf{I}_{k}-B^{T}D^{-1}AC
in (
17
). The determinant of the diagonal block matrix
D
is:

(18)

{\sf det}(D)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\sf det}(e_{1}(X_{i+1})\mathsf{I}_{p_{i}})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i+1})^{p_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{p_{i-1}}.

Observe that each
\mathbf{1}_{p_{i}}^{T}\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}=e_{1}(X_{i}\setminus U_{i})
, so the diagonal matrix
B^{T}D^{-1}A
simplifies as:

\displaystyle B^{T}D^{-1}A

\displaystyle=\mathsf{diag}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{T}(e_{1}(X_{i+1})\mathsf{I}_{p_{i}})^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{p_{i}}\right)_{i=1}^{k}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{diag}\left(\frac{e_{1}(X_{1}\setminus U_{1})}{e_{1}(X_{2})},\frac{e_{1}(X_{2}\setminus U_{2})}{e_{1}(X_{3})},\ldots,\frac{e_{1}(X_{k}\setminus U_{k})}{e_{1}(X_{1})}\right),

and
M
has the form:

M=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{e_{1}(X_{1}\setminus U_{1})}{e_{1}(X_{2})}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&1&-\frac{e_{1}(X_{2}\setminus U_{2})}{e_{1}(X_{3})}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1&-\frac{e_{1}(X_{k-1}\setminus U_{k-1})}{e_{1}(X_{k})}\\
-\frac{e_{1}(X_{k}\setminus U_{k})}{e_{1}(X_{1})}&0&0&\cdots&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

We can calculate
{\sf det}(M)
by direct application of the Leibniz formula:

{\sf det}(M)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\mathsf{sgn}(\sigma)M_{1,\sigma(1)}\cdots M_{k,\sigma(k)},

where
\sigma
is a permutation of the elements of the symmetric group
S_{k}
and
\mathsf{sgn}(\sigma)
is the sign of
\sigma
.
Only two permutations correspond to nonzero summands. The identity
\mathsf{diag}(1,\ldots,1)
indexes the diagonal product
(1)^{k}
and has sign
1
, and the
k
-cycle
j\mapsto j+1
indexes the cyclic super-diagonal product

\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(-\frac{e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus U_{j})}{e_{1}(X_{j+1})}\right)

and has sign
(-1)^{k-1}
. Thus:

(19)

{\sf det}(M)=1^{k}+(-1)^{k-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(-\frac{e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus U_{j})}{e_{1}(X_{j+1})}\right)=1-\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus U_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})}.

Returning to (
17
), multiplying
{\sf det}(D)
and
{\sf det}(M)
yields:

\displaystyle{\sf det}(H)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{p_{i-1}}\left(1-\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus U_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{p_{i-1}-1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus U_{j})\right).

Substituting
w(\mathcal{F}_{U})={\sf det}(H)
and
n_{i-1}-(N+1)=|X_{i-1}\setminus U_{i-1}|-1=p_{i-1}-1
yields (
15
).
∎

3.
Multisymmetric polynomials associated to eventually constant functions

A
k
-tuple of cyclic functions
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
with
f_{i}:X_{i}\to X_{i+1}
is
eventually constant
if iterating the composition
f_{k}\cdots f_{1}:X_{1}\to X_{1}
eventually stabilizes to a constant map
(f_{k}\cdots f_{1})^{m}
. We denote the set of all eventually constant
k
-tuples as
\mathsf{EC}
, and define the generating polynomial
P(\mathbf{x})
as:

P(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\in\mathsf{EC}}w(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})=w(\mathsf{EC}).

Recall that a
choice set

U\subseteq X
is a vertex subset with exactly
1
element in each component
U\cap X_{i}
, denoted
u_{i}
.

3.1.
Choice set decomposition

We consider the subsets
\mathcal{D}_{U}
,
\mathcal{C}_{U}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{U}
, indexed over all choice sets
U
.

For each choice set
U
,
\mathcal{C}_{U}
is the set of cyclic subgraphs of
\Gamma
with vertex set
U
. Since
u_{1}
is a single vertex, there is only one digraph in
\mathcal{C}_{U}
, the cycle along the vertices:

u_{1},\,u_{2},\,\ldots,\,u_{k-1},\,u_{k}.

Thus,
\mathcal{D}_{U}
is the set of functional subgraphs of
\Gamma
with a unique cycle on
U
. The set
\mathcal{F}_{U}
remains the set of subforests of
\Gamma
rooted at
U
, which has weight:

w(\mathcal{F}_{U})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-2}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus\{u_{j}\})\right)

by Lemma
2.4
.

By Lemma
2.2
, each weight
w(\mathcal{D}_{U})
factors by the weights of
\mathcal{C}_{U}
and
\mathcal{F}_{U}
, giving:

(20)

\displaystyle w(\mathcal{D}_{U})

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{C}_{U}|\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{u_{i}}\right)w(\mathcal{F}_{U})

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{u_{i}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-2}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus\{u_{j}\})\right).

Next, we re-express
P(\mathbf{x})
as a sum over the weights
w(\mathcal{D}_{U})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Ranging over all choice sets
U
, the classes
\mathcal{D}_{U}
partition
\mathsf{EC}
.

Proof.

Let
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
be a spanning functional subgraph of
\Gamma
with induced graph
D
. Then,
D\in\mathsf{EC}
if and only if
f_{k}\cdots f_{1}
stabilizes to some singleton on
X_{1}
. By the remark following Lemma
2.1
, this occurs if and only if
D
has a unique cycle whose vertex set is some choice set
U
, precisely if
D\in\mathcal{D}_{U}
. Therefore,
\mathsf{EC}=\bigcup_{U}\mathcal{D}_{U}
.

The sets
\mathcal{D}_{U}
are pairwise disjoint since the cyclic part of any digraph
D
has a unique vertex set
V(C(D))
. Thus, the sets induce a partition.
∎

By (
20
) and the partition, we can express
P(\mathbf{x})
as the following sum over choice sets
U
:

(21)

\displaystyle P(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{U}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{u_{i}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-2}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus\{u_{j}\})\right).

3.2.
Multisymmetric polynomials of eventually constant functions

We now prove the following two results which are stated in Theorem
1.1
and (
5
).

Theorem 3.2
.

The weighted enumeration of all eventually constant
k
-tuples is given by:

(22)

P(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-2}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})^{2}-2^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{2}(X_{j})\right).

Proof.

First, we distribute the cycle weights in the choice set-sum (
21
):

(23)

\displaystyle P(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-2}\sum_{U}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{u_{j}}e_{1}(X_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{u_{j}}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus\{u_{j}\})\right).

By distributivity, we can re-express the sums over choice sets as:

\sum_{U}\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{u_{j}}e_{1}(X_{j})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in X_{j}}x_{u}e_{1}(X_{j}),\quad\sum_{U}\prod_{j=1}^{k}x_{u_{j}}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus\{u_{j}\})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in X_{j}}x_{u}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus\{u\}).

Next, we reduce the resulting factors to elementary symmetric polynomials:

\sum_{u\in X_{i}}x_{u}e_{1}(X_{i})=e_{1}(X_{i})^{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{u\in X_{i}}x_{u}e_{1}(X_{i}\setminus\{u\})=\sum_{u\in X_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in X_{i}\\
v\neq u\end{subarray}}x_{u}x_{v}=2e_{2}(X_{i}).

Substituting back into (
23
) yields the result.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{GL}(n_{i})
and for each
i
, let
V_{i}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}}
denote the fundamental representation
of
\mathsf{GL}(n_{i})
. Then
P(\mathbf{x})
can be written
as the virtual character:

P(\mathbf{x})=\chi\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{\otimes n_{i-1}}-2^{k}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\left(V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-2)}\otimes\Lambda^{2}V_{i}\right)\right),

with indices again understood mod k.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.2
, we have

P(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{\,n_{i-1}-2}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})^{2}-2^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{2}(X_{j})\right).

We use the standard identities

e_{1}(X_{j})=\chi(V_{j}),\quad e_{1}(X_{j})^{2}=\chi(V_{j}^{\otimes 2}),\quad\mbox{ and }\quad e_{2}(X_{j})=\chi(\Lambda^{2}V_{j})

to obtain

P(\mathbf{x})=\chi\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-2)}\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{\otimes 2}-2^{k}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda^{2}V_{i}\right)\right).

Combining tensor powers yields

P(\mathbf{x})=\chi\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{\otimes n_{i-1}}-2^{k}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\left(V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-2)}\otimes\Lambda^{2}V_{i}\right)\right),

as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If any
n_{j}=1
then all
k
-tuples
(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
are eventually constant. Furthermore,
\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{2}(X_{i})=0
, and
P(\mathbf{x})
can be expressed with non-negative coefficients:

P(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}}.

Also,
V_{i}\cong\mathbb{C}
and hence
\Lambda^{2}V_{i}=0
, so

P(\mathbf{x})=\chi\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{\otimes n_{i-1}}\right).

3.3.
Enumeration of eventually constant functions

In this section, we enumerate the number of eventually constant functions for a
k
-tuple.

Proposition 3.5
.

The cardinality of
\mathsf{EC}
is:

\#\mathsf{EC}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}^{n_{i-1}-1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}(n_{j}-1)\right).

The proof of the above proposition is a straightforward computation using Theorem
3.2
, evaluating each variable
x_{v}
at 1.

Corollary 3.6
.

The number of
k
-tuples
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
that are eventually constant and stabilize to any fixed choice set is:

\prod_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}^{n_{i-1}-2}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}(n_{j}-1)\right).

Proof.

By symmetry, the cardinality of the set
\mathcal{D}_{U}
is identical for each choice set
U
. Dividing the total cardinality
\#\mathsf{EC}
in Proposition
3.5
by the number of choice sets,
\prod_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}
, yields the result.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

If all components
X_{i}
are of equal cardinality
|X_{i}|:=n
, then the number of eventually constant
k
-tuples on
X
becomes:

n^{k(n-1)}\left(n^{k}-(n-1)^{k}\right).

Corollary 3.8
.

For each integer
n\geq 1
, let
p_{k}(n)
denote the probability that a
k
-tuple of functions between
k
sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
of cardinality
|X_{i}|=n
is eventually constant. Then

(24)

p_{k}(n)=1-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{k}.

It follows that

(25)

p_{k}(n)=\frac{k}{n}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Fix any integer
n\geq 1
and sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
of cardinality
|X_{i}|=n
. The total number of
k
-tuples
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
of functions with
f_{i}:X_{i}\to X_{i+1}
is
\prod_{i=1}^{k}n^{n}=n^{kn}
. Dividing the number of eventually constant
k
-tuples calculated in Corollary
3.7
by this total yields (
24
):

p_{k}(n)=\frac{n^{k(n-1)}\big(n^{k}-(n-1)^{k}\big)}{n^{kn}}=\frac{n^{k}-(n-1)^{k}}{n^{k}}=1-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{k}.

Expansion using the binomial theorem gives (
25
):

1-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{k}=1-\left(1-\frac{k}{n}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right)=\frac{k}{n}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

∎

For
k=1
, this recovers (
1
), the classical probability recently highlighted in
[
15
]
as a set-theoretic analogue to nilpotent linear operators.

As expected, if we fix
n\geq 1
instead, then the chance of picking an eventually constant
k
-tuple grows as
k
does, approaching
1
as
k\to\infty
.

Finally, if we vary both parameters
k
and
n
together, we recover the exponential function. For any fixed positive constant
c\in\mathbb{N}
, we see:

\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{cn}(n)=1-\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{cn}\right)=1-e^{-c}.

4.
Multisymmetric polynomials associated to eventually cyclic functions

Fix a positive integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
. A
k
-tuple
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
is
eventually
N
-cyclic
if iterated images of composition
f_{k}\cdots f_{1}:X_{1}\to X_{1}
eventually stabilize to a subset
(f_{k}\cdots f_{1})^{m}(X_{1})\subseteq X_{1}
of cardinality
N
, and
f_{k}\cdots f_{1}
acts as an
N
-cycle on this set. Let
\mathsf{EC}(N)
be the set of eventually
N
-cyclic tuples, and let
P_{N}(\mathbf{x})
be the generating polynomial:

P_{N}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{D\in\mathsf{EC}(N)}w(D)=w(\mathsf{EC}(N)).

Note that
\mathsf{EC}=\mathsf{EC}(1)
.

Recall, an
N
-choice set

U\subseteq X
is a vertex subset that contains exactly
N
elements in each component, denoted
U_{i}:=X_{i}\cap U
.

4.1.
N
-choice set decomposition

To calculate
P_{N}(\mathbf{x})
, we adapt the methods in Section
3.1
for eventually constant functions. Generalizing, we shift from standard choice sets to
N
-choice sets
U
, and work with subsets
\mathcal{C}_{U}^{(N)}
and
\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}
of
\mathcal{C}_{U}
and
\mathcal{D}_{U}
. Then as before, we weigh each subset
\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}
by Lemma
2.2
and partition the set
\mathsf{EC}(N)
by
\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}
.

We restrict the sets
\mathcal{D}_{U}
and
\mathcal{C}_{U}
to full cycles. Discarding disjoint unions of distinct cycles in
\mathcal{C}_{U}
, we consider only the subset
\mathcal{C}_{U}^{(N)}
of (single) cycle subgraphs of
\Gamma
on
U
. Accordingly, we define the subset
\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{U}
of spanning
\Gamma
subgraphs whose cyclic part belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{U}^{(N)}
, or equivalently, whose cyclic part is a (single) cycle on
U
.

Note that although
\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}
may be a proper subset of
\mathcal{D}_{U}
, Lemma
2.1
still allows us to calculate each weight
w(\mathcal{D}^{(N)}_{U})
in terms of the corresponding subset
\mathcal{C}_{U}^{(N)}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{U}
and
\mathcal{F}_{U}
:

(26)

w(\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)})=|\mathcal{C}_{U}^{(N)}|\left(\prod_{u\in U}x_{u}\right)w(\mathcal{F}_{U}).

Lemma 4.1
.

Ranging over all
N
-choice sets
U
, the sets
\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}
partition
\mathsf{EC}(N)
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a spanning functional subgraph of
\Gamma
with corresponding
k
-tuple
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
. Then,
D\in\mathsf{EC}(N)
if and only if the cyclic part of its composition
f_{k}\cdots f_{1}
is a single
N
-cycle on some subset
U_{1}\subseteq X_{1}
of cardinality
N
. By remark following Lemma
2.1
, this occurs if and only if
D
contains a unique cycle whose vertex set forms an
N
-choice set extending
U_{1}
, which holds if and only if
D\in\bigcup_{U}\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}
.

This induces a partition since the sets
\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}
are pairwise disjoint.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

The cardinality of
\mathcal{C}^{(N)}_{U}
is:

|\mathcal{C}^{(N)}_{U}|=\frac{(N!)^{k}}{N}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
,
\mathcal{C}^{(N)}_{U}
identifies with the set of
k
-tuples
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
, such that each map
f_{i}:U_{i}\to U_{i+1}
is a bijection and
f_{k}\cdots f_{1}:U_{1}\to U_{1}
is an
N
-cycle.

By right-composition on the final term:

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\mapsto(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k-1},f_{k}\cdots f_{1}),

bijects
\mathcal{C}_{U}^{(N)}
with the
k
-tuples
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k-1},g)
for which each
f_{i}:U_{i}\to U_{i+1}
is a bijection and
g:U_{1}\to U_{1}
is an
N
-cycle. There are
N!
such bijections
f_{i}:U_{i}\to U_{i+1}
for each component
i=1,\ldots,k-1
, and there are
(N-1)!
such
N
-cycles on
U_{1}
.
∎

By the partition over
\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}
in Lemma
4.1
, the cardinality in Lemma
4.2
, and (
26
),
we obtain

(27)

\displaystyle P_{N}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{U}w(\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)})=\frac{(N!)^{k}}{N}\sum_{U}\left(\prod_{u\in U}x_{u}\right)w(\mathcal{F}_{U}).

4.2.
Multisymmetric polynomials of eventually cyclic functions

Theorem 4.3
.

The weighted enumeration of all eventually
N
-cyclic
k
-tuples is given by:

P_{N}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{(N!)^{k}}{N}\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-(N+1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})e_{N}(X_{j})-(N+1)^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{N+1}(X_{j})\right).

Proof.

To simplify, let
A_{N}
abbreviate the common factored weight:

A_{N}:=\frac{(N!)^{k}}{N}\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-(N+1)}.

By Lemma
2.4
, the weight of the subforests rooted at each
N
-choice set
U
is:

w(\mathcal{F}_{U})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-(N+1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus U_{j})\right).

We apply the forest weights to (
27
) and distribute the terms
\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}
to get the following sum over
N
-choice sets
U
:

(28)

\displaystyle P_{N}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=A_{N}\sum_{U}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{j}\setminus U_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=A_{N}\sum_{U}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}-\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i}\setminus U_{i})\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}\right).

By distributivity, we can re-express the sums over
N
-choice sets as products over
i=1,\ldots,k
:

\displaystyle\sum_{U}\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{i}\subseteq X_{i}\\
|U_{i}|=N\end{subarray}}e_{1}(X_{i})\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u},

\displaystyle\sum_{U}\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i}\setminus U_{i})\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{i}\subseteq X_{i}\\
|U_{i}|=N\end{subarray}}e_{1}(X_{i}\setminus U_{i})\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}.

Next, we reduce the factors of the resulting products to elementary symmetric polynomials. The first factors simplify immediately:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{i}\subseteq X_{i}\\
|U_{i}|=N\end{subarray}}e_{1}(X_{i})\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}=e_{1}(X_{i})e_{N}(X_{i}).

For the next set of factors, note that there are
N+1
ways to form each subset
U^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq X_{i}
of cardinality
N+1
from a subset
U_{i}\subseteq X_{i}
of cardinality
N
and an element
v\in X_{i}\setminus U_{i}
. Thus, expanding
e_{1}(X_{i}\setminus U_{i})
yields:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{i}\subseteq X_{i}\\
|U_{i}|=N\end{subarray}}e_{1}(X_{i}\setminus U_{i})\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{i}\subseteq X_{i}\\
|U_{i}|=N\end{subarray}}\sum_{v\in X_{i}\setminus U_{i}}x_{v}\prod_{u\in U_{i}}x_{u}=(N+1)e_{N+1}(X_{i}).

Substituting back into (
28
) yields:

P_{N}(\mathbf{x})=A_{N}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})e_{N}(X_{i})-(N+1)^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{N+1}(X_{i})\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

If any
n_{j}<N+1
, then
\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{n+1}(X_{i})=0
, and the weight becomes:

P_{N}(\mathbf{x})=(N!)^{k-1}(N-1)!\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{n_{i-1}-N}e_{N}(X_{i}).

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
be finite sets with
|X_{i}|=n_{i}\geq 2
, and let
V_{i}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}}
be the fundamental representation of
\mathsf{GL}(n_{i})
. Then

(29)

\begin{split}P_{n}(\mathbf{x})&=(n!)^{k-1}(n-1)!\;\chi\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\left(V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-n)}\otimes\Lambda^{n}V_{i}\right)\right.\\
&\quad-\left.(n+1)^{k}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\left(V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-(n+1))}\otimes\Lambda^{n+1}V_{i}\right)\right),\end{split}

where indices are taken modulo
k
.

Proof.

We begin from

P_{n}(\mathbf{x})=(n!)^{k-1}(n-1)!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{1}(X_{i})^{\,n_{i-1}-(n+1)}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{n}(X_{i})e_{1}(X_{i})-(n+1)^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}e_{n+1}(X_{i})\right).

Substituting standard character identities

e_{1}(X_{i})=\chi(V_{i}),\qquad e_{n}(X_{i})=\chi(\Lambda^{n}V_{i}),\qquad e_{n+1}(X_{i})=\chi(\Lambda^{n+1}V_{i}),

into (
29
) gives

P_{n}(\mathbf{x})=(n!)^{k-1}(n-1)!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi(V_{i})^{\,n_{i-1}-(n+1)}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi(\Lambda^{n}V_{i})\chi(V_{i})-(n+1)^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi(\Lambda^{n+1}V_{i})\right).

We rewrite each product as a single character:

\begin{split}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi(V_{i})^{\,n_{i-1}-(n+1)}=\chi\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-(n+1))}\right),\quad&\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi(\Lambda^{n}V_{i})\chi(V_{i})=\chi\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}(\Lambda^{n}V_{i}\otimes V_{i})\right),\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi(\Lambda^{n+1}V_{i})&=\chi\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda^{n+1}V_{i}\right).\end{split}

Combining these, we obtain

P_{n}(\mathbf{x})=(n!)^{k-1}(n-1)!\;\chi\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-(n+1))}\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}(\Lambda^{n}V_{i}\otimes V_{i})-(n+1)^{k}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda^{n+1}V_{i}\right)\right).

Finally, regrouping tensor factors inside each
i
gives
V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-(n+1))}\otimes V_{i}=V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-n)}
, so that
V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-(n+1))}\otimes(\Lambda^{n}V_{i}\otimes V_{i})=V_{i}^{\otimes(n_{i-1}-n)}\otimes\Lambda^{n}V_{i}.
Substituting this yields the claimed expression (
29
).
∎

4.3.
Enumeration of eventually cyclic functions

Proposition 4.6
.

The cardinality of
\mathsf{EC}(N)
is given by

(30)

\#\mathsf{EC}(N)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n_{i}}{N}\frac{(N!)^{k}}{N}n_{i}^{n_{i-1}-(N+1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}(n_{j}-N)\right),

or, equivalenty,

(31)

\#\mathsf{EC}(N)=\frac{1}{N}\prod_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}^{n_{i-1}-N}\frac{(n_{i}-1)!}{(n_{i}-N)!}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}(n_{j}-N)\right).

The proof of the above proposition is a straightforward computation using Theorem
4.3
, evaluating each variable
x_{v}
at 1.

In (
30
), the factor
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n_{i}}{N}
enumerates the
N
-choice sets; we choose
N
elements from each component
X_{i}
. The coefficient
\frac{(N!)^{k}}{N}
enumerates the cycle subgraphs on
U
by
4.2
. The third term enumerates the spanning in-forests of
\Gamma
rooted at
U
, obtained by evaluating the forest weight polynomial
w(\mathcal{F}_{U})
at
x_{u}=1
.

Also, the cardinality of the subfamily
\mathcal{D}_{U}^{(N)}
is identical for each
N
-choice set
U
, and we know there are
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n_{i}}{N}
many
N
-choice sets. Therefore, the number of
k
-tuples that are eventually
N
-cyclic and stabilize to any single
N
-choice set is:

\frac{(N!)^{k}}{N}\prod_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}^{n_{i-1}-(N+1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}(n_{j}-N)\right).

Corollary 4.7
.

If all components
X_{i}
are of equal cardinality
|X_{i}|:=n
, then the number of eventually
N
-cyclic
k
-tuples on
X
becomes:

\frac{1}{N}n^{k(n-N)}\left(\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-N)!}\right)^{k}\left(n^{k}-(n-N)^{k}\right).

Corollary 4.8
.

For each integer
n\geq N+1
, let
p_{k,N}(n)
denote the probability that a
k
-tuple of functions between
k
sets of cardinality
n
is eventually
N
-cyclic. Then:

(32)

p_{k,N}(n)=\frac{1}{N}n^{-k(N-1)}\left(\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-N)!}\right)^{k}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{N}{n}\right)^{k}\right),

and we derive the same limit behavior from Corollary
3.8
:

(33)

p_{k,N}(n)=\frac{k}{n}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Fix any integer
n\geq N+1
. We divide the number of eventually
N
-cyclic
k
-tuples calculated in Corollary
4.7
by
n^{kn}
, the number of all
k
-tuples of functions, to get (
32
):

\displaystyle p_{k,N}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\frac{n^{k(n-N)}}{n^{kn}}\left(\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-N)!}\right)^{k}\left(n^{k}-(n-N)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(n^{-(N-1)}\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-N)!}\right)^{k}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{N}{n}\right)^{k}\right).

For the bracketed term, we distribute a factor
n
of
n^{N-1}
into the denominator of each of the
N-1
factors of
\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-N)!}
to get the
1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
term:

n^{-(N-1)}\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-N)!}=\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)\left(\frac{n-2}{n}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{n-N+1}{n}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(1-\frac{j}{n}\right).

Next, expansion of the rightmost parenthesis using the binomial theorem gives:

1-\left(1-\frac{N}{n}\right)^{k}=\frac{kN}{n}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Multiplying these asymptotic expansions together yields (
33
):

p_{k,N}(n)=\frac{1}{N}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)^{k}\left(\frac{kN}{n}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right)=\frac{k}{n}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Fix any positive integer
c\in\mathbb{N}
. As
n\to\infty
, the probability
p_{cn,N}(n)
that a
cn
-tuple is eventually
N
-cyclic equals:

\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{cn,N}(n)=\frac{1}{N}e^{-c\binom{N}{2}}\left(1-e^{-cN}\right).

Proof.

We substitute
k=cn
into the exact probability formula (
32
) and analyze the limit of the two factors. First, using the standard limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n}=e^{-x}
, the rightmost term approaches:

\lim_{n\to\infty}1-\left(1-\frac{N}{n}\right)^{cn}=1-(e^{-N})^{c}=1-e^{-cN}.

Secondly, we apply the same limit to the factors of the bracketed term. Since the cycle size
N
is fixed, we can pass the limit inside the finite product:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(1-\frac{j}{n}\right)\right)^{cn}=\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1-\frac{j}{n}\right)^{n}\right)^{c}=\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}e^{-jc}=e^{-c\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}j}=e^{-c\binom{N}{2}}.

Multiplying these limits together with the coefficient
\frac{1}{N}
yields the result.
∎

&=(n!)^{2}(n-1)!\,\chi\!\ \Big(\left(V_{1}^{\otimes(n_{3}-(N+1))}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes(n_{1}-(N+1))}\otimes V_{3}^{\otimes(n_{2}-(N+1))}\right)\\
&\hskip 11.38109pt\otimes\left(V_{1}^{\otimes N}\otimes V_{2}^{\otimes N}\otimes V_{3}^{\otimes N}-(N+1)^{3}\Lambda^{N+1}V_{1}\otimes\Lambda^{N+1}V_{2}\otimes\Lambda^{N+1}V_{3}\right)\Big).\end{split}

Applying Proposition
4.6
, the cardinality of eventually
N
-cyclic triples is:

\#\mathsf{EC}(N)=\prod_{i=1}^{3}\binom{n_{i}}{N}\frac{(N!)^{3}}{N}\ n_{i}^{\,n_{i}-(N+1)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{3}n_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{3}(n_{j}-N)\right).

These formulas recover the eventually constant case when

.

5.
Multisymmetric polynomials associated to eventually
\lambda
-cyclic functions

Fix a positive integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
and an integer partition
\lambda\vdash N
. For each number
l\leq N
, let
m_{l}
denote the multiplicity of
l
in
\lambda
.

A
k
-tuple
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
is
eventually
\lambda
-cyclic
if iterated images of the composition
f_{k}\cdots f_{1}:X_{1}\to X_{1}
eventually stabilize to a subset
(f_{k}\cdots f_{1})^{m}(X_{1})\subseteq X_{1}
of cardinality
N
, on which
f_{k}\cdots f_{1}
acts as a permutation of
cycle type
\lambda
. That is, for each
l\leq N
,
f_{k}\cdots f_{1}
has
m_{l}
disjoint cycles of length
l
. Let
\mathsf{EC}(\lambda)
be the set of eventually
\lambda
-cyclic
k
-tuples, and define the generating function:

P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\in\mathsf{EC}(\lambda)}w(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})=w(\mathsf{EC}(\lambda)).

5.1.
Multisymmetric polynomials of eventually
\lambda
-cyclic functions

We will be brief since calculating

involves similar methods used in Sections

and

. We are still analyzing functions whose composition eventually stabilizes on sets of cardinality
N
, so we again decompose by
N
-choice sets

and
\mathcal{D}_{U}

and

For each
N
-choice set

of
\Gamma
on

Lemma 5.1
.

For each
N

Proof.

The subfamily
\mathcal{C}_{U}^{(\lambda)}
corresponds to the

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
of bijections
f_{i}:U_{i}\to U_{i+1}

f_{k}\cdots f_{1}:U_{1}\to U_{1}
has cycle type
\lambda
. It is known (see
[
18
]
) that the number of permutations of type
\lambda
is:

(34)

\frac{N!}{\prod_{l=1}^{N}l^{m_{l}}m_{l}!}.

The remainder of the follows by the right-composition:

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\mapsto(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k-1},f_{k}\circ(f_{k-1}\cdots f_{1})),

as in Lemma
4.2
. We simply replace the set of
N
-cycles on

, of cardinality
(N-1)!
, with the set of permutations

∎

Consider any spanning functional subgraph
D
of
\Gamma
with
k
-tuple
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
whose composition

of size

. By the correspondence following Lemma
2.1
, any vertex

belongs to

Note that
C(D)
corresponds to the
k

(f_{1}|_{U_{1}},f_{2}|_{U_{2}},\ldots,f_{k}|_{U_{k}}),
