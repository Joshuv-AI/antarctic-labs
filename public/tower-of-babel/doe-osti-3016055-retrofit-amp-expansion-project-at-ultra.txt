1. Abstract

The UTEC-1 LIBS Retroﬁt and Expansion Project aimed to retroﬁt and expand Braskem’s UHMWPE unit in La Porte,
TX to produce lithium-ion battery separator (LIBS) grade material and increase capacity. The project sought to
strengthen the domestic supply chain for advanced battery manufacturing and reduce reliance on imports. While
signiﬁcant progress was achieved in conceptual design and FEL-2 engineering, strategic realignment and external
factors led to project termination prior to FEL-3 and FEED execution.

2. Executive Summary

 • Objective: Enable production of LIBS-grade UHMWPE and expand capacity.

 • Strategic Importance: Supports U.S. energy security and EV supply chain resilience.

 • Status: FEL-2 completed; groundwork for FEED prepared; project terminated due to market conditions,
 leadership transition, EV demand uncertainty, and policy inﬂuences.

3. Technical Narrative

3.1 Project Scope

 • Install puriﬁcation systems for ethylene, hexane, and nitrogen.

 • Upgrade reactors, pumps, compressors, and cooling systems.

 • Enhance powder drying, classiﬁcation, and conveying systems.

 • Modernize electrical infrastructure and process control systems.

3.2 Work Completed

 • Conceptual Design (FEL-2):

 o Developed process ﬂow diagrams, heat & material balances, and preliminary equipment layouts.

 o Completed design basis and assumptions for LIBS-grade production.

 o Identiﬁed long-lead equipment.

 • Safety & Permitting: NEPA review completed; DOE issued Categorical Exclusion.

4. Results & Findings

 • FEL-2 deliverables conﬁrmed technical feasibility for LIBS-grade UHMWPE production.

 • Identiﬁed critical upgrades for reactor cooling, slurry handling, and powder classiﬁcation.

 • Developed implementation strategy requiring 60–90-day outage for major tie-ins.

5. Impact

 • Planned Impact: Strengthen domestic LIBS supply chain; create high-paying jobs.

 • Actual Impact: Advanced engineering knowledge for UHMWPE LIBS production; groundwork for future
 projects.

6. Reason for Termination:

Braskem has undertaken a comprehensive review of its strategic priorities considering evolving market and
corporate conditions. The decision to terminate this project is inﬂuenced by multiple factors, including:

 • Persistent petrochemical downcycle impacting Braskem’s core business market conditions and
 ﬁnancial outlook.

 • Impending change in shareholder ownership, creating uncertainty in long-term investment strategies.

 • Executive leadership transition, resulting in a shift in strategic priorities away from this project.

 • Uncertainties surrounding electric vehicle demand, which affect downstream markets and project
 viability.
