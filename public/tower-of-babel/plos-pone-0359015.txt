Data-driven and feature-sifting based state-of-health estimation for li-ion batteries using KPCA and NRBO-optimized transformer-LSTM hybrid model

Xiangcai Luo, Jinwei Chen, Haijiang Zhang, Chunhua Hu, Jinyong Zhao, Fei Xu, Rui Zhen, Chengchen Deng

ABSTRACT
Electrochemical energy storage technologies, such as lithium-ion batteries, are widely used in various scenarios, but their capacity, lifespan, and state-of-health (SOH) are susceptible to numerous influencing factors. To improve the accuracy of state-of-health (SOH) prediction for lithium-ion battery cells, this paper builds up a feature-based analysis framework, and proposes an enhanced predicting model, which integrates the principles of incremental capacity analysis (ICA) and differential voltage analysis (DVA), and the ideas of kernel principal component analysis (KPCA), and Pearson correlation analysis. The former ones are used to extract a set of health related features (HFs) from the filtered incremental capacity (IC) and differential voltage (DV) curves of batteries, while the latter ones are employed to eliminate redundant features. Furthermore, this work bridges the gap between the charging data of the cells and their SOH by building up a Newton-Raphson-based optimization Transformer-LSTM (NRBO-Transformer-LSTM) model, where NRBO algorithm is introduced to find out the optimized number of attention heads, learning rates and the regularization coefficient. Experimental validation on the NASA and CALCE battery datasets shows that, with 70% of the data used as the training set, the proposed method achieves maximum mean absolute errors (MAE) of 0.0084 and 0.0071, and root mean square errors (RMSE) of 0.0091 and 0.0089, respectively, significantly outperforming multiple benchmark algorithms in both prediction accuracy and stability.

1. Introduction Electrochemical energy storage components, represented by lithium-ion batteries, are highly favored in renewable energy power systems due to their specific capacity characteristic, their ability to smooth energy fluctuations, and their excellent cycling performance [ 1 ]. However, due to various health-related factors such as the variations of electrolyte concentration, changing working temperatures, and charge and discharge rates, the SOH of lithium-ion battery cells can be altered, which reduce the reliability and economic viability of projects in large-scale applications such as energy storage in the power grids [ 2 – 5 ]. Therefore, accurately predicting the SOH of lithium-ion battery cells is essential for the design of the thermal management, charge-discharge strategies, ensuring component performance, extending cell lifespan, and ensuring safety [ 6 ]. As the SOH of battery cells cannot be measured directly, numerous studies have focused on its estimation and prediction, with common approaches including direct measurement methods, model-based techniques, and data-driven strategies, each with their own pros and pons, and applicable scenarios. Generally, direct measurement methods measure characteristic parameters that reflect battery aging at first, such as capacity, internal resistance, and impedance, and then analyze and calculate the corresponding SOH. The advantage of this type of method is that it can intuitively correlate the measurement results with the battery SOH. However, the requirements for measurement results have also brought negative impacts, especially on the requirements and limitations of measurement environments, resulting in measurement results that cannot fully reflect all influencing factors [ 7 ]. For example, literature [ 8 ] applies a short-duration DC pulse to measure internal resistance, successfully distinguishing batteries at different SOH levels. In contrast, literature [ 9 ] uses electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), but finds that temperature and state of charge (SOC) variations introduce measurement errors, compromising the accuracy of SOH estimation. To address the shortcomings of direct measurement methods and reveal the factors affecting battery SOH at the mechanism level in an all-round way, several model-based analysis techniques have emerged. Typical SOH prediction models include equivalent circuit models (ECMs) and electrochemical models. Currently, the ECMS used for estimating the SOH of lithium-ion batteries primarily consist of standard and improved structures. Traditional models, such as the first-order Thevenin model and the second-order RC model, represent battery ageing through changes in parameters such as ohmic resistance or multiple polarisation parameters, and are applicable under general operating conditions [ 10 , 11 ]. Based on this foundation, recent studies have further expanded the ECM architecture and application scenarios. Reference [ 12 ] presents a new ECM based on an RL network, where the traditional RC branch is replaced by two parallel resistor-inductor (R-L) branches to examine the current decay behaviour in the constant-voltage (CV) charging stage. By decoupling the fast and slow dynamic components, this method obtains a time constant that has high correlation with capacity degradation, thereby improving the sensitivity and accuracy of SOH estimation during CV charging to a great extent. Reference [ 13 ] presents an improved ECM (E-ECM) that employs a standard second-order RC structure, supplemented by a first-order inertial block, to detect the concentration gradient between the electrode surface and the bulk caused by solid-phase diffusion. The aforementioned model can achieve higher voltage prediction accuracy when the SOC is low, thus its parameters are closer to representing the non-linear electro-chemical characteristics of the battery degradation. A large number of established electrochemical model-based SOH estimation approaches have progressed from high-fidelity but computationally expensive pseudo-two-dimensional (P2D) models to simplified frameworks balancing mechanistic fidelity and practicability. Some studies have used a fractional-order transfer function to approximate solid-phase diffusion and constructed a single-electrode model that only includes the positive electrode, using parameters to represent capacity decay and internal resistance increase, respectively [ 14 ]. Literature [ 15 ] incorporates SEI growth and stress-induced particle cracking mechanisms into a single-particle model (SPM) to reveal the synergistic ageing effects of active lithium loss and increased interfacial impedance. In addition, fully coupled multi-physics P2D ageing models can comprehensively integrate electrochemical, thermal and mechanical processes to reproduce capacity and power loss caused by simultaneous SEI film growth, lithium electrodeposition and active material loss at different temperatures [ 16 ]. Among them, the most recent advancement is the FOMeA model that integrates fractional-order Padé approximation and a two-state electrolyte system to significantly improve computational efficiency and explicitly incorporate SEI growth and lithium plating/stripping mechanisms, including the formation of “dead lithium.” This method can dynamically update the resistance of SEI film and lithium-plating, thus accurately reflecting the battery’s aging characteristics at all stages of its entire life cycle, such as capacity attenuation, impedance increase, and poor high-rate performance [ 17 ]. In summary, the direction of development for model lightweighting by rationally simplifying the structure has been validated; meanwhile, it still retains key features such as electrochemical reactions to achieve online estimation of battery SOH. A certain degree of the model-based analysis and prediction methods has a certain level universal but there is a direct relationship between the accuracy of prediction of this model complexity. At present, most existing models are developing in the direction of incorporating more elements to better represent various influencing factors. However, the increase in model complexity has made it more difficult to identify model parameters [ 18 ]. Given the rapid development of artificial intelligence technology, as one of its typical applications, data-driven predictive methods have gradually become an essential tool for assessing lithium-ion Batteries. Usually, these approaches do not consider the underlying reasons why electrochemical reactions occur in batteries; therefore, batteries are regarded as “black boxes,” and changes in charge and discharge currents are used to estimate their conditions. Using data obtained from tests or public databases as a sample set for training the prediction model; after training, this model can be used for battery SOH predictions. Compared with the two methods mentioned above, a data-driven approach is more appropriate for online or real-time estimation; it has a low dependence on precise special equipment; It can achieve high computational efficiency. Data-driven methods provide another path for estimation. In recent years, many research efforts have been made, and a large number of algorithms have been developed [ 19 – 29 ]. Reference [ 19 ] proposes the ResBiGRU-AttnAE model, which has a light structure and high accuracy. It only needs charging-phase features to achieve fast online SOH estimation, making it suitable for embedded deployment; however, it lacks physical interpretability. Reference [ 20 ] uses a multi-scale CNN-BiLSTM fusion structure to enhance robustness and noise resistance through the combination of raw sensor signals and time-domain health information, and has shown stable performance in multiple datasets; however, its high computational cost limits real-time implementation on resource-constrained battery management systems (BMS), and some of its features depend on full charge-discharge cycles. Reference [ 21 ] proposes a hybrid framework combining a simplified electrochemical model with LSTM-based correction, which provides some physical interpretability and reduces the need for a large number of training data sets; However, this method requires battery chemistry-specific parameter calibration, has weak cross-chemistry generalisation ability, and its system is relatively complex, making it difficult to be applied in practical engineering. Reference [ 22 ] proposes an improved TWSVM-based method for joint SOH and RUL estimation, achieving high prediction accuracy across multiple datasets and requiring only the easily obtainable constant-current charging time as input, demonstrating strong engineering practicality; however, it lacks physical interpretability, and its overall computational complexity is relatively high, making it unsuitable for embedded deployment. In general, although the four approaches mentioned above differ in focus-accuracy, efficiency or interpretability-they all have limitations; data quality is required, generalisation performance is lacking in a real environment, and prediction reliability cannot be guaranteed. To address the aforementioned issues with existing works, this paper presents a data-driven feature-sifting analysis framework for SOH prediction of battery cells. Through analysis of the IC and DV curves to determine the aging behaviour, a new set of HFs is generated. Using Pearson correlation analysis to determine the extent of association between HFs and battery capacity. In addition, to reduce computational complexity, KPCA is applied to the selected health features for dimensionality reduction, and indirect health features are obtained. To enhance prediction results, a Transformer-LSTM Predictive Model with temporal attention mechanisms can be introduced. Some important hyperparameters, such as the number of attention heads, learning rate, and regularisation coefficient, are optimised using a Newton-Raphson-based algorithm at the same time. In this Model, transformer can capture global temporal dependence, LSTM is good at capturing local dynamic characteristics, and attention mechanism pays more attention to the effect of health status. By combining the forward and backward sequence information, the proposed method can achieve high-accuracy prediction of battery SOH, thereby obtaining high-accuracy and strong robustness SOH predictions. Analysis and feature extraction of IC/DV curves Incremental Capacity Analysis and Differential Voltage Analysis have been applied in the study of battery aging mechanisms [ 30 , 31 ]. Through the peaks and valleys in the corresponding curves, aging-related information is obtained as health features for SOH prediction. Based on the terminal voltage-capacity data obtained in the constant-current charging stage of the battery, Savitzky-Golay filtering was applied to the IC curve and Gaussian filtering was used for the DV curve to reduce noise caused by measurement errors during the experiment. As shown in Figs 2(a) and 3 (a), the IC curves of cells B0005 and CS2_ 35 during charging at different cycle numbers are plotted against the terminal voltage. The horizontal coordinate of the x-axis is set to terminal voltage, and the vertical coordinate is incremental capacity (dQ/dV). With battery ageing, the peak height in the IC curve decreases, the position of the peak moves to higher voltages, and the area under the peak also decreases. This change is mainly due to the loss of lithium inventory (LLI): The continuous increase of the solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) layer is an irreversible consumption of cyclable lithium ions, which weakens the phase transition reaction and manifests as a decrease in peak amplitude. At the same time, a more significant electrochemical driving force needs to be applied, corresponding to an increased voltage, in order to insert fewer lithium ions; therefore, the phase transition potential rises and manifests as a peak shift to the right. Therefore, peak height, peak position and peak area can be used as health indicators to achieve precise and mechanistic interpretable battery ageing assessment. As shown in Figures 2(b) and 3 (b), the differential voltage (dV/dQ) curves of cells B0005 and CS2_ 35 during charging over their entire cycle life are plotted against the terminal voltage. As the number of cycle counts increases, the peak amplitudes in the DV curve gradually decrease; as for the peak positions, they shift towards higher voltage levels; Additionally, at this time, the entire shape tends to flatten and lose distinct characteristics. This change is mainly due to the decrease in LLI and LAM within the battery: LLI reduces the number of cyclable lithium ions, weakens the phase transition reaction (reflected in a reduced peak height), and requires a higher voltage to drive the remaining reactions (manifested as a rightward peak shift); at the same time, LAM further deteriorates the electrode reaction kinetics, causing a reduction in the rate of change of the voltage. Considering that they are highly correlated with battery capacity decay, the inflection points of the charging differential voltage in the DV curve can be used as health indicators to achieve SOH forecasting. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359015.g002 Fig 2 Incremental Capacity (a) and Differential Voltage (b) Curves of NASA Battery B0005. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359015.g003 Fig 3 Incremental Capacity (a) and Differential Voltage (b) Curves of NASA Battery CS2_ 35. 2.3. Newton-Raphson-based optimization algorithm In 2024, the research team led by S. Ravichandran developed a novel swarm intelligence algorithm called the Newton-Raphson-based Optimization algorithm. The algorithm integrates two core mechanisms: first, it introduces the Newton-Raphson Search Rule (NRSR), which constructs a gradient-responsive model to expand the exploration range of the solution space, thereby significantly enhancing global optimization efficiency; second, it incorporates a parameter perturbation escape mechanism, termed the Trap Avoidance Operator (TAO), which employs a probabilistic update strategy to break the correlation among individual solutions, effectively addressing the issue of conventional optimization algorithms becoming trapped in local optima. SOH estimation procedure This study presents a SOH estimation framework that combines feature engineering with intelligent optimization. First, a group of novel battery degradation indicators is derived from the filtered incremental capacity and differential voltage curves. Nonlinear dimensionality reduction is then performed in the high-dimensional feature space using KPCA to obtain the key components that effectively characterize battery degradation. Based on different data partitioning strategies, the preprocessed dataset is split into training and test subsets. During the model training phase, the NRBO algorithm is employed to globally optimize three critical hyperparameters of the Transformer-LSTM model: the number of attention heads, the learning rate, and the L2 regularization coefficient, thereby establishing an adaptive battery degradation prediction model. Finally, the performance of the proposed NRBO-Transformer-LSTM framework for SOH estimation is evaluated on both the NASA and CALCE battery datasets. The NRBO algorithm is compared against other optimization algorithms to highlight its advantages, and both longitudinal and cross-sectional comparative experiments are conducted to comprehensively validate the superiority of the proposed method. 4. Discussion The hybrid deep learning framework proposed in this study-integrating electrochemical mechanism-derived features with intelligent optimization-achieves high accuracy and reliability in lithium-ion battery SOH estimation. Its core strength lies in combining the flexibility of data-driven modeling with the physical constraints of battery aging mechanisms. HF2-HF4 extracted from IC curves directly reflect fundamental degradation processes: shifts in the anode peak indicate LLI, while cathode changes correlate with LAM. This physics-informed feature engineering embeds domain knowledge into the model, guiding it to learn true degradation patterns rather than superficial correlations. KPCA further refines these into compact, noise-robust IHF. To capture both long-term trends and short-term fluctuations in aging dynamics, we employ a Transformer-LSTM hybrid architecture. The Transformer models global degradation via long-range attention across the full cycle life, while the LSTM captures local transient effects caused by operational variations such as charge/discharge rate and temperature. Their synergy enables accurate reconstruction of complex, nonlinear SOH trajectories. Optimal performance is ensured by NRBO, which efficiently tunes hyperparameters to avoid local minima and enhance prediction smoothness. Unlike pure black-box models, the proposed SOH estimation scheme improves interpretability and generalization through embedded physics; unlike purely mechanistic models, it retains strong adaptability via data-driven learning. This “gray-box” paradigm-where data informs physics and physics guides data—offers a robust, deployable solution for next-generation battery management systems that balances accuracy with physical fidelity. 6. Conclusion To achieve high-accuracy SOH prediction of lithium-ion batteries, this paper builds up a feature-sifting based analysis framework. Building upon these processed features, an enhanced NRBO-Transformer-LSTM model is developed for SOH forecasting. This architecture leverages the Transformer’s attention mechanism and combines it with LSTM’s capacity to model temporal dependencies. The model effectively incorporates backward contextual information and emphasizes critical features throughout the sequence. The proposed SOH prediction scheme was validated on the NASA and CALCE battery dataset, with the NRBO algorithm compared against other optimization algorithms to highlight its superiority. Both longitudinal and cross-sectional comparative experiments were designed to demonstrate the superiority of the proposed method. Experimental results show that, with a 70% training set, the proposed method achieves a maximum MAE of 0.0084 and a RMSE of 0.0091, significantly outperforming other benchmark approaches and demonstrating superior performance in both accuracy and robustness. These results confirm that the framework achieves not only high prediction accuracy but also strong robustness, offering a promising solution for reliable SOH estimation in practical battery management systems.
