Au-IDE/LFA biosensor: an integrated sensing platform with lateral flow immunoassay for quantitative detection of hCG hormone using non-faradaic continuous FFT-admittance voltammetry
Authors: Nazanin Behrooz, Farzaneh Shemirani, Reza Faridi-Majidi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The development of the novel Au-IDE/LFA (gold interdigitated electrode with lateral flow immunoassay) biosensor, an integrated electrochemical sensing platform with lateral flow immunoassay, for quantitative detection of hCG (human Chorionic Gonadotropin) hormone levels is successfully directed here. To fabricate the Au-IDE/LFA setup, a free multi-electrode array of AU-IDE was configured exactly on five zones of LFA on the flow direction, employing non-faradaic measurements through a combination of two electrodes. The impedance behavior of each zone, connected with an Au surface electrode in the Au-IDE/LFA setup was characterized by frequency response analysis (FRA) using non-faradaic electrochemical impedance spectroscopy. Precisely, FRA validated that Au surfaces exhibit similar current homogeneity due to produce the repeatable impedance. The assay optimization using CFFT (Continuous Fast Fourier Transform)-admittance voltammetry, a new method, pinpointed that optimizing the excitation potential by utilizing lower SW frequencies at 88 Hz, amplitude at 10 mV, and DC potential at 10 mV resulted in excellent admittance responses of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. Both bare and standard LFA were used for signal baseline establishment to ignore the background signal noise induced by the anti-β-hCG-AuNPs (6.01 µA/V), PBS buffer (11.51 µA/V), and urine background (22.11 µA/V). Admittance response showed a high sensitivity about three times more than impedance response with limit of detection by 2.53 mIU/mL in a linear range of 5-500 mIU/mL for hCG with R2= 0.998. This performance metric was utilized to determine the performance efficiency of proposed biosensor. The lack of any cross-interference with other human hormone like hCG and discriminating beta unit from alpha unit of hCG confirmed high selectivity and specificity of fabricated biosensor. The functional performance of biosensor resulted in the direct and quantitative detection of the native hCG in urine biological matrix, which offers a promising approach with high potential for early diagnosis of pregnancy test in clinical samples. Introduction

Qualitative or quantitative real-time measurement and detection of concentration level of a target analyte are fundamental for making decisions for diagnosis of some applications, e.g., pregnancy and diabetes tests. Accordingly, human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG) consists of two subunits, α-hCG and β-hCG, with the β-hCG subunit being widely used in clinical medicine as a specific biomarker to predict pregnancy [1]. It is well-documented that the levels of hCG persevere below 5 mIU/mL in pre-menopausal women while rising to over 110 000 mIU/mL in pregnant women. To date, numerous detection systems have been developed for quantitative monitoring therapy of hCG, including electrochemical immunoassay [2], chemiluminesence immunoassay [3], fluoroimmunoassay [4], resonance scattering spectroscopy [5], atomic emission spectroscopy [6], radioimmunoassay [7–8], enzyme-linked immunoassay (ELISA) [9, 10], lateral flow immunoassay strips (LFA) [11], and more [12]. However, many of these methods suffer from high costs and methodological difficulties.

In the last decades, screening and monitoring therapy of hCG hormone using LFA has garnered significant attention to validate pregnancy in a broad range of urine or serum samples [11, 13, 14]. In this regard, due to having a low-cost manner by non-specialized users, the facile fabrication process, rapidity and simplicity, in-field applicability, time-consuming, potential for integration with other systems, and suitability for yes/no detection of an analyte in a sample through a visual colorimetric approach of LFA (e.g., home pregnancy test based on LFAs manner) made it as a popular commercial tool for both clinical and point-of-care (POC) testing of pregnancy at early stages through concentrations sensing of hCG hormone [14]. Despite these advantages, LFA devices or kits, as important valuable commercial techniques for POC testing, suffer from low sensitivity and frequently yield false negative results, as they depend on colorimetric detection. Consequently, instances involving low levels of hCG concentration can lead to ambiguous outcomes in negative readings [15–18]. Overall, deficient of detection sensitivity and accuracy for the diagnosis of the lowest concentration of target analyte, reliance on colorimetric detection, and poor repeatability are the main limitations of LFAs, which only still offers the qualitative or at best semi-quantitative assays of one highly concentrated target analyte in complex biological samples [19–23]. However, enhancing the sensitivity of the LFA is crucial to enable accurate quantitative output. Subsequently, many strategies in an integrate or hybrid manner with LFAs have been established to modify or upgrade these limitations.

Until now, to enhance the sensitivity of LFA, researchers have explored integrating various technologies, such as optical [24, 25], thermal readers [26], chemiluminescence [27], Raman spectroscopy [28, 29], magnetic field [30], and electrochemical readouts [31, 32] to develop quantitative LFA with highly sensitive and quantitative detection of biological analytes. Lately, application strategies based on electrochemical sensing platforms, that can be integrated with LFA, have attracted remarkable interest due to their high sensitivity and accuracy, quick response, selectivity, cost-effectiveness, and being superior to traditional sensing platforms in detecting the lowest amount of target analyte [14, 33, 34]. Electrochemical measurement shows promise as an integration candidate with LFA, enabling the development of low-cost, simplicity, fast signal generation, high sensitivity, single-step, and disposable point-of-care technology [14]. Electrochemical lateral flow immunoassays (eLFIAs) are a kind of LFA that was generated by assembling flexible screen-printed electrodes with lateral flow immuno-chromatographic test strips. These integrated systems have been successfully miniaturized for maintaining the suitable analytical performance, high reproducibility, and the mode diversity of signal generation techniques such as voltammetry, amperometry, and impedimetric measurements. These advantages provide the high potential of eLFIAs for the real-time screening with large-scale detection range from complex sample matrices [35, 36]. Considering these conditions, the development of the quantitative eLFIAs can be used as an alternate technique to attain high sensitivity against conventional LFA for true quantitative outputs of hCG hormone even in its lowest amount. Although eLFIAs have been applied to detect many biomarker analytes [14], recent several quantitative eLFIAs have developed by integrating electrochemical transducers into LFAs including screen-printed electrodes (SPEs), screen-printed micro-electrodes, paper-based ion concentration polarization (ICP) pre-concentrators integrated with LFA to highly sensitive detection of the lowest amount of hCG for validating pregnancy test [37–39]. It is noticeable that most of these developed eLFIAs use a faradaic mode approach of cyclic voltammetry and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) to detect hCG in serum and urine samples. In these modes, electroactive molecules experience oxidation and reduction on the electrode surface and generate measurable electrical current. As a result, eLFIAs in the faradaic mode, utilize electroactive materials as tracers and place electrodes underneath and above the test zone [40]. These electrodes are situated on either side of the nitrocellulose (NC) membrane or at both ends of the strips [41, 42]. Clips or plastic wraps are employed to maintain a good connection between the membrane and electrodes, facilitating efficient diffusion of electroactive material to reach the electrode swiftly [14]. However, this assembly often face challenges with reproducibility exhibiting a relative standard deviation (RSD) of approximately 10% due to the poor interaction between immobilized antibody onto NC membrane and electrode, which led to poor sensitivity followed by long response time of eFLIAs assay due to poor electron transfer capability of the screen-printed electrode’s solid surface [19, 42]. Additionally, requiring the existence of redox reagents/labels and the utilization of DC conditions, demanding high input voltage bias for a signal response via reduction-oxidation redox-probe, and avoiding crosstalk during signal transduction are the main limitations for developing eLFIAs using faradaic EIS, which reduce the sensitivity for real-time measurement of target analytes [43, 44]. Utilizing non-Faradaic EIS (nf- EIS) is introduced as a substitute and powerful strategy to overcome these disadvantages, electrochemical measurement allows the detection of minute changes at the electrode-solution interface without the need for a redox label or tagged molecule [45]. Despite several attempts to develop the eLFIAs devices using faradaic EIS, the capability of integrating electrochemical transducers into LFA test strip assays using non-faradaic EIS to highly sensitive and real-time detection of hCG hormone for pregnancy test is lacking.

The current study aims to introduce a unique commercialized eLFIAs technique sensing platform by integrating of electrochemical-based sensor with a LFA using non-faradaic EIS for the sensitive detection and real-time quantification of hCG in urine samples. By combining the simplicity and cost-effectiveness of LFA with the enhanced analytical performance offered by non-faradaic EIS, this technique holds great potential for improving diagnostic capabilities in-home testing devices and allows to take quick decisions for the quantitative determination of hCG hormone levels.

Materials and methods

The hCG purification, chemicals and equipment

The native β-hCG form was purified by using trypsin affinity chromatography described by Khademi et al. [46] from pregnancy urine sample, refolded [47, 48] and stored at -20 °C until next utilized as an antigen for analysis. The specific anti-β-HCG monoclonal antibodies (230715) and goat anti-mouse-IgG polyclonal antibodies (231143) were provided from Safecare Biotech (Hangzhou) Co.,Ltd (China). The serial dilutions of recombinant hCG concentrations, prepared by Phosphate Buffered Saline (PBS, pH = 7.4), were used for experimental measurements. The PBS Buffer and healthy urine samples were applied as a blank solution for electrochemical baseline characterization. Additionally, α-hCG, Luteinizing Hormone (LH), Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), and Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH) hormones were sourced from Pishtaz Teb (Rapid Test Manufacturing Company, Iran). Bovine serum albumin (BSA), acetone, isopropyl alcohol (IPA), titanium, and silver glue were ordered from Sigma-Aldrich Company. NC membrane was obtained from Advanced Microdevices PVT LTD (CNPC-SS12; Ambala, India). The LFA devices were sourced from Zist-Abzar Pajoohan (Rima Group, Nanotechnology Company, Iran). All reagents were purchased at analytical grade and utilized without the need for additional purification. Zeta potential of the biomolecules on the fabricated sensing platform was measured using a Zetasizer Nano ZS (Malvern, UK). An Autolab General Purpose System PGSTAT 101 from Eco Chemie, Netherlands and the custom-designed potentiostat were used to impedance and admittance measurements, respectively.

The design, preparation and fabrication of gold interdigitated electrode (Au-IDE)

The primary design of electrodes was planned with Autodesk AutoCAD 2025.1 software and was fabricated at MEMS & NEMS Laboratory, School of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Tehran, Iran (Fig. 1a). The Au-IDE electrodes used in this study were fabricated by lithography on the microscope slide glass through following steps (Fig. 1b). First, a glass slide was thoroughly cleaned with acetone and IPA, then rinsed with deionized water. The glass was blow-dried afterward and transferred to the sputtering chamber. The chamber was pumped down to 10− 6 torr and Argon gas was introduced at a flow rate of 10 sccm. A 100 nm of Au layer was sputtered onto the slide via DC sputtering technique, with a 10 nm thick of titanium adhesion layer beneath. The glass-coated gold was then spin-coated with a positive tone photoresist, Shipley S1813 at 5000 rpm for 30 s. Subsequently, the gold substrate was soft-baked at 110 °C for 60 s before transfer into the contact lithography unit. The substrate was illuminated under i-line UV light (Photolithography using 365 nm wavelength UV radiation) for 12 s with a power density of 15 mW/cm2. Upon development in a basic solution, the substrate was hard-baked at 120 °C for 2 minutes. The substrate was then submerged in a freshly prepared aqua regia solution for 10 s to etch the gold layer, and the adhesion layer was later removed using a titanium etching solution (hydrofluoric acid 48%: hydrogen peroxide: deionized water (1:1:20 v/v)). Finally, the photoresist was stripped by acetone in a sonication bath for 5 min. After fabrication, the gold electrode was cleaned using IPA, and a copper wire was attached to the electrode using silver glue.

Au-IDE/LFA biosensor configuration: the multi-electrode array of AU-IDE electrodes on LFA

The graphical representation for Au-IDE/LFA (gold interdigitated electrode with lateral flow immunoassay) biosensor is illustrated in Fig. 2. The LFA strips assembly was completed once the Au-IDE electrode was prepared. The sensing platform employs non-faradaic measurements through a combination of two electrodes (Fig. 2a). One electrode function as the working electrode while the other simultaneously performs the functions of a reference and counter electrode, as shown in Fig. 1b. The dimensions of Au-IDE working, and reference/counter electrodes are 0.5 mm × 1 mm. Au-IDE electrodes were then correctly positioned to ensure good contact on the LFA strips, enabling the investigation of every five zones on the surface of the LFA: sample pad (SP), conjugation pad (CP), test line (TL), control line (CL), and nitrocellulose beyond the control line (NCL), as illustrated in Fig. 2. In the next step, the integrated-Au-IDE/LFA, including Au-IDE/SP, Au-IDE/CP, Au-IDE/TL, Au-IDE/CL, and Au-IDE/NCL, was held by installing four expansion bolts and nut washers at the top and bottom to ensure an appropriate connection between them.

The preparation and functionalize of bare LFA and sandwich-based (standard) LFA strips

Two types of LFA paper tests, including bare LFA and sandwich-based LFA, were considered for electrochemical assays of hCG antigen by developed Au-IDE/LFA biosensing platform. Bare LFA (using as blank test) was composed of the succeeding elements: SP, CP (without any labelled anti-β-HCG-AuNP antibodies), TL (without any immobilized anti-β-HCG monoclonal antibodies), CL (without any immobilized goat anti-mouse-IgG polyclonal antibodies), and an absorbent pad. The sandwich-based LFA (standard LFA) was consisted of the SP, CP (pre-loaded with anti-β-HCG-AuNP antibodies), TL (immobilized by anti-β-HCG monoclonal antibodies), CL (immobilized by goat anti-mouse-IgG polyclonal antibodies), and an absorbent pad. The schematic of bare LFA and sandwich-based LFA are represented in Supplementary Fig. S1. For functionalization of the LFA paper, the SP and CP were submerged separately in the PBS buffer (containing 1% BSA, 5% sucrose, 0.05% Tween 20; pH 7.4) for 30 min, then were dried for 60 min at 37 °C. Ultimately, the fixed concentration of anti-β-HCG-AuNP antibodies was immobilized by spraying at a rate of 5 µl/cm via Biodot XYZ 3060 device on the CP. Similarly, after diluting target antibodies in the 10 mM PBS buffer (supplemented with 0.9% NaCl, 0.3% sucrose, 0.2% BSA; pH 7.4), the fixed concentration of anti-β-HCG monoclonal and goat anti-mouse-IgG polyclonal antibodies were immobilized by spraying at a rate of 1 µl/cm via Biodot XYZ 3060 device on the TL and CL, respectively.

Specific electrochemical signal characterization of each pair of the Au-IDE electrode by paper cutting

The determination of specific electrochemical signal of each pair electrode or each zone’s share of LFA on the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor was performed by nf-EIS (non-faradaic electrochemical impedance spectroscopy) detection mode to quantify hCG on the standard LFA. In this regard, the impedance spectrum measurement was conducted at five designated zones of the standard LFA contented with 5 Au-IDEs using a two-electrodes setup as following steps: (1) Empty SP was manually cut into 2.5 mm × 6 mm using a guillotine paper cutter and located at the lower end of the CP with an overlap of 2 mm, connecting the Au-IDE/SP setup. A fixed volume of hCG antigen (20 µL consisting of 500 mIU hCG) was loaded on the SP on the flow direction. After 160 s passed and the sample solution reached the zone of the absorbent pad, when was thoroughly wet, only the surface electrochemical changes related to the SP were recorded. (2) The zones from the SP to the CP were cut into 2.5 mm × 15 mm and positioned on the LFA at the lower end of the NC membrane with an overlap of 2 mm, which connected with Au-IDE/SP and Au-IDE/CP setup, respectively. Under this condition, the surface electrochemical changes related to the SP and CP were recorded after reaching the loaded solution to the absorbent pad for 160 s. (3) The zones from the SP to the TL was cut into 2.5 mm × 26 mm and positioned on the LFA at the higher TL zone of the NC membrane, which connected with the Au-IDE/SP, Au-IDE/CP, and Au-IDE/TL setups, respectively. The surface electrochemical changes related to the SP, CP, and TL were recorded as the previous steps. (4) The zones from the SP to the CL were cut into 2.5 mm × 31 mm and positioned on the LFA at the higher CL zone of the NC membrane, which connected with the Au-IDE/SP, Au-IDE/CP, Au-IDE/TL, and Au-IDE/CL setups, respectively. Subsequently, the surface electrochemical changes related to the four mentioned zones were recorded after loading the sample solution from the SP to the absorbent pad for 160 s. (5) Ultimately, the zones from the SP to the absorbent pad were cut into 2.5 mm × 41 mm and placed on the LFA at the higher end of the NC membrane with an overlap of 2 mm, which connected with the Au-IDE/SP, Au-IDE/CP, Au-IDE/TL, Au-IDE/CL, and Au-IDE/NCL setups, respectively.

The nf-EIS electrochemical measurements of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor

FRA analysis

In this paper, we have employed non-faradaic EIS integrated with LFA for the detection of hCG in PBS. All impedance measurements were carried out by an Autolab General Purpose System PGSTAT 101 from Eco Chemie, Netherlands with an AC input voltage of 10 mV over a frequency range of 1 Hz to 0.1 MHz superimposed on a DC voltage at the open circuit potential (OCP). The impedance data were collected for PBS solutions applied to both bare and standard LFA, as well as for various concentrations of hCG analyte solutions applied to the standard LFA in the fabricated Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. FRA studies on the surface of the LFA were investigated using a pair Au-IDE electrode in the five zones of the LFA including SP, CP, TL, CL, and NCL. The five pairs Au-IDE electrodes were faced down on the nitrocellulose surface of the LFA on the fabricated Au-IDE/LFA biosensor and the FRA analysis was investigated on the first electrode (Au-IDE/SP), first to second electrode (Au-IDE/SP to Au-IDE/CP), first to third electrode (Au-IDE/SP to Au-IDE/TL), first to fourth electrode (Au-IDE/SP to Au-IDE/CL), and first to fifth electrode (Au-IDE/SP to Au-IDE/NCL). Ultimately, the net imaginary impedance response related to the hCG detection was obtained by subtracting the imaginary impedance, Z” at 88 Hz when the hCG solution migrates across the LFA, from Z” measured when PBS migrates across the LFA, for the TL and CL zones separately.

CFFT-admittance voltammetry

To explore the non-faradaic processes on the LFA surface, an especially custom-designed potentiostat was employed. it was interfaced with a computer featuring a data acquisition board (PCL-818PG, PC-Lab Card Co.), which facilitated the monitoring and analysis of current and admittance. The experimental setup utilized electrochemical software programmed in Delphi 6.0. The custom-designed potentiostat was applied to perform the CFFT-admittance voltammetry measurements on the TL and CL zones of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor (Supplementary Fig. S2). The equations governing these measurements indicate that, in FFT admittance measurements, frequency, DC potential, and amplitude are significant factors affecting the performance of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. The excitation potential depends on the amplitude (Esw) ranging from 10 to 50 mV, the frequency which ranges from 88 to 11,300 Hz, and DC potential which varies from − 200 to + 200 mV. It should be noted that initial changes in electrode admittance occur when the analyte and solution on the surface of the nitrocellulose adsorb onto the electrode surface.

Assay optimization of the developed Au-IDE/LFA biosensor

LFA assay comprises different fundamental components, each component demanding systematic optimization to establish a precise and accurate detection system on the fabricated Au-IDE/LFA biosensor.

The controlled conditions of analysis

The pH value of PBS buffer (pH 7.4), capillary flow rate (3.31 ± 0.18 µL/min (± 6%)), flow time up to end of NC membrane (160 s), structure and porosity of NC membrane (10-µm pore, 30 mm wide: CNPC-SS12; Ambala, India), sufficient time needed for formatting of the antibody-antigen complex along the flow of fluid on the NC membrane at the test and control lines (1 min), concentration of capture antibody on the control line (anti-rabbit-IgG: at 1/1000 of 1 mg/mL), concentration of conjugated antibody with AuNPs on the conjugate pad (anti-β-HCG-AuNPs: 100 µg/L) and concentration of anti-β-HCG antibody on the test line (100 µg/L), signal amplification by gold nanoparticles (size of the AuNPs: 40-nm), and the viscosity of urine samples (0.623–0.635 mPas at 37 °C) as typical effecting components on the performance of Au-IDE/LFA system were controlled during experiments [16, 49].

Surface homogeneity test of multi-electrode array on Au-IDE/LFA system

To assay the homogeneity test of each pair of Au-IDE electrodes surface associated with five zones of the LFA, 20 µL of PBS 50 mM was loaded onto the sample pad of the bare LFA and allowed to migrate to the end of the absorption pad for 160 s. The lower and upper parts of TL zone of the LFA were cut into 5 mm segments and placed face-down on each surface of the pair Au-IDE electrodes separately and then FRA study was conducted exclusively in TL zone. The nf-EIS measurement with an AC input voltage of 10 mV over a frequency range of 1 Hz to 100 kHz superimposed on a DC voltage at OCP was performed separately for each pair of Au-IDE electrode surface. The nf-EIS measurement was conducted at three replications. The paper weight of the test line zone was measured before and after adding the PBS solution to validate that the amount of PBS was equal for each FRA analysis of the electrode surface.

The volume sample and the concentration of background dilution solution (PBS buffer)

After validating the homogeneity of each pair of Au-IDE electrode surfaces, the concentrations of PBS buffer used for diluting hCG antigen, and the volume sample of PBS buffer were optimized for Au-IDE/LFA system. For this regard, to minimize the effect of background solution on hormone detection, 500 mIU/ml hCG diluted in different concentrations of PBS buffer (1, 50, and 100 mM). Then, 20 µL of each solution was loaded on the sample pad of the standard LFA and allowed to migrate to the absorbent pad for 160 s. Ultimately, LFA paper was cut, and FRA measurements were conducted at three replications on the SP zone to the end of the TL zone. In addition, for optimizing the volume sample of hCG analyte, the diluted-hCG in PBS 50 mM (500 mIU/mL of hCG) at volume samples of 10, 15, 20, and 30 µL were loaded on SP zone of the standard LFA, allowing it to migrate to the absorbent pad for 160 s. Ultimately, LFA paper was cut and FRA measurements were conducted in triplicate along the flow direction at NCL zone (Au-IDE/NCL).

Optimizing the effecting parameters of CFFT-admittance voltammetry technique

A calibration technique with applied bias pointedly boosts the measurement accuracy. In this paper, the non-faradaic CFFT-admittance technique as a new method combined with LFA, is employed for enhanced hCG determination. In this technique SWV is based on measurements of electrode admittance as a function of potential. In this approach, an alternating current (AC) potential with an amplitude (10 to 50 mV) is superimposed on a DC potential (+ 200 to -200) at a particular frequency (88, 354, 708, 1420, 5670, and 11300 Hz). In this study, we emphasize the significance of factors such as frequency, DC potential, and amplitude in the detection process, as they influence the signal of analyte during detection. To optimize the mentioned parameters, a series of experiments were conducted using blank solution (PBS buffer) and hCG at a concentration of 500 mIU/ml on a standard LFA for the developed Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. For selecting the optimized conditions, frequency values of 88, 354, 708, 1420, 5670, and 11,300 Hz, with an amplitude range of 10 to 50 mV, and DC potential varying from − 200 to 200 were examined.

Quantitative detection of hGC hormone in real samples

Human pregnancy urine samples from pregnant women and healthy urine sample from author of this study were collected by a trained healthcare worker at the Vali-e-Asr Reproductive Health Research Center in Tehran, Iran. The positive (˃25 mIU/mL) and negative (≤ 5 mIU/mL) urine samples for hCG hormone were checked by ELISA serological technique using a monoclonal antibody. Artificial samples were generated by analyzing the healthy urine samples spiked with known concentrations (was determined by ELISA method) of hCG (25, 50, 100, 250, 500, 750, and 950 mIU/mL), which migrated across both bare and standard LFAs. Subsequently, nf-CFFT admittance voltammetry measurement was conducted at three replications using an AC input voltage of 10 mV amplitude at a frequency of 88 Hz, superimposed on a + 10 mV DC potential applied on the bare LFA and standard LFA in the developed Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. Additionally, a mixture of healthy urine samples from five different women were spiked with five hCG concentrations (25, 50, 100, 250, and 500 mIU/mL) to generated calibration curve. The serial dilution of hCG in positive pregnancy urine were detected by ELISA method. These samples were then allowed to migrate across both bare and standard LFAs. The obtained results from artificial sample were compared with the established calibration curve to evaluate the sensor’s performance across diverse urine matrixes.

Results and discussion

EIS is an interesting technique for biosensing application and the characterization of the electrical properties of a wide board range of biological materials in both faradaic (f-EIS) and nf-EIS modes [50]. Accordingly, it has been appropriately manipulated to detect pregnancy-associated hCG biomarkers by eLFIA sensing system in the f-EIS mode through the integration of an electrochemical sensor with a lateral flow immunoassay [37–39]. In the current study, for first time, a highly sensitive Au-IDE/LFA biosensor is introduced as an eLFIA device for the quantification and detection of hCG pregnancy hormone based on the nf-FRA and nf-CFFT-admittance voltammetry techniques. Notably, this approach does not require oxidation or reduction targets. This section illuminates the electrochemical characterization of strips associated with the developed Au-IDE/LFA system, assay optimization, baseline determination of measurements, quantification of hCG binding occurrence, and sensor’s performance assessment.

Impedance behavior of the electrode surfaces on Au-IDE/LFA biosensor under PBS loading

As the start of generation of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor, where the surface of electrodes setup was overlapped or touched with LFA-specific zones. Subsequently, an initial nf-EIS measurement to validate the appropriate physical connection between Au-IDE electrodes and five zones associated with the LFA device was implemented electrochemically. In this regard, the electrochemical characterization of Au-IDE/LFA surfaces including Au-IDE/SP, Au-IDE/CP, Au-IDE/TL, Au-IDE/CL, and Au-IDE/NCL contacted electrodes were performed by using the FRA technique when the 20 µL of 50 mM PBS buffer was migrated along the flow direction on both bare and standard LFA. As apparently detected from Fig. 3a and b, the Nyquist plots of impedance behavior of the Au-IDE/LFA surfaces are displayed.

The FRA analysis of the strips on the bare LFA showed a specific trend in impedance response across the measured range (Fig. 3a). Indeed, the impedance changes associated with each surface was aggregated on the subsequent surface with a certain pattern of decreasing values on flow direction. The reduction of impedance elements corresponds to the increasing surface area of the electrode from Au-IDE/SP to Au-IDE/NCL against the flow direction of PBS on bare LFA. In contrast, when 50 mM PBS buffer was introduced onto the standard LFA, the impedance elements gradually declined from Au-IDE/SP to Au-IDE/NCL, which can be attributed to two main factors: the expanding electrode surface area from Au-IDE/SP to Au-IDE/NCL, and the presence of anti-β-hCG antibodies conjugated with AuNPs, which significantly reduce the impedance of the electrode (Fig. 3c). Furthermore, Z’’ decrease patterns were detected in surfaces such as Au-IDE/CP, Au-IDE/TL, and Au-IDE/CL (Fig. 3b, c), owing to the anti-β-hCG-AuNPs present on these surfaces and high conductivity of PBS solution due to the large quantities of mobile ions [44].

The Z baseline determination for obtaining the net impedance under PBS loading

Under status of the aggregation of impedance changes associated with surfaces in the Au-IDE/LFA setup, it is complicate to assay the fluidic samples such as PBS buffer, hCG, and more biologically related state on flow direction. More specifically, determining a distinct Z’’ baseline at a frequency of 88 Hz, for the FRA behavior of each surface of Au-IDE/LFA setup is highly important step to present a correction methodology that estimates the impedance variation of specific surface distinctly, when PBS buffer migrates through the target zones on the flow direction. In this regard, the bare and standard LFAs were cut from each surface zone including SP, then end of the CP, end of TL, end of CL, and at last beyond the CL, respectively. Subsequently, each zone was located on the surface of electrode and nf-EIS measurement was conducted using the FRA technique under a load of 50 mM PBS. However, the Z’’ belong to PBS on bare LFA was considered as Z’’ baseline impedance and which was determined for all five target zones of the Au-IDE/LFA setup. For illustration, the Z’’ baseline impedance of the TL on bare and standard LFAs under PBS loading was calculated as difference the Z’’ between TL and CP zone. Critical thinking on baseline corrections for electrochemical sensing surface has previously been reported by several works [51, 52]. Indeed, applying a Z’’ baseline in Au-IDE/LFA sensing platform allows differentiation between impedance changes induced by anti-β-HCG conjugated with AuNPs (Z’’ anti-β-HCG-AuNPs) and maintaining the effective concentration of PBS solution applied on bare LFA (in the absence of anti-β-hCG-AuNPs) that has a reduction trend on the impedance variation, as confirmed in Fig. 3c. Optimization of the PBS concentrations and sampling volume rate for maximization of sensitivity in electrochemical sensing

Recent studies have been proclaimed that the sample volume of the target sample influences the flow velocity at TL zone on the standard LFA, which might be because of the different situations in an exhausted reservoir and a flow in an unsaturated status [55, 56]. After confirming the surface homogeneity of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor, the sample volumes of PBS 50 mM at 10µL, 15µL, 20µL, 25µL and 30µL were investigated by FRA technique to determine an optimal sample volume, as the sample migrates from SP to NCL zone on standard LFA for 160 s. The Nyquist plots of the impedance variation for the different sample volume of PBS is exhibited in Fig. 5a. The FRA analysis indicated an apparent relationship between the Z″ and Z′ elements of impedance and the PBS sampling volume. Notably, the Rs on the NCL zone declined by 66.18% (from 1828.00 Ω to 618.23 Ω) as the sample volume enhanced from 10µL to 20 µL, after which the Rs showed a continued, decreasing trend (Fig. 5b). Afterward, the impedance variation revealed a constant trend on NCL zone when PBS sample volume promoted from 20 µL to 30 µL (Fig. 5a). Accordingly, LFA zones indicated similar impedance behavior, and the obtained Z″ and Z′ elements of PBS sample volume from 20 µL to 30 µL did not disclose a significant difference at confidence level of 95% according to LSD test (Fig. 5b). By increasing the sample volume up to 20 µL, a volume-dependent trend detected until the standard LFA reached saturation and the impedance remains constant. It can be remarked also that the Rs value of PBS sampling volume from 20 µL to 30 µL exhibited a non-significant manner, indicating a uniform current homogeneity following by a good agreement with the obtained FRA-impedance spectroscopy results. So, the impedance variation, which is related to sample volume, will be limited and PBS sample volume at optimal level of 20 µL was chosen as the optimal condition for the rest of the experiments. This approves the possibility of improving the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor sensitivity by regulating the sample volume (at 20 µL) to maintain a persistent PBS concentration. Certainly, for monitoring the flow velocity across LFA zones, it should be considered the impacts of the sample volume at into account cautiously [56, 57]. Under this condition, the flow velocity by 3.31 mm/s at the TL was considered as a controlled factor when the 20 µL of PBS 50 mM migrated on the standard LFA in the flow direction for 160 s.

Meanwhile, Tris-HCl and PBS buffers have been frequently used for immunostimulatory experiment as solution buffer to dilute biomolecules or microorganisms. It has been stated that the biological analysis of different biomolecules effectively impacted by the composition of the PBS buffer solution [58]. Using PBS buffer as a background matrix with the target analyte not only improves sensitivity of biosensing system, but also offers a medium with optimal conditions for protein coupling reactions [58, 59]. However, the impact of background solution buffer as an effective factor in the impedance variation of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor cannot be ignored in electrochemical biosensing of hCG hormone and thus, it should be optimized. In this regard, the 500 mIU/mL hCG was diluted in the 20 µL of different PBS concentrations (1 mM, 25 mM, 50 mM, 75 mM, and 100 mM) and FRA measurements were analyzed as they migrated from SP to TL zone on standard LFA. Figure 5c exhibits the optimization results for PBS background solution buffer using Nyquist plot attained from nf-EIS measurement. The results clearly revealed that the reduction in impedance, due to high conductivity of PBS concentration, reached a plateau from 50 mM to 100 mM PBS concentrations indicating that the interference interactions of PBS solution with the biochemical components existing in hCG hormone samples (protein and non-protein substances) becomes significantly restricted. Likewise, as the PBS concentration in the diluted solution increased up to 50 mM in the presence of hCG hormone, the obtained Z″ and Z′ elements decreased dramatically and afterwards showed a plateau for the Z″ and Z′ elements (Fig. 5d). Due to the unwanted interactions of protein rely on PBS chemical properties, it has been found that PBS buffer can be varied the affinity of antibody-antigen interactions and subsequently altered the results of the used immunoassay technique [58]. Hence, optimizing the appropriate concentration of PBS buffer to dilute the hCG samples without interfering with the hormone detection for the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor is a crucial step. The optimization process confirmed that not only the use of 50 mM PBS for diluting of the 500 mIU/mL hCG had no interfere with the hCG detection, but also PBS buffer may help to improve the sensitivity of developed Au-IDE/LFA biosensor throughout maintaining a constant pH condition and conserving the protein uniformity for configuring a proper antibody-antigen complex.

Optimizing the parameters of CFFT-admittance voltammetry and the Y baseline determination under PBS loading

In this study, CFFT-admittance voltammetry technique along with FRA impedance spectroscopy were utilized to quantitative detect of hCG hormone on the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor under nf-EIS condition. In this method the admittance of a square wave signal is calculated based on application of discrete FFT. The approach is designed to process the voltammetric signal, which has a special potential waveform. Due to this fact that in this method, admittance response of the sensor depends on the applied conditions on excitation waveform [60, 61]. To obtain the optimum SW wave form condition for maximum signal for admittance, for this electrochemical combination method (eLFA) in Au-IDE/LFA system, DC potential (-200, -100, -50, 0, 50, 100, 200 mV), amplitude (10, 20, 30, 40, 50 mV) and frequency (88, 354, 708, 1420, 5670, 11300 Hz) were studied at three conditions including PBS on bare LFA, PBS on standard LFA, and 500 mIU/mL hCG applied to the standard LFA.

At first step, to attain the net admittance of analyte (Y″net), the Y″ baseline for PBS solution on bare and standard LFA and the Y″ anti-β-HCG-AuNPs were calculated by the applied formula for Z″ baseline and Z″ anti-β-HCG-AuNPs in Au-IDE/LFA system. As such, the absolute Y″ anti-β-HCG-AuNPs was gained by following formula: ∆Y″(anti-β-HCG-AuNPs at specific zone) = ∆Y″(PBS applied on standard LFA at every zone) - ∆Y″(PBS applied on bare LFA at specific zone), where Y″ is the imaginary element of the admittance response under non faradaic manner at optimized potential excitation condition, as mentioned in 2.6 section. For example: ∆Y″ baseline at TL zone and ∆Y″ anti-β-hCG-AuNPs at TL zone are attained by the equation below:

∆Y” baseline PBS at TL zone = Y” PBS on bare LFA at TL (48.64 µA/V) - Y” PBS on bare LFA at CP (37.12 µA/V) = 11.52 µA/V.

∆Y” baseline anti-βhCG-AuNPs at TL zone = Y” PBS on standard LFA at TL (68.82 µA/V) - Y” PBS on standard LFA at CP (51.30 µA/V) - ∆Y” baseline PBS at TL zone (11.52 µA/V) = 6.01 µA/V.

The results verified that in the presence of unbounded anti-β-HCG-AuNPs the admittance amplified by 22.78 µA/V, 6.01 µA/V, and 53.85 µA/V in the Au-IDE/CP, Au-IDE/TL and Au-IDE/CL, respectively. However, due to the high importance of the CL and TL zones in the diagnosis of hCG analyte in Au-IDE/LFA system, the obtained Y″ analyte of the mentioned-zones was included in the optimization process. As exhibited in Fig. 6a, it can be observed that SW frequency, amplitude and DC potential significantly affected the admittance response of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. Accordingly, the optimization of these effective factors can be associated with succeeding a high response with high reproducibility of the fabricated sensor [60–61].

Herein, together with Au-IDE/LFA biosensor system, admittance signals of Au-IDE surface can be electrochemically amplified by adsorbing the charged positive or negative counter ions derived from reservoirs including AuNPs conjugated with anti-β-HCG antibodies, and antigen-antibody complex in thin-layer and PBS as bulk solution [62, 63]. As expected, the admittance response of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor was not significantly affected by the 50 mM PBS solution on standard LFAs, while the SW frequency at 88 Hz following by increasing the positive DC potential from + 10 mV to + 40 mV was amplified the admittance response up to 30.52 µA/V (Supplementary Fig. S4). Due to having similar ion concentrations and osmolality to a human body, PBS solution is widely utilized as the accessible buffer over an extensive range of pH (from 2 to 12) in biological research to electrochemical response of fabricated devices [62, 64]. Earlier findings on monitoring the specific adsorption of phosphate anions of PBS at the electrochemical interface to Au electrode have been distinguished that the dominant phosphate anions adsorb onto Au electrode surface undergoes pH-dependent manner [62]. Since the PBS buffer at pH 7.4 was applied as blank on bare LFA to assay the baseline of electrochemical signals, HPO42− as a dominant phosphate anion can be adsorbed by Au surface of electrodes on Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. Hence, it advocates that the amplified admittance signal by 50 mM PBS at TL zone under SW frequency of 88 Hz, amplitude at 10 mV and + 200 mV DC potential could be facilitating the adsorption of predominantly negative phosphate ions by the Au electrode surface in TL zone (Supplementary Fig. S3). Consequently, it can be observed that admittance signals become smaller at higher values of frequency and amplitude and negative DC potential due to the insufficient time for the electrochemical process or electrostatic repulsion between the anionic dominant of phosphate ions in PBS and the negatively charged Au electrode surface [62, 65].

Correspondingly, based on the result of the optimization assay (Fig. 6 from a to k), the maximum admittance response (349 µA/V) was amplified by utilizing lower SW frequencies at 88 Hz, amplitude at 10 mV, and DC potential at + 10 mV, when hCG 500 mIU/mL migrated on the standard LFA. Equally, the admittance declined abruptly when the applied amplitude and DC potential were increased continuously (Fig. 6). Indeed, the anti-β-HCG-AuNPs/ hCG antigen complexes onto CP, and TL zones of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor are organized under optimal conditions by migrating hCG 500 mIU/mL for 160 s in the flow direction.

Therefore, under this status of nf-CFFT-admittance detection, the charge densities of the released counterions (positive or negative type) and their accessibility due to immunocomplex formation, the capacity of immunocomplex formation at target zones, their specific adsorption by the Au electrode surface through electrostatic interactions, and the status of charged surface will play a main role in determining the response of induced admittance, which is affected by the applied factors of CFFT-admittance voltammetry (SW frequencies, amplitude, and DC potential) [65, 66]. All-encompassing, the high amplified admittance signals at positive DC potentials is attributed to the provision of optimal conditions for the accessibility and convenient facilitation of released negative ions due to the immunocomplex and the specific adsorption of target ions with high numbers by Au electrode [62–64]. This is a sign of the comprehensive covered active target surfaces area of the Au-IDE electrode (TL and CL zones) for the detectable signals of admittance. In contrast, the decline or fading in the admittance signals at negative DC potentials could be associated with lower negative ions adsorbed by Au surfaces resulting the weak induced the detectable signal in TL or CL zones. Selection the best working frequency from impedance spectrum rely on data obtained by CFFT-admittance voltammetry

In the current study, identifying the optimal working frequency for analyte detection based on impedance signals requires precise tuning across the entire impedance spectrum. Under this status, the best or optimal working frequency of the impedance signals employed to the analyte detection must be tuned to precisely identify the frequencies across the width of the impedance spectrum. However, attaining the precisely identify the best working frequency demand an experienced operator and do not permit an automatic tuning of the efficient working frequency during operation [66]. To overcome this barrier, selection of the true frequency relied on data obtained by the electrochemical measurements’ method is a reasonable option for the present study, where the chosen method provides appropriate and reliable overlap. Corresponding earlier investigations, such a principle of the frequency’s selection to discovery the best working frequency in transversal bulk resonator acoustophoretic [67], minimizing the impact of individual differences in the electrical impedance spectra of fruits [68], and evaluating rice seed vigor using selected frequencies of electrical impedance spectroscopy [69] have been performed previously. The challenge in this paper depends on the determining the optimal frequency of CFFT-admittance voltammetry for analyte detection symbolizes the feasibility of detecting a candidate of the effective frequency in the impedance spectra. The obtained data supported that the selection of frequency at 88 Hz based on CFFT-admittance voltammetry, as optimal frequency, was also able to provide adequate results as a best working frequency for analyte detection by FRA method. Thus, the frequency at 88 Hz was applied to FRA analysis of the impedance measurements of hCG hormone in Au-IDE/LFA biosensor.

Analytical performance of the developed Au-IDE/LFA sensor

Sensitivity and calibration curve

After ignoring the contributions of admittance and impedance associated with the anti-β-HCG-AuNPs antibodies and 50 mM PBS buffer, to explain how the impedance spectroscopy and admittance voltammetry are affected by hCG concentrations, a wide range of the diluted hCG concentrations in the 50 mM PBS between 5-950 mIU/mL was migrated onto the standard LFA in the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. Under optimized conditions, the concentrations of hCG were quantified by decreased average of the net impedance (∆Z’’ net) and the increase in the net admittance responses (∆Y’’ net) at TL zone, ∆Z’’ or ∆Y’’ (hCG on standard LFA at TL zone) - ∆Z’’ or ∆Y’’ (baseline at TL zone) of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor, which were measured by FRA (Z” at frequency of 88 Hz) and CFFT-admittance voltammetry (Y” at frequency of 88 Hz, amplitude at + 10 mV, and DC potential at + 10 mV), respectively (Fig. 7). Briefly, the aggregated impedance signals diminished promptly while the admittance signals enhanced abruptly with increasing the concentration of hCG from 5 to 500 mIU/mL, when the impedance and admittance signals of TL zone were not subtracted from its prior zone, Au-IDE/CP zone (Fig. 7a and b). In contrast, the dependence examination of the ∆Z’’ net and ∆Y’’ net on the fixed concentration of target hCG indicated that both ∆Z’’ net and ∆Y’’ net amplified strongly with increasing the concentration of hCG from 5 to 100 mIU/mL and from 5 to 500 mIU/mL, respectively, and the attained a constant trend for both of them (Fig. 7 from c to f).

Previous challenges and opportunities in experimental investigations, based on utilizing a set of engineered molecules to discover the complexity of electrostatic interactions of particle adsorption during immobilization process on the surface, have widely performed to design the electrokinetic sensor to further enhance the detection sensitivity [70–71]. Indeed, the predictable sensing strategy in Au-IDE/LFA biosensor is based on the electrostatic attraction between the negatively charged hCG of the anti-β-HCG-AuNPs/hCG immunocomplexes and the positively charged Au electrode surface, as presented in Fig. 1. According to the electrostatic influences for signal transduction in Au-IDE/LFA system, the behavior of different charged of biomolecules available on the standard LFA zones functionalized with anti-β-HCG/AuNPs antibodies and hCG antigen must be characterized. The electrostatic interaction is originated typically from the adsorption of the charged ions in the aqueous solution or the charged ions between target surfaces of layered biomolecules, which is subsequently undoubtedly controlled modifying the pH value, isoelectric point (pI), and ionic strength or type of the electrolytes [70–72]. The anti-β-hCG adsorbed onto the AuNPs (zeta potential was measured − 35 mV) through a combination of ionic, hydrophobic and non-covalent interactions to form anti-β-HCG/AuNPs antibodies (zeta potential was measured − 23 mV). Moreover, hCG, a glycoprotein with the alpha and beta subunits, is negatively charged at near neutral pH due to having a low pI (3.1) i.e., a pI lower than the pH of the solution. It binds to negatively charged anti-β-HCG-AuNPs for formatting the immunocomplex. These aspects have already been proven by earlier studies that stated the carry net charge of biomolecules including protein and antibodies determined by environmental pH value or solution pH value associated with their pI [72, 73]. Consequently, to detect hCG which has a low pI, the electrostatic interaction between the anti-β-HCG/AuNPs/hCG immunocomplex in the Au-IDE/LFA system is a specific detection based on amplifying positively charged on the Au surface at a positive DC potential.

Hence, electrostatic interaction between a negatively charged immunocomplex and a positively charged controllable Au electrode surface offers an ideal basis for quantitative detection of hCG with high sensitivity. However, the results showed clearly that the impedance and admittance signals logarithmically amplified when the concentration of the hCG increased up to 100 and 500 mIU/mL, respectively (Fig. 7c and e). This signifying that the electrostatic attractive predominantly occurs between the negatively charged immunocomplex and positively charged Au surface and thus the induced signals from the immunocomplex formation are uniquely or directly measured by FRA and CFFT-admittance voltammetry. Likewise, the occurrence of electrostatic repulsion between the negatively charged immunocomplex and negatively charged Au surface at negative DC potential confirms this aspect, which as optimization assay showed that admittance signal wasn’t amplified at negative DC potentials from − 10 to -200 mV (Fig. 6). Since the charge numbers depends on pH and ion strength determine the strength of electrostatic interaction of intermolecular [72, 74], there is a possibility that the growth in the number of charges due to the increase in hCG concentration leads to a strengthening of the electrostatic adsorption towards the Au electrode surface and subsequently persuade significant admittance and impedance signals onto Au surface. Beside this, it was distinguished that admittance signals at high concentrations of hCG antigen (more than 750 mIU/mL) maintained a constant trend as the hCG increased (Fig. 7e). Such as this response of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor can be attributed to the weakening of the force of electrostatic attractive between the layer of immunocomplex and the Au surface for various reasons, including: (1) It might be caused by saturation of the specific binding sites for molecular recognition between anti-β-HCG-AuNPs and hCG for formatting the immunocomplex for further signal amplification onto the Au electrode surface (Fig. 2b). The saturation of binding site of the antibodies for antigen recognition and showing an constant trend in signal amplification under high concentration of analyte has previously been claimed by developed electrochemical biosensors for biomolecule detection in biological researches [34, 75] (2) The emergence of spatial barrier due to the accumulation of antibodies and antigen immune-complexes on the Au electrode surface (Fig. 2b) (3) The formation of a physical barrier due to fast electrostatic adsorption of the negatively charged of AuNPs conjugated with anti-β-HCG onto the Au electrode surface [76]. The occurrence of a network of anti-β-HCG-AuNPs-spotted on the Au electrode surface discontinuously inhibited negative charge transfer of immunocomplex toward Au electrode surface resulting to electrostatic attractive for further signal amplification (4) When anti-β-HCG-AuNPs/hCG forms on the Au electrode surface due to the accumulation of a high concentration of hCG antigen, there is a likelihood that the high charge number, the distribution of negatively charged residues on the protein surface, ionic strength, and counter ion layer shaped near the surface will have undesirable effect the electrostatic interaction between the immunocomplex and the electrode surface. Therefore, under this condition, not only is hCG adsorption behavior and electrostatic attractive confined on charged Au surface, but also the hCG binding with specific site of the anti-β-HCG-AuNPs restricted as charge number and ion strength increased. Consistent with these results, the critical effect of the distribution of charged residues on the protein surface and high ion strength on the electrostatically protein adsorption behavior on positively charged target surface have previously been proved by several studies [72, 77, 78]. The earlier study on the investigation the binding nanoparticle with target antibody have been proved that negatively charged of nanoparticle (with zeta potential of around − 35 mV) can be quickly bounded with positively charged antibody [76]. As mentioned above, the electrostatic force between negative charges of anti-β-HCG-AuNPs/hCG and positive charge electrode surface at DC potential of + 10 mV increases. As a result, β-HCG-AuNPs/hCG immunocomplex with large size and strong negative charge are electrostatically adsorbed onto the positive charged surface, followed by the formation of a physical barrier caused by the immunocomplex on the electrode surface, preventing further signal amplification. Therefore, it can be inferred that the charge competition between different adsorbates depend on the charge type of DC potential and the magnitude of DC charge on Au electrode surface govern the dominant net charge of adsorbing on Au electrode surface. With considering these aspects, the non-significant admittance signal amplification on Au surface with negative DC potential for the assay optimization of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor can be associated with the occurrence of electrostatic repulsion between negatively charged immunocomplex and negatively charged electrode surface. In contrast, the significant decrease of admittance signal amplification on Au surface with positive DC potentials higher than + 10 mV can be related to the further accumulation of anti-βhCG-AuNPs/hCG on Au electrode surface, as shown in Fig. 6.

Currently, fundamental strategies depend on the pI of the analyte, electrostatic interaction, and the charge modulation of surface have been applied to enhance the detection sensitivity of a developed biosensor [70]. Results of the calibration curve for the obtained response of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor based on differentiated net impedance and net admittance vs hCG concentrations (Fig. 7d and f) show that the impedance calibration curve of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor revealed a linear range of response toward hCG concentration (5 to 100 mIU/mL; Z’’ = 68.647hCG + 827.59 with R2= 0.993). Besides, a linear range of sensor response toward hCG concentration (5 to 500 mIU/mL; Y’’ = 0.6732 hCG + 11.006 with R2= 0.998) for admittance signal. From the admittance calibration plot, the derived limit of quantitation (LOQ) (at 10 Sb/m; Sb is the standard deviation of the 50 mM PBS and m is the slope of the linear regression) and limit of detection (LOD) (at 3 Sb/m) [79] of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor were achieved to be 8.46 and 2.5 mIU/mL, respectively (blank variance with ten replicates was averaged by 18.17 and Sb was calculated by 0.57). In contrast, the calibration plot of impedance signal indicated a LOQ and a LOD by 35.19 and 11.61 mIU/mL, respectively. Despite determining the direct frequency-impedance matching as best working frequency for impedance method rely on the obtained data of admittance method, the sensitivity of impedance method was 5 times less than that of admittance method in detecting the hCG analyte in Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. Since the immunocomplex arrangement on the TL zone of LFA could deeply affect the surface resistance, the probability of sensitive detecting hCG analyte at lower concentrations (less than 5 mIU/mL) from impedimetric point of view declines due to high surface resistance, as confirmed by Ebrahimi et al. [80] for detecting the tumor necrosis factor-alpha via label-free immunosensor using FFT admittance voltammetry. The weakness of direct frequency-impedance and the insufficient sensitivity to detect hCG analyte at lower concentrations demands the use of a CFFT-admittance voltammetry for high-sensitivity detection and the performance assessment of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor. However, the calibration plot of admittance exhibited that the LOD value of 2.5 mIU/mL (using 20 µL) for the hCG detection by Au-IDE/LFA biosensor is lower than LOD value of 2 mIU/mL (using 50 µL) of LFA integrated with the test-zone pre-enrichment method [81] and is approximately three times lower than the LOD value of 6.25 mIU/mL (using 50 µL) for LFA technique using Taguchi approach [82]. This is representing excellent accuracy of developed sensor in quantifying hCG hormone for pregnancy test.

Admittance specificity and selectivity of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor

Verifying the reliable performance of a novel developed biosensor is relied on the proficiency of target sensor to detect specific signal response induced by analyte [82]. Once the sensitivity of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor was distinguished by 2.5 mIU/mL hCG, the specificity and cross-reactivity of biosensor were evaluated by its ability to discern admittance signal induced by similar analytes between the target β-hCG hormone and the non-target α-hCG hormone. In this regard, three levels of β-hCG and α-hCG concentrations (at 100, 250, and 500 mIU/mL) were migrated on the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor and subsequently their CFFT-admittance voltammetry responses were recorded under optimized condition. The result of testing the biosensor’s response to α-hCG analytes indicated that the non-signification admittance signal detected for α-hCG and consistently amplified admittance signal lower than 20 µA/V even with increasing its concentration (Fig. 8a). In contrast, a significant of the amplified admittance signal produced to the β-hCG analyte because the anti-β-HCG-AuNPs/hCG monoclonal antibodies immobilized in the TL zone of the LFA are only competent of specifically detecting β-hCG antigens. This confirming that the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor did not amplified the false positive admittance signal. As well, no cross-reactivity was detected with other non-target hormone, further supporting the biosensor’s high specificity.

To assay the admittance selectivity of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor, an interferes examination was performed by using the potential human hormones including LH, FSH, and TSH glycoproteins, each at tenfold the concentration of the hCG antigen. Y” baseline PBS was conducted for the interference samples to attain the net admittance response. The plot among admittance signal intensity of hCG and the several hormones like hCG was conducted (Fig. 8b). The results clearly demonstrated that admittance signal of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor to detect interfering hormones was < 3% of what was amplified for 500 mIU/mL hCG. The interfering hormones showed a same behavior of admittance signal and their admittance value significantly lower than PBS buffer, revealing that Au-IDE/LFA biosensor does not bind and cross-react with interfering hormones like hCG. The sensed negligible interference amplified by several hormones like, validates that only the strongly bound hCG can generate immuno-complexes with antibodies, producing the electrostatically detectable of admittance signals compared to unmatched interfering hormones. The obtained data strongly support the high specificity and selectivity of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor.

Admittance reproducibility and storage stability of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor

The performance of sensing system can be remarkably affected by stability and reproducibility factors, which that should be investigated [75, 83]. As well, the reproducibility of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor was probed at the presence of different levels of hCG concentrations (25, 50, 250, and 500 mIU/mL) with five replications. The ANOVA analysis (one-way analysis of variance) statistically exhibits that the attained P value for admittance signals less than p < 0.01, revealing that the admittance signals at various concentrations of analyte were significantly generated a reproducible signal according to LSD test (Fig. 8c). Furthermore, the obtained RSD of each group (with five replications) at same concentration is ranged from 0.63 to 2.67%, implying the high reproducibility of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor with minimum variation between admittance signals.

The durability or storage stability is one of the significant advantages of the developed sensor supporting with the sources of environmental sustainability [84]. Moreover, the Au-IDE/LFA system was kept at 20 °C to investigate its stability analysis over a long time for three months. In this regard, the CFFT-admittance response of hCG 500 mIU/mL for the integrated biosensor was recorded once every seven days. The results exhibited that the admittance response persisted constant after four weeks, with minimal non-significant difference between amplified signals, and gradually declined and endured at about 80.98% and 67.34% of the original value after ten and fourteen weeks (Fig. 8d). These results confirm the long-term storage stability of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor to quantitative hCG detection in a reusable manner. This high storage stability could be due to the absence of biomolecular deposition on the Au-IDE electrode surface and the sensitive measurement conditions under nf-CFFT-admittance voltammetry.

Detection of native hCG in urine sample

At this point, the artificial urine samples by diluting the different concentration of the purified hCG (from 25 to 950 mIU/mL) into negative or healthy urine sample were prepared, and subsequently the admittance signals of the undiluted negative sample as control followed by the artificial samples (20 µL) were recorded under optimized conditions via migrating onto bare and standard LFA. Under this status, the Y’’ baseline signal of free urine sample was replaced to the signal Y’’ baseline of PBS. The net admittance signal of samples determined after ignoring the signals associated with control.

The outcome admittance signals of urine samples unveiled a linear range between normalized admittance amplification (µA/V) and hCG concentrations (from 25 to 500 mIU/mL) similar to obtained calibration curve for purified hCG analyte, as observed in the inset of Fig. 8e and f. Comparative analysis spotted that the slope of linear range (0.593) from artificial samples was slightly lower than the slope reached from the purified hCG (0.67) in PBS buffer, when the admittance distribution of the urine background solution was not subtracted. In contrast, when the admittance distribution of urine background solution was subtracted by urine baseline establishment, the obtained slope of the linear range (0.597) confirmed a same admittance response similar to that observed for the non-subtraction condition (Fig. 8f). This absolutely indicates the efficiency of immunocomplex formation and thus, the sensitivity of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor in real sample can be exaggerated by the biological matrix. The exaggerating the electrochemical signal of target sensors for the real sample detection due to biological matrix has previously been confirmed for detecting other analytes by many works [34, 65, 75]. However, high background noise signals, associated with the immobilization of functionalized chemical layers onto the electrode surface and the biological matrix can be significantly declined the developed sensor’s performance for target analyte detection [85]. Indeed, the modulation of the Au surface electrode without immobilizing the functionalized chemical groups under nf-CFFT-admittance voltammetry measurements and the prominent admittance signal amplification of immunocomplex formation via electrostatic attractive are the key features of the proposed Au-IDE/LFA biosensor, which it provides a sensitive and a specific response with multiplexing capabilities. Besides, the elimination of admittance background signal noises caused by free anti-β-HCG-AuNPs, background PBS solution, and the biological matrix of urine is achieved by establishing an admittance signal baseline is a power capability of the integrated Au-IDE/LFA biosensor system for reliable detection of hCG. Hence, it can be inferred that the fabricated biosensor only amplified the admittance signal derived from the diluted hCG antigen in the artificially urine sample. The comparative analysis of slop linear range confirmed that no false positives or false negatives were observed due to the biological matrix of urine sample. Furthermore, when the difference in admittance was calculated for hCG concentrations ranging from 25 to 500 mIU/mL in the artificial sample onto both bare and standard LFA, the results showed admittance range by 11.51, 6.01, and 22.11 µA/V for PBS buffer, free anti-β-HCG-AuNPs, and urine sample, respectively. This clearly indicates that not only the admittance signal of analyte specifically detected by Au-IDE/LFA sensor but also the amplified specific admittance signal was not affected by background solution and urine biological matrix with increasing the concentration level of hCG. The non-significant difference between the linear slopes of the artificial hCG samples with urine baseline subtraction (0.593) and purified hCG samples with PBS baseline subtraction (0.67) validates this claim (Fig. 8f). Besides, the difference of 0.07 in the linear slopes obtained for the artificial sample and the purified sample in PBS could be due to the negative effect of the biological matrix of urine on the efficiency reduction of the immunocomplex at high concentrations and the higher amplified admittance of urine background solution compared PBS buffer, as mentioned in Fig. 8e. The negative effect of the biological matrix was observed in other studies [86–89]. Overall, these results suggest a high potential application of Au-IDE/LFA biosensor to detect of the native hCG in relatively complex biological matrix of urine sample including water (95%), urea (2%), creatinine (0.1%), potassium (0.6%), sodium (0.1%), chloride (0.6%), pigments, hormones, metabolites, and amino acids. To data, the impedance electrode chip [90], electrochemical sensor based on carbon nano-onions/gold nanoparticles/polyethylene nanocomposite [2], electrochemical combo-strip based on progesterone and β-hCG [91], and target-mediated silver deposition-based electrochemical [92] have previously been developed for monitoring pregnancy. Although these sensors have showed a good potential for rapid and label-free detection of hCG, several limitations still restrict their practical application in clinical pregnancy monitoring. Low sensitivity due to small impedance change and strongly influenced by parasitic capacitances in the measurement system, signal draft over time, unstable impedance signal during repeated use due to surface fouling or degradation of the biorecognition, and cross-reactivity with other glycoprotein hormones are main limitations for the impedance electrode chip sensing system [12, 16]. In contrast, electrochemical sensing based on nanomaterials faces several drawbacks, including non-uniform composite formation, limited stability of the biological recognition layer, signal draft due to polymer dielectric behavior, and interference from biological matrices [11, 50, 93]. Many traditional electrochemical (faradic) biosensors are prone to interference from electroactive species present in complex biological samples like serum and urine. Non-faradaic sensors, by focusing on capacitance or admittance changes, are inherently less susceptible to interference from redox-active compounds, leading to cleaner signals and higher specificity in real sample [50]. In this regard, the Au-IDE/LFA sensor as non-faradaic sensor offer a promising platform for hCG detection by providing a sensitive, specific, and rapid detection at low analyte concentration that is less prone to the effect of biological matrix compared to the mentioned sensors based on faradic mode. Likewise, the Au-IDE/LFA system’s capability to establish an individual urine sample baseline for substantial reduction/elimination biological matrix effect and alongside miniaturization potential, making it highly attractive for early pregnancy diagnosis and point-of-care applications.

Conclusion

In the current work, the development of the integrated Au-IDE/LFA biosensor using non-faradaic electrochemical biosensing platform based on the electrostatic charge contrast between antibody-hCG analyte and Au electrode surface has been successfully established for quantitative detection of the hCG hormone. The non-faradaic FRA and CFFT-admittance voltammetry were employed to explore the charge contrast between the antibody-hCG analyte and the charged Au electrode surface with different DC potentials for the amplification signal of admittance responses. The specific signals were achieved by sensor with the largest charge contrast between the negatively charged hCG analyte and the positively charged Au surface at DC potential + 10 mV according to assay optimization. The admittance response of the Au-IDE/LFA biosensor showed a high sensitivity about three times more than impedance response with a LOD of 2.5 mIU/mL in a linear range of 5-500 mIU/mL of hCG. Consequently, admittance response was utilized to determine the performance efficiency of the proposed biosensor. Along with outcome results of biosensor’s performance, it can be concluded that: (1) Modulating the charge contrast between hCG analyte and Au electrode surface rely on pI and pH solution led to electrostatic adsorbing the specific amplification of admittance signals on the Au-IDE electrode. Likewise, this also causes the excess charged groups in the biological matrix of urine sample and PBS buffer to undergo electrostatic repulsion moving away from the electrode surface. These remarkably decreased the signal background noises and highly increased the sensitivity of integrated Au-IDE/LFA biosensor compared to other techniques used to integrate with LFA device (2) The fabricated biosensor exhibited an excellent admittance selectivity without cross-reactivity for β-hCG detection to other human hormones like hCG and also, revealed a high admittance specificity to distinguish the beta unit from the alpha unit of the hCG hormone. (3) This biosensing platform offers implementation of multiple arrays of Au electrodes without the chemical functionalization groups and complex signal molecular labelling which would allow admittance measurement of urine samples with high reproducibility and reusability during storage. (4) The performance of fabricated Au-IDE/LFA biosensor confirmed its high potential for directly detecting the native hCG in urine samples, which quantitatively exhibits the high performance of proposed biosensor as a promising tool for early detection of pregnancy, where the LFA paper test may be ineffective in diagnosing.
