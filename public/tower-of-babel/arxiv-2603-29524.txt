Geometric Inverse Semigroup Theory: a note on the Milnor-Schwarz Lemma for inverse monoids
Giorgio Mangioni, Francesco Tesolin

Abstract
. We generalise the Milnor-Schwarz lemma to inverse monoids acting on presheaves of geodesic metric spaces. We provide two proofs of this fact: one only uses elementary techniques, inspired by the arguments for group actions on metric spaces; the other involves a version of the Vietoris-Rips complex, and builds on work of Chung-Martínez-Szakács.

Introduction

Where groups can be thought of as sets of symmetries, an inverse semigroup
S
is a set of partial symmetries, that is, each element has a “local”
inverse and their composition produces an
idempotent
element (that is, one such that
ee=e
). The set of all idempotents
E(S)
forms a meet-semilattice, with the meet operation corresponding to multiplication.
The fundamental example of an inverse semigroup is the collection of partial bijections on a set, with each idempotent being the identity on some subset; see
1.5
for details.

There has been a recent growing interest in geometric inverse semigroup theory
[
4
,
7
,
3
,
1
]
, where the authors study inverse semigroups as extended metric spaces. For example, given a semigroup
S
, together with a
quasi-generating set

\mathcal{M}\subseteq S
(that is, a subset which generates
S
together with
E(S)
), one can equip
S
with the
Cayley metric

d_{\mathcal{M}}
, which is the direct analogue of the word metric for groups. However, up to this point, there has been
no “proper” analogue of the renowned
Schwarz-Milnor lemma
from geometric group theory, which allows one to study the geometry of a group via the geometry of a metric space on which the group acts “nicely”
[
9
,
8
]
.

To fill this gap, we first need the correct notion of action of an inverse semigroup, which is that of an
étale action
introduced in
[
10
]
and studied further in
[
12
]
. Namely, given a semigroup
S
, with idempotents
E(S)
, we consider an action of
S
on a
presheaf
over
E(S)
: see
1.9
for further details. This approach of acting on a presheaf is an effective tool to generalise ideas from group theory, as it recovers the usual definition of group action when
S
is a group (seen as an inverse monoid with a unique idempotent).

In order to do geometric inverse semigroup theory, we then restrict to actions by
1
-Lipschitz maps on presheaves of geodesic metric spaces (see
1.10
). In this framework there are natural analogues of the notions of properness and coboundedness, which we defer to
2.1
below. We now state our main theorem, which recovers the usual Schwarz-Milnor lemma for groups:

Theorem A
.

Let
S
be an inverse monoid, with identity element
1
, and
(X,E(S),p)
be a presheaf of geodesic metric spaces.
If
S
acts on
X
properly and coboundedly then:

(1)

There exists a finite, quasi-generating set
\mathcal{M}\subseteq S
.

(2)

There exists
x_{1}\in X_{1}
such that, if
d_{\mathcal{M}}
is the Cayley metric associated to
\mathcal{M}
, the orbit map
(S,d_{\mathcal{M}})\to X
sending
s\in S
to
x_{1}\cdot s
is an order-preserving quasi-isometry.

Note that, in general, one cannot hope to get a genuinely finite generating set. This is because any meet-semilattice
E
with a top element is itself an inverse monoid, and if
E
is uncountable then it cannot be finitely generated (see
2.6
).

Outline of the paper

In
Section

1
we give the background on inverse semigroups and how they act on presheaves of metric spaces. In
1.14

we reinterpret the Cayley graph of an inverse semigroup as a presheaf of geodesic metric spaces (the Schützenberger graphs).

In the two following sections, we give two proofs of
A
. The first is
an elementary argument adapting the proof of the usual Schwarz-Milnor lemma for groups.
The second uses a theorem of Chung-Martínez-Szakács
[
1
]
, which roughly states that any two “Cayley-like” metrics on an inverse semigroup are coarsely equivalent. In order to produce such a metric, we introduce an analogue of the
Vietoris-Rips complex
for group actions, which we believe to be of independent interest.

Further Questions

This work provides further evidence that presheaves (of metric spaces) are the natural objects on which inverse semigroups act.
Using our generalisation of the Schwarz-Milnor lemma, we hope that more examples of quasi-finitely generated
inverse monoids with interesting geometric properties may be produced. In this direction,
[
3
]
considered the properties of finitely generated inverse monoids with hyperbolic Schützenberger graphs, so we ask the following vague question:

Question B
.

What are the properties of a (finitely generated?) inverse monoid that acts properly and coboundedly on a presheaf of CAT(0) (resp.
systolic, injective, median) spaces?

The main results of
[
3
]
show that, while finitely presented inverse monoids whose Schützenberger graphs are quasitrees enjoy several nice algorithmic and language-theoretic properties, just requiring hyperbolicity of the Schützenberger graphs is not even enough to get a decidable word problem. However, besides the metric spaces appearing as fibers, we expect that the presheaf structure itself might provide another element of flexibility in studying the geometry of inverse semigroups. In other words, it might be possible to infer better properties of an inverse monoid by looking at actions where not only the fibers are restricted to specific geometries (for example hyperbolicity), but also the restriction maps of the presheaf satisfy suitable properties.

1.
Background on inverse semigroups

Definition 1.1

(Inverse semigroup, domain and range)
.

An
inverse semigroup
is a semigroup
S
such that for all
s\in S
there exists a unique
s^{-1}\in S
such that
ss^{-1}s=s
and
s^{-1}ss^{-1}=s^{-1}
. An
inverse monoid
is an inverse semigroup
S
with an element
1\in S
such that
1s=s1=s
for all
s\in S
. For each element
s\in S
we call the element
s^{-1}s
the
domain
of
s
and denote it by
\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
, and call
ss^{-1}
the
range
of
s
and denote it by
\mathbf{ran}\left(s\right)
.

Definition 1.2

(Idempotents)
.

An
idempotent
of an inverse semigroup
S
is an element
e\in S
such that
ee=e
. The set of all idempotents in
S
is denoted by
E(S)
. Notice that, for every
s\in S
, both
\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
and
\mathbf{ran}\left(s\right)
are idempotents by construction. Furthermore, an immediate corollary of the definition, as shown in
[
5
]
, is that any two idempotents commute.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that a group is therefore an inverse semigroup with a unique idempotent element.

Definition 1.4

(Partial order)
.

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup. Set
s\leq t
if
and only if
s=et
for some idempotent
e\in E(S)
. As
shown in
[
5
]
, this is a partial order, termed the
natural partial order
of
S
.
With respect to the natural partial order, the idempotents
E(S)

form a meet-semilattice, with meet operation
e\wedge f=ef
.

Example 1.5

(Partial bijections)
.

Let
X
be a set, and let
S
be the collection of all partial bijections, that is, bijections between subsets of
X
.
S
is an inverse monoid with the following multiplication: given
A,B,C,D
subsets of
X
and partial bijections
f\colon A\to B
and
g\colon C\to D
, the product
gf
is the map
(g\circ f)|_{f^{-1}(B\cap C)}\colon f^{-1}(B\cap C)\to g(B\cap C)
. Furthermore, given
f\colon A\to B
, the domain and range of
f
are the identity maps on
A
and
B
, respectively. If
e\in S
is an idempotent, then
e
is the identity on some subset
A\subseteq X
; hence, given
f,g\in S
,
f\leq g
if and only if
f
is a restriction of
g
.

Remark 1.6
.

Given
s,t\in S
, we have that
\mathbf{dom}\left(st\right)\leq\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)
, since

\mathbf{dom}\left(st\right)\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)=t^{-1}s^{-1}stt^{-1}t=t^{-1}s^{-1}st=\mathbf{dom}\left(st\right).

Definition 1.7

(Congruence and Green’s relations)
.

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup. A
congruence
on
S
is an equivalence relation

\sim
such that for all
s,t,a,b\in S
with
s\sim t
,
a\sim b
then
sa\sim tb
.
Two important congruences are

\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
, given by

(s,t)\in\mathcal{L}\iff\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)\mbox{ and }(s,t)\in\mathcal{R}\iff\mathbf{ran}\left(s\right)=\mathbf{ran}\left(t\right).

It is shown in
[
5
]
that

(a,b)\in\mathcal{L}\text{ if and only if }Sa=Sb.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
s,t,x\in S
. If
(s,t)\in\mathcal{L}
and
s=xt
then
t=x^{-1}s
. Furthermore, if
x\in E(S)
then
t=s
.

Proof.

Since
(s,t)\in\mathcal{L}
we have that

\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)=t^{-1}t=t^{-1}x^{-1}xt=\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right).

If we multiply by
t
on the left we get

t=tt^{-1}x^{-1}xt=x^{-1}xtt^{-1}t=x^{-1}xt=x^{-1}s,

where we used that the idempotents
tt^{-1}
and
x^{-1}x
commute.

For the “furthermore” part, if
x\in E(S)
then
x^{-1}=x
, so

t=x^{-1}s=x^{-1}xt=xt=s,

as required.
∎

We now recall the definition of a presheaf over a meet-semilattice, dating back to work of

[
2
]
. It was first described in
[
10
]
that inverse semigroups act naturally on such
objects; see also
[
12
]
for further details.

Definition 1.9

(Presheaf of metric spaces)
.

Let
E
be a meet-semilattice, and denote the meet of two elements
e,f\in E
by
ef
.
A
presheaf
over
E
is a set
X
equipped with functions
p\colon X\to E
and
(\cdot)\colon X\times E\to X
satisfying

(1)

(x\cdot e)\cdot f=x\cdot ef
;

(2)

x\cdot p(x)=x
;

(3)

p(x\cdot e)=p(x)e
.

Denote such a presheaf by the triple
(X,E,p)
. We call a presheaf
global
when the function
p
is surjective.

We denote the
fiber

p^{-1}(e)
by
X_{e}
. For every
e\geq f
, the map
X_{e}\to X_{f}
sending
x
to
x\cdot f
is called the
restriction
from
X_{e}
to
X_{f}
. For
x,y\in X
set
x\leq y
if
x
is the restriction of
y
, equivalently
x=y\cdot p(x)
. This defines a partial order on
X
.

A
presheaf of metric spaces
is a global presheaf where each fiber
X_{e}
is a metric space with metric

d_{e}\colon X^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and such that, for all
x,y\in X
and
e,f\in E
,

d_{e}(x,y)\geq d_{ef}(x\cdot f,y\cdot f).

For the ease of notation, we set
d(x,y)=d_{e}(x,y)
if
x,y\in X_{e}
, and
d(x,y)=\infty
otherwise. This makes
(X,d)
an
extended metric space
, i.e. a metric space where distances are allowed to be infinite.

A presheaf of metric spaces
(X,d)
, and more generally an extended metric space, is
geodesic
if, for every
x,y\in X
such that
D=d(x,y)<\infty
, there exists an isometric embedding of an interval
\gamma\colon[0,D]\to X
such that
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(D)=y
.

Definition 1.10

(Acting on a presheaf)
.

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup and
(X,E(S),p)
be a presheaf of metric spaces over the idempotents of
S
. We say that
S

acts on

(X,E(S),p)
if there exists an action
(\cdot)\colon X\times S\to X
which restricts to the action
X\times E(S)\to X
and satisfies the following for all
x,y\in X
,
e\in E(S)
and
s\in S
:

•

The action preserves fibers:
p(x\cdot s)=s^{-1}p(x)s
;

•

The action is by
1
-Lipschitz maps: if
x,y\in X_{e}
,
d_{e}(x,y)\geq d_{s^{-1}es}(x\cdot s,y\cdot s)
.

Remark 1.11
.

Notice that, by definition, every idempotent acts as the restriction to the corresponding fiber.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
S
act on
(X,E(S),p)
a presheaf of metric spaces. Each
s\in S
induces an isometry

\theta_{s}\colon X\cdot ss^{-1}\to X\cdot s^{-1}s
by acting on the right by
s
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0},y_{0}\in X
, and let
x=x_{0}\cdot ss^{-1}
and
y=y_{0}\cdot ss^{-1}
. Notice that
p(x)=p(y)
if and only if

p(x\cdot s)=p(y\cdot s)
, and therefore

d(x,y)=\infty
if and only if
d(x\cdot s,y\cdot s)=\infty
.
Thus suppose that
p(x)=p(y)=e
. Then
e\leq ss^{-1}
by
1.6
and, since the action is by 1-Lipschitz maps,

d_{e}(x,y)\geq d_{s^{-1}es}(x\cdot s,y\cdot s)\geq d_{e}(x\cdot ss^{-1},y\cdot ss^{-1})=d_{e}(x,y).

∎

Natural examples of presheaves on which a semigroup acts arise from its
Schützenberger graphs
, first introduced in
[
11
]
. These are the biconnected components of the Cayley graph, and have a well-defined metric.

Definition 1.13

(Quasi-generating set)
.

A subset
\mathcal{M}\subseteq S
is a
quasi-generating set
if
S=\langle\mathcal{M}\cup E(S)\rangle
. We always assume that a quasi-generating set is
symmetric
, meaning that
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{-1}\coloneq\{x^{-1}\mid x\in\mathcal{M}\}
.

We say that
S
is
quasi-finitely generated
(resp. quasi-countable) if it admits a finite (resp. countable) quasi-generating set. For reference, being quasi-finitely generated is weaker than admitting a
finite labeling
as in
[
6
, Definition 3.16]
.

Definition 1.14

(Cayley graph and Schützemberger graphs)
.

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup with quasi-generating set
\mathcal{G}
. Define

\operatorname{Cay}\left(S,\mathcal{G}\right)
to be the directed graph whose vertices are elements of
S
,
and filling in an oriented edge between
s
and
t
, labelled by some
g\in\mathcal{G}
, if and only if
gs=t
.

Notice that the biconnected components of
\operatorname{Cay}\left(S,\mathcal{G}\right)
, called
Schützenberger graphs
, coincide with
\mathcal{L}
-equivalence classes: this is because there is an oriented path from
s
to
t
if and only if
xs=t
for some
x\in S
. Furthermore, within a Schützenberger graph, edges come in inverse pairs by
1.8
. Hence one defines the
Cayley metric

d_{\mathcal{G}}
on
S
by setting

d_{\mathcal{G}}(s,t)=\infty
if
(s,t)\not\in\mathcal{L}
, and the path distance in their common Schützenberger graph if
(s,t)\in\mathcal{L}
. This makes
(S,d_{\mathcal{G}})
a presheaf of geodesic metric spaces over
E(S)
, with projection
s\to\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
and action
S\times S\to S
given by right multiplication.

The Cayley metric can actually be computed using only elements of a
quasi
-generating set:

Lemma 1.15
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a quasi-generating set for
S
. Then
d_{\mathcal{M}\cup E(S)}=d_{\mathcal{M}}
, where

d_{\mathcal{M}}(s,t)=\begin{cases}\min\{k\mid\exists m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\in\mathcal{M}\mbox{ s.t. }s=m_{1}\ldots m_{k}t\}&\mbox{if }(s,t)\in\mathcal{L};\\
\infty&\mbox{if }(s,t)\not\in\mathcal{L}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
(s,t)\not\in\mathcal{L}
there is nothing to prove, so suppose otherwise. Write
s=m_{1}e_{1}\ldots m_{k}e_{k}t
, where each
m_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
and
e_{i}\in E(S)
. Notice that
m_{1}e_{1}m_{2}e_{2}=m_{1}m_{2}(m_{2}^{-1}e_{1}m_{2})e_{2}
; furthermore,
m_{2}^{-1}e_{1}m_{2}
is an idempotent, and therefore so is the product
m_{2}^{-1}e_{1}m_{2}e_{2}
. If we repeat the process for all
i
, we eventually find an idempotent
f\in E(S)
such that
s=m_{1}\ldots m_{k}ft
.
By
1.6
,
\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)\leq\mathbf{dom}\left(ft\right)\leq\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
, so
(ft,t)\in\mathcal{L}
. In turn, since
f
is an idempotent, the furthermore part of
1.8
yields that
ft=t
, so
s=m_{1}\ldots m_{k}t
, as required.
∎

As in the group case, two finite quasi-generating sets give biLipschitz equivalent Cayley metrics:

Lemma 1.16

(
[
7
, Prop. 5.2.4]
)
.

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup, and let
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
be two finite quasi-generating sets. Then the identity map on
S
induces an order-preserving biLipschitz equivalence
(S,d_{\mathcal{M}})\to(S,d_{\mathcal{N}})
.

2.
Milnor-Schwarz for inverse monoids

In this section we present a self-contained proof of the Milnor-Schwarz lemma, which is
2.4
below. For the next definition, given an extended metric space
(X,d)
, we denote the ball of radius
R
around a point
x\in X
by
B(x,R)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
S
be an inverse monoid, acting on a presheaf of metric spaces
(X,E(S),p)
. The action is:

(1)

proper
if for every
y_{1}\in X_{1}
and every
R\geq 0
, there exists a finite subset
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(y_{1},R)\subseteq S
such that

\{s\in S\mid d(y_{1}\cdot s,y_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))\leq R\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}E(S).

(2)

cobounded
if there exist
x_{1}\in X_{1}
and
T\geq 0
such that
B(x_{1},T)\cdot S=X
. When we want to stress the constant
T
, we say the action is
T
-cobounded.

Remark 2.2
.

In the definition of properness, we could have equivalently required the existence of
some
y_{1}
such that, for every
R\geq 0
, the set
\{s\in S\mid d(y_{1}\cdot s,y_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))\leq R\}
is covered by finitely many
E(S)
-cosets. Indeed, let
z_{1}\in X_{1}
be any other point, and let
D=d(y,z)
. If
d(z_{1}\cdot s,z_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))\leq R
for some
R\geq 0
, then

d(y_{1}\cdot s,y_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))\\
\leq d(y_{1}\cdot s,z_{1}\cdot s)+d(z_{1}\cdot s,z_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))+d(z_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right),y_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))\\
\leq R+2D,

where we used that the action is by
1
-Lipschitz maps. This shows that

\{s\in S\mid d(z_{1}\cdot s,z_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))\leq R\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(y_{1},R+2D)E(S).

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a finite quasi-generating set for
S
, then the
S
-action on
(S,d_{\mathcal{M}})
is proper and cobounded.

Proof.

Let
x_{1}=1
be the identity element. Since
S=1\cdot S
, we immediately see that the action is cobounded. Moving to properness, by
2.2
we can restrict to
y_{1}=1
. Moreover, if
d_{\mathcal{M}}(s,\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))\leq R
, then
s=m_{1}\ldots m_{k}\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
for some
m_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
and
k\leq R
(here we are using that the Cayley distance can be computed only using non-idempotent elements, in view of
1.15
). This shows that
s\in\mathcal{M}^{\leq\lceil R\rceil}E(S)
, where
\lceil R\rceil
is the smallest integer greater than
R
and

\mathcal{M}^{\leq\lceil R\rceil}=\{m_{1}\ldots m_{k}\mid k\leq\lceil R\rceil,\,m_{i}\in\mathcal{M}\mbox{ for all }i=1,\ldots,k\}.

Hence we can set
\mathcal{C}(1,R)=\mathcal{M}^{\leq\lceil R\rceil}
.
∎

We are finally ready to state the main theorem of this paper:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
S
be an inverse monoid and
(X,E(S),p)
be a presheaf of geodesic metric spaces.
If
S
acts on
X
properly and coboundedly then:

(1)

There exists a finite, quasi-generating set
\mathcal{M}\subseteq S
.

(2)

There exists
x_{1}\in X_{1}
such that the orbit map
(S,d_{\mathcal{M}})\to X
sending
s\in S
to
x_{1}\cdot s
is an order-preserving quasi-isometry.

Proof.

By coboundedness there exists an

x_{1}\in X_{1}
and
T\geq 0
such that
B(x_{1},T)\cdot S=X
. Let

G\coloneq\{s\in S\mid d(x_{1}\cdot s,x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))\leq 2T+1\},

we work to show that
G
is a generating set of
S
. For every
s\in S
,
X_{\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)}
is a geodesic metric space, so there exists a geodesic

\gamma
from
x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
to
x_{1}\cdot s
in
X_{\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)}
.
Take points along the geodesic at most a distance
1
apart: concretely,
let

x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)=p_{1},\,p_{2},\,\cdots,\,p_{k}=x_{1}\cdot s

be a series of points
such that
d(p_{i},p_{i+1})\leq 1
and
k\leq d(x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right),x_{1}\cdot s)+2
.
Since each
p_{i}
is in
B(x_{1},T)\cdot S
there exists elements
s_{i}
in
S

such that
d(p_{i},x_{1}\cdot s_{i})\leq T
; for
i=k
we can choose
s_{k}=s
. This implies that
x_{1}\cdot s_{i}\in X_{\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)}
and hence

\mathbf{dom}\left(s_{i}\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
for all
i
.
Define
u_{1}=s_{1}
and
u_{i}=s_{i}s_{i-1}^{-1}
for
2\leq i\leq k
; notice that
u_{k}=ss_{k-1}^{-1}
. Then,

s=s\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)^{k-1}=s\mathbf{dom}\left(s_{k-1}\right)\ldots\mathbf{dom}\left(s_{1}\right)=(ss_{k-1}^{-1})(s_{k-1}s_{k-2}^{-1})\ldots(s_{2}s_{1}^{-1})s_{1}=u_{k}\ldots u_{1}.

Now, by assumption

d(x_{1}\cdot s_{1},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s_{1}\right))\leq T
, so
s_{1}\in G
. Moreover, for all
2\leq i\leq k
,

d(x_{1}\cdot u_{i},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(u_{i}\right))=d(x_{1}\cdot s_{i}s_{i-1}^{-1},x_{1}\cdot s_{i-1}s_{i}^{-1}s_{i}s_{i-1}^{-1})

=d(x_{1}\cdot s_{i}s_{i-1}^{-1},x_{1}\cdot s_{i-1}s_{i-1}^{-1})\leq d(x_{1}\cdot s_{i},x_{1}\cdot s_{i-1})\leq 2T+1,

where we used that
s_{i}^{-1}s_{i}=\mathbf{dom}\left(s_{i}\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(s_{i-1}\right)=s_{i-1}^{-1}s_{i-1}
.
Hence, all
u_{i}
are in
G
, so
G
is a generating set.
By properness
G\subseteq\mathcal{C}E(S)
where
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(x_{1},2T+1)
, so

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{C}
is a finite quasi-generating set of
S
.

We now work to prove (2). The orbit map
S\to x_{1}\cdot S
is
T
-coarsely surjective by coboundedness, so we focus on showing it is a quasi-isometric embedding. Note that

d_{\mathcal{M}}(s,t)<\infty\iff\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)\iff d(x_{1}\cdot s,x_{1}\cdot t)<\infty.

Furthermore, if
d_{\mathcal{M}}(s,t)<\infty
then
\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)
, so

d(x_{1}\cdot s,x_{1}\cdot t)=d(x_{1}\cdot s\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right),x_{1}\cdot t\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))=d(x_{1}\cdot st^{-1},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(st^{-1}\right)),

where we used
1.12
for the last equality. The same argument applies to the word metric
d_{\mathcal{M}}
; hence it is enough to show that, for every
s\in S
,
d(x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right),x_{1}\cdot s)
is linearly bounded above and below in terms of
d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right),s)
.

Write

s=u_{k}\cdots u_{1}
as before. For each
i
denote
v_{i}=u_{i}\cdots u_{1}\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
, with
v_{0}=\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
. By
1.6

\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(v_{k}\right)\leq\mathbf{dom}\left(v_{k-1}\right)\leq\mathbf{dom}\left(v_{k-2}\right)\cdots\leq\mathbf{dom}\left(v_{0}\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right).

Hence,
\mathbf{dom}\left(v_{i}\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
for all
i
, so each
u_{i}
labels a (possibly loop) edge in the Schützenberger graph of
s
connecting
v_{i-1}
to
v_{i}
. Therefore, by construction of the
u_{i}
,

d_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right),s)\leq k\leq d(x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right),x_{1}\cdot s)+2.

For the other inequality, let
n=d_{\mathcal{M}}(s,\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))
, and write
s=t_{n}\ldots t_{1}\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
, where each
t_{i}\in\mathcal{M}E(S)
. Set
v_{i}=t_{i}\ldots t_{1}\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
, with
v_{0}=\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
. Note that, for all
i
,

\mathbf{dom}\left(v_{i}\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(v_{i+1}\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i+1}v_{i}\right)=v_{i}^{-1}\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i+1}\right)v_{i};

hence

\mathbf{ran}\left(v_{i}\right)=v_{i}\mathbf{dom}\left(v_{i}\right)v_{i}^{-1}=\mathbf{ran}\left(v_{i}\right)\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i+1}\right)\mathbf{ran}\left(v_{i}\right)=\mathbf{ran}\left(v_{i}\right)\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i+1}\right)\leq\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i+1}\right),

where we used that idempotents commute. Therefore, combining
1.12
and the fact that the action is by
1
-Lipschitz maps, we get

d(x_{1}\cdot v_{i+1},x_{1}\cdot v_{i})=d(x_{1}\cdot t_{i+1}v_{i},x_{1}\cdot v_{i})=d(x_{1}\cdot t_{i+1}\mathbf{ran}\left(v_{i}\right),x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{ran}\left(v_{i}\right))\leq d(x_{1}\cdot t_{i+1},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i+1}\right)).

Hence

d(x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right),x_{1}\cdot s)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}d(x_{1}\cdot v_{i-1},x_{1}\cdot v_{i})\leq n\max_{i=1,\ldots,n}\{d(x_{1}\cdot t_{i},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i}\right))\}.

Thus the proof is complete if we bound
d(x_{1}\cdot t_{i},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i}\right))
uniformly. Let
t_{i}=m_{i}e_{i}
, where
m_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
and
e_{i}\in E(S)
. Then
\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i}\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(m_{i}\right)e_{i}
, so

d(x_{1}\cdot m_{i},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(m_{i}\right))\geq d(x_{1}\cdot t_{i},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(m_{i}\right)e_{i})=d(x_{1}\cdot t_{i},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i}\right)).

Notice that the left-hand-side of the inequality is uniformly bounded since
\mathcal{M}
is finite, and we are done.
∎

Remark 2.5

(Non-proper actions)
.

Without the properness assumption, the above proof still produces a generating set
G
such that the orbit map
(S,d_{G})\to X
is a quasi-isometry (the only difference is that, for the converse inequality, one writes
s=t_{n}\ldots t_{1}
where each
t_{i}
belongs to
G
instead of
\mathcal{M}E(S)
, and then uses that
d(x_{1}\cdot t_{i+1},x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(t_{i+1}\right))\leq 2T+1
by assumption).

Remark 2.6
.

Notice that, in general, one cannot hope to get finite generation from an action on a presheaf, without further assumptions on the structure of the poset
E(S)
. Indeed, let
E
be any uncountable meet-semilattice with a top element
1_{e}
, and let
G
be any finitely generated group. Then
S=E\times G
is an inverse monoid, with multiplication
(e,g)\cdot(f,h)=(ef,gh)
and
E(S)=E
. Hence
S
is quasi-finitely generated, but cannot be finitely generated as it is uncountable. Since
G
can be any finitely generated group, and since the restriction maps
\{e\}\times G\to\{ef\}\times G
are isometries, this example also shows that no geometric properties of the fibers, nor of the restriction maps of a presheaf, is enough to ensure finite generation.

3.
Comparison to work of Chung-Martínez-Szakács

We present here an alternative proof of
2.4
, building on work of Chung-Martínez-Szakács
[
1
]
and a construction analogue to the Vietoris-Rips complex for groups acting on metric spaces.

Definition 3.1

(Uniform discreteness)
.

An extended metric space
(S,d)
is
uniformly discrete
if
\inf_{s\neq t}d(s,t)>0
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup, and let
d
be an extended metric on
S
whose components are the
\mathcal{L}
-classes. We say
d
is:

•

right-subinvariant
if
d(sx,tx)\leq d(s,t)
for every
s,t,x\in S
;

•

proper
if for every
r\geq 0
there is some finite set
F_{r}\subseteq S
such that, for every
x,y\in S
with
x\neq y
and
d(x,y)\leq r
, there exists
f\in F_{r}
such that
y=fx
.

•

Uniformly proper
if there exists some finite set
F_{1}\subseteq S
such that, for every
r\geq 0
and every
x,y\in S
with
x\neq y
and
d(x,y)\leq r
, there exists
f\in(F_{1})^{\leq\lceil r\rceil}
such that
y=fx
.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
1
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

S
is quasi-countable;

(2)

S
admits a proper and right-subinvariant uniformly discrete metric whose components are the
\mathcal{L}
-classes.

Moreover, such a metric is unique up to bijective coarse equivalence.

In the quasi-finitely generated case, one can promote properness to uniform properness:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
S
be an inverse semigroup. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

S
is quasi-finitely generated;

(2)

S
admits a uniformly proper and right-subinvariant uniformly discrete metric whose components are the
\mathcal{L}
-classes.

Proof.

(
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
): If
S
is quasi-finitely generated, then the Cayley metric corresponding to the finite quasi-generating set satisfies the requirements, as pointed out in
[
1
, Example 3.4]
.

(
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
): We argue exactly as in the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 3.23]
. Let
F_{1}
be as in the definition of uniform properness, and let
s\in S-E(S)
. If we set
D\coloneq d(s,\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right))
, which is finite since the components of
d
are the
\mathcal{L}
-classes, then
s\in(F_{1})^{\leq\lceil D\rceil}\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)
, proving that
F_{1}
is a quasi-generating set.
∎

Construction 3.5

(Vietoris-Rips graph)
.

Let
S
be an inverse monoid and
(X,E(S),p)
be a presheaf of geodesic metric spaces, and let
x_{1}\in X_{1}
be a basepoint. Given a constant
R\geq 0
, let
S^{R}
be the simplicial graph whose vertex set is
S
and where two
s,t\in S
are adjacent if and only if
d(x_{1}\cdot s,x_{1}\cdot t)\leq R
. We equip
S
with the path metric
d^{R}
where edges of
S^{R}
have length
1
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
S
be an inverse monoid and
(X,E(S),p)
be a (global) presheaf of geodesic metric spaces, and suppose that
S
acts on
X
properly and coboundedly. For every
R\geq 0
,
d^{R}
is a uniformly discrete, proper, right-subinvariant extended metric whose components coincide with the
\mathcal{L}
-classes.

Proof.

We check one requirement at a time.

•

Components
: Since the metric spaces in
X
are geodesic, two elements
s,t\in S
are within finite distance if and only if
x_{1}\cdot s
and
x_{1}\cdot t
belong to the same fiber
X_{e}
. Since the underlying action on the base
E(S)
is by conjugation, this happens if and only if
\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)
, i.e.
(s,t)\in\mathcal{L}
.

•

Uniform discreteness
: this is simply because
S^{R}
is a graph with edges of length
1
, so any two distinct point lie at distance at least
1
.

•

Uniform properness
: The proof will resemble the arguments from
2.4
. Since
d^{R}
is a path metric, it is enough to prove that there exists a finite subset
F_{1}\subseteq S
such that, if
d^{R}(s,t)\leq 1
, then
s\in F_{1}t
.

We claim that we can actually set
F_{1}=\mathcal{C}(R,x_{1})
, as in the definition of properness. Indeed, by definition
d(x_{1}\cdot s,x_{1}\cdot t)\leq R
, and therefore
d(x_{1}\cdot st^{-1},x_{1}\cdot tt^{-1})\leq R
. Notice that

\mathbf{dom}\left(st^{-1}\right)=ts^{-1}st^{-1}=t\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)t^{-1}=t\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)t^{-1}=tt^{-1},

where we used that
\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)
as their
d^{R}
-distance is finite. Hence
r\coloneq st^{-1}
satisfies
d(x_{1}\cdot r,x_{1}\cdot\mathbf{dom}\left(r\right))\leq R
, so
r\in F_{1}E(S)
. Summing up what we did so far, we got

s=s\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)=s\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)=st^{-1}t=rt=met,

where
m\in F_{1}
and
e\in E(S)
.

Finally, from
s=met
we get that
\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)\leq\mathbf{dom}\left(et\right)\leq\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)
by
1.6
, and since
\mathbf{dom}\left(s\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)
we get that
\mathbf{dom}\left(et\right)=\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)
. In turn

t=t\mathbf{dom}\left(t\right)=t\mathbf{dom}\left(et\right)=tt^{-1}et=et.

This shows that
s=met=mt
, so
s\in F_{1}t
, as required.

•

Right-subinvariance
: Since
S
acts on
X
on the right by
1
-Lipschitz maps, the action preserves pairs of points within distance
R
, and therefore maps edges of
S^{R}
to edges.∎

As a consequence of
3.4
, we immediately get:

Corollary 3.7
.

Any
S
as in
3.6
is quasi-finitely generated.

Furthermore, the coarse equivalence from
3.3
can be improved to a quasi-isometry:

Corollary 3.8
.

The metric
d^{R}
is quasi-isometric to the word metric with respect to any finite quasi-generating set.

Proof.

By
3.3
, the two metrics are coarsely equivalent. Moreover, word metrics with respect to finite quasi-generating sets and path metrics on graphs are both quasi-geodesic, and coarsely equivalent quasi-geodesic (extended) metric spaces are actually quasi-isometric.
∎

Hence our
2.4
follows from the next observation:

Lemma 3.9
.

There exists
T\geq 0
such that, if
R\geq T
, then
(S,d^{R})
is quasi-isometric to
X
.

Proof.

Consider the map
\phi\colon S\to X
mapping
s
to
x_{1}\cdot s
. This map is
R
-Lipschitz since, if
s,t\in S
are adjacent in
S^{R}
then
d(x_{1}\cdot s,x_{1}\cdot t)\leq R
by construction. Furthermore
d^{R}(s,t)\leq d(x_{1}\cdot s,x_{1}\cdot t)/R+1
, since any geodesic
\gamma
connecting
x_{1}\cdot s
to
x_{1}\cdot t
can be subdivided into at most
d(x_{1}\cdot s,x_{1}\cdot t)/R+1
subsegments of length at most
R
. Finally, coarse surjectivity follows from coboundedness: if
R
is greater than the coboundedness constant
T
, then for every
y\in X
there exists
s\in S
such that
y\in B(x_{1},T)\cdot s
, and therefore
d(y,x_{1}\cdot s)\leq R
as the action is by
1
-Lipschitz maps.
∎
