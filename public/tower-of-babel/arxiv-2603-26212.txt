Abstract
. We develop an unfitted compatible finite element discretisation for the Darcy problem based on H ⁡ ( div ) H(\mathrm{div}) -conforming flux spaces and discontinuous pressure spaces. The method is designed to preserve pointwise discrete mass conservation while remaining robust in the presence of arbitrarily small cut cells arising from unfitted meshes. Robustness is achieved by combining an L 2 L^{2} -stabilisation of the flux with an additional mixed-term stabilisation that enhances pressure control without destroying the local conservation structure. We consider both cell-wise (bulk) and face-based ghost-penalty realisations of the stabilisation. Mixed boundary conditions are handled by weak imposition of both flux and pressure traces on unfitted boundaries. We prove stability and optimal-order a priori error estimates with constants independent of the cut configuration, and establish pressure-robust flux error bounds in the case of pure pressure boundary conditions. We also introduce an augmented Lagrangian variant that improves control of the conservation constraint and is amenable to efficient preconditioning strategies. Numerical experiments for a range of cut configurations, boundary-condition regimes and parameter choices confirm the theoretical results, demonstrating optimal convergence, cut-independent conditioning and mass conservation up to solver tolerance.

1.
Introduction

Standard
fe
(
fe
) methods typically require body-fitted triangulations of the physical domain in which one wants to discretise the
pde
(
pde
). These meshes are difficult to generate for complex or moving domains.
In contrast, unfitted (or immersed) methods decouple the discretisation of the geometry from that of the
pde
and employ simple background meshes that do not need to conform to the domain boundary
[
12
]
. Instead of mesh generation algorithms, the geometrical discretisation relies on algorithms that compute the intersection between the background mesh and the geometry
[
5
,
26
]
, which is an embarrassingly parallel cell-wise operation. On these intersections, one must design quadrature rules using, for example, moment-fitting techniques
[
30
]
. While this approach is highly efficient, it introduces the small cut-cell problem. This occurs when arbitrarily small intersections between the mesh and the boundary lead to severe numerical instabilities and ill-conditioning
[
16
]
. To restore robustness, several stabilisation strategies have been developed. Ghost-penalty schemes stabilise the system by penalising derivative jumps across facets cutting the boundary
[
11
]
. Alternatively, cell-aggregation techniques, such as aggregated finite element methods (AgFEM), address the problem by extending well-posed degrees of freedom to ill-posed ones through discrete extension operators
[
7
]
. Most work on unfitted methods has focused on grad-conforming approximations and nodal Lagrangian FEM.

H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming discretisations of the mixed Poisson or Darcy problem have been considered in
[
29
]
, where a ghost-penalty pressure-stabilisation term is added to achieve robustness with respect to small cut cells. However, the resulting method does not satisfy pointwise discrete mass conservation. Exact mass conservation has been attained in
[
19
]
for an unfitted interface Darcy problem by using indefinite stabilisation terms that do not perturb the discrete mass-conservation equation, while the boundary remains body-fitted. Using ideas similar to those in
[
13
]
, exact mass conservation is attained in
[
25
]
in the boundary-unfitted setting. The method proposed in
[
25
]
relies on an extension of the mass-conservation equation to an active mesh instead of ghost-penalty stabilisation. Although the method is analysed for pressure boundary conditions, an extension to mixed boundary conditions using Lagrange multipliers is also discussed. In addition, the weak imposition of flux boundary conditions in
H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming discretisations has been considered in
[
14
]
for the Darcy problem via a consistent penalty method; this method was previously applied to an interface problem in
[
17
]
.

In this work, we propose a novel unfitted finite element method for Darcy flow that satisfies pointwise discrete mass conservation and is robust with respect to cut-cell locations. The method incorporates weak imposition of flux and pressure boundary conditions at unfitted boundaries and relies on an
L^{2}
-stabilisation of the flux together with a mixed stabilisation term that provides enhanced control of the pressure, using ideas similar to those in
[
19
]
for interface problems. The proposed stabilisation is a ghost-penalty method, which can be defined in terms of face jumps (as in
[
19
]
) or bulk penalty terms (as in, for example, the original stabilisation proposed in
[
15
]
or the modification proposed in
[
6
]
). We prove that the method is stable, optimally convergent and satisfies pointwise discrete mass conservation in the boundary-unfitted case with mixed boundary conditions. In addition, in the case of pure pressure boundary conditions, we prove a pressure-robust error estimate for the flux. We also consider an augmented Lagrangian formulation of the proposed method, which may be used to design effective preconditioners and to better preserve mass conservation in the case of mixed boundary conditions.

2.
Geometrical discretisation

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d
the space dimension, be homeomorphic to an open polyhedral domain.
\Omega
represents the physical domain of the
pde
to be solved. Standard
fe
methods are formulated on a so-called
body-fitted
mesh, which is a partition of
\Omega
(or of an approximation of it). Unfitted discretisation techniques, by contrast, do not rely on a
body-fitted
mesh. Instead, the physical domain is embedded into a background domain
\Omega^{\mathrm{bg}}
, such that
\Omega\subset\Omega^{\mathrm{bg}}
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{bg}}
be a conforming, quasi-uniform and shape-regular partition of
\Omega_{h}^{\mathrm{bg}}
. We denote by
h_{T}
the diameter of a cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{bg}}
and the characteristic mesh size is
h\doteq\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{bg}}}h_{T}
. Next, we introduce some geometrical definitions in order to define unfitted
fe
discretisations.

First, we let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{out}}\}
denote a partition of
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{bg}}
into cells intersecting
\Omega
(active cells) and cells not intersecting
\Omega
(outer cells), respectively. Since outer cells do not play any role in the discretisation, they are discarded. We further partition
\mathcal{T}_{h}
into
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{in}},\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}\}
, where

(2.1)

{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{in}}\doteq\left\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:T\subset\Omega\right\},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}\doteq\mathcal{T}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{in}}.}

The cells in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{in}}
are called
interior
cells, while the cells in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}
are called
cut
cells.

We define the active domain
\Omega_{h}
as the interior of the closure of
\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}T
and denote by
\Omega_{h}^{\mathrm{in}}
and
\Omega_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}
the unions of cells in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{in}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}
, respectively.

To complete the geometrical definitions, we extend the previous classifications to the
facets
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{h}
denote the set of all facets in the active mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, where a
facet
refers to 1-faces (edges) in 2D and 2-faces (faces) in 3D. We also define
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}
as the set of interior facets of the active mesh that are faces of at least one cut cell in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}
.

Based on this classification, we introduce an aggregated mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{ag}}
, obtained by associating each cell in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}
with exactly one cell in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{in}}
, called the
root
cell, through a chain of facet-adjacent cells; see
[
7
,
24
]
. Each cell in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
belongs to one, and only one, aggregate, while cells in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{in}}
that do not absorb cut cells define singleton aggregates. We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial,\mathrm{ag}}
the set of non-trivial boundary aggregates, i.e., the aggregates containing at least one cell in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}
. The goal of aggregation is to obtain patches that intersect
\Omega
with measure bounded away from zero. We assume that every aggregate has diameter uniformly bounded by
h
. This aggregate structure will be used in the proposed stabilisation methods below.

For the numerical experiments in Section
8
, we use a slightly more general definition of interior and cut cells, depending on a parameter
\delta\in(0,1]
:

(2.2)

{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{in}}\doteq\left\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:\frac{|T\cap\Omega|}{|T|}\geq\delta\right\},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}\doteq\mathcal{T}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{in}}.}

The analysis presented in this work can readily be extended to this
\delta
-based classification.

Finally, we introduce a fixed smooth domain
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
such that
\Omega_{h}\subset\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
for all
0<h\leq h_{0}
. This outer domain will be used in the stability and error analysis below.

3.
Problem formulation and preliminaries

As a model problem, we consider Darcy’s system in
\Omega
. We consider a partition of the boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
into
\Gamma_{u}
and
\Gamma_{p}
, on which the flux and pressure traces are prescribed, respectively. The outward unit normal vector to the boundary is denoted by
n
. The problem reads as follows: find the flux
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the pressure
p:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\eta}u+\nabla p=f\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,\quad\mathrm{div}\,u=-g\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,\quad u\cdot n=u_{\Gamma}\quad\text{on}\ \Gamma_{u},\quad p=p_{\Gamma}\quad\text{on}\ \Gamma_{p},

where
{\eta}
is the inverse permeability tensor,
f
is the source term,
g
is the divergence source term and
u_{\Gamma}
and
p_{\Gamma}
are the boundary traces for the normal flux and pressure, respectively.
When
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, i.e., when pure flux boundary conditions are considered, the solvability constraint
\int_{\partial\Omega}{u}_{\Gamma}={-}\int_{\Omega}g
must be satisfied and the pressure is only defined up to a constant.

Let
H_{u_{\Gamma}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
and
H_{0}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
be the spaces of functions in
H(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
whose normal trace on
\Gamma_{u}
is equal to
u_{\Gamma}
and zero, respectively. Assume that
\eta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\eta\geq\eta_{0}>0
a.e. in
\Omega
for some constant
\eta_{0}>0
,
f\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}
,
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
p_{\Gamma}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma_{p})
and
u_{\Gamma}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{u})
. The weak form of (
3.1
) reads: find
u\in H_{u_{\Gamma}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
and
p\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

(3.2a)

\displaystyle a(u,v)+b(v,p)

\displaystyle=(f,v)_{\Omega}-\langle v\cdot n,p_{\Gamma}\rangle_{\Gamma_{p}},

\displaystyle\forall v\in H_{0}(\mathrm{div},\Omega),

(3.2b)

\displaystyle b(u,q)

\displaystyle=(g,q)_{\Omega},

\displaystyle\forall q\in L^{2}(\Omega),

where

(3.3)

\displaystyle a(u,v)\doteq(\eta u,v)_{\Omega},\quad\text{ and }\quad b(v,p)\doteq-(\mathrm{div}\,v,p)_{\Omega}.

Here,
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\omega}
denotes the
L^{2}
inner product over
\omega
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\omega}
denotes the duality pairing between
H^{-1/2}(\omega)
and
H^{1/2}(\omega)
. When
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, we seek
p\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e., the space of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with zero mean in
\Omega
, test the second equation in (
3.2
) with
q\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, and assume that the compatibility condition
\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{\Gamma}=-\int_{\Omega}g
on the data is satisfied.

3.1.
Discrete spaces

Let
V_{\mathrm{d},h}\subset H(\mathrm{div},\Omega_{h})
and
V_{\mathrm{0},h}\subset L^{2}(\Omega_{h})
be
H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming and
L^{2}
-conforming
fe
spaces defined on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, respectively. Let
\mathbb{P}_{k}
denote the space of polynomials of degree at most
k
and let
\mathbb{Q}_{k}
denote the space of tensor-product polynomials of degree at most
k
in each variable. We make the following assumption on these discrete spaces.

Assumption 3.1
.

The global flux space
V_{\mathrm{d},h}
can be defined in terms of a space of vector fields
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{d}}
with polynomial components as follows:

(3.4)

V_{\mathrm{d},h}=\left\{v_{h}\in H(\mathrm{div},\Omega_{h}):v_{h}|_{T}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{d}}(T),\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

where
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{d}}(T)
denotes the restriction of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{d}}
to
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
The pressure space
V_{\mathrm{0},h}
consists of discontinuous piecewise polynomials
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{0}}(T)
on each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. These two spaces determine a discrete de Rham complex between
d-1
and
d
-forms with bounded cochain interpolators
\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}
and
\Pi_{0,h}

[
3
]
.

Let
k_{u},k_{p}\in\{0,1,\ldots\}
be the largest values such that
[\mathbb{P}_{k_{u}}(T)]^{d}\subset\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{d}}(T)
and
\mathbb{P}_{k_{p}}(T)\subset\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{0}}(T)
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, respectively. We refer to
k_{u}
and
k_{p}
as the polynomial degrees of
V_{\mathrm{d},h}
and
V_{\mathrm{0},h}
, respectively. We assume that the interpolators
\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}
and
\Pi_{0,h}
satisfy the following approximation properties: given
u\in[H^{r}(\Omega_{h})]^{d}
and
p\in H^{t}(\Omega_{h})
, it holds

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|u-\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\lesssim h^{r}\|u\|_{H^{r}(\Omega_{h})},\quad\hbox{for }r=0,\ldots,k_{u}+1,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|p-\Pi_{0,h}(p)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\lesssim h^{t}\|p\|_{H^{t}(\Omega_{h})},\quad\hbox{for }t=0,\ldots,k_{p}+1.

This choice implies a discrete inf-sup condition, i.e., the restriction of
\mathrm{div}
to
V_{\mathrm{d},h}
is surjective onto
V_{\mathrm{0},h}
, which is necessary for the stability of the mixed formulation. In particular, for such a spatial discretisation, it holds that
\mathrm{div}\,V_{\mathrm{d},h}=V_{\mathrm{0},h}
, due to the trivial exactness of the relevant portion of the discrete complex. Possible choices for these spaces can be found in
[
3
]
for simplicial and quadrilateral meshes. They include Raviart–Thomas and Brezzi–Douglas–Marini spaces, as well as the serendipity
fe
in
[
1
]
. The proposed methodology is agnostic to the mesh topology; in Section
8
, we use both quadrilateral and triangular meshes. In particular, in the numerical experiments, we use
\mathbb{RT}_{k}\times\mathbb{Q}_{k}
on quadrilateral meshes and
\mathbb{RT}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}_{k}
and
\mathbb{BDM}_{k+1}\times\mathbb{P}_{k}
on triangular meshes, for
k\geq 0
. Note that, since
\mathrm{div}\,V_{\mathrm{d},h}=V_{\mathrm{0},h}
,
k_{p}\leq k_{u}
and, given
\mathrm{div}\,u\in H^{t}(\Omega_{h})
, it holds that:

(3.7)

\|\mathrm{div}\,u-\Pi_{0,h}(\mathrm{div}\,u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\lesssim h^{t}\|\mathrm{div}\,u\|_{H^{t}(\Omega_{h})},\quad\hbox{for }t=0,\ldots,k_{p}+1.

3.2.
Discrete formulation

Next, we consider the discrete formulation of (
3.2
). First, we weakly impose the flux boundary conditions since the boundary
\Gamma
is embedded in
\Omega_{h}
. Second, we add stabilisation terms to the discrete problem to ensure stability and robustness of the solution with respect to small cut cells. In particular, we make the following assumption on the stabilisation terms. Later, we propose several stabilisation terms that satisfy this assumption. We use
a\lesssim b
(respectively,
a\gtrsim b
) to denote that
a\leq Cb
(respectively
a\geq Cb
) for some constant
C>0
independent of the characteristic mesh size
h
and the cut location; we use
a\simeq b
if both
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
hold. For simplicity of exposition, we take
\eta
to be scalar and equal to one.

Assumption 3.2
.

Let
s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}
and
s^{0}_{h}
be the stabilisation terms for the flux and pressure, respectively. The stabilisation terms satisfy the following properties:

(3.8a)

\displaystyle\|w_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}(w_{h},w_{h})

\displaystyle\simeq\|w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2},

\displaystyle\forall w_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h},

(3.8b)

\displaystyle\|r_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+s^{0}_{h}(r_{h},r_{h})

\displaystyle\simeq\|r_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2},

\displaystyle\forall r_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}.

Furthermore, the stabilisation terms satisfy the following weak consistency estimates:

(3.9a)

\displaystyle s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}(\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(w),v_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}\|w\|_{H^{s}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})},

\displaystyle 0\leq s\leq k_{u}+1,

(3.9b)

\displaystyle s^{0}_{h}(\Pi_{0,h}(r),q_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{t}\|r\|_{H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})},

\displaystyle 0\leq t\leq k_{p}+1,

for any
w\in[H^{s}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})]^{d}
,
r\in H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
,
v_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}
and
q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
.
In addition, the pressure stabilisation vanishes on constants, i.e.,
s^{0}_{h}(c,q_{h})=0
, for every constant
c
and every
q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
.

Note that, taking
s=0
in (
3.9a
) and
t=0
in (
3.9b
), and using that the interpolators act as the identity on the discrete spaces, the weak consistency implies continuity of the stabilisation terms in the
L^{2}(\Omega_{h})
-norm in the discrete spaces.

In order to state the weak form of (
3.1
) with weak imposition of flux boundary conditions, we define the discrete forms:

(3.10)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\doteq a(u_{h},v_{h})+\gamma h^{-1}(u_{h}\cdot n,v_{h}\cdot n)_{\Gamma_{u}}+\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h}),

(3.11)

\displaystyle b_{h}(v_{h},p_{h})

\displaystyle\doteq b(v_{h},p_{h})-\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},p_{h}),

(3.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{h}(v_{h},p_{h})

\displaystyle\doteq\tilde{b}(v_{h},p_{h})-\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},p_{h}),

where
\tilde{b}(v_{h},p_{h})\doteq b(v_{h},p_{h})+(v_{h}\cdot n,p_{h})_{\Gamma_{u}}
, and
\gamma,\tau_{\mathrm{d}},\tau_{0}>0
are positive numerical parameters. The discrete formulation reads: find
(u_{h},p_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}
such that

(3.13a)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})+\tilde{b}_{h}(v_{h},p_{h})

\displaystyle=(f,v_{h})_{\Omega}+\gamma h^{-1}(u_{\Gamma},v_{h}\cdot n)_{\Gamma_{u}}-(v_{h}\cdot n,p_{\Gamma})_{\Gamma_{p}},

(3.13b)

\displaystyle b_{h}(u_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle=(g,q_{h})_{\Omega},

for all
(v_{h},q_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}
. In the case
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, the pressure trial and test spaces are replaced by
V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Let us define the following stabilised
L^{2}
-projection
\pi_{h}^{0}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{\mathrm{0},h}
: given
r\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, find
\pi_{h}^{0}(r)\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
such that

(3.14)

\displaystyle(\pi_{h}^{0}(r),q_{h})_{\Omega}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(\pi_{h}^{0}(r),q_{h})=(r,q_{h})_{\Omega},\quad\forall q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}.

Next, using this stabilised
L^{2}
-projection we show that the discrete formulation satisfies the following property.

Lemma 3.3

(Discrete mass conservation)
.

If
\Gamma_{p}\neq\emptyset
, the discrete solution
u_{h}
satisfies
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}=-\pi_{h}^{0}(g)
on
\Omega_{h}
. If
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, there exists a constant
c_{h}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}=-\pi_{h}^{0}(g)+c_{h}
on
\Omega_{h}
. For
g=0
, the discrete flux divergence is zero for
\Gamma_{p}\neq\emptyset
and constant otherwise.

Proof.

If
\Gamma_{p}\neq\emptyset
, the claim follows directly from (
3.13b
) and the definition of the projection in (
3.14
).
Assume now that
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
. Then (
3.13b
) holds for all
q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Combining (
3.13b
) with (
3.14
), we obtain

(\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}+\pi_{h}^{0}(g),q_{h})_{\Omega}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}+\pi_{h}^{0}(g),q_{h})=0,\quad\forall q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

Since
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}+\pi_{h}^{0}(g)\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
by Assumption
3.1
, we write
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}+\pi_{h}^{0}(g)=z_{h}+c_{h}
with
z_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
c_{h}
constant. Taking
q_{h}=z_{h}
in the previous identity and using that
(c_{h},z_{h})_{\Omega}=0
together with
s^{0}_{h}(c_{h},z_{h})=0
by Assumption
3.2
, we obtain

\|z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(z_{h},z_{h})=0.

Hence,
z_{h}=0
, and therefore
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}=-\pi_{h}^{0}(g)+c_{h}
on
\Omega_{h}
. The results for
g=0
relies on the fact that
\pi_{h}^{0}(0)=0
.

Remark 3.4
.

In the case
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, the pressure space in the discrete formulation is replaced by
V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. In general one does not have
\mathrm{div}\,V_{\mathrm{d},h}\subset V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, since the flux boundary condition is imposed weakly and therefore the mean value of
\mathrm{div}\,v_{h}
is not constrained for
v_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}
. As a consequence, the discrete conservation equation only determines the zero-mean component of
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}
, while its constant mode remains undetermined. To address this issue, one can impose the additional constraint
\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{h}\cdot n=-\int_{\Omega}g
via a Lagrange multiplier in (
3.13
), so that the previous lemma holds with
c_{h}=0
when
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
. We have used this technique in the numerical experiments in Section
8
. The analysis below can readily be extended to this corrected formulation, but it is not included here for conciseness.

Remark 3.5
.

The constant
c_{h}
converges to zero as
h\to 0
. Indeed, since
\mathrm{div}\,u=-g
in
\Omega
and the stabilised projection preserves constants, one has

|\Omega|^{1/2}|c_{h}|=\|\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}+\pi_{h}^{0}(g)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\mathrm{div}\,u-\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g-\pi_{h}^{0}(g)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The a priori estimate in Section
5
together with Lemma
5.4
imply that
c_{h}\to 0
as
h\to 0
.

Remark 3.6
.

We note that we could choose to add
\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(g,q_{h})
to the right-hand side of (
3.13b
). We avoid this choice because it requires extending
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
to the active domain
\Omega_{h}

[
25
]
.

Remark 3.7
.

The proposed method is also of interest in the body-fitted case for the weak imposition of boundary conditions. In this case, the stabilisation terms vanish since
\Omega=\Omega_{h}
and there are no cut cells. The method can be seen as a consistent penalty method for the weak imposition of flux boundary conditions in
H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming discretisations, which has not been considered in the literature to the best of the authors’ knowledge. The analysis below readily applies to this case without the need to assume the existence of extensions of the solution to a larger domain
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
.

4.
Stability analysis

In this section, we prove the stability of the discrete problem (
3.13
). Let us write the system in compact form as
\tilde{A}_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))=\ell_{h}(v_{h},q_{h})
, where

(4.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))

\displaystyle\doteq a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})+\tilde{b}_{h}(v_{h},p_{h})+b_{h}(u_{h},q_{h}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\ell_{h}(v_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle\doteq(f,v_{h})_{\Omega}+\gamma h^{-1}(u_{\Gamma},v_{h}\cdot n)_{\Gamma_{u}}-(v_{h}\cdot n,p_{\Gamma})_{\Gamma_{p}}+(g,q_{h})_{\Omega}.

We note that the analysis does not rely on a continuous inf-sup condition whose constant is uniform with respect to
h
over the family of active domains
\Omega_{h}
. Indeed,
\Omega_{h}
is a family of
h
-dependent domains that may fail to satisfy the assumptions required at the continuous level to obtain an inf-sup constant bounded away from zero as
h\downarrow 0
.
Instead, we consider the discrete multilinear form

(4.3)

A_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))\doteq a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})+b_{h}(v_{h},p_{h})+b_{h}(u_{h},q_{h})

without the boundary term on
\Gamma_{u}
and prove a discrete weak inf-sup condition in
\Omega_{h}
. We then rely on this result to analyse the full multilinear form
\tilde{A}_{h}
, which contains the boundary term
(v_{h}\cdot n,p_{h})_{\Gamma_{u}}
.

We define the norms:

\displaystyle\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}

\displaystyle\doteq\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\gamma h^{-1}\|u_{h}\cdot n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})},

\displaystyle\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h}

\displaystyle\doteq\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+h\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})}^{2},

and note that

(4.4)

\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\|p_{h}\|_{0,h}\lesssim\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})},\qquad\forall p_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}.

The lower bound is immediate from the definition. For the converse, we use the trace inequality on cut elements

(4.5)

h\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\cap T)}^{2}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+h^{2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2},\qquad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}},

which holds for every
\phi\in H^{1}(T)
; see
[
12
]
. Since
\Gamma_{u}
is covered by cut elements, (
4.5
) followed by a standard inverse inequality
[
10
]
gives the upper bound in (
4.4
).

Theorem 4.1

(Stability)
.

Assuming that the domain
\Omega
enjoys elliptic regularity, the multilinear form
\tilde{A}_{h}
in (
4.1
) satisfies the discrete inf-sup condition

(4.6)

\displaystyle\inf_{(u_{h},p_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}}\sup_{(v_{h},q_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}}\frac{\tilde{A}_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))}{(\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h})^{1/2}(\|v_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h})^{1/2}}\geq\tilde{\beta},

where, in the case
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, the pressure spaces in the infimum and supremum are replaced by
V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. The constant
\tilde{\beta}>0
is independent of
h
and the cut locations, provided
\gamma
is large enough and
h
is small enough.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in two steps. First, we establish a discrete inf-sup condition for the bilinear form
b_{h}
appearing in (
3.11
). Using this result together with coercivity of
a_{h}
, we can show an inf-sup condition for the reduced multilinear form
A_{h}
. In the second step, we estimate the boundary contribution
(v_{h}\cdot n,p_{h})_{\Gamma_{u}}
in terms of the mesh-dependent norms of
v_{h}
and
p_{h}
and show that, for
\gamma
sufficiently large, stability of the full multilinear form
\tilde{A}_{h}
follows.

We begin by constructing, for a given
p_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
(with
p_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
when
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
), a function
v_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}
that guarantees the inf-sup condition for the bilinear form
b_{h}
. Let
\xi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
solve

(4.7)

\displaystyle-\Delta\xi

\displaystyle=p_{h}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad\partial_{n}\xi=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{u},\qquad\xi=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{p}.

Note that if
\Gamma_{u}=\partial\Omega
, we have
\int_{\Omega}p_{h}=0
and
\xi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. By elliptic regularity, it holds that

\|\xi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|p_{h}\|_{\Omega}.

Next, defining
v_{\Omega}\doteq\nabla\xi\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
, we obtain

(4.8)

\|v_{\Omega}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|p_{h}\|_{\Omega},\qquad\mathrm{div}\,v_{\Omega}=-p_{h}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Next, let
S=\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}\setminus\Omega
and
\chi_{\Omega_{h}}
be the characteristic function of
\Omega_{h}
. By
[
21
, Theorem III.3.1]
and the trace extension theorem, one can show that there exists
v_{S}\in[H^{1}(S)]^{d}
such that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\,v_{S}=-\chi_{\Omega_{h}}p_{h}\quad\text{in }S,\qquad v_{S}=v_{\Omega}\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

and the stability estimate

(4.10)

\|v_{S}\|_{H^{1}(S)}\lesssim\|\chi_{\Omega_{h}}p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(S)}+\|v_{\Omega}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}

holds. Note that there are no conditions imposed on
\partial S\setminus\partial\Omega
and hence there are no compatibility conditions. We can now define
v\in[H^{1}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})]^{d}
such that

(4.11)

v=v_{\Omega}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad v=v_{S}\quad\text{in }S.

Then

(4.12)

\mathrm{div}\,v=-\chi_{\Omega_{h}}p_{h}\quad\text{in }\,\Omega_{h}\subset\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}=\Omega\cup S,\qquad v\cdot n=0\quad\text{on }\,\Gamma_{u},

and the following estimate holds by the stability estimate for
v_{S}
, elliptic regularity for
v_{\Omega}
and the trace theorem on
\partial\Omega
:

(4.13)

\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}\lesssim\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\chi_{\Omega_{h}}p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(S)}+\|v_{\Omega}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.

Taking
v_{h}\doteq\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(v)
and invoking the commutativity properties of the interpolator in Assumption
3.1
, we have that
v_{h}
satisfies
\mathrm{div}\,v_{h}=-p_{h}
on
\Omega_{h}
and
\|v_{h}\|_{H(\mathrm{div},\Omega_{h})}\lesssim\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}
. Thus, we obtain:

(4.14)

\displaystyle{b}_{h}(v_{h},p_{h})=-(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},p_{h})_{\Omega}-\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},p_{h})=\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(p_{h},p_{h})\gtrsim\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})},

where we have used (
3.8b
) in Assumption
3.2
. By (
4.4
), the norms
\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{\Omega_{h}}
and
\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h}
are equivalent, and

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}

\displaystyle=\|v_{h}\|^{2}_{H(\mathrm{div},\Omega_{h})}+\gamma h^{-1}\|v_{h}\cdot n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})}

(4.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\gamma h^{-1}\|(v-v_{h})\cdot n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})}\lesssim\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2},

where in the last inequality we used the properties of
v
, the trace inequality (
4.5
) and the local interpolation estimate for
\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}
to obtain

\displaystyle h^{-1}\|(v-v_{h})\cdot n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{cut}}}h^{-2}\|(v-v_{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(T)}+\|\nabla(v-v_{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|v\|_{H^{1}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}.

Next, we prove the stability of
a_{h}
on
\mathrm{ker}({b}_{h})
. We have, for any
u_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}
:

(4.17)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},u_{h})

\displaystyle=(u_{h},u_{h})_{\Omega}+\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}(u_{h},u_{h})+\gamma h^{-1}\|u_{h}\cdot n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\simeq\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\gamma h^{-1}\|u_{h}\cdot n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})},

where we have used (
3.8a
) in Assumption
3.2
. Next, we note that if
u_{h}\in\mathrm{ker}({b}_{h})
, then

0={b}_{h}(u_{h},q_{h})=-(\mathrm{div}\,u_{h},q_{h})_{\Omega}-\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(\mathrm{div}\,u_{h},q_{h}),\qquad\forall q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}.

If
\Gamma_{p}\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
and uniqueness of the stabilised projection problem (
3.14
) with vanishing right-hand side implies that
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}=0
on
\Omega_{h}
. If
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, then the identity above holds for all
q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
, we write
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}=w_{h}+c
with
w_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
c
constant. Using that
s^{0}_{h}(c,q_{h})=0
for all
q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
, the same uniqueness argument yields
w_{h}=0
, and therefore
\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}=c
on
\Omega_{h}
. In addition, since
\Gamma_{u}=\partial\Omega
in this case, the divergence theorem gives
|\Omega|c=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}=\int_{\Gamma_{u}}u_{h}\cdot n
. Hence, by Cauchy-Schwarz, we obtain,

\|\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}=|\Omega_{h}|c^{2}=\frac{|\Omega_{h}|}{|\Omega|^{2}}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{u}}u_{h}\cdot n\right)^{2}\leq\frac{|\Omega_{h}||\Gamma_{u}|}{|\Omega|^{2}}\|u_{h}\cdot n\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})}^{2}\lesssim h^{-1}\|u_{h}\cdot n\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})}^{2},

where we have used that
|\Omega_{h}|\lesssim|\Omega|
. Combining this bound with (
4.17
), we obtain the following stability result:

(4.19)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},u_{h})\simeq\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h},\quad\forall u_{h}\in\mathrm{ker}({b}_{h}).

Thus, using the standard Babuška–Brezzi theory
[
9
]
, we obtain the global inf-sup condition:

(4.20)

\displaystyle\inf_{(u_{h},p_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}}\sup_{(v_{h},q_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}}\frac{A_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))}{(\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h})^{1/2}(\|v_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h})^{1/2}}\geq\beta,

for some constant
\beta>0
independent of
h
and the cut configuration. When
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, the pressure spaces in (
4.20
) are replaced by
V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Next, we consider the full multilinear form
\tilde{A}_{h}
. We have

(4.21)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))

\displaystyle=A_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))+(v_{h}\cdot n,p_{h})_{\Gamma_{u}}.

By the global inf-sup condition for
A_{h}
, for every admissible pair
(u_{h},p_{h})
there exists a pair
(v_{h},q_{h})
such that

\displaystyle\beta^{-1}A_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))

\displaystyle\geq\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h},\quad\|v_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|q_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h}=\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h}.

We bound the boundary term using Young’s inequality and the definition of the norms as follows:

(4.22)

\displaystyle(v_{h}\cdot n,p_{h})_{\Gamma_{u}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\gamma h^{-1}\|v_{h}\cdot n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})}+\frac{h}{4\alpha\gamma}\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{u})}\leq\alpha\|v_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\frac{1}{4\alpha\gamma}\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h}.

Combining the estimates yields

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))

\displaystyle\geq\beta\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\beta\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h}-\alpha(\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h})-\frac{1}{4\alpha\gamma}\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h}

\displaystyle\geq(\beta-\alpha)\|u_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\left(\beta-\alpha-\frac{1}{4\alpha\gamma}\right)\|p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h}.

Choosing
\alpha<\beta
and assuming
\gamma
is sufficiently large so that
\left(\beta-\alpha-\frac{1}{4\alpha\gamma}\right)>0
, we obtain the discrete inf-sup condition for
\tilde{A}_{h}
. For instance, taking
\alpha=\frac{\beta}{2}
and
\gamma=\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}
yields the inf-sup constant
\tilde{\beta}=\beta/4
.
∎

5.
A priori error analysis

In this section, we prove a priori error estimates for the discrete problem (
3.13
). The main result is stated in the following theorem, which provides an error bound in terms of the best-approximation errors in the mesh-dependent norms.

Assumption 5.1
.

We assume that
u
,
p
and
\mathrm{div}\,u
admit extensions to
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
, still denoted by the same symbols, such that
u\in[H^{r}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})]^{d}
,
p\in H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
and
\mathrm{div}\,u\in H^{s}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
for
0\leq r\leq k_{u}+1
,
0\leq s\leq k_{p}+1
and
0\leq t\leq k_{p}+1
, and these extensions satisfy the stability bound

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{r}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)},\qquad\|\mathrm{div}\,u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}\lesssim\|\mathrm{div}\,u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)},\qquad\|p\|_{H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}\lesssim\|p\|_{H^{t}(\Omega)}.

Theorem 5.2

(Error estimate)
.

Let
(u,p)
be the solution of the continuous problem (
3.1
) and let
(u_{h},p_{h})
be the solution of the discrete problem (
3.13
). Under Assumption
5.1
and the conditions of Theorem
4.1
, the following error estimate holds:

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\|u-u_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}^{2}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}}\lesssim h^{2r}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{r}(\Omega)}+h^{2s}\|\mathrm{div}\,u\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\Omega)}+h^{2t}\|p\|^{2}_{H^{t}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We prove the result in the case
\Gamma_{p}\neq\emptyset
. When
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, the pressure quantities above are understood in
V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, and the proof is analogous to the one below.
Let
(u,p)
be the solution of the continuous problem (
3.1
) and let
(u_{h},p_{h})
be the solution of the discrete problem (
3.13
). Let
(w_{h},r_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}
. Using the expression of the discrete problem (
3.13
) and the stability estimate in Theorem
4.1
, there exists
(v_{h},q_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p_{h}-r_{h}\|^{2}_{0,h}\lesssim a_{h}(u_{h}-w_{h},v_{h})+\tilde{b}_{h}(v_{h},p_{h}-r_{h})+b_{h}(u_{h}-w_{h},q_{h})

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\ell_{h}(v_{h},q_{h})-a_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})-\tilde{b}_{h}(v_{h},r_{h})-b_{h}(w_{h},q_{h})\doteq(*).

Moreover, the pair
(v_{h},q_{h})
can be chosen so that
\|v_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|q_{h}\|_{0,h}\lesssim\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p_{h}-r_{h}\|_{0,h}
.
Using the definition of
\ell_{h}
in (
4.1
), the strong form of (
3.1
), integration by parts and the boundary conditions, we obtain

\displaystyle\ell_{h}(v_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle=(f,v_{h})_{\Omega}+\gamma h^{-1}(u_{\Gamma},v_{h}\cdot n)_{\Gamma_{u}}-(v_{h}\cdot n,p_{\Gamma})_{\Gamma_{p}}+(g,q_{h})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(\eta u+\nabla p,v_{h})_{\Omega}+\gamma h^{-1}(u_{\Gamma},v_{h}\cdot n)_{\Gamma_{u}}-(v_{h}\cdot n,p_{\Gamma})_{\Gamma_{p}}+(g,q_{h})_{\Omega}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=a(u,v_{h})+\gamma h^{-1}(u\cdot n,v_{h}\cdot n)_{\Gamma_{u}}+\tilde{b}(v_{h},p)+b(u,q_{h}).

Invoking (
5.3
) in (
5.2
), we can express the right-hand side of (
5.2
) as:

(5.4)

\displaystyle(*)=

\displaystyle a(u-w_{h},v_{h})+\gamma h^{-1}((u-w_{h})\cdot n,v_{h}\cdot n)_{\Gamma_{u}}+\tilde{b}(v_{h},p-r_{h})+b(u-w_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle-\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}{(w_{h},v_{h})}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},r_{h})}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,w_{h},q_{h})}.

The first four terms on the right-hand side of (
5.4
) can be bounded using the Cauchy–Schwarz and Young inequalities together with the definition of the discrete norms:

(5.5)

\displaystyle a(u-w_{h},v_{h})+\gamma h^{-1}((u-w_{h})\cdot n,v_{h}\cdot n)_{\Gamma_{u}}+\tilde{b}(v_{h},p-r_{h})+b(u-w_{h},q_{h})

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|u-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p-r_{h}\|_{0,h})(\|v_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|q_{h}\|_{0,h}).

For the stabilisation terms, we proceed as follows:

(5.7)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}{(w_{h},v_{h})}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},r_{h})}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,w_{h},q_{h})}

(5.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}{(\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u-w_{h}),v_{h})}-\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},\Pi_{0,h}(p-r_{h}))}-\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u-w_{h}),q_{h})}

(5.9)

\displaystyle-\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}{(\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u),v_{h})}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},\Pi_{0,h}(p))}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u),q_{h})}.

The first three terms can readily be bounded by the continuity of the stabilisation bilinear forms and the stability of the interpolants
\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}
and
\Pi_{0,h}
:

(5.10)

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}{(\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u-w_{h}),v_{h})}-\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},\Pi_{0,h}(p-r_{h}))}-\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u-w_{h}),q_{h})}

(5.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|u-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p-r_{h}\|_{0,h})(\|v_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|q_{h}\|_{0,h}).

The last three terms in (
5.9
) can be bounded by the weak consistency of the stabilisation bilinear forms in Assumption
3.2
:

\displaystyle-\tau_{\mathrm{d}}

\displaystyle s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}{(\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u),v_{h})}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h},\Pi_{0,h}(p))}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}{(\mathrm{div}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u),q_{h})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(h^{r}\|u\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}+h^{s}\|\mathrm{div}\,u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}+h^{t}\|p\|_{H^{t}(\Omega)})(\|v_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|q_{h}\|_{0,h}),

where, for the last term, we have used the commutativity property
\mathrm{div}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u)=\Pi_{0,h}(\mathrm{div}\,u)
together with the weak consistency of
s^{0}_{h}
applied to
\mathrm{div}\,u
.
Using the estimate for
(v_{h},q_{h})
stated after (
5.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p_{h}-r_{h}\|_{0,h}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p-r_{h}\|_{0,h}+h^{r}\|u\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}+h^{s}\|\mathrm{div}\,u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}+h^{t}\|p\|_{H^{t}(\Omega)},

after dividing by
\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|p_{h}-r_{h}\|_{0,h}
when this quantity is nonzero. Choosing
w_{h}=\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u)
and
r_{h}=\Pi_{0,h}(p)
, the triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle{\|u-u_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}}

\displaystyle{\leq\|u-\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u)\|_{\mathrm{d},h}+\|u_{h}-\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u)\|_{\mathrm{d},h},}

\displaystyle{\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,h}}

\displaystyle{\leq\|p-\Pi_{0,h}(p)\|_{0,h}+\|p_{h}-\Pi_{0,h}(p)\|_{0,h}.}

The interpolation estimates for
u
,
\mathrm{div}\,u
and
p
, together with the trace inequality for the boundary term in
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{d},h}
and Assumption
5.1
, then prove the theorem.
∎

Pressure-robust flux error estimate

We can improve the error estimate for the flux using ideas similar to those used in divergence-free mixed
fem
(
fem
) for the Stokes system
[
23
]
. However, since we consider the weak imposition of the flux boundary conditions, the results only hold for
\Gamma_{u}=\emptyset
; we note that we cannot enforce
w_{h}\cdot n=0
on
\Gamma_{u}
since
\Gamma_{u}
is embedded in
\Omega_{h}
. We define the space
V_{\mathrm{d},h}(g)=\{v_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\ :\ \mathrm{div}\,v_{h}=-\pi_{h}^{0}(g)\}
. In the following, we use Assumption
5.1
and write
\tilde{g}
for the extension of

Assumption 5.3
.

We assume that the kernel of the pressure stabilisation,
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{p}\doteq\{q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}\ :\ s^{0}_{h}(q_{h},\phi_{h})=0\ \forall\phi_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}\}

Lemma 5.4

(Approximation of the stabilised projection)
.

Let
\tilde{r}\in H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
with
0\leq t\leq k_{p}+1
, and let
r
denote its restriction to
\Omega
. Then, the stabilised projection
\pi_{h}^{0}(r)\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
defined in (

(5.12)

\|\tilde{r}-\pi_{h}^{0}(r)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\lesssim h^{t}\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}.

Proof.

We consider
w_{h}\in\mathcal{K}_{h}^{p}
. Using the definition of the stabilised projection and the fact that
s^{0}_{h}(w_{h},q_{h})=0
for all
q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
, we get

\displaystyle(\pi_{h}^{0}(r)-w_{h},q_{h})_{\Omega}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(\pi_{h}^{0}(r)-w_{h},q_{h})=(r-w_{h},q_{h})_{\Omega},\qquad\forall q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}.

Taking

and using (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\pi_{h}^{0}(r)-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(r-w_{h},\pi_{h}^{0}(r)-w_{h})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|r-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\pi_{h}^{0}(r)-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|r-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\pi_{h}^{0}(r)-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.

\|\pi_{h}^{0}(r)-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\lesssim\|r-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

\|\tilde{r}-\pi_{h}^{0}(r)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\|\tilde{r}-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\pi_{h}^{0}(r)-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\lesssim\|\tilde{r}-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|r-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Invoking Assumption

Theorem 5.5

(Pressure-robust error estimate)
.

Let
u\in[H^{r}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})]^{d}
and
\mathrm{div}\,u\in H^{s}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
for
0\leq r\leq k_{u}+1
and
0\leq s\leq k_{p}+1
. Under Assumption
5.1
, Assumption
5.3
, and the conditions of Theorem
4.1
, the following error estimate holds for
\Gamma_{u}=\emptyset

(5.13)

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{r}\|u\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}+h^{s}\|\mathrm{div}\,u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)},

(5.14)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{div}\,u-\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{s}\|\mathrm{div}\,u\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

For
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{p}
, let
\tilde{g}\in H^{s}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
be the stable extension of
-\mathrm{div}\,u
introduced above and define

\delta_{h}\doteq\Pi_{0,h}(\tilde{g})-\pi_{h}^{0}(g)\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}.

Using the discrete inf-sup construction in the proof of Theorem

, there exists

such that

\mathrm{div}\,z_{h}=\delta_{h}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{h},\qquad\|z_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}\lesssim\|\delta_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.

Next, we define the divergence-corrected interpolant

w_{h}\doteq\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u)+z_{h}.

Since
\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}
commutes with the divergence on
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}
and

in
\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}}

Using the discrete problem (
3.13
), the continuous problem (
3.2
), the fact that
\Gamma_{u}=\emptyset
, and that

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h}-w_{h},v_{h})=(u-w_{h},v_{h})_{\Omega}-\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}(w_{h},v_{h}),\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}(0).

Taking
v_{h}=u_{h}-w_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}(0)
and using the coercivity of

in

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}^{2}

\displaystyle-\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}(w_{h},u_{h}-w_{h})=-\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}(\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u),u_{h}-w_{h})-\tau_{\mathrm{d}}s^{\mathrm{d}}_{h}(z_{h},u_{h}-w_{h}).

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the first term on the right-hand side of (

in Assumption

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\|z_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}.

Dividing by

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h^{r}\|u\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}+\|z_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}.

\displaystyle\|u-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|z_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-\Pi_{\mathrm{d},h}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|z_{h}\|_{\mathrm{d},h}.

Combining the triangle inequality with (

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|\delta_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

Putting together the previous estimates with the approximation properties of

\mathrm{div}\,u-\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}=-g+\pi_{h}^{0}(g)=-\tilde{g}+\pi_{h}^{0}(g)

6.
Augmented Lagrangian formulation

In this section, we extend the discrete problem (
3.13
) using an augmented Lagrangian approach. When
\Gamma_{p}\neq\emptyset
, Lemma
3.3
implies that the solution
u_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}
of (
3.13
) satisfies

(6.1)

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{AL}}(u_{h},v_{h})=\ell_{\mathrm{AL}}(v_{h}),

with

(6.2)

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{AL}}(u_{h},v_{h})\doteq(\mathrm{div}\,u_{h},\mathrm{div}\,v_{h})_{\Omega}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(\mathrm{div}\,u_{h},\mathrm{div}\,v_{h}),\qquad\ell_{\mathrm{AL}}(v_{h})\doteq-(g,\mathrm{div}\,v_{h})_{\Omega}.

Accordingly, the augmented Lagrangian formulation of the discrete problem (
3.13
) is defined as follows: find
(u_{h},p_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}
such that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{h}((u_{h},p_{h}),(v_{h},q_{h}))+\tau_{\mathrm{AL}}c_{\mathrm{AL}}(u_{h},v_{h})=\ell_{h}(v_{h},q_{h})+\tau_{\mathrm{AL}}\ell_{\mathrm{AL}}(v_{h}),

for all
(v_{h},q_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}\times V_{\mathrm{0},h}
, where
\tau_{\mathrm{AL}}>0
is a user-defined parameter.
In the case
\Gamma_{p}=\emptyset
, the argument is slightly modified in view of the remark after Lemma
3.3
. Let
m_{\Omega}(r)\doteq|\Omega|^{-1}(r,1)_{\Omega}
denote the mean value of
r\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Since the conservation equation is tested only with functions in
V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, taking
q_{h}=\mathrm{div}\,v_{h}-m_{\Omega}(\mathrm{div}\,v_{h})\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
shows that the solution
u_{h}
satisfies (
6.1
) with the following modified bilinear and linear forms:

(6.4)

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{AL}}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\doteq(\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}-m_{\Omega}(\mathrm{div}\,u_{h}),\mathrm{div}\,v_{h})_{\Omega}+\tau_{0}s^{0}_{h}(\mathrm{div}\,u_{h},\mathrm{div}\,v_{h}),

(6.5)

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{AL}}(v_{h})

\displaystyle\doteq-(g-m_{\Omega}(g),\mathrm{div}\,v_{h})_{\Omega},\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{d},h}.

In this case, the augmented Lagrangian formulation is then obtained by using
V_{\mathrm{0},h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
as pressure space in (
6.3
).

In both cases, the solution of the augmented Lagrangian formulation coincides with the solution of the discrete problem (
3.13
). Moreover, this formulation is more amenable to operator preconditioning. The flux block is now elliptic in
H(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
and the pressure Schur complement is spectrally equivalent to the mass matrix of the pressure space as
\tau_{\mathrm{AL}}\to\infty
. These blocks can be efficiently preconditioned using robust multigrid methods
[
2
]
. We show experimentally the performance of the augmented Lagrangian formulation in Section
8
, but we do not analyse solvers for this formulation in this work. We also note that the stability and convergence analysis in the previous sections can be readily extended to the augmented Lagrangian formulation, since the additional terms vanish for the solution of the discrete problem (
3.13
).

Lemma 7.1
.

The stabilisation terms (

3.2

Proof.

First, we prove the enhanced stability results. For the pressure stabilisation, the proof for

q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
.
Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
7.5
) gives

(7.6)

\displaystyle s^{0}_{h}(\Pi_{0,h}(r),q_{h})

\displaystyle\leq s^{0}_{h}(\Pi_{0,h}(r),\Pi_{0,h}(r))^{1/2}\,s^{0}_{h}(q_{h},q_{h})^{1/2}\lesssim s^{0}_{h}(\Pi_{0,h}(r),\Pi_{0,h}(r))^{1/2}\,\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.

To bound
s^{0}_{h}(\Pi_{0,h}(r),\Pi_{0,h}(r))
, consider an arbitrary
r_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
and split the sum over derivatives into low- and high-order terms relative to the regularity
t
:

(7.7)

\displaystyle s^{0}_{h}(r_{h},r_{h})=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{stab}}}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}h^{2j+1}\|[D^{j}r_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{stab}}}\sum_{j=t}^{k_{p}}h^{2j+1}\|[D^{j}r_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}.

For the high-order terms
j\geq t
, apply standard trace and inverse inequalities
[
10
]
to bound them in terms of
\|D^{t}r_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}
:

(7.8)

\displaystyle s^{0}_{h}(r_{h},r_{h})\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{stab}}}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}h^{2j+1}\|[D^{j}r_{h}]\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}+h^{2t}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|D^{t}r_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}.

Now set
r_{h}=\Pi_{0,h}(r)
and subtract
r
for the low-order terms and use that

(7.9)

[D^{j}\Pi_{0,h}(r)]=[D^{j}(\Pi_{0,h}(r)-r)]+[D^{j}r]=[D^{j}(\Pi_{0,h}(r)-r)],\quad j<t,

since
r\in H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
implies
[D^{j}r]=0
for
j=0,\cdots,t-1
.
Using the standard trace inequality on each face
F
:

(7.10)

\|[D^{j}(\Pi_{0,h}(r)-r)]\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\lesssim h^{-1}\|D^{j}(\Pi_{0,h}(r)-r)\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+h\|D^{j+1}(\Pi_{0,h}(r)-r)\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}

and standard interpolation estimates

(7.11)

\|D^{j}(\Pi_{0,h}(r)-r)\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim h^{t-j}\,|r|_{H^{t}(T)},\qquad\|D^{j+1}(\Pi_{0,h}(r)-r)\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim h^{t-j-1}\,|r|_{H^{t}(T)},

we readily obtain

(7.12)

\displaystyle h^{2j+1}\|[D^{j}(\Pi_{0,h}(r)-r)]\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\lesssim h^{2t}|r|_{H^{t}(T)}^{2}.

Summing over all faces
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{stab}}
and adding the high-order term gives

(7.13)

\displaystyle s^{0}_{h}(\Pi_{0,h}(r),\Pi_{0,h}(r))

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2t}\|r\|_{H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}^{2}+h^{2t}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|D^{t}\Pi_{0,h}(r)\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim h^{2t}\|r\|_{H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}^{2},

where in the last inequality we used the stability of the interpolant

(7.14)

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|D^{t}\Pi_{0,h}(r)\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|D^{t}(\Pi_{0,h}(r)-r)\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+\|D^{t}r\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\|r\|_{H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}^{2}.

Hence we conclude the weak consistency bound:

(7.15)

\displaystyle s^{0}_{h}(\Pi_{0,h}(r),q_{h})\lesssim h^{t}\|r\|_{H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}\,\|q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})},\qquad 0\leq t\leq k_{p}+1.

Finally,
s^{0}_{h}(c,q_{h})=0
for every constant
c
and every
q_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
, since all jumps of a constant and of its derivatives vanish identically.
The proof of Assumption
5.3
follows the same lines as above. Let
\tilde{r}\in H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})
with
0\leq t\leq k_{p}+1
. Since the patches induced by
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{stab}}
are the aggregates in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial,\mathrm{ag}}
, a Bramble–Hilbert argument on these aggregates yields
w_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
, polynomial of degree at most
k_{p}
on each patch, such that
\|\tilde{r}-w_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\lesssim h^{t}\|\tilde{r}\|_{H^{t}(\Omega^{\mathrm{ext}})}
. Since
w_{h}
is given on each patch by a single polynomial, all jumps
[D^{j}w_{h}]
vanish on every facet in
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\mathrm{stab}}
for
j=0,\dots,k_{p}
, and therefore
s^{0}_{h}(w_{h},\phi_{h})=0
for every
\phi_{h}\in V_{\mathrm{0},h}
. Hence
w_{h}\in\mathcal{K}_{h}^{p}
, and Assumption
5.3
follows.
The corresponding estimate for
u_{h}
follows by the same argument, applied component-wise to each component of
u_{h}
.
∎

9.
Conclusions

We have introduced divergence-free unfitted mixed finite element discretisations for Darcy flow that preserve pointwise discrete mass conservation and remain stable for arbitrarily small cut cells. The formulation admits both cell-wise (bulk) and face-based ghost-penalty realisations. We proved stability and optimal error estimates, including pressure-robust flux bounds for pure pressure boundary conditions, and demonstrated numerically that the methods deliver optimal convergence, cut-independent conditioning, and robust performance across different boundary conditions and parameter regimes.
