I. Major Goals of the Project

 1. Earth System Predictability Capability (ESPC)
An interagency collaboration initiated between Navy, Air Force and NOAA and
expanded to DOE, NASA, and NSF in 2012, for coordination of research to operations of
a National earth system analysis and prediction capability. The goal of ESPC is to meet
broad but specific agency requirements for an earth system analysis and prediction
framework to support one-day to decadal global prediction at appropriate horizontal and
vertical resolution; including the atmosphere, ocean, land, cryosphere, and space.

 2. Integrated Observing Systems (CIOS)
The CIOS coordinates Federal engagement in fostering an atmosphere where sharing of
weather data across the meteorological enterprise is encouraged and facilitated by
stakeholders. The OFCM also coordinates the planning and implementation of
advancements in observing, as well as the development and implementation of standards
and infrastructure that support Federal participation in enterprise data sharing efforts.

 3. Atmospheric Transport and Dispersion (ATD)/Urban Meteorology
The Working Group for Atmospheric Transport and Dispersion (ATD)/Urban
Meteorology supports general information exchange on relevant Federal activities, as
well as supporting and encouraging interagency activities that benefit DOE. The group
also facilitates coordination of specific actions to strengthen collaborative efforts to
develop innovative uses of data.

 4. Tropical Cyclone Operations and Research
OFCM hosts the Tropical Cyclone Operations Research Forum
(TCORF)/Interdepartmental Hurricane Conference (IHC) to provide a forum for the
responsible federal agencies, together with representatives of the user communities such
as emergency management, to review the nation’s tropical cyclone forecast and warning
program, and to make recommendations on how to improve the program. The TCORF,
through the Working Group for Hurricane and Winter Storm Operations and Research,
will address a variety of issues of the interagency user community, including DOE, such
as observations strategies, high resolution modeling requirements, and research on
landfall impacts.

 5. Federal Meteorological Coordination

On an annual basis, the OFCM publishes Annual Federal Weather Enterprise (FWE)
Budget and Coordination Report (BCR). It is a comprehensive publication that
documents proposed programs for the current fiscal year and reviews agency programs
for the previous fiscal year. The BCR is mandated by Public Law 87-843 and
demonstrates to the Congress and to the Executive Branch how the federal agencies work
together to accomplish their missions in an effective and efficient manner. The goal is to
increase collaborative initiatives and eliminate unneeded duplication of effort among the
federal agencies. The BCR documents the Department of Energy’s operational and
research weather programs in several key service sectors and the responsibilities of the
DOE Meteorological Coordinating Council (DMCC), which the OFCM actively
supports.

 6. Space Weather Operations Research and Mitigation
OFCM has supported DOE participation in space weather related activities through
OFCM's management of the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) Space
Weather Operations, Research, and Mitigation (SWORM) Working Group. The core
space weather program within DOE and its national laboratories supports research,
applications, and operations in the detection of nuclear explosions from satellites. The
program develops and supplies federal agencies with instruments that are flown on the
Global Positioning System and other geosynchronous platforms to measure local plasma
and particle distributions and remote ionospheric electron content, all in near real time.
Related efforts also provide the databases, assimilative models, and scientific support to
the broader community, including research on possible impacts on electricity delivery
and reliability.

II. Accomplishments

In July 2020, the Executive Office of the President Office of Science and Technology
Policy (OSTP) and the Undersecretary of Commerce For Oceans and Atmosphere
established the dissolved the coordinating infrastructure managed by OFCM and
established Interagency Council for Advancing Meteorological Services (ICAMS) to
replace the coordinating infrastructure managed by the OFCM. In December 2020, the
ICAMS dissolved the Federal Committee for Meteorological Services and Supporting
Research (FCMSSR), the Interdepartmental Committee for Meteorological Services and
Supporting Research (ICMSSR) and the Earth System Prediction Capability (ESPC)
Executive Steering Group (ESG) assuming leadership responsibilities of these groups
through the OFCM which will be renamed as the Interagency Meteorological
Coordination Office (IMCO). All legacy OFCM coordination functions are being
transitioned to the new ICAMS infrastructure under IMCO management.

Accomplishments in this section refer to both the legacy OFCM functions and emerging
IMCO.

 1. ESPC
In its role as Earth System Prediction Capability (ESPC) Executive Steering Group

(ESG) Executive Secretary, OFCM ensured ESG member agency (including DOE)
awareness of ESPC sub-committee and working group efforts, including the High
Performance Computing, Coupled Global Modeling, and Common Model Architecture
groups. OFCM provided venues for DOE, federal agencies, and associated laboratories
and facilities to coordinate efforts on complex ESPC infrastructure and policy issues, and
facilitated definition of additional key focus areas to address national needs and priorities
as related to the global prediction challenge. OFCM also participated in coordinating
events and forums to ensure agency awareness engagement in the transition to the
National ESPC along with the rest of the Federal Weather Enterprise to the new
Interagency Council for Advancing Meteorological Services (ICAMS) framework. These
events included the multiagency Subseasonal to Seasonal Forecast Forum at the
American Geophysical Union fall meeting, and the AMS annual meeting.

Since in December 2020, OFCM has been supporting DOE as the functions of the ESPC
project and the leadership of the ESPC ESG are being transitioned under the newly
established Interagency Council for Advancing Meteorological Services (ICAMS).

 2. CIOS
OFCM coordinates the activities of several Federal Weather Enterprise stakeholder
organizations, including DOE, involved in environmental satellite systems and utilization
through our Committee for Operational Environmental Satellites (COES) and weather
radar through the NEXRAD Program Council. These functions will persist under the
ICAMS / IMCO paradigm.

Through the Interagency Coordinating Committee for Airborne Geophysical Research
Aircraft (ICCAGRA), OFCM supported the DOE Airborne Research programs using
piloted and Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAVs) by providing a direct link to the senior
level of the Federal Weather Enterprise. OFCM / IMCO will continue to support
coordination with ICCAGRA.

Through participation on the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC)
Subcommittee for U.S. Group for Earth Observations (USGEO), OFCM supported the
advancement of DOE’s observation developments, including the Satellite Needs Working
Group providing a means for DOE to leverage emerging NASA, NOAA, USGS, and
DoD technologies and systems, as well as the USGEO International Working Group to
provide DOE interagency perspective on foreign earth observing systems.

Additionally, following the 2018 Observational Data Workshop, OFCM’s Working
Group for Observational Data (WG/OD) began an interagency initiative, to be
coordinated through the Interdepartmental Committee for Meteorological Services and
Supporting Research (ICMSSR), to include DOE in a coordinated, interagency effort to
develop a U.S. systematic approach for assigning new U.S. WIGOS (WMO Integrated
Global Observing System) station identifiers. This is a multi-year, multi-agency effort.
The U.S. guidelines of agencies to transition to the new station IDs was published in the
Spring of 2020.

 3. ATD/Urban Meteorology
Due to COVID restrictions, the 24rd annual George Mason University (GMU)
Atmospheric Transport and Dispersion (ATD) Modeling Conference was cancelled,
hence, OFCM support was not provided. In lieu of the annual conference support, OFCM
continues to support interagency coordination on the status and plans of federal agencies’
atmospheric transport and dispersion experimental, observational, and modeling efforts.
OFCM continued to work with subject matter experts from the Department of Commerce
(NOAA and NIST); the Department of Defense, including the Army Research Laboratory
and the Navy Research Laboratory; the Department of Energy’s Brookhaven National
Laboratory; and NASA.

OFCM also participated in the 101st American Geophysical Union (AGU) Fall Meeting,
in December 2020. The conference addressed a wide array of scientific programs and
research, including atmospheric science developments, global environmental change,
Earth surface processes, hydrology, biogeosciences, and natural hazards. DOE provided
a presentation and discussion on the Integrated Coastal Sciences, which outlined the
numerous influences and impacts on coastline environment and infrastructure.

 4. Tropical Cyclone Operations and Research
OFCM hosted the annual Tropical Cyclone Operations Research Forum
(TCORF)/Interdepartmental Hurricane Conference (IHC), providing a forum for the
responsible federal agencies, together with representatives of the user communities such
as emergency management, to review the nation’s tropical cyclone forecast and warning
program, and to make recommendations on how to improve the program. The TCORF,
through the Working Group for Hurricane and Winter Storm Operations and Research,
addressed a variety of issues of the interagency user community, including DOE, such as
observations strategies, high resolution modeling requirements, and research on landfall
impacts. In addition, as part of the forum, OFCM coordinated a review of the upcoming
hurricane season and published the annual National Hurricane Operations Plan.

 5. Federal Meteorological Coordination
OFCM produced the FY2022 Annual Federal Weather Enterprise (FWE) Budget and
Coordination Report (BCR) which includes a crosscut of Agency budget information
related to meteorological services and supporting research. The report also describes
progress made by the FWE in support of the goals and objectives outlined in the Strategic
Plan for Federal Weather Coordination. The OFCM working group for the BCR, which
includes DOE representation, collaborates on specific inputs to the report and ensures the
DOE input to the report is comprehensive, correct and efficiently delivered to the Office
of Management and Budget and Congress.

 6. Space Weather Operations Research and Mitigation
The OFCM and the Space Weather Enterprise Forum (SWEF) Working Group hosted the
2020 Space Weather Enterprise Forum to address national needs through “whole of
government”, academic, and commercial partnerships. During the Forum, participants
shared information on space weather and its potential impacts to raise public awareness
and improve agency responses. The goal of the Forum is to make customers and

stakeholders, including DOE, cognizant of the impacts that space weather can have on
satellite, navigation, and communications systems.

OFCM also continued to serve as the Executive Secretary to provide ongoing
administrative support and services for the National Science and Technology Council
Space Weather Operations, Research and Mitigation (SWORM) Interagency Working
Group, ensuring the DOE and all other Federal Agency concerns are addressed with the
Executive Office of the President/Office of Science Technology and Policy.

III. Opportunities for Training and Professional Development

Numerous projects provide technical and professional development opportunities. The
OFCM participation in the American Geophysical Union Fall Meeting provided an
excellent opportunity to learn about the effects of the dispersion of chemical and other
weapons and their impacts across urban areas. The SWEF conference was open to the
public and is part of a larger opportunity for staying current on space policy issues. The
TCORF involves participation and outreach to other Federal agencies, emergency
managers, and the public.

IV. Results Disseminated to Communities

 1. ESPC
ESPC is briefed at a broad range of conferences including the AMS and the American
Geophysical Union annual meetings.

 2. CIOS
The ICCAGRA and USGEO representatives routinely meet with airborne research and
observation industry leaders to seek input and keep industry informed of Federal agency
efforts to better understand and develop observing systems.

Additionally, the Federal Weather Enterprise, along with the academic and private
sectors, will be kept apprised of the status of the U.S. transition to new WIGOS Station
IDs and provided guidance as it develops.

 3. ATD/Urban Meteorology
The summaries from prior year sessions are available from OFCM.

 4. TCORF
OFCM hosts the TCORF to bring together the Federal hurricane community: the
operational forecast centers; weather modelers; aerial, ocean, satellite, and radar
observing groups; and the supporting research functions. The summary report is available
on the OFCM web page.

 5. BCR

By Congressional mandate, plans are reported to Congress and disseminated to the
Federal Meteorological Enterprise and related constituents at large on an annual basis,
and are available on the OFCM web site.

 6. Space Weather
The Space Weather Enterprise Forum Summary Report was completed and posted on the
OFCM website.

V. Future Plans to Accomplish Goals

 1. ESPC
OFCM staff will work with DOE and the ESPC staff to continue existing National ESPC
working level activities to include High Performance Computing, Coupled Global
Modeling, and Common Model Architecture groups. OFCM will continue to do so until
an appropriate ICAMS body is established and ready to take on responsibility for
management and oversight of these groups.

 2. CIOS
OFCM staff will work with DOE and other agencies to ensure meteorological observing
functions are effectively transitioned to the ICAMS Committee for Observational
Systems and other ICAMS elements.

 3. ATD/Urban Meteorology
OFCM will support the 2021 GMU conference.

 4. TCORF
OFCM will support, plan, and coordinate the Annual Tropical Cyclone Operations
Research Forum (TCORF)/Interdepartmental Hurricane Conference (IHC), as well as
coordinate and publish the 2021 National Hurricane Operations Plan.

 5. BCR
OFCM/IMCO will coordinate and publish the FY2022 annual, Federal Weather
Enterprise (FWE) Budget and Coordination Report (BCR). The format and content of
this report are expected to be revised under guidance from the OMB and the newly
created, Interagency Council for Advancing Weather Services (ICAMS) within the
Office of Science and Technology Policy/EOP.

 6. Space Weather
Continue to support several actions in the Nation Space Weather Strategy and Action
Plan (NSW-SAP) published by the SWORM. Plan and host the 2022 SWEF.
