A class of weight modules over the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and gap- p p Virasoro algebras
Chengkang Xu, Fen Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct a class of simple weight modules over the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and gap- p p Virasoro algebras from restricted modules over some positive part subalgebra of the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra. These modules are new. In particular, when p = 2 p=2 , the gap- p p Virasoro algebra is the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and we obtain many new simple weight modules for the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra.

1.
Introduction

Representation theory of the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
{\mathbb{T}}
has been extensively studied for the past decades.
In
[
10
]
, irreducible Harish-Chandra modules were classified for
{\mathbb{T}}
. They are modules of intermediate series, highest weight modules or lowest weight modules.
Tensor products of modules of intermediate series and the highest weight modules were studied in
[
11
]
.
Non-weight modules over
{\mathbb{T}}
have also been considered by many mathematicians.
Generalized oscillator representations were constructed using restricted modules over the Heisenberg subalgebra in
[
11
]
.
Restricted modules over
{\mathbb{T}}
were classified in
[
2
,
4
]
and
[
12
]
separately.
They are essentially tensor products of restricted modules over the Virasoro algebra and restricted modules over the Heisenberg algebra,
or modules induced from some finite dimensional solvable subquotients of
{\mathbb{T}}
.

The mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra was first studied in
[
1
]
and named in
[
9
]
. Its structure is similar to the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra.
In
[
9
]
, the authors classified irreducible Harish-Chandra modules for the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathbb{M}
,
which are modules of intermediate series, highest weight modules or lowest weight modules.
They also classified irreducible restricted modules of level zero for
\mathbb{M}
.
Irreducible restricted modules of nonzero-level for
\mathbb{M}
are classified in
[
12
]
.
Like the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra,
irreducible restricted modules for
\mathbb{M}
are some tensor product modules or modules induced from some finite dimensional solvable subquotients of
\mathbb{M}
.
Tensor products of modules of intermediate series and highest weight modules of
\mathbb{M}
were studied in
[
6
]
.

Let
p>1
be an integer.
The gap-
p
Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}
was first introduced in
[
13
]
, where irreducible Harish-Chandra modules were classified.
They are modules of intermediate series, highest weight modules or lowest weight modules.
The algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has a close relation to the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and the algebra of derivations over a rational quantum torus.
Moreover, when
p=2
,
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathbb{M}
(see Section
5
).
Irreducible restricted modules of level zero are classified in
[
5
]
.
They are modules induced from some finite dimensional solvable subquotients of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Recently, some non-weight
\mathfrak{g}
-modules were classified in
[
14
]
,
including Whittaker modules and
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-free modules.

In
[
3
]
, a class of simple weight modules over the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
{\mathbb{T}}
with trivial central actions were constructed from irreducible restricted modules over some positive part subalgebras of
{\mathbb{T}}
.
In this paper, we use the same mechanism to construct simple weight modules with trivial central actions for the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
{\mathbb{T}}
, the gap-
p
Virasoro algebras (in particular, for the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathbb{M}
) from irreducible restricted modules over some positive part subalgebras of
{\mathbb{T}}
.
The modules constructed here for
{\mathbb{T}}
generalize the ones in
[
3
]
.
We determine the irreducibilities of these newly constructed modules and their isomorphism classes.
We also construct some non-weight modules for
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
by using a twisting technique
from
[
8
]
.

Let us now briefly describe how this paper is organized.
In Section 2, we recall the algebras
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
, and introduce some useful operators in
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathcal{U}({\mathbb{T}})
.
In Section 3, we give a construction of the above-mentioned modules for
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
, and prove that these modules are irreducible under a minor condition.
Section 4 is dedicated to determine the isomorphism classes of the modules over
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
,
and Section 5 is to demonstrate that these modules are new.
In the last section, we use the modules obtained in Section 3 to construct some non-weight modules for
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
.

Throughout this paper, the symbols
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
are to denote the sets of integers, non-negative integers, positive integers, complex numbers, and nonzero complex numbers, respectively.
All vector spaces and algebras are over
\mathbb{C}
and the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
is denoted by
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{L})
.
Finally, we fix a positive integer
p>1
for this paper,
and for any

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and gap-
p
Virasoro algebra, and introduce some related notations.

Definition 2.1
.

The
twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro
is a complex Lie algebra
{\mathbb{T}}
with a basis

\left\{\,L_{n},I_{n},C_{L},C_{LI},C_{I}\,\mid\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\,\right\}

and Lie brackets

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(n-m)L_{m+n}+\frac{1}{12}(m^{3}-m)C_{L}\delta_{m+n,0},

(2.1)

\displaystyle[L_{m},I_{n}]=nI_{m+n}+(m^{2}+m)C_{LI}\delta_{m+n,0},\ \quad[I_{m},I_{n}]=mC_{I}\delta_{m+n,0},

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
C_{L},C_{LI},C_{I}
being central.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the subspace

{\mathbb{T}}_{n}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{k},I_{n+k}\,\mid\,k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is a subalgebra of
{\mathbb{T}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A module
V
over the Lie algebra
{\mathbb{T}}_{n}
is called
restricted

if for any
v\in V
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L_{m}v=I_{m}v=0
for all
m\geq N.

Let
h,q\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
h\geq n
.
We say
V
has the
annihilating level

(h,q)
if

I_{h}V\neq 0,L_{q}V\neq 0
and
I_{k}V=L_{l}V=0
for all
k>h,l>q
.

Remark 2.3
.

(1) Let
V
be an irreducible
{\mathbb{T}}_{n}
-module with annihilating level
(h,q)
.
Then
I_{h}
acts on
V
as a linear isomorphism (see
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
).

(2) An example of irreducible restricted
{\mathbb{T}}_{0}
(or
{\mathbb{T}}_{1}
)-module of a fixed annihilating level was given in
[
3
, Example 3.4]
, and
a general construction of such modules was presented in
[
2
, section 4]
.

The gap-
p
Virasoro algebra may be realized as the universal central extension of some subalgebra of the centerless twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra

{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{k},I_{k}\,\mid\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

satisfying the Lie brackets in (
2.1
) with
C_{L},C_{LI},C_{I}
omitted (see
[
13
]
).

Definition 2.4
.

The
gap-
p
Virasoro algebra
is a complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a basis

\left\{L_{pn},I_{pn+i},C_{j}\,\mid\,n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq j\leq[\frac{p}{2}]\right\}

satisfying the following Lie brackets

\displaystyle[L_{pm},L_{pn}]=p(n-m)L_{pm+pn}+\frac{1}{12}(m^{3}-m)C_{0}\delta_{m+n,0},

\displaystyle[I_{pm+i},I_{pn+j}]=(pm+i)\delta_{i+j,p}\delta_{m+n+1,0}C_{\min\{i,p-i\}},\ \ \ \ \ [C_{k},\mathfrak{g}]=0,

\displaystyle[L_{pm},I_{pn+i}]=(pn+i)I_{pm+pn+i},

where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
1\leq i,j\leq p-1
,
0\leq k\leq[\frac{p}{2}]
, and

[\frac{p}{2}]
denotes the largest integer that is less than or equal to
\frac{p}{2}
.

Remark 2.5
.

(1) When
p=2
, the gap-2 Virasoro algebra is exactly the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra introduced in
[
1
]
and named in
[
9
]
.

(2) Since the modules studied in this paper have trivial central actions,
we may consider
\mathfrak{g}
as a subalgebra of
{\mathbb{T}}
by neglecting the central elements

C_{L},C_{LI},C_{I},C_{i},0\leq i\leq[\frac{p}{2}]
.
This is also the reason for us to denote elements in
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
in a same fashion.

Definition 2.6
.

A module
V
over the Lie algebra
{\mathbb{T}}
or
\mathfrak{g}
is called a
weight module

if
V
can be decomposed as a direct sum of eigenspaces under the
L_{0}
-action.

To end this section we introduce some operators in
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathcal{U}({\mathbb{T}})
for later use.
For any
s\in\mathbb{N},l,m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
1\leq i,j\leq p-1
, define

\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,s)}=\sum_{k=0}^{s}(-1)^{s-k}\binom{s}{k}I_{pl-pm-pk+i}I_{pm+pk+j}.

(2.2)

Clearly,
\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,0)}=I_{pl-pm+i}I_{pm+j}
and it is easy to prove the following recursive formula

\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,s)}=\Omega_{l,m+1}^{(i,j,s-1)}-\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,s-1)}\quad\text{ for }s>0.

(2.3)

3.
Constructing weight modules

In this section we construct some weight modules over
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
from restricted
{\mathbb{T}}_{0}
(or
{\mathbb{T}}_{1}
)-modules, and determine their irreducibility.

Fix a
p
-tuple
{\bm{d}}=(d_{0},d_{1},\dots,d_{p-1})\in\{0,1\}^{p}

and a subset
P
of
\{0,1,\dots,{p-1}\}
in this section.
Denote

\min\{{\bm{d}}\}=\min\{d_{0},d_{1},\dots,d_{p-1}\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}=\min\{d_{i}\mid i\in P\}.

Then we have subalgebras
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}}\}}
and
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}
of
{\mathbb{T}}
, and
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}\subseteq{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}}\}}
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C}
,
V
be a restricted
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}}\}}
-module and let
\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
denote the Laurent polynomial ring in the indeterminant
x
.
We define a
{\mathbb{T}}
-action on the space
V\otimes\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
by

\displaystyle L_{m}\cdot v(k)=\left(a+k+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}L_{j}\right)v(m+k);

(3.1)

\displaystyle I_{pm+i}\cdot v(k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(pm+i)^{j+d_{i}}}{(j+d_{i})!}I_{j+d_{i}}v(pm+i+k);

\displaystyle C_{L}\cdot v(k)=C_{LI}\cdot v(k)=C_{I}\cdot v(k)=0,

where
m,k\in\mathbb{Z},v\in V,0\leq i\leq p-1
and
v(k)=v\otimes x^{k}
.

Denote

\mathbb{C}[P]=\bigoplus_{r\in P}x^{r}\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm p}]\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{P}=\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\overline{k}\in P\right\}=\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid x^{k}\in\mathbb{C}[P]\right\}.

Consider
V
as a
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}
-module and
we define a
\mathfrak{g}
-action on the space
V\otimes\mathbb{C}[P]
by

\displaystyle L_{pm}\cdot v(k)=\left(a+k+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(pm)^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}L_{j}\right)v(pm+k);

(3.2)

\displaystyle I_{pm+i}\cdot v(k)=\delta_{\overline{i+k},P}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(pm+i)^{j+d_{i}}}{(j+d_{i})!}I_{j+d_{i}}v(pm+i+k);

\displaystyle C_{l}\cdot v(k)=0,

where
m\in\mathbb{Z},k\in\mathcal{P},v\in V,1\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq l\leq[\frac{p}{2}]
and

\delta_{j,P}
is the Kronecker symbol defined by

\delta_{j,P}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }j\in P;\\
0&\text{ if }j\notin P.\end{cases}

Since
V
is restricted, the actions in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)
contain only finitely many terms, hence make sense.
Furthermore, we have the following

Proposition 3.1
.

(1) The actions in (
3.1
) make
V\otimes\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
a weight
{\mathbb{T}}
-module.

(2) The actions in (
3.2
) make
V\otimes\mathbb{C}[P]
a weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

Proof.

(1) For any
k,m,n\in\mathbb{Z},0\leq i,j\leq p-1
and
v\in V
, it is clear that

\left(I_{pm+i}I_{pn+j}-I_{pn+j}I_{pm+i}\right)\cdot v(k)=0,

and we have

\displaystyle\left(L_{m}I_{pn+i}-I_{pn+i}L_{m}\right)\cdot v(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(a+pn+i+k+\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{q+1}}{(q+1)!}L_{q}\right)\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(pn+i)^{l+d_{i}}}{(l+d_{i})!}I_{l+d_{i}}\right)v(m+pn+i+k)

\displaystyle-\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(pn+i)^{l+d_{i}}}{(l+d_{i})!}I_{l+d_{i}}\right)\left(a+k+\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{q+1}}{(q+1)!}L_{q}\right)v(m+pn+i+k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(pn+i)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(pn+i)^{l+d_{i}}}{(l+d_{i})!}I_{l+d_{i}}v(m+pn+i+k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\frac{(pn+i)^{l+d_{i}}m^{q+1}}{(l+d_{i})!(q+1)!}[L_{q},I_{l+d_{i}}]v(m+pn+i+k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(pn+i)\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=\delta_{d_{i},0}}^{q+1}\frac{(pn+i)^{l+d_{i}-1}m^{q-l+1}}{(l+d_{i}-1)!(q-l+1)!}I_{q+d_{i}}v(m+pn+i+k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(pn+i)\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m+pn+i)^{q+d_{i}}}{(q+d_{i})!}I_{q+d_{i}}v(m+pn+i+k)=(pn+i)I_{m+pn+i}\cdot v(k).

Furthermore, for any
k,m,n\in\mathbb{Z},v\in V
, we have

\displaystyle(L_{m}L_{n}-L_{n}L_{m})\cdot v(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(a+n+k+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{i+1}}{(i+1)!}L_{i}\right)\left(a+k+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}L_{j}\right)v(m+n+k)

\displaystyle-\left(a+m+k+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}L_{j}\right)\left(a+k+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{i+1}}{(i+1)!}L_{i}\right)v(m+n+k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-m)(a+k)v(m+n+k)+\left(n\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}-m\sum\frac{m^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}\right)L_{j}v(m+n+k)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{m^{i+1}n^{j+1}}{(i+1)!(j+1)!}(j-i)L_{i+j}v(m+n+k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-m)(a+k)v(m+n+k)

\displaystyle+\left(n\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{j+1}\frac{m^{i+1}n^{j-i}}{(i+1)!(j-i)!}-m\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{j+1}\frac{m^{i}n^{j-i+1}}{i!(j-i+1)!}\right)L_{j}v(m+n+k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-m)\left(a+k+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m+n)^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}L_{j}\right)v(m+n+k)=(n-m)L_{m+n}\cdot v(k).

This shows that
V\otimes\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
is a
{\mathbb{T}}
-module.
Since
L_{0}\cdot v(k)=(a+k)v(k)
for any
v\in V,k\in\mathbb{Z}
and

V\otimes\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}V\otimes x^{k},

we see that
V\otimes\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
is a weight
{\mathbb{T}}
-module.

(2) The proof is similar to that of (1) and we omit the details.
∎

We denote by
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
the
{\mathbb{T}}
-module defined in (
3.1
),
and by
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
the
\mathfrak{g}
-module defined in (
3.2
) respectively.
In the following, we determine their irreducibility.
To do this, we need the following result about the operator
\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,s)}
defined in (
2.2
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
l,m\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i,j\leq p-1
and
V
be a
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}}\}}
-module of annihilating level
(h,q)
and
v\in V
. Then we have the following equations

\displaystyle\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,2h)}\cdot v(k)=(-1)^{h}p^{2h}\binom{2h}{h}I_{h}^{2}v(pl+k+i+j),

\displaystyle\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,s)}\cdot v(k)=0\quad\text{ for }s>2h

on the
{\mathbb{T}}
-module
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
and on the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
provided further that
k,j+k,i+j+k\in\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Consider
\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,s)}\cdot v(k)
as a polynomial in
m
with coefficients in
V\otimes x^{pl+k+i+j}
.
Using induction on
s\geq 0
and (
2.3
),
one can prove that the leading term of
\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,s)}\cdot v(k)
is

\frac{(-1)^{h}p^{2h}m^{2h-s}(2h)!}{(h!)^{2}(2h-s)!}I_{h}^{2}v(pl+k+i+j).

Then the lemma follows by taking
s=2h
.
∎

Now we can prove the main results in this section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C},h,q\in\mathbb{N},{\bm{d}}=(d_{0},d_{1},\cdots,d_{p-1})\in\{0,1\}^{p}
and
P\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
be such that
h\geq\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}
.
Let
V
be a
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}
-module with annihilating level
(h,q)
.
Then the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
is irreducible if and only if the
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}
-module
V
is irreducible.

Proof.

The “only if” part is clear and to prove the “if” part we assume
W
is a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
. Since
W
is a weight module, we may write

W=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathcal{P}}\tilde{W}_{k}\otimes x^{k},

where
\tilde{W}_{k}
’s are subspaces of
V
. Set

\tilde{W}=\bigcap_{k\in\mathcal{P}}\tilde{W}_{k}.

We claim that
\tilde{W}\neq 0
.
Pick a nonzero vector
v(k)\in W
for some
k\in\mathcal{P},v\in\tilde{W}_{k}
.
For any
l,m\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i,j\leq p-1
such that
\overline{j+k},\overline{i+j+k}\in P
,
we have by Lemma
3.2
and Remark
2.3

\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,2h)}\cdot v(k)=(-1)^{h}p^{2h}\binom{2h}{h}I_{h}^{2}v(pl+k+i+j)\in\tilde{W}_{pl+k+i+j}\otimes x^{pl+k+i+j}\setminus\{0\},

which implies
I_{h}^{2}v\in\tilde{W}
, proving the claim.

For any
v\in\tilde{W},l,m\in\mathbb{Z},k\in\mathcal{P},i\in P\setminus\{0\}
such that
\overline{k-i}\in P
, we have

v(k-pm)\in\tilde{W}_{k-pm}\otimes x^{k-pm}\quad\text{ and }\quad v(k-pm-i)\in\tilde{W}_{k-pm-i}\otimes x^{k-pm-i}.

From the following equations

\displaystyle L_{pm}\cdot v(k-pm)=\left(a+k-pm+\sum_{j=0}^{q}\frac{(pm)^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}L_{j}\right)v(k)\in\tilde{W}_{k}\otimes x^{k},

\displaystyle I_{pm+i}\cdot v(k-pm-i)=\sum_{j=0}^{h-d_{i}}\frac{(pm+i)^{j+d_{i}}}{(j+d_{i})!}I_{j+d_{i}}v(k)\in\tilde{W}_{k}\otimes x^{k},

we see that

\sum_{j=0}^{q}\frac{(pm)^{j+1}}{(j+1)!}L_{j}v\in\tilde{W}_{k}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{j=0}^{h-d_{i}}\frac{(pm+i)^{j+d_{i}}}{(j+d_{i})!}I_{j+d_{i}}v\in\tilde{W}_{k}\quad\text{ for any }m\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1.

Thus
L_{j}v,I_{j+\min\{{\bm{d}},P\setminus\{0\}\}}v\in\tilde{W}_{k}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N},k\in\mathcal{P}
.
So
L_{j}v,I_{j+\min\{{\bm{d}},P\setminus\{0\}\}}v\in\tilde{W}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
This proves that
\tilde{W}
is a nonzero
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\setminus\{0\}\}}
-submodule of
V
.

If
\min\{{\bm{d}},P\setminus\{0\}\}>\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}
, such case happens only when

0\in P,d_{0}=0
and
d_{i}=1
for all
i\in P\setminus\{0\}
,
or equivalently,
I_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}\setminus{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\setminus\{0\}\}}
.
Notice that
I_{0}
acts on
V
as a fixed scalar since

I_{0}
is central in
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}
and
V
is irreducible. So
I_{0}v\in\tilde{W}
and
\tilde{W}
is a nonzero
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}
-submodule of
V
.
So
\tilde{W}=V
by the irreducibility of
V
, and hence
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
is irreducible.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C},h,q\in\mathbb{N},{\bm{d}}=(d_{0},d_{1},\cdots,d_{p-1})\in\{0,1\}^{p}
and
let
V
be a
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}}\}}
-module with annihilating level
(h,q)
.
Then the
{\mathbb{T}}
-module
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
is irreducible if and only if the
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}}\}}
-module
V
is irreducible.

Proof.

The “only if” is clear. To prove the “if” part we
consider
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
as the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
with
P=\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
,
which is irreducible by Theorem
3.3
.
So
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
is irreducible as a
{\mathbb{T}}
-module.
∎

4.
Isomorphism classes

In this section we determine the isomorphism classes of the modules constructed in the above section for the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and gap-
p
Virasoro algebras.
We first do this for the gap-
p
Virasoro algebras.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\bm{d}},{\bm{e}}\in\{0,1\}^{p},a,b\in\mathbb{C},h,q,n,t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h\geq\min\{{\bm{d}}\},n\geq\min\{{\bm{e}}\}
,
and
P,Q
be subsets of
\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
.
Let
V
be an irreducible
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}}\}}
-module of annihilating level
(h,q)
and
W
be an irreducible
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{e}}\}}
-module of annihilating level
(n,t)
.
Then
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)\cong{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(W,b,{\bm{e}},Q\right)
as
\mathfrak{g}
-modules if and only if
a-b\in\mathbb{Z},h=n,q=t,Q=\sigma^{a-b}(P),d_{i}=e_{i}
for all
i\in P-P
and
V\cong W
as
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P-P\}}
-modules,
where
P-P=\left\{\overline{i-j}\mid i,j\in P\right\}
and
\sigma
is the permutation
(0,1,\dots,p-1)
acting on the set
\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
.

Proof.

The “if” part follows from the fact that the linear map
\varphi:{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)\longrightarrow{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(W,b,{\bm{e}},Q\right)
defined by

\varphi(v(l))=\psi(v)(l+a-b)\quad\text{ for any }v\in V,l\in\mathbb{Z},

where
\psi:V\longrightarrow W
is a
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P-P\}}
-module isomorphism,
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module isomorphism.

In the next we prove the “only if” part.
Let
\varphi:{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)\longrightarrow{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(W,b,{\bm{e}},Q\right)
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module isomorphism.
For any
v\in V,m\in\mathcal{P}
, write

\varphi(v(m))=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{Q}}w_{i,m}(i),

where
\mathcal{Q}=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\overline{k}\in Q\},w_{i,m}\in W
and only finitely many
w_{i,m}
’s are nonzero.
For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(a+m)^{k}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{Q}}w_{i,m}(i)=\varphi\left(L_{0}^{k}\cdot v(m)\right)=L_{0}^{k}\cdot\varphi\left(v(m)\right)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{Q}}(b+i)^{k}w_{i,m}(i).

This implies
a-b\in\mathbb{Z},Q=\sigma^{a-b}(P)
and

\varphi(v(m))=w_{m+a-b,m}(m+a-b)\in W\otimes x^{m+a-b}\quad\text{for any }v\in V,m\in\mathbb{Z}

For any
i\in P
, we may define linear maps
\psi_{i}:V\longrightarrow V
by

\varphi(v(i))=\psi_{i}(v)(i+a-b)\quad\text{for any }v\in V.

Clearly, all
\psi_{i}
’s are linear isomorphisms.

For any
l,m\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i,j\leq p-1,v\in V,k\in\mathcal{P}
such that
j+k\in\mathcal{P},i+j+k\in\mathcal{P}
, from the equation

\displaystyle\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,2h)}\cdot\left(\psi_{\overline{k}}(v)(k+a-b)\right)=\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,2h)}\cdot\varphi(v(k))=\varphi\left(\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,2h)}\cdot v(k)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{h}p^{2h}\binom{2h}{h}\varphi\left(I_{h}^{2}v(pl+i+j+k)\right)\in W\otimes x^{pl+i+j+k+a-b}\setminus\{0\}

we see by Lemma
3.2
that
h\leq n
.
Apply
\varphi^{-1}
to the above argument and we get
n\leq q
. Hence
h=n
.
Then the above equation becomes

\varphi\left(I_{h}^{2}v(pl+i+j+k)\right)=I_{h}^{2}\psi_{\overline{k}}(v)(pl+i+j+k+a-b)

by Lemma
3.2
, or (noticing that
I_{h}
acts on
V
as a linear isomorphism)

\varphi\left(v(pl+i+j+k)\right)=I_{h}^{2}\psi_{\overline{k}}\left(I_{h}^{-2}v\right)(pl+i+j+k+a-b)

for any
l\in\mathbb{Z},v\in V,1\leq i,j\leq p-1,k\in\mathcal{P}
such that
j+k\in\mathcal{P},i+j+k\in\mathcal{P}
.
Take
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
pl+i+j+k=\overline{i+j+k}
and we get

\psi_{\overline{i+j+k}}(v)=I_{h}^{2}\psi_{\overline{k}}\left(I_{h}^{-2}v\right),

which implies that
\psi_{i}=\psi_{j}
for all
i,j\in P
.
Set
\psi=\psi_{i}
for any
i\in P
.
Then we have

\varphi(v(l))=\psi(x)(l+a-b)\quad\text{ for any }v\in V,l\in\mathcal{P}.

From
\varphi(L_{pm}\cdot v(j-pm))=L_{pm}\cdot\varphi(v(j-pm))
with
m\in\mathbb{Z},v\in V,j\in P
,
we have

\psi\left(\left(a+j-pm+\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{(pm)^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}L_{k}\right)v\right)=\left(a+j-pm+\sum_{k=0}^{t}\frac{(pm)^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}L_{k}\right)\psi(v).

This implies that
q=t
and

\sum_{k=0}^{q}\frac{(pm)^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}(\psi(L_{k}v)-L_{k}\psi(v))=0\quad\text{ for any }m\in\mathbb{Z}.

So
\psi(L_{k}v)=L_{k}\psi(v)
for any
v\in V,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

From
\varphi(I_{pm+i}\cdot v(j-pm))=I_{pm+i}\cdot\varphi(v(j-pm))
with
m\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1,j\in\ P
and
\overline{i+j}\in P
, we see that

\sum_{k=0}^{h-d_{i}}\frac{(pm+i)^{k+d_{i}}}{(k+d_{i})!}\psi(I_{k+d_{i}}v)=\sum_{k=0}^{h-e_{i}}\frac{(pm+i)^{k+e_{i}}}{(k+e_{i})!}I_{k+e_{i}}\psi(v)\quad\text{ for any }m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Consider the both sides of the above equation as polynomials in
m
with coefficients in
W
, and we get that
d_{i}=e_{i}
for all
i\in P-P
, and
\psi(I_{k+d_{i}}v)=I_{k+d_{i}}\psi(v)
for any
k\geq 0
.
Equivalently,

\psi(I_{k}v)=I_{k}\psi(v)\quad\text{ for any }v\in V,k\geq\min\{{\bm{d}},P-P\}.

This proves that
\psi
is a
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P-P\}}
-module isomorphism and we complete the proof.
∎

Now, we determine the isomorphism classes of the modules
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
for
{\mathbb{T}}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\bm{d}},{\bm{e}},a,b,h,q,n,t,V,W
be as in Theorem
4.1
.
Then
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)\cong{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(W,b,{\bm{e}}\right)
as
{\mathbb{T}}
-modules if and only if
a-b\in\mathbb{Z},h=n,q=t,{\bm{d}}={\bm{e}}
and
V\cong W
as
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}}\}}
-modules.

Proof.

Notice that the statement that
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)\cong{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(W,b,{\bm{e}}\right)
as
{\mathbb{T}}
-modules implies that the
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
and
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(W,b,{\bm{e}},Q\right)
are isomorphic with
P=Q=\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
.
Then the proof follows from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}v
be a
{\mathbb{T}}_{0}
-module with
L_{0}v=bv,I_{0}v=cv,L_{k}v=I_{k}v=0
for
k>0
.

(1) Then there is a
{\mathbb{T}}
-module structure on
v\otimes\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
with actions

L_{m}\cdot v(k)=(a+k+bm)v(m+k),\ I_{m}\cdot v(k)=cv(m+k)\quad\text{ for any }m,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

The resulting
{\mathbb{T}}
-module is the module
A_{a,b,c}
of intermediate series (see
[
7
]
).

(2) Let
P
be a subset of
\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
and
(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{p-1})\in\{0,1\}^{p-1}
such that

\min\{d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{p-1}\}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{i+j}\in P\text{ provided that }j\in P,d_{i}=0.

Let
F=(F_{i,j})
be the
(p-1)\times p
matrix with indexes
1\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq j\leq p-1
and
F_{i,j}=c\delta_{d_{i},0}\delta_{j,P}
.
Then there is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
v\otimes\mathbb{C}[P]
with actions

L_{pm}\cdot v(k)=(a+k+bpm)v(m+k),\ I_{pm+i}\cdot v(k)=F_{i,\overline{k}}v(pm+i+k)\quad\text{ for }m\in\mathbb{Z},k\in\mathcal{P},1\leq i\leq p-1.

The resulting
\mathfrak{g}
-module is the module
V(a,b,F)
of intermediate series constructed in
[
13
]
.

5.
The modules are new

In this section we demonstrate that the modules constructed in Section
3
are new modules for both
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
.

The
{\mathbb{T}}
-modules
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
generalize the ones
M(V,a)
in
[
3
]
.
As far as we know, these modules are new simple weight modules for
{\mathbb{T}}
.

For
p>2
, the only kind of weight modules over the gap-
p
Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}
studied before this paper to the best of our knowledge are the Harish-Chandra modules.
Notice that any irreducible
{\mathbb{T}}_{0}
(or
{\mathbb{T}}_{1}
)-module
V
must be infinite dimensional if
\dim V>1
.
Therefore, the
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
with
\dim V>1
are new simple weight modules for
\mathfrak{g}
with infinite dimensional weight spaces.

In the rest of this section, we set
p=2
, in which case
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra.

Definition 5.1
.

The mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra is a complex Lie algebra
\mathbb{M}
with a basis

\left\{d_{i},h_{r},\bm{c},\bm{l}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z},r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}\right\}

and commutation relations

\displaystyle[d_{m},d_{n}]=(m-n)d_{m+n}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\bm{c}\delta_{m+n,0};

\displaystyle[d_{m},h_{r}]=-rh_{m+r};\quad[h_{r},h_{s}]=r\bm{l}\delta_{r+s,0},\quad[\bm{c},\mathbb{M}]=[\bm{l},\mathbb{M}]=0,

where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z},r,s\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
.

One can see easily that the linear map defined by

L_{2m}\mapsto 2d_{-m},\quad I_{2m+1}\mapsto 2h_{-m-\frac{1}{2}},\quad C_{0}\mapsto 4\bm{c},\quad C_{1}\mapsto-2\bm{l}

for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, is a Lie algebra isomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathbb{M}
.

Since the modules
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
for
\mathfrak{g}
with
\dim V>1
have infinite dimensional weight spaces,
they are not the Harish-Chandra modules classified in
[
9
]
,
and they are certainly not the non-highest-weight-type restricted modules studied in
[
2
,
4
]
and
[
12
]
.

In
[
6
]
, tensor products of irreducible highest weight modules and modules of intermediate series over
\mathbb{M}
were studied.
These modules have infinite dimensional weight spaces.
We recall highest weight modules and modules of intermediate series over
\mathbb{M}
in the following.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C},\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and let
Q
be a nonempty subset of
\{0,1\}
.
Then the vector space
A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q)=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{v_{r+2k}\mid r\in Q,k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
carries a
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure with actions

\displaystyle L_{2m}v_{r+2k}=(\alpha+2\beta m+2k+r)v_{r+2m+2k};\quad\bm{c}v_{k}=\bm{l}v_{k}=0;

\displaystyle I_{2m+1}v_{r+2k}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }Q=\{0\}\text{ or }Q=\{1\};\\
v_{2m+2n+1}&\text{ if }Q=\{0,1\},r=0;\\
\gamma v_{2m+2n+2}&\text{ if }Q=\{0,1\},r=1\end{cases}

for
m,n,k\in\mathbb{Z},r\in Q
.
The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q)
is reducible if and only if

Q\neq\{0,1\},\alpha\in 2\mathbb{Z}
and
\beta\in\{0,1\}
.
We denote by
A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q)
the unique irreducible subquotient of
A(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q)
.
Recall the operator
\Omega_{l,m}^{(i,j,s)}
in (
2.2
) and it is easy to check that

\Omega_{l,m}^{(1,1,s)}u=0\quad\text{for any }l,m\in\mathbb{Z},s\in\mathbb{N},u\in A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q).

(5.1)

The following result is from
[
9
, Theorem 3.9]
and
[
13
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Any irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module of intermediate series is isomorphic to
A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q)
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C},\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{\times},\emptyset\neq Q\subseteq\{0,1\}
.

Set
\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{2m},I_{2m+1},C_{0},C_{1}\mid m\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
.
For
c,h,l\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\mathbb{C}\bm{1}
be the 1-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
-module with

L_{0}\bm{1}=h\bm{1},\quad C_{0}\bm{1}=c\bm{1},\quad C_{1}\bm{1}=l\bm{1},\quad L_{2m}\bm{1}=I_{2m-1}\bm{1}=0\quad\text{ for any }m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

The Verma module over
\mathfrak{g}
is defined to be

M(c,h,l)=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{+})}\mathbb{C}\bm{1}.

Denote by
J
the unique maximal submodule of
M(c,h,l)
and set

L(c,h,l)=M(c,h,l)/J,

which is called the highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module of level
(c,h,l)
.
Denote by
\overline{\bm{1}}
the image of
\bm{1}
in
L(c,h,l)
.

Irreducibility of the tensor product
L(c,h,l)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q)
was given in
[
6
, Theorem 3.11]
.
In the following proposition we prove that the modules studied in Section 3 are not isomorphic to any of these tensor product modules.
Thus we conclude that a new class of irreducible weight modules for the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra are constructed.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C},{\bm{d}}=(d_{0},d_{1})\in\{0,1\}^{2},P\subseteq\{0,1\}
and let
V
be an irreducible
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}
-module of annihilating level
(n,t)
.
Then the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
is not isomorphic to
L(c,h,l)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q)
for any
c,h,l,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C},\emptyset\neq Q\subseteq\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

Take
l,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
l-m-n>0
.
Then it is easy to see that
I_{2m+2k+1}\overline{\bm{1}}=I_{2l-2m-2k+1}\overline{\bm{1}}=0
for any
0\leq k\leq n
.
For any
u\in A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q)
, it follows from (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\Omega_{l,m}^{(1,1,2n)}(\overline{\bm{1}}\otimes u)=\sum_{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^{2n-k}\binom{2n}{k}I_{2l-2m-2k+1}I_{2m+2k+1}(\overline{\bm{1}}\otimes u)

\displaystyle\ =\overline{\bm{1}}\otimes\left(\sum_{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^{2n-k}\binom{2n}{k}I_{2l-2m-2k+1}I_{2m+2k+1}u\right)=\overline{\bm{1}}\otimes\left(\Omega_{l,m}^{(1,1,2n)}u\right)=0.

On the other hand, the operator
\Omega_{l,m}^{(1,1,2n)}
acts on
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
as a linear isomorphism by Lemma
3.2
.
So
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
can not be isomorphic to any
L(c,h,l)\otimes A^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,Q)
.
∎

6.
Some irreducible non-weight modules

The purpose of this section is to construct a new class of non-weight modules with trivial central actions for
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathfrak{g}
by using the twisting technique in
[
8
]
to the modules
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
and
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
.

Let
f=\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
. There is a new
{\mathbb{T}}
-module structure on
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)

defined by

L_{m}\circ v=\left(L_{m}+\sum_{i}a_{i}I_{m+i}\right)\cdot v;\quad I_{m}\circ v=I_{m}\cdot v\quad\text{for any }m\in\mathbb{Z},v\in{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right).

The resulting
{\mathbb{T}}
-module is denoted by
{M}^{f}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\bm{d}}\in\{0,1\}^{p},a\in\mathbb{C}
and
V
be an irreducible
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}}\}}
-module of some fixed annihilating level.
Then for any
f\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
, the
{\mathbb{T}}
-module
{M}^{f}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
is irreducible.

Proof.

Denote

\alpha=\sum_{i}\frac{a_{i}}{i}I_{i}\in{\mathbb{T}}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{\alpha}=\exp(\text{ad}\alpha).

Then one can see that the
{\mathbb{T}}
-module
{M}^{f}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
is the
\theta_{\alpha}
-twist of
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
, i.e. we have

u\circ v=\theta_{\alpha}(u)\cdot v\quad\text{for any}\quad u\in{\mathbb{T}},v\in{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right).

Then the irreducibility of
{M}^{f}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
is equivalent to that of
{M}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
,
which follows from Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Let
g=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
. There is a new
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
defined by

L_{pm}\circ v=\left(L_{pm}+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}}a_{i}I_{pm+i}\right)\cdot v;\quad I_{pm+j}\circ v=I_{2m+j}\cdot v

for any
1\leq j\leq p-1,m\in\mathbb{Z},v\in{M}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
.
We denote the resulting
\mathfrak{g}
-module by
M^{g}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
{\bm{d}}\in\{0,1\}^{p},a\in\mathbb{C},P\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
and
V
be an irreducible
{\mathbb{T}}_{\min\{{\bm{d}},P\}}
-module of some fixed annihilating level.
Then for any
g=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus p\mathbb{Z}}a_{i}x^{i}\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
, the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M^{g}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
is irreducible.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
6.1
and we omit the details.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

(1) The
{\mathbb{T}}
-modules
{M}^{f}_{\mathbb{T}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}}\right)
generalize the ones studied in
[
3
, Section 5]
, hence are not isomorphic to the restricted modules studied in
[
2
,
4
]
and
[
12
]
(see
[
3
, Proposition 5.1]
).
Thus they are new non-weight modules for
{\mathbb{T}}
.

(2) By a similar proof to Proposition
5.3
, one can show that
the
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
M^{g}_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(V,a,{\bm{d}},P\right)
are not isomorphic to the restricted modules studied in
[
5
]
if
p>2
,
and not isomorphic to the restricted modules studied in
[
12
]
if
p=2
.
Thus they are new non-weight modules for
\mathfrak{g}
.
