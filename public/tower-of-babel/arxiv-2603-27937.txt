On the locally analytic completed cohomology of modular curves
Lue Pan

Abstract
. We survey our works on the locally analytic vectors of completed cohomology of modular curves.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number. Emerton introduced the (
p
-adically) completed cohomology in
[
17
,
19
]
which he described as “a suitable surrogate for a space of
p
-adic automorphic forms”. In his highly influential unpublished manuscript
[
18
]
, Emerton carefully studied the completed cohomology of modular curves and successfully applied it to attack the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture. We briefly recall its definition. Let
K
be an open subgroup
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
and consider the modular curve of level
K

Y_{K}(\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\left((\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K\right).

Throughout this paper fix an open compact subgroup
K^{p}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})
. The completed cohomology of tame level
K^{p}
is defined as

\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p}):=\varprojlim_{n}\varinjlim_{K_{p}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}H^{i}(Y_{K^{p}K_{p}}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}).

It is
p
-adically complete and equipped with natural actions of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}
(generated by spherical Hecke operators). Moreover because modular curves are algebraic varieties canonically defined over
\mathbb{Q}
,
\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})
also admits a natural action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}=\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
.
The key question here is to understand how the action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
interacts with other two symmetries.
Emerton’s approach is to show the interplay between the actions of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=\Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is compatible with the
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
established by Breuil, Colmez, Berger, Kisin, Paškūnas…

In our recent works
[
27
,
29
]
we found a new approach to understand the completed cohomology. Our method is based on the study of the
p
-adic geometry of the modular curve with infinite level at
p
introduced by Scholze in
[
33
]
. Roughly speaking, we adapt Sen’s method (reformulated by Berger-Colmez in
[
5
,
6
]
) to study the locally analytic functions at the infinite level and show that the locally analytic vectors of the completed cohomology, as a
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-representation, can be “localized” (in the sense of Beilinson-Bernstein
[
3
]
) naturally to certain
D
-modules on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. A majority of our works is about understanding the structures of these
D
-modules. Our method has applications to the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture, study of overconvergent modular forms and the
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence.

We will review our works below and discuss some recent development in the field.

2.
Sen theory and localization of locally analytic completed cohomology

2.1.
Relative/Geometric Sen theory

In
[
27
, §3]
, we explain how to adapt Sen’s construction of the Sen operator for
p
-adic Galois representations to define a canonical Higgs field over the pro-étale site of a smooth affinoid one-dimensional rigid analytic variety and call it “Relative Sen theory”. Later Rodriguez Camargo
[
31
]
systematically generalize the method to arbitrary dimension and call it “Geometric Sen theory”. We summarize some of the results here. Denote by
C
the completion of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid analytic variety over
C
of dimension
d
and
V
a pro-étale
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-local system on
X
. One can canonically define a map on the pro-étale site
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}

\theta_{V}:V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\Omega^{1}_{X}(-1),

where
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
denotes the complete structural sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
and
(-1)
denotes the inverse of the Tate twist, satisfying the following

(1)

\theta_{V}
is a Higgs field, i.e.
\theta_{V}
is
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear and
\theta_{V}\wedge\theta_{V}=0
.

(2)

\theta_{V}
is functorial in
V
. Moreover if
W
is another
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-local system, then
\theta_{V\oplus W}=(\theta_{V},\theta_{W})
and
\theta_{V\otimes W}=\theta_{V}\otimes 1+1\otimes\theta_{W}
.

(3)

Let
\nu:X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}\to X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
denote the projection of sites. Then
\nu_{*}(V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=\nu_{*}\ker\theta_{V}
.

(4)

\theta_{V}
can be explicitly described in local toric charts.

Remark 2.2
.

When
X
has a log structure defined by a normal crossing divisor, there is a logarithmic version of the theorem over the pro-Kummer-étale site.

Remark 2.3
.

V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is a pro-étale vector bundle.
[
31
]
actually constructs Higgs fields for general pro-étale vector bundles and certain infinite-dimensional vector bundles
[
31
, Def. 1.0.1]
, and shows that the Higgs field vanishes if and only the pro-étale vector bundle descends to the étale site.

Remark 2.4
.

For part (3), one can compute higher
R^{i}\nu_{*}(V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
as well using the Higgs complex
[
31
, Th.1.0.3 ]
.

Remark 2.5
.

If
G
is a compact
p
-adic Lie group and
\tilde{X}
is a pro-étale
G
-torsor of
X
, one can take
V
to be the local system associated to the space of locally analytic functions on
G
(which is infinite-dimensional but the construction of
\theta_{V}
works here). Now
H^{0}(\nu_{*}(V\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X}))=\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\tilde{X})^{\mathrm{la}}
computes the
G
-locally analytic functions of
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\tilde{X})
. Part (3) says that they satisfy the first-order differential equation defined by
\theta_{V}
. We call it the
p
-adic Cauchy-Riemann equation. This point of view is inspired by the interpretation of the Sen operator in
[
6
]
.

Remark 2.6
.

The explicit description in part (4) is as follows: suppose
U=\Spa(A,A^{+})\subseteq X
is an affinoid and admits a morphism to
Y:=\Spa(C\langle T_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,T_{d}^{\pm 1}\rangle,O_{C}\langle T_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,T_{d}^{\pm 1}\rangle)
which can be written as a composite of finite
étale maps and rational localizations. Let
U_{\infty}
denote
the pullback of the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{d}
-cover
\Spa(C\langle T_{1}^{\pm 1/p^{\infty}},\cdots,T_{d}^{\pm 1/p^{\infty}}\rangle,O_{C}\langle T_{1}^{\pm 1/p^{\infty}},\cdots,T_{d}^{\pm 1/p^{\infty}}\rangle)
of
Y
. If
\theta_{V}
is viewed as a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear map
\Omega^{1}_{X}(-1)^{\vee}\to\End(V\otimes\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
,
the action of the dual basis of
\frac{dT_{1}}{T_{1}},\cdots,\frac{dT_{d}}{T_{d}}
on the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{d}
-locally analytic vectors of
V\otimes\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(U_{\infty})
agrees with the action of the standard basis of
\Lie\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)^{d}
.

When both
X
and
V
are defined over a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
V
is a de Rham local system, the Higgs field is closely related to the flat connection associated to
V
. We make this more precise in the case of modular curves. Fix a level
K
and let
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{K}
denote the adic space associated to the compactification of
Y_{K}\times_{\mathbb{Q}}C
. Let
f:E\to Y_{K}
be the universal elliptic curve. The (canonical extension of) relative de Rham cohomology
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(E/Y_{k})
defines a filtered vector bundle
(D,\Fil^{\bullet})
on
\mathcal{X}
with a logarithm connection
\nabla:D\to D\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{C})
where
\mathcal{C}
denotes the cusps. On the other hand,
R^{1}f_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t},*}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
defines a pro-Kummer-étale local system
V
of rank
2
on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{prok\acute{e}t}}
.
We have the relative Hodge-Tate filtration on
V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}

0\to\gr^{0}D\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}\to V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\gr^{1}D\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}(-1)\to 0.

The following result is essentially due to Faltings
[
20
, Th.5]

[
27
, Th.4.2.2, 4.2.4]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

\theta_{V}|_{\gr^{0}D\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}}=0
and
\theta_{V}(V\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}})\subseteq\gr^{0}D\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{C})(-1)
. Moreover
the induced map

\gr^{1}D\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}(-1)\to\gr^{0}D\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{C})(-1)

agrees with the tensor product of the Kodaira-Spencer map and
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}(-1)
up to a sign, hence it’s an isomorphism for modular curves.

Remark 2.8
.

Recall that the Kodaira-Spencer map is defined as the composite of

\gr^{1}D=\Fil^{1}D\xrightarrow{\nabla}\Fil^{0}D\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{C})\to\gr^{0}D\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{C}).

Remark 2.9
.

The calculation of
\theta_{V}
has been generalized to arbitrary Shimura varieties by Rodriguez Camargo
[
32
, §5]
using the main result of
[
14
]
.

2.2.
Localization: the sheaf
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}

In
[
33
]
, Scholze showed that
\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}\sim\varprojlim_{K_{p}}\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}K_{p}}
exists as a perfectoid space (the modular curve with infinite level at
p
). The local system
V
is canonically trivialized on
\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}
and the relative Hodge-Tate filtration defines a
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant map called the Hodge-Tate period morphism:

\pi_{\mathrm{HT}}:\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}\to{\mathscr{F}\!\ell}=\mathbb{P}^{1},

where
{\mathscr{F}\!\ell}
denotes the adic space associated to the flag variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}/C
.

Definition 2.10
.

Define
\mathcal{O}_{K^{p}}:=\pi_{\mathrm{HT},*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}}
and define
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}_{K^{p}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K^{p}}
as the subsheaf of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-locally analytic sections
[
27
, §4.2.6]
. We will drop the subscript
K^{p}
below.

The cohomology of these sheaves computes the completed cohomology. In the following theorem, the first isomorphism is essentially a result of Scholze, and the second isomorphism
[
27
, Th.4.4.6]
is a consequence of our calculation of the Higgs field in Theorem
2.7
.

Theorem 2.11
.

There are natural isomorphisms

\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}C\cong H^{i}({\mathscr{F}\!\ell},\mathcal{O}),

\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C\cong H^{i}({\mathscr{F}\!\ell},\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}),

where
\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}\subseteq\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
denotes the subspace of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-locally analytic vectors (so an LB-space). We call it the locally analytic completed cohomology.

To justify that
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
localizes
\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C
as a representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(C)
, we show that
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
behaves as a
\tilde{\mathscr{D}}
-module on the flag variety in the sense of
[
3
, §C]
, and this is exactly provided by our differential equation
2.5
. We follow the construction on the flag variety from
[
2
]
. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(C)
. For a
C
-point
x
of the flag variety
{\mathscr{F}\!\ell}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}/C
, let
\mathfrak{b}_{x},\mathfrak{n}_{x}\subset\mathfrak{g}
denote its corresponding Borel subalgebra and nilpotent subalgebra. Let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{0}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{{\mathscr{F}\!\ell}}\otimes_{C}\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}^{0}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathfrak{g}^{0}\,|\,f_{x}\in\mathfrak{b}_{x},\mbox{ for all }x\in{\mathscr{F}\!\ell}(C)\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}^{0}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathfrak{g}^{0}\,|\,f_{x}\in\mathfrak{n}_{x},\mbox{ for all }x\in{\mathscr{F}\!\ell}(C).\}

\mathfrak{g}^{0}
acts on the left of
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
in an obvious way. Here is
[
27
, Th.3.2.7]
.

Theorem 2.12
.

\mathfrak{n}^{0}
acts trivially on
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
.

There is an induced action of
\mathfrak{b}^{0}/\mathfrak{n}^{0}\cong\mathcal{O}_{{\mathscr{F}\!\ell}}\otimes\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
, where
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. We view
\mathfrak{h}
as the Levi quotient of the upper triangular Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
and identify it with the diagonal matrices. Hence we get an (horizontal) action
\theta_{\mathfrak{h}}
of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
, which, by Harish-Chandra’s theory, is closely related to the action of the centre
Z(U(\mathfrak{g}))
. Our next result gives a
p
-adic Hodge-theoretic interpretation of
\theta_{\mathfrak{h}}

[
27
, Th.5.1.11]
.

Theorem 2.13
.

\theta_{\mathfrak{h}}(\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix})
is the Sen operator on
H^{i}({\mathscr{F}\!\ell},\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}})\cong\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C
.

Remark 2.14
.

This should be viewed as a
p
-adic analogue of results
[
7
, §II.4]
. It shows a close relationship between the infinitesimal characters and Hodge-Tate weights.

Remark 2.15
.

The existence of the Sen operator on
\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C
is a result of Sen
[
34
]
by observing that the Galois action on
\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}
is locally analytic in the sense of
[
28
]
.

Remark 2.16
.

All the results of this section have been generalized to arbitrary Shimura varieties in
[
32
]
with the caveat that we only know
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
is a sheaf before pushforward along the Hodge-Tate period morphism to the flag variety, because it’s not known whether general
\pi_{\mathrm{HT}}
is affine or not.

Remark 2.17
.

In a series of papers Ardakov developed a theory of localization of coadmissible locally analytic
p
-adic representations of
p
-adic groups
[
1
]
. We compare his localization with our
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
here. There are two subtle differences. First Ardakov localizes coadmissible representation, which is naturally dual to admissible locally analytic representation like
\tilde{H}^{i}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}
. It’s natural to guess this duality comes from a Serre duality. Secondly the sheaf
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
is a natural family over all infinitesimal characters while it seems that the infinitesimal character has to be fixed in Ardakov’s work. Over a general Shimura variety,
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
will exist on a partial flag variety (defined by the Hodge cocharacter) rather than the full flag variety, making the comparison of two localizations more indirect.

2.3.
Explicit description of
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}

Sections of
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}

3.
Hodge-Tate structure:
[
27
]

Let
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(C)
be the upper-triangular Borel subalgebra and
\mu:\mathfrak{b}\to C
a character. In
[

To state the result, we introduce some notation. For

\omega^{k}_{K^{p}}:=\pi_{\mathrm{HT},*}\pi_{K_{p}}^{*}\omega_{K^{p}K_{p}}^{\otimes k},

Denote by

-smooth sections. (Here “smooth” means fixed by an open subgroup.) Its global sections

M_{k}:=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\omega^{k,\mathrm{sm}})

are classical modular forms of weight
k
(with tame level
K^{p}
). Similarly

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\omega^{k,\mathrm{sm}})=\varinjlim_{K_{p}}H^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}K_{p}},\omega^{k}_{K^{p}K_{p}}).

The stalk at

is what we call overconvergent modular forms of weight
k
(with tame level

(3.1)

\displaystyle 0\to H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\omega^{k,\mathrm{sm}})\to H^{0}(\infty,\omega^{k,\mathrm{sm}})\xrightarrow{\delta}H_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\omega^{k,\mathrm{sm}})\to H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\omega^{k,\mathrm{sm}})\to 0.

3.1.
Regular weights:
k\neq 1

If

N_{k,1}
sits inside of the exact sequence
0\to H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\omega^{k,\mathrm{sm}})\to N_{k,1}\to M^{\dagger}_{k}\to 0
if
k<0
.

When
k=0,2
, there are similar statements with small modifications. The decomposition is Hecke-equivariant if both terms have the correct twists as in
[
27
, Th.1.0.1]
.

This result clarifies the relationship between the completed cohomology and overconvergent modular forms, without restricting to the finite slope or ordinary part.
It allows us to characterize overconvergent eigenforms from the
p
-adic Hodge theory perspective. Essentially the only condition is their Hodge-Tate-Sen weights
[
27
, Th.6.4.11]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
\lambda
is a system of Hecke eigenvalues showing up in
M^{\dagger}_{k}
, then its associated Galois representation
\rho_{\lambda}
has Hodge-Tate-Sen weights
0,k-1
at
p
if it’s irreducible. Conversely, suppose
\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
is an odd continuous representation unramified at almost all places and

•

\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is irreducible and has Hodge-Tate-Sen weights
0,k-1
.

Then under some mild condition on its residual representation
\bar{\rho}
,
\rho
is attached to an eigenform in
M^{\dagger}_{k}
(for some
K^{p}
).

Remark 3.3
.

This confirms Gouvêa’s conjecture in
[
22
, Conjecture 4]
and his guess under the given hypotheses.
Howe also proved Gouvêa’s conjecture in
[
23
]
independently. Later we found a more direct way to prove Gouvêa’s conjecture in
[
28
]
using Scholze’s fake-Hasse invariants. But the methods in both works do not seem enough to give a converse result. Indeed, one key ingredient in our proof is Colmez’s Kirillov model
[
12
, Chap. VI]
, which is a deep result in
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

3.2.
Irregular weight:
k=1

When
k=1
, we don’t get a decomposition because of the existence of non-Hodge-Tate representations
[
27
, Th.5.4.6]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

There is a natural exact sequence

0\to H_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\omega^{1,\mathrm{sm}})\to\tilde{H}^{1}(C)^{\mathrm{la}}_{\mu_{1}}\to M^{\dagger}_{1}\to 0.

The action of the Sen operator on
\tilde{H}^{1}(C)^{\mathrm{la}}_{\mu_{1}}
is zero on
H_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\omega^{1,\mathrm{sm}})
and induces a map

M^{\dagger}_{1}\to H_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\omega^{1,\mathrm{sm}})

which agrees with the connecting homomorphism
\delta
in the long exact sequence (
3.1
).

Remark 3.5
.

The finite slope part of the sequence
M^{\dagger}_{1}\to H_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\omega^{1,\mathrm{sm}})
is what Boxer-Pilloni called Cousin complex in their work on higher Coleman theory
[
9
]
. Our theorem gives a
p
-adic Hodge theoretic interpretation of the connecting homomorphism in the Cousin complex, even for the infinite slope part.

One corollary is the following classicality result for weight one forms
[
27
, Th.6.4.7]
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose
\lambda
is a system of Hecke eigenvalues associated to a
U_{p}
-eigenform in
M^{\dagger}_{1}
and the attached Galois representation
\rho_{\lambda}
is Hodge-Tate at
p
. Then
\lambda
is the Hecke eigenvalue of a classical weight one form. In particular,
\rho_{\lambda}
is an Artin representation.

Remark 3.7
.

If
\lambda
is associated to an ordinary eigenform, this result was first obtained by Buzzard-Taylor
[
10
]
. Our work has no restriction on the
U_{p}
-eigenvalue, for example it can be zero. Using Emerton’s local-global compatibility result and Colmez’s Kirillov model to produce
U_{p}
-eigenform, we’re able to reprove many cases of the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture when the Hodge-Tate numbers are equal
[
27
, Cor.6.4.9]
.

Remark 3.8
.

In a recent preprint
[
8
]
Boxer-Calegari-Gee-Pilloni generalize such “Sen=Cousin” type of result to Siegel threefold and use it to prove many abelian surfaces over
\mathbb{Q}
are modular.

4.
de Rham structure:
[
29
]

The main focus of
[
27
]
is the Hodge-Tate structure of the locally analytic completed cohomology governed by the Sen operator. In
[
29
]
, we study the de Rham structure of the locally analytic completed cohomology. In this case the key operator is what we call the Fontaine operator.

4.1.
Fontaine operator

In
[
21
, §4]
Fontaine generalized Sen’s method and gave a classification of almost
B_{\mathrm{dR}}
-representations. For simplicity we will only discuss his result for Hodge-Tate representations here.

Suppose
V
is a Hodge-Tate representation of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
of dimension
d
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Fontaine defined a triple
(D_{\mathrm{pdR}}(V),\Fil^{\bullet},\nu)
where
D_{\mathrm{pdR}}(V)
is a
d
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
\Fil^{\bullet}
is a decreasing filtration on
D_{\mathrm{pdR}}(V)
and
\nu\in\End(D_{\mathrm{pdR}}(V))
is a nilpotent operator preserving the filtration
1
1

1

It actually sends
\Fil^{i}
to
\Fil^{i+1}
here because
V
is Hodge-Tate.
. We call
\nu
the Fontaine operator. There is a natural isomorphism

\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\gr^{k}D_{\mathrm{pdR}}(V)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C(-k)\cong V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C.

Moreover
\nu=0
if and only if
V
is de Rham (in this case
D_{\mathrm{pdR}}(V)=D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
). In particular when
V
only has two Hodge-Tate weights
0
and
k>0
,
\nu
induces a map

N_{W}:W_{0}\to W_{k}(k)

where
W_{i}=\gr^{i}D_{\mathrm{pdR}}(V)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C(-i)
is the Hodge-Tate weight
i
part of
W=V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C
, and
N_{W}=0
if and only if
V
is de Rham.

We apply Fontaine’s construction to the locally analytic completed cohomology. Let
k\geq 1
and
\tilde{\chi}_{k}:Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})))\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}
denote the infinitesimal character of
\Sym^{k-1}\mathrm{Std}^{*}
where
\mathrm{Std}
is the standard representation. The following result is a consequence of Theorem
2.13
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
.

(1)

The
\tilde{\chi}_{k}
-isotypic part
\tilde{H}^{1}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la},\tilde{\chi}_{k}}\subseteq\tilde{H}^{1}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}
is Hodge-Tate of weights
0,k
, i.e
there is a natural Hodge-Tate decomposition

\tilde{H}^{1}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la},\tilde{\chi}_{k}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C=W_{0}\oplus W_{k}

where
W_{i}
denotes the Hodge-Tate weight
i
part.

(2)

Given a weight
(k_{1},k_{2})
of
\mathfrak{h}
we denote by
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(k_{1},k_{2})}
the subsheaf of
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la}}
on which the
\theta_{\mathfrak{h}}
-action of
\mathfrak{h}
is
(k_{1},k_{2})
. There are natural isomorphisms

•

W_{0}\cong H^{1}({\mathscr{F}\!\ell},\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(1-k,0)})
when
k\geq 2
;

•

W_{k}\cong H^{1}({\mathscr{F}\!\ell},\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(1,-k)})
.

When
k=1
, there is a natural map
H^{1}({\mathscr{F}\!\ell},\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}_{K^{p}})\to W_{0}
and we recommend the reader to ignore the difference here.

Fontaine’s result implies that there is a natural map

N:H^{1}({\mathscr{F}\!\ell},\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(1-k,0)})\to H^{1}({\mathscr{F}\!\ell},\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(1,-k)}(k))

whose kernel sees the system of Hecke eigenvalues associated to de Rham Galois representations of Hodge-Tate weights
0,k
.

4.2.
Geometric description of
N

The main result of
[
29
, §6]
gives a geometric description of the (graded) Fontaine operator
N
. We will only discuss the case
k=1
here.

In Section
2.3
we introduce open rigid analytic subvarieties

\mathcal{Y}_{K^{p}K_{p},n}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}K_{p}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

for
n>0
and
K_{p}\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
sufficiently small. On the product
\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}K_{p}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we can consider derivations along each factor: (recall
\mathcal{C}
denotes the cusps)

d_{\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}K_{p}}}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{K^{p}K_{p},n}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{K^{p}K_{p},n}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}K_{p}}}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}K_{p}}}(\mathcal{C}),

d_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{K^{p}K_{p},n}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{K^{p}K_{p},n}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}.

Both commute with each other. By Theorem
2.19

\varinjlim_{K_{p},n}p_{K^{p}K_{p},n,*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{K^{p}K_{p},n}}=\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}

where
p_{K^{p}K_{p},n}:\mathcal{Y}_{K^{p}K_{p},n}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the projection morphism. Here is a strengthening version.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\Omega^{1,\mathrm{sm}}=\varinjlim_{K_{p},n}p_{K^{p}K_{p},n,*}\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}K_{p}}}(\mathcal{C})
. There is a natural isomorphism coming from the Kodaira-Spencer isomorphism

\Omega^{1,\mathrm{sm}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{sm}}}\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\cong\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(1,-1)}(1).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let

d^{1}:\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}\to\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{sm}}}\Omega^{1,\mathrm{sm}}

\bar{d}^{1}:\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}\to\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}

denote the direct limit of the pushforward of
d_{\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}K_{p}}}
and
d_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
respectively, and let
I_{0}
be the composition

\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}\xrightarrow{d^{1}}\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{sm}}}\Omega^{1,\mathrm{sm}}\xrightarrow{\bar{d}^{1}\otimes 1}\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{sm}}}\Omega^{1,\mathrm{sm}}\cong\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(1,-1)}(1).

Then
H^{1}(I_{0})=cN:H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)})\to H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(1,-1)}(1))
for some
c\in C^{\times}
.

Remark 4.4
.

We chose the notation
\bar{d}
to denote the derivation along
\mathbb{P}^{1}
due to the analogy between the Hodge-Tate period morphism and the anti-holomorphic Borel embedding.

Remark 4.5
.

When
k>1
, formally
N
will be “
H^{1}(\bar{d}^{k}\circ d^{k})
”.

Remark 4.6
.

Our original proof is by a detailed study of the de Rham period sheaves on
\mathcal{X}_{K^{p}}
. It was pointed out by a referee that
\bar{d}^{1}\circ d^{1}
is the only
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and Hecke equivariant differential operator
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}\to\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(1,-1)}(1)
of second-order (of
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{la},(0,0)}
-modules) up to scaling. On the other hand a careful study of Fontaine’s construction shows that
N=H^{1}(\tilde{N})
for some second-order operator
\tilde{N}
. This explains why one would expect “Fontaine = c
\bar{d}\circ d
” for some constant
c
.

4.3.
Application

We now discuss several applications of our geometric description of the Fontaine operator.

We are able to reprove a classicality result
[
29
, Th.7.1.2]
obtained firstly by Emerton.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\rho\subseteq\tilde{H}^{1}(K^{p})
be an absolutely irreducible two-dimensional subrepresentation of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Suppose
\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is de Rham of Hodge-Tate weights
0,k>0
, then it arises from a cuspidal eigenform of weight
k+1
.

Remark 4.8
.

Our method is fundamentally different from Emerton’s approach as we completely avoid the usage of
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. In fact we completely ignore the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-symmetry and directly prove the classicality by showing that the system of Hecke eigenvalue associated to
\rho
has to be classical (by applying the Jacquet-Langlands transfer). This even allows us to prove the same result for completed cohomology of Shimura curve of a division algebra
ramified
at
p
, though some of these Galois representations (for example those crystalline at
p
) don’t show up in the usual cohomology of such Shimura curves at all.

Remark 4.9
.

Yuanyang Jiang has recently generalized our work to the Hilbert case for regular parallel weights
[
24
]
.

Our method gives a description of the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-representation
\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\rho,\tilde{H}^{1}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}C)
. By the known local-global compatibility result, we get a geometric construction of certain locally analytic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
showing up in the
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence. See
[
29
, §7.3]
. Again we assume
k=1
for simplicity.

Fix an isomorphism
C\cong\mathbb{C}
. Suppose
f
is a classical weight
2
cuspidal eigenform and denote by
\pi^{\infty}=\otimes^{\prime}_{l}\pi_{l}
the irreducible automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
over
C
attached to
f
. Denote by
E\subseteq C
the finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
generated by the Hecke eigenvalues of
f
. Then one can attach
\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(E)
to
f
. Set
\Pi^{\mathrm{la}}=\Hom_{E[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]}(\rho,\tilde{H}^{1}(K^{p})^{\mathrm{la}}\otimes E)\widehat{\otimes}_{E}C
. Our goal is to give a formula for this
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-representation.

When
\pi_{p}
is supercuspidal, the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence gives a smooth irreducible representation
\pi^{\prime}_{p}
of
D_{p}^{\times}
with
D_{p}
the quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
\Omega=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{C}\setminus\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be the Drinfeld upper half plane. There is a natural
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant
D_{p}^{\times}
-torsor over
\Omega
. Hence
\pi^{\prime}_{p}
defines a
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant
C
-local system
\mathbb{L}
on
\Omega_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
. Denote by
\alpha:\Omega_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\to\Omega
the projection morphism to the analytic site and
j:\Omega\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
the inclusion map.

Theorem 4.10
.

There is a
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant injective map

i_{\pi}:\pi_{p}\to H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},j_{!}\alpha_{*}(\mathbb{L}\otimes_{C}\mathcal{O}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}))

such that

\Pi^{\mathrm{la}}\cong(\pi^{\infty,p})^{K^{p}}\otimes_{C}\coker i_{\pi}.

Remark 4.11
.

The map
i_{\pi}
can be made more precise: there is a natural isomorphism

D_{\mathrm{dR}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})\otimes_{E}\pi_{p}\cong\ker H^{1}(j_{!}\alpha_{*}1\otimes d_{\Omega})

where
1\otimes d_{\Omega}:\mathbb{L}\otimes_{C}\mathcal{O}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}\to\mathbb{L}\otimes_{C}\Omega^{1}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}
with
d_{\Omega}
the usual derivation on
\Omega
, and the map
i_{\pi}
exactly comes from the line
\Fil^{1}D_{\mathrm{dR}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
. This is essentially a conjecture of Breuil-Strauch on
\Pi^{\mathrm{la}}
in the dual form, which was completely solved by Dospinescu-Le Bras
[
15
]
before.

When
\pi_{p}
is a principal series, we set
D_{\mathrm{pcris}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}):=(\rho\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}B_{\mathrm{cris}})^{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{n}})}}
for some sufficiently large
n
, which is a two-dimensional
C
-vector space by the assumption on
\pi_{p}
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
B
be the upper-triangular Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
One can naturally equip
D_{\mathrm{pcris}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
with a smooth
B
-action and define an embedding of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-representations

i_{\pi}:\pi_{p}\to\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}D_{\mathrm{pcris}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})\otimes_{E}C

where
\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
denotes the locally analytic induction, such that

\Pi^{\mathrm{la}}\cong(\pi^{\infty,p})^{K^{p}}\otimes_{C}\coker i_{\pi}.

Remark 4.13
.

The
B
-action comes from identifying
D_{\mathrm{pcris}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
with the log crystalline cohomology of the tower of Igusa curves. Explicitly it’s given by the congruence relation as in
[
11
, §1.6.4]
. Again
i_{\pi}
is induced from
\Fil^{1}D_{\mathrm{dR}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})\subseteq D_{\mathrm{dR}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})=D_{\mathrm{pcris}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
. The local form of this isomorphism was essentially conjectured by Berger-Breuil in
[
4
, Conjecture 5.3.7]
and by Emerton
[
16
, Conjecture 6.7.3]
, and was proved by Liu-Xie-Zhang
[
26
]
and Colmez
[
13
]
.

Remark 4.14
.

The
B
-representation
D_{\mathrm{pcris}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
defines naturally a
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant local system
\mathbb{L^{\prime}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and the locally analytic induction
\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}D_{\mathrm{pcris}}(\rho|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})\otimes_{E}C
is nothing but
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},i_{*}\mathbb{L}^{\prime}\otimes_{E}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})
where
i:\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the inclusion map.

So in both supercuspidal and principal series cases, we see that
\Pi^{\mathrm{la}}
has a uniform description but over different
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-invariant strata of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. This is quite natural from our geometric point of view, because the local systems
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{L^{\prime}}
actually come from pushing forward the de Rham complex of the modular curves towards the flag variety via the Hodge-Tate period map. It also explains why
\Pi^{\mathrm{la}}
will get contributions from both strata when
\pi_{p}
is special.

Constructing representations from an equivariant local system on a stratum of the flag variety is not new over real numbers
[
25
]
. It’s natural to ask: can we construct locally analytic representations of
p
-adic groups similarly? If so, which arise in this way?
