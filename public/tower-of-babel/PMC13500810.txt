Solitons, bifurcation, chaos, and stability of the fractional Schrödinger-Hirota equation with beta derivative
Authors: Md Al Amin, Tobibur Rahman, Md Tarikul Islam, Md Abu Bakar Pk, M Ali Akbar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe fractional nonlinear Schrödinger-Hirota equation is a dynamic model for describing optical soliton propagation in dispersive fibers, capturing complex higher-order dispersion and fractional scaling effects. In this study, we investigate the underlying dynamics of this framework by incorporating the β-fractional derivative. By employing the extended Riccati scheme, we derive a broad spectrum of novel exact analytical solutions, including kink, dark, periodic, and mixed bright-dark soliton profiles. Beyond exact solutions, this work provides a qualitative assessment of the system’s dynamics using planar dynamical system theory and bifurcation analysis to classify equilibrium states. Furthermore, the introduction of external periodic perturbations reveals a distinct transition from stable oscillatory motion to chaotic behavior. This chaotic transition is precisely quantified through Poincaré sections and the Lyapunov exponent, demonstrating the system’s sensitivity to fractional scaling and perturbation parameters. Finally, linear stability analysis confirms the robustness and physical viability of the derived solitons under disruptive conditions. The results enrich the mathematical understanding of fractional nonlinear dynamics and provide valuable physical insights for optimizing stable, high-capacity optical fiber communication systems and next-generation photonic devices.Keywords: Fractional Schrödinger-Hirota equation, Extended Riccati scheme, Optical solitons, β-fractional derivative, Chaotic dynamics and bifurcation analysis, Lyapunov exponentSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

Nonlinear evolution equations (NLEEs) have become an important part of nonlinear science and engineering. These equations provide useful mathematical descriptions of complex physical phenomena governed by nonlinear interactions. They are found in many domains of applied science, including optical fiber communication, nonlinear optics, electrodynamics, plasma physics, signal processing, fluid dynamics, quantum mechanics, and biophysics. Many natural and engineering processes are nonlinear, and the inclusion of such terms in mathematical models is crucial for obtaining realistic descriptions of system behavior. In the last decades, nonlinear dynamics modeled using NLEEs have gained significant attention due to their ability to capture rich qualitative behaviors such as stability, bifurcation, chaos, and phase-space structures. These properties are key for understanding turbulence, plasma instability, atmospheric dynamics, and other time-evolving phenomena. The bifurcation properties of these models mean that small changes in their parameters can lead to great qualitative changes in system dynamics, providing insight into transitions between stable, unstable, and chaotic states.

The waves that remain stable and do not change their shape, amplitude, or speed as they move and interact with each other are known as solitons. In 1834, John Scott Russell first observed the solitary wave. The Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation is known as the definitive model of such wave motion. Zabusky and Kruskal established that there is a balance between nonlinearity and dispersion such that the soliton solution behaves like particles when they interact with each other. Solitons are now fundamental in quantum systems, plasma physics, optical communications, and fluid dynamics. To study nonlinear wave phenomena, various analytical and computational methods have been developed, resulting in a large number of works on soliton and chaotic solutions of the NLEEs. These methods include the improved F-expansion method1, modified simple equation method2, Sardar sub-equation scheme3, Bäcklund transformation4, extended auxiliary equation mapping method5, trial equation method6, Kudryashov sine-Gordon hybrid method7, extended sinh-Gordon expansion8, generalized Kudryashov approach9, symmetry analysis10, auxiliary equation method11, improved exp(-)-expansion technique12, -expansion approach13, soliton ansatz scheme14, direct algebraic approach15, first-integral method16, sine-cosine technique17, -expansion scheme18, homogeneous balance scheme19, modified tanh-family method20–22, and extended F-expansion framework23. These tools together make up a complete process for unraveling NLEEs and studying soliton dynamics.

The parameter c is self-steepening, and the parameter b (as part of the third-order terms) is third-order dispersion (TOD). This model is important for the description of the propagation of pulses through optical fibers, plasma waves, nonlinear dispersive media, and other quantum systems. Earlier research has been conducted on the dynamics of solitons affected by Kerr nonlinearity, self-steepening, self-phase modulation, and TOD. Soliton solutions of nonlinear equations provide important qualitative information on the nonlinear wave phenomena, including spatial localization, energy transport, peak regimes, and the existence of steady states.

Analytical soliton solutions are crucial as they elucidate foundational theoretical concepts and help to determine empirical parameters in physical theories. Numerous studies have extracted exact soliton solutions to the SH equation using various mathematical techniques. For instance, Khatun et al.24 studied geometric optical solitons, and Osman et al.25 employed an improved ()-expansion technique, Kudryashov method, and modified Jacobi elliptic function26–28 approach. A number of studies have used fractional derivatives (e.g., conformable, Caputo, or Atangana-Baleanu operators) to study memory- and non-memory-dependent optical phenomena and planar bifurcations of cubic-nonlinear models29,34–42. These studies generally do not investigate the chaotic issues and geometric scaling properties of the β-fractional derivative, nor have certain sophisticated analytical methods yet been fully investigated11,43,44.

Furthermore, the study of bifurcation theory and chaotic dynamics is important for analyzing nonlinear optical systems. Bifurcation theory allows for the mapping of critical transitions where stable solitary waves may lose their structural integrity due to parameter variations. In fractional systems, dynamical instability and chaotic behavior represent major challenges, as they can lead to severe signal degradation and unpredictable wave propagation in high-capacity fiber networks. For instance, recent studies by Abdulkareem and Ismael have explored phase plane dynamics and bifurcation structures across complex nonlinear optical media45,46. Understanding these phase transitions provides essential theoretical predictions for controlling signal instabilities in complex dispersive media.

In light of these research gaps, we employ a dual approach using the β-fractional derivative. First, we use the powerful extended Riccati method to derive new analytical solutions for the fractional SH equation with kink, bright-dark mixed and periodic soliton structures. Also, we study key physical properties, such as the transition of the GVD from normal to anomalous. Second, we build a fractional dynamical system subjected to an external periodic perturbation . Using the fractional time-series, Poincaré sections, and the Lyapunov exponent, we quantify the onset of chaos due to -fractional scaling effects. We obtain the exact structures of solitons, and the results provide valuable insights into the oscillatory behavior of perturbed fractional nonlinear systems.

Justification for selecting the extended Riccati method

Despite recent progress in the formulation of general methods to address the integrable and non-integrable NLEEs, the general implementation of a universal framework for these models remains undefined. The extended Riccati scheme yields exact wave solutions with excellent rigor and accuracy. The obtained results are important for understanding the underlying mechanisms of these physical systems. The advantage of this strategy over most other strategies is its ability to yield general solutions for all possible parameters. By controlling these variables, the structures generated can be compared with other known results. In spite of its many advantages, there are drawbacks to this method. Occasionally, the results are simply re-formulated versions of solutions obtained using other methods. Similarly, if the balance number of the reduced ordinary differential equations exceeds two, the method may not be able to provide a solution for the algebraic equations.

Physically, marginal stability implies that small perturbations neither grow exponentially nor decay with time, so the wave profile remains at a threshold state between stability and instability. This suggests that the system is neutrally balanced, where persistent oscillations or weak long-term modulations may occur under external disturbances. Moreover, it should be noted that linear stability analysis is valid only for infinitesimal perturbations around the stationary state. Therefore, it may not fully capture nonlinear interactions, finite-amplitude disturbances, or the long-term post-instability dynamics of the system.

Discussion, conclusion and future directions

In this study, comprehensive analytical solutions of the fractional nonlinear Schrödinger-Hirota equation are investigated utilizing the β-fractional derivative to accurately analyze higher-order dispersion, nonlinearity, and fractional scaling effects in optical fibers. By employing the extended Riccati equation scheme, we expanded the solution space of this model, generating a diverse array of exact analytical structures, including kink, anti-bell-shaped, periodic, and mixed optical solitons. Compared to previous classical integer-order Schrödinger-Hirota studies, the primary contribution of this work lies in the qualitative mapping of the model’s dynamics through rigorous phase-plane analysis. Specifically, we demonstrated how the fractional parameter acts as a critical bifurcation parameter that modulates the topological dynamics and modifies the system’s stability. Furthermore, by introducing an external periodic force, we systematically captured the transition from regular periodic oscillations to complex chaotic wave patterns. This transition was quantified using Poincaré sections and the Lyapunov exponent, where positive exponent values provided supporting evidence of chaotic sensitivity driven by fractional scaling. Understanding this threshold of chaos is critical, as it offers theoretical predictability for signal degradation and instabilities in next-generation nonlinear dispersive media. Finally, linear stability analysis validated the physical viability of these wave structures, confirming their structural integrity under disruptive conditions. Ultimately, this research underscores that the fractional-order Schrödinger-Hirota model provides a more robust and accurate representation of nonlinear wave propagation than its classical integer-order counterparts, offering valuable insights for optimizing high-capacity photonic devices. However, the current framework is limited to constant coefficients and a specific fractional operator. Future research directions may explore the inclusion of stochastic perturbations, variable coefficients, and the extension of this approach to higher-dimensional fractional models to capture more complex physical phenomena.
