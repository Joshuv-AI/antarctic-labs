Non-existence of Lyapunov exponents in the Newhouse domain
Shin Kiriki, Xiaolong Li, Yushi Nakano, Teruhiko Soma

Abstract
. We show that within the Newhouse domain of C r C^{r} surface diffeomorphisms ( r ∈ [ 2 , ∞ ) r\in[2,\infty) ), there exists a dense subset 𝒟 \mathcal{D} such that for any f ∈ 𝒟 f\in\mathcal{D} , Lyapunov exponents fail to exist for all points in some open set U U and all nonzero tangent vectors in some open cone V ⊂ ℝ 2 V\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} . This demonstrates that the non-existence of Lyapunov exponents is a persistent phenomenon in the setting of robust homoclinic tangencies. The proof relies on constructing diffeomorphisms exhibiting specific oscillatory return times near a homoclinic tangency, incorporating techniques from Newhouse theory and recent results on Lyapunov irregularity, alongside several refinements and new arguments.

1.
Introduction

Dynamical systems with a robust homoclinic tangency – those in the so-called Newhouse domain – are a major source of extremely non-hyperbolic dynamics and have long been known to exhibit a rich variety of complex dynamical phenomena,
such as infinitely many sinks
[
17
]
, infinitely many strange attractors
[
6
]
, super-exponential growth of the number of periodic points
[
7
]
, universal dynamics
[
23
]
, historic wandering domains
[
13
,
12
]
and pluripotency
[
9
]
.
This paper focuses on statistical irregularities associated with one of the most fundamental quantities in dynamical systems: the Lyapunov exponent, which quantifies the sensitivity of an orbit to initial conditions and is widely used as an indicator of chaos in the natural sciences.
We show that, within the Newhouse domain, Lyapunov exponents persistently fail to exist on open sets of points and tangent vectors:

Theorem A
.

Given a closed surface
M
and
r\in[2,\infty)
, there is a dense subset
\mathcal{D}
of the Newhouse domain of

\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)

such that for any
f\in\mathcal{D}
, there exist nonempty open sets
U\subset M
and
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, under the identification of
TU
with
U\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that the Lyapunov exponent

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log||Df^{n}(x)v||}{n}
does not exist

for all
x\in U
and nonzero
v\in V
.

In light of the widely believed dichotomy suggesting that ‘generic’ surface diffeomorphisms are either
hyperbolic
or
contain homoclinic tangencies, together with its higher-dimensional counterpart involving dynamics with heterodimensional cycles
[
21
,
4
,
20
]
, our result provides significant insight into the statistical properties of non-hyperbolic dynamics. It highlights that complex behavior regarding Lyapunov exponents, specifically their non-existence, widely occurs within these non-hyperbolic systems, potentially contributing to a more complete understanding of dynamics beyond uniform hyperbolicity.

Despite the fundamental importance of Lyapunov exponents in characterizing dynamical behavior and their applications in various scientific fields (cf.
[
24
]
), the phenomenon where these exponents fail to converge for observable points or vectors (i.e. those in a positive Lebesgue measure set) has remained relatively less explored.
Early work by Ott and Yorke
[
18
]
presented the first example of such non-convergence in a surface flow with an attracting homoclinic loop.
More recently, our previous work
[
10
]
aimed to uncover the underlying mechanisms driving the non-existence of Lyapunov exponents
for certain surface diffeomorphisms.
This mechanism was subsequently adapted to demonstrate the non-existence of Lyapunov exponents in a class of two-dimensional piecewise expanding maps
[
15
]
.

This paper builds upon and integrates the techniques developed in the previous work
[
10
]
with the rich technical machinery accumulated since the era of Newhouse, Palis and Takens
[
19
]
for studying the Newhouse domain.
In particular, it leverages the methods for constructing diffeomorphisms with prescribed return time properties of wandering domains developed by Kiriki and Soma
[
13
]
, which were themselves inspired by earlier work of Colli and Vargas
[
5
]
.
Furthermore, we introduce several new arguments and refinements.
Specifically,
accurately estimating certain newly arising off-diagonal terms (cf. the (1,2)-entry of (
4.20
)) in the derivative matrices presents significant challenges.
This necessitated modifications to the arguments, the development of a new ‘Modified Rectangle Lemma’ (Section
3
), an inductive analysis of the products of Jacobian matrices for the lower bounds needed to control the relevant coefficients despite the presence of newly arising off-diagonal terms (Section
5
),
and a fine-tuning of the arguments both in
[
13
]
and
[
10
]
to establish the required properties, including specific conditions on
strong dissipativity, and exponential oscillations of return times (conditions detailed in Section
2
).

Another observation in our construction suggests that the mechanism leading to the non-existence of Lyapunov exponents identified here operates independently of Birkhoff irregularity (non-existence of time averages).
Indeed, one can show the convergence of time averages for any continuous observable at every point within the open set
U
of Theorem
A
on which the Lyapunov exponent does not exist (see Remark
2.6
), emphasizing that these two quantities are independent.

We close this introduction by presenting an application of Theorem
A
to the Hénon family,
i.e., diffeomorphisms on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by
f_{a,b}(x,y)=(1-ax^{2}+by,x)
with
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
in sharp contrast to pioneering works
[
1
,
14
,
3
,
2
]
on the existence and positivity of Lyapunov exponents of the Hénon family.
The following is an immediate consequence of Theorem
A
.

Corollary 1.1
.

There is an open set
\mathcal{O}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

with

(2,0)\in\overline{\mathcal{O}}
such that, for every

(a,b)\in\mathcal{O}
,
the Hénon map
f_{a,b}
can be
C^{r}
-approximated by diffeomorphisms with a nonempty open set
on which Lyapunov exponents do not exist.

2.
Non-existence of Lyapunov exponents in abstract settings

Throughout this paper, let
r
be a fixed real number with
r\geq 2
,
M
a
C^{r}
closed surface and
\mbox{Diff}^{r}(M)
the space of all
C^{r}
diffeomorphisms of
M
endowed with the
C^{r}
topology.
A guiding principle of this paper is that one important source of oscillation of Lyapunov exponents is the interaction between hyperbolic dynamics and tangential returns. The former produces strong anisotropic growth, while the latter reconfigures the expanding and contracting directions. The following conditions are designed to capture this hybrid mechanism in an explicit and quantitative form.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
satisfies
DH
,
H
,
QT
and
OE
if there are two sequences
(n_{k}^{H})_{k\geq 1}
,
(n_{k}^{T})_{k\geq 1}
of positive integers with
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}n_{k}^{H}=+\infty
and a sequence of points
(\zeta_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of
M
such that the following conditions hold.

DH

(Dominance of hyperbolic return times)
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, with the notation
n_{k}=n_{k}^{H}+n_{k}^{T}
, we have

f^{n_{k}}(\zeta_{k})=\zeta_{k+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{n_{k}^{H}}{n_{k}}=1.

H

(Strongly dissipative hyperbolic region)
There exist
an open set
K\subset M

and a
C^{r}
coordinate on
K
in which

f(x,y)=(\lambda_{s}x,\,\lambda_{u}y)

with constants
0<\lambda_{s}<1<\lambda_{u}
satisfying

\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{3}<1.

Furthermore, there exists a nonempty closed set
K^{\prime}\subset K
such that

f^{j}(\zeta_{k})\in K^{\prime}

for every
k\geq 1
and
0\leq j\leq n_{k}^{H}
.

QT

(Quadratic tangential return)
For each
k\geq 1
, writing
\zeta_{k+1}^{\prime}=f^{n_{k}^{H}}(\zeta_{k})
, we have

Df^{n_{k}^{T}}(\zeta_{k+1}^{\prime})v^{u}\in\mathbb{R}v^{s}.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
, independent of
k
, such that for every line segment

\gamma\subset\ell^{u}(\zeta_{k+1}^{\prime})\cap K

containing
\zeta_{k+1}^{\prime}
,

\bigl|\pi^{u}_{k+1}(f^{n_{k}^{T}}(\gamma))\bigr|\leq C_{T}\bigl|\pi^{s}_{k+1}(f^{n_{k}^{T}}(\gamma))\bigr|^{2}.

Here
v^{s}=(1,0)
and
v^{u}=(0,1)
are the horizontal and vertical unit vectors, respectively,
\ell^{\sigma}(z)
denotes the lines in
K
through
z
parallel to
v^{\sigma}
(
\sigma\in\{s,u\}
), and
\pi^{\sigma}_{k}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
\ell^{\sigma}(\zeta_{k})
.

OE

(Oscillatory and exponential growth of return times)
There are real numbers
\alpha,\beta
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&1<\alpha<\beta<2,\quad\dfrac{5}{2}\alpha-2-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}<0,\quad\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6}{2-\beta}}<1,\quad\lambda_{u}(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{3})^{\alpha\beta-1}>1,\\
&\lambda_{s}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{6\beta}{2-\beta}-\frac{3}{2-\alpha}-\frac{9\beta-6}{2(2-\beta)}}<1,\quad\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}}>\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6}{2-\beta}},\quad\Bigl(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}}\Bigr)^{\alpha\beta}>\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6}{2-\beta}}.\end{split}

Moreover,
n_{k}^{H}
can be expressed with a positive integer

, according to whether
k
is odd or even, as

n_{2p}^{H}=\lfloor n_{0}^{H}\alpha^{p}\beta^{p}\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad n_{2p+1}^{H}=\lfloor n_{0}^{H}\alpha^{p+1}\beta^{p}\rfloor,

where

is the greatest integer not exceeding a real number
x
.

x\in U
:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\varphi(f^{j}(x))=\varphi(a).

(In particular, the Dirac measure
\delta_{a}
is the so-called Dirac physical measure of saddle type.)
See
[
10
, Section 4.4]
for further details.

3.
Modified rectangle lemma

As explained in Section
2
, the construction in
[
13
]
provides the geometric ingredients underlying
DH
,
H
, and
QT
in the Newhouse domain. It also yields a sequence of mutually disjoint rectangles
(R_{k})_{k\geq k_{0}}
such that the center of
R_{k}
is
\zeta_{k}
and
f^{n_{k}}(R_{k})\subset R_{k+1}
(called Rectangle Lemma there).
A similar family of rectangles satisfying the same forward inclusion also appeared in
[
10
]
for a more concrete class of surface diffeomorphisms, and in that setting smaller-scale sets
(U_{k,m})_{m\geq 0}
were built inside those rectangles as an important step to show the non-existence of Lyapunov exponents.
In the present situation, however, the rectangles
R_{k}
from
[
13
]
are not located in the neighborhood
K
where the linearizing coordinate is available. Since this makes the calculations in Sections
4
and
5
prohibitively difficult, we instead construct a family of sets
(U_{k,m})_{m\geq 0}
in a different region adapted to the linearizing coordinate.

Suppose that
f
satisfies
DH
,
H
,
QT
and
OE
with the same notations in Section
2
.
In this section, for every sufficiently large positive integer
k
and every non-negative integer
m
, we aim to define a rectangle
U_{k,m}
satisfying
f^{n_{k+m}}(U_{k,m})\subset U_{k,m+1}
.

3.1.
Construction of
U_{k,m}

According to the third inequality of (
2.1
) in
OE
, we are allowed to fix
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that

(3.1)

\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6+18(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}{2-\beta}}<1.

Note that (
3.1
) also holds for every subscript greater than
k_{0}
. For every integer
k\geq k_{0}
, let

(3.2)

b_{k}=\lambda_{u}^{-3\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{n_{k+i}^{H}}{2^{i}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\epsilon_{k}=\Big(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6+18(n_{k}^{H})^{-1}}{2-\beta}}\Big)^{n_{k}^{H}}.

Thus, by (
3.1
) we have
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
when
k\to+\infty
. Moreover, by direct calculation, we have

(3.3)

\lambda_{u}^{3n_{k}^{H}}b_{k}=\lambda_{u}^{-3\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{H}_{k+i}}{2^{i}}}=\sqrt{b_{k+1}}.

Finally, for every non-negative integer
m
, let us define
\epsilon_{k,m}
as

(3.4)

\epsilon_{k,m}=\epsilon_{k}^{(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}}.

Note that
\epsilon_{k,m}\neq\epsilon_{k+m}
in general.
These quantities
b_{k+m}
and
\epsilon_{k,m}
will be used to construct the desired rectangle
U_{k,m}
. Before doing so, we state the following claim, which will play a key role in the next subsection.

Claim 3.1
.

For every sufficiently large integer
k
and every non-negative integer
m
, it holds that

\epsilon_{k,m}\leq\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}\epsilon_{k,m+1}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\epsilon_{k,m}
in (
3.4
), it is equivalent to show that

\epsilon_{k}^{(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}}\leq\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}\epsilon_{k}^{(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor}}.

If
m
is even, then
\lfloor m/2\rfloor=\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor
, and there is nothing to prove since
\lambda_{u}>1
.
Thus, we only need to consider the case where
m
is odd, say
m=2t+1
with
t\geq 0
.
Then it is enough to show that

(3.5)

\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+2t+1}^{H}}\epsilon_{k}^{(\alpha\beta)^{t}(\alpha\beta-1)}\geq 1

for every sufficiently large
k
and every
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

It holds that

(3.6)

n_{k+2t+1}^{H}>\alpha(\alpha\beta)^{t}n_{k}^{H}-1

for every
k\geq 1
and every
t\geq 0
.
Indeed, if
k=2q
is even, then by
OE
,

n_{k+2t+1}^{H}=\lfloor n_{0}^{H}\alpha^{q+t+1}\beta^{q+t}\rfloor>\alpha^{t+1}\beta^{t}n_{k}^{H}-1=\alpha(\alpha\beta)^{t}n_{k}^{H}-1.

If
k=2q+1
is odd, then

n_{k+2t+1}^{H}=\lfloor n_{0}^{H}\alpha^{q+t+1}\beta^{q+t+1}\rfloor>\beta(\alpha\beta)^{t}n_{k}^{H}-1>\alpha(\alpha\beta)^{t}n_{k}^{H}-1,

because
\beta>\alpha
.
This proves (
3.6
).

Using (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+2t+1}^{H}}\epsilon_{k}^{(\alpha\beta)^{t}(\alpha\beta-1)}&>\lambda_{u}^{-1}\lambda_{u}^{\alpha(\alpha\beta)^{t}n_{k}^{H}}\Big(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6+18(n_{k}^{H})^{-1}}{2-\beta}}\Big)^{n_{k}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{t}(\alpha\beta-1)}\\
&=\lambda_{u}^{-1}\Big(\lambda_{u}^{\alpha}\Big(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6+18(n_{k}^{H})^{-1}}{2-\beta}}\Big)^{\alpha\beta-1}\Big)^{(\alpha\beta)^{t}n_{k}^{H}}\\
&\geq\lambda_{u}^{-1}\Big(\lambda_{u}(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{3})^{\alpha\beta-1}\Big)^{(\alpha\beta)^{t}n_{k}^{H}},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used
\alpha>1
and

\frac{9\beta-6+18(n_{k}^{H})^{-1}}{2-\beta}>3.

By the fourth inequality of (
2.1
), the base in the last line of (
3.7
) is greater than
1
.
Hence the right-hand side of (
3.7
) is greater than
1
for every sufficiently large
k
, uniformly in
t
.
Thus (
3.5
) holds, and the proof is complete.
∎

Now, let us construct the rectangles
U_{k,m}
. Recall that by condition
H
, we know that
\zeta_{k+m}
is contained in an open set
K
where a
C^{r}
coordinate chart was defined.
Let
\zeta_{k+m}=(\zeta^{s}_{k+m},\zeta^{u}_{k+m})
and define

\displaystyle\ell^{s}_{k+m}

\displaystyle:=\Big[\zeta^{s}_{k+m}-\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}},\quad\zeta^{s}_{k+m}+\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}}\Big]\times\{\zeta^{u}_{k+m}\},

\displaystyle\ell^{u}_{k+m}

\displaystyle:=\{\zeta^{s}_{k+m}\}\times\Big[\zeta^{u}_{k+m}-\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k,m}b_{k+m},\quad\zeta^{u}_{k+m}+\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k,m}b_{k+m}\Big].

Obviously,
\ell^{s}_{k+m}
and
\ell^{u}_{k+m}
are horizontal and vertical line segments parallel to the coordinate axes. Now, we define the rectangle
U_{k,m}
as

\displaystyle U_{k,m}:=

\displaystyle\Big[\zeta^{s}_{k+m}-\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}},\quad\zeta^{s}_{k+m}+\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}}\Big]

\displaystyle\times\Big[\zeta^{u}_{k+m}-\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k,m}b_{k+m},\quad\zeta^{u}_{k+m}+\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k,m}b_{k+m}\Big],

See Figure
1
below. For simplicity, we call
2\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}}
the
width
of
U_{k,m}
and
\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{k,m}b_{k+m}
the
height
of
U_{k,m}
.

In addition, for every
x=(x^{s},x^{u})\in\ell_{k+m}^{u}
, let

\ell^{s}_{k+m}(x)=\Big[\zeta^{s}_{k+m}-\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}},\quad\zeta^{s}_{k+m}+\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}}\Big]\times\{x^{u}\}.

Thus, it follows immediately that

U_{k,m}=\bigcup_{x\in\ell_{k+m}^{u}}\ell_{k+m}^{s}(x).

Lemma 3.2
.

For every sufficiently large integer
k
and every non-negative integer
m
, it holds that

U_{k,m}\subset\bigcap_{j=0}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}f^{-j}(K)

Proof.

Since
M
is compact and
K
is open, if
k
is sufficiently large, then

2\epsilon_{k}<\rho:=\mathrm{dist}(K^{\prime},M\setminus K),

where
K^{\prime}
is the non-empty closed subset of
K
in
H
.
Fix such a
k
. For every integer
m\geq 0
and
x=(x^{s},x^{u})\in U_{k,m}
,
we prove by induction on
j=0,\dots,n_{k+m}^{H}
that

f^{j}(x)\in K,\quad\bigl|(f^{j}(x))^{s}-(f^{j}(\zeta_{k+m}))^{s}\bigr|\leq\epsilon_{k},\quad\bigl|(f^{j}(x))^{u}-(f^{j}(\zeta_{k+m}))^{u}\bigr|\leq\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k}.

For
j=0
, since
x\in U_{k,m}
, we have

|x^{s}-\zeta_{k+m}^{s}|\leq\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}}\leq\epsilon_{k}

and

|x^{u}-\zeta_{k+m}^{u}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k,m}b_{k+m}\leq\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k}.

Hence

\|x-\zeta_{k+m}\|<2\epsilon_{k}<\rho.

Since
\zeta_{k+m}\in K^{\prime}
, this implies
x\in K
.

Assume now that
f^{i}(x)\in K
for every
0\leq i\leq j-1
, where
1\leq j\leq n_{k+m}^{H}
.
Since also
f^{i}(\zeta_{k+m})\in K
for
0\leq i\leq j
, it follows from
H
that

\bigl|(f^{j}(x))^{s}-(f^{j}(\zeta_{k+m}))^{s}\bigr|=\lambda_{s}^{j}|x^{s}-\zeta_{k+m}^{s}|\leq\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}}\leq\epsilon_{k},

and

\bigl|(f^{j}(x))^{u}-(f^{j}(\zeta_{k+m}))^{u}\bigr|=\lambda_{u}^{j}|x^{u}-\zeta_{k+m}^{u}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k,m}b_{k+m}\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}.

By (
3.3
),

b_{k+m}\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}=\lambda_{u}^{-2n_{k+m}^{H}}\sqrt{b_{k+m+1}}<1,

and therefore

\bigl|(f^{j}(x))^{u}-(f^{j}(\zeta_{k+m}))^{u}\bigr|<\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k,m}\leq\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k}.

Hence

\|f^{j}(x)-f^{j}(\zeta_{k+m})\|<2\epsilon_{k}<\rho.

Since
f^{j}(\zeta_{k+m})\in K^{\prime}
, we conclude that
f^{j}(x)\in K
.
This proves the claim.
∎

3.2.
Modified rectangle lemma

Now, we are in the position to prove the following main result of Section
3
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(M)
satisfies
DH
,
H
,
QT
and
OE
. Then, for every sufficiently large integer
k
and every non-negative integer
m
, it holds that

f^{n_{k+m}}(U_{k,m})\subset U_{k,m+1}.

The proof of this lemma follows immediately from the next two claims. Let
\frac{1}{2}U_{k,m}
denote the rectangle defined in the same way as
U_{k,m}
but replacing its width and height by half of those of
U_{k,m}
.

Figure 1.
The modified rectangle lemma

Claim 3.4
.

For every non-negative integer
m
, we have

f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell_{k+m}^{u})\subset\dfrac{1}{2}U_{k,m+1}.

Claim 3.5
.

For every non-negative integer
m
, we have

\dfrac{|f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell^{s}_{k+m}(x))|}{\mathrm{diam}(U_{k,m+1})}<\dfrac{1}{8}

for every
x\in\ell^{u}_{k+m}
.

Proof of Claim
3.4
.

We use

\pi^{s}_{k+m}:U_{k,m}\to\ell_{k+m}^{s}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\pi^{u}_{k+m}:U_{k,m}\to\ell_{k+m}^{u}

to denote the orthogonal projections to
\ell_{k+m}^{s}
and
\ell_{k+m}^{u}
respectively. Notice that
f^{n_{k+m}}(\zeta_{k+m})=\zeta_{k+m+1}
by condition
DH
. Thus, to prove the claim, let us estimate the lengths of the orthogonal projections of
f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell_{k+m}^{u})
. Define

\Lambda=\sup\big\{\|Df(x)v\|:x\in M,\ v\in T_{x}M,\ \|v\|=1\big\}.

For the orthogonal projections to
\ell^{s}_{k+m+1}
, since the segment
\ell_{k+m}^{u}
stays inside
K
for the first

n_{k+m}^{H}
iterates by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle|\pi^{s}_{k+m+1}(f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell^{u}_{k+m}))|

\displaystyle\leq|f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell^{u}_{k+m})|=|f^{n_{k+m}^{T}}\circ f^{n_{k+m}^{H}}(\ell^{u}_{k+m})|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda^{n_{k+m}^{T}}\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}|\ell_{k+m}^{u}|\leq\Lambda^{n_{k+m}^{T}}\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{k,m}b_{k+m}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{k,m}\Lambda^{n_{k+m}^{T}}\dfrac{\sqrt{b_{k+m+1}}}{\lambda_{u}^{2n_{k+m}^{H}}},

where the last equality comes from (
3.3
). By applying Claim
3.1
in the last inequality, we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\pi^{s}_{k+m+1}(f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell^{u}_{k+m}))|&\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{k,m+1}\sqrt{b_{k+m+1}}\dfrac{\Lambda^{n_{k+m}^{T}}}{\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}}\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{k,m+1}\sqrt{b_{k+m+1}}.\end{split}

The last inequality holds for every sufficiently large
k
because by
DH
, we have

n^{T}_{k+m}\log\Lambda-n^{H}_{k+m}\log\lambda_{u}=n^{H}_{k+m}\left(\dfrac{n^{T}_{k+m}}{n^{H}_{k+m}}\log\Lambda-\log\lambda_{u}\right)\to-\infty,\ (k\to\infty)

which gives
\Lambda^{n_{k+m}^{T}}/\lambda_{u}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}<1
for sufficiently large
k
.

For the orthogonal projections to
\ell^{u}_{k+m+1}
, by
QT
we have

\displaystyle|\pi^{u}_{k+m+1}(f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell^{u}_{k+m}))|

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}|\pi^{s}_{k+m+1}(f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell^{u}_{k+m}))|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\Big(\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{k,m+1}\sqrt{b_{k+m+1}}\Big)^{2}=\dfrac{C_{T}}{4}\epsilon^{2}_{k,m+1}b_{k+m+1}.

But the height of
U_{k,m+1}
is
\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{k,m+1}b_{k+m+1}
by our construction. Hence, the ratio of
|\pi^{u}_{k+m+1}(f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell^{u}_{k+m}))|
to the height of
U_{k,m+1}
is less than

\frac{1}{2}C_{T}\epsilon_{k,m+1}.

On the other hand, by the definition of

\epsilon_{k,m+1}
, we have

\epsilon_{k,m+1}\leq\epsilon_{k}

for every
m\geq 0
. Since
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, the above ratio tends to
0
uniformly in
m
. In particular, it is less than
\frac{1}{2}
for every sufficiently large
k
and every
m
. Combining (
3.8
), we obtain

f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell^{u}_{k+m})\subset\frac{1}{2}U_{k,m+1}

which completes the proof of Claim
3.4
.
∎

Proof of Claim
3.5
.

On the one hand, by Lemma
3.2
, for every
x\in\ell^{u}_{k+m}
, we have

\displaystyle|f^{n_{k+m}}(\ell^{s}_{k+m}(x))|

\displaystyle=|f^{n^{T}_{k+m}}\circ f^{n^{H}_{k+m}}(\ell^{s}_{k+m}(x))|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda^{n_{k+m}^{T}}\lambda_{s}^{n_{k+m}^{H}}|\ell^{s}_{k+m}(x)|

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{n_{k+m}^{T}}\lambda_{s}^{n^{H}_{k+m}}\cdot 2\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}}.

On the other hand, according to our construction,

\mathrm{diam}(U_{k,m+1})\geq\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k,m+1}b_{k+m+1}.

Therefore, it is enough to prove that

(3.9)

\sup_{m\geq 0}\Lambda^{n_{k+m}^{T}}\lambda_{s}^{n^{H}_{k+m}}\frac{\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}}}{\epsilon_{k,m+1}b_{k+m+1}}\longrightarrow 0\qquad(k\to\infty).

Set

R_{k,m}:=\Lambda^{n_{k+m}^{T}}\lambda_{s}^{n^{H}_{k+m}}\frac{\epsilon_{k,m}\sqrt{b_{k+m}}}{\epsilon_{k,m+1}b_{k+m+1}}.

By the fifth inequality of (
2.1
), choose
\beta^{\prime}>\beta
so close to
\beta
that

(3.10)

\theta:=\lambda_{s}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{6\beta^{\prime}}{2-\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2-\alpha}-\frac{9\beta-6}{2(2-\beta)}}<1.

For every
k,i\geq 0
, condition
OE
gives

(3.11)

n_{k+2i}^{H}>(\alpha\beta)^{i}n_{k}^{H}-1\qquad\text{and}\qquad n_{k+2i+1}^{H}>\alpha(\alpha\beta)^{i}n_{k}^{H}-1,

and also

n_{k+2i}^{H}<(\alpha\beta)^{i}(n_{k}^{H}+1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad n_{k+2i+1}^{H}<\beta(\alpha\beta)^{i}(n_{k}^{H}+1).

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{n_{k+i}^{H}}{2^{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{n_{k+2j}^{H}}{4^{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{n_{k+2j+1}^{H}}{4^{j}}

\displaystyle>\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha\beta)^{j}n_{k}^{H}-1}{4^{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha(\alpha\beta)^{j}n_{k}^{H}-1}{4^{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\alpha/2}{1-\alpha\beta/4}\,n_{k}^{H}-2,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{n_{k+i}^{H}}{2^{i}}

\displaystyle<\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha\beta)^{j}(n_{k}^{H}+1)}{4^{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\beta(\alpha\beta)^{j}(n_{k}^{H}+1)}{4^{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\beta/2}{1-\alpha\beta/4}\,(n_{k}^{H}+1).

Since

\frac{1+\alpha/2}{1-\alpha\beta/4}>\frac{2}{2-\alpha}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1+\beta/2}{1-\alpha\beta/4}<\frac{2}{2-\beta}<\frac{2}{2-\beta^{\prime}},

it follows that, for every sufficiently large
k
,

\frac{2}{2-\alpha}n_{k}^{H}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{n_{k+i}^{H}}{2^{i}}\leq\frac{2}{2-\beta^{\prime}}n_{k}^{H}.

Combining this with the definition of
b_{k}
in (
3.2
), we obtain

(3.12)

\lambda_{u}^{-\frac{6}{2-\beta^{\prime}}n_{k}^{H}}\leq b_{k}\leq\lambda_{u}^{-\frac{6}{2-\alpha}n_{k}^{H}}

for every sufficiently large
k
.
Furthermore,
since

\frac{n_{2p+1}^{H}}{n_{2p}^{H}}\to\alpha,\quad\frac{n_{2p+2}^{H}}{n_{2p+1}^{H}}\to\beta,\quad n_{j}^{H}\to\infty\quad\frac{n_{j}^{T}}{n_{j}^{H}}\to 0,

by
DH
and
OE
,
we have

(3.13)

n_{j}^{H}\leq n_{j+1}^{H}\leq\beta^{\prime}n_{j}^{H},\quad n_{j}^{H}\leq n_{j}^{H}+1\leq 2n_{j}^{H},\quad\Lambda^{n_{j}^{T}}\leq\theta^{-\,n_{j}^{H}/(4\beta^{\prime})}

for sufficiently large
j
.

Next, we claim that

\alpha\beta<\frac{3}{2}.

Indeed, if
\alpha\beta\geq\frac{3}{2}
, then the second inequality of (
2.1
) gives

0>\frac{5}{2}\alpha-2-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\geq\frac{5}{2}\alpha-2-\frac{2}{3},

and hence

\alpha<\frac{16}{15}.

On the other hand, the inequality

\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}}>\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6}{2-\beta}}

in (
2.1
) implies

\beta<\frac{2(7\alpha+6)}{18+7\alpha}.

Therefore,

\alpha\beta<\alpha\cdot\frac{2(7\alpha+6)}{18+7\alpha}\leq\frac{16}{15}\cdot\frac{2(7\cdot\frac{16}{15}+6)}{18+7\cdot\frac{16}{15}}<\frac{3}{2},

which is a contradiction.
Consequently, we get

(3.14)

\epsilon_{k,m+1}\geq\epsilon_{k,m}^{3/2}\qquad\text{for every }m\geq 0.

Indeed, since
0<\epsilon_{k,m}<1
, if
m
is even, then

\epsilon_{k,m+1}=\epsilon_{k,m}\geq\epsilon_{k,m}^{3/2},

while if
m
is odd, then

\epsilon_{k,m+1}=\epsilon_{k,m}^{\alpha\beta}\geq\epsilon_{k,m}^{3/2}.

Now fix
k
so large that
(
3.12
) and (
3.13
) hold for every integer
j\geq k
.
Then, we obtain

\sqrt{b_{k+m}}\leq\lambda_{u}^{-\frac{3}{2-\alpha}n_{k+m}^{H}}
and

\frac{1}{b_{k+m+1}}\leq\lambda_{u}^{\frac{6}{2-\beta^{\prime}}n_{k+m+1}^{H}}
from (
3.12
).
Therefore, it follows from (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle R_{k,m}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda^{n^{T}_{k+m}}\lambda_{s}^{n^{H}_{k+m}}\frac{\epsilon_{k,m}}{\epsilon_{k,m+1}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{6}{2-\beta^{\prime}}n^{H}_{k+m+1}-\frac{3}{2-\alpha}n^{H}_{k+m}}.

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda^{n^{T}_{k+m}}\lambda_{s}^{n^{H}_{k+m}}\frac{\epsilon_{k,m}}{\epsilon_{k,m+1}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{6\beta^{\prime}}{2-\beta^{\prime}}n^{H}_{k+m}-\frac{3}{2-\alpha}n^{H}_{k+m}}.

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda^{n^{T}_{k+m}}\frac{\Bigl(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{6\beta^{\prime}}{2-\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2-\alpha}}\Bigr)^{n^{H}_{k+m}}}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{k,m}}}.

Hence, since
n_{k+m}^{H}\geq(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}n_{k}^{H}-1
by (
3.11
), setting
C:=\Bigl(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{6\beta^{\prime}}{2-\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2-\alpha}}\Bigr)^{-1}
, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{k,m}&\leq C\Lambda^{n^{T}_{k+m}}\frac{\Bigl(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{6\beta^{\prime}}{2-\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2-\alpha}}\Bigr)^{(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}n_{k}^{H}}}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{k,m}}}\\
&=C\Lambda^{n^{T}_{k+m}}\Biggl(\lambda_{s}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{6\beta^{\prime}}{2-\beta^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2-\alpha}-\frac{9\beta-6+18(n_{k}^{H})^{-1}}{2(2-\beta)}}\Biggr)^{(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}n_{k}^{H}}\\
&\leq C\Lambda^{n^{T}_{k+m}}\theta^{(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}n_{k}^{H}},\end{split}

where we used
\lambda_{u}>1
in the last inequality.

Now we compare
(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}n_{k}^{H}
with
n_{k+m}^{H}
.
If
m=2t
, then by
OE
and (
3.13

(\alpha\beta)^{\lceil\frac{l}{2}\rceil}\log\Bigl(\lambda_{s}^{1+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}}\Bigr)+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}\log\xi\geq(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}\log\Bigl(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6+18(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}{2-\beta}}\Bigr)+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}\log(2\lambda_{u}),

which is equivalent to

\xi^{(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}\Bigl(\lambda_{s}^{1+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}}\Bigr)^{(\alpha\beta)^{\lceil l/2\rceil}}\geq(2\lambda_{u})^{(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}\Bigl(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6+18(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}{2-\beta}}\Bigr)^{(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor l/2\rfloor}}.

Raising both sides to the power

, we obtain

(4.16)

\lambda_{u}^{-1}\xi\Bigl(\lambda_{s}^{1+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}}\Bigr)^{n_{k_{0}}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{\lceil l/2\rceil}}\geq 2\Bigl(\lambda_{s}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{9\beta-6+18(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}{2-\beta}}\Bigr)^{n_{k_{0}}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor l/2\rfloor}}=2\epsilon_{k_{0},l}.

Since

is even, the explicit formulas in
OE
give

n_{k_{0}+l}^{H}\geq n_{k_{0}}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor l/2\rfloor}-1\qquad\text{and}\qquad n_{k_{0}+l+1}^{H}\geq n_{k_{0}}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{\lceil l/2\rceil}-1.

Hence

(4.17)

\begin{split}\lambda_{u}^{-n_{k_{0}+l}^{H}}&\leq\Bigl(\lambda_{u}^{-(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor l/2\rfloor-\lceil l/2\rceil}+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}(\alpha\beta)^{-\lceil l/2\rceil}}\Bigr)^{n_{k_{0}}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{\lceil l/2\rceil}},\\
\lambda_{u}^{-n_{k_{0}+l+1}^{H}}&\leq\Bigl(\lambda_{u}^{-1+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}(\alpha\beta)^{-\lceil l/2\rceil}}\Bigr)^{n_{k_{0}}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{\lceil l/2\rceil}}.\end{split}

From the construction (

) of

, it holds that

Indeed, since

by repeated use of (

Using this together with (
3.3
), (
4.4
), (
4.16

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k_{0},l}b_{k_{0}+l}\lambda_{u}^{2n_{k_{0}+l}^{H}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{k_{0},l}\lambda_{u}^{-n_{k_{0}+l}^{H}}\sqrt{b_{k_{0}+l+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{k_{0},l}\lambda_{u}^{-n_{k_{0}+l}^{H}}\lambda_{u}^{-3n_{k_{0}+l+1}^{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{u}^{-1}\xi\Bigl(\lambda_{s}^{1+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}}\Bigr)^{n_{k_{0}}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{\lceil l/2\rceil}}\lambda_{u}^{-n_{k_{0}+l}^{H}}\lambda_{u}^{-3n_{k_{0}+l+1}^{H}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{u}^{-1}\xi\Bigl(\lambda_{s}^{1+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}}\Bigr)^{n_{k_{0}}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{\lceil l/2\rceil}}\lambda_{u}^{-2n_{k_{0}+l+1}^{H}}\lambda_{u}^{-n_{k_{0}+l}^{H}}\lambda_{u}^{-n_{k_{0}+l+1}^{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{u}^{-1}\xi\Bigl(\lambda_{s}^{1+(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}}\lambda_{u}^{\frac{7\alpha}{2}-(\alpha\beta)^{\lfloor l/2\rfloor-\lceil l/2\rceil}-2+3(n_{k_{0}}^{H})^{-1}(\alpha\beta)^{-\lceil l/2\rceil}}\Bigr)^{n_{k_{0}}^{H}(\alpha\beta)^{\lceil l/2\rceil}}\lambda_{u}^{-n_{k_{0}+l+1}^{H}}

(4.18)

Lemma 5.2
.

It holds that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\log\Lambda_{u}^{(2m)}}{N_{2m}}=\frac{\alpha\log\lambda_{u}+\log\lambda_{s}}{1+\alpha}<\frac{\beta\log\lambda_{u}+\log\lambda_{s}}{1+\beta}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\log\Lambda_{u}^{(2m-1)}}{N_{2m-1}}.
