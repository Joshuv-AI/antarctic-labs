Introduction

Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be the one-dimensional unit torus. Throughout, we will use the identification
\mathbb{T}\simeq[0,1)
. We consider the problem of uniformly distributing a given number of points in the two-dimensional torus
[0,1)^{2}
. To analyze this problem rigorously we need to quantify the
uniformity
of a finite point set
X\subseteq[0,1)^{2}
.

Three point sets
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
(from left to right) of size
N=45
with
e_{2,6}(X_{1})=0.037156\dots
,
e_{2,6}(X_{2})=0.006865\dots
and
e_{2,6}(X_{3})=0.001879\dots
(
(\sigma,p)=(2,6)
corresponding to the notion of periodic
L_{2}

). Observe how point sets with high and low density regions (
clusters
and
holes
respectively) correspond to bigger
e_{\sigma,p}(X)
.

1.1
Uniformity via energy minimization

Discrepancy theory
and the analysis of
quasi-Monte Carlo integration
are natural areas where the notion of the uniformity of a point set shows up. Background and details in these directions can be found for example in
[
27
,
40
,
42
,
49
,
50
,
51
]
. For the purposes of this paper, a finite point set
X\subseteq[0,1)^{2},\#X=N
(which may be a multiset, that is it may contain the same point multiple times) will be thought of as uniform if it minimizes

e_{\sigma,p}(X)\coloneqq-1+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{(x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\in X}K_{\sigma,p}(x_{1}-y_{1})K_{\sigma,p}(x_{2}-y_{2}),

where
\sigma>1
,
p>0
are parameters and the function
K_{\sigma,p}:[0,1)\mathbb{R}
is given by

K_{\sigma,p}(t)\coloneqq 1+p\sum_{m\neq 0}\frac{\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}mt)}{|2\pi m|^{\sigma}}.

Figure
1.1
illustrates how
e_{\sigma,p}(X)
corresponds to the “uniformity” of
X
. The quantities
e_{\sigma,p}(X)
can be interpreted as the squared worst-case error of the quasi-Monte Carlo integration algorithm for periodic functions of prescribed smoothness, see for example
[
8
,
22
,
33
]
, formalizing the connection between low
e_{\sigma,p}(X)
and uniform
X
. The case of
\sigma=2
corresponds to the notions of
periodic
L_{2}
-discrepancy
and
diaphony
, see
[
8
,
32
]
for details.

For a given
N\in\mathbb{N}
the main objective is to find a point set
X\subseteq[0,1)^{2},\#X=N
such that for all
Y\subseteq[0,1)^{2},\#Y=N
it holds
e_{\sigma,p}(Y)\geq e_{\sigma,p}(X)
. This is equivalent to minimizing the double sum

E_{\sigma,p}(X)\coloneqq\sum_{(x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\in X}K_{\sigma,p}(x_{1}-y_{1})K_{\sigma,p}(x_{2}-y_{2}),

which (in analogy to the
Thomson problem

[
55
]
, also see
[
12
,
17
,
31
]
) we interpret as the
energy
of a point configuration
X
with
potential interaction

K_{\sigma,p}(x_{1}-y_{1})K_{\sigma,p}(x_{2}-y_{2})
between two points
(x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\in[0,1)^{2}
. If
\sigma=2s,s\in\mathbb{N}
is an even integer, we have more explicitly

K_{2s,p}(t)=1+p\frac{(-1)^{s-1}}{(2s)!}B_{2s}(t)

with the (periodized)
Bernoulli polynomials

B_{2s}(t)
of degree
2s
, in general given via

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}e^{tx}

for
t\in[0,1)
. The next section introduces a class of point sets which will yield candidates for minimizers of
e_{\sigma,p}(X)
.

3,5,8,13

21

1.2
Lattices

Given two numbers
N\in\mathbb{N},h\in\{1,\dots,N\}
with
\gcd(N,h)=1
, the
(rational/integration) lattice

\Lambda_{N,h}\subseteq[0,1)^{2}
is given by

\Lambda_{N,h}\coloneqq\left\{\left(\frac{k}{N},\left\{\frac{hk}{N}\right\}\right):k=0,1,\dots,N-1\right\},

where
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the fractional part of
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Letting
(F_{n})_{n}
denote the
Fibonacci numbers
given by
F_{0}=0,F_{1}=1
and the recurrence
F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
, the special lattices given by

\Phi_{n}\coloneqq\Lambda_{F_{n},F_{n-1}}

are called
Fibonacci lattices
, see Figure
1.2
. It is known that Fibonacci lattices achieve the optimal asymptotic rate
[
28
]

e_{\sigma,p}(\Phi_{n})\asymp\frac{n}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}.

It has been observed on multiple occasions, based on numerical computations
[
33
]
and partial results
[
8
,
46
]
, also see
[
45
]
, that one even seems to have
e_{\sigma,p}(Y)\geq e_{\sigma,p}(\Phi_{n})
for all
Y\subseteq[0,1)^{2},\#Y=F_{n}
given that
0<p\leq\overline{p}(\sigma)
for some constant
\overline{p}(\sigma)>0
. That is, Fibonacci lattices are conjectured to be global minimizers for a certain range of parameters. This, however, remains an open problem. In this paper we aim to study the special structure of the Fibonacci lattice by considering the asymptotics of
e_{\sigma,p}(\Phi_{n})
in more detail.

To start, we will consider more general energies of the form

\displaystyle E_{c}(X)\coloneqq\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X}c(x_{1}-y_{1})c(x_{2}-y_{2}),

(1)

where
X\subseteq[0,1)^{2}
and
c:[0,1)\mathbb{R}
is a
1
-periodic, continuous and symmetric function in the sense of
c(t)=c(1-t)
for
t\in[0,1)
. Functions
c
fulfilling these criteria will be called
potentials
. The systematic study of these
tensor product energies
has recently been initiated in
[
8
,
44
,
46
]
. Such energies behave well together with the structure of lattices as shown by the following result.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\Lambda_{N,h}\subseteq[0,1)^{2}
be a lattice and let
c:[0,1)\mathbb{R}
be a potential. Assume that we have the discrete Fourier expansion

\displaystyle c\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\hat{c}_{N}(m)\exp\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}m\frac{k}{N}\right),

(2)

valid for all
k=0,1,\dots,N-1
. Then

E_{c}(\Lambda_{N,h})=N^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\hat{c}_{N}(m)\hat{c}_{N}(hm),

where
\hat{c}_{N}
is regarded as an
N
-periodic function.

Proof.

Inserting (
2
) into (
1
) one obtains

\displaystyle E_{c}(\Lambda_{N,h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{N-1}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\hat{c}_{N}(m)\exp\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}m\frac{k-\ell}{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~\times\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\hat{c}_{N}(n)\exp\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}nh\frac{k-\ell}{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{N-1}\hat{c}_{N}(m)\hat{c}_{N}(n)\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{N-1}\exp\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}(m+nh)\frac{k-\ell}{N}\right).

The last double sum is zero unless
m+hn\equiv 0\mod N
, in which case it equates to
N^{2}
, and thus

E_{c}(\Lambda_{N,h})=N^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\hat{c}_{N}(-hn)\hat{c}_{N}(n)=N^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\hat{c}_{N}(m)\hat{c}_{N}(hm),

since, by symmetry
c(t)=c(1-t)
, it holds
\hat{c}_{N}(m)=\hat{c}_{N}(-m)
.
∎

If the potential is given by
c(t)=K_{\sigma,p}(t)
, so that
E_{c}(X)=E_{\sigma,p}(X)
, the discrete Fourier representation takes the form
[
20
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}&1+p\sum_{m\neq 0}\frac{\exp\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}m\frac{k}{N}\right)}{|2\pi m|^{\sigma}}\\
=~&1+p\frac{2\zeta(\sigma)}{(2\pi N)^{\sigma}}+p\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\frac{\zeta\left(\sigma,\frac{m}{N}\right)+\zeta\left(\sigma,1-\frac{m}{N}\right)}{(2\pi N)^{\sigma}}\exp\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}m\frac{k}{N}\right)\end{split}

(3)

for
k=0,1,\dots,N-1
. Here
\zeta(\sigma)=\zeta(\sigma,1)
denotes the
Riemann zeta function
and more generally
\zeta(\sigma,a)
the
Hurwitz zeta function

\zeta(\sigma,a)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+a)^{\sigma}}.

In the even integer case
\sigma=2s,s\in\mathbb{N}
these expressions can be given more explicitly by
[
21
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}&1+p\sum_{m\neq 0}\frac{\exp\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}m\frac{k}{N}\right)}{(2\pi m)^{2s}}=1+p\frac{(-1)^{s-1}}{(2s)!}B_{2s}\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\\
=~&1+p\frac{(-1)^{s-1}B_{2s}}{(2s)!N^{2s}}+\frac{p}{(2s-1)!(2N)^{2s}}\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\frac{g^{(2s-1)}\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)}{\pi^{2s-1}}\exp\left(2\pi\mathrm{i}m\frac{k}{N}\right)\end{split}

(4)

with
g^{(2s-1)}(x)
denoting the
(2s-1)
-th derivative of
g(x)\coloneqq-\cot(\pi x)
and
B_{2s}=B_{2s}(0)
being the
Bernoulli numbers
. In terms of
e_{\sigma,p}(\Lambda_{N,h})
, equivalently the energy
E_{\sigma,p}(\Lambda_{N,h})
, by Proposition
1.1
we thus arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{split}e_{\sigma,p}(\Lambda_{N,h})=~&p\frac{4\zeta(\sigma)}{(2\pi N)^{\sigma}}+p^{2}\frac{4\zeta(\sigma)^{2}}{(2\pi N)^{2\sigma}}\\
&+\frac{p^{2}}{(2\pi N)^{2\sigma}}\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\left(\zeta\left(\sigma,\frac{m}{N}\right)+\zeta\left(\sigma,1-\frac{m}{N}\right)\right)\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times\left(\zeta\left(\sigma,\left\{h\frac{m}{N}\right\}\right)+\zeta\left(\sigma,1-\left\{h\frac{m}{N}\right\}\right)\right).\end{split}

(5)

Analyzing the last sum in the case of the Fibonacci lattice
\Phi_{n}=\Lambda_{F_{n},F_{n-1}}
will be the main objective of this paper.

1.3
Main result and intuition

Since
\zeta(\sigma,a)=a^{-\sigma}+\zeta(\sigma,a+1)=a^{-\sigma}+O(1)
as
a\searrow 0
, we see that for
\sigma>1
there is a
1
-periodic and continuously differentiable function
f_{\sigma}:[0,1)\mathbb{R}
such that

\zeta(\sigma,a)+\zeta(\sigma,1-a)=\frac{f_{\sigma}(a)}{\sin(\pi a)^{\sigma}}

for all
0<a<1
(with
f_{\sigma}(0)=f_{\sigma}(1)=\pi^{\sigma}
and
f_{\sigma}^{\prime}(0)=f_{\sigma}^{\prime}(1)=0
).

For the statement below we will say that a
1
-periodic function
f:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous
for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
if there is a constant
L_{f}\geq 0
such that
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq L_{f}|x-y|^{\alpha}
for all
x,y\in[0,1)
. By the above,
f_{\sigma}
is
1
-Hölder continuous as a continuously differentiable function, that is
Lipschitz continuous
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\sigma>1
and let
f:[0,1)\mathbb{R}
be
1
-periodic, symmetric (in the sense of
f(t)=f(1-t)
) and
\alpha
-Hölder continuous for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}\sum_{m=1}^{F_{n}-1}\frac{f(m/F_{n})}{\left|{\sin(\pi m/F_{n})}\right|^{\sigma}}\frac{f(F_{n-1}m/F_{n})}{\left|{\sin(\pi F_{n-1}m/F_{n})}\right|^{\sigma}}=Cn+D+O\left(\frac{n^{2}}{\phi^{\min\{\alpha,\sigma-1\}n/2}}\right)

(6)

as
n\infty
for some constants
C=C(\sigma,f),D=D(\sigma,f)\in\mathbb{R}
.

The constants
C
and
D
will depend only on
\sigma
and
f
and can be determined to arbitrary precision via certain infinite series, see (
27
) and (
28
) below, where
C
can be given explicitly via values of a certain Dedekind zeta function, see (
32
) below. While the value for
C
was essentially already determined in
[
11
]
, to the knowledge of the authors no investigation into lower-order terms has been carried out before. Our main takeaway here is the following:

If Fibonacci lattices turn out to be minimizers for the quantities
e_{\sigma,p}
(or even general energies
E_{c}
), then Theorem
1.2
gives a precise control of their asymptotics via (
5
).

Since we want to give a for the most part self-contained account (only using certain number theoretic formulas in the last two parts of this paper for calculating specific values) and do not assume deeper prerequisites in number theory, in particular avoiding class field theory as is frequently employed in this context
[
3
,
10
,
11
]
, we aim to give some intuition for Theorem
1.2
demonstrated on the example of

\displaystyle\Sigma\coloneqq\sum_{m=1}^{F_{n}-1}\frac{1}{F_{n}^{2}\sin(\pi m/{F_{n}})^{2}\sin(\pi F_{n-1}m/{F_{n}})^{2}}.

(7)

This is, up to a simple factor, the final sum in (

) with

. Consider the plot in Figure

2.1
Basic definitions and properties

Let

\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}

denote the golden ration. The
Wythoff array

(W_{i,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}},i\in\mathbb{N}

[
43
]
is given by

W_{i,1}=\lfloor\phi\lfloor\phi i\rfloor\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad W_{i,2}=\lfloor\phi^{2}\lfloor\phi i\rfloor\rfloor

for
k=1,2
and via the recurrence

\displaystyle W_{i,k}=W_{i,k-1}+W_{i,k-2}

(10)

for
k\geq 3
. Up to a shift in the index, the first sequence
(W_{1,k})_{k}
is given by the Fibonacci numbers
W_{1,k}=F_{k+1}
. Some basic and classic results will be summarized in the following statement.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(W_{i,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}},i\in\mathbb{N}
be the Wythoff array.

(i)

For
i,k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

W_{i,k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(w_{+}(i)\phi^{k}-w_{-}(i)\left(-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)^{k}\right)

with
w_{+}(i)=\phi^{-2}W_{i,1}+\phi^{-1}W_{i,2}
and
w_{-}(i)=\phi^{2}W_{i,1}-\phi W_{i,2}
.

(ii)

For
i\in\mathbb{N}
set
\eta_{i}\coloneqq-w_{+}(i)w_{-}(i)\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for all
k\geq 2
it holds

\eta_{i}=(-1)^{k}\left(W_{i,k}^{2}-W_{i,k}W_{i,k-1}-W_{i,k-1}^{2}\right).

(iii)

For
i,k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

W_{i,k+1}=\lfloor\phi W_{i,k}+\phi^{-1}\rfloor.

(iv)

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
there is a unique tuple
(i,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
N=W_{i,k}
.

(v)

If
(U_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence with
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
fulfilling the Fibonacci recurrence
U_{n}=U_{n-1}+U_{n-2}
, then there is a unique
i\in\mathbb{N}
for which there are
\nu\in\mathbb{N},\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
U_{n}=W_{i,n+\kappa}
for all
n\geq\nu
.

Proof.

Points (i) and (ii) are simple consequences of the recurrence (
10

\eta_{i}>0

and from Lemma
A.1
(i) with

we have

w_{-}(i)=\phi^{2}\lfloor\phi\lfloor\phi i\rfloor\rfloor-\phi\lfloor\phi^{2}\lfloor\phi i\rfloor\rfloor<0

(also see (
13

A.1
(ii) with
N=\lfloor\phi i\rfloor
we obtain the base case
W_{i,2}=\lfloor\phi W_{i,1}+\phi^{-1}\rfloor
. By Lemma
A.1
(iii) with
N=W_{i,k-1}
we then have inductively for

\displaystyle W_{i,k+1}

\displaystyle=W_{i,k}+W_{i,k-1}=\lfloor\phi W_{i,k-1}+\phi^{-1}\rfloor+W_{i,k-1}

\displaystyle=\lfloor\phi\lfloor\phi W_{i,k-1}+\phi^{-1}\rfloor+\phi^{-1}\rfloor=\lfloor\phi W_{i,k}+\phi^{-1}\rfloor.

Points (iv) and (v) are proven in

By Theorem

(i) thus

\displaystyle w_{+}(i)=\phi\lfloor\phi i\rfloor+i-1\quad\text{and}\quad w_{-}(i)=-\phi^{-1}\lfloor\phi i\rfloor+i-1=\phi^{-1}\{\phi i\}-1.

(12)

In particular we see that

\displaystyle\phi^{-2}<-w_{-}(i)<1

(13)

for
i\in\mathbb{N}
. For
\eta_{i}
we obtain

\eta_{i}=\sqrt{5}i(\phi-\{\phi i\})-\{\phi i\}(1-\{\phi i\})-1.

The quantities
w_{+}(i)
and
\eta_{i}
thus grow linearly in
i
with the explicit bounds

\displaystyle\phi i\leq w_{+}(i)<(\phi^{2}+1)i\quad\text{and}\quad i\leq\eta_{i}<(\phi^{2}+1)i

(14)

holding for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

The Wythoff array will be an essential tool in analyzing the sum in (
6
). Important aspects will be discussed in the next part.

2.2
Dual array

In (
8
) we have seen that we are only interested in those terms
W_{i,k}
which are strictly less than
F_{n}/2
. In this subsection we will analyze which terms these are. To understand the edge case, we will first look where equality occurs. Notice that
F_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
is even if and only if
n=3\ell
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
i,k,n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

W_{i,k}=\frac{F_{n}}{2}

if and only if

(i,k,n)=\left(\frac{F_{3\ell-2}+1}{2},1,3\ell\right)

for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Since by Theorem
2.1
(iv) the equation
W_{i,k}={F_{n}}/2
has a unique solution in
i,k\in\mathbb{N}
for a fixed
n=3\ell
, it suffices to show that

W_{\frac{1}{2}(F_{3\ell-2}+1),1}=\frac{F_{3\ell}}{2}

holds for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. We will use the well-known formula

\displaystyle F_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\phi^{n}-\left(-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)^{n}\right)

(15)

and the straightforward facts that

\frac{F_{3\ell-2}+1}{2},\frac{F_{3\ell-1}+1}{2},\frac{F_{3\ell}}{2}\in\mathbb{N}.

By

\phi\frac{F_{3\ell-2}+1}{2}-\frac{F_{3\ell-1}+1}{2}=\frac{\phi-1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)^{3\ell-2}\in(0,1)

and

\phi\frac{F_{3\ell-1}+1}{2}-\frac{F_{3\ell}}{2}=\frac{\phi}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)^{3\ell-1}\in(0,1)

we have

W_{\frac{1}{2}(F_{3\ell-2}+1),1}=\left\lfloor\phi\left\lfloor\phi\frac{F_{3\ell-2}+1}{2}\right\rfloor\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\phi\frac{F_{3\ell-1}+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\frac{F_{3\ell}}{2}

as desired.
∎

In particular we see that the numbers
F_{3\ell}/2
always appear at the very start of a sequence
(W_{i,k})_{k}
.

Remark 2.4
.

Having shown above that

\left\lfloor\phi\frac{F_{3\ell-2}+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\frac{F_{3\ell-1}+1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lfloor\phi\frac{F_{3\ell-1}+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\frac{F_{3\ell}}{2},

it is natural to ask if one can similarly go from
\frac{1}{2}F_{3\ell}
to
\frac{1}{2}(F_{3\ell+1}+1)
. This is indeed the case with some modification. By

\phi\frac{F_{3\ell}}{2}+\phi^{-1}-\frac{F_{3\ell+1}+1}{2}=\frac{2-\phi}{2\phi}-\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)^{3\ell}\in(0,1)

we get

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\phi\frac{F_{3\ell}}{2}+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor=\frac{F_{3\ell+1}+1}{2}.

(16)

Note the similarities with Theorem
2.1
(iii).

We now characterize the indices
k
for which
W_{i,k}<F_{n}/2
holds.

Theorem 2.5
.

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
there is a
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

W_{i,k}<\frac{1}{2}F_{n}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad n-k>\mu_{i}.

Proof.

The proof is via induction on
k
. The case of
k=1
gives us the value for
\mu_{i}
. For the induction step, the cases
k=2
and
k\geq 3
will be considered separately. To better organize the proof we will do it in multiple small steps.

Step 1,
k=1
.
Set

\displaystyle\mu_{i}\coloneqq\min\left\{m\in\mathbb{N}:W_{i,1}<\frac{1}{2}F_{m+2}\right\}\geq 2,

(17)

so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+1}\leq W_{i,1}<\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+2}.

(18)

By the strict monotonicity of
(F_{n})_{n\geq 2}
this is equivalent to the statement for
k=1
.

Step 2,
k=2
.
Before coming to the case of general
k
we will first show that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+2}<W_{i,2}<\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+3},

(19)

which is the statement for
k=2
in a slightly stronger form.

Step 2.1, the right inequality.
Starting from (
18
), since
W_{i,1},F_{\mu_{i}+2}\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

W_{i,1}<\frac{1}{2}(2W_{i,1}+1)\leq\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+2}.

By Lemma
A.1
(iv) with
N=W_{i,1}
and Theorem
2.1
(iii) we then have

\displaystyle W_{i,2}

\displaystyle=\lfloor\phi W_{i,1}+\phi^{-1}\rfloor<\frac{1}{2}\lfloor\phi(2W_{i,1}+1)+\phi^{-1}\rfloor

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\lfloor\phi F_{\mu_{i}+2}+\phi^{-1}\rfloor=\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+3},

giving the right inequality in (
19
).

Step 2.2, the left inequality.
For the left inequality we need to consider two cases.

Step 2.2.1, the case of a strict inequality.
If
\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+1}<W_{i,1}
, then the left inequality of (
19
) follows in an analogous manner as in step 2.1, now using the left inequality of Lemma
A.1
(iv).

Step 2.2.2, the case of equality.
If
\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+1}=W_{i,1}
then by Lemma
2.3
we have (among others)
F_{\mu_{i}+1}=F_{3\ell}
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Using (
16
) it then follows that

\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+2}=\frac{1}{2}F_{3\ell+1}<\left\lfloor\phi\frac{F_{3\ell}}{2}+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor=\lfloor\phi W_{i,1}+\phi^{-1}\rfloor=W_{i,2},

also giving the left inequality of (
19
). This fully establishes (
19
).

Step 3,
k\geq 3
.
Combining (
18
) and (
19
) with the recurrence relation (
10
) for
(W_{i,k})_{k}
the desired claim follows.
∎

The number
\mu_{i}
can be seen as a characteristic of
(W_{i,k})_{k}
, similar to
\eta_{i}
above. We even have the following simple expression.

Proposition 2.6
.

For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\mu_{i}=\lfloor\log_{\phi}(2w_{+}(i))\rfloor.

For the proof we need an auxiliary result.

Lemma 2.7
.

For all
m,i\in\mathbb{N}
it holds
\phi^{m}\neq 2w_{+}(i)
.

Proof.

For the sake of contradiction assume there are
m,i\in\mathbb{N}
with
\phi^{m}=2w_{+}(i)
. Using (
12
) this would be equivalent to

F_{m}\phi+F_{m-1}=\phi^{m}=2w_{+}(i)=2\lfloor\phi i\rfloor\phi+2(i-1),

by irrationality of
\phi
thus
F_{m}=2\lfloor\phi i\rfloor
and
F_{m-1}=2(i-1)
. However, no two consecutive terms of the Fibonacci sequences are both even (see the remark before Lemma
2.3
), giving the desired contradiction.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

From Theorem
2.5
we have
\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+k}\leq W_{i,k}<\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu_{i}+k+1}
for all

, which by (

) and Theorem

\phi^{\mu_{i}+k}-\left(-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)^{\mu_{i}+k}\leq 2\left(w_{+}(i)\phi^{k}-w_{-}(i)\left(-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)^{k}\right)<\phi^{\mu_{i}+k+1}-\left(-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)^{\mu_{i}+k+1}.

Dividing by
\phi^{k}
and letting

go to infinity we get
\phi^{\mu_{i}}\leq 2w_{+}(i)\leq\phi^{\mu_{i}+1}
. By Lemma
2.7
the equality cases cannot occur, so that even

and the claim follows.
∎

For

Lemma 2.8
.

For

with

3

In this section we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
and a closed-form expression of the constant

3.1
Approximations

Note that, using (
20
), (
23
) as well as the periodicity and symmetry of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{f(F_{n-1}W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{\left|{\sin(\pi F_{n-1}W_{i,k}/F_{n})}\right|^{\sigma}}=\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}.

(24)

Using the asymptotics of
W_{i,k}
and
W_{i,n-k}^{*}
we approximate

\frac{1}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\approx\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}

for small
k
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\approx\frac{f(w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(n-k)})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(n-k)})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}(W_{i,n-k}^{*})^{\sigma}}

for large
k
. We will bound the error in these approximations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\sigma>1
and
f:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-periodic,
\alpha
-Hölder continuous function for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
. For
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mu_{i}
be as in Theorem
2.5
and let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k<n-\mu_{i}
. There are constants
A=A(\sigma,f,\alpha),A^{*}=A^{*}(\sigma,f,\alpha)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}\right|\leq\frac{A}{\phi^{\alpha(n-k)}i^{\sigma-\alpha}}

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(n-k)})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(n-k)})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}(W_{i,n-k}^{*})^{\sigma}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\frac{A^{*}}{\phi^{\alpha k}i^{\sigma-\alpha}}.

Proof.

We first make some preparation. Every time we write
x\lesssim y
or
x\gtrsim y
we mean the corresponding inequality up to a constant independent of
n,k,i
(but possibly depending on
\sigma,f,\alpha
). The inequality

\displaystyle 0<-\frac{w_{-}(i)}{w_{+}(i)}\leq\phi^{-2},

(25)

easily derived from (
12
), will be used multiple times. From Proposition
2.6
we have
n-k>\mu_{i}=\lfloor\log_{\phi}(2w_{+}(i))\rfloor
and thus even

\displaystyle\phi^{n-k}>2w_{+}(i)\gtrsim i.

(26)

Furthermore, from (
17
) we note that
n>k+\mu_{i}\geq 1+2
, so that we may assume
n\geq 4
. By Lemma
2.8
in particular

\frac{W_{i,n-k}^{*}}{F_{n}}<\frac{F_{n-k}}{F_{n}}\leq\frac{F_{n-1}}{F_{n}}\leq\frac{2}{3},

similar to

\frac{W_{i,k}}{F_{n}}<\frac{1}{2}

by the defining property of
\mu_{i}
. We thus may (and will) apply the inequalities of Lemma
A.3
for
x,y\in\{W_{i,k}/F_{n},W_{i,n-k}^{*}/F_{n}\}
.

We will frequently use that
f
is bounded as a periodic and continuous function, in particular
|f(t)|\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1
for all
t\in[0,1)
with the supremum norm.

We will show the first inequality, the second can be dealt with in an analogous way. First and foremost, by the triangle inequality we can write

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{F_{n}^{\sigma}{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle|\text{I}|+|\text{II}|+|\text{III}|+|\text{IV}|,

where the symbols
|\text{I}|,|\text{II}|,|\text{III}|,|\text{IV}|
denote the expressions

\displaystyle|\text{I}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{F_{n}^{\sigma}{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{{\sin(\pi W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{F_{n}^{\sigma}{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}\right|,

\displaystyle|\text{II}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{F_{n}^{\sigma}{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}\right|,

\displaystyle|\text{III}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}\right|,

\displaystyle|\text{IV}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W^{*}_{i,n-k}/F_{n})}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}\right|.

These four terms will be bounded individually.

For the first term we have by Lemma
A.3
(i)

\displaystyle|\text{I}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\left|\frac{1}{\sin(\pi W_{i,n-k}^{*}/F_{n})^{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}\right|.

Noting that by (
14
) and (
25
)

\frac{1}{W_{i,k}}=\frac{1}{w_{+}(i)\phi^{k}}\frac{\sqrt{5}}{1-\frac{w_{-}(i)}{w_{+}(i)}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\phi^{2k}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{k}i}\frac{\sqrt{5}}{1-\phi^{-2}\cdot\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{k}i},

by (
13
) and (
26
)

\displaystyle\frac{F_{n}}{W_{i,n-k}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi^{n}}{\phi^{n-k}}\frac{1-(-1)^{n}\phi^{-2n}}{w_{+}^{*}(i)-w_{-}^{*}(i)\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{\phi^{2(n-k)}}}=\phi^{k}\frac{1-(-1)^{n}\phi^{-2n}}{-w_{-}(i)+\frac{w_{+}(i)}{\phi^{n-k}}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{\phi^{n-k}}}\leq\phi^{k}\frac{1+\phi^{-10}}{\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\phi^{3}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\phi^{k}.

and by Lemma
A.3
(ii), (
13
), (
25
) and (
26
)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\sin\left(\pi\frac{W_{i,n-k}^{*}}{F_{n}}\right)^{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{\sin\left(\pi\frac{w_{+}^{*}(i)}{\phi^{k}}\right)^{\sigma}}\right|\leq\left(\frac{F_{n}}{W_{i,n-k}^{*}}+\frac{\phi^{k}}{w_{+}^{*}(i)}\right)^{\sigma-1}\left|\frac{F_{n}}{W_{i,n-k}^{*}}-\frac{\phi^{k}}{w_{+}^{*}(i)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim~

\displaystyle\phi^{(\sigma-1)k}\left|\frac{\phi^{n}}{w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{n-k}}\frac{1-(-1)^{n}\phi^{-2n}}{1-\frac{w_{-}^{*}(i)}{w_{+}^{*}(i)}(-1)^{n-k}\phi^{-2(n-k)}}-\frac{\phi^{k}}{w_{+}^{*}(i)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim~

\displaystyle\phi^{(\sigma-1)k}\cdot\phi^{k}\left|\frac{\frac{w_{-}^{*}(i)}{w_{+}^{*}(i)}(-1)^{n-k}\phi^{-2(n-k)}-(-1)^{n}\phi^{-2n}}{1-\frac{w_{-}^{*}(i)}{w_{+}^{*}(i)}(-1)^{n-k}\phi^{-2(n-k)}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim~

\displaystyle w_{+}(i)\phi^{(2+\sigma)k-2n}\left|\frac{1-\frac{w_{-}(i)}{w_{+}(i)}(-1)^{k}\phi^{-2k}}{-w_{-}(i)+\frac{w_{+}(i)}{\phi^{n-k}}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{\phi^{n-k}}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1+\phi^{-2}\cdot\phi^{-2}}{\phi^{-2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\phi^{3}}}\frac{i}{\phi^{2n-(\sigma+2)k}}\lesssim\frac{i}{\phi^{2n-(\sigma+2)k}},

we thus get once more by (
26
) the desired

|\text{I}|\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{k}i}\right)^{\sigma}\frac{i}{\phi^{2n-(\sigma+2)k}}=\frac{1}{\phi^{2(n-k)}i^{\sigma-1}}\lesssim{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{\phi^{n-k}i^{\sigma}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{\alpha(n-k)}i^{\sigma-\alpha}}.

We will abbreviate the argument a bit for the other terms. For the second term we have, using Lemma
A.3
(iii) and (
26
),

\displaystyle|\text{II}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\frac{1}{F_{n}^{\sigma}\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})^{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\right|\phi^{\sigma k}=\frac{\phi^{\sigma k}}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\left|\left(\frac{\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n}}{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})}\right)^{\sigma}-1\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\phi^{\sigma k}}{\phi^{\sigma k}i^{\sigma}}\left(\frac{W_{i,k}}{F_{n}}\right)^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\frac{i^{2}}{\phi^{2(n-k)}}=\frac{1}{\phi^{\alpha(n-k)}\phi^{(2-\alpha)(n-k)}i^{\sigma-2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{\alpha(n-k)}i^{\sigma-\alpha}}.

For the third term we have, by
\alpha
-Hölder continuity of
f
,

\displaystyle|\text{III}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{k}i}\right)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}\left|f\left(\frac{W_{i,k}}{F_{n}}\right)-f(0)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\left(\frac{W_{i,k}}{F_{n}}\right)^{\alpha}\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\left(\frac{i}{\phi^{n-k}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\phi^{n-k}i^{\sigma-\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{\alpha(n-k)}i^{\sigma-\alpha}}.

Lastly, for the fourth term we similarly have

\displaystyle|\text{IV}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{k}i}\right)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}\left|f\left(\frac{W_{i,n-k}^{*}}{F_{n}}\right)-f\left(\frac{w_{+}^{*}(i)}{\phi^{k}}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\left|\frac{W_{i,n-k}^{*}}{F_{n}}-\frac{w_{+}^{*}(i)}{\phi^{k}}\right|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\left|\frac{-w_{-}^{*}(i)}{\phi^{2n-k}}\frac{1-\frac{w_{+}^{*}(i)}{w_{-}^{*}(i)}(-1)^{k}\phi^{-2k}}{1-(-1)^{n}\phi^{-2n}}\right|^{\alpha}\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\left(\frac{i}{\phi^{2n-k}}\right)^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\phi^{\alpha(2n-k)}i^{\sigma-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{\alpha(n-k)}i^{\sigma-\alpha}}.

It now remains to combine
|\text{I}|+|\text{II}|+|\text{III}|+|\text{IV}|
to get the desired bound.
∎

We will further approximate

\displaystyle\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}\approx\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}\approx\frac{f(w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(n-k)})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(n-k)})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}(W_{i,n-k}^{*})^{\sigma}}.

The next result shows that the errors in these approximations decay exponentially.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\sigma>1
and let
f:[0,1)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be
1
-periodic and
\alpha
-Hölder continuous for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
. There is a constant
B=B(\sigma,f,\alpha)>0
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\left|\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}\right|\leq\frac{B}{\phi^{\alpha k}i^{\sigma}}.

Also, there is a constant
B^{*}=B^{*}(\sigma,f,\alpha)>0
such that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\left|\frac{f(w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(\mu_{i}+j)})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(\mu_{i}+j)})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}(W_{i,\mu_{i}+j}^{*})^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}\right|\leq\frac{B^{*}}{\phi^{\alpha(\mu_{i}+j)}i^{\sigma-\alpha}}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of the previous lemma and we will again only discuss the first expression in detail. Bound

\left|\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}\right|\leq|\text{I}|+|\text{II}|+|\text{III}|,

where now

\displaystyle|\text{I}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{\sigma}w_{+}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}\right|,

\displaystyle|\text{II}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{f(0)5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{\sigma}w_{+}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{\sigma}w_{+}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}}\frac{f(0)}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}\right|,

\displaystyle|\text{III}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{f(0)5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{\sigma}w_{+}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}}\frac{f(0)}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{\sigma}w_{+}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}}\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}w_{+}^{*}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{-\sigma k}}\right|,

recalling that
w_{+}(i)w_{+}^{*}(i)=\eta_{i}
. These terms can be treated individually. For the first one we have, using Lemma
A.3
(iv),

\displaystyle|\text{I}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{-\sigma k}}\left|\frac{1}{W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}-\frac{5^{\sigma/2}}{w_{+}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}}\right|\lesssim\phi^{\sigma k}\left|\left(\frac{1}{w_{+}(i)\phi^{k}-w_{-}(i)\left(-\frac{1}{\phi}\right)^{k}}\right)^{\sigma}-\frac{1}{w_{+}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\left|\left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{w_{-}(i)}{w_{+}(i)}(-1)^{k}\phi^{-2k}}\right)^{\sigma}-1\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\left|\frac{w_{-}(i)}{w_{+}(i)}\frac{1}{\phi^{2k}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{2k}i^{\sigma+1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{\alpha k}i^{\sigma}}.

For the second term, by
\alpha
-Hölder continuity of
f
it holds

\displaystyle|\text{II}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{w_{+}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}}\frac{1}{\phi^{-\sigma k}}\left|f\left(\frac{w_{+}^{*}(i)}{\phi^{k}}\right)-f(0)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\left(\frac{w_{+}^{*}(i)}{\phi^{k}}\right)^{\alpha}\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{\alpha k}i^{\sigma}}.

Finally, for the third term we have by Lemma
A.3
(iii)

\displaystyle|\text{III}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{w_{+}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{\sigma k}}\left|\frac{1}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{\pi^{\sigma}w_{+}^{*}(i)^{\sigma}\phi^{-\sigma k}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\left|\left(\frac{\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k}}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}\right)^{\sigma}-1\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}\frac{1}{\phi^{2k}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\phi^{\alpha k}i^{\sigma}}.

The terms
|\text{I}|+|\text{II}|+|\text{III}|
together then yield the claim.
∎

The previous lemma shows that the sums

\delta_{f,\sigma}(i)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma}}

and

\delta_{f,\sigma}^{*}(i)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{f(w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(\mu_{i}+j)})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(\mu_{i}+j)})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}(W_{i,\mu_{i}+j}^{*})^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{i^{\sigma-\alpha}}

converge with geometric convergence rate. In particular, truncating the sum after the first
n
terms, compared to the infinite sum this leaves an error of order
O(\phi^{-\alpha n}i^{-\sigma})
and
O(\phi^{-\alpha n}i^{-(\sigma-\alpha)})
respectively.

3.2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We are now in a position to prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Any
\alpha
-Hölder continuous, symmetric,
1
-periodic function

is also
\beta
-Hölder continuous for all
0<\beta\leq\alpha
. This, in particular, holds then for
\beta\coloneq\min\{\alpha,\sigma-1\}
. Also set
\gamma\coloneqq\beta/2
so that in total
0<\gamma<\beta\leq\alpha
. We will apply the previous two lemmas with the exponent
\beta
(and at one instance with
\gamma
).

By symmetry of
f
we may write

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}\sum_{m=1}^{F_{n}-1}\frac{f(m/F_{n})}{\left|{\sin(\pi m/F_{n})}\right|^{\sigma}}\frac{f(F_{n-1}m/F_{n})}{\left|{\sin(\pi F_{n-1}m/F_{n})}\right|^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}\sum_{1\leq m<F_{n}/2}\frac{f(m/F_{n})}{\left|{\sin(\pi m/F_{n})}\right|^{\sigma}}\frac{f(F_{n-1}m/F_{n})}{\left|{\sin(\pi F_{n-1}m/F_{n})}\right|^{\sigma}}+O(\phi^{-\sigma n}),

where the error is
0
if
F_{n}
is odd and (by periodicity of

)

\frac{1}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}\left(\frac{f(1/2)}{\left|{\sin(\pi/2)}\right|^{\sigma}}\right)^{2}=O(\phi^{-\sigma n})

if
F_{n}
is even. Partition now the index set
\{m\in\mathbb{N}:m<F_{n}/2\}
according to the Wythoff array, also using (
24
),

\Sigma=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}:\\
n-\mu_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-\mu_{i}-1}\frac{1}{F_{n}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W_{i,k}/F_{n})}{\sin(\pi W_{i,k}/F_{n})^{\sigma}}\frac{f(W_{i,n-k}^{*}/F_{n})}{\sin(\pi W_{i,n-k}^{*}/F_{n})^{\sigma}}+O(\phi^{-\sigma n}).

Splitting up the inner sum into
1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}
and (the possibly vacuous)
\frac{n}{2}\leq k<n-\mu_{i}
and applying Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
we get

\displaystyle\Sigma=~

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}:\\
n-\mu_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\Bigg[\sum_{1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})^{\sigma}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{\beta n/2}i^{\sigma-\beta}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~+\sum_{\frac{n}{2}\leq k<n-\mu_{i}}\left(\frac{f(w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(n-k)})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(n-k)})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}(W_{i,n-k}^{*})^{\sigma}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{\beta n/2}i^{\sigma-\beta}}\right)\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle+O(\phi^{-\sigma n})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}\\
n-\mu_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\Bigg[(n-\mu_{i}-1)\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~+\sum_{1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}W_{i,k}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}^{*}(i)\phi^{-k})}^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~+\sum_{1\leq j<\frac{n}{2}-\mu_{i}}\left(\frac{f(w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(\mu_{i}+j)})}{{\sin(\pi w_{+}(i)\phi^{-(\mu_{i}+j)})}^{\sigma}}\frac{f(0)}{\pi^{\sigma}(W_{i,\mu_{i}+j}^{*})^{\sigma}}-\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~+O\left(\frac{n}{\phi^{\beta n/2}i^{\sigma-\beta}}\right)\Bigg]+O(\phi^{-\sigma n})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}\\
n-\mu_{i}>1\end{subarray}}\Bigg[(n-\mu_{i}-1)\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}+\left(\delta_{f,\sigma}(i)+O\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{\beta n/2}i^{\sigma}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~+\left(\delta_{f,\sigma}^{*}(i)+O\left(\frac{1}{\phi^{\beta n/2}i^{\sigma-\beta}}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{n}{\phi^{\beta n/2}i^{\sigma-\beta}}\right)\Bigg]+O(\phi^{-\sigma n})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{f(0)^{2}5^{\sigma/2}}{\pi^{2\sigma}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}\\

n-\mu_{i}>1\end{subarray}}O\left(\frac{n}{\phi^{\beta n/2}i^{\sigma-\beta}}\right)+O(\phi^{-\sigma n}).

It remains to look at the errors we introduce when replacing the finite sums by infinite ones. Using Proposition
2.6
we have the equivalence

and by (

Since

, going from the finite sum

to the infinite sum

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{n^{2}}{\phi^{\beta n/2}}\right).

\displaystyle\Sigma=~

so that the constants

(28)

finishing the proof.
∎

3.3
The number field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
and its Dedekind zeta function

Number theory, in a simplified way, is concerned with the arithmetic properties of domains of numbers, in particular the structure of its prime elements. While classically this was restricted to the integers
\mathbb{Z}
, it was realized that similar problems may be studied over, for example, the
Gaussian integers

\mathbb{Z}[i]
, the
Eisenstein integers

\mathbb{Z}[\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}/3)]
or more generally the
cyclotomic integers

\mathbb{Z}[\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}/n)]
. The development of a general theory for the
ring of integers

\mathcal{O}_{K}
of an
algebraic number field

K\supseteq\mathbb{Q}
then lead to
algebraic number theory
and
class field theory
. For details on these topics see
[
3
,
15
,
16
,
19
,
39
,
47
,
48
,
56
]
. Here we only need to look at the concrete example of the properties of
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\phi)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
.

An important tool in the study of the primes in
\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
is the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(\sigma)
as already appeared in (
3
). The
Dedekind zeta function

\zeta_{K}(\sigma)
is an analogous object for general number fields
K\supseteq\mathbb{Q}
, where
\zeta=\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}
. We will be interested in
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})}
, so we start with collecting some properties of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
. For general information on Dedekind zeta functions see
[
39
,
47
,
57
]
.

The field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})\cong\mathbb{Q}(x)/(x^{2}-5)
is a degree-two extension of the rationals
\mathbb{Q}
and thus an algebraic number field. Its ring of integers is given by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})}=\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
and we will give a brief exposition of its properties. Elements in this ring are given by
a+b\phi
for
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and multiplied via

(a+b\phi)(c+d\phi)=(ac+bd)+(ad+bc+bd)\phi.

This ring is a
Euclidean domain
(with Euclidean function
|\nu(x)|
as below), in particular a
principal ideal domain
and thus also a
unique factorization domain
. The map

\nu:\mathbb{Z}[\phi]\mathbb{Z},\nu(a+b\phi)\coloneqq a^{2}+ab-b^{2}

is called the
norm
. It is multiplicative
\nu(xy)=\nu(x)\nu(y)
and if
(x)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
denotes the ideal generated by
x\neq 0
then for the quotient ring it holds

\displaystyle\#(\mathbb{Z}[\phi]/(x))=|\nu(x)|.

(29)

Via the factorization
\nu(a+b\phi)=(a+b\phi)(a+b-b\phi)
, the element
(a+b)-b\phi
may be regarded as the conjugate of
a+b\phi
in
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
. The
units
of
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
are precisely those elements
u\in\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
for which
\nu(u)\in\{-1,1\}
and can be given explicitly by

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}(1)=\{\pm\phi^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ even}\},\quad\nu^{-1}(-1)=\{\pm\phi^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ odd}\},

(30)

noting that
\phi^{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n}\phi
. This makes
\phi
a
fundamental unit
of
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
. The prime elements of
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
can be characterized (up to multiplication by units) as follows:

(i)

\sqrt{5}=-1+2\phi\in\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
is prime,

(ii)

if
p\in\mathbb{N}
is prime and
p\equiv 2,3\mod 5
then
p\in\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
is prime,

(iii)

if
p\in\mathbb{N}
is prime and
p\equiv 1,4\mod 5
then there are
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
with
p=a^{2}+ab-b^{2}
and
a+b\phi,(a+b)-b\phi\in\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
are distinct primes.

That
\sqrt{5}\in\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
needs to be treated separately is reflected by the fact that
(5)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is the only prime ideal which ramifies as
(5)=(\sqrt{5})^{2}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
.

Points (ii) and (iii) can be seen as an analogue of Fermat’s theorem on the sum of two squares for the binary quadratic form
x^{2}+xy-y^{2}

[
13
,
16
,
19
]
. This shows that the structure of
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
is intimately connected to the properties of this form. Indeed, the fact that
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
is a unique factorization domain (in terms of class field theory the field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
has
class number

h(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}))=1
) is equivalent to the fact that every binary quadratic form
q(x,y)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}
with
a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}
of
discriminant

b^{2}-4ac=5
is equivalent to
x^{2}+xy-y^{2}

[
16
]
, meaning that

q(\alpha x+\beta y,\gamma x+\beta y)=x^{2}+xy-y^{2}

for some
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma=1
.

The
Dedekind zeta function
of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
is defined as

\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})}(\sigma)\coloneqq\sum_{0\neq I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\phi]}\frac{1}{\#(\mathbb{Z}[\phi]/I)^{\sigma}}

for
\sigma>1
, where the sum goes over all non-zero ideals of
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
. The convergence of this sum for
\sigma>1
is non-trivial but seen from Proposition
3.3
below using
\eta_{i}\asymp i
. One often studies such functions by first extending them to all of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}
via analytic continuation, but we will not need this here. Using the above characterization of the prime elements of
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
,
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})}
admits the factorization into the
Euler product

\displaystyle\begin{split}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})}(\sigma)&=\prod_{P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\phi]}\frac{1}{1-\#(\mathbb{Z}[\phi]/P)^{-\sigma}}\\
&=\frac{1}{1-5^{-\sigma}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 1,4\mod 5\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{1-p^{-\sigma}}\right)^{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 2,3\mod 5\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-p^{-2\sigma}}\\
&=L(\sigma,\chi)\zeta(\sigma),\end{split}

(31)

where the first product goes through all prime ideals
P
of
\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
, the products in the second line go through all primes in
\mathbb{N}
and in the last line

is the
Dirichlet L-function
for the

\chi(n)\coloneqq\begin{cases}0,&n\equiv 0\mod 5,\\
1,&n\equiv 1,4\mod 5,\\
-1,&n\equiv 2,3\mod 5,\end{cases}

that is the
Legendre symbol

(\frac{\cdot}{5})
modulo
5
. With
\eta_{i}
as in Theorem

Proposition 3.3
.

For all
\sigma>1
it holds

\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})}(\sigma)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\eta_{i}^{\sigma}}.

Proof.

We will show that there is a bijection between the rows of the Wythoff array
(W_{i,k})_{k}
and the non-zero ideals

\mathbb{Z}[\phi]
such that
\#(\mathbb{Z}[\phi]/I)=\eta_{i}

For

To see that it is surjective, note that if

4
Closed-form expressions

It is natural to ask if one can determine even more lower-order terms beyond the constant one. With a more detailed analysis and possibly stronger assumptions on the functions
f
this seems doable, but tedious without a more systematic approach. In this last part we want to discuss a different and to the authors surprising aspect, that one can even determine simple closed-form expressions for certain sums of this type. With the
Lucas numbers

(L_{n})_{n}
given by
L_{0}=2,L_{1}=1
and the recurrence
L_{n}=L_{n-1}+L_{n-2}
for
n\geq 2

Theorem 4.1
.

For all
n\geq 2
it holds

\sum_{m=1}^{F_{n}-1}\frac{1}{\sin(\pi m/F_{n})^{2}}\frac{1}{\sin(\pi F_{n-1}m/F_{n})^{2}}=\frac{4n}{75}F_{2n}-\frac{17}{1125}L_{2n}-(-1)^{n}\frac{116}{1125}-\frac{1}{9}.

Although this sum in particular already showed up for example in

A.1
Floor function

Here we collect some results concerning the floor function that are used throughout Section
2
. Similar relations have been proven and used for example in
[
18
,
34
,
36
,
43
]
. Recall that for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
\lfloor x\rfloor=n
if and only if
x-n\in[0,1)
.

Lemma A.1
.

For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
the following equalities and inequalities hold:

(i)

\phi\lfloor\phi N\rfloor<\lfloor\phi^{2}N\rfloor
,

(ii)

\lfloor\phi^{2}N\rfloor=\lfloor\phi\lfloor\phi N\rfloor+\phi^{-1}\rfloor
,

(iii)

N+\lfloor\phi N+\phi^{-1}\rfloor=\lfloor\phi\lfloor\phi N+\phi^{-1}\rfloor+\phi^{-1}\rfloor
,

(iv)

\frac{1}{2}\left\lfloor\phi(2N-1)+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor<\left\lfloor\phi N+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor<\frac{1}{2}\left\lfloor\phi(2N+1)+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

We will use the relation
\phi^{2}=\phi+1
and variations thereof as well as the irrationality of
\phi
multiple times throughout the proof.

(i):
Starting with
\phi^{-1}\lfloor\phi N\rfloor<N
we have

\phi\lfloor\phi N\rfloor=\phi^{-1}\lfloor\phi N\rfloor+\lfloor\phi N\rfloor<N+\lfloor\phi N\rfloor=\lfloor\phi^{2}N\rfloor.

(ii):
From
-1<\lfloor\phi N\rfloor-\phi N<0
we get
\phi^{-1}\lfloor\phi N\rfloor+\phi^{-1}-N\in(0,\phi^{-1})
. This gives
\lfloor\phi^{-1}\lfloor\phi N\rfloor+\phi^{-1}\rfloor=N
and thus

\left\lfloor\phi^{2}N\right\rfloor=\lfloor\phi N\rfloor+N=\lfloor\phi N\rfloor+\left\lfloor\phi^{-1}\lfloor\phi N\rfloor+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\phi\lfloor\phi N\rfloor+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor.

(iii):
Since
\phi N+\phi^{-1}=\frac{N-1}{2}+\frac{N+1}{2}\sqrt{5}\notin\mathbb{N}
we have

\phi N-\phi^{-2}<\left\lfloor\phi N+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor<\phi N+\phi^{-1},

so that

\displaystyle\phi\left\lfloor\phi N+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor+\phi^{-1}-\left(\left\lfloor\phi N+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor+N\right)=\phi^{-1}\left(\left\lfloor\phi N+\phi^{-1}\right\rfloor+1\right)-N

lies in the interval
(\phi^{-2},1)
. This gives the claim.

(iv):
For the left inequality note the following sequence of implications:

\displaystyle-\phi+\phi^{-1}<2\phi^{-1}-2

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\phi(2N-1)+\phi^{-1}<2\phi N+2\phi^{-1}-2

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\lfloor\phi(2N-1)+\phi^{-1}\rfloor<2\phi N+2\phi^{-1}-2

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lfloor\phi(2N-1)+\phi^{-1}\rfloor+1<\phi N+\phi^{-1}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lfloor\phi(2N-1)+\phi^{-1}\rfloor<\lfloor\phi N+\phi^{-1}\rfloor.

For the right inequality we similarly have

\displaystyle 2\phi^{-1}+1=\phi+\phi^{-1}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle 2\phi N+2\phi^{-1}+1=\phi(2N+1)+\phi^{-1}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle 2\phi N+2\phi^{-1}<\lfloor\phi(2N+1)+\phi^{-1}\rfloor

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\phi N+\phi^{-1}<\frac{1}{2}\lfloor\phi(2N+1)+\phi^{-1}\rfloor

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\lfloor\phi N+\phi^{-1}\rfloor<\frac{1}{2}\lfloor\phi(2N+1)+\phi^{-1}\rfloor.

∎

The following results are related to how well the golden ratio
\phi
can be approximated by rational numbers.

Lemma A.2
.

For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

1+\frac{1}{(\phi+2)i^{2}}<\frac{\phi i}{\lfloor\phi i\rfloor}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\phi i}{\lfloor\phi i\rfloor+1}\leq 1-\frac{1}{2\phi^{2}i^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
. The first inequality is easily seen to be implied by the stronger statement

\displaystyle\{\phi i\}>\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}i}.

(36)

To show this inequality note that, using
\lfloor\phi i\rfloor=\phi i-\{\phi i\}
,

\sqrt{5}i\{\phi i\}-(i^{2}+i\lfloor\phi i\rfloor-\lfloor\phi i\rfloor^{2})=\{\phi i\}^{2}>0,

so
\sqrt{5}i\{\phi i\}>i^{2}+i\lfloor\phi i\rfloor-\lfloor\phi i\rfloor^{2}
. The expression on the right hand side is an integer which by

i^{2}+i\lfloor\phi i\rfloor-\lfloor\phi i\rfloor^{2}=(i-\phi^{-1}\lfloor\phi i\rfloor)(i+\phi\lfloor\phi i\rfloor)>0

(since
\phi
is irrational) is positive, that is at least
1
. This gives (
36
) and thus the first inequality of the statement.

For the second inequality, which is implied by (using
1+\lfloor\phi i\rfloor\leq 2i
)

\displaystyle 1-\{\phi i\}\geq\frac{1}{\phi^{2}i},

(37)

we proceed in a similar manner to above. Noting that
\lceil\phi i\rceil=\lfloor\phi i\rfloor+1=\phi i+1-\{\phi i\}
by irrationality of
\phi
, this follows from

\phi^{2}i(1-\{\phi i\})-(\lceil\phi i\rceil^{2}-i\lceil\phi i\rceil-i^{2})=(1-\{\phi i\})(\phi^{-2}i-(1-\{\phi i\}))\geq 0,

so that analogously to above we get

\phi^{2}i(1-\{\phi i\})\geq\lceil\phi i\rceil^{2}-i\lceil\phi i\rceil-i^{2}\geq 1,

giving (
37
). This establishes both inequalities in the statement.
∎

Note that the auxiliary sequences

and
\lceil\phi i\rceil^{2}-i\lceil\phi i\rceil-i^{2}
together already showed up in
[

]
.

A.2
Basic inequalities

Here we collect some inequalities from elementary calculus that are used throughout Section
3
.

Lemma A.3
.

The following inequalities hold:

(i)

For
0<x\leq\frac{2}{3}
it holds

\frac{1}{\sin(\pi x)}\leq\frac{1}{x}.

(ii)

For
\sigma>1
and
0<x,y\leq\frac{2}{3}
it holds

\left|\frac{1}{\sin(\pi x)^{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{\sin(\pi y)^{\sigma}}\right|\leq\sigma\pi^{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}\right)^{\sigma-1}\left|\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{y}\right|.

(iii)

For
\sigma>1
and
0<x\leq\frac{2}{3}
it holds

\left(\frac{\pi x}{\sin(\pi x)}\right)^{\sigma}-1\leq 4^{\sigma+1}x^{2}.

(iv)

For
\sigma>1
and
|x|\leq\frac{2}{3}
it holds

\left|\left(\frac{1}{1-x}\right)^{\sigma}-1\right|\leq 4^{\sigma}|x|.

Proof.

(i):
The function
\sin(\pi x)
is concave on the interval
x\in[0,\frac{2}{3}]
(by non-positivity of its second derivative there), so that

\sin(\pi x)\geq\frac{3}{2}\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)x=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}x\geq x

for all
0\leq x\leq\frac{2}{3}
. The statement now follows by taking the inverses.

(ii):
If
x=y
, both sides are 0 and the inequality is obviously true. Therefore, without loss of generality let
x<y
and consider the ratio

R(x,y)\coloneqq\frac{\left|\frac{1}{\sin(\pi x)^{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{\sin(\pi y)^{\sigma}}\right|}{\left(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}\right)^{\sigma-1}\left|\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{y}\right|}=\frac{x^{\sigma}y^{\sigma}|\sin(\pi y)^{\sigma}-\sin(\pi x)^{\sigma}|}{\sin(\pi x)^{\sigma}\sin(\pi y)^{\sigma}(x+y)^{\sigma-1}|y-x|}.

By (i),
x^{\sigma}\sin(\pi x)^{-\sigma}\leq 1
, respectively for
y
. Furthermore, the mean value theorem guarantees the existence of a
\xi\in[x,y]
with

\sin(\pi y)^{\sigma}-\sin(\pi x)^{\sigma}=\sigma\pi\sin(\pi\xi)^{\sigma-1}\cos(\pi\xi)(y-x),

so that

\left|\frac{\sin(\pi y)^{\sigma}-\sin(\pi x)^{\sigma}}{y-x}\right|=|\sigma\pi\sin(\pi\xi)^{\sigma-1}\cos(\pi\xi)|\leq\sigma\pi(\pi\xi)^{\sigma-1}\leq\sigma\pi^{\sigma}(x+y)^{\sigma-1}

since
\sin(\pi x)\leq\pi x
for
x\geq 0
(by concavity, considering the tangent at
x=0
),
\sigma-1>0
and
\xi\leq y<x+y
.
All in all, it follows that
R(x,y)\leq\sigma\pi^{\sigma}
, hence the assertion.

(iii):
Consider the function
(0,\frac{2}{3}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R},x\mapsto x^{-2}((1-\frac{\pi^{2}x^{2}}{6})^{-\sigma}-1)
. We have the expansion

\frac{1}{x^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}x^{2}\right)^{\sigma}}-1\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{n+\sigma\choose n+1}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)^{n+1}x^{2n}

for
0<x\leq\frac{2}{3}
and by positivity of the coefficients it becomes apparent that this function is increasing on
(0,\frac{2}{3}]
. One then has

\frac{1}{x^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}x^{2}\right)^{\sigma}}-1\right)\leq\frac{1}{(2/3)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}(2/3)^{2}\right)^{\sigma}}-1\right)\leq 4^{\sigma+1}

for
0<x\leq\frac{2}{3}
.

Now it is easily seen that
\sin(\pi x)\geq\pi x-\frac{\pi^{3}x^{3}}{6}
for all
x\geq 0
(equality for
x=0
and consider the derivatives up to order 3). Consequently, for all
x\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
we obtain the desired

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\pi x}{\sin(\pi x)}\right)^{\sigma}-1

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\pi x}{\pi x-\frac{\pi^{3}x^{3}}{6}}\right)^{\sigma}-1=\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}x^{2}}{6}\right)^{-\sigma}-1\leq 4^{\sigma+1}x^{2}.

(iv):
The function
(-\infty,1)\mathbb{R},x\mapsto(1-x)^{-\sigma}-1
is convex in this range (by a simple check of its second derivative), in particular for all
0\leq x\leq\frac{2}{3}
it holds

\left(\frac{1}{1-x}\right)^{\sigma}-1\leq\frac{3}{2}(3^{\sigma}-1)x\leq 4^{\sigma}x.

On the other hand,
1-(1-x)^{-\sigma}
is concave for
x\in(-\infty,1)
(as the negative of the convex function above). By considering the tangent at
x=0
we thus get for
x\leq 0

1-\left(\frac{1}{1-x}\right)^{\sigma}\leq-\sigma x\leq-4^{\sigma}x.

This fully establishes the desired inequality.
∎
