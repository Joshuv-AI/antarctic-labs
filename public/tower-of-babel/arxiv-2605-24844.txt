Geo-Expert: Towards Expert-Level Geological Reasoning via Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
Chenyou Guo, Zongqi Liu, Yizhou Zhang, Zhaorui Jiang, Ze Liu

Abstract
While general-purpose Large Language Models (LLMs) applied to Geology often hallucinate when reasoning about subsurface structures and deep-time evolution, current AI in Earth sciences predominantly targets surface remote sensing and GIS. To bridge this gap, we introduce Geo-Expert, a family of parameter-efficient geological LLMs fine-tuned on a custom-curated, high-quality instruction dataset processed using our custom instruction synthesis pipeline. We investigate the impact of model scaling and architecture by fine-tuning three base models: Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B, and Gemma-3-27B, with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) method. Our extensive evaluation on a novel domain-specific benchmark, Geo-Eval, reveals that a domain-aligned 8B model can outperform open-weight 70B generalists and proprietary GPT-4o on specialized geological reasoning, while a 32B variant approaches frontier reasoning models. The optimized 8B model further offers a competitive cost-performance ratio for deployment. This work provides a reproducible recipe for democratizing scientific LLMs and establishes a baseline for geological artificial intelligence.



1
Introduction

Geology plays a vital role in underground resource exploration, geologic hazard mitigation, and understanding planetary evolution. Unlike general knowledge domains, geological reasoning requires interpreting complex spatio-temporal relationships and a large amount of professional data. However, LLMs often struggle with the specialized logic required in geology. General-purpose models frequently exhibit severe hallucinations when asked to perform a rigorous deduction on stratigraphy or structural geology, limiting their utility in geological research and industrial applications.

Current large models in the vertical field of geography predominantly focus on Geography Knowledge Agent and Earth Observation, demonstrating proficiency in general geospatial intelligence and utilizing computer vision to analyze imagery for surface monitoring. They effectively handle tasks related to surface geography, environmental monitoring, and human-geography interactions. However, a critical gap remains between general
Geography
and specialized
Geology
. While existing models can retrieve facts about Earth’s surface, they often lack the deep-domain reasoning required to understand the solid Earth such as interpreting subsurface stratigraphic sequences, reconstructing deep-time tectonic evolution, or analyzing complex petrogenetic processes.

To bridge this gap, we introduce Geo-Expert, a suite of parameter-efficient geological LLMs. We construct a high-quality, domain-specific instruction dataset derived from five canonical geology textbooks. Utilizing the custom framework, we synthesize structured instruction-response pairs to instill rigorous domain knowledge. We investigate the scaling laws of geological reasoning by fine-tuning three base models: Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B, and Gemma-3-27B, employing Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to improve training efficiency.

In sum, our main contributions are as follows:

•

Dataset Construction:
We present a reproducible pipeline for converting unstructured geological textbooks into high-quality instruction-tuning datasets.

•

Model Scaling Analysis:
We provide a comparative analysis of model architectures (Qwen vs. Gemma) and parameters scales (8B vs. 27B/32B), revealing that while smaller models are efficient and easy to deploy, larger parameters are crucial for complex structural reasoning.

•

Geo-Expert Benchmark:
We establish Geo-Eval, a benchmark for geological reasoning, demonstrating our fine-tuned models significantly outperform general-purpose baselines in specialized tasks.

2.2
Dataset Construction

Transforming static textbook content into a dynamic instruction-tuning dataset involves a pipeline designed to maximize data fidelity and reduce noise. We implemented a multi-stage process: digitization, heuristic cleaning, and instruction synthesis.

Text Extraction and Sanitization.

We first used MinerU
(
16
)
, a high-precision PDF-to-Markdown tool, to extract textual content while preserving the structure of the original textbooks. Following the extraction, we developed a custom Python-based cleaning module to process the raw Markdown. This pipeline first segments the text into discrete blocks based on paragraph lines containing metadata keywords like page numbers and copy notices. Furthermore, to prevent redundancy caused by layout overlaps, we implemented a global deduplication mechanism using a hash-based method to filter out identical text blocks across the entire corpus.

Instruction Synthesis.

To transform the cleaned Markdown segments into a high-quality, geoscientific instruction-tuning corpus, we engineered a custom, domain-specific instruction synthesis pipeline. This pipeline operates on a project-based workflow that deeply integrates earth science document processing, geological domain modeling, and reasoning-enhanced generation. The synthesis process consists of three key stages:

1.

Chapter-Aware Recursive Chunking:
Unlike simple fixed-length splitting, the framework utilizes a semantic segmentation algorithm based on the Markdown header structure. This ensures that each text chunk retains complete semantic context within its hierarchical scope, preventing the fragmentation of complex geological descriptions.

2.

Domain-Structured Question Generation:
An LLM first constructs a hierarchical domain tree (extracting outlines and concepts) to bind specific tags to text segments. Questions are then dynamically generated based on these domain tags and character density, minimizing redundancy and ensuring broad coverage of geological concepts.

3.

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Answer Construction:
To capture the rigorous logic required for geological reasoning, the system employs reasoning-oriented models to generate answers. This process produces not only the final ground truth but also the intermediate reasoning steps (CoT), ensuring strict alignment with the source material while explicitly detailing the deduction process.

This automated pipeline enabled us to efficiently scale the dataset to 11,518 high-quality instruction pairs while preserving the authoritative accuracy of the original canonical sources.

2.4
Benchmark Construction: Geo-Eval

Standard benchmarks in Earth sciences predominantly focus on remote sensing tasks or general geography trivia, failing to capture the rigorous deductive logic required for solid earth geology. To bridge this evaluation gap and comprehensively evaluate the true reasoning capabilities of LLMs in the vertical domain of geology, we constructed Geo-Eval, a novel domain-specific benchmark consisting of high-fidelity, open-ended reasoning questions. Unlike traditional static datasets manually extracted from exams, Geo-Eval was constructed through an automated adversarial mining pipeline coupled with expert human-in-the-loop verification. The construction process proceeded in four distinct phases:

1.

Large-Scale Generative Extraction:
We initially deployed DeepSeek-R1
(
3
)
to perform a comprehensive semantic analysis of the source geology textbooks. Through this process, we extracted a foundational pool of 2,591 complex geological questions and synthesized their corresponding preliminary reference answers.

2.

Comparative Inference:
To evaluate the baseline domain capability, both our fine-tuned Qwen3-8B-Geo and DeepSeek-R1 were tasked with generating independent responses to the entire pool of 2,591 questions.

3.

Difficulty-Aware Evaluation Paradigm (LLM-as-a-Judge):
To move beyond static, undifferentiated testing, we introduce a
difficulty-aware evaluation paradigm
designed to identify the true cognitive frontier of geological reasoning. We utilized GLM-4.5
(
23
)
as an impartial evaluator to blindly score both models’ responses on a 10-point scale. Rather than evaluating on a random distribution, we systematically isolated a subset of 387 “hard boundary” questions where the score differential between our Qwen3-8B-geo and the frontier generalist DeepSeek-R1 was marginal (score difference
\leq
4). This dynamic calibration of difficulty yields exceptionally valuable boundary samples. They represent complex cognitive tasks where general-purpose giants exhaust their zero-shot deductive capacities and lose their decisive advantage. Consequently, this paradigm shifts the focus from basic factual recall to a much stricter, boundary-aware stress test of true expert-level geological reasoning.

4.

Expert Vetting and Ground Truth Finalization:
To ensure absolute scientific validity, the isolated 387 candidate questions were submitted to geology professors for rigorous manual screening, refinement, and calibration. Then we get the test dataset with the reference answers.

The resulting finalized dataset serves as a highly targeted, expert-verified ground truth. By deliberately focusing on these high-complexity boundary problems, Geo-Eval provides a robust metric for assessing whether a model has truly internalized deep geological logic, moving far beyond superficial pattern matching.

2.5
Mitigating Selection Bias

We acknowledge that the boundary mining process uses Qwen3-8B-Geo as one comparator, which could in principle introduce selection bias. We mitigate this in three ways: (i) the 387 candidate questions were independently re-vetted, refined, and—where necessary—rewritten by geology professors, and the reference answers used for scoring are expert-authored ground truth, not model outputs; (ii) the final evaluation employs GPT-4o as a reference-guided judge against this expert-verified ground truth, decoupling scoring from the mining process; (iii) two of our three reported models (Qwen3-32B-Geo and Gemma-3-27B-Geo) were not used in the boundary-mining loop, yet exhibit consistent gains, suggesting the observed improvements generalize beyond the mining comparator.

3
Benchmark and Evaluation: Geo-Eval

3.1
Benchmark Content

To avoid one-dimensional evaluation, we rigorously partition the Geo-Eval test dataset into three cognitive levels:

•

Concept:
Focuses on the identification and definition of core terminology, such as mineralogy, petrology, and stratigraphy.

•

Process:
Focuses on the logical deduction of dynamic geological mechanisms, including tectonic movements and diagenesis.

•

Engineering:
Focuses on the comprehensive application analysis of geological conditions in practical scenarios, such as engineering site selection and geological hazard prevention.

3.2
Benchmark Baselines

After applying Geo-Eval to different models, we adopt the widely recognized LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm, utilizing GPT-4o as a reference-guided LLM evaluator comparing against expert-verified ground truth. We impose strict domain-fact-checking constraints within the prompt to generate a quantitative score ranging from 0 to 10.
We select 11 highly representative models for a comprehensive comparison, categorized into three groups: proprietary state-of-the-art models (e.g., GPT-5.4, DeepSeek-V3.2), open-weight generalist models of varying parameter scales (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen3-32B), and our domain-specific models fine-tuned via the proposed Geo-Expert framework.

3.3
Main Results and Insights

The experimental results fully demonstrate the significant advantages of the Geo-Expert framework in breaking through the knowledge barriers of generalist models. We analyze the results from three core dimensions:

1.

Remarkable Performance Breakthrough

As shown in Table 1, general open-weight base models struggle with high-depth geological questions. However, after fine-tuning with the Geo-Expert framework, all models achieve a substantial leap in performance. For instance, the average score of Qwen3-32B surges from 5.00 to 6.82, and Gemma-3-27B jumps from 5.16 to 6.59. Notably, in the “Engineering” dimension—which most rigorously tests comprehensive deductive reasoning—the score of Qwen3-8B rises from 4.41 to 6.44 post-fine-tuning. This strongly suggests that in highly specialized STEM branches, relying solely on general knowledge injected during the pre-training phase is severely limited. The high-quality data synthesis and alignment strategies provided by Geo-Expert effectively reconstruct the models’ domain cognitive networks. Furthermore, a paired t-test on the 387 samples reveals that the performance gains of the Geo-Expert models are statistically extremely significant (
p=3.7121\times 10^{-106}<0.001
), proving that the improvements stem from deep domain alignment rather than random variance.

2.

Extreme Parameter Efficiency

One of the most striking findings of this study is the extreme parameter efficiency exhibited by the fine-tuned models. The Qwen3-8B-geo model, with merely 8B parameters, comprehensively outperforms the internationally leading open-weight benchmark, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, across all three dimensions (overall score 6.27 vs. 4.12), and even surpasses the proprietary industry benchmark, GPT-4o (5.93). The suboptimal performance of Llama-3.1-70B profoundly reveals the limitations of the Scaling Law in extremely vertical domains: large parameter models lacking deep alignment with specific scientific corpora are highly prone to superficial term usage and commonsense hallucinations. Conversely, a “small” model with refined domain injection can replace bloated generalist giants in professional scenarios.

3.

Approaching Frontier Performance

Our best-performing model, Qwen3-32B-geo, achieves an outstanding score of 6.82, exhibiting remarkable balance across the Concept (6.78), Process (6.79), and Engineering (6.90) dimensions. This performance not only significantly leads GPT-4o but also trails only marginally behind the current top-tier proprietary model, GPT-5.4 (7.15), which relies on massive computational power and reinforcement learning-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT). Considering its substantial advantages in inference cost and parameter scale, Geo-Expert models demonstrate exceptional value for practical engineering deployment and academic application.

Evaluating LLMs in specialized scientific domains poses a significant challenge. Traditional benchmarks often rely on static, multiple-choice questions extracted from standardized exams

By isolating “hard boundary” samples where general-purpose models fail but domain-aligned models succeed, Geo-Eval provides a much more rigorous and discriminative metric for assessing true expert-level geological reasoning.

5
Conclusion and Future Work

In this work, we present Geo-Expert, a framework that addresses the critical reasoning bottlenecks of generalist LLMs in highly specialized scientific domains. At the heart of our contribution is a robust, scalable dataset construction and instruction synthesis pipeline. Rather than passively fine-tuning, this pipeline achieves deep domain alignment by systematically recalibrating the model’s semantic representations. By doing so, Geo-Expert successfully transforms text generation into well-rounded scientific reasoning.

Beyond achieving state-of-the-art domain performance, this research exposes a profound paradigm shift regarding the Scaling Law in vertical disciplines. Our empirical results demonstrate extreme parameter efficiency: a domain-aligned 8B model can comprehensively eclipse a 70B open-weight giant. This unequivocally proves that high-quality data curation and targeted logical alignment profoundly outweigh sheer parameter volume. Consequently, Geo-Expert provides a highly cost-effective and easily deployable solution. It enables localized, flexible deployment for real-world geological engineering and on-site exploration, freeing researchers from the reliance on prohibitive computational clusters.

Our work has several limitations. First, the training corpus is built from five canonical textbooks, biased toward structural geology, stratigraphy, and tectonics; mineralogy, geochemistry, and geophysics are under-represented. Second, the Geo-Eval benchmark contains 387 expert-vetted questions—sufficient for boundary-aware stress testing but smaller than general-purpose benchmarks. Finally, the current framework is text-only and does not yet handle the inherently multimodal nature of geological data (cross-sections, well logs, field photos).

While Geo-Expert establishes a formidable foundation for text-based geological AI, we acknowledge that Earth sciences are inherently multimodal. Future work will extend this parameter-efficient framework by integrating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to parse stratigraphic profiles and remote sensing imagery. Coupled with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
(
11
)
to dynamically track evolving literature, we aim to evolve Geo-Expert from a static reasoning engine into a dynamic, autonomous agent for geoscience discovery.

Appendix B
Prompt Templates

B.1
LLM-as-a-Judge Prompt for Geo-Eval

To conduct a rigorous and impartial evaluation during the hard sample mining phase of Geo-Eval, we utilized GLM-4.5 as the evaluator. We designed a zero-shot prompt template that constrains the judge to focus purely on geological factual accuracy and logical deduction, penalizing superficial or hallucinatory responses. The specific prompt used is as follows:

“You are an expert professor in solid earth geology. Your task is to evaluate the responses of two AI models to a complex geological question.

Question:
[Insert Question]

Model A Response:
[Insert Model A Answer]

Model B Response:
[Insert Model B Answer]

Evaluation Criteria:

1. Factual Accuracy (40%): Does the response align strictly with established geological principles (e.g., Andersonian faulting, sequence stratigraphy)?
2. Deductive Logic (40%): Is the chain of thought coherent?
3. Professional Terminology (20%): Are the geological terms used accurately without cross-domain confusion?

Task:
Score each model from 0 to 10. You must provide a brief justification for your score, explicitly pointing out any hallucinations or logical flaws. Output your final scores in the format: Score A: [X], Score B: [Y].”

B.2
Reference-Based Evaluator Prompt for Final Scoring

To conduct the final quantitative evaluation, we utilized GPT-4o as a reference-guided evaluator. Unlike zero-shot blind testing, this phase employs a strict Reference-Based Evaluation paradigm, where the model compares candidate responses against expert-verified ground truth. The prompt explicitly enforces terminology flexibility to accurately accommodate the nuances of geological nomenclature.

You are an authoritative Professor of Geology and an expert academic evaluator.
Your task is to grade a Model’s Answer to a complex geological question against
a provided professional Reference Answer.

[Grading Criteria]
1. Score from 0 to 10.
2. Focus on SEMANTIC CORRECTNESS and GEOLOGICAL ACCURACY, not verbatim matching.
3. Terminology Flexibility: Be lenient with accepted geological synonyms (e.g.,
"igneous" vs "magmatic", "thrust fault" vs "reverse fault" depending on context).
4. Completeness: A 10/10 answer must contain all core geological facts mentioned
in the reference.
5. Penalty: Deduct heavily for hallucinations, factually incorrect statements,
or mixing up crucial concepts (e.g., misidentifying rock types or eras).

[Output Format]
Output ONLY a JSON object with this exact structure:
{"score": 8, "reason": "brief professional explanation"}

Question: {question}
Reference Answer: {reference}
Model Answer: {model_ans}

Question:
