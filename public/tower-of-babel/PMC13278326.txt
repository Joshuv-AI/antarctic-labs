Maxillary Sinus Mucocele following Zygomaticomaxillary Complex Fracture Repair
Authors: Kazuma Iwasaki, Nariaki Takamura, Akimitsu Sato, Jun Suzuki, Koji Hara, Toshiro Imai, Yu Yusa, Kenichi Amo, Chieko Miura, Yoshimichi Imai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary:Maxillary sinus cysts typically occur as postoperative maxillary cysts arising years after sinonasal surgery. In contrast, trauma-related cysts are uncommon, and cases specifically following zygomaticomaxillary complex fracture repair are exceedingly rare. We report a maxillary sinus cyst that developed 28 years after open reduction and internal fixation of a zygomaticomaxillary complex fracture. At the initial injury, the orbital floor fracture remained untreated, and the posterior maxillary wall was displaced anteriorly. These structures likely contacted each other and formed a bony partition, creating a closed intramaxillary cavity adjacent to the orbit and lined with mucosa, in which a cyst gradually developed. The patient presented with worsening left cheek pain. Imaging revealed a cystic lesion in the left maxillary sinus abutting the orbital contents. Although plate infection was initially suspected, symptoms persisted after plate removal and partial curettage. On referral, no signs of local infection were present. Computed tomography (CT) demonstrated the bony partition and a posteriorly expanding cyst with bone erosion. Because the cyst occupied a closed compartment, establishing drainage was essential. A combined approach with otolaryngologists was performed. Through a subciliary incision with endoscopic endonasal assistance, the bony septum was removed, creating communication with the native maxillary sinus. The orbital floor was reconstructed using an iliac inner table graft. Postoperatively, pain resolved, and computed tomography confirmed patency of the drainage route. No recurrence was observed at 1 year. This case illustrates that long-standing anatomical deformities after facial trauma may predispose to delayed cyst formation and require long-term attention.

Maxillary sinus cysts usually arise as postoperative complications of sinus surgery,1–3 whereas trauma-related cysts are rare.3–6 We report a maxillary sinus cyst that developed 28 years after open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) for a zygomaticomaxillary complex fracture. In this patient, the markedly displaced orbital floor created an intramaxillary bony partition, forming a closed cavity adjacent to the orbit. This abnormal anatomy likely contributed to cyst formation. We achieved a favorable outcome by removing the intramaxillary partition via a subciliary approach with endoscopic endonasal assistance and establishing a drainage route into the residual maxillary sinus.

CASE REPORT

The patient was a 44-year-old man with a chief complaint of left cheek pain. His medical history included type II diabetes mellitus with good glycemic control, and his family history was unremarkable.

History of Present Illness

The patient sustained a left zygomaticomaxillary complex fracture involving the orbital floor in a motor vehicle accident 28 years earlier. At that time, ORIF using titanium plates was performed at another hospital, but the orbital floor fracture was left untreated. Postoperatively, persistent left cheek discomfort and pain were attributed to infraorbital nerve impairment and observed. Approximately one year before presentation, worsening cheek pain led him to revisit the previous hospital. Computed tomography revealed a cystic lesion in the left maxillary sinus abutting the orbital contents and infraorbital rim plate, raising suspicion of plate infection. The plate was removed under general anesthesia, and partial curettage of the cyst was performed. The cyst contained turbid mucous fluid, and bacterial cultures were negative. Although the pain temporarily improved, symptoms recurred within one week, and he was referred to our department.

FINDINGS AT INITIAL PRESENTATION

No signs of local infection were observed. A scar extending from the left lower eyelid to the temporal region was present. No ocular motility disorder, diplopia, or sensory deficit in the V2 distribution was noted. Laboratory findings were within normal limits (white blood cell count 6600/µL; C-reactive protein 0.24 mg/dL).

Imaging Findings

CT at our institution showed a cyst in the left maxillary sinus (Fig. 1) The inferiorly displaced orbital floor and anteriorly displaced posterior maxillary wall were in contact, forming an intramaxillary bony septum and a closed cavity adjacent to the orbit, which likely led to cyst formation. The cyst had expanded posteriorly with bone resorption (Fig. 2).

Coronal CT image at the initial presentation. The inferiorly displaced orbital floor forms a bony partition (green arrow), creating a closed cavity in which entrapped maxillary sinus mucosa developed into a cyst that abuts the orbital fat, with associated bone destruction of the zygomatic body (red arrow).

Axial CT image at the initial presentation. The cyst extends posteriorly with accompanying bone destruction (red arrow).

Treatment Plan

A combined procedure with the otolaryngology team was planned to remove the intramaxillary septum, establish drainage into the residual maxillary sinus, and reconstruct the orbital floor with an iliac inner table graft.

Surgical Findings

Through a left subciliary incision, the cyst wall was dissected from the orbital fat and resected as much as safely possible. Because of posterior extension with bone destruction, complete excision was avoided to reduce the risk of vascular injury. Under endoscopic endonasal visualization, the intramaxillary septum was removed from the subciliary side with a chisel, creating a wide drainage route into the remaining maxillary sinus. (See figure, Supplemental Digital Content 1, which displays the intraoperative view through the subciliary approach. The light from the endoscopic endonasal system is visible beyond the intramaxillary bony septum, serving as a guide for fracturing the septum with a chisel, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E980.) The specimen consisted of a portion of the cyst wall. Histopathologic examination showed pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with scattered goblet cells, consistent with respiratory mucosa, without atypia or malignancy (Fig. 3). The orbital floor defect measured approximately 30 × 25 mm and was reconstructed with a 30 × 25 mm iliac inner table graft.

Histopathologic findings of the cyst wall on hematoxylin and eosin staining at 10× and 40× magnification. The lesion is lined by pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with scattered goblet cells (red arrow), consistent with respiratory mucosa, and cilia are visible along the luminal surface (green arrow) without epithelial atypia.

Postoperative Course

Postoperative CT confirmed patency of the drainage route into the residual maxillary sinus (Fig. 4). Cheek pain improved, and no ocular motility restriction or recurrence was noted at 2 years.

Coronal CT image obtained the day after surgery. The previously displaced orbital floor forming the bony septum has been removed, and the reconstructed orbital floor using an iliac inner table graft (red arrow) maintains a patent opening into the residual maxillary sinus.

DISCUSSION

Maxillary sinus cysts are most commonly recognized as postoperative maxillary cysts (PMCs) that develop as late complications after sinonasal surgery.1,2 These lesions include mucoceles, retention cysts, pseudocysts, and PMCs, also known as surgical ciliated cysts. PMC is a delayed complication after maxillary sinus surgery and was originally described by Kubo in 1927.2,3 Histologically, PMC is lined by pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with goblet cells, whereas pseudocysts lack an epithelial lining, radicular cysts are lined by stratified squamous epithelium and associated with infected teeth, and maxillary mucoceles are relatively uncommon.7 In the present case, the histopathologic findings supported a respiratory mucosal origin consistent with PMC/surgical ciliated cyst.

Primary or trauma-related cysts are uncommon, although several cases have been reported.4–6 Delayed maxillary sinus cysts have also been described after orthognathic surgery, indicating that altered sinus anatomy and mucosal entrapment can lead to late-onset cyst formation.8 Regardless of etiology, the fundamental mechanism is formation of a pathologic closed cavity lined by residual mucosa.4,5,8 In the present case, the untreated orbital floor fracture and anterior displacement of the posterior maxillary wall likely formed a bony partition, generating an isolated intramaxillary cavity in which a cyst gradually developed. Another possible mechanism is cystic degeneration of entrapped orbital fat and maxillary sinus mucosa, initially in continuity with the maxillary sinus and later sequestered as surrounding bone remodeled. PMCs have been reported more frequently in Japan than in other regions.9

Although trauma-related maxillary sinus or orbital cysts have been reported,4,6 only 1 prior case involved cyst formation after zygomaticomaxillary complex fracture repair.5 Delayed cysts may cause serious complications, such as skull base invasion or visual disturbance, requiring long-term vigilance.4–6 Establishing adequate drainage is essential for definitive treatment.10 In our case, the cyst was confined within a closed bony compartment, requiring precise septum removal in a limited field. A subciliary approach allowed direct access to the orbital floor deformity and simultaneous orbital floor reconstruction, whereas endoscopic endonasal assistance improved visualization and safety. Caldwell-Luc access was considered, but the combined subciliary and endoscopic approach was more appropriate given the lesion’s proximity to the orbital contents and the need for orbital floor reconstruction. Collaboration with otolaryngologists enabled septum removal and secure drainage into the natural maxillary sinus pathway.1,10 Postoperative CT and endoscopic examinations confirmed patency without recurrence. Reported recurrence rates of 6%–20% support continued follow-up.1

CONCLUSIONS

We report a rare case of a maxillary sinus cyst developing decades after zygomaticomaxillary complex fracture repair. Endoscope-assisted removal of the bony septum with orbital floor reconstruction using an iliac inner table graft resulted in a good outcome. Clinicians should recognize that altered sinus anatomy after trauma or surgery may be predisposed to delayed cyst formation and require long-term attention.
