Bornological Metrics on Groups
A. A. Arutyunov, A. V. Perelygin

Abstract




1
Introduction

Let
G
be a group. In many mathematical contexts it is natural to
regard
G
as a metric space, with the connection between the metric
and the algebraic structure expressed by left invariance: a metric

\rho\colon G\times G\to\mathbb{R}
is
left-invariant
if

\rho(x,y)=\rho(gx,gy),\qquad\forall\,g,x,y\in G.

In
[
Smi06
]
J. Smith proved that a proper left-invariant
metric is unique up to coarse equivalence, where properness means
that every ball of finite radius contains only finitely many elements
of the group.

In the present paper we give a description of left-invariant

improper
metrics, using the construction of bornological
groups introduced by R. Tessera and J. Winkel
in
[
TW22
]
. In the course of studying bornological
groups we also clarify some of their properties; in particular, we
establish a metrizability criterion for a bornological group.

It turns out that, from the point of view of coarse geometry, there
is no difference between the classical left-invariance condition and
its coarse analogue, namely the condition we call

bornologicity
of a metric: a metric
\rho
is bornological if
for every
C>0
there exists a constant
S_{C}>0
such that for any
pair
x,y\in G
with
\rho(x,y)<C
one has

\rho(gx,gy)<S_{C},\qquad\forall\,g\in G.

We show that each coarse-equivalence class of bornological metrics is
determined by a bornology (see Definition
2.1
).

Metrics subject to similar conditions appear in various contexts.
Besides
[
Smi06
]
, we mention the application of this
construction to the study of uniform Roe algebras
[
CL21
,
CMS24
]
, as well as its use in the study of asymptotic
dimension
[
BF08
,
BD08
]
. In
[
AF26
]

the opposite conditions are imposed on the metric, leading to the
study of expansivity metrics.

The main results of the paper are as follows.

Proposition
2.4
.

Let
\rho
be a metric on a group
G
. Then
\rho
is
bornological if and only if there exists a bornology
\mathfrak{B}

such that the left coarse structure
\mathcal{E}_{L}
coincides with
the bounded coarse structure
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
. The bornology

\mathfrak{B}
consists of all sets of finite diameter.

Moreover, within the coarse-equivalence class of bornological metrics
there is a canonical representative, namely a left-invariant metric
on the group.

Theorem
2.4
.

In every coarse-equivalence class of bornological metrics on
a group
G
there exists a left-invariant metric.

Coarsely equivalent metrics can be identified by the classes of sets
that are bounded with respect to them.

Theorem
2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be coarsely
connected, strongly
G
-invariant left coarse structures with the
same families of coarsely bounded sets,

\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E}_{1})=\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
. Then
the coarse spaces
(G,\mathcal{E}_{1})
and
(G,\mathcal{E}_{2})
are
coarsely equivalent.

It is natural to ask which bornologies arise from metrics. The
answer takes the form natural to coarse geometry, via the notion of
countable generation. For the precise statement we need the concept
of a strongly
G
-invariant coarse structure
(see Definition
2.4
): a coarse structure
\mathcal{E}

is strongly
G
-invariant if for every controlled set
E\in\mathcal{E}

and every decomposition
E=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}
with

E_{i}\in\mathcal{E}
, and for every sequence
g=\{g_{i}\mid g_{i}\in G\}
,
one has

gE:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}E_{i}\in\mathcal{E},

where
g_{i}E_{i}:=\{(g_{i}x,\,g_{i}y)\mid(x,y)\in E_{i}\}
.

Theorem
2.5
.

(1)

If the coarse structure
\mathcal{E}
on a group
G
is
countably generated and strongly
G
-invariant, then

\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E})
is a metrizable bornology.

(2)

If the bornology
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E})
on a
bornological group
G
is metrizable, then the coarse structure

\mathcal{E}
is countably generated and strongly
G
-invariant
on the left.

The connection between metrizability and the existence of
left-invariant metrics was previously discussed in
[
MRD21
]

(see Section 7); however, a complete description of improper metric
structures in terms of bornologies appears to be new.

In the final part of the paper we study various classes of
non-equivalent improper bornological metrics. In particular, the
following criterion is useful for constructing such examples.

Theorem
3.1
.

Let
G
be an infinite finitely generated group and let
d

be a proper left-invariant metric on
G
. If
N
is a normal
subgroup of finite index and
\rho_{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}}
is the word
metric on the quotient group, then
(G,d)
is not coarsely equivalent
to
\bigl({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},\rho_{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}}\bigr)
.

1.1
Bornological and left-invariant metrics on groups

We begin by fixing the definition and notation for a metric on an
abstract space
X
.

Definition 1.1
.

A map
\rho\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called a

metric
if:

1.

\rho(x,y)=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ x=y
;

2.

\rho(x,y)=\rho(y,x)
for all
x,y
;

3.

\rho(x,z)\leqslant\rho(x,y)+\rho(y,z)
for all
x,y,z
.

We are primarily interested in metrics on groups, which are
conveniently defined via norms. Let
G
be a countable group; we
denote its identity element by
e_{G}
.

Definition 1.2
.

A map
d\colon G\to[0,+\infty)
is called a
norm
on the
group
G
if:

•

d(g)=0
if and only if
g=e_{G}
;

•

d(g^{-1})=d(g)
for every
g\in G
;

•

d(gh)\leq d(g)+d(h)
for all
g,h\in G
.

A norm
\|\cdot\|
induces a metric
\rho_{\|\cdot\|}
on
G
by

\rho_{\|\cdot\|}(g,h):=\|g^{-1}h\|.

The induced metric is left-invariant:
\rho(ag,ah)=\rho(g,h)
for
every
a\in G
.

We also give the definition of a proper norm.

Definition 1.3
.

A norm
d
on
G
is called
proper
if for every
R>0
there
are only finitely many
g\in G
with
d(g)\leq R
. A metric induced
by a proper norm is called proper.

A finitely generated group is naturally equipped with a
word
norm
and the induced metric with respect to a fixed generating set

\mathcal{X}
. The word norm
\|g\|
of an element
g\in G
is
defined as the minimal length of a word in the generators

\mathcal{X}
representing
g
. This metric is both proper and
left-invariant.

A more general statement for countable (not necessarily finitely
generated) groups is proved in
[
Smi06
, Proposition 1]
. The
notion of coarse equivalence is defined below
(see Definition
1.13
).

Theorem 1.1

(J. Smith)
.

On a countable group, any two proper left-invariant metrics are
coarsely equivalent.

We now drop the requirement that metrics (and norms) be proper and
study all left-invariant metrics. The left-invariance condition itself
can also be relaxed.

Definition 1.4
.

A metric
\rho
on a group
G
is called
left bornological

if for every
C>0
there exists a constant
S_{C}>0
such that for
any pair
x,y\in G
with
\rho(x,y)<C
one has

\rho(gx,gy)<S_{C},\qquad\forall\,g\in G.

One can analogously define a right bornological metric, but we do not
consider this notion here. Accordingly, we henceforth refer to left
bornological metrics simply as bornological metrics.

1.2
Coarse structures

To define coarse equivalence of metrics we recall the standard
definitions from coarse geometry that we shall use. As in most other
standard definitions in coarse geometry, we follow the terminology
of
[
Roe03
]
.

Definition 1.5
.

A pair
(X,\mathcal{E})
is called a
coarse space
, and the
family of subsets
\mathcal{E}\subset 2^{X\times X}
is called a

coarse structure
on
X
, if
\mathcal{E}
satisfies the
following conditions:

1.

If
E\in\mathcal{E}
and
E_{1}\subset E
, then

E_{1}\in\mathcal{E}
.

2.

If
E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\in\mathcal{E}
, then

\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\in\mathcal{E}
.

3.

If
E\in\mathcal{E}
, then
E^{-1}\in\mathcal{E}
, where

E^{-1}=\{(y,x)\mid(x,y)\in E\}.

4.

If
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
, then

E_{1}\circ E_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
, where

E_{1}\circ E_{2}=\{(x,z)\mid\exists\,y\colon(x,y)\in E_{1}\text{ and }(y,z)\in E_{2}\}.

5.

\operatorname{diag}_{X\times X}=\{(x,x)\mid x\in X\}\in\mathcal{E}
.

The central example of a coarse structure is the bounded coarse
structure generated by a metric space
(X,\rho)
.

Definition 1.6
.

The
bounded coarse structure

\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
on a metric
space
(X,\rho)
is the family of subsets of
X\times X
defined by

E\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \bigl\{\exists\,C>0\colon\forall\,(x,x^{\prime})\in E,\quad\rho(x,x^{\prime})<C\bigr\}.

(1.1)

That
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
is indeed a coarse structure follows directly
from the definitions.

Closely related to the notion of a coarse structure is the notion of
a coarsely bounded set.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{E})
be a coarse space. A subset
B\subset X
is
called
coarsely bounded
if
B\times B\in\mathcal{E}
. We
denote the family of coarsely bounded sets by
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E})
.

For the bounded coarse structure of
Definition
1.6
, coarse boundedness
coincides with ordinary metric boundedness.

We record the following lemma for completeness; it will be used later.

Lemma 1.1
.

The following conditions for a coarse space
(X,\mathcal{E})
are
equivalent:

1.

B\times B\in\mathcal{E}
;

2.

there exists a point
x\in X
such that

B\times\{x\}\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

1.

By the first axiom of a coarse structure,
B\times B\in\mathcal{E}
implies
B\times\{x\}\in\mathcal{E}
for every

x\in B
.

2.

Suppose
B\times\{x\}\in\mathcal{E}
. By the axioms of a
coarse structure,
\{x\}\times B=(B\times\{x\})^{-1}\in\mathcal{E}
.
Applying the composition operation to
B\times\{x\}
and

\{x\}\times B
gives
B\times B\in\mathcal{E}
.∎

We now give the general definition of coarse equivalence. Let
X

and
Y
be coarse spaces.

Definition 1.8
.

A map
f\colon X\to Y
is called
proper
if for every
coarsely bounded set
B\subset Y
the preimage
f^{-1}(B)
is also
bounded.

Definition 1.9
.

A map
f\colon X\to Y
is called
bornologous
if for every
controlled set
E
the set
(f\times f)(E)
is also controlled.

Definition 1.10
.

A map
f\colon X\to Y
is called a
coarse map
if it is both
proper and bornologous.

Let
M
be an arbitrary set (not necessarily equipped with a coarse
structure) and let
(X,\mathcal{E})
be a coarse space.

Definition 1.11
.

Two maps
f,f^{\prime}\colon M\to X
are called
close
if the set

\{(f(m),f^{\prime}(m))\mid m\in M\}

is controlled.

When
X
is a metric space with the bounded coarse structure
(Definition
1.6
), closeness is
equivalent to the existence of a uniform bound on the distances
between images: for some constant
C\geq 0
,

\rho(f(m),f^{\prime}(m))\leq C.

We can now give the definition of coarse equivalence.

Definition 1.12
.

Coarse spaces
X
and
Y
are
coarsely equivalent

(X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}Y)
if there exist coarse maps
f\colon X\to Y

and
g\colon Y\to X
such that
f\circ g
is close to

\operatorname{id}_{Y}
and
g\circ f
is close to

\operatorname{id}_{X}
.

For metric spaces this specializes as follows.

Definition 1.13
.

Two metrics
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
on a group
G
are called

coarsely equivalent
if the coarse space
(G,\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{1}})

is coarsely equivalent to
(G,\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{2}})
.

A natural example of coarsely equivalent metrics is provided by
proportional metrics; however, coarsely equivalent metrics need not
be proportional.

2
Bornological groups

We follow the definitions of
[
TW22
]
. Let
G
be
a group; we denote by
2^{G}
the set of all subsets of
G
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
bornology
on
G
is a family of subsets

\mathfrak{B}\subset 2^{G}
satisfying the following axioms:

1.

For every
g\in G
the singleton
\{g\}
belongs to

\mathfrak{B}
.

2.

If
A\subset B\subset G
and
B\in\mathfrak{B}
, then

A\in\mathfrak{B}
.

3.

If
A\in\mathfrak{B}
, then
A^{-1}\in\mathfrak{B}
.

4.

If
A,B\in\mathfrak{B}
, then
A\cup B\in\mathfrak{B}

and
A\cdot B\in\mathfrak{B}
.

A group equipped with a bornology is naturally called a

bornological group
. The elements of the bornology, as will
become clear below, are naturally called
bounded sets
.

Definition 2.2
.

The pair
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is called a
bornological group
.
A set
A\in\mathfrak{B}
is called
bornologically bounded

(or simply
bounded
when no confusion can arise).

Example 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group. The family of all finite subsets of
G
forms a
bornology
\mathfrak{B}_{\min}
.

In this bornology the bounded sets are precisely the finite sets. At
the same time, it is easy to see from the axioms that finite subsets
belong to every bornology, so
\mathfrak{B}_{\min}
is the minimal
bornological structure on
G
.

The maximal bornology is defined analogously.

Example 2.2
.

The family of all subsets
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathrm{full}}:=2^{G}

forms a bornology. In this bornology every subset of
G
is
bounded.

2.1
Left and right bornological coarse structures

A bornology
\mathfrak{B}
naturally gives rise to two coarse
structures, which we now define.

Definition 2.3
.

1.

The
left bornological coarse structure
is defined
by

E\in\mathcal{E}_{L}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \{x^{-1}y\mid(x,y)\in E\}\in\mathfrak{B}.

(2.1)

2.

The
right bornological coarse structure
is defined
by

E\in\mathcal{E}_{R}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \{xy^{-1}\mid(x,y)\in E\}\in\mathfrak{B}.

(2.2)

The family of coarsely bounded sets for both the left and right
bornological coarse structures coincides with the bornology.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
\mathcal{E}_{L}
(respectively
\mathcal{E}_{R}
) one has

\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E}_{L})=\mathfrak{B}
, that is, the coarsely
bounded sets coincide with the bornologically bounded sets.

Proof.

Suppose
B\in\mathfrak{B}
. By Lemma
1.1
it
suffices to show that
B\times\{g\}\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
for some

g\in G
. Indeed,
\{x^{-1}g\mid x\in B\}=B^{-1}\cdot g\in\mathfrak{B}

by the axioms of a bornology, so
B\times\{g\}\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
by
definition. Hence
\mathfrak{B}\subset\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E}_{L})
.

Conversely, suppose
B\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E}_{L})
. Then there
exists
E\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
such that
B=E_{\{g\}}:=\{x\mid(x,g)\in E\}
for some
g\in G
. Since
E\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
, the
set
A=\{x^{-1}y\mid(x,y)\in E\}\in\mathfrak{B}
. By definition

B\subset\{g\}\cdot A^{-1}\in\mathfrak{B}
, so

\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E}_{L})\subset\mathfrak{B}
.
∎

As expected, the coarse spaces
(G,\mathcal{E}_{L})
and

(G,\mathcal{E}_{R})
are coarsely equivalent.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group with a bornology. Then

(G,\mathfrak{B},\mathcal{E}_{L})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}(G,\mathfrak{B},\mathcal{E}_{R}).

Proof.

Consider the map
\theta\colon x\mapsto x^{-1}
. We show that it
is a coarse equivalence.

•

Properness.
The preimage of a bounded set is
bounded, since the bornology is closed under taking
inverses.

•

Bornologicity.
Let
E=\{(x,y)\}\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
,
so that
A_{E}=\{x^{-1}y\mid(x,y)\in E\}\in\mathfrak{B}
.
Then
(\theta\times\theta)(E)=\{(x^{-1},y^{-1})\mid(x,y)\in E\}
. Since

A_{(\theta\times\theta)(E)}=\{x^{-1}y\}=A_{E}\in\mathfrak{B}
,
we have
(\theta\times\theta)(E)\in\mathcal{E}_{R}
.

•

Since
\theta
is a bijection, the compositions

\theta\circ\theta^{-1}
and
\theta^{-1}\circ\theta

are equal (and hence close) to the identity.∎

2.2
Coarse structures associated with a bornology

We define a class of coarse structures compatible with the group
structure.

Definition 2.4
.

A coarse structure
\mathcal{E}
is called
strongly

G
-invariant on the left
if for every
E\in\mathcal{E}
, every
decomposition
E=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}E_{i}
with
E_{i}\in\mathcal{E}

(where
I
is an index set and some
E_{i}
may be empty), and every
family
\bar{g}=\{g_{h}\mid h\in G\}
, the set

\bar{g}E:=\bigcup_{h\in G}g_{h}E_{h}

is controlled, where
g_{h}E_{h}:=\{(g_{h}x,g_{h}y)\mid(x,y)\in E_{h}\}
.

Example 2.3
.

The left bornological coarse structure
\mathcal{E}_{L}
is strongly

G
-invariant on the left.

Definition 2.5
.

A coarse structure
\mathcal{E}
on a space
X
is called

coarsely connected
if for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
there exists a
controlled set
E\in\mathcal{E}
such that
(x,x^{\prime})\in E
.

Coarse connectedness can be informally understood as the
commensurability of any two points. For the bounded coarse structure
generated by a metric, coarse connectedness is equivalent to ordinary
metric connectedness.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is strongly
G
-invariant on the left and coarsely
connected, then the family

\mathfrak{B}:=\bigl\{B_{E}=\{x^{-1}y\mid(x,y)\in E\}\mid E\in\mathcal{E}\bigr\}

is a bornology and
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{L}
.

Proof.

We verify the axioms of a bornology.

1.

Singletons belong to
\mathfrak{B}
.

2.

Closure under taking subsets, finite unions, and inverses
follows from the axioms of a coarse structure.

3.

Closure under products follows from strong

G
-invariance. Let

A=\{x^{-1}y\mid(x,y)\in E_{A}\},\qquad B=\{a^{-1}b\mid(a,b)\in E_{B}\}.

Then

A\cdot B=\{x^{-1}ya^{-1}b\mid(x,y)\in E_{A},\,(a,b)\in E_{B}\}.

Using strong
G
-invariance applied to
E_{A}
, define the
controlled sets

E:=\{(xy^{-1},e)\},\qquad E^{\prime}:=\{(e,a^{-1}b)\}.

Applying Lemma
1.1
to
E\circ E^{\prime}
gives
the required result.

The inclusion
\mathcal{E}_{L}\subset\mathcal{E}
is clear. The
construction of the bornology gives the reverse inclusion

\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{E}_{L}
.
∎

Corollary 2.0.1
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is strongly
G
-invariant on the left and coarsely
connected, then
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E})
is a bornology
consisting of the sets described in
Proposition
2.3
.

Considering strongly
G
-invariant coarse structures allows us to
strengthen Proposition
2.2
. We show that two such
coarse structures on
G
are coarsely equivalent whenever their
bornologies coincide. The proof uses the following result
from
[
BDM06
, Theorem 6.3]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Brodskiy–Dydak–Mitra)
.

Let
\alpha_{i}\colon G_{i}\times X\to X
,
i=1,2
, be two commuting
left actions of groups
G_{i}
on a set
X
. Suppose the coarse
structures
\mathcal{E}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, have the same families of
bounded sets and each action
\alpha_{i}
is coarse with respect to

\mathcal{E}_{i}
. Then:

1.

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are coarsely equivalent;

2.

(X,\mathcal{E}_{1})
is coarsely equivalent to

(X,\mathcal{E}_{2})
.

We now define a coarse action, following
[
BDM06
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

An action
\alpha\colon G\times X\to X
of a group on a coarse
space
(X,\mathcal{E})
is called
coarse
if:

1.

For each
x\in X
the map
\phi_{x}\colon G\to G\cdot x

is coarsely proper, i.e., the preimage of every bounded
set is bounded.

2.

The action is cobounded: for some bounded set
U
one has

X=G\cdot U
.

3.

The action is uniformly bornological: for every controlled
set
E
there exists a controlled set
E^{\prime}
such that

(g\cdot x,g\cdot y)\in E^{\prime}
for all
(x,y)\in E
and

g\in G
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be coarsely connected,
strongly
G
-invariant left coarse structures with the same families
of coarsely bounded sets,

\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E}_{1})=\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
. Then
the coarse spaces
(G,\mathcal{E}_{1})
and
(G,\mathcal{E}_{2})
are
coarsely equivalent.

Proof.

We show that the natural left action of a bornological group on
itself,
G\times G\to G
, is coarse.

•

Coboundedness.
The action is transitive and free,
so any single point serves as
U
.

•

Uniform bornologicity.
This follows immediately
from strong
G
-invariance applied to
E
with the
constant sequence
\bar{g}=(g,g,\ldots)
.

•

Coarse properness.
This follows from the
definition of a bornological group.

It remains to apply Theorem
2.1

from
[
BDM06
]
to the left
translation action of
G
on itself.
∎

2.3
Examples

We give several examples of bornological groups and the resulting
left coarse structures. We begin with various bornological structures
on the additive group of integers. The maximal bornology, as in
Example
2.2
, consists of all subsets of the group.

Example 2.4
.

The group
\mathbb{Z}
with the bornology

\mathfrak{B}_{\mathrm{full}}=2^{\mathbb{Z}}
has left bornological coarse
structure
\mathcal{E}_{L}=2^{\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}}
.

The intersection of any family of bornologies is again a bornology,
so the minimal bornology (with respect to inclusion) is the family of
finite subsets.

Example 2.5
.

The group
\mathbb{Z}
with the minimal bornology

\mathfrak{B}_{\min}
has left bornological coarse structure
\mathcal{E}_{L}

coinciding with
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
, where
\rho
is the metric

\rho(x,y)=|x-y|
.

Proof.

Minimality of the bornology gives
\mathcal{E}_{L}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.

For the reverse inclusion, let
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
. Then the
set
\{x^{-1}y\mid(x,y)\in E\}=\{y-x\mid(x,y)\in E\}
is
finite, since
|x-y|\leq C
for some
C
implies that
y-x

takes only finitely many values. Hence
E\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
.
∎

We turn to a more interesting example: the Heisenberg group, which is
the group of upper unitriangular integer matrices of size
3\times 3
.

Equip the Heisenberg group
\mathcal{H}
with the metric

\rho(A,B)=\max_{i,j}|a_{ij}-b_{ij}|,

and denote the corresponding coarse structure by
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.
As the bornology we take the family of sets that are bounded with
respect to this metric; these are precisely the finite sets.

Example 2.6
.

The left bornological coarse structure of
\mathcal{H}
does not
coincide with the bounded coarse structure
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
:

\mathcal{E}_{L}\neq\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.

Proof.

Let

B_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1&n+1&1\\
0&1&1\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1&n&1\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\rho(A_{n},B_{n})=1
, but
\rho(B_{n}^{-1}A_{n},E)=n+1
. Hence the
set
E=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(B_{n},A_{n})
is controlled in

\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
but not in
\mathcal{E}_{L}
, since

\{B_{n}^{-1}A_{n}\}
is unbounded. Therefore

\mathcal{E}_{\rho}\neq\mathcal{E}_{L}
.
∎

Thus, although the bornology and the bounded coarse structure are
both generated by the same metric, the two coarse structures do not
coincide.

Hence bornological coarse structures need not coincide in general.
For the case where the bornology consists of finite sets it is known
(see
[
BDM06
, Proposition 2.1]
)
that the left and right coarse structures coincide if and only if
G

is an FC-group, i.e., every conjugacy class in
G
is finite.

Before giving another useful example of a bornological structure, we
recall the definition of a pseudometric.

Definition 2.7
.

A map
\rho\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is called a

pseudometric
if:

1.

\rho(x,x)=0
for all
x
;

2.

\rho(x,y)=\rho(y,x)
for all
x,y
;

3.

\rho(x,z)\leqslant\rho(x,y)+\rho(y,z)
for all
x,y,z
.

A pseudometric differs from a metric in that
\rho(x,y)
may equal
zero for
x\neq y
.

Example 2.7
.

Consider the same group as in Example
2.6
,
but equipped with the pseudometric
\rho(A,B)=|a_{12}-b_{12}|
.

Proof.

Call a set bounded if it has finite diameter. Axioms 1–3 of a
bornology are obvious; for axiom 4 it remains to verify closure
under products, which follows from the matrix multiplication
formula. Hence we indeed obtain a bornology.

For the pseudometric defined above, the bounded coarse structure

\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
is well defined. The matrix multiplication
formula gives
\rho(E,A^{-1}\cdot B)=\rho(A,B)
for this
pseudometric, whence
\mathcal{E}_{L}=\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.
∎

This example is of interest because it shows that pseudometrics can
generate bornologies.

2.4
Metrizability of bornological coarse structures

Recall that metrizability of a coarse structure means, informally,
that there exists a metric (not uniquely determined) generating it as
a bounded coarse structure.

Definition 2.8
.

A coarse structure
\mathcal{E}
on a set
X
is
metrizable

if there exists a metric
\rho
on
X
such that

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.

The following metrizability criterion is proved
in
[
Roe03
, Theorem 2.55]
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let us say that a family of sets
\{S_{i}\mid S_{i}\subset 2^{X\times X}\}_{i\in I}
, where
I
is a set of indices, generates a coarse structure
\mathcal{E}
on the set
X
if:

•

S_{i}\subset\mathcal{E}
for all
i\in I
;

•

Structure
\mathcal{E}
has the minimum inclusion among all structures containing the entire set
S_{i}
.

In that case, we will write
\mathcal{E}=\langle S_{i}\rangle
.

Theorem 2.3
.

A coarse structure
\mathcal{E}
is metrizable if and only if it is
countably generated.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\rho
be a metric on a group
G
. Then
\rho
is bornological
if and only if there exists a bornology
\mathfrak{B}
such that
the left coarse structure
\mathcal{E}_{L}
coincides with the
bounded coarse structure
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
. The bornology

\mathfrak{B}
consists of all sets of finite diameter.

Proof.

Suppose
\rho
is bornological. Consider the family
\mathfrak{B}

consisting of all subsets of
G
of the form

\{x^{-1}y\mid(x,y)\in E\},\qquad E\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho}.

•

\mathfrak{B}
is a bornology.

1.

Singletons belong to
\mathfrak{B}
.

2.

A subset of a bounded set is bounded, since

E^{\prime}\subset E
and
E\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
imply

E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
.

3.

Closure under inverses is verified analogously.

4.

Closure under products: consider the set

\{x^{-1}ya^{-1}b\mid(x,y)\in E,\,(a,b)\in E^{\prime}\}.

To prove boundedness it suffices to show this set
arises from a controlled set. Consider pairs of the
form
(y^{-1}x,a^{-1}b)
. Then

\rho(y^{-1}x,a^{-1}b)\leq\rho(y^{-1}x,e)+\rho(e,a^{-1}b)<C

by bornologicity of
\rho
, which gives the required
boundedness.

5.

Closure under finite unions follows from the axioms
of a coarse structure.

•

\mathcal{E}_{L}=\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.
The inclusion

\mathcal{E}_{L}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
follows from the
construction. For the reverse inclusion, let

E\in\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
; then by construction

\{x^{-1}y\mid(x,y)\in E\}\in\mathfrak{B}
, so

E\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
.

•

Necessity.
Suppose there exists a bornology

\mathfrak{B}
with
\mathcal{E}_{L}=\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
,
and assume for contradiction that
\rho
is not
bornological. Then there exists
C>0
such that for every

S_{C}>0
one can find
(x,y)
with
\rho(x,y)<C
and

g\in G
with
\rho(gx,gy)\geq S_{C}
. Consider the
controlled set

E_{C}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X\mid\rho(x,y)\leq C\}.

Then the set
\{(gx,gy)\}
belongs to
\mathcal{E}_{L}

but not to
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
, contradicting

\mathcal{E}_{L}=\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.∎

We now begin to investigate metrizability conditions. First we
establish the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(G,\mathfrak{B},\mathcal{E}_{L})
be a bornological group. If

\mathcal{E}_{L}
is metrizable by a metric
\rho
, then for every
subset
B\subset G
,

B\in\mathfrak{B}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \operatorname{diam}_{\rho}(B)<+\infty.

Proof.

Suppose
B\in\mathfrak{B}
. Consider the set

E_{B}=\{(e,x)\mid x\in B\}
. Since

\{e^{-1}x\mid x\in B\}=B\in\mathfrak{B}
, we have

E_{B}\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
. By metrizability,

\exists\,C>0\colon\forall\,(e,x)\in E_{B},\quad\rho(e,x)<C,

so
\operatorname{diam}_{\rho}(B)\leq 2C
.

Conversely, suppose
B\subset G
and

\operatorname{diam}_{\rho}(B)<+\infty
. Set

C=\operatorname{diam}_{\rho}(B)
and fix
x\in B
. Then

\rho(x,y)<C
for all
y\in B
, so

\rho(e,y)\leq\rho(e,x)+C\qquad\forall\,y\in B.

Hence
E=\{(e,y)\mid y\in B\}\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
, and therefore

B\in\mathfrak{B}
.
∎

This proposition leads naturally to the following definition.

Definition 2.10
.

A bornology
\mathfrak{B}
is called
metrizable
if there
exists a metric
\rho
such that
B\in\mathfrak{B}
if and only if

\operatorname{diam}_{\rho}(B)<\infty
.

Given a bornological metric
\rho
on
G
, define

\rho^{+}(x,y):=\sup_{g\in G}\rho(gx,gy).

As we show below, this is a left-invariant metric coarsely equivalent
to
\rho
.

Theorem 2.4
.

In every coarse-equivalence class of bornological metrics on a
group
G
there exists a left-invariant metric.

Proof.

•

\rho^{+}
is a metric.
Symmetry, positive
definiteness, and non-degeneracy are clear. The triangle
inequality:

\begin{split}\rho^{+}(x,z)&=\sup_{g\in G}\rho(gx,gz)\leq\sup_{g\in G}\bigl(\rho(gx,gy)+\rho(gy,gz)\bigr)\\
&\leq\sup_{g\in G}\rho(gx,gy)+\sup_{g\in G}\rho(gy,gz)=\rho^{+}(x,y)+\rho^{+}(y,z).\end{split}

•

Left invariance of
\rho^{+}
follows immediately
from the definition.

•

(G,\mathcal{E}_{\rho})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}(G,\mathcal{E}_{\rho^{+}})
.
It suffices to show that the
identity map

\operatorname{id}\colon(G,\mathcal{E}_{\rho})\to(G,\mathcal{E}_{\rho^{+}})
is a coarse equivalence.
Bornologicity of
\operatorname{id}
in both directions
follows from the bornologicity of
\rho
and
\rho^{+}
.
Properness follows from the fact that both bornologies
consist of sets of finite diameter
(Proposition
2.4
). Closeness of the
compositions to the identity is clear.∎

It is now clear that bornological metrics are in bijective
correspondence with metrizable bornologies: metrizability of a
bornology is equivalent to metrizability of the left bornological
coarse structure coinciding with
\mathcal{E}_{\rho}
.

As we now show, metrizability of the bornological coarse structure is
equivalent to the existence of a countable basis for the bornology.

Definition 2.11
.

A bornology
\mathfrak{B}
has a
countable basis
if there
exists a sequence
\{B_{i}\mid B_{i}\in\mathfrak{B}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
,
called a basis of the bornology, such that for every

B\in\mathfrak{B}
there exists
N
with
B\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}B_{j}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

For a group
G
the following are equivalent:

1.

\mathcal{E}_{L}
is countably generated;

2.

\mathcal{E}_{R}
is countably generated;

3.

\mathfrak{B}
has a countable basis.

Proof.

Define the map
\Omega\colon X\times X\to X
by
\Omega(x,y)=x^{-1}y
.

3\Rightarrow 1
.

Let
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a
countable basis of
\mathfrak{B}
. Set

E_{n}:=\{(x,y)\mid\Omega(x,y)\in B_{n}\}.

Clearly
\langle E_{n}\rangle\subset\mathcal{E}_{L}
. Let

E\in\mathcal{E}_{L}
; then
\Omega(E)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}B_{i}

for some
k
, so
E\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}E_{i}
. Hence

\mathcal{E}_{L}\subset\langle E_{n}\rangle
.

1\Rightarrow 3
.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{L}
is countably
generated it is metrizable (Theorem
2.3
), so
Proposition
2.5

applies: the sets
E_{n}(e)=\{g\mid\rho(e,g)<n+1\}
form a
countable basis of
\mathfrak{B}
.

1\Leftrightarrow 2
.

By
Proposition
2.2
the coarse spaces are
equivalent, hence their coarse structures are metrizable
simultaneously. By Theorem
2.3
this is
equivalent to countable generation.∎

The equivalence between a bornology having a countable basis and
being metrizable was noted in
[
MRD21
, Proposition 7.1]
,
where an explicit formula for the corresponding metric is also given.

Corollary 2.4.1
.

A bornology
\mathfrak{B}
has a countable basis if and only if it
is metrizable.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{B}
is metrizable then it has a countable basis (see
the end of the proof of Proposition
2.6
). Conversely, if

\mathfrak{B}
has a countable basis, then by
Proposition
2.6
the structure
\mathcal{E}_{L}
is countably
generated, hence metrizable, and
Proposition
2.5

gives metrizability of
\mathfrak{B}
.
∎

For finitely generated groups the word metric corresponds to the
minimal bornology.

Proposition 2.7
.

If
G
is finitely generated, then
\mathfrak{B}_{\min}
is
generated by the word metric.

Proof.

The bornology
\mathfrak{B}_{\min}
consists of finite sets. Every
finite set has finite diameter with respect to the word metric.
Conversely, every set of finite diameter with respect to the word
metric in a finitely generated group is finite, by definition of
the word metric.
∎

For countably generated groups the construction of the corresponding
metric is more involved; this was addressed in
[
Smi06
]
.

Combining Propositions
2.5
,

2.6
, and
2.4
, we obtain the following
description of the relation between metrizability and coarse
structures.

Theorem 2.5
.

1.

If the coarse structure
\mathcal{E}
on a group
G
is
countably generated and strongly
G
-invariant on the
left, then
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E})
is a metrizable
bornology.

2.

If the bornology
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E})
on a
bornological group
G
is metrizable, then
\mathcal{E}

is countably generated and strongly
G
-invariant on the
left.

Proof.

1.

Since
\mathcal{E}
is countably generated it is
metrizable. Since
\mathcal{E}
is strongly

G
-invariant, the corresponding metric is left
bornological. By Corollary
2.0.1
,
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E})
is a
bornology, and by Proposition
2.5
it is metrizable.

2.

If
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{E})
is metrizable then it has
a countable basis. By Proposition
2.6
,

\mathcal{E}
is countably generated, hence metrizable.
By Proposition
2.4
the corresponding
metric is left bornological, so
\mathcal{E}
is strongly

G
-invariant on the left.∎

2.5
Equivalence of bornological groups

We carry over the coarse equivalence relation defined above for
coarse spaces to bornological groups.

Definition 2.12
.

A bornological group
(G,\mathfrak{B})
is
coarsely
equivalent
to a bornological group
(H,\mathfrak{R})
, written

(G,\mathfrak{B})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}(H,\mathfrak{R})
, if
G
and

H
are coarsely equivalent as coarse spaces with structures

\mathcal{E}_{L_{\mathfrak{B}}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{L_{\mathfrak{R}}}

respectively.

For bornologies with a countable basis this definition is equivalent
to coarse equivalence of the corresponding metric spaces.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f
be a surjective coarse map from a bornological group

(G,\mathfrak{B})
with countable basis to a bornological group

(H,\mathfrak{R})
with countable basis. Then

f(\mathfrak{B})=\mathfrak{R}
.

As will be clear from the proof, it suffices to require surjectivity
only onto bounded sets.

Proof.

Since
f\colon(G,\mathfrak{B})\to(H,\mathfrak{R})
is a coarse
map, every controlled set
E\in\mathcal{E}_{L_{\mathfrak{B}}}

maps to a controlled set
(f\times f)(E)\in\mathcal{E}_{L_{\mathfrak{R}}}
.
Since both bornologies have countable bases, by
Proposition
2.6
the left coarse structures are countably
generated, hence metrizable. The condition that
f\times f
maps
controlled sets to controlled sets means

\forall\,x,y\in G\;\bigl(\rho_{1}(x,y)<C\Rightarrow\rho_{2}(f(x),f(y))<S_{C}\bigr).

Hence sets of finite diameter map to sets of finite diameter; by
Theorem
2.5
this
gives
f(\mathfrak{B})\subset\mathfrak{R}
. Since
f
is proper,

f^{-1}(\mathfrak{R})\subset\mathfrak{B}
, and surjectivity gives

\mathfrak{R}\subset f(\mathfrak{B})
. Hence

\mathfrak{R}=f(\mathfrak{B})
.
∎

We now give a criterion for coarse equivalence of metrizable
bornological structures on a given group. Let
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
and

\mathfrak{B}_{2}
be countably generated bornologies with countable
bases
\{B_{i}^{1}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{B_{i}^{2}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

respectively.

Proposition 2.9
.

1.

(Necessary condition for surjections.)
For
bornologies
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{B}_{2}
with
countable bases
\{B_{i}^{1}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and

\{B_{i}^{2}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
to be coarsely equivalent via
surjections, i.e.,
(G,\mathfrak{B}_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}(G,\mathfrak{B}_{2})
, it is necessary that every coarse
map sends the basis of
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
to a basis of

\mathfrak{B}_{2}
.

2.

(Sufficient condition.)
Bornologies
\mathfrak{B}_{1}

and
\mathfrak{B}_{2}
on
G
with countable bases are
coarsely equivalent,
(G,\mathfrak{B}_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}(G,\mathfrak{B}_{2})
, if there exists a bijection

f\colon G\to G
such that the image of the basis of

\mathfrak{B}_{1}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{B}_{2}
.

We recall that the definition of a basis of a bornology does not
require minimality in any sense.

Proof.

1.

By Lemma
2.8
, every coarse map
f

satisfies
\mathfrak{B}_{2}=f(\mathfrak{B}_{1})
. Hence for
every
B\in\mathfrak{B}_{2}
there exists
k
such that

f^{-1}(B)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}B_{i}^{1},

and therefore
B\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}f(B_{i}^{1})
.
Thus
\{f(B_{i}^{1})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a basis of

\mathfrak{B}_{2}
.

2.

We show that the bijection
f\colon(G,\mathfrak{B}_{1})\to(G,\mathfrak{B}_{2})
and its inverse
f^{-1}
form a
coarse equivalence. The preimage of a bounded set is
bounded since
f
is a bijection sending the basis of

\mathfrak{B}_{1}
to the basis of
\mathfrak{B}_{2}
.
Similarly, bounded sets map to bounded sets, so for every

E\in\mathcal{E}_{L_{\mathfrak{B}_{1}}}
we have

(f\times f)(E)\in\mathcal{E}_{L_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}}
;
hence
f
is bornological and therefore a coarse map. The
same argument applies to
f^{-1}
. Therefore

(G,\mathfrak{B}_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}(G,\mathfrak{B}_{2})
.∎

Combining Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
1.1
, we
obtain that on a countable group all bornologies with a countable
basis that generate bornological metrics coarsely equivalent to some
proper left-invariant metric are coarsely equivalent to one another.

3
Examples of improper metrics

To construct examples of improper left-invariant metrics we need to
understand which metrics defined on subgroups can be lifted to the
whole group, and how.

We reformulate the notion of a basis of a bornology
(Definition
2.11
) in a form more convenient for our
purposes.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a group and let
A\subset G
. We denote by
\langle A\rangle

the minimal bornology on
G
containing the set
A
.

By definition,
\langle A\rangle
contains all singletons, all sets
of the form
gA
and
Ag
, all finite powers of
A
, and all their
finite products, finite unions, and arbitrary subsets. An explicit
description of this bornology is rather cumbersome; it follows from
the definition that the minimal bornology containing
A
coincides
with the intersection of all bornologies containing
A
.

Definition
3.1
is related to the notion of a basis
(Definition
2.11
) as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a countable group. If a bornology
\mathfrak{B}
is
countably generated by sets
B_{i}
, that is,

\mathfrak{B}=\langle B_{i}\rangle
, then it has a countable basis.

Proof.

All finite combinations of the sets
B_{i}
and elements of
G

obtainable by group multiplication, shifts, and finite unions form
a countable basis, since there are countably many sets
B_{i}
and
the group itself is countable.
∎

Let
G
be a countable group and let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
.
Equip the quotient group
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}
with the word metric

\rho_{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}}
, and define a pseudometric on
G
by

\rho_{G}(a,b):=\rho_{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}}(\pi(a),\pi(b)),

where
\pi\colon G\to{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}
is the canonical quotient
homomorphism.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
G
is finitely generated, then

(G,\langle N\rangle)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}\Bigl({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},\rho_{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}}\Bigr).

(3.1)

Proof.

•

Since
N
is a normal subgroup, the bornology

\langle N\rangle
consists of finite unions of singletons
and cosets.

•

We show that
\langle N\rangle
coincides with the
bornology
\mathfrak{B}_{\rho_{G}}
constructed from the
pseudometric
\rho_{G}
(see Theorem
2.4
).
From the description of
\langle N\rangle
it is clear
that
\langle N\rangle\subset\mathfrak{B}_{\rho_{G}}
, since
every set in
\langle N\rangle
has finite diameter. Now
let
A\subset G
satisfy
\operatorname{diam}_{\rho_{G}}(A)<\infty
.
By definition of
\rho_{G}
we have

\operatorname{diam}_{\rho_{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.19583pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.19583pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{0.85416pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.85416pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}}}(\pi(A))<\infty
,
so by Proposition
2.7
the
set
\pi(A)
is finite. Hence
A
is contained in a
finite union of cosets of
N
, so
A\in\langle N\rangle
.
Therefore
\mathfrak{B}_{\rho_{G}}=\langle N\rangle
.

•

We show that the quotient map
\pi\colon G\to{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}

is a coarse map. If
B\subset{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}
is finite then

\pi^{-1}(B)\in\langle N\rangle
, since it is a finite
union of cosets of
N
. Hence
\pi
is proper. Bornologicity
of
\pi
follows from the description of
\langle N\rangle

and the definition of
\rho_{G}
.

•

The map
i\colon{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}\to G
sends each element of
the quotient to a chosen representative. It is proper since
the preimage of any finite set or finite union of cosets
in
G
is finite. Bornologicity follows from the definition
of
\rho_{G}
.

•

Since
\pi\circ i=\operatorname{id}
, closeness to the
identity requires no verification for this composition.
For
i\circ\pi
: the set

\{(i\circ\pi(g))^{-1}g\mid g\in G\}

belongs to
\langle N\rangle
, since
i\circ\pi(g)
lies
in the same coset as
g
, so
(i\circ\pi(g))^{-1}g\in N

for every
g\in G
.∎

Definition 3.2
.

Metrics defined by formula (
3.1
) are called

metrics realized via the normal subgroup
N
.

We now turn to examples of improper metrics on
\mathbb{Z}
. Every
subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}
is normal and has the form

k\mathbb{Z}:=\{kn\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Example 3.1
.

All metrics on
\mathbb{Z}
realized via subgroups
k\mathbb{Z}

are not coarsely equivalent to any proper left-invariant metric on
this group.

Proof.

•

The bornologies
\langle k\mathbb{Z}\rangle
are countable
and, by Corollary
2.4.1
,
metrizable. Denote the corresponding metric by
\rho_{k}
.

•

Suppose
\rho_{k}
is proper. By the uniqueness of the
proper metric (Theorem
1.1
) we have

(\mathbb{Z},\rho_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}(\mathbb{Z},d)
,
where
d(x,y)=|x-y|
.

•

By Proposition
3.2
,

(\mathbb{Z},\rho_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{k\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{k\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{k\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{k\mathbb{Z}}$}}},\mathfrak{B}_{\min})
.
Hence
(\mathbb{Z},d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{k\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{k\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{k\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{k\mathbb{Z}}$}}},\mathfrak{B}_{\min})
,
which is impossible: it would imply that
\mathbb{Z}
is
bounded with respect to
d
, contradicting properness.

This contradiction shows that
\rho_{k}
is not coarsely
equivalent to any proper left-invariant metric.∎

The proof uses no specific properties of
\mathbb{Z}
beyond finite
generation, infiniteness, and the description of its normal subgroups
of finite index. Thus we have in fact produced a class of improper
left-invariant metrics for all infinite finitely generated groups
admitting a normal subgroup of finite index, and we have proved the
following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be an infinite finitely generated group and let
d
be a
proper left-invariant metric on
G
. If
N
is a normal subgroup
of finite index and
\rho_{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}}
is the word metric on
the quotient group, then
(G,d)
is not coarsely equivalent to

\bigl({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},\rho_{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.67416pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.19583pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}}\bigr)
.

Not every countable bornology is generated by normal subgroups. We
now give an example of a metrizable bornological structure on

\mathbb{Z}
that generates a metric
\rho
not realizable via any
subgroup in the sense of Definition
3.2
: there is
no subgroup whose quotient is coarsely equivalent to
(\mathbb{Z},\rho)
.

In the group
\mathbb{Z}
choose the subset

A:=\{0,10,100,\ldots,10^{k},\ldots\}.

Example 3.2
.

There is no nontrivial subgroup
N\leq\mathbb{Z}
such that

(\mathbb{Z},\langle A\rangle)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\sim}}({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},\mathfrak{B}_{\min})
.

Proof.

•

The bornology
\langle A\rangle
is countably generated,
since
\mathbb{Z}
is abelian. Its basis consists of the
sets
kA:=A+A+\cdots+A
(
k
summands), all integer
translates of
A
, and all finite sets. Every bounded set
in
\langle A\rangle
is contained in a finite union of
sets of this form. By
Corollary
2.4.1
we
obtain a bornological metric
\rho
.

•

Since every nontrivial subgroup
N\leq\mathbb{Z}
gives a
finite quotient
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}}
, it suffices to
show that
\langle A\rangle\neq 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

•

The bornology equals
2^{\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if it
contains the set of even integers.

•

The set
2\mathbb{N}
cannot be expressed as a finite
union of basis sets of
\langle A\rangle
: the set
kA

consists of multiples of
10
, and the gaps between
consecutive elements of
A
grow, so no finite collection
of translates and finite sums involving
A
covers all
even integers. Hence
2\mathbb{N}\notin\langle A\rangle
,
as required.∎

This example generalizes to similarly defined sets. Choose a countable
family of subsets

A_{j}:=\{0,\,10+10j,\,(10+10j)^{2},\,\ldots,\,(10+10j)^{k},\,\ldots\}.

Example 3.3
.

In the group
\mathbb{Z}
, the bornology

\langle A_{j}\rangle
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, is not realizable via any
subgroup and generates an improper metric not coarsely equivalent
to any proper left-invariant metric on
\mathbb{Z}
.

Recall that a coarse structure is called
monogenic
if it is
generated by a single set. Monogenicity is a coarse invariant
(see
[
Roe03
, Proposition 2.57]
) and is equivalent
to the coarse space being coarsely equivalent to a geodesic space.

In particular, the space
(\mathbb{Z},d)
with
d(x,y)=|x-y|
has
the monogenic bounded coarse structure
\mathcal{E}_{d}
generated by

E=\{(x,y)\mid d(x,y)\leq 1\}
.

Proof.

•

Non-realizability via a subgroup follows by the same
argument as in the previous example:
\mathbb{Z}
itself
does not belong to this bornology, so it is not a bounded
set.

•

The bornology
\langle A_{j}\rangle
generated by the
entire family
\{A_{j}\}
has a countable basis consisting
of finite sums of the sets
A_{i}
(with repetitions) and
their translates. Hence it is countable and therefore
metrizable.

•

As noted above,
\mathcal{E}_{d}
is monogenic. On the
other hand, the left bornological coarse structure
generated by
\langle A_{j}\rangle
is not monogenic,
since there are infinitely many indices
j
for which

A_{j}
cannot be obtained from the other
A_{i}
by finitely
many operations (see the proof of
Proposition
2.6
, where a basis of the coarse
structure is constructed explicitly). Hence the metric
generated by
\langle A_{j}\rangle
is not coarsely
equivalent to any proper left-invariant metric on

\mathbb{Z}
.∎

Thus, even in elementary groups, improper left-invariant metrics can
generate the structure of a non-geodesic space.
