On some results of Korobov and Larcher and Zaremba’s conjecture
I.D. Shkredov

Abstract




Annotation.

We prove, in particular, the well–known Zaremba conjecture from the theory of continued fractions for any
prime denominator.
More precisely, we show, firstly, that under some mild conditions, for any sufficiently large
q
,
there exists

a
coprime to
q
such that all partial quotients of
a/q
are bounded by

O(\sqrt{\log q})
, and,
moreover we find asymptotically tight lower bound for the number of such
a
.
Secondly,
we obtain
a good
lower bound for the number
a
such that the sum of all partial quotients of
a/q
is bounded by
O(\log q\cdot\sqrt{\log\log q})
.
This, accordingly, improves on some results of Korobov and Larcher.
Finally, we show that for all sufficiently large
\mathcal{M}
there are
\Omega(q^{1-O(1/\mathcal{M})})
numbers
a
coprime to
q
such that all partial quotients of
a/q
are bounded by
\mathcal{M}
.

1.
Introduction

Let
a
and
q
be two positive coprime integers,
0<a<q
.
By the Euclidean algorithm, a rational
a/q
can be uniquely represented as a regular continued fraction

(1)

\frac{a}{q}=[0;c_{1},\dots,c_{s}]=\cfrac{1}{c_{1}+\cfrac{1}{c_{2}+\cfrac{1}{c_{3}+\cdots+\cfrac{1}{c_{s}}}}}\,,\qquad c_{s}\geqslant 2.

Assuming
q
is known, we use
c_{j}(a)
,
j=1,\ldots,s=s(a)
to denote the partial quotients of
a/q
; that is,

(2)

\frac{a}{q}:=[0;c_{1}(a),\ldots,c_{s}(a)].

Also, for any
a
put

(3)

M(a):=\max\{c_{1}(a),\ldots,c_{s}(a)\}\,.

Zaremba’s famous conjecture
[
54

posits that there is an absolute constant
\mathcal{M}
with the following property:
for any positive integer

coprime to

c_{j}(a)\leqslant\mathcal{M},\,\,\quad\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant s=s(a).

In other words, there is
a
coprime to
q
such that
M(a)\leqslant\mathcal{M}
.
Similar questions were considered by Korobov in sixties, see
[

and
[

]
.
In fact, Zaremba conjectured that

For large prime

[

Corollary 1
.

There is an absolute constant
\mathcal{M}\geqslant 2
such that for any prime (or square–free) number
p
there exists
a
,
(a,p)=1
such that

(5)

\frac{a}{p}=[0;c_{1},\dots,c_{s}]\,,\quad\quad c_{j}\leqslant\mathcal{M}\,,\quad\quad j=1,\dots,s\,.

Before discussing the proofs of Corollary
5
and Theorem
22
,
let us say a few words about the motivation for such questions.
Zaremba’s conjecture is connected with some problems of numerical integration.
First we state the fundamental result of 1961 due to Koksma and Hlawka (see
[
26
]
,
[
22
]
or
[
33
, Chap. 2]
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
f:[0,1]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function of bounded variation
\mathrm{V}(f)
and
X\subseteq[0,1]^{d}
be a finite set. Then

(6)

\left|\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f(u)\,du-\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}f(x)\right|\leqslant\mathrm{V}(f)\cdot\mathrm{Disc}(X)\,,

where

(7)

\mathrm{Disc}(X):=\sup_{R=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[a_{i},b_{i}]}\left|\frac{|X\cap R|}{|X|}-\mathrm{Vol}(R)\right|\,.

We call
\mathrm{Disc}(X)
in (
7
) the
discrepancy
of a sequence of points
X
. It measures the uniform distribution of
X
in the cube
[0,1]^{d}
and, in the light of (
6
), it determines the quality of the approximation of the integral of
f
by a finite sum over
X
. Thus, the problem reduces to finding sequences
X
whose discrepancy has as nice an upper bound as possible.

A remarkable theorem due to Schmidt
[
47
]
in the theory of irregularity of distributions gives a lower bound for the discrepancy of the distribution of an
arbitrary
finite sequence
X\subseteq[0,1]^{2}
, namely,
\mathrm{Disc}(X)\gg\frac{\log|X|}{|X|}
.
Thus, the discrepancy of any sequence cannot be too small.
The Monte Carlo method can be shown to provide only very weak estimates for discrepancy:

\frac{1}{|X|^{1/2+o(1)}}\ll\mathrm{Disc}(X)\ll\frac{1}{|X|^{1/2-o(1)}}
.
Therefore, there is a need for efficient construction of sequences
X
with small
\mathrm{Disc}(X)
.
We content ourselves with the two–dimensional case below.

In
[
53
]
Zaremba (and before that Korobov in
[
28
,
29
]
) proposed to consider a discrete winding of a two dimensional torus:

(8)

X=X(a,q)=\left\{\left(\frac{j}{q},\frac{aj}{q}\right)\right\}_{j=1}^{q}\subseteq[0,1]^{2}\,.

Here
a
and
q
are, as above, two coprime positive integers such that
0<a<q
. For
X(a,q)
the discrepancy can be estimated in terms of the continued fraction expansion of
a/q
(see
[
53
, Corollary 5.2]
).

Theorem 3
.

Let
a,q
be positive coprime integers,
\frac{a}{q}=[0;c_{1},\dots,c_{s}]

and let
M=M(a)
.
Then

(9)

\mathrm{Disc}(X(a,q))\leqslant\left(\frac{4M}{\log(M+1)}+\frac{4M+1}{\log q}\right)\frac{\log q}{q}\,.

It was later shown that the discrepancy in (
9
) can be estimated using a more flexible quantity, namely, the
sum
of the partial quotients

(10)

S(a):=\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}(a)\,,

see

[
34
]
and also
[
33
]
,
[
35
]
.
Moreover, in terms of the
sum

S(a)
, Larcher
[
34
]
found a series of further applications for evaluating the discrepancies of other sequences. For example, if
q
is a prime number and
g
is a primitive root modulo

, then the discrepancies

(11)

\mathrm{Disc}\left\{\frac{jg}{q}\right\}_{j=1}^{q}\,,\quad\quad\mathrm{Disc}\left\{\left(\frac{j}{q},\frac{gj}{q}\right)\right\}_{j=1}^{q}\,,\quad\quad\mathrm{Disc}\left\{\left(\frac{g^{j}}{q},\frac{g^{j+1}}{q}\right)\right\}_{j=1}^{q}

are all bounded by
S(g)/q
.
The corresponding conjecture on
S(a)
, namely,

(12)

\min_{a~:~(a,q)=1}\,S(a)=O(\log q)

is attributed to Moser (see
[
35
]
).
Of course, Zaremba’s conjecture implies (
12

The best known bound for
the minimum in (
12
)
belongs to Larcher, see
[
34
, Corollary 3]

Let

Then there is a positive integer

(a,q)=1

\frac{a}{q}=[0;c_{1},\dots,c_{s}]

\frac{1}{\log q}\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}\ll\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\cdot\log\log q\,.

Using some

The most important case when

Theorem 5
.

Let

be a

Then there is a positive integer

,
(a,q)=1

Also, if

Finally, if

In our first new result, we improve Theorem

By

In this section we present some notations and preliminary results, following mainly the paper

We

It says that, basically, the partial quotients of a rational number are controlled via the hyperbola

Lemma 9

Let

Consider the equation

ax\equiv y\pmod{q}\,,\quad\quad 1\leqslant x<q\,,\quad 1\leqslant|y|<q\,.

If for all solutions

of the equation above one has

, then

,

.
On the other hand, if for all

, then all solutions

of (

.

Remark 10
.

To formulate further results we need one more definition from the real setting.

On Ahlfors–David sets in the theory of continued fractions

In this section we recall some results concerning the structure of the set
Z_{M}(t)
, namely, we show that
Z_{M}(t)
is an Ahlfors–David set, see

[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
.
The material in this part of our paper is probably contained somewhere, but we could not find an exact reference, so we have included it for completeness and to help the reader to understand better the fractal structure of the set
Z_{M}(t)
.
Recall that a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
is an
Ahlfors–David set (
N
—Ahlfors–David set) of dimension
w\in[0,1)
if
A=I\dotplus\Lambda
, where
I=[N]
is an interval,
\Lambda
is an arbitrary set and there are absolute constants
C_{1},C_{2}\geqslant 1
such that for any interval
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
the following holds

(51)

|A\cap\mathcal{I}|\leqslant C_{1}|\mathcal{I}|^{w}N^{1-w}\,,

and for any interval
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
,
|\mathcal{I}|\geqslant N
with the center at the origin and
a\in A

one has

(52)

|A\cap(\mathcal{I}+a)|\geqslant C_{2}^{-1}|\mathcal{I}|^{w}N^{1-w}\,.

Put
t:=q^{1/2-\varepsilon}
and let
N:=q^{2\varepsilon}
, so

t=\sqrt{q/N}
.
In this section we consider
Z_{M}(t)
as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
.
We are now ready to show that
Z_{M}(t)
is an Ahlfors–David set in the sense given above.

Lemma 20
.

Let
M\geqslant 2
be an integer,
t\gg M
be a real number, and
let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
be an arbitrary interval.
Then

(53)

|Z_{M}(t)\cap\mathcal{I}|\ll M^{4}|\mathcal{I}|^{w_{M}}N^{1-w_{M}}\,.

Similarly, if
|\mathcal{I}|\geqslant N

and the center of
\mathcal{I}
belongs to
Z_{M}(t)
, then

(54)

|Z_{M}(t)\cap\mathcal{I}|\gg M^{-1}|\mathcal{I}|^{w_{M}}N^{1-w_{M}}\,.

P r o o f.

Let
\Delta=|\mathcal{I}|\leqslant q
.
Without loosing of the generality we can assume in the first case that
\Delta\geqslant N
(and even
\Delta\gg M^{4/(1-w_{M})}N\gg M^{4}N
, provided
\Delta\leqslant q
) and that the center
a
of
\mathcal{I}
belongs to
Z_{M}(t)
. It allows us to obtain formulae (
53
)
and
(
54
)
simultaneously.

Recall that if we consider the set
Z_{M}(t)
as the set of rationals, then

Z_{M}(t)=Q_{M}(t)\cap R_{q}
.
Choose
t^{2}_{*}=16(M+1)q/\Delta<t^{2}
and find the unique fraction
u_{*}/v_{*}\in\overline{Q_{M}(t_{*})}
such that
u_{*}/v_{*}
is a convergent or a semiconvergent to
a/q
, see
[
39
, Lemma 1]
.
Let us show that

(55)

J_{u_{*}/v_{*}}\cap Q_{M}(t)\cap R_{q}\subseteq Z_{M}(t)\cap\mathcal{I}\,.

Indeed, by the definition of the set
\overline{Q_{M}(t_{*})}
one has
t_{*}/2\leqslant v_{*}<t_{*}
and since
u_{*}/v_{*}
is a convergent or a semiconvergent to
a/q
, it follows for any
u/v\in J_{u_{*}/v_{*}}
that (see formula (
30
))

\left|\frac{a}{q}-\frac{u}{v}\right|\leqslant|J_{u_{*}/v_{*}}|\leqslant\frac{2(M+1)}{v^{2}_{*}}\leqslant\frac{8(M+1)}{t^{2}_{*}}\leqslant\frac{\Delta}{2q}

and (
55
) follows.
Conversely, put
(\tilde{t})^{2}=q/((M+2)\Delta)<t_{*}^{2}

and
find the unique fraction
\alpha=\tilde{u}/\tilde{v}=p_{l}(\alpha)/q_{l}(\alpha)\in\overline{Q_{M}(\tilde{t})}
such that
\tilde{u}/\tilde{v}
is a convergent or a semiconvergent to
a/q
.
Let us show that

(56)

Z_{M}(t)\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subseteq J_{\tilde{u}/\tilde{v}}\cap Q_{M}(t)\cap R_{q}\,,

where
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}

either left or right
half
of the interval
\mathcal{I}
.
Suppose that there exists
b/q\in Z_{M}(t)\cap\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
such that
b/q\notin J_{\tilde{u}/\tilde{v}}
.
It means that
b/q
is not a convergent or a semiconvergent to
a/q
.
Hence

(57)

\frac{1}{2(M+2)(\tilde{t})^{2}}<\frac{1}{2q_{l}(\alpha)(q_{l}(\alpha)+q_{l+1}(\alpha))}\leqslant\left|\frac{a}{q}-\frac{b}{q}\right|\leqslant\frac{\Delta}{2q}

and this is a contradiction.
So, we have covered a half of the interval
\mathcal{I}
.
Take the nearest point
a_{2}/q
in the uncovered part of
Z_{M}(t)\cap\mathcal{I}
(if it exists) and apply the previous argument with
a_{2}/q
.
We find another interval
J_{\tilde{u}_{2}/\tilde{v}_{2}}
such that
\tilde{u}_{2}/\tilde{v}_{2}
is a convergent or a semiconvergent to
a_{2}/q
.
It remains to recall that

J_{\tilde{u}/\tilde{v}}\cap J_{\tilde{u}_{2}/\tilde{v}_{2}}=\emptyset
and then computations in (
57
) show that the union of these two intervals cover the whole set
Z_{M}(t)\cap\mathcal{I}
.

Now our task is
to count the number of elements of
R_{q}

in the sets (
55
), (
56
).
For example, consider
J_{u_{*}/v_{*}}
,
\frac{u_{*}}{v_{*}}=[0;r_{1},\dots,r_{l}]=\frac{p_{l}}{q_{l}}

and then we need to find the number of fractions
u/v\in Q_{M}(t)
which starts with
r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1}
in the continued fraction expansion, see formula (
29
).
Let

\frac{u}{v}=[0;r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1},c_{l},\dots,c_{s}]\in Q_{M}(t)\,.

We have
t_{*}>\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1})\geqslant\frac{t_{*}}{2(M+1)}
.
Using (
31
) it follows that

(58)

\frac{\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1},c_{l},\dots,c_{s})}{2\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1})}\leqslant\mathcal{K}(c_{l},\dots,c_{s})\leqslant\frac{2(M+1)t}{t_{*}}

and hence, in particular, the ratio
\mathcal{K}(c_{l+1},\dots,c_{s})/\mathcal{K}(c_{l},\dots,c_{s})
belongs to the set
Q_{M}(2(M+1)t/t_{*})
.
On the other hand, by Lemma
12

we know that
the
intervals of
Z_{M}(t)
(we know that all of them have lengths at least
N
) indexed by the
continuants
\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1},c_{l},\dots,c_{s})
.
Similarly, take continuants

\mathcal{K}(c_{l},\dots,c_{s})

such that the ratio

\mathcal{K}(c_{l+1},\dots,c_{s})/\mathcal{K}(c_{l},\dots,c_{s})

belongs to the set

\overline{Q_{M}(\epsilon t/t_{*})}\subseteq Q_{M}(\epsilon t/t_{*})
, where
\epsilon>0
is a constant.
By the first inequality in (
58
)
the later set belongs to (
55
) for a sufficiently small constant
\epsilon
.
Thus by Lemma
12
and by the definition of the number
t_{*}
we have at least

\left(\frac{\epsilon t}{t_{*}}\right)^{2w_{M}}N\gg M^{-1}\Delta^{w_{M}}N^{1-w_{M}}

fractions
in (
55
).
Thus we have
proved
(
54
).
The upper bound (
53
) can be obtained in a similar way, in which case we work with the set

Q_{M}(2(M+1)t/\tilde{t})
.
This completes the proof.

4.
On critical denominators of elements from
Z_{M}(t)\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t)

Put
t:=q^{1/2-\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, and let
N:=q^{2\varepsilon}
, so

t=\sqrt{q/N}
.
Sometimes we consider the set
Z_{M}(t)
as a subset of

\mathbb{Z}
.
Notice right now that conditions (
61
), (
75
), (
99
) below give us an
upper
bound for the parameter
\varepsilon
.
In other words, even at this stage of the proof one has to deal with rather
short
intervals where classical analytical methods do not work.

In this section we study the denominators

(59)

x=x(a)=x_{s}(a)=\mathcal{K}(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{s})\in[t,q/t]

of some convergents to
a/q
, where

a\in Z_{M}(t)\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t)
,
which we call
critical denominators
.
Given an
a\in Z_{M}(t)\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t)
one can has several of such
x(a)
and the collection of them is denoted by
\mathcal{X}(a)
.
Similarly, given a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
we put
\mathcal{X}(S)=\bigcup_{s\in S}\mathcal{X}(s)
.
We mostly study critical denominators
x
with the additional
constraint

x\leqslant\sqrt{q}
, which we call
critical denominators of Type I
, and if
x>\sqrt{q}
, then we say that
x
has
Type II
.
Below we will see (consult Section
5
) that critical denominators of Type II are easily transformed into Type I due to the symmetry of our problem.
In a similar way a critical denominator is called
\tilde{M}
–critical denominator
if in addition
x|ax|\leqslant q/\tilde{M}
.
Notice that
if there are no critical denominators, then by Lemma
9
all partial quotients of
a
are bounded by
\max\{M,\tilde{M}\}
.
So, if the reader does not interested in obtaining correct number of
a
in Theorems
17
,
7
, then it is possible to assume

for
any

a\in Z_{M}(t)\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t)
there exists
\tilde{M}
–critical denominator
x(a)

(with a suitable
\tilde{M}
).
To obtain a lower bound for the number of such
a
we make the following stronger assumption.

Assumption 21
.

Let
I\subseteq Z_{M}(t)
be a
N^{1/100}
–good interval. Split
Z_{M}(t)
into subintervals of length
N^{1/100}
and let
\delta\in[1/2,1]
be a real parameter.
We say that

I
satisfies
\delta
–Assumption if

\delta
–proportion
of such subintervals contain a point
a\in I\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t)
such that there exists
\tilde{M}
–critical denominator
x(a)
.
Furthermore, we assume that condition (
50
) holds for this
a
with
c_{*}=1/1000
, say.

If Assumption
21
violates for a half of intervals of
Z_{M}(t)
with
\delta=1/2
, say, than we obtain Theorems
17
,
7
,
22
immediately (if
M
is any number equal to
o(\sqrt{\log q})
, then we actually obtain
a huge set of

a
in Theorem
22
, namely, the number of these
a
exceeds the expectation).
Indeed, let
\tilde{Z}_{M}(t)
be a collection of subintervals of
Z_{M}(t)
having no
a
with a
\tilde{M}
–critical denominator
x(a)
,
|\tilde{Z}_{M}(t)|\geqslant(1-\delta)|Z_{M}(t)|/2=|Z_{M}(t)|/4
.
Than thanks to Corollary
34
(or see the beginning of Section
5
) there is an asymptotic formula for the cardinality of the set
\tilde{Z}_{M}(t)\cap\tilde{Z}^{-1}_{M}(t)
and all partial quotients of an arbitrary
a\in\tilde{Z}_{M}(t)\cap\tilde{Z}^{-1}_{M}(t)
are bounded by
\max\{M,\tilde{M}\}
.
Similarly, if the reader is not interested in the case of composite
q
, then the second part of Assumption
21
holds trivially and can therefore be ignored.

Let us pay attention right now that
the set
Z_{M}(t)\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t)
has some special properties and therefore the corresponding collection of critical denominators
\mathcal{X}(Z_{M}(t)\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t))
is rather specific.
For example, it is easy to see (or consult Section
5
) that
elements of
Z_{M}(t)\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t)

have the following simple repulsion property.
For simplicity, we consider here the case of a prime number

.

Proposition 22
.

Let
q
be a prime number and
a\in Z_{M}(t)\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t)
.
Then for
any

1\leqslant x\leqslant q/(4Mt)
one has
|ax|\geqslant t
.

P r o o f.

If
|ax|<t
, then
x|ax|<q/4M
.
But then we know that both
x,|ax|\in[t,q/(4Mt)]
(or see formulae (
114
), (
115
)) and this is a contradiction.

4.1.
Independency of critical denominators

The main observation of this section is that the elements of an interval
J_{u/v}
are quite close to each other (both in the Euclidean sense and in the continued fraction expansion sense), and hence the behavior of the various
x(a_{k})
, where
a_{k}\in J_{u/v}
, resembles the behavior of the denominators of
x(a)
for a single
a\in J_{u/v}
.
In particular, it can be shown that the set of critical denominators
x(a)
,
a\in J_{u/v}
is quite sparse and even “independent” in
the sense that we are ready to define.

Definition 23
.

Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\geqslant 1
be some parameters and
\vec{C}:=(C_{1},\dots,C_{k})
. We say that elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
are
\vec{C}
–independent if any equation

\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{k}x_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{q}\,,\quad\quad\mbox{where}\quad\quad\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{Z},\,\quad|\alpha_{j}|\leqslant C_{j}

implies that
\alpha_{1}=\dots=\alpha_{k}=0
.

For example, in the next lemma we study
(C,C)
–independency of two critical denominators.

Lemma 24
.

Let
M\geqslant 2
,
1\leqslant C<t/4
,
a_{1},a_{2}\in J_{u/v}\subseteq Z_{M}(t)
,
u/v=[0;r_{1},\dots,r_{l}]

and

t<x_{1}\neq x_{2}<q/t

be denominators of some convergents to
a_{1}/q,a_{2}/q
.
Then there are no
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\alpha\beta\neq 0
,
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leqslant C

such that

(60)

\alpha x_{1}\equiv\beta x_{2}\pmod{q}\,,

provided

(61)

N^{3/2}\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{q}}{2(M+2)^{2}C}\,.

P r o o f.

Suppose that (
60
) takes place.
The assumption
C<4t
implies that actually this equation takes place in
\mathbb{Z}
.
By (
29
)
we have

\frac{a_{1}}{q}=[0;r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1},r^{(1)}_{l},\dots,r^{(1)}_{s_{1}}]\,,\quad\quad\frac{a_{2}}{q}=[0;r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1},r^{(2)}_{l},\dots,r^{(2)}_{s_{2}}]\,.

Let
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1})
and
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-2})
.
Later we will show that
l>2
, so
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}>0
is correctly defined.
Now thanks to the definition of the interval
J_{u/v}
, as well as formulae (
29
), (
31
) one has

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(M+2)\leqslant\mathcal{K}(M+1)+\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\leqslant t

and

t\leqslant\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})<(M+2)\mathcal{K}<(M+1)(M+2)\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\,.

Therefore, in view of the inequality
t\geqslant 8M^{2}
, which follows from (
61
) (of course, one can assume that
M
is sufficiently small in terms of
q
), we derive

(62)

\frac{t}{M+2}<\mathcal{K}<\frac{t}{M+1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad 1<\frac{t}{(M+1)(M+2)}<\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\leqslant\frac{t}{M+2}\,.

In particular,
l>2
.
Using formula (
31
) again, we get

(63)

x_{1}=c_{1}\mathcal{K}+\widehat{c}_{1}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad x_{2}=c_{2}\mathcal{K}+\widehat{c}_{2}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\,,

where
c_{1}\geqslant x_{1}/(2\mathcal{K})>1
,
c_{2}\geqslant x_{2}/(2\mathcal{K})>1
, thanks to the bounds
\mathcal{K}<\frac{t}{M+1}
and
x_{1},x_{2}>t
.
Similarly, from (
63
), we see that

(64)

1\leqslant\widehat{c}_{1}\leqslant c_{1}<x_{1}/\mathcal{K}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad 1\leqslant\widehat{c}_{2}\leqslant c_{2}<x_{2}/\mathcal{K}\,.

Here
c_{1},\widehat{c}_{1}
depend on
x_{1}
and
c_{2},\widehat{c}_{2}
depend on
x_{2}
.
Later we will use
stronger
lower bounds for
\widehat{c}_{1},\widehat{c}_{2}
.
Namely, one has
c_{1}\leqslant(M+1)\widehat{c}_{1}
,
c_{2}\leqslant(M+1)\widehat{c}_{2}
and therefore

(65)

\widehat{c}_{1}\geqslant\frac{x_{1}}{2(M+1)\mathcal{K}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\widehat{c}_{2}\geqslant\frac{x_{2}}{2(M+1)\mathcal{K}}\,.

Finally, substituting (
63
) into (
60
), we obtain

(\alpha c_{1}-\beta c_{2})\mathcal{K}=(\beta\widehat{c}_{2}-\alpha\widehat{c}_{1})\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\,.

We know that
\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
are coprime numbers and therefore
\mathcal{K}
divides
\beta\widehat{c}_{2}-\alpha\widehat{c}_{1}
.
But
using (
64
)
and our assumptions on
|\alpha|,|\beta|
, we see that the last number does not exceed

(66)

\frac{2Cx_{1}}{\mathcal{K}}\leqslant\frac{2Cq}{t\mathcal{K}}<\mathcal{K}

thanks to the bound
\mathcal{K}>\frac{t}{M+2}
and the condition
(
61
).
Hence
\beta\widehat{c}_{2}=\alpha\widehat{c}_{1}
and
\alpha c_{1}=\beta c_{2}
.
It follows that
c_{1}\widehat{c}_{2}=c_{2}\widehat{c}_{1}
.
We know from the formula (
31
) that
c_{1},c_{2},\widehat{c}_{1},\widehat{c}_{2}
are some continuants defined by
continuants
x_{1},x_{2}
.
Hence it is easy to see that
(c_{1},\widehat{c}_{1})=(c_{2},\widehat{c}_{2})=1
.
Thus the identity
c_{1}\widehat{c}_{2}=c_{2}\widehat{c}_{1}
gives us

c_{2}=kc_{1}
and
\widehat{c}_{2}=k\widehat{c}_{1}
for some integer
k
.
But then system (
63
) implies that
x_{2}=kx_{1}
, thus
x_{1}
divides
x_{2}
, and, similarly,
x_{2}
divides
x_{1}
.
Thus
x_{1}=x_{2}
and therefore relation (
60
) is impossible.
This concludes the proof.

Remark 25
.

The same argument shows that
any two critical denominators
x_{1}=x_{1}(a_{1})
,
x_{2}=x_{2}(a_{2})
are almost coprime, that is,

g:=(x_{1},x_{2})<4(M+2)^{2}N^{2}
.
Indeed, let
x_{1}=g\tilde{\alpha}
,
x_{2}=g\tilde{\beta}
, where
(\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\beta})=1
.
Then
\tilde{\beta}x_{1}=\tilde{\alpha}x_{2}
and
we have that either
|\tilde{\alpha}|
or
|\tilde{\beta}|
is at least
C
, where
C>\mathcal{K}^{2}/2x_{1}
, see computations in (
66
).
It follows that

g=(x_{1},x_{2})\leqslant x_{1}/C\leqslant 4x^{2}_{1}/\mathcal{K}^{2}<4(M+2)^{2}x^{2}_{1}/t^{2}\leqslant 4(M+2)^{2}N^{2}\,.

Thus, the collection of critical denominators is not just sparse or “independent” in the sense of Lemmas
61
,
78
,
but they are also almost coprime to each other. This is reminiscent of the behavior of the denominators of
x(a)
for a fixed fraction
a/q
.

Now we study

\vec{C}
–independency
between three critical denominators
from an interval
J_{u/v}
.
There is no need for independence at a deeper level, as this would only improve some absolute constants below.
Let us remark that in contrast to Lemma
61
for a giving
a\in Z_{M}(t)

there are
such linear dependencies between its continuants, e.g.,
q_{n}(a)=Aq_{m}(a)+Bq_{m-1}(a)
and, moreover,

q_{n}(a)=Cq_{m_{1}}(a)+Dq_{m_{2}}(a)
, where
n>m
,
n>m_{1}>m_{2}
, see formula (
31
).
Besides
the coefficients
A,B,C,D
can be
rather
small even if we perturb
a
a little bit.
Finally, there is a general formula (
72
) of the cross–ratio–type which connects
q_{n}(a)
for distinct
a
(but coefficients in this formula are
rather
large).
So, it is important to choose
q_{n_{1}}(a),q_{n_{2}}(b),q_{n_{3}}(c)
for
distinct

and specific

a,b,c
and we will take
elements

a,b,c\in Z_{M}(t)
to be rather “far” from each other
in the sense that we are ready to define.

In our study of
\vec{C}
–independency between three critical denominators we use the basic
quantity

d(x,y)
.
Namely, let
x=x(a)=\mathcal{K}(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{s})
be the denominator of a convergent to
a/q
and
y=y(b)=\mathcal{K}(r^{\prime}_{1},r^{\prime}_{2},\dots,r^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
be the denominator of a convergent to
b/q
.
Then we put

(67)

\widehat{x}=\mathcal{K}(r_{2},\dots,r_{s})\,,\quad\quad\widehat{y}=\mathcal{K}(r^{\prime}_{2},\dots,r^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})\,,\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad d(x,y):=x\widehat{y}-y\widehat{x}\,.

Recall that there are two natural ways how to measure the distance between
x(a)
and
y(b)
.
The first one is the Euclidean distance between
a
and
b
(or, equivalently, between
a/q
and
b/q
).
The second way is a non–archimedean: we say that
x=\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1},r_{l},\dots,r_{s})
is close to
y=\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1},r^{\prime}_{l},\dots,r_{s^{\prime}})
if
l
is large.
In other words,
x,y
are close if they belong to the same fundamental interval defined by
r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1}
or, equivalently, the same cylinder defined by the initial digits
r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1}
.
Therefore,
the modulus of the
wedge product

|d(x,y)|
in (
67
) can be considered as a version of this non–archimedean distance.
The following instructive Lemma
70
below reflects this intuition.
For example, formula (
69
) is a version of the well–known fact that if two fractions are close in the non–archimedean sense, then they are automatically close in the Euclidean sense.

Lemma 26
.

Let
s,s^{\prime},l
be positive integers and

x=x(a)=\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1},r_{l},\dots,r_{s})\,,\quad y=y(b)=\mathcal{K}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l-1},r^{\prime}_{l},\dots,r^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})

be
\tilde{M}
–critical denominators
x,y
.
Then

(68)

\left|\frac{|a-b|}{q}-\frac{|d(x,y)|}{xy}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{\tilde{M}x^{2}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{M}y^{2}}\,.

Also, for any
x,y
, assuming
r_{l}\neq r^{\prime}_{l}
and
s,s^{\prime}\geqslant l+1

one has

(69)

\frac{a^{-1}}{q}=\left[0;c_{s},c_{s-1}\ldots,c_{1}\right]\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{if }s\text{ is even }

\frac{a^{-1}}{q}=\left[0;1,c_{s}-1,c_{s-1}\ldots,c_{1}\right]\quad\text{ if }s\text{ is odd. }

Thus
in view of formulae (

113
) for any

and
1\leqslant|y|<q

.
The last modular equation is equivalent to

and hence any solution of (
23
)
satisfy

x|y|=x|ax|\geqslant q_{M}\quad\quad\mbox{for}\quad\quad x\in[t]\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\quad x\in\left[q_{M}/t,q\right)\,.

Now suppose that there are

.
Recall that we call such denominators

as

.
Notice that since

that either

less than

, see formula (

.
After that we chose

in
[

, but at the same time,

(for details, see
[

]
).

Finally, let us give a rough upper bound for the partial quotients of elements of
Z_{*}
, which is, firstly, very instructive, and secondly, can in principle be used in an alternative proof of Proposition

, replacing the argument with the quantity
M_{*}
.
For simplicity, assume that

is a prime number.
Suppose that

and then putting

in (

|x||ax|=|ax||a^{-1}(ax)|\geqslant q_{M}\quad\quad\mbox{for}\quad\quad|ax|\in[t]\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\quad|ax|\in\left[q_{M}/t,q\right)\,.

, then

\pm[\Delta_{2},2\Delta_{2}]/[\Delta_{1},2\Delta_{1}]
and hence

Thus thanks to our main condition (

of course), we get

Therefore, the number of such elements

in

In other words,

(117)

Thus, in particular, we see that our set
Z_{M}(t)\cap Z^{-1}_{M}(t)
is far from being random (we have already seen this in Proposition

Remark 33

If one is interested in obtaining optimal bounds for the Zaremba numbers

, then

that the limit of our method is

and hence the upper bound in (

For simplicity, let us assume that

In the notation above

(also, see estimate (
