Fields with no everywhere good abelian varieties
Armand Brumer, Kenneth Kramer

Abstract
. We extend methods of Fontaine, Abrashkin and Schoof to obtain criteria determining number fields K over which no non-zero abelian variety with everywhere good reduction exists. As an application, under the GRH, we find 24744 such fields of various degrees up to 16.

Assume first that
F/E
is a Galois extension of
p
-adic fields with Galois group
G
. Using the lower numbering of
[
Ser
, Ch. IV]
, let
G_{j}
run over the ramification subgroups of
G
and let
\varphi_{F/E}
be the Herbrand function:

\varphi_{F/E}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\frac{dt}{[{G_{0}}\!:\!{G_{t}}]}.

The upper numbering is given by
G^{\alpha}=G_{j}
with
\alpha=\varphi_{F/E}(j)
. Let

(2.1)

c_{F/E}=\max\{j\,|\,G_{j}\neq 1\}\quad\text{ and }\quad m_{F/E}=\varphi_{F/E}(c_{F/E}),

with
c_{F/E}=m_{F/E}=-1
when
F/E
is unramified. If
m=m_{F/E}\geq 0
, then
G^{m}\neq 1
but
G^{m+\epsilon}=1
for all
\epsilon>0
. For a tower of fields
L\supseteq F\supseteq E
with
L/E
and
F/E
Galois, we have
m_{F/E}\leq m_{L/E}
because of the surjection

\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)^{\alpha}\xrightarrow{\rm res}\operatorname{Gal}(F/E)^{\alpha}\quad\text{ for all }\alpha.

Let
{\mathfrak{f}}={\mathfrak{f}}(F/E)
be the conductor exponent for an abelian extension
F/E
. Equivalently, the ray class modulus for
F/E
is
{\mathfrak{p}}^{{\mathfrak{f}}}
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}
is the prime of
E
. According to
[
Ser
, XV, §2]
,

{\mathfrak{f}}(F/E)=m_{F/E}+1,

with
{\mathfrak{f}}(F/E)=0
when
F/E
is unramified. Translation by an unramified extension of the base does not affect the conductor. We state the bound on higher ramification using Serre’s notation: Fontaine’s numbering is larger by 1.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
Fo
, Théorème A]
.
Let
V
be a finite flat group scheme of exponent
p^{n}
over the ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
and let
e
be the ramification index of
E/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. If
F
is contained in the field of points
E(V)
, then

m_{F/E}\leq e\left(n+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)-1.

In our applications,
p=2
,
e=1

n=1
, so
m_{F/E}\leq 1
. In particular, if
F/E
is abelian then
{\mathfrak{f}}(F/E)\leq 2
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E/{\mathbb{Q}}_{2}
be unramified and let
L/E
be a Galois extension with Galois group
G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)
and trivial tame ramification.

i)

Then
m_{L/E}\leq 1
if and only if
G_{2}=\{1\}
. If so, the inertia group
{\mathcal{I}}(L/E)=G_{0}=G_{1}
is an elementary
2
-group.

ii)

Suppose that
L\supset F\supset E
, with
F/E
also Galois. Then
m_{L/E}\leq 1
if and only if both
m_{L/F}\leq 1
and
m_{F/E}\leq 1
.

Proof.

[
BK1
, Lemma 6]
.
It suffices to assume that
L/E
has some wild ramification. Suppose that
m_{L/E}\leq 1
. Let
g_{j}
be the order of the
j
-th non-trivial ramification group
G_{j}
for
0\leq j\leq c
, with
G_{c+1}=\{1\}
. Since tame ramification is trivial,
g_{0}=g_{1}
. If
c\geq 2
, the Herbrand function gives a contradiction:

m=\varphi_{L/E}(c)\geq\frac{1}{g_{0}}(g_{1}+g_{2})=1+\frac{g_{2}}{g_{1}}>1,

Thus
m=c=1
and
G_{2}=\{1\}
. Then
G_{1}
is an elementary
2
-group, since that holds for successive quotients in the ramificaton filtration. For the converse,
c_{L/E}=1
, so
m_{L/E}=\varphi_{L/E}(1)=1
and (i) is proved.

For item (ii), let
H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)
and
\overline{G}=\operatorname{Gal}(F/E)
. For
\alpha\geq 0
, let
\beta=\varphi_{L/E}(\alpha)
, so that
G^{\beta}=G_{\alpha}
. Since the upper ramification numbering is compatible with quotients and
H_{\alpha}=H\cap G_{\alpha}
, the sequence

1\to H_{\alpha}\to G^{\beta}\to\overline{G}^{\,\beta}\to 1

is exact. If
m_{L/E}\leq 1
, then
G^{\beta}=\{1\}
for all
\beta>1
. The same therefore holds for
\overline{G}^{\,\beta}
and so
m_{F/E}\leq 1
. Also,
G_{2}=\{1\}
by (i), so
H_{2}=\{1\}
and thus
m_{L/F}\leq 1
. Conversely, given
m_{F/E}\leq 1
, we have
\overline{G}^{\,\beta}=\{1\}
for all
\beta>1
. Also given
m_{L/F}\leq 1
, we have
H_{2}=\{1\}
. Hence
G^{\beta}=\{1\}
.
∎

3.
Some group theory

Here we provide group theoretic tools to study the maximal solvable extension of
K
inside
{\mathcal{F}}
. We write
G^{\prime}
for the commutator subgroup of a group
G
and
G^{\prime\prime}=(G^{\prime})^{\prime}
. Then
G^{ab}=G/G^{\prime}
is the abelianization of
G
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group with the following properties.

i)

\Gamma^{\prime}
is abelian and an odd prime
p
divides
|{\Gamma^{\prime}}|
.

ii)

\Gamma^{ab}
is an elementary
2
-group.

Then
\Gamma
has a quotient isomorphic to the dihedral group
D_{p}
.

Proof.

Replace
\Gamma
by
G=\Gamma/(\Gamma^{\prime})^{p}
. Then
G^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}/(\Gamma^{\prime})^{p}
is an elementary abelian
p
-group and
G^{ab}\simeq\Gamma^{ab}
is an elementary 2-group. It suffices to show that
G
has a
D_{p}
-quotient.

If
H
is any 2-Sylow subgroup of
G
, then
G=G^{\prime}\rtimes H
is a semidirect product of
H
by the normal subgroup
G^{\prime}
. As an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[H]
-module,
G^{\prime}=\oplus W_{j}
is a direct sum of 1-dimensional eigenspaces for
H
. Since
G
is not abelian, there is at least one such eigenspace, say
W_{1}=\langle{b}\rangle\simeq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
on which some involution
a
in
H
acts by inversion. Since
G
acts on
G^{\prime}
via
G/G^{\prime}\simeq H
, the centralizer
H_{0}=\{h\in H\mid hbh^{-1}=b\}
of
b
in
H
is a normal subgroup of
G
and
H/H_{0}\simeq\langle{a}\rangle
. Also,
V=\oplus_{j\geq 2}W_{j}
is a normal subgroup of
G
with
G^{\prime}/V\simeq\langle{b}\rangle
and thus
G/H_{0}V\simeq\langle{a,b}\rangle\simeq D_{p}
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group with the following properties.

i)

\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
is abelian and
|{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}|
is divisible by an odd prime
p
.

ii)

G=\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
is a
2
-group with a subgroup
H
of index
2
and exponent
2
.

Let
\Gamma_{0}
be the inverse image of
H
under the projection map
\Gamma\to G
. Then
\Gamma_{0}
has a
D_{p}
-quotient.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
projects trivially to
G
, it is contained in
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
is isomorphic to
H
. But
H
is abelian, so
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}
is contained in
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
. For the reverse inclusion, note that
\Gamma_{0}
is normal in
\Gamma
and

\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}\simeq(\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime\prime})/(\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma^{\prime\prime})\simeq G/H\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}.

Since this quotient is abelian,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is contained in
\Gamma_{0}
. Hence
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
is contained in and therefore equal to
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}
. Thus
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}
is abelian and
p
divides
|{\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}}|
. In addition

\Gamma_{0}^{ab}=\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}=\Gamma_{0}/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\simeq H

is an elementary 2-group. By Proposition
3.1
,
\Gamma_{0}
has a
D_{p}
-quotient.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian
2
-group. The following are equivalent:

i)

G
contains a subgroup
H
of index
2
and exponent
2
.

ii)

G\simeq H_{1}\times W
, where
H_{1}\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{e}
for some
e\geq 0
and
W
is the wreath product
({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{s}\wr{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
with
s\geq 1
.

Under these conditions,
G
has a dihedral quotient
D_{4}\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})\wr{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Assume (i). Since
G/H\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
is abelian,
G^{\prime}
is contained in
H
and so
G^{\prime}
is elementary abelian. Let
s\geq 1
be the rank
G^{\prime}
. Because
G
is not abelian, it contains an element
b
of order 4. Then
b^{2}
is in
H
and
G=\langle{H,b}\rangle
. The quotient
G/H
acts on
H
via conjugation by
b
. As an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-vector space for this action,
H
decomposes into Jordon blocks (written additively):

H=\left(\oplus_{j=1}^{d}\,Y_{j}\right)\oplus\left(\oplus_{i=1}^{e}\,X_{i}\right)

where
\dim Y_{j}=2
,
\dim X_{i}=1
and so
\dim H=2d+e
.

For
1\leq j\leq d
, we can find
a_{j}
such that
Y_{j}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}[G/H]\,a_{j}
and so
Y_{j}=\langle{a_{j},a_{d+j}}\rangle
with
a_{d+j}=ba_{j}b^{-1}
. Let
A=\langle{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}}\rangle
,
\widetilde{A}=\langle{a_{d+1},\dots,a_{2d}}\rangle
and
H_{1}=\oplus_{i=1}^{e}\,X_{i}
. Then
H
is an internal direct product

H=H_{1}\times A\times\widetilde{A}\,\text{ and }\,G=\langle{H_{1},A,b}\rangle.

It follows that an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-basis for
G^{\prime}
is given by the commutators
[a_{j},b]=a_{j}a_{d+j}
Hence
d=s=\operatorname{rank}G^{\prime}
and
\operatorname{rank}H_{1}=e
.

The action of
G/H
interchanges
A
and
\widetilde{A}
. If
W=\langle{A,b}\rangle
, then
W
is a normal subgroup of
G
isomorphic to
({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{s}\!\wr\!({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})
and
|{W}|=2|{A}|^{2}=2^{2s+1}
. Also,
G=\langle{H_{1},A,b}\rangle=H_{1}W
. This is an internal direct product because

\begin{array}[]{rcl}2^{2s+e+1}&=&2|{H}|=|{G}|=|{H_{1}W}|=|{H_{1}}||{W}|/|{H_{1}\cap W}|\\[4.0pt]
&=&2^{2s+e+1}/|{H_{1}\cap W}|\end{array}

and so
H_{1}\cap W=\{1\}
. It follows that (i) implies (ii).

Assume (ii). By construction of the wreath product, there are subgroups
A
and
\widetilde{A}
of
W
isomorphic to
({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{s}
and interchanged by the action of
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
A\times\widetilde{A}
is an elementary
2
-group of index 2 in
W
and so
H=H_{1}\times A\times\widetilde{A}
satisfies (i).

If
B=\langle{a_{2},\dots,a_{d}}\rangle
, then
G/(H_{1}\times B\times bBb^{-1})
is isomorphic to
D_{4}
.
∎
