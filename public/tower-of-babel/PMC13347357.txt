Reconstruction of Tectonic Stress Magnitude of the Yanshanian Period in the Pingdingshan Mining Area by Using the Microstructural Estimation Method
Authors: Xiaosheng Chuai, Longyong Shu, Zhonggang Huo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To investigate the tectonic deformation mechanism and quantitative characteristics of the paleo-tectonic stress field in the Pingdingshan mining area, based on the theoretical method of tectonophysics and rock mechanics, the macroscopic, microscopic, and ultra-microscopic tectonic deformation characteristics and mechanisms were studied with the observation data of the tectonic deformation. The evolution periods of the paleo-tectonic stress field were obtained, and the main diagenetic period of tectonite was determined in the mining area. The magnitudes of paleo-tectonic stress were quantitatively calculated in the Late Yanshanian period of the mining area. The results showed that the macro-fractures of the Permian–Carboniferous sandstone formation were mainly tectonic fractures and some fractures were diagenetic fractures. The types of tectonic fractures included the layer-controlled tensile fractures, the cross-cutting shear fractures, and the bedding-parallel shear fractures. The types of microscopic fractures included tensile fractures, shear fractures, compressive-shear fractures, and tenso-shear fractures in the research area. The ultra-microscopic tectonic types of tectonites included the dislocation lines and dislocation walls. And the intracrystalline plastic deformation mechanism of tectonites was dominated by the dislocation glide and dislocation climb. The Pingdingshan mining area mainly got through three periods of tectonic stress field movement, including the Indosinian period, Yanshanian period, and Himalayan period. The direction of the maximum principal stress had experienced a transformation process of NS → NW → NE → NNE. Under a multistage tectonic stress field, the mining area formed a geological structure on a series of NWW–NW direction folds and faults with NNE–NE direction secondary faults under the control of the Likou syncline. The main diagenetic period of tectonites was the Late Yanshanian period in the mining area. Based on the lattice dislocation density measurement method, the ultra-microscopic structure of the secondary quartz enlarged edge on tectonite samples was tested in the diagenetic period. The magnitude of paleo-tectonic stress was obtained in the Late Yanshanian period, and the values were 40.70–62.50 MPa. The result accuracy was verified by the subparticle size method. And the closer the Likou synclinal axis was, the greater the paleo-tectonic stress was. Research results provide the theoretical basis for quantitative analysis of the paleo-tectonic stress field in the Pingdingshan mining area.

Introduction

There are three aspects of research content on paleo-tectonic stress, including the timing, directional, and quantitative characteristics of the stress field. On the timing characteristics study of stress the field, on one hand, the precedence order of each period stress field was determined based on the cutting, limitation, and superposition relation of various tectonic deformation characteristics. − On the other hand, the absolute formation ages of each period tectonic stress field was determined by using geochemistry isotopic dating and other testing approaches. , On the directional characteristics study of paleo-tectonic stress, the direction of paleo-tectonic stress can be obtained based on the various structural features, such as conjugated shear joint, filling vein, small fold, fracture surface, coal vitrinite reflectance, and anisotropy. − On the quantitative characteristics study of the stress field, the magnitude of the stress field can be obtained based on the rock mechanics test reckoning method, mathematical analysis estimating method, microstructure estimating method, and ultramicro structure estimating method. However, it is still difficult to estimate accurately the magnitude of paleo-tectonic stress. At present, the ultramicro structure estimating method has the highest accuracy, and the method has been most widely used. The relevant research has demonstrated that the submicroscopic deformation features and the magnitude of paleo-tectonic stress during rock formation have an extremely stable relationship. − Therefore, the magnitude of paleo-tectonic stress can be estimated by statistically analyzing the dislocation density, the size of dynamic recrystallization grains, and the size of the subparticles.

With the rapid development of computer technology, finite element numerical simulation has become an essential method for quantitative studies of paleo-tectonic stress fields. − By inverting paleo-tectonic stress fields to reconstruct stress regimes at different stages, it can provide a scientific basis for predicting the development of tectonic fractures and mineralization, as well as for hydrocarbon exploration and engineering geological assessment. − For example, based on the geological analysis and fractal theory, Li et al. used the paleo-tectonic stress numerical simulation to predict the multiscale fracture distribution pattern. Combined with the core, well-log, seismic, and production-test data, Li et al. explained the primary controlling factors of the fractures development and conducted the paleo-tectonic stress field numerical simulation to predict the spatial distribution of tectonic fractures. Wang et al. and Wu et al. used a geological survey combined with an ANSYS finite element simulation method to analyze the relationship between the distribution pattern of the tectonic stress field and tectonic fractures and oil and gas production potential. Zhang et al. and Yang et al. performed the two-dimensional numerical simulation of the tectonic stress field to obtain the principal factors controlling fracture development and proposed that high-stress zones were the favorable condition for mineralization. Dong et al. and Zhang et al. employed ANSYS finite element numerical simulation to invert the different stages of tectonic stress and predicted the spatial distribution patterns under the superposition of a multiple-phase tectonic stress field. Based on regional tectonic dynamics and finite element numerical methods, Li et al. and Song and Wang reconstructed the different periods of the tectonic stress field and introduced the comprehensive fracture development coefficient to quantitatively characterize the fractures distribution. Li et al. , performed the numerical simulation of the tectonic stress field to predict quantitatively the fractures distribution and quantify the effects of fault scale and fault strike on shale gas production. Through construction of a high-precision three-dimensional heterogeneous geologic model and optimization of the adaptive boundary condition, Han et al., Zhu et al. and Wang et al. improved the accuracy of tectonic stress field inversion to predict the development degree of fractures failure.

The paleo-tectonic stress field of the Pingdingshan mining area was short of systematic research and deep discussion. It led to different views being formed on the evolution law of the tectonic stress field in this research area. − The main cause of the existence of the different views was that the predecessors ignored the overall research of timing, directional, and quantitative characteristics of the paleo-stress field, and the research was short of adopting a reasonable method to solve the action strength of tectonic stress. Usually, the action strength of tectonic stress can be expressed by the stress magnitude. The stress magnitude was equal to the difference value of maximum principal stress and minimum principal stress, and the difference value was short for differential stress value.

Therefore, the tectonic stress field of the Pingdingshan mining area was studied as a whole. On the basis of the series of geological structure data, the tectonic deformation characteristics and tectonic deformation mechanism were studied in the mining area. By testing the joint fracture occurrences of the field and underground, the action direction of paleotectonic stress was judged and the diagenetic period of tectonites was determined. Based on observing the ultramicroscopic structure of the secondary quartz enlarged edge on tectonite samples in the diagenetic period, the intracrystalline dislocation density was calculated to estimate the magnitude of paleo-tectonic stress intensity in the research area.

Geological Setting

The Pingdingshan mining area is located in the southern margin of the North China Block. It belongs to the Mianchi–Yima–Yiyang–Lushan–Pingdingshan–Wuyang section of the thrust-nappe tectonic system on the northern side of the Qinling–Dabie orogen (Figure ).

Regional geological background of the study area.

Since the formation of Permian–Carboniferous coal-bearing strata, the region has mainly experienced three periods of structural movements including the Indosinian period, Yanshanian period, and Himalayan period. During the Indosinian period (257–205 Ma), the Yangtze Plate collided and jointed with the North China Block, and the southern part of the North China Block was involved in the Qinling–Dabie orogenic belt. It led the Yangtze Plate, the Qinling–Dabie orogenic belt, and the North China Block to form the A-type of intracontinental subduction from the south to the north. With the crustal generally uplifting, the wide uplifts and the long-narrow depressions of the nearly EW direction were formed. During this period, the development of the geological structure in the research area was basically controlled by the intracontinental collision orogenic movement, and it formed the tectonic deformation of mantle fold.

During the Early Yanshanian period (205–135 Ma), the Pacific Plate pitched down the North China Block from the SE direction to the NW direction, and it resulted in the formation of a series of NNE-trending compressive fault–fold structures in the eastern part of the North China Block. Due to the reduction of diving energy transmitted to the western part of the North China Block, it meant that the Pingdingshan mining area located in the southern margin of the North China Block had not formed the controlling structure during this period. The Late Yanshanian period (135–52 Ma) belonged to the strong compression period of the continent crust. The Indian Ocean Block underwent rapid northward subduction, and it formed the NE-trending compressional stress field. It meant the Qinling mountains orogenic belt had experienced the strong movement of the thrust-nappe structure. It resulted in generating the reverse thrust structural deformation activities of the NW-trending folds and NW-trending faults in the mining area, and the NW-trending structural pattern mainly formed in this period. In the period, the coal-bearing stratum experienced the most intense compressional deformation activity. The active time corresponded with the activity period of Qinling mountainmaking.

The Himalayan period (52–0.78 Ma) happened in the last phase of Late Cretaceous. The collision orogeny gradually stopped in the interior of the Qinling tectonic belt on the south side of the coalfield. And the east side of the Pacific Plate changed from the early extrusion uplift to the tectonic active period dominated by crustal extension and fractured depression. With the significant change of the regional tectonic framework, the mining area had entered a new structural development stage dominated by tensile fault-depression. The region was mainly manifested as the NNE-trending horizontal compression movement and NWW-trending horizontal extensile movement, and the intraplate deformation was relatively weak.

During the Neotectonic period (0.78 Ma to the present), the eastern region of the mainland China was pushed by the subduction of the Pacific Plate and Philippine Sea Plate to the Eurasian Continent, and the southwest region was squeezed by the collision of the Indosinian Plate from the south direction to the north direction. It led to the generation of the NEE-trending compressive action in the North China region. In this tectonic movement period, the tectonic deformation generated in the continent interior was very weak. It had a smaller influence on the tectonic deformation of the research area, and the mining area formed no new geological structure. It could be seen that the geological structure of the mining area was obviously affected by the tectonic evolution of the large area, and it led to the formation of the current structural pattern. That is, the mining area formed a geological structure on a series of NWW–NW directions folds and faults with NNE–NE directions secondary faults under the control of the Likou syncline.

The Field Emission Transmission Electron Microscopy Experiment

Experimental Methods

In the previous studies, the paleo-tectonic stress magnitude was mainly estimated by the rock mechanics test method. However, due to the poor effect of the rock mechanics test to simulate the temporal factor of stress effect, it resulted in a lower accuracy of the estimated results. With the development of microscopy techniques, based on the results of the rock rheological test, the dislocation structures of the mineral crystal were observed by the Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM), and the paleo-tectonic stress magnitude was calculated by counting the dislocation density, the size of dynamic recrystallization grains, and the size of the subparticles. −

The dislocation structure was a structural feature in which the internal crystalline structure was broken along a specific lattice plane grid, resulting in the dislocation of the adjacent particles , (Figure ). Under the action of the late tectonic movement, it only led to an increase in the sliding distance of the early period dislocation structure and did not increase the density of the dislocation structure. , Therefore, the intragranular dislocation structure density was not influenced by the late tectonic movement. When the formation time of tectonites in the stratum rock had been determined, it was a relatively accurate method to calculate the paleo-tectonic stress magnitude by using the intra granular dislocation structure information on quartz in the tectonite interior. − Therefore, the dislocation density has a stable relationship with paleo-tectonic stress magnitude, and it was not affected by the subsequent paleo-stress field. The dislocation density was independent of the duration, temperature, and strain rate during the period of paleo-stress. The method is currently regarded as one of the most accurate technical methods for calculating paleo-tectonic stress magnitude. However, this method has two limitations. First, the calculated results of paleo-stress magnitudes show relatively large deviations when the dislocation density of mineral crystals is low. Second, the relationship between the dislocation density and paleo-stress magnitude is derived under idealized assumptions. And the presence of bubbles or fluid inclusions within the analyzed quartz can directly affect the measured accuracy of dislocation density. Thus, the sampling should target the diagenetic secondary quartz that contains only a small number of bubbles or fluid inclusions to ensure accurate paleo-tectonic stress magnitude. Meanwhile, the accuracy of the results should be verified by using the subparticle size method.

Experimental Setup

When the quartz sandstones of sedimentary rock are used to estimate the paleotectonic stress magnitude, the quartz should be selected from the diagenesis period and it should observe the secondary quartz enlarged edge on tectonite samples. The quartz of the diagenesis period could accurately reflect the action characteristics of the first strong tectonic stress after the tectonite developed. In the paper, the dislocation density method was used to solve the paleotectonic stress magnitude. The main steps of the method are as follows. First, the tectonites were collected from the field and underground, and the samples should be polished and made into the slices. Second, the slices should be placed in the precise ion polishing system to further produce the ultramicroslices. The ultramicroslices were observed and photographed by the transmission electron microscope. Finally, the stress magnitude was calculated by counting the dislocation density, the size of dynamic recrystallization grains, and the size of the subparticles (Figure ).

Experimental instrument and technology roadmap for solving the paleo-tectonic stress magnitude.

244 groups of samples were collected from the field and underground, and 10 groups of samples that met the requirements were selected to test (Figure ). The ultramicro slices were produced by the precise ion polishing system with the Leica EM RES1 (Figures and ). The making procedures are as follows. First, the tectonite samples should be polished into 0.03 mm laminas, and the laminas should be pasted to glass flaked by lengshan balsam. Second, the rock laminas were moved to the observatory of the ion polishing system, and a central hole was made in the laminas by an ion beam with a 5–7° incident angle. Finally, the edge of the breakdown hole was trimmed by an ion beam with a 3–4° incident angle, and the requirements for the TEM test of the lower energy and the thinner area were obtained. The dislocation structures were observed by the type of the field emission TEM with the FEI Tecnai G2 F30 (Figure ). And the TEM test procedures are as follows. The ultramicro slices were placed on the specimen stage of the transmission electron microsocpe, and the vacuum system was opened to pump the vacuum environment. The electron beam should be aimed at the samples by turning the regulating lever, and the appropriate multiple and magnification should be selected. Finally, the target dislocation structure images were found by adjusting the focusing handwheel and the bright-ness handwheel. The images were recorded by using the detector.

Results and Discussion

Tectonic Deformation Characteristics and Mechanism in the Mining Area

Macroscopic Tectonic Deformation Characteristics and Mechanism

The mining area was bordered by the Xiangjia fracture, Jiaxian fracture, Luye fracture, and Luogang fracture with a wide angle and kilometer fault throw, and it formed the particular blocky uplifted unit. The mining area was controlled by the NW-trending Likou syncline. The secondary folds on both sides of the Likou syncline were more developed, and they were, respectively, the Xiangjia anticline, Lingwushan syncline, Baishishan anticline, Guozhuang anticline, Zhugemiao anticline, and Haotang syncline. The mining area mainly developed two groups of fault structures with NWW–NW directions and NNE–NE directions. The trend of fault structures was generally consistent with the axial direction of the Likou syncline, including the Guodishan fracture, Huoyan fracture, Baishigou fracture, Renzhuang fracture, etc. The fault structures were mostly distributed in the 5 tectonic belts in the NW direction, including the Xiangjia fracture belt, Baishigou–Huoyan fracture belt, original Shiyikuang–Niuzhuang fracture belt, Guodishan fracture belt, and Jiulishan fracture belt from the north to the south (Figure ). The characteristics of structural association were very obvious in the mining area. The compound structures with NW–NWW and NNE–NE directions were comparatively developed. Such as, the no. 8 mine was not only controlled by the structural association of the NNE-trending Jiaozan syncline and NE-trending Xindian fracture but also affected by the NE-trending Xindian fracture and NWW-trending Zhangwan fracture. The no. 10 and no. 12 mines were not only affected by the NW-trending structural assemblage of the Niuzhuang fracture, the original Shiyikuang fracture, and the Guozhuang anticline but also controlled by the NW-trending structural association of the Niuzhuang syncline and no. 12 mine fracture.

Based on the combined observation of the field and underground, the Permian–Carboniferous sandstone formation mainly developed tectonic fractures in the mining area. According to the mechanical cause of tectonic fractures, the fractures can be divided into the layer-controlled tensile fractures, the cross-cutting shear fractures, and the bedding-parallel shear fractures in the study area. Layer-controlled tensile fractures were formed under the action of the tensile tectonic stress field. The mechanical property of the stress field is mainly dominated by the normal fault stress field. Under the action of the tensile stress field, the heterogeneity of the rock stratum led to the formation of cracks along the weak structural plane. The fractures were mostly of a high-angle morphology or near-vertical shape, and the fracture width was relatively large. It was filled with a weathered accumulation. Usually, the layer control of tensile fractures ended at the high-strength lithologic interface (Figure a). The cross-cutting shear fractures were formed under the action of the strongly compressive stress field. The mechanical property of the stress field is mainly dominated by the strike-slip fault stress field. Under the action of the compressive stress field, it led to the shear dislocation of the horizontal direction along the weak structural plane. The developed fractures were dominated by the medium- and high-angle morphology, and the fracture surface was relatively uniform and smooth, accompanied by obvious scratching characteristics. The extension length of fractures was relatively large (Figure b). The bedding-parallel shear fractures were formed under the action of the compressive tectonic stress field. The mechanical property of the stress field is mainly dominated by the reverse fault stress field. Under the action of the compressive tectonic stress field, it led to the shear slip of a nearly horizontal direction along the plane of weakness. The developed fractures were dominated by the low-angle morphology, and the filling substance of white quartz can be seen in the fractures (Figure c). Some study regions developed the diagenetic fractures, and the fractures were dominated by the layered cracks. The fractures were distributed in a nearly horizontal orientation. The fractures were relatively close, and the extension length was lower (Figure d).

Developed characteristics of fractures on Permian–Carboniferous sandstone formation in the mining area.

It indicated that the Permian–Carboniferous sandstone formation mainly developed tectonic fractures in the mining area, and the tectonic fractures can be divided into the layer-controlled tensile fractures, the cross-cutting shear fractures, and the bedding-parallel shear fractures. Some regions developed the diagenetic fractures. The compressive-shear tectonic stress field was the main stress action form for the fracture formation.

At present, the classification criteria of ultramicroscopic tectonic deformation mechanisms have not been unified. − However, it usually believed that the cataclasis action and cataclastic flow belonged to the brittle deformation mechanism, and the others were of the plastic deformation mechanism. Therefore, combined with the above dislocation structure characteristics, it could be seen that the intracrystalline plastic deformation mechanism of tectonites in the study area was dominated by the dislocation glide and dislocation climb.

Period Division of the Paleo-Tectonic Stress Field in the Mining Area

Determination of the Tectonic Stress Field Direction

Data for 2600 joint attitudes were obtained. The measured positions and data are shown in Figure and Table . On the basis of the mutual relationship between shear fractures and the rock strata, the fractures can be further classified into plane shear fractures and profile shear fractures (Figure a). The plane shear fractures commonly form before the strata have undergone significant deformation; however, the profile shear fractures typically develop after the strata have experienced substantial deformation. On the basis of the relationship between conjugate shear joints and the stress field, the orientations of the paleotectonic stress field can be reconstructed. , Based on the stereographic projection method, the joint data were analyzed by Stereonet 11 software. Beforehand, the bedding plane of the joints developed before the fold should be adjusted to judge the precedence order of joint formation. Then, the joint data were projected to the Wulff net to obtain the preferred occurrence of joints. The stress state analysis graphs of joints were drawn to determine the direction of principal stress. Finally, based on the results of joint periodization and complement, the paleotectonic stress direction information was counted in the same period. The isopycnic graphs of principal stress were drawn to determine the preferred direction of the different stages of the tectonic stress field.

Attitude of Conjugate Joints and Analytical Results of the Paleo-Stress Field in the Research Area

The analysis shows that the shear joints dominated in the study area. The joint attitudes were stable, and the joint planes were straight and smooth. The joint extension was farther, and the dip angle of the joints was larger. The study area mainly developed 5 joint groups, and the typical joints were selected to analyze, as shown in Figure . The NNE and NNW directions of plane-conjugated shear joints were the most developed in the T1l strata, and the plane joints were the least developed in the P2s and P2sh strata. The conjugated shear joints could be found in the T1l strata of the Likou synclinal shaft and the Lingwushan synclinal shaft, the conjugate relationship of joints was obvious, and the joint system were of rhomboidal shape (Figure C14). The NNE and near EW directions of plane-conjugated shear joints were developed in the P2s, P2sh, and T1l strata, and the development scale of shear joints was larger. The conjugated shear joints could be found in the outcrop region of the Lingwushan syncline, the conjugate relationship of joints was obvious, and the joint system was of chessboard shape (Figure C24). The NE and NNW directions of the joint system were developed in the P2s, P2sh, and T1l strata, and the development scale of the joints was smaller. The conjugated shear joints could be found in the P2s strata of the Baishishan anticlinal shaft, and the sharp angle of the joint system existed with an obvious passivation phenomenon. The conjugate relationship of the joint system was obvious, and the NNE and near EW directions of the joint system were cut by the NE and NNW directions of the joint system (Figure C10). The NNW and NWW directions of conjugated shear joints were mostly developed in the P2sh and T1l strata. The conjugated shear joints could be found in the southern outcrop region of the Lingwushan syncline. The conjugate relationship of the joint system was obvious, and the NNW and NWW directions of the joint system were cut by the NNE and near EW directions of conjugated shear joints (Figure C28).

On the basis of the joints located in the stratum and the intersection relationship of the joints, the joints could be counted and divided into different periods. The NNE and NNW directions of plane-conjugated shear joints were the most developed in the T1l strata, and the joints were the least developed in the P2s and P2sh strata. According to the regional tectonic evolution characteristics, the joint system belonged to the Indosinian period of product formed by the nearly NS-trending compressive tectonic stress field, and the development time of the joint system was the most early. The previous research results showed that the Late Yanshanian tectonic movement formed two groups of plane-conjugated shear joints with the highest development degree in the research area. And the two groups of shear joints were, respectively, the characteristics of sinistral shear movement with the nearly EW strike direction and the characteristics of dextral shear movement with the NNE strike direction. The measured results showed that the NNE and near EW directions of the conjugated shear joint system were consistent with the above results, and it could be seen that the joint system belonged to the late Yanshanian period. The NNE and near EW directions of the conjugated shear joint system were dislocated by the NE and NNW directions of the conjugated shear joint system (Figure C10), and it indicated that the former was developed earlier than the latter. The NNW and NWW directions of the conjugated shear joint system were cut by the NNE and near EW directions of the conjugated shear joint system, and it manifested that the former was formed earlier than the latter (Figure C28). The time sequence relationship of the above-mentioned joint system formation could be obtained. The NNE and NNW directions of the joint system were the earliest developments. The NNW and NWW directions of the joint system formed later than the NNE and NNW directions of the joint system. The NNE and near EW directions of the joint system were developed later than the NNW and NWW directions of the joint system, and the NE and NNW directions of the joint system were the latest development. According to the relation between the conjugated shear joint and principal stress axis, the paleotectonic stress field could be divided into four periods. It included the first period of the stress field with the near NS direction, the second period of the stress field with the NW direction, the third period of the stress field with the NE direction, and the fourth period of the stress field with the NNE direction (Table ).

The results of paleo-tectonic stress are shown in Table . It could be seen that the maximum principal stress direction of the first-period stress field was distributed in 2°–14° and 175°–182° and the dip angle was distributed in 3°–14°. And it reflected the nearly NS-trending compression action characteristics. The maximum principal stress direction in the second period was distributed in 280°–337°, and the dip angle was distributed in 7°–21°. And it reflected the NW-trending compression action characteristics. The maximum principal stress direction in the third-period was distributed in 33°–74°, and the dip angle was distributed in 3°–18°. And it reflected the NE-trending compression characteristics. The maximum principal stress direction in the fourth-period was distributed in 16°–21°, and the dip angle was distributed in 4°–24°. And it reflected the NNE-trending compression characteristics.

Determination of the Tectonic Stress Field Period

The first-period tectonic stress field reflected the nearly NS-trending compression action characteristics. According to the regional tectonic evolution history, the Yangtze Block collided and jointed with the North China Block in the Indosinian period, and it produced the nearly NS-trending compressive stress in the Pingdingshan mining area of the southern margin of the North China Block. It could be seen that the first-period tectonic stress field belonged to the Indosinian period. The second period of the tectonic stress field reflected the NW-trending compression action characteristics. During the Early Yanshanian period, the Pacific Plate pitched down the North China Block from the SE direction to the NW direction, and it led to the formation of a series of strike-slip fracture systems in the North China Block. Meanwhile, the mining area developed the NWW-trending fault-slip faults, including the Xiangjia fracture. Therefore, the second period of the tectonic stress field belonged to the Early Yanshanian period. The third period of the tectonic stress field reflected the NE-trending compression action characteristics. Under the action of Later Yanshanian tectonic movement, the mining area formed the highest developing degree of nearly NW-trending and NNE-trending plane conjugated shear joint systems, and the Later Yanshanian period corresponded to this third period of the stress field. In the meantime, in the region, the NW-trending thrust-nappe structure system and the NE-trending tensile fault system were matched with this period of the stress field. It can be seen that this period stress field belonged to the Later Yanshanian period. The isotopic evidence showed that the rock mass age of the NE-trending fault was 100–135 Ma in the area (Later Yanshanian period), and the above-mentioned views were further confirmed. The fourth-period tectonic stress field reflected the NNE-trending compression action characteristics. During the Himalayan period, the North China Block was influenced by the combined effect of the Pacific Plate and Indian Ocean Plate, and it produced the NNE-trending dextral rotation of force couple. Under the action of force couple, the pre-existing NWW–NW direction fault showed the compression–torsion activity and the pre-existing NNE–NE direction fault showed the tension–torsional activity. Therefore, the fourth-period tectonic stress field belonged to the Himalayan period.

The evolution law of the stress field was as follows. In the Indosinian period of the tectonic stress field, the preferred direction of maximum principal stress σ1 was 3° ∠ 6°. The stress field had little influence on the Pingdingshan mining area, and the area almost did not develop the EW-trending structures. In the Early Yanshanian period of the tectonic stress field, the preferred direction of the maximum principal stress σ1 was 313° ∠ 13°. Under the action of the stress field, the mining area developed the NW–NWW direction of strike-slip faults, such as the Xiangjia fracture in the northern boundary of the mining area. However, the mining area did not form the NNE–NE direction of strong deformation folds and inverse faults. In the Late Yanshanian period of the tectonic stress field, the preferred direction of the maximum principal stress σ1 was 54° ∠ 9°. Under the action of the stress field, the mining area formed a series of NWW–NW direction broad fold structures accompanied by the NW-trending compresso-shear faults and the NE-trending tenso-shear faults, such as the Likou syncline, Baishishan anticline, Jiulishan fracture, Guodishan fracture, Baishigou–Huoyan fracture, etc. In the Himalayan period, the preferred direction of maximum principal stress σ1 was 18° ∠ 15°. Under the action of the tectonic stress field, the pre-existing NNE–NE direction faults presented the tension–torsional activity in the mining area. For example, the Jiaxian fracture and the Luogang fracture presented the dextral transtension at this time, and it reversed to the normal fault when the landmass uplifted.

10 groups of tectonite samples were collected from the Gaogou inverse fracture, Goulifeng fracture, Baishishan anticline axis, Xindian fracture, Zhangwan fracture, and Jiaozan syncline axis. The samples belonged to the diagenetic products in the late Yanshanian period. It could be seen that the main diagenesis period of tectonites was the late Yanshanian period in the research area.

In summary, the research area mainly got through three periods of tectonic stress field movement, including the Indosinian period, Yanshanian period, and Himalayan period, and the direction of the maximum principal stress had experienced a transformation process of NS→NW→NE→NNE. Under the multistage tectonic stress field, the mining area formed a geological structure on a series of NWW–NW direction folds and faults with NNE–NE direction secondary faults under the control of the Likou syncline. The main diagenetic period of tectonites was the Late Yanshanian period in the mining area.

Dislocation Density Method

The typical dislocation structure images of samples were obtained by the TEM observation technique. The dislocation structure included the straight, smooth, and parallel dislocation lines, the curved and disorderly dislocation lines, and the circular, elliptical, irregular, and distorted dislocation lines.

Twiss proposed the theoretical model between dislocation density and differential stresses. Based on the rock mechanics test, the theoretical model of the dislocation structure was further modified by Kohlstedt and Beeman. 1 Δσ=αμbρ1/2 where Δσ is the differential stresses; α is a dimensionless coefficient; μ is the shear modulus; b is the Burg vector; and ρ is the dislocation density.

At present, the Smith–Gurthamn method is commonly used to calculate the lattice dislocation density. The calculation method was as follows. First, the multiple random lines in the study area were plotted on the clear ultramicro structural images. Then, the dislocation density could be obtained by counting the intersect point N of the random line and the dislocation line. 2 ρ=2N/L×t where ρ is the dislocation density; L is the random line length; N is the number of intersection points between the free dislocation line and random line; and t is the average thickness of the laminas.

Based on the dislocation density method, the dislocation structure density of images could be calculated by counting the length of the random line, the thickness of the slices, and the number of intersection points between the random line and the dislocation line. According to the image scale, the length of the random line was set at 0.0123184 cm. However, the thickness of the region thinned by the ion mill was not uniform. It was found that the specimen appears the clearest in TEM observations when the thickness of the slices was 0.00005 cm. Therefore, this thickness was used to calculate the dislocation density. Discussion

Based on the analyzing results of macroscopic, microscopic, and ultra-microscopic tectonic deformation characteristics, it was determined that the main diagenesis period of tectonites was the Late Yanshanian period in the research area. And the paleo-tectonic stress magnitude was determined to be 40.70–60.50 MPa by using the dislocation density method and subgrain size method. On the paleo-tectonic stress magnitude of the Gaogou reverse fracture, Baishishan anticline, F2# fault of the 12130 roadway in the Shoushan no. 1 mine, Xindian fracture, Likou syncline, F1# fault, and F2# fault of the 21050 roadway in the no. 8 mine, the paleo-tectonic stress magnitudes obtained by the subparticle size method were essentially consistent with the results obtained by the dislocation density method. The differential ratios of the two methods were 2.39%, 6.67%, 6.25%, 3.99%, 4.69%, 3.10%, and 1.36%, respectively, and the all differential ratios were less than 10%. On the paleo-tectonic stress magnitudes of the Goulifeng fracture in the Shoushan no. 1 mine and the Zhangwan fracture in the no. 8 mine, the values obtained by the dislocation density method were slightly different from the results obtained by the subparticle size method. The differential ratios of two methods were 14.77% and 11.72%, respectively, and all differential ratios were less than 15%. On the paleo-tectonic stress magnitude of the F1# fault in the 12130 roadway of the Shoushan no. 1 mine, the value obtained by the dislocation density method had the larger difference with the result obtained by the subparticle size method. The differential ratio was 57.79%. The main reason was that the F1# fault in the 12130 roadway was smaller scale with the shallower in cutting and lower in deformation temperature. Even under the effect of a strong tectonic stress field, it was still in the deformation stage of cold processing. Therefore, the tectonite of F1# fault in the 12130 roadway developed lower density dislocation structures and an extremely small amount of big size subparticles. However, as a whole, the results obtained by the subparticle size method were in general accord with the results obtained by the dislocation density method. The results obtained by the dislocation density method were more reliable.

The Shoushan no. 1 mine and no. 8 mine were, respectively, located at both sides of the Likou synclinal shaft. The Shoushan no. 1 mine has 2 measuring points of intensive deformation mylonite in the field. The paleotectonic stress magnitudes in the Gaogou inverse fracture and Goulifeng fracture were 49.8 and 59.8 MPa, respectively. It presented the law that the closer the Likou synclinal axis was, the greater the paleotectonic stress was. The no. 8 mine has 2 measuring points of intensive deformation mylonite in the field. The paleotectonic stress magnitudes in the Zhangwan fracture and Xindian fracture were 56.5 and 61.1 MPa, respectively. It also presented the law that the closer the Likou synclinal axis was, the greater the paleotectonic stress was. The Shoushan no. 1 mine has 2 measuring points of intensive deformation mylonite in the underground, and the mean value of paleotectonic stress was 47.80 MPa. The no. 8 mine also has 2 measuring points of intensive deformation mylonite in the underground, and the mean value of paleo-tectonic stress was 48.55 MPa. The 21050 roadway in the no. 8 mine was close to the Likou synclinal axis, and the 12130 roadway in the Shoushan no. 1 mine was farther from the Likou synclinal axis. Therefore, it also presented the rule that the closer the Likou synclinal axis was, the greater the paleotectonic stress was.

In summary, the paleo-tectonic stress magnitudes of the Late Yanshanian period were 40.70–62.50 MPa in the mining area. And the closer the Likou synclinal axis was, the greater the paleo-tectonic stress was.

Conclusion

1.The macrofractures of the Permian–Carboniferous sandstone formation were mainly tectonic fractures, and some fractures were diagenetic fractures. The types of tectonic fractures included the layer-controlled tensile fractures, cross-cutting shear fractures, and bedding-parallel shear fractures. The types of microscopic fractures included tensile fractures, shear fractures, compressive-shear fractures, and tenso-shear fractures in the research area. The ultramicroscopic tectonic types of tectonites included the dislocation lines and dislocation walls. The intracrystalline plastic deformation mechanism of tectonites is dominated by the dislocation glide and dislocation climb.2The Pingdingshan mining area mainly got through three periods of tectonic stress field movement, including the Indosinian period, Yanshanian period and Himalayan period. The direction of the maximum principal stress had experienced a transform process of NS → NW → NE → NNE. Under the multistage tectonic stress field, the mining area formed a geological structure on a series of NWW–NW direction folds and faults with NNE–NE direction secondary faults under the control of the Likou syncline. The main diagenetic period of tectonites was the Late Yanshanian period in the mining area.3.Based on the dislocation density measurement method, the ultra-microscopic structure of the secondary quartz enlarged edge on tectonite samples was tested in the diagenetic period. The magnitude of paleo-tectonic stress was obtained in the Late Yanshanian period, and the values were 40.70–62.50 MPa. The result accuracy was verified by the subparticle size method. And the closer the Likou synclinal axis was, the greater the paleo-tectonic stress was.
