Visons in Kitaev Spin Liquids with Majorana Fermi Surfaces
Caio V. S. Soares, Rodrigo G. Pereira

Abstract
The excitation spectrum of Kitaev quantum spin liquids consists of itinerant Majorana fermions, which can be gapless or gapped, and vortices of a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} gauge field, known as visons, which are always gapped. In this work, we investigate visons in Kitaev-type models where the Majorana fermions form a Fermi surface. In this case, the creation of a vison pair is analogous to introducing a local impurity potential in a metal. Since the gapless modes lead to strong finite-size effects, we compare the numerical calculation of the vison gap on finite lattices with the result from an analytical approach based on Green’s function techniques. We find that the vison gap decreases as the size of the Fermi surface increases, signalling an instability of the quantum spin liquid ground state. We also show that larger Fermi surfaces tend to suppress the change in local spin correlations due to the Majorana-vison scattering potential.

The KHM describes spins

H_{K}=-\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z}J_{\alpha}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle_{\alpha}}\sigma_{i}^{\alpha}\sigma_{j}^{\alpha},

\alpha=x,y,z

This model has an extensive set of mutually commuting conserved plaquette operators,

W_{p}=\prod_{i\in p}\sigma_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}

\alpha_{i}
for sites around the plaquette are chosen as shown in Fig.
1

w_{p}=\pm 1

We perturb the KHM by adding the three-spin interaction

H_{\rm pert}=-\sum_{\alpha}J^{\prime}_{\alpha}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle_{\alpha}\langle j,k\rangle_{\beta}}(-1)^{s_{i}}\sigma^{\alpha}_{i}\sigma^{\gamma}_{j}\sigma^{\beta}_{k},

(i,j,k)
forming two nearest-neighbor bonds labeled by
\alpha
and
\beta

24

(i,j,k)
contains two sites in sublattice A or two sites in sublattice B. Thus, the perturbation preserves the symmetry that combines time reversal with sublattice inversion, similarly to the effects of a staggered magnetic field
54
;
42
;
58
.

The analytical solution is obtained by mapping the spin operators at each site onto four Majorana fermions
\left(b_{i}^{x},b_{i}^{y},b_{i}^{z},c_{i}\right)
. The latter are Hermitian, obey canonical anticommutation relations, and square to the identity:
(b_{i}^{\alpha})^{2}=(c_{i})^{2}=1
. The componentes of the spin operators are represented by

\sigma_{i}^{\alpha}=\text{i}b^{\alpha}_{i}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i},

(3)

along with the local constraint
D_{i}=b^{x}_{i}b^{y}_{i}b^{z}_{i}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}=1

\forall i
, which selects physical spin-1/2 states. In this representation, the Hamiltonian
H^{\prime}_{K}=H_{K}+H_{\rm pert}
assumes the form

\displaystyle H^{\prime}_{K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{i}\sum_{\alpha}J_{\alpha}\sum_{\left\langle i,j\right\rangle_{\alpha}}\hat{u}^{\alpha}_{ij}c_{i}c_{j}

(4)

\displaystyle-\text{i}\sum_{\alpha}J^{\prime}_{\alpha}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle_{\alpha}\langle j,k\rangle_{\beta}}(-1)^{s_{i}}\hat{u}^{\alpha}_{ij}\hat{u}_{jk}^{\beta}c_{i}c_{k},

where the operators
\hat{u}^{\alpha}_{ij}=\text{i}b^{\alpha}_{i}b^{\alpha}_{j}
are associated with the bonds
\langle i,j\rangle_{\alpha}
. They commute with the Hamiltonian and have eigenvalues
u^{\alpha}_{ij}=\pm 1
. Here
\hat{u}^{\alpha}_{ij}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\text{i}\left(J_{x}e^{\text{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{1}}+J_{y}e^{\text{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{2}}+J_{z}\right),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2J^{\prime}_{x}\sin[\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{a}_{2}-\mathbf{a}_{1})]-2J^{\prime}_{y}\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{2})

In addition to itinerant Majorana fermions, the KHM has excitations associated with changing the flux configuration. The simplest flux excitation can be accomplished by flipping the eigenvalue of a single
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

H^{K}_{\rm 2v}=H_{0}^{K}+\hat{V},

where

\displaystyle\hat{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\text{i}J_{z}c_{\rm A}(\mathbf{0})c_{\rm B}(\mathbf{0})+2\text{i}J_{z}^{\prime}c_{\rm A}(\mathbf{0})[c_{\rm A}(\mathbf{a}_{1})-c_{\rm A}(\mathbf{a}_{2})]

Chua-Yao-Fiette model on the kagome lattice

\displaystyle=

III
Vison gap

In this section, we analyze the two-vison gap in the KHM and CYF models using numerical diagonalization for finite-size lattices and an analytical approach based on scattering phase shifts. We compare the results of the two approaches and analyze the influence of a Majorana Fermi surface on the vison gap.

Let us briefly review the analytical approach for the vison gap in the unperturbed KHM
46
, obtained by setting
J_{\alpha}^{\prime}=0
in Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
). The key idea is to relate the vison gap to the phase shift for Majorana fermions scattering off the local potential in the Hamiltonian for the two-vison sector. For
J_{\alpha}^{\prime}=0
, we rewrite the Hamiltonian as

H_{2\text{v}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\Psi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}h_{K}(\mathbf{k})\Psi^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{k}}+\frac{1}{2}{c}^{T}(\mathbf{0})\left(2J_{z}\tau_{y}\right){c}(\mathbf{0}),

(32)

where
{c}(\mathbf{0})=\left(c_{A}(\mathbf{0}),c_{B}(\mathbf{0})\right)^{T}
and
\tau_{y}
is the Pauli matrix acting in sublattice space. We introduce the noninteracting Green’s function

G_{0}(\mathbf{k},z)=[z\mathbb{I}_{2}-h_{K}(\mathbf{k})]^{-1},

(33)

where
z
is a complex frequency and
\mathbb{I}_{2}
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix. Notice that the scattering potential is completely local in real space, which implies the absence of momentum dependence in reciprocal space. In this case, one can analytically solve the Dyson equation for the exact Green’s function, as done in the context of impurity problems
15
;
17
. The solution is expressed in terms of the local Green’s function, defined as

g(z)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}G_{0}(\mathbf{k},z).

(34)

In the thermodynamic limit, it is convenient to compute the integral over the Brillouin zone as

g(z)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk_{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk_{2}\,G_{0}(\mathbf{k},z),

(35)

where
k_{1}=\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{1}
and
k_{2}=\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{2}
.
The phase shift is given by
46

\delta_{\text{v}}(E)=\text{Im}\left\{\ln\left[\det\left(\mathbb{I}_{2}-\hat{V}_{0}\,g(E+i\eta)\right)\right]\right\},

(36)

where
\hat{V}_{0}=2J_{z}\tau_{y}
and we take
\eta\to 0^{+}
. One can then use Fumi’s theorem
20
;
37
;
38
;
36
to calculate the change in the free energy due to the presence of the scattering potential by integrating the phase shift. At zero temperature, the result is exactly the gap
\Delta_{\rm v}
for two adjacent visons in the thermodynamic limit, given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dE\,\delta_{\rm v}(E)\text{ sgn}(E).

(37)

IV
Spin correlations

The results presented in Sec.
III
indicate that the vison gap decreases as the Majorana Fermi surface increases. Importantly, the gap can be expressed as an integral of the exact phase shift, which sums up Majorana-vison scattering processes to all orders. This relation suggests that the effective scattering amplitude becomes weaker for larger Majorana Fermi surfaces. To probe this effect, in this section we consider local spin correlations in the vicinity of a vortex pair in the perturbed KHM.

Spin correlations in the ground state of the KHM can be calculated using the conservation of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
bond operators
5
. The nonzero correlation on a nearest-neighbor bond
\langle i,j\rangle_{\alpha}
is given by

C^{\alpha}_{ij}=\langle\sigma^{\alpha}_{i}\sigma^{\alpha}_{j}\rangle=-\text{i}u_{ij}^{\alpha}\langle c_{i}c_{j}\rangle.

(41)

In the flux-free sector, the correlation is uniform, and we denote the corresponding value by
C_{0}
, with
C_{0}>0
for ferromagnetic Kitaev interactions. In the two-vison sector, the correlation varies with the position relative to the unit cell
\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{0}
where we flip the sign of
u^{z}_{ij}
. Let us denote by
C^{\alpha}(\mathbf{R})
the correlation between the spin in sublattice A of unit cell
\mathbf{R}
and its nearest neighbor on an
\alpha
bond.
From the diagonalization of the Hamiltonian on finite lattices, we find that the correlation
C^{z}(\mathbf{0})
at the central
z
bond is suppressed upon the creation of the vison pair, whereas other bonds can show enhanced correlations. We define the relative change in the spin correlation as

\delta C^{\alpha}(\mathbf{R})=\frac{C^{\alpha}(\mathbf{R})-C_{0}}{C_{0}}.

(42)

The spatial pattern is illustrated in Fig.
9

\delta C^{\alpha}(\mathbf{R})
are already present for
J^{\prime}=0
, but they become more extended in the presence of a Majorana Fermi surface. This slow decay of the response with the distance from the vison pair accounts for the strong finite-size effects in the calculation of observables such as the vison gap.

V
Discussion

We discussed gapped excitations associated with the creation of a pair of adjacent visons in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
spin liquids with a Majorana Fermi surface. We considered two exactly solvable models, namely a perturbed Kitaev honeycomb model and the CYF kagome model. In both models, the Majorana Fermi surface is formed by tuning a parameter that moves a Dirac point in the fermionic dispersion away from zero energy, a mechanism analogous to electron doping or pseudoscalar potentials in graphene and Dirac semimetals.

We have shown that the vison gap exhibits oscillations as a function of system size when the low-energy modes define incommensurate wave vectors, such as the characteristic size of the Majorana Fermi surface or the momentum of a Dirac point located away from high-symmetry points in the Brillouin zone. If one is interested in the vison gap in the thermodynamic limit, the analytical approach that relates the vison gap to scattering phase shifts becomes instrumental in this case. We have demonstrated an application of this approach by generalizing it to the CYF model, which contains multiple Majorana bands. On the other hand, the strong finite-size effects should be taken into account when analyzing properties of such gapless
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
spin liquids in finite geometries.

We found that the vison gap is maximum when the Dirac point lies at zero energy and decreases as the Majorana Fermi surface increases. This effect can be interpreted by noting that, similarly to how metals screen charged impurities, the Majorana Fermi surface suppresses the change in spin correlations near the visons. In a simple picture, the system behaves as if the dressed Majorana-vison scattering potential becomes effectively weaker. Since the vison gap is equivalent to the energy cost for introducing the local potential and given by an integral of the exact scattering phase shift, increasing the Majorana Fermi surface tends to lower the vison gap. In the solvable models considered here, the vanishing gap signals first-order phase transitions corresponding to level crossings with other quantum spin liquid ground states in different flux sectors. However, in principle continuous transitions associated with condensation of isolated visons or vison pairs are also possible
59
. The effect uncovered in this work may be useful to assess the stability of spin liquid phases with Majorana Fermi surfaces.

An important open problem is to investigate how the energy gap varies with the distance between the visons, which can be interpreted in terms of an effective vison-vison interaction mediated by gapless Majorana modes
31
. For this purpose, it would be important to
extend the analytical technique to calculate the vison gap for spatially separated visons in the thermodynamic limit. In addition, while we have focused on a regime of nearly circular Majorana Fermi surfaces, we note that the CYF model also harbors states with line Fermi surfaces with zero curvature. In this case, the Majorana fermion Green’s function shows a rather different spatial dependence
6
;
44
, which may lead to qualitatively different behavior in the vison gap and local spin correlations. Finally, it would be interesting to study dynamical response functions, as the Majorana Fermi surface may give rise to power-law threshold singularities in close analogy with the orthogonality catastrophe in metals.
