Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? REVEAL (pRospEctiVe charactErization of asthma patients treated with dupilumAb in a reaL-world setting) is an observational, prospective registry of patients initiating dupilumab for the treatment of severe, uncontrolled asthma in 12 countries from Latin America, the Middle East, the Russian Federation, and Singapore.The study is aimed at documenting the treatment of asthma with dupilumab in real-world clinical practice from the above geographical regions, to better understand the disease burden and unmet needs of patients. Introduction

Asthma is a heterogeneous disease, typically characterized by chronic airway inflammation that manifests as burdensome respiratory symptoms and expiratory airflow limitation [1]. Inhaled therapy consisting of corticosteroids combined with a long-acting β2-agonist (LABA) is the standard-of-care treatment for asthma and is effective in most patients. However, some patients continue to experience asthma symptoms and exacerbations despite receiving standard-of-care therapy [1]. Severe asthma is a form of the disease that is difficult to treat, requiring treatments such as high-dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) in combination with LABA to prevent it from becoming uncontrolled, or the condition remains uncontrolled due to factors such as poor adherence, incorrect inhaler technique, or comorbidities [1]. Since uncontrolled asthma with frequent exacerbations is associated with long-term lung function decline [2–4] and an increased risk of mortality [5, 6], targeted drugs that inhibit the underlying dysregulated inflammatory pathways are needed to treat patients with severe disease.

Up to 70% of patients with asthma have elevated blood eosinophil counts and fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) levels, both of which serve as biomarkers of type 2 inflammation [7, 8]. Patients often exhibit coexisting conditions, which have been shown to cluster by phenotype throughout childhood [9]. For patients with uncontrolled, moderate-to-severe asthma and evidence of type 2 inflammation despite receiving maximal standard-of-care inhaled therapy, add-on biologic therapy that targets type 2 inflammatory pathways is recommended [1].

Dupilumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody [10, 11], blocks the shared receptor component for interleukin-4 and interleukin-13, which are key and central drivers of type 2 inflammation in multiple diseases, including asthma [12, 13]. In multiple phase 2 and 3 clinical trials, dupilumab significantly reduced asthma exacerbations and improved lung function and quality of life in adults and adolescents with uncontrolled, moderate-to-severe asthma; safety was consistent with the known safety profile of dupilumab [14–18].

Although clinical trials are critical for evaluating the efficacy and safety of drugs, they generally rely on highly selected patient populations and operate under tightly regulated conditions [19]. Moreover, they may not capture global events, such as the COVID-19 pandemic, that may influence asthma prevalence or treatment patterns. For example, after accounting for the COVID-19 pandemic, the prevalence of asthma decreased in children and younger adults in South Korea but did not change in adults ≥ 40 years old [20]. Therefore, real-world data are needed to assess the safety and effectiveness of therapies in routine clinical practice and to better understand their full therapeutic impact [19].

Two longitudinal, prospective, registry studies in patients receiving dupilumab for the treatment of asthma are ongoing. These registries aim to characterize the safety and effectiveness among a globally representative population of patients who are prescribed dupilumab outside of clinical trials, in routine clinical practice. More than 700 patients treated with dupilumab for asthma in North America, Europe, and Japan are enrolled in RAPID (Registry of Asthma Patients Initiating DUPIXENT®; NCT04287621) [21, 22]. The second registry, REVEAL (pRospEctiVe charactErization of asthma patients treated with dupilumAb in a reaL-world setting; NCT04550962), is a longitudinal, prospective, 3-year, observational study of adults and adolescents with uncontrolled asthma in Latin America, the Middle East, Russian Federation, and Singapore who have been prescribed dupilumab according to country-specific prescribing information.

The objective of the analysis reported here is to describe the baseline characteristics of 374 patients in the effectiveness analysis set for REVEAL prior to them initiating dupilumab for the treatment of their asthma.

Methods

Study Design

REVEAL (NCT04550962) is an ongoing, longitudinal, prospective, multinational, 3 year, observational registry study of patients initiating dupilumab for the treatment of asthma according to country-specific Prescribing Information (Fig. 1). REVEAL will enroll up to 800 patients and, at the time of this analysis, 376 patients are enrolled at 40 study sites in Latin America (Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Mexico), the Middle East (Israel, Kuwait, Lebanon, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE), the Russian Federation, and Singapore.

REVEAL is being conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, the International Conference on Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guideline, and applicable regulatory requirements. The initial approving ethics institution for REVEAL was King Fahad Medical City (Project Investigator: Prof. Riyad Al-Lehebi), 2642, Makkah Al Mukarramah Br Rd, As Sulimaniyah, Riyadh 12,231, Saudi Arabia. Subsequent approvals for the study were granted by appropriate ethics committees or institutional review boards in the other participating countries before patients were recruited. All patients provided written informed consent. For adolescent patients, both consent from parents or guardians and assent from patients were obtained.

Patients

The patients included in this analysis were enrolled in REVEAL between November 2, 2020 and June 19, 2023. Patients were eligible to enroll in REVEAL if they were ≥ 12 years old, initiating dupilumab treatment for a primary indication of asthma according to country-specific Prescribing Information, and were willing and able to comply with required clinic visits and study procedures and assessments. Patients for whom dupilumab was contraindicated, who received dupilumab ≤ 6 months before the baseline visit, or who are currently participating in an interventional clinical trial that modified patient care were not eligible to enroll in REVEAL. In addition, patients with any medical condition that, in the opinion of the investigator, would potentially interfere with their ability to participate in the study were excluded.

Assessments

Data collected at the baseline visit included demographic data and clinical characteristics related to asthma. General medical history, asthma history, including family history and a history of coexisting type 2 inflammatory diseases, and medication history were also collected at the baseline visit. Spirometry and the type 2 inflammatory biomarker levels (blood eosinophil counts, FeNO, and total IgE) were assessed. Asthma control was evaluated with the 6-item Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ-6), which assesses the severity of asthma symptoms on a 7-point scale (0 = no impairment; 6 = maximum impairment). Higher ACQ-6 scores indicate worse asthma control. The minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for a change or difference in ACQ-6 scores is 0.5 points [23, 24]. Data on healthcare resource utilization, including any unscheduled asthma-related inpatient visits, emergency department visits, and physician office visits, as well as hospitalizations for any reason, were collected for the 12 months prior to enrollment in REVEAL and during the study.

The severity of coexisting type 2 inflammatory conditions was also assessed using validated questionnaires in patients with relevant comorbidities. Patients rated the severity of their allergic rhinitis (AR) symptoms using the Allergic Rhinitis Visual Analog Scale (AR-VAS) on a continuum from “not at all bothersome” to “extremely bothersome” [25]. AR-VAS scores were categorized as follows: < 20 = well controlled, 20–50 = partly controlled, and > 50 = uncontrolled. Patients with chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) with or without nasal polyposis (NP) rated the impact of CRS with or without NP on their health-related quality of life during the past 2 weeks using the 22-item Sino-Nasal Outcome Test (SNOT-22). Scores ranged from 0 to 110, with lower scores indicating a lower impact of CRS with or without NP on quality of life. The MCID for SNOT-22 is 8.9 [26]. The burden of atopic dermatitis (AD) was evaluated in patients with AD using the 7-item Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure (POEM). POEM scores range from 0 to 28 points, with higher scores indicating more frequent symptoms over a 1-week period [27]. The MCID for POEM is 3.4 [28].

In addition to the baseline visit, which was considered to be the last available visit on the same day dupilumab was initiated or within 3 months of dupilumab treatment, assessments and questionnaires were completed on month 1 and then every 3 months during the first year and every 6 months through month 36.

In this analysis, an asthma exacerbation was defined by asthma symptoms that were aggravated and required treatment. A severe exacerbation was defined as an asthma exacerbation that resulted in hospitalization, emergency department visit, or systemic corticosteroid use for at least 3 days.

Statistical Analysis

The enrolled population consisted of all eligible patients who were prescribed dupilumab and provided written informed consent. All enrolled patients who received ≥ 1 dose of dupilumab for the treatment of asthma comprised the effectiveness analysis population.

Continuous data were summarized using descriptive statistics, including mean, median, first and third quartiles, standard deviation (SD), minimum, and maximum. Where appropriate, percentages were calculated for categorical data.

Results

Demographic Characteristics

The enrolled population for REVEAL comprised 376 patients from 12 countries (see Table S1 in the electronic supplementary material for details on patient numbers enrolled by country and their respective site[s]). Patients were predominantly enrolled in Saudi Arabia (25.5%), Russian Federation (19.7%), and Kuwait (10.1%); the remaining patients were enrolled in other Middle Eastern countries, Latin America, and Singapore (Fig. 2). Baseline data from 374 patients in the effectiveness analysis set were compiled for this analysis. Of the 376 enrolled patients, two patients were not included in the effectiveness analysis since neither patient took dupilumab and both discontinued from the study, completing the baseline visit but had no follow-up visits. All measures presented are those for patients with available data with corresponding patient numbers or percentages indicated in parentheses. Full baseline demographics are summarized in Table 1. Mean (SD) age was 47.8 (13.89) years, with a range of 15–84 years. Nearly two-thirds (62.6%) of the patients were female. Most patients were white (50.3%) and not Hispanic or Latino (51.3%); 89.0% lived in urban areas. Mean (SD) body mass index among 362 patients with available data was 29.2 (6.79) kg/m2, indicating that excess body weight was common among patients enrolled in REVEAL prior to initiating dupilumab. Overall, 7.2% had no health insurance coverage, whereas 67.6% had public insurance, 13.1% had private insurance, and 9.6% had both public and private health insurance (n = 365). Of the 40 treating physicians who enrolled the 374 patients in REVEAL, 25 were pneumologists and 17 were allergists. Of the investigators, 15 (37.5%) were in private practice, 14 (35.0%) were affiliated with academic institutions, 8 (20.0%) worked at community health centers, and 10 (25.0%) were employed at other types of sites (see Table S2 in the electronic supplementary material for details).

Clinical Characteristics

Totals of 66 (17.8%) and 258 (69.5%) patients were classified as Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) treatment steps 4 and 5, respectively (n = 371) (Table 2). A small number of patients had mild asthma as defined by GINA steps 1 (n = 6; 1.6%) and 2 (n = 12; 3.2%); all but two of these patients had ongoing coexisting type 2 inflammatory conditions. A history of tobacco use was rare, with 312 (83.4%) patients reporting that they had never smoked. A total of 14 (3.7%) patients were current smokers at enrollment, with an additional 48 (12.8%) reporting that they had stopped smoking. The mean (SD) ACQ-6 score was 2.7 (1.34) (n = 336), suggesting that the overall population had poorly controlled asthma before initiating dupilumab. Spirometry data indicated that 38.0% of patients with available data had a normal percent predicted (pp) pre-bronchodilator forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1; i.e., pre-bronchodilator ppFEV1 > 80%). The mean (SD) pre-bronchodilator FEV1 and ppFEV1 were 2.2 L (0.83) (n = 306) and 72.7% (23.44) (n = 300), respectively. With regards to mean (SD) post-bronchodilator spirometry measures, FEV1 was 2.3 L (0.86) (n = 212) and ppFEV1 was 78.6% (25.07) (n = 209). The mean (SD) pre-bronchodilator and post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratios were 0.7 (0.13) (n = 301) and 0.8 (0.16) (n = 205), respectively. Other spirometry parameters are summarized in Table 2.

Type 2 inflammatory biomarkers were generally elevated among patients in REVEAL. Median (Q1–Q3) blood eosinophil counts were 390.0 (185.0–700.0) cells/µL (n = 348); 76.7% had blood eosinophil counts ≥ 150 cells/µL. Median (Q1–Q3) FeNO levels were 34.0 (19.0–60.0) parts per billion (ppb) (n = 314). FeNO levels ≥ 25 ppb were present in 65% of the population. Median (Q1–Q3) IgE levels were 247.6 (72.1–576.0) IU/mL (n = 293).

Asthma History

Details of the asthma history of patients enrolled in REVEAL are summarized in Table 3. Of the 373 patients with available data, 282 (75.6%) were diagnosed with asthma as adults; mean (SD) age at diagnosis was 29.8 (17.27) years. The mean (SD) number of asthma exacerbations in the year before enrolling in REVEAL was 3.5 (6.04), with a minimum of 0 exacerbations up to 65 exacerbations; seven patients reported more than 15 exacerbations in the year prior to enrollment with a range of 20 exacerbations to 65 exacerbations. Of these, the mean (SD) number of severe exacerbations was 2.0 (4.53) with a range of 0–60 exacerbations. For the 324 patients with available data, a mean (SD) of 7.2 (17.86) months had elapsed since the last asthma exacerbation. On average, patients were hospitalized for 2.8 days (SD = 8.07) (n = 369) in the year before enrolling in REVEAL. Asthma was burdensome in the 370 patients with available data; in the past year, asthma exacerbations caused patients to miss school or work for a mean (SD) of 12.2 (40.92) days or rendered them unable to perform activities of daily life for a mean (SD) of 24.5 (53.52) days.

Asthma Medications Used Before Enrollment and at Baseline

Table 4 shows a summary of the last asthma controller medications used by patients prior to dupilumab treatment and asthma controller medications used at baseline in addition to dupilumab administration. A total of 288 (77.0%) patients received at least one prior treatment for asthma in the three months before starting dupilumab. Of these, 64 (17.1%) received ICS alone as the last medication before they initiated dupilumab, whereas 104 (27.8%) received ICS in combination with LABA. Oral corticosteroids (OCS) were administered to 58 (15.5%) patients. A total of 11 patients (2.9%) had received biologics. Of these, seven received biologics targeting IL-5 or its receptor and four received biologics targeting IgE.

At baseline, background treatments were analyzed as asthma controller medications in addition to dupilumab. Most patients reported receiving ICS + LABA (n = 49; 13.1%) or ICS + LABA with add-on leukotriene receptor antagonists (LTRA) or long-acting muscarinic antagonists (LAMA) (n = 40; 10.7% for each). A total of 36 patients (9.6%) received ICS + LABA + LAMA + LTRA at baseline. Only six patients (1.6%) were reported to receive OCS alone at baseline. Eighty-nine patients (23.8%) did not receive any inhaled or oral baseline asthma therapy except for dupilumab.

Coexisting Type 2 Inflammatory Conditions

Rates and symptom burden of coexisting type 2 inflammatory diseases were high among the initial 374 patients enrolled in REVEAL (Tables 5 and 6). The most common coexisting type 2 inflammatory disease in this population was AR, with 242 patients (64.7%) reporting a history of AR. Of 210 patients with available data, mean (SD) AR-VAS score was 64.9 (30.89) on a 100-mm scale. Over two-thirds (67.1%) had AR-VAS scores > 50, indicating uncontrolled AR.

The second most prevalent coexisting type 2 inflammatory condition in this population was CRS with NP. A total of 231 patients (61.8%) reported a history of CRS with NP and 34 patients (9.1%) reported CRS without NP. The SNOT-22 questionnaire data were available for 229 patients with CRS with or without NP; mean (SD) score was 60.9 (23.03) with 27.9% and 66.8% of respondents receiving scores of > 20–50 (moderate impact on quality of life) and > 50 (severe impact on quality of life), respectively.

A history of AD was present in 51 patients (13.6%). Among the 43 patients with available patient-reported outcome data, mean (SD) POEM total score was 9.2 (8.06). The severity of AD varied, with 32.6% receiving POEM total scores of 0–2 points (clear/almost clear), 14.0% receiving scores of 3–7 points (mild), 32.6% receiving scores of 8–16 points (moderate), 16.3% receiving scores of 17–24 points (severe), and 4.7% receiving scores of 25–28 points (very severe).

Other type 2 inflammatory conditions reported were histories of hypersensitivity to nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (22.7%), allergic conjunctivitis (13.6%), food allergy (12.0%), hives/urticaria (9.1%), and eosinophilic esophagitis (1.1%).

Discussion

In this first report from the REVEAL clinical registry, we presented a detailed characterization of patients who initiated dupilumab for the treatment of asthma in routine clinical practice across Latin America, the Middle East, Mexico, Russian Federation, and Singapore. The REVEAL population comprised predominantly adult female patients as well as patients with obesity and evidence of severe, uncontrolled asthma. The disease burden at enrollment was high, as reflected by frequent exacerbations in the year prior to enrollment in REVEAL, a high prevalence of coexisting type 2 inflammatory diseases, and elevated markers of type 2 inflammation.

The safety and efficacy of dupilumab was evaluated as an add-on treatment for patients on medium- or high-dose ICS + LABA in clinical trials [14–18]. Surprisingly, 23.8% of the patients receiving dupilumab in REVEAL were prescribed dupilumab alone without any background therapies for the treatment of asthma. Whether this finding reflects an inconsistency in baseline asthma controller medication reporting between investigators or local prescribing practice is unknown. While it is likely that the high percentage of patients receiving dupilumab monotherapy can be explained by missing data, it is possible that other clinical scenarios reflecting real-world prescribing behavior may explain this surprising finding. First, ICS may have been contraindicated in some patients because of adverse effects, leading physicians to prescribe dupilumab without any background therapy. Second, ICS may have been discontinued in patients successfully treated with another biologic. If patients’ asthma worsened upon ICS withdrawal, physicians may have immediately ceased therapy and switched patients to dupilumab without reinitiating ICS.

The characteristics of patients initiating dupilumab for asthma in REVEAL are consistent with those observed in other severe asthma registries. The International Severe Asthma Registry (ISAR) is a large, global registry study that includes data from adults with asthma on GINA treatment steps 4 or 5 located in 28 countries, including many of the countries represented in REVEAL [29]. The demographic characteristics of 2116 patients from ISAR who initiated biologics for the treatment of asthma between May 2017 and October 2021 were similar to those of the REVEAL population [30]. Patients from ISAR who initiated biologics were predominantly female (62%) with a mean (SD) body mass index of 29.1 (7.0) kg/m2, although the mean (SD) age was slightly higher among the ISAR cohort at 53 (15) years relative to the REVEAL population. Unlike the ISAR group who initiated biologics for asthma, the REVEAL population had a more racially diverse population (50% white vs. 78% white in ISAR) and a substantially higher percentage of patients reporting no smoking history (83.4% vs. 62% in ISAR), which may reflect the demographic and cultural characteristics of the countries contributing the most patients to the REVEAL population. Baseline asthma history and disease characteristics were also similar between populations. Consistent with the REVEAL study, patients from ISAR initiating biologics had a mean (SD) age of asthma onset of 29 (19) years and a high burden of asthma exacerbations at baseline (mean [SD] 3.8 [4.0]). Seven patients in REVEAL reported more than 15 exacerbations in the year before enrollment, ranging from 20 to 65 exacerbations, which could represent exceptionally severe cases or reflect miscommunication on the definition of an asthma exacerbation or data entry errors. The effect of treatment with dupilumab on the high rates of exacerbations in these patients remains to be elucidated in the outcomes analysis of this registry. Lung function was slightly better in the REVEAL population, with mean (SD) absolute and percent predicted pre-bronchodilator FEV1 amounting to 1.9 (0.8) L and 70% (22), respectively, in patients from ISAR initiating biologics. Biomarkers of type 2 inflammation were also elevated in the ISAR cohort, with mean (SD) blood eosinophil counts of 598 (893) cells/µL and FeNO levels of 49 (46) ppb.

Demographic and clinical characteristics of patients with severe asthma from smaller registries in Singapore, Kuwait, and Latin America are also consistent with REVEAL, although the patients enrolled in the registries did not necessarily start dupilumab or other biologics for the treatment of asthma [30–33].

The safety and effectiveness of dupilumab in real-world clinical settings in North America, Europe, and Japan are also being evaluated in the ongoing RAPID clinical registry [22]. Although the registries enrolled patients from different geographic regions, the demographic and baseline clinical characteristics of patients initiating dupilumab for the treatment of asthma were generally similar between RAPID and REVEAL. Consistent with REVEAL, analysis of baseline data from the first 205 patients enrolled in RAPID showed that the population was predominantly female, with a high prevalence of obesity. As expected, most patients regardless of registry were on GINA treatment step 4 or 5 at enrollment. Interestingly, both studies included a small number of patients with mild asthma classified as GINA step 1 or 2 who were prescribed dupilumab. Most of the 18 patients in REVEAL with mild asthma reported having coexisting type 2 inflammatory conditions, viz., AD, AR, CRSsNP, or CRSwNP. These patients represent off-label prescription of dupilumab likely to treat their coexisting type 2 conditions. In some cases, it remains unknown whether patients with GINA-conforming low asthma severity received dupilumab or whether treatment severity was inaccurately characterized. This may reflect real-world prescribing practices, where treatment decisions consider coexisting type 2 inflammatory diseases or overall disease burden. Additionally, inconsistencies in reporting ICS use, symptoms, or exacerbations may have led to underestimation of asthma severity in some patients.

Patients in both RAPID and REVEAL showed evidence of poorly controlled asthma at baseline. Lung function parameters were also remarkably consistent between studies, with patients demonstrating generally acceptable lung function, as indicated by mean pre-bronchodilator ppFEV1 values exceeding 70%, and normal to mild lung obstruction, as indicated by pre-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratios exceeding 0.7. Evidence of type 2 inflammation was prevalent in RAPID and REVEAL and coexisting type 2 inflammatory diseases were also common in both populations. Although RAPID enrolled a higher proportion of patients with a history of coexisting AR (84.6% vs. 65.1%) and a lower proportion of patients with a history of CRS (41.5% vs. 70.5%) compared to REVEAL, the overall trends in coexisting type 2 inflammatory diseases were consistent between the two studies.

Real-world data on the safety and effectiveness of dupilumab are particularly valuable when collected from patients living in countries with historically limited representation in clinical trials. For example, in the phase 3 QUEST and VENTURE pivotal studies that evaluated the safety and efficacy of dupilumab in patients with moderate-to-severe asthma [15] and OCS-dependent severe asthma [16], respectively, no investigators were located in Middle Eastern countries. In REVEAL, nearly half (49.4%) of the population included in this analysis originated from Middle Eastern countries. This finding underscores the importance of real-world data and emphasizes its potential to complement clinical trial data. Improved characterization of patient populations around the world who are eligible for treatment with dupilumab will help physicians select the most appropriate therapies for their patients and may ultimately improve asthma outcomes in populations that have been underrepresented in clinical research to date.

The effectiveness of dupilumab will be evaluated in future analyses as the REVEAL study progresses. In addition to clinical parameters, such as exacerbation rates, lung function, patient-reported outcomes, and biomarker levels, healthcare utilization data will be collected. Real-word hospitalization rates may inform the overall burden of disease in REVEAL, especially in the context of global events, as has been shown elsewhere [34].

The limitations of the REVEAL study are consistent with its design as a non-interventional, observational registry. By nature, registry studies are conducted in a real-world clinical setting across a diverse range of sites and geographic regions, which introduces inherent variability. As a result, the data collected may be incomplete for certain baseline parameters because of differences in standard-of-care practices and patient preferences across countries. For example, in some countries participating in REVEAL, standard clinical practice does not include post-bronchodilator FEV1 testing. In others, post-bronchodilator spirometry is only completed if patients are determined to have clinically significant lung function abnormalities. Patients may also refuse bronchodilators due to fasting, side effects, or contraindications, or additional cost. In addition, some data points may not be verifiable against uniform clinical standards, as clinical documentation practices vary by site and region. Additionally, the lack of standardized assessments and potential differences in diagnostic criteria, patient follow-up frequency, or treatment approaches across sites and physician specialties may further contribute to heterogeneity in the dataset. These factors can impact the comparability of certain variables but are difficult to avoid in registry studies that are designed to capture real-world data.

Despite these limitations, the REVEAL registry offers valuable real-world insights into clinical practice and patient characteristics across a broad, international patient population. Our findings align with those from the RAPID registry and clinical trials, showing that patients initiating dupilumab for asthma typically present with uncontrolled, severe disease despite receiving maximum standard-of-care therapy. Real-world registries like REVEAL are essential complements to clinical trials, as they capture diverse patient populations and treatment patterns in routine practice, thereby enhancing the generalizability and applicability of clinical trial findings.

Conclusion

In this clinical registry of patients initiating dupilumab for the treatment of asthma, patients exhibited clinical characteristics consistent with severe disease. Many patients showed evidence of a substantial disease burden prior to receiving dupilumab, as indicated by a history of frequent exacerbations, high rates of coexisting conditions associated with type 2 inflammation, and elevated type 2 inflammatory biomarkers. REVEAL provides valuable insights into the characteristics of patients initiating dupilumab in real-world settings, particularly in countries that have been historically underrepresented in clinical trials. Taken together, these findings may help inform clinical decision-making and contribute to optimizing treatment for patients with uncontrolled asthma worldwide.
