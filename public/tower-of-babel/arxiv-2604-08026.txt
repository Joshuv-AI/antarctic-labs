Quasi-Compactness in Infinite Dimension
A. Bernhard Zeidler

Abstract
. We give extensive characterizations for an open subset of an affine space of arbitrary dimension, resp. of an inverse limit of prime spectra to be quasi-compact. Among other things weak stability, retro-compactness, and cylinder sets provide equivalent criteria in both settings. We also exhibit an example of a non–quasi-compact affine space.

1.
The main Results

In this article, we investigate quasi-compactness of open subsets
U
in two different
settings of inverse limits of topological spaces: in affine spaces
F^{L}
of arbitrary
dimension
L
over an algebraically closed field
F
on one hand, and in inverse
limits of prime spectra of noetherian rings on the other. Our main results (Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
) provide extensive lists of
equivalent conditions characterizing the quasi-compactness of
U
in these settings.

Among other things, we relate standard concepts and ideas from the theory of arc
spaces and motivic integration such as
weak stability
see e.g. Denef-Loeser

[
3
, Def. 2.4]
, and
cylinder sets
as used by Veys
[
7
, Def. 3.1]

and others. Furthermore, our characterizing condition (f) in Theorem
1.1

is a necessary requirement in the definition of
stable sets
as given in
Campesato-Fukui-Kurdyka-Parusiński
[
1
, Def. 3.33]
or Veys
[
7
, Def. 3.2]
.
Our aim is to present a unified, self-contained treatment and a consolidated survey.
Some implications we prove may already be known, but we were unable to locate appropriate
references.

We come to our first result. There and throughout the whole article, the affine
space
F^{L}
is endowed with the Zariski topology, that means that its closed sets
are common zero-loci of sets of polynomials of
F[t_{i}\mid i\in L]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L
be any set and
F
an algebraically closed field of cardinality
|L|<|F|
. For
I\subseteq\,L
let
\pi_{I}\colon F^{L}\,\twoheadrightarrow\,F^{I}
be the canonical
projection. Let
C\subseteq\,F^{L}
be a closed set,
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}:=\mathds{I}(C)
its vanishing
ideal in
F[t_{i}\mid i\in L]
and
U:=F^{L}\setminus C
its (open) complement.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\mathfrak{u}

is finitely generated.

(b)

U
is quasi-compact.

(c)

U
is a
cylinder set
, that is there is a finite subset
I\subseteq\,L
and
a constructible subset
W\subseteq\,F^{I}
such that
U
is the preimage
U=\pi_{I}^{-1}(W)
.

(d)

U
is
retro-compact
, that is for any open, quasi-compact subset
V\subseteq\,F^{L}
the intersection
U\cap V
is quasi-compact.

(e)

Given a set
\Lambda
and for any
\lambda\in\Lambda
a finite set
I(\lambda)\subseteq\,L
and an open set
A_{\lambda}\subseteq\,F^{I(\lambda)}
such that
U
is
covered by the open sets

U\ =\ \bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}W_{\lambda}\ \ \mbox{ where }\ \ W_{\lambda}\ :=\ \pi_{I(\lambda)}^{-1}\big(A_{\lambda}\big)

this cover admits a sub-cover consisting only of open base sets of a bounded level.
That is, for some finite set
K\subseteq\,L
we have

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda[K]}W_{\lambda}\ \ \mbox{ where }

\displaystyle\Lambda[K]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\exists\,B_{\lambda}\subseteq\,F^{K}\mbox{ open}\ :\ W_{\lambda}=\pi_{K}^{-1}\big(B_{\lambda})\right\}.

(f)

There is a finite subset
J\subseteq\,L
such that
U=\pi_{J}^{-1}\big(\pi_{J}(U)\big)
.

(g)

There is a finite subset
J\subseteq\,L
such that
C=\pi_{J}^{-1}\big(\pi_{J}(C)\big)
.

(h)

U
is
weakly stable
, that is there are a finite subset
K\subseteq\,L
and
an open subset
U_{K}\subseteq\,F^{K}
such that
U=\pi_{K}^{-1}\big(U_{K}\big)
.

(i)

There are a finite subset
K\subseteq\,L
and a closed subset
C_{K}\subseteq\,F^{K}

such that
C=\pi_{K}^{-1}\big(C_{K}\big)
.

In particular (under the assumptions of
1.1
) we find that
F^{L}

is quasi-compact, as it is weakly stable. The assumption that the cardinality of

F
exceeds that of
L
is needed in this theorem: In Example
4.1

we show that, when
F
is the field of algebraic numbers, the affine space
F^{\mathds{N}}

is not quasi-compact.

Note that
F^{L}
can be regarded as the inverse limit of the
F^{I}
, where
I
ranges
over the finite subsets of
L
, and the limit topology of the
F^{I}
coincides with the
Zariski topology of
F^{L}
. From this point of view, Theorem
1.1
is a
statement on inverse limits of finite-dimensional affine spaces
F^{n}
, in other words,
the maximal spectra of the polynomial rings
F[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
. If we consider the
prime spectrum of
F[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
instead, then we are in our second setting:
inverse limits of prime spectra of noetherian commutative rings; also see
Remark
4.5
for further discussion. Our second main result is
as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(I,\leq)
be a net,
\big(R_{i},\varphi_{i}^{j}\big)
a direct system of noetherian
commutative rings over
I
and
R_{\infty}
the associated direct limit. For
i\leq j\in I
denote
X_{i}:=\mbox{\rm{Spec}}(R_{i})
and
f_{j}^{i}:=\mbox{\rm{Spec}}(\varphi_{i}^{j})
. Denote by
X_{\infty}
the inverse limit of the system
\big(X_{i},f_{j}^{i}\big)
of topological spaces.
Then, for any open subset
U\subseteq\,X_{\infty}
, the following statements
are equivalent:

(a)

U
is quasi-compact.

(b)

U
is quasi-stable in the sense of Def.
3.3
.

(c)

U
is weakly stable in the sense of Def.
3.1
.

(d)

U
is retro-compact.

(e)

U
is a cylinder set in the sense of Def.
3.2
.

(f)

There is a quasi-finite ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}\unlhd R_{\infty}
, see
Def.
2.1
, such that
U=X_{\infty}\setminus\mathds{V}({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}})
.

The two main results are proven in Sections
2
and
3
,
respectively. Moreover, in the latter, we present Corollary
3.6
,
extending Corollaire 8.2.11 of
[
4
]
and, as an itermediate step in the proof of
Theorem
1.2
, we obtain Corollary
3.7
:
If a cylinder set is covered by an arbitrary number of cylinder sets, then it is the
union of finitely many of them. Note that this generalizes
[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
.
In the final Section
4
, we present examples that contrast
our two settings of inverse limits with each other.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

For any set
I
let us abbreviate the polynomial ring
F[I]:=F[t_{i}\mid i\in I]

in the variables
t_{i}
over
F
. For the proof we need to study ideals in the polynomial
ring
F[L]

F[L]=\varinjlim F[I]
of polynomial rings in
finitely many variables
I\in\mbox{$\mathfrak{I}$}:=\left\{I\subseteq\,L\mid I\mbox{ finite}\right\}

we start with an algebraic version of the compactness property:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(I,\leq)
be a net and
\big(R_{i},\varphi_{i}^{j}\big)
a direct system of
commutative rings rings over
I
. We abbreviate its direct limit, by
R_{\infty}
.
An ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}\unlhd R_{\infty}
is said to be
quasi-finite of level

, if it is of the form
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}R_{\infty}
for some ideal

{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\unlhd R_{i}
. And

\mathfrak{u}

is said to be
quasi-finite
, if it
is quasi-finite of some level
i\in I
.

Definition 2.2
.

We continue with the setting of
2.1
and regard an arbitrary set

\Lambda\not=\emptyset
and for any
\lambda\in\Lambda
some quasi-finite
ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}_{\lambda}\unlhd R_{\infty}
. For any
i\in I
let
\Lambda[i]

be the set of all indices
\lambda
of ideals
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}_{\lambda}
that are quasi-finite
of level
i
, that is

\Lambda[i]\ :=\ \left\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\exists\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{b}$}}_{i}\unlhd R_{i}\,:\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}_{\lambda}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{b}$}}_{i}R_{\infty}\right\}.

Then we will denote the following ideals (for any
i\in I
) of the direct limit

R_{\infty}

\displaystyle{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[i]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda[i]}\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}_{\lambda},

\displaystyle{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[\infty]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}_{\lambda}.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(I,\leq)
be a net,
\big(R_{i},\varphi_{i}^{j}\big)
be a direct system of
commutative rings over
I
and
R_{\infty}
its direct limit. Also let
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}_{\lambda}\unlhd R_{\infty}
be a family (where
\lambda\in\Lambda
) of quasi-finite
ideals. Then we get the following properties for the ideals defined above:

(i)

For any
i\leq j\in I
we have
\Lambda[i]\subseteq\,\Lambda[j]
and
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[i]\subseteq\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[j]
.

(ii)

{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[i]
is quasi-finite of level
i
and
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[\infty]
is the union
of the
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[i]
.

(iii)

For any ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}\unlhd R_{\infty}
we have the equivalence of (a)

\mathfrak{u}

is finitely generated and (b) there are some
k\in I
and some finitely generated
ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{b}$}}\unlhd R_{k}
such that
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{b}$}}R_{\infty}
. In
particular finitely generated implies quasi-finite.

(vi)

If
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[\infty]
is finitely generated, then
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[\infty]={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[m]

for some
m\in I
.

(v)

If
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{v}$}}\unlhd R_{\infty}
is some finitely generated ideal, then we obtain
the implication

\sqrt{{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[\infty]}\,=\,\sqrt{{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{v}$}}}\ \,\Longrightarrow\,\ \exists\,m\in I\ \colon\ \sqrt{{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[\infty]}\,=\,\sqrt{{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}[m]}.

.

\displaystyle=

∎

We continue with the situation of

is open and quasi-compact.

(b)

open and

Proof of
3.6
.

The implication (a)

(b)
\,\Longrightarrow\,
(a) only: For any

\mbox{$\mathfrak{B}$}_{i}:=\left\{B(a_{i})\mid a_{i}\in R_{i}\right\}
form a basis of the topology of

so as

is open there are

(where

By

.(i) we have
\pi_{i}^{-1}\big(B(a_{\lambda})\big)=s\big(B([i,a_{\lambda}])\big)

it is quasi-compact (this is true for all commutative rings).
And as

4.
Examples and Discussion

Example 4.1
.

For the field
F=\overline{\mathds{Q}}
of algebraic numbers, the topological space

F^{\mathds{N}}
is not quasi-compact. More generally, consider any countable, algebraically
closed field
F=\left\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\right\}
and the
F
-algebra epimorphism onto the
function field

\eta\colon\,F[t_{i}\mid i\in\mathds{N}]\,\twoheadrightarrow\,F(s)\ :\ t_{i}\,\mapsto\,\begin{cases}s,&\quad i=0,\\
\frac{1}{s-a_{i}},&\quad i\geq 1.\end{cases}

Note that surjectivity is guaranteed by
F
being algebraically closed. As
F(s)

is a field,
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}:=\mbox{\rm{ker}}(\eta)
is a maximal ideal in
F[t_{i}\mid i\in\mathds{N}]
. Consider its restrictions

{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{p}$}}_{i}\ :=\ {\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\cap F[t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{i}]\ \subseteq\ F[t_{0},\ldots,t_{i}].

As

\mathfrak{m}

is a proper ideal, each
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{p}$}}_{i}
is so, too. Hence
\mathds{V}({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{p}$}}_{i})\subseteq\,F^{I}
is non-empty, by the weak Nullstellensatz. For
I=\{0,\ldots,i\}
consider
the projection
\pi_{i}\colon F^{\mathds{N}}\to F^{I}
. Then the following closed set is
non-empty, as well:

C_{i}\ :=\ \pi_{i}^{-1}\Big(\mathds{V}({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{p}$}}_{i})\Big)\ \subseteq\,\ F^{\mathds{N}}.

By construction,
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{p}$}}_{i}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\cap F[t_{0},\ldots,t_{i}]\subseteq\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\cap F[t_{0},\ldots,t_{i},t_{i+1}]={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{p}$}}_{i+1}
, such that
\mathds{V}({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{p}$}}_{i+1})\subseteq\,\mathds{V}({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{p}$}}_{i})
and hence
C_{i+i}\subseteq\,C_{i}
. That is, the
C_{i}
form a descending
chain of closed sets. We claim

C\ :=\ \bigcap_{i\geq 1}C_{i}\ =\ \emptyset.

Suppose there was an
x
in
C
. Then
x=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)\in F^{\mathds{N}}
and, as

x_{0}\in F
, we have
x_{0}=a_{k}
for some
k\geq 1
. Consider the polynomial

f_{k}\ :=\ (t_{0}-a_{k})t_{k}-1\ \in\ {\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\cap F[t_{0},\ldots,t_{k}]\ =\ {\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{p}$}}_{k}.

Then
f_{k}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k})=-1\not=0
, hence
x\not\in C_{k}
; a contradiction.
Consequently
C
is empty. Let
U_{i}:=F^{\mathds{N}}\setminus C_{i}
be the complement
of
C_{i}
. By the above, we obtain

\bigcup_{i\geq 1}U_{i}\ =\ \bigcup_{i\geq 1}F^{\mathds{N}}\setminus C_{i}\ =\ F^{\mathds{N}}\setminus\bigcap_{i\geq 1}C_{i}\ =\ F^{\mathds{N}}.

Thus the
U_{i}
form an open cover of
F^{\mathds{N}}
. Moreover, as the
C_{i}
form a
descending chain, the
U_{i}
form an ascending chain. If
F^{\mathds{N}}
were quasi-compact,
then we would have
F^{\mathds{N}}=U_{m}
for some
m\in\mathds{N}
. Since
C_{m}\neq\emptyset
,
this would lead to a contradiction:

F^{\mathds{N}}\ =\ U_{m}\ =\ F^{\mathds{N}}\setminus C_{m}\ \neq\ F^{\mathds{N}}.

Remark 4.2
.

One can turn
F^{\mathds{N}}
into another topological space by regarding it not as the

inverse
limit
\varprojlim F^{n}
, but as the
direct
limit
\varinjlim F^{n}
,
where
F^{n}\subseteq\,F^{n+1}
is defined by
x_{n+1}=0
. Then
F^{\mathds{N}}
is a basic
example of an ind-scheme and will never be quasi-compact, as the inclusions
F^{n}\subset F^{n+1}
are strict; see
[
6
, Lem. 1.22]
.

Drawing on Example
4.1
, we point out limits on generalizing
Chevalley’s classical theorem: morphisms of finite type between noetherian schemes
map constructible sets to constructible sets.

Example 4.3
.

For any countable algebraically closed field
F=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\}
, consider the
inclusion of polynomial rings
F[t_{1}]\subseteq F[t_{i}\mid i\in\mathds{N}]
and let

\pi\colon X_{\infty}\to X_{1}
be the corresponding morphism of the associated
prime spectra. As in Example
4.1
, look at the epimorphism

\eta\colon\,F[t_{i}\mid i\in\mathds{N}]\,\twoheadrightarrow\,F(s)\ :\ t_{i}\,\mapsto\,\begin{cases}s,&\quad i=0,\\
\frac{1}{s-a_{i}},&\quad i\geq 1\end{cases}

and the maximal ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}:=\mbox{\rm{ker}}(\eta)
in
F[t_{i}\mid i\in\mathds{N}]
.
We claim
\pi({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}})=0
. Indeed,
f\in{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\cap F[t_{1}]
means
f\in F[t_{1}]
and
\eta(f)=0
. The latter is
f\big(1/(s-a_{1})\big)=0
.
But as
\{1/(s-a_{1})\}\subseteq\,F(s)
is algebraically independent over
F
, this
implies
f=0
.

As

\mathfrak{m}

is maximal,
\left\{{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\right\}\subseteq\,X_{\infty}
is closed and finite,
hence constructible. The only constructible sets of
X_{1}=\mbox{\rm{Spec}}\big(F[t_{1}]\big)

are the closed and the open sets. However,
\pi(\left\{{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\right\})=\left\{0\right\}\subseteq\,X_{1}
is neither closed (it contains the generic point) nor open (it is not cofinite)
and hence not constructible.

Recall the situation of Theorem
1.2
,
X_{\infty}
is the
inverse limit of prime spectra of noetherian rings. If
U\subseteq\,X_{\infty}
is an
open, quasi-compact set, then
U=\pi_{k}^{-1}(A)
for some
k\in I
and

A\subseteq\,X_{k}
open. In particular
U=\pi_{k}^{-1}\big(\pi_{k}(U)\big)
,
that is
U
satisfies the stability condition (f) in affine spaces, as given in

1.1
. The following example points out that quasi-compactness
and this stability condition cannot be equivalent, for prime spectra of general
commutative rings.

Example 4.4
.

Let
S:=\mathds{C}(t_{0},t_{2},t_{4},\dots)[t_{1},t_{3},t_{5},\dots]
. For any
i\in\mathds{N}
we
may regard
R_{i}:=\mathds{C}(t_{0},t_{2},\dots,t_{2i})[t_{j}\mid j\in\mathds{N}\setminus\left\{0,2,\dots,2i\right\}]
as a subring of
S
. These rings form a direct system, as
R_{i}\subseteq\,R_{i+1}
canonically and
S=R_{\infty}
is the associate direct limit.

S
contains the maximal ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}:=\langle t_{1},t_{3},t_{5},\dots\rangle
. We let
C:=\left\{{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\right\}
and
U=X_{\infty}\setminus C
. Then
U
is open, but
not

quasi-compact, as

\mathfrak{m}

contains infinitely many variables
t_{j}
. As
S
can
be seen as a localization of
R_{i}
, the canonical projections
\pi_{i}:X_{\infty}\to X_{i}
are injective. Thereby we have (for any
i\in\mathds{N}
)

U\;\;=\;\;\pi_{i}^{-1}\big(\pi_{i}(U)\big).

Remark 4.5
.

Let
F
be an algebraically closed field,
L
a set and
\mathfrak{I}
be the set of
all finite subsets of
L
. We summarize key facts showing that inverse limits
of maximal spectra and of prime spectra can exhibit different quasi-compactness
properties:

(1)

The affine space
F^{L}
is the inverse limit of
F^{I}
where
I\in\mbox{$\mathfrak{I}$}
.
Hereby
F^{I}
identifies with the maximal spectrum of
F[I]
.

(2)

F^{L}
is quasi-compact when
|L|<|F|
according to Theorem
1.1
,
but need not be so, if
|L|=|F|
, see Example
4.1
.

(3)

Likewise the prime spectrum
\mbox{\rm{Spec}}\big(F[L]\big)
is the inverse limit of the
prime spectra
\mbox{\rm{Spec}}\big(F[I]\big)
where
I\in\mbox{$\mathfrak{I}$}
, due to
Lemma
3.4
.

(4)

\mbox{\rm{Spec}}\big(F[L]\big)
always is quasi-compact, this is true for any commutative
ring.
