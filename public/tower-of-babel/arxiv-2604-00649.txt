Baryogenesis in S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L SU(2)_{L} multiplet models
Kiyoto Ogawa, Masanori Tanaka

Abstract
We investigate baryogenesis in Standard Model (SM) extensions with new S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L SU(2)_{L} multiplet fields. We focus on sphalerogenesis, in which the baryon asymmetry of the Universe (BAU) is generated through the gradual decoupling of CP-violating electroweak (EW) sphaleron-like processes. We show that the observed BAU can be reproduced when the new fields possess CP-violating Yukawa interactions, which leave a CP-violating dimension-six operator involving the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L SU(2)_{L} gauge fields at low energies. As representative examples, we study models with fermionic S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L SU(2)_{L} quintuplets and septuplets, and find that these field masses should be 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) ​ TeV \mathcal{O}(1)\,\mathrm{TeV} to explain the BAU. We also show that viable parameter regions for the BAU are consistent with current bounds on the electron electric dipole moment and thoroughly probed by future measurements such as ACME III and by mono-lepton searches at the HL-LHC. Our results provide a concrete and phenomenologically testable ultraviolet completion of sphalerogenesis.



I
Introduction

The origin of the baryon asymmetry of the universe (BAU) remains one of the central questions in particle cosmology.
Measurements of the cosmic microwave background strongly constrain the baryon-to-entropy ratio as
4

\displaystyle 8.41\times 10^{-11}<\frac{n_{B}}{s}<8.75\times 10^{-11}\,.

(1)

If the universe started from a baryon-symmetric initial state, a mechanism satisfying the Sakharov conditions is required to reproduce the observed baryon asymmetry in Eq. (
1
)
58
.
The Sakharov conditions consist of three requirements: (i) baryon number violation, (ii) C and CP violation, and (iii) departure from thermal equilibrium.

To satisfy the third Sakharov condition, the dynamics of the electroweak (EW) phase transition is often invoked, as in EW baryogenesis
44
.
However, lattice simulations have shown that the EW phase transition in the Standard Model (SM) is a smooth crossover for the observed Higgs boson mass
38
;
23
;
7
;
25
;
26
.
Therefore, the SM cannot realize a first-order EW phase transition, motivating physics beyond the SM to provide the required departure from thermal equilibrium.

On the other hand, a new possibility for baryogenesis during smooth EW symmetry breaking was proposed
40
;
36
;
59
.
In this scenario, the Sakharov conditions are satisfied solely through EW sphaleron dynamics: (i) baryon number violation by EW sphaleron processes, (ii) CP asymmetry in EW sphaleron transitions, and (iii) departure from equilibrium through the gradual decoupling of sphaleron-like processes.
This mechanism is called sphalerogenesis
59
.
In Ref.
40
, the CP asymmetry in the sphaleron process was estimated from interactions between the EW gauge fields in the sphaleron configuration and fermions emitted via sphaleron explosions.
Using this result, it was found that sphalerogenesis within the SM produces a baryon asymmetry about three orders of magnitude smaller than the observed value in Eq. (
1
)
40
;
36
.
This indicates that sphalerogenesis does not work within the SM alone, and naturally leads to the question: what kind of new physics can make this mechanism viable?

In Ref.
59
, it was shown that sphalerogenesis is feasible in the presence of a dimension-six operator involving the
SU(2)_{L}
gauge fields in Eq. (
5
), called the EW-Weinberg operator, which acts as a source of CP asymmetry in the sphaleron process.
In that analysis, the CP asymmetry was estimated by focusing on the difference in sphaleron transition rates along the positive and negative Chern-Simons number directions, instead of relying on sphaleron explosion dynamics.
While the feasibility of sphalerogenesis was established within an effective field theoretical framework, it remains unclear what ultraviolet-complete theories can realize this scenario.

In this paper, we consider SM extensions with new
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet fields
9
;
10
, which we refer to as the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet models.
Such extensions have been widely discussed because the neutral component of a
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet can serve as dark matter (DM) candidate
20
;
21
;
22
.
In the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet model, Yukawa interactions among the new multiplet fields can contain a CP-violating phase.
After integrating out the new particles, this CP violation induces the EW-Weinberg operator, which in turn generates CP asymmetry in the EW sphaleron process
9
.
Moreover, the same source of CP violation can be probed by current and future measurements of the electron electric dipole moment (EDM), such as ACME II
5
, JILA
57
, and ACME III
35
.
Recently, the electron EDM in the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet models was evaluated at the full three-loop level, and it was shown that these models can be tested in upcoming EDM experiments even when the new particle mass scale is
\mathcal{O}(1)\,\mathrm{TeV}

10
.

We show that the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet models can provide a viable realization of sphalerogenesis while remaining consistent with the current stringent bound from the electron EDM measurement
57
as well as LHC search bounds
55
;
48
;
49
.
On the other hand, we find that the conventional freeze-out dark matter scenario is difficult to reconcile with sphalerogenesis, because successful baryogenesis prefers relatively light
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet fields.
This indicates that explaining the baryon asymmetry and dark matter simultaneously in this framework requires a non-freeze-out mechanism for generating the dark matter relic abundance.

Electroweak sphaleron configuration

We briefly review the derivation of the sphaleron transition action, which is the basis for estimating the CP asymmetry in the EW sphaleron process.
We consider the bosonic action

\displaystyle S=S_{\rm SM}+S_{\rm CP}\,.

(2)

III
Estimation of CP asymmetry in EW sphaleron transition

We then estimate the CP asymmetry in the EW sphaleron process using the effective action introduced in the previous section.
According to Eq. (
14
), the corresponding effective Lagrangian in terms of
Q
is

\displaystyle L=\frac{M(Q)}{2}\left(\frac{dQ}{dt}\right)^{2}+G(Q)\left(\frac{dQ}{dt}\right)^{3}-V(Q)\,.

(21)

In order to move on to the Hamilton formalism, we define the canonical conjugate momentum of
Q
as

\displaystyle\pi_{Q}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{Q}}=M(Q)\dot{Q}+3G(Q)\dot{Q}^{2}\,.

(22)

The corresponding Hamiltonian is then given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{2M(Q)}\pi_{Q}^{2}-\frac{G(Q)}{M^{3}(Q)}\pi_{Q}^{3}+V(Q)\,.

(23)

Because the effective Hamiltonian contains the cubic term
\pi_{Q}^{3}
, the kinematics for positive and negative
\pi_{Q}
are no longer identical.
Equivalently, the cubic term induces a difference between the sphaleron transition rates toward positive and negative Chern-Simons numbers.
This asymmetry is the origin of CP violation in the EW sphaleron process.

To quantify the CP asymmetry relevant for baryogenesis, we focus on the probability current associated with the sphaleron-like transition.
This is different from the equilibrium diffusion discussed in Refs.
52
;
51
, where the net probability current vanishes.
When sphaleron-like configurations are decoupled from the thermal bath, their subsequent evolution is no longer balanced by the inverse process.
Thus, it is natural to characterize the CP asymmetry by the difference between the probability currents along the positive and negative
Q
directions.
We define the corresponding probability current at the saddle-point
\pm Q_{\rm sph}=\pm\pi/(2gv)
along the positive and negative
Q
directions by
3

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\pm}(Q_{\rm sph})=\frac{1}{Z_{0}}\iint d\pi_{Q}\,dQ\;\pi_{Q}\,e^{-\mathcal{H}(Q,\pi_{Q})/T}\,\Theta(\pm\pi_{Q})\,\delta(Q\mp Q_{\rm sph})\,,

(24)

where
Z_{0}
is a normalization factor.

\Theta(x)
and
\delta(x)
denote the step and Dirac delta functions, respectively.
The step function is inserted to remove the contribution of inverse sphaleron-like transitions.
Evaluating the integral for
Q
and expanding to the linear order in the cubic term because of
G(Q_{\rm sph})\ll T,\,M(Q_{\rm sph})
, one finds

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\pm}(Q_{\rm sph})\simeq\frac{1}{Z_{0}}e^{-V(Q_{\rm sph})/T}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\pi_{Q}\,\pi_{Q}\,\left(1\pm\frac{G(Q_{\rm sph})}{M(Q_{\rm sph})^{3}}\frac{\pi_{Q}^{3}}{T}\right)\exp\left[-\frac{\pi_{Q}^{2}}{2M(Q_{\rm sph})T}\right]\,.

(25)

One may be concerned with the dynamical instability of the system due to the cubic term in Eq. (
23
) at the large
\pi_{Q}
region.
However, since the thermal averaged
\pi_{Q}
is roughly given by
\braket{\pi_{Q}}\sim\sqrt{TM(Q_{\rm sph})}
, the second term in Eq. (
23
) is significantly smaller than the first term.
Therefore, we expect that such a dynamical instability does not appear, and the above approximation is plausible.

We here assume that the CP asymmetry in EW sphaleron processes can be quantified by

\displaystyle A_{\rm CP}\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{+}(Q_{\rm sph})-\Gamma_{-}(Q_{\rm sph})}{\Gamma_{+}(Q_{\rm sph})+\Gamma_{-}(Q_{\rm sph})}\,.

(26)

Using Eq. (
24
) and performing Gauss integrals, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{\rm CP}=\frac{3\pi}{4}\sqrt{\frac{8T}{\pi}}\frac{G(Q_{\rm sph})}{M^{3/2}(Q_{\rm sph})}\,.

(27)

Compared with the result in Ref.
59
, the CP asymmetry in the EW sphaleron processes is enhanced by the additional factor
3\pi/4
.

Our derivation of the effective CP asymmetry relevant for baryogenesis is subject to several sources of theoretical uncertainty.
In particular, our results include uncertainties related to the reduced model, such as the treatment of the effective kinetic term
M(\mu)

30
;
60
, the use of time-independent profile functions
f(\xi)
and
h(\xi)
(see Ref.
47
), and the limitation of the reduced model description itself.
Some of these uncertainties can already modify the predicted asymmetry at the level of a factor of order unity.
For example, different reduced constructions of the sphaleron action lead to different effective masses
M(Q)
, which in turn induce an
O(1)
variation in the CP asymmetry because of
A_{\rm CP}\propto M^{-3/2}(Q)

30
;
60
.
A fully systematic treatment of all these effects requires a time-dependent and multidimensional analysis of the sphaleron decoupling dynamics, which is beyond the scope of the present work.
Instead, we absorb their effects into a single phenomenological factor
\kappa_{\rm CP}
, and write the effective CP asymmetry in sphaleron processes as

\displaystyle A_{\rm CP}^{\rm eff}=\kappa_{\rm CP}A_{\rm CP}\,.

(28)

In the following, we assume that
A_{\rm CP}^{\rm eff}
quantifies the CP asymmetry in the sphaleron-like transition entering the source term of the Boltzmann equation.
For the value of
\kappa_{\rm CP}
, we mainly focus on the following region in this paper

\displaystyle 0.5\leq\kappa_{\rm CP}\leq 3\,.

Sphalerogenesis with the EW-Weinberg operator

IV.1
Sphaleron-like transition rate

In this section, we briefly review the mechanism of sphalerogenesis discussed in Ref.

In this scenario, the sphaleron-like transition rate plays a central role.
Following Ref.

V
Application to the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet models

V.1
EW-Weinberg operator in the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet models

We now discuss the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet model as a representaive ultraviolet-complete model that generates the EW-Weinberg operator in Eq. (
5
) at low energies.
The following discussion is based on Refs.
9
;
10
.

We consider SM extensions with new
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet fields,
\psi_{A}
,
\psi_{B}
, and
S
, together with CP-violating Yukawa interactions.
The relevant interaction terms are

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\ni-\bar{\psi}_{B}g_{\bar{B}AS}\psi_{A}S-\bar{\psi}_{A}g_{\bar{A}B\bar{S}}\psi_{B}S^{*}\,,

(41)

where
\psi_{A}
and
\psi_{B}
are fermions, and
S
is a complex scalar, each transforming in each representation of the
SU(2)_{L}
gauge group.
The Yukawa couplings are written as

\displaystyle g_{\bar{B}AS}

\displaystyle=X_{\bar{B}AS}(s+\gamma_{5}a)\,,

(42)

\displaystyle g_{\bar{A}B\bar{S}}

\displaystyle=X_{\bar{A}B\bar{S}}(s^{*}-\gamma_{5}a^{*})\,,

(43)

where
s
and
a
are in general complex parameters.
The factors
X_{\bar{B}AS}
and
X_{\bar{A}B\bar{S}}
are
SU(2)_{L}
-invariant tensors determined by the representations of the new fields, and their explicit forms are given in Ref.
9
.
This setup generates the EW-Weinberg operator at the two-loop level
9
;
2
.

For simplicity, we focus on the case in which the new fields transform as
(A,B,S)=(r,r,1)
under the
SU(2)_{L}
gauge group, where
r
denotes an arbitrary representation.
In this case,
X_{\bar{B}AS}
and
X_{\bar{A}B\bar{S}}
reduce to Kronecker delta
\delta^{a_{r}b_{r}}
, where its indices are related to the representation of the fermions
A
and
B
.
Then the cutoff scale
\Lambda
in Eq. (
5
) is given by
2
;
9

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}=\frac{\alpha_{2}\,r(r^{2}-1)}{128\pi^{3}}\operatorname{Im}(sa^{*})\,m_{A}m_{B}\,F(m_{A}^{2},m_{B}^{2},m_{S}^{2})\,,

(44)

with

\displaystyle F(m_{A}^{2},m_{B}^{2},m_{S}^{2})\equiv g_{1}(m_{A}^{2},m_{B}^{2},m_{S}^{2})+g_{2}(m_{A}^{2},m_{B}^{2},m_{S}^{2})+(m_{A}\leftrightarrow m_{B})\,,

(45)

where
\alpha_{2}=g^{2}/(4\pi)
and
(m_{A}\leftrightarrow m_{B})
denotes the exchange of the two fermion masses in
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
.
The functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are combinations of two-loop functions:

\displaystyle g_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=\left(2\bar{I}_{(4;1)}+4x_{1}\bar{I}_{(5;1)}\right)(x_{1};x_{2};x_{3})\,,

(46)

\displaystyle g_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{I}_{(3;2)}+x_{1}\bar{I}_{(4;2)}\right)(x_{1};x_{2};x_{3})\,,

(47)

where
\bar{I}_{(n;m)}
is defined in terms of the finite master integral
\bar{I}(x_{1};x_{2};x_{3})
as

\displaystyle\bar{I}_{(n;m)}(x_{1};x_{2};x_{3})=\frac{1}{(n-1)!(m-1)!}\frac{d^{n-1}}{dx_{1}^{n-1}}\frac{d^{m-1}}{dx_{2}^{m-1}}\bar{I}(x_{1};x_{2};x_{3})\,.

(48)

Analytic expressions for
\bar{I}(x_{1};x_{2};x_{3})
can be found in Refs.
28
;
27
;
46
.
The factor
r(r^{2}-1)/12
in Eq. (
44
) arises from the
SU(2)_{L}
group structure,

\displaystyle\Tr\left[T^{3}[T^{+},T^{-}]\right]=\frac{r(r^{2}-1)}{12}\,,

(49)

where
T^{a}~(a=1,2,3)
are the generators of
SU(2)_{L}
and
T^{\pm}=T^{1}\pm iT^{2}
.
In general, CP-violating observables must be invariant under field redefinitions.
Eq. (
44
) respects this property because the combination
\operatorname{Im}(sa^{*})m_{A}m_{B}
, arising from the
\gamma_{5}
structure in the fermion trace together with two chirality-flipping mass insertions, is rephasing invariant
10
;
11
.

Combining Eq. (
44
) with Eq. (
40
), we can identify the parameter region capable of explaining the observed BAU.
Before doing so, however, we first discuss the electron EDM constraint in this model.

V.2
Electron electric dipole moment with
SU(2)_{L}
multiplets

We briefly review the full-loop result for the electron EDM in the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet models, following Ref.
10
.

The electron EDM induced through the EW-Weinberg operator can be estimated by combining Eqs. (
39
) and (
44
).
An important point is that the matching between the EW-Weinberg operator and the electron EDM does not receive logarithmic enhancement from renormalization-group running, because the corresponding anomalous dimension vanishes
37
.
Therefore, this contribution should be regarded as a threshold correction in the low-energy effective theory below the EW scale, and it enters at the same order as the lepton dipole operator treated within the SMEFT framework.

In Ref.
10
, the electron EDM induced by the CP-violating Yukawa interactions in Eq. (
41
) was evaluated at the full three-loop level, including the contribution of the lepton dipole operator.
The resulting EDM is

\displaystyle\frac{d^{\rm Full}_{e}}{e}=\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}}{(16\pi^{2})^{2}}\frac{m_{e}}{2}\frac{r(r^{2}-1)}{12}\operatorname{Im}(sa^{*})m_{A}m_{B}B(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S},m_{W})\,,

(50)

where
B(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S},m_{W})
denotes a combination of three-loop integrals.
Eq. (
50
) is invariant under field redefinitions for the same reason as Eq. (
44
).
When the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet fields are heavier than the
W
boson,
B(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S},m_{W})
can be expanded as

\displaystyle B(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S},m_{W})\simeq B_{0}(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S})+m^{2}_{W}B_{1}(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{m^{4}_{W}}{M^{4}}\right)\,,

(51)

where
M
denotes the typical heavy-particle mass scale, namely that of
\psi_{A/B}
or
S
.
Explicit expressions for
B_{0}(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S})
and
B_{1}(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S})
are given in Ref.
10
.

Let us compare the full result with the contribution induced only through the EW-Weinberg operator in the special case
m_{A}=m_{B}=m_{S}
.
The full three-loop result is
10

\displaystyle\frac{d_{e}^{\rm Full}}{e}=\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}m_{e}}{(16\pi^{2})^{2}}\frac{r(r^{2}-1)}{12}\operatorname{Im}(sa^{*})\left(\frac{0.41}{m_{A}^{2}}+\frac{0.22m^{2}_{W}}{m_{A}^{4}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{m_{W}^{4}}{m_{A}^{4}}\right)\,,

(52)

where the first term comes from
B_{0}(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S})
and the second from
B_{1}(m_{A},m_{B},m_{S})
in Eq. (
50
).
On the other hand, the contribution mediated only by the EW-Weinberg operator is given by
9

\displaystyle\frac{d^{\rm EFT}_{e}}{e}=\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}m_{e}}{(16\pi^{2})^{2}}\frac{r(r^{2}-1)}{12}\operatorname{Im}(sa^{*})\frac{0.14}{m_{A}^{2}}\,.

(53)

Hence, in the large
m_{A}
limit, we obtain
d_{e}^{\rm Full}\simeq 3d_{e}^{\rm EFT}
.
This relation remains valid even when the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet masses are not degenerate.
As a result, the electron EDM constraint in
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet models is significantly stronger than the one shown in Fig.
2(b)
.
Although Eq. (
40
) gives the cutoff range required to explain the BAU within the SMEFT description, the lower bound increases to
57\,{\rm TeV}
once the full result
d_{e}^{\rm Full}\simeq 3d_{e}^{\rm EFT}
is used.
Thus, the viable BAU window is significantly compressed in the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet models.

VI
Discussions and Conclusions

We have studied the feasibility of sphalerogenesis in the SM extension with new
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet fields.
We have focused on the scenario in which the CP asymmetry in the EW sphaleron process is induced by the EW-Weinberg operator generated from CP-violating Yukawa interactions of the new multiplets.
Using the reduced description of sphaleron-like processes together with the lattice-based sphaleron-like transition rate, we have estimated the baryon asymmetry produced during the gradual decoupling of CP-violating sphaleron-like processes.
We then applied this framework to the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet model and identified the parameter regions in which the observed baryon asymmetry can be reproduced.

Our main results are summarized in Fig.
4
.
It shows that viable parameter regions remain consistent with current constraints from the electron EDM measurement and collider searches.
At the same time, these regions are expected to be thoroughly tested in the near future by improved electron EDM measurements, such as ACME III, as well as by mono-lepton searches at the HL-LHC.
Therefore, the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet models provide a concrete and phenomenologically testable ultraviolet realization of sphalerogenesis.

Finally, we comment on the implications of our results for DM phenomenology.
If the
SU(2)_{L}
multiplet contains a neutral component, it can be a DM candidate
20
;
21
;
22
.
When the relic abundance is determined by the conventional freeze-out mechanism, the multiplet fields are typically required to be heavier than
\mathcal{O}(10)\,{\rm TeV}
for
r\geq 5

50
;
14
;
15
;
12
.
However, Fig.
4
shows that successful sphalerogenesis favors higher
SU(2)_{L}
representations, such as
r\geq 5
, with
\mathcal{O}(1)\,{\rm TeV}
fermions.
This reveals a clear tension between the BAU-favored region and the conventional freeze-out DM scenario.
Therefore, explaining both the observed baryon asymmetry and the DM relic abundance within our framework likely requires a non-freeze-out origin of DM.
We leave such an intriging direction as a future work.
