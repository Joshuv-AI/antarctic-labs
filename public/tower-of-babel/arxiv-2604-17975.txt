Localisation of 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(2,2) theories on spindles of both twists
Imtak Jeon a, Hyojoong Kim b, Nakwoo Kim c, Aaron Poole c, and Augniva Ray b

Abstract
We consider two-dimensional 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(2,2) supersymmetric field theories living on a spindle 𝕎 ​ ℂ ​ ℙ [ n 1 , n 2 ] 1 \mathbb{WCP}_{[n_{1},n_{2}]}^{1} . Starting from the spindle solutions of five-dimensional STU gauged supergravity, we construct theories on a spindle which preserve supersymmetry via either the twist or anti-twist mechanism and admit two Killing spinors of opposite R-charge. While the study of field theories on anti-twisted spindles has already been undertaken in some detail, the advantage of our approach allows for the derivation of analogous results in the twist case. We apply the technique of supersymmetric localisation to compute the exact partition function for a theory consisting of an abelian vector multiplet and a charged chiral multiplet in the presence of a Fayet-Iliopoulos term. We compare and contrast the results for the twisted and anti-twisted spindle and find a general formula which encompasses the partition function for both cases simultaneously.

We start with the presentation of the Killing spinor equations for the STU model in our conventions:

In order to demonstrate the proof of concept of localisation on the spindle, we will consider a simple case of a supersymmetric action on

4.1
BPS equations

In this section we consider the process of supersymmetric localisation
Duistermaat:1982vw
;
Atiyah:1984px
;
Witten:1988ze
;
Witten:1988xj
;
Nekrasov:2002qd
;
Pestun:2007rz
;
Cremonesi:2013twh
on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
in earnest. We follow the general argument as outlined in section 4.1 of
Jeon:2025rfc
and the problem of determining the localisation locus of the theory amounts to solving the BPS equations

\lambda=\widetilde{\lambda}=\psi=\widetilde{\psi}=0\,,\quad Q_{eq}\lambda=Q_{eq}\widetilde{\lambda}=Q_{eq}\psi=Q_{eq}\widetilde{\psi}=0\,.

(99)

These equations can be formulated as the equations of motion arising from an action which is a
Q_{eq}
-exact deformation of the classical action (
91
). As in
Jeon:2025rfc
, we take the vector multiplet deformation to be

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}^{\text{v.m.}}\bigg|_{\text{bos.}}=2\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\,\epsilon^{\dagger}\epsilon\bigg[&(D_{\mu}\rho)^{2}+\left(\mathcal{F}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}\rho\right)^{2}\\
&+\left(D_{\mu}\sigma+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{G}\sigma\frac{\epsilon^{\dagger}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{\mu}\epsilon}{\epsilon^{\dagger}\epsilon}\right)^{2}+\left(D+\mathcal{G}\sigma\frac{\epsilon^{\dagger}\gamma_{3}\epsilon}{\epsilon^{\dagger}\epsilon}\right)^{2}\bigg]\,,\end{split}

(100)

which, as in the purely anti-twisted case considered in
Jeon:2025rfc
, is postive-definite. One can verify this via (
60
), where
\mathcal{H}
is imaginary and
\mathcal{G}
is real, making the sum-of-squares above non-negative assuming the reality condition (
290
). The equations of motion arising from such an action are thus simply the vanishing of all squared terms above and correspond to the BPS equations
Q_{eq}\lambda=Q_{eq}\widetilde{\lambda}=0
. Using (
60
) and (
62
), we solve the vanishing of the first two squares in (
100
) to be

\rho=\rho_{0}=\textrm{const.}\,,\quad\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}\rho_{0}=-\frac{2\rho_{0}}{3LH^{1/2}}\sum_{I=1}^{3}X^{(I)}q_{I}\,,

(101)

while
solving the third square for
\sigma
we obtain the equation

\partial_{\mu}\sigma-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{3L}\sigma\frac{\epsilon^{\dagger}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{\mu}\epsilon}{\epsilon^{\dagger}\epsilon}\sum_{I=1}^{3}X^{(I)}=0\,.

(102)

Assuming
\sigma=\sigma(y)
, the
z
-component of this equation is solved automatically and using (
60
) and (
62
), the
y
-component becomes

\sigma^{\prime}(y)+\frac{H^{\prime}}{6H}\sigma(y)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sigma(y)=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Delta z}\sigma_{0}\right)H^{-1/6}\,,

(103)

where
\sigma_{0}
is a (real) constant and the
2\pi/\Delta z
scaling is chosen in order to avoid clutter in later equations. The vanishing of the final square in (
100
) gives

D=-\mathcal{G}\frac{\epsilon^{\dagger}\gamma_{3}\epsilon}{\epsilon^{\dagger}\epsilon}\sigma(y)=\frac{2\pi}{\Delta z}\frac{\sigma_{0}}{3L}\frac{yH^{\prime}}{H^{4/3}}\,,

(104)

which completes the form of the general solution to the equations of motion from (
100
). We note that the solution space is parameterised by two real constants
\rho_{0},\sigma_{0}
.

In order to determine the full BPS locus, we also need to apply the gauge flux quantisation condition

\mathfrak{f}_{G}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}d^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\mathcal{F}=\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(106)

which can be written explicitly in terms of the flux and spindle data

We can also use the BPS locus value for
\mathcal{F}
to compute the expression for the

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{z}\textrm{d}z\,,

\partial_{y}\mathcal{A}_{z}=\mathcal{F}_{yz}=\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}\rho_{0}\sqrt{g_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}}\,,

which can be directly integrated to find

\mathcal{A}_{z}=-L\rho_{0}\frac{y}{H^{1/3}}+\beta\,,

(109)

where

\left.\beta\right|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}=L\rho_{0}y_{i}^{1/3}\,,

moves into gauges which are regular at the poles, and choosing

\mathcal{A}(y_{i})=\frac{2\pi}{\Delta z}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}{n_{i}}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(112)

where, as in

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{2}n_{1}-\mathfrak{m}_{1}n_{2}\,,

(113)

where
\mathfrak{m}_{i}

\mathfrak{m}
and additional

\mathfrak{m}_{i}=\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\,,\qquad 1=\mathfrak{a}_{2}n_{1}-\mathfrak{a}_{1}n_{2}\,,

(114)

where the pair
(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\mathfrak{a}_{2})
is only defined up to transformations of the form
(\mathfrak{a}_{1}+n_{1}\delta\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}_{2}+n_{2}\delta\mathfrak{a})

Inglese:2023wky

Using the BPS values of the vector multiplet fields, we can now return to (

and in regular gauges (

\left.\Lambda^{\text{G}}\right|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}=\Lambda^{\text{G}}+\frac{L_{0}}{L}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}{n_{i}}\,

In a similar vein, we construct the chiral multiplet deformation action as in
Jeon:2025rfc
, finding a bosonic part of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}^{\text{c.m.}}\bigg|_{\text{bos.}}=\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}&\textrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\,\bigg\{\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}\mathfrak{F}+\bigg|\left(\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\phi-\mathrm{i}\left(\sigma+\frac{r}{2}\mathcal{H}\right)\phi+\gamma_{3}\left(\rho+\frac{r}{2}\mathcal{G}\right)\phi\right)\epsilon\bigg|^{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad\>+\bigg|\left(\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\widetilde{\phi}-\mathrm{i}\left(\sigma+\frac{r}{2}\mathcal{H}\right)\widetilde{\phi}-\gamma_{3}\left(\rho+\frac{r}{2}\mathcal{G}\right)\phi\right)\widetilde{\epsilon}\bigg|^{2}\bigg\}\,,\end{split}

(118)

which is again positive-definite provided one uses the reality condition (
293
). Yet again, the equations of motion arising from this action are simply the vanishing of each term due to the “sum of squares” structure and correspond to the BPS equations
Q_{eq}\psi=Q_{eq}\widetilde{\psi}=0
. We immediately see that the vanishing of the first term gives

\mathfrak{F}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}=0\,,

(119)

and the remaining equations can be massaged into the forms

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{v}+\mathrm{i}r\Lambda^{\text{R}}+\mathrm{i}\Lambda^{\text{G}})\phi

\displaystyle=0\,\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad Q^{2}_{eq}\phi=0\,,

(120a)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{v}-\mathrm{i}r\Lambda^{\text{R}}-\mathrm{i}\Lambda^{\text{G}})\widetilde{\phi}

\displaystyle=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad Q^{2}_{eq}\widetilde{\phi}=0\,,

(120b)

which can be reached by multiplying the second term in (
118
) by
\epsilon
and the third by
\widetilde{\epsilon}
and using the definitions (
79
), (
83
) and (
84
). We note that
\Lambda^{\text{R}},\Lambda^{\text{G}}
are
U(1)_{\text{R}},U(1)_{\text{G}}
dependent quantities respectively, though the equations (
120
) are gauge invariant due to the charges of the scalars
\phi,\widetilde{\phi}
. Due to the reality condition (
293
), we can focus the analysis purely on (
120a
) and for simplicity we choose the gauges (
85
) and (
116
). We find the
z
-dependence of
\phi
to be

\phi(y,z)=\Phi(y)\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}\frac{L}{k_{0}}\Lambda^{\text{G}}_{0}z\right)\,.

(121)

Now contracting the second term in (
118
) with
\epsilon

\mathrm{i}\epsilon\gamma^{\mu}\epsilon D_{\mu}\phi+\epsilon\gamma_{3}\epsilon\left(\rho+\frac{r}{2}\mathcal{G}\right)\phi=0\,,

(122)

which we can also write as

L_{P_{-}}\phi+\epsilon\gamma_{3}\epsilon\left(\rho+\frac{r}{2}\mathcal{G}\right)\phi=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad D_{10}\phi=0\,,

(123)

where we introduced
D_{10}
as in (5.13a) of
Jeon:2025rfc
. Non-trivial chiral multiplet BPS configurations will be
everywhere regular
solutions to the equations above, for a generic vector multiplet configuration. In order to examine this regularity, we use the fact that the regular solutions of
D_{10}\phi=0
have the same regularity properties at the poles of the spindle as the solutions of
L_{P_{-}}\phi=0
, an equation we examine in detail in section
5.1
. Jumping ahead slightly in using the results of that section, we note real solutions are only regular at the South Pole when
\sigma_{0}=0
and

\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\frac{1}{1-\left(\frac{y_{1}}{y_{2}}\right)^{1/3}}\geq\frac{r}{2n_{2}}\,,

(124)

which will clearly not be valid for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We can thus immediately conclude that for a generic configuration on the vector multiplet BPS locus, the only globally regular chiral multiplet solution is

\phi=\widetilde{\phi}=0\,,

(125)

indicating that matter fields will not enter any classical contributions to the partition function and will enter at the level of the one-loop determinants only. We also note as in
GonzalezLezcano:2023cuh
;
Jeon:2025rfc
that the vanishing of the chiral multiplet fields on the BPS locus confirms that we are using the Coulomb branch localisation scheme.

4.2
Action on the BPS locus

In order to compute the partition function, we need to evaluate the action (
91
) on the BPS locus that we have computed in the previous sections. Due to the
Q_{eq}
-exactness (up to boundary terms) of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{v.m.}}
(
93
) and
\mathcal{L}_{\text{c.m.}}
(
95
)
GonzalezLezcano:2023cuh
, we immediately note that these terms give no contribution. The only non-trivial contribution to the classical part of the path integral thus comes from the FI (
97
) and topological (
98
) terms, which we now compute in detail.

We begin with

\left.S_{\text{FI}}\right|_{\text{BPS}}=-\mathrm{i}\xi\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\widehat{D}\,,

(126)

where

\widehat{D}=D+\mathrm{i}(\mathcal{H}\sigma+\mathcal{G}\rho)\,.

(127)

After a bit of computation we find

\sqrt{g}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\widehat{D}=-\frac{2\pi}{\Delta z}L\sigma_{0}\partial_{y}\left(yH^{-1/2}\right)-\mathrm{i}L\rho_{0}\partial_{y}\left(H^{1/6}\right)\,,

(128)

which leads to the anti-twist case result of

\left.S^{\eta=1}_{\text{FI}}\right|_{\text{BPS}}=4\pi\mathrm{i}\xi L\sigma_{0}-2\pi\xi\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\frac{(y_{2}^{1/3}+y_{1}^{1/3})}{(y_{1}^{1/3}-y_{2}^{1/3})}=4\pi\xi\gamma_{\text{G}}\,,

(129)

where, as in
Jeon:2025rfc
, we define
\gamma_{\text{G}}
as

\gamma_{\text{G}}\equiv\frac{L}{L_{0}}\Lambda^{\text{G}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}{n_{1}}+\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{2}}{n_{2}}\right)\,.

(130)

We observe that the structural form of the FI term on the BPS locus for the anti-twist case is identical to that of
Jeon:2025rfc
, even though we started with the STU solution as opposed to minimal gauged supergravity. This is to be expected since the two-dimensional field theory calculation should not depend on the local form of the metric that one chooses, but just the topology of the background. We will comment further on this in section
6
.

More interestingly, the twist case gives

\left.S^{\eta=-1}_{\text{FI}}\right|_{\text{BPS}}=2\pi\xi\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\,,

(131)

this looks quite odd as the
\sigma_{0}
dependence has dropped out completely! This will have a significant effect on the final partition function, giving a difference between the twist and anti-twist cases.

We also need to compute the topological action

\left.S_{\text{top}}\right|_{\text{BPS}}=\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}d^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{i}\theta\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\,,

(132)

which for the twist case shows that the parameter
\theta
enters in exactly the same way as the parameter
\xi
, explicitly

\left.S^{\eta=-1}_{\text{FI}}\right|_{\text{BPS}}+\left.S_{\text{top}}\right|_{\text{BPS}}=\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{n_{1}n_{2}}(2\pi\xi+\mathrm{i}\theta)\equiv\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\tilde{\xi}\,.

(133)

5
One-loop determinants

5.1
Unpaired eigenvalues

Following the approach as discussed in section 5.1 of and 5.2 of
Jeon:2025rfc
, see also
GonzalezLezcano:2023cuh
;
Inglese:2023tyc
;
Pittelli:2024ugf
we can compute the chiral multiplet one-loop determinants entering the partition function via

Z^{Q_{eq}{\cal V}}_{1\text{-loop}}=\sqrt{\frac{\textrm{det}~(Q_{eq}^{2})_{{}_{\Psi}}}{\textrm{det}~(Q_{eq}^{2})_{{}_{\Phi}}}}=\sqrt{\frac{\textrm{det}_{\text{Ker}(P^{(\epsilon\psi)}_{+})}~Q_{eq}^{2}\cdot\textrm{det}_{\text{Ker}(P^{(\widetilde{\epsilon}\widetilde{\psi})}_{-})}~Q_{eq}^{2}}{\textrm{det}_{\text{Ker}(P^{(\widetilde{\phi})}_{+})}~Q_{eq}^{2}\cdot\textrm{det}_{\text{Ker}(P^{(\phi)}_{-})}~Q_{eq}^{2}}}\,,

(134)

and we thus want to examine the kernels of the operators
P_{\pm}
acting on the cohomological variables
\Phi,\Psi
respectively. These kernel elements will be the only contributions to the one-loop determinant as they are the “unpaired eigenmodes” of the
Q^{2}_{eq}
operator, with all other modes cancelling due to supersymmetry.

We first want to look for solutions to the equation

P_{-}^{(\phi)}\phi=0\,,

(135)

where

P_{-}=\mathrm{i}\epsilon\gamma^{\mu}\epsilon D_{\mu}=\mathrm{i}\epsilon\gamma^{\mu}\epsilon\left(\nabla_{\mu}-\mathrm{i}\widehat{q}_{\text{R}}A_{\mu}-\mathrm{i}\widehat{q}_{\text{G}}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\right)\,,

(136)

and thus we look to solve

\partial_{y}\phi=-\frac{\mathrm{i}y}{2P}(\partial_{z}-\mathrm{i}rA_{z}-\mathrm{i}q_{\text{G}}\mathcal{A}_{z})\phi\,,

(137)

we want
\phi
to be an eigenfunction of
Q^{2}_{eq}
(
82
) (equivalently an eigenfunction of
\partial_{z}
) and thus we demand

\phi=\exp\left(-2\pi\mathrm{i}n\frac{z}{\Delta z}\right)\phi_{n}(y)\,,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(138)

and so the kernel equation (
137
) becomes

\frac{\phi_{n}^{\prime}}{\phi_{n}}=-\frac{y}{2P}\left(\frac{2\pi n}{\Delta z}+rA_{z}+q_{\text{G}}\mathcal{A}_{z}\right)\,,

(139)

so near
y=y_{1}
we have

\phi_{n}(y)\sim(y-y_{1})^{\frac{2\pi}{\Delta z}\frac{-y_{1}}{2(y_{1}-y_{2})(y_{1}-y_{3})}\left(n-r\frac{\eta}{2n_{1}}+q_{\text{G}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}{n_{1}}\right)}\,,

(140)

and near
y=y_{2}
we have

\phi_{n}(y)\sim(y_{2}-y)^{\frac{2\pi}{\Delta z}\frac{-y_{2}}{2(y_{2}-y_{1})(y_{2}-y_{3})}\left(n-\frac{r}{2n_{1}}+q_{\text{G}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{2}}{n_{2}}\right)}\,,

(141)

the analysis at the South Pole is identical for both twist and anti-twist, namely in that one has regularity for

n\geq\frac{r}{2n_{2}}-q_{\text{G}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{2}}{n_{2}}\,,

(142)

but the North Pole will differ for twist and anti-twist. For the anti-twist case,
y_{1}<0
and we get

n\geq\frac{r}{2n_{1}}-q_{\text{G}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}{n_{1}}\,,

(143)

for the twist we have
y_{1}>0
and thus we get

n\leq-\frac{r}{2n_{1}}-q_{\text{G}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}{n_{1}}\,,

(144)

we analysed the anti-twist case rather extensively in
Jeon:2025rfc
and the present analysis shows that the result will not be any different for the anti-twisted spindle when starting from the STU model as opposed to minimal gauged supergravity. We can thus focus on the twist case in computing our one-loop determinants in this section, as this will be the novel calculation.

(168)

(169)

(171)

and thus the chiral one-loop determinant computed via the fixed point theorem agrees precisely with the method of unpaired eigenvalues.

In summary, the one-loop determinant for the twisted spindle is given by

Z_{\text{1-L}}=\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)^{\mathfrak{b}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(1-\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{c})+\frac{r}{4}\chi_{-}+q_{\text{G}}\gamma_{\text{G}}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(1+\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{c})+\frac{r}{4}\chi_{-}+q_{\text{G}}\gamma_{\text{G}}\right)}\,,

(209)

and thus we are almost ready to compute the partition function. Before doing this, we first provide a brief discussion of the analogous vector multiplet one-loop determinant contribution.

5.3
Comment on the vector multiplet one-loop determinant

We now turn to computing the vector multiplet one-loop determinant, which will require us taking into account the contribution of zero modes more carefully. First we recall
Inglese:2023tyc
in that the non-equivariant
\mathfrak{q}\rightarrow 1
limit of the index (
202
) (equivalently (
206
)) gives

\lim_{\mathfrak{q}\rightarrow 1}\text{ind}_{\text{orb}}(D_{10};g)=\text{ind}_{\text{orb}}(D_{10})=2\mathfrak{b}\,,

(210)

which counts (minus) the number of zero-modes of the operator
D_{10}
and reproduces the Riemann-Roch-Kawasaki theorem for genus zero orbifolds
zbMATH03629358
;
Closset:2018ghr
;
Inglese:2023tyc
.

When computing the contribution from zero modes in the partition function, we are only concerned about the contribution to the vector multiplet. As explained in
GonzalezLezcano:2023cuh
, the chiral multiplet does not admit any zero modes but the vector multiplet does admit two fermionic zero modes, namely the constant modes of
c,\widetilde{c}
, which are Ghost fields added to the vector multiplet. These zero modes are fermionic and removing these from the cohomological complex we have

(\Phi,Q_{eq}\Phi,\Psi^{\prime},Q_{eq}\Psi^{\prime})\,,

(211)

which has one-to-one correspondence between bosonic and fermionic variables. Following
GonzalezLezcano:2023cuh
, we then compute

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mbox{Tr}_{\Psi^{\prime}}\textrm{e}^{tQ^{2}_{eq}}-\mbox{Tr}_{\Phi}\textrm{e}^{tQ^{2}_{eq}}&=\mbox{Tr}_{\Psi}\textrm{e}^{tQ^{2}_{eq}}-\mbox{Tr}_{\Phi}\textrm{e}^{tQ^{2}_{eq}}-\mbox{Tr}_{\Psi_{\text{zm}}}\mathbb{I}\\
&=-\text{ind}(D_{10})(t)-n_{\text{zm}}^{\Psi}\,,\end{split}

(212)

where
\text{ind}(D_{10})(t)
is what we have just been computing for the chiral multiplet in order to evaluate the chiral multiplet one loop determinants.

For the vector multiplet we now have

n_{\text{zm}}^{\Psi}=2\,,

(213)

which will contribute to the index via the following regularisation

-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\overline{\upvarepsilon}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t}\left.(-n_{\text{zm}}^{\Psi})\right|_{\text{reg}}=\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n^{\Psi}_{\text{zm}}}\,,

(214)

where the UV cutoff is
\overline{\upvarepsilon}\equiv\upvarepsilon\left(\frac{L_{0}}{L}\right)
. The full one-loop determinant with the zero modes correctly excluded is thus

Z^{\prime Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}_{\text{v.m.}}}_{1-\text{loop}}=\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n^{\Psi}_{\text{zm}}}\prod_{n}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}a(n)}\,,

(215)

where we again make use of the expansion of the index as in (
204
). We thus need to evaluate the index for the vector multiplet cohomological complex

\left.\text{ind}_{\text{orb}}(D_{10};g)\right|_{q_{\text{G}}=0,r=2}=-2=a(0)\,,

(216)

which also agrees with (
210
) as
\mathfrak{b}|_{r=2,q_{\text{G}}=0}=-1
. Applying the same regularisation procedure as (
214
) for the eigenvalues, we thus find a trivial vector multiplet contribution of the form

Z^{\prime Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}_{\text{v.m.}}}_{1-\text{loop}}=\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n^{\Psi}_{\text{zm}}+\frac{1}{2}a(0)}=\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)^{1-1}=1\,.

(217)

Finally, we note that the same phenomenon occurs for the anti-twisted spindle, and thus we also expect the vector multiplet one-loop determinant to be trivial in that context.

6.1
Contour integral

We begin this section with a summary of the remaining integral and sum that one needs to perform after reducing the full partition function to a sum over BPS locus moduli. For both twist and anti-twist cases we have the following general expression for the full partition function

}}}=\textrm{e}^{-4\pi\xi\gamma_{\text{G}}-\mathrm{i}\theta\mathfrak{f}_{G}}\,,

(219)

\displaystyle Z^{Q_{eq}{\cal V}}_{1\text{-loop}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)^{1-\frac{r}{2}\chi+2q_{\text{G}}\gamma_{\text{G}}-\mathfrak{c}}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}+\mathfrak{c}-1}{2}+\frac{r}{4}\chi-{q_{\text{G}}\gamma_{\text{G}}}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{c}+1}{2}-\frac{r}{4}\chi+{q_{\text{G}}\gamma_{\text{G}}}\right)}\,.

Twist case:

In order to identify all poles we first rewrite the ratio of gamma functions as

I_{0}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{c}}{2}+\frac{r}{4}\chi_{-}+q_{\text{G}}\gamma_{\text{G}}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{c}+1}{2}+\frac{r}{4}\chi_{-}+q_{\text{G}}\gamma_{\text{G}}\right)}=\frac{\Gamma(\widetilde{\sigma}_{0})}{\Gamma(\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}+\mathfrak{b})}\,,

where we used the change of variable

\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}=\frac{1-\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{c}}{2}+\frac{r}{4}\chi_{-}+q_{\text{G}}\gamma_{\text{G}}\,,

which corresponds to shifting the contour of integration in

\textrm{d}(\sigma_{0}L)=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{q_{\text{G}}}\textrm{d}\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}\,,

Using the elementary properties of the gamma functions, we see that there are poles in the numerator of (

\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}=-n\,,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots\,.

However, the argument of the gamma function in the denominator evaluated at these potential poles is

\mathfrak{b}-n=-h\,,\qquad h=0,1,2,\ldots\,,

(231)

i.e., for

\mathfrak{b}\leq n\,.
We can thus conclude that the one-loop integrand has uncancelled poles for
\mathfrak{b}>n_{\textrm{min}}=0
and no poles otherwise. Recalling the definition of

\mathfrak{b}>0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\left\lfloor\frac{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}{n_{1}}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor-\frac{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}{n_{2}}\right\rfloor\geq 0\,,

\mathfrak{m}

Note that for

The (JK)-residues of the integrand at these poles are

and with this in hand we can compute the partition function (

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}&=\frac{1}{q_{\text{G}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)^{\mathfrak{b}}\textrm{e}^{-\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\widetilde{\xi}}\int\frac{-\mathrm{i}\,\textrm{d}\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma(\widetilde{\sigma}_{0})}{\Gamma(\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}+\mathfrak{b})}\\

where in moving from the second to the third line we used

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\mathfrak{b}-1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\frac{1}{(\mathfrak{b}-n-1)!}=\delta_{\mathfrak{b},1}\,\,,

(235)

which arises as a simple consequence of the binomial theorem.

We now need to find a way to perform the remaining summation in (

\mathfrak{m}=n_{1}n_{2}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{l}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\quad\mathfrak{l}=0,1,\ldots,n_{1}n_{2}-1\,,

(237)

and thus the partition function (

Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=\frac{1}{q_{\text{G}}}\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)\sum_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}:\mathfrak{b}=1}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}\textrm{e}^{-\left(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}+\frac{\mathfrak{l}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\right)\widetilde{\xi}}=\frac{1}{q_{\text{G}}}\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}\textrm{e}^{-\left(\frac{1-\mathfrak{b}(\mathfrak{l})}{q_{\text{G}}}+\frac{\mathfrak{l}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\right)\widetilde{\xi}}\,,

(238)

where we dealt with the summation over
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
using (
237

Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=\frac{1}{q_{\text{G}}}\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)\textrm{e}^{-\frac{r}{2}\chi\widetilde{\xi}\frac{1}{q_{\text{G}}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}\textrm{e}^{\left(-\frac{\llbracket-\frac{r}{2}-q_{\text{G}}a_{1}\mathfrak{l}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}}{q_{\text{G}}n_{1}}-\frac{\llbracket-\frac{r}{2}+q_{\text{G}}a_{2}\mathfrak{l}\rrbracket_{n_{2}}}{q_{\text{G}}n_{2}}\right)\widetilde{\xi}}\,,

(239)

\left.Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\right|_{q_{\text{G}}=1}=\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)\textrm{e}^{-\frac{r}{2}\chi\widetilde{\xi}}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}\textrm{e}^{\left(-\frac{\llbracket-\frac{r}{2}-a_{1}\mathfrak{l}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}}{n_{1}}-\frac{\llbracket-\frac{r}{2}+a_{2}\mathfrak{l}\rrbracket_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}}\right)\widetilde{\xi}}\,,

\mathfrak{l}=n_{1}\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{o}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}=0,1,\ldots,n_{2}-1\,,\quad\mathfrak{o}=0,1,\ldots n_{1}-1\,,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left.Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\right|_{q_{\text{G}}=1}&=\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)\textrm{e}^{-\frac{r}{2}\chi\widetilde{\xi}}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}\textrm{e}^{\left(-\frac{\llbracket-\frac{r}{2}-a_{1}\mathfrak{o}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}}{n_{1}}-\frac{\llbracket-\frac{r}{2}+a_{2}\mathfrak{o}+\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}}\right)\widetilde{\xi}}\\

(242)

completing the derivation of the partition function for an abelian theory defined on the spindle which preserves supersymmetry via the twist mechanism.

6.2
Comments on the spindle partition function: twist and anti-twist cases

The result (
242
) warrants several comments which we make below:

Factorised form:

We observe (
242
) to have a factorised form between contributions arising from the North (
n_{1}
) and South Poles (
n_{2}
) of the spindle, much in the same way the anti-twisted partition function does as was shown in
Jeon:2025rfc
. Such a structure is reminiscent of various related phenomena in both two
Benini:2012ui
;
Doroud:2012xw
and three dimensions
Pasquetti:2011fj
where the partition function factorises into contributions arising from the fixed points. This structure would be expected from the alternative scheme of Higgs branch localisation
Benini:2012ui
;
Closset:2015rna
, where the localisation locus consists of vortex solutions on the fixed points of the background. Verifying our result either via direct Higgs branch localisation on the spindle, or via gluing the partition functions of singular discs
Bah:2021hei
;
Kim:2025ziz
, would be interesting directions to pursue in the future.

S^{2}_{\Omega}
limit:

We note that

\lim_{n_{1,2}\rightarrow 1}\left.Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\right|_{q_{\text{G}}=1}=\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)\textrm{e}^{-r\widetilde{\xi}}\,,

(243)

and upon setting
r=0
, we recover the result for the partition function of the abelian Higgs model on
S^{2}_{\Omega}

\left.Z_{S^{2}_{\Omega}}\right|_{q_{\text{G}}=1}=\frac{L}{L_{0}}\,,

(244)

which matches
Closset:2015rna
up to the unimportant numerical factor of the ratio
L/L_{0}
, which is merely due to a different scaling of the integration in that work.

Vacuum degeneracy:

An alternative limit that one can take is the vacuum limit, i.e. the vanishing of both the FI and topological parameters

\widetilde{\xi}\rightarrow 0\,.

(245)

and taking this limit directly upon (
242
) we find

\lim_{\widetilde{\xi}\rightarrow 0}\left.Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\right|_{q_{\text{G}}=1}=\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)n_{1}n_{2}\,,

(246)

which counts a degeneracy a
n_{1}n_{2}
vacua of the theory. In a similar vein to
Inglese:2023tyc
;
Jeon:2025rfc
, the reason for this degeneracy is the fact that the constraint (
237
) of
\mathfrak{b}(\mathfrak{m})=1
has
n_{1}n_{2}
solutions for
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Contrast with the anti-twist result:

By making use of the parameter
\eta
, we can combine the main result of this work with our main result of
Jeon:2025rfc
and write the partition function for both twist & anti-twist in a unified way as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left.Z^{\eta}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\right|_{q_{\text{G}}=1}&=\frac{L}{L_{0}}\textrm{e}^{-\frac{r}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi\xi-\mathrm{i}\eta\theta}{n_{1}}+\frac{2\pi\xi+\mathrm{i}\theta}{n_{2}}\right)}\textrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(1+\eta)\frac{L}{L_{0}}\textrm{e}^{-2\pi\xi}\sin\theta}\\
&\qquad\times\left(\frac{1-\textrm{e}^{-(2\pi\xi-\mathrm{i}\eta\theta)}}{1-\textrm{e}^{-(2\pi\xi-\mathrm{i}\eta\theta)/n_{1}}}\right)\left(\frac{1-\textrm{e}^{-(2\pi\xi+\mathrm{i}\theta)}}{1-\textrm{e}^{-(2\pi\xi+\mathrm{i}\theta)/n_{2}}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(247)

We note that while the twist case depends entirely on the “holomorphic” complexified parameter
\widetilde{\xi}
, the anti-twist case depends on
\widetilde{\xi}
where the South Pole integer
n_{2}
appears, but the “anti-holomorphic” parameter
\widetilde{\xi}^{*}
in the presence of the North Pole integer
n_{1}
. This observation is somewhat consistent with the fact that the contribution to the one-loop determinants arising from the South Pole are the same for both twists, but the North pole contributions differ.

Globally, we note that the twist case depends on the parameter
\widetilde{\xi}
,

\left.Z^{\eta=-1}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\right|_{q_{\text{G}}=1}=Z(\widetilde{\xi})\,,

(248)

but the anti-twist case does not depend globally on
\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{\xi}^{*}
i.e. we have

\left.Z^{\eta=1}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\right|_{q_{\text{G}}=1}\neq Z(\widetilde{\xi})Z(\widetilde{\xi}^{*})\,,

(249)

due to the non-zero exponential appearing as the last factor in the first line of (
247
). The meaning of this, and whether there may be a connection with dualities such as mirror symmetry
Hori:2000kt
;
Benini:2012ui
is another topic ripe for further exploration.

Model independence:

In performing the calculation of localisation on spindles of both twists, we started with the spindle solutions of
D=5
STU gauged supergravity (
4
). As discussed below (
38
), these solutions are parameterised by the flux and spindle data
(p_{I},n_{i},\eta)
together with the constraint (
34
). When constructing the two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theory on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
, our intermediate expressions such as the one-loop determinants (
209
) thus have dependence on all of parameters of the data, however the final result of the partition function (
247
) only depends on the parameters
(n_{i},\eta)
. This must be the case as the two-dimensional theory only comes equipped with the
U(1)_{\text{R}}
gauge field, and is insensitive to the
U(1)^{3}
structure of the STU model. Another way to see this model independence is that the expression for the partition function of the theory on the anti-twisted spindle is the same whether one starts with STU gauged supergravity, as in this work, or minimal gauged supergravity, as in
Jeon:2025rfc
.

We emphasise again that the starting point of the spindle solutions to
D=5
STU gauged supergravity is a useful computational tool in order to solve the Killing spinor equations (
58
) and not an essential step in performing localisation calculations on the spindle. In future, it would be interesting to revisit these calculations without choosing an explicit metric on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
, an approach which has been followed in the related works
Inglese:2023wky
;
Inglese:2023tyc
;
Pittelli:2024ugf
.

7
Conclusions and outlook

In this work we have applied the technique of supersymmetric localisation in order to compute the partition functions of
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theories defined on the spindle
\mathbb{\Sigma}
. The main feature of this work is the application to theories which admit
U(1)_{\text{R}}
flux of both twist and anti-twist type, extending the earlier work
Jeon:2025rfc
which only encompassed anti-twist.

In order to achieve this new result, we began by considering the spindle solutions
Ferrero:2021etw
of
D=5
STU gauged supergravity, a theory which is suitably general as to allow solutions of both twists (which can be differentiated between via use of a single sign parameter
\eta
). We used these supergravity solutions to construct a 2d
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theory on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
, in particular by providing the choice of metric and R-symmetry gauge field, as well as the functions
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}
which appear in the 2d Killing spinor equations (
58
). We then considered a supersymmetric theory (
91
) whose field content consisted of an abelian vector multiplet and a charged chiral multiplet, together with the inclusion of FI and topological terms. We utilised the technique of supersymmetric localisation in order to compute the partition function of such a theory, with the final result being a unified presentation of the partition function for both twists, given in (
247
).

Many of the intermediate results reported in this work, such as the one-loop determinants, can be verified via comparison with
Inglese:2023wky
;
Inglese:2023tyc
. In that work, the expressions for the
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
one-loop determinants on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
were obtained via reduction of their 3d results on

. It is not clear whether this is because we fix a particular (real) choice of metric and R-symmetry gauge field on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
and perhaps working with generic, complex fields would allow for more general application of our 2d results.

Further extensions include applications to abelian theory consisting of
N_{f}>1
chiral multiplets as has been performed in the

cases

;

;

\xi^{\text{UV}}=\xi\,,\qquad\sum_{I}q_{I}=0\,,

(250)

a condition which would also allow for application to non-linear sigma models defined on Calabi-Yau manifolds, such as the quintic threefold as studied in various localisation contexts

;

.

Even more pressing would be the extension to study non-abelian gauge theories defined on the spindle. For the abelian theory in this work, we utilised the same residue prescription as that for the abelian Higgs model on
S^{2}_{\Omega}
as considered in
Closset:2015rna
. Despite this, we currently lack a deeper understanding of the integration contour and how the JK residue prescription applies more generally for the twisted spindle. The prescription has already been derived in various 2d contexts: the A-twisted theories as studied in
Closset:2015rna
, and in calculating elliptic genera
Benini:2013nda
;
Benini:2013xpa
. Similar considerations also arise in 1d index computations
Cordova:2014oxa
;
Hori:2014tda
and in topologically twisted theories in 3d

Closset:2019hyt
, modes whose existence may be highly sensitive to the structure of the spindle orbifold, see e.g.
Bawane:2014uka
for a discussion of the role of the manifold (specifically, its Betti number) and the number of fermionic zero modes of the Laplacian operator. This issue requires further examination on the spindle and we hope to return to it in future work. It would also be of interest to investigate whether non-abelian localisation techniques
Witten:1992xu
could be applied to theories on the spindle, potentially giving a localisation formula which circumvents the need for the JK contour. For some related recent work considering such a localisation scheme for A-twisted theories on

, see
Leeb-Lundberg:2023jsj
;
Leeb-Lundberg:2025baz
.

These directions will allow for a more complete presentation of the spindle partition function and will allow for more general investigations into the nature of such an object. It would be interesting to examine whether this object would provide a tool to compute orbifold Gromov-Witten invariants
Witten:1988xj
;
Gromov
;
chen2001orbifoldgromovwittentheory
;
Coates2013AMT
;
Cheong2014OrbifoldQT
, whether it satisfies supersymmetric dualities
Hori:2000kt
;
Hori:2002fa
;
Hori:2006dk
;
Benini:2016qnm
, and whether the “large-
N

\mathbb{\Sigma}\times S^{1}
index was shown to in
Colombo:2024mts
. Application to SCFT may allow one to construct “2+2 = 4” dualities

involving the spindle, potentially via utilisation of our results for the twist case together with those of
Pittelli:2024ugf
for the localisation calculation on
\mathbb{\Sigma}\times T^{2}
.

Finally, we note that all of the discussions above are for
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

A.2
Gamma matrix conventions

In five dimensions with Lorentzian signature (mostly plus signature), a consistent choice of gamma matrix satisfies the following relations:

\begin{array}[]{llll}\Gamma_{A}^{\dagger}\;=\;-A\Gamma_{A}A^{-1}\,,&~A\;=\;\Gamma_{0}\,,&~A^{\dagger}\;=\;A^{-1}\;=\;-\Gamma_{0}\,,&\\
\Gamma_{A}^{T}\;=\;{\cal{C}}\Gamma_{A}{\cal{C}}^{-1}\,,&~{\cal{C}}^{T}\;=\;-{\cal{C}}\,,&~{\cal{C}}^{\dagger}={\cal{C}}^{-1}\,,&\\
\Gamma_{A}^{*}\;=\;-B\Gamma_{A}B^{-1}\,,&~B^{T}\;=\;{\cal{C}}A^{-1}\,,&~B^{\dagger}\;=\;B^{-1}\,,~~~~B^{*}B\;=\;-1\,.\end{array}

(252)

This is followed by the property, regarding the charge conjugation matrix

({\cal{C}}\Gamma_{A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}})^{T}=-(-)^{p(p-1)/2}{\cal{C}}\Gamma_{A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}}\,.

(253)

Due to the property of the charge conjugation matrix, we can use the spinor representation satisfying the symplectic-Majorana condition

(\psi^{i})^{\dagger}\gamma_{0}\;=\;\varepsilon_{ij}(\psi^{j})^{T}\mathcal{C}\,,\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad(\psi^{i})^{\ast}=\varepsilon_{ij}B\psi^{j}\,,

(254)

where

is an SU(2)
{}_{\text{R}}
index
with
\varepsilon_{ij}
being the SU(2) symplectic metric
\varepsilon_{12}=-\varepsilon_{21}=1
.

Appendix C
Graviphoton fluxes

We have taken the metric (
25
) and the
U(1)_{\text{R}}
gauge field (
59
) directly from the solution (
13
), (
14
) as discussed in
Hosseini:2021fge
;
Boido:2021szx
;
Ferrero:2021etw
. However, in general one does not need the supergravity background to be on-shell in order to examine the supersymmetric field theory placed on a curved space
Festuccia:2011ws
;
Closset:2014pda

(g_{\mu\nu}\,,A_{\mu}\,,C_{\mu}\,,\widetilde{C}_{\mu})\,,

(273)

where
C_{\mu}
,
\widetilde{C}_{\mu}
form a complex graviphoton which couples to the conserved current for the central charge
Z,\widetilde{Z}
. One can use the functions
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G}
which appear in the Killing spinor equations (
58
) to determine the graviphoton field strengths via

-\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}C_{\nu}=\mathcal{H}+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{G}\,,\qquad-\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{C}_{\nu}=\mathcal{H}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{G}\,,

(274)

and one may also do this for the spindle. We first assume

C_{\mu}\textrm{d}x^{\mu}=C_{\mu}(y)\textrm{d}x^{\mu}=C_{z}(y)\textrm{d}z\,,\qquad\widetilde{C}_{\mu}\textrm{d}x^{\mu}=\widetilde{C}_{\mu}(y)\textrm{d}x^{\mu}=\widetilde{C}_{z}(y)\textrm{d}z\,,

(275)

which is natural as
\partial_{z}
is a Killing direction on the spindle and we can remove the components along the
\textrm{d}y
direction via a gauge transformation. We can then consider the graviphoton fluxes through the spindle

\mathfrak{f}_{C}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}C=\frac{\Delta z}{2\pi}\int_{y_{1}}^{y_{2}}\partial_{y}C_{z}\,\textrm{d}y\,,\qquad\mathfrak{f}_{\widetilde{C}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}\widetilde{C}=\frac{\Delta z}{2\pi}\int_{y_{1}}^{y_{2}}\partial_{y}\widetilde{C}_{z}\,\textrm{d}y\,,

(276)

we can now proceed with computing the explicit expressions for the fluxes using (
274

\partial_{y}C_{z}=\sqrt{g}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{G})\,,\qquad\partial_{y}\widetilde{C}_{z}=\sqrt{g}_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{G})\,,

(277)

we find

C_{z}=-4L(yH^{-1/3}-H^{1/6})\,,\qquad\widetilde{C}_{z}=-4L(yH^{-1/3}+H^{1/6})\,,

(278)

and thus the graviphoton flux
\mathfrak{f}_{C}
(
276

\mathfrak{f}_{C}=\left.-4L\frac{\Delta z}{2\pi}(yH^{-1/3}-H^{1/6})\right|^{y=y_{2}}_{y=y_{1}}=\left.4L\frac{\Delta z}{2\pi}(yH^{-1/3}-H^{1/6})\right|_{y=y_{1}}\,,

(279)

where we used the fact that
H(y_{i})=y_{i}^{2}
and
y_{2}>0
in order to remove the contribution from the South Pole. The North Pole contribution now splits into two different values depending on whether the spindle is of the twist or anti-twist class. For twist we have

\mathfrak{f}^{\text{twist}}_{C}=0\,,

(280)

since
y_{1}>0

\mathfrak{f}^{\text{anti-twist}}_{C}=8L\frac{\Delta z}{2\pi}y_{1}^{1/3}\,,

(281)

which can be written explicitly in terms of the flux and spindle data using (
37
) and (
38

Similarly, we can evaluate the graviphoton flux

\mathfrak{f}_{\widetilde{C}}=\left.-4L\frac{\Delta z}{2\pi}(yH^{-1/3}+H^{1/6})\right|^{y=y_{2}}_{y=y_{1}}\,,

(282)

\mathfrak{f}_{\widetilde{C}}^{\text{twist}}=8L\frac{\Delta z}{2\pi}(y_{1}^{1/3}-y_{2}^{1/3})\,,

\mathfrak{f}_{\widetilde{C}}^{\text{anti-twist}}=-8L\frac{\Delta z}{2\pi}y_{2}^{1/3}\,,

(284)

Appendix D
Multiplets and Supersymmetry transformations

In this subsection we give details of the supersymmetry multiplets for our theory living on the spindle. We will consider a theory consisting of one vector and one chiral multiplet, giving the basic details of the various fields in these multiplets, as well as their supersymmetry transformations.

D.1
Vector multiplet

The vector multiplet consists of a vector, two real scalars, two Dirac spinors and an auxiliary real scalar

\text{Vector}:\{\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\sigma,\rho,\lambda,\widetilde{\lambda},\widehat{D}\}\,,

(285)

where the R-charge assignment is
(0,0,0,-1,1,0)
.

Let us denote the equivariant supercharge
Q_{eq}
by combining the supercharge with the BRST charge for the
G=U(1)
gauge symmetry as

Q_{eq}\;\equiv\;Q+Q_{\text{brst}}\,.

(286)

where we denote the supercharge as
Q\equiv Q_{\epsilon}+Q_{\widetilde{\epsilon}}
. Under this equivariant supercharge, the transformations of the vector multiplet fields are given by

\begin{array}[]{lll}Q_{eq}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}&=&-\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{\epsilon}\gamma_{\mu}\lambda+\epsilon\gamma_{\mu}\widetilde{\lambda})+\partial_{\mu}c\,,\\
Q_{eq}{\sigma}&=&-\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{\epsilon}\lambda-\epsilon\widetilde{\lambda})\,,\\
Q_{eq}{\rho}&=&-\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{\epsilon}\gamma_{3}\lambda+\epsilon\gamma_{3}\widetilde{\lambda})\,,\\
Q_{eq}\lambda&=&\mathrm{i}\gamma_{3}\epsilon\mathcal{F}-\widehat{D}\epsilon-\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}\epsilon\,\partial_{\mu}{\sigma}-\gamma_{3}\gamma^{\mu}\epsilon\,\partial_{\mu}{\rho}+(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{G}\gamma_{3})\epsilon\,{\sigma}+(\mathcal{H}\gamma_{3}+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{G})\epsilon\,{\rho}\\
&=&\mathrm{i}\gamma_{3}\epsilon\mathcal{F}-\widehat{D}\epsilon-\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}(\epsilon{\sigma})-\gamma_{3}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}(\epsilon{\rho})\,,\\
Q_{eq}\widetilde{\lambda}&=&\mathrm{i}\gamma_{3}\widetilde{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}+\widehat{D}\widetilde{\epsilon}+\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}\widetilde{\epsilon}\,\partial_{\mu}{\sigma}-\gamma_{3}\gamma^{\mu}\widetilde{\epsilon}\,\partial_{\mu}{\rho}-(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{G}\gamma_{3})\widetilde{\epsilon}\,{\sigma}+(\mathcal{H}\gamma_{3}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{G})\widetilde{\epsilon}\,{\rho}\\
&=&\mathrm{i}\gamma_{3}\widetilde{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}+\widehat{D}\widetilde{\epsilon}+\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}(\widetilde{\epsilon}{\sigma})-\gamma_{3}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}(\widetilde{\epsilon}{\rho})\,,\\
Q_{eq}\widehat{D}&=&-\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\epsilon}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\lambda+\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\widetilde{\lambda}-\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}\overline{\epsilon}(\mathcal{H}+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{G}\gamma_{3})\lambda+\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}\epsilon(\mathcal{H}-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{G}\gamma_{3})\widetilde{\lambda}\par\\

(287)

where
\mathcal{F}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}
and
c
is an additional ghost field required for systematic treatment of gauge fixing using BRST quantisation
GonzalezLezcano:2023cuh
.

The transformation of the ghost

is

Q_{eq}c\;=\;-\Lambda^{\text{G}}+\Lambda^{\text{G}}_{0}\,,\qquad\Lambda^{\text{G}}\equiv-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\epsilon}\gamma^{\mu}\epsilon\mathcal{A}_{\mu}-\widetilde{\epsilon}\epsilon\sigma-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\epsilon}\gamma_{3}\epsilon\rho\,,

(288)

where
\Lambda^{\text{G}}_{0}
is the constant part of the field dependent parameter
\Lambda^{\text{G}}
.
Here, the covariant derivative on each field is summarised as

D_{\mu}\;=\;\nabla_{\mu}-\mathrm{i}\widehat{q}_{\text{R}}A_{\mu}-\mathrm{i}\widehat{q}_{\text{G}}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\,,

(289)

where
\widehat{q}_{\text{R}}

\widehat{q}_{\text{G}}

As in

\mathcal{A}^{*}=\mathcal{A}\,,\quad\sigma^{*}=\sigma\,,\quad\rho^{*}=\rho\,,\quad D^{*}=D\,,

(290)

which allows for the functional integration of the Euclidean theory to be well-defined, at the price of giving up commutativity of
Q_{eq}

\begin{array}[]{l}Q_{eq}\phi=\widetilde{\epsilon}\psi+\mathrm{i}c\phi\,,\\
