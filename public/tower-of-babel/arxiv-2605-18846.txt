Lost and Found in Translation: Variational Diagnostics for Neural Codebook Channels
Yusuke Hayashi

Abstract
Classical communication systems fail not only through random noise but also when transmitter and receiver use incompatible operational codebooks. Variational autoencoders (VAEs) train an encoder q ϕ q_{\phi} and decoder p θ p_{\theta} jointly, and practitioners treat the resulting latent space as a discrete code—for clustering, conditional generation, and mechanistic interpretability. Yet standard VAE diagnostics—ELBO, active units, mutual information, and code histograms—certify only whether this code is used , never whether the decoder reads each latent under the encoder’s code. We close this gap with the neural codebook channel 𝖪 e → d ​ ( j ∣ i ) \mathsf{K}_{e\to d}(j\mid i) , a coupled encoder–decoder diagnostic whose off-diagonal mass is bounded by an architecture-free Bernoulli-KL certificate d bin ( 1 − 𝒜 ∥ η ¯ p ) ≤ Δ ¯ d_{\rm bin}(1-\mathcal{A}\,\|\,\bar{\eta}_{p})\leq\bar{\Delta} controlled by the variational gap. The certificate is the operational specialization of the classical KL chain rule under disintegration to the encoder–decoder disagreement event; it is complemented by a constructive marginal-impossibility result—no combination of marginal histograms, entropies, active-code counts, or mutual information determines 𝖪 e → d \mathsf{K}_{e\to d} , so 𝖪 e → d \mathsf{K}_{e\to d} is provably non-redundant with existing summaries. We audit the certificate on four sklearn datasets (finite-grid exact, 5 / 5 5/5 seeds, 20 / 20 20/20 pairs satisfy the bound), a 2D model where the bound is non-vacuous at × 2.71\!\times the observed disagreement and the four-term identity closes within 10 − 4 10^{-4} , MNIST under importance-sampling control, and a VQ-VAE attaining the predicted limit 𝒜 ^ = 1.000 \hat{\mathcal{A}}{=}1.000 . The package ( 𝖪 e → d , 𝒜 , R eff , R , AU ) (\mathsf{K}_{e\to d},\,\mathcal{A},\,R_{\rm eff},\,R,\,\mathrm{AU}) is an audit-ready reporting unit that complements existing VAE diagnostics by closing the coupled question they leave open. More broadly, the framework makes mismatched decoding —a failure mode classical communication theory named decades ago—visible inside a single deep generative model, with transport to other code-like continuous representations as a scope roadmap rather than audited evidence.

What this changes.

The framework reframes VAE latent analysis from asking only whether codes are

used
to asking whether the encoder’s codes are
read consistently

by the decoder, and supplies a portable, audit-ready reporting unit—the

the missing diagnostic object, and the variational gap specifies when its
binary mismatch certificate is mathematically licensed.

The Neural Codebook Channel

This section defines the diagnostic object the rest of the paper studies:
the coupled encoder–decoder channel
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
, together with its scalar summary

\mathcal{A}
(Codebook Agreement) and its mutual-information summary
R_{\mathrm{eff}}

about whether the VAE was trained to expose discrete structure, only that
encoder and decoder each induce a measurable hard-code map on latent space.

2.1
Encoder and decoder code maps

A codebook in this paper is an
operational statistic
, not a primitive
discrete latent variable. Let

c^{\star}_{\rm enc}:\mathcal{Z}\to\{1,\ldots,K\},\qquad c^{\star}_{\rm dec}:\mathcal{Z}\to\{1,\ldots,K\}

be measurable encoder-side and decoder-side hard-code maps on latent space.
Concrete instances appear in §
5
:
c^{\star}_{\rm enc}
from
the GMM-summarized aggregate posterior,
c^{\star}_{\rm dec}
from a Bregman
nearest-prototype rule in decoder-output space. The variational certificate of
§
4
requires only measurability and absolute continuity

q_{\phi}(\cdot|x)\ll p_{\theta}(\cdot|x)
; it does not require either map to be
trained or even smooth.

2.2
Codebook agreement and the coupled channel

The diagnostic question this paper asks is whether a single shared latent
draw is given the same label by encoder and decoder.

Definition 1

(Codebook Agreement)
.

With
\bar{q}:=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}q_{\phi}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})
the encoder aggregate posterior, the
posterior-sampled agreement is

\mathcal{A}\;\equiv\;\mathcal{A}_{q}\;=\;\Pr_{Z\sim\bar{q}}\big[c^{\star}_{\rm enc}(Z)=c^{\star}_{\rm dec}(Z)\big].

The deterministic mean-code variant
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}=\Pr_{\mathbf{x}}[c^{\star}_{\rm enc}(\mu_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}))=c^{\star}_{\rm dec}(\mu_{\phi}(\mathbf{x}))]

is reported separately when posterior mass crosses code boundaries. The
model-posterior reference rate
\bar{\eta}_{p}=\mathbb{E}_{x}p_{\theta}(E\mid x)
, where

E=\{c^{\star}_{\rm enc}\neq c^{\star}_{\rm dec}\}
, is the off-diagonal
probability under the model posterior; the certificate of
§
4
compares the encoder-observed mass
1-\mathcal{A}_{q}
to this
reference rate.

Definition 2

(Neural codebook channel)
.

With
C^{\mathrm{enc}}=c^{\star}_{\rm enc}(Z),C^{\mathrm{dec}}=c^{\star}_{\rm dec}(Z)
for
Z\sim\bar{q}
,
the joint table
P_{\mathrm{ed}}(i,j)=\Pr[C^{\mathrm{enc}}=i,C^{\mathrm{dec}}=j]

\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}(j\mid i)=\Pr[C^{\mathrm{dec}}=j\mid C^{\mathrm{enc}}=i]=\frac{P_{\mathrm{ed}}(i,j)}{\sum_{j^{\prime}}P_{\mathrm{ed}}(i,j^{\prime})}

\mathcal{A}=\sum_{i}P_{\mathrm{ed}}(i,i)

With

3
Marginal Impossibility for the Coupled Channel

The neural codebook channel cannot be replaced by any combination of marginal
or aggregate diagnostics. We make this precise as a constructive
impossibility result: it is the formal reason
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
is a strictly new
diagnostic unit rather than a re-packaging of existing ones.

Proposition 4

(Marginal diagnostics do not identify raw code semantics)
.

For every
K\geq 2
, there exist two coupled channels
P,P^{\prime}
on

(C^{\mathrm{enc}},C^{\mathrm{dec}})\in\{1,\ldots,K\}^{2}
with identical encoder marginals, identical
decoder marginals, identical marginal entropies, identical active-code counts,
and identical mutual information, but opposite raw codebook agreement.
Concretely, with any derangement
\pi
of
\{1,\ldots,K\}
,

P(i,j)=\frac{1}{K}\mathbf{1}\{j=i\},\qquad P^{\prime}(i,j)=\frac{1}{K}\mathbf{1}\{j=\pi(i)\}.

Both channels have uniform marginals and
I(C^{\mathrm{enc}};C^{\mathrm{dec}})=\log K
, yet

\mathcal{A}(P)=1
while
\mathcal{A}(P^{\prime})=0
.

Proof.

Both tables are deterministic bijections; row and column marginals are
uniform, all
K
codes are active, and

H(C^{\mathrm{enc}})=H(C^{\mathrm{dec}})=I(C^{\mathrm{enc}};C^{\mathrm{dec}})=\log K
. The identity table places all mass on the
semantic diagonal; the deranged table places none.
∎

Remark 5

(Raw vs. matched, and the connection to identifiability)
.

Proposition
4
concerns raw agreement against
fixed semantic labels. A Hungarian-matched score can hide the deranged case;
that is precisely why the matrix
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
, together with the label semantics
that define the decoder reading, must be reported rather than only a scalar
matched score. The construction also recovers a population-level analogue of
the permutation indeterminacy familiar from
identifiability
hyvarinen2019nonlinear
;
khemakhem2020ivae
;
locatello2019challenging
:
in the absence of side information, latent codes are identifiable only up to
relabeling. Our framing is operational—this indeterminacy is exactly the
failure mode that marginal VAE diagnostics cannot detect and that
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}

exposes.

Remark 6

(Agreement and channel rate are independent diagnostics)
.

There are code maps with
\mathcal{A}=1
and
R_{\mathrm{eff}}=0
(set

C^{\mathrm{enc}}=C^{\mathrm{dec}}\equiv 1
) and code maps with
\mathcal{A}=0
and
R_{\mathrm{eff}}=\log K
(the
deranged construction above). Raw agreement, entropy, and mutual information
answer different questions and must be reported jointly. The construction
also extends beyond uniform marginals: for any
\{p_{i}\}
with
K\geq 2
and
at most one
p_{i}
exceeding
1/2
, a derangement
\pi
exists, and the joints

P_{\rm id}(i,j)=p_{i}\mathbf{1}\{j=i\}
and
P_{\pi}(i,j)=p_{i}\mathbf{1}\{j=\pi(i)\}

again share encoder marginals/decoder marginals/MI but have
\mathcal{A}=1

versus
\mathcal{A}=0
, so the impossibility is generic, not an artifact of
uniformity (Appendix
A.4
).

Necessity established, the next section shows that
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
is also auditable
through the VAE training loss: a one-dimensional binary-KL inequality bounds
inter-code interference by the variational gap.

4
Variational Audit Certificate

We show that the off-diagonal mass
1-\mathcal{A}
of
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
is constrained by
the VAE variational gap whenever the gap is exactly enumerated or otherwise
controlled. The argument is a standard KL chain rule under disintegration
specialized to the binary disagreement event; the contribution is the
operational specialization, not the chain rule.

Classical pieces versus new specialization.

The classical pieces are the ELBO/evidence identity, the KL chain rule under
disintegration, and convexity of KL. We package these as a Lemma
(§
4.1
) rather than a Theorem precisely to be honest about
this status. The substantive (theorem-grade) step is the choice of the
post-processing statistic to be the encoder–decoder disagreement event
(§
4.2
, Corollary
8
),
which converts an otherwise generic identity into an audit layer for a
coupled codebook failure mode that marginal VAE diagnostics provably miss.

7
Discussion

7.1
Limitations

Five qualifications constrain the framework.
(i) Code maps
c^{\star}_{\rm enc},c^{\star}_{\rm dec}
are researcher-chosen,
and different choices produce different within-cell residuals
\bar{\rho}_{T}

in (
2
); this is a feature (it aligns the diagnostic
with the downstream task) but
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
values are not portable across
specifications, so reporting agreement without the maps that produced it
reports almost nothing.
(ii) The Bernoulli-KL audit (
4
) is proof-backed only when

\bar{\Delta}
is certified—IWAE–ELBO is a scale diagnostic, not a certified
gap
burda2016importance
—and even when the audit passes, the bound is
one-sided: the MNIST audited regime has
d_{\rm bin}\!\approx\!0.07
versus

\bar{\Delta}_{\mathrm{IWAE}}^{K=100}\!\approx\!10.8
(
\sim\!160\times
slack
at
\mathrm{ESS}/K\!=\!2.7/100
),
so the gap is dominated by the residual budget
\mathcal{J}_{E}+\bar{\rho}_{E}

and the bound is an audit, not a sharp estimator of
1-\mathcal{A}
.
The slack scales with latent dimensionality and proposal quality
(Setting 1:
2.71\times
at
d{=}2
, finite-grid exact); a tighter regime
(
\mathrm{ESS}/K\!\gtrsim\!0.5
) is the principled lever to sharpen the
audit, not a structural defect of (
4
).
(iii)
\mathcal{A}=1
is trivially achievable when

c^{\star}_{\rm enc}\equiv c^{\star}_{\rm dec}\equiv\text{const}
, with

R_{\mathrm{eff}}
, rate, and AU all collapsing to zero; the reporting unit is
therefore the full package
(\mathsf{K}_{e\to d},\mathcal{A},R_{\mathrm{eff}},R,\mathrm{AU})
, not

\mathcal{A}
alone.
(iv) The Bregman/Fisher analyses (§
5
,
Appendix
E.3
) are sufficient conditions, not hypotheses
of Lemma
7
or Corollary
8
; the
certificate operates on any measurable hard-code maps with

q_{\phi}(\cdot|x)\ll p_{\theta}(\cdot|x)
.
(v) The framework is diagnostic, not prescriptive:
\beta
-VAE training
implicitly bounds
d_{\rm bin}(1-\mathcal{A}\|\bar{\eta}_{p})
via
\bar{\Delta}

with no implied monotonicity of
\mathcal{A}
; the plateau-then-takeoff
pattern (Appendix Figure
6
) is regime-dependent
calibration (sklearn audit:
0/20
), not a training-dynamics theorem.

7.2
Future work

The most natural extensions follow from the limitations above. (a) A

continuous-law certificate with explicit quadrature error
would lift
the proof-backed claim from finite-grid posteriors to continuous latents;
the slack scaling (
2.71\times
at
d{=}2
finite-grid,
\sim\!160\times

at
d{=}10
IWAE-loose) suggests a roadmap toward
d\!\geq\!64

Conv-VAE-large or CelebA-32 with higher-quality SNIS proposals
(
\mathrm{ESS}/K\!\gtrsim\!0.5
) tightening the bound at scale.
(b) A training-dynamics theorem,
(c) an
\mathcal{A}
-aware training objective via a Bernoulli-KL surrogate,
and (d) mechanistic-interpretability instantiations on SAE
dictionaries
cunningham2023sparse
;
elhage2022superposition
—together
with multimodal extensions and signaling-equilibrium
connections
lewis1969convention
;
lazaridou2017multi
—are further open.

7.3
Broader implications

The framework reframes a question that VAE practitioners have been asking
implicitly whenever they cluster, retrieve, conditionally generate, or
probe a trained latent space:
the latents you are using as codes—are
they read by the decoder under the same code that the encoder placed them
in?
Existing diagnostics cannot answer this question;
Proposition
4
shows it is strictly more than
a marginal usage question. The paper does not argue that ELBO, rate, AU, MI,
or disentanglement are unhelpful—each remains a valid answer to its own
question—but identifies the one they collectively leave open and provides a
self-contained diagnostic unit that closes it. The construction is portable
to any continuous representation later treated as a discrete code, making
mismatched decoding—the failure mode classical communication theory named
decades ago
shannon1948
;
scarlett2020mismatched
—visible inside a
single deep generative model.

Appendix A
Proof of the Universal Decomposition (Lemma
7
)
and the Codebook Specialisation (Corollary
8
)

This appendix proves the two architecture-free identities on which the
rest of the paper rests. The universal decomposition
(§
A.1
) follows from three classical facts—
the ELBO/evidence identity, the KL chain rule under disintegration,
and a Jensen residual—applied to an arbitrary measurable
post-processing
T:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Y}
.
The codebook specialisation (§
A.2
)
is the binary instance
T=\mathbf{1}[\mathbf{z}\in E]
.
The empirical estimator used in §
6

is recorded in §
A.3
.

A.1
Proof of Lemma
7

Throughout,
(q_{\phi},p_{\theta})
are encoder and true-posterior
conditional densities with
q_{\phi}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})\ll p_{\theta}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})

for
p_{\mathrm{data}}
-a.e.
\mathbf{x}
and
\bar{\Delta}<\infty
.
Let
T:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Y}
be measurable,
\mathcal{Y}
standard
Borel.

Step 1 (ELBO/evidence identity).

Bayes’ rule gives

\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})=\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})+\log p(\mathbf{z})-\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x})
;
substituting into
\Delta(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi}}[\log q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})-\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})]

yields the pointwise identity
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x})=-\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x})+\Delta(\mathbf{x})
,
hence
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{x}}+\bar{\Delta}
on average. This step is

T
-independent.

Step 2 (KL chain rule under disintegration).

For probability measures
\mu\ll\nu
on a standard Borel space and
any measurable
T
, the disintegration theorem
[
11
, Thm. 2.16]

gives regular conditionals and the equality

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(T_{*}\mu\|T_{*}\nu)+\int_{\mathcal{Y}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(\mu(\cdot|T{=}y)\,\big\|\,\nu(\cdot|T{=}y)\bigr)\,d(T_{*}\mu)(y).

(5)

This is an equality for any measurable
T
; sufficiency would make the
integral vanish.

Step 3 (pointwise
T
-decomposition).

Apply (
5
) with
\mu=q_{\phi}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})
,

\nu=p_{\theta}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})
. Writing
q_{T}(\cdot|\mathbf{x}):=T_{*}q_{\phi}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})
,

p_{T}(\cdot|\mathbf{x}):=T_{*}p_{\theta}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})
, and

\rho(\mathbf{x};T):=\!\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\!D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(q_{\phi}(\cdot|\mathbf{x},T{=}y)\,\big\|\,p_{\theta}(\cdot|\mathbf{x},T{=}y)\bigr)\,dq_{T}(y|\mathbf{x})\;\geq\;0,

(6)

gives

\Delta(\mathbf{x})\;=\;D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(q_{T}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})\,\big\|\,p_{T}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})\bigr)\;+\;\rho(\mathbf{x};T).

(7)

Step 4 (Jensen residual).

KL is jointly convex on probability measures

[
12
, Thm. 2.7.2]
. Averaging (
7
)
over
\mathbf{x}
and applying Jensen to the mixture over
\mathbf{x}
,

D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(\bar{q}_{T}\,\big\|\,\bar{p}_{T}\bigr)\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}\!\bigl[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{T}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})\|p_{T}(\cdot|\mathbf{x}))\bigr],

(8)

with slack

\mathcal{J}_{T}\;:=\;\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{T}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})\|p_{T}(\cdot|\mathbf{x}))]-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{q}_{T}\|\bar{p}_{T})\;\geq\;0.

(9)

Step 5 (assembly).

Combining steps 3–4 gives

\bar{\Delta}\;=\;D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{q}_{T}\|\bar{p}_{T})+\mathcal{J}_{T}+\bar{\rho}_{T},\qquad\bar{\rho}_{T}:=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}[\rho(\mathbf{x};T)],

(10)

which is Lemma
7
. Non-negativity of all three
summands is by construction.
\square

\mathcal{Y}=\{0,1\}

\square

A.4
Non-uniform marginal extension of Proposition
4

The construction in Proposition
4
uses
uniform marginals for clarity; we record here the extension to arbitrary

\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
promised in Remark
6
.

Construction.

For any encoder marginal
\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
with
p_{i}>0
, fix

\Pr[C^{\mathrm{enc}}=i]=p_{i}
. The deterministic diagonal joint

P_{\rm id}(i,j)=p_{i}\mathbf{1}\{j=i\}
and any deterministic permutation

P_{\pi}(i,j)=p_{i}\mathbf{1}\{j=\pi(i)\}
both have:
encoder marginal
\{p_{i}\}
;
decoder marginal
\{p_{\pi^{-1}(j)}\}
(a relabeling of
\{p_{i}\}
,
hence identical entropy
H(\{p_{i}\})
);
and mutual information
I(C^{\mathrm{enc}};C^{\mathrm{dec}})=H(\{p_{i}\})
.
However,
\mathcal{A}(P_{\rm id})=1
while

\mathcal{A}(P_{\pi})=\sum_{i:\pi(i)=i}p_{i}
.

Quantitative ambiguity bound.

For fixed marginals, the worst-case ambiguity in
\mathcal{A}
across
permutations consistent with those marginals is

\max_{\pi\in S_{K}}\mathcal{A}(P_{\pi})=\sum_{i:\pi^{\star}(i)=i}p_{i}\;-\;\min_{\pi\in S_{K}}\mathcal{A}(P_{\pi}),

where
\pi^{\star}
maximises and the minimum is achieved by any derangement
when one exists. A derangement of
\{1,\ldots,K\}
exists whenever

K\geq 2
and at most one
p_{i}
exceeds
1/2
(so that no single index is
forced as a fixed point by a marginal-matching argument). Under this very
mild condition,
\min_{\pi}\mathcal{A}(P_{\pi})=0
and

\max_{\pi}\mathcal{A}(P_{\pi})=1
, recovering exactly the
\mathcal{A}=0
vs

\mathcal{A}=1
separation of Proposition
4
.

What this rules out.

A reviewer might hope that highly non-uniform marginals (low marginal
entropy) attenuate the impossibility — e.g., by forcing the decoder
distribution to concentrate. The bound above shows this is not the case:

the ambiguity does not shrink with marginal entropy
, so reporting
encoder and decoder marginals (and their entropies) cannot localise

\mathcal{A}
at any value in
[0,1]
. The coupled channel
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
remains
the strict required reporting unit even in the heavily non-uniform regime.

This appendix proves the dynamical companion to the universal

Statement of the theorem.

The dynamical companion to Lemma
7
is the following

H
-linear path-space KL bound on the mismatch between encoder- and
model-posterior-induced Gibbs kernels.

Theorem 17

(Posterior-Gibbs kernel mismatch; finite-horizon extension)
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
7
, let
\mathsf{K}_{q},\mathsf{K}_{p}
denote
the posterior-Gibbs kernels induced by the encoder and the model posterior
on latent space (defined in (

)), and let

P_{q}^{H},P_{p}^{H}
be the
H

distribution
\bar{q}

(i)
the one-step kernel mismatch admits the bound

\mathbb{E}_{\bar{q}}\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathsf{K}_{q}(\mathbf{z},\cdot)\|\mathsf{K}_{p}(\mathbf{z},\cdot))\;\leq\;2\bar{\Delta}-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{q}\|\bar{p});

(13)

(ii)
the path-space mismatch is
H
-linear,

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{q}^{H}\|P_{p}^{H})\;\leq\;H\bigl(2\bar{\Delta}-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{q}\|\bar{p})\bigr),

(14)

and any finite-horizon endpoint label

inherits the
same bound by the data processing inequality.

The proof, given in §

–§

,
combines the KL chain rule on the joint
\Gamma_{q}=p_{\rm data}(d\mathbf{x})q_{\phi}(d\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})

with data processing from the lifted kernel
L_{q}
to its
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}
-marginal
\mathsf{K}_{q}
.
Infinite-horizon attractor alignment is
not
claimed; it would require

(§
C.6
–§
C.7
).

Corollary 18

(Path-space audit from a common initial law)
.

If both chains

start from the same initial law (e.g. both

), then for the binary disagreement endpoint statistic

T_{H}^{\,\mathrm{enc\text{-}dec}}=\mathbf{1}\{c^{\star}_{\rm enc}(\mathbf{z}_{H})\neq c^{\star}_{\rm dec}(\mathbf{z}_{H})\}
,
the Bernoulli-KL bound of Corollary

extends to

d_{\rm bin}\!\bigl(1-\mathcal{A}_{H}\,\big\|\,\bar{\eta}_{p}^{(H)}\bigr)\;\leq\;H\bigl(2\bar{\Delta}-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{q}\|\bar{p})\bigr),

where
\mathcal{A}_{H},\bar{\eta}_{p}^{(H)}
are the path-space analogues of

\mathcal{A},\bar{\eta}_{p}
at horizon
H
. Initialising the
\mathsf{K}_{p}
chain
at
\bar{p}
instead would add a fixed initial-mismatch term.

C.1
Four operational notions of “codebook”

A codebook in this paper is an
operational statistic
—a measurable
hard-code map
\mathcal{Z}\to\{1,\ldots,K\}
such as the encoder and decoder
maps
c^{\star}_{\rm enc},c^{\star}_{\rm dec}
used throughout this paper—not a
unique latent object.
Four common choices in the literature are:

(a) aggregate-density codes
, modes/mixtures of
\bar{q}
or
\bar{p}
;

(b) conditional-concentration codes
, clustered supports of
q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})
or
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})
;

(c) metastable-dynamics codes
, slow modes of
\mathsf{K}_{q}
or
\mathsf{K}_{p}
;

(d) information-optimal codes
, those that maximise
I(X;C^{\mathrm{enc}})
or
I(X;C^{\mathrm{dec}})
subject to a cardinality or entropy constraint.
These choices generally differ; they coincide only under additional
assumptions (well-separated mixtures, low cross-cell conductance,
identifiability, or score-aligned contraction).
The theorem says that once a measurable choice is made, its
encoder/model-posterior mismatch is exactly visible inside the
variational gap.

C.2
Posterior joints and reverse conditionals

Recall

\Gamma_{q}(d\mathbf{x},d\mathbf{z})=p_{\mathrm{data}}(d\mathbf{x})q_{\phi}(d\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}),\qquad\Gamma_{p}(d\mathbf{x},d\mathbf{z})=p_{\mathrm{data}}(d\mathbf{x})p_{\theta}(d\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x}),

with latent marginals
\bar{q},\bar{p}
. Disintegration gives

\Gamma_{q}(d\mathbf{x},d\mathbf{z})=\bar{q}(d\mathbf{z})r_{q}(d\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})
and

\Gamma_{p}(d\mathbf{x},d\mathbf{z})=\bar{p}(d\mathbf{z})r_{p}(d\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})
. The

posterior-Gibbs kernels
are defined as the
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}
-marginals

\mathsf{K}_{q}(\mathbf{z},d\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\;:=\;\int r_{q}(d\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})\,q_{\phi}(d\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|\mathbf{x}),\qquad\mathsf{K}_{p}(\mathbf{z},d\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\;:=\;\int r_{p}(d\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})\,p_{\theta}(d\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|\mathbf{x}),

(15)

with lifted versions

L_{q}(\mathbf{z},d\mathbf{x},d\mathbf{z}^{\prime}):=r_{q}(d\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})q_{\phi}(d\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|\mathbf{x})
and

L_{p}(\mathbf{z},d\mathbf{x},d\mathbf{z}^{\prime}):=r_{p}(d\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})p_{\theta}(d\mathbf{z}^{\prime}|\mathbf{x})
. The KL
chain rule applied to
\Gamma_{q}\ll\Gamma_{p}
yields

\bar{\Delta}=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\Gamma_{q}\|\Gamma_{p})=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{q}\|\bar{p})+\mathbb{E}_{\bar{q}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r_{q}(\cdot|\mathbf{z})\|r_{p}(\cdot|\mathbf{z})),

(16)

hence

\mathbb{E}_{\bar{q}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r_{q}\|r_{p})=\bar{\Delta}-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{q}\|\bar{p})
.

C.3
One-step kernel mismatch

Data processing from
L_{q}(\mathbf{z},\cdot)
to its
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}
-marginal

\mathsf{K}_{q}(\mathbf{z},\cdot)
gives

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathsf{K}_{q}(\mathbf{z},\cdot)\|\mathsf{K}_{p}(\mathbf{z},\cdot))\leq D_{\mathrm{KL}}(L_{q}(\mathbf{z},\cdot)\|L_{p}(\mathbf{z},\cdot)).

(17)

Averaging over
\bar{q}
and applying the KL chain rule to
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}^{\prime})

gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bar{q}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(L_{q}\|L_{p})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bar{q}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r_{q}(\cdot|\mathbf{z})\|r_{p}(\cdot|\mathbf{z}))+\mathbb{E}_{\bar{q}}\mathbb{E}_{r_{q}(\cdot|\mathbf{z})}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{\phi}(\cdot|\mathbf{x})\|p_{\theta}(\cdot|\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle=\bigl(\bar{\Delta}-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\bar{q}\|\bar{p})\bigr)+\bar{\Delta},

(18)

since
\bar{q}(d\mathbf{z})r_{q}(d\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})=p_{\mathrm{data}}(d\mathbf{x})q_{\phi}(d\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})

and integrating out
\mathbf{z}
leaves
p_{\mathrm{data}}(d\mathbf{x})
. Combining
(
17
)–(
18
) gives
(
13
) of the main text.

C.4
Finite-horizon path-space extension

Let both chains start from
\mathbf{z}_{0}\sim\bar{q}
and let
P_{q}^{H},P_{p}^{H}
be
the
H
-step path laws under
\mathsf{K}_{q},\mathsf{K}_{p}
. Because
\mathsf{K}_{q}
has stationary
marginal
\bar{q}
, each
\mathbf{z}_{t}
under
P_{q}^{H}
has law
\bar{q}
. The
path-space KL chain rule then gives

and data processing from path to endpoint yields the second inequality

Any finite-horizon endpoint label inherits the same bound by data

C.5
Fisher-level identity for conditional score flows

For fixed
\mathbf{x}
, deterministic score-flow updates

\mathbf{z}\mapsto\mathbf{z}+\eta S_{\bullet}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x})
define Dirac kernels whose KL is
infinite unless the updates coincide. With Gaussian smoothing

\mathsf{K}_{\bullet,\sigma}^{(\mathbf{x})}=\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{z}+\eta S_{\bullet}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x}),\sigma^{2}I)
,
the KL between two Gaussians of common covariance
\sigma^{2}I
is the
squared mean difference divided by
2\sigma^{2}
, so averaging over

q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})
gives

\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathsf{K}_{q,\sigma}^{(\mathbf{x})}\|\mathsf{K}_{p,\sigma}^{(\mathbf{x})})=\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\,\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{x}),\quad\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi}}\|S_{q}-S_{p}\|^{2}.

This is a Fisher-information identity, not a KL-gap bound. Bounding

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{x})
by
\bar{\Delta}
requires a separate inverse Fisher–KL
assumption.

C.6
Relation to deterministic attractors

When a posterior-Gibbs kernel concentrates near a locally contractive
deterministic map, metastable sets become ordinary basins of
attraction
[
26
]
. If the residual field is
score-aligned with a density
\mu
, fixed points are local modes of

\mu
; if
\mu
is well approximated by a separated mixture, those
modes lie near component centres. These are useful interpretations,
not assumptions used by Theorem
17
.
In particular,
\bar{q}
can equal the prior at an exact VAE optimum,
so semantic code structure is better treated as an operational

primitive aggregate-density mode.

C.7
Local stability of attractor alignment

The following perturbation lemma supports the finite-horizon
interpretations above. It is deliberately basin-local: a global
contraction would force a single fixed point and would describe
collapse rather than a multi-atom codebook.

Proposition 19

(Local attractor stability)
.

Let

be closed with

,

G_{\mathrm{dec}}(B_{c})\subseteq B_{c}
, both maps

Banach gives uniqueness. The fixed-point identities and the triangle

\|\mathbf{a}_{c}^{\mathrm{enc}}-\mathbf{a}_{c}^{\mathrm{dec}}\|=\|G_{\mathrm{enc}}(\mathbf{a}_{c}^{\mathrm{enc}})-G_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbf{a}_{c}^{\mathrm{dec}})\|\leq\varepsilon+\lambda\|\mathbf{a}_{c}^{\mathrm{enc}}-\mathbf{a}_{c}^{\mathrm{dec}}\|

∎

Appendix G
Detailed Related Work

Communication-theoretic mismatch and interference.

Classical communication theory separates random channel noise from
structural receiver mismatch. Mismatched decoding studies the rate and
error consequences of decoding with a metric or channel model
different from the true channel
[
2
]
;
noisy-channel vector quantisation shows that index assignment can turn
small symbol confusions into large distortion
[
3
]
;
synchronisation channels study insertion/deletion/timing errors rather
than substitutions
[
4
]
; and inter-symbol
interference is a temporal-memory phenomenon, not a generic synonym
for static code confusion
[
5
]
.
We import these distinctions carefully: static VAEs exhibit neural

inter-code interference
through the off-diagonal mass of
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
,
while sequential or finite-horizon latent processes may additionally
be probed by lagged statistics such as (
19
).

Rate–distortion and information-bottleneck views.

Alemi et al.
[
8
]
decompose ELBO into rate and
distortion;
\beta
-VAE
[
9
]
makes this tradeoff
explicit; the information bottleneck
[
10
]
is a
related variational framework. Our codebook perspective is
complementary: we characterise the
geometry
of the resulting
partition and couple it to the variational gap via DPI for KL
divergence
[
11
,
12
]
.

Existing VAE diagnostics.

Common representation diagnostics report rate, distortion, active
units
[
24
]
, MIG and total-correlation
decompositions
[
25
]
, or aggregate code usage. These
are largely marginal: they can be invariant under a receiver-side
permutation or many-to-one reading. The neural codebook channel is
designed for the complementary question of
coupled
encoder–decoder
confusion; Table
1
of the main text
isolates cases where scalar diagnostics are intentionally insufficient.

Bregman divergences, vector quantisation, and information geometry.

Banerjee et al.
[
13
]
establish a bijection
between exponential families and Bregman divergences;
Nielsen et al.
[
14
]
develop three types of Bregman
Voronoi diagrams via Legendre duality. We instantiate this in a trained
VAE and give the encoder–decoder reading.
VQ-VAE
[
15
]
explicitly designs a discrete
codebook with commitment; we place it at a Tier-3 endpoint
(§
E.4
). Lewis signaling games
[
16
,
17
]

model emergent communication; the encoder–decoder pair is a single-agent
analog (§
H
).
Arvanitidis et al.
[
18
]
study the Riemannian
structure of VAE latent spaces; we characterise the combinatorial
(cell-complex) structure via Bregman geometry
(Proposition
22
, §
E.3
).

Latent dynamics and representation alignment.

Fumero et al.
[
26
]
study latent vector
fields induced by the deterministic round trip
z\mapsto E(D(z))
.
Stochastic decoders require Markov kernels rather than deterministic
maps, so we work with the posterior-Gibbs kernels of
§
C
and treat Fumero-style attractors as a
low-noise locally contractive limit
(§
C.6
). The cross-model alignment program
connects to Relative Representations
[
27
]
,
Latent Functional Maps
[
28
]
, and the
Platonic Representation Hypothesis
[
29
]
; our paper
supplies a discrete, variational-gap-controlled version for VAE code
maps.

Appendix H
Extended Discussion, Limitations, and Outlook

This appendix collects the discussion material that would either
duplicate §
7
/§
7
of the main
text or take it off-topic. It is organised around five questions a
reviewer might raise but for which a brief answer is enough.

Q1: Are static VAE channel impairments literal?

No. A static VAE does not have waveform inter-symbol interference; it
has a paired-label confusion channel
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
whose off-diagonal mass
measures inter-code interference (§
G
).
Mismatched decoding corresponds to
q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})
and

p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})
inducing different operational partitions;
noisy-channel index-assignment distortion corresponds to off-diagonal
cells whose decoder prototypes are semantically far apart;
synchronisation errors correspond to ordered settings where a code
intended for one time/token/layer/horizon is read at another.

Q2: How does the codebook channel relate to mechanistic interpretability?

A sparse-autoencoder dictionary
[
34
]
can be
read as an encoder-side feature proposer paired with a circuit that
implements a receiver-side codebook;

\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
’s off-diagonal mass is then a direct diagnostic of feature
splitting and merging
[
33
]
.
Concretely: train an SAE on layer-
\ell
activations; define

c_{\mathrm{enc}}^{\star}(h):=\arg\max_{k}a_{k}
from SAE feature activations;
choose a (possibly many-to-one) matching
\Pi
from features to circuit
outputs; define
c_{\mathrm{dec}}^{\star}(h):=\Pi^{-1}(\phi^{\star}(g_{\mathrm{SAE}}(f_{\mathrm{SAE}}(h))))
;
form
\hat{\mathsf{K}}_{e\to d}
on a held-out audit set; read feature splitting from
multi-modal rows and feature merging from multi-modal columns.
We do not run this experiment in the present paper; we offer the
recipe as one concrete way to instantiate
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d}
outside VAEs.

Q3: What is the relation to Lewis signaling games?

The encoder–decoder pair can be viewed as an idealised cooperative
Lewis signaling game
[
16
,
17
]

with states
c\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, signals
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
, actions

\hat{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{z})
, joint utility
\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})
, and channel
cost
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{\phi}(\mathbf{z}|\mathbf{x})\|p(\mathbf{z}))
; the negative
\beta
-VAE
ELBO is the cost-augmented objective. Under the symbol correspondence

\mathbf{z}\leftrightarrow m
,
q_{\phi}\leftrightarrow S_{\phi}
,

p_{\theta}\leftrightarrow R_{\theta}
, this is identical to the

\beta
-VAE ELBO. Codebook Agreement therefore provides a geometric
compatibility measure for this idealised abstraction, not a complete
equilibrium characterisation. A recent loss decomposition into
information loss and co-adaptation loss admits a structural parallel
with Corollary
8
—
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{info}}\!\leftrightarrow\!d_{\mathrm{bin}}
,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adapt}}\!\leftrightarrow\!\mathcal{J}+\bar{\rho}
,
co-adaptation overfitting
\!\leftrightarrow\!\sum_{c}\pi_{c}\kappa_{c}
—but
the correspondences are qualitative (population-level KL chain rule
vs. finite-sample train/test gap; continuous
\mathbf{z}
vs. discrete
m
).

Q4: Is high agreement always good?

No. The framework predicts that posterior collapse appears as high
apparent agreement over a low-entropy, low-information code, with
small
I(X;C^{\mathrm{enc}})
, low decoder sensitivity

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla_{z}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{z})\|^{2}
, and low effective decoder
Fisher rank. Codebook Agreement is therefore uninformative
stand-alone; it should be paired with code entropy,
I(X;C^{\mathrm{enc}})
,
rate, finite-horizon multiplicity, and decoder-rank diagnostics.
Pre-trained encoder–decoder pairs such as Optimus
(BERT
\to
GPT-2)
[
32
]
—where the autoregressive
Categorical decoder makes Assumption
9
(b)
applicable position-wise—are a natural future-work target.

Beyond the four points in §
7
we flag four:

(L5.1)

(L5.3)

Cross-model latent-vector-field alignment:

experiment (train two VAEs with different seeds, compute

Differentiation from prior encoder–decoder consistency work.

Three lines of prior work address related encoder–decoder questions but
ask materially different questions and use materially different objects.
Cemgil et al.
[
38
]
(NeurIPS 2020) ask whether a trained
VAE consistently encodes typical samples generated from its decoder, and
answer “no”; their fix is a self-consistency objective implemented
through a Markov chain that alternates encoder and decoder. Wang et
al.
[
39
]
formalize encoder–decoder incompatibility via
differential geometry of chart maps and propose a Coupled-VAE objective
that ties a deterministic-autoencoder branch to the VAE branch. Dang
et al.
[
40
]
(ICLR 2024) develop posterior-collapse
detection theory for conditional and hierarchical VAEs from the

marginal
side. Our framework is complementary to all three: rather
than (a) propose a training-time fix
(
[
38
,
39
]
) or (b) detect a marginal-side
failure mode (
[
40
]
), we (c) construct a

coupled-channel finite-codebook diagnostic

(
\mathsf{K}_{e\to d},\mathcal{A},R_{\mathrm{eff}}
) whose off-diagonal mass receives a
one-dimensional Bernoulli-KL audit certificate via the variational gap
(Corollary
8
). Proposition
4

delineates exactly the diagnostic gap that motivates this coupled view,
and the operational reporting unit

(\mathsf{K}_{e\to d},\mathcal{A},R_{\mathrm{eff}},R,\mathrm{AU})
is downstream-task agnostic by
construction. None of the three prior frameworks supplies a
finite-codebook channel diagnostic with this property.

Appendix I
Practitioner Notes

This appendix records implementation conventions and failure modes
that are easy to miss when reproducing the diagnostics. None of these
notes adds a theoretical claim; they are intended to make the main
identities harder to mis-implement.

Remark 34

(Encoder/decoder
x
-consistency requirement)
.

The certificate of Corollary
8
is built around the
true model posterior
p_{\theta}(z|x)\propto p_{\theta}(x|z)p(z)
, so

the encoder input and the decoder output must be the same
x
.
This rules out conditional models in which the encoder consumes one
variable (e.g. an image) and the decoder produces another (e.g. a
class label, a caption, or an action). Such image-to-label or
multimodal-conditional models are out of scope for the audit identity:
the relevant variational gap is well-defined, but the disagreement
event
E=\{c^{\star}_{\rm enc}\neq c^{\star}_{\rm dec}\}
does not couple
to the same posterior, so the Bernoulli-KL bound does not apply.

Bernoulli geometry on continuous-valued pixels.

For
x\in[0,1]^{K}
,
\prod_{k}d_{k}^{x_{k}}(1-d_{k})^{1-x_{k}}
is not normalised
unless
x
is binary. The correct continuous-Bernoulli density
includes a normaliser
C(\lambda)
depending on the decoder parameter,
not on
x

[
30
]
. Dropping this is therefore not
an additive constant in
\theta
. In this paper, the Bernoulli/BCE
construction should be read as a surrogate reconstruction geometry
for grey-scale pixels unless the continuous-Bernoulli normaliser is
explicitly included; this is harmless for Setting 1’s one-hot labels
but matters when interpreting MNIST or CIFAR-10 as likelihood models.

Per-example versus aggregate gaps.

\bar{\Delta}
denotes the per-example mean true variational gap.
The independent identity-verification check also reports the aggregate
batch sum
