Mazus zhewanensis (Mazaceae): A new species from Eastern China

Ke-Lang Lou, Wen-Yuan Xie, Pan Xu, Hong-Wei Zhang, Feng Chen, Jun-Ping Li, Jin-Bao Pu, Zheng-Hai Chen

ABSTRACT
A new species, M. zhewanensis sp. nov. (Mazaceae), is described from East China. Morphologically, it is similar to M. spicatus but differs in having elliptic cauline leaves with shallowly and densely serrate margins (vs. obovate to obovate-spatulate with incised-serrate margins) and a larger corolla. Phylogenetically, it is recovered as sister to M. caducifer with high support (ML = 95%, BI = 1). A key to the pubescent-fruited species of Mazus in China, including the new species, is also provided.

Introduction The genus Mazus Lour. (Mazaceae) comprises approximately 45 species, distributed primarily across temperate and subtropical Asia, as well as parts of Australia and New Zealand [ 1 , 2 ]. As currently circumscribed, the genus is characterized by small herbaceous plants with a distinctly two-lipped corolla, a palate with two longitudinal plaits, fibrous roots, and a capsule enclosed within the persistent calyx [ 1 , 3 ]. China is the center of diversity for Mazus , with more than 30 species [ 1 ]. Historically, the genus was placed in Scrophulariaceae, but molecular phylogenetic studies have since transferred it to the recently established family Mazaceae, in which it forms a clade with Lancea and Dodartia [ 4 – 6 ]. Within Mazus , early classifications recognized two sections based on stolon presence [ 7 , 8 ], this scheme was later expanded to three sections [ 1 , 9 ]. The monotypic section Lanceifoliae was recently segregated as the new genus Puchiumazus [ 10 ], while a revised infrageneric classification based on molecular and biogeographic evidence proposed two subgenera— M. subg. Mazus (Asia) and M. subg. Notomazus (Australia) [ 5 ]. A comprehensive taxonomic revision of the family worldwide was later provided by Lü [ 11 ]. Recent years have witnessed accelerated taxonomic discovery within the genus. Between 2016 and 2026, more than ten new species have been described from China [ 5 , 10 , 12 – 17 ], revealing substantial hidden diversity, particularly in eastern China. These include M. sunhangii from Hubei [ 12 ], M. danxiacola from Jiangxi [ 13 ], M. unguiculatus from Anhui [ 14 ], M. motuoensis from Xizang [ 15 ], M. jingtaiensis from Zhejiang and Fujian [ 16 ], and M. jiangshiense from Fujian [ 17 ]. Molecular phylogenetic analyses have resolved five major subclades within Asian Mazus , with a well-supported clade (subclade IV) containing M. caducifer , M. spicatus , and M. xiuningensis —highlighting the close affinities and complex evolutionary history among these taxa [ 5 ]. Eastern China has emerged as an important center of endemism for Mazus . In addition to the widespread taxa M. stachydifolius , M. caducifer , M. pumilus , M. miquelii , and M. gracilis [ 1 , 18 – 22 ], the region hosts a growing number of endemic species, including M. fukienensis [ 23 ], M. wanmuliensis [ 24 ], M. jiangshiense [ 17 ], M. xiuningensis [ 25 ], M. apicaliracemosus [ 26 ], M. jingtaiensis [ 16 ], and M. unguiculatus [ 14 ]. Species such as M. saltuarius [ 27 ] and M. jingtaiensis [ 16 ] have distributions spanning adjacent provinces, further underscoring the region‘s significance as a diversification center. Despite these advances, species delimitation within Mazus remains challenging due to considerable morphological variability and overlapping diagnostic characters [ 7 , 8 ]. The genus exhibits substantial plasticity in vegetative and floral traits, which has historically led to taxonomic confusion and differing species concepts [ 1 , 5 , 7 ]. Recent integrative studies have emphasized the importance of combining morphological, molecular phylogenetic, and ecological evidence for robust species delimitation [ 5 , 13 , 14 , 17 ]. During recent field investigations in Zhejiang and Anhui provinces, we discovered an unknown Mazus taxon referable to section Trichogynus , characterized by a pubescent ovary and distinctively elongated stolons. Morphological examination, comparison with type material, and phylogenetic analyses based on chloroplast DNA regions ( rps16 , rbcL , and trnL-trnF ) support its recognition as a distinct species. Here, we describe this taxon as Mazus zhewanensis sp. nov. , provide diagnostic characters distinguishing it from related species, and present molecular evidence supporting its species status. A key to the pubescent-fruited species of Mazus in China is also provided. Materials and methods Morphological examination We examined herbarium collections from PE, ZM, ZJMR, and 17 other herbaria (full list in S1 Table ), supplemented by fresh field collections. In total, over 120 specimens representing more than 20 species of Mazus were studied, including type material of M. spicatus , M. xiuningensis , M. jiangshiense , and M. caducifer . For the new species, we measured ≥20 living plants in the field using digital calipers (0.1 mm accuracy), recording both qualitative (shape, color, indumentum) and quantitative (length, width, ratios) characters. A stereomicroscope (Olympus SZX16) was used to examine fine morphological features such as indumentum and serration margins. Field identifications were later verified against voucher specimens. Data for other taxa came from the literature and from direct examination of herbarium specimens. Voucher information for all material examined is provided in S1 Table . Taxon sampling Fresh leaves from three populations were collected for DNA extraction. The leaves were placed in non-woven fabric bags and dehydrated in sealed bags with color-indicator silica gel. Voucher specimens from each population were deposited in the Herbarium of Medicinal Resources, Zhejiang Academy of Traditional Chinese Medicine (ZJMR); the Zhejiang Museum of Natural History (ZM); and the Institute of Botany, Chinese Academy of Sciences (PE). DNA extraction, PCR amplification, and sequencing To elucidate the phylogenetic relationships and taxonomic placement of the proposed new species within Mazus , we conducted phylogenetic analyses based on three chloroplast DNA regions ( rbcL , rps16 , and trnL-trnF ) and the nuclear ribosomal internal transcribed spacer (ITS). Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification was performed using the following primers: rbcL , RH1/Z1352RC [ 28 ]; rps 16, rps 16-2F/ rps 16-R3 [ 29 ]; trnL-trnF , the c/f primers [ 30 ]; and ITS, ITS1/ITS4 [ 31 ]. Laboratory protocols and downstream data processing followed a published workflow [ 17 ]. To further resolve the phylogenetic placement of M. zhewanensis within the M. caducifer complex, we expanded our sampling of M. caducifer by collecting and sequencing additional specimens from three populations in Zhejiang Province. These newly obtained sequences were combined with publicly available data from GenBank to generate an expanded dataset for phylogenetic reconstruction. Genetic distance and molecular diagnostic characters Genetic distances between and within species were calculated using the ape package [ 32 ] in R [ 33 ], based on the Kimura 2-parameter (K2P) model [ 34 ]. To identify fixed nucleotide diagnostic sites between M. zhewanensis and its closest relative M. caducifer , we examined aligned chloroplast sequences ( rps16 , rbcL , and trnL-trnF ) using the seqinr package in R [ 33 , 35 ]. All phylogenetic analyses were conducted using PhyloSuite [ 38 ]. We employed three inference methods: Neighbor-Joining (NJ), Maximum Likelihood (ML), and Bayesian Inference (BI). For ML analyses, we used IQ-TREE 3.0.1 [ 39 ] implemented in PhyloSuite, with the best-fit substitution models selected by ModelFinder [ 40 ] and node support assessed using the Ultrafast Bootstrap [ 41 ] method with 1,000 replicates. BI was conducted using MrBayes [ 42 ] within PhyloSuite, with Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) chains run until convergence was achieved. NJ analyses were performed using MEGA X [ 43 ] via PhyloSuite, based on Kimura 2-parameter (K2P) distances with 1,000 bootstrap replicates. Additionally, we conducted separate phylogenetic analyses for the ITS and chloroplast datasets to assess cytonuclear discordance. The congruent results from these analyses were used to infer the evolutionary relationships among the species. To quantitatively assess the congruence between the ITS and chloroplast datasets, we performed a partition homogeneity test (ILD) [ 44 ] implemented in PAUP* 4.0a [ 45 ], with 1,000 replicates, using parsimony as the optimality criterion. Gaps were treated as missing data, and heuristic searches were conducted with random stepwise addition (10 replicates) and tree-bisection-reconnection (TBR) branch-swapping. Results and discussion Morphological comparison M. zhewanensis is morphologically distinct from all pubescent-fruited Mazus species in China, characterized by erect stems, long stolons, elliptic cauline leaves with shallowly and densely serrate margins, and a relatively large corolla. Among these, it is most similar to M. spicatus , but the two are readily distinguished by the shape and margin of the cauline leaves: elliptic with shallow, dense serrations in M. zhewanensis versus obovate to obovate-spatulate with incised-serrate margins in M. spicatus . Phylogenetically, M. zhewanensis is sister to M. caducifer ( Fig 1 ). They differ markedly in stolons and calyx morphology: M. zhewanensis produces long, trailing stolons that root adventitiously at the nodes, whereas stolons are completely absent in M. caducifer ; the calyx is campanulate in M. zhewanensis but infundibular in M. caducifer . M. zhewanensis is also distinguished from stoloniferous species without a distinct erect stem, such as M. xiuningensis and M. jiangshiense , by its well-developed erect stem and corresponding leaf morphology. Molecular phylogenetic relationship The chloroplast dataset strongly supported the monophyly of M. zhewanensis (ML = 95%; BI = 1) and recovered it as sister to M. caducifer (ML = 96%; NJ = 0.91; BI = 1) ( Fig 2 ). In contrast, the ITS dataset alone failed to resolve the relationship between M. zhewanensis and M. caducifer , with individuals of the two species intermingled and bootstrap support generally below 50% ( S1 – S3 Figs ). The ILD test revealed significant incongruence between the ITS and chloroplast datasets (P = 0.001, based on 1,000 replicates), indicating conflicting phylogenetic signals between the two partitions. A combined analysis was therefore not performed. The incongruence between the chloroplast and ITS phylogenies indicates cytonuclear discordance, a phenomenon previously documented in Mazus [ 5 , 13 , 17 ], likely reflecting rapid diversification, incomplete lineage sorting, or chloroplast capture following historical hybridization. Marker choice also affects resolution: chloroplast regions, being maternally inherited and slower-evolving, typically resolve deeper nodes, whereas ITS often fails to recover recent divergences [ 5 , 13 , 17 ]. This new species is similar in appearance to M. spicatus Vaniot, but differs in its stolons 25–70 (–100) cm long (vs. 10–30 cm), leaf blade of erect stem elliptic with shallowly and densely serrate margin (vs. obovate to obovate-spatulate with incised-serrate margin), and corolla 1.4–2.0 cm long (vs. 0.8–1.2 cm). It is closely related to M. caducifer Hance, but differs in having long trailing stolons with adventitious roots produced at the nodes where they touch the ground (vs. no stolons and no adventitious roots at the nodes) and campanulate calyx (vs. infundibular calyx). Description. Perennial herb , taproot 1–2 cm long or inconspicuous; lateral roots slender, numerous, fascicled. Erect stems solitary, obtusely quadrangular, woody at the base with age, 6–38 cm in total height including the inflorescence, densely covered with spreading multicellular white villous; new branches often produced from leaf axils near the base of inflorescence late in flowering. Stolons arise from the base of erect stem or leaf axils near inflorescence base, somewhat slender, arching, pendent, or decumbent, 25–70(–100) cm long, nodes distant, producing adventitious roots where touching ground, internodes 3–13 cm, much longer than leaves. Basal leaves often withered at anthesis, subrosulate; blade ovate-elliptic, 1.7–4.5 × 1.3–2.0 cm; petiole 1.0–2.5 cm. Erect stem leaves 5–9, opposite or alternate in upper portion; blade elliptic, 2.5–4.0(–7.0) × 1.0–2.3(–3.0) cm; petiole 0.5–1.0 cm. Stolon leaves opposite or alternate in upper portion, basal 1–2 pairs often withered, the largest leaves located in middle-lower section; blade 2–3.8 × 1–2 cm, nearly sessile, leaves gradually smaller toward both ends. All leaves membranous, apex obtuse, base attenuate-cuneate and decurrent, margin shallowly and densely serrate, both surfaces sparsely white-strigose, veins beneath multicellular white villous. Inflorescence a terminal raceme on the main stem, 11–28 cm long, rachis densely multicellular white villous; flowers (6–)10–20 or more, loosely arranged; bracts narrowly linear-lanceolate, apex acuminate; pedicel 6–10 mm long, multicellular white villous. Calyx campanulate, 5–6 mm long at anthesis, 8–9 mm in fruit, ca. 4 mm in diameter, externally white-strigose and glandular-pubescent, glabrous within; veins 10, distinct, sparsely multicellular white villous; 5-lobed to middle, lobes lanceolate, nearly equal to or slightly longer than tube, margin ciliate. Corolla pale purple, 1.4–2.0 cm long, bilabiate; tube 6–8 mm; upper lip erect, 6–8 mm, apex 2-lobed, lobes narrowly triangular, entire; lower lip broad, 3-lobed, lateral lobes 6–10 mm, median lobe 8–12 mm, apex obtuse to emarginate, interior with 2 raised rugae at middle and throat, yellow-spotted, densely clavate-glandular. Stamens 4, the anterior pair longer than posterior; filaments coherent in pairs, adnate to corolla tube at middle-lower part, free portion of anterior filaments ca. 4.2 mm, posterior pair ca. 3 mm; anther cells 2, divaricate. Ovary obovoid, apex densely short-pilose intermixed with sparse, long hirsute hairs; style slender-cylindric; stigma deeply 2-lobed, lobes ovate, sparsely short-pilose along the margins. Capsule obovoid, ca. 2–3 mm in diameter, apex short-pilose intermixed with sparse, long hirsute hairs, dehiscing by 2-valves. Seeds small, numerous, surface finely reticulate. Habitat and distribution. M. zhewanensis is primarily distributed in Lin’an District of Zhejiang Province and Jixi County of Anhui Province. It grows on shady, moist steep slopes at forest edges and moist ravine forests, at elevations of 650–1,350 m. Phenology. Flowering from April to August, fruiting from June to October. Etymology. The epithet “zhewanensis” is derived from the abbreviations of Zhejiang Province and Anhui Province, the type locality of the new species. Conservation assessment. Based on currently available data, M. zhewanensis is confirmed from only two localities in Zhejiang and Anhui provinces, which likely represent two subpopulations. Preliminary estimates indicate a restricted Area of Occupancy (AOO) and a presumably small total population size. However, precise calculation of its Extent of Occurrence (EOO) and a comprehensive census of mature individuals are not yet available. Consequently, according to the IUCN Red List Categories and Criteria [ 46 ], this species is preliminarily assessed as Data Deficient (DD) due to the current uncertainties regarding its full distribution range and population parameters. M. zhewanensis Integrative taxonomy and species delimitation We adopted an integrative taxonomic framework combining morphological, molecular phylogenetic, and ecological evidence for species delimitation. This framework follows contemporary standards emphasizing multiple independent lines of evidence, population-level sampling, and open data accessibility. Integration of these complementary datasets robustly supports M. zhewanensis as a distinct species and avoids taxonomic decisions based solely on minor morphological differences.
