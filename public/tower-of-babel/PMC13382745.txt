Modes of Occurrence of Gallium in Al-Rich Coal: A Case Study of Permian Coal in Jungar Coalfield
Authors: Linghao Huang, Bo Jiu, Wenhui Huang, Nana Mu, Zhenguang Shang, Huidi Hao, Zhaoguo Wang, Binchao Qin, Yingying Zhao, Haonan Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Al-rich coal deposits serve as a strategic alternative source for Gallium (Ga), a critical metal essential for the semiconductor industry. While boehmite is a well-known host for Ga in Al-rich coals, the modes of occurrence of Ga in boehmite-free, Al-rich coals remain poorly understood. This study investigates the mineralogy and in situ geochemistry of the Permian No. 4 coal seam from the Huangyuchuan Mine, Jungar Coalfield (Ordos Basin, China), employing a combination of optical microscopy, Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)-EDS, LA-ICP-MS, and TOF-SIMS. Bulk geochemical analysis reveals that the studied coal samples have average Al2O3 and Ga concentrations of 10.43 wt % and 15.96 ppm, respectively. Within this sample set, a strong positive correlation between Al2O3 and Ga (Pearson coefficient r = 0.89) is observed for the majority of samples, excluding the anomalous sample 4–12. This specific sample (sample 4–12) exhibits a significantly lower Al2O3 content (3.66 wt %) but a high Ga concentration (19.7 ppm), deviating from the established inorganic-hosted trend and serving as a critical specimen to investigate the organic affinity of Ga. Five kaolinite morphotypes were identified: cell-filling, epigenetic layered, epigenetic clay-like, clastic, and vermicular. However, contrary to the bulk geochemical correlation, in situ mapping via TOF-SIMS and LA-ICP-MS reveals that Ga is decoupled from authigenic aluminosilicates at the microscale. Instead, Ga exhibits a strong affinity for the organic matrix (specifically collotelinite) rather than secondary kaolinite phases. Geochemical proxies (e.g., Al2O3/TiO2, Sr/Ba, V/Ni) indicate the coal formed in an anoxic, freshwater peat-forming environment. We propose that Ga was initially liberated from the weathering of terrigenous feldspathic detritus and subsequently adsorbed by organic matter during peat accumulation, which limited its remobilization into later-stage authigenic minerals. This study highlights that while inorganic minerals govern Ga distribution broadly, organic associations act as a crucial complementary mechanism for Ga enrichment, particularly in the low-ash intervals of Al-rich coal systems lacking boehmite.

Introduction

Gallium (Ga) is recognized globally as a strategic critical metal, serving as the cornerstone of the modern semiconductor and electronics industries. Due to its unique physicochemical properties, Ga is indispensable in advanced applications ranging from optics and optoelectronics to clean energy technologies. − While the global demand for Ga continues to surge, the reserves of high-grade bauxiteits primary industrial sourceare facing rapid depletion. , Consequently, the exploration and recovery of Ga from alternative nonbauxite repositories, particularly Al-rich coal deposits, have emerged as a strategic imperative for sustaining the supply chain of critical materials. ,,

As a typical dispersed element, Ga rarely forms independent minerals in nature. Instead, it is primarily enriched in deposits such as bauxite, coal, and lead–zinc ores through isomorphous substitution. , According to global estimates, the average Ga concentration is 6.0 ppm in hard coals and 5.5 ppm in brown coals; while the weighted average for Chinese coals is slightly higher at 6.55 ppm. Ga-enriched coals with elevated aluminum (Al) concentrations in China are predominantly distributed within the Heidaigou Mine (No. 6 coal, 44.8 ppm), Haerwusu Mine (No. 6 coal, 18 ppm), and Guanbanwusu Mine (No. 6 coal, 12.9 ppm) of the Jungar and Daqingshan coalfields in Inner Mongolia, necessitating detailed investigation into the distribution and occurrence modes of Ga in these Al-rich coal systems.

The modes of occurrence of Ga in coal are complex, involving partition between both inorganic and organic phases. ,, Geochemically, owing to the similar ionic radii of Ga3+ (0.62 Å) and Al3+ (0.54 Å), Ga tends to substitute for Al in aluminosilicates and aluminum hydroxides via isomorphous substitution. In the well-studied coals of the Jungar Coalfield (e.g., Heidaigou), boehmite (γ-AlOOH) is widely recognized as the primary carrier, ,,, alongside kaolinite.

Conversely, the affinity of Ga for organic matter offers an alternative enrichment mechanism. Bonnett and Czechowski identified Ga-porphyrins in coal, providing direct molecular evidence for the binding of Ga to organic matrices. Furthermore, microanalysis by Lyons et al. revealed significant Ga concentrations within vitrinite macerals, which in some instances exceeded the concentrations associated with inorganic elements like Al, Ti, and Fe. This distinct organic association challenges the assumption that Ga is exclusively hosted by minerals in all Al-rich coal systems. Hower et al. similarly showed that the presence of Ga in a bench from Blue Gem coal (Knox County, Kentucky) is closely related to vitrinite. Jiu et al. , showed that Ga can concentrate in specific vitrinite by using the mapping spectra derived from LA-ICP-MS (laser ablation-inductively coupled plasma-mass spectrometry), and the in situ analysis proved that the modes of occurrence of Ga is related to organic matter.

The analytical methods for the study of elemental assignment in coal are mainly divided into two categories, indirect methods and direct methods. At present, most of the studies on elemental assignment rely on indirect methods. For example, Dai et al. , and Wang et al. concluded that Ga in coal has high correlation with the concentrations of boehmite, and deduced that boehmite is the main carrier of Ga; Shao et al. showed that Ga has a strong inorganic affinity and kaolinite is the main carrier of Ga by correlation analysis of Ga concentrations with ash and Al2O3. Indirect methods involving bulk statistical correlations, although widely used, contain inherent uncertainties regarding the precise submicron scale distribution of elements.Direct methods for in situ analysis of Ga in coal by energy spectroscopy, spectral analysis, and mass spectrometry. These include optical microscopy, proton induced X-ray emission (PIXE), sensitive high resolution ion microprobe (SHRIMP), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), scanning electron microscopy with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM-EDS), electron microprobe analysis (EMPA), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), LA-ICP-MS and time-of-flight of secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS).

For example, Jiu et al. demonstrated that Ga can also concentrate in specific organic matter by using the mapping spectra derived from LA-ICP-MS. Dai et al. demonstrated that Ga mainly existed in the aluminosilicates in the coal parting by using the mapping spectra derived from TOF-SIMS. Compared to indirect statistical correlations, direct in situ analysis offers precise visualization of elemental distribution within complex coal matrices. While LA-ICP-MS is widely employed for trace element quantification, its spatial resolution is constrained by the laser spot diameter, which limits the ability to distinguish elemental partitioning within fine-grained mineral intergrowths. To overcome this limitation, this study integrates TOF-SIMS with LA-ICP-MS. The submicron spatial resolution of TOF-SIMS allows for the detailed mapping of Ga distribution across organic macerals and authigenic clay minerals, complementing the quantitative concentration data provided by LA-ICP-MS. This dual-technique approach provides a more robust and comprehensive characterization of Ga modes of occurrence than either method alone.

Extensive investigations have characterized the distribution and modes of occurrence of Ga in the Carboniferous coals of the Jungar Coalfield, specifically within the boehmite-rich seams of the Haerwusu, Heidaigou, and Guanbanwusu mines. ,,, However, the enrichment mechanisms of Ga in the overlying Permian Al-rich coals, which are often distinct in mineralogical composition (e.g., boehmite-free), remain poorly understood despite their significant resource potential. To bridge this knowledge gap, this study focuses on the No. 4 coal seam of the Lower Permian Shanxi Formation at the Huangyuchuan Mine. We employ a multiscale analytical framework, integrating thin section-based petrographic observation and bulk geochemistry with high-resolution in situ imaging techniques, specifically TOF-SIMS and LA-ICP-MS mapping. This approach, utilizing polished thin sections rather than standard particulate pellets, allows us to preserve the critical spatial petrographic context and establish a direct correlation between macerals and their associated mineral phases. By correlating these microscale elemental distributions with paleoenvironmental proxies, this work aims to clarify the disputed affinity of Ga for organic matter versus clay minerals and elucidate the metallogenic processes controlling critical metal accumulation in Permian coal-bearing strata.

Geological Settings

The Jungar Coalfield is situated along the northeastern margin of the Ordos Cratonic Basin on the North China Plate (Figure ), characterized by a NW–SE-striking monoclinal structure dipping westward, superimposed by secondary N–S-trending folds. Post-Cambrian tectonic quiescence has prevailed in this region, with minimal orogenic activity recorded since the Early Paleozoic. During the Early–Middle Ordovician, crustal subsidence established a shallow marine carbonate platform. Termination of this regime occurred during the late Middle Ordovician Caledonian Orogeny, triggering regional uplift and prolonged subaerial exposure that resulted in stratigraphic absence of Late Ordovician through Early Carboniferous successions. Renewed subsidence during the Late Pennsylvanian (C–P transition) facilitated paralic sedimentation under transgressive-regressive cycles, preserved in the Upper Carboniferous Benxi (fluvial-estuarine) and Taiyuan (barrier-lagoon) formations. Early Permian continental conditions promoted deltaic-alluvial plain deposition, culminating in the Shanxi Formation. The 110–160 m thick Carboniferous–Permian coal measures unconformably overlie Middle Ordovician limestone karst surfaces, comprising 11 vertically stacked seams (designated 1–10, with Seam 6 subdivided into upper/lower subunits) that yield an aggregate thickness of 30.50 m (individual seams: 2.8–6.3 m).

(a) Location of the Junger Coalfield in the Ordos Basin, China. , (b) Location and sampling position of the Huangyuchuan Coal Mine in the Junger Coalfield. (c) Sedimentary sequence histogram of Carboniferous-Permian Strata in the Jungar Coalfield.

The Huangyuchuan Coal Mine is situated in the southwestern Jungar Coalfield of Inner Mongolia, structurally positioned within the Tianjiashanxiang Flexure Belt. The stratigraphic framework exhibits a westward-dipping monoclinal configuration with gentle inclinations of 1°–3°. This investigation focuses on the Permian Shanxi Formation’s No. 4 coal seam, a shallow-buried (burial depth <150 m) stratigraphic unit demonstrating thickness variability from 1.40 to 5.55 m (mean 3.27 m). The immediate roof succession comprises sandy mudstone interbedded with coarse- to fine-grained sandstone units, whereas the floor strata are dominated by mechanically weak lithologies (mudstone and sandy mudstone) with subordinate semicompetent beds. The seam architecture displays moderate complexity, containing 1–5 (typically 2) partingscomposed primarily of mudstone and tonstein (volcanic ash)  yet it maintains a regionally consistent geometry characteristic of a ’structurally complex but stratigraphically stable’ coal reservoir type. The tonstein partings represent significant episodic inputs of felsic volcanic-derived materials into the peat-forming environment. This stratigraphic feature is consistent with the regional depositional characteristics of the Lower Permian Shanxi Formation in the Jungar Coalfield.

Samples and Methods

Samples

A total of 12 samples were collected from the No. 4 coal seam of the Lower Permian Shanxi Formation at the Huangyuchuan Coal Mine, Jungar Coalfield. To ensure the representativeness of the entire coal seam, the samples were collected as a vertical channel profile with a consistent sampling interval of approximately 10 cm. The sample set comprises seven coal bench samples (HYC-4–3, −5, −6, −8, −10, −11, −12), three roof/floor mudstones (HYC-4–1, −4, −9), and two tonstein partings (HYC-4–2, −7).

An integrated analytical workflow was employed, progressing from bulk characterization to high-resolution in situ analysis. First, polished thin sections were prepared for petrographic observation and mineralogical identification using optical microscopy. Second, bulk geochemical compositions were quantified, with major oxides determined by X-ray fluorescence (XRF) and trace elements by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS). Finally, to characterize the microscale distribution of Ga, selected representative samples underwent advanced in situ analysis, including SEM-EDS, LA-ICP-MS mapping, and TOF-SIMS ion imaging.

Methods

Microscopic Observation and Analysis

Coal samples were prepared as polished thin sections (approximately 30 μm thick) to facilitate correlated observation of organic macerals under oil-immersion reflected light and minerals under transmitted light. While standard maceral analysis typically utilizes particulate pellets, the polished thin section technique was adopted in this study to preserve the spatial petrographic context essential for the subsequent in situ TOF-SIMS imaging. Petrographic analysis was conducted using an Olympus BX51 microscope equipped with an Olympus DP73 camera.

Maceral identification and classification were performed on polished thin sections using combined transmitted and reflected light microscopy. The classification adhered to the nomenclature and genetic principles established by the ICCP. − It should be noted that while these standards were originally designed for reflected-light observation of particulate blocks, we applied these criteria to thin-section microscopy by correlating morphological features under transmitted light with the corresponding reflectance characteristics observed in reflected light. Quantitative maceral analysis was conducted via the point-counting method (minimum of 500 points per sample) to ensure statistical representativeness, following the general analytical principles of organic petrology. This dual-light approach ensures a comprehensive identification of both organic macerals and associated mineral phases in their original spatial context.

For the specific requirements of in situ geochemical analysis, a dual-observation strategy was adopted: Reflected light observation was used to identify maceral groups (vitrinite, inertinite, liptinite) and locate target areas for TOF-SIMS mapping; Transmitted light observation was employed to characterize the morphology and paragenesis of translucent clay minerals (e.g., cell-filling kaolinite).

Elemental Geochemistry

For major-element oxides, measurements were conducted using an Axios Max X-ray fluorescence spectrometer (XRF) in accordance with ASTM D4326 analytical standards. Trace elements were analyzed by iCAP Q inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometer (ICP-MS) following ASTM D6357 protocols. Loss on ignition (LOI) was determined using a P124S electronic analytical balance as per ASTM D3174 testing standards. All analyses of major-element oxides, trace elements, and LOI were performed at the laboratory of Hebei Institute of Regional Geological and Mineral Survey.

SEM-EDS Analysis

To characterize the micromorphology and chemical composition of mineral phases, polished thin sections were sputter-coated with carbon to enhance conductivity and prevent surface charging. Analyses were conducted using an FEI Quanta 650 FEG scanning electron microscope equipped with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometer (SEM-EDS).

The instrument was operated at an accelerating voltage of 25 kV and a beam current of 9 nA, with a working distance maintained between 10 and 15 mm. Backscattered electron (BSE) imaging was employed to distinguish mineral phases from the organic matrix based on atomic number contrast. Simultaneously, EDS point analysis was utilized to determine the semiquantitative major-element oxide compositions, facilitating the identification of specific mineral species (e.g., kaolinite, calcite) prior to trace element analysis. These experiments were performed at the Key Laboratory of Orogenic Zone and Crustal Evolution, Peking University.

LA-ICP-MS Single-Mineral Assessment

For characterizing the Ga concentration within specific minerals in the samples, LA-ICP-MS analysis was employed due to the lower detection limit of SEM-EDS. After thin section preparation, samples were loaded into the instrument. Surface purging was conducted prior to laser ablation and subsequent mass spectrometric analysis using the individual spot mode. Specific analytical parameters are detailed in Table . The LA-ICP-MS instrumentation used comprised an NWR ImageGEO laser ablation system coupled to a PlasmaQuant MS-Q ICP-MS (Elemental Scientific Lasers & Analytik Jena). This analysis was performed at the Center for High Pressure Science & Technology Advanced Research (HPSTAR).

TOF-SIMS In Situ Analysis

Based on the petrographic and SEM observations, representative regions of interest (ROIs) exhibiting distinct maceral-mineral associations were selected for high-resolution mass spectrometric imaging. In-situ analyses were conducted using a TOF.SIMS 5–100 instrument (ION-TOF GmbH, Münster, Germany) at the Analysis Center, Tsinghua University.

The instrument was equipped with a Bi+ primary ion gun operating at an energy of 30 keV with a 45° incidence angle to generate secondary ions. To remove surface contamination and establish chemical equilibrium prior to analysis, the sample surface was presputtered using a 2 keV O2 + ion beam (45° incidence) at a calibrated sputtering rate of 1.31 nm/s (relative to SiO2). Positive secondary ion spectra were acquired over a mass range of 0–900 amu.

Following sample loading into the ultrahigh vacuum (UHV) chamber, ion images were acquired by rastering the primary beam over the selected ROIs. Data acquisition and postprocessing were performed using the SurfaceLab software (ION-TOF GmbH). The resulting high-resolution 2D ion maps visualized the lateral distribution of key isotopes (e.g., 69Ga+, 71Ga+, 27Al+, 28Si+, 40Ca+, 7Li+, 39K+), allowing for the precise correlation of elemental signals with microtextural features observed during the aforementioned petrographic analysis.

Results

Coal Petrography

Petrographic analysis of the No. 4 coal seam reveals a maceral assemblage dominated by the vitrinite group, with subordinate amounts of inertinite and liptinite. The vitrinite group consists predominantly of collotelinite (subgroup telovitrinite) and minor collodetrinite (subgroup detrovitrinite). The inertinite fraction is characterized by fusinite and semifusinite, while the liptinite group occurs primarily as sporinite.

Notably, the target sample for in situ analysis (HYC-4–12) displays exceptional preservation of botanical cell structures. As shown in Figure a, the cellular cavities within the telinite/collotelinite matrix are frequently filled with authigenic kaolinite and granular calcite. Due to compaction, sporinite bodies appear as lenticular or ribbon-like intercalations embedded within the collotelinite matrix (Figure a). Quantitative analysis (based on >600 points) yielded a maceral composition of 97.8% vitrinite and 2.2% liptinite (mineral-free basis), with inertinite being virtually absent in this specific sample.

Minerals in Coal

SEM-EDS characterization reveals that kaolinite is the predominant mineral phase in the studied samples, exhibiting diverse morphologies that reflect distinct genetic stages. Based on microtextural relationships and occurrence modes, five distinct kaolinite morphotypes were identified: cell-filling kaolinite, epigenetic layered kaolinite, epigenetic clay-like kaolinite, clastic kaolinite, vermicular kaolinite, with representative micrographs presented in Figures and .

Cell-filling kaolinite predominantly occurs in sample 4–12 (Figure a), with sporadic occurrences in sample 4–9 (Figure a), displaying discrete particulate forms within telinite cellular cavities.

Epigenetic layered kaolinite exhibits concentrated distributions in samples 4–12 (Figures b, a, a) and 4–7 (Figures d, a). In sample 4–12, the layered kaolinite demonstrates epigenetic characteristics through compaction-induced intercalation within collotelinite, containing trace Fe (Spectrum 82). The kaolinite layer in sample 4–7 exhibit authigenic cleavage patterns under transmitted light (Figure b), showing pure Al–Si–O compositions. Epigenetic clay-like kaolinite was identified in sample 4–9 (Figure c), exhibiting irregular morphology with diffuse boundaries and variable grain sizes ranging from ca. 20 to 200 μm. This morphotype demonstrates pervasive distribution throughout the sample matrix.

Clastic kaolinite occurs primarily in sample 4–7 (Figure c) and 4–9 (Figure g). In sample 4–7, sub-20 μm angular fragments are dispersed within collotelinite matrix with homogeneous Al–Si–O signatures. Contrastingly, sample 4–9 contains coarser (ca. 100 μm) detrital grains displaying irregular, sharply defined boundaries.

Vermicular kaolinite in sample 4–9 (Figures b, e) reveal distinct cleavage patterns under both reflected and transmitted light. Comparative analysis of color-zoned domains demonstrates compositional partitioning: dark regions (Spectrum 45) maintain pure Al–Si–O signatures, while brighter zones (Spectrum 47) exhibit significant rare earth element (REE) enrichment (La, Ce, Nd). Systematic multipoint verification confirms this REE partitioning pattern, suggesting selective metal adsorption during vermicular kaolinite formation.

Notably, sample 4–9 contains a zircon-kaolinite assemblage (Figure c), where spectrum 42 corresponds to a bright zircon grain overlying a larger, darker kaolinite particle.

According to previous methods, we use CC (CC = ratio of element concentration in investigated coals vs other coals) as the ratio between the different elemental concentrations in the sample coals and the average values of Chinese coal (CC1), American coal (CC2), and world coal (CC3). Meanwhile, we categorized CC into six intervals, i.e., CC > 100, 100 > CC > 10, 10 > CC> 5, 5 > CC > 2, 2 > CC > 0.5, and 0.5 > CC, which denote the anomalous enrichment, superenrichment, significant enrichment, slight enrichment, normal, and deficit, respectively (Figure ).

Figure illustrates the enrichment characteristics of trace elements in the No. 4 coal seam relative to the averages of Chinese, American, and world coals.

Overall, the studied samples exhibit consistent enrichment in Ga across all comparison standards. When evaluated against average Chinese coal, most elements display normal abundances (0.5 < CC1 < 2), with the exception of slightly enriched Be (1.42) and Ga (1.55), and distinct depletion in Zn, Rb, Sr, Cd, Ba, and Tl. In contrast, comparisons with American and world coal averages reveal a broader suite of enriched elements, particularly high-field-strength elements (HFSEs) such as Zr, Nb, Hf, and Th; notably, Zr reaches an CC2 value of 5.21, indicative of anomalous accumulation. Conversely, Zn, Rb, Sr, Cd, and Ba remain consistently depleted relative to all three baselines.

Regarding rare earth elements (REEs), the total concentration in the studied samples averages 94.6 ppm. This value falls between the global coal average (68.40 ppm , ) and the Chinese coal average (137.90 ppm), indicating a moderate enrichment relative to world coals but a slight depletion compared to the regional Chinese background. LA-ICP-MS Result

In-situ LA-ICP-MS analysis was conducted to quantify Ga concentrations within individual mineral phases, with the results summarized in Table . Based on petrographic textures and genetic origins, kaolinite was categorized into four distinct morphotypes: authigenic layered, authigenic clay-like (massive), vermicular, and clastic (detrital) kaolinite (Figure ). The analytical data reveal significant heterogeneity in Ga enrichment across these morphotypes. Specifically, in sample HYC-4–9, Ga concentrations followed a descending order: clastic kaolinite (25.64 ppm) > authigenic clay-like kaolinite (20.05 ppm) > vermicular kaolinite (14.15 ppm). Crucially, however, the Ga concentrations within all measured kaolinite phases were consistently lower than the bulk-rock Ga concentration of the host coal (Figure i). This mass balance deficit implies that kaolinite is not the sole carrier of Ga and points toward a significant association with the organic matrix.

TOF-SIMS Mapping Result

Before performing the TOF-SIMS analysis, we examined the distribution of Ga concentrations across all samples. We observed that sample 4–12 deviates from the positive Al–Ga correlation trend established by the other coal samples (Figure ); specifically, it exhibits a significantly lower Al2O3 content but a higher Ga concentration relative to the expected values predicted by the linear regression of the other samples. This concentration decoupling suggests that, unlike the mineral-hosted Ga in the majority of our samples, Ga in sample 4–12 is likely sequestered within the organic matrix. Consequently, sample 4–12 was selected for high-resolution TOF-SIMS mapping, with three characteristic surfaces chosen to further elucidate this anomalous occurrence. Discussion

Modes of Occurrence of Ga

Previous studies generally postulate a strong geochemical coherence between Ga and Al in coal systems, attributing Ga enrichment primarily to clay minerals (e.g., kaolinite) via isomorphic substitution. , Indeed, our bulk geochemical data support this rule for most samples (Pearson r = 0.89, calculated excluding sample 4–12; Figure ). However, sample 4–12 presents a notable anomaly: it exhibits a low Al2O3 content (3.66%) yet a high Ga concentration (19.7 ppm), deviating significantly from the established Al–Ga correlation line.

In-situ TOF-SIMS mapping provides direct evidence explaining this anomaly. Across all mapped regions (Figures –), Ga exhibits a distinct decoupling from Al-rich mineral phases. Despite the pervasive presence of kaoliniteoccurring as epigenetic layered forms (Figure ), laminar aggregates (Figure ), and cell-lumen infillings (Figure )the elemental maps reveal that these kaolinite phases are remarkably depleted in Ga. For instance, in Figure , sharp geochemical discontinuities are observed at the interfaces between collotelinite and kaolinite-filled cell lumens, where Ga signals abruptly drop to background levels within the mineral phase. Similarly, fracture-filling calcite and sporadic pyrite/Illite particles show negligible Ga uptake.

TOF-SIMS Mapping of sample 4–12 from the Huangyuchuan Coal Mine, Area 3. (a) Reflected light image. (b) Transmitted light image. (c) Orthogonal light image. Ct: collotelinite. Kln: kaolinite. Cal: calcite.

These microscopic observations align with our LA-ICP-MS findings (Table ), which demonstrated that Ga concentrations in authigenic and vermicular kaolinite are consistently lower than the whole-coal average. The kaolinite in sample 4–12, characterized by its occurrence as cell fillings and compaction-deformed laminae, is interpreted as an authigenic precipitate derived from solutions or colloidal fluids rather than detrital input. Based on the observed absence of Ga in these late-stage authigenic minerals, we infer that Ga was relatively immobile during the epigenetic mineralization phase. Consequently, it is highly likely that Ga had already been fixed within the organic matrix during early diagenesis.

With the inorganic phases excluded as primary carriers in this specific microenvironment, the TOF-SIMS mapping unequivocally identifies the organic matter as the dominant host for Ga within sample 4–12. This indicates that in the low-ash intervals of the No. 4 coal seam, organic affinity plays a leading role in Ga accumulation. Furthermore, this organic affinity nd homogeneously distributed within collotelinite (telovitrinite subgroup) but is notably absent in sporinite (liptinite group) (Figure ).

The mapping results show a clear elemental boundary between the Ga-rich collotelinite matrix and the Ga-poor sporinite lenses. This selective enrichment suggests that Ga incorporation is not merely a physical adsorption process. Although direct molecular spectroscopic evidence is not available in this study, based on the diverse chemical properties of macerals, we postulate a plausible interpretation: the organic enrichment likely involves chemical complexation with specific oxygen-containing functional groups (e.g., carboxyl or phenolic hydroxyl groups). These functional groups are known to be abundant in vitrinite (derived from humic substances) but relatively scarce in liptinite (derived from lipid-rich plant materials). , The homogeneous distribution of Ga throughout the collotelinite, unaffected by the extensive compaction fabrics or the later injection of calcite/kaolinite veins, further confirms that Ga was incorporated into the organic structure prior to significant coalification and compaction.

In summary, Ga exhibits a mixed mode of occurrence in the No. 4 coal seam (Figure ). While the strong Al–Ga bulk correlation suggests a widespread inorganic association for the overall seam, direct in situ mapping demonstrates that in low-ash intervals (represented by sample 4–12), Ga predominantly occurs in an organically bound state within collotelinite. This localized organic mode of occurrence is interpreted to be driven by the preferential chelation of Ga by humic matter during peat accumulation. It is hypothesized that such early organic complexation prevented the subsequent mobilization of Ga into late-stage authigenic mineral precipitates.

Modes of Ga occurrence in Al-rich coal

Source of Ga in Al-Rich Coal

The Al2O3/TiO2 ratio is widely considered a robust proxy for constraining sediment provenance, particularly for igneous sources, as it exhibits minimal fractionation between source rocks and derived sediments. ,− Data summarized by Hayashi et al. showed that the ratio of Al2O3/TiO2 in mafic igneous rocks was 3–8 (SiO2 = 45–52 wt %); 8–21 (SiO2 = 53–66 wt %) for intermediate igneous rocks; and 21–70 (SiO2 = 66–76 wt %) for felsic igneous rocks. For the No. 4 coal seam, the Al2O3/TiO2 ratios of the top mudstones and partings mostly fall within the range of 20–70 (Figure b), with an average value of 63.44. Although tonsteins reflect episodic felsic volcanic ash-fall and mudstones indicate terrigenous clastic input, the consistency of these ratios across all lithologies suggests that the coal-bearing strata in the study area were fed by a common felsic igneous source. This interpretation corroborates the high Al2O3/TiO2 content observed in the study area, which significantly exceeds the average value for Chinese coals, pointing to a persistent felsic-dominated provenance during peat accumulation.

Ga concentrations of different samples and ratios of Al2O3/TiO2 ratios. (a) Ga concentration. (b) Al2O3/TiO2 ratios.

Mineral assemblages including quartz, zircon, goyazite, apatite (hexagonal), vermicular kaolinite identified within Carboniferous–Permian coal measures of the Weibei Coalfield by Wang et al. and Qin et al. demonstrated substantial input of felsic volcanic ash during peat accumulation, exerting critical control on the enrichment of strategic metals (Li, Ga, Zr). ,, SEM-EDS analysis of sample 4–9 identified a diagnostic mineral assemblage comprising quartz, zircon, and vermicular kaolinite (Figure e–g). The presence of these minerals, particularly the vermicular kaolinite and euhedral zircon, strongly serves as an indicator of felsic pyroclastic (volcanic ash) input into the No. 4 coal seam during peat accumulation. Geochemically, this interpretation is supported by the data shown in Figure (a), where the mean Ga concentration in the partings (mudstone and tonstein; 23.36 ppm) is significantly higher than that in the bulk coal (15.96 ppm). This contrast suggests that the detrital material derived from felsic igneous precursors is the primary geological source of Ga in the study area. Regarding the migration mechanism, Dai et al. proposed that during the weathering of Ga-enriched aluminous detritus (e.g., bauxite or volcanic ash) in peat mires, a significant proportion of Ga can be mobilized and subsequently immobilized through adsorption onto humic substances. Drawing upon this mechanism, we propose a genetic model for the studied coal: Ga was initially introduced via felsic volcaniclastic materials. Under the acidic and humid conditions of the peat swamp, Ga was leached from the unstable volcanic glass or feldspars and preferentially adsorbed by the surrounding organic matter. We propose that this early diagenetic process effectively sequestered Ga within the organic matrix, providing a plausible mechanism for its currently observed preservation within the collotelinite.

Paleoenvironment of the Peat for Al-Rich Coal

The concentrations of rare earth elements can be used as geochemical indicators for studying the depositional environment of paleosediments because they are particularly sensitive to changes in the environment, − such as Sr, Ba, U, V, Ni, Co, Cr, Eu and Ce. However, for coal-bearing sediments, the enrichment factors of trace elements are affected by a variety of factors, and only trace elements of sedimentary genesis can be analyzed for paleoenvironmental analysis. In this study, three types of data, w­(Sr)/w­(Ba), w­(V)/w­(Ni+V), and w­(Sr)/w­(Cu), were analyzed for paleo-salinity, redox conditions, and paleo-climate during the peat-forming period to comprehensively reconstruct the paleo-environment in the study area.

For w­(Sr)/w­(Ba) to judge paleo-salinity, we usually consider greater than 1 as a marine (brackish water) environment and less than 1 as a terrestrial (freshwater) environment; , for w­(V)/w­(Ni+V) to judge the redox environment, we usually consider a value of w­(V)/w­(Ni+V) of 1 to 0.83 as a quiescent environment, 0.83 to 0.57 as an anoxic environment, and 0.57 to 0.46 is an oxidized environment, and less than 0.46 is a more oxidized environment; , for w­(Sr)/w­(Cu) to judge the paleoclimate, we usually consider w­(Sr)/w­(Cu) values between 1 and 10 as a temperate and humid climate, and greater than 10 indicates a dry and hot climate. , The specific results are shown in Figure :

W­(Sr)/w­(Ba), w­(V)/w­(Ni+V) and w­(Sr)/w­(Cu) ratios of samples in Huangyuchuan Coal Mine. (a) w­(Sr)/w­(Ba). (b) w­(V)/w­(Ni+V). (c) w­(V)/w­(Ni+V).

From Figure , we can see that w­(Sr)/w­(Ba) ratios in the study area are all less than 1 (the average value is 0.514), indicating that the paleo-depositional environment in the study area was characterized by terrestrial (freshwater) sedimentation; w­(V)/w­(Ni+V) ratios are basically located in the range of 0.57–0.83 (the average value is 0.677), indicating that the paleoredox conditions in the study area were characterized by an anoxic environment.; w­(Sr)/w­(Cu) ratios are all in the range of 1 to 10 (the average value is 3.153), indicating that the study area experienced a temperate-humid paleoclimate during peat accumulation. Synthesizing the geochemical indicators (e.g., Sr/Ba < 1), the paleo-environment during the peat accumulation of the No. 4 coal seam is characterized as an anoxic, warm, and humid freshwater setting. Specifically, while the underlying formations represent marine-continental transitional sequences, regional geological evidence and our proxy data indicate that the specific peat-forming interval of the No. 4 coal seam developed within a terrestrial, fluvial-dominated mire environment. This distinction precludes marine transgression as a controlling factor during peat formation. Consequently, we conclude that seawater intrusion was not a source of Ga enrichment in the study area.

Conclusion

The No. 4 coal seam of the Lower Permian Shanxi Formation, situated in the Huangyuchuan Coal Mine, Junggar Coalfield, is characterized by a maceral assemblage dominated by vitrinite, with subordinate liptinite and trace amounts of inertinite. Geochemical analyses reveal significant enrichment of Al2O3 (10.43 wt %), Ga (15.96 ppm), Li, Zr, and Nb, alongside depletion in CaO, MgO, and REEs compared to average Chinese coals. A strong correlation exists between Al2O3 and Ga concentrations (Pearson’s r = 0.89, calculated excluding the anomalous sample 4–12). Paleo-environmental proxies including mean Sr/Ba (0.514), V/(V+Ni) (0.677), and Sr/Cu (3.153) ratios collectively indicate deposition in an anoxic, humid, freshwater fluvial-dominated mire environment.

Five kaolinite morphotypes were identified: clastic, epigenetic clay-like, epigenetic layered, cell-filling, and vermicular kaolinite, coexisting with vein calcite, zircon, and Illite. TOF-SIMS mapping of the anomalous low-ash sample − demonstrates Ga is homogeneously distributed within collotelinite but absent in sporinite and cellular cavities, indicating differential organic affinity. Notably, Ga shows no spatial correlation with cell-filling kaolinite, calcite, or epigenetic layered kaolinite. LA-ICP-MS analyses confirm that Ga concentrations in epigenetic banded kaolinite, epigenetic clay-like kaolinite, detrital kaolinite, and vermicular kaolinite are 18.13, 20.05, 25.64, and 14.15 ppm, respectively. Crucially, these in situ values are lower than their corresponding bulk-rock Ga concentrations determined by conventional ICP-MS analysis, demonstrating that kaolinite is not the primary Ga host.″

The mineral assemblage of quartz-zircon-vermicular kaolinite in selected sample, integrated with geochemical evidence, supports a volcaniclastic origin for Ga rather than marine influence. We propose Ga was liberated during weathering of volcanic debris, adsorbed by organic matter, and fixed homogeneously within collotelinite. Despite widespread epigenetic kaolinite formation, Ga exhibits limited chemical mobility into secondary clay minerals, highlighting its stability in organic phases under moderate hydrothermal conditions.

The absence of discrete Ga-enriched minerals, such as boehmite or chlorite, in the studied samples underscores the vital role of organic matter in Ga retention, particularly acting as the dominant host within the low-ash intervals of the No. 4 coal seam. While this study elucidates the specific association between collotelinite and Ga in the absence of competing mineral phases, the mineralogical simplicity of the No. 4 coal seam contrasts with other complex Ga-rich deposits. Future investigations should therefore target coal systems exhibiting more diverse paragenetic assemblagesspecifically those containing coexisting boehmite, colloidal precipitates, and multistage kaolinite morphotypes. Such comparative research is essential to quantitatively analysis the partitioning of Ga among different genetic types of aluminosilicates; and unravel the mechanisms of Ga remobilization and redistribution during organic–inorganic interactions and diagenetic mineral transformation.
