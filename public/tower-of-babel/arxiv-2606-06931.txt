Derivations and local derivations on Euclidean Lie algebras


Abstract
The present paper is devoted to studying derivations and local derivations on the Euclidean Lie algebras 𝔢 ⁡ ( n ) \mathfrak{e}(n) . We give a complete desrciption of the derivation algebra of 𝔢 ⁡ ( n ) \mathfrak{e}(n) for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 . Furthermore, we prove that every local derivations on the 𝔢 ⁡ ( n ) \mathfrak{e}(n) is a derivation.

Introduction

The notion of a local derivation was introduced and studied independently by Kadison
[
10
]
and Larson and Sourour
[
12
]
in 1990 for Banach (and associative) algebras. Given an algebra
A
, a linear map
\Delta:A\to A
is called a
local derivation
if for every
x\in A
there exists a derivation
D_{x}:A\to A
(depending on
x
) such that
\Delta(x)=D_{x}(x)
. Local derivations reflect an interesting class of local-to-global phenomena on various algebraic structures; see
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
5
,
9
,
13
,
15
]
and the references therein.

Over the past decade, considerable attention has been devoted to local derivations on Lie (super)algebras. A central theme in this line of research is to determine whether all local derivations are indeed derivations for a given Lie (super)algebra. This property has been established for finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebras
[
7
]
, Borel subalgebras of finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras
[
20
]
, the Witt algebra
[
8
]
, the Witt algebra over fields of prime characteristic
[
19
]
, the
W(2,2)
algebra
[
17
]
, the super Virasoro algebra
[
18
]
, the conformal Galilei algebra
[
2
]
, the Schrödinger algebras
[
16
,
3
]
, and solvable Lie algebras of maximal rank
[
11
]
. On the other hand, certain Lie algebras admit
pure
local derivations-that is, local derivations which fail to be derivations. Examples include finite-dimensional filiform Lie algebras
[
7
]
and the
1
-spatial ageing algebra
\mathfrak{age}(1)
[
14
]
.

Let
E(n)
denote the group of Euclidean motions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
; this is the noncompact semidirect product group
SO(n)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The complexification of its Lie algebra, denoted by
\mathfrak{e}(n)
, admits a basis

\{E_{i,j},H_{k}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n,\ 1\leq k\leq n\}

with nonzero commutation relations given by

[E_{i,j},E_{j,k}]=E_{i,k},\quad[E_{i,j},H_{j}]=H_{i},\quad[E_{i,j},H_{i}]=-H_{j},

where we set
E_{i,j}=-E_{j,i}
throughout. The
2
-local derivations and bi-derivations of
\mathfrak{e}(3)
were studied in
[
1
]
. For
n\geq 4
, the problem of characterizing local derivations on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
has remained open.

In this paper, we determine all derivations and local derivations on the Euclidean Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(n)
for
n\geq 4
. Our main results can be summarized as follows:

•

We give a complete description of
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{e}(n))
(Theorem
3.4
);

•

We prove that every local derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
is a derivation (Theorem
4.4
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we collect basic definitions and establish notation. In Section
3
, we characterize the derivations of
\mathfrak{e}(n)
. In Section
4
, we show that every local derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
is a derivation.

Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{C}
denotes the field of complex numbers, and all vector spaces and algebras are understood to be over
\mathbb{C}
unless stated otherwise.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some definitions, symbols and notations for later use in this paper.

The group
E(n)
of Euclidean motions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the noncompact semidirect product group
SO(n)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The complexification of its Lie algebra called
\mathfrak{e}(n)
, which admits a basis
\{E_{i,j},H_{k}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n,\,1\leq k\leq n\}
with non-zero commutation relations given by

[E_{i,j},E_{j,k}]=E_{i,k},\quad[E_{i,j},H_{j}]=H_{i},\quad[E_{i,j},H_{i}]=-H_{j},

assuming
E_{i,j}=-E_{j,i}
.

In fact, the matrix realization of the Euclidean Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(n)
can be implemented by the following block matrix form:

\begin{pmatrix}&&&x_{1}\\
&A&&\vdots\\
&&&x_{n}\\
0&0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}

where the matrix
A
is an
n\times n
skew-symmetric matrix. In this realization,

E_{i,j}=e_{i,j}-e_{j,i},\quad 1\leq i\neq j\leq n,\qquad H_{k}=e_{k,n+1},\quad 1\leq k\leq n

with the matrix units
e_{i,j}
. The Euclidean Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(n)
can be viewed as a semidirect product
\mathfrak{e}(n)=\mathfrak{so}(n)\ltimes H(n)
of two subalgebras:
the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{so}(n)=\operatorname{span}\{E_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\}
and
the abelian subalgebra

H(n)=\operatorname{span}\{H_{k}\mid 1\leq k\leq n\}
.
Define
p_{0}:\mathfrak{e}(n)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{so}(n)
and
p_{1}:\mathfrak{e}(n)\longrightarrow H(n)
as the projections onto the rotation and translation parts, respectively, in the decomposition
\mathfrak{e}(n)=\mathfrak{so}(n)\ltimes H(n)
.

A derivation on a Lie algebra
L
is a linear map
D:L\to L
which satisfies the Leibniz rule:

D([x,y])=[D(x),y]+[x,D(y)]\quad\text{for any }x,y\in L.

The set of all derivations of
L
is denoted by
\operatorname{Der}(L)
and is a Lie algebra with respect to the commutation operation.

For
x\in L
, the map
\operatorname{ad}x:L\to L
, defined by
\operatorname{ad}x(z)=[x,z]
for all
z\in L
, is a derivation of
L
, and derivations of this form are called inner derivations. The set of all inner derivations of
L
, denoted by
\operatorname{IDer}(L)
, is an ideal in
\operatorname{Der}(L)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
D
be any derivation on the Euclidean Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(n)=\mathfrak{so}(n)\ltimes H(n)
. Define the linear maps
D_{0},D_{1}:\mathfrak{e}(n)\to\mathfrak{e}(n)
by

D_{0}=p_{0}\circ D\circ p_{0}+p_{1}\circ D\circ p_{1},\quad D_{1}=p_{0}\circ D\circ p_{1}+p_{1}\circ D\circ p_{0},

where
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
are the projections onto the subspaces
\mathfrak{so}(n)
and
H(n)
, respectively. Then
D=D_{0}+D_{1}
and

D_{0},\,D_{1}
are also derivations on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
.

Proof.

The identity
D=D_{0}+D_{1}
follows immediately from

p_{0}+p_{1}=\text{Id}_{\mathfrak{e}(n)}
.

It remains to verify the Leibniz rule for
D_{0}
and
D_{1}
.
For any elements
x=x_{0}+x_{1}
,
y=y_{0}+y_{1}

with
x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathfrak{so}(n)
and
x_{1},y_{1}\in H(n)
,
we have the Lie bracket decomposition

[x,y]=[x_{0},y_{0}]+[x_{1},y_{0}]+[x_{0},y_{1}],

(2.1)

where
[x_{0},y_{0}]\in\mathfrak{so}(n)
and
[x_{1},y_{0}],[x_{0},y_{1}]\in H(n)
.
(Note that
H(n)
is an abelian ideal of
\mathfrak{e}(n)
,
so
[x_{1},y_{1}]=0
.)

By hypothesis,
D
is a derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
,
and the projections satisfy the compatibility conditions:

\displaystyle D_{0}(\mathfrak{so}(n)))\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n),

\displaystyle D_{0}(H(n))\subseteq H(n),

(2.2)

\displaystyle D_{1}(\mathfrak{so}(n))\subseteq H(n),

\displaystyle D_{1}(H(n))\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(n).

We now verify that
D_{0}
satisfies the Leibniz rule.
Using (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),

\displaystyle D_{0}([x,y])

\displaystyle=p_{0}\bigl(D(p_{0}([x,y]))\bigr)+p_{1}\bigl(D(p_{1}([x,y]))\bigr)

\displaystyle=p_{0}\bigl(D([x_{0},y_{0}])\bigr)+p_{1}\Bigl(D\bigl([x_{1},y_{0}]+[x_{0},y_{1}]\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=p_{0}\bigl([D(x_{0}),y_{0}]+[x_{0},D(y_{0})]\bigr)+p_{1}\bigl([D(x_{1}),y_{0}]+[x_{1},D(y_{0})]\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+p_{1}\bigl([D(x_{0}),y_{1}]+[x_{0},D(y_{1})]\bigr)

(2.3)

\displaystyle=[D_{0}(x_{0}),y_{0}]+[x_{0},D_{0}(y_{0})]+[D_{0}(x_{1}),y_{0}]+[x_{1},D_{0}(y_{0})]

\displaystyle\quad+[D_{0}(x_{0}),y_{1}]+[x_{0},D_{0}(y_{1})]

\displaystyle=[D_{0}(x_{0}),y]+[x_{0},D_{0}(y)]+[D_{0}(x_{1}),y]+[x_{1},D_{0}(y)]

\displaystyle=[D_{0}(x),y]+[x,D_{0}(y)].

Thus
D_{0}
is a derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
.

A completely analogous computation shows that
D_{1}

is also a derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

A linear map
\Delta
is called a
local derivation
if for any
x\in L
, there exists a derivation
D_{x}:L\to L
(depending on
x
) such that
\Delta(x)=D_{x}(x)
. The set of all local derivations on
L
is denoted by
\operatorname{LocDer}(L)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Delta
be a local derivation on the Euclidean algebra
\mathfrak{e}(n)
. Defined
\Delta_{0,0}:\mathfrak{e}(n)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{e}(n)
as

\Delta_{0,0}=p_{0}\circ\Delta\circ p_{0}

Then
\Delta_{0,0}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is a local derivation on

\mathfrak{so}(n)
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be a local derivation on the Euclidean Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(n)
. Then the restriction
\Delta_{0,0}|_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
defines a linear map from
\mathfrak{so}(n)
to itself. For any
x\in\mathfrak{so}(n)
, we have

\Delta_{0,0}(x)=p_{0}\circ\Delta(x)=p_{0}\circ D_{x}(x)=(D_{x})_{0}(x).

Moreover,
(D_{x})_{0}|_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is a derivation on
\mathfrak{so}(n)
, which implies that
\Delta_{0,0}|_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is a local derivation on
\mathfrak{so}(n)
.
∎

3
Derivations of
\mathfrak{e}(n)

In this section, we determine the derivations of
the Euclidean Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(n)
.

Define

\delta
to be a linear map from
\mathfrak{e}(n)
to itself by

\delta\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=0,\ \delta\mid_{H(n)}=\text{Id}_{H(n)}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D
be any derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
. Then
D_{0}=\text{ad}X+\lambda\delta
for some
X\in\mathfrak{so}(n),\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Clearly,
\delta
is a derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
and
\delta_{0}=\delta
.

For any
D\in\text{Der}(\mathfrak{e}(n))
,
D_{0}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is a derivation on
\mathfrak{so}(n)
. As
n\geq 4
,
\mathfrak{so}(n)
is a semisimple Lie algebra. Then
D_{0}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is an inner derivation on
\mathfrak{so}(n)
, i.e.,
D_{0}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=\text{ad}\,X
for some
X\in\mathfrak{so}(n)
.

Set
D_{0}^{\prime}=D_{0}-\text{ad}\,X
. Then
D_{0}^{\prime}(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{so}(n)
. For any
1\leq i\leq n
, we can write

D_{0}^{\prime}(H_{i})=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}d_{i,j}H_{j},\,\text{where}\ d_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}.

Choose distinct indices
i,j
with
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. For any
k\notin\{i,j\}
, we have
[E_{k,j},H_{i}]=0
, and therefore

0=D_{0}^{\prime}([E_{k,j},H_{i}])=[E_{k,j},D_{0}^{\prime}(H_{i})]=\left[E_{k,j},\sum_{1\leq l\leq n}d_{i,l}H_{l}\right]=d_{i,j}H_{k}-d_{i,k}H_{j}.

It follows that
d_{i,j}=0
for all distinct
i,j
. Then
D_{0}^{\prime}(H_{i})=d_{i,i}H_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Now using the relation
[E_{i,j},H_{j}]=H_{i}
, we obtain

D_{0}^{\prime}(H_{i})=D_{0}^{\prime}([E_{i,j},H_{j}])=[E_{i,j},D_{0}^{\prime}(H_{j})]=[E_{i,j},d_{j,j}H_{j}]=d_{j,j}H_{i}.

Since we also have
D_{0}^{\prime}(H_{i})=d_{i,i}H_{i}
, it follows that
d_{i,i}=d_{j,j}
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. Hence
D_{0}^{\prime}=\lambda\delta
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, and consequently
D_{0}=\operatorname{ad}X+\lambda\delta
.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D
be any derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
. Then
D_{1}\mid_{H(n)}=0
.

Proof.

For each
1\leq k\leq n
, write

D_{1}(H_{k})=\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}c_{i,j}^{(k)}E_{i,j}.

Fix indices
i_{0},j_{0}
with
1\leq i_{0}<j_{0}\leq n
and
k\notin\{i_{0},j_{0}\}
.
Since
n\geq 4
, we can choose an index
l
such that
1\leq l\leq n
and
l\notin\{i_{0},j_{0},k\}
.
Then
[E_{i_{0},l},H_{k}]=0
.
Because
D_{1}(E_{i_{0},l})\in H(n)
and Lemma
2.1
,
we obtain

[E_{i_{0},l},D_{1}(H_{k})]=D_{1}([E_{i_{0},l},H_{k}])-[D_{1}(E_{i_{0},l}),H_{k}]=0.

By the following equation

\displaystyle\left[E_{i_{0},j_{0}},\left[[D_{1}(H_{k}),E_{i_{0},l}],H_{l}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\left[E_{i_{0},j_{0}},\left[\left[\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}c_{i,j}^{(k)}E_{i,j},E_{i_{0},l}\right],H_{l}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\left[E_{i_{0},j_{0}},\left[\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}c_{i,j}^{(k)}\left[E_{i,j},E_{i_{0},l}\right],H_{l}\right]\right]

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\left[E_{i_{0},j_{0}},\left[\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}c_{i,j}^{(k)}(\delta_{j,i_{0}}E_{i,l}-\delta_{l,i}E_{i_{0},j}-\delta_{i,i_{0}}E_{j,l}-\delta_{j,l}E_{i,i_{0}}),H_{l}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\left[E_{i_{0},j_{0}},\sum\limits_{1\leq i<i_{0}\leq n,\,i\neq l}c_{i,i_{0}}^{(k)}H_{i}-\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{0}<j\leq n,j\neq l}c_{i_{0},j}^{(k)}H_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=-c_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{(k)}H_{i_{0}}=0,

we have
c_{i_{0},j_{0}}^{(k)}=0
.

Next, we consider the coefficients
c_{k,j}^{(k)}
for
j>k
.
In view of Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle D_{1}(H_{k})

\displaystyle=D_{1}([E_{k,j},H_{j}])

\displaystyle=[D_{1}(E_{k,j}),H_{j}]+[E_{k,j},D_{1}(H_{j})]

\displaystyle=[E_{k,j},D_{1}(H_{j})]

\displaystyle=\left[E_{k,j},\sum\limits_{1\leq s<t\leq n}c_{s,t}^{(j)}E_{s,t}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{1\leq s<t\leq n}c_{s,t}^{(j)}(\delta_{j,s}E_{k,t}-\delta_{t,k}E_{s,j}-\delta_{j,t}E_{k,s}-\delta_{k,s}E_{j,t})

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{1\leq j<t\leq n}c_{j,t}^{(j)}E_{k,t}-\sum\limits_{1\leq s<k\leq n}c_{s,k}^{(j)}E_{s,j}-\sum\limits_{1\leq s<j\leq n,\,s\neq k}c_{s,j}^{(j)}E_{k,s}

\displaystyle\hskip 17.07164pt-\sum\limits_{1\leq k<t\leq n,\ t\neq j}c_{k,t}^{(j)}E_{j,t}.

From
Section

3
, it implies that
D_{1}(H_{k})
does not include any term of the form
c_{k,j}^{(k)}E_{k,j}
; thus
c_{k,j}^{(k)}=0
for all
j>k
.

Similarly, we show that
c_{i,k}^{(k)}=0
for all
i<k
. Consequently,
D_{1}(H_{k})=0
for each
k=1,\ldots,n
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
D
be any derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
. Then
D_{1}
is an inner derivation.

Proof.

For any pair
(i,j)
with
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, we write

D_{1}(E_{i,j})=\sum\limits_{1\leq p\leq n}b_{(i,j)}^{p}H_{p}.

Indeed,
b_{(i,j)}^{p}=-b_{(j,i)}^{p}
.
Then, for any
1\leq s<t\leq n
, we have

[D_{1}(E_{i,j}),E_{s,t}]\in\mathbb{C}H_{s}\oplus\mathbb{C}H_{t}.

(3.3)

Choose
1\leq k\leq n
with

k\notin\{i,j\}
, then there exists
l
such that
l\notin\{i,j,k\}
. Then
[E_{i,j},E_{k,l}]=0
and hence

\displaystyle[D_{1}(E_{i,j}),E_{k,l}]=-[E_{i,j},D_{1}(E_{k,l})].

(3.4)

Combining
Eqs.

3.3
and
3.4
, we obtain

[D_{1}(E_{i,j}),E_{k,l}]=b_{(i,j)}^{k}H_{l}-b_{(i,j)}^{l}H_{k}=0,

which implies that
b_{(i,j)}^{k}=0
for all
k\neq i,j
. Consequently,

D_{1}(E_{i,j})=b_{(i,j)}^{i}H_{i}+b_{(i,j)}^{j}H_{j}.

Since
[E_{k,j},E_{i,j}]=-E_{k,i}
for any
k\notin\{i,j\}
, we have

\displaystyle D_{1}(E_{k,i})

\displaystyle=b_{(k,i)}^{k}H_{k}+b_{(k,i)}^{i}H_{i}

\displaystyle=-[D_{1}(E_{k,j}),E_{i,j}]-[E_{k,j},D_{1}(E_{i,j})]

\displaystyle=-[b_{(k,j)}^{k}H_{k}+b_{(k,j)}^{j}H_{j},\,E_{i,j}]-[E_{k,j},\,b_{(i,j)}^{i}H_{i}+b_{(i,j)}^{j}H_{j}]

(3.5)

\displaystyle=b_{(k,j)}^{j}H_{i}-b_{(i,j)}^{j}H_{k}.

Comparing the coefficients of
H_{i}
and
H_{k}
in the expressions for
D_{1}(E_{k,i})
, one yields

\displaystyle b_{(k,i)}^{i}=b_{(k,j)}^{j},\quad b_{(k,i)}^{k}=-b_{(i,j)}^{j}=b_{(j,i)}^{j}.

(3.6)

Let
Y=-\sum\limits_{2\leq j\leq n}b_{(1,j)}^{1}H_{j}-b_{(2,1)}^{2}H_{1}
.
Assume that
1<i<j\leq n
. Then for
Eq.

3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\text{ad}(Y)(E_{i,j})

\displaystyle=[-b_{(1,i)}^{1}H_{i}-b_{(1,j)}^{1}H_{j},E_{i,j}]

\displaystyle=b_{(1,j)}^{1}H_{i}-b_{(1,i)}^{1}H_{j}

\displaystyle=b_{(i,j)}^{i}H_{i}-b_{(j,i)}^{j}H_{j}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=b_{(i,j)}^{i}H_{i}+b_{(i,j)}^{j}H_{j}=D_{1}(E_{i,j}).

Suppose that
1=i<j\leq n
. Then for
Eq.

3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\text{ad}(Y)(E_{1,j})

\displaystyle=[-b_{(2,1)}^{2}H_{1}-b_{(1,j)}^{1}H_{j},E_{1,j}]

\displaystyle=b_{(1,j)}^{1}H_{1}-b_{(2,1)}^{2}H_{j}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=b_{(1,j)}^{1}H_{1}+b_{(1,j)}^{j}H_{j}=D_{1}(E_{1,j}).

It then follows from Lemma
3.2
and
Sections

3
and
3
that

D_{1}=\text{ad}\,Y,

thereby completing the proof.
∎

Together with Lemma

, and Proposition

4
Local derivations on
\mathfrak{e}(n)

In this section, we determine the local derivations of
\mathfrak{e}(n)
.

For any local derivation
\Delta
on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
, by Theorem
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta(x)=[t_{x},x]+\lambda_{x}\delta(x)\quad\text{for all}\;x\in\mathfrak{e}(n).

Then we have the following Lemma for later use.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a local derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
. Then

(i)

For all
1\leq i<j\leq n
,

\Delta(E_{i,j})\in\sum\limits_{s\neq i,j}\mathbb{C}E_{s,i}+\sum\limits_{s\neq i,j}\mathbb{C}E_{s,j}+\mathbb{C}H_{i}+\mathbb{C}H_{j}
.

(ii)

For all
1\leq k\leq n
,
\Delta(H_{k})\in\sum\limits_{1\leq l\leq n}\mathbb{C}H_{l}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a local derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
such that
\Delta(\mathfrak{so}(n))\subseteq H(n)

(i)

For
n=2m,m\geq 2

, then
\Delta\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=0
.

(ii)

For
n=2m+1,m\geq 2
, if
\Delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{2i-1,2i}\right)=0
, then
(\Delta-\lambda\,\text{ad}H_{n})\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=
with some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

By
\Delta(\mathfrak{so}(n)\subseteq H(n)
and Lemma
4.1
, we can assume that

\displaystyle\Delta(E_{i,j})=b^{i}_{(i,j)}H_{i}+b^{j}_{(i,j)}H_{j},

(4.1)

for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
.

(i) By
\Delta(\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{2i-1,2i})=0
and
Eq.

4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Delta(\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{2i-1,2i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(b_{(2i-1,2i)}^{2i-1}H_{2i-1}+b_{(2i-1,2i)}^{2i}H_{2i}).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Delta
be a local derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
such that
\Delta(\mathfrak{so}(n))=0
. Then
\Delta=\lambda\,\delta
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be a local derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
such that
\Delta(\mathfrak{so(n)})=0
.
For any
1\leq i\leq n
, choose
j
such that
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, then
\Delta(E_{i,j})=0
.
Let
x=H_{i}+E_{i,j}
, by Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{i,k}H_{k}

\displaystyle=\Delta(H_{i})=\Delta(H_{i}+E_{i,j})

\displaystyle=D_{x}(H_{i}+E_{i,j})

\displaystyle=(\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq n}\lambda^{x}_{s,t}\,\text{ad}E_{s,t}+\sum_{1\leq l\leq n}\mu^{x}_{l}\,\text{ad}H_{l}+\lambda_{x}\delta)(H_{i}+E_{i,j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq s<i\leq n,s\neq j}\lambda^{x}_{s,i}(H_{s}+E_{s,j})-\sum_{1\leq i<t\leq n,t\neq j}\lambda^{x}_{i,t}(H_{t}+E_{t,j})-\sum_{1\leq j<t\leq n,t\neq i}\lambda^{x}_{j,t}E_{i,t}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{1\leq s<j\leq n,s\neq i}\lambda^{x}_{s,j}E_{s,i}+\mu_{i}^{x}H_{j}-\mu_{j}^{x}H_{i}+\lambda_{x}H_{i}.

Comparing the coefficients of
H_{k}
, we get
d_{i,k}=\lambda_{k,i}^{x}=0,\ \text{for}\ k<i
and
d_{i,k}=-\lambda_{i,k}^{x}=0,\ \text{for}\ i<k\neq j\leq n
. This shows that
\Delta(H_{i})\in\mathbb{C}H_{i}+\mathbb{C}H_{j}
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, and therefore
\Delta(H_{i})\in\mathbb{C}H_{i}
.

Suppose that
\Delta(H_{1})=\lambda H_{1}
. Let
\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta-\lambda\,\delta
. Then

\Delta^{\prime}(\mathfrak{so}(n))=0,\ \Delta^{\prime}(H_{1})=0.

For
2\leq i\leq n
, we assume that

\Delta^{\prime}(H_{i})=d_{i,i}^{\prime}H_{i}
.
Choose that
j\notin\{1,i\}
,
let
y=E_{1,i}+E_{1,j}+E_{i,j}+H_{1}+H_{i}
, then

\displaystyle d_{i,i}^{\prime}H_{i}

\displaystyle=\Delta^{\prime}(H_{i})=\Delta^{\prime}(E_{1,i}+E_{1,j}+E_{i,j}+H_{1}+H_{i})

\displaystyle=D_{y}(E_{1,i}+E_{1,j}+E_{i,j}+H_{1}+H_{i})

\displaystyle=(\sum_{1\leq s\neq t\leq n}\lambda^{y}_{s,t}\,\text{ad}E_{s,t}+\sum_{1\leq l\leq n}\mu^{y}_{l}\,\text{ad}H_{l}+\lambda_{y}\delta)(E_{1,i}+E_{1,j}+E_{i,j}+H_{1}+H_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\neq 1,i}\lambda_{s,1}^{y}E_{s,i}-\sum_{s\neq 1,i}\lambda_{s,i}^{y}E_{s,1}-\sum_{t\neq 1,i}\lambda_{1,t}^{y}E_{t,i}+\sum_{t\neq 1,i}\lambda_{i,t}^{y}E_{t,1}+\mu_{1}^{y}H_{i}-\mu_{i}^{y}H_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{s\neq 1,j}\lambda_{s,1}^{y}E_{s,j}-\sum_{s\neq 1,j}\lambda_{s,j}^{y}E_{s,1}-\sum_{t\neq 1,j}\lambda_{1,t}^{y}E_{t,j}+\sum_{t\neq 1,j}\lambda_{j,t}^{y}E_{t,1}+\mu_{1}^{y}H_{j}-\mu_{j}^{y}H_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{s\neq i,j}\lambda_{s,i}^{y}E_{s,j}-\sum_{s\neq i,j}\lambda_{s,j}^{y}E_{s,i}-\sum_{t\neq i,j}\lambda_{i,t}^{y}E_{t,j}+\sum_{t\neq i,j}\lambda_{j,t}^{y}E_{t,i}+\mu_{i}^{y}H_{j}-\mu_{j}^{y}H_{i}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{y}H_{1}+\sum_{1<s}\lambda_{s,1}^{y}H_{s}-\sum_{1<t}\lambda_{1,t}^{y}H_{t}+\lambda_{y}H_{i}+\sum_{i\neq s}\lambda_{s,i}^{y}H_{s}-\sum_{i\neq t}\lambda_{i,t}^{y}H_{t}.

Comparing the coefficients of
H_{1},H_{i},H_{j},E_{1,j},E_{j,i}
, we see that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\mu_{i}^{y}-\mu_{j}^{y}+\lambda_{y}+\lambda_{1,i}^{y}-\lambda_{i,1}^{y}

\displaystyle d_{i,i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}^{y}-\mu_{j}^{y}+\lambda_{y}+\lambda_{i,1}^{y}-\lambda_{1,i}^{y}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}^{y}+\mu_{i}^{y}+\lambda_{j,1}^{y}-\lambda_{1,j}^{y}+\lambda_{j,i}^{y}-\lambda_{i,j}^{y}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j,i}^{y}-\lambda_{i,j}^{y}+\lambda_{1,i}^{y}-\lambda_{i,1}^{y}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j,1}^{y}-\lambda_{1,j}^{y}-\lambda_{i,1}^{y}+\lambda_{1,i}^{y}.

Theorem 4.4
.

All local derivations on

\mathfrak{e}(n)
are derivations.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be a local derivation on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
.
Then
\Delta_{0,0}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is a local derivation on
\mathfrak{so}(n)
.
In view of
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
,

\Delta_{0,0}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
is an inner derivation of
\mathfrak{so}(n)
, i.e.,

\Delta_{0,0}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=\text{ad}\,X\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
for some
X\in\mathfrak{so}(n)
.

Set
\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta-\text{ad}\,X
. Then
\Delta^{\prime}
is also a local derivation
on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
such that

\Delta^{\prime}(\mathfrak{so}(n))\subseteq H(n)
. We divide the proof into two cases: (1)
n
is even, (2)
n
is odd.

Case 1.

n
is even. Let
u=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n}{2}}E_{2i-1,2i}
.
Then there exists a derivation
D_{u}
on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
such that
\Delta^{\prime}(u)=D_{u}(u)
. Set

\Delta^{\prime}_{1}(x)=\Delta^{\prime}(x)-D_{u}(x).

Then
\Delta^{\prime}_{1}

is also a
local derivation of
\mathfrak{e}(n)
, which satisfies the condition of Lemma

4.2
(i):
\Delta^{\prime}_{1}(u)=0
. Hence,

\Delta^{\prime}_{1}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=0
. By Lemma
4.3
, we have
\Delta^{\prime}_{1}=\lambda\,\delta
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

\Delta=\Delta^{\prime}+\text{ad}\,X=\Delta_{1}^{\prime}+D_{u}+\text{ad}\,X=\lambda\,\delta+D_{u}+\text{ad}\,X.

Case 2.

n
is odd. Let
v=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}E_{2i-1,2i}
.
Then there exists a derivation
D_{v}
on
\mathfrak{e}(n)
such that
\Delta^{\prime}(v)=D_{v}(v)
. Set

\Delta^{\prime}_{2}(x)=\Delta^{\prime}(x)-D_{v}(x).

Then
\Delta^{\prime}_{2}

is also a
local derivation of
\mathfrak{e}(n)
, which satisfies the condition of Lemma

4.2
(ii):
\Delta^{\prime}_{2}(v)=0
. Hence,

\Delta^{\prime}_{2}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}=k\,\text{ad}H_{n}\mid_{\mathfrak{so}(n)}
. By Lemma
4.3
, we have
\Delta^{\prime}_{2}-k\,\text{ad}H_{n}=l\,\delta
for some
l\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

\Delta=\Delta^{\prime}+\text{ad}\,X=\Delta_{2}^{\prime}+D_{v}+\text{ad}\,X=k\,\text{ad}\,H_{n}+l\,\delta+D_{v}+\text{ad}\,X.

The proof is complete.
∎
