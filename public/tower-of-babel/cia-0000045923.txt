FULL TEXT OF ARTICLE:

1. [Text] Hanoi, July 20 (AFP)-Vietnam sent Washington & letter
Saturday disputing the authenticity of a recently published
photograph of three U.S. servicemen missing since the Vietnam war
(MIAs) and offering to help clear up the issue.

2. According to a copy of the official letter obtained by AFP, the
Foreign Ministry said that in April 1990 Vietnam handed back the
remains of Lieutenant John L. Robertson whose plane crashed on
September 16, 1966, in the northern province of Hai Hung.

3. The other two pilots depicted, Albro Lynn Lundy and Larry James
Stevens whose planes were downed in Laos in 1966 and 1969, are on the
list of U.S. servicemen killed in combat included in the MIA file
given to Vietnam by the United States, it said.

4. The ministry also said Vietnam was concerned by the recent
publication of the photograph in the United States and was ready ‘‘to
cooperate with the United States to clear up (this) problem.’

5. The note reaffirmed that ‘‘all U.S. prisoners held during the
Vietnam war were handed back to the U.S. Government following the
Paris accords on Vietnam,''

6. According to U.S. sources, the three men, shown holding up a
pats sign saying ‘'25-5-90,'' have been identified by their
amilies.

7. ‘’The results of the ninth Vietnam-U.S. inquiry (February-March
1990) show that Robertson died when his plane crashed,'’ the note
said.

8. ‘‘Vietnamese experts have recovered some of his bones from the
ditch where the plane landed and they were returned to the United
States on April 10,’’ it said, adding that the ‘so-called
information that Robertson is still alive is without basis.’’

9. The U.S. Government has said its experts could not identify the
remains handed over by Vietnam. 8

10. Washington has for several years been conducting searches here
with the cooperation of authorities to recover the remains of MIAs
for identification. :

11. ‘‘John Leightson Robertson and Hubert F. Buchanan were pilots of
the F4C which crashed. Buchanan survived, (was) arrested (then)
handed over to the United States after the Paris Accords’! of 1973
allowing for the return of prisoners, the note said.

12. ‘‘J. Robertson was considered a priority case by the United
States and Vietnam cooperated fully with the United States in looking
for him,’’ it said.

13. In the letter, Vietnam asked the United States to take steps to
encourage those responsible for the photograph’s appearance to
cooperate with both governments in resolving the issue.

14. ‘‘If they refuse to cooperate and if inquiries show that the
photograph was created for financial gain, those people ought to be
punished and criticised for having prolonged the unhappiness of the
MIAs’ families,’’ it concluded.

15. The U.S. Government has said it could not confirm the
photograph’s authenticity,

16. The United States has in the past offered large rewards for
proof of MIAs in Indochina, but has had no response until now.
