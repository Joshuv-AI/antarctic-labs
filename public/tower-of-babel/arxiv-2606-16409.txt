PathRouter: Aligning Rewards with Retrieval Quality in Agentic Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation
Bo Wang, Heyan Huang, Yaolin Li, Wei Tang, Yuan Zhang, Wenbo Li, Mingze Gao, Ge Shi, Chong Feng

Abstract
Agentic GraphRAG trains language-model agents to iteratively retrieve and reason over graph-structured evidence, enabling more accurate and context-aware decision-making by efficiently navigating complex information networks. However, outcome-only reinforcement learning suffers from answer-path reward aliasing , where correct answers may come from shortcuts rather than useful evidence paths. It also exhibits search-update ambiguity , as scalar trajectory-level feedback does not indicate which retrieval actions to adjust. To mitigate these shortcomings, we present PathRouter , a path-aware training framework for agentic GraphRAG. PathRouter jointly evaluates each trajectory along answer correctness and evidence-path overlap, yielding four trajectory categories with differentiated GRPO advantage scaling that suppresses shortcut reinforcement while preserving evidence-seeking behavior. For evidence-poor trajectories, a frozen gold-evidence teacher provides token-level KL guidance on reasoning and search-query tokens, excluding answer tokens to avoid direct response imitation. Experiments on six QA benchmarks across three model sizes show that PathRouter consistently improves answer F1 and evidence-path overlap, achieving average F1 gains of 3.1 on 3B and 4.9 on 7B models compared to a strong baseline.

Introduction

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) reduces hallucinations in large language models by grounding output in external knowledge
(
12
;
5
;
23
)
.
Standard chunk-based pipelines, however, treat passages independently and fail to capture relational structure among entities.
Recent methods organize knowledge as entity-relation graphs, enabling multi-step retrieval over structured evidence paths
(
3
;
6
;
1
;
16
;
7
)
, while agentic approaches model retrieval as iterative agent-environment interactions optimized via reinforcement learning
(
9
;
21
;
15
)
.

Despite these advances, existing agentic GraphRAG methods rely solely on answer correctness for trajectory rewards, ignoring whether retrieved evidence truly supports the answer.
As illustrated in Figure
1
, this gives rise to two failure modes: a correct answer may arise from parametric memory despite failed evidence retrieval, while an incorrect answer may occur even when key evidence is retrieved. This
answer-path reward aliasing
conflates trajectories of differing retrieval quality, and scalar trajectory-level feedback creates
search-update ambiguity
, leaving the agent without actionable guidance to improve its retrieval strategy.

Motivated by these failure modes, we propose
PathRouter
, a path-aware training framework that jointly addresses answer-path reward aliasing and search-update ambiguity.
To resolve reward aliasing,
PathRouter
evaluates each trajectory along two axes: answer correctness and evidence-path overlap (EO).
By categorizing trajectories into four types based on these axes, the framework applies differentiated GRPO advantage scaling to suppress reinforcement of shortcut trajectories while preserving evidence-seeking behavior, ensuring that policy updates reflect retrieval quality rather than only final answer correctness.

To mitigate search-update ambiguity,
PathRouter
selectively invokes a frozen gold-evidence teacher for evidence-poor trajectories.
The teacher, prompted with gold supporting passages, provides token-level KL supervision on reasoning and search-query tokens, while excluding answer tokens to avoid direct response imitation.
This guidance supplies actionable information for refining search strategies at the token level, allowing the agent to identify which entities, relations, or queries need adjustment to uncover missing evidence paths.

Through this design,
PathRouter
enables agentic GraphRAG models to learn retrieval policies that are both
answer-accurate
and
evidence-faithful
.
Experiments on six QA benchmarks across three model sizes demonstrate consistent improvements, with enhanced cross-dataset generalization and robust performance for both single-hop and multi-hop reasoning tasks.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We identify answer-path reward aliasing as a failure mode for agentic GraphRAG and propose route-conditioned GRPO advantage scaling based on jointly evaluating answer correctness and evidence-path overlap.

•

We introduce a selective gold-evidence teacher that applies token-level KL to reasoning and search-query tokens for evidence-poor trajectories, resolving search-update ambiguity without directly imitating final answers.

•

We demonstrate consistent improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods across six QA benchmarks and three model sizes, with strong cross-dataset OOD transfer suggesting that path-aware training yields generalizable retrieval behavior rather than dataset-specific shortcuts.

Related Work

RAG and GraphRAG.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG;
12
) grounds LLMs in external evidence, but chunk-based retrieval lacks explicit relational modeling
(
5
)
. GraphRAG methods address this by organizing knowledge as entity-relation graphs
(
3
;
6
;
24
)
, with path-based strategies exposing multi-hop reasoning chains via relational pruning
(
1
)
or iterative traversal
(
14
)
. However, these methods primarily optimize graph construction, path selection, or traversal heuristics; they do not directly train the generator to align its reasoning with annotated evidence paths.
PathRouter
treats evidence-path overlap as a first-class training signal, routing updates based on whether the agent’s retrieval covers the gold reasoning chain.

Reinforcement Learning for Retrieval Agents.

RL has become central to LLM reasoning, with GRPO
(
20
)
providing scalable group-relative optimization popularized by DeepSeek-R1
(
2
)
. Search-R1
(
9
)
and R1-Searcher
(
21
)
apply GRPO to chunk-based retrieval-augmented reasoning, while Graph-R1
(
15
)
extends this to graph-structured knowledge. These systems primarily rely on outcome-level answer rewards, which can conflate retrieval quality with parametric knowledge and reinforce spurious shortcuts.
PathRouter
introduces evidence-path overlap as a second diagnostic axis, enabling a 2
\times
2 trajectory taxonomy that distinguishes faithful from lucky success and applies route-conditioned advantage scaling accordingly.

Knowledge Distillation and Sample Routing.

On-policy distillation
(
19
)
complements RL by providing token-level supervision on the student’s own rollouts. Privileged-context teachers condition on gold evidence
(
28
;
30
)
or unify distillation with GRPO
(
25
;
29
)
. Sample routing directs trajectories to different signals: SRPO
(
13
)
routes by correctness, GiGPO
(
4
)
decomposes advantages hierarchically, and RLSD
(
26
)
highlights answer-token leakage risks. These methods generally apply teacher signals uniformly or route by answer correctness alone.
PathRouter
conditions distillation on evidence-path overlap: a frozen teacher provides token-level KL only for evidence-poor trajectories, restricted to reasoning and query tokens, reducing answer leakage while targeting search-update ambiguity.

3
Approach

3.1
Task Formulation

We formalize agentic GraphRAG as a multi-turn agent-environment interaction following
15
.
Given a knowledge graph
\mathcal{G}
constructed from a document collection, an LLM-based agent
\pi_{\theta}
interacts with a graph retrieval environment to answer a query
q
.
At each turn
t
, the agent either emits a reasoning segment
r_{t}
within
<think>
tags followed by a search query
q_{t}
within
<query>
tags, or emits a final answer
a
within
<answer>
tags to terminate the trajectory. The environment processes
q_{t}
and returns retrieved passages within
<knowledge>
tags as the environment observation
o_{t}
. A reasoning trajectory is
\tau=((r_{1},q_{1},o_{1}),\ldots,(r_{T},q_{T},o_{T}),a)
, where
T
is the number of retrieval turns.

Building on this formulation,
PathRouter
extends GRPO with a path-aware design.
Trajectories are evaluated along answer correctness and evidence-path overlap (EO) to modulate GRPO advantage scaling, distinguishing shortcut answers from evidence-seeking ones.
For evidence-poor trajectories, a selective gold-evidence teacher provides token-level KL guidance on reasoning and search-query steps without imitating final answers, encouraging retrieval that is both answer-accurate and evidence-faithful.

3.2
Path-Aware Routing

PathRouter
conditions the training signal on both answer correctness and evidence-path overlap.

Trajectory Evaluation.

For each trajectory
\tau_{i}
, we compute two diagnostic scores:

\displaystyle C_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}\bigl[\text{EM}(a_{i},a^{*})>0\;\lor\;\text{F1}(a_{i},a^{*})\geq\theta_{C}\bigr],

(1)

\displaystyle P_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|S_{\text{gt}}|}\sum_{s\in S_{\text{gt}}}\text{F1}\bigl(\text{retrieved}(\tau_{i}),\,s\bigr),

(2)

where
C_{i}
is binary answer correctness, and
P_{i}
measures the average token-level F1 overlap between the concatenation of all passages retrieved along the trajectory and each gold supporting passage.
We determine correctness
C_{i}
by either exact match or whether the token-level F1 exceeds a threshold
\theta_{C}
.

P_{i}
serves as a proxy for evidence-path quality: higher overlap indicates that the agent retrieved evidence relevant to the gold reasoning chain.

Route Classification.

Each trajectory is classified into one of four categories based on
C_{i}
and an evidence-overlap threshold
\theta_{P}
:

\text{Route}(\tau_{i})\!=\!\begin{cases}C\!\uparrow P\!\uparrow&C_{i}=1,\;P_{i}\geq\theta_{P},\\
C\!\uparrow P\!\downarrow&C_{i}=1,\;P_{i}<\theta_{P},\\
C\!\downarrow P\!\uparrow&C_{i}=0,\;P_{i}\geq\theta_{P},\\
C\!\downarrow P\!\downarrow&C_{i}=0,\;P_{i}<\theta_{P}.\end{cases}

(3)

Route-Conditioned Advantage Scaling.

Rather than assigning separate loss functions to each category, we modulate the GRPO advantage by a non-negative route-dependent weight:

\tilde{A}(\tau_{i})=w^{\text{route}}(C_{i},P_{i})\cdot\hat{A}(\tau_{i}).

(4)

The route weight modulates update magnitude, and the sign of the advantage remains determined by the group-normalized reward. The weights serve distinct purposes:

•

C\!\uparrow P\!\uparrow
:
Full weight
(
w_{\text{full}}
). The answer is correct and evidence overlap is high.

•

C\!\uparrow P\!\downarrow
:
Reduced weight
(
w_{\text{down}}
). Answer is correct but evidence overlap is low, indicating a potential shortcut.
Reduced weight mitigates over-reinforcement of trajectories that achieve the correct answer without proper evidence retrieval.

•

C\!\downarrow P\!\uparrow
:
Attenuated weight
(
w_{\text{preserve}}
). Evidence overlap is high but the answer is wrong, which is likely due to a final-reasoning failure.
Attenuated weight mitigates over-penalizing useful retrieval behavior.

•

C\!\downarrow P\!\downarrow
:
Full weight
(
w_{\text{full}}
). Both answer correctness and evidence overlap are low.

3.3
Distillation for Retrieval-Token

Route-conditioned scaling adjusts the magnitude of policy updates but leaves each trajectory with a single scalar advantage that does not specify which search actions should change.
For evidence-poor trajectories (
P_{i}<\theta_{P}
) identified by the routing diagnostics in §
3.2
, we address this
search-update ambiguity
by invoking a gold-evidence teacher that provides token-level distributional supervision on reasoning and search-query tokens, converting trajectory-level evidence failure into localized guidance for retrieval improvement.
The teacher is used
only during training
, whereas at inference the student acts without any privileged information.

Inspired by
28
, we use the frozen reference model as the teacher.
Crucially, the teacher does not generate its own trajectory during training, but instead scores the student’s on-policy rollout under a privileged context.
For each training sample
(q,\mathcal{G},S_{\text{gt}},a^{*})
, the student first samples a trajectory
\tau_{i}
.
At each token position
t
, both the student and teacher condition on the same response prefix
r_{<t}
from this trajectory, differing only in their respective prompt context:

STUDENT:

\displaystyle\quad\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid q,\mathcal{G},r_{<t}),

(5)

TEACHER:

\displaystyle\quad\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid q,\mathcal{G},S_{\text{gt}},r_{<t}),

(6)

where
S_{\text{gt}}
is the set of gold supporting passages appended to the teacher’s prompt.
The teacher thus provides evidence-informed next-token guidance on the student’s own on-policy trajectory, rather than sequence-level imitation of a separate teacher rollout.
The frozen reference model ensures a stable distributional target throughout training.

In each response position
t
, both the student and the teacher observe the same prefix
r_{<t}
but differ in prompt context.
We retain the teacher’s top-
K_{\text{vocab}}
vocabulary tokens under
\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid q,\mathcal{G},S_{\text{gt}},r_{<t})
, renormalized to a valid distribution
p_{\text{ref}}^{K}
.
The student’s logits are gathered at the same
K_{\text{vocab}}
token indices and renormalized to
p_{\theta}^{K}
.
This top-
K
restriction reduces computational cost while focusing on the distillation of the teacher’s high-confidence predictions.

Retrieval-Token Masking.

The KL supervision from the teacher is applied only to tokens within reasoning (
r_{t}
) and search-query (
q_{t}
) segments, i.e., the agent’s own generated tokens, and excludes retrieved observation blocks (
o_{t}
) and the final answer (
a
).
We denote the set of such positions in trajectory
\tau_{i}
as
\mathcal{T}_{i}^{\text{rq}}
.
This masking ensures that the teacher provides guidance on the agent’s reasoning and evidence search, rather than directly instructing the final answer.

Selective Teacher KL Loss.

Trajectories with low evidence overlap (
P_{i}<\theta_{P}
) receive token-level KL supervision, while those with sufficient overlap are excluded from distillation to avoid interfering with effective evidence-seeking behavior.
For eligible trajectories, we compute KL divergence over reasoning and query tokens:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{TKL}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\text{low}}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\text{low}}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{i}^{\text{rq}}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{i}^{\text{rq}}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\bigl(p_{\text{ref}}^{K}\,\big\|\,p_{\theta}^{K}\bigr)_{\!t},

(7)

where
\mathcal{I}_{\text{low}}=\{i:P_{i}<\theta_{P}\}
is the set of evidence-poor trajectories and the per-position KL is computed over the teacher’s top-
K_{\text{vocab}}
tokens under the teacher distribution.
We use forward KL since the teacher distribution provides a gold-evidence-conditioned target, and minimizing
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\text{ref}}\|p_{\theta})
encourages the student to cover the teacher’s high-probability reasoning and search-query tokens.
In early training, most trajectories have low evidence overlap and would all qualify for teacher KL, which risks suppressing the on-policy exploration needed for the student to discover effective retrieval strategies.
To mitigate this, we linearly warm up the KL coefficient
\lambda_{T}(s)=\bar{\lambda}_{T}\cdot\min(1,s/W)
over the first
W
steps, allowing the student to first learn from GRPO reward signals before the teacher constraint gradually takes effect.

3.4
Training Objective

Reward Design.

The reward combines task completion with evidence-path quality and exploration shaping:

R(\tau_{i})=r_{a}+\alpha\,r_{p}+r_{s},

(8)

where
r_{a}=\text{F1}(a_{i},a^{*})
measures answer quality,
r_{p}=P_{i}
incorporates evidence-path overlap into the reward signal, and
r_{s}
aggregates exploration shaping terms.

r_{s}=r_{e}+r_{l}+r_{o}+r_{d}.

(9)

The exploration bonus
r_{e}
rewards multi-turn retrieval for correctly-answered questions. The lazy penalty
r_{l}
penalizes single-turn stops with insufficient evidence coverage.
The timeout penalty
r_{o}
penalizes reaching the maximum turn limit.
The redundancy penalty
r_{d}
discourages re-retrieving already-covered evidence.

Routed GRPO Loss.

For each training question, GRPO samples a group of
K
trajectories from the behavior policy
\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}
.
Each trajectory receives a reward
R(\tau_{i})
, and its group-relative advantage
\hat{A}(\tau_{i})
is scaled by the weight of the route
\tilde{A}(\tau_{i})=w^{\text{route}}(C_{i},P_{i})\cdot\hat{A}(\tau_{i})
from §
3.2
.
The clipped policy-gradient loss is as follows:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\text{GRPO}}=-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\min\!\bigl(&\rho_{i}\,\tilde{A}(\tau_{i}),\\
&\text{clip}(\rho_{i},1\!-\!\epsilon,1\!+\!\epsilon)\,\tilde{A}(\tau_{i})\bigr),\end{split}

(10)

The overall training objective combines routed GRPO with selective teacher KL:

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{GRPO}}+\lambda_{T}(s)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\text{TKL}}.

(11)

Table

In the reward group, removing the path reward

As shown in Table

Comparing

with its no-TKL variant reveals that teacher KL is not a generic regularizer but a capacity-dependent mechanism for resolving search-update ambiguity.

This pattern clarifies the division of labor: route-conditioned rewards address answer-path aliasing, while teacher KL reduces search-update ambiguity when the student has sufficient capacity to exploit it.

We examine whether the reduced benefit of teacher KL at smaller scales stems from limited teacher quality or limited student capacity, and how the KL signal should be scheduled.
To disentangle teacher quality from student capacity, Table

compares online, same-size frozen, and cross-size frozen teachers across three model sizes on MuSiQue.

Online self-distillation is consistently inferior, with UAR above 51% and reduced multi-turn exploration, confirming that an evolving teacher cannot provide a stable correction signal.
Same-size frozen teachers perform best at all scales, resolving search-update ambiguity while providing guidance that the student can effectively absorb.
Crucially, supervising the 1.5B student with a stronger 7B frozen teacher sharply degrades F1 and raises UAR above 63%, indicating that the bottleneck is student capacity than teacher quality.

To identify the best scheduling strategy, Table

Warmup scheduling achieves the highest F1 while avoiding the over-exploration observed under constant
\lambda

Online variants remain worse than their frozen counterparts across all schedules.

5
Conclusion

We introduced
PathRouter
, a path-aware training framework for agentic GraphRAG that addresses the credit assignment limitations of outcome-only reinforcement learning.

PathRouter
evaluates trajectories by answer correctness and evidence-path overlap, then modulates GRPO advantage weights to suppress shortcut reinforcement while preserving faithful-but-wrong explorations.
For evidence-poor trajectories, a frozen gold-evidence teacher provides selective top-
K
token-level KL guidance on reasoning and search-query tokens, with masking and warmup to prevent answer imitation and stabilize training.
This path-conditioned design enables more targeted policy optimization than uniform distillation or outcome-only reinforcement.

Limitations

Our approach introduces additional hyperparameters for routing and teacher scheduling, which may require tuning when applied to smaller models or new domains. In addition, the path-aware reward encourages more exploration turns during training, and the teacher forward passes for selective KL add per-step cost.
PathRouter
thus trades training efficiency for improved faithfulness and robustness.

Ethical Considerations

The datasets and corpora used in our experiments are all open-access resources, and no private or sensitive information is involved. The models and APIs adopted in this work are obtained from publicly accessible platforms and are used in accordance with their respective terms and policies. Our study focuses on improving retrieval and reasoning over public knowledge corpora, and we do not anticipate any direct ethical concerns arising from this research.

Appendix A
Evaluation Metrics

We define the five evaluation metrics used throughout the paper.

Token-level F1.

Given a predicted answer
a
and gold answer
a^{*}
, both tokenized into word sets, precision
P=|a\cap a^{*}|/|a|
and recall
R=|a\cap a^{*}|/|a^{*}|
, then
\text{F1}=2PR/(P+R)
. This is the standard SQuAD-style token-level F1
(
18
)
.

Exact Match (EM).

EM equals 1 if the normalized predicted answer string exactly matches the normalized gold answer, and 0 otherwise. Normalization includes lowercasing, removing articles, punctuation, and extra whitespace.

Supporting Fact F1 (SF-F1).

SF-F1 measures the average token-level F1 between the trajectory’s retrieved passages and the gold supporting facts
S_{\text{gt}}
. Formally,
\text{SF-F1}=P_{i}=\frac{1}{|S_{\text{gt}}|}\sum_{s\in S_{\text{gt}}}\text{F1}(\text{retrieved}(\tau_{i}),s)
. This metric is equivalent to the evidence-overlap score
P_{i}
defined in §
3.2
.

Unsupported Answer Rate (UAR,
\downarrow
).

UAR measures the fraction of answer tokens that are not grounded in the retrieved evidence. For each answer token
w\in a_{i}
, we check whether
w
appears in any retrieved passage;
\text{UAR}=|\\
{w\in a_{i}:w\notin\text{retrieved}(\tau_{i})\\
}|/|a_{i}|
. Lower UAR indicates better evidence grounding.

GPT-4o-mini Evaluation (G-E).

Following
15
, we use GPT-4o-mini as an LLM judge to score answer quality on a 0–100 scale. The judge receives the question, gold answer, and predicted answer, and evaluates correctness, completeness, and relevance. We report the average score across all test questions.
