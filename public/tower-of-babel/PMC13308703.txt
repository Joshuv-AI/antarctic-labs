Persistent Dominance of Serotype 3 in Noninvasive Pneumococcal Disease in Belgium (2022–2025)
Authors: I Passaris, S Depickère, M Maex, P J Ceyssens
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNoninvasive pneumococcal disease (NIPD) represents a substantial portion of the total pneumococcal disease burden worldwide. In this cross-sectional study, the serotype distribution for isolates originating from NIPD patients in Belgium (2022–2025) is described. Serotype 3 was the serotype with the largest NIPD burden in Belgium (n = 178, 12.7%), and detailed analyses revealed that its burden was lower in infants <2 years when compared to older age groups. Pneumococcal conjugate vaccines PCV20 and PCV21 cover approximately 50% to 80%, respectively, of circulating serotypes responsible for NIPD in Belgium, which underlines the importance of improving vaccine uptake in the adult population.

Despite the wide implementation of pneumococcal conjugate vaccines (PCV), pneumococcal disease keeps imposing a large global burden on populations worldwide, with over 500 000 deaths in 2021 globally [1]. Invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) infections affect normally sterile sites in the human body (blood, cerebrospinal fluid, and pleural fluids), while noninvasive pneumococcal disease (NIPD) affects the upper and lower respiratory tracts, causing community-acquired pneumonia, otitis media (OM), or sinusitis (SIN) [2]. Monitoring serotypes that cause both IPD and NIPD is important to assess the full pneumococcal disease burden.

In Belgium, the 7-valent PCV7 vaccine (including serotypes 4, 6B, 9V, 14, 18C, 19F, and 23F) was added to the childhood vaccination program in 2007, followed by PCV13 (including PCV7 serotypes and serotypes 1, 3, 5, 6A, 7F and 19A) in 2011, and uptake above >90% was quickly achieved [3]. Next, PCV10 was introduced between 2015 and 2019, after which it was replaced by PCV13 due to an increased incidence of serotype 19A IPD, particularly observed in children [4].

Higher-valency vaccines (ie, PCV15; including PCV13 serotypes and serotypes 22F and 33F [5], and PCV20; including PCV15 serotypes and serotypes 8, 10A, 11A, 12F, and 15B [6]) are now on the market and recommended for adults in certain age and/or risk groups. In Belgium, however, vaccine uptake in adults has been low, most likely due to the lack of reimbursement [7], although a recent health survey did reveal a significant increase in pneumococcal vaccination in at-risk adults from 8.6% in 2018 to 13.6% in 2023–2024 [8]. The latter might be partly attributable to the reimbursement of PCV20 since August 2022, for adults aged 65–80 years with 1 or more comorbidities that either were not previously vaccinated with any pneumococcal vaccine or that were vaccinated with PPV23 but more than 5 years ago. In addition to PCV15 and PCV20, PCV21 (Capvaxive) was recently licensed for adults in Europe (April 2025) and offers a complementary vaccine to combat pneumococcal disease by targeting certain serotypes that have not been targeted by any vaccine to date (serotypes 15A, 15C, 16F, 23A, 23B, 24F, 31, 35B) [9]. In this respect, the Belgian health research council has recently published new recommendations for pneumococcal vaccinations in adults, and PCV20 and PCV21 are now the preferred vaccines for pneumococcal prevention in adults.

Despite the large burden of disease, NIPD surveillance is less common, and serotyping data are scarce when compared to IPD. The limited evidence published to date points largely to similar trends for both NIPD and IPD serotype distributions [10–15]. Recently, our group published the serotype distribution of clinical isolates originating from NIPD patients in Belgium (2020–2023) and found certain serotypes to be predilected to cause either IPD or NIPD [16]. Moreover, it was reported that serotype 3 caused most NIPD cases and that antimicrobial resistance profiles were significantly different between IPD and NIPD populations [16]. In this short follow-up report, we further address this knowledge gap by describing the NIPD serotype distribution in Belgium in the post-COVID era (October 2022-May 2025) and stratifying our results according to specific patient metadata (clinical diagnosis and patient age), while grouping serotypes based on their inclusion in relevant higher-valency vaccines (PCV20, PCV21). The results presented in this short communication can facilitate decisions on which adult pneumococcal vaccination programs to implement.

RESULTS

Demographic and Clinical Characteristics of NIPD Patients

A total of 1404 isolates were included in the study, collected between October 2022 and May 2025, and the associated patient metadata are summarized in Table 1. The majority of patients lived in Flanders (n = 719, 51.2%) and were aged 65–84 years (n = 482, 34.3%) and male (n = 785, 55.9%). Patients received either ambulatory care (n = 654, 46.6%), were hospitalized (n = 657, 46.8%) or required admission to an intensive care unit (ICU) (n = 86, 6.1%), and around 44% (n = 624) had at least 1 underlying medical condition, of which chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) was the most common one (n = 238, 17.0%). The large majority of patients suffered from lower respiratory tract infections (LRTIs, n = 1052; 74.9%), followed by otitis media (OM, n = 251, 17.9%) and sinusitis (SIN, n = 101, 7.2%). Additionally, most LRTIs were diagnosed in patients <5 years and in patients between 61–75 years, while the large majority of OM cases were found in children <5 years (Supplementary Figure 1). The vaccination status of the patients, for pneumococcal vaccines administered in the patients’ lifetime, was mostly unknown (n = 857, 47.8%), but whenever the vaccination status was known, PCV13 was most often administered (n = 141).

Serotype Distribution of NIPD Isolates in Belgium, October 2022–May 2025

All 1404 isolates were serotyped, and the resulting serotype distribution is shown in Supplementary Table 1. The total number of serotyped isolates was 1,406, as 2 isolates were found to be a mixture of 2 different serotypes. Serotypes 3 (n = 178, 12.7%), 11A (n = 113, 8.0%), 23B (n = 90, 6.4%), 6C (n = 65, 4.6%) and 19A (n = 57, 4.1%) were the 5 serotypes causing most NIPD in Belgium during this surveillance period and the 10 most prevalent serotypes caused over 50% of NIPD.

Next, serotypes were stratified according to the patients’ clinical diagnosis (lower respiratory tract infection [LRTI], OM, or SIN) and grouped based on inclusion in relevant pneumococcal vaccines (Figure 1A, Supplementary Table 2). The only significant difference was observed for the PCV13-non-PCV7 serotypes (eg, 3, 5, 6A, 7F, and 19A), which showed to cause significantly less SIN (n = 5, 3.0%) when compared to OM (n = 59, 26.1%, P < .001) and, to a lesser extent, LRTIs (n = 178, 18.6%, P < .01). Serotype 3 was mostly responsible for this observed difference (Figure 1B). Next to serotype 3, serotypes 10A (PCV20-non-PCV15 serotype) and 21 (non-vaccine serotype) were also more often isolated in patients with OM when compared to LRTIs, albeit significance after pairwise comparisons was only met for serotype 10A. Finally, serotypes 6C (non-vaccine serotype) and 31 (PCV21-non-PCV20 serotype) were found more often in patients with LRTIs when compared to OM (Figure 1B). When analyzing serotypes exclusively present in higher-valency PCVs, it was found that PCV21-non-PCV20 serotypes (eg, 9N, 15A, 15C, 16F, 17F, 20, 23A, 23B, 24F, 31, and 35B) cause a substantial amount of NIPD (ranging from 35.4% to 43.0% for the different clinical diagnosis groups), and this was in contrast to PCV20-non-PCV21 serotypes (eg, 1, 4, 5, 6B, 9V, 14, 18C, 19F, and 23F), which were found at proportions below 10% (ranging from 8.8% to 9.7% for the different clinical diagnosis groups) (Figure 1A).

Subsequently, serotypes were stratified according to the patient age group (<2 years, 2–17 years, 18–49 years, 50–64 years, 65–84 years, and >85 years) and grouped based on inclusion in relevant pneumococcal vaccines (Figure 2A, Supplementary Table 3). These data indicated that a lower proportion of PCV7 and PCV13-non-PCV7 serotypes (resp. n = 5; 3.1% and n = 18; 11.3%) caused NIPD in infants <2 years when compared to all other age groups (ranging from 6.8% to 8.5% for PCV7 serotypes and from 18.9% to 23.4% for PCV13-non-PCV7 serotypes), albeit statistical significance was not reached. Serotype 3, in particular, was found to cause significantly less NIPD in infants <2 years (3.4%, P < .01) when compared to all other age groups (ranging from 11.4% to 15.3%, Figure 2B). In contrast, PCV15-non-PCV13 (eg, serotypes 22F and 33F) and PCV20-non-PCV15 (eg, serotypes 8, 10A, 11A, 12F and 15B) serotypes caused more NIPD in infants <2 years when compared to all other age groups, although the differences between these age groups varied more as well, and statistical significance was only reached for PCV20-non-PCV15 serotypes when comparing the <2 years group with the 65–85 years group (Figure 2A, P < .01). Again PCV21-non-PCV20 serotypes were found to cause substantial amounts of NIPD across all age groups (ranging from 32.4% to 41.4%), unlike PCV20-non-PCV21 serotypes which were found at lower proportions in all age groups (ranging from 8.0% to 11.9%). Finally, serotypes 15B (PCV20-non-PCV21 serotype), 10A (PCV20-non-PCV15 serotype), and 21 (non-vaccine serotype) caused more NIPD in the <2 years of age group compared to the other age groups, while serotype 4 (PCV20-non-PCV21 serotype) was only observed in the adult population (Figure 2B).

DISCUSSION

In this study, we present the serotype distribution of our cross-sectional NIPD surveillance between October 2022 and May 2025 in Belgium. Serotype 3 persistently dominates in the NIPD population during this surveillance period, but decreased slightly in proportion when compared to a previous surveillance study (2020–2023) of our group (from 16.6% to 12.7%) [16]. Our results indicate that serotype 3 accounts for a high proportion of OM (17.9% of cases) and LRTI (12.3% of cases) but is less frequently associated with sinusitis, which is in line with the findings of our previous work. Interestingly, serotype 3 cases are disproportionately low in children younger than 2 years (3.4%) when compared to other age groups (ranging from 11.4% to 15.3%). This observation might be associated with the high vaccine uptake in children in Belgium (>95%), which might confer protection in early life (<2 years) after which immunity quickly wanes. Similarly, IPD data from the NRC in Belgium (2023–2024) indicate that serotype 3 causes only 4.1% of IPD in infants <2 years as opposed to other age groups where proportions reach 10% or more [17]. Although clinical studies have reported PCV13 to be effective against serotype 3 disease, the extent of protection is lower when compared to other PCV13 serotypes [18]. It is thought that controlled shedding of the mucoid serotype 3 capsule leads to decreased antibody-mediated clearance, ultimately limiting PCV13 protection [19]. Another possible explanation is that part of the population in the 2–17 years age group is not vaccinated with PCV13 but with PCV10 (which does not include serotype 3), as this vaccine was used between 2015 and 2019 in the childhood vaccination program in Belgium [4].

Our data further indicate that certain serotypes are more often associated with a specific clinical diagnosis and/or patient age group. For some serotypes this phenomenon seems robust, as similar findings were reported in our previous surveillance study [16]. Notably, serotypes 10A and 15B are more associated with younger patients (particularly infants <2 years) and, in the case of serotype 10A, with patients who are diagnosed with OM, while serotypes 6C and 31 are found to cause more LRTIs when compared to OM and SIN. This type of information is important to consider when implementing higher-valency vaccine programs in both children and adults, as there are substantial differences in serotype coverage between specific vaccines (eg, PCV20 vs PCV21). This observation suggests that certain vaccines would be more effective in specific age groups and that recommendations for pneumococcal vaccine use should take this into account.

Proportions of vaccine-preventable NIPD are largely similar for different clinical diagnosis groups (LRTI, OM, and SIN) and between patient age groups. Only in the SIN group, non-vaccine serotypes seem to play a more important role when compared to LRTI and OM, although the sample size is too low to draw definite conclusions. Additionally, patients <2 years have a decreased proportion of PCV7 and PCV13 serotypes (14.4%) when compared to the other age groups (ranging from 25.8% to 31.9%), and this might be associated with the direct impact of PCV13 vaccination in this subpopulation. PCV21 gives the highest theoretical coverage (defined as the total proportion of serotypes included in a specific vaccine) for all clinical diagnosis and patient age groups (around 80%), while PCV20 reaches coverages of around 50%. These data suggest that PCV21 vaccination might have the biggest impact on the NIPD serotype distribution in Belgium. In contrast, coverage of PCV20 against adult IPD in 2023–2024 reaches higher proportions when compared to NIPD in Belgium (ranging from 67.6% to 84.0%, depending on the age group) [17]. Besides, PCV20 includes 2 serotypes (4 and 14) that cause over 10% of IPD cases [20], and these serotypes are not included in PCV21. Finally, it is important to note that inclusion of serotypes into a specific vaccine does not necessarily mean sufficient protection against pneumococcal disease. Only vaccine effectiveness data could provide this kind of information. Therefore, selecting an optimal vaccine strategy for adult pneumococcal protection requires integrating multiple data sources—including theoretical coverage for IPD and NIPD, immunogenicity results, pediatric pneumococcal vaccination program, serotype invasiveness, and cost-effectiveness analyses—making such policy decisions increasingly complex.

This study has certain limitations. Although the participating centers were chosen to represent the different geographic regions in Belgium, 4 centers were responsible for over half (53.1%) of all included isolates, potentially biasing the serotype distribution and limiting the generalizability of our results. Detection of other (respiratory) pathogens in clinical specimens occurred in almost 50% of cases, potentially overestimating NIPD, and care should be taken when interpreting the overall pneumococcal burden using this dataset.

In conclusion, the results of this cross-sectional surveillance study show that monitoring the serotypes that cause NIPD is imperative to ensure the evaluation of the full burden of pneumococcal disease on the population. Our results can be used to facilitate decisions on which adult pneumococcal vaccination programs to implement in the future.
