Nonlinear maps preserving the polynomial
Andrey Yurkov

Abstract
Let 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} be a field and P ∈ 𝔽 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] be a homogeneous polynomial such that | 𝔽 | > deg ⁡ ( P ) |{\mathbb{F}}|>\deg(P) and ϕ , ψ : 𝔽 n → 𝔽 n \phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n} be two maps such that P ⁡ ( 𝐱 + λ ​ 𝐲 ) = P ⁡ ( ϕ ⁡ ( 𝐱 ) + λ ​ ψ ​ ( 𝐲 ) ) P(\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{y})=P(\phi(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{y})) for all λ ∈ 𝔽 \lambda\in{\mathbb{F}} and 𝐱 , 𝐲 ∈ 𝔽 n . \mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}. We provide the characterization of all such ϕ \phi and ψ \psi for all polynomials in the case if char ⁡ ( 𝔽 ) = 0 \Char({\mathbb{F}})=0 and for all polynomials satisfying certain condition in the case if char ⁡ ( 𝔽 ) > 0 \Char({\mathbb{F}})>0 . This characterization generalizes the existing results regarding the linear maps on matrices preserving the determinant, the immanant and other homogeneous polynomial functions of matrix entries. To obtain the main result of this paper, we introduce the vector space ℒ P ⊆ 𝔽 n ∗ \mathcal{L}_{P}\subseteq{{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}^{*} spanned by the range of the gradient field of P ∈ 𝔽 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] . Being a linear invariant associated with P , P, this space has several remarkable properties and may also be used for studying the linear maps preserving P P . In addition, we demonstrate how the main result could be applied to the particular polynomial matrix invariants. Namely, we provide an explicit description of corresponding pairs of nonlinear maps ϕ , ψ \phi,\psi for the case where P P is equal to the Cullis’ determinant of n × k n\times k rectangular matrix (with the assumption that n ≥ k + 2 n\geq k+2 and k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 ).

Introduction

The investigations of linear maps between the matrix spaces that preserve different matrix invariants go back to Frobenius and his theorem about linear maps preserving matrix determinant published in 1897 (see
[
5
]
).

Theorem 1.1

(Frobenius,
[
5
, §7, Theorem I]
)
.

Let
S\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a bijective linear map satisfying
\det(S(X))=\det(X)
for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, where
\mathbb{C}
denotes the field of complex numbers and
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
denotes the space of all square matrices of size
n
with entries from
\mathbb{C}
. Then there exist matrices
M,N\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with
\det(MN)=1
such that

S(X)=MXN\;\;\mbox{for all}\;X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\;\;\text{or}\;\;S(X)=MX^{t}N\;\;\mbox{for all}\;X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

This field of mathematics continues to be a subject of active research and has applications in operator theory, functional analysis and quantum information theory. We refer the reader to
[
13
]
for the comprehensive survey of results up to the end of the 20th century.

In the recent works (
[
4
,
15
,
10
,
2
]
and others) the linearity of the considered maps has been replaced to a slightly weaker condition. In 2002, Dolinar and Šemrl provided a characterisation of the surjective maps
\phi\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
on the space of all square matrices of size
n
such that

\det(A+\lambda B)=\det(\phi(A)+\lambda\phi(B))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;A,B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

(1.1)

(see
[
4
]
). Namely, they established that every surjective map
\phi\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying the condition (
1.1
) is linear. Then, Theorem
1.1
is consequently applied to obtain the required characterization. Soon after, in 2003, Tan and Wang showed that the surjectivity of
\phi
could be omitted and the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers could be replaced to an arbitrary field of sufficient cardinality (see
[
15
]
).

In 2008, Kuzma provided the characterization of
\phi
satisfying the condition (
1.1
), where the determinant is replaced to the matrix immanant (see
[
10
]
). Moreover, he showed that the map
\phi
is linear even if the immanants on the left-hand side and the right-hand side of this condition are not equal (for example, if we put the matrix permanent on the left-hand side, and the matrix determinant on the right-hand side).

In 2021, Costara provided another extension of the results of Dolinar, Šemrl, Tan and Wang discussed above (see
[
2
]
). He considered the pairs of maps
\phi
and
\psi
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that

E_{k}(A+\lambda B)=E_{k}(\phi(A)+\lambda\psi(B))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;A,B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

(1.2)

and one of the maps
\phi,\psi
is surjective. In this condition by
E_{k}(X)
we denote the coefficient of
t^{n-k}
in the of expansion the characteristic polynomial of
X,
i.e., the polynomial
\det(tI_{n}+X).
He established that if
k\geq 2
,
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy the condition (
1.2
), then
\phi=\psi
and both
\phi
and
\psi
are linear. Since
E_{n}(X)=\det(X)
for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, this fact indeed extends the results from
[
4
,
15
]
and
[
2
]
.

In this paper we make the further steps in this direction. Namely, we show that the Costara’s result holds for all homogeneous polynomial functions on
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
, where
{\mathbb{F}}
denotes a field with zero characteristic, and omit the condition of surjectivity as it is formulated in the theorem below.

Theorem 1.2

(see Theorem
5.7
)
.

Assume that
\Char({\mathbb{F}})=0
. Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial. If
\phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
are such that

P(\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{y})=P(\phi(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{y}))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n},\;\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}},

then there exists a unique linear map
T_{\rad}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)
preserving
P_{\rad(P)}
such that

\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\phi=\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\psi=T_{\rad(P)}\circ\pi_{\rad(P)},

where by
\rad(P)
we denote the radical of
P
defined by

\rad(P)=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\mid P(\mathbf{a}+\lambda\mathbf{x})=P(\mathbf{a})\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n},\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}\}.

The notion of the radical of the function (Definition
3.5
) was introduced by Waterhouse in
[
16
]
. This notion plays an important role in the theory of linear maps preserving matrix invariants. For example, the radical of the function
f
is equal to zero if and only if every linear map preserving
f
is invertible (Proposition 1 in
[
16
]
). Accordingly, it is possible to show that
\rad(E_{k})=\{0\}
for
k\geq 2
(i.e.,
[
2
, Corollary 3.2]
), which implies that Theorem
1.2
indeed extends the result of Costara.

Furthermore, the statement of Theorem
1.2
could be generalized for the fields
{\mathbb{F}}
of positive characteristic and every polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
satisfying certain restriction. This comprises the main result of this paper. In order to formulate it we introduce the notion of vector space
\mathcal{L}_{P}\subseteq\left({\mathbb{F}}^{n}\right)^{*}
associated with
P
(here
\left({\mathbb{F}}^{n}\right)^{*}
denotes the dual vector space). Namely, by
\mathcal{L}_{P}
we denote the vector space generated by all linear functions on
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
having the form
\mathbf{v}\mapsto\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{a})
for some fixed
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
(Definition
4.2
). We also need to notice that the definition of the radical implies that
\rad(P)
forms a vector space. Thus, the main theorem of this paper is formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(see Theorem
5.5
)
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial such that
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
and
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n
. If
\phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
are such that

P(\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{y})=P(\phi(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{y}))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n},\;\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}},

(1.3)

then there exists a unique linear map
T_{\rad}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)
preserving
P_{\rad(P)}
such that

\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\phi=\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\psi=T_{\rad(P)}\circ\pi_{\rad(P)}.

To establish Theorem
1.3
, we adopt the beautiful argument of Tan and Wang to the case where
\rad(P)
could be arbitrary and show that
\psi
is a bijective linear map when it is considered modulo
\rad(P)
(see Lemma
5.2
). The proof employs the properties of
\mathcal{L}_{P}
which are studied in Section
4
. In particular, we show that
\rad(P)
belongs to the intersection of kernels of all elements of
\mathcal{L}_{P}
(see Lemma
4.4
) and the converse inclusion holds if and only if
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n
(see Lemma
4.7
). Then, using the homogeneity of
P
, we establish the required proposition. In addition, the properties of
\mathcal{L}_{P}
discussed in Section
4
could be applied for finding
\rad(P)
. Consequently, the notion of
\mathcal{L}_{P}
is useful in the theory of linear maps preserving matrix invariants (see Remark
4.8
).

Accordingly, the condition (
1.3
) is restrictive enough to imply that the considered maps are linear modulo
\rad(P)
. In general, for a given polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, the condition
P(\phi(\mathbf{x}))=P(\mathbf{x})
for all
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
does not imply that
\phi
is linear modulo
\rad(P)
(consider for example the case when
{\mathbb{F}}=\mathbb{R}
,
P=x^{2}
,
\phi(x)=|x|
). On the other hand, in certain cases it is possible to characterize nonlinear maps preserving the zero set of certain matrix polynomials. For instance, in
[
14
]
the corresponding characterisation is provided for commutativity preserving continuous maps
\phi
on the set of square complex matrices. Furthermore, Guterman and Kuzma in
[
6
]
obtained the description of maps preserving the fixed polynomial belonging to a wide class of matrix polynomials without any preliminary assumption on
\phi
.

Beyond obtaining general results discussed above, we demonstrate in Section
6
how they are applied in order to extend the Costara’s theorem to the particular polynomial matrix invariants, namely to the Cullis’ determinant (Theorem
6.15
and Theorem
6.27
). The Cullis’ determinant was introduced by Cullis in 1913 in his monograph
[
3
]
. It is denoted by
\det_{n\,k}
and extends the notion of the ordinary determinant for rectangular matrices. That is, if
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
, then
\det_{n\,k}(X)
is defined as an alternating sum of the maximal minors of
X
(Definition
6.1
). The complete description of linear maps preserving determinant for the case if
|{\mathbb{F}}|>\deg(\det_{n\,k})=k
has been obtained recently in
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
. Note that
\rad(\det_{n\,k})
is nonzero if
n+k
is odd (Lemma
6.25
) which implies that the maps
\phi
and
\psi
in (
1.3
) could be nonsurjective (see Proposition 1 in
[
16
]
for the case if
\phi
and
\psi
are linear and
\phi=\psi
).

This paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we introduce the notation used throughout this paper; in Section
3
we provide the preliminary facts from algebra and the theory of radical of a function; in Section
4
we introduce the vector space
\mathcal{L}_{P}
associated with every polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and prove its properties; in Section
5
we study the properties of pairs of maps satisfying the condition (
1.3
) and prove the main theorem; in Section
6
we obtain the description of pairs of maps satisfying the condition (
1.3
) for the case if
P=\det_{n\,k}
. Finally, Section
7
contains our comments about the possible further work.

Notation and basic definitions

By

We denote by

We use the bold font to denote vectors and lower indices to denote their coordinates. In the case if we need the series of vectors, we use the upper indices placed in braces. For example, if

Assume that

4
Definition and properties of
\mathcal{L}_{P}

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. By
l_{P,\mathbf{a}}\colon\mathbb{F}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}
we denote a linear function defined by

l_{P,\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{a}).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. By
\mathcal{L}_{P}\subseteq({\mathbb{F}}^{n})^{*}
we denote a vector space spanned by all the linear functions of the form
l_{P,\mathbf{a}}
for some
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}.
That is,

\mathcal{L}_{P}=\Span(\{\mathbf{v}\mapsto\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{a})\mid\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}\}).

Equivalently,
\mathcal{L}_{P}\subseteq{{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}^{*}
spanned by the range of the gradient field of
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

Remark 4.3
.

Although in certain cases the set
S=\{\mathbf{v}\mapsto\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{a})\mid\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}\}
is a vector space, in the general case it is not true. For example, if
{\mathbb{F}}=\mathbb{R},P=x^{3},
then
S=\{\mathbf{v}\mapsto\alpha\mathbf{v}\mid\alpha\geq 0\},
which is not a vector space.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. If
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{\rad}(P)
and
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
, then
l(\mathbf{v})=0
for all
l\in\mathcal{L}_{P}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
and
\mathbf{v}\in\rad(P)
. Then

P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}(t)=0\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;t\in{\mathbb{F}}

(4.1)

by the definition of
P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{F}}[t]
(Definition
3.15
). Since
\deg(P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}})\leq\deg(P)
by Lemma
3.16
and
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
by the statement of the lemma, then
\deg(P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}})<|{\mathbb{F}}|
. Hence,

P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}=0\;\;\mbox{as an element of}\;\;{\mathbb{F}}[t]

(4.2)

by Lemma
3.3
. Using the definition of
P^{\prime}_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}
(Definition
3.10
), we conclude from (
4.2
) that

P^{\prime}_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}(0)=0.

(4.3)

Therefore,

\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{a})\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize Lemma\penalty\ \ref{def:partDer}}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}P^{\prime}_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}(0)\overset{\eqref{lem:RadAnnLP:eq4}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=}0.

(4.4)

Thus,
l_{P,\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{v})=0
for all
\mathbf{a}
. Since every
l\in\mathcal{L}_{P}
is a linear combination of
l_{P,\mathbf{a}_{1}},\ldots,l_{P,\mathbf{a}_{k}}
for some
k\geq 0
and
\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
, then we conclude that
l(\mathbf{v})=0
for all
l\in\mathcal{L}_{P}.

∎

Now we discuss when the converse if also true.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume that
\Char({\mathbb{F}})=0
. Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}.
If
l(\mathbf{v})=0
for all
l\in\mathcal{L}_{P}
, then
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{\rad}(P).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
,
\kappa\in{\mathbb{F}}
and
\mathbf{v}\in\rad(P)
. Since
\Char({\mathbb{F}})=0
, then
{\mathbb{F}}
is an infinite field. Hence, we have the following equality in
{\mathbb{F}}[t]

P_{\mathbf{a}+\kappa\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}}=P(\mathbf{a}+\kappa\mathbf{v}+t\mathbf{v})=P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}(t+\kappa),

(4.5)

where
P_{\mathbf{a}+\kappa\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}}
and
P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}
defined in Definition
3.15
. Since
l_{P,\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{L}_{P}
for all
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
,

P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}(\kappa)=P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}(t+\kappa)^{\prime}(0)\overset{\eqref{lem:charzerokp:eq1}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=}P_{\mathbf{a}+\kappa\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}(0)\\
\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize Definition\penalty\ \ref{def:PAV}}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}l_{P,\mathbf{a}+\kappa\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{v})\overset{\mbox{\scriptsize the conditions of the lemma}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}0\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}.

(4.6)

Since the equality (
4.6
) holds for all
\kappa\in{\mathbb{F}}
and
\Char({\mathbb{F}})=0
by the statement of the lemma, then

P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}=\mathrm{const}

by Lemma
3.12
. By the definition of
P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}
this means that

P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{a}+\kappa\mathbf{v})=P_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{a})\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\kappa\in{\mathbb{F}}.

(4.7)

The equality (
4.7
) holds for all
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Therefore,
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{\rad}(P).

∎

Remark 4.6

In the case

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be such that
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
. Then

(a)

\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))\leqslant n
for all
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

(b)

\{\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\mid l(\mathbf{v})=0\;\mbox{for all}\;l\in\mathcal{L}_{P}\}=\rad(P)
if and only if
\dim(\rad(P))+\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})=n.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{\rad(P)}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)
be a canonic projection. Since
\pi_{\rad(P)}
is surjection, then
\pi_{\rad(P)}^{*}
is injection and consequently

\dim(\Img(\pi_{\rad(P)}^{*}))=\dim(({\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P))^{*})

(4.8)

In addition, Lemma
4.4
and the universal property of
\pi_{\rad(P)}
imply that

\mathcal{L}_{P}\subseteq\Img(\pi_{\rad(P)}^{*})

(4.9)

Now let us proof the part
(a)
of the lemma. We have

\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})\overset{\eqref{lem:dzeroeqk:eq6}}{\leq}\Img(\pi_{\rad(P)}^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lem:dzeroeqk:eq1}}}{{=\joinrel=\joinrel=}}\dim(({\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P))^{*})=n-\dim(\rad(P)).

(4.10)

Therefore,

\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))\leq n.

This establishes the part
(a)
of the lemma.

Let us prove the part
(b)
of the lemma. Let
(\mathcal{L}_{P})^{0}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be defined by

(\mathcal{L}_{P})^{0}=\{\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\mid l(\mathbf{v})=0\;\mbox{for all}\;l\in\mathcal{L}_{P}\}.

(4.11)

From the basic properties of the vector spaces and their annihilators we have

\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim((\mathcal{L}_{P})^{0})=n.

(4.12)

Now let us prove sufficiency. Assume that

\{\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\mid l(\mathbf{v})=0\;\mbox{for all}\;l\in\mathcal{L}_{P}\}=\rad(P).

(4.13)

By aligning (
4.11
) and (
4.13
) together and substituting the result into (
4.12
) we conclude that

\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n

(4.14)

which establishes the sufficiency.

Let us prove the necessity. Assume that the equality (
4.14
) holds. Hence,

\dim(\rad(P))=\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P}^{0})

(4.15)

by (
4.12
) and (
4.14
). From Lemma
4.4
we have an inclusion

\rad(P)\subseteq(\mathcal{L}_{P})^{0}.

(4.16)

From (
4.15
) and (
4.16
) we conclude that

\rad(P)=(\mathcal{L}_{P})^{0}\overset{\eqref{lem:dzeroeqk:eq11}}{}\{\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\mid l(\mathbf{v})=0\;\mbox{for all}\;l\in\mathcal{L}_{P}\}

which establishes the necessity.
∎

5

\phi,\psi
satisfying the condition
P(\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{y})=P(\phi(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{y}))
for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}

In this section we prove the main theorem of this article (Theorem
5.5
). This theorem relies on Lemma
5.2
which holds without an assumption that the polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is homogeneous.

The following simple observation preceds the formulation of Lemma
5.2
and is used in its proof.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and let
\phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be two maps such that

P(\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{y})=P(\phi(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{y}))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n},\;\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}.

(5.1)

Then
P(\phi(\mathbf{x}))=P(\mathbf{x})
for all
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}.

Proof.

Consider (
5.1
) for
\lambda=0.

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial such that
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
and
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n
. If
\phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
are two maps such that

P(\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{y})=P(\phi(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{y}))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n},\;\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}},

(5.2)

then there exists a unique bijective linear map
\psi_{\rad(P)}
on
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)
such that

\psi_{\rad(P)}\circ\pi_{\rad(P)}=\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\psi,

(5.3)

where
\pi_{\rad(P)}
is a canonical projection on
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)
.

Proof.

Let
\phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be two maps satisfying the condition (
5.2
). Let
d=\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})
and
\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots\mathbf{a}_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}
be such that
\beta\colon l_{P,\mathbf{a}_{1}},\ldots,l_{P,\mathbf{a}_{d}}
forms a basis of
{\mathcal{L}}_{P}
. Let
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be linearly independent vectors such that

l_{P,\mathbf{a}_{i}}(\mathbf{e}_{j})=\delta_{i\,j}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;1\leq i,j\leq d

(5.4)

(such vectors could be obtained by extending of
\beta
to a basis of
\left({\mathbb{F}}^{n}\right)^{*}
and finding dual of this basis). Let
\tilde{\psi}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be defined by

\tilde{\psi}(\mathbf{x})=l_{P,\mathbf{a}_{1}}\left(\psi(\mathbf{x})\right)\mathbf{e}_{1}+\ldots+l_{P,\mathbf{a}_{d}}\left(\psi(\mathbf{x})\right)\mathbf{e}_{d}\quad\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}.

(5.5)

The following four claims will be proven sequentially:

(i)

The map
\tilde{\psi}
is linear and

\tilde{\psi}(\rad(P))=\{\mathbf{0}\}.

(5.6)

(ii)

\pi_{\rad}\circ\tilde{\psi}=\pi_{\rad}\circ\psi.

(5.7)

(iii)

There exists a unique linear map
\psi_{\rad(P)}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)
such that

\psi_{\rad(P)}\circ\pi_{\rad(P)}=\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\tilde{\psi}.

(5.8)

(iv)

Every linear map
\psi_{\rad(P)}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)
satisfying the equation (
5.3
) is bijective.

The claims
(ii)
,
(iii)
and
(iv)
together clearly imply the statement of the lemma.

Claim
(i)

The proof of this claim employs a certain form of the beautiful argument of Tan and Wang (see Proposition 2.1 in
[
15
]
).

Since
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy condition (
5.2
), by Lemma
4.10
we have

l_{P,\mathbf{a}_{i}}(\mathbf{x})=l_{P,\phi(\mathbf{a}_{i})}(\psi(\mathbf{x}))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;1\leq i\leq d\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}.

(5.9)

Since
\beta
is a basis of
{\mathcal{L}}_{P}
, then there exists
C=(c_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{d\,d}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

l_{P,\phi(\mathbf{a}_{i})}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{i\,j}l_{P,\mathbf{a}_{j}}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;1\leq i\leq d.

(5.10)

Hence, by substituting (
5.10
) into (
5.9

Claim
(ii)

(5.12)

Since

(5.13)

(b)

Claim
(iii)

Let

If we assume that

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial such that
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
and
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n
. If
\phi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
is such that

P(\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{y})=P(\phi(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\phi(\mathbf{y}))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n},\;\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}},

then there exists a unique linear map
T_{\rad}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)
preserving
P_{\rad(P)}
such that

\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\phi=T_{\rad(P)}\circ\pi_{\rad(P)}.

Remark 5.4
.

If we do not assume that
\psi=\phi
, then we cannot conclude from the statement of Lemma
5.1
that
\psi_{\rad(P)}
should preserve
P
or
\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\phi
should be linear. Let
P=x_{1}(x_{2}+1)\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},x_{2}].
Let us choose some

such that
k\neq 0,1
and consider maps
\phi((x_{1},x_{2}))=(\frac{1}{k}x_{1},kx_{2}+k-1),\psi((x_{1},x_{2}))=(\frac{1}{k}x_{1},kx_{2}).

As it is stated in the theorem below, the discussed conclusions could be made if we assume that
P
is homogeneous.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial such that
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
and
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n

\phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}

T_{\rad}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)

\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\phi=\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\psi=T_{\rad(P)}\circ\pi_{\rad(P)}.

\psi_{\rad}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)

5.3
). Such a linear map exists by Lemma (
5.2
). The following five claims will be proven sequently:

(i)

If
\deg(P)>1

\phi(\mathbf{0})\in\rad(P)

(ii)

\psi
preserves
P
.

(iii)

\psi_{\rad(P)}

(iv)

\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\phi=\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\psi

(v)

\psi_{\rad(P)}
is a unique linear map satisfying the conditions of the lemma.

The claim
(v)

(i)

Let
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}

Since
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy the condition (
5.19

.

Remark 5.6
.

It is not known for the author if is it possible to omit the condition
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n
from the statement of Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
5.5
. Notably, this condition holds automatically in the case if
\Char({\mathbb{F}})=0
as it is formulated in Theorem
5.7
below.

Theorem 5.7
.

Assume that
\Char({\mathbb{F}})=0
. Let
P\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial. If
\phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
are such that

P(\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{y})=P(\phi(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{y}))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n},\;\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}},

then there exists a unique linear map
T_{\rad}\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}/\rad(P)
preserving
P_{\rad(P)}
such that

\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\phi=\pi_{\rad(P)}\circ\psi=T_{\rad(P)}\circ\pi_{\rad(P)}.

(5.36)

Proof.

Since
\Char({\mathbb{F}})=0
, then
{\mathbb{F}}
is infinite. Hence, the condition
|{\mathbb{F}}|>\deg(P)
is satisfied. The condition
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n
holds by Corollary
4.9
. Therefore,
P
,
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy all the necessary conditions of Theorem
5.5
and consequently the equality (
5.36
) holds.
∎

The example below shows that in the case if
\Char({\mathbb{F}})>0
the condition
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n
is not necessary at least for certain polynomials.

Example 5.8
.

Assume that
\Char({\mathbb{F}})=p\neq 0
. Let
P=x^{p}\in{\mathbb{F}}[x].
Then
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=0,
but every pair
(\phi,\psi)
satisfying condition
5.19
also satisfy this condition for
Q=x\in F[x].
Since
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{Q})+\dim(\rad(Q))=1,
Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
5.5
hold for
Q,\phi,\psi
and therefore they also hold for
P,\phi,\psi.

The following theorem is useful to generate new elements of
\mathcal{L}_{P}

Lemma 5.9
.

Let

be a polynomial such that
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
. If

are such that

for all

Proof.

Let

and

Since
\phi
is surjective, then there exist
\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{m}
such that
\phi(\mathbf{b}_{i})=\mathbf{a}_{i}
for all

Since

P(\mathbf{a}_{i}+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{v}))=P(\phi\mathbf{(}\mathbf{a}_{i})+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{v}))\\
=P(\mathbf{b}_{i}+\lambda\mathbf{v})\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{v}\in F^{n},\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}},\;1\leq i\leq m,

by Lemma
4.10
for

∎

Definition 6.1
.

The Cullis’ determinant
\det_{n\,k}
is a polynomial function on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
defined as an alternating sum of basic minors of
X
. That is,

\det_{n\,k}(X)=\sum_{1<i_{1}\ldots<i_{k}\leq n}(-1)^{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{k}-1-\ldots-k}\det(X[i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}|))

for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}}).
We also denote
\det_{n\,k}(X)
as follows

\det_{n\,k}(X)=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1\,1}&\cdots&x_{1\,k}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
x_{n\,1}&\cdots&x_{n\,k}\end{vmatrix}_{n\,k}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;X=(x_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}}).

In the case if
n=k
, then
\det_{n\,k}
is also denoted as
\det_{k}
and is clearly equal to a classical determinant of a square matrix.

Let us list the properties of
\det_{n\,k}
which are similar to corresponding properties of the ordinary determinant (see
[
3
, §5, §27, §32]
or
[
12
]
for detailed proofs).

Theorem 6.2

(
[
12
, Theorem 13, Theorem 16]
)
.

1.

For
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{F}),

\det_{n\,n}(X)=\det(X).

2.

For
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}(\mathbb{F}),

\det_{n\,k}(X)
is a linear function of columns of
X
.

3.

If a matrix
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}(\mathbb{F})
has two identical columns or one of its columns is a linear combination of other columns, then
\det_{n\,k}(X)
is equal to zero.

4.

For

interchanging any two columns of

changes the sign of
\det_{n\,k}(X)
.

5.

Adding a linear combination of columns of
X
to another column of

does not change
\det_{n\,k}(X)
.

6.

For
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}(\mathbb{F}),

\det_{n\,k}(X)
can be calculated using the Laplace expansion along a column of

.

l_{\det_{n\,k},A}=\frac{\partial\det_{n\,k}}{\partial X}(A)=(-1)^{k-1}\left(x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}\right)\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;X=(x_{i\,j})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}}),

(6.15)

where
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
is defined by

A=E_{3\,2}+E_{4\,3}+\ldots+E_{k\,k-1}+E_{3\,k}+\ldots+E_{n\,k}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&0&\cdots&1\\
0&0&1&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix}.

This fact clearly implies the statement of the lemma.

Relying on Lemma
3.16
, we have the equality

\frac{\partial\det_{n\,k}}{\partial X}(A)=g_{A,X}^{\prime}(0),

(6.16)

where
g_{A,X}=\det_{n\,k}(A+tX)\in{\mathbb{F}}[t]
. By Corollary
6.4
we conclude that there exist
a_{0},\ldots,a_{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}
such that

g_{A,X}=\det_{n\,k}(A+tY)=a_{0}+a_{1}t+\ldots+a_{k}t^{k}.

The Definition
3.10
implies that

g_{A,X}^{\prime}(0)=a_{1}.

(6.17)

Using the expansion (
6.2
), we obtain that

a_{1}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq k}\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|1]\Big|\ldots\Big|X(|i_{1}]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]\Bigr).

Since
A(|1]
is a zero column, we conclude by splitting off the first term that

a_{1}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq k}\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|1]\Big|\ldots\Big|X(|i_{1}]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]\Bigr)\\
=\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(X(|1]\Big|A(|2]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]\Bigr)+\sum_{2\leq i_{1}\leq k}\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|1]\Big|\ldots\Big|X(|i_{1}]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]\Bigr)\\
=\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(X(|1]\Big|A(|2]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]\Bigr)+\sum_{2\leq i_{1}\leq k}\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(0\Big|\ldots\Big|X(|i_{1}]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]\Bigr)\\
=\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(X(|1]\Big|A(|2]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]\Bigr)+\sum_{2\leq i_{1}\leq k}0=\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(X(|1]\Big|A(|2]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]\Bigr).

(6.18)

Now note that
X(|1]\Big|A(|2]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]
has the form described in the statement of Lemma
6.21
for
x_{1}=x_{1\,1},\ldots,x_{n}=x_{n\,1}
. This implies that

\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(X(|1]\Big|A(|2]\Big|\ldots\Big|A(|k]\Bigr)=(-1)^{k-1}\left(x_{1\,1}-x_{2\,1}\right).

(6.19)

By aligning the equalities (
6.16
), (
6.17
), (
6.18
) and (
6.19
) together, we obtain the equality (
6.15
), which concludes the proof.
∎

.

Proof.

6.22
implies that

by Lemma

Theorem 6.27
.

Assume that
n\geq k+2,

|{\mathbb{F}}|>k
,
k\geq 3
and
n+k
is odd. If
\phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
are two maps satisfying the condition (
6.1
), then there exist
A\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,n}({\mathbb{F}})
and
B\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
such that

\det_{n\,k}\Bigl(A(|i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}]\Bigr)\det_{k}\Bigl(B\Bigr)=(-1)^{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{k}-1-\ldots-k}

(6.26)

for all increasing sequences
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n
and

\phi(X)=\psi(X)=AXB\pmod{W_{n\,k}}\qquad\mbox{for all}\;\;X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}}).

(6.27)

Proof.

The definition of
\det_{n\,k}
implies that
\det_{n\,k}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
. By Corollary
6.14
we conclude that
\det_{n\,k}
,
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
5.5
. Together with Lemma
6.25
this implies that there exists a linear map
T_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})/W_{n\,k}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})/W_{n\,k}
preserving
\left(\det_{n\,k}\right)_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}
such that

\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\phi=\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\psi=T_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}.

(6.28)

Let
\iota\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})/W_{n\,k}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})
be any linear right inverse of
\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}
, that is,
\iota
is a linear map satisfying the condition

\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\iota=\id_{{\mathcal{M}}_{n\,k}({\mathbb{F}})/W_{n\,k}}.

(6.29)

Definition
3.6
implies that

\det_{n\,k}\circ\iota=\left(\left(\det_{n\,k}\right)_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\right)\circ\iota\\
=\left(\det_{n\,k}\right)_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\left(\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\iota\right)\overset{\eqref{thm:homogenphipsiDetNKOdd:eq2}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}\left(\det_{n\,k}\right)_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}.

Let

be defined by

Then

\overset{\scriptsize T_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\;\mbox{preserves}\;\left(\det_{n\,k}\right)_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}\left(\det_{n\,k}\right)_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}=\det_{n\,k}.

T
is a linear map preserving
\det_{n\,k}
. By Lemma

, there exist

and

The left-hand side of (

\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ T\overset{\eqref{thm:homogenphipsiDetNKOdd:eq4}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=\joinrel=}\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\left(\iota\circ T_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\circ\pi_{\rad(\det_{n\,k})}\right)\\

7
Further work

The author supposes that it is not possible to omit the conditions
\deg(P)>|{\mathbb{F}}|
and
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n
in the statement of Theorem
5.5
, but did not succeed to provide an example proving it. Any progress in this direction will be interesting.

When
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
, the main difficulty in constructing such example is that the condition

P(\mathbf{x}+\lambda\mathbf{y})=P(\phi(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\psi(\mathbf{y}))\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{F}}}^{n},\;\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}},

(7.1)

provides
\deg(P)
equations on
\phi
and
\psi
obtained by comparing coefficients at
\lambda^{k}
on both the sides of the condition (
7.1
). From the author’s experience, in most cases these equations imply that
\phi=\psi=\id
and consequently
\psi
and
\phi
are linear. If
\deg(P)>|{\mathbb{F}}|
, then author tried the brute-force computer search, but it did not help.

Nevertheless, these conditions do not seem to be restrictive or difficult to verify, at least for the polynomial matrix invariants known to the author. The condition
\deg(P)<|{\mathbb{F}}|
is frequently assumed when the characterisation of linear preservers is established. As the reader can observe in Section
6
, verifying the condition
\dim(\mathcal{L}_{P})+\dim(\rad(P))=n
for
P=\det_{n\,k}
(Definition
6.1
) is a quite technical procedure. Moreover, the author notes that this procedure employs the same observations as the solution to the corresponding linear preserver problem. The same holds for the other polynomial matrix invariants discussed in Section
1
.
