Introduction
Figure 1 shows a simplified model schematic of the Tetracage target design.

 Figure 1: Tetracage Model with Labeled Geometry

In this figure the green lines represent the Tetracage yarns, which consist of two parallel yarns on the top
and two parallel yarns on the bottom with the pairs in a cross hatched pattern. For simplicity, as we are
only considering vertical forces, we will perform calculations without consideration for the two pairs of
yarns being perpendicular to each other. Also worth noting is the model shows the yarns extending directly
to the capsule then closely hugging it – in reality, the yarns behave as you would expect where they hug
the capsule only as much as the geometry of their deflection allows, similarly to if you held a tennis ball
with a pair of parallel strings anchored at opposing ends. The dashed black lines show the path the yarns
take straight across from slot glue point to slot glue point before the target is closed and the capsule
deforms them. The variable r corresponds to the capsule outer radius. The variable L corresponds to the
hohlraum inner radius. The variable a corresponds to the yarn offset from the center of the diagnostic
band. The variable d corresponds to the wire deflection vertically.

Now, Figure 1 shows an ideal scenario without consideration of gravity. In reality, the weight of the capsule
causes the lower yarns to deflect more than the upper yarns. This of course follows the assumption that
all the yarns are equal in mechanical properties – please refer to the Tetracage device summary report for
further information on the mechanical property variance that allows for this assumption to be made. To
denote the additional deflection due to gravity, we introduce the variable Sg, standing for sag due to
gravity. Figure 2 is a simplified alteration of Figure 1 that shows the geometry of this additional sag which
will be a part of the calculations.

 Figure 2: Simplified Tetracage Model with Sag

In Figure 2 the Sag Variable is seen as the downward displacement from the capsules position in Figure 1.
The blue lines correspond to the resulting wire positions as a result of sag and the red lines correspond to
the wire positions without consideration of gravity.
Given the geometry of the system is defined, we can consider the two main goals of this analysis:
 1. Perform a force balance at room temperature (RT) to solve for RT sag due to capsule weight
 2. Perform a force balance at empirical cryogenic sag conditions to solve for the yarns CTE

Solving Yarn Elongation
The first course of action in solving the yarn forces is to determine the elongation of the yarns as this is
directly correlated. The length of the yarn by default is 2L as seen in Figure 1. This is the inner diameter of
the hohlraum, which before close is the distance the yarns span from slot to slot. By calculating an estimate
for the yarn length when it is wrapped around the capsule we can subtract the default length of the yarn,
2L, and have the yarn elongation.

Consider a side view of the capsule, as shown in Figure 4, where the bottom yarns are pointing in/out of
the screen.

 Figure 4: Geometry of Yarn Position Wrapping Around Capsule

Where e is yarn deflection in the x direction, 2b is the gap between wires centered about the capsule
center, a is the yarn offset, and d is the wire deflection. Intuitively in an ideal world we would expect upon
close when the yarns are hugged around the capsule for the yarns to settle in the position along the
capsules curvature such that the restoring force (FR) of the yarn is exactly parallel to the normal force (FN)
of the capsule on the yarn. In reality, we have verified on Sagometer utilizing actual closed Tetracage
targets that the yarn typically settles at a stopping point before it reaches the point along the capsule
where the restoring and normal forces are parallel. The additional force that is introduced to allow the
system to achieve a static state we denote (Ff,e) which stands for a force either from friction of the yarn on
the capsule surface or an elastic force from the yarn winding itself up along the outside of the capsule.
Free body diagrams of both the ideal and actual force balances can be seen in Figure 5.

Solving Yarn Coefficient of Thermal Expansion
Solving for the yarn CTE is only possible given empirical results to back out values from. These empirical
values show ~50 microns of sag at cryogenic temperature. The yarn is composed of a spindled forest of
carbon nanotubes, and we will determine the total yarn CTE of this yarn as a system.

The actual behavior of these yarns at cryo is not fully understood and poses many difficulties during
cryogenic proofing, which during the most recent Tetracage target series of 2022-2023 posed significant
problems. The added complexity of spindled yarns at cryo is they have been observed to have a tendency
to wind/unwind with a shift in temperature, causing many adverse effects that are difficult to deal with.

Due to the complexity of the yarn behavior at cryo, coupled with the empirical data available being from
full target builds, solving for CTE is largely an estimate. Even so, two methods were developed that present
similar results when numbers are tested.

Important Note + Future Work
This document is the most up to date version of these calculations. Both the scanned handwork and the
code are slightly outdated. Should this ever be used a new code should be made using these equations.
The forces section is applicable to any type of yarn used, whereas the CTE section uses empirical data
specific to the yarns we use for builds – this can vary with yarn process. These calculations can be expanded
to modify parameters to determine yarn loads and system response for a given setup of a Tetracage target.
The real test of Tetracage targets is the behavior under cryogenic conditions. The tents work so well in
typical targets because they have a higher CTE than the hohlraums, meaning they tighten around the
capsule holding it firm. Ideally we would want yarns that could do the same without becoming too tight
and breaking. There are ample vibrations in a targets lifecycle as well from proofing, transporting, etc. and
these are not captured in these calculations.
