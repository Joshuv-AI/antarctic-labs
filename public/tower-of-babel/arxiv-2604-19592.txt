An Efficient Black-Box Reduction from Online Learning to Multicalibration, and a New Route to Φ \Phi -Regret Minimization
Gabriele Farina, Juan Carlos Perdomo

Abstract
We give a Gordon-Greenwald-Marks (GGM) style black-box reduction from online learning to online multicalibration. Concretely, we show that to achieve high-dimensional multicalibration with respect to a class of functions ℋ \mathcal{H} , it suffices to combine any no-regret learner over ℋ \mathcal{H} with an expected variational inequality (EVI) solver. We also prove a converse statement showing that efficient multicalibration implies efficient EVI solving, highlighting how EVIs in multicalibration mirror the role of fixed points in the GGM result for Φ \Phi -regret. This first set of results resolves the main open question in Garg, Jung, Reingold, and Roth (SODA ’24), showing that oracle-efficient online multicalibration with T \sqrt{T} -type guarantees is possible in full generality. Furthermore, our GGM-style reduction unifies the analyses of existing online multicalibration algorithms, enables new algorithms for challenging environments with delayed observations or censored outcomes, and yields the first efficient black-box reduction between online learning and multiclass omniprediction. Our second main result is a fine-grained reduction from high-dimensional online multicalibration to (contextual) Φ \Phi -regret minimization. Together with our first result, this establishes a new route from external regret to Φ \Phi -regret that bypasses sophisticated fixed-point or semi-separation machinery, dramatically simplifies a result of Daskalakis, Farina, Fishelson, Pipis, and Schneider (STOC ’25) while improving rates, and yields new algorithms that are robust to richer deviation classes, such as those belonging to any reproducing kernel Hilbert space.

Introduction

Originally developed as a definition of algorithmic fairness, multicalibration has turned out to have interesting and important implications beyond its initial scope.
Within learning theory, multicalibrated predictors are outcome indistinguishable; they constitute generative models that are computationally unfalsifiable
[
12
]
.
Multicalibration also implies robustness to distribution shift
[
41
]
and loss minimization, not just for one loss, but for many losses simultaneously
[
27
]
. Within complexity theory, recent work has started to explore how multicalibration enables stronger versions of classical regularity lemmas, such as the Dense Model Theorem and the Hardcore Lemma
[
4
,
38
]
. In game theory, notions of calibration are intimately tied to swap regret minimization and thus to correlated equilibria
[
21
,
22
,
40
]
.

Despite this growing web of connections, the
algorithmic
understanding of how to achieve multicalibration remains rather fragmented.
In the batch setting, the only known algorithms are boosting-style procedures that use polynomially more samples than necessary.
In the online setting, where procedures enjoy
worst-case guarantees and yield batch algorithms with optimal
\mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon^{2})
sample
complexity via online-to-batch reductions, we have a complicated patchwork of
designs.
Each algorithm targets a specific outcome type (e.g., binary) or hypothesis class (e.g., linear functions) and requires its own idiosyncratic and often lengthy analysis.
In short, we lack a clean algorithmic template that works in full generality.

There is, however, a compelling precedent for a solution to this issue. Multicalibration is reminiscent of a different and older problem in online learning, namely
\Phi
-regret minimization
[
30
]
. Both are parameterized by a family of functions: tests in the case of multicalibration, and a set
\Phi
of strategy transformations
\phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
in the case of
\Phi
-regret on a strategy set
\mathcal{Z}
. Since the seminal result of
[
29
]
(henceforth abbreviated as Gordon-Greenwald-Marks or GGM), it is known that
\Phi
-regret minimization can be efficiently reduced, black-box, to standard regret minimization over
\Phi
, converting the outputs over
\Phi
into strategies in
\mathcal{Z}
by computing a
fixed point
of the transformations. Since 2008, this black-box reduction has been the standard approach for
\Phi
-regret algorithms, and has led to a flurry of results for equilibrium computation (e.g.,
[
14
,
23
,
9
,
2
]
and references therein).

In light of the above, this paper is motivated by two questions.

(Q1)

Is there a Gordon-Greenwald-Marks-like reduction for online multicalibration?
That is, can online multicalibration be black-box reduced to regret minimization, and if so, what nonlinear optimization primitive plays the role that fixed points play for
\Phi
-regret?

(Q2)

How formal is the resemblance between multicalibrated forecasting and
\Phi
-regret? Can one devise a more fine-grained connection between online
\mathcal{H}
-multicalibration and
\Phi
-regret with
\mathcal{H}
scaling naturally with the complexity of
\Phi
and recovering the well-known connection between strong calibration and swap regret in the limit?

We establish positive answers to both questions.

Our first contribution, addressing
(Q1)
, is a Gordon-Greenwald-Marks-style theorem for online multicalibration. This resolves one of the main open questions in
[
24
]
regarding the existence of efficient oracle-based constructions for online multicalibration. Specifically, we show that online, high-dimensional multicalibration can be obtained efficiently and in a black-box fashion with respect to any class
\mathcal{H}
by combining external regret minimization over test functions in
\mathcal{H}
with the nonlinear optimization primitive of expected variational inequalities (EVIs). This black-box reduction is especially appealing in light of recent algorithmic progress on EVIs, demonstrating that they can be solved very efficiently
[
56
]
. Furthermore, we show that EVIs are not only sufficient but also necessary: contingent on an efficient oracle for online learning over
\mathcal{H}
, an efficient online multicalibrated forecasting algorithm exists if and only if one can efficiently solve EVIs defined by the test functions in
\mathcal{H}
.

Our reduction enables a technical transfer of ideas from the rich literature on online learning to the more recent area of multicalibration. Looking backward, we recover and dramatically simplify the proofs of prior online multicalibration algorithms. By leveraging recent advances in EVIs, these reinterpretations also yield stronger guarantees. Looking forward, we leverage our reduction to design new algorithms for previously unstudied versions of online multicalibration. In particular, we develop forecasting procedures with guarantees in challenging learning environments, such as settings with delayed feedback or censored observations, simply by swapping in the appropriate off-the-shelf regret minimizer.
Given how online problems generate offline problems, these new algorithms also imply better batch algorithms via online-to-batch reductions.

Our second contribution, addressing
(Q2)
, runs in the opposite direction: from forecasting to decision making. We show that an efficient no-
\Phi
-regret algorithm can be constructed by first building an online forecaster for the losses that is multicalibrated with respect to a set of tests defined in terms of
\Phi
,
and then best responding. This result yields a substantially finer-grained
reduction from decision making to forecasting than the folklore connection between swap regret
and
\ell_{1}
-multicalibration (e.g.,
[
21
]
), and yields state-of-the-art
\Phi
-regret algorithms for new domains.

Taken together, we show that our two main reductions provide an alternative path between external regret and
\Phi
-regret to that established by
[
29
]
: Rather than optimizing directly over endomorphisms and then computing fixed points, one can route through multicalibration and EVIs.
Recent work on
\Phi
-regret minimization for expressive deviation classes has increasingly run into the limits of the GGM path: extending it to richer classes of deviations over general action sets
\mathcal{Z}
has required increasingly elaborate machinery, including semi-separation oracles and careful relaxations of sets of endomorphisms
[
9
]
. Certain natural classes, such as deviations lying in any reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), have remained out of reach. Our multicalibration route provides a simplified technical approach that not only recovers prior results on linear and low-degree polynomial swap regret
[
9
,
2
,
57
]
but also establishes the first known no-
\Phi
-regret algorithms for new settings, such as RKHSs.

Definition 1

A GGM-Style Reduction for Online Multicalibration.

Our first result establishes an analogous result to that of
[
29
]

\Phi

\mathcal{H}
using a nonlinear optimization tool called an
expected variational inequality

EVIs have appeared in different literatures with different names, including
“outgoing minimax problems”
[

[

[

Given a function
S:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathcal{D}

\mathcal{Y}

\mathbb{E}_{p\sim\mathcal{D}}\Big[S(p)^{\top}(y-p)\Big]\leq\varepsilon\qquad\forall y\in\mathcal{Y}.

(EVI)

As we recall in
1

The following theorem summarizes our first main result.

Theorem 2

(Informal)
.

The following statements are true for a general high-dimensional outcome space
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and function class
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
.

•

(Online Learning and EVI
\to
Multicalibration) Let
\mathcal{A}
be any online optimization algorithm over the class
\mathcal{H}
with external regret bounded by
\textrm{Regret}_{T}
.
When paired with an EVI solver, we obtain an online algorithm whose multicalibration error with respect to
\mathcal{H}
is at most
\textrm{Regret}_{T}
.

•

(Multicalibration
\to
EVI) Let
\mathcal{A}
be any algorithm that efficiently guarantees online multicalibration with respect to
\mathcal{H}
, then
\mathcal{A}
can be black-box converted into an efficient algorithm for solving EVIs over
\mathcal{H}
.

The reduction and its proofs both fit in a few lines (see
Theorem

4
), and are fully black-box. It makes no assumptions on
\mathcal{H}
, works for any convex compact
\mathcal{Y}
, does not rely on binning arguments, and is hence amenable to general high-dimensional problems. As an immediate consequence, it resolves one of the main open questions of
[
24

, who asked whether oracle-efficient multicalibration algorithms with
\sqrt{T}

As with the celebrated GGM theorem for
\Phi
-regret, this general reduction has several implications for the algorithmic landscape of online multicalibration. To start, we show how previous algorithms with lengthy, elaborate proofs can be recovered and improved by selecting different online learning algorithms and EVI solvers in the reduction. These include the K29 algorithm, as proposed in
[
53
]
and extended in
[

15
]

[

]

Beyond its conceptual simplicity and ability to recover known results, we also show how to use our reduction to derive simple algorithms for more complex multicalibrated forecasting tasks.
In particular, by drawing on the well-developed literature on online learning, we design the first online multicalibration algorithms that succeed even in the face of delayed or censored outcomes. All of these follow as simple corollaries of
Theorem

Furthermore, our contributions for online multicalibration flow through to yield improved algorithms for solution concepts that are implications of multicalibration, such as online omniprediction or outcome indistinguishability. In particular, our results yield an efficient black-box reduction for online multiclass omniprediction for general loss functions and hypothesis classes.

An Alternative Path to
\Phi
-regret.

Our second main contribution runs in the opposite direction, from forecasting to decision-making.

Theorem 3

(Informal)
.

Let
\sigma(p)\in\arg\min_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\;p^{\top}z
denote the best-response map. For every deviation
\phi\in\Phi
, define the test function
h_{\phi}(p)\coloneqq\sigma(p)-\phi(\sigma(p))
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\coloneqq\{h_{\phi}:\phi\in\Phi\}
. Then, any online forecaster of the losses
\ell_{t}
that is multicalibrated with respect to the class
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
can be black-box converted to an efficient no-
\Phi
-regret algorithm by simply best-responding to the forecasts.

This theorem yields a substantially finer-grained reduction from decision-making to forecasting than the folklore connection between the demanding notion of swap regret and the demanding notion of
\ell_{1}
-calibration. As discussed previously, swap regret minimization is inefficient on general sets (e.g.,
[
10
]
), which begs the question: What is the richest class of deviations
\Phi
that one can handle?
We address that question by establishing a more precise connection between deviations
\phi
and multicalibration tests
h_{\phi}
, thus obtaining reductions in which the required multicalibration class scales naturally with the complexity of
\Phi
. This fine-grained reduction recovers the classical connection between strong calibration and swap regret only in the limit when
\Phi
is the set of all deviations.

Together with the previous result, this theorem gives an alternative to the reduction of
[
29
]
(see
Figure

1
). This route, grounded in forecasting and EVIs rather than fixed points, has features that make it appealing when the set of deviations
\Phi
is large. Perhaps most importantly, unlike the GGM framework, this route does not require an understanding of the geometry of endomorphisms of the action space, dispensing with much of the technical complexity in recent extensions. We highlight that this leads to a substantially different construction for the recent STOC paper on linear swap regret
[
9
]
: we improve the rates, completely avoid the semiseparation machinery needed there to handle endomorphisms, and even obtain an extension to general RKHS deviation classes, yielding the first algorithm for that setting. Furthermore, we highlight that these results hold not just for
\Phi
-regret, but also for
contextual

\Phi
-regret as we explain in

Related Work

Our work lies at the intersection of various areas. Here, we briefly outline how our results build on and contribute to the most closely related work in each.

Multicalibration.

Multicalibration was introduced in the batch setting by
[
37
]
as a definition of algorithmic fairness. As discussed previously, multicalibration has a wide range of interesting consequences, including outcome indistinguishability
[

]
, omniprediction
[

,
26
,
11
,

[

[

5

4
,

Batch notions of multicalibration are achievable via boosting algorithms that repeatedly call a weak agnostic learning oracle. These procedures are sample inefficient by at least a quadratic factor
[

\sqrt{T}

[

, or any vector-valued reproducing kernel Hilbert space

]

Prior work has established reductions between multicalibration and regret minimization in specific settings.

]

\varepsilon

\Phi

Virtually all algorithms for

A GGM-Like Theorem for Multicalibration, and Finer-Grained Connections between Decision-Making and Forecasting

Multicalibration with respect to finite sets.

To start, suppose we would like to produce forecasts of high-dimensional outcomes

2.1.1
The Necessity of EVIs: Reducing EVIs to Online Multicalibration

The previous theorem shows that one can construct a
\mathcal{H}
-multicalibrated forecaster from a no-regret learner over
\mathcal{H}
, provided that EVIs can be solved efficiently. Thus, EVIs are
sufficient
to construct multicalibrated forecasters. A natural question, however, is whether they are also the “right” primitive for this problem.
In this subsection, we show that EVIs are necessary as well, in the sense that the existence of an efficient
\mathcal{H}
-multicalibrated forecaster automatically implies the existence of an efficient EVI solver over functions in
\mathcal{H}
. In this respect, our result parallels the reduction of
[
29
]
between external regret and
\Phi
-regret, where (expected) fixed points are sufficient and necessary in the analogous sense
[
35
]
.

To show necessity, suppose that we have an
\mathcal{H}
-multicalibrated forecaster, that is, an algorithm that produces distributions
\mathcal{D}_{t}
such that for any
h\in\mathcal{H}
and arbitrary sequence
\{(x_{t},y_{t})\}_{t=1}^{T}
,

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\Big[h(x_{t},p_{t})^{\top}(y_{t}-p_{t})\Big]\leq\frac{1}{T}\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}

where
\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}
is
o(T)
.
We show that the above algorithm can be turned into an algorithm for solving EVIs defined in terms of any
x
and
h
to any error
\varepsilon>0
:

\mathbb{E}_{p\sim\mathcal{D}}\Big[h(x,p)^{\top}(y-p)\Big]\leq\varepsilon\qquad\forall y\in\mathcal{Y}.

To see this, consider the scenario in which Nature picks
x_{t}=x
at all times. Let
\mathcal{D}_{t}
be the distribution returned by the
\mathcal{H}
-calibrated forecaster. We will let the
y_{t}
be a best response

\displaystyle y_{t}\coloneqq\argmax_{\hat{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}\hat{y}^{\top}\left[\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}h(x,p_{t})\right].

Then, we have that for any fixed
y_{\star}\in\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\Big[h(x,p_{t})^{\top}(y_{t}-p_{t})\Big]\geq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\Big[h(x,p_{t})^{\top}(y_{\star}-p_{t})\Big].

As
T\to\infty
,
\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}/T\to 0
and the uniform mixture
\mathcal{D}
over
\mathcal{D}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{D}_{T}
is a solution to the EVI defined by
h(x,\cdot)
.

Our main contribution here establishes that less demanding notions of

\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\coloneqq\{(x,p)\mapsto\sigma(p)-\phi(\sigma(p)):\phi\in\Phi\},\qquad\sigma(p)\coloneqq\argmin_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}p^{\top}z.

of multicalibration tests.

3
Algorithmic Implications for Online Multicalibration

and Omniprediction

In this section, we examine some of the consequences of our reduction from regret minimization to online multicalibration (
Theorem

4
). We highlight three main implications.

(a)

We show that several existing online high-dimensional multicalibration algorithms are special cases of our reduction, thereby unifying their previously disparate analyses. These reinterpretations sometimes yield improved runtimes as well.

(b)

We demonstrate how the reduction facilitates the technical transfer of ideas between well-studied variants of online learning and online multicalibration, yielding the first known online multicalibration algorithms that succeed in more challenging environments where outcomes are only partially observed.

(c)

We show how reduction yields an oracle-efficient algorithm for online omniprediction in the multiclass setting, solving another open problem from
[

]
.

3.2.1
Online Multicalibration with Delayed Observations

One immediate implication of

are not immediately observed.

To motivate the problem, consider the setting where at every day

Despite these difficulties, the doctor aims to make predictions that are not just calibrated, but multicalibrated without making any assumptions about how the data is generated. That is, they aim to sequentially produce predictions that satisfy the following definition:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\left[\ell(\pi_{\ell}(p_{t}),y_{t})\right]\leq\min_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell(c(x_{t}),y_{t})+o(T).

Intuitively, an omnipredictor is an algorithm that produces predictions that can be
postprocessed
to yield good decisions. They guarantee that the best response function to any function
\ell
,

\displaystyle\pi_{\ell}(p_{t})=\argmin_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{y}_{t}\sim p_{t}}\ell(z,\tilde{y}_{t})=\argmin_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell(z,i)\cdot p_{t,i},

has low excess risk to the best decision rule in
\mathcal{C}
for that loss
\ell
. Note that by definition, the postprocessing of
p_{t}
has lower loss than
any
decision rule
c:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
on outcomes sampled from
p_{t}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{y}\sim p}[\ell(\pi_{\ell}(p_{t}),\tilde{y})]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{y}\sim p}[\ell(h(x),\tilde{y})].

It is by now well known
[
27
,
24
,
26
,
11
,
42
]
that given any set of loss functions
\mathcal{L}
and decision rules
\mathcal{C}
, one can construct a set of test functions
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}}
(defined in terms of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{C}
) such that multicalibration with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}}
implies online omniprediction with respect to
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{C}
.

What is new in our work is that by exploiting this connection, and our new path between regret minimization and online multicalibration, we establish a path between regret minimization and omniprediction that yields oracle-efficient algorithms for omniprediction.

We start by stating the following (now folklore) lemma, which we specialize to the multiclass setting, proving that multicalibration implies omniprediction.

Lemma 2
.

Fix a class of loss functions
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\{\mathcal{Z}\times[k]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}
and a set of decision rules
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\{\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}\}
. Define the set of test functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}}=\{h_{\ell}:\ell\in\mathcal{L}\}\cup\{h_{\ell,c}:\ell\in\mathcal{L},c\in\mathcal{C}\}\subseteq\{\mathcal{X}\times\Delta_{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\}

(7)

where
h_{\ell}
and
h_{\ell,c}
are defined as:

\displaystyle h_{\ell}(p_{t})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big(\ell(\pi_{\ell}(p_{t}),1),\dots,\ell(\pi_{\ell}(p_{t}),k)\big)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

\displaystyle h_{\ell,c}(x_{t})

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\big(\ell(c(x_{t}),1),\dots,\ell(c(x_{t}),k)\big)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}.

If an online algorithm
\mathcal{A}
produces distributions
\mathcal{D}_{t}
with multicalibration error bounded by
\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}
with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}}
, then
\mathcal{A}
is an omnipredictor with respect to
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{C}
with omniprediction error bounded by
2\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}
. That is, for every
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\left[\ell(\pi_{\ell}(p_{t}),y_{t})\right]\leq\min_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell(c(x_{t}),y_{t})+2\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}.

Proof.

The proof follows the same template pioneered by
[
26
]
, showing that outcome indistinguishability implies omniprediction. In particular, using the notation
\tilde{y}_{t}\sim p_{t}
to denote sampling a multiclass outcome from the distribution specified by
p_{t}\in\Delta_{k}
where
\Pr[\tilde{y}=i]=p_{t,i}
, we have the following rewriting,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underset{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}{\mathbb{E}}[\ell(\pi_{\ell}(p_{t}),y_{t})]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underset{\tilde{y}_{t}\sim p_{t}}{\underset{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}{\mathbb{E}}}[\ell(\pi_{\ell}(p_{t}),\tilde{y}_{t})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\left[h_{\ell}(p_{t})^{\top}(\vec{y}_{t}-p_{t})\right]\leq\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}

(8)

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underset{\tilde{y}_{t}\sim p_{t}}{\underset{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}{\mathbb{E}}}[\ell(c(x_{t}),\tilde{y}_{t})]-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell(c(x_{t}),y_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\left[h_{\ell,c}(x_{t})^{\top}(\vec{y}_{t}-p_{t})\right]\leq\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}.

(9)

Using these inequalities, for any function
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
and
c\in\mathcal{C}
, we have that,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}[\ell(\pi_{\ell}(p_{t}),y_{t})]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underset{\tilde{y}_{t}\sim p_{t}}{\underset{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}{\mathbb{E}}}\bigl[\ell(\pi_{\ell}(p_{t}),\tilde{y})\bigr]+\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}

(By
Equation

8
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underset{\tilde{y}_{t}\sim p_{t}}{\underset{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}{\mathbb{E}}}[\ell(c(x_{t}),\tilde{y}_{t})]+\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}

(By definition of
\pi(p_{t})
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell(c(x_{t}),y_{t})+2\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}\,.

(By
Equation

9
)

Since
\ell
and
c
were arbitrary, the argument holds for the best
c\in\mathcal{C}
, thereby proving the lemma.
∎

Using this lemma, and our black-box reduction between multicalibration and online learning
Theorem

4
, we get the following oracle-efficient algorithm for omniprediction.

Corollary 3
.

Fix a class of loss functions
\mathcal{L}
and decision rules
\mathcal{C}
. Define the class of test functions
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}}
as in
Equation

7
and let
\mathcal{A}
be an online algorithm that guarantees external regret at most
\textrm{Regret}_{T}
with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C}}
, as per
Equation

2
.

Then, using
\mathcal{A}
as the online learner in
Algorithm

1
yields an online omniprediction algorithm with respect to
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{C}
with omniprediction error bounded by
2\textrm{Regret}_{T}
.

In short, this corollary is an end-to-end reduction between omniprediction and online learning. Depending on the pair
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C})
, there are many online algorithms one could use to guarantee omniprediction. For instance, one could always run Multiplicative Weights over finite classes
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C})
and get an online omniprediction algorithm with error bounded by
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T\log(|\mathcal{L}|\cdot|\mathcal{C}|)})
. If
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{C})
belongs to an RKHS, one could run defensive forecasting algorithms (see, e.g.,
[
11
,
15
]
) and get a
\sqrt{T}
bound with additional dependence on the norms of the functions in the RKHS.

[
47
]
present oracle efficient algorithms for the specific case of omniprediction with respect to binary outcomes. Relative to their work, our results holds for general multiclass settings. Furthermore, our construction can also be extended to work for general real-valued or vector-valued outcomes by using ideas in
[
42
,
28
]
where one rewrites nonlinear loss functions as linear functions of a high-dimensional vector of statistics. We omit this construction to keep our presentation short.

4
Algorithmic Implications for
\Phi
-Regret Minimization

We now expand on how our new path between external regret minimization and
\Phi
-regret minimization, via multicalibration and EVIs, enables algorithms with improved rates and simpler analyses.

Recent approaches to
\Phi
-regret minimization have considered ever-increasing sets of transformations
\Phi
and general convex action sets
\mathcal{Z}

[
9
,
2
,
57
]
.
In all these applications, a recurring technical challenge is that GGM requires that the transformations
\phi\in\Phi
have fixed points in
\mathcal{Z}
. And unfortunately, the set of functions that admit fixed points is not convex.

To circumvent the issue,
[
9
]
was the first to introduce intricate
semiseparation
techniques to operate on supersets of linear endomorphisms of the action set
\mathcal{Z}
that admit fixed points in
\mathcal{Z}
. Extensions of these techniques have been critical to enable the results of
[
2
]
and
[
57
]
. Given the sophistication of semi-separation, it should not come as a surprise that these more recent results come at the cost of extremely technically involved algorithms and undesirably high dependency on the dimensionality of the action set (
cf.

[
1
]
).

In this section, we show that the EVI-based reduction from online learning to multicalibration (
Theorem

4
), combined with the reduction from multicalibration to
\Phi
-regret minimization (
Theorem

5
), yields an EVI-based, forecasting-centric alternative to the otherwise fixed-point-based construction of
[
29
]
that overcomes many of the aforementioned shortcomings.

4.1
Improving Rates for Linear Swap Minimization

To fix ideas, consider the main problem studied by
[
9
]
: constructing a linear swap-regret minimizer for a well-bounded (
Definition

3
) convex compact set
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, facing losses from a well-bounded convex compact set
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For both sets, we assume efficient oracle access.
In this setting, the set of transformations
\Phi
is the set of all linear mappings from
\mathcal{Z}
to
\mathcal{Z}
,
i.e.
, all linear endomorphisms of
\mathcal{Z}
.
We assume that the set of loss vectors
\mathcal{L}
has maximum Euclidean norm
L
, each point in
\mathcal{Z}
has maximum Euclidean norm
B
, and that the geometry of
\mathcal{Z}
is such that the every linear endomorphism of
\mathcal{Z}
has maximum spectral norm
S
. We remark that the Fritz John’s theorem implies that any convex body admits a linear transformation ensuring that
S=B=d
.

In this setting, the classical GGM approach to
\Phi
-regret minimization would reduce to regret minimization over
\Phi
. Unfortunately, the latter computational task is daunting, since testing membership in the set of endomorphisms is provably hard
[
9
]
.
By instead taking a forecasting point of view, we can construct a no-linear-swap regret minimizer for
\Phi
starting from any online forecaster for the convex loss set
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is multicalibrated with respect to the class of tests

\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}\coloneqq\{(x,p)\mapsto\sigma(p)-\phi(\sigma(p)):\phi\in\Phi\},

where
\sigma
is the best-response function on
\mathcal{Z}
(see
Equation

4
). Since the
\phi\in\Phi
are linear functions, each element of
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
is a linear function of
\sigma(p)
. Hence, one way to multicalibrate with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
is to multicalibrate with respect to the ball of
all
linear functions

\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\{(x,p)\mapsto M\sigma(p):\|M\|_{F}\leq\rho\},

where set the parameter
\rho>0
to be large enough. In particular, since we assumed that each linear endomorphism has maximum spectral norm
S
, and
\|\cdot\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{d}\|\cdot\|_{\text{spectral}}
, it suffices to set
\rho=\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{d}S
to ensure capturing all functions in
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
.

By leveraging
Theorems

4
and
5
, we can then reduce
\mathcal{H}
-multicalibration to online learning on
\mathcal{H}
with linear losses where we optimize over matrices
\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:\|M\|_{F}\leq\rho\}
. Crucially, several standard no-regret algorithms can be applied off-the-shelf on the Frobenius ball. In particular, by invoking the standard analysis of online projected gradient descent, we obtain the following.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be well-bounded convex bodies given via efficient oracle access. We let
L
denote the maximum Euclidean norm of any vector in
\mathcal{L}
,
B
denote the maximum Euclidean norm of any point in
\mathcal{Z}
, and
S
denote the maximum spectral (operator) norm of any linear endomorphism of
\mathcal{Z}
.
There exists a linear swap minimization algorithm for strategy set
\mathcal{Z}
and loss set
\mathcal{L}
with polynomial-time iterations that guarantees
\mathcal{O}(BLS\sqrt{dT})
linear swap regret for any time horizon
T
, no matter the sequence of losses
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{T}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

We construct the
\mathcal{H}
-multicalibrated forecaster by instantiating
Theorem

4
using projected gradient descent on
\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:\|M\|_{F}\leq\rho\}
. At every time
t
, the algorithm outputs a matrix
M_{t}
inducing the test
p\mapsto M\sigma(p)\in\mathcal{H}
, and incurs the linear loss

\displaystyle f_{t}(M)=-\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\Big[(M\sigma(p_{t}))^{\top}(\ell_{t}-p_{t})\Big]=-\Big\langle M,\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}\Big[(\ell_{t}-p_{t})\sigma(p_{t})^{\top}\Big]\Big\rangle_{F}.

Since
p_{t},\ell_{t}\in\mathcal{L}
and
\sigma(p_{t})\in\mathcal{Z}
, the norm of the gradient of the loss function is therefore bounded by
\mathcal{O}(LB)
. Furthermore, by construction of
\mathcal{H}
the Frobenius norm of the domain is bounded above by
\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{d}S
. Since
S\geq 1
(the identity map is always an endomorphism of
\mathcal{Z}
, and has unit spectral norm), the bound on the norm of the domain is therefore
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{d}S)
. Since the regret incurred by projected gradient descent scales as the norm of the domain, times the norm of the loss set, times
\sqrt{T}
, we obtain a regret bound on
\mathcal{H}
of the order
\mathcal{O}(BLS\sqrt{dT})
. By
Theorem

4
, if we solve the EVI problems to precision
\varepsilon_{t}=t^{-2}
, we get that
\textrm{EVI}_{T}\leq\mathcal{O}(1)
and for any linear endomorphism
\phi
:

\displaystyle\textrm{Regret}^{T}_{\Phi}(\phi)\leq\textrm{MC-Err}_{T}(h_{\phi})\leq\textrm{EVI}_{T}+\textrm{Regret}_{T}(h_{\phi})\leq\mathcal{O}(BLS\sqrt{dT}).

We remark that the construction leads to polynomial-time iterates, in light of
1
and the polynomial dimension of
M
.
∎

If the set
\mathcal{Z}
is already in John’s position and under the standard normalization assumption that
|\ell^{\top}z|\leq 1
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{L},z\in\mathcal{Z}
(i.e.,
B=d,L=1,S=d
), this yields a rate of
\mathcal{O}(d^{5/2}\sqrt{T})
which improves upon the rate
\mathcal{O}(d^{4}\sqrt{T})
of
[
9
]
.

However, the benefit of this forecasting approach to
\Phi
-regret is not only its improved dependence on the dimension
d
, but rather that it establishes a dramatically simpler algorithmic template.
Indeed, while GGM-based approaches necessarily need to grapple with the complicated geometry of endomorphisms (expected fixed points of non-endomorphic maps need not exist, invalidating the GGM reduction), the multicalibration route is much more permissive: it is always easy to multicalibrate with respect to more tests. In our case, we promoted the set of tests from
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
, to the set of
all
linear multicalibration tests of sufficiently large norm, and the latter is no harder than performing online projected gradient descent on a Euclidean ball. We did not have to concern ourselves with whether the transformations in the enlarged set
\mathcal{H}\supseteq\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
admit fixed points in
\mathcal{Z}
.

4.2
No-
\Phi
-Regret with Respect to RKHS Deviations

Follow-up work to
[
9
]
has begun to explore
\Phi
-regret minimization beyond linear deviations, including cases where the deviations are low-degree polynomials or endomorphisms given by a linear combination of a finite, polynomial number of nonlinear features
[
57
]
.

In this section, we point out that the same approach outlined in
Section

4.1
directly extends to these settings too. In fact, by multicalibrating over infinite sets of test functions using the techniques developed in
Section

3.1
, we can obtain, with no effort and for the first time,
\Phi
-regret minimization algorithms in which
\Phi
contains all transformations that lie in an RKHS ball (including RKHS with potentially infinitely many features, such as the Gaussian RKHS). This recovers low-degree polynomials as a special case.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be convex and compact with efficient oracle access, and let
\Phi
be the subset of endomorphic functions
\phi:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
belonging to a vector-valued RKHS
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
with corresponding matrix-valued kernel
\Gamma((x,z),(x^{\prime},z^{\prime}))
. Assume
\Gamma
can be evaluated in polynomial time for any inputs and let
\rho\coloneqq\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the maximum RKHS norm of any of the functions in
\Phi
. Then, using Defensive Forecasting (
Algorithm

3
,
Proposition

1
) with the following kernel,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\prime}((x,p),(x^{\prime},p^{\prime}))=\sigma(p)\sigma(p^{\prime})^{\top}+\Gamma((x,\sigma(p)),(x^{\prime},\sigma(p^{\prime}))),

(10)

as the loss forecaster in the black-box reduction between multicalibration and
\Phi
-regret minimization (
Algorithm

2
) produces distributions
\mu_{t}
over
\mathcal{Z}
satisfying the following for any sequence
\{\ell_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi\in\Phi}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{z_{t}\sim\mu_{t}}\Big[\ell_{t}^{\top}(z_{t}-\phi(x_{t},z_{t}))\Big]\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\rho\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}(p_{t}-\ell_{t})^{\top}\Gamma^{\prime}((x_{t},p_{t}),(x_{t},p_{t}))(p_{t}-\ell_{t})}\right).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{Z}
has an efficient linear optimization oracle, evaluations of the best response map
\sigma:p\mapsto\argmin_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}p^{\top}z
are also efficient.
From
Theorem

5
, it suffices to construct an
\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}
multicalibrated forecaster for the loss set
\mathcal{L}=\{\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\ell^{\top}z|\leq 1~~\forall z\in\mathcal{Z}\}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}=\{(x,p)\mapsto\sigma(p)-\phi(\sigma(p)):\phi\in\Phi\}.

(11)

The crucial observation is that since every deviation
\phi
belongs to an RKHS with kernel
\Gamma((x,p),(x^{\prime},p^{\prime}))
, then
\phi(\sigma(p))
belongs to an RKHS with kernel
\Gamma((x,\sigma(p)),(x^{\prime},\sigma(p^{\prime})))
and the functions
\sigma(p)-\phi(x,\sigma(p))
belong the RKHS corresponding to the kernel in
Equation

10
.

This makes use of the fact that if
f_{1}
is in the RKHS defined by kernel
\Gamma_{1}
and
f_{2}
is in the RKHS defined by
\Gamma_{2}
, then
\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}
is a kernel that contains
f_{1}+f_{2}
. Furthermore, the norm of
f_{1}+f_{2}
in the new RKHS is at most the square root of the sum of the squared norms.

The functions
\sigma(p)
lie in the RKHS corresponding to the linear kernel
\sigma(p)\sigma(p^{\prime})^{\top}
and have norm at most 1. Furthermore,
\phi(x,\sigma(p))
lie in the RKHS corresponding to
\Gamma((x,\sigma(p)),(x^{\prime},\sigma(p^{\prime})))
and have norm at most
\rho
by our assumption on
\Gamma
and Theorem 5.14 in
[
49
]
. Therefore,
Proposition

1
guarantees that the multicalibration error of the forecaster with respect to the functions in
Equation

11
is at most

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\rho\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{p_{t}\sim\mathcal{D}_{t}}(p_{t}-\ell_{t})^{\top}\Gamma^{\prime}((x_{t},p_{t}),(x_{t},p_{t}))(p_{t}-\ell_{t})}\right).

The bound on
\Phi
-regret follows from our main result in
Theorem

5
.
∎

Note that this result is in fact stated for the stronger notion of
contextual
regret where the decision maker can observe features each round, and considers deviations of their actions that depend on such contexts. Furthermore, we state the result for general kernels
\Gamma
to illustrate a tighter sequence-dependent bound. However, virtually all known kernels (polynomial, Gaussian, etc.) have
\|\Gamma((x,p),(x,p))\|_{\mathrm{op}}
bounded by a constant
B
. In this case, we can replace the bound by one of the form
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})
where the
\mathcal{O}(\cdot)
hides dependence on
B
and the norms of
\ell_{t}
.
