𝕋 \mathbb{T} -homogeneous locally nilpotent derivations of trinomial algebras Thanks: The work is supported by the grant RSF 25-11-00302
Timofey Krasikov and Kirill Rassolov

Abstract
. A trinomial algebra is a commutative finitely generated algebra given by a system of compatible relations each of which is a polynomial with three terms. Such algebras arise as the Cox rings of varieties admitting a complexity one torus action. We describe locally nilpotent derivations of a trinomial algebra that are homogeneous under a natural torus action of complexity one.

Primary 13A50, 13R20; Secondary 14J50, 14L30, 14M25

Introduction

A
trinomial variety
is an affine variety given by a system of equations of the form
aM_{i}+bM_{i+1}+cM_{i+2}=0
, compatible in a special way. Here the
M_{i}
denote fixed monomials in disjoint sets of variables; see Section
1
for details. Following
[
15
]
, we distinguish two types of trinomial varieties depending on whether the monomial corresponding to the empty set of variables (i.e., equal to
1
) is allowed. The coordinate algebra of a trinomial variety is called a
trinomial algebra
. The ground field
\Bbbk
is assumed algebraically closed and of characteristic zero.

Recall that the
complexity
of an action of a reductive group
G
on a variety
X
is the codimension of a general orbit of a Borel subgroup
B\subseteq G
. For an effective action of an algebraic torus
T
, the complexity equals
\dim X-\dim T
. A trinomial variety is endowed with a natural torus action of complexity one, which comes from the standard torus action on affine space by scaling the variables. Moreover, there is a close connection between trinomial algebras and arbitrary varieties with a torus action of complexity one via Cox rigns.

The Cox construction (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
) is fruitfully applied to toric varieties, that is to say, normal varieties with a torus action of complexity zero. The Cox ring of a toric variety is a polynomial ring, which allows one to realize, say, an affine toric variety as the categorical quotient of an affine space by an action of a diagonalizable group
[
6
]
. In turn, trinomial algebras are the Cox rings of normal rational semiprojective varieties with a torus action of complexity one, finitely generated divisor class group, and with no nonconstant invertible functions. In particular, every affine variety satisfying these conditions is the categorical quotient of a trinomial variety by an action of a diagonalizable group
[
14
,
15
]
.

Consider a finitely generated commutative associative algebra
R
over the field
\Bbbk
. A
derivation
is a linear map
\delta\colon R\to R
satisfying the Leibniz rule:
\delta(fg)=\delta(f)g+f\delta(g)
for all
f,g\in R
.
Suppose
R
is equipped with a grading
R=\bigoplus_{g\in G}R_{g}
by an abelian group
G
. A derivation
\delta
is called
homogeneous
if there exists an element
d\in G
, called the
degree
of
\delta
, such that
\delta(R_{g})\subseteq R_{g+d}
for all
g\in G
. It is well known that specifying a grading on
R
by a finitely generated abelian group is equivalent to specifying an action of a quasitorus, i.e., a diagonalizable group. Moreover, gradings by free abelian groups correspond to torus actions. We say that a derivation
\delta
is
homogeneous
under a quasitorus if
\delta
is homogeneous with respect to the corresponding grading.

A derivation
\delta
is called
locally nilpotent
, or an
LND
, if for every
f\in R
, there exists an
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\delta^{n}(f)=0
. The exponential map establishes a bijection between locally nilpotent derivations up to scaling and
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroups in the automorphism group of a given algebra. By a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroup we mean a one-parameter algebraic subgroup isomorphic to the additive group of the ground field
\Bbbk
. Namely, an LND
\delta
corresponds to the subgroup
\{\exp(t\delta)\,|\,t\in\Bbbk\}
. Furthermore, LNDs homogeneous with respect to the action of a quasitorus
H
correspond to
H
-normalized
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroups.

Homogeneous LNDs of trinomial algebras were studied in
[
20
,
13
,
19
]
. Explicit formulae for the homogeneous LNDs were obtained in the case of complexity one action of a special quasitorus
\mathbb{H}
coming from the standard torus action on affine space by scaling the variables. The
\mathbb{H}
-homogeneous LNDs were called
elementary
. In the present paper, we consider LNDs homogeneous with respect to the torus
{\mathbb{T}}
which is the neutral component of the group
\mathbb{H}
. For factorial varieties, the quasitorus
\mathbb{H}
is connected and therefore coincides with
{\mathbb{T}}
. However in non-factorial case we have the strict inclusion
{\mathbb{T}}\subset\mathbb{H}
, and there may appear
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs which are not
\mathbb{H}
-homogeneus; see Remark
5.7
for specific example.

Our interest in LNDs homogeneous with respect to a torus is motivated by their applications to study of arbitrary LNDs. Given a derivation of a finitely generated algebra, one can decompose it into a sum of homogeneous derivations, which are called the
homogeneous components
. However, the homogeneous components of a locally nilpotent derivation are, generally speaking, not locally nilpotent. The importance of free gradings is that for certain homogeneous components of a given LND, local nilpotency can be guaranteed. To be more precise, one should consider the Newton polytop of the degrees of the non-zero homogeneous components; then the homogeneous components corresponding to the vertices turn out to be locally nilpotent. Thus the study of homogeneous LNDs for free gradings provides more information about an arbitrary LND than for gradings by a non-free abelian group. In particular, we give a new proof of the rigidity criterion obtained earlier in
[
12
]
.

Recall that an algebra
R
is called
rigid
if its automorphism group contains no
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroups, i.e.,
R
admits no non-zero LNDs. Two LNDs are called
equivalent
if their kernels, i.e., the algebras of invariants of the corresponding
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroups, coincide.
R
is called
semirigid
if all nonzero LNDs are equivalent.

We show that for a trinomial algebra of Type
1
, all
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs are replicas of elementary LNDs, and we provide explicit formulae (Theorem
2.2
). As an application, besides a new proof of the rigidity criterion (Corollary
2.4
), we also obtain a semirigidity criterion for Type
1
, see Corollary
3.2
.

For a trinomial algebra of Type
2
, we obtain a description of the equivalence classes of
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs (Theorem
4.1
). This implies a semirigidity criterion, see Corollary
4.2
. Finally, Theorem
4.4
provides explicit formulae for
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs in the case when the algebra is defined by a single relation. In contrast to Type
1
, there appear
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs that are not replicas of elementary ones.

Our proofs for Type
2
use the combinatorial description of homogeneous LNDs on an affine variety with a torus action of complexity one from
[
17
]
. It is based on the representation of varieties with a torus action via proper polyhedral divisors on semiprojective varieties, see
[
1
]
. However when a high-dimensional affine variety is explicitly given by equations, this requires complex computations associated with multidimensional rational polyhedra. Therefore, we use the technique of polyhedral divisors only for trinomial surfaces. Next, we show that a
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LND on an arbitrary trinomial variety of Type
2
corresponds to a homogeneous LND on a trinomial surface.

1.
Preliminaries

Trinomial algebras

We begin with some notation. Fix integers
r\geq 2,

d\geq 0,r_{0}\in\{0,1\}
and consider the variables
T_{ij},\,S_{k}
with
i\in\{r_{0},\ldots,r\},

j\in\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\},

n_{i}\geq 1
,
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. We will view the
T_{ij},S_{k}
as coordinates in the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n+d}
,
n=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{r}
, and abbreviate the corresponding polynomial algebra in
n+d
variables by
\Bbbk[T_{ij},S_{k}]
. Following Construction 1 of
[
15
]
, we now define two types of trinomial varieties.

Type
1
.
Set
r_{0}=1
and choose pairwise distinct elements
a_{i}\in\Bbbk
. Then the subvariety in the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n+d}
given by:

(1.1)

T_{i}^{l_{i}}-T_{i+1}^{l_{i+1}}-(a_{i+1}-a_{i})=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,r-1

is called a
trinomial variety of Type
1
. Note that (
1.1
) is equivalent to that any three columns of the matrix:

M=\begin{pmatrix}1&T_{1}^{l_{1}}&\ldots&T_{r}^{l_{r}}\\
-1&a_{1}&\ldots&a_{r}\\
0&1&\ldots&1\end{pmatrix}

are linearly dependent over
\Bbbk
.

Type
2
. Set
r_{0}=0
. Fix also a matrix
A=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{r})
with pairwise linearly independent column vectors
a_{i}\in\Bbbk^{2}
. We define a
trinomial variety of Type
2
in
\mathbb{A}^{n+d}
by requiring that any three columns of the
3\times(r+1)
-matrix:

M=\begin{pmatrix}T_{0}^{l_{0}}&\ldots&T_{r}^{l_{r}}\\
a_{0}&\ldots&a_{r}\end{pmatrix}

are linearly dependent over
\Bbbk
. In turn, this can be rewritten as

(1.2)

\det\begin{pmatrix}T_{i}^{l_{i}}&T_{i+1}^{l_{i+1}}&T_{i+2}^{l_{i+2}}\\
a_{i}&a_{i+1}&a_{i+2}\end{pmatrix}=0,\quad i=0,\ldots,r-2.

Definition 1.1
.

A trinomial algebra
of Type
1
(resp.
2
) is the quotient algebra of
\Bbbk[T_{ij},S_{k}]
by the ideal generated by relations (
1.1
) (resp. (
1.2
)).

It was proved in
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
that any trinomial algebra is a normal domain of dimension
n+d-r+1
. Thus, it is the algebra of global functions of the corresponding trinomial variety
X
; the latter is also normal and of dimension
n+d-r+1
. We endow
X
with a complexity one torus action as follows.

Construction 1.2
.

Consider the torus of dimension
n+d
acting on the variables
T_{ij}
and
S_{k}
in
\mathbb{A}^{n+d}
by multiplication. Let
\mathbb{H}
be the stabilizer of equations (
1.1
) or (
1.2
) respectively; it is given by requiring that the monomials
T_{i}^{l_{i}}
are invariant in case of Type
1
, and semi-invariant, of the same weight in case of Type
2
. Let
{\mathbb{T}}
stand for the identity component of
\mathbb{H}
. Clearly,
{\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathbb{H}
act on the trinomial variety
X
and
\dim{\mathbb{T}}=\dim X-1
.

Remark 1.3
.

The
\mathbb{H}
-action corresponds to a grading of
\Bbbk[X]
by the character group of
\mathbb{H}
; this grading was defined in
[
15
, Construction 1.1]
. It can be viewed as the finest grading such that the variables
T_{ij},S_{k}
are homogeneous; see
[
20
, Lemma 1]
. The
{\mathbb{T}}
-action is obtained by identifying the character lattice of
{\mathbb{T}}
with the torsion-free part of the character group of
\mathbb{H}
.

Remark 1.4
.

One can obtain the grading corresponding to the
{\mathbb{T}}
-action more directly. Namely, fix a variable
T_{ib_{i}}
in each monomial
T_{i}^{l_{i}}
.

In the case of Type
1
, we consider the group
\mathbb{Z}^{n+d-r}
with the canonical basis
e_{ij},\,e_{k}
where
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\}\setminus\{b_{i}\}
,
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Set

\deg T_{ij}=e_{ij}\prod_{k\neq j}l_{ik},\quad j\neq b_{i},\qquad\qquad\deg T_{ib_{i}}=-\sum_{j\neq b_{i}}e_{ij}\prod_{k\neq b_{i}}l_{ik},

\deg S_{k}=e_{k},\quad\mbox{all $k$}.

For Type
2
, consider the group
\mathbb{Z}^{n+d-r}
with the canonical basis
e_{ij},\,e_{k},\,e
where
i\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\}\setminus\{b_{i}\}
,
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Set

\deg T_{ij}=e_{ij}\prod_{k\neq j}l_{ik},\quad\mbox{$j\neq b_{i}$,}\quad\qquad\deg T_{ib_{i}}=e\prod_{p\neq i}l_{pb_{p}}-\sum_{j\neq b_{i}}e_{ij}\prod_{k\neq b_{i}}l_{ik},

\deg S_{k}=e_{k},\quad\mbox{all $k$}.

Then one can think of the character lattice of
{\mathbb{T}}
as the subgroup (of finite index) of
\mathbb{Z}^{n+d-r}
generated by all
\deg T_{ij},

\deg S_{k}
.

Remark 1.5
.

Suppose that
n_{i}l_{ij}>1
for all
i,j
; this always can be achieved by eliminating the variables that occur as a linear term in some relation. It follows from
[
15
, Theorem 1.2
(iv)
]
that
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{T}
(i.e.,
\mathbb{H}
is connected) if and only if the trinomial algebra is factorial. Furthermore, in case of Type
1
, this is equivalent to the condition
\gcd(l_{i1},\ldots,l_{in_{i}})=1
for all
i
. In case of Type
2
, a necessary and sufficient condition is that the integers
d_{i}=\gcd(l_{i1},\ldots,l_{in_{i}})
are pairwise coprime.

We will say that nonzero nonunit elements
a,b
of a trinomial algebra
R
are
coprime
if, for all
f,g\in R
, the condition
fa=gb
implies that
a\mid g
(hence also
b\mid f
).

Lemma 1.6
.

The variables
T_{ij},S_{k}
are pairwise coprime.

Proof.

The grading by the character group of
\mathbb{H}
is factorial and the
T_{ij},S_{k}
are homogeneously prime; see
[
15
, Theorem 1.2
(ii,\,iii)
]
. Thus the assertion follows from the following fact: Let
R
be a factorially graded domain and
a,b\in R
nonassociated homogeneously primes; then
a,b
are coprime.

In fact, take
f,g\in R
. Consider the homogeneous decomposition
f=f_{1}+\ldots+f_{k}
and
g=g_{1}+\ldots+g_{l}
where
f_{p}\neq 0,\,g_{q}\neq 0
for all
p,\,q
. Then
fa=gb
implies that
k=l
and, after a suitable permutation of the
g_{p}
’s, we have
f_{p}a=g_{p}b
for all
p
. Hence
a\mid g_{p}
for all
p
, and so
a\mid g
.
∎

When an integer
a
divides
l_{ij}
for all
j
, we will write
T_{i}^{l_{i}/a}
rather than
\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}T_{ij}^{l_{ij}/a}
.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
R
be a trinomial algebra of Type
2
. Then any element of the field of rational invariants
(\Quot R)^{{\mathbb{T}}}
is a rational function in

\frac{T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{ij}}}{T_{j}^{l_{j}/d_{ij}}}\qquad\mbox{where}\quad d_{ij}=\gcd(d_{i},d_{j}),\quad d_{i}=\gcd(l_{i1},\ldots,l_{in_{i}}).

Proof.

Take a rational
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
f/g\in(\Quot R)^{\mathbb{T}}
. We may assume both
f
and
g
to be
{\mathbb{T}}
-semi-invariant; see
[
18
, Theorem 3.3]
. The assertion will follow from considering the weight of each monomial of
f
and
g
.

Note that, for any
i
,
{\mathbb{T}}
contains the
(n_{i}-1)
-dimensional subtorus
{\mathbb{T}}_{i}
acting only on the variables that appear in
T_{i}^{l_{i}}
. Fix
i
and consider a monomial
P
in the variables
T_{ij}
. Then
P
has weight zero (with respect to
{\mathbb{T}}_{i}
) if and only if
P
is a power of
T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{i}}
. More generally, regardless of the weight of
P
, we have
P=Q\cdot(T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{i}})^{\alpha}
, where
Q
is a monomial not divisible by
T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{i}}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Since, for any
i
,
f
and
g
are of the same
{\mathbb{T}}_{i}
-weight, these
Q
are the same for any monomial appearing in
f
and
g
and thus may be cancelled. Repeating for all
i
, we represent both
f
and
g
as polynomials in the
T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{i}}
whose monomials are all of the same
{\mathbb{T}}
-weight.

Finally, consider the one-dimensional torus
\Bbbk^{\times}\subset{\mathbb{T}}
acting only on the variables
T_{ib_{i}}
(notation as in Remark
1.4
). Two monomials

(T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{i}})^{\alpha}
and
(T_{j}^{l_{j}/d_{j}})^{\beta}
are of the same
\Bbbk^{\times}
-weight if and only if
\alpha/d_{i}=\beta/d_{j}
. Thus the ratio of these monomials is a power of

T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{ij}}/T_{j}^{l_{j}/d_{ij}}
.

It remains to divide both
f
and
g
by any one of the monomials appearing among them to rewrite
f/g
as a rational function in the
{T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{ij}}}/{T_{j}^{l_{j}/d_{ij}}}
.
∎

Locally nilpotent derivations

The following lemma gathers some basic properties of locally nilpotent derivations. We refer the reader to
[
10
, Principles 1, 5, and 7 and Corollary 1.23]
for the proof.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
R
be a
\Bbbk
-domain,
\delta
an LND of
R
and
f,g\in R.
Then:

(i)

f\delta
is a derivation; it is locally nilpotent if and only if
f\in\ker\delta
.

(ii)

\ker\delta
is factorially closed, meaning that
fg\in\ker\delta
implies
f,g\in\ker\delta
.

(iii)

Suppose that
f\,|\,\delta(f)
; then
\delta(f)=0
.

(iv)

Suppose that
f\,|\,\delta(g)
and
g\,|\,\delta(f)
; then either
\delta(f)=0
or
\delta(g)=0
.

The locally nilpotent derivation
f\delta
,
f\in\ker\delta
, is called a
replica
of
\delta
.

Recall that an Abelian group
G
is called
ordered
if it is endowed with a total order which is translation invariant, i.e.,
a\leq b
implies
a+c\leq b+c
for any
a,b,c\in G
. Clearly, a finitely generated Abelian group can be ordered if and only if it is torsion-free. The following lemma is a particular case of
[
17
, Lemma 1.10]
.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
G\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{k}
be an ordered group and
R
a
G
-graded algebra. Then any LND
\delta\colon R\to R
admits the decomposition

\delta=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\delta_{k},\qquad\deg\delta_{1}<\ldots<\deg\delta_{p}

where
\delta_{k}\neq 0
are
G
-homogeneous derivations, and
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{p}
are LNDs.

In particular,
R
is rigid if and only if it admits no nonzero
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-homogeneous LND.

For the end of this section, let us state some specific properties of
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs.

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
\delta
be a
\mathbb{T}
-homogeneous LND of a trinomial algebra. Then

(i)

One has either
\delta(T_{ij})=0
for all
i,j
, or
\delta(S_{k})=0
for all
k
;

(ii)

Suppose that
\delta(T_{ij})=0
for all
i,j
; then there exists a
k
such that

\delta=\delta(S_{k}){\partial\over\partial S_{k}};

(iii)

For each monomial
T_{i}^{l_{i}}
there exists a variable
T_{ic_{i}}
such that
\delta(T_{ij})=0
whenever
j\neq c_{i}
.

The lemma is proved in
[
19
, Lemmas 6, 7, 8]
for the case when
\delta
is an
\mathbb{H}
-homogeneous LND. The proof proceeds by considering the gradings by certain subgroups of the character group of
\mathbb{H}
. One readily verifies that these subgroups are in fact contained in the character lattice of
{\mathbb{T}}
, so the same argument applies to the case of
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs.

2.
Main result on trinomial algebras of Type
1

Construction 2.1
.

Let
R
be a trinomial algebra of Type
1
. Suppose that there exists a tuple
C=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})
,
1\leq c_{i}\leq n_{i}
, such that
l_{ic_{i}}=1
for all
i
except maybe one
i_{0}
. We set

\begin{split}\delta_{C}(T_{ij})&=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{k\neq i}\frac{\partial T_{k}^{l_{k}}}{\partial T_{kc_{k}}},&\text{$j=c_{i}$,}\\
0,&\text{$j\neq c_{i}$;}\end{cases}\\
\delta_{C}(S_{k})&=0,\quad\text{all }k.\end{split}

There is a unique way to extend this assignment to a derivation of
\Bbbk[T_{ij},S_{k}]
by the Leibniz rule. It is straightforward to show that the derivations obtained in this way are locally nilpotent (being nilpotent at the variables), well-defined on the quotient algebra
R
(taking the relations between the
T_{ij}
to
0
), and
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous (taking the variables to
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous elements).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
R
be a trinomial algebra of Type
1
.

(i)

Any
\mathbb{T}
-homogeneous LND
\delta
of
R
is of the form:

\delta=h{\partial\over\partial S_{k}}\qquad\textit{or}\qquad\delta=h\delta_{C}

for some
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous
h
in
\ker\partial/\partial S_{k}
or respectively
\ker\delta_{C}
.

(ii)

Any
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous element
h\in\ker\partial/\partial S_{k}
is of the form:

h=F\prod T_{ij}^{\alpha_{ij}}\prod\limits_{p\neq k}S_{p}^{\gamma_{p}};

here
F
is a polynomial in
{T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{i}}},\,i=1,\ldots,r
,
d_{i}=\gcd(l_{i1},\ldots,l_{in_{i}})
, and
\alpha_{ij},\,\gamma_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(iii)

Any
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous element
h\in\ker\delta_{C}
is of the form:

h=\lambda\prod\limits_{j\neq c_{i}}T_{ij}^{\alpha_{ij}}\prod S_{p}^{\gamma_{p}};

here
\lambda\in\Bbbk
and
\alpha_{ij},\gamma_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
is given after one preliminary result, which will be useful also in the next section; note that in the following lemma, we do not require that
\delta
is
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
R
be a trinomial algebra of Type 1 and
\delta\neq 0
an LND of R possessing the following properties:

(a)

\delta(S_{k})=0
for all
k
;

(b)

there exists a tuple
C=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})
such that
\delta(T_{ij})=0
whenever
j\neq c_{i}
.

Then
\delta=h\delta_{C}
for a suitable
h\in\ker\delta_{C}
.

Proof.

For each pair
i,j
, the equation

T_{i}^{l_{i}}-T_{j}^{l_{j}}+a_{i}-a_{j}=0

holds. Applying
\delta
, we obtain:

(2.1)

\delta(T_{ic_{i}}){\partial T_{i}^{l_{i}}\over\partial T_{ic_{i}}}=\delta(T_{jc_{j}}){\partial T_{j}^{l_{j}}\over\partial T_{jc_{j}}}.

We claim that
\delta(T_{ic_{i}})\neq 0
for all
i
. In fact, suppose that
\delta(T_{ic_{i}})=0
for some
i
; then (
2.1
) implies that
\delta(T_{jc_{j}})=0
for all
j
. But this means
\delta=0
, a contradiction.

Now we claim that
l_{ic_{i}}=1
for all
i
except maybe one
i_{0}
. Assume the converse:
l_{ic_{i}}>1
and
l_{jc_{j}}>1
for two
i,j
. Then
T_{ic_{i}}
divides the left-hand side of (
2.1
), and
T_{jc_{j}}
divides the right-hand side. Since the variables
T_{pq}
are pairwise coprime (Lemma
1.6
), we have

T_{ic_{i}}\,|\,\delta(T_{j{c_{j}}})
and
T_{jc_{j}}\,|\,\delta(T_{ic_{i}}).

Then Lemma
1.8
(iv)
asserts that
\delta(T_{ic_{i}})=0
or
\delta(T_{jc_{j}})=0
, contradicting our previous argument.

We have verified that the tuple
C
satisfies the assumption of Construction
2.1
, i.e., the derivation
\delta_{C}
is well-defined. By (
2.1
) we also have, for any pair
i\neq j
:

{\partial T_{j}^{l_{j}}\over\partial T_{jc_{j}}}\mid\delta(T_{ic_{i}}).

Then coprimeness of the variables (Lemma
1.6
) implies that

\delta(T_{ic_{i}})=h_{i}\prod_{k\neq{i}}{\partial T_{k}^{l_{k}}\over\partial T_{kc_{k}}},\qquad i=1,\ldots,r.

Using (
2.1
) again, we obtain
h_{i}=h_{j}=:h
for all
i,j
. Thus
\delta=h\delta_{C}
. It follows that
h\in\ker\delta
by Lemma
1.8
(i)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Part
(i)
. Let
\delta
be a nonzero
\mathbb{T}
-homogeneous LND of
R
. Lemma
1.10
(i)
leaves two opportunities:

(a)\quad\delta(T_{ij})=0\mbox{ for all $i,j$}\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad(b)\quad\delta(S_{k})=0\mbox{ for all $k$.}

In case
(a)
, it follows from Lemma
1.10
(ii)
that
\delta
is a replica of some
\partial/\partial S_{k}
.

Consider case
(b)
. By Lemma
1.10
(iii)
, there exists a tuple
C
such that
T_{ij}\in\ker\delta
whenever
j\neq c_{i}
. Then Lemma
2.3
asserts that
\delta=h\delta_{C}
for a suitable
h\in\ker\delta_{C}
. Since both
\delta
and
\delta_{C}
are homogeneous, it follows that
h
is. This concludes the proof of part
(i)
.

Parts
(ii)
and
(iii)
.
Any monomial of weight zero with respect to
{\mathbb{T}}
is easily seen to be a product of some
T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{i}}
. It follows that each
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous element
h
has the form

h=F\prod T_{ij}^{\alpha_{ij}}\prod S_{p}^{\gamma_{p}},

where
F
is a polynomial in
T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{i}},\,i=1,\ldots,r
. The kernel of an LND is factorially closed (Lemma
1.8
). Thus we have
h\in\ker{\partial/\partial S_{k}}
if and only if
\gamma_{k}=0
. This proves part
(ii)
.

In the same way, since
\ker\delta_{C}
is factorially closed, we conclude that
h\in\ker\delta_{C}
if and only if all
\alpha_{ic_{i}}=0
and
\delta_{C}(F)=0
. Let us rewrite the latter condition on

more explicitly. According to Construction
2.1
, there exists an
i_{0}
such that
l_{ic_{i}}=1
for
i\neq i_{0}
. We may assume that
i_{0}=1
, i.e.,
T_{i}^{l_{i}/d_{i}}=T_{i}^{l_{i}}
whenever
i\neq 1
. By relations (
1.1
), each
T_{i}^{l_{i}}
is the sum of
T_{1}^{l_{1}}
and a constant. Hence
F
is a polynomial in
T_{1}^{l_{1}/d_{1}}
. Using the Leibniz rule, we get:

0=\delta_{C}(F)=\delta_{C}\left(T_{1}^{l_{1}/d_{1}}\,\right)F^{\prime},

F^{\prime}
denoting the derivative of
F
with respect to
T_{1}^{l_{1}/d_{1}}
. By construction,
\delta_{C}(T_{1}^{l_{1}/d_{1}})\neq 0
. Thus we have
\delta_{C}(F)=0
if and only if
F=\mathrm{const}
.
∎

Theorem
2.2
provides another proof of the rigidity criterion for trinomial algebras of Type
1
, stated and proved in a different way in
[

, Theorem 3]
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
R
be a trinomial algebras of Type
1
. Then
R
is not rigid if and only if one of the following two conditions holds:

(a)

d>0
, i.e., there exists
S_{k}\in R
;

(b)

One can find an

such that, for all

, there exist
c_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\}
with

Proof.

By Lemma

,

-homogeneous LND. The assertion follows.
∎

3.
Semirigid trinomial algebras of Type
1

Here we apply Theorem
2.2
to obtain a criterion for a trinomial algebra of Type
1
to be semirigid, following the idea of
[
11
, Section 6]
. Recall that the
Makar-Limanov invariant
of an algebra
R
, abbreviated
\ML(R)
, is the intersection of the kernels of all LNDs of
R
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a trinomial algebra of Type
1
. Suppose that the following conditions hold:

(a)

d=0
, i.e., one has no free variable
S_{k}\in R

(b)

There exists an
i_{0}
such that
T_{i_{0}}^{l_{i_{0}}}=T_{i_{0}1}^{l_{i_{0}1}}
and
l_{i_{0}1}>1
.

(c)

For each
i\neq i_{0}
, there exists a
c_{i}
such that
l_{ic_{i}}=1
and
l_{ij}>1
whenever
j\neq c_{i}
.

Then
\ML(R)=\Bbbk[\,T_{ij}\mid i\neq i_{0},\,j\neq c_{i}\,].

Proof.

Theorem
2.2
asserts that any
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LND of
R
is a replica of the
\delta_{C}
,
C=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{i_{0}-1},1,c_{i_{0}+1},\ldots,c_{r})
. Thus we have:

\ML(R)\subseteq\ker\delta_{C}=\Bbbk[\,T_{ij}\,|\,i\neq i_{0},\,j\neq c_{i}\,].

To get the reverse inclusion, fix a variable
T_{ij}\in\ker\delta_{C}
. We use Remark
1.4
, putting
b_{i}=c_{i}
. Then
\deg T_{ij}=\alpha e_{ij}
,
\alpha>0
, and
\deg\delta
has nonzero coefficient at
e_{ij}
, for any
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LND
\delta
. Let
\mathbb{Z}^{n+d-r}
be ordered in such a way that, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+d-r}
, the inequality
a\leq b
implies the same inequality on the coefficients at
e_{ij}
.

By Lemma
1.9
, any LND
\partial
admits the decomposition

\partial=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\partial_{k}
; here
\deg\partial_{1}<\ldots<\deg\partial_{p},

all the
\partial_{k}
are
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous, and
\partial_{1}
is locally nilpotent. Hence, for any
k
,
\deg\partial_{k}
has positive coefficient at
e_{ij}
, and the same holds for
\deg\partial_{k}(T_{ij})
. But
T_{ij}
is the only variable whose weight has positive coefficient at
e_{ij}
. It follows that
T_{ij}\mid\partial_{k}(T_{ij})
for all
k
, whence
T_{ij}\mid\partial(T_{ij})
. Now
\partial(T_{ij})=0
by Lemma
1.8
.

Thus we obtain
\ker\delta_{C}\subseteq\ker\partial
for any LND
\partial
. It follows that
\ML(R)=\ker\delta_{C}
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

A trinomial algebra
R
of Type 1 is semirigid if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(a)

R
is rigid;

(b)

d=1
and the trinomial algebra
R_{1}
obtained from
R
by excluding
S_{1}
from the system of generators, is rigid;

(c)

The assumption of Lemma
3.1
is fulfilled.

Proof.

Clearly, if
R
is semirigid, then
d\leq 1
; otherwise we would have non-equivalent LNDs
{\partial/\partial S_{1}}
and
{\partial/\partial S_{2}}
.

Case
d=1
. We have
R=R_{1}\otimes\Bbbk[S_{1}]
where
R_{1}
does not contain the free variables
S_{k}
. Suppose that
R
is semirigid; then all
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs are replicas of
{\partial/\partial S_{1}}
. Hence no tuple
C
satisfies the assumption of Construction
2.1
, so Corollary
2.4
implies that
R_{1}
is rigid. Thus we obtain
(b)
.
Conversely, if
R_{1}
is rigid, semirigidity of
R
follows from
[
10
, Theorem 2.24]
.

Case
d=0
. We clearly get the assertion when
R
is rigid. Now suppose that
R
is semirigid but not rigid. Then there exists a unique
\delta_{C}
, which immediately implies
(c)
. Conversely, suppose we have
(c)
. By Lemma
3.1
, any LND
\partial
satisfies
\ker\partial\supseteq\Bbbk[\,T_{ij}\,|\,i\neq i_{0},\,j\neq c_{i}\,]
. Hence
\partial=h\delta_{C}
by Lemma
2.3
, so
R
is semirigid.
∎

4.
Main result on trinomial algebras of Type 2

In this section, we state our main results on a trinomial algebra
R
of Type 2. The proofs are given in subsequent sections and consist of two steps. In Section
5
we obtain formulae for
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs when
\Spec R
is a surface. Then in Section
6
we reduce Theorems
4.1
and
4.4
to the surface case.

The following theorem describes the equivalence classes of
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs. Recall that two LNDs
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
are called
equivalent
if
\ker\delta_{1}=\ker\delta_{2}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
R
be a trinomial algebra of Type
2
.

(i)

Any
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LND of
R
either is a replica of
{\partial/\partial S_{k}}
, or corresponds to a tuple
C=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})
,
c_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\}
such that
\delta(T_{ij})=0
whenever
j\neq c_{i}
. In the latter case,
\delta(S_{k})=0
for all
k
and one of the following conditions holds:

(a)

There exist two integers
i_{1},i_{2}
such that
l_{ic_{i}}=1
for all
i\neq i_{1},i_{2}
.

(b)

There exist exactly three integers
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}
such that
l_{ic_{i}}=1
for
i\neq i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}
. Furthermore, for
i=i_{1},i_{2}
, all
l_{ij}
are even and
l_{ic_{i}}=2
.

(ii)

Suppose that a tuple
C
satisfies
(a)
in
(i)
. If there exists
k\in\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
such that
l_{kc_{k}}\mid l_{ij}
for
i=i_{1},i_{2}
and all
j
, then the tuple
C
corresponds to an infinite family of equivalence classes of
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs. Otherwise
C
corresponds to exactly two classes of
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs.

(iii)

Suppose that
C
satisfies
(b)
in
(i)
. If
l_{i_{3}c_{i_{3}}}=2
and all
l_{i_{3}j}
are even then
C
corresponds to an infinite family of
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs. Otherwise
C
corresponds to exactly two classes of
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs.

A straightforward corollary of Theorem
4.1
and
[
10
, Theorem 2.24]
is the following:

Corollary 4.2
.

A trinomial algebra
R
of Type
2
is semi-rigid if and only if either
R
is rigid, or there exists a unique variable
S_{1}
and the algebra
R_{1}
is rigid, where
R_{1}
is obtained from
R
by removing
S_{1}
from the set of generators.

The following construction provides explicit formulae for the
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs when
R
is given by a single equation. It is convenient to assume that all coefficients in the equation equal
1
; this can always be achieved by a linear change of variables. A square root of
-1
is denoted by
\mathbf{i}
.

Construction 4.3
.

Consider the trinomial variety given by
T_{0}^{l_{0}}+T_{1}^{l_{1}}+T_{2}^{l_{2}}=0
. Let
C=(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2})
with
c_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\}
.

Recall that
T_{i}^{l_{i}/\alpha}
denotes
\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}T_{ij}^{l_{ij}/\alpha}
whenever
\alpha\mid l_{ij}
for all
j
.

(a)

Suppose
l_{2c_{2}}=1
. Let

\delta(T_{0c_{0}})=\frac{\partial T_{2}^{l_{2}}}{\partial T_{2c_{2}}},\qquad\qquad\delta(T_{2c_{2}})=-\frac{\partial T_{0}^{l_{0}}}{\partial T_{0c_{0}}},

\delta(T_{ij})=0,\quad\mbox{ other $i,j$},\qquad\quad\delta(S_{k})=0,\quad\text{all }k.

(b)

Suppose
l_{2c_{2}}=1
and
l_{0c_{0}}\mid l_{ij}
for
i=0,1
,
j=1,\ldots,n_{i}
. Take any
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}
and let

\delta(T_{0c_{0}})=\frac{\partial T_{1}^{l_{1}/l_{0c_{0}}}}{\partial T_{1c_{1}}}{\partial T_{2}^{l_{2}}\over\partial T_{2c_{2}}},\qquad\qquad\delta(T_{1c_{1}})=\lambda\frac{\partial T_{0}^{l_{0}/l_{0c_{0}}}}{\partial T_{0c_{0}}}{\partial T_{2}^{l_{2}}\over\partial T_{2c_{2}}},

\delta(T_{2c_{2}})=-\left({\partial T_{0}^{l_{0}}\over\partial T_{01}}{\partial T_{1}^{l_{1}/l_{0c_{0}}}\over\partial T_{11}}+\lambda{\partial T_{0}^{l_{0}/l_{0c_{0}}}\over\partial T_{01}}{\partial T_{1}^{l_{1}}\over\partial T_{11}}\right),

\delta(T_{ij})=0,\quad\mbox{ other $i,j,$}\qquad\quad\delta(S_{k})=0,\quad\mbox{ all $k$.}

(c)

Suppose
l_{0c_{0}}=l_{1c_{1}}=2
,
l_{2c_{2}}\geq 2
, and all
l_{ij}
are even for
i=0,1
. Take
\mu\in\{\pm\mathbf{i}\}
and let

\delta(T_{0c_{0}})=\mu{\partial T_{1}^{l_{1}/2}\over\partial T_{1c_{1}}}{\partial T_{2}^{l_{2}}\over\partial T_{2c_{2}}},\qquad\qquad\delta(T_{1c_{1}})={\partial T_{0}^{l_{0}/2}\over\partial T_{0c_{0}}}{\partial T_{2}^{l_{2}}\over\partial T_{2c_{2}}},

\delta(T_{2c_{2}})=-\left(\mu{\partial T_{0}^{l_{0}}\over\partial T_{0c_{0}}}{\partial T_{1}^{l_{1}/2}\over\partial T_{1c_{1}}}+{\partial T_{0}^{l_{0}/2}\over\partial T_{0c_{0}}}{\partial T_{1}^{l_{1}}\over\partial T_{1c_{1}}}\right).

\delta(T_{ij})=0,\quad\mbox{ other $i,j$},\qquad\quad\delta(S_{k})=0,\quad\text{ all }k.

(d)

Suppose
l_{ic_{i}}=2
for any
i
and all
l_{ij}
are even. Take
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}
and let

\begin{split}\delta(T_{0c_{0}})&=\prod_{i\neq 0}{\partial T_{i}^{l_{i}/2}\over\partial T_{ic_{i}}}\left(2\lambda\,T_{1}^{l_{1}/2}+(1+\lambda^{2})\mathbf{i}\,T_{2}^{l_{2}/2}\right),\\
\delta(T_{1c_{1}})&=\prod_{i\neq 1}{\partial T_{i}^{l_{i}/2}\over\partial T_{ic_{i}}}\left(-2\lambda\,T_{0}^{l_{0}/2}+(1-\lambda^{2})\,T_{2}^{l_{2}/2}\right),\\
\delta(T_{2c_{2}})&=\prod_{i\neq 2}{\partial T_{i}^{l_{i}/2}\over\partial T_{ic_{i}}}\left(-(1+\lambda^{2})\mathbf{i}\,T_{0}^{l_{0}/2}-(1-\lambda^{2})\,T_{1}^{l_{1}/2}\right).\end{split}

\delta(T_{ij})=0,\quad\mbox{ other $i,j$},\qquad\quad\delta(S_{k})=0,\quad\text{ all }k.

The assignments just described give rise to derivations of the polynomial algebra by the Leibniz rule. It will follow from the proof of Theorem
4.4
that these formulae indeed give
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs of the trinomial algebra.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
R
be the trinomial algebra of Type
2
given by a single equation
T_{0}^{l_{0}}+T_{1}^{l_{1}}+T_{2}^{l_{2}}=0
. Any
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LND of

is a replica either of
{\partial/\partial S_{k}}
or of one of the derivations described in Construction
4.3
(after a permutation of the monomials
T_{i}^{l_{i}}
, if necessary).

Moreover, in the notation of Construction
4.3
, the algebra
\ker\delta
is generated by
T_{ij}
,
i=0,1,2
,
j\neq c_{i}
, together with the following elements:

\displaystyle T_{1c_{1}}\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{ in Case $(a)$},

\displaystyle\lambda\,{T_{0}^{l_{0}/l_{0c_{0}}}}-{T_{1}^{l_{1}/l_{0c_{0}}}}\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{ in Case $(b)$},

\displaystyle\mu\,{T_{0}^{l_{0}/2}}+{T_{1}^{l_{1}/2}}\qquad

\displaystyle\mbox{ in Case $(c)$},

\displaystyle(1-\lambda^{2})\,T_{0}^{l_{0}/2}-(1+\lambda^{2})\mathbf{i}\,{T_{1}^{l_{1}/2}}+2\lambda\,{T_{2}^{l_{2}/2}}\qquad

5.
Non-rigid trinomial surfaces of Type 2

According to the rigidity criterion
[
9
, Theorem 4]
, a non-rigid trinomial algebra of Type
2
with three generators
x,y,z
is given by one of the following relations:

(a)

x^{\alpha}+y^{\beta}+z=0
where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
;

(b)

x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{\gamma}=0
where
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
.

In fact,
any
non-rigid trinomial surface of Type 2 can be given by a single equation of the form
(a)
or
(b)
after eliminating linear terms. This section provides an explicit description of the
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs on such a surface.

5.1.
Case
x^{\alpha}+y^{\beta}+z=0

Let
X=\{x^{\alpha}+y^{\beta}+z=0\}
where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Suppose
\alpha=a\gcd(\alpha,\beta),\,\beta=b\gcd(\alpha,\beta)
. Then
X
is equivariantly isomorphic to the affine plane with coordinate functions
x,y
acted on by the one-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}
via
t\colon\,x\longmapsto t^{b}x,\,\,y\longmapsto t^{a}y
.

Definition 5.1
.

A polynomial
v\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is called a
variable
if there exist
n-1
polynomials
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-1}\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
that, along with
v
, generate
\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

[
10
, Corollary 4.6]

A derivation
\delta
of the polynomial algebra
\Bbbk[x,y]
is an LND if and only if there exists a variable
v
and a polynomial
h\in\Bbbk[v]
such that

\delta\,\colon x\longmapsto{\partial h\over\partial y},\qquad y\longmapsto-{\partial h\over\partial x}.

Proposition 5.3
.

Consider
\mathbb{A}^{2}
with coordinate functions
x,y
acted on by the one-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}
via
t\colon\,x\longmapsto t^{b}x,\,\,y\longmapsto t^{a}y
where
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Let
\delta
be a
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LND of
\Bbbk[x,y]
. Then one of the following holds:

(i)

\delta
is a replica of
{\partial/\partial x}
;

(ii)

\delta
is a replica of
{\partial/\partial y}
;

(iii)

a=1
and
\delta
is a replica of

x\longmapsto by^{b-1},\qquad y\longmapsto\lambda,\qquad\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}

where
\ker\delta=\Bbbk[\lambda x-y^{b}]
;

(iv)

b=1
and
\delta
is a replica of

x\longmapsto\lambda,\qquad y\longmapsto ax^{a-1},\qquad\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}

where
\ker\delta=\Bbbk[x^{a}-\lambda y]
.

Proof.

We make use of Lemma
5.2
. The elements

\delta(x)={{dh}\over{dv}}{\partial v\over\partial y}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\delta(y)=-{dh\over{dv}}{\partial v\over\partial x}

are
{\mathbb{T}}
-semi-invariant. Since the character group of
{\mathbb{T}}
is torsion-free,
{\partial v/\partial y}
and
{\partial v/\partial x}
are also
{\mathbb{T}}
-semi-invariant; see, for example
[
2
, Lemma 2.3 (iii)]
. It follows in turn that
v
is
{\mathbb{T}}
-semi-invariant; hence
v=x^{p}y^{q}\sum\limits_{i}\lambda_{i}\left(x^{a}/y^{b}\right)^{i}
where
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\,\lambda_{i}\in\Bbbk
. Furthermore,
h
is a monomial in
v
. As
v
is irreducible (being a variable), the possibilities are:

v=\lambda_{1}x
,
v=\lambda_{2}y
or
v=\lambda_{1}x^{a}+\lambda_{2}y^{b}
where
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
.
The first two cases lead directly to statements
(i)
and
(ii)
.

Consider the case
v=\lambda_{1}x^{a}+\lambda_{2}y^{b}
where
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
. An element
v
of this form can be included in a transcendence basis of
\Bbbk[x,y]
if and only if
a=1
or
b=1
. Suppose for instance that
a=1
. We also may assume that
v=\lambda x-y^{b}
and
h
is proportional (over
\Bbbk
) to
v^{q}
,
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Thus
\delta
is proportional over
\Bbbk
to the derivation

x\longmapsto by^{b-1}v^{q-1}
,
y\longmapsto\lambda v^{q-1},

a replica of the one in
(iii)
. Then
\ker\delta=\Bbbk[v]
provided by Lemma
1.8

(i)
.

Case
b=1
leads to statement
(iv)
in the same manner.
∎

5.2.
Case
x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{\gamma}=0
,
\gamma>2

Let the surface given by this equation be denoted by
X
. For
\gamma
odd, the one-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}
acts by the rule
t\colon\,x\longmapsto t^{\gamma}x,

y\longmapsto t^{\gamma}y,

z\longmapsto t^{2}z
where
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
. If
\gamma
is even, the action is given by
t\colon\,x\longmapsto t^{\gamma/2}x,

y\longmapsto t^{\gamma/2}y,

z\longmapsto tz
.

Consider the toric variety
\{uv=z^{\gamma}\}
where the torus
(\Bbbk^{\times})^{2}
acts via
(t_{1},\,t_{2})\colon\,u\longmapsto t_{1}^{\gamma}t_{2}^{-1}u
,
v\longmapsto t_{2}v
,
z\longmapsto t_{1}z
. Under the linear change of variables
u=\mathbf{i}x-y,\,v=\mathbf{i}x+y
, this variety is isomorphic to
X
. Moreover, the actions of
{\mathbb{T}}
and
(\Bbbk^{\times})^{2}
are compatible via the embedding
\varphi\colon\,{\mathbb{T}}\hookrightarrow(\Bbbk^{\times})^{2}
, where
\varphi(t)=(t^{2},\,t^{\gamma})
for
\gamma
odd and
\varphi(t)=(t,\,t^{\gamma/2})
for
\gamma
even. Demazure roots, introduced in
[
8
]

Definition 5.5

Let
N,M
be dual lattices and
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
a polyhedral cone. A

associated to the ray

\sigma

\langle n_{\rho},\,e\rangle=-1
and
\langle n_{\rho^{\prime}},\,e\rangle\geq 0

To describe the homogeneous LNDs on

Proposition 5.6
.

Any
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LND on the variety

X=\{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{\gamma}=0\},\quad\mbox{ where $\gamma>2$},

is a replica of the derivation

\delta_{0}\,\colon\,x\longmapsto\mathbf{i}\gamma z^{\gamma-1},\quad y\longmapsto\gamma z^{\gamma-1},\quad z\longmapsto-2(\mathbf{i}x+y),\qquad\ker\delta_{0}=\Bbbk[\mathbf{i}x+y],

or

\delta_{\infty}\,\colon\,x\longmapsto-\mathbf{i}\gamma z^{\gamma-1},\quad y\longmapsto\gamma z^{\gamma-1},\quad z\longmapsto 2(\mathbf{i}x-y),\qquad\ker\delta_{\infty}=\Bbbk[\mathbf{i}x-y].

Proof.

Let us first show that all
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs on
X
are
(\Bbbk^{\times})^{2}
-homogeneous. The action
(\Bbbk^{\times})^{2}\curvearrowright X
corresponds to the cones

\sigma_{X}^{\vee}=\cone((1,0),\,(1,\gamma))\subset M,\qquad\sigma_{X}=\cone((0,1),\,(\gamma,-1))\subset N.

The embedding
{\mathbb{T}}\subset(\Bbbk^{\times})^{2}
induces an embedding of lattices of one-parameter subgroups
\Hom(\Bbbk^{\times},{\mathbb{T}})\subset\Hom(\Bbbk^{\times},(\Bbbk^{\times})^{2})=N
, whose image we denote by
\Gamma_{T}
. It is easy to see that the line
\Gamma_{T}
is generated by the vector
(2,\gamma)
when
\gamma
is odd, and by
(1,\gamma/2)
when
\gamma
is even. In both cases we are in the situation of item 3.2 of Proposition 6 in
[
16
]
. Thus, all
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs are
(\Bbbk^{\times})^{2}
-homogeneous.

Recall that the algebra
\Bbbk[X]
is generated by the characters
\chi^{m}\in\sigma_{X}^{\vee}\cap M
. In our case, one can take as generators

\chi^{(\gamma,-1)}=u=\mathbf{i}x-y,\quad\chi^{(0,1)}=v=\mathbf{i}x+y,\quad\chi^{(1,0)}=z.

It follows from
[
17
, Theorem 2.7]
that each
(\Bbbk^{\times})^{2}
-homogeneous LND is proportional over
\Bbbk
to a derivation
\partial_{\rho,e}\,\colon\,\chi^{m}\longmapsto\langle m,\,\rho\rangle\,\chi^{m+e}
for some ray
\rho
of the cone
\sigma_{X}
and some Demazure root
e\in S_{\rho}
. Let the ray
\rho_{1}
be generated by the vector
(0,1)
and
\rho_{2}
by
(\gamma,-1)
. A straightforward computation shows that the Demazure roots are of the form

S_{\rho_{1}}=\{\,(-1,p),\quad p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\,\}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad S_{\rho_{2}}=\{\,(\gamma p-1,-p),\quad p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\,\}.

Consequently,

\partial_{\rho_{1},\,e}\,\colon\,u\longmapsto\gamma z^{\gamma-1}v^{p-1},\quad v\longmapsto 0,\quad z\longmapsto v^{p},\quad e=(-1,p),\quad p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

and

\partial_{\rho_{2},\,e}\,\colon\,u\longmapsto 0,\quad v\longmapsto\gamma z^{\gamma-1}u^{p-1},\quad z\longmapsto u^{p},\quad e=(\gamma p-1,-p),\quad p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

Passing to the coordinates
x,y,z
, we obtain

\partial_{\rho_{1},e}=-2(\mathbf{i}x+y)^{p-1}\delta_{0}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\partial_{\rho_{2},e}=2(\mathbf{i}x-y)^{p-1}\delta_{\infty},

where the
\delta_{i}
are described in the statement.

By Lemma
1.8

(i)
, any
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous element
h\in\ker\delta_{i}
is a monomial in
\mathbf{i}x+y
or
\mathbf{i}x-y
, respectively. Since
\ker\delta_{i}
is generated as a subalgebra by homogeneous elements, we obtain
\ker\delta_{0}=\Bbbk[\mathbf{i}x+y]
and
\ker\delta_{\infty}=\Bbbk[\mathbf{i}x-y]
.
∎

Notation 5.8

Remark 5.10

Let

Definition 5.11

Proposition 5.12
.

Any
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LND on the variety

is a replica of
\delta_{\infty}
or of
\delta_{\lambda}
,

, where

\delta_{\infty}\,\colon\,x\longmapsto-\mathbf{i}z,\quad y\longmapsto z,\quad z\longmapsto\mathbf{i}x-y,\qquad\ker\delta_{\infty}=\Bbbk[\mathbf{i}x-y],

and

\delta_{\lambda}\,\colon\,x\longmapsto 2\lambda y+\mathbf{i}(1+\lambda^{2})z,\qquad y\longmapsto-2\lambda x+(1-\lambda^{2})z,

Proof.

We use the representation
\Bbbk[X]\simeq A[\mathbb{P}^{1},\,\mathfrak{D}]
, where
\mathfrak{D}=\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 2}\{\infty\}
, from Lemma
5.9
and Remark
5.10
. By
[
3
, Lemma 2]
, there are no
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs of vertical type on
\Bbbk[X]
. This fact also follows from
[
5
, Theorem 1.7]
, since the only Demazure root for
\sigma
is

, and the sheaf
\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{D}(-1))
has no nonzero global sections on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

The
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LNDs of horizontal type on an algebra of the form
A[\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathfrak{D}]
correspond to coherent pairs
(\tilde{\mathfrak{D}},\,e)
, where
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
is a coloring of the divisor
\mathfrak{D}
; see
[
5
, Definitions 1.8, 1.9 and Theorem 1.10]
. The explicit form of the LND is given by formula (2) in
[
5
]
. In our case, any coloring of the divisor
\mathfrak{D}
has the form
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}=\{\mathfrak{D};\,v_{p},\,p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{p_{\infty}\}\}
for some point
p_{\infty}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. From the form of the divisor
\mathfrak{D}
, it follows that
v_{p}=0
for
p\neq\infty
, and
v_{\infty}=2
.

Case
p_{\infty}=\infty
.
The divisor
\mathfrak{D}
restricts trivially to
\mathbb{A}^{1}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{p_{\infty}\}
, i.e., all coefficients of
\mathfrak{D}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{p_{\infty}\}}
equal
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
. Thus, we have the cone
\tilde{\omega}=\cone((0,1),\,(2,-1))
with distinguished ray
\tilde{\rho}
spanned by the vector
(0,1)
. The set of Demazure roots has the form

S_{\tilde{\rho}}=\{(e,-1)\mid e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

Since all coefficients of the divisor
\mathfrak{D}
have exactly one vertex, which moreover has integer nonnegative coordinate, every pair
(\tilde{\mathfrak{D}},e),\,\,e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},
is coherent. By formula (2) in
[
5
]
, the corresponding LNDs
\partial_{e}
are of the form
\partial_{e}(\chi^{m}\xi^{r})=r\chi^{m+e}\xi^{r-1}
. Consequently,

\begin{split}\partial_{e}(u)&=\partial_{e}(\chi)=0,\\
\partial_{e}(v)&=\partial_{e}(\chi\xi^{2})=2\chi^{1+e}\xi=2zu^{e},\\
\partial_{e}(z)&=\partial_{e}(\chi\xi)=\chi^{1+e}=u^{1+e}.\end{split}

Thus,
\partial_{e}=u^{e}\delta_{\infty}
, where
\delta_{\infty}\,\colon\,u\longmapsto 0,

v\longmapsto 2z,

z\longmapsto u.
By Lemma
1.8
(i)
,
\ker\delta_{\infty}=\Bbbk[u]
. Passing to the coordinates
x,y,z
, we obtain

\delta_{\infty}\,\colon\,x\longmapsto-\mathbf{i}z,\quad y\longmapsto z,\quad z\longmapsto\mathbf{i}x-y,\qquad\ker\delta_{\infty}=\Bbbk[\mathbf{i}x-y].

Case
p_{\infty}\neq\infty
.
Then
\mathfrak{D}|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{p_{\infty}\}}=\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 2}\{\infty\}
,
\tilde{\omega}=\cone((2,1),\,(0,-1))
and the distinguished ray
\tilde{\rho}
is spanned by the vector
(2,1)
. The set of Demazure roots has the form

S_{\tilde{\rho}}=\{(e,-1-2e)\mid e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

Let
\lambda\in\Bbbk
denote the
\xi
-coordinate of the point
p_{\infty}
. We need to choose a coordinate
\eta
such that
p_{\infty}
is given by the equality
\eta=\infty
. Set
\eta=(\xi-\lambda)^{-1}
, i.e.,
\xi=\eta^{-1}+\lambda
, or
\eta^{-1}=\xi-\lambda
. Then formula (2) of
[
5
]
gives

\partial_{e}(\chi^{m}\eta^{r})=(2m+r)\chi^{m+e}\eta^{r-1-2e}.

Let us express the variables
u,v,z
in terms of
\chi,\eta
. We have

u=\chi,\qquad v=\chi\xi^{2}=\chi\eta^{-2}+2\lambda\chi\eta^{-1}+\lambda^{2}\chi,\qquad z=\chi\xi=\chi\eta^{-1}+\lambda\chi.

Substituting into the formula for
\partial_{e}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{e}(u)&=2(z-\lambda u)(v-2\lambda z+\lambda^{2}u)^{e},\\
\partial_{e}(v)&=2\lambda(v-\lambda z)(v-2\lambda z+\lambda^{2}u)^{e},\\
\partial_{e}(z)&=(v-\lambda^{2}u)(v-2\lambda z+\lambda^{2}u)^{e}.\end{split}

Thus,
\partial_{e}
is a replica of the derivation

\delta\,\colon\,u\longmapsto 2(z-\lambda u),\quad v\longmapsto 2\lambda(v-\lambda z),\qquad z\longmapsto v-\lambda^{2}u

and
\ker\delta=\Bbbk[v-2\lambda z+\lambda^{2}u]
by Lemma
1.8
(i)

\delta\colon\,x\longmapsto-2\mathbf{i}\lambda y-\mathbf{i}(1-\lambda^{2})z,\qquad y\longmapsto 2\mathbf{i}\lambda x-(1+\lambda^{2})z,

z\longmapsto\mathbf{i}(1-\lambda^{2})x+(1+\lambda^{2})y,

\ker\delta=\Bbbk[\mathbf{i}(1+\lambda^{2})x+(1-\lambda^{2})y-2\lambda z].

It is easy to see that
\delta=-\delta_{\mathbf{i}\lambda}
.
∎

\delta(T_{21}){\partial T_{2}^{l_{2}}\over\partial T_{21}}=-h\left({\partial T_{0}^{l_{0}}\over\partial T_{01}}{\partial T_{1}^{l_{1}/\alpha}\over\partial T_{11}}+\lambda{\partial T_{0}^{l_{0}/\alpha}\over\partial T_{01}}{\partial T_{1}^{l_{1}}\over\partial T_{11}}\right).

Note that the monomial on the left-hand side and the expression in parentheses on the right-hand side are not, in general, coprime in

Lemma 6.1
.

Let a monomial
P
and polynomials
Q,F
be such that
P\mid T_{2}^{l_{2}}
,

contains no monomials divisible by
T_{2}^{l_{2}}/P
, and

does not depend on any of the variables
T_{2j}
. Suppose that
FP=hQ
in

for some

. Then
P\mid h
in
R
.

Proof.

Choose a polynomial
H
representing
h\in R
in such a way that it contains no monomials divisible by
T_{2}^{l_{2}}
. Then

FP-HQ\in I

and each term in
FP-HQ
contains no monomials divisible by
T_{2}^{l_{2}}
. Consequently,

FP=HQ.
Since the polynomials
P
and
Q
are coprime, we obtain
P\mid H
.
∎

It follows from relations (

\delta(T_{21})
can be expressed as a polynomial not depending on
T_{21}
.
Applying Lemma
6.1
to relation (

\delta

from Construction

.
Moreover, the subalgebra

\lambda{T_{0}^{l_{0}/\alpha}}-{T_{1}^{l_{1}/\alpha}}

Case (B).

We also have

\delta(T_{01}){T_{02}^{l_{02}/2}\dots T_{0n_{0}}^{l_{0n_{0}}/2}}=\mathbf{i}\delta(T_{11}){T_{12}^{l_{12}/2}\dots T_{1n_{1}}^{l_{1n_{1}}/2}},

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
\delta
be a nonzero
{\mathbb{T}}
-homogeneous LND on

. By Lemma

, either

{\partial/\partial S_{k}}
, or there exists a tuple

satisfying the stated conditions. Let

and
