Typical entanglement entropy with charge conservation
Eugenio Bianchi, a ​ b {}^{ab}\; Pietro Donà c \,{}^{c}\; and Erick Muiño a ​ b \,{}^{ab}

Abstract
We consider a many-body Hilbert space with a fixed global charge and show that the typical entanglement entropy of a subsystem, at the leading and subleading order in the thermodynamic limit, can be expressed in terms of a single quantity which represents the local thermal entropy at fixed charge density. We find a general formula which applies both to abelian U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) symmetry and non-abelian S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) symmetry, including the case of a local Hilbert space which transforms under a general reducible representation of the symmetry group. We illustrate the general formula with model systems and discuss the relevance of the results as a probe of quantum chaos for physical Hamiltonians.

2
Many-body Hilbert space and global conserved charge

We introduce the general structure of many-body systems and derive the relation between the local entropy
\eta(s)
and the asymptotic dimension of the Hilbert space at a fixed global charge.

2.1
k-level local system and global conserved charge

We consider a many-body system consisting of
N
bodies, each with a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{k}
with
k
levels, i.e.,
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}=k
. For
k=2
, each body is a
qubit
and, for larger
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a
qukit
. The Hilbert space of the system is the tensor product of local Hilbert spaces:

\mathcal{H}_{N}=\;\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\,}_{N}\,.

(2)

We assume that each body carries a unitary (possibly reducible)
representation of a group
G
. We focus here on the abelian group

G=U(1)
and the non-abelian group
G=SU(2)
. The generalization
to any semisimple Lie group will be presented in a forthcoming
paper
[
49
]
.

The Hilbert space of the many-body system decomposes into a direct sum over sectors of fixed charge
q
, i.e.,

\mathcal{H}_{N}=\bigoplus_{q}\big(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q)}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(q)}\big)\,.

(3)

For
U(1)
, the charge is the magnetic number,
q\equiv m\in\mathbb{Z}
, and each irreducible representation is one-dimensional,
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q)}=1
.
For
SU(2)
, the charge is the spin,
q\equiv j=0,\frac{1}{2},1,\frac{3}{2},\ldots
, and the dimension of the irreducible representation of spin
j
is
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(j)}=2j+1
.

We denote an orthonormal basis of the irreducible representation space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q)}
by
|q,\mu\rangle
, where
\mu
takes
\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q)}
distinct values and splits the degeneracy of the states with charge
q
. Specifically, for
U(1)
, there is no need for an additional quantum number; conventionally,
\mu
is taken equal to the charge value, i.e.,
\mu\equiv m
. For
SU(2)
, this extra quantum number can be taken as the eigenvalue of the
z
-component of the spin, i.e., the magnetic number
\mu\equiv m=-j,\ldots,+j
.

In the decomposition (
3
), the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(q)}
carries the internal degrees of freedom of the many-body system which are invariant under
G
-transformations at fixed charge
q
. This Hilbert space is defined as the intertwiner space for representations of the group
G
,
[
50
,
51
]
:

\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(q)}=\mathrm{Inv}_{G}\big(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q)*}\otimes\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\,}_{N}\big)\,,

(4)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q)*}
is the dual Hilbert space of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q)}
, with dual basis
\langle q,\mu|
. We refer to
[
5
]
for a discussion of their role in quantum geometry
[
52
,
53
]
and loop quantum gravity
[
54
,
55
]
.

The local one-body Hilbert space can also be decomposed as a direct sum over the local charge
q_{\mathrm{loc}}
:

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}=\bigoplus_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}\big(\mathcal{H}^{(q_{\mathrm{loc}})}_{\mathrm{irrep}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{(q_{\mathrm{loc}})}\big)\,,

(5)

and its dimension is
k=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}=\sum_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\dim\mathcal{H}^{(q_{\mathrm{loc}})}_{\mathrm{irrep}})\,a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}
, where
a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{(q_{\mathrm{loc}})}
is the multiplicity of the irreducible representation
q_{\mathrm{loc}}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
An orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
has the form
|q_{\mathrm{loc}},\mu_{\mathrm{loc}},\nu_{\mathrm{loc}}\rangle=|q_{\mathrm{loc}},\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}\rangle|q_{\mathrm{loc}},\nu_{\mathrm{loc}}\rangle
, with
\nu_{\mathrm{loc}}=1,\ldots,a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}
.
Specifically, for
U(1)
, the irreducible representations are one-dimensional, and the dimension of the local Hilbert space is simply
k=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}=\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}
where
m_{\mathrm{loc}}
is the
U(1)
charge. For
SU(2)
, the irreducible representations have dimension
2j+1
, and the dimension of the local Hilbert space is
k=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}=\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}(2j_{\mathrm{loc}}+1)\,a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}
.

The dimension of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(q)}
defined in (
4
), which carries the internal degrees of freedom of the many-body system at fixed charge, can be computed as an integral over the group of the characters of the representations, as shown for instance in App. A of
[
5
]
. Specifically, we have

D_{q}\equiv\dim\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(q)}=\;\int_{G}\overline{\chi^{(q)}(g)}\;\big(\chi_{\mathrm{loc}}(g)\big)^{N}\,d\mu(g)\,,

(6)

where we have defined the character
[
50
,
51
]
of the local Hilbert space representation as the weighted sum over irreducible representations of the group,

\chi_{\mathrm{loc}}(g)=\sum_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\chi^{(q_{\mathrm{loc}})}(g)\,.

(7)

The integral is taken with respect to the normalized Haar measure
d\mu(g)
on the group. The explicit form of the characters depends on the group and the parametrization used for the group elements. The formula (
6
) is exact and can be used to compute the dimension
D_{q}
as shown in
[
5
]
. Our goal is to study the thermodynamic limit; in the following we focus only on the asymptotic properties of these dimensions.

2.2
Hilbert space dimension and the local entropy at fixed charge density

We investigate the many-body limit,
N\to\infty
, in the regime where the total charge
q
is scaled extensively, i.e.,
q=Ns
with a fixed charge density
s
. This regime is commonly called the
thermodynamic limit
.

The asymptotic behavior of the dimensions
D_{q}
can be computed using Laplace’s method to evaluate the integral (
6
). Rather than providing the technical details of the asymptotic analysis, which depend on the specific group and decomposition of the local Hilbert space, we present here only the final result for the asymptotic expansion of
D_{q}
as a function of
s
:

D(s)\equiv D_{Ns}=\;\alpha_{0}(s)\Big(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}(s)}{N}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})\Big)\,\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi\,N}}\;\mathrm{e}^{N\eta(s)}\,.

(8)

The function
\eta(s)
has a general form which depends on the group and on the decomposition of the local Hilbert space into irreducible representations of charge
q_{\mathrm{loc}}
, as we discuss below. The function
\alpha_{0}(s)
turns out to be completely determined by the group and by
\eta(s)
; specifically
\alpha_{0}(s)=1
for
U(1)
(as shown in Sec.
5
), and
\alpha_{0}(s)=1-\mathrm{e}^{\eta^{\prime}(s)}
for
SU(2)
(as shown in Sec.
6
). Finally, as we will show in Sec.
4
, the asymptotic expressions for the typical entanglement entropy at order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
require that we take into account the term
\alpha_{1}(s)/N
in (
8
), but the dependence on the specific value of
\alpha_{1}(s)
cancels out in the final result.

The function
\eta(s)
has a fascinating interpretation in terms of statistical mechanics and relative entropy.
Consider a probability distribution
p_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)
over the local charge
q_{\mathrm{loc}}
with magnetic number
\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}
. This distribution is required to maximize the Shannon entropy at fixed average magnetic number, i.e.,

\sum_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}\;p_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*})\;=\;s\,.

(9)

As usual
[
56
]
, the solution takes the Gibbs form

p_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)=\frac{a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}}{\sum_{q^{\prime}_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{q^{\prime}_{\mathrm{loc}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\mu^{\prime}_{\mathrm{loc}}}}\,,

(10)

where the parameter
\beta
plays the role of inverse
temperature
, which is expressed as a function of the charge density
s
by solving the condition (
9
), i.e.,
\beta_{*}=\beta_{*}(s)
. The probability distribution (
10
) is fully determined by the decomposition of the local Hilbert space (
5
) into irreducible representations of charge
q_{\mathrm{loc}}
, and it does not depend on the many-body limit or the scaling of the total charge.

We express the function
\eta(s)
in (
8
) as:

\boxed{\quad\eta(s)\,=\,\log(k)\,-\sum_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}p_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*}(s))\,\log\Big(\frac{p_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*}(s))}{p_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(0)}\Big)\quad}

(11)

This expression shows that
\eta(s)
is the
local entropy
. The first term
\log(k)
is the logarithm of the dimension of the local Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. The second term is minus the relative entropy, or Kullback-Leibler divergence
\mathcal{D}(p_{s}\|p_{\circledast})
, of the probability distribution
p_{s}=p_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*}(s))
at finite temperature with respect to the same distribution at infinite temperature
p_{\circledast}=p_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(0)=a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}/k
, where
k=\sum_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}
. We note that, while the infinite temperature distribution is equiprobable over charge sectors,
p_{\circledast}
is not simply
1/k
because in general the multiplicities
a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}
need not equal 1. An alternative description in terms of equiprobable distinguishable states
|q_{\mathrm{loc}},\mu_{\mathrm{loc}},\nu_{\mathrm{loc}}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is possible, as done for instance in Ch. 3 of
[
56
]
where the Shannon entropy is discussed instead. By introducing a probability distribution
\tilde{p}_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}\nu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)
which is equiprobable over the auxiliary label
\nu_{\mathrm{loc}}=1,\ldots,a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}
such that
p_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)=\sum_{\nu_{\mathrm{loc}}}\tilde{p}_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}\nu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)
, one finds that
\eta(s)
is simply the Shannon entropy of
\tilde{p}_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}\nu_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)
. Therefore, the function
\eta(s)
given by (
11
) and appearing in the asymptotic expression (
8
) equals the entropy of the local thermal state in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
at fixed average charge density
s
.

At leading order, the asymptotic formula (
8
) can be written as

\dim\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(Ns)}\,\approx\,k^{N}\,\mathrm{e}^{-N\mathcal{D}(p_{s}\|p_{\circledast})}\,.

(12)

The constraint of fixed charge
q=Ns
reduces the dimension
k^{N}
of the many-body Hilbert space by a factor which scales exponentially in
N
with rate given by the one-body, or local, relative entropy
\mathcal{D}(p_{s}\|p_{\circledast})=\sum\,p_{s}\log(p_{s}/p_{\circledast})
.

The function
\eta(s)
and the density
s
have the thermodynamic properties of local entropy and
energy density
. Specifically, the first derivative of (
11
) is the inverse
temperature

\beta_{*}(s)=\eta^{\prime}(s)\,,

(13)

and the second derivative is related to the
local heat capacity

c_{*}(s)\equiv\left.\frac{\partial s}{\partial\beta^{-1}}\right|_{\beta_{*}}=-\left.\beta^{2}\frac{\partial s}{\partial\beta}\right|_{\beta_{*}}=-\frac{\beta_{*}(s)^{2}}{\beta^{\prime}_{*}(s)}=-\frac{\eta^{\prime}(s)^{2}}{\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}\,.

(14)

The temperature
\beta_{*}^{-1}
can be either positive or negative. If the temperature is positive, the dimension
D(s)
of the Hilbert space increases when we increase the local charge
s
. Conversely, it decreases for negative temperature. However, the local heat capacity
c_{*}
is always positive, which means that an increase in the charge density corresponds to an increase in temperature. The proof is immediate: from (
9
),
-\partial s/\partial\beta
equals the variance of the local magnetic number
\mu_{\mathrm{loc}}
, which is a positive quantity by definition. The positivity of
c_{*}(s)
implies that
\eta(s)
is a concave function, i.e.,

\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)<0\,.

(15)

We will provide concrete examples of
\eta(s)
for
U(1)
and
SU(2)
charges in Sec.
5
and
6
.

3
Subsystem decomposition and subsystem charge distribution

We discuss the decomposition into subsystems of the many-body Hilbert space at fixed charge, and determine the asymptotic dimensions of the subsystems.

3.1
Subsystem decomposition

The Hilbert space of the many-body system (
2
) decomposes as a tensor product over local subsystems. Observables that probe only a subset of
N_{A}
bodies define a subsystem
A
with Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and a decomposition of the Hilbert space as a tensor product,

\mathcal{H}_{N}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\,,

(16)

where

\mathcal{H}_{A}=\;\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\,}_{N_{A}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}_{B}=\;\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\,}_{N_{B}}\,,

(17)

where the complement of
A
consists of
N_{B}=N-N_{A}
bodies.

We now consider the case of fixed charge
q
corresponding to the decomposition (
3
) of the Hilbert space of the system. If we restrict to observables in the subsystem
A
which are also
invariant
under
G
-transformations, i.e.,
G
-local observables
[
5
]
, then the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(q)}
of the internal degrees of freedom of the system at fixed global charge
q
decomposes as

\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(q)}=\bigoplus_{q_{A}}\big(\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(q_{A})}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}^{(q,q_{A})}\big)\,,

(18)

where
q_{A}
is the charge of the subsystem
A
. Concretely, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(q_{A})}=

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{Inv}_{G}\big(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q_{A})*}\otimes\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\,}_{N_{A}}\big)\,,

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{B}^{(q,q_{A})}=

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{Inv}_{G}\big(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q)*}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(q_{A})}\otimes\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}\,}_{N_{B}}\big)\,.

(20)

While the Hilbert space of the subsystem
A
depends only on its charge
q_{A}
, the Hilbert space of the complement,
\mathcal{H}_{B}^{(q,q_{A})}
, depends on both the system and subsystem charges
(q,q_{A})
.

We introduce shorthand notation for the dimensions of these Hilbert spaces:

D_{q}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(q)}\,,\quad d_{q_{A}}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(q_{A})}\,,\quad b_{q,q_{A}}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{B}^{(q,q_{A})}\,,

(21)

satisfying, by construction, the relation
D_{q}=\sum_{q_{A}}d_{q_{A}}b_{q,q_{A}}
. To obtain the exact expression for the dimensions, we can write them as the integral over the group of the characters of the representations involved, as we did in (
6
). Specifically, we have

\displaystyle d_{q_{A}}

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(q_{A})}=\int_{G}\overline{\chi^{(q_{A})}(g)}\big(\chi_{\mathrm{loc}}(g)\big)^{N_{A}}\,d\mu(g)\,,

(22)

\displaystyle b_{q,q_{A}}

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{H}_{B}^{(q,q_{A})}=\int_{G}\overline{\chi^{(q)}(g)}\,\chi^{(q_{A})}(g)\big(\chi_{\mathrm{loc}}(g)\big)^{N_{B}}\,d\mu(g)\,,

(23)

where
\chi_{\mathrm{loc}}(g)
is defined in (
7
).

We investigate the
thermodynamic limit

N\to\infty
, where the subsystem size
N_{A}
, the total charge
q
, and the subsystem charge
q_{A}
scale extensively, i.e., we introduce the densities:

f=\frac{N_{A}}{N}\,,\qquad s=\frac{q}{N}\,,\qquad t=\frac{q_{A}}{N_{A}}\,,

(24)

that stay fixed in the limit
N\to\infty
. To keep the discussion as general as possible, instead of deriving explicit formulas (as done for specific examples in Sec.
5
and
6
), we write the asymptotic behavior of the dimensions in the implicit form

\displaystyle d(t)

\displaystyle\equiv d_{Ntf}=\;\alpha_{0}(t)\Big(1+\tfrac{\alpha_{1}(t)}{fN}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})\Big)\,\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(t)}{2\pi\,fN\,}}\,\;\mathrm{e}^{fN\eta(t)}\,,

(25)

\displaystyle b(s,t)

\displaystyle\equiv b_{Ns,Ntf}=\Big(1+\tfrac{\gamma_{1}(\frac{s-ft}{1-f})-(1-f)^{2}\,\alpha_{1}(\frac{s-ft}{1-f})}{(1-f)fN}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})\Big)\,\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(\frac{s-ft}{1-f})}{2\pi\,(1-f)N\,}}\,\;\mathrm{e}^{(1-f)N\eta(\frac{s-ft}{1-f})}\,.

The functions
\eta(s)
,
\alpha_{0}(s)
, and
\alpha_{1}(s)
are defined in (
8
). The formula for
d(t)
has the same form as the formula for
D(s)
. The formula for
b(s,t)
is slightly different, because the integrand in (
22
) contains the product of two characters rather than a single one. In both cases,
U(1)
and
SU(2)
, the leading contribution is a character integral of the same type as
(
6
), evaluated for the complement charge

\frac{q-q_{A}}{N_{B}}=\frac{s-ft}{1-f}
, which explains the dependence on the same function
\eta
and its argument in
b(s,t)
.

The function
\gamma_{1}(s)
is determined by the normalization condition
D_{q}=\sum_{q_{A}}d_{q_{A}}b_{q,q_{A}}
for the asymptotic expression. Sums over the subsystem charge
q_{A}
can be evaluated asymptotically by converting them to an integral over the subsystem charge density
t
, using the Riemann sum approximation:

\sum_{q_{A}}\to\int fN\,dt\,.

(26)

The relation (
18
) between the Hilbert space of the system and of the subsystems
A
and
B
implies that

\int d(t)b(s,t)\,fNdt\;=\big(1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})\big)D(s)\,.

(27)

This condition allows us to express
\gamma_{1}(s)
in terms of
\eta(s)
and
\alpha_{0}(s)
:

\textstyle\gamma_{1}(s)=\;\frac{(1-f)^{2}}{2}\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime}(s)}{\alpha_{0}(s)\,\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}+\frac{1-(1-f)f}{24}\Big(\frac{3\eta^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(s)}{\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)^{2}}-\frac{4\eta^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)^{2}}{\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)^{3}}\Big)\,.

(28)

3.2
Probability distribution for the subsystem charge density
t

With these definitions, we introduce the probability distribution function
\varrho_{N}(t)
, defined as

\varrho_{N}(t)\equiv\frac{d(t)\,b(s,t)}{D(s)}Nf\,.

(29)

By construction, we have that the probability is normalized up to subleading corrections of order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
,

\int\varrho_{N}(t)dt\,=\,1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})\,.

(30)

The average of any function
F(t)
which is independent of
N
and is smooth in a neighborhood of
t=s
(which is the stationary point of the probability distribution
\varrho_{N}(t)
) is

\overline{F\,}(s)\,\equiv\,\int F(t)\,\varrho_{N}(t)\,dt\,=\,F(s)+\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(s)}{\alpha_{0}(s)}F^{\prime}(s)+\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(s)\right)\frac{1-f}{(-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s))\,f\,}\frac{1}{N}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})\,.

(31)

The average is independent of
\alpha_{1}(t)
at this order. Equivalently, the formula (
31
) for the average can be written in terms of the Taylor series of
F(t)
around the point
t=s
, using the moments of the local charge density
t
:

\displaystyle\overline{\,(t-s)\,}\textstyle=\,\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(s)}{\alpha_{0}(s)}\frac{1-f}{(-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s))\,f}\,\frac{1}{N}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})\,,\qquad\overline{\,(t-s)^{2}\,}\textstyle=\,\frac{1-f}{(-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s))\,f}\,\frac{1}{N}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})\,,

(32)

while higher order moments are subleading, i.e.,
\overline{\,(t-s)^{k}\,}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
for
k\geq 3
. On the other hand, if the function
F(t)
is not smooth in a neighborhood of the stationary point
t=s
, i.e., a function of the form

F(t)=\begin{cases}\;F_{-}(t)\quad&t<s\,,\\[1.99997pt]
\;F_{+}(t)&t>s\,,\end{cases}

(33)

then we have that the average is given by

\overline{F\,}(s)=\int F(t)\,\varrho_{N}(t)\,dt\;=\;\frac{F_{+}(s)+F_{-}(s)}{2}\;+\;\tfrac{C_{1/2}(s)}{\sqrt{N}}+\tfrac{C_{1}(s)\vphantom{C_{1/2}}}{N\vphantom{\sqrt{N}}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2})\,,

(34)

with the coefficients

\displaystyle C_{1/2}(s)=

\displaystyle\,\textstyle\left(\Big(\frac{(1-2f)\,\eta^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)}{6(1-f)\,\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}+\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(s)}{\alpha_{0}(s)}\Big)(F_{+}(s)-F_{-}(s))+(F_{+}^{\prime}(s)-F_{-}^{\prime}(s))\right)\sqrt{\frac{1-f}{2\pi\,(-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s))\,f}}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle C_{1}(s)=

\displaystyle\,\textstyle\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(s)}{\alpha_{0}(s)}\frac{F_{+}^{\prime}(s)+F_{-}^{\prime}(s)}{2}+\frac{F_{+}^{\prime\prime}(s)+F_{-}^{\prime\prime}(s)}{4}\right)\frac{1-f}{(-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s))\,f}\,.

(36)

As concrete examples, we report the average of the functions
\theta(t-s)
and
|t-s|
:

\displaystyle\textstyle\overline{\,\theta(t-s)\,}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})\,,\qquad\overline{\,|t-s|\,}\,=\,\sqrt{\frac{2(1-f)}{\pi(-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s))f}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,.

(37)

In the next section, we use these expressions for computing the average entanglement entropy of a subsystem.

4
Typical entanglement entropy at fixed charge

We derive the general formula for the average entanglement entropy at fixed global charge and its variance using Laplace’s method, and establish typicality in the thermodynamic limit.

4.1
Average entanglement entropy: exact formula

We consider a random pure state in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(q)}
with a fixed charge
q
, and its restriction to the
G
-invariant reduced state in the subsystem
A
given by the decomposition (
18

G
, the
G
-invariant entanglement entropy for this subsystem is defined in
[
5
]
. The average over random states of fixed charge is given by the exact formulas (98)–(99) in
[

\displaystyle\langle S_{A}\rangle_{q}\;=\;\Psi(D_{q}+1)\;

\displaystyle-\sum_{q_{A}}\Big(\Psi\big(\!\max(d_{q_{A}},b_{q,q_{A}})+1\big)-\frac{1}{2\max(d_{q_{A}},b_{q,q_{A}})}\Big)\;\tfrac{d_{q_{A}}\,b_{q,q_{A}}}{D_{q}}

(38)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{q_{A}}\min\big(\tfrac{d_{q_{A}}}{b_{q,q_{A}}},\tfrac{b_{q,q_{A}}}{d_{q_{A}}}\big)\,\tfrac{d_{q_{A}}\,b_{q,q_{A}}}{D_{q}}\,,

(39)

where
\Psi(x)=\Gamma^{\prime}(x)/\Gamma(x)
is the digamma function, i.e., the logarithmic derivative of the gamma function
\Gamma(x)
. We are interested in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
of
\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}
at fixed fraction

and fixed charge density

24

) and (

D_{q}

q_{A}
with an integral over

, up to order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
, as done in (

).

4.2
Asymptotics and typicality

We consider separately the three terms appearing in the expression (
38
)–(
39
) for the average entanglement entropy
\langle S_{A}\rangle_{q}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+Y_{3}
:

4.2.1
Y_{1}

The first term in Eq. (

Y_{1}=\Psi(D_{q}+1)=\log(D(s))\,+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})
, as follows from the asymptotic expansion of the digamma function for

\Psi(x+1)-\frac{1}{2x}=\log x+\mathcal{O}(x^{-2})\,.

(40)

Therefore, using (

\textstyle Y_{1}=\eta(s)N-\frac{1}{2}\log N+\log\big(\alpha_{0}(s)\big)+\log\Big(\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi}}\,\Big)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,.

(41)

4.2.2
Y_{2}
term

The second term
Y_{2}
in Eq. (
38

Y_{2}=-\int\max\!\big(\log d(t),\log b(s,t)\big)\;\varrho_{N}(t)dt\;\,+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,,

(42)

where we have used (
40
) for the digamma function. Moreover, using the asymptotics (
25
) of the dimensions, we find the structure

\max\!\big(\log d(t),\log b(s,t)\big)=\,F(t)N-\tfrac{1}{2}\log N+G(t)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,.

(43)

where the function
F(t)
in a neighborhood of
t=s
up to the order
\mathcal{O}((t-s)^{2})
is

\displaystyle F(t)=\begin{cases}\;\textstyle(1-f)\,\eta(s)-f\eta^{\prime}(s)\,(t-s)+\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{f^{2}}{1-f}\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)\,(t-s)^{2}\,+\,\mathcal{O}((t-s)^{3})\;\;&f<\frac{1}{2}\,,\\[5.0pt]
\;\textstyle\tfrac{1}{2}\eta(s)+\tfrac{1}{2}|\eta^{\prime}(s)|\,|t-s|+\tfrac{1}{4}\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)\,(t-s)^{2}\,+\,\mathcal{O}((t-s)^{3})\quad&f=\frac{1}{2}\,,\\[5.0pt]
\;\textstyle f\eta(s)+f\eta^{\prime}(s)\,(t-s)+\tfrac{1}{2}f\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)\,(t-s)^{2}\,+\,\mathcal{O}((t-s)^{3})\,&f>\frac{1}{2}\,,\end{cases}

(44)

and the function
G(t)
in a neighborhood of
t=s
up to the order
\mathcal{O}((t-s)^{0})
is

\displaystyle G(t)=\begin{cases}\;\textstyle-\frac{\log(1-f)}{2}+\log\Big(\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi}}\Big)\,+\,\mathcal{O}(t-s)\;\;&f<\frac{1}{2}\,,\\[5.0pt]
\;\textstyle-\frac{\log(1/2)}{2}+\log(\alpha_{0}(s))\,\theta\big((t-s)\eta^{\prime}(s)\big)+\log\Big(\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi}}\Big)\,+\,\mathcal{O}(t-s)\!&f=\frac{1}{2}\,,\\[5.0pt]
\;\textstyle-\frac{\log f}{2}\,+\,\log(\alpha_{0}(s))+\log\Big(\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi}}\Big)\,+\,\mathcal{O}(t-s)&f>\frac{1}{2}\,.\end{cases}

(45)

Using (
31
)–(
34
), we can compute the averages
\overline{F}(s)
and
\overline{G}(s)
, and therefore the term
Y_{2}
.

For
f<1/2
, we find

\displaystyle\textstyle Y_{2}\big|_{f<\frac{1}{2}}=

\displaystyle\textstyle-(1-f)\eta(s)N+\frac{1}{2}\log N+\frac{\log(1-f)+f}{2}

(46)

\displaystyle\textstyle-(1-f)\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(s)\eta^{\prime}(s)}{\alpha_{0}(s)\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}-\log\Big(\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi}}\Big)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,.\hskip 60.00009pt

(47)

For
f=1/2
, using (
34
), we find

\displaystyle\textstyle Y_{2}\big|_{f=\frac{1}{2}}=

\displaystyle\textstyle-\frac{1}{2}\eta(s)N-\sqrt{\frac{\eta^{\prime}(s)^{2}}{-2\pi\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}}\sqrt{N}+\frac{1}{2}\log N+\frac{\log(1/2)+1/2}{2}

(48)

\displaystyle\textstyle-\frac{1}{2}\log(\alpha_{0}(s))-\log\Big(\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi}}\Big)\,+\,\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})\,.\hskip 66.00015pt

(49)

Finally, for
f>1/2
, we find

\displaystyle\textstyle Y_{2}\big|_{f>\frac{1}{2}}=

\displaystyle\textstyle-f\eta(s)N+\frac{1}{2}\log N+\frac{\log(f)+1-f}{2}

(50)

\displaystyle\textstyle+(1-f)\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(s)\eta^{\prime}(s)}{\alpha_{0}(s)\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}-\log\big(\alpha_{0}(s)\big)-\log\Big(\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi}}\Big)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,.

(51)

4.2.3
Y_{3}
term

The third term
Y_{3}
in the expression (
39

\langle S_{A}\rangle_{q}
is bounded,
|Y_{3}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and is given by

Y_{3}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\int\min\!\Big(\tfrac{d(t)}{b(s,t)},\tfrac{b(s,t)}{d(t)}\Big)\;\varrho_{N}(t)\,dt\;\,+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,.

(52)

The minimum can be written as an exponential

\min\!\Big(\tfrac{d(t)}{b(s,t)},\tfrac{b(s,t)}{d(t)}\Big)=\mathrm{e}^{-\left|\log\frac{d(t)}{b(s,t)}\right|}=(1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1}))\,\tilde{h}(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-|\omega(t)|N}\,,

(53)

where
\tilde{h}(t)
is a piecewise continuous function of

of order
\mathcal{O}(N^{0})
. The function
\omega(t)
in the exponent is

\omega(t)=(1-f)\,\eta(\tfrac{s-ft}{1-f})-f\,\eta(t)\,.

(54)

Expanding around

, we find

\omega(t)\,=\,(1-2f)\eta(s)-2f\eta^{\prime}(s)\,(t-s)+\mathcal{O}((t-s)^{2})\,,

(55)

which implies that

is exponentially suppressed, unless

. Under this condition, we have
|\omega(t)|=|\eta^{\prime}(s)|\,|t-s|+\mathcal{O}((t-s)^{2})
. Using (

\overline{\,|\omega(t)|\,}N\propto\sqrt{N}
, unless the subsystem fraction
f_{\circledast}
and the global charge density

satisfy

f_{\circledast}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\eta^{\prime}(s_{\circledast})=0\,.

(56)

Under these conditions, we can compute the value of

, which is derived in App.

Y_{3}=-\tfrac{\alpha_{0}(s_{\circledast})+\alpha_{0}(s_{\circledast})^{-1}}{4}\,\delta_{s,s_{\circledast}}\,\delta_{f,\frac{1}{2}}\,+\,\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})\,.

(57)

4.2.4
Average entanglement entropy

Combining the results above, the average entanglement entropy
\langle S_{A}\rangle_{q}
for

is

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}=\begin{cases}\;\textstyle f\eta(s)N+\frac{\log(1-f)+f}{2}+\log(\alpha_{0}(s))-(1-f)\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(s)\eta^{\prime}(s)}{\alpha_{0}(s)\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\;\;&f<\frac{1}{2}\,,\\[10.00002pt]
\;\textstyle\frac{1}{2}\eta(s)N-\sqrt{\frac{\eta^{\prime}(s)^{2}}{-2\pi\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}}\sqrt{N}+\frac{\log(1/2)+1/2}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\log(\alpha_{0}(s))\,&f=\frac{1}{2}\,,\\[5.0pt]
\hskip 113.0002pt-\frac{\alpha_{0}(s_{\circledast})+\alpha_{0}(s_{\circledast})^{-1}}{4}\delta_{s,s_{\circledast}}\,+\,\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})&\\[10.00002pt]
\;\textstyle(1-f)\eta(s)N+\frac{\log(f)+1-f}{2}+(1-f)\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(s)\eta^{\prime}(s)}{\alpha_{0}(s)\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,&f>\frac{1}{2}\,.\end{cases}

(58)

The structure of
\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}
encompasses both the results of
[
3

for the systems with particle number conservation and of
[

for non-abelian

, i.e., it is proportional to the number

of bodies in the subsystem

, the leading order is Page-like

. The coefficient of the leading order term is the local entropy
\eta(s)
defined in (

, a term of order

appears
[

, with coefficient determined by the local heat capacity (

[

. This term vanishes in the infinite temperature limit

. At order
\mathcal{O}(N^{0})

\frac{\log(1-f)+f}{2}
appears which was identified in
[

for eigenstates of a physical Hamiltonian with particle number conservation. This term was shown to be universal for random states with
U(1)
charge in
[

charge, generalizing the result of

4.2.5
Variance of the entanglement entropy

The variance of the entanglement entropy for a random state of fixed charge
q
is given by Eq. (203) of
[
5
]
, (see also
[
2
,
3
]
). In the thermodynamic limit, using the same methods described above, we find

(\Delta S_{A})^{2}_{Ns}=\left(\Big(\sqrt{2\pi}\,f(1-f)-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\delta_{f,\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\frac{\eta^{\prime}(s)^{2}}{\alpha_{0}(s)(-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s))^{3/2}}\,N^{3/2}+\mathcal{O}(N)\right)\mathrm{e}^{-N\eta(s)}\,.

(59)

For charge density

corresponding to a non-vanishing local entropy,
\eta(s)\neq 0

. Therefore, in the thermodynamic limit, the average entanglement entropy
\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}
is typical: the probability
P(S_{A})
that a random state of charge

has entropy
S_{A}
is sharply peaked at the average.

The proportionality coefficient in (

c_{*}(s)^{3/2}\,|\beta_{*}(s)|^{-1}
of the local heat capacity and the temperature. At this order, the formula shows that the variance is symmetric under the exchange

.

5

U(1)

We derive the asymptotic formulas for general systems at fixed
U(1)

5.1
Asymptotic formulas for general systems with a
U(1)
charge

In this section we specialize the general formulas derived above to the case of a conserved
U(1)
charge. In particular, we compute the functions
\eta(s)
and
\alpha_{0}(s)
appearing in the asymptotic expansion (
8
). We start from the expression (
6
) for the dimension of the Hilbert space at fixed charge
q=Ns
. For
U(1)
, since the irreducible representations are one-dimensional, the character with charge
q=m
is simply
\chi^{(m)}(g)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m\theta}
, where
\theta
parametrizes the group element
g=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}
. The Haar measure is
d\mu(g)=\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
, and the character of the local Hilbert-space representation is

\chi_{\mathrm{loc}}(g)=\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m_{\mathrm{loc}}\theta}\,.

(60)

With these definitions, the dimension of the Hilbert space at fixed charge
m=Ns
can be written in the exact integral form

D_{Ns}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}Ns\theta}\Big(\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m_{\mathrm{loc}}\theta}\Big)^{N}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{e}^{N\tilde{\eta}(-\mathrm{i}\theta,\,s)}\,,

(61)

where we have defined the function

\tilde{\eta}(\beta,s)=\log\Big(\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\Big)+\beta\,s\,.

(62)

The integral is dominated by the stationary point
\beta_{*}(s)
, determined by the condition

\partial_{\beta}\tilde{\eta}(\beta,s)|_{\beta_{*}(s)}=0\quad\rightarrow\quad\frac{\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}m_{\mathrm{loc}}a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta_{*}(s)\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}}{\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta_{*}(s)\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}}=s\,.

(63)

Thus
\beta_{*}(s)
is the inverse temperature of the thermal distribution (
10
) that maximizes the Shannon entropy at fixed average charge
s
. The stationary point
\beta_{*}(s)
does not lie on the original integration contour, which is the imaginary axis, but it can be reached by deforming the contour in the complex plane.

Evaluating the function
\tilde{\eta}(\beta,s)
at the stationary point
\beta_{*}(s)
gives the function
\eta(s)
appearing in (
8
). Completing the stationary-point calculation of
D_{Ns}
, we find

D_{Ns}\,=\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{N\,\partial_{\beta}^{2}\tilde{\eta}(\beta,s)|_{\beta_{*}(s)}}}\big(1+\mathcal{O}(1/N)\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{N\tilde{\eta}(\beta_{*}(s),\,s)}\,.

(64)

Defining
\eta(s)=\tilde{\eta}(\beta_{*}(s),\,s)
and using the relation between the derivatives of
\eta(s)
and those of
\tilde{\eta}(\beta,s)
at the stationary point
\beta_{*}(s)
,

\eta^{\prime}(s)=\beta_{*}(s)\quad\text{and}\quad\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)=-\frac{1}{\partial_{\beta}^{2}\tilde{\eta}(\beta,s)|_{\beta_{*}(s)}}\,,

(65)

we can express the asymptotic expansion of
D_{Ns}
in the form (
8
) as

D_{Ns}=\big(1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\big)\,\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi N}}\,\mathrm{e}^{N\eta(s)}\,.

(66)

We read off the functions
\eta(s)
and
\alpha_{0}(s)
from the asymptotic expansion of
D_{Ns}
as

\eta(s)=\log\Big(\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta_{*}(s)\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\Big)+\beta_{*}(s)\,s\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{0}(s)=1\,.

(67)

The coefficient
\alpha_{1}(s)
can also be read off from a next-to-leading order calculation, but is not needed here.
To make these formulas explicit, we now specify the decomposition of the local Hilbert space into irreducible representations of charge
m_{\mathrm{loc}}
, namely the coefficients
a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}
.

Let us summarize the generic features uncovered for the
U(1)
charge case. The probability distribution over the local charge
m_{\mathrm{loc}}
is given by

p_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)=\frac{a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta m_{\mathrm{loc}}}}{\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta m_{\mathrm{loc}}}}\,,

(68)

From the probability distribution
p_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)
, we define an inverse temperature
\beta_{*}(s)
by fixing the average charge to be the system charge density
s
. Implicitly we have

\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}m_{\mathrm{loc}}\;p_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*})\;=\;s\,.

(69)

The function
\eta(s)
is given by
\log(k)
minus the relative entropy of the probability distribution
p_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*}(s))
with respect to the infinite-temperature distribution
p_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(0)=a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}/\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}
,

\eta(s)=\log(k)-\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}p_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*}(s))\log\Big(\frac{p_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*}(s))}{p_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(0)}\Big)\,.

(70)

In terms of
\eta
, the inverse temperature
\beta_{*}(s)
is given by the first derivative of
\eta(s)
, suggesting the interpretation of
\eta(s)
as a thermodynamic potential:

\beta_{*}(s)=\eta^{\prime}(s)\,.

(71)

Similarly, we define the heat capacity from the second derivative of
\eta(s)
:

c_{*}(s)=\frac{\eta^{\prime}(s)^{2}}{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}\,.

(72)

Finally, the coefficient
\alpha_{0}(s)
appearing in the asymptotic expansion of
D_{Ns}
is equal to
1
for all
s
. This allows us to simplify the asymptotic expansion of the average entanglement entropy
\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}
computed in (
58

U(1)
charge is:

\boxed{\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}=\begin{cases}\;\textstyle f\eta(s)N+\frac{\log(1-f)+f}{2}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,&f<\frac{1}{2}\\[10.00002pt]
\;\textstyle\frac{1}{2}\eta(s)N-\sqrt{\frac{c_{*}(s)}{2\pi}}\sqrt{N}+\frac{\log(1/2)+1/2}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{s,s_{\circledast}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-{1/2}})\,&f=\frac{1}{2}\\[10.00002pt]
\;\textstyle(1-f)\eta(s)N+\frac{\log(f)+1-f}{2}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\,&f>\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}}

(73)

The variance of the entanglement entropy with a fixed
U(1)
charge is

(\Delta S_{A})^{2}_{Ns}=\left(\Big(\sqrt{2\pi}\,f(1-f)-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\delta_{f,\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\frac{c_{*}(s)^{3/2}}{|\beta_{*}(s)|}\,N^{3/2}+\mathcal{O}(N)\right)\mathrm{e}^{-N\eta(s)}\,,

(74)

which is exponentially suppressed in
N
for any charge density
s
such that
\eta(s)\neq 0

\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}
is typical in the thermodynamic limit.

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(-1/2)}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(+1/2)}\,,

(75)

with

. We compute the average entanglement entropy of this

charge
m=Ns

6.1
Asymptotic formulas for general systems with a
SU(2)
charge

Following the same steps as in the previous section, we can also compute the functions
\eta(s)
and
\alpha_{0}(s)
for the case of a conserved
SU(2)
charge. The main difference with respect to the
U(1)
case is that the characters of the irreducible representations of
SU(2)
are more complicated than those of
U(1)
, and the Haar measure is also different.

The starting point is the dimension of the Hilbert space at fixed charge written using the expression (
6
) for
SU(2)
.
The character of the irreducible representation of charge
q=j
is

\chi^{(j)}(g)=\sum_{m=-j}^{j}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m\theta}=\frac{\sin((2j+1)\theta/2)}{\sin(\theta/2)}\ ,

(94)

where
\theta
parametrizes the diagonal group elements
g=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta\sigma_{z}/2}
. The character is invariant under group conjugation, so we can restrict to the diagonal elements without any loss of generality. In terms of the angular variable
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, the Haar measure for
SU(2)
is given by
d\mu(g)=\frac{1}{\pi}\sin^{2}(\theta/2)d\theta
, which is the measure on the maximal torus of
SU(2)
, normalized such that
\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\mu(g)=1
. The character of the local Hilbert-space representation is

\chi_{\mathrm{loc}}(g)=\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\chi^{(j_{\mathrm{loc}})}(g)\,=\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m_{\mathrm{loc}}\theta}\,,

(95)

where, in this compact notation, the magnetic quantum number
m_{\mathrm{loc}}
runs from
-j_{\mathrm{loc}}
to
+j_{\mathrm{loc}}
in integer steps which could be evaluated explicitly as in (
94
), but it is more convenient to leave in this form for the moment. With these definitions, the dimension of the Hilbert space at fixed charge
j=Ns
can be written as

D_{Ns}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\sin((2Ns+1)\theta/2)}{\sin(\theta/2)}\Big(\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m_{\mathrm{loc}}\theta}\Big)^{N}\,\frac{1}{\pi}\sin^{2}(\theta/2)\,d\theta\,.

(96)

To evaluate the integral in the large
N
limit using the Laplace method, we can manipulate the integrand to write it in a more suitable form. First, we can split
\sin((2Ns+1)\theta/2)
into two exponentials and write the integrand as a sum of two terms. Then, we can perform the change of variable
\theta\to-\theta
in one of the two terms, which leaves the integration measure and the local character invariant. This allows us to write the dimension as

D_{Ns}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\big(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}Ns\theta}\Big(\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m_{\mathrm{loc}}\theta}\Big)^{N}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\big(1-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{N\tilde{\eta}(-\mathrm{i}\theta,\,s)}\,.

(97)

Here we have defined the function

\tilde{\eta}(\beta,s)=\log\Big(\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\mathrm{loc}}\beta}\Big)+s\beta\,.

(98)

The integral is dominated by the stationary point
\beta_{*}(s)
, determined by the condition

\partial_{\beta}\tilde{\eta}(\beta,s)|_{\beta_{*}(s)}=0\quad\rightarrow\quad\frac{\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}m_{\mathrm{loc}}}m_{\mathrm{loc}}a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\mathrm{loc}}\beta_{*}(s)}}{\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\mathrm{loc}}\beta_{*}(s)}}=s\,.

(99)

Formally, this is the same condition as in the
U(1)
case, but now the sum runs over the magnetic quantum numbers
m_{\mathrm{loc}}
of the irreducible representations of
SU(2)
, which are weighted by their multiplicity
a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}
. Also in this case, the stationary point
\beta_{*}(s)
does not lie on the original integration contour, which is the imaginary axis, but it can be reached by deforming the contour in the complex plane.

A few comments are in order about the stationary-point condition (
99
). Positivity of the charge implies that the inverse temperature
\beta_{*}(s)
is negative, which in turn implies that the function
\eta(s)
is monotonically decreasing (its derivative is negative). Since
\eta(s)
is also concave (its second derivative is negative), it follows that the maximum of
\eta(s)
is attained at
s_{\circledast}=0
. This extreme point is special, since it corresponds to the case of zero charge. The leading order of the dimension
D_{Ns}
at
s=s_{\circledast}
vanishes and the next-to-leading order becomes relevant. We do not report the explicit form of the entanglement entropy at
s=s_{\circledast}
here, since it is not relevant for the present calculations. We refer to Sec. 6.2 of
[
5
]
for an analysis of these extremal cases.

Evaluating the function
\tilde{\eta}(\beta,s)
at the stationary point
\beta_{*}(s)
gives the function
\eta(s)
appearing in (
8
). Completing the stationary-point calculation of
D_{Ns}
, and simplifying the result using the properties of
\eta(s)
, we find

D_{Ns}=\big(1-\mathrm{e}^{\eta^{\prime}(s)}\big)\sqrt{\frac{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}{2\pi N}}\big(1+\mathcal{O}(1/N)\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{N\eta(s)}\,.

(100)

From this expression for
D_{Ns}
we can read the functions
\eta(s)
and
\alpha_{0}(s)
defined in (
8
):

\eta(s)=\log\Big(\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m_{\mathrm{loc}}\beta_{*}(s)}\Big)+s\beta_{*}(s)\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{0}(s)=1-\mathrm{e}^{\eta^{\prime}(s)}\,.

(101)

The next-to-leading order correction
\alpha_{1}(s)
can be computed in a similar way. We do not report its value as it cancels out in the expression of the average entanglement entropy at order
\mathcal{O}(N^{0})
.

Let us summarize the generic features of the case with fixed global
SU(2)
charge. The probability distribution over the magnetic quantum number
m_{\mathrm{loc}}
in the irreducible representation

j_{\mathrm{loc}}
is

p_{j_{\mathrm{loc}},m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)=\frac{a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta m_{\mathrm{loc}}}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta m_{\mathrm{loc}}}}\,.

(102)

The inverse temperature
\beta_{*}(s)
is fixed by imposing that the average magnetic quantum number equals the charge density
s
,

\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}m_{\mathrm{loc}}\;p_{j_{\mathrm{loc}},m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*})\;=\;s\,.

(103)

The function
\eta(s)
is
\log(k)
minus the relative entropy of the probability distribution at temperature
\beta_{*}(s)^{-1}
with respect to its infinite temperature limit
p_{j_{\mathrm{loc}},m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(0)=a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}/k
, where

k=\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}(2j_{\mathrm{loc}}+1)a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}
is the local Hilbert space dimension:

\eta(s)=\log(k)-\sum_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}\,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}p_{j_{\mathrm{loc}},m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*}(s))\log\!\Big(\frac{p_{j_{\mathrm{loc}},m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*}(s))}{p_{j_{\mathrm{loc}},m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(0)}\Big)\,.

(104)

In terms of
\eta
, the inverse temperature is the first derivative,

\beta_{*}(s)=\eta^{\prime}(s)\,,

(105)

motivating the interpretation of
\eta(s)
as a thermodynamic potential, and the heat capacity is

c_{*}(s)=\frac{\eta^{\prime}(s)^{2}}{-\eta^{\prime\prime}(s)}\,.

(106)

The key difference with respect to the
U(1)
case is that the coefficient
\alpha_{0}(s)
is no longer trivial. It takes the
SU(2)
-specific value

\alpha_{0}(s)=1-\mathrm{e}^{\eta^{\prime}(s)}=1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}\,.

(107)

Since
s\geq 0
implies
\beta_{*}(s)\leq 0
, the prefactor satisfies
0\leq\alpha_{0}(s)<1
throughout the physical domain, with
\alpha_{0}(s)=0
only at the boundary
s=0
where the stationary-point formula breaks down (See Sec. 6.2 of
[
5
]
for the analysis of this extremal case). The condition
\eta^{\prime}(s_{\circledast})=0
is therefore only met at
s_{\circledast}=0
and, as a result, the
\delta_{s,s_{\circledast}}
contribution present in the
U(1)
formula at
f=\frac{1}{2}
does not appear for non-extremal
SU(2)
charge. Substituting
\alpha_{0}(s)=1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}
into (
58
), we find that the average entanglement entropy at fixed global
SU(2)
charge
j=Ns
takes the form

\displaystyle\boxed{\!\!\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}\!=\!\begin{cases}\;\textstyle f\eta(s)N+\frac{\log(1-f)+f}{2}+\log\!\big(1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}\big)+(1-f)\frac{\beta_{*}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\!\!&f<\tfrac{1}{2}\\[11.99998pt]
\;\textstyle\tfrac{1}{2}\eta(s)N-\sqrt{\frac{c_{*}(s)}{2\pi}}\sqrt{N}+\frac{\log(1/2)+1/2}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\log\!\big(1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}\big)+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})&f=\tfrac{1}{2}\\[11.99998pt]
\;\textstyle(1-f)\eta(s)N+\frac{\log(f)+1-f}{2}-(1-f)\frac{\beta_{*}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})&f>\tfrac{1}{2}\end{cases}}

(108)

The variance of the entanglement entropy with a fixed global
SU(2)
charge is

(\Delta S_{A})^{2}_{Ns}=\left(\Big(\sqrt{2\pi}\,f(1-f)-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\delta_{f,\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\frac{c_{*}(s)^{3/2}}{(1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)})\,|\beta_{*}(s)|}\,N^{3/2}+\mathcal{O}(N)\right)\mathrm{e}^{-N\eta(s)}\,,

(109)

which is exponentially suppressed in
N
for any charge density
s
such that
\eta(s)\neq 0
. Therefore, the average entanglement entropy
\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}
is typical in the thermodynamic limit.

p_{1,-1}(s)=\frac{4-3s-\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{6}\,,\ p_{1,0}(s)=\frac{-1+\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{3}\,,\ p_{1,+1}(s)=\frac{4+3s-\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{6}\,.

The function
\eta(s)=-\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}}p_{1,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}\log p_{1,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}
can be computed explicitly and takes the form

\displaystyle\eta_{{SU(2)\text{-qutrit}}}(s)\,=

\displaystyle-\frac{4-3s-\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{6}\log\!\Big(\frac{4-3s-\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{6}\Big)

(119)

\displaystyle-\frac{-1+\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{3}\log\!\Big(\frac{-1+\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{3}\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{4+3s-\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{6}\log\!\Big(\frac{4+3s-\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{6}\Big)\,.

This is the same function
\eta(s)
as for the
U(1)
qutrit (
83
), since the local charge spectrum
m_{\mathrm{loc}}\in\{-1,0,+1\}
and the multiplicities coincide in the two cases. The group-dependent prefactor, however, is different and is given by

\alpha_{0}(s)=1-\mathrm{e}^{\eta^{\prime}(s)}=1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}=\frac{2+3s-\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{2(1+s)}\,.

(120)

The entanglement entropy at fixed total spin can then be obtained by plugging (
119
) and
\alpha_{0}(s)
into the general formulas
(
58
).

From
\eta(s)
we can compute the temperature and the local heat capacity:

\displaystyle\beta_{*}(s)

\displaystyle=\log\Big(\frac{-s+\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{2(1+s)}\Big)\,,

(121)

\displaystyle c_{*}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}\big(\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}-1\big)}{12}\Big(\log\frac{4+3s-\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}{4-3s-\sqrt{4-3s^{2}}}\,\Big)^{2}\,.

(122)

6.4
Example:
N
trimers of spin-
\frac{1}{2}
at fixed
SU(2)

We consider the case of
N
qukits,
k=8
. Each local Hilbert space is made of three spin-
1/2

SU(2)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(1/2)}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(1/2)}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(1/2)}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(1/2)}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(1/2)}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{irrep}}^{(3/2)}\,.

(123)

Equivalently, the only non-vanishing multiplicities are
a_{1/2}=2
,
a_{3/2}=1
. We compute the average entanglement entropy of this
N
-body system at fixed global
SU(2)
charge
j=Ns
and fixed subsystem fraction
f
. Microscopically, each of the bodies can be viewed as
a local trimer of three exchange-coupled spin-
1/2
moments, as realized for instance in triangular molecular magnets
[
57

and in semiconductor double-quantum-dot
spin trimers
[
58

The probability distribution entering the asymptotic analysis is

p_{j_{\mathrm{loc}},m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta)=\frac{a_{j_{\mathrm{loc}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta m_{\mathrm{loc}}}}{\mathrm{e}^{-3\beta/2}+3\mathrm{e}^{-\beta/2}+3\mathrm{e}^{\beta/2}+\mathrm{e}^{3\beta/2}}\,.

(124)

The inverse temperature
\beta_{*}(s)
is fixed by the saddle-point condition (

), which gives

\frac{\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-3\beta_{*}(s)/2}+\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta_{*}(s)/2}-\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)/2}-\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{e}^{3\beta_{*}(s)/2}}{\mathrm{e}^{-3\beta_{*}(s)/2}+3\mathrm{e}^{-\beta_{*}(s)/2}+3\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)/2}+\mathrm{e}^{3\beta_{*}(s)/2}}=-\frac{3}{2}\tanh\!\big(\beta_{*}(s)/2\big)=s\,,

(125)

so that

\beta_{*}(s)=-2\,\mathrm{arctanh}\!\left(\tfrac{2s}{3}\right)\,,\qquad 0<s<\tfrac{3}{2}\,.

(126)

With these definitions, the probabilities
p_{j_{\mathrm{loc}},m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(\beta_{*}(s))
can be written explicitly in terms of
s
as

\displaystyle p_{3/2,-3/2}(s)=\frac{(3-2s)^{3}}{6^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle p_{3/2,-1/2}(s)=\frac{(3-2s)^{2}(3+2s)}{6^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle p_{3/2,+1/2}(s)=\frac{(3-2s)(3+2s)^{2}}{6^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle p_{3/2,+3/2}(s)=\frac{(3+2s)^{3}}{6^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle p_{1/2,-1/2}(s)=2\frac{(3-2s)^{2}(3+2s)}{6^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle p_{1/2,+1/2}(s)=2\frac{(3-2s)(3+2s)^{2}}{6^{3}}\,.

The function
\eta(s)
can be computed explicitly as

\eta(s)=-\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}=-3/2}^{3/2}p_{3/2,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(s)\log p_{3/2,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(s)-\sum_{m_{\mathrm{loc}}=-1/2}^{1/2}p_{1/2,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(s)\log\frac{p_{1/2,m_{\mathrm{loc}}}(s)}{2}\,,

(127)

and takes the explicit form

\eta_{SU(2)\text{-trimer}}(s)\,=\,-\frac{3-2s}{2}\log\Big(\frac{3-2s}{6}\Big)-\frac{3+2s}{2}\log\Big(\frac{3+2s}{6}\Big)\,.

(128)

Equivalently, this can be written as

\eta_{SU(2)\text{-trimer}}(s)=3\,\eta_{{SU(2)-\mathrm{qubit}}}(s/3)\,,

(129)

showing that this example is equivalent to three independent spin-
1/2
grouped into a single local
SU(2)
representation. The group-dependent prefactor is

\alpha_{0}(s)=1-\mathrm{e}^{\eta^{\prime}(s)}=1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta_{*}(s)}=\frac{4s}{3+2s}\,.

(130)

The entanglement entropy at fixed total spin can then be obtained by plugging (
128
) and
\alpha_{0}(s)
into the general formulas (
58
).

From
\eta(s)
we can compute the temperature and the local heat capacity:

\beta_{*}(s)=\log\Big(\frac{3-2s}{3+2s}\Big)\,,\qquad c_{*}(s)=\Big(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{s^{2}}{3}\Big)\Big(\!\log\frac{3-2s}{3+2s}\,\Big)^{2}\,.

(131)

Discussion

The analysis presented in this paper provides a general treatment of typical entanglement entropy for any many-body system with a fixed global charge and a general local Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
. The known results on typical entanglement entropy
\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}
with fixed abelian and non-abelian charge are recovered as special cases of the general formula (
58
):

\scriptstyle\bullet

In the case of fixed abelian
U(1)
charge, the average entanglement entropy (
58
) reduces to the expression (
73
) found in
[
6
]
for
k
-dimensional local Hilbert spaces with particle-number conservation, and in
[
3
]
for the special case of systems of qubits.

\scriptstyle\bullet

In the case of fixed non-abelian
SU(2)
charge, the average entanglement entropy (
58
) reduces to the expression (
108
) for general
k
-dimensional local Hilbert spaces, generalizing the result of
[
5
,
20
]
for the special case of systems of qubits.

The construction presented here applies to any many-body system with a local single-charge symmetry group and a fixed global charge. We showed that the typical entanglement entropy admits a thermodynamic interpretation in terms of the local entropy
\eta(s)
defined in (
11
), where the charge density
s
plays the role of the energy density of the local system. The function
\eta(s)
is fully determined by the structure of the local Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and its decomposition into irreducible representations of the local symmetry group. It is given by the expression
\eta(s)=\log(k)-\mathcal{D}(p_{s}\|p_{\circledast})
, (
11
),
where
k
is the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, the probability distribution
p_{s}
is the thermal distribution at fixed average charge
s
, the probability distribution
p_{\circledast}
is the thermal distribution at infinite temperature, and
\mathcal{D}(p_{s}\|p_{\circledast})
is their relative entropy, or Kullback-Leibler divergence. When all local multiplicities are trivial (
a_{q_{\mathrm{loc}}}=1
), the infinite-temperature distribution
p_{\circledast}=1/k
is uniform, and
\eta(s)
reduces to the Shannon entropy of
p_{s}
. To our knowledge, the formula (
11
) and the thermodynamic interpretation of
\eta(s)
are new.

Starting from the thermodynamic interpretation of the charge density
s
as local energy and the function
\eta(s)
as local entropy, we defined the inverse temperature
\beta_{*}(s)=\eta^{\prime}(s)
and the local heat capacity
c_{*}(s)=\partial s/\partial(\beta_{*}^{-1})
. The infinite-temperature limit
\beta_{*}(s_{\circledast})=0
defines the critical charge density
s_{\circledast}
. With these definitions, we can characterize the behavior of the average entanglement entropy
\langle S_{A}\rangle_{Ns}
in thermodynamic terms:

\scriptstyle\bullet

At the leading order
\mathcal{O}(N)
and up to half-system size
f\leq 1/2
, the average entanglement entropy is extensive, i.e., it is proportional to the number
N_{A}
of bodies in the subsystem
A
. The coefficient of the leading order term is the local entropy
\eta(s)
defined in (
11
) and described above, (
1
). In the case of local qubit Hilbert space (
k=2

78
) and (
113
) for fixed global
U(1)
charge
m
(with
s=m/N
) and global
SU(2)
charge
j
(with
s=j/N
) have the same form
\eta_{U(1)-\mathrm{qubit}}(s)=\eta_{SU(2)-\mathrm{qubit}}(s)
and match the expressions found in
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,

,

,

,

]
. In the case of local qutrit Hilbert space (

and (

[

\scriptstyle\bullet

At the leading order

\scriptstyle\bullet

At half-system size,

\scriptstyle\bullet

At the order

\scriptstyle\bullet

The results presented here apply to random pure states, sampled uniformly in a Hilbert space sector of fixed global charge. The construction does not rely on a specific Hamiltonian. Energy eigenstates of physical Hamiltonians also sample this same Hilbert space sector, provided that they have the same abelian or non-abelian symmetries and the same local Hilbert space structure as discussed here. Appendix A
Laplace approximation and discontinuities

We report here the relevant formula for the asymptotic evaluation, for
N\gg 1
, of integrals of the form

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}h(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t)}\,dt\;=\;\Big(1+\frac{C_{1}}{N}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})\Big)\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{-g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})\,N\,}}\;h(t_{0})\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t_{0})}\,,

(135)

using the Laplace method. This is a standard textbook treatment, but it is difficult to find all the formulas we need in one place in the literature. Here we assume that the functions
g(t)
and
h(t)
are smooth, and there is a single maximum of
g(t)
at the point
t_{0}
in the interval
(t_{1},t_{2})
, i.e.,

g^{\prime}(t_{0})=0\,,\quad g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})<0\,.

(136)

Since
g(t)<g(t_{0})
for all
t\neq t_{0}
in
(t_{1},t_{2})
, the integral is exponentially concentrated near
t_{0}
. Expanding both functions around
t_{0}
,

g(t)=g(t_{0})+\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0})^{2}+O((t-t_{0})^{3})\,,\qquad h(t)=h(t_{0})+O(t-t_{0})\,,

(137)

the integral becomes

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}h(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t)}\,dt\;=\;h(t_{0})\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t_{0})}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{N}{2}g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0})^{2}}\,\bigl(1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\bigr)\,dt\,.

Changing variables to
\tau=t-t_{0}
and extending the limits to
\pm\infty
at exponentially small cost, the remaining integral is a standard Gaussian, giving the leading term in (
135
):

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}h(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t)}\,dt\;=\;\bigl(1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\bigr)\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{-g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})\,N}}\,h(t_{0})\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t_{0})}\,.

To compute the correction
C_{1}
, we retain higher-order terms in the Taylor expansions and integrate them against the Gaussian weight. The Gaussian moments vanish for odd
k
by symmetry, and for even
k
evaluate to

\overline{(t-t_{0})^{k}}\;\equiv\;\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tau^{k}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{N}{2}g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})\tau^{2}}\,d\tau\;=\;\frac{(k-1)!!}{\bigl(-g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})\bigr)^{k/2}}\frac{1}{N^{(k+1)/2}}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{-g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})}}\,.

We factorize the Gaussian factor in the integrand

h(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t)}=\bigl(h(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t)-\frac{N}{2}g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0})^{2}}\bigr)\;\mathrm{e}^{\frac{N}{2}g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0})^{2}}

and Taylor-expand the prefactor. A term of the form
N^{k_{1}}(t-t_{0})^{k_{2}}
, integrated against the Gaussian, contributes at order
\mathcal{O}(N^{k_{1}-(k_{2}+1)/2})
for even
k_{2}
. The contributions at relative order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
, i.e., those with
k_{2}=2(k_{1}+1)
, are:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad(k_{1}=0,k_{2}=2)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{N}{6}h^{\prime}(t_{0})g^{\prime\prime\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0})^{4}

\displaystyle\quad(k_{1}=1,k_{2}=4)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{N}{24}h(t_{0})g^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0})^{4}

\displaystyle\quad(k_{1}=1,k_{2}=4)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{N^{2}}{36}h(t_{0})(g^{\prime\prime\prime}(t_{0}))^{2}(t-t_{0})^{6}

\displaystyle\quad(k_{1}=2,k_{2}=6)\,.

Evaluating each contribution and normalizing by the leading result gives
C_{1}
:

\textstyle C_{1}\,=\,-\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})}{2\,h(t_{0})\,g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})}+\frac{h^{\prime}(t_{0})\,g^{\prime\prime\prime}(t_{0})}{2\,h(t_{0})\,g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}-\frac{5\,g^{\prime\prime\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}{24\,g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})^{3}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(t_{0})}{8\,g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}\,.

(138)

This result can be generalized to the case where the function
h(t)
is not smooth. Consider
h(t)
discontinuous, with discontinuous derivatives, at the maximum
t_{0}
, i.e.,

h(t)=\begin{cases}\;h_{-}(t)\quad&t<t_{0}\,,\\[1.99997pt]
\;h_{+}(t)&t>t_{0}\,.\end{cases}

(139)

The main difference from the smooth case is that the leading term is proportional to the average
\frac{h_{-}(t_{0})+h_{+}(t_{0})}{2}
of the one-sided limits at
t_{0}
, rather than
h(t_{0})
, which is no longer well defined. Moreover, the discontinuity introduces half-integer power corrections in
N
, absent in the smooth case.

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}h(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t)}dt\;=\Big(1+\tfrac{\tilde{C}_{1/2}}{\sqrt{N}}+\tfrac{\tilde{C}_{1\vphantom{1/2}}}{N\vphantom{\sqrt{N}}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-3/2})\Big)\sqrt{\tfrac{2\pi}{-g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})\,N\,}}\;\tfrac{h_{-}(t_{0})+h_{+}(t_{0})}{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{Ng(t_{0})}\,.

(140)

The derivation follows the same steps as the smooth case, but with the integration domain split at
t_{0}
into
[t_{1},t_{0})
and
(t_{0},t_{2}]
, on each of which
h_{\pm}
is smooth. The discontinuity of
h(t)
at
t_{0}
breaks the left-right symmetry of the integrand, so odd Gaussian moments no longer cancel between the two halves. In particular, for odd
k_{2}
,

\int_{-\infty}^{0}N^{k_{1}}\tau^{k_{2}}\,e^{\frac{N}{2}g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})\tau^{2}}\,d\tau\;=\;-\frac{(k_{2}-1)!!}{\bigl(-g^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})\bigr)^{(k_{2}+1)/2}}N^{k_{1}-(k_{2}+1)/2}\,,\qquad(\text{with }k_{2}\text{ odd})\,,

and similarly for
(0,\infty)
, giving non-zero half-integer power corrections. The terms contributing to
\tilde{C}_{1/2}
, i.e., those with
k_{2}=2k_{1}+1
, are:

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0})

\displaystyle\quad(k_{1}=0,k_{2}=1)\,,
