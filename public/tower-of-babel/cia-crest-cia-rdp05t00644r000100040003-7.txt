wanted me to leave the NATO Command over there to become
the Director of Central Intelligence, I soon found that your
reputation as a great persuader was indeed well earned. A
week ago this very day, however, as I stood outside under a
magnificent warm, blue sky in Naples and turned over that
NATO Command, I began to question my judgment. Th^n I arrived
here today and find that this improbable time of year, this
absolutely equally magnificent weather, and I now know that
you are truly the great persuader. Seriously, Sir, I thank
you for your confidence in me. I assure you that I will
bend my every effort to fulfill the mandate you have given
me well. I also am confident that every man and woman of
the Intelligence Community will do the same- -together we
will endeavor to give you that objective intelligence which
you need for your decision making. Thank you.

(The President)

A long time ago at the Naval Academy I shared the
responsibility of being a Midshipman v;ith Stan Turner. I
didn't know him personally; or rather I knew him but he
didn't know me. He was the leader of our class. He was
superlative in everything he did in athletics, military
beaming and leadership capabilities, and academics. He was
a source of so much admiration among his classmates that
there was not a competitive feeling; nor was there one of
jealousy- -he was superb. Later he tried for Rhodes Scholar-
ship; so did I. He was selected by the committee; I was
not. I might say the committee made the right decision.

Not too long ago I was faced with the responsibility of
choosing a new Director for a Community that has been in-
vestigated and perhaps damaged by revelation of past mistakes--
a Community that inherently is divided, but which shares a
common responsibility, and a common purpose. A Community
that must face the most difficult possible conflicts between
openness and frankness and public scrutiny inherent in the
principle of a democracy with a mandatory requirement for
confidentiality in the collection of crucial intelligence
information and relationship with our allies and friends
throughout the world. I was faced with the responsibility of
choosing someone who could deal with these complications.

When I decided to ask Stan Turner to be the Director of
