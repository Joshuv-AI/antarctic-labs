Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

This project will improve the hydrodynamic performance of the undershot waterwheel turbine
with a flow concentrator designed by the Participant, using a computational fluid dynamics
study. The influence of different result parameters including the number of blades, turbine
profile, submerged depth, tip speed ratio, concentrator geometry and wave + current conditions
will be assessed to maximize power performance and minimize structural loads.

Summary of Research Results:

Task 1: The Participant will provide a baseline geometry of undershot water wheel turbine with
a flow concentrator geometry. Additionally, the Participant will provide operating conditions as
well as practical limits of the design parameters, including the maximum length of turbine and
flow concentrator geometry, practical distance between blade tip and flow concentrator,
submerged depth and the number of blades, and current and wave conditions.

Task 1 Results:

The FAU team provided necessary design inputs of the undershot water wheel turbine system
with a flow concentrator geometry to the NREL team. The inputs were used for CFD simulations
in the next task conducted by the NREL team. Figure 1 displays the system including the
undershot water wheel turbine with three different flow concentrator concepts used in this
project.

Task 2: The Contractor will run the computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations based on
the input conditions provided by the Participant. The runs will be performed on the Contractor’s
supercomputer, Eagle.

Task 2 Results:

The NREL team conducted both 2D and 3D CFD simulations of FAU’s water wheel turbine with
and without a flow concentrator geometry considering different design inputs. To ensure
numerical convergence solutions, both spatial and temporal resolutions were conducted. Based
on the convergence study, selected mesh and timestep size were used for further simulations
while balancing computational cost and solution accuracy. Figure 2 displays an example of mesh
resolution in 2D cases.

Task 3: The Contractor will post-process the CFD output data to quantify net power and loads
for provided simulation inputs. The Contractor will also use time-history CFD data to plot the
quantity of interests to inform the design performance.

Task 3 Results:

The medium grid resolution was selected for 3D CFD simulation model. Figure 4 shows a
comparison of velocity flowfield around the undershot water wheel turbine with and without a
vertical flow concentrator geometry. This flowfield is displayed at a cross-section below the
water surface. As one can be seen through the velocity contour, there are slightly different flow
behavior around the turbine due to the presence of the flow concentrator geometry. However, the
average turbine torque and its variation amplitude are not affected as shown in Figure 5.

The effect of different flow concentrator design concepts was also investigated turbine torque
output. As one can be seen, the vertical flow concentrator provides a higher averaged torque
value as well as larger and smoother peak torque. On the other hand, the horizontal flow
concentrator with different positions relative to the rotating turbine point (blue line) does not
provide better solution which we expect to have higher turbine performance.

Task 4: The Parties will work together to analyze the CFD output across different design
parameters. Using quantitative and qualitative metrics such as net power, loads, and flow contour
around the turbine and concentrator, the Parties will evaluate to inform any potential
improvements of the system.

Task 4 Results:

The Parties have worked together to analyze the CFD simulation outputs as shown in several
figures above. Through 3D CFD simulation, we observed high dynamics of blade-flow
interaction as well as entry splash, departure water pickup, water build-up, and blades-water
interference. Additionally, we also demonstrated hydraulic performance prediction using
different turbulent models. The result of this study is further utilized to design and improve the
power performance of this floating marine hydrokinetic turbine with a flow accelerator geometry
which help fluid flow accelerate through the concentrator flow geometry.

Task 5: The Parties will prepare a manuscript for a conference and/or a journal article as the
outcome of this project.

Task 5 Results:

Both the NREL and FAU team have been collaborating to draft a manuscript to submit for a
conference/journal based on the simulation results of this project.

Task 6: The Contractor will complete a post access report including simulation input and data at
the end of the project. The Participant will review, edit, and submit it to the Marine and
Hydrokinetic Data Repository (MHK DR) portal.
