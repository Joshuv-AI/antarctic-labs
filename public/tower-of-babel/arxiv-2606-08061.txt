Cheeger-type inequalities for the second largest spectral gap from 1 1 of the normalized Laplacian
Lies Beers, Raffaella Mulas, Jan Petr

Abstract
We study the second largest spectral gap from 1 1 of the normalized Laplacian of a graph, a quantity that appears in the literature in connection with random walks, expander graphs, and Ramanujan graphs. We relate it to the classical Cheeger and dual Cheeger constants, and we introduce a new Cheeger-type constant admitting a probabilistic interpretation in terms of two-step random walks. For this constant, we establish sharp inequalities analogous to the classical Cheeger inequalities.



1
Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph on
n
nodes with no isolated vertices, and let

0=\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq 2

denote the eigenvalues of its normalized Laplacian. Since the foundational work of Fan Chung
[
5
]
, these eigenvalues have been extensively studied and are known to encode important geometric and combinatorial properties of graphs. In particular, the classical Cheeger inequalities relate the eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}
to the Cheeger constant
h

[
5
]
, while the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
is linked to the dual Cheeger constant
\overline{h}
through the dual Cheeger inequalities
[
1
,
4
,
2
]
.

In
[
9
]
, the smallest spectral distance from
1
,

\varepsilon:=\min_{i\neq 1}|1-\lambda_{i}|

was investigated, and the sharp bound
\varepsilon\leq 1/2
was established for connected graphs on
n\geq 3
vertices. Here, we study instead the second largest spectral distance from
1
,

\tau:=\max_{i\neq 1}|1-\lambda_{i}|,

which controls the rate of convergence of a random walk on
G
to its stationary distribution, as explained in Remark
2.2
. This quantity also appears in Fan Chung’s book
[
5
, Chapters 1.5, 5, and 6]
, in relation to random walks and expander graphs.

Among other results, in Proposition
3.1
we show that

\tau=\max\{1-\lambda_{2},\lambda_{n}-1\},

and as a consequence we relate
\tau
to the classical Cheeger and dual Cheeger constants in Proposition
3.2
. We further introduce a new Cheeger-type constant
\tilde{h}
which admits an interpretation in terms of the probability of returning to a set of vertices after two steps (Definition
3.5
and Remark
3.6
) for which we establish the following sharp inequalities (Corollary
5.5
and Theorem
6.1
):

2\tilde{h}-1\leq\tau^{2}\leq\sqrt{1-(1-\tilde{h})^{2}}.

Our work is also related to Ramanujan graphs
[
12
,
13
]
. In fact, if
G
is
d
-regular, then the normalized Laplacian eigenvalues satisfy
\lambda_{i}=1-\mu_{i}/d
,
where
\mu_{i}
are the eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix. Since, by definition, a Ramanujan graph is a
d
-regular graph satisfying

\max_{i\,:\,\mu_{i}\neq\pm d}|\mu_{i}|\leq 2\sqrt{d-1},

Ramanujan graphs can equivalently be characterized as
d
-regular graphs such that

\max_{i\,:\,\lambda_{i}\neq 0,2}|1-\lambda_{i}|\leq\frac{2\sqrt{d-1}}{d}.

For non-bipartite connected graphs, this is equivalent to

\tau\leq\frac{2\sqrt{d-1}}{d}.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

, and we relate

2
Background

Fix a simple graph
G=(V,E)
on
n
nodes and
m
edges, with no isolated vertices, and let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
denote the vertices of
G
. The
adjacency matrix
of
G
is the
n\times n
matrix
A:=A(G)
with entries

A_{ij}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }v_{i}\sim v_{j}\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The
normalized Laplacian
of
G
is the matrix

L:=L(G):=\id-D^{-1}A,

where
\id
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix, and
D:=D(G):=\textrm{diag}(\deg v_{1},\ldots,\deg v_{n})
is the
degree matrix
of
G
. We denote the eigenvalues of
L
by

0=\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq 2.

Now, let
C(V)
denote the vector space of functions
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and, given
f,g\in C(V)
, let

\langle f,g\rangle:=\sum_{v\in V}\deg v\cdot f(v)\cdot g(v).

(2)

We can see then see the normalized Laplacian
L
as an operator
C(V)\rightarrow C(V)
such that

Lf(v)=f(v)-\frac{1}{\deg v}\sum_{w\sim v}f(w).

Moreover,
L
is self-adjoint with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, i.e.,

\langle Lf,g\rangle=\langle f,Lg\rangle\quad\forall f,g\in C(V).

In analogy with
[
9
]
(proof of Lemma 3), we note that the values
(1-\lambda_{1})^{2},\ldots,(1-\lambda_{n})^{2}
are precisely the eigenvalues of the matrix

M:=(\id-L)^{2}=(D^{-1}A)^{2},

(3)

whose entries are

M_{vu}=\sum_{w\in V}(D^{-1}A)_{vw}(D^{-1}A)_{wu}=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{N}(v)\cap\mathcal{N}(u)}\frac{1}{\deg v\,\deg w},\quad\text{ for }v,u\in V.

(4)

In particular,
\varepsilon^{2}
is the smallest eigenvalue of
M
, while
\tau^{2}
is the second largest. Moreover, similarly to
L
,
M
is also self-adjoint with respect to the weighted inner product in (
2
), implying that its eigenvalues can be characterized using the Courant–Fischer–Weyl min-max Principle via the Rayleigh Quotients

\textrm{RQ}(f):=\frac{\langle Mf,f\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle}=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\left(\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{N}(w)}f(v)\right)^{2}}{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)^{2}},

for functions
f\in C(V)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
. In particular,

\varepsilon^{2}=\min_{f\in C(V)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\RQ(f),

while

\tau^{2}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in C(V)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}:\\
\sum_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)=0\end{subarray}}\RQ(f).

(5)

Remark 2.1
.

The approach adopted here differs slightly from that in
[
9
]
. In particular, instead of working with the matrix
(D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2})^{2}
, here we consider
M=(D^{-1}A)^{2}
, together with the weighted inner product in (
2
). This allows us to work within the same framework as
L
, as they are self-adjoint with respect to the same inner product. Nevertheless, the characterization obtained above for
\varepsilon^{2}
is equivalent to that of Lemma 3 in
[
9
]
. The characterization of
\tau^{2}
can be derived analogously by observing that
\tau^{2}
is the second largest eigenvalue of
M
, while the eigenfunctions corresponding to the largest eigenvalue of
M
(which is
(1-\lambda_{1})^{2}=1
) are precisely the constant functions. Hence, by the Courant–Fischer–Weyl min-max Principle,
\tau^{2}
is obtained by maximizing the Rayleigh Quotient over all functions that are orthogonal to the constant functions with respect to the inner product in (
2
), namely over all
f\in C(V)
such that

\langle f,\mathbf{1}\rangle=\sum_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)=0.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
T:=D^{-1}A
. Then,

T_{vu}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\deg v}&\text{ if }v\sim u\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Therefore,
T
is the transition probability matrix of a random walk on
G
, while
M=T^{2}
is the two-step transition probability matrix of the random walk. In particular,
M_{vu}
in (
4
) is the probability that a random walk starting at
v
is at
u
after two steps. Moreover, since
\tau
is the absolute value of the second largest eigenvalue of
T
, it controls the rate of convergence of a random walk on
G
to its stationary distribution (see for instance Section 2.2 in
[
8
]
, Section 12.2 in
[
11
]
, or Section 9 in
[
2
]
).

We now recall the classical geometric quantities associated with
\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{n}
, namely the Cheeger and dual Cheeger constants, together with the corresponding inequalities relating them to
\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{n}
. To this end, we first introduce some preliminary notation.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
U,S\subseteq V
. We denote by

E(U,S):=\Bigl\{\{u,s\}\in E:u\in U,\ s\in S\Bigr\}

the set of edges between
U
and
S
, and we let

e(U,S):=|E(U,S)|.

Similarly, for
w\in V
, we define
E(w,S):=E(\{w\},S)
and we let
e(w,S):=|E(w,S)|
denote its cardinality. Moreover, we let
\bar{S}:=V\setminus S
denote the
complement
of
S
, and we let

\vol(S):=\sum_{v\in S}\deg v

be the
volume
of
S
. Finally, the
neighborhood
of
S
is defined as

\mathcal{N}(S):=\{w\in V:E(w,S)\neq\emptyset\}.

We can now define the Cheeger and dual Cheeger constants.

Definition 2.4
.

The
Cheeger constant
of
G
is

h:=h(G):=\min_{\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq V}\frac{e(S,\overline{S})}{\min\{\vol(S),\vol(\overline{S})\}}.

The
dual Cheeger constant
of
G
is

\bar{h}:=\bar{h}(G):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{partitions}\\
V=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}\sqcup S_{3}\,:\\
S_{1},S_{2}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\frac{2\cdot e(S_{1},S_{2})}{\vol(S_{1})+\vol(S_{2})}.

Remark 2.5
.

A set
S
realizing the Cheeger constant corresponds to a sparse edge cut
E(S,\overline{S})
which separates the graph into two parts of comparable volume. Thus, small values of
h
indicate that the graph is close to being disconnected. Moreover, we always have
0\leq h\leq 1
, with
h=0
if and only if
G
is disconnected.

Similarly, a partition
V=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}\sqcup S_{3}
realizing the dual Cheeger constant corresponds to a large set of edges between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, with relatively few edges inside
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. Thus, large values of
\bar{h}
indicate that a large portion of the graph is close to bipartite. Moreover, by Theorem 3.1 in
[
2
]
, since we are not admitting isolated vertices, we always have
0<\bar{h}\leq 1
, and
\bar{h}=1
if and only if
G
is bipartite.

The Cheeger inequalities
[
5
,
10
]
state that

1-\sqrt{1-h^{2}}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq 2h,

(6)

while the dual Cheeger inequalities
[
1
,
4
,
2
]
state that

2\overline{h}\leq\lambda_{n}\leq 1+\sqrt{1-\left(1-\overline{h}\right)^{2}}.

(7)

We conclude this section by introducing additional notation that will be used throughout the paper.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
U\subseteq V
. The
indicator function

\mathbbm{1}_{U}\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\mathbbm{1}_{U}(v)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }v\in U,\\
0,&\text{ if }v\notin U.\end{cases}

3
The quantities
\tau
and
\tilde{h}

In this section, we study basic properties of
\tau
and introduce the new Cheeger-type constant
\tilde{h}
. We again let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph on
n
nodes with no isolated vertices, and we let

0=\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq 2

denote the eigenvalues of its normalized Laplacian
L
. Recall that, in the Introduction, we defined

\tau:=\max_{i\neq 1}|1-\lambda_{i}|.

Moreover, in (
5
), we established the characterization

\tau^{2}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in C(V):\\
\sum_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)=0\end{subarray}}\RQ(f).

We also note that an estimate for
\tau
was obtained in
[
9
, Proposition 2]
, where it was shown that

\frac{1}{n-1}\leq\tau\leq 1.

We now characterize
\tau
as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

For every graph
G
,

\tau=\max\{1-\lambda_{2},\lambda_{n}-1\}.

Proof.

By definition of
\tau
, and since
0=\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq 2
, we have that

\tau=\max\left\{|1-\lambda_{2}|,\ldots,|1-\lambda_{n}|\right\}=\max\left\{|1-\lambda_{2}|,|1-\lambda_{n}|\right\}.

Moreover, since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=n
for all graphs, it follows that
\lambda_{n}>1
. In fact, if
\lambda_{n}\leq 1
, then
\lambda_{i}\leq 1
for all
i
, and therefore

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\lambda_{i}\leq n-1<n,

which is a contradiction. Hence,

\tau=\max\left\{|1-\lambda_{2}|,\lambda_{n}-1\right\}.

(8)

We now consider two cases.

•

Case 1:
\lambda_{2}\leq 1
. In this case,
|1-\lambda_{2}|=1-\lambda_{2}
, and therefore, by (
8
),

\tau=\max\{1-\lambda_{2},\lambda_{n}-1\}.

•

Case 2:
\lambda_{2}>1
. In this case,
|1-\lambda_{2}|=\lambda_{2}-1\leq\lambda_{n}-1
. Therefore, by (
8
),

\tau=\max\{|1-\lambda_{2}|,\lambda_{n}-1\}=\lambda_{n}-1.

Moreover, since
1-\lambda_{2}<0
in this case, it is also true that

\tau=\lambda_{n}-1=\max\{1-\lambda_{2},\lambda_{n}-1\}.

This proves the claim.

∎

Now, let
\widehat{h}:=\min\left\{h,1-\bar{h}\right\}
. Combining the Cheeger inequalities in (
6
)
with the dual Cheeger inequalities in (
7
), we can infer the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

For every graph,

1-\sqrt{1-\widehat{h}^{2}}\leq 1-\tau\leq 2\widehat{h},

or equivalently,

1-2\widehat{h}\leq\tau\leq\sqrt{1-\widehat{h}^{2}}.

The proof of Proposition
3.2
uses the following observations.

Remark 3.3
.

If
a\leq A
and
b\leq B
, then
\min\{a,b\}\leq a\leq A
and
\min\{a,b\}\leq b\leq B
, therefore

\min\{a,b\}\leq\min\{A,B\}.

Remark 3.4
.

If a function
f
is increasing on an interval
I
, then for all
a,b\in I
,

\min\{f(a),f(b)\}=f(\min\{a,b\}).

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Since the two statements are clearly equivalent, it suffices to prove the first one. By Proposition
3.1
,

\tau=\max\{1-\lambda_{2},\lambda_{n}-1\}
, implying that

1-\tau=\min\{\lambda_{2},2-\lambda_{n}\}.

Moreover, by (
7
), we have that

1-\sqrt{1-\left(1-\overline{h}\right)^{2}}\leq 2-\lambda_{n}\leq 2(1-\overline{h}).

(9)

By (
6
) and (
9
), together with Remark
3.3
, we infer that

1-\tau=\min\{\lambda_{2},2-\lambda_{n}\}\leq\min\{2h,2(1-\overline{h})\}=2\widehat{h}

and

1-\tau=\min\{\lambda_{2},2-\lambda_{n}\}\geq\min\left\{1-\sqrt{1-h^{2}},1-\sqrt{1-\left(1-\overline{h}\right)^{2}}\right\}.

Thus, it remains to show that

\min\left\{1-\sqrt{1-h^{2}},1-\sqrt{1-\left(1-\overline{h}\right)^{2}}\right\}=1-\sqrt{1-\widehat{h}^{2}},

This follows from Remark
3.4
, together with the fact that the function

f(x):=1-\sqrt{1-x^{2}}

is increasing on
[0,1]
, which implies that

\min\{f(h),f(1-\overline{h})\}=f(\min\{h,1-\overline{h}\})=f(\widehat{h}).

∎

Proposition
3.2
bounds
\tau
in terms of
\widehat{h}:=\min\left\{h,1-\bar{h}\right\}
, and therefore in terms of both
h
and
\bar{h}
. Our goal is instead to establish sharp inequalities for
\tau
(or
\tau^{2}
) involving a single geometric quantity. To this end, we introduce the following new Cheeger-type constant.

Definition 3.5
.

Given a nonempty set
S\subseteq V
, we let

\tilde{h}(S):=\frac{1}{\vol S}\cdot\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}=\frac{1}{\vol S}\cdot\sum_{w\in\mathcal{N}(S)}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}.

Moreover, we let

\tilde{h}:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq V:\\
\vol S\leq\vol V/2\end{subarray}}\tilde{h}(S).

Remark 3.6
.

The quantity
\tilde{h}(S)
becomes large when the vertices in
\mathcal{N}(S)
devote a large proportion of their degrees to edges connecting to
S
. Moreover,

\tilde{h}(S)=\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)}{\vol S}\cdot\frac{e(w,S)}{\deg w},

where
e(w,S)/\vol S
is the probability that a one-step random walk starting from
S
reaches
w
, assuming that the starting vertex in
S
is selected with probability proportional to its degree, while
e(w,S)/\deg w
is the probability of returning from
w
to
S
in one step. Therefore, the quantity
\tilde{h}(S)
can be interpreted as the probability that a random walk starting in
S
returns to
S
after two steps. This probabilistic interpretation suggests a deep connection between
\tilde{h}
and
\tau^{2}
in view of Remark
2.2
. Moreover, the Cheeger constant of
G
(Definition
2.4
) can be written as

h=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq V:\\
\vol S\leq\vol V/2\end{subarray}}h(S),

where

h(S):=\frac{e(S,\overline{S})}{\vol(S)}

is the probability that a one-step random walk starting from
S
leaves
S
, and therefore
1-h(S)
is the probability that a one-step random walk starting from
S
remains in
S
. Hence,
\tilde{h}
may be seen as a two-step analogue of
1-h
.

In Section
4
, we investigate several properties of
\tilde{h}
. Sections
5
,
6
and
7
are devoted to proving the following sharp inequalities:

2\tilde{h}-1\leq\tau^{2}\leq\sqrt{1-(1-\tilde{h})^{2}}.

4
Properties of
\tilde{h}

This section is devoted to investigating properties of
\tilde{h}
. We begin by proving the sharp upper bound
\tilde{h}\leq 1
, before deriving several lower bounds, discussing a few examples and posing two open questions. Also in this section, all graphs are assumed to be simple and to have no isolated vertices.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any graph,

\tilde{h}\leq 1,

with equality if and only if
G
is bipartite or disconnected.

Proof.

Let
S\subset V
be a nonempty subset such that
\vol S\leq\vol V/2
.
Then,

\displaystyle\RQ(\mathbbm{1}_{S})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\left(\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{N}(w)}\mathbbm{1}_{S}(v)\right)^{2}}{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\cdot e(w,S)^{2}}{\sum\limits_{w\in S}\deg w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vol S}\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}

\displaystyle=\tilde{h}(S).

Since
0\leq\RQ(f)\leq 1
for all functions
f\in C(V)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, it follows immediately that
\tilde{h}(S)\leq 1
, and consequently,
\tilde{h}\leq 1
.

Moreover,
\tilde{h}=1
if and only if there exists a nonempty set of vertices
S
such that
\vol S\leq\vol V/2
and
\RQ(\mathbbm{1}_{S})=1
. As
\mathbbm{1}_{S}\neq\mathbf{1}
, this can occur if and only if either the normalized Laplacian admits
0
as an eigenvalue with multiplicity at least
2
, in which case the graph
G
is disconnected, or the normalized Laplacian has eigenvalue
2
, in which case a connected component of
G
is bipartite. In the case where the graph is connected, it must therefore be bipartite, and the statement follows.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Proposition
4.1
is consistent with the probabilistic interpretation of
\tilde{h}
discussed in Remark
3.6
. In fact, since
\tilde{h}(S)
is the probability that a random walk starting in
S
returns to
S
after two steps, it is clear that
\tilde{h}(S)\leq 1
. Moreover, equality can occur only when the random walk returns to
S
after two steps with probability
1
, which corresponds precisely to disconnected or bipartite cases.

The main lower bound for
\tilde{h}
in this section is the following.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any graph,

\tilde{h}\geq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq V:\\
\vol S\leq\vol V/2\end{subarray}}\frac{\vol S}{\vol\mathcal{N}(S)}.

Remark 4.4
.

The quantity
\vol S/\vol\mathcal{N}(S)
in Proposition
4.3
becomes larger when the vertices in
\mathcal{N}(S)
devote a larger fraction of their degrees to edges connecting to
S
. This is in line with Remark
3.6
, and helps us understand the geometric intuition behind
\tilde{h}
. This quantity also appears in Lemma 6.2 in
[
5
]
, which shows that, if
G
is not a complete graph and
S\subseteq V
, then

\tau^{2}+(1-\tau^{2})\cdot\frac{\vol S}{\vol V}=1-(1-\tau^{2})\cdot\frac{\vol\overline{S}}{\vol V}>\frac{\vol S}{\vol\mathcal{N}(S)}.

In particular, if
\vol S\leq\vol V/2
, this implies that

\frac{\tau^{2}+1}{2}>\frac{\vol S}{\vol\mathcal{N}(S)},

therefore

\frac{\tau^{2}+1}{2}>\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq V:\\
\vol S\leq\vol V/2\end{subarray}}\frac{\vol S}{\vol\mathcal{N}(S)}.

Interestingly, Corollary
5.5
below will show that
(\tau^{2}+1)/2\geq\tilde{h}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

By Sedrakyan’s inequality, given real numbers
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
and positive real numbers
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
,

\sum_{i}\frac{u_{i}^{2}}{v_{i}}\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{i}u_{i}\right)^{2}}{\sum_{i}v_{i}}.

Therefore, given a nonempty set
S\subseteq V
,

\displaystyle\tilde{h}(S)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vol S}\cdot\sum_{w\in\mathcal{N}(S)}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\vol S}\cdot\frac{\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{N}(S)}e(w,S)\right)^{2}}{\sum_{w\in\mathcal{N}(S)}\deg w}

\displaystyle=\frac{\vol S}{\vol\mathcal{N}(S)},

implying that

\tilde{h}\geq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq V:\\
\vol S\leq\vol V/2\end{subarray}}\frac{\vol S}{\vol\mathcal{N}(S)}.

∎

Example 4.5
.

Consider a bipartite graph, and fix the sets
S
and
\overline{S}
that give the bipartition. Then,
\overline{S}=\mathcal{N}(S)
, and
\vol S=\vol\mathcal{N}(S)
. Therefore, by Proposition
4.3
,
\tilde{h}\geq 1
, while by Proposition
4.1
we have
\tilde{h}\leq 1
. This implies that
\tilde{h}=1
, and both bounds are sharp in this case.

Remark 4.6
.

If
G
is bipartite, then
\lambda_{2}\geq 2-\lambda_{n}=0
, implying that
1-\tau=\min\{\lambda_{2},2-\lambda_{n}\}=0
, and therefore
\tau=1
. By Example
4.5
, this implies that
\tilde{h}=\tau=1
for bipartite graphs.

Proposition
4.3
has the following corollaries.

Corollary 4.7
.

If there exists a set
S\subseteq V
such that
\vol S=\vol V/2
, then

\tilde{h}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
, we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{h}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{h}(S)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\vol S}{\vol\mathcal{N}(S)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\vol S}{\vol V}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}.

∎

Corollary 4.8
.

If
G
is
k
-regular and
n
is even, then

\tilde{h}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

The next proposition gives a lower bound for
\tilde{h}
whenever there exists a nonempty set
S\subseteq V
such that
\vol S\leq\vol V/2
and the vertices in
\mathcal{N}(S)
devote the same proportion of their degrees to edges connecting to
S
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Assume that there exist a nonempty set
S\subseteq V
such that
\vol S\leq\vol V/2
and a constant
\nu\in(0,1]
such that, for each
w\in\mathcal{N}(S)
,
\nu=e(w,S)/\deg w
. Then,

\tilde{h}\geq\nu.

Proof.

We have that

\tilde{h}\geq\tilde{h}(S)=\frac{1}{\vol S}\cdot\sum_{w\in\mathcal{N}(S)}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}=\frac{1}{\vol S}\cdot\nu\cdot\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{N}(S)}e(w,S)\right)=\nu.

∎

In spectral graph theory, it is an open problem to characterize all graphs satisfying

\lambda_{n}=\frac{\chi}{\chi-1},

and several classes of graphs for which equality holds are known, including bipartite graphs, complete graphs, and petal graphs
[
7
,
14
,
6
,
15
,
3
]
. The next corollary of Proposition
4.9
provides a lower bound for
\tilde{h}
for such graphs.

Corollary 4.10
.

If a graph has chromatic number
\chi
and is such that
\lambda_{n}=\chi/(\chi-1)
, then

\tilde{h}\geq\frac{1}{\chi-1}.

Proof.

By Theorem 3.4 in
[
6
]
, if
\lambda_{n}=\chi/(\chi-1)
, then for a fixed proper
\chi
-coloring of
G
with coloring classes
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\chi}
, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,\chi\}
and for every vertex
w
,

e(w,V_{i})=\begin{cases}\frac{\deg w}{\chi-1},&\text{ if }w\notin V_{i},\\
0,&\text{ if }w\in V_{i}.\end{cases}

Hence, if we fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,\chi\}
, then clearly
\vol V_{i}\leq\vol V/2
(since each coloring class is an independent set), and Proposition
4.9
for
\nu=1/(\chi-1)
gives

\tilde{h}\geq\frac{1}{\chi-1}.

∎

Remark 4.11
.

For bipartite graphs,
\chi=2
and
\lambda_{n}=2=\chi/(\chi-1)
. Hence, in this case, the bound in Corollary
4.10
is sharp.

Similarly, for the cycle graph on three vertices,
\chi=3
,
\lambda_{n}=3/2=\chi/(\chi-1)
, and one can check that
\tilde{h}=1/2
(cf. Example
4.13
below). Therefore, the bound in Corollary
4.10
is sharp also in this case.

The next result shows that
\tilde{h}(S)
is symmetric with respect to taking the complement whenever
S
and
\overline{S}
have the same volume.

Proposition 4.12
.

If a set
S\subseteq V
is such that
\vol S=\vol V/2
, then

\tilde{h}(S)=\tilde{h}(\overline{S}).

Proof.

Let
S
be such that
\vol S=\vol V/2
. In this case, also
\vol\overline{S}=\vol V/2
. Hence,

\displaystyle\tilde{h}(\overline{S})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,\overline{S})^{2}}{\deg w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\sum_{w\in V}\frac{(\deg w-e(w,S))^{2}}{\deg w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\sum_{w\in V}\biggl(\deg w-2e(w,S)+\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vol S}\biggl(\vol V-2\cdot\frac{\vol V}{2}+\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\vol S}\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}

\displaystyle=\tilde{h}(S).

∎

We now consider several examples illustrating the behavior of
\tilde{h}
. We begin by computing
\tilde{h}
for complete graphs.

Example 4.13
.

Consider the complete graph
K_{n}
on
n\geq 2
vertices, and let
S\subset V
with
|S|\leq n/2
. Then,

\displaystyle\tilde{h}(S)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{w\in V}e(w,S)^{2}}{(n-1)\cdot(n-1)|S|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|S|(|S|-1)^{2}+(n-|S|)\cdot|S|^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}|S|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|S|(|S|^{2}-2|S|+1)+(n-|S|)\cdot|S|^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}|S|}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2|S|^{2}+|S|+n|S|^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}|S|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|S|(n-2)+1}{(n-1)^{2}}.

If
n
is even, then
\tilde{h}(S)
is maximal when
|S|=n/2
. Hence,

\displaystyle\tilde{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{n(n-2)+2}{(n-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{(n-1)^{2}+1}{(n-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\biggl(1+\frac{1}{(n-1)^{2}}\biggr).

Furthermore, if
n
is odd, then
\tilde{h}(S)
is maximal if
|S|=(n-1)/2
. Hence, in this case,

\displaystyle\tilde{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{(n-1)(n-2)+2}{(n-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\biggl(1-\frac{n-3}{(n-1)^{2}}\biggr).

Summarizing,

\tilde{h}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\biggl(1+\frac{1}{(n-1)^{2}}\biggr),&\text{ if $n$ is even},\\
\frac{1}{2}\biggl(1-\frac{n-3}{(n-1)^{2}}\biggr),&\text{ if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

In particular, this illustrates that Corollary
4.8
does not hold if
n

5
Lower bounds for
\tau^{2}

This section is devoted to proving two sharp lower bounds for
\tau^{2}
. First, in Theorem
5.1
we establish a general lower bound that does not involve
\tilde{h}
. As a consequence, we then derive the sharp inequality in Corollary
5.5
which is expressed in terms of
\tilde{h}
. Again, all graphs are assumed to be simple and to have no isolated vertices.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every graph,

\tau^{2}\geq\max_{\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq V}\left(\frac{\vol V}{\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\vol S}\cdot\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}\right)-\frac{\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}\right).

Moreover, the inequality is sharp.

The proof of Theorem
5.1
is based on evaluating the Rayleigh Quotient of a function that is constant on
S
and on its complement. The beginning of the proof is analogous to the classical proof
[
5
]
of the upper bound in the Cheeger inequalities (
6
).

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Fix
\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq V
, let

\alpha:=\vol S/\vol\overline{S}
, and let
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be such that
f:=1
on
S
and
f:=-\alpha
on
\overline{S}
. Then,
\sum_{v\in V}\deg v\cdot f(v)=0
, implying that

\tau^{2}\geq\textrm{RQ}(f)=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\left(\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{N}(w)}f(v)\right)^{2}}{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)^{2}}.

(10)

Since
\alpha\vol\overline{S}=\vol S
, the denominator in (
10
) can be rewritten as

\sum\limits_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)^{2}=\vol S+\alpha^{2}\vol\overline{S}=(1+\alpha)\vol S.

Moreover, the numerator satisfies

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\left(\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{N}(w)}f(v)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\left(\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{N}(w)\cap S}1-\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{N}(w)\cap\overline{S}}\alpha\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\biggl(e(w,S)-\alpha e(w,\overline{S})\biggr)^{2}.

Note also that

\vol S=\sum_{v\in S}\deg v=\sum_{w\in V}e(w,S)

and, for all
w\in V
,

e(w,\overline{S})=\deg w-e(w,S).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\textrm{RQ}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\biggl(e(w,S)-\alpha e(w,\overline{S})\biggr)^{2}}{(1+\alpha)\vol S}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\biggl(e(w,S)-\alpha\left(\deg w-e(w,S)\right)\biggr)^{2}}{(1+\alpha)\vol S}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\biggl((1+\alpha)e(w,S)-\alpha\deg w\biggr)^{2}}{(1+\alpha)\vol S}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\biggl((1+\alpha)^{2}e(w,S)^{2}+\alpha^{2}(\deg w)^{2}-2\alpha\deg w(1+\alpha)e(w,S)\biggr)}{(1+\alpha)\vol S}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\biggl(\frac{(1+\alpha)^{2}e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}+\alpha^{2}\deg w-2\alpha(1+\alpha)e(w,S)\biggr)}{(1+\alpha)\vol S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V}\frac{(1+\alpha)e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w\cdot\vol S}+\sum_{w\in V}\frac{\alpha^{2}\deg w}{(1+\alpha)\vol S}-\sum_{w\in V}\frac{2\alpha e(w,S)}{\vol S}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\alpha)}{\vol S}\cdot\left(\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}\right)+\frac{\alpha^{2}\vol V}{(1+\alpha)\vol S}-2\alpha.

Now, observe that

1+\alpha=1+\frac{\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}=\frac{\vol\overline{S}+\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}=\frac{\vol V}{\vol\overline{S}}.

Hence,

\frac{\alpha^{2}\vol V}{(1+\alpha)\vol S}=\frac{\vol\overline{S}}{\vol V}\cdot\frac{(\vol S)^{2}}{(\vol\overline{S})^{2}}\cdot\frac{\vol V}{\vol S}=\frac{\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}=\alpha,

and similarly

\frac{(1+\alpha)}{\vol S}=\frac{\vol V}{\vol S\cdot\vol\overline{S}}.

This implies that

\tau^{2}\geq\textrm{RQ}(f)=\frac{\vol V}{\vol S\cdot\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\left(\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}\right)-\alpha.

(11)

This proves the first claim. To show that the inequality is sharp, assume that
G
is the complete graph. In this case,
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{n}=n/(n-1)
, implying that

\tau=\frac{1}{n-1}.

If we let
S=\{x\}
, then
\vol S=n-1
and
\vol\overline{S}=(n-1)^{2}
. Hence, (
11
) becomes

\tau^{2}\geq\frac{n(n-1)}{(n-1)^{3}}\cdot\left(\sum_{w\in V\setminus\{x\}}\frac{1}{n-1}\right)-\frac{n-1}{(n-1)^{2}}=\frac{n}{(n-1)^{2}}-\frac{n-1}{(n-1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{(n-1)^{2}}.

Equivalently,
\tau\geq 1/(n-1)
. Since we have shown that
\tau=1/(n-1)
in this case, the inequality is sharp.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

If
G
is bipartite, then
\tau=1
by Remark
4.6
. In this case, the sets
S
and
\overline{S}
that give the bipartition satisfy

\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}=\sum_{w\in\overline{S}}\frac{(\deg w)^{2}}{\deg w}=\vol\overline{S}.

Moreover, in this case
\vol S=\vol\overline{S}=\vol V/2
, hence (
11
) becomes

\tau^{2}\geq\frac{\vol V}{\vol S\cdot\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\vol\overline{S}-\frac{\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}=1,

or equivalently,
\tau\geq 1
. Hence, the inequality from Theorem
5.1
is sharp also in this case.

Remark 5.3
.

If
G
is disconnected, then
\tau=1
, since
\lambda_{2}=0
. In this case, there exists a set
S\subset V
of volume
\vol S\leq\vol V/2
that forms the vertex set of a connected component. In this case, Equation (
10
) becomes

\displaystyle\tau^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\vol V}{\vol S\cdot\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\biggl(\sum_{w\in S}\frac{(\deg w)^{2}}{\deg w}\biggr)-\frac{\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\vol S+\vol\overline{S}}{\vol\overline{S}}-\frac{\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}

\displaystyle=1

or equivalently,
\tau\geq 1
. Hence, the inequality from Theorem
5.1
is also sharp in this case.

Remark 5.4
.

The expression from the statement of Theorem
5.1
is symmetric in
S
and
\overline{S}
. To see this, consider
f
as defined in the proof, and define
f^{\prime}
by reversing the roles of
S
and
\overline{S}
. Then,

f^{\prime}=-\frac{f}{\alpha}.

Since
f
and
f^{\prime}
are multiples of one another, they have the same Rayleigh quotient. Hence,

\displaystyle\RQ(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{\vol V}{\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\vol S}\cdot\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S)^{2}}{\deg w}\right)-\frac{\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}

\displaystyle=\RQ(f^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\vol V}{\vol{S}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,\overline{S})^{2}}{\deg w}\right)-\frac{\vol\overline{S}}{\vol{S}}\right).

We are now ready to prove the following sharp lower bound for
\tau^{2}
in terms of
\tilde{h}
, which follows from Theorem
5.1
.

Corollary 5.5
.

For every graph,

\tau^{2}\geq 2\tilde{h}-1.

Moreover, the inequality is sharp.

Proof.

Fix a nonempty set
S\subseteq V
such that
\vol S\leq\vol V/2
, or equivalently,
\vol S\leq\vol\overline{S}
. Then, by Theorem
5.1
,

\tau^{2}\geq\frac{\vol V}{\vol\overline{S}}\cdot\tilde{h}(S)-\frac{\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}.

Now, let
\alpha:=\vol S/\vol\overline{S}
. Then,
\alpha\leq 1
and

\frac{\vol V}{\vol\overline{S}}=\frac{\vol\overline{S}+\vol S}{\vol\overline{S}}=1+\alpha.

Hence,

\tau^{2}\geq(1+\alpha)\tilde{h}(S)-\alpha=\tilde{h}(S)+\alpha(\tilde{h}(S)-1).

Now, the inequalities in the proof of Proposition
4.1
show that
\tilde{h}(S)\leq 1
for any
S
. Therefore,
\tilde{h}(S)-1\leq 0
, and together with the fact that
\alpha\leq 1
, this implies that

\tau^{2}\geq\tilde{h}(S)+\alpha(\tilde{h}(S)-1)\geq\tilde{h}(S)+\tilde{h}(S)-1=2\tilde{h}(S)-1.

Maximizing over all nonempty sets
S\subseteq V
with
\vol S\leq\vol V/2
gives

\tau^{2}\geq 2\tilde{h}-1.

By Remark
4.6
, equality holds for bipartite graphs, showing that the inequality is sharp.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

For the
m
-petal graph on
n=2m+1
vertices, with an even number
m
of petals, we have
\tilde{h}=5/8
by Example
4.14
, while
\tau^{2}=1/4
by
[
9
]
. Hence, the inequality in Corollary
5.5
is sharp also in this case.

Remark 5.7
.

If
G
is a connected non-bipartite
d
-regular Ramanujan graph, then by (
1
),

\tau^{2}\leq\frac{4(d-1)}{d^{2}}.

Together with Corollary
5.5
this implies that, in this case,

\tilde{h}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2(d-1)}{d^{2}}.

6
Upper bounds for
\tau^{2}

Also in this section, all graphs are assumed to be simple and to have no isolated vertices. The main upper bound for
\tau^{2}
is given by the following theorem, whose proof is presented in Section
7
.

Theorem 6.1
.

For every graph
G
,

\tau^{2}\leq\sqrt{1-(1-\tilde{h})^{2}}.

(12)

Moreover, the inequality is an equality if and only if
G
is bipartite or disconnected.

Applying Bernoulli’s inequality in a standard fashion, from Theorem
6.1
one can obtain a weaker upper bound on
\tau^{2}
without a square root that is a good approximation when
\tilde{h}
is close to
1
.

Corollary 6.2
.

For every graph without isolated vertices we have

\tau^{2}\leq 1-\frac{(1-\tilde{h})^{2}}{2}.

Moreover, the inequality is sharp if and only if
G
is disconnected or bipartite.

Proof.

Bernoulli’s inequality states that for any real numbers
r\geq 1
and
x\geq-1
, we have

(1+x)^{r}\leq 1+rx.

Setting
r=1/2
and
x=-(1-\tilde{h})^{2}
, and applying Bernoulli’s inequality to the upper bound on
\tau^{2}
from Theorem
6.1
, we get

\tau^{2}\leq\sqrt{1-(1-\tilde{h}^{2})}\leq 1-\frac{(1-\tilde{h})^{2}}{2},

(13)

proving the first claim.

If
\tau^{2}=1-(1-\tilde{h})^{2}/2
, then from (
13
) we have in particular
\tau^{2}=\sqrt{1-(1-\tilde{h}^{2})}
. By the equality case from Theorem
6.1
, we know that this equality holds if and only if
G
is disconnected or bipartite, that is, if and only if
\tilde{h}=1
. Therefore, for
\tau^{2}=1-(1-\tilde{h})^{2}/2
to hold, it is necessary that
\tilde{h}=1
.

On the other hand, if
\tilde{h}=1
, then
\tau^{2}=1
(by the equality case of Theorem
6.1
), and also
1-(1-\tilde{h})^{2}/2=1
. Therefore,
\tilde{h}=1
is both a necessary and sufficient condition for
\tau^{2}=1-(1-\tilde{h})^{2}/2
. Thus, the inequality from the statement is an equality if and only if
G
is disconnected or bipartite, as claimed.
∎

We remark that while the bound in Corollary
6.2
is weaker than the one in Theorem
6.1
, it is not uncommon to use an expression avoiding the square root in the Cheeger inequality

1-\sqrt{1-h^{2}}\leq\lambda_{2}.

Compare, for example, the quantity

h^{2}/2
in Theorem 2.2 in Chung’s book
[
5
]
,
with the quantity
1-\sqrt{1-h^{2}}

in Theorem 2.3 in the same book
[
5
]
.

7
Proof of Theorem
6.1

This section is dedicated to proving Theorem
6.1
.
Much of the proof of Theorem
6.1
uses similar techniques as one of the proofs of the lower bound of the Cheeger inequalities in (
6
), as presented for example by Luca Trevisan in
[
16
]
. However, our proof has several additional technicalities due to the form of the Rayleigh Quotient of the operator
M
.

First, we introduce a few results that will be helpful for the proof. We start with the following basic property of fractions.

Remark 7.1
.

Let
p,r\geq 0
and
q,s>0
. Then,

\min\biggl\{\frac{p}{q},\frac{r}{s}\biggr\}\leq\frac{p+r}{q+s}\leq\max\biggl\{\frac{p}{q},\frac{r}{s}\biggr\}.

Another ingredient is the following lemma, which is essentially Fact 5.2 from
[
16
]
, differing only in the inequality inside of the probability. Although the proof is almost identical, we reproduce it here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be random variables such that
\mathbb{P}(Y>0)=1
. Then,

\mathbb{P}\biggl(\frac{X}{Y}\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}X}{\mathbb{E}Y}\biggr)>0.

Proof of Lemma
7.2
.

By linearity of expectation, we see that

\mathbb{E}\biggl(X-\frac{\mathbb{E}(X)}{\mathbb{E}(Y)}\cdot Y\biggr)=\mathbb{E}(X)-\frac{\mathbb{E}(X)}{\mathbb{E}(Y)}\cdot\mathbb{E}(Y)=0.

Therefore,

\mathbbm{P}\biggl(X\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}(X)}{\mathbb{E}(Y)}\cdot Y\biggr)>0,

and the claim follows by dividing both sides of the internal inequality by
Y
, which we may do because
Y>0
almost surely.
∎

We are now ready to go through the proof of Theorem
6.1
, which is divided into several claims in order to make the argument easier to follow.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Recall that, by (
5
), we have the following characterization:

\tau^{2}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in C(V)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}:\\
\sum_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)=0\end{subarray}}\textrm{RQ}(f)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in C(V)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}:\\
\sum_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\left(\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{N}(w)}f(v)\right)^{2}}{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)^{2}}.

We first find a convenient function whose Rayleigh Quotient gives an upper bound to
\tau^{2}
, similarly to Lemma 4.5 in
[
16
]
.

Claim 1
.

There exists a function
f\in C(V)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
such that

\tau^{2}\leq\RQ(f),

satisfying

\vol{{\mathrm{supp}(f)}}\in\biggl(0,\frac{\vol V}{2}\biggr]\quad\text{ and }\quad 1\in\range(f)\subset[0,1].

Proof of Claim
1
.

Let
g\in C(V)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
be a function satisfying

\sum_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot g(w)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\tau^{2}=\mathrm{RQ}(g).

Notice that, as a consequence,
g
attains at least one positive and one negative value.

For any
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\mathrm{RQ}(g-c\cdot\mathbf{1})\geq\mathrm{RQ}(g)
. In fact, for
c\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\RQ(g-c\cdot\mathbf{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle g-c\cdot\mathbf{1},M(g-c\cdot\mathbf{1})\rangle}{\langle g-c\cdot\mathbf{1},g-c\cdot\mathbf{1}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle g,Mg\rangle+\langle-c\cdot\mathbf{1},M(-c\cdot\mathbf{1})\rangle}{\langle g,g\rangle+\langle-c\cdot\mathbf{1},-c\cdot\mathbf{1}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle g,Mg\rangle+c^{2}\langle\mathbf{1},M\mathbf{1}\rangle}{\langle g,g\rangle+c^{2}\cdot\langle\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\rangle}

\displaystyle\geq\min\biggl\{\frac{\langle g,Mg\rangle}{\langle g,g\rangle},\frac{c^{2}\langle\mathbf{1},M\mathbf{1}\rangle}{c^{2}\cdot\langle\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\rangle}\biggr\}

\displaystyle=\min\bigl\{\RQ(g),\RQ(\mathbf{1})\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\RQ(g),

where we have used Remark
7.1
for the inequality, and afterward used the fact that
1=\RQ(\mathbf{1})
is the largest eigenvalue of
M
.

We now pick
c
such that

\vol{\{v:g(v)-c\geq 0\}}\geq\frac{\vol V}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\vol{\{v:g(v)-c\leq 0\}}\geq\frac{\vol V}{2}.

To see that this can be done, first recall that
g
attains only finitely many values, say
g_{1}<\ldots<g_{k}
, which then satisfy

\vol{\{v:g(v)-g_{1}\geq 0\}}=\vol{V}\quad\text{and}\quad\vol{\{v:g(v)-g_{k}\leq 0\}}=\vol{V}.

Hence, there exists
i\in\{1\ldots,k\}
such that

\vol{\{v:g(v)-g_{i}\geq 0\}}\geq\frac{\vol V}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\vol{\{v:g(v)-g_{i+1}\leq 0\}}\geq\frac{\vol V}{2}.

If neither
g_{i}
nor
g_{i+1}
can be chosen as
c
, then

\vol\left\{v:g(v)-\frac{g_{i}+g_{i+1}}{2}<0\right\}<\frac{\vol V}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\vol{\left\{v:g(v)-\frac{g_{i}+g_{i+1}}{2}>0\right\}}<\frac{\vol V}{2},

which contradicts that these two volumes add up to
\vol{V}
, because
(g_{i}+g_{i+1}/2)
is not in the range of
g
. Hence,
g_{i}
or
g_{i+1}
can be chosen as
c
.

In particular, this choice of
c
also implies that

\vol{\{v:g(v)+c<0\}}\leq\frac{\vol V}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\vol{\{v:g(v)+c>0\}}\leq\frac{\vol V}{2}.

Set now
\tilde{g}:=g-c\cdot\mathbf{1}
,
\tilde{g}^{+}:=\max{\{\tilde{g},\mathbf{0}\}}
, and
\tilde{g}^{-}:=\max{\{-\tilde{g},\mathbf{0}\}}
, so that
\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}^{+}-\tilde{g}^{-}
. Then,

\vol{\mathrm{supp}(\tilde{g}^{+})}\leq\frac{\vol V}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\vol{\mathrm{supp}(\tilde{g}^{-})}\leq\frac{\vol V}{2}.

Furthermore, note that

\supp\bigl(\tilde{g}^{+}\bigr)\cap\supp\bigl(\tilde{g}^{-}\bigr)=\emptyset.

We now claim that at least one
\tilde{f}\in\{\tilde{g}^{+},\tilde{g}^{-}\}
satisfies
\tilde{f}\neq\mathbf{0}
and
\mathrm{RQ}(\tilde{g})\leq\mathrm{RQ}(\tilde{f})
.

First, note that
\tilde{g}\neq 0
since
g
attains at least two different values. Now,
if
\tilde{g}^{+}=0
, then
\RQ(\tilde{g})=\RQ(\tilde{g}^{-})
, proving the claim.
Analogously, if
\tilde{g}^{-}=0
, then
\RQ(\tilde{g})=\RQ(\tilde{g}^{+})
.

If both
\tilde{g}^{+}
and
\tilde{g}^{-}
are nonzero, then

\displaystyle\RQ(\tilde{g})

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\tilde{g}^{+}-\tilde{g}^{-},M(\tilde{g}^{+}-\tilde{g}^{-})\rangle}{\langle\tilde{g}^{+}-\tilde{g}^{-},\tilde{g}^{+}-\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\tilde{g}^{+},M\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle+\langle\tilde{g}^{-},M\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle-\langle\tilde{g}^{+},M\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle-\langle\tilde{g}^{-},M\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle}{\langle\tilde{g}^{+},\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle+\langle\tilde{g}^{-},\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle-2\langle\tilde{g}^{+},\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\tilde{g}^{+},M\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle+\langle\tilde{g}^{-},M\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle-\langle\tilde{g}^{+},M\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle-\langle\tilde{g}^{-},M\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle}{\langle\tilde{g}^{+},\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle+\langle\tilde{g}^{-},\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle}

\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{\leq}\frac{\langle\tilde{g}^{+},M\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle+\langle\tilde{g}^{-},M\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle}{\langle\tilde{g}^{+},\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle+\langle\tilde{g}^{-},\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle}

\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{\leq}\max\biggl\{\frac{\langle\tilde{g}^{+},M\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle}{\langle\tilde{g}^{+},\tilde{g}^{+}\rangle},\frac{\langle\tilde{g}^{-},M\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle}{\langle\tilde{g}^{-},\tilde{g}^{-}\rangle}\biggr\}

\displaystyle=\max\bigr\{\RQ\bigl(\tilde{g}^{+}\bigr),\RQ\bigl(\tilde{g}^{-}\bigr)\bigr\}.

In
(i)
, we used the fact that all entries of
M
are nonnegative by Equation (
4
), and so are all entries of
\tilde{g}^{+}
and
\tilde{g}^{-}
. Furthermore, in
(ii)
, we used Remark
7.1
.

Finally, we set

f:=\frac{\tilde{f}}{\max{\{\tilde{f}(v):v\in V\}}},

and we note that this is well-defined. The function
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
then satisfies

\vol{{\mathrm{supp}(f)}}\in\left(0,\frac{\vol V}{2}\right]\quad\text{ and }\quad 1\in\range(f)\subset[0,1],

as well as the chain of (in)equalities

\tau^{2}=\RQ(g)\leq\RQ(g-c\cdot\mathbf{1})\leq\RQ(\tilde{f})=\RQ(f).

This proves Claim
1
.
∎

Next, we use
f
from Claim
1
to find a set
S\subset V
such that
\vol{S}\in(0,\vol{V}/2]
and

\mathrm{RQ}(f)\leq\sqrt{1-(1-\tilde{h}(S))^{2}}.

(14)

To this end, we introduce a random subset
S_{t}
of
V
, following Chapter 5 of Trevisan
[
16
]
.
Due to the more complicated form of our Rayleigh quotient, our proof then deviates from Trevisan’s argument and becomes more involved.

Claim 2
.

There is a set
S
satisfying

\RQ(f)\leq\sqrt{1-\left(1-\tilde{h}(S)\right)^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\vol S\leq\frac{\vol V}{2}.

Proof of Claim
2
.

To show the existence of such
S
, we consider the random set

S_{t}:=\{v:f(v)\geq t\},

where
t
is a random variable distributed as
t^{2}\sim U[0,1]
. We thus have

\mathbb{P}(v\in S_{t})=\mathbb{P}(f(v)\geq t)=\mathbb{P}(f(v)^{2}\geq t^{2})=f(v)^{2}.

Moreover, for two vertices
u,v\in V
, we have

\mathbb{P}(u,v\in S_{t})=\min\{f(u)^{2},f(v)^{2}\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{P}((u\in S_{t})\lor(v\in S_{t}))=\max\{f(u)^{2},f(v)^{2}\}.

(15)

As
\max\{f(v):v\in V\}=1
by Claim
1
, we also have that
\mathbb{P}(S_{t}=\emptyset)=0
. Moreover, since
\mathbb{P}(t=0)=0
, almost surely
S_{t}\subset\mathrm{supp}(f)
. Therefore, since
\vol{{\mathrm{supp}(f)}}\in(0,\vol{V}/2]
by Claim
1
, it follows that, almost surely,
\vol{S_{t}}\in(0,\vol{V}/2]
. Moreover, since

\vol S_{t}=\sum_{v\in V}\deg v\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(v),

we have that

\mathbb{E}\left(\vol S_{t}\right)=\sum_{v\in V}\deg v\cdot\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{S_{t}}(v)\right)=\sum_{v\in V}\deg v\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(v\in S_{t}\right)=\sum_{w\in V}\deg v\cdot f(v)^{2}.

Now, to prove (
14
), we first bound its left-hand side by rewriting the Rayleigh quotient in a form that is suitable for the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality as follows.

\displaystyle\RQ(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\frac{1}{\deg w}\left(\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{N}(w)}f(v)\right)^{2}}{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(u)f(v)}{\deg w}}{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\min\{f(u),f(v)\}\cdot\max\{f(u),f(v)\}}{\deg w}}{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\min\{f(u),f(v)\}^{2}}{\deg w}\biggr)^{1/2}\cdot\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\max\{f(u),f(v)\}^{2}}{\deg w}\biggr)^{1/2}}{\sum\limits_{w\in V}\deg w\cdot f(w)^{2}},

(16)

where in (
16
) we applied the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the vectors

\biggl(\frac{\min\{f(u),f(v)\}}{\sqrt{\deg w}}\biggr)_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\biggl(\frac{\max\{f(u),f(v)\}}{\sqrt{\deg w}}\biggr)_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}.

We can now use (
15
), together with linearity of expectation, to further bound
\RQ(f)
by rewriting (
16
) as follows:

\displaystyle\RQ(f)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathbb{P}(u,v\in S_{t})}{\deg w}\biggr)^{1/2}\cdot\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathbb{P}(u\in S_{t}\vee v\in S_{t})}{\deg w}\biggr)^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}(\vol S_{t})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathbb{E}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{u,v\in S_{t}\}})}{\deg w}\biggr)^{1/2}\cdot\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathbb{E}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{u\in S_{t}\vee v\in S_{t}\}})}{\deg w}\biggr)^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}(\vol S_{t})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{u,v\in S_{t}\}}}{\deg w}\bigr)\biggr)^{1/2}\cdot\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{u\in S_{t}\vee v\in S_{t}\}}}{\deg w}\bigr)\biggr)^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}(\vol S_{t})}.

(17)

Now, note that

\mathbbm{1}((u\in S_{t})\lor(v\in S_{t}))=\mathbbm{1}(u,v\in S_{t})+\mathbbm{1}((u\in S_{t})\dot{\lor}(v\in S_{t})),

where
\dot{\lor}
denotes the exclusive or, and that

\sum\limits_{u,v\in\mathcal{N}(w)}\mathbbm{1}(u,v\in S_{t})=\left(\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{N}(w)}\mathbbm{1}(v\in S_{t})\right)^{2}=e(w,S_{t})^{2}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}}\mathbbm{1}(u\in S_{t}\dot{\vee}v\in S_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}}\mathbbm{1}((u\in S_{t})\land(v\notin S_{t}))+\sum\limits_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}}\mathbbm{1}((u\notin S_{t})\land(v\in S_{t}))

\displaystyle={2}e(w,S_{t})\cdot e(w,\bar{S_{t}}).

The right-hand side of (
17
) can thus be further rewritten in the following way:

\displaystyle\RQ(f)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S_{t})^{2}}{\deg w}\bigr)\biggr)^{1/2}\cdot\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S_{t})^{2}+2e(w,S_{t})\cdot e(w,\overline{S_{t}})}{\deg w}\bigr)\biggr)^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}(\vol S_{t})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S_{t})^{2}}{\deg w}\bigr)\biggr)^{1/2}\cdot\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S_{t})^{2}+2e(w,S_{t})(\deg w-e(w,S_{t}))}{\deg w}\bigr)\biggr)^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}(\vol S_{t})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S_{t})^{2}}{\deg w}\bigr)\biggr)^{1/2}\cdot\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{w\in V}(\frac{-e(w,S_{t})^{2}}{\deg w}+2e(w,S_{t}))\bigr)\biggr)^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}(\vol S_{t})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S_{t})^{2}}{\deg w}\bigr)\biggr)^{1/2}\cdot\biggl(-\mathbb{E}(\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S_{t})^{2}}{\deg w})+2\cdot\mathbb{E}(\vol S_{t})\biggr)^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}(\vol S_{t})}.

(18)

We now define two random variables

X_{t}\coloneqq\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S_{t})^{2}}{\deg w}\quad\text{ and }Y_{t}\coloneqq\vol S_{t},

and we note that
\mathbb{P}(Y_{t}>0)=1
, since we have seen that
\vol{S_{t}}\in(0,\vol{V}/2]
almost surely. Using this notation, we can rewrite (
18
) to obtain

\displaystyle\RQ(f)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\biggl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(X_{t})\biggr)^{1/2}\cdot\biggl(-\mathbb{E}(X_{t})+2\cdot\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})\biggr)^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{t})^{2}}{\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})^{2}}+2\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{t})}{\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\left(1-\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{t})}{\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})}\right)^{2}}.

By Lemma
7.2
, there is some
t_{0}\in[0,1]
such that

\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{t})}{\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})}\leq\frac{X_{t_{0}}}{Y_{t_{0}}}.

(19)

Using (
19
) together with the fact that the function
x\mapsto\sqrt{1-(1-x)^{2}}
is increasing on the interval
[0,1]
, we thus immediately get

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-\left(1-\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{t})}{\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1-\left(1-\frac{X_{t_{0}}}{Y_{t_{0}}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\vol S_{t_{0}}}\sum_{w\in V}\frac{e(w,S_{t_{0}})^{2}}{\deg w}\Bigr)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-(1-\tilde{h}(S_{t_{0}}))^{2}}.

This proves the existence of
S=S_{t_{0}}
satisfying (
14
) and
\vol S\leq\vol V/2
.
∎

Combining Claims
1
and
2
together with the fact that the function
x\mapsto\sqrt{1-(1-x)^{2}}
is increasing on the interval
[0,1]
, we have

\tau^{2}\leq\RQ(f)\leq\sqrt{1-\left(1-\tilde{h}(S)\right)^{2}}\leq\sqrt{1-\left(1-\tilde{h}\right)^{2}},

concluding the proof of the upper bound on
\tau^{2}
.

In the remainder of the proof, we analyze the equality case.

Claim 3
.

The inequality from (
12
) is an equality if and only if
G
is bipartite or disconnected.

Proof of Claim
3
.

If the inequality is sharp, then the Cauchy–Schwarz bound in (
16
) must itself be attained sharply. This occurs if and only if, for every
w\in V
and every pair
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}
, either

\min\{f(u),f(v)\}=t\cdot\max\{f(u),f(v)\}\quad\text{or}\quad t\cdot\min\{f(u),f(v)\}=\max\{f(u),f(v)\},

for some
t\geq 0
. In particular, if
u=v
, then necessarily
f(u)=f(v)
, which implies
t=1
and thus

f(u)=f(v)\text{ for all }w\in V\text{ and all }(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}(w)^{2}.

(20)

If
G
is disconnected, then
\tilde{h}=\tau^{2}=1
, and hence the bound in (
12
) is an equality.

Therefore, we may assume that
G
is connected.
Fix a vertex
v_{0}\in V
. Observe that, by (
20
), all vertices in the neighborhood
\mathcal{N}(v_{0})
necessarily assume a common function value; we denote this value by
a_{1}
. Moreover, the vertices in the neighborhood of
\mathcal{N}(v_{0})
, which in particular includes
v_{0}
itself, must assume another common function value; we denote this value by
a_{0}
.

This reasoning can be extended inductively to the entire graph. Let
S_{0}
denote the set of vertices at an even distance from
v_{0}
, and let
S_{1}
denote the set of vertices at an odd distance from
v_{0}
.
By construction, every vertex in
S_{0}
has at least one neighbor in
S_{1}
and vice versa, since
v_{0}
has at least one neighbor in
S_{1}
, and every vertex
w\neq v_{0}
has a neighbor
v
satisfying
d(v,v_{0})=d(w,v_{0})-1.
One obtains that all vertices in
S_{0}
must attain the common function value
a_{0}
, and analogously, all vertices in
S_{1}
attain the common function value
a_{1}
.

Recall that the function
f
attains at least two function values by construction (one being equal to zero), from which it follows that
a_{0}\neq a_{1}
. Furthermore, by Equation (
20
), no vertex can have a neighbor in
S_{0}
and a neighbor in
S_{1}
.

We conclude that
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
are independent sets, and that
G
is bipartite, with bipartition
(S_{0},S_{1})
.

Conversely, if
G
is disconnected or bipartite, then
\tau^{2}=\tilde{h}=1
, in which case Equation (
12
) is an equality.
∎

This concludes the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
