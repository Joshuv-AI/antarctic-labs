Project Overview
As datasets for science and security research missions continue to grow in size, it has become
critical to provide scalable tools to semi-automatically explore these datasets to identify regions
of interest, outliers, and structure. Prior to this project, the majority of scalable clustering
techniques in the literature focused on low-dimensional, Euclidean data, which is but a narrow
subset of the billion-scale and beyond problems currently facing researchers. This project took
inspiration from HDBSCAN (Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with
Noise), a popular density-based clustering algorithm that mines clusters from a minimum
spanning tree (MST) of the complete weighted metric graph induced by the points’ pairwise
distances, and generalized it to a scalable, distributed memory software capability. This
capability is entitled ClaMS: Clustering at Massive Scale and is available as open-source
software [3]. As realizing the complete metric graph of a billion-scale cloud of points is
infeasible, we developed methods to sample sparse, structure-preserving subgraphs via K
nearest neighbor graph (KNNG) construction and a novel hierarchical K-centering algorithm. In
practice, these methods produce a disconnected graph, which prompted us to identify the
metric forest completion (MFC) problem of approximating a true MST from such a subgraph by
approximately optimally identifying edges to connect the subgraph components. We produced
learning-augmented approximation algorithms solving MFC. The team combined these
methods with a novel approximate distributed MST capability to implicitly approximate the
MST of a complete metric graph at the billion scale and furthermore developed a novel
distributed generalization of the HDBSCAN dendrogram mining algorithm to scale this entire
clustering pipeline. We have applied ClaMS to several billion-scale high-dimensional and non-
Euclidean data problems, including machine learning model embeddings, biological sequence
data, and graphs. In parallel, we developed a numerical linear algebra technique that applies
sparse, randomized dimension-reducing transforms to graph matrices that produce
logarithmically sized, learning-free embeddings of spectral embeddings of the graph vertices.
We also developed an advanced version of this technique that, at the cost of additional
randomness and a larger required memory footprint, allows for the maintenance of these
embeddings as the graph evolves over time – all while provably maintaining cluster separation
in the embedding space. We have tested these techniques on billion scale synthetic graphs and
released them as the powersqueeze software capability [4]. These techniques address the need
for metric-agnostic, billion-scale clustering capabilities that allow scientists and researchers to
perform data analysis on large, complex data sources at a vastly reduced energy expenditure
compared to neural and large language model approaches.

Mission Impact
ClaMS has already been applied to meet science and national security mission needs at LLNL
and in the US government sponsor ecosystem. For instance, we have applied our clustering
techniques with a novel outlier dendrogram traversal technique to an antibody clustering
problem of high interest to the national biosecurity mission, publication pending. Furthermore,
both ClaMS and powersqueeze have been deployed on intelligence sponsor systems of interest
and are in the early stages of adoption for national security missions.
