 ABSTRACT

This report summarizes the reactor analysis work performed by Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL)
for Antares Nuclear Incorporated’s R1 Mark-1 heat pipe reactor design. The work was performed under a
collaboration through the Department of Energy GAIN Nuclear Energy Voucher program. The purpose of
this work is to perform an independent reactor analysis of the Antares reactor design and, where possible,
compare ORNL’s results with the results obtained by Antares. The overarching goal is to provide Antares
with an independent review and calculation of their design, thereby contributing to Antares’ mission to
further their reactor design concept. A report with all ORNL technical results and proprietary details was
provided to Antares Nuclear Incorporated separately; the present report provides a summary of the work
completed, and all proprietary information is omitted. The independent reactor analysis was performed
using both the SCALE code system for neutronics analysis and Flownex for thermal hydraulics analysis.

 v

 1. SCOPE OF WORK

The scope of work for CRADA No. NFE-25-10665 was as follows. Antares Nuclear Inc., designated as
the “Participant,” provided Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), designated as the “Contractor,” with
the design specifications of the R1 Mark-1 heat pipe reactor (i.e., 3D geometric description, material
compositions and densities, and operational parameters). The Contractor used their unique expertise and
simulation tools to analyze the reactor and compare the results of their analyses to those calculated by the
Participant. Key results that were calculated include steady-state and time-dependent keff (criticality)
calculations, reactivity coefficients, power distribution, peak and average fuel temperature, and fuel
isotopic composition with burnup. In addition, the Contractor performed shielding calculations to inform
the Participant in the shielding design and any need for further design optimizations. The Contractor also
assessed the performance of the reactor utilizing the system specifications delivered by the Participant.

In the course of this work, the Contractor provided feedback on the design as well as input on design
optimization to improve upon the extensive design work already performed by the Participant.

The work scope was divided into three tasks. Each task was coordinated between the Participant and
Contractor through weekly teleconference calls. Working documents and input/output files were shared
through the agreed upon secure file transfer system Globalscape EFT.

The first task was entitled “Initial System Modeling and Steady State Analysis.” The Contractor worked
with the Participant to understand the reactor design and learn from the previous analysis performed by
the Participant. The Contractor and Participant developed a list of design parameters to evaluate,
including keff, reactivity coefficients, power distribution, and peak/average fuel temperature. The
Contractor performed independent calculations and compared evaluations with previous work performed
by the Participant. A draft report was delivered from Contractor to Participant on June 27, 2025, which
was the completion deliverable for Task 1.

The second task was entitled “Shielding and Depletion Analysis and Final Assessment.” The Contractor
and Participant developed a list of design parameters to evaluate, including fuel lifetime, burnup-
dependent fuel isotopic composition, dose maps, and activation of vessel and structural components. The
Contractor performed independent calculations and compared evaluations with previous work performed
by the Participant. This task was completed in concert with Task 3.

The third task was entitled “Final CRADA Report.” The Contractor provided the Participant a near-final
draft of a technical report entitled “Reactor Analysis of the Antares R1 Mark-1 Conceptual Reactor
Design” on October 31, 2025, and is in the process of uploading inputs and outputs of their models as
required by the CRADA scope of work. The Participant provided feedback to the Contractor on the
contents of both this report and the comprehensive technical report. Both are to be finalized by November
30, 2025.

 2. PROJECT OUTCOMES

All tasks in the Statement of Work were completed successfully, and all deliverables were provided to
Antares Inc. within the extended project deadline. Note that a no-cost extension was granted to extend the
scheduled completion date to January 1, 2026. The 60-day extension was necessary due to unanticipated
delays during the comparative assessment of the Contractor’s and Participant’s neutronics models, as
described below.

 1

Further discussion of ORNL’s analysis approach and findings is given below. This includes discussion of
the main analysis focuses—neutronics, thermal, depletion, shielding, and activation—as defined in the
Statement of Work.

The analysis codes used by ORNL for this work are as follows.
 • SCALE/CSAS-Shift: neutronic analysis—including criticality, flux distribution, and reactor
 physics parameter calculations
 • SCALE/Omnibus-Shift: detailed depletion analyses using high-fidelity geometry representation
 • SCALE/MAVRIC-Shift and SCALE/Denovo: shielding and dose rate analyses using hybrid
 discrete ordinates and Monte Carlo methods
 • SCALE/ORIGEN: activation analyses—including spatial nuclide decay chain calculations
 • Flownex: thermal analyses relying on core-scale and unit cell heat transfer calculations through
 the reactor and surroundings

During the course of the neutronics analyses and communications with Antares, it was determined that
Antares used the theoretical UCO fuel density instead of the expected as-fabricated density assumed by
ORNL. Consequently, Antares’ and ORNL’s neutronics models differed, so the results from Antares and
ORNL are not directly comparable. Despite this difference, ORNL proceeded with analyses and
nevertheless compared its results with the Antares results. Overall, reasonable agreement between the
Antares and ORNL results was obtained for various parameters, which provides confidence in the
Antares-performed analyses.

Regarding the shielding analyses, it was established that Antares’ analyses were conceptual and thus used
approximate methods and assumptions, so the ORNL values could not be directly compared with the
Antares values. ORNL proceeded to analyze the present R1 Mark-1 design for the shielding cases that
were analyzed by Antares. Results for the neutron activation of the heat pipes and the tungsten shield
were also obtained.

The ORNL thermal analyses produced fuel and vessel temperatures within 20 °C of Antares’ predictions
during nominal steady-state operation, which constitutes excellent agreement and affirms Antares’
nominal steady-state temperature calculations. The ORNL failed heat pipe analysis determined that the
TRISO fuel temperature safety limit was satisfied even under a highly conservative assumption of several
coincident heat pipe failures during steady-state operation. ORNL also confirmed Antares’ finding that no
fuel temperature increase is expected under a passive decay heat transient scenario in which all heat pipes
are assumed to fail. The ORNL thermal analyses relied on standalone thermal physics calculations, but
coupling to neutron kinetics could be readily implemented in future work to enable confirmatory analysis
of design basis accident scenarios, to provide useful input to the design of the reactor protection system,
and to facilitate analysis of anticipated transient without scram scenarios.

The independent ORNL analyses of the Antares R1 Mark-1 design proved valuable in many respects. The
Antares model was independently reviewed, and technical feedback was provided to Antares. Overall
reasonable agreement between the neutronics results were obtained. The results of ORNL’s thermal
analyses were also in very good agreement with the Antares results, thereby also providing confirmation
of the Antares analyses.
