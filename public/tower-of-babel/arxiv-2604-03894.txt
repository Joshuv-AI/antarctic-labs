On normal forms of gradient Ricci 4-solitons
Amir Babak Aazami

Abstract
. In this note we analyze the normal form of the operator R ^ + 1 2 ​ H ^ \hat{R}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{H} of a gradient Ricci 4-soliton in [ 5 ] . In particular, we show that the curvature operator R ^ \hat{R} of the Koiso–Cao soliton inherits this normal form. By [ 10 ] , this yields a normal form for the curvature operator of the Koiso–Cao soliton relative to the space of algebraic Kähler curvature operators.



1.
Introduction

The concept of a
normal form
for a Riemannian manifold
[
2
,
14
]
is motivated by a well known fact from linear algebra, namely, that on an inner product space
(V,\langle{\,},{}\rangle)
, a self-adjoint linear map
T\colon V\longrightarrow V
is completely determined by the critical points and values of its quadratic form
v\mapsto\langle{Tv},{v}\rangle
restricted to the unit sphere. The relevance of this fact for Riemannian geometry is that the quadratic form of the curvature operator
\hat{R}\colon\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}
of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is (up to sign) precisely the sectional curvature: For any orthonormal pair
x,y\in T_{p}M
,

x\wedge y\mapsto\langle{\hat{R}(x\wedge y)},{x\wedge y}\rangle=\underbrace{\,\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(x,y,x,y)\,}_{\text{$-\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(x\wedge y)$}}.

Because the sectional curvature
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
determines the curvature tensor
\text{Rm}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, a natural question arises: When is
\hat{R}
determined by
just
the critical points and values of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
(relative to some class of algebraic curvature operators)? Here the latter is to be thought of as a smooth function
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}\colon\text{Gr}_{2}(T_{p}M)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
on the Grassmannian of 2-planes at
T_{p}M
. If so, then we say that the curvature operator has a “normal form” at
p\in M
; this is a pointwise condition.

Space forms, locally conformally flat manifolds, any Riemannian submanifold of a space form with a flat normal bundle — all of these have normal forms. By
[
10
]
, so do Kähler 4-manifolds with positive-definite Ricci tensor (relative to the space of algebraic Kähler curvature operators). But the most famous instances of normal forms are the class of
Einstein
curvature operators in dimension 4, because here the normal form is derived from the fact that
\hat{R}
commutes with the Hodge star operator
*
:

\text{Ric}=\lambda g\iff*\hat{R}=\hat{R}*.

Hence
\hat{R},*
have a common orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. This fact, together with the splitting of
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}\oplus\Lambda^{-}
into the self-dual
(\Lambda^{+})
and anti-self-dual
(\Lambda^{-})
eigenspaces of
*
, and the fact that a 2-plane
P
is a critical point of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
if and only if
\hat{R}P\in\text{span}\{P,*P\}
, was used to derive an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}\subseteq T_{p}M
all of whose corresponding 2-planes
e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
are critical points of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, with respect to which

(1)

\displaystyle\hat{R}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{0}&B_{0}\\
B_{0}&A_{0}\end{bmatrix}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743ptA_{0}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743ptB_{0}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{diag}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}).

Here the
\lambda_{i}
are critical values of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, and the
\mu_{i}
are not critical values but can be derived from them (with
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}=0
by the algebraic Bianchi identity). Furthermore, two Einstein operators with the same critical points and values are necessarily pointwise isometric; hence, within the Einstein class, the operator is determined up to orthogonal change by this data.

The purpose of this note is to analyze another very interesting and recent example of a normal form in dimension 4. Namely,
[
5
]
showed that a normal form also exists for
gradient Ricci 4-solitons
,

(2)

\displaystyle\text{Ric}+\text{Hess}f=\lambda g,

although not for the curvature operator
\hat{R}
itself, but rather for the modified operator

\hat{S}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\hat{R}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{H}\colon\Lambda^{2}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2}
, where
H\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{Hess}f\raisebox{0.39993pt}{\,${\scriptstyle\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}$\,}g
is given by

\displaystyle\langle{\hat{H}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})},{e_{k}\wedge e_{l}}\rangle\!\!

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=

\displaystyle\!\!H(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\text{Hess}f(e_{i},e_{l})\,g_{jk}+\text{Hess}f(e_{j},e_{k})\,g_{il}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt-\,\text{Hess}f(e_{i},e_{k})\,g_{jl}-\text{Hess}f(e_{j},e_{l})\,g_{ik}.

In
[
5
, Remark 2.6]
, it was asked whether
\hat{R}
inherits the normal form of
\hat{S}
. We show that in at least one instance, the answer is yes:

Theorem
.

The curvature operator of the Koiso–Cao soliton on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#(-\mathbb{CP}^{2})
inherits the normal form of
\hat{S}
. As a consequence, the curvature operator of the Koiso–Cao soliton has a normal form relative to the space of algebraic Kähler curvature operators.

The Koiso–Cao soliton
[

,
4
]
will be analyzed in Theorem
1

2.
Overview of the normal form of
\hat{S}=\hat{R}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{H}

First, let
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}=\{\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3},\partial_{4}\}
arise from normal coordinates centered at
p\in M
, so that
\text{Hess}f(e_{i},e_{j})\big|_{p}=f_{ij}\big|_{p}
and hence

(3)

\displaystyle H_{ijkl}\big|_{p}=f_{il}\delta_{jk}+f_{jk}\delta_{il}-f_{ik}\delta_{jl}-f_{jl}\delta_{ik}\,\big|_{p}.

What makes the normal form of
\hat{S}
particularly interesting is that it is “Einstein-like,” namely, the normal form arises from the fact that
\hat{S}
commutes with
*
,

*\hat{S}=\hat{S}*,

and so the normal form basis
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}\subseteq\Lambda^{2}(T_{p}M)
of critical 2-planes is derived just as it was for
\hat{R}
in
[
2
,
14
]
in the Einstein case (once again, a 2-plane
P
will be a critical point of this quadratic form if and only if
\hat{S}P\in\text{span}\{P,*P\}
, by the same proof as in
[
14
]
). Of course, the quadratic form here is not the sectional curvature
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, but rather that of
\hat{S}
:

\displaystyle e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\mapsto\langle{\hat{S}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})},{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}}\rangle\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\langle{\hat{R}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})},{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle{\hat{H}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})},{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})-f_{ik}\delta_{jl}-f_{jl}\delta_{ik}.

We would like to investigate what
\hat{S}
’s normal form says about
\hat{R}
. To do so, let us use the
Hodge basis

\{\xi_{1}^{+},\xi_{2}^{+},\xi_{3}^{+},\xi_{1}^{-},\xi_{2}^{-},\xi_{3}^{-}\}
, where

\Big\{\underbrace{\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\pm e_{3}\wedge e_{4})\,}_{\text{$\xi_{1}^{\pm}$}},\underbrace{\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{3}\pm e_{4}\wedge e_{2})\,}_{\text{$\xi_{2}^{\pm}$}},\underbrace{\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{1}\wedge e_{4}\pm e_{2}\wedge e_{3})\,}_{\text{$\xi_{3}^{\pm}$}}\Big\}

comprise the self-dual and anti-self-dual eigenvectors of
*

(
*\xi_{i}^{\pm}=\pm\xi_{i}^{\pm}
)
. With respect to this basis,

\hat{R}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{W}^{+}+\frac{\text{scal}}{12}I&K\\
K^{t}&\hat{W}^{-}+\frac{\text{scal}}{12}I\end{bmatrix}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\hat{H}=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\Delta f}{2}I&C\\
C^{t}&-\frac{\Delta f}{2}I\end{bmatrix},

where
I
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix,
scal
is the scalar curvature of
g
at
p
,
\hat{W}^{\pm}
are the self-dual and anti-self dual portions of the Weyl curvature operator
\hat{W}
that leave invariant the
\pm 1
-eigenspaces of
*
, and

-\frac{1}{2}K=C\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{-f_{11}-f_{22}+f_{33}+f_{44}}{2}&f_{14}-f_{23}&-f_{13}-f_{24}\\
-f_{14}-f_{23}&\frac{-f_{11}+f_{22}-f_{33}+f_{44}}{2}&f_{12}-f_{34}\\
f_{13}-f_{24}&-f_{12}-f_{34}&\frac{-f_{11}+f_{22}+f_{33}-f_{44}}{2}\end{bmatrix}\cdot

(See
[
5
, Lemma 2.3]
, though note that in the latter the opposite sign convention for

{\scriptstyle\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\bigcirc$\cr$\displaystyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\textstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}{\mathbin{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcirc$\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\wedge$\cr}}}{}}}

is being used.) When
\hat{S}=\hat{R}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{H}
is expressed with respect to this Hodge basis, we have

(4)

\displaystyle\hat{S}=\begin{bmatrix}A+B&O\\
O&A-B\end{bmatrix}\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377pt\hat{R}=\begin{bmatrix}A+B+\frac{\Delta f}{4}I&-\frac{1}{2}C\\
-\frac{1}{2}C^{t}&A-B+\frac{\Delta f}{4}I\end{bmatrix},

where
A\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
and
B\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{diag}(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
. (If
\hat{S}
had been expressed with respect to
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
directly, then
\hat{S}=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}
, similarly to (
1
).)
With these preliminaries established, we now explore some properties of this
\hat{S}
-normal form basis
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
and its relation to
\hat{R}
.

3.
Properties of the normal form of
\hat{S}=\hat{R}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{H}

Unfortunately, the existence of
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
does not in general yield any topological information that was not otherwise known directly from the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula for
\chi(M)
in dimension 4 (even though a normal form-argument was used in deriving the Hitchin-Thorpe inequality in
[
9
]
, it was not essential; see Section
5
below). Having said that, here is one case in which
\hat{S}
’s normal form basis does yield topological information:

Proposition 1
.

If the normal form
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
for
\hat{S}
is “pure”, meaning that the
e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
are eigenvectors of
\hat{S}
, then
\tau(M)=0
.

Proof.

As we saw above, the normal form basis
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
yields

\hat{S}=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptA=\text{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptB=\text{diag}(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}).

In terms of these, the signature is

\tau(M)=\frac{1}{3\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{3}b_{i}\Big(a_{i}+\frac{\Delta f}{4}-\frac{\text{scal}}{12}\Big)dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

(For the derivation of this formula, see (
10
) below.) But if the
e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
are eigenvectors of
\hat{S}
, then
b_{1}=b_{2}=b_{3}=0
.
∎

(The Koiso–Cao soliton on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#(-\mathbb{CP}^{2})
has
\tau=0
.) Rather, the more interesting question is whether the normal form for
\hat{S}
is sufficient to yield a normal form for
\hat{R}
, as was posed in
[
5
]
. Here something can be said, though once again in a special case. First, let us recall that, with respect to our normal form basis
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
,

*(e_{1}\wedge e_{2})=e_{3}\wedge e_{4}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt*(e_{1}\wedge e_{3})=e_{4}\wedge e_{2}\hskip 7.22743pt,\hskip 7.22743pt*(e_{1}\wedge e_{4})=e_{2}\wedge e_{3}.

Using (
3
), and setting
e_{ij}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
, a direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\hat{H}(e_{12})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-(f_{11}+f_{22})e_{12}-f_{23}e_{13}-f_{24}e_{14}-f_{14}e_{42}+f_{13}e_{23},

\displaystyle\hat{H}(e_{13})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-(f_{11}+f_{33})e_{13}-f_{23}e_{12}+f_{34}e_{14}+f_{12}e_{42}-f_{14}e_{34},

\displaystyle\hat{H}(e_{14})\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-(f_{11}+f_{44})e_{14}-f_{24}e_{12}-f_{34}e_{13}+f_{12}e_{23}+f_{13}e_{34}.

This allows us to determine when the basis
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
will also be critical for the sectional curvature
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
:

Proposition 2
.

Let
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
be the normal form basis for
\hat{S}
as in
[
5
]
, and set
P_{1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}
. Then the following are equivalent:

i.

P_{1}
is a critical point of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
.

ii.

f_{13}=f_{14}=f_{23}=f_{24}=0
.

iii.

\text{Ric}(P_{1},*P_{1})=0
.

The analogous statements hold cyclically for
P_{2}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{1}\wedge e_{3}
and
P_{3}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=e_{1}\wedge e_{4}
.
Furthermore, if
\mathscr{O}_{i}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
\hat{R}(P_{i})
onto
\operatorname{span}\{P_{i},*P_{i}\}^{\perp}
, then

\displaystyle|\mathscr{O}_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\bigl(f_{13}^{2}+f_{14}^{2}+f_{23}^{2}+f_{24}^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle|\mathscr{O}_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\bigl(f_{12}^{2}+f_{14}^{2}+f_{23}^{2}+f_{34}^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle|\mathscr{O}_{3}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\bigl(f_{12}^{2}+f_{13}^{2}+f_{24}^{2}+f_{34}^{2}\bigr).

Hence

(5)

\displaystyle|\mathscr{O}_{1}|^{2}+|\mathscr{O}_{2}|^{2}+|\mathscr{O}_{3}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}f_{ij}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}\emph{\text{Ric}}(e_{i},e_{j})^{2}.

Proof.

Because
\hat{S}(P_{1})=a_{1}P_{1}+b_{1}(*P_{1})
, the 2-plane
P_{1}
is critical for
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
if and only if
\hat{H}(P_{1})
has no component in
\operatorname{span}\{e_{13},e_{14},e_{42},e_{23}\}
; this is ii. The equivalence with iii. follows from (
2
), which yields
\text{Ric}(e_{i},e_{j})=-f_{ij}
for
i\neq j
. The formulas for
|\mathscr{O}_{i}|^{2}
follow by inspection, and summing them shows that every off-diagonal entry appears exactly twice.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
be as in Proposition
2
. Then the following are equivalent:

i.

P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
are all critical for
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
.

ii.

\text{Hess}f
is diagonal with respect to
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
.

iii.

Ric
is diagonal with respect to
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
.

Under these conditions,
\hat{R}
admits a normal form in the same frame.

In other words, the off-diagonal Ricci tensor is the exact obstruction to the normal form basis
\{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\}
of
\hat{S}
being a normal form basis for
\hat{R}
. Interestingly, in at least one well known case there is no such obstruction:

Corollary 2
.

The Koiso–Cao soliton on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#(-\mathbb{CP}^{2})
satisfies Corollary
1
. Thus its curvature operator admits a normal frame in the same frame.

Proof.

This is proved in Theorem
1
below.
∎

Short of Corollary
1
, one would need some knowledge of the
second
derivatives of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, too:

Proposition 3
.

Suppose that some
P_{i}
in Proposition
2
is critical for
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
. Then so is
*P_{i}
, and
\hat{R}
is determined by the two critical values
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(P_{i}),\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(*P_{i})
together with the Hessian of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
at
P_{i}
.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Theorem 9]
, if
P_{i},*P_{i}
are both critical 2-planes of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, then
\hat{R}
can be determined from the critical values
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(P_{i}),\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}(*P_{i})
, and the Hessian of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
at
P_{i}
(this is true at any point of an oriented Riemannian 4-manifold). Thus we need only show that
*P_{i}
is critical for
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
. But if
P_{i}
is critical for
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, then the same off-diagonal
\text{Hess}f
entries that obstruct
P_{i}
also obstruct
*P_{i}
(e.g., the obstruction set for
P_{1}=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}
and
*P_{1}=e_{3}\wedge e_{4}
is the same four entries
f_{13},f_{14},f_{23},f_{24}
; similarly with the others). It follows that
*P_{i}
is likewise critical for
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
.
∎

(Thus whenever one pair
P_{i},*P_{i}
is critical for
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
, the combination of the
\hat{S}
-normal form with the Hessian of
\text{sec}_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}
at
P_{i}
recovers
\hat{R}
completely. In this sense, the
\hat{S}
-normal form plus one second-variation datum plays the role that a genuine normal form for
\hat{R}
would play.) We close this section by placing
\hat{S}
in the classical framework of
[
15
,
16
]
; in particular, we show that the critical pairs
\{P_{i},*P_{i}\}
are intrinsic Grassmannian slices of a canonical kernel of
\hat{S}
. Set
E_{i}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{span}\{P_{i},*P_{i}\}
and
G\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{Gr}_{2}(T_{p}M)
. Since
P_{i},*P_{i}
are critical for
\hat{S}
, we know that

\hat{S}(P_{i})=a_{i}P_{i}+b_{i}(*P_{i})\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\hat{S}(*P_{i})=b_{i}P_{i}+a_{i}(*P_{i}).

In particular,

(\hat{S}-a_{i}I-b_{i}*)(E_{i})=0.

Hence
E_{i}\subseteq\ker(\hat{S}-a_{i}I-b_{i}*)
, and therefore

\{\pm P_{i},\pm*P_{i}\}=G\cap E_{i}\subseteq G\cap\ker(\hat{S}-a_{i}I-b_{i}*).

Moreover,
G\cap E_{i}=\{\pm P_{i},\pm(*P_{i})\}
because any unit bivector of the form

is decomposable only when

. Thus, if the eigenvalues

of

are both simple, then
\ker(\hat{S}-a_{i}I-b_{i}*)=E_{i}
, so that

G\cap\ker(\hat{S}-a_{i}I-b_{i}*)=\{\pm P_{i},\pm*P_{i}\}.

Without this simplicity hypothesis, the kernel may be larger, but the pair
\{P_{i},*P_{i}\}
always sits canonically inside it.

4.
The Spectrum of
\hat{S}=\hat{R}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{H}

In the Hodge basis, we saw in (
4
) that

\hat{S}=\begin{bmatrix}S_{+}&0\\
0&S_{-}\end{bmatrix},\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptS_{+}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=A+B\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377ptS_{-}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.07639pt}{$\cdot$}}=A-B.

Since

\displaystyle\hat{W}^{\pm}=S_{\pm}+\Bigl(\frac{\Delta f}{4}-\frac{\text{scal}}{12}\Bigr)I\hskip 14.45377pt,\hskip 14.45377pt\text{scal}+\Delta f=4\lambda,

we also have

This makes several familiar structures visible as spectral patterns of

.

Proposition 4
.

At any point of a gradient Ricci
4
-soliton:

i.

If

is Kähler and the orientation is the complex orientation, then the spectrum of

is

\Bigl\{\frac{\emph{\text{scal}}}{2}-\lambda,\ \frac{\emph{\text{scal}}}{4}-\lambda,\ \frac{\emph{\text{scal}}}{4}-\lambda\Bigr\}\cdot

In particular, the self-dual block has a

eigenvalue pattern.

ii.

If
W^{+}=0
, then
S_{+}=\bigl(\frac{\emph{\text{scal}}}{3}-\lambda\bigr)I
; if
W^{-}=0
, then
S_{-}=\bigl(\frac{\emph{\text{scal}}}{3}-\lambda\bigr)I
.

Proof.

For i., on a Kähler surface in the complex orientation the eigenvalues of
\hat{W}^{+}
are given by
[
6
]
:

\Big\{\frac{\text{scal}}{6},\ -\frac{\text{scal}}{12},\ -\frac{\text{scal}}{12}\Big\}\cdot

Adding the scalar shift from (

) gives the stated spectrum. Part ii. follows immediately from (
7
).
∎

The quantity

|S_{+}|^{2}-|S_{-}|^{2}=4\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}b_{i}

\int_{M}(|S_{+}|^{2}-|S_{-}|^{2})\,dV_{g}=0.

has the same

4

for Kähler curvature operators with positive-definite Ricci tensor. Of course,
[

is a statement about
\hat{R}

\hat{S}
-normal form basis

On the open dense regular set

Proof.

For the properties of the
U(2)
-invariant orthonormal frame (

[

]

U(2)
-invariant function

\text{Hess}f
is diagonal in
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}

\text{Hess}f

J

\hat{S}

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}

\omega=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}+e_{3}\wedge e_{4}=\sqrt{2}\,\xi_{1}^{+},

\operatorname{span}\{\xi_{2}^{+},\xi_{3}^{+}\}=\Lambda^{2,0}\oplus\Lambda^{0,2}

\hat{H}

5.
The Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula

In this final section, we compute the Euler characteristic and signature using the
\hat{S}
-normal form; we will show that it does not in general yield any new topological information (for this and the signature formula below, see, e.g.,
[
3
, p. 371]
):

\displaystyle\chi(M)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\text{tr}\Big[\Big(A+B+\frac{\Delta f}{4}I\Big)^{\!2}-\frac{1}{2}CC^{t}+\Big(A-B+\frac{\Delta f}{4}I\Big)^{\!2}\,\Big]dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\text{tr}\Big[2\Big(A+\frac{\Delta f}{4}I\Big)^{\!2}+2B^{2}-\frac{1}{2}CC^{t}\Big]dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\bigg[2\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(a_{i}+\frac{\Delta f}{4}\Big)^{\!2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{3}b_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}(CC^{t})\bigg]dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

However,
\text{tr}(CC^{t})
simplifies to

\text{tr}(CC^{t})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}c_{ij}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(f_{ii}-\frac{\Delta f}{4}\Big)^{\!2}+2\sum_{i<j}f_{ij}^{2}=\Big|\underbrace{\text{Hess}f-\frac{\Delta f}{4}g}_{\text{$\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{0.75346pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-0.75346pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathring{\text{Hess}}f$}}\Big|^{2},

so that the Euler characteristic can be written as

(9)

\displaystyle\chi(M)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(a_{i}+\frac{\Delta f}{4}\Big)^{\!2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}b_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|\mathring{\text{Hess}}f|^{2}\bigg]dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

Next, we compute the signature, for which we will need the self-dual and anti-self-dual portions (
6
) of the Weyl curvature operator. The Thom-Hirzebruch formula for the signature then gives

(10)

\displaystyle\tau(M)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\int_{M}(|\hat{W}^{+}|^{2}-|\hat{W}^{-}|^{2})dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{12\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(a_{i}+b_{i}+\frac{\Delta f}{4}-\frac{\text{scal}}{12}\Big)^{\!2}

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(a_{i}-b_{i}+\frac{\Delta f}{4}-\frac{\text{scal}}{12}\Big)^{\!2}\bigg]dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{3\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{3}b_{i}\Big(a_{i}+\frac{\Delta f}{4}-\frac{\text{scal}}{12}\Big)dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.

Combining (
9
) and (
10
),

\displaystyle\chi(M)-\frac{3}{2}\tau(M)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(a_{i}+\frac{\Delta f}{4}\Big)^{\!2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}b_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|\mathring{\text{Hess}}f|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 36.135pt-2\sum_{i=1}^{3}b_{i}\Big(a_{i}+\frac{\Delta f}{4}-\frac{\text{scal}}{12}\Big)\bigg]dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(a_{i}+\frac{\Delta f}{4}-b_{i}\Big)^{\!2}

\displaystyle\hskip 36.135pt-\frac{1}{4}|\mathring{\text{Hess}}f|^{2}+\frac{\text{scal}}{6}\underbrace{(b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3})}_{0}\bigg]dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}},

where
b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}=R_{3412}+R_{4213}+R_{2314}=0
via the algebraic Bianchi identity. But this is not helpful; indeed, from (

(\text{Ric}+\text{Hess}f)-\frac{\Delta f}{4}g=\lambda g-\frac{\Delta f}{4}g\hskip 14.45377pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.45377pt\mathring{\text{Hess}}f=-\mathring{\text{Ric}},

(11)

\displaystyle\chi(M)-\frac{3}{2}\tau(M)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\Big(|\hat{W}^{-}|^{2}+\frac{\text{scal}^{2}}{48}-\frac{1}{4}|\mathring{\text{Ric}}|^{2}\Big)dV_{\scalebox{0.6}{$g$}}.
