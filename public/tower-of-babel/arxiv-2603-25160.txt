The Ptolemy-Alhazen problem with source at infinity
Masayo Fujimura, Matti Vuorinen

Abstract
. We study the well-known Ptolemy-Alhazen problem on reflection of light at the surface of a spherical mirror in the case when the source of light is very far from the mirror.

1.
Introduction

The well-known
Ptolemy–Alhazen problem reads
[
S
]
,
[
GBL
, p.1010]
: "Given a light source and a spherical mirror, find the point on the mirror where the light
will be reflected to the eye of an observer." The long history of this problem is described in
[
S
,
FMV2
,
W
]
. The problem is of current interest for instance in the design of
computer vision of drones, in the study of GPS signal transmission, in many scattering
problems of electromagnetic signals (e.g. radar signals), acoustics, and in various ray-tracing problems of
astrophysics
[
ATR
,
DSGGH
,
LE
,
M
,
DG1
,
DG2
]
. We have proved that this problem is equivalent
to the question of finding a formula for the so called triangular ratio metric of geometric function theory
and also studied the problem in the case of quartic mirrors
[
FHMV
,
FMV1
,
FMV2
,
HKV
]
.

Since the publication of our work
[
FHMV
]
, our solution has been implemented and validated in astrophysics applications in
[
DG1
,
DG2
]
and its performance compared to other methods. V.H. Almeida Jr., one of the authors of
[
DG1
,
DG2
]
,
has sent us an email
[
D
]
where he pointed out the need for the solution of the problem in the case of the source at
infinity. A few days after the receipt of his email we informed him about the solution of this
problem and gave the solution in Theorem
3.1
below. The solution
is given by a fourth degree polynomial equation which one can solve with symbolic
computation.

2.
Preliminaries

For the reader’s benefit we recall here some of our earlier
work
[
FHMV
,
HKV
]
.

The triangular
ratio metric
s_{G}
of a given domain
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is defined as
follows for
z_{1},z_{2}\in G

[
HKV
]

s_{G}(z_{1},z_{2})=\sup_{z\in\partial G}\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|}{|z_{1}-z|+|z-z_{2}|}\,.

By compactness, this supremum is attained at some point
w\in\partial G\,.
If
G
is convex, it is simple to see that
w
is the point of
contact of the boundary with an ellipse, with foci
z_{1},z_{2}\,,
contained in

G\,.
Now for the case
G=\mathbb{B}^{2}
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{B}^{2}\,
, if the extremal point is
w\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{2}\,,
then

(2.1)

s_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|}{|z_{1}-w|+|z_{2}-w|}\,.

This definition also makes sense for the domain
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{2},
we call the cases

\mathbb{B}^{2}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{2}

the interior and exterior problems,
resp.
The connection between the triangular ratio
distance
and the Ptolemy-Alhazen interior problem is now clear: if the unit circle is a reflecting mirror and
z_{1}
is the light source and
z_{2}
is the observer, then the point
w
is the point of reflection.
We also see that an ellipse with foci
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
through
the point
w
is internally tangent to the unit circle.

2.2
.

Ellipse eccentricity.

Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{B}^{2}
and
w\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{2}
be the extremal point of (
2.1
). Then the focal sum of the ellipse is

c=|z_{1}-w|+|z_{2}-w|=|z_{1}-z_{2}|/s_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})\,.

Let
\lambda,\sigma
be the lengths of the major and minor semiaxes of the ellipse. Then

\lambda=c/2\,,\quad\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{c^{2}-|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}}

and the eccentricity equals

\sqrt{\frac{\lambda^{2}-\sigma^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}=\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{\sigma}{\lambda}\right)^{2}}=s_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})\,.

In conclusion,
s_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})
is equal to the eccentricity of the maximal ellipse in
{\mathbb{B}^{2}}
with foci
z_{1},z_{2}\,.

Figure 1
.
If the unit circle is a mirror, then a light signal from the source
z_{1}
to the observer at
z_{2}
is reflected at the point
w.
Moreover,
s_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})
is equal to the eccentricity of the maximal ellipse.

The main result of
[
FHMV
]
,
Theorem
2.3
, gives a polynomial equation of degree four that yields the
reflection point on the unit circle.
The solution of the quartic equation is given by Cardano’s formula which is
available in standard symbolic computation software. We also study the Ptolemy-Alhazen exterior
problem for
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{2},
and in this case the formula for the reflection point, if such a point exists,
is the same as for the interior problem
[
FHMV
, Rmk 2.7]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{B}}^{2},
the point
w
in (
2.1
) is given as a
solution of the equation

(2.4)

\overline{z_{1}}\overline{z_{2}}w^{4}-(\overline{z_{1}}+\overline{z_{2}})w^{3}+(z_{1}+z_{2})w-z_{1}z_{2}=0\,.

2.5
.

Remark.
The roots of equation
(
2.4
) have been studied in detail
in
[
FHMV
]
. The root
w
at the extremal point for (
2.1
) is called the minimizing root. For instance, it is known that the minimizing root need not be unique and that some of the roots of (
2.4
) may be off
\partial{\mathbb{B}^{2}}.

A computer algorithm for finding
the minimizing root and a formula for
s_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})
based on this root is given in
[
FHMV
]
.

Corollary 2.6
.

For
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{B}^{2},
we have

s_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|}{|z_{1}-w|+|z_{2}-w|}

where
w\in\partial{\mathbb{B}^{2}}
is the minimizing root of
(
2.4
) .

3.
Quartic equation

Assume that light rays reach the unit circle from the right side,
parallel to the real axis.

Figure 2
.
Light rays reach the unit circle from the right side.

Let
f=re^{i\theta}
be the observation point,
where we can assume
r>1
and
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}

from symmetry.

Let
w
be the reflection point and
\widetilde{w}

with
\widetilde{w}=w+1
.
Note that
w
and
\widetilde{w}
are points on a line
parallel to the real axis.

Figure 3
.
The light ray reflects at the point
w
on the unit circle
and reaches the point
f
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The reflection point
w
is given as a solution of the equation

(3.2)

re^{-i\theta}w^{4}-w^{3}+w-re^{i\theta}=0.

Proof.

From the reflection property, the following holds

\measuredangle(0,w,\widetilde{w})=\measuredangle(f,w,0).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\arg\dfrac{\widetilde{w}-w}{0-w}=\arg\dfrac{0-w}{f-w}

holds and

\displaystyle\arg\dfrac{\widetilde{w}-w}{-w}-\arg\dfrac{-w}{f-w}

\displaystyle=\arg\dfrac{w-\widetilde{w}}{w}\cdot\dfrac{w-f}{w}

\displaystyle=\arg\dfrac{(w-(1+w))(w-f)}{w^{2}}=\arg\dfrac{f-w}{w^{2}}=0.

The last equality implies that
\dfrac{f-w}{w^{2}}
is a real number.
Since this complex conjugate is also real, we have

\frac{f-w}{w^{2}}=\frac{\overline{f}-\overline{w}}{\overline{w}^{2}}.

Hence, we have

\overline{w}^{2}(re^{i\theta}-w)=w^{2}(re^{-i\theta}-\overline{w}).

Since
w
is a point on the unit circle and satisfies
w\overline{w}=1
,
the above equation can be written as

re^{-i\theta}w^{4}-w^{3}+w-re^{i\theta}=0.

∎

3.3
.

Remark.
Cardano’s formula gives
the four roots of equation (
3.2
).
Under the assumptions here, we can choose the root that can
be written as
e^{i\varphi}
(
0\leq\varphi\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
).

The point
w
is the point of tangency of the unit circle to
the parabola whose directrix is perpendicular to the real axis
and whose focus is
f
.

Figure 4
.
Tangential parabola and the four roots of
a quartic equation on the unit circle. See Theorem
3.4
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For
r>1
, the four roots of the equation

(3.5)

re^{-i\theta}w^{4}-w^{3}+w-re^{i\theta}=0

lie on the unit circle.

Proof.

If (
3.5
) has
w=1
as its solution,
then
\theta=0
follows from
r(e^{-i\theta}-e^{i\theta})=0
.
In this case, the corresponding parabola degenerates to a
half-line on the real-axis because the light ray arrives
directly above the observer.
So from now on we will assume that (
3.5
)
does not have
w=1
as its solution.

Consider the following two equations

(3.6)

(w-w_{1})(w-w_{2})(w-w_{3})(w-w_{4})=0

and

(3.7)

(z-z_{1})(z-z_{2})(z-z_{3})(z-z_{4})=0,

where
z_{k}=i\dfrac{1+w_{k}}{1-w_{k}}
(
k=1,2,3,4
).
We remark that, for
k=1,\cdots,4
,
w_{k}\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{2}

and
z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
are equivalent.

Suppose that
w_{1},\cdots,w_{4}
are the solutions to (
3.5
).
Then, from Vieta’s formulas, the following equalities hold

\displaystyle(w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}+w_{4})r-e^{i\theta}=0,

\displaystyle w_{1}(w_{2}+w_{3}+w_{4})+w_{2}(w_{3}+w_{4})+w_{3}w_{4}=0,

\displaystyle(w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}+w_{1}w_{2}w_{4}+w_{1}w_{3}w_{4}+w_{2}w_{3}w_{4})r+e^{i\theta}=0,

(3.8)

\displaystyle w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}w_{4}+e^{2i\theta}=0.

The equation (
3.7
) can be written as

\displaystyle(w_{1}-1)(w_{2}-1)(w_{3}-1)(w_{4}-1)z^{4}

\displaystyle\quad+2i\big((w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}+w_{4})-(w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}+w_{1}w_{2}w_{4}+w_{1}w_{3}w_{4}+w_{2}w_{3}w_{4})

\displaystyle\qquad+2w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}w_{4}-2\big)z^{3}

\displaystyle\quad+2\big((w_{1}(w_{2}+w_{3}+w_{4})+w_{2}(w_{3}+w_{4})+w_{3}w_{4})-3w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}w_{4}-3\big)z^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2i\big((w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}+w_{4})+(w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}+w_{1}w_{2}w_{4}+w_{1}w_{3}w_{4}+w_{2}w_{3}w_{4})

\displaystyle\qquad-2w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}w_{4}+2\big)z

(3.9)

\displaystyle\quad+(w_{1}+1)(w_{2}+1)(w_{3}+1)(w_{4}+1)=0

and the following hold,

\displaystyle(w_{1}-1)(w_{2}-1)(w_{3}-1)(w_{4}-1)

\displaystyle\quad=1-(w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}+w_{4})+(w_{1}(w_{2}+w_{3}+w_{4})+w_{2}(w_{3}+w_{4})+w_{3}w_{4})

\displaystyle\qquad-(w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}+w_{1}w_{2}w_{4}+w_{1}w_{3}w_{4}+w_{2}w_{3}w_{4})+w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}w_{4},

\displaystyle(w_{1}+1)(w_{2}+1)(w_{3}+1)(w_{4}+1)

\displaystyle\quad=1+(w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}+w_{4})+(w_{1}(w_{2}+w_{3}+w_{4})+w_{2}(w_{3}+w_{4})+w_{3}w_{4})

\displaystyle\qquad+(w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}+w_{1}w_{2}w_{4}+w_{1}w_{3}w_{4}+w_{2}w_{3}w_{4})+w_{1}w_{2}w_{3}w_{4}.

Substituting (
3.8
) into (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle(e^{2i\theta}-1)rz^{4}+4i((e^{2i\theta}+1)r-e^{i\theta})z^{3}-6(e^{2i\theta}-1)rz^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-4i((e^{2i\theta}+1)r+e^{i\theta})z+(e^{2i\theta}-1)r=0.

Dividing the above equation by
2ie^{i\theta}
gives,

(3.10)

r\sin\theta z^{4}+2(2r\cos\theta-1)z^{3}-6r\sin\theta z^{2}-2(2r\cos\theta-1)z+r\sin\theta=0.

Note that equation (
3.10
) is an equation of degree four
with real coefficients.
It is known that the equation

ax^{4}+bx^{3}+cx^{2}+dx+e=0\quad(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R})

has four real roots if the following conditions are satisfied
(see, for example
[
Ree22
]
),

\Delta>0,\qquad P<0\quad\mbox{and}\quad D<0,

where

\displaystyle\Delta=

\displaystyle 256e^{3}a^{3}+(-192e^{2}db-128e^{2}c^{2}+144ed^{2}c-27d^{4})a^{2}

\displaystyle+\big((144e^{2}c-6ed^{2})b^{2}+(-80edc^{2}+18d^{3}c)b+16ec^{4}-4d^{2}c^{3}\big)a

\displaystyle-27e^{2}b^{4}+(18edc-4d^{3})b^{3}+(-4ec^{3}+d^{2}c^{2})b^{2},

\displaystyle P=

\displaystyle 8ac-3b^{2},

\displaystyle D=

\displaystyle 64a^{3}e-16a^{2}c^{2}+16ab^{2}c-16a^{2}db-3b^{4}.

For
r>1
,
a=r\sin\theta
,
b=2(2r\cos\theta-1)
,
c=-6r\sin\theta
,

d=-2(2r\cos\theta-1)
, and
e=r\sin\theta
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=256((64\cos^{6}\theta+192\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{4}\theta+192\sin^{4}\theta\cos^{2}\theta+64\sin^{6}\theta)r^{6}

\displaystyle\quad+(-48\cos^{4}\theta-96\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta-48\sin^{4}\theta)r^{4}+(12\cos^{2}\theta-15\sin^{2}\theta)r^{2}-1)

\displaystyle=256(64r^{6}-48r^{4}+(-27\sin^{2}\theta+12)r^{2}-1)

\displaystyle>256(64r^{6}-48r^{4}-15r^{2}-1)>256(64r^{6}-64r^{4})>0,

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=-12((4\cos^{2}\theta+4\sin^{2}\theta)r^{2}-4r\cos\theta+1)

\displaystyle=-12(4r^{2}-4r\cos\theta+1)<-12\big(4r(r-\cos\theta)+1\big)<0,

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=-16((48\cos^{4}\theta+80\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta+32\sin^{4}\theta)r^{4}+(-96\cos^{3}\theta-96\sin^{2}\theta\cos\theta)r^{3}

\displaystyle\quad+(72\cos^{2}\theta+28\sin^{2}\theta)r^{2}-24\cos\theta r+3)

\displaystyle=-16\bigg((4r^{2}+11)\Big(2r\cos\theta-\dfrac{6(4r^{2}+1)}{4r^{2}+11}\Big)^{2}+\dfrac{(4r^{2}-1)^{2}(8r^{2}-3)}{4r^{2}+11}\bigg)<0.

Hence, (
3.10
) has four real roots, and the assertion is obtained.
∎

4.
Envelope of parabola directrices: tear drop curve

Let
a
(
a>1
) be a point on the real axis.
Suppose light arriving from a certain direction is
reflected at point
w
on the unit circle and reaches the point
a
.
Since the light rays cannot pass through the interior of
the unit circle, we assume that

\arg w\leq|\sin^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}{a}|
.

Figure 5
.
A light signal is reflected at the point
w

of the unit circle and reaches the point
a
.
It comes from an infinitely distant location
in a specific direction.

Lemma 4.1
.

The directrix of the parabola, tangent to the unit circle at
w
and with focus at
a
, is given by

(4.2)

(a-w)z+w^{3}(wa-1)\overline{z}+2w^{2}a^{2}-3w(w^{2}+1)a+4w^{2}=0.

4.3
.

Remark.

(
4.2
) can also be written as,

(a\overline{w}^{2}-\overline{w})z+(aw^{2}-w)\overline{z}+2a^{2}-3(w+\overline{w})a+4=0.

Proof.

The line
l_{w}
tangent to the unit circle at point
w
is given by,

(4.4)

l_{w}\,:\,\ L_{w}(z)=z+w^{2}\overline{z}-2w=0.

Let
a^{\ast}
be the symmetric point of a point
a

with respect to the line
l_{w}
.
Therefore, the perpendicular bisector of line segment
[a,a^{\ast}]

coincides with
l_{w}
.
Here, the perpendicular bisector of line segment
[a,a^{\ast}]
is given by

|z-a|=|z-a^{\ast}|
, i.e.

PB(z)=(a-\overline{a^{\ast}})z+(a-a^{\ast})\overline{z}-(a^{2}-|a^{\ast}|^{2})=0.

Since the above equation is equivalent to (
4.4
),
and hence

(a-\overline{a^{\ast}})L_{w}(z)-PB(z)\equiv 0
holds.
Comparing the coefficients, we have

(-w^{2}+1)a-a^{\ast}+\overline{a^{\ast}}w^{2}=0,\quad\textrm{and}\quad-a^{2}+2wa+a^{\ast}\overline{a^{\ast}}-2\overline{a^{\ast}}w=0.

Hence we have,
a^{\ast}=w(2-aw)\,.

Here, the directrix is perpendicular to the line passing through

a^{\ast},w
and passes through
a^{\ast}
,
so the equation is given by

(\overline{w}-\overline{a^{\ast}})(z-a^{\ast})+(w-a^{\ast})(\overline{z}-\overline{a^{\ast}})=0.

Substituting
a^{\ast}=w(2-aw)
into the above equation, we have

(a-w)z+w^{3}(wa-1)\overline{z}+2w^{2}a^{2}-3w(w^{2}+1)a+4w^{2}=0.

∎

Figure 6
.
The blue line indicates the directrix with respect
to the red parabola.

Proposition 4.5
.

When
a>1
is fixed and the direction of the light source changes,
that is, when
w
ranges over the unit circle,
the envelope of the family of directrices is given by the following equation

(4.6)

z^{2}\overline{z}^{2}-2(a^{2}+2)z\overline{z}+4a(z+\overline{z})+a^{4}-4a^{2}=0.

Proof.

The envelope of the family of lines
\{l_{w}(z)\}_{w}

is obtained by eliminating
w
from

L_{w}(z)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial w}L_{w}(z)=0.

Eliminating
w
from the above equations, we have
e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=0
,
where

\displaystyle E_{1}\,:\

\displaystyle e_{1}=27a^{2}z^{2}+(-256a^{6}+192a^{4}+6a^{2}+4)z\overline{z}+27a^{2}\overline{z}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(288a^{5}-252a^{3}-36a)z+(288a^{5}-252a^{3}-36a)\overline{z}-324a^{4}+324a^{2},

\displaystyle E_{2}\,:\

\displaystyle e_{2}=z^{2}\overline{z}^{2}+(-2a^{2}-4)z\overline{z}+4az+4a\overline{z}+a^{4}-4a^{2}.

If we set
z=x+iy
,
E_{1}
is written as

(a^{2}-1)\big((8a^{2}+1)x-9a\big)^{2}+(4a^{2}-1)^{3}y^{2}=0.

So,
E_{1}
does not represent the envelope, and the equation of
the envelope is given by
E_{2}\,.

∎

4.7
.

Remark.
If we set
z=x+iy
,
E_{2}
is written as

(4.8)

\big((x-a)^{2}+y^{2}+2a(x-a)\big)^{2}-4\big((x-a)^{2}+y^{2}\big)=0.

Therefore, the envelope is the limaçon of Pascal
[
L
, Chapter 5]
.
The standard form of the limaçon of Pascal is

(x^{2}+y^{2}-px)^{2}-q^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})=0
.
It is known that this curve contains an internal loop for
q<p
.
So, the curve defined by (
4.8
) always contains
a loop, "a tear drop".

Eliminating
\overline{z}
from (
4.2
) and (
4.6
),
we have the following point of tangency between
the envelope and the directrix

z=2w-aw^{2}.

Setting
w=e^{i\theta}
gives the parametric representation of the envelope,

E_{2}\,:\ z=2e^{i\theta}-ae^{2i\theta}\quad(-\pi<\theta\leq\pi).

Using the expression for
E_{2}
above, we can see that
the teardrop-shaped part of this curve corresponds to the subfamily

\{l_{w}\}_{w}
, where
\theta=\arg w\leq|\sin^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}{a}|
.

.

)

a=2
