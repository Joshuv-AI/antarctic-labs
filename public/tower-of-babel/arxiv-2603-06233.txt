The generalized Lefschetz number and loop braid groups
Stavroula Makri

Abstract
. We study the interplay between braid group theory and topological dynamics in three dimensions. While classical braid theory has been extensively applied to surface homeomorphisms to analyze fixed and periodic points, an analogous framework in three-dimensional manifolds has been lacking. In this work, we introduce the use of loop braid groups as a three-dimensional generalization of classical braid groups to investigate homeomorphisms of the 3-ball that leave invariant a finite collection of circles. In our main theorem we associate the Burau matrix representations of loop braid elements to the generalized Lefschetz number. This result provides important information on the existence and interaction of fixed and periodic points. In addition, an application of our theorem gives an estimate of the number of periodic points. Our result extends a classical two-dimensional theorem to a 3-dimensional setting, providing a rich 3-dimensional framework, whose topological and algebraic aspects have been extensively investigated, for studying its topological dynamical properties. Keywords: Generalized Lefschetz number, Nielsen theory, Loop braid groups, Burau representation, 3-manifolds, Fixed points, Periodic orbits



1.
Introduction

The connection between braid group theory and topological dynamics arises from the remarkable
applications of braid group theory to the study of the periodic orbit structure of iterated surface homeomorphisms.
In dynamical systems, topological invariants are often used to study the qualitative and quantitative properties of
the system. Elements of braid groups are such invariants, characterizing the topological behavior of periodic orbits
in the case of surface homeomorphisms isotopic to the identity. Let
S
be a compact surface and
f\colon S\rightarrow S
a homeomorphism isotopic to the identity map.
Let
P
be a finite invariant set of
f
, that is, a union of finitely many periodic orbits of
f
.
Choose and fix an isotopy
\{f_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
that deforms
\mathrm{id}
to
f
.
The image of
P
under
f_{t}
defines a braid on
S
. The use of braids in surface dynamics began in the early 1980s and has since grown
into a significant area within the theory of low-dimensional dynamical systems. For a detailed overview of topological dynamics on surfaces and braid theory, we refer the reader to
[
5
,
24
]
.

One of the main interactions between braid group theory and surface dynamics is the use of matrix representations of braid elements in order to obtain information about the existence of fixed points of the
f
, which is associated to the given braid element. More precisely, the trace of the matrix representations of braid groups of the
2
-disk is closely related to the abelianized generalized Lefschetz number of
f
to
S-P
, as established by Fadell, Husseini, and Fried in the early 1980’s. The generalized Lefschetz number describes how periodic orbits not belonging to
P
are linked with it. Thus, by computing the matrix representation one can obtain information about the existence and linking behavior of fixed points and periodic orbits, see Theorem 3.3 in
[
24
]
for the case of the punctured
2
-disk. Similar results have been obtained for the torus by Huang and Jiang in
[
17
]
and for the annulus by Jiang
[
20
]
. The main focus of this work is to extend this result in a
3
-dimensional setting.

As we know that braid theory is a very powerful and important tool in studying the existence of fixed and periodic points as well as important properties of a dynamical system,
[
21
,
14
,
5
,
16
,
22
,
7
]
, a natural question that arises is what happens in the case of
3
-dimensional manifolds.
Can we extend such techniques, namely the use of braid theory, in order to study fixed points and periodic
orbits on
3
-dimensional manifolds? While a considerable amount of research has been carried out on studying the complexity
of orbit structures in dynamical systems on
3
-dimensional manifolds
[
11
,
26
,
8
]
,
there are no known results about dynamics on
3
-dimensional manifolds that are analogous
to the known results about the orbit structure on surfaces that use braid theory. One of
the main reasons for this is that the classical braid group is trivial on
3
-dimensional
manifolds. Given that classical braid group theory is trivial on three-dimensional manifolds,
the existing connection between braid theory and topological dynamics does not
extend beyond two-dimensional manifolds.

In this article we use the theory of loop braid groups, a three-dimensional analogue
of classical braid groups, in order to expand the existing body of literature to the
three-dimensional setting. The idea of studying fixed and periodic points of homeomorphisms of the
3
-ball using loop
braid groups, which presents the generalization in
3
dimensions of the study of fixed points and
periodic orbits of a surface homeomorphism using braid theory, is a new approach. It is important to note that, apart from Nielsen theory, a
precise methodology for studying periodic orbits of homeomorphisms of

3
-dimensional manifolds has not yet been established.

The loop braid groups
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
appear
in the literature under many different names: conjugating automorphisms of the free
group
F_{n}
, due to Savushkina
[
27
]
, loop braid groups, due to Baez–Crans–Wise
[
2
]
,
and groups of untwisted rings, due to Brendle–Hatcher
[
6
]
. The group
\mathrm{LB}_{n}

is in fact a
3
-dimensional analogue of the Artin braid group
B_{n}
, where instead of
considering points on a surface we consider circles in the
3
-ball. There are several
interpretations of
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
, which will be presented in Section
3
: as the fundamental group of the space of specific configuration, as automorphisms of the free group
F_{n}
and in terms of mapping class groups.
We consider
f\colon B^{3}\rightarrow B^{3}
to be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
isotopic to the identity, and
C
a finite invariant set of
f
, that is, a union of finitely
many circles lying in the interior of
B^{3}
. Choose and fix an isotopy
\{f_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
that deforms
\mathrm{id}

to
f
. The image of
C
under
f_{t}
defines a loop braid.

In this work we extend in a
3
-dimensional setting the result that associates the trace of the matrix representation of a braid with the abelianization of the generalized Lefschetz number of
f
to the punctured
2
-disk. This theorem wes presented initially by Fadell and Husseini
[
12
]

as an application of Nielsen fixed point theory, then appeared as theorem
explicitly by Matsuoka in
[
23
]
, and given with a detailed proof by Huang and Jiang
[
17
]
. In our setting, we associate the generalized Lefschetz number of
f
with the trace of a matrix associated to the corresponding loop braid, that is linked to its Burau representation, which will be introduced in Section
3
. To be more precise, we prove that given an element
b_{f,C}
of the loop braid group
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
, which corresponds to a homeomorphism
f\in\operatorname{Homeo}(B^{3},C)
, where
C
is a trivial link of
n
components in the interior of
B^{3}
, the trace of precise matrices
\bar{R}(b),R(b)
, given in Subsection
4.1
, that are associated with their Burau representation, equals the abelianized generalized Lefschetz number of
f
to
B^{3}-C
. Thus, we obtain information about the existence of fixed points of
f
and moreover how they are interacting with
C
. The theorem below summarizes our main result; the full formal statement appears in Section
4
as Theorem
4.4
. Let
\operatorname{ind}
denote the fixed point index.

Theorem 1
.

Let
b=b_{f,C}
. Then

1+\operatorname{tr}S(b)^{\pi_{\mu}}=\sum_{I\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\operatorname{ind}\big(\operatorname{Fix}_{I}(\bar{f})\big)\,t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{m}^{\pm 1}],

where
S(b)=\bar{R}(b)-R(b)
.

In the theorem, the right hand side of the equality corresponds to the abelianization of the generalized Lefschetz number of
\bar{f}
. In our setting, we consider homeomorphisms
f
of
B^{3}
that leave invariant in its interior a trivial link of
n
components. Equivalently, we may consider homeomorphisms of the space
B=B^{3}\setminus C
, where
C:=S^{1}_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup S^{1}_{n}
and since we are interested in compact spaces, we define its compactification
\bar{B}
and thus
\bar{f}:\bar{B}\rightarrow\bar{B}
, as explained in detail in Subsection
4.2
. Moreover, each monomial
t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}
corresponds to a fixed point
x
and each exponent corresponds to the linking number of the fixed point
x
and a subset of
C
, as described in Subsection
4.4

It is important to note that this theorem carries considerable importance on several fronts. It extends a significant theorem of a 2-dimensional setting to a 3-dimensional one. In addition, it provides an interesting three-dimensional setting, that is the
3
-ball with orientation-preserving homeomorphisms that leave invariant a set of
n
circles in its interior, whose algebraic and topological aspects have already been extensively studied
[
15
,
13
,
28
,
6
,
9
,
3
,
25
,
10
]
, offering a rich framework to investigate its topological dynamical properties, including the analysis of fixed and periodic points. Last but not least, it presents a very powerful application of the Burau representation of the loop braid groups to the study of fixed and periodic points of a 3-dimensional dynamical system.

The theorem below presents an application of Theorem
4.4
, which provides an estimate for the cardinality of periodic points of
f
. The full formal statement appears in Section
5
as Theorem
5.2
.
Let
\operatorname{Per}_{p,C}(f)
be the set of periodic points of
f
of minimal period
p
not on
S^{1}_{i}\in C
, for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the following holds:

|\operatorname{Per}_{p,C}(f)|\geq p(M_{p,C}-n_{p}),

where
M_{p,C}
is the number of monomials
t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}
, with non-zero coefficient, appearing in
1-\operatorname{tr}(R(b_{f,C})^{\pi_{\mu}})^{p}+\operatorname{tr}(\bar{R}(b_{f,C})^{\pi_{\mu}})^{p}
with
\gcd(p,i_{1},\dots,i_{m})=1
and
n_{p}
denotes the number of indices
1\leq i\leq n
, such that
S^{1}_{i}
has minimal period that divides
p
.

The paper has the following structure. In Section
2
, we provide the necessary introduction to Nielsen fixed point theory as well as to the generalized Lefschetz number. In Section
3
, we present the loop braid groups and give all necessary tools that we will need.
In Section
4
, we associate a matrix representation to each loop braid, which is associated to its Burau representation, we also define the linking number of a fixed point of
f
and
C
and we prove our main result, Theorem
4.4
. We conclude this work with Section
5
where we explain the importance of our result via an example as well as with a derived application, which provides a lower bound for the number of periodic points of
f
.

2.
Generalized Lefschetz number

In this section we give the necessary prerequisites about Nielsen fixed point classes and we define the generalized Lefschetz number.

Let
X
be a connected finite cell complex, and
f:X\rightarrow X
a continuous map. We denote by
\text{Fix}(f)
the fixed point set of
f
, that is,
\text{Fix}(f)=\{x\in X\ |\ f(x)=x\}
.
We recall that two fixed points
x,y
of
f
are said to be
Nielsen equivalent
if
there exists a path
\ell
in
X
from
x
to
y
such that
\ell
and its image
f\circ\ell
are homotopic relative to their endpoints. The equivalence class of a point
x\in\mathrm{Fix}(f)
is called the
Nielsen class
of
x
. We denote by
\mathrm{NC}(f)
the set of Nielsen classes of
f
. A Nielsen class is
essential
if it has nonzero fixed point index, as defined in
[
19
]
and the
Nielsen number

N(f)
is defined as the number of essential Nielsen classes of
f
.

In general, determining whether two fixed points belong to the same Nielsen class is a difficult problem and Reidemeister showed that this geometric problem can be translated into an algebraic one as follows. Choose a base point
x_{0}\in X
and a path
w
from
x_{0}
to
f(x_{0})
, called
base path
. For simplicity, let
\pi=\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
be the fundamental group of
X
based at
x_{0}
. We define
f_{\pi}:\pi\rightarrow\pi
as the composition of the induced map
f_{*}:\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X,f(x_{0}))
with
w_{*}:\pi_{1}(X,f(x_{0}))\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
. Two elements
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\pi
are said to be
Reidemeister equivalent
if there exists
\lambda\in\pi
such that

\lambda_{2}=f_{\pi}(\lambda)\cdot\lambda_{1}\cdot\lambda^{-1}.

An equivalence class of elements of
\pi
under this relation is called a
Reidemeister class
and we denote by
\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})
the set of all Reidemeister classes.

Now we are ready to assign a Reidemeister class to a fixed point of
f
. For
x\in\mathrm{Fix}(f)
, choose a path
\ell
from
x_{0}
to
x
. The class
[w(f\circ\ell)\ell^{-1}]\in\pi
is denoted by
R(x)
and is called the Reidemeister class of
x
. Note that the Reidemeister class represented by
[w(f\circ\ell)\ell^{-1}]

is independent of the choice of
\ell
, since for another path
\ell^{\prime}
from
x_{0}
to
x
, the loops
w(f\circ\ell)\ell^{-1}
and
w(f\circ\ell^{\prime})(\ell^{\prime})^{-1}
have the same Reidemeister class. All points in a Nielsen class
F
share the same Reidemeister class, which is denoted by
R(F)
and called the Reidemeister class of
F
or
coordinate of
F
. Two fixed points belong to the same Nielsen class if and only if they have the same Reidemeister class. Thus,
\mathrm{NC}(f)
can be viewed as a subset of
\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})
.

For
\alpha\in\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})
, define
\mathrm{Fix}_{\alpha}(f)=\{x\in\mathrm{Fix}(f)\ |\ R(x)=\alpha\}
. Then,

\text{Fix}(f)=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})}\mathrm{Fix}_{\alpha}(f).

If
\mathrm{Fix}_{\alpha}(f)
is non-empty, it forms a Nielsen class with Reidemeister class
\alpha
. Note that the compactness of
X
implies that
\mathrm{Fix}_{\alpha}(f)
is empty except for finitely many
\alpha
. For an isolated set
S
of fixed points of
f
, we denote by
\text{ind}(S)
the fixed point index of
S
with respect to
f
.
Moreover, we denote by
\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})
the free abelian group generated by the elements of
\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})
.

We can now define the generalized Lefschetz number or Reidemeister trace.

Definition 2.1
.

The
generalized Lefschetz number

\mathcal{L}(f)
, also called the
Reidemeister trace
, of
f
is defined by

(1)

\mathcal{L}(f)=\sum_{F\in\mathrm{NC}(f)}\mathrm{ind}(F)\cdot R(F)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})}\mathrm{ind}(\mathrm{Fix}_{\alpha}(f))\,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi}).

We remark that the generalized Lefschetz number is a homotopy invariant. Let
g:X\rightarrow X
be a continuous map homotopic to
f
through a homotopy
\{h_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
. We consider as base path for
g
the composition of
w
with the path
h_{t}(x_{0})
, for
0\leq t\leq 1
, so that we have
f_{\pi}=g_{\pi}
. From Nielsen fixed point theory we deduce that
\mathcal{L}(f)=\mathcal{L}(g)
.

From the generalized Lefschetz number we can deduce the Nielsen nuumber as well as the classical Lefschetz number. The Nielsen number
N(f)
is the number of Reidemeister classes with non-zero coefficients in
\mathcal{L}(f)
and the classical Lefschetz number is equal to the sum of the coefficients in
\mathcal{L}(f)
.

We will now present a trace formula for the generalized Lefschetz number, using the universal covering space of
X
. Let
\widetilde{X}
be the universal covering space of
X
. The cell structure on
X
induces a corresponding cell structure on
\widetilde{X}
. We denote by
C_{q}(\widetilde{X})
the group of cellular
q
-chains over
\mathbb{Z}
.
The action of
\pi
on
\tilde{X}
induces an action of the group ring
\mathbb{Z}\pi
on
C_{q}(\widetilde{X})
. Thus, the group
C_{q}(\widetilde{X})
becomes free, finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}\pi
-module. Assume that
f:X\rightarrow X
is a cellular map, and let
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\widetilde{X}
be a lift of
f
. Then
\widetilde{f}
induces the homomorphism

\widetilde{f}_{q}:C_{q}(\widetilde{X})\rightarrow C_{q}(\widetilde{X}).

The map
\widetilde{f}_{q}
is an
f_{\pi}
-twisted homomorphism
, meaning that

(2)

\widetilde{f}_{q}(\lambda u)=f_{\pi}(\lambda)\,\widetilde{f}_{q}(u),\quad\lambda\in\pi,\,u\in C_{q}(\widetilde{X}).

We can define now the trace
\mathrm{tr}\,\widetilde{f}_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})
in the following way. Let
\tilde{e}_{1},\dots,\tilde{e}_{n}
be the
q
-cells of
\widetilde{X}
forming a basis of
C_{q}(\widetilde{X})
over
\mathbb{Z}\pi
and
A=(a_{ij})
the matrix over
\mathbb{Z}\pi
representing the
f_{\pi}
-homomorphism
\widetilde{f}_{q}
with respect to this basis. That is, the entries
a_{ij}
are given by

\widetilde{f}_{q}(\tilde{e}_{i})=\sum_{j}a_{ij}\tilde{e}_{j}.

Then, we define the trace of
\tilde{f}_{q}
as follows:

\mathrm{tr}\,\widetilde{f}_{q}=[\mathrm{tr}\,A],

where
[\,\cdot\,]
denotes the projection from
\mathbb{Z}\pi
to
\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})
.

We note that by choosing another basis of
C_{q}(\tilde{X})
, the new basis will be related to the previous basis via a matrix
C
, such that the matrix representing
\tilde{f}_{q}
with respect to the new basis will be given by
C^{f_{\pi}}AC
, where
C^{f_{\pi}}
presents the matrix
C
with entries replaced to their
f_{\pi}
-images. It follows that the trace
\mathrm{tr}\,\tilde{f}_{q}
is well defined, since
[\mathrm{tr}\,(C^{f_{\pi}}AC^{-1})]=[\mathrm{tr}\,A]\in\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi})
.

In
[
18
]
, Husseini proved the following trace formula for the generalized Lefschetz number:

(3)

\mathcal{L}(f)=\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}\,\mathrm{tr}\,\widetilde{f}_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{R}(f_{\pi}).

We will use this trace formula to prove our main theorem in Section
4
.

3.
loop braid groups

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The loop braid groups
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
appear in the literature under many different names; conjugating automorphisms of the free group
F_{n}
due to Savushkina,
[
27
]
, welded braid groups due to Fenn–Rimányi–Rourke,
[
13
]
and groups of untwisted rings due to Brendle–Hatcher,
[
6
]
. The name of loop braid groups, which we will be using and denoting by
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
, was introduced by Baez–Crans–Wise,
[
2
]
.

The groups
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
are actually a
3-
dimensional analogue of the braid groups
B_{n}
, introduced by Artin
[
1
]
, and there are several interpretations of it; in terms of mapping class groups, fundamental group of specific configuration spaces and automorphisms of the free group on
n
generators
F_{n}
. We refer the reader to
[
9
]
for a complete presentation of the equivalent definitions of the loop braid groups.

In this work we will mainly treat loop braid elements in terms of
mapping classes of a
3
-ball with
n
circles in its interior that are left setwise invariant. Let
M
be a compact, connected, orientable manifold, possibly with boundary, and

N
an orientable submanifold contained in the interior of
M
. A self-homeomorphism of the pair of manifolds
(M,N)
is a homeomorphism

f\colon M\rightarrow M
that fixes
\partial M
pointwise and fixes
N
globally. Every self-homeomorphism of
(M,N)
induces a permutation on the
connected components of
N
in the natural way. We denote by
\mathrm{Homeo}(M,N)
the group of self-homeomorphisms of
(M,N)
that preserve
orientation on both
M
and
N
. The multiplication in
\mathrm{Homeo}(M,N)
is given by the
usual composition.
Moreover, we denote by
\mathrm{PHomeo}(M,N)
the subgroup of self-homeomorphisms of
(M,N)

that send each connected component of
N
to itself.

We say that
f,g\in\mathrm{Homeo}(M,N)
are isotopic relative to
N
if there exists
an isotopy
h_{t}\in\mathrm{Homeo}(M,N)
from
f
to
g
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
mapping class group
of
M
with respect to
N
,
denoted by
\mathrm{MCG}(M,N)
, is the group of isotopy classes of self-homeomorphisms in

\mathrm{Homeo}(M,N)
, with multiplication determined by composition.
The
pure mapping class group
of
M
with respect to
N
, denoted by
\mathrm{PMCG}(M,N)
, is the subgroup of elements of

\mathrm{MCG}(M,N)
that send each connected component of
N
to itself.

We will give now the definition of the loop braid group in terms of a mapping class group.
Fix
n\geq 1
, and let
C=S^{1}_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup S^{1}_{n}
be a collection of
n
disjoint,
unknotted, oriented circles forming a trivial link of
n
components in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The exact position of
C
is irrelevant and thus we assume that

C
is contained in the
xy
-plane inside the
3
–ball
B^{3}
.

Definition 3.2
.

The
loop braid group
on
n
components, denoted by
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
, is the mapping class
group
\mathrm{MCG}(B^{3},C)
. The
pure loop braid group
on
n
components, denoted by

\mathrm{PLB}_{n}
, is the pure mapping class group
\mathrm{PMCG}(B^{3},C)
.

To give a geometric interpretation of the loop braid group
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
we define the untwisted ring group, given by Brendle–Hatcher in
[
6
]
, which is actually isomorphic to
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
. We note that the following definition appears in
[
15
]
under the name of motion groups.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathcal{UR}_{n}
be the space of all configurations of
n
disjoint pairwise unlinked unordered Euclidean circles in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
lying in planes parallel to a fixed one. The untwisted ring group
UR_{n}
is its fundamental group.

Theorem 3.4

(Brendle–Hatcher,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The untwisted ring group
UR_{n}
admits the following presentation:

\langle\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1},\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{n-1}\ |\ R\rangle,

where
R
is the set of the following relations:

I.

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}
, for
i=1,\dots,n-2
,

II.

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}
, for
|i-j|>1
, where
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
,

III.

\rho_{i}\rho_{i+1}\rho_{i}=\rho_{i+1}\rho_{i}\rho_{i+1}
, for
i=1,\dots,n-2
,

IV.

\rho_{i}\rho_{j}=\rho_{j}\rho_{i}
, for
|i-j|>1
, where
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
,

V.

\rho_{i}^{2}=1
, for
i=1,\dots,n-1
,

VI.

\sigma_{i}\rho_{j}=\rho_{j}\sigma_{i}
, for
|i-j|>1
, where
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
,

VII.

\sigma_{i}\rho_{i+1}\rho_{i}=\rho_{i+1}\rho_{i}\sigma_{i+1}
, for
i=1,\dots,n-2
,

VIII.

\rho_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\rho_{i+1}
, for
i=1,\dots,n-2
.

Remark 3.5
.

In Theorem
3.4
we see that relations (I.)-(II.) are the standard braid group relations, relations (III.)-(V.) are permutation group relations and relations (VI.)-(VIII.) are the so-called mixed relations.

\sigma_{i},\rho_{i}

circles by passing the

circle through the

Remark 3.6

From
[

]
, we know that the groups

As already mentioned, we can consider the loop braid group also as automorphisms of the free group. The following definition will play an important role in associating a matrix to each generator of
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
, which we will use in proving our main result in Section
4

Definition 3.7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
F_{n}
denote the free group of rank
n
with generators
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and let
\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
denote its
automorphism group. The group
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
that consists of the so-called conjugating automorphisms,

a:x_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow W_{i}x_{\pi(i)}W_{i}^{-1},

where
\pi
is a permutation and
W_{i}
is a word in

. Let
\sigma_{i}\in\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n}),\ i=1,\dots,n-1
be given by

(4)

\sigma_{i}:\begin{cases}x_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow x_{i}x_{i+1}x_{i}^{-1}\\
x_{i+1}\mapstochar\rightarrow x_{i}\\
x_{j}\mapstochar\rightarrow x_{j},\ j\neq i,i+1\end{cases}

and let
\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n}),\ i=1,\dots,n-1
be given by

(5)

\rho_{i}:\begin{cases}x_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow x_{i+1}\\
x_{i+1}\mapstochar\rightarrow x_{i}\\
x_{j}\mapstochar\rightarrow x_{j},\ j\neq i,i+1\end{cases}

The loop braid group
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
is the one generated by both sets of elements
\sigma_{i}
and
\rho_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n-1
.

Remark 3.8
.

The elements
\sigma_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n-1
generate the braid subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
, which is isomorphic to the braid group
B_{n}
. Moreover, the elements
\rho_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n-1
generate the permutation subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
which is a copy of the symmetric group

.

Before concluding this section, we present the unreduced Burau representation of
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
. We recall that the classical braid groups
B_{n}
can be defined as the fundamental groups of the configuration spaces of points in the plane and we know that
B_{n}
is isomorphic to the group of isotopy classes of homeomorphisms of the
2
-disk that act by the identity on its boundary and that leave invariant a subset
Q_{n}
of
n
points in its interior.
One of the oldest interesting representations of
B_{n}

is the Burau representation which was defined by assigning precise matrices to the standard generators of
B_{n}
, but as a homological representation it admits a more natural interpretation.
The unreduced Burau representation of

has been extended to
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
by Vershinin in
[
28
]
. We refer the reader to
[
25
]
for more insights into the representation of loop braid groups.

The matrices for the unreduced Burau representation
LB_{n}\rightarrow GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}])

are given by

\tau_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow I_{i-1}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\oplus I_{n-i-1}

and

\sigma_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow I_{i-1}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}1-t&t\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\oplus I_{n-i-1},

where
I_{k}
denotes the identity matrix of size
k
.
We note that the Burau representation is sometimes defined using the transposes of
these matrices which defines an equivalent representation.
In the following section we will see how we associate these two representations with the abelianized generalized Lefschetz number.

Before concluding this section we want to emphasize that the loop braid elements is indeed a three dimensional analogue of the braid elements on the
2
-disk. Exactly as in the
2
-dimensional case, where
\operatorname{MCG}(\mathbb{D}^{2},Q_{n})\cong B_{n}
with

an

-point set, in our setting we have the isomorphsim
\operatorname{MCG}(B^{3},C)\cong\mathrm{LB}_{n}

and choosing an isotopy

from

to

under the isotopy
\{f_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
.

4.
Main result

In this section, we prove the main result of this work. Before doing so, we first establish the necessary setting. We are interested in homeomorphism of the 3-ball that leave invariant a trivial link
C
of
n
components in its interior. That is, we consider the elements
f\in\mathrm{MCG}(B^{3},C)
. As explained in Section
3
any such element

can be seen as an element generated by
\sigma_{i}

\rho_{j}

. This is precisely the three-dimensional analogue of the well-known isomorphism between the mapping class group of the punctured 2-disk and the braid group. The aim of this section is to show how the Burau representation of an element in

, that corresponds to a given

and in addition their linking behavior, which we will define in a while, with the trivial link

.

4.1.
Twisted representation of
\mathrm{LB}_{n}

We shall start by introducing the

of
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
.

Given a ring

be the group of invertible

A homomorphism

to another ring

induces a homomorphism, which, for simplicity, we also denote by

GL_{k}(A)\rightarrow GL_{k}(A^{\prime})

.
For
R\in GL_{k}(A)
, its image

.

Let

be the ring of Laurent polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}
in the variables

. We shall define a map

R:\mathrm{LB}_{n}\rightarrow GL_{n}(\Lambda),\quad\sigma_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow R_{\sigma_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow R_{\tau_{i}},

which as we will see later corresponds to the action of
\tilde{f}_{1}
on

as defined in Section
2
, as follows.
For
i=1,\dots,n-1
, let

be the matrix defined by

R_{\sigma_{i}}=I_{i-1}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}1-a_{i+1}&a_{i}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\oplus I_{n-i-1}

and, let
R_{\tau_{i}}
be the matrix defined by

R_{\tau_{i}}=I_{i-1}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\oplus I_{n-i-1}

where
I_{j}
denotes the identity matrix of size
j
.

In addition, we shall define a second map

\bar{R}:\mathrm{LB}_{n}\rightarrow GL_{n}(\Lambda),\quad\sigma_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow\bar{R}_{\sigma_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{i}\mapstochar\rightarrow\bar{R}_{\tau_{i}},

which, as we will see later, corresponds to the action of
\tilde{f}_{2}
on
C_{2}(\tilde{X})
as defined in Section in
2
, as follows.
For
i=1,\dots,n-1
, let
\bar{R}_{\sigma_{i}}
be the matrix defined by

\bar{R}_{\sigma_{i}}=I_{i-1}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{i}\\
1&1-a_{i}\end{pmatrix}\oplus I_{n-i-1}

and, let
\bar{R}_{\tau_{i}}
be the matrices defined by

\bar{R}_{\tau_{i}}=I_{i-1}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\oplus I_{n-i-1}

where
I_{j}
denotes the identity matrix of size

.

We call these two maps

and

of

because they are defined by the following rule, which coincides with the way

is defined in relation (
2

To an element

we can always associate an element of the symmetric group

, which actually corresponds to the induced permutation on the set of the trivial link of
n
components. Thus, let

with permutation

be the ring automorphism defined by

That is, the permutation

The maps
R:\mathrm{LB}_{n}\rightarrow GL_{n}(\Lambda)
and

are then defined by the rule

(6)

R(bb^{\prime})=R(b)^{\nu(b^{\prime})}\,R(b^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{R}(bb^{\prime})=\bar{R}(b)^{\nu(b^{\prime})}\,\bar{R}(b^{\prime}).

To sum up, we have associated to each generator
\sigma_{i}
and

4.2.
Compactification

As in the case of surfaces, it is often useful to use a diffeomorphism on a closed surface to obtain one on a
surface with boundary by replacing periodic points with boundary components.
This is achieved through the blow-up procedure due to Bowen, see
[
4
]
. We will follow a similar procedure but in a 3-dimensional setting.
In our setting, we consider homeomorphisms
f
of
B^{3}
that leave invariant in its interior a trivial link of
n
components. Equivalently, we may consider homeomorphisms of the space
B=B^{3}\setminus C
, where
C:=S^{1}_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup S^{1}_{n}
and since we are interested in compact spaces, we define its compactification
\bar{B}
in the following way.

For each circle
S^{1}_{i}
of
C
we consider its normal bundle
NS_{i}^{1}
and define its tubular neighborhood
\mathcal{N}(S^{1}_{i})
, seen as a solid torus. Then, the boundary of the tubular neighborhood is homeomorphic to a torus
\mathbb{T}_{i}
. We can now define the compactification of
B
as
\bar{B}=(B^{3}\setminus C)\cup T
, where
T:=\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{T}_{i}
. In other words,
\bar{B}
is the compactification of
B
where a torus appears as boundary component for each
S^{1}_{i}
deleted. The restriction of
f
to
B^{3}\setminus C
extends to a homeomorphism
\bar{f}:\bar{B}\rightarrow\bar{B}
by defining
\bar{f}:T\rightarrow T
via the differentials and
f=\bar{f}
elsewhere. In other words,
\bar{f}=f\text{ on }B
, and on each
T_{i}
,
\bar{f}
is induced by the linear action of
Df(p)
on the normal directions at
p\in S^{1}_{i}
. We should consider smooth and non-singular homeomorphisms, where the restriction of
f
to
B^{3}\setminus C
is extendable to a homeomorphism

\bar{f}:\bar{B}\rightarrow\bar{B}
.

Note that it may happen that
\bar{f}
has extra fixed points, which can only occur on
T
. The fixed points on
T
can be determined explicitly
from the differentials
Df(p)
,
p\in S^{1}_{i}
. In particular, if
C
contains no
fixed points of
f
, or if
Df(p)
does not have positive real eigenvalues
for each
p\in S^{1}_{i}
, then
\bar{f}
has no fixed points on
T
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{Fix}(\bar{f})=\operatorname{Fix}(f).

4.3.
Linking number

We recall that given a loop braid
b\in\mathrm{LB}_{n}
there exists
f\in\mathrm{Homeo}(B^{3},C)
where
f
is isotopic to the identity map
\mathrm{id}
that corresponds to

in the following way. We choose and we fix the isotopy
f_{t}
from
\mathrm{id}
to
f
such that
f_{t}(C)
corresponds to the given
b\in\mathrm{LB}_{n}
. To be more precise we denote the loop braid
b
as
b_{f,C}
.

We shall present now the notion of linking number for a point
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Fix}(f)
and
A\subset C
. Given
b=b_{f,C}\in\mathrm{LB}_{n}
we consider its corresponding isotopy
f_{t}
, as explained previously. We fix this isotopy and based on it we will define the linking number for a point
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Fix}(f)
and
A\subset C
. The loop
\{f_{t}(x_{0})\ |\ t\in[0,1]\}
can potentially generate a non trivial link that has
A
as component plus one more. That is, we consider the trivial
k
-component link
A
and by tracing out the loop
C_{k+1}
generated by
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Fix(f)}
, while following the isotopy
f_{t}
, we obtain a link of
k+1
components, that may be non trivial. Note that the
(k+1)
-th component, encodes the trajectory of the fixed point
x_{0}
under the isotopy
f_{t}
. Thus, we define as linking number of
x_{0}\in B^{3}
with
C
the following quantity:

\ell k(A,x_{0}):=\sum_{S^{1}_{i}\in A}\ell k(S^{1}_{i},C_{k+1}),

where we recall that
\ell k(S^{1}_{i},S^{1}_{j})=\text{(sum of crossing of}\ S^{1}_{i}\ \text{with}\ S^{1}_{j})/2
, for
S^{1}_{i},S^{1}_{j}
two distinct components of the oriented link diagram. Note that there is a natural orientation defined on the component
C_{k+1}
given by the isotopy.

Clearly, we can extend the notion of linking number for a point
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Fix}(\bar{f})
and
A\subset C
. Notice that the compactification
\bar{B}
of
B^{3}\setminus C
is homeomorphic to
B^{3}\setminus\displaystyle\bigcup_{i}^{n}\mathrm{Int}N(C_{i})
. We shall extend the blow-up
\bar{f}:\bar{B}\rightarrow\bar{B}

to a homeomorphism
\hat{f}:B^{3}\rightarrow B^{3}
so that
\hat{f}
coincides with
f
on
C
and
that
\hat{f}
has no fixed points on
\mathrm{Int}N(C_{i})\setminus S^{1}_{i}
for each
S^{1}_{i}\in C
. We can take an isotopy
\hat{f}_{t}:B^{3}\rightarrow B^{3}
from
\mathrm{id}
to
\hat{f}
so that, with
respect to this isotopy, the braid
b_{\hat{f},C}
is equal to
b_{f,C}
. Hence, the linking number of a fixed point
x_{0}
of
\bar{f}
and
A\subset C
is defined as the linking number of
x_{0}
seen as a fixed point of
\hat{f}
, and
A\subset C
, with respect to the isotopy
\hat{f}_{t}
, as described at the beginning of this subsection.

Let

on

\operatorname{Coker}(f_{*}-\mathrm{id})=H_{1}(B^{3}\setminus C)\big/\operatorname{Im}(f_{*}-\mathrm{id}).

n

\overline{\mathrm{Ab}}\bigl(\mathcal{L}(\bar{f})\bigr)=\sum_{I\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\operatorname{ind}\bigl(\operatorname{Fix}_{I}(\bar{f})\bigr)\,t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}\in\Lambda_{\mu}.

4.6.
Main theorem

We are now ready to present and prove our main theorem, but let us first introduce a homomorphism that we will need for the theorem.
Let
\mu
be a permutation of
\{1,\dots,n\}
, decomposed as a product
of
m
disjoint cycles
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
. We consider
m
variables

t_{1},\dots,t_{m}
and let

\Lambda_{\mu}=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{m}^{\pm 1}]
. For
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}[a_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,a_{n}^{\pm 1}]
, consider the surjective homomorphism

\pi_{\mu}:\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda_{\mu},\qquad\pi_{\mu}(a_{i})=t_{j},

where
j
is determined by the condition
i\in\mu_{j}
. For example, the permutation
\mu
of
\{1,2,3,4\}
that corresponds to
(12)
and
(34)
decomposes into 2 disjoint cycle
\mu_{1}=(12)
and
\mu_{2}=(34)
. Then,
\pi_{\mu}(a_{1})=t_{1},\pi_{\mu}(a_{2})=t_{1},\pi_{\mu}(a_{3})=t_{2}
and
\pi_{\mu}(a_{4})=t_{2}
. We recall that, for a matrix
S\in GL_{n}(\Lambda)
, we denote by
S^{\pi_{\mu}}\in GL_{n}(\Lambda_{\mu})
the matrix obtained from
S
by replacing each entry with its image under
\pi_{\mu}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
b=b_{f,C}
. Then

1+\operatorname{tr}S(b)^{\pi_{\mu}}=\sum_{I\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\operatorname{ind}\big(\operatorname{Fix}_{I}(\bar{f})\big)\,t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{m}^{\pm 1}],

where
S(b)=\bar{R}(b)-R(b)
.

Let
b=b_{f,C}\in\mathrm{LB}_{n}
. From Lemma
4.1
we have

\overline{\mathrm{Ab}}(\mathcal{L}(\bar{f}))=\sum_{I\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\operatorname{ind}(\operatorname{Fix}_{I}(\bar{f}))\,t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}.

In addition, from (
3
) it follows that

\overline{\mathrm{Ab}}(\mathcal{L}(\bar{f}))=\overline{\mathrm{Ab}}\big([1]-[\mathrm{tr}\,\widetilde{f}_{1}]+[\mathrm{tr}\,\widetilde{f}_{2}]\big).

By the defined matrices in (
7
), (
8
) and from the fact that,

\text{for}\ \alpha\in\mathbb{Z}F_{n},\ \text{and}\ [\alpha]\in\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{R}(\psi),\ \overline{\mathrm{Ab}}([\alpha])=(\pi_{\mu}\circ\mathrm{Ab})(\alpha),

we obtain
the equality

\overline{\mathrm{Ab}}\big([1]-[\mathrm{tr}\,\widetilde{f}_{1}]+[\mathrm{tr}\,\widetilde{f}_{2}]\big)=(\pi_{\mu}\circ\mathrm{Ab})\big(1-\mathrm{tr}\,A_{1}(b)+\mathrm{tr}\,A_{2}(b)\big).

After the abelianization of the entries of the matrices
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
the resulting matrices coincide with the matrices, defined in Subsection
4.1
,
R
and
\bar{R}
respectively. Thus,

\overline{\mathrm{Ab}}(\mathcal{L}(\bar{f}))=\pi_{\mu}\big(1-\operatorname{tr}R(b)+\operatorname{tr}\bar{R}(b)\big)

Let
S(b):=\bar{R}(b)-R(b)
. Then,

\overline{\mathrm{Ab}}(\mathcal{L}(\bar{f}))=\pi_{\mu}\big(1+\operatorname{tr}S(b)\big).

We conclude that

1+\operatorname{tr}S(b)^{\pi_{\mu}}=\sum_{I\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\operatorname{ind}(\operatorname{Fix}_{I}(\bar{f}))\,t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}.

∎

Let
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
be the ring of Laurent polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}
in the variable
t
. We define the homomorphism

\pi:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]\ \text{by}\ \pi(a_{i})=t\ \text{for each}\ i.

The image of
R(b)
under this homomorphism
\pi
coincides with the

unreduced Burau matrix
, and the homomorphism

\pi\circ R:\mathrm{LB}_{n}\rightarrow GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}])

is called the
unreduced Burau representation
of the loop braid groups
\mathrm{LB}_{n}
. Moreover, the matrix
R(b)^{\pi}
coincides with the image of

R(b)^{\pi_{\mu}}
under the homomorphism from

\Lambda_{\mu}
to
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
sending each
t_{j}
to
t
.
Similarly, consider the image of
\bar{R}(b)
under this homomorphism
\pi
. By taking its transpose and then reversing the ordering, that is, using instead the ordered basis
(\tilde{b}_{n},\dots,\tilde{b}_{1})
, we obtain again the

unreduced Burau matrix
.

Some classical matrix representations of braid groups have been shown to play an important role in the study of periodic orbits of surface homeomorphisms, see
[
24
]
. Our theorem extends this development to a three-dimensional setting. Specifically, it demonstrates the importance of representations of loop braid groups in the study of fixed points of elements in
\operatorname{Homeo}(B^{3},C)
. This result is also noteworthy as it introduces a compelling three-dimensional context, whose algebraic and topological structure is well-established, for exploring its dynamical properties.

Remark 4.5
.

Note that if the loop braid is a product only of
\rho_{i}
generators, or if its corresponding permutation consists of a single cycle, then our theorem does not provide insight into the linking behavior of the existing fixed points. In particular, the only information obtained is that the abelianized generalized Lefschetz number equals
1
, since
-\operatorname{tr}R(b_{f,C})^{\pi_{\mu}}
and
\operatorname{tr}\bar{R}(b_{f,C})^{\pi_{\mu}}
cancel each other. This means that there exists a fixed point with zero linking number with
C
.

5.
Examples and applications

We will conclude our work with an example, where we present the importance of our main theorem in the study of fixed points, and with an application of our main result concerning the number of periodic points.

5.1.
Example

Let
C=S^{1}_{1}\sqcup S^{1}_{2}\sqcup S^{1}_{3}\sqcup S^{1}_{4}

f\in\operatorname{Homeo(B^{3},C)}

is a periodic orbit of circles of period

\mu\in S_{4}
equals
(12)(34)

\mu_{1}=(12)

\mu_{2}=(34)
. It follows that
m=2

\Lambda_{\mu}=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1}],

,
\pi_{\mu}(a_{2})=t_{1}
,
\pi_{\mu}(a_{3})=t_{2}
and

We will calculate
1+\operatorname{tr}S(b)^{\pi_{\mu}}

\displaystyle 1+\operatorname{tr}S(b)^{\pi_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=1+\operatorname{tr}(\bar{R}(b)-R(b))^{\pi_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=1+\operatorname{tr}\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}-1&-t_{1}(1-t_{2})&0\\
0&0&(1-t_{1})t_{2}\\
0&0&1-t_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=1+t_{1}-1+1-t_{2}

\displaystyle=1+t_{1}-t_{2}

From Theorem
4.4
we can conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{(i_{1},i_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\operatorname{ind}(\mathrm{Fix_{(i_{1},i_{2})}}(\bar{f}))t_{1}^{i_{1}}t_{2}^{i_{2}}=1+t_{1}-t_{2}.

Since
C
contains no fixed points, we have

\operatorname{Fix}(\bar{f})=\operatorname{Fix}(f).

Therefore, by Theorem
4.4

has at least three fixed points. One fixed point with

0
with
C
, another fixed point with linking number
1
with
\{S^{1}_{1},S^{1}_{2}\}\subset C
and one fixed point with linking number
1

\{S^{1}_{3},S^{1}_{4}\}\subset C
.

Note that the fixed points of
f
on each circle

1\leq i\leq n
, of

5.2.

As an application of our main Theorem
4.4

f
. Once again we assume

of minimal period

not on

, for

Theorem 5.2
.

Let

, the following holds:

|\operatorname{Per}_{p,C}(f)|\geq p(M_{p,C}-n_{p}),

where

, with non-zero coefficient, appearing in

\gcd(p,i_{1},\dots,i_{m})=1

denotes the number of indices

, such that

has minimal period that divides

.
