Operator-Norm Transfer and Cohomological Rigidity for Quaternionic Quasi-Lie Structures with Application to Sliding Mode β \beta -Exponential Stability
Nassim Athmouni, Nejib Brahmia, Tarek Fajraoui, Fehmi Mabrouk

Abstract
. We develop operator-theoretic and cohomological tools for quaternionic quasi-Lie structures, with sliding mode control as a motivating application. Three main results are established. First, an exact operator-norm transfer under the quaternionic anti-isomorphism a ↦ a ¯ a\mapsto\bar{a} , which enables quantitative bounds from [ 1 ] to transfer between left- and right-module conventions with no constant factor. Second, a transcription of the cohomological rigidity result of [ 1 ] into a form usable here: in the homogeneous case, under a local cohomological non-obstruction hypothesis, an explicit bilinear correction Ω \Omega produces a bracket satisfying the Jacobi identity exactly on a ball of admissible radius, with all quantitative constants expressed through C 2 C_{2} and the admissible radius. Third, the projected Jacobi defect is shown to satisfy a generalized one-sided Lipschitz condition with computable constants, obtained via a uniform-selection argument handling state-dependence of the measurable selection. As an application, we develop a robust control framework with a cohomological matching condition replacing pointwise verification: an integral sliding surface yields β \beta -exponential stability via an iterative linear matrix inequality (LMI) scheme. The work is purely analytical; closed-loop numerical simulations for multidimensional systems are deferred to a companion paper. The framework is restricted to the homogeneous quasi-Lie case; the Sobolev extension is conjectural, and algorithm termination is established conditionally on sufficient continuity assumptions on the LMI solution map.

Introduction

Working with quaternionic systems forces a choice of convention. The state
space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
carries both a left- and a right-
\mathbb{H}
-module structure,
and operator bounds proved under one convention do not, in general,
translate verbatim to the other — conjugation
a\mapsto\bar{a}
is
anti-multiplicative, not multiplicative. The recent quantitative theory
of quasi-Lie brackets on quaternionic Banach modules
[
1
]
,
on which we rely heavily, is set up for right modules. Applications in
attitude dynamics, where rotation quaternions act on the left, naturally
favor the opposite convention. Reconciling the two requires care.

The first half of this paper supplies the missing operator-theoretic
ingredient. Given a bounded right-
\mathbb{H}
-linear operator

T:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we consider its conjugate

T^{L}:=\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}\circ T\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}
, acting on the left-module
structure via componentwise conjugation
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}
. Because
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}
is
an isometric involution, one expects
\|T^{L}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
to be
comparable to
\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}
; the question is whether equality
holds, or only a bounded ratio. Theorem
2.1
shows
that equality holds. The proof is short and uses only the isometric
property of
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}
, but the consequence is what we need:

every quantitative bound of
[
1
]
transfers to the
left-module setting without a constant factor.

A separate question concerns the Jacobi defect itself. Given a bilinear
bracket
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
, write

\mathcal{J}(x,y,z)
for its Jacobi defect and assume

\|\mathcal{J}(x,y,z)\|\leq 6C_{2}\|x\|\|y\|\|z\|
. The rigidity result
of
[
1
, Theorem 4.12]
produces, in the homogeneous case,
a bilinear correction
\Omega
such that the corrected bracket

\{x,y\}:=\mathcal{L}(x,y)-\Omega(x,y)
satisfies the Jacobi identity

exactly
on a ball of admissible radius

\varepsilon^{*}=\min\{1/(16A),\sqrt{1/(16C_{1})},1/(4C_{2}),\varepsilon_{0}\}
,
with
\|\Omega\|_{\varepsilon}\leq 4C_{2}\varepsilon
. We restate this
result (Theorem
2.2
) in a form adapted to the control
application that follows, and isolate the constants that will reappear
throughout.

Why control? The Jacobi defect arises in linearized attitude
dynamics
[
6
,
7
]
, and a system with non-commutative
uncertainties is exactly the setting where existing sliding-mode
techniques run into trouble. The standard matching condition

\delta(x,t)\in\mathrm{Im}(B)
is checked trajectory by trajectory;
the switching gain
\eta
must dominate
\|\delta\|_{\infty}
uniformly,
producing chattering. We use the cohomological correction
\Omega
to
build an explicit feedforward term

\Delta_{\mathrm{app}}(x,t):=B^{+}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}\,d\omega_{0}(\ldots)

that pre-cancels most of
\delta
, leaving only the non-commutative
residual
R_{\mathrm{nc}}^{B}
for
\eta
to absorb. The required gain
drops from
O(\|\delta\|_{\infty})
to
O(C_{\mathrm{nc}}R_{\max})
, an improvement
that is meaningful precisely when the bracket is close to satisfying
Jacobi.

Two further ingredients make the construction usable. The projected
Jacobi defect
\delta(x,t)=\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}\mathcal{J}_{t}(x,\cdot,\cdot)

involves a measurable selection
\xi^{*}(x,t)
that depends on the
state, and naive estimates fail to give one-sided Lipschitz constants.
Lemma
2.8
circumvents this difficulty via a

frozen-selection trick
: rather than tracking the optimizer, we
lift to a uniform supremum over both selection arguments and obtain
explicit constants

(\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}},\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}})=(2\rho_{\delta},4\bar{\delta}_{\max})
.
The factors of
2
and
4
reflect the suboptimality gap of a
state-dependent selection; they collapse to
1
when a state-independent
maximizer exists.

The pointwise condition
\delta(x,t)\in\mathrm{Im}(B)
is replaced by
a structural condition on
(B,\mathcal{L})
alone — the

Cohomological Matching Condition
(Definition
4.1
),

\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{eval}}(d)\subseteq\mathrm{Im}(B)
. This is
verified once, at design time, and covers every trajectory of the
system. The remaining algorithmic problem is the well-known circular
dependence between the gain
\eta
(which depends on the invariant set
size) and the invariant set itself (which depends on
\eta
).
Algorithm
1

ordering of computations and a halving loop; termination is established

LMI solution map. The LMI in Algorithm

\mu_{k}(2\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}+\mu_{\mathrm{Y}})+(\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}-\mu_{\mathrm{Y}})

with explicit separation of the LMI scaling parameter

The sliding-mode machinery itself — integral sliding variable,

Preliminaries and Main Operator-Theoretic Results

We begin with the algebraic facts needed throughout, then prove the three
operator-theoretic results announced in the introduction. The first two

Theorem

2.1.
Quaternionic Algebra

The skew field
\mathbb{H}
consists of elements

q=q_{0}+q_{1}\mathbf{i}+q_{2}\mathbf{j}+q_{3}\mathbf{k}
with

q_{0},\ldots,q_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
and

\mathbf{i}^{2}=\mathbf{j}^{2}=\mathbf{k}^{2}=\mathbf{ijk}=-1
.
The conjugate
\bar{q}=q_{0}-q_{1}\mathbf{i}-q_{2}\mathbf{j}-q_{3}\mathbf{k}

satisfies
\|q\|^{2}=q\bar{q}=\bar{q}q
and

\overline{pq}=\bar{q}\bar{p}
(anti-multiplicativity).
Multiplication satisfies
pq\neq qp
in general.

2.2.
Module Conventions and the Anti-isomorphism

Definition 2.1

(Left and Right Quaternionic Modules)
.

\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a
left

\mathbb{H}
-module when scalar multiplication is

(a,x)\mapsto ax
(component-wise left multiplication). It is a
right

\mathbb{H}
-module when
(x,a)\mapsto xa
. The two structures are related by the
anti-isomorphism
a\mapsto\bar{a}
: a right module becomes a left module by
redefining
a\cdot_{L}x:=x\cdot_{R}\bar{a}
, and vice versa.

Remark 2.1

(Convention for this Paper)
.

The state space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
carries both left- and right-
\mathbb{H}
-module
structures. The bracket
\mathcal{L}
and the bilinear correction
\Omega

of
[
1
]
are presented for right modules in the cited
reference; we adopt the same right-module convention
for
\mathcal{L}
and
\Omega
. Matrix actions
u\mapsto Bu
for

B\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times m}
,
u\in\mathbb{H}^{m}
are the standard ones, compatible
with right-scalar multiplication (
B(uq)=(Bu)q
for all
q\in\mathbb{H}
by
associativity of
\mathbb{H}
).
Theorem
2.1
provides the tool to transfer quantitative
bounds to the left-module convention, should this be needed for specific
attitude-dynamics applications where rotation quaternions act on the left.
All stability analysis is performed in the real representation
\mathbb{R}^{4n}

via the map
\Phi
of Remark
2.11
, which is
convention-independent.

Definition 2.2

(Componentwise Conjugation)
.

Define
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
by

\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(x)_{i}=\bar{x}_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Lemma 2.1

(Properties of
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}
)
.

(i)

Isometry:
\|\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(x)\|=\|x\|
for all
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

(ii)

Involution:
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}=\mathrm{id}
,
hence
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}
is bijective.

(iii)

\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(x\cdot a)=\bar{a}\cdot\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
a\in\mathbb{H}
.

(iv)

\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(a\cdot x)=\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(x)\cdot\bar{a}

for all
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
a\in\mathbb{H}
.

Proof.

(i)
\|\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(x)\|^{2}=\sum_{i}\|\bar{x}_{i}\|^{2}=\sum_{i}\|x_{i}\|^{2}
.
(ii)
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(x))_{i}=\bar{\bar{x}}_{i}=x_{i}
.
(iii)
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(x\cdot a)_{i}=\overline{x_{i}a}=\bar{a}\bar{x}_{i}

by
\overline{pq}=\bar{q}\bar{p}
.
(iv)
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(a\cdot x)_{i}=\overline{ax_{i}}=\bar{x}_{i}\bar{a}
.
∎

2.3.
Norm Transfer under the Anti-Isomorphism

Theorem 2.1

(Operator Norm Transfer under the Anti-isomorphism)
.

Let
T:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
be bounded and right-
\mathbb{H}
-linear:

T(x\cdot a)=T(x)\cdot a
for all
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
a\in\mathbb{H}
.
Define
T^{L}:=\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}\circ T\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}
. Then:

(i)

T^{L}
is left-
\mathbb{H}
-linear:

T^{L}(a\cdot y)=a\cdot T^{L}(y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
a\in\mathbb{H}
.

(ii)

\|T^{L}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}
exactly.

Proof.

Part (i).

(Lemma
2.1
(iv))

\displaystyle T^{L}(a\cdot y)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}\bigl(T\bigl(\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(y)\cdot\bar{a}\bigr)\bigr)

(right-linearity of
T
)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}\bigl(T\bigl(\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(y)\bigr)\cdot\bar{a}\bigr)

(Lemma
2.1
(iii))

\displaystyle=a\cdot\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}\bigl(T\bigl(\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(y)\bigr)\bigr)=a\cdot T^{L}(y).

Part (ii).

Since
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}
is an isometric bijection (Lemma
2.1
(i),(ii))
mapping the unit sphere of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
bijectively onto itself, we compute:

\displaystyle\|T^{L}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|y\|=1}\|T^{L}(y)\|=\sup_{\|y\|=1}\|\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(T(\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(y)))\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}\sup_{\|y\|=1}\|T(\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(y))\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{=}}\sup_{\|z\|=1}\|T(z)\|=\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}},

where
(*)
uses the isometry of
\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}
(Lemma
2.1
(i))
and
(**)
uses the substitution
z=\Phi_{\mathrm{conj}}(y)
, with
\|z\|=\|y\|=1
by the
isometry. ∎

Remark 2.2

(Comparison with Complex Case)
.

For complex Hilbert spaces, the conjugation map
z\mapsto\bar{z}
is

\mathbb{R}
-linear but not
\mathbb{C}
-linear, and the relation between
T
and

\bar{T}:=\mathrm{conj}\circ T\circ\mathrm{conj}
is more subtle.
In the quaternionic case, the anti-multiplicativity
\overline{pq}=\bar{q}\bar{p}

is precisely what enables the exact norm equality in
Theorem
2.1
(ii). This phenomenon has no direct
complex analogue and is specific to the structure of
\mathbb{H}
as a real
division algebra with involution.

2.4.
Cohomological Rigidity of Quasi-Lie Brackets

Definition 2.3

(Quasi-Lie Bracket and Jacobiator)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
,

(x,y,t)\mapsto\mathcal{L}(x,y,t)
, be a family of
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear maps in
(x,y)

parametrized by
t
. We say
\mathcal{L}
is a
(time-dependent) quasi-Lie
bracket
if it is skew-symmetric in
(x,y)
for every
t
and its
time-dependent Jacobiator

\mathcal{J}(x,y,z,t)=\mathcal{L}(x,\mathcal{L}(y,z,t),t)+\mathcal{L}(y,\mathcal{L}(z,x,t),t)+\mathcal{L}(z,\mathcal{L}(x,y,t),t)

\|\mathcal{J}(x,y,z,t)\|\leq 6C_{2}\|x\|\|y\|\|z\|
uniformly in

t\geq 0
, for some
C_{2}>0
. When
\mathcal{L}
is time-independent, the symbol

\mathcal{J}(x,y,z)
is used and
\mathcal{J}_{t}\equiv\mathcal{J}
becomes
constant in

(this is the case for the test bracket of
Section
7
). Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{J}_{t}
and

Definition 2.5

(Real-Valued Inner Product and Norm)
.

(8)

\left\langle x,\,y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathrm{Re}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{x}_{i}\,y_{i}\right)\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\|x\|=\sqrt{\left\langle x,\,x\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}}.

\mathbb{R}

Lemma 2.2

(Cauchy–Schwarz)
.

|\left\langle x,\,y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}|\leq\|x\|\,\|y\|
for all

.

Proof.

The embedding
\Phi:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{4n}

gives:

|\left\langle x,\,y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}|=|\langle\Phi(x),\Phi(y)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{4n}}|\leq\|\Phi(x)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{4n}}\|\Phi(y)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{4n}}=\|x\|\,\|y\|.\qed

Definition 2.6

(Conjugate Transpose, Hermitian, Positive Definite)
.

A^{\dagger}=\bar{A}^{T}
.

Lemma 2.3

(Real-Valued Quadratic Form)
.

If

for all

Proof.

Via the real representation

of

(

\Phi
. Since

(iii)), the matrix
\Phi(P)
is real and

Alternative direct argument (no representation theory).

Lemma 2.5

(Projection onto
\mathrm{Im}(B)
via the Real Representation)
.

Under Assumption
3.1

.
Then

\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}=B_{\mathbb{R}}(B_{\mathbb{R}}^{T}B_{\mathbb{R}})^{-1}B_{\mathbb{R}}^{T}

\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}(x)=\Phi^{-1}\left(\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}\Phi(x)\right).

This operator satisfies
\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}^{2}=\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}
,

\mathrm{Im}(\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)})=\mathrm{Im}(B)

\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}(y)=y

\|\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1

Proof.

Full column rank of
B_{\mathbb{R}}
.

The real representation
\Phi:\mathbb{H}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{4m}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear
isomorphism (bijection). Hence every

has a unique preimage

. If
B_{\mathbb{R}}v=0
, then

\Phi(B)\Phi(u)=0
, so
\Phi(Bu)=0
by Lemma
2.4
,
and therefore
Bu=0
by injectivity of
\Phi
. Since
B
has full column
rank
m
over
\mathbb{H}
(Assumption
3.1
), we conclude
u=0
, hence

. Thus
\ker(B_{\mathbb{R}})=\{0\}
and
\mathrm{rank}(B_{\mathbb{R}})=4m
.

Consequently

is symmetric positive definite and invertible.

Define
\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}=B_{\mathbb{R}}(B_{\mathbb{R}}^{T}B_{\mathbb{R}})^{-1}B_{\mathbb{R}}^{T}
. This is the
standard orthogonal projection onto
\mathrm{Im}(B_{\mathbb{R}})
in
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
,
satisfying
\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}=\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}
and
\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}\Phi(B)=\Phi(B)

\Phi
is a bijection between
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{4n}

\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}^{2}(x)=\Phi^{-1}(\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}\Phi(x))))=\Phi^{-1}(\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}\Phi(x))=\Phi^{-1}(\Pi_{B_{\mathbb{R}}}\Phi(x))=\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}(x)

Image:

.

Thus
\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}
is indeed the projection onto
\mathrm{Im}(B)
.

holds strictly for generic boundary
points.

Lemma 2.6

(Eigenvalue Bounds)
.

For Hermitian
P\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
and
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
(so that

x^{\dagger}Px\in\mathbb{R}
by Lemma
2.3
):

(9)

\lambda_{\min}(P)\|x\|^{2}\leq x^{\dagger}Px\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)\|x\|^{2}.

For
P\succ 0
:

x^{\dagger}P^{2}x\leq\tfrac{\lambda_{\max}(P)^{2}}{\lambda_{\min}(P)}\,x^{\dagger}Px

and
\|Px\|^{2}=x^{\dagger}P^{2}x\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)^{2}\|x\|^{2}
,
so
\|Px\|\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)\|x\|
for all
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

For Hermitian
P
which is
not necessarily positive semi-definite
, the
operator-norm bound becomes

(10)

\|Px\|\leq\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|x\|=\max\{|\lambda_{\max}(P)|,|\lambda_{\min}(P)|\}\|x\|,

and the bound
\|Px\|\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)\|x\|
may fail in general.

Proof.

By Remark
2.13
,
\Phi(P)
is symmetric. Using the isometry

\Phi
and
x^{\dagger}Px=\langle\Phi(P)\Phi(x),\Phi(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{4n}}
,
one obtains:

\lambda_{\min}(\Phi(P))\|\Phi(x)\|^{2}\leq\langle\Phi(P)\Phi(x),\Phi(x)\rangle\leq\lambda_{\max}(\Phi(P))\|\Phi(x)\|^{2},

which gives the first inequality since
\|\Phi(x)\|=\|x\|
and

\lambda_{\min/\max}(P)=\lambda_{\min/\max}(\Phi(P))
.

Assume now
P\succ 0
. By Lemma
2.4
,

\Phi(P^{2})=\Phi(P)^{2}
.
Since
\Phi(P)
is symmetric and positive definite (Remark
2.13
),
its square has all eigenvalues equal to squares of those of
\Phi(P)

(which are all positive), giving

\lambda_{\max}(\Phi(P)^{2})=\lambda_{\max}(\Phi(P))^{2}
.
Hence
x^{\dagger}P^{2}x\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)^{2}\|x\|^{2}
.
From
x^{\dagger}Px\geq\lambda_{\min}(P)\|x\|^{2}
:

\|x\|^{2}\leq\lambda_{\min}(P)^{-1}x^{\dagger}Px
. Combining:

x^{\dagger}P^{2}x\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)^{2}\cdot\lambda_{\min}(P)^{-1}x^{\dagger}Px
.

For the operator norm bound under
P\succ 0
: working in the real representation,

\|Px\|^{2}=\|\Phi(P)\Phi(x)\|_{\mathbb{R}^{4n}}^{2}=\langle\Phi(P)^{2}\Phi(x),\Phi(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{4n}}\leq\lambda_{\max}(\Phi(P))^{2}\|\Phi(x)\|^{2}=\lambda_{\max}(P)^{2}\|x\|^{2},

giving
\|Px\|\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)\|x\|
.

For the general Hermitian case,
\Phi(P)
is symmetric and the spectral
theorem gives
\|\Phi(P)\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\max\{|\lambda_{\max}|,|\lambda_{\min}|\}
,
yielding (
10
).
∎

Remark 2.15

(Spectrum convention via
\Phi
)
.

Throughout this paper, for
P\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
Hermitian, the symbols

\lambda_{\min}(P)

\lambda_{\max}(P)

\Phi(P)\in\mathbb{R}^{4n\times 4n}

2.1
(iii)). Each

in the

-spectrum sense appears with multiplicity

Lemma 2.7

(Spectral Properties of Hermitian Quaternionic Operators)
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n}
be Hermitian (
P=P^{\dagger}
). Then:

(i)

All eigenvalues of
\Phi(P)
are real;

(ii)

\Phi(P)
is orthogonally diagonalizable over

(iii)

\lambda_{\min}(P)\leq\frac{x^{\dagger}Px}{\|x\|^{2}}\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)

By Proposition

Remark 2.16

(Connection to
S
-Spectrum Theory)

For general (non-Hermitian)

-spectrum

\sigma_{S}(T):=\{s\in\mathbb{H}:Q_{s}[T]\text{ is not boundedly invertible}\},

the standard object of the slice hyperholomorphic functional

[

, see also
[

.
The set

is axially symmetric:
for each

2.6.
GOSL Characterization of the Jacobi Defect

Assumption 2.1

(Carathéodory Regularity)
.

The map
(x,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},t)\mapsto\mathcal{J}(x,x+\xi_{1},x+\xi_{2},t)

is jointly Carathéodory in
(x,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
for every fixed
t
and
Lebesgue measurable in
t
for each fixed
(x,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
. Equivalently,

(x,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},t)\mapsto\|\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}[\mathcal{J}(x,x+\xi_{1},x+\xi_{2},t)]\|

is a normal integrand on
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n}\times[0,\infty)

in the sense of
[
12
, Chapter 14.D]
.

Remark 2.17

(Measurable Selection: Attainment and Joint Measurability)
.

Two points underlying Definition
2.8
warrant explicit
justification.

Attainment.
The constraint set
\{\|\xi_{i}\|\leq\varepsilon^{*}\}
is a
closed ball in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{4n}
, which is compact (Heine–Borel in
finite dimensions). The integrand

(x,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},t)\mapsto\|\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}\mathcal{J}(x,x+\xi_{1},x+\xi_{2},t)\|

is Carathéodory by Assumption
2.1
, hence continuous
in
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
for a.e.
t
. Maximization of a continuous function
over a compact set is therefore attained, so
\bar{\delta}(x,t)
in
equation (
12
) is indeed a maximum.

Joint measurability.
Under Assumption
2.1
, the
integrand above is a normal integrand in the sense
of
[
12
, Chapter 14.D]
. By the measurable selection
theorems of
[
12
, Chapter 14.A]
applied to argmax
correspondences of normal integrands, the argmax correspondence

(\xi_{1}^{*}(x,t),\xi_{2}^{*}(x,t))
admits a Borel-measurable selection
jointly in
(x,t)
.

State dependence of the selection.
The selection
\xi^{*}(x,t)

depends on
x
in general; this state dependence is the technical reason
why a naive trilinearity argument cannot establish the GOSL bound directly.
The proof of Lemma
2.8
addresses this issue by lifting
to a uniform supremum over both arguments simultaneously
(see (
19
)).

Definition 2.8

(Jacobi Defect)
.

Fix bracket constants
A>0
,
C_{1}\geq 0
,
C_{2}>0
, and
\varepsilon_{0}>0

(see Remark
2.5
for the meaning of
A
and
C_{1}
).
With the convention
\sqrt{1/(16\cdot 0)}:=+\infty
(i.e., the constraint is
inactive when
\mathcal{L}
is exactly antisymmetric), set:

(11)

\varepsilon^{*}=\min\!\left\{\tfrac{1}{16A},\;\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{16C_{1}}},\;\tfrac{1}{4C_{2}},\;\varepsilon_{0}\right\}.

Define the worst-case defect over a compact set
\mathcal{X}
with

M_{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{prior}}
an
a priori
bound:

(12)

\bar{\delta}(x,t)=\sup_{\|\xi_{i}\|\leq\varepsilon^{*}}\bigl\|\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}[\mathcal{J}(x,x+\xi_{1},x+\xi_{2},t)]\bigr\|,

(13)

\bar{\delta}_{\max}=6C_{2}\,M_{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{prior}}(M_{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{prior}}+\varepsilon^{*})^{2}.

By Remark
2.17
, the supremum is attained and admits
a jointly measurable selection
(\xi_{1}^{*}(x,t),\xi_{2}^{*}(x,t))
with

\|\xi_{i}^{*}\|\leq\varepsilon^{*}
, giving:

(14)

\delta(x,t)=\Pi_{\mathrm{Im}(B)}[\mathcal{J}(x,x+\xi_{1}^{*}(x,t),x+\xi_{2}^{*}(x,t),t)],\quad\|\delta(x,t)\|=\bar{\delta}(x,t)\leq\bar{\delta}_{\max}.

Consistency condition.

The
a posteriori
invariant set radius
M_{0}
is determined by
Algorithm
1
. Consistency requires
M_{0}\leq M_{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{prior}}
.
This is guaranteed by the following argument: Algorithm
1

initializes
M_{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{prior}}\leftarrow\|x(0)\|+\epsilon_{0}

(Step 1) and halves
M_{0}
until the invariance condition is satisfied.
Lemma
3.1
establishes finite termination

conditionally
on the sufficient conditions stated in
Remark
3.5
.

Definition 2.9

(Generalized OSL (GOSL))
.

f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
satisfies GOSL with constants
(\rho,\ell_{f})
,

\ell_{f}\geq 0
, on
\mathcal{X}
if:

(15)

\left\langle f(x)-f(z),\,x-z\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\leq\rho\|x-z\|^{2}+\ell_{f}\|x-z\|,\quad\forall\,x,z\in\mathcal{X}.

When
\ell_{f}=0
this reduces to the standard OSL condition.

Assumption 2.2

(Ball Domain)
.

Throughout Lemma
2.8
and Proposition
3.2
,

\mathcal{X}=\overline{B}_{M_{\mathcal{X}}}:=\{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\|x\|\leq M_{\mathcal{X}}\}

is a closed ball centered at the origin. Under this assumption the diameter satisfies

D_{\mathcal{X}}=\sup_{x,z\in\mathcal{X}}\|x-z\|\leq 2M_{\mathcal{X}}
.

Lemma 2.8

(GOSL Bound for the Projected Defect)
.

Under Definition
2.3
, Assumption
2.1
, and
Assumption
2.2
, the projected Jacobi defect
\delta
satisfies a
GOSL bound:

(16)

\left\langle\delta(x,t)-\delta(z,t),\,x-z\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\leq\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}\|x-z\|^{2}+\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}\|x-z\|,

with the
tight constants

(17)

\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}=2\rho_{\delta},\qquad\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}=4\bar{\delta}_{\max},\qquad\rho_{\delta}:=6C_{2}(M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*})(3M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*}).

The factor 2 in
\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}
and the factor 4 in

\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}
arise from the sub-optimality gap of the
state-dependent measurable selection
\xi^{*}(\cdot,t)
; they collapse to 1
when
\xi^{*}
admits a state-independent maximizer
(Remark
2.18
). The quantity
\rho_{\delta}

above is the
base
(non-tight) trilinear coefficient.

Proof.

Let
\Pi_{B}
denote the orthogonal projector onto
\mathrm{Im}(B)
, satisfying

\|\Pi_{B}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
(Lemma
2.5
).
Set
h=x-z
and
K=\overline{B}_{\varepsilon^{*}}^{\,\mathbb{H}^{n}}
.

Step 1 (Frozen-selection trick).

For any pair
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in K\times K
, define the

frozen-selection map

(18)

G_{\xi}(y,t):=\Pi_{B}\,\mathcal{J}_{t}(y,\,y+\xi_{1},\,y+\xi_{2}).

For each fixed
\xi
, the map
y\mapsto G_{\xi}(y,t)
is polynomial of degree
at most 3 in
y
(by
\mathbb{R}
-trilinearity of
\mathcal{J}_{t}
in its three
arguments, after expanding each
y+\xi_{i}
). The difference
G_{\xi}(y,t)-G_{\xi}(z,t)

admits an exact multilinear expansion (Step 4 below). The uniform bound

\|G_{\xi}(y,t)\|\leq\bar{\delta}_{\max}
holds uniformly in
\xi\in K\times K
and

y\in\mathcal{X}
(using the consistency
M_{\mathcal{X}}\leq M_{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{prior}}

of Definition
2.8
).

By Definition
2.8
,

\delta(x,t)=G_{\xi^{*}(x,t)}(x,t)
and
\delta(z,t)=G_{\xi^{*}(z,t)}(z,t)

where
\xi^{*}(\cdot,t)
is the (state-dependent) measurable selection.

Step 2 (Decomposition via the frozen
z
-selection).

Insert the intermediate term
G_{\xi^{*}(z,t)}(x,t)
:

(19)

\delta(x,t)-\delta(z,t)=\underbrace{\bigl[G_{\xi^{*}(x,t)}(x,t)-G_{\xi^{*}(z,t)}(x,t)\bigr]}_{=:E(x,z,t)}+\underbrace{\bigl[G_{\xi^{*}(z,t)}(x,t)-G_{\xi^{*}(z,t)}(z,t)\bigr]}_{=:D(x,z,t)}.

The decomposition is exact. The term
D(x,z,t)
has the
same
frozen
selection
\xi=\xi^{*}(z,t)
in both summands, so
\mathbb{R}
-trilinearity applies
directly. The term
E(x,z,t)
measures the sub-optimality gap arising from the
state dependence of
\xi^{*}
.

Step 3 (Bound on
E(x,z,t)
).

By the optimality of
\xi^{*}(x,t)
and the uniform bound

\|G_{\xi}(\cdot,t)\|\leq\bar{\delta}_{\max}
:

(20)

\|E(x,z,t)\|\leq\|G_{\xi^{*}(x,t)}(x,t)\|+\|G_{\xi^{*}(z,t)}(x,t)\|\leq 2\bar{\delta}_{\max}.

Hence by Cauchy–Schwarz (Lemma
2.2
):

(21)

\left\langle E(x,z,t),\,h\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\leq\|E(x,z,t)\|\,\|h\|\leq 2\bar{\delta}_{\max}\,\|h\|.

Step 4 (Bound on
D(x,z,t)
via
\mathbb{R}
-trilinearity at fixed
\xi
).

Set
\xi:=\xi^{*}(z,t)
and write
a=z
,
b=z+\xi_{1}
,
c=z+\xi_{2}
.
Using
\mathbb{R}
-trilinearity of
\mathcal{J}_{t}
at
fixed

\xi
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{t}(z+h,z+h+\xi_{1},z+h+\xi_{2})-\mathcal{J}_{t}(z,z+\xi_{1},z+\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\xi}+\Delta_{\mathrm{quad}}^{\xi}+\mathcal{J}_{t}(h,h,h),

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\xi}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{t}(h,b,c)+\mathcal{J}_{t}(a,h,c)+\mathcal{J}_{t}(a,b,h),

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{quad}}^{\xi}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{t}(h,h,c)+\mathcal{J}_{t}(h,b,h)+\mathcal{J}_{t}(a,h,h).

Using the
6C_{2}
trilinear bound and
\|a\|,\|z\|\leq M_{\mathcal{X}}
,

\|b\|,\|c\|\leq M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*}
:

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\xi}\|

\displaystyle\leq 6C_{2}\bigl[(M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*})^{2}+2M_{\mathcal{X}}(M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*})\bigr]\|h\|

(22)

\displaystyle=6C_{2}(M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*})(3M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*})\|h\|=\rho_{\delta}\|h\|.

For the quadratic term:

(23)

\|\Delta_{\mathrm{quad}}^{\xi}\|\leq 6C_{2}\bigl[2(M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*})+M_{\mathcal{X}}\bigr]\|h\|^{2}\leq 6C_{2}(3M_{\mathcal{X}}+2\varepsilon^{*})\|h\|^{2}.

Finally,
\|\mathcal{J}_{t}(h,h,h)\|\leq 6C_{2}\|h\|^{3}
.

Step 5 (Inner product bound on
D
).

A direct Cauchy–Schwarz bound
\left\langle D,\,h\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\leq\|D\|\,\|h\|
would
yield a
\|h\|^{3}
contribution from the cubic part
\mathcal{J}_{t}(h,h,h)
,
which is undesirable for the GOSL bound. We split:

(24)

D(x,z,t)=\Pi_{B}(\Delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\xi})+\Pi_{B}(\Delta_{\mathrm{quad}}^{\xi})+\Pi_{B}(\mathcal{J}_{t}(h,h,h)).

Linear part.
By Cauchy–Schwarz and (
22
):

(25)

\left\langle\Pi_{B}\Delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\xi},\,h\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\leq\|\Delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\xi}\|\,\|h\|\leq\rho_{\delta}\|h\|^{2}.

Quadratic and cubic parts (absorbed via uniform admissibility).

Let
Q:=\Pi_{B}(\Delta_{\mathrm{quad}}^{\xi})+\Pi_{B}(\mathcal{J}_{t}(h,h,h))
.
From the decomposition (
24
) we have

Q=D(x,z,t)-\Pi_{B}\Delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\xi}
. By the triangle inequality:

(26)

\|Q\|\leq\|D(x,z,t)\|+\|\Pi_{B}\Delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\xi}\|.

For the first summand,
\|D\|=\|G_{\xi^{*}(z,t)}(x,t)-G_{\xi^{*}(z,t)}(z,t)\|\leq 2\bar{\delta}_{\max}
since both evaluations of
G_{\xi^{*}(z,t)}
are admissible
(
\xi^{*}(z,t)\in K\times K
, and
x,z\in\mathcal{X}
, so the
uniform bound
\|G_{\xi}(\cdot,t)\|\leq\bar{\delta}_{\max}
from Step 1 applies to each).
For the second summand,
\|\Pi_{B}\Delta_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\xi}\|\leq\rho_{\delta}\|h\|

by (
22
) and
\|\Pi_{B}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
. Hence:

(27)

\|Q\|\leq 2\bar{\delta}_{\max}+\rho_{\delta}\|h\|.

By Cauchy–Schwarz:

(28)

\left\langle Q,\,h\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\leq\|Q\|\,\|h\|\leq 2\bar{\delta}_{\max}\|h\|+\rho_{\delta}\|h\|^{2}.

Combining (
25
) and (
28
):

(29)

\left\langle D(x,z,t),\,h\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\leq 2\rho_{\delta}\|h\|^{2}+2\bar{\delta}_{\max}\|h\|.

Step 6 (Combination).

From (
19
), (
21
), and (
29
):

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta(x,t)-\delta(z,t),\,h\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle E(x,z,t),\,h\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}+\left\langle D(x,z,t),\,h\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\bar{\delta}_{\max}\|h\|+2\rho_{\delta}\|h\|^{2}+2\bar{\delta}_{\max}\|h\|

\displaystyle=2\rho_{\delta}\|h\|^{2}+4\bar{\delta}_{\max}\|h\|.

This is precisely the GOSL bound (
16
) with the tight
constants
\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}=2\rho_{\delta}
and

\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}=4\bar{\delta}_{\max}
stated
in (
17
).
∎

Remark 2.18

(On the constant factors and comparison with classical OSL)
.

The factors
2
and
4
in the tight constants arise from the sub-optimality
gap
E(x,z,t)
(factor 2 for
\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}
from doubling
the trilinear bound; factor 4 in
\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}
from the
symmetric bound for both selections
\xi^{*}(x,t)
and
\xi^{*}(z,t)
). They
cannot be removed without an additional hypothesis on the regularity of the
selection
\xi^{*}(\cdot,t)
(e.g., Lipschitz continuity in
x
); when the
problem admits a state-independent maximizing selection (generic for
analytic
\mathcal{J}
on small domains), these factors collapse to
1
.
The affine term
\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}\|x-z\|
itself is a novel
feature arising from the Jacobi defect structure: in classical OSL
theory
[
10
]
one typically has
\ell_{f}=0
, whereas here

\delta(x,t)
need not vanish at
x=z
when the Jacobiator is non-zero.

Remark 2.19

(Conservativeness of the bound)
.

The GOSL bound established in Lemma
2.8
is

rigorous but not necessarily tight
. In particular, the cubic term

\mathcal{J}(h,h,h)
arising in the decomposition of
D(x,z,t)
(Step 5 of
the proof) is absorbed into the global bound
\|D\|\leq 2\bar{\delta}_{\max}

rather than separated and bounded by
6C_{2}\|h\|^{3}
. This is a

deliberate choice of estimation strategy
, not an error: separating
the cubic term would yield a sharper inequality of the form

\left\langle\delta(x,t)-\delta(z,t),\,x-z\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\leq\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{sharp}}\|h\|^{2}+\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{sharp}}\|h\|+C_{\mathrm{cubic}}\|h\|^{4},

which is no longer a standard GOSL form (it contains a quartic term). The
present formulation absorbs the cubic contribution into the affine

\ell
-term via
\|h\|\leq M_{\mathcal{X}}
, yielding an effective
upper bound consistent with the standard GOSL inequality and directly
usable in the LMI machinery of Section
6
. The price is a
conservative numerical estimate of the GOSL constants; the gain is full
compatibility with classical one-sided Lipschitz theory and the LMI
formulation of
\beta
-exponential stability
(Theorem
A.1
). A sharper GOSL-with-cubic-term formulation
remains an open direction.

Proposition 2.2

(Explicit Constants for Small Domains)
.

For
M_{\mathcal{X}}\ll\varepsilon^{*}
, the GOSL constants simplify to:

\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}\approx 12\,C_{2}(\varepsilon^{*})^{2},\qquad\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}\approx 24\,C_{2}M_{\mathcal{X}}(\varepsilon^{*})^{2}.

Proof.

Substitute
M_{\mathcal{X}}\ll\varepsilon^{*}
into (
17
):

(M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*})\approx\varepsilon^{*}
and
(3M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*})\approx\varepsilon^{*}
,
so
\rho_{\delta}\approx 6C_{2}{\varepsilon^{*}}^{2}
and the tight constant

\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}=2\rho_{\delta}\approx 12C_{2}(\varepsilon^{*})^{2}
.
Similarly
\bar{\delta}_{\max}\approx 6C_{2}M_{\mathcal{X}}{\varepsilon^{*}}^{2}
,
giving
\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}=4\bar{\delta}_{\max}\approx 24C_{2}M_{\mathcal{X}}(\varepsilon^{*})^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 3.1

Consequently, halving

of (

satisfies the invariance condition.

2
) with a

\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}

M_{0}
, exploiting the

\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}(M_{0})\to 2\rho_{\delta}(0)=12C_{2}(\varepsilon^{*})^{2}

(using

,

; see also

), plausibly yielding a feasible

3.5
.

Proof.

Scaling computations.

For
M_{0}\to 0
at
\varepsilon^{*}
fixed:
(M_{0}+\varepsilon^{*})^{2}\to(\varepsilon^{*})^{2}

(constant), so

and similarly
for
\bar{\delta}_{\max}
, hence
\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}=4\bar{\delta}_{\max}\sim 24C_{2}M_{0}(\varepsilon^{*})^{2}
. All linear in
M_{0}
.
The formula (
59
) (with
\mu_{*}=\mu_{\mathrm{LMI}}
and

\mu

C_{\infty}(\mu,M_{0})=\frac{\mu_{*}^{2}(c_{C}\,c_{B}\,c_{B^{+}}R_{\max})^{2}}{\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}-\mu}+\frac{\mu_{*}(\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}})^{2}}{\mu}=O(M_{0}^{2}).

The constant

in (

\kappa_{\infty}=\frac{\mu_{*}^{2}(c_{C}\,c_{B}\,c_{B^{+}}C_{\mathrm{nc}}(\varepsilon^{*})^{2})^{2}}{\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}-\mu}+\frac{\mu_{*}(24C_{2}(\varepsilon^{*})^{2})^{2}}{\mu}+O(M_{0}).

Both sides of

Remark 3.5

(Status of the termination argument)
.

The termination of Algorithm
1
is established only
conditionally in this paper, under sufficient conditions that are
classically expected but not formally proved here.
Specifically, the
argument of Lemma
3.1
rests on the following two

sufficient conditions
, each of which we treat as a standing
conjecture of the algorithmic framework:

(a)

Continuity of the LMI solution map
(conjectured).
The map
M_{0}\mapsto(P^{*}(M_{0}),\beta^{*}(M_{0}))

is conjectured to be locally Lipschitz continuous on the open set of strict
feasibility. This is classically expected (one can argue heuristically via
the implicit function theorem applied to the KKT conditions of the
LMI (
57
); see Remark
6.3
(i)
for the framework), but
a formal proof is not provided in this paper
.
The local Lipschitz constant, if it exists, can in principle be bounded
explicitly via the protocol of Remark
6.3
(ii).

(b)

Strict feasibility at the limit (assumed).
The
limit LMI (with
\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}=12C_{2}(\varepsilon^{*})^{2}
) is

assumed
to be strictly feasible (Assumption
6.2
).
This is verified analytically for normal
A_{s}

(Lemma
6.1
) and must be verified numerically
otherwise.

Acknowledged limitation.
Under (a) and (b),
termination of the halving loop is
plausible
but not formally
guaranteed here. In particular:

•

No quantitative
a priori
bound on the number of halvings is
provided (this would require explicit Lipschitz constants for the LMI map,
which depend on the specific
A_{s}
structure).

•

The continuity (a) is a
sufficient condition
,
conjectured but not proved here. A full proof would
require a careful application of the implicit function theorem to the
parametric SDP defining the LMI solution, accounting for active-set
transitions; this is beyond our scope.

•

In practice, termination is observed empirically within a handful of
halvings (the test system of Section
7
terminates after one
iteration), but this empirical evidence does not constitute a proof.

A fully unconditional termination proof, together with a quantitative
bound on the number of halvings, is an open problem listed in the future
directions of Section
8
.

4.
Cohomological Matching Condition

Classical sliding-mode control requires the matched-perturbation condition

\delta(x,t)\in\mathrm{Im}(B)
for every state and time — a check that
must be performed trajectory by trajectory. We replace it with a
structural condition that splits the requirement in two: a cohomological
part, which holds automatically in the homogeneous case, and an algebraic
part on the pair
(B,\mathcal{L})
alone, checked once at design time.

Definition 4.1

(CMC)
.

For clarity, define the
evaluated coboundary image

\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{eval}}(d):=\{d\omega(x,y,z):\omega\in C^{2}_{\varepsilon},\;x,y,z\in B(0,\varepsilon^{*})\}\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}.

The system satisfies the
Cohomological Matching Condition
if:

(CMC-1)

Cohomological condition:

\delta(x,t)\in\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{eval}}(d)
for all
(x,t)

(Assumption
3.3
). This holds unconditionally for homogeneous
quasi-Lie brackets (
[
1
, Theorem 4.12, Lemma B.1]
).

(CMC-2)

Structural condition:

\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{eval}}(d)\subseteq\mathrm{Im}(B)\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
This is a condition on the pair
(B,\mathcal{L})
alone (independent of
trajectories) and is verified once at design time; see
Remark
4.1
.

Both conditions are formulated within the Chevalley–Eilenberg
complex
(C^{*}_{\varepsilon},d)
of Definition
2.4

(where
d^{2}=0
holds in the homogeneous case
by
[
1
, Lemma B.1]
). We abbreviate

\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{eval}}(d)
as
\mathrm{im}(d)
in the sequel when the
context is unambiguous.

Remark 4.1

(Verification of CMC-2: Structural Condition on
(B,\mathcal{L})
)
.

Condition (CMC-2) is not a consequence of (CMC-1); it is an independent
algebraic condition on the control matrix
B
and the bracket
\mathcal{L}
.
Concretely, it requires that for all
\omega\in C^{2}_{\varepsilon}

and all
x,y,z\in B(0,\varepsilon^{*})
:

d\omega(x,y,z)\in\mathrm{Im}(B).

This can be verified as follows: compute a generating family

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
for
\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{eval}}(d)

using the structure of
\mathcal{L}
(e.g., by evaluating
d\omega_{0}
for the test bracket
of Section
7
at sufficiently many triplets), and check that each

e_{i}
belongs to
\mathrm{Im}(B_{\mathbb{R}})=\Phi(\mathrm{Im}(B))\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{4n}

in the real representation. For the test system of Section
7
,
this is verified directly in Lemma
7.1
.

(CMC Implies Defect Matching)

Corollary 4.1

(Exact Defect Rejection and Cohomological Decomposition)

Under Assumptions

(i)

The feedforward term

(ii)

\delta

exactly (part (i)), the cohomological decomposition does

defined
in (

Proof.

(i)
\delta\in\mathrm{Im}(B)
by Proposition

(ii) Apply

(iii) Substituting

enters only when

Closed-loop dynamics.

Remark 5.1

(Implementability of
\Delta_{\mathrm{app}}
: Quantitative
Bounds for the Approximation Error)
.

The feedforward
\Delta_{\mathrm{app}}
in (
52
) requires
knowledge of
\xi^{*}(x,t)
, the maximizing selection of the Jacobi defect
amplitude, which is in general non-trivial to compute online. We discuss
three implementation strategies with their respective error bounds.

(i) Worst-case fixed selection.
Replace
\xi^{*}(x,t)
by a fixed

\xi^{\mathrm{wc}}\in K\times K
achieving the supremum of

\|G_{\xi}(\cdot,t)\|
over a numerical grid. The implementation error is

\|\delta(x,t)-G_{\xi^{\mathrm{wc}}}(x,t)\|
, which by (
20
)
of the proof of Lemma
2.8
is bounded by

2\bar{\delta}_{\max}
. The closed-loop sliding dynamics
in (
55
) then carry an additional disturbance term of
magnitude at most
c_{C}\,c_{B}(c_{B^{+}}R_{\max}+2\bar{\delta}_{\max})
, which
is absorbed by enlarging
\eta
to

\eta=c_{C}\,c_{B}(c_{B^{+}}R_{\max}+2\bar{\delta}_{\max})+\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}c_{C}M_{0}+\eta_{0}.

All stability conclusions of Theorem
5.1
remain valid under
this enlarged gain.

(ii) Grid-based sampling.
Sample the constraint set
K\times K
on a

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{grid}}
-net and compute
\xi^{\mathrm{grid}}(x,t)
as
the discrete maximizer. By Lipschitz continuity of the Jacobiator in
\xi

(via Definition
2.3
and Assumption
2.1
), the
error is bounded by

\|\delta(x,t)-G_{\xi^{\mathrm{grid}}(x,t)}(x,t)\|\leq L_{\mathcal{J}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{grid}},

where
L_{\mathcal{J}}
is the Lipschitz constant of
\mathcal{J}
in

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
on
K\times K
. For the test system,

L_{\mathcal{J}}\leq 6C_{2}(M_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{*})^{2}
. Choosing

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{grid}}=c_{B^{+}}R_{\max}/L_{\mathcal{J}}
ensures the
grid error matches the existing residual margin and no enlargement of
\eta

is needed.

(iii) Convex approximation.
If
\mathcal{J}(x,x+\xi_{1},x+\xi_{2},t)

is a polynomial of degree at most 3 in
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
(as in the

\mathbb{R}
-multilinear case), the supremum over the compact set
K\times K
can
be computed via polynomial optimization techniques (e.g., the Lasserre
hierarchy
[
12
]
) with guaranteed convergence to within

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cvx}}
in finite time. The approximation error is then
bounded by
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cvx}}
, absorbed similarly to (ii).

General principle.
The mathematical analysis of
Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
6.1
remains
valid as long as the implemented feedforward
\widetilde{\Delta}
satisfies

\|B\widetilde{\Delta}-\delta\|\leq c_{B^{+}}R_{\max}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{impl}}

pointwise for some additional implementation error
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{impl}}\geq 0
, with the proviso that
\eta
in (
54
) is enlarged
to
\eta+c_{C}\,c_{B}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{impl}}
to absorb the
implementation residual.

The

from
[

The effective trilinear constant

Lemma 6.1

(Sufficient Condition for LMI Monotonicity: Normal
A_{s}
)
.

Suppose
\Phi(A_{s})\in\mathbb{R}^{4n\times 4n}
is
normal
, i.e.,

\Phi(A_{s})^{T}\Phi(A_{s})=\Phi(A_{s})\Phi(A_{s})^{T}

\Phi(A_{s})
admits an orthonormal eigenbasis with eigenvalues

\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4n}\subset\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{i})<0

(Hurwitz, by Assumption

). Then LMI (
57
)
admits a closed-form solution

P^{*}(\mu)=\tau(\mu)I,\qquad\beta^{*}(\mu)=-\frac{2\alpha_{s}-\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}\mu}{\tau(\mu)},

where
\alpha_{s}=-\max_{i}\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{i})>0
and
\tau(\mu)\in[1,\mu]

is any feasible scalar (e.g.,
\tau(\mu)=1
). In particular,

\mu\mapsto\lambda_{\max}(P^{*}(\mu))=\tau(\mu)
can be chosen constant,
so monotonicity holds trivially.

Proof.

Since
\Phi(A_{s})
is normal,
\Phi(A_{s})^{T}\Phi(A_{s})
is diagonalizable
by the same orthonormal eigenbasis as
\Phi(A_{s})
; under

3.8
,
(\Phi(A_{s})^{T}+\Phi(A_{s}))/2
has spectral

. We work with the simplified form of the LMI
recorded in Remark

(which is a conservative

) obtained by omitting the

\mu_{\mathrm{Y}}
); setting

:

\tau(\Phi(A_{s})^{T}+\Phi(A_{s}))+\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}\mu I\prec-\beta\tau I,

\Phi(A_{s})^{T}+\Phi(A_{s})\prec-(\beta+\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}\mu/\tau)I
.
Using
\Phi(A_{s})^{T}+\Phi(A_{s})\preceq-2\alpha_{s}I

(from the spectral abscissa bound), the LMI is strictly feasible whenever

\tau=1
,

\beta<2\alpha_{s}-\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}\mu

and positive iff

The analogous calculation for the rigorous

Remark 6.3

(Scope of the normality condition and verification for general
A_{s}
)
.

The normality condition
\Phi(A_{s})^{T}\Phi(A_{s})=\Phi(A_{s})\Phi(A_{s})^{T}

is automatically satisfied when

is itself a real diagonal matrix
(the test system in Section
7
has

, hence

, the monotonicity
condition of Assumption

must be checked
case-by-case. The following observations make the numerical verification

robust
:

(i)

Continuity of

.
The map

\mu\mapsto P^{*}(\mu)
defined as the solution of the LMI parametrized by

by the implicit function theorem applied to the KKT conditions of the LMI
(this is a standard consequence of the smoothness of the SDP solution
map; see
[
14
]
for background on parameter-dependent Lyapunov
inequalities and the regularity of their solutions). Consequently,

\mu\mapsto\lambda_{\max}(P^{*}(\mu))
is continuous, and monotonicity can be
verified on a finite
\varepsilon
-grid with error bounded by

L_{P}\,\varepsilon
(where
L_{P}
is the local Lipschitz constant of

\lambda_{\max}(P^{*}(\cdot))
).

(ii)

Practical verification protocol.
For a given
non-normal
A_{s}
:

(a)

Choose a grid
\{\mu_{0}>\mu_{1}>\cdots>\mu_{N}\}
on the
interval
[\mu_{\infty},\mu_{0}]
with step
\varepsilon
.

(b)

Solve LMI (
57
) at each

to obtain

and
\lambda_{\max}(P^{*}(\mu_{i}))
.

(c)

Check that the discrete sequence

is non-increasing in

.

(d)

By (i), if the discrete sequence is non-increasing with margin

, then the continuous map is non-increasing on

(iii)

Sufficient algebraic conditions.
Beyond
normality, monotonicity holds in the following cases:

•

\Phi(A_{s})
commutes with
\Phi(A_{s})^{T}
(normality);

•

is symmetric stable:

on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
;

•

\Phi(A_{s})
admits a Schur decomposition
\Phi(A_{s})=Q^{T}TQ

For the broader class of non-normal

outside these cases,

is verified numerically via the

(

Remark 6.6

(Sub-optimality of the Young parameter choice)
.

The bound
C_{\infty}(\mu)
depends on the Young parameter
\mu\in(0,\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}})
,
which can be chosen independently of the LMI scaling
\mu_{*}=\lambda_{\max}(P^{*})
.
The analytically optimal value of
\mu
minimizing
C_{\infty}(\mu)
is obtained
by setting
\partial C_{\infty}/\partial\mu=0
. From

C_{\infty}(\mu)=\mu_{*}^{2}w_{\max}^{2}/(\rho-\mu)+\mu_{*}\ell_{g}^{2}/\mu

(with
w_{\max}=c_{C}c_{B}c_{B^{+}}R_{\max}
,
\ell_{g}=\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}
,

\rho=\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}

\frac{\partial C_{\infty}}{\partial\mu}=\frac{\mu_{*}^{2}w_{\max}^{2}}{(\rho-\mu)^{2}}-\frac{\mu_{*}\ell_{g}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\mu_{*}w_{\max}\,\mu=\sqrt{\mu_{*}}\,\ell_{g}\,(\rho-\mu),

yielding

(60)

\mu_{\mathrm{opt}}=\frac{\rho\,\sqrt{\mu_{*}}\,\ell_{g}}{\mu_{*}\,w_{\max}+\sqrt{\mu_{*}}\,\ell_{g}}=\frac{\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{eff}}\,(\mu_{*})^{1/2}\,\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}}{\mu_{*}\,c_{C}\,c_{B}\,c_{B^{+}}\,R_{\max}+(\mu_{*})^{1/2}\,\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}}}.

\mu_{*}^{1/2}
in the numerator and in the second term of
the denominator;
\mu_{*}^{1}
in the first term of the denominator.
For the test system (

,

Test System: A Quasi-Lie Bracket on

Consider

\mathcal{L}(x,y):=\varepsilon_{b}\bigl(\bar{x}y-\bar{y}x\bigr),\qquad\varepsilon_{b}=0.1.

Remark 7.1

(Properties of the test bracket)
.

The bracket (
61
) is:

•

\mathbb{R}
-bilinear
(the conjugation

is

\mathbb{R}
-linear on
\mathbb{H}

\mathbb{H}
-bilinear in the right-module sense.

•

Skew-symmetric

•

Genuinely quasi-Lie

(Appendix

\mathcal{J}(x,y,z):=\mathcal{L}(x,\mathcal{L}(y,z))+\mathcal{L}(y,\mathcal{L}(z,x))+\mathcal{L}(z,\mathcal{L}(x,y))

is non-zero (e.g.,
\mathcal{J}(1+\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})=0.04\,\mathbf{i}\neq 0

for
\varepsilon_{b}=0.1

vanishes when

, which satisfies Jacobi exactly).
This distinguishes (
61
) from the standard quaternionic
commutator

, which is
\mathbb{H}
-linear and satisfies
the Jacobi identity exactly.

The Carathéodory regularity (Assumption
2.1
) is satisfied
because

is a polynomial map.

(Numerical computation of constants)
.

For (

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\sup_{x,y\neq 0}\frac{\|\mathcal{L}(x,y)\|}{\|x\|\,\|y\|}=2\varepsilon_{b}=0.2,

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=0\quad(\text{$\mathcal{L}$ exactly antisymmetric: $\mathcal{L}(y,x)=-\mathcal{L}(x,y)$}),

\displaystyle 6\,C_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x,y,z\neq 0}\frac{\|\mathcal{J}(x,y,z)\|}{\|x\|\,\|y\|\,\|z\|}\approx 0.0399,\quad\text{hence}\quad C_{2}\approx 0.00665.

\bar{x}y-\bar{y}x

Note.
The quantity

8.
Conclusion

We close with a brief assessment of what was achieved, what was not, and
where the natural follow-up problems lie.

On the operator-theoretic side, Theorem
2.1
gives an
exact identity rather than the bounded ratio one would expect from
elementary considerations:
\|T^{L}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}
,
with no constant factor. The proof is short, but its consequence is
substantial — every quantitative bound of
[
1
]
now
applies verbatim to left-module formulations, which is the convention
preferred in attitude dynamics. We also restate the rigidity theorem
of
[
1
, Theorem 4.12]
in a form (Theorem
2.2
)
that tracks the constants needed in the control argument, and isolate
the auxiliary residual
R_{0}=\mathcal{J}-d\omega_{0}
that appears
in the cohomological feedforward. The third operator-theoretic
ingredient, Lemma
2.8
, is a GOSL bound for the
projected Jacobi defect with constants

(\rho_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}},\ell_{\delta}^{\mathrm{tight}})=(2\rho_{\delta},4\bar{\delta}_{\max})
. The factors of
2
and
4

encode the suboptimality cost of using a state-dependent measurable
selection; they collapse to
1
in the rare case where a
state-independent maximizer exists. The proof handles the state
dependence by freezing the selection and using
\mathbb{R}
-trilinearity —
not a difficult trick, but one that is easy to overlook.

These ingredients combine in Section
5
into a sliding-mode
controller whose feedforward
\Delta_{\mathrm{app}}
uses the explicit

\omega_{0}
rather than the unknown
\delta
. The closed-loop sliding
dynamics reduce to

\dot{s}=-\eta\,\mathrm{sgn}_{\mathbb{H}}(s)-Ks+\tilde{w}

with
\tilde{w}=G\,R_{\mathrm{nc}}^{B}
and

\|\tilde{w}\|\leq c_{C}\,c_{B}\,c_{B^{+}}\,R_{\max}
. Finite-time
reaching follows from Theorem
5.1
, and

\beta
-exponential stability of the reduced dynamics from
Proposition
6.1
. The circular dependence between
gain and domain — a well-known practical nuisance — is resolved by
the explicit ordering and halving loop of Algorithm
1
.
Termination is established conditionally, under the sufficient
conditions of Remark
3.5
; both conditions are
classically expected but not proved here.

What remains open is worth stating clearly. The forward-invariance
result (Proposition
3.2
) gives a trajectory bound

\|x_{r}(t)\|\leq R
in the general case, but strict inclusion

\overline{B}_{R}\subseteq\overline{B}_{M_{0}}
requires
\kappa(P^{*})=1
.
For non-trivial Lyapunov shaping
P^{*}\neq I
the strict inclusion
remains beyond reach without further hypotheses. Termination of the
halving loop is conditional on continuity of the LMI solution map and
strict feasibility at the limit; both are expected to hold under mild
hypotheses but a formal proof is not given. The strict feasibility of
the iterative LMI (
57
) is verified case by case in
the test bracket; a general structural characterization is open.
Sobolev-type generalizations of the rigidity theorem would broaden the
scope significantly but are conjectural. Multidimensional closed-loop
simulations are deferred to a companion paper, where the cohomological
feedforward will be tested on rigid-body attitude control (
n=4
,
m=3
)
and coupled oscillator networks.

Further directions.

An adaptive gain
\eta
using online estimates of
R_{\max}
would
reduce conservatism in the switching gain; a quantitative bound on the
number of halvings in Algorithm
1
, exploiting explicit
Lipschitz constants of the LMI parameter map, would give an
unconditional complexity estimate. On the algorithmic side,
verifying (CMC-2) when
\mathrm{Im}(B)\subsetneq\mathbb{H}^{n}
requires
computing
\mathrm{im}(d)\cap\mathrm{Im}(B)
explicitly; effective
methods for this in higher dimensions are an open question. The
interplay between the
S
-spectrum of
\Phi(A_{s})
and the
cohomological structure of
\mathcal{L}
also deserves attention.

Theorem A.1

(
\beta
-Exponential Stability under Dissipative Perturbations)
.

Consider the perturbed linear system

(64)

\dot{x}=Ax+g(x,t)+w(t),

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is Hurwitz,
g
satisfies the GOSL bound

(65)

\langle g(x,t)-g(z,t),x-z\rangle\leq\rho\|x-z\|^{2}+\ell_{g}\|x-z\|

with constants
\rho\geq 0
,
\ell_{g}\geq 0
,
\|g(0,t)\|\leq\ell_{g}

for all
t
, the dissipativity-type bound
\|g(x,t)-g(0,t)\|\leq\rho\|x\|

holds (so
\|g(x,t)\|\leq\rho\|x\|+\ell_{g}
), and
\|w(t)\|\leq w_{\max}
.

The GOSL bound (
65
) is recorded here as the
characterization of the defect that motivates the present setting; the
proof below uses only the dissipativity-type bound

\|g(x,t)\|\leq\rho\|x\|+\ell_{g}
, which is the consequence relevant for
the Lyapunov calculation.

Fix
\mu\in(0,\rho)
and suppose there exist
P=P^{T}\succ 0
and
\beta>0

such that

(66)

A^{T}P+PA+\bigl[\mu_{*}(2\rho+\mu)+(\rho-\mu)\bigr]I\preceq-\beta P,\qquad I\preceq P\preceq\mu_{*}I,

where
\mu_{*}:=\lambda_{\max}(P)
. Then for
V(x):=x^{T}Px
and any
trajectory of (
64
):

(67)

V(x(t))\leq e^{-\beta t}V(x(0))+\frac{\Gamma(\mu)}{\beta},

where

(68)

\Gamma(\mu):=\frac{\mu_{*}^{2}w_{\max}^{2}}{\rho-\mu}+\frac{\mu_{*}\ell_{g}^{2}}{\mu},\qquad\mu\in(0,\rho).

Proof.

Let
V=x^{T}Px
. Then

\dot{V}=2x^{T}PAx+2x^{T}Pg(x,t)+2x^{T}Pw(t).

Step 1 (Bound on the GOSL term).
By Cauchy–Schwarz and

\|Px\|\leq\mu_{*}\|x\|
:

2x^{T}Pg(x,t)\leq 2\|Px\|\,\|g(x,t)\|\leq 2\mu_{*}\|x\|(\rho\|x\|+\ell_{g})=2\mu_{*}\rho\|x\|^{2}+2\mu_{*}\ell_{g}\|x\|.

Apply Young’s inequality
2ab\leq a^{2}/\theta+\theta b^{2}
with

a=\mu_{*}\ell_{g}
,
b=\|x\|
,
\theta=\mu_{*}\mu
:

2\mu_{*}\ell_{g}\|x\|\leq\frac{(\mu_{*}\ell_{g})^{2}}{\mu_{*}\mu}+\mu_{*}\mu\|x\|^{2}=\frac{\mu_{*}\ell_{g}^{2}}{\mu}+\mu_{*}\mu\|x\|^{2}.

Therefore

(69)

2x^{T}Pg(x,t)\leq\mu_{*}(2\rho+\mu)\|x\|^{2}+\frac{\mu_{*}\ell_{g}^{2}}{\mu}.

Step 2 (Bound on the disturbance term).
By Cauchy–Schwarz and Young
with
a=\mu_{*}w_{\max}
,
b=\|x\|
,
\theta=\rho-\mu>0
:

(70)

2x^{T}Pw\leq 2\mu_{*}w_{\max}\|x\|\leq\frac{\mu_{*}^{2}w_{\max}^{2}}{\rho-\mu}+(\rho-\mu)\|x\|^{2}.

Step 3 (Linear part).
The LMI (
66
) gives directly

(71)

2x^{T}PAx=x^{T}(A^{T}P+PA)x\leq-\bigl[\mu_{*}(2\rho+\mu)+(\rho-\mu)\bigr]\|x\|^{2}-\beta x^{T}Px.

Step 4 (Combination).
Adding (
69
), (
70
),
(
71
), the terms in
\|x\|^{2}
cancel exactly:

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle\leq-\beta V\underbrace{-\bigl[\mu_{*}(2\rho+\mu)+(\rho-\mu)\bigr]\|x\|^{2}+\mu_{*}(2\rho+\mu)\|x\|^{2}+(\rho-\mu)\|x\|^{2}}_{=\,0}+\frac{\mu_{*}\ell_{g}^{2}}{\mu}+\frac{\mu_{*}^{2}w_{\max}^{2}}{\rho-\mu}

\displaystyle=-\beta V+\Gamma(\mu),

with
\Gamma(\mu)
as in (
68
). Gronwall’s inequality applied
to
\dot{V}\leq-\beta V+\Gamma(\mu)
yields (
67
).
∎

.

Appendix B
Verification of the Test Bracket

We verify that the test bracket (
61
)

\mathcal{L}(x,y)=\varepsilon_{b}(\bar{x}y-\bar{y}x)
has a non-zero
Jacobiator. Direct computation with
x=\mathbf{i}
,
y=\mathbf{j}
,

z=\mathbf{k}
yields:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{b}(\overline{\mathbf{i}}\mathbf{j}-\overline{\mathbf{j}}\mathbf{i})=\varepsilon_{b}(-\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}-(-\mathbf{j})\mathbf{i})=\varepsilon_{b}(-\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{j}\mathbf{i})=\varepsilon_{b}(-\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k})=-2\varepsilon_{b}\,\mathbf{k},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=-2\varepsilon_{b}\,\mathbf{i},\quad\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{i})=-2\varepsilon_{b}\,\mathbf{j}.

Then, for
x,y,z
all purely imaginary, the bracket reduces to

\mathcal{L}(p,q)=\varepsilon_{b}(-pq+qp)=-\varepsilon_{b}\,[p,q]_{\mathbb{H}}

(using
\bar{p}=-p
for
p
purely imaginary). Hence on the imaginary
sub-algebra (isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}(3)
),
\mathcal{L}
is proportional
to the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{so}(3)
, which
satisfies the Jacobi
identity exactly
. In particular:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{i},\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{i},-2\varepsilon_{b}\mathbf{i})=-2\varepsilon_{b}^{2}(\overline{\mathbf{i}}\mathbf{i}-\overline{\mathbf{i}}\mathbf{i})=0,

and similarly the other two terms vanish, so
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})=0
.
To exhibit a non-zero Jacobiator, we must choose at least one argument with
non-zero real part. Taking
x=1+\mathbf{i}
,
y=\mathbf{j}
,

z=\mathbf{k}
and
\varepsilon_{b}=0.1
, direct computation yields

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})=-2\varepsilon_{b}\,\mathbf{i}
,

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{k},1+\mathbf{i})=-2\varepsilon_{b}(\mathbf{j}+\mathbf{k})
,

\mathcal{L}(1+\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j})=2\varepsilon_{b}(\mathbf{j}-\mathbf{k})
,
and

\mathcal{J}(1+\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})=0.04\,\mathbf{i}\neq 0,

so
\|\mathcal{J}\|=0.04
for these inputs. The general supremum bound

\sup_{x,y,z}\|\mathcal{J}(x,y,z)\|/(\|x\|\|y\|\|z\|)\approx 0.0399

reported in Remark
7.2
establishes that the bracket is
genuinely quasi-Lie.
