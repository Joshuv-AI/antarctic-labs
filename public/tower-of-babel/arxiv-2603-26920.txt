Obstructions to Smooth Full-Holonomy Cayley Fibrations
Viktor Majewski, Jacek Rzemieniecki

Abstract
We study smooth fibrations of compact torsion-free Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) {\mathrm{Spin}}(7) -manifolds by Cayley submanifolds. Using geometric and topological constraints coming from the Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) {\mathrm{Spin}}(7) -structure, we show that only two topological configurations can arise. One of these is excluded by a spinnability criterion for fiber bundles, with the relevant hypothesis verified using gauge-theoretic input, while the remaining case is reduced to an open conjecture in 4 4 -manifold topology. In particular, this rules out smooth Cayley fibrations on all known examples of compact torsion-free Spin ⁡ ( 7 ) {\mathrm{Spin}}(7) -manifolds.

Introduction

Manifolds with exceptional holonomy provide a fundamental class of Riemannian manifolds with rigid geometric structure. In dimension eight, torsion-free
{\mathrm{Spin}}(7)
-manifolds are characterized by a parallel Cayley calibration, whose calibrated submanifolds are volume-minimizing
4
-dimensional submanifolds. This naturally leads to the question of whether such manifolds contain global fibrations by Cayley submanifolds, and to what extent the existence of such a fibration is compatible with full
{\mathrm{Spin}}(7)
-holonomy.

These fibrations are the
{\mathrm{Spin}}(7)
-analogues of special Lagrangian fibrations in Calabi-Yau geometry and coassociative fibrations in
G_{2}
-geometry. A guiding principle in all these settings is that global fibrations by calibrated submanifolds are highly constrained and typically forced to develop singularities.

This phenomenon is already visible in
G_{2}
-geometry. In the study of semi-flat
G_{2}
-manifolds and their coassociative fibrations, Baraglia
2

G_{2}
-holonomy manifolds fibering over a
3

{\mathrm{Spin}}(7)
-version is conjectured to be true
(
13

Conjecture 1.1
.

A torsion-free
{\mathrm{Spin}}(7)
-manifold with full holonomy does not admit a smooth Cayley fibration.

Spin(7)-Background

Let
(W,g)
be an eight dimensional Euclidean vector space. The group
{\mathrm{Spin}}(W,g)\cong\mathrm{Spin}(8)
has three real eight-dimensional representations: the vector representation on the Euclidean space
(W,g)
, and two chiral spinor representations
\mathbb{S}^{+}_{g}
and
\mathbb{S}^{-}_{g}
. Both
\mathbb{S}^{+}_{g}
and
\mathbb{S}^{-}_{g}
can be endowed with
\mathrm{Spin}(8)
-invariant inner products, such that the Clifford multiplication

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}_{g}\colon W\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{S}^{+}_{g},\mathbb{S}^{-}_{g})\oplus\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{S}^{-}_{g},\mathbb{S}^{+}_{g})

is skew-adjoint. Given a unit length positive spinor
\psi_{g}\in\mathbb{S}^{+}
, we can identify both

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}_{g}(\bullet)\psi_{g}\colon W\cong\mathbb{S}^{-}_{g}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274pt{\mathrm{Stab}}(\psi_{g})\cong{\mathrm{Spin}}(7).

Since
{\mathrm{Stab}}(\psi_{g})
fixes the Euclidean structure on
(W,g)
we deduce that
{\mathrm{Spin}}(7)\subset{\mathrm{SO}}(8)
. Using
\psi_{g}
we can define the
Cayley form

\displaystyle\Phi\coloneqq\left<\psi_{g},{\mathrm{cl}}^{4}_{g}\circ\psi_{g}\right>\in\wedge^{4}W^{*}.

The stabilizer of
\Phi
under the
{\mathrm{GL}}(W)
action on
\wedge^{4}W^{*}
agrees with
{\mathrm{Spin}}(7)
and (up to a sign) we can recover
\psi_{g}
and

from the associated Cayley form.

The exterior algebra
\wedge^{\bullet}W^{*}
decomposes into the following
{\mathrm{Spin}}(7)
-representations

\displaystyle\wedge^{0}W^{*}\cong\wedge^{0}_{1},\hskip 28.45274pt\wedge^{1}W^{*}\cong\wedge^{1}_{\Phi,8}

\displaystyle\,\hskip 28.45274pt\wedge^{2}W^{*}\cong\wedge^{2}_{\Phi,21}\oplus\wedge^{2}_{\Phi,7},\hskip 28.45274pt\wedge^{3}W^{*}\cong\wedge^{3}_{\Phi,8}\oplus\wedge^{3}_{\Phi,48}

and

\displaystyle\wedge^{4}W^{*}\cong\wedge^{4}_{\Phi,1}\oplus\wedge^{4}_{\Phi,7}\oplus\wedge^{4}_{\Phi,27}\oplus\wedge^{4}_{\Phi,35}

indexed by their dimension, such that
\wedge^{k}_{\Phi,\rho}\cong\wedge^{k^{\prime}}_{\Phi,\rho}
and moreover
\wedge^{k}W^{*}\cong\wedge^{8-k}W^{*}

The Cayley form is an example of a (linear) calibration and satisfies the calibration equality

\displaystyle|P|^{2}=\Phi(P)^{2}+|\pi_{\psi_{g}^{\perp}}({\mathrm{cl}}_{g}(P))\psi_{g}|^{2}

for all oriented four planes

. Such a four plane will be referred to as a
Cayley plane
if

\displaystyle\Phi(P)=1\hskip 28.45274pt\text{or equivalently}\hskip 28.45274pt\pi_{\psi_{g}^{\perp}}({\mathrm{cl}}_{g}(P))\psi_{g}=0.

.

In particular, a four plane

is Cayley and the stabilizer of a Cayley plane decomposition of

cannot be spin which contradicts that
F
satisfies Property (S).
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

A smooth manifold
F
homeomorphic to a
K3
surface satisfies Property (S).

Proof.

If the fiber is a
K3
surface then this is already contained in
(
23
, Proposition 2.1)
. In general, we can deduce it from the following result of Baraglia.

Proposition 4.5
.

(
4
, Proposition 7.6)

Let
p\colon E\to B
be a smooth fiber bundle with a fiber homeomorphic to a
K3
surface. Then
w_{2}(V_{p})=w_{2}(H^{+})
, where
H^{+}\to B
denotes the bundle whose fiber over
b
is a maximal positive definite subspace of
{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(E_{b};\mathbb{R})
.

Let us use this for
B=S^{2}
. The flat cohomology bundle
{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(E/B;\mathbb{R})\to B
is classified by the monodromy representation of
\pi_{1}(B)=0
, i.e. is trivial for
B=S^{2}
. Now, this means that the bundle
H^{+}\to S^{2}
can be identified with a map
S^{2}\to\textnormal{Gr}^{+}_{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3,19})
. But this Grassmannian is contractible
(
3
, p. 1735-1736)
, i.e. such a map is nullhomotopic, and thus
H^{+}
is trivial.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Case 1 in Theorem
3.4
cannot occur. ∎

Remark 4.7
.

To the authors’ understanding, the question whether
K3\#K3\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})
satisfies Property (S) is an open problem, closely related to the existence of exotic boundary Dehn twists of
K3\#K3\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})\setminus B^{4}
. Recently, Tilton
29
proved that
K3\#K3
satisfies Property (S). In an email exchange, he informed us that an extension of the ideas present in
29
could possibly be used to prove that
K3\#K3\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})
satisfies Property (S) as well.

Even if this approach proves fruitful, extending it to any smooth manifold homeomorphic to
K3\#K3\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})
seems outside of reach of current technology. Indeed,
(
4
, Proposition 7.6)
relies on the fact that family Seiberg–Witten theory gives information on
w_{2}(V_{\pi})
only when the when the signature of the four-dimensional fiber is
16\mod 32
which is true in the case of
K3
and fails for
K3\#K3\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})
where the signature equals
-32
. Nevertheless, if Tilton’s approach proves successful, it would make a case for the following.

Conjecture 4.8
.

Any smooth manifold homeomorphic to
K3\#K3\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})
satisfies Property (S).

Appendix A
Boundary Dehn Twists and Property (S)

The purpose of this section is to explain the geometric meaning of Property (S) in terms of mapping class groups of
4
-manifolds. These ideas are not new and are an abstraction of the discussion in
(
4
, Section 7)
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
F
be a closed smooth simply connected spin
4
-manifold. Let
B\subset F
be a small embedded
4
-ball. Denote by
F^{\circ}\coloneqq F\setminus{B}
the resulting compact manifold with boundary
\partial F^{\circ}\cong\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Define the
mapping class groups

{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}(F)\coloneq\pi_{0}({\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F))\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathrm{MCG}}_{1}^{+}(F)\coloneq\pi_{0}({\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F^{\circ},\partial F^{\circ})),

where
{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F^{\circ},\partial F^{\circ})
denotes the diffeomorphisms of
F^{\circ}
fixing the boundary.

These groups fit in naturally in an exact sequence

\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F))\longrightarrow\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Emb}}(B^{4},F))\longrightarrow{\mathrm{MCG}}_{1}^{+}(F)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}(F)\longrightarrow\pi_{0}({\mathrm{Emb}}(B^{4},F))

(7)

associated to the fibration

{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F^{\circ},\partial F^{\circ})\hookrightarrow{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F)\twoheadrightarrow{\mathrm{Emb}}(B^{4},F).

In fact,
{\mathrm{Emb}}(B^{4},F)
can be seen to be homotopy equivalent to the bundle of oriented frames of
F
denoted by
{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}(F)

(
4
, p. 15)
. Since
F
is simply connected and spin, it follows that
\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}(F))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and (
7
) can therefore be written as

\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F))\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\longrightarrow{\mathrm{MCG}}_{1}^{+}(F)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}(F)\longrightarrow 1.

(8)

Definition A.2
.

A
boundary Dehn twist
is a diffeomorphism
\tau\in{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F^{\circ},\partial F^{\circ})
which is supported in a collar neighbourhood of the boundary, diffeomorphic to
[0,1]\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
, and whose mapping class
[\tau]\in{\mathrm{MCG}}_{1}^{+}(F)
is the image of the nonzero element of

\displaystyle\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Emb}}(B^{4},F))\cong\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}(F))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}

under the connecting homomorphism in (
8
).

Extending
\tau
by the identity over
B^{4}
yields a diffeomorphism of
F
, which we also denote by
\tau
. By the discussion above, the class
[\tau]\in{\mathrm{MCG}}_{1}^{+}(F)
lies in the kernel of the map

{\mathrm{MCG}}_{1}^{+}(F)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}(F)

in (
8
). We call a boundary Dehn twist
nontrivial
if
[\tau]\in{\mathrm{MCG}}_{1}^{+}(F)
does not lie in the connected component of the identity. Conversely, any nontrivial element in the kernel of the map
{\mathrm{MCG}}_{1}^{+}(F)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}(F)
can be represented by a boundary Dehn twist.

Before we can explain the connection between boundary Dehn twists and Property (S) another ingredient is necessary for the discussion. As before, let
F
be a closed smooth simply connected spin
4
-manifold. Now, since
\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}(F))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
we have the universal (double) cover
\widetilde{{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}}(F)\to{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}(F)
which is the universal spin structure
\widetilde{{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}}(F)\rightarrow{\mathrm{Met}}_{+}(F)\coloneqq{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}(F)/{\mathrm{SO}}(4)
.

Using this structure we define

{\mathrm{SpinDiff}}(F)\coloneq\{(f,\hat{f})\;|\;f\in{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F)\;\text{and}\;\hat{f}\in\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}}(F))\;\textnormal{covers }f_{*}:{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}(F)\to{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}(F)\},

where
\mathcal{G}(\widetilde{{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}}(F))
denotes the gauge group of
\widetilde{{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}}(F)
. Since for any
f\in{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F)
there are exactly two lifts
\hat{f}
of
f_{*}
, and they are related by the deck involution of the double cover
\widetilde{{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}}(F)\to{\mathrm{Fr}}_{+}(F)
, it follows that
{\mathrm{SpinDiff}}(F)
fits in the following short exact sequence

1\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\longrightarrow{\mathrm{SpinDiff}}(F)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F)\longrightarrow 1.

(9)

This induces a connecting homomorphism

\phi:\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F))\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

The map
\phi
provides a bridge between boundary Dehn twists and Property (S) as can be seen in the following proposition.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
F
be a closed smooth simply connected spin
4
-manifold. Then the following are equivalent

(i)

there exists a boundary Dehn twist
\tau
whose mapping class
[\tau]\in{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}_{1}(F)
is nontrivial;

(ii)

the homomorphism
\phi:\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F))\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is trivial;

(iii)

F
satisfies Property (S).

Proof.

We prove the following equivalences

(i)
\iff
(ii):

From the preceding discussion, the exact sequence (
8
) and the long exact sequence associated with (
9
) we have the following commutative diagram which also appears in
(
4
, p. 15)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}_{1}(F)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}(F)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{0}({\mathrm{SpinDiff}}(F))}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

Indeed, the rightmost triangle of this diagram comes from the fact that there is a unique lift (by identity over
\partial F^{\circ}
) of
{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F^{\circ},\partial F^{\circ})\to{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F)
to
{\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F^{\circ},\partial F^{\circ})\to{\mathrm{SpinDiff}}(F)
. Now the commutativity of the diagram and the exactness of horizontal sequences imply that the map
{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}_{1}(F)\to\pi_{0}({\mathrm{SpinDiff}}(F))
is an isomorphism. This implies that
\phi
is trivial if and only if
\ker({\mathrm{MCG}}_{1}^{+}(F)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{MCG}}^{+}(F))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, which proves the equivalence of items (i) and (ii).

(ii)
\iff
(iii):

A smooth bundle
\pi:E\to S^{2}
with fiber
F
is classified by
[\gamma]\in\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F))
by means of the clutching construction, i.e. by gluing two trivial
F
-bundles over two hemispheres of
S^{2}
along
\gamma
from the
S^{1}
-equator yielding

E_{\gamma}\coloneqq(D^{2}_{+}\times F)\cup_{\gamma}(D^{2}_{-}\times F).

Over the two hemispheres the vertical tangent bundle
V_{\pi}
can be identified with
D^{2}_{\pm}\times TF
which carries the unique spin structure of
F
. These two spin structures glue across the equator if and only if the loop
\gamma
lifts to a loop
\tilde{\gamma}:S^{1}\to{\mathrm{SpinDiff}}(F)
. By exactness of

\pi_{1}({\mathrm{SpinDiff}}(F))\longrightarrow\pi_{1}({\mathrm{Diff}}^{+}(F))\xlongrightarrow{\phi}\mathbb{Z}_{2}

it is true that every loop
\gamma
lifts if and only if the map
\phi
vanishes. This proves the equivalence of items (ii) and (iii). ∎
