Functorial stratifications of singularities in characteristic 0
Vicente Monreal

Abstract
. We prove that the riso-stratifications of singularities of affine algebraic sets introduced by Bradley-Williams–Halupczok are embedding independent and can be computed étale locally. Building on this, we upgrade their procedure, originally formulated in non-archimedean and model-theoretic language, into a sharp canonical and functorial stratification process for schemes of finite type over fields of characteristic 0. Our work enables comparisons with classical approaches to singularities and makes these stratifications available without requiring familiarity with the original methods.



1.
Introduction

The notion of stratifying a scheme
X
of finite type over a field refers to a decomposition of
X
into a disjoint union of locally closed subschemes which are smooth and of pure dimension. Conceptually, a stratification should detect the smooth locus
X^{\mathrm{sm}}
and reflect how singular each point is within the singular locus.

A given object usually admits more than one stratification. Determining a notion of well behaved stratification that allows canonical constructions is a central problem in singularity theory. This is difficult both because there are several candidates for what well behaved should mean (Whitney’s conditions
[
W65
]
, Verdier’s regularity
[
V76
]
, compatibility with classical algebraic invariants
[
Z65
]
), and because canonical constructions are rarely available, even when the desired properties are fixed. This problem is particularly important in the context of resolution of singularities, where stratifications play a central role in the resolution algorithm introduced by Hironaka over fields of characteristic 0
[
Hir64
]
. Here, stratifications are needed for selecting the smooth centers of the blow-ups defining the algorithm. The canonicity of the stratification is what makes the algorithm itself canonical, and sharpness of the stratification can be converted into both termination and functoriality of the resolution process. The classical solution to this requires, at each step of selecting a blow-up center, a choice of a hypersurface of maximal contact. The canonicity problem raised by these choices was addressed by Villamayor
[
V89
]
and Bierstone–Milman
[
BM97
]
. Further refinements of functoriality of the process have been obtained by Encinas–Hauser
[
EH02
]
and Włodarczyk
[
W05
]
in the classical setting, and by Abramovich–Temkin–Włodarczyk
[
ATW24
]
, who replaced ordinary blowups by stack-theoretic weighted blow-ups. However, all of these constructions still depend, directly or indirectly, on the notion of maximal contact. This remains the principal obstruction to extending resolutions to positive characteristic
[
H10
]
.

Some approaches to resolutions try to avoid stratifications altogether. The most prominent heuristic is the Nash blow-up proposed by Nash
[
N95
]
and Semple
[
S54
]
, which is known to resolve curves and surfaces when iterated with normalization
[
S90
]
, and some further families in higher dimensions
[
GT14
]
. However, this method was recently shown to fail in dimensions four and higher
[
C25
,
C26
]
. The problem of producing canonical stratifications therefore seems unavoidable.

A recent line of work has approached the problem of canonical stratifications in characteristic 0 from a completely different direction. Using non-archimedean and model-theoretic techniques, in particular cell decomposition in valued fields, Bradley-Williams and Halupczok
[
BWH25
]
introduced a stratification process for algebraic subsets of a given affine space
{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{n}
(or more generally, definable objects in a suitable language, e.g. semialgebraic sets). For a fixed embedded object
X\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{n}
, their process uniquely determines a stratification of
X
which they name the “riso-stratification of
X\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{n}
”. Riso-stratifications of embedded objects are strictly finer than any minimal Whitney stratification, and capture motivic information not visible to classical regularity conditions. In this paper, we prove that such stratifications are embedding independent and can be computed étale locally. From this, we obtain an improved version of them in global contexts:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorems
5.18
,
5.26
and
5.27
)
.

Let
X
be a reduced scheme of pure dimension which is of finite type over a field
k
of characteristic 0. There is a canonical sequence of subschemes of
X
:

\mathcal{S}_{\bullet}(X/k)=\{\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)\}_{0\leq d\leq\dim X},

determining what we call the riso-stratification of
X
over
k
. The following properties and regularity conditions are satisfied by this decomposition
\mathcal{S}_{\bullet}(X/k)
:

(1)

It is a stratification. Namely,
X=\bigsqcup_{0\leq d\leq\dim X}\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k),
each finite union
\mathcal{S}_{\leq d}(X/k)
is a closed subscheme of
X
and each stratum
\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)
is either empty or a smooth subscheme of
X
of pure dimension
d
. In particular,
\mathcal{S}_{\dim X}(X/k)=X^{\mathrm{sm}}
.

(2)

It satisfies Whitney conditions, in the sense of
[
BWH25
]
*4.6.4. In particular, multiplicities are constant along connected components of each stratum
[
T73
,
T77
,
T82
]
.

(3)

It captures motivic information of
X
. More precisely, local motivic Poincaré series are trivial along each stratum in the sense of
[
BWH25
]
*5.4.4(2).

(4)

It is compatible with base change along smooth morphisms. That is, for every smooth morphism
f\colon Y\to X
over
k
of relative dimension
N
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{r}(Y/k)=\varnothing
for
r<N
and:

\mathcal{S}_{N+d}(Y/k)=f^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k),

for every
0\leq d\leq\dim X
. In particular, riso-stratifications over
k
commute with étale localizations.

(5)

It is compatible with base field extensions. That is, for every field extension
F/k
we have:

\mathcal{S}_{d}(X_{F}/F)=\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)\times_{k}\spec(F),

for every
0\leq d\leq\dim X
, where
X_{F}=X\times_{k}\spec(F)
is regarded as a scheme of finite type over
F
.

(6)

It has good spreading properties. More precisely, suppose there is a domain
R
with
\mathrm{Frac}(R)=k
and a reduced scheme
\tilde{X}
of finite presentation over
R
such that
X\simeq\tilde{X}\times_{R}\spec(k)
. Then, there are constructible subschemes
\mathcal{S}_{d}(\tilde{X})\subset\tilde{X}
uniquely determined by
\tilde{X}
such that:

\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)=\mathcal{S}_{d}(\tilde{X})\times_{R}\spec(k),

for each
0\leq d\leq\dim X
.

We point out that local properties (1) to (3) were already described by Bradley-Williams–Halupczok in their work. Similarly, properties (5) and (6) essentially follow from the definability results already obtained in the embedded context. On the other hand, property (4) and the globalization of the previous are due to our reconstruction of the stratification process.

The local stratification process underlying Theorem
1.1
is complicated and, for the moment, can only be performed following its original formulation. That is, we need to appeal to some heavy model-theoretic results to even define riso-stratifications. Let us briefly describe this in the embedded situation, leaving a more detailed discussion for the later section §2. If
X\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{n}
is an affine scheme of pure dimension over a field of characteristic 0, its riso-stratification is computed by first considering a base change of the form:

X_{K}=X\times_{k}\spec(K)\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{K}^{n},

where
K
is a suitable valued field extending
k
(see Definition
2.4
). Upstairs, the non-archimedean techniques involving
risometries
(which play the same role as isometries in classical analytic situations) are used to measure the singularities of
X_{K}
via the invariants called
riso-triviality spaces
. These invariants are used to decompose
X_{K}(K)\subset K^{n}
into sets whose regularity properties are inductively refined through the
shadow iteration process
. This iterative construction outputs a decomposition
(S_{d})_{0\leq d\leq\dim X}
of
X_{K}(K)
into constructible sets (this is highly non-trivial, since every notion involved is formulated in terms of analytic information captured by the valuation of
K
and the additive structure of the ambient space
{\mathbb{A}}_{K}^{n}
). These constructible sets
S_{d}
are proved to descend back to constructible subsets of
X
over
k
, which define a stratification of
X
that is independent of the chosen valued field
K
. This output is precisely
\mathcal{S}_{\bullet}(X/k)
.

Our main results rely on a proof of embedding independence for the riso-stratification process, and this requires, in particular, making sense of the procedure in algebro-geometric terms. For example, the original definition of
riso-triviality spaces
uses a model-theoretic language that obscures their intrinsic nature. We reconstruct them with a perspective that allows us to regard them as invariants of formal germs of singularities. If
k
is a field of characteristic 0,
X
is a reduced scheme of finite type over
k
and
x\in X
is a closed point, then the riso-triviality space of
X
at
x
is a canonical
\kappa(x)
-linear subspace of the tangent space of
X
at
x
(see Definition
4.7
and Corollary
5.12
):

\mathrm{rtsp_{x}}(X)\subset T_{x}X.

The first step in the riso-stratification process of
X
is determined by the level sets of riso-triviality dimensions:

\mathrm{rtd}_{x}(X):=\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathrm{rtsp}_{x}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

as
x
ranges over closed points of
X
(it is still not known if these level sets already compute the riso-stratification in algebraic situations. See Definition
5.16
and Question
6.4
).

We define riso-triviality spaces in arbitrary characteristic and find the first algebraic constraint for them, expressed as a relation with the tangent cone. This is the main tool for the explicit computation of riso-stratifications of simple objects in Section §6:

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.13
and Corollary
5.12
)
.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over a field
k
of arbitrary characteristic,
x\in X
a closed point. Then, we have:

C_{x}(X)(k)+\mathrm{rtsp}_{x}(X)(k)=C_{x}(X)(k),

where
C_{x}(X)
denotes the tangent cone of
X
at
x
. In particular, there is a canonical containment
\mathrm{rtsp}_{x}(X)\subset C_{x}(X)
and a dimensional bound
\mathrm{rtd}_{x}(X)\leq\dim X.

Remark 1.3
.

This is the first evidence of riso-triviality spaces being well behaved in characteristic
p>0
. In this context, it is unclear whether the level sets of
\mathrm{rtd}_{x}(X)
are constructible, since we cannot appeal to model-theoretic results.

Remark 1.4
.

This result is conceptually motivated by the results of García-Ramirez about t-stratifications (the model-theoretic predecessors of riso-stratifications), and their relations to tangent cones in more analytic settings
[
G17
]
.

There are two main questions arising naturally from our work. The first one is:

Question 1.5
.

Is it possible to describe riso-stratifications without appealing to model-theoretic techniques?

By this we mean in particular understanding the
shadow iteration process
in more depth (see Question
6.4
for a more technical phrasing of this). The second, and perhaps more important, question is whether riso-stratifications are compatible with existing algorithms for resolutions of singularities. More precisely:

Question 1.6
.

Is it possible to resolve the singularities of a given
k
-scheme
X
by repeatedly blowing-up regular centers of the form
\mathcal{S}_{\leq d}(X/k)
?

Of course, just blowing-up the worst stratum of a Whitney stratification is known to fail in general when seeking algorithms of resolutions. However, it is not clear whether those situations can be overcome by blowing-up further finite unions of strata (e.g. Whitney umbrella). Alternatively, one can also use only the worst stratum by replacing ordinary blow-ups with stack-theoretic weighted blow-ups, as explained and performed by McQuillan
[
McQuillan20
]
.

Motivated by our results, we propose a conjecture that aims to give a partial answer to Question
1.5
and a positive answer to Question
1.6
. Let us recall some definitions before giving a precise statement:

Definition 1.7
.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over a field
k
and
x\in X
a closed point. The ridge invariant of
X
at
x
is defined by:

\mathrm{Rid}_{x}(X):=\mathrm{Stab}_{T_{x}X}\big(C_{x}(X)\big).

Here the stabilizer construction captures non-reduced structure in a delicate manner and can be formalized in terms of representability of a functor, see
[
BHM10
]
*2.1.

Stratifying singularities in a way that is compatible with the ridge invariant is one of the key conceptual steps within Hironaka’s process of resolution of singularities
[
Hir70
]
. Indeed, Giraud’s results
[
Gir72
,
Gir74
,
Gi75
]
characterize the ridge as the tangent cone of a maximal contact variety.

Conjecture 1.8
.

Let
X
be a reduced scheme of finite type over a field
k
of characteristic 0 and
x\in X
a closed point. Then, we have a canonical inclusion

\mathrm{rtsp}_{x}(X)\subset\mathrm{Rid}_{x}(X).

Remark 1.9
.

Our Theorem
1.2
establishes that
\mathrm{rtsp}_{x}(X)
stabilizes

C_{x}(X)_{\mathrm{red}}
inside
T_{x}X
. The conjecture asserts the stronger statement that
\mathrm{rtsp_{x}}(X)
also stabilizes the non-reduced structure of
C_{x}(X)
.

A proof of this conjecture for normal germs of singularities
x\in X
will be the subject of forthcoming work.

Our methods are characteristic independent. Therefore, any extension of the results of
[
BWH25
]
to positive characteristic would be compatible with our improvements of the riso-stratifying procedure. However, extensions of this kind seem impossible from the model-theoretic side, and so a more promising path to determining whether Theorem
1.1
extends to positive characteristic is to find an alternative proof that does not appeal to model-theoretic arguments, and this in turn is essentially Question
1.5
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In §2 we fix conventions and put our work into context by giving an informal explanation of the original riso-stratification procedure. In §3 we develop an algebraic version of the non-archimedean notions of RV-quotients, risometries and riso-triviality dimension invariants. In §4 we establish some properties of riso-triviality dimensions: They are intrinsic, depend only on formal germs of singularities, and are obstructed by the tangent cone via Theorem
1.2
. In §5 we reconcile our setup with the original by showing that our notions of riso-triviality agree in a suitable sense. We then deduce embedding independence and smooth functoriality of the embedded riso-stratification process from the properties satisfied by our constructions, and culminate in a proof of Theorem
1.1
. In §6 we compute explicit examples of riso-stratifications and raise two technical open questions.

2.
Preliminaries on Riso-Stratifications, Setup and Conventions

The techniques underlying the riso-stratification process were developed systematically over the last decade
[
BWH25
,
CH25
,
G20
,
Hal10
,
Hal11
,
Hal14
,
Hal14b
,
HalYin18
]
. In this paper we reconstruct a modified version of their geometric notions and invariants. Before doing so it is worth having in hand a conceptual picture of
what
the riso-stratification of a scheme is and
how
it is built, avoiding the model-theoretic formalism on which this was originally formulated. The purpose of this section is to provide such a picture, fix our conventions, and make precise the obstructions that arise when trying to translate this model-theoretic machinery to algebro-geometric situations. We refer to
[
Hal23
]
for a more elementary introduction, closer to the original heuristic and containing further instructive examples.

2.1.
Risometries: A non-Archimedean Analog of Isometries

Let
K
be a valued field of characteristic 0 that is algebraically closed and spherically complete
1
1

1

We will only use the fact that spherically complete fields have a complete valuative topology, in the sense that every Cauchy sequence has a unique limit; see
[
BWH25
]
*2.2.6 for a formal definition.
, with valuation
v\colon K\to\Gamma\cup\{\infty\}
, valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and residue field
\kappa
which is also a subfield of
K
via a section of the residue map. We use additive notation for
v
.

The valuation endows the affine space
K^{n}
with a non-archimedean notion of distance: The
valuative distance
between two points
x,y\in K^{n}
is the valuation
v(x-y)
of their difference, where
v(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\min_{i}v(x_{i})
. The closed ball of radius
\gamma\in\Gamma
centered at
x
is:

B_{\geq\gamma}(x)=\{\,x^{\prime}\in K^{n}\mid v(x^{\prime}-x)\geq\gamma\,\}.

Taking these balls as a basis turns
K^{n}
into a topological space. Because the valuative distance defines an ultrametric, every element of a ball is a center, every ball is at once open and closed, and the resulting topology on
K^{n}
is totally disconnected. This is a genuine complication when one wishes to analyze an embedded algebraic set
X\subset K^{n}
, since the topological and analytic tools that are so effective over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
cannot be replaced directly.

The standard non-archimedean approach to deal with this lack of structure is to improve the space itself. Rather than work with the bare set of points under a single valuation, it is possible to enlarge the input by admitting many valuations, or multiplicative seminorms, at once and equipping the geometric result with a structure sheaf, passing to the analytic spaces of Berkovich or the adic spaces of Huber.

The riso-approach proceeds along an entirely different line which allows the study of more general definable objects. Instead of enriching the space in question to restore good topological properties, it leaves
K^{n}
untouched and seeks a suitable replacement for the analytic notion of
isometry
, requiring compatibility with the model-theoretic techniques available over valued fields such as cell decomposition or its generalized version of Hensel minimality.

A first guess for a replacement would be to keep the analytic definition untouched, calling a map isometry in case it preserves valuative distances of the form
v(x-y)
. This is too coarse: This condition encodes order of vanishing of differences of points but not the direction along which two points separate. A way to fix this is to remember, alongside the valuation of differences, leading terms. This enriched datum is encoded in what we call the
\mathrm{RV}
-quotient of
K^{n}
, and produces the following genuine analog of isometries:

Definition 2.1

(informal; cf. Definitions
3.15
and
5.4
)
.

A
risometry
is a bijection
\varphi
between subsets of
K^{n}
that preserves the
\mathrm{RV}
-class of every difference. That is, for all
x,y
in its domain,
\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)
has the same valuation
and
the same leading term as
x-y
, equivalently

v\big(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)-(x-y)\big)>v(x-y).

The terminology is purely a choice of name: A risometry is an
\mathrm{RV}
-isometry
. This is the source of the prefix
riso-
which is present in almost every notion built from this equivalence, namely riso-triviality, the riso-stratification, and the riso-tree of
[
BWH25
]
. Translations are risometries, and risometries are stable under composition and inversion. This entire story is about how to work
up to risometry
.

2.2.
Riso-triviality: Degree of Smoothness up to Risometries

Our purpose is to measure singularities of algebraic subsets of
K^{n}
by non-archimedean means. This requires us to first fix a local notion of smoothness. The geometric insight to be exploited is that, infinitesimally, a smooth point of an algebraic set looks like a linear subspace, and is in particular translation-invariant along as many directions as its dimension. This suggests measuring the local degree of smoothness of an algebraic set
Z\subset K^{n}
at a point in relation to the additive structure of the ambient space, namely by the number of directions along which
Z(K)
is translation-invariant on small valuative ball.

Definition 2.2

(informal; cf. Definition
3.14
)
.

Let
W\subset\kappa^{n}
be a
\kappa
-linear subspace. A subset
A\subset K^{n}
is
W
-translation-invariant on a valuative ball
B\subset K^{n}
if
(A+W(K))\cap B=A
.

Demanding local translation-invariance to measure smoothness is, however, far too rigid. Even smooth algebraic subsets of
K^{n}
are, in general, locally invariant under no translation. We therefore relax the definition by asking for local translation-invariance only
up to risometry
.

Definition 2.3

(informal; cf. Definitions
3.17
and
5.7
)
.

A subset
A
of a valuative ball
B\subset K^{n}
is
W
-riso-trivial on
B
if there is a risometry
\varphi\colon B\to B
for which
\varphi(A)
is
W
-translation-invariant on
B
. The
riso-triviality dimension
of
A
on
B
is

\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(A):=\max\{\,\dim_{\kappa}W\mid A\text{ is $W$-riso-trivial on }B\,\}.

This notion can already be used to provide invariants measuring the singularities of geometric objects. Let
Z\subset\kappa^{n}
be an algebraic set of pure dimension and
z\in Z(\kappa)
a closed point. Restricting the set
Z(K)
to the infinitesimal ball
B_{\geq 0}(z)\subset K^{n}
and computing its riso-triviality dimension associates to the point
z
a non-negative integer:

\mathrm{rtd}_{z}(Z)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

the local degree of smoothness of
Z
at
z
, measured up to risometry. It is a non-trivial fact that this attains its maximal value
\mathrm{rtd}_{x}(Z)=\dim Z
at smooth points (Corollary
4.3
). However, it is still not known if this invariant alone detects algebraic smoothness.

2.3.
The Embedded Riso-Stratification Procedure

Sorting the
\kappa
-points of
Z
by the value of the riso-triviality dimension invariant produces a first partition called the
first shadow of
Z
. For each
0\leq d\leq\dim Z
this first shadow is determining the set:

\mathrm{Sh}_{d}\big(Z\big)(\kappa)=\{\,z\mid\mathrm{rtd}_{z}(Z)=d\,\}\subset Z(\kappa).

A first surprising result that comes from the model theoretic formalisms is constructibility of these
\kappa
-point sets. The first shadow of
Z
is a good approximation to a stratification of its singularities, but not yet a stratification in the strict sense. The further obstruction is the
locally closed
requirement on the strata. There is no immediate reason for the partial unions
\bigsqcup_{e\leq d}\mathrm{Sh}_{e}
to be Zariski closed. This is expected to be true for algebraic sets (see question
6.4
), but does not hold in the full generality of definable objects such as semi-algebraic ones, see
[
BWH25
]
*4.2.4.

This first shadow construction can be refined by an inductive procedure, the
iterated shadow
. In order to formalize this, one extends the definition of riso-triviality dimensions to handle tuples of sets. Then, by knowing that each stratum of a first shadow is constructible, it is possible to again decompose
Z(\kappa)
using the level sets of the following riso-triviality dimensions:

\mathrm{rtd}_{B_{\geq 0}(z)}\big(Z(K),\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(Z)(K),\dots,\mathrm{Sh}_{\dim Z}(Z)(K)\big)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

as
z
ranges in
Z(\kappa)
. This process can be iterated appealing to the model theoretic results that assert constructible strata at each step, see Definition
5.13
. Another non-trivial fact that comes from the model-theoretic world is that this iterative process stabilizes, and the resulting decomposition of
Z(\kappa)
is precisely what we call the
riso-stratification of
Z\subset K^{n}
.

This already explains conceptually the riso-stratification procedure when working under a fixed embedding
Z\subset K^{n}
, for particularly well behaved valued fields
K
. This is rarely a geometric situation of interest. Nonetheless, the techniques can be extended to more general contexts. Let us describe more precisely the general stratifying procedure:

Definition 2.4
.

A field extension
K/\kappa
is
admissible over a domain
R
if:

•

K
is an algebraically closed, spherically complete valued field whose valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
, satisfies
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\simeq\kappa
, inducing a section of the residue map.

•

The algebraically closed field
\kappa
extends
\mathrm{Frac}(R)
.

Riso-stratification procedure
.

Let
R
be a domain of characteristic 0 and
I\subset R[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
a finitely generated radical ideal corresponding to a subscheme
Z\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{R}
of pure relative dimension over
R
. The riso-stratification of
Z
with respect to this embedding, in the sense of
[
BWH25
]
*4, is obtained by applying the following conceptual steps:

(1)

Choose an admissible field extension
K/\kappa
over
R
.

(2)

Base change to obtain the embedding
Z_{\kappa}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
. Perform the shadow iteration construction for the set
Z_{\kappa}(K)\subset K^{n}
. This returns uniquely determined constructible subsets
S_{d}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
stratifying
Z_{\kappa}
with further regularity conditions.

(3)

Prove that the stratification
\{S_{d}\}_{0\leq d\leq\dim Z_{\kappa}}
of
Z_{\kappa}
is the base change of a unique stratification of
Z
which does not depend on the choice made in step (1).

The resulting constructible sets
\{S_{d}\}_{0\leq d\leq\dim Z}
are what we call the
riso-stratification of
Z\subset R^{n}
. When
R
is a field, this is indeed a stratification satisfying further regularity conditions.

This general procedure is based upon Hensel minimality and cell decomposition in valued fields, which are techniques previously developed in
[
CH11
,
CHal14
,
CH20
]
. Some important remarks, that stress the relevance of these model-theoretic tools, is that Step (2) works without information about
R
. Similarly, step (3) depends only on the field
\kappa
and not on the choice of a valued field
K
for which
K/\kappa
is admissible over
R
.

2.4.
Drawbacks of the Original Formulation

Despite having results that establish riso-stratifications as meaningful constructions for embedded algebraic sets, in the sense of compatibility with their intrinsic structure (Whitney conditions, motivic information), it is unclear in
[
BWH25
]
whether these stratifications are embedding independent. In fact, the model theoretic language and geometric setup they use is incompatible with the mere question, which only makes sense after restricting our attention to algebraic sets rather than definable objects over more general languages. When trying to clarify this situation one encounters the following obstacles:

•

The RV-relation used to define risometry classes depends on a choice of origin. Even when working up to translations, one can check that this quotient is not compatible with localizations at
K
-points of
Z
which do not factor through the étale stalk of a geometric point
x\in Z(\kappa)
.

•

The definition of risometry and riso-triviality depend on the additive structure of an ambient space, even after fixing the RV-quotient. As it was remarked by Halupczok
[
Hal23
]
*3.4.4, this quotient does not retain the additive structure. This is to say, working up-to-risometries is sensitive to the dimension of the ambient space.

•

Any modified intrinsic construction of riso-triviality dimension invariants used to run step (2) of the riso-stratification procedure must lose information: The original construction recovers in particular the codimension of the embedding.

In what follows, we will deal with all these obstacles by redefining riso-triviality dimensions of subsets of
Z_{\kappa}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
. That is, we will only use the restricted information recovered by the inclusion:

({\mathcal{O}}_{K})^{n}\subset K^{n}.

This has enough geometric content to see valuative balls
B\subset K^{n}
of radius
\geq 0
centered at
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points, which will be sufficient for recovering the riso-stratification procedure. With this change of perspective, it is possible to define the valuative topology and RV-quotient in a more intrinsic way. This will be used to prove that riso-triviality dimension invariants are intrinsic to formal germs of singularities.

Conventions

For a ring
R
, the term
R
-scheme will mean a reduced scheme which is of finite presentation over
R
and of pure relative dimension. For
K/\kappa
an admissible extension over
R
and a
\kappa
-scheme
X
, we use the term
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points of
X
when referring to the set
X({\mathcal{O}}_{K}):=\Hom_{k}(\spec{\mathcal{O}}_{K},X).

3.
Riso-Triviality Spaces and Dimensions

In this section, we fix a field extension
K/\kappa
which is admissible over a domain
R
(of arbitrary characteristic) and develop a modified version of the riso-triviality dimension invariants introduced in
[
BWH25
]
for studying algebraic subsets of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}(K)
. We use additive notation for the valuation
v\colon K\to\Gamma\cup\{\infty\}
.

3.1.
The Valuative Topology and its RV-structure

Let
Z
be a
\kappa
-scheme. The following definitions endow the set of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points of
Z
with a valuative structure.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
\alpha\in Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is an arc on
Z
. The
base of
\alpha
is the closed point of
Z
defined by composition with the canonical morphism
\spec\kappa\to\spec{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
.

Definition 3.2
.

For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
consider the ideal
I_{>\gamma}:=\{f\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}~|~v(f)>\gamma\}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
and its associated quotient
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma}={\mathcal{O}}_{K}/I_{>\gamma}
. The
base of

\eta\in Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma})
is the
\kappa
-point of
Z
defined by the image of the closed point of
\spec({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma})
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
. The
\gamma
-th truncation map

\theta_{\gamma}\colon Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\longrightarrow Z\big({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma}\big)

is the one induced by the quotient map
\rho_{\gamma}\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma}
. These maps commute, and do not alter base points. More formally, for every
\gamma_{1}>\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
we have
I_{>\gamma_{1}}\subset I_{>\gamma_{2}}
and the further ring quotient
\rho_{\gamma_{2},\gamma_{1}}\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma_{2}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma_{1}}
induces a map
\theta_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}
that makes the following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z\big({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma_{1}}\big)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z\big({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma_{2}}\big)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{\gamma_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{\gamma_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}}

Remark 3.4
.

Truncation maps are very often not surjective over singular base points.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
z\subset Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. The
closed valuative ball on
Z
of radius
\gamma
centered at
z
is

B_{Z,z}(\gamma):=\theta_{\gamma}^{-1}\circ\theta_{\gamma}(z).

As a convention, we write
B_{Z,z}=B_{Z,z}(0)
for the ball consisting of all
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points of
Z
based at
z
. We say that
A\subset Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is
infinitesimal
if it is composed of arcs having a same base point.

Since
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points factor through formal neighborhood of their base points, and such local arcs can be lifted uniquely
[
Artin
]
, we have a canonical correspondence:

B_{Z,z}\cong\Hom_{\text{loc }\kappa\text{-alg}}\big(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,z}},{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\big).

In this way, elements of the free
\kappa
-module
\kappa[B_{Z,z}]
can be regarded as
\kappa
-linear functions in
\mathrm{Fun}_{\kappa}\big(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,z}},{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\big)
. We use this to extend the valuation of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
to a function:

\displaystyle v\colon\kappa[B_{Z,z}]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\longmapsto\min_{l\in\mathfrak{m}_{z}}v\big(\rho(l)\big),

where
\mathfrak{m}_{Z,z}
denotes the associated finitely generated maximal ideal.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
be two arcs based at
z\in Z(\kappa)
. The
valuative distance between
\alpha
and
\beta
is:

v(\alpha,\beta):=v(\alpha-\beta)=\min_{l\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{Z,z}}v\big(\alpha(l)-\beta(l)\big).

We also define the valuative order of
\alpha
by
v(\alpha):=v(\alpha,z)
.

The assumption of
K
being spherically complete says that every Cauchy sequence with respect to the valuative distance on
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points of
Z
has a unique limit. This notion will play a role via the following definition:

Definition 3.7
.

A set
A\subset Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is
valuatively closed
if it is closed under sequential limits. In particular, every closed valuative ball
B\subset Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is indeed valuatively closed.

The objects which we will analyze during the following sections are the following:

Definition 3.8
.

A
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple of
Z
is a finite tuple of sets of the form:

\mathcal{A}=\big(A_{1},\dots,A_{\rho}\big),

such that
A_{i}\subset Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
for each
1\leq i\leq\rho
and its support
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A}):=\bigcup_{i}A_{i}
is valuatively closed. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is trivial if
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})=\varnothing
. If
f\colon C\to C^{\prime}
is a set theoretic function such that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})\subset C
and
C^{\prime}\subset Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
, then we denote by
f(\mathcal{A})
the
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple of
Z
consisting of elements
f(A)
with
A\in\mathcal{A}
. For every
C\subset Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
we denote by
\mathcal{A}|_{C}
the
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple composed of elements of the form
A\cap C
with
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 3.9
.

For a fixed
\kappa
-scheme
Z
we define the
rv-relation for arcs
\alpha,\beta\in Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
as follows:

\alpha\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}\beta\iff\underset{\displaystyle\big(v(\alpha,\beta)>v(\alpha)\big)~\vee~(\alpha=\beta).}{\text{ Both arcs have the same base point and}}

We denote
\mathrm{RV}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(Z):=Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})/\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}
, and the quotient map by
\mathrm{rv}_{Z}\colon Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\to\mathrm{RV}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(Z)
. This makes sense because of the following:

Lemma 3.10
.

The relation
\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}
on
Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is an equivalence relation.

Proof.

Reflexivity follows from the definition. Let
\alpha,\beta\in Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
be two different arcs, suppose
\alpha\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}\beta
and let
z\in Z(k)
be the common base point. Consider the finitely generated maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{z}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,z}
and take
l\in\mathfrak{m}_{z}
be such that
v(\alpha(l))=v(\alpha)
. The strong triangle inequality implies

v\big(\alpha(l)-\beta(l)\big)\geq\min\big\{v\big(\alpha(l)\big),v\big(\beta(l)\big)\big\}
and equality cannot hold because of the rv-condition. Thus,
v(\alpha(l))=v(\beta(l))
which implies
v(\alpha)\geq v(\beta)
and we have symmetry. For transitivity, the same argument says that
\alpha\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}\beta
and
\beta\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}\varepsilon
implies
v(\alpha)=v(\beta)=v(\varepsilon)
and the result follows.
∎

Example 3.11
.

Consider
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}:=\Spec(\kappa[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])
. Once we have chosen coordinates, we get a set theoretic identification that describes the additive group structure of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
:

{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\simeq({\mathcal{O}}_{K})^{n}.

This can be used to check that the rv-relation recovers directional information with respect to base points. For example, for a pair of arcs
\alpha=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
and
\beta=(r^{\prime}_{1},\dots,r^{\prime}_{n})
based at the origin, we have:

\alpha\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}\beta\iff r_{i}\equiv r^{\prime}_{i}\mod{I_{v(\alpha)}}~\forall i.

Consider an embedding of
\kappa
-schemes
Z_{1}\hookrightarrow Z_{2}
. The induced set-theoretic injection on arcs is compatible with truncation maps, i.e. for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z_{1}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma}\big)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z_{2}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\gamma}\big)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{\gamma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{\gamma}}

This implies preservation of valuative distances, in particular each valuative ball of
Z_{1}
is the restriction of a valuative ball of
Z_{2}
. We will repeatedly make use of this concept, and so we fix some notation for it:

Definition 3.12
.

Let
Z_{1}\hookrightarrow Z_{2}
be an embedding of
\kappa
-schemes and
B\subset Z_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
a valuative ball. The
restriction of
B
to
Z_{2}
is the valuative ball
B|_{Z_{2}}=B\cap Z_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
of
Z_{2}
, we also say that
B
is a lift of the valuative ball

.

Embeddings are also compatible with RV-quotients. i.e. the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{RV}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(Z_{1})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{RV}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(Z_{2})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{rv}_{Z_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{rv}_{Z_{2}}}

This notion is particularly interesting when applied to affine embeddings
Z\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{\kappa}
, since it concludes that part of the directional information featured by
Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
in the ambient space is already contained in
\mathrm{RV}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(Z)
.

Example 3.13
.

Suppose
\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
in a compatible way with the valuation of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
and consider
Z=\spec(\kappa[x,y]/(y-x^{2}))
. This induces
Z(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)\subset Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
and we have a set theoretic identification:

Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\simeq\{\big(a,b\big)\in({\mathcal{O}}_{K})^{2}~|~a^{2}=b\}.

In this way, the valuative order of an arc in

based at the origin is just the

-adic order of its
x
-coordinate. Thus, for every pair of arcs
(a,b),(c,d)\in B_{Z,0_{{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}}\cap Z(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)
we have:

\displaystyle(a,b)\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}(c,d)

\displaystyle\iff a\text{ and }c\text{ have the same }t\text{-adic order and leading $\kappa$-coefficient.}

3.2.
Risometries and Riso-Triviality

In this section we define the central notion of risometries, which allows the construction of riso-triviality dimension invariants associated to
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuples of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
. For this purpose, we fix a presentation
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}=\spec(\kappa[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}])
and consequently a group scheme isomorphism
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
,
B
a valuative ball of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
and
W\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
a
\kappa
-linear subspace. We say
\mathcal{A}
is
W
-translation-invariant on
B
if
\mathcal{A}|_{B}
is a non-trivial
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple such that:

A+B_{W,0}(\gamma)=A,

for every
A\in\mathcal{A}|_{B}
, where
\gamma\in\Gamma
is the radius of
B
.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
A,A^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
be infinitesimal subsets. A set-theoretic bijection
\varphi\colon A\to A^{\prime}
is a
risometry
, if for every
a,b\in A
:

\mathrm{rv}\big(\varphi(a)-\varphi(b)\big)=\mathrm{rv}(a-b).

This comparison makes sense since both
a-b
and
\varphi(a)-\varphi(b)
are arcs based at the origin of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
.

Remark 3.16
.

Risometries preserve valuative distances. Translations are risometries. Compositions and inverses of risometries are risometries. A function with infinitesimal domain that preserves rv-classes of differences is automatically a risometry to its image.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
and
B
a valuative ball. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
W
-riso-trivial on
B
, if there exists a risometry
\varphi\colon\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A}|_{B})\to C
, with
C\subset B
, such that
\varphi(\mathcal{A}|_{B})
is
W
-translation-invariant on
B
. A risometry
\varphi
satisfying this condition will be called a
W
-straightener of
\mathcal{A}
on
B
. The
riso-triviality dimension of
\mathcal{A}
on
B
is:

\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(\mathcal{A}):=\max_{\underset{\mathcal{A}\textit{ is $V$-riso-trivial}}{V\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}}}\dim_{\kappa}V.

We say that
A\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is
W
-riso-trivial on
B
when the singleton
\{A\}
is.

Remark 3.18
.

The riso-triviality dimension of a
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple
\mathcal{A}
does not depend on the group isomorphism
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\simeq({\mathcal{O}}_{K})^{n}
.

Remark 3.19
.

A
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple
\mathcal{A}
being
W
-riso-trivial on a valuative ball
B\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is a strictly stronger condition than each one of its elements being
W
-riso-trivial on
B
.

Example 3.20
.

Consider the parabola
Z=\spec(\kappa[x,y]/(y-x^{2}))
, the
\kappa
-linear subspace
W=\spec(\kappa[y])\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{2}
and the valutive ball
B=B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{2},0}
. We claim that
Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is
W
-riso-trivial on
B
: The linear projection to
W
induces a set-theoretic map
\pi_{W}\colon Z({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\to W({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
. This restricts to a bijection:

\displaystyle\varphi\colon B_{Z,0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow B_{W,0}

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\longmapsto\pi_{W}(\alpha).

It is easy to check that this bijection is a risometry: From the identification
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{\kappa}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\simeq({\mathcal{O}}_{K})^{2}
each arc
\alpha\in B_{Z,0}
can be regarded as a tuple of the form
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}^{2})
such that
v(\alpha_{1})>0
. In this way, for every pair of arcs
\alpha,\beta\in B_{Z,z}
we have:

\displaystyle v\big(\varphi(\alpha)-\varphi(\beta)-\alpha+\beta\big)

\displaystyle=v\big((\alpha_{1},0)-(\beta_{1},0)-(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}^{2})+(\beta_{1},\beta_{1}^{2})\big)

\displaystyle=v\big(\alpha_{1}^{2}-\beta_{1}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle>v(\alpha-\beta).

This is to say,
\varphi(\alpha)-\varphi(\beta)\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}\alpha-\beta
as arcs of
B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{2},0}
and so
\varphi
is a riometry.

We will very frequently restrict our attention to
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuples
\mathcal{A}
whose support is defined on
\kappa
-linear subspaces of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
. In this setting, it is important to detect that the riso-triviality dimension invariant is compatible with restriction to subspaces:

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
V,W\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
be
\kappa
-linear subspaces and
\mathcal{A}
a
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple of
V({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
. If
\mathcal{A}
is
W
-riso-trivial on a valuative ball
B
of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
, then
W\subset V
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}
is also
W
-riso-trivial on the valuative ball
B|_{V}
of
V
and we have
\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{rtd}_{B|_{V}}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

By contradiction, suppose
\mathcal{A}
is
W
-riso-trivial on a valuative ball
B
of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
and
W\not\subset V
. We can reduce the analysis to the case
W\cap V=\{0_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}}\}
,
\mathcal{A}|_{B}=\mathcal{A}
and
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\simeq V\oplus W
where both are proper subspaces. Let
\varphi\colon\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})\to C
be a
W
-straightener of
\mathcal{A}
on
B
, and let
A\in\mathcal{A}
. Up to translations both infinitesimal sets
A,\varphi(A)
contain the fixed point
0_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}}
. Set linear coordinates
l_{1},\dots,l_{n}
to
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
such that
W
is defined by the vanishing of the first
r
of them. For
a\in A
such that
\varphi(a)\in W
is non-trivial, then:

\displaystyle v(a)

\displaystyle=\min_{1\leq i\leq r}v\big(l_{i}(a)\big)

\displaystyle=\min_{1\leq i\leq r}v\big(l_{i}\big(a-\varphi(a)\big)\big)

\displaystyle\geq v\big(a-\varphi(a)\big).

This contradicts the risometry condition
v\big(a-\varphi(a)\big)>v(a)
.
∎

3.3.
Adapted Risometries and Riso-Triviality Spaces

One of the most important properties of riso-triviality is that it is additive, in terms of its compatibility with operations on
\kappa
-linear subspaces of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
. This notion is the underlying reason of existence of riso-triviality spaces associated to
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuples, which geometrize the construction of the riso-triviality dimension invariant. We will make sense of this via developing adapted risometries, which will also be relevant when describing algebraic obstructions to riso-triviality later on.

We will recurrently use some translation functions for the following proofs, let us fix the notation: For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
define the translation by
\alpha
as the function:

\displaystyle\tau_{\alpha}\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle\longmapsto\beta+\alpha~.

Lemma 3.22
.

If a
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple
\mathcal{A}
of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
is
W
-riso-trivial on a valuative ball
B\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
, then there exists a
W
-straightener
\varphi\colon\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A}|_{B})\to C
, such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A}|_{B})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{W({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{W}}

Where
\pi_{W}
is any linear projection to the subspace
W\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
. If a risometry
\varphi
satisfies this condition, then we say it is a
W
-straightener of
\mathcal{A}
on
B
adapted to
W
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{A}|_{B}=\mathcal{A}
and consider
\varphi_{0}\colon\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})\to C^{\prime}
a
W
-straightener of
\mathcal{A}
on the valuative ball
B
. Up to translations, we can assume
0\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
and that it is a fixed point. We claim that the map
\tau\colon C^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
defined by
\varphi_{0}(\alpha)\mapsto\tau_{\pi_{W}(\alpha-\varphi_{0}(\alpha))}\big(\varphi_{0}(\alpha)\big)
is a risometry to its image
C
. Indeed, set
l_{1},\dots,l_{n}
linear coordinates of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
such that
W
is defined by the vanishing of the first
r
of them. The images under
\varphi_{0}
of any pair of distinct arcs
\alpha,\beta\in A
have a same base point, moreover:

\displaystyle v\big(\tau\big(\varphi_{0}(\alpha)\big)-\tau\big(\varphi_{0}(\beta)\big)-\varphi_{0}(\alpha)+\varphi_{0}(\beta)\big)

\displaystyle=v\big(\pi_{W}\circ\varphi_{0}(\alpha)-\pi_{W}\circ\varphi_{0}(\beta)-\pi_{W}(\alpha)+\pi_{W}(\beta)\big)

\displaystyle=\min_{i>r}v\big(l_{i}\big(\varphi_{0}(\alpha)\big)-l_{i}\big(\varphi_{0}(\beta)\big)-l_{i}(\alpha)+l_{i}(\beta)\big)

\displaystyle\geq v\big(\varphi_{0}(\alpha)-\varphi_{0}(\beta)-\alpha+\beta\big)

\displaystyle>v(\alpha-\beta).

By construction
l_{i}(\alpha)=l_{i}\big(\tau\circ\varphi_{0}(\alpha)\big)
for
i>r
. Therefore, the result follows from considering
\tau\circ\varphi_{0}
, which inherits the property of being a
W
-straightener of
\mathcal{A}
on
B
.
∎

Proposition 3.23
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
,

a
\kappa
-linear subspace and
\pi_{W}\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\to W
any linear projection. Then,
\mathcal{A}
is
W
-riso-trivial on a valuative ball
B\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
if and only if for every

there exists a
W
-straightener of
\mathcal{A}
adapted to
W
on
B
of the form:

\varphi_{w}\colon\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A}|_{B})\longrightarrow\big(\pi_{W}|_{\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A}|_{B})}\big)^{-1}(w)+B_{W,w_{0}}(\gamma),

where
\gamma\in\Gamma
is the radius of
B
. In particular, if
\mathcal{A}
is
W
-riso-trivial on

Corollary 3.24

(W\oplus V^{\perp})

\mathcal{A}|_{B}=\mathcal{A}

\pi_{V^{\perp}}\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\to V^{\perp}

\pi_{W\oplus V^{\perp}}\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\to W\oplus V^{\perp}

(W\oplus V^{\perp})

∎

Proposition 3.25
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
is closed in the valuative topology, consider
V,W\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
two
\kappa
-linear subspaces and a valuative ball
B\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
. If
\mathcal{A}
is
V
-riso-trivial and
W
-riso-trivial on
B
, then it is also
(V\oplus W)
-riso-trivial on
B
.

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that
V\cap W={0_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}}}
and
\mathcal{A}|_{B}=\mathcal{A}
. Choose a group scheme isomorphism
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\simeq V\oplus V^{\perp}
, so that
W\subset V^{\perp}
. By Proposition
3.23
, we can assume that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
and all their elements define trivial families with respect to the projection
\pi_{V}
determined by the isomorphism, with central fiber
F=(\pi_{V}|_{\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})})^{-1}(0)\subset V^{\perp}
. By the same Proposition, there exists a
W
-straightener of
\mathcal{A}
on
B
of the form
\psi\colon\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})\to C
adapted to
W
. Moreover, up to translations we have
0\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
and
\pi_{W}\big(\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})\big)=B_{W,0}(\gamma)
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
the radius of
B
. Now, for every
w\in B_{W,0}(\gamma)
denote
F_{w}=(\pi_{W}|_{F})^{-1}(w)
and consider the map:

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{w}\colon F_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow F_{w}

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\longmapsto\pi_{V^{\perp}}\circ\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\alpha)

The image of this map is indeed
F_{w}
, but this is a non-trivial check: Let
\beta\in F_{w}
and consider
H=\beta+B_{V,0}(\gamma)
. Of course
H
is closed in the valuative topology, and so its image via a risometry
H^{\prime}=\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{-w}\circ\psi(H)\subset(\pi_{W}|_{\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})})^{-1}(0)
also is. Since
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
is closed,
F_{0}
also is, and by spherical completeness we can choose
\alpha\in F_{0}
and
\tilde{\alpha}\in H^{\prime}
such that:

v(\alpha,\tilde{\alpha})=\max_{u\in F_{0},v\in H^{\prime}}v(u,v).

We claim that
\alpha=\tilde{\alpha}
: Of course
\pi_{V^{\perp}}(\tilde{\alpha})=\alpha
, and so
\alpha-\tilde{\alpha}\in B_{V,0}(\gamma)
. Then
\mu=\phi^{-1}\circ\tau_{-w}\circ\phi\circ\tau_{\alpha-\tilde{\alpha}}\circ\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\tilde{\alpha})\in H^{\prime}
is such that:

\mathrm{rv}(\mu-\tilde{\alpha})=\mathrm{rv}(\alpha-\tilde{\alpha}),

which implies
v(\alpha-\mu)>v(\alpha-\tilde{\alpha})
a contradiction. In other words, we have
\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(\alpha)=\beta
and so
\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(F_{0})=F_{w}
. Now, we verify that
\tilde{\varphi}_{w}
is a risometry: Set
l_{1},\dots,l_{n}
linear coordinates of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
such that
V
is defined by the vanishing of the first
r
of them, for
\alpha,\beta\in F_{0}
we have:

\displaystyle v\big(\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(\alpha)-\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(\beta)-\alpha+\beta\big)

\displaystyle=v\big(\pi_{V^{\perp}}\circ\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\alpha)-\pi_{V^{\perp}}\circ\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\beta)-\alpha+\beta\big)

\displaystyle=\min_{i\leq r}v\left(l_{i}\big(\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\alpha)-\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\beta)-\alpha+\beta\big)\right)

\displaystyle>v(\alpha-\beta)

where we use the fact that
\alpha,\beta
and their images under
\tilde{\varphi}_{w}
live in
V^{\perp}
, together with the risometry condition satisfied by the composition
\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi
. Recall that this rv-condition implies injectivity, so now we can guarantee the existence of the risometry
\tilde{\varphi}_{w}^{-1}\colon F_{w}\to F_{0}
for each
w\in B_{W,0}(\gamma)
. Consider then the map
\varphi\colon F\to F_{0}\times B_{W,0}(\gamma)
defined by
\alpha\mapsto\tau_{\pi_{W}(\alpha)}\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}_{\pi_{W}(\alpha)}(\alpha)
. We claim that
\varphi
is a risometry: First, note that for every
\alpha,\beta\in F_{0}
and
u,w\in B_{W,0}(\gamma)
we have

\displaystyle v\big(\psi(\alpha)-\psi(\beta)-(\alpha-\beta)\big)

\displaystyle>v(\alpha-\beta)

\displaystyle\geq\min\{v(\alpha-\beta),v(u-v)\}

\displaystyle=v(\alpha-\beta+u-w)

where the last equality holds since
\alpha-\beta\in W^{\perp}
and
u-w\in W
, and so we cannot have
\mathrm{rv}(\alpha-\beta)=\mathrm{rv}(u-v)
unless both differences are trivial, in which case the condition holds. In other words, we have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{rv}\big(\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{u}\circ\psi(\alpha)-\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\beta)\big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{rv}\big(\tau_{u}\circ\psi(\alpha)-\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\beta)\big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{rv}(\alpha-\beta+u-w)

where we also used the risometry condition on
\psi^{-1}
. Similarly, we have:

\mathrm{rv}\big(\pi_{V}(\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{u}\circ\psi(\alpha)-\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\beta))\big)\neq\mathrm{rv}(\alpha-\beta+u-v),

unless both vectors are trivial where the result hods. Indeed, we are comparing the class of an element of
V
with the class of an element of
V^{\perp}
. Therefore, a similar computation as before yields:

\mathrm{rv}\big(\tilde{\varphi}_{u}(\alpha)-\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(\beta)\big)=\mathrm{rv}\big(\alpha-\beta+u-w+\pi_{V}\big(\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{u}\circ\psi(\alpha)-\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\beta)\big)\big).

Note that from our previous identities we can deduce:

v\big(\pi_{V}\big(\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{u}\circ\psi(\alpha)-\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\beta)\big)\big)>v(\alpha-\beta+u-w),

This holds since the not-projected representative on the left has the same rv-class as the latter which is in
V^{\perp}
, so its minimal valuation is not attained at the
V
-coordinates. This valuation inequality implies the following:

\mathrm{rv}(\alpha-\beta+u-v)=\mathrm{rv}\big(\alpha-\beta+u-w+\pi_{V}\big(\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{u}\circ\psi(\alpha)-\psi^{-1}\circ\tau_{w}\circ\psi(\beta)\big)\big),

which together with the above equalities of rv-classes yields:

\mathrm{rv}\big(\tilde{\varphi}_{u}(\alpha)-\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(\beta)\big)=\mathrm{rv}\big(\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{u}(\alpha)-\varphi\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(\beta)\big).

This verifies that
\varphi
is a risometry. Consider the
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple
\mathcal{F}
in
V^{\perp}
defined component-wise via
F=(\pi_{V}|_{A})^{-1}(0)
for
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we claim that
\varphi
is a
W
-straightener for
\mathcal{F}
on
B|_{V^{\perp}}
: Indeed, this just follows from the property of
\psi
being a
W
-straightener for
\mathcal{A}
on
B
adapted to
W
, and the nature of the construction of
\varphi
. We conclude the Proposition by observing that there is an expression of the form
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{F}\times B_{V,0}(\gamma)
, where
\gamma
is the radius of
B
, and this can be used via Proposition
3.24
to conclude
(V\oplus W)
-riso-triviality of
\mathcal{A}
on
B
.
∎

.

4.
On The Algebraicity of Riso-Triviality

Consider an admissible extension
K/\kappa
over a domain
R
of arbitrary characteristic. Let
X
be a
\kappa
-scheme and
\mathcal{A}
a
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple of
X
. In this section, we prove that the value
\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(\mathcal{A})
, where
B\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is a valuative ball with respect to a fixed embedding, is intrinsic to
X
and depends only on
B|_{X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}
as a non-embedded valuative ball of
X
. We proceed with this idea, in a way that unlocks connections between riso-triviality dimensions and classical algebraic invariants of singularities.

4.1.
Tangent Spaces and Riso-Triviality Intrinsicness

The riso-triviality information of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuples when restricted to
B_{X,x}
is, in a suitable sense of the word, intrinsic and encoded entirely by the formal germ of the singularity that defines
x\in X(\kappa)
. We elaborate on this:

Definition 4.1
.

The geometric Zariski tangent space of
X
at a closed point
x
is:

T_{x}X:=\spec\left(\text{Sym}_{\kappa}\big(\mathfrak{m}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{X,x}^{2}\big)\right).

For the given closed embedding
X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
, every coordinate choice on
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
comes with a geometric presentation
T_{x}X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\kappa}^{n}

that exhibits the tangent space of
X
at
x
, as a subscheme of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
containing
x
. Up to a translation, we can assume that
T_{x}X
is a
\kappa
-linear subspace and that the coordinates are adapted to it. This choice comes with an associated splitting
s\colon\mathfrak{m}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{X,x}^{2}\to\mathfrak{m}_{X,x}
and a projection
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\to T_{x}X
which also induces a set theoretic map:

\pi_{s}\colon{X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}\to T_{x}{X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}.

It follows from Cohen’s structure theorem that this map is injective when restricted to
B_{X,x}
. More precisely, the splitting induces a closed immersion of formal schemes:

\pi_{s}\colon\mathrm{Spf}\left(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}}\right)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Spf}\left(\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}_{T_{x}X,x}}\right),

which in turn induces a injection from
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points of
X
based at
x
, to
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points of
T_{x}X
based at
x
. The following proposition both recovers this injectivity and detects further structure on the restricted map:

Proposition 4.2
.

The map
\pi_{s}\colon B_{X,x}\to T_{x}{X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}
restricted to its image, is a risometry.

Proof.

Set linear coordinates
l_{1},\dots l_{n}
of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
such that
T_{x}X
is defined by the vanishing of the first
r
of them and
\pi_{s}\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\to T_{x}X
corresponds to the inclusion of
\kappa[l_{r+1},\dots,l_{n}]
in
\kappa[l_{1},\dots,l_{n}]
. Denote by
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
the coordinate ring of
X
, whose maximal ideal corresponding to the origin is generated by the images of
l_{r+1},\dots,l_{n}
. Consider a pair of different arcs
\alpha,\beta\in B_{X,x}
, and notice that:

\min_{1\leq i\leq n}v\big(l_{i}(\alpha-\beta)\big)=\min_{r<i}v\big(l_{i}(\alpha-\beta)\big)<\min_{i\leq r}v\big(l_{i}(\alpha-\beta)\big).

Indeed, this comes from the fact that the images
\overline{l_{i}}
with
i\leq r
in
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}}
lie in
\mathfrak{m}_{X,x}^{2}
, and so their valuation on
\alpha-\beta
is strictly greater than
v(\alpha,\beta)
, which has to be detected by the images
\overline{l_{j}}
with
j>r
. This is to say, we have the following relation:

\displaystyle v\big(\pi_{s}(\alpha-\beta)-\alpha+\beta\big)

\displaystyle=\min_{i\leq r}v\big(l_{i}(\alpha-\beta)\big)

\displaystyle>v(\alpha-\beta).

This is exactly the risometry condition for
\pi_{s}
when restricted to its image.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Consider a closed embedding
X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
and
x\in X(\kappa)
defining a smooth point. Then
\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(B|_{X})=\dim X
for every valuative ball
B
of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
based at
x
such that
\mathcal{A}|_{B}
is non-trivial.

Proof.

This is just a consequence of Proposition
4.2
and the fact that any projection
\pi_{s}:B_{X,x}\to B_{T_{x}X,x}
is surjective by formal smoothness.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Consider a closed embedding
X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
and
x\in X(\kappa)
. Fix a coordinates of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
determining a geometric presentation
T_{x}X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
, and choose a section
s\colon{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,x}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,x}^{2}\to{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,x}
with associated map
\pi_{s}\colon B_{X,x}\to T_{x}{X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}
. Then, we have for every
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple
\mathcal{A}
of
X
a dimension preserving correspondence:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\underset{k\text{-linear subsp.}}{W\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}}~\Big|\text{\scriptsize$\mathcal{A}|_{B}$ is $W$-riso-trivial on $B$}\end{array}\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1-1}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\underset{k\text{-linear subsp.}}{\tilde{W}\subset T_{x}X}~\Big|\text{\scriptsize$\pi_{s}(\mathcal{A}|_{B})$ is $\tilde{W}$-riso-trivial on $\pi_{s}(B)$}\end{array}\right\}

for every valuative ball
B
of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
based at
x
such that
\mathcal{A}|_{B}
is non-trivial. In particular, the riso-triviality space
\mathrm{rtsp}_{B}(\mathcal{A})\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
sits canonically inside
T_{x}X
up to translating the latter to the origin and we have:

\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{rtd}_{\pi_{s}(B)}\big(\pi_{s}(\mathcal{A})\big).

This says that the dimension
\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(\mathcal{A})
is intrinsic to
X
and depends only on
B|_{X}
.

Proof.

We may assume
x=0_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}}
, and so we can choose coordinates of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
and a projection to the realized tangent space
T_{x}X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
in such a way that it agrees with the morphism
\pi_{s}\colon X\to T_{x}X
defined by
s
. By Proposition
4.2
and the fact that inverses of risometries are risometries,
\mathcal{A}
is
W
-riso-trivial on
B
if and only if
\pi_{s}(\mathcal{A})
is on a valuative ball
\tilde{B}
of
V
of the same radius centered at a point in
\pi_{s}(\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A}|_{B}))
. When
\mathcal{A}|_{B}
is non-trivial, we can take
\tilde{B}
to be
\pi_{s}(B)
. Furthermore, Lemma
3.21
guarantees
W\subset T_{x}X
in the cases where

-riso-triviality of
\mathcal{A}
is satisfied and the claimed correspondence is provided by
s
. We also obtain that
\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(\mathcal{A})
is intrinsic to
X
, since this amounts to the fact that two different embeddings can be compared through
T_{x}X
via a same chosen section
s
.
∎

Now it is possible to extend our main definitions to the non-embedded, global context. First, we have to observe that every projection to linear subspace
\pi_{V}\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\to V
not only sends valuative balls of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
to valuative balls of
V
, but it does so in a surjective way. Similarly, when
B_{X,x}\subset V({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
every valuative ball of
X
based at
x\in X(\kappa)
can be regarded as a valuative ball of
V
restricted to
X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
. This can be used to make sense of the following:

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple of a
\kappa
-scheme
X
,
x\in X(\kappa)
and
B\subset B_{X,x}
a valuative ball. The
riso-triviality space of
\mathcal{A}
on
B
is the canonical
\kappa
-linear subspace:

\mathrm{rtsp}_{B}(\mathcal{A})\subset T_{x}X,

corresponding to
\mathrm{rtsp}_{\tilde{B}}(\mathcal{A})
via Proposition
4.4
among all affine opens
U\subset X
through which
x
factors,
U\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
closed embeddings featuring
x
as the origin, linear projections
\pi_{s}\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\to T_{x}X
and valuative balls
\tilde{B}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
such that
\pi_{s}(\tilde{B})|_{X}=B
and
\mathcal{A}|_{\tilde{B}}
is non-trivial. The riso-triviality dimension of
\mathcal{A}
is
\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(\mathcal{A})=\dim_{\kappa}\mathrm{rtsp}_{B}(\mathcal{A})
.

The following Proposition says that our non-embedded notion of riso-triviality dimensions can be recovered from the formal germs of the singularities defined by points
x\in X(\kappa)
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
X,Y
be two
\kappa
-schemes. Suppose there are points
x\in X(\kappa)
and
y\in Y(\kappa)
with a given isomorphism:

\Psi\colon\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,y}}.

Then, the induced bijection
\psi\colon B_{X,x}\to B_{Y,y}
preserves riso-triviality dimensions. That is, for every
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuple
\mathcal{A}
of
X
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})\subset B_{X,x}
and valuative ball
B\subset B_{X,x}
we have:

\mathrm{rtd}_{B}(\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{rtd}_{\psi(B)}\big(\psi(\mathcal{A})\big).

Proof.

Recall that
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points of
X
centered at
x
factor through the formal neighborhood of
x
in
X
, and all
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-points of this formal neighborhood lift; the same happens for
Y
, therefore the induced bijection
\psi\colon B_{X,x}\to B_{Y,y}
exists. The isomorphism also induces an isomorphism
\tilde{\Psi}\colon T_{x}X\to T_{y}Y
, and furthermore allow us to associate to every splitting
s\colon\mathfrak{m}_{{X},x}/\mathfrak{m}_{{X},x}^{2}\to\mathfrak{m}_{{X},x}
a splitting
\tilde{s}\colon\mathfrak{m}_{{Y},y}/\mathfrak{m}_{{Y},y}^{2}\to\mathfrak{m}_{{Y},y}
in such a way that the projections
\pi_{X}\colon B_{X,x}\to T_{x}{X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}
and
\pi_{Y}\colon B_{{Y},y}\to T_{y}Y({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
make the following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B_{X,x}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{(X)}_{x}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{(Y)}_{y}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B_{Y,y}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{x}{X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{y}Y({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\tilde{s}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\Psi}}

Since both
\pi_{X}
and
\pi_{Y}
are risometries on any chosen local embedding by Proposition
4.2
, the situation is reduced to a
\kappa
-scheme isomorphism of affine spaces, which are base changes of
\kappa
-schemes, and so the result follows from Proposition
4.4
.
∎

4.2.
Tangent Cones and Obstructions to Riso-Triviality

Having established riso-triviality spaces and dimensions of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuples on valuative balls as formal invariants of singularities, it is a natural task to study how these notions behave at the more canonical
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuples and valuative balls at our disposal.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
k
be a field. The
riso-triviality space of a
k
-scheme
X
at a closed point
x
is the maximal
\kappa(x)
-linear subspace
\mathrm{rtsp}_{x}(X)\subset T_{x}X
such that for every admissible field extension
K/\kappa
over
k
:

\mathrm{rtsp}_{x}(X)\times_{k}\spec(\kappa)=\mathrm{rtsp}_{B_{X_{\kappa},x}}\big(X_{\kappa}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\big),

as an equality of
\kappa
-linear subspaces of
T_{x}X_{\kappa}
. The riso-triviality dimension of
X
at
x
is

\mathrm{rtd}_{x}(X):=\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathrm{rtsp}_{x}(X).

We will find constraints for this kind of riso-triviality spaces. For the rest of the section, we will restrict without loss of generality to the situation where
k
is algebraically closed and we have a fixed admissible extension
K/\kappa
over
k
for which
\kappa=k
.

Let
x\in X(\kappa)
, set
A=\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}}
,
B=\kappa\llbracket x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rrbracket
and consider a radical ideal
I=(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})\subset B
such that
A\simeq B/I
. We fix an injective local
\kappa
-algebra homomorphism
\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
. This induces a further containment:

\widehat{X}_{x}(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)\subset\widehat{X}_{x}({\mathcal{O}}_{K}).

This also comes with an associated injective group homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\subset\Gamma
. We use this for making sense of
t
-adic valuation in relation to the valuative structure of
\widehat{X}_{x}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
.

Definition 4.8
.

We identify
\mathrm{gr}(B)\simeq\kappa[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. The geometric tangent cone of
X
at
x
is:

C_{x}(X):=\spec\big(\kappa[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\mathrm{in}(I)\big)\hookrightarrow T_{x}X\simeq\spec\big(\mathrm{gr}(B)\big),

where
\mathrm{in}(I)=\langle f_{\mathrm{in}}~|f\in I\rangle
, and for each
f
we denote its lowest degree part by
f_{\mathrm{in}}
.

Definition 4.9
.

For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
consider the following ideals of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
:

I_{\geq\gamma}:=\{r\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}~|~v(r)\geq\gamma\}~~~,~~~I_{>\gamma}:=\{r\in R~|~v(r)>\gamma\}.

From the quotients
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\gamma}:=I_{\geq\gamma}/I_{>\gamma}
the valuative grading of the ring can be defined:

\mathrm{gr}_{v}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\gamma}.

Remark 4.10
.

It follows from Definition
3.9
and Example
3.11
, that the RV-quotient of an arc
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in T_{x}{X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}
with valuative order
\gamma
, is determined by the image of each
\alpha_{i}
in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\gamma}
.

Definition 4.11
.

The RV-closure of
\widehat{X}_{x}(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)\subset\widehat{X}_{x}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is the following set:

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{B}}:=

\displaystyle~\big(\mathrm{rv}|_{\widehat{X}_{x}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}\big)^{-1}\circ\mathrm{rv}\big(T_{x}X(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)\big)

We clearly have
\widehat{X}_{x}(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)\subset\overline{\mathcal{B}}
, and this is generally a strict inclusion.

For each
\alpha\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}
, take
\nu=v({\alpha})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. By construction, there exists an element
\beta\in T_{x}X(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)
such that the truncated image
\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)
, viewed as an element of
T_{x}X({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\nu})
, fits in a commutative diagram as follows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\nu}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)}

From this, we have
\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)=\big(\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)_{1},\dots,\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)_{n}\big)=\big(\theta_{\nu}(\beta)_{1},\dots,\theta_{\nu}(\beta)_{n}\big)\in T_{x}X({\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\leq\nu})
and so every coordinate of this tuple can be regarded as an element of
(t^{\nu})/(t^{\nu+1})\simeq\kappa
. Using this isomorphism, denote:

\psi(\alpha):=\big(\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)_{1},\dots,\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)_{n}\big)\in T_{x}X(\kappa).

Of course, this identification depends only on the presentation of
\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}}
and the inclusion
\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket\subset R
, so we get a well defined set theoretic map:

\displaystyle\psi\colon\overline{\mathcal{B}}\longrightarrow T_{x}X(\kappa)

The restricted map
\psi|_{\widehat{X}_{x}(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)}
can be described intrinsically in a different way: Every arc
\gamma\colon A\to\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket
induces a graded map
\mathrm{gr}(\gamma)\colon\mathrm{gr}~A\to\mathrm{gr}~\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket\simeq\kappa[T]
. The further evaluation
T=1
defines a
\kappa
-point of the tangent cone
C_{x}(X)\subset T_{x}X
which is precisely
\psi(\gamma)
. From this, we see that
\psi|_{\widehat{X}_{x}(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)}
factors through the inclusion
C_{x}(X)(\kappa)\hookrightarrow T_{x}X(\kappa)
. The following Lemma generalizes this property.

Proposition 4.12
.

The set-theoretic map
\psi\colon\overline{\mathcal{B}}\to T_{x}X(\kappa)
satisfies
\mathrm{Im}(\psi)=C_{x}(X)(\kappa)
.

Proof.

We first verify
\mathrm{Im}(\psi)\subset C_{x}(X)(\kappa)
. Let
\alpha\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}
and consider its order
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}\subset\Gamma
. Notice that the image of
\alpha(f)
in
\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\gamma_{0}}
, where
\gamma_{0}=\deg f_{\mathrm{in}}\cdot\nu
, can be identified naturally with
f_{\mathrm{in}}\big(\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)_{1},\dots,\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)_{n}\big)\in(t^{\gamma_{0}})/(t^{\gamma_{0}+1})\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\gamma_{0}}
. Indeed, from the valuation properties we have for any monomial
x^{u}=\prod_{i}x_{i}^{u_{i}}
:

\displaystyle v(\alpha(x^{u}))

\displaystyle=u_{1}v\big(\alpha(x_{1})\big)+\dots+u_{n}v\big(\alpha(x_{n})\big)

\displaystyle\geq(u_{1}+\dots+u_{n})\cdot v(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\deg(x^{u})\cdot v(\alpha)

Therefore,
\alpha(f)|_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\gamma_{0}}}
depends only on the minimal graded part of
f
evaluated at the
\nu
-graded part of each image
\alpha(x_{i})
. In this way, for any
f\in I
, this latter evaluation
f_{\mathrm{in}}\big(\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)_{1},\dots,\theta_{\nu}(\alpha)_{n}\big)
has to vanish. This says, under the isomorphism
(t^{\nu})/(t^{\nu+1})\simeq\kappa
, that
\psi(\alpha)
is a
\kappa
-point of
T_{x}X
vanishing at the initial ideal of
I
. Now we verify surjectivity of
\psi\colon\overline{\mathcal{B}}\to C_{x}(X)(\kappa)
: Every
\lambda\in\kappa^{*}
defines an automorphism
\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket\to\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket
, sending
t\mapsto\lambda t
. Since
\kappa
is algebraically closed, this automatically implies that
\psi(\overline{\mathcal{B}})
is stable by scalar multiplication. In this way, it suffices to prove that the further composition:

\overline{\mathcal{B}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}C_{x}(X)(\kappa)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}C_{x}(X)(\kappa)

is surjective, where
\mathbb{P}C_{x}(X)
stands for the projectivized tangent cone of
X
at
x
. Let
y\in\mathbb{P}C_{x}(X)(\kappa)
and consider
\sigma\colon\tilde{X}\to X
the blow-up of
X
at
x
. We have
E=\mathrm{Exc(\sigma)}\simeq\mathbb{P}C_{x}(X)
, so
y
defines a closed point of
E
lying over
x
. Since
\tilde{X}
is reduced,
E
is Cartier, and because of prime avoidance, there exists an irreducible curve
C=\spec(S)\subset\tilde{X}
passing through
y
not contained in
E
. Since finite extensions of
\kappa
are trivial, one of the components of the normalization of
S
defines an arc
\beta\colon\spec(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)\to\tilde{X}
such that
\sigma(\beta)\colon\spec(\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket)\to X
corresponds to a non-constant arc based at
x
. It follows from the construction that the associated graded map
\mathrm{gr}(\sigma(\beta))\colon\mathrm{gr}A\to\mathrm{gr}~\kappa\llbracket t\rrbracket\simeq\kappa[T]
defines a non-trivial
\kappa
-point of
C_{x}(X)
, via evaluation
T=1
, supported on the line that corresponds to
y\in\mathbb{P}C_{x}(X)
. Surjectivity of
\psi
follows from the fact that
\psi(\sigma(\beta))
is precisely the
\kappa
-point defined in this way.
∎

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
x\in X(\kappa)
, and consider
W=\mathrm{rtsp}_{x}(X)
as a
\kappa
-linear subspace of
T_{x}X
. Then, we have:

C_{x}(X)(\kappa)+W(\kappa)=C_{x}(X)(\kappa).

In particular, we have a canonical containment
W\subset C_{x}(X)
and a dimensional bound:

\mathrm{rtd}_{x}(X)\leq\dim X.

Proof.

Note that there is a set theoretic function
\overline{\psi}\colon\mathrm{RV}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(T_{x}X)\to T_{x}X(\kappa)
making the following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{x}X(\kappa)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{RV}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(T_{x}X)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{rv}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\psi}}

Indeed, the image of an arc
\alpha\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}
depends only on its tuple decomposition and the grading of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
, thus from the same reasoning as in Remark
4.10
we get that the map
\psi
factors through the RV-quotient
\mathrm{rv}\colon\overline{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathrm{RV}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(T_{x}X)
, and so the extension
\overline{\psi}
exists. Consider the associated projection
\pi_{W}\colon T_{x}{X({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}\to W({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
. By Proposition
3.23
there exists a
W
-straightener
\varphi\colon B_{X,x}\to C=(\pi_{W}|_{B_{X,x}})^{-1}(x)+B_{W,x}
adapted to
W
and preserving RV-classes. In this way, we get the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B_{X,x}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{x}X(\kappa)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{RV}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}(T_{x}X)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{rv}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{rv}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{\psi}}}

Recall that
C
is
W
-translation invariant on
B_{T_{x}X,0}
with the further restriction
(\pi_{W}|_{C})\circ\varphi=\pi_{W}|_{B_{X,x}}
. Thus, for every
\alpha\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}\subset B_{X,x}
and
w\in W(\kappa)
, the element
\varphi(\alpha)+t^{v(\alpha)}w
is by construction an element of
\varphi\big(\overline{\mathcal{B}}\big)
satisfying:

\displaystyle\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(\alpha)+t^{v(\alpha)}w)

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}\circ\mathrm{rv}(\varphi(\alpha)+t^{v(\alpha)}w)

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}\circ\mathrm{rv}(\alpha+t^{v(\alpha)}w)

\displaystyle=\psi(\alpha)+w.

This concludes
\psi\big(\overline{\mathcal{B}}\big)+W(\kappa)\subset\psi\big(\overline{\mathcal{B}}\big)
, and the result follows from Proposition
4.12
.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

Notice that the construction of the rv-closure

. Indeed, the element

by

, lies by definition in

.

.

The statement in Theorem

.

5.
Riso-Stratifications

In this section we check that our riso-triviality notions are compatible with the original ones driving the riso-stratification process. This allow us to prove embedding independence of riso-stratifications and globalize their construction with a resulting canonical and functorial procedure for
k
-schemes, where
k
is any field of characteristic 0.

5.1.
On The Compatibility of Geometric Setups

Let
K/\kappa
be an admissible extension over a domain
R
of characteristic 0. In this subsection we summarize the original geometric construction of the riso-triviality dimension invariant for subsets of
K^{n}
, which is the central notion underlying the riso-stratification process. Then, we prove compatibility with our notion of riso-triviality dimension for
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuples of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
.

Fix a group scheme isomorphism
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{G}_{a}^{m}
, which yields an associated additive group isomorphism
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}(K)\simeq K^{n}
. We use the inclusion
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}(K)
to make sense of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-tuples of
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
as tuples of subsets of
K^{n}
. Just to make a distinction between the setups, we will use the adjective “strong” when recalling definitions from the original model-theoretic setting.

Definition 5.1
.

[
BWH25
]
*2.1.12
The valuation of
K
is extended to
K^{n}
via:

v\big((x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\big):=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}v(x_{i}).

The closed valuative ball of
K^{n}
with radius
\gamma\in\Gamma
centered at
x\in K^{n}
is the set:

B_{\geq\gamma}(x)=\{x^{\prime}\in K^{n}~\big|~v(x^{\prime}-x)\geq\gamma\}.

Similarly, the open ball with the same radius and center is:

B_{>\gamma}(x)=\{x^{\prime}\in K^{n}~\big|~v(x^{\prime}-x)>\gamma\}.

Remark 5.2
.

We use different sub-indices which are compatible with our additive notation for the valuation, in contrast to our reference.

Remark 5.3
.

It is straightforward to check that our notions of valuative balls and distances are compatible with this extended valuation on
K^{n}
. In particular, for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma>0
, we have a natural comparison of closed valuative balls:

B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n},\alpha}(\gamma)=B_{\geq\gamma}(\alpha)\subset K^{n}.

However, be careful that our slightly different definitions give a different comparison for open balls of radius
0
centered at
\kappa
-points
x
:

B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n},x}=B_{>0}(x).

Definition 5.4
.

[
BWH25
]
*2.3.1
The
strong rv-relation on
K^{n}
is defined via:

x\sim_{\mathrm{(rv)}}y\iff v(x-y)>v(x)\text{ or }x=y.

A strong risometry is a set-theoretic map
\varphi\colon A\to A^{\prime}
such that for every
x,y
in
A
:

\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\sim_{(rv)}x-y.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
A,A^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
be two infinitesimal sets and
\varphi\colon A\to A^{\prime}
a set-theoretic function. Then,
\varphi
is a risometry in
{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
if and only if,
\varphi
is a strong risometry in
K^{n}
.

Proof.

This just follows from the fact that our rv-relation
\sim_{\mathrm{rv}}
from Definition
3.9
coincides in
B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n},0}=B_{>0}(0_{K^{n}})
with the restricted strong rv-relation
\sim_{(rv)}
. Indeed, from Definition
3.6
we see that
v(x,y)=v(x-y)
for each
x,y\in B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n},0}
, where the latter can be considered as the restricted valuation from
K^{n}
.
∎

Definition 5.6
.

[
BWH25
]
*2.3.6
Let
B\subset K^{n}
be a valuative ball,
\mathcal{A}
a non-empty finite tuple of subsets of
B
and
\tilde{W}\subset K^{n}
a
K
-vector subspace. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
\tilde{W}
-translation-invariant on

if for every
x,x^{\prime}\in B
with
x-x^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
we have:

x\in A\iff x^{\prime}\in A,

for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 5.7
.

[
BWH25
]
*2.3.8 and 2.3.13
Let
B\subset K^{n}
be a closed valuative ball of radius
\gamma
,
\mathcal{A}
a finite tuple of sets and

a
\kappa
-linear subspace. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is strongly

\tilde{\varphi}\colon B\to B
such that the tuple
\tilde{\varphi}(\mathcal{A}|_{B})
is
W(K)
-translation-invariant on

. The riso-triviality dimension of
\mathcal{A}
on

is:

\underline{\mathrm{rtd}}_{B}(\mathcal{A}):=\max_{\underset{\mathcal{A}\textit{ is strongly $V$-riso-trivial on $B$}}{V\subset\kappa^{n}}}\dim_{\kappa}V.

Remark 5.8
.

We are using
[
BWH25
]
*2.3.9 to give a slightly modified but equivalent version of the definition of

-riso-triviality. That is, we are fixing a particular lift
\tilde{W}=W({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
of the
\kappa
-linear subspace

with respect to the residue map defined in
[
BWH25
]
*2.3.7. We also omit the necessity of existence of riso-triviality spaces for this setup.

Remark 5.9
.

Because we are specializing to the algebraic situation, we only define translation-invariantness and riso-triviality for sets, and omit the use of indicator functions
[
BWH25
]
*2.3.5.

Our definition of

-riso-triviality for

on a valuative ball

is less restrictive, in the sense that it allows

as a domain of the corresponding

-straightener instead of the whole

. This change of perspective, which was indispensable for Proposition

, recovers the same information:

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
A,A^{\prime}\subset B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n},x}(\gamma)
be two subsets and
\varphi\colon A\to A^{\prime}
a given risometry. Then, there exists a risometry
\tilde{\varphi}\colon B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n},x}(\gamma)\to B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n},x}(\gamma)
extending
\varphi
.

Proof.

Set

f\prec g\iff\operatorname{dom}(f)\subset\operatorname{dom}(g)\ \text{and}\ g|_{\operatorname{dom}(f)}\equiv f
.
Every ascendant chain

in

is bounded above by the risometry

defined by

for any

. Thus, by Zorn’s Lemma, we get a maximal object
\tilde{\varphi}\in\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}
. Since we can use spherical completeness to get limits of Cauchy sequences, the equality
\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\varphi})=B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n},x}
is forced: Every

can be extended to a risometry with valuatively closed domain by valuative continuity. Once we have closed domains, every

has an associated

. In this situation, it suffices to set

5.3.
Global and Functorial Riso-Stratifications

We are now in position to define a canonical and global riso-stratification process. We perform this over a field
k
of characteristic 0. The strength of the model theoretic results of
[
BWH25
]
, together with our previous results, will be converted to functorial properties satisfied by this global procedure.

Definition 5.22
.

Let
X
be a
k
-scheme. For every
0\leq d\leq\dim X
, the
d
-th riso-stratifying sheaf of
X
is the sheaf of ideals defined by:

\mathscr{I}_{X,d}(U):=I_{\mathcal{S}^{\psi}_{\leq d}(U)}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(U),

for every affine open
U\subset X
, fixed closed embedding
\psi\colon U\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\kappa}^{n}
and admissible extension
K/\kappa
over
k
. This association produces radical ideals which are well defined because of Corollary
5.20
and properties (2) and (5) in Theorem
5.18
. The definition of
\mathscr{I}_{X,d}
at affine opens indeed determines a unique sheaf on
X
since compatibility with restrictions follows from Theorem
5.19
.

To every
k
-scheme
X
we are associating a canonical nested sequence of sheaf of ideals:

\mathscr{I}_{X,\dim X}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{X,0}

This is exactly what we use to globalize riso-stratifications.

Definition 5.23
.

The
riso-stratification of
X
over
k
is the canonical tuple:

\mathcal{S}_{\bullet}(X/k)=\{\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)\}_{0\leq d\leq\dim X},

of locally closed sub-schemes of
X
defined by
\mathcal{S}_{0}(X/k)=V\big(\mathscr{I}_{X,0}\big)
and recursively for
0<d\leq\dim X
:

\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)=V(\mathscr{I}_{X,d})\setminus V(\mathscr{I}_{X,{d-1}}).

Remark 5.24
.

Since this is just a globalization of the procedure introduced in
[
BWH25
]
, every local property from Theorem
5.18
is inherited for free to the general construction. In particular, each stratum
\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)
is either empty or smooth of pure dimension
d
,
\mathcal{S}_{\dim X}(X/k)=X^{\mathrm{sm}}
.

Remark 5.25
.

Riso-stratifications are compatible with restrictions to subschemes of the same dimension. That is, if
Y\subset X
is a subscheme, of finite type over
k
and of pure dimension
\dim Y=\dim X
, then
\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)|_{Y}=\mathcal{S}_{d}(Y/k)
for every
0\leq d\leq\dim Y
. This follows easily from Theorem
5.19
. This can be used to extend the definition to schemes which are essentially of finite type over
k
.

We finalize our work by summarizing the functorial properties that this globalized stratification process satisfies:

Theorem 5.26
.

Riso-stratifications are compatible with base change along smooth morphisms and field extensions. That is, for every smooth morphism of
f\colon Y\to X
over
k
of relative dimension
N
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{r}(Y/k)=\varnothing
for
r<N
and:

\mathcal{S}_{N+d}(Y/k)=f^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k),

for every
0\leq d\leq\dim X
. In particular, riso-stratifications commute with étale localizations.
Similarly, for every field extension
F/k
we have:

\mathcal{S}_{d}(X_{F}/F)=\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)\times_{k}\spec(F),

for every
0\leq d\leq\dim X
, as
F
-schemes.

Proof.

Both properties are local on
X
, so we may take
X
and
Y
to be affine. The first assertion follows easily from Theorem
5.19
under any local embedding, and the second assertion is a direct consequence of the scalar extension compatibility on Corollary
5.20
.
∎

Theorem 5.27
.

Riso-stratifications have good spreading properties. More precisely, if
X
is a
k
-scheme and
R
is a domain such that
k=\mathrm{Frac}(R)
and
\tilde{X}
is an
R
-scheme such that

, then each stratum
\mathcal{S}_{d}(X/k)\subset X
is the base change of a uniquely determined locally closed subscheme
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(\tilde{X})
that is of finite presentation over

.

Proof.

This is a local property, so we can assume that
X
is affine. In this case, the result is just a consequence of the scalar extension compatibility property of Corollary
5.20
applied to the local riso-stratification of
\tilde{X}
as an
R
-scheme.
∎

It is in general a highly non-trivial task to find the riso-stratification of a given

Example 6.1

Consider the cusp

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}(C/k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sh}^{1}_{1}\big(C(K)\big)=C_{\mathrm{sm}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0}(C/k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sh}^{1}_{0}\big(C(K)\big)=C_{\mathrm{sing}}

It follows from Corollary

and Remark

. We claim that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}(X/k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sh}^{2}_{1}\big(X(K)\big)=X_{\mathrm{sm}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}(X/k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sh}^{1}_{1}\big(X(K)\big)=X_{\mathrm{sing}}\setminus\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0}(X/k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sh}^{1}_{0}\big(X(K)\big)=\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}

where
p_{1}=(0,0,0),~p_{2}=(0,0,-1),~p_{3}=(0,0,1)
as closed points of
X
.

Proof.

It follows from Corollary
4.3
and Remark
5.17
that
X_{\mathrm{sm}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{2}(X/k)
. Notice that
V=\{x=y=0\}=X_{\mathrm{sing}}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{3}
, and
X(K)
is at most
V
-riso-trivial on
B_{X,x_{0}}
for a singular point
x_{0}\in X_{\mathrm{sing}}
according to
4.13
. Consider
x_{0}=(0,0,z_{0})\in X_{\mathrm{sing}}(k)\setminus\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}
, we have an embedded set-theoretic description:

(6.1)

\displaystyle B_{X,x_{0}}=B_{H_{1},x_{0}}\cup B_{H_{2},x_{0}}\cup B_{H_{3},x_{0}}\cup B_{S,x_{0}},

where
H_{1}=\{x=0\},H_{2}=\{x-y=0\},H_{3}=\{x+y=0\},S=\{xz=y\}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{3}
. Using this decomposition, it is possible to define the function:

\displaystyle\varphi\colon B_{X,x_{0}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{3}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})

\displaystyle\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\begin{cases}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}z_{0},\alpha_{3})~,~\text{if $\alpha\in S({\mathcal{O}}_{K})$}\\
\alpha~,~\text{else.}\end{cases}

It is an exercise to prove that this map defines a risometry to its image, which is
V
-translation-invariant on
B_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{3},x}
. This confirms
X_{\mathrm{sing}}\setminus\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}(X/k)
. The same decomposition (
6.1
) can be used to detect impossibility of a risometry as in Proposition
3.23
adapted to the projection
\pi_{V}\colon B_{X,p_{i}}\to V({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
. Indeed, there are more rv-classes of differences away from the central fiber. This confirms that
\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{0}(X/k)
, and concludes that the first shadow has locally closed strata, hence it already computes the riso-stratification.
∎

Example 6.3

(Whitney Umbrella)
.

Consider the surface
X=\spec\big(k[x,y,z]/(g)\big)
, where
g=x^{2}-y^{2}z
. We claim that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}(X/k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sh}^{2}_{1}\big(X(K)\big)=X_{\mathrm{sm}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}(X/k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sh}^{1}_{1}\big(X(K)\big)=X_{\mathrm{sing}}\setminus\{p\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0}(X/k)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sh}^{1}_{0}\big(X(K)\big)=\{p\}

where
p=(0,0,0)
a closed point of
X
.

Proof.

It follows from Corollary
4.3
and Remark
5.17
that
X_{\mathrm{sm}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{2}(X/k)
. Notice that
V=\{x=y=0\}=X_{\mathrm{sing}}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{3}
, and
X(K)
is at most
V
-riso-trivial on
B_{X,x_{0}}
for a singular point
x_{0}\in X_{\mathrm{sing}}
according to Proposition
4.13
. Now, let
\pi_{V}\colon B_{X,x_{0}}\to V({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
be the projection that forgets the
x,y
-coordinates. Let
x_{0}=(0,0,z_{0})\in X_{\mathrm{sing}}(k)\setminus\{p\}
, notice that we have an embedded set-theoretic description:

(6.2)

\displaystyle\pi_{V}^{-1}(v)=B_{S_{v},v}\cup B_{H_{v},v}

for every
v\in B_{V,x_{0}}
, where
S_{v}=\{x+\omega y=0\},~H_{v}=\{x-\omega y=0\}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{3}
and
\omega\in R
is a square root of
v
(which exists since
K
is algebraically closed, and
v\neq 0
). Moreover, if we set
\omega_{0}\in K
a square root of
z_{0}
, then we can choose
\omega
uniformly for each
v\in B_{V,x_{0}}
in the sense of
S_{v}
and
H_{v}
corresponding respectively to
S_{x_{0}}
and
H_{x_{0}}
in terms of the rv-classes they define when taking differences to its image by
\pi_{V}
. From this, we construct the following function:

\displaystyle\varphi\colon B_{X,x_{0}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{k}^{3}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})

\displaystyle\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\begin{cases}(-\alpha_{1}\omega_{0},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})~,~\text{if $\alpha\in B_{S_{\alpha_{3}},\alpha_{3}}$}\\
(\alpha_{1}\omega_{0},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})~,~\text{if $\alpha\in B_{H_{\alpha_{3}},\alpha_{3}}$}.\end{cases}

It is an exercise to check that this defines a risometry to its image
\pi^{-1}_{V}(x_{0})+B_{V,x_{0}}
. This confirms
X_{\mathrm{sing}}\setminus\{p\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}(X/k)
. Finally, this same decomposition (
6.2
) can be used to measure the fibers of
\pi_{V}\colon B_{X,p}\to V({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
, and check that the central fiber has strictly less rv-classes of differences than any other fiber, hence Proposition
3.23
cannot hold. Therefore,
\{p\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{0}(X/k)
which concludes that the first shadow has locally closed strata, hence it already computes the riso-stratification.
∎
