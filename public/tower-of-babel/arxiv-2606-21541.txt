1.
Introduction

A central problem in geometric flows, and more generally in parabolic PDEs, is to understand the asymptotic behavior of solutions near a limiting solution. This question is particularly important when the limiting solution arises as a parabolic blow-up limit at a singularity of the flow. In many such cases, the limit is self-similar. The first-order approximation of the flow near the limiting solution is then governed by the linearized operator at the limit. When the limit is smooth, the linearized operator admits a suitable spectral decomposition in an
L^{2}
space, and the nonlinear error terms can be controlled, the asymptotic behavior of the flow is determined by the spectral properties of this operator.
This strategy has played a central role in the construction of self-similar blow-up solutions and has been used to obtain sharp estimates near limiting solutions; see, for instance, Merle–Zaag
[
19
]
, Angenent–Daskalopoulos–Sesum
[
2
]
, Choi–Haslhofer–Hershkovits
[
5
]
, the first named author
[
26
]
, Lee-Zhao
[
16
]
, and Bamler-Lai
[
3
]
.

The preceding scheme becomes considerably more delicate when the self-similar solution arising as a parabolic blow-up limit is singular. In this situation, the singular behavior of the original flow develops at a scale much smaller than the self-similar scale, often called the Type II scale. Consequently, the analysis of the asymptotic behavior becomes a genuinely multi-scale problem: at the self-similar scale, the flow is described by the linearization around the singular self-similar solution, whereas at Type II scales, the flow is often modeled on a desingularization of that singular self-similar solution.

There are two distinct regimes. The first is the case where the desingularization can be viewed as an
L^{2}
perturbation of the self-similar solution. In this regime, the linearized operator on the self-similar solution remains dominant, and the analysis is still more or less dictated by the spectral theory described above. See, for example, Velázquez’s construction of Type II blow-up in mean curvature flow
[
28
]
, Herrero-Velázquez in the semi-linear heat equation
[
13
]
, and the first named author in the Ricci flow
[
27
]
. The second regime arises when the desingularization is not an
L^{2}
perturbation of the self-similar solution. In this case, the deviation at the Type II scale is sufficiently large that the linear theory around the self-similar solution no longer governs the leading-order behavior. This is the regime addressed in the present paper.

The difficulty in the non-
L^{2}
regime can be addressed by linearizing around the desingularization itself, rather than around the self-similar solution. This shifts the problem to the construction of
L^{2}
eigenfunctions on the desingularization, which serve as the analytic foundation for the asymptotic analysis. Since the desingularization is a small, though non-
L^{2}
, perturbation of the self-similar solution, one expects its spectral theory to be related to that of the self-similar solution through a perturbative mechanism. When the desingularization is asymptotic to the self-similar solution at infinity, the relevant eigenfunctions can be constructed by a gluing procedure, matching eigenfunctions associated with the self-similar solution to eigenfunctions associated with the desingularization. This type of perturbative spectral theory was studied and used by, for example, Hadžić–Raphaël
[
11
]
, Collot–Merle–Raphaël
[
8
]
, Collot–Ghoul–Masmoudi–Nguyen
[
7
,
6
]
, and Bensouilah–Duong–Ghoul
[
4
]
.

In this paper, we develop a perturbative spectral theory for the linearized Lagrangian self-shrinker operator on the special Lagrangian desingularization of a special Lagrangian cone in a cohomogeneity-one setting. This will provide the analytic foundation for the construction of Type II singularities in Lagrangian mean curvature flow in the companion paper
[
24
]
.

A family of Lagrangian submanifolds
\{L(t)\}_{t\in[0,T)}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
evolves by Lagrangian mean curvature flow (LMCF) if

(1)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{x}\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}_{L(t)}(\mathbf{x}),\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in L(t),

where
\mathbf{H}_{L(t)}(\mathbf{x})=\Delta_{L(t)}\mathbf{x}
is the mean curvature of
L(t)
, and
\Delta_{L(t)}
denotes the Laplace–Beltrami operator of the induced metric on
L(t)
. Suppose that, as
t\to T
, the flow develops a finite-time singularity at the origin. In the self-similar variables

\displaystyle{\mathbf{s}}=e^{\tau/2}\mathbf{x},\qquad\tau=-\log(T-t),

the LMCF (
1
) becomes the rescaled flow

(2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{d\tau}{\mathbf{s}}\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}_{{M}(\tau)}({\mathbf{s}})+\frac{{\mathbf{s}}^{\perp}}{2},\quad\forall{\mathbf{s}}\in{M}(\tau):=e^{\tau/2}L(T-e^{-\tau}).

By the compactness result of Neves
[
20
]
, every subsequential limit of
{M}_{\tau}
as
\tau\to\infty
converges weakly to a union of special Lagrangian cones. Thus, given a special Lagrangian cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
admitting a special Lagrangian desingularization
a\overline{L}\to\mathcal{C}
as
a\to 0
, the formation of a singularity modeled on
\mathcal{C}
is naturally modeled by perturbations of
a(\tau)\overline{L}
, where
a(\tau)\to 0
as
\tau\to\infty
.

Under a cohomogeneity-one symmetry assumption, described in Sections 2.2 and 2.3, the LMCF equation (
1
) reduces to the quasilinear parabolic equation

(3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h=\frac{h_{xx}}{1+h_{x}^{2}}+(n-1)\frac{xh_{x}-h}{x^{2}+h^{2}}

for a function
h:\mathbb{R}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
. There exists a unique even stationary solution

f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
of (
3
) satisfying
f(0)=1
and
f^{\prime}(0)=0
. By scale-invariance, for every
a>0
,
f_{a}(x):=af(\frac{x}{a})
is also a stationary solution. This family of stationary solutions
\{f_{a}\}_{a>0}
satisfies

\displaystyle f_{a}(x)\to x\cot\left(\frac{\pi}{2n}\right)

smoothly on compact subsets of
(0,\infty)
as
a\to 0
. For each
a>0
, the graph of
f_{a}
in
\mathbb{C}
is the profile curve of an equivariant special Lagrangian
a\overline{L}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
asymptotic to a special Lagrangian cone
\mathcal{C}
at infinity.

In the self-similar variable
s=e^{\tau/2}x
, and writing
h=\partial_{s}u
, the rescaled flow (
2
) becomes the fully nonlinear parabolic equation

(4)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}u=\arctan(u_{ss})+(n-1)\arctan(s^{-1}u_{s})-\frac{s}{2}u_{s}+u.

Linearizing the right-hand side at the desingularization determined by
f_{a}
gives the operator

(5)

\displaystyle H_{a}=L_{s,a}-\frac{s}{2}\partial_{s}+1,

where

\displaystyle L_{s,a}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bigl(1+(\partial_{s}f_{a})^{2}\bigr)\bigl(s^{2}+f_{a}^{2}\bigr)^{n-1}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\bigl(s^{2}+f_{a}^{2}\bigr)^{n-1}}{1+(\partial_{s}f_{a})^{2}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)

is the Laplace–Beltrami operator for the special Lagrangian
a\overline{L}
acting on equivariant functions.

H_{a}
is self-adjoint with respect to the weighted
L^{2}
inner product

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{L^{2}_{a}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}uv\>e^{-F_{a}(s)}dV_{a},

with

dV_{a}:=\sqrt{(1+(\partial_{s}f_{a})^{2})(s^{2}+f_{a}^{2})^{n-1}}\,ds\text{ and }F_{a}(s):=\frac{s^{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{s}(\partial_{s}f_{a})^{2}d\tilde{s}.

The main result of this paper gives a precise description of an arbitrary
finite portion of the spectrum of
H_{a}
in
L^{2}_{a}
near the singular limit

a\to 0
. Since the special Lagrangian desingularization

a\overline{L}
degenerates to the cone
\mathcal{C}
in this limit,
the spectral problem for
H_{a}
is not a regular perturbation problem. Instead,
it has two natural regions. In the inner region, after rescaling by
a^{-1}
,
the operator is modeled on the special Lagrangian desingularization
\overline{L}
.
In the outer region, it is modeled on the linearized operator associated with
the limiting cone, whose eigenfunctions can be computed explicitly. We construct
eigenfunctions by solving the corresponding interior and exterior eigenvalue problems
and matching their values and first derivatives at an intermediate point.
This produces eigenfunctions of
H_{a}
with eigenvalues
asymptotic to
1-k
as
a\to 0
, and it yields a spectral gap estimate on the
orthogonal complement of these modes.

Our main spectral theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Eigenfunctions and Spectral Gap)
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
K\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\epsilon>0
.
There exist

s_{0}^{*}=s_{0}^{*}(n,K)>0
and, for each
0<s_{0}\leq s_{0}^{*}
,

a^{*}=a^{*}(n,K,s_{0},\epsilon)>0
and
C=C(n,K,s_{0})>0
with the following property.

For every
0<a\leq a^{*}
and every integer
0\leq k\leq K
, there exists
\widetilde{\lambda}_{k}=\widetilde{\lambda}_{k}(a;n,k,s_{0})\in\mathbb{R}

and a smooth eigenfunction
\phi_{k,a}=\phi_{k,a,s_{0},n}
such that

H_{a}\phi_{k,a}=(1-k+\widetilde{\lambda}_{k})\phi_{k,a}\qquad\text{with}\qquad|\widetilde{\lambda}_{k}(a)|\leq Ca\quad\text{and}\quad|\partial_{a}\widetilde{\lambda}_{k}(a)|\leq C.

Moreover, the eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{k,a}\}_{0\leq k\leq K}
satisfy the following spectral gap estimate.
If
v:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an odd
C^{2}
function such that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}_{a}}+\|\partial_{s}v\|_{L^{2}_{a}}+\|H_{a}v\|_{L^{2}_{a}}<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle v,\phi_{k,a}\rangle_{L^{2}_{a}}=0\quad\text{for all }0\leq k\leq K,

then

\langle v,H_{a}v\rangle_{L^{2}_{a}}\leq(-K+\epsilon)\|v\|_{L^{2}_{a}}^{2}.

We also identify the geometric meaning of the lowest mode. The eigenfunction

\phi_{0,a}
corresponds to the infinitesimal change of scale
of the special Lagrangian desingularization. More precisely, if
\beta_{a}
denotes the
potential associated with the scaling deformation, then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{da}f_{a}=2a^{-1}\partial_{s}\beta_{a}.

The following result shows that
\phi_{0,a}
is close in
L^{2}_{a}
to the
normalized scaling mode
a^{-2}\beta_{a}
. This identification will be used in
the companion paper to modulate the scale parameter
a
while keeping the
remaining modes under control.

Proposition 1.2

(Identification of the Scaling Mode)
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
K\in\mathbb{N}
. There exist

s_{0}^{*}=s_{0}^{*}(n,K)>0
and, for each
0<s_{0}\leq s_{0}^{*}
,

a^{*}=a^{*}(n,K,s_{0})>0
with the following property.

Suppose
0<a\leq a^{*}
, and let

\phi_{k,a}=\phi_{k,a,s_{0},n}
,
0\leq k\leq K
, be the eigenfunctions
constructed in Theorem
1.1
. Let
\beta_{a}
be the potential for the scaling deformation as in
Lemma
2.8
. Then

\|\phi_{0,a}-a^{-2}\beta_{a}\|_{L^{2}_{a}}\leq Ca,

for some constant
C=C(n,s_{0})>0
. In particular,
there exists
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(n,K,s_{0})>0
such that

\left|\langle\phi_{0,a},a^{-2}\beta_{a}\rangle_{L^{2}_{a}}-\|\phi_{0,a}\|_{L^{2}_{a}}^{2}\right|+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left|\langle\phi_{k,a},a^{-2}\beta_{a}\rangle_{L^{2}_{a}}\right|\leq C^{\prime}a.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Lagrangian Mean Curvature Flow in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

Throughout, we take the ambient manifold to be
\mathbb{C}^{n}
equipped with its
standard flat Calabi–Yau structure
(\overline{g},J,\omega,\Omega)
. In terms of
the complex coordinates
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
, these are given by

\displaystyle\overline{g}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(dx_{j}^{2}+dy_{j}^{2}),\qquad J=i\,\cdot,\qquad\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{n}dx_{j}\wedge dy_{j},\qquad\Omega=dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}.

We denote by
\lambda
the Liouville form

\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(y_{j}dx_{j}-x_{j}dy_{j}\big),

such that
\omega=-d\lambda
.

An
n
-dimensional immersed submanifold
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is called

Lagrangian
if
\omega\big|_{L}=0
. We say that a one-parameter family of
immersed submanifolds
\{L(t)\}_{t\in[0,T)}
satisfies the
Lagrangian mean curvature flow
(LMCF) if each time-slice

L(t)
is Lagrangian and

(6)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{x}_{L(t)}\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}_{L(t)},\qquad t\in(0,T),

where
\mathbf{x}_{L(t)}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the position vector and
\mathbf{H}_{L(t)}
denotes the mean curvature vector of
L(t)
.

If
L
is oriented and Lagrangian, then

\displaystyle\Omega|_{L}=e^{i\theta_{L}}\,dV_{L}

for some function
\theta_{L}:L\to\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, called the

Lagrangian angle
. The Lagrangian angle is a potential for the mean
curvature vector, in the sense that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{L}=J\nabla\theta_{L}.

It follows that a connected Lagrangian
L
is minimal if and only if
\theta_{L}
is constant
\overline{\theta}
. In this
case, we call
L
a
special Lagrangian (SL)
submanifold with phase
\overline{\theta}
.

We say a Lagrangian submanifold
L
is
zero-Maslov
if
\theta_{L}
can be lifted to a real-valued function
\theta_{L}:L\to\mathbb{R}
. A zero-Maslov Lagrangian submanifold
L
is
almost-calibrated
if
\osc_{L}\theta_{L}<\pi
, where
\osc_{L}\theta_{L}:=\sup_{L}\theta_{L}-\inf_{L}\theta_{L}
denotes the oscillation.
A Lagrangian submanifold
L
is
exact
if
\lambda\big|_{L}=d\beta_{L}
for some
\beta_{L}:L\to\mathbb{R}
. In this case, the position vector
\mathbf{x}_{L}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
L
satisfies
\mathbf{x}_{L}^{\perp}=2J\nabla\beta_{L}
, where
(\cdot)^{\perp}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the normal space of
L
. In other words, if
L
is exact, the normal part of the position vector of
L
has a potential
2\beta_{L}
.
Exactness, zero-Maslov, and almost-calibrated conditions are preserved under LMCF.

Suppose that a LMCF
L(t)
develops a finite-time singularity at

(P,T)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. That is,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in L(t),\,x\to P\\
t\nearrow T\end{subarray}}|\mathbf{A}_{L(t)}(x)|=\infty,

where
\mathbf{A}_{L(t)}
denotes the second fundamental form of
L(t)
in

\mathbb{C}^{n}
. A coarse asymptotic structure of the singularity is captured
by its
tangent flows
, which arise as subsequential limits of the
parabolically rescaled flows

\displaystyle L^{i}(t):=\lambda_{i}\big(L(T+t/\lambda_{i}^{2})-P\big),\qquad t<0,

where
\lambda_{i}\to\infty
. Neves
[
20
]
showed that if the LMCF has
zero-Maslov class
, then
every tangent flow at a finite-time singularity is a union of special
Lagrangian cones. Equivalently, consider the
rescaled flow

\displaystyle M(\tau)=e^{\tau/2}\big(L(T-e^{-\tau})-P\big),

which satisfies

(7)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathbf{x}_{M(\tau)}\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}_{M(\tau)}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}_{M(\tau)}^{\perp},

then every subsequential limit of
M(\tau)
as
\tau\to\infty
is a union of SL cones. If, in addition,

L(t)
is
exact
, then Neves
[
20
]
showed that
every tangent flow is a union of SL cones of
same phase
.

The tangent flow captures the geometry of the flow near the singularity at the
parabolic scale
\sqrt{T-t}
. If the tangent flow is itself singular, then one
must look at smaller,
Type II scales
in order to detect the smooth geometry
forming near the singularity. We call any smooth limiting flow obtained at such
a scale a
Type II model
for the singularity.

Assuming that the tangent flow is a SL cone, one
expects the Type II model to be modeled on an asymptotically conical SL submanifold. More precisely, one expects the existence of a SL submanifold
\overline{L}
asymptotic to the tangent cone

\mathcal{C}
at infinity, together with a scale function

\displaystyle a(t)=o(\sqrt{T-t})\qquad\text{as }t\nearrow T,

such that, in a neighborhood of the singularity, the flow is asymptotic to the
rescaled model
a(t)\overline{L}
, which collapses to
\mathcal{C}
as

t\nearrow T
.

\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\gamma_{a})-(n-1)\frac{\gamma_{a}^{\perp}}{|\gamma_{a}|^{2}}=0.

Rotating the profile curve by

is symmetric with respect to the imaginary axis and gives rise to an
asymptotically conical SL
\overline{L}
with phase

Definition 3.2

For

H_{s,0}v_{\lambda_{k}}=(1-k)v_{\lambda_{k}}\qquad(s>0)

given by

(73)

It has asymptotics

(74)

\displaystyle v_{\lambda_{k}}(s)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-1)^{k}\frac{2\cdot 4^{k}\cdot k!}{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})^{k+1}}s^{-2k-n}e^{\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}}+O\left(s^{-2k-n-2}e^{\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}};n,k\right),&\text{as }s&\nearrow+\infty\\
&\frac{1}{(2-n)u_{\lambda_{k}}(0)}s^{2-n}+O(s^{3-n};n,k),&\text{as }s&\searrow 0\end{aligned}\right.

(75)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}v_{\lambda_{k}}(s)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-1)^{k}\frac{4^{k}\cdot k!}{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})^{k}}s^{-2k-n+1}e^{\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}}+O\left(s^{-2k-n-1}e^{\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}};n,k\right),&\text{as }s&\nearrow+\infty\\
&\frac{1}{u_{\lambda_{k}}(0)}s^{1-n}+O(s^{3-n};n,k),&\text{as }s&\searrow 0.\end{aligned}\right.

u_{\lambda_{k}},v_{\lambda_{k}}
are linearly independent and have Wronskian

(76)

W[u_{\lambda_{k}},v_{\lambda_{k}}]=s^{1-n}e^{\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}}.

Moreover, there exists a generalized eigenmode
\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(77)

\displaystyle[H_{s,0}-(1-k)]\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}=u_{\lambda_{k}}\qquad(\forall s>0),

(78)

\displaystyle\text{or equivalently}\quad\frac{\partial_{ss}\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}}{1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2}}+\left(\frac{n-1}{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s}-\frac{s}{2}\right)\partial_{s}\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}+k\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}=u_{\lambda_{k}}\qquad(\forall s>0),

which has asymptotics

(79)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}(s)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-1)^{k+1}\frac{2\cdot(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})^{k-1}}{4^{k}\cdot k!}s^{2k}\ln(s)+O(s^{2k};n,k),\qquad&\text{as }s&\nearrow+\infty\\
&-\frac{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\sigma^{n-1}e^{-\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})\sigma^{2}}{4}}d\sigma\right)}{(2-n)u_{\lambda_{k}}(0)}s^{2-n}+O(s^{3-n};n,k),&\text{as }s&\searrow 0\end{aligned}\right.

(80)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}(s)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-1)^{k+1}\frac{2\cdot(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})^{k-1}}{4^{k}\cdot k!}2ks^{2k-1}\ln(s)+O(s^{2k-1};n,k),,&\text{as }s&\nearrow+\infty\\
&-\frac{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{2}\sigma^{n-1}e^{-\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})\sigma^{2}}{4}}d\sigma\right)}{u_{\lambda_{k}}(0)}s^{1-n}+O(s^{2-n};n,k),&\text{as }s&\searrow 0.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

The change of variables
r=\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}
transforms the operator

(81)

\displaystyle H_{s,0}=\frac{\partial_{ss}}{1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2}}+\left(\frac{n-1}{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s}-\frac{s}{2}\right)\partial_{s}+1

(82)

\displaystyle\text{to }\quad\tilde{H}_{r,0}:=r\partial_{rr}+\left(\frac{n}{2}-r\right)\partial_{r}+1.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
,

(83)

\tilde{H}_{r,0}u=(1-k)u\quad\text{or equivalently}\quad r\partial_{rr}u+\left(\frac{n}{2}-r\right)\partial_{r}u+ku=0

is the Laguerre differential equation. It has a polynomial solution given by the generalized Laguerre polynomial

u_{\lambda_{k}}=L_{k}^{\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)}(r)=L_{k}^{\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}\right),

which is known to have the asymptotics (
70
), (
71
) (see e.g.
[
1
]
*Ch. 22).

u_{\lambda_{k}}
solves
H_{s,0}u=(1-k)u
, which in divergence form becomes

\frac{d}{ds}\left(s^{n-1}e^{-\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}}\frac{du}{ds}\right)=-k(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})u.

The associated Wronskian

satisfies

\frac{d}{ds}\left(s^{n-1}e^{-\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}}W\right)=0\qquad(\forall s>0).

It follows that

(
72

v_{\lambda_{k}}(s)=u_{\lambda_{k}}(s)\int_{1}^{s}\frac{1}{u_{\lambda_{k}}^{2}(\sigma)}\sigma^{1-n}e^{\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})\sigma^{2}}{4}}d\sigma

gives a solution of
H_{s,0}v_{\lambda_{k}}=(1-k)v_{\lambda_{k}}
such that
u_{\lambda_{k}},v_{\lambda_{k}}
are linearly independent and have Wronskian

W[u_{\lambda_{k}},v_{\lambda_{k}}]=u_{\lambda_{k}}v^{\prime}_{\lambda_{k}}-u_{\lambda_{k}}^{\prime}v_{\lambda_{k}}=s^{1-n}e^{\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s^{2}}{4}}.

The asymptotics of

can then be computed from (
72
) and the asymptotics (
70
), (
71
).

Finally, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
, the generalized eigenmode
\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
solving (
77
) can be obtained from the variation of parameters formula

(84)

\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}(s)=-u_{\lambda_{k}}(s)\int_{1}^{s}\frac{v_{\lambda_{k}}u_{\lambda_{k}}}{W}d\sigma+v_{\lambda_{k}}(s)\int_{1}^{s}\frac{u_{\lambda_{k}}^{2}}{W}d\sigma-v_{\lambda_{k}}(s)\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{u_{\lambda_{k}}^{2}}{W}d\sigma\\
=-u_{\lambda_{k}}(s)\int_{1}^{s}u_{\lambda_{k}}v_{\lambda_{k}}\sigma^{n-1}e^{-\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})\sigma^{2}}{4}}d\sigma-v_{\lambda_{k}}(s)\int_{s}^{\infty}u_{\lambda_{k}}^{2}\sigma^{n-1}e^{-\frac{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})\sigma^{2}}{4}}d\sigma.

The asymptotics (
79
), (
80
) of
\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}},\partial_{s}\tilde{u}_{\lambda_{k}}
can then be deduced from (

) and the asymptotics (
70
), (
71
), (
74
), (
75

3.2.1.
Exterior Eigenfunctions

In this subsection, we construct eigenfunctions for

outside of a region near

.
These will be matched with the interior eigenfunctions from Subsection
3.1.2
to form globally defined eigenfunctions for small enough
a>0
.
Here, it will suffice to work with
s>0
and perform an odd reflection for the matching argument later.

As usual, we consider the operators

(
33
)

\displaystyle H_{a}=H_{s,a}=L_{s,a}-\frac{s}{2}\partial_{s}+1\qquad(a>0)

(
68
)

\displaystyle\text{and }H_{0}=\frac{\partial_{ss}}{1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2}}+\left(\frac{n-1}{(1+\overline{c}_{0}^{2})s}-\frac{s}{2}\right)\partial_{s}+1.

As in Subsection

, we begin by defining relevant weighted

-norms for the construction of the exterior eigenfunctions.

Definition 3.5
.

Given

and
0<s_{0}<1
, define weighted

-norms

(85)

\|u\|_{Y_{s_{0}}^{b,b^{\prime}}}=\|u\|_{Y_{s_{0}}^{2;b,b^{\prime}}}:=\sup_{s_{0}<s\leq 1}\left(s^{-b}|u|+s^{-b+1}|\partial_{s}u|+s^{-b+2}|\partial_{ss}u|\right)\\
+\sup_{s\geq 1}\left(s^{-b^{\prime}}(|u|+|\partial_{ss}u|)+s^{-b^{\prime}+1}|\partial_{s}u|\right)

and its “order 0” analog

(86)

\|u\|_{Y_{s_{0}}^{0;b,b^{\prime}}}:=\sup_{s_{0}<s\leq 1}\left(s^{-b}|u|\right)+\sup_{s\geq 1}\left(s^{-b^{\prime}}|u|\right).

These norms naturally define Banach spaces

(87)

\displaystyle Y^{2;b,b^{\prime}}_{s_{0}}:=\left\{u\in C^{2}((s_{0},\infty))\,\colon\,\|u\|_{Y^{2;b,b^{\prime}}_{s_{0}}}<\infty\right\}\text{ and}

(88)

\displaystyle Y^{0;b,b^{\prime}}_{s_{0}}:=\left\{u\in C^{0}((s_{0},\infty))\,\colon\,\|u\|_{Y^{0;b,b^{\prime}}_{s_{0}}}<\infty\right\}.

Remark 5
.

Informally,
Y^{0;b,b^{\prime}}_{s_{0}}
captures functions

that grow at most like

near

and at most like

near
\infty

The

-norm involves (for

)

but not

because
solutions of say

have

|\partial_{ss}u|\lesssim|s\partial_{s}u|+|u|+|\psi|\qquad(\text{for }s\gg 1),

but

|s^{2}\partial_{ss}u|\not\lesssim|s\partial_{s}u|+|u|+|\psi|\qquad(\text{for }s\gg 1)

because of the presence of the drift term
-\frac{s}{2}\partial_{s}
in

.

The mean curvature
\mathbf{H}_{\widetilde{L}_{K}(\tau)}

{\sqrt{1+(\partial_{s}f_{a}+\partial_{ss}u)^{2}}}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{\widetilde{L}_{K}(\tau)}=\mathcal{H}[f_{a}+u_{s}]=\cancelto{0}{\mathcal{H}[f_{a}]}+(L_{s,a}u)_{s}+\mathcal{Q}_{s,a}(u)\\

),
we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}[f_{a}+u_{s}]|

\displaystyle\approx{}

\displaystyle|(L_{s,a}u)_{s}|

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left|\left(H_{s,a}u+\frac{s}{2}\partial_{s}u-u\right)_{s}\right|

\displaystyle(\text{since }H_{s,a}=L_{s,a}-\frac{s}{2}\partial_{s}+1)

\displaystyle C_{0}e^{(1-K)\tau}\left|\left((1-K+\tilde{\lambda}_{K,a})\phi_{K,a}-(1+\tilde{\lambda}_{0,a})\phi_{0,a}\right.\right.

\displaystyle a^{2}\left(|\partial_{s}\phi_{K,a}|+|\partial_{s}\phi_{0,a}|+|s||\partial_{ss}\phi_{K,a}|+|s||\partial_{ss}\phi_{0,a}|\right)

Proposition A.7
.

There exists

(290)

For a constant

(291)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0\leq{}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}(C_{0}\partial_{s}u-su)^{2}d\mu_{a}\\

2.1

1+s\partial_{s}\left(\ln\left(e^{-F_{a}(s)}\sqrt{(1+\partial_{s}f_{a}^{2})(s^{2}+f_{a}^{2})^{n-1}}\right)\right)=-\frac{s^{2}}{2}-\frac{s^{2}}{2}(\partial_{s}f_{a})^{2}+O(1;n,a).
