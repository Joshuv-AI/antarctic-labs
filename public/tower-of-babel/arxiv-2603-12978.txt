Existence and uniqueness of the global conservative solutions for the generalized Camassa-Holm equation with dual-power nonlinearities
Xiaoxin Chen, Jian Chen, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the global conservative solutions to the generalized Camassa-Holm equation with dual-power nonlinearities. By introducing a new set of variables, we transform the original equation into an equivalent semi-linear system, which allows us to establish the global existence of conservative solutions. Furthermore, for a given global conservative solution, we construct some auxiliary variables tailored to its specific structure and demonstrate that they satisfy a semi-linear system with a unique solution, thereby deriving the uniqueness of conservative solutions to the original equation. Keywords : The generalized Camassa-Holm equation; Dual-power nonlinearities; Global conservative solutions; Uniqueness



1
Introduction

In this paper, we are mainly concerned with the global conservative solutions for the generalized Camassa-Holm equation with dual-power nonlinearities

u_{t}-\mu u_{txx}+2ku_{x}+\eta u_{xxx}=f(u)u_{x}+s(2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx}),

(1.1)

where
f(u)=Au+Bu^{m},\ \mu>0,\ k,\eta,s,A,B\in\mathbb{R},\ m\in\mathbb{N}
and the function
u=u(t,x)
stands for the average fluid velocity. (
1.1
) was first proposed by Nanta et al. in
[
30
]
as a new water wave model with important physical significance, in which they also derived a class of analytical solitary wave solutions and rigorously proved second-order convergence of the numerical scheme. Recently, Dong
[
14
]
studied the local well-posedness, blow-up mechanism and wave-breaking phenomena for the Cauchy problem associated to (
1.1
). Qiu et al.
[
31
]
utilized the dynamical system method to classify the traveling wave solutions of (
1.1
), including peakons, compactons, and solitary waves. Li et al.
[
25
]
further provided a complete classification of all traveling wave solutions.

When
\eta=\mu=1,\ A=-1,\ k=s=B=0
, (
1.1
) reduces to the BBM-KdV equation

\displaystyle u_{t}+u_{txx}+u_{xxx}+uu_{x}=0,

(1.2)

which was originally proposed by Bona and Smith
[
1
]
, serving as a fundamental model for unidirectional waves incorporating dissipation and nonlinear dispersion. Dutykh and Pelinovsky
[
16
]
utilized numerical simulations to explore solitonic gas dynamics and collective soliton behaviors in (
1.2
). Moreover, Mancas and Adams
[
28
]
established both local and global well-posedness for the initial value problem of (
1.2
) in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
(
s\geq 1
) by contraction mapping and energy estimates.

When
s=\mu,\ A+B=-3,\ m=1
, (
1.1
) reduces to the Dullin-Gottwald-Holm (DGH) equation

\displaystyle u_{t}-\mu u_{txx}+2ku_{x}+\mu u_{xxx}+3uu_{x}-\mu(2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx})=0,

(1.3)

which was derived by Dullin et al.
[
15
]
from water wave theory via asymptotic analysis and near-identity normal form transformations, describing the unidirectional propagation of surface waves in the shallow water regime. (
1.3
) possesses a bi-Hamiltonian structure and admits exact peakons
[
15
]
. The local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for (
1.3
) in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
(
s>3/2
), along with the limiting behavior of solutions as
\mu\to 0
, the stability of solitary waves, and scattering data, were investigated in
[
32
]
. Furthermore, blow-up mechanism and wave-breaking phenomena for both line and periodic cases to (
1.3
) have been presented respectively in
[
26
,
34
]
.

When
\eta=B=0,\ A=-3,\ \mu=s=1
, (
1.1
) reduces to the celebrated Camassa-Holm (CH) equation

\displaystyle u_{t}-u_{txx}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx},

(1.4)

which was derived in
[
4
,
18
]
, models the unidirectional propagation of shallow water waves under gravity, and is renowned for its peaked solitons. In the last three decades, the CH equation has been studied extensively from various perspectives. The literature covers a wide range of topics, ranging from complete integrability
[
17
,
18
]
and geometric structures
[
23
,
29
]
to well-posedness issues
[
12
,
13
,
24
]
. Furthermore, the wave-breaking phenomena
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
22
]
, stability of solutions
[
10
,
11
]
, and ill-posedness
[
20
,
21
]
have been thoroughly studied.

To extend solutions beyond finite-time wave breaking, Bressan and Constantin
[
3
]
pioneered a characteristic method to establish the global existence of conservative solutions for the CH equation. Building upon this, Bressan et al.
[
2
]
subsequently proved the uniqueness of such solutions by developing a direct inverse route that singles out a unique characteristic curve. These seminal frameworks have been successfully adapted to other models, including, but not limited to, the Constantin-Lannes equation
[
9
,
36
]
, the rotation Camassa-Holm equation
[
19
,
33
]
and the nonlinear dispersive wave equations
[
37
,
38
]
.

Motivated by the works mentioned above, we herein investigate the global existence and uniqueness of conservative solutions to (
1.1
). It is convenient to rewrite the Cauchy problem of (
1.1
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}+\left(\frac{s}{\mu}u-\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u_{x}=-P_{x},&t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

where the non-local source term
P
is defined by

P\triangleq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mu}}e^{-\frac{|\cdot|}{\sqrt{\mu}}}*\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}+\frac{s}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u\right).

(1.6)

The existence proof relies on introducing new variables that resolve all finite-time singularities, yielding an equivalent semi-linear system whose global well-posedness follows from standard ODE theory. Reverting to the original variables then recovers the global conservative solutions. A key technical difficulty in this procedure is obtaining an
L^{\infty}
-bound for the convolution
e^{-\frac{|\cdot|}{\sqrt{\mu}}}*u
, which we resolve by adapting the variable transformations and contradiction arguments from
[
27
]
. For uniqueness, we associate any global conservative solution with specific auxiliary variables. This allows us to derive an integral equation that determines a unique characteristic, ultimately reducing the uniqueness of
u
to that of a uniquely solvable semi-linear system.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide some preliminary definitions and state our main result. In Section
3
, by introducing a new set of variables, we convert the original equation into an equivalent semi-linear system, from which the existence of global conservative solutions is derived. In Section
4
, we establish the uniqueness of solutions to (
1.5
) by constructing an auxiliary system of ordinary differential equations.

2
Basic definitions and results

In this section, we recall some definitions of global conservative solutions for (
1.5
) and give our main result.

For smooth solutions, differentiating (
1.5
) with respect to
x
, we have

u_{xt}+\left(\frac{s}{\mu}u-\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u_{xx}=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(-\frac{s}{2}u_{x}^{2}-P-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u\right).

(2.1)

It follows from (
1.5
) and (
2.1
) that

\left(u^{2}\right)_{t}+\left(\frac{2s}{3\mu}u^{3}-\frac{\eta}{\mu}u^{2}+2uP\right)_{x}=2u_{x}P,

\left(u_{x}^{2}\right)_{t}+\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\left(su-\eta\right)u_{x}^{2}+\left(\frac{A}{3}+\frac{s}{3\mu}\right)u^{3}+\frac{2B}{(m+1)(m+2)}u^{m+2}-\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u^{2}\right)_{x}=-\frac{2}{\mu}u_{x}P.

(2.2)

Hence, for smooth solutions,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(t,x)+\mu u_{x}^{2}(t,x)dx

(2.3)

is constant in time. Let
w=u_{x}^{2}
, then (
2.2
) implies

w_{t}+\left(\left(\frac{s}{\mu}u-\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)w\right)_{x}=\frac{2}{\mu}u_{x}\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right).

(2.4)

Before providing our main result, we give some definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

We call
u=u(t,x)
a solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.5
) on
[0,T]
if
u
is a Hölder continuous function defined on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
with the following properties.

1.
u(t,\cdot)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\ \forall\ t\in[0,T]
.

2. the map
t\mapsto u(t,\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous from
[0,T]
into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and satisfies the initial condition
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
together with

\frac{d}{dt}u=-\left(\frac{s}{\mu}u-\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u_{x}-P_{x}

(2.5)

for a.e.
t
. Here (
2.5
) is understood as an equality between functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 2.2
.

We call
u=u(t,x)
a global conservative solution of (
1.5
) if
u
satisfies the following properties.

1.
u
provides a solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.5
) on
[0,\infty)
in sense of Definition
2.1
.

2. There exists a family of bounded Radon measures
\left\{\mu_{(t)},\ t\geq 0\right\}
, depending continuously on time with respect to the topology of weak convergence of measures, whose absolutely continuous part has density
u_{x}^{2}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. This family provides a measure-valued solution
w
to the balance law (
2.4
), namely

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t}+\varphi_{x}\left(\frac{s}{\mu}u-\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)d\mu_{(t)}dt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0x}^{2}\varphi(0,x)dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)u_{x}\varphi dxdt=0

(2.6)

for every test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R})
.

The main theorem of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
s\neq 0
, then for any initial data
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the Cauchy problem (
1.5
) has a unique global conservative solution in the sense of
Definition
2.2
.

3
Existence

This section is devoted to proving the existence of global conservative solutions for the Cauchy problem (
1.5
). We first introduce an equivalent semi-linear system and establish the existence of its global solutions. This result is then applied to obtain the global conservative solutions for the original equation.

3.1.  An equivalent semi-linear system

Given the initial data
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and a new variable
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the non-decreasing map
\xi\mapsto y_{0}(\xi)
by the following equation:

\int_{0}^{y_{0}(\xi)}(1+u_{0x}^{2})dx=\xi.

(3.1)

Assuming that the solution
u
to (
1.5
) is Lipschitz continuous for
t\in[0,T]
, we now derive a system equivalent to (
1.5
) in the independent variables
(t,\xi)
. We define the characteristics
t\mapsto y(t,\xi)
as the solutions of

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}y(t,\xi)=\frac{s}{\mu}u\left(t,y(t,\xi)\right)-\frac{\eta}{\mu},\quad y(0,\xi)=y_{0}(\xi).

(3.2)

Introduce some new variables

u(t,\xi)\triangleq u\left(t,y(t,\xi)\right),\quad P(t,\xi)\triangleq P\left(t,y(t,\xi)\right),\quad P_{x}(t,\xi)\triangleq P_{x}\left(t,y(t,\xi)\right),

v(t,\xi)\triangleq 2\arctan u_{x},\quad q(t,\xi)\triangleq(1+u_{x}^{2})\cdot\frac{\partial y}{\partial\xi},

(3.3)

with
u_{x}=u_{x}(t,y(t,\xi))
. From (
3.1
), we have

q(0,\xi)\equiv 1.

A straightforward calculation establishes the following equalities:

\frac{1}{1+u_{x}^{2}}=\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2},\quad\frac{u_{x}}{1+u_{x}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sin v,\quad\frac{u_{x}^{2}}{1+u_{x}^{2}}=\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2},\quad\frac{\partial y}{\partial\xi}=\frac{q}{1+u_{x}^{2}}=\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}\cdot q.

(3.4)

From (
3.4
), we obtain an expression for
P
and
P_{x}
in terms of the new variable
\xi
, namely

\displaystyle P(\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mu}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\xi}^{\xi^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}\cdot q(s)ds\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left\{\left[-(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu})u^{2}(\xi^{\prime})-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}(\xi^{\prime})+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})u(\xi^{\prime})\right]\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}+\frac{s}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}\right\}q(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime},

(3.5)

\displaystyle P_{x}(\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(\int_{\xi}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\xi}^{\xi^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}\cdot q(s)ds\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left\{\left[-(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu})u^{2}(\xi^{\prime})-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}(\xi^{\prime})+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})u(\xi^{\prime})\right]\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}+\frac{s}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}\right\}q(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}.

(3.6)

Owing to (
1.5
) and (
3.2
), we deduce that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,\xi)=-P_{x}(t,\xi),

with
P_{x}
given by (
3.6
). Similarly, using (
3.2
)-(
3.4
) and (
2.1
), we can obtain

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v(t,\xi)=\frac{1}{\mu}(1+\cos v)\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)-\frac{s}{\mu}\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}

and

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}q(t,\xi)=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\frac{s}{2}-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)\sin v\cdot q,

with
P
given by (
3.5
).

3.2.  Global solutions of the semi-linear system

In this subsection, we study the global solutions of the semi-linear system (
3.7
). Based on the analysis in Section
3.1
, we rewrite the Cauchy problem for the variables
(u,v,q)
in the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-P_{x},\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{\mu}(1+\cos v)\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)-\frac{s}{\mu}\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2},\\
\frac{\partial q}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\frac{s}{2}-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)\sin v\cdot q,\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u(0,\xi)=u_{0}\left(y_{0}(\xi)\right),\\
v(0,\xi)=2\arctan u_{0x}\left(y_{0}(\xi)\right),\\
q(0,\xi)=1.\end{array}\right.

(3.8)

We consider (
3.7
) as an ordinary differential equation in the Banach space

X\triangleq H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times\left[L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\right]\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),

with norm

\|(u,v,q)\|_{X}\triangleq\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|q\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then the Cauchy problem (
3.7
)-(
3.8
) has a unique global solution.

Proof.

We consider only the case
s\neq 0
, since the case
s=0
is similar and even simpler.

Step 1: Local existence

By the standard theory of ordinary differential equations in Banach spaces, it suffices to prove that the right hand side of (
3.7
) is Lipschitz continuous on every bounded domain
\Omega\subset X
, which is defined as

\displaystyle\Omega=\left\{(u,v,q):\ \|u\|_{H^{1}}\leq\alpha,\ \|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\beta,\ \|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{3\pi}{2},\ q(x)\in[q^{-},q^{+}]\ \mathrm{for\ a.e.}x\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

for any constants
\alpha,\beta,q^{-},q^{+}>0
. It’s easy to check that the maps

(1+\cos v)\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u\right),\quad\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}

and

\left(\frac{s}{2}-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u\right)\sin v\cdot q

are all Lipschitz continuous from
\Omega
into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Hence, the proof reduces to showing that

(u,v,q)\mapsto P,\quad(u,v,q)\mapsto P_{x}

(3.9)

are Lipschitz continuous from
\Omega
into
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Since
|v|\leq\frac{3\pi}{2}
implies

\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}\leq\frac{v^{2}}{4}\leq\frac{9\pi^{2}}{8}\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2},

we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{meas}\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:\left|\frac{v(\xi)}{2}\right|\geq\frac{\pi}{4}\right\}\leq 18\int_{\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:18\sin^{2}\frac{v(\xi)}{2}\geq 1\right\}}\sin^{2}\frac{v(\xi)}{2}d\xi\leq\frac{9}{2}\beta^{2}.

Thus, for any
\xi_{1}<\xi_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi_{2}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi)}{2}\cdot q(\xi)d\xi\geq\int_{\left\{\xi\in[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]:\mid\frac{v(\xi)}{2}\mid\leq\frac{\pi}{4}\right\}}\frac{q^{-}}{2}d\xi\geq\left(\frac{\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}}{2}-\frac{9}{4}\beta^{2}\right)q^{-}.

Introducing the exponentially decaying function

\Gamma(\zeta)\triangleq\min\left\{1,\exp\left[\frac{q^{-}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left(\frac{9}{4}\beta^{2}-\frac{|\zeta|}{2}\right)\right]\right\},

it’s easy to show that
\|\Gamma\|_{L^{1}}=9\beta^{2}+\frac{4\sqrt{\mu}}{q^{-}}<\infty
.

To establish the Lipschitz continuity of the maps given in (
3.9
), we first prove that
P,\ P_{x}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, namely
P,\ \partial_{\xi}P,\ P_{x},\ \partial_{\xi}P_{x}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. For simplicity, we only focus on the estimates for
P_{x}
and
\partial_{\xi}P_{x}
, as the arguments for
P
and
\partial_{\xi}P
are similar. Since (
3.6
) yields

\mid P_{x}\mid\leq\frac{q^{+}}{2\mu}\Gamma*\left|\left(-(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu})u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})u\right)\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}+\frac{s}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}\right|,

we obtain

\|P_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{q^{+}}{2\mu}\|\Gamma\|_{L^{1}}C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|_{L^{2}})<\infty.

Differentiating (
3.6
) with respect to
\xi
, we see

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}P_{x}(\xi)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\mu}\left\{\left[-(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu})u^{2}(\xi)-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}(\xi)+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})u(\xi)\right]\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi)}{2}+\frac{s}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{v(\xi)}{2}\right\}q(\xi)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\mu\sqrt{\mu}}\left(\int_{\xi}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\xi}^{\xi^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}\cdot q(s)ds\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left\{\left[-(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu})u^{2}(\xi^{\prime})-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}(\xi^{\prime})+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})u(\xi^{\prime})\right]\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}+\frac{s}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}\right\}q(\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\cdot\mathrm{sign}(\xi^{\prime}-\xi)\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi)}{2}\cdot q(\xi)d\xi^{\prime},

(3.10)

which implies

\|\partial_{\xi}P_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\frac{q^{+}}{\mu}+\frac{q^{+}\cdot q^{+}}{2\mu\sqrt{\mu}}\|\Gamma\|_{L^{1}}\right)(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m}\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|_{L^{2}})<\infty.

Next, we show that

\frac{\partial P}{\partial u},\quad\frac{\partial P}{\partial v},\quad\frac{\partial P}{\partial q},\quad\frac{\partial P_{x}}{\partial u},\quad\frac{\partial P_{x}}{\partial v},\quad\frac{\partial P_{x}}{\partial q}

are uniformly bounded linear operators from the appropriate spaces into
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. By way of example, the detailed estimates for
\frac{\partial P_{x}}{\partial u}
are provided. The other derivatives can be handled analogously.

For
(m,v,q)\in\Omega
, the linear operator
\frac{\partial P_{x}}{\partial u}
is defined by

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial P_{x}(u,v,q)}{\partial u}\cdot\widetilde{u}\right](\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(\int_{\xi}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\xi}^{\xi^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}\cdot q(s)ds\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[-(A+\frac{s}{\mu})u(\xi^{\prime})-Bu^{m}(\xi^{\prime})+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})\right]\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}\cdot q(\xi^{\prime})\widetilde{u}(\xi)d\xi^{\prime}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial P_{x}(u,v,q)}{\partial u}\cdot\widetilde{u}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{2\mu}\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Gamma\|_{L^{1}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m}+1\right)q^{+}\leq C\|\widetilde{u}\|_{H^{1}}.

Similarly, the linear operator
\frac{\partial(\partial_{\xi}P_{x})}{\partial u}
is defined by

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial(\partial_{\xi}P_{x})(u,v,q)}{\partial u}\cdot\widetilde{u}\right](\xi)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\mu}\left[-(A+\frac{s}{\mu})u(\xi)-Bu^{m}(\xi)+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})\right]\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi)}{2}q(\xi)\widetilde{u}(\xi)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\mu\sqrt{\mu}}\left(\int_{\xi}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\xi}^{\xi^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}\cdot q(s)ds\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[-(A+\frac{s}{\mu})u(\xi^{\prime})-Bu^{m}(\xi^{\prime})+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})\right]\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}q(\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\cdot\mathrm{sign}(\xi^{\prime}-\xi)\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi)}{2}\cdot q(\xi)\widetilde{u}(\xi)d\xi^{\prime},

which implies

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial(\partial_{\xi}P_{x})(u,v,q)}{\partial u}\cdot\widetilde{u}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{q^{+}}{2\mu\sqrt{\mu}}\|\Gamma\|_{L^{1}}\right)\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m}+1\right)q^{+}\|\widetilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\widetilde{u}\|_{H^{1}}.

Therefore,
\frac{\partial P_{x}}{\partial u}
is a uniformly linear operator from
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
into
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

In conclusion, the semi-linear system (
3.7
)-(
3.8
) admits a unique solution in
C([0,T];X)
for some
T>0
.

Step 2: Extension to a global solution

The global extension of the local solution to (
3.7
)-(
3.8
) is guaranteed provided

\|q(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\frac{1}{q(t)}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}

(3.11)

stays uniformly bounded on every bounded time interval. The proof is based on the conservation law (
2.3
) and a contradiction argument.

Firstly, we claim that

u_{\xi}=\frac{q}{2}\sin v.

(3.12)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q}{2}\sin v\right)_{t}=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{t}}{2}\sin v+\frac{q}{2}\cos v\cdot v_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{2\mu}\sin^{2}v\left(\frac{s}{2}-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{q}{2\mu}\cos v\left(2\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)-s\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{\mu}\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)+\frac{sq}{2\mu}\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}.

Combining this result with (
3.5
), (
3.7
) and (
3.10
), we obtain

\left(\frac{q}{2}\sin v\right)_{t}=u_{\xi t},

which, together with
\left(\frac{q}{2}\sin v-u_{\xi}\right)\mid_{t=0}=0
, yields (
3.12
).

Next, we prove that

E(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{2}\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}+\mu\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}\right)qd\xi=E(0)\triangleq E_{0}.

(3.13)

In fact, from (
3.5
)-(
3.6
), we infer

P_{\xi}=q\cdot\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}\cdot P_{x}.

Thus, using (
3.7
) and (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{2}\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}+\mu\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}\right)qd\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{2}\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}+\mu\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}\right)q_{t}d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}}2uu_{t}\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}\cdot qd\xi+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mu-u^{2})\sin v\cdot v_{t}qd\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sin v\cdot q\left(-\frac{A}{2}u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)-2u\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}\cdot qP_{x}d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{\xi}\left(2uP+\frac{A}{3}u^{3}+\frac{2B}{(m+1)(m+2)}u^{m+2}-\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u^{2}\right)d\xi=0,

which proves (
3.13
).

Therefore, as long as the solution is defined, (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) give

\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}|u^{2}(t,\xi)|\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}|uu_{\xi}|d\xi\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u||\sin\frac{v}{2}\cos\frac{v}{2}|qd\xi\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}E_{0}.

(3.14)

It follows from (
3.5
), (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\|P\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mu}}\left(2|k|+\frac{|\eta|}{\mu}\right)\left\|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\xi}^{\xi^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}\cdot q(s)ds\right|\right\}u(\xi^{\prime})\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}q(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mu}}\left(\frac{|A|}{2}+\frac{|s|}{2\mu}+\frac{|B|}{m+1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m-1}+\frac{|s|}{2\mu}\right)E_{0}.

A crucial step is to bound

\left\|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\xi}^{\xi^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}\cdot q(s)ds\right|\right\}u(\xi^{\prime})\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}q(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

This can be achieved by employing variable transformations and contradiction arguments. For this purpose, we introduce the following lemma and its corollary regarding variable transformations.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
35
]

If
g(t)
is differentiable on a.e.
[a,b]
,
f(x)\in L^{1}([c,d])
, and
g([a,b])\subset[c,d]
, then we have that
F(g(t))
is absolutely
continuous on
[a,b]
if and only if
f(g(t))g^{\prime}(t)\in L^{1}([a,b])
and
\int_{g(a)}^{g(b)}f(x)dx=\int_{a}^{b}f(g(t))g^{\prime}(t)dt
, with
F(t)=\int_{c}^{t}f(x)dx
.

Corollary 3.3
.

[
35
]

Assume that
g(t)
is absolutely continuous on
[a,b]
,
f(x)\in L^{1}([c,d])
, and
g([a,b])\subset[c,d]
. If
g(t)
is monotonous
or
f(x)\in L^{\infty}([c,d])
, then we have
\int_{g(a)}^{g(b)}f(x)dx=\int_{a}^{b}f(g(t))g^{\prime}(t)dt
.

Define
y(t,\xi)
as the solutions of

y_{t}(t,\xi)=\frac{s}{\mu}u(t,\xi)-\frac{\eta}{\mu},\quad y(0,\xi)=y_{0}(\xi).

(3.15)

Then we have

y(t,\xi)=y_{0}(\xi)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{s}{\mu}u(t^{\prime},\xi)-\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)dt^{\prime}.

(3.16)

It follows from (
3.1
) that
y_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}
is strictly monotonous and satisfies

|y_{0}(\xi_{1})-y_{0}(\xi_{2})|=\left|\int_{y_{0}(\xi_{2})}^{y_{0}(\xi_{1})}1\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\left|\int_{y_{0}(\xi_{2})}^{y_{0}(\xi_{1})}1+u_{0x}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|,

which implies that
y_{0}
is local Lipschitz continuous. Consequently, we see from Step 1 and (
3.16
) that there exists a time
T>0
such that
y(t,\xi)\in H^{1}_{loc}
for
t\in[0,T),
which means
y(t,\xi)
is local absolutely continuous for
t\in[0,T)
. Now we show that
T
can be any positive number.

We claim that

y_{\xi}=q\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}.

(3.17)

Indeed, (
3.12
) and (
3.15
) give

\partial_{t}y_{\xi}=\frac{s}{\mu}u_{\xi}=\frac{s}{2\mu}q\sin v.

Furthermore, (
3.7
) yields

\partial_{t}\left(q\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}\right)=q_{t}\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}-\frac{1}{2}qv_{t}\sin v=\frac{s}{2\mu}q\sin v.

Since
(y_{\xi}-q\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2})\mid_{t=0}=0
, (
3.17
) holds for
t\in[0,T)
.

Consequently, Corollary
3.3
implies that, for
t\in[0,T)
and
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{a}^{b}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\xi}^{\xi^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}\cdot q(s)ds\right|\right\}|u(\xi^{\prime})|\cos^{2}\frac{v(\xi^{\prime})}{2}q(\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\int_{a}^{b}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}|y(\xi)-y(\xi^{\prime})|\right\}y_{\xi^{\prime}}d\xi^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}|s|}ds.

(3.18)

When
a\to-\infty
and
b\to+\infty
, the monotone convergence theorem guarantees the existence of the limit on the left-hand side of (
3.18
). Thus,

\|P\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left(E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+E_{0}+E_{0}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\right).

(3.19)

Similarly, we have

\|P_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left(E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+E_{0}+E_{0}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\right).

(3.20)

From (
3.7
), we see that

|q_{t}|\leq C\left(1+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+E_{0}+E_{0}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\right)q,

which implies

\exp\left\{-C\left(1+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+E_{0}+E_{0}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\right)t\right\}\leq q(t)\leq\exp\left\{C\left(1+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+E_{0}+E_{0}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\right)t\right\}.

(3.21)

Therefore, (
3.17
) yields

\|y_{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|q\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\exp\left\{C\left(1+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+E_{0}+E_{0}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\right)T\right\}.

In addition, it follows from (
3.14
) and (
3.16
) that

y_{0}(\xi)-C\left(1+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)t\leq y(t,\xi)\leq y_{0}(\xi)+C\left(1+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)t,

(3.22)

which means
y(t,\xi)\in L^{\infty}_{loc}
for
t\in[0,T]
. Hence, we have
y(t,\xi)\in H^{1}_{loc}
for
t\in[0,T]
. A contradiction argument shows that
T
cannot be bounded above, which means that the results above hold for all
t\geq 0
.

Using (
3.7
), we derive

\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq B(1+t)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}2uu_{t}d\xi\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|P_{x}\|_{L^{2}},

where
B=B(E_{0})>0
. Let
\kappa
be the right-hand side of (
3.21
) and

\widetilde{\Gamma}(\zeta)\triangleq\min\left\{1,\exp\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left(\frac{9E_{0}}{\mu}-\frac{|\zeta|}{\kappa}\right)\right]\right\}.

Then we have
\kappa^{-1}\leq q(t)\leq\kappa
and

\|\widetilde{\Gamma}\|_{L^{1}}\leq 4\kappa\sqrt{\mu}+\frac{36\kappa E_{0}}{\mu}<\infty.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|P_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\widetilde{\Gamma}\|_{L^{1}}\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left|\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right|E_{0}+\left|\frac{B}{m+1}\right|E_{0}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}+\left|2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right|E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left|\frac{s}{2}\right|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq C,

where
C=C(E_{0},\kappa)>0
. The estimate for
\|P\|_{L^{2}}
is entirely similar. Thus, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(E_{0},\kappa)\|u\|_{L^{2}},

which establishes the boundedness of
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
for
t
in bounded intervals. Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle 2\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|P\|_{L^{2}}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

From the previous bounds, we deduce that
\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded for
t
in bounded intervals. It follows from (
3.12
) that

\|u_{\xi}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\frac{q}{2}\sin v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\kappa}{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}},

which guarantees the boundedness of
\|u_{\xi}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
on bounded intervals of time. Hence, we complete the proof that the solution of (
3.7
)-(
3.8
) can be extended globally in time.
∎

For future use, we state an important property of the above solutions, which is the very reason for requiring
s\neq 0
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
s\neq 0
,

\mathcal{N}\triangleq\left\{t\geq 0:\mathrm{meas}\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:v(t,\xi)=-\pi\}>0\right\}.

Then we have

\mathrm{meas}(\mathcal{N})=0.

Proof.

When
v=-\pi
, we have
1+\cos v=0
and
v_{t}=-\frac{s}{\mu}
by (
3.7
). Given the bounds on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|P\|_{L^{\infty}}
, there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that, whenever
1+\cos v<\delta
, we have

v_{t}<-\frac{s}{2\mu}\ \mathrm{for}\ s>0,\quad\mathrm{and}\quad v_{t}>-\frac{s}{2\mu}\ \mathrm{for}\ s<0.

Since the map
t\mapsto v(t,\xi)
is absolutely continuous at each fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain
v_{t}=0
a.e. on
\{v(t,\xi)=-\pi\}
. Hence, a contradiction argument shows that
\mathrm{meas}(\mathcal{N})=0
.
∎

3.3.  Global conservative solutions for the original equation

In this subsection, we use the global solutions of (
3.7
)-(
3.8
) to construct a global conservative solution for the original equation (
1.5
).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
s\neq 0,\ u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then the Cauchy problem (
1.5
) has a global conservative solution in the sense of Definition
2.2
.

Proof.

Given a global solution
(u,v,q)
of (
3.7
)-(
3.8
), we set

u(t,x)\triangleq u(t,\xi),\quad\mathrm{if}\quad x=y(t,\xi),

(3.23)

with
y(t,\xi)
given by (
3.15
). Now we check that (
3.23
) is well defined. From (
3.1
) and (
3.22
), we see

\lim_{\xi\to\pm\infty}y(t,\xi)=\pm\infty.

Next, (
3.17
) implies that the map
\xi\mapsto y(t,\xi)
is non-decreasing. If
\xi_{1}<\xi_{2}
but
y(t,\xi_{1})=y(t,\xi_{2})
, then

\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi_{2}}q(t,s)\cos^{2}\frac{v(t,s)}{2}ds=\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi_{2}}y_{\xi}(t,s)ds=0,

which yields
\cos\frac{v}{2}=0
throughout the interval of integration. Thus, (
3.12
) gives

u(t,\xi_{2})-u(t,\xi_{1})=\int_{\xi_{1}}^{\xi_{2}}\frac{q(t,s)}{2}\sin v(t,s)ds=0,

which proves that (
3.23
) is well defined for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

According to the definition (
3.23
), we have

u_{x}(t,x)=\frac{\sin v(t,\xi)}{1+\cos v(t,\xi)}\quad\mathrm{if}\quad x=y(t,\xi),\quad\cos v(t,\xi)\neq-1,

which, together with (
3.17
), implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(t,x)+\mu u_{x}^{2}(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{\cos v>-1\}}\left(u^{2}(t,\xi)\cos^{2}\frac{v(t,\xi)}{2}+\mu\sin^{2}\frac{v(t,\xi)}{2}\right)q(t,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{0}.

Since
H^{1}\hookrightarrow C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}
, we see that
u
is uniformly Hölder continuous with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
as a function of
x
. According to (
3.7
) and (
3.20
), the map
t\mapsto u(t,y(t))
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous along each characteristic curve
t\mapsto y(t)
. Hence,
u=u(t,x)
is globally Hölder continuous.

Now we claim that the map
t\mapsto u(t,\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous with values in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, for any interval
[\tau,\tau+h]
and a given point
x
, we choose
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
such that the characteristic
t\mapsto y(t,\xi)
passes through
(\tau,x)
. It follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.14
) that

\|y_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{|s|E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mu^{\frac{5}{4}}}+\frac{|\eta|}{\mu}\triangleq\widetilde{C}

and

\displaystyle\left|u(\tau+h,x)-u(\tau,x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|u(\tau+h,x)-u\left(\tau+h,y(\tau+h,\xi)\right)\right|+\left|u\left(\tau+h,y(\tau+h,\xi)\right)-u(\tau,x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{|y-x|\leq\widetilde{C}h}\left|u(\tau+h,y)-u(\tau+h,x)\right|+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+h}\left|P_{x}(t,\xi)\right|dt.

Integrating over
\mathbb{R}
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u(\tau+h,x)-u(\tau,x)\right|^{2}dx\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{x-\widetilde{C}h}^{x+\widetilde{C}h}\left|u_{x}(\tau+h,y)\right|dy\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\tau}^{\tau+h}\left|P_{x}(t,\xi)\right|dt\right)^{2}q(\tau,\xi)\cos^{2}\frac{v(\tau,\xi)}{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8\widetilde{C}^{2}h^{2}\|u_{x}(\tau+h)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2h\|q(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+h}\|P_{x}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2},

thus proving the claim.

Since
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is a reflexive space, the left-hand side of (
2.5
) is well defined for a.e.
t\geq 0
and the right-hand side of (
2.5
) also lies in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for a.e.
t\geq 0
. From (
3.7
), we observe that for any
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

\frac{d}{dt}u\left(t,y(t,\xi)\right)=-P_{x}(t,\xi),

where
P_{x}
is the function defined at (
3.6
). Moreover, for every
t\notin\mathcal{N}
, the map
\xi\mapsto y(t,\xi)
is one-to-one and a
change of variables yields

\displaystyle P_{x}(t,\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(\int_{y(t,\xi)}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{y(t,\xi)}\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|y(t,\xi)-x\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}+\frac{s}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u\right)(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{x}(t,y(t,\xi)).

Since Lemma
3.4
guarantees
\mathrm{meas}(\mathcal{N})=0
, the identity (
2.5
) holds for almost every
t\geq 0
.

Finally, we consider the balance law. Let
\left\{\mu_{(t)},\ t\geq 0\right\}
be the bounded Radon measures defined by

\mu_{(t)}\left([a,b]\right)\triangleq\int_{\{\xi;y(t,\xi)\in[a,b]\}}\sin^{2}\frac{v(t,\xi)}{2}\cdot q(t,\xi)d\xi.

(3.24)

If
t\notin\mathcal{N}
, then the measure
\mu_{(t)}
is absolutely continuous, possessing density
u_{x}^{2}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Moreover, for
t\in\mathcal{N}
,
\mu_{(t)}
is the weak limit of
\mu_{(t^{\prime})}
, as
t^{\prime}\to t,\ t^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{N}
. It follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.12
) that for any test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t}+\varphi_{x}\left(\frac{s}{\mu}u-\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)d\mu_{(t)}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}(\varphi(t,y(t,\xi)))\cdot\sin^{2}\frac{v(t,\xi)}{2}\cdot q(t,\xi)d\xi dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t,y(t,\xi))\cdot\left[\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)\sin v\cdot q\right](t,\xi)d\xi dt

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(0,y(0,\xi))\cdot\sin^{2}\frac{v(0,\xi)}{2}\cdot q(0,\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)u_{x}\varphi dxdt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(0,x)u_{0x}^{2}dx.

Hence, we complete the proof that
u
is a global conservative solution of (
1.5
) in the sense of Definition
2.2
.
∎

4
Uniqueness

In this section, we establish the following theorem on the uniqueness of global conservative solutions for the Cauchy problem (
1.5
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u(t,x)
be a global conservative solution of (
1.5
) with
s\neq 0
in the sense of Definition
2.2
. Then
u(t,x)
is unique.

4.1.  Preliminary lemmas

Let
u=u(t,x)
be a global conservative solution of (
1.5
) with
s\neq 0
in the sense of Definition
2.2
. Let
y
still denote the solutions to

\frac{d}{dt}y(t)=\frac{s}{\mu}u(t,y(t))-\frac{\eta}{\mu},\quad y(0)=y_{0}.

(4.1)

Since
u
is only Hölder continuous, uniqueness of solutions to (
4.1
) is not guaranteed. However, the balance law (
2.4
) and (
4.1
) yield

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-\infty}^{y(t)}d\mu_{(t)}=\frac{2}{\mu}\int_{-\infty}^{y(t)}u_{x}\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)(t,x)dx.

(4.2)

In the following, we will prove that the solution to both (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) is unique.

Instead of the variables
(t,x)
, it is convenient to work with an adapted set of variables
(t,\beta)
. At any time
t
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define
y(t,\beta)
to be the unique
y
such that

y(t,\beta)+\mu_{(t)}\{(-\infty,y)\}\leq\beta\leq y(t,\beta)+\mu_{(t)}\{(-\infty,y]\}.

(4.3)

When
\mu_{(t)}
is absolutely continuous with density
u_{x}^{2}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure, the above definition shows that

y(t,\beta)+\int_{-\infty}^{y(t,\beta)}u_{x}^{2}(t,z)dz=\beta.

(4.4)

We now establish the Lipschitz continuity of
x
and
u
as functions of
t
and
\beta
through a series of lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u=u(t,x)
be a conservative solution of (
1.5
) with
s\neq 0
. Then, for any
t\geq 0
, the maps
\beta\mapsto y(t,\beta)
and
\beta\mapsto u(t,\beta)\triangleq u(t,y(t,\beta))
are Lipschitz continuous with constant 1. Moreover, the map
t\mapsto y(t,\beta)
is also Lipschitz
continuous with a constant depending only on
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}
.

Proof.

We carry out the proof in three steps.

Step 1.
For any fixed time
t\geq 0
, the map

y\mapsto F_{t}(y)\triangleq y+\mu_{(t)}\{(-\infty,y]\}

Step 2.

Lemma 4.3

\displaystyle=\bar{\beta}_{1}-\bar{\beta}_{2}+\int_{\tau}^{t}G(t^{\prime},\beta(t^{\prime};\tau,\bar{\beta}_{1}))-G(t^{\prime},\beta(t^{\prime};\tau,\bar{\beta}_{2}))dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq(\bar{\beta}_{1}-\bar{\beta}_{2})(1-C_{u_{0}}(t-\tau)),

which gives monotonicity when
t
is sufficiently close to
\tau
. By the continuous method, this property can be extended to all
t
of interest.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
u=u(t,x)
be a conservative solution of (
1.5
) with
s\neq 0
. Then the map
(t,\beta)\mapsto u(t,\beta)\triangleq u(t,y(t,\beta))
is Lipschitz continuous with a constant depending only on
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}
.

Proof.

The Lipschitz continuity of
\beta\mapsto u(t,\beta)
is given by Lemma
4.2
. Thus, it remains to verify that
t\mapsto u(t,\beta)
is Lipschitz continuous.

Let
\beta(t)
be the solution of (
4.23
). Then (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) yield

\displaystyle|u(t,y(t,\bar{\beta}))-u(\tau,y(\tau,\bar{\beta}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|u(t,y(t,\bar{\beta}))-u(t,y(t,\beta(t)))|+|u(t,y(t,\beta(t)))-u(\tau,y(\tau,\beta(\tau)))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\beta(t)-\bar{\beta}|+|t-\tau|\|P_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|t-\tau|\left(\frac{1}{2}\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|P_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{u_{0}}|t-\tau|.\qed

4.2.  Proof of uniqueness

This subsection is devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.1
. We study how the gradient
u_{x}
of a conservative solution varies along a good characteristic. Then we construct a semi-linear system admitting a unique solution, which in turn proves the uniqueness of the conservative solution
u
in the original variables.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

The proof proceeds in five steps.

Step 1.
It follows from Lemmas
4.2
and
4.5
that
(t,\beta)\mapsto(y,u)(t,\beta)
is Lipschitz continuous. Similarly, we can check that the maps
\beta\mapsto G(t,\beta)\triangleq G(t,y(t,\beta))
and
\beta\mapsto P_{x}(t,\beta)\triangleq P_{x}(t,y(t,\beta))
are also Lipschitz continuous. By Rademacher’s theorem, the partial derivatives
y_{t}
,
y_{\beta}
,
u_{t}
,
u_{\beta}
,
G_{\beta}
,
P_{x,\beta}
exist almost everywhere. Furthermore, a.e. point
(t,\beta)
is a Lebesgue point for these derivatives. Let
t\mapsto\beta(t,\bar{\beta})
be the unique solution of (
4.23
) with
\tau=0
. We see from Lemma
4.4
that the following assertion holds for a.e.
\bar{\beta}
.

(GC)
For a.e.
t>0
, the point
(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))
is a Lebesgue point for the partial derivatives
y_{t}
,
y_{\beta}
,
u_{t}
,
u_{\beta}
,
G_{\beta}
,
P_{x,\beta}
. Moreover,
y_{\beta}(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))>0
for a.e.
t>0
.

If (GC) holds, we then call
t\mapsto y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))
a good characteristic.

Step 2.
We aim to obtain an ODE for
y_{\beta}
and
u_{\beta}
along a good characteristic. Let
t\mapsto\beta(t,\bar{\beta})
still denote the unique solution of (
4.23
) with
\tau=0
. Assume that
t\mapsto y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))
is a good characteristic. Differentiating (
4.23
) with respect to
\bar{\beta}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\beta}}\beta(t,\bar{\beta})=1+\int_{0}^{t}G_{\beta}(t^{\prime},\beta(t^{\prime},\bar{\beta}))\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\beta}}\beta(t^{\prime},\bar{\beta})dt^{\prime}.

(4.24)

Next, integrating (
4.1
) over
(0,t)
gives

\displaystyle y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))=y(0,\bar{\beta})+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{s}{\mu}u(t^{\prime},y(t^{\prime},\beta(t^{\prime},\bar{\beta})))-\frac{\eta}{\mu}dt^{\prime}.

Differentiating the above equation with respect to
\bar{\beta}
, we have

\displaystyle y_{\beta}(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\beta}}\beta(t,\bar{\beta})=y_{\beta}(0,\bar{\beta})+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{s}{\mu}u_{\beta}(t^{\prime},\beta(t^{\prime},\bar{\beta}))\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\beta}}\beta(t^{\prime},\bar{\beta})dt^{\prime}.

(4.25)

Finally, differentiating (
4.6
) with respect to
\bar{\beta}
, we get

\displaystyle u_{\beta}(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\beta}}\beta(t,\bar{\beta})=u_{\beta}(0,\bar{\beta})-\int_{0}^{t}P_{x,\beta}(t^{\prime},\beta(t^{\prime},\bar{\beta}))\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\beta}}\beta(t^{\prime},\bar{\beta})dt^{\prime}.

(4.26)

In particular, the left hand sides of (
4.24
)-(
4.26
) are absolutely continuous. Combining these three equations, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d}{dt}y_{\beta}+G_{\beta}y_{\beta}=\frac{s}{\mu}u_{\beta},\\
\frac{d}{dt}u_{\beta}+G_{\beta}u_{\beta}=-P_{xx}\cdot y_{\beta},\end{array}\right.

(4.27)

where

P_{xx}=-\frac{1}{\mu}\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}+\frac{s}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right).

Step 3.
We now return to the original
(t,x)
coordinates and derive an evolution equation for
u_{x}
along a good characteristic. Suppose that
\bar{x}
is a Lebesgue point for the map
x\mapsto u_{x}(0,x)
. Let
\bar{\beta}
be such that
\bar{x}=y(0,\bar{\beta})
and assume that
t\mapsto y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))
is a good characteristic.
We see from (
4.4
) that for
t\notin\mathcal{N}
,

y_{\beta}(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))=\frac{1}{1+u_{x}^{2}(t,y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta})))}>0.

Whenever
y_{\beta}>0
, along the characteristic through
(0,\bar{x})
the partial derivative
u_{x}
can be computed as

u_{x}\left(t,y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))\right)=\frac{u_{\beta}(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))}{y_{\beta}(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))}.

Thus, it follows from Step 2 that as long as
y_{\beta}>0
, the map
t\mapsto u_{x}(t,y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta})))
is absolutely continuous and satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}u_{x}(t,y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta})))=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{u_{\beta}}{y_{\beta}}\right)=\frac{-y_{\beta}\left(P_{xx}y_{\beta}+G_{\beta}u_{\beta}\right)-u_{\beta}\left(\frac{s}{\mu}u_{\beta}-G_{\beta}y_{\beta}\right)}{y_{\beta}^{2}}=-P_{xx}-\frac{s}{\mu}\frac{u_{\beta}^{2}}{y_{\beta}^{2}},

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\arctan u_{x}(t,y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta})))=\frac{1}{1+u_{x}^{2}}\cdot\frac{d}{dt}u_{x}=\left(-P_{xx}-\frac{s}{\mu}\frac{u_{\beta}^{2}}{y_{\beta}^{2}}\right)y_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{s}{2}u_{x}^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P\right)y_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\left(-\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu}\right)u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+\left(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu}\right)u-P+\frac{s}{2}\right)y_{\beta}-\frac{s}{2\mu}.

(4.28)

Step 4.
Define

v\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2\arctan u_{x}&\quad\mathrm{if}\quad 0<y_{\beta}\leq 1,\\
\pi&\quad\mathrm{if}\quad y_{\beta}=0.\end{array}\right.

(4.29)

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle y_{\beta}=\frac{1}{1+u_{x}^{2}}=\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2},\quad\frac{u_{x}}{1+u_{x}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sin v,\quad\frac{u_{x}^{2}}{1+u_{x}^{2}}=\sin^{2}\frac{v}{2}.

Based on the preceding analysis, we obtain the semi-linear system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d}{dt}\beta(t,\bar{\beta})=G(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta})),\\
\frac{d}{dt}y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))=\frac{s}{\mu}u(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))-\frac{\eta}{\mu},\\
\frac{d}{dt}u(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))=-P_{x}(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta})),\\
\frac{d}{dt}v(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))=\frac{2}{\mu}\cos^{2}\frac{v}{2}\left(-(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu})u^{2}-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})u-P+\frac{s}{2}\right)-\frac{s}{\mu},\end{array}\right.

(4.30)

with the initial condition

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\beta(0,\bar{\beta})=\bar{\beta},\\
y(0,\bar{\beta})=y_{0}(\bar{\beta}),\\
u(0,\bar{\beta})=u_{0}(y(0,\bar{\beta})),\\
v(0,\bar{\beta})=2\arctan u_{0x}(y(0,\bar{\beta})),\end{array}\right.

(4.31)

where
P
and
P_{x}
can be expressed in terms of the variable
\beta
as

\displaystyle P(y(\beta))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mu}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}ds\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left\{\left[-(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu})u^{2}(\beta^{\prime})-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}(\beta^{\prime})+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})u(\beta^{\prime})\right]\cos^{2}\frac{v(\beta^{\prime})}{2}+\frac{s}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{v(\beta^{\prime})}{2}\right\}d\beta^{\prime},

\displaystyle P_{x}(y(\beta))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(\int_{\beta}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{\beta}\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\left|\int_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}\cos^{2}\frac{v(s)}{2}ds\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left\{\left[-(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{s}{2\mu})u^{2}(\beta^{\prime})-\frac{B}{m+1}u^{m+1}(\beta^{\prime})+(2k+\frac{\eta}{\mu})u(\beta^{\prime})\right]\cos^{2}\frac{v(\beta^{\prime})}{2}+\frac{s}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{v(\beta^{\prime})}{2}\right\}d\beta^{\prime}.

By the Lipschitz continuity of all coefficients, the Cauchy problem (
4.30
)-(
4.31
) has a unique global solution.

Step 5.
Let
u
and
\tilde{u}
be two conservative solutions of (
1.5
) with the same initial data
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. The corresponding Lipschitz continuous maps
\beta\mapsto y(t,\beta)
,
\beta\mapsto\tilde{y}(t,\beta)
are strictly increasing for a.e.
t\geq 0
. According to Lemma
4.4
, the maps
\bar{\beta}\mapsto\beta(t,\bar{\beta})
and
\bar{\beta}\mapsto\tilde{\beta}(t,\bar{\beta})
are strictly increasing. Thus, we see that the maps
\bar{\beta}\mapsto y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))
,
\bar{\beta}\mapsto\tilde{y}(t,\tilde{\beta}(t,\bar{\beta}))
are strictly increasing for a.e.
t\geq 0
. Consequently, they have continuous inverses
x\mapsto\beta^{-1}(t,x)
,
x\mapsto\tilde{\beta}^{-1}(t,x)
.

It follows from Step 4 that

y(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))=\tilde{y}(t,\tilde{\beta}(t,\bar{\beta})),\quad u(t,\beta(t,\bar{\beta}))=\tilde{u}(t,\tilde{\beta}(t,\bar{\beta})),

which also implies

\beta^{-1}(t,x)=\tilde{\beta}^{-1}(t,x).

Hence, for a.e.
t\geq 0
, we have

u(t,x)=u(t,\beta(t,\beta^{-1}(t,x)))=\tilde{u}(t,\tilde{\beta}(t,\tilde{\beta}^{-1}(t,x)))=\tilde{u}(t,x).

This completes the proof of uniqueness.
