Harder’s conjecture and Hermitian automorphic forms
Hidenori KATSURADA, Nobuki TAKEDA

Abstract
. Let k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 and j ≥ 2 j\geq 2 be integers with j j even, and let f f be a primitive elliptic cusp form of weight 2 ​ k + j − 2 2k+j-2 for SL 2 ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) . We study congruences between a Hermitian Klingen–Eisenstein lift associated with f f and Hermitian cusp forms on the quasi-split unitary group U 2 , 2 \mathrm{U}_{2,2} . Under explicit arithmetic hypotheses on a congruence prime, we prove that the Hermitian cusp eigenform appearing in such a congruence is the Hermitian spin lift of a Siegel cusp eigenform of weight det k Sym j {\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j} . As a consequence, we obtain the spinor L L -polynomial congruence predicted by Harder’s conjecture. The proof combines Mok’s endoscopic classification, Skinner’s Galois representations for unitary groups, and Selmer-group vanishing arguments.

1.
Introduction

Harder’s conjecture predicts congruences between elliptic modular forms and Siegel modular forms of degree two.
Let
k\geq 3
and let
j>0
be even.
For a primitive elliptic cusp form
f\in S_{2k+j-2}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{Q}(f)
dividing the algebraic critical value
L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j,f)
,
the conjecture predicts a Siegel cusp eigenform

F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})
and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
above
\mathfrak{p}

such that

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{\ell}(X,f)(1-\ell^{k-2}X)(1-\ell^{k+j-1}X)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

for every prime
\ell
.
This conjecture goes back to Harder
[
14
]
.
We use the formulation given in
[
3
, Conjecture 3.7]
.
We also use the non-congruence method developed in
[
4
]
, in which residual extension classes arising from Galois representations are excluded by Selmer-group arguments.

There has been considerable work on Harder’s conjecture and related congruence problems.
Ibukiyama related Harder’s conjecture to Shimura-type correspondences and half-integral weight forms
[
18
,
19
]
.
Chenevier–Lannes developed a different approach through automorphic forms arising from even unimodular lattices and Kneser neighbors of the Niemeier lattices
[
9
]
.
Using Arthur’s classification, they proved, among other applications, a congruence predicted by Harder.
More generally, Bergström and Dummigan formulated Eisenstein congruences for split reductive groups in a general automorphic framework
[
6
]
.
Atobe–Chida–Ibukiyama–Katsurada–Yamauchi formulated a stronger version of Harder’s conjecture in terms of a Klingen–Eisenstein lift of the Duke–Imamoğlu–Ikeda lift and a lift from
\mathrm{Sp}_{2}
, and proved it in several cases with even
k

[
3
]
.
Their sequel
[
4
]
proves further cases by combining explicit congruence calculations with Galois-theoretic and Selmer-theoretic arguments.
For odd
k
, Katsurada–Lee proved Harder-type congruences in some cases by using Miyawaki lifts
[
23
]
.

In this paper we work in the range
j\geq 2
and
k\geq 4
.
No parity condition is imposed on
k
, and the argument treats even and odd
k
uniformly in this range.

The restriction
k\geq 4
is imposed only to avoid a boundary case in the use of Mok’s classification.
When
k=3
, the relevant Hermitian weight becomes scalar-valued,
{\det}^{j/2+2}\boxtimes{\det}^{j/2+2}
.
Although the infinitesimal character at infinity remains regular, the corresponding Arthur parameters may involve a non-trivial
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-factor.
Thus the case
k=3
requires a separate discussion in the classification and descent steps.
Since the Hermitian Klingen–Eisenstein congruence theorem used below also includes this scalar-valued boundary case,
the case
k=3
should be accessible by a minor modification of the arguments.
We impose
k\geq 4
in order to avoid this additional case distinction.

The aim of this paper is to derive a conditional Harder-type congruence from congruences for Hermitian automorphic forms.
We fix an imaginary quadratic field
E
and consider the quasi-split unitary group
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
attached to
E/\mathbb{Q}
.
The field
E
is auxiliary.
It may be chosen flexibly, subject to the explicit arithmetic conditions in the main theorem.
In
[
41
]
, congruences between Hermitian Klingen–Eisenstein lifts and Hermitian cusp forms were obtained from congruence primes of algebraic standard
L
-values.
In the case relevant to Harder’s conjecture, this gives a Hermitian cusp eigenform
G
on
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
satisfying

G\equiv_{ev}[f]^{2}_{j+4}\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}
.
The main problem is to determine when
G
is the Hermitian spin lift of a Siegel cusp eigenform
F
.
If
G=\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
, then the Hermitian congruence can be translated into the spinor
L
-polynomial congruence predicted by Harder’s conjecture.

Our main result is the following conditional form of Harder-type congruence.
The precise statement is Theorem
5.8
.

Theorem 1.1

(Main theorem)
.

Let
k\geq 4
and
j\geq 2
be integers with
j
even.
Let
f
be a primitive elliptic cusp form of weight
2k+j-2
.
Choose a sufficiently large number field
K
containing
\mathbb{Q}(f)
and the Hecke fields of all Hecke eigenforms in
\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})
.
Assume that there exist a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{Q}(f)
, an imaginary quadratic field
E
,
and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
K
lying above
\mathfrak{p}
, such that the following conditions are satisfied.
Let
\widetilde{\omega}
be the Teichmüller lift of the mod
\mathfrak{P}
cyclotomic character.

(1)

The rational prime
p=p_{\mathfrak{p}}
satisfies
p>2k+j-2
.

(2)

The class number of
E
is one, and
E/\mathbb{Q}
has a unique ramified rational prime
q
, with
q\neq p
.

(3)

f
is not congruent modulo
\mathfrak{p}
to any other Hecke cusp eigenform of weight
2k+j-2
.

(4)

\mathfrak{p}
divides
L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j,f)
.

(5)

There exists
S_{0}\in\mathrm{Her}_{2}(E)_{>0}
such that

v_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left(a(S_{0};[f]^{2}_{j+4})\right)=-v_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left(L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j,f)\right).

Here the valuation of the vector-valued Fourier coefficient is understood as in Section
3.4
.

(6)

\zeta(-j-1)L(-j,\chi_{E})
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-unit.

(7)

After a suitable choice of a lattice, the image of
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{\infty}})}
under the Galois representation over
K_{\mathfrak{P}}
attached to
f
contains
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}})
.

(8)

We have
q^{j+2}\not\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(9)

\mathfrak{P}
divides none of
L(0,\widetilde{\omega}^{j+1})
,
L(0,\chi_{E}\widetilde{\omega}^{j+2})
, and
L(0,\chi_{E}\widetilde{\omega}^{-j})
.

(10)

\mathfrak{p}
divides neither
L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j,f\otimes\chi_{E})
nor
L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j-1,f\otimes\chi_{E})
.

Then there exists a Hecke cusp eigenform
F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})

such that

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{\ell}(X,f)(1-\ell^{k+j-1}X)(1-\ell^{k-2}X)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

for every rational prime
\ell
.

Except for the essential divisibility condition
\mathfrak{p}\mid L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j,f)
, the hypotheses in Theorem
5.8
are technical and can be checked explicitly in the examples considered below.

The proof has two main parts.

The first part takes place on the automorphic representation side.
We construct Hermitian spin lifts
\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
by using Mok’s endoscopic classification for quasi-split unitary groups
[
25
]
, together with the functorial transfer from
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
associated with the spin representation.
For these lifts, the normalized standard
L
-polynomial of
\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
factors as the product of the spinor
L
-polynomial of
F
and its
\chi_{E}
-twist.
Combining this factorization with the explicit standard
L
-polynomial of the Hermitian Klingen–Eisenstein lift, we prove that

\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)\equiv_{ev}[f]^{2}_{j+4}\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

implies

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{\ell}(X,f)(1-\ell^{k-2}X)(1-\ell^{k+j-1}X)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

for every prime
\ell
.
This is Theorem
3.8
.

The second part gives sufficient conditions for the Hermitian cusp form
G
obtained from the Hermitian congruence to be of the form
\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
.
We use Galois representations attached to automorphic representations of unitary groups by Skinner
[
37
]
.
From the congruence

G\equiv_{ev}[f]^{2}_{j+4}\pmod{\mathfrak{P}},

we obtain residual extension classes, and we exclude the unwanted ones by Selmer-group vanishing.
The strategy follows the Galois-theoretic argument of
[
4
]
.
However, the Selmer groups used here have weaker local conditions at the primes ramified in
E/\mathbb{Q}
, and this requires some modifications in the local arguments.

First, we prove that the Galois representation
R_{G}^{\natural}
associated with
G
is absolutely irreducible under suitable hypotheses; this is Proposition
5.6
.
Next, we prove that the global Arthur parameter of
G
is invariant under conjugation; this is Theorem
5.7
.
In this step, we use the sign formalism for self-dual pseudocharacters due to Bellaïche–Chenevier
[
5
]
.

Once conjugate invariance is known, the associated cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
descends to
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.
The resulting automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
is then identified with the transfer associated with a Siegel cusp eigenform.
This gives
G=\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
, and hence the desired Harder-type congruence.

Combining these ingredients, we obtain Theorem
5.8
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall the automorphic preliminaries.
In Section
3
, we construct and study Hermitian spin lifts.
In Section
4
, we collect the Galois and Selmer preliminaries.
In Section
5
, we prove the results on residual extension classes and descent,
including Proposition
5.6
, Theorem
5.7
, and Theorem
5.8
.
Finally, Section
6
gives numerical examples.

2.1.1.
Unitary groups

Let

We define the Hermitian symmetric domain attached to

\mathcal{H}^{(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.2ptI\hskip-1.2ptI})}_{a,b}=\left\{Z=\begin{pmatrix}Z_{1}\\
Z_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{a,b}(\mathbb{C})\ \middle|\ \begin{array}[]{l}Z_{1}\in M_{b}(\mathbb{C}),\ Z_{2}\in M_{r,b}(\mathbb{C}),\\
\sqrt{-1}({}^{t}\overline{Z_{1}}-Z_{1})-{}^{t}\overline{Z_{2}}Z_{2}>0\end{array}\right\}.

M_{r,b}(\mathbb{C})=0

For

C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{U}(a,b),

2.2.
Hermitian and Siegel automorphic forms

Let

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\rho,V)
be a polynomial representation of

We fix a

-invariant Hermitian inner product

(1)

For all

Definition 2.2

We now define Fourier coefficients for Hermitian automorphic forms on

\mathbf{n}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&X\\

For each place

For
f,h\in\mathcal{A}_{\rho}(G)
, the Petersson inner product is

(f,h)_{G}=\int_{G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})}\langle f(g),h(g)\rangle\,dg.

If either
f
or
h
is cuspidal, this integral converges absolutely.

Remark 2.3
.

The degree one Siegel case recovers the usual theory of elliptic modular forms.
More precisely, we identify
\mathcal{A}_{{\det}^{2\nu}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{1})

with the space
M_{2\nu}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
of elliptic modular forms of weight
2\nu
,
and
\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{2\nu}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{1})
with the space
S_{2\nu}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
of elliptic cusp forms of weight
2\nu
.
Under this identification, a form
f\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{2\nu}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{1})
is regarded as an element of
S_{2\nu}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
.
We write its Fourier expansion at
\infty
as

f(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a(m;f)q^{m},

where
q=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}
.
When we say that
f
is a primitive elliptic cusp form, we mean that
f
is a Hecke eigenform in
S_{2\nu}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
with
a(1;f)=1
.

Suppose moreover that the class number of
E
is one.
Then the space
\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{\nu}\boxtimes{\det}^{\nu}}(\mathrm{U}_{1,1})
may
be identified with a space of elliptic cusp forms.
In particular, via this identification, we often
regard a Hermitian automorphic form
in
\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{\nu}\boxtimes{\det}^{\nu}}(\mathrm{U}_{1,1})
as an elliptic cusp form
of weight
2\nu
.

2.3.
Algebraic
L
-values

Let
F\in\mathcal{S}_{\rho_{(n,\bm{k})}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{n})
be a Hecke cusp eigenform.
For a prime number
p
, let
\alpha_{0}(p),\alpha_{1}(p),\ldots,\alpha_{n}(p)

be the
p
-Satake parameters of
F
, normalized so that

\alpha_{0}(p)^{2}\alpha_{1}(p)\cdots\alpha_{n}(p)=p^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}-n(n+1)/2}.

For a subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we put

\alpha_{I}(p)=\prod_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}(p)
, with the convention

\alpha_{\emptyset}(p)=1
.
We define the local spinor
L
-polynomial by

L_{p}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})=\prod_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}}\left(1-\alpha_{0}(p)\alpha_{I}(p)X\right),

and the spinor
L
-function is given by

L(s,F,\mathrm{Spin})=\prod_{p}L_{p}(p^{-s},F,\mathrm{Spin})^{-1}.

For a Dirichlet character
\chi
, we define

L(s,F,\mathrm{Spin}\otimes\chi)=\prod_{p}L_{p}(\chi(p)p^{-s},F,\mathrm{Spin})^{-1},

where we use the convention
\chi(p)=0
if
p
divides the conductor of
\chi
.

For a primitive elliptic cusp form

f=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a(m;f)q^{m}\in S_{2\nu}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
,
we define the local
L
-polynomial by

L_{p}(X,f)=1-a(p;f)X+p^{2\nu-1}X^{2},

and define

L(s,f)=\prod_{p}L_{p}(p^{-s},f)^{-1}.

We shall also use the standard
L
-function of Hermitian cusp forms.
Let
F\in\mathcal{S}_{\rho}(\mathrm{U}_{n,n})
be a Hecke cusp eigenform.
To fix its normalization, we first specify the representation of the

L
-group that defines the local standard factor.
Throughout this paper,
\mathrm{St}
denotes the composition

\mathrm{St}:{\vphantom{U_{n,n}}}^{L}{U_{n,n}}\xrightarrow{\ \xi_{\mathbf{1}}\ }{\vphantom{(\mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GL}_{2n})}}^{L}{(\mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GL}_{2n})}=\bigl(\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)\rtimes W_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{4n}(\mathbb{C}),

where the action of
W_{\mathbb{Q}}
factors through
\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})
.
Here,
\xi_{\mathbf{1}}
is defined in (
3.1
), and the last arrow
restricts on
\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
to the direct sum of the two standard representations.

For each prime
p
at which
F
is spherical, let
s_{p}(F)
denote its Satake parameter.
We normalize
s_{p}(F)
, viewed as a semisimple conjugacy class in the
L
-group, by requiring the identity

L_{p}(X,F,\mathrm{St})=\det\left(1-\mathrm{St}(s_{p}(F))X\right).

Choose representatives of
s_{p}(F)
as follows.
If
p
splits in
E
, write the corresponding parameters as

\alpha_{1}(p),\ldots,\alpha_{2n}(p)
.
If
p
is non-split in
E
, write them as
\alpha_{1}(p),\ldots,\alpha_{n}(p)
.

With these conventions, the same local standard
L
-polynomial is given explicitly by

L_{p}(X,F,\mathrm{St})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{2n}(1-\alpha_{i}(p)X)(1-\alpha_{i}(p)^{-1}X),&p\text{ is split in }E,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i}(p)X^{2})(1-\alpha_{i}(p)^{-1}X^{2}),&p\text{ is inert in }E,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i}(p)X)(1-\alpha_{i}(p)^{-1}X),&p\text{ is ramified in }E.\end{cases}

The global standard
L
-function is then given by the Euler product

L(s,F,\mathrm{St})=\prod_{p}L_{p}(p^{-s},F,\mathrm{St})^{-1}.

We recall the algebraicity results for the
L
-functions used below.
Let
h
be a normalized primitive newform of weight
2\nu
, level
N_{h}
,
and nebentypus
\psi_{h}
.
Let
\mathbb{Q}(h)
be its Hecke field.
Let
K
be a number field containing
\mathbb{Q}(h)
, the values of all Dirichlet
characters which occur below, and all period quotients which occur below.
Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime of
K
above
p
.
We assume that
p\nmid N_{h}
.

We first recall Shimura’s algebraicity theorem.
Fix Shimura periods

u^{\pm}(h)
as in
[
33
,
34
]

so that

u^{+}(h)u^{-}(h)=(h,h)_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}.

For a primitive Dirichlet character
\eta
, we denote by
G(\eta)
its Gauss sum.
For an integer
s
with
1\leq s\leq 2\nu-1
, put

\alpha(s,\eta)=(-1)^{s}\eta(-1)
.
We put

L^{\mathrm{sh}}(s,h\otimes\eta)=\frac{L(s,h\otimes\eta)}{(2\pi i)^{s}G(\eta)u^{\alpha(s,\eta)}(h)}.

Here
u^{\alpha(s,\eta)}(h)
means
u^{+}(h)
if
\alpha(s,\eta)=1
,
and
u^{-}(h)
if
\alpha(s,\eta)=-1
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
33
,
34
]
)
.

Let
\eta
be a primitive Dirichlet character, and let
s

be an integer with
1\leq s\leq 2\nu-1
.
Then
L^{\mathrm{sh}}(s,h\otimes\eta)\in\mathbb{Q}(h)(\eta)
.

We now replace Shimura’s periods by Kato’s periods.
Let
\Omega^{\pm}(h)
be Kato’s periods associated with
h
, as in
[
22
]
.
After enlarging
K
, we may write

\Omega^{\pm}(h)=\lambda^{\pm}(h)u^{\pm}(h)
with
\lambda^{\pm}(h)\in K^{\times}
.
From now on, we use Kato’s periods in the normalization of special values.
Thus we define

L^{\mathrm{alg}}(s,h\otimes\eta)=\frac{L(s,h\otimes\eta)}{(2\pi i)^{s}G(\eta)\Omega^{\alpha(s,\eta)}(h)}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}.

The following fact about Kato’s periods is known (see, for example,
[
4
, Lemma 9.1]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
h
be a primitive form in
S_{2\nu}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
.
Fix a prime
p
satisfying
p>2\nu-2
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}(h)
dividing
p
.
Assume that
h
is not congruent modulo
\mathfrak{p}
to any other primitive form in
S_{2\nu}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
.
Then the product of Kato’s periods
\Omega^{+}(h)\Omega^{-}(h)
coincides with the Petersson inner product
(h,h)
up to a
\mathfrak{p}
-adic unit.

We shall also use the following algebraicity
result for the standard
L
-function of a Hermitian cusp form.
For
\mathrm{U}_{n,n}
, we identify
K_{n,n,\infty}^{\mathbb{C}}
with

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
in the usual way.
By a dominant integral weight

(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n};l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})
, we mean that

k_{1}\geq\cdots\geq k_{n}\geq 0,\qquad l_{1}\geq\cdots\geq l_{n}\geq 0,\qquad k_{i},l_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}.

We use the same symbol

(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n};l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})

for the irreducible polynomial representation of

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
whose two highest weights are

(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
and
(l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})
.

Proposition 2.6

(Corollary 5.14 in
[
41
]
)
.

Let
F
be a Hermitian cusp form of degree
n
and weight given by a dominant integral weight

(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n};l_{1},\ldots,l_{n})
.
Let
s

be an integer with
0\leq s\leq(k_{n}+l_{n})/2-n
.
Then

L^{\mathrm{alg}}(s+1/2,F,\mathrm{St}):=\frac{L\bigl(s+1/2,F,\mathrm{St}\bigr)}{|D_{E}|^{n/2}\cdot\pi^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(k_{i}+l_{i})+2n(s+1/2)-n}\cdot(F,F)_{\mathrm{U}_{n,n}}}\in\mathbb{Q}(F).

Remark 2.7
.

Suppose that
n=1
and that the class number of
E
is one.
Under the usual identification with elliptic modular forms, we have

L\bigl(s_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2},h,\mathrm{St}\bigr)=L\bigl(s_{0}+\nu,h\bigr)L\bigl(s_{0}+\nu,h\otimes\chi_{E}\bigr)

for a primitive form
h\in S_{2\nu}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
.
Moreover, with our normalization, the Petersson norm on the Hermitian upper half-plane
agrees with the classical Petersson norm of
h
(cf.
[
20
, Appendix to § 10]
).
Consequently, under the assumptions of Lemma
2.5
,
there exists a
\mathfrak{p}
-unit

such that

L^{\mathrm{alg}}\bigl(s_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2},h,\mathrm{St}\bigr)=u\,L^{\mathrm{alg}}\bigl(s_{0}+\nu,h\bigr)L^{\mathrm{alg}}\bigl(s_{0}+\nu,h\otimes\chi_{E}\bigr).

2.4.
Harder’s conjecture

We recall Harder’s conjecture in the form used in this paper.
The original congruence was proposed by Harder in
[
14
]
.
The form below is the corresponding congruence for the spinor
L
-polynomials, as in
[
3
, Conjecture 3.7]
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
j\geq 2
be integers with
j
even.
Let

f=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a(m;f)q^{m}\in S_{2k+j-2}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

be a primitive elliptic cusp form.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
\mathbb{Q}(f)
.
Denote by
p_{\mathfrak{p}}
the rational prime below
\mathfrak{p}
.

For a Hecke eigenform
F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})
and a rational prime

\ell
, let
T(\ell)
denote the standard degree two Siegel Hecke operator
at
\ell
, and write
\lambda_{F}(T(\ell))
for the
T(\ell)
-eigenvalue
of
F
.

Conjecture 2.8

(Harder’s conjecture)
.

Assume that
p_{\mathfrak{p}}>2k+j-2
and
\mathfrak{p}\mid L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j,f)
.
Then there exist a Hecke cusp eigenform

F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})

and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathbb{Q}(f)\mathbb{Q}(F)
lying above
\mathfrak{p}

such that, for every prime number
\ell
, we have

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{\ell}(X,f)\left(1-\ell^{k-2}X\right)\left(1-\ell^{k+j-1}X\right)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}.

We call the congruence in Conjecture
2.8
a Harder-type congruence
for
f
.

Conjecture
2.8
implies, in particular,

\lambda_{F}(T(\ell))\equiv a(\ell;f)+\ell^{k-2}+\ell^{k+j-1}\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

for every prime number
\ell
.
This is the Hecke-eigenvalue form of the Harder-type congruence.

Remark 2.9
.

In the scalar-valued case
j=0
with
k
even,
the spinor
L
-polynomial congruence in Conjecture
2.8
can be upgraded to an equality for a special choice of
F
.
As noted in
[
3
, Remark 3.8(4)]
, this follows from the Saito–Kurokawa lift.
More precisely, if
F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})
is the Saito–Kurokawa lift of the primitive elliptic cusp form
f\in S_{2k-2}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
,
then its spinor
L
-polynomial satisfies

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})=L_{\ell}(X,f)(1-\ell^{k-1}X)(1-\ell^{k-2}X)

for every prime
\ell
.

3.
Hermitian spin lifts

3.1.
Mok’s classification for unitary groups

Let
G_{N}
be the quasi-split unitary group over
\mathbb{Q}

attached to
E/\mathbb{Q}
and of rank
N
.
We write

(a_{N},b_{N})=\begin{cases}(n,n),&N=2n,\\
(n+1,n),&N=2n+1.\end{cases}

Thus
G_{N}=\mathrm{U}_{a_{N},b_{N}}
.

We fix unitary Hecke characters

\chi_{\pm}:\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}/E^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

such that
\chi_{+}|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}}
,
and
\chi_{-}|_{\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}}=\chi_{E}
.
Here
\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}}
is the trivial character, and
\chi_{E}
is the quadratic character
associated with
E/\mathbb{Q}
.

Although
\chi_{+}
can in fact be chosen arbitrarily as long as it satisfies
the above conditions, for the sake of simplicity in the following discussion,
we set
\chi_{+}
to the trivial character
\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}}
.

The classification due to Mok
[
25
]
describes the discrete
automorphic spectrum of
G_{N}
in terms of conjugate self-dual Arthur
parameters for
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
, together with the
L
-embedding
determined by
\chi_{\kappa}
, where
\kappa\in\{\pm\}
.
We regard
\chi_{\kappa}
as a character of
W_{E}
by global class field theory.

Choose
c\in W_{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus W_{E}
such that
\chi_{\pm}(c^{2})=\pm 1
.
Then we define the
L
-embedding

\xi_{\chi_{\kappa}}:{\vphantom{G_{N}}}^{L}{G_{N}}\rightarrow{\vphantom{(\mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GL}_{N})}}^{L}{(\mathrm{Res}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GL}_{N})}=\bigl(\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\bigr)\rtimes W_{\mathbb{Q}}

by

(3.1)

\begin{cases}g\rtimes 1\mapsto(g,{}^{t}g^{-1})\rtimes 1,\\[4.0pt]
I_{N}\rtimes\gamma\mapsto\bigl(\chi_{\kappa}(\gamma)I_{N},\chi_{\kappa}(\gamma)^{-1}I_{N}\bigr)\rtimes\gamma\qquad(\gamma\in W_{E}),\\[4.0pt]
I_{N}\rtimes c\mapsto(\kappa J,J^{-1})\rtimes c.\end{cases}

Here the action of
W_{\mathbb{Q}}
on the dual group factors through
\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})
.
The matrix
J=J_{a_{N},b_{N}}
represents the Hermitian form defining the quasi-split unitary group
G_{N}
.

We denote by
\Psi_{2}(G_{N},\xi_{\chi_{\kappa}})
the set of square-integrable global Arthur parameters
for
G_{N}
compatible with
\xi_{\chi_{\kappa}}
,
in the sense of Mok
[
25
, Definitions 2.4.5 and 2.4.7]
.
Such a parameter is represented by a formal sum

\psi=\boxplus_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{i}\boxtimes\nu(d_{i}),

with
\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}d_{i}=N
, whose summands
\mu_{i}\boxtimes\nu(d_{i})
are pairwise distinct.
Here
\mu_{i}
is a unitary cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{m_{i}}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
,
and
\nu(d_{i})
is the
d_{i}
-dimensional irreducible algebraic representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

For a representation
\mu
of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
, put

\mu^{\star}=(\mu^{c})^{\vee},

where
c
denotes the non-trivial automorphism of
E/\mathbb{Q}
.
We say that
\mu
is conjugate self-dual if
\mu\simeq\mu^{\star}
.
The parameters in
\Psi_{2}(G_{N},\xi_{\chi_{\kappa}})

are precisely those whose simple constituents are conjugate
self-dual and satisfy the following parity condition.

Let
\mu
be a conjugate self-dual cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
.
There are two Asai
L
-functions,
L(s,\mu,\mathrm{Asai}^{+})
and
L(s,\mu,\mathrm{Asai}^{-})
.
Exactly one of them has a pole at
s=1
.
We denote by
\eta(\mu)\in\{\pm 1\}

the sign characterized by the condition that
L(s,\mu,\mathrm{Asai}^{\eta(\mu)})
has
a pole at
s=1
.

We define
\delta(\mu)\in\{\pm 1\}
by
\eta(\mu)=(-1)^{m-1}\delta(\mu)
.
For the
i
-th summand of
\psi
, write

\displaystyle\eta_{i}

\displaystyle=\eta(\mu_{i}),

\displaystyle\delta_{i}

\displaystyle=\delta(\mu_{i}),

\displaystyle N_{i}

\displaystyle=m_{i}d_{i}.

Following Mok
[
25
, Remark 2.4.6 and Definition 2.4.7]
, put

\kappa_{i}=\delta_{i}(-1)^{N_{i}-m_{i}-d_{i}+1}.

Then the condition that
\psi
belongs to
\Psi_{2}(G_{N},\xi_{\chi_{\kappa}})
is that

\kappa_{i}(-1)^{N_{i}-1}=\kappa(-1)^{N-1}\qquad(1\leq i\leq r).

This condition can be rewritten as

(3.2)

\delta_{i}=\kappa(-1)^{N-1+m_{i}+d_{i}}.

For each
\psi\in\Psi_{2}(G_{N},\xi_{\chi_{\kappa}})
, Mok attaches local Arthur packets

\Pi_{\psi_{v}}(G_{N}(\mathbb{Q}_{v}))
and the global packet

\Pi_{\psi}=\left\{\pi={\bigotimes_{v}}^{\prime}\pi_{v}\;\middle|\;\pi_{v}\in\Pi_{\psi_{v}}(G_{N}(\mathbb{Q}_{v}))\right\}.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\psi}
a component group attached to
\psi

(
[
25
, Definition 2.4.8]
).
We denote by

\epsilon_{\psi}:\mathcal{S}_{\psi}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}

the canonical sign character (
[
2
, Section 1.5]
).
For
\pi\in\Pi_{\psi}
, the product of the local packet characters gives a character

\langle\,\cdot\,,\pi\rangle_{\psi}:\mathcal{S}_{\psi}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}.

We recall the local spherical property.
We say that
\psi_{p}
is spherical
if
\Pi_{\psi_{p}}
contains a
K_{p}
-spherical representation.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
25
, Theorem 2.5.1 (a), § 7.6]
,
[
31
,
32
]
)
.

If a local Arthur packet
\Pi_{\psi_{p}}
contains a
K_{p}
-spherical
representation
\pi_{p}^{\mathrm{sph}}
, then this representation
is unique in
\Pi_{\psi_{p}}
.
Moreover, its local packet character

\langle\,\cdot\,,\pi_{p}^{\mathrm{sph}}\rangle_{\psi}
is trivial.

3.2.
Spin
L
-homomorphism

In this section we construct explicitly the
L
-homomorphism

\iota:{\vphantom{H}}^{L}{H}\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}{G}

associated with the Spin representation,
where
H=\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}
and
G=\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
.

The character and cocharacter lattices of
H
are given by

\displaystyle X_{H}

\displaystyle=\{a_{1}e_{1}+a_{2}e_{2}\mid a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},\ a_{1}+a_{2}\in 2\mathbb{Z}\,\},

\displaystyle X_{H}^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}e_{1}^{\vee}\oplus\mathbb{Z}e_{2}^{\vee}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\frac{e_{1}^{\vee}+e_{2}^{\vee}}{2},

equipped with the perfect pairing
\langle e_{i},e_{j}^{\vee}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
.
The root and coroot systems are

\Phi_{H}=\{\pm e_{1}\pm e_{2},\ \pm 2e_{1},\ \pm 2e_{2}\},

and

\Phi_{H}^{\vee}=\{\pm e_{1}^{\vee}\pm e_{2}^{\vee},\ \pm e_{1}^{\vee},\ \pm e_{2}^{\vee}\}.

We fix the simple roots

\{\alpha_{1}=e_{1}-e_{2},\ \alpha_{2}=2e_{2}\}

with corresponding simple coroots

\{\alpha_{1}^{\vee}=e_{1}^{\vee}-e_{2}^{\vee},\ \alpha_{2}^{\vee}=e_{2}^{\vee}\}.

The dual group is
\hat{H}=\mathrm{Spin}_{5}(\mathbb{C})
, which is of type
B_{2}
.

The character and cocharacter lattices of
G
are

\displaystyle X_{G}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{Z}f_{i},

\displaystyle X_{G}^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{Z}f_{i}^{\vee}.

The root and coroot systems are

\displaystyle\Phi_{G}

\displaystyle=\{f_{i}-f_{j}\mid i\neq j\},

\displaystyle\Phi_{G}^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\{f_{i}^{\vee}-f_{j}^{\vee}\mid i\neq j\}.

We choose the simple roots

\{\beta_{1}=f_{1}-f_{2},\ \beta_{2}=f_{2}-f_{3},\ \beta_{3}=f_{3}-f_{4}\}

with corresponding simple coroots

\{\beta_{1}^{\vee}=f_{1}^{\vee}-f_{2}^{\vee},\ \beta_{2}^{\vee}=f_{2}^{\vee}-f_{3}^{\vee},\ \beta_{3}^{\vee}=f_{3}^{\vee}-f_{4}^{\vee}\}.

The dual group is
\hat{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{C})
.

Since
H
is split over
\mathbb{Q}
, the action of
W_{\mathbb{Q}}
on
\hat{H}
is trivial.
For
G
, the action of
W_{\mathbb{Q}}
factors through

\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\{1,\sigma\}
, and the non-trivial element
\sigma

acts on
\hat{G}
by the outer automorphism

h\mapsto J_{2,2}\,{}^{t}h^{-1}J_{2,2}^{-1}.

On the based root datum this induces

\displaystyle\sigma(\beta_{1})

\displaystyle=\beta_{3},

\displaystyle\sigma(\beta_{2})

\displaystyle=\beta_{2},

\displaystyle\sigma(\beta_{3})

\displaystyle=\beta_{1},

\displaystyle\sigma(\beta_{1}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\beta_{3}^{\vee},

\displaystyle\sigma(\beta_{2}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\beta_{2}^{\vee},

\displaystyle\sigma(\beta_{3}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}^{\vee}.

We define the
L
-groups of
H
and
G
as semidirect products

{\vphantom{H}}{}^{L}{H}

\displaystyle=\hat{H}\rtimes W_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathrm{Spin}_{5}(\mathbb{C})\times W_{\mathbb{Q}},

{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}{G}

\displaystyle=\hat{G}\rtimes W_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes W_{\mathbb{Q}}.

We define
\iota:{\vphantom{H}}^{L}{H}\to{\vphantom{G}}^{L}{G}
using the Spin representation
\rho_{\mathrm{Spin}}
of
\hat{H}=\mathrm{Spin}_{5}(\mathbb{C})
:

\iota(h,\gamma)=(\rho_{\mathrm{Spin}}(h),\gamma)

for
h\in\hat{H}
and
\gamma\in W_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We note that
\rho_{\mathrm{Spin}}
identifies
\mathrm{Spin}_{5}(\mathbb{C})
with the symplectic group

\mathrm{Sp}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\subset\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{C})
.

Identifying
X_{\hat{G}}=X_{G}^{\vee}
and
X_{\hat{H}}=X_{H}^{\vee}
,
and ordering the weights compatibly with the chosen simple roots of
G
,
we define
\iota^{*}:X_{G}^{\vee}\to X_{H}^{\vee}
by

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(f_{1}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{1}^{\vee}+e_{2}^{\vee}}{2},

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(f_{2}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{1}^{\vee}-e_{2}^{\vee}}{2},

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(f_{3}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\frac{-e_{1}^{\vee}+e_{2}^{\vee}}{2},

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(f_{4}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\frac{-e_{1}^{\vee}-e_{2}^{\vee}}{2}.

Evaluating on the simple coroots of
G
gives

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(\beta_{1}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{2}^{\vee},

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(\beta_{2}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}^{\vee},

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(\beta_{3}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{2}^{\vee}.

Dually,

\displaystyle\iota_{*}(e_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(f_{1}+f_{2}-f_{3}-f_{4}),

\displaystyle\iota_{*}(e_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(f_{1}-f_{2}+f_{3}-f_{4}),

so that
\iota_{*}(\alpha_{1})=\beta_{2}
,
\iota_{*}(\alpha_{2})=\beta_{1}+\beta_{3}
.
Thus the two outer nodes of the
A_{3}
Dynkin diagram are identified
under the folding, while the middle node corresponds to the remaining
simple root of
C_{2}
.

Finally, since
\sigma
exchanges
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{3}
and fixes
\beta_{2}
,
whereas the
W_{\mathbb{Q}}
-action on
\hat{H}
is trivial, we have

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(\sigma(\beta_{i}^{\vee}))

\displaystyle=\sigma(\iota^{*}(\beta_{i}^{\vee})),

\displaystyle\iota_{*}(\sigma(\beta_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sigma(\iota_{*}(\beta_{i})).

Therefore
\iota
is
W_{\mathbb{Q}}
-equivariant and hence defines a well-defined
homomorphism of
L
-groups

\iota:{\vphantom{H}}^{L}{H}\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}{G},

realizing the folding
A_{3}\to C_{2}
at the level of based root data.

3.3.
Construction of the Hermitian spin lift

Let
k\geq 4
and let
j>0
be even.
The construction in this subsection is adelic and does not require
any assumption on the class number of
E
.
The Hermitian spin lift will be defined by applying Mok’s classification to
the parameter obtained from the Siegel parameter through the spin
L
-homomorphism

\iota:{\vphantom{\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}}}^{L}{\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}}\rightarrow{\vphantom{\mathrm{U}_{2,2}}}^{L}{\mathrm{U}_{2,2}}

constructed above.

For simplicity, we put

\tau_{k,j}={\det}^{j/2+2}\mathrm{Sym}^{k-3}\boxtimes{\det}^{j/2+2}\mathrm{Sym}^{k-3}.

Proposition 3.2
.

For a Hecke cusp eigenform
F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})
,
there exists a Hecke cusp eigenform

\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)\in\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{k,j}}(\mathrm{U}_{2,2})
such that

L(s,\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F),\mathrm{St})=L\left(s+\frac{2k+j-3}{2},F,\mathrm{Spin}\right)L\left(s+\frac{2k+j-3}{2},F,\mathrm{Spin}\otimes\chi_{E}\right).

Proof.

Let
\pi_{F}
be the cuspidal automorphic representation of

\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by
F
.
Let
\psi_{F}^{\mathrm{PGSp}}
be its global Arthur parameter for
\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}
.
In this level-one setting, the condition
j>0
forces

\psi_{F}^{\mathrm{PGSp}}
to be simple.

For each place
v
, let
\varphi_{F,v}\colon W_{\mathbb{Q}_{v}}\rightarrow{\vphantom{\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}}}^{L}{\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}}

be the local parameter of
\pi_{F,v}
.
Since
\iota(h,w)=(\rho_{\mathrm{Spin}}(h),w)
, we may write

(\iota\circ\varphi_{F,v})(w)=(\varphi_{F,v}^{\mathrm{Spin}}(w),w),

with
\varphi_{F,v}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
valued in
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{C})
.
Let
\Pi_{F,v}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
be the representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})

with local parameter
\varphi_{F,v}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
.
The simplicity of
\psi_{F}^{\mathrm{PGSp}}
implies that the restricted tensor product

\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}}={\bigotimes_{v}}^{\prime}\Pi_{F,v}^{\mathrm{Spin}}

is a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Then

L(s,\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\mathrm{St})=L\left(s+\frac{2k+j-3}{2},F,\mathrm{Spin}\right)

Let
\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
be the quadratic base change of

\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
to
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
.

We check the sign required in Mok’s notation.
Since
\rho_{\mathrm{Spin}}
is self-dual, the local parameters satisfy

(\varphi_{F,v}^{\mathrm{Spin}})^{\vee}\simeq\varphi_{F,v}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
for every

v
. Thus
(\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}})^{\vee}\simeq\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
, and its
quadratic base change
\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
is conjugate self-dual.

For the quadratic base change
\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
, the Asai
L
-functions satisfy

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\mathrm{Asai}^{+})

\displaystyle=L(s,\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\mathrm{Sym}^{2})L(s,\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}}\otimes\chi_{E},\wedge^{2}),

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\mathrm{Asai}^{-})

\displaystyle=L(s,\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\wedge^{2})L(s,\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}}\otimes\chi_{E},\mathrm{Sym}^{2})

(see, for example, Krishnamurthy
[
24
]
).
The exterior-square
L
-function of
\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
decomposes as

\displaystyle L(s,\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\wedge^{2})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{F},\wedge^{2}\rho_{\mathrm{Spin}})

\displaystyle=\zeta(s)\,L(s,\pi_{F},\mathrm{St}),

where
\mathrm{St}
is the degree-five standard representation of
\mathrm{Spin}_{5}(\mathbb{C})
.
Thus
L(s,\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\wedge^{2})
has a pole at
s=1
.
The Asai factorization above then shows that

L(s,\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\mathrm{Asai}^{-})
has a pole at
s=1
, and hence

\eta(\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}})=-1
.
Since
m=4
, this gives
\delta(\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}})=+1
.
By (
3.2
), with
N=m=4
and
d=1
, the corresponding sign is

\kappa=+
.

Therefore the
L
-homomorphism
\iota
gives, in Mok’s notation, the
simple stable parameter

\psi_{F,E}=\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}}\boxtimes\nu(1)\in\Psi_{2}(\mathrm{U}_{2,2},\xi_{\chi_{+}}).

Since
\psi_{F,E}
is simple and stable, the component group imposes no further
global sign condition.
Thus a global representation in the packet is obtained by choosing compatible
local members of the local packets.

We choose the holomorphic member at the infinite place.
The Harish-Chandra parameter of the holomorphic discrete series representation associated with
F
is

\lambda_{F}=(k+j-1)e_{1}+(k-2)e_{2}.

Under the spin embedding relevant to the transfer to
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
,
this gives the infinitesimal character

\frac{2k+j-3}{2}(f_{1}-f_{4})+\frac{j+1}{2}(f_{2}-f_{3}).

This parameter is regular.
The corresponding real
L
-packet for
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R})
contains a holomorphic discrete series representation.
We denote it by
\pi_{U,\infty}^{\mathrm{hol}}
.
Its lowest weight is

\left(\frac{2k+j}{2}-1,\,\frac{j}{2}+2;\,\frac{2k+j}{2}-1,\,\frac{j}{2}+2\right).

This is exactly the weight of
\tau_{k,j}
.

We now choose the finite part.
Since
F
is of full level,
\pi_{F,p}
is spherical for every finite prime
p
.
Denote by

\varphi_{F,p}:W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow{\vphantom{\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}}}^{L}{\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}}

the local parameter of
\pi_{F,p}
.
On the unitary side, the local parameter

\varphi_{U,p}:W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow{\vphantom{\mathrm{U}_{2,2}}}^{L}{\mathrm{U}_{2,2}}

associated with
\psi_{F,E,p}
is

\varphi_{U,p}=\iota\circ\varphi_{F,p}.

Since
\varphi_{F,p}
is unramified, the parameter
\psi_{F,E,p}
is
spherical in the sense of the local construction of

[
25
, § 7.1]
.
More precisely, in the notation of
[
25
, § 7.1]
,

\psi_{F,E,p}
is obtained from a spherical character

\pi_{M_{0},p}:M_{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

of the standard minimal Levi subgroup
M_{0}
of
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
.
The local packet
\Pi_{\psi_{F,E,p}}
is described by the irreducible
constituents of the normalized principal series
\mathcal{I}_{P_{0}}(\pi_{M_{0},p})
.
Since
\pi_{M_{0},p}
is spherical, this induced representation has a non-zero
K_{p}
-fixed vector.
Hence
\Pi_{\psi_{F,E,p}}
contains a
K_{p}
-spherical representation.

By Lemma
3.1
, this representation is unique.
We denote it by
\pi_{U,p}^{\mathrm{sph}}\in\Pi_{\psi_{F,E,p}}
.

We define the finite part by

\pi_{U,f}={\bigotimes_{p<\infty}}^{\prime}\pi_{U,p}^{\mathrm{sph}},

and put

\pi_{U}=\pi_{U,\infty}^{\mathrm{hol}}\otimes\pi_{U,f}.

Since the finite local packet characters are trivial and
\psi_{F,E}
is a
simple stable parameter,
\pi_{U}
satisfies the global multiplicity condition.
Therefore
\pi_{U}
occurs in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{disc}}\bigl(G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})\bigr)
.

Hence
\pi_{U}
is generated by a holomorphic Hermitian Hecke cusp eigenform

\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)\in\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{k,j}}(\mathrm{U}_{2,2}).

Finally, by standard base change for
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
,
the standard
L
-function of
\pi_{U}

is the standard
L
-function of
\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle L(s,\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F),\mathrm{St})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{U},\mathrm{St})

\displaystyle=L(s,\Pi_{F,E}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\mathrm{St})

\displaystyle=L(s,\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}},\mathrm{St})L(s,\Pi_{F}^{\mathrm{Spin}}\otimes\chi_{E},\mathrm{St})

\displaystyle=L\left(s+\frac{2k+j-3}{2},F,\mathrm{Spin}\right)L\left(s+\frac{2k+j-3}{2},F,\mathrm{Spin}\otimes\chi_{E}\right).

This proves the proposition.
∎

We call the resulting form
\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
the Hermitian spin lift of
F
with respect to the quadratic extension
E/\mathbb{Q}
.

.

Dummigan conjectured the existence of the lift

\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
in
[

,
in the more general framework of a functorial
lift from

to

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{k,j}}(\mathrm{U}_{2,2})
be a Hecke cusp eigenform on
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
,
and let
\pi_{G}
be the automorphic representation generated by
G
.
Assume the following conditions:

(1)

The global Arthur parameter of
\pi_{G}
is simple.
We write it as

\psi_{G}=\Pi_{G}\boxtimes\nu(1),

where
\Pi_{G}
is a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
.

(2)

There is a cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})

whose base change to
E
is
\Pi_{G}
.
We assume that
\Pi_{q}
is unramified for every finite rational prime
q
.

Then there exists a Hecke cusp eigenform
F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})

such that

G=\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F).

Proof.

We first show that
\Pi
is a self-dual representation of symplectic type.
Since
\Pi_{G}
is conjugate self-dual and is the base change of
\Pi
,
we have
\Pi_{G}^{\vee}\simeq\Pi_{G}
.
Hence, by the uniqueness of quadratic base change for
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
,

\Pi^{\vee}\simeq\Pi
or
\Pi^{\vee}\simeq\Pi\otimes\chi_{E}
.
The second possibility cannot occur.
Indeed, if
q\mid D_{E}
, then
\chi_{E,q}
is ramified.
Since
\Pi_{q}
is unramified by assumption,

\Pi_{q}\otimes\chi_{E,q}
is ramified, and hence cannot be isomorphic to the unramified representation
\Pi_{q}^{\vee}
.

Since
G
has holomorphic weight
\tau_{k,j}
,
the archimedean component of any descent
\Pi
satisfies

\varphi_{\Pi_{\infty}}\simeq I_{2k+j-3}\oplus I_{j+1},

where
I_{m}=\mathrm{Ind}_{W_{\mathbb{C}}}^{W_{\mathbb{R}}}((z/|z|)^{m})
.
Since
j
is even, both
2k+j-3
and
j+1
are odd.
Hence the archimedean parameter has symplectic sign.
For a regular algebraic self-dual cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
,
this archimedean sign determines the global self-dual sign.
Therefore
\Pi
is of symplectic type.

Thus
\Pi
defines a simple global Arthur parameter
\psi_{\Pi}
for
\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}
.
Applying the
L
-homomorphism
\iota
constructed above to the

{}^{L}\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}
-valued parameter underlying
\psi_{\Pi}

gives the simple parameter
\Pi_{G}\boxtimes\nu(1)
for
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
.

Let
q
be a finite rational prime,
and let
\varphi_{\Pi,q}:W_{\mathbb{Q}_{q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{C})
be the unramified local parameter of
\Pi_{q}
.
Since
\Pi
is of symplectic type, after replacing
\varphi_{\Pi,q}
by a
conjugate representative, its image is contained in

\mathrm{Sp}_{2}(\mathbb{C})=\rho_{\mathrm{Spin}}(\mathrm{Spin}_{5}(\mathbb{C}))
.
We define the corresponding unramified parameter for
\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}
by

\varphi_{\psi,q}=\rho_{\mathrm{Spin}}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{\Pi,q}\colon W_{\mathbb{Q}_{q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Spin}_{5}(\mathbb{C}).

The local packet attached to
\varphi_{\psi,q}
contains a unique unramified representation.
Denote it by
\pi_{q}^{\mathrm{ur}}
.

Put

\pi_{F}=\pi_{\infty}\otimes{\bigotimes_{q<\infty}}^{\prime}\pi_{q}^{\mathrm{ur}}.

By Arthur’s multiplicity formula for
\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}
,
together with the triviality of the component group in this simple parameter case,

\pi_{F}
is automorphic.
It has holomorphic archimedean component of lowest

K_{\infty}
-type
{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}
and unramified finite components.
Hence
\pi_{F}
is generated by a Hecke cusp eigenform

F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})
.

By construction, the global Arthur parameter of
\pi_{F}
is
\psi_{\Pi}
.
Applying
\iota
to the
{}^{L}\mathrm{PGSp}_{2}
-valued parameter underlying
\psi_{\Pi}

gives
\Pi_{G}\boxtimes\nu(1)
,
which is the global Arthur parameter of
G
.
Therefore the Hermitian spin lift
\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
and
G
have the same
global Arthur parameter, and hence the same Hecke eigenvalues at all finite places.
Choosing
F
in this Hecke eigenspace suitably, we obtain
G=\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
E/\mathbb{Q}
has a unique ramified rational prime.
Let
G\in\mathcal{S}_{\tau_{k,j}}(\mathrm{U}_{2,2})
be a Hecke cusp eigenform on
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
, and let

\psi_{G}=\Pi_{G}\boxtimes\nu(1)

be a simple global Arthur parameter for
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}
,
where
\Pi_{G}
is a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
.

If
\Pi_{G}
descends to a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
,
then
\Pi_{G}
admits a descent
\Pi
to
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})

such that
\Pi_{q}
is unramified for every finite rational prime
q
.

Proof.

Let
\Pi
be any cuspidal automorphic representation of

\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
whose base change to
E
is
\Pi_{G}
.
We show that, after replacing
\Pi
by
\Pi\otimes\chi_{E}

if necessary, all finite local components of
\Pi
are unramified.

Let
q
be a finite rational prime with
q\nmid D_{E}
.
Since
G
is of level one, the local component of
G
at
q
is spherical.
Hence its local base change
\Pi_{G,w}
is unramified.
Since
\Pi_{G,w}
is also the local base change of
\Pi_{q}
,
the local Langlands parameter of
\Pi_{q}
is unramified.
Hence
\Pi_{q}
is unramified for every
q\nmid D_{E}
.

Denote by
q_{0}
the unique rational prime ramified in
E/\mathbb{Q}
,
and by
w_{0}
the unique place of
E
above
q_{0}
.
At
q_{0}
, the level-one subgroup is the special maximal parahoric subgroup of
\mathrm{U}_{2,2}(\mathbb{Q}_{q_{0}})
.
Hence
\pi_{G,q_{0}}
is spherical in the sense of Mok.

By
[
25
, § 7.1]
, the local packet containing

\pi_{G,q_{0}}
is obtained from a spherical character of the standard minimal Levi

M_{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{q_{0}})\simeq(E_{w_{0}}^{\times})^{2}.

This character is trivial on
(\mathcal{O}_{E_{w_{0}}}^{\times})^{2}
.
Therefore the corresponding
W_{E_{w_{0}}}
-characters are unramified.

We use the standard base-change
L
-embedding with
\chi_{+}=\mathbf{1}
.
Under this embedding, the stable base-change parameter is the restriction of the unitary-group parameter to
W_{E_{w_{0}}}
.
Hence the local base change
\Pi_{G,w_{0}}
is an unramified representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(E_{w_{0}})
.

Let
\phi_{G,w_{0}}
be its unramified Langlands parameter.
Since
E_{w_{0}}/\mathbb{Q}_{q_{0}}
is ramified quadratic, its residue degree is one.
Thus
\phi_{G,w_{0}}
has an unramified extension to
W_{\mathbb{Q}_{q_{0}}}

obtained by sending
\mathrm{Frob}_{q_{0}}
to the same semisimple conjugacy class as

\mathrm{Frob}_{w_{0}}
and by making inertia act trivially.
Let
\Pi_{q_{0}}^{\mathrm{ur}}
be the corresponding unramified representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{q_{0}})
.
Its local base change to
E_{w_{0}}
is
\Pi_{G,w_{0}}
.

By local cyclic base change for
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
, we have

\Pi_{q_{0}}\simeq\Pi_{q_{0}}^{\mathrm{ur}}\quad\text{or}\quad\Pi_{q_{0}}\simeq\Pi_{q_{0}}^{\mathrm{ur}}\otimes\chi_{E,q_{0}}.

In the first case
\Pi_{q_{0}}
is unramified, and in the second case
(\Pi\otimes\chi_{E})_{q_{0}}
is unramified.

Since
q_{0}
is the only ramified rational prime of
E/\mathbb{Q}
,
the global twist by
\chi_{E}
does not introduce ramification at any prime
q\nmid D_{E}
.
Hence either
\Pi
or
\Pi\otimes\chi_{E}
is unramified at every finite rational prime.
Replacing
\Pi
by that descent proves the lemma.
∎

To simplify notation, for a Hermitian automorphic form

Proposition 3.6

, and let

be a primitive elliptic cusp form of weight
2\nu
.
Assume that there exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{Q}(f)
satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

The form
f
is not congruent modulo
\mathfrak{p}
to any other
Hecke cusp eigenform of weight
2\nu
.

(2)

\zeta(3-2\mu)L(4-2\mu,\chi_{E})
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-unit.

(3)

There exists
S_{0}\in\mathrm{Her}_{2}(E)_{>0}
such that

v_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left(L^{\mathrm{alg}}((\mu-1)+1/2,f,\mathrm{St})\right)=-v_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left(a(S_{0};[f]^{2}_{2\mu})\right)>0.

(4)

The rational prime
p_{\mathfrak{p}}
underlying
\mathfrak{p}
satisfies
p_{\mathfrak{p}}>2\mu+1
and
p_{\mathfrak{p}}\nmid D_{E}
.

Then there exists a Hecke cusp eigenform

G\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma_{\mu,\nu}}(\mathrm{U}_{2,2})

such that

G\equiv_{ev}[f]_{2\mu}^{2}\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

for some prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
K=\mathbb{Q}(f,G)
lying above
\mathfrak{p}
.

Remark 3.7
.

The pullback formula, together with the formula of Böcherer–Satoh–Yamazaki
[
8
]

for Fourier coefficients, shows that, after unfolding,
the coefficient
a(S_{0};[f]^{2}_{2\mu})
can be expressed in terms of Rankin–Selberg-type Dirichlet series
involving the Fourier coefficients of
f
,
local Hermitian Siegel series, and explicit archimedean factors.
Thus condition (3) can in principle be reformulated as a valuation condition for these Rankin–Selberg-type quantities.
We retain the Fourier-coefficient formulation, since it is better suited to the explicit computations carried out below.

Let
k\geq 4
and
j\geq 2
be integers with
j
even.
Put
\mu=j/2+2
,
\nu=k+j/2-1
.
Then Proposition
3.6
may apply to a primitive elliptic cusp form

f
of weight
2k+j-2
.
For this choice of
\mu
and
\nu
,
we have
\sigma_{\mu,\nu}=\tau_{k,j}
and

[f]_{2\mu}^{2}=[f]_{j+4}^{2}
.

If the cusp form
G
obtained in Proposition
3.6
is the lift

\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
of a Siegel cusp form constructed in the previous section,
then the congruence can be translated into the Harder-type congruence.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let

f\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k+j/2-1}\boxtimes{\det}^{k+j/2-1}}(\mathrm{U}_{1,1})

be a primitive elliptic cusp form of weight
2k+j-2
, and let

F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})
be a Hecke cusp eigenform.
Assume that
p_{\mathfrak{P}}>2k+j-2
and
p_{\mathfrak{P}}\nmid D_{E}
.
Assume also that

\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)\equiv_{ev}[f]^{2}_{j+4}\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}.

Then we have

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{\ell}(X,f)(1-\ell^{k+j-1}X)(1-\ell^{k-2}X)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

for every prime
\ell
.

To prove the theorem, we recall the Galois representations attached to these
automorphic forms and define some notations.

By the work of Deligne
[
11
]
,
there is a
2
-dimensional continuous semisimple Galois representation

\rho_{f}\colon\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K_{\mathfrak{P}})

associated with the elliptic modular form
f
,
characterized by the property that for any prime
\ell\neq p_{\mathfrak{P}}
,

L_{\ell}(X,f)=\det(1-\rho_{f}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell})X).

Similarly, by the work of Weissauer
[
43
]
,
the cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
associated with
F

admits a
4
-dimensional continuous semisimple Galois representation

\rho_{F}\colon\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{4}(K_{\mathfrak{P}})

such that, for any prime
\ell\neq p_{\mathfrak{P}}
,

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})=\det(1-\rho_{F}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell})X).

We denote by
\overline{\rho}_{f}^{ss}
and
\overline{\rho}_{F}^{ss}

the semisimplifications of their respective residual representations modulo
\mathfrak{P}
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}})
the Grothendieck
ring of finite-dimensional continuous representations of
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}

over
\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
.
By abuse of notation, we use the same
symbol for a residual representation and for its
class in
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Addition is induced by direct sum,
and multiplication is induced by tensor product.
Throughout this section, all decompositions and equalities of Galois representations
are understood in the Grothendieck ring
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}})
,
unless otherwise stated.

We denote by
\overline{\omega}\colon\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}

the mod
\mathfrak{P}
cyclotomic character, and by

\widetilde{\omega}\colon\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}

its Teichmüller lift, viewed also as the corresponding Dirichlet character.
We use geometric Frobenius elements.
Thus, for any rational prime
\ell\neq p_{\mathfrak{P}}
, we have

\overline{\omega}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell})=\ell^{-1}\in\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times}.

We define an element
\mathcal{B}_{f}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}})
by

\mathcal{B}_{f}=\overline{\rho}_{f}^{ss}+\overline{\omega}^{-k+2}+\overline{\omega}^{-k-j+1}.

Proof of Theorem
3.8
.

Put

A_{\ell}(X)=(1-\ell^{k+j-1}X)(1-\ell^{k-2}X).

The assumed congruence of Hecke eigenvalues gives

L_{\ell}^{\natural}(X,\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F),\mathrm{St})\equiv L_{\ell}^{\natural}(X,[f]^{2}_{j+4},\mathrm{St})\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

for every prime
\ell\neq p_{\mathfrak{P}}
.
By Proposition
3.2
, we have

L_{\ell}^{\natural}(X,\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F),\mathrm{St})=L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})L_{\ell}(\chi_{E}(\ell)X,F,\mathrm{Spin}).

On the other hand, the local standard
L
-polynomial of the Hermitian Klingen–Eisenstein lift is

L_{\ell}^{\natural}(X,[f]^{2}_{j+4},\mathrm{St})=L_{\ell}(X,f)L_{\ell}(\chi_{E}(\ell)X,f)A_{\ell}(X)A_{\ell}(\chi_{E}(\ell)X).

Hence

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})L_{\ell}(\chi_{E}(\ell)X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{\ell}(X,f)L_{\ell}(\chi_{E}(\ell)X,f)A_{\ell}(X)A_{\ell}(\chi_{E}(\ell)X)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

for every prime
\ell\neq p_{\mathfrak{P}}
.

In the Grothendieck group
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, we have

(1+\overline{\chi}_{E})\overline{\rho}_{F}^{ss}=(1+\overline{\chi}_{E})\mathcal{B}_{f}.

Therefore every Jordan–Hölder constituent of

\overline{\rho}_{F}^{ss}
is a Jordan–Hölder constituent of either

\mathcal{B}_{f}
or
\overline{\chi}_{E}\mathcal{B}_{f}
.

Let
q_{0}\mid D_{E}
be a rational prime with
q_{0}\neq p_{\mathfrak{P}}
.
Since
F
has full level,
the representation
\overline{\rho}_{F}^{ss}
is unramified at
q_{0}
.
The constituents of
\mathcal{B}_{f}
are unramified at
q_{0}
,
because
q_{0}\neq p_{\mathfrak{P}}
and
f
has level one.
On the other hand,
\overline{\chi}_{E}
is ramified at
q_{0}
.
Hence every constituent of
\overline{\chi}_{E}\mathcal{B}_{f}
is ramified at
q_{0}
.
Thus no constituent of
\overline{\chi}_{E}\mathcal{B}_{f}
can occur in
\overline{\rho}_{F}^{ss}
.

It follows that
\overline{\rho}_{F}^{ss}=\mathcal{B}_{f}
.
Therefore, for every prime
\ell\neq p_{\mathfrak{P}}
, we have

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{\ell}(X,f)(1-\ell^{k+j-1}X)(1-\ell^{k-2}X)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}.

It remains to treat the prime
\ell=p=p_{\mathfrak{P}}
.
The Hodge–Tate weights of the spin representation
\rho_{F}
are
\{-(2k+j-3),-(k+j-1),-(k-2),0\}
.
Since
p>2k+j-2
, these weights lie in the interval
[-(p-2),0]
.
Since
\rho_{F}
is crystalline in the Fontaine–Laffaille range,
the
p
-local
L
-polynomial is compatible with reduction modulo
\mathfrak{P}
.
Thus its reduction is computed from the residual representation by the action
of geometric Frobenius on inertia invariants:

L_{p}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv\det\left(1-X\mathrm{Frob}_{p}\mid(\overline{\rho}_{F}^{ss})^{I_{p}}\right)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}.

By the residual congruence already proved,

\overline{\rho}_{F}^{ss}=\mathcal{B}_{f}=\overline{\rho}_{f}+\overline{\omega}^{-k+2}+\overline{\omega}^{-k-j+1}.

Since
0<k-2<k+j-1<p-1
,
the characters
\overline{\omega}^{-k+2}
and
\overline{\omega}^{-k-j+1}
are nontrivial on
I_{p}
.
Thus

(\overline{\rho}_{F}^{ss})^{I_{p}}=\overline{\rho}_{f}^{I_{p}}.

Applying the same
p
-local calculation to
f
, we get

\det\left(1-X\mathrm{Frob}_{p}\mid\overline{\rho}_{f}^{I_{p}}\right)\equiv L_{p}(X,f)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}.

Hence

L_{p}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{p}(X,f)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}.

Finally,

(1-p^{k+j-1}X)(1-p^{k-2}X)\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{P}},

so

L_{p}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{p}(X,f)(1-p^{k+j-1}X)(1-p^{k-2}X)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}.

This proves the desired congruence at
\ell=p
.
∎

4.1.
Local conditions for Selmer groups

We review the local conditions used in the Selmer groups below.
For a topological group
G
and a continuous
G
-module
M
,
let
H^{1}(G,M)
be the first continuous cohomology
group of
G
with coefficients in
M
.
If
L
is a field,
we write
H^{1}(L,M)
for
H^{1}(\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{L}/L),M)
.

If
v\mid p
, let
E_{v,0}
be the maximal unramified subfield of
E_{v}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
v\mid p
, and let
V
be a finite-dimensional

K_{\mathfrak{P}}
-representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{E_{v}}
.
We put

D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)=\left(B_{\mathrm{cris}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V\right)^{\mathrm{Gal}_{E_{v}}},

where
B_{\mathrm{cris}}
is Fontaine’s crystalline period ring
[
13
]
.
We say that
V
is crystalline if

\dim_{E_{v,0}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}K_{\mathfrak{P}}}D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)=\dim_{K_{\mathfrak{P}}}V.

If
V
is crystalline,
then
D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=\left(B_{\mathrm{dR}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V\right)^{\mathrm{Gal}_{E_{v}}}

is identified with
E_{v}\otimes_{E_{v,0}}D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)
.
The decreasing filtration on
D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
gives the Hodge–Tate weights of
V
.
We use the convention

\mathrm{HT}(V)=\left\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\mathrm{Fil}^{-j}D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)/\mathrm{Fil}^{-j+1}D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\neq 0\right\}.

With this convention, the
p
-adic cyclotomic character
\omega_{p}
satisfies

\mathrm{HT}(\omega_{p})=\{1\}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
v\mid p
, and let
V
be a crystalline

K_{\mathfrak{P}}
-representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{E_{v}}
.
We say that
V
is short crystalline if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

\mathrm{Fil}^{0}D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
and
\mathrm{Fil}^{p}D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=0
.

(2)

For every non-zero quotient
V^{\prime}
of
V
, the representation

V^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p-1)
is ramified.

Condition
(1)
is equivalent to
\mathrm{HT}(V)\subset[-(p-1),0]
.
In particular, if
\mathrm{HT}(V)\subset[-(p-2),0]
, then
V
is short crystalline.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
K_{\mathfrak{P}}
-representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{E_{v}}
.
We define
H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},V)\subset H^{1}(E_{v},V)
by

H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},V)=\begin{cases}\ker\left(H^{1}(E_{v},V)\rightarrow H^{1}(I_{v},V)\right),&v\nmid p,\\
\ker\left(H^{1}(E_{v},V)\rightarrow H^{1}(E_{v},V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}B_{\mathrm{cris}})\right),&v\mid p,\end{cases}

where
I_{v}\subset\mathrm{Gal}_{E_{v}}
is the inertia subgroup.

Thus, for
v\nmid p
, the finite local condition is the unramified condition.
For
v\mid p
, it is the crystalline Bloch–Kato local condition

[
7
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
K_{\mathfrak{P}}
-representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{E}
,
and let
T\subset V
be a
\mathrm{Gal}_{E}
-stable
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
-lattice.
Put
A=V/T
.
For each finite place
v
of
E
, we define

H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},A)=\operatorname{Im}\left(H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},V)\rightarrow H^{1}(E_{v},A)\right).

The Bloch–Kato Selmer group of
A
is

H^{1}_{f}(E,A)=\ker\left(H^{1}(E,A)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v}H^{1}(E_{v},A)/H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},A)\right),

where
v
runs over all finite places of
E
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
K_{\mathfrak{P}}
-representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{E}
,
and let
T\subset V
be a
\mathrm{Gal}_{E}
-stable
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
-lattice.
Put

\overline{T}=T/\mathfrak{P}T
.
For each finite place
v
of
E
, define

H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},\overline{T})=\begin{cases}H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(E_{v},\overline{T}),&v\nmid p,\\
\operatorname{Im}\left(H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},T)\rightarrow H^{1}(E_{v},\overline{T})\right),&v\mid p,\end{cases}

where
H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},T)
is the inverse image of
H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},V)

under
H^{1}(E_{v},T)\rightarrow H^{1}(E_{v},V)
.
Thus the residual finite condition is unramified away from
p
, and is obtained
above
p
by imposing the Bloch–Kato condition before reducing modulo

\mathfrak{P}
.

More generally, if
M
is a finite-dimensional

\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
-representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{E}
endowed with local subgroups

H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},M)\subset H^{1}(E_{v},M)
for all finite places
v
, we put

H^{1}_{f}(E,M)=\ker\left(H^{1}(E,M)\rightarrow\prod_{v}H^{1}(E_{v},M)/H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},M)\right),

where
v
runs over all finite places of
E
.

Let
S_{E}
be the set of rational primes consisting of
p
and the primes
ramified in
E/\mathbb{Q}
.
We shall also use the
S_{E}
-imprimitive Selmer group, obtained by relaxing
the local conditions at the places above
D_{E}
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
M
be a finite-dimensional
\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
-representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{E}
.
For each finite place
v
of
E
, define

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(E_{v},M)=\begin{cases}H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(E_{v},M),&v\nmid pD_{E},\\
H^{1}_{f}(E_{v},M),&v\mid p,\\
H^{1}(E_{v},M),&v\nmid p\text{ and }v\mid D_{E}.\end{cases}

We define

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(E,M)=\ker\left(H^{1}(E,M)\rightarrow\prod_{v}H^{1}(E_{v},M)/H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(E_{v},M)\right).

Definition 4.7
.

Let
N
be a finite-dimensional
\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
-representation of
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Suppose that local finite subgroups
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N)\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N)
are fixed for all rational primes

, with the unramified condition at all but finitely many primes.
We define the Bloch–Kato Selmer group over
\mathbb{Q}
by

H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},N)=\ker\left(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},N)\rightarrow\prod_{q}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N)/H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N)\right).

For each rational prime
q
, define

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N)=\begin{cases}H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N),&q\nmid pD_{E},\\
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N),&q=p,\\
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N),&q\mid D_{E}.\end{cases}

We define

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\mathbb{Q},N)=\ker\left(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},N)\rightarrow\prod_{q}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N)/H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},N)\right).

A class
c\in H^{1}(L,M)
, with
L=E
or
\mathbb{Q}
, is said to satisfy the Bloch–Kato local conditions if

.
If
0\rightarrow S_{a}\rightarrow X\rightarrow S_{b}\rightarrow 0
is an exact sequence of
\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}[\mathrm{Gal}_{L}]
-modules,
it defines a class
c_{X}\in H^{1}(L,\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(S_{b},S_{a}))
.
We say that the exact sequence satisfies the Bloch–Kato local conditions if
c_{X}\in H^{1}_{f}(L,\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(S_{b},S_{a}))
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
L
be either
E
or
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
R\colon\mathrm{Gal}_{L}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V)
be a continuous representation over
K_{\mathfrak{P}}
,
and let
T\subset V
be a
\mathrm{Gal}_{L}
-stable
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
-lattice.
Put

\overline{T}=T\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}}\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}.

Assume the following conditions.

(1)

R
is absolutely irreducible.

(2)

R
is unramified outside the finite places of
L
above

pD_{E}
, and
R|_{\mathrm{Gal}_{L_{v}}}
is short crystalline for every

v\mid p
.

(3)

The residual semisimplification has the form

\overline{T}^{ss}=S_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus S_{r},

where the
S_{i}
are pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible

\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}[\mathrm{Gal}_{L}]
-modules and
r\geq 2
.

Then, after replacing
T
by another
\mathrm{Gal}_{L}
-stable lattice and taking
a residual subquotient, there exist distinct indices
a\neq b
and a non-split exact sequence

0\rightarrow S_{a}\rightarrow X\rightarrow S_{b}\rightarrow 0.

The corresponding extension class gives a non-zero element

0\neq c_{X}\in H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}\left(L,\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(S_{b},S_{a})\right).

Proof.

Since
R
is absolutely irreducible and
\overline{T}^{ss}
has at least two pairwise non-isomorphic constituents,
the lattice argument of
[
4
, Corollary 7.5]
gives,
after replacing
T
and taking a residual subquotient,
a non-split exact sequence

0\rightarrow S_{a}\rightarrow X\rightarrow S_{b}\rightarrow 0

for some
a\neq b
.
This exact sequence defines a non-zero class

0\neq c_{X}\in H^{1}\left(L,\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(S_{b},S_{a})\right).

We verify the local conditions.
If
v\nmid pD_{E}
, then
R
is unramified at
v
.
Hence the residual extension obtained from a stable lattice is unramified at
v
.
If
v\mid p
, then
R|_{\mathrm{Gal}_{L_{v}}}
is short crystalline, and
[
4
, Lemma 7.6]
, applied over
L_{v}
,
shows that

lies in the residual Bloch–Kato finite local condition.
If
v\mid D_{E}
, no local condition is imposed by definition.
Therefore

0\neq c_{X}\in H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}\left(L,\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(S_{b},S_{a})\right).

∎

Lemma 4.10

Proof.

Put

It suffices to show that every class

Since

is trivial on

We denote by
\widetilde{c}
the image of

in

H^{1}(L,\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}})^{\overline{\omega}^{-a}}

We first show that
\widetilde{c}
is unramified outside

At rational primes

, this follows from the definition of

Fix a rational prime

L
above

.
Since
q\neq p
, the extension

is unramified at

.
Suppose that
\widetilde{c}
is ramified at

Then there exists an element

in the tame inertia quotient at

Let

be a geometric Frobenius lift at

Then the tame relation is

\sigma x\sigma^{-1}=x^{q^{-1}}.

Applying
\widetilde{c}
, we get

\widetilde{c}(\sigma x\sigma^{-1})=q^{-1}b.

On the other hand, since
\widetilde{c}
is the restriction of the cocycle

the cocycle relation gives

Therefore

is unramified at every prime of

above

.

We next consider primes above

Let

be a prime of

The residual finite local condition at

Since

[

Since

.
Thus
\widetilde{c}
is unramified at every finite prime of

.

By global class field theory,
\widetilde{c}
defines an element of

where

is the

-primary part of the ideal class group of

.
By Herbrand’s theorem (see
[
15
]
,
[

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(C_{L}/pC_{L},\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}})^{\overline{\omega}^{-a}}=0.

.
Therefore

∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Put
h_{E}=\#\mathrm{Cl}(E)
, the class number of

.
Assume that
p\nmid h_{E}
and
\mathrm{Tame}(0)

Then

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\mathbb{Q},\overline{\chi}_{E})=0.

Proof.

Since

We put

H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\overline{\chi}_{E})=0.

Take a class

We denote by

the image of

in

By the local conditions defining

the character

above

and is trivial at the primes of

above

Hence, by global class field theory,

factors through a

.

Thus any

∎

Lemma 4.13

\widetilde{\epsilon}

Proposition 4.14
.

Let

be a primitive elliptic cusp form.
Let

be a number field containing the Hecke field of

be a prime of

above

We denote by

and choose a
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-stable
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
-lattice

For

.
Assume that
\bar{\rho}_{h\otimes\chi_{E}}

\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{\infty}})}

\mathfrak{P}\nmid L^{\mathrm{alg}}(2\nu-m,h\otimes\chi_{E})

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\mathbb{Q},\bar{T})=0.

Proof.

Put
\Sigma_{E}=\{q:q\mid D_{E}\}
.
Since
p\nmid D_{E}
, every prime
q\in\Sigma_{E}
satisfies

.

By the definition of the
S_{E}
-imprimitive Selmer structure, we have an exact sequence

Remark 4.15
.

and

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
V
be the underlying
\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
-vector space of

\overline{\rho}_{f}
, and put
\delta=\det(\overline{\rho}_{f})
.
Let

\langle\,,\,\rangle:V\times V\rightarrow{\wedge}^{2}V=\delta

be the determinant pairing.
Let

c\in H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}\left(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(V\cdot\overline{\chi}_{E},V)\right)

be represented by a cocycle
a
.
Assume that, for every
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
the bilinear form

B_{\sigma}:V\times V\rightarrow\delta,\qquad B_{\sigma}(x,y)=\langle a(\sigma)x,y\rangle

satisfies

B_{\sigma}(y,x)=-B_{\sigma}(x,y).

If
c\neq 0
, then

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\mathbb{Q},\overline{\chi}_{E})\neq 0.

Proof.

We follow the argument of
[
4
, Theorem 8.1]
.

Since every alternating bilinear form
V\times V\to\delta
is uniquely a scalar multiple of the determinant pairing,
for each
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
there is a unique element
\beta(\sigma)\in\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
such that

\langle a(\sigma)x,y\rangle=\beta(\sigma)\langle x,y\rangle\qquad(x,y\in V).

We show that
\beta
is a cocycle with values in
the one-dimensional representation
\overline{\chi}_{E}
.

Since
a
represents a class in

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(V\cdot\overline{\chi}_{E},V))
, it satisfies

a(\sigma\tau)=a(\sigma)+\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)a(\tau)\left(\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)\overline{\chi}_{E}(\sigma)\right)^{-1}

for
\sigma,\tau\in\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\langle a(\sigma\tau)x,y\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a(\sigma)x,y\rangle+\overline{\chi}_{E}(\sigma)^{-1}\left\langle\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)a(\tau)\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)^{-1}x,y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\beta(\sigma)\langle x,y\rangle+\overline{\chi}_{E}(\sigma)^{-1}\det(\overline{\rho}_{f})(\sigma)\left\langle a(\tau)\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)^{-1}x,\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)^{-1}y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\beta(\sigma)\langle x,y\rangle+\overline{\chi}_{E}(\sigma)^{-1}\det(\overline{\rho}_{f})(\sigma)\beta(\tau)\left\langle\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)^{-1}x,\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)^{-1}y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\beta(\sigma)+\overline{\chi}_{E}(\sigma)^{-1}\beta(\tau)\right)\langle x,y\rangle.

Since
\overline{\chi}_{E}^{-1}=\overline{\chi}_{E}
, we obtain

\beta(\sigma\tau)=\beta(\sigma)+\overline{\chi}_{E}(\sigma)\beta(\tau).

Thus
\beta
defines a class in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\overline{\chi}_{E})
.

The construction of
\beta
is functorial for restriction to local Galois
groups and for the finite local condition at primes above
p
.
At primes away from
pD_{E}
it preserves the unramified condition, and at primes
dividing
D_{E}
the
S_{E}
-imprimitive condition imposes no restriction.
Therefore the class of
\beta
belongs to
H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\mathbb{Q},\overline{\chi}_{E})
.

Finally, we show that the class of
\beta
is non-zero if
c\neq 0
.
Suppose that the class of
\beta
is zero in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\overline{\chi}_{E})
.
Then there exists
b\in\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
such that

\beta(\sigma)=\overline{\chi}_{E}(\sigma)b-b\qquad(\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}).

Put
u=b\cdot\mathrm{id}_{V}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(V\cdot\overline{\chi}_{E},V)
.
Since the scalar subspace of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(V\cdot\overline{\chi}_{E},V)
is isomorphic to
\overline{\chi}_{E}
, we have

\sigma u-u=\left(\overline{\chi}_{E}(\sigma)b-b\right)\mathrm{id}_{V}=\beta(\sigma)\mathrm{id}_{V}=a(\sigma).

Thus
a
is a coboundary.
Hence
c=0
, contradicting the assumption
c\neq 0
.
Therefore the class of
\beta
is non-zero.
This proves the lemma.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

If
G\equiv_{ev}[f]^{2}_{j+4}\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

\overline{R}_{G}^{\natural,ss}=\mathcal{B}_{f,E}.

Proof.

\ell\nmid pD_{E}
be a rational prime which splits in
E
,
and write
\ell=ww^{c}
.
Under the Satake

\mathrm{U}_{2,2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

P_{w}^{\natural}(X,G)\equiv P_{w}^{\natural}(X,[f]^{2}_{j+4})\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}.

\det\left(1-R_{G}^{\natural}(\mathrm{Frob}_{w})X\right)=P_{w}^{\natural}(X,G),

\mathfrak{P}
gives

\det\left(1-\overline{R}_{G}^{\natural,ss}(\mathrm{Frob}_{w})X\right)\equiv P_{w}^{\natural}(X,G)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}.

On the other hand, the explicit split Euler

\ell\nmid pD_{E}

,

, we have

\det\left(1-\overline{R}_{G}^{\natural,ss}(\mathrm{Frob}_{w})X\right)=\det\left(1-\mathcal{B}_{f,E}(\mathrm{Frob}_{w})X\right).

Let
L/E
be a finite Galois extension through which the two residual representations factor,
and let
L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension containing the Galois closure of
L/\mathbb{Q}
and
E
.
By the Chebotarev density theorem applied to
L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
,
Frobenius elements at places

of

lying above rational primes that split in
E
are sufficient to determine the semisimple characters on
\mathrm{Gal}(L/E)
.
Hence the equality of the characteristic polynomials at all such

implies

Therefore

\overline{R}_{G}^{\natural,ss}=\mathcal{B}_{f,E}.

∎

[
5
, Lemma 1.8.7]
.

Choose a
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-stable lattice for which
B
is integral and non-zero modulo
\mathfrak{P}
.
Passing to the residual subquotient generated by the above
V
-
W
extension and its paired dual,
and then quotienting by the radical of the induced residual pairing,
we may assume that
\overline{T}_{\rho}
carries a non-degenerate
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-equivariant pairing

(\,,\,)_{\overline{T}}\colon\overline{T}_{\rho}\times\overline{T}_{\rho}\rightarrow\overline{\Lambda},\qquad\overline{\Lambda}=\overline{\omega}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{-2k-j+3}\overline{\chi}_{E},

and that the
V
-
W
extension class remains non-zero.
Otherwise a non-zero off-diagonal component would remain only in one of the components already shown to vanish.

Put
\delta=\det(\overline{\rho}_{f})=\overline{\omega}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{-2k-j+3}
.
Then
\overline{\Lambda}=\delta\overline{\chi}_{E}
, and hence

\mathcal{B}_{f}^{\vee}\cdot\overline{\Lambda}=\mathcal{B}_{f}\cdot\overline{\chi}_{E}.

Thus the residual pairing pairs the two residual blocks
\mathcal{B}_{f}
and
\mathcal{B}_{f}\cdot\overline{\chi}_{E}
.
In particular it pairs
V
with
W
.

The restrictions of the residual pairing to
V\times V
and to
W\times W
vanish.
Indeed, a non-zero restriction would give an isomorphism
V\simeq W
of
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-representations,
contradicting the fact that
V
is unramified at
q
whereas
V\cdot\overline{\chi}_{E}
is ramified at
q
.
Hence
V
and
W
are totally isotropic,
and the cross pairing
V\times W\rightarrow\overline{\Lambda}
is perfect.

Choose a
\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
-linear splitting of

0\rightarrow V\rightarrow\overline{T}_{\rho}\rightarrow W\rightarrow 0

whose image of
W
is isotropic.
Such a splitting exists because
p>2
and the cross pairing is perfect.
With respect to this splitting, the action of
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
has the form

\sigma\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{V}(\sigma)&a(\sigma)\rho_{W}(\sigma)\\
0&\rho_{W}(\sigma)\end{pmatrix},

where
a\in Z^{1}(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathrm{Hom}_{\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}}(W,V))
represents the above non-zero Selmer class.

Identify the underlying vector space of
W=V\cdot\overline{\chi}_{E}
with
V
.
Then the cross pairing
V\times W\to\overline{\Lambda}
becomes a
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-equivariant pairing
V\times V\to\delta
.
By Schur’s lemma, it is a scalar multiple of the determinant pairing

\langle\,,\,\rangle\colon V\times V\rightarrow\delta.

Rescaling
(\,,\,)_{\overline{T}}
, we identify the cross pairing with
\langle\,,\,\rangle
.

We now use the sign
+1
self-duality just constructed.
By
[
5
, Proposition 1.8.10]
, the relevant off-diagonal extension class lies in the skew part.
In the present notation, this is also seen directly as follows.
Let
x,y\in W
, and let
\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y}\in\overline{T}_{\rho}
be their lifts with respect to the chosen isotropic splitting.
Since the image of
W
is isotropic, we have

(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})_{\overline{T}}=0.

By
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-equivariance, we also have

(\sigma\widetilde{x},\sigma\widetilde{y})_{\overline{T}}=0.

Writing
u=\rho_{W}(\sigma)x
,
v=\rho_{W}(\sigma)y
, and
A=a(\sigma)
, we have

\sigma\widetilde{x}=\widetilde{u}+Au,\qquad\sigma\widetilde{y}=\widetilde{v}+Av.

Expanding the equality
(\sigma\widetilde{x},\sigma\widetilde{y})_{\overline{T}}=0
,
and using the isotropy of
V
and
W
, gives

(\widetilde{u},Av)_{\overline{T}}+(Au,\widetilde{v})_{\overline{T}}=0.

Since the residual pairing is symmetric, this is equivalent to

\langle Au,v\rangle+\langle Av,u\rangle=0.

As
\rho_{W}(\sigma)
is invertible, we obtain

\langle a(\sigma)x,y\rangle+\langle a(\sigma)y,x\rangle=0.

Thus the class represented by
a
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
4.17
.

Since this class is non-zero, Lemma
4.17
gives

H^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\mathbb{Q},\overline{\chi}_{E})\neq 0,

contradicting the vanishing proved above.
Hence
R_{G}^{\natural}
is conjugate invariant.

By the Chebotarev density theorem,
the conjugate invariance of
R_{G}^{\natural}
implies that
\Pi_{G}^{c}
and
\Pi_{G}
have the same unramified Frobenius characteristic polynomials
at almost all places of
E
.
Hence

\Pi_{G}^{c}\simeq\Pi_{G}.

By cyclic base change for the quadratic extension
E/\mathbb{Q}

[
1
]
,
together with (
2
) and Lemma
3.5
,
there exists a cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
,
unramified at every finite place, whose base change to
E
is
\Pi_{G}
.

Proposition
3.4
then gives a Hecke cusp eigenform

F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})

such that
G=\mathrm{Lift}_{E}(F)
.
∎

We now combine the ingredients used above.
Proposition
3.6
gives the Hermitian cusp form
G
congruent to the Klingen–Eisenstein lift.
By Proposition
5.6
and Remark
5.1
,
the global Arthur parameter of
G
is simple.
Proposition
5.6
and Theorem
5.7
,
together with Lemma
3.5
and Proposition
3.4
, then give the descent to a Siegel eigenform.
Thus we obtain the following conditional form of the Harder-type congruence.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
k\geq 4
and
j\geq 2
be integers with
j
even.
Let
f
be a primitive elliptic cusp form of weight
2k+j-2
.
Choose a sufficiently large number field
K
containing
\mathbb{Q}(f)
and the Hecke fields of all Hecke eigenforms in
\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})
.
Assume that there exist a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{Q}(f)
, an imaginary quadratic field
E
,
and a prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
K
lying above
\mathfrak{p}
, such that the following conditions are satisfied.

(1)

The rational prime
p=p_{\mathfrak{p}}
satisfies
p>2k+j-2
.

(2)

The class number of
E
is one, and
E/\mathbb{Q}
has a unique ramified rational prime
q
, with
q\neq p
.

(3)

f
is not congruent modulo
\mathfrak{p}
to any other Hecke cusp eigenform of weight
2k+j-2
.

(4)

\mathfrak{p}
divides
L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j,f)
.

(5)

There exists
S_{0}\in\mathrm{Her}_{2}(E)_{>0}
such that

v_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left(L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j,f)\right)=-v_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left(a(S_{0};[f]^{2}_{j+4})\right).

(6)

\zeta(-j-1)L(-j,\chi_{E})
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-unit.

(7)

After a suitable choice of a lattice, the image of
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{\infty}})}
under the Galois representation over
K_{\mathfrak{P}}
attached to
f
contains
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}})
.

(8)

We have
q^{j+2}\not\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(9)

\mathfrak{P}
divides none of
L(0,\widetilde{\omega}^{j+1})
,
L(0,\chi_{E}\widetilde{\omega}^{j+2})
, and
L(0,\chi_{E}\widetilde{\omega}^{-j})
.

(10)

\mathfrak{p}
divides neither
L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j,f\otimes\chi_{E})
nor
L^{\mathrm{alg}}(k+j-1,f\otimes\chi_{E})
.

Then there exists a Hecke cusp eigenform
F\in\mathcal{S}_{{\det}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{j}}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2})
such that

L_{\ell}(X,F,\mathrm{Spin})\equiv L_{\ell}(X,f)(1-\ell^{k+j-1}X)(1-\ell^{k-2}X)\pmod{\mathfrak{P}}

for every rational prime
\ell
.

Remark 5.9
.

Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character of conductor prime to
p
,
and let
\widetilde{\omega}
be the Teichmüller lift of the mod
\mathfrak{P}
cyclotomic character.
By the generalized Kummer congruences, for
1\leq m\leq p-2
,
the value
L(0,\chi\widetilde{\omega}^{m})
is a
\mathfrak{P}
-adic unit
if and only if
L(-m,\chi)
is a
\mathfrak{P}
-adic unit.

In the present situation, the non-divisibility conditions involving

L(0,\widetilde{\omega}^{j+1})
,

L(0,\chi_{E}\widetilde{\omega}^{j+2})
, and

L(0,\chi_{E}\widetilde{\omega}^{-j})
may also be checked
by using
\zeta(-j-1)
,
L(-j-2,\chi_{E})
,
L(j+1-p,\chi_{E})
, respectively.

6.
Numerical examples

Before turning to the examples, we record practical criteria for verifying that,
after a suitable choice of a lattice,
the image of
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{\infty}})}
under the Galois representation attached to
f
contains
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}})
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
h\in S_{2\nu}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
be a primitive elliptic cusp form of level one.
Put
K_{h}=\mathbb{Q}(h)
.
Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime ideal of
K_{h}
, and let
p\geq 7
be the rational prime below
\mathfrak{P}
.
Let
\overline{\omega}
be the mod
\mathfrak{P}
cyclotomic character.
We denote by
\rho_{h}\colon\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K_{h,\mathfrak{P}})
the
p
-adic Galois representation attached to
h
,
and by
\overline{\rho}_{h,\mathfrak{P}}\colon\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}})
its residual representation.

For a rational prime
\ell\neq p
, put

\displaystyle t_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\overline{a(\ell;h)}\in\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}},

\displaystyle x_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\overline{a(\ell;h)^{2}\ell^{1-2\nu}}\in\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}},

\displaystyle y_{\ell}

\displaystyle=a(\ell;h)^{2}\ell^{1-2\nu}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}.

Assume the following conditions:

(1)

For every
i\in\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a rational prime

\ell\neq p
such that

t_{\ell}\neq\ell^{i}+\ell^{2\nu-1-i}.

(2)

There exists a rational prime
\ell\neq p
such that

\left(\frac{\ell}{p}\right)=-1\quad\text{and}\quad t_{\ell}\neq 0.

(3)

There exists a rational prime
q\neq p
such that

x_{q}\notin\{0,1,2,4\},\quad x_{q}^{2}-3x_{q}+1\neq 0.

(4)

For some finite set
S
of rational primes not containing
p
, we have

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[y_{\ell}\mid\ell\in S].

Then, after a suitable choice of a
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-stable

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
-lattice in the representation space of
\rho_{h}
, we have

\rho_{h}\bigl(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{\infty}})}\bigr)\supset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}).

Proof.

Put
\overline{G}=\overline{\rho}_{h,\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}})\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}})
, and let
\overline{G}^{\mathrm{proj}}
be its image in
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}})
.
Since
h
has level one,
\overline{\rho}_{h,\mathfrak{P}}
is unramified outside
p
,
and its determinant is the appropriate power of the mod
p
cyclotomic character.
If
\overline{\rho}_{h,\mathfrak{P}}
were reducible, then its semisimplification would be a sum of two characters unramified outside
p
.
Since we use geometric Frobenius and
\overline{\omega}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell})=\ell^{-1}
, we may write

\overline{\rho}_{h,\mathfrak{P}}^{ss}=\overline{\omega}^{-i}+\overline{\omega}^{\,i-(2\nu-1)}

for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence

t_{\ell}=\ell^{i}+\ell^{2\nu-1-i}

for every
\ell\neq p
, contradicting condition
(1)
.
Thus

\overline{\rho}_{h,\mathfrak{P}}
is absolutely irreducible.

The dihedral case is also excluded by condition
(2)
.
Indeed, if

\overline{G}^{\mathrm{proj}}
were contained in the normalizer of a Cartan subgroup
but not in the Cartan subgroup itself, then there would be a quadratic character
\eta

such that
t_{\ell}=0
whenever
\eta(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell})=-1
.
Since the representation is unramified outside
p
,
the corresponding quadratic character is the unique quadratic character of
conductor
p
, namely
\ell\mapsto(\ell/p)
on primes
\ell\neq p
.
This contradicts the existence of a nonsquare
\ell
with
t_{\ell}\neq 0
.

It remains to exclude the exceptional projective images.
If
\alpha_{\ell},\beta_{\ell}
are the eigenvalues of
\overline{\rho}_{h,\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell})
,
then

x_{\ell}=\frac{(\alpha_{\ell}+\beta_{\ell})^{2}}{\alpha_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}}=r_{\ell}+r_{\ell}^{-1}+2,\qquad r_{\ell}=\alpha_{\ell}/\beta_{\ell}.

Thus
x_{\ell}=4,0,1,2
when the projective order of

\overline{\rho}_{h,\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell})
is
1,2,3,4
, respectively,
and
x_{\ell}^{2}-3x_{\ell}+1=0
when the projective order is
5
.
Since every element of
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
, and
A_{5}
has order at most
5
, condition
(3)
excludes the exceptional cases.
By the subgroup classification used in
[
26
, Section 2]
,
it follows that
\overline{G}^{\mathrm{proj}}
contains
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(k_{0})
for some subfield
k_{0}\subset\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
.

The invariant
(\operatorname{tr}g)^{2}/\det g
is projective.
Hence, if the projective image were defined over a proper subfield
k_{0}\subsetneq\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
, then all
x_{\ell}
would lie in
k_{0}
.
But condition
(4)
, after reduction modulo
\mathfrak{P}
, gives

\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}(x_{\ell}\mid\ell\in S).

Therefore
k_{0}=\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}
, and

\overline{G}^{\mathrm{proj}}\supset\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{P}}).

In particular, the residual image is irreducible and has order divisible by
p
.

Since
h
has level one and trivial character, it has no non-trivial inner twists.
Together with condition
(4)
, this says that the
p
-adic trace algebra in the sense of Ribet is the full ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
.
Hence
[
27
, Theorem 3.1]
applies.
After replacing the lattice if necessary, it gives

\rho_{h}(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}})\supset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}),

since
p\geq 7
.

Finally, the commutator subgroup of
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is contained in
\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{\infty}})}
,
because the
p
-adic cyclotomic character is abelian.
Since
p\geq 7
, the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}})
is topologically perfect.
Hence the above inclusion implies

\rho_{h}\bigl(\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{\infty}})}\bigr)\supset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}),

as desired.
∎
