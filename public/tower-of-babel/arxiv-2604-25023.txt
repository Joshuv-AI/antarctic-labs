The Mukai conjecture via Cox rings for special toric ambient embeddings
Heath Pearson

Abstract
. We prove the Mukai conjecture on the characterisation of products of projective spaces among Fano varieties for a class of locally factorial Fano varieties defined in terms of their Cox rings. The Fano varieties of this class are characterised in terms of the property that they admit an embedding into a smooth projective toric variety via the bunched ring theory of Mori dream spaces. Our approach inherits the Mukai conjecture for this class from a log version of the Mukai conjecture on the toric ambient embedding.

1.
Introduction

The Mukai conjecture proposes a numerical characterisation of powers of projective spaces among the smooth Fano varieties, and it has been researched extensively, including recent progress from various perspectives (see for example
[
14
,
4
,
1
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
8
,
11
]
), yet it remains an open question in general.

Conjecture 1.1

(The Mukai conjecture
[
12
, Conjecture 4]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano variety of dimension
n
, Picard number
\rho_{X}\coloneq\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Pic}(X)
, and Fano index
i_{X}\coloneq\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid kH=[-K_{X}],\,H\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)\}
, then

(i_{X}-1)\rho_{X}\leq n,

with
(i_{X}-1)\rho_{X}=n
if and only if
X\cong(\mathbb{P}^{i_{X}-1})^{\rho_{X}}
.

1.1
has been proved in several special cases, even in the setting of non-smooth Fano varieties with controlled singularities. For example,
1.1
is proved for the toric
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Gorenstein Fano varieties by C. Casagrande in
[
6
]
. Furthermore, the most general version of the Mukai conjecture stated is the
generalised Mukai conjecture for log Fano pairs
, proposed by K. Fujita in
[
10
]
where it is proved for the toric log Fano pairs. In this more general conjecture, smooth Fano varieties are replaced with not-necessarily
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial log Fano pairs
(Z,\Delta)
, and the Fano index is exchanged for the
log pseudo-index

\iota_{(Z,\Delta)}\coloneq\min\{([-(K_{Z}+\Delta)]\cdot\gamma)\mid\gamma\text{ is a rational curve in }Z\}.

In this paper we aim to build from these approaches
[
6
,
10
]
by extending their ideas beyond the class of toric varieties. Here we prove
1.1
for a class of locally factorial Fano varieties
X
which embed into a smooth projective toric variety
Z
via their Cox ring. In this approach we look at a discrete approximation of
\iota_{(Z,\Delta)}
defined when
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is a Cartier divisor

i_{(Z,\Delta)}\coloneq\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid kH=[-(K_{Z}+\Delta)],\,H\in\operatorname{Pic}(Z)\}\leq\iota_{(Z,\Delta)}.

Since smooth Fano varieties are Mori dream spaces
[
3
]
, we use the theory of
[
2
, Section 3]
(introduced here in
Section

2
) which embeds a Mori dream space
X
with specified Cox ring into a toric variety
Z
. Furthermore, in this construction, when
X
is Fano we get a canonical closed embedding
X\hookrightarrow Z
into a projective toric variety. This embedding has the property that
\operatorname{Cl}(X)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
and under this isomorphism, in
1.2
we introduce a class of Fano varieties with
[-K_{X}]=[-(K_{Z}+\Delta)]
, so
i_{X}=i_{(Z,\Delta)}
. Our approach is motivated by the idea of proving the Mukai conjecture for
X
by inheriting it from the log version of the Mukai conjecture for
Z
.

Construction 1.2
.

We now define the class of Fano varieties studied in this paper. There are two steps to their construction.

(1)

Let
Z
be a smooth toric variety, and
\Delta
a torus invariant
\mathbb{Z}
-Weil divisor such that
-(K_{Z}+\Delta)
is ample. Let its
\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
-graded Cox ring be
\mathrm{Cox}(Z)\cong\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]
.

(2)

Let
R\coloneq\mathrm{Cox}(Z)/\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\rangle
be a factorial complete intersection ring such that
\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\rangle
is a
\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
-homogeneous ideal, and
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg g_{j}=[\Delta]
. We require that the degrees of the relations are not ‘relatively small’ in the sense that for
j=1,\dots,r
,

\deg(g_{j})\notin\operatorname{conv}(0,\{\deg(T_{i})\mid i=1,\dots,m\})\setminus\operatorname{conv}\{\deg(T_{i})\mid i=1,\dots,m\}.

We also require that the group of homogeneous invertible elements of
R
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. By following
1.2
, there is up to isomorphism a unique locally factorial Fano variety
X
with graded Cox ring
\mathrm{Cox}(X)=R
.

Theorem 1.3
.

The Mukai conjecture holds for any Fano variety from
1.2
.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In
Section

2
, we introduce the background on Mori dream spaces which we require. Next, in
Section

3
we prove
1.3
. Among the steps of this proof, in
Proposition

3.2
we bound the Fano index of
X
using the convex geometry of the moment polytope of
(Z,-(K_{Z}+\Delta))
, and in
Proposition

3.3
we find that
i_{(Z,\Delta)}\rho_{Z}\leq\sum_{D}a_{D}
is bounded by the sum of coefficients of a certain decomposition
\sum_{D}a_{D}[D]=[-K_{X}]
. For example, in the toric case with
X=Z
and
\Delta=0
, the decomposition obtained here is the decomposition of the standard toric anticanonical divisor into all the
T
-invariant prime divisors, giving
\sum_{D}a_{D}=\dim Z+\rho_{Z}
, reproving the toric Mukai conjecture. In
Proposition

3.1
we use our assumptions on the Cox ring in
1.2
to bound the sum of coefficients appearing in such a decomposition of
[-K_{X}]
by
n+\rho_{X}
. Finally, in
Proposition

3.4
we classify the boundary case of the Mukai conjecture for our varieties.

Remark 1.4
.

We may rewrite our findings in the following way

n+\rho_{X}-i_{X}\rho_{X}\geq n+\rho_{X}-\sum_{D}a_{D}=\gamma(X,-K_{X})\geq 0,

which is the
complexity
\gamma
of a decomposition of a log pair
. This is roughly a quantity measuring how far certain log pairs are from a toric pair (defined in
[
5
]
). We remark that the appearance of such a quantity has recently begun to emerge concretely in other special cases of the Mukai conjecture: in the case that the nef cone equals the effective cone and is a simplicial cone
[
8
]
, and in the generalised Mukai conjecture for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial spherical varieties
[
11
]
.

In this paper we work over the field of complex numbers.

2.
Background

We begin by introducing the elements of the theory of Cox rings used in this paper. For a detailed account please refer to
[
2
]
, which acts as the reference for this section.

Let
X
be an irreducible, normal variety with finitely generated class group
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
. Then the
Cox ring
of
X
is the
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
-graded ring

\mathrm{Cox}(X)\coloneq\bigoplus_{[D]\in\operatorname{Cl}(X)}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(D)),

whose definition is made precise in
[
2
]
. If we have further that
\mathrm{Cox}(X)
is finitely generated and
X
is projective, then
X
is called a
Mori dream space
. In this case, we can fix a system of pairwise non-associated
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
-prime generators of
\mathrm{Cox}(X)
to obtain a presentation

\mathrm{Cox}(X)=\frac{\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]}{I}.

It is an important fact that passing to the
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
-graded polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]
gives us the graded Cox ring of a toric variety, and that there are finitely many toric varieties sharing this graded polynomial Cox ring. Furthermore, each Mori dream space
X
sharing the Cox ring
\mathrm{Cox}(X)
admits a canonical embedding into such a toric variety
Z
, with
\operatorname{Cl}(X)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
, and moreover the isomorphism class of
X
is determined by the data of its graded Cox ring presentation and its canonical toric ambient embedding.

We now introduce this theory. We assume the reader is familiar with the well-known characterisation of toric varieties in terms of lattice fans. Firstly, let us fix some notation. For a
d
-dimensional toric variety
Z
, let
N\coloneq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{*},(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d})
, and let
\Sigma
be its fan in
N_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathbb{Q}^{d}
. For any
k=1,\dots,d
write
\Sigma^{(k)}
for the set of
k
-dimensional cones in
\Sigma
, for example
\Sigma^{(1)}
is the set of rays of
\Sigma
. Next, for any subset of cones
S\subseteq\Sigma
, we write
\rho(S)
for the set of primitive ray generators of the rays of the cones in
S
. We always assume that
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma)=N_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

To begin, consider the following exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules, where
m=d+\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
is the number of torus invariant prime divisors
\{D_{1}^{Z},\dots,D_{m}^{Z}\}
of
Z
,

Let
\{e_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,m\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, and let the corresponding dual basis of
(\mathbb{Z}^{m})^{*}
be
\{e_{i}^{*}\mid i=1,\dots,m\}
. Here, the
i
th coordinate corresponds to the
i
th torus invariant prime divisor
D_{i}^{Z}
in the following way: we have
P\colon\mathbb{Z}^{m}\to M
is a choice of toric generator matrix, with
P(e_{i})
the coordinate of the
i
th ray of
\Sigma
, and
K\cong\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
, with
Q(e_{i}^{*})
equal to the divisor class of

. We say the vector configuration

. For shorthand, if

we write

for its

We now introduce the Gale dual version of a lattice fan. Since each
\sigma\in\Sigma
is of the form
\sigma=\operatorname{cone}(P(e_{i})\mid i\in I(\sigma))
for some

, we may define the multi-set

\sigma^{\downarrow}\coloneq(Q(e_{i}^{*})\mid i\notin I(\sigma)),

and the set
\Sigma^{\downarrow}\coloneq\{\sigma^{\downarrow}\mid\sigma\in\Sigma\}
. Now
\mathrm{Cox}(Z)=\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]
is graded by
\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
with
\deg(T_{i})=Q(e_{i}^{*})
for
i=1,\dots,m
, and
(\mathrm{Cox}(Z),\Sigma^{\downarrow})
is called the
bunched polynomial ring
of
Z
, from which we can recover the fan
\Sigma
determining the isomorphism class of
Z
.

We now describe the Mori dream spaces
X
which are Fano in terms of
bunched rings
. Suppose
X
has Cox ring presentation
\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]/I
. Then the movable cone of
X
is

\mathrm{Mov}(R)\coloneq\bigcap_{j\in\{1,\dots,m\}}\operatorname{cone}(Q(e_{i}^{*})\mid i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\setminus\{j\})\subset K_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Since
X
is Fano, it is polarised by
\mathcal{L}\coloneq[-K_{X}]\in\mathrm{Mov}(R)^{\circ}\cap\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. From this, the isomorphism class of
X
is determined by the
bunched ring

(\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]/I,\Phi(\mathcal{L}))
, where

\Phi(\mathcal{L})\coloneq\left\{\tau_{J}\coloneq(Q(e_{j}^{*})\mid j\in J)\,\Big|\,\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{cone}(\tau_{J}),\,\,\prod_{j\in J}T_{j}\notin\mathrm{rad}\langle\prod_{j\notin J}T_{j}\rangle\right\}.

From this data we may also canonically obtain the bunched polynomial ring of a toric variety
Z
(as in
[
2
, Construction 3.2.5.7]
) as
(\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}],\Sigma(\mathcal{L})^{\downarrow})
, where

\Sigma(\mathcal{L})^{\downarrow}\coloneq\left\{\tau_{J}\coloneq(Q(e_{j}^{*})\mid j\in J)\mid\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{cone}(\tau_{J})\right\}\supseteq\Phi(\mathcal{L}).

We now state some important facts arising from this construction. Firstly, there is a closed embedding
X\hookrightarrow Z
, called the
canonical toric ambient embedding
of
(\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]/I,\Phi(\mathcal{L}))
.

Remark 2.1
.

Remark that
Z
is a projective toric variety. In the non-Fano setting while there remains a canonical toric ambient embedding (as in
[
2

), it is not necessarily projective, yet it always admits a possibly non-canonical projective completion. Furthermore, the theory of bunched rings can be extended to classify not necessarily complete varieties with finitely generated Cox ring (see
[

There is a finite set of prime divisors
\{D_{1},\dots,D_{m}\}
in
X
such that
D_{i}^{Z}\cap X=D_{i}
for each
i=1,\dots,m
. These divisors give us an isomorphism
\operatorname{Cl}(Z)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(X)

[

Next, we have an explicit description of the Picard group

\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\bigcap_{\tau\in\Phi(\mathcal{L})}\langle\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}},

where

\mathrm{Cox}(X)=\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]/I

[-K_{X}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg(T_{i})-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(g_{j}).

Next,
X
is locally factorial if and only if for all
\tau\in\Phi(\mathcal{L})
, we have that
\langle\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
generates the torsion free part of
K

[
2
, Corollary 3.3.1.8]
. Finally, we end by collecting some basic quantities associated to
X
.

•

\rho_{X}=\rho_{Z}=\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
,

•

m
is the number of generators of
\mathrm{Cox}(X)
,

•

r
is the number of relations of the defining ideal
\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\rangle
of
\mathrm{Cox}(X)
,

•

n+\rho_{X}=m-r
,

•

d=m-\rho_{Z}
is the dimension of the canonical ambient toric variety
Z
.

We now recall some well-known elements of the theory of toric varieties (for a reference see

7
]
). Let
(Z,E)
be a polarised toric variety, where

is the following compact intersection of half-spaces

\mathcal{P}(Z,E)\coloneq\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\{y\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\langle P(e_{i}),y\rangle+a_{i}\geq 0\},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon N\times M\to\mathbb{Z}
is the dual pairing. This polytope has normal fan
\Sigma
. Let the vertices of
\mathcal{P}(Z,E)
be
\{C_{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}\}
, which we call the
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier data
of

; and which satisfy
\langle P(e_{i}),C_{\sigma}\rangle+a_{i}\geq 0
, with
\langle P(e_{i}),C_{\sigma}\rangle+a_{i}=0
if and only if
P(e_{i})\in\sigma
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The varieties obtained in
1.2
are Fano varieties with locally factorial singularities.

Proof.

Since
Z
is smooth and complete,
\operatorname{Cl}(Z)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\rho_{X}}
is free, so by
[
2
, Theorem 3.4.1.11]

R
being factorial implies the required conditions in
[
2
, Definition 3.2.1.2]
to define a bunched ring. The varieties of
1.2
have bunched ring
(\mathrm{Cox}(X),\Phi(\mathcal{L}))
with
\mathcal{L}=[-(K_{Z}+\Delta)]
. Under the isomorphism
\operatorname{Cl}(Z)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(X)
we may consider

\mathcal{L}=[-K_{Z}]-[\Delta]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg(T_{i})-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(g_{j})=[-K_{X}],

so
X
is a Fano variety. In our construction,
Z
has bunched ring
(\mathrm{Cox}(Z),\Sigma(\mathcal{L})^{\downarrow})
. Since
Z
is smooth, we have
\langle\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}=\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
for all
\tau\in\Sigma(\mathcal{L})^{\downarrow}
. As
\Phi(\mathcal{L})\subseteq\Sigma(\mathcal{L})^{\downarrow}
, we obtain that
Z
is locally factorial.

3.
The Mukai conjecture

In this section we prove
1.3
. All results in this section are new. We begin by selecting a
\mathbb{Q}
-representative of the anticanonical class in terms of the Cox ring divisors
\{D_{i}\coloneq D_{i}^{Z}\cap X\mid i=1,\dots,m\}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be as in
1.2
, then there exists an anticanonical representative of the form
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}E\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}D_{i}>0
with
0<a_{i}\leq 1
for all
i=1,\dots,m
and

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\leq n+\rho_{X}.

Proof.

We examine the points in
A\coloneq\{y\in(\mathbb{Q}^{m})^{*}\mid Q(y)=[-K_{X}]\}
. Firstly, since

[-K_{X}]=[-K_{Z}]-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(g_{j}),

and since
\operatorname{Nef}(X)^{\circ}\subseteq\operatorname{Eff}(X)^{\circ}=Q((\mathbb{Q}^{m})^{*}_{>0})
, we have

[-K_{X}]\in\operatorname{Eff}(X)^{\circ}\cap([-K_{Z}]-\operatorname{Eff}(X))=Q((0,1]^{m}).

So there exists an anticanonical representative of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}D_{i}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-K_{X}
with
0<a_{i}\leq 1
for all
i=1,\dots,m
, which we construct by selecting
y=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}e_{i}^{*}
with
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in(0,1]^{m}
, and
Q(y)=Q(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}e_{i}^{*})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\deg(T_{i})
, and then taking the representatives
D_{i}\in\deg(T_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,m
.

We now show it is possible to choose an anticanonical representative of the above form with a small coefficient sum. Firstly, every point in
A
may be written as

y=(1,\dots,1)-(y_{g_{1}}+\dots+y_{g_{r}}),

where
Q(y_{g_{j}})=\deg(g_{j})
for
j=1,\dots,r
. We now consider the function
h\colon(\mathbb{Q}^{m})^{*}\to\mathbb{Q}
defined by
h(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})\coloneq\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m}
, and we show that for each
\ell=1,\dots,r
, we can find a representative
y_{g_{\ell}}
such that
h(y_{g_{\ell}})\geq 1
. We have that

[-K_{Z}]-\deg(g_{\ell})\in[-K_{Z}]-\operatorname{Eff}(X),

and

[-K_{Z}]-\deg(g_{\ell})=[-K_{X}]+\sum_{j=1,\,j\neq\ell}^{m}\deg(g_{j})\in\operatorname{Nef}(X)^{\circ}+\operatorname{Eff}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Eff}(X)^{\circ}.

Therefore
[-K_{Z}]-\deg(g_{\ell})\in\operatorname{Eff}(X)^{\circ}\cap([-K_{Z}]-\operatorname{Eff}(X))=Q((0,1]^{m})
, so there is a representative of
[-K_{Z}]-\deg(g_{\ell})
of the form
(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{m})\in(0,1]^{m}
. Now we choose the representative
(1,\dots,1)
for
[-K_{Z}]
, which uniquely determines a representative
y_{g_{\ell}}\in Q^{-1}(\deg(g_{\ell}))
, and it satisfies

(1,\dots,1)-y_{g_{j}}=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{m}),

that is
y_{g_{j}}=(1-\eta_{1},\dots,1-\eta_{m})\in[0,1)^{m}
. Next, suppose that
h(y_{g_{\ell}})<1
, then

y_{g_{\ell}}\in\{x\in(\mathbb{Q}^{m})^{*}_{\geq 0}\mid h(x)<1\}=\operatorname{conv}(\{0\}\cup\{e_{i}^{*}\mid i=1,\dots,m\})\setminus\operatorname{conv}\{e_{i}^{*}\mid i=1,\dots,m\}.

Therefore

Q(y_{g_{\ell}})=\deg(g_{\ell})\in\operatorname{conv}(0,\{\deg(T_{i})\mid i=1,\dots,m\})\setminus\operatorname{conv}\{\deg(T_{i})\mid i=1,\dots,m\},

which contradicts our assumption from
1.2
that the degrees of the relations are ‘relatively small’. Therefore

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}=h(y)=h(1,\dots,1)-(h(y_{g_{1}})+\dots+h(y_{g_{r}}))\leq m-r=n+\rho_{X}.\qed

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be as in
1.2
. For any anticanonical representative of the form
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}E=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}D_{i}>0
with
0<a_{i}\leq 1
for all
i=1,\dots,m
, as constructed in
Proposition

3.1
(which we may write in the equivalent notation
E=\sum_{v\in\rho(\Sigma)}a_{v}D_{v}
), we have for all
\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}
and all
v\in\rho(\Sigma)\setminus\rho(\mathfrak{\sigma})
,

i_{X}\leq\langle v,C_{\sigma}\rangle+a_{v},

where
C_{\sigma}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier data of
E
on
\sigma
.

Proof.

Since
Z
is smooth, we have
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\operatorname{Cl}(X)=\operatorname{Cl}(Z)=\operatorname{Pic}(Z)=\langle\tau\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
\tau\in\Phi([-(K_{Z}+\Delta)])
. Therefore
[-K_{X}]
may be viewed as a Cartier class on
Z
, and
E
as a torus invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Z
.

We now apply a weighted homogenisation operation to the rays of
\Sigma
. It is defined by sending
v\mapsto\overline{v}\coloneq(v,a_{v})
for all
v\in\rho(\Sigma)
, and incorporating a new vector
((0,\dots,0),1)
. That is, we consider the vector configuration

\mathcal{V}\coloneq\{\overline{v}\mid v\in\rho(\Sigma)\}\cup\{((0,\dots,0),1)\}\subset N\times\mathbb{Q}.

Notice that the following vector configuration is Gale dual to
\mathcal{V}
.

\mathcal{V}^{\downarrow}=\{v^{\downarrow}\mid v\in\rho(\Sigma)\}\cup\{-\sum_{v\in\rho(\Sigma)}a_{v}v^{\downarrow}=[K_{X}]\}\subset K,

with
\overline{v}^{\downarrow}=v^{\downarrow}
for
v\in\rho(\Sigma)
, and
((0,\dots,0),1)^{\downarrow}=[K_{X}]
.

Since
E
is a torus invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, it has a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier data, which we write as
\{C_{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}\}
. These vectors satisfy
\langle v,C_{\sigma}\rangle+a_{v}=0
for each
v\in\rho(\sigma)
, and since
[-K_{X}]
is ample on
Z
, we have further that this
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier data equals the vertices of the moment polytope
\mathcal{P}\coloneq\mathcal{P}(Z,E)
. Next, we look at the polar dual of
\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{P}^{*}\coloneq\bigcap_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}\{x\in N_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\langle x,C_{\sigma}\rangle+1\geq 0\},

which has set of vertices
\{v/a_{v}\mid v\in\rho(\Sigma)\}
, and which is defined as
a_{v}>0
for all
v\in\rho(\Sigma)
. We now consider the cone over this polytope

\mathcal{C}\coloneq\operatorname{cone}\{(v/a_{v},1)\mid v\in\rho(\Sigma)\}=\operatorname{cone}\{\overline{v}\mid v\in\rho(\Sigma)\}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\mathbb{Q}.

We have that

\mathcal{C}=\bigcap_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}\{(x,y)\in N_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\mathbb{Q}\mid\langle(x,y),(C_{\sigma},1)\rangle\geq 0\}

has its facets cut out by the inner normal vectors
\{(\mathcal{C}_{\sigma},1)\mid\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}\}
.
Therefore for each
\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}
, the linear form
(C_{\sigma},1)\in\operatorname{Hom}(M_{\mathbb{Q}}\oplus\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Q})
is non-negative on
\mathcal{C}
, and on
\mathcal{V}
it attains the values
(C_{\sigma},1)((0,\dots,0),1)=1
, and
(C_{\sigma},1)(\overline{v})=\langle v,C_{\sigma}\rangle+a_{v}>0
for
v\in\rho(\Sigma)\setminus\rho(\sigma)
, and
(C_{\sigma},1)(\overline{w})=0
for each
w\in\rho(\sigma)
. Therefore by Gale duality, this linear form induces the following linear relation in
\mathcal{V}^{\downarrow}
,

[K_{X}]+\sum_{v\in\rho(\Sigma)\setminus\rho(\sigma)}(\langle v,C_{\sigma}\rangle+a_{v})v^{\downarrow}=0.

We now construct a linear form which extracts coefficients from equation (
3
). Fix any
v\in\rho(\Sigma)\setminus\rho(\sigma)
, then since
\sigma
is full dimensional, we can find a linear relation

v+\sum_{w\in\rho(\sigma)}\lambda_{w}w=0,\quad\lambda_{w}\in\mathbb{Q}.

By Gale duality on the rays of
\Sigma
, the relation above induces a linear form
\ell_{\sigma,v}\in L_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\ell_{\sigma,v}(v^{\downarrow})=1
, and
\ell_{\sigma,v}(w^{\downarrow})=\lambda_{w}
for
w\in\rho(\sigma)
, and
\ell_{\sigma,v}(u^{\downarrow})=0
for all
u\notin\rho(\sigma)\cup\{v\}
.
Therefore
\ell_{\sigma,v}(\sigma^{\downarrow})=\{0,1\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, so
\ell_{\sigma,v}\in\operatorname{Hom}(\langle\sigma^{\downarrow}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Z})=\operatorname{Cl}(Z)^{*}=\operatorname{Pic}(Z)^{*}=L
. This means
\ell_{\sigma,v}([-K_{X}])\in i_{X}\cdot\mathbb{Z}
. Now we apply
\ell_{\sigma,v}
to (
3
) to obtain

0<\langle C_{\sigma},v\rangle+a_{v}=\ell_{\sigma,v}([-K_{X}])\in i_{X}\cdot\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore

i_{X}\leq\langle C_{\sigma},v\rangle+a_{v}.\qed

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
X
from
1.2
we have

(i_{X}-1)\rho_{X}\leq n.

Proof.

We fix an anticanonical representative
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}E=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}D_{i}
with
0<a_{i}\leq 1
for
i=1,\dots,m
, as constructed in
Proposition

3.1
. Now since
\mathcal{P}=\operatorname{conv}\{C_{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}\}
is polar dual to
\mathcal{P}^{*}
, we have
0\in\mathcal{P}
, so there is a linear relation

0=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}m_{\sigma}C_{\sigma},

with
\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}m_{\sigma}=1
, and
0\leq m_{\sigma}\leq 1
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}
. Now using
Proposition

3.2
, for any
v\in\rho(\Sigma)
we have

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}m_{\sigma}\langle C_{\sigma},v\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)},\,v\in\rho(\sigma)}a_{v}m_{\sigma}+\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)},\,v\notin\rho(\sigma)}(i_{X}-a_{v})m_{\sigma},

\displaystyle=(i_{X}-a_{v})\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}m_{\sigma}-\left(i_{X}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)},\,v\in\rho(\sigma)}m_{\sigma}\right).

Rearranging this gives

i_{X}-a_{v}=(i_{X}-a_{v})\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}m_{\sigma}\leq\left(i_{X}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)},\,v\in\rho(\sigma)}m_{\sigma}\right).

Now we take the sum of the left hand term above over all
v\in\rho(\Sigma)
to get

\displaystyle i_{X}\cdot|\rho(\Sigma)|-\sum_{v\in\rho(\Sigma)}a_{v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\rho(\Sigma)}(i_{X}-a_{v})\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}m_{\sigma},

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in\rho(\Sigma)}\left(i_{X}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)},\,v\in\rho(\sigma)}m_{\sigma}\right),

\displaystyle=i_{X}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}\sum_{v\in\rho(\sigma)}m_{\sigma},

\displaystyle=i_{X}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}}(m_{\sigma}\cdot|\rho(\sigma)|),

\displaystyle=i_{X}\cdot d,

and from this we obtain

i_{X}\cdot(m-d)=i_{X}\cdot\rho_{X}\leq\sum_{v\in\rho(\Sigma)}a_{v}.

So by
Proposition

3.1
we have

(i_{X}-1)\rho_{X}\leq n.\qed

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be from
1.2
such that
(i_{X}-1)\rho_{X}=n
, then

X\cong(\mathbb{P}^{i_{X}-1})^{\rho_{X}}.

Proof.

Our proof is inspired by the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 3(ii)]
. For equality to hold in the equation above, we must have equality in
Proposition

3.2
. That is,
i_{X}=\langle v,C_{\sigma}\rangle+a_{v}
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}
and all
v\in\rho(\Sigma)\setminus\rho(\sigma)
. Now fix
\sigma\in\Sigma^{(d)}
, so we have

\langle v,C_{\sigma}\rangle=\begin{cases}-a_{v}&\text{if }v\in\rho(\sigma),\\
i_{X}-a_{v}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{d}\}=\rho(\sigma)
, which by smoothness of
Z
, form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, so we have
C_{\sigma}=-(a_{w_{1}}w_{1}^{*}+\dots+a_{w_{d}}w_{d}^{*})
. Next, for any
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{(d)}
we find

\displaystyle C_{\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\rho(\sigma)}\langle w,C_{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle w^{*},

\displaystyle=-\sum_{w\in\rho(\sigma)\cap\rho(\sigma^{\prime})}a_{w}w^{*}+\sum_{w\in\rho(\sigma)\setminus\rho(\sigma^{\prime})}(i_{X}-a_{w})w^{*},

\displaystyle=C_{\sigma}+i_{X}\cdot\sum_{w\in\rho(\sigma)\setminus\rho(\sigma^{\prime})}w^{*},

and we now use this formula to determine the locations of all
v\in\rho(\Sigma)
. Since
\Sigma
is a complete simplicial fan, for all
i=1,\dots,d
we can define a unique
\sigma_{i}\in\Sigma^{(d)}
such that
\sigma\cap\sigma_{i}
is a facet of
\sigma
and
w_{i}\notin\rho(\sigma_{i})
. Then for any
v\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{\rho_{X}}\}\coloneq\rho(\Sigma)\setminus\{w_{1},\dots,w_{d}\}
we have

\langle v,w_{i}^{*}\rangle=\langle v,C_{\sigma_{i}}-C_{\sigma}\rangle/{i_{X}}=(\langle v,C_{\sigma_{i}}\rangle-(i_{X}-a_{v}))/i_{X}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }v\in\rho(\sigma_{i}),\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Therefore, in the coordinate system
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{d}\}
we can write
v=-\sum_{i\in I(v)}w_{i}
where
I(v)\coloneq\{i=1,\dots,d\mid v\in\rho(\sigma_{i})\}
. Since
\Sigma
is simplicial,
v
is uniquely determined by the property that
v\in\rho(\sigma_{i})
for any one of the
i=1,\dots,d
, so we have
I(v)\cap I(v^{\prime})=\emptyset
for
v\neq v^{\prime}
, so
\{1,\dots,d\}=\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^{\rho_{X}}I(v_{\ell})
is a partition. We have found that

\rho(\Sigma)=\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^{\rho_{X}}\left(\{w_{i}\mid i\in I(v_{\ell})\}\cup\{-\sum_{i\in I(v_{\ell})}w_{i}\}\right)=\rho(\Sigma(\mathbb{P}^{|I(v_{1})|}\times\dots\times\mathbb{P}^{|I(v_{\rho_{X}})|})).

That is, for
\mathrm{Cox}(X)=\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]/\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\rangle
, we have
\mathbb{C}[T_{1},\dots,T_{m}]
is the graded Cox ring of a product of projective spaces. So
\{\deg(T_{i})\mid i=1,\dots,m\}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{\rho_{X}}\}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{\rho_{X}}
.

For the equality
(i_{X}-1)\rho_{X}=n
to hold, we also require equality in
Proposition

3.1
. Using the notation from the proof of
Proposition

3.1
, this implies that for each
j=1,\dots,r
we have some
y_{g_{j}}\in(\mathbb{Q}^{m})^{*}_{\geq 0}
with
Q(y_{g_{j}})=\deg(g_{j})
and
h(y_{g_{j}})=1
. Since for products of projective spaces,
Q(\operatorname{conv}\{e_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,m\})=\operatorname{conv}\{u_{1},\dots,u_{\rho_{X}}\}
, so
\deg(g_{j})=u_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\{1,\dots,\rho_{X}\}
which means
g_{j}
is a Cox ring generator. So
\mathrm{Cox}(X)
is a polynomial ring, and
X=Z
is a product of projective spaces. In
Proposition

3.1
we find
a_{v}=1
for all
v\in\rho(\Sigma)
, so we can take
E
to be the standard toric anticanonical divisor. Finally we require
|I(v_{\ell})|=\langle v_{\ell},C_{\sigma}\rangle=i_{X}-1
for all
\ell=1,\dots,\rho_{X}
. We have proved

X\cong(\mathbb{P}^{i_{X}-1})^{\rho_{X}}.\qed

This completes the proof of the Mukai conjecture for the varieties of
1.2
.
