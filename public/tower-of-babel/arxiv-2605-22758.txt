A sharp interaction-degree threshold for simulating QAOA
Ralfs Āboliņš, Andris Ambainis

Abstract
. We identify a sharp interaction-degree threshold for the classical simulation of QAOA with 2 2 -local cost functions. At degree 3 3 , classical sampling from depth- 1 1 QAOA with small multiplicative error would collapse the polynomial hierarchy to its third level. At degree 2 2 , exact classical sampling from depth- p p QAOA on n n qubits runs in time n O ⁡ ( 1 ) n^{O(1)} whenever p = O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) p=O(\log n) . The hard degree- 3 3 instances have trivially optimizable cost functions, so sampling hardness does not by itself imply a quantum optimization advantage.



1.
Introduction

QAOA (the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm,
[
5
]
)
is one of the main near-term quantum algorithms. Farhi and
Harrow
[
6
]
showed that, already at depth
1
, classical
sampling from the output distribution of QAOA with sufficiently small
multiplicative error would collapse the polynomial hierarchy. Their result is
often cited as evidence that even very shallow QAOA can exhibit behavior
inaccessible to classical sampling.

We examine the structural conditions under which this hardness persists. We
consider
2
-local cost functions
C(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{i}C_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, where each term depends on at most two variables, and
parameterize instances by their interaction degree, the maximum number of other
variables coupled to any single variable. We show that depth-
1
QAOA remains
classically hard to sample from when the interaction degree is at most
3
.
Moreover, the hard instances can be chosen so that the cost function is trivial
to optimize classically, so sampling hardness alone does not yield a quantum
advantage for optimization.

We then show that at interaction degree
2
, every marginal output probability
of a depth-
p
QAOA circuit can be computed classically in time polynomial
in
n
and exponential only in
p
. In particular, exact sampling is
polynomial-time whenever
p=O(\log n)
. The simulation uses the Markov–Shi
algorithm for quantum circuits of small cut width
[
10
]
.

A comparable dichotomy holds classically for constraint satisfaction. SAT
restricted to instances in which every variable occurs in at most
3
clauses
remains
\mathsf{NP}
-complete, but becomes polynomial-time solvable when every
variable occurs in at most
2
clauses
[
11
]
. The
transition from 2-SAT to 3-SAT
[
9
,
7
]
is the most
familiar instance of the same pattern. In the classical case the parameter
separates
\mathsf{P}
from
\mathsf{NP}
-completeness, while for QAOA it
separates classical simulability from sampling hardness.

All simulation bounds in this paper count arithmetic operations on matrix
entries. Since every gate entry in our constructions lies in

\mathbf{Q}(e^{i\pi/4})
, every intermediate value in the simulation belongs to
the same field and is represented by four rational coefficients whose bit
lengths grow at most linearly in the circuit size. Consequently, the same
asymptotic bounds hold in the bit model.

2.
Preliminaries

QAOA takes as input a cost function
C(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
on
n
binary variables
together with a decomposition
C=\sum_{i}C_{i}
into terms each depending on at
most two variables. As a diagonal operator on
n
qubits,
C
acts by
C\ket{z}=C(z)\ket{z}
. The algorithm constructs the depth-
p
state

\ket{\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\beta}}=e^{-i\beta_{p}B}\,e^{-i\gamma_{p}C}\,\cdots\,e^{-i\beta_{1}B}\,e^{-i\gamma_{1}C}\,\ket{+}^{\otimes n},

where
B=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}
is the mixing operator and
\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathbf{R}^{p}
are variational parameters. The state is measured in the
computational basis. Since each
C_{i}
acts on at most two qubits,

e^{-i\gamma_{k}C}
factors into commuting one- and two-qubit diagonal gates, and

e^{-i\beta_{k}B}
into single-qubit rotations. Each
2
-local term coupling
variables
j
and
k
produces a two-qubit diagonal gate on those qubits, and
the set of all such couplings forms a graph.

Definition 1
.

Given a cost function expressed as a sum of
2
-local terms
C=\sum_{i}C_{i}
,
the
interaction graph
of this decomposition is the simple graph on vertex
set
[n]
with an edge
\{j,k\}
whenever some term
C_{i}
depends nontrivially
on both variables
j
and
k
(that is, flipping either bit can change the value
of
C_{i}
). The
interaction degree
is the maximum vertex degree of this
graph. A QAOA instance specifies the decomposition, and we write
degree

for interaction degree when the meaning is clear.

In a post-selected circuit the output distribution is conditioned on a
designated register yielding
\ket{0\cdots 0}
. Since there is no bound on how
small this conditioning probability may be, post-selection is not physically
realizable but is a theoretical tool for relating complexity classes. The
output probability of such a circuit is a ratio of two event probabilities, and
the relevant error notion must preserve such ratios.

Definition 2
.

A classical algorithm samples with
multiplicative error

c\geq 1
from a
distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
\{0,1\}^{n}
if it produces samples from a
distribution
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
satisfying
c^{-1}\mathcal{D}(x)\leq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(x)\leq c\,\mathcal{D}(x)
for every
x
.

By summing the pointwise bounds, multiplicative error extends to event
probabilities. A multiplicative-error sampler therefore approximates both the
numerator and denominator of a post-selected acceptance probability, which is
the mechanism behind the hardness proofs below.

We use two standard facts about post-selection,

\mathsf{PostBQP}=\mathsf{PP}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathsf{PostBPP}\subseteq\Delta_{3}.

The first is due to Aaronson
[
1
]
, and the second to Han,
Hemaspaandra, and Thierauf
[
8
]
. Together with Toda’s theorem,
they imply that a multiplicative-error classical sampler for any circuit family
whose post-selected version equals
\mathsf{PostBQP}
would collapse
\mathsf{PH}

to
\Delta_{3}
.

We now fix the post-selected QAOA class used in the degree-restricted statement.

Definition 3
.

A language
L
is in
\mathsf{PostQAOA}_{p}^{\deg\leq d}
if there exist a constant
0<\epsilon<\tfrac{1}{2}
and a uniform family of depth-
p
QAOA circuits of
interaction degree at most
d
, each equipped with a designated output qubit and
a post-selection register, such that for every input
w
:

(i)

the probability of the post-selection register returning
\ket{0\cdots 0}

is nonzero;

(ii)

if
w\in L
, the output qubit is
1
with probability at least
1-\epsilon
, conditioned on post-selection;

(iii)

if
w\notin L
, the output qubit is
0
with probability at least
1-\epsilon
, conditioned on post-selection.

We write
\mathsf{PostQAOA}_{p}=\bigcup_{d}\mathsf{PostQAOA}_{p}^{\deg\leq d}
for the class with
no degree restriction.

3.
Hardness at interaction degree three

Two prior results establish sampling hardness for restricted circuit families
using similar post-selection arguments. Bremner, Jozsa, and
Shepherd
[
3
]
proved
\mathsf{PostIQP}=\mathsf{PP}
, where IQP circuits
have the form
H^{\otimes n}D\,H^{\otimes n}\ket{0^{n}}
with
D
diagonal in
the computational basis. Farhi and Harrow
[
6
]
extended this
to QAOA by proving
\mathsf{PostQAOA}_{1}=\mathsf{PostBQP}
. Both constructions reduce an
arbitrary
\mathsf{PostBQP}
circuit to the restricted family by replacing each
intermediate Hadamard with a post-selected diagonal coupling to a fresh
auxiliary qubit. The two constructions differ only in the single-qubit gate
F

that completes each step, and hence in the diagonal coupling it requires.

We construct the diagonal coupling for general
F
and add a preprocessing step
that reduces the interaction degree from unbounded to
3
. That is, we prove

\mathsf{PostQAOA}_{1}^{\deg\leq 3}=\mathsf{PostBQP}
and obtain
\mathsf{PostIQP}^{\deg\leq 3}=\mathsf{PP}

(where
\mathsf{PostIQP}^{\deg\leq d}
denotes the subclass of
\mathsf{PostIQP}
in which
D

has interaction degree at most
d
) as a corollary.

Our general construction will transform the input circuit so that intermediate
Hadamard gates are replaced by post-selected diagonal couplings, and all
remaining pre-mixer gates commute and can be collected into a single QAOA phase
layer. In the universal gate set
\mathscr{G}=\{H,T^{\dagger},CZ\}

[
2
]
, every gate except the Hadamard is diagonal.
Diagonal operators on
n
qubits form a commutative subalgebra, so any product
of such gates remains diagonal and merges into one phase layer. The Hadamard
lies outside this subalgebra, so each occurrence must be replaced by a diagonal
coupling to an auxiliary qubit. To replace a Hadamard on qubit
j
, we prepare
an auxiliary
a
in
\ket{+}
and apply a diagonal two-qubit gate
W
to

(a,j)
, then apply
F
to
j
and post-select
j
on
\ket{0}
. Consequently,
qubit
a
carries the logical wire, and we want to choose
W
so that the map
from
j
to
a
acts as a Hadamard. Figure
1
b illustrates the
coupling substitution. For brevity, let us write
w_{ab}=\bra{a,b}W\ket{a,b}

and
r_{b}=\bra{0}F\ket{b}
. The map from
j
to
a
is then
V\colon\mathbf{C}^{2}_{j}\to\mathbf{C}^{2}_{a}
defined by

V\ket{b}_{j}=\bra{0}_{j}F_{j}W_{a,j}\bigl(\ket{+}_{a}\otimes\ket{b}_{j}\bigr)

and has entries
V_{ab}=w_{ab}r_{b}/\sqrt{2}
. The condition
V=\lambda H

determines
W
entrywise.

Proposition 4
.

Suppose
r_{0}r_{1}\neq 0
. Then
V=\lambda H
if and only if

w_{ab}=\frac{\lambda\,(-1)^{ab}}{r_{b}}.

(1)

A unitary diagonal
W
with this property exists if and only if
|r_{0}|=|r_{1}|
,
and is then unique up to global phase.

Proof.

If
w_{ab}
is given by (
1
), then
V_{ab}=\lambda(-1)^{ab}/\sqrt{2}
, so
V=\lambda H
. Conversely,
V=\lambda H

forces
w_{ab}\,r_{b}=\lambda(-1)^{ab}
, which is (
1
). For
the unitarity claim,
|w_{ab}|=1
for all
a,b
holds if and only if

|\lambda|=|r_{0}|=|r_{1}|
. Only the phase of
\lambda
remains free, and it
acts as a global phase on
W
.
∎

Theorem 6
.

For every language
L\in\mathsf{PostBQP}
, there exists a uniform family of
post-selected depth-
1
QAOA circuits of interaction degree at most
3
that
decides
L
with bounded error. Equivalently,

\mathsf{PostQAOA}_{1}^{\deg\leq 3}=\mathsf{PostBQP}.

Moreover, the reduction preserves the post-selected output distribution exactly.

Proof.

The inclusion
\mathsf{PostQAOA}_{1}^{\deg\leq 3}\subseteq\mathsf{PostBQP}
is immediate. For
the reverse, we will show that decomposing over
\mathscr{G}
and applying our
coupling to each Hadamard converts an arbitrary
\mathsf{PostBQP}
circuit into
depth-
1
QAOA, and then bound the interaction degree. For
F=e^{-i\beta X}
,
Proposition
4
forces
\beta=\pi/4
and gives
W=\diag(1,i,1,-i)
. One verifies that for all states
\ket{\phi_{0}}
and

\ket{\phi_{1}}
,

\bra{0}_{j}\tilde{H}_{j}W_{a,j}\bigl(\ket{+}_{a}\otimes(\ket{0}_{j}\ket{\phi_{0}}+\ket{1}_{j}\ket{\phi_{1}})\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,H_{a}\bigl(\ket{0}_{a}\ket{\phi_{0}}+\ket{1}_{a}\ket{\phi_{1}}\bigr).

This identity replaces each intermediate Hadamard by a diagonal coupling to an
auxiliary, but applying it directly leaves the degree unbounded, since
consecutive diagonal gates on one wire all contribute edges to the same qubit.
We preprocess the circuit to enforce the invariant that consecutive diagonal
gates on each wire are separated by a Hadamard.

If a wire begins with a Hadamard, absorb it into the preparation
\ket{+}=H\ket{0}
. If it begins with a diagonal gate, prepend
H^{2}
and absorb the
first
H
into the preparation. We separate consecutive diagonal gates by
inserting
I=H^{2}
(Figure
1
a). At the end of each wire, insert
I=\tilde{H}\tilde{H}^{\dagger}
, absorb
\tilde{H}
into the mixing layer, and
decompose
\tilde{H}^{\dagger}=H\,e^{i\pi Z/4}\,H
, producing two Hadamards
separated by a
1
-local diagonal. After preprocessing, every wire begins in

\ket{+}
, ends with a single
\tilde{H}
, and has at most one diagonal gate
between consecutive Hadamards.

With the invariant in place, we replace each intermediate Hadamard by the
coupling (Figure
1
b). After all substitutions, every qubit starts
in
\ket{+}
and receives exactly one
\tilde{H}
from the mixing layer. All
remaining gates before the mixer are diagonal, with phases that are eighth roots
of unity. Setting
\gamma=\pi/4
therefore collects them into a single
integer-valued cost function
C
. The compiled circuit is

\tilde{H}^{\otimes n}\,e^{-i\tfrac{\pi}{4}C}\,\ket{+}^{\otimes n}

with post-selection on some qubits, which is depth-
1
QAOA with
\beta=\gamma=\pi/4
.

No gate acts on a qubit after its projection onto
\ket{0}
, so the
post-selections commute with all subsequent operations and form a single
register, and each coupling contributes a factor of
1/\sqrt{2}
that cancels in
the post-selected distribution. The output distribution equals that of the
original circuit, and the compilation is polynomial-time.

It remains to bound the degree. Along each wire the active qubits form a chain
of couplings (Figure
1
c). Each coupling moves the wire to a new
qubit, so each qubit participates in at most two coupling edges. Preprocessing
ensured at most one diagonal gate per segment, so each qubit carries at most one
original
2
-local gate, contributing at most one additional edge. The maximum
degree is
2+1=3
.
∎

Corollary 7
.

\mathsf{PostIQP}^{\deg\leq 3}=\mathsf{PP}
.

Proof.

Repeat the proof of Theorem
6
with the IQP specialization
F=H
,
W=CZ
, and combine with
\mathsf{PostBQP}=\mathsf{PP}
.
∎

Corollary 8
.

If depth-
1
QAOA circuits of interaction degree at most
3
could be sampled
classically with multiplicative error
1\leq c<\sqrt{2}
, then
\mathsf{PH}\subseteq\Delta_{3}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
6
,
\mathsf{PostQAOA}_{1}^{\deg\leq 3}=\mathsf{PP}
. Given
L\in\mathsf{PP}
and a constant
\epsilon>0
, amplification within

\mathsf{PostBQP}

[
1
, §3]
followed by Theorem
6

produces a degree-
3
depth-
1
QAOA circuit whose post-selected acceptance
probability
q
satisfies
q\geq 1-\epsilon
on yes-instances and
q\leq\epsilon
on no-instances. A multiplicative-error
c
sampler approximates
every event probability within factor
c
, so it estimates
q
to within
c^{2}
.
On a yes-instance the estimate is at least
(1-\epsilon)/c^{2}>\tfrac{1}{2}
,
and on a no-instance at most
c^{2}\epsilon<\tfrac{1}{2}
, provided
c<\sqrt{2}
and
\epsilon
is small enough. Thus
\mathsf{PP}\subseteq\mathsf{PostBPP}
and

\mathsf{PH}\;\subseteq\;\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{PP}}\;\subseteq\;\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{PostBPP}}\;\subseteq\;\mathsf{P}^{\Delta_{3}}\;=\;\Delta_{3}

by Toda’s theorem and
\mathsf{PostBPP}\subseteq\Delta_{3}
.
∎

Remark 9
.

Additive error would not suffice in Corollary
8
. The
post-selection event may have exponentially small probability, so an
additive-error guarantee on the joint distribution gives no useful bound on the
conditional. Extending the hardness to additive error requires an average-case
conjecture, as illustrated by the IQP case
[
4
]
.

The hard instances of Theorem
6
have a trivially optimizable
cost function.

Proposition 10
.

The cost function
C
constructed in Theorem
6
can be made
monotone and maximized at
x=1^{n}
without altering the QAOA circuit.

Proof.

Since
e^{-i(\pi/4)C}
depends only on
C
modulo
8
, individual diagonal
entries may be shifted by multiples of
8
. Write
W=e^{-i(\pi/4)C_{W}}
with

C_{W}=6\ket{01}\!\bra{01}+2\ket{11}\!\bra{11}
. The
T^{\dagger}
and
CZ
contributions

\ket{1}\!\bra{1}
and
4\ket{11}\!\bra{11}
already have nonnegative coefficients. Replacing
the
C_{W}
diagonal
(0,6,0,2)
by the congruent
(0,6,0,10)
and the endpoint
diagonal
(7,1)
by
(7,9)
makes every coefficient nonnegative, so
C
is
monotone and maximized at
x=1^{n}
.
∎

4.
Tractability at interaction degree two

We now show that the degree-
3
bound is tight. A graph of maximum degree
2

decomposes into paths, cycles, and isolated vertices, and the QAOA circuit on
each component has small cut width.

Definition 11
.

A gate on qubits
S

crosses
cut
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
if
\min S\leq i<\max S
. Single-qubit gates cross no cut. The
cut width
of a
circuit on qubits
[n]
is the maximum number of gates crossing any single cut.

Markov and Shi simulate a circuit by contracting its tensor network along a
linear qubit ordering, at cost exponential in the number of gates crossing any
single cut.

Proposition 12

(Markov–Shi
[
10
, Proposition 5.1]
)
.

A quantum circuit of size
T
and cut width
r
, in which each gate has an equal
number of input and output qubits, can be simulated deterministically in time

T^{O(1)}\exp[O(r)]
.

By simulation we mean computing
\Pr[Z_{S}=z_{S}]
for a specified subset
S\subseteq[n]
and outcome
z_{S}
. Unmeasured qubits are traced out during
contraction at no additional asymptotic cost.

Theorem 13
.

Let
Q
be a depth-
p
QAOA circuit on
n
qubits whose interaction graph has
maximum degree
2
. For any
S\subseteq[n]
and
z_{S}\in\{0,1\}^{|S|}
, the
marginal probability
\Pr[Z_{S}=z_{S}]
can be computed deterministically in time

(np)^{O(1)}\exp(O(p))
.

Proof.

Phase gates couple only qubits within the same component and mixer gates act on
single qubits, so the state factors as
\ket{\psi}=\bigotimes_{c}\ket{\psi_{c}}
.
It therefore suffices to simulate each component independently.

A graph of maximum degree
2
decomposes into isolated vertices, paths, and
cycles. An isolated vertex sees only single-qubit gates, so its output
distribution is computable in
O(p)
operations. For a linear ordering of the
vertices, let
\delta(i)
denote the number of interaction-graph edges crossing
cut
i
. Each such edge contributes one gate per phase layer, so the circuit
cut width is
p\cdot\max_{i}\delta(i)
. For a path in the natural ordering,
each edge
\{i,i{+}1\}
crosses only cut
i
, so
\max_{i}\delta(i)=1
and the
cut width is
p
. For a cycle, the wrap-around edge
\{1,k\}
crosses every
cut, so
\max_{i}\delta(i)=2
and the cut width is
2p
.

In every case the cut width is
O(p)
and the circuit has size
O(kp)
.
Proposition
12
gives runtime
(kp)^{O(1)}\exp(O(p))
per
component, and summing over components whose sizes total
n
gives

(np)^{O(1)}\exp(O(p))
.
∎

Corollary 14
.

The output distribution of a depth-
p
QAOA circuit on
n
qubits with
interaction degree at most
2
can be sampled exactly by a classical randomized
algorithm in time
(np)^{O(1)}\exp(O(p))
. When
p=O(\log n)
, this equals

n^{O(1)}
.

Proof.

Sample
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
sequentially by the chain rule. At step
t
, two
calls to Theorem
13
give
\Pr[Z_{1}=z_{1},\ldots,Z_{t-1}=z_{t-1},\,Z_{t}=b]
for
b\in\{0,1\}
, and we draw
z_{t}
from the conditional
distribution. After
n
steps,
z=z_{1}\cdots z_{n}
is drawn from the exact
output distribution. The
2n
calls cost
(np)^{O(1)}\exp(O(p))
total, which
equals
n^{O(1)}
when
p=O(\log n)
.
∎

5.
Discussion

The simulation threshold for
2
-local QAOA lies between interaction degrees
2

and
3
. Since exact simulation implies simulation with any multiplicative
error, degree-
2
QAOA is classically easy under both error models when
p=O(\log n)
.

The degree-
2
simulation runs in time exponential in
p
, and whether a
different method can improve this is open. Corollary
8
assumes
multiplicative error, and extending the hardness to additive error would require
an anticoncentration bound and an average-case hardness conjecture for random

2
-local cost functions on degree-
3
graphs. The analogous results for
IQP
[
4
]
do not transfer because the anticoncentration
proof uses completeness of the interaction graph and the average-case families
have unbounded degree. The hard instances have trivially optimizable cost
functions (Proposition
10
), and whether there exist
families for which both optimization and sampling are hard remains open.
