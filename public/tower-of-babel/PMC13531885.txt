An LSCC-specific R-loop-related model predicts prognosis and neoadjuvant immunotherapy response and identifies EIF5A2-mediated tumor–immune crosstalk
Authors: Jie Dai, Ziyu Qiao, Jia Wang, Ze Chen, Ming Yan, Jian Qiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLaryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) lacks robust biomarkers for prognostic stratification and immunotherapy prediction. R-loops have been increasingly implicated in transcriptional dysregulation and tumor progression, but their clinical significance in LSCC remains unclear.MethodsWe generated an LSCC-specific R-loop-related gene set by intersecting R-loop-associated genes with differentially expressed genes in the TCGA-LSCC cohort, and constructed a prognostic model using LASSO-Cox regression. The model was externally validated in GSE25727 and GSE27020 and further evaluated for neoadjuvant immunotherapy relevance in GSE210287. Single-cell RNA sequencing data from GSE290927 were used to identify the core immune-regulatory gene within the model and explore its potential mechanism in tumor–immune interaction.ResultsWe identified 56 LSCC-specific R-loop-related candidate genes and established a three-gene model that successfully stratified patients into distinct risk groups, with stable performance in external validation cohorts. The model retained prognostic value, distinguished different immune microenvironment states, and was associated with neoadjuvant immunotherapy response. Among the model genes, EIF5A2 showed the greatest consistency with the overall model in terms of high-risk association, poor prognosis, and unfavorable immune-related changes, and was therefore identified as the core gene. Single-cell analysis further showed that EIF5A2 was mainly expressed in tumor cells, while cell-cell communication analysis revealed enhanced interactions between EIF5A2-high tumor cells and exhausted CD8 T cells, with MIF-CD74-related signaling representing the dominant ligand–receptor axis.ConclusionsWe established an LSCC-specific R-loop-related model with value in prognostic stratification and neoadjuvant immunotherapy prediction, and further identified EIF5A2 as a core immune-regulatory gene within the model. These findings provide a biologically interpretable framework linking R-loop-related tumor states to prognosis, immunotherapy response, and immune remodeling in LSCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12957-026-04456-7.

Background

Laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) is one of the most common malignancies of the head and neck, and its clinical management remains challenging because of marked biological heterogeneity, variable prognosis, and the lack of robust biomarkers for individualized stratification [1, 2]. Although multiple prognostic signatures have been reported in LSCC, many of them are primarily transcriptome-based statistical models and are not tightly linked to a clearly defined upstream biological process, which limits their biological interpretability and translational value [3, 4].

R-loops are three-stranded nucleic acid structures composed of an RNA: DNA hybrid and a displaced single-stranded DNA, and their dysregulation has been increasingly implicated in transcription-replication conflict, replication stress, DNA damage, and genomic instability in cancer [5, 6]. Beyond tumor-intrinsic effects, recent studies have further shown that aberrant R-loop accumulation can activate inflammatory and immune-related pathways, including cGAS-STING- and NF-κB-associated signaling, suggesting that R-loop dysregulation may also contribute to tumor immune remodeling [7]. However, the clinical significance of R-loop-related genes in LSCC has not yet been systematically defined.

This issue is particularly relevant in the immunotherapy era, as immune checkpoint blockade has become an important treatment strategy in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, while neoadjuvant immunotherapy is also being actively investigated and increasingly incorporated into clinical practice [8–10]. However, treatment responses remain highly heterogeneous, and currently available biomarkers, especially PD-L1-based approaches, are limited by insufficient predictive power as well as spatial and temporal heterogeneity [11–13]. Recent studies and reviews have therefore emphasized the need for more biologically informed composite predictors that can better capture both tumor-intrinsic states and immune context [11, 14].

At the same time, bulk transcriptomic analyses alone are often insufficient to explain how a prognostic or treatment-associated model is implemented at the cellular level [15, 16]. Single-cell studies in LSCC have revealed marked epithelial plasticity and complex immune heterogeneity, indicating that the biological meaning of a bulk-derived model cannot be fully understood without resolving its cellular basis [17]. This is particularly important for studies aiming to connect tumor-associated transcriptional states with dysfunctional immune populations such as exhausted CD8 T cells [18].

Based on these considerations, we sought to establish an LSCC-specific R-loop-related model by intersecting R-loop-associated genes with LSCC-related differentially expressed genes. We then evaluated its value in prognostic stratification and neoadjuvant immunotherapy prediction, and further used single-cell transcriptomic and cell-cell communication analyses to identify the core immune-regulatory gene within the model and explore its potential mechanism in tumor-immune interaction.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and generation of the LSCC-specific R-loop-related gene set

Transcriptomic profiles and corresponding clinical information of laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and used as the training cohort. External validation datasets GSE25727 and GSE27020 were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) for survival validation. The GSE210287 dataset was used for neoadjuvant immunotherapy response analysis, and GSE290927 was used for single-cell RNA sequencing analysis. A predefined R-loop-related gene set was collected from published resources and used as the reference R-loop gene set. Differential expression analysis between tumor and normal tissues in the TCGA cohort was performed to identify LSCC-associated dysregulated genes, and intersection analysis was then conducted to generate an LSCC-specific R-loop-related candidate gene set.

Functional enrichment analysis

To characterize the biological relevance of the LSCC-specific R-loop-related candidate genes, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed using the clusterProfiler package in R. GO analysis included biological process, cellular component, and molecular function categories. Terms with adjusted P < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched.

Construction of the R-loop-related prognostic model

To evaluate whether the LSCC-specific R-loop-related candidate genes could be translated into a clinically relevant prognostic tool, a prognostic model was constructed in the TCGA cohort using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression implemented in the glmnet package. The optimal penalty parameter was determined by tenfold cross-validation. Genes with nonzero coefficients at the optimal lambda value were retained to generate the final model. A risk score was calculated for each patient based on the expression levels and corresponding coefficients of the selected genes. Patients in the training and validation cohorts were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups according to the cohort-specific median risk score. Kaplan–Meier analysis and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis were used to assess model performance.

External validation and prognostic evaluation

To assess the robustness of the model, external validation was performed in the GEO cohorts GSE25727 and GSE27020 using the same risk formula. Kaplan–Meier curves and time-dependent ROC analyses were applied to evaluate the reproducibility of the model in independent datasets. To further determine its clinical value, Cox regression analysis was performed by integrating the risk score with clinicopathological variables in the TCGA cohort. A nomogram was subsequently constructed using the rms package to estimate 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year overall survival, and calibration analysis was performed to evaluate the agreement between predicted and observed survival probabilities.

Immune microenvironment and neoadjuvant immunotherapy analysis

To investigate the immunological relevance of the R-loop-related model, the relative abundance of tumor-infiltrating immune cells in the TCGA cohort was estimated using the CIBERSORT algorithm. Differences in immune cell composition between the high-risk and low-risk groups were compared, and Spearman correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the relationships between the risk score and representative immune checkpoint molecules. To assess the potential treatment relevance of the model, the GSE210287 neoadjuvant immunotherapy cohort was analyzed. Risk scores were calculated for each sample, and differences in risk score distribution between responders and non-responders were compared.

Identification of the core immune-regulatory gene within the model

To identify the core immune-regulatory gene within the model, the selected model genes were further compared in terms of their associations with immune checkpoints, immune infiltration patterns, and survival outcomes. Correlation analyses between gene expression and immune checkpoint molecules were conducted using Spearman’s rank correlation. Immune-cell infiltration differences were further evaluated after stratification by individual gene expression. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis and univariate Cox regression were then performed to determine which model gene showed the highest consistency with the overall model in terms of high-risk association, unfavorable immune context, and poor prognosis. The immune-regulatory candidate gene was selected based on its overall consistency with the model in terms of immune infiltration pattern, immune checkpoint correlation, high-risk association, and prognostic relevance.

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis

The scRNA-seq dataset GSE290927 was processed using the Seurat workflow in R. Low-quality cells were removed according to standard quality-control criteria, including gene count, UMI count, and mitochondrial transcript proportion. After normalization, highly variable genes were identified, followed by dimensionality reduction and clustering. Batch effects across samples were corrected using Harmony. Major cell populations were annotated based on canonical marker genes.

To further refine the epithelial compartment, epithelial subclusters were jointly annotated using epithelial-state signature scoring and inferCNV profiles. Based on the combined interpretation of transcriptional signatures and copy number variation patterns, epithelial cells were classified into distinct epithelial states, including tumor-associated populations. Because CD8 T cells are key effectors in immunotherapy and showed differential abundance in the EIF5A2-high and EIF5A2-low groups, the T-cell compartment was further reclustered. Marker gene sets representing different T-cell functional states were used to score each T-cell subcluster, and subtype names were assigned according to the highest-scoring functional signature.

Trajectory analysis and EIF5A2-associated transcriptomic perturbation analysis

To explore the dynamic relationship between EIF5A2 and epithelial progression, trajectory analysis was performed in the epithelial compartment using a pseudotime inference framework. The distribution of epithelial subtypes along the trajectory and the dynamic expression pattern of EIF5A2 were subsequently evaluated. To further characterize the biological programs associated with EIF5A2, transcriptomic perturbation data generated after EIF5A2 knockout were analyzed. Differentially expressed genes after EIF5A2 depletion were identified, followed by KEGG and GO enrichment analyses to examine tumor-intrinsic and immune-related biological processes associated with EIF5A2.

Cell-cell communication analysis

To investigate how EIF5A2-high tumor cells may regulate the immune microenvironment, cell-cell communication analysis was performed using CellChat. Communication networks were compared between the EIF5A2-high and EIF5A2-low conditions at both the global interaction and pathway levels. Because previous analyses suggested that EIF5A2-associated immune changes were closely related to CD8 T-cell dysfunction, particular attention was paid to signaling interactions between tumor cells and exhausted CD8 T cells. Differential pathway strength analysis and ligand–receptor pair analysis were then used to identify the major communication axes associated with the EIF5A2-high state.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed in R. Differential expression between two groups was assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test unless otherwise specified. Survival differences were evaluated using Kaplan–Meier curves with the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards models were used to estimate hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals. Correlations were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. For analyses involving multiple comparisons, including TIL comparisons and gene expression correlation analyses, FDR correction was applied, and FDR-adjusted P values were used to determine statistical significance.

Result

Identification of LSCC-specific R-loop-related candidate genes

To generate an LSCC-specific R-loop-related candidate gene set for subsequent model construction, differential expression analysis was first performed between tumor and normal tissues in the TCGA laryngeal cancer cohort. A substantial number of dysregulated genes were identified, and principal component analysis further confirmed the overall transcriptional distinction between tumor and normal samples (Fig. 1A-B).

These differentially expressed genes were then intersected with the predefined R-loop-related gene set, yielding 56 LSCC-specific R-loop-related candidate genes (Fig. 1D). The heatmap and volcano plot showed that these intersecting genes could clearly distinguish tumor tissues from normal controls and were predominantly distributed among the upregulated genes, suggesting that the LSCC-specific R-loop gene set was mainly enriched in cancer-associated transcriptional alterations (Fig. 1C, E).

To further characterize the biological relevance of this candidate gene set, functional enrichment analysis was performed. KEGG analysis showed that these genes were mainly enriched in DNA replication and other tumor-related pathways, while also involving immune- and host response-associated pathways, including viral carcinogenesis, human immunodeficiency virus 1 infection, Epstein-Barr virus infection, and integrin signaling. GO analysis further indicated significant enrichment in chromosome segregation, nuclear division, chromosomal region, and DNA helicase activity, suggesting that the R-loop-related candidate genes are closely associated with proliferative, genome-maintenance, and immune microenvironment-related biological programs in LSCC (Fig. 1F-I).

Taken together, these results established an LSCC-specific R-loop-related candidate gene set and laid the foundation for subsequent model construction and downstream analyses.

Construction and external validation of the R-loop-related prognostic model

To determine whether the LSCC-specific R-loop-related candidate genes could be translated into a clinically relevant prognostic tool, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression was performed in the TCGA laryngeal cancer cohort. The optimal penalty parameter was determined by tenfold cross validation, and three genes were ultimately retained for model construction (Fig. 2A-B). Based on the derived risk score, patients were subsequently classified into low-risk and high-risk groups (Fig. 2C).

Using the established formula, each patient was assigned a risk score and categorized accordingly. The risk score distribution plot showed that patients in the high-risk group tended to cluster at the upper end of the score spectrum and exhibited a higher frequency of death events than those in the low-risk group. The heatmap further showed that the high-risk group was characterized by relatively higher expression of EIF5A2 and AP2M1 and lower expression of ARHGAP4, whereas the opposite pattern was observed in the low-risk group, indicating that the three-gene signature captured distinct transcriptional features associated with risk stratification (Fig. 2D).

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that patients in the high-risk group had significantly worse overall survival than those in the low-risk group in the TCGA cohort (p = 0.00016; Fig. 2E). Time-dependent ROC analysis showed that the three-gene signature achieved AUC values of 0.593, 0.640, and 0.627 for predicting 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year overall survival, respectively (Fig. 2F), supporting its prognostic value in the training cohort.

To further assess the robustness of the model, external validation was performed in two independent GEO cohorts. According to the cohort-specific median risk score, patients in each validation cohort were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups for survival analysis. In the GSE25727 cohort, the high-risk group showed significantly shorter disease-free survival than the low-risk group (p = 0.024; Fig. 2G), and time-dependent ROC analysis yielded AUC values of 0.629, 0.547, and 0.520 at 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years, respectively (Fig. 2H). Similarly, in the GSE27020 cohort, disease-free survival was also significantly poorer in the high-risk group (p = 0.034; Fig. 2I). Time-dependent ROC analysis in GSE27020 yielded AUC values of 0.543, 0.593, and 0.605 at 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years, respectively (Fig. 2J), further confirming the applicability of the signature in independent external cohorts.

Taken together, these results indicate that the three-gene R-loop-related prognostic model can effectively stratify patients with laryngeal cancer into distinct risk groups and retains reliable prognostic value across independent cohorts.

Prognostic and immunological relevance of the R-loop-related model

After confirming the stability of the R-loop-related model in external cohorts, we next evaluated its clinical and immunological relevance in the TCGA laryngeal cancer cohort. Cox regression analysis incorporating clinicopathological variables and the risk score showed that the model-derived risk score remained significantly associated with overall survival, indicating that it could provide prognostic information beyond conventional clinical parameters (Fig. 3A). To improve the clinical applicability of the model, a nomogram integrating the risk score with clinicopathological factors was subsequently constructed to predict 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year overall survival (Fig. 3B). Calibration analysis showed favorable agreement between the predicted and observed survival probabilities at different time points (Fig. 3C), and time-dependent ROC analysis further demonstrated that the nomogram achieved AUC values of 0.736, 0.829, and 0.846 for predicting 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year overall survival, respectively (Fig. 3D). To further evaluate the incremental value of the R-loop-related signature, we compared the predictive performance of the clinical model, gene signature, and combined nomogram model. The combined model showed the highest AUCs at 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years, outperforming both the clinical model alone and the gene signature alone (Fig. 3E-G). These results suggest that the R-loop-related signature provides additional prognostic information when integrated with clinicopathological variables.

Given the close relationship between patient outcome and the tumor immune microenvironment, we next investigated whether the R-loop-related model could distinguish different immune states. Immune infiltration analysis revealed clear differences in multiple immune-cell populations between the high-risk and low-risk groups, particularly in plasma cells, CD8 T cells, T follicular helper cells, and macrophages M0 (Fig. 4A). Overall, the high-risk group tended to exhibit a less favorable immune context, characterized by reduced infiltration of anti-tumor immune populations and increased enrichment of macrophage-related components. Correlation analysis further showed that the risk score was closely associated with multiple immune checkpoint molecules, suggesting that the model-related transcriptional state may be linked to immune regulatory pathways (Fig. 4B). To further evaluate its translational relevance, we analyzed the GSE210287 neoadjuvant immunotherapy cohort and found that non-responders had significantly higher risk scores than responders (Fig. 4C), while response distribution analysis further demonstrated that responders were enriched in the low-risk group, whereas non-responders were more frequent in the high-risk group (Fig. 4D). Taken together, these findings indicate that the R-loop-related model not only has meaningful prognostic value in LSCC, but can also distinguish immune microenvironment states and predict the likelihood of benefit from neoadjuvant immunotherapy.

Identification of EIF5A2 as the core immune-regulatory gene within the model

To identify the core immune-regulatory gene within the model, we first compared the three model genes in terms of their associations with immune checkpoints and immune infiltration patterns. Correlation analyses showed that the three genes displayed distinct checkpoint-related patterns (Fig. 4E-F). Specifically, EIF5A2 was more positively associated with PD-L1 and PD-L2, whereas ARHGAP4 showed stronger positive associations with PD-1 and CTLA4; in contrast, AP2M1 displayed relatively weak and less consistent correlations with these checkpoint molecules (Fig. 4E-F). We next compared the immune infiltration patterns associated with each model gene and found that the immune landscape associated with EIF5A2 most closely resembled that of the overall high-risk model, including a less favorable immune context with reduced anti-tumor immune-cell infiltration and relatively increased macrophage-related components (Fig. 4H). By comparison, ARHGAP4 showed a largely opposite immune pattern (Fig. 4J), whereas AP2M1 showed a weaker and less coherent relationship with immune infiltration changes (Fig. 4I). These findings suggest that among the three model genes, EIF5A2 showed the highest consistency with the immune features represented by the model.

We further compared their prognostic relevance to determine which gene most faithfully reflected the clinical behavior of the model. Univariate Cox analysis showed that EIF5A2 and AP2M1 tended to act as risk-associated factors, whereas ARHGAP4 showed a protective trend (Fig. 4G). Kaplan-Meier analysis further demonstrated that high EIF5A2 expression was significantly associated with worse overall survival, while high ARHGAP4 expression was associated with better survival, and AP2M1 showed no significant survival difference after dichotomization (Fig. 4K-M). Taken together, EIF5A2 showed the greatest consistency with the model in terms of immune infiltration pattern, immune checkpoint correlation, high-risk association, and poor prognosis; therefore, it was selected as the core immune-regulatory candidate gene for downstream mechanistic analysis.

Single-cell profiling of epithelial and CD8 T-cell heterogeneity

To provide a cellular framework for mechanistic analysis, we next analyzed the scRNA-seq dataset GSE290927 and constructed the single-cell atlas of LSCC. Initial annotation identified multiple major cell populations, including epithelial cells, T cells, B cells, monocytes, neutrophils, NK cells, endothelial cells, and smooth muscle cells, which were supported by canonical marker expression patterns (Fig. 5A-C). After sample integration and batch correction, cells from different samples showed satisfactory mixing and were grouped into transcriptionally distinct clusters, indicating the robustness of the clustering results (Fig. 5D-E). Because epithelial cells were expected to represent the major malignant compartment, inferCNV analysis was further performed and confirmed the presence of epithelial subclusters with tumor-like copy number variation patterns (Fig. 5F).

To further refine the epithelial compartment, epithelial subclusters were evaluated using both epithelial-state signature scoring and inferCNV profiles before final subtype annotation. Based on the combined interpretation of transcriptional scoring and CNV patterns, the epithelial compartment was resolved into multiple transcriptionally distinct states, including basal-like, differentiated squamous, secretory/junctional, suprabasal-like, and tumor-associated epithelial populations, while subcluster-level CNV analysis further supported the separation of normal-like and tumor-like epithelial states (Fig. 5G-I).

As the CIBERSORT analysis in Fig. 4H showed a significant difference in CD8 T-cell infiltration between the EIF5A2-high and EIF5A2-low groups, and because CD8 T cells represent a key effector population in immunotherapy, we next performed a more detailed analysis of the T-cell compartment. Before final subtype annotation, marker gene sets representing different T-cell functional states were used to score each T-cell subcluster, and subtype names were assigned according to the highest-scoring functional signature. This analysis resolved the T-cell compartment into several subpopulations, including CD4 T cells, cytotoxic CD8 T cells, effector CD8 T cells, exhausted CD8 T cells, Treg cells, and APC-like CD8 T cells (Fig. 5J-K). The resulting subtype annotation was further supported by the heatmap of top marker genes and the dot plot of canonical markers, which clearly distinguished cytotoxic, effector, and exhausted CD8 T-cell states (Fig. 5L-M). These results indicate that LSCC contains substantial epithelial and T-cell heterogeneity and, importantly, establish a refined CD8 T-cell classification framework for subsequent analyses of how EIF5A2-high tumor cells may regulate dysfunctional CD8 T-cell populations.

EIF5A2 is enriched in tumor cells and immune-related programs

After identifying EIF5A2 as the core immune-regulatory gene within the model, we next explored its cellular distribution and biological relevance in the single-cell dataset. Mapping EIF5A2 expression onto the LSCC single-cell atlas provided an overall view of its distribution across the tumor ecosystem (Fig. 6A-B). To further connect EIF5A2 with the model-related tumor state, we compared the cellular composition between EIF5A2-high and EIF5A2-low conditions, which suggested that EIF5A2-associated changes were mainly linked to the epithelial compartment and therefore supported further tracing of its tumor-cell-related biological effects (Fig. 6C). Consistently, cell-type-specific expression analysis further showed that EIF5A2 was mainly expressed in tumor cells rather than in immune or stromal populations (Fig. 6D), indicating that EIF5A2 is primarily a tumor-cell-derived signal.

To further clarify its relationship with tumor progression, trajectory analysis was performed in epithelial cells. The epithelial compartment showed a continuous developmental distribution across pseudotime (Fig. 6E-F), and EIF5A2 expression gradually increased along this trajectory, with higher levels observed in later epithelial states (Fig. 6G). These findings suggest that EIF5A2 is associated with progressive malignant epithelial evolution rather than merely reflecting static expression differences.

To further characterize the biological programs associated with EIF5A2, transcriptomic perturbation analysis after EIF5A2 knockout was examined. EIF5A2 depletion analysis within the GSE290927 single-cell dataset identified a set of affected genes for downstream enrichment analysis (Fig. 6H-I). Functional enrichment analysis showed that these genes were enriched not only in tumor-intrinsic programs such as cell cycle, mitotic regulation, and chromosome segregation, but also in immune- or host response-related pathways, particularly Human T-cell leukemia virus 1 infection, as well as apoptosis, p53 signaling, and ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis, which may be relevant to immune regulation (Fig. 6J-M). Taken together, these results indicate that EIF5A2 is predominantly enriched in malignant epithelial cells and is associated with both tumor progression-related and immune-relevant biological programs, providing the basis for subsequent analysis of how EIF5A2-high tumor cells regulate the immune microenvironment.

EIF5A2-high tumor cells interact with exhausted CD8 T cells

To further investigate how EIF5A2-high tumor cells may reshape the immune microenvironment, we performed cell-cell communication analysis stratified by EIF5A2 expression status. Overall pathway-level analysis showed that the EIF5A2-high group was enriched in multiple immune- and microenvironment-related signaling pathways, such as CHEMERIN, PERIOSTIN, EDN, and TRAIL, suggesting broader activation of immune-associated intercellular communication programs under the EIF5A2-high state (Fig. 7A). At the global interaction level, the EIF5A2-high group exhibited a greater number of inferred cell-cell interactions, whereas the EIF5A2-low group showed relatively higher overall interaction strength (Fig. 7B-C), suggesting that EIF5A2-high and EIF5A2-low tumors differ not only in the intensity but also in the architecture of intercellular communication. Consistently, the global communication networks differed visibly between the two groups (Fig. 7D-E).

Because our previous analyses suggested that EIF5A2-related immune changes were closely linked to CD8 T-cell dysfunction, we next focused on tumor-cell-directed signaling toward T-cell populations. Network analysis showed that tumor cells had prominent outgoing communication toward exhausted CD8 T cells in both groups, with stronger and more preferential interactions observed in the EIF5A2-high condition (Fig. 7F-G). Differential pathway analysis further demonstrated that among the tumor-to-Tex signaling pathways, MIF showed the most pronounced enhancement in the EIF5A2-high group (Fig. 7H). Consistently, ligand-receptor analysis identified MIF-(CD74 + CXCR4), MIF-(CD74 + CXCR2), and MIF-(CD74 + CD44) as the major interaction pairs involved in tumor-to-exhausted CD8 T-cell communication (Fig. 7I). Taken together, these findings suggest that EIF5A2-high tumor cells may contribute to an immunosuppressive microenvironment by strengthening MIF-CD74-related crosstalk with exhausted CD8 T cells.

Discussion

Compared with earlier LSCC prognostic studies, the main contribution of our work is not simply the construction of another survival signature, but the establishment of a biologically anchored R-loop-related model. Previous LSCC models have more often been derived from broad immune, methylation/autophagy, mismatch-repair, or other transcriptomic gene sets, which are useful for risk stratification but often remain weakly connected to a specific upstream biological process [19, 20]. By starting from the intersection of an R-loop gene set and LSCC-related dysregulated genes, our study frames prognosis prediction within a more defined context of transcription-replication stress and genome-maintenance biology. This direction is also consistent with recent cancer literature showing that R-loops are increasingly viewed as functionally relevant structures in tumor progression rather than passive transcriptional by-products [21–23].

A second point that distinguishes this study from many prior LSCC models is its extension from prognosis prediction to immunotherapy relevance. In head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, PD-1/PD-L1 blockade has clear clinical value, but response heterogeneity remains substantial, and existing biomarkers—especially PD-L1-based approaches—are limited by biological and spatial heterogeneity [24–26]. More recent work has even argued that no single biomarker is likely to consistently predict immunotherapy efficacy in HNSCC [27]. Against that background, the value of our model is not that it replaces established biomarkers, but that it provides an additional transcriptome-based layer that captures both tumor-intrinsic biology and immune-context differences. In this sense, our study moves beyond the common “prognosis-only” design and is closer to the current direction of HNSCC biomarker research, which increasingly favors composite or biologically informed predictors rather than isolated markers [11, 28]. This view is also consistent with recent gastric cancer studies using multimodal digital biopsy and virtual biopsy strategies, which showed that integrated prediction frameworks can better capture occult aggressive tumor biology than isolated markers alone [29–33]. Although our study used transcriptomic and single-cell data rather than imaging-based multimodal data, it follows a similar precision-oncology logic by linking tumor-intrinsic states with clinically meaningful stratification.

The third important advance is that we did not stop at model construction, but used single-cell analysis to interpret the immune mechanism behind the model, with EIF5A2 emerging as a tumor-derived immune-regulatory candidate within the R-loop-related model. Prior single-cell studies in LSCC have already shown that this disease contains marked epithelial plasticity and complex immune heterogeneity, including multiple malignant epithelial states and functionally distinct lymphoid populations [34, 35]. Our study builds on that literature but takes a more mechanistic route: instead of using single-cell data only to describe heterogeneity, we used it to trace how a model-related tumor program may be implemented at the cellular level, then narrowed that program to EIF5A2, and finally linked it to CD8 T-cell dysfunction. This is an important difference from many prior signature papers, where single-cell data, if included at all, often serve as descriptive support rather than as a bridge between bulk-model performance and immune regulation. The broader conceptual basis for this strategy is also supported by recent studies showing that dysregulated R-loops can influence inflammation, immune evasion, and cell–cell signaling in cancer [5, 6, 23].

Finally, the identification of MIF-CD74-centered communication between tumor cells and exhausted CD8 T cells provides a mechanistically plausible endpoint that aligns well with the current HNSCC literature. Recent work has shown that MIF is not only broadly involved in tumor immunoregulation, but is also functionally important in HNSCC, where targeting MIF can reduce immunosuppression and improve antitumoral T-cell activity [36]. Related studies have further linked MIF-rich states to exhausted CD8 T cells and emphasized CD74-containing receptor complexes as relevant mediators of this biology [37, 38]. Therefore, our finding does not introduce MIF as a completely new pathway; rather, it places the EIF5A2-high, R-loop-related tumor state into an existing and biologically credible immunosuppressive framework. This is also why we view the present study less as a conventional prognostic-model paper and more as an integrated framework connecting R-loop-related tumor biology, prognosis prediction, immunotherapy stratification, and tumor-to-CD8 T-cell communication. At the same time, the model still requires prospective validation, and the EIF5A2–MIF-CD74–Tex axis remains computationally inferred rather than experimentally proven, which should be addressed in future work. Nevertheless, the EIF5A2–MIF–CD74–Tex axis remains computationally inferred and requires further experimental validation.

This study has several limitations. First, all analyses were based on retrospective public datasets and may therefore be affected by cohort heterogeneity and incomplete clinical information. Second, although the model showed reproducible prognostic and immunotherapy-related associations, its predictive performance remains insufficient for immediate clinical translation. In addition, the external validation cohorts were relatively small and differed in platform and survival endpoint, which may limit the generalizability of the model and warrants further validation in larger LSCC cohorts with consistent clinical endpoints. Third, although GSE210287 is not LSCC-specific, it is a head and neck squamous cell carcinoma neoadjuvant immunotherapy cohort and therefore provides relevant evidence in a related disease context; however, LSCC-specific validation is still required. Fourth, the identification of EIF5A2 as a core immune-regulatory gene and the proposed MIF-CD74-mediated communication with exhausted CD8 T cells were inferred from computational analyses and still require experimental validation. Therefore, the present findings should be regarded as a biologically plausible and clinically relevant framework rather than definitive mechanistic proof. Notably, the gene signature alone showed moderate predictive performance, whereas the combined nomogram model achieved better discrimination than either the clinical model or the signature alone. Therefore, the R-loop-related signature should be interpreted as a biologically informative risk stratification component rather than a standalone high-accuracy clinical prediction tool.
