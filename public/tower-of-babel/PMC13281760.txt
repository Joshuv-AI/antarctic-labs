CLDN18.2-Directed Therapeutics in Gastric and Gastroesophageal Junction Adenocarcinoma: Biomarker Assessment, Expression Dynamics, and Treatment Sequencing
Authors: Jiadi Liu, Yufei Liu, Yu Du, Gang Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractClaudin-18 isoform 2 (CLDN18.2) has rapidly moved from a gastric-lineage surface antigen to an actionable therapeutic target in advanced gastric and gastroesophageal junction (G/GEJ) adenocarcinoma, following the SPOTLIGHT and GLOW Phase 3 trials, which established zolbetuximab plus fluoropyrimidine–platinum chemotherapy as a first-line option for patients with CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative disease. While many existing CLDN18.2 reviews have centered on target biology, assay development, or individual therapeutic platforms, this review focuses on how CLDN18.2-directed therapies can be selected and sequenced within a multi-biomarker landscape in which CLDN18.2 expression is heterogeneous and treatment-modifiable, and its clinical interpretation is modality-dependent. At the same time, the field has expanded beyond conventional monoclonal antibodies to include antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs), bispecific antibodies, and chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapies, each with distinct implications for biomarker threshold requirements, toxicity, and clinical positioning. A central unresolved issue is therefore no longer whether CLDN18.2 can be therapeutically targeted, but how different CLDN18.2-directed modalities should be positioned across treatment lines and after prior CLDN18.2 exposure. This review examines the biologic rationale for CLDN18.2 targeting, current approaches to assay interpretation and patient selection, and the available clinical evidence for the major therapeutic platforms. Particular emphasis is placed on three practice-relevant issues: heterogeneity and sampling in biomarker assessment, treatment-associated modulation of CLDN18.2 expression, and the consequences of these dynamics for post-zolbetuximab decision-making. We also compare next-generation platforms according to mechanistic class, expression-threshold assumptions, toxicity trade-offs, and feasibility of integration into real-world treatment algorithms. Collectively, current evidence supports a reassessment-based rather than assumption-based approach to CLDN18.2 sequencing. However, key gaps remain, including the optimal interface with PD-1-based first-line therapy, the geographic generalizability of several next-generation datasets, standardized retesting strategies at progression, and prospective validation of modality-specific sequencing after target modulation.

Introduction

Gastric cancer remains a formidable global health burden. According to the GLOBOCAN 2022 estimates, gastric cancer ranks as the fifth most commonly diagnosed malignancy and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, with 968,350 new cases and 659,853 deaths in 2022.1 For patients with advanced, unresectable, or metastatic G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, first-line systemic therapy has increasingly moved from chemotherapy alone toward biomarker-stratified combinations, although durable disease control remains limited in many patients. This shift has made treatment selection dependent on a baseline biomarker matrix rather than on a single therapeutic target.

For patients with advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma without HER2-positive disease, several phase 3 trials have established PD-1 inhibitor plus fluoropyrimidine–platinum chemotherapy as a major first-line therapeutic backbone. CheckMate 649 showed improved survival with nivolumab plus chemotherapy over chemotherapy alone, with benefit enriched in PD-L1 CPS–defined populations; KEYNOTE-859 showed an overall survival advantage with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in HER2-negative advanced gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma; RATIONALE-305 showed improved overall survival with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy, with benefit most evident in patients with PD-L1 TAP ≥5% while also meeting the endpoint in the overall randomized population. These studies define the immunotherapy-based competitive background against which first-line zolbetuximab must now be positioned, especially when CLDN18.2 positivity coexists with PD-L1-high or MSI-H/dMMR biology.2–4

HER2 status remains an independent first-line decision node. In HER2-positive disease, the first-line pathway is anchored by HER2-directed therapy; CLDN18.2 co-expression, when present, is best interpreted as a potential later-line or trial-relevant feature rather than as a reason to displace the HER2-directed pathway. In HER2-negative disease, by contrast, CLDN18.2 positivity, PD-L1 expression, and MSI/MMR status may create overlapping treatment rationales, particularly when zolbetuximab eligibility coexists with strong immunotherapy-relevant biology. These intersections mean that CLDN18.2 should be assessed as part of the baseline multi-biomarker matrix, not as an isolated target considered after first-line therapy has already been chosen.2–8

Within this context, CLDN18.2 has emerged as one of the most clinically advanced non-HER2 targets in gastric oncology. First proposed as a therapeutic antibody target by Sahin et al in 2008,9 CLDN18.2 is a tight junction protein with expression tightly restricted to differentiated gastric mucosal epithelial cells under normal physiological conditions. During malignant transformation, disruption of cellular polarity and tight junction architecture exposes CLDN18.2 epitopes on the surface of tumor cells, rendering them accessible to therapeutic antibodies while maintaining relative inaccessibility on normal gastric tissue.10 This combination of lineage-restricted expression and tumor-selective surface exposure provides CLDN18.2 with a favorable therapeutic window that distinguishes it from most conventional oncogenic targets.

The clinical validation of CLDN18.2 as a targetable biomarker was established through the SPOTLIGHT and GLOW phase 3 trials, which together showed that adding zolbetuximab to fluoropyrimidine–platinum chemotherapy improved progression-free and overall survival in patients with CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma. These data supported regulatory approvals in multiple jurisdictions and established zolbetuximab-based therapy as the current first-line CLDN18.2-directed option in this biomarker-defined population.11,12

Nevertheless, the CLDN18.2 therapeutic landscape is becoming increasingly heterogeneous in both modality and clinical positioning. Zolbetuximab carries three practical constraints: eligibility is restricted to tumors with high CLDN18.2 expression (IHC 2+/3+ membranous staining in ≥75% of tumor cells; ~38% of screened patients), on-target gastrointestinal toxicity (notably nausea and vomiting) is frequent, and CLDN18.2 expression above the ≥75% cut-off may decline after zolbetuximab-containing therapy—potentially affecting eligibility and efficacy of subsequent CLDN18.2-directed strategies and supporting reassessment at progression.11–13 Against this background, a diverse portfolio of next-generation CLDN18.2-targeting modalities—including ADCs with bystander-killing capacity at lower expression thresholds, bispecific antibodies harnessing T-cell or costimulatory pathways, and CAR-T cell therapies offering direct cytolytic activity—has entered clinical development with the potential to address these shortcomings.

Because recent CLDN18.2 publications have already reviewed target biology, assay development, and individual therapeutic classes in detail, the contribution of this review is a sequencing-oriented synthesis rather than another agent-by-agent catalogue. We use the contemporary first-line biomarker landscape as the entry point to examine how CLDN18.2 testing should be interpreted alongside HER2, PD-L1, and MSI/MMR status; how expression heterogeneity and treatment-associated modulation affect eligibility; and how modality-specific thresholds, toxicity trade-offs, feasibility, and evidence maturity should inform post-zolbetuximab decision-making. This framing treats CLDN18.2 as a dynamic therapeutic variable and supports reassessment-based rather than assumption-based sequencing.

Molecular Biology of CLDN18.2 as a Therapeutic Target

The Claudin Family and Tight Junction Physiology

Claudins are tight-junction transmembrane proteins that help maintain epithelial barrier function and apicobasal polarity.14,15 For this review, however, the central biologic point is not general claudin physiology per se, but why CLDN18.2 is therapeutically tractable: it is a gastric-lineage isoform normally confined to differentiated gastric epithelium, yet becomes more accessible on tumour cells when polarity and junctional architecture are disrupted. This restricted physiologic distribution together with tumour-associated surface exposure underlies both the therapeutic window and the on-target gastrointestinal liability of CLDN18.2-directed therapies.16

CLDN18.2 Expression in Malignancy: Retention, Exposure, and Ectopic Expression

In normal gastric epithelium, CLDN18.2 is sequestered within the tight junction complex at the apicolateral membrane, rendering it largely inaccessible to circulating antibodies. During malignant transformation, however, loss of epithelial polarity and tight-junction disorganization can promote redistribution and surface exposure of CLDN18.2, making it available for therapeutic targeting on tumour cells. This represents a change in spatial accessibility rather than de novo tumour-specific antigen expression: CLDN18.2 remains a lineage-associated antigen whose therapeutic tractability increases as epithelial architecture is lost.16

Quantitative assessments of CLDN18.2 expression in gastric cancer have yielded variable results, owing in part to heterogeneity in immunohistochemical methodology and scoring criteria. Reported CLDN18.2 prevalence varies substantially according to antibody clone, staining platform, and positivity threshold.17 In large therapy-aligned datasets using the VENTANA 43–14A assay with a ≥75% cutoff, CLDN18.2 positivity is observed in approximately 38–44% of gastric/GEJ adenocarcinomas and is somewhat more frequent in diffuse-type than in intestinal-type tumours; however, it remains common across Lauren subtypes and therefore cannot be inferred reliably from histology alone.18 Geographic variation has been reported, but cross-cohort estimates remain difficult to compare in the absence of harmonized testing and should therefore be interpreted cautiously.17

Beyond gastric cancer, CLDN18.2 expression has also been reported in selected other epithelial malignancies. These observations have supported exploratory development of CLDN18.2-directed therapies beyond gastric/GEJ disease, although the strongest clinical validation to date remains in gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

CLDN18.2 as a Non-Oncogenic, Lineage-Associated Target

A fundamental distinction between CLDN18.2 and conventional oncogenic targets (for example, HER2, EGFR, or FGFR2) lies in how the biomarker relates to tumor biology and patient selection. In gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma, canonical oncogenic targets are more commonly anchored to recurrent genomic activation events—most often gene amplification or other recurrent molecular abnormalities identified through molecular profiling19—whereas CLDN18.2 is selected on the basis of IHC-defined expression of a gastric-lineage tight-junction protein.9 Importantly, this “surface-lineage target” concept should be distinguished from CLDN18 genomic rearrangements, including CLDN18–ARHGAP26/6 fusions, which represent a distinct fusion-defined molecular subset associated with diffuse/signet-ring phenotypes.20 These fusions are relevant to molecular taxonomy and invasive biology, but they should not be conflated with the IHC-defined CLDN18.2 surface-expression biomarker used for zolbetuximab or next-generation CLDN18.2-directed therapies. Their implications for CLDN18.2-targeted treatment selection remain uncertain and should be treated as a parallel molecular-biology literature rather than as a validated therapeutic-selection framework. More broadly, dysregulated claudin biology can modulate oncogenic signaling programs, epithelial plasticity, and immune phenotypes in a context- and isoform-dependent manner; however, for CLDN18.2 these putative secondary roles should be interpreted cautiously and their clinical significance remains to be fully elucidated.21

This biology has practical therapeutic implications. Because patient selection depends on IHC thresholds and sampling, intratumoral heterogeneity can materially affect classification: in a 400-patient metastatic/recurrent/unresectable cohort, 61.3% of tumors with CLDN18.2 positivity in at least one of four tissue cores showed heterogeneous expression across cores, underscoring the need for careful assay interpretation and adequate tissue representation.22 Mechanistically, zolbetuximab is a chimeric IgG1 monoclonal antibody that targets exposed CLDN18.2 epitopes and mediates specific killing of CLDN18.2-positive cells through immune effector mechanisms, consistent with reliance on effector functions rather than direct inhibition of a kinase pathway.23 More generally, payload-delivery strategies such as ADCs exploit surface targets but require distinct pharmacological and toxicity-management considerations.24

Molecular Correlates and Biomarker Overlap

The relationship between CLDN18.2 expression and selected biomarker-defined gastric cancer subsets has been explored in several correlative analyses. In contemporary correlative cohorts, CLDN18.2 positivity has been associated with EBV-positive and PD-L1-positive tumors in some datasets, although the exact pattern of biomarker overlap varies across cohorts.25 These overlaps are therapeutically relevant because they provide a biologic rationale for evaluating combination strategies with CLDN18.2-targeted agents and immune checkpoint inhibitors. In addition, overlap between CLDN18.2 and HER2 appears limited rather than absent, suggesting that CLDN18.2-positive disease may represent a largely—though not completely—distinct therapeutic subset from HER2-positive disease.18 This limited but non-zero overlap is clinically important because it means CLDN18.2 cannot be interpreted in isolation at baseline; rather, it must be positioned within a broader first-line biomarker matrix that includes HER2 and PD-1-relevant biology.

Detection, Scoring, and Patient Selection

Immunohistochemical Assessment: Methodology and Thresholds

For approved zolbetuximab, patient selection currently relies on immunohistochemical assessment of tumour tissue. The VENTANA CLDN18 (43–14A) RxDx Assay, used as the companion diagnostic for zolbetuximab, determines case status according to the proportion of viable tumour cells showing moderate-to-strong membranous CLDN18 staining, with positivity called at a ≥75% cutoff.17 In the SPOTLIGHT and GLOW trials, CLDN18.2 positivity was defined as ≥75% of tumour cells demonstrating moderate-to-strong (2+/3+) membranous staining; this cutoff identified approximately 38% of all assessable screened patients overall, and about 42–43% of HER2-negative assessable patients, as biomarker-positive.11,12 This stringent cutoff was selected to enrich for high-expression disease on the basis of earlier zolbetuximab data, but it also excludes patients with lower-level expression, in whom the likelihood of benefit has not been prospectively established.

The definition of CLDN18.2 positivity varies across clinical programs and therapeutic modalities, likely reflecting differences in mechanism of action, development strategy, and presumed expression-level requirements across agents.26–28 Some early-phase ADC programs have used lower thresholds than zolbetuximab trials. In the Phase 1 IBI343 study, CLDN18.2 positivity was defined as membranous staining of any intensity in ≥1% of tumour cells.26 In the published CMG901 phase 1 study, antitumour activity was reported both in the overall gastric/GEJ cohort and in a CLDN18.2-high subgroup defined as ≥2+ intensity in ≥20% of tumour cells.27 These efficacy-enriched subgroup thresholds should not be conflated with broader exploratory entry criteria used earlier in program development. Accordingly, eligibility thresholds and efficacy-enriched analytic thresholds should be interpreted separately when comparing ADC programs. In the pivotal satri-cel Phase 2 trial, eligibility required immunohistochemical expression intensity ≥2+ in ≥40% of tumour cells.28

Intratumoral Heterogeneity and Sampling Considerations

CLDN18.2 expression within gastric adenocarcinomas is frequently heterogeneous, both spatially within primary tumors and between primary and metastatic sites. This intratumoral heterogeneity poses a practical challenge for accurate biomarker assessment based on endoscopic biopsy specimens, which sample only a small fraction of the total tumor volume. Available journal data support obtaining multiple viable endoscopic biopsy fragments for CLDN18.2 assessment; single-biopsy evaluation has limited sensitivity, whereas performance improves substantially when six or more biopsies are sampled.29 Paired-sample studies indicate moderate rather than uniform concordance between primary and metastatic lesions, with discordance reported in approximately one-quarter of cases and variation across metastatic sites.30 Together with emerging evidence that CLDN18.2 expression above the ≥75% treatment cutoff can decline after zolbetuximab, these findings support considering re-biopsy and repeat CLDN18.2 assessment at disease progression when clinically feasible, particularly for treatment-sequencing decisions.13

Toward Standardized and Dynamic Assessment

The establishment of standardized immunostaining and scoring protocols is essential for generating prospectively comparable data across clinical trials and selecting patients for therapy. A clinically validated companion diagnostic assay (VENTANA CLDN18 [43–14A] RxDx) is available, but assay harmonization and interlaboratory quality assurance remain necessary for routine implementation.31,32 By contrast, robust journal-level evidence for non-invasive longitudinal monitoring remains limited, so tissue-based reassessment remains the more defensible approach when reevaluation is clinically required. The clinical implementation of such tools is an active area of investigation that holds particular relevance for the management of patients progressing on CLDN18.2-targeted therapy.

Monoclonal Antibodies: Zolbetuximab and Second-Generation Agents

Zolbetuximab: Mechanism of Action

Zolbetuximab (IMAB362) is a chimeric IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds to the first extracellular loop of CLDN18.2 with high affinity and specificity. Its antitumor activity is mediated primarily through two Fc-dependent immune effector mechanisms: ADCC, involving the recruitment and activation of natural killer (NK) cells and macrophages via Fcγ receptor engagement, and CDC, through the activation of the classical complement cascade.5,6,30 Preclinical studies demonstrated that zolbetuximab induces potent concentration-dependent ADCC and CDC activity against CLDN18.2-expressing gastric cancer cell lines, with the magnitude of cytotoxicity correlating with surface CLDN18.2 density.5

Pivotal Phase 3 Trials

The clinical development of zolbetuximab progressed through the phase IIa MONO study and the phase IIb FAST trial; MONO showed limited but measurable single-agent activity, whereas FAST showed improved progression-free and overall survival with zolbetuximab plus EOX versus EOX alone in CLDN18.2-positive disease, with greater benefit in higher-expression subgroups.23,33 Two concurrently conducted global phase 3 trials then provided the definitive efficacy evidence.

The SPOTLIGHT trial (NCT03504397) randomized 565 patients with CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma to receive zolbetuximab (800 mg/m2 loading dose, then 600 mg/m2 every 3 weeks) plus mFOLFOX6 or placebo plus mFOLFOX6. The study met both its primary endpoint of PFS (median 10.61 vs. 8.67 months; HR 0.75; 95% CI, 0.60–0.94; P = 0.0066) and the key secondary endpoint of OS (median 18.23 vs. 15.54 months; HR 0.75; 95% CI, 0.60–0.94; P = 0.0053); as an ad hoc analysis in patients with measurable disease, objective response rates were similar between arms (61% vs. 62%).11

The GLOW trial (NCT03653507) evaluated zolbetuximab plus CAPOX versus placebo plus CAPOX in 507 patients with the same eligibility criteria. PFS was significantly improved (median 8.21 vs. 6.80 months; HR 0.687; 95% CI, 0.544–0.866; P = 0.0007), as was OS (median 14.39 vs. 12.16 months; HR 0.771; 95% CI, 0.615–0.965; P = 0.0118).12 Cross-trial comparisons should be interpreted cautiously; the longer control-arm overall survival in SPOTLIGHT than in GLOW may partly relate to differences in regional enrollment, with SPOTLIGHT enrolling more patients from Japan and South Korea, whereas GLOW enrolled more patients from mainland China.11,12

A pooled meta-analysis of the FAST, SPOTLIGHT, and GLOW trials encompassing 1233 patients demonstrated significant extensions in both PFS (HR 0.64; 95% CI, 0.49–0.84; P < 0.01) and OS (HR 0.72; 95% CI, 0.62–0.83; P < 0.01) with the addition of zolbetuximab to chemotherapy.34

Toxicity Profile and on-Target Gastrointestinal Effects

Zolbetuximab is characterized by a predominantly upper gastrointestinal toxicity profile, consistent with on-target/off-tumour engagement of CLDN18.2 on intact gastric epithelium. In the Phase II ILUSTRO first-line cohort evaluating zolbetuximab plus mFOLFOX6 (n = 21), any-grade nausea and vomiting were reported in 90.5% and 66.7% of patients, respectively, whereas grade ≥3 events were less frequent (4.8% and 9.5%); nausea and vomiting were largely grade 1–2; nevertheless, dose interruptions were common, and nausea/vomiting were among adverse events leading to zolbetuximab discontinuation. In ILUSTRO, all patients received antiemetic premedication (including but not limited to NK-1 and 5-HT3 receptor antagonists), and zolbetuximab was delivered as a ≥2-hour infusion, highlighting that intensified prophylaxis and early infusion-day monitoring are integral to maintaining treatment continuity.35 Mechanistic and clinicopathologic evidence now directly links these symptoms to gastric mucosal injury: in a translational standard-of-care series incorporating paired upper endoscopy during zolbetuximab-based therapy, endoscopic changes consistent with gastritis (redness, erosion and/or superficial ulcers) were observed in ~90% of evaluated patients, and biopsies from tumour-uninvolved mucosa demonstrated erosive gastritis with preserved CLDN18.2 expression. Notably, patients were able to continue zolbetuximab-based treatment, and gastric injury often improved on subsequent assessments.36 Collectively, these data position zolbetuximab-associated nausea/vomiting as a predictable on-target liability that is usually manageable with proactive supportive care, yet sufficiently frequent to constitute a practical constraint—and therefore a key driver for next-generation CLDN18.2 formats designed to widen the therapeutic window.

Second-Generation Monoclonal Antibodies

Several next-generation anti-CLDN18.2 monoclonal antibodies are in early-phase clinical development (including Fc-engineered variants intended to enhance effector function), but any efficacy or tolerability advantage over zolbetuximab remains unproven. Osemitamab (TST001) is a humanized IgG1 anti-CLDN18.2 monoclonal antibody under clinical investigation, with Fc optimization reported to enhance immune effector mechanisms (eg, ADCC/CDC).37 However, the frequently cited TranStar102 Cohort G activity readouts have so far been reported primarily in conference-abstract form rather than as a dedicated peer-reviewed clinical trial manuscript, and therefore remains insufficient as core evidence for efficacy claims. Conceptually, this CLDN18.2 antibody–chemotherapy–PD-1 triplet strategy differs from the chemotherapy-backbone paradigm established by the pivotal Phase III zolbetuximab program; however, zolbetuximab development has not been limited to chemotherapy combinations, because phase II ILUSTRO also evaluated zolbetuximab in combination with pembrolizumab.35 Whether such triplet strategies confer meaningful benefit over established doublet approaches remains uncertain and requires confirmation in randomized trials.

Antibody–Drug Conjugates: Precision Payload Delivery

Rationale and Classification

ADCs represent one of the most rapidly expanding modalities in the CLDN18.2-targeting portfolio. By combining target-specific antibody binding with antibody-guided delivery of potent cytotoxic payloads, ADCs primarily mediate tumor cell killing through payload release following antigen binding and internalization, and are therefore generally less reliant on Fc-dependent effector mechanisms (such as ADCC and CDC) than unconjugated antibodies. ADC-mediated cytotoxicity typically proceeds through antigen-mediated internalization and lysosomal processing, where linker cleavage and/or proteolytic catabolism releases the cytotoxic warhead within the tumor cell. An additional mechanism of particular relevance to CLDN18.2-heterogeneous tumors is the bystander effect, which can occur for ADCs with cleavable linkers and membrane-permeable payloads, whereby released payload diffuses into neighboring cells and kills adjacent antigen-negative tumor cells. This property could, in principle, mitigate the impact of intratumoral CLDN18.2 heterogeneity and may broaden activity in lower-expression settings, although this hypothesis requires clinical validation.38,39

CLDN18.2-targeting ADCs can be broadly grouped by payload class into topoisomerase I inhibitor (TOP1i)-based agents (eg, IBI343; SHR-A1904) and MMAE-based agents (eg, CMG901; tecotabart vedotin [LM-302]).26,27,40,41 These payload classes—together with linker chemistry and Fc engineering—tend to differ in cytotoxic mechanisms, bystander-effect potential, and adverse-event profiles.

IBI343: An Fc-Silenced Topoisomerase I Inhibitor ADC

IBI343 is a CLDN18.2-targeting ADC and, to our knowledge, the first reported to incorporate an Fc-silenced antibody backbone designed to attenuate Fcγ receptor binding and downstream Fc-mediated effector functions (ADCC/CDC), together with an exatecan-derivative topoisomerase I inhibitor payload conjugated via site-specific glycol conjugation at a drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) of 4.

The recommended phase 2 dose of IBI343 was determined to be 6 mg/kg every 3 weeks, yielding a confirmed objective response rate of 29.0% and a median progression-free survival of 5.5 months in patients with CLDN18.2-high (2+/3+ ≥75%) G/GEJ adenocarcinoma in the phase 1 dose-escalation/dose-expansion study. The confirmed ORR was 29.0% (95% CI, 14.2–48.0) and the disease control rate was 90.3% (95% CI, 74.2–98.0), with a median duration of response of 5.6 months (95% CI, 2.8–7.0). Grade ≥3 nausea and vomiting were infrequent (nausea 1.7% and vomiting 2.6% at 6 mg/kg), and no interstitial lung disease was reported. Cross-trial comparisons should be interpreted cautiously; in the IBI343 phase 1 report, the authors noted that the CMG901 phase 1 dose-expansion experience reported higher nausea/vomiting rates despite intensive prophylaxis, whereas numerically lower nausea/vomiting rates were observed with IBI343. This difference is hypothesis-generating and may reflect differences in antiemetic prophylaxis and/or the Fc-silenced design, rather than a definitive causal effect.26

In the same report, a randomized phase 3 study comparing IBI343 versus investigator’s choice chemotherapy in previously treated, CLDN18.2-high, HER2-negative G/GEJ adenocarcinoma has been initiated (G-HOPE-001; NCT06238843).

CMG901 (AZD0901): An MMAE-Based ADC

CMG901 is a CLDN18.2-targeting ADC comprising a humanized IgG1 antibody conjugated to MMAE through a protease-cleavable linker, with a DAR of approximately 4. Preclinical studies showed that CMG901 can mediate ADCC and CDC in addition to MMAE-driven cytotoxicity and bystander killing, consistent with an IgG1 backbone without Fc-silencing.42

At a median follow-up of 9.0 months, the confirmed objective response rate was 28% (32/113; 95% CI, 20–38) among patients with gastric or gastro-oesophageal junction cancer in the 2.2–3.0 mg/kg cohort (full analysis set across dose-escalation plus dose-expansion). Based on overall safety, activity, and pharmacokinetics, 2.2 mg/kg every 3 weeks was proposed as the recommended phase 2 dose. Importantly, the ≥20% definition used for the CLDN18.2-high subgroup was an efficacy-enrichment analysis threshold within the published phase 1 dataset and should not be conflated with broader exploratory entry thresholds used elsewhere in program development.27 Among 93 patients with CLDN18.2-high tumours (≥2+ intensity in ≥20% of tumour cells), the confirmed ORR was 33% (31/93; 95% CI, 24–44), with a median duration of response of 7.9 months (95% CI, 5.3–12.1). In the dose-expansion cohort (n=107), grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 68% (73/107); the most common grade 3–4 events were neutrophil count decreased (22/107; 21%), anaemia (15/107; 14%), and vomiting (11/107; 10%). Peripheral neuropathy (all hypoaesthesia) occurred in 20% of patients (21/107), predominantly grade 1–2 (17 grade 1; four grade 2).27

CMG901 has been granted an exclusive, global out-license to AstraZeneca to manufacture, develop, and commercialise, and is now referred to as AZD0901. AZD0901 has advanced to phase 3 evaluation in CLDN18.2-expressing advanced gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma; detailed trial design (comparator and line of therapy) should be confirmed in the full peer-reviewed report describing the phase 3 protocol before being stated.

Other ADCs in Clinical Development

LM-302 (TPX-4589) is an anti-CLDN18.2 ADC utilizing the same MMAE payload as CMG901 but with distinct antibody and linker characteristics. In a Bayesian phase 1/2 trial, tecotabart vedotin (LM-302) demonstrated antitumor activity in previously treated patients with CLDN18.2-positive (per study definition; IHC ≥2+ in ≥50% of tumour cells) advanced G/GEJ cancer.41 A published case report described a marked response to LM-302 in a multiply refractory gastric cancer patient, followed by surgical resection of residual metastatic disease with pathologic no residual tumor and ongoing disease-free status on follow-up; however, this outcome reflects combined systemic therapy and surgery rather than LM-302 monotherapy alone.43

JS107 is a CLDN18.2-targeting ADC in early clinical development, and combination strategies with immune checkpoint blockade plus chemotherapy are being explored; however, peer-reviewed full-length trial data supporting quantitative first-line efficacy estimates for JS107-based regimens are not yet available. Accordingly, JS107-based combinations should be presented here as an emerging hypothesis for ADC–immunotherapy synergy rather than as an established high-response benchmark, pending peer-reviewed full publication and randomized confirmation.

Comparative Analysis: A Clinical Framework for Positioning ADCs

The most clinically useful way to compare CLDN18.2-targeting ADCs is not as a generic ADC class, but as candidate post-zolbetuximab or broader-expression options defined by three practical variables: biomarker threshold, payload-related toxicity, and the extent to which Fc effector function is retained. In this framework, agents such as IBI343 and CMG901 should be viewed less as interchangeable members of one class and more as distinct pharmacologic strategies being developed under different assumptions about tolerability and expression dependence.26,27

From a treatment-selection perspective, topoisomerase I inhibitor–based ADCs and MMAE-based ADCs may occupy different clinical niches. Fc-silenced topoisomerase I inhibitor ADCs may be attractive when reducing zolbetuximab-like upper gastrointestinal toxicity is a priority, although any such advantage remains indirect rather than proven. By contrast, MMAE-based ADCs may offer potent cytotoxic and bystander activity, but peripheral neuropathy and cytopenias can become more clinically prominent. Thus, the relevant question is not whether one ADC design is globally superior, but which toxicity trade-off is more acceptable for a given patient.

Biomarker threshold is equally important. Several ADC programs have enrolled patients at lower CLDN18.2 thresholds than those used for zolbetuximab, which broadens eligibility conceptually; however, broader entry criteria should not be mistaken for established efficacy in truly low-expression disease. Current data more strongly support ADC development as a way to expand the actionable CLDN18.2 space beyond the zolbetuximab companion-diagnostic population than as proof that minimal-expression tumours derive comparable benefit.26,27

Accordingly, ADCs are best positioned at present as the leading next-generation systemic CLDN18.2 modality, particularly in the post-zolbetuximab setting or in patients for whom broader threshold-enrolling trials are available. However, their exact role relative to zolbetuximab, chemotherapy, and other CLDN18.2-directed modalities remains unresolved because head-to-head randomized comparative data are not yet available.

Bispecific Antibodies: Immune Bridging Strategies

Mechanistic Rationale

CLDN18.2-targeting bispecific antibodies exploit immune-bridging strategies that are mechanistically related but not identical, including CD3-based T-cell redirection and conditional costimulatory activation exemplified by CLDN18.2×4-1BB constructs. Unlike Fc-active anti-CLDN18.2 monoclonal antibodies, which substantially rely on endogenous effector mechanisms such as ADCC and CDC, CD3-redirecting bispecific antibodies can trigger T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity without requiring NK-cell or complement effector function; by contrast, CLDN18.2×4-1BB bispecifics are designed to conditionally enhance local T-cell activation rather than simply mimic CD3 redirection.44,45 These properties provide a mechanistic rationale for CLDN18.2-directed immune-bridging strategies in gastric cancer, although any clinical efficacy or tolerability advantage over conventional anti-CLDN18.2 antibodies remains unproven.

CLDN18.2 × CD3 Bispecific Antibodies

IBI389, a CLDN18.2 × CD3 bispecific T-cell engager developed by Innovent Biologics, has been evaluated in a phase 1 dose-escalation and expansion study. Among 27 patients with GC/GEJC whose tumours expressed CLDN18.2 at IHC 2+/3+ in ≥10% of cells, IBI389 achieved an ORR of 22.2% (6/27), a DCR of 74.1% (20/27), and a median PFS of 4.3 months; CRS occurred in 59.5% of the overall safety population, with only one grade 3 event (0.8%), and no neurotoxicity or treatment-related deaths were reported.46 These phase 1 data support the clinical feasibility and preliminary antitumor activity of CLDN18.2-directed T-cell engagement, with a median overall survival of 10.3 months in the biomarker-positive GC/GEJC subgroup and CRS representing the principal immune-mediated toxicity observed to date; importantly, CRS severity decreased with repeated dosing.

CLDN18.2 × 4-1BB Conditional Costimulatory Agonists

Givastomig represents a mechanistically novel approach: rather than directly redirecting T cells to tumour cells, this bispecific antibody co-engages CLDN18.2 on tumour cells and 4–1BB on adjacent immune cells, particularly activated T cells, thereby delivering CLDN18.2-dependent costimulatory activation within the tumour microenvironment. This conditional activation paradigm is intended to enhance T-cell effector function while limiting systemic immune and hepatic toxicity, a major constraint in the development of systemically administered 4–1BB agonists.47 In the first-in-human phase 1 study, part 2 was designed to evaluate givastomig in combination with nivolumab and fluoropyrimidine/oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy in frontline metastatic CLDN18.2-positive gastroesophageal carcinoma; that combination cohort was ongoing and not reported in the publication.45 By coupling CLDN18.2 targeting with spatially restricted immune costimulation, this approach provides a biologic rationale for combination with checkpoint blockade; however, clinical confirmation of synergistic benefit remains pending.

CAR-T Cell Therapy: The Most Clinically Advanced CLDN18.2-Directed Cellular Modality

Preclinical Rationale and CAR Design

CAR T-cell therapy represents a highly active CLDN18.2-directed cellular immunotherapy modality. CLDN18.2-specific CAR-T cells are generated by ex vivo genetic engineering of autologous T lymphocytes to express a synthetic receptor containing a CLDN18.2-binding single-chain variable fragment linked to intracellular activation and costimulatory modules; published preclinical CLDN18.2 CAR constructs in gastric cancer used a second-generation design. Upon antigen engagement, these cells mediate direct, antigen-dependent cytotoxicity and cytokine release against CLDN18.2-expressing tumour cells.48

Satricabtagene Autoleucel (Satri-Cel/CT041): Clinical Evidence

Satricabtagene autoleucel (satri-cel, formerly CT041), developed by CARsgen Therapeutics, is the most clinically advanced CLDN18.2-targeting CAR-T cell product. Phase 1 interim results in patients with CLDN18.2-positive gastrointestinal cancers, published in Nature Medicine, demonstrated promising activity in the gastric cancer subgroup (ORR 57.1%; median PFS 3.7 months), with grade 1–2 CRS occurring in 94.6% of treated patients and no grade ≥3 CRS reported.49 The phase 1 final analysis further supported the therapeutic potential of satri-cel, with sustained antitumor activity and a manageable safety profile.50

The randomized phase 2 CT041-ST-01 trial moved satri-cel beyond proof-of-concept and provides the strongest peer-reviewed evidence so far for a CLDN18.2-directed cellular approach in advanced gastric cancer. Even so, satri-cel is best described as a randomized phase 2-supported, investigational late-line cellular strategy for previously treated CLDN18.2-positive G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, rather than as a routine standard parallel to approved off-the-shelf systemic agents. Its future positioning will depend on external validation, access, manufacturing logistics, and the extent to which benefit can be reproduced outside the original trial context.28

Safety Considerations and Challenges in Solid Tumors

In CT041-ST-01, the most common grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events related to treatment were decreased lymphocyte, white blood cell, and neutrophil counts, and CRS occurred in 95% of treated patients. In the phase 1 final analysis, CRS occurred in 96.9% of patients and all events were grade 1–2. Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 99% of the satri-cel arm; this pattern was numerically dominated by cytopenias and is compatible with expected toxicity from the lymphodepleting/preconditioning regimen, although this interpretation is inferred from the reported event spectrum rather than from a formally separated attribution analysis.28,50

The fundamental challenges confronting CAR-T therapy in solid tumours—including restricted trafficking or intratumoral infiltration, antigen heterogeneity, the immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment, and T-cell dysfunction or exhaustion—remain relevant for CLDN18.2-directed CAR-T cells.51 The CT041-ST-01 trial was conducted in China, and the generalisability of these results to non-Chinese populations remains to be established.28 The logistical complexity of autologous CAR-T therapy—including leukapheresis, ex vivo manufacture and expansion, product release and transport, lymphodepletion, and infusion—also poses substantial barriers to broad clinical implementation relative to off-the-shelf ADC or antibody therapies.52

CLDN18.2 Expression Dynamics and Resistance Mechanisms

Treatment-Induced Expression Changes: An Emerging Clinical Problem

The temporal stability of CLDN18.2 expression during and after zolbetuximab-containing therapy has emerged as a clinically relevant question with direct implications for treatment sequencing. Unlike HER2, which is a canonical oncogenic driver commonly identified through amplification-based testing, CLDN18.2 is a lineage-associated tight-junction protein identified by IHC-defined surface expression in gastric epithelial differentiation. This biological distinction supports consideration of post-treatment reassessment of CLDN18.2.16

A retrospective correlative study by Yamamoto et al evaluated CLDN18.2 expression dynamics in paired tumour samples obtained at baseline and at disease progression after zolbetuximab-containing therapy. Among 65 treated patients, 15 had assessable paired samples at disease progression; in this subset, CLDN18.2 positivity, defined as IHC 2+/3+ staining in ≥75% of tumour cells, was lost in 53.3% of cases, whereas 66.7% and 73.3% of patients retained CLDN18.2 positivity when 40% and 25% cutoffs were applied, respectively.13 Representative cases illustrated intrapatient fluctuation in CLDN18.2 expression, including decline during treatment with partial recovery at disease progression in one case; however, these observations do not establish cohort-level reversibility of CLDN18.2 loss.

Biological Mechanisms of Expression Modulation

The mechanistic basis for treatment-induced CLDN18.2 expression change remains uncertain, and current explanations should be regarded as inferential rather than established. EMT-related epithelial plasticity is a plausible conceptual model because CLDN18.2 is linked to epithelial differentiation and junctional integrity; however, direct evidence assigning post-zolbetuximab target loss primarily to EMT is lacking.56 Likewise, treatment-driven selection is biologically reasonable given the antigen-density dependence of zolbetuximab-mediated cytotoxicity, but this mechanism has not been prospectively demonstrated in paired translational datasets.57

At present, several contributors may operate simultaneously, including baseline intratumoural heterogeneity, sampling effects, lineage plasticity, and therapy-associated selection. Available single-cell and transcriptomic observations are therefore better interpreted as hypothesis-generating correlates than as mechanistic proof of a dominant resistance pathway.58

Tumor Microenvironment Remodeling in CLDN18.2-Positive Tumors

Current translational studies suggest that CLDN18.2-positive tumours may show stromal, fibrotic, and immunoregulatory features with possible relevance to therapeutic sensitivity, particularly in the setting of systemic therapy and chemo-immunotherapy exposure.58,59 These findings link CLDN18.2 biology with the broader gastric cancer tumor microenvironment rather than a purely static surface-marker model. Gene-expression and single-cell analyses indicate associations with fibrotic programmes, suppressive immune-cell interactions, and therapy-associated microenvironmental remodeling, although these studies do not establish that such features specifically mediate resistance to zolbetuximab, ADCs, bispecific antibodies, or CAR-T therapy.58,59

Taken together, current data suggest that the CLDN18.2-positive microenvironment may influence treatment responsiveness and immunotherapy integration, but they do not yet define a CLDN18.2-specific resistant microenvironment. Prospective biomarker-embedded studies linking serial CLDN18.2 assessment, TME profiling, and treatment outcomes remain needed before these features can be used to guide modality selection.

Implications for Subsequent CLDN18.2-Directed Therapy

Although CLDN18.2 expression frequently declines after zolbetuximab-based therapy, some tumours retain residual expression below the ≥75% cutoff used for zolbetuximab eligibility.13 This observation supports reassessment rather than assumption. When repeat biopsy is feasible, subsequent CLDN18.2-directed treatment should be considered in the context of the threshold required by the intended modality, tissue adequacy, and the degree of spatial or temporal heterogeneity. ADC and CAR-T programs have used broader clinical eligibility thresholds than zolbetuximab in at least some studies, which provides a biologic rationale for further investigation in residual-expression disease; however, direct post-zolbetuximab clinical evidence remains limited, and activity in very low-expression disease has not been established.26–28 Accordingly, repeat tissue-based reassessment should be prioritized for post-zolbetuximab decision-making, whereas liquid-biopsy-based CLDN18.2 selection remains investigational.60

Decisions should instead move through five practical nodes: baseline biomarker definition, first-line prioritization within a broader biomarker matrix, biomarker reassessment at progression, post-zolbetuximab modality selection, and management of biomarker-complex populations. Baseline Decision Node: Define CLDN18.2 Within a Broader Biomarker Matrix

At diagnosis of advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, clinicians should interpret CLDN18.2 within a baseline biomarker matrix that includes HER2, PD-L1, and MSI/MMR status, with other guideline-relevant biomarkers assessed according to local practice. CLDN18.2 functions as a treatment-enabling surface biomarker rather than a standalone dominant driver, so its clinical meaning depends on line of therapy, assay threshold, and the competing implications of coexisting biomarkers.

First-Line Considerations

In previously untreated CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative disease, zolbetuximab plus fluoropyrimidine–platinum chemotherapy currently provides the only randomized phase 3 and regulatory-supported CLDN18.2-directed first-line option.11,12 This evidence base does not establish superiority over PD-1 inhibitor–chemotherapy in biomarker-overlap scenarios, because SPOTLIGHT/GLOW and CheckMate 649/KEYNOTE-859/RATIONALE-305 did not directly compare these strategies in a CLDN18.2-positive, PD-L1-positive population.2–4,11,12

The most difficult first-line scenario is CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative disease with strong immunotherapy-relevant features, particularly high PD-L1 expression and/or MSI-H/dMMR status. Here, CLDN18.2 supports zolbetuximab-based chemotherapy, whereas PD-L1 expression and MSI/MMR status support a PD-1-based pathway. Until randomized data define hierarchy, sequence, or combination strategy, treatment selection should weigh the strength of the PD-1 indication, CLDN18.2 expression level and assay confidence, expected gastrointestinal toxicity, autoimmune risk, disease tempo, regulatory availability, and trial access. In HER2-positive disease, HER2-directed treatment remains the primary first-line pathway; CLDN18.2 co-expression is better reserved for later reassessment or trial eligibility than used to displace HER2-based treatment.

Progression After First-Line Zolbetuximab: Reassess Before Resequencing

At progression after zolbetuximab-containing therapy, clinicians should first determine whether CLDN18.2 remains present at a level and pattern relevant to the next modality. Re-biopsy is therefore most useful when prior tissue was limited, the disease course suggests biologic shift, or enrollment in a threshold-dependent trial is being considered.13,17,22,29,30 Repeat biopsy can confirm retained target expression, characterize lower or more heterogeneous expression, and ensure that tissue adequacy matches the assay requirements of the intended study or agent.

Post-Zolbetuximab Modality Selection

If repeat assessment shows clearly retained CLDN18.2 expression and the patient is trial-eligible, ADCs currently offer the most pragmatic next systemic platform because they are off-the-shelf, relatively advanced in development, and often less tied to the ≥75% threshold used for zolbetuximab.26,27 Bispecific antibodies remain trial-based options when T-cell redirection or conditional costimulation is clinically plausible, but their sequencing role is less mature.45,46 For patients with retained expression after at least two prior lines, satri-cel is best viewed as a randomized phase 2-supported investigational cellular strategy rather than a routine standard parallel to approved off-the-shelf drugs.28 Its use depends on disease tempo, manufacturing feasibility, lymphodepletion tolerance, access, and patient willingness to accept autologous cell-therapy logistics. If repeat biopsy shows very low, highly heterogeneous, or indeterminate CLDN18.2 expression, further CLDN18.2-directed therapy becomes less biologically compelling, and treatment should shift toward non-CLDN18.2 standards or other biomarker-guided options.

Special Populations and Governing Principles

In biomarker-complex disease, trial participation should be preferred over empiric dual-target layering, because evidence-based combination and sequence rules are still lacking. Across treatment lines, four principles should guide interpretation: CLDN18.2 positivity does not confer perpetual eligibility across modalities; early-phase activity cannot be extrapolated across non-interchangeable thresholds; biologic rationale should remain separate from clinical proof; and CLDN18.2 sequencing should follow reassessment rather than a fixed within-class ladder.

Challenges and Future Directions

Standardization of CLDN18.2 Assessment

The heterogeneity in CLDN18.2 positivity definitions across clinical programs—ranging from ≥1% of tumour cells with membranous staining of any intensity, to ≥40% of tumour cells with ≥2+ staining, to ≥75% of tumour cells with moderate-to-strong membranous staining—creates a fragmented biomarker landscape that complicates cross-trial interpretation and clinical decision-making. Future standardization should be anchored not only in harmonized reporting thresholds but also in analytically validated assays, interlaboratory concordance testing, and clearer separation of companion-diagnostic criteria from exploratory trial-entry definitions.17,31,32,61 Noninvasive dynamic monitoring of CLDN18.2 remains exploratory; a small proof-of-concept study supports the analytical feasibility of circulating tumour cell–based CLDN18.2 RNA detection, but prospective validation and demonstration of treatment-selection utility are still lacking.62

Overcoming Expression Heterogeneity and Target Loss

Intratumoral heterogeneity in CLDN18.2 expression, discordance across sampling sites, and dynamic modulation across treatment timepoints remain major obstacles to durable CLDN18.2-directed benefit.22,58,63 Several strategies are being explored to address these limitations: (i) ADC designs with preclinically demonstrated bystander killing to reduce the impact of adjacent CLDN18.2-negative cells; (ii) dual-target approaches that pair CLDN18.2 with a second tumour-associated antigen; (iii) armored or cytokine-resistant CAR-T designs intended to improve cellular fitness within an immunosuppressive microenvironment; and (iv) prospective biomarker-reassessment strategies to determine whether dynamic retesting can meaningfully guide modality selection across treatment lines. At present, each of these approaches remains investigational rather than practice-changing.

Integration with Established and Emerging Targets

Although CLDN18.2 expression has also been described in selected non-gastric epithelial malignancies, current therapeutic validation remains strongest in gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma. Accordingly, broader pan-cancer expansion is better viewed as a background development trajectory than as a key determinant of current treatment sequencing in gastric cancer.64–66

The future positioning of CLDN18.2-directed therapy is more likely to be shaped by its interface with established treatment-decision nodes than by an open-ended expansion of target lists. In practical terms, the most relevant competitive or complementary relationships are with HER2-directed therapy, PD-1-based strategies, and other biomarker-selected approaches that influence first-line or subsequent-line treatment selection in gastric cancer.5–8 CheckMate 649, KEYNOTE-859, and RATIONALE-305 define the competitive PD-1 inhibitor–chemotherapy backbone for many patients with HER2-negative advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, whereas SPOTLIGHT and GLOW define the zolbetuximab–chemotherapy pathway for CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative disease.2–4,11,12 Because these trials selected patients using different biomarkers and did not directly compare CLDN18.2-directed therapy with PD-1-based therapy in biomarker-overlap populations, cross-trial ranking is inappropriate.

The geographic distribution of current next-generation CLDN18.2 evidence also requires explicit caution. Several of the most mature peer-reviewed datasets for ADCs, bispecific antibodies, and CAR-T therapy have come from China-based or Asia-heavy development programs.26–28,46 This limitation applies across platforms rather than only to cellular therapy: ADC and bispecific antibody datasets may also be influenced by population structure, prior treatment exposure, regional treatment backbones, access pathways, and biomarker-testing workflows. At the same time, Japan-led and Asia-based translational studies of CLDN18.2 expression dynamics, mucosal toxicity, and tumour microenvironment features provide important complementary context for interpreting how this target behaves across treatment settings,13,36,58,59 while international assay-implementation studies help define how CLDN18.2 testing workflows should be interpreted across practice settings.17,31,32 Accordingly, globally developed programs and Western datasets remain important for external validation, but evidence weighting should continue to be guided by data maturity rather than program geography.

Future trials should therefore be designed around clinical decision points rather than around drug class expansion alone. Priority designs include biomarker-overlap first-line studies that stratify or randomize CLDN18.2-positive/HER2-negative patients by PD-L1 expression and MSI/MMR status; post-zolbetuximab studies that mandate baseline and progression biopsies to define retained, intermediate, or lost CLDN18.2 expression; and modality-specific trials that test whether ADCs, bispecific antibodies, or cellular therapies retain activity after prior CLDN18.2 exposure. Correlative programs should pair serial CLDN18.2 IHC with spatial or single-cell TME profiling, so that target expression, immune contexture, and treatment outcomes can be interpreted together. Such biomarker-embedded designs would move the field beyond descriptive target expansion toward clinically testable sequencing rules.

Exploratory Longer-Horizon Platforms

mRNA-based antibody-encoding therapeutics and vaccine-oriented immune strategies remain conceptually important but are not yet part of the actionable CLDN18.2 sequencing discussion. BNT141 provides preclinical proof of concept that in vivo production of an anti-CLDN18.2 antibody can be achieved using an mRNA–lipid nanoparticle platform, but peer-reviewed clinical efficacy data are not yet available.53 Likewise, vaccine and antigen-specific cellular immune approaches remain largely preclinical.54,55 Their relevance to the field is therefore strategic rather than immediate: they may eventually expand how CLDN18.2 is drugged, but they do not currently change first-line selection, post-zolbetuximab reassessment, or late-line modality choice. These platforms are best positioned in the future-directions section as longer-horizon technologies rather than as current components of the treatment algorithm.

Conclusions

The CLDN18.2-directed treatment landscape in gastric and gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma has expanded rapidly from a single antibody platform to multiple mechanistically distinct modalities, most notably ADCs, bispecific antibodies, and CAR-T cell therapies, while mRNA-based approaches remain exploratory longer-horizon strategies. Zolbetuximab established CLDN18.2 as a clinically actionable biomarker in HER2-negative advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, but its use is constrained by stringent expression requirements, frequent on-target gastrointestinal toxicity, and the possibility of post-treatment expression change. These limitations explain why next-generation development has focused not only on activity, but also on eligibility thresholds, toxicity trade-offs, and post-zolbetuximab treatment positioning.

Based on current evidence, three points appear most clinically relevant. First, ADCs are among the most clinically advanced next-generation CLDN18.2-directed modalities, but their precise role relative to zolbetuximab has not yet been defined by randomized comparative data. Second, post-treatment modulation of CLDN18.2 expression is clinically relevant and supports repeat tissue reassessment when further CLDN18.2-directed therapy is being considered, rather than assuming persistent eligibility. Third, satri-cel represents the best-supported investigational CLDN18.2-directed cellular strategy in previously treated gastric cancer, supported by randomized phase 2 data but still constrained by manufacturing complexity, access, and the need for broader validation.

Several practice implications follow from this framework. At baseline, CLDN18.2 should be interpreted within a biomarker matrix that includes HER2, PD-L1, and MSI/MMR status. In CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative disease, zolbetuximab plus fluoropyrimidine–platinum chemotherapy is the established CLDN18.2-directed first-line option, but biomarker overlap requires individualized positioning relative to PD-1-based pathways. At progression, clinicians should not assume retained CLDN18.2 expression; when feasible, repeat tissue assessment should guide subsequent CLDN18.2-directed therapy according to retained expression level, assay confidence, patient fitness, disease tempo, toxicity profile, and trial availability.

The broader implication is that CLDN18.2 should be managed as a dynamic therapeutic variable rather than a static baseline label. This principle may also inform development of other lineage-associated surface targets, where baseline positivity, spatial heterogeneity, treatment-associated modulation, assay thresholds, and target-density requirements can all shape eligibility and therapeutic responsiveness. Because several next-generation ADC, bispecific antibody, and CAR-T datasets remain early-phase, geographically concentrated, or externally unvalidated, prospective trials should now define the zolbetuximab–PD-1 interface, standardize post-treatment retesting, validate modality-specific thresholds after target modulation, and test where ADCs, bispecific antibodies, and CAR-T strategies fit after zolbetuximab or in broader-expression disease. Reassessment-based sequencing is therefore both a practical CLDN18.2 framework and a model for the next phase of biomarker-driven therapy development in gastric and gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma.
