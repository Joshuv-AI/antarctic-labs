On the elementary theory of the real exponential field Thanks: Partially supported by the Italian research project PRIN 2022, Models, sets and classifications, Prot. 2022TECZJA
Alessandro Berarducci, Francesco Gallinaro

Abstract
. Assuming Schanuel’s conjecture, we prove that the complete theory T exp T_{\exp} of the real exponential field is axiomatized by the axioms of definably complete exponential fields satisfying exp ′ = exp \exp^{\prime}=\exp . This implies the result of Macintyre and Wilkie that, under the same conjecture, T exp T_{\exp} is decidable. Our approach is based on the model completeness of a similar set of axioms for the exponential function restricted to ( − 1 , 1 ) (-1,1) , which we prove unconditionally.

1.
Introduction

In
[
Tar51
]
, Tarski proved that the (first-order) theory of the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
admits quantifier elimination and is decidable, that is, there exists an algorithm to determine whether a first-order sentence
\varphi
in the language
L=\{\leq,0,1,+,\cdot\}
of ordered rings is true in
\mathbb{R}
. A modern treatment can be found in
[
Poi00
, Theorem 6.41]
, including a proof that the theory of
\mathbb{R}
is axiomatized by the axioms of real closed ordered fields.

In the same paper, Tarski asked whether the decidability result could be extended if one adds exponentiation to the language, considering the real exponential field
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}=(\mathbb{R},\exp)
. A first obstacle is that the complete theory
T_{\exp}
of
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
does not admit quantifier elimination due to a counterexample of Osgood
[
Osg16
]
(see also
[
Gab07
, Equation (1.1)]
). Nevertheless, in
[
Wil96
]
, Wilkie proved that
T_{\exp}
is
model complete
: every formula is equivalent to an existential formula (a quantifier-free formula preceded by a block of existential quantifiers).
In geometric terms, this means that every definable set in the structure
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
is a projection of a set defined by equations of the form
p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},e^{x_{1}},\ldots,e^{x_{n}})=0
, where
p
is a polynomial. The sets defined by such equations have a finite number of connected components by
[
Kho80
]
(see also
[
Wil96
, Theorem 5.3]
), hence the same property holds for every definable set in
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
. In particular,
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
is
o-minimal
: every subset of
\mathbb{R}
definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
is a finite union of intervals. Definable sets in o-minimal structures share many of the finiteness properties of semialgebraic sets, see
[
Dri98
]
for a systematic treatment. Wilkie’s results on
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
have been a driving motivation for the study of o-minimality.

In
[
MW96
]
Macintyre and Wilkie proved the decidability of the theory of
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
assuming the real form of
Schanuel’s conjecture
(
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
for short): given a
n
-tuple of
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent real numbers and their exponentials, the resulting
2n
-tuple has transcendence degree at least
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
. More precisely, Macintyre and Wilkie show that, if
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
holds, then there is an algorithm to test whether an exponential polynomial
p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},e^{x_{1}},\ldots,e^{x_{n}})
over
\mathbb{Q}
has a real zero. They then prove that the existence of such an an algorithm implies the decidability of
T_{\exp}
.

Here we prove, assuming
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
, that
T_{\exp}
can be axiomatized by the axioms
T^{dc}_{\exp}
of
definably complete
exponential fields satisfying
\exp^{\prime}=\exp
(Theorem
14.3
). This gives a positive answer, modulo
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
, to a conjecture considered in
[
Mil96
, Section 5]
,
[
BS04
, Theorem 2.8]
,
[
Ser08b
, Theorem 4.7.25]
,
[
Kra23
]
.

We recall that an ordered structure is definably complete if every bounded definable subset of its domain has a least upper bound (a first order version of Dedekind completeness). An ordered field is real closed if and only if it is definably complete, hence
T^{dc}_{\exp}
is a natural extension of the theory of real closed fields.
Since a complete theory is decidable if and only if it is recursively axiomatized, we also obtain a new proof of the decidability result of
[
MW96
]
.

Towards the proof that
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
implies the completeness of
T^{dc}_{\exp}
, in Theorem
12.4
we establish unconditionally the model completeness of a set of axioms
T^{dc}_{\texp}
(very similar to
T^{dc}_{\exp}
) for the exponential function restricted to
(-1,1)
. The models of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
are the definably complete structures expanding an ordered field equipped with an exponential function restricted to
(-1,1)
and satisfying
\exp^{\prime}=\exp
on that interval.

Granted the model completeness of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
, assuming
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
we deduce the completeness of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
(Theorem
14.2
) by showing that the prime submodel of
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
embeds in every models of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
(in Remark
14.4
we relate this to a similar embedding result of Krapp).
The completeness of
T^{dc}_{\exp}
(Theorem
14.3
) in turn follows by a result of Ressayre
[
Res93
]
.

In the last section we strengthen the embedding result above by showing, under
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
(in fact, under a weaker hypothesis), that the residue field of a convex subring of a model
M
of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
can be embedded in
M
preserving the restricted exponential function (Theorem
15.1
).

To prove the model completeness of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
we work by induction on the complexity of formulas as in Wilkie’s proof of model completeness of expansions of
\mathbb{R}
by restricted Pfaffian functions
[
Wil96
]
.
As in that paper, a crucial step consists in bounding the norm of regular solutions, here called Khovanskii points, to certain
n\times n
systems of equations. Our context is different because we cannot assume that
T^{dc}_{\texp}
is a complete theory, but we can take advantage of the fact that models of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
are o-minimal by the results in
[
FS10
,
Hie11
]
.

A major difficulty, however, is that we are not able to prove that
T^{dc}_{\texp}
is a polynomially bounded theory (i.e., in every model, every definable function in one variable is ultimately bounded by a polynomial), so we have to depart from the approach in
[
Wil96
]
which uses in an essential way consequences of the polynomial boundedness of the theory
T_{\text{an}}
studied in
[
Dri86
,
DD88
]
, see in particular
[
Wil96
, Corollary 3.5]
.

In our approach, given a model
M
of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
, we consider the residue field
R=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}
of the local ring
\mathcal{O}
of germs at
+\infty
of polynomially bounded definable functions
f:M\to M
. We show that
R
has an induced restricted exponential function and a compatible derivation. We then apply Ax’s functional transcendence theorem in
[
Ax71
]
to prove that, if for a contradiction there is an unbounded Khovanskii point, then two specific germs of polynomially bounded definable functions are not equal modulo the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Using the inductive hypothesis, this allows us to reach another contradiction and establish the boundedness of Khovanskii points.

We have seen that, under
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
, the theory
T^{dc}_{\texp}
coincides with the complete theory of
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
, so it is polynomially bounded. The role of
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
is precisely to ensure that any model of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
has an archimedean submodel. An unconditional proof of polynomial boundedness remains an open problem even after the model completeness has been established.

A large body of papers deal with tameness properties of semianalytic or subanalytic sets
[
Łoj71
,
Dri86
,
DD88
,
Gab68
]
that can be used to study o-minimal expansions of the reals. These approaches require tools, such as the Weierstrass preparation theorem, that are not available in the non-archimedean context. Other papers, such as
[
JW08
,
LG08
]
, assume forms of local polynomial boundedness as a hypothesis. Possible approaches to the polynomial boundedness of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
may come from Miller’s dychotomy results
[
Mil94
,
Mil96
]
or attempts to extend the results of
[
RS15
]
to the non-archimedean context, but this seems rather challenging.

2.
Model-theoretic preliminaries

We assume familiarity with the model-theoretic notions of a
structure
and a (first-order)
formula
in a given
language

L
. A
sentence
is a formula without free variables. Given a language
L
, we write
M\models\varphi
to express the fact that
\varphi
is an
L
-sentence,
M
is an
L
-structure, and
\varphi
holds in
M
. A comprehensive introduction to model theory can be found in
[
Hod93
]
.

A
theory
is a collection of sentences in a given language. Given a theory
T
and a structure
M
in the same language, we say that
M
is a
model
of
T
if
M\models\varphi
for every
\varphi\in T
. We write in this case
M\models T
.

We write
T\vdash\varphi
if the formula
\varphi
is
provable
in the theory
T
, or equivalently (by Gödel’s completeness theorem),
\varphi
holds in every model of
T
.
A theory
T
is
complete
if, for every sentence
\varphi
in its language, either
\varphi
or its negation
\lnot\varphi
is provable in
T
, but not both.

Given a structure
M
in a language
L
, the theory
\text{Th}(M)
of
M
is the collection of all sentences of
L
that are true in
M
. Clearly
\text{Th}(M)
is a complete theory.

Two structures
M
and
N
are
elementarily equivalent
, written
M\equiv N
, if they have the same complete theory. The theory
RCF
of real closed fields (in the language
L=\{\leq,0,1,+,\cdot\}
) is complete, so its models are elementarily equivalent to the ordered field
\mathbb{R}
of real numbers
[
Tar51
]
.

If
\varphi
is a formula with free variables included in
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, after fixing an ordering of the variables, we may write
\varphi
in the form
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. In this case, if
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in M^{n}
, we write
M\models\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
if
\varphi
holds in
M
when we assign to the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
the values
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
respectively. A set
X\subseteq M^{n}
is
definable
if there are a formula
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
and parameters
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
such that
X=\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in M^{n}:M\models\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\}
.
We say in this case that
X
is defined by the
formula with parameters

\phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})
and we write
X=\phi(M)
. For instance a circle of radius
r
is defined in
\mathbb{R}
by the formula
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}=r^{2}
, with parameter
r
.

An
expansion
of a structure
\mathscr{M}
is a structure
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
with the same domain and a possibly larger language
L^{\prime}\supseteq L
in which the symbols of
L
are interpreted as in
\mathscr{M}
. For example an ordered ring
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}=(M,\leq,0,1,+,\cdot)
is an expansion of its underlying ordered additive group
\mathscr{M}=(M,\leq,0,+)
. If
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
is an expansion of
\mathscr{M}
, we say that
\mathscr{M}
is a
reduct
of
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
. When the language is understood from the context, we use the same notation for a structure and its underlying domain. Thus, in the case of an ordered ring, we may write
M
instead of
(M,\leq,0,1,+,\cdot)
.

Expansions should not be confused with extensions. We say that a structure
M
is an
extension
of a structure
M
, written
M\supseteq N
, if
M
and
N
are structures in the same language and
M
is a
substructure
of
N
.

If
N\supseteq M
is an extension of
M
and
X=\phi(M)\subseteq M^{n}
is a definable set in
M
, we write
\phi(N)
for the set defined in
N
by the same formula
\phi
(where
\phi
may contain parameters from
M
). If
\phi
is quantifier-free, then
\phi(M)=\phi(N)\cap M^{n}
, but in general this equality fails. For instance if
\phi(x)
is the formula
\exists y(x=y^{2})
, then
\phi(\mathbb{Q})\neq\phi(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathbb{Q}
.

We say that
M
is an
elementary substructure
of
N
, written
M\preceq N
, if
\phi(M)=\phi(N)\cap M^{n}
for every formula
\phi=\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
with parameters from
M
. Note that
M\preceq N
implies
M\equiv N
.

A theory
T
is
model complete
if and only if, for any pair of models
M\subseteq N
of
T
, we have
M\preceq N
. Model completeness can also be characterized by the property that every formula
\varphi(\bar{x})
in the language of
T
, where
\bar{x}
is a finite tuple of variables, is equivalent, in all models of
T
, to an existential formula
\exists\bar{y}\theta(\bar{x},\bar{y})
(with
\theta
quantifier free).
This is also equivalent to the fact that, for any pair of models
M\subseteq N
of
T
,

M
is
existentially closed
in
N
, namely for every existential formula
\exists\bar{x}\theta(\bar{x},\bar{a})
with parameters
\bar{a}
from
M
, we have
M\models\exists\bar{x}\theta(\bar{x},\bar{a})\iff N\models\exists\bar{x}\theta(\bar{x},\bar{a})
, where
\bar{x}
is a finite tuple of variables. A proof of these equivalences can be found in
[
Wil15
, Theorem 1.2]
or in
[
Hod93
, Theorem 8.3.1]
.

The theory
RCF
of real closed fields is model complete, since it has the stronger property of admitting
quantifier elimination

[
Tar51
]
, see
[
Poi00
, Theorem 6.41]
for a modern treatement.

3.
Definable completeness and o-minimality

If
M=(M,\leq,\ldots)
is an expansion of an ordered structure, we put on
M
the topology generated by the open intervals, and on
M^{n}
the product topology. If
M
is an expansion of a non-archimedean ordered field, this topology is neither locally compact nor locally connected. This is the typical situation we shall encounter in this paper.

Given an
L
-structure
\mathscr{K}=(K,\leq,\ldots)
which expands a dense totally ordered set
(K,\leq)
without end-points, we say that
\mathscr{K}
is
definably complete
if every non-empty definable subset of
K
that is bounded from above has a least upper bound in
K
. We recall that
\mathscr{K}
is
o-minimal
if every definable subset of
K
is already definable in the reduct
(K,\leq)
. Equivalently,
\mathscr{K}
is o-minimal if every definable subset of
K
is a finite union of intervals.
We assume some familiarity with the basic results on o-minimal structures, including the definition of
dimension
of a definable set and the
cell decomposition
theorem. A standard reference is
[
Dri98
]
. Given an o-minimal structure
M
, or more generally a definably complete structure, a definable subset of
M^{n}
is called
definably compact
if it is closed and bounded, and
definably connected
if it is not the union of two non-empty definable clopen subsets.
Unlike o-minimality, the notion of definable completeness is given by a set of (first-order) axioms, in fact an axiom scheme.

In general o-minimality implies definable completeness, but the converse is not always true. For instance
(\mathbb{R},\leq,0,1,+,\cdot,\sin)
is not o-minimal (because the zero set of
\sin
is not a finite union of intervals), but it is definably complete (because every bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}
has a supremum, be it definable or not).

Many classical results of the differential calculus extend to definably complete expansions of an ordered field, for instance we have the following form of the Taylor formula with Lagrange form of the remainder.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
Ser08a
, Theorem 5]
)
.

Let
M
be a definably complete expansion of an ordered field and let
f:(a,b)\to M
be a definable function of class
C^{n}
. Then for every
x,x_{0}\in(a,b)

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(x_{0})}{k!}\,(x-x_{0})^{k}+\frac{f^{(n)}(\xi)}{n!}\,(x-x_{0})^{n}

for some
\xi
in the interval between
x
and
x_{0}
.

4.
Exponential fields

Let
\mathbb{R}
be the field of real numbers. We consider
\mathbb{R}
as a first-order structure in the language
L=\{\leq,0,1,+,\cdot\}
(with the natural interpretation of the symbols) and we use the same notation for
\mathbb{R}
and its domain.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}=(\mathbb{R},\exp)
be the expansion of
\mathbb{R}
with the exponential function
\exp(x)=e^{x}
and let
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}=(\mathbb{R},\texp)
be the expansion of
\mathbb{R}
with the function
\texp:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\texp(x)=\begin{cases}\exp(x)&\text{ for }x\not\in(-1,1)\\
0&\text{ for }x\in(-1,1)\end{cases}

We call
\texp
the restricted exponential function. We consider
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
as structures in the languages
\{\leq,0,1,+,\cdot,\exp\}
and
\{\leq,0,1,+,\cdot,\texp\}
respectively. Let
T_{\exp}
be the complete theory of
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
and
T_{\texp}
be the complete theory of
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
.

We shall consider recursive subtheories
T^{dc}_{\exp}\subseteq T_{\exp}
and
T^{dc}_{\texp}\subseteq T_{\texp}
axiomatized by the scheme of definable completeness plus the natural differential equations for
\exp
and
\texp
respectively. We need a couple of preliminary observations.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
M
be a definably complete expansion of an ordered field and let
f:M\to M
be a definable function. Suppose that
f^{\prime}(x)=f(x)
for all
x
in an open interval
(-a,a)
and that
f(0)=1
. Then

f(x+y)=f(x)f(y)

whenever
x,y,x+y\in(-a,a)
.

Proof.

For
y\in(-a,a)
, consider the definable function
g_{y}
defined on
I_{y}=(-a,a)\cap(-a-y,a-y)
by
g_{y}(x):=f(x+y)/f(y)
. Note that if
x
and
y
are as in the statement then
x
lies in the domain of
g_{y}
. An easy computation shows that
g_{y}^{\prime}=g_{y}
on
I_{y}
, so
f
and
g_{y}
are two solutions on
I_{y}
of the differential equation
Y^{\prime}=Y
, both taking the value
1
at
x=0
.
By
[
Fra08
, Proposition 2.7]
(based on
[
OPP96
, Theorem 2.3]
) it follows that
g_{y}=f
on
I_{y}
, hence
f(x+y)/f(y)=g_{y}(x)=f(x)
when
x,y,x+y\in(-a,a)
and the desired result follows.
∎

The following converse also holds.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
M
be a definably complete expansion of an ordered field and let
f:M\to M
be a definable monotone function. Suppose that
f(0)=1
and
f(x+y)=f(x)f(y)
whenever
x,y,x+y\in(-a,a)
. Then
f
is differentiable on
(-a,a)
and

f^{\prime}(x)=f^{\prime}(0)f(x)

for all
x\in(-a,a)
.

Proof.

Every definable monotone function
f:M\to M
in a definably complete expansion
M
of a ordered field is continuous almost everywhere
[
FH15
, Lemma 6.6]
and it is differentiable on a dense subset
[
FH15
, Theorem B]
. If
f:M\to M
satisfies our hypothesis, continuity at one point implies continuity everywhere, and similarly for differentiability. Granted this, we have

f^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{f(x+\varepsilon)-f(x)}{\varepsilon}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}f(x)\frac{f(\varepsilon)-f(0)}{\varepsilon}=f^{\prime}(0)f(x).

∎

Definition 4.4
.

Given a field of characteristic zero
F
, an
exponential function
is a morphism
E:F\to F^{\times}
from the additive to the multiplicative group of the field. We then call
(F,E)
an
exponential field
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
T^{dc}_{\exp}\subseteq T_{\exp}
be the theory in the language
\{\leq,0,1,+,\cdot,\exp\}
whose models are the definably complete expansions of an ordered field with a differentiable function
\exp
satisfying the following:

•

\exp^{\prime}(x)=\exp(x)
for all
x
;

•

\exp(0)=1
.

By Proposition
4.2
, a model of
T^{dc}_{\exp}
satisfies
\exp(x+y)=\exp(x)\exp(y)
for all
x,y
, so
\exp
is an exponential function. Moreover, any model of
T^{dc}_{\exp}
satisfies
\exp^{\prime}=\exp
, so the models of
T^{dc}_{\exp}
coincide with the EXP-fields in the sense of
[
Kra23
]
.

Remark 4.6
.

By
[
DW83
, Theorem 14]
, if
M
is an exponential field satisfying
\exp(x)\geq 1+x
for every
x
, then
\exp
is continuous and differentiable and satisfies
\exp^{\prime}=\exp
, so
T^{dc}_{\exp}
can be alternatively axiomatized using the above inequality.

Definition 4.7
.

A
restricted exponential field
is an ordered field
M
equipped with a function
\texp:M\to M
satisfying
\texp(0)=1
and
\texp(x+y)=\texp(x)\texp(y)
whenever
x,y,x+y\in(-1,1)
and
\texp(x)=0
for
x\not\in(-1,1)
.
We call
\texp
a
restricted exponential function
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
T^{dc}_{\texp}\subseteq T_{\texp}
be the theory in the language
\{\leq,0,1,+,\cdot,\texp\}
whose models are the definably complete structures
(M,\leq,0,1,+,\cdot,\texp)
where

(M,\leq,0,1,+,\cdot)
is an ordered field and
\texp:M\to M
is a function that is differentiable on
(-1,1)
and satisfies:

•

\texp^{\prime}(x)=\texp(x)
for
x\in(-1,1)
;

•

\texp(0)=1
;

•

\texp(x)=0
for
x\not\in(-1,1)
.

By Proposition
4.2
, a model of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
satisfies
\texp(x+y)=\texp(x)\texp(y)
whenever
x,y,x+y\in(-1,1)
, so it is a restricted exponential field.

Theorem 4.9

(
[
FS10
,
Hie11
]
)
.

We have:

•

Every model of
T^{dc}_{\exp}
is o-minimal.

•

Every model of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
is o-minimal.

Proof.

This is a special case of a more general result concerning Pfaffian functions, of which exponentiation is an example. More precisely, in
[
FS10
]
the authors prove the o-minimality of every definably complete expansion of an ordered field with Pfaffian functions assuming a definable version of the Baire property. In
[
Hie11
]
it is shown that the Baire property already follows from definable completeness.
∎

A result of Ressayre in
[
Res93
]
shows that
T_{\exp}
is recursively axiomatized modulo
T_{\texp}
(another proof appears in
[
DMM94
, (4.10)]
). The following minor variant shows that
T_{\exp}
is in fact finitely axiomatized modulo
T_{\texp}
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
R
be an ordered field and let
E:R\to R
be a function on
R
.
Then
(R,E)
is elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
if and only if the following hold:

(1)

E:R\to R^{>0}
be an increasing surjective exponential function.

(2)

E(x)\geq x+1
for all
x
.

(3)

(R,E_{|(-1,1)})
is elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
.

Proof.

The result appears in
[
Res93
]
but with axiom
(2)
replaced by an axiom scheme
(2^{\prime})
whose
n
-th instance
(2^{\prime})_{n}
says that
E(x)>x^{n}
for every
x>f(n)
where
f(n)
is a recursive function of
n
. It therefore suffices to show that, in the presence of the other axioms,
(2)
implies
(2^{\prime})_{n}
. For simplicity we take
f(n)=4n^{2}
(in
[
DMM94
]
the authors take
f(n)=n^{2}
, but this is irrelevant). So assume
x>4n^{2}
. Then
\sqrt{x}>2n
and we obtain

E(x)>E(2n\sqrt{x})=E(\sqrt{x})^{2n}\geq(\sqrt{x}+1)^{2n}\geq x^{n}
.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
M\models T^{dc}_{\exp}
. Then
M
satisfies conditions (1) and (2) in Proposition
4.10
.

Proof.

Condition (1) clearly holds. For (2) observe that, by the Taylor formula,
\exp(x)=1+x+\exp(\xi)\tfrac{x^{2}}{2}
for some
\xi\in(0,x)
, so
\exp(x)\geq x+1
for all
x
.
∎

Besides exponential functions and restricted exponential functions, we also need the notion of partial exponential function defined as follows.

Definition 4.12

(
[
Kir10
]
)
.

Given a field
F=(F,0,1,+,\cdot)
of characteristic
0
, a
partial exponential function
is a homomorphism
\exp:(D(F),+)\to(F,\cdot)
where
D(F)=\dom(\exp)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector subspace of
F
. We then say that
(F,\exp)=(F,0,1,+,\cdot,\exp)
is a
partial exponential field
.

Definition 4.13
.

Let
M
be an expansion of an ordered field. An element
x\in M
is
finite
if there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x\in(-N,N)
. It is
infinitesimal
if for every positive
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have
x\in\left(-\frac{1}{N},\frac{1}{N}\right)
.

Proposition 4.14
.

Given an ordered field
M
with a restricted exponential function
\texp:M\to M
, there is unique partial exponential function

\Exp:\Fin(M)\to\Fin(M)

that agrees with
\texp
on
(-1,1)
. Moreover, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the restriction of
\Exp
to any interval
(-n,n)
is definable in
(M,\texp)
.

Proof.

Given
x\in\Fin(M)
, choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x\in(-n,n)
and define
\Exp(x)=\texp(x/n)^{n}
.
Using the functional equation
\texp(x+y)=\texp(x)\texp(y)
for
x,y\in(-1,1)
, it is easy to see that the definition does not depend on the choice of
n
(e.g.
\texp(x/n)^{n}=\texp(x/nk)^{nk}=\texp(x/k)^{k}
).
∎

Proposition 4.15
.

If
(M,\texp)\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
and
\Exp:\Fin(M)\to\Fin(M)
is the induced partial exponential function, then

(1)

\Exp
is smooth on
\Fin(M)
,

(2)

\Exp^{\prime}=\Exp
on
\Fin(M)
.

Proof.

In models of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
the function
\texp
is smooth on
(-1,1)
and satisfies
\texp(0)=1
and
\texp^{\prime}(x)=\texp(x)
for
x\in(-1,1)
. Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and, for
x\in(-n,n)
, define
h(x)=\Exp(x/n)=\texp(x/n)
. Then
\Exp(x)=h(x)^{n}
, and by the chain rule (which holds in definably complete expansions of fields),

\Exp^{\prime}(x)=n\,h(x)^{n-1}h^{\prime}(x)
.
Moreover,

h^{\prime}(x)=\tfrac{1}{n}\,h(x)
, so

\Exp^{\prime}(x)=h(x)^{n}=\Exp(x)

for every
x\in(-n,n)
.
Finally observe that
\Exp^{\prime}(x)=\Exp^{\prime}(0)\Exp(x)
by Proposition
4.2
(applied to the restriction of
\Exp
to
(-n,n)
) and
\Exp^{\prime}(0)=\texp^{\prime}(0)=1
.
∎

5.
Convex valuation rings

Krapp showed that if
M\models T^{dc}_{\exp}
and
\mu
is the maximal ideal of
\Fin(M)
, then the residue field
\Fin(M)/\mu
has an induced exponential function
[
Kra19
, Proposition 3.2]
and a unique embedding into
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
as an exponential field. The argument uses some compatibility results between exponential functions and convex valuations studied by Salma Kuhlmann, see in particular
[
Kuh00
, Lemma 1.17]
. Here we adapt some of the arguments of Krapp and S. Kuhlmann to models of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
M\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
and let
\Exp:\Fin(M)\to\Fin(M)
be the induced partial exponential function. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
odd we have:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}<\Exp(-1)<\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}

Proof.

Since the restriction of
\Exp
to
(-2,2)
is definable,
we can use the Taylor formula in
M
to obtain
\Exp(-1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}+\Exp(\xi)\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}
for some
\xi\in(-1,0)
. Since
\Exp(\xi)=\Exp(\xi/2)^{2}
is positive, it follows that if
n
is even
\Exp(-1)>\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}
and if
n
is odd
\Exp(-1)<\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
M\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
and let
\mathcal{O}\subset M
be a convex subring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}
. Then
\texp((-1,1))\subset\mathcal{O}^{\times}
and
\texp(\mathfrak{m})\subseteq 1+\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, for every
q\in\mathbb{Q}
we have
\Exp(q)=\Exp(-1)^{-q}\in\Fin(M)^{\times}
. Since
\Exp
is increasing,
\Exp
maps
\Fin(M)
into
\Fin(M)^{\times}
, hence
\texp
maps
(-1,1)
into
\Fin(M)^{\times}
, and the latter is contained in
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
.
By the Taylor formula (Proposition
3.1
), for
\varepsilon\in\mathfrak{m}
we have
\texp(\varepsilon)=1+\varepsilon+\exp(\xi)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}
for some
\xi
between
0
and
\varepsilon
. Since
\exp(\xi)\in\mathcal{O}
, it follows that
\exp(\xi)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\in\mathfrak{m}
, hence
\texp(\varepsilon)\in 1+\mathfrak{m}
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
M\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
and let
\mathcal{O}\subset M
be a convex subring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{O}
.
Then the residue field
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}
has an induced restricted exponential function
\overline{\texp}:\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}\to\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}
defined by

\overline{\texp}(x+\mathfrak{m})=\begin{cases}\texp(x)+\mathfrak{m}&\text{ if }x+\mathfrak{m}\subset(-1,1)\\
0&\text{ if }x+\mathfrak{m}\not\subset(-1,1).\end{cases}

Moreover, if
\overline{\Exp}:\Fin(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m})\to\Fin(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m})
is the induced partial exponential function (as in Proposition
4.14
),
then
\overline{\Exp}(x+\mathfrak{m})=\Exp(x)+\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

If
x+\mathfrak{m}\subset(-1,1)
and
x-y\in\mathfrak{m}
, then by Proposition
5.2
we have
\frac{\texp(x)}{\texp(y)}=\texp(x-y)\in 1+\mathfrak{m}
. It follows that
\texp(x)\in\texp(y)+\mathfrak{m}\texp(y)
, and since
\texp(y)\in\mathcal{O}
we have
\texp(x)-\texp(y)\in\mathfrak{m}
. So the definition of
\overline{\texp}
is well-posed.

Finally, if
x+\mathfrak{m}\in\Fin(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m})
then there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{x+\mathfrak{m}}{n}\subset(-1,1)
and

\overline{\Exp}(x+\mathfrak{m})=\overline{\texp}\left(\frac{x}{n}+\mathfrak{m}\right)^{n}=\left(\texp\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)+\mathfrak{m}\right)^{n}=\texp\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n}+\mathfrak{m}=\Exp(x)+\mathfrak{m}.

∎

We do not know whether the restricted exponential field
(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m},\overline{\texp})
defined above is a model of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
, since a priori it may not be definably complete.

Consider now the case
\mathcal{O}=\Fin(M)
. In this case the residue field is archimedean and we obtain the following result.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
M\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
, and let
\mu
denote the maximal ideal of
\Fin(M)
. Then
\Fin(M)/\mu
has a unique embedding into
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
as a restricted exponential field.

Proof.

Since
\Fin(M)/\mu
is archimedean, it has a unique ordered field embedding
\iota:\Fin(M)/\mu\to\mathbb{R}
into the reals
[
Kap42
]
. We need to show that the embedding preserves the restricted exponential function. Let
\overline{M}:=\img(\iota)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. For
x\in\Fin(M)
, let
\overline{x}=\iota(x+\mu)\in\overline{M}
. Since
\Fin(\overline{M})=\overline{M}
, by Corollary
5.3
there is a well-defined exponential function
\overline{\Exp}:\overline{M}\to\overline{M}
given by
\overline{\Exp}(\overline{x})=\overline{\Exp(x)}
.

It follows from Lemma
5.1
that
\overline{\Exp}(-1)=e^{-1}
, and therefore for every
q\in\mathbb{Q}
we obtain
\overline{\Exp}(q)=\overline{\Exp}(-1)^{-q}=e^{-q}
, so
\overline{\Exp}
coincides with the real exponential on
\mathbb{Q}
and by continuity
\overline{\Exp}(x)=e^{x}
for every
x\in\overline{M}
. Considering the restriction of
\overline{\Exp}
to
(-1,1)
the desired result follows.
∎

In Theorem
15.1
we will show that, under Schanuel’s conjecture,
\Fin(M)/\mu
embeds in
M
as a restricted exponential field.

6.
Tangent spaces and regular points

We need a few results about differentiable definable functions in the o-minimal setting, in particular regarding regular points and transversality, see for instance
[
BO01
,
Ser08a
,
Ser08b
]
.

Let
N
be an o-minimal expansion of an ordered field, for instance a model of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
or
T^{dc}_{\exp}
.
Let
0<p\in\mathbb{N}
and let
f:X\to Y
be a definable map between two definable sets
X\subseteq N^{n}
and
Y\subseteq N^{k}
. We say that
f

is a
definable
C^{p}
-map
around
x\in X
if there is a
definable open neighborbood
U\subset N^{n}
of
x
and a definable map

F\colon U\to N^{k}
extending
f_{|U\cap X}
such that all the
partial derivatives
\partial^{s}F/\partial x_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{i_{s}}
exist and are continuous for each
s\leq p
. If this happens around every point of
X
, we say that
f
is a definable
C^{p}
-map.
A
definable
C^{p}
-diffeomorphism
is a bijective definable
C^{p}
-map whose inverse is a definable
C^{p}
-map.

Let
X
be a definable subset of
N^{n}
. Given
x\in X
we define
T_{x}(X)\subseteq N^{n}
as the set of all
v\in N^{n}
such that there is a definable curve
\gamma:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to X
of class
C^{1}
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=v
, where
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\gamma(t)-\gamma(0)}{t}
. Without additional hypotheses, the sum of two vectors of
T_{x}(X)
may not lie in
T_{x}(X)
. However,
if
X\subseteq N^{n}
is locally definably
C^{1}
-diffeomorphic to an open subset of
N^{d}
around
x\in X
, then
T_{x}(X)
is an
N
-linear subspace of
N^{n}=T_{x}(N^{n})
of dimension
d
. In this case we say that
X
is a
definable
C^{1}
-manifold
of dimension
d
around
p
and
T_{x}(X)
is its
tangent space at
x
. The notion of definable
C^{p}
-manifold is defined similarly.

If
f:X\to Y
is a definable
C^{1}
-map around
x\in X
and
v=\gamma^{\prime}(0)\in T_{x}(X)
(where
\gamma:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to X
is a definable function of class
C^{1}
with
\gamma(0)=x
), then
df_{x}:T_{x}(X)\to T_{f(x)}(Y)
is defined by
df_{x}(v)=(f\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(0)
. If
X
is a definable
C^{1}
-manifold around
x
and
Y
is a definable
C^{1}
-manifold around
f(x)
, then
df_{x}
is a linear map.

In particular, if
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}):N^{n}\to N^{k}
is a definable function of class
C^{1}
around
x\in N^{n}
, then
df_{x}:N^{n}\to N^{k}
is the linear map given by the Jacobian matrix
J(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})(x)=(\partial f_{i}/\partial x_{j})_{ij}(x)
.

Definition 6.1
.

Given an open definable set
U\subseteq N^{n}
and definable functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}:U\to N
, we write
V_{U}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
for the set of common zeros of
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
in
U
. When
U=N^{n}
, we write
V_{n}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
.

If
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
is of class
C^{1}
around a point
x\in V_{U}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
, and
df_{x}
has maximal rank (equal to
k
), we say that
x
is a
regular point
of
f
. We write
V_{U}^{\reg}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\subseteq V_{U}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
for the set of regular points.

If
X=V_{U}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\subseteq N^{n}
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
are definable
C^{1}
-maps around
x\in X
, then
T_{x}(X)\subseteq\ker(df_{x})
. The equality
T_{x}(X)=\ker(df_{x})
holds if and only if
x
is a regular point of
f
. In this case
T_{x}(X)
has dimension
n-k
and
X
is locally definably
C^{1}
-diffeomorphic to an open subset of
N^{n-k}
around
x
. We thus have:

Proposition 6.2

(
[
BO01
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

If
X=V_{U}^{\reg}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\subseteq N^{n}
is non-empty, then it has dimension
n-k
. In particular, if
n=k
, then
\dim(X)=0
, so
X
is a finite set by o-minimality.

Remark 6.3
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
N^{n}
.
Let
f:U\to N^{k}
and
g:U\to N^{m}
be definable
C^{1}
-maps around
x
with sets of zeros
X=V_{U}(f)
and
Y=V_{U}(g)
, and let
x\in X\cap Y
be a regular point of both
f
and
g
. Then
x
is a regular point of
(f,g):U\to N^{k+m}
if and only if
X
and
Y
meet
transversally
at
x
, i.e.
T_{x}(X)+T_{x}(Y)=N^{n}
.

7.
Restricted exponential polynomials

Definition 7.1
.

Let
N
be a restricted exponential field,
R\subseteq N
a subring. An
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-polynomial
over
R
is an expression of the form

F_{p}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\texp(x_{1}),\ldots,\texp(x_{\ell}))

where
\ell\leq n
and
p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell})\in R[\bar{x},\bar{y}]
is a polynomial over
R
.

An
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-polynomial defines a function
F_{p}
from
N^{n}
to
N
which is possibly discontinuous (since
\texp
is discontinuous at
-1,1
). If
N\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
, the restriction of
F_{p}
to
(-1,1)^{\ell}\times N^{n-\ell}
is continuous and smooth.

Definition 7.2
.

We write
U_{\ell,n}
for the set
(-1,1)^{\ell}\times N^{n-\ell}\subseteq N^{n}
and
V_{\ell,n}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
for
V_{U_{\ell,n}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
, namely the set of common zeros of
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
in
U_{\ell,n}
. Similarly we define
V_{\ell,n}^{\reg}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})=V_{U_{\ell,n}}^{\reg}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
.

Definition 7.3
.

Let
N
be a restricted exponential field,
R\subseteq N
a subring.

•

An
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-variety
over
R
is a set of the form
V_{\ell,n}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})
where
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
are
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-polynomials over
R
.

•

An
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii point
over
R
is an
n
-tuple

(\alpha,\beta)=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-\ell})\in U_{\ell,n}\subseteq N^{n}

that belongs to a set of the form
V_{\ell,n}^{\reg}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
are
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-polynomials over
R
.

•

An
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii system
over
R
is a system of equations and inequations of the form

\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0\;\wedge\;\det(J(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))\neq 0

where
f_{i}=F_{p_{i}}
are
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-polynomials over
R
and
J(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})=\left(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)_{ij}
is the Jacobian matrix of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
defined formally using the rule
\frac{\partial\texp(x)}{\partial x}=\texp(x)
.

Note that these definitions assume a given ordering of the variables, namely the first
\ell
variables are restricted to
(-1,1)
.
When there is no need to specify
n,\ell,R
, we simply speak of
\texp
-polynomial,
\texp
-variety, Khovanskii point, Khovanskii system.

It follows from the definitions that the
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii points are the points that satisfy some
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii system.

Definition 7.4
.

We say an
\texp
-polynomial has
complexity

\leq\ell
if it is an
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-polynomial for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We similarly define the complexity of an
\texp
-variety, Khovanskii point, or Khovanskii system.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
N\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
. Every non-empty
\texp
-variety of complexity
\leq\ell
contains a projection of an
\texp
-Khovanskii point of complexity
\leq\ell
defined over the same parameters.

Similar results can be found in
[
Wil89
, Section 4]
and
[
Wil96
, Theorem 5.1]
, but they are stated under the assumption that the ambient structure is elementarily equivalent to an expansion of the reals. The proposition can however be proved by induction on the dimension using the next lemma. We say that an
\texp
-variety
V_{\ell,n}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
is
regular
if it coincides with
V_{\ell,n}^{\reg}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
, and we say a definable set
Z
is a

of complexity
\leq\ell
if there is an
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-variety
V_{\ell,n}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
such that

is a clopen subset of
V_{\ell,n}^{\reg}(f_{1},\dots,f_{k})
. We then call

a regular component of the variety

. These notions depend not only on the variety, but on its presentation as the set of zeros of a given system.

Lemma 7.6

(
[
Ser08a
, Theorem 33, Remark 42, Theorem 44]
)
.

Let

, and let

-
\texp
-variety over a subfield

.

(1)

can be written as a finite union of regular components of
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-varieties over

.

(2)

The set of regular points of

is the projection of a finite union of regular
\texp
-varieties over

of complexity
\leq\ell
and dimension
\leq\dim V
.

(3)

If
V
is regular and
\dim(V)>0
, there are
\texp
-polynomials

over

of complexity
\leq\ell
such that for every non-empty clopen definable subset

.

Lemma

is a special case of results in
[

]
, observing that the ring of

-
\texp

The only minor issue is that the results of

9.
Germs of polynomially bounded functions

Let
M
be an expansion of an ordered field, and let
f:M\to M
be a definable function. We recall that
f
is said to be
polynomially bounded
if there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|f(x)|\leq x^{n}
for all sufficiently large

.
If every definable function
f:M\to M
is polynomially bounded, then the structure
M
is called polynomially bounded. A theory

is polynomially bounded if every model of
T
is polynomially bounded.
The theory
T_{an}
studied in
[
DD88
,
Dri86
,
DMM94
]
is polynomially bounded and complete, hence so is its reduct
T_{\texp}
. We cannot however deduce that the subtheory
T^{dc}_{\texp}\subseteq T_{\texp}
is polynomially bounded without knowing whether it is complete. This justifies the following definition.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
M\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
.

•

Given a definable function
f:M\to M
, let
\germ(f)
be the germ of
f
at
+\infty
and let
\mathcal{G}_{M}
be the family of all such germs. Since
M
is o-minimal,
\mathcal{G}_{M}
is an ordered field (with the natural addition and multiplication of germs).

•

Consider the convex subring
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{M}
defined by

\mathcal{O}=\{\germ(f)\in\mathcal{G}_{M}\mid f\text{ is polynomially bounded}\}.

Let
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
be the maximal ideal and
R=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}
the residue field.

•

We denote by

[f]=\germ(f)+\mathfrak{m}\in R

the class of
\germ(f)
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
.

Clearly if
M
is polynomially bounded, then
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{G}_{M}
and
\mathfrak{m}=(0)
.

Remark 9.2
.

Let
M\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
and let
R=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}
be as above.

•

\germ(f)\in\mathfrak{m}
if and only if
|f(t)|<t^{-n}
for all

and all sufficiently large

, namely
f
is
flat
at
+\infty
.

•

\mathcal{G}_{M}
is an elementary extension of

with a restricted exponential function defined by

.

For the second point, note that, since

is o-minimal,

can be naturally identified with the definable closure of

inside an elementary extension

with an element

Proposition 9.3
.

Let

be definable functions not eventually constant with

\lim_{t\to+\infty}f(t)/g(t)=0\iff\lim_{t\to+\infty}f^{\prime}(t)/g^{\prime}(t)=0.

Proof.

Consider the derivation
D:\mathcal{G}_{M}\to\mathcal{G}_{M}
given by

(this makes sense since for every definable function

exists for all sufficiently large

is an

[

∎

By an application of the compactness theorem for first-order logic we obtain the following uniform version of Theorem

Corollary 10.2

Consider the set

D_{t}:=\pi(W_{t})\subset M^{\ell}\cap(-1,1)^{\ell}
has exactly
k
elements. Moreover,
k>0
, as otherwise the quantifier-free formula
(x\in W\wedge|x|>t_{0})
, which is satisfied in
N
by
(\alpha,\beta,\texp(\alpha))
, would not be satisfied in
M
, contradicting
A_{\ell}
and Proposition
11.4
. It follows that there are
M
-definable functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
(
k\neq 0
) such that, for
t\geq t_{0}
in
M
,
D_{t}=\{f_{1}(t),\ldots,f_{k}(t)\}\subseteq(-1,1)^{\ell}
. By o-minimality the limits
a_{i}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}f_{i}(t)
exist in
M^{\ell}\cap[-1,1]^{\ell}
. Let
D_{\infty}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}\subseteq[-1,1]^{\ell}
. Then for every
\varepsilon\in M
there is
r\in M
such that the implication

\left(x\in W\wedge|x|>r\right)\implies\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}|\pi(x)-a_{i}|\leq\varepsilon

holds for every

. By
A_{<\ell}
and Proposition
11.4
the implication holds for all
x\in N^{n+\ell}
, since it is expressible by a quantifier-free formula of complexity
<\ell
.
Since
(\alpha,\beta)
is not
M
-bounded, we have
|(\alpha,\beta,\texp(\alpha))|>r
for every
r\in M
. Since
\pi(\alpha,\beta,\texp(\alpha))=\alpha
, by the above implication
\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}|\alpha-a_{i}|\leq\varepsilon
holds for all positive
\varepsilon\in M
. It follows that
\alpha
has a standard part in
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}\subset M
.
∎

Proposition 11.8
.

A_{<\ell}\implies B_{\ell}
.

Proof.

Assume
A_{<\ell}
and let
P=(\alpha,\beta)\in N^{n}
be a
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-Khovanskii point over
M
. We need to prove that
P
is
M
-bounded.

If
\ell=0
,
P
is algebraic over
M
, so it belongs to
M
. So assume
\ell>0
. By Lemma
11.5
we may assume
\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha/M)=\ell
. By Lemma

,
(\alpha,\beta,\texp(\alpha))

. Assume for a contradiction that
(\alpha,\beta)
is not

,
\alpha

in

. Moreover, since

, we have

Let
\pi
denote the projection map that sends
(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell},y_{1},\dots,y_{n-\ell},z_{1},\dots,z_{\ell})
to
(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell},z_{1},\dots,z_{\ell})

\pi(\alpha,\beta,\texp(\alpha))=(\alpha,\texp(\alpha))

be the topological closure of
\pi(V)
. We can define

by a semialgebraic formula with parameters from

(x,z)\in Z:\iff\forall\varepsilon>0\;\exists(x^{\prime},y,z^{\prime})\in V:|(x,z)-(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})|<\varepsilon

are
\ell
-tuples and

.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{M}
,

and

be as in Definition

and

[
Dri98
, Chapter IV, (1.18)]
. Moreover the dimension cannot increase under a projection, so
\dim Z\leq\dim V=\ell
.

Consider the derivation
\delta:R\to R
given by Lemma
9.4
. Since
\dim Z\leq\ell
,
by Corollary
10.2
there is a finite set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
such that, in

with
\bigwedge_{i=1}^{\ell}\delta(z_{i})/z_{i}=\delta(x_{i})
, there is

such that
\delta(u\cdot x)=0
, where

is the scalar product.

Now recall that
a=\operatorname{st}(\alpha)\in M^{\ell}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is finite, there is

such that for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
we have
|d\cdot\alpha-d\cdot a|\leq|u\cdot\alpha-u\cdot a|
. Since
\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha/M)=\ell
, we have
|d\cdot\alpha-d\cdot a|\neq 0
. By permuting the coordinates of
\alpha
, we may assume that
d_{\ell}\neq 0
.

Since
a\in[-1,1]^{\ell}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
, there is some

such that
|d\cdot a|<n_{0}
and
|d_{i}a_{i}|<n_{0}
for

. We can then define

as
\texp(x/n_{0})^{n_{0}}
, so that the function

is smooth on

and satisfies

whenever

are all in
(-n_{0},n_{0})
(Propositions
4.14
and
4.15
).

Let

W\subseteq V\cap G_{\exp}^{n,\ell,-}

denote the set of points of transversal intersection between
G_{\exp}^{n,\ell,-}
and
V
, which is a
1
-dimensional definable smooth manifold containing
(\alpha,\beta,\texp(\alpha))
.
For every
t\in M^{>0}
, consider the quantifier-free formula
\chi_{t}(x,y,z)
given by the conjunction of the following:

(i)

(x,y,z)\in W
,

(ii)

|x-a|\leq t^{-1}\wedge|z-e^{a}|\leq t^{-1}
,

(iii)

\bigwedge_{u\in\mathcal{U}}0<|d\cdot x-d\cdot a|\leq|u\cdot x-u\cdot a|
,

where
x,z
are
\ell
-tuples and
y
is a tuple of length
n-\ell
.

The Khovanskii point
(\alpha,\beta,e^{\alpha})\in N^{n+\ell}
satisfies
\chi_{t}
for every
t\in M^{>0}
(for point (iii) use
\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha/M)=\ell>0
).
Since
\chi_{t}(x,y,z)
has complexity
<\ell
and we are assuming
A_{<\ell}
, it follows that the formula
\chi_{t}(x,y,z)
is satisfiable in
M
for every
t\in M^{>0}
. Since
\chi_{t^{\prime}}(M)\subseteq\chi_{t}(M)
for
t^{\prime}>t
, in each
\chi_{t}(M)
there are points
(x,y,z)
with
x
arbitrarily close to
a
(by point (ii)), but different from
a
(by point (iii)). It follows that, given
t_{0}\in M^{>0}
, there is
t_{1}\in M^{>0}
such that, for each
t\in M
with
t\geq t_{1}
, there is
(x,y,z)\in\chi_{t_{0}}(M)
such that
|d\cdot x-d\cdot a|=t^{-1}
.

The sets

(1)

L_{t}=\{(x,y,z)\in M^{n+\ell}:|d\cdot x-d\cdot a|=t^{-1}\}

are all disjoint as
t
varies, so for all
t
outside a finite set the intersection
L_{t}\cap\chi_{t_{0}}(M)
is finite (because by point (i)
\chi_{t_{0}}(M)\subseteq W
, which has dimension one). By o-minimality we can assume (increasing
t_{1}
if necessary), that there is
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(2)

L_{t}\cap\chi_{t_{0}}(M)=\{f_{1}(t),\ldots,f_{k}(t)\}

for each
t\geq t_{1}
, where
k\neq 0
and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
are continuous
M
-definable functions from
[t_{1},+\infty)
to
M^{n+\ell}
. Consider one of the functions

f\in\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\}.

Since
f(t)\in\chi_{t_{0}}(M)\subseteq G^{n,\ell,-}_{\exp}
, we can write the
n+\ell
components of
f(t)
in the form

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=(\phi_{1}(t),\ldots,\phi_{\ell}(t),\theta_{1}(t),\ldots,\theta_{n-\ell}(t),\psi_{1}(t),\ldots,\psi_{\ell}(t))

\displaystyle=(\Phi(t),\Theta(t),\Psi(t))

where
\phi_{i}(t)\in(-1,1)
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
and
\psi_{i}(t)=e^{\phi_{i}(t)}
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell-1
.

By construction,

(3)

(\Phi(t),\Theta(t),\Psi(t))\in L_{t}\cap\chi_{t_{0}}(M)

for every sufficiently large
t\in M
.
By (i), for each
t\in M
with
t\geq t_{1}
we have
(\Phi(t),\Theta(t),\Psi(t))\in V
, and hence

(\germ(\Phi),\germ(\Theta),\germ(\Psi))\in V(\mathcal{G}_{M})

so we have

(\germ(\Phi),\germ(\Psi))\in\pi(V)(\mathcal{G}_{M}).

The components of
\Phi
and
\Psi
are bounded by (ii) (hence polynomially bounded), so
(\germ(\Phi),\germ(\Psi))\in\mathcal{O}^{2\ell}
.
We may then take their residues
[\Phi]=\germ(\Phi)+\mathfrak{m}
and
[\Psi]=\germ(\Psi)+\mathfrak{m}
in
R=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}
.
By Lemma
9.4
(
5
), since
Z=\overline{\pi(V)}
, it follows that

([\Phi],[\Psi])\in Z(R).

Recall that
e^{\phi_{i}(t)}=\psi_{i}(t)
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell-1
.

Claim 1
.

We have
[e^{\phi_{\ell}}]\neq[\psi_{\ell}]
.

Proof.

For a contradiction, assume
[e^{\phi_{\ell}}]=[\psi_{\ell}]
. Then
([\Phi],[e^{\Phi}])=([\Phi],[\Psi])\in Z(R)
and
\delta([\psi_{i}])/[\psi_{i}]=\delta([\phi_{i}])
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
by Lemma
9.4
(
3
). By definition of
\mathcal{U}
, there is
u\in\mathcal{U}
such that
\delta(u\cdot[\Phi])=0
, so there is a constant
c\in\ker(\delta)=M
such that
u\cdot[\Phi]=c
.
This means that
\germ(u\cdot\Phi-c)
is in the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, and in particular
u\cdot\Phi(t)-c
tends to
0
when
t\to+\infty
.
By (ii)
\Phi(t)\to a
, so we have that
c=u\cdot a
. By (iii) it follows that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=|u\cdot[\Phi]-u\cdot a|

\displaystyle\geq|d\cdot[\Phi]-d\cdot a|,

and therefore
\germ(d\cdot\Phi-d\cdot a)
is in the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. By (
3
),
f(t)=(\Phi(t),\Theta(t),\Psi(t))\in L_{t}
, so
|d\cdot\Phi(t)-d\cdot a|=t^{-1}
by (
1
), which is absurd since the germ of
t\mapsto t^{-1}
is not in the maximal ideal.
This proves the claim.
∎

Let
r=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\psi_{i}(t)^{d_{i}}\in M
.

Then
r>0
and for all sufficiently large
t\in M

\displaystyle|e^{d\cdot\Phi(t)}-\Psi(t)^{d}|

\displaystyle=\left|e^{d_{1}\phi_{1}(t)}\cdots e^{d_{\ell}\phi_{\ell}(t)}-\psi_{1}(t)^{d_{1}}\cdots\psi_{\ell}(t)^{d_{\ell}}\right|

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}(t)^{d_{1}}\cdots\psi_{\ell-1}(t)^{d_{\ell-1}}\left|e^{d_{\ell}\phi_{\ell}(t)}-\psi_{\ell}(t)^{d_{\ell}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{r}{2}|e^{d_{\ell}\phi_{\ell}(t)}-\psi_{\ell}(t)^{d_{\ell}}|.

By Claim
1

[e^{\phi_{\ell}}]\neq[\psi_{\ell}]
and since by assumption
d_{\ell}\neq 0
, we have
[e^{\phi_{\ell}}]^{d_{\ell}}\neq[\psi_{\ell}]^{d_{\ell}}
, namely the germ of
e^{d_{\ell}\phi_{\ell}}-\psi_{\ell}^{d_{\ell}}
is not in the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. This implies that there is some
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all sufficiently large
t\in M
we have

\tfrac{r}{2}|e^{d_{\ell}\phi_{\ell}(t)}-\psi_{\ell}(t)^{d_{\ell}}|\geq t^{-m}=|d\cdot\Phi(t)-d\cdot a|^{m}.

where the equality follows from (
1
).
Combining this with the previous inequalities we obtain

(4)

|e^{d\cdot\Phi(t)}-\Psi(t)^{d}|\geq|d\cdot\Phi(t)-d\cdot a|^{m}.

By the Taylor formula for
e^{t}=\texp(t/n_{0})^{n_{0}}
centered at
d\cdot a\in M\cap(-n_{0},n_{0})
there is a polynomial
h
over
M
and some
\varepsilon\in M^{>0}
such that

(5)

|h(t)-e^{t}|\leq|t-d\cdot a|^{m+1}

whenever
|t-d\cdot a|<\varepsilon
. This inequality is also valid for
t
in the big model
N\supseteq M
. Since
\varepsilon\in M
and
a=\operatorname{st}(\alpha)
, the inequality holds for
t=d\cdot\alpha
, that is

(6)

|h(d\cdot\alpha)-e^{d\cdot\alpha}|\leq|d\cdot\alpha-d\cdot a|^{m+1}.

Recall that
m
, hence
h
, depend on the choice of
f\in\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}
. Define

S_{f}:=\{(x,y,z):|h(d\cdot x)-z^{d}|>|d\cdot x-d\cdot a|^{m+1}\}

where
z^{d}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}z_{i}^{d_{i}}
.
By (
6
) we have

(\alpha,\beta,e^{\alpha})\notin S_{f}.

On the other hand
for any sufficiently large
t\in M
we have
0<|d\cdot\Phi(t)-d\cdot a|<\varepsilon
and, by (

) and (

),

\displaystyle|h(d\cdot\Phi(t))-\Psi(t)^{d}|

\displaystyle=|h(d\cdot\Phi(t))-e^{d\cdot\Phi(t)}+e^{d\cdot\Phi(t)}-\Psi(t)^{d}|

\displaystyle\geq|e^{d\cdot\Phi(t)}-\Psi(t)^{d}|-|h(d\cdot\Phi(t))-e^{d\cdot\Phi(t)}|

\displaystyle\geq|d\cdot\Phi(t)-d\cdot a|^{m}-|d\cdot\Phi(t)-d\cdot a|^{m+1}

\displaystyle>|d\cdot\Phi(t)-d\cdot a|^{m+1}.

So for every sufficiently large
t
we have:

(7)

f(t)=(\Phi(t),\Theta(t),\Psi(t))\in S_{f}.

Now consider the union
S=\bigcup_{f}S_{f}
where
f
ranges in
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\}
. Then
S
does not contain
(\alpha,\beta,e^{\alpha})
and is semialgebraic over
M
.

Claim 2
.

For all
t\in M
sufficiently large,
\chi_{t}(M)\subseteq S
.

Proof.

By (
2
) and (
7
),
L_{t}\cap\chi_{t_{0}}(M)=\{f_{1}(t),\ldots,f_{k}(t)\}\subseteq S
for all
t\in M
sufficiently large, say
t\geq t_{2}
. Hence it suffices to show that, for all sufficiently large
t^{\prime}
, we have
\chi_{t^{\prime}}(M)\subseteq\bigcup_{t\geq t_{2}}L_{t}\cap\chi_{t_{0}}(M)
. To this end, we observe that if
t^{\prime}
is sufficiently large then
\forall x(0<|x-a|\leq t^{\prime-1}\implies|d\cdot x-d\cdot a|^{-1}\geq t_{2})
holds, hence if
x\in\chi_{t^{\prime}}(M)
then
x\in L_{t}\cap\chi_{t_{0}}(M)
with
t=|d\cdot x-d\cdot a|^{-1}\geq t_{2}
.
∎

We have thus proved that for all sufficiently large
t
the quantifier-free formula
\chi_{t}(x,y,z)\wedge(x,y,z)\notin S
is not satisfiable in
M
. However it is satisfied in
N
by
(\alpha,\beta,e^{\alpha})
, and since it has complexity
<\ell
we have a contradiction with the hypothesis
A_{<\ell}
and Proposition
11.4
.
∎

Corollary 11.9
.

Assume
A_{<\ell}
. Let
Q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
be an
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-Khovanskii point over
M
. If one of the coordinates
q_{i}
has a standard part in
M
, then
q_{i}\in M
.

Proof.

Assume
Q
solves a Khovanskii system of
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-polynomials
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
and that some coordinate
q_{i}
has a standard part
\operatorname{st}(q_{i})\neq q_{i}
in
M
. Consider the polynomial
g_{n+1}:=x_{n+1}(x_{i}-\operatorname{st}(q_{i}))-1
. Then
(q_{1},\dots,q_{n},1/(q_{i}-\operatorname{st}(q_{i}))
solves the Khovanskii system given by the
(\ell,n+1)
-
\texp
-polynomials

, so it is a Khovanskii point of complexity

which is not

-bounded. This contradicts the fact that

implies

(Proposition

).
∎

12.
Model completeness

The next two propositions are a reformulation of

, although the hypotheses are different. We include proofs to keep the paper self-contained. As in the previous section, we fix models

of

.

Proposition 12.1
.

Assume
A_{<\ell}
.
Let
Q\in N^{n}
be an
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii point given by a system of
(\ell,n)
-
\texp
-polynomials
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
over
M
. Suppose that:

(i)

g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1}
have complexity
\leq\ell-1
.

(ii)

There is

such that
\det\left(\frac{\partial(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1})}{\partial(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n})}\right)(x)\neq 0
for all

.

(iii)

\det\left(\frac{\partial(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})}{\partial(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})}\right)(x)>0
for all

.

Proof.

Let
V:=V_{\ell,n}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n-1})
. By assumption (ii), every point of
V
is regular, so
V
has dimension
1
. For simplicity we assume the index
i
in (ii) is

, the other cases being similar. Thus (ii) becomes
\det\left(\frac{\partial(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1})}{\partial(x_{2},\dots,x_{n})}\right)(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in V
. This implies that
the projection
\pi:V\to N
on the first coordinate is a local diffeomorphism and therefore it has finite fibers by o-minimality.

By
A_{<\ell}
and Proposition
11.8
, every Khovanskii point over
M
of complexity
\leq\ell
is
M
-bounded, so the coordinates
(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
of

are
M
-bounded.

Claim 1
.

There are

,
a<b
in
M
and a positive

such that, letting
X=[a,b]
and
Y=\{y\in N^{n-1}:|y|\leq c\}
, the set

contains

and it can be written as a disjoint union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}
where each

is an
N
-definable continuous differentiable function from

to

.

Similarly,

can be written as a disjoint union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}f^{\prime}_{i}
where each
f^{\prime}_{i}
is an
M
-definable continuous differentiable function from
X
to
Y
(interpreted in
M
).

Note that we are not claiming that
f_{i}^{\prime}
is the trace of

in the small model

, and in fact a priori the trace may not be definable.

Proof of claim.

Work in the big model
N
. Since
V
has dimension
1
, for all but finitely many

the set

V(r)=\{(x,y)\in V:x\in N,y\in N^{n-1},|y|=r\}

is finite.
Let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in N^{n-1}
be pairwise distinct points such that the
M
-bounded points of
\pi^{-1}(q_{1})
are exactly
(q_{1},\xi_{1}),\dots,(q_{1},\xi_{k})
.

Let
c\in M
be strictly larger than
\max_{i=1}^{k}|\xi_{i}|
and such that
V(c)
is finite. Since
V(c)
is defined by a formula of complexity
<\ell
with parameters in
M
, it follows by
A_{<\ell}
and Proposition
11.4
that

.

Consider the projection
D=\pi(V(c))
on the first coordinate. Then
D
is a finite subset of
(-1,1)\cap M
, and it does not contain

by the choice of

. Let

be the maximal open sub-interval of

containing
q_{1}
and disjoint from

. If

has standard part in

it coincides with that endpoint, which is absurd since

with endpoints in
M
, such that
q_{1}\in X
.
Define

Y=\{y\in N^{n-1}:|y|\leq c\},\qquad F=(X\times Y)\cap V.

Since
X\cap D=\emptyset
, there are no points
(x,y)\in F
with
|y|=c
.

For

, let

be the set of all

has cardinality
\geq m
.
Note that
\pi:F\to X

for all

We will show that

is closed. To this end consider a point

. Then there are an open interval

having

in its boundary and definable continuous functions

from

contains

and these

, then

We have thus proved that each

13.
Lifting Khovanskii points from the residue field

In the rest of the paper we prove that, under a suitable hypothesis, the residue field of a convex subring of a model
N
of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
can be embedded in
N
. This will be used to prove the completeness of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
and
T^{dc}_{\exp}
assuming
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Fix
N\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
and a convex subring
A\subset N
.

Lemma 13.1
.

Let
F\subseteq A
be a subfield of
N
and let
\underline{F}=\operatorname{res}(F)\subset A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
be its image under the residue map.

Let
(a,b)\in(A/\mathfrak{m}_{A})^{n}
be an
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii point over
\underline{F}
satisfying the
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii system
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
in
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
. Then there is a unique
(\alpha,\beta)\in A^{m}
with
\operatorname{res}(\alpha,\beta)=(a,b)
that satisfies the corresponding system over
F
(obtained by identifying
F
with
\underline{F}
via the residue map).

Proof.

Since
F
is contained in
A
, the restriction of the residue map to
F
is injective, so we can identify
F
with
\underline{F}
and consider
F
as a common subfield of
N
and
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
. Hence we may consider
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
as
\texp
-polynomials over
F
and look for solutions in
N
.

Let
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in A^{m}
be an arbitrary point with
\operatorname{res}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})=(a,b)
. Points (1)–(3) below are all consequences of the fact that taking residues commutes with the ring operations and
\texp
(Corollary
5.3
).

(1)

f_{i}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{m}_{A}
for
i=1,\dots,n

(2)

For each
i,j
,
\operatorname{res}\left(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\right)=\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(a,b)
, hence the Jacobian determinant of
(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
computed at
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
does not lie in
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
.

(3)

For each
i,j,k
,
\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in A
(since
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in A^{n}
and the coefficients of the
\texp
-polynomials
\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}
lie in
F
).

Intuitively this says that
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
is very small, the Jacobian determinant is not too small, and the second derivatives are not too large. It is easy to see that (1)–(3) ensure that the hypotheses of Newton’s method for definably complete expansions of a field (see
[
Ser08a
, Theorem 7]
) are satisfied, so there is a zero
(\alpha,\beta)
of
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
with
\operatorname{res}(\alpha,\beta)=\operatorname{res}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})=(a,b)
. Moreover
(\alpha,\beta)
must be a regular zero since its residue
(a,b)
is a regular zero. Uniqueness follows from the fact that if there are two such zeros, then the Jacobian determinant must vanish at some point of the segment that joins them, but this would contradict point (
2
) above.
∎

[

Lemma 13.2

Let

Suppose that

Lemma 13.3
.

Let
F\subseteq A
be a subfield of
N
. Let
(a,b)\in(A/\mathfrak{m}_{A})^{n}
be an
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii point over
\underline{F}=\operatorname{res}(F)
. Suppose that

. Then there is

\operatorname{res}(\alpha,\beta)=(a,b)

.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
13.1
we identify
F
and
\underline{F}
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be the ideal of all polynomials in
F[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell}]
that vanish at
(a,b,\texp(a))\in(A/\mathfrak{m}_{A})^{n+\ell}
. Since

, the fraction field of

has transcendence degree
\ell
over

, hence by Lemma

, there are
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\in\mathfrak{p}
and

not in
\mathfrak{p}
, such that

.

Since
(a,b)
is an
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii point over
F
, there are
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in\mathfrak{p}
such that
(a,b)
is a regular zero of
F_{p_{1}},\ldots,F_{p_{n}}
. We claim that
(a,b)
is a regular zero also of the system
F_{h_{1}},\ldots,F_{h_{n}}
.

By construction,

belong to
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})
. We can then write polynomial equations of the form

. Substituting
\texp(x_{i})
for

for

and differentiating, we obtain that

lie in the

-linear span of
\{\nabla F_{h_{1}}(a,b),\dots,\nabla F_{h_{n}}(a,b)\}
. Since
(a,b)
is a regular zero of
(F_{p_{1}},\ldots,F_{p_{n}})
, the vectors
\nabla F_{p_{i}}(a,b)
are

\nabla F_{h_{i}}(a,b)
must also be
F
-linearly independent, namely

is a regular zero of

, as desired.

By Lemma

, we can lift

of the system

, so in particular

is a zero of
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})
. Let us also observe that

is not zero (as

).

We claim that there is section

is a polynomial over

(a,b,\texp(a))
, then

also vanishes at
(\alpha,\beta,\texp(\alpha))
when evaluated in

. Assuming the antecedent, we have

. Since

for all

and

The existence of the section

.
It remains to observe that this inclusion implies that

is the unique lifting to

of any

g\in M
. Since
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k},a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\in(-1,1)
, there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g/dm\in(-1,1)
, so we can write
e^{\frac{g}{d}}
for the element
\texp(g/dm)^{m}\in M
.
By the assumptions
(a,b)
is a regular zero of a system of
\texp
-polynomials
F_{p_{1}}(\bar{x},\bar{u}),\ldots,F_{p_{n}}(\bar{x},\bar{u})
over
M(t,\texp(t)^{\mathbb{Q}})
in the variables
\bar{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
and
\bar{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-\ell})
. Let
\bar{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell-1})
be a new list of variables and consider the substitution

x_{i}=dX_{i}
for
i<\ell
and

x_{\ell}=\sum_{j\leq k}r_{j}\tfrac{t_{j}}{d}+\sum_{i<\ell}k_{i}X_{i}+\tfrac{g}{d}.

The differential of the map

L(\bar{X},\bar{u})=(\bar{x},\bar{u})

has rank
n-1
and
L(a^{\prime},b)=(a,b)
, so the differential of
(F_{p_{1}},\ldots,F_{p_{n}})\circ L
has rank
n-1
at
(a^{\prime},b)
.

Under the substitution
L
,

\texp(x_{\ell})=e^{\frac{g}{d}}\prod_{j\leq\ell}\texp(t_{j})^{r_{j}/d}\prod_{i<\ell}\texp(X_{i})^{k_{i}}

and
\texp(x_{i})=\texp(X_{i})^{d}
for
i<\ell
. Moreover,
e^{\frac{g}{d}}\in M
(because
M
is closed under
\texp
), so choosing
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large one can find
\texp
-polynomials
f_{i}(\bar{X},\bar{u})
over
M(t,\texp(t)^{\mathbb{Q}})
such that

(F_{p_{i}}\circ L)(\bar{X},\bar{u})=\frac{f_{i}(\bar{X},\bar{u})}{\texp(t)^{n_{0}}\texp(X)^{n_{0}}}.

Since
\texp(t)
and
\texp(X)
are never zero, the rank of the differential of
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
at
(a^{\prime},b)
coincides with the rank of
(F_{p_{1}},\ldots,F_{p_{n}})\circ L
at
(a^{\prime},b)
and it is therefore equal to
n-1
. By omitting a suitable
f_{i}
we obtain a non-zero minor of rank
n-1
, hence a system of
\texp
-polynomial witnessing the fact that
(a^{\prime},b)
is a
(\ell-1,n-1)
-Khovanskii point over
M(t,\texp(t)^{\mathbb{Q}})
. This completes the proof of (2).

The proof of (1) follows the same strategy, but it is simpler: we write
da_{\ell}=\sum_{i<\ell}k_{i}a_{i}
and obtain
\texp
-polynomial
F_{p_{1}},\dots,F_{p_{n}}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. The rest of the modifications is clear.
∎

Theorem 14.2
.

Assume
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
T^{dc}_{\texp}
is complete.

Proof.

Since
T^{dc}_{\texp}
is model complete (Theorem
12.4
), it suffices to show that every
\aleph_{0}
-saturated model
N
of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
contains a substructure
M\subset N
that is elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
. So let
N\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
be
\aleph_{0}
-saturated. Let
\Fin(N)\subset N
be the subring of finite elements and let
\mu\subset\Fin(N)
be its maximal ideal. For every
\alpha\in A
there is a unique real number
a\in\mathbb{R}
that determines the same Dedekind cut over
\mathbb{Q}
and we say that
a:=\operatorname{st}(\alpha)
is the
standard part
of
\alpha
. By saturation
\operatorname{st}:\Fin(N)\to\mathbb{R}
is surjective.
By Proposition
5.4
, we can identify
\Fin(N)/\mu
with
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
and the standard part map
\operatorname{st}
with the residue map
\operatorname{res}:\Fin(N)\to\Fin(N)/\mu
. Let

\ecl^{\mathbb{R}_{\texp}}(\emptyset)
be the
\ecl
-closure of
\emptyset
in
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
. In
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
the exponential closure coincides with the definable closure (see
[
JW08
, Theorem 4.2]
or Corollary
12.5
), so
\ecl^{\mathbb{R}_{\texp}}(\emptyset)
is an elementary substructure of
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
. Every element of
\ecl^{\mathbb{R}_{\texp}}(\emptyset)
is a component of an
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii point
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Fix such a point
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and suppose that
the components of
a\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent. By
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
\td(a,b,\texp(a)/\mathbb{Q})=\ell
. By Lemma
13.3
it then follows that there is a unique tuple
(\alpha,\beta)\in A^{n}
with (componentwise) residue
(a,b)=\operatorname{st}(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta,\texp(\alpha))\subset A
. The same holds without the assumption that the components of
a
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent because we can reduce to that case by a repeated application of Lemma
14.1
(
1
).
Letting
a,b
vary, the union
P
of the fields
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta,\texp(\alpha))
is contained in
\Fin(N)
and is isomorphic to
\ecl^{\mathbb{R}_{\exp}}(\emptyset)
under the residue map, so in particular it is elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{R}_{\texp}
.
∎

Theorem 14.3
.

Assume
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
T^{dc}_{\exp}
is complete.

Proof.

This follows from the completeness of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
and Ressayre’s theorem (Theorem

).
∎

Remark 14.4
.

(1)

In an attempt to prove Theorem

, Krapp proved, assuming
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
, that the prime model of

can be embedded into any o-minimal exponential field
[

]
, but the embedding could not be proved to be elementary.

(2)

The saturation hypothesis in the proof of Theorem

was introduced to ensure that

is a model of

15.
Embedding the residue restricted exponential field

In this section we prove an embedding result for the residue field of a convex subring of a model of
T^{dc}_{\texp}
, similar to the one implicitly used in the proof of Theorem
14.2
. The theorem is reminiscent of the results in
[
DL95
]
, but the assumptions are weaker. In particular, we do not assume that the convex subring
A\subset N
is
T
-convex in the sense of that paper.

Theorem 15.1
.

Let
N\models T^{dc}_{\texp}
and let
A\subseteq N
be a convex subring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
. Assume that
A
contains a substructure
C
of
N
whose image under the residue map
\operatorname{res}:A\to A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
is
\ecl
-closed in
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
. Then there is a restricted exponential subfield
R_{A}
of
N
containing
C
such that
A=R_{A}+\mathfrak{m}_{A}
.

In particular, if
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
holds or if
A
contains an elementary substructure of
N
, the residue field
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
can be embedded in
N
through a section of the residue map that preserves
\texp
.

Proof.

First notice that if
\text{SC}_{\mathbb{R}}
holds, then one can take for
C
a copy of the prime model of
T_{\exp}
and embed it in
N
as in the proof of Theorem
14.2
. Moreover, it is not difficult to see that the residue of an elementary substructure contained in
A
is
\ecl
-closed. It is then sufficient to prove the first part of the statement.

By Proposition
8.2
the exponential closure defines a pregeometry on
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, so we can fix a
\ecl
-basis
\mathcal{B}
of
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
over
\underline{C}=\operatorname{res}(C)
. We assume without loss of generality that each element of the basis is in
(-1,1)
(we can reduce to this case taking the reciprocal of the elements not in
(-1,1)
). Now lift
\mathcal{B}
to a family
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
of elements of
A
that is mapped bijectively to
\mathcal{B}
via the residue map. For each
c\in A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
there is a finite tuple
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})
from
\mathcal{B}
such that
c\in\ecl(\underline{C}t)
. This implies that there are
\ell,n\in\mathbb{N}
and an
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii point
(a,b)\in(A/\mathfrak{m}_{A})^{n}
over
\underline{C}(t)
such that
c
is a component of
(a,b)
.

In particular this shows that
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
is a union of subfields of the form

\underline{C}_{t,a,b}=\underline{C}(t,a,b,\texp(t)^{\mathbb{Q}},\texp(a))\subset A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}

where:

•

t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})
is a
k
-tuple from
\mathcal{B}
and
t\subset(-1,1)
.

•

(a,b)
is an
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii point over
\underline{C}(t,\texp(t)^{\mathbb{Q}})
.

•

\texp(a)=(\texp(a_{1}),\ldots,\texp(a_{\ell}))
where
a_{i}
is the
i
-th component of
a
.

•

\texp(t)^{\mathbb{Q}}
is the set of all elements of
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
of the form
\texp(t_{i})^{q}
where
t_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
is a component of
t
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}
.

•

k,\ell,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now fix
t,a,b
as above and let

\tau=(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{k})
be the unique
k
-tuple in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
with
\operatorname{res}(\tau)=t
.
We will prove that there is a unique
n
-tuple
(\alpha,\beta)\in A^{n}
with
\operatorname{res}(\alpha,\beta)=(a,b)
such that

C_{\tau,\alpha,\beta}=C(\tau,\alpha,\beta,\texp(\tau)^{\mathbb{Q}},\texp(\alpha))\subset A

where the operations are performed in
N
.

Claim 1
.

\td(t,\texp(t)/\underline{C})=2k
.

Proof.

Since
\underline{C}
is
\ecl
-closed, by the predimension inequality (Theorem
8.4
), we have
\td(t,\texp(t)/\underline{C})\geq\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}(t/\underline{C})+\etd(t/\underline{C})=2k
. The other inequality is clear since
(t,\texp(t))
is a tuple of length
2k
.
∎

Claim 2
.

C(\tau,\texp(\tau))\subseteq A
.

Proof.

By Claim
1
, the components of
(t,\texp(t))
are algebraically independent over
\underline{C}
. Thus there is a ring homomorphism
s:\underline{C}[t,\texp(t)]\to C[\tau,\texp(\tau)]
which is a partial section of the residue map, hence it is an isomorphism.
In particular
C[\tau,\texp(\tau)]
contains no elements of
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, so its fraction field is contained in
A
.
∎

Claim 3
.

There is a unique tuple
(\alpha,\beta)\in A^{n}
with residue
(a,b)\in(A/\mathfrak{m}_{A})^{n}
such that
C(\alpha,\tau,\beta,\texp(\alpha),\texp(\tau))\subset A
.

Proof.

By a repeated application of Lemma
14.1
(
2
), we can assume that the components of
a\in(A/\mathfrak{m}_{A})^{\ell}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent modulo
\spn_{\mathbb{Q}}(\underline{C}\cup t)
. (This also includes the case when
\ell=0
and
a
is the empty tuple).

Granted the linear independence, by the predimension inequality (Theorem
8.4
),

\displaystyle\td(a,t,\texp(a),\texp(t)/\underline{C})

\displaystyle\geq\ldim_{\mathbb{Q}}(a,t/\underline{C})+\etd(a,t/\underline{C})

\displaystyle=(\ell+k)+k

\displaystyle=\ell+2k.

and since
\td(t,\texp(t)/\underline{C})=2k
we obtain

\td(a,\texp(a)/\underline{C}(t,\texp(t))\geq\ell
. By Lemma
7.8
the other inequality also holds because
(a,b)
is an
(\ell,n)
-Khovanskii point over
\underline{C}(t,\texp(t)^{\mathbb{Q}})
, so
\td(a,\texp(a)/\underline{C}(t,\texp(t)^{\mathbb{Q}})=\ell
. By Lemma
13.3
, applied to
F=C(\tau,\texp(\tau)^{\mathbb{Q}})
, it follows that
C(\alpha,\tau,\beta,\texp(\alpha),\texp(\tau))\subset A
.
∎

To finish the proof, note that the fields of the form
C_{\tau,\alpha,\beta}
form an upper directed system with

C_{\tau,\alpha,\beta}\cup C_{\tau^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\subseteq C_{\tau\cup\tau^{\prime},\alpha\alpha^{\prime},\beta\beta^{\prime}}.

We can thus define
R_{A}
as the union of these fields and note that, by construction,
R_{A}
is closed under
\texp
.
∎
