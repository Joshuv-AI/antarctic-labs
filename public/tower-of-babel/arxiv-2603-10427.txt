Quark spin correlation inside hyperons
Lucia Oliva, Qun Wang, Xin-Nian Wang

Abstract
The global spin polarization of hyperons in heavy-ion collisions have been investigated by including spin correlation effects among their constituent quarks. The available data on global spin polarizations of hyperons and spin alignments of vector mesons provide constraints on phase space functions of the spin polarization and correlation. These constraints can lead to inequalities under some approximations, which might provide possible clues for the presence of quark spin correlation inside hyperons at lower collision energies.



I
Introduction

Spin physics in heavy-ion collisions has attracted a lot of interest
since the first measurement of the global polarization of
\Lambda

hyperons by the STAR Collaboration at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider
(RHIC)
(
STAR:2017ckg
)
followed by a series of later experiments
at different energies or under different conditions
(
STAR:2018gyt
;
STAR:2020xbm
;
STAR:2021beb
;
ALICE:2019onw
;
HADES:2022enx
)
.
This confirms the theoretical predictions made almost two decades
ago
(
Liang:2004ph
;
Liang:2004xn
;
Voloshin:2004ha
;
Betz:2007kg
;
Gao:2007bc
;
Becattini:2007sr
)
.

Recently the STAR Collaboration measured nonvanishing global spin
alignment of the
\phi
vector meson
(
STAR:2022fan
)
, implying
a strong spin correlation between the strange and antistrange quark
that combine to form the
\phi
meson
(
Sheng:2019kmk
;
Sheng:2022wsy
;
Sheng:2022ffb
)
,
for recent reviews, see, e.g. Refs.
(
Chen:2023hnb
;
Chen:2024afy
)
.
The experimental observations and corresponding theoretical studies
provide an opportunity to study the spin correlations
(
Pang:2016igs
;
Sheng:2025puj
)

in high energy heavy-ion collisions for the first time. Very recently
the STAR Collaboration also measured the spin correlation between

\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
in proton-proton collisions
(
STAR:2025njp
)

which are inherited from spin-correlated strange quark-antiquark virtual
pairs
(
Tornqvist:1980af
;
Tornqvist:1986pe
;
Ellis:2011kq
;
Gong:2021bcp
;
Wu:2024asu
)
,
which could serve as probes into QCD confinement.

A systematic description of quark (antiquark) spin correlations in
quark matter can be provided by the multi-quark spin density matrix
(SDM) in spin space
(
Lv:2024uev
;
Zhang:2024hyq
)
. From the multi-quark
SDM, one can obtain the SDM for a vector meson/hyperon through the
transition operator to convert quark-antiquark/three quarks to the
vector meson/hyperon. The hadron’s spin polarization can then be determined
by its SDM and depends not only on those of its constituent quarks
but also on the spin correlations among them. The spin correlations
in previous calculations of global and local spin polarizations for
hyperons were all neglected: it seems that the data could be well
described without the need for spin correlations
(
Xia:2018tes
;
Karpenko:2016jyx
;
Sun:2017xhx
;
Li:2017slc
;
Wei:2018zfb
;
Vitiuk:2019rfv
;
Becattini:2020ngo
;
Fu:2020oxj
;
Ryu:2021lnx
;
Li:2021zwq
;
Palermo:2024tza
;
Sapna:2025yss
)
.
However, there is an indication for a possible disagreement between
the data and model calculations for the global polarization of
\Omega

hyperons
(
zhiwan_xu:qm2025
)
. A natural question arises if the
possible disagreement has to do with neglecting the spin correlation
among strange quarks in
\Omega
. In this paper, we try to examine
this problem by including the spin correlation effects into the spin
polarizations of hyperons. Based on density operators for quarks (antiquarks)
in spin-momentum space, we can derive spin Wigner functions as the
SDM elements in phase space for vector mesons and baryons which are
expressed as phase space integrals of their constituent quarks’ SDM
weighted by their coordinate wave functions, constituent quark distributions
and Clebsch-Gordan coefficients. In this way we obtain spin polarizations
of vector mesons and hyperons depending on spin polarization and correlation
functions for quarks. In comparison with data for global spin polarizations
of hyperons and spin alignments of vector mesons, one can derive constraints
for quark spin correlations under some approximations.

By convention, the components of a three-vector can be expressed as

\boldsymbol{a}=(a_{x},a_{y},a_{z})=(a^{x},a^{y},a^{z})
, where we
do not distinguish superscripts and subscripts for its spatial components.

The spin Wigner functions (SWF) are actually the spin density matrix

In this section, we will derive the SWF in phase space for vector
mesons and baryons in non-relativistic quantum theory.

II.1
Spin density operators for one-particle, two-particle and three-particle
systems

We start from the density operator for a non-relativistic spin-1/2
particle on mass-shell defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{(1)}^{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r,s}\int[d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}_{1}][d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}_{2}]W(r,s;\boldsymbol{p}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})\left|r,\boldsymbol{p}_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle s,\boldsymbol{p}_{2}\right|

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r,s}\int[d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}][d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}]d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}e^{-i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}W(r,s;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\left|r,\boldsymbol{p}+\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right\rangle\left\langle s,\boldsymbol{p}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r,s}\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}\int[d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}]W(r,s;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\int[d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}]e^{-i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\left|r,\boldsymbol{p}+\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right\rangle\left\langle s,\boldsymbol{p}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right|.

Here
r,s=\pm 1/2
denote the spin states along the spin quantization
direction. We have used the shorthand notation
[d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}]\equiv d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}/(2\pi)^{3}
,
and changed in second line the momenta
(\boldsymbol{p}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})

to
(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{q})
with
\boldsymbol{p}_{1,2}=\boldsymbol{p}\pm\boldsymbol{q}/2
,
we also defined the Wigner function
W(r,s;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})

as

\displaystyle W(r,s;\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{p})\equiv

\displaystyle W(r,s;\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{q}/2,\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{q}/2)=\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}e^{-i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}W(r,s;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p}),

\displaystyle W(r,s;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})=

\displaystyle\int[d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}]e^{i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}W(r,s;\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int[d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}]e^{i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\left\langle r,\boldsymbol{p}+\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\left|\rho_{(1)}^{\mathrm{op}}\right|s,\boldsymbol{p}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right\rangle,

(2)

where
\boldsymbol{x}
is the particle’s position conjugate to the
relative momentum
\boldsymbol{q}
, and we have used the orthogonality
relation for momentum states

\left\langle r,\boldsymbol{p}\right.\left|s,\boldsymbol{k}\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{k}).

(3)

We assume that the Wigner function of a non-relativistic particle
can be factorized into the polarized and unpolarized parts

\displaystyle W(r,s;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})=

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\left\langle r\right|\hat{\rho}_{(1)}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\left|s\right\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\frac{1}{2}\left[\delta_{rs}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{rs}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\right]

(4)

where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
denote
the Pauli matrices,
f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})
and
\boldsymbol{P}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})

are the unpolarized and polarized distribution functions respectively,
and the second line defines the SDM for one particle
\hat{\rho}_{(1)}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})
.

The density operator for two spin-1/2 particles can be defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{(12)}^{\mathrm{op}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1},s_{1},r_{2},s_{2}}\int\prod_{n=1,2}[d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}_{n}][d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}_{n}]d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}_{n}e^{-i\boldsymbol{q}_{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{n}}

\displaystyle\times W\left(\{r_{n},s_{n};\boldsymbol{x}_{n},\boldsymbol{p}_{n}\},n=1,2\right)\prod_{n=1,2}^{\otimes}\left|r_{n},\boldsymbol{p}_{n}+\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{n}}{2}\right\rangle\left\langle s_{n},\boldsymbol{p}_{n}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{n}}{2}\right|,

(5)

where the joint Wigner function for two particles can be put into
the form

W\left(\{r_{n},s_{n};\boldsymbol{x}_{n},\boldsymbol{p}_{n}\},n=1,2\right)=f_{1}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1})f_{2}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(12)}\left|s_{1}s_{2}\right\rangle,

(6)

where
f_{n}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n},\boldsymbol{p}_{n})
with
n=1,2

are one-particle distribution functions, and the SDM for two particles

\hat{\rho}_{(12)}
can be parameterized as

\hat{\rho}_{(12)}=\hat{\rho}_{(1)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1})\otimes\hat{\rho}_{(2)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})+\frac{1}{2^{2}}c_{ij}^{(12)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1},\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})\sigma_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\sigma_{j}^{(2)},

(7)

with
i,j=x,y,z
being the indices of Pauli matrices. Note that
\hat{\rho}_{(n)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n},\boldsymbol{p}_{n})

with
n=1,2
are one-particle SDM defined in (
4
)
and
c_{ij}^{(12)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1},\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})

is the spin correlation function between two particles.

Similar to Eq. (
5
), we can define the density operator

\rho_{(123)}^{\mathrm{op}}
for three particles with the joint Wigner
function similar to (
6
),

W\left(\{r_{n},s_{n};\boldsymbol{x}_{n},\boldsymbol{p}_{n}\},n=1,2,3\right)=\prod_{n=1,2,3}f_{n}(\boldsymbol{x}_{n},\boldsymbol{p}_{n})\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(123)}\left|s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right\rangle,

(8)

where the SDM for three particles is given as

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{(123)}=

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{(1)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1})\otimes\hat{\rho}^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})\otimes\hat{\rho}^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{3},\boldsymbol{p}_{3})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2^{3}}c_{ijk}^{(123)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1};\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2};\boldsymbol{x}_{3},\boldsymbol{p}_{3})\sigma_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\sigma_{j}^{(2)}\otimes\sigma_{k}^{(3)}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2^{2}}\left[c_{ij}^{(12)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1};\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})\sigma_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\sigma_{j}^{(2)}\otimes\hat{\rho}^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{3},\boldsymbol{p}_{3})\right.

\displaystyle+c_{jk}^{(23)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2};\boldsymbol{x}_{3},\boldsymbol{p}_{3})\hat{\rho}^{(1)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1})\otimes\sigma_{j}^{(2)}\otimes\sigma_{k}^{(3)}

\displaystyle\left.+c_{ik}^{(13)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1};\boldsymbol{x}_{3},\boldsymbol{p}_{3})\sigma_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{\rho}^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})\otimes\sigma_{k}^{(3)}\right].

(9)

Here
i,j,k=x,y,z
are the indices of Pauli matrices, and
c_{ijk}^{(123)}

is the spin correlation function for three particles.

II.2
Spin Wigner function for vector mesons and baryons

Now we can apply the density operators
\rho_{(12)}^{\mathrm{op}}

and
\rho_{(123)}^{\mathrm{op}}
to the quark-antiquark and three-quark
system respectively, in which we used the shorthand notations
(12)\equiv(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})

and
(123)\equiv(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})
. We consider hadronization processes

q_{1}\bar{q}_{2}\to V
and
q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}\to B
. The spin density
operators of the vector meson and baryon are given by

\displaystyle\rho_{V}^{\mathrm{op}}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{V}\rho_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\dagger},

\displaystyle\rho_{B}^{\mathrm{op}}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{B}\rho_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\dagger},

(10)

where
\mathcal{M}_{V/B}
denote the transition operators for
q_{1}\bar{q}_{2}

and
q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}
to form the vector meson
V
and baryon
B

respectively. The SWF for the vector meson and baryon in phase space
can be obtained by

\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{V/B}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})=\int[d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}]e^{i\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\left\langle jm^{\prime};\boldsymbol{p}+\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right|\rho_{V/B}^{\mathrm{op}}\left|jm;\boldsymbol{p}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right\rangle,

(11)

where
j
and
m(m^{\prime})
denote the spin and magnetic quantum
numbers for the vector meson and baryon.

Evaluation of
\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{V/B}
by Eq. (
11
)
involves the amplitudes of
\mathcal{M}
which map the quarks to
hadrons in the forms

\displaystyle\left\langle s_{1}s_{2};\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{1}}{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{2}}{2}\right|\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\dagger}\left|jm;\boldsymbol{p}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left\langle s_{1}s_{2}\right|\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\dagger\mathrm{spin}}\left|jm\right\rangle\left\langle\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{1}}{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{2}}{2}\right|\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\dagger\mathrm{mom}}\left|\boldsymbol{p}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right\rangle,

(12)

\displaystyle\left\langle s_{1}s_{2}s_{3};\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{1}}{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{2}}{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{3}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{3}}{2}\right|\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\dagger}\left|jm;\boldsymbol{p}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left\langle s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right|\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\dagger\mathrm{spin}}\left|jm\right\rangle\left\langle\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{1}}{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{2}}{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{3}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{3}}{2}\right|\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\dagger\mathrm{mom}}\left|\boldsymbol{p}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}\right\rangle.

(13)

Here we have used the factorization ansatz for the transition operators

\mathcal{M}_{V/B}=\mathcal{M}_{V/B}^{\mathrm{spin}}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{V/B}^{\mathrm{mom}}
,
where
\mathcal{M}_{V/B}^{\mathrm{spin}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{V/B}^{\mathrm{mom}}

are the spin and momentum parts of
\mathcal{M}_{V/B}
respectively.
The spin parts of the amplitudes are assumed to be proportional to
the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients

\displaystyle\left\langle s_{1}s_{2}\right|\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\dagger\mathrm{spin}}\left|jm\right\rangle=

\displaystyle N_{V}\left\langle s_{1}s_{2}\right|\left.jm\right\rangle=N_{V}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2}),

\displaystyle\left\langle s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right|\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\dagger\mathrm{spin}}\left|jm\right\rangle=

\displaystyle N_{B}\left\langle s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right|\left.jm\right\rangle=N_{B}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}).

(14)

Here
N_{V}
and
N_{B}
are two proportionality constants. The
momentum parts of the amplitudes are assumed to be proportional to
the vector meson’s and baryon’s non-relativistic wave functions

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{p}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{2}\right|\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\dagger\mathrm{mom}}\left|\boldsymbol{p}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\psi_{V}(\boldsymbol{p}_{b})\delta^{(3)}\left(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\boldsymbol{p}_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{p}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{3}\right|\mathcal{M}_{B}^{\dagger\mathrm{mom}}\left|\boldsymbol{p}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\psi_{B}(\boldsymbol{p}_{b},\boldsymbol{p}_{c})\delta^{(3)}\left(\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\boldsymbol{p}_{2}-\boldsymbol{p}_{3}\right),

(15)

where
\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\equiv(\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\boldsymbol{p}_{2})/2
,

\boldsymbol{p}_{c}\equiv(\boldsymbol{p}_{1}+\boldsymbol{p}_{2}-2\boldsymbol{p}_{3})/3
,
and
\psi_{V,B}
are the vector meson’s and baryon’s wave functions
in momentum space.

Using Eqs. (
7
) and (
12
) in Eq. (
11
),
we finally obtain the SWF for vector mesons as

\displaystyle\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{V}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\approx

\displaystyle N_{V}^{2}\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}_{b}[d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}_{b}][d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}_{b}]\exp\left(-i\boldsymbol{q}_{b}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{b}\right)

\displaystyle\times f_{q}\left(\boldsymbol{x}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{x}_{b},\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\right)f_{\bar{q}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{x}_{b},\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\right)

\displaystyle\times\psi_{V}^{*}\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{b}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{b}\right)\psi_{V}\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{b}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{b}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{r_{1},s_{1},r_{2},s_{2}}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2})C^{*}(jm^{\prime};r_{1}r_{2})\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}\right\rangle.

(16)

In the elements of SDM
\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}\right\rangle
,
we have set
\boldsymbol{x}_{1,2}=\boldsymbol{x}\pm\boldsymbol{x}_{b}/2

and
\boldsymbol{p}_{1,2}=\boldsymbol{p}/2\pm\boldsymbol{p}_{b}
,
where
\boldsymbol{x}_{1,2}
and
\boldsymbol{p}_{1,2}
are the
positions and momenta of the quark and antiquark defined in Eq. (
5
).
Note that the meson’s wave function in momentum space is used in (
16
).
We can rewrite (
16
) in terms of the meson’s wave
function in coordinate space as

\displaystyle\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{V}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\approx

\displaystyle N_{V}^{2}\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{y}_{1}d^{3}\boldsymbol{y}_{2}[d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}_{b}]\exp\left[i\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\cdot(\boldsymbol{y}_{1}-\boldsymbol{y}_{2})\right]\psi_{V}^{*}\left(\boldsymbol{y}_{1}\right)\psi_{V}\left(\boldsymbol{y}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times f_{q}\left(\boldsymbol{x}+\frac{1}{4}(\boldsymbol{y}_{1}+\boldsymbol{y}_{2}),\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\right)f_{\bar{q}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{1}{4}(\boldsymbol{y}_{1}+\boldsymbol{y}_{2}),\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{r_{1},s_{1},r_{2},s_{2}}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2})C^{*}(jm^{\prime};r_{1}r_{2})\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}\right\rangle,

(17)

where we have used the Fourier transformation for the meson’s wave
functions (the upper/lower sign corresponds to
\boldsymbol{y}_{1}
/
\boldsymbol{y}_{2}
)

\psi_{V}\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\pm\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{b}\right)=\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{y}_{1,2}\psi_{V}(\boldsymbol{y}_{1,2})\exp\left[-i\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\pm\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{b}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{1,2}\right],

(18)

and set
\boldsymbol{x}_{1,2}=\boldsymbol{x}\pm(\boldsymbol{y}_{1}+\boldsymbol{y}_{2})/4

and
\boldsymbol{p}_{1,2}=\boldsymbol{p}/2\pm\boldsymbol{p}_{b}

in all phase space functions in
\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}\right\rangle
.

Similarly, using Eqs. (
9
) and (
13
)
in Eq. (
11
), we can derive the SWF for baryons as

\displaystyle\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{B}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\approx

\displaystyle N_{B}^{2}\int\prod_{n=b,c}[d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}_{n}][d^{3}\boldsymbol{q}_{n}]d^{3}\boldsymbol{x}_{n}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-i\boldsymbol{q}_{b}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{b}-i\boldsymbol{q}_{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{c}\right)f_{q_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1})f_{q_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})f_{q_{3}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{3},\boldsymbol{p}_{3})

\displaystyle\times\psi_{B}^{*}\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{b}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{b},\boldsymbol{p}_{c}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{c}\right)\psi_{B}\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{b}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{b},\boldsymbol{p}_{c}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{c}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\{r_{n},s_{n},n=1,2,3\}}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2}s_{3})C^{*}(jm^{\prime};r_{1}r_{2}r_{3})

\displaystyle\times\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right\rangle,

(19)

where we have set the positions and momenta of three quarks in the
distribution functions and in
\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right\rangle

as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}_{1}=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{x}_{b}+\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{x}_{c},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}_{2}=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{x}_{b}+\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{x}_{c},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}_{3}=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{2}{3}\boldsymbol{x}_{c},

(20)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{p}_{b}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{p}_{c},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{b}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{p}_{c},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_{c}.

(21)

We can also rewrite (
19
) in terms of the baryon’s
wave functions in coordiante space as

\displaystyle\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{B}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\approx

\displaystyle N_{B}^{2}\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{y}_{1}d^{3}\boldsymbol{y}_{2}d^{3}\boldsymbol{z}_{1}d^{3}\boldsymbol{z}_{2}[d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}_{b}][d^{3}\boldsymbol{p}_{c}]

\displaystyle\times\exp\left[i\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\cdot(\boldsymbol{y}_{1}-\boldsymbol{y}_{2})+i\boldsymbol{p}_{c}\cdot(\boldsymbol{z}_{1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{2})\right]

\displaystyle\times f_{q_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1})f_{q_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})f_{q_{3}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{3},\boldsymbol{p}_{3})\psi_{B}^{*}\left(\boldsymbol{y}_{1},\boldsymbol{z}_{1}\right)\psi_{B}\left(\boldsymbol{y}_{2},\boldsymbol{z}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\{r_{n},s_{n},n=1,2,3\}}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2}s_{3})C^{*}(jm^{\prime};r_{1}r_{2}r_{3})\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right\rangle,

(22)

where we have set the positions of three quarks in the distribution
functions and in
\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right\rangle

as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}_{1}=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}+\frac{1}{4}(\boldsymbol{y}_{1}+\boldsymbol{y}_{2})+\frac{1}{6}(\boldsymbol{z}_{1}+\boldsymbol{z}_{2}),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}_{2}=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{1}{4}(\boldsymbol{y}_{1}+\boldsymbol{y}_{2})+\frac{1}{6}(\boldsymbol{z}_{1}+\boldsymbol{z}_{2}),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}_{3}=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}-\frac{1}{3}(\boldsymbol{z}_{1}+\boldsymbol{z}_{2}),

(23)

while the momenta of three quarks are the same as in Eq. (
21
).
We also used the Fourier transformation to convert the baryon’s wave
functions from momentum space to coordinate space

\displaystyle\psi_{B}\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\pm\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{b},\boldsymbol{p}_{c}\pm\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{c}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}\boldsymbol{y}_{1,2}d^{3}\boldsymbol{z}_{1,2}\psi_{B}\left(\boldsymbol{y}_{1,2},\boldsymbol{z}_{1,2}\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left[-i\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{b}\pm\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{b}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{1,2}-i\left(\boldsymbol{p}_{c}\pm\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{c}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{z}_{1,2}\right],

(24)

where the upper/lower sign corresponds to
(\boldsymbol{y}_{1},\boldsymbol{z}_{1})
/
(\boldsymbol{y}_{2},\boldsymbol{z}_{2})
.

II.3
Short- and long-range correlations

We have finally derived in Eqs. (
17
) and (
22
)
the SWF for the vector meson and baryon in phase space, which are
the main results of this section. In the SWF for the vector meson
given by (
17
), we see integrals over coordinates

\boldsymbol{y}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{y}_{2}
in the vector meson’s
wave functions
\psi_{V}^{*}(\boldsymbol{y}_{1})\psi_{V}(\boldsymbol{y}_{2})

and also an integral over
\boldsymbol{p}_{b}
(the relative momentum
of
q
and
\bar{q}
) which is conjugate to
\boldsymbol{y}_{1}-\boldsymbol{y}_{2}
.
In the integrand, there are phase space distributions for
q
and

\bar{q}
which depend on
\boldsymbol{y}_{1}+\boldsymbol{y}_{2}

and
\boldsymbol{p}_{b}
. As part of the integrand, the polarization
functions
\boldsymbol{P}_{q}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1})
,

\boldsymbol{P}_{\bar{q}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})

and correlation function
c_{ij}^{(12)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1},\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})

or any product of them appear in the SDM. In other words, the SWF
for the vector meson involves an average of the polarization and correlation
functions for
q
and
\bar{q}
over the phase space volume defined
by the meson’s wave function and weighted by the distribution functions
of
q
and
\bar{q}
. So the averages (or integrals) over
\boldsymbol{y}_{1}
,

\boldsymbol{y}_{2}
and
\boldsymbol{p}_{b}
is restricted by the
meson’s wave function and correspond to the short-range correlation
for
q
and
\bar{q}
. In contrast, an average over the phase space
variables
(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})
of the vector meson corresponds
to the long-range correlation outside the meson’s internal space.

Similar to the case of the vector meson, there are also short- and
long-range correlations of three quarks in the baryon. The only difference
from the meson’s case is that the short-range correlation in the baryon
involves the integrals over
(\boldsymbol{y}_{1},\boldsymbol{z}_{1})
,

(\boldsymbol{y}_{2},\boldsymbol{z}_{2})
,
\boldsymbol{p}_{b}

(conjugate to
\boldsymbol{y}_{1}-\boldsymbol{y}_{2}
) and
\boldsymbol{p}_{c}

(conjugate to
\boldsymbol{z}_{1}-\boldsymbol{z}_{2}
) in the baryon’s
SWF. This is because the baryon’s wave function depends on two relative
coordinates
\boldsymbol{y}
and
\boldsymbol{z}
of three quarks
as shown in (
23
).

We can rewrite Eqs. (
17
) and (
22
)
into compact forms:

\displaystyle\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{V}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\approx

\displaystyle N_{V}^{2}\sum_{r_{1},s_{1},r_{2},s_{2}}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2})C^{*}(jm^{\prime};r_{1}r_{2})

\displaystyle\times\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}\right|\left\langle\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1},\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2})\right\rangle_{V}\left|s_{1}s_{2}\right\rangle,

(25)

\displaystyle\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{B}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{p})\approx

\displaystyle N_{B}^{2}\sum_{\{r_{n},s_{n},n=1,2,3\}}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2}s_{3})C^{*}(jm^{\prime};r_{1}r_{2}r_{3})

\displaystyle\times\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\right|\left\langle\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{p}_{1},\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{p}_{2},\boldsymbol{x}_{3},\boldsymbol{p}_{3})\right\rangle_{B}\left|s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right\rangle,

(26)

where
\left\langle\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\right\rangle_{V}

and
\left\langle\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\right\rangle_{B}

represent the averages (integrals) in Eqs. (
17
) and
(
22
) over phase space volumes inside the meson
and baryon respectively that give the short-range correlations.

II.4
Hadronization process as a quantum measurement

In Quantum Information Science (QIS), a quantum measurement process
is in general made through a collection of measurement operators satisfying
a completeness relation, which define a generalized measurement
(
Nielsen2015
)
.

Projective (von Neumann) measurements arise as a special
case in which the measurement operators are orthogonal projectors.
In Ref.
(
Wu:2024mtj
)
, the decays of hyperons are described
as effective generalized measurements acting on the spin degrees of
freedom. In the following we argue that the hadronization process
can also be interpreted as a type of generalized measurement.

The key observation is that the transition operators
\mathcal{M}_{V/B}=\mathcal{M}_{V/B}^{\mathrm{spin}}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{V/B}^{\mathrm{mom}}

in (
10
) play the role of measurement operators. The
effects of
\mathcal{M}_{V/B}
are shown in Eqs. (
17
)
and (
22
) or in compact forms in Eqs. (
25
)
and (
26
). We can look at a very simple case in which
the space and momentum dependence of all variables is neglected. Then
the spin density matrices for vector mesons and baryons in Eqs. (
25
)
and (
26
) can be put into simple forms (we neglected
the normalization of spin density matrices which will be done later):

\displaystyle\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{V}=

\displaystyle\sum_{r_{1},s_{1},r_{2},s_{2}}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2})C^{*}(jm^{\prime};r_{1}r_{2})\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{B}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\{r_{n},s_{n},n=1,2,3\}}C(jm;s_{1}s_{2}s_{3})C^{*}(jm^{\prime};r_{1}r_{2}r_{3})

\displaystyle\times\left\langle r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\right|\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\left|s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\right\rangle,

(27)

where
j=1
and
m=\pm 1,0
in
\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{V}
for the
vector meson, and
j=1/2
or
3/2
and
m=\pm 1/2
or
m=\pm 1/2,\pm 3/2

in
\rho_{m^{\prime}m}^{B}
for the baryon. We see that the Clebsch-Gordan
coefficients are actually measurement operators (matrices) in spin
space

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{M}^{(j)}(m;r_{1}r_{2})\equiv

\displaystyle C^{*}(jm;r_{1}r_{2}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)}(m;r_{1}r_{2}r_{3})\equiv

\displaystyle C^{*}(jm;r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}),

(28)

where the subscript “
M
” in
\mathcal{M}_{M}^{(j)}
stands
for mesons with
j=0,1
correpsonding to the scalar and vector meson
respectively. Note that the measurement operator for the vector meson
is denoted as
\mathcal{M}_{V}^{(j)}\equiv\mathcal{M}_{M}^{(j=1)}
.
So
\mathcal{M}_{M}^{(j)}
is a
(2j+1)\times 4
matrix and
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)}

is a
(2j+1)\times 8
matrix, while
\mathcal{M}_{M}^{(j)\dagger}

is a
4\times(2j+1)
matrix and
\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)\dagger}
is
a
8\times(2j+1)
matrix.

With the measurement operators (
28
) and their Hermitian
conjugates, Eq. (
27
) can be put into the compact form

\displaystyle\rho_{V}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{V}^{(j)}\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\mathcal{M}_{V}^{(j)\dagger},

\displaystyle\rho_{B}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)}\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)\dagger},

(29)

where we have put the superscripts “
V
” and “
B
” in Eq.
(
27
) to subscripts for notational convenience. The
probabilities of obtaining the vector meson and the spin-
j
baryon
are

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{M}(j=1)=

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\rho_{V}=\textrm{Tr}\left[\mathcal{M}_{V}^{(j)}\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\mathcal{M}_{V}^{(j)\dagger}\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{B}(j)=

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\rho_{B}=\textrm{Tr}\left[\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)}\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)\dagger}\right],

(30)

where the traces are taken in spin space. Note that
\left\{\mathcal{P}_{M}(j)\right\}

and
\left\{\mathcal{P}_{B}(j)\right\}
are probabilities for all
possible
j
for mesons and baryons with the normalization conditions

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0,1}\mathcal{P}_{M}(j)=

\displaystyle 1,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1/2,1/2,3/2}\mathcal{P}_{B}(j)=

\displaystyle 1.

(31)

For the measurement operators, we have the properties of the positive
semidefiniteness

\mathcal{M}_{M/B}^{(j)\dagger}\mathcal{M}_{M/B}^{(j)}\geq 0,

(32)

and the completeness

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0,1}\mathcal{M}_{M}^{(j)\dagger}\mathcal{M}_{M}^{(j)}=

\displaystyle 1_{4},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1/2,1/2,3/2}\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)\dagger}\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)}=

\displaystyle 1_{8},

(33)

where
1_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
unit matrix, and there are
two spin doublets (corresponding to the spin triplet and singlet of
two quarks) in forming the baryon from three quarks. Based on the
measurement postulate in quantum information science, by the generalized
measurement, the initial states
\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}

and
\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}
instantaneously transform to
the states
\hat{\rho}_{M}^{(j)}
and
\hat{\rho}_{B}^{(j)}
respectively

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\mapsto\hat{\rho}_{M}^{(j)}\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{M}(j)}\mathcal{M}_{M}^{(j)}\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\mathcal{M}_{M}^{(j)\dagger},

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\mapsto\hat{\rho}_{B}^{(j)}\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{B}(j)}\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)}\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\mathcal{M}_{B}^{(j)\dagger}.

(34)

Note that
\hat{\rho}_{M}^{(j)}
and
\hat{\rho}_{B}^{(j)}
are
normalized so that we have
\mathrm{Tr}\hat{\rho}_{M}^{(j)}=1
and

\mathrm{Tr}\hat{\rho}_{B}^{(j)}=1
.

We can calculate the von Neumann entropy for
q_{1}\bar{q}_{2}
and

q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}
as well as for the meson and baryon through their
normalized SDMs as

S\left(\hat{\rho}_{i}\right)=-\mathrm{Tr}\left(\hat{\rho}_{i}\ln\hat{\rho}_{i}\right),

(35)

where
i=q_{1}\bar{q}_{2},q_{1}q_{2}q_{3},M,B
. We can rewrite
\hat{\rho}

(
\hat{\rho}\equiv\hat{\rho}_{i}
, we suppress the index
i
) as

\hat{\rho}=\hat{\rho}_{0}+\delta\hat{\rho}
, where
\hat{\rho}_{0}=1_{D}/D

with
1_{D}
being the unit matrix of dimension
D
. When the matrix
elements
\delta\hat{\rho}_{mn}\ll 1
we can approximate the von Neumann
entropy to the quadratic order
(\delta\hat{\rho})^{2}

S\left(\hat{\rho}\right)\approx\ln D-\frac{D}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left[(\delta\hat{\rho})^{2}\right].

(36)

For a maximall-mixed state we have
\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\rho}^{2})=\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\rho}_{0}^{2})=1/D
,
while for a pure state we have
\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\rho}^{2})=1
. Then
we obtain the inequality

0\leq\mathrm{Tr}\left[(\delta\hat{\rho})^{2}\right]\leq 1-1/D.

(37)

So we see that the second term of the von Neumann entropy (
36
)
is always negative.

Using Eq. (
36
) we can calculate
S\left(\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\right)

and
S\left(\hat{\rho}_{V}\right)
to the quadratic order of the
spin density’s deviation from
\hat{\rho}_{0}
. By assuming
P_{q_{1}/\bar{q}_{2}}\equiv\sqrt{\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{1}/\bar{q}_{2}}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{1}/\bar{q}_{2}}}\ll 1
,

c_{ij}^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\ll 1
and
P_{q_{1}/\bar{q}_{2}}\sim c_{ij}^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}

in Eq. (
7
) we obtain to the quadratic order in

P_{q_{1}/\bar{q}_{2}}
and
c_{ij}^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}

\displaystyle S\left(\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\right)\approx

\displaystyle 2\ln 2-\frac{1}{2}\left[P_{q_{1}}^{2}+P_{\bar{q}_{2}}^{2}+\mathrm{Tr}\left(c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})T}\right)\right],

\displaystyle S\left(\hat{\rho}_{V}\right)\approx

\displaystyle\ln 3-\frac{1}{3}\left[\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{1}}+\boldsymbol{P}_{\bar{q}_{2}}\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\mathrm{Tr}\left(c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})T}\right)-\frac{2}{3}\left(\mathrm{Tr}c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\right)^{2}\right],

(38)

where the superscript “
T
” denotes the transpose of a matrix,
so we have
c_{ij}^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})T}=c_{ji}^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}
.
By the inequality (
37
), one can show

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})T}\right)\geq

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})T}\right)-\frac{2}{3}\left(\mathrm{Tr}c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle 0.

(39)

So we see from Eq. (
38
) that non-zero quark polarizations
or correlations always lower the entropy. It is easy to check the
inequalities in (
39
) by rewriting
c^{(12)}\equiv c^{(q_{1}\bar{q}_{2})}

as a sum over symmetric (S) and anti-symmetric (A) part

c^{(12)}=c_{S}^{(12)}+c_{A}^{(12)},

(40)

where
c_{S,ij}^{(12)}=c_{S,ji}^{(12)}
and
c_{A,ij}^{(12)}=-c_{A,ji}^{(12)}
.
Then the inequalities in (
39
) now become

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(c_{S}^{(12)}\right)^{2}-\mathrm{Tr}\left(c_{A}^{(12)}\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle F\left[c^{(12)}\right]\equiv\mathrm{Tr}\left(c_{S}^{(12)}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\left(\mathrm{Tr}c_{S}^{(12)}\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle 0,

(41)

which hold obviously.

In a similar way, we can also calculate with Eq. (
36
)

S\left(\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\right)
and
S\left(\hat{\rho}_{B}\right)

to the quadratic order of the spin density’s deviation from
\hat{\rho}_{0}
.
The results are

\displaystyle S\left(\hat{\rho}_{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\right)\approx

\displaystyle 3\ln 2-\frac{1}{2}\left[P_{q_{1}}^{2}+P_{q_{2}}^{2}+P_{q_{3}}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\mathrm{Tr}\left(c^{(12)}c^{(12)T}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(c^{(23)}c^{(23)T}\right)+\mathrm{Tr}\left(c^{(13)}c^{(13)T}\right)\right],

\displaystyle S\left(\hat{\rho}_{(3/2)B}\right)\approx

\displaystyle\ln 4-\frac{5}{18}\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{1}}+\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{2}}+\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{3}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}F\left[c^{(12)}+c^{(23)}+c^{(13)}\right],

\displaystyle S\left(\hat{\rho}_{(1/2)B}^{MS}\right)\approx

\displaystyle\ln 2-\frac{1}{18}\left(2\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{1}}+2\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{2}}-\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{3}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle S\left(\hat{\rho}_{(1/2)B}^{MA}\right)\approx

\displaystyle\ln 2-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{3}}^{2},

(42)

where “MS” and “MA” mean the mixed symmetric (spin triplet)
and anti-symmetric (spin singlet) state respectively, for
q_{1}

and
q_{2}
. Among ground states of octet baryons with spin-partity

(1/2)^{+}
, the proton, neutron,
\Sigma^{\pm}
and
\Xi^{-,0}

are in mixed symmetric states with the flavor configuration
q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}=aab

(flavor
a\neq b
),
\Sigma^{0}
is also in the mixed symmetric
state with the flavor configuration
q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}=uds
, while
the
\Lambda
hyperon is in the mixed anti-symmetric state with the
flavor configuration
q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}=uds
. We observe in Eq. (
42
)
that there are no spin correlation terms in the entropy for octet
baryons at the quadratic order since they are of cubic or higher order,
while there are spin correlation terms for decuplet baryons. This
means that decuplet baryons are more entangled than octet baryons
in case of no quark polarization.

The comparison of the entropies of
q_{1}\bar{q}_{2}
and
q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}

with those of the corresponding meson and baryon states can provide
insights into the role of polarization and spin correlations in the
entropy evolution associated with quark recombination
(
Greco:2003xt
;
Fries:2003vb
;
Greco:2003mm
;
Fries:2003kq
;
Hwa:2003bn
;
Shao:2004cn
)

in the hadronization process of relativistic heavy-ion collisions.
It can also shed light on the entropy puzzle in the quark combination
model
(
Song:2010bi
)
. Note that the discussion in this subsection
is based on the simplified SDM for the vector meson and baryon in
which we neglected the space and momentum dependence of all variables.
By using the SWFs for the vector meson and baryon in Eqs. (
25
)
and (
26
), the von Neumann entropies for the quark
and hadron systems should have more sophisticated features which will
be reserved for a future study.

III
Spin polarizations of vector mesons and baryons

From the SWF for the vector meson and baryon we can obtain their spin
polarizations
(
Choi:1989yf
;
Kim:1992az
;
Becattini:2016gvu
;
Zhao:2022lbw
)
,

\boldsymbol{P}=\frac{1}{S}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho\boldsymbol{S}\right)}{\mathrm{Tr}\rho},

(43)

where
\boldsymbol{S}
is the spin operator (matrix) for the vector
meson or baryon,
S
is the spin quantum number with the eigenvalue
of
\boldsymbol{S}^{2}
being
S(S+1)
, the polarization has an
upper bound,
P\equiv\sqrt{\boldsymbol{P}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}}\leq 1
.
Here
\rho
denotes the SWF as a spin density matrix given in Eq.
(
17
) for the vector meson and Eq. (
22
)
for the baryon. If we choose the
z
-direction as the spin quantization
direction, the spin polarizations for the vector meson, spin-1/2 and
-3/2 baryons are given by

\displaystyle P_{V}^{z}=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{11}-\rho_{-1,-1}}{\rho_{11}+\rho_{-1,-1}+\rho_{00}},

\displaystyle P_{B_{1/2}}^{z}=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{1/2,1/2}-\rho_{-1/2,-1/2}}{\rho_{1/2,1/2}+\rho_{-1/2,-1/2}},

\displaystyle P_{B_{3/2}}^{z}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\frac{\sum_{s_{z}=\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2}S_{z}\rho_{S_{z},S_{z}}}{\sum_{s_{z}=\pm 3/2,\pm 1/2}\rho_{S_{z},S_{z}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{3/2,3/2}-\rho_{-3/2,-3/2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(\rho_{1/2,1/2}-\rho_{-1/2,-1/2}\right)}{\rho_{3/2,3/2}+\rho_{-3/2,-3/2}+\rho_{1/2,1/2}+\rho_{-1/2,-1/2}},

(44)

where we have suppressed the indices
V
,
B_{1/2}
and
B_{3/2}

in
\rho
for the vector meson, spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 baryons respectively.
Note that the spin polarizations in Eq. (
44
) are
functions of phase space variables of mesons and baryons before taking
long-range average. The spin polarizations of hyperons can be measured
through their weak decays, but vector mesons only decay through strong
interaction so their polarizations cannot be measured in a conventional
way.

Using the spin-flavor or SU(6) wave functions of mesons and baryons

(
Close:1979bt
)
, we obtain the spin polarizations for the spin-1/2
baryons
\Xi^{-}(ssd)
,
\Xi^{0}(ssu)
and
\Lambda(usd)

(
Lv:2024uev
)

and the spin-3/2 baryon
\Omega^{-}(sss)

(
Zhang:2024hyq
)

including spin correlations among their constituent quarks

\displaystyle P_{\Xi^{-}}^{z}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\langle C_{\Xi^{-}}\right\rangle_{B}}\left\langle\frac{1}{3}\left(4P_{s}^{z}-P_{d}^{z}\right)C_{\Xi^{-}}+\delta\rho_{\Xi^{-}}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle P_{\Xi^{0}}^{z}=

\displaystyle P_{\Xi^{-}}(d\rightarrow u),

\displaystyle P_{\varLambda}^{z}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\langle C_{\Lambda}\right\rangle_{B}}\left\langle P_{s}^{z}C_{\Lambda}-\delta\rho_{\Lambda}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}^{z}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\langle C_{\Omega}\right\rangle_{B}}\left\langle\frac{5}{3}P_{s}^{z}C_{\Omega}+\delta\rho_{\Omega}\right\rangle_{B},

(45)

where

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\Xi^{-}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{3}\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{s}+c_{ii}^{(ss)}-\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{d}-c_{ii}^{(sd)}\right)\left(P_{s}^{z}-P_{d}^{z}\right)

\displaystyle-4c_{iz}^{(ss)}P_{d}^{i}+2\left(c_{iz}^{(sd)}-2c_{zi}^{(sd)}\right)P_{s}^{i}+c_{iiz}^{(ssd)}-4c_{zii}^{(ssd)},

\displaystyle C_{\Xi^{-}}=

\displaystyle 3+\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{s}+c_{ii}^{(ss)}-4\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{d}+c_{ii}^{(sd)}\right),

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\Xi^{0}}=

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\Xi^{-}}(d\rightarrow u),

\displaystyle C_{\Xi^{0}}=

\displaystyle C_{\Xi^{-}}(d\rightarrow u),

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\Lambda}=

\displaystyle c_{iz}^{(us)}P_{d}^{i}+c_{iz}^{(ds)}P_{u}^{i}+c_{iiz}^{(uds)},

\displaystyle C_{\Lambda}=

\displaystyle 1-\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{d}+c_{ii}^{(ud)}\right),

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\Omega}=

\displaystyle c_{zii}^{(sss)}+2c_{zi}^{(ss)}P_{s}^{i}-4\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{s}+c_{ii}^{(ss)}\right)P_{s}^{z},

\displaystyle C_{\Omega}=

\displaystyle 3+3\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{s}+c_{ii}^{(ss)}\right).

(46)

In Eq. (
45
) the short-range average is taken inside
a phase space volume defined by a specific baryon’s wave function
and weighted by the distribution functions of the baryon’s constituent
quarks as described in Eq. (
26
) and Sect.
II.3
.

For the following discussion, we assume that all baryon wave functions
in coordinate or momentum space have a similar form at the hadronic
scale, which provides a good approximation for the present problem.
In other words, we adopt the same wave-function form for all baryon
species.

The 00-element of the spin density matrix for the
\phi
meson is
usually called spin alignment and given by

\rho_{00}^{\phi}=\frac{1}{\left\langle C_{\phi}\right\rangle_{V}}\left[1+\left\langle\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{\bar{s}}+c_{ii}^{(s\bar{s})}-2\left(P_{s}^{z}P_{\bar{s}}^{z}+c_{zz}^{(s\bar{s})}\right)\right\rangle_{V}\right],

(47)

where

C_{\phi}=3+\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{\bar{s}}+c_{ii}^{(s\bar{s})}.

(48)

In Eq. (
47
) the short-range average is taken inside
the
\phi
meson with its spatial or momentum wave function.

IV
Inequalities for spin correlations

The spin polarizations of hyperons and spin alignment of vector mesons
can be measured in experiments. From Eqs. (
45
) and
(
47
), we see that these observables depend on the
spin polarizations and correlation functions of constituent quarks
which have a huge number of unknown components or variables. In order
to identify the relevant features of quark polarizations and correlations,
we have to reduce the number of variables by making appropriate approximations.
In this section we will derive a few inequalities for spin correlations
implied by experimental data under these approximations.

IV.1
Mean field approximation

We make a mean-field approximation for all quark flavors in the short-range
averages in hyperon polarizations given by Eq. (
45
).
This means we do not distinguish flavor dependences in
\boldsymbol{P}_{q}
,

c_{ij}^{(q_{1}q_{2})}
and
c_{ijk}^{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}
. By this
way we imply that
c_{ij}^{(q_{1}q_{2})}
and
c_{ijk}^{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}

are symmetric for the interchange of any two spatial indices. There
are four types of terms:

\displaystyle B_{1}=

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{zi}^{(q_{1}q_{2})}P_{q_{3}}^{i}\right\rangle_{B}\equiv\left\langle c_{zi}^{(qq)}P_{q}^{i}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle B_{2}=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(c_{ii}^{(q_{1}q_{2})}+\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{1}}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{2}}\right)P_{q_{3}}^{z}\right\rangle_{B}\equiv\left\langle\left(c_{ii}^{(qq)}+\boldsymbol{P}_{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{q}\right)P_{q}^{z}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle B_{3}=

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{zii}^{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}\right\rangle_{B}\equiv\left\langle c_{zii}^{(qqq)}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle B_{4}=

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{ii}^{(q_{1}q_{2})}+\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{1}}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{2}}\right\rangle_{B}\left\langle P_{q_{3}}^{z}\right\rangle_{B}\equiv\left\langle c_{ii}^{(qq)}+\boldsymbol{P}_{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{q}\right\rangle_{B}\left\langle P_{q}^{z}\right\rangle_{B},

(49)

and we assume
B_{i}\ll 1
with
i=1,2,3,4
. Then we obtain from
Eq. (
45
)

\displaystyle P_{\varLambda}^{z}\approx

\displaystyle\left\langle P_{q}^{z}\right\rangle_{B}-2B_{1}-B_{2}-B_{3}+B_{4},

\displaystyle P_{\Xi^{-}}^{z}\approx

\displaystyle P_{\varLambda}^{z},

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}^{z}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{5}{3}\left\langle P_{q}^{z}\right\rangle_{B}+\frac{1}{3}\left(2B_{1}+B_{2}+B_{3}\right)-\frac{5}{3}B_{4}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{5}{3}P_{\varLambda}^{z}+2\left(2B_{1}+B_{2}+B_{3}\right)-\frac{10}{3}B_{4}.

(50)

Therefore, within the mean-field approximation we clearly see that

P_{\Xi^{-}}\approx P_{\varLambda}
and
P_{\Omega}\neq(5/3)P_{\varLambda}

with

P_{\Omega}^{z}-\frac{5}{3}P_{\varLambda}^{z}\approx 2\left(2B_{1}+B_{2}+B_{3}\right)-\frac{10}{3}B_{4},

(51)

which should be larger than zero in heavy-ion collisions at energies
lower than 20 GeV, as implied by data on
\Omega
baryon’s global
polarization in Au+Au collisions
(
zhiwan_xu:qm2025
)
. This leads
to an inequality

2B_{1}+B_{2}+B_{3}-\frac{5}{3}B_{4}>0,

(52)

when the collisional energy is less than 20 GeV in Au+Au collisions.

The inequality (
52
) clearly shows the presence
of the spin correlation in heavy-ion collisions at energies lower
than 20 GeV in Au+Au collisions. If quarks are not polarized the inequality
(
52
) is reduced to
\left\langle c_{zii}^{(qqq)}\right\rangle_{B}>0
,
i.e. the three-quark correlation inside hyperons is non-vanishing.
On the other hand, if spin correlation functions are all zero, the
inequality (
52
) is reduced to
\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{q}\right)P_{q}^{z}\right\rangle_{B}>(5/3)\left\langle\boldsymbol{P}_{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{q}\right\rangle_{B}\left\langle P_{q}^{z}\right\rangle_{B}
,
i.e. the three quarks’ polarizations are correlated inside hyperons.

IV.2
With only
s
quark correlations inside hyperons

In this subsection we only consider short-range correlations among
strange quarks and antiquarks inside hyperons, neglecting mixed correlations
between quarks of different flavors. We can then relate the average
polarization of
\Xi
and
\Omega
hyperons with the spin alignment
of the
\phi
meson.

IV.2.1
Short- and long-range components of spin polarizations and correlations

The spin polarizations for quarks and antiquarks
\boldsymbol{P}_{q/\bar{q}}

can be decomposed into the long and short range components. The long
range component
\overline{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q/\bar{q}}
is induced
by local vorticity and has the same sign for the quark and antiquark.
The short range component
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q/\bar{q}}

is from vector fields and has the opposite sign for the quark and
antiquark. Namely the polarization vectors can be written as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{P}_{q}=

\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{P}_{\bar{q}}=

\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\bar{q}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\bar{q}},

(53)

for
q=u,d,s
. We assume that the averages of short range components
inside hyperons are restricted by their wave functions and satisfy

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q}\right\rangle_{B}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{1}}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{2}}\right)\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{3}}\right\rangle_{B}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q}\right\rangle_{B}\neq

\displaystyle 0,

(54)

where
B
represents the baryon wave function. So we have
\left\langle\boldsymbol{P}_{q}\right\rangle_{B}=\overline{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q}
.
We can further assume that long range components do not distinguish
the quark, antiquark and flavor,
\overline{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{i}}=\overline{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\bar{q}_{j}}
,
with
i,j=u,d,s
. All spin correlation matrices
c_{ij}^{(q_{1}q_{2})}

and
c_{ijk}^{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}
are assumed to be of short range.

When taking the short range average of
\delta\rho_{B}
over the
hadron volume we can approximate

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{ij}^{(q_{1}q_{2})}P_{q_{3}}^{k}\right\rangle_{B}\approx

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{ij}^{(q_{1}q_{2})}\right\rangle_{B}\overline{P}_{q_{3}}^{k},

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{1}}\cdot\boldsymbol{P}_{q_{2}}\right)P_{q_{3}}^{z}\right\rangle_{B}\approx

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{1}}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{2}}\right\rangle_{B}\overline{P}_{q_{3}}^{z}+\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{1}}^{i}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{3}}^{z}\right\rangle_{B}\overline{P}_{q_{2}}^{i},

\displaystyle+\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{2}}^{i}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{3}}^{z}\right\rangle_{B}\overline{P}_{q_{1}}^{i},

\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{1}}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{2}}\ll

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{1}}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{q_{2}}\right\rangle_{B}\ll 1.

(55)

In this way we can relate the average polarization of
\Xi
and
\Omega

hyperons with the spin alignment of the
\phi
meson.

IV.2.2
Spin polarizations of hyperons and spin alignment of
\phi
meson

With the decomposition (
53
) and the approximations
in short range averages in Eqs. (
54
) and
(
55
), we obtain

\displaystyle P_{\Lambda}^{z}\approx

\displaystyle\overline{P}_{s}^{z}=\overline{P}_{q}^{z},

\displaystyle P_{\Xi^{-}}^{z}\approx

\displaystyle\overline{P}_{q}^{z}\left(1+\epsilon_{\Xi}\right)-\frac{4}{3}\delta,

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}^{z}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{5}{3}\overline{P}_{q}^{z}\left(1+\epsilon_{\Omega}\right)+\frac{2}{3}\delta+\frac{1}{3}\delta^{\prime},

(56)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\Xi}\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{3}\left\langle\left(\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}\right)^{2}+c_{zz}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\Omega}\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{5}\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}+c_{ii}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}+\frac{2}{5}\left\langle\left(P_{s}^{z}\right)^{2}+c_{zz}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle\delta\equiv

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{zx}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}\overline{P}_{q}^{x}+\left\langle c_{zy}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}\overline{P}_{q}^{y},

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}\equiv

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{zii}^{(sss)}\right\rangle_{B}.

(57)

In the derivation of Eq. (
56
) we have kept

c^{(ss)}
and
c^{(sss)}
only and neglected all other spin correlation
functions.

For the spin alignment of the
\phi
meson, we obtain from Eq. (
47
)

\rho_{00}^{\phi}\approx\frac{1}{3}\left[1+\frac{2}{3}\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\bar{s}}+c_{ii}^{(s\bar{s})}\right\rangle_{V}-2\left\langle\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}\widetilde{P}_{\bar{s}}^{z}+c_{zz}^{(s\bar{s})}\right\rangle_{V}\right].

(58)

If the short range spin polarization and correlation are caused by
vector fields which distinguish particles and antiparticles, we can
make an approximation on the short-range effective correlations

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{\bar{s}}+c_{ii}^{(s\bar{s})}\right\rangle_{V}\approx

\displaystyle-\eta\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}+c_{ii}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}\widetilde{P}_{\bar{s}}^{z}+c_{zz}^{(s\bar{s})}\right\rangle_{V}\approx

\displaystyle-\eta\left\langle\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}+c_{zz}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B},

(59)

where
\eta>0
is a proportionality constant. Then Eq. (
58
)
becomes

\rho_{00}^{\phi}\approx\frac{1}{3}\left(1+\epsilon_{\phi}\right),

(60)

where we defined

\epsilon_{\phi}\equiv-\frac{2}{3}\eta\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}+c_{ii}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}+2\eta\left\langle\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}+c_{zz}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}.

(61)

The quantities
\epsilon_{\Xi}
,
\epsilon_{\Omega}
and
\epsilon_{\phi}

depend on spin correlations in the spin quantization direction and
in transverse directions perpendicular to it. Therefore, we can define
longitudinal and transverse effective correlations as

\displaystyle A_{L}=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}\right)^{2}+c_{zz}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle A_{T}=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\widetilde{P}_{s}^{x}\right)^{2}+\left(\widetilde{P}_{s}^{y}\right)^{2}+c_{xx}^{(ss)}+c_{yy}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B},

(62)

in terms of which the
\epsilon
quantities in Eqs. (
57
)
and (
60
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\Xi}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{3}A_{L},

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\Omega}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{5}(2A_{T}+A_{L}),

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\phi}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3}\eta(A_{T}-2A_{L}).

(63)

IV.2.3
Inequalities for quark spin correlations

By comparing Eqs. (
56
) and (
60
)
with the experimental data, we obtain the following constraints:

\displaystyle P_{\Xi^{-}}-P_{\Lambda}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{3}\overline{P}_{q}^{z}A_{L}-\frac{4}{3}\delta\approx 0,

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}-\frac{5}{3}P_{\Lambda}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3}\overline{P}_{q}^{z}(2A_{T}+A_{L})+\frac{2}{3}\delta+\frac{1}{3}\delta^{\prime}>0,

\displaystyle\rho_{00}^{\phi}-\frac{1}{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{9}\eta(2A_{L}-A_{T})>0,

(64)

which leads to one equality and two inequalities for quark spin correlations

\displaystyle\delta\approx-\overline{P}_{q}^{z}A_{L}

,

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}-4\overline{P}_{q}^{z}(A_{T}+A_{L})>

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle 2A_{L}-A_{T}>

\displaystyle 0.

(65)

The explicit expressions of above equality and inequalities are

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{zx}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}\overline{P}_{q}^{x}+\left\langle c_{zy}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}\overline{P}_{q}^{y}\approx

\displaystyle-\overline{P}_{q}^{z}\left\langle\left(\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}\right)^{2}+c_{zz}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle 4\overline{P}_{q}^{z}\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}+c_{ii}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}<

\displaystyle\left\langle c_{zii}^{(sss)}\right\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{P}}_{s}+c_{ii}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}<

\displaystyle 3\left\langle\left(\widetilde{P}_{s}^{z}\right)^{2}+c_{zz}^{(ss)}\right\rangle_{B}.

(66)

The results in (
66
) clearly show the presence of
spin correlations between
s
quarks in hyperons. If we neglect the
three-quark spin correlation, i.e.
\left\langle c_{zii}^{(sss)}\right\rangle_{B}=0
,
from the first inequality we obtain non-vanishing spin correlation
between two
s
quarks

c_{xx}^{(ss)}+c_{yy}^{(ss)}+c_{zz}^{(ss)}<0.

(67)

The second inequality in (
66
) looks like a quadrupole
structure in spin correlations.

Estimates of quark spin correlations

In order to extract some quantitative estimates of quark spin correlations
inside hyperons, in this subsection we make a further assumption that
long-range correlations are only along the
z
direction, which is
assumed to be the direction of the global orbital angular momentum

\overline{P}_{q}^{z}\approx P_{\Lambda},\;\overline{P}_{q}^{x}\approx\overline{P}_{q}^{y}\approx 0.

This leads to
\delta\approx 0

).
Then, the experimental constraints on
P_{\Xi^{-}}
,

\displaystyle A_{T}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{3}{2\eta}\left(1-3\rho_{00}^{\phi}\right),

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\approx 3P_{\Omega}-P_{\Lambda}\left[5-\frac{6}{\eta}\left(1-3\bar{\rho}_{00}^{\phi}\right)\right].

(70)

V
Summary and conclusion

The global spin polarization of hyperons have been investigated by
including spin correlation effects among their constituent quarks.
Based on density operators for multi-quarks (including antiquarks)
in spin-momentum space, we derived the Spin Wigner Functions (SWF)
for vector mesons or baryons by computing elements of density operators
between two spin-momentum states of the vector meson or baryon. The
SWF are actually spin density matrices in phase space for vector mesons
and baryons which are expressed as phase space integrals of their
constituent quarks’ reduced spin density matrices weighted by their
coordinate wave functions, constituent quark distributions and CG
coefficients. The short range correlation is defined by the average
over phase space of quarks (antiquarks) inside the hadron, while the
long range correlation is by the average over phase space of the hadron.
The available data in global spin polarizations of hyperons and spin
alignments of vector mesons provide constraints on phase space functions
of the spin polarization and correlation appearing in the reduced
spin density matrices for constituent quarks, which consist of large
number of unknown variables. In order to reduce the number of variables
and derive meaningful and simplified constraints, we make two approximations.
In the first one, which is called the mean field approximation, we
do not distinguish different flavors in
\boldsymbol{P}_{q}
,
c_{ij}^{(q_{1}q_{2})}

and
c_{ijk}^{(q_{1}q_{2}q_{3})}
. In the second approximation, we
only consider spin correlation among
s
quarks and neglect others.
In each approximation, we derived a set of inequalities for spin polarization
and correlation functions. These inequalities might provide possible
clues for the presence of spin correlation inside hyperons at lower
collision energies.
