Implementation and Validation of an Emergency Department Consult Tracking Tool for Monitoring Consultant Turnaround Times
Authors: Timothy Layng, Katie Cronin, Noel Mukubwa, Moira Smith, Lauren Smaltz, Colleen Mullins, Jackson Agraz, Michael Chilmaid, R. Andrew Taylor, Andrew Muck, Sarah K. Wendel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo evaluate the implementation of an electronic health record (EHR)-integrated consult tracking tool and assess its utilization in capturing emergency department (ED) specialty consultant turnaround times at an academic, tertiary care medical center.MethodsThis quality improvement study was conducted at a tertiary academic medical center from December 2023 through February 2025. The Epic ASAP Consult Tracker was implemented to capture discrete timestamps for ED specialty consultations. A 3-month validation period (December 2023-February 2024) used manual scribe review of 5401 consultations to verify tool accuracy against surrogate documentation methods. From March 2024 onward, the tool operated independently with automated monthly reporting. Primary outcomes were tool utilization rate and median consultation turnaround time, defined as the interval from consult order entry to receipt of actionable consultant recommendations.ResultsOver the 12-month post-validation period (March 2024-February 2025), 34,663 consultations were tracked across 26 specialty services. During validation, the tracking tool captured shorter median turnaround times (139 minutes) than surrogate methods, including ED course documentation (238 minutes), disposition time (179 minutes), and consultant note signature (273 minutes). The mean monthly utilization rate was 53.3% (SD 4%; range 44.4%-57.8%). The overall median consultation turnaround time was 108 minutes, with substantial specialty-specific variation: the medical intensive care unit had the shortest median response time at 59.5 (IQR: 37.4-97.1), whereas psychiatry had the longest at 195.6 (IQR: 122.6-299.6).ConclusionAn EHR-integrated consult tracking tool is a feasible method for measuring ED consultation turnaround times. The tool was used to document consultation milestones and identified and revealed specialty-specific variation in consultation response times. These findings provide a foundation for future efforts to evaluate consultation processes and improve ED operations.

The Bottom LineSpecialist response times in the emergency department vary dramatically, and until now, we had no reliable way to measure them. We utilized a tracking tool directly into our electronic health record and measured over 34,000 consults across 26 specialties over 12 months. Response times varied widely depending on the specialty, from rapid responses for critical care teams to prolonged waits for others. Critically, our tool captured consultant response times more accurately than older surrogate methods, which substantially overstated delays. You cannot fix what you cannot measure, and now we can measure it.

Introduction

Background

Emergency department (ED) consultations are essential for patient care, influencing diagnostic decisions, treatment plans, and overall length of stay (LOS). Delays in consultant response time and recommendations can contribute to ED overcrowding, prolonged patient wait times, and inefficiencies in care delivery.1 Although delays in consultations are widely recognized and anecdotal reports suggest prolonged response times, systematic tracking and analysis of consultation recommendation times remain limited.

Prior research has demonstrated that prolonged consultations negatively impact ED efficiency, with specialty consultations being associated with increased LOS in tertiary care settings.2, 3, 4 Conversely, improving consultation efficiency has been shown to reduce ED LOS and alleviate overcrowding.5 System-based interventions, such as computerized consultation management systems, institutional guidelines, and structured communication protocols, have been effective in improving response times and workflow efficiency.6,7 Additionally, dedicated consultation teams and real-time tracking systems have demonstrated measurable improvements in consultant responsiveness.8

Specialty-specific initiatives further highlight the need for tailored interventions to streamline workflows and optimize care delivery.9 Furthermore, novel metrics for assessing consultation efficiency provide a framework for evaluating and improving ED operations.3

Importance

Efficient ED consultations are critical for optimizing patient flow, reducing crowding, and ensuring timely initiation of definitive care. However, measurements of consultation turnaround times are often anecdotal with few studies describing the widespread collection of such data. Delayed consultant response times contribute to delays in decision making, increased LOS, and potential adverse patient outcomes4,5,10, 11, 12 Given the growing demands on emergency services and rising patient volumes, addressing consultation inefficiencies is crucial for maintaining high-quality, timely patient care.13 Streamlining consultation workflows can improve operational efficiency, patient satisfaction, and resource utilization.2,14 By systematically tracking and optimizing consultant response times, health care systems can enhance ED efficiency, minimize bottlenecks, and ultimately improve patient outcomes.

Goals of This Investigation

This project aims to implement a novel electronic health record (EHR)-integrated consult tracking tool and characterize ED specialty consultant turnaround times at an academic tertiary care medical center.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

We implemented a new EHR-integrated consult tracking tool as part of an EDquality improvement project, aligned with institutional quality improvement (QI) guidelines. This work took place at an academic, tertiary care medical center with over 75,000 ED visits annually. The analysis utilized existing data within the EHR (Epic Systems, Verona, WI).

To ensure the accuracy of the consultation tracking tool, a structured validation process was conducted over a 3-month period. ED medical scribes manually reviewed all consultation requests placed between December 2023 and February 2024. If the tracking tool was utilized, its recorded timestamps were cross-validated with documentation in Epic. In cases where the tool was not used, scribes employed a systematic approach to determine the earliest documented time at which a plan was communicated by the consultant. The scribe first referenced the ED course within the ED provider note for the earliest mention of a consultant’s plan. If this was unsuccessful, the scribe then compared the first-sign time of the consultant’s note and the disposition time of the patient and chose the earlier of the 2 timestamps.

Following the validation phase, beginning in March 2024, the consultation tracking tool operated independently without additional manual review, and automated reports were generated monthly to monitor performance. Comparisons for the following months were made on consult turnaround times to ensure accuracy. An electronic dashboard was developed to visualize consultation metrics, including turnaround times by specialty service.

Selection of Participants

This analysis included all patients who were seen at the study ED between December 2023 and February 2025 with an ED consultation order placed to a specialty service. Included services were cardiology, dermatology, family medicine, general surgery, gynecologic oncology, gynecology, hand surgery, hematology, internal medicine, medical intensive care unit (micu), neurology, neurosurgery, obstetrics, oncology, ophthalmology, orthopedic surgery, otolaryngology/ent, pediatrics, plastic surgery, psychiatry, spine services, stroke alert, vascular surgery, toxicology, trauma surgery, urology. These specialty services were selected based on their high utilization rates in the ED or their involvement in performance initiatives led by the School of Medicine leadership. Patients were excluded if they left before being seen, or had a consultation placed after a hospital admission order had been placed. Canceled consults or consults with completed times that were negative or zero were removed from data.

Interventions

We implemented the Epic Systems ASAP Consult Tracker (Verona, WI) to enhance the tracking of ED specialty consultations. An ED consult was defined as any consultation occurring before the patient’s admission order or ED discharge. To ensure a unique order was placed for each consulting service, we conducted a comprehensive audit of all existing ED consult orders.

To systematically capture key time points, we developed discrete events linked to each consult order. These events included consultant contact, recontact (if applicable), response time, ED arrival, and plan communication. Each event was manually recorded by a member of the ED team (physician, advanced practice provider [APP], nurse [RN] or scribe). Consultation turnaround time is defined as the elapsed time between an ED consultation request and receipt of actionable recommendations from the consulting service.

To improve visibility, we incorporated a default column into all ED track boards, displaying the number of ordered and outstanding consults per patient. Additionally, we optimized access to the tracking tool by embedding shortcuts in multiple locations, including track boards, chart headers, and high-use chart areas.

The integration of discrete event tracking facilitated the development of a dashboard displaying turnaround metrics, including daily, 7-day, and 30-day rolling median turnaround times, stratified by consulting service. Each dashboard section featured a summary tile highlighting key performance indicators: the total number of consults, the proportion tracked using the tool, median turnaround time from consult order to plan communication, percentage of consults linked to consultant notes, and median turnaround time from consult order to note signature.

Measurements

Extracted variables included ED arrival date and time, type of ED consult, consult order timestamp, consult service name, ordering provider, consult completion timestamp, and the documenting user for consult completion. Notably, the ED service does not have admission privileges at our institution; therefore, consultations for admission were included in the analysis, which is particularly relevant for specialties such as cardiology and internal medicine.

Outcomes and Data Analyses

The primary outcome metric was the utilization rate of the consultation tracking tool, calculated as the proportion of consultations where the tool was used compared with all consultations placed. Secondary metrics included median consultation response time, defined as the time from consult order entry to documented consultant response, and the SDs of consultation turnaround times to assess variability across specialties. Additionally, specialty-specific response efficiency was evaluated by comparing median turnaround times between specialties to identify variability and outliers. Descriptive statistics were used throughout; continuous variables are reported as medians with interquartile ranges, and categorical variables as counts and proportions. Data analysis was conducted using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA) and Tableau (Salesforce, Seattle, WA).

Results

Consultation Volume

A total of 40,064 specialty consultations met inclusion criteria during the 15-month study period (December 2023-February 2025). The validation phase included 5401 consultations, and the subsequent 12-month implementation phase included 34,663 consultations (monthly mean 2889; SD 188). Canceled consults and those with zero or negative completion times were excluded from the data set (N = 7567).

The mean number of consultations per day was 95 (range 48-138). Monthly consultation volume remained relatively stable over the study period (Table 1, Figure 1). Validation Phase

Of the 5401 consultations reviewed during the validation period, 1926 (35.7%) had completion times recorded via the tracking tool. The remaining consultations were documented through surrogate methods: 1583 (29.3%) via the ED course note, 1041 (19.3%) via the ED disposition time, and 860 (15.9%) via the consultant’s note first-sign time. The median turnaround time recorded by the tracking tool was 139 minutes, compared with 179 minutes using disposition time, 238 minutes using ED course documentation, and 273 minutes using note signature. The tracking tool, thus, captured the earliest documentation of plan communication and was the most frequently used single data source.

Consultation Turnaround Times

The overall median consultation turnaround time across all tracked consultations was 108 minutes (IQR: 65-181). There was marked specialty-specific variation: the MICU had the shortest median turnaround time at 59.5 minutes (IQR: 37.4-97), followed by obstetrics at 74.1 minutes (IQR: 25.1-165.6), hematology at 79 minutes (IQR: 46.1-120.1), and pediatrics at 84.5 (IQR: 54.0-127.7). The longest median turnaround times were observed for psychiatry at 195.6 (IQR: 122.6-299.6), spine services at 191.8 (IQR: 110.2-366.5), neurology at 178 minutes (IQR: 108-274), and cardiology consults at 164 minutes (IQR: 97-244). Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted at a single academic medical center where the ED does not hold admission privileges, and all admissions require a specialty consultation. This institutional structure likely increases consultation volume relative to community EDs and may limit generalizability to settings with different admission models. Second, the tracking tool requires manual timestamp entry, introducing the possibility of recording error and contributing to incomplete capture. The 53% utilization rate means that nearly half of consultations were not tracked, and the consultations that were tracked may not be representative of all consultations (eg, more complex or prolonged consultations may be more likely to be tracked, or vice versa). Third, the validation phase relied on ED medical scribes, a resource not universally available, which may limit the reproducibility of the validation methodology. Fourth, turnaround times for services that routinely require additional imaging or laboratory evaluation before providing recommendations (eg, orthopedic surgery, neurosurgery) may appear prolonged relative to services that can provide immediate clinical recommendations, limiting the comparability of raw turnaround times across specialties. Finally, internal medicine was not included in the initial manual validation due to its large volume, though subsequent automated data were collected for this service.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that an EHR-integrated consult tracking tool can be successfully implemented in a high-volume academic ED, achieving sustained adoption over 12 months and providing structured, real-time measurement of specialty consultation turnaround times across 26 services. The tool captured consultation completion earlier than all 3 surrogate documentation methods evaluated during the validation phase, suggesting that retrospective methods systematically overestimate true turnaround times, likely because they reflect documentation delays rather than the actual timing of clinical communication.

This finding has practical implications. If institutions rely on surrogate metrics, such as disposition time or note signature, to benchmark consultation responsiveness, they may substantially overestimate turnaround times, potentially misidentifying the source and magnitude of delays. The difference between the tracking tool’s median (139 minutes during validation) and the note-signature surrogate (273 minutes) underscores the degree to which documentation lag can distort performance measurement. Accurate measurement is a prerequisite for meaningful QI; without it, interventions may be misdirected or their effects unmeasurable.

The observed specialty-specific variation in turnaround times is notable and likely reflects multiple structural factors, including consultant availability, on-call coverage models, the need for ancillary testing before recommendations can be made, and the complexity of the clinical questions posed. The MICU’s short median turnaround time of 59.5 minutes (IQR: 37.4-97.1 minutes) is consistent with a service model that typically involves in-house, immediately available physicians. Conversely, psychiatry and spine services had among the longest median consultation turnaround times, 195.6 minutes (IQR: 122.6-299.6) and 191.8 minutes (IQR: 110.2-366.5), respectively. These longer turnaround times may reflect call-from-home coverage, limited specialist availability, or diagnostic complexity requiring additional evaluation before consultation can be completed.

The mean utilization rate of 53.3% represents a reasonable level of adoption for a manually recorded tool in a busy ED, particularly given that no mandatory compliance mechanism or hard stop was in place. Utilization varied by specialty and likely reflects differences in the workflow familiarity of the clinical teams placing consultations, the complexity of the consultation interaction, and the availability of scribes during the encounter. Strategies to improve utilization could include integration with automated EHR events (eg, consultant note filing or admission order placement), targeted feedback to clinical teams, or incorporation of tracking into scribe workflows as a standard responsibility.

Prior literature supports the value of structured consultation management. Cho et al6 demonstrated that a computerized consultation management system reduced ED LOS, and Geskey et al7 showed that institutional response-time guidelines improved consultant responsiveness. Our work extends this evidence by demonstrating that a commercially available EHR module, requiring no custom software development, can provide the measurement infrastructure needed to support such interventions. The operational dashboard developed alongside the tracking tool enables ongoing performance monitoring, facilitating a continuous QI cycle.

Beyond operational efficiency, robust consultation data may inform broader care redesign. Identifying specialties with consistently prolonged turnaround times could prompt the development of alternative care pathways (eg, protocol-driven ED management of specific conditions, direct admission pathways, or expedited outpatient follow-up) that reduce the need for time-sensitive ED consultation altogether.

An EHR-integrated consult tracking tool is a feasible, sustainable approach to measuring ED specialty consultation turnaround times. The tool was used for 53.3% of consultations without mandatory compliance and revealed specialty-specific variation in consultation response times, providing a foundation for targeted QI. Future work should focus on increasing utilization through automation, evaluating the impact of turnaround time data on ED operational outcomes such as LOS, and testing whether specialty-specific interventions informed by these data improve consultation responsiveness and patient throughput.
