A Rank-Preserving Locality Theorem
Jan Dreier, Szymon Toruńczyk

Abstract
We prove a rank-preserving locality theorem for a syntactic variant of first-order logic, in the spirit of Gaifman’s locality theorem and the rank-preserving locality theorem of Grohe, Kreutzer, and Siebertz. Our result allows for a weak form of scatter sentences, which can be evaluated more efficiently than usual scatter sentences considered in prior work. This is crucial in our application to graphs of bounded merge-width.

Introduction

The goal of this note is to prove the following locality result for a variant of first-order logic. All relevant notions are defined in detail in
Section

2
, and discussed briefly below.

Theorem 1.1

(Locality theorem for dist-FO)
.

Fix
k,q\in\mathbb{N}
. Every
dist-FO
formula
\varphi(\bar{x})
of distance rank
(k,q)

is equivalent to a boolean combination of
local
dist-FO
formulas and
dist-FO
scatter sentences, all of distance rank
(k,q)
.
This boolean combination can be effectively computed, given
k,q
, and
\varphi
.

The logic dist-FO is equivalent in expressive power to first-order logic, but its syntax allows for a more refined measure of quantifier rank, called
distance rank
.
Local
dist-FO formulas are a fragment which only allow for
local quantification
, which ranges over a neighborhood of the free variables of fixed radius.

This statement is in the spirit of Gaifman’s locality theorem
[
Gai82
]
,
and is
almost the same as a statement in our earlier work
[
DT25
,
DT25a
, Theorem 4.1]
.
In this note we fix an error in the proof from
[
DT25a
]
, which was spotted by Nikolas Mählmann. Apart from that, we also simplify the proof significantly.

Our result is inspired by a locality theorem of Grohe, Kreutzer, and Siebertz
[
GKS17
]
.
Similarly as in their result, the challenge is to
rewrite a formula into a boolean combination of scatter sentences and local formulas in a way which does not increase some notion of rank.
This is achieved by changing the syntax, by introducing
distance atoms
and
local quantification
.
The original motivation for those results was to prove that
the problem of evaluating first-order formulas is fixed-parameter tractable in various graph classes (nowhere dense classes in
[
GKS17
]
and classes of bounded merge-width in
[
DT25
]
).

Comparison with the work of Grohe, Kreutzer, and Siebertz.

Comparing
Theorem

1.1
to the locality theorem
[
GKS17
, Theorem 7.5]
of Grohe, Kreutzer, and Siebertz, there are three main differences.
The first one is that we consider a weak notion of
scatter sentences
. In Gaifman’s result, as well as the result of Grohe, Kreutzer, and Siebertz, scatter sentences assert the existence of
t
elements
of some set
X
which are mutually at distance greater than
r
in the Gaifman graph, where
r
and
t
are fixed constants, and
X
is specified by a formula
\alpha(x)
. Determining this is algorithmically hard in some contexts – from the perspective of parametrized complexity, this problem is at least as hard as the distance-
r
independent set problem, which thus needs to be solved separately
[
GKS17
, Theorem 5.1]
.
In contrast, the scatter sentences in
Theorem

1.1

merely ask whether a predetermined inclusion-wise maximal
r
-scattered subset of
X
has size at least
t
. Such a set can be easily computed by a greedy algorithm. This is crucial in our algorithmic application of
Theorem

1.1
to graphs of bounded merge-width, as it allows to circumvent the independent set problem.

Next, the result of
[
GKS17
]
evaluates the resulting formulas in a structure expanded by suitable unary predicates, whereas our resulting formulas are equivalent to the input formulas on any given (unmodified) structure. This is also crucial in our application to graphs of bounded merge-width
[
DT25
]
.

Finally, the result of
[
GKS17
]
applies only to formulas
\varphi(x)
with one free variable, whereas in our statement,
\varphi(\bar{x})
is a formula with any set
\bar{x}
of free variables.

Comparison with the work of Grohe and Schweikardt.

Independently of this work, Grohe and Schweikardt have very recently published a note
[
GS26
]
.
The results of
[
GS26
]
use a very similar syntactic fragment of first-order logic to ours, and measure the distance rank of formulas in a very similar way. The overall goal is also very similar – to provide a locality theorem for first-order logic, in which formulas are expressed as boolean combinations of local formulas and scatter sentences,
and where the rewriting preserves some notion of
rank
(and also
to fix an error in a previous paper
[
GS18
]
).

Overall, the results of the two notes are incomparable, however, two results –

Corollary

2.4
below and the core result
[
GS26
, Theorem 6.3]
– are in spirit the same statements, although minor details differ.
Some differences in the two notes are as follows:

1.

Our result considers more flexible scatter sentences, which express that a single, arbitrarily chosen, inclusion-wise maximal
r
-scattered set has a given size, while
[
GS26
]
considers scatter sentences which express the existence of an
r
-scattered set of a given size. The former is more general and allows for more efficient algorithmic applications, circumventing the independent set problem. In particular, the more flexible form of scatter sentences allows us to apply the locality theorem in the setting of model-checking of first-order formulas on graphs of bounded merge-width
[
DT25a
,
DT25
]
.

2.

The horizon function in our note is of the order
2^{\Theta(q^{2})}
, which is larger than the horizon function in
[
GS26
]
, which is of the order
2^{\Theta(q)}
.
This seems to be related to the more
flexible form of scatter sentences in this paper.

3.

The note
[
GS26
]
derives further locality results (Theorems 7.1 and 8.2), which have no analogue in our note.

4.

The proof in
[
GS26
]
is based on Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé games and types, similarly to our original approach in
[
DT25a
]
. The present proof is based on a syntactic rewriting of formulas, which we find more direct, transparent, and concise. The two approaches are ultimately equivalent, but
we believe that the game-based approach is more prone to errors (as have been spotted in
[
DT25a
,
GS18
]
), since tracing invariants for games turns out to be often subtle.

5.

Finally, the syntactic rewriting approach is algorithmic, and allows for an effective procedure to compute the boolean combination of local formulas and scatter sentences.

[
GS26
]
are also able to derive – a posteriori – the existence of an algorithm computing the equivalent boolean combination, by appealing to Gödel’s completeness theorem for first-order logic. This approach does not provide any upper bound on the duration of the algorithm. Our syntactic approach provides an explicit algorithm whose running time can in principle be analyzed and upper bounded by an explicit computable function (which is enormous but elementary for fixed
q
and
k
), although we refrain from doing so in this note.

Organization of the paper.

In
Section

2
we introduce the notions needed to state the locality theorem,
Theorem

1.1
.
We derive
Corollary

2.4
from it, which gives a rank-preserving normal form for dist-FO formulas. In
Section

3
we prove the locality theorem.

Preliminaries

\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of non-negative integers. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote
[n]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

We consider finite relational signatures only.
A structure
A
over the signature
\sigma
,
or a
\sigma
-structure
, consists of a (possibly infinite) domain
V(A)
and the interpretation

R^{A}\subseteq V(A)^{k}
of each relation symbol
R\in\sigma
of arity
k
.
The
Gaifman graph
of a structure
A
is the graph with vertices
V(A)
and edges connecting pairs of distinct vertices which occur together in some tuple of some relation of
A
. By
\mathrm{dist}^{A}(\cdot,\cdot)
we denote the shortest path metric in the Gaifman graph of
A
, and for
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
u\in V(A)
denote
N^{A}_{r}(u)\coloneqq\{v\in V(A)\,|\,\mathrm{dist}^{A}(u,v)\leqslant r\}
.

2.1
Distance logic and scatter sentences

First-order distance logic.

We define a logic called dist-FO that extends FO by the following
distance atoms
.

For each radius
r\in\mathbb{N}
, dist-FO introduces
a
binary distance atom

\mathrm{dist}(x,y)\leqslant r
expressing that the distance between
x
and
y
in the Gaifman graph is at most
r
.
We call
r
the
radius
of the distance atom.

For each radius
r\in\mathbb{N}
and unary relation symbol
Y
, dist-FO introduces
a
unary distance atom

\mathrm{dist}(x,Y)<r
expressing that the distance from
x
to
Y
in the Gaifman graph is smaller than
r
;
equivalently,
\exists y~Y(y)\land\mathrm{dist}(x,y)<r
.
We call
r
the
radius
of the distance atom.

Formulas of dist-FO are built inductively,
starting with usual atomic formulas of first-order logic (relation symbols or equality applied to variables), distance atoms, boolean connectives, and existential quantification (universal quantification is then expressible using negation).

For a dist-FO formula
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
, structure
A
, and tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in V(A)^{k}

we write
A\models\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})

to denote
that
\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
holds in
A
, which is defined by induction on the structure of
\varphi
, in the expected way.

For a set
\bar{x}
of variables,
we write
\varphi(\bar{x})
to indicate that
\bar{x}
contains the free variables of the formula
\varphi
.
We write
\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},y)\leqslant r
as shorthand for
\bigvee_{x\in\bar{x}}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)\leqslant r
, and

\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leqslant r
as shorthand for

\bigvee_{x\in\bar{x},y\in\bar{y}}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)\leqslant r
.

Note that binary distance atoms can be expressed
using usual first-order formulas (assuming
\sigma
contains finitely many relation symbols of arity
{>}1
).
However, we treat them as primitive atomic formulas, as
we will need to have a fine control over the quantifier rank of the constructed formulas. For this reason, the possibility to measure distances using quantifier-free formulas is essential, similarly as in
[
GKS17
]
.

Horizon functions.

Fix two
horizon functions

\rho^{-},\rho^{+}\colon\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 1}
satisfying the following properties for all
k,q\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle\rho^{-}(k,q)

\displaystyle\geqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)+\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)

\displaystyle\text{for $q\geqslant 1$},

(1)

\displaystyle\rho^{+}(k,q)

\displaystyle\geqslant 9^{k}\cdot\rho^{-}(k,q).

For instance, it is easy to check that the following
functions satisfy (
1
):

\displaystyle\rho^{-}(k,q)\coloneqq 9^{(k+q+1)q}\quad\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\quad\rho^{+}(k,q)\coloneqq 9^{(k+q)(q+1)}.

The functions
\rho^{-}
and
\rho^{+}
will be used to define the allowed radius of distance atoms in dist-FO formulas and scatter sentences of a given
distance rank
(defined below), as well as the radius at which
local quantification
is allowed.

Distance rank.

Let
k,q\in\mathbb{N}
.
We define
dist-FO
formulas of distance rank

(k,q)
by induction on
q
,
as formulas with at most
k
free variables and
quantifier rank at most
q
which are boolean combinations of:

•

atoms of first-order logic (relation symbols in
\sigma\cup\{=\}
applied to variables),

•

unary and binary distance atoms with radius
r\leqslant\rho^{-}(k,q)
,

•

if
q\geqslant 1
, formulas

\exists y~\varphi(\bar{x}y)\quad\quad\text{or}\quad\quad\exists y~\bigl(\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},y)\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\bigr)\land\varphi(\bar{x}y)

where
\varphi(\bar{x}y)
has distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
.

A dist-FO formula is
local

if it never uses the
unrestricted quantification

\exists y~\varphi

(only
local quantification
, that is, of the latter form above, is allowed).

Property (
1
) implies
\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)<\rho^{-}(k,q)
for all
k,q\in\mathbb{N}
with
q\geqslant 1
. Hence, the distance atoms have progressively shorter radii as one traverses the quantifiers of a dist-FO formula inwards. It moreover implies:

Observation 2.1
.

Every
dist-FO
formula of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
with at most
k
free variables also has distance rank
(k,q)
.

Semantic locality.

We first prove a property of local dist-FO formulas, which states that their truth value is determined by a bounded-radius neighborhood of the free variables. We say that a formula
\varphi(\bar{x})
is
semantically
r
-local

if for every structure
A
and tuple
\bar{a}\in A^{\bar{x}}
,
we have that

A\models\varphi(\bar{a})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A[N_{r}(\bar{a})]\models\varphi(\bar{a}).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k,q\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geqslant 1
, and let

\varphi(\bar{x})
be a local
dist-FO
formula of distance rank
(k,q)
.
Then
\varphi
is semantically
\rho^{-}(k,q)
-local.

Proof.

We use the following consequence of (
1
). For every

j\in\{0,\ldots,q\}
,

\sum_{h=1}^{j}\rho^{+}(k+h,q-h)+\rho^{-}(k+j,q-j)\leqslant\rho^{-}(k,q),

where the case
j=0
is trivial, and the case
j>0
follows by applying

\rho^{+}(k+h,q-h)\leqslant\rho^{-}(k+h-1,q-h+1)-\rho^{-}(k+h,q-h)

for
h=1,\ldots,j
and telescoping.

Now fix a structure
A
and a tuple
\bar{a}\in A^{\bar{x}}
. If, during
the evaluation of
\varphi(\bar{a})
, a variable is introduced after
j

local quantifiers, then it is at distance at most

\sum_{h=1}^{j}\rho^{+}(k+h,q-h)
from
\bar{a}
. Any distance atom in the
scope of these
j
quantifiers has radius at most
\rho^{-}(k+j,q-j)
.
By the displayed bound, all vertices that can affect the truth of such an atom
lie in
N_{\rho^{-}(k,q)}^{A}(\bar{a})
. Hence removing all vertices outside this
neighborhood does not change the truth value of
\varphi(\bar{a})
.
∎

Scatter sentences.

A set
S
of vertices of a structure
A
is
r
-scattered
if all vertices in
S

have pairwise distance larger than
r
in the Gaifman graph of
A
. (Thus, a
1
-scattered set is an independent set.)

For every structure
A
, radius
r
, formula
\beta(x)

we fix arbitrarily and once and for all
fix a value
s_{A,r,\beta}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that there is some inclusion-wise maximal
r
-scattered subset of
\{a\in V(A)\mid A\models\beta(a)\}
of size
s_{A,r,\beta}
; the value
\infty
indicates that there exist such sets of arbitrarily large finite size.

Two possible choices of
s_{A,r,\beta}
are described below. The particular choice is not important for the statement of
Theorem

1.1
, but it is relevant in applications of the result, as it might affect the efficiency of evaluation of scatter sentences.

Our locality theorem relies on the fixed values
s_{A,r,\beta}
.
To incorporate it into logical statements, we define logical
scatter sentences

\textbf{scatter}(r,\beta(x),t)
with
A\models\textbf{scatter}(r,\beta(x),t)
if and only if
s_{A,r,\beta}\geqslant t
.
We define
scatter sentences of distance rank
(k,q)
, for
q\geqslant 1
,
as all scatter sentences
\mathbf{scatter}(r,\beta(x),t)
such that

t\leqslant k+q
and
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
,

\beta(x)
is a local
dist-FO
formula of distance rank
(k+i,q-i)
, and

\displaystyle 4\rho^{-}(k+i,q-i)\leqslant r\leqslant 9^{k+i}\rho^{-}(k+i,q-i).

(2)

Note that

9^{k+i}\rho^{-}(k+i,q-i)\leqslant\rho^{-}(k,q)
, which follows from (
1
): first

9^{k+i}\rho^{-}(k+i,q-i)\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+i,q-i)\leqslant\rho^{-}(k+i-1,q-i+1),

and then we iterate
\rho^{-}(a,b)\leqslant\rho^{+}(a,b)\leqslant\rho^{-}(a-1,b+1)
.
Moreover, by
Lemma

2.2
, the formula
\beta(x)
as above
semantically
\rho^{-}(k+i,q-i)
-local, and hence semantically
r/4
-local.

We have the following.

Observation 2.3
.

Every scatter sentence of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
is also a scatter sentence of distance rank
(k,q)
.

We now describe two possible choices of
s_{A,r,\beta}
, each having its own benefits. The first one is more algorithmic, while the second one is more logical.

Greedy choice

For finite structures
A
, an inclusion-wise maximal subset of
X\coloneqq\{a\in V(A)\mid A\models\beta(a)\}
can be constructed by a simple greedy process as follows:
Assuming
v_{1},\dots,v_{i}
have already been chosen, the process selects
v_{i+1}
as the next element of
X
(with respect to some arbitrary, fixed order on
V(A)
)
and is of distance larger than
r
in the Gaifman graph of
A
from
v_{1},\dots,v_{i}
.
If no such
v_{i+1}
can be found, the process terminates and we set
s_{A,r,\beta}\coloneqq i
.
The benefit of this choice of
s_{A,r,\beta}
is that it can be efficiently computed, given
A
, an order on
V(A)
, and
X
.

Maximum size

Another way to define
s_{A,r,\beta}
is to set it to the maximum size of an
r
-scattered subset of
\{a\in V(A)\mid A\models\beta(a)\}
and to
\infty
if arbitrarily large such sets exist. The benefit of this definition is that it does not rely on the choice of an arbitrary order on
V(A)
, and for each
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the property
s_{A,r,\beta}\geqslant t
can be expressed in first-order logic (for fixed
r
and
t
), by the following sentence:

\displaystyle\exists x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\,\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\left(\mathrm{dist}(x_{i},x_{j})>r\right)\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{t}\beta(x_{i}).

(3)

Locality theorem.

We have now stated all the notions occurring in
Theorem

1.1
, repeated below for convenience.
See
1.1

As a corollary of
Theorem

1.1
, we obtain a normal form for dist-FO formulas, which is an analogue of Gaifman’s normal form for first-order logic

]
.
The proof is a straightforward application of

and the definition of scatter sentences.

Corollary 2.4

(Normal form for dist-FO formulas)
.

Fix
k,q\in\mathbb{N}
with
q\geqslant 1
. Every
dist-FO
formula

\varphi(\bar{x})
of distance rank
(k,q)
is equivalent to a boolean
combination of local
dist-FO
formulas of distance rank
(k,q)

and sentences of the form

\exists x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\,\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\left(\mathrm{dist}(x_{i},x_{j})>r\right)\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{t}\beta(x_{i}).

where
t\leqslant k+q
, the formula

\beta(x)
is a local
dist-FO
formula of distance rank

(k+1,q-1)
, the radius
r
satisfies
r\leqslant\rho^{-}(k,q)
,
and
\beta(x)
is semantically
r/4
-local. This boolean combination can be effectively computed, given

k,q
, and
\varphi
.

Proof.

In the definition of scatter sentences, choose
s_{A,r,\beta}
to be the
maximum size of an
r
-scattered subset of

\{a\in V(A)\mid A\models\beta(a)\}
, as in the “Maximum size” choice
above. With this choice, for every
r,t\in\mathbb{N}
and every formula

\beta(x)
, the scatter sentence
\textbf{scatter}(r,\beta(x),t)
is
equivalent to the sentence (
3
).

Apply
Theorem

1.1
to
\varphi(\bar{x})
. We obtain an effectively
computed boolean combination of local
dist-FO
formulas of distance rank

(k,q)
and scatter sentences of distance rank
(k,q)
. Replace each
scatter sentence
\textbf{scatter}(r,\beta(x),t)
in this boolean
combination by the equivalent sentence (
3
).

It remains only to check that the resulting sentences have the parameters
stated in the corollary. Since the scatter sentence has distance rank

(k,q)
, by definition
t\leqslant k+q
. The same definition also gives an
auxiliary
i\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
such that
\beta(x)
is local of distance
rank
(k+i,q-i)
and

4\rho^{-}(k+i,q-i)\leqslant r\leqslant 9^{k+i}\rho^{-}(k+i,q-i)\leqslant\rho^{-}(k,q).

This inequality gives the required bound

.
Repeatedly applying

shows that the same formula
\beta(x)
has distance

(k+1,q-1)
. Finally,

says that

\beta(x)
is semantically
\rho^{-}(k+i,q-i)

-local by the left inequality above.

3
Proof of the locality theorem

3.1
Separation lemma

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
k,q\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
be a local
dist-FO
formula of distance rank
(k,q)
.
The formula

\Big(\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},\bar{y})>\rho^{-}(k,q)\Big)\ \land\ \varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})

is equivalent to a disjunction of formulas of the form

\Big(\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},\bar{y})>\rho^{-}(k,q)\Big)\ \land\ \alpha(\bar{x})\land\beta(\bar{y}),

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are local
dist-FO
formulas of distance rank
(k,q)
.
Moreover, this disjunction can be effectively computed, given
k,q
, and
\varphi
.

Proof.

We prove that assuming the background axiom

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},\bar{y})>\rho^{-}(k,q),

(4)

the formula

\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})

is equivalent to a disjunction of
formulas of the form
\alpha(\bar{x})\land\beta(\bar{y})
, where

\alpha
and
\beta
are local dist-FO formulas of distance rank
(k,q)
.
The only nontrivial properties of the functions
\rho^{-}
,
\rho^{+}
we will use are

\rho^{+}(k,q)\geqslant\rho^{-}(k,q)\geqslant 1
, which follows from the second condition in
(
1
), and, for
q\geqslant 1
,

\rho^{-}(k,q)-\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)\geqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1),

(5)

which is precisely the first condition in (
1
).

We proceed by structural induction on
\varphi
.

In the base case,
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})

is an atomic formula, a binary distance atom with radius up to
\rho^{-}(k,q)
, or a unary distance atom with radius up to
\rho^{-}(k,q)
.
Unary distance atoms involve only one variable, hence they belong entirely to the
\bar{x}
-side or to the
\bar{y}
-side.
The same is true for first-order atoms and binary distance atoms which involve only variables from
\bar{x}
, or only variables from
\bar{y}
.
Otherwise,
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
evaluates to false, by (
4
) and
\rho^{-}(k,q)\geqslant 1
.

Consider the induction step. If
\varphi
is a a disjunction of two simpler formulas, the statement follows trivially by induction.
If
\varphi
is the negation of a simpler formula, we can use

\neg\bigvee_{i}(\alpha_{i}\land\beta_{i})\iff\bigwedge_{i}(\neg\alpha_{i}\lor\neg\beta_{i})
and then distribute. Conjunction can be reduced to negation and disjunction.

Finally, suppose that

\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})=\exists z~\Big(\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x}\bar{y})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\psi(\bar{x}\bar{y}z),

where
\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y}z)

has distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
and
q\geqslant 1
.
Then

\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
is equivalent to the disjunction of the following formulas:

1.

\exists z~(\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1))\land\psi(\bar{x}\bar{y}z)
,

2.

\exists z~(\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{y})\leqslant\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1))\land\psi(\bar{x}\bar{y}z)
,

3.

\exists z~\Big(\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x}\bar{y})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\psi(\bar{x}\bar{y}z)
.

In case 1, the condition

\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)

together with (
4
), (
5
)
and the triangle inequality imply

\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{y})\geqslant\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},\bar{y})-\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})>\rho^{-}(k,q)-\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)\geqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\geqslant\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1).

We can therefore use the inductive assumption to rewrite
\psi(\bar{x}\bar{y}z)

into a disjunction of formulas
\alpha(\bar{x}z)\land\beta(\bar{y})
,
where
\alpha
and
\beta
are local dist-FO formulas of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
.
Therefore, using the fact that existential quantifications and disjunctions commute,
the formula in case 1
is equivalent to a disjunction of formulas of the form

\exists z~\Bigl(\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)\Bigr)\land\alpha(\bar{x}z)\land\beta(\bar{y}),

which is equivalent to the formula

\beta(\bar{y})\ \land\ \exists z~\Bigl(\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)\Bigr)\land\alpha(\bar{x}z).

Each disjunct is (equivalent to) a local dist-FO formula of distance rank
(k,q)
, finishing case 1.
Case 2 follows analogously.

In case 3, we apply the inductive assumption to
\psi

and rewrite it into an equivalent disjunction of formulas of the form

\exists z~\Big(\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{y}\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\alpha(\bar{x}\bar{y})\land\beta(z),

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are local dist-FO formulas of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
.
This again uses the fact that existential quantifications and disjunctions commute.
The above formula is equivalent to

\displaystyle\alpha(\bar{x}\bar{y})\land\exists z~\Big(\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{y}\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\beta(z).

(6)

We argue that, assuming (
4
), the following formulas are equivalent:

\displaystyle\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x}\bar{y})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1),

(7a)

\displaystyle\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)

\displaystyle\lor\quad

\displaystyle\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{y})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1).

(7b)

Indeed, clearly, (
7a
) implies (
7b
). For the converse,
suppose (by symmetry) that

\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)
holds.
Using the triangle inequality and (
5
), we obtain

\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{y})\geqslant\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},\bar{y})-\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})>\rho^{-}(k,q)-\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\geqslant\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1),

which implies (
7a
).

Therefore, the formula (
6
)
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\alpha(\bar{x}\bar{y})\land\exists z~

\displaystyle\Big(\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\beta(z)

\displaystyle\lor\quad\alpha(\bar{x}\bar{y})\land\exists z~

\displaystyle\Big(\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{y})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\beta(z).

(8)

We may apply the inductive assumption to the formula
\alpha(\bar{x},\bar{y})
of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
to obtain an equivalent
disjunction of formulas of the form
\alpha^{\prime}(\bar{x})\land\beta^{\prime}(\bar{y})
.
By basic distributivity laws, (
3.1
) is equivalent to a disjunction of formulas of the forms

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(\bar{x})\land\beta^{\prime}(\bar{y})\land\exists z~\Big(\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\beta(z),

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(\bar{x})\land\beta^{\prime}(\bar{y})\land\exists z~\Big(\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)<\mathrm{dist}(z,\bar{y})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\beta(z).

This construction is effective and yields the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

3.2
Far quantification lemma

The following key lemma establishes an important special case of
Theorem

1.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
k,q\geqslant 1
, and let
\beta(x)
be a local
dist-FO
formula of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
and let
\bar{y}
be a set of
k
variables.
Then the formula

\exists x~\Big(\mathrm{dist}(x,\bar{y})>\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\ \land\ \beta(x)

is equivalent to a boolean combination of local
dist-FO
formulas of
distance rank
(k,q)
and of scatter sentences of distance rank
(k,q)
.
Moreover, this boolean combination can be effectively computed, given
k,q
, and
\beta
.

The proof of
Lemma

3.2

relies on a combinatorial argument which is encapsulated in
Lemma

3.5
below.
We start with the following observation, also made by
[
BGR22
, Lem. 3]
,
which is similar in spirit to the Vitali covering lemma.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
BGR22
]
)
.

Given
r,c\in\mathbb{N}
, a graph, and a set
K
of its vertices, there are
R\in\mathbb{N}
and a set
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
,
such that

1.

r\leqslant R\leqslant r\cdot(c+1)^{|K|-1}
,

2.

the vertices in
K^{\prime}
have pairwise distance larger than
cR
, and

3.

every vertex in
K
has distance at most
R
to
K^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For
t=0,1,2,\ldots
, we construct a subset
K_{t}\subseteq K
of size
|K|-t

such that every vertex in
K
has distance at most
R(t)\coloneqq r(c+1)^{t}
to
K_{t}
.
Set
K_{0}\coloneqq K
.
For
t\geqslant 0
, if every pair of vertices in
K_{t}
has distance larger than
cR(t)
, we are done.
Otherwise, pick vertices
u,v\in K_{t}
at distance at most
cR(t)
.
Set
K_{t+1}\coloneqq K_{t}-\{u\}
,
and observe that every vertex that has distance
R(t)
to
u
has distance
R(t)+cR(t)=R(t+1)
to
v
.
This process terminates at the latest when
t=|K|-1
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Fix
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
G
be a graph and
\bar{a}\in V(G)^{k}
. There is some
R
with
r\leqslant R\leqslant 9^{k-1}r

and a partition of
N_{r}^{G}(\bar{a})
into
clusters
with the following properties:

•

every cluster is a union of sets of the form
N_{r}^{G}(a_{i})
, for
i\in[k]
,

•

every cluster is contained in a ball of radius
2R
, and

•

clusters have pairwise distance larger than
4R
.

Moreover the number
R

and the partition of
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
into clusters
can be determined by tests of the form
\mathrm{dist}^{G}(a_{i},a_{j})\leqslant d
, for
i,j\in[k]
and
d\leqslant 8\cdot 9^{k-1}r
.

Proof.

Apply

Lemma

3.3
to
K\coloneqq\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
and
c\coloneqq 8
.
We get
I\subseteq[k]
,
m:[k]\to I
, and
r\leqslant R\leqslant 9^{k-1}r
such that

\mathrm{dist}^{G}(a_{i},a_{j})>8R
for all
i\neq j\in I
, and

\mathrm{dist}^{G}(a_{i},a_{m(i)})\leqslant R
for all
i\in[k]
.
As the clusters, take the sets
\bigcup_{j\in m^{-1}(i)}N^{G}_{r}(a_{j})
, for
i\in I
.

Then every cluster is contained in a ball of radius
R+r\leqslant 2R
,
and clusters have pairwise distance larger than
8R-2r-2R\geqslant 4R
.

For the “moreover” part, observe that for every fixed set
I\subseteq[k]
,mapping
m:[k]\to I
, and radius
R
with
r\leqslant R\leqslant 9^{k-1}r
,
the conditions

\mathrm{dist}^{G}(a_{i},a_{j})>8R

and
\mathrm{dist}^{G}(a_{i},a_{m(i)})\leqslant R
can be verified
using tests of the form
\mathrm{dist}^{G}(a_{i},a_{j})\leqslant d
, for
i,j\in[k]
and
d\leqslant 9^{k-1}\cdot 8\cdot r
.
∎

The following lemma is
the combinatorial counterpart of
Lemma

3.2
,
and forms
the core of the
proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geqslant 1
.
Let
G
be a graph,
\bar{a}\in V(G)^{k}
a tuple of its vertices, and
let
R
with
r\leqslant R\leqslant 9^{k-1}r
and the partition of
N_{r}^{G}(\bar{a})
into clusters be as in
Corollary

3.4
.
Let
X\subseteq V(G)
, and let
s
be the size of some inclusion-wise maximal
4R
-scattered subset of
X
. Let
H\geqslant 4R+r
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

there is some
x\in X
with
\mathrm{dist}(x,\bar{a})>r
,

2.

(a)

either there is some
x\in X
with
r<\mathrm{dist}(x,\bar{a})\leqslant H
,

(b)

or less than
s
clusters intersect
X
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
there is some
x\in X
with
r<\mathrm{dist}(x,\bar{a})\leqslant 4R+r
.
In this case, clearly both conditions (1) and (2) are satisfied,
as
4R+r\leqslant H
.
Assume therefore that this is not the case, that is,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(x,\bar{a})\leqslant 4R+r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathrm{dist}(x,\bar{a})\leqslant r\qquad\text{for all $x\in X$.}

(9)

We show that then conditions (1) and (2b) are equivalent.
Since clearly (2a) implies (1), in fact (1) and (2) are equivalent in this case, proving the lemma.

Let
S
be an inclusion-wise maximal
4R
-scattered subset of
X
of size
s
.
In particular,
X\subseteq N_{4R}(S)
.

We show that each cluster
C
which intersects
X
contains exactly one element of
S
.
Indeed, suppose
v\in C\cap X
. As
v\in X\subseteq N_{4R}(S)
,
there is some
s\in S
with
\mathrm{dist}(s,v)\leqslant 4R
and so

\mathrm{dist}(s,C)\leqslant 4R.

Since
v\in C\subseteq N_{r}(\bar{a})
,
this
implies
\mathrm{dist}(s,\bar{a})\leqslant 4R+r
.
By (
9
), we have that
\mathrm{dist}(s,\bar{a})\leqslant r
, in other words,
s\in N_{r}(\bar{a})
.
As
N_{r}(\bar{a})
is the union of all clusters,

s
belongs to some cluster.
Since the clusters have pairwise distance larger than
4R
and
\mathrm{dist}(s,C)\leqslant 4R
,
we conclude that
s\in C
.
Moreover, because
C
is contained in a ball of radius
2R
, it cannot contain two elements of
S
, so
|S\cap C|=1
.

By the above,
the number of
clusters intersected by
X
is equal to
|S\cap N_{r}(\bar{a})|
.
Condition (2b) is thus equivalent to
|S\cap N_{r}(\bar{a})|<|S|
. We show that this is equivalent to condition (1).

Suppose
condition (1) holds. By (
9
),
there is
some
x\in X
with
\mathrm{dist}(x,\bar{a})>4R+r
.
Therefore,

(S\cap N_{r}(\bar{a}))\cup\{x\}

is a
4R
-scattered subset of
X
,
so
S\cap N_{r}(\bar{a})
is not an inclusion-wise maximal
4R
-scattered subset of
X
,
which implies
|S\cap N_{r}(\bar{a})|<|S|
.

Conversely, if
|S\cap N_{r}(\bar{a})|<|S|
then
there is some

s\in S-N_{r}(\bar{a})
.
In particular, condition (1) holds.
Thus, assuming (
9
),
conditions (1) and (2b) are equivalent.
∎

Lemma

Set

3.3
Proof of the locality theorem

We now prove
Theorem

1.1
, repeated below.
See
1.1

Proof.

We prove the statement by structural induction on
\varphi(\bar{x})
.
Atoms of first-order logic, binary distance atoms, and unary distance atoms are local dist-FO formulas, and boolean combinations are immediate.
The case of local existential quantification is also straightforward: apply the inductive assumption to the quantified subformula, pull scatter sentences outside the quantifier, and distribute; the guarded existential quantification of each resulting local formula is again local.
The only nontrivial case is when

\varphi(\bar{x})
is of the form

\displaystyle\exists y~\psi(y,\bar{x}),

(10)

where
\psi
is a dist-FO formula of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
which,
by inductive assumption,
may be assumed to be a boolean combination of scatter sentences and
local dist-FO formulas of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
.
Since scatter sentences can be pulled outside the existential quantifier
(and
2.3
allows to assume that they have distance rank
(k,q)
),
and local dist-FO formulas are closed under boolean combinations, we may furthermore assume that

\psi(y,\bar{x})

is a single local dist-FO formula of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
.

In the case when
\bar{x}
is empty,
\psi(y)
is a local dist-FO formula of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
, and
\varphi
is a sentence which is equivalent to the scatter sentence
\textbf{scatter}(4\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1),\psi(y),1)
of distance rank
(k,q)
, and we are done.
We hence assume that
\bar{x}
is non-empty, so
k\geqslant 1
.

Clearly, as
\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\geqslant\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)
, the formula (
10
)
is equivalent to the disjunction

\displaystyle\exists y

\displaystyle\,\Big(\mathrm{dist}(y,\bar{x})\leqslant\rho^{+}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\psi(y,\bar{x})

(11)

\displaystyle\lor

\displaystyle\exists y

\displaystyle\,\Big(\mathrm{dist}(y,\bar{x})>\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\psi(y,\bar{x}).

(12)

The formula (
11
)
already is a local dist-FO formula
of distance rank
(k,q)
.
By
Lemma

3.1
,
the formula
(
12
)
is equivalent
to
a disjunction of formulas of the form

\exists y~\Big(\mathrm{dist}(y,\bar{x})>\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\alpha(\bar{x})\land\beta(y),

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are local dist-FO formulas of distance rank
(k+1,q-1)
.
In turn, such a formula is equivalent to

\alpha(\bar{x})\land\exists y~\Big(\mathrm{dist}(y,\bar{x})>\rho^{-}(k+1,q-1)\Big)\land\beta(y).

The conclusion follows by
Lemma

3.2
.
∎
