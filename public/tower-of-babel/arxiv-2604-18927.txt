Hyper relative differential operators on Lie algebras
Sofiane Bouarroudj, Jiefeng Liu, Liwen Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we first introduce the notion of a hyper relative differential operator on a Lie algebra, in which Nijenhuis operators are used to characterize the relative differential operators and their inverse. We then introduce the notions of DN-structures, KN-structures, and KD-structures on Lie algebras and study the relationships between DN-structures, KD-structures, KN-structures, and hyper relative differential operators. Finally, we investigate hyper symplectic structures and hyper Hessian structures from the view point of hyper relative differential operators, and provide equivalent descriptions for both hyper symplectic structures and hyper Hessian structures.

17B38, 17B60, 53C30

1.
Introduction

Hyper Kähler structures on manifolds first appeared in Berger’s classification of holonomy groups of Riemannian manifolds, and they are closely related to Yau’s solution to the Calabi conjecture, gauge theory, and Rozansky–Witten theory. For further details and references on hyper Kähler structures, see
[
20
]
. In pursuit of investigating hyper Kähler structures on manifolds from a symplectic geometric perspective, Xu naturally introduced the concept of hyper symplectic structures in
[
29
]
. More precisely, a hyper Kähler structure on a manifold
M
consists of three symplectic structures
\omega_{1}
,
\omega_{2}
, and
\omega_{3}
satisfying
\big((\omega^{\natural}_{j})^{-1}\circ\omega_{i}^{\natural}\big)^{2}=-\rm{Id}
for
i\neq j
. Analogous to hyper Kähler structures in manifolds, Hitchin introduced the notion of a para-hyper Kähler structure (also called a hyper symplectic structure) on manifold in
[
19
]
. Due to its important applications in string theory, integrable systems, and differential geometry, para-hyper Kähler structures have become an important object in mathematics and mathematical physics. For further details and references on para-hyper Kähler structures, see
[
15
]
. Similar to the definition of hyper Kähler structures on manifolds given by Xu, a para-hyper Kähler structure on a manifold
M
consists of three symplectic structures
\omega_{1}
,
\omega_{2}
, and
\omega_{3}
satisfying
\big((\omega^{\natural}_{3})^{-1}\circ\omega_{2}^{\natural}\big)^{2}=\rm{Id}
,
\big((\omega^{\natural}_{1})^{-1}\circ\omega_{3}^{\natural}\big)^{2}=\rm{Id}
and
\big((\omega^{\natural}_{2})^{-1}\circ\omega_{1}^{\natural}\big)^{2}=-\rm{Id}
. The authors in
[
5
]
introduced and studied hyper symplectic structures on Lie algebroids, including hyper Kähler structures and para-hyper Kähler structures as special cases. If the manifold is a Lie group
G
and the symplectic structures are left-invariant, hyper Kähler structures and para-hyper Kähler structures are completely determined by their corresponding hyper Kähler structures and para-hyper Kähler structures on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. See
[
1
,
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
for more details on hyper Kähler Lie algebras and para-hyper Kähler Lie algebras.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and let
(V;\rho)
be a representation. A linear map
d:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
is called a relative differential operator if it satisfies

d[x,y]=\rho(x)d(y)-\rho(y)d(x),\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

When the representation is the adjoint representation, a relative differential operator reduces to an ordinary derivation on the Lie algebra. See
[
14
,
17
,
18
,
22
]
for more details on relative differential operators on Lie algebras and associative algebras. Relative differential operators are closely related to symplectic structures on Lie algebras. More precisely, if
\omega
is a symplectic structure on
\mathfrak{g}
, then
\omega^{\natural}
is a relative differential operator with respect to the coadjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast};\operatorname{ad}^{\ast})
, defined by
\langle\omega^{\natural}(x),y\rangle=\omega(x,y)
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since the definitions of hyper Kähler Lie algebras and para-hyper Kähler Lie algebras rely fundamentally on compositions of three symplectic structures, it is natural to extend these constructions to the setting of relative differential operators. To this end, we introduce the notion of an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator on a Lie algebra, which consists of three invertible relative differential operators
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}
such that

\big(T_{i-1}\circ d_{i+1})^{2}=\varepsilon_{i}\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1,

where
T_{i}=d^{-1}_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
. In particular, a hyper Kähler Lie algebra gives rise to an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator with
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,-1)
, and a para-hyper Kähler Lie algebra gives rise to an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator with
\varepsilon=(1,1,-1)
(see Proposition
5.4
).

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
N_{i}:=T_{i-1}\circ d_{i+1}
, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, are Nijenhuis operators on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(see Proposition
2.8
). We refer to
[
16
]
for more details on Nijenhuis operators and their applications. In particular, if
\varepsilon_{i}=-1
, then
N_{i}
is a complex structure on
\mathfrak{g}
, while if
\varepsilon_{i}=1
, then
N_{i}
is a para-complex structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. For more details on complex and para-complex structures on Lie algebras, see
[
3
]
.

Relative differential operators are also closely related to
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Lie algebras. If a relative differential operator is invertible, then its inverse is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. The notion of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator was introduced by Kupershmidt in
[
24
]
as a tool to better understand the interrelation between the classical Yang-Baxter equation and related integrable systems. In particular, a Rota-Baxter operator as given by Semonov-Tian-Shansky (
[
26
]
) is precisely an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the adjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad})
, while a skew-symmetric solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator with respect to the coadjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};\mathrm{ad}^{*})
. Analogous to the Poisson–Nijenhuis structures in
[
23
]
, the authors in
[
21
]
introduced the notion of a Kupershmidt-dual-Nijenhuis structure (KN-structure), which consists of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator and a dual-Nijenhuis pair satisfying certain compatibility conditions. In this paper, by adding compatibility conditions between a relative differential operator and an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, we introduce the notion of a KD-structure on a Lie algebra, and by adding compatibility conditions between a relative differential operator and a Nijenhuis operator, we introduce the notion of a DN-structure on a Lie algebra. The relationships among KD-structures, DN-structures, and KN-structures are studied thoroughly. More notably, these structures can be constructed using hyper relative differential operators.

Another interesting example of relative differential operators arises from Hessian structures on pre-Lie algebras. A Hessian structure on a pre-Lie algebra corresponds to an affine Lie group
G
equipped with a
G
-invariant Hessian structure. Hessian structures on manifolds are related to Kählerian geometry, symplectic geometry, affine differential geometry, and information geometry (
[
27
]
). For example, the tangent bundle of a Hessian manifold naturally produces a Kählerian structure, and many important families of probability distributions, such as exponential families, admit Hessian structures. Recall that a Hessian structure on a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathscr{B}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\mathscr{B}(x\cdot y,z)-\mathscr{B}(x,y\cdot z)=\mathscr{B}(y\cdot x,z)-\mathscr{B}(y,x\cdot z),\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Furthermore, the nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathscr{B}
defines a Hessian structure on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\mathscr{B}^{\natural}
is a relative differential operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
with respect to the coregular representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};L^{*})
, where
\mathscr{B}^{\natural}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by
\langle\mathscr{B}^{\natural}(x),y\rangle=\mathscr{B}(x,y)
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and the coregular representation
L^{*}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by
\langle L^{*}_{x}\xi,y\rangle=-\langle\xi,x\cdot y\rangle
for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. As an application of relative differential operators on Lie algebras, we introduce the notion of a hyper Hessian structure on a pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, which consists of three Hessian structures
\mathfrak{B}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{B}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{B}_{3}
such that
(\mathfrak{B}_{1}^{\natural},\mathfrak{B}_{2}^{\natural},\mathfrak{B}_{3}^{\natural})
is a hyper relative differential operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
with respect to the coregular representation. Moreover, the relationships between hyper anti-Kähler structures, para-hyper anti-Kähler structures, and hyper Hessian structures are well studied.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notion of a hyper differential operator on a Lie algebra and show that it gives rise to three Nijenhuis operators on the Lie algebra via compositions of relative differential operators and their inverses. We also analyze the basic properties of hyper differential operators. In Section
3
, we introduce the notions of DN-structures and KD-structures on Lie algebras, and show that hyper relative differential operators naturally induce such structures. We then recall KN-structures on Lie algebras and study the relationships among DN-structures, KD-structures, and KN-structures. In particular, we prove that hyper relative differential operators also give rise to KN-structures. In Section
4
, we study two kinds of
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operators, namely those with
\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}=1
and those with
\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}=-1
. More compatible structures and equivalent descriptions of hyper relative differential operators are provided. In Section
5
, we apply hyper differential operators on Lie algebras to study hyper symplectic structures and hyper Hessian structures. In particular, we show that a hyper symplectic structure is equivalent to a hyper differential operator on the Lie algebra for which the differential operators are skew-symmetric, while a hyper Hessian structure is equivalent to a hyper differential operator on the pre-Lie algebra for which the differential operators are symmetric.

2.
Hyper relative differential operators on Lie algebras

In this section, we first recall the definition of a relative differential operator on a Lie algebra, defined as a
1
-cocycle on the Lie algebra with values in a representation. We then recall the relationships between relative differential operators,
\mathcal{O}
-operators, symplectic structures and Hessian structures. Finally, we introduce and develop the notion of
hyper relative differential operators
and analyze their properties.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra and
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
. A linear map
d:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
is called a
relative differential operator
if
d
satisfies

(1)

d[x,y]=\rho(x)d(y)-\rho(y)d(x),\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

This operator is the usual derivation on
\mathfrak{g}
in the case where
\rho
is the adjoint representation. Moreover, since the
1
-st cohomology group of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is trivial, the set of relative differential operators on
\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\{d\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V)\mid\exists~u\in V\,\text{ with }\;d=\rho(\cdot)\,u\}.

There are no invertible relative differential operators on semisimple Lie algebras.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra and
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
. Following
[
24
]
, an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
is a linear map
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(2)

[Tu,Tv]=T(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u),\quad\text{ for all }u,v\in V.

The following proposition demonstrates the relationship between relative differential operators and
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
d:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
be an invertible linear map. Then
d:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
is a relative differential operator with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
if and only if
d^{-1}:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
.

Recall that a
symplectic structure
1
1

1

This structure is sometimes refer to as quasi-Frobenuis, see
[
11
]
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a nondegenerate
2
-form
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
satisfying

(3)

\omega([x,y],z)+\omega([z,x],y)+\omega([y,z],x)=0,\quad\text{ for all }\,x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

This means that
\omega
is a 2-cocycle on
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
\mathbb{K}
. Such structures exist only in even dimensions. For classifications in low dimensions, see
[
25
]
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\omega
be a nondegenerate
2
-form on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Define
\omega^{\natural}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by

(4)

\langle\omega^{\natural}(x),y\rangle=\omega(x,y),\quad x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\omega
is a symplectic structure on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\omega^{\natural}
is a relative differential operator with respect to the coadjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};\mathrm{ad}^{*})
, or equivalently,
(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator with respect to
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};\mathrm{ad}^{*})
.

Recall that a
pre-Lie algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a binary operation
\cdot:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(5)

(x\cdot y)\cdot z-x\cdot(y\cdot z)=(y\cdot x)\cdot z-y\cdot(x\cdot z),\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Pre-Lie algebras are also referred to as
left-symmetric
algebras in the literature; see
[
7
,
13
]
and references therein.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
be a pre-Lie algebra. The commutator
[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=x\cdot y-y\cdot x
defines a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
, called the
sub-adjacent Lie algebra
of

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
. In this case,
\mathfrak{g}
is called a
compatible pre-Lie algebra structure
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
. Furthermore, the map
L:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
with
x\mapsto L_{x}
, where
L_{x}y=x\cdot y
, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, defines a representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. This representation is called a regular representation of
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
, and its dual representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};L^{*})
is called a coregular representation. For further details, see
[
7
]
and the references therein.

Following
[
27
]
, a
Hessian structure
on a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathscr{B}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(6)

\mathscr{B}(x\cdot y,z)-\mathscr{B}(x,y\cdot z)=\mathscr{B}(y\cdot x,z)-\mathscr{B}(y,x\cdot z),\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Example 2.4
.

We present several examples of Hessian structures on Lie algebras. The Lie algebras themselves are taken from
[
12
]
, whereas the Hessian structures are computed here.

(i) Two-dimensional pre-Lie algebras were classified in
[
12
]
. None of them admits a Hessian structure.

(ii) The pre-Lie algebra
I_{4}
is defined, with respect to the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, by:

e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=2\,e_{1},\quad e_{1}\cdot e_{i}=e_{i},\,\text{ for }i=1,\cdots,4,\quad e_{j}\cdot e_{j}=e_{1},\,\text{ for }j=2,3,4.

The Hessian structure is given by

\mathscr{B}=2e_{1}^{*}\otimes e_{1}^{*}+e_{2}^{*}\otimes e_{2}^{*}+e_{3}^{*}\otimes e_{3}^{*}+e_{4}^{*}\otimes e_{4}^{*}.

(iii) The pre-Lie algebra
B_{4}
defined, with respect to the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, by

e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=e_{4},\;\;e_{2}\cdot e_{3}=2\,e_{1},\;\;e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=e_{1},\;\;e_{4}\cdot e_{2}=-e_{2},\;\;e_{4}\cdot e_{3}=e_{3},\;\;e_{4}\cdot e_{4}=-e_{4}.

doesn’t have a Hessian structure.

(iv) The pre-algebra
A_{4}
is defined, with respect to the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
, by

e_{1}\cdot e_{i}=\alpha_{i}\,e_{i},\quad e_{j}\cdot e_{6-j}=e_{1},\quad\text{for }\,i=1,\ldots,4\,\text{ and }j=2,3,4,

where
\alpha_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{4}=\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}
are constant. The Hessian structure is given by

\mathscr{B}=\alpha_{1}e_{1}^{*}\otimes e_{1}^{*}+e_{2}^{*}\otimes e_{4}^{*}+e_{4}^{*}\otimes e_{2}^{*}+e_{3}^{*}\otimes e_{3}^{*}.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on a pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Define
\mathscr{B}^{\natural}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by

(7)

\langle\mathscr{B}^{\natural}(x),y\rangle=\mathscr{B}(x,y),\quad\text{ for every }\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\mathscr{B}
is a Hessian structure on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\mathscr{B}^{\natural}
is a relative differential operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
with respect to the coregular representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};L^{*})
, or equivalently,
(\mathscr{B}^{\natural})^{-1}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
with respect to
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};L^{*})
.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}(x\cdot y,z)-\mathscr{B}(x,y\cdot z)-\mathscr{B}(y\cdot x,z)+\mathscr{B}(y,x\cdot z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{B}^{\natural}([x,y]),z\rangle-\langle\mathscr{B}^{\natural}(x),y\cdot z\rangle+\langle\mathscr{B}^{\natural}(y),x\cdot z\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{B}^{\natural}([x,y])-L_{x}^{*}\mathscr{B}^{\natural}(y)+L_{y}^{*}\mathscr{B}^{\natural}(x),z\rangle,

which implies that
\mathscr{B}
is a Hessian structure on the pre-Lie algebra if and only if
\mathscr{B}^{\natural}
is a relative differential operator with respect to the coregular representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};L^{*})
.

Now, we are ready to introduce our main definition.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}
and
d_{3}
be invertible relative differential operators with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
. Set
T_{i}:=d_{i}^{-1}
and
N_{i}:=T_{i-1}\circ d_{i+1}
, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. If
N_{i}
satisfies
N_{i}^{2}=\varepsilon_{i}\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
with
\varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1
for
i=1,2,3
, then the triple

(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is called an
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})
-hyper relative differential operator
.

Example 2.7
.

Consider the Lie algebra
L
given in the basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{4}\}
by

[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{2},\quad[e_{1},e_{3}]=-e_{3},\quad[e_{1},e_{4}]=e_{4}.

There are three symplectic structures on
L
given by

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\omega_{1}&=&e_{1}^{*}\wedge e_{2}^{*}+e_{1}^{*}\wedge e_{3}^{*}+e_{3}^{*}\wedge e_{4}^{*},\\[5.69054pt]
\omega_{2}&=&e_{1}^{*}\wedge e_{4}^{*}+e_{2}^{*}\wedge e_{3}^{*},\\[5.69054pt]
\omega_{3}&=&-e_{1}^{*}\wedge e_{2}^{*}+e_{1}^{*}\wedge e_{3}^{*}+e_{3}^{*}\wedge e_{4}^{*}.\end{array}

The triple
(\omega^{\natural}_{1},\omega^{\natural}_{2},\omega^{\natural}_{3})
yields a
(1,1,-1)
-hyper relative differential operator.

Following
[
16
]
, a
Nijenhuis operator
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map
N:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(8)

[N(x),N(y)]=N\big([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)]-N[x,y]\big),\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Furthermore, the deformed bracket
[-,-]_{N}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
given by

(9)

[x,y]_{N}:=[N(x),y]+[x,N(y)]-N([x,y]),

is a Lie bracket and
N
is a Lie algebra morphism from
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{N})
to
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
.

In particular, the Nijenhuis operator
N
satisfying
N^{2}=-\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a
complex structure
, while the Nijenhuis operator
N
satisfying
N^{2}=\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a
para-complex structure
.

In
[
28
]
, the author showed that the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot)
is a real pre-Lie algebra if and only if the right multiplication on
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear Nijenhuis operator on the real affine space
\mathfrak{g}
(it is not a Nijenhuis operator on the sub-adjacent Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
).

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, the operator
N_{i}:=T_{i-1}\circ d_{i+1}
, is a Nijenhuis operator on
\mathfrak{g}
. In particular, if
\varepsilon_{i}=-1
, then
N_{i}
is a complex structure on
\mathfrak{g}
, while if
\varepsilon_{i}=1
, then
N_{i}
is a para-complex structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

By the fact that
d_{i}
is a relative differential operator and
T_{i-1}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, the left-hand side of Eq. (
8
) is equal to

\displaystyle[N_{i}x,N_{i}y]

\displaystyle=[T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}x),T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}y)]

\displaystyle=T_{i-1}\Big(\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}x)\big)(d_{i+1}y)-\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}y)\big)(d_{i+1}x)\Big).

Furthermore, since
N_{i}^{2}=\varepsilon_{i}\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have
d_{i+1}\circ T_{i-1}\circ d_{i+1}=d_{i-1}\varepsilon_{i}
. Combining with the fact that
d_{i+1}
is a relative differential operator, we have

\displaystyle N_{i}([N_{i}x,y])

\displaystyle=T_{i-1}\big(d_{i+1}[T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}x),y]\big)

\displaystyle=T_{i-1}\Big(\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}x)\big)(d_{i+1}y)-\rho(y)\big((d_{i+1}\circ T_{i-1}\circ d_{i+1})(x)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=T_{i-1}\Big(\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}x)\big)(d_{i+1}y)-\varepsilon_{i}\rho(y)\big(d_{i-1}x\big)\Big).

Similarly, we have

N_{i}([x,N_{i}y])=T_{i-1}\Big(\varepsilon_{i}\rho(x)\big(d_{i-1}y\big)-\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}y)\big)(d_{i+1}x)\Big).

By the fact that
d_{i-1}
is a relative differential operator and
T_{i-1}\circ d_{i-1}=\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, the right-hand side of Eq. (
8
) is equal to

\displaystyle N_{i}\big([N_{i}x,y]+[x,N_{i}y]-N_{i}[x,y]\big)

\displaystyle=T_{i-1}\Big(\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}x)\big)(d_{i+1}y)-\varepsilon_{i}\rho(y)\big(d_{i-1}x\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+T_{i-1}\Big(\varepsilon_{i}\rho(x)\big(d_{i-1}y\big)-\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}y)\big)(d_{i+1}x)\Big)-\varepsilon_{i}[x,y]

\displaystyle=T_{i-1}\Big(\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}x)\big)(d_{i+1}y)-\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}y)\big)(d_{i+1}x)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon_{i}T_{i-1}\Big(\rho(x)\big(d_{i-1}y\big)-\rho(y)\big(d_{i-1}x\big)\Big)-\varepsilon_{i}[x,y]

\displaystyle=T_{i-1}\Big(\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}x)\big)(d_{i+1}y)-\rho\big(T_{i-1}(d_{i+1}y)\big)(d_{i+1}x)\Big)+\varepsilon_{i}T_{i-1}(d_{i-1}[x,y])-\varepsilon_{i}[x,y]

\displaystyle=[N_{i}x,N_{i}y].

Thus,
N_{i}
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

Define
h^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
by

(10)

h^{\flat}=\varepsilon_{3}\varepsilon_{2}\,d_{3}\circ T_{1}\circ d_{2}.

This map will be useful in describing hyper relative differential operators in Section
4
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then,
h^{\flat}
satisfies

(11)

h^{\flat}=\varepsilon_{i-1}\varepsilon_{i+1}\,d_{i-1}\circ T_{i}\circ d_{i+1}.

Furthermore, the relations between
h^{\flat}
,
d_{i}
and
N_{i}
are given by

(12)

\displaystyle T_{i}\circ S_{i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\,N_{i}\circ T_{i}=\varepsilon_{i+1}(h^{\flat})^{-1},

(13)

\displaystyle d_{i}\circ N_{i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\,S_{i}\circ d_{i}=\varepsilon_{i-1}h^{\flat},

(14)

\displaystyle h^{\flat}\circ N_{i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\,S_{i}\circ h^{\flat}=\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{i-1}\,d_{i},

where
S_{i}=d_{i+1}\circ T_{i-1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
N_{i}
and
N_{i}^{2}=\varepsilon_{i}\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

(15)

\displaystyle d_{i+1}\circ T_{i-1}=\varepsilon_{i}d_{i-1}\circ T_{i+1},

(16)

\displaystyle T_{i+1}\circ d_{i-1}=\varepsilon_{i}T_{i-1}\circ d_{i+1}.

By Eq. (
15
) and Eq. (
16
), we obtain

\displaystyle h^{\flat}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{3}\varepsilon_{2}\,d_{3}\circ T_{1}\circ d_{2}=\varepsilon_{2}\,d_{3}\circ T_{2}\circ d_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{1}\,d_{2}\circ T_{3}\circ d_{1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}\,d_{2}\circ T_{1}\circ d_{3}=\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{3}d_{1}\circ T_{2}\circ d_{3},

which implies that
h^{\flat}=\varepsilon_{i-1}\varepsilon_{i+1}\,d_{i-1}\circ T_{i}\circ d_{i+1}
for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Furthermore, by Eq. (
15
), we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i-1}h^{\flat}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{i+1}d_{i-1}\circ T_{i}\circ d_{i+1}=d_{i}\circ T_{i-1}\circ d_{i+1}=d_{i}\circ N_{i}.

By Eq. (
11
), we have

\displaystyle(h^{\flat})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{i-1}\varepsilon_{i+1}d_{i-1}\circ T_{i}\circ d_{i+1})^{-1}=\varepsilon_{i-1}\varepsilon_{i+1}T_{i+1}\circ d_{i}\circ T_{i-1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{i+1}\varepsilon_{i}T_{i}\circ d_{i-1}\circ T_{i+1}=\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{i-1}T_{i-1}\circ d_{i+1}\circ T_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{i-1}N_{i}\circ T_{i}.

By Eq. (
15
), we have

\displaystyle T_{i}\circ S_{i}

\displaystyle=T_{i}\circ d_{i+1}\circ T_{i-1}=\varepsilon_{i}T_{i}\circ d_{i-1}\circ T_{i+1}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{i+1})^{2}\varepsilon_{i}T_{i}\circ d_{i-1}\circ T_{i+1}=\varepsilon_{i+1}(h^{\flat})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{i+1}\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{i-1}N_{i}\circ T_{i}=\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}N_{i}\circ T_{i}.

This proves Eq. (
12
). The proofs of Eq.(
13
) and Eq.(
14
) are analogous.

The following properties of hyper relative differential operators play an important role in the study of their relationships with KN-structures, KD-structures, and DN-structures in the subsequent sections.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. For all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
such that
i\neq j
, we have

(i)

T_{i}\circ S_{j}=N_{j}\circ T_{i}=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{i+1}\,T_{i-1},&j=i+1,\\
T_{i+1},&j=i-1.\end{cases}

(ii)

d_{i}\circ N_{j}=S_{j}\circ d_{i}=\begin{cases}d_{i-1},&j=i+1,\\
\varepsilon_{i-1}\,d_{i+1},&j=i-1.\end{cases}

(iii)

N_{i}\circ N_{j}=\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\,N_{j}\circ N_{i}=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{i+1}\,N_{i-1},&j=i+1,\\
\varepsilon_{i+1}N_{i+1},&j=i-1.\end{cases}

(iv)

S_{i}\circ S_{j}=\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\,S_{j}\circ S_{i}=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{i+1}\,S_{i-1},&j=i+1,\\
\varepsilon_{i+1}\,S_{i+1},&j=i-1,\end{cases}

and
S_{i}^{2}=\varepsilon_{i}\,\rm{Id}_{V}
.

(v)

T_{k}\circ d_{i}=\begin{cases}\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}},&k=i,\\
N_{i-1},&k=i+1,\\
\varepsilon_{i+1}\,N_{i+1},&k=i-1.\end{cases}

(vi)

d_{i}\circ T_{k}=\begin{cases}\rm{Id}_{V},&k=i,\\
S_{i-1},&k=i+1,\\
\varepsilon_{i+1}\,S_{i+1},&k=i-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

(i) For
j=i+1
, we have

T_{i}\circ S_{j}=T_{i}\circ S_{i+1}=T_{i}\circ d_{i-1}\circ T_{i}=N_{i+1}\circ T_{i}=N_{j}\circ T_{i}.

By Eq. (
15
), we get

T_{i}\circ S_{j}=N_{j}\circ T_{i}=\varepsilon_{i+1}T_{i}\circ d_{i}\circ T_{i-1}=\varepsilon_{i+1}T_{i-1}.

For
j=i-1
, we have

T_{i}\circ S_{j}=T_{i}\circ S_{i-1}=T_{i}\circ d_{i}\circ T_{i+1}=T_{i+1}=T_{i+1}\circ d_{i}\circ T_{i}=N_{i-1}\circ T_{i}=N_{j}\circ T_{i}.

Items (ii) – (vi) can be proved similarly.

3.
Hyper relative differential operators and compatible structures on Lie algebras

In this section, we first introduce DN-structures and KD-structures on Lie algebras, and show that hyper relative differential operators naturally induce such structures. We then recall the notion of KN-structures on Lie algebras and study the relationships among DN-structures, KD-structures, and KN-structures. In particular, we prove that hyper relative differential operators also give rise to KN-structures.

3.1.
Hyper relative differential operators, DN-structures and KD-structures on Lie algebras

In a KD-structure, K refers to an
\mathcal{O}
-operator introduced by Kupersdmit, while D stands for a relative differential operator. Similarly, in a DN-structure, D refers to a relative differential operator, while N refers to a Nijenhuis operator.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
d
be a relative differential operator and
T
an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
. A pair
(T,d)
is called a
KD-structure
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
if
d_{N}=d\circ N
is also a relative differential operator on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
, where
N:=T\circ d
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(T_{i},d_{k})
are
KD
-structures for all
i\neq k\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Proof.

By (ii) in Prop.
2.10
and the fact that
d_{i+1}
is a relative differential operator, letting
d=d_{i}
and
N=T_{i+1}\circ d_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(x)d_{N}(y)-\rho(y)d_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=\rho(x)(d_{i}T_{i+1}d_{i})(y)-\rho(y)(d_{i}T_{i+1}d_{i})(x)

\displaystyle=\rho(x)(\varepsilon_{i-1}d_{i+1})(y)-\rho(y)(\varepsilon_{i-1}d_{i+1})(x)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{i-1}d_{i+1}[x,y]=d_{i}T_{i+1}d_{i}[x,y]

\displaystyle=d_{N}[x,y],

which implies that
d_{N}=d\circ N
is also a relative differential operator and thus
(T_{i+1},d_{i})
is a KD-structure. Similarly, we can prove that
(T_{i-1},d_{i})
is also a KD-structure. These facts imply that
(T_{i},d_{k})
are KD-structures for all
i\neq k\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
d
be a relative differential operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
and
N:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a Nijenhuis operator. A pair
(d,N)
is called a
DN-structure
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho)
if
d_{N}=d\circ N
is a relative differential operator on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(d,N)
be a
DN
-structure on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
. Then
d_{N^{k}}=d\circ N^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
are relative differential operators on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho)
. Furthermore,
(d_{N^{k}},N^{k})
are
DN
-structures for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

For
k=1
, we have
d_{N}=d\circ N
, which is a relative differential operator by definition of a DN-structure.

Now we assume that
d_{N^{k}}=d\circ N^{k}
are relative differential operators for all positive integers
1<k\leq m
. We will prove that
d_{N^{m+1}}=d\circ N^{m+1}
is a relative differential operator. Since
N
is a Nijenhuis operator, we have

\displaystyle d_{N^{m+1}}[x,y]

\displaystyle=(d\circ N^{m+1})[x,y]=(d\circ N^{m-1})(N^{2}[x,y])

\displaystyle=(d\circ N^{m-1})\big(N[Nx,y]+N[x,Ny]-[Nx,Ny]\big)

\displaystyle=(d\circ N^{m})[Nx,y]+(d\circ N^{m})[x,Ny]-(d\circ N^{m-1})([Nx,Ny]).

Since
d\circ N^{m}
and
d\circ N^{m-1}
are relative differential operators by inductive hypothesis, we get

\displaystyle(d\circ N^{m})[Nx,y]=\rho(Nx)(d\circ N^{m})(y)-\rho(y)(d\circ N^{m})(Nx),

\displaystyle(d\circ N^{m})[x,Ny]=\rho(x)(d\circ N^{m})(Ny)-\rho(Ny)(d\circ N^{m})(x),

\displaystyle(d\circ N^{m-1})[Nx,Ny]=\rho(Nx)(d\circ N^{m})(y)-\rho(Ny)(d\circ N^{m})(x).

Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle d_{N^{m+1}}[x,y]

\displaystyle=\rho(Nx)(d\circ N^{m})(y)-\rho(y)(d\circ N^{m})(Nx)+\rho(x)(d\circ N^{m})(Ny)-\rho(Ny)(d\circ N^{m})(x)

\displaystyle\quad-\big(\rho(Nx)(d\circ N^{m})(y)-\rho(Ny)(d\circ N^{m})(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\rho(x)(d\circ N^{m})(Ny)-\rho(y)(d\circ N^{m})(Nx)

\displaystyle=\rho(x)(d\circ N^{m+1})(y)-\rho(y)(d\circ N^{m+1})(x)

\displaystyle=\rho(x)(d_{N^{m+1}})(y)-\rho(y)(d_{N^{m+1}})(x),

which implies that
d_{N^{k}}=d\circ N^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
are relative differential operators on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho)
. Since
N^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
are also Nijenhuis operators on
\mathfrak{g}
, it follows that
(d_{N^{k}},N^{k})
are DN-structures on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho)
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

The following proposition demonstrates the relationships between DN-structres and KD-structures.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
(d,N)
is a
DN
-structure on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
and
d
is invertible, then
(T=N\circ d^{-1},d)
is a
KD
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho)
. Conversely, if
(T,d)
is a
KD
-structure, then
(d,N=T\circ d)
is a
DN
-structure.

Proof.

Let
(d,N)
be a DN-structure. Since
N
is a Nijenhuis operator, we have

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv]

\displaystyle=[N\circ d^{-1}u,N\circ d^{-1}v]

\displaystyle=N\big([N\circ d^{-1}u,d^{-1}v]+[d^{-1}u,N\circ d^{-1}v]-N[d^{-1}u,d^{-1}v]\big)

\displaystyle=(T\circ d)\big([Tu,d^{-1}v]+[d^{-1}u,Tv]-N[d^{-1}u,d^{-1}v]\big).

On the other hand, since
d
and
d\circ N
are relative differential operators, we have

\displaystyle(T\circ d)[Tu,d^{-1}v]

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(d^{-1}v)d(Tu)\big),

\displaystyle(T\circ d)[d^{-1}u,Tv]

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(d^{-1}u)d(Tv)-\rho(Tv)u\big),

\displaystyle(T\circ d\circ N)[d^{-1}u,d^{-1}v]

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(d^{-1}u)d(Tv)-\rho(d^{-1}v)d(Tu)\big).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv]

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(d^{-1}v)d(Tu)\big)+T\big(\rho(d^{-1}u)d(Tv)-\rho(Tv)u\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\Big(T\big(\rho(d^{-1}u)d(Tv)-\rho(d^{-1}v)d(Tu)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=T(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u),

which implies that
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator and thus
(T=N\circ d^{-1},d)
is a KD-structure.

Conversely, since
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, we have

\displaystyle[Nx,Ny]=[T\circ dx,T\circ dy]=T\big(\rho(Nx)d(y)-\rho(Ny)d(x)\big).

Since
d
and
d\circ N
are relative differential operators, we have

\displaystyle N[Nx,y]

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(Nx)d(y)-\rho(y)d(Nx)\big),

\displaystyle N[x,Ny]

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(x)d(Ny)-\rho(Ny)d(x)\big),

\displaystyle N^{2}[x,y]

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(x)d(Ny)-\rho(y)d(Nx)\big).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle N\big([Nx,y]+[x,Ny]-N[x,y]\big)

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(Nx)d(y)-\rho(y)d(Nx)\big)+T\big(\rho(x)d(Ny)-\rho(Ny)d(x)\big)-\Big(T\big(\rho(x)d(Ny)-\rho(y)d(Nx)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=T\big(\rho(Nx)d(y)-\rho(Ny)d(x)\big)=[Nx,Ny],

which implies that
N
is a Nijenhuis operator and thus
(d,N=T\circ d)
is a DN-structure.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(d_{i},N_{k})
are
DN
-structures for all
i\neq k\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Proof.

By Prop.
3.2
,
(T_{i},d_{k})
are KD-structures for all
i\neq k\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. By Prop.
3.5
,
(d_{i},N_{k})
are DN-structures for all
i\neq k\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

3.2.
Hyper relative differential operators, dual-Nijenhuis pairs and KN-structures

Following
[
21
]
), a pair
(N,S)
, where
N\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
S\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, is called a
dual-Nijenhuis pair
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a representation
(V;\rho)
if
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on
\mathfrak{g}
and
S
satisfies the following condition:

(17)

\rho({Nx})(Sv)=S(\rho({Nx})v)+\rho({x})(S^{2}(v))-S(\rho({x})(Sv)).

Let
(N,S)
be a dual-Nijenhuis pair on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
with a representation
\rho
. Define
{\varrho}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
by

(18)

{\varrho}(x):=\rho(Nx)-[\rho(x),S],\quad x\in\mathfrak{g}.

It was shown in
[
21
]
that
{\varrho}
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{N})
on
V
, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{N}
is given by Eq. (
9
).

Let
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
. Define a multiplication
\star^{T}
on
V
by

(19)

u\star^{T}v:=\rho(Tu)(v),\quad\text{ for all }\,u,v\in V.

Then,
(V,\star^{T})
is a pre-Lie algebra. We denote by
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]^{T})
the sub-adjacent Lie algebra of the pre-Lie algebra
(V,\star^{T})
. More precisely,

(20)

~[u,v]^{T}:=\rho(Tu)(v)-\rho(Tv)(u),\quad\text{ for all }\,u,v\in V.

Moreover,
T
is a Lie algebra homomorphism from
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]^{T})
to
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
, see
[
6
]
for more details.

Let
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator and
(N,S)
be a dual-Nijenhuis pair on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a representation
(V;\rho)
. We define the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]^{T}_{S}:\wedge^{2}V\longrightarrow V
to be the deformed bracket of
[\cdot,\cdot]^{T}
by
S
, i.e.

\displaystyle{[u,v]}^{T}_{S}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle[S(u),v]^{T}+[u,S(v)]^{T}-S[u,v]^{T},\quad\text{ for all }\,u,v\in V.

Define the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{{\varrho}}^{T}:\wedge^{2}V\longrightarrow V
by

(21)

\displaystyle\{u,v\}_{{\varrho}}^{T}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\varrho}(Tu)(v)-{\varrho}(Tv)(u),\quad\text{ for all }\,u,v\in V.

Definition 3.7
.

(
[
21
]
)

Let
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator and
(N,S)
a dual-Nijenhuis pair on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a representation
(V;\rho)
. The triple
(T,S,N)
is called a
Kupershmidt-dual-Nijenhuis structure (KN-structure)
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho)
if
T
and
(N,S)
satisfy the following conditions

(22)

\displaystyle N\circ T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\circ S,

(23)

\displaystyle{[u,v]}^{N\circ T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{[u,v]}^{T}_{S}.

Note that if
(T,S,N)
is a KN-structure, then
S
is a Nijenhuis operator on the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]^{T})
, and furthermore, the three brackets
{[\cdot,\cdot]}^{T}_{S},

\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{{\varrho}}^{T}
and
[\cdot,\cdot]^{N\circ T}
coincide and are all Lie brackets.

Proposition 3.8
.

(
[
21
]
)

Let
(T,S,N)
be a
KN
-structure with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
. Then, we have

\rm(i)

T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the deformed Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{N})
with respect to the representation
(V;{\varrho})
;

\rm(ii)

N\circ T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
T_{1},T_{2}:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be two
\mathcal{O}
-operators on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
. If for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the operator
k_{1}T_{1}+k_{2}T_{2}
is still an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, then
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are called
compatible
.

The presence of a KN-structure gives rise to a hierarchy of compatible

\mathcal{O}
-operators.

Proposition 3.10
.

(
[
21
]
)

Let
(T,S,N)
be a
KN
-structure with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
. Then all
N^{k}\circ T
are
\mathcal{O}
-operators with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
, and for all
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
N^{k}\circ T
and
N^{l}\circ T
are compatible.

Proposition 3.11
.

If
(T,d)
is a
KD
-structure, then
(T,S=d\circ T,N=T\circ d)
is a
KN
-structure.

Proof.

Let
(T,d)
be a KD-structure. It is obvious that
N\circ T=T\circ d\circ T=T\circ S
. First we show that
(N,S)
is a dual-Nijenhuis pair. Since
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator and
d
is a relative differential operator, we have

\displaystyle dT\big(\rho(Tv)dx-\rho(Tdx)v\big)=d([Tv,Tdx])=\rho(Tv)dTdx-\rho(Tdx)dTv.

Due to
S=d\circ T
, the above equation implies

(24)

S\rho(Tv)dx-S\rho(Tdx)v=\rho(Tv)Sdx-\rho(Tdx)Sv.

Since
S\circ d=d\circ N
is a relative differential operator, we have

Sd[x,Tv]=\rho(x)(SdTv)-\rho(Tv)(Sdx),

which implies that

(25)

\displaystyle\rho(Tv)\big(Sdx\big)-S\big(\rho(Tv)(dx)\big)-\rho(x)\big(S^{2}(v)\big)+S\big(\rho(x)(Sv)\big)=0.

Combining Eq. (
24
) with Eq. (
25
), letting
T\circ d=N
, we have

\displaystyle\rho({Nx})(Sv)-S\big(\rho({Nx})v\big)=\rho({x})(S^{2}(v))-S\big(\rho({x})(Sv)\big),

which means that
(N,S)
is a dual-Nijenhuis pair.

Since
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator and
d
is a relative differential operator, we have

\displaystyle\rho(Tv)(Su)-\rho(Tu)(Sv)+S\big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Tv)(dTu)-\rho(Tu)(dTv)+dT\big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Tv)(dTu)-\rho(Tu)(dTv)+d[Tu,Tv]=0,

which implies that

(26)

\rho(Tv)(Su)-\rho(Tu)(Sv)+S\big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u)\big)=0.

By Eq. (
26
), we have

\displaystyle{[u,v]}^{N\circ T}-{[u,v]}^{T}_{S}={[u,v]}^{T\circ S}-{[u,v]}^{T}_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\big(TS(u)\big)v-\rho\big(TS(v)\big)u-\Big(\rho\big(TS(u)\big)v-\rho(Tv)\big(S(u)\big)

\displaystyle+\rho(Tu)\big(S(v)\big)-\rho\big(TS(v)\big)u-S\big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Tv)\big(S(u)\big)-\rho(Tu)\big(S(v)\big)+S\big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}=1
. Then, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
,
(d_{i},N_{i})
are
DN
-structures.

Proof.

In order to prove that
(d_{i},N_{i})
is a DN-structure for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, we only need to prove that
d_{i}\circ N_{i}
is a relative differential operator; that is,
d_{i}N_{i}[x,y]=\rho(x)(d_{i}N_{i}y)-\rho(y)(d_{i}N_{i}x)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}=1
, by Eq. (
12
), we have
d_{i}\circ N_{i}=S_{i}\circ d_{i}
. Letting
x=T_{i}u
and
y=T_{i}v
, we have

\displaystyle d_{i}N_{i}[x,y]-\rho(x)(d_{i}N_{i}y)+\rho(y)(d_{i}N_{i}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{i}d_{i}[T_{i}u,T_{i}v]-\rho(T_{i}u)(S_{i}d_{i}T_{i}v)+\rho(T_{i}v)(S_{i}d_{i}T_{i}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{i}\rho(T_{i}u)v-S_{i}\rho(T_{i}v)u-\rho(T_{i}u)(S_{i}v)+\rho(T_{i}v)(S_{i}u),

which means that
d_{i}\circ N_{i}
is a relative differential operator if and only if the following equation holds:

(27)

S_{i}\rho(T_{i}u)v-S_{i}\rho(T_{i}v)u-\rho(T_{i}u)(S_{i}v)+\rho(T_{i}v)(S_{i}u)=0.

By (vi) in Prop.
2.10
, we have
S_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}d_{i-1}\circ T_{i+1}
. Considering that
T_{i}
and
T_{i+1}
are compatible and
d_{i-1}
is a relative differential operator, and using
[
21
, Proposition 4.2]
, we obtain

\displaystyle[T_{i}u,T_{i+1}v]+[T_{i+1}u,T_{i}v]=T_{i}\big(\rho(T_{i+1}u)v-\rho(T_{i+1}v)u\big)+T_{i+1}\big(\rho(T_{i}u)v-\rho(T_{i}v)u\big),

which implies

\displaystyle d_{i-1}[T_{i}u,T_{i+1}v]-d_{i-1}[T_{i}v,T_{i+1}u]=d_{i-1}T_{i}\big(\rho(T_{i+1}u)v-\rho(T_{i+1}v)u\big)+d_{i-1}T_{i+1}\big(\rho(T_{i}u)v-\rho(T_{i}v)u\big).

Thus,

\displaystyle S_{i}\rho(T_{i}u)v-S_{i}(\rho T_{i}v)u-\rho(T_{i}u)(S_{i}v)+\rho(T_{i}v)(S_{i}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}\Big(d_{i-1}T_{i+1}\rho(T_{i}u)v-d_{i-1}T_{i+1}\rho(T_{i}v)u-\rho(T_{i}u)(d_{i-1}T_{i+1}v)+\rho(T_{i}v)(d_{i-1}T_{i+1}u)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}\Big(d_{i-1}T_{i+1}\rho(T_{i}u)v-d_{i-1}T_{i+1}\rho(T_{i}v)u-d_{i-1}[T_{i}u,T_{i+1}v]-\rho(T_{i+1}v)(d_{i-1}T_{i}u)

\displaystyle+d_{i-1}[T_{i}v,T_{i+1}u]+\rho(T_{i+1}u)(d_{i-1}T_{i}v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}\Big(d_{i-1}T_{i+1}\rho(T_{i}u)v-d_{i-1}T_{i+1}\rho(T_{i}v)u-d_{i-1}T_{i}\big(\rho(T_{i+1}u)v-\rho(T_{i+1}v)u\big)

\displaystyle-d_{i-1}T_{i+1}\big(\rho(T_{i}u)v-\rho(T_{i}v)u\big)-\rho(T_{i+1}v)(d_{i-1}T_{i}u)+\rho(T_{i+1}u)(d_{i-1}T_{i}v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon_{i}\Big(d_{i-1}T_{i}\rho(T_{i+1}u)v-d_{i-1}T_{i}\rho(T_{i+1}v)u+\rho(T_{i+1}v)(d_{i-1}T_{i}u)-\rho(T_{i+1}u)(d_{i-1}T_{i}v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon_{i}\Big(S_{i+1}\rho(T_{i+1}u)v-S_{i+1}\rho(T_{i+1}v)u-\rho(T_{i+1}u)(S_{i+1}v)+\rho(T_{i+1}v)(S_{i+1}u)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{i+1}\Big(S_{i+2}\rho(T_{i+2}u)v-S_{i+2}\rho(T_{i+2}v)u-\rho(T_{i+2}u)(S_{i+2}v)+\rho(T_{i+2}v)(S_{i+2}u)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{i+1}\varepsilon_{i+2}\Big(S_{i}\rho(T_{i}u)v-S_{i}\rho(T_{i}v)u-\rho(T_{i}u)(S_{i}v)+\rho(T_{i}v)(S_{i}u)\Big).

Due to
\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}=1

Therefore, for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
,
(d_{i},N_{i})
is a DN-structure.

In this section, we discuss two kinds of

Definition 4.7
.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}=-1
. If
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon_{3}=-1
, then
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is called a
hyper relative differential operator
on
\mathfrak{g}
, while otherwise,
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is called a
para-hyper relative differential operator
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

With a cyclic permutation of the indices, all para-hyper relative differential operators satisfy
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}=1
and
\varepsilon_{3}=-1
. Thus, we just need to study para-hyper relative differential operators of this form in the following sections.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
V
be a vector space. Then
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is a (para)-hyper relative differential operator if and only if there exist an invertible linear map
h^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\to V
and (para-)complex structures
I_{i}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
for
i=1,2
satisfying
I_{1}\circ I_{2}=-I_{2}\circ I_{1},
and
d_{i}=h^{\flat}\circ I_{i}
are relative differential operators for all
i=1,2,3
with
I_{3}=I_{1}\circ I_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be a hyper relative differential operator. Letting
h^{\flat}=d_{3}\circ T_{1}\circ d_{2}
,
I_{1}=N_{1}
and
I_{2}=N_{2}
, by (iii) in Prop.
2.10
, we have
I_{1}\circ I_{2}=-I_{2}\circ I_{1}
and
I_{3}=N_{3}
. By Eq.(
14
), we have
d_{i}=h^{\flat}\circ N_{i}=h^{\flat}\circ I_{i}
and thus
h^{\flat}\circ I_{i}
are relative differential operators for all
i=1,2,3
.

Conversely, by
I_{3}=I_{1}\circ I_{2}
, we have

d_{3}=h^{\flat}\circ I_{3}=h^{\flat}\circ I_{1}\circ I_{2}=d_{1}\circ I_{2},

which implies that
I_{2}=N_{2}=T_{1}\circ d_{3}
. Similarly, we have
I_{1}=N_{1}
and
I_{2}=N_{2}
. Since
I_{1}^{2}=I_{2}^{2}=-\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have
I_{3}^{2}=-\rm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Hence
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is a hyper relative differential operator.

The second claim can be proved similarly.

.

.

]

.

\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle B(d_{i}[x,y],z)-B(d_{i}(y),[z,x])-B(d_{i}(x),[y,z])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(d_{i}[x,y],z)-\langle\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{*}B^{\sharp}(d_{i}(y)),z\rangle+\langle\mathrm{ad}_{y}^{*}B^{\sharp}(d_{i}(x)),z\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(d_{i}[x,y]-[d_{i}(x),y]-[x,d_{i}(y)],z)=0,

which implies that
\omega_{i}
are symplectic structures on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Also, we have

N_{i}=(\omega_{i-1}^{\natural})^{-1}\circ\omega_{i+1}^{\natural}=d_{i-1}^{-1}\circ d_{i+1}.

Thus
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper symplectic structure.

The converse can be proved similarly.

By Theorem
5.6
and Proposition
5.4
, we have

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
d_{i}
be skew-symmetric endomorphisms of
(\mathfrak{g},B)
for all
i=1,2,3
. Then

(i)

(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator with
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,-1)
if and only if the quadruple
(h,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is a hyper Kähler structure on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, where
h^{\flat}=d_{3}\circ d_{1}^{-1}\circ d_{2}
and
I_{i}=(d_{i-1})^{-1}\circ d_{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

(ii)

(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator with
\varepsilon=(1,1,-1)
if and only if the quadruple
(h,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is a para-hyper Kähler structure on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, where
h^{\flat}=-d_{3}\circ d_{1}^{-1}\circ d_{2}
and
I_{i}=(d_{i-1})^{-1}\circ d_{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Proof.

(i) By Theorem
5.6
,
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator with
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,-1)
if and only if
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper symplectic structure with
\omega_{i}(x,y)=B(d_{i}(x),y)
for all
i=1,2,3
. Furthermore, by Proposition
5.4
,
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper symplectic structure with
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,-1)
and
\omega_{i}(x,y)=B(d_{i}(x),y)
for all
i=1,2,3
if and only if the quadruple
(h,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is a hyper Kähler structure on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, where
h(x,y)=d_{3}\circ d_{1}^{-1}\circ d_{2}
and
I_{i}=(d_{i-1})^{-1}\circ d_{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

Part (ii) can be proved similarly.

5.2.
\varepsilon
-hyper Hessian structures

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a Hessian structure on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Recall that
\mathscr{B}^{\natural}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by (
7
) is a relative differential operator with respect to the coregular representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},L^{*})
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathscr{B}_{1},\mathscr{B}_{2}
and
\mathscr{B}_{3}
be Hessian structures on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. If the triple

(\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{1},\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{2},\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
with respect to the coregular representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};L^{*})
, then
(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mathscr{B}_{2},\mathscr{B}_{3})
is called an

\varepsilon
-hyper Hessian structure
.

Example 5.9
.

Consider the 4-dimensional pre-Lie algebra given in the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
by

e_{3}\cdot e_{1}=e_{2},\quad e_{3}\cdot e_{2}=-e_{1}.

Let us define:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathscr{B}_{1}&=&e_{1}^{*}\otimes e_{1}^{*}+e_{2}^{*}\otimes e_{2}^{*}+2e_{3}^{*}\otimes e_{3}^{*}+e_{3}^{*}\otimes e_{4}^{*}+e_{4}^{*}\otimes e_{3}^{*},\\
\mathscr{B}_{2}&=&-e_{1}^{*}\otimes e_{1}^{*}-e_{2}^{*}\otimes e_{2}^{*}+2e_{3}^{*}\otimes e_{3}^{*}+e_{4}^{*}\otimes e_{4}^{*}+e_{3}^{*}\otimes e_{4}^{*}+e_{4}^{*}\otimes e_{3}^{*},\\
\mathscr{B}_{3}&=&i\,e_{1}^{*}\otimes e_{1}^{*}+i\,e_{2}^{*}\otimes e_{2}^{*}+2i\,e_{3}^{*}\otimes e_{3}^{*}+i\,e_{4}^{*}\otimes e_{4}^{*}+i\,e_{3}^{*}\otimes e_{4}^{*}+i\,e_{4}^{*}\otimes e_{3}^{*}.\\
\end{array}

A direct computation shows that
(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mathscr{B}_{2},\mathscr{B}_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper Hessian structure for
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,1).

Definition 5.10
.

An
anti-Hermitian structure
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a pair
(\omega,I)
, where
\omega
is a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear and
I:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a complex structure satisfying

(33)

\omega(I(x),I(y))=\omega(x,y),\quad\text{ for all }\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

while a
para-anti-Hermitian structure
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a pair
(\omega,I)
, where
\omega
is a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear and
I:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a para-complex structure satisfying

(34)

\omega(I(x),I(y))=-\omega(x,y),\quad\text{ for all }\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Example 5.11
.

Consider the 4-dimensional symplectic Lie algebra
L
given in the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
by

[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3},\quad\omega=e_{2}^{*}\wedge e_{3}^{*}-e_{1}^{*}\wedge e_{4}^{*}.

There exists a complex structure
I
on
L
given by

I(e_{1})=-e_{2},\quad I(e_{2})=e_{1},\quad I(e_{3})=-e_{4},\quad I(e_{4})=e_{3}.

Moreover, a direct computation shows that Eq. (
33
) hold.

Definition 5.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a pre-Lie algebra. Let
(\omega,I_{1})
and
(\omega,I_{2})
be (para-)anti-Hermitian structures on the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
and
I_{3}=I_{1}I_{2}
. If
I_{1}I_{2}=-I_{2}I_{1}
and
\mathscr{B}_{i}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
defined by

(35)

\mathscr{B}_{i}(x,y)=\omega(I_{i}(x),y),\quad\text{ for all }\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

are Hessian structures on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
for
i=1,2,3
, then
(\omega,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is called a
(para-)hyper anti-Kähler structure
on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 5.13
.

(i)

The quadruple
(\omega,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is a hyper anti-Kähler structure on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mathscr{B}_{2},\mathscr{B}_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper Hessian structure with
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,-1)
, where
\mathscr{B}_{i}
are given by (
35
) for
i=1,2,3
.

(ii)

The quadruple
(\omega,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is a para-hyper anti-Kähler structure on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mathscr{B}_{2},\mathscr{B}_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper Hessian structure with
\varepsilon=(1,1,-1)
, where
\mathscr{B}_{i}
are given by (
35
) for
i=1,2,3
.

Proof.

Let
(\omega,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
be a hyper Hessian structure on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. By Theorem
4.8
,
(\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{1},\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{2},\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator with respect to the coregular representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};L^{*})
with
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,-1)
, where
h^{\flat}=\omega^{\natural}
. Furthermore, for
i=1,2
, by Eq. (
33
) and
I_{i}^{2}=-\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{i}(x,y)=\omega(I_{i}(x),y)=-\omega(x,I_{i}(y))=\omega(I_{i}(y),x)=\mathscr{B}_{i}(y,x),

and by
I_{1}I_{2}=-I_{2}I_{1}
, we also have

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{3}(x,y)=\omega(I_{3}(x),y)=\omega(I_{1}I_{2}(x),y)=\omega(x,I_{2}I_{1}(y))=\omega(I_{3}(y),x)=\mathscr{B}_{3}(y,x),

which implies that
\mathscr{B}_{1}
,
\mathscr{B}_{2}
and
\mathscr{B}_{3}
are symmetric and hence are Hessian structures on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus,
(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mathscr{B}_{2},\mathscr{B}_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper Hessian structure with
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,-1)
.

Conversely, if
(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mathscr{B}_{2},\mathscr{B}_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper Hessian structure with
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,-1)
, by Theorem
4.8
, there exist an invertible linear map
h^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\to V
, and complex structures
I_{i}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
for
i=1,2
such that
I_{1}\circ I_{2}=-I_{2}\circ I_{1},
and
\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{i}=h^{\flat}\circ I_{i}
. Set
\omega(x,y)=\langle h^{\flat}(x),y\rangle
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Note that
h^{\flat}=\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{3}\circ(\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{1})^{-1}\circ\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{2}=-\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{2}\circ(\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{1})^{-1}\circ\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{3}
. By the symmetry of
\mathscr{B}_{i}
, we have

\omega(x,y)=\langle h^{\flat}(x),y\rangle=\langle\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{3}\circ(\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{1})^{-1}\circ\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{2}(x),y\rangle=\langle x,\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{2}\circ(\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{1})^{-1}\circ\mathscr{B}^{\natural}_{1}(y)\rangle=-\omega(y,x),

and
for
i=1,2
, we have

\omega(I_{i}(x),I_{i}(y))=\mathscr{B}_{i}(x,I_{i}(y))=\mathscr{B}_{i}(I_{i}(y),x)=-\omega(y,x)=\omega(x,y).

Thus
(\omega,I_{1})
and
(\omega,I_{2})
are anti-Hermitian structures on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
.

The second claim can be proved similarly.

Recall that a
derivation
on a pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map
d:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
d(x\cdot y)=d(x)\cdot y+x\cdot d(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 5.14
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}
and
d_{3}
be invertible derivations on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. If the triple

(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper relative differential operator with respect to the regular representation
(\mathfrak{g};L)
, then
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is called an

\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator
on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

A nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{*}
on a pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called
invariant
if

(36)

\omega(x\cdot y,z)+\omega(y,[x,z])=0,\quad\text{ for all }\,x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a pre-Lie algebra with a non-degenerate, invariant, skew-symmetric bilinear form
\omega\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then
\omega
induces a bijective linear map
\omega^{\natural}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
given by
\langle\omega^{\natural}(x),y\rangle=\omega(x,y)
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

By the invariance of
\omega
, we have

(37)

\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{x}\omega^{\natural}(y)=\omega^{\natural}(L_{x}y),~\mbox{or equivalently,}~L^{*}_{x}\omega^{\natural}(y)=\omega^{\natural}(\mathrm{ad}_{x}y).

A
symmetric endomorphism of
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
is a linear map
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that
\omega^{\natural}\circ\varphi:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is symmetric, that is,

\langle\omega^{\natural}\varphi(x),y\rangle=\langle\omega^{\natural}\varphi(y),x\rangle,\quad\text{ for all }\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
d_{i}
be symmetric endomorphisms of
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
and set
\mathscr{B}_{i}(x,y)=\omega(d_{i}(x),y)
for all
i=1,2,3
. Then,
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mathscr{B}_{2},\mathscr{B}_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper Hessian structure.

Proof.

Let
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
be an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
d_{i}
for all
i=1,2,3
are invertible symmetric endomorphisms, all
\mathscr{B}_{i}
are non-degenerate and symmetric. Since
d_{i}
are derivations on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, by invariance of
\omega
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{i}(x\cdot y,z)-\mathscr{B}_{i}(x,y\cdot z)-\mathscr{B}_{i}(y\cdot x,z)+\mathscr{B}_{i}(y,x\cdot z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(d_{i}[x,y],z)-\omega(d_{i}(x),y\cdot z)+\omega(d_{i}(y),x\cdot z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(d_{i}[x,y],z)+\langle L_{y}^{*}\omega^{\natural}(d_{i}(x)),z\rangle-\langle L_{x}^{*}\omega^{\natural}(d_{i}(y)),z\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(d_{i}[x,y]-[d_{i}(x),y]-[x,d_{i}(y)],z)=0,

which implies that
\mathscr{B}_{i}
are Hessian structures on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Also, we have

N_{i}=(\mathscr{B}_{i-1}^{\natural})^{-1}\circ\mathscr{B}_{i+1}^{\natural}=d_{i-1}^{-1}\circ d_{i+1}.

Thus
(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mathscr{B}_{2},\mathscr{B}_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper Hessian structure.

Conversely, since
N_{i}=(\mathscr{B}_{i-1}^{\natural})^{-1}\circ\mathscr{B}_{i+1}^{\natural}=d_{i-1}^{-1}\circ d_{i+1}
, we only need to check that
d_{i}
for all
i=1,2,3
are derivations on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. By the fact that
\mathscr{B}_{i}
are Hessian structures on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we have shown that
d_{i}
are the derivations on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
. Furthermore, by invariance of
\omega
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(d_{i}(x\cdot y)-d_{i}(x)\cdot y-x\cdot d_{i}(y),z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(d_{i}(z),x\cdot y)-\omega(d_{i}(x)\cdot y,z)-\omega(x\cdot d_{i}(y),z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega([x,d_{i}(z)],y)-\omega([d_{i}(x),z],y)+\omega(d_{i}(y),[x,z])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega([x,d_{i}(z)]+[d_{i}(x),z]-d_{i}([x,z]),y)=0,

which implies that
d_{i}
for all
i=1,2,3
are derivations on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

By Theorem
5.15
and Proposition
5.13
, we have

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
d_{i}
be symmetric endomorphisms of
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
for all
i=1,2,3
. Then

(i)

(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with
\varepsilon=(-1,-1,-1)
if and only if the quadruple
(\omega,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is a hyper anti-Kähler structure on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\omega^{\sharp}=d_{3}\circ d_{1}^{-1}\circ d_{2}
and
I_{i}=(d_{i-1})^{-1}\circ d_{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

(ii)

(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
is an
\varepsilon
-hyper differential operator on the pre-Lie algebra with
\varepsilon=(1,1,-1)
if and only if the quadruple
(\omega,I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
is a para-hyper anti-Kähler structure on the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\omega^{\sharp}=-d_{3}\circ d_{1}^{-1}\circ d_{2}
and
I_{i}=(d_{i-1})^{-1}\circ d_{i+1}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.
