I
Introduction

The backreaction problem in cosmology asks whether the spatial inhomogeneities of
the real universe modify the expansion history relative to the idealised
Friedmann–Robertson–Walker (FRW) background. After two decades of
investigation—from the foundational averaging formalism of
Buchert
Buchert2000
;
Buchert2001
, through the accelerated-expansion
proposals of Räsänen
Rasanen2006
and Wiltshire
Wiltshire2007
;
Wiltshire2011
, to the gauge-dependent results of
Clarkson–Uzan–Umeh
ClarksonUzan2011
—the problem remains open.
Three specific gaps persist:

(i)

No non-perturbative bound on
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
.
All quantitative estimates
of the backreaction kinematic term
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
rely on expansions in the density
contrast or the metric perturbation. There is no rigorous inequality bounding

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
away from zero or away from a perturbative estimate.

(ii)

No first-principles explanation for the breakdown of CPT at
k_{\mathrm{NL}}
.

It is empirically known that standard cosmological perturbation theory (CPT)
fails for
k>k_{\mathrm{NL}}\simeq 0.17h\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}
, but this is usually
explained by dimensional arguments (density contrast of order unity) rather
than by a convergence theorem.

(iii)

No gauge-invariant, data-computable measure of backreaction.

Current definitions of backreaction depend on the choice of gauge and
averaging domain; there is no quantity that (a) is manifestly gauge-invariant,
(b) reduces to an expression computable from observed
P(k)
, and (c) connects
to the precise information-theoretic statement of the deviation from FRW.

This paper addresses each gap using the mesoscopic statistical mechanics framework of
OsanoExtensivity
;
OsanoPerturbation
. The central objects are the combined
coarse-graining operator
\mathcal{C}
, the mesoscopic partition function
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
, and the connection formula

F(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\sum_{i<j}I(i,j;\lambda)+O\!\left(|\Lambda|\ell^{-d}e^{-2\ell/\xi}\right),

(1)

established in
OsanoPerturbation
, which expresses the full free energy as the mesoscopic free energy minus the total inter-cell mutual information.

II
The Mesoscopic Framework in Cosmology

II.1
Buchert Averaging and the Mesoscopic Operator

Let
(\mathcal{M},g_{\mu\nu})
be a spacetime satisfying the Einstein equations with
pressureless dust, and let
\mathcal{D}_{t}
be a spatial domain comoving with the
matter flow at cosmic time
t
. The Buchert spatial average of a scalar
A
over

\mathcal{D}_{t}
is

\langle A\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}:=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}A\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm{d}^{3}x}{\int_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm{d}^{3}x},

(2)

where
h
is the determinant of the induced spatial metric.

The Buchert equations read
Buchert2000

\displaystyle 3\dot{a}_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}/a_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}

\displaystyle=8\pi G\langle\varrho\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}+\Lambda,

(3)

\displaystyle 3\ddot{a}_{\mathcal{D}}/a_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle=-4\pi G\langle\varrho\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}+\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}+\Lambda,

(4)

where
a_{\mathcal{D}}
is the effective scale factor,
\langle\varrho\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}
is the
averaged matter density, and the
kinematic backreaction
is

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}=\tfrac{2}{3}\bigl(\langle\theta^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}-\langle\theta\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}\bigr)-2\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}.

(5)

Here
\theta=\nabla_{\mu}u^{\mu}
is the expansion scalar and
\sigma^{2}=\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}/2
is the shear scalar of the matter flow

u^{\mu}
.

Remark II.1

(Structural identification with the second cumulant)
.

The term
\langle\theta^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}-\langle\theta\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
in (
5
) is the spatial
variance of
\theta
over
\mathcal{D}
, precisely analogous to the second
cumulant
\kappa_{2}=\langle(\delta\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}})^{2}\rangle_{0}
of the perturbation in the mesoscopic
framework
OsanoPerturbation
. The shear term
-2\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}
contributes
a negative-definite correction, exactly as the third-cumulant term in the SM
expansion. This structural correspondence is exact, not approximate.

\Sigma_{t}

\xi_{\rm corr}\ll\ell\ll L_{H},

(6)

\xi_{\rm corr}

5

The combined coarse-graining operator

III
Result I: Non-Perturbative Bound on Backreaction

III.1
The Gibbs–Bogoliubov Inequality in Statistical Mechanics

The Gibbs–Bogoliubov (G-B) inequality
Peierls1938
;
BarkerHenderson1967

states that for any splitting
H=H_{0}+\lambda V
,

F(\lambda)\leq F_{0}+\lambda\langle V\rangle_{0},

(8)

proved in
OsanoPerturbation
via Jensen’s inequality applied to

e^{-\beta\lambda V}
. The second-order correction
-(\beta\lambda^{2}/2)\kappa_{2}\leq 0

always lowers
F
below the G-B bound, giving the two-sided estimate

F_{0}+\lambda\kappa_{1}-\tfrac{\beta\lambda^{2}}{2}\kappa_{2}\leq F(\lambda)\leq F_{0}+\lambda\kappa_{1}.

(9)

Crucially, (
9
) holds non-perturbatively: it requires only
convexity of the exponential, not a small-
\lambda
expansion.

III.2
Conjecture: The G-B Inequality Extends to the Gravitational Setting

Definition III.1

(Effective cosmological free energy)
.

For the effective (averaged) cosmological system in domain
\mathcal{D}
, define
the
effective free energy
as

F_{\rm cosmo}:=-\frac{1}{\beta_{\rm eff}}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\rm cosmo},

(10)

where
\mathcal{Z}_{\rm cosmo}=\int\mathcal{D}[a]e^{-\beta_{\rm eff}S_{\rm eff}[a]}
is the partition function over the
effective scale factor with action

S_{\rm eff}[a]=\int(3\dot{a}^{2}a-8\pi G\bar{\varrho}a^{3}/3)\,\mathrm{d}t
,
and
\beta_{\rm eff}=(k_{B}T_{\rm eff})^{-1}
with
k_{B}T_{\rm eff}
the
kinetic energy scale of matter peculiar velocities.

Conjecture III.2

(Cosmological Gibbs–Bogoliubov inequality)
.

Under the decomposition
S_{\rm eff}[a]=S_{\rm FRW}[a]+\lambda S_{\rm pert}[a]
, the effective cosmological free energy satisfies

F_{\rm cosmo}(\lambda)\leq F_{\rm FRW}+\lambda\langle S_{\rm pert}\rangle_{\rm FRW},

(11)

where the average on the right is taken with respect to the FRW path-integral
measure.

Remark III.3
.

III.2
is the gravitational analogue of (
8
). In
the quantum mechanical setting, the analogous statement (Peierls’s
inequality
Peierls1938
) holds for any self-adjoint Hamiltonian. In the
classical path-integral context used here, the conjecture rests on the
convexity of
e^{-\beta S_{\rm pert}}
, which holds whenever
S_{\rm pert}
is
real and bounded below—conditions satisfied in synchronous comoving gauge for
the standard matter perturbation. A full proof would require careful treatment
of the path-integral measure over metrics, which we defer to future work.

III.3
The Backreaction Bound

Theorem III.4

(Non-perturbative lower bound on
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
)
.

Assume
III.2
. Then for dust matter in the synchronous comoving
gauge, the kinematic backreaction satisfies

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}\geq\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}^{(1)}:=\tfrac{2}{3}\langle(\delta\theta^{(1)})^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}-2\langle(\sigma^{(1)})^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}},

(12)

where
\theta^{(1)}
and
\sigma^{(1)}
are the first-order (linear) expansion
scalar and shear computed from the linearised Einstein equations, and the
inequality holds non-perturbatively (without expanding in powers of
\delta
).

Proof.

From the Buchert equations (
3
)–(
4
) with mass
conservation
\langle\varrho\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}a_{\mathcal{D}}^{3}=\mathrm{const}
, the kinematic
backreaction appears in the acceleration equation as

3\ddot{a}_{\mathcal{D}}/a_{\mathcal{D}}+4\pi G\langle\varrho\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
.
The left-hand side is determined by the dynamics of the effective scale factor

a_{\mathcal{D}}
, which we identify with the physical degree of freedom
conjugate to the effective free energy
F_{\rm cosmo}
in
definition

III.1
.

Under
III.2
, the true
F_{\rm cosmo}(\lambda)\leq F_{\rm FRW}+\lambda\langle S_{\rm pert}\rangle
, i.e., the true effective action is

lower
(more negative) than the linear approximation. Since

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}=-\partial^{2}F_{\rm cosmo}/\partial V^{2}\big|_{T,N}
at leading order
(by the analogy between the backreaction and the second pressure derivative),
a lower
F
corresponds to a larger (more positive)
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
. Specifically,
applying the two-sided estimate (
9
) to
F_{\rm cosmo}
:

F_{\rm FRW}+\kappa_{1}^{\rm cosmo}-\tfrac{\beta}{2}\kappa_{2}^{\rm cosmo}\leq F_{\rm cosmo}(\lambda)\leq F_{\rm FRW}+\kappa_{1}^{\rm cosmo},

and differentiating twice with respect to volume (which maps
\kappa_{2}^{\rm cosmo}\mapsto\langle\theta^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}-\langle\theta\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
via the kinematic identity

\dot{\mathcal{V}}/\mathcal{V}=\langle\theta\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}
):

\tfrac{2}{3}\langle(\delta\theta)^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D},\mathrm{full}}-2\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D},\mathrm{full}}\geq\tfrac{2}{3}\langle(\delta\theta^{(1)})^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}-2\langle(\sigma^{(1)})^{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}},

(13)

which is exactly (
12
).
∎

Corollary III.5

(Backreaction cannot be entirely eliminated by non-linear effects)
.

Any mechanism that attempts to cancel
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
through non-linear effects (such as
vorticity generation or frame dragging) must overcome the lower bound

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}\geq\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}^{(1)}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}^{(1)}>0
in a given domain,
then
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}>0
and backreaction contributes positively to the effective
acceleration, non-perturbatively.

Remark III.6

(Relation to the perturbative debate)
.

Much of the backreaction debate (see
IshibashiWald2006
;
FlanaganHughes1996
)
concerns whether
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
is large enough to explain dark energy without
\Lambda
.

theorem

III.4
does not settle this question, but it does establish a
non-perturbative floor: whatever the non-linear dynamics does, it can only

increase

\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
above the linearised value. The often-cited argument that
non-linearities suppress backreaction
IshibashiWald2006
is therefore
inconsistent with
III.2
and with the G-B inequality in the SM
context.

IV
Result II: The KAM Radius and the Non-Linear Scale

IV.1
Radius of Convergence of the Mesoscopic Cumulant Series

The mesoscopic cumulant expansion
OsanoPerturbation

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n+1}\beta^{n-1}\lambda^{n}\kappa_{n}^{\rm meso}/n!

converges absolutely for
|\lambda|<R_{\mathrm{meso}}
where, from the stability bound proved
in
OsanoPerturbation
:

R_{\mathrm{meso}}=\frac{1}{\beta\max_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}\left|\bar{v}_{ij}\right|},

(14)

with
\bar{v}_{ij}
the cell-averaged inter-cell interaction (
7
).

IV.2
The Gravitational Cell-Averaged Potential

For the gravitational potential perturbation in Newtonian gauge, Poisson’s
equation gives
\nabla^{2}\Phi=4\pi G\bar{\varrho}\delta
, so in Fourier space

\tilde{\Phi}(k)=-4\pi G\bar{\varrho}\tilde{\delta}(k)/k^{2}
. The cell-averaged
potential interaction (
7
) between cells
V_{i}
and
V_{j}

separated by
r_{ij}=|\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{x}_{j}|
is

\bar{v}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}k^{2}P_{\Phi}(k)W^{2}(k\ell)j_{0}(kr_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}k,

(15)

where
P_{\Phi}(k)=(4\pi G\bar{\varrho})^{2}P_{\delta}(k)/k^{4}
is the potential power
spectrum and
W(u)=3j_{1}(u)/u
is the spherical top-hat window. The maximum
value occurs at adjacent cells (
r_{ij}=\ell
) and at the non-linear scale where

P_{\delta}(k)k^{3}/(2\pi^{2})\sim 1
:

\max|\bar{v}_{ij}|=\bar{v}(\ell,\ell)\simeq\left(\frac{4\pi G\bar{\varrho}}{k_{\mathrm{NL}}^{2}}\right)^{2}\cdot\frac{k_{\mathrm{NL}}^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}=\frac{(4\pi G\bar{\varrho})^{2}}{2\pi^{2}k_{\mathrm{NL}}},

(16)

where we used
P_{\delta}(k_{\mathrm{NL}})=2\pi^{2}/k_{\mathrm{NL}}^{3}
at the non-linear scale
(by definition of
k_{\mathrm{NL}}
via
\Delta^{2}(k_{\mathrm{NL}})=1
).

IV.3
The KAM (Kolmogorov-Arnold-Moser) Identification

Theorem IV.1

(Convergence radius equals the non-linear scale)
.

The radius of convergence of the mesoscopic cumulant expansion for the
cosmological perturbation problem is

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{meso}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{v}^{2}}{(4\pi G\bar{\varrho})^{2}/(2\pi^{2}k_{\mathrm{NL}})}=\frac{2\pi^{2}\sigma_{v}^{2}k_{\mathrm{NL}}}{(4\pi G\bar{\varrho})^{2}}

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{v}^{2}}{H^{2}f^{2}\Omega_{m}^{2}}\cdot k_{\mathrm{NL}},

where
f=\,\mathrm{d}\ln D/\,\mathrm{d}\ln a\approx\Omega_{m}^{0.55}
is the linear growth rate
and
D
is the growth factor. In the matter-dominated era,

4\pi G\bar{\varrho}=3H^{2}\Omega_{m}/2
, so

R_{\mathrm{meso}}=\frac{8\pi^{2}\sigma_{v}^{2}k_{\mathrm{NL}}}{9H^{4}\Omega_{m}^{2}f^{2}}.

(18)

Using the linear-theory relation
\sigma_{v}^{2}=H^{2}f^{2}P_{v}
where

P_{v}=\int k^{-2}P_{\delta}(k)\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k/(2\pi)^{3}
is the velocity power
spectrum, and noting
P_{v}\sim k_{\rm eq}^{-3}
for the peak of the power
spectrum, one obtains

R_{\mathrm{meso}}=O(k_{\mathrm{NL}}/k_{\rm eq}^{3})=O(k_{\mathrm{NL}}^{-1})

up to dimensionless factors of order unity that depend on the shape of
P(k)
.

Proof.

Substituting (
16
) into (
14
) and using

\beta^{-1}_{\rm eff}=k_{B}T_{\rm eff}\simeq\frac{3}{2}m\sigma_{v}^{2}

(the kinetic energy per CDM particle), we obtain (
17
) directly.
The identification
R_{\mathrm{meso}}=O(k_{\mathrm{NL}}^{-1})
follows from dimensional analysis
together with the definition of
k_{\mathrm{NL}}
as the scale at which

\Delta^{2}(k)\equiv k^{3}P(k)/(2\pi^{2})=1
.
∎

Remark IV.2

(KAM interpretation)
.

In the language of the KAM theorem for Hamiltonian
dynamics
Kolmogorov1954
;
Arnold1963
;
Moser1962
, the perturbation

\lambda\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}
of the ADM Hamiltonian preserves the FRW “tori” (orbits in
the ADM phase space) for
\lambda<\lambda_{c}\simeq R_{\mathrm{meso}}
. For

\lambda>R_{\mathrm{meso}}
the tori break, the perturbation series diverges, and the
system enters the chaotic (non-linear structure formation) regime. This
provides the first dynamical-systems explanation for why standard CPT fails at

k>k_{\mathrm{NL}}
: it is not simply that
\delta>1
, but that the convergence radius
of the underlying series is exceeded.

Remark IV.3

(Numerical value)
.

For
\Lambda
CDM with
\Omega_{m}=0.31
,
H_{0}=67.4\mathrm{km}\mathrm{s}^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}

V
Result III: Gauge-Invariant Mutual Information from
P(k)

V.1
The Gaussian Density Field

On scales
\ell>R_{\mathrm{NL}}
where the density contrast
\delta(\bm{x})
is well
approximated by a Gaussian random field, its statistics are entirely determined by
the two-point function. We work at linear order in the matter-dominated era in
Newtonian gauge, where
\delta
is related to
\Phi
by Poisson’s equation and is
gauge-invariant
Bardeen1980
;
KodamaSasaki1984
to this order. We partition
the spatial hypersurface into cubic cells
\{V_{i}\}
of side
\ell
and define the
smoothed density contrast in cell
i
:

\bar{\delta}_{i}:=\frac{1}{\ell^{3}}\int_{V_{i}}\delta(\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}^{3}x.

(19)

This is a linear functional of
\delta
, hence Gaussian, with zero mean and
variance

\sigma_{\ell}^{2}:=\langle\bar{\delta}_{i}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}k^{2}P(k)W^{2}(k\ell)\,\mathrm{d}k,

(20)

where
W(u)=3j_{1}(u)/u
is the spherical top-hat window function.

V.2
Pearson Correlation and Mutual Information

The cross-correlation between cells
i
and
j
separated by
r=|\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{x}_{j}|
is

\xi_{\ell}(r):=\langle\bar{\delta}_{i}\bar{\delta}_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}k^{2}P(k)W^{2}(k\ell)j_{0}(kr)\,\mathrm{d}k,

(21)

where
j_{0}(x)=\sin(x)/x
. The Pearson correlation coefficient is

r(r):=\frac{\xi_{\ell}(r)}{\sigma_{\ell}^{2}}.

(22)

Theorem V.1

(Gauge-invariant mutual information)
.

For a Gaussian matter density field, the mutual information between the
smoothed density contrasts of cells
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
at separation
r_{ij}
,
defined as in
OsanoExtensivity
;
OsanoPerturbation
, is

\boxed{I(i,j)=-\frac{1}{2}\ln\!\left(1-r^{2}(r_{ij})\right),}

(23)

where
r(r_{ij})
is given by (
22
) with
P(k)
the matter power
spectrum. The formula (
23
) is

(a)

exact
for Gaussian fields,

(b)

gauge-invariant
at linear order, since
\delta
is gauge-invariant
at first order in Newtonian gauge in the matter era,

(c)

directly computable
from the observed matter power spectrum.

Proof.

The joint distribution of
(\bar{\delta}_{i},\bar{\delta}_{j})
is bivariate Gaussian
with covariance matrix

\Sigma=\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&r\\
r&1\end{pmatrix}
.
The mutual information of a bivariate Gaussian is the standard result

I=-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln\det(\Sigma)/(\sigma_{\ell}^{2})^{2}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(1-r^{2})
.
Gauge invariance at linear order follows from the well-known property that the
matter density contrast
\delta\varrho/\bar{\varrho}
is gauge-invariant at first
order in the matter-dominated era
Bardeen1980
;
KodamaSasaki1984
. Since

\bar{\delta}_{i}
is a linear functional of
\delta
,
r_{ij}
and hence
I(i,j)

are gauge-invariant to the same order.
∎

V.3
Non-Linear Corrections to the Gauge Invariance

At second order in
\delta
, the density contrast in different gauges differs by
a term of order
\delta^{2}\sim\sigma_{\ell}^{2}
. The correction to
r(r_{ij})

from gauge transformation is therefore

\delta r|_{\rm gauge}=O(\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\cdot r(r_{ij}))=O(\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\cdot r).

(24)

The corresponding correction to the mutual information:

\delta I|_{\rm gauge}=O\!\left(\frac{r\sigma_{\ell}^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\right).

(25)

VI.1

Buchert equations.

the full non-linear variance cannot be smaller than the linear-order one.

Gauge dependence.

Clarkson, Uzan, and Umeh

The most directly relevant observational work is

VII
Conclusion

We have derived three new results at the intersection of mesoscopic statistical
mechanics and cosmological perturbation theory.

Result I
(
theorem

III.4
): Under the Gibbs–Bogoliubov
conjecture extended to the gravitational setting
(
III.2
), the kinematic backreaction
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}
satisfies the
non-perturbative lower bound
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}\geq\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}^{(1)}
: non-linear effects can only
increase the backreaction above its linear-order value, never reduce it below.
Arguments that non-linear dynamics suppress backreaction are inconsistent with
the G-B inequality.

Result II
(
theorem

IV.1
): The radius of convergence
R_{\mathrm{meso}}
of the
mesoscopic cumulant expansion for the gravitational perturbation problem equals

O(k_{\mathrm{NL}}^{-1})
, the non-linear scale of the matter power spectrum. This provides
a dynamical-systems (KAM) explanation for why standard CPT fails at
k>k_{\mathrm{NL}}
:
the relevant series diverges exactly at the non-linear scale. For
\Lambda
CDM,

k_{\mathrm{NL}}=0.169h\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}
(
R_{\mathrm{NL}}=5.91\mathrm{Mpc}h^{-1}
).

Result III
(
theorems

V.1
and
V.3
): For a Gaussian density field,
the inter-cell mutual information is exactly
I(i,j)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(1-r_{ij}^{2})
,
gauge-invariant at linear order and fully computable from the observed
P(k)
.
For
\Lambda
CDM at
z=0
, the nearest-neighbour mutual information ranges from

I_{\rm NN}\approx 0.22
at
\ell=10h^{-1}
Mpc to
I_{\rm NN}\approx 0.05
at

\ell=150h^{-1}
Mpc (
table

1
). The connection formula

F=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\sum I
then gives a data-driven, gauge-invariant measure of the
total backreaction correction to the FRW free energy.

The primary open problem is the identification of the effective temperature scale

k_{B}T_{\rm eff}
in the gravitational partition function. Resolving this is the
essential step for converting the mutual-information measure into a quantitative
prediction for the dark energy equation of state. We expect this to require a
quantum-gravitational or thermodynamic formulation of the ADM partition function,
which we defer to future work.

During the final stages of preparing this manuscript, we became aware of the work of Galoppo, Buchert, and Mourier
Buchert2026
, who present the first direct computation of spatially averaged dynamical quantities in the local Universe using the Cosmicflows-4++ reconstruction and the Buchert averaging formalism. Three points of contact with the present paper are worth stating explicitly.

First, Galoppo et al. find that the kinematic backreaction
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}

is positive throughout their survey volume (30–300Mpc
h^{-1}
) and is not
suppressed by non-linear effects. This is consistent with
corollary

III.5
:
if the linear-order value
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}^{(1)}>0
, then the full
non-linear
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}\geq\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}}^{(1)}>0

non-perturbatively, so any mechanism attempting to cancel the backreaction through non-linear dynamics must overcome the G-B lower bound.

Second, Galoppo et al. find no convergence of the domain-averaged energy budget to the global
\Lambda
CDM background even at 300Mpc
h^{-1}
. This is the
observational manifestation of the divergent-
\sum I
prediction of the present framework: for gravity (
|\phi(r)|\sim r^{-1}
,
s_{0}=1\leq d=3
), temperedness fails in the sense of
OsanoExtensivity
, the inter-cell mutual information decays only as
I(i,j)\sim r^{-2s_{0}}
, and the series
\sum_{i<j}I(i,j)
diverges, so FRW extensivity is never restored at any finite averaging scale.

Third, Galoppo et al. report that the average spatial curvature
\langle{}^{(3)}R\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}
contributes at the
\mathcal{O}(10\%)
level
to the energy budget, an order of magnitude larger than the kinematic term (
\mathcal{O}(1\%)
). The present paper derives a non-perturbative bound and
a mutual information formula for the kinematic term (
5
); it does not yet provide an analogous bound for the curvature term. The Galoppo et al. result
therefore motivates an extension of
theorem

III.4
to the curvature backreaction
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{D}}:=\langle{}^{(3)}R\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}-\langle{}^{(3)}R\rangle_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}
, which we identify as the primary target for future work.
