A nonlocal curve evolution for an immersed elastic filament: global existence and convergence to resistive force theory
Laurel Ohm

Abstract
. We consider a nonlocal curve evolution belonging to a hierarchy of models for the dynamics of an inextensible elastic filament in a 3D Stokes fluid. This model captures the principal part of a full free boundary problem for an elastic filament in Stokes flow. The fluid effects on the filament evolution are encoded in a pseudodifferential force-to-velocity operator which may be regarded as an interpolation between resistive force theory at low wavenumbers and a Stokes boundary value problem at high wavenumbers. Here the curve is considered to be the centerline of a 3D filament with constant cross sectional radius ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 . We show global well-posedness for the curve evolution in the natural energy space. This loosely suggests that the full evolution may be globally well-posed if the large-scale geometry is controlled. Furthermore, we prove convergence to resistive force theory dynamics as ϵ → 0 \epsilon\to 0 , which illustrates how resistive force theory emerges from more detailed models.

Introduction

We consider a nonlocal curve evolution approximating the motion of an inextensible elastic filament immersed in a Stokes fluid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

Our model belongs to a family of curve evolution equations arising in the following way. The thin filament is treated as a rod with rigid cross sections of constant radius

\bm{X}(s,t):\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

\bm{f}(s)=(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\,,\qquad(\cdot)_{s}:=\frac{\partial\cdot}{\partial s}\,,

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}(\bm{X}):\,\bm{f}(s)\mapsto\bm{v}(s)

\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}=-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}(\bm{X})\big[(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\big]\,,\quad\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}=1\,.

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}

This brings us to the curve evolution studied in this paper. Here we consider a natural and novel intermediate model of the form (
1.3
) between the full slender body free boundary problem (
1.26
) and the classical resistive force theory model (
1.18
). Our choice of the force-to-velocity map, which we will denote by
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
, is a pseudodifferential operator whose symbol in the tangential and normal directions to the filament centerline is the explicit Fourier multiplier corresponding to the exact slender body NtD map about a straight cylinder. In particular, we simply apply the straight map to a curved filament (see Definition
1.1
). At low wavenumbers, the map behaves like multiplication by
\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert
, with the tangential direction along the filament weighted twice as much as the normal directions. At high wavenumbers
|k|\gtrsim\frac{1}{\epsilon}
, the map behaves like multiplication by
(\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert)^{-1}
on the Fourier side, i.e., a (scaled) inverse derivative.
In a sense, the force-to-velocity map
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\epsilon}
interpolates between resistive force theory at low wavenumbers and the full slender body NtD map at high wavenumbers.

Our aim is twofold:
(I.) We seek to probe the global well-posedness question for the full slender body free boundary problem (
1.26
) by replacing
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}
with its principal part. Here we prove global well-posedness for (
1.3
) using the map
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
in the natural energy space. One caveat is that we treat (
1.3
) with
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
as a pure curve evolution and do not worry about self-intersection of the filament. While self-intersection would lead to breakdown of the full free boundary problem, we can continue to make sense of the model with
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
even if the filament passes through itself. Self-intersection is inherently a low-wavenumber phenomenon, and at low wavenumbers,
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
looks like resistive force theory rather than
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}
. However, our global well-posedness result for (
1.3
) using
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
loosely suggests that if the large-scale geometry of the full slender body free boundary evolution remains under control, then the full free boundary problem should be globally well-posed.

(II.) In addition, we seek to illustrate how resistive force theory dynamics using
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,{\rm RFT}}
emerge from more detailed models in the singular limit
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
. We prove that the evolution (
1.3
) with
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
converges to the resistive force theory evolution with
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,{\rm RFT}}
as the radius parameter
\epsilon\to 0
, thereby establishing the first rigorous dynamical connection between different levels of the curve evolution hierarchy. The convergence is at an expected but very slow logarithmic-in-
\epsilon
rate, since (
1.4
) only captures the very leading order local effects of the Stokes fluid on the filament. The slow convergence does make the proof delicate, as there is no room for waste.

In
[
1
]
, we recognize (
1.3
) with
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,{\rm RFT}}
as a gradient flow of the filament bending energy
\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,ds
, which allows us to show long-time convergence to 3D Euler elasticae, the critical points of the bending energy. The evolution with
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
is also a gradient flow of the bending energy with respect to the metric

(1.6)

\langle\bm{V},\bm{U}\rangle:=\langle\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\bm{V},\bm{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

defined on perturbations
\bm{V},\bm{U}
of an inextensible curve
\bm{X}
. We may thus frame our convergence result as that of two different gradient flows for the same energy whose metrics converge as
\epsilon\to 0
.

1.1.
The force-to-velocity map

We now define the force-to-velocity map
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
used in (
1.3
). Given
\bm{h}(s):\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined along a filament centerline
\bm{X}
, we denote the projection of
\bm{h}
onto the tangential and normal directions about
\bm{X}
as

(1.7)

\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{h}=(\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{h})\bm{X}_{s}\,,\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{h}=\bm{h}-\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{h}\,.

In addition, we denote the Fourier transform of
\bm{h}
by

(1.8)

\mathcal{F}[\bm{h}(s)](k)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bm{h}(s)e^{i2\pi ks}\,ds\,.

Given a scalar-valued Fourier multiplier
m(k)
, we will use the notation
T_{m}
to denote the operator

(1.9)

T_{m}\bm{h}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m\,\mathcal{F}[\bm{h}]]\,.

Definition 1.1

(Force to velocity map)
.

Given
\bm{X}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
and a line force density
\bm{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, the force-to-velocity map
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
is given by

(1.10)

\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\bm{f}]=\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\,T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f})+\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\,T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{f})\,,

where, for
k\neq 0
, the multipliers
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)
,
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)
are given by

(1.11)

\displaystyle m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{2K_{0}K_{1}+2\pi\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert\big(K_{0}^{2}-K_{1}^{2}\big)}{8\pi^{2}\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert K_{1}^{2}}

(1.12)

\displaystyle m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{2K_{0}K_{1}K_{2}+2\pi\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert\big(K_{1}^{2}(K_{0}+K_{2})-2K_{0}^{2}K_{2}\big)}{4\pi^{2}\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert\big(4K_{1}^{2}K_{2}+2\pi\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert K_{1}(K_{1}^{2}-K_{0}K_{2})\big)}\,,

where
K_{j}=K_{j}(2\pi\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert)
,
j=0,1,2
, are
j^{\text{t}h}
order modified Bessel functions of the second kind.

For
k=0
, we define

(1.13)

m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(0)=\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}\,,\qquad m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(0)=\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\,.

We provide plots of the
inverse
multipliers
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)^{-1}
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)^{-1}
in Figure

to more clearly display the transition from logarithmic behavior (
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)^{-1},m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)^{-1}\sim\left\lvert\log(\epsilon|k|)\right\rvert^{-1}
) at low wavenumbers to linear growth
(m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)^{-1},m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)^{-1}\sim\epsilon|k|)
at high wavenumbers.

For

The regularization (

1.11

\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
in a sense interpolates between resistive force theory at low wavenumbers and the full slender body NtD map at high wavenumbers.

1.2.
Main theorem statements

Through multiplier and tension bounds outlined in the following sections, we show that the evolution

(1.14)

\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}=-\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(\bm{X})\big[(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\big]\,,\quad\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}=1\,.

is a third-order, semilinear, parabolic equation for
\bm{X}
, coupled with an elliptic equation for the filament tension
\tau
. It therefore shares features of both resistive force theory and the full slender body free boundary problem.

In section
3
, we show:

Theorem 1.2

(Global well-posedness)
.

Given
\epsilon>0
and an initial curve
\bm{X}^{\rm in}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, the evolution (
1.14
) with
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
as in Definition
1.1
admits a unique global solution

(1.15)

\bm{X}\in C([0,+\infty);H^{2}_{s})\cap L^{2}_{t,{\rm loc}}H^{7/2}_{s}([0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T})

with initial data
\bm{X}^{\rm in}
.

The starting point for Theorem
1.2
is the energy identity

(1.16)

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,ds=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\,ds\,,

which is explored in greater detail in sections
2.2
and

\bm{X}_{ssss}
alone, due to the presence of the tension. In addition, necessary ingredients in the proof of local well-posedness in the energy space are
a priori

1.3.
Connection with the slender body Neumann-to-Dirichlet map

As mentioned, arguably the most detailed method for incorporating 3D fluid effects into the force-to-velocity map
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
is by solving the following
slender body boundary value problem
, introduced by the author together with Mori and Spirn in
[
30
,
31
]
. Given
0<\epsilon\ll 1
sufficiently small, we may define a ‘fattened’ version of the curve
\bm{X}
as

(1.21)

\Sigma_{\epsilon}(t)=\big\{\bm{x}\in\bm{X}_{s}(s,t)^{\perp}\,:\,{\rm dist}(\bm{x},\bm{X}(s,t))<\epsilon\,,\,s\in\mathbb{T}\big\}\,.

The 3D body
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
may be considered as a regularization of the filament centerline which allows us to seek
(\bm{u},p):\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying the boundary value problem

(1.22)

\displaystyle-\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad{\rm{div}\,}\bm{u}=0\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\bm{\sigma}[\bm{u}]\bm{n})\,\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(s,\theta)\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\bm{f}(s)\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\text{on }\partial\Sigma_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\bm{u}\big|_{\partial\Sigma_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=\bm{v}(s)\,,\qquad\qquad\quad\;\;\,\text{unknown but independent of }\theta\,,

and
\left\lvert\bm{u}\right\rvert\to 0
as
\left\lvert\bm{x}\right\rvert\to\infty
. In this boundary value problem, we make sense of the force data
\bm{f}(s)
defined along a 1D curve as the total surface stress
\bm{\sigma}[\bm{u}]\bm{n}=(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{\rm T}-p{\bf I})\bm{n}
per cross section of the 3D body
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
, weighted by the body’s surface area through a Jacobian factor
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}
. Since this is only a partial, ‘angle-averaged’ Neumann boundary condition, it must be supplemented with an additional partial boundary condition in the form of a geometric constraint on the Dirichlet boundary value of
\bm{u}
known as a fiber integrity condition. In particular,
\bm{u}\big|_{\partial\Sigma_{\epsilon}}
is unknown but constrained to be a function of arclength
s
only.

The
slender body Neumann-to-Dirichlet (NtD) map

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}
is then defined as the map

(1.23)

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(\bm{X}):\bm{f}(s)\mapsto\bm{v}(s)

which comes from solving the (Neumann) boundary value problem (
1.22
) for
\bm{u}
and evaluating the Dirichlet boundary value on
\partial\Sigma_{\epsilon}
. The solution to this boundary value problem depends on both the filament radius
\epsilon
and the centerline geometry
\bm{X}(s)
in a complicated way. To isolate these complexities, we can consider the slender body NtD map in the simplest possible geometry, which here is the straight, constant-radius filament with periodic boundary conditions at the ends.

In particular, let
\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}
denote the body (
1.21
) with straight centerline
\bm{X}(s)=s\bm{e}_{z}
,
s\in\mathbb{T}
. The behavior of the slender body NtD map (
1.23
) about
\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}
conveniently diagonalizes, with differing behavior in directions tangent and normal to the filament centerline. In particular, for
k\neq 0
, it may be shown (see
[
28
]
) that

(1.24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(s\bm{e}_{z})[e^{2\pi iks}\bm{e}_{z}]

\displaystyle=m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)e^{2\pi iks}\bm{e}_{z}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(s\bm{e}_{z})[e^{2\pi iks}\bm{e}_{j}]

\displaystyle=m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)e^{2\pi iks}\bm{e}_{j}\,,\quad j=x,y\,,

where the eigenvalues
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)
are as in Definition
1.1
. More specifically, for the straight filament, we have that the slender body NtD map
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(s\bm{e}_{z})[\cdot]
is exactly the operator
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(s\bm{e}_{z})[\cdot]
of Definition
1.1
(after removing the
k=0
mode).

For a general curved filament, the map
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(\bm{X})
no longer admits an explicit Fourier multiplier representation. However, in
[
33
, Theorem 1.5 & Corollary 1.6]
, it is shown that the principal part of the general operator
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(\bm{X})
is given by the straight NtD map
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(s\bm{e}_{z})
in the sense that

(1.25)

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(\bm{X})[\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X}]=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(s\bm{e}_{z})[\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X}]+\mathcal{R}_{\rm err}\,,

where the remainder terms
\mathcal{R}_{\rm err}
are lower order with respect to regularity or size in
\epsilon
. In
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
, this decomposition is leveraged to show qualitative local well-posedness for the full
slender body free boundary problem

(1.26)

\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}=-\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(\bm{X})\big[(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\big]\,,\quad\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}=1\,,

where
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm NtD}(\bm{X})
is given by (
1.23
) for the instantaneous curve shape
\bm{X}
. More precisely, local well-posedness is shown in
C([0,T];h^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}))
for an initial curve belonging to the little Hölder space
h^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
. Note that, by (
1.25
), the evolution (
1.26
) is a third order, quasilinear parabolic equation for
\bm{X}
. In particular, this regularity is shared by the evolution (
1.14
) considered here.

The setting of Hölder spaces is more convenient for the operator decomposition (
1.25
), but less convenient for obtaining insight into long-time dynamics. Here our choice of operator
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
as in Definition
1.1
is inspired by the principal straight part of the slender body NtD map, adapted slightly for application directly to a curved filament. This allows us to develop the solution theory of the principal dynamics in a natural energy space while avoiding the additional difficulties associated with (a.) decomposing the full operator as in (
1.25
) in lower regularity spaces, and (b.) accounting for filament self-intersection.

2.
Multiplier bounds and energetics

Before proceeding to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we make note of some useful bounds for the multipliers
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}
comprising the operator
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
. These may be used to show that the energy identity satisfied by solutions to (
1.14
) is in fact signed, which will be crucial for the global-in-time behavior of the filament.

2.1.
Multiplier bounds

We begin with a collection of lemmas bounding the behavior of the multipliers
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)
in both
k
and
\epsilon
.
First, from
[
33
, Lemmas 3.4 & 3.5]
, we note the following general bounds for the behavior of
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}
, which differs at high versus low wavenumbers.

Lemma 2.1

(Multiplier behavior
[
33
]
)
.

Given
\epsilon>0
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)
as in (
1.11
), (
1.12
), there exist constants
c>0
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)\,,\;m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\,,&\left\lvert k\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\\
c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{-1}\,,&\left\lvert k\right\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\end{cases}\,,

\displaystyle m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)^{-1}\,,\;m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}\,,&\left\lvert k\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\\
c\,\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert\,,&\left\lvert k\right\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\,.\end{cases}

This behavior is displayed in Figure
2
.
In addition, we will require some refinements to Lemma
2.1
at both high and low wavenumbers. For high wavenumbers, by
[
28
, Proposition 1.4]
, we may obtain a more precise linear growth rate for
(m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t})^{-1}
and
(m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n})^{-1}
as follows.

Lemma 2.2

(High wavenumber refinement
[
28
]
)
.

There exist constants
c_{\rm t}
,
c_{\rm n}>0
such that for all
k>0
, the multipliers
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}
in (
1.11
), (
1.12
) satisfy

(2.2)

\displaystyle c_{\rm t}^{-1}\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)^{-1}\leq c_{\rm t}^{-1}(\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert+1)\qquad c_{\rm n}^{-1}\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)^{-1}\leq c_{\rm n}^{-1}(\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert+1)\,.

For low wavenumbers, we note the following refined asymptotics, which will allow us to prove the convergence result of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 2.3

(Low wavenumber refinement)
.

For
0<\epsilon<1
and for
0<\left\lvert k\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}
, we have the refined low wavenumber bounds

(2.3)

m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}\leq c(1+\left\lvert\log\left\lvert k\right\rvert\right\rvert)\,,\quad m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\leq c(1+\left\lvert\log\left\lvert k\right\rvert\right\rvert)\,,

along with
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(0)-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}=m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(0)-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}=0
.

In particular, this difference is bounded independent of
\epsilon
at low wavenumbers.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Expanding
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)
in a Taylor series about
\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0
yields

(2.4)

\displaystyle m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1-2\gamma+2\log\pi-2\log(\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert)}{4\pi}+O(\epsilon^{2}k^{2}\log^{2}(\epsilon|k|))

\displaystyle m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-2\gamma-2\log\pi-2\log(\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert)}{8\pi}+O(\epsilon^{2}k^{2}\log^{2}(\epsilon|k|))\,.

Here
\gamma\approx 0.5772
is the Euler gamma. Subtracting
\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}
from
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)
and
\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}
from
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)
yields remainder terms satisfying (
2.3
).
∎

2.2.
Energetics

The filament evolution (
1.14
) admits the following energy identity:

(2.5)

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,ds=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\,ds\,,

which may be obtained by dotting equation (
1.14
) with
(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}
and integrating over
\mathbb{T}
. Noting that the inextensibility constraint
\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}=1
implies
\partial_{t}\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{s}=0
, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}\cdot(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\partial\bm{X}_{s}}{\partial t}\cdot(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})\,ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\partial\bm{X}_{s}}{\partial t}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}\,ds=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,ds\,,

yielding the left hand side of (
2.5
).

To see that the right hand side of (
2.5
) is signed, we make note of the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

Given
\bm{f}\in H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})
, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bm{f}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\bm{f}]\,ds

\displaystyle\geq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\big(\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\geq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{f}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\,.

We note that this lower bound is a bit pessimistic (in
\epsilon
) due to the differing
\epsilon
-scaling of the multipliers at low versus high wavenumbers (Lemma
2.1
); however, it will be sufficient for establishing local and global well-posedness.

Proof.

Using Definition
1.1
for
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}
along with Parseval’s theorem, we may write

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bm{f}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\bm{f}]\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg((\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f})\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f})+(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{f})\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{f})\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f}](k)\right\rvert^{2}m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)+\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{f}](k)\right\rvert^{2}m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)

\displaystyle\geq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f}](k)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{f}](k)\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{-1}\bigg(\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f}](k)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{f}](k)\right\rvert^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\big(\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\|\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\geq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{f}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\,,

by the triangle inequality. Here in the third line we have used the behavior of the multipliers
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}
and
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}
from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Using Proposition
2.4
, the filament bending energy is non-increasing in time; in particular,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq-c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\,.

The energy inequality (
2.9
) will form the basis for our proof of global well-posedness.

3.
Global well-posedness

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We begin with bounds for the tension
\tau(s,t)
appearing as a Lagrange multiplier in (
1.14
). These bounds are leveraged to show local well-posedness in the energy space, which is then upgraded to global well-posedness using the energy inequality (
2.9
).

3.1.
Preliminaries

Here we collect some identities and bounds that will be used throughout the following sections. We begin with the observation that, by differentiating the inextensibility constraint
\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}=1
, we obtain the following identities:

(3.1)

\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{ss}=0\,,\quad\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}=-\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}\,,\quad 3\bm{X}_{sss}\cdot\bm{X}_{ss}=-\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{ssss}\,.

Next, we make note of some useful interpolation inequalities on
\mathbb{T}
. Given
h:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{\mathbb{T}}h\,ds=0
, we may bound

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\partial_{s}h\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\,,

\displaystyle\left\lVert h_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert h_{s}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/3}\,,

\displaystyle\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert h_{s}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2/3}\,.

The proof of these may be found, for example, in
[
2
]
.

Finally, we will require the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given scalar-valued
h\in H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})
and vector-valued
\bm{v}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
with
\left\lvert\bm{v}\right\rvert=1
, we may bound

(3.3)

\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\left\lVert h\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}\,.

In addition, we may bound

(3.4)

\left\lVert h\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}\,.

Proof.

For any scalar-valued
\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
with
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}=1
, we may calculate

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}h\,\varphi\,ds\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}(h\bm{v})\cdot(\bm{v}\varphi)\,ds\right\rvert\leq c\left\lVert h\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\left\lVert\bm{v}\varphi\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert h\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}=c\left\lVert h\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\,.

Taking the supremum over all such
\varphi
yields (
3.3
).

Similarly, taking any vector-valued
\bm{\varphi}\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
with
\left\lVert\bm{\varphi}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}=1
, we may calculate

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}h\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\,ds\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\left\lVert\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\varphi}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\bm{\varphi}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}=c\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\,.

Again taking the supremum over all such
\bm{\varphi}
yields (
3.4
).
∎

3.2.
Tension estimates

As in the analogous tension determination problems of
[
29
,
21
,
10
,
1
,
36
]
, we will use that
\partial_{t}\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{s}=0
to obtain an equation and subsequent estimates for the tension
\tau
. Differentiating (
1.14
) in
s
and dotting with
\bm{X}_{s}
, we have that at each time
t
(which we will omit from our notation throughout this section),
\tau
satisfies the equation

(3.7)

\big(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[(\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\big)_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{s}=\big(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]\big)_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{s}\,.

We show the following.

Lemma 3.2

(Tension bounds)
.

Given
\epsilon>0
and
\bm{X}\in H^{7/2}(\mathbb{T})
, the tension equation (
3.7
) admits a unique solution
\tau\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
. Furthermore,
\tau
satisfies the bound

(3.8)

\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{4})\big(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{3/4}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{5/4}+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{10/3}\big)\,.

Given two nearby curves
\bm{X}_{1},\bm{X}_{2}\in H^{7/2}(\mathbb{T})
, the solutions
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
to the corresponding tension determination problems (
3.7
) satisfy

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}(1+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{9}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{9})\times

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\bigg(\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{3/4}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3}\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2/3}(\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3})

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{3/4}\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1/4}\bigg]\,.

Note that the right hand side of (
3.8
) involves less than one copy of the highest derivative of
\bm{X}
(here we use
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{3/4}
). In contrast, the analogous bound (
1.17
) in the resistive force theory setting involves a full copy of the highest derivative of the curve in the corresponding energy space, which makes the tension behavior there critical.

Proof.

For
(\tau,\varphi)\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\times H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
, we may define the bilinear form

(3.10)

\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(\tau,\varphi):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[(\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\cdot(\varphi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\,ds\,,

which, using Parseval’s theorem, may be seen to satisfy

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(\tau,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg((\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s})+(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\tau\bm{X}_{ss})\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}\bigg(m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)\mathcal{F}[\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}[\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}]}+m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)\mathcal{F}[\varphi\bm{X}_{ss}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}[\tau\bm{X}_{ss}]}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\cdot(\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s})+T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\cdot(\tau\bm{X}_{ss})\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(\varphi,\tau)\,.

In addition, using the multiplier bounds of Lemma
2.1
and Parseval’s theorem, we may bound

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(\tau,\varphi)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor}\bigg(\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}]\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}]\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\varphi\bm{X}_{ss}]\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\tau\bm{X}_{ss}]\right\rvert\bigg)

\displaystyle+c\,\epsilon^{-1}\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{-1}\bigg(\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}]\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}]\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\varphi\bm{X}_{ss}]\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\tau\bm{X}_{ss}]\right\rvert\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\bigg(\left\lVert\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\left\lVert\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}+\left\lVert\varphi\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\left\lVert\tau\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}\,.

Finally, we note that, by Proposition
2.4
, we may bound
\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(\tau,\tau)
below as

(3.13)

\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(\tau,\tau)\geq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\big(\left\lVert\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\left\lVert\tau\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\big)\,.

Using Proposition
3.1
, bound (
3.3
), we may estimate

(3.14)

\left\lVert\tau_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\leq c\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\,.

To obtain a bound for the
L^{2}
part of
\tau
, as in
[
1
]
, we will need Fenchel’s Theorem
[
6
]
: A closed
C^{2}
curve
\bm{X}
must satisfy

(3.15)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,ds\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert\,ds\geq 2\pi\,.

Letting
\tau_{0}
denote the mean of
\tau
,

(3.16)

\tau_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tau\,ds\,,

on
\mathbb{T}
, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\tau-\tau_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\tau_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq c(\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2}+\left\lvert\tau_{0}\right\rvert^{2})

\displaystyle\leq c(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\left\lVert\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}^{2}+\left\lvert\tau_{0}\right\rvert^{2})\,.

We may estimate
\tau_{0}
as

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\tau_{0}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\tau_{0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,ds}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}\right\rvert=\frac{1}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}}\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tau_{0}\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{ss}\,ds\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tau_{0}\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\frac{\bm{X}_{ss}}{\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}}\,ds\right\rvert\leq c\left\lVert\tau_{0}\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\,.

Noting that for any
\bm{v}\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
with
\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}=1
we may calculate

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tau-\tau_{0})\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{v}\,ds\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\tau-\tau_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{v}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq c\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\left\lVert\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\,,

we may bound

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tau_{0}\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert(\tau-\tau_{0})\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}+\left\lVert\tau\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\left\lVert\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}+\left\lVert\tau\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\,.

Thus the mean of
\tau

(3.21)

\left\lvert\tau_{0}\right\rvert\leq c\big(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\left\lVert\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}+\left\lVert\tau\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\big)\,,

and altogether we may bound

(3.22)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\big((\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\left\lVert\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}+\left\lVert\tau\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1/2}(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(\tau,\tau)^{1/2}\,.

By the Lax-Milgram lemma, given

, there is then a unique
\tau\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
satisfying

(3.23)

\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(\tau,\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}g\,\varphi\,ds\qquad\forall\;\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})\,.

We consider

given by the right hand side of (
3.7
). In particular, upon multiplying both sides of (

) by

\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}(\tau,\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]\cdot(\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\,ds\,.

Using Parseval’s theorem and the multiplier behavior of Lemma
2.1
, the right hand side of (

(3.25)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]\cdot(\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\,ds\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{X}_{ssss}\,ds\right\rvert+\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tau\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{X}_{ssss}\,ds\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)\mathcal{F}[\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{X}_{ssss}]}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)\mathcal{F}[\tau\bm{X}_{ss}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{X}_{ssss}]}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{X}_{ssss}]}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\tau\bm{X}_{ss}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{X}_{ssss}]}\right\rvert

\displaystyle+c\,\epsilon^{-1}\sum_{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{-1}\bigg(\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\tau_{s}\bm{X}_{s}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{X}_{ssss}]}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\tau\bm{X}_{ss}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{X}_{ssss}]}\right\rvert\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\bigg(\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2/3}+1\bigg)

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2/3}\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3}(\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3})

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1/3}\bigg]\,.

Proof.

We begin by writing

(3.43)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X}]

\displaystyle=T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})+\mathcal{R}[\bm{X}]\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}[\bm{X}]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\big(\bm{X}_{s}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})\big)\bm{X}_{s}}_{\mathcal{R}_{a}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})}_{\mathcal{R}_{b}}-\underbrace{\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})}_{\mathcal{R}_{c}}\,.

The terms
\mathcal{R}_{b}
and
\mathcal{R}_{c}
are the simplest to estimate. In particular, using the identities (
3.1
) coming from the inextensibility constraint, we may write

(3.44)

\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X}=-3(\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss})\bm{X}_{s}

so that, using Parseval’s theorem, we may bound

(3.45)

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)^{2}(1+\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X}]\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{2}\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor}(1+\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X}]\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+c\,\epsilon^{-2}\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{-2}(1+\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X}]\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-2}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{2}\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss})\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-2}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{2}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\left\lVert\partial_{s}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}^{2}\,.

Here we have used the multiplier bounds of Lemma
2.1
in the first inequality, and the second bound of Proposition
3.1
in the final inequality. The interpolation inequalities (
3.2
) then yield

(3.46)

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{3/2}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{7/3}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\,.

We may thus estimate
\mathcal{R}_{b}
as

(3.47)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{b}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}=\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{10/3}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\,.

A similar calculation for the normal direction term
\mathcal{R}_{c}
yields

(3.48)

\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{c}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}=\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{10/3}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\,.

To estimate
\mathcal{R}_{a}
, we first use that
\partial_{s}
commutes with
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}
to write

(3.49)

\bm{X}_{s}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})=\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\partial_{s}^{3}(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\bm{X}_{s})\,.

We then consider the difference

(3.50)

\mathcal{R}_{a,\rm diff}=\partial_{s}^{3}(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\bm{X}_{s})-c_{\rm n}\epsilon^{-1}\partial_{s}^{2}\bm{X}_{s}\,,

where
c_{\rm n}
is the constant from Lemma
2.2
. Then, using the bounds of Lemma
2.2
, we may estimate

(3.51)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{a,\rm diff}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}(1+\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}\big(\left\lvert k\right\rvert m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)-c_{\rm n}\epsilon^{-1}\big)^{2}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\bm{X}_{s}]\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}(1+\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}(c_{\rm n}\epsilon^{-1})^{2}(\epsilon\left\lvert k\right\rvert+1)^{-2}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{4}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\bm{X}_{s}]\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-3}\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}(1+\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{3}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\bm{X}_{s}]\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-3}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\,.

Using (
3.49
), we may write

(3.52)

\bm{X}_{s}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X})=c_{\rm n}\epsilon^{-1}\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}+\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{a,\rm diff}=-c_{\rm n}\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}+\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{a,\rm diff}\,,

so that, by (
3.51
) and the interpolation inequalities (
3.2
), we may estimate the final remainder term as

(3.53)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{a}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\rm n}\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{a,\rm diff}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\big(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{7/3}+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{8/3}\big)

\displaystyle+c\,\epsilon^{-3/2}\big(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{4/3}+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-3/2}\big(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{4/3}+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})\,.

Combining the bounds for
\mathcal{R}_{a}
,
\mathcal{R}_{b}
, and
\mathcal{R}_{c}
, we obtain the estimate (
3.41
).

To show the Lipschitz bound (
3.42
), we again begin with
\mathcal{R}_{b}
and
\mathcal{R}_{c}
. Using (
3.44
), we have

(3.54)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{b}[\bm{X}_{1}]-\mathcal{R}_{b}[\bm{X}_{2}]\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg[\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}\big(\partial_{s}\left\lvert(\bm{X}_{1})_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\big)\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}\big(\partial_{s}\left\lvert(\bm{X}_{2})_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\big)\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}\big(\partial_{s}(\left\lvert(\bm{X}_{1})_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert(\bm{X}_{2})_{ss}\right\rvert^{2})(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\big)\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\bigg[\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(1+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\times

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\left\lVert\left\lvert(\bm{X}_{1})_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}+\left\lVert\left\lvert(\bm{X}_{2})_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert((\bm{X}_{1})_{ss}-(\bm{X}_{2})_{ss})\cdot((\bm{X}_{1})_{ss}+(\bm{X}_{2})_{ss})\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert(1+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{10/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{10/3})\times

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\bigg(\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2/3}\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3}(\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{1/3})\bigg]\,.

An identical bound holds for
\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{c}[\bm{X}_{1}]-\mathcal{R}_{c}[\bm{X}_{2}]\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}
by an analogous calculation.

For
\mathcal{R}_{a}
, as above, we first note the linear estimate

(3.55)

\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{a,{\rm diff}}[\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2}]\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-3/2}\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\,.

We then have

(3.56)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{a}[\bm{X}_{1}]-\mathcal{R}_{a}[\bm{X}_{2}]\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}\leq c\bigg[\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\bigg(\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert\left\lvert(\bm{X}_{1})_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle+(\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{a,{\rm diff}}[\bm{X}_{1}]\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+(1+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\bigg(\epsilon^{-1}\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{ss}\cdot(\bm{X}_{1}+\bm{X}_{2})_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}+\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{a,{\rm diff}}[\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2}]\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-3/2}(1+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{3})\bigg[\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1/3}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1}-\bm{X}_{2})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\bigg(\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{2})_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}+\left\lVert(\bm{X}_{1})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2/3}+1\bigg)\bigg]\,.

Again combining the bounds for
\mathcal{R}_{a}
,
\mathcal{R}_{b}
, and
\mathcal{R}_{c}
, we obtain the Lipschitz estimate (
3.42
).
∎

Given the decomposition of Lemma
3.3
, we consider the curve evolution (
1.14
) as

(3.57)

\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}=-T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{X}-\mathcal{R}[\bm{X}]+\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[(\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\,,\quad\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}=1\,,

where the principal linear part of the evolution is given by
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\partial_{s}^{4}\sim\epsilon^{-1}\partial_{s}^{3}
, and the tension term and
\mathcal{R}
will be treated as remainders. We will make use of some general estimates for the linear part of (
3.57
).
Given a forcing function
f\in L^{2}_{t}H^{1/2}_{s}
, we consider the following linear equation on
\mathbb{T}
:

(3.58)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=-T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\partial_{s}^{4}u+f\,,\qquad u\big|_{t=0}=u^{\rm in}\,.

Lemma 3.4

(Linear estimates)
.

For
T>0
and any
u^{\rm in}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, there exists a unique weak solution
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{7/2}_{s}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T})
to the linear equation (
3.58
) satisfying

(3.59)

\left\lVert u-u^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{s}}\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to 0^{+}\,.

In addition,
u
is continuous on
[0,T]
with values in
H^{2}_{s}
and satisfies the estimate

(3.60)

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}}+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\partial_{s}^{4}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}H^{-1/2}_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq c\big(\left\lVert u^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{s}}+(T^{1/2}+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1/2})\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1/2}_{s}}\big)

on
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

The mapping properties of the solution operator for
u
are immediate from the explicit solution formula using the Fourier multiplier behavior of Lemma
2.1
, so it remains to show the bound (
3.60
).
We begin with a bound for
\partial_{s}^{2}u
. Upon differentiating (
3.58
) in
s
, we have that
\partial_{s}^{2}u
satisfies

(3.61)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\partial_{s}^{2}u)

\displaystyle=-T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\partial_{s}^{4}(\partial_{s}^{2}u)+\partial_{s}^{2}f

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}u\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}^{2}u^{\rm in}\,.

Multiplying by
\partial_{s}^{2}u
and integrating by parts, we have that
\partial_{s}^{2}u
satisfies the energy identity

(3.62)

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\partial_{s}^{2}u)^{2}\,ds+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{s}^{4}u\,T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\partial_{s}^{4}u\,ds=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{s}^{4}uf\,ds\,,

where we have used that
\partial_{s}
commutes with
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}
. We may bound the term involving
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}
as

(3.63)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{s}^{4}u\,T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\partial_{s}^{4}u\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[u](k)\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{8}m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)

\displaystyle\geq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[u](k)\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{8}+c\,\epsilon^{-1}\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{7}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[u](k)\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\partial_{s}^{4}u\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\,.

Integrating (
3.62
) in
t
, we then obtain the bound

(3.64)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{s}^{2}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{s}}^{2}+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\partial_{s}^{4}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}H^{-1/2}_{s}}^{2}\leq c\big(\left\lVert\partial_{s}^{2}u^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{s}}^{2}+\left\lVert\partial_{s}^{4}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}H^{-1/2}_{s}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1/2}_{s}}\big)\,.

An application of Young’s inequality yields (
3.60
) with
L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{2}_{s}
in place of
L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}
.

It remains to bound the mean of
u
, which we denote by
u_{0}
. We have that
u_{0}
satisfies

(3.65)

\partial_{t}u_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\,ds\,,\qquad u_{0}\big|_{t=0}=u_{0}^{\rm in}\,,

so that

(3.66)

\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}}\leq\left\lvert u_{0}^{\rm in}\right\rvert+T^{1/2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{s}}\,.

∎

3.4.
Local well-posedness

Here we establish local-in-time well-posedness for the evolution (
1.14
), rewritten as (
3.57
).

Lemma 3.5

(Local well-posedness)
.

Given
\epsilon>0
and
\bm{X}^{\rm in}\in H^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{T})
, there exists
T>0
and a unique solution
\bm{X}\in C([0,T];H^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{2}_{t}\dot{H}^{7/2}_{s}
to (
3.57
) with initial condition
\bm{X}^{\rm in}
.

Given
T_{\rm f}>0
, if
\bm{X}\in C([0,T];H^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{2}_{t}\dot{H}^{7/2}_{s}
is a solution for all
T\in(0,T_{\rm f})
and

(3.67)

\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t}\dot{H}^{7/2}_{s}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})}<+\infty\,,

then the solution may be extended continuously in time past
T_{\rm f}
, i.e. there exists
\delta>0
such that
\bm{X}\in C([0,T_{\rm f}+\delta];H^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{2}_{t}\dot{H}^{7/2}_{s}
.

Proof.

For
T>0
, let
\Phi^{T}(\bm{X})
denote the time-
T
solution map for the evolution (
3.57
). We proceed by a fixed point argument in the space
\mathcal{Y}_{0}\cap\mathcal{Y}_{1}
, where

(3.68)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{0}(T)

\displaystyle=C([0,T];H^{2}_{s})\,,\qquad\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{1}(T)

\displaystyle=L^{2}_{t}\dot{H}^{7/2}_{s}\,,\qquad\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{7/2}_{s}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\,dt^{\prime}\bigg)^{1/2}\,.

For some
M_{0},M_{1}>0
, we consider
\bm{X}
belonging to the closed ball

(3.69)

{\bm{B}}_{0,1}=\{\bm{X}\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}(T)\cap\mathcal{Y}_{1}(T)\,:\,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}\leq M_{0}\,,\;\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\leq M_{1}\}\,,

and show that for some choice of
T
, the solution map
\Phi^{T}(\cdot)
maps
{\bm{B}}_{0,1}
to itself.

We begin with the tension terms. First, we may note the following general bounds. Using Parseval’s theorem, the form (
1.9
) of
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}
, and the multiplier bounds of Lemma
2.1
, we have that for any
\bm{f}\in H^{-1/2}(\mathbb{T})
,

(3.70)

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f})\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\infty}m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)^{2}(1+\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f}]\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{2}\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor}(1+\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f}]\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+c\,\epsilon^{-2}\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\right\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{-2}(1+\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2})^{1/2}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}[\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f}]\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\epsilon^{-2}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{2}\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{f}\right\rVert_{H^{-1/2}}^{2}\,.

Similarly,

(3.71)

3.5.
Global well-posedness

Given the local well-posedness result of Lemma

, we may proceed to the proof of Theorem (

Given the form (
2.9
) of the energy inequality satisfied by the filament and following
[

(3.84)

(3.85)

E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\,,\qquad D(t):=\left\lVert\bm{Z}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{s}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\,,

Lemma 3.6

(Control on

)
.

A curve

(3.87)

\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}_{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c\big(D(t)^{1/2}(1+E(t)^{1/2})+E(t)^{1/2}+E(t)^{4}\big)\,.

Proof.

We begin by noting that the quantities
D
and
E
control the full
H^{1/2}_{s}
norm of
\bm{Z}
in the following way. Using (
3.1
), we may calculate that the mean of
\bm{Z}
satisfies

(3.88)

\displaystyle\bm{Z}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bm{Z}\,ds=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s})\,ds=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tau\bm{X}_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big((\bm{Z}-\bm{Z}_{0})\cdot\bm{X}_{s}+\bm{Z}_{0}\cdot\bm{X}_{s}+\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\big)\bm{X}_{s}\,ds\,.

From this we obtain

(3.89)

\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\bf I}+\bm{X}_{s}\otimes\bm{X}_{s})\,ds\bigg)\bm{Z}_{0}=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big((\bm{Z}-\bm{Z}_{0})\cdot\bm{X}_{s}+\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\big)\bm{X}_{s}\,ds\,.

Using that the left hand side integrand is bounded below pointwise and that, on

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{Z}-\bm{Z}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}=\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}[{\bm{Z}}]\overline{\mathcal{F}[{\bm{Z}}]}\leq\sum_{\left\lvert k\right\rvert=1}^{\infty}\left\lvert k\right\rvert\mathcal{F}[{\bm{Z}}]\overline{\mathcal{F}[{\bm{Z}}]}\leq\left\lVert\bm{Z}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{s}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\,,

we may then estimate

(3.90)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{Z}_{0}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\big(\left\lVert\bm{Z}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{s}}+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{2}\big)\leq c(D(t)^{1/2}+E(t))\,.

We thus have

(3.91)

\left\lVert\bm{Z}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}_{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c(D(t)^{1/2}+E(t))\,.

We now proceed to estimate
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}_{s}}

as

Using (
3.91

(3.93)

\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{Z}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{s}}\leq\left\lVert\bm{Z}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}_{s}}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\otimes\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}\leq c\,(D(t)^{1/2}+E(t))(1+E(t)^{1/2})\,,

so that

(3.94)

\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{s}}\leq c\,(D(t)^{1/2}+E(t))(1+E(t)^{1/2})\,.

Next, using (

), we may write the tangential component of

\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{X}_{sss}=(\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss})\bm{X}_{s}=-\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s}\,.

To estimate this in

, we will make use of the interpolation inequalities (
3.2
) on
\mathbb{T}
to obtain

(3.96)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert(\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{1/2}\left\lVert\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert(\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{5/6}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{s}}^{1/6}\,.

Expanding

(3.97)

(\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s})_{s}=2(\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss})\bm{X}_{s}+\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{ss}\,,

we may further estimate

(3.98)

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}

In total, the right hand side of (

Using (

\displaystyle\leq c(D(t)^{1/2}+E(t))(1+E(t)^{1/2})+c\big(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{s}}^{2/3}E(t)^{4/3}+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{s}}^{1/2}E(t)^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\big(D(t)^{1/2}(1+E(t)^{1/2})+E(t)+E(t)^{4}\big)\,,

where we have used Young’s inequality to split the quantities in the second line. Using (

) as

(4.19)

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}(\tau_{Y}\bm{W}_{s})_{s}\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}(\tau_{Y}\bm{W}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert(\tau_{Y})_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{2}}^{4}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}})\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\,,

with an identical bound for the normal direction term
\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}(\tau_{Y}\bm{W}_{s})_{s}\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
.

We will additionally require a bound for the terms in
\bm{A}^{\parallel}
and
\bm{A}^{\perp}
involving the difference
\tau_{W}=\tau_{X}-\tau_{Y}
. We show the following.

Lemma 4.4

(Tension difference)
.

The difference
\tau_{W}=\tau_{X}-\tau_{Y}
between the tensions corresponding to curves
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Y}
, respectively, satisfies the bound

(4.20)

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}((\tau_{W})_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\tau_{W}\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq c\bigg(\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2}\bigg)\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2}\big)\bigg)\,,

where
\bm{W}=\bm{X}-\bm{Y}
.

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
, defining the bilinear forms

(4.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{X}(\tau_{X},\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(\bm{X})[(\tau_{X}\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\cdot(\varphi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\,ds\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{Y}(\tau_{Y},\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(({\bf I}+\bm{Y}_{s}\otimes\bm{Y}_{s})(\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}\big)\cdot(\varphi\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}\,ds\,,

we may write the difference between the weak form of the equations satisfied by
\tau_{X}
and
\tau_{Y}
as

(4.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{X}(\tau_{X},\varphi)-\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{Y}(\tau_{Y},\varphi)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(\bm{X})[\bm{X}_{ssss}]\cdot(\varphi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\big(({\bf I}+\bm{Y}_{s}\otimes\bm{Y}_{s})\bm{Y}_{ssss}\big)\cdot(\varphi\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}\bigg)\,ds}_{{\rm RHS}}\,.

The left hand side of (
4.22
) may be written as

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{X}(\tau_{X},\varphi)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{Y}(\tau_{Y},\varphi)=B^{\parallel}+B^{\perp}\,,

\displaystyle B^{\parallel}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\big(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}((\tau_{X})_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\big)\cdot(\varphi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}(\tau_{Y})_{s}\bm{Y}_{s}\cdot(\varphi\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle B^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\big(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\tau_{X}\bm{X}_{ss})\big)\cdot(\varphi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{ss}\cdot(\varphi\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}\bigg)\,ds\,.

We may further write

(4.24)

\displaystyle B^{\parallel}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\big(\bm{X}_{s}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}((\tau_{X})_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\big)-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}(\tau_{Y})_{s}\bigg)\varphi_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\big(\bm{X}_{s}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}((\tau_{W})_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\big)+\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bigg(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}\bigg)\big[(\tau_{Y})_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\big]\bigg)\varphi_{s}\,ds

as well as

(4.25)

\displaystyle B^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\tau_{X}\bm{X}_{ss})-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\tau_{Y}\left\lvert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bigg)\varphi\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\tau_{W}\bm{X}_{ss})+\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bigg(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\bigg)[\tau_{Y}\bm{X}_{ss}]

\displaystyle+\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\tau_{Y}(\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2})\bigg)\varphi\,ds\,.

We may thus rewrite the left hand side of (
4.22
) as

(4.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{X}(\tau_{X},\varphi)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{Y}(\tau_{Y},\varphi)=\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}^{X}(\tau_{W},\varphi)+{\rm LHS}\,,

\displaystyle{\rm LHS}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bigg(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}\bigg)\big[(\tau_{Y})_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\big]

\displaystyle+\varphi\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bigg(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\bigg)[\tau_{Y}\bm{X}_{ss}]+\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\tau_{Y}(\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2})\varphi\bigg)\,ds\,.

Using Lemma
4.2
and the
\tau_{Y}
bound (
4.18
), we may bound the terms appearing in LHS as

(4.27)

\displaystyle\left\lvert{\rm LHS}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert(\tau_{Y})_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\tau_{Y}\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\tau_{Y}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}(\left\lVert\bm{W}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}})\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\bm{W}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1/3}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})\times

\displaystyle\times\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{-1}T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\varphi\bm{X}_{s})\cdot\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(\left\lVert\bm{W}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1/3}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\varphi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{4/3}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\big)\,.

Here we have also exploited Lemma
4.1
to bound
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{-1}
, and have used that we may opt not to use the regularity gain from
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}
to avoid an additional factor of
\epsilon^{-1/2}
from the high wavenumber scaling of
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)
. We have further used that
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)\lesssim m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)\lesssim m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)
, so we may interchange
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}
and
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}
in the above bounds.

We next turn to the right hand side terms RHS of (
4.22
), which we may write as

(4.28)

\displaystyle{\rm RHS}

\displaystyle=R^{\parallel}+R^{\perp}\,,

\displaystyle R^{\parallel}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\bm{X}_{s}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{ssss}\bm{X}_{s})-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}\bm{Y}_{s}\cdot\bm{Y}_{ssss}\bigg)\varphi_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle R^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{X}_{ssss})-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\bm{Y}_{ssss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{ss}\bigg)\varphi\,ds\,.

Using the inextensibility identities (
3.1
), we may write

(4.29)

\displaystyle R^{\parallel}

\displaystyle=-3\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}\big((\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{W}_{sss}+\bm{W}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{sss})\bm{X}_{s}\big)\,ds

\displaystyle-3\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bigg(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}\bigg)\big[\bm{X}_{s}\bm{Y}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{sss}\big]\,ds

\displaystyle=-3\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{s}\left\lvert\bm{W}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s}+\partial_{s}(\bm{Y}_{ss}\cdot\bm{W}_{ss})\bm{X}_{s}\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bigg(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{2\pi}\bigg)\big[\partial_{s}\left\lvert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s}\big]\,ds\,.

Using Lemma
4.2
and the interpolation inequalities (
3.2
), we may estimate
R^{\parallel}
as

(4.30)

\displaystyle\left\lvert R^{\parallel}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\partial_{s}\left\lvert\bm{W}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\partial_{s}(\bm{W}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{ss})\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\bm{Y}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{sss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\left\lvert\bm{W}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\left\lvert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}(\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}})

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\varphi_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}(\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}})+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}\bigg)\,.

Here, as in the estimate (
4.27
) for LHS, we have opted to forego the regularity gain from
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}
in order to avoid an additional factor of
\epsilon^{-1/2}
from the high wavenumber behavior of
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)
. Furthermore, in the last line, we have used that

(4.31)

\left\lVert\bm{W}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}=\|T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{-1/2}T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{sss}\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1/2}\|T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{sss}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\,,

by Lemma
4.1
.

We may similarly write
R^{\perp}
as

(4.32)

\displaystyle R^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\bm{X}_{ssss}+3\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}\bm{X}_{s})-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}(\bm{Y}_{ssss}+3\bm{Y}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{sss}\bm{Y}_{s})\cdot\bm{Y}_{ss}\bigg)\varphi\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}\big(\bm{W}_{ssss}+3\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{W}_{sss}\bm{X}_{s}+3\bm{W}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{sss}\bm{X}_{s}+3\bm{Y}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{sss}\bm{W}_{s}\big)\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi\bigg(\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bigg(T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}-\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\bigg)+\frac{\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert}{4\pi}\bm{W}_{ss}\cdot\bigg)[\bm{Y}_{ssss}+3\bm{Y}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{sss}\bm{Y}_{s}]\,ds\,.

Again using Lemma
4.2
and the inequalities (
3.2
), we may estimate

(4.33)

\displaystyle\left\lvert R^{\perp}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\partial_{s}\left\lvert\bm{W}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\partial_{s}(\bm{W}_{ss}\cdot\bm{Y}_{ss})\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\partial_{s}\left\lvert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\bm{W}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+c\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)\big(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}(1+\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}})

\displaystyle+(1+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\bigg)

\displaystyle+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{-1}T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\varphi\bm{X}_{s})\cdot\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1/3}\big(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\varphi\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}(1+\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}})

\displaystyle+(1+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\bigg)

\displaystyle+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\varphi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1/3}\big(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)\,.

Here we have reused some of the estimates from the LHS bound (
4.27
) and the
R^{\parallel}
bound (
4.30
).

Altogether, taking
\varphi=\tau_{W}
in (
4.22
) and combining the bounds for LHS and RHS, we may estimate

(4.34)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{B}_{\epsilon}^{X}(\tau_{W},\tau_{W})\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\rm LHS\right\rvert+\left\lvert\rm RHS\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}((\tau_{W})_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\tau_{W}\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{4/3}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}(1+\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}})

\displaystyle+(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}((\tau_{W})_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\tau_{W}\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})

\displaystyle+(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{ss}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)\bigg)\,.

Here, to simplify, we have used that
m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)\lesssim m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}(k)\lesssim m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}(k)
, so we may interchange
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}
and
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}
. In addition, we have replaced one instance of
\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}
, and used that

(4.35)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{W}_{sss}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1/2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1/3}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1/3}\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2/3}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{1/3}

\displaystyle\leq c(\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1/2}\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}})\,.

Finally, using that

(4.36)

\bm{B}_{\epsilon}^{X}(\tau_{W},\tau_{W})=\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}((\tau_{W})_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\tau_{W}\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,,

applying Young’s inequality to (
4.34
) then yields (
4.20
).
∎

4.4.
Energy bound for curve difference

Using the results of sections
4.2
and
4.3
, we may proceed to estimate the terms
\bm{A}^{\parallel}
and
\bm{A}^{\perp}
appearing on the right hand side of the energy identity (
4.6
).

We may first apply Corollary
4.3
to estimate
\bm{A}^{\parallel}
as

(4.37)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{A}^{\parallel}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\big((\tau_{W})_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}(\tau_{Y}\bm{W}_{s})_{s}\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+c\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\big(\bm{Y}_{sss}-\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s}\big)_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{sss}-\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{3/2}}\,,

and
\bm{A}^{\perp}
as

(4.38)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{A}^{\perp}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\tau_{W}\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}(\tau_{Y}\bm{W}_{s})_{s}\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+c\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\big(\bm{Y}_{sss}-\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s}\big)_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{sss}-\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{3/2}}\,.

Together, using Lemma
4.4
and the bound (
4.19
) for the tension terms involving
\tau_{Y}
, we obtain the bound

(4.39)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{A}^{\parallel}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\bm{A}^{\perp}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)\times

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{5}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{7/2}})

\displaystyle+\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2}\bigg)\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2}\big)\bigg)\,.

Now, recalling the form of the energy identity (
4.6
) under the time rescaling (
4.3
), we have

(4.40)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\frac{\partial}{\partial\underline{t}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{W}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,ds+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{2}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{2}\leq\left\lvert\bm{A}^{\parallel}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\bm{A}^{\perp}\right\rvert\,.

Applying Young’s inequality to (
4.39
) thus yields the energy bound

(4.41)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\underline{t}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\bm{W}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,ds+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{2}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2}(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2})^{2}(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}_{s}}^{5}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{7/2}_{s}})^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}\bm{W}_{ssss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{2}+(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}_{s}}^{2})^{2}\bigg)\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}_{s}}^{2}\big)^{2}\bigg)\,.

We may define the energy and dissipation quantities

(4.42)

E_{W}(\underline{t})=\left\lVert\bm{W}_{ss}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{2}\,,\quad D_{W}(\underline{t})=\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{2}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}^{2}\,,

and note that
\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2}\lesssim E_{W}(\underline{t})
since
\bm{W}_{s}
has zero mean on
\mathbb{T}
. We further define the quantities

(4.43)

\displaystyle C_{XY1}(\underline{t})

\displaystyle=c(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2})^{2}(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}_{s}}^{5}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{7/2}_{s}})^{2}\,,

\displaystyle C_{XY2}(\underline{t})

\displaystyle=c\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}_{s}}^{2}\big)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle F_{XY}(\underline{t})

\displaystyle=c(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}_{s}}^{2})^{2}\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}_{s}}^{2}\big)^{2}\,.

By (
2.9
), we have that
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}
is uniformly bounded in time, independent of
\epsilon
. Furthermore, since
\bm{Y}\in C_{t}H^{4}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t,{\rm loc}}H^{6}_{s}
, we have
C_{XY1}\in L^{1}_{t}
while
C_{XY2}
,
F_{XY}\in L^{\infty}_{t}
with
\epsilon
-independent bounds, as the time-rescaled evolution for
\bm{Y}
no longer depends on
\epsilon
.

We may thus rewrite (
4.41
) as

(4.44)

\partial_{\underline{t}}E_{W}(\underline{t})+D_{W}(\underline{t})\leq C_{XY1}(\underline{t})E_{W}(\underline{t})+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}C_{XY2}(\underline{t})D_{W}(\underline{t})+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}F_{XY}(\underline{t})\,.

Then, given a fixed time interval
[0,T]
, for
\epsilon
sufficiently small, we may absorb
\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}C_{XY2}D_{W}(\underline{t})
into the left hand side terms to obtain

(4.45)

\partial_{\underline{t}}E_{W}(\underline{t})+\frac{1}{2}D_{W}(\underline{t})\leq C_{XY1}(\underline{t})E_{W}(\underline{t})+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}F_{XY}(\underline{t})\,,\qquad E_{W}(0)=0\,.

For every
T<\infty
, a Grönwall inequality applied to (
4.45
) then yields that there exists
C_{W}=C_{W}(T,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}},\left\lVert\bm{Y}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{4}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{6}_{s}})
independent of
\epsilon
such that

(4.46)

\displaystyle E_{W}(\underline{t})+\int_{0}^{\underline{t}}D_{W}(\underline{t}^{\prime})d\underline{t}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}\,C_{W}(T,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}},\left\lVert\bm{Y}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{4}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{6}_{s}})\,.

In particular, on any fixed time interval, the left hand side goes to zero as
\epsilon\to 0
.

It remains to control the mean of
\bm{W}
, which we denote by
\bm{W}_{0}
. Using equation (
4.2
) under the time rescaling (
4.3
), we have

(4.47)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{W}_{0}}{\partial\underline{t}}

\displaystyle=-\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(\bm{X})[(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau_{X}\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\,ds+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}({\bf I}+\bm{Y}_{s}\otimes\bm{Y}_{s})(\bm{Y}_{sss}-\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle=-\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(\bm{X})[(\bm{W}_{sss}-\tau_{W}\bm{X}_{s}-\tau_{Y}\bm{W}_{s})_{s}]\,ds}_{\bm{J}_{1}}

\displaystyle-\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}(\bm{X})-\frac{1}{4\pi}({\bf I}+\bm{Y}_{s}\otimes\bm{Y}_{s})\bigg)[(\bm{Y}_{sss}-\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}]\,ds}_{\bm{J}_{2}}\,,

with
\bm{W}_{0}\big|_{\underline{t}=0}=0
. Using equation (
4.19
) and Lemma
4.4
, we may estimate
\bm{J}_{1}
as

(4.48)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{J}_{1}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\big(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big)

\displaystyle+c\big(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}((\tau_{W})_{s}\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\tau_{W}\bm{X}_{ss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big)

\displaystyle+c\big(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}(\tau_{Y}\bm{W}_{s})_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}(\tau_{Y}\bm{W}_{s})_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}D_{W}(\underline{t})^{1/2}+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\bigg(\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\big(D_{W}(\underline{t})^{1/2}+1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2}\big)(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2})\bigg)

\displaystyle+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}(\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{2}}^{4}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{2}}\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}})

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}D_{W}(\underline{t})^{1/2}+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert E_{W}(\underline{t})^{1/2}(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{4})

\displaystyle+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2}\big)(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\big(cD_{W}(\underline{t})^{1/2}+C(T,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}},\left\lVert\bm{Y}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{4}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{6}_{s}})\big)\,,

using (
4.46
).
Furthermore, using Corollary
4.3
, we may bound
\bm{J}_{2}
as

(4.49)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{J}_{2}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\bm{X}_{s}\otimes\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}({\bf I}-\bm{X}_{s}\otimes\bm{X}_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}\bigg)\times

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}(\bm{Y}_{sss}-\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}+\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}^{\perp}(\bm{Y}_{sss}-\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{s}}

\displaystyle+\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\bm{W}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2})\left\lVert(\bm{Y}_{sss}-\tau_{Y}\bm{Y}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1/2}_{s}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}\big(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}_{s}}^{3}\big)

\displaystyle+c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert E_{W}^{1/2}(\underline{t})(1+\left\lVert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{s}}^{2})(1+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{3}_{s}}^{5}+\left\lVert\bm{Y}_{s}\right\rVert_{H^{7/2}_{s}})

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{1/2}C(T,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}},\left\lVert\bm{Y}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{4}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{6}_{s}})\,,

again by (
4.46
).
In total, we have that
\bm{W}_{0}
satisfies

(4.50)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\underline{t}}\left\lvert\bm{W}_{0}\right\rvert^{2}\leq\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}\big(cD_{W}(\underline{t})+C(T,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}},\left\lVert\bm{Y}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{4}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{6}_{s}})\big)\,,

with
\left\lvert\bm{W}_{0}\right\rvert^{2}\big|_{\underline{t}=0}=0
. Integrating in time from 0 to
T
, we obtain

(4.51)

\left\lvert\bm{W}_{0}\right\rvert^{2}\leq\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}C(T,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}},\left\lVert\bm{Y}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{4}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{6}_{s}})\,.

Combining the bounds for
E_{W}
,
D_{W}
, and
\left\lvert\bm{W}_{0}\right\rvert^{2}
, we obtain

(4.52)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{W}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{s}}+\left\lVert T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{P}_{\bm{X}_{s}}\bm{W}_{ssss})\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1/2}C(T,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{2}_{s}},\left\lVert\bm{Y}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{4}_{s}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{6}_{s}})\,.

Using the lower bound of Lemma
2.1
for
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm t}}
and
T_{m_{\epsilon}^{\rm n}}
yields Theorem
1.3
.

\square
