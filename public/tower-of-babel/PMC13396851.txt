Regarding “Determining Physical Function Measures for Estimating VO2Peak in Patients on Hemodialysis” by Groningen et al.
Authors: Rodrigo Torres-Castro
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

Following the recent publication by Groningen et al,1 I would like to offer a necessary conceptual clarification regarding the distinction between physical capacity and physical function, terms that are occasionally used interchangeably despite representing divergent physiological constructs.

Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) is, by definition, a test of maximal physical capacity. Its objective is to reach the individual's physiological ceiling (the VO2peak), evaluating systemic integration under aerobic metabolic stress.2 In contrast, most clinical tests assess physical function. For instance, the sit-to-stand test primarily measures lower-limb strength and power, whereas the 6-minute walk test—the only other capacity measure used—is fundamentally submaximal in most clinical populations.3,4

The authors report a correlation between CPET results and the 30-second sit-to-stand. However, this finding should be interpreted with extreme caution and viewed as nearly anecdotal. It is highly risky to use strength-based or submaximal performance tests to estimate VO2peak. These tests rely on different metabolic pathways and are often limited by peripheral factors (such as balance or local muscle fatigue) that do not accurately reflect central cardiopulmonary reserve.

Equating these 2 domains may lead to a dangerous misinterpretation of a patient’s cardiovascular risk. Clinical proficiency in functional tasks does not guarantee an adequate metabolic ceiling. Practitioners should be cautious when extrapolating functional performance to the realm of maximal aerobic capacity.
