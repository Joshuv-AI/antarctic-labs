Survey on the Current Status of COPD Diagnosis and Management Capabilities Among Primary Care Physicians in Pudong New Area, Shanghai
Authors: Li Ling, Ying Huang, Yan Li, Hao Huang, Xin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo assess chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)-related clinical competency, confidence, and management practices among general practitioners (GPs) in Pudong community health centers.MethodsA cross-sectional survey was conducted. Stratified random sampling across four regional strata yielded 457 valid responses (99.34% response rate).ResultsWhile 56.02% of GPs managed 11–30 COPD patients monthly, 96.06% reported COPD constituted <30% of their caseload. Clinical engagement was suboptimal: 80.74% performed spirometry ≤10 times annually, and 45.73% referred cough/dyspnea patients directly to specialists. Significant competency gaps were identified in inhaled pharmacotherapy, classification, CAT/mMRC usage, spirometry interpretation, and inhaler instruction. Confidence in management was low (mean score: 2.85/5). Regression analysis revealed that proficiency correlated with standardized residency training, COPD-specific continuing medical education (CME) attendance, and physician age. Both competency and confidence levels correlated positively with the frequency of management practices.ConclusionGPs in Pudong exhibit inadequate COPD knowledge depth, poor clinical application skills, and low decision-making confidence. These findings highlight the need for training programs integrating theoretical instruction with hands-on practice to support improvements in competency and promote standardized community-based COPD management.Keywords: community healthcare center, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, clinical competency

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a condition characterized by airflow limitation. According to the 2021 Global Burden of Disease study, COPD has become one of the top four causes of death worldwide, imposing a significant economic burden on healthcare systems.1 According to the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) 2024 report, over 390 million people globally live with COPD, yet the disease is frequently underdiagnosed, particularly in primary care settings where early symptoms are often overlooked.2 In China, COPD has also become a major public health issue severely threatening residents’ health. The 2024 update to China’s Basic Public Health Service Content added health services for COPD patients, laying a solid foundation for preventing and responding to common and emerging respiratory infectious diseases.3 In recent years, numerous studies have focused on the availability of cough and asthma diagnosis and treatment equipment and medications in Shanghai’s community health service centers, finding that the current situation is relatively well-established.4,5 However, recent data reveal persistent disparities between urban and suburban Shanghai, where a significant gap exists between the high availability of basic diagnostic equipment (>90%) and the suboptimal clinical competency of General Practitioners (eg, <16% proficiency in glucocorticoid application).6 Against this backdrop, this paper aims to survey GPs at community health centers in Pudong New Area, Shanghai, to understand their current capabilities and confidence in diagnosing and treating COPD. This will provide further evidence for implementing COPD prevention and control measures in the community.

Subjects and Methods

Ethical Statement and Informed Consent

This study was determined to be exempt from formal ethical review by the Editorial Office of Journal of Multidisciplinary Healthcare and the authors’ affiliated institution, in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, due to the anonymous nature of the survey and the minimal risk involved. All participants provided implied informed consent by voluntarily accessing and completing the online questionnaire hosted on Wjx.cn. The introductory page of the online survey clearly outlined the study’s purpose, voluntary participation, and data anonymity. Participants proceeded to the questionnaire only after indicating their agreement. The data were analyzed anonymously in aggregate form.

Study Population

The study population comprised general practitioners (GPs) in Pudong New Area. A stratified random sampling method was employed based on the defined sampling frame. The sampling frame consisted of all licensed General Practitioners registered at the 48 community health service centers in Pudong New Area. Community health service centers were first stratified by region (remote rural, general rural, urban-rural fringe, urban). From each stratum, 115 physicians were randomly selected to complete a questionnaire assessing their COPD diagnosis and treatment capabilities. Valid questionnaires were returned from 457 general practitioners (457/460), yielding a response rate of 99.34%.

Survey Methodology and Content

This survey was conducted from March 25 to May 20, 2025. The questionnaire design was based on the “Chinese Guidelines for Primary Care Diagnosis, Treatment, and Management of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (2024)”7 and the “Evaluation Criteria for Demonstration Projects to Enhance Standardized Diagnosis and Treatment Capabilities for Cough and Dyspnea in Primary Healthcare Institutions (Trial)”.8 It was finalized after pilot testing, incorporating practical considerations and expert feedback. To evaluate the reliability of the questionnaire, a pilot test was conducted with 30 general practitioners not included in the main study. The internal consistency was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient. The overall Cronbach’s alpha for the questionnaire was 0.72. Content validity was established through a review process involving three senior respiratory experts who confirmed that the questionnaire items adequately covered the domains of COPD diagnosis, treatment, and management.

The questionnaire comprises 37 items covering: (1) GP basic information including age, gender, professional title, and COPD training history; (2) COPD diagnosis and treatment implementation status; (3) COPD diagnosis, treatment, and management capabilities, including clinical diagnosis, staging and grouping assessment, questionnaire application skills, inhaled medication administration methods, acute exacerbation management, and follow-up care; (4) Self-assessment of clinical confidence. Clinical confidence was measured using a 5-point Likert scale: “Not confident”, “Somewhat not confident”, “Neutral”, “Somewhat confident”, and “Confident”, scored numerically from 1 (least confident) to 5 (most confident). The content involved GPs self-assessing their confidence in disease diagnosis, disease grouping, questionnaire application, acute exacerbation management, drug therapy, interpretation of pulmonary function reports, inhaler device guidance, and follow-up management. The questionnaire was distributed via Wjx.cn, with surveyed GPs completing and submitting it independently via mobile devices. Responses took less than 8 minutes; questionnaires with missing items were deemed invalid.

Statistical Methods Sample Size Estimation

A priori power analysis was performed using G*Power software (version 3.1.9.7, Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf, Germany). Assuming a medium effect size (f2 = 0.15), α = 0.05, and power (1-β) = 0.80, the minimum required sample size was calculated to be approximately 400 participants. Our final sample of 457 exceeded this requirement, ensuring adequate statistical power. SPSS 26.0 software was used for statistical analysis. Count data were expressed as frequency (n) and percentage (%). Measurement data were presented as mean ± standard deviation ( ± s). Logistic regression analysis was applied to examine the impact of basic characteristics and clinical practices on primary care physicians’ COPD management capabilities. Pearson correlation analysis assessed the relationship between clinical practices and diagnostic confidence. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Basic Information of General Practitioners

Among the 46 community health service centers in Pudong New Area, a total of 457 community general practitioners participated in the survey. Their included 339 females (74.2%) and 118 males (25.8%). Age distribution: 24 (5.25%) aged 20–30, 185 (40.48%) aged 31–40, 166 (36.32%) aged 41–50, and 82 (17.94%) aged 50+. Educational attainment included 15 participants (3.28%) with associate degrees, 353 (77.24%) with bachelor’s degrees, and 89 (19.47%) with master’s degrees or higher. Professional titles comprised 26 participants (5.69%) at the junior level, 319 (69.8%) at the intermediate level, and 112 (24.5%) at the associate senior level or above.

255 (55.8%) had completed standardized training as general practitioners, with 36 (7.88%) specializing in respiratory diseases. Among them, 109 (23.85%) had never received COPD continuing education training within the past year, while 348 (76.15%) had received it one or more times. All participants practiced in general medicine or traditional Chinese medicine general practice.

COPD Diagnosis and Treatment Practices and Related Examination Implementation

Among surveyed physicians, the highest proportion (56.02%) saw 11–30 COPD patients monthly, and the highest proportion (96.06%) reported COPD patients accounting for less than 30% of their total monthly caseload. Relationship Between Diagnostic Knowledge, Capability, and Diagnostic Practice

Binary logistic regression analysis revealed that physicians who saw a higher proportion of COPD patients and chose to manage cases independently demonstrated greater proficiency in diagnosing and staging COPD, with OR values of 1.38 (95% CI 1.16, 1.93) and 1.70 (95% CI 1.54, 1.91), respectively. Physicians who independently managed patients with acute exacerbations of COPD demonstrated greater proficiency in treating acute exacerbations, with an OR of 1.04 (95% CI 1.02, 1.09). Physicians who ordered more pulmonary function tests, independently saw COPD patients, and independently managed acute exacerbations demonstrated greater accuracy in interpreting pulmonary function reports, with ORs of 1.57 (95% CI 1.10, 2.24), 1.61 (95% CI 1.47, 1.79), and 1.53 (95% CI 1.34, 1.82), respectively. Physicians who performed more spirometry tests and chest imaging examinations, and who chose to independently manage COPD patients and acute exacerbation patients, demonstrated stronger inhalation device guidance capabilities, with OR values of 1.60 (95% CI 1.10, 2.33), 1.39 (95% CI 1.05, 1.85), 1.52 (95% CI 1.39, 1.68), and 1.52 (95% CI 1.27, 1.69), respectively. Physicians who independently selected patients demonstrated higher proficiency in follow-up management, with an OR of 1.53 (95% CI 1.34, 1.82). Discussion

As health gatekeepers, community general practitioners bear the responsibility of screening high-risk populations for COPD, diagnosing and managing stable COPD patients, and managing patients with mild acute exacerbations. Recent studies have revealed concerning levels of COPD knowledge among primary care physicians in China.9–11 Enhancing the diagnostic and management capabilities of community general practitioners regarding COPD has thus become a critical issue.

Distinct from previous descriptive studies, this study provides a high-fidelity assessment of the COPD management landscape in Pudong New Area—a benchmark region representing China’s most advanced urban healthcare system. Our findings reveal a paradoxical “Competence-Confidence Gap” among GPs in this resource-rich setting, where low proficiency in key clinical tasks co-exists with inadequate decision-making confidence. Crucially, through logistic regression analysis, we identify that modifiable factors related to daily clinical exposure and the quality of continuing education—rather than mere static knowledge deficits—are the primary drivers limiting effective COPD care. This shifts the focus from simple “knowledge dissemination” to the optimization of clinical workflow and training design.

In the preliminary phase of this study, we conducted an initial survey of the diagnostic and treatment equipment available at community health service centers across Pudong New Area, as well as the clinical capabilities of general practitioners. The findings revealed that while the coverage of COPD diagnostic and treatment equipment and medications was high across these facilities, there were still gaps in key medications, and there was a lack of proficiency in their use. Community general practitioners demonstrated some understanding of COPD diagnosis and treatment, but their knowledge was not sufficiently in-depth, their clinical application skills were inadequate, and they lacked confidence in managing patients.12 Building on this foundation, this paper conducts an in-depth analysis of general practitioners’ COPD diagnostic and treatment practices, knowledge, and confidence in Pudong New Area, and explores the relationships among these factors. The results indicate that general practitioners have insufficient knowledge and skills regarding COPD diagnosis and treatment, lack confidence in their clinical practice, and exhibit inadequate implementation of diagnostic and treatment protocols, with these factors mutually influencing one another.

Regarding diagnostic knowledge, the majority of general practitioners in Pudong New Area surveyed have mastered the diagnostic criteria, grading methods, and management of acute exacerbations for COPD. A minority of physicians (23.19%) could correctly apply the mMRC questionnaire and CAT questionnaire, though this rate exceeded the questionnaire utilization capability among Shanghai community GPs (12.34%).13 Only 17.07% of physicians could select the appropriate inhaled bronchodilator based on patient grouping. This indicates that while general practitioners are familiar with COPD fundamentals and emergency care, they lack in-depth understanding of COPD patient stratification and treatment.

It is crucial to understand that these deficiencies are not merely attributable to individual physician shortcomings, but are deeply rooted in the structural context of primary care in Shanghai. While international best practices advocate for a Multidisciplinary Team (MDT) approach involving nurses, physiotherapists, and technicians, the operational reality in Pudong New Area remains fundamentally GP-centric. In this system, General Practitioners serve as the sole gatekeepers; although spirometry technicians and nurses provide supportive functions, the GP retains exclusive responsibility for diagnosis, pharmacotherapy decisions, and referral. Consequently, the observed “limited engagement” reflects not an over-assignment of responsibility, but the structural necessity that the GP is the responsible clinician in the community setting. Furthermore, our logistic regression analysis suggests that the lack of in-depth knowledge stems from systemic barriers, specifically insufficient opportunities for independent patient consultation and a lack of high-quality, practical training.

Numerous Chinese studies have identified deficiencies in GPs’ COPD diagnostic knowledge and treatment capabilities.14,15 An Indian study similarly found that GPs prioritized immediate relief of acute symptoms but were unfamiliar with guideline-recommended stratification and treatment protocols.16 This may represent a common challenge among primary care GPs, whose role is often positioned as “stable patient management” with a primary focus on reducing acute exacerbations. Complex drug therapies are frequently referred to higher-level hospitals, further weakening primary care physicians’ medication decision-making capabilities.

Regarding clinical competence, this survey indicates that 58.21% of physicians can correctly instruct patients on inhaler device use, 54.49% can accurately interpret pulmonary function reports, and 84.02% can conduct standardized follow-ups for COPD patients. However, 45.73% of GPs directly refer cough/wheeze patients to respiratory specialists during outpatient visits, regardless of whether referral criteria are met. This indicates deficiencies in GPs’ COPD management capabilities, partly related to insufficient clinical confidence (2.85±1.41 points). Several domestic and international studies have found that GPs lack continuity and consistency in managing COPD patients and providing information, failing to deliver comprehensive and efficient healthcare services.17,18 This may be one reason for the deficiency in physicians’ diagnostic and treatment capabilities and confidence. How to enhance GPs’ comprehensive understanding of COPD guidelines, improve their diagnostic and treatment capabilities, and strengthen their confidence in diagnosis and treatment requires focused attention.

This study employed logistic regression analysis to explore factors influencing general practitioners’ knowledge, competence, and confidence in COPD management, identifying two key determinants. First, improvements in GPs’ COPD management competence and confidence correlate with clinical practice patterns. When physicians see a higher proportion of COPD patients and opt for independent consultations, their mastery of COPD diagnostic criteria and staging/grouping significantly increases. Independent consultations compel physicians to comprehensively lead clinical decision-making—including history-taking, differential diagnosis, and staging/grouping assessments—thereby deepening mastery of core knowledge such as GOLD staging criteria and spirometry interpretation. Physicians independently managing COPD exacerbation cases demonstrate greater proficiency in acute treatment principles. When confronting acute patients, clinicians must rapidly integrate multisystem issues and formulate treatment plans. This high-pressure practice setting significantly enhances clinical decision-making confidence. Research indicates that physicians frequently managing acute exacerbations are more likely to adhere to guidelines, exhibit greater confidence in treating such patients, and demonstrate fewer treatment deviations.19 This survey also reveals a positive correlation between the frequency of ordering pulmonary function and imaging tests and proficiency in interpreting pulmonary function reports. Physicians who order these tests more frequently gain familiarity through repeated practice, leading to more accurate interpretation of pulmonary function results and CT features such as emphysema. Similarly, proficiency in inhaler instruction correlates with both examination frequency and independent patient management, as device operation requires hands-on teaching. Accumulated experience enhances communication skills and error correction abilities. Physicians managing patients independently also gain deeper familiarity with disease progression trajectories and better grasp follow-up content and timing. This survey reveals that practical experience is the core driver of clinical competence and confidence. Clinical practice experience significantly shapes diagnostic and treatment capabilities and confidence.20 Although Pudong New Area currently has adequate COPD diagnostic equipment and medication resources, the annual number of COPD patients seen by general practitioners and the frequency of pulmonary function tests ordered remain low. This limits further improvement in physicians’ diagnostic capabilities and confidence. Clinical competence represents cognitive mastery, while clinical confidence reflects physicians’ self-assessment of ability and decision-making willingness. Together, they form the dual pillars of clinical quality. Clinical confidence facilitates greater accumulation of practical experience—such as independently managing patients and conducting relevant tests—further enhancing clinical competence. These three elements exert synergistic effects.

Second, this survey found that physicians with more training sessions demonstrated greater proficiency in correctly diagnosing and staging COPD, interpreting pulmonary function test results, and identifying referral criteria. They also exhibited greater familiarity with inhaler device usage. This suggests that enhancing clinical competence and confidence relies on high-quality training.

By comparing the current state of primary care physicians’ medical service capabilities across different regions and between urban and rural areas in China, Lian Lu found it was concluded that improved training can provide primary care physicians with greater support in diagnosis and treatment management.21 This survey found that 76.15% of general practitioners had participated in COPD training within the past year, yet overall diagnostic and treatment capabilities and confidence remained inadequate. This indicates that enhancing COPD diagnosis and treatment must be deeply integrated with both the “quantity” and “quality” of clinical training. Issues such as inconsistent training methods, overly complex and lengthy curricula, and a lack of practical content are currently prevalent in continuing education training systems.22,23 Such training not only lacks quality but may even lead to a decline in physicians’ understanding of the disease. Training design in healthcare institutions must integrate theory with practice to deepen GPs’ understanding of COPD. This includes establishing tiered practice pathways, accelerating skill transfer through clinical scenario-based modules, and reinforcing acquired knowledge via quality control feedback to achieve synergistic enhancement of diagnostic competence and clinical confidence.24 Optimizing the methods and content of continuing medical education for GPs, along with standardizing training quality, may help elevate the clinical proficiency of the majority of physicians.

Summary

This survey reveals that despite Pudong New Area’s emphasis on COPD diagnosis and management, the current status of community-based COPD care remains suboptimal. Community general practitioners exhibit significant deficiencies in COPD knowledge, clinical competence, and confidence. Notably, while 76.15% of physicians reported receiving COPD-related training within the past year, substantial competency gaps persist, suggesting that current continuing medical education (CME) programs may lack quality or practical relevance. Therefore, simply increasing the quantity of training is insufficient; there is an urgent need to optimize the design of CME programs by shifting towards standardized, high-quality curricula that emphasize practical, hands-on components. Such reforms are essential to translate theoretical knowledge into clinical competence, thereby enhancing physicians’ diagnostic capabilities and confidence, and ultimately improving the quality of COPD care in the community.

Limitations of this study include its reliance on a single regional sample and a lack of in-depth analysis regarding specific barriers to practice. Future research should expand sample sizes and refine survey content to better enhance primary care hospitals’ capacity for diagnosing, treating, and managing COPD.
