Profinite rigidity for two-bridge links and 3-tangle Montesinos links
Tamunonye Cheetham-West, Xiaoyu Xu

Abstract
. For any two-bridge link or 3-tangle Montesinos link L ⊂ S 3 L\subset S^{3} (including knot), this paper proves that π 1 ​ ( S 3 − L ) \pi_{1}(S^{3}-L) is profinitely rigid among the fundamental groups of compact orientable 3-manifolds.



1.
Introduction

For a compact 3-manifold
M
(possibly with boundary), finite quotients of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M)
are useful for distinguishing
M
from other 3-manifolds in practice. This idea can be well illustrated by its application to knot complements. For example, when
M
the exterior of a non-trivial knot
K
in
S^{3}
, it is known by
[
Kup14
]
that this non-triviality can be certified by finding a non-abelian finite cover of
M
whose degree depends only on the crossing number of
K
. According to the results of Whitten
[
Whi87
]
and Gordon–Luecke
[
GL89
]
, two prime knots
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
in
S^{3}
with
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-K_{1})\cong\pi_{1}(S^{3}-K_{2})
are equivalent up to isotopy and mirroring. A further question, stated by Boileau and Friedl
[
BF20
, Question 1.3]
, is whether a prime knot
K
in
S^{3}
is determined, up to isotopy and mirroring, by the set of finite quotients of
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-K)
.

For a finitely generated group
\Gamma
, let
\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)
denote the set of finite quotient groups of
\Gamma
. Two finitely generated groups
\Gamma
and
\Delta
are said to be
profinitely equivalent
if
\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
. This is equivalent to say that
\Gamma
and
\Delta
have isomorphic profinite completions
\widehat{\Gamma}\cong\widehat{\Delta}

[
DFPR82
]
. Given a collection
\mathscr{A}
of finitely generated groups, a group
\Gamma\in\mathscr{A}
is said to be
profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{A}
if any group
\Delta\in\mathscr{A}
that is profinitely equivalent to
\Gamma
is isomorphic with
\Gamma
.

Throughout this paper, let
\mathscr{M}
denote the class of fundamental groups of compact orientable 3-manifolds. In this paper, we give a positive answer to a class of examples for Boileau–Friedl’s question that also generalizes for links in
S^{3}
. In particular, we prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any two-bridge link (including knot)
L\subseteq S^{3}
,
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-L)
is profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{M}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any Montesinos link (including knot)
L\subseteq S^{3}
with 3 rational tangles,
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-L)
is profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{M}
.

We remark that two-bridge links are exactly Montesinos links with no more than 2 rational tangles. However, the results are listed separately since they involve slightly different technical details.
Theorem 1.1
is proven as
Theorem 5.6
and
Theorem 1.2
is proven as a combination of
Theorem 6.13
and
Theorem 6.15
.

A significance of profinite rigidity is its relation to the isomorphism problem.
Indeed, if a group
\Gamma
is profinitely rigid within a class
\mathscr{A}
of finitely presented groups, then there exists an algorithm that, given a finite presentation of any
\Delta\in\mathscr{A}
, decides in finite time whether
\Gamma
and
\Delta
are isomorphic; see
[
BCR16
, Page 5]
. This proceeds by simultaneously searching either for a finite group belonging to
\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)\vartriangle\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
– which terminates in finite time if
\Gamma
and
\Delta
are not profinitely equivalent, or for an explicit isomorphism between
\Gamma
and
\Delta
– which terminates in finite time as long as they are isomorphic.
In particular, the groups
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-L)
as appearing in
Theorem 1.1
and
Theorem 1.2
have solvable isomorphism problem in
\mathscr{M}
. This gives a new solution to the recognition problem of two-bridge knots and 3-tangle Montesinos knots, which is different from
[
Hem93
]
based on the theory of normal surfaces.

Corollary 1.3
.

Fix
K\subseteq S^{3}
to be a two-bridge knot or a Montesinos knot with 3 rational tangles. There exists an algorithm that inputs a polygonal knot digram
D
and determines whether the diagram
D
represents the knot
K
.

Proof.

Let
K^{\prime}
be the knot represented by the digram
D
. One can obtain a finite presentation of
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-K^{\prime})
via the Wirtinger presentation. Then one apply the preceding algorithm to determine whether
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-K^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-K)
. If they are not isomorphic, then clearly
D
does not represent the knot
K
. If they are isomorphic, since
K
is a prime knot,
[
Whi87
]
and
[
GL89
]
imply that
D
represents either
K
or its mirror image. One can fix knot diagrams for
K
and its mirror image, and through a finite search of Reidemeister moves, one can certify that
D
represents
K
or that
D
represents the mirror image of
K
. If
D
represents
K
, then we are done. Otherwise, if
D
represents the mirror image of
K
, then
D
represents
K
if and only if
K
is isotopic to its mirror image, and this can be verified through the complete classification for these families of knots (
Theorem 5.1
,
Proposition 6.1
).
∎

As a historical remark,
Theorem 1.1
and
Theorem 1.2
cover some earlier results of knots and links in
S^{3}
whose complements have fundamental groups that are profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{M}
. These include the figure-eight knot by Bridson-Reid
[
BR20
]
via a distinct approach, and a range of examples by the second author
[
Xu24
]
such as the Whitehead link, some families of Pretzel links including
(-2,3,7)
and
(-2,3,8)
, (full) twist knots, and
\mathbf{b}(10,3)
etc. More results on profinite rigidity of knot groups can be found at
[
Wil19
,
BF20
,
CW23
]
.

The proofs for
Theorem 1.1
and
Theorem 1.2
are motivated by the observation that the links involved are characterized, up to isotopy and mirror image, by the homeomorphism type of their two-fold branched covers. We briefly outline here the strategy for
Theorem 1.1
. Consider as an example that
L\subseteq S^{3}
is a hyperbolic two-bridge link, and
N
is a compact orientable 3-manifold with
\pi_{1}(N)
profinitely equivalent with
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-L)
.
[
Xu24
, Theorem 4.1]
implies that
N
is the exterior of a hyperbolic link
L^{\prime}\subseteq S^{3}
. The
\pi
-orbifold group of
L
is a finite dihedral group which appears as a finite quotient of
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-L)
. Thus, this finite dihedral group also appears as a finite quotient of
\pi_{1}(N)
. A careful application of
[
Xu24
]
implies that this finite dihedral quotient of
\pi_{1}(N)
also arises from the
\pi
-orbifold group of
L^{\prime}
. Then, a classification theorem (e.g.
[
BZ89
, Proposition 3.2]
) can be applied to show that
L^{\prime}
is also a two-bridge link. However, there exist non-equivalent two-bridge links with isomorphic
\pi
-orbifold groups. To eliminate this possibility, we apply a theorem of Burde
[
Bur75
]
that distinguishes these two-bridge knots via linking number invariants in a finite-sheeted great circle link cover of the link complement corresponding to the kernel of this finite-dihedral quotient.

Although they are not directly applicable to the proofs of
Theorem 1.1
and
Theorem 1.2
, we present here two further criteria for the profinite rigidity of knot or link groups in
\mathscr{M}
that follow the same spirit.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
L
is a hyperbolic link in
S^{3}
which is not a two-bridge link. If the
\pi
-orbifold group of
L
is profinitely rigid among all
\pi
-orbifold groups of hyperbolic links in
S^{3}
, then
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-L)
is profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{M}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
K
be a hyperbolic knot in
S^{3}
. Suppose that for infinitely many integers
r\geq 2
, the
r
-fold cyclic cover of
S^{3}
branched along
K
has its fundamental group profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{M}
. Then,
\pi_{1}(S^{3}-K)
is also profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{M}
.

Theorem 1.4
and
Theorem 1.5
will be proven in Section
7
.

2.
Preliminaries.

2.1.
Profinite completion

In addition to the materials covered in this section, we refer the readers to
[
RZ10
]
for a standard reference on profinite groups.

A
profinite group
is an inverse limit of finite groups indexed over a directed partially ordered set, equipped with the subspace topology inherited from the product
topology.
The
profinite completion
of an abstract group
\Gamma
is defined as a profinite group

\widehat{\Gamma}=\varprojlim_{N\in\mathcal{N}}\Gamma/N

where
\mathcal{N}
is the collection of all finite-index normal subgroups of
\Gamma
indexed by reverse inclusion.

There is a canonical homomorphism
i_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\to\widehat{\Gamma}
with dense image sending every element
\gamma\in\Gamma
to a tuple
(\pi_{N}(\gamma))_{N\in\mathcal{N}}\in\widehat{\Gamma}
where
\pi_{N}:\Gamma\to\Gamma/N
is the quotient map.
It is clear that
i_{\Gamma}
is injective if and only if
\Gamma
is residually finite. For ease of notation, when
\Gamma
is residually finite, we always identify
\Gamma
as its image in
\widehat{\Gamma}
, and elements in
\Gamma

are also viewed as elements in
\widehat{\Gamma}
.

By a
homomorphism of profinite groups

\Phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
, we always mean a continuous homomorphism. However, a deep theorem of Nikolov–Segal
[
NS07
]
implies that any abstract homomorphism from a topologically finitely generated profinite group to a profinite group is continuous. Thus, we shall not stress upon the continuity when
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are topologically finitely generated, especially when they are the profinite completions of finitely generated groups.

The profinite completion encodes the full data of finite quotients.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
DFPR82
]
)
.

For two finitely generated abstract groups
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
,
\mathcal{C}(\Gamma_{1})=\mathcal{C}(\Gamma_{2})
if and only if
\widehat{\Gamma_{1}}\cong\widehat{\Gamma_{2}}
.

In addition to the set of finite quotients, the profinite completion of an abstract group also encodes its lattice of finite-index subgroups.

(
[
RZ10
, Proposition 3.2.2]
)
.

For an abstract group
\Gamma
, there is an isomorphism between the lattice of finite-index subgroups of
\Gamma
and the lattice of open subgroups in
\widehat{\Gamma}
given
as follows.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{i_{\Gamma}(H)}\cong\widehat{H}}

In addition, this correspondence sends normal subgroups to normal subgroups.

.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
be abstract groups. Given an isomorphism
\Phi:\widehat{\Gamma_{1}}\to\widehat{\Gamma_{2}}
, we say that two finite-index subgroups

. This resulting map is defined as

. It is clear that
\hat{\phi}\circ i_{\Gamma_{1}}=i_{\Gamma_{2}}\circ\phi
.

2.2.
Profinite completions of 3-manifold groups

For
M
a compact 3-manifold,
\pi_{1}(M)
is residually finite. This follows by combining a theorem of Hempel
[
Hem16
]
with Agol’s positive resolution to Thurston’s Virtual Haken Conjecture
[
Ago13
]
. As such, 3-manifolds have an abundance of finite covers. Since we are only focusing on the fundamental groups, we always assume that compact 3-manifolds stated in this paper do not have boundary spheres.

In their influential papers, Wilton–Zalesskii showed that profinite completions of 3-manifold groups detect geometric decompositions. The following theorem serves as a brief conclusion of their results, see also
[
Wil18
]
for generalizations to 3-manifolds with boundary.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
WZ17a
,
WZ17b
,
WZ19
]
)
.

For a compact orientable 3-manifold
M
with empty or toral boundary, the isomorphism type of
\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M)
determines whether
M
is geometric in the sense of Thurston. It determines the geometry type when
M
is geometric, and determines the prime decomposition and the JSJ-decomposition when
M
is non-geometric.

Profinite rigidity for seven of the eight Thurston geometries is well understood, see
[
Fun13
]
for
Sol
geometry and
[
Hem14
,
Wil17
]
for Seifert fibered spaces. The question of profinite rigidity for hyperbolic 3-manifold groups remains.

2.3.
Cohomology of profinite groups

The standard reference for cohomology theory of profinite groups is
[
RZ10
, Chapter 6]
. A complete review of profinite cohomology theory is beyond the scope of this article, so we will only present a brief introduction here.

For a profinite group
G
, the profinite cohomology of
G
– denoted by
\mathbf{H}^{\ast}(G;-)
– is the continuous cohomology of
G
usually defined for coefficients in discrete
G
-modules, and the profinite homology of
G
– denoted by
\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(G;-)
– is the continuous homology of
G
usually defined for coefficients in profinite
G
-modules, where the modules are equipped with continuous
G
-actions. One may take the following properties as equivalent definitions for profinite (co)homology:

(1)

for any discrete
G
-module
A
,
\mathbf{H}^{k}(G;A)=\varinjlim_{U}H^{k}(G/U;\mathrm{Fix}_{U}A)
,

(2)

for any profinite
G
-module
B
,
\mathbf{H}_{k}(G;B)=\varprojlim_{U}H_{k}(G/U;\mathrm{Cofix}_{U}B)
,

where
U
ranges through all open normal subgroups in
G
in both statements, see
[
RZ10
, Corollary 6.5.6 and Corollary 6.5.8]
.

Let
\Phi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be a homomorphism of profinite groups. Then,
\Phi
induces homomorphisms
\Phi^{\ast}:\mathbf{H}^{\ast}(G_{2};A)\to\mathbf{H}^{\ast}(G_{1};A)
for every discrete
G_{2}
-module
A
, and
\Phi_{\ast}:\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(G_{1};B)\to\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(G_{2};B)
for every profinite
G_{2}
-module
B
.

Given a homomorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to G
from an abstract group
\Gamma
to a profinite group
G
,
\phi
also induces a homomorphism
\phi^{\ast}:\mathbf{H}^{\ast}(G;A)\to H^{\ast}(\Gamma;A)
for every discrete
G
-module
A
. We also note that any finite discrete
\Gamma
-module is naturally a
\widehat{\Gamma}
-module.

Definition 2.5
.

An abstract group
\Gamma
is
cohomologically good
if for every finite
\Gamma
-module
M
, the homomorphism
i_{\Gamma}^{\ast}:\mathbf{H}^{\ast}(\widehat{\Gamma};M)\to H^{\ast}(\Gamma;M)
induced by the canonical homomorphism is an isomorphism.

The concept of cohomological goodness was first introduced by Serre in
[
Ser01
]
. In this paper, we mainly apply this concept to virtually surface groups.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
GJZZ08
, Lemma 3.2 and Proposition 3.6]
)
.

Virtually surface groups are cohomologically good.

4.
Link complements

Fix an orientation of the ambient space
S^{3}
throughout this paper.
By a link
L
in
S^{3}
, we always mean a tame link. We denote by
n(L)
the open regular neighborhood of
L
, and by
N(L)
the closure of
n(L)
. Finally,
E_{L}=S^{3}\setminus n(L)
denotes the compact exterior of
L
.

A link
L
is
component-ordered
if we fix an ordering of its components as
L=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}
. For a component-ordered link
L=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}
, the
linking matrix

\mathrm{lk}(L)
is an
n\times n
integral matrix with entries
\mathrm{lk}(L)_{i,j}=\mathrm{lk}(K_{i},K_{j})
when
i\neq j
, and
\mathrm{lk}(L)_{i,i}=0
.

Suppose
L=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}
is an oriented link in
S^{3}
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
(m_{i},l_{i})
be the meridian-longitude basis of
\pi_{1}(\partial N(K_{i}))
such that
l_{i}
follows the orientation of
K_{i}
, and
(m_{i},l_{i})
is positively oriented with respect to the boundary orientation of
N(K_{i})
inherited from
S^{3}
. We also identify
(m_{i},l_{i})
with their images in
\pi_{1}(E_{L})
through the homomorphism
\pi_{1}(\partial N(K_{i}))\to\pi_{1}(E_{L})
up to a choice of basepoints, and regard them as the
preferred meridian-longitude basis
for
L
. Then,
m_{i}
and
l_{i}
generate a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in
\pi_{1}(E_{L})
depending on whether
K_{i}
is a splitted unknotted component.

4.1.
Perfect isomorphism

Definition 4.1
.

Let
L=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}
and
L^{\prime}=K_{1}^{\prime}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}^{\prime}
be two component-ordered oriented links in
S^{3}
, and let
(m_{i},l_{i})
and
(m_{i}^{\prime},l_{i}^{\prime})
be the preferred meridian-longitude bases for
L
and
L^{\prime}
. An isomorphism
\Phi:\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{L})\to\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{L^{\prime}})
is called
a perfect isomorphism
if
\Phi
is peripheral regular with respect to a homeomorphism
h:\partial(E_{L})\to\partial(E_{L^{\prime}})
such that
h(m_{i})=m_{i}^{\prime}
and
h(l_{i})=l_{i}^{\prime}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
L=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}
and
L^{\prime}=K_{1}^{\prime}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}^{\prime}
are two component-ordered oriented links in
S^{3}
, and
\Phi:\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{L})\to\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{L^{\prime}})
is a perfect isomorphism. Then
\mathrm{lk}(L)=\mathrm{lk}(L^{\prime})

5.
Two-bridge links

5.1.
Schubert normal form

Two-bridge links (including knots) were introduced by Schubert
[
Sch56
]
and classified by their Schubert normal forms
\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta)
, where
-\alpha<\beta<\alpha
are coprime integers and
\beta
is odd. In particular,
\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta)
is a knot when
\alpha
is odd, and
\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta)
is a two-component link when
\alpha
is even. Conventionally,
\alpha
is called the
torsion
and
\beta
is called the
crossing number
of
\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta)
.

The Schubert diagram of a two-bridge link consists of two arcs above the projection plane, depicted as straight segments, and two arcs below the projection plane. The Schubert diagram is always equipped with a standard orientation, such that the two arcs above the projection plane are assigned opposite orientations, see
Figure 1
.

When we mention a two-bridge link
L=\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta)
,
L
is always equipped with this orientation.

Figure 1.
The oriented Schubert diagram for
\mathbf{b}(8,3)
(the Whitehead link)

Theorem 5.1

(
[
Sch56
]
)
.

Let
L=\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta)
and
L=\mathbf{b}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
be two-bridge links.

(1)

L
and
L^{\prime}
are isotopic as oriented links if and only if
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}\equiv\beta^{\pm 1}\pmod{2\alpha}
.

(2)

L
and
L^{\prime}
are isotopic as unoriented links if and only if
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}\equiv\beta^{\pm 1}\pmod{\alpha}
.

Remark 5.2
.

All two-bridge links are invertible; and for two-component two-bridge links, the two components are interchangeable, and altering the orientation of one component switches
\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta)
to
\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta\pm\alpha)
. Thus, given an unoriented two-bridge link, any assignment of orientation is in fact a standard orientation within some Schubert normal form.

5.2.
Double branched cover

For a two-bridge link (including knot)
L=\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta)
, it is known that
M_{2}(L)
is the (oriented) lens space
\mathrm{L}(\alpha,\beta)
(see
[
BZ02
, Proposition 12.3]
for example). Conversely, Hodgson and Rubinstein
[
HR85
]
proved, through classifying involutions on lens spaces, that two-bridge links are actually characterized by their two-fold branched covers.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
HR85
]
)
.

Suppose
L
is an unoriented link in
S^{3}
so that the two-fold branched cover

is the oriented lens space
\mathrm{L}(\alpha,\beta)
. Then,

is isotopic to the two-bridge link
\mathbf{b}(\alpha,\beta^{\ast})
, where
\beta^{\ast}\equiv\beta\pmod{\alpha}
.

6.1.
Definition and classification

Montesinos links are generalizations of two-bridge links. A Montesinos link is constructed by combining
n
rational tangles of slopes
r_{1}=\frac{p_{1}}{q_{1}},\cdots,r_{n}=\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\in\mathbb{Q}
left-to-right in order, and then taking a numerator closure as shown in
Figure 2
. A Montesinos link so described is denoted by
\mathbf{M}(\frac{p_{1}}{q_{1}},\cdots,\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}})
, where
p_{i}
and
q_{i}
are coprime integers. In this paper, we require that:

(i)

r_{i}\neq\infty
, since then the Montesinos link can be decomposed as a connected sum of two-bridge links;

(ii)

r_{i}\neq 0
, since the rational tangles of slope
0
can be omitted;

(iii)

r_{i}\notin\mathbb{Z}
if
n\geq 2
, since a rational tangle with integral slope can be merged into an adjacent tangle to obtain new rational tangle that gives a simpler presentation of the Montesinos link.

We remind the readers that Montesinos links with no more than

Proposition 6.1
.

(1)

The mirror image of a Montesinos link

(2)

Suppose

The Dehn filling alignment theorem (
Theorem 3.4
) also holds for non-primitive slopes yielding orbifolds, see
[
Xu24
, Remark 3.6]
; and the conclusion follows directly from this generalized version.
∎

The following criterion focuses on the
\pi
-orbifold group of a hyperbolic link.
Let
\mathscr{O}=\{\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O_{2}(L))\mid L\text{ is a hyperbolic link in }S^{3}\}
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Suppose
L
is a hyperbolic link in
S^{3}
which is not a two-bridge link, and
\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O_{2}(L))
is profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{O}
. Then,
\pi_{1}(E_{L})
is profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{M}
.

Proof.

Equip
L
with an arbitrary orientation.
Suppose
M
is a compact orientable 3-manifold with
\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{L})\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M)
. According to
Theorem 4.4
, there is an oriented hyperbolic link
L^{\prime}\subseteq S^{3}
such that
M\cong E_{L^{\prime}}
and there is a perfect isomorphism
\Phi:\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{L})\to\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{L^{\prime}})
. By
Proposition 7.1
,
\widehat{\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}}(O_{2}(L))\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}}(O_{2}(L^{\prime}))
, so our assumption implies that
\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O_{2}(L))\cong\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O_{2}(L^{\prime}))
. Since a hyperbolic link must be prime and unsplittable, and
L
is not a two-bridge link, by
[
BZ89
, Theorem 3.1]
,
E_{L}
and
E_{L^{\prime}}
are diffeomorphic, and so
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\pi_{1}(E_{L^{\prime}})\cong\pi_{1}(E_{L})
.
∎

7.2.
More on cyclic branched covers

We may also generalize
Proposition 4.5
to
r
-fold cyclic branched covers with
r\geq 3
. To avoid ambiguities in cyclic branched covers, we restrict our attention to knots in this subsection.

Let
K
be a knot in
S^{3}
. There is a unique
r
-fold cyclic cover of
E_{K}
, denoted by
C_{r}(K)
, which is given by the kernel of the unique epimorphism
\pi_{1}(E_{K})\twoheadrightarrow H_{1}(E_{K})\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
. The
r
-fold cyclic branched cover of
S^{3}
along
K
, denoted by
M_{r}(K)
, is obtained by Dehn filling
C_{r}(K)
along the lift of the meridian of
E_{K}
. From another perspective,
M_{r}(K)
is the
r
-fold orbifold cover of
O_{r}(K)
corresponding to the kernel of
\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O_{r}(K))\to\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(O_{r}(K))^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Suppose
K
and
K^{\prime}
are oriented knots in
S^{3}
, and
\Phi:\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{K})\to\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{K^{\prime}})
is a perfect isomorphism. Then,
\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M_{r}(K))\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M_{r}(K^{\prime}))
for any
r\geq 2
.

Proof.

Proposition 7.1
gives an isomorphism
\Phi_{r}:\widehat{\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}}(O_{r}(K))\to\widehat{\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}}(O_{r}(K^{\prime}))
. Taking profinite abelianizations gives the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}}(O_{r}(K))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}}(O_{2}(L^{\prime}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ab}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{r}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ab}}

The kernels of the abelianizations are exactly the closures of
\pi_{1}(M_{r}(K))
and
\pi_{1}(M_{r}(K^{\prime}))
, which are isomorphic to
\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M_{r}(K))
and
\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M_{r}(K^{\prime}))
. Thus, restricting
\Phi_{r}
to the kernels gives an isomorphism
\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M_{r}(K))\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M_{r}(K^{\prime}))
.
∎

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
K
be a hyperbolic knot in
S^{3}
. Suppose that for infinitely many integers
r\geq 2
,
\pi_{1}(M_{r}(K))
is profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{M}
. Then,
\pi_{1}(E_{K})
is profinitely rigid in
\mathscr{M}
.

Proof.

Equip
K
with an arbitrary orientation.
Suppose
M
is a compact orientable 3-manifold with
\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{K})\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M)
. According to
Theorem 4.4
, there is an oriented hyperbolic knot
K^{\prime}\subseteq S^{3}
such that
M\cong E_{K^{\prime}}
and there exists a perfect isomorphism
\Phi:\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{K})\to\widehat{\pi_{1}}(E_{K^{\prime}})
. According to
Proposition 7.3
,
\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M_{r}(K))\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}}(M_{r}(K^{\prime}))
for all
r\geq 2
. The assumption then implies that
\pi_{1}(M_{r}(K))\cong\pi_{1}(M_{r}(K^{\prime}))
for infinitely many
r\geq 2
.

According to
[
Koj86
, Lemma 4]
,
M_{r}(K)
is a hyperbolic manifold for
r
sufficiently large. Thus, Mostow’s rigidity theorem implies that
M_{r}(K)\cong M_{r}(K^{\prime})
for infinitely many
r
. Finally, the main theorem of
[
Koj86
]
implies that
K
and
K^{\prime}
are equivalent up to isotopy and mirroring. Thus,
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\pi_{1}(E_{K^{\prime}})\cong\pi_{1}(E_{K})
.
∎
