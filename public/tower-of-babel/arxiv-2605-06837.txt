The strong and doubly metric dimensions of Johnson and Kneser graphs
Jozef Kratica, Mirjana Čangalović, Vera Kovačević-Vujčić

Abstract
In this paper, the strong and doubly metric dimensions of Johnson and Kneser graphs are considered. The exact value of the strong metric dimension of Johnson graph J n , k J_{n,k} is obtained using the well-known results from the literature. The strong metric dimension of Kneser graph K n , k K_{n,k} has been obtained for n ≥ 3 ​ k − 1 n\geq 3k-1 . Finally, it has been shown that the doubly metric dimensions of Johnson graph J n , 2 J_{n,2} and Kneser graph K n , 2 K_{n,2} are both ⌈ 2 ​ n 3 ⌉ \lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil .

Introduction and previous work

For any vertex
v\in V(G)
of graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
its open neighborhood
is the set
N(v)=\{w\in V(G)|vw\in E(G)\}
and its closed neighborhood is

N[v]=N(v)\bigcup\{v\}
. The degree of a vertex
v
, denoted by
deg(v)
,
is defined as the cardinality of
N(v)
.
The maximum degree of
G
is

\Delta(G)=max\{deg(v)|v\in V(G)\}
and its minimum degree is

\delta(G)=min\{deg(v)|v\in V(G)\}
.
If
\Delta(G)=\delta(G)=r
, we say
that graph
G
is
r
-regular.
The diameter of
G
is
Diam(G)=max\{d(v,w)|v,w\in V(G)\}
.

The concepts of resolving sets and the metric dimension of graph G were introduced
independently by Slater (1975) in
[
1
]
and by Harary and Melter (1976) in
[
2
]
.
For a simple connected undirected graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
the distance between vertices
u
and

v
, i.e. the length of a shortest
u-v
path, is denoted by
d(u,v)
.
Vertex
w
of graph
G
is said to resolve vertices
u,v\in V(G)
if
d(u,w)\neq d(v,w)
.
Set
R\subseteq V(G)
is called a resolving set of
G
if any pair of distinct vertices of
G

is resolved by some vertex from
R
. The metric dimension of
G
, denoted by
\beta(G)
,
is the minimum cardinality of its resolving sets, while the metric basis is any resolving set
with cardinality
\beta(G)
. In
[
3
]
it is proved that that the problem of computing
the metric dimension of an arbitrary graph is NP-hard.

The strong metric dimension of
G
, introduced in 2004 by Sebo and Tannier
[
4
]
,
is a more restricted invariant than
\beta(G)
. A vertex
w
strongly resolves two vertices

u
and
v
if
u
belongs to a shortest
v-w
path, or if
v
belongs to a shortest
u-w
path.
A vertex set
S
of
G
is a strong resolving set of
G
if every two distinct vertices of
G

are strongly resolved by some vertex of
S
. The strong metric dimension of
G
,
denoted by
\beta_{S}(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of its strong resolving sets.
The strong metric basis of
G
is any strong resolving set with the cardinality
\beta_{S}(G)
.
Note that if a vertex
w
strongly resolves vertices
u
and
v
then
w
also resolves these vertices.
Hence, every strong resolving set is a resolving set and
\beta(G)\leq\beta_{S}(G)
.
The problem of finding
\beta_{S}(G)
is proved to be NP-hard
[
5
]
.
A survey of the state-of-the-art (in 2013) on the strong metric dimension can be found in
[
6
]
.

In the sequel we will present some properties of the strong metric dimension from literature which use the concepts
of vertex covering and independence number of graphs. A vertex cover of graph
G
is a set
C

of vertices from
V(G)
such that for each edge
uv\in E(G)
it holds
u\in C
or
v\in C
.
The vertex covering number of
G
, denoted by
vc(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of its vertex covers.
On the other hand, the maximum cardinality of a set of vertices of
G
, no two of which are adjacent, is called
the independence number of graph
G
and is denoted by
ind(G)
. The clique number of
G
, denoted by
\omega(G)
,
is the cardinality of a maximum complete subgraph (clique) of graph
G
. It is well-known that
ind(G)=\omega(\overline{G})
,
where
\overline{G}
represents the complement of graph
G
, i.e.
V(\overline{G})=V(G)
and

E(\overline{G})=\overline{E(G)}
.

A well-known result obtained by Gallai
[
7
]
states:

Proposition 1
.

(
[
7
]
)
For any graph
G
without isolated vertices, it holds

vc(G)=|V(G)|-ind(G)
.

, introduced in

The concept of doubly resolving sets of

. A tight lower bound for the

[

]
.

Several other NP-hard graph invariants related to the metric dimension have been recently introduced,

Since problems of computing the metric dimension and related invariants are NP-hard in

the exact values or good bounds for some classes of graphs. In particular, the metric
dimension and resolving sets of Johnson and Kneser graphs have been studied in

[

metric dimension problems from literature, which will be used in order to obtain

1.1
Integer linear programming formulations from literature

Given a simple connected undirected graph
G
= (
V
,
E
), where

V=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
,
|E|=m
, it is easy to determine the length

d(u,v)
of a shortest
u-v
path for all
u,v\in V
, using any
shortest path algorithm.

First we present the integer linear programming (ILP) formulation of the strong metric dimension problem

[
19
]
.

The coefficient matrix
A
is defined as follows:

A_{(u,v),i}=\begin{cases}1,&d(u,i)=d(u,v)+d(v,i)\\
1,&d(v,i)=d(v,u)+d(u,i)\\
0,&otherwise\end{cases}

(1)

where
1\leq u<v\leq n,1\leq i\leq n
. Variable
y_{i}

described by (2) determines whether vertex
i
belongs to a strong
resolving set
S
.

y_{i}=\begin{cases}1,&i\in S\\
0,&i\notin S\end{cases}

(2)

The ILP model of the strong metric dimension problem can now be
formulated as:

\min\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{y_{i}}

(3)

subject to:

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{A_{(u,v),i}\cdot y_{i}}\geq 1\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad 1\leq u<v\leq n

(4)

y_{i}\in\{0,1\}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad 1\leq i\leq n

(5)

The next ILP formulation of the doubly metric dimension problem is propsed in

[
10
]
.

The coefficient matrix
A
is defined as
follows:

A_{(u,v),(i,j)}=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}c}{1,\quad d(u,i)-d(v,i)\neq d(u,j)-d(v,j)}\\
{0,\quad d(u,i)-d(v,i)=d(u,j)-d(v,j)}\\
\end{array}}\right.

(6)

where
1\leq u<v\leq n,1\leq i<j\leq n
. Variable
y_{i}
described by (4)
determines whether vertex
i
belongs to a doubly resolving set
D
. Similarly,

x_{ij}
determines whether both
i,j
are in
D
.

y_{i}=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}c}{1,\quad i\in D}\\
{0,\quad i\notin D}\\
\end{array}}\right.

(7)

x_{ij}=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}c}{1,\quad\quad i,j\in D}\\
{0,\quad otherwise}\\
\end{array}}\right.

(8)

The ILP model of the doubly metric dimension problem can now be formulated as:

\min\quad\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{y_{k}}

(9)

subject to:

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}{\sum\limits_{j=i+1}^{n}{A_{(u,v),(i,j)}\cdot x_{ij}}}\geq 1\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad 1\leq u<v\leq n

(10)

x_{ij}\leq\frac{1}{2}y_{i}+\frac{1}{2}y_{j}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad 1\leq i<j\leq n

(11)

x_{ij}\geq y_{i}+y_{j}-1\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad 1\leq i<j\leq n

(12)

x_{ij}\in\{0,1\},\;y_{k}\in\{0,1\}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad 1\leq i<j\leq n,\;1\leq k\leq n

1.2
Johnson and Kneser graphs and related results from literature

Let
n
and
k
be positive integers (
n>k
) and
[n]=\{1,2,...,n\}
.
Then
k
-subsets are subsets of
[n]
which have cardinality equal to
k
.
Johnson graph
J_{n,k}
is an undirected graph defined on all
k
-subsets of set
[n]
as vertices,
where two
k
-subsets are adjacent if their intersection has cardinality equal to
k-1
.
Formally,
V(J_{n,k})=\{A|A\subset[n],|A|=k\}
and

E(J_{n,k})=\{AB|A,B\subset[n],|A|=|B|=k,|A\bigcap B|=k-1,lex(A)<lex(B)\}
,
where
lex
is any lexicographic order of
k
-subsets of
[n]
.

It is easy to see that
J_{n,k}
and
J_{n,n-k}
are isomorphic, so
we shall only consider Johnson graphs with
n\geq 2k
.
The distance between two vertices
A
and
B
in
J_{n,k}

can be computed by Property
1
.

Property 1
.

For
A,B\in V(J_{n,k})
it holds

d(A,B)=|A\setminus B|=|B\setminus A|=k-|A\bigcap B|
.

In a special case when
n=2k
distance between
\overline{A}=[n]\setminus A
and
B
can be
computed by Property
2
.

Property 2
.

For
A,B\in V(J_{2k,k})
it holds
d(\overline{A},B)=k-d(A,B)=|A\bigcap B|

Considering Property
1
, it is easy to see that Johnson graph
J_{n,k}
is a
k(n-k)
-regular graph
of diameter
k
.

Kneser graph
K_{n,k}
is an undirected graph also defined on all
k
-subsets of set
[n]
as vertices,
where two
k
-subsets are adjacent if their intersection is the empty set.
Formally,
V(K_{n,k})=\{A|A\subset[n],|A|=k\}
and

E(K_{n,k})=\{AB|A,B\subset[n],|A|=|B|=k,A\bigcap B=\emptyset,lex(A)<lex(B)\}
.

Kneser graph is connected only if
n>2\cdot k
, it is also
\binom{n-k}{k}
-regular graph.
Specially, for
k=2
, Kneser graph
K_{n,2}
is the complement of the corresponding
Johnson graph
J_{n,2}
, and both graphs have diameter 2. Hence, if
d_{K_{n,2}}(A,B)=1

then
d_{J_{n,2}}(A,B)=2
, and vice versa. Therefore, for
A\neq B
it holds

d_{K_{n,2}}(A,B)=3-d_{J_{n,2}}(A,B)
.

Distances and diameter of Kneser graphs are investigated by Valencia-Pabon and Vera in
[
20
]

Diam(K_{n,k})=\lceil\frac{k-1}{n-2k}\rceil+1.

(14)

Consequently, if
n\geq 3k-1
then

Diam(K_{n,k})=2.

(15)

In this case
d(A,B)=2\Leftrightarrow AB\notin E(K_{n,k})\Leftrightarrow AB\in E((K_{n,k})_{SR})
.
This implies
(K_{n,k})_{SR}=\overline{K_{n,k}}
.

Moreover, in case when

from (
14

.
In this case distances can be computed using the result by Stahl in
[

Lemma 1

(
[

]

Let

(i)

If

|A\bigcap B|\geq k(2p+1)-pn

(ii)

If d(A,B)=2p+1 then

Moreover, Valencia-Pabon and Vera in

d(A,B)=min\{2\lceil\frac{k-s}{n-2k}\rceil,2\lceil\frac{s}{n-2k}\rceil+1\}

2
The strong metric dimension of Johnson and Kneser graphs

Lemma 2
.

Vertices
A,B\in V(J_{n,k})
are mutually maximally distant if and only if
d(A,B)=k
.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) If
d(A,B)=k=Diam(J_{n,k})
it follows that all distances are at most
k
. Therefore,

A
and
B
are mutually maximally distant.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose the opposite, i.e.
d(A,B)<k=Diam(J_{n,k})\Rightarrow A\bigcap B\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow|A\bigcup B|<2k\leq n
.
Then there exist
a\in A\bigcap B
and
b\in V(J_{n,k})\setminus(A\bigcup B)
. Let
C=(B\setminus\{a\})\bigcup\{b\}
.
Then
A\bigcap C\subset A\bigcap B\Rightarrow|A\bigcap C|<|A\bigcap B|\Rightarrow d(A,C)=k-|A\bigcap C|>k-|A\bigcap B|=d(A,B)
. Since
|C|=k
,
d(A,C)>d(A,B)
and
|B\bigcap C|=|B|-1=k-1\Rightarrow d(B,C)=1\Rightarrow C\in N(B)
,
so
A
and
B
are not mutually maximally distant in graph
J_{n,k}
.
Therefore, by contra-position, we have proved that if vertices
A,B\in V(J_{n,k})
are mutually maximally distant then
d(A,B)=k
.
∎

Proposition 5
.

\beta_{S}(J_{n,k})=\binom{n-1}{k}

Proof.

From Property
1
it follows that
Diam(J_{n,k})=k
.
Next, by Lemma
2
, vertices
A,B\in V(J_{n,k})
are mutually maximally distant if and only if

.
Therefore, by Definition
1
and Definition

, we have
(J_{n,k})_{SR}=K_{n,k}

Finally, by Theorem

\beta_{S}(J_{n,k})
=

The following theorem gives the exact value of the strong metric dimension of

K_{n,k}
for
n\geq 3k-1

Theorem 6
.

For
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 3k-1
it holds
\beta_{S}(K_{n,k})=\binom{n}{k}-\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor
.

Proof.

Step 1.
\beta_{S}(K_{n,k})\geq\binom{n}{k}-\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor

Suppose the contrary, i.e.
\beta_{S}(K_{n,k})<\binom{n}{k}-\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor
.
Let
m=\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor
and
S
be a strong resolving base of
K_{n,k}
, so
|S|=\beta_{S}(K_{n,k})<\binom{n}{k}-m
.
Let
A,B\in V(K_{n,k})\setminus S
. Since
|V(K_{n,k})\setminus S|\geq m+1
then
(m+1)\cdot k>n
.
There exist at least
m+1
vertices (sets) which are not in
S
, composed of at least
(m+1)\cdot k
indices.
Since
\{1,2,...,n\}
has
n
indices, and
(m+1)\cdot k>n
then there exists
A,B\notin S
such
that
A\bigcap B\neq\emptyset
which implies
AB\in V(\overline{K_{n,k}})
. Since
S
is a
strong resolving set of
K_{n,k}
, and
A,B\notin S
then

(\exists C\in S)(d(C,A)+d(A,B)=d(C,B)\vee d(C,B)+d(B,A)=d(C,A))

Since
d(A,B)=2
and
n\geq 3k-1
according to (
15
),
Diam(K_{n,k})=2=Diam(\overline{K_{n,k}})
.
Then
C=A
or
C=B
,
which is a contradiction with

!

Step 2.
\beta_{S}(K_{n,k})\leq\binom{n}{k}-\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor

Let
A_{i}=\{ik+1,ik+2,....,(i+1)k\}
, for all
0\leq i\leq m-1
,

For

and

and

.
Since

.
From

it follows that there exists

.
Let
C=(B\setminus\{jk+1\})\bigcup\{l\}
then
|C|=k
,
B\bigcap C\neq\emptyset

and

. Consequently,

It is obvious that

The next theorem obtains the exact value of the doubly metric dimension of
J_{n,2}
.

Theorem 7
.

For
n\geq 15
it holds
\psi(J_{n,2})=\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3
it holds
\beta(J_{n,2})=\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil
.
Having in mind that for every graph
G
,
\psi(G)\geq\beta(G)
so
\psi(J_{n,2})\geq\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil
.
Let
S
be a resolving set construncted in the proof of Theorem 3.32 in
[
16
]
(Theorem
3
in this paper).
We will prove that set
S
is also a doubly resolving set.

The construction is the following.

Let
S^{\prime}=\{\{3i-2,3i-1\},\{3i-1,3i\}|1\leq i\leq t\}
, where
t=\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor
.

Now

S=\begin{cases}S^{\prime},\quad n=3t\\
S^{\prime}\bigcup\{\{3t-1,3t+1\}\},\quad n=3t+1\\
S^{\prime}\bigcup\{\{3t-1,3t+1\},\{3t-1,3t+2\}\},\quad n=3t+2\\
\end{cases}

(17)

.

Let
P=(P_{1},...,P_{t})
be a partition of indices {1,2,…,n},
such that

P_{i}=\begin{cases}\{3i-2,3i-1,3i\},\quad i<t\\
P\setminus\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{t-1}{{P_{j}}},\quad i=t\end{cases}
.

In Theorem 3.32 in
[
16
]
it is proved that
S
is a resolving set, i.e.
(\forall A,B\in V(J_{n,2}))(A\neq B)(\exists X\in S)

d(A,X)\neq d(B,X)
.

Let us prove that
(\forall A,B\in V(J_{n,2}))(A\neq B)(\exists Y\in S)\quad d(A,Y)=d(B,Y)
.
Let
A=\{a,b\}
and
B=\{c,d\}
, such that
a\in P_{i_{a}}
,
b\in P_{i_{b}}
,
c\in P_{i_{c}}
and

d\in P_{i_{d}}
. It should be noted that some indices
i_{a},i_{b},i_{c},i_{d}
can be equal.
Therefore, set
I=\{i_{a},i_{b},i_{c},i_{d}\}
has cardinality
|I|\leq 4
.
Since
n\geq 15
then
t\geq 5
, and there exists
i_{y}
such that
1\leq i_{y}\leq t
and
i_{y}\notin\{i_{a},i_{b},i_{c},i_{d}\}
.
For
Y=\{3i_{y}-2,3i_{y}-1\}
it holds that
A\bigcap Y=B\bigcap Y=\emptyset
implying
d(A,Y)=d(B,Y)=2
.

Since
d(A,X)\neq d(B,X)
and
d(A,Y)=d(B,Y)
it follows
d(A,X)-d(A,Y)\neq d(B,X)-d(B,Y)
,
which implies that set
S
is a dobly resolving set, and
\psi(J_{n,2})\leq\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil
.
∎

17
)

7

The next theorem obtains the exact value of the doubly metric dimension of
K_{n,2}
.

Theorem 8
.

For
n\geq 15
it holds
\psi(K_{n,2})=\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3
from
[
16
]
it holds
\beta(K_{n,2})=\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil
.
Having in mind that for every graph
G
,
\psi(G)\geq\beta(G)
, so
\psi(K_{n,2})\geq\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil
.

Let
S
be the set defined by (
17
) from Theorem
7
.
As already noticed
\overline{J_{n,2}}=K_{n,2}
and
Diam(J_{n,2})=Diam(K_{n,2})=2
, so
V(J_{n,2})=V(K_{n,2})

and for each two vertices
A,B\in V(K_{n,2})
with
A\neq B
it holds
d(A,B)=3-d_{J_{n,2}}(A,B)
,
where
d(A,B)
and
d_{J_{n,2}}(A,B)
are distances between
A
and
B
in Kneser graph
K_{n,2}
and
Johnson graph
J_{n,2}
, respectively.

In Theorem
7
it is proved

(\forall A,B\in V(J_{n,2})=V(K_{n,2}))(\exists X\in S)\quad A\bigcap X=B\bigcap X=\emptyset

This implies
d(A,X)=d(B,X)=1
.

In Theorem
7
it is proved

(\forall A,B\in V(J_{n,2})=V(K_{n,2}))(A\neq B)(\exists Y\in S)\quad d_{J_{n,2}}(A,Y)\neq d_{J_{n,2}}(B,Y)

If
A\neq Y
and
B\neq Y
, from
d(A,Y)=3-d_{J_{n,2}}(A,Y)
and
d(B,Y)=3-d_{J_{n,2}}(B,Y)
it follows
d(A,Y)\neq d(B,Y)
.

In the case when
A=Y
then
0=d(A,Y)\neq d(B,Y)\geq 1
. Similarly, if
B=Y
then
0=d(B,Y)\neq d(A,Y)\geq 1

Therefore,
d(A,X)-d(A,Y)\neq d(B,X)-d(B,Y)
implying that set
S
is a doubly resolving set for graph
K_{n,2}
, which implies
\psi(K_{n,2})\leq\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil
.
∎

4
Conclusions

The doubly and strong metric dimensions of both Johnson and Kneser graphs have been studied.
Exact values
\beta_{S}(J_{n,k})=\binom{n-1}{k}
and
\psi(J_{n,2})=\psi(K_{n,2})=\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil

are obtained. Furthermore, it is shown that for
n\geq 3k-1
, the value of
\beta_{S}(K_{n,k})
is equal to
\binom{n}{k}-\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor
.

In future research, it would be interesting to investigate the values of
\beta_{S}(K_{n,k})
for
n<3k-1
,
as well as those of
\psi(J_{n,k})
and
\psi(K_{n,k})
for
k\geq 3
.
Furthermore, efforts could be directed towards determining the strong and/or doubly metric dimensions of other interesting classes of graphs.
