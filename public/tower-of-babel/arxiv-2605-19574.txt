Flowing to free boundary minimal surfaces
Melanie Rupflin, Michael Struwe and Christopher Wright

Abstract
. We introduce a flow that is designed to flow maps u : Σ → ℝ n u:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n} which map the boundary of a general domain surface Σ \Sigma into a given (not necessarily connected) submanifold N ↪ ℝ n N\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n} towards a free boundary (branched) minimal immersion supported by N N . In the case when Σ \Sigma is the unit disc D D , this task can be achieved by means of the Plateau-flow introduced in the work [ 15 ] of the second author. When Σ ≠ D \Sigma\neq D , however, also the conformal type of the domain metric plays a role and it no longer suffices to deform the trace of the given map into a half-harmonic map as in [ 15 ] . In order to overcome this issue, here we combine ideas of the Plateau-flow from [ 15 ] with ideas of the Teichmüller harmonic flow from [ 12 ] , in order to flow both an initial map u 0 u_{0} with trace u 0 : ∂ Σ → N u_{0}\colon{\partial\Sigma}\to N and an initial domain metric g 0 g_{0} in a way that produces, as time tends to infinity, a half-harmonic map from ∂ Σ {\partial\Sigma} into N N whose harmonic extension is conformal and hence is a (branched) minimal immersion.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Half-harmonic maps

Let
N\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a smooth, not necessarily connected, closed submanifold of any dimension and let
\Sigma
be a closed surface with boundary
\partial\Sigma\neq\emptyset
. Given a metric
g
on
\Sigma
, following
[
5
]
and
[
4
]
, we define the half-energy
E_{\frac{1}{2}}(u,g)
of a function
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Sigma;N)
as the Dirichlet energy of its harmonic extension, i.e.

(1.1)

E_{\frac{1}{2}}(u,g):=E_{g}(u_{g}):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\mathrm{d}u_{g}|_{g}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}.

Here and in the following
E_{g}
is the standard Dirichlet energy with respect to the metric
g
and
u_{g}\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
denotes the harmonic extension with respect to the metric
g
of a given map
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial\Sigma};N)=H^{\frac{1}{2}}((\partial\Sigma,\mathrm{d}s_{g});N)
, i.e. the unique function
u_{g}:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\Delta_{g}u_{g}=0
and trace
u_{g}|_{{\partial\Sigma}}=u
.

We furthermore denote by
\pi:N_{\eta}\to N
the nearest point projection onto
N
, which is well-defined and smooth on the tubular neighbourhood

N_{\eta}:=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\text{dist}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(y,N)<\eta\}

of
N
if
\eta>0
is chosen small enough, and recall that for any
p\in N
the orthogonal projection
P_{p}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to T_{p}N
of the ambient
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
onto the tangent space
T_{p}N
to
N
at
p
can be equivalently written as
P_{p}=\mathrm{d}\pi(p)
.

As in
[
5
]
and
[
4
]
, we call a map
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Sigma;N)
half-harmonic if it is a critical point of
E_{\frac{1}{2}}(u,g)
in the sense that
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi(u+\varepsilon v),g)=0
for every
v\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial\Sigma};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. As

(1.2)

\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\dd}{\dd\ep}$}}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi(u+\varepsilon v),g)=\int_{{\partial\Sigma}}\partial_{\varepsilon}\pi(u+\varepsilon v)|_{\varepsilon=0}\cdot{\partial_{\nu_{g}}}u_{g}\mathrm{d}s_{g}=\int_{{\partial\Sigma}}v\cdot P_{u}({\partial_{\nu_{g}}}u_{g})\mathrm{d}s_{g},

where
\nu_{g}
is the outwards unit normal of
(\Sigma,g)
along
{\partial\Sigma}
, this is equivalent to asking that the harmonic extension
u_{g}
of
u
satisfies the equation

(1.3)

P_{u}({\partial_{\nu_{g}}}u_{g})=0\ \hbox{ on }{\partial\Sigma}.

Half-harmonic maps are related to free boundary minimal surfaces supported by
N
, that is, parametrisations
u\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of minimal surfaces with trace
u({\partial\Sigma})\subset N
that meet the supporting surface
N
orthogonally. Recall that a map
u_{g}\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
whose components are harmonic functions parametrises a (possibly branched) minimal surface if and only if
u_{g}
is (weakly) conformal, i.e. so that
u_{g}^{*}g_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}=\rho^{2}g
for a function
\rho\geq 0
. Orthogonality being implied by (
1.3
), the harmonic extension of a half-harmonic map
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Sigma;N)
thus parametrises a free boundary minimal surface whenever this extension is (weakly) conformal. (In the following we will always allow branch points.)

We note that if
\Sigma=D
, then as observed in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
, the equation (
1.3
) is in fact a sufficient condition for weak conformality of the harmonic function
u_{g}
. While for surfaces
\Sigma
of higher genus or higher connectivity this condition is not sufficient, we can exploit that maps
u_{g}\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are weakly conformal if and only if the half-energy is critical with respect to variations of the metric. This was shown by Da Lio and Pigati
[
4
]
in the setting of the half-energy and of half-harmonic maps considered here. Since we will use the corresponding properties of the variation of the half-energy in our construction of the flow, we briefly recall how this key property can be obtained.

To this end, we note that the first variation of the Dirichlet energy
E_{g}
(for fixed metric
g
) along maps
v_{\varepsilon}=u_{g_{\varepsilon}}
that are obtained as harmonic extensions of a fixed map
u:{\partial\Sigma}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with respect to a family of metrics
g_{\varepsilon}
with
g_{\varepsilon=0}=g
is always so that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}E_{g}(v_{\varepsilon})=\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla_{g}(\partial_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}),\nabla_{g}u_{g}\rangle_{g}\mathrm{d}v_{g}=-\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}\cdot\Delta_{g}u_{g}\mathrm{d}v_{g}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\partial_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\partial_{\nu_{g}}}u_{g}\mathrm{d}s_{g}=0

as
u_{g}
is harmonic and
\partial_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}|_{\partial\Sigma}=0
. Here and in the following we use the convention that all derivatives with respect to
\varepsilon
are evaluated at
\varepsilon=0
. The variation of the half-energy with respect to the metric hence reduces to the variation of the Dirichlet energy of the fixed map
u_{g}
with respect to a varying metric
g_{\varepsilon}
, which we recall is given by

(1.4)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(u,g_{\varepsilon})=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}E_{g_{\varepsilon}}(u_{g})=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\langle\partial_{\varepsilon}g_{\varepsilon},k(u_{g},g)\rangle_{g}\mathrm{d}v_{g},

see for instance
[
1
, Lemma 3.4.1]
. Here and in the following

(1.5)

k(v,g)=v^{*}g_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}-\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}v|_{g}^{2}g

denotes the stress-energy tensor of maps
v:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which we note is always trace-free and of course vanishes if and only if
v
is conformal.

1.2.
Gradient flow

In view of the above, in order to define a flow that will evolve an initial (parametrised) surface to a free boundary minimal surface it is natural to consider a gradient flow of the half-energy.

In the special case of the disc, where the moduli space of metrics is given by a single point, this was achieved by Wettstein in
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
and by the second author in
[
15
]
. In fact, in this case we see an amazing similarity of the salient features of the heat flow for half-harmonic maps, called “Plateau flow”, and the heat flow of harmonic maps from a closed surface into smooth closed target manifolds laid out in
[
14
]
. In particular, for any given initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Sigma;N)
, it was shown in
[
15
]
that there is a unique global weak solution to the Plateau flow whose energy is non-increasing and which is smooth for
t>0
away from finitely many points in space-time where non-trivial disc-type free boundary minimal surfaces ”bubble off”. Furthermore, the results of
[
15
]
ensure that along a sequence of times going to infinity, the solution converges weakly to a disc-type free boundary minimal surface, again away from finitely many ”bubble points” where non-trivial disc-type free boundary minimal surfaces emerge after rescaling. As recently shown by the third author
[
20
]
, uniqueness in this case holds even in an a priori much larger class of weak solutions to the Plateau flow, which in particular includes all weak solutions with non-increasing energy, again in exact analogy with what is known in the case of the harmonic map heat flow from
[
6
]
and
[
9
]
.

In the present paper, we now consider the corresponding problem for surfaces
\Sigma
of higher genus and/or connectivity. We introduce a coupled flow which evolves both an initial map
u_{0}\colon\partial\Sigma\to N
and an initial domain metric
g_{0}
by (a projection of) the negative gradient of
E_{\frac{1}{2}}
so as to yield a critical point of the half-energy with respect to both
u
and
g
, hence inducing a free boundary minimal surface.

Remark 1.1
.

In the following, we will only consider domain surfaces
\Sigma
which are orientable as for non-orientable
\Sigma
all results can be recovered by passing to the orientable double cover and working with metrics and function spaces which are invariant under the non-trivial covering space transformation.

Since the energy is conformally invariant, we can restrict the class of admissible metrics to consist of a unique representative for each conformal class of metric. For this we make use of standard uniformisation results, see e.g.
[
8
]
. For (orientable)
\Sigma\neq D
of general type we will work with the unique representative
g
of each conformal class for which the boundary curves are geodesics in
(\Sigma,g)
and which is hyperbolic (i.e. has Gauss curvature
K_{g}\equiv-1
). When
\Sigma
is a cylinder we instead choose as our unique representative the flat metric
g
(
K_{g}\equiv 0
) for which the boundary curves are geodesics and
(\Sigma,g)
has unit area. We denote by
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
the space of such constant curvature metrics and note that our definition of the flow will ensure that for an initial metric
g_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
, the evolving metrics always will be in
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
.

We recall that at any
g\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
the tangent space splits
L^{2}(\Sigma,g)
-orthogonally as

(1.6)

T_{g}\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)=\{L_{X}g\}+H(g),

where
\{L_{X}g\}
is the set of Lie-derivatives generated by vector fields that are parallel to
{\partial\Sigma}
on
{\partial\Sigma}
, giving rise to
1
-parameter families of diffeomorphisms of
\Sigma
, and where the
horizontal space

H(g)
consists of all symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors
h
which are trace-free and divergence-free and which satisfy
h(\nu_{g},\tau_{g})=0
on
\partial\Sigma
,
\tau_{g}
a unit tangent vector field along
({\partial\Sigma},\mathrm{d}s_{g})
, c.f.
[
16
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

We note that for a half-harmonic map
u:(\partial\Sigma,g)\rightarrow N
the tensor
k(u_{g},g)
is always an element of
H(g)
. Indeed since
u_{g}
is harmonic
k(u_{g},g)
is divergence free, while (
1.3
) implies that
k(u_{g},g)(\nu_{g},\tau_{g})=\langle\partial_{\nu_{g}}u_{g},{\partial_{\tau_{g}}}u_{g}\rangle
vanishes on the boundary since
u
maps into
N
. Hence the harmonic extension of a half-harmonic map is conformal if and only if
P_{g}^{H}(k(u_{g},g))=0
, where we define
P_{g}^{H}
as the
L^{2}(\Sigma,g)
-orthogonal projection from the space of symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors to
H(g)
.

Following the construction of the Teichmüller harmonic map flow in
[
12
]
, we now exploit the fact that the energy is invariant under simultaneous pull-back of both the map and the metric (by the same diffeomorphism) to restrict the movement of the metric component to be
L^{2}
-orthogonal to the space of tensors
\{L_{X}g\}
that is generated by the action of diffeomorphisms on the metric. We hence define our flow as

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=-\nabla^{L^{2}}_{u}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(u,g)=-P_{u}({\partial_{\nu_{g}}}u_{g})

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g

\displaystyle=-P_{g}^{H}\big(\nabla_{g}^{L^{2}}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(u,g)\big)=\frac{1}{2}P^{H}_{g}(k(u_{g},g)).

The evolution of the map component
u=u(t)
is hence described by a variant of the Plateau flow
studied by the second author in
[
15
]
, albeit now considered on a general surface
\Sigma
with a time-dependent metric
g(t)
rather than on a disc with fixed metric; the evolution of the metric
g=g(t)
on the other hand can be viewed as an evolution equation in the infinite dimensional manifold
{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma)
of constant curvature metrics with geodesic boundary curves described above. We observe that in the case when
\Sigma=D
we have
H(g)=\{0\}
for any metric
g
on
D
, and so our coupled gradient flow (
1.7
) reduces to the Plateau flow for maps
u(t)
on the disc (with fixed metric) considered in
[
15
]
.

1.3.
Main results

Our first main result establishes the existence of solutions to this new geometric flow and gives a description of the potential singularities that the components of this system of equations might form.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma\neq D
be any orientable surface with boundary and let
{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma)
be the set of metrics with constant curvature and geodesic boundary considered above. Then to any initial metric
g_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
and any initial map
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(({\partial\Sigma},g_{0});N)
there exists a weak solution
(u,g)
of the coupled flow

(1.8)

\partial_{t}u=-P_{u}({\partial_{\nu_{g}}}u_{g}),\qquad\partial_{t}g=\frac{1}{2}P^{H}_{g}(k(u_{g},g))

which has non-increasing energy and is defined on a maximal interval
[0,T_{\infty})
where
T_{\infty}=\infty
unless the domain metrics degenerate in finite time, i.e. unless

\inj(\Sigma,g(t))\to 0\text{ as }t\uparrow T_{\infty}\text{ for a finite }T_{\infty}.

Furthermore,

(1)

Away from a finite number of singular times
0<T_{i}^{s}<T_{\infty}
, both the map and metric component of the flow are smooth and the energy decays according to

(1.9)

\frac{d}{dt}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(u,g)=-\|P_{u}({\partial_{\nu_{g}}}u_{g})\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Sigma},g)}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\|P_{g}^{H}(k(u_{g},g))\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g)}^{2}.

(2)

Across each
T_{i}^{s}
the flow of metrics remains regular in the sense that
g(t)
is Lipschitz continuous in time with respect to any
C^{k}
-metric in space.

(3)

Any such singular time
T_{i}^{s}
is characterised by the
bubbling-off
of a finite number of minimal discs of the map component, exactly as in
[
15
]
, see also Section
5.5
.

Here and in the following we say that
(u,g)
is a weak solution of the flow to initial data
(u_{0},g_{0})
on an interval
[0,T)
if

•

the metric component is a continuous curve
g:[0,T)\to({\mathcal{M}}^{3}(\Sigma),\text{dist}_{H^{3}})
with
g(0)=g_{0}
which is differentiable at a.e.
t\in[0,T)
and so that the second equation in (
1.8
) is satisfied at a.e. such
t
. Here
\mathcal{M}^{3}
denotes the set of constant curvature metrics with coefficients in
H^{3}
as discussed in Section
2
below.

•

the map component is given by a
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{\frac{1}{2}}(({\partial\Sigma},\mathrm{d}s_{g});N)
which is so that
\partial_{t}u\in L_{loc}^{2}([0,T);L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,g))
and so that the first equation of (
1.8
) is satisfied in the sense of distributions.

In the absence of singularities at infinity, the following result ensures that the flow deforms the given initial map into a free boundary minimal surface as desired.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
(u,g)
is a global weak solution of (
1.8
) for which
\inj(\Sigma,g)
remains bounded away from zero and for which energy does not concentrate as
t\to\infty
, i.e. so that
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in{\partial\Sigma}}E_{g}(u_{g}(t),B_{r}^{g(t)}(x))\to 0
as
r\to 0
.

Then there exist
t_{j}\to\infty
so that after pull-back by diffeomorphisms the pairs
(u(t_{j}),g(t_{j}))
converge smoothly to a limiting pair
(u^{*},g^{*})\in C^{\infty}({\partial\Sigma};N)\times\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
which, if
u^{*}
is non-constant, is so that
u^{*}_{g^{*}}:(\Sigma,g^{*})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is (weakly) conformal, harmonic and so that
u^{*}_{g^{*}}(\Sigma)
meets
N
orthogonally; that is,
u^{*}_{g^{*}}(\Sigma)
represents a (possibly branched) free boundary minimal surface supported by
N
.

Remark 1.5
.

We note that in the special case where
N
is given by a collection of
k
disjoint closed curves
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{k}
,
k
the number of boundary components
\sigma_{i}
of
\Sigma
, and where each
u_{0}|_{\sigma_{i}}
is a map into
\Gamma_{i}
with non-zero degree, this property is preserved along regular solutions of the flow. In this case the minimal surface obtained in the above theorem can be thought of as a solution of a generalised version of the Douglas-Plateau problem,
compare also the discussion in Sections 1.5 and 1.6 of
[
15
]
.

Outline of the paper.
We discuss the key steps of the proof of short time existence of solutions to the flow in Section
2
and carry out the required detailed analysis of the equations satisfied by the metric and map components in the subsequent Sections
3
and
4
. In Section
5
, we prove higher regularity estimates which enable us to deduce the smoothness of the solution until either the metric degenerates or energy concentrates. Combined with the analysis of finite time singularities, which is carried out in Section
5.5
and closely follows
[
15
]
, this allows us to complete the proof of our first main result, Theorem
1.3
. Finally, in Section
6
we study the asymptotic behaviour, proving Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Short time existence of solutions

In this section, we explain the key steps needed to prove local existence of solutions of (
1.8
) for given smooth initial data
(u_{0},g_{0})
. This proof will be based on an iteration argument that is carried out on a sufficiently small interval
[0,T]
and in suitable Sobolev spaces
X_{m}(T)
of maps of regularity
H^{m}
and curves of metrics in
\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
of regularity
C^{1}_{t}H^{m+1}
as made precise below. This argument will be based on results about (related) equations for the metric and map component which are stated in a series of lemmas in the present section, but whose proofs are postponed to Section
3
(for results about the metric) and Section
4
(for results about the map).

In the following, we will focus mainly on the case of domains of general type as the analysis of the metric component simplifies very significantly if
\Sigma
is a cylinder, as in this case the equation for the metric reduces to a well-controlled ODE. We will discuss this special case in Section
3.3
and for now simply note that all results stated below are applicable also for
\Sigma
a cylinder and the corresponding space
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
of flat unit area metrics.

2.1.
Set-up

Given a surface
\Sigma
with boundary, we recall that we may think of this domain as a fixed subset of the Schottky double
\hat{\Sigma}
, which is the closed surface that we obtain as quotient of
\Sigma\times\{-1,1\}
under the identification
(p,1)\sim(p,-1)
for
p\in\partial\Sigma
. Moreover, we note that if
g
is any hyperbolic metric on
\Sigma
for which the boundary curves are geodesics, then
g
can be extended by (even) symmetry to a hyperbolic metric
\hat{g}
on
\hat{\Sigma}
.

Following the approach of Tromba
[
16
]
, given any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 3}
and any fixed finite (and symmetric) set of (smooth) coordinate charts on
\hat{\Sigma}
we can consider the set
\mathcal{M}^{k}=\mathcal{M}^{k}(\Sigma)
of metrics
g
on
\Sigma
which we obtain as restrictions of hyperbolic metrics
\hat{g}
on
\hat{\Sigma}
which have the above even symmetry across
{\partial\Sigma}
and whose coefficients are of class
H^{k}
in these charts. We measure the distance between two elements of
\mathcal{M}^{k}
by letting

\text{dist}_{H^{k}}(g_{1},g_{2}):=\inf\int_{1}^{2}\|\partial_{s}g(s)\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma,g(s))}\mathrm{d}s,

where the infimum is taken over the set of all
C^{1}
-paths
g=g(s)\in\mathcal{M}^{k}(\Sigma)
,
1\leq s\leq 2
, that connect
g_{1}=g(1)
and
g_{2}=g(2)
. Here and in the following all Sobolev norms are computed using the Levi-Cività connection with respect to the indicated metric.

Given any
m\geq 2
and a curve of metrics
g\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathcal{M}^{k})
we will work with maps that are contained in the space

(2.1)

\displaystyle X_{m}(T,g)=\{u:{\partial\Sigma}\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\displaystyle:u_{g}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\ H^{m}(\Sigma,g))

\displaystyle\text{ and }\partial_{t}(u_{g})\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Sigma,g))\},

which we equip with the corresponding norms

(2.2)

\|u\|_{X_{m}(T,g)}^{2}:=\|u_{g}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{m}(\Sigma,g))}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}(u_{g})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Sigma,g))}^{2}.

While these norms depend on the specific choice of
g
, we will see that the spaces
X_{m}(T,g)
themselves are indeed independent of the choice of
g
as the change of harmonic extensions and Sobolev norms along such curves of metrics is well controlled, compare Lemma
3.8
. We hence often drop the reference to
g
in the notation for
X_{m}(T,g)
and simply write
X_{m}(T)
, and will at times also use the shorthand
X_{m,loc}(T):=\bigcup_{T^{\prime}<T}X_{m}(T^{\prime})
. We furthermore note that in the proof of short-time existence we will for the most part only need to consider curves of metrics which are contained in a small neighbourhood
{\mathcal{U}}
of the initial metric
g_{0}
and whose velocity is uniformly bounded. For such curves of metrics, these norms are uniformly equivalent, compare Lemma
3.3
below, which will allow us to work with respect to the fixed norm
\|\cdot\|_{X_{m}(T,g_{0})}
in the relevant fixed point argument.

We furthermore note that we can always work with the spaces
\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
which we obtain by fixing coordinate charts in which the initial metric is smooth, and will see that this property is preserved along the flow. We can hence in the following always assume that the initial metric is an element of
\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

In the following we also use the convention that all constants are allowed to depend on the setting we consider, i.e. the fixed (topology of the) domain surface
\Sigma
, the fixed manifold
N\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and later on also on the fixed extensions
P_{\cdot}
and
P_{\cdot}^{\perp}
of the orthogonal projections onto
T_{\cdot}N
respectively
T_{\cdot}^{\perp}N
chosen in Remark
2.4
, without further mentioning this.

2.2.
Evolution of the metric component

We first consider the problem of solving the equation

(2.3)

\partial_{t}g=\frac{1}{2}P^{H}_{g}(k(v_{g},g))\text{ with }g(0)=g_{0}

for a given map
v\in X_{2}(T,g_{0})
,
k
the stress-energy tensor defined in (
1.5
). In Section
3
we will prove

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
m\geq 2
and any
\iota_{0}>0
there exist constants
C,\delta>0
so that the following holds true for any
T>0
, any
g_{0}\in\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
with
\inj(\Sigma,g_{0})\geq 2\iota_{0}
and the neighbourhood
{\mathcal{U}}
of
g_{0}
in
\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
described in Lemma
3.3
.

For any
v\in X_{2}(T)
there exists a unique solution
g_{v}\in C^{1}([0,T_{0}],\mathcal{M}^{m+1})
of (
2.3
) which is defined and so that
g_{v}(t)\in{\mathcal{U}}
, and hence in particular

(2.4)

\inj(\Sigma,g_{v}(t))\geq\frac{1}{2}\inj(\Sigma,g_{0})\geq\iota_{0},

at least on the interval
I=[0,T_{0}]
for
T_{0}:=\min(T,\delta/\hat{E})
,
\hat{E}
chosen so that

(2.5)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(v(t),g_{0})\leq\hat{E}.

Moreover, for every
t,\tilde{t}\in[0,T_{0}]
we have

(2.6)

\|\partial_{t}g_{v}(t)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\leq CE_{\frac{1}{2}}(v(t),g_{0}),

and

(2.7)

\|\partial_{t}g_{v}(t)-\partial_{t}g_{v}(\tilde{t})\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\leq C|t-\tilde{t}|\big[\hat{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{t}v_{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;H^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0}))}+\hat{E}\big].

Moreover, the map
v\mapsto g_{v}
is Lipschitz the sense that the estimates

(2.8)

\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{v}(t),g_{\tilde{v}}(t))\leq C\hat{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}t\|(v-\tilde{v})_{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;H^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0}))}

and

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}(g_{v}-g_{\tilde{v}})(t)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\hat{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(v-\tilde{v})_{g_{0}}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

\displaystyle+C\hat{E}^{\frac{3}{2}}t\|(v-\tilde{v})_{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;H^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0}))}

hold for all
t\in[0,T_{0}]
and all for all
v
and
\tilde{v}
which satisfy (
2.5
) for a given
\hat{E}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The neighbourhood
{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{U}}^{m+1}(g_{0})
in Lemma
3.3
is given by a ball in
\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
around
g_{0}
whose radius only depends on
\iota_{0}
and which is chosen so that metrics in
{\mathcal{U}}
are uniformly equivalent and induce uniformly equivalent
H^{m+1}
-norms. Hence the above estimates all remain valid also if some (or all) of the norms are computed with respect to another metric of
{\mathcal{U}}
, such as
g(t)
, instead of
g_{0}
.

We can apply the above lemma iteratively to see that unless the injectivity radius tends to zero as
t
approaches some time
T_{1}<T
, the solution of (
2.3
) is guaranteed to exist on the whole interval
[0,T]
where
v
is defined. Moreover, the above lemma shows that that the estimate

(2.10)

\|\partial_{t}g_{v}(t)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g(t))}\leq CE_{\frac{1}{2}}(v(t),g(t))

holds true with a constant
C
that only depends on a lower bound on the injectivity radius of
(\Sigma,g(t))
at that specific time
t
and the exponent
m
.

We note that for the proof of short-time existence of a solution of our coupled flow (
1.8
), a weaker version of the above lemma, in which the constants (and the size of the neighbourhood) are allowed to depend on the initial metric
g_{0}
itself, instead of just on
\iota_{0}
, would suffice, but that we choose to formulate Proposition
2.1
and (
2.10
) in the above more precise form to make them applicable also for the long-term analysis of the flow. Namely we will use that the above proposition immediately implies the following.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
(u,g)(t)
,
t\in[0,T]
, be any weak solution of our flow (
1.8
) with non-increasing energy. Then the metric component remains in the neighbourhood
{\mathcal{U}}(g(0))
described in Lemma
3.3
at least on a time interval of the form
[0,\min(T,T_{0})]
, for a number
T_{0}=T_{0}(\hat{E},\iota_{0})>0
that only depends on an upper bound on the initial energy and a lower bound
2\iota_{0}
on the injectivity radius of the initial metric.

2.3.
Regularized equation for the map component

Instead of directly proving a result similar to Proposition
2.1
also for the map component, we follow the approach of the second author from
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
, and first consider the simpler, and in particular linear, problem of determining a solution
u=u_{\varepsilon,g,v}
of the regularized equation

(2.11)

\partial_{t}u=-(\varepsilon+P_{v})\partial_{\nu_{g}}u_{g}\ \hbox{ on }{\partial\Sigma}

for given
\varepsilon>0
and given curves of maps
v:{\partial\Sigma}\to N_{\eta}
and metrics
g
.

Remark 2.4
.

Here and in the following we use fixed extensions
P_{\cdot},P_{\cdot}^{\perp}\in C_{b}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n})
of the orthogonal projections
P_{p}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to T_{p}N
,
P_{p}^{\perp}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to T_{p}^{\perp}N
which are chosen so that

(2.12)

P_{p}=P_{\pi(p)}\text{ and }P_{p}^{\perp}=P_{\pi(p)}^{\perp}\text{ for all }p\in N_{\eta}

on a small tubular neighbourhood
N_{\eta}
where
\eta>0
is chosen so that the nearest point projection is well defined and smooth on the closure
\bar{N}_{\eta}
of
N_{\eta}
. We note that this choice of extension in particular ensures that
P_{v}
and
P_{v}^{\perp}
are orthogonal projections with
P_{v}+P_{v}^{\perp}=\textit{Id}
for maps
v
whose image is contained in this tubular neighbourhood
N_{\eta}
.

We note that we should not expect solutions of the above equation (
2.11
) to remain in
N
even if
v
maps into
N
, since we no longer evaluate the projection
P
at the points
u(x,t)
and since the term
\varepsilon\partial_{\nu_{g}}u_{g}
in general will not be tangential to
N
. Thus, in order to later be able to apply fixed point arguments, we consider (
2.11
) not only for maps whose image is contained in
N
, but allow the maps
v
to take values in this (fixed) tubular neighbourhood
N_{\eta}
.

Since the term
-\varepsilon\partial_{\nu_{g}}u_{g}
compensates for the degeneracy introduced by the projection operator, we can use a Galerkin approximation to establish local existence for (
2.11
). While this proof of existence of solutions to the regularised equation (
2.11
) crucially uses that
\varepsilon>0
, we will be able to obtain
H^{m}
-bounds for these solutions which are independent of
\varepsilon
. This will be essential in later arguments where we will send
\varepsilon
to zero in order to obtain a solution of our original equation (
1.7
).

The relevant results on the regularised equation (
2.11
), which will be proven in Section
4.1
, can be summarised by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
m\geq 4
and
T>0
. Then for any given
u_{0}\in H^{m-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, any
g_{0}\in\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
, any
g\in C^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{M}^{m+1})
with
g(0)=g_{0}
, and any
v:[0,T]\times\partial\Sigma\rightarrow N_{\eta}
with
v\in X_{m}(T)
, there exists a unique solution
u=u_{\varepsilon,g,v}\in X_{m}(T)
of (
2.11
) with
u(0)=u_{0}
, and this solution is furthermore so that
u\in L^{2}([0,T];H^{m}({\partial\Sigma},g))

and so that the estimates

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|u_{g}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{m}(\Sigma,g)))}^{2}\leq e^{CT}\|(u_{0})_{g_{0}}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g_{0})}^{2}

and

(2.14)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,g))}^{2}\leq 2e^{CT}\|(u_{0})_{g_{0}}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g_{0})}^{2}

hold for a constant
C
that only depends on
m
and numbers
0<\iota_{0},M,\Lambda<\infty
, which are chosen so that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

(2.15)

\inj(g(t))\geq\iota_{0},\quad\|\partial_{t}g(t)\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))}\leq M,\quad\|(v(t))_{g(t)}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))}\leq\Lambda.

We stress in particular that
C
is
independent
of
\varepsilon
.

In addition to this result, we will also show that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, the map
(v,g)\mapsto u_{\varepsilon,v,g}
is Lipschitz in the sense described in Lemma
4.4
.

Combining these results on the map and metric component will then allow us to prove that for suitably small
T=T(\varepsilon,u_{0},g_{0},m)>0
, the map

\Psi=\Psi_{\varepsilon,u_{0},g_{0}}:v\mapsto u_{\varepsilon,v,g_{v}}

is a contraction on a suitably chosen subset of
X_{m}(T)
, see Lemma
4.5
for details. We can hence deduce that for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, the regularised system

(2.16)

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=-(\varepsilon+P_{u_{\varepsilon}})\partial_{\nu_{g_{\varepsilon}}}u_{g_{\varepsilon}},\quad\partial_{t}g_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}P_{g_{\varepsilon}}^{H}(k(u_{g_{\varepsilon}},g_{\varepsilon}))

can be solved at least on a small, a priori
\varepsilon
-
dependent
, time interval interval
[0,T_{\varepsilon}]
.

The fact that the estimate (
2.13
) on the evolution of the
H^{m}
norm of the map component is
\varepsilon
-independent, combined with the uniform control on the metric component obtained in Proposition
2.1
, will then allow us to establish that these solutions of the regularised system (
2.16
) indeed exist, and remain well controlled, on an
\varepsilon
-
independent
time interval. To be more precise, in Section
4.2
we will show the following proposition.

Proposition 2.6
.

For any
m\geq 4
, any
\iota_{0}>0
and any
\Lambda_{0}<\infty
, there exists a time
T>0
and a constant
C
so that for any initial metric
g_{0}\in\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
with
\inj(\Sigma,g_{0})\geq 2\iota_{0}
and any initial map
u_{0}\in H^{m-\frac{1}{2}}((\partial\Sigma,g_{0});N)
with
\|(u_{0})_{g_{0}}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\leq\Lambda_{0}
the following holds true.

For every
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
there exists a unique solution
(u_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})\in X_{m}(T)\times C^{1}([0,T];{\mathcal{M}}^{m+1})
of the regularised system (
2.16
) with
u_{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0}
,
g_{\varepsilon}(0)=g_{0}
and on this
\varepsilon
-independent interval
[0,T]
the map component remains bounded by

(2.17)

\|(u_{\varepsilon})_{g_{\varepsilon}}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g_{\varepsilon})}\leq 2\Lambda_{0}

while the metric component
g_{\varepsilon}
remains in the neighbourhood
{\mathcal{U}}\subset\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
of
g_{0}
obtained in Lemma
3.3
and is so that

(2.18)

\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1,1}([0,T];\mathcal{M}^{m+1})}\leq C.

As neither
T>0
nor
C
depend on
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, we can exploit the above uniform bounds on the solutions
(u_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})
of the regularised flow (
2.16
) and pass to the limit along suitable

to obtain a solution of our original problem on the interval
[0,T]
for
T=T(\Lambda_{0},\iota_{0},m)>0
as in the above Proposition
2.6
. Iteratively applying this short-time existence result which,
in view of Remark
2.3
and the fact that the energy is non-increasing along regular solutions of the flow,
yields a solution for an additional time interval whose length is bounded away from zero
unless the injectivity radius goes to zero or the

-norm goes to infinity,
we can hence deduce

Proposition 2.7
.

For every initial data
(u_{0},g_{0})\in H^{m-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Sigma;N)\times\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
,
m\geq 4
, there exists a solution
(u,g)\in X_{m,loc}(T_{m}^{*})\times C_{loc}^{1}([0,T_{m}^{*});\mathcal{M}^{m+1})
to the coupled flow (
1.8
) on a maximal time interval
[0,T_{m}^{*})
, where
T_{m}^{*}=\infty
unless either

(2.19)

\lim_{t\uparrow T_{m}^{*}}\inj(g(t))=0\text{ or }\lim_{t\uparrow T_{m}^{*}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))}=\infty.

In Section
5
we will establish that the

-norm remains indeed controlled for as long as there is no concentration of energy. Combined with the analysis of potential finite time singularities of the map component that is carried out in Section
5.5
, following
[

]
, this will complete the proof of our main Theorem

.

3.
Analysis of the metric component

We will first carry out the analysis of the metric component in detail in the more difficult case where our (orientable) domain surface
\Sigma
is neither a cylinder nor a disc. We will discuss the adaptions, and the significant simplifications, of this analysis for the case where
\Sigma
is cylinder later in Section
3.3
and again recall that in the case where
\Sigma
is a disc, the flow reduces to the Plateau flow with fixed metric analysed by the second author in
[
15
]
.

3.1.
Basic properties of set
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
of hyperbolic metrics

Let
\Sigma
be an orientable surface of general type and let
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
be the set of hyperbolic metrics for which the boundary curves are geodesics as considered above. In this section we collect some useful properties of these metrics which will be used not only in the analysis of horizontal curves as considered in Proposition
2.1
, but also later on in the paper. To lighten the notation we will often use the short-hand

(3.1)

\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}:=\{g\in\mathcal{M}:\inj(\Sigma,g)\geq\iota_{0}\}\text{ for }\iota_{0}>0

as well as
\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}^{m+1}:=\{g\in\mathcal{M}^{m+1}:\inj(\Sigma,g)\geq\iota_{0}\}
and note the following.

Remark 3.1
.

It is easy to see that the subset of moduli space which corresponds to metrics with
\inj(g)\geq\iota_{0}
,
\iota_{0}>0
any fixed number, is compact. Hence the usual Sobolev embedding theorems (for both functions and tensors) are all applicable on surfaces
(\Sigma,g)
,
g\in\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}
, with constants that do not depend on the specific choice of metric
g\in\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}
, but only on
\iota_{0}>0
(and of course the exponents of the involved Sobolev spaces and as usual the topology of
\Sigma
). We also stress that all Sobolev spaces and norms are to be understood with respect to the corresponding metric
g
(at the relevant time if
g
is time dependent) unless specified otherwise. We also note that while Sobolev space
H^{s}({\partial\Sigma},g)
and
H^{s}(\Sigma,g)
of any order
s
are defined for all metrics
g\in\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
, we will only ever consider such spaces for exponents
s\leq m
to ensure that the spaces that we obtain are independent of the choice of metric
g\in\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
and note that the dependence on
g
of the corresponding norms can be controlled as described in Lemma
3.8
.

We furthermore recall that for such hyperbolic metrics on
\Sigma
a neighbourhood
\mathcal{C}(\sigma_{i},g)
of each boundary curve
\sigma_{i}
of
\Sigma
is isometric to the hyperbolic cylinder

(3.2)

\big([0,X(\ell))\times S^{1},\rho_{\ell}^{2}(\mathrm{d}s^{2}+\mathrm{d}\theta^{2})\big),

for
\ell=L_{g}(\sigma_{i})
the length of
\sigma_{i}
and with
\sigma_{i}
corresponding to
\{0\}\times S^{1}
, where

(3.3)

X(\ell)=\tfrac{2\pi}{\ell}[\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\arctan(\sinh\tfrac{\ell}{2})],\qquad\rho_{\ell}(s)=\tfrac{\ell}{2\pi}[\cos(\tfrac{\ell s}{2\pi})]^{-1}.

By passing to the double
\hat{\Sigma}
and using the corresponding version of the collar lemma for all simple closed geodesics, or exploiting the above Remark
3.1
, it is furthermore easy to see that the lengths
L_{g}(\sigma_{i})
of the boundary curves are bounded both away from zero and from above uniformly for all metrics
g\in\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}
.

As a result, we obtain the following uniform control on the behaviour of the metric on the neighbourhood
\mathcal{C}({\partial\Sigma},g):=\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{C}(\sigma_{i},g)
of the boundary of such surfaces
(\Sigma,g)
,
g\in\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

For any
g\in\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}
,
\iota_{0}>0
any given number, estimates of the form

(3.4)

0<c\leq X(\ell_{i})\leq C\text{ and }0<c\leq\rho_{\ell_{i}}\leq C\text{ on }[0,X(\ell_{i})),\quad\ell_{i}:=L_{g}(\sigma_{i})

are valid for constants
c>0
and
C>0
that only depend on
\iota_{0}
(and as usual the topology of
\Sigma
), while for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
the estimate
\|\rho_{\ell_{i}}\|_{C^{k}(\mathcal{C}(\sigma_{i},g))}\leq C
holds true for a constant that additionally depends on
k
.

For each such surface, we can hence in particular choose a cut-off function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}({\partial\Sigma},g))
with
\varphi\equiv 1
in a neighbourhood of
{\partial\Sigma}
which is so that
\|\varphi\|_{C^{k}(\mathcal{C}({\partial\Sigma},g))}\leq C=C(\iota_{0},k)
and so that
\text{dist}_{g}(\text{supp}(\nabla\varphi),{\partial\Sigma})\geq c(\iota_{0})>0
.

On occasion we will also want to extend the unit tangent
\tau_{g}
and the unit normal
\nu_{g}
from
({\partial\Sigma},g)
to this collar neighbourhood and will always do this by choosing the vector fields which are given in collar coordinates as the fixed rescalings of the standard coordinate vector fields

(3.5)

\tau:=\rho_{\ell_{i}}(0)^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\text{ and }\nu:=-\rho_{\ell_{i}}(0)^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}.

We note that while this does not yield vector fields with unit length, this choice of extension is convenient since it implies
\partial_{\nu}^{2}U+\partial_{\tau}^{2}U=0
for any function
U
which is harmonic with respect to
g
.

We note that for (orientable) surfaces
\Sigma
different from the disc or the cylinder, it has already been pointed out by Tromba in
[
16
]
that the map
g\mapsto H(g)
is not integrable in the sense of Frobenius’s theorem; that is, there exists no submanifold
\mathcal{M}_{H}
of
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
(or of
\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}^{m+1}(\Sigma)
for any finite
m\geq 2
) whose tangent spaces
T_{g}\mathcal{M}_{H}
coincide with the corresponding horizontal spaces

(3.6)

H(g):=\{h\in\Gamma^{sym}(T^{*}\Sigma\otimes T^{*}\Sigma):\text{tr}_{g}(h)=0,\quad\Div_{g}(k)=0,\quad h(\nu_{g},\tau_{g})|_{\partial\Sigma}=0\}.

In practice this means that curves which are horizontal, i.e. whose velocity
\partial_{t}g
at each time is given by an element of the corresponding horizontal space
H(g(t))
and which start at a given initial metric
g_{0}
will not be constrained to a finite dimensional manifold. By contrast, in the case where
\Sigma
is a cylinder such horizontal curves are constrained to an explicit
1
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathcal{M}
, compare Section
3.3
below.

We also stress that the equation for the metric component of our coupled flow (
1.8
) cannot be viewed as an equation on Teichmüller space but that it is important to keep track of the metric
g(t)
itself as different representatives
\tilde{g}(t)=f_{t}^{*}g(t)
of the same curve in Teichmüller space lead to different PDEs for the map component.

For the proof of short-time existence we will be working with metrics which are contained in a small neighbourhood of a given
g_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{m+1}
which is chosen as in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
\iota_{0}>0
and
m\geq 2
there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(\iota_{0},m)>0
so that for each
g_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{2\iota_{0}}^{m+1}

all metrics in the neighbourhood

(3.7)

{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{U}}_{g_{0},r_{0}}^{m+1}:=\{g\in\mathcal{M}^{m+1}:\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{0},g)\leq r_{0}\}

of
g_{0}
are uniformly equivalent in the sense that

(3.8)

\frac{1}{4}g\leq\tilde{g}\leq 4g\text{ for all }g,\tilde{g}\in{\mathcal{U}}

and hence in particular so that

(3.9)

\inj(\Sigma,g)\geq\frac{1}{2}\inj(\Sigma,g_{0})\geq\iota_{0}\qquad\text{ for all }g\in{\mathcal{U}}.

Furthermore, all metrics in
{\mathcal{U}}
induce uniformly equivalent Sobolev norms in the sense that there exists a constant
C=C(m,\iota_{0})
so that the estimates

(3.10)

\|v\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma,g)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma,\tilde{g})}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\|h\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma,g)}\leq C\|h\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma,\tilde{g})}

hold true for all
g,\tilde{g}\in{\mathcal{U}}
, all
0\leq k\leq m+1
, all functions
v\in H^{k}(\Sigma,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and all
(0,2)
-tensors
h
on
\Sigma
whose coefficients are in
H^{k}
.

Remark 3.4
.

As explained in Section
3.4
, such metrics also yield uniformly equivalent half-energies, namely are so that the estimate

(3.11)

E_{\frac{1}{2}}(v,g)\leq CE_{\frac{1}{2}}(v,\tilde{g})\text{ for all }g,\tilde{g}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{g_{0},r_{0}}^{m+1}

holds true for all
v\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial\Sigma};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for a constant
C=C(\iota_{0})
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be as defined above for a number
r_{0}>0
that we determine below. Given any
g_{1}\in{\mathcal{U}}
we can consider a curve of metrics
g(t),t\in[0,1]
, with
g(0)=g_{0}
and
g(1)=g_{1}
so that
\int_{0}^{1}\|\partial_{t}g(t)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g(t))}\mathrm{d}t<2r_{0}
and we let
t_{0}\in(0,1]
be the maximal time so that (
3.9
) holds for all metrics
g(t)
with
t\in[0,t_{0}]
.

As we have pointwise bounds of

(3.12)

|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|X|_{g}^{2}|=|(\partial_{t}g)(X,X)|\leq|\partial_{t}g|_{g}|X|_{g}^{2}\leq\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g)}|X|_{g}^{2}

for any vector field
X
on
\Sigma
, and as the Sobolev-embedding theorem
H^{2}(\Sigma,g)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g)
is valid with the same constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(\iota_{0})
for all metrics
g(t)
with
t\in[0,t_{0}]
, we can integrate the resulting estimate of
|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\log(|X|_{g}^{2})|\leq\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g)}\leq C_{0}\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g)}
over any subinterval of
[0,t_{0}]
and use that
\int_{0}^{1}\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g)}<2r_{0}
to deduce that

(3.13)

g(t)\leq e^{2C_{0}r_{0}}g(\tilde{t})\leq 2g(\tilde{t})\text{ for all }t,\tilde{t}\in[0,t_{0}],

where the last inequality holds provided
r_{0}>0
is small enough.

We conclude that the lengths of any curve
\sigma
in
\Sigma
with respect to two such metrics are related by
L_{g(t)}(\sigma)\leq\sqrt{2}L_{g(\tilde{t})}(\sigma)
. Since the injectivity radius of hyperbolic surfaces
(\Sigma,g)
is given by half of the length of the shortest curve which is either closed and non-contractible or so that it connects two points of
{\partial\Sigma}
, we can hence deduce that
\inj(\Sigma,g(t))\leq\sqrt{2}\inj(\Sigma,g(\tilde{t}))
for all
t,\tilde{t}\in[0,t_{0}]
. In particular,

\inj(\Sigma,g(t_{0}))\geq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\inj(\Sigma,g_{0})>\tfrac{1}{2}\inj(\Sigma,g_{0}),

so as
t_{0}
was chosen as the maximal number
t_{0}\leq 1
so that (
3.9
) holds on
[0,t_{0}]
we must have
t_{0}=1
.

The estimates on
g(t)
and
\inj(\Sigma,g(t))
obtained above are hence in particular applicable for
g_{1}=g(1)
which immediately yields the first two claims (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) of the lemma.

We can then use that pointwise estimates of the form

(3.14)

|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\nabla_{g(t)}^{k}v|_{g(t)}\leq C\sum_{i+j\leq k}|\nabla_{g(t)}^{i}\partial_{t}g(t)|_{g(t)}\cdot|\nabla_{g(t)}^{j}v|_{g(t)},\qquad C=C(k)

hold true for every
k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
, for all sufficiently smooth
v:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and every curve of hyperbolic metrics. As (
3.9
) and Remark
3.1
ensure that the standard Sobolev embeddings
H^{2}(\Sigma,g)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g)
and
H^{1}(\Sigma,g)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Sigma,g)
are applicable with the same constant for all
g\in{\mathcal{U}}
, and as the change in the volume element is controlled by
\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g)}
, we can hence bound

(3.15)

|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|v\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma,g))}^{2}|\leq C\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g)}^{2}\|v\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma,g)}^{2}\text{ for some }C=C(m,\iota_{0},\Sigma)

for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,m+1\}
and all functions
v\in H^{k}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
as claimed. Integrated over

this immediately yields the claimed equivalence of norms of functions, and we note that the same argument also applies for tensors.
∎

3.2.
Analysis of horizontal curves of hyperbolic metrics

The key tool needed for the proof of Proposition

is the following Lipschitz property of the projection

.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
\iota_{0}>0
and
m\geq 2
, there exist constants

and
r_{1}\in(0,r_{0})
,
r_{0}>0
as in the above Lemma
3.3
, so that for any
g_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{2\iota_{0}}^{m+1}
the following assertions hold true for every metric

in the neighbourhood
{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{U}}_{g_{0},r_{1}}^{m+1}:=\{g\in\mathcal{M}^{m+1}:\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{0},g)\leq r_{1}\}
of
g_{0}
in
\mathcal{M}^{m+1}

Let

be the

-orthogonal projection from the space

of symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors on
\Sigma

-norm to the horizontal space

\|P_{g}^{H}(h)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

as well as

(3.17)

\|P_{g}^{H}(h)-P_{\tilde{g}}^{H}(h)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\leq C\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g,\tilde{g})\|h\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0})},

and for any
C^{1}
-curve of metrics

in

(3.18)

\|\partial_{\varepsilon}P_{g_{\varepsilon}}^{H}(h)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\leq C\|\partial_{\varepsilon}g_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\|h\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}.

We note that (
3.16
) in particular ensures that the projection has a unique continuous extension to the space
\Gamma^{sym}_{L^{1}(\Sigma,g)}(T^{*}\Sigma\otimes T^{*}\Sigma)
of tensors with finite

norm, and remark that the above estimates remain valid if we compute some or all of the norms with respect to

ensures that the metrics in

and the induced

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

As in the introduction we let
\hat{\Sigma}
be the Schottky double of
\Sigma
and let
\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{m+1}
be the manifold of all hyperbolic metrics on
\hat{\Sigma}
whose coefficients are in the Sobolev space

with respect to the fixed coordinate charts on
\hat{\Sigma}
. Given
\hat{g}\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{m+1}
we let
\hat{H}(\hat{g})=\hat{H}(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{g})
be the space of all symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors on
\hat{\Sigma}
which are trace free and divergence free and let

be the

-orthogonal projection from the space of
(0,2)
-tensors to
\hat{H}
.

We now make use of Lemma 2.9 from
[
10
]
, which ensures that for every
\hat{g}\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{m+1}
there exists a neighbourhood
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
of
\hat{g}
in
\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{m+1}
and a constant

so that

satisfies the analogues of the claims on

made in Lemma
3.5
. While the results in
[

]
are stated using a different notion of

norm, there computed using a fixed set of coordinate chart, we note that for each
fixed

this norm is equivalent to the

norm which is computed using the Levi-Cività connection we use in the present paper. The results of
[
10
]
hence guarantee that there exists a neighbourhood
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
of
\hat{g}_{0}
and a constant

so that for any

-tensor
\hat{h}
on
\hat{\Sigma}
, any

and any

-curve of metrics

in

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|P_{\hat{g}_{1}}^{\hat{H}}(\hat{h})\|_{H^{m+1}(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{g}_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\hat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{g}_{0})},

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|P_{\hat{g}_{1}}^{\hat{H}}(\hat{h})-P_{\hat{g}_{2}}^{\hat{H}}(\hat{h})\|_{H^{m+1}(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{g}_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(\hat{g}_{1},\hat{g}_{2})\|\hat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{g}_{0})},

as well as

(3.21)

\|\partial_{\varepsilon}P_{\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}}^{\hat{H}}(\hat{h})\|_{H^{m+1}(\hat{\Sigma},g_{0})}\leq C\|\partial_{\varepsilon}\hat{g}_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{m+1}(\hat{\Sigma},g_{0})}\|\hat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{g}_{0})};

compare also Proposition 2.1 of
[
10
]
. We now observe that since these claims are invariant under pull-back by diffeomorphism, and since the subset of moduli space that corresponds to metrics with

is compact, we indeed obtain that the above estimates (
3.19
)-(
3.21
) hold true on a ball
\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{\hat{g}_{0},\hat{r}_{1}}:=\{\hat{g}\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}^{m}:\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(\hat{g},\hat{g}_{0})\leq\hat{r}_{1}\}
whose radius
\hat{r}_{1}>0
only depends on

and a lower bound
2\hat{\iota}_{0}
on
\inj(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{g}_{0})
and with a constant
C=C(\hat{\iota}_{0},m)
.

To deduce the claims of Lemma
3.5
from these facts we can now argue as follows: given any
h\in\Gamma^{sym}(T^{*}\Sigma\otimes T^{*}\Sigma)
we extend

to the disjoint union
\hat{\Sigma}=\Sigma\times\{-1,1\}
by simply setting
\hat{h}(p,\pm 1)=h(p)
for each

. Away from
\partial\Sigma
, and hence almost everywhere, this yields a well defined tensor
\hat{h}
on the double
\hat{\Sigma}
which is even with respect to
{\partial\Sigma}
in the sense that

(3.22)

\hat{h}(p,1)(w_{1},w_{2})=\hat{h}(p,-1)(w_{1},w_{2})\text{ for }p\in\Sigma\setminus{\partial\Sigma}\text{ and }w_{1,2}\in T_{p}\Sigma{\hat{=}}T_{(p,\pm 1)}\hat{\Sigma}.

In this way we can extend any
L^{2}
-tensor field on
\Sigma
to an
L^{2}
-tensor field on the double with even symmetry. We do not claim that for smooth
h
this extension always yields a smooth, or even just continuous, tensor field
\hat{h}
as some of the components of
\hat{h}
can jump across
{\partial\Sigma}
in
\hat{\Sigma}
.

To see this, we can use the description and coordinates of the collar neighbourhoods
\mathcal{C}(\sigma_{i})
of the boundary curves
\sigma_{i}
which we recalled in Section
3.1
. On the corresponding neighbourhood
\mathcal{C}(\sigma_{i})\times\{-1,+1\}/\sim
of
\sigma_{i}
in the double
(\hat{\Sigma},\hat{g})
we can then work in the coordinates that we obtain by identifying
((s,\theta),\pm 1)
with
(\pm s,\theta)
. The components of the extended tensor
\hat{h}
in these coordinates at
(\pm s,\theta)
are so that
\hat{h}_{ss}(\pm s,\theta)=h_{ss}(s,\theta)
and
\hat{h}_{\theta\theta}(\pm s,\theta)=h_{\theta\theta}(s,\theta)
are continuous across
{\partial\Sigma}
whenever
h
is continuous, but so that
\hat{h}_{s\theta}(\pm s,\theta)=\pm h_{s\theta}(s,\theta)
jumps unless
h_{s\theta}
vanishes on
\partial\Sigma
, that is, unless
h(\nu_{g},\tau_{g})|_{\partial\Sigma}\equiv 0
.

In the special case where we extend the metric tensor
g
itself in this way, the resulting tensor
\hat{g}
is not only continuous but indeed smooth across
{\partial\Sigma}
since
\rho_{\ell}(s)=\rho_{\ell}(-s)
.

Similarly, the extension of any tensor
h\in H(g)
yields an element of
\hat{H}(\hat{g})

h=\text{Re}(\Psi)
for a holomorphic quadratic differential
\Psi
on
\Sigma
which is real on the boundary in the sense that in collar coordinates
(s,\theta)
near any
\sigma_{i}
we have
\Psi=\psi(\mathrm{d}s+i\mathrm{d}\theta)^{2}
for a holomorphic

which is real on the circle
\{0\}\times S^{1}
. The extended tensor
\hat{h}
is then given by
\text{Re}(\hat{\psi}(\mathrm{d}s+i\mathrm{d}\theta)^{2})
for
\hat{\psi}
defined by
\text{Re}(\hat{\psi}(\pm s,\theta))=\text{Re}(\psi(s,\theta))
and
\text{Im}(\hat{\psi}(\pm s,\theta))=\pm\text{Im}(\psi(s,\theta))
. As
\text{Im}(\psi(0,\theta))=0
on
{\partial\Sigma}
this function
\hat{\psi}
is holomorphic on
\hat{\Sigma}
, and hence in particular smooth across
\partial\Sigma
, and the extended tensor
\hat{h}=\text{Re}(\hat{\psi}\mathrm{d}z^{2})
is an element of
\hat{H}(\hat{g})
as elements of this space can be equivalently characterised as real parts of holomorphic quadratic differentials.

We now claim that to project a given
h\in\Gamma^{sym}_{L^{2}}(T^{*}\Sigma\otimes T^{*}\Sigma)
onto

we can equivalently first extend
h
to the even

-tensor
\hat{h}
on the double as described above, then project this tensor onto
\hat{H}
, and, finally, again restrict
\hat{h}
to
\Sigma
. That is, we claim that

(3.23)

P_{g}^{H}(h)=P_{\hat{g}}^{\hat{H}}(\hat{h})|_{\Sigma}.

To see that this holds true, we split
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{even}\oplus\hat{H}_{odd}
into the tensors with even or odd symmetry with respect to
\partial\Sigma
, respectively, that is, into tensors on
\hat{\Sigma}
corresponding to tensors
h
on
\Sigma\times\{-1,1\}
with
h(p,1)=h(p,-1)
for all
p
or
h(p,1)=-h(p,-1)
for all

, respectively. As discussed above, the extension of any element of
H(g)
yields an element of
\hat{H}_{even}
and conversely the restriction of elements of
\hat{H}_{even}
to
\Sigma
is contained in
H(g)
since the
(s,\theta)
component of such smooth even tensors on
\hat{\Sigma}
must vanish on
{\partial\Sigma}
. As the extension
\hat{h}
of any

is even we hence deduce that

. Combined with the fact that being even forces the extended tensor
\hat{h}
to be

-orthogonal to
\hat{H}_{odd}
, this gives (
3.23

As choosing
r_{1}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{r}_{1}
ensures that the extension of metrics

is contained in the neighbourhood

of

on which (

from these properties of the projection

that were proven in
[

]
.
∎

Based on these Lipschitz estimates we can now deduce

Lemma 3.6

For any

and
\iota_{0}>0
there exist constants

so that the following holds true. Let

be any metric with

, let

(3.24)

of

where both Lemmas

and

of (

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Let
g_{0}
,
{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{U}}_{g_{0},r_{1}}^{m+1}
,
h
and
M_{1}
be as in the lemma and let

for
\delta_{1}>0
to be determined below.

Given any curve of metrics

we define

(3.27)

G_{g}(t)=g_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}P^{H}_{g}(h(t^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

and note that a sufficiently small choice of
\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}(\iota_{0},m)>0
ensures that
G_{g}(t)\in{\mathcal{U}}
for all
t\in[0,T_{1}]
as the estimate (
3.16
) of Lemma
3.5
allows us to bound

(3.28)

\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{0},G_{g}(t))\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|h(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\leq CM_{1}t

for as long as

is in
{\mathcal{U}}
, hence ensuring that
\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{0},G_{g}(t))
remains strictly less than

on all of
[0,T_{1}]
if
\delta_{1}<C^{-1}r_{1}
.

To obtain the desired solution of (
3.24

\delta_{1}
ensures that this map

, which we have just established is mapping

to itself, is a contraction.

Given
g_{1,2}\in C^{0}([0,T_{1}],{\mathcal{U}})
we can always choose metrics

is continuously differentiable, interpolates between

and

and is so that

, while for each

is continuous away from a finite set of (

.

As the estimate (
3.28
) is applicable also for such piecewise continuous curves of metrics
t\mapsto g(t)\in{\mathcal{U}}
, the curves of metrics
t\mapsto G_{s}(t):=G_{g_{s}}(t)
,

are again contained in

for all

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}G_{s}(t)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,G_{s}(t))}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{s}P_{g_{s}(t^{\prime})}(h(t^{\prime}))\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq CM_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{s}g_{s}(t^{\prime})\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

for every

and

and for a constant

.
Upon integrating over

we hence deduce that

\sup_{[0,T_{1}]}\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(G_{g_{1}},G_{g_{2}})\leq C\delta_{1}\sup_{[0,T_{1}]}\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{1},g_{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{[0,T_{1}]}\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{1},g_{2}),

where the last estimate holds
after reducing

to itself. As

is defined as a closed ball of a complete metric space, we hence obtain the existence of a unique solution

3.24

To prove the second part of the theorem we can argue similarly, except that we now consider the family

Thanks to (
3.16
) and (
3.29
), we can furthermore bound

\|\partial_{t}g_{v}(t)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\leq C\|k(v_{g(t)},g(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}\leq CE_{\frac{1}{2}}(v(t),g_{0})

as claimed in (
2.6
), while a combination of (
3.16
), (
3.18
), (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) and (
2.5
) yields

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}g_{v}(t)-\partial_{t}g_{v}(\tilde{t})\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\big(P_{g_{v}(t)}-P_{g_{v}(\tilde{t})}\big)(k(v_{g_{v}},g_{v})(t))\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

\displaystyle+\|P_{g_{v}(\tilde{t})}\big((k(v_{g_{v}},g_{v})(t)-k(v_{g_{v}},g_{v})(\tilde{t})\big)\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{v}(t),g_{v}(\tilde{t}))\hat{E}+C\hat{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(v(t)-v(\tilde{t}))_{g_{0}}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

for all
t,\tilde{t}\in[0,T_{0}]
. As the estimate (
2.6
), which we have proven above, ensures that

\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{v}(t),g_{v}(\tilde{t}))\leq C\hat{E}|t-\tilde{t}|,

this immediately yields the second a priori bound (
2.7
) claimed in the lemma.

Given
v
and
\tilde{v}
which satisfy (
2.5
) for some given
\hat{E}
, we can then combine (
3.26
) with (
3.30
) to see that for any
t\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,t]}\text{dist}(g_{v},g_{\tilde{v}})

\displaystyle\leq Ct\|k(v_{g_{v}},g_{v})-k(\tilde{v}_{g_{\tilde{v}}},g_{\tilde{v}})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t];L^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq Ct\hat{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(v-\tilde{v})_{g_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0}))}+Ct\hat{E}\sup_{[0,t]}\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{v},g_{\tilde{v}}).

After reducing
\delta>0
if necessary to ensure that
CT_{0}\hat{E}\leq C\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we can absorb the last term into the left hand side resulting in the claimed Lipschitz estimate (
2.8
).

Similarly, (
3.17
), (
3.16
) and (
3.30
) allow us to bound

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}(g_{v}-g_{\tilde{v}})\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(P_{g_{v}}-P_{g_{\tilde{v}}})(k(v_{g_{v}},g_{v}))\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

\displaystyle+\|P_{g_{\tilde{v}}}(k(v_{g_{v}},g_{v})-k(\tilde{v}_{g_{\tilde{v}}},g_{\tilde{v}}))\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\text{dist}_{H^{m+1}}(g_{v},g_{\tilde{v}})\hat{E}+C\hat{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(v-\tilde{v})_{g_{0}}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma,g_{0})},

which, when combined with (
2.8
), yields the final claim (
2.9
) of the lemma.
∎

3.3.
Simplified analysis of the metric components for the cylinder

We finally turn to the case where
\Sigma
is a cylinder where the above arguments simplify significantly since the horizontal space
H(g)
is not only one-dimensional, but also so that
g\mapsto H(g)
is an
integrable
distribution on the space
\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
of flat unit area metrics with geodesic boundary curves. This ensures that every horizontal curve that starts at an initial metric
g_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
evolves in a one-dimensional integral manifold
\mathcal{M}_{H}(g_{0})\subset\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)
. We can hence either prove Proposition
2.1
directly, using that the evolution of the metric reduces to an ODE, or simply observe that the above proof still applies but that it can be simplified in the following way.

Given a flat unit cylinder
(\Sigma,g_{0})
with geodesic boundary curves, we can always introduce coordinates
(x,\theta)
in which

(3.32)

\Sigma=[-\pi,\pi]\times S^{1}\text{ and }g_{0}=g_{a_{0}}:=(2\pi)^{-2}(a_{0}^{-1}\mathrm{d}x^{2}+a_{0}\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}),

where
a_{0}
is determined by the length
\ell_{0}=L_{g_{0}}(\sigma_{1})=L_{g_{0}}(\sigma_{2})
of the boundary curves via
a_{0}=\ell_{0}^{2}
and related to the injectivity radius by
\inj(g_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\min(a_{0},a_{0}^{-1})^{1/2}
. It can then be easily checked that the one-dimensional submanifold

\mathcal{M}_{H}=\{g_{a}:=(2\pi)^{-2}(a^{-1}\mathrm{d}x^{2}+a\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}):a>0\}

of
\mathcal{M}
is an integral manifold of
g\mapsto H(g)
, i.e. that
T_{g}\mathcal{M}_{H}=H(g)
for all
g\in\mathcal{M}_{H}
. One way to see this is to set
s:=a^{-1}x
to obtain coordinates
(s,\theta)
in which
g_{a}
is conformal to the standard cylindrical metric, namely given by
a(2\pi)^{-2}(\mathrm{d}s^{2}+\mathrm{d}\theta^{2})
, and use that in these coordinates each horizontal tensor can be written as
h=\text{Re}(\psi(s+i\theta)(\mathrm{d}s+i\mathrm{d}\theta)^{2})
for a function
\psi
which is holomorphic and real on the boundary curves and hence given by
\psi\equiv c
for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus, each such
h
is given by

h=c(\mathrm{d}s^{2}-\mathrm{d}\theta^{2})=c(a^{-2}\mathrm{d}x^{2}-\mathrm{d}\theta^{2})=-c(2\pi)^{2}\partial_{a}g_{a}.

Instead of working with metrics
g
in a full
H^{m+1}
neighbourhood of
g_{0}
in
\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
, the existence of such an integral manifold means that it suffices to consider metrics in a neighbourhood
{\mathcal{U}}(g_{a_{0}})=\{g_{a},\frac{1}{2}a_{0}\leq a\leq 2a_{0}\}
of
g_{0}=g_{a_{0}}
in this explicit one-dimensional set. These metrics trivially satisfy the equivalence properties stated in Lemma
3.3
while the properties of the projection onto
H(g)
for metrics
g\in{\mathcal{U}}(g_{0})
are an immediate consequence of the explicit formula

\displaystyle P_{g_{a}}^{H}(h)

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{a}g_{a}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{a})}^{-2}\langle h,\partial_{a}g_{a}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{a})}\partial_{a}g_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{2}}{2(2\pi)^{4}}\langle h,a^{-2}\mathrm{d}x^{2}-\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{a})}\left(a^{-2}\mathrm{d}x^{2}-\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}\right).

The proof of Proposition
2.1
in this simpler case can hence again be obtained based on a fixed point argument as carried out above.

3.4.
Further properties of metrics in
\mathcal{M}
and the associated norms and harmonic extensions

In the next section we will often need to switch back and forth between norms computed with respect to different metrics. For this, it is helpful to record that while a change of the metric
g
leads to a change of the harmonic extensions and of the Sobolev norms of maps, these changes are well controlled along horizontal curves of metrics as considered here, as we can combine the estimates on the
H^{m+1}
norm for the velocity of such curves obtained above with the following basic lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

For any integer
m\geq 2
and any
\iota_{0}>0
, there exists a constant
C
so that the following holds true for any curve
g\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}^{m+1})
.

For any
j\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
and any
v\in H^{j}(\Sigma)
we can bound

(3.33)

|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla_{g}^{j}v\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g(t))}^{2}|\leq C\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))}\|v\|_{H^{j}(\Sigma,g(t))}^{2}

while for maps
v\in H^{j+\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma)
we can also bound

(3.34)

|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla_{g}^{j}v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,g(t))}^{2}|\leq C\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{m+1}(\Sigma,g(t))}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\|\nabla_{g}^{i}v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,g(t))}^{2}.

Furthermore, the change of the harmonic extension of any
f\in H^{m-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Sigma)
is bounded by

(3.35)

\|\partial_{t}[f_{g(t)}]\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))}\leq C\|\partial_{t}g(t)\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))}\|f_{g(t)}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))},

and the change of the half-energy by

(3.36)

|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(f,g(t))|\leq C\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g(t))}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(f,g(t)).

In particular, if
M
is chosen so that
\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g)}\leq M
on
[0,T]
then we can bound

(3.37)

\|f_{g(t_{0})}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t_{0}))}^{2}\leq e^{C|t_{1}-t_{0}|M}\|f_{g(t_{1})}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t_{1}))}^{2}\text{ for all }t_{0,1}\in[0,T]

as well as

(3.38)

\|v\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t_{0}))}^{2}\leq e^{C|t_{0}-t_{1}|M}\|v\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t_{1}))}^{2}\text{ for all }t_{0,1}\in[0,T].

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

We first note that the estimates (
3.33
) and (
3.34
) follow from the pointwise estimate (
3.14
), the fact that the change of the volume form
\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}v_{g(t)}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\partial_{t}g)\mathrm{d}v_{g}
is controlled by
\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}
and that
H^{m}(\Sigma,g)
embeds into both
W^{m-1,4}(\Sigma,g)
and
L^{\infty}(\Sigma,g)
.

The estimate (
3.36
) for the half-energy is a direct consequence of the formula (
1.4
) for the variation of the half-energy and the estimate (
3.29
) on the stress-energy tensor. It thus remains to control the evolution of the harmonic extension. For this, we can use that
\partial_{t}(f_{g(t)})=0
on
\partial\Sigma
to apply standard
L^{2}
elliptic estimates and so bound

(3.39)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}(f_{g(t)})\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta_{g(t)}\partial_{t}(f_{g(t)})\|_{H^{m-2}(\Sigma,g(t))}

\displaystyle=C\|-\partial_{\varepsilon}\Delta_{g(t+\varepsilon)}f_{g(t)}\big|_{\varepsilon=0}\|_{H^{m-2}(\Sigma,g(t))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}\|f_{g(t)}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))}

where the second step follows since
\Delta_{g(t)}f_{g(t)}=0
for all
t
.
∎

4.
Analysis of the map component

In this section we complete the analysis of the map component that is required to establish the existence and properties of solutions of our coupled flow (
1.7
) for initial maps
u_{0}\in H^{m-\frac{1}{2}}({\partial\Sigma};N)
,
m\geq 4
, claimed in Proposition
2.6
. As outlined in Section
2
we will proceed in several steps, first considering the linearised and regularised equation (
2.11
) for given curves of maps
v
and metrics
g
, see Section
4.1
, then the non-linear, but still regularised, coupled system of equations (
2.16
), see Section
4.2
, before using the obtained uniform control on the solutions of these auxiliary problems to deduce the desired short-time existence results for our flow (
1.8
) in Section
4.3
.

4.1.
Analysis of the linearised and regularised equation (
2.11
) for
u

The main goal of this section is to establish the claims made in Proposition
2.5
about the existence and properties of solutions of (
2.11
) for given curves of maps
v:[0,T]\times{\partial\Sigma}\to N_{\eta}
and metrics
g
as described in this proposition. For this it will be convenient to write equation (
2.11
) for short as

\partial_{t}u=Q_{\varepsilon,v}\partial_{\nu_{g}}u_{g}\text{ for }Q_{\varepsilon,v}=-(\varepsilon\text{Id}+P_{v}),

where we recall that the chosen extension of the projection is so that
P_{\cdot}=P_{\pi(\cdot)}
on the closure of
N_{\eta}
. This in particular ensures that
P_{\cdot}
is an orthogonal projection on
\bar{N}_{\eta}
and hence allows us to view
x\mapsto(\varepsilon\text{Id}+P_{x})
as a smooth map from
\bar{N}_{\eta}
into the set of invertible matrices and to use that also
x\mapsto(\varepsilon\text{Id}+P_{x})^{-1}
is smooth on
\bar{N}_{\eta}.

If it is clear from the context what
\varepsilon
and
v
are we will drop the indices and simply write
Q=Q_{\varepsilon,v}
to lighten the notation and similarly, we will often drop references to the metric for geometric quantities and operators, such as volume forms
\mathrm{d}v_{g}=\mathrm{d}v
,
\mathrm{d}s_{g}=\mathrm{d}s
and gradients
\nabla_{g}=\nabla
, in situations where we only work with one, possibly time dependent, curve of metrics and continue to use the convention that all Sobolev spaces of maps from
\Sigma
(or
{\partial\Sigma}
) and their norms are to be considered with respect to
g
at the relevant time unless indicated otherwise.

As in
[
15
]
, we want to use Galerkin’s method with Steklov eigenfunctions to establish the existence of solutions to this linear equation (
2.11
). To use this method, we first consider the problem for a time-independent metric
g
, to allow us to work with time-independent finite dimensional spaces of functions. To simplify the proof of the regularity of weak solutions, compare Lemma
4.2
, we will furthermore initially also only consider maps
v
which are independent of time, and will later remove both of these restrictions by using a time-discretisation argument.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
,
T>0
,
g\in\mathcal{M}^{3}
and let
v\in H^{1/2}({\partial\Sigma};N_{\eta})
. Then for any
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
there exists a unique weak solution
u:[0,T]\times{\partial\Sigma}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of (
2.11
) with

u\in H^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\partial\Sigma))\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}(\partial\Sigma))\text{ and }u_{g}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Sigma))

whose
L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})
trace at
t=0
is given by
u_{0}
. Furthermore, along any weak solution with this regularity we have

(4.1)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{\frac{1}{2}}(u,g)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

As
g
is independent of time, we can consider a fixed orthonormal basis
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\ldots
of
L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})
which consists of Steklov eigenfunctions corresponding to non-decreasing eigenvalues
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots
, i.e. functions
\varphi_{j}:{\partial\Sigma}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\big[(\varphi_{j})_{g}\big]=\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}\text{ on }\partial\Sigma,

and work with the
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
-valued spans

V_{\ell}:=\big\{u:{\partial\Sigma}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:u=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\varphi_{j}b_{j}\text{ for some }b_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\big\},\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}.

For any
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we consider the functions
u_{\ell}(t)(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}a_{j,\ell}(t)\varphi_{j}(x)\in C^{\infty}([0,T];V_{\ell})
, and their harmonic extensions
U_{\ell}=(u_{\ell})_{g}
, which are chosen so that
u_{\ell}(0)
is given as the
L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})
-orthonormal projection of
u_{0}
onto
V_{\ell}
and which are so that

(4.2)

\int_{\partial\Sigma}\partial_{t}u_{\ell}\cdot w\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\partial\Sigma}Q(\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell})\cdot w\mathrm{d}s\ \text{ for all }w\in V_{\ell}

holds at each time in
[0,T]
. As
\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}(t)|_{{\partial\Sigma}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}a_{j,\ell}(t)\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}\in V_{\ell}
at each such time, and as the
\varphi_{j}
are orthonormal, we can obtain these functions
u_{\ell}
simply by solving the corresponding system of ODEs

\partial_{t}a_{j,\ell}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}\int_{\partial\Sigma}Q(a_{i,\ell})\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}\mathrm{d}s,\ 0\leq j\leq\ell,

with the initial condition

a_{j,\ell}(0)=a_{j}(u_{0}):=\int_{\partial\Sigma}u_{0}\varphi_{j}\mathrm{d}s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ 0\leq j\leq\ell.

As (
4.2
) is in particular applicable for
v=\partial_{\nu}u_{\ell}(t)\in V_{\ell}
and as
\Delta U_{\ell}=0
we note that the energy of these Galerkin approximates decays according to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla U_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla\partial_{t}U_{\ell},\nabla U_{\ell}\rangle\mathrm{d}v=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\partial_{t}u_{\ell}\cdot\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}\mathrm{d}s

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}Q(\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell})\cdot\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}\mathrm{d}s=-\varepsilon\|\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}-\|P_{v}\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2},

where the last step follows since
P_{\cdot}
is an orthogonal projection on
N_{\eta}
, and hence

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(\varepsilon+P_{v})\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}\|\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+(1+2\varepsilon)\|P_{v}\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2},

and since
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
. We can hence deduce that

(4.4)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla U_{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\partial\Sigma)}^{2}\leq 3\|\nabla U_{\ell}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}

which also ensures that

\begin{split}\|U_{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}&\leq C\|u_{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}\leq C\big(\|u_{\ell}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{t}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\partial\Sigma)}\big)\\
&\leq C\|U_{\ell}(0)\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma)}\end{split}

for every
t\in[0,T]
, for a constant that is allowed to depend on
T
and a lower bound
\iota_{0}
on
\inj(g)
, but that is independent of
\ell
.
Hence we have that

(4.5)

\|U_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Sigma))}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}u_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{\nu}U_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\partial\Sigma)}^{2}\leq C\|U_{\ell}(0)\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma)}^{2},

again with a constant
C=C(T,\iota_{0})
.

By construction,
\|u_{\ell}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}
since
u_{\ell}(0)
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection of
u_{0}
onto
V_{\ell}
. Importantly, the analogous
\dot{H}^{1}(\Sigma)
-estimate
\|\nabla U_{\ell}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq\|\nabla(u_{0})_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}
also holds, since the relation

\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla(\varphi_{j})_{g},\nabla w_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}v=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\partial_{\nu}(\varphi_{j})_{g}w\,\mathrm{d}s=\lambda_{j}\int_{\partial\Sigma}\varphi_{j}w\,\mathrm{d}s\text{ for every }w\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial\Sigma})

ensures that the
\lambda_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\varphi_{j})_{g}
,
j\geq 1
, are
\dot{H}^{1}(\Sigma)
-orthonormal and that
U_{\ell}(0)-a_{0}(u_{0})
can alternatively be obtained as the
\dot{H}^{1}(\Sigma)
-orthogonal projection of
(u_{0})_{g}
onto the span of
(\varphi_{1})_{g},\dots,(\varphi_{\ell})_{g}
.

Combining the resulting uniform bounds

\displaystyle\|U_{\ell}(0)\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|U_{\ell}(0)\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})}+\|\nabla U_{\ell}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})}+\|\nabla(u_{0})_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)})\leq C\|(u_{0})_{g}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma)}

on the initial maps
U_{\ell}(0)
with the
\ell
-independent estimate (
4.5
) thus allows us to conclude that the functions
U_{\ell}=(u_{\ell})_{g}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Sigma))
and, as
\varepsilon>0
is fixed, that the functions
\partial_{t}u_{\ell}
and
\partial_{\nu_{g}}U_{\ell}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\partial\Sigma)
. This allows us to pass to a subsequence, still indexed by
\ell
, along which
U_{\ell}
converges
\text{weak-}*
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Sigma))
to a function
U=(u)_{g}
and along which
\partial_{t}u_{\ell}
as well as
\partial_{\nu_{g}}U_{\ell}
converge weakly in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\partial\Sigma)
to
\partial_{t}u
and
\partial_{\nu_{g}}U
, respectively.

This function hence has the regularity asked for in the lemma and since
u_{\ell}
satisfies

(4.6)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial\Sigma}\partial_{t}u_{\ell}\cdot w\mathrm{d}s_{g}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial\Sigma}Q(\partial_{\nu}(u_{\ell})_{g})\cdot w\mathrm{d}s_{g}\mathrm{d}t

for all
w\in C^{\infty}([0,T];V_{\ell})
, we find that
u
satisfies (
4.6
) for all
w\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}C^{\infty}([0,T];V_{j})
and is hence a weak solution of (
2.11
) as required.

We furthermore observe that for solutions
u
of this regularity, the computation carried out in (
4.1
) yields the claimed estimate (
4.1
) on the decay of the energy. Finally we note that as our equation is
linear
this in turn ensures the uniqueness of the solution in the considered class of functions.
∎

We recall that in the case of
\Sigma=D
discussed in
[
15
]
, improved regularity of these solutions for initial maps satisfying stronger regularity assumptions was obtained by establishing uniform estimates on the Galerkin approximates in higher order Sobolev spaces. In the present setting this argument is no longer applicable since for general surfaces we cannot expect derivatives of
\varphi_{k}
to be contained in
V_{k}
and hence cannot use these functions as test-functions in (
4.2
) above.

However, we can easily circumvent this difficulty by differentiating (
2.11
) in time. Since
v
and
g
are time independent, this leaves the equation unchanged allowing us to use a simple iterative argument that combines Lemma
4.1
with the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
,
T>0
,
m\geq 2
. Then for any
g\in\mathcal{M}^{m+1}
, any
v\in H^{m-\frac{1}{2}}((\partial\Sigma,g);N_{\eta})
and any
u_{0}\in H^{m-\frac{1}{2}}((\partial\Sigma,g);{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the corresponding solution
u
of (
2.11
) obtained in Lemma
4.1
is so that

u\in L^{2}([0,T];H^{m}(\partial\Sigma))\text{ and
}u_{g}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{m}(\Sigma)).

We note that this claim on the regularity could be equivalently stated as

u\in L^{2}([0,T];H^{m}(\partial\Sigma))\cap X_{m}(T,g)

since
\partial_{t}u_{g}=(Q\partial_{\nu}u)_{g}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Sigma,g))
if
u_{g}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{2}(\Sigma))
.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

We first consider the case
m=2
. We let
w
be the solution to (
2.11
) with initial data
w_{0}:=Q_{v,\varepsilon}\partial_{\nu}(u_{0})_{g}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Sigma;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
obtained in Lemma

.
We then note that

\tilde{u}(x,t):=u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}w(x,t^{\prime})\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

not only satisfies the initial condition
\tilde{u}(0)=u_{0}
but also solves (

). Indeed, since

and
g
, and hence also

and

, are independent of time, the harmonic extension
\tilde{u}_{g}
satisfies

Q\partial_{\nu}\tilde{u}_{g}(t)=Q\partial_{\nu}(u_{0})_{g}+\int_{0}^{t}Q\partial_{\nu}w_{g}(t^{\prime})\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}=Q\partial_{\nu}(u_{0})_{g}+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}w(t^{\prime})\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}=w(t)=\partial_{t}\tilde{u}(t).

By the uniqueness of solutions of (

) established in Lemma

, we thus have
u=\tilde{u}
which implies that
Q\partial_{\nu}u_{g}=\partial_{t}u=w\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial\Sigma}))\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}({\partial\Sigma}))
. As

is smooth and as

we thus find that both
\partial_{\nu}u_{g}
and
\partial_{t}u
are in
L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial\Sigma}))\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}({\partial\Sigma}))
. Elliptic regularity hence allows us to deduce that

, and that
\partial_{t}u_{g}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
, which gives that

as claimed.

The claim for general
m\geq 3
can now be obtained by iterating this argument.
∎

We next establish the following a priori estimates on the

-norm of solutions of equation (

), where we note that we now also allow

and

to depend on time.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
m\geq 3
,
T>0
,
g\in C^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{M}^{m+1})
and
v:[0,T]\times\partial\Sigma\rightarrow N_{\eta}
with
v\in X_{m}(T,g)
. Then for any
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
and any solution
u
of (
2.11
) with

(4.7)

u\in L^{2}([0,T];H^{m}(\partial\Sigma))\text{ and }u_{g}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{m}(\Sigma)),

we can bound

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|u_{g}(t)\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(t))}^{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\|\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}([0,t];L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,g))}^{2}\leq e^{C_{1}t}\|(u(0))_{g(0)}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(0))}^{2}

for every

and for a constant
C_{1}=C(\iota_{0},m,M)\Lambda^{m}
where
\Lambda\geq 1
,
M
and
\iota_{0}
are as in (
2.15
).

We stress that the constant
C_{1}
is
independent
of
\varepsilon
, and it is this key feature of the above lemma which will allow us to obtain a solution of the original problem from solutions of the regularised equation as
\varepsilon\to 0
later in Section
4.3
.

We note that as our domain surface is in general not flat, we cannot expect higher derivatives to commute, which results in many additional error terms when compared with the case of
\Sigma=D
treated in
[
15
]
, though these will all be of lower order.

Throughout this proof, and also later on in Section
5
, we will also often want to exchange the order in which operations such as derivatives, projections and harmonic extensions are applied. To do this efficiently, we have collected the relevant estimates on the resulting commutator terms in Appendix
A.2
, see in particular Lemma

and Remark

.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

As (
4.7
) ensures that
\partial_{\nu}u_{g}\in L^{2}([0,T];H^{m-1}({\partial\Sigma},g))

we have that also
\partial_{t}u=Q_{\varepsilon,v}\partial_{\nu}u_{g}\in L^{2}([0,T];H^{m-1}({\partial\Sigma},g))
, which gives us sufficient regularity to compute

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\|u_{g(\cdot+\varepsilon)}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g(\cdot+\varepsilon))}^{2}+2\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla^{j}u_{g},\nabla^{j}(\partial_{t}u)_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g)}\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g)}^{2}+2\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla^{j}u_{g},\nabla^{j}(\partial_{t}u)_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}v,

where we use Lemma
3.8
in the second step and recall that
\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g)}\leq M
. The claimed estimate (
4.8
) hence follows once we establish that
J_{j}:=\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla^{j}u_{g},\nabla^{j}(\partial_{t}u_{g})\rangle\mathrm{d}v
satisfies

(4.9)

J_{j}\leq C\big(1+\|v_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}^{m}\big)\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}^{2}\text{ for }j=0,1,\dots,m-1

while

(4.10)

\displaystyle J_{m}\leq-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\|\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+C\big(1+\|v_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}^{m}\big)\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}^{2}

where here and in the following
C
denotes a constant that is allowed to depend on
\iota_{0}
,
m
and as usual our setting, but that is
independent
of
\varepsilon
.

The required bound (
4.9
) on the lower order terms immediately follows from the estimate

(4.11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{j}(\partial_{t}u)_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla^{j}(\varepsilon\partial_{\nu}u_{g}+P_{v}\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|v_{g}\|_{H^{m-1}(\Sigma)}^{m-1})\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m-1}(\Sigma)}+C\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}

which we can e.g. obtain by observing that
\|P_{v}\nabla^{j}\partial_{\nu}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq\|\nabla^{j}\partial_{\nu}u\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}
, compare (
A.4
), and that each
P_{v}\nabla^{j}\partial_{\nu}u_{g}-\nabla^{j}(P_{v}\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}
can be thought of as a commutator term
C_{j}(v_{g},u_{g})
of the form (
A.13
) for which Lemma
A.1
is applicable.

To estimate the leading order term
J_{m}
, we integrate by parts to rewrite

\displaystyle J_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{t}u)_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\Sigma}\langle\Delta\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{t}u)_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},\nabla^{m-1}(P_{v}\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\text{err}_{1}

and exploit that
u_{g}
is harmonic, and hence
\|\Delta\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}
, as well as (
4.11
) to bound the resulting error
\text{err}_{1}:=\int_{\Sigma}\langle\Delta\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{t}u)_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}v
by

(4.12)

\text{err}_{1}\leq C\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}\|\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{t}u)_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C(1+\|v_{g}\|_{H^{m-1}(\Sigma)}^{m-1})\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}^{2}.

We now further rewrite

(4.13)

J_{m}=-\varepsilon\|\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}-\|P_{v}\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+\text{err}_{1}+\text{err}_{2}+\text{err}_{3}

for

\displaystyle\text{err}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},P_{v}\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}-\nabla^{m-1}(P_{v}\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}s

and

\displaystyle\text{err}_{3}

\displaystyle:=\varepsilon\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}-\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},P_{v}\left(\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}-\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\right)\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle\varepsilon\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}+P_{v}\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}-\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\rangle\mathrm{d}s.

Thanks to (
A.4
) we can bound

\displaystyle\text{err}_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}+C\|P_{v}\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\|\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|P_{v}\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+C\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}^{2}

where we stress that
C
is independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
. To bound
\text{err}_{2}
, we note that
C_{m-1}(v_{g},u_{g})=P_{v_{g}}\nabla^{m-1}(\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}-\nabla^{m-1}(P_{v}\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}
and
\nabla C_{m-1}(v_{g},u_{g})
are commutator terms of the form (
A.13
) (for
j=m-1
and
j=m
, respectively) and that Lemma
A.1
hence in particular implies that

(4.14)

\|C_{m-1}(v_{g},u_{g})\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma)}\leq C(1+\|v_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}^{m})\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}.

Rewriting
\text{err}_{2}
as an integral over the surface and using again that
\Delta\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}
is of lower order we hence obtain that

\displaystyle\text{err}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g},C_{m-1}(v_{g},u_{g})\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla^{m}u_{g},\nabla C_{m-1}(v_{g},u_{g})\rangle\mathrm{d}v+C\|\Delta\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\|C_{m-1}(v_{g},u_{g})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\|v_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}^{m}\right)\|u_{g}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma)}^{2}.

Inserting these error estimates into (
4.13
) yields the claimed bound (
4.10
) on
J_{m}
and hence completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

On each individual interval

-norm and injectivity radius in (

\displaystyle\|(\tilde{u}(t))_{\tilde{g}(t)}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,\tilde{g}(t))}^{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\|\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{m-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}([t_{k-1},t];L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,\tilde{g}(t_{k-1}))}^{2}

\|(\tilde{u}(t_{k}))_{\tilde{g}(t_{k})}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,\tilde{g}(t_{k}))}^{2}\leq e^{CM|t_{k}-t_{k-1}|}\lim_{t\nearrow t_{k}}\|(\tilde{u}(t))_{\tilde{g}(t)}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,\tilde{g}(t))}^{2}

Combined with (

\tilde{U}=(\tilde{u})_{\tilde{g}}
obtained from any such

is so that

\|\tilde{U}(t)\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,\tilde{g}(t))}^{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\|\partial_{\nu}\nabla_{\tilde{g}}^{m-1}\tilde{U}\|_{L^{2}([0,t];L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,\tilde{g}))}^{2}\leq e^{Ct}\|(u_{0})_{g_{0}}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g_{0})}^{2}

for every

and a constant

\|\tilde{U}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{m}(\Sigma,g_{0}))}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla^{m}\tilde{U}\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,g_{0}))}^{2}\leq C\|(u_{0})_{g_{0}}\|_{H^{m}(\Sigma,g_{0})}^{2},

C=C(\iota_{0},T,m)
for all
\tilde{U}=(\tilde{u})_{\tilde{g}}
obtained by such a time discretisation.

Applying this argument for a sequence

\tilde{U}_{\ell}=(\tilde{u}_{\ell})_{\tilde{g}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle w_{g_{1}})(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,g_{1}(t))}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon\|\partial_{\nu_{1}}w_{g_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,g_{1})}^{2}+\|P_{v_{1}}\partial_{\nu_{1}}w_{g_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma,g_{1})}^{2}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

5.
Higher Regularity and long-time existence of solutions

In the previous section, we constructed a solution

To this end, we will in particular use the following lemma, which extends Proposition 3.3 from

Lemma 5.1

For any
\iota_{0}>0

For the proofs of both Lemma

Lemma 5.7
.

Let

and
\iota_{0}>0
be any fixed numbers and let

and

and

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|P_{u}^{\perp}\partial_{\nu}\nabla u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+\|\nabla(P_{u}^{\perp}\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|P_{u}^{\perp}\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{k-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+\|\nabla^{k-1}(P_{u}^{\perp}\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}

Here and in the following, we use the shorthand

\displaystyle S_{s}(u,g):=\sum_{\{\tilde{s}\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}:\tilde{s}\leq s\}}I_{\tilde{s}}(u,g),\quad\text{ for }I_{\tilde{s}}(u,g)\text{ defined in \eqref{def:I_k},}

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|P_{u}^{\perp}\partial_{\nu}\nabla^{k-1}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}+\|\nabla^{k-1}(P_{u}^{\perp}\partial_{\nu}u_{g})_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}^{2}

Proof of Lemma

.

onto

into

(P_{u}^{\perp}\partial_{\tau}u)_{g}\equiv 0
and commutator terms, whose improved regularity properties are discussed in the appendix.

To make this precise, we recall that Lemma
A.1
and Remark
A.2
ensure that commutator terms

,

As

a cut-off function as in Remark

In collar coordinates

Combined, these estimates on

5.3.
Proof of Lemma
5.1

We will derive Lemma

,
\mathcal{H}:=[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

For any
c_{0}\in(0,1)
there exist constants

such that the following holds true. Let

, let

into
N

Then we can bound

(5.27)

\displaystyle\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1}\cap\partial\mathcal{H})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|P_{w}\partial_{\nu_{\mathcal{H}}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0}\cap\partial\mathcal{H})}^{2}+r^{-1}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}^{2}+r^{-3}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}^{2})

To derive Lemma
5.1
from this statement, we can work in the collar coordinates
(s,\theta)\in[0,X(\ell_{i})]\times S^{1}
of the boundary curve
\sigma_{i}
that contains
x_{0}

X(\ell_{i})\geq c_{3}(\iota_{0})>0
and that the conformal factor satisfies

, compare Remark

.

For
c_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}\min(\iota_{0},c_{3}C_{2}^{-1})
we hence know that
B_{2r}^{g}(x_{0})
,

is contained in this collar neighbourhood and that the subsets
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{0}
of

which represent

are so that

\Omega_{0}\subset B_{2C_{2}r}^{g_{0}}\text{ and }\text{dist}_{g_{0}}(\partial\Omega_{1}\setminus\sigma_{i},\partial\Omega_{0}\setminus\sigma_{i})\geq\inf\rho^{-1}r\geq C_{2}^{-1}r,\text{ for }g_{0}=\mathrm{d}s^{2}+\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}.

We can hence apply Lemma
5.8
for

, set
\delta_{1}(\iota_{0}):=\delta_{2}(c_{0}(\iota_{0}))
and exploit the conformal invariance of the energy to deduce that the function

that represents

in collar coordinates satisfies (

5.2
) for

.

Proof of Lemma
5.8
.

As all terms in (
5.27
) have the same scaling behaviour under dilations, we can assume without loss of generality that
r=1
. Given
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{0}
as in the lemma, we can then choose a cut-off function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})
such that
\varphi\equiv 1
on
\Omega_{1}
and
\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}}\leq C=C(c_{0})
by taking the convolution of the characteristic function of
\{x:\text{dist}_{g_{0}}(x,\Omega_{1})\leq\tfrac{c_{0}}{2}\}
with a mollifying kernel that is supported on
\mathbb{D}_{c_{0}/2}
.

For the rest of the proof we will viewing these subsets of
\mathbb{D}_{1}^{+}\subset[0,1]\times(-\pi,\pi)\subset\mathcal{H}
as subsets of the cylinder
(\Sigma_{0}:=[0,1]\times S^{1},g_{0}=\mathrm{d}s^{2}+\mathrm{d}\theta^{2})
and carry out all computations using these coordinates
(s,\theta)
and metric
g_{0}
. We furthermore write for short
\sigma_{0}=\{0\}\times S^{1}
for the boundary curve of
\Sigma_{0}
which contains the curve
\mathbb{D}_{1}^{+}\cap\partial\mathcal{H}
that is mapped to
N
under
w
and denote by
v_{\Sigma_{0}}
the harmonic extension of maps
v:\partial\Sigma_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
(\Sigma_{0},g_{0})
.

We first recall that for any function
v:\Omega_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we can bound

(5.28)

\|\nabla(\varphi v)_{\Sigma_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}\leq C\|\partial_{\nu_{\Sigma_{0}}}(\varphi v)_{\Sigma_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}=C\|\partial_{s}(\varphi v)_{\Sigma_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}.

As
\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}}\leq C
we can furthermore bound
\|v\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}\leq C\|v\nabla\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{0})}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}
and
\|\nabla(\varphi v-(\varphi v)_{\Sigma_{0}})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}\leq C\|\Delta(\varphi v)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{0})}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}
, where the last estimate holds provided

is harmonic. Combined we hence get

\displaystyle\|\varphi\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(\varphi v)_{\Sigma_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}+\|\nabla(\varphi v-(\varphi v)_{\Sigma_{0}})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}+C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\partial_{s}[(\varphi v)_{\Sigma_{0}}-\varphi v]\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}+\|\partial_{s}[\varphi v]\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})})+C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\partial_{s}v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}+C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}

for any harmonic
v:\text{supp}(\varphi)\cap\Sigma_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Applied for
w
, which maps
\text{supp}(\varphi)\cap{\partial\Sigma}=\text{supp}(\varphi)\cap\sigma_{0}
into
N
, this yields

(5.29)

\|\varphi\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}^{2}\leq C\|\varphi P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{0})}^{2}+C\|\varphi P_{w}\partial_{s}w\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{0})}^{2}+C\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}^{2}.

To bound the first term on the right hand side, we recall that
w
is harmonic and that
\varphi
vanishes on
{\partial\Sigma}\setminus\sigma_{0}
and use the divergence theorem to write

(5.30)

\displaystyle\|\varphi P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma_{0}}\partial_{s}w\cdot\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w\mathrm{d}\theta=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla(\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w)\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\partial_{s}w\cdot\partial_{s}(\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w)+\partial_{\theta}w\cdot\partial_{\theta}(\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w)\mathrm{d}v.

We can now use that
|\partial_{\theta}(\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w)-\partial_{s}(\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{\theta}w)|\leq C(\varphi^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}+\varphi|\nabla w|)
since error terms that result from commuting a derivative with the projection are bounded by
C\varphi^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}
while terms that result from a derivative falling onto a cut-off function are bounded by
C\varphi|\nabla w|
. As
\partial_{ss}u+\partial_{\theta\theta}w=0
we can also bound
|\partial_{s}(\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w)+\partial_{\theta}(\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{\theta}w)|\leq C(\varphi^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}+\varphi|\nabla w|)
.

Denoting the error term which results from inserting these bounds into (
5.30
) by
\text{err}_{1}
, we now observe that the main term in the resulting formula of

\displaystyle\|\varphi P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}-\partial_{s}w\cdot\partial_{\theta}(\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{\theta}w)+\partial_{\theta}w\cdot\partial_{s}(\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{\theta}w)\mathrm{d}v+\text{err}_{1}

vanishes identically, since integration by parts (with respect to
\theta
for the first term and with respect to
s

\Sigma_{0}
which cancel out and since the integrand
\partial_{\theta}w\cdot\varphi^{2}P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{\theta}w
of the resulting boundary terms vanishes identically on
{\partial\Sigma}
as
w
maps
{\partial\Sigma}\cap\text{supp}(\phi)\subset\sigma_{0}
into
N
.

We hence conclude that

(5.31)

\displaystyle\|\varphi P_{w}^{\perp}\partial_{s}w\|_{L^{2}(\sigma_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\text{err}_{1}\leq C\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\varphi^{2}|\nabla w|^{3}\mathrm{d}v+\varphi|\nabla w|^{2}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\|\varphi\nabla w\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma_{0})}^{2}+C\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}^{2}.

We now recall that
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{0})\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Sigma_{0})
and that standard theory for harmonic functions allows us to bound

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\varphi w)-(\nabla(\varphi w))_{\Sigma_{0}}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(\varphi w)-(\nabla(\varphi w))_{\Sigma_{0}}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{0})}\leq C\|\Delta\nabla(\varphi w)\|_{H^{-1}(\Sigma_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta(\varphi w)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{0})}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})},

\displaystyle\|\varphi\nabla w\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(\varphi w)\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma_{0})}+\|\nabla(\varphi)w\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\nabla(\varphi w))_{\Sigma_{0}}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{0})}+C\|\nabla(\varphi w)-(\nabla(\varphi w))_{\Sigma_{0}}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma_{0})}+C\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla(\varphi w)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}+C\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma_{0})}+C\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{0})}.

Combined with (

), (

∎

For any fixed function

We recall that (

\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C=C(\iota_{0},\bar{E})

\|\partial_{t}(u,g)\|_{L^{2}([t^{\prime},t];L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})\times L^{2}(\Sigma))}^{2}\leq E(t^{\prime})-E(t)

.

Proof of Lemma

From the equation (
1.8
) of the flow and (

As

\bigg|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{\varphi}+\int_{{\partial\Sigma}}\varphi^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg|\leq C\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}E_{\varphi}+CE_{\varphi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}\|\partial_{t}u_{g}\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}

for some

To show this, we split

and use Lemmas
3.8

A.2.
Commutator estimates

While many of the operations we use repeatedly throughout the paper, such as harmonic extensions, derivatives and projections, do not commute, the error terms resulting from changing the order of operation will in general be of lower order. The purpose of this appendix is to collect and prove the relevant estimates for such commutators which will be used throughout the paper.

To begin with we note that terms which are obtained from commuting two differential operators
L_{1,2}
(which are defined in terms of the metric) of total order
j_{1}+j_{2}=j\in{\mathbb{N}}
can be bounded by

(A.2)

\|L_{1}L_{2}w-L_{2}L_{1}w\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{j-1}(\Sigma)}\text{ and }\|L_{1}L_{2}w-L_{2}L_{1}w\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})}\leq C\sum_{k\leq j-1}\|\nabla^{k}w\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})}.

We also recall that the extensions
\tau
and
\nu
of the unit tangent and normal vector fields on
{\partial\Sigma}
are chosen so that
\partial_{\eta}u_{g}
,
\eta\in\{\tau,\nu\}
are again harmonic on the corresponding collar neighbourhood
\mathcal{C}({\partial\Sigma})
of the boundary, compare Remark
3.2
, and let
\varphi
be a cut-off function with
\varphi\equiv 1
near
{\partial\Sigma}
as in that remark. As the
C^{k}
semi-norm of harmonic functions can be controlled just in terms of the energy away from the boundary, we can hence bound the difference between
\partial_{\eta}u_{g}
and its harmonic extension by

(A.3)

\|\varphi\big[\partial_{\eta}u_{g}-(\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g})\big]\|_{C^{k}(\Sigma)}\leq CE(u,g)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, any
g\in\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}
and a constant
C
that only depends on
k
and
\iota_{0}
.

Combined, these estimates in particular allow us to bound

(A.4)

\|\nabla^{j}(\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g}-\partial_{\eta}\nabla^{j}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Sigma)}\leq C\sum_{k\leq j}\|\nabla^{k}u_{g}\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})}\leq C\|u_{g}\|_{H^{j+1}(\Sigma)}

where the last estimate is not optimal, but will often be sufficient in applications.

We will need commutator estimates mostly for derivatives and harmonic extensions of functions of the form
w=P_{v_{g}}(\partial_{\eta}u_{g})

and
w=P_{v_{g}}^{\perp}(\partial_{\eta}u_{g})
, where

P_{p},P^{\perp}_{p}\in C_{b}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n})
denote the (fixed) extensions of the projections onto the tangent and normal space of our support manifold
chosen in Remark
2.4
.

To state the relevant estimates in a form that makes them applicable to treat both types of terms, we let

A\in C_{b}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n})
be any bounded matrix valued function with bounded derivatives
and consider commutator terms of the form

(A.5)

C_{j}(U,V):=L_{1}L_{2}\big(\varphi A(V)(\partial_{\eta}U)\big)-L_{1}\big(\varphi A(V)(\partial_{\eta}L_{2}U)\big)

for linear differential operators
L_{1,2}
of order
j_{1}\geq 0
,
j_{2}\geq 1
with
j_{1}+j_{2}\leq j
and a cut-off function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}({\partial\Sigma}))
which is chosen as in Remark
3.2
and is in particular so that
\varphi\equiv 1
near
{\partial\Sigma}
. A short argument, which is carried out below, allows us to prove the following basic, but useful, estimates.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{M}_{\iota_{0}}
,
A\in C_{b}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n})
and let
U,V\in H^{j}(\mathcal{C}({\partial\Sigma}))
, for some
j\geq 2
,
\mathcal{C}({\partial\Sigma})=\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{C}(\sigma_{i},g)
the union of the neighbourhoods of the boundary curves
\sigma_{i}
described in Section
3.1
. Then the following holds true for commutator terms

C_{j}(U,V)
and
C_{j-1}(U,V)
of the form (
A.5
) obtained from differential operators
L_{1,2}
of order
j_{1,2}
with
j_{2}\geq 1
and
j_{1}+j_{2}\leq j
respectively
j_{1}+j_{2}\leq j-1
.

If
j=2
then
C_{2}(U,V)\in L^{4/3}(\Sigma)
and
C_{1}(U,V)\in L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})
and we can bound

(A.6)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|C_{2}(U,V)\|_{L^{4/3}(\Sigma)}+\|C_{1}(U,V)\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|\nabla V\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)})\|U\|_{H^{2}(\Sigma)}+C\|V\|_{H^{2}(\Sigma)}\|\nabla U\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle+C(1+\|\nabla V\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}^{2})\|\nabla U\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}

while for functions
U,V\in H^{5/2}(\Sigma)
we furthermore have that
C_{2}(U,V)\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and

(A.7)

\displaystyle\|C_{2}(U,V)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|V\|_{H^{3/2}(\Sigma)})\|U\|_{H^{5/2}(\Sigma)}+C\|V\|_{H^{5/2}(\Sigma)}\|U\|_{H^{3/2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle+C(1+\|V\|_{H^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2})\|U\|_{H^{3/2}(\Sigma)}.

If
j\geq 3
then

C_{j}(U,V)\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and
C_{j-1}(U,V)\in L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})

and we can bound

(A.8)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|C_{j}(U,V)\|_{L^{4/3}(\Sigma)}+\|C_{j-1}(U,V)\|_{L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|V\|_{H^{3/2}(\Sigma)})\|U\|_{H^{j}(\Sigma)}+C\|U\|_{H^{3/2}(\Sigma)}\|V\|_{H^{j}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle+C\|V\|_{H^{5/2}(\Sigma)}\|U\|_{H^{j-1}(\Sigma)}+C\|U\|_{H^{5/2}(\Sigma)}\|V\|_{H^{j-1}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle+C(1+\|V\|_{H^{j-1}(\Sigma)}^{j})\|U\|_{H^{j-1}(\Sigma)}

and

(A.9)

\displaystyle\|C_{j}(U,V)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|V\|_{H^{5/2}(\Sigma)})\|U\|_{H^{j}(\Sigma)}+C\|U\|_{H^{5/2}(\Sigma)}\|V\|_{H^{j}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle+C(\|V\|_{H^{5/2}}+\|V\|_{H^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2})\|U\|_{H^{j-\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle+C(\|U\|_{H^{5/2}(\Sigma)}+\|V\|_{H^{2}(\Sigma)}\|U\|_{H^{2}(\Sigma)})\|V\|_{H^{j-1/2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle+C(1+\|V\|_{H^{j-1}(\Sigma)}^{j})\|U\|_{H^{j-1}(\Sigma)}

for a constant
C
that only depends on the operators
L_{1,2}
,
\iota_{0}
and
A
.

At times, it will suffice to use that the above estimates in particular imply that for
j\geq 3

(A.10)

\|C_{j}(u_{g},u_{g})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}\leq S_{j-\frac{1}{2}}(u,g)^{j}S_{j}(u,g),\text{ for }S_{s}(u,g)\text{ as in \eqref{def:S_k}}

We can apply the above lemma not only to control error terms that result from commuting derivatives and projections, but also to compare functions of the form
w=P_{v_{g}}\partial_{\eta}u_{g}
with their harmonic extensions.
To this end, we note that
\nabla^{j-2}\Delta(\varphi A(v_{g})\partial_{\eta}u_{g})
can be viewed as a commutator term

C_{j}(v_{g},u_{g})
of the above form for

L_{1}=\nabla_{g}^{j-2}
and
L_{2}=\Delta_{g}
since
\Delta_{g}u_{g}\equiv 0
.
To bound the difference between such
w=\varphi A(v_{g})\partial_{\eta}u_{g}
and their harmonic extension
w_{g}
we will hence be able to combine the above commutator estimates with the standard
elliptic estimate

(A.11)

\|w-w_{g}\|_{W^{k,p}(\Sigma)}\leq C\|\Delta_{g}w\|_{W^{k-2,p}(\Sigma)},\quad C=C(k,p,\iota_{0}),\quad 1<p<\infty

and the fact that
W^{1,\frac{4}{3}}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})
.

This immediately allows us to deduce that the above commutator estimates also apply for
terms of the form
\nabla^{j}(\varphi\big[A(v_{g})\partial_{\eta}u_{g}-(A(v_{g})\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g}\big])

and, as
\varphi[(\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g}-\partial_{\eta}u_{g}]
is controlled by (
A.3
), also for terms of the form
\nabla^{j}(\varphi\big[A(v_{g})(\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g}-(A(v_{g})\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g}\big])
.
Combined with the
C^{k}
control on harmonic functions on
\text{supp}(1-\varphi)\subset\Sigma\setminus{\partial\Sigma}
,
we hence obtain the following useful consequence of the above lemma.

Remark A.2
.

The commutator estimates stated in the above Lemma
A.1
are all valid also for commutator terms of the form

(A.12)

\displaystyle C_{j}(v_{g},u_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\big(\nabla_{g}^{j}\big[(A(v_{g})\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g}\big]-\nabla_{g}^{j_{1}}\big[A(v_{g})\partial_{\eta}\nabla_{g}^{j_{2}}u_{g}\big]\big),\qquad\,j_{1}+j_{2}=j,

(A.13)

\displaystyle C_{j}(v_{g},u_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\big(\nabla_{g}^{j}\big[(A(v_{g})\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g}\big]-\nabla_{g}^{j_{1}}\big[A(v_{g})\nabla_{g}^{j_{2}}(\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g}\big]\big),\quad j_{1}+j_{2}=j,

and for cut-off terms that are supported away from
{\partial\Sigma}
of the form

(A.14)

\displaystyle C_{j}(v_{g},u_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}^{j_{0}}\left[(1-\varphi)\nabla_{g}^{j_{1}}(A(v_{g})\nabla_{g}^{j_{2}}(\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g})\right],\qquad j_{0}+j_{1}+j_{2}=j

(A.15)

\displaystyle C_{j}(v_{g},u_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{g}^{j_{0}}\left[(1-\varphi)\nabla_{g}^{j_{1}}(A(v_{g})\partial_{\eta}u_{g})_{g}\right],\qquad\qquad\,\qquad j_{0}+j_{1}=j.

Proof of Lemma
A.1
.

To obtain the claim for
j=2
we can use that
\nabla C_{1}
and
C_{2}
satisfy pointwise bounds of

\displaystyle|C_{2}(U,V)|+|\nabla C_{1}(U,V)|\leq C\big[|\nabla^{2}U|(1+|\nabla V|)+|\nabla^{2}V||\nabla U|+(1+|\nabla V|^{2})|\nabla U|\big]

C
as in the lemma. The claims in this case can hence be obtained by using that
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Sigma)
and
W^{1,\frac{4}{3}}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow L^{2}({\partial\Sigma})
and applying Hölder’s inequality.

Similarly, for
j\geq 3
we have pointwise estimates of

\displaystyle|C_{j}(U,V)|+|\nabla C_{j-1}(U,V)|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|\nabla V|)|\nabla^{j}U|+|\nabla^{j}V||\nabla U|

\displaystyle+C(|\nabla V|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}V|+1)|\nabla^{j-1}U|+(|\nabla U||\nabla V|+|\nabla^{2}U|)|\nabla^{j-1}V|

\displaystyle+C\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq j+1\\
1\leq\alpha_{i}\leq j-2\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{\alpha_{1}}V|\cdots|\nabla^{\alpha_{\ell-1}}V||\nabla^{\alpha_{\ell}}U|.

The claims in this case can hence be obtained by using the Sobolev embeddings
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Sigma)
and
H^{j-1}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow W^{j-2,p}(\Sigma)
,
p<\infty
, and
H^{3/2}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
as well as Hölder’s inequality.
∎
