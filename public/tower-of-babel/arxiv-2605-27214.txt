On the cycle structure of the symmetric tensor power of permutations
Sebastian Caballero, Diego Villamizar

Abstract
. Problem 8.1 in [ 1 ] asks about the relationship between the cycle decomposition of a permutation σ \sigma and that of its symmetric tensor power σ ⊙ k \sigma^{\odot k} . In this paper, we investigate this question and give formulas for computing the number of fixed points and, in the case of a permutation containing at most one cycle of length greater than one, the number of s s -cycles.



1.
Introduction

In
[
1
]
the authors define a way to produce the symmetric tensor power of a finite graph
G=(V,E)
on
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 1}
vertices by using the symmetric tensor power of the graph’s adjacency matrix, defined as a
n\times n
matrix
A_{G}
with entries given by

[A_{G}]_{i,j}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }v_{i},v_{j}\text{ are adjacent,}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The authors give a formula to compute the entries of the symmetric tensor power of any matrix
A
, denoted by
A^{\odot k}
, given by Equation
1
(Lemma 2.2 in
[
1
]
)

(1)

[A^{\odot k}]_{\textbf{i},\textbf{j}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{k}{\textbf{m}(\textbf{i})}\binom{k}{\textbf{m}(\textbf{j})}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{p}\in\operatorname{Orb}(\textbf{i})\\
\textbf{q}\in\operatorname{Orb}(\textbf{j})\end{subarray}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}[A]_{p_{\ell},q_{\ell}}.

The formula in Equation
1
is in terms of
\textbf{i}=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})
and
\textbf{j}=(j_{1},\cdots,j_{k})
, size
n
compositions of
k
that we represent here as
k
tuples with entries in
[n]=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
such that
i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}
and
j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\cdots\leq j_{k}
respectively. To denote the set of all size
n
compositions of
k
we use
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
, for example, all size
3
compositions of
2
are listed in
\mathcal{C}_{3,2}=\{11,22,33,12,13,23\}
, when parentheses and commas are omitted. The number of such compositions when
n
and
k
are fixed is given by
\left|\mathcal{C}_{n,k}\right|=\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}
by the Stars and Bars counting technique (see Section 1.2 in
[
6
]
).

Given
i
a size
n
composition of
k
, we define
\textbf{m}(\textbf{i})
to be the vector of multiplicities of each element
1\leq s\leq n
in the vector
i
. That is
\textbf{m}(\textbf{i})=(m_{1}(\textbf{i}),m_{2}(\textbf{i}),\cdots,m_{n}(\textbf{i}))
where
m_{\ell}(\textbf{i})
is the number of indices

such that
i_{j}=\ell
. As an example, the values of

in all size
3

2
are
\textbf{m}(11)=(2,0,0),\,\textbf{m}(22)=(0,2,0),\,\textbf{m}(33)=(0,0,2),\,\textbf{m}(12)=(1,1,0),\,\textbf{m}(13)=(1,0,1),\,\textbf{m}(23)=(0,1,1).

The normalization in Equation
1
uses the term
\binom{k}{\textbf{m}(\textbf{i})}=\frac{k!}{m_{1}(\textbf{i})!\cdot m_{2}(\textbf{i})!\,\cdots\,m_{n-1}(\textbf{i})!\cdot m_{n}(\textbf{i})!}
, the multinomial coefficient, and the sum is over sets
\operatorname{Orb}(\textbf{i})
defined as all tuples
x\in[n]^{k}
that can be constructed by permuting the entries of

in all possible ways. As an example,
\operatorname{Orb}(12)=\{12,21\}
. Notice that the size of
\operatorname{Orb}(\textbf{i})
is given by the multinomial coefficient
\binom{k}{\textbf{m}(\textbf{i})}
, this can be seen by a standard counting argument of all possible permutations over permutations that fix the
m_{\ell}(\textbf{i})
appearances of
\ell
for all
\ell
’s in
[n]
.

We will denote the set of permutations of
[n]
as
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and use the line notation to write down permutations, that is,
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
will be written as
\sigma=\sigma(1)\,\sigma(2)\,\cdots\,\sigma(n-1)\,\sigma(n)
. We use the classic notation for writing down the cycle decomposition of a permutation. For example, if
\sigma=4\,6\,3\,5\,1\,2\,9\,8\,7\in\mathfrak{S}_{9}

(1\,4\,5)(2\,6)(3)(7\,9)(8)
. To a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}

\sigma

\sigma=4\,6\,3\,5\,1\,2\,9\,8\,7

C\left(\sigma\right)=(2,2,1,0,0,0,0,0,0)
as
\sigma

Problem 8.1 in
[
1
]
asks about the relationship between the cycle decomposition of a permutation
\sigma

\sigma^{\odot k}

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes the symmetric tensor power of permutations and some basic properties. 2.
Symmetric Tensor Power of Permutations

One can represent a given permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
as a
n\times n
matrix
A_{\sigma}
defined by

(2)

\displaystyle\left[A_{\sigma}\right]_{i,j}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\sigma_{i}=j,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Left multiplication by
\textbf{v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})
produces as an image the vector
(v_{\sigma(1)},v_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(n)})
. This representation of a permutation by a matrix can be used to define the
k

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 1}
,
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and
k\geq 1
a positive integer. We denote by
\sigma^{\odot k}
the permutation defined by the matrix
A_{\sigma^{\odot k}}:=A_{\sigma}^{\odot k}
.

That is, to create the symmetric tensor power, first convert the permutation
\sigma
into its corresponding permutation matrix
A_{\sigma}
, apply the symmetric tensor power
A_{\sigma}^{\odot k}
using Equation
1
. The permutation that defines the symmetric tensor power of
A_{\sigma}
will be the symmetric tensor power
\sigma^{\odot k}
of
\sigma
. The following Proposition (Lemma A.1 in
[
1
]
), stated here without a proof, guaranties that

is a permutation matrix.

Proposition 2.1

(Lemma A.1 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 1}
,
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and
A_{\sigma}
be the

matrix defined as in Equation
2
. For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 1}
, the matrix
A_{\sigma^{\odot k}}=A_{\sigma}^{\odot k}
is a permutation matrix of size
\binom{n+k-1}{k}\times\binom{n+k-1}{k}

Given a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, one can compute its order, indicated by
\mathrm{ord}(\sigma)
, and defined as the smallest number greater than zero such that
\sigma^{\operatorname{ord}(\sigma)}:=\underbrace{\sigma\circ\sigma\circ\cdots\circ\sigma}_{\operatorname{ord}(\sigma)\text{ times}}
produces the identity permutation
\mathtt{Id}=1\,2\,\cdots\,n
. The value
\mathrm{ord}(\sigma)
can be computed using the least common multiple of the lengths of the cycles of
\sigma
. The following Theorem shows that the order is fixed by taking symmetric tensor powers.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n
and
k
be positive integers. Given
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we have
\mathrm{ord}(\sigma)=\mathrm{ord}(\sigma^{\odot k})
.

Proof.

Let
p=\mathrm{ord}(\sigma)
. As shown in Example
2
, we can think of
\sigma^{\odot k}
as a permutation of elements in
\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
by letting
\sigma
act diagonally on the composition sequence; for a composition
i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\odot k})^{p}(i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k})

\displaystyle=\sigma^{p}(i_{1})\sigma^{p}(i_{2})\cdots\sigma^{p}(i_{k})

\displaystyle=i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}.

This implies
\mathrm{ord(\sigma^{\odot k}})\leq p
. Now, suppose that
q:=\mathrm{ord}(\sigma^{\odot k})<p
, then for each
i\in[n]
, we have
(\sigma^{\odot k})^{q}(ii\cdots i)=\sigma^{q}(i)\cdots\sigma^{q}(i)=ii\cdots i
, and hence
\sigma^{q}(i)=i
for all
i\in[n]
, implying that
p\leq q<p
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\mathrm{ord}(\sigma)=\mathrm{ord}(\sigma^{\odot k})
.
∎

In the following, we state some basic facts about the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials (see Section 7.4 in
[
6
]
), the trace of the symmetric tensor power, and the eigenvalues of permutation matrices that will be used in Section 3 to compute the number of fixed points of the symmetric tensor power of a permutation. The connection of the fixed points of a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with the trace of some matrix is that if
A_{\sigma}
is defined as in Equation
2
, then
\operatorname{tr}(A_{\sigma})=[A_{\sigma}]_{1,1}+[A_{\sigma}]_{2,2}+\cdots[A_{\sigma}]_{n,n}
which counts the number of elements
i\in[n]
such that
\sigma(i)=i
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
n,k
be positive integers. The complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial in variables
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}
is defined as

(3)

\displaystyle h_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}):=\sum_{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}=k}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a_{i}},

where the sum is over all size
n
compositions of
k
.

These polynomials are symmetric, permuting the variables leaves the polynomial unchanged, i.e.,
h_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=h_{k}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})
, for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
One way to compute these polynomials
h_{k}
is to use the following recursion.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k,n
be positive integers and
\ell\in[n]
, then

h_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{\ell},\cdots,x_{n})=h_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x}_{\ell},\cdots.\,x_{n})+x_{\ell}h_{k-1}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{\ell},\cdots,x_{n}),

where
\hat{x}_{\ell}
denotes that the variable is missing.

Proof.

From Equation
3
, we have the following

\displaystyle h_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{\ell}+\cdots+a_{n}=k}x_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}}\prod_{i\neq\ell}x_{i}^{a_{i}}.

There are two cases to consider, either
a_{\ell}=0
or
a_{\ell}>0
. If the former is true, then the sum is over size
n-1
compositions of
k
and
x_{\ell}
does not appear, which yields
h_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x}_{\ell},\cdots,x_{n})
. For the case
a_{\ell}>0
, we can factor out a
x_{\ell}
and the degree
k
of each monomial decreases by one, which yields
x_{\ell}h_{k-1}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{\ell},\cdots,x_{n})
. Adding the two cases gives the result.

∎

The connection between the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial and the symmetric tensor power is that the trace of the
k
-th symmetric tensor power corresponds to the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial evaluated at the eigenvalues of the matrix
A
. This is stated without a proof below.

Proposition 2.2

(Corollary 4.1 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
matrix with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
and let
k\geq 1
, then
\operatorname{tr}\left(A^{\odot k}\right)=h_{k}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
.

To use Proposition
2.2
when
A
is a permutation matrix, we must understand the eigenvalues of a permutation matrix. The following proposition tells us that the eigenvalues and their multiplicities are totally determined by the cycle type of the permutation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
\ell\in[n]
. A cycle of length
\ell
in the decomposition of
\sigma
contributes once to the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{\ell}}
for
0\leq k<\ell-1
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma=(a_{1}\,a_{2}\,\dots\,a_{\ell})
be any cycle of length
\ell
, and let
k
be a non-negative integer
0\leq k<\ell-1
. We explicitly construct an eigenvector for
\lambda=e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{\ell}}
. Let
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})
be defined as
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda^{i-1}e_{a_{i}}
, where
e_{i}=(0,\cdots,1,\cdots,0)
denotes the
i
-th element of the standard basis.
v
contains powers of
\lambda
exactly at the positions of the elements that belong to the cycle.
This construction implies that
\lambda
is an eigenvalue for
A_{\sigma}
with eigenvector
\mathbf{v}
noticing that for
j
not in the cycle,
(A_{\sigma}\mathbf{v})_{j}=0=(\lambda\mathbf{v})_{j}
; for
i\in[\ell-1]
, we have
(A\mathbf{v})_{a_{i}}=v_{a_{i+1}}=(\lambda\mathbf{v})_{a_{i}}
; and for the case
i=\ell
, we have
(A\mathbf{v})_{a_{\ell}}=\mathbf{v}_{1}=1=(\lambda\mathbf{v})_{a_{\ell}}

therefore,
A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}
.
Each cycle of length
\ell
contributes exactly
\ell
eigenvalues. All eigenvectors corresponding to different cycles are linearly independent, even if their eigenvalues coincide, so the set of all eigenvectors has size
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ell\cdot C_{\ell}(\sigma)=n
. This set is a basis for
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, ensuring that each cycle contributes exactly once for the multiplicity of each of its eigenvalues.
∎

Lemma
2.2
implies that for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the multiplicity of
\lambda=1
counts the number of cycles in the decomposition of
\sigma
and the multiplicity of
-1
counts the number of cycles of even length in the decomposition of
\sigma
.

for the two cycles of lenghts

each with multiplicity one.

3.
The number of Fixed Points of the symmetric tensor power

Given a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the number of fixed points, that is, elements
\ell\in[n]
such that
\sigma(\ell)=\ell
, is hereafter denoted by
\texttt{Fix}(\sigma)
, i.e.,
\texttt{Fix}(\sigma)=\#\{\ell\in[n]:\sigma(\ell)=\ell\}
. For example,
\texttt{Fix}(21{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{3}}6{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{5}}4)=2
. This quantity also corresponds to
C_{1}(\sigma)
, the number of cycles of length one in the cycle type of
\sigma
. The aim of this section is to give an expression for the number of fixed points of the symmetric tensor power of any permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.

We will first start with the fixed points of the symmetric tensor power of an involution. An involution is a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
such that
\sigma^{2}=\texttt{id}
. This implies that every cycle of
\sigma
is of length one (a fixed point) or of length two. First, we notice that the identity permutation
\mathtt{Id}=1\,2\,\cdots\,n
is a trivial involution that fixes all elements, and taking the symmetric tensor power, we get the identity permutation, which implies that
\operatorname{Fix}(\mathtt{Id}^{\odot k})=\binom{n+k-1}{k}
by means of Proposition
2.1
. It is worth noticing that a non-trivial involution is an order two permutation, and so, Theorem
2.1
implies that the symmetric tensor power is also an order two permutation and hence an involution.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
1\leq r\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. Let
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be an involution with cycle decomposition
\sigma=(a_{1}b_{1})\cdots(a_{r}b_{r})
where all
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{r},b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{r}
are different. The number of fixed points in the symmetric tensor power of
\sigma
is given by

(4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fix}\left(\sigma^{\odot k}\right)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{\ell+r-1}{\ell}\binom{k-2\ell+n-2r-1}{k-2\ell}.

Proof.

To construct a fixed point
\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
of
\sigma^{\odot k}
, we have to ensure that
\textbf{m}_{a_{i}}(\textbf{x})=\textbf{m}_{b_{i}}(\textbf{x})
for all
i\in[r]
. Let
q_{i}=\textbf{m}_{a_{i}}(\textbf{x})
, by the definition of composition, we have

2(q_{1}+\cdots+q_{r})+\sum_{i\not\in\{a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2},\cdots,a_{r},b_{r}\}}\textbf{m}_{i}(\textbf{x})=k.

Let
\ell=q_{1}+\cdots+q_{r}
, then
0\leq\ell\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor
. If
\ell
is fixed, then we can choose the sequence
\{q_{i}\}_{i\in[r]}
in
\binom{\ell+r-1}{\ell}
ways using a Stars and Bars argument. If we eliminate all
a_{i}
’s and
b_{i}
’s from the composition, then we have a size
n-2r
composition of
k-2\ell
that can be counted in
\binom{(n-2r)+(k-2\ell)-1}{(n-2r)-1}
. By the multiplication principle, there are
\binom{\ell+r-1}{\ell}\binom{(n-2r)+(k-2\ell)-1}{(n-2r)-1}
such compositions. The result follows by adding over all possible
\ell
’s.
∎

Remark 1
.

For a particular case of Theorem
3.1
, when
r=1
, plugging into Equation
4
we obtain
\displaystyle\operatorname{Fix}\left((ab)^{\odot k}\right)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-2\ell+n-3}{k-2\ell}.

Instead, we can also compute the number of fixed points using Proposition
2.2
. Lemma
2.2
implies that
\lambda_{1}=1
is an eigenvalue with multiplicity
n-1
and
\lambda_{2}=-1
with multiplicity one. Using Proposition
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fix}\left((ab)^{\odot k}\right)

\displaystyle=h_{k}(\underbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-1},-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}=k}1^{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}}(-1)^{a_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\sum_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}+\ell=k}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n+k-\ell-2}{k-\ell}.

As noted above, the symmetric tensor power of an involution remains an involution, and so the permutation
(ab)^{\odot k}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, as in Remark
1
, consists only of cycles of length two and fixed points, which allows us to conclude on the number of cycles of length two as follows.

Corollary 3.1
.

The number of two cycles on the symmetric tensor power of the permutation
(ab)^{\odot k}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, where
1\leq a<b\leq n
, is given by

\displaystyle C_{2}\left((ab)^{\odot k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-2\ell+n-3}{n-3}\right)

In the following, we will determine the number of fixed points of an arbitrary length cycle using Proposition
2.2
and Lemma
2.2
. For a fixed number
\ell>1
, we denote by
f(n,k)
the number of fixed points of the
k
-th symmetric tensor power of the permutation
(1\,2\,\dots\,\ell)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, that is,
f^{(\ell)}_{n,k}=\operatorname{Fix}\left(((1\,2\,\dots\,\ell)(\ell+1)(\ell+2)\cdots(n)\right)^{\odot k})
. The next theorem describes a recursion for
f^{(\ell)}_{n,k}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\ell>1
be an integer number. For
k\geq 1
and
n\geq\ell
the following recursion is satisfied

\displaystyle f^{(\ell)}_{n,k}=\begin{cases}n-\ell&\text{if }k=1,\\
1&\text{if }n=\ell\text{ and }k\equiv 0\pmod{\ell},\\
0&\text{if }n=\ell\text{ and }k\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell},\\
f^{(\ell)}_{n-1,k}+f^{(\ell)}_{n,k-1}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The case
k=1
does not change the permutation; therefore, all numbers except those in the
\ell
-cycle are fixed points, and so there are
n-\ell
fixed points.
For the case
n=\ell
, consider the trace of the symmetric tensor power of the permutation matrix, by Proposition
2.2
,
f^{(\ell)}_{\ell,k}=h_{k}(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-1})
where
\lambda=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{\ell}}
. Now, by multiplying all the parameters by
\lambda
, that is, shifting the roots of unity, and using Equation
3
, the definition of
h_{k}
, the following equation holds
h_{k}(\lambda\cdot\lambda^{0},\lambda\cdot\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda\cdot\lambda^{\ell-1})=\lambda^{k}h_{k}(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-1}),

and by the fact that
h_{k}
is symmetric, we can rearrange the parameters to obtain

h_{k}(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-1})=\lambda^{k}h_{k}(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-1}).

This last equation implies that if
\lambda^{k}\neq 1
, then the polynomial must be zero, and hence the number of fixed points
f(\ell,k)=0
if
k\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
. Now suppose that
\ell
divides
k
, and using Lemma
2.1
, we conclude that
h_{k}(\lambda^{0},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-1})=h_{k}(\lambda^{0},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-2})+\lambda^{\ell-1}h_{k-1}(\lambda^{0},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-1}),

and since
\ell
does not divide
k-1
,
h_{k-1}(\lambda^{0},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-1})=0
. Therefore,
h_{k}(\lambda^{0},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-1})=h_{k}(\lambda^{0},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-2})
; using the argument inductively, we conclude that
h_{k}(\lambda^{0},\dots,\lambda^{\ell-1})=h_{k}(\lambda^{0})=h_{k}(1)=1
. For the recursive step, assume that
n>\ell
and hence the eigenvalue
\lambda=1
has multiplicity greater than one, and so
f^{(\ell)}_{n,k}=h_{k}(1,1,\lambda_{3},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
. Using Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle f^{(\ell)}_{n,k}

\displaystyle=h_{k}(1,1,\lambda_{3},\cdots,\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle=h_{k}(1,\lambda_{3},\cdots,\lambda_{n})+1\cdot h_{k-1}(1,1,\lambda_{3},\cdots,\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle=f^{(\ell)}_{n-1,k}+f^{(\ell)}_{n,k-1}.

The result follows.
∎

Example 4
.

In the case
\ell=3
and
k=2
, one can compute the following

\displaystyle f^{(3)}_{3,2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle f^{(3)}_{4,2}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle f^{(3)}_{5,2}

\displaystyle=3,

\displaystyle f^{(3)}_{6,2}

\displaystyle=6.

Theorem
3.2
gives the recursion
f^{(3)}_{n,2}=f^{(3)}_{n-1,2}+f^{(3)}_{n,1}
. Solving it yields
f^{(3)}_{n,2}=\binom{n-2}{2}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Given
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
having cycle type
C(\sigma)=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})
. The number of fixed points of
\sigma^{\odot k}
is given by

\operatorname{Fix}\left(\sigma^{\odot k}\right)=\sum_{s_{1}+2s_{2}+\cdots+ns_{n}=k}\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\binom{s_{\ell}+c_{\ell}-1}{s_{\ell}},

where the sum is over sequences of non-negative integers
s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{n}
.

Proof.

To construct a fixed point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
of
\sigma^{\odot k}
, the element
\mathbf{x}
must satisfy
\mathbf{m}_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{m}_{\sigma(i)}(\mathbf{x})
for all
i\in[n]
. Consequently, for each cycle
(i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{\ell})
of
\sigma
, all elements
i_{j}
must appear in
\mathbf{x}
the same number of times, that is
\textbf{m}_{i_{p}}(\textbf{x})=\textbf{m}_{i_{q}}(\textbf{x})>0
for
1\leq p,q\leq\ell
. Let
t_{\ell}
denote the total number of times an element belonging to an
\ell
-cycles in
\sigma
appears in
x
, as seen above this number must be divisible by
\ell
, and so let
s_{\ell}=\frac{t_{\ell}}{\ell}
; these numbers must satisfy
s_{1}+2s_{2}+\dots+ns_{n}=k
. For each
\ell
, we must assign the number of times each of the elements of an
\ell
-cycles appear in
x
, so this corresponds to creating a size
c_{\ell}
(the number of
\ell
-cycles) composition of
s_{\ell}
. Thus, by the Stars and Bars counting technique, the number of choices is given by
\displaystyle\binom{s_{\ell}+c_{\ell}-1}{s_{\ell}}
and by the multiplication principle there are

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\binom{s_{\ell}+c_{\ell}-1}{s_{\ell}}
options.
The result follows by adding all valid choices of
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}
.
∎

4.
Cycle Structure of the Symmetric Tensor Power of a Cycle

In this section, we extend Theorem
3.2
to give a formula for the number of
s
-cycles of the symmetric tensor power of the permutation
(1\,2\,\cdots\,\ell)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Let
\ell>1
and
s\leq\ell
and consider
C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k}:=C_{s}((1\,2\,\cdots\,\ell)^{\odot k})
. The case
\ell=1
was considered in Section 3 and gives
C^{(1,1)}_{n,k}=\binom{n+k-1}{k}
and
C^{(1,s)}_{n,k}=0
for
s>1
. The following Theorem gives a recursive expression for
C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
s
and
\ell
be integer numbers such that
1\leq s\leq\ell
and
1<\ell
. For integer numbers
n
and
k
such that
\ell\leq n
, the following recursion is satisfied.

(5)

\displaystyle C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }k=1\text{ and }s=\ell,\\
n-\ell&\text{ if }k=1\text{ and }s=1,\\
0&\text{ if }k=1\text{ and }s\not\in\{1,\ell\},\\
\displaystyle\left[s\mid\ell\text{ and }\frac{\ell}{s}\mid k\right]\frac{\ell}{s\ell+sk}\sum_{e|(s,(sk)/\ell)}\mu(e)\binom{(s+sk/\ell)/e}{s/e}&\text{ if }k>1\text{ and }n=\ell,\\
C^{(\ell,s)}_{n-1,k}+C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k-1}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Here, the expression
\left[P\right]=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }P\text{ is true}\\
0&\text{ if }P\text{ is false}\end{cases},
denotes the Iverson Bracket(see Section 2.1 in
[
4
]
), and
\mu(m)
is the Möbius multiplicative function(see Section 3.7 in
[
6
]
).

Proof.

For
k=1
, we recover the same permutation we started with, and by definition, this contains one cycle of length
\ell
and
n-\ell
fixed points. Let
k>1
and
n>\ell
, and consider a composition
\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
in a cycle of length
s
. There are two options:
\textbf{m}_{n}(\textbf{x})=0
or
\textbf{m}_{n}(\textbf{x})>0
. If the former is true, then
\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{n-1,k}
as it is still a composition of
k
that belongs to a cycle of length
s
, and if the latter is true, then by eliminating one of the
n
’s from
x
we can form a new composition
\textbf{y}\in\mathcal{C}_{n,k-1}
, a composition of
k-1
that belongs to a cycle of length
s
. This gives the recursive step
C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k}=C^{(\ell,s)}_{n-1,k}+C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k-1}
. For the case
n=\ell
, notice that there are no cycles of length
s
if
s
does not divide
\ell
, the order of the permutation, by Theorem
2.1
. If
s
divides
\ell
and
\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
is in a cycle of length
s
, then
\textbf{m}_{i}(\textbf{x})=\textbf{m}_{j}(\textbf{x})
if and only if
i\equiv j\pmod{s}
, and we have

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{\ell}{s}(\textbf{m}_{1}(\textbf{x})+\textbf{m}_{2}(\textbf{x})+\cdots+\textbf{m}_{s}(\textbf{x}))=k,

this implies that
\frac{\ell}{s}
must divide
k
for this element
x
to exist. The only case left is if
n=\ell
,
s\mid\ell
and
\frac{\ell}{s}\mid k
. For this case, consider the following equation

\displaystyle\sum_{e\mid s}e\cdot C^{(\ell,e)}_{\ell,k}=\binom{s+\frac{k}{\ell/s}-1}{s-1}.

The right hand side of this equation is the number of compositions
\textbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{n,k}
that satisfy Equation
6
. The left hand side is another way to count these compositions. Notice that these compositions are such that
\sigma^{s}(\textbf{x})=\textbf{x}
and this implies that
x
is in a cycle of length
e\in\mathbb{Z}
, a divisor of
s
. Adding the number of compositions over all possible cycles of length
e
, a divisor of
s
, the equation is satisfied. We can condense all these cases when
n=\ell
in the following equation using the Iverson Bracket.

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\mid s}e\cdot C^{(\ell,e)}_{\ell,k}=\left[s\mid\ell\text{ and }\frac{\ell}{s}\mid k\right]\binom{s+\frac{k}{\ell/s}-1}{s-1}.

Applying the Möbius inversion formula (see Section 3.7 in
[
6
]
) to Equation
7
, we obtain

(8)

\displaystyle s\cdot C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,k}=\sum_{e\mid s}\mu(e)\left[\frac{s}{e}\mid\ell\text{ and }\frac{\ell}{\frac{s}{e}}\mid k\right]\binom{s/e+\frac{k}{\ell/(s/e)}-1}{s/e-1}.

Notice first that if
\frac{\ell}{\frac{s}{e}}\mid k
then there exists
q\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
q\cdot\frac{e\ell}{s}=k
, hence
q\cdot e=\frac{s\cdot k}{\ell}
and so for the Iverson Bracket to be true we must impose
e\mid\frac{s\cdot k}{\ell}
. Now notice that the binomial coefficient on the right hand side of Equation
8
can be written as

\binom{s/e+\frac{k}{\ell/(s/e)}-1}{s/e-1}=\frac{s/e}{s/e+\frac{k}{\ell/(s/e)}}\binom{s/e+\frac{k}{\ell/(s/e)}}{s/e}=\frac{\ell}{\ell+k}\binom{\frac{s+sk/\ell}{e}}{s/e}
. Plugging these two observations into Equation
8
, we get the result.
∎

(see Table

for the case

) a sequence that was studied in the context of the Hermite reciprocity law (see

). It also appears as cases of the number of ordered
k

s
modulo
n
(see
[
2

). Let
\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)=\sum_{k\geq 1,n\geq\ell}C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k}x^{n}y^{k}
be the generating function of the sequence
C_{s}(n,k)
. The following proposition gives an expression for
\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
s,\ell
be integer numbers such that
1\leq s\leq\ell
and
1<\ell
. The bivariate generating function
\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)
is given in closed form by

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{s}(x,y)(1-y)+\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)(1-x)-[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y}{1-x-y},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)

\displaystyle=[s=l]x^{l}y+\frac{[s|\ell]}{s}x^{\ell}\sum_{e|s}\mu(s/e)\left(\frac{1}{(1-y^{\frac{e\ell}{s}})^{e}}-\sum_{r=0}^{\lceil 2e/\ell\rceil-1}\binom{e+r-1}{e-1}y^{re\ell/s}\right),\,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)

\displaystyle=[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y+[s=1]\frac{x^{\ell+1}y}{(1-x)^{2}}.

Moreover,

(10)

\displaystyle C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k}=\displaystyle\frac{[s\mid\ell]}{s}\sum_{e\mid s}\mu(s/e)\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor\frac{ke}{\ell}\rfloor}\binom{e+r-1}{e-1}\binom{k-\frac{r\ell}{e}+n-\ell-1}{k-\frac{r\ell}{e}}

Proof.

First, letting
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)=\sum_{k\geq 1}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,k}x^{\ell}y^{k}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq\ell}C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,1}x^{n}y^{1}
, it is clear that the following is a valid decomposition
\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)=\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)+\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)+\sum_{n>\ell,k>1}C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k}x^{n}y^{k}-C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,1}x^{\ell}y.

The last term involves
C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,1}
which is one exactly when
s=\ell
and zero otherwise. Using the recursion in Theorem
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)+\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n>\ell\\
k>1\end{subarray}}\left(C^{(\ell,s)}_{n-1,k}+C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k-1}\right)x^{n}y^{k}-[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)+\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n>\ell\\
k>1\end{subarray}}C^{(\ell,s)}_{n-1,k}x^{n}y^{k}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n>\ell\\
k>1\end{subarray}}C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k-1}x^{n}y^{k}-[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)+\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)+x\left(\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)-\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)\right)+y\left(\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)-\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)\right)-[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y.

Solving for
\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)
, Equation
9
follows. Now, consider rewriting Equation
9
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)(1-y)}{(1-y)-x}+\frac{\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)(1-x)}{(1-x)-y}-\frac{[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y}{1-x-y}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)}{1-\frac{x}{1-y}}+\frac{\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)}{1-\frac{y}{1-x}}-\frac{[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y}{1-x-y}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}}{(1-y)^{n}}+\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{y^{k}}{(1-x)^{k}}-\frac{[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y}{1-x-y}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{k+n-1}{k}y^{k}}_{*_{1}}+\underbrace{\sum_{k\geq 0}y^{k}\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{k+n-1}{n}x^{n}}_{*_{2}}-\frac{[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y}{1-x-y}.

Plugging
\mathcal{A}_{s,\ell}(x,y)
into the summation
*_{1}
yields

\displaystyle*_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,k}x^{\ell}y^{k}\right)\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{k+n-1}{k}y^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n}x^{\ell}\sum_{k\geq 1}y^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}=k\\
k_{1}\geq 1,k_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,k_{1}}\binom{k_{2}+n-1}{k_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}x^{n+\ell}\sum_{k\geq 1}y^{k}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{k}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,k_{1}}\binom{k-k_{1}+n-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq\ell\\
k\geq 1\end{subarray}}x^{n}y^{k}\sum_{r=1}^{k}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,r}\binom{k-r+n-\ell-1}{k-r}.

Analogously, a similar expression for the summation
*_{2}
involving
\mathcal{B}_{s,\ell}(x,y)
, gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{s,\ell}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq\ell\\
k\geq 1\end{subarray}}x^{n}y^{k}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,r}\binom{k-r+n-\ell-1}{k-r}+\sum_{r=\ell}^{n}C^{(\ell,s)}_{r,1}\binom{k+n-r-2}{n-r}\right)-\frac{[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y}{1-x-y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq\ell\\
k\geq 1\end{subarray}}x^{n}y^{k}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,r}\binom{k-r+n-\ell-1}{k-r}+\sum_{r=\ell}^{n}C^{(\ell,s)}_{r,1}\binom{k+n-r-2}{n-r}\right)-[s=\ell]x^{\ell}y\sum_{n\geq 0}(x+y)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq\ell\\
k\geq 1\end{subarray}}x^{n}y^{k}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,r}\binom{k-r+n-\ell-1}{k-r}+\sum_{r=\ell}^{n}C^{(\ell,s)}_{r,1}\binom{k+n-r-2}{n-r}\right)-[s=\ell]\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{n}{k}y^{k}x^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq\ell\\
k\geq 1\end{subarray}}x^{n}y^{k}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,r}\binom{k-r+n-\ell-1}{k-r}+\sum_{r=\ell}^{n}C^{(\ell,s)}_{r,1}\binom{k+n-r-2}{n-r}-[s=\ell]\binom{n-\ell+k-1}{k-1}\right).

Taking the coefficient of

[

\displaystyle C^{(\ell,s)}_{n,k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,r}\binom{k-r+n-\ell-1}{k-r}+\sum_{r=\ell}^{n}C^{(\ell,s)}_{r,1}\binom{k+n-r-2}{n-r}-[s=\ell]\binom{n-\ell+k-1}{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}C^{(\ell,s)}_{\ell,r}\binom{k-r+n-\ell-1}{k-r}+[s=1]\binom{k+n-\ell-1}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{[s\mid\ell]}{s}\sum_{e\mid s}\mu(s/e)\sum_{r=1}^{k}\left[\frac{\ell}{e}\mid r\right]\binom{e+\frac{er}{\ell}-1}{e-1}\binom{k-r+n-\ell-1}{k-r}+[s=1]\binom{k+n-\ell-1}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{[s\mid\ell]}{s}\sum_{e\mid s}\mu(s/e)\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{ke}{\ell}\rfloor}\binom{e+r-1}{e-1}\binom{k-\frac{r\ell}{e}+n-\ell-1}{k-\frac{r\ell}{e}}+[s=1]\binom{k+n-\ell-1}{k},
