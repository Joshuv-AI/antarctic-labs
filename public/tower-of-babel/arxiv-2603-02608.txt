1.
Introduction

The Brauer group is an important invariant of schemes and algebraic stacks. The cohomological Brauer group of an algebraic variety
Y
is defined as the torsion subgroup of
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\mathbb{G}_{m})
; moreover, if
Y
is smooth, then
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Y,\mathbb{G}_{m})
is torsion. The Brauer group of the moduli spaces measures the obstruction to the existence of a universal bundle. It also plays a central role in questions of rationality. In this article, we aim to compute the Brauer groups of certain moduli spaces.

Parabolic vector bundles, introduced by V. B. Mehta and C. S. Seshadri
[
MS
]
, refine classical vector bundles by incorporating weighted flags along a divisor. Parabolic connections encode linear differential equations with regular singularities compatible with these filtrations. Via the Hodge moduli space of
\lambda
-connections, introduced by Carlos Simpson, the moduli space of parabolic Higgs bundles arises as the
\lambda=0
fiber, while the moduli space of parabolic connections appears as the
\lambda=1
fiber, thereby relating the two spaces through a natural deformation. The Brauer groups of the moduli space of stable parabolic bundles and the moduli space of vector bundles with logarithmic connections are studied in
[
BBS
,
BD
]
. In this article, we extend those results to the moduli space of stable parabolic connections. In a similar spirit, we compute the Brauer group of the moduli space of stable strongly parabolic Higgs bundles. Our main results are as follows.

Two logarithmic connections

\scriptstyle{\nabla^{\prime}}
\textstyle{E^{\prime}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)}

We now come to the notion of the residue of a logarithmic connection. For each point

using Poincaré adjunction formula. To be more explicit, if

\omega=a\,\frac{dz}{z}+\eta

.
The adjunction map is given by sending
\omega
to

.
This description is independent of the choice of the local coordinate

E\xrightarrow{\,\,\,\nabla\,\,\,}E\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(S)\longrightarrow\left(E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(S)\right)_{x}\simeq E_{x}

-linear. This gives rise to a

-linear endomorphism of

2.2.
parabolic vector bundles and parabolic connections

Definition 2.1
.

A

of rank

E

r

\displaystyle E_{x}=:E^{x}_{1}

\displaystyle\,\supsetneq\,E^{x}_{2}\,\supsetneq\,\cdots\,\supsetneq\,E^{x}_{\ell(x)}\,\supsetneq\,E^{x}_{\ell(x)+1}\,=\,0

(2.5)

\displaystyle 0\,\leq\,

\displaystyle\alpha^{x}_{1}\,<\,\alpha^{x}_{2}\,<\,\cdots\,<\,\alpha^{x}_{\ell(x)}\,<\,1.

\bullet

\bullet

We shall often denote a parabolic vector bundle simply by

and suppress the parabolic data, when there is no scope of confusion. When

, The

The

of

is defined as the real number

p\mu(E_{*}):=\frac{p\mathrm{deg}(E_{*})}{r}\,.

Let

be a parabolic bundle and

The vector bundle

A parabolic vector bundle

Definition 2.3

Let

be a parabolic vector bundle on

(i)

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider a parabolic data
(\boldsymbol{m,\alpha})
along

\boldsymbol{\alpha}

Proof.

Let
\text{PC}_{\xi}(E_{*})

Given
\Phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(E_{*})

\text{PC}^{s}_{\xi}(E_{*})/\mathrm{Aut}(E_{*})

\mathrm{Aut}(E_{*})

\text{PC}_{\xi}(E_{*})

\text{PC}^{s}_{\xi}(E_{*})/\mathrm{Aut}(E_{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\,-\chi(\mathrm{ParEnd}^{\prime}(E_{*})),

.

.

The space of all isomorphism classes of parabolic vector bundles over
X
whose Harder-Narasimhan polygon coincides with the given parabolic vector bundle

is of dimension at most

\displaystyle r^{2}(g-1)-(r-1)(g-2)-g+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\sum_{j^{\prime}>j}m^{i}_{j}m^{i}_{j^{\prime}}.

(4.6)

This follows from the analogous techniques of
[
BM
, Lemma 3.1, page no. 303]
.

By Lemma
4.1
, the dimension of the space of all isomorphism classes of parabolic connections on any given parabolic vector bundle

is at most

\displaystyle(r^{2}-1)(g-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\sum_{j^{\prime}>j}m^{i}_{j}m^{i}_{j^{\prime}}.

(4.7)

It follows that the dimension

is at most the sum of (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), namely

\dim(Z)\leq r^{2}(g-1)-(r-1)(g-2)-g+(r^{2}-1)(g-1)+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\sum_{j^{\prime}>j}m^{i}_{j}m^{i}_{j^{\prime}}\,.

Since we have (cf. (
3.4
))

\mathrm{dim}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,\xi)\right)=2(r^{2}-1)(g-1)+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\sum_{j^{\prime}>j}m^{i}_{j}m^{i}_{j^{\prime}}\,,

we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,\xi)\right)-\dim Z

\displaystyle\geq(r^{2}-1)(g-1)-r^{2}(g-1)+(r-1)(g-2)+g

\displaystyle=(r-1)(g-2)+1\,.

(4.8)

Our claim thus follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}\left(r,\xi\right)
denotes the moduli space of stable parabolic vector bundles on

of rank

, determinant
\xi

\displaystyle\pi:\,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\,\xrightarrow{\hskip 16.38895pt\hskip 8.19447pt}\,\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}\left(r,\xi\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle(E_{*},\nabla)\mapsto E_{*}

The morphism

∎

We are now in a position to compute the Brauer group for the moduli space

Theorem 4.4
.

If
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is a generic system of weights as in Definition

6.

\text{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-connections

6.1.
Action of torsion line bundles on the moduli of parabolic connections

Let

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\{M\in\Pic(X)\,\mid\,M^{\otimes r}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}\}

(6.1)

denote the group of
r
-torsion line bundles on
X
. It is a finite abelian group of order
r^{2g}
. We aim to construct an action of
\Gamma
on the moduli of parabolic stable connections
\mathcal{M}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,\xi)
(see Definition
3.1
), and study its fixed point locus.

The trivial line bundle always admits a holomorphic connection, namely the differential
d:\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X}
. This will be called as the
trivial connection
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The following result should be well-known to experts; we provide a proof here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
L
a holomorphic line bundle on
X
satisfying
L^{\otimes r}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
. There exists a unique holomorphic connection
D_{L}
on
L
such that the induced connection
D_{L}^{\otimes r}
on
L^{\otimes r}
coincides with the trivial connection
d
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
under the identification.

Proof.

For any line bundle
M
, let
at(M)\in H^{1}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
denote its Atiyah class. It is well-known that
M
admits a holomorphic connection iff
at(M)=0

[
A
, Theorem 2]
.

Choose an isomorphism
\varphi:L^{\otimes r}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The trivial connection
d
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
can be transferred as a holomorphic connection on
L^{\otimes r}
via
\varphi
. Since the Atiyah class of a line bundle coincides with its first Chern class, we get

\displaystyle at(L^{\otimes r})=c_{1}(L^{\otimes r})

\displaystyle=0

(6.2)

\displaystyle\implies r\cdot c_{1}(L)

\displaystyle=0.

(6.3)

It follows that
c_{1}(L)=at(L)=0
, as
c_{1}(L)\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. Consequently,
L
admits a holomorphic connection, say
D
. Consider
D^{\otimes r}
as a connection on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
by transferring it via
\varphi
. In other words, consider the following connection
\mathcal{O}_{X}
:

D^{\prime}:=(\varphi\otimes\Id_{\Omega^{1}_{X}})\circ D^{\otimes r}\circ\varphi^{-1}\,.

Now, since the space of holomorphic connections on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is an affine space modeled over
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
, it follows that there exists a holomorphic 1-form
\omega
on
X
satisfying

\displaystyle D^{\prime}-\omega\,=\,d\,.

(6.4)

Let
\omega^{\prime}:=\Id_{L^{\otimes r}}\otimes\omega\in H^{0}(X,\,\text{End}(L^{\otimes r})\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1})
, and let
D_{L}:=D-\dfrac{1}{r}\omega^{\prime}
be the resulting connection on
L
. It follows that

D_{L}^{\otimes r}=D^{\otimes r}-r\cdot\dfrac{1}{r}\omega^{\prime}=D^{\otimes r}-\omega^{\prime}\,,

and consequently,

\displaystyle(\varphi\otimes\Id_{\Omega^{1}_{X}})\circ D_{L}^{\otimes r}\circ\varphi^{-1}=(\varphi\otimes\Id_{\Omega^{1}_{X}})\circ(D^{\otimes r}-\omega^{\prime})\circ\varphi^{-1}\,=\,D^{\prime}-\omega=d.

(6.5)

In other words,
D_{L}^{\otimes r}
coincides with the trivial connection
d
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
via
\varphi
.

To show uniqueness, suppose
L
admits another connection
D_{0}
satisfying

(\varphi\otimes\Id_{\Omega^{1}_{X}})\circ D_{0}^{\otimes r}\circ\varphi^{-1}=d.

Now,
D_{L}=D_{0}+(\Id_{L})\otimes\eta
for some holomorphic
1
-form
\eta
on
X
. It follows that

D^{\otimes r}_{L}=D^{\otimes r}_{0}+r\cdot(\Id_{L^{\otimes r}})\otimes\eta\,,

which implies
(\varphi\otimes\Id_{\Omega^{1}_{X}})\circ D^{\otimes r}_{L}\circ\varphi^{-1}=(\varphi\otimes\Id_{\Omega^{1}_{X}})\circ D^{\otimes r}_{0}\circ\varphi^{-1}+r\cdot\eta
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Since

(\varphi\otimes\Id_{\Omega^{1}_{X}})\circ D^{\otimes r}_{L}\circ\varphi^{-1}=(\varphi\otimes\Id_{\Omega^{1}_{X}})\circ D^{\otimes r}_{0}\circ\varphi^{-1}=d\,,

we obtain
\eta=0
, showing uniqueness of
D_{L}
. Finally, if we choose another isomorphism
\varphi^{\prime}
between
L^{\otimes r}
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, then
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
differ by a nonzero scalar. It then follows from the above calculation that
D_{L}
is independent of the choice of the isomorphism
\varphi
.
∎

Using Lemma

, one can define an action of
\Gamma
on

as follows: given

where

for locally defined sections

Let us give some details on why this indeed defines an action. First, note that

\det(E\otimes L)\simeq\det(E)\otimes L^{\otimes r}\simeq\xi.

Also, it is straightforward to see that each square in the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{\det(E\otimes L)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\det(\nabla\otimes D_{L})}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\det(E\otimes L)\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\det(E)\otimes L^{\otimes r}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\det(\nabla)\otimes D_{L}^{\otimes r}}
\scriptstyle{\Id_{\det(E)}\otimes\varphi}
\textstyle{\det(E)\otimes L^{\otimes r}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}(D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The outermost arrows imply that

as logarithmic connections. Moreover, at each

. Consider the underlying vector bundle
E
with the logarithmic connection
\nabla
along
S
. By
[
BH
, Theorem 3.1]
, there exists a vector bundle
F
of rank
\frac{r}{m}
over
Y
together with a logarithmic connection
\nabla_{F}
along
\gamma^{-1}(S)
satisfying
(\gamma_{*}F,\gamma_{*}\nabla_{F})\xrightarrow{\simeq}(E,\nabla)
.

Let us briefly recall the construction of
F
. Since
(E_{*},\nabla)
is a fixed point under the action of
L
, there exists an isomorphism
\varphi:E\xrightarrow{\,\,\simeq\,\,}E\otimes L
. The line bundle
\gamma^{*}L
admits a tautological trivialization
\theta:\gamma^{*}L\xrightarrow{\,\,\simeq\,\,}\mathcal{O}_{Y_{L}}
. Composing the pullback
\gamma^{*}\varphi
with
\theta
gives rise to an automorphism
\phi:\gamma^{*}E\xrightarrow{\,\,\simeq\,\,}\gamma^{*}E
. Then
\gamma^{*}E
splits as a direct sum of eigenspace sub-bundles, and
F
is obtained as one eigenspace sub-bundle of
\gamma^{*}E
under
\phi
.
Moreover, for any
x\in S
and
y\in\gamma^{-1}(S)
, there exists a filtration of
F_{y}
by eigenspaces of
\phi_{y}

[
BC
, Proposition 3.3]
. Assigning a system of weights appropriately to these filtrations induces a parabolic structure on
F_{*}
along
\gamma^{-1}(S)
, such that the parabolic pushforward
\gamma_{*}(F_{*})
coincides with
E_{*}
(see
[
BC
, Proposition 3.3]
for more details).
Moreover,
F
is preserved by
\gamma^{*}\nabla
by construction, and the logarithmic connection
\nabla_{F}
is nothing but the restriction of
\gamma^{*}\nabla
to
F

[
BH
, Theorem 3.1]
.

We claim that
\nabla_{F}
is a parabolic connection with respect to the induced parabolic structure on
F
as above. First of all, since
\gamma
is étale and thus unramified, we have
\text{Res}_{y}(\gamma^{*}\nabla)=\text{Res}_{x}(\nabla)
under the identification
(\gamma^{*}E)_{y}=E_{x}

[
BA
, § 5.2]
. Consequently,
\text{Res}_{y}(\gamma^{*}\nabla)
is a semisimple endomorphism of
(\gamma^{*}E)_{y}
. Since
\nabla_{F}
is the restriction of
\gamma^{*}\nabla
to
F
, it immediately follows that
\text{Res}_{y}(\nabla_{F})
is a semisimple endomorphism of
F_{y}
. Moreover, since
\text{Res}_{y}(\gamma^{*}D_{L})=0
due to
D_{L}
being holomorphic, we have

\text{Res}_{y}\left(\gamma^{*}\nabla\otimes\gamma^{*}D_{L}\right)=\text{Res}_{y}(\gamma^{*}\nabla)\otimes\Id_{(\gamma^{*}L)_{y}}.

From this, and the description of the automorphism
\varphi
of
\gamma^{*}E
given above, it can be easily shown that
\text{Res}_{y}(\gamma^{*}\nabla)
commutes with
\phi_{y}
. Thus,
\text{Res}_{y}(\gamma^{*}\nabla)
preserves the parabolic filtration of
F_{y}
given by eigenspaces of
\phi_{y}
constructed above. Finally, it is not difficult to see from the construction of the weighted filtration of
F_{y}
in
[
BC
, Proposition 3.3]
that
\nabla_{F}
acts as a scalar given by the appropriate weight on the successive quotients of the filtration, arising from the fact
\text{Res}_{y}(\nabla_{F})=\text{Res}_{x}(\nabla)
and that
\nabla
is a parabolic connection. It follows that
\nabla_{F}
satisfies all conditions of Definition
2.3
. Thus,
(F_{*},\,\nabla_{F})
is a parabolic connection on
Y
of rank
\frac{r}{m}
satisfying

(\gamma_{*}F_{*},\,\gamma_{*}\nabla_{F})\xrightarrow{\,\,\simeq\,\,}(E_{*},\nabla)\,.

Moreover, by
[
BA
, Theorem 1.1 (2)]
,
(F_{*},\nabla_{F})
is parabolic semistable, and from the parabolic stability of
(E_{*},\nabla)
it follows that
(F_{*},\nabla_{F})
must be parabolic stable as well, since otherwise the existence of a sub-bundle
F^{\prime}\subset F
preserved under
\nabla_{F}
satisfying
p\mu(F^{\prime}_{*})\geq p\mu(F_{*})
would imply that
\gamma_{*}F^{\prime}_{*}\subset\gamma_{*}F=E
is a sub-bundle preserved under
\nabla
which satisfies
p\mu(\gamma_{*}{F^{\prime}_{*}})\geq p\mu(\gamma_{*}{F_{*}})=p\mu(E_{*})
. This contradicts the parabolic stability of
(E_{*},\nabla)
. Thus
f
is surjective.
∎

If the cardinality of

. Say, for example,

;

analogous to

), and replace

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
be a generic system of weights. The codimension of the closed subscheme

\displaystyle Z_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:=\bigcup_{L\in\Gamma\setminus\{\mathcal{O}_{X}\}}\mathcal{M}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,\xi)^{L}\,\subset\,\mathcal{M}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,\xi)

(6.14)

is at least three.

Proof.

The calculations for the codimension estimate are very similar to the codimension estimate considered in
[
BC
, Corollary 3.5]
. The heuristic principle here is the fact that the dimension of a space consisting of parabolic connections with a certain property like stability is
twice
the dimension of the space consisting of ordinary parabolic vector bundles having the same property. (For example, we have
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,\xi)=2\dim\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,\xi)
; see (
3.4
)).
Apart from this distinction, all calculations remain exactly similar as in
[
BC
, Corollary 3.5]
. We omit the details.
∎

7.
Brauer group of the moduli stack of stable parabolic
\text{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-connections

Definition 7.1
.

A
stable parabolic
\textnormal{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-
connection
is an equivalence class of stable parabolic connections, where two parabolic stable connections
(E_{*},\,\nabla)
and
(E^{\prime}_{*},\,\nabla^{\prime})

are considered equivalent if there exists an
r
-torsion line bundle
L
together with an isomorphism of parabolic connections
(E^{\prime}_{*},\,\nabla^{\prime})\,\xrightarrow{\,\,\simeq\,\,}\,(E_{*}\otimes L,\,\nabla\otimes D_{L})
, where
D_{L}
is the unique connection on
L
constructed in Lemma
6.1
.

Definition 7.2
.

Fix a line bundle
\xi
on
X
. Suppose
i\in[0,r-1]
satisfies
i\equiv d\,\,(\text{mod}\,r)
, where
d=\deg(\xi)
. The
coarse moduli space of stable parabolic
\text{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-connections on

X
having parabolic data
(\boldsymbol{m,\alpha})
is defined as the quotient space

N_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,i):=\mathcal{M}_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,\xi)/\Gamma\,.

Also, denote by
\mathfrak{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,i)
the moduli
stack
of stable parabolic

\text{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-connections on
X
of topological type
i
and systems of multiplicities and
weights
(\boldsymbol{m,\,\alpha})
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
be a generic system of weights for connections, as in Definition
2.6
. Let
N_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,i)^{sm}
denote the smooth locus of
N_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,i)
. We have

\textnormal{Br}\left(\mathfrak{N}_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,i)\right)\,\ \simeq\,\ \textnormal{Br}\left(N_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,i)^{sm}\right).

Proof.

The idea of the proof is similar to
[
BC
, Theorem 4.2]
, so we omit some details. Let
Z_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
be as in Corollary
6.4
, and denote

U\,:=\,N_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,i)\setminus Z_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
. Then
\Gamma
acts freely on
U
. Thus the quotient variety
U/\Gamma
is smooth. Since taking quotient by the finite group
\Gamma
is a finite morphism and hence codimension-preserving, it follows from Corollary
6.4
that the complement of
U/\Gamma
in
\left(N_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,i)\right)^{sm}
is of codimension at least
3
. For a similar reason, the complement of the open sub-stack
[U/\Gamma]
in
\mathfrak{N}_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,i)
is of codimension at least
3
as well.
As
\mathfrak{N}_{pc}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}(r,i)
is a Deligne-Mumford stack, it follows that

\Br\left([U/\Gamma]\right)\,\ \simeq\,\ \Br\left(\mathfrak{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,i)\right)\,\,\,\text{(see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{BCD23}{}{}, Proposition 4.2]}).}

Since
\Gamma
acts freely on
U
, we have
U/\Gamma\simeq[U/\Gamma]
. Consequently,

\Br\left([U/\Gamma]\right)\ \simeq\ \Br\left(U/\Gamma\right).

Also, as
\Gamma
acts freely on
U
and
U
is smooth, the quotient
U/\Gamma
is also
smooth. The complement of
U/\Gamma
in
\left(\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,i)\right)^{sm}

clearly has codimension at least
3
as well. Thus the above isomorphisms imply

\Br\left(\mathfrak{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,i)\right)\,\simeq\,\Br\left(U/\Gamma\right)\,\simeq\,\Br\left(\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{m,\alpha}}_{pc}(r,i)^{sm}\right),

where the last isomorphism follows from cohomological purity
[
C
, Theorem 1.1]
. This completes the proof.
∎
