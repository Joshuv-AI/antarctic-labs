An order-theoretic characterization of C*-algebras
Samuel Tiersma

Abstract
We give an order-theoretic characterization of the essential image of the forgetful functor from the category of real/complex unital C*-algebras to the category of real/complex unital operator systems. It is based on the characterization of JB-algebras among the order unit spaces in terms of the existence of gauge-reversing bijections obtained by M. Roelands and the author in [ 22 ] . To this end, we show that a unital operator system is completely order isomorphic to a C*-algebra if and only if each of its matrix spaces admits a compatible JB-algebra structure. As an application, we prove that for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 the range of a unital n n -positive projection on a unital real C*-algebra is unitally n n -order isomorphic to a unital real C*-algebra, which is the analogue of a result proven for complex C*-algebras by Choi–Effros.



Keywords:
operator systems, JB-algebras, real C*-algebras, gauge-reversing bijections, order unit spaces, positive projections on real C*-algebras, C*-algebras

Subject Classification: 46L07, 17C65, 46L05, 46L70

C*-algebras form a natural framework for the mathematical formulation of quantum mechanics. For this reason, there has been a substantial interest in characterizations of C*-algebras in terms of the underlying order or Banach space structure
[

[

[

]
and the references therein. Impetus to the study of the noncommutative case was given by Connes’ characterization of the ordered vector spaces underlying
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebras
[
9
]
. Alfsen–Shultz gave a convex-geometric characterization of the state spaces of unital C*-algebras
[
1
]
. Characterizations of C*-algebras and related objects in terms of the existence of
holomorphic
(or analytic) symmetries have been given in various categories
[
30
,
19
,
11
,
8
]
. The main result of this article is the following
order-theoretic
characterization of unital C*-algebras among the operator systems (pertinent definitions are given later in this introduction).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be a real (resp. complex) complete unital operator system. Then
E
is unitally completely order isomorphic to a unital real (resp. complex) C*-algebra if and only if for arbitrarily large integers
n\geq 1
there exists an order-anti-isomorphism
\Phi_{n}\colon M_{n}(E)^{\circ}_{+}\to M_{n}(E)^{\circ}_{+}
which is homogeneous of degree
-1
of the interior of the positive cone of
M_{n}(E)
.

We remark that no smoothness assumptions are made on the maps
\Phi_{n}
, which are known as
gauge-reversing bijections
. The key ingredient in the proof is a characterization of JB-algebras among the order unit spaces obtain by M. Roelands and the author in
[
22
]
. We shall first discuss this result for JB-algebras, and then make the connection with operator systems.

The prototypical example of a JB-algebra is the self-adjoint part
A_{sa}
of a real or complex C*-algebra
A
, endowed with the nonassociative
Jordan product

x\circ y:=\frac{1}{2}(xy+yx)
. If
J
is a JB-algebra with identity
1_{J}
, then
J
is partially ordered by its cone of squares
J_{+}=\{x^{2}\colon x\in J\}
and
(J,J_{+},1_{J})
is an order unit space. The interior
J_{+}^{\circ}
of
J_{+}
consists of the positive invertible elements. We record the following observation which will be of cardinal importance. The inversion map
i\colon J_{+}^{\circ}\to J_{+}^{\circ}
,
i(x)=x^{-1}
is an order-anti-isomorphism which is homogeneous of degree
-1
(see e.g.
[
3
, Lemma 1.31]
).

Now let
(V,V_{+},v)
be an order unit space and denote
V^{\circ}_{+}
the interior of its positive cone. A mapping
\Phi\colon V^{\circ}_{+}\to V^{\circ}_{+}
is called a
gauge-reversing bijection
if it is an order-anti-isomorphism and homogeneous of degree
-1
, that is, for all
x,y\in V_{+}^{\circ}
and
\lambda>0
it holds that

x\leq y\iff\Phi(x)\geq\Phi(y)\quad\text{ and }\quad\Phi(\lambda x)=\lambda^{-1}\Phi(x).

(1)

Let us call a
compatible JB-algebra structure on

(V,V_{+},v)
to be a structure of JB-algebra on
V
for which
v
is the identity element and
V_{+}=\{x^{2}:x\in V\}
the cone of squares; it is unique if it exists
[
3
, Thm. 2.80]
. In view of the above observation about the inversion map in a JB-algebra, a necessary condition for the existence of a compatible JB-algebra structure on
(V,V_{+},v)
is the existence of a gauge-reversing bijection on
V_{+}^{\circ}
. Remarkably, this conditions is also sufficient.

Theorem

(
[
22
]
)
.

A complete order unit space
(V,V_{+},v)
has a compatible JB-algebra structure if and only if there exists a gauge-reversing bijection
\Phi\colon V^{\circ}_{+}\to V^{\circ}_{+}
.

We refer to the introduction of
[
22
]
for a discussion of this and related characterizations of Jordan structures, and will now turn to C*-algebras.

Let
E=(E,*,\{M_{n}(E)_{+}\}_{n},e)
be a unital operator system, i.e.
E
is a matrix-ordered *-vector space with Archimedean matrix order unit
e
, defined over the base field
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
. Each unital C*-algebra
A
over
\mathbb{K}
defines a unital operator system as follows from the fact that
M_{n}(A)=A\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}M_{n}(\mathbb{K})
is again a C*-algebra. Hence
M_{n}(A)_{sa}
is a JB-algebra whose inversion map defines a gauge-reversing bijection
i_{n}\colon M_{n}(A)_{+}^{\circ}\to M_{n}(A)_{+}^{\circ}
. This shows necessity of the condition in
Theorem

1.1
for
E
to be isomorphic, in the category of unital operator systems, to a unital C*-algebra
A
. We now give an outline of the proof of sufficiency.

First, the order-theoretic characterization of JB-algebras is invoked to obtain a compatible JB-algebra structure on
(M_{n}(E)_{sa},M_{n}(E)_{+},e\otimes I_{n})
, whose product we write as
(x,y)\mapsto x\circ y
. It turns out (cf.
Theorem

3.2
) that the product
\circ
is
automatically compatible
with the operator system structure, in the sense that for all
a\in M_{n}(\mathbb{K})_{sa}
and
x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
it holds that

a\circ x=\frac{1}{2}(a\cdot x+x\cdot a),

where the right hand side is given by the
M_{n}(\mathbb{K})
-bimodule structure on
M_{n}(E)
. This equality is deduced from an order-theoretic characterization of compressions in a unital JB-algebra in terms of bicomplementary projections due to Alfsen–Shultz.

We are then prepared to apply coordinatization theorems for JB-algebras containing an isomorphic copy of
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})_{sa}
or
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})_{sa}
, due to Hanche–Olsen resp. Jacobson. This yields a JB-isomorphism
M_{n}(E)_{sa}\cong M_{n}(D)_{sa}
. The ‘coordinate’ algebra
D
is an associative unital *-algebra with a proper involution, whose self-adjoint part
D_{sa}
is a JB-algebra, and is shown to be a C*-algebra in the norm
\lVert x\rVert_{D}=\lVert x^{*}x\rVert_{D_{sa}}^{1/2}
. In this way we shall obtain the following result, which is an alternative formulation of our main theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(E,*,\{M_{n}(E)_{+}\}_{n},e)
be a (real or complex) unital operator system. Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer such that
m\geq 2
if
E
is complex and
m\geq 4
if
E
is real. Then:

(1)
E
is unitally
m
-order isomorphic to a C*-algebra if and only if there exists a compatible
JB-algebra
structure on the order unit space
(M_{m}(E),M_{m}(E)_{+},e\otimes I_{m})
.

(2)
E
is unitally completely order isomorphic to a C*-algebra if and only if there exists a compatible JB-algebra structure on the order unit space
(M_{n}(E),M_{n}(E)_{+},e\otimes I_{n})
for arbitrarily large
n\geq 1
.

This theorem is inspired by and should be compared with the holomorphic characterization of complex C*-algebras obtained by Neal–Russo in
[
19
]
. They showed that for each integer
m\geq 2
a complex operator space
E
is
m
-isometrically isomorphic to a C*-algebra if and only if the open unit ball of
M_{m}(E)
is a bounded symmetric domain of tube type. The latter condition is equivalent to the Banach space
M_{m}(E)
being isometric to a JB*-algebra, i.e. to the complexification of a JB-algebra.

We would like to point out that any complete order isomorphism between operator systems is a complete isometry, so that Neal and Russo’s result implies part (2) of
Theorem

1.2
in the complex case. However, operator systems can be
n
-order isomorphic without being
n
-isometrically isomorphic, so a new approach seems necessary for part (1).
Moreover, their proof, which takes place in the categories of JB*-triples and ternary rings of operators, does not seem to apply to real C*-algebras. By contrast, the JB-algebra framework allows for a uniform treatment of complex and real C*-algebras alike. This opens the possibility of proving theorems at once for real and complex C*-algebras as a corollary of the analogous result for JB-algebras. We illustrate this method on the Choi–Effros theorem concerning the range of a unital completely positive projection on a C*-algebra. This approach also yields a version of that theorem for a unital
n
-positive projection on a unital real C*-algebra, where
n\geq 4
.

2.1
Order unit spaces and gauge-reversing bijections

Let
(V,\leq)
be a partially ordered vector space with positive cone
V_{+}
.
A positive element
v\in V_{+}
is called an
order unit
if for every
x\in V
there exists
\lambda\geq 0
such that
x\leq\lambda v
. An order unit
v
is called
Archimedean
if
x\leq\lambda v
for all
\lambda\geq 0
implies
x\leq 0
. An
order unit space
is a triple
(V,V_{+},v)
consisting of a partially ordered vector space and an Archimedean order unit.
One defines a norm on
V
, called the
order unit norm
, by

\lVert x\rVert_{v}:=\inf\{\lambda\geq 0:-\lambda v\leq x\leq\lambda v\}.

(2)

The order unit space
(V,V_{+},v)
is said to be
complete
if
(V,\lVert\cdot\rVert_{v})
is a Banach space. Each positive linear map
T\colon V\to V
is automatically bounded with respect to the order unit norm, and the
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{v}
-operator norm is given by
\lVert T\rVert=\lVert T(v)\rVert_{v}
.

The
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{v}
-interior of
V_{+}
is denoted
V_{+}^{\circ}
and referred to as the
open cone
of
V_{+}
. An element
x\in V
belongs to
V_{+}^{\circ}
if and only if there exists
\delta>0
such that
x\geq\delta v
. It follows that
V^{\circ}_{+}
consists precisely of the order units of
(V,V_{+})
. Therefore there exists a well-defined
gauge-function

M\colon V_{+}^{\circ}\times V_{+}^{\circ}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
given by

M(x/y):=\inf\{\lambda>0:x\leq\lambda y\}.

(3)

Definition 2.1
.

A map
\Phi\colon V_{+}^{\circ}\to V_{+}^{\circ}
is called
gauge-reversing
if it reverses gauges, i.e.

M(\Phi(x)/\Phi(y))=M(y/x)\text{ for all }x,y\in V_{+}^{\circ}.

(4)

To give an equivalent definition, let us say that
\Phi\colon V_{+}^{\circ}\to V_{+}^{\circ}
is
homogeneous of degree

-1
if
\Phi(\lambda x)=\lambda^{-1}\Phi(x)
for all
\lambda>0
and
x\in V_{+}^{\circ}
. We call
\Phi
an
order-anti-isomorphism
if it is bijective and
\Phi(x)\leq\Phi(y)\iff y\leq x
holds for all
x,y\in V_{+}^{\circ}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(V,V_{+},v)
be an order unit space. A mapping
\Phi\colon V_{+}^{\circ}\to V_{+}^{\circ}
is a gauge-reversing bijection if and only if it is an order-anti-isomorphism and homogeneous of degree
-1
.

Proof.

See for example
[
16
, Lemma 2.5]

∎

It is known that gauge-reversing bijections are automatically infinitely differentiable and, in fact, real-analytic
[
22
, Thm. 5.20]
. Each gauge-reversing bijection
\Phi
is an isometry for

Thompson’s metric
, defined by

d_{T}(x,y):=\log\max\{M(x/y),M(y/x)\},\quad x,y\in V_{+}^{\circ}.

(5)

A
Thompson symmetry
of
V_{+}^{\circ}
at a point
p\in V_{+}^{\circ}
is an involutive isometry of
(V_{+}^{\circ},d_{T})
having
p
as an isolated fixed point. It was shown in
[
16
, Theorem 3.5]
that each Thompson symmetry is a gauge-reversing bijection. Conversely, if
V_{+}^{\circ}
admits any gauge-reversing bijection, then at each point of
V_{+}^{\circ}
there exists a unique Thompson symmetry
[
22
, Lemmas 5.7, 5.9 and 5.10]
.

The relation between gauge-reversing bijections and JB-algebras is discussed in
Section

2.4
.

We briefly introduce operator systems. We refer to the book

Given a vector space

2.3
Real C*-algebras

We assume the reader to be familiar with the basic theory of complex C*-algebras, but will give a brief discussion of real C*-algebras based on
[
27
]
and
[
23
]
. For a more in-depth treatment of real C*-algebras, the reader may consult
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
real
C^{*}
-algebra
is a real associative
*
-algebra
A
which is complete for a norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
satisfying for all
a,b\in A
:

(i)

\lVert ab\rVert\leq\lVert a\rVert\lVert b\rVert
,

(ii)

\lVert a^{*}a\rVert=\lVert a\rVert^{2}
, and,

(iii)

\lVert a^{*}a\rVert\leq\lVert a^{*}a+b^{*}b\rVert
.

Conditions (i)-(iii) may be replaced by the requirement that
A
be isometrically isomorphic to a norm closed *-algebra of bounded operators on a real Hilbert space. If
A
is a real C*-algebra, then its complexification
A_{\mathbb{C}}:=A\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
becomes a complex C*-algebra. On the other hand, if a complex C*-algebra
B
admits an involutive complex-linear
*
-antiautomorphism
\theta
, then
B
is the complexification
A_{\mathbb{C}}
of the real C*-algebra
A:=\{x\in B\colon\theta(x)=x^{*}\}
. Moreover, each complex C*-algebra
B
becomes a real C*-algebra by restriction of scalars.

The category of commutative real C*-algebras is equivalent to the category of pairs
(X,\tau)
where
X
is a locally compact Hausdorff space and
\tau
is an involutive autohomeomorphism of
X
. This equivalence is implemented by the functor sending the pair
(X,\tau)
to the real C*-algebra

C_{0}(X,\tau):=\{f\in C_{0}(X):f(\tau(x))=\overline{f(x)}\text{ for all }x\in X\}.

The following definition should be clear.

Definition 2.5
.

A (real or complex) normed *-algebra
A
is called a
pre-C*-algebra
if it satisfies all conditions to be a C*-algebra except that its norm need not be complete.

It is straightforward to verify that the norm completion of a real (resp. complex) pre-C*-algebra is a real (resp. complex) C*-algebra.

2.4
JB-algebras

We give the definition of and preliminary results on JB-algebras.

A real
Jordan algebra
is a real vector space
J
endowed with a bilinear commutative (not necessarily associative) product
(a,b)\mapsto a\circ b
, which satisfies for all
a,b\in J
the
Jordan identity

a\circ(b\circ a^{2})=(a\circ b)\circ a^{2}.

(8)

We call
J

unital
if it has an
identity element

1
, i.e.
1\circ a=a
for all
a\in J
. Each element
a\in J
defines a linear map
T_{a}\in\End_{\mathbb{R}}(J)
given by

T_{a}(x)=a\circ x

(9)

and a quadratic representation map

U_{a}:=2T_{a}^{2}-T_{a^{2}}\in\End_{\mathbb{R}}(J).

(10)

Furthermore, for
a,b,c\in J
we define the
triple product

\{a,b,c\}:=a\circ(b\circ c)+(a\circ b)\circ c-b\circ(a\circ c).

(11)

Since
\{a,b,a\}=U_{a}(b)
, the triple product is the linearization of the quadratic product given by
(a,b)\mapsto U_{a}(b)
. Elements
a,b\in J
are said to
operator commute
if
T_{a}T_{b}=T_{b}T_{a}
. A Jordan algebra is called
associative
if its product
\circ
is associative, equivalently, if any two of its elements operator commute.

Definition 2.6
.

A
JB
-algebra is a real Jordan algebra
A
which is complete for a norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
satisfying for all
a,b\in A
:

(i)

\lVert a\circ b\rVert\leq\lVert a\rVert\lVert b\rVert
;

(ii)

\lVert a^{2}\rVert=\lVert a\rVert^{2}
;

(iii)

\lVert a^{2}\rVert\leq\lVert a^{2}+b^{2}\rVert
.

Each norm closed Jordan subalgebra
B
of a JB-algebra
A
is again a JB-algebra for the restriction of the norm. We call
B
a
JB-subalgebra
of
A
.

Example 2.7

(JC-algebras)
.

Let
A

\displaystyle\{a,b,c\}

, the set of those elements in
J
which operator commute with all elements of
S
is denoted

S^{\prime}:=\{a\in J:[T_{a},T_{b}]=0\text{ for each }b\in S\}

(16)

and called the
operator commutant
of
S
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
S
be a subset of a JB-algebra
J
. Then the operator commutant
S^{\prime}
is a JB-subalgebra of
J
.

Proof.

Bilinearity and continuity of the product readily imply that
S^{\prime}
is a closed linear subspace of
J
. It follows from
[
31
, Theorem 3.13(a),(d)]
that
S^{\prime}
is closed under taking squares. Therefore,
S^{\prime}
is a JB-subalgebra of
J
.
∎

A JB-algebra
J
is partially ordered by its
cone of squares

J_{+}:=\{x^{2}:x\in J\}.

(17)

For every
x\in J
the quadratic representation map
U_{x}\colon J\to J
is positive with operator norm
\lVert U_{x}\rVert=\lVert x\rVert^{2}

[
3
, Thm. 1.25]
. If
J
is a nonunital JB-algebra, then
\tilde{J}:=J\oplus\mathbb{R}1
endowed with the product
(a+\lambda 1)\circ(b+\mu 1)=(a\circ b+\lambda b+\mu a)+\lambda\mu 1
is a unital JB-algebra
[
13
, Thm. 3.3.9]
which (isometrically) contains
J
as a JB-subalgebra.

Now assume that
J
has an identity element
1
. Then
(J,J_{+},1)
is an order unit space, and the order unit norm coincides with the JB-algebra norm. An element
a\in J
is called
invertible
if there exists an element
b\in J
which operator commutes with
a
and satisfies
a\circ b=1
. The element
b
, if it exists, is unique and will be denoted
b:=a^{-1}
. A positive element
a\in J_{+}
is invertible if and only if it belongs to the open cone of
J
, i.e.
a\in J_{+}^{\circ}
, in which case
a^{-1}\in J_{+}^{\circ}
as well.

Lemma 2.9
.

The inversion map
i\colon J_{+}^{\circ}\to J_{+}^{\circ}
,
i(x)=x^{-1}
is a gauge-reversing bijection.

Proof.

Clearly
i
is homogeneous of degree
-1
. The proof that
i
is an order-anti-isomorphism is given in
[
3
, Lemma 1.31]
.
∎

The
spectrum
of an element
a
in the unital JB-algebra
J
is the set

\sigma_{J}(a):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\lambda 1-a\text{ is not invertible in }J\}.

(18)

The spectrum
\sigma_{J}(a)
is a non-empty compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
and
\lVert a\rVert=\max\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(a)\}
. The Jordan algebra structure of a unital JB-algebra is uniquely determined by the underlying order unit space structure, as the following theorem shows.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\Phi\colon H\to J
be a unital order isomorphism between unital JB-algebras. Then
\Phi
is an (isometric) isomorphism of JB-algebras.

Proof.

See
[
3
, Thm. 2.80]
.
∎

Let
(V,V_{+},v)
be a complete order unit space. A
compatible JB-algebra structure
on
(V,V_{+},v)
is a structure of JB-algebra on
V
for which
v
is an identity element and the cone of squares equals
V_{+}
. It follows from
Theorem

2.10
that there can be at most one compatible JB-algebra structure on
(V,V_{+},v)
. In view of
Lemma

2.9
, a necessary condition for existence is that
V_{+}^{\circ}
admit a gauge-reversing bijection. M. Roelands and the author have shown that this condition is also sufficient in
[
22
]
. There, the following order-theoretic characterization of the class of order unit spaces admitting a compatible JB-algebra structure was proven.

Theorem 2.11

(
[
22
]
)
.

A complete order unit space admits a compatible JB-algebra structure if and only if the interior of its positive cone admits a gauge-reversing bijection.

2.4.1
Positive projections

In this section, we state for later use two results concerning positive projections on a JB-algebra.

Let
F
be an ordered normed space. Two positive idempotent linear maps
P
and
Q
on
F
are called
complementary
if

\ker_{+}P=\im_{+}Q\text{ and }\ker_{+}Q=\im_{+}P.

(19)

Here we set
\ker_{+}P:=F_{+}\cap\ker P
and
\im_{+}P:=F_{+}\cap\im P=P(F_{+})
. The norm dual
F^{*}
of
F
has a natural ordering given by the cone
F^{*}_{+}:=\{\phi\in F:\phi(F_{+})\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
. Two contractive complementary projections
P,Q\colon F\to F
whose duals
P^{*},Q^{*}\colon F^{*}\to F^{*}
are also complementary are called a pair of
bicomplementary projections
on
F
.

If
p
and
q:=1-p
are complementary projections in a unital JB-algebra
J
, then their quadratic representation maps
U_{p},U_{q}\colon J\to J
are bicomplementary projections
[
3
, Thm. 7.12]
. Moreover, any pair of bicomplementary projections on a unital JB-algebra arises in this way.

Theorem 2.12

(Alfsen–Shultz)
.

Let
J
be a unital JB-algebra. Let
P,Q\colon J\to J
be bicomplementary projections. Then
p=P(1)
and
q=Q(1)
are complementary projections with
P=U_{p}
and
Q=U_{q}
.

Proof.

Cf.
[
3
, Thm. 2.83]
and the remark following its proof.
∎

The range of a positive unital projection is itself a JB-algebra under the Choi–Effrøs product.

Theorem 2.13

(Effros-Størmer)
.

Let
J
be a unital JB-algebra and suppose that
P\colon J\to J
is a unital positive idempotent linear map. Then

is a unital JB-algebra under the product
(r,s)\mapsto P(r\circ s)
.

Proof.

See
[
10
, Thm. 1.4]

∎

2.4.2
Coordinatization theorems

As follows from
Example

2.7
, if
D
is a real or complex C*-algebra and
n\geq 1
is an integer, then
M_{n}(D)_{sa}
is a JB-algebra. In this section, we discuss two
coordinatization theorems
for JB-algebras, which give a sufficient condition in order that a JB-algebra is isomorphic to
M_{n}(D)
for some real or complex *-algebra
D
. To show that the coordinate *-algebra
D
is in fact a real or complex C*-algebra, we require a preliminary result.

Alfsen–Schultz proved in
[
2
, Thm. 1.96]
that if
A
is a complex unital *-algebra
A
whose self-adjoint part
J:=A_{sa}
is a JB-algebra for some norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
, then
A
is a C*-algebra for a unique norm extending
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
. For a real *-algebra
A
, the analogous result is not true, cf.
Remark

2.16
.
However, if we make the additional assumption that the involution on
A
is
proper
, i.e.
x^{*}x=0
implies
x=0
for
x\in A
, we can show that
A
becomes a real pre-C*-algebra for a unique, not necessarily complete, norm extending
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
A
be an associative real (resp. complex)
*
-algebra such that
J:=A_{sa}
with the induced Jordan product is a JB-algebra for some norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
. In the real case, assume that the involution is proper. Then setting
\lVert x\rVert:=\lVert x^{*}x\rVert_{J}^{1/2}
defines a norm on
A
which extends
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
and makes
A
into a real pre-C*-algebra (resp. a complex C*-algebra).

The various steps in the proof are essentially adaptions from the arguments given in
[
2
, Lemma 1.92, Thm. 1.96, Prop. 1.100 and 1.103]
and
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
, barring the real case of step 3. In this step we invoke the automatic continuity of Jordan derivations from a JB-algebra into itself. Recall that a
Jordan derivation
of a JB-algebra
J
into itself is a map
D\colon J\to J
such that
D(a\circ b)=D(a)\circ b+a\circ D(b)
for all
a,b\in J
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
J
be a JB-algebra. Each Jordan derivation
D\colon J\to J
is continuous.

Proof.

As has been observed in
[
29
, Sec. 1.1]
, the proof that associative derivations on C*-algebras are norm continuous given in
[
25
, Lemma 4.1.3]
may be modified to prove the lemma.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

2.14
.

Step 1.
If
A
is not unital, then we adjoin a unit element
1
to
A
, to obtain an associative real *-algebra
\tilde{A}=A\oplus\mathbb{R}1
with identity element
1
. Moreover, the unitization of the JB-algebra
J
is a JB-algebra
\tilde{J}=J\oplus\mathbb{R}1
. Since we have a Jordan isomorphism
\tilde{A}_{sa}\cong A_{sa}\oplus\mathbb{R}1=J
, we are reduced to the the unital case.

Step 2.
For
x\in J
we have
\sigma_{J}(x)=\sigma_{A}(x)
.

It suffices to prove that
x
is invertible in
A
if and only if
x
has a Jordan inverse in
J
. Suppose that
y\in A
satisfies
xy=1=yx
. Applying the involution yields
y^{*}x=1=xy^{*}
. Hence
y^{*}
is also an associative inverse to
x
; by uniqueness
y=y^{*}
. Since
x
and
y
associate in
J
and
x\circ y=\frac{1}{2}(xy+yx)=1
, we conclude that
y
is the Jordan inverse of
x
in
J
.

Conversely, assume that
y\in J
is a Jordan-inverse of
x
. Then
x
and
y
associate in
J
, hence commute in
A
. This gives that
xy=yx=x\circ y=1
, proving that
y
is invertible in
A
.

Step 3.
If
x\in A
satisfies
-x^{*}x\in J_{+}
, then
x=0
.

According to a general fact about unital associative algebras, if
a,b\in A
, then
1-ab
is invertible if and only if
1-ba
is invertible. This fact entails that
\sigma_{A}(-x^{*}x)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma_{A}(-xx^{*})\setminus\{0\}
. The previous claim yields
\sigma_{J}(-x^{*}x)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma_{J}(-xx^{*})\setminus\{0\}
. Since the positive cone in the JB-algebra
J
is described as
J_{+}=\{a\in A\colon\sigma_{J}(a)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
, it follows that
-xx^{*}\in J_{+}
and thus
x^{*}x+xx^{*}\in-A_{+}
.

Complex case.
Write
x=a+ib
with
a,b\in J
. We find

x^{*}x+xx^{*}=(a-ib)(a+ib)+(a+ib)(a-ib)=2a^{2}+2b^{2}\in J_{+}\cap-J_{+}=\{0\}.

Since
J
is formally real,
a=b=0
and
x=0
follow.

Real case.
Put
a=\frac{1}{2}(x+x^{*})
and
b=\frac{1}{2}(x-x^{*})
, so that
x=a+b
with
a=a^{*}
and
b=-b^{*}
. Then we have
x^{*}x+xx^{*}=(a-b)(a+b)+(a+b)(a-b)=2a^{2}-2b^{2}
. Since
a\in J
, we have
a^{2}\geq 0
. This shows that
b^{2}=a^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(x^{*}x+xx^{*})\in J_{+}
.
Hence there exists a square root
u\in\JB(B^{2})
of
b^{2}
in the JB-subalgebra
\JB(b^{2})
of
J
generated by
b^{2}
. We contend that
u
and
b
commute in
A
.

Consider the real linear map
D_{A}\colon A\to A
given by
D_{A}(y):=[b,y]:=by-yb
. Then
D_{A}(J)\subset J
holds since for every
y\in J
we have
[b,y]^{*}=[y^{*},b^{*}]=[y,-b]=[b,y]
. Let
D_{J}\colon J\to J
be the restriction of
D_{A}
to
J
. Since
D_{A}
is an associative derivation on
A
, we have that
D_{J}
is a Jordan derivation from
J
into itself. It follows that
\ker D_{J}
is a Jordan subalgebra of
J
. Moreover,
D_{J}
is continuous by
Lemma

2.15
, so
\ker D_{J}\subset J
is closed, hence a JB-subalgebra of
D
. Since
b^{2}\in\ker D_{J}
and
\JB(b^{2})
is the smallest JB-subalgebra of
J
containing
b^{2}
, it follows that
\JB(b^{2})\subset\ker D_{J}
. Since
u\in\JB(b^{2})
, we find that
bu-ub=D_{J}(u)=0
. Therefore
u
and
b
commute in
A
, as contended.

Now let
y=u+b
. Then we have that
y^{*}y=(u-b)(u+b)=u^{2}-b^{2}=0
. It follows that
y=0
, hence
b=\frac{1}{2}(y-y^{*})=0
and
x=a\in J
. Since
J
is formally real,
x\in J
and
x^{2}=x^{*}x\in-J_{+}
imply
x=0
, proving the claim.

Step 4.
If
x\in A
then
x^{*}x\in J_{+}
.

Let
x^{*}x=a^{+}-a^{-}
be the Jordan decomposition of
x^{*}x\in J
, where
a^{+},a^{-}\in J
are orthogonal. Let
y=xa^{-}
. Then
-y^{*}y=-a^{-}(x^{*}x)a^{-}=-a^{-}(a^{+}-a^{-})a^{-}=(a^{-})^{3}\in A_{+}
. By the previous claim, we have
y=0
. Now
a^{-}\in J
cubes to
0
, hence
x^{*}x=a^{+}-a^{-}=a^{+}\in J_{+}
.

Step 5.

\lVert\cdot\rVert
is a norm on
A
extending
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
.

Let
x,y\in A
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
. For
x\in J
we have
\lVert x\rVert:=\lVert x^{*}x\rVert_{J}^{1/2}=\lVert x^{2}\rVert_{J}^{1/2}=\lVert x\rVert_{J}
since
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
is a JB-norm. Therefore, the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
extends
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
. Also,
(\lambda x)^{*}=\overline{\lambda}x^{*}

yields
\lVert\lambda x\rVert=|\lambda|\lVert x\rVert
.
To prove the triangle inequality, consider a state
\rho
on
J
, i.e. a positive linear functional with
\rho(1)=1
. It follows from step 4 that a semi-inner product on
J
is defined by
(z|w):=\rho(z^{*}w)
. It follows from the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality that
|\rho(x^{*}y)|\leq\rho(x^{*}x)^{1/2}\rho(y^{*}y)^{1/2}
. This yields

\rho((x+y)^{*}(x+y))\leq\rho(x^{*}x)+\rho(y^{*}y)+2\rho(x^{*}x)^{1/2}\rho(y^{*}y)^{1/2}=(\rho(x^{*}x)^{1/2}+\rho(y^{*}y)^{1/2})^{2}\leq(\lVert x\rVert+\lVert y\rVert)^{2}.

Taking the supremum over all states
\rho
gives, since
(x+y)^{*}(x+y)\in J_{+}
that

\lVert x+y\rVert^{2}=\lVert x^{*}x+y^{*}y\rVert_{J}=\sup_{\rho\in J^{*}_{+},\rho(1)=1}\rho((x+y)^{*}(x+y))\leq(\lVert x\rVert+\lVert y\rVert)^{2}.

Step 6.

\lVert\cdot\rVert
satisfies axioms (i), (ii) and (iii) of a pre-C*-algebra.

Let
x,y\in A
. Since
\lVert\cdot\rVert
extends
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
by the previous step, we have
\lVert x^{*}x\rVert=\lVert x^{*}x\rVert_{J}:=\lVert x\rVert^{2}
, i.e. axiom (ii) holds.
Note that the map
R_{y}\colon J\to J
,
x\mapsto y^{*}xy
is positive. Indeed, if
a\in J_{+}
then
a=u^{2}
for some
u\in J
, hence
R_{y}(a)=y^{*}u^{2}y=(uy)^{*}(uy)\in J_{+}
by the last claim. Since
R_{y}
is a positive operator, its operator norm is given by
\lVert R_{y}\rVert=\lVert R_{y}(1)\rVert_{J}=\lVert y^{*}y\rVert_{J}
. It follows that

\lVert xy\rVert^{2}=\lVert y^{*}x^{*}xy^{*}\rVert_{J}=\lVert R_{y}(x^{*}x)\rVert_{J}\leq\lVert R_{y}\rVert\lVert x^{*}x\rVert_{J}=\lVert x^{*}x\rVert_{J}\lVert y^{*}y\rVert_{J}=(\lVert x\rVert\lVert y\rVert)^{2},

proving (i). Since
x^{*}x,y^{*}y\in J_{+}
by step 5 and the JB-norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{J}
is monotone on
J_{+}
, we obtain

\lVert x^{*}x\rVert=\lVert x^{*}x\rVert_{J}\leq\lVert x^{*}x+y^{*}y\rVert_{J}=\lVert x^{*}x+y^{*}y\rVert.

Thus axiom (iii) holds. We conclude that
\lVert\cdot\rVert
makes
A
into a pre-C*-algebra.

Step 7.
In the complex case,
A
is complete.

Let
x=a+ib\in A
with
a,b\in J
. Since
a=\frac{1}{2}(x+x^{*})
and
b=\frac{1}{2i}(x-x^{*})
we have

\max\{\lVert a\rVert_{J},\lVert b\rVert_{J}\}\leq\lVert a+ib\rVert\leq\lVert a\rVert_{J}+\lVert b\rVert_{J}.

From these inequalities it is clear that
A
is complete whenever
J
is complete.
∎

Remark 2.16
.

(1) In contrast with the complex case, it is necessary to assume that
x^{*}x=0
implies
x=0
, as the following example shows. Let
J=C_{0}(X)
be an associative JB-algebra and let
A=J\times J
be the real *-algebra having coordinatewise product and involution
(a,b)^{*}=(b,a)
. The diagonal embedding yields a Jordan isomorphism
J\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}A_{sa}=\{(a,a)\colon a\in J\}
, so
A_{sa}
admits a JB-norm. However, for
x:=(a,0)
with
a\in J\setminus\{0\}
we have
x^{*}x=(0,a)(a,0)=(0,0)
, which implies that every norm on
A
violates axiom (ii) of a C*-algebra.

(2) In the real case, it need not hold that
A
is complete. For example, let
I
be the non-closed ideal of
C([0,1];\mathbb{R})
consisting of those functions which vanish in a neighborhood of
0
. Consider the non-closed real *-subalgebra of
C([0,1];\mathbb{C})
given by

A:=\{f\in C([0,1];\mathbb{C}):\Im f\in I\}.

Then
A
is not complete, despite the completeness of its self-adjoint part
A_{sa}=C([0,1];\mathbb{R})
.

We now state a coordinatization theorem due to Hanche-Olsen, characterizing those JB-algebras which are the self-adjoint part of a complex C*-algebra.

Theorem 2.17

(Hanche-Olsen)
.

Let
J
be a unital JB-algebra and suppose
(J\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))_{sa}
is endowed with a Jordan product
\circ
satisfying for all
x,y\in J
and
a,b\in M_{2}(\mathbb{C})_{sa}
:

\displaystyle(x\otimes I_{2})\circ(y\otimes I_{2})

\displaystyle=(x\circ y)\otimes I_{2},

(20)

\displaystyle(1\otimes a)\circ(1\otimes b)

\displaystyle=1\otimes(a\circ b),

(21)

\displaystyle(1\otimes a)\circ(x\otimes I_{2})

\displaystyle=x\otimes a,

(22)

\displaystyle[L(1\otimes a),L(x\otimes 1)]

\displaystyle=0.

(23)

Then there exists a unique associative product in
A:=J\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
inducing the given Jordan products both in
J
and in
(J\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}M_{2}(\mathbb{C}))_{sa}
. Moreover,
A
with involution
(x\otimes\lambda)^{*}=x\otimes\overline{\lambda}
is a C*-algebra for a norm extending
\lVert\cdot\rVert
such that
A_{sa}=J
.

Proof.

Except for the last sentence, this is shown in
[
14
, Thm. 4]
. The proof of the remaining part is not given in detail in
[
14
, Rmk. 11]
, so will be discussed here. Let us show that the conjugation on
A=J\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
is indeed an anti-homomorphism. For
x,y\in J
we have

2\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\circ\begin{pmatrix}0&y\\
y&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&xy\\
yx&0\end{pmatrix}.

(24)

Since the rightmost matrix is self-adjoint, it follows that
(xy)^{*}=yx=y^{*}x^{*}
. Since
A=J+iJ
, we obtain
(xy)^{*}=y^{*}x^{*}
for all
x,y\in A
. The
C^{*}
-norm on
J
is constructed in
Theorem

2.14
.
∎

We now state a coordinatization theorem due to Jacobson, and then show that for a JB-algebra the coordinate algebra is in fact a real C*-algebra.

Theorem 2.18

(Jacobson)
.

Let
J
be a unital Jordan algebra over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
\operatorname{char}\mathbb{K}\neq 2
. Let
n\geq 4
and let
i\colon M_{n}(\mathbb{K})_{sa}\to J
be a unital Jordan homomorphism. Then there exists an associative
*
-algebra
D
over
\mathbb{K}
and a Jordan isomorphism
\Phi\colon M_{n}(D)_{sa}\to J
extending
i
.

Proof.

See
[
13
, Theorem 2.8.9]
or
[
18
, Thm. 17.1.1]
.
∎

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
J
be a unital JB-algebra. Let
n\geq 4
and let
i\colon M_{n}(\mathbb{R})_{sa}\to J
be a unital Jordan homomorphism. Then there exists a real C*-algebra
D
and a JB-algebra isomorphism
\Phi\colon M_{n}(D)_{sa}\to J
extending
i
.

Proof.

After applying
Theorem

2.18
to obtain a unital Jordan isomorphism
\Phi\colon M_{n}(D)_{sa}\to J
, it remains to construct a C*-algebra norm on
D
for which
\Phi
is an isometry. By
Lemma

2.8
the operator commutant
i(M_{n}(\mathbb{R})_{sa})^{\prime}
is a JB-subalgebra of
J
. Its preimage under
\Phi
is
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})_{sa}^{\prime}=D_{sa}\otimes I_{n}
. It follows that
\lVert d\rVert_{D_{sa}}:=\lVert\Phi(d\otimes I_{n})\rVert
defines a JB-norm on
D_{sa}
.
Let
x\in D
; we claim that
x^{*}x=0
implies
x=0
. Indeed, we have
(x\otimes E_{12}+x^{*}\otimes E_{21})^{2}=xx^{*}\otimes E_{12}+x^{*}x\otimes E_{21}
and the Jordan algebra
M_{n}(D)_{sa}
contains no non-zero nilpotent elements, because the Jordan-isomorphic JB-algebra
J
does not. According to
Theorem

2.14
,
D
is a real pre-C*-algebra for a norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
extending
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{D_{sa}}
. We shall prove that
D
is complete. Denote by
\hat{D}
the real C*-algebra which is the completion of
(D,\lVert\cdot\rVert)
. Then
\Phi^{-1}\colon J\to M_{n}(\hat{D})_{sa}
is an injective Jordan homomorphism between JB-algebras, so it is an isometry according to
[
3
, Prop. 1.35]
. Consequently, its image
M_{n}(D)_{sa}=\Phi^{-1}(J)
is closed in
M_{n}(\hat{D})_{sa}
, which implies that
D=\hat{D}
. We conclude that
D
is a real C*-algebra and that
\Phi\colon M_{n}(D)_{sa}\to J
is an isometric isomorphism of JB-algebras.
∎

3
JB-algebra structures on operator systems

The main result of this section is
Theorem

3.2
which asserts that a JB-algebra structure on the matrix space of an operator system is automatically compatible with the bimodule structure. The proof is based on
Theorem

2.12
and the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(E,*,\{M_{n}(E)_{+}\}_{n},e)
be a real or complex operator system and let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Let
p
and
q=1-p
be complementary projections in
M_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. Define
P,Q\colon M_{n}(E)_{sa}\to M_{n}(E)_{sa}
by
P(x)=p\cdot x\cdot p
and
Q(x)=q\cdot x\cdot q
. Then
P
and
Q
are bicomplementary projections on the ordered normed space
M_{n}(E)_{sa}
.

Proof.

Clearly
P
and
Q
are idempotent linear maps. The matrix properties of the order (
6
) and norm (
7
) on
M_{n}(E)
yield that
P
and
Q
are positive and contractive. To prove that
P
and
Q
are complementary projections, by symmetry it suffices to show that
\im_{+}P=\ker_{+}Q
.
Since
(Q\circ P)(x)=q\cdot(p\cdot x\cdot p)\cdot q=(qp)\cdot x\cdot(pq)=0\cdot x\cdot 0=0
for
x\in M_{n}(E)_{sa}
, one has
Q\circ P=0
, hence
\im_{+}P\subset\ker_{+}Q
. To show the converse, let
x\in\ker_{+}Q
, i.e.
x\in M_{n}(E)_{+}
and
q\cdot x\cdot q=0
. Let
\phi\colon E\to\mathbb{K}
be any state. Since states are completely positive, the induced map
\phi_{n}\colon M_{n}(E)\to M_{n}(\mathbb{K})_{n}
is positive, hence
\phi_{n}(x)\in M_{n}(\mathbb{K})_{+}
and
q\phi_{n}(x)q=0
. Let

with
a^{2}=\phi_{n}(x)
. Then
(aq)^{*}aq=qa^{2}q=0
, hence
aq=0
and
\phi_{n}(x)q=a^{2}q=0
. Similarly,
q\phi_{n}(x)=0
. This implies that
\phi_{n}(x)=p\phi_{n}(x)p=\phi_{n}(p\cdot x\cdot p)
. Since the states on
E
separate points,
x=p\cdot x\cdot p\in\im_{+}P
, as desired.

It remains to show that
P^{*}
and
Q^{*}
are also complementary. Again by symmetry we need only prove that
\im_{+}P^{*}=\ker_{+}Q^{*}
. Since
P\circ Q=0
, we have
Q^{*}\circ P^{*}=0
and
\im_{+}P^{*}\subset\ker_{+}Q^{*}
. Now let

, so
\phi(M_{n}(E)_{+})\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\phi(q\cdot M_{n}(E)_{sa}\cdot q)=0
.

Let

be self-adjoint. We will prove that

is positive. Indeed, since

Since
\phi(q)=0

for every

\phi(y)\geq 0

, hence
\phi(y)=0

Now let

be arbitrary. By the previous paragraph, we find that

\phi(x)=\phi(p\cdot x\cdot p)+\phi(p\cdot x\cdot q+q\cdot x\cdot p)+\phi(q\cdot x\cdot q)=\phi(p\cdot x\cdot p)=\phi(P(x)),

so

as desired. This shows that
\ker_{+}Q^{*}\subset\im_{+}P
, hence
\im_{+}P^{*}=\ker_{+}Q^{*}
. We conclude that
P
and

are bicomplementary projections on
M_{n}(E)_{sa}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Automatic compatibility of JB-algebra and operator system structures)
.

Let
(E,*,\{M_{n}(E)_{+}\}_{n},e)
be a real or complex operator system and let

. Assume that the order unit space
(M_{n}(E)_{sa},M_{n}(E)_{+},I_{n})
is given a compatible structure of unital JB-algebra. Then:

(1) For all

and

we have

U_{a}(x)=a\cdot x\cdot a,

(25)

and

\{a,x,b\}=\tfrac{1}{2}(a\cdot x\cdot b+b\cdot x\cdot a).

(26)

M_{n}(E)_{sa}
, while the right hand side is given by the

-bimodule structure on

.

(2) The inclusion map

is a unital Jordan homomorphism.

(3) The operator commutant of

is the JB-subalgebra

Proof.

(1)
Note that the members of (

and

Let

be a projection in

. As in

3.1
, the maps
P
and
Q
defined by

and

2.12
yields that

are complementary projections in the JB-algebra

such that

and

Now let

where the

(2) Set

(3) Let

Example 4.3
.

The following example demonstrates that, in contrast to the complex case, the real case of part (1) of

does not hold if one merely assumes

Let

The author does not know of a counterexample with

.
