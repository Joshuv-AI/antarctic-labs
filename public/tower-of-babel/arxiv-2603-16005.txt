Breakdown properties of optimal transport maps: general transportation costs
Alberto González-Sanz, Marco Avella Medina

Abstract
Two recent works, Avella-Medina and González-Sanz (2026) and Passeggeri and Paindaveine (2026), studied the robustness of the optimal transport map through its breakdown point, i.e., the smallest fraction of contamination that can make the map take arbitrarily aberrant values. Their main finding is the following: let P P and Q Q denote the target and reference measures, respectively, and let T T be the optimal transport map for the squared Euclidean cost. Then, the breakdown point of T ⁡ ( u ) T(u) , when P P is perturbed and Q Q is fixed, coincides with the Tukey depth of u u relative to Q Q . In this note, we extend this result to general convex cost functions, demonstrating that the cost function does not have any impact on the breakdown point of the optimal transport map. Our contribution provides a definitive characterization of the breakdown point of the optimal transport map. In particular, it shows that for a broad class of regular cost functions, all transport-based quantiles enjoy the same high breakdown point properties.



Keywords
Breakdown point; Multivariate quantiles; Optimal Transport; Robustness; Transport map.

62G35, 62G30.

1
Introduction

We consider the general Monge optimal transport problem
(
Monge 1781
;
Gangbo and McCann 1996
)

\displaystyle\inf_{T:\,T\#Q=P}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c({x},T({x}))dQ({x}).

(MP)

Here
c:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is the cost function and
T\#P
denotes the
push-forward
measure, that is, the measure such that for each measurable set
A
we have
T\#P(A):=P(T^{-1}(A))
. We are interested in the solution
T
of (
MP
), i.e. the Monge map. Optimal transport and Monge maps are important tools in applied mathematics
(
Santambrogio 2015
)
that in recent years have been used to develop interesting methodology in statistics
(
Hallin et al. 2021
;
Chewi et al. 2025
)
and data science
(
Peyré and Cuturi 2019
)
.

The robustness properties of the Monge map are not yet well-established, as highlighted by
Ronchetti 2023
. Key concepts from robust statistics—such as the influence function for local stability and the breakdown point for global reliability—lack a comprehensive theory in this context. The influence function is a Gâteaux derivative under Dirac-mass perturbations, but only directional derivatives under certain smooth perturbations have been studied for transport maps
(
Loeper 2009
;
González-Sanz and
Sheng 2024
)
. Hence an influence function framework is still absent.

A significant recent development concerns the breakdown point. In parallel works,
Paindaveine and Passeggeri 2026
and
Avella Medina and González-Sanz 2026
established a remarkable connection for the squared Euclidean cost: the breakdown point of the Monge map (under perturbations of the target measure
P
) evaluated at a point
u
is precisely the Tukey depth of
u
with respect to the source measure
Q
. Their proofs, although quite different, hinge on the monotonicity of the Monge map and appear difficult to extend beyond the quadratic cost.

In this note, we generalize this result. We prove that the equivalence between the breakdown point and the Tukey depth holds for costs of the form
c(x,y)=h(x-y)
, under the standard regularity conditions of
Gangbo and McCann 1996
. A particularly interesting consequence of this finding is that for all such general cost functions, the resulting transport-based quantile estimators enjoy the same high breakdown points.
Avella Medina and González-Sanz 2026
;
Paindaveine and Passeggeri 2026
had shown that the transport-based quantiles built with the quadratic cost, as proposed in
Hallin et al. 2021
;
Ghosal and Sen 2022
, enjoy essentially the same breakdown as univariate quantiles. Our work shows that the same result is true for a general class of strictly convex transportation costs. This is in sharp contrast to M-quantiles
(
Breckling and Chambers 1988
)
and spatial quantiles
(
Chaudhuri 1996
;
Dudley and Koltchinskii 1992
)
, as the choice of the cost function in these cases determines the breakdown point of those multivariate quantiles
(
Konen and Paindaveine 2025
;
Konen and Paindaveine 2026
)
.

2
Main results

2.1
Background in optimal transportation

The existence and uniqueness of Monge maps for general cost functions was shown by
Gangbo and McCann 1996
under the following assumptions. Let
\|\cdot\|
denote the Euclidean norm and
c(x,y)=h(x-y)
be such that

(A1)

h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
is strictly convex;

(A2)

given a height
r>0
and an angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, there exists some
M:=M(r,\theta)>0
such that for all
\|{p}\|>M
, one can find a (truncated) cone

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cone}(r,\theta,{z},{p}):=\left\{{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|{x}-{p}\|\|{z}\|\cos(\theta/2)\leq\left\langle{z},{x}-{p}\right\rangle\leq r\|{z}\|\right\},

with vertex at
{p}
(and
{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{{0}\}
) on which
h
attains its maximum at
{p}
;

(A3)

\lim_{\|{x}\|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h({x})}{\|{x}\|}=\infty
.

Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A3)
are easy to understand from an optimization standpoint. In contrast, Assumption
(A2)
is geometric in nature as it requires that the level sets
\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h(z)\leq\lambda\}
have a vanishing curvature as
\lambda\to\infty
. Note that the cost
c(x,y)=\|x-y\|^{p}
satisfies this condition for
p>1
. Other relevant examples are the cost functions implicitly defined in
(
Catoni 2012
, p. 1151)
.

The characterization of Monge maps is typically achieved through the superdifferentials of
c
-concave functions; see
Villani 2009
. These can be seen as generalizations of subdifferentials of convex functions. Recall that a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is called
c
-concave
if it can be expressed as

f(x)=\inf_{(y,t)\in\mathcal{T}}\big\{c(x,y)-t\big\},

(1)

for some set
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
. For such a function, the
c
-superdifferential

\partial^{c}f
is the set of pairs
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

f(z)\leq f(x)+\big[c(z,y)-c(x,y)\big]\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2)

Note that in the case of the quadratic cost
h(x-y)=\|x-y\|^{2}
, one recovers the usual subdifferentials. Let
\mathcal{L}_{d}
be the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
We denote by
\partial^{c}f(x)
the set of points
y
such that
(x,y)\in\partial^{c}f
, and for any
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define
\partial^{c}f(U)=\bigcup_{x\in U}\partial^{c}f(x)
. For
\mathcal{L}_{d}
-a.e.
x
in the domain of
f
,
\partial^{c}f(x)
is a singleton (see
Gangbo and McCann 1996
). In this case we denote the unique element of
\partial^{c}f(x)
by
\nabla^{c}f(x)
. A set
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called
c
-
cyclically monotone
if for any finite number of points
(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}),\dots,(x_{N},y_{N})\in\Gamma
, and for any permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\dots,N\}
, the following inequality holds:

\sum_{i=1}^{N}c(x_{i},y_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}c(x_{i},y_{\sigma(i)}).

A celebrated result in convex analysis shows that the subdifferentials of convex functions are maximal cyclically monotone
(
Rockafellar 1970
)
. A similar relation was also shown to hold between
c
-cyclically monotone sets and
c
-concave functions in
Smith and Knott 1992
; see also
(
Gangbo and McCann 1996
, Theorem 2.7)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

A set
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
c
-cyclically
monotone if and only if there exists a
c
-concave function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
such that
\Gamma\subset\partial^{c}f
.

Let
Q
be absolutely continuous with respect to the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, written as
Q\ll\mathcal{L}_{d}
. The following key result is due to
Gangbo and McCann 1996
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
Gangbo and McCann 1996
, Theorems 3.7 and 4.5)

Assume that
Q\ll\mathcal{L}_{d}
and that
c
satisfies
(A1)
-
(A3)
. Then, there exists a unique
Q
-a.e. defined Borel map
T_{Q\to P}
such that

(i)

T_{Q\to P}(x)\in\partial^{c}f_{Q\to P}(x)
for
Q
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some
c
-concave
f_{Q\to P}
;

(ii)

T_{Q\to P}(x)=\nabla^{c}f_{Q\to P}(x)
for
Q
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(iii)

T_{Q\to P}
pushes
Q
forward to
P
.

Moreover, if
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c({x},y)dQ(x)dP(y)<\infty,
then
T_{Q\to P}
is the unique solution of (
MP
).

2.2
Breakdown point of the Monge map

The breakdown point quantifies an estimator’s robustness by measuring the minimum proportion of outliers required to make the estimator take arbitrarily aberrant values
(
Huber and Ronchetti 2009
)
.
To define the notion of breakdown point of
T_{Q\to P}(u)
we need to have
T_{Q\to P}
well-defined everywhere in
{\rm supp}(Q)
. However, the mapping
T_{Q\to P}
is only defined
Q
-a.e., by
Theorem

2.2
. Then a natural extension of
T_{Q\to P}(u)
is given by any set-valued
c
-superdifferential

{\bf T}_{Q\to P}=\partial^{c}f_{Q\to P}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\{A:A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\},

such that
\partial^{c}f_{Q\to P}(x)=\{{T}_{Q\to P}(x)\}
for
Q
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; see also Section 2.2 of
Avella Medina and González-Sanz 2026
.

Definition 2.3
.

The breakdown point of
{\bf T}_{Q\to P}(u)
is defined as

{\rm BP}({\bf T}_{Q\to P}(u),P)=\inf\left\{\varepsilon\in(0,1):\sup_{{\bf T}_{\varepsilon}\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(P)}\sup_{v\in{\bf T}_{\varepsilon}(u)}\|v\|=\infty\right\},

where
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(P)
denotes the set of
{\bf T}_{Q\to(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon\mu}
for some probability measure
\mu
.

Now we can state the announced result. We recall that the Tukey depth
(
Tukey 1975
)
of
u
with respect to
Q
is

{\rm TD}(u;Q)=\inf_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Q(\{x:\langle v,x-u\rangle\leq 0\}),

where
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
denotes the unit sphere.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that
c
satisfies
(A1)
-
(A3)
and
Q\ll\mathcal{L}_{d}
. Fix
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
{\rm TD}(u;Q)\in[0,1/2]
. Then,

{\rm BP}({\bf T}_{Q\to P}(u),P)={\rm TD}(u;Q).

Remark 2.5

(The direction of the perturbation)
.

For
u\in\operatorname{supp}(Q)
, the Monge map
{\bf T}_{Q\to P}(u)
breaks down with contamination in the direction of the mass point distribution

\delta_{z_{n}}
(cf.
Lemma

3.4
), where

z_{n}=u-\nabla h^{*}(n\,v_{u}),\qquad v_{u}\in\arg\min_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Q\big(\{x:\langle v,x-u\rangle\leq 0\}\big),

and
h^{*}
denotes the convex conjugate of
h
, i.e.,
h^{*}(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{\langle x,y\rangle-h(x)\}
. We recall here that
\nabla h^{*}
is well defined (single-valued) as
h
is strictly convex.
When
h(x)=\|x\|^{2}
, the breakdown direction simplifies to
u-nv_{u}
, as in

Avella Medina and González-Sanz 2026
and
Paindaveine and Passeggeri 2026
.
For radial costs, the contaminating direction is also of the form
u-r_{n}v_{u}
with
r_{n}\to+\infty
.
In general, however, the mapping
\nabla h^{*}
modifies this direction to reflect the geometry induced by the cost function
h
.

Continuous breakdown point

Lemma 3.3

4
Final remarks

We believe that the equivalence between the breakdown of transport maps and the Tukey depth in the reference measure suggests a natural definition of the Tukey depth in metric spaces. Indeed, for a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
, we could define the Tukey depth of a point
u\in\mathcal{M}
with respect to a probability measure
Q
on
\mathcal{M}
as the breakdown point of the Monge map at
u
, when transporting
Q
to itself (i.e.,
P=Q
) with the squared distance cost
c(x,y)=d^{2}(x,y)
.

Our main result

Theorem

2.4
establishes that the choice of cost function does not affect the robustness properties of the Monge map. This implies that the breakdown point is determined solely by intrinsic geometric properties of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, rather than by the specific distance function employed. It would therefore be interesting to investigate whether different metrics
d
can lead to the same definition of Tukey depth for general spaces and to study whether such a notion of depth agrees with the metric Tukey depth proposed in
Dai and Lopez-Pintado 2023
.

\|u_{n}-\tilde{w}_{n,m}\|\leq 1/m
and
f_{n}(\tilde{w}_{n,m})\leq-m
whenever

n\geq n_{m}
. Setting
w_{n}^{\prime}=\tilde{w}_{n,n}
, we obtain a sequence with

\|u_{n}-w_{n}^{\prime}\|\leq 1/n\quad{\rm and}\quad f_{n}(w_{n}^{\prime})\leq-n,\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(18)

Since
w_{n}^{\prime}
eventually
lies in the interior of
{\rm dom}(f_{n})
, Theorem 3.3 and Proposition C.4
in
Gangbo and McCann 1996
guarantee that

\partial^{c}f_{n}(w_{n}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
.

We now claim that either
f_{n}(u_{n})\to-\infty
along a subsequence or that

\{\partial^{c}f_{n}(w_{n}^{\prime})\}_{n}
escapes to the horizon. Indeed, if

\{\partial^{c}f_{n}(w_{n}^{\prime})\}_{n}
does not escape to the horizon, then there exists a bounded
subsequence
\{q_{n_{k}}\}_{k}
with
q_{n_{k}}\in\partial^{c}f_{n}(w_{n_{k}}^{\prime})
. In that case,
the inequality

f_{n_{k}}(u_{n_{k}})\leq f_{n_{k}}(w_{n_{k}}^{\prime})+\bigl(h(u_{n_{k}}-q_{n_{k}})-h(w_{n_{k}}^{\prime}-q_{n_{k}})\bigr)\leq-n_{k}+\bigl(h(u_{n_{k}}-q_{n_{k}})-h(w_{n_{k}}^{\prime}-q_{n_{k}})\bigr)

(19)

implies that
f_{n}(u_{n_{k}})\to-\infty
, which proves the claim. Hence, the sequence

w_{n}=\begin{cases}u_{n}&\text{if }-f_{n}(u_{n})\geq\min_{y\in\partial^{c}f_{n}(w_{n}^{\prime})}\|y\|,\\
w_{n}^{\prime}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

satisfies the conclusion of (i).
To check this claim, we argue by contradiction. First, if
f_{n_{k}}(w_{n_{k}})\to-C
for some subsequence, then by (
18
),
w_{n_{k}}=u_{n_{k}}
. Hence,
-f_{n_{k}}(u_{n_{k}})\geq\min_{y\in\partial^{c}f_{n_{k}}(w_{n_{k}}^{\prime})}\|y\|
, which contradicts (
19
). Second, if now
\partial f_{n_{k}}(w_{n_{k}})
is bounded for some subsequence, as
\|p_{n}\|\to\infty
and
(u_{n},p_{n})\in\partial^{c}f_{n}
, it must follow that
w_{n_{k}}=w^{\prime}_{n_{k}}
– or equivalently
\min_{y\in\partial^{c}f_{n_{k}}(w_{n_{k}}^{\prime})}\|y\|>-f_{n_{k}}(u_{n_{k}})
. Hence,
\min_{y\in\partial^{c}f_{n_{k}}(w_{n_{k}}^{\prime})}\|y\|
is bounded and
a fortiori

f_{n_{k}}(u_{n_{k}})
cannot diverge. This again contradicts (
19
).

Step 3: Proof of (ii).
Let
K
be as in (
8
). Since
(w_{n},q_{n})\in\partial^{c}f_{n}
, for every
x\in K
,

f_{n}(x)\leq f_{n}(w_{n})+[h(x-q_{n})-h(w_{n}-q_{n})]\leq f_{n}(w_{n}),

so that

\sup_{x\in K}f_{n}(x)\to-\infty.

(20)

For any
y_{x}\in\partial^{c}f(x),
it follows from (
17
) that

f_{n}(x)+C(\|y_{x}\|)\geq f_{n}(x)+f_{n}^{c}(y_{x})=h(x-y_{x})\geq 0,

which implies

f_{n}(x)\geq-C(\|y_{x}\|)
. From here and (
20
), we deduce that
\{\partial^{c}f_{n}(K)\}_{n}
escapes to the horizon.

Proof of (iii).
Note that the limit points of
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
are those of
\{w_{n}\}_{n}
. Let
u
be a limit point of
\{w_{n}\}_{n}
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
r_{n}
be the largest
r>0
such that there exists a direction
v_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
satisfying

{\rm Cone}\left(r_{n},\frac{\pi}{1+r_{n}^{-1}},{v}_{n},w_{n}-q_{n}\right)\subset\{x:h(x)\leq h(w_{n}-q_{n})\}.

After taking subsequences, we can assume that
w_{n}\to u
and
v_{n}\to v_{*}=-v
. Fix a compact set

K\subset\{z:\langle v_{*},z-u\rangle>0\}=\{z:\langle v,z-u\rangle<0\}.

By
(A2)
and the fact that
\|q_{n}\|\to\infty
, it follows that
r_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to\infty
, which implies

\cos\left(\frac{\pi/2}{1+r_{n}^{-1}}\right)\to 0.

(21)

Since
K
is compact, there exists some
\alpha>0
such that
K\subset\{z:\langle v_{*},z-u\rangle\geq\alpha\}
.
Hence, for every
x\in K
,

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{n},x-w_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle v_{n}-v_{*},x-w_{n}\right\rangle+\left\langle v_{*},{x}-w_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle v_{*},x-u\right\rangle-\|v_{n}-v_{*}\|\|x-w_{n}\|-\|w_{n}-u\|\geq\alpha-\|v_{n}-v_{*}\|\|x-w_{n}\|-\|w_{n}-u\|,

which yields the existence of
n_{0}
such that
\left\langle v_{n},x-w_{n}\right\rangle\geq\alpha/2
for
n\geq n_{0}
. As a consequence, from (
21
),
r_{n}\to+\infty
and the boundedness of
K
and
w_{n}
, we derive the existence of
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
x\in K
and
n\geq n_{0}
,

\|x-w_{n}\|\cos\left(\frac{\pi/2}{1+r_{n}^{-1}}\right)\leq\left\langle v_{n},x-w_{n}\right\rangle\leq r_{n}.

That is, for
n\geq n_{0}
we have

K\subset{\rm Cone}\left(r_{n},\frac{\pi}{1+r_{n}^{-1}},{v}_{n},w_{n}\right),

which by construction of the cone yields

\{x-q_{n}:x\in K\}\subset{\rm Cone}\left(r_{n},\frac{\pi}{1+r_{n}^{-1}},{v}_{n},w_{n}-q_{n}\right)\subset\{x:h(x)\leq h(w_{n}-q_{n})\}.

From this we conclude that

K\subset\{x:h(x-q_{n})\leq h(w_{n}-q_{n})\}
for
n
large enough. Hence, (iii) follows from (ii).
∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.2
.

Let
K
and
K^{\prime}
be a compact sets such that
Q(K)\geq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}
and

. Since

, then

\displaystyle=Q(K)+Q((\nabla^{c}f_{n})^{-1}(K^{\prime}))-1\geq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}>0.

From this, it follows that
