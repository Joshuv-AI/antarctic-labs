Continuous-Time Analysis for Minimax and Bilevel Problems
Hyunwoo Lee, Jeongyeol Kwon, Dohyun Kwon

Abstract
We study single-loop gradient-flow dynamics for nested optimization, where the outer variable evolves while auxiliary variables track the inner solution map. While existing analyses typically rely on problem- and condition-specific Lyapunov constructions, we propose, to our knowledge, the first unified Lyapunov template for continuous-time analysis that covers minimax, bilevel via a lifted penalty formulation, and min–min–max. Our proof is modular, built from reusable lemmas that yield a unified characterization of time-scale separation. This characterization bridges regimes from strong convexity/concavity to mere convexity through an error-bound condition, and produces explicit closed-form thresholds that avoid the coupled ratio conditions common in discrete-time analyses. We further compare the penalty dynamics with the ideal hyper-gradient flow, derive a finite-time tracking bound, and discuss an Euler one-step analogue; hypercleaning diagnostics show that the predicted relative time-scale regions remain visible under stable forward-Euler discretization.

3
Unified Continuous Time Framework for Nested Optimization

This paper investigates minimax
P1
and bilevel
P2
nested problem through a unified Lyapunov perspective. In addition, we consider analysis for the auxiliary min–min–max problem in Appendix
K
.
All three share a common obstacle: the outer descent direction depends on an inner optimizer that varies with
x
, which makes the outer-gradient dynamics hard to compute directly.
Therefore, we focus on single-loop coupled dynamics that track inner solutions along the trajectory.
In continuous time, sufficient time-scale separation makes tracking errors dissipative, so the cross terms can be absorbed in a Lyapunov inequality and descent of the relevant outer value function follows.
Building on this, we introduce a common Lyapunov construction that augments the outer value term with nonnegative tracking gaps, and we state two reusable lemmas that serve as shared analysis modules across these settings.

Our ideal outer-gradient flow is given by

\dot{x}=-\nabla F(x).

(1)

In
P1
and
P2
, the outer objective takes the composite form

F(x)=f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)
, where
y^{*}(x)
is defined implicitly as the optimizer of an inner problem.
As a result, evaluating
\nabla F(x)
typically requires nontrivial inner computation along the trajectory.
In particular, even for
P1
one has
\nabla F(x)=\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)
, which depends on the
current inner maximizer
y^{*}(x)
.
For
P2
, the dependence is more explicit:
\nabla F(x)
is an implicit hyper-gradient involving
second-order information,

\displaystyle\nabla F(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)-\nabla^{2}_{xy}g\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)\times\bigl(\nabla^{2}_{yy}g\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)\bigr)^{-1}\nabla_{y}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr).

(2)

Consequently, computing
\nabla F(x)
in the bilevel setting entails solving the lower-level problem to obtain
y^{*}(x)
and solving a linear system involving
\nabla^{2}_{yy}g
.
To avoid repeatedly resolving inner optimizers and explicitly forming such hyper-gradients, we adopt single-loop first-order coupled dynamics as in
[
16
]
. Accordingly, we introduce the coupled flows below together with their time-scale parameters.

3.1
Coupled Flows and Time Scale Parameters

We specify the above single-loop principle by evolving the outer variable together with inner variables, and, when needed, additional tracking variables.
The resulting continuous time systems are parameterized by explicit time-scale factors that control how fast the inner dynamics track their instantaneous targets relative to the
x
-dynamics.

For
P1
, we consider the two-variable gradient flow

\dot{x}(t)=-\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x(t),y(t)\bigr),\ \dot{y}(t)=\gamma\,\nabla_{y}f\bigl(x(t),y(t)\bigr).

(3)

where
\gamma>0
scales the ascent speed of

.
When

is sufficiently large,

tracks the maximizer of

For
P2
, fix
\lambda>0
and define the lower-level function
g^{*}(x):=\min_{y}g(x,y)
and the penalized objective

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=f(x,y)+\lambda\bigl(g(x,y)-g^{*}(x)\bigr),\ \mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x):=\min_{y}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(x,y).

(4)

To avoid explicit evaluation of
g^{*}(x)
along the trajectory, we introduce a tracker
z(t)
via the following reformulation:

E_{\lambda}(x,y,z):=f(x,y)+\lambda\bigl(g(x,y)-g(x,z)\bigr).

(5)

In this reformulation, maximizing over
z

g^{*}(x)=\min_{z}g(x,z)

We study the three-variable gradient flow with time-scale parameters
\delta>0
and
\eta>0

\displaystyle\dot{x}=-\nabla_{x}E_{\lambda}(x,y,z),\ \dot{y}=-\delta\,\nabla_{y}E_{\lambda}(x,y,z),\ \dot{z}=\eta\,\nabla_{z}E_{\lambda}(x,y,z).

(6)

3.2
A Unified Lyapunov Toolkit

We present a common Lyapunov construction for minimax and bilevel problems, and later instantiate the same construction for the auxiliary min–min–max problem in Appendix
K
. In all cases, the Lyapunov function has the same form: an outer value term plus nonnegative tracking gaps. Its dissipation reduces to controlling cross terms caused by imperfect tracking, which we handle using two shared ingredients: a deviation bound and a time-scale threshold rule that absorbs these terms under sufficient separation.

These two lemmas are the key tools behind the ergodic
O(1/T)
stationarity guarantees proved in Section
4
for all three problems
P1
–
P3

A Unified Lyapunov Construction.

Across all three settings, our Lyapunov function combines a target value function and nonnegative gap terms (tracking errors):

W\;:=\;\Phi(x)\;+\;\alpha\,H_{y}(\cdot)\;+\;\beta\,H_{z}(\cdot),

(7)

where
\Phi(x)
denotes the target outer objective, and
H_{y}\geq 0
and
H_{z}\geq 0
are gap terms that quantify the tracking errors of the inner variables.
Along the coupled dynamics, differentiating

yields a descent term for

Choosing the time-scale parameters sufficiently large allows these cross terms to be absorbed, leading to
\dot{W}\leq-c\,\|\nabla\Phi(x(t))\|^{2}

For

For

4.3
Unified Construction for Convex Inner Problems

Our unified Lyapunov analysis for
P1
–
P3
relies on two structural ingredients:
(i) a deviation-to-residual conversion that links
x
–gradient mismatches to inner residuals,
and (ii) a smooth envelope property for the outer value function.
Strong convexity/concavity is a convenient sufficient condition that simultaneously guarantees both. This section makes precise, step-by-step, where the
O(1/T)
ergodic stationarity template breaks when the inner problems are merely convex/concave, and how to repair the argument using modular replacements (error bounds, PL/sharpness, or regularization) while keeping the same Lyapunov construction.

We first focus on the convex/concave regime where the lower-level optimizer map is single-valued,
so the template breaks only at the two modules
S1
–
S2
.
When the lower-level solution is set-valued, an additional difficulty arises (nonsmooth envelopes and selection dependence);
we defer this extension to Appendix,
where we show that under suitable sensitivity regularity one can still proceed by
modifying only
S1
–
S2
while keeping the same Lyapunov construction.

If the inner objectives are mere convex/concave, then:

•

Failure at
S1

In general,
\arg\min
/
\arg\max
sets are multi-valued, and value functions such as

\max_{y}f(x,y)
or
\min_{y}g(x,y)
need not be differentiable.
Thus the identity

\nabla\Phi(x)=\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u^{*}(x))

(13)

used to produce the descent term may break.

•

Failure at
S2

Lemma
3.1
requires
\mu
–strong convexity of
\psi(x,\cdot)
to obtain

\|D\|\;=\;\|\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u)-\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u^{*})\|\;\lesssim\;\|u-u^{*}\|,\;\|u-u^{*}\|\;\lesssim\;\|\nabla_{u}\psi(x,u)\|.

Without strong convexity/concavity of the inner objective, Lemma
3.1
is not guaranteed to hold.

When strong convexity/concavity is dropped, the proof template breaks exactly at these two modules, and the repairs can be described in a problem-agnostic way.

Repairing
S1
.

When inner optimizers are nonunique,
\Phi
can be nonsmooth and (
13
) may fail.
A standard remedy is to use nonsmooth stationarity

\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial\Phi(x))
with a nonsmooth chain rule, or to restore differentiability via smoothing/regularization.
We do not pursue these nonsmooth-envelope variants here, as they would require a separate nonsmooth analysis
(e.g., subgradient chain rules and differential inclusions), and instead focus on the modular repair of
S2
below.

Assumption 4.9

(Error Bound)
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable and convex in
u
.
Let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}
be a region of interest and

satisfies a (local) gradient error bound on

such that

\operatorname{dist}\bigl(u,U^{*}(x)\bigr)\ \leq\ \kappa\,\|\nabla_{u}\psi(x,u)\|\quad(\forall\,(x,u)\in\mathcal{R})
.

Repairing
S2
.

Assumption
4.9
reinstates the missing metric control needed for Lyapunov absorption.
In smooth convex settings, it is implied (up to constants) by standard sharpness conditions on the relevant sublevel set,
such as PL/gradient-dominance and quadratic growth; see Appendix

4.4
Discrete-time interpretation and diagnostics.

This continuous-time viewpoint also provides a useful reference point for stable Euler discretizations. The S1–S3 normal form also gives a direct Euler one-step interpretation:
for
\dot{u}=G(u)
and
u_{k+1}=u_{k}+hG(u_{k})
,

W(u_{k+1})-W(u_{k})\leq h\dot{W}(u_{k})+O(h^{2}\|G(u_{k})\|^{2}).

Hence discretization preserves the continuous-time dissipation at first order and adds only a controllable remainder; see Appendix
J
. Under weaker geometry, the EB/PL/QG interface changes the conversion constant
C

C^{2}
rule. Figure

,

]

\rho_{\rm reg}
is reduced from

to

6.25
, the observed shifts are
5.8
–
6.0
, and the above-threshold region remains favorable. Details are in Appendix
J.1
.

5
Conclusion

We introduced a value-function-level continuous-time Lyapunov template for minimax, lifted-penalty bilevel, and min–min–max dynamics. The S1–S3 decomposition separates deviation-to-residual conversion from time-scale absorption, yielding closed-form thresholds and making the dependence on inner geometry explicit through EB constants. The finite-time tracking bound connects the penalty dynamics to the ideal hyper-gradient flow, while the Euler analogue and hypercleaning diagnostics indicate that the resulting relative time-scale structure remains visible under stable discretization. Overall, the framework provides a modular route for analyzing nested optimization dynamics across problem classes and weaker inner geometries.

and for every

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq f(x^{\prime},y)+\big\langle\nabla_{x}f(x^{\prime},y),\,x-x^{\prime}\big\rangle,

(16)

\displaystyle\leq f(x,y^{\prime})+\big\langle\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{\prime}),\,y-y^{\prime}\big\rangle.

Proof.

B.1
Proof of Proposition
4.1

Proof.

[S1: smooth envelope.]

Under
\mu
–strong concavity of
f(x,\cdot)
, for each
x
the maximizer
y^{*}(x)
is unique and
Danskin’s theorem gives the smooth-envelope identity

\Phi(x)\ :=\ \max_{y\in Y}f(x,y)\ =\ f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr),\qquad\nabla\Phi(x)\ =\ \nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr).

Lyapunov choice (unified template).

Define the nonnegative gap
H_{y}(x,y):=\Phi(x)-f(x,y)\geq 0
and

W_{\alpha}(x,y)\ :=\ \Phi(x)\ +\ \alpha\,H_{y}(x,y)\qquad(\alpha>0).

(18)

Along the coupled flow (
3
), let

A\ :=\ \nabla\Phi(x)\ =\ \nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr),\qquad B\ :=\ \nabla_{x}f(x,y),\qquad r_{y}\ :=\ \nabla_{y}f(x,y).

Define the
x
–gradient deviation (mismatch)

D_{y}\ :=\ B-A\ =\ \nabla_{x}f(x,y)-\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr).

Then
\dot{x}=-B
and
\dot{y}=\gamma r_{y}
.

We have

\frac{d}{dt}\Phi(x(t))=\langle\nabla\Phi(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle=-\langle A,B\rangle.

Using
\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*}(x))=0
and the chain rule,

\frac{d}{dt}f\bigl(x(t),y^{*}(x(t))\bigr)=\langle A,\dot{x}\rangle,\qquad\frac{d}{dt}f\bigl(x(t),y(t)\bigr)=\langle B,\dot{x}\rangle+\langle r_{y},\dot{y}\rangle.

Hence
\dot{H}_{y}=\langle A-B,\dot{x}\rangle-\langle r_{y},\dot{y}\rangle
and therefore

\dot{H}_{y}=\langle-D_{y},-B\rangle-\gamma\|r_{y}\|^{2}=-\langle D_{y},B\rangle-\gamma\|r_{y}\|^{2}.

From (
18
),

\dot{W}_{\alpha}=\dot{\Phi}+\alpha\dot{H}_{y}=-(1+\alpha)\langle A,B\rangle+\alpha\|B\|^{2}-\alpha\gamma\|r_{y}\|^{2}.

Substituting
B=A+D_{y}
and expanding gives

\dot{W}_{\alpha}=-\|A\|^{2}+(\alpha-1)\langle A,D_{y}\rangle+\alpha\|D_{y}\|^{2}-\alpha\gamma\|r_{y}\|^{2}.

(19)

[S2: deviation-to-residual conversion.]

By the cross-Lipschitz assumption
\|\nabla_{x}f(x,y)-\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{\prime})\|\leq L_{xy}\|y-y^{\prime}\|
and

\mu
–strong concavity of
f(x,\cdot)
(equivalently
\mu
–strong convexity of
-f(x,\cdot)
),
Lemma
3.1
(applied to
\psi(x,y)=-f(x,y)
) yields

\|D_{y}\|\ \leq\ \frac{L_{xy}}{\mu}\,\|r_{y}\|.

(20)

[S3: absorption via time-scale separation.]

Since
(1-\alpha)>0
when
\alpha\in(0,1)
, Young’s inequality gives for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(\alpha-1)\langle A,D_{y}\rangle\leq(1-\alpha)\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|A\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\|D_{y}\|^{2}\Bigr).

Inserting this into (
19
) yields

\dot{W}_{\alpha}\leq-\Bigl(1-\frac{(1-\alpha)\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr)\|A\|^{2}+\Bigl(\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha}{2\varepsilon}\Bigr)\|D_{y}\|^{2}-\alpha\gamma\|r_{y}\|^{2}.

(21)

Using (
20
), we obtain

\dot{W}_{\alpha}\leq-\Bigl(1-\frac{(1-\alpha)\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr)\|A\|^{2}+\Biggl(-\alpha\gamma+\Bigl(\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha}{2\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{L_{xy}}{\mu}\Bigr)^{2}\Biggr)\|r_{y}\|^{2}.

Set
\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2}
and
\varepsilon=2

\dot{W}_{1/2}(t)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|A\|^{2}-\Bigl(\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{5}{8}\Bigl(\frac{L_{xy}}{\mu}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)\|r_{y}\|^{2}.

Thus, if
\gamma\geq\frac{5}{4}\bigl(\frac{L_{xy}}{\mu}\bigr)^{2}
, we have
\dot{W}_{1/2}(t)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\|A\|^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\Phi(x(t))\|^{2}
.

Since
H_{y}\geq 0
, we have
W_{1/2}(x,y)\geq\Phi(x)\geq\Phi_{\inf}:=\inf_{x}\Phi(x)
.
Integrating over
[0,T]
and letting

yields

\mathbb{E}_{t}\big[\|\nabla\Phi(x(t))\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{W_{1/2}(x(0),y(0))-\Phi_{\inf}}{(1/2)\,T},

which is the desired
O(1/T)
ergodic stationarity bound.
∎

Proof of Corollary
4.2

Proof.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
in fact yields a joint dissipation bound that controls

\|\nabla F(x(t))\|
but also the inner residual

\|\nabla_{y}f(x(t),y(t))\|

W_{1/2}(x,y)\ :=\ F(x)\ +\ \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(F(x)-f(x,y)\bigr).

Starting from the general Lyapunov derivative estimate in the proof of

\dot{W}_{\alpha}\leq-\Bigl(1-\frac{(1-\alpha)\varepsilon}{2}\Bigr)\|A\|^{2}+\Biggl(-\alpha\gamma+\Bigl(\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha}{2\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{L_{xy}}{\mu}\Bigr)^{2}\Biggr)\|C\|^{2}.

(22)

(23)

. Since

(26)

[0,T]

W(x(T),y(T),z(T))-W(x(0),y(0),z(0))\ \leq\ -\,c_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x(t))\|^{2}\,dt.

Dropping the nonpositive left-hand side term and using

W(x(T),y(T),z(T))\geq\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x(T))\geq\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,\inf}^{*}
, we obtain

c_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x(t))\|^{2}\,dt\ \leq\ W(x(0),y(0),z(0))-\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,\inf}^{*}.

Letting
t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]
gives

\mathbb{E}_{t}\!\left[\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x(t))\|^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x(t))\|^{2}\,dt\ \leq\ \frac{W(x(0),y(0),z(0))-\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,\inf}^{*}}{c_{1}\,T},

which proves the theorem.
∎

C.2
Proof of Lemma
4.6

Proof.

Fix
x
and abbreviate

y^{*}:=y^{*}(x)
,
y_{\lambda}^{*}:=y_{\lambda}^{*}(x)
, and
\Delta:=y_{\lambda}^{*}-y^{*}
. Since
y^{*}
is the unique minimizer of
g(x,\cdot)
, Danskin’s theorem yields

\nabla_{x}g^{*}(x)=\nabla_{x}g(x,y^{*})
.
Moreover, by the envelope theorem applied to
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)=\min_{y}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(x,y)
,

\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)=\nabla_{x}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})+\lambda\nabla_{x}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})-\lambda\nabla_{x}g(x,y^{*}).

(44)

On the other hand, differentiating
F(x)=f(x,y^{*}(x))
via the implicit-function formula
(see (
2
)) gives

\nabla F(x)=\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{*})-\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\,\bigl(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\bigr)^{-1}\,\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*}).

(45)

The first-order optimality conditions for
y^{*}
and
y_{\lambda}^{*}
are

\nabla_{y}g(x,y^{*})=0,\qquad\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})+\lambda\nabla_{y}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})=0.

By strong convexity of
g(x,\cdot)
,

\|\Delta\|=\|y_{\lambda}^{*}-y^{*}\|\ \leq\ \frac{1}{\mu}\,\|\nabla_{y}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\|=\frac{1}{\mu\lambda}\,\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\|.

\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\|\leq\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|+L_{fy}\|\Delta\|.

Combining the last two displays yields

\|\Delta\|\leq\frac{1}{\mu\lambda}\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|+\frac{L_{fy}}{\mu\lambda}\|\Delta\|.

For any
\lambda\geq\bar{\lambda}=2L_{fy}/\mu
, we have
1-\frac{L_{fy}}{\mu\lambda}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, and hence

\|\Delta\|\ \leq\ \frac{2}{\mu\lambda}\,\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|.

(46)

Moreover, the same estimates imply

\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\|\leq\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|+L_{fy}\|\Delta\|\leq 2\,\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|.

(47)

We now rewrite the term

\lambda\bigl(\nabla_{x}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})-\nabla_{x}g(x,y^{*})\bigr)

in a form that matches the hyper-gradient structure

\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g)^{-1}\nabla_{y}f
appearing in (
45
).

(i) Relating
\Delta
to an inverse Hessian times
\nabla_{y}f
.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to the map
y\mapsto\nabla_{y}g(x,y)
along the segment

y^{*}\to y_{\lambda}^{*}=y^{*}+\Delta
, we have

\nabla_{y}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})-\nabla_{y}g(x,y^{*})=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{yy}^{2}g\bigl(x,y^{*}+s\Delta\bigr)\,\Delta\,ds.

Since
y^{*}
is the minimizer of
g(x,\cdot)
,
\nabla_{y}g(x,y^{*})=0
, and thus the left-hand side equals

\nabla_{y}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})
.
Define the averaged Hessian

\bar{B}\ :=\ \int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{yy}^{2}g\bigl(x,y^{*}+s\Delta\bigr)\,ds,\qquad\text{so that}\qquad\nabla_{y}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})=\bar{B}\,\Delta.

By
\mu
-strong convexity of
g(x,\cdot)
, we have
\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,\cdot)\succeq\mu I
, hence

\bar{B}\succeq\mu I
and
\|\bar{B}^{-1}\|\leq 1/\mu
.

On the other hand, the optimality condition defining
y_{\lambda}^{*}
is

\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})+\lambda\nabla_{y}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})=0
, i.e.,

\nabla_{y}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*}).

Combining this with
\nabla_{y}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})=\bar{B}\Delta
yields the key identity

\lambda\Delta\ =\ -\,\bar{B}^{-1}\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*}),

which already has the desired “inverse-Hessian times
\nabla_{y}f
” form.

(ii) Converting the
x
-gradient difference to a mixed-Hessian integral.

Next, apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to the map
y\mapsto\nabla_{x}g(x,y)
:

\nabla_{x}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})-\nabla_{x}g(x,y^{*})=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{xy}^{2}g\bigl(x,y^{*}+s\Delta\bigr)\,\Delta\,ds.

Multiplying by
\lambda
and substituting
\lambda\Delta=-\bar{B}^{-1}\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})
gives

\lambda\bigl(\nabla_{x}g(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})-\nabla_{x}g(x,y^{*})\bigr)=-\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{xy}^{2}g\bigl(x,y^{*}+s\Delta\bigr)\,\bar{B}^{-1}\,\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\,ds.

(48)

This representation makes explicit that the penalty term is an averaged version of

\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g)^{-1}\nabla_{y}f
, which is precisely the second term in the hyper-gradient
(
45
).

Combining (
44
), (
45
), and (
48
), we can write

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)-\nabla F(x)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\bigl(\nabla_{x}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})-\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{*})\bigr)}_{(\mathrm{I})}

(49)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\Biggl(\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\bigl(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\bigr)^{-1}\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})-\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*}+s\Delta)\,\bar{B}^{-1}\,\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\,ds\Biggr)}_{(\mathrm{II})}.

For term (
\mathrm{I}
), Assumptions
2.2
.2 gives
\|(\mathrm{I})\|\leq L_{fx}\|\Delta\|
.

For term (
\mathrm{II}
), add and subtract

\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\bar{B}^{-1}\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})
and use the triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{II})\|

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left\|\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\bigl(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})-\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\bigr)\right\|}_{(\mathrm{II}\text{-}a)}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\left\|\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\Bigl(\bigl(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\bigr)^{-1}-\bar{B}^{-1}\Bigr)\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\right\|}_{(\mathrm{II}\text{-}b)}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})-\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*}+s\Delta)\bigr)\bar{B}^{-1}\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\,ds\right\|}_{(\mathrm{II}\text{-}c)}.

(
\mathrm{II}
-a) Using
\|\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\|\leq M_{gxy}
,
\|(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*}))^{-1}\|\leq 1/\mu
,

\|(\mathrm{II}\text{-}a)\|\leq\frac{M_{gxy}}{\mu}\,L_{fy}\,\|\Delta\|.

(
\mathrm{II}
-b) First,
\|\nabla_{xy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})\|\leq M_{gxy}
and (
47
) gives

\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y_{\lambda}^{*})\|\leq 2\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|
.
Next, since
\bar{B}\succeq\mu I
, we have
\|\bar{B}^{-1}\|\leq 1/\mu
, and hence

\Bigl\|(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*}))^{-1}-\bar{B}^{-1}\Bigr\|\leq\|(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*}))^{-1}\|\,\|\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})-\bar{B}\|\,\|\bar{B}^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\,\|\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})-\bar{B}\|.

\|\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})-\bar{B}\|=\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*})-\nabla_{yy}^{2}g(x,y^{*}+s\Delta)\bigr)\,ds\right\|\leq\int_{0}^{1}L_{gyy}\,s\|\Delta\|\,ds=\frac{L_{gyy}}{2}\|\Delta\|.

Therefore,

\|(\mathrm{II}\text{-}b)\|\leq M_{gxy}\cdot\frac{L_{gyy}}{2\mu^{2}}\|\Delta\|\cdot 2\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|=\frac{M_{gxy}L_{gyy}}{\mu^{2}}\,\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|\,\|\Delta\|.

(
\mathrm{II}
-c)
\|\bar{B}^{-1}\|\leq 1/\mu
, and (

\|(\mathrm{II}\text{-}c)\|\leq\int_{0}^{1}L_{gxy}\,s\|\Delta\|\,ds\cdot\frac{1}{\mu}\cdot 2\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|=\frac{L_{gxy}}{\mu}\,\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|\,\|\Delta\|.

Combining these bounds yields

\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)-\nabla F(x)\|\leq\Biggl(L_{fx}+\frac{M_{gxy}L_{fy}}{\mu}+\Bigl(\frac{L_{gxy}}{\mu}+\frac{M_{gxy}L_{gyy}}{\mu^{2}}\Bigr)\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|\Biggr)\,\|\Delta\|.

Finally, applying (
46
) gives

\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)-\nabla F(x)\|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\frac{2}{\mu}\Bigl(L_{fx}+\frac{M_{gxy}L_{fy}}{\mu}\Bigr)\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|+\frac{2}{\mu^{2}}\Bigl(L_{gxy}+\frac{M_{gxy}L_{gyy}}{\mu}\Bigr)\|\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*})\|^{2}\right].

(x,z)

\|D_{z}\|\ \leq\ \frac{L_{gx}}{\mu}\,\|r_{z}\|\ =:\ C_{z}\,\|r_{z}\|.

(60)

(ii) Bound for
D_{y}
.

Let
\phi_{\lambda}(x,y):=f(x,y)+\lambda g(x,y)
and assume that for every
x
,
the mapping
y\mapsto\phi_{\lambda}(x,y)
is
\mu_{\lambda}
–strongly convex for some
\mu_{\lambda}>0
.
Then for all
(x,y)
,

\|D_{y}\|\ \leq\ \frac{L_{fx}+\lambda L_{gx}}{\mu_{\lambda}}\,\|r_{y}\|\ =:\ C_{y}(\lambda)\,\|r_{y}\|.

(61)

In particular, if
y\mapsto f(x,y)
is
\rho
–weakly convex and
y\mapsto g(x,y)
is
\mu
–strongly convex,
then
\phi_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)
is
(\lambda\mu-\rho)
–strongly convex, so (
61
) holds with

\mu_{\lambda}=\lambda\mu-\rho>0
whenever
\lambda>\rho/\mu
.

Proof.

We first prove (
61
). Define
\phi_{\lambda}(x,y):=f(x,y)+\lambda g(x,y)
so that

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(x,y)=\phi_{\lambda}(x,y)-\lambda g^{*}(x).

Since
g^{*}(x)
is independent of
y
, we have

\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(x,y)=\nabla_{y}\phi_{\lambda}(x,y),\qquad\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(x,y^{\prime})=\nabla_{x}\phi_{\lambda}(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\phi_{\lambda}(x,y^{\prime}).

By Assumptions
2.2
,

\|\nabla_{x}\phi_{\lambda}(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\phi_{\lambda}(x,y^{\prime})\|\leq\|\nabla_{x}f(x,y)-\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{\prime})\|+\lambda\|\nabla_{x}g(x,y)-\nabla_{x}g(x,y^{\prime})\|\leq(L_{fx}+\lambda L_{gx})\|y-y^{\prime}\|.

Applying Lemma
3.1
to
\psi=\phi_{\lambda}
yields

\bigl\|\nabla_{x}\phi_{\lambda}(x,y)-\nabla_{x}\phi_{\lambda}\bigl(x,y_{\lambda}^{*}(x)\bigr)\bigr\|\leq\frac{L_{fx}+\lambda L_{gx}}{\mu_{\lambda}}\,\bigl\|\nabla_{y}\phi_{\lambda}(x,y)\bigr\|.

Using the identities above, this is exactly (
61
).

We now prove (
60
). Since
g(x,\cdot)
is
\mu
–strongly convex, its minimizer

y^{*}(x)\in\arg\min_{u}g(x,u)
is unique and satisfies
\nabla_{y}g(x,y^{*}(x))=0
.
By Danskin’s theorem,
\nabla g^{*}(x)=\nabla_{x}g(x,y^{*}(x))
.
Moreover, by Assumption
2.2
.3,
\nabla_{x}g(x,\cdot)
is
L_{gx}
–Lipschitz in
y
.
Applying Lemma
3.1
to
\psi=g
(with
u=z
and
u^{*}=y^{*}(x)
) gives

\|\nabla_{x}g(x,z)-\nabla_{x}g(x,y^{*}(x))\|\leq\frac{L_{gx}}{\mu}\,\|\nabla_{y}g(x,z)\|.

Substituting
D_{z}=\nabla_{x}g(x,z)-\nabla g^{*}(x)=\nabla_{x}g(x,z)-\nabla_{x}g(x,y^{*}(x))

and
r_{z}=\nabla_{y}g(x,z)
yields (
60
).
∎

Appendix E
Relaxing inner strong convexity/concavity: error-bound instantiations for
P1
–
P3

This appendix complements Section
4.3
by instantiating the
error bound (Assumption
4.9
) in place of strong convexity/concavity repair for
P1
–
P3
.
Throughout, the unified Lyapunov construction
W=\Phi+\alpha H_{y}+\beta H_{z}
is unchanged.
Only the deviation module is replaced: instead of strong convexity/concavity, we assume a gradient error bound (EB)
that yields a deviation-to-residual conversion of the form
\|D\|\leq C\|r\|
on a region of interest
(typically a Lyapunov sublevel set).

Why we focus on Assumption
4.9
(EB).

The Lyapunov absorption step (Lemma
3.2
) only requires the conversion

\|D\|\leq C\|r\|
.
Assumption
4.9
is exactly an interface condition that delivers this conversion.
Conditions such as PL/gradient-dominance and quadratic growth (QG) are widely used sufficient mechanisms
to guarantee an EB on the relevant sublevel set.
Hence it is enough to develop the theory under EB, and treat PL/QG as standard instantiations that imply EB
under the usual smooth convex regularity.

E.1
Notation: PL and QG, and their implication to EB

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable and let

h^{*}:=\inf_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(u)
and
U^{*}:=\arg\min_{u}h(u)
(assumed nonempty).
We write
\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*}):=\inf_{v\in U^{*}}\|u-v\|
.

Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL) inequality.

We say that
h
satisfies the PL inequality on a set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with parameter
\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}>0
if

\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla h(u)\|^{2}\ \geq\ \mu_{\mathrm{PL}}\bigl(h(u)-h^{*}\bigr)\qquad(\forall\,u\in\mathcal{U}).

(62)

Equivalently,
h(u)-h^{*}\leq\frac{1}{2\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}}\|\nabla h(u)\|^{2}
on
\mathcal{U}
.

Quadratic growth (QG).

We say that
h
satisfies QG on
\mathcal{U}
with parameter
\mu_{\mathrm{QG}}>0
if

h(u)-h^{*}\ \geq\ \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{QG}}}{2}\,\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*})^{2}\qquad(\forall\,u\in\mathcal{U}).

(63)

EB as the interface condition.

Assumption
4.9
specializes here to the gradient error bound

\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*})\ \leq\ \kappa\,\|\nabla h(u)\|\qquad(\forall\,u\in\mathcal{U}),

(64)

for some
\kappa>0
.

QG + convexity
\Rightarrow
EB.

If
h
is convex and differentiable, then for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
u^{*}\in U^{*}
,
convexity gives

h(u)-h^{*}\leq\langle\nabla h(u),u-u^{*}\rangle\leq\|\nabla h(u)\|\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*})
.
Combining this with QG (
63
) on
\mathcal{U}
yields, for all
u\in\mathcal{U}
,

\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{QG}}}{2}\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*})^{2}\ \leq\ h(u)-h^{*}\ \leq\ \|\nabla h(u)\|\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*}),

and therefore

\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*})\ \leq\ \frac{2}{\mu_{\mathrm{QG}}}\,\|\nabla h(u)\|\qquad(\forall\,u\in\mathcal{U}).

(65)

Thus QG (together with convexity) implies EB with
\kappa=2/\mu_{\mathrm{QG}}
.

PL + QG
\Rightarrow
EB.

Even without invoking convexity, if both PL (
62
) and QG (
63
) hold on
\mathcal{U}
, then

\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*})^{2}\ \leq\ \frac{2}{\mu_{\mathrm{QG}}}\bigl(h(u)-h^{*}\bigr)\ \leq\ \frac{1}{\mu_{\mathrm{QG}}\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}}\|\nabla h(u)\|^{2},

hence EB holds with
\kappa=(\mu_{\mathrm{QG}}\mu_{\mathrm{PL}})^{-1/2}
.
In many smooth convex settings, PL implies QG on sublevel sets under standard regularity;
in such cases PL alone can be used as a convenient sufficient condition for EB via the intermediate QG step.
We emphasize, however, that for our Lyapunov template EB is the only required condition:
any route that establishes EB on the relevant region can be plugged in without altering the Lyapunov function.

E.2
A generic deviation-to-residual conversion under an EB

We return to the parametric setting
\psi(x,u)
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}
.
Let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}
be a region of interest.
Assume
\psi
is differentiable and convex in
u
and define

U^{*}(x)\ :=\ \arg\min_{u}\psi(x,u),\qquad r_{u}(x,u)\ :=\ \nabla_{u}\psi(x,u).

The
x
–gradient mismatch is the deviation

D_{u}(x,u;u^{*})\ :=\ \nabla_{x}\psi(x,u)-\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u^{*}),\qquad u^{*}\in U^{*}(x).

Assume that
\nabla_{x}\psi(x,\cdot)
is
L_{xu}
–Lipschitz on
\mathcal{R}
, i.e.,

\|\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u_{1})-\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u_{2})\|\ \leq\ L_{xu}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|\qquad(\forall\,(x,u_{1}),(x,u_{2})\in\mathcal{R}).

If
\psi
satisfies the EB condition (Assumption
4.9
) on
\mathcal{R}
, then Lemma
4.10
gives

\inf_{u^{*}\in U^{*}(x)}\|D_{u}(x,u;u^{*})\|\ \leq\ L_{xu}\kappa\,\|r_{u}(x,u)\|\qquad(\forall\,(x,u)\in\mathcal{R}),

(66)

so the deviation-to-residual conversion holds with the constant

C\ =\ L_{xu}\kappa.

Plugging (
66
) into Lemma
3.2
preserves the algebraic form of the time-scale thresholds;
only the conversion constant changes, and the thresholds inherit the universal dependence on
C^{2}
.

E.3
Minimax without strong convexity/concavity

In
P1
, the inner maximization in
y
corresponds to minimizing
\psi(x,y):=-f(x,y)
in
y
.
The mismatch/residual pair in Table

is

D_{y}=\nabla_{x}f(x,y)-\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr),\qquad r_{y}=\nabla_{y}f(x,y),

with effective time scale

.

Assume

is concave and differentiable for each

\mathcal{R}_{1}:=\{(x,y):W(x,y)\leq W(x(0),y(0))\}

Under strong concavity with parameter

Appendix F
Nonunique inner solutions via penalty envelopes

F.1
What fails beyond
S1
–
S2
under nonuniqueness

In the strongly convex/concave setting, the optimizer maps are single-valued and the envelope identity

\nabla\Phi(x)=\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u^{*}(x))
is immediate, which pins down a unique reference gradient at the inner optimum.
When the inner problems are merely convex/concave, optimizer sets are typically multi-valued, and two additional issues arise:

•

(Envelope nonsmoothness / selection dependence).

Even if
\psi(x,\cdot)
is convex, the value function
\Phi(x)=\min_{u}\psi(x,u)
or
\Phi(x)=\max_{u}\psi(x,u)
can be nonsmooth.
Then
S1
cannot be justified by uniqueness, and the descent term must be formulated either in a nonsmooth sense
(e.g.,
\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial\Phi(x))
) or via a smooth surrogate.

•

(Deviation ambiguity).

If
U^{*}(x)
is set-valued, an expression of the form

D(x,u;u^{*})=\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u)-\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u^{*})
depends on the choice of
u^{*}\in U^{*}(x)
.
This is not a separate difficulty if
\Phi
is differentiable: differentiability forces the set of partial gradients at optimizers to be a singleton
(see Lemma
F.3
below), hence the deviation becomes unambiguous.

Our goal in this appendix is to keep the unified Lyapunov construction unchanged and only swap the two steps

S1
–
S2
. To do so in the nonunique regime, we work with a smooth penalty envelope surrogate
and invoke a standard local sensitivity condition (solution-set Lipschitz continuity + active-set stability) that implies
differentiability of the surrogate value function even when inner solutions are not unique.

F.2
Penalty-envelope reformulation for
P2

We specialize to
P2
and rewrite the penalized outer value in a form that matches the penalty envelope studied in
[
17
]
.
Let
Y
be compact and define, for
\sigma>0
,

h_{\sigma}(x,y)\ :=\ g(x,y)+\sigma f(x,y),\qquad\ell(x,\sigma)\ :=\ \min_{y\in Y}h_{\sigma}(x,y),\qquad T(x,\sigma)\ :=\ \arg\min_{y\in Y}h_{\sigma}(x,y).

(70)

Define the penalty-envelope value function

\psi_{\sigma}(x)\ :=\ \frac{\ell(x,\sigma)-\ell(x,0)}{\sigma},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\ell(x,0)=\min_{y\in Y}g(x,y)=:g^{*}(x).

(71)

Lemma F.1
.

Let
\lambda:=1/\sigma
. Then

\psi_{1/\lambda}(x)=\min_{y\in Y}\Bigl(f(x,y)+\lambda g(x,y)\Bigr)-\lambda g^{*}(x)=\min_{y\in Y}\Bigl(f(x,y)+\lambda\bigl(g(x,y)-g^{*}(x)\bigr)\Bigr)=:\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x),

(72)

which is precisely the penalized outer value used in
P2
.

Proof.

By
\sigma=1/\lambda
, we have
h_{1/\lambda}(x,y)=g(x,y)+\tfrac{1}{\lambda}f(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\bigl(f(x,y)+\lambda g(x,y)\bigr)
.
Thus
\ell(x,1/\lambda)=\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\min_{y\in Y}\bigl(f(x,y)+\lambda g(x,y)\bigr)
and
\ell(x,0)=g^{*}(x)
.
Plugging into (
71
) gives (
72
).
∎

Scope of the appendix.

All Lyapunov/ergodic statements below are for a fixed
\sigma>0
(equivalently, a fixed penalty
\lambda=1/\sigma
),
i.e., stationarity for
\Phi=\psi_{\sigma}=\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}
.
Relating this surrogate stationarity to the limiting bilevel hyper-objective

\psi(x):=\partial_{\sigma}\ell(x,\sigma)|_{\sigma=0+}
requires additional compatibility at
\sigma=0+
and is treated separately.

F.3
A local sensitivity assumption

We now state assumptions in
[
17
]
that imply differentiability of the penalty envelope
without assuming uniqueness of
T(x,\sigma)
.

Assumption F.2
.

Fix a region of interest
\mathcal{X}
(e.g., the
x
-projection of a Lyapunov sublevel set).
Assume that for each
\sigma\in\{0,\bar{\sigma}\}
(where
\bar{\sigma}:=1/\lambda
is the fixed surrogate parameter),
the solution mapping
x\mapsto T(x,\sigma)
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{X}
:
for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
there exist
\delta>0
and
L_{T}<\infty
such that

\operatorname{dist}\bigl(T(x_{1},\sigma),T(x_{2},\sigma)\bigr)\ \leq\ L_{T}\,\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\qquad(\forall\,x_{1},x_{2}\in B(x,\delta)\cap\mathcal{X}).

Here
\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes a set-distance (e.g., Hausdorff distance), and
\operatorname{dist}(y,S):=\inf_{s\in S}\|y-s\|
for point-to-set distance
and
B(x,\delta):=\{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}:\|x^{\prime}-x\|<\delta\}
denotes the (open) Euclidean ball.

Assumption
F.2
is local and does not require global uniqueness of
T(x,\sigma)
.
It imposes a Lipschitz stability of the solution set mapping with respect to
x
, which rules out abrupt set-valued
jumps of minimizers on the region of interest and, in turn, supports a smooth-envelope characterization of the surrogate
value function at fixed
\sigma
.

F.4
Repairing
S1
: smooth envelope without unique minimizers

We first provide a standard consequence of Danskin’s theorem: differentiability implies a singleton set of optimizer-gradients.

Lemma F.3
.

Let
U
be compact and let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\times U\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable in
x
with
\nabla_{x}\psi
continuous.
Define
\Phi(x):=\max_{u\in U}\psi(x,u)
and
U^{*}(x):=\arg\max_{u\in U}\psi(x,u)
.
If
\Phi
is differentiable at
x
, then

\nabla\Phi(x)=\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u)\qquad(\forall\,u\in U^{*}(x)),

and consequently the set
\{\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u):u\in U^{*}(x)\}
is a singleton.

Proof.

Danskin’s theorem gives
\partial\Phi(x)=\mathrm{conv}\{\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u):u\in U^{*}(x)\}
.
If
\Phi
is differentiable, then
\partial\Phi(x)=\{\nabla\Phi(x)\}
is a singleton.
The convex hull of a set of vectors can be a singleton only if the set itself is contained in that singleton,
hence
\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u)
is identical for all
u\in U^{*}(x)
and equals
\nabla\Phi(x)
.
∎

We now apply this to the penalty envelope
\psi_{\sigma}
.

Fix
\bar{\sigma}>0
and recall

\ell(x,\sigma):=\min_{y\in Y}h_{\sigma}(x,y),\qquad h_{\sigma}(x,y):=g(x,y)+\sigma f(x,y),\qquad\psi_{\sigma}(x):=\frac{\ell(x,\sigma)-\ell(x,0)}{\sigma}.

Under Assumption
F.2
, Lemma A.2 of
[
17
]
implies that the value functions

x\mapsto\ell(x,\bar{\sigma})
and
x\mapsto\ell(x,0)
are differentiable on
\mathcal{X}
.
Moreover, for each
\sigma\in\{0,\bar{\sigma}\}
, differentiability of
\ell(\cdot,\sigma)
together with
Lemma
F.3
yields the smooth envelope identity

\nabla_{x}\ell(x,\sigma)=\nabla_{x}h_{\sigma}(x,y^{*})\qquad(\forall\,y^{*}\in T(x,\sigma)).

Consequently,
\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}
is differentiable on
\mathcal{X}
and satisfies

\nabla\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(x)=\frac{1}{\bar{\sigma}}\Bigl(\nabla_{x}h_{\bar{\sigma}}(x,y_{\bar{\sigma}}^{*})-\nabla_{x}h_{0}(x,y_{0}^{*})\Bigr)=\nabla_{x}f(x,y_{\bar{\sigma}}^{*})+\frac{1}{\bar{\sigma}}\Bigl(\nabla_{x}g(x,y_{\bar{\sigma}}^{*})-\nabla_{x}g(x,y_{0}^{*})\Bigr),

(73)

where
y_{\bar{\sigma}}^{*}\in T(x,\bar{\sigma})
and
y_{0}^{*}\in T(x,0)
are arbitrary minimizers.
By Lemma
F.3
, the right-hand side of (
73
) is independent of these choices.

Interpretation for
P2
.

With
\bar{\sigma}=1/\lambda
, Lemma
F.1
and (
73
) recover exactly the smooth-envelope module
S1

for
\Phi(x)=\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)
, using the same first-order objects tracked by our flow
(
y
tracks
T(x,1/\lambda)
and
z
tracks
T(x,0)
).

F.5
Repairing
S2
: deviation-to-residual under nonunique minimizers

Once
S1
is restored, the remaining obstruction is
S2
: we must convert
x
-gradient deviations
(induced by imperfect inner tracking) into residuals dissipated by the inner dynamics.

For the
y
-subproblem at
\sigma=\bar{\sigma}
, define the
x
-gradient deviation as

D_{y}(x,y)\ :=\ \nabla_{x}h_{\bar{\sigma}}(x,y)-\nabla_{x}h_{\bar{\sigma}}(x,y^{*}),\qquad y^{*}\in T(x,\bar{\sigma}).

By the smooth-envelope property established in Appendix
F.4
, the choice of
y^{*}\in T(x,\bar{\sigma})
does not matter, since
\nabla_{x}h_{\bar{\sigma}}(x,y^{*})
is identical for all minimizers in
T(x,\bar{\sigma})
.

Similarly for the
z
-subproblem at
\sigma=0
,

D_{z}(x,z)\ :=\ \nabla_{x}g(x,z)-\nabla_{x}g(x,z^{*}),\qquad z^{*}\in T(x,0).

To recover the conversion

without strong convexity, we use the EB module
(Assumption

in the main text), which is already formulated in terms of distance to a

of minimizers
and hence is compatible with nonuniqueness.

Proposition F.4
.

Let
\psi(x,u)
be differentiable and convex in
u
on a region
\mathcal{R}
.
Assume
\nabla_{x}\psi(x,\cdot)
is
L_{xu}
-Lipschitz in
u
on
\mathcal{R}
and the EB condition holds on
\mathcal{R}
:

\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*}(x))\leq\kappa\|\nabla_{u}\psi(x,u)\|
, where
U^{*}(x)=\arg\min_{u}\psi(x,u)
.
Then for all
(x,u)\in\mathcal{R}
,

\inf_{u^{*}\in U^{*}(x)}\|\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u)-\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u^{*})\|\ \leq\ L_{xu}\,\kappa\,\|\nabla_{u}\psi(x,u)\|.

Proof.

Fix
(x,u)\in\mathcal{R}
. By Lipschitzness,

\inf_{u^{*}\in U^{*}(x)}\|\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u)-\nabla_{x}\psi(x,u^{*})\|\ \leq\ L_{xu}\,\inf_{u^{*}\in U^{*}(x)}\|u-u^{*}\|\ =\ L_{xu}\,\operatorname{dist}(u,U^{*}(x)).

Applying EB yields the claim.
∎

Applying Proposition

to

\|D_{y}\|\leq C_{y}\|r_{y}\|,\qquad C_{y}=L_{xy}^{(h_{\bar{\sigma}})}\kappa_{h_{\bar{\sigma}}},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|D_{z}\|\leq C_{z}\|r_{z}\|,\qquad C_{z}=L_{xz}^{(g)}\kappa_{g},

F.6
Ergodic stationarity for
P2
under nonunique inner solutions

We show that the unified Lyapunov proof for
P2
extends to nonunique lower-level solutions
without changing the Lyapunov construction.
The only modifications are the two modules
S1
–
S2
.

Fix
\lambda>0
and let
\bar{\sigma}:=1/\lambda
.
By Lemma
F.1
, the penalized outer value in
P2
admits the penalty-envelope form

\Phi(x)=\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)=\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(x)
.
Under the local sensitivity assumption in Appendix
F.4

(solution-set Lipschitz continuity and the existence of a regular stable optimizer at
\sigma\in\{0,\bar{\sigma}\}
),
the value functions
x\mapsto\ell(x,\bar{\sigma})
and
x\mapsto\ell(x,0)
are differentiable on the trajectory region,
and hence
\Phi
is differentiable and satisfies the smooth-envelope identity required by
S1
.
Moreover, Lemma
F.3
implies that the optimizer-gradient is unique at each
\sigma\in\{0,\bar{\sigma}\}
,
so the choice of minimizers

and
z^{*}\in T(x,0)
does not affect the envelope gradients.

To execute the absorption step, we control the
x
-gradient mismatches by residuals dissipated by the auxiliary dynamics.
Assume that on the Lyapunov sublevel set: (i) the subproblems
y\mapsto f(x,y)+\lambda g(x,y)
and
z\mapsto g(x,z)

are convex and differentiable with nonempty minimizer sets, (ii) the gradient error-bound (EB) conditions hold for both subproblems,
and (iii)
\nabla_{x}f
and
\nabla_{x}g
are Lipschitz in the inner variable.
Then Lemma
4.10
yields the deviation-to-residual conversion

:

\|D_{y}\|\leq C_{y}\|r_{y}\|,\qquad\|D_{z}\|\leq C_{z}\|r_{z}\|,

with constants determined by the cross-Lipschitz moduli and EB constants on the same sublevel set.
With
S1
–
S2
in place, the Lyapunov derivative admits the normal form
(

), and Lemma
3.2
absorbs the cross terms once the time scales exceed the

Extension to
P1
and
P3
.

The same modular argument applies to any nested value function of the form

\Phi(x)=\operatorname{opt}_{u\in U}\psi(x,u)
(min or max), with a coupled flow that tracks the corresponding inner solutions.
In
P1
,
\Phi(x)=F(x)=\max_{y\in Y}f(x,y)
and the coupled flow tracks the inner maximizers.
Nonuniqueness affects only

: we require a local sensitivity assumption ensuring differentiability of

(and hence an envelope gradient that is independent of the maximizer selection), while

is provided by EB
for the inner maximization written as minimization of

In

and

, and EB for each inner

separately for

Remark.

Our ergodic Lyapunov analysis is carried out for a fixed surrogate parameter

–
S2
.
Linking surrogate stationarity to the limiting hyper-objective at

As shown in

(e.g., local stability of solution sets and active constraints) one can invoke a Theorem 3.1-type result to justify

Importantly, this

does not modify the Lyapunov differentiation or the time-scale absorption steps.

Proof of Theorems in Section

Assumption H.1
.

Suppose that

\|\nabla F(x)-\nabla F(x^{\prime})\|\leq L_{F}\|x-x^{\prime}\|,\qquad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}.

H.1
Proof of Theorem
G.1

Proof.

Let
e(t):=x(t)-w(t)
be the trajectory error. We analyze the growth of
\|e(t)\|
by differentiating its squared norm:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|e(t)\|^{2}\;=\;\langle x(t)-w(t),\,\dot{x}(t)-\dot{w}(t)\rangle.

Recall the dynamics of the ideal flow is
\dot{w}=-\nabla F(w)
. For the coupled system, as derived in (
26
), the dynamics of
x(t)
satisfy:

\dot{x}\;=\;-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)-D_{y}+\lambda D_{z},

where
D_{y}
and
D_{z}
are the deviation terms defined in Proposition
D.1
.
Substituting these dynamics into the derivative yields:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|e\|^{2}\;

\displaystyle=\;\langle x-w,\,-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)-D_{y}+\lambda D_{z}+\nabla F(w)\rangle

\displaystyle=\;\langle x-w,\,\nabla F(w)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)-D_{y}+\lambda D_{z}\rangle.

We decompose the gradient difference term by adding and subtracting
\nabla F(x)
:

\displaystyle\nabla F(w)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)\;=\;\underbrace{\bigl(\nabla F(w)-\nabla F(x)\bigr)}_{\text{Smoothness}}\;+\;\underbrace{\bigl(\nabla F(x)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)\bigr)}_{\text{Approximation Bias}}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we bound each component:

(i)

Smoothness:
Since
F
is
L_{F}
-smooth for some
L_{F}>0
(Assumption
H.1
),

\langle x-w,\nabla F(w)-\nabla F(x)\rangle\;\leq\;\|e\|\,\|\nabla F(w)-\nabla F(x)\|\;\leq\;L_{F}\|e\|^{2}.

(ii)

Bias:
By the Bridge Lemma (Lemma
4.6
),
\|\nabla F(x)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)\|\leq C_{\mathrm{br}}/\lambda
. Thus,

\langle x-w,\nabla F(x)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)\rangle\;\leq\;\|e\|\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}}{\lambda}.

(iii)

Tracking Errors:

\langle x-w,-D_{y}+\lambda D_{z}\rangle\;\leq\;\|e\|\bigl(\|D_{y}\|+\lambda\|D_{z}\|\bigr).

Combining these bounds results in a differential inequality for
\|e(t)\|
(assuming
\|e(t)\|\neq 0
):

\frac{d}{dt}\|e(t)\|\;\leq\;L_{F}\|e(t)\|\;+\;\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}}{\lambda}\;+\;\|D_{y}(t)\|\;+\;\lambda\|D_{z}(t)\|\right).

Applying Grönwall’s inequality with
e(0)=0
:

\|e(t)\|\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{t}e^{L_{F}(t-s)}\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}}{\lambda}+\|D_{y}(s)\|+\lambda\|D_{z}(s)\|\right)ds.

(76)

Taking the supremum over
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|e(t)\|\;\leq\;e^{L_{F}T}\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}T}{\lambda}+\int_{0}^{T}\|D_{y}(s)\|ds+\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\|D_{z}(s)\|ds\right).

(77)

We bound the integrals using the Lyapunov dissipation.
Combining Lemma
C.1
with the deviation bounds
\|D_{y}\|\leq\ C_{y}(\lambda)\,\|r_{y}\|
and
\|D_{z}\|\leq C_{z}\|r_{z}\|
, and applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the integral:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|D_{y}(s)\|ds\;

\displaystyle\leq\;\sqrt{T}\,C_{y}(\lambda)\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|r_{y}(s)\|^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\;\sqrt{T}\,C_{y}(\lambda)\left(\frac{W(0)-W(T)}{c_{y}(\delta,\lambda)}\right)^{1/2}\;\leq\;\sqrt{TV_{0}}\frac{C_{y}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{c_{y}(\delta,\lambda)}}.

Similarly for the
z
-term:

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\|D_{z}(s)\|ds\;

\displaystyle\leq\;\lambda\sqrt{T}\,C_{z}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|r_{z}(s)\|^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}\leq\lambda\sqrt{TV_{0}}\frac{C_{z}}{\sqrt{c_{z}(\eta,\lambda)}}.

Plugging these integral bounds back into (
77
) yields the stated theorem.
∎

H.2
Proof of Theorem
G.3

Proof.

Let
e(t):=x(t)-w(t)
. Differentiating the squared norm
\frac{1}{2}\|e(t)\|^{2}
:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|e\|^{2}\;=\;\langle x-w,\dot{x}-\dot{w}\rangle.

Substituting the dynamics
\dot{x}=-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)-D_{y}+\lambda D_{z}
and
\dot{w}=-\nabla F(w)
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|e\|^{2}\;

\displaystyle=\;\langle x-w,-\nabla F(w)-(-\nabla F(x))\rangle+\langle x-w,-\nabla F(x)-(-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x))\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle x-w,-D_{y}+\lambda D_{z}\rangle.

We bound each term using the assumptions:

(i)

Strong Convexity:
Since
F
is
\mu_{F}
-strongly convex,

\langle x-w,-(\nabla F(x)-\nabla F(w))\rangle\;\leq\;-\mu_{F}\|x-w\|^{2}\;=\;-\mu_{F}\|e\|^{2}.

(ii)

Bias:
Using the Bridge Lemma,
\|\nabla F(x)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)\|\leq\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}}{\lambda}
, so:

\langle x-w,\nabla F(w)-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{*}(x)\rangle\;\leq\;\|e\|\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}}{\lambda}.

(iii)

Tracking Errors:

\langle x-w,-D_{y}+\lambda D_{z}\rangle\;\leq\;\|e\|(\|D_{y}\|+\lambda\|D_{z}\|).

Combining these yields the differential inequality for the norm
\|e(t)\|
:

\frac{d}{dt}\|e(t)\|\;\leq\;-\mu_{F}\|e(t)\|\;+\;\underbrace{\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}}{\lambda}+\|D_{y}(t)\|+\lambda\|D_{z}(t)\|\right)}_{u(t)}.

Solving this ODE with
e(0)=0
:

\|e(t)\|\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{F}(t-s)}u(s)\,ds.

We now bound the integral of each component of
u(s)
.

1.
Bias Term:

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{F}(t-s)}\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}}{\lambda}\,ds\;=\;\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}}{\lambda}\frac{1-e^{-\mu_{F}t}}{\mu_{F}}\;\leq\;\frac{C_{\mathrm{br}}}{\mu_{F}\lambda}.

2.
Tracking Terms:
We apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the convolution integral. For any non-negative function
\phi(s)
:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{F}(t-s)}\phi(s)\,ds\right)^{2}\;

\displaystyle\leq\;\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{F}(t-s)}\,ds\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{F}(t-s)}\phi(s)^{2}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{1}{\mu_{F}}\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)^{2}\,ds.

Therefore,
\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{F}(t-s)}\phi(s)\,ds\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{F}}}\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)^{2}ds}
.

Using the Lyapunov dissipation bounds
\int_{0}^{\infty}\|D_{y}\|^{2}ds\leq\frac{C_{y}^{2}V_{0}}{c_{y}}
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2}\|D_{z}\|^{2}ds\leq\frac{C_{z}^{2}V_{0}}{c_{z}}
(where simplified margin notation is used), we get:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{F}(t-s)}\|D_{y}(s)\|\,ds\;

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{F}}}\left(\frac{C_{y}(\lambda)^{2}V_{0}}{c_{y}(\delta,\lambda)}\right)^{1/2}\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{V_{0}}{\mu_{F}}}\frac{C_{y}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{c_{y}(\delta,\lambda)}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\mu_{F}(t-s)}\lambda\|D_{z}(s)\|\,ds\;

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{F}}}\left(\lambda^{2}\frac{C_{z}^{2}V_{0}}{c_{z}(\eta,\lambda)}\right)^{1/2}\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{V_{0}}{\mu_{F}}}\frac{C_{z}}{\sqrt{\beta(\eta-\eta_{0})}}.

Note that for the
z
-term, substituting
c_{z}=\beta\lambda^{2}(\eta-\eta_{0})
cancels the
\lambda
factor, resulting in the expression in the theorem. Summing these bounds completes the proof.
∎

Appendix J
Euler discretization analogue

This appendix records a deterministic Euler analogue of the continuous-time
Lyapunov normal form. The purpose is not to give a complete discrete-time
complexity theorem for every possible discretization, but to make explicit how
the continuous-time S1–S3 structure transfers to a one-step Lyapunov analysis.

Proposition J.1
.

Let
u:=(x,y,z)
and let
G(u):=(\dot{x},\dot{y},\dot{z})
denote the right-hand
side of a coupled flow. Let
\mathcal{R}
be a region containing the relevant
Euler segments

\{u_{k}+shG(u_{k}):\;s\in[0,1],\ k\geq 0\}.

Assume that
\nabla W
is
L_{W}
-Lipschitz on
\mathcal{R}
.

Suppose that, on
\mathcal{R}
, the continuous-time Lyapunov normal form satisfies

\dot{W}(u)\leq-c_{1}\|\nabla\Phi(x)\|^{2}-c_{2}\|r_{y}\|^{2}-c_{3}\|r_{z}\|^{2},

(78)

for some
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
. Suppose further that the vector field is controlled by
the same quantities: there exist constants
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3}\geq 0
such that

\|G(u)\|^{2}\leq M_{1}\|\nabla\Phi(x)\|^{2}+M_{2}\|r_{y}\|^{2}+M_{3}\|r_{z}\|^{2}\qquad(u\in\mathcal{R}).

(79)

Consider the explicit Euler discretization

u_{k+1}=u_{k}+hG(u_{k}),

or equivalently

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+h\dot{x}_{k},\qquad y_{k+1}=y_{k}+h\dot{y}_{k},\qquad z_{k+1}=z_{k}+h\dot{z}_{k}.

If

0<h\leq\min_{i:\,M_{i}>0}\frac{c_{i}}{L_{W}M_{i}},

(80)

where
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
and
(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})
are matched componentwise, then

W(u_{k+1})-W(u_{k})\leq-\frac{hc_{1}}{2}\|\nabla\Phi(x_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{hc_{2}}{2}\|r_{y,k}\|^{2}-\frac{hc_{3}}{2}\|r_{z,k}\|^{2}.

(81)

Consequently, if
W_{\inf}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{R}}W(u)>-\infty
, then

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\|\nabla\Phi(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{2(W(u_{0})-W_{\inf})}{c_{1}hN},

(82)

and analogous averaged bounds hold for the residual terms.

Proof.

By the
L_{W}
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla W
on
\mathcal{R}
, the standard
one-step descent estimate gives

W(u_{k+1})-W(u_{k})\leq\langle\nabla W(u_{k}),u_{k+1}-u_{k}\rangle+\frac{L_{W}}{2}\|u_{k+1}-u_{k}\|^{2}.

Using
u_{k+1}=u_{k}+hG(u_{k})
, we obtain

W(u_{k+1})-W(u_{k})\leq h\langle\nabla W(u_{k}),G(u_{k})\rangle+\frac{L_{W}}{2}h^{2}\|G(u_{k})\|^{2}.

The first-order term is exactly the continuous-time derivative of
W
along the
flow:

\langle\nabla W(u_{k}),G(u_{k})\rangle=\dot{W}(u_{k}).

Therefore, by (
78
) and
(
79
),

\displaystyle W(u_{k+1})-W(u_{k})\leq

\displaystyle-hc_{1}\|\nabla\Phi(x_{k})\|^{2}-hc_{2}\|r_{y,k}\|^{2}-hc_{3}\|r_{z,k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{W}}{2}h^{2}\Bigl(M_{1}\|\nabla\Phi(x_{k})\|^{2}+M_{2}\|r_{y,k}\|^{2}+M_{3}\|r_{z,k}\|^{2}\Bigr).

Equivalently,

\displaystyle W(u_{k+1})-W(u_{k})\leq

\displaystyle-h\Bigl(c_{1}-\frac{L_{W}hM_{1}}{2}\Bigr)\|\nabla\Phi(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle-h\Bigl(c_{2}-\frac{L_{W}hM_{2}}{2}\Bigr)\|r_{y,k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-h\Bigl(c_{3}-\frac{L_{W}hM_{3}}{2}\Bigr)\|r_{z,k}\|^{2}.

The step-size condition (
80
) implies

c_{i}-\frac{L_{W}hM_{i}}{2}\geq\frac{c_{i}}{2},\qquad i=1,2,3,

with the convention that no restriction is needed when
M_{i}=0
. This proves
(
81
).

Summing (
81
) over
k=0,\ldots,N-1
gives

\frac{hc_{1}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\|\nabla\Phi(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq W(u_{0})-W(u_{N})\leq W(u_{0})-W_{\inf}.

Dividing by
hc_{1}N/2
yields (
82
). The averaged
residual bounds follow identically by retaining the corresponding residual
terms in (
81
).
∎

Remark J.2
.

Proposition
J.1
separates the discretization issue from
the geometry issue. The Euler step only adds the Taylor remainder

O(h^{2}\|G(u_{k})\|^{2})
, while the first-order term is the same Lyapunov
dissipation obtained in continuous time. Consequently, the geometry-dependent
part of the argument is still the deviation-to-residual interface:

\|D_{y}\|\leq C_{y}\|r_{y}\|,\qquad\|D_{z}\|\leq C_{z}\|r_{z}\|.

Under strong convexity, these constants come from Lemma
3.1
.
Under weaker geometry, they are replaced by the EB/sensitivity constants in
Section
4.3
and Appendix
E
. Once this interface
is available, both the continuous-time absorption thresholds and the Euler
one-step step-size restriction follow from the same normal-form quantities

(c_{i},M_{i})
. Thus the unified template identifies which parts of a discrete-time
analysis are structural and reusable, and which parts change as the inner
geometry weakens.

J.1
Forward-Euler hypercleaning diagnostics

Setup for Figure
1
.

Figure
1
reports forward-Euler diagnostics for the
lifted-penalty dynamics on Wine and Digits. We use a standard hypercleaning
setup with one outer variable
w_{i}
per training example and sample weight

s_{i}(w_{i})=(1+\exp(-w_{i}))^{-1}
. The inner model is a multiclass logistic
classifier with parameter
\theta
. With
30\%
uniformly corrupted training
labels, the lower-level and validation objectives are

g(w,\theta)=\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{tr}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{tr}}}s_{i}(w_{i})\ell(\theta;x_{i},\widetilde{y}_{i})+\frac{\rho_{\rm reg}}{2}\|\theta\|^{2},\qquad f(\theta)=\frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{val}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{val}}}\ell(\theta;x_{j}^{\mathrm{val}},y_{j}^{\mathrm{val}}).

We use the full Wine dataset and a subset of
80
Digits examples, both with a

60/40
train/validation split.

Euler dynamics and thresholds.

We instantiate the lifted penalty

E_{\lambda}(w,\theta,\zeta)=f(\theta)+\lambda\bigl(g(w,\theta)-g(w,\zeta)\bigr),

where
\theta
tracks the penalized inner minimizer and
\zeta
tracks the
lower-level minimizer associated with
g^{*}(w)
. We discretize the corresponding
flow by forward Euler and scan
(\delta,\eta)
on logarithmic grids at fixed

\lambda=10
. For the symmetric Lyapunov choice
\alpha=\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}
, the
thresholds reduce to

\delta_{0}=\frac{1}{2}C^{2},\qquad\eta_{0}=2C^{2}.

Since
f
does not depend directly on
w
, we use the empirical conversion scale

\widehat{C}=\frac{\widehat{L}_{w\theta}}{\widehat{\mu}_{g}},

where
\widehat{L}_{w\theta}
is estimated by finite-difference power iteration
and
\widehat{\mu}_{g}
is the empirical curvature of the regularized lower-level
objective. Thus

\widehat{\delta}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{C}^{2},\qquad\widehat{\eta}_{0}=2\widehat{C}^{2}.

Metric and interpretation.

For each pair
(\delta,\eta)
, we report

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left\|\nabla_{w}E_{\lambda}(w_{k},\theta_{k},\zeta_{k})\right\|^{2},

displayed on a base-
10
logarithmic scale. This is the forward-Euler analogue
of the outer stationarity quantity controlled by the continuous-time Lyapunov
normal form. In both datasets and in both geometry regimes, the region

\delta\geq\widehat{\delta}_{0},\eta\geq\widehat{\eta}_{0}
gives the smallest average
stationarity. Moreover, reducing
\rho_{\rm reg}
from
0.05
to
0.02
weakens
the empirical inner geometry by roughly a factor of
2.5
. Since the theory
predicts

\widehat{\delta}_{0},\widehat{\eta}_{0}\propto\widehat{C}^{2}=\left(\frac{\widehat{L}_{w\theta}}{\widehat{\mu}_{g}}\right)^{2},

the thresholds should increase by about
2.5^{2}=6.25
. The observed
deterioration factors are approximately
5.8
–
6.0
across Wine and Digits,
which is consistent with the predicted
C^{2}
scaling up to estimation noise.
Thus the experiment supports the modular S1–S3 interpretation: weakening the
inner geometry changes the deviation-to-residual conversion constant and shifts
the threshold scale, while the Lyapunov construction and absorption rule remain
unchanged. Forward Euler adds a one-step discretization remainder and hence
requires a stable absolute step size, but the relative time-scale boundary
predicted by the continuous-time normal form remains visible in the discretized
dynamics.

Appendix K
min-min-max

Why include min–min–max in the unified framework.

Although the main text focuses on the bilevel case, the Lyapunov/time-scale template in
Section
3
applies verbatim to other hierarchical games as well.
The min–min–max problem (
83
) is a clean illustrative instance:
the inner problems in
y
and
z
decouple into two smooth envelopes
f^{*}(x)
and
g^{*}(x)
,
so the outer objective is the difference
\mathcal{L}(x)=f^{*}(x)-g^{*}(x)
.
Accordingly, the proof follows the same three steps as before:

[S1]
smooth envelope gradients,

[S2]
deviation-to-residual conversion for
(D_{y},D_{z})
,
and
[S3]
absorption of cross terms via sufficiently large time-scale parameters
(\delta,\eta)
.

Assumption K.1
.

Suppose that:

1.

There exist constants
\mu_{f},\mu_{g}>0
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
,
the mappings
y\mapsto f(x,y)
and
z\mapsto g(x,z)
are
\mu_{f}
– and
\mu_{g}
–strongly convex,
respectively.

2.

There exist constants
L_{fx},L_{gx}\geq 0
such that for all
x
and all
y,y^{\prime},z,z^{\prime}
,

\|\nabla_{x}f(x,y)-\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{\prime})\|\leq L_{fx}\|y-y^{\prime}\|,\qquad\|\nabla_{x}g(x,z)-\nabla_{x}g(x,z^{\prime})\|\leq L_{gx}\|z-z^{\prime}\|.

3.

Let
f^{*}(x):=\min_{y}f(x,y)
,
g^{*}(x):=\min_{z}g(x,z)
, and define

\mathcal{L}(x):=f^{*}(x)-g^{*}(x).

Assume
\mathcal{L}_{\inf}:=\inf_{x}\mathcal{L}(x)>-\infty
.

Problem Setting.

We consider

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}}\ \min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}}\ \max_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{z}}}E(x,y,z),\qquad E(x,y,z):=f(x,y)-g(x,z).

(83)

For each fixed
x
,

\min_{y}\max_{z}E(x,y,z)=\min_{y}\bigl(f(x,y)-\min_{z}g(x,z)\bigr)=f^{*}(x)-g^{*}(x)=\mathcal{L}(x).

We study the gradient flow associated with (
83
):

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{x}E\bigl(x(t),y(t),z(t)\bigr),

(84)

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=-\delta\,\nabla_{y}E\bigl(x(t),y(t),z(t)\bigr),

\displaystyle\dot{z}(t)

\displaystyle=\eta\,\nabla_{z}E\bigl(x(t),y(t),z(t)\bigr),

with
\delta>0
and
\eta>0
.
Since
\nabla_{y}E=\nabla_{y}f
and
\nabla_{z}E=-\nabla_{z}g
, (
84
) becomes

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{x}f(x,y)+\nabla_{x}g(x,z),

(85)

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=-\delta\,\nabla_{y}f(x,y),

\displaystyle\dot{z}

\displaystyle=-\eta\,\nabla_{z}g(x,z).

Define the nonnegative gaps

H_{f}(x,y):=f(x,y)-f^{*}(x)\ (\geq 0),\qquad H_{g}(x,z):=g(x,z)-g^{*}(x)\ (\geq 0),

and for
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
define

W(x,y,z):=\mathcal{L}(x)+\alpha\,H_{f}(x,y)+\beta\,H_{g}(x,z).

(86)

Theorem K.2
.

Assume Assumption
K.1
and fix
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
.
Then there exist constants
c_{1}>0
and thresholds
\delta_{0}>0
,
\eta_{0}>0

such that for any
\delta\geq\delta_{0}
and
\eta\geq\eta_{0}
, along any solution

(x(t),y(t),z(t))
of (
85
) we have for a.e.
t\geq 0
,

\frac{d}{dt}W(x(t),y(t),z(t))\ \leq\ -\,c_{1}\,\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(x(t))\|^{2}.

(87)

Consequently, for every
T>0
, if
t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]
, then

\mathbb{E}_{t}\bigl[\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(x(t))\|^{2}\bigr]\ \leq\ \frac{W(x(0),y(0),z(0))-\mathcal{L}_{\inf}}{c_{1}\,T}.

(88)

Proof.

[S1: Smooth envelopes.]

By (A1), for each
x
the minimizers

y^{*}(x)\in\arg\min_{y}f(x,y),\qquad z^{*}(x)\in\arg\min_{z}g(x,z)

are unique and satisfy

\nabla_{y}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)=\mathbf{0},\qquad\nabla_{z}g\bigl(x,z^{*}(x)\bigr)=\mathbf{0}.

Define

\displaystyle f^{*}(x)

\displaystyle:=\min_{y}f(x,y)\;=\;f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr),

\displaystyle g^{*}(x)

\displaystyle:=\min_{z}g(x,z)\;=\;g\bigl(x,z^{*}(x)\bigr),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x)

\displaystyle:=f^{*}(x)-g^{*}(x).

We compute

\nabla f^{*}(x)=\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)+\bigl(Dy^{*}(x)\bigr)^{\top}\nabla_{y}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)=\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr),

and similarly,

\nabla g^{*}(x)=\nabla_{x}g\bigl(x,z^{*}(x)\bigr)+\bigl(Dz^{*}(x)\bigr)^{\top}\nabla_{z}g\bigl(x,z^{*}(x)\bigr)=\nabla_{x}g\bigl(x,z^{*}(x)\bigr).

Therefore,

\nabla\mathcal{L}(x)=\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)-\nabla_{x}g\bigl(x,z^{*}(x)\bigr).

(89)

Set

A:=\nabla\mathcal{L}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}},\qquad r_{y}:=\nabla_{y}f(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}},\qquad r_{z}:=\nabla_{z}g(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}},

and define the
x
-gradient deviations
(mismatches)

D_{y}:=\nabla_{x}f(x,y)-\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr),\qquad D_{z}:=\nabla_{x}g(x,z)-\nabla_{x}g\bigl(x,z^{*}(x)\bigr).

[S2: Deviation-to-residual conversion.]

Fix
x
. Since
y\mapsto f(x,y)
is
\mu_{f}
–strongly convex, for all
y
,

\big\langle\nabla_{y}f(x,y)-\nabla_{y}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr),\,y-y^{*}(x)\big\rangle\ \geq\ \mu_{f}\|y-y^{*}(x)\|^{2}.

Using
\nabla_{y}f(x,y^{*}(x))=\mathbf{0}
, we obtain

\mu_{f}\|y-y^{*}(x)\|^{2}\leq\langle r_{y},\,y-y^{*}(x)\rangle\leq\|r_{y}\|\,\|y-y^{*}(x)\|,

hence

\|y-y^{*}(x)\|\ \leq\ \frac{1}{\mu_{f}}\,\|r_{y}\|.

(90)

Similarly, since
z\mapsto g(x,z)
is
\mu_{g}
–strongly convex,

\|z-z^{*}(x)\|\ \leq\ \frac{1}{\mu_{g}}\,\|r_{z}\|.

(91)

By Assumption
K.1
,

\|D_{y}\|=\bigl\|\nabla_{x}f(x,y)-\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{*}(x))\bigr\|\leq L_{fx}\|y-y^{*}(x)\|\leq\frac{L_{fx}}{\mu_{f}}\|r_{y}\|=:C_{y}\|r_{y}\|,

and

\|D_{z}\|=\bigl\|\nabla_{x}g(x,z)-\nabla_{x}g(x,z^{*}(x))\bigr\|\leq L_{gx}\|z-z^{*}(x)\|\leq\frac{L_{gx}}{\mu_{g}}\|r_{z}\|=:C_{z}\|r_{z}\|.

Thus,

\|D_{y}\|\leq C_{y}\|r_{y}\|,\qquad\|D_{z}\|\leq C_{z}\|r_{z}\|.

(92)

[S3: Lyapunov derivative and absorption via time-scale separation.]

Recall
E(x,y,z)=f(x,y)-g(x,z)
. Then

\nabla_{x}E=\nabla_{x}f(x,y)-\nabla_{x}g(x,z),\qquad\nabla_{y}E=\nabla_{y}f(x,y)=r_{y},\qquad\nabla_{z}E=-\nabla_{z}g(x,z)=-r_{z}.

Hence the scaled gradient flow (
85
) can be written as

\dot{y}=-\delta r_{y},\qquad\dot{z}=-\eta r_{z}.

Moreover, by (
89
) and the definitions of
D_{y},D_{z}
,

\nabla_{x}f(x,y)=\nabla_{x}f\bigl(x,y^{*}(x)\bigr)+D_{y},\qquad\nabla_{x}g(x,z)=\nabla_{x}g\bigl(x,z^{*}(x)\bigr)+D_{z},

so

\dot{x}=-\nabla_{x}f(x,y)+\nabla_{x}g(x,z)=-(\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{*}(x))+D_{y})+(\nabla_{x}g(x,z^{*}(x))+D_{z})=-A-D_{y}+D_{z}.

Thus

\dot{x}=-A-D_{y}+D_{z}.

(93)

Since
\mathcal{L}
depends only on
x
,

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{L}}(x(t))

\displaystyle=\big\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x),\,\dot{x}\big\rangle=\langle A,-A-D_{y}+D_{z}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\|A\|^{2}-\langle A,D_{y}\rangle+\langle A,D_{z}\rangle.

(94)

Define the nonnegative gaps (unified notation)

H_{y}(x,y):=f(x,y)-f^{*}(x),\qquad H_{z}(x,z):=g(x,z)-g^{*}(x).

Using
\nabla f^{*}(x)=\nabla_{x}f(x,y^{*}(x))
and
\nabla g^{*}(x)=\nabla_{x}g(x,z^{*}(x))
, we have

\nabla_{x}H_{y}(x,y)=D_{y},\quad\nabla_{y}H_{y}(x,y)=r_{y},\qquad\nabla_{x}H_{z}(x,z)=D_{z},\quad\nabla_{z}H_{z}(x,z)=r_{z}.

Therefore, along the flow,

\displaystyle\dot{H}_{y}

\displaystyle=\langle D_{y},\dot{x}\rangle+\langle r_{y},\dot{y}\rangle=\langle D_{y},-A-D_{y}+D_{z}\rangle-\delta\|r_{y}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle D_{y},A\rangle-\|D_{y}\|^{2}+\langle D_{y},D_{z}\rangle-\delta\|r_{y}\|^{2},

(95)

and

\displaystyle\dot{H}_{z}

\displaystyle=\langle D_{z},\dot{x}\rangle+\langle r_{z},\dot{z}\rangle=\langle D_{z},-A-D_{y}+D_{z}\rangle-\eta\|r_{z}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle D_{z},A\rangle-\langle D_{z},D_{y}\rangle+\|D_{z}\|^{2}-\eta\|r_{z}\|^{2}.

(96)

Recall
W=\mathcal{L}+\alpha H_{y}+\beta H_{z}
. Combining (
94
), (
95
), and (
96
),

\displaystyle\dot{W}

\displaystyle=-\|A\|^{2}-(1+\alpha)\langle A,D_{y}\rangle+(1-\beta)\langle A,D_{z}\rangle-\alpha\|D_{y}\|^{2}+(\alpha-\beta)\langle D_{y},D_{z}\rangle+\beta\|D_{z}\|^{2}-\alpha\delta\|r_{y}\|^{2}-\beta\eta\|r_{z}\|^{2}.

(97)

For an upper bound, we may drop the nonpositive term
-\alpha\|D_{y}\|^{2}\leq 0
.

Fix
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
. By Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequality,

-(1+\alpha)\langle A,D_{y}\rangle\leq(1+\alpha)\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}\|A\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{1}}\|D_{y}\|^{2}\Bigr),

(1-\beta)\langle A,D_{z}\rangle\leq(1-\beta)\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{2}\|A\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{2}}\|D_{z}\|^{2}\Bigr),

(\alpha-\beta)\langle D_{y},D_{z}\rangle\leq\frac{|\alpha-\beta|}{2}\bigl(\|D_{y}\|^{2}+\|D_{z}\|^{2}\bigr).

Substituting into (
97
) yields

\dot{W}\leq-\,c_{1}\|A\|^{2}+K_{y}\|D_{y}\|^{2}+K_{z}\|D_{z}\|^{2}-\alpha\delta\|r_{y}\|^{2}-\beta\eta\|r_{z}\|^{2},

(98)

where

c_{1}:=1-\frac{(1+\alpha)\varepsilon_{1}}{2}-\frac{(1-\beta)\varepsilon_{2}}{2},\qquad K_{y}:=\frac{1+\alpha}{2\varepsilon_{1}}+\frac{|\alpha-\beta|}{2},\qquad K_{z}:=\frac{1-\beta}{2\varepsilon_{2}}+\frac{|\alpha-\beta|}{2}+\beta.

Choose
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
sufficiently small so that
c_{1}>0
.

Using (
92
) in (
98
),

K_{y}\|D_{y}\|^{2}-\alpha\delta\|r_{y}\|^{2}\leq(K_{y}C_{y}^{2}-\alpha\delta)\|r_{y}\|^{2},\qquad K_{z}\|D_{z}\|^{2}-\beta\eta\|r_{z}\|^{2}\leq(K_{z}C_{z}^{2}-\beta\eta)\|r_{z}\|^{2}.

Hence

\dot{W}\leq-c_{1}\|A\|^{2}+(K_{y}C_{y}^{2}-\alpha\delta)\|r_{y}\|^{2}+(K_{z}C_{z}^{2}-\beta\eta)\|r_{z}\|^{2}.

Define thresholds

\delta_{0}:=\frac{K_{y}C_{y}^{2}}{\alpha},\qquad\eta_{0}:=\frac{K_{z}C_{z}^{2}}{\beta}.

If
\delta\geq\delta_{0}
and
\eta\geq\eta_{0}
, then the last two coefficients are nonpositive, and we obtain

\dot{W}\ \leq\ -c_{1}\|A\|^{2}\ =\ -c_{1}\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(x)\|^{2},

(99)

as desired.

Since
H_{y}\geq 0
and
H_{z}\geq 0
, we have
W(x,y,z)\geq\mathcal{L}(x)\geq\mathcal{L}_{\inf}
.
Integrating (
99
) over
[0,T]
yields

c_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(x(t))\|^{2}\,dt\leq W(x(0),y(0),z(0))-W(x(T),y(T),z(T))\leq W(x(0),y(0),z(0))-\mathcal{L}_{\inf}.

If
t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,T]
, dividing by
T
gives (
88
).
∎
