Sprout Symmetric Functions Part 1
Tewodros Amdeberhan John Shareshian Richard P. Stanley

Abstract
. A sprout sequence is a sequence ℜ = ( R 0 = 1 , R 1 , R 2 , … ) \mathfrak{R}=(R_{0}=1,R_{1},R_{2},\dots) of symmetric functions in the variables 𝒙 = ( x 1 , x 2 , … ) \boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots) over a field K K generated from a power series F ⁡ ( t ) = 1 + a 1 ​ t + a 2 ​ t 2 + ⋯ F(t)=1+a_{1}t+a_{2}t^{2}+\cdots by the rule ∑ n ≥ 0 R n ​ t n = ∏ i ≥ 1 F ⁡ ( x i ​ t ) \sum_{n\geq 0}R_{n}t^{n}=\prod_{i\geq 1}F(x_{i}t) . The power series F ⁡ ( t ) F(t) is called the seed of ℜ \mathfrak{R} . This concept originated in the work of Littlewood and Richardson (though not with the name “sprout sequence”), and numerous examples of sprout sequences have appeared in the literature. They are related to chromatic Tutte polynomials of complete graphs and complete hypergraphs, binomial posets, upper homogeneous (upho) posets, topological genera, etc. We first develop the basic theory of sprout sequences and then look at the special case F ⁡ ( t ) = sec ⁡ ( t ) F(t)=\sec(\sqrt{t}) . We give five characterizations of sprout sequences and consider the expansion of sprout symmetric functions in terms of well-known symmetric function bases. The Schur positivity, elementary symmetric function positivity, and complete homogeneous symmetric function positivity of R n R_{n} for all n n are completely characterized using the Edrei-Thoma theorem from the theory of total positivity. The seed F ⁡ ( t ) = sec ⁡ ( t ) F(t)=\sec(\sqrt{t}) is especially interesting. The expansion of R n R_{n} in the power sum or monomial basis is related to alternating permutations. The Schur function expansion is related to standard Young skew tableaux. The expansion in terms of the complete symmetric functions has nonnegative integer coefficients, but we don’t know a combinatorial interpretation. Finally we give a formula for R n R_{n} as a sum of chromatic symmetric functions of interval orders.

2.
Basic properties

We begin with five characterizations of sprout sequences, including
the original definition. Condition (e) arose from a conversation with
Jesse Kim.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}=(R_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})=1,R_{1}(\boldsymbol{x}),R_{2}(\boldsymbol{x}),\dots)
be a
sequence of symmetric functions, where
R_{n}(\boldsymbol{x})
is homogeneous of
degree
n
. Let
\mathcal{A}(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}R_{n}t^{n}
. The following five
conditions are equivalent.

(a)

\mathfrak{R}
is a sprout sequence.

(b)

There exist elements
b_{1},b_{2},\dots\in K
such that

(2.1)

\log\mathcal{A}(t)=\sum b_{n}p_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n}.

Moreover,
\log F(t)=\sum b_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n}
.

(c)

There exist elements
a_{0}=1,a_{1},a_{2},\dots\in K
such
that for all
n\geq 1
,

(2.2)

R_{n}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}a_{\lambda_{1}}a_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots m_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}).

Moreover,
F(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}t^{n}
.

(d)

There exist elements
b_{0}=1,b_{1},b_{2},\dots\in K
such
that for all
n\geq 1
,

(2.3)

R_{n}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}z_{\lambda}^{-1}b_{\lambda_{1}}b_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots p_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}).

Moreover,
\log F(t)=\sum b_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n}
.

(e)

For all
f,g\in\hat{\Lambda}_{K}
(the set of all
symmetric formal power series over
K
in the indeterminates

\boldsymbol{x}
) we have

\mathcal{A}(t)*(fg)=(\mathcal{A}(t)*f)(\mathcal{A}(t)*g),

where

denotes internal (or Kronecker) product

[
18
, Exer. 7.78]
. Equivalently, the map
\hat{\Lambda}_{K}\to\hat{\Lambda}_{K}
defined by

is an algebra
homomorphism.

Proof.

(a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b). Assume (a). We have

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{A}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\log F(x_{i}t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}x_{i}^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}p_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n},

so (b) holds. The steps are reversible, so (b)
implies (a).

(a)
\Leftrightarrow
(c). Assume (a). Expanding
\prod_{i}F(x_{i}t)

gives (c). The steps are reversible, so (c) implies (a).

(b)
\Leftrightarrow
(d). Assume (b). Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}p_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{n}\exp\left(b_{n}p_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n}\right).

Expand each exponential and multiply to get (d). The steps are
reversible, so (d) implies (b).

(d)
\Leftrightarrow
(e). Assume (d). By bilinearity of
*
it
suffices to prove (e) for
f=p_{\lambda}
and
g=p_{\mu}
.
From the formula
[
18
, Exer. 7.78(d)]

p_{\lambda}*p_{\mu}=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}z_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}
it follows that

\displaystyle[p_{\nu}]\mathcal{A}(t)*p_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda,\nu}b_{\lambda_{1}}b_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots

\displaystyle{[p_{\nu}]}\mathcal{A}(t)*p_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mu,\nu}b_{\mu_{1}}b_{\mu_{2}}\cdots

\displaystyle{[p_{\nu}]}\mathcal{A}(t)*p_{\lambda}p_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda\cup\mu,\nu}b_{\lambda_{1}}b_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots b_{\mu_{1}}b_{\mu_{2}}\cdots.

It follows that (d)
\Rightarrow
(e). The steps are reversible,
so (e) implies (d).

is

, the number of

, i.e.,

.

(d)

Write

\frac{1}{2}\left(\binom{2n}{n}(E^{\prime}_{n})^{2}-E^{\prime}_{2n}\right),&i=j=n/2.\end{array}\right.

(b)

Use Theorem

(a).

(c)

Use Theorem
2.7
(c). To see that

6.
The Schur expansion of
(2n)!A_{n}

We now turn to the Schur expansion (or
s
-expansion) of
A_{n}
.
Given
\lambda\vdash n
, let
\mu=2\lambda^{\prime}
, i.e., take the conjugate
partition
\lambda^{\prime}
to
\lambda
and double each part. Let

\rho(\lambda)
be the skew partition (or skew shape) obtained from

\lambda
as follows: the row lengths of
\rho(\lambda)
are the parts
of
\mu
, and each row of
\rho(\lambda)
begins one square to the
left of the row above. In symbols,
\rho(\lambda)=\sigma/\tau
, where
\sigma_{i}=2\lambda^{\prime}+\ell(\lambda^{\prime})-i
and

\tau_{j}=\ell(\lambda^{\prime})-j
.

. Then

, and

, as

.

Theorem 6.2
.

For any
\lambda\vdash n
we have
\langle(2n)!A_{n},s_{\lambda}\rangle=f^{\rho(\lambda)}
, the number of standard Young tableaux of the skew

.

Proof.

By Theorem

7.
A connection with chromatic symmetric functions and interval
orders

To each perfect matching
M
on vertex set
[2n]
we associate a
partially ordered set
P_{M}
by declaring that
\{a,b\}<_{P_{M}}\{c,d\}

if
\max\{a,b\}<\min\{c,d\}
. The posets
P_{M}
are interval orders:
identify each edge
\{a,b\}
(
a<b
) with the closed interval
[a,b]

on the real line, and declare
\{a,b\}<\{c,d\}
if
[a,b]
lies
entirely to the left of
[c,d]
. We write
{\mathsf{I}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{c}}(P)
for the
incomparability graph of a poset
P
and
X_{G}
for the chromatic
symmetric function of a graph
G
(see
[
15
]
).

Theorem 7.1
.

Given a positive integer
n
, let

{{\mathcal{M}}(2n)}
be the set of all perfect matchings on the
set
[2n]
. Then

(2n)!A_{n}=\sum_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}(2n)}\omega X_{{\mathsf{I}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{c}}(P_{M})}.

Proof.

Given
M\in{\mathcal{M}}(2n)
, let
S_{M}
be the set of ordered lists

\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\ldots\sigma_{n}
of the elements of
P_{M}
.
Each
\sigma\in S_{M}
has descent set

{\mathbf{D}}{\mathbf{E}}{\mathbf{S}}(\sigma):=\{i\in[n-1]:\sigma_{i}\not\leq_{P_{M}}\sigma_{i+1}\}

and ascent set
{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{C}}(\sigma):=[n-1]\setminus{\mathbf{D}}{\mathbf{E}}{\mathbf{S}}(\sigma)
. Given
T\subseteq[n-1]
, we write
L_{T,n}
for the associated fundamental
quasisymmetric function and
M_{T,n}
for the associated monomial
quasisymmetric function (see for example
[
18
, Section 7.19]
for
definitions). By
[
3
, Corollary 2]
,

\displaystyle X_{{\mathsf{I}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{c}}(P_{M})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}L_{{\mathbf{D}}{\mathbf{E}}{\mathbf{S}}(\sigma),n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sum_{{\mathbf{D}}{\mathbf{E}}{\mathbf{S}}(\sigma)\subseteq T\subseteq[n-1]}M_{T,n},

the second equality following from
[
18
, Theorem 7.19.1]
.

By
[
18
, Exercise 7.94]
,

\omega X_{{\mathsf{I}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{c}}(P_{M})}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sum_{{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{C}}(\sigma)\subseteq T\subseteq[n-1]}M_{T,n}.

We define

\iota:\bigcup_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}(2n)}S_{M}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{2n}

as follows. If
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{n}\in S_{M}
with

\sigma_{i}=\{a_{i},b_{i}\}
and
a_{i}<b_{i}
for each
i\in[n]
, then

\iota(\sigma):=a_{1}b_{1}a_{2}b_{2}\ldots a_{n}b_{n}.

Writing
{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{C}}(w)
for the usual ascent set of
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{2n}
, we observe that

{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{C}}(\iota(\sigma))=\{1,3,5,\ldots,2n-1\}\cup\{2j:j\in{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{C}}(\sigma)\}.

Moreover,
\iota
is injective with image

\iota\left(\bigcup_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}(2n)}S_{M}\right)=\{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{2n}:\{1,3,5,\ldots,2n-1\}\subseteq{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{C}}(w)\}.

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
of
n
and

j\in[\ell-1]
, we set

v_{j}(\lambda):=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\lambda_{i}

and define

T(\lambda):=\{v_{j}(\lambda):j\in[\ell-1]\}.

We observe now that the coefficient of
m_{\lambda}
in the monomial
symmetric expansion of
\sum_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}(2n)}\omega X_{{\mathsf{I}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{c}}(P_{M})}
is
the same as the coefficient of
M_{T(\lambda),n}
in the monomial
quasisymmetric expansion. We have shown that this second coefficient
is the number of
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{2n}
satisfying

\{1,3,5,\ldots,2n-1\}\subseteq{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{C}}(w)\subseteq\{1,3,5,\ldots,2n-1\}\cup\{2v_{j}(\lambda):j\in[\ell-1]\},

which is equal to the number of
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{2n}
satisfying

\{1,3,5,\ldots,2n-1\}\subseteq{\mathbf{D}}{\mathbf{E}}{\mathbf{S}}(w)\subseteq\{1,3,5,\ldots,2n-1\}\cup\{2v_{j}(\lambda):j\in[\ell-1]\}.

The proof now follows from Theorem
4.2
.
∎

\sum_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}(2n)}\omega X_{{\mathsf{I}}{\mathsf{n}}{\mathsf{c}}(P_{M})}

is

is
the complete bipartite graph

. See
