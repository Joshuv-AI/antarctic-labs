Predictive value of neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio and fibrinogen degradation products for renal involvement in pediatric immunoglobulin A vasculitis
Authors: Guangbo Li, Jianying Liao, Zhuqin Zhan, Jinhua Zeng, Dequan Su
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmunoglobulin A vasculitis nephritis (IgAVN) is the most severe complication of IgA vasculitis (IgAV) in children, yet early predictors of renal involvement remain limited. The aim of this study is to analyse the risk factors for the development of IgAVN in children with IgAV, with a view to improving early diagnosis and treatment, reducing complications and enhancing patient prognosis.MethodsThis was a retrospective analysis. A total of 100 pediatric patients with IgAV hospitalized at Children’s Hospital of Fudan University Xiamen Branch (Xiamen Children’s Hospital) between January 2023 and June 2025 were included in the study. Patients were divided into two groups based on the presence or absence of IgAVN. Clinical characteristics and laboratory parameters were collected. Risk factors were identified using univariate and multivariate analyses, and a risk prediction model was established using logistic regression. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC).ResultsA total of 100 children with IgAV were ultimately included in this study, comprising 56 males and 44 females. They were divided into an IgAVN group (n=32) and a non-IgAVN group (n=68). Univariate analysis revealed that three laboratory parameters [C‑reactive protein (CRP), neutrophil‑to‑lymphocyte ratio (NLR), fibrinogen degradation products (FDP)] showed statistically significant differences between the IgAVN and non-IgAVN groups (P<0.05). We included the three laboratory parameters (CRP, NLR, and FDP) that showed statistically significant differences in the univariate analysis into the multivariate analysis and found that elevated NLR [odds ratio: odds ratio (OR) =1.446, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.080–1.934, P=0.01] and elevated FDP (OR =1.854, 95% CI: 1.133–3.034, P=0.01) were independent risk factors for the development of IgAVN in children with IgAV. ROC curve analysis results showed that the AUCs for NLR >5.23 and FDP >12.35 µg/mL were 0.634 and 0.830, respectively, while the AUC for the combined NLR and FDP model was 0.882. Compared with individual indicators, the combined model demonstrated superior predictive value for IgAVN, with a sensitivity of 86.95% and a specificity of 87.40%.ConclusionsElevated NLR and FDP are independent risk factors for the development of IgAVN in patients with IgAV. The combination of NLR >5.23 and FDP >12.35 µg/mL demonstrates good clinical predictive value for IgAVN and warrants attention from clinicians.Keywords: Fibrinogen degradation products (FDP), immunoglobulin A vasculitis (IgAV), immunoglobulin A vasculitis nephritis (IgAVN), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), risk factor

Highlight box

Key findings

• Elevated neutrophil‑to‑lymphocyte ratio (NLR) >5.23 and fibrinogen degradation products (FDP) >12.35 µg/mL are independent risk factors for immunoglobulin A vasculitis nephritis (IgAVN) in children.

• The combined model (NLR + FDP) yields an area under the curve of 0.882, a sensitivity of 86.95%, and a specificity of 87.40%, outperforming either marker alone.

What is known and what is new?

• IgAVN is a common and serious complication of IgAV, but reliable non‑invasive biomarkers for early detection are lacking.

• This study demonstrates that a simple combination of two widely available blood tests (NLR and FDP) measured at disease onset provides excellent predictive value for subsequent renal involvement.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Clinicians should routinely monitor NLR and FDP in children with IgAV at initial presentation. Those with values exceeding the thresholds should be considered for closer renal surveillance and early intervention.

Introduction

Immunoglobulin A vasculitis (IgAV), formerly known as Henoch-Schönlein purpura (HSP), is a systemic vasculitic disease commonly seen in children. It is characterized by Immunoglobulin A (IgA)-predominant immune complex deposition in multiple systems, leading to vasculitis (1,2). The annual incidence of IgAV in individuals under 18 years of age is 10–20 per 100,000 (3). Deposition of IgA immune complexes in the skin leads to prominent purpura and petechiae, primarily distributed on the lower extremities and buttocks; in a small number of patients, the trunk and upper extremities may also be affected. Renal involvement may result in mild proliferative or severe crescentic glomerulonephritis (4).

IgA vasculitis nephritis (IgAVN) is characterized by the presence of hematuria and/or proteinuria during the course of IgAV; in a small number of cases, it may present as nephritic syndrome or nephrotic syndrome. It represents the most severe complication of IgAV. In a small number of pediatric patients, the condition may become chronic and recurrent, potentially leading to end-stage renal disease in children, which has a significant impact on their quality of life and overall health (5). DygaK et al. found that 30–50% of children with IgAV develop renal symptoms during the course of the disease, with 91% of these children experiencing renal symptoms within 6 weeks of the onset of initial symptoms. Studies indicate that end-stage renal disease in children caused by IgAV accounts for 1–2% of all pediatric end-stage renal disease cases (6). A recent large international study by Rohner et al. (7) analysed 1,148 children with biopsy-proven IgAVN from 41 paediatric nephrology centres across 25 countries, providing important insights into the long-term outcomes of this condition. At a median follow-up of 3.7 years, 29% of patients had an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <90 mL/min/1.73 m2, 36% had persistent proteinuria, and 3% had progressed to chronic kidney disease stage 4–5. The same study identified older age, lower eGFR at onset, hypertension, and histological features of tubular atrophy and segmental sclerosis as predictors of poor outcome, and importantly demonstrated that delayed initiation of immunosuppressive treatment was associated with lower eGFR at last follow-up (7). These findings highlight the substantial long-term renal morbidity associated with IgAVN and underscore the urgent need for early predictive biomarkers that can identify high-risk children before irreversible renal damage occurs. IgAVN may present without obvious symptoms in the early stages, making it difficult to detect. Currently, renal biopsy is the gold standard for clinically determining the presence and extent of renal damage. Recent studies on IgAVN have increased, revealing that its development results from the combined action of multiple factors; however, there is still no consensus on the analysis of risk factors for IgAVN (7,8).

This study retrospectively analyzed the clinical data of pediatric patients with IgAV complicated by IgAVN who were hospitalized at Children’s Hospital of Fudan University Xiamen Branch (Xiamen Children’s Hospital). The aim was to investigate the predictive value of relevant laboratory parameters for IgAVN, thereby providing a basis for clinicians to promptly select appropriate treatment regimens and improve the quality of life for these children.

Methods

Study population

A total of 100 pediatric patients with IgAV who were hospitalized at Children’s Hospital of Fudan University Xiamen Branch (Xiamen Children’s Hospital) between January 2023 and June 2025 were included in the study. Among them, 56 were male and 44 were female, with a male-to-female ratio of 1.27:1. The patients were divided into an IgAVN group (n=32) and a non-IgAVN group (n=68). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Xiamen Children’s Hospital (approval No. [202604]). Informed consent has been obtained from the parents or legally authorized representatives of the patients in this study.

Inclusion criteria

Diagnostic criteria for IgAV

All pediatric patients in this study met the diagnostic criteria for IgAV recognized by European League Against Rheumatism/Paediatric Rheumatology International Trials Organization/Paediatric Rheumatology European Society (EULAR/PRINTO/PRES) (9).

Diagnostic criteria for IgAVN (9,10)

The diagnostic criteria endorsed by the Nephrology Group of the Pediatrics Branch of the Chinese Medical Association and EULAR/PRINTO/PRES were adopted: the development of proteinuria and/or hematuria within 6 months of the onset of IgAV. (I) Proteinuria: proteinuria is considered present if any of the following criteria are met: (i) three urine routine tests showing positive for protein within one week; (ii) 24-hour urine protein quantification >0.15 g; (iii) urinary microalbumin levels exceeding the upper limit of the normal reference range on three occasions within one week. (II) Hematuria: gross hematuria or microscopic hematuria ≥3 red blood cells per high-power field on three urine routine tests within one week.

Exclusion criteria

Exclude cutaneous purpura caused by other conditions, such as idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura;

Exclude cutaneous purpura caused by other conditions, such as idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura;

Patients with various primary, secondary, or genetic kidney diseases;

Patients with various primary, secondary, or genetic kidney diseases;

Patients with concomitant hematological disorders, malignant tumors, acute or chronic infections, autoimmune diseases, metabolic disorders, or coagulation disorders such as hemophilia.

Patients with concomitant hematological disorders, malignant tumors, acute or chronic infections, autoimmune diseases, metabolic disorders, or coagulation disorders such as hemophilia.

Study methods

According to the groupings, we recorded the children’s demographic information, length of hospitalization, season of onset, and the following blood parameters: white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NE), lymphocyte count (LY), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), hemoglobin (HGB), C-reactive protein (CRP), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), serum albumin (ALB), complement C3 (C3), complement C4 (C4), prothrombin time (PT), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) and D-dimer (D-D). All blood samples were obtained on the first day of admission, which corresponds to the early phase of the disease at initial clinical presentation (typically within 48 hours of the appearance of skin purpura), prior to the confirmation of renal involvement. Thus, the laboratory parameters measured reflect the baseline status at disease onset rather than a response to established nephritis.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 22.0 was used for statistical analysis. Quantitative data that followed a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and t-tests were used for comparisons between groups; data that did not follow a normal distribution were expressed as median (interquartile range), and Mann-Whitney U tests were used for comparisons between groups; categorical data are expressed as n (%) and compared between groups using the Chi-squared test; receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to calculate the area under the curve (AUC), P value, and cutoff values corresponding to the maximum Youden index, as well as sensitivity and specificity for each continuous variable. Multivariate analysis was performed using logistic regression; P<0.05 indicates statistical significance.

Results

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics

This study ultimately included 32 children with IgAV complicated by IgAVN for analysis, comprising 18 males and 14 females. Additionally, 68 children with IgAV without IgAVN who were hospitalized during the same period were included as the control group, comprising 38 males and 30 females. Comparison of demographic characteristics between the two groups revealed no statistically significant differences in age, gender, place of residence, extent of rash (whether extending beyond the buttocks), presence of joint swelling and pain, abdominal pain, or bloody stools (P>0.05).The length of hospital stay was significantly longer in the IgAVN group than in the non-IgAVN group (14.76±7.05 vs. 10.33±6.80 days) (Table 1).

Analysis of risk factors for IgAV combined with IgAVN

Multivariate logistic regression analysis

The three laboratory parameters (CRP, NLR, and FDP) that showed statistically significant differences in univariate comparisons were included in a binary logistic regression equation for multivariate analysis. The results of the multivariate analysis showed that elevated NLR [odds ratio (OR) =1.446, 95% CI: 1.080–1.934, P=0.01] and FDP (OR =1.854, 95% CI: 1.133–3.034, P=0.01) were independent risk factors for the development of IgAVN in children with IgAV (Table 4).

Predictive value of NLR combined with FDP for IgAVN

In this study, ROC curves were plotted to determine the AUC, P values, sensitivity, specificity, and cutoff values for NLR and FDP (based on maximizing the Youden index). The AUCs for NLR and FDP were 0.634 and 0.830, respectively, while the AUC for the combined NLR and FDP prediction was 0.882. Compared to single indicators, the combination of the two indicators demonstrated superior predictive value for IgAVN, with a sensitivity of 86.95% and a specificity of 87.40% (Table 5 and Figure 1).

Discussion

In our study, by analyzing the clinical symptoms and laboratory indicators of 100 children with IgAVN, we found that elevated NLR and FDP levels are independent risk factors for the progression of IgAV to IgAVN in children. A combined predictive model using NLR >5.23 and FDP >12.35 µg/mL demonstrated good clinical predictive performance for the development of IgAVN.

This study found that NLR is a risk factor for the progression of IgAV to IgAVN. As a potential indicator reflecting systemic inflammatory burden and subclinical inflammation, the predictive value of NLR lies in its integration of two distinct yet complementary immune pathways (11,12). Bhat et al. (13) pointed out that the NLR incorporates both the activating effect of neutrophils on nonspecific inflammation and the regulatory role of lymphocytes in inflammation; its ratio reflects a disruption in the body’s inflammatory balance. A study by Podraza (14) involving 173 children with IgAV further demonstrated that NLR is an independent risk factor for IgAVN, with an area under the ROC curve of 0.71, a sensitivity of 87%, and a specificity of 58%; moreover, children with high NLR exhibited more severe pathological damage associated with IgAVN.A retrospective analysis by Xi et al. (15) of 146 children with IgAV similarly confirmed that NLR is an independent risk factor for the development of IgAVN (OR =1.537, P=0.006). These results are consistent with the conclusions of this study: an elevated NLR has good predictive value for IgAVN, with a sensitivity of 55.60% and a specificity of 83.42% in this study, and should be given due attention by clinicians.

This study found that elevated FDP is a risk factor for the development of IgAVN in children with IgAV. FDP is a fragment produced by the breakdown of Fib and fibrin under the action of fibrinolysin; elevated levels indicate activation of the body’s fibrinolytic system. IgAV is essentially a systemic vasculitis caused by the deposition of IgA-mediated immune complexes on the walls of small blood vessels. The deposition of immune complexes on the vascular wall not only activates the complement system, inducing inflammatory cell infiltration and tissue damage, but also activates both the intrinsic and extrinsic coagulation pathways, leading to the formation of local microthrombi (15). As an organ rich in capillary networks, the kidney has abundant blood flow and powerful filtration capabilities, making it particularly susceptible to microcirculatory lesions. Microthrombi can cause occlusion and ischemia of the glomerular capillary loops, damaging endothelial cells and the basement membrane structure, ultimately leading to the development of proteinuria and hematuria (16). Therefore, elevated FDP levels can be regarded as a sensitive serological marker of hypercoagulable and hyperfibrinolytic states within the renal microvasculature of children with IgAV. In recent years, multiple studies have confirmed that abnormalities in the coagulation-fibrinolysis system play a significant role in IgAV-related kidney damage. Liu et al. (17) found in a study of 127 children with IgAV that plasma D-dimer and FDP levels in the IgAVN group were significantly positively correlated with 24-hour urinary protein excretion (r=0.475, P<0.01; r=0.529, P<0.01), suggesting that secondary hyperfibrinolysis may be involved in the renal injury process of this disease. Another study also indicated that levels of Fib, FDP, and D-dimer in the IgAVN group were significantly higher than those in the typical IgAV group, and patients with higher levels of these markers were more likely to develop early renal damage and had a poorer prognosis (18). Tian et al. (19) found through a randomized controlled trial involving 89 children with IgAV that the fibrinolytic system may be involved in the renal injury process in children with Henoch-Schönlein purpura nephritis (HSPN), and that low-molecular-weight heparin calcium can exert a renoprotective effect by improving renal blood supply through the inhibition of a hyperfibrinolytic state. The results of this study show that when FDP >12.35 µg/mL, its AUC for predicting IgAVN is 0.830, with a sensitivity of 82.83% and a specificity of 89.21%, indicating that FDP has good clinical value in predicting IgAVN.

This study combined the use of NLR, which reflects immune-inflammatory imbalance, with FDP, which reflects coagulation-fibrinolysis activation, for the early prediction of IgAVN. Previous studies have primarily focused on individual markers such as NLR or D-dimer; however, the pathogenesis of IgAVN involves multiple pathways, including immune complex deposition, inflammatory cascade reactions, and microvascular thrombosis, making it difficult for a single marker to comprehensively assess disease risk. This study found that the AUC for predicting IgAVN using NLR alone was 0.634, and that for FDP was 0.830; however, when combined, the AUC increased to 0.882, with a sensitivity of 86.95% and a specificity of 87.40%, significantly outperforming either single indicator. This suggests that NLR and FDP provide information through two independent yet complementary pathways—“immune-inflammatory imbalance” and “hyperfibrinolysis-microthrombosis”, respectively—and that their combination can more comprehensively capture the key events in the progression of IgAV to renal injury. This combined model holds promise as a simple, non-invasive tool for the early clinical identification of high-risk children with IgAV. A Chinese experience reported by Lee et al. further underscores the importance of early diagnosis and timely treatment in paediatric IgAVN (20). In their cohort of 42 Chinese children with biopsy-proven IgAVN, all patients received corticosteroid after kidney biopsy, with the majority (76%) receiving calcineurin inhibitors and 33% receiving cyclophosphamide pulses for severe cases. Over a median follow-up of 6.0 years, 86% of patients attained complete remission, and all patients had normal kidney function at the last follow-up (20). The authors attributed these favourable outcomes to timely kidney biopsy and individualised dual immunosuppression. These findings highlight that in Chinese paediatric populations, early identification and prompt, individualised treatment can lead to excellent long-term outcomes, reinforcing the clinical need for early predictive biomarkers to identify children at risk before irreversible renal damage develops.

In univariate analysis, CRP was identified as a risk factor for HSPN, with CRP levels in the IgAVN group being significantly higher than those in the non-IgAVN group. This is consistent with the findings of Yoshizaki et al. (21), who found that CRP is an independent risk factor for the development of IgAVN in IgAV. On the one hand, this may be due to infection-induced elevation of CRP; on the other hand, it may result from immune imbalance in the acute phase of IgAV in children, where hepatocytes produce and release large amounts of CRP into the bloodstream, promoting the secretion of inflammatory factors such as IL-6 and TNF-α, which further enhance the body’s immune response and lead to renal damage (22,23). In this study, although univariate analysis indicated an association between CRP and IgAVN, CRP was not identified as an independent risk factor after inclusion in multivariate analysis, likely due to the relatively small sample size.

This study also has certain limitations. First, this is a single-center, retrospective study with a relatively small sample size, which may introduce selection bias. Second, we primarily analyzed laboratory indicators at admission and were unable to dynamically observe changes in these indicators (especially FDP) during the course of the disease or their relationship with the timing and severity of renal injury. Third, the diagnosis and grading of IgAVN were primarily based on clinical manifestations and laboratory tests, and renal biopsy pathology data were lacking for all pediatric patients. Future studies should be multicenter, prospective, and large-scale, incorporating renal biopsy pathology data to further validate the predictive value of NLR and FDP and to explore their correlation with the extent of pathological damage. Additionally, comprehensive analysis combining these with other novel biomarkers (such as vascular endothelial growth factor and podocyte-associated proteins) may lead to the development of more accurate predictive models.

Conclusions

This study found that a combination of NLR >5.23 and FDP >12.35 µg/mL, when measured at initial presentation, has good predictive value for the development of IgAVN in children with IgAV. This finding warrants the attention of clinicians, as early identification of high-risk patients may facilitate timely monitoring and management.
