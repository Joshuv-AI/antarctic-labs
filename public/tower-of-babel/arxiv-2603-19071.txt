Abstract
. We establish upper bounds for the weak and strong error resulting from a perturbation of the noise driving the stochastic Burgers equation, where we assume the noise to be additive and of trace class and the initial value to be sufficiently regular. More specifically, replacing the covariance operator of the driving noise Q 1 ∈ ℒ 1 ​ ( L 2 ) Q_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(L^{2}) in the Burgers equation by a covariance operator Q 2 ∈ ℒ 1 ​ ( L 2 ) Q_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(L^{2}) results in a weak error of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ‖ ( − A ) − 1 − ​ ( Q 1 − Q 2 ) ‖ ℒ 1 ​ ( L 2 ) ) \mathcal{O}\big(\|(-A)^{-1^{-}}(Q_{1}-Q_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}(L^{2})}\big) and a strong error of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ‖ ( − A ) − 1 / 2 − ​ | Q 1 1 / 2 − Q 2 1 / 2 | ‖ ℒ 2 ​ ( L 2 ) ) \mathcal{O}\big(\big\|(-A)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}^{-}}\big|Q_{1}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}-Q_{2}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\big|\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2})}\big) . Here ∥ ⋅ ∥ ℒ 1 \|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}} is the trace class norm, ∥ ⋅ ∥ ℒ 2 \|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}} is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, and A A is the one-dimensional Dirichlet Laplacian that represents the leading term in the Burgers equation. In particular, our results provide upper bounds for the weak and strong error arising when approximating the trace class noise by finite-dimensional noise; the rates we obtain reflect the general philosophy that the weak convergence rate should be twice the strong rate.



1.
Introduction

We consider noise perturbations for the stochastic viscous Burgers equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and additive trace class noise. More specifically, for
Q_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(L^{2})
a positive operator we let
\bigl(W^{Q_{1}}(t)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
be the
L^{2}
-valued
Q_{1}
-Wiener process given by

(1)

W^{Q_{1}}(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}W^{(k)}(t)Q_{1}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}h_{k},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

where
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}
and
(W^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
sequence of independent standard real-valued Wiener processes independent of the initial value
X_{0}
. The Burgers equation we consider is formally given by

(2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}X_{1}(t,z)=\Delta X_{1}(t,z)+2X_{1}(t,z)\nabla(X_{1}(t,z))+\dot{W}^{Q_{1}}(t,z),\quad t>0,z\in(0,1),\\
&X_{1}(t,0)=X_{1}(t,1)=0,\quad t>0,\\
&X_{1}(0,z)=X_{0}(z),\quad z\in(0,1),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\nabla
and
\Delta
denote the first and second order derivatives with respect to
z
, and where
X_{0}
is assumed to be a given (possibly random) initial value. We refer to Section
2.2
for a rigorous interpretation of a solution to (
2
).

The goal of this article is to establish upper bounds for the strong and weak error resulting from replacing
Q_{1}
by a different positive operator
Q_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(L^{2})
. More specifically, let
X_{2}\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow L^{2}
be the solution to (
2
) with
W^{Q_{1}}
replaced by
W^{Q_{2}}
; we obtain the following bounds (see Corollaries
3.4
and
4.3
below):

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that there exist
\gamma_{0}>0
and
p>32
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\gamma_0\|X_0\|_{L^2}^2)\right]<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\|(-A)^{\frac{1}{2}}X_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]<\infty
. Let
K_{\max}\in(0,\infty)
and let
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(L^{2})
be positive and self-adjoint, such that
\max\{\tr(Q_1),\tr(Q_2)\}\leq K_{\max}
. Let
\varphi:L^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be twice Fréchet differentiable with continuous and bounded first and second order derivatives. Then, for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
C_{\epsilon,\gamma_{0},p,T}(X_{0},K_{\max},\varphi)\in(0,\infty)
such that

(3)

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi(X_{1}(T))\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi(X_{2}(T))\big]\big|\leq C_{\epsilon,\gamma_{0},p,T}(X_{0},K_{\max},\varphi)\|(-A)^{-(1-\epsilon)}(Q_{1}-Q_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}(L^{2})},

and for all
\epsilon>0
,
r\in[1,\frac{p}{4})
there exists
C_{\epsilon,\gamma_{0},p,r,T}(X_{0},K_{\max})\in(0,\infty)
such that

(4)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{1}(t)-X_{2}(t)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{2})}\leq C_{\epsilon,\gamma_{0},p,r,T}(X_{0},K_{\max})\big\|(-A)^{-(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\epsilon)}\big|Q_{1}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}-Q_{2}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\big|\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2})}.

Note that the weak bound is roughly the square of the strong bound. This aligns with the general principle in numerical analysis for stochastic differential equations that states that the weak convergence rate is twice the strong rate. We believe the bound in (
4
) to be essentially sharp (see Remark
4.6
).

The main motivation for our work lies in the numerical approximation of the Burgers equation. More specifically, both weak and strong convergence rates for the Galerkin approximation of (
2
) are known, see
[
1
,
9
]
. These Galerkin approximations lead to finite-dimensional systems driven by finite-dimensional noise, where the covariance operator of the noise is given by
P_{M}Q_{1}P_{M}
(where
P_{M}
is
M^{\textnormal{th}}
Galerkin projection, i.e., the projection onto the first
M
eigenvectors of the Laplacian). However, sampling from a
P_{M}Q_{1}P_{M}
-Brownian motion is not straightforward due to the infinite-dimensional nature of
Q_{1}
. This becomes easier if we can replace
Q_{1}
by a finite-dimensional operator, and Theorem
1.1
quantifies the error introduced in doing so – see also Corollaries
3.6
and
4.5
below.

The question of approximating the noise goes back at least as far as
[
2
]
, where in Section 5 it is proven that approximating the noise in a semilinear equation by a finite-dimensional noise results in a strongly convergent sequence of solutions. In
[
12
,
13
]
the authors establish that the solution to a semilinear equation depends continuously on both the drift and the diffusion coefficient. Neither
[
2
]
nor
[
12
,
13
]
provide quantitative results, the first result in that direction seems to be in
[
10
]
, where both weak and strong bounds are provided for noise approximation for hyperbolic equations with globally Lipschitz coefficients. As the equations considered in
[
10
]
do not involve an analytic operator in the leading term, the obtained rates do not involve (and benefit from) negative powers of
A
. Finally, a powerful abstract perturbation result for stochastic differential equations with monotone coefficients was obtained in
[
9
]
, indeed, this abstract result is the key ingredient for obtaining the strong error bound (
4
). The weak error bound (
3
) relies on regularity results for the Kolmogorov equation associated with the Burgers equation established in
[
1
]
. For practical purposes, we first show that the bounds (
3
) and (
53
) hold for the Galerkin approximations of the Burgers equation, with constants independent of the Galerkin projection. This allows us to pass to the limit and ultimately obtain (
3
) and (
53
).

2.2.
The setting for the 1D stochastic Burgers equation

The formal setting we use throughout this paper to describe the 1D stochastic Burgers equation with trace class noise is taken from
[
1
, Section 2.3]
. We repeat this setting here for the reader’s convenience.

We let
T\in(0,\infty)
denote the terminal time.

We let
A\colon W^{1,2}_{0}\cap W^{2,2}\subseteq L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}
denote the Dirichlet Laplace operator on
L^{2}
, i.e.,

(9)

Ax=-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(\pi k)^{2}\langle x,h_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}h_{k},\quad\forall x\in W^{2,2}\cap W^{1,2}_{0},

where
h_{k}=\sqrt{2}\sin(k\pi\cdot)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The eigenvectors
\big(h_{k}\big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
define a complete orthonormal system of
L^{2}
. Note that
A
generates an analytic

-semigroup
(e^{tA})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}
.

We let
B\colon W^{1,2}\times W^{1,2}\rightarrow L^{1}
denote the bilinear operator defined by

(10)

B[x_{1},x_{2}]=x_{1}\nabla x_{2}+x_{2}\nabla x_{1},\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in W^{1,2},

and we set
B(x)=B[x,x]
for
x\in W^{1,2}
. Note that an integration by parts yields the identity
\langle B(x),x\rangle_{L^{2}}=0
for all
x\in W^{1,2}
.

We fix a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]})
, which is assumed to be large enough to allow for the existence of a sequence of independent standard
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
-Brownian motions

. Let
(\tilde{h}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis for

(the choice of

W^{Q}(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}W^{(k)}(t)Q^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\tilde{h}_{k}

(note that the distribution of
W^{Q}
is independent of the choice of
(\tilde{h}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
).

It follows from
[
14
, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 3.1]
(see also
[
1
, Section 2.3]
) that for every
p\in[4,\infty)
, every positive self-adjoint
Q\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(L^{2})
, and every
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable

there exists a unique continuous (up to versions)
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
-adapted process

such that
\mathbb{P}(X^{Q}(t)\in W^{1,2}_{0})=1
for all

,

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X^{Q}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla X^{Q}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\,\right]<\infty,

and

(12)

X^{Q}(t)=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\big[AX^{Q}(s)+B(X^{Q}(s))\big]\,ds+W^{Q}(t),\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

We refer to the process
X^{Q}
as the
solution to the Burgers equation driven by a
Q
-Brownian motion (with initial value
X_{0}
)
.

As explained in the introduction, our proofs rely heavily on the spectral Galerkin approximations of (
12
), which we shall now introduce. For
M\in\mathbb{N}
, set

H_{M}=\operatorname{span}(\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{M}\})\subseteq W^{1,2}_{0}\cap W^{2,2}

Define the linear operator

(

) and

(13)

B_{M}[x_{1},x_{2}]=P_{M}B[P_{M}x_{1},P_{M}x_{2}],\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in L^{2}.

We set
B_{M}(x)=B_{M}[x,x]
for all

. Note that for

(14)

\displaystyle\left\langle y,B_{M}[x_{1},x_{2}]\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle y,B[x_{1},x_{2}]\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

It again follows from

Proposition 2.2

∎

As for the operator

2.4.
Estimates for stochastic convolutions

Let

From (

more explicit. Consider the inequality provided just above
[
1
, inequality (A.3)]
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(t\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|^{p}\right]+p\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big\|\nabla X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p-2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|P_{M}X_{0}\right]^{p}\big\|_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{4}p^{2}\tr(Q)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\big(\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+1\big)ds\right].

Since
\|P_{M}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}=1
, one has
\left\|P_{M}X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}
, and hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(t\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+\tfrac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)+\tfrac{1}{4}p^{2}\tr(Q)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]ds.

An application of Grönwall’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Lemma C.1.1]
) shows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(t\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+\tfrac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)\right)\left(1+e^{\frac{1}{4}p^{2}t\tr(Q)}\right).

Taking supremum over
t\in[0,T]
, we deduce

(79)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(t\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+\tfrac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)\right)\left(1+e^{\frac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\tfrac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)\right)\left(1+e^{\frac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)}\right)(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+1)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+e^{\frac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)}\right)^{2}(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+1).

Moreover, the following inequality is shown on
[
1
, p. 26]
:

(80)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(t\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+p\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big\|\nabla X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p-2}ds\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\Big]+\tfrac{19}{4}p^{2}\tr(Q)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+1\right)ds\right].

Note that
\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\leq R^{p}+\big\|X_{0}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}<\infty
. Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\Big]<\infty,

so that it can be subtracted from both sides of (
80
). Furthermore, using (
79
) for the term inside the integral in (
80
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(t\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+p\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big\|\nabla X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p-2}ds\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+\tfrac{19}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)\left(\left(1+e^{\frac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)}\right)^{2}(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+1)+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\tfrac{19}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)+\tfrac{19}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)\left(1+e^{\frac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)}\right)^{2}\right)(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+1).

Multiplying by a factor of
2
and taking supremum over
M\in\mathbb{N},R\in(0,\infty)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\sup_{M\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{R\in(0,\infty)}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(t\wedge\tau_{R})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+2p\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big\|\nabla X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{p-2}ds\right)\right]

(81)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(1+\tfrac{19}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)+\tfrac{19}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)\left(1+e^{\frac{1}{4}p^{2}T\tr(Q)}\right)^{2}\right)(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+1).

Letting
R\to\infty
, recalling that
\tau_{R}\to T
a.s. and applying monotone convergence theorem shows the desired inequality, with

\displaystyle F_{p,T}(x)=2\left(1+\tfrac{19}{4}p^{2}Tx+\tfrac{19}{4}p^{2}Tx\left(1+e^{\frac{1}{4}p^{2}Tx}\right)^{2}\right).

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

We take over from the last inequality given in the proof of
[
1
, Lemma 3.2]
, which reads

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T],z\in[0,1]}|X_{M}^{Q}(t)(z)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\left\|(-A)^{\alpha}X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|I_{M}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{\tilde{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{4\tilde{p}-2}\big\|\nabla X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\right)^{\frac{3}{4\tilde{p}}},

where
I_{M}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}P_{M}dW^{Q}(s)
and
\tilde{p}=\frac{3p}{4}
. Raising this to
p=\frac{4\tilde{p}}{3}
and taking expectations, one obtains

(82)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T],z\in[0,1]}|X_{M}^{Q}(t)(z)|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p}\bigg(C^{p}_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(-A)^{\alpha}X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+2^{p}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|I_{M}(t)\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}}\Big]+C^{p}_{\tilde{p}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{4\tilde{p}-2}\big\|\nabla X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\right]\bigg).

By Lemma
2.4
, one has

(83)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|I_{M}(t)\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}}\right]\leq C_{p,T}\tr(Q)^{p/2},

while by Lemma
2.6
(i), since
4\tilde{p}=3p\geq 8
, there exists an increasing
F_{p,T}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

(84)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\big\|X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{4\tilde{p}-2}\big\|\nabla X_{M}^{Q}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\right]\leq F_{p,T}(\tr(Q))(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{3p}\right]+1).

Plugging (
83
) and (
84
) in the inequality (
82
), and taking supremum over
M\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{M\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T],z\in[0,1]}|X_{M}^{Q}(t)(z)|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p}\Big(C^{p}_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(-A)^{\alpha}X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+2^{p}C_{p,T}\tr(Q)^{p/2}+C^{p}_{\tilde{p}}F_{p,T}(\tr(Q))(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{3p}\right]+1)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\alpha,T}\max\{1,\tr(Q)^{p/2},F_{p,T}(\tr(Q))\}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(-A)^{\alpha}X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{3p}\right]\right),

for some
C_{p,\alpha,T}\in(0,\infty)
. The function
(0,\infty)\ni x\mapsto\max\{1,x^{p/2},F_{p,T}(x)\}
is non-decreasing and only depends on
p
and
T
, finishing the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.8
.

Recall the mild formulation of
X_{M}^{Q}
. Using the triangle inequality in
C^{\gamma}([0,T],L^{2})
and inequalities (A.20), (A.21) and Lemma A.1 in
[
1
]
, one has

\displaystyle\sup_{M\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\|(-A)^{\lambda}X_{M}^{Q}\|^{p}_{C^{\gamma}([0,T],L^{2})}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(-A)^{\alpha}X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+C_{\gamma,\lambda,T}\sup_{M\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|X_{M}^{Q}\big\|^{2p}_{L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{4})}\right]+C_{\lambda,\gamma,p,T}\tr(Q)^{p/2}\bigg).

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+C_{\lambda,\gamma,p,T}\tr(Q)^{p/2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\alpha,\gamma,\lambda,T}\max\{1,F_{p,T}(\tr(Q)),\tr(Q)^{p/2}\}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|(-A)^{\alpha}X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{6p}\right]\right),

. The function

Proof of Lemma
2.9
.

The proof presented here is nearly identical to the one of
[
1
, Lemma 5.4]
, with differing parts highlighted in
red
. Note that the changes are justified by the more explicit bounds given in Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.8
.
Recall that
\|(-A)^{\alpha}x\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|(-A)^{\beta}x\|_{L^{2}}
for all
x\in D((-A)^{\beta})
and all
\alpha<\beta

(see (
18
)). Thus, without loss of generality, we can assume
\delta_{0}<\frac{1}{4}
.
Applying Hölder’s inequality, one has for all

M\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Psi_{\delta,\epsilon,q}(X_{M}^{Q}(t))\right]\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\big(\tfrac{p\epsilon}{p-2q}\|X_M^Q(t)\|_{L^2}^2 \big)\right]\right)^{1-\frac{2q}{p}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(1+\|(-A)^{\frac{1}{4}+\delta}X_{M}^{Q}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{q}\big)^{\frac{p}{2q}}\right]\right)^{\frac{2q}{p}}.

Applying the exponential moment bounds
(
31
)
from Lemma
2.6
(with
\beta=\frac{p\epsilon}{p-2q}
) and the inequality
(
34
)
from Lemma
2.8
(with
\lambda=\frac{1}{4}+\delta
,
\gamma\in(0,\delta_{0}-\delta)
,
\alpha=\frac{1}{4}+\delta_{0}
) one obtains that there exists
a non-decreasing
F_{p,T}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
(dependent on
p
and
T
) and
C_{\delta,\delta_{0},p,T}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\big(\tfrac{p\epsilon}{p-2q}\|X_M^Q(t)\|_{L^2}^2 \big)\right]\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}2e^{\frac{\gamma_{0}T\tr(Q)}{1+2\gamma_{0}\|Q\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}}}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\big(\gamma_0 \|X_0\|_{L^2}^2 \big)\right]\right)^{\frac{p\epsilon}{(p-2q)\gamma_{0}}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(1+\|(-A)^{\frac{1}{4}+\delta}X_{M}^{Q}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{q}\big)^{\frac{p}{2q}}\right]\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}C_{\delta,\delta_{0},p,T}F_{p,T}(\tr(Q))}\Big(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\|(-A)^{\frac{1}{4}+\delta_{0}}X_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\|X_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{3p}\right]\Big).

Note that
\epsilon<\gamma_{0}
, therefore one has

\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\big(\gamma_0 \|X_0\|_{L^2}^2 \big)\right]\right)^{\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma_{0}}}\leq 1+\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\big(\gamma_0 \|X_0\|_{L^2}^2 \big)\right].

Moreover, there exists
C_{\gamma_{0},p}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle 1+\mathbb{E}\left[\|(-A)^{\frac{1}{4}+\delta_{0}}X_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\|X_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{3p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\big(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\|(-A)^{\frac{1}{4}+\delta_{0}}X_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\big)\big(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\|X_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{3p}\right]\big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma_{0},p}\big(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\|(-A)^{\frac{1}{4}+\delta_{0}}X_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\right]\big)\big(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\big(\gamma_0 \|X_0\|_{L^2}^2 \big)\right]\big).

Combining the upper bounds then yields the inequality
(
36
)
and concludes the proof of Lemma
2.9
.
∎
