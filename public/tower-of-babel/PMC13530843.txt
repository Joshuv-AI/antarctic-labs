Poster 18. Coronal Plane Alignment After Pre-Pubertal ACL Reconstruction: Results From the PLUTO Study Group
Authors: Joshua T Bram, Meghan Whalen, Subrina Farah, Grace Saville, Dai Sugimoto, Daniel W Green, Theodore J Ganley, John Lawrence, Crystal Perkins, Michael Busch, Andrew Pennock, Henry Chambers, Eric Edmonds, Frank A Cordasco, Christian Anderson, Benton E Heyworth, Matthew Matava, Lyle J Micheli, Matthew D Milewski, Jeffrey J Nepple, Shital N Parikh, Paul Saluan, Kevin Shea, Eric Wall, Samuel C Willimon, Mininder S Kocher, Peter D Fabricant
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:Several ACL reconstruction (ACLR) techniques have been described for patients with open growth plates and significant growth remaining (i.e. >2 years). The most common methods include a physeal-sparing combined intra/extra-articular reconstruction with iliotibial band (ITB) autograft (i.e. modified MacIntosh) and a physeal-sparing all-epiphyseal reconstruction (i.e. Anderson). While these techniques are designed to avoid fixation across the physis, rare cases of clinically significant angular deformity or leg length discrepancy have been described after physeal-sparing ACLR. These cases, however, have largely been clinically evaluated in a retrospective fashion without formal post-operative hip-to-ankle alignment radiographs. Additionally, authors typically do not report on more subtle changes in alignment that may predispose to future injury. This study aimed to compare changes in lower extremity coronal plane alignment after modified MacIntosh ACLR and all-epiphyseal ACLR. We tested the null hypothesis that there would be no change from pre-operative to 1-year postoperative between groups in the difference in radiographic measures of coronal alignment between the injured and uninjured limbs.Methods:This was a multi-center prospective cohort study focused on two cohorts of pre-pubescent patients (skeletal age <12 for females and <14 for males) who underwent either primary 1) modified MacIntosh ACLR or 2) all-epiphyseal ACLR. All patients had a skeletal age assessment within 3 months of surgery and pre-operative standing hip-to-ankle alignment radiographs with documented knee extension to within 5° of full knee extension in order to avoid knee flexion affecting assessment of coronal plane alignment. Post-operatively, alignment radiographs were obtained at 1-year (9 to 18 months). Patients were excluded for: undergoing concomitant guided growth, multi-ligament knee injuries, congenital ACL deficiency, a neuromuscular or genetic condition, or a prior injury (e.g. tibial spine fracture, meniscus tear, collateral ligament injury, osteochondritis dissecans lesion, patellofemoral instability) to either knee. The mechanical axis deviation (zones), anatomic lateral distal femoral angle (aLDFA), mechanical medial proximal tibial angle (mMPTA), posterior tibial slope (PTS), and limb length difference (LLD) were obtained by individual institutions with previously described methodology given high inter-rater reliability. Measurements were performed on both the involved and uninvolved limbs to allow for calculation of the change from pre- to post-operative in the difference between extremities (difference of differences = D2), allowing for control of coronal plane changes that may otherwise occur due to natural growth. A D2 of >=5° (aLDFA, mMPTA, PTS), >=1cm (LLD), or >=1 zone (MAD) was considered clinically relevant. For each variable, median and interquartile range (IQR) was calculated after normality testing. Wilcoxon Mann-Whitney tests were used to compare the D2 between groups.Results:After exclusion criteria were applied, 112 patients were included in the final analyses (Table 1): 77 modified MacIntosh and 35 all-epiphyseal. The modified MacIntosh group had a younger median skeletal age (11.00 vs 12.00, p=0.0492), with no other demographic differences. In the all-epiphyseal group, 21 (60%) reconstructions utilized hamstrings autograft and 14 (40%) used quadriceps autograft. Three (9%) patients in this cohort underwent a concomitant lateral extra-articular augmentation procedure. Alignment films were obtained at a median 12.29 months (IQR 11.93-13.53) and 11.96 months (11.70-12.45, p=0.0227) for the modified MacIntosh and all-epiphyseal groups, respectively. No differences in D2 were observed for any radiographic parameter between cohorts (Table 2). Median D2 values were overall small, ranging from -2.0 PTS to 0.3 mMPTA across both cohorts. Pre- and post-operative parameters were similar between the injured and uninjured limbs as well (Tables 3-4). However, 31-40% of individuals experienced a >=1 zone D2 in mechanical axis, and up to 20% of patients had a D2 in aLDFA or mMPTA >=5°. Three individuals (3.9%) developed notable growth disturbances requiring further monitoring or intervention. One male patient developed a LLD with 1.6cm over-growth on the operative limb after modified MacIntosh ACLR at age 5 years 10 months, which was managed conservatively without further progression. Another two male patients developed valgus angular deformities after modified MacIntosh ACLR at ages 10 years 9 months and 13 years 7 months, respectively. Both patients underwent hemi-epiphysiodesis, the first at 13 months post-operatively and the second at 12 months.Conclusions:Among patients with >2 years of growth remaining, both modified MacIntosh and all-epiphyseal ACLR are safe options with few growth disturbances requiring further intervention based on this large, multi-center prospective evaluation. Despite this, up to one quarter of patients developed clinically relevant changes in coronal alignment compared to the uninvolved limb including valgus that could predispose to future injury. These may not be reliably quantified with clinical assessments alone. Therefore, young patients require routine radiographic lower extremity coronal plane length and angular deformity assessments both pre- and post-operatively.
