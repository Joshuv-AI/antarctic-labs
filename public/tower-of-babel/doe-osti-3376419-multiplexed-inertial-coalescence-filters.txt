Nature of Work
Describe the research (summary of Scope of Work and principal objectives of the CRADA):
The aim of this project is to support the development of a disruptive method for deploying liquids in liquid-gas
chemical processes to transform carbon dioxide capture from flue gas and ambient air streams. The proposed project
is based on the development of a novel filtration method called the Helix MICRA™ (Multiplexed Inertial
Coalescence Refining Apparatus) filters. Helix MICRA™ filters are a novel, patented filter that enable high-
efficiency, low-pressure drop capture of droplet streams. Liquid droplets have a large net-surface area per unit
volume and have correspondingly rapid mass transfer rates. By effectively capturing these droplets after
deployment, we enable high-rate carbon dioxide capture from air streams unlike any other technology. This project
aims at using Helix MICRA™ filters to create efficient and compact carbon dioxide capture systems that would
dramatically reduce system size and capital costs.
The technical objectives for this project include experimental and computational tasks. Argonne National
Laboratory has overseen the computational effort, while Helix Earth Technologies has led the design and
experimental testing of the proposed technology.
ANL-1060 (02/13/2023)

Argonne and Helix Earth Technologies will jointly select an atomizer for producing liquid sprays used in Helix
MICRA™ computational models, then develop a suite of models to predict filter performance, droplet-scale CO2
uptake, and overall system-level CO2 capture performance for a liquid-amine spray configuration. In parallel, a sub-
scale (<5"×5") capture system will be tested across a range of inlet CO2 concentrations for model assessment and
validation. Validated CFD simulation will then be used to explore further design structure and inform potential
design and scale-up of the technology.

Conclusions drawn from this CRADA; include any major accomplishments:
The list below reports the major accomplishment of the project:
 1) A computational framework capable of modeling amine spray injection, entrainment, and filtration has
 been developed for the open-source CFD platform OpenFOAM. The model is based on the Lagrangian-
 Eulerian spray modeling approach and fully coupled mass, momentum, energy, and species transport
 between the liquid and gas phases. The framework can represent the spray characteristics selected by
 Helix Earth Technologies for their test bench.

 2) A model for the patented Helix MICRA™ filter has been developed, implementing both solid and
 continuous porous media approaches to represent the micro-scale feature of the filter matrix. The model is
 fully coupled with both liquid and gas phases and has been validated against experimental data provided
 by the project partner.

 3) A completely automated parametrization routine of the filter geometry has been implemented and coupled
 with the multi-phase CFD model, defining a numerical tool to explore the performance of the filter –
 quantifies in terms of pressure drop, liquid filtration, and spray suction rates – as a function of its design.
 The model allowed us to explore configurations of the filter that were not included in the initial design,
 such as tapering and variable channel length.

 4) The results of the model have been imported by Helix Earth Technologies in their 3D printing filter
 manufacturing process and are currently under investigation in their real-world performance at conditions
 of interest for carbon capture systems.

Summary of technology transfer benefits to industry and, if applicable, path forward/anticipated next steps
towards commercialization:
The project benefitted the industry partner through the definition of an open-source higher-fidelity CFD simulation
approach, which is based on fully coupled Lagrangian-Eulerian framework, which will be available for transfer.
During the process, the tool enabled the exploration of further filter configurations, defining a path for further
performance optimization.
Helix Earth Technologies also was able to secure a Phase I SBIR ($275k) and Phase II SBIR ($1.25 million) from
the National Science Foundation during its time participating in CRI. CRI resources were directly used to help with
securing these grants. CRI resources helped with grant writing and editing, and budgeting help. Helix Earth also
raised two rounds of investor capital in its time at CRI, a Pre-Seed round of $1.3 million, and a Seed round of $6
million.

Helix Earth Technologies also built-up strong customer traction throughout our time in CRI. We built a major
commercial pipeline now exceeding $1 billion for our HVAC product, including name brand customers such as
CVS, Cinemark, and McDonald’s. Beyond topline pipeline growth, we now have a $137 million total addressable
market represented by signed letters of intent with major brands and regional distributors, with additional letters of
intent in progress that we expect to convert in the near term. We are seeing growing inbound interest for our product
as well as partner led demand for a retrofit solution that improves ventilation compliance and humidity control while
protecting operating budgets. Many buildings are struggling to meet ventilation needs without increasing air
conditioning capital cost and operating cost. Importantly, these engagements are translating into an expanding set
of qualified opportunities as we line up the next wave of demonstrations and pilots.

Helix Earth Technologies (Participant) has successfully extended the collaboration with Argonne National
Laboratory by securing a DOE funded project through the HPC4EI program ($400k for 1 year), to leverage National
Lab computational resources and assess the performance of the proposed capture technology to coal based plants in
conditions of interest of DOE. The modeling effort involves also the definition of a ML-based optimization
workflow, which will couple both experimental and numerical to explore novel configuration and operating maps.
