Bilateral superselective adrenal artery embolization for primary aldosteronism: A comparative evaluation of clinical efficacy and safety
Authors: Dongliang Cao, Xiaochun Zhang, Bo Tang, Huajun Zeng, Min He, Sha Lei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveSuperselective adrenal arterial embolization with ethanol has demonstrated promising efficacy in patients with primary aldosteronism and lateralized aldosterone secretion. However, approximately 40% of patients have nonlateralized disease, defined as bilateral primary aldosteronism based on adrenal venous sampling.MethodsIn this prospective observational cohort study, 30 patients with primary aldosteronism underwent superselective adrenal arterial embolization, including 16 patients with unilateral primary aldosteronism and 14 patients with bilateral primary aldosteronism confirmed by adrenal venous sampling. Patients were stratified into the unilateral primary aldosteronism and bilateral primary aldosteronism groups. Blood pressure and biochemical parameters were assessed at 15–40 h and at 1, 3, and 6 months after the procedure.ResultsPlasma aldosterone concentration, plasma renin activity, and the aldosterone-to-renin ratio improved significantly in both groups within 15–40 h after superselective adrenal arterial embolization compared with baseline. At 6 months, the rates of complete or partial clinical and biochemical success were 85.7% and 92.9%, respectively, in the bilateral primary aldosteronism group compared with 81.3% and 87.5%, respectively, in the unilateral primary aldosteronism group. Significant reductions in serum sodium levels and microalbuminuria were observed in patients undergoing bilateral superselective adrenal arterial embolization. Body mass index also decreased significantly at 1, 3, and 6 months in both groups. No procedure-related complications were observed during a median follow-up of 6 months.ConclusionsBilateral superselective adrenal arterial embolization may represent a valuable adjunctive treatment strategy for patients with bilateral primary aldosteronism.

Introduction

Primary aldosteronism (PA) is the most common cause of secondary hypertension, accounting for 5%–10% of all cases of hypertension and up to 20% of resistant hypertension.1,2 Excess aldosterone secretion is associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular complications, including myocardial hypertrophy, heart failure, and vascular dysfunction. 3 Therefore, early diagnosis and appropriate management of PA are critical.

Laparoscopic adrenalectomy is the preferred treatment for unilateral primary aldosteronism (UPA), whereas mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) are recommended for patients with bilateral primary aldosteronism (BPA). 4 However, MRA therapy does not always adequately suppress aldosterone levels or prevent long-term complications,5,6 and some patients are intolerant to its adverse effects, leading to poor adherence 4

Superselective adrenal arterial embolization (SAAE) is a minimally invasive technique that induces targeted necrosis of hyperfunctioning adrenal tissue.1,7 Previous studies have demonstrated its efficacy in patients with UPA, with outcomes comparable to those of adrenalectomy.8–11 However, evidence regarding its application in patients with BPA remains limited. 12 To date, only two studies, both conducted at the same center, have investigated the efficacy of SAAE in patients with BPA,13,14 neither concurrently evaluated its impact on metabolic parameters. Therefore, additional data are needed to strengthen the evidence base and further validate the efficacy and safety of SAAE in patients with BPA.

Methods

Patients and follow-up

This study was designed as a prospective observational cohort study, 30 consecutive patients with a confirmed diagnosis of PA were enrolled from the Department of Cardiology at the Third Hospital of Mianyang between November 2022 and November 2023. This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, as revised in 2024. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Third Hospital of Mianyang (Approval Number: 2022-14-27). The diagnosis of PA was based on current clinical guidelines. 4 Patients with a positive aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR > 20 ng/dL per ng/mL/h) underwent confirmatory testing, including saline infusion or captopril challenge tests. Antihypertensive medications that interfere with ARR were discontinued for 2–4 weeks before testing, and blood pressure was managed with verapamil and/or terazosin. Hypokalemia was corrected before testing.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) a history of serious contrast agent allergy; (b) complication with severe liver or renal diseases; (c) adrenal adenomas >2 cm in diameter, other forms of secondary hypertension, or APA; (d) hemorrhagic or ischemic stroke or myocardial infarction within the previous 3 months; (e) pregnancy or lactation; (f) history of participation in another clinical trial within the previous 3 months; or (g) any other serious comorbidity. Patient selection and follow-up flow diagram.

All patients underwent adrenal venous sampling (AVS) for subtype classification. A lateralization index ≥2 indicated unilateral aldosterone excess (UPA), whereas <2 indicated BPA.15,16 The reporting of this study conforms to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. 17

SAAE procedure

SAAE was performed using a Philips UNIQ FD20 angiography system. In patients with UPA, the inferior and/or middle adrenal artery supplying the affected gland was embolized. In patients with BPA, one adrenal artery on each side was selectively embolized.

To reduce intraoperative and postoperative pain, a patient-controlled intravenous analgesia pump was initiated 2–4 h before the procedure according to an individualized regimen prescribed by an anesthesiologist. The patient was placed in the supine position, and routine disinfection of the puncture site, draping, and local anesthesia were performed. The standard analgesic regimen consisted of 200 mL of normal saline, 200 μg of sufentanil citrate injection, and 6 mg of granisetron hydrochloride administered at 4–6 mL/h.

The right brachial artery was routinely used for vascular access, whereas the right femoral artery was selected when the brachial approach was technically challenging. After local anesthesia, a 6-F arterial sheath was inserted using a modified Seldinger technique, and heparin sodium (100 U/kg) was administered for anticoagulation. Under fluoroscopic guidance, selective adrenal angiography was performed using a 6-F JR4 guiding catheter and a microcatheter system to identify the target adrenal artery.

After confirmation of the catheter position, absolute ethanol (typically 2–4 mL, not exceeding 5 mL per target vessel) was slowly injected for embolization. If pain remained intolerable despite continuous analgesia, 3–5 mg of morphine hydrochloride was administered subcutaneously. Repeat angiography was performed 5 min after embolization. Additional ethanol was injected when persistent arterial flow was observed until contrast retention or complete occlusion of the target adrenal artery was achieved.

Absolute ethanol was used as the embolic agent. Hemodynamic parameters were continuously monitored, and hypertensive episodes were managed as needed. The procedure was performed according to the method described by Dong et al. 8 Hypertension and tachycardia resulting from the massive release of catecholamines were closely monitored and managed during embolization. Blood pressure, heart rate, and patient complaints were carefully monitored during and after the SAAE procedure. Outcomes

Blood pressure reduction and biochemical improvement were the primary outcomes used to assess the clinical and biochemical efficacy of SAAE, respectively. Outcomes were evaluated according to the Primary Aldosteronism Surgical Outcome (PASO) criteria and classified as complete, partial, or absent success.

Clinical success was assessed using home blood pressure measurements, with normal blood pressure defined as systolic <135mmHg and diastolic <85mmHg. Complete clinical success was defined as normotension without antihypertensive medication. Partial clinical success was defined as either maintained blood pressure with reduced medication or lower blood pressure with the same or reduced medication. Absent clinical success was defined as no change or an increase in blood pressure despite the same or increased medication.

Biochemical success was defined as normalization of the ARR and correction of hypokalemia (if present at baseline). Partial biochemical success was defined as correction of hypokalemia with a persistently elevated ARR but a ≥50% reduction in plasma aldosterone concentration. Absent biochemical success was defined as persistent hypokalemia or a <50% reduction in plasma aldosterone concentration.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD or median (interquartile range (IQR)). Group comparisons were performed using Student's t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher's exact test. A two-sided p <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients

A total of 30 patients were included in this study. Unilateral embolization was successfully performed in 16 patients with UPA, targeting all identifiable adrenal arterial branches. In 14 patients with BPA, bilateral embolization was successfully performed, with one arterial branch embolized per adrenal gland. All patients were followed at 1, 3, and 6 months after SAAE, with assessments including home blood pressure, heart rate, body weight, and biochemical parameters. Compared with the UPA group, patients with BPA had significantly higher body mass index (BMI) and serum lipid levels as well as larger left atrial diameter (LAD) and left ventricular posterior wall thickness (LVPW). Although not statistically significant, patients with BPA tended to have higher plasma aldosterone levels, lower high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) levels, younger age, and a higher prevalence of smoking. No significant differences were observed in other baseline clinical or biochemical characteristics between the two groups (Table 2).

Procedural characteristics

Among the 30 patients, 27 successfully underwent SAAE via the brachial artery approach, whereas the remaining 3 underwent the procedure via the femoral artery approach. The median procedural time, defined as the interval from arterial puncture to sheath removal, was 74 (55.75–116.25) min. The mean volume of absolute ethanol used for embolization was 3.88 ± 1.59 mL, and the median volume of contrast medium administered was 122.5 (95–135) mL. Safety event

Common adverse events included abdominal pain, fever, nausea and vomiting, chest pain, lumbago, dorsalgia, and shoulder pain. Because pain and catecholamine release may induce intraoperative blood pressure fluctuations during SAAE, sodium nitroprusside was routinely prepared for blood pressure control. No intraoperative hypertensive crises were observed. One patient developed sinus bradycardia due to a vagal reflex, which resolved promptly after analgesia and intravenous atropine administration. No procedure-related complications were observed during a median follow-up of 6 months (Table 6).

Discussion

SAAE achieved complete or partial clinical and biochemical success rates of 85.7% and 92.9%, respectively, in the BPA group, compared with 81.3% and 87.5%, respectively, in the UPA group. Bilateral SAAE was also associated with significant reductions in serum sodium levels and microalbuminuria. BMI decreased significantly at 1, 3, and 6 months in both groups, suggesting potential metabolic benefits. Higher clinical efficacy was observed in female patients and patients younger than 60 years. No major adverse events or postoperative adrenal insufficiency were observed.

When AVS demonstrates BPA, the standard treatment dogma is to prescribe lifelong MRA therapy.7,18 However, medical therapy is often associated with adverse effects and may not adequately reduce the risk of long-term cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events. Over the past few decades, several studies have explored adrenalectomy as an alternative treatment for patients with BPA. Sukor et al.19 reported outcomes of unilateral adrenalectomy in patients with BPA, demonstrating that hypertension was cured in 15% of patients and improved in 20%. More recently, Lu et al.20 and Szabo Yamashita et al.21 reported improvements in blood pressure in 60% and 65% of patients with BPA, respectively. These investigators concluded that although unilateral adrenalectomy cannot cure patients with BPA, it may benefit selected patients with apparent BPA.

SAAE is an effective approach that selectively embolizes the adrenal artery supplying the affected adrenal gland, resulting in tissue necrosis and reduced aldosterone secretion. Over the past few decades, an increasing number of studies have demonstrated the clinical and biochemical efficacy of SAAE for the treatment of PA.8,9,11However, most of these studies were focused on the treatment of UPA, and revealed a similar efficacy of SAAE as surgical adrenalectomy.19,20

There are limited data on the use of SAAE in patients with BPA. Dong et al. were the first to perform unilateral SAAE in 31 patients with BPA. After a median follow-up of 12 months, complete or partial clinical success was achieved in 38.7% of patients, whereas 58.6% achieved complete or partial biochemical success. More recently, Li et al.14 reported bilateral SAAE in patients with BPA. In that study, patients with BPA confirmed by AVS underwent bilateral adrenal arterial embolization, whereas patients with UPA received unilateral embolization. When multiple adrenal arterial branches were present, the dominant supplying artery was selected for embolization.

At 12 months, clinical success rates were 83.3% and 71.4% in the UPA and BPA groups, respectively, whereas biochemical success rates at 3 months were 61.9% and 58.3%, respectively. However, biochemical outcomes at 12 months were not assessed because of loss to follow-up.

Although existing studies suggest a potential role for SAAE in patients with BPA, the current evidence remains insufficient to establish the superiority of bilateral embolization. The management of BPA remains challenging, and unilateral embolization fails to address aldosterone overproduction from the contralateral gland, potentially leading to treatment failure and recurrence. In contrast, bilateral embolization may provide more comprehensive control of aldosterone secretion and improved efficacy. In our study, only one of the three adrenal arterial branches per gland was embolized, preserving partial blood supply and avoiding complete adrenal necrosis.21,22 No adverse events were recorded during or after SAAE, and no cases of postoperative adrenal insufficiency were observed.

This study compared unilateral embolization in patients with UPA with BPA. At 6 months, clinical success rates were 81.3% and 85.7%, whereas biochemical success rates were 87.5% and 92.9% in the UPA and BPA groups, respectively. These outcomes were comparable to those reported by Li et al.14 However, biochemical outcomes at 12 months were not evaluated in that study because of loss to follow-up. Furthermore, patients with BPA group exhibited significant reductions in microalbuminuria and serum sodium levels at 6 months, suggesting improved renal function.

Female patients demonstrated higher efficacy rates after SAAE, which may be attributable to a lower burden of cardiovascular risk factors and fewer comorbidities affecting blood pressure control. Patients younger than 60 years also showed superior outcomes. In contrast, prolonged exposure to hypertension and hyperaldosteronism in older patients may lead to vascular remodeling and atherosclerosis, thereby reducing treatment responsiveness. 23

To explore additional benefits of SAAE, we evaluated changes in BMI. BMI decreased significantly at 1, 3, and 6 months after the procedure, suggesting potential metabolic benefits, possibly mediated by endocrine mechanisms. To date, weight reduction following SAAE has not been reported. These findings suggest that SAAE may represent a novel approach to weight management; however, further basic research and well-designed prospective studies are needed to confirm its efficacy and elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the lack of a comparison between SAAE and medical therapy, primarily because of the limited sample size, restricts the ability to draw definitive conclusions regarding their relative efficacy. Second, this was a single-center study with a relatively small cohort, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to the broader population of patients with BPA. Further studies with larger sample sizes are warranted to validate these results. In particular, well-designed cohort studies comparing bilateral and unilateral SAAE would provide valuable evidence to further elucidate the efficacy and safety of SAAE in patients with BPA.

Conclusions

SAAE is an effective and safe treatment option for both UPA and BPA. Bilateral embolization may offer additional benefits for patients with BPA, particularly by improving renal function and metabolic parameters.
