Analytic structure of q q -pseudoconcave subsets of continuous graphs
Filippo Valnegri

Abstract
. Let Z ⊂ ℂ N Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{N} be an n n -pseudoconcave subset, for 1 ≤ n < N 1\leq n<N , which is locally the graph of a continuous function over a closed subset of ℂ n × ℝ \mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} . We show that Z Z can be realised as the disjoint union of n n -dimensional complex manifolds. In particular, the same conclusion can be made for any n n -pseudoconcave subset Z Z of the graph Γ ⁡ ( g ) \Gamma(g) of a continuous function g : D ⊂ ℂ n × ℝ → ℝ × ℂ p g:D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{p} , for n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 and p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 .



Introduction

The main purpose of this work is to show the following result.

Main Theorem
.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be an
n
-pseudoconcave subset (for
1\leq n<N
) which is locally the graph of a continuous function
g:X\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
(for
p=N-n-1\geq 0
), where
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is a closed subset. Then
Z
is foliated by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.

Remark 1
.

Here and in what follows, we use the term
foliated
not in the classical sense, but to indicate indicate a slightly different object (see Definition
1.3
).

The study of the existence of an analytic structure on a subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
is an interesting and fundamental problem in the field of several complex variables. Its origin can be traced back to Tréprau (
[
18
]
), who approached this problem with the aim to study the holomorphic extension of CR functions defined on the boundary of domains of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. However, he actually proved a much more general result, which is a characterisation of the existence of a (maximal) analytic structure on a (reasonably smooth) real hypersurface of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

Namely, let
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a connected real hypersurface. Fixed
z_{0}\in S
, it is interesting to ask under which conditions there exists a complex hypersurface which passes through
z_{0}
and is contained in
S
.

It is easy to find some necessary conditions. For example, if such complex hypersurface does exist, we can find a holomorphic function on the complement of
S
, which does not extend holomorphically to
z_{0}
. Indeed, the complex hypersurface can be written as the zero locus of a holomorphic function, i.e.,
\{f=0\}
. If we consider the function
h=f^{-1}
, then
h
is a holomorphic function on the complement of
S
, which does not extend holomorphically to
\{f=0\}
.

It is then natural to ask whether this necessary condition is also sufficient. Namely, if there exists a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{N}\smallsetminus S
which does not extend holomorphically to
z_{0}
(in this case we say that the set
\mathbb{C}^{N}\smallsetminus S
itself
does not extend holomorphically to
z_{0}
), can we conclude that there exists a complex hypersurface in
S
which passes through
z_{0}
?

Trépreau answered affirmatively to this question when
S
is the graph of a reasonably smooth function. Namely, let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
be a domain and let
g:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathscr{C}^{2}
function. Then the graph
\Gamma(g)
of
g
is a real
\mathscr{C}^{2}
hypersurface in
D\times i\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. We denote by
\Gamma_{+}(g)
the epigraph of
g
in
D\times i\mathbb{R}
and by
\Gamma_{-}(g)
the hypograph of
g
in the same domain. In 1986, Trépreau proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Trépreau
[
18
]
)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
be a domain and let
g:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathscr{C}^{2}
function. If
z_{0}\in\Gamma(g)
is not in the envelope of holomorphy of neither
\Gamma_{+}(g)
nor
\Gamma_{-}(g)
, then there exists a complex hypersurface which passes through
z_{0}
and is contained in
\Gamma(g)
.

This was achieved by attaching analytic discs to
\Gamma(g)
, passing through
z_{0}
, and then gluing them together using Frobenius’ Theorem.

The difficulty is now trying to reduce the regularity assumptions on
g
. In Trépreau’s proof, the
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-smoothness of
g
is crucial, due to the use of Frobenius’ Theorem. For this reason, the result obtained by Shcherbina in 1993 (for
n=1
) was a significant breakthrough in this direction.

Theorem 2

(Shcherbina
[
13
]
)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
be a bounded strictly convex domain and let
\varphi:bD\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Then
\widehat{\Gamma(\varphi)}\smallsetminus\Gamma(\varphi)
is the disjoint union of complex analytic discs, where
\widehat{\Gamma(\varphi)}
denotes the polynomial hull of
\Gamma(\varphi)
.

Afterwards, in 2001 Chirka succeeded in extending this theorem to any dimension.

Theorem 3

(Chirka
[
3
]
)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
be a domain and let
g:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. If
z_{0}\in\Gamma(g)
is such that neither
\Gamma_{+}(g)
nor
\Gamma_{-}(g)
extend holomorphically to it, then there exists a contractible domain of holomorphy
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a holomorphic function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
such that
z_{0}\in\Gamma(f)\subset\Gamma(g)
and
\Gamma(f)
is closed in
\Gamma(g)
.

In the particular case where neither
\Gamma_{+}(g)
nor
\Gamma_{-}(g)
extend holomorphically to any point of
\Gamma(g)
, Theorem
3
can be rephrased in terms of pseudoconvexity.

Theorem 3
′
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
be a domain and let
g:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. If the complement of
\Gamma(g)
in
D\times i\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is pseudoconvex, then there exists a foliation on
\Gamma(g)
given by complex hypersurfaces.

Adopting this perspective, in 2022 Pawlaschyk and Shcherbina extended Theorem
3′
to the case of graphs of higher codimension, under suitable pseudoconvexity conditions on the complement of the graph.

Theorem 4

(Pawlaschyk, Shcherbina
[
10
]
)
.

For
n\geq 1
,
k\in\{0,1\}
and
p\geq 0
, let
B\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
be an open ball and let
g:B\to\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
be a continuous function. If the complement of
\Gamma(g)
in
B\times i\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
(where
N=n+k+p\geq 2
) is
(k+p-1)
-pseudoconvex in
B\times i\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
, then
\Gamma(g)
is foliated by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.

Remark 2
.

In Theorem
4
and in what follows we use the term
q
-pseudoconvex
in the sense of Słodkowski (see Definition
1.1
).

Remark 3
.

The case
k=0
and
p=1
of Theorem
4
is a classical result due to Hartogs (see
[
5
]
), while the case
k=0
and
p>1
can be derived from it.

Since the pseudoconvexity assumption concerns the complement of the graph, we can rephrase Theorem
4
in terms of a complementary condition to pseudoconvexity: pseudoconcavity (see Definition
1.2
).

Theorem 4
′

(
[
10
, Theorem 5.6]
)
.

For
n\geq 1
,
k\in\{0,1\}
and
p\geq 0
, let
B\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
be an open ball and let
g:B\to\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
be a continuous function. If
\Gamma(g)
is an
n
-pseudoconcave subset of
B\times i\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
(where
N=n+k+p\geq 2
), then
\Gamma(g)
is foliated by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.

Even more, we can also reformulate Theorem
4
in terms of another property equivalent to pseudoconcavity: the local maximum property (see Definition
2.1
).

A priori, this property does not seem related to pseudoconcavity. However, Słodkowski proved that local maximum sets are complementary to pseudoconvex ones, actually obtaining an equivalence with pseudoconcave sets.

Theorem 5

(Słodkowski
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open subset and
Z\subset V
be a closed one. For
0\leq q\leq n-2
,
Z
is a
q
-local maximum set if and only if
U\coloneqq V\smallsetminus Z
is
(n-q-2)
-pseudoconvex in
V
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open subset and let
Z\subset V
be a closed one. For
1\leq q\leq n-1
,
Z
is a
q
-pseudoconcave subset of
V
if and only if it is a
(q-1)
-local maximum set.

In what follows (in particular, in Section
4
), we will interchange pseudoconcavity and the local maximum property naturally.

The reformulation of the problem in terms of the local maximum property has various motivations and advantages. For example, the local maximum property is a key tool in the study of obstructions to the existence of strictly plurisubharmonic exhaustion functions on complex manifolds, as shown in
[
8
,
9
,
14
]
. But its usefulness is not limited to this problem, as it also arises in various studies of problems of this nature (e.g., see
[
11
,
16
]
).

Thus to study the former problem in terms of local maximum sets is both natural and interesting. In particular, this further underlines the link between the existence of a complex analytic structure and the behaviour of plurisubharmonic functions. Moreover, it is fundamental to allow lower regularity. Finally, as a consequence we also obtain some information on the geometry of local maximum sets.

Using then the flexibility of the local maximum property, we are able to prove our Main Theorem, which is a more general version of Theorem
4′
(for
k=1
).

Organisation of the article.

In Section
1
we extend Theorem
3
to the case of an
n
-pseudoconcave subset
Z
of a continuous graph
\Gamma(g)
. In particular, we can extend Theorem
3′
to graphs of real-valued continuous functions over closed subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

In Section
2
we gather some properties about local maximum sets and the existence of certain plurisubharmonic functions when a continuous graph does not have the local maximum property.

In Section
3
we prove that the upper closed limit of a net of local maximum sets is still a local maximum set.

In Section
4
we prove our Main Theorem (see Theorem
4.1
). This is done constructing a foliation on an underlying set to the one considered and then lifting this foliation using the function which defines the graph.

In Section
5
we gather some applications of our Main Theorem. For example, we obtain foliations on
n
-pseudoconcave sets contained in subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
which are locally graphs of differentiable functions over domains of
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
.

In Section
6
we show that the index of pseudoconcavity used in our Main Theorem is sharp. Namely, we construct an
(n-1)
-pseudoconcave subset of a continuous (actually, smooth) graph, defined over
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, which has no analytic structure.

1.
The hypersurface case

In this section, we prove that every
n
-pseudoconcave subset of a continuous graph, which is a real hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, is foliated by complex hypersurfaces.

First of all, let us recall the definition of
q
-pseudoconvexity and
q
-pseudoconcavity we are referring to.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be two open subsets, with
U\subset V
. We say that
U
is
q
-pseudoconvex in
V
(for
0\leq q\leq n-1
) if there exists an open neighbourhood
W\subset V
of
V\cap bU
such that the function

\varphi:W\cap U\to\mathbb{R},\quad\varphi(z)\coloneqq-\log\bigl(\dist(z,bU)\bigr)

is
q
-plurisubharmonic, where
bU
denotes the topological boundary of
U
.

Remark 1.1
.

There are various definitions of pseudoconvexity. The one stated in Definition
1.1
is due to Słodkowski. However, in the smooth setting the following are equivalent:

(a)

U
is
q
-pseudoconvex in
V
in the sense of Słodkowski;

(b)

U
is Hartogs
(n-q-1)
-pseudoconvex relatively to
V
;

(c)

U
is Levi
q
-pseudoconvex in
V
;

(d)

U
is
(q+1)
-convex in the sense of Grauert–Andreotti.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open subset and let
Z\subset V
be a closed one. We say that
Z
is a
q
-pseudoconcave subset
of
V
(for
1\leq q\leq n-1
) if
U\coloneqq V\smallsetminus Z
is
(n-q-1)
-pseudoconvex in
V
.

As anticipated in the introduction, our aim is to construct a foliation on a closed subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Since a closed subset does not have a priori any differentiable structure (e.g., local charts), we cannot construct on it a foliation in the classical sense. We will then use the term
foliated
is the following sense (the name is motivated by analogy with the
\mathscr{C}^{2}
case).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a closed subset. We say that
Z
is
foliated
if it can be written as the disjoint union of some embedded submanifolds, all of the same dimension.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that we do not assume any kind of transversal geometry for such foliations.

After these preliminary definitions, we can now delve into the main purpose of this section. Let us formally state its main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
be a domain. Let
g:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function and let

\Gamma(g)=\bigl\{(z,w)\in D\times i\mathbb{R}\mid\Impart(w)=g\bigl(z,\Realpart(w)\bigr)\bigr\}

be its graph in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}=\mathbb{C}^{n}_{z}\times(\mathbb{R}\times i\mathbb{R})_{w}
. If
Z_{0}\subset\Gamma(g)
is an
n
-pseudoconcave subset, then there exists a unique foliation on it given by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.

Remark 1.3
.

When we say that
Z_{0}\subset\Gamma(g)
is an
n
-pseudoconcave subset, we mean that
Z_{0}
is a subset of
\Gamma(g)
which is
n
-pseudoconcave in
D\times i\mathbb{R}
.

In order to obtain a foliation on
Z_{0}
, we will consider a foliated set in
\Gamma(g)
(constructed by Chirka), prove that it contains
Z_{0}
and then show that
Z_{0}
is the union of some leaves of this foliation.

First of all, let us recall a classical construction (see
[
18
, Lemma 1.2]
). Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
be a domain and let
g:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Then there exist functions
u,v:D\to\mathbb{R}
upper and lower semicontinuous (respectively), with
u\leq g\leq v
, such that the domains

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(u)

\displaystyle=\bigl\{(z,w)\in D\times i\mathbb{R}\mid\Impart(w)>u\bigl(z,\Realpart(w)\bigr)\bigr\}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(v)

\displaystyle=\bigl\{(z,w)\in D\times i\mathbb{R}\mid\Impart(w)<v\bigl(z,\Realpart(w)\bigr)\bigr\}

are pseudoconvex. Namely,
u
is the infimum of all the upper semicontinuous functions
\tilde{u}\leq g
on
D
such that every holomorphic function on
\Gamma_{+}(g)
extends holomorphically to
\Gamma_{+}(\tilde{u})
and
v
is the supremum of all the lower semicontinuous functions
\tilde{v}\geq g
on
D
such that every holomorphic function on
\Gamma_{-}(g)
extends holomorphically to
\Gamma_{-}(\tilde{v})
.

Remark 1.4
.

By construction,
\Gamma_{+}(u)
is the envelope of holomorphy of
\Gamma_{+}(g)
and
\Gamma_{-}(v)
is the envelope of holomorphy of
\Gamma_{-}(g)
.

Let

E\coloneqq\Gamma(u)\cap\Gamma(v)\subset\Gamma(g)

By
[
3
, Corollary to Theorem 2]
,
E
is foliated by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.

Remark 1.5
.

The set
E
was first introduced by Trépreau in the
\mathscr{C}^{2}
setting (see
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
Z_{0}\subset\Gamma(g)
be an
n
-pseudoconcave subset. Then
Z_{0}
is contained in
E
.

Proof.

To prove this, we need to show that
Z_{0}
is contained in both the graphs of
u
and
v
. For this purpose, we will show that
Z_{0}
is disjoint from both the epigraph and the hypograph of these functions.

First of all, let us prove that
Z_{0}
is disjoint from
\Gamma_{-}(u)
. Since
Z_{0}\subset\Gamma(g)
, we have that
Z_{0}\cap\Gamma_{-}(g)=\emptyset
. On the other hand,
\Gamma_{-}(u)\subset\Gamma_{-}(g)
, since
u\leq g
. Hence
Z_{0}
is disjoint from
\Gamma_{-}(u)
.

Now let us prove that
Z_{0}
is disjoint from
\Gamma_{+}(u)
. By contradiction, suppose that
Z_{0}\cap\Gamma_{+}(u)\neq\emptyset
. Let
W
be the connected component of
\Gamma_{+}(u)\smallsetminus Z_{0}
containing
\Gamma_{+}(g)
and let
p\in Z_{0}\cap\Gamma_{+}(u)
be a point belonging to the boundary of
W
. Since
Z_{0}
is
n
-pseudoconcave, then
\Gamma_{+}(u)\smallsetminus Z_{0}
is pseudoconvex and thus
W
is a domain of holomorphy. Hence there exists a holomorphic function
F:W\to\mathbb{C}
which cannot be extended to any neighbourhood of
p
. However,
F|_{\Gamma_{+}(g)}
can be extended to some holomorphic function
H:\Gamma_{+}(u)\to\mathbb{C}
, by Remark
1.4
. Since
\Gamma_{+}(g)\subset W
is open, then
F=H|_{W}
, i.e.,
H
extends
F
to a neighbourhood of
p
. This is a contradiction. Therefore
Z_{0}
is disjoint from
\Gamma_{+}(u)
.

Therefore
Z_{0}
is contained in
\Gamma(u)
. Analogously,
Z_{0}
is contained in
\Gamma(v)
. Thus

Z_{0}\subset\Gamma(u)\cap\Gamma(v)=E\qed

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation of
E
by complex hypersurfaces. Then
Z_{0}
is union of some leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
\ell
be a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
which intersects
Z_{0}
and let
(z_{0},w_{0})\in\ell\cap Z_{0}
. It is sufficient to show that there exists a neighbourhood of
(z_{0},w_{0})
in
\ell
which is all contained in
Z_{0}
. Note that, by Theorem
3
,

(1)

\ell=\Gamma(f)=\{(z,f(z))\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\mid z\in G\}

where
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic function and
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a contractible domain of holomorphy. Since
(z_{0},w_{0})\in\ell
, then
z_{0}\in G
and
f(z_{0})=w_{0}
. On the other hand, since
Z_{0}\subset V\coloneqq D\times i\mathbb{R}
is
n
-pseudoconcave, then
U\coloneqq V\smallsetminus Z_{0}
is pseudoconvex.

Let
\lambda\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a 1-dimensional affine complex subspace passing through
z_{0}
. Then
(z_{0},w_{0})\in(\lambda\times\mathbb{C})\cap E
. Let

W=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid|z-z_{0}|<r\}

be an open ball centred at
z_{0}
of radius
r>0
, such that
\overline{W}\subset G
. Let
W_{\lambda}\coloneqq W\cap\lambda
, which is a 1-dimensional disc centred at
z_{0}
, and set
\Delta_{\lambda}\coloneqq\Gamma(f|_{W_{\lambda}})
. Then
\Delta_{\lambda}
is an analytic disc in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. By (
1
), we have that

\ell\supset\bigcup_{\lambda\ni z_{0}}\Delta_{\lambda}=\Gamma(f|_{W})\eqqcolon\Delta

Note that
\Delta
is a neighbourhood of
(z_{0},w_{0})
in
\ell
. Now, for
s\in(0,S]
let

\Delta_{\lambda}^{s}\coloneqq\Delta_{\lambda}+(\boldsymbol{0},is)=\{(z,f(z)+is)\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\mid z\in W_{\lambda}\}

Since
\overline{\Delta_{\lambda}^{s}}\subset\Gamma(g+s)
and
Z_{0}\subset\Gamma(g)
, then
\overline{\Delta_{\lambda}^{s}}\cap Z_{0}=\emptyset
and thus
\overline{\Delta_{\lambda}^{s}}\subset U
. Note that
\overline{\Delta_{\lambda}^{s}}\to\overline{\Delta_{\lambda}}
and
b\Delta_{\lambda}^{s}\to b\Delta_{\lambda}
for
s\to 0^{+}
, in the Hausdorff metric. If
\overline{\Delta_{\lambda}}\cap U\neq\emptyset
, then
\overline{\Delta_{\lambda}}\subset U
, by the “strong continuity principle” (see
[
19
, Section 17.3]
). This is a contradiction, since
(z_{0},w_{0})\in\Delta_{\lambda}
. Thus
\overline{\Delta_{\lambda}}\subset Z_{0}
.

By the arbitrariness of
\lambda
, we obtain that
\Delta\subset Z_{0}
. Note that
\Delta
is a neighbourhood of
(z_{0},w_{0})
and thus
\ell\cap Z_{0}
is open in
\ell
. On the other hand,
\ell\cap Z_{0}
is clearly closed in
\ell
, since
Z_{0}
is closed. Hence
\ell\subset Z_{0}
, since
\ell
is connected. Therefore
Z_{0}
is union of leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.
∎

Remark 1.6
.

The foliation obtained on
Z_{0}
is unique. Namely, consider the foliation
\{\ell_{\alpha}\}
constructed above and suppose that there exists another foliation
\{\ell^{\prime}_{\beta}\}
by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds on
Z_{0}
. Note that a leaf
\ell_{\alpha}
is a connected
n
-dimensional complex analytic set in
Z_{0}\subset E\subset\Gamma(g)
, which is closed in
D\times i\mathbb{R}
, by Theorem
3
. Take a leaf
\ell^{\prime}_{\beta}
of the second foliation such that
\ell_{\alpha}\cap\ell^{\prime}_{\beta}\neq\emptyset
. Since
\ell^{\prime}_{\beta}
is a connected complex analytic set, then
\ell^{\prime}_{\beta}\subset\ell_{\alpha}
, by
[
3
, Lemma 2]
. Therefore we have the uniqueness of the foliation.

We have thus proved Theorem
1.1
.

Furthermore, we can see
Z_{0}
not only as a closed subset of a graph, but as a graph itself.

Corollary 1.1
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
X_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
be a closed subset. Let
g_{0}:X_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function and let
Z_{0}\coloneqq\Gamma(g_{0})
be its graph. If
Z_{0}
is an
n
-pseudoconcave subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, then there exists a unique foliation on it given by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.

Proof.

By Tietze Extension Theorem, there exists a continuous function
g:\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which extends
g_{0}
. Then
Z_{0}
is an
n
-pseudoconcave subset of
\Gamma(g)
. Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
, we conclude.
∎

Finally, since the result we have proven is local, we can also ask
Z_{0}
to be just locally a continuous graph, instead of globally.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be an
n
-pseudoconcave subset, which is locally the graph of a continuous function over a closed subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a unique foliation on
Z
given by complex hypersurfaces.

2.
Some properties of
q
-local maximum sets

In this section, we prove some properties regarding
q
-local maximum sets, which will be fundamental in Section
4
.

First of all, let us recall their definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a locally closed subset. We say that
Z
is a
q
-local maximum set
(for
0\leq q\leq n-1
) if for every
z\in Z
there exists an open neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
z
such that for every compact subset
K\subset U
, with
Z\cap K\neq\emptyset
compact, and for every
q
-plurisubharmonic function
\varphi:U\to\mathbb{R}
we have that

\max_{Z\cap K}\varphi=\max_{Z\cap bK}\varphi

where
bK
denotes the topological boundary of
K
.

In
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
, a characterisation of
q
-local maximum sets is given. We are interested, in particular, in property (iii):
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a
q
-local maximum set if and only if it does not exist a
q
-plurisubharmonic function
\rho:Z\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\rho(z_{0})=0
and
\rho<0
on
Z\smallsetminus\{z_{0}\}
, for some
z_{0}\in Z
. Note that the function
\rho
is, a priori, only upper semicontinuous. We show that, at least in a neighbourhood of a point, this function can be taken continuous.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a closed subset. If
Z
is not a
q
-local maximum set, then there exists a point
z_{0}\in Z
such that: for every neighbourhood
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
z_{0}
there exists a continuous
q
-plurisubharmonic function
\rho:U\to\mathbb{R}
, where
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a neighbourhood of
Z\cap V
, and a point
w_{0}\in Z\cap V
such that

\displaystyle\rho(w_{0})=0

\displaystyle\rho(w)<0\quad\forall w\in(Z\cap V)\smallsetminus\{w_{0}\}

Proof.

Since
Z
has not the
q
-local maximum property, then there exists a point
z_{0}\in Z
such that for each neighbourhood
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
z_{0}
there exists a compact subset
C\subset V
and a smooth
q
-plurisubharmonic function
\varphi:V\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\max_{Z\cap C}\varphi>\max_{Z\cap bC}\varphi

Fix
V
and take the respective
C
and
\varphi
.

We construct the continuous function
\rho
using the same strategy of
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
. By
[
15
, Lemma 2.2]
, there exists a point
w_{0}\in Z\cap\mathring{C}
and a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle(\varphi+f)(w_{0})=0

\displaystyle(\varphi+f)(w)<0\quad\forall w\in(Z\cap C)\smallsetminus\{w_{0}\}

Take
d<0
such that

\max_{Z\cap bC}(\varphi+f)<d

and let
H=\{z\in V\mid(\varphi+f)(z)<d\}
. Then
H\subset V
is open and
Z\cap bC\subset H
. Let
U=\mathring{C}\cup H\cup(V\smallsetminus C)
, then
U
is an open neighbourhood of
Z\cap V
. Let us define

\rho:U\to\mathbb{R},\quad\rho(z)=\begin{cases}\max\{(\varphi+f)(z),d\},&z\in\mathring{C}\\
d,&z\in H\cup(V\smallsetminus C)\end{cases}

Since
\varphi+f<d
on
H
, then
\rho
is well-defined. By construction, we have that
\rho
is a continuous
q
-plurisubharmonic function and
w_{0}
is a strict global maximum point for
\rho|_{Z\cap V}
.
∎

Now we prove a topological property of continuous functions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be topological spaces and let
g:X\to Y
be a continuous function. Let
\mathcal{B}_{X}
be a basis for the topology of
X
and
\mathcal{B}_{Y}
be a basis for the topology of
Y
. Then for every open subset
V\subset X\times Y
, which intersects the graph
\Gamma(g)
, and for every point
(x_{0},g(x_{0}))\in V\cap\Gamma(g)
there exist two basic open sets
B\in\mathcal{B}_{X}
and
D\in\mathcal{B}_{Y}
such that
(x_{0},g(x_{0}))\in B\times D\subset V
and
g(\overline{B})\subset D
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{B}_{X}\times\mathcal{B}_{Y}
is a basis for the topology of
X\times Y
. Hence, fixed
(x_{0},g(x_{0}))\in V\cap\Gamma(g)
, there exists a basic open
B\times D\in\mathcal{B}_{X}\times\mathcal{B}_{Y}
such that

(x_{0},g(x_{0}))\in B\times D\subset V

Note that
B\cap g^{-1}(D)
is an open neighbourhood of
x_{0}
in
X
. Hence there exists a basic open set
B_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{X}
such that
x_{0}\in\overline{B_{0}}\subset B\cap g^{-1}(D)
. In particular, we have that

g(\overline{B_{0}})\subset g(B\cap g^{-1}(D))\subset g(g^{-1}(D))\subset D

Therefore, substituting
B
with
B_{0}
, we conclude.
∎

Combining Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, we obtain the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
g:U\to\mathbb{C}^{p}
be a continuous function, where
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is an open subset. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a basis for the Euclidean topology of
U
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be a basis for the Euclidean topology of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
, both given by relatively compact open sets. If
\Gamma(g)\subset U\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
is not a
q
-local maximum set, then there exist two basic open sets
B\in\mathcal{B}
and
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, and a continuous
q
-plurisubharmonic function
\rho:V\to\mathbb{R}
, where
V\subset U\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
is a neighbourhood of
K\coloneqq\overline{B\times B^{\prime}}
, such that

\displaystyle\max_{\Gamma(g)\cap K}\rho

\displaystyle>\max_{\Gamma(g)\cap bK}\rho

\displaystyle g(\overline{B})

\displaystyle\subset B^{\prime}

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, there exists a point
z_{0}\in\Gamma(g)
such that: for every neighbourhood
V\subset U\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
of
z_{0}
there exists a continuous
q
-plurisubharmonic function
\rho:V^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
V^{\prime}\subset U\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
is a neighbourhood of
\Gamma(g)\cap V
, and a point
w_{0}\in\Gamma(g)\cap V
such that

\displaystyle\rho(w_{0})=0

\displaystyle\rho(w)<0\quad\forall w\in(\Gamma(g)\cap V)\smallsetminus\{w_{0}\}

Fix
V
relatively compact and take the respective
\rho
and
w_{0}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
V^{\prime}=V
.

By Lemma
2.2
, there exist two basic open sets
B\in\mathcal{B}
and
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
such that
w_{0}\in B\times B^{\prime}\subset V
and
g(\overline{B})\subset B^{\prime}
. Since the basic open sets can be taken arbitrarily small, we can assume that
K\coloneqq\overline{B\times B^{\prime}}\subset V
. By construction,
w_{0}\in\mathring{K}
is a strict global maximum point for
\rho|_{\Gamma(g)\cap K}
.
∎

Before concluding this section, we prove that a real vector subspace with the local maximum property has a certain form.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a
(2n+1)
-dimensional real vector subspace with the
(n-1)
-local maximum property, for
1\leq n<N
. Then
L
is of type
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
H\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be the maximal complex vector subspace contained in
L
and let
k=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
. By contradiction, suppose that
k<n
.

Take a
(N-k)
-dimensional complex vector subspace
\tilde{H}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
such that

(2)

\tilde{H}\cap H=\{0\}

Let
\tilde{L}\coloneqq L\cap\tilde{H}
. Since
\codim_{\mathbb{C}}(\tilde{H})=k\leq n-1
, then
\tilde{L}
has the local maximum property. Moreover, note that

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde{L})=(2n+1)+2(N-k)-2N=2(n-k)+1\eqqcolon 2r+1\geq 3

By (
2
),
\tilde{L}
does not contain complex lines and thus
\tilde{L}\cap i\tilde{L}=\{0\}
. Let

\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\{v_{1},\dots,v_{2r+1}\}

be a real basis of
\tilde{L}
. Then
\mathcal{B}
is a complex basis of
\tilde{L}+i\tilde{L}
and we can extend
\mathcal{B}
to a complex basis
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}\coloneqq\{v_{1},\dots,v_{N}\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. For each
z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, we can write

z=\sum_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}v_{j}

for some
z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
. Let us define

f:\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathbb{C},\quad f(z)=-\sum_{j=1}^{2r+1}z_{j}^{2}

Note that
f
is holomorphic and thus the function
u\coloneqq\Realpart(f)
is pluriharmonic on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. By construction, for each
z\in\tilde{L}
we have that

z=\sum_{j=1}^{2r+1}x_{j}v_{j}

for some
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and hence

u(z)=-\sum_{j=1}^{2r+1}x_{j}^{2}\quad\forall z\in\tilde{L}

Thus
u
is a pluriharmonic function on
\tilde{L}
which has a strict maximum at
0
and therefore
\tilde{L}
does not have the local maximum property. This is a contradiction.
∎

3.
Upper closed limit and local maximum property

In this section, we prove that the upper closed limit of a net preserves the local maximum property.

First of all, let us recall some definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\Lambda,\leq)
be a directed set and let
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
be a subset. We say that
\Lambda^{\prime}
is
cofinal
if for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
such that
\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}
. We say, instead, that
\Lambda^{\prime}
is
residual
if there exists
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
such that

\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}\}\subset\Lambda^{\prime}

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a topological space and let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net of closed subsets of
X
. A point
x\in X
is called
cluster point
(resp.,
limit point
) of
\{A_{\lambda}\}
if for every neighbourhood
U\subset X
of
x
there exists a cofinal (resp., residual) subset
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
such that
U\cap A_{\lambda}\neq\emptyset
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.3
.

The
upper closed limit
of
\{A_{\lambda}\}
is

\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}=\bigl\{x\in X\mid\text{$x$ is a cluster point of $\{A_{\lambda}\}$}\bigr\}

The
lower closed limit
of
\{A_{\lambda}\}
is

\liminf_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}=\bigl\{x\in X\mid\text{$x$ is a limit point of $\{A_{\lambda}\}$}\bigr\}

Let us also recall the following characterisations (see
[
1
, Proposition 5.2.2]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space and let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net of closed subsets. Then

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda\\
\text{\emph{residual}}\end{subarray}}\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}}A_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\liminf_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda\\
\text{\emph{cofinal}}\end{subarray}}\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}}A_{\lambda}}

Our goal is to prove that the upper closed limit of a net of
q
-local maximum sets in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a closed
q
-local maximum set. In order to do so, we proceed by steps. The first step is to prove that the union and the intersection (the latter, with some further conditions) of a family of
q
-local maximum sets remain
q
-local maximum sets.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be an open subset and let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a family of closed subsets of
U
. If
A_{\lambda}
is a
q
-local maximum set for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then

A\coloneqq\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}}^{U}

is a
q
-local maximum set, where
\overline{\cdot}^{U}
denotes the closure in
U
.

Proof.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a complex affine subspace of codimension
q
. To conclude, it is sufficient to show that
A\cap L
is a local maximum set, if non-empty.

Let
a\in A\cap L
, then there exists a sequence
\{a_{n}\}\subset\bigcup_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}\subset U
which converges to
a
. Note that for every
n
there exists
\lambda_{n}\in\Lambda
such that
a_{n}\in A_{\lambda_{n}}
. Let
L_{0}\coloneqq L-a
be the complex vector subspace associated to
L
. Then
a_{n}\in(L_{0}+a_{n})\cap A_{\lambda_{n}}\subset U
. Let

Z_{a}\coloneqq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\,\!\!^{U}(L_{0}+a_{n})\cap A_{\lambda_{n}}

where
\limsup\,\!\!^{U}
denotes the upper closed limit with respect to
U
. Since every
A_{\lambda_{n}}
is a
q
-local maximum set, then every
(L_{0}+a_{n})\cap A_{\lambda_{n}}
has the local maximum property and thus
Z_{a}
is a local maximum set, by
[
17
, Proposition 1.2(d)]
. Clearly,
Z_{a}\subset A
. Moreover,

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}(L_{0}+a_{n})=L

and thus
Z_{a}\subset A\cap L
. Since
a\in A\cap L
was arbitrary, we have that

A\cap L=\bigcup_{a\in A\cap L}Z_{a}

Note that
A\cap L
is closed in
U
and hence we can write

A\cap L=\overline{\bigcup_{a\in A\cap L}Z_{a}}^{U}

Since this is the closure of a union of local maximum sets, then it is a local maximum set, by
[
17
, Proposition 1.2(e)]
. Thus
A
has the
q
-local maximum property.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space and let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net of closed subsets of
X
. If
A_{\lambda^{\prime}}\subset A_{\lambda}
whenever
\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda
, then

\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}

Proof.

If
x\in\bigcap_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}
, then for every neighbourhood
V\subset X
of
x
it is clear that
x\in A_{\lambda}\cap V\neq\emptyset
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Since
\Lambda
is trivially a cofinal subset of itself, we conclude that
x\in\limsup_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}
.

Viceversa, let

x\in\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda\\
\text{residual}\end{subarray}}\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}}A_{\lambda}}

Fix
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
. To conclude, we need to show that
x\in A_{\lambda_{0}}
. Since
\Lambda
is a directed set, then there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{0}
. Set

\Lambda^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}\}

Then
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
is a residual subset and thus

x\in\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}}A_{\lambda}}

Hence there exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}\subset\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}}A_{\lambda}
which converges to
x
. Note that for every
n
there exists
\lambda_{n}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
such that
x_{n}\in A_{\lambda_{n}}
. By construction, we have that, for every
n
,
\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{0}
and thus
A_{\lambda_{n}}\subset A_{\lambda_{0}}
, by hypothesis. In particular,
x_{n}\in A_{\lambda_{0}}
for every
n
. Therefore

x=\lim_{n\to+\infty}x_{n}\in A_{\lambda_{0}}

since
A_{\lambda_{0}}
is closed.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be an open subset and let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net of closed subsets of
U
, such that
\bigcap_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}
is non-empty. Assume that
A_{\lambda^{\prime}}\subset A_{\lambda}
whenever
\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda
and that every
A_{\lambda}
is a
q
-local maximum set. Then

A\coloneqq\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}

is a
q
-local maximum set.

Proof.

Let
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a complex affine subspace of codimension
q
intersecting
A
. Then

A\cap L=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}\cap L

Note that every
A_{\lambda}\cap L
is a local maximum set. Since
A_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cap L\subset A_{\lambda}\cap L
whenever
\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda
, then

\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}\cap L=\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}\cap L

by Lemma
3.1
. By
[
17
, Proposition 1.2(d)]
, the right hand member is a local maximum set and thus so is
A\cap L
. Therefore
A
is a
q
-local maximum set.
∎

The second step is to prove that the upper closed limit preserves the local maximum property when all the
q
-local maximum sets considered are closed. In order to do so, we use another characterisation of this limit.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space and let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net of closed subsets of
X
. We say that
\{A_{\lambda}\}

escapes to infinity
if for every compact
K\subset X
there exists a residual subset
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
such that

A_{\lambda}\cap K=\emptyset\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}

Remark 3.1
.

We have that
\limsup_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}
is non-empty if and only if
\{A_{\lambda}\}
does not escape to infinity.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net of subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Then
\{A_{\lambda}\}
escapes to infinity if and only if

\lim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\operatorname{dist}(0,A_{\lambda})=+\infty

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a Hausdorff space and let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net of closed subsets of
X
. Then

\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}=\bigcap_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda}\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}}A_{\lambda}}

Proof.

Recall that

A\coloneqq\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda\\
\text{residual}\end{subarray}}\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}}A_{\lambda}}

and set

A_{0}\coloneqq\bigcap_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda}\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}}A_{\lambda}}

Let
x\in A
and fix
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
. If we set

\Lambda_{0}\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}\}

then
\Lambda_{0}
is a residual subset of
\Lambda
.
Hence

x\in\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}}A_{\lambda}}

By the arbitrariness of
\lambda_{0}
, we obtain that
A\subset A_{0}
. Note that this argument also shows that if
A_{0}
is empty, then so is
A
.

Viceversa, let
x\in A_{0}
and fix
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
residual. Then there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that

\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}\}\subset\Lambda^{\prime}

Hence

x\in\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}}A_{\lambda}}\subset\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}}A_{\lambda}}

By the arbitrariness of
\Lambda^{\prime}
, we obtain that
A_{0}\subset A
. Again, note that this argument also shows that if
A
is empty, then so is
A_{0}
.

Therefore, in all cases,
A=A_{0}
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be an open subset and let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net of closed subsets of
U
. Assume that every
A_{\lambda}
is a
q
-local maximum set and that
\{A_{\lambda}\}
does not escape to infinity (relatively to
U
). Then

A\coloneqq\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\,\!\!^{U}A_{\lambda}

is a closed
q
-local maximum set.

Proof.

First of all, note that
A
is non-empty by Remark
3.1
. By Lemma
3.2
, we have that

A=\bigcap_{\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda}\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}}A_{\lambda}}^{U}

Note that

\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}_{0}}A_{\lambda}}^{U}\subset\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}}A_{\lambda}}^{U}\quad\text{whenever }\lambda^{\prime}_{0}\geq\lambda_{0}

Therefore, by Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
, we conclude.
∎

Now we can finally prove the main statement of this section.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net of
q
-local maximum sets in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. For every
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let
B_{\lambda}\coloneqq\overline{A_{\lambda}}\smallsetminus A_{\lambda}
and assume that
\{B_{\lambda}\}
escapes to infinity but
\{A_{\lambda}\}
does not. Then

A\coloneqq\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}

is a closed subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
with the
q
-local maximum property.

Proof.

Let
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be an open ball of radius
R>0
centred at the origin. To conclude, it is sufficient to show that
A\cap B_{R}
is a
q
-local maximum set, provided it is non-empty. Note that the non-emptiness assumption happens if we choose
R>0
large enough such that
\overline{B_{R}}
intersects infinitely many
A_{\lambda}
’s, which is possible since
\{A_{\lambda}\}
does not escape to infinity.

By Lemma
3.2
, we have that

B_{R}\cap A=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\biggl(B_{R}\cap\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}A_{\lambda^{\prime}}}\biggr)

Choose
\lambda_{0}
such that
\overline{B_{R}}\cap B_{\lambda}=\emptyset
for every
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, which exists since
\{B_{\lambda}\}
escapes to infinity. Note that

B_{R}\cap A=\bigcap_{\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}}\biggl(B_{R}\cap\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}A_{\lambda^{\prime}}}\biggr)

We claim that

(
\star
)

B_{R}\cap\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}A_{\lambda^{\prime}}}=\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}B_{R}\cap A_{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{B_{R}}\quad\forall\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}

where
\overline{\cdot}^{B_{R}}
denotes the closure in
B_{R}
. Fix
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
. Clearly, we have that

\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}B_{R}\cap A_{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{B_{R}}=\overline{B_{R}\cap\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}A_{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{B_{R}}\subset\overline{B_{R}}^{B_{R}}\cap\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}A_{\lambda^{\prime}}}^{B_{R}}\subset B_{R}\cap\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}A_{\lambda^{\prime}}}

Viceversa, let

z\in B_{R}\cap\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}A_{\lambda^{\prime}}}

Then there exists a sequence
\{z_{n}\}\subset\bigcup_{\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda}A_{\lambda^{\prime}}
which converges to
z
. Since
z\in B_{R}
, then there exists
n_{0}
such that
z_{n}\in B_{R}
for every
n\geq n_{0}
. Hence

C\subset Z_{0,I}^{j}\cap\tilde{V}=\tilde{Z}_{0,I}^{j}

Note that

, since

. On the other hand,

. Thus

. On the other hand, note that

bC=\overline{C}\smallsetminus C=\biggl(\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in[\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime}]}b\Gamma^{\prime}_{\alpha,j}\biggr)\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime},j}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime},j}

Hence

(21)

bC\subset\overline{\Gamma^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\Delta_{r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime}}^{n}\times\Delta_{\delta}\times\Delta_{\sigma})}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime},j}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime},j}

Therefore

\max_{C}\psi\geq\psi(\bar{z},0,\bar{\zeta}_{j})=0>\max_{bC}\psi

by (

), (
20
) and (

). Thus
\tilde{Z}_{0,I}^{j}
is not an
(n-1)
-local maximum set, since

and

(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic function defined on a neighbourhood

\overline{C}
. This is a contradiction.

Case
\alpha_{0}\in bI

Without loss of generality, we can assume that

is the left endpoint of

\alpha_{0}

\ell^{\prime}_{\alpha}\subset\Delta_{r^{\prime}}^{n}\times\Delta_{\delta}

g_{\alpha,j}(\ell^{\prime}_{\alpha})\subset\Delta_{\sigma}
for all

Let us define

\Omega\coloneqq(\ell_{\alpha_{0}}\cup\ell_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}})\cap(\Delta_{r}^{n}\times\Delta_{\delta})

and let

h\equiv 1&\text{on $\ell_{\alpha_{0}}$}\end{cases}

Then

By (

C\subset Z_{0,I}^{j}\cap\tilde{V}=\tilde{Z}_{0,I}^{j}

(26)

bC=\overline{C}\smallsetminus C\subset\overline{\Gamma^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\Delta_{r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime}}^{n}\times\Delta_{\delta}\times\Delta_{\sigma})}\cup\Gamma^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime},j}

Therefore the maps

5.
Some applications

In this section, we gather some applications of Theorem
4.1
.

First of all, we show that we can obtain a foliation on an
n
-pseudoconcave subset of a
(2n+1)
-dimensional real submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, of some regularity.

Corollary 5.1
.

For
1\leq n<N
, let
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a
(2n+1)
-dimensional real
\mathscr{C}^{2}
submanifold and let
Z\subset S
be an
n
-pseudoconcave subset. Then
Z
is foliated by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.

Proof.

Fix
\xi_{0}\in Z\subset S
. Since
S
is a
(2n+1)
-dimensional
\mathscr{C}^{2}
real submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, then there exists a neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
of
\xi_{0}
and
\mathscr{C}^{2}
functions
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}:U\to\mathbb{R}
(where
k=2N-2n-1>0
) such that

S\cap U=\{r_{1}=\dots=r_{k}=0\}

Let us define
\varphi\coloneqq r_{1}^{2}+\dots+r_{k}^{2}:U\to\mathbb{R}
. Then
\varphi
is a
\mathscr{C}^{2}
function. Note that

\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\bar{\xi}_{j}}=\sum_{l=1}^{k}2\frac{\partial r_{l}}{\partial\xi_{i}}\frac{\partial r_{l}}{\partial\bar{\xi}_{j}}+2r_{l}\frac{\partial^{2}r_{l}}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\bar{\xi}_{j}}

and then, for all
\xi\in S\cap U
,

\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\bar{\xi}_{j}}(\xi)=2\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{\partial r_{l}}{\partial\xi_{i}}(\xi)\frac{\partial r_{l}}{\partial\bar{\xi}_{j}}(\xi)

Thus

(\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi)_{\xi}=2\sum_{l=1}^{k}(\mathrm{d}r_{l}\wedge\mathrm{d^{c}}r_{l})_{\xi}

is a positive semidefinite form. In particular,
(\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi)_{\xi}
is null on
H_{\xi}S
and positive definite on
H_{\xi}S^{\perp}=\mathbb{C}^{N}/H_{\xi}S
, where
H_{\xi}S
denotes the holomorphic tangent space to
S
at
\xi
.

Suppose that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(H_{\xi}S)<n
. Let
\operatorname{L}_{\varphi}
be the Levi matrix of
\varphi
and

\sigma(\operatorname{L}_{\varphi}(\xi))=\{\alpha_{1}(\xi),\dots,\alpha_{N}(\xi)\}

be its relative spectrum in
\xi
(with repetitions allowed). By the assumption,

\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,N\}\mid\alpha_{j}(\xi)=0\bigr\}\leq n-1

Let

J_{\xi}\coloneqq\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,N\}\mid\alpha_{j}(\xi)>0\bigr\}

Then
\#J_{\xi}\geq N-n+1
. Note that the
\alpha_{j}
’s are continuous functions in
\xi
, since the entries of
\operatorname{L}_{\varphi}
are continuous. Hence there exists an open neighbourhood
V_{\xi}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
of
\xi
such that
\alpha_{j}(\xi^{\prime})>0
for all
\xi^{\prime}\in V_{\xi}
and for all
j\in J_{\xi}
. Let

V\coloneqq\bigcup_{\xi\in S\cap U}V_{\xi}

Then
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
is an open neighbourhood of
S\cap U
. At most shrinking
U
, we can assume that
V=U
. By construction, we have that, for every
\xi^{\prime}\in U
,
\alpha_{j}(\xi^{\prime})>0
for at least
N-n+1
indices. Hence
\varphi
is strictly
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic on
U
. Then we can find a non-negative function
u\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(U)
such that

u(\xi_{0})>u(\xi)\quad\forall\xi\in(Z\cap U)\smallsetminus\{\xi_{0}\}

and
\varphi+u
is still
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic on
U
. Since
\varphi|_{S\cap U}\equiv 0
, then

(\varphi+u)(\xi_{0})>(\varphi+u)(\xi)\quad\forall\xi\in(Z\cap U)\smallsetminus\{\xi_{0}\}

and thus
Z\cap U
is not an
(n-1)
-local maximum set. This is a contradiction. Therefore
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(H_{\xi}S)=n
, for all
\xi\in S\cap U
.

In particular, there exists a complex linear coordinate system in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
with respect to which

T_{\xi_{0}}S=\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

Hence there exists an open ball
B\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
(centred at the origin), an open neighbourhood
U^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
of
\xi_{0}
and a
\mathscr{C}^{2}
function
g:B\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
(where
p=N-n-1\geq 0
) such that
S\cap U^{\prime}=\Gamma(g)
. In particular,
Z_{0}\coloneqq Z\cap U^{\prime}
is the graph of
g|_{X_{0}}
, for some
X_{0}\subset B
closed. Therefore, by Theorem
4.1
,
Z
is foliated by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.
∎

Furthermore, we prove a statement analogous to Corollary
5.1
, but under weaker hypotheses of regularity on
S
. To achieve this, we will use some of the results obtained in Section
3
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a subset such that for each
\xi_{0}\in S
there exists a neighbourhood
W\subset S
of
\xi_{0}
and a real linear coordinate system with respect to which
W
is the graph of a differentiable function
g:B\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
, with the identification
\mathbb{C}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{2N}=\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}_{y}
(for
1\leq n<N
and
q=2N-2n-1>0
), where
\pi:\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is the projection and where
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is an open ball centred at
x_{0}=\pi(\xi_{0})
. For each
\xi=(x,g(x))\in W
, set

T_{\xi}W\coloneqq\Gamma\bigl(Dg(x)\bigr)

where
Dg(x):\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
denotes the differential of
g
at
x
. At most shrinking
W
, assume that

(27)

\#\bigl\{\xi\in W\mid T_{\xi}W\cap(T_{\xi_{0}}W)^{\perp}\neq\{0\}\bigr\}<+\infty

Remark 5.1
.

If
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a
(2n+1)
-dimensional real
\mathscr{C}^{1}
submanifold, then it satisfies all these properties.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a subset as above. If
Z\subset S
is an
n
-pseudoconcave subset (for
1\leq n<N
), then it is foliated by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.

Proof.

Fix
\xi_{0}\in Z\subset S
. By assumption, there exists an open neighbourhood
W\subset S
of
\xi_{0}
and a differentiable function
g:B\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
such that
W=\Gamma(g)
, where
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is an open ball centred at
x_{0}=\pi(\xi_{0})
.

By construction, we have that
W=S\cap(B\times\mathbb{R}^{q})
and thus

Z_{0}\coloneqq W\cap Z=(B\times\mathbb{R}^{q})\cap Z

is relatively open in
Z
. In particular,
Z_{0}
is an
(n-1)
-local maximum set.

Consider the set
\Lambda=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid\lambda>0\}
and define on
\Lambda
the preorder

\lambda\preceq\lambda^{\prime}\iff\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda

Then
(\Lambda,\preceq)
is a directed set. For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let us define

Z_{0,\lambda}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\lambda}(Z_{0}-\xi_{0})

Since
Z_{0}
is an
(n-1)
-local maximum set, then so is
Z_{0,\lambda}
. Therefore
\{Z_{0,\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a net of
(n-1)
-local maximum sets in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

Note that
0\in Z_{0,\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and thus
0\in\limsup_{\lambda}Z_{0,\lambda}
. In particular,
\{Z_{0,\lambda}\}
does not escape to infinity, by Remark
3.1
. Now, for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let us define
Y_{\lambda}\coloneqq\overline{Z_{0,\lambda}}\smallsetminus Z_{0,\lambda}
. We want to prove that
\{Y_{\lambda}\}
escapes to infinity. First of all, set
Y\coloneqq\overline{Z_{0}}\smallsetminus Z_{0}
and note that

Y_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}(Y-\xi_{0})

Since
Z_{0}\subset B\times\mathbb{R}^{q}
is closed and contained in
\Gamma(g)
, then
Y\subset bB\times\mathbb{R}^{q}
. Note that, if
r>0
is the radius of the ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
, then
\lVert\tilde{z}-\xi_{0}\rVert\geq r
for all
\tilde{z}\in bB\times\mathbb{R}^{q}
. Hence

\lVert z\rVert=\frac{1}{\lambda}\lVert\tilde{z}-\xi_{0}\rVert\geq\frac{r}{\lambda}\quad\forall z=\frac{1}{\lambda}(\tilde{z}-\xi_{0})\in Y_{\lambda}

and thus

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{dist}(0,Y_{\lambda})=+\infty

Therefore
\{Y_{\lambda}\}
escapes to infinity, by Remark
3.2
.

Thus the contingent cone to
Z_{0}
at
\xi_{0}

T^{+}_{\xi_{0}}Z_{0}\coloneqq\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}Z_{0,\lambda}

is a closed
(n-1)
-local maximum set, by Proposition
3.5
. On the other hand, we can consider the contingent cone to
W
at
\xi_{0}

T^{+}_{\xi_{0}}W\coloneqq\limsup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{1}{\lambda}(W-\xi_{0})

Since
Z_{0}\subset W
, then
T_{\xi_{0}}^{+}Z_{0}\subset T_{\xi_{0}}^{+}W
. Moreover,
T_{\xi_{0}}^{+}W\subset T_{\xi_{0}}W
, by
[
2
, Proposition 2.3]
. Hence
T_{\xi_{0}}^{+}Z_{0}\subset T_{\xi_{0}}W
and thus

T_{\xi_{0}}W=\bigcup_{v\in T_{\xi_{0}}W}(v+T_{\xi_{0}}^{+}Z_{0})

Therefore
T_{\xi_{0}}W
has the
(n-1)
-local maximum property. By Lemma
2.4
, we obtain that

T_{\xi_{0}}W=\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

In particular, we also obtain that
(T_{\xi_{0}}W)^{\perp}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
, for
p=N-n-1\geq 0
.

Let
\pi_{1}:\mathbb{C}^{N}\to T_{\xi_{0}}W
and
\pi_{2}:\mathbb{C}^{N}\to(T_{\xi_{0}}W)^{\perp}
be two orthogonal projections, and let
\varpi_{1}\coloneqq\pi_{1}|_{W}
and
\varpi_{2}\coloneqq\pi_{2}|_{W}
. Moreover, consider the homeomorphism

G:B\to W,\quad G(x)=\bigl(x,g(x)\bigr)

Note that both
G
and
G^{-1}
are differentiable maps, since
g
is so. Let us define

F:B\to T_{\xi_{0}}W,\quad F\coloneqq\varpi_{1}\circ G

Then
F
is a differentiable map. Note that, for each
x\in B
,
DF(x)=D\varpi_{1}(\xi)\circ DG(x)
, where
\xi=G(x)
. Since both
G
and
G^{-1}
are differentiable maps, then
DG(x)
is invertible. On the other hand, we have that
D\varpi_{1}(\xi)=\pi_{1}|_{T_{\xi}W}
. By (
27
), we obtain that
D\varpi_{1}
has at most finitely many critical points. Hence also
DF
has at most finitely many critical points. Therefore, by a version of the Inverse Function Theorem for differentiable maps (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
), there exists a neighbourhood
U\subset B
of
x_{0}
and a neighbourhood
V\subset T_{\xi_{0}}W
of
0
such that
F|_{U}:U\to V
is a homeomorphism. Set
H\coloneqq(F|_{U})^{-1}:V\to U
.

Let us define

f:V\to(T_{\xi_{0}}W)^{\perp},\quad f\coloneqq\varpi_{2}\circ G\circ H

Then
f
is continuous. Set
\tilde{W}\coloneqq G(U)
. Then
\tilde{W}
is an open subset of
W
. Clearly, for each
\xi\in\tilde{W}
we have that
\xi=(\varpi_{1}(\xi),\varpi_{2}(\xi))
and, moreover, there exists a unique
x\in U
such that
\xi=G(x)
. Note that

\varpi_{1}(\xi)=\varpi_{1}\bigl(G(x)\bigr)=F(x)=\nu

for a unique
\nu\in V
. On the other hand,

\varpi_{2}(\xi)=\varpi_{2}\bigl(G(x)\bigr)=\varpi_{2}\bigl(G(H(\nu))\bigr)=f(\nu)

Thus
\xi=(\nu,f(\nu))
for a unique
\nu\in V
. Therefore
\tilde{W}=\Gamma(f)
.

Hence we have obtained a continuous function
f:V\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
, where
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is an open ball centred at the origin, such that
\tilde{W}=\Gamma(f)
. In particular,
\tilde{Z}_{0}\coloneqq Z_{0}\cap\tilde{W}
is the graph of
f|_{X_{0}}
, for some
X_{0}\subset V
closed. Therefore, by Theorem
4.1
,
Z
is foliated by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.
∎

We conclude with an immediate consequence of Corollary
5.2
. Before stating it, let us recall the notion of Zariski tangent space in the
\mathscr{C}^{k}
category (for
k\geq 1
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
M
be a
\mathscr{C}^{k}
manifold (for
k\geq 1
) and let

be an arbitrary subset. Let

\mathcal{I}_{Z}^{k}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathscr{C}^{k}(M)\mid f|_{Z}\equiv 0\}

For each

T_{p}^{k}Z\coloneqq\{v\in T_{p}M\mid\mathrm{d}_{p}f(v)=0\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{I}_{Z}^{k}\}

is called

at

, of class
\mathscr{C}^{k}
.

Remark 5.2
.

It is straightforward to prove that the dimension of

is the minimum
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
Z
is locally contained, near
p
, in an embedded
n
-dimensional
\mathscr{C}^{k}
submanifold
N
of

and that, in this case,

(see
[
4
, Proposition 2.6(c)]
).

By Remark
5.2
and Corollary
5.2
, we immediately obtain the following (recall also Remark
5.1
).

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be an
n
-pseudoconcave subset (for
1\leq n<N
) such that

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(T_{p}^{k}Z)=2n+1\quad\forall p\in Z

(for

). Then

is foliated by
n
-dimensional complex manifolds.

6.
Sharpness of the index of pseudoconcavity

In this final section, we show that the index of pseudoconcavity found in Theorem
4.1
is sharp. Namely, for
m<n
we construct an
m
-pseudoconcave subset of a smooth graph, defined over
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, which has no analytic structure.

To this end, let
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{q}
be a holomorphic function, for
n\geq 2
and
q\geq 1
, and let
\pi:\mathbb{C}^{n+q}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the projection onto the first
n
coordinates. Then
\tilde{\pi}\coloneqq\pi|_{\Gamma(f)}
is a biholomorphism. Now let
E\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the closed subset given by
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
. Then
E
has no analytic structure and
\mathbb{C}^{n}\smallsetminus E
is pseudoconvex, i.e.,
E
is an
(n-1)
-pseudoconcave subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let

Z\coloneqq\Gamma(f|_{E})

Then
Z
is clearly a closed subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n+q}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The subset
Z
has no analytic structure.

Proof.

By contradiction, suppose that there exists an analytic variety
A\subset Z
, of positive dimension. Let
B\coloneqq\tilde{\pi}(A)
. Then
B\subset\tilde{\pi}(Z)=E
and
B
is an analytic variety of positive dimension, since
\tilde{\pi}
is a biholomorphism. This contradicts the fact that
E
has no analytic structure.
∎

Now recall that
E
is an
(n-1)
-pseudoconcave subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, i.e., it has the
(n-2)
-local maximum property in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The closed subset
Z
is an
(n-2)
-local maximum set in
\mathbb{C}^{n+q}
.

Proof.

By contradiction, suppose that there exists
p_{0}\in Z
such that for each neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+q}
of
p_{0}
there exists a compact subset
K\subset U
, with
Z\cap K\neq\emptyset
compact, and a smooth
(n-2)
-plurisubharmonic function
\varphi:U\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\max_{Z\cap K}\varphi>\max_{Z\cap bK}\varphi

where
bK
denotes the topological boundary of
K
. In particular, there exists
\xi_{0}\in Z\cap\mathring{K}
such that

\varphi(\xi_{0})>\varphi(\xi)\quad\forall\xi\in Z\cap bK

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+q}
be a neighbourhood of
p_{0}
, with coordinates
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},w_{1},\dots,w_{q})
, such that

\Gamma(f)\cap U=\{w_{1}=\dots=w_{q}=0\}

Take
K,\varphi,\xi_{0}
relative to this
U
.

Set
V\coloneqq\pi(U)
. Then
V
is an open neighbourhood of
z_{0}\coloneqq\pi(\xi_{0})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Define

\tilde{\varphi}:V\to\mathbb{R},\quad\tilde{\varphi}(z)=\varphi(z,0)

(here we are denoting as
(z,w)
the coordinates on
U
). Then
\tilde{\varphi}
is a smooth function. We want to prove the
\tilde{\varphi}
is also
(n-2)
-plurisubharmonic. Note that, for each
z\in V
,

\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\varphi}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}}(z)=\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}}(z,0)\quad\forall j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}

and thus
\mathrm{L}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(z)=(\mathrm{L}_{\varphi}(z,0))^{n}_{n}
, where
\mathrm{L}_{\psi}
denotes the Levi matrix of a function
\psi
and
A^{n}_{n}\in\Mat(n\times n)
denotes the principal minor of a matrix
A\in\Mat(N\times N)
given by the first
n
rows and
n
columns, i.e.,

\mathrm{L}_{\varphi}(z,0)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{L}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(z)&*\\
*&*\end{pmatrix}

For
\xi\in U
, let

\sigma\bigl(\mathrm{L}_{\varphi}(\xi)\bigr)=\{\alpha_{1}(\xi),\dots,\alpha_{n+q}(\xi)\}

be the spectrum of
\mathrm{L}_{\varphi}(\xi)
(with repetitions allowed), ordered decreasingly. Since
\varphi
is
(n-2)
-plurisubharmonic, then
\mathrm{L}_{\varphi}(\xi)
has at most
n-2
negative eigenvalues and thus
\alpha_{q+2}(\xi)\geq 0
. Now, for
z\in V
, let

\sigma\bigl(\mathrm{L}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(z)\bigr)=\{\beta_{1}(z),\dots,\beta_{n}(z)\}

be the spectrum of
\mathrm{L}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(z)
(with repetitions allowed), ordered decreasingly. If

A\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{I}_{n}\\
\boldsymbol{0}_{q\times n}\end{pmatrix}\in\Mat_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl((n+q)\times n\bigr)

then
A^{\dagger}A=\mathrm{I}_{n}
and
A^{\dagger}\mathrm{L}_{\varphi}(z,0)A=\mathrm{L}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(z)
, where
A^{\dagger}
denotes the conjugate transpose of
A
. Thus, by Poincaré Separation Theorem (see
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
),

\beta_{2}(z)\geq\alpha_{q+2}(z,0)\geq 0

Therefore
\mathrm{L}_{\tilde{\varphi}}(z)
has at most
n-2
negative eigenvalues. By the arbitrariness of
z\in V
, we conclude that
\tilde{\varphi}
is an
(n-2)
-plurisubharmonic function.

Since
\xi_{0}\in Z\cap\mathring{K}\subset\Gamma(f)\cap U
, then
\xi_{0}=(z_{0},0)
. Analogously, for each
\xi\in Z\cap bK
we have that
\xi=(z,0)
for a unique
z\in\pi(Z)=E
. By assumption, we have that

(28)

\tilde{\varphi}(z_{0})=\varphi(\xi_{0})>\varphi(\xi)=\tilde{\varphi}(z)\quad\forall z\in\pi(Z\cap bK)

Take a compact subset
C\subset V
such that
z_{0}\in E\cap\mathring{C}
and
E\cap bC\subset\pi(Z\cap bK)
. By (
28
), we obtain that

\max_{E\cap C}\tilde{\varphi}\geq\tilde{\varphi}(z_{0})>\max_{E\cap bC}\tilde{\varphi}

Therefore
E
does not have the
(n-2)
-local maximum property. This is a contradiction.
∎

We have thus constructed, given a holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{q}
, an
(n-1)
-pseudoconcave subset
Z
of
\Gamma(f)
with no analytic structure. Now consider the identification
\mathbb{C}^{n+q}=\mathbb{C}^{n}_{z}\times\mathbb{C}_{w}\times\mathbb{C}^{q-1}_{\zeta}
and let

\varpi:\mathbb{C}^{q}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{q-1},\quad\varpi(w,\zeta)=\bigl(\Impart(w),\zeta\bigr)

be the projection. Define

g:\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{q-1},\quad g(z,\lambda)=\varpi\bigl(f(z)\bigr)

Clearly,
g
is a smooth function. Writing
f=(f_{1},f_{2}):\mathbb{C}^{n}_{z}\to\mathbb{C}_{w}\times\mathbb{C}^{q-1}_{\zeta}
, we have that

g(z,\lambda)=\varpi\bigl(f_{1}(z),f_{2}(z)\bigr)=\bigl(\Impart(f_{1}(z)),f_{2}(z)\bigr)

Hence
\Gamma(f)\subset\Gamma(g)
. On the other hand, let
h:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function such that
h|_{E}\equiv 0
and
h|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\smallsetminus E}>0
. We can take
h
such that all its derivatives are null on
E
. Define

\tilde{h}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{q-1},\quad\tilde{h}(z,\lambda)=\bigl(h(z),0\bigr)

and let

\tilde{g}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{q-1},\quad\tilde{g}=g+\tilde{h}

Then
\tilde{g}
is a smooth function.

Lemma 6.3
.

The graphs
\Gamma(\tilde{g})
and
\Gamma(f)
intersect exactly at
Z
.

Proof.

Note that every
\xi\in\Gamma(\tilde{g})
is of the form
(\eta,\tilde{g}(\eta))
for a unique
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand, every
\xi\in\Gamma(f)
is of the form
(z,f_{1}(z),f_{2}(z))
for a unique
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Hence
\xi\in\Gamma(\tilde{g})\cap\Gamma(f)
if and only if
\eta=(z,\Realpart(f_{1}(z)))
and
\tilde{g}(\eta)=(\Impart(f_{1}(z)),f_{2}(z))
. Note that

\displaystyle\tilde{g}\bigl(z,\Realpart(f_{1}(z))\bigr)

\displaystyle=g\bigl(z,\Realpart(f_{1}(z))\bigr)+\tilde{h}\bigl(z,\Realpart(f_{1}(z))\bigr)

\displaystyle=\varpi\bigl(f(z)\bigr)+\bigl(h(z),0\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\Impart(f_{1}(z))+h(z),f_{2}(z)\bigr)

Thus
\xi
is in the intersection if and only if
h(z)=0
. This holds if and only if
z\in E
, i.e.,
\xi\in Z
. Therefore
\Gamma(\tilde{g})\cap\Gamma(f)=Z
.
∎

Therefore we have constructed a smooth function
\tilde{g}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{p}
(for
n\geq 2
and
p\geq 0
) and a closed
(n-1)
-pseudoconcave subset
Z\subset\Gamma(\tilde{g})
which has no analytic structure. Note that we cannot find a holomorphic function
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{q}
such that
Z\subset\Gamma(\tilde{f})\subset\Gamma(\tilde{g})
, as it happens, for example, with
\Gamma(g)
.

We have thus proved that the index of pseudoconcavity stated in Theorem
4.1
is sharp. Namely, let
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be an
m
-pseudoconcave subset which is locally the graph of a continuous function over a closed subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. Recall the following fact.

Remark 6.1
.

Since
Z
is
m
-pseudoconcave, then it is also
k
-pseudoconcave, for every
k\leq m
.

Therefore we have two cases:

•

if
m\geq n
, then
Z
is
n
-pseudoconcave and thus it is foliated, by Theorem
4.1
;

•

if
m<n
, then
Z
can have no analytic structure, as shown above.
