A Gödel Modal Logic Over Witnessed Models
Mauro Ferrari ∗, Camillo Fiorentini, Paolo Giardini, Ricardo Oscar Rodriguez

Abstract
We introduce 𝐆𝐖 \mathbf{GW} , a Gödel modal logic based on Kripke models in which the value of each modal formula is witnessed by an accessible world. This witnessed semantics eliminates the limit-based phenomena that preclude the finite model property in the usual Kripke semantics for Gödel modal logics, thereby yielding a more constructive semantic framework. We present a sound and complete refutation calculus for 𝐆𝐖 \mathbf{GW} and design a terminating backward proof-search procedure with countermodel generation. As a direct consequence of this procedure, 𝐆𝐖 \mathbf{GW} enjoys the finite model property.



1
Introduction

Gödel modal logics,
\mathbf{GK}
s, provide a natural framework for reasoning
about graded notions of incompleteness, uncertainty, and vagueness by
means of necessity and possibility operators evaluated over truth
degrees. When interpreted over the real unit interval, Gödel logic
captures a well-behaved notion of implication based on residuation,
which makes it particularly suitable for applications in knowledge
representation, as well as in multi-agent and distributed systems.

In general, the semantics of
\mathbf{GK}
is based on Gödel Kripke models
(
\mathrm{G}
-models
), a natural generalization of classical Kripke
semantics in which both the valuation of propositions at each world
and the accessibility relation take values in the standard Gödel
algebra
[0,1]
, i.e. the algebraic semantics of the intermediate
Gödel–Dummett Logic. The minimal logics over
\mathrm{G}
-models were
thoroughly investigated by Caicedo and Rodríguez
in
[
6
,
7
]
. In this setting, the interpretation
of the modal operators is defined by means of infima and suprema
computed over (possibly infinite) sets of accessible worlds. However,
these bounds need not be attained: the infimum may be strictly smaller
than every truth value in the set, and dually for the
supremum. Consequently, the truth value of a modal formula may depend
on limit constructions rather than on concrete worlds of the model.
This phenomenon has significant logical consequences. For instance,

[
6
]
shows that the formula
\Box\neg\neg\varphi\to\neg\neg\Box\varphi
is valid in all finite Gödel Kripke models,
but fails in a suitable infinite model (see
Fig.
1
). Hence, the underlying logic does not enjoy the
finite model property, and semantic arguments may require genuinely
infinitary behaviour.

Witnessed models provide a principled way to overcome these
difficulties. By requiring that the relevant infima and suprema be
realized by actual accessible worlds, witnessed semantics ensures that
existential and universal behaviour is supported by explicit
witnesses. In this way, the evaluation of modal operators more closely
mirrors the behaviour of guarded quantification in first-order logic
and restores a constructive reading of the semantics. Each modal
statement is thus justified by concrete worlds rather than by purely
asymptotic approximations.

The notion of witnessed models has appeared in several related
contexts. For example, in
[
8
]
an axiomatization is given for
witnessed semantics in fuzzy predicate logics. Since quantified
statements can often be translated into modal ones, this connection is
conceptually well grounded. Along these lines, Hájek considers
in
[
14
]
the axioms
\Diamond(\varphi\to\Box\varphi)
and

\Diamond(\Diamond\varphi\to\varphi)
, and proves that the resulting
logic
S5(G)^{w}
is strongly complete with respect to witnessed
universal Kripke models.

Witnessed semantics has also proved fruitful in applied settings. In
the context of Fuzzy Description Logics,
[
5
]
studies a
system based on witnessed Gödel semantics and shows that it enjoys
the finite model property and a decidable satisfiability problem with
exponential-time complexity. More recently,
[
19
]
provides an
axiomatization for Gödel modal logic interpreted over witnessed
Kripke models.

In general, witnessed models tend to ensure compactness-like properties and
often yield the finite model property and decidability
results. These features are crucial for automated reasoning,
since they allow infinitary semantic arguments to be replaced by
finite combinatorial ones. For these reasons, studying Gödel modal
logics over witnessed models not only clarifies their semantic
foundations, but also strengthens their connections with well-behaved
fragments of first-order logic that play a central role in computer
science, thereby enhancing both their theoretical robustness and their
applicability.

From a proof-theoretic perspective, witnessed semantics aligns more
closely with syntactic calculi.
The existence of witnesses enables canonical model constructions that
are finite or finitely generated, supports filtration techniques, and
allows completeness proofs to proceed by standard algebraic or
canonical methods. Therefore, establishing a sound and complete
calculus for Gödel modal logics over witnessed models is not only
feasible but also conceptually justified, providing a proof-theoretic
counterpart to their improved semantic behaviour and paving the way for
effective automated reasoning procedures.

In line with the above considerations, in
[
12
]
we
introduced the logic
\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}
characterized by witnessed crisp Gödel
Kripke models, where “crisp” means that the accessibility relation
takes values in the set
\{0,1\}
. For this logic, we developed a
constraint-based refutation calculus inspired by the tableau system
of
[
4
]
, together with a terminating proof-search
procedure that yields finite countermodels whenever proof search
fails. As a consequence, we established that
\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}
enjoys the finite
model property. In the present work, we extend these results to the
logic
\mathbf{GW}
, characterized by witnessed Gödel Kripke models without
the crispness restriction. Our development follows the
strategy adopted in
[
12
]
. We introduce a
constraint-based refutation calculus for
\mathbf{GW}
, called
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GW}}
, and
prove its soundness and completeness with respect to witnessed
models. Moreover, we design a terminating proof-search procedure for

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GW}}
based on a standard backward search strategy. Notably, when
proof search fails, the computation can be traced to construct a
finite countermodel for the given formula, and this proves the finite
model property for
\mathbf{GW}
. Finally, we provide an implementation of
the procedure, called
gwref
(available
at
[
11
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha
be a formula of
\mathbf{K}
.

(i)

If
\alpha\in\mathbf{K}
then
\alpha^{*}\in\mathbf{GK}
.

(ii)

If
\alpha^{*}\in\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}
then
\alpha\in\mathbf{K}
.

Proof.

The proof of point
(ii)
is immediate, since

\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}\subseteq\mathbf{K}
and the formula
\alpha\leftrightarrow\alpha^{*}
is valid in
\mathbf{K}
.
We prove point
(i)
. Let us assume that
\alpha^{*}\not\in\mathbf{GK}
.
There exists a
\mathrm{G}
-model
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,R,e\rangle
and a world
w
in
W
such that
e(w,\alpha^{*})<1
.
Let
\mathfrak{M}_{1}=\langle W,R_{1},e_{1}\rangle
be the
\mathrm{K}
-model defined as follows:

R_{1}(w,w^{\prime})\;=\;\begin{cases}1&\text{if $R(w,w^{\prime})>0$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\hskip 40.00006pte_{1}(w,p)\;=\;\begin{cases}1&\text{if $e(w,p)>0$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Let
w\in W
and let
\varphi
be any formula of
\mathbf{K}
; by induction on the size of
\varphi
, we prove that:

(A)

e_{1}(w,\varphi)=e(w,\varphi^{*})
.

The case
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{\bot\}
is immediate.
Let
\varphi=\Box\psi
;
note that
\varphi^{*}=\Box\psi^{*}
.
Assume
e_{1}(w,\Box\psi)=0
.
There exists
w^{\prime}\in W
such that
R_{1}(w,w^{\prime})=1
and
e_{1}(w^{\prime},\psi)=0
.
Then,
R(w,w^{\prime})>0
and, by the induction hypothesis,
e(w^{\prime},\psi^{*})=0
.
It follows that
R(w,w^{\prime})\to e(w^{\prime},\psi^{*})=0
, hence
e(w,\Box\psi^{*})=0
.
Let
e_{1}(w,\Box\psi)=1
and assume, by contradiction,
e(w,\Box\psi^{*})<1
.
By the induction hypothesis, for every
w^{\prime}\in W
it holds that
e(w^{\prime},\psi^{*})\in\{0,1\}
,
hence
R(w,w^{\prime})\to e(w^{\prime},\psi^{*})\in\{0,1\}
and
e(w,\Box\psi^{*})\in\{0,1\}
.
Thus,
e(w,\Box\psi^{*})=0
and
there is
w^{\prime}\in W
such that
R(w,w^{\prime})\to e(w^{\prime},\psi^{*})=0
.
We get
R(w,w^{\prime})>0
and
e(w^{\prime},\psi^{*})=0
,
hence
R_{1}(w,w^{\prime})=1
and
e_{1}(w^{\prime},\psi)=0
,
contradicting the assumption
e_{1}(w,\Box\psi)=1
.
Since the assumption
e(w,\Box\psi^{*})<1
yields a contradiction,
we conclude
e(w,\Box\psi^{*})=1
.
The proof of
(A)
in the remaining cases easily follows from the induction hypothesis.
Since
e(w,\alpha^{*})<1
, from
(A)
it follows that

e_{1}(w,\alpha)=0
. Accordingly,
\alpha\not\in\mathbf{K}
,
and this proves
(i)
.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
2.1
, we get:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a formula of
\mathbf{K}
and let
L
be any of the logics
\mathbf{GK}
,
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{c}}
,
\mathbf{GW}
and
\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
Then,
\alpha\in\mathbf{K}
iff
\alpha^{*}\in L
.

This was established for

\mathrm{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}

(P1)

there is no
\Box

\Box p\leftrightarrow\varphi

\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}

(P2)

there is no
\lozenge
-free formula
\psi
such that

\lozenge p\leftrightarrow\psi
is valid in
\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}
.

Lemma 2.3

Let
\mathfrak{M}^{\ast}=\langle W,R,e\rangle

\mathrm{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}
-model defined in Fig.

(i)

For every
\Box
-free formula
\varphi

e(w_{0},\varphi)\in\{0,\,0.5,\,1\}

(ii)

For every
\lozenge
-free formula
\psi

e(w_{0},\psi)\in\{0,\,0.4,\,1\}

Proof.

One can easily prove that, for every formula
\alpha
, the following
facts hold:

(1)

e(w_{1},\alpha)\in\{0,\,0.4,\,1\}
.

(2)

e(w_{2},\alpha)=\Phi(e(w_{1},\alpha))
,
where
\Phi(0)=0
,
\Phi(0.4)=0.5
,
\Phi(1)=1
.

We prove
(i)
, by induction on the size of

\varphi
. If
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{\bot\}
, we have
e(w_{0},\varphi)=0
. The cases
\varphi=\alpha\land\beta
,
\varphi=\alpha\lor\beta
and

\varphi=\alpha\to\beta
easily follow from the induction hypothesis. Let

\varphi=\lozenge\alpha
. By
(1)

and
(2)
, one of the following
properties
(a)
–
(c)
holds:

(a)

e(w_{1},\alpha)=e(w_{2},\alpha)=0
.

(b)

e(w_{1},\alpha)=0.4
and
e(w_{2},\alpha)=0.5
.

(c)

e(w_{1},\alpha)=e(w_{2},\alpha)=1
.

In case
(a)
we get
e(w_{0},\lozenge\alpha)=0
, in
case
(b)

e(w_{0},\lozenge\alpha)=0.5
, in
case
(c)

e(w_{0},\lozenge\alpha)=1
; this concludes the
proof of
(i)
. The proof of
(ii)
is
similar.
∎

(i)
. This proves

holds. The proof of
(P2)
is similar.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

\Box
and
\lozenge
are not interdefinable in any modal logic

Following

4
,

, we introduce the
refutation calculus
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GW}}
for
\mathbf{GW}
, namely, a calculus to certify
that a formula is not valid in any
\mathrm{GW}
-model. The calculus
exploits the constraint language
\mathcal{L}_{c}
defined over a countable set
of labels, used to represent the worlds of a
\mathrm{GW}
-model. In the
definitions below

,
w^{\prime}
are labels,
\varphi
is a
formula,
p\in\mathcal{V}
,
R
is a designated relation symbol, representing
the accessibility relation.

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mbox{labelled formula}&\;\coloneqq&\;w:\varphi\\
\mbox{atomic c-term $t$}&\;\coloneqq&\;0~|~1~|~w:p~|~w:\bot~|~R(w,w^{\prime})\\[2.15277pt]
\mbox{c-term $u$}&\;\coloneqq&\;t~|~w:\varphi~|~R(w,w^{\prime})\land w^{\prime}:\varphi~|~R(w,w^{\prime})\to w^{\prime}:\varphi\\[2.15277pt]
\mbox{constraint $\chi$}&\;\coloneqq&\;u\,\blacktriangledown\,t\qquad\,\blacktriangledown\,\in\{\,<,\,\leq,\,>,\,\geq\,\}\end{array}

If
w:\varphi
occurs in a c-term

u
has label
w
.
A constraint of the form

is called
atomic
;
if
\chi=w:\varphi\,\blacktriangledown\,t
is non-atomic and
\sharp
is
the main connective of
\varphi

\chi
is a

\sharp
-constraint

Constraints where

Given a multiset of constraints

we denote the set of atomic constraints in
\Gamma

Let
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,R,e\rangle
be a
\mathrm{GW}
-model. An
\mathfrak{M}
-interpretation
of

\mathcal{L}_{c}

mapping labels of
\mathcal{L}_{c}

We extend

to c-terms as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{I}(k)=k,\;k\in\{0,1\}\qquad\mathcal{I}(w:\varphi)=e(\mathcal{I}(w),\varphi)\qquad\mathcal{I}\left(R(w,w^{\prime})\right)=R\left(\mathcal{I}(w),\mathcal{I}(w^{\prime})\right)\\[4.30554pt]
\mathcal{I}\left(R(w,w^{\prime})\star w^{\prime}:\varphi\right)=R\left(\mathcal{I}(w),\mathcal{I}(w^{\prime})\right)\,\star\,e\left(\mathcal{I}(w^{\prime}),\varphi\right),\;\star\in\{\land,\to\}\end{array}

Note that, for every c-term

,
\mathcal{I}(u)
belongs to
[0,1]
.
We introduce the relations

\models_{\mathcal{I}}
and
\models
, where
\mathfrak{M}
is a
\mathrm{GW}
-model,
\mathcal{I}
an

\mathfrak{M}
-interpretation,

•

\mathcal{I}(u)\,\blacktriangledown\,\mathcal{I}(t)
;

•

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}\Gamma

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}\chi

•

\mathfrak{M}\models\Gamma

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}\Gamma

\mathfrak{M}
-interpretation

.

A

is a function mapping each atomic c-term of

to a rational number in

is extended to all the atomic c-terms by setting

. Let

Note that
\sigma(\Gamma_{\mathrm{at}})
is a set of rational constraints of the form

and

To build a derivation of

We show that

(1)
.

Let
\mathrm{wg}(\chi)>0
and let us assume that
\chi\not\in\Gamma_{n}
.
Then, there exists
k\geq 0
such that

\Gamma_{k}\mapsto_{\chi}\Gamma_{k+1}
.
We proceed by a case analysis on the structure of
\chi
.

Let
\chi=w:\alpha\land\beta\rhd t
. Since
\Gamma_{k}\mapsto_{\chi}\Gamma_{k+1}
,
the applied rule is
\land\rhd
, hence
w:\alpha\rhd t\in\Gamma_{k+1}
and

w:\beta\rhd t\in\Gamma_{k+1}
. By the induction hypothesis

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\alpha\rhd t
and
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\beta\rhd t
, and
this implies
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\alpha\land\beta\rhd t
. The case
\chi=w:\alpha\lor\beta\lhd t
is similar.

Let
\chi=w:\alpha\land\beta\lhd t
. Since
\Gamma_{k}\mapsto_{\chi}\Gamma_{k+1}
,
the applied rule is
\land\lhd
, hence
w:\alpha\lhd t\in\Gamma_{k+1}
or

w:\beta\lhd t\in\Gamma_{k+1}
. By the induction hypothesis

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\alpha\lhd t
or
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\beta\lhd t
, hence

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\alpha\land\beta\lhd t
. The case
\chi=w:\alpha\lor\beta\rhd t
is similar.

Let
\chi=w:\alpha\to\beta<t
; we only consider the case

\beta\not\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{\bot\}
. Since
\Gamma_{k}\mapsto_{\chi}\Gamma_{k+1}
, the
applied rule is
\to<
, hence:

(A)

\{\,w:\alpha>w:q,\,w:\beta\leq w:q,\,w:q<t\,\}\subseteq\Gamma_{k+1}
.

By the induction hypothesis,
for every constraint
\chi^{\prime}
displayed in
(A)
,

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}\chi^{\prime}
, hence:

e(w,\alpha)>e(w,q)\qquad e(w,\beta)\leq e(w,q)\qquad e(w,q)<\mathcal{I}(t)

Since
e(w,\alpha)>e(w,\beta)
, we get
e(w,\alpha\to\beta)=e(w,\beta)
, hence
e(w,\alpha\to\beta)<\mathcal{I}(t)
,
which implies
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\alpha\to\beta<t
.

Let
\chi=w:\alpha\to\beta\leq t
; we only consider the case

\beta\not\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{\bot\}
. Since
\Gamma_{k}\mapsto_{\chi}\Gamma_{k+1}
, the
applied rule is
\to\leq
, hence one of the following two subcases
holds:

(B1)

t\geq 1\in\Gamma_{k+1}
;

(B2)

\{\,w:\alpha>w:q,\,w:\beta\leq w:q,\,w:q\leq t\,\}\subseteq\Gamma_{k+1}
.

Let us assume that
(B1)
holds. By the
induction hypothesis, we get
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}t\geq 1
, and this
implies
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\alpha\to\beta\leq t
. Let us assume
that
(B2)
holds. By the induction hypothesis,
for every constraint
\chi^{\prime}
in
(B2)
,

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}\chi^{\prime}
, hence:

e(w,\alpha)>e(w,q)\qquad e(w,\beta)\leq e(w,q)\qquad e(w,q)\leq\mathcal{I}(t)

Since
e(w,\alpha)>e(w,\beta)
, we get
e(w,\alpha\to\beta)=e(w,\beta)
, hence

e(w,\alpha\to\beta)\leq\mathcal{I}(t)
, which implies
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\alpha\to\beta\leq t
.

Let
\chi=w:\alpha\to\beta\rhd t
; we only consider the case

\beta\not\in\mathcal{V}\cup\{\bot\}
. Since
\Gamma_{k}\mapsto_{\chi}\Gamma_{k+1}
, the
applied rule is
\to\rhd
, hence one of the following two subcases
holds:

(C1)

\{\,w:\alpha\leq w:q,\,w:\beta\geq w:q,\,1\rhd t\,\}\subseteq\Gamma_{k+1}
;

(C2)

w:\beta\rhd t\in\Gamma_{k+1}
.

Assume
(C1)
. By the induction hypothesis, for
every constraint
\chi^{\prime}
in
(C1)
,

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}\chi^{\prime}
, hence:

e(w,\alpha)\leq e(w,q)\qquad e(w,\beta)\geq e(w,q)\qquad 1\rhd\mathcal{I}(t)

Since
e(w,\alpha)\leq e(w,\beta)
, we get
e(w,\alpha\to\beta)=1
, hence

e(w,\alpha\to\beta)\rhd\mathcal{I}(t)
, which implies
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}\chi
. In
case
(C2)
, by the induction hypothesis we get

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\beta\rhd t
, namely
e(w,\beta)\rhd\mathcal{I}(t)
. Since

e(w,\alpha\to\beta)\geq e(w,\beta)
, we get
e(w,\alpha\to\beta)\rhd\mathcal{I}(t)
, hence

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\alpha\to\beta\rhd t
. The cases concerning

R
-constraints can be proved as the corresponding propositional
cases.

Let
\chi=w:\Box\alpha\lhd t
. Since
\Gamma_{k}\mapsto_{\chi}\Gamma_{k+1}
, the
applied rule is
\Box\lhd
, hence:

(D)

R(w,w_{1})\to w_{1}:\alpha\lhd t\,\in\Gamma_{k+1}
.

Let
r_{1}
be the value of
R(w,w_{1})\to e(w_{1}:\alpha)
. By the induction
hypothesis
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}R(w,w_{1})\to w_{1}:\alpha\lhd t
, hence
r_{1}\lhd\ \mathcal{I}(t)
. Since
e(w,\Box\alpha)\leq r_{1}
, we get
e(w,\Box\alpha)\lhd\mathcal{I}(t)
, and this implies
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\Box\alpha\lhd t
. The
case
\chi=w:\lozenge\alpha\rhd t
is similar.

It remains to consider the cases where
\mathrm{wg}(\chi)>0
and
\chi\in\Gamma_{n}
, thus
\chi=w:\Box\alpha\rhd t
or
\chi=w:\lozenge\alpha\lhd t
. Let

\chi=w:\Box\alpha\rhd t
; we show that:

(E)

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}R(w,w^{\prime})\to w^{\prime}:\alpha\rhd t
, for every
w^{\prime}\in W
.

Let
w^{\prime}\in W
; we show that
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}R(w,w^{\prime})\to w^{\prime}:\alpha\rhd t
.
Let us assume that
R(w,w^{\prime})
does not occur in
\Gamma_{n}
. By definition
of
\mathfrak{M}
, the value of
R(w,w^{\prime})
is 0, hence we have to show that

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}1\rhd t
. The case where
\rhd
is
\geq
is
immediate. Let
\rhd
be
>
. Since
\chi\in\Gamma_{n}
, we get
1>t\in\mathrm{At}^{+}\!(\Gamma_{n})
, thus
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}1>t

by
(1)
. Let us assume that
R(w,w^{\prime})
occurs in

\Gamma_{n}
. Then, there exists
k>0
such that
R(w,w^{\prime})
is
introduced in
\Gamma_{k}
by an application of a modal rule
\rho
,
namely:

•

\Gamma_{k-1}\mapsto_{\chi^{\prime}}\Gamma_{k}
, with
\chi^{\prime}=w:\Box\varphi\lhd^{\prime}t^{\prime}

or
\chi^{\prime}=w:\lozenge\varphi\rhd^{\prime}t^{\prime}
,
and the new label introduced by
\rho
is
w^{\prime}
.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is saturated,
\Gamma_{k-1}
is plain, hence the non-atomic
constraints in
\Gamma_{k-1}
are modal. As a consequence, no rule
applied in the sub-branch
\langle\Gamma_{k-1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}\rangle
can introduce
in the premise new constraints
u\,\blacktriangledown\,t^{\prime}
such that
u
has
label
w
; this implies that
\chi\in\Gamma_{k-1}
. By definition of

\rho
, we get
\Phi^{\Box,\lozenge}(\Gamma_{k-1},w,w^{\prime})\subseteq\Gamma_{k}
, hence

R(w,w^{\prime})\to w^{\prime}:\alpha\rhd t\in\Gamma_{k}
. By the induction hypothesis, we
get
\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}R(w,w^{\prime})\to w^{\prime}:\alpha\rhd t
, and this concludes the
proof of
(E)
. Let
e(w,\Box\alpha)=r
. By the witnessing condition,
there exists
w^{\star}\in W
such that the value of

R(w,w^{\star})\to e(w^{\star},\alpha)
is
r
. By
(E)
,

r\rhd\mathcal{I}(t)
, namely
e(w,\Box\alpha)\rhd\mathcal{I}(t)
; we conclude

\mathfrak{M}\models_{\mathcal{I}}w:\Box\alpha\rhd t
. The case
\chi=w:\lozenge\alpha\lhd t
is
similar.
∎

As a consequence, we get:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\langle\Gamma_{0},\dots,\Gamma_{n}\rangle
be a saturated branch.

(i)

For every solution
\sigma
to

\mathrm{At}^{+}\!(\Gamma_{n})
,
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathrm{At}^{+}\!(\Gamma_{n}),\sigma)
is a discrete
countermodel for
\Gamma_{0}
.

(ii)

If
\Gamma_{0}=\{w:\varphi<1\}
, then

\varphi\not\in\mathbf{GW}
.

Let
Bs
be a backward proof search strategy for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GW}}
; we say
that
Bs
is
plain
iff all the modal rule applications
performed by
Bs
are plain.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
Bs
be a plain proof search strategy for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GW}}
and let

\Gamma_{0}
be a finite multiset of constraints of the form

w:\varphi\,\blacktriangledown\,t
. If
Bs
fails to prove
\Gamma_{0}
, then a
discrete countermodel for
\Gamma_{0}
can be built.

Proof.

Assume that
Bs
fails. By tracing the computation, we can build
an open branch
\mathcal{B}=\langle\Gamma_{0},\dots,\Gamma_{n}\rangle
. Since
Bs
is
plain, the branch
\mathcal{B}
is saturated. Let
\sigma
be a solution to

\mathrm{At}^{+}\!(\Gamma_{n})
; by Prop.
3.7
(i)
,
we get that
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathrm{At}^{+}\!(\Gamma_{n}),\sigma)
is a discrete countermodel for

\Gamma_{0}
.
∎

To sum up, we get the completeness of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GW}}
and the finite model
property for
\mathbf{GW}
.

Proposition 3.9

(Completeness)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite multiset of constraints of the form

w:\varphi\,\blacktriangledown\,t
.

(i)

\vdash_{\mathrm{GW}}\Gamma
iff, for every
\mathrm{GW}
-model
\mathfrak{M}
,
\mathfrak{M}\not\models\Gamma
.

(ii)

\varphi\in\mathbf{GW}
iff
\vdash_{\mathrm{GW}}w:\varphi<1
; moreover, if

\varphi\not\in\mathbf{GW}
,
there exists a discrete countermodel for
\varphi
.

Let
\varphi\notin\mathbf{GW}
and let
\mathfrak{M}
be the discrete countermodel
extracted from an opennn branch with root
w_{0}:\varphi<1
. One can
easily show that the depth of
\mathfrak{M}
is bounded by
|\varphi|
and
that every world in
\mathfrak{M}
has at most
|\varphi|

R
-successors.
Consequently, the size of
\mathfrak{M}
is

O(|\varphi|^{|\varphi|})
; moreover, we can define formulas

\varphi
having countermodels of size
2^{|\varphi|}
(e.g.,
consider the translation
\varphi^{*}
of a
\mathbf{K}
-formula
\varphi
such
that
\varphi
has a countermodel of exponential size). Note that

\mathfrak{M}
cannot be constructed incrementally, one branch at a time, since
the constraints generated during the expansion of a branch have global
validity and must be preserved throughout the entire construction.
Nevertheless, we can define a PSPACE algorithm to check if such a
countermodel exists without effectively constructing it; this is
discussed in the proof of the next proposition.

Proposition 3.10
.

The validity problem for
\mathbf{GW}
is PSPACE-complete.

Proof.

Since the modal logic
\mathbf{K}
can be embedded into
\mathbf{GW}

(Prop.
2.2
) and
\mathbf{K}
is
PSPACE-complete
[
15
]
, the validity problem for
\mathbf{GW}
is
PSPACE-hard. It remains to prove that
\mathbf{GW}
-validity can be
checked in PSPACE. To this aim, we sketch a PSPACE procedure to
decide the membership of a formula to
\mathbf{GW}
. Henceforth, we assume
that the formula to be checked is
\varphi
. As discussed above, if

\varphi\not\in\mathbf{GW}
, then there exists a tree-like countermodel

\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,R,e\rangle
for
\varphi
such that the number of worlds is

O(|\varphi|^{|\varphi|})
. We can also bound the number

N
of values needed to define
R
and
e
: in the worst case, the
values
e(w,p)
and
R(w,w^{\prime})
must all be pairwise distinct, for
every world
w
,
w^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{M}
and every propositional variable
p

occurring in
\varphi
; accordingly,
N
is in

O(|\varphi|^{|\varphi|})
. We set

\mathcal{Q}=\{0\}\cup\{~1/k~|~1\leq k\leq N\}
; note that the elements of

\mathcal{Q}
can be generated in space
O(|\varphi|)
. By the above
discussion, if
\varphi\not\in\mathbf{GW}
there exists a countermodel
\mathfrak{M}

such that the images of
R
and
e
are contained in
\mathcal{Q}
. To
define the procedure, we need a global map
\Phi
that specifies the
intended values of the subformulas of
\varphi
at each world of the
model under construction. More precisely, given a world name
w
,

\Phi(w)
is a set of assignments of the form
e(w,\alpha)=r
, where

\alpha
is a subformula of
\varphi
and
r\in\mathcal{Q}
. We remark that

\Phi(w)
can be stored in space
O(|\varphi|)
; moreover, to
implement the search procedure, we do no not need to store the whole
map
\Phi
, but at most
O(|\varphi|)
entries of
\Phi
. A

\mathrm{GW}
-model
\mathfrak{M}=\langle W,R,e\rangle
complies with
\Phi
iff, for every

w
in
W
and every assignment
e(w,\alpha)=r
stored in
\Phi(w)
, the
value of
e(w,\alpha)
(in
\mathfrak{M}
) is
r
. The decision procedure is
implemented by the recursive procedure
Search
having the
following specification:

•

Let
w
be a world name and let us assume that
\Phi(w)\neq\emptyset
. The procedure
\textsc{Search}(w)
returns
Success
if a

\mathrm{GW}
-model complying with
\Phi
can be built,
Fail
otherwise.

The procedure, at some points, requires the selection of a value from

\mathcal{Q}
, possibly satisfying some conditions: we call this a

\mathcal{Q}
-choice.
\mathcal{Q}
-choices can be revoked and redone; such
backtrack steps can be implemented in space
O(|\varphi|)
. The
computation of
\textsc{Search}(w)
consists of two main steps.

(Step 1)

We apply propositional saturation reductions to

\Phi(w)
as long as possible. At each step, an assignment

e(w,\alpha\star\beta)=r
(with
\star\in\{\,\land,\,\lor,\,\to\,\}
) in

\Phi(w)
is removed and replaced by the assignments
e(w,\alpha)=r_{1}

and
e(w,\beta)=r_{2}
required to justifies the value
r
, where the
values
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are selected with
\mathcal{Q}
-choices. For
instance, let us assume that the assignment at hand is
e(w,\alpha\to\beta)=r
.

•

If
r=1
, we must have
e(w,\alpha)\leq e(w,\beta)
; the values

r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are selected with
\mathcal{Q}
-choices so that
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
; in
\Phi(w)
,
e(w,\alpha\to\beta)=r
is replaced with
e(w,\alpha)=r_{1}
and
e(w,\beta)=r_{2}
.

•

If
r<1
, a value
r_{1}
is selected with a
\mathcal{Q}
-choice so
that
r_{1}>r
; in
\Phi(w)
,
e(w,\alpha\to\beta)=r
is replaced with

e(w,\alpha)=r_{1}
and
e(w,\beta)=r
(thus,
r_{2}
=r).

At the end of
(Step 1)
, the formulas occurring in
\Phi(w)
are
either atomic or modal.

•

If
\Phi(w)
contains two evaluations
e(w,\alpha)=r_{1}
and

e(w,\alpha)=r_{2}
such that
r_{1}\neq r_{2}
, a backtrack step is needed.
If no new
\mathcal{Q}
-choice is possible, the call
\textsc{Search}(w)

returns
Fail
.

•

Otherwise, if
\Phi(w)
contains only atomic constraints, the
call
\textsc{Search}(w)
returns
Success
.

•

Otherwise,
\Phi(w)
contains at least a
modal formula and the computation continues with
(Step 2)
.

(Step 2)

Let
e(w,\#_{1}\alpha_{1})=r_{1},\dots,e(w,\#_{n}\alpha_{n})=r_{n}

(with
\#_{k}\in\{\Box,\Diamond\}
) be all the assignments in
\Phi(w)

involving modal formulas. We start a loop indexed by
k
ranging
over
\{1,\dots,n\}
. Let
k
be the current iteration and let us
assume that the assignment of index
k
has the form

e(w,\Box\alpha_{k})=r_{k}
(the case
e(w,\lozenge\alpha_{k})=r_{k}
is similar). We
generate a new world
w_{k}
and select with a
\mathcal{Q}
-choice a value

r^{\prime}_{k}
for
R(w,w_{k})
and a value
r^{\prime\prime}_{k}
for
e(w_{k},\alpha_{k})
such
that
r^{\prime}_{k}\to r^{\prime\prime}_{k}=r_{k}
. The world
w_{k}
is intended to represent
the world witnessing that
e(w,\Box\alpha_{k})=r_{k}
. We set

\Phi(w_{k})=\{e(w_{k},\alpha_{k})=r^{\prime\prime}_{k}\}
. We expand
\Phi(w_{k})
by adding
the assignments required to account for the assignments

e(w,\#_{j}\alpha_{j})=r_{j}
occurring in
\Phi(w)
, where
j\neq k
. More
precisely:

•

for every
e(w,\Box\alpha_{j})=r_{j}
in
\Phi(w)
(
j\neq k
), we
select with a
\mathcal{Q}
-choice a value
r^{\prime}_{j}
such that
r^{\prime}_{k}\to r^{\prime}_{j}\geq r_{j}
and we add
e(w_{k},\alpha_{j})=r^{\prime}_{j}
to
\Phi(w_{k})
;

•

for every
e(w,\lozenge\alpha_{j})=r_{j}
in
\Phi
(
j\neq k
), we
select with a
\mathcal{Q}
-choice a value
r^{\prime}_{j}
such that
r^{\prime}_{k}\land r^{\prime}_{j}\leq r_{j}
and we add
e(w_{k},\alpha_{j})=r^{\prime}_{j}
to
\Phi(w_{k})
.

We compute recursively
\textsc{Search}(w_{k})
; notice that the modal
operators occurring in
\Phi(w_{k})
are fewer than the modal
operators in
\Phi(w)
, thus the recursion is terminating. We also
stress that there are at most
|\varphi|
nested recursive calls.
Let us assume that
\textsc{Search}(w_{k})
returns
Success
. If
k=n

(last iteration), the call
\textsc{Search}(w)
returns
Success
.
Otherwise the computation continues with next iteration
k+1
. We
point out that the entries of the map
\Phi
exploited in the
computation of
\textsc{Search}(w_{k})
are no longer required, so they can be
removed from the memory. Let us assume that
\textsc{Search}(w_{k})
returns

Fail
. Then, a backtrack step is needed. If no new

\mathcal{Q}
-choice is possible, the call
\textsc{Search}(w)
returns
Fail
.

The procedure can be implemented so that
\textsc{Search}(w)
is computed in
space
O(|\varphi|)
.

We can exploit
Search
to decide the validity of
\varphi
in

\mathbf{GW}
. We initialize
\Phi
by setting
\Phi(w_{0})=\{e(w_{0},\varphi)=r_{0}\}
, where
r_{0}
is any value in
\mathcal{Q}
different from 1. If

\textsc{Search}(w_{0})
returns
Success
, then a model
\mathfrak{M}
containing a
world
w_{0}
such that
e(w_{0},\varphi)=r_{0}
can be built; since
r_{0}<1
, we conclude
\varphi\not\in\mathbf{GW}
. If
\textsc{Search}(w_{0})
returns

Fail
, for every possible choice of
r_{0}
, it follows that no

\mathrm{GW}
-countermodel for
\varphi
can be built; accordingly,

\varphi\in\mathbf{GW}
.
∎

4
Conclusions and Future Work

We have implemented a proof-search procedure for the calculus
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GW}}

that supports countermodel extraction. The prover, called

gwref

[
11
]
, performs a standard backward
depth-first proof search, relying on the JTabWb
engine
[
10
]
. The consistency of atomic constraints is
verified using the Choco-solver Java library
[
17
]
.
We emphasize that the calculus
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{GW}}
and the associated
proof-search strategy can be smoothly adapted to treat the logic

\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}

[
12
]
, characterized by crisp
\mathrm{GW}
-models,
by refining the notion of consistency for a set of atomic constraints

\Gamma_{\mathrm{at}}
: a solution
\sigma
to
\Gamma_{\mathrm{at}}
must satisfy
\sigma(R(w,w^{\prime}))\in\{0,1\}
for every pair of labels
w
,
w^{\prime}
. To the best of our
knowledge, the only other prover available for modal fuzzy logics is
mNiBLoS
[
21
]
, an SMT-based solver designed for continuous
t-norm-based logics, including Gödel–Dummett Logic. However,
neither
\mathbf{GW}
nor
\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}
are supported by mNiBLoS.

The results presented in this paper are highly relevant to the study
of intermediate predicate logics. It is well known that modal logics
can be used to characterize relevant fragments of intermediate
first-order logics, particularly certain one-variable fragments. From
this perspective, the witnessed semantics, together with the consequent
finite model property established here, point to deep connections with
proof-theoretic properties of intermediate predicate logics, such as
Herbrand’s Theorem, interpolation, and alternative forms of
Skolemization investigated in
[
3
]
for broad classes of
intermediate intuitionistic predicate logics with the finite model
property. Motivated by these connections, and with the aim of
situating
\mathbf{GW}
within the broader landscape of intuitionistic modal
logics, we plan to develop a birelational Kripke semantics for
\mathbf{GW}
,
extending to this setting the approach introduced for
\mathbf{GW}^{\mathrm{c}}

in
[
12
]
.

We also plan to extend the procedure to other witnessed Gödel modal
logics that impose additional constraints on the accessibility
relation (e.g., reflexivity, transitivity, seriality). In contrast,
treating Gödel modal logics with non-witnessed models seems to be
considerably more challenging.
