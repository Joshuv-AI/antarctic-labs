# Bodin, Rémy, and Weyer Combined — 1698 German Edition

**Archive.org:** https://archive.org/details/desweylandhochge00bodi

---

## What Is This?

This remarkable 1698 German edition bundles together three foundational texts on witchcraft — one extreme pro-persecution (Bodin), one moderate pro-persecution (Rémy), and one skeptical (Weyer) — published together as a kind of reference compendium on witchcraft. The editor's intent was clearly comparative: let the reader decide.

## The Three Authors

### Jean Bodin — *Daemonomania* (1580)
French jurist. The most influential pro-persecution legal treatise in European history. Bodin argued that denying the reality of witchcraft was itself a form of heresy. (See ToB-0094 for full detail.)

### Nicolas Rémy — *Daemonolatriae Libri Tres* (1578)
French lieutenant-general of the Duchy of Bar. Rémy was a sitting judge who personally presided over witchcraft trials. His book claimed to document over 900 witchcraft cases from his own career — one of the most extreme pro-persecution accounts. Unlike Bodin, Rémy was a field prosecutor, not a theoretical lawyer.

### Johann Weyer — *De Lamiis* (1563)
Dutch physician. The first systematic skeptical voice in witchcraft literature. Weyer argued that supposed witches were mentally ill victims, not malevolent agents of Satan. (See ToB-0096 for full detail.)

## Why Published Together?

The 1698 German editor clearly had scholarly intent — placing all three major positions side by side. This was unusual. Most publishers chose one camp or the other. Having all three in one volume is a goldmine for studying the full range of 16th–17th century thought on witchcraft.

## Key Features of This Edition

- **Comparative format** — All three major positions in one volume
- **Language** — The Bodin and Rémy texts are in German (1698 translations); Weyer is also in German
- **Historical range** — Texts spanning 1563–1698, covering the height of the witch trial era
