Introduction

Background and rationale

Respiratory physiotherapy as a part of a preoperative physical training programme may reduce the risk of developing postoperative complications, thereby improving postoperative results and optimising the physical condition of patients diagnosed with non-small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC) undergoing lung resection surgery.1 2 During the postoperative period, performing physical exercise has also been shown to increase the capacity for exercise, improve health-related quality of life and reduce the feeling of breathlessness.3 However, these findings should be interpreted with caution due to disparities between the studies and the risk of bias.

The numerous respiratory physiotherapy protocols available vary by type of exercise, duration, repetition, intensity and modality (outpatient or under specialised personal supervision). There is also variability when measuring the results of the protocol. Some studies report a reduction in respiratory complications or hospital stay,2 4 while others only demonstrate improvements in the physical condition or quality of life of patients.5 The heterogeneity in the design, implementation and evaluation of physiotherapy programmes makes it difficult to demonstrate their effectiveness or to generalise their real-life benefits.6 Further studies are needed with larger samples and better methodological quality to clarify the potential benefits of perioperative physical training in NSCLC patients.7

Fissios App, a software tool developed by thoracic surgeons, physiotherapists and a specialist doctor in physical medicine and rehabilitation, has been successfully used with thoracic surgery patients.8 It contains a programme of standardised respiratory physiotherapy exercises with a defined time and number of repetitions. The possibility of recording individual patient usage time of the app and the degree of compliance with the programme is also offered on the Fissios Research platform.9 The usage data are complemented with the patient’s personal history, the surgical procedure performed and the postoperative course. With all the information centralised in a single platform, the aim is to establish the relationship between compliance with a standardised respiratory physiotherapy programme, postoperative outcomes and the development of postoperative complications in a group of patients diagnosed with NSCLC who underwent lung resection surgery.

Fundamental rationale for the study

Most thoracic surgeons prescribe respiratory physiotherapy exercises before and after surgery. However, studies published on the topic present various unresolved issues, including interinstitutional variability, a lack of homogeneity in the respiratory physiotherapy exercises, low sample numbers and surgical patients from different specialties. Therefore, there is an urgent need to perform clinical trials with protocolised exercise programmes applied to a carefully selected population of patients in order to strengthen the current evidence.

Trial design

The Fissios and postoperative cpostoperative complications (FPoC) trial is designed as a multicentre, prospective, randomised, parallel, two-group, controlled, open-label, superiority study of the use of only face-to-face respiratory physiotherapy or the use of Fissios App as a complement to respiratory physiotherapy in patients scheduled for lung resection surgery.

To guarantee homogeneity in the respiratory physiotherapy treatment that the patient will receive, the study will be performed in tertiary hospitals in Spain that jointly offer thoracic surgery and physiotherapy and rehabilitation services.

Patients taking part in the study will be randomly assigned to the experimental or control group before the surgery. All patients will undertake a face-to-face respiratory physiotherapy programme, while those in the experimental group will complement this programme with use of the Fissios App tool. Perioperative care will be performed in accordance with national and international recommendation guidelines.10 11 Postoperative complications will be recorded following the definitions set out in the European Society of Thoracic Surgeons (ESTS) database and in this protocol.12 Finally, there will be a 90-day follow-up period following the date of surgery, with the patient receiving check-ups according to the protocols of each centre.

Interventions

Intervention description

Intervention in the control group

Perioperative respiratory physiotherapy will be prescribed after assessment by the investigator. The patient will attend an evaluation by a specialist doctor in respiratory rehabilitation and physiotherapy who will prescribe the exercises to be performed. The patient will attend face-to-face respiratory physiotherapy sessions given by physiotherapists before and after surgery at the place and times established in each centre, undertaking at least one session and with no limit on the number of sessions. Investigators will encourage the patient to attend and undergo face-to-face physiotherapy sessions and must record their attendance and degree of compliance on the eCRF. The study team will draw up a protocol with the minimum number of respiratory physiotherapy exercises that should be performed to guarantee treatment homogeneity between the control group and the experimental group (online supplemental material 4). The investigator will fill in the preoperative, surgery and postoperative data for each patient on the Fissios Research platform.

Intervention in the experimental group

The same protocol as in the control group will be applied in the experimental group and, in addition, the steps to download and implement the application will be explained to the participant (online supplemental material 3). The investigator will provide a unique registry code that the patient must enter in the application to activate their participation in the study. If possible, this should be done on the same day as the consultation. The investigator will encourage the patient to use the Fissios App tool freely and without restrictions in terms of hour of the day, usage time or location, to read all the perioperative medical recommendations and to perform the respiratory physiotherapy exercises. All Fissios App usage data will be automatically stored on the Fissios Research platform. The investigator will fill in the preoperative, surgery and postoperative data for each patient on the Fissios Research platform.

Description of the health product

Fissios is a computerised, interactive application developed by doctors and physiotherapists with the aim of reducing the probability of developing postoperative respiratory complications. It comprises 40 perioperative recommendations and a programme of 10 respiratory physiotherapy exercises. The recommendations, in the form of advice, are intended to inform the patient about basic aspects that contribute to the perioperative course. These include stopping smoking, oral hygiene and physical exercises. The respiratory physiotherapy exercises are explained with animations and are aimed at strengthening the respiratory muscles, promoting coughing and the mobilisation of secretions (figure 1). The Fissios App tool is a native 22 MB application developed using Ionic Framework V.3, Angular and Cordova technologies.

Adherence

Patients in the experimental group that configure and personalise the Fissios App tool will receive a notification on their mobile device 15 days before and after surgery at 10:00. These notifications contain perioperative recommendations and messages to positively strengthen the patient’s attitude. The intention of these notifications is to increase the patient’s interaction with the application and, indirectly, adherence to the interventions. To monitor adherence to the intervention, investigators can consult, via the Fissios Research platform, the participant’s usage time of the Fissios App tool, their compliance in terms of performing the exercises and whether the recommendations have been read. Notifications sent to the mobile device and the ability to monitor usage of the application are some of the advantages of new technologies in medicine that we aim to highlight in this study.

Concomitant care

Patients from both groups are permitted to receive other perioperative treatments to reduce the risk of developing postoperative complications.

Participant timeline

Duration of treatment

The minimum duration of the treatment will be 2 weeks over the perioperative period: at least 1 week prior to surgery and 1 week after medical discharge. Performing the treatment for more days will not be penalised in the study.

Definition of end of study

The study will be concluded when the follow-up of all patients is completed (90 days after surgery of the last randomised patient), all the data are in the database, the statistical analysis is completed and when access to the database is blocked.

The end of the study for the patient is when he/she has completed all follow-up visits (90 days after surgery) or withdraws from the study.

Sample size

The sample size is calculated based on the percentage of postoperative respiratory complications that occurred in 2021 in the thoracic surgery department (24%) of Hospital Clínico San Carlos (HCSC) and the anticipated reduction in the experimental group using the Fissios App tool (12%). To achieve a power of 90%, considering a significance level of 5% and an additional 20% of patients for possible loss to follow-up, a total of 560 patients will be required (280 patients in each group).

Recruitment

Patients will be recruited in thoracic surgery outpatient departments by thoracic surgeons when recommending the surgery. An explanation of the study will be given along with the invitation to take part. All patients will be invited to take part. There will be no recruitment incentive for either the potential participants or surgeons (hereinafter also referred to as investigators).

Allocation

Description of randomisation

Subjects will be randomly assigned to the experimental or control group in a 1:1 ratio, in blocks of six for each centre. Randomisation will be electronically performed using Fissios Research software. Once the patient has signed the informed consent form and their basic data have been recorded on the eCRF (on the Fissios Research platform), the patient will be automatically randomised according to the defined sequence, and the patient’s treatment arm will be recorded on the eCRF.

Blinding (masking)

Blinding mechanism

This is an open-label type study. As it is the surgeon who will randomise the patients and record their data on the eCRF, there will be no blinding. Because the patients in the experimental group will need to use the Fissios App tool, the participants will know the group they belong to. To reduce bias, the patient randomisation sequence will be performed by staff members who do not form part of the research team and will be kept confidential until the patient is assigned to a group. The informed consent document (online supplemental material 1) and an information sheet (online supplemental material 2) given to the participants will describe the control group and experimental group treatments and their potential effects with the aim of avoiding the generation of strong expectations favouring one method over the other.

Data collection

Trial procedures and evaluations

Data collection methods

All data will be collected by qualified thoracic surgeons trained to record all the variables in the database (table 2). The source of clinical information will be the patient’s clinical history kept by each investigator at their centre. Data management

All data are entered and stored on the Fissios Research platform. Each investigator can access the platform through a unique username and password. The variables will be recorded by a single designated investigator at each centre. Numerical coding is employed for all the variables. Nominal dichotomous qualitative variables concerning the presence/absence of a characteristic are entered as 0 = no, 1 = yes. Nominal qualitative variables of various categories are entered in ascending order from 1, with 0 representing a null value (no response or multiple responses). Quantitative variables are entered as their corresponding numeric value. The translation of these codes is available in the dictionary of variables (online supplemental material 5).

Data security information

To access data on the Fissios Research platform, a unique username and password will be used which only the corresponding investigator will know. Data will be stored in a back-end server. Google’s Firebase technology will be used, which meets the highest security standards and complies with the EU’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the ISO 27001 standard.15 All communications will be performed securely using HTTPS technology, and under no circumstances will personal data of any patient be sent. The patient number on the Fissios Research platform will be randomly assigned and bear no relationship to the patient’s personal data or medical history.

Statistical methods

Outcomes

Descriptive analysis

The statistical analysis will be performed by the Methodology Unit for Clinical Research and Epidemiology of HCSC in Madrid, Spain. In the descriptive analysis, the qualitative variables will be presented with their frequency distribution. The quantitative variables will be summarised as the mean and SD. Quantitative variables that show an asymmetric distribution will be summarised as the median and IQR.

EuroLung1 (morbidity): the risk of postoperative morbidity is calculated using an aggregate score (0–19 points) based on weighted clinical and surgical variables:

Based on the cumulative score, patients are stratified into six incremental risk classes (A–F), which have shown significant predictive value for postoperative complications (p<0.0001).

Patients are subsequently grouped into six incremental mortality risk classes (A–F), with significant prognostic stratification (p<0.0001).

Initial comparison of the groups

An initial comparison of baseline characteristics will be made between the two study groups. The association between qualitative variables will be evaluated with the chi-squared (χ2) test or Fisher’s exact test if more than 25% of the expected values are under 5. For the quantitative variables, the means will be compared using the Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test if the quantitative variables do not fit a normal distribution.

Analysis of the aims

To study the effectiveness of the Fissios App tool, postoperative complications will be analysed by groups using the chi-squared (χ2) test or Fisher’s exact test if more than 25% of the expected values are under 5. In addition, logistic regression models will be used to ascertain the effect produced and if there is a need to adjust for baseline variables that were not homogeneous between study groups in the initial test.

The repeated outcomes measured more than once will be analysed by mixed models which take time into account. With this method we can control the evolution over time.

The adverse events (AES) will be summarised by the percentage of occurrences and the 95% CI.

A level of statistical significance of 5% will be considered. The analysis will be performed with the IBM SPSS Statistics V.26 and R V.4.2.1 programmes.

Limitations

A mobile device compatible with Fissios App is required. To address this limitation, the App is available on the two main digital stores, App Store and Google Play Store. Such availability should allow most patients to download and instal the latest version of the App. For users without a compatible smart mobile device, a web version of the App is also available, allowing access from any computer with internet connectivity.

The nature of the intervention and the type of device used prevent participants from being blinded. The randomisation of patients and the recording of data on the eCRF is conducted by the surgeon, and participants will be aware of their assigned group. To minimise bias, external staff, independent of the research team, will perform the randomisation sequence, keeping it confidential until the patient’s assignment. The informed consent form (online supplemental material 1) and an information sheet (online supplemental material 2) given to the participants will describe the treatments in the control and experimental groups and their potential effects with the aim of avoiding the generation of strong expectations favouring one method over the other.

Numerous protocols for respiratory physiotherapy exist, differing in exercise type, duration, intensity, frequency, supervision and location. To ensure treatment homogeneity, the study will be performed in tertiary hospitals in Spain that jointly offer thoracic surgery and physiotherapy and rehabilitation services. Additionally, a protocol outlining a minimum set of respiratory physiotherapy exercises for the face-to-face sessions will be available (online supplemental material 4) to maximise similarity between treatments in both groups.

Perioperative patient management may vary between centres based on medical criteria and available resources. To address this variability, a request will be made for compliance with national and international perioperative guidelines known to all researchers.10 11 The same principle will apply to postoperative respiratory complication definitions, with authors urged to adhere to the definitions established by the ESTS and this protocol to ensure consistency.12
