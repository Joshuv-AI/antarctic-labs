Effects of climate change and anthropogenic activities on vegetation coverage changes in the Taihang Mountains, China
Authors: Zhigao Zhang, Yibo Zhao, Yan Li, Ganen Wang, Heyang Chen, Jiayi Liu, Yixin Geng, Huayong Li, Linsong Yu, Qianyu Tang, Maotang Cai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Taihang Mountains, situated in the transitional zone between the Loess Plateau and the North China Plain, serve as a crucial ecological barrier in northern China. Analysing the spatiotemporal dynamics of vegetation cover and identifying the underlying drivers are fundamental for effective regional resource management and ecological conservation. In this study, the spatiotemporal patterns of vegetation change in the Taihang Mountains from 2000 to 2024 were analysed on the basis of MODIS normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) data, climate records, and vegetation maps. Trend analysis, Mann‒Kendall significance tests, and the Hurst index were applied to characterize changes, while residual analysis was used to decompose and quantify the impacts of climate change versus human activities. Our findings reveal a significant greening trend, with the NDVI increasing by 0.0036 per year. High NDVI values were primarily found in southern regions, whereas low values were concentrated in the northwest. Increasing NDVI trends dominated 92.90% of the total area, while decreasing trends were limited (7.10%) and concentrated mainly in the eastern low-elevation foothills and populated urban areas, such as Jincheng. Hurst index analysis indicated that future vegetation changes are predominantly anti-persistent, with 56.97% of the area projected to experience degradation. Human activities dominated the variation in the NDVI (86.16%), compared to 13.84% from climatic factors, and contributed over 70.09% of the changes across all vegetation types. Among vegetation types, coniferous forests showed the most robust improvement under human interventions, whereas the “others” category and cultivated vegetation exhibited higher degradation levels. These findings offer a scientific basis for guiding ecological management strategies in the Taihang Mountains.

Introduction

Vegetation is a fundamental component of terrestrial ecosystems and serves as a key link among the atmosphere, hydrosphere, pedosphere, and biosphere. By regulating energy exchange, water cycles, and material flows, vegetation plays a crucial role in climate regulation, ecosystem functioning, and the maintenance of human living environments. Consequently, vegetation dynamics are widely recognized as important indicators of ecological environmental quality and land surface processes1–3. According to the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), the global mean near-surface temperature in 2024 exceeded the 1850–1900 baseline by 1.55 ± 0.13°C4. Ongoing global warming has profoundly altered the hydrological cycle and disrupted regional water resource balances5. Changes in temperature and precipitation directly affect the light, heat, and moisture conditions that affect vegetation growth, thereby altering vegetation patterns and ecosystem stability6. In this context, understanding long-term vegetation dynamics and their driving mechanisms is essential for developing effective strategies for ecological conservation and environmental management under climate change7.

Conducting precise field surveys in mountainous regions is challenging, and obtaining extensive, long-term vegetation cover data through ground-based methods is often impractical. Consequently, remote sensing has become a primary tool for monitoring vegetation distribution over such areas. Many researchers have widely utilized the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), which is calculated from optical remote sensing satellite imagery, to characterize vegetation cover and dynamics8–10. Consequently, numerous studies have reported a pronounced global “greening” trend over recent decades. Between 1982 and 2012, approximately 68% of global vegetated land areas exhibited increasing NDVI, particularly in mid- to low-latitude regions of the Northern Hemisphere, such as Western Europe, southeastern United States, South Asia, and East Asia. In contrast, vegetation “browning” was observed mainly in Central Asia, northern North America, southern South America, and Central Africa, highlighting strong spatial heterogeneity in vegetation responses11,12. Climate factors, particularly temperature and precipitation, are the primary natural drivers of vegetation change13,14. However, vegetation responses to climate variability vary substantially across regions because of differences in climatic regimes and environmental constraints15,16. For example, temperature plays a dominant role in controlling NDVI variations in eastern China17, whereas precipitation is the main limiting factor on the northern Loess Plateau18. In mountainous areas, topography modulates vegetation dynamics by influencing local climate and resource availability. Specifically, temperature and moisture exhibit distinct gradients with elevation19, whereas slope and aspect shape microclimates by altering solar radiation and soil water retention, thereby affecting vegetation growth patterns20,21. In addition to natural control, anthropogenic activities have become increasingly important drivers of vegetation change22. Ecological restoration projects, such as afforestation and the conversion of cropland to forestland or grassland, generally promote vegetation recovery and ecosystem improvement23. Conversely, rapid urban expansion and intensive resource exploitation often lead to vegetation degradation and landscape fragmentation24,25.

The Taihang Mountains, located along the boundary between the second and third topographic steps of China, constitute a critical ecological barrier in northern China. This region is characterized by abundant ecological resources, high population pressure, overall ecological fragility, and relatively low levels of economic development26. Previous studies have shown that vegetation dynamics in the Taihang Mountains are positively correlated with precipitation and negatively correlated with temperature27. The NDVI increases with increasing elevation up to a certain threshold before it decreases at higher altitudes28, and vegetation improvement is most pronounced on slopes that are gentler than 13° and weakens on steeper terrain29. In addition to natural factors, anthropogenic activities have increasingly influenced vegetation changes in this region30. Nevertheless, most existing studies rely primarily on conventional statistical approaches, which make it difficult to disentangle the independent effects and relative contributions of different driving factors31.

To address these limitations, this study integrates MODIS NDVI data with climatic and ancillary datasets to analyse the spatiotemporal patterns of vegetation activity and explore their relationships with climate change and human activities in the Taihang Mountains from 2000 to 2024. Our specific objectives were as follows: (1) to evaluate the spatiotemporal distribution patterns of the NDVI in the Taihang Mountains; (2) to explore the actual and relative contributions of climate change and anthropogenic activities to the NDVI in different vegetation types and identify the dominant factors; and (3) to predict the future change patterns of vegetation in the Taihang Mountains.

Materials and methods

Study area

The Taihang Mountains (34°35′–40°19′ N, 110°15′–116°27′ E) form an important mountain range and geographical boundary in eastern China (Fig. 1a). They also act as a key ecological barrier and sensitive area for the Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei region. The mountains run northeast–southwest and span four provinces/municipalities (Beijing, Hebei, Shanxi, and Henan), covering 22 prefecture-level cities, with a total area of approximately 13.7 × 10⁴ km². The elevation decreases from northwest to southeast, with a maximum elevation of 3019 m (Fig. 1b). The region has a warm temperate semi-humid continental monsoon climate with four distinct seasons. The multiyear average temperature is approximately 10 °C, and the multiyear average precipitation is approximately 500 mm32. Complex topography and hydrothermal conditions jointly shape distinct vertical vegetation zonation: farmland and shrubs dominate low mountains and plains; deciduous broad-leaved forests prevail in the mid-mountain zone; and mixed coniferous-broadleaf forests, coniferous forests, and mountain meadows are distributed on high mountains and windward slopes (Fig. 1c) .

Data sources and preprocessing

The data used in this study include vegetation indices, climatic elements, and vegetation types. The NDVI data were selected from the MODIS MOD13Q1 product provided by NASA, covering 2000–2024 with a spatial resolution of 250 m (https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-mod13q1-061). Annual average NDVI values were subsequently calculated from these data. Temperature and precipitation data were derived from the 1 km×1 km monthly temperature and precipitation dataset of China provided by the National Tibetan Plateau Science Data Center. Solar radiation data were sourced from the MODIS MCD18A1 dataset (NASA; 10.5067/MODIS/MCD18A1.062). The vegetation type data were obtained from the MODIS MCD12Q1 global land cover product for the years 2001, 2012, and 2024 (https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-mcd12q1-061). A raster overlay of the three land cover layers was performed to obtain the vegetation type data for the study area. The original 17 land cover types were consolidated into eight major vegetation categories: cultivated vegetation (CV), meadow, grass clump (GC), grassland, shrub, broadleaf forest (BF), coniferous forest (CF), and others. The original spatial and temporal resolutions were 500 m and 1 year, respectively. All the data were unified to the WGS1984 coordinate system and exported in GeoTIFF format for subsequent analysis.

Contributions of climate change and anthropogenic activities to vegetation cover change

By constructing a vegetation–climate response baseline model and employing spatial residual decomposition techniques, the NDVI time series was decomposed into a climate-driven component and a human disturbance component. Scenario simulation evaluation was then used to quantify the relative contribution rates of the two driving factors to vegetation cover change37. A pixel-based climate-vegetation response analytical framework was subsequently adopted, and a gridded multiple linear regression model was constructed to achieve quantitative decoupling of the driving factors and obtain the predicted NDVI value driven by climate (NDVIP). Considering only the influence of climatic factors and anthropogenic activities on vegetation cover, the residual NDVI value can be regarded as the impact of anthropogenic factors on vegetation cover38,39. Results

Interannual variation in the NDVI in the Taihang Mountains

From 2000 to 2024, the average NDVI in the Taihang Mountains varied between 0.360 and 0.469 (Fig. 2), with a multiyear mean of 0.419. Following the minimum value recorded in 2001, the NDVI exhibited a fluctuating but overall upward trend, reaching its peak of 0.469 in 2024. The annual growth rate of the NDVI in the Taihang Mountains was 0.0036 per year (P < 0.01), indicating that over the past 25 years, under the combined influence of climate and anthropogenic activities, vegetation coverage in the Taihang Mountains has tended to improve overall.

Analysis of temporal and spatial variations in the NDVI in the Taihang Mountains

From 2000 to 2024, the mean multiyear NDVI in the Taihang Mountains ranged from − 0.109 to 0.768, with approximately 90.25% of the values concentrated between 0.28 and 0.58 (Fig. 3a). The spatial distribution of the NDVI exhibited pronounced heterogeneity, characterized by higher values in the southern region, lower values in the northwestern region, and a mosaic of high and low values in the central area. High NDVI values were primarily observed in the southern Taihang Mountains, including the Zhongtiao Mountains, Jincheng, western Anyang, and Xinxiang, as well as in the northeastern region. In contrast, vegetation cover was relatively sparse along the Zhangjiakou–Datong–Xinzhou corridor. Over a span of 25 years, the annual NDVI trend across the Taihang Mountains ranged from − 0.0191 to 0.0213 per year (Fig. 3b). Overall, vegetation conditions clearly improved, with 92.90% of the vegetated area exhibiting an increasing trend. Among these areas, extremely significant increases accounted for 80.15% of the total area, suggesting a strong increase in vegetation cover. This widespread greening is likely associated with long-term ecological restoration initiatives, such as the conversion of cropland to forestland or grassland and other ecological improvement programs. In contrast, areas with a declining NDVI trend accounted for only 7.10% of the total area, of which extremely significant decreases accounted for approximately 2.16% (Fig. 3c). These declining regions were predominantly concentrated in zones of intense anthropogenic activity, particularly along the eastern Taihang urban corridor (Baoding–Shijiazhuang–Anyang–Xinxiang) and the western Jinzhong area, indicating that rapid urbanization and related anthropogenic disturbances may have contributed to local vegetation degradation.

From the perspective of vegetation types (Fig. 4), the vegetation dynamics in the study area exhibited an overall positive trend. For all vegetation categories, areas with increasing NDVI were substantially larger than those showing degradation, indicating widespread vegetation improvement across the region. The proportion of area with an increasing NDVI trend varied among vegetation types. These proportions were as follows: CF (100.00%), BF (99.48%), meadow (98.75%), shrubland (98.02%), GC (97.19%), grassland (97.18%), CV (90.64%), and “others” (70.37%). Notably, more than 70% of the area for each of the eight vegetation types exhibited an increasing NDVI trend, underscoring the general enhancement of vegetation conditions. Among all vegetation types, the improvement in the CF was most pronounced, with areas showing extremely significant increases in the NDVI accounting for 99.66% of its total area. In contrast, the “others” category exhibited the smallest proportion of extremely significant increase (45.28%), reflecting comparatively weaker vegetation recovery. The extent of vegetation degradation was limited for most vegetation types. The “others” category had the greatest proportion of declining NDVI (29.63%), which was characterized mainly by nonsignificant and extremely significant decreases. These degraded areas were primarily distributed along the eastern Taihang Mountains corridor (Beijing–Shijiazhuang–Anyang–Jiaozuo) and in western Jinzhong. Apart from the “others” category, the proportion of declining NDVI for all the other vegetation types remained below 10%, with the CV at 9.36%, and all the natural vegetation types remained strictly below 2.82%.

Future NDVI trend in the Taihang Mountains

The Hurst index of the NDVI in the Taihang Mountains ranged from 0.192 to 0.976 (Fig. 5a), indicating pronounced spatial heterogeneity in the persistence of vegetation dynamics. Areas with H > 0.5, suggesting a persistent trend, accounted for 38.43% of the study area, whereas regions with H < 0.5, indicating anti-persistence, accounted for 61.57%. By integrating the NDVI slope trend with the Hurst index, future vegetation change patterns were identified (Fig. 5b). Continuous improvement was observed in more than 41.52% of the area, while regions showing a future improvement trend accounted for only 1.51%. In contrast, areas with a projected degradation trend dominated the landscape, covering 56.28% of the study area. Continuous degradation was limited to 0.69% and was distributed mainly along the eastern corridor (Baoding–Shijiazhuang–Anyang–Jiaozuo) and the western part of Jinzhong.

Response of the NDVI from different vegetation types to climate change and anthropogenic activities

The effects of climatic factors on vegetation dynamics are illustrated in Fig. 6a. From 2000 to 2024, climate change led to an increasing trend in the NDVI across 62.19% of the study area, mainly in the northern and eastern parts of the Taihang Mountains, whereas a decreasing trend was observed in 37.81% of the area, primarily in the central and southern parts. When only climatic influences were considered, the variations in the NDVI were characterized by highly diverse responses. Notably, the proportion of areas with an extremely significant increase was the greatest, accounting for 28.31% of the total study area. Areas with a nonsignificant increase accounted for 25.48%, and those with a significant increase accounted for 8.38%, indicating that climate has a strong and positive effect on vegetation cover in specific regions. In contrast, nonsignificant decreases accounted for 21.49% of the area, and significant and extremely significant decreases accounted for 16.32% (5.20% and 11.12%, respectively), primarily in regions such as Jinzhong, Changzhi, and southern Jincheng. These spatial patterns suggest that climate change exerts a dual effect, driving substantial greening in some areas while acting as a considerable stressor and causing degradation in others. In comparison, the influence of anthropogenic activities on the change in the NDVI was much more pronounced and widespread (Fig. 6b). Human activities induced increasing and decreasing NDVI trends of 90.73% and 9.27%, respectively, of the study area. The strong imbalance between areas of increase and decrease indicates that anthropogenic activities have a dominant and largely positive influence on vegetation cover improvement across the region. In terms of the strength of this influence, the vegetation in the Taihang Mountains strongly responded to anthropogenic activities. The areas with extremely significant increasing NDVI trends accounted for the greatest share (78.44% of the total area) and were distributed mainly in central mountainous regions and areas with relatively low levels of human disturbance. In contrast, areas with decreasing NDVI were limited and characterized mainly by nonsignificant declines (4.58%). Significant and extremely significant decreases accounted for only 1.06% and 3.63% of the area, respectively, and were concentrated mainly in densely populated regions along the eastern corridor (Beijing–Shijiazhuang–Anyang–Xinxiang), southern Jiaozuo, and western Jincheng.

Under the influence of climatic factors alone (Fig. 7a), the NDVI trends across vegetation types significantly differed. The “CV” category and the “others” category showed relatively strong positive responses to climate change, with significant and extremely significant increases accounting for 45.57% and 49.40% of their respective areas, respectively. In stark contrast, natural forest and meadow ecosystems are substantially stressed by climate variability. CF was the most adversely affected, with 77.78% of its area experiencing varying degrees of decline (including 30.13% showing an extremely significant decrease). High proportions of climate-induced decreases were also observed in meadow (61.24%) and BF (54.21%), indicating a relatively weak climatic resilience of these natural vegetation types under recent climatic conditions.

In contrast, the effects of anthropogenic activities on the NDVI were predominantly positive and overwhelmingly robust across most vegetation types (Fig. 7b). Under positive human interventions, CF showed the greatest improvement, with extremely significant and significant increases accounting for an impressive 100.00% of its area. With the exception of CV and the “others” category, all the remaining vegetation types exhibited significant and extremely significant human-induced increases, consistently exceeding 75.45% (and mostly above 89.99%), highlighting the highly effective role of ecological engineering and management in promoting regional vegetation growth. The “others” category displayed the weakest positive response, with 48.33% of its area showing significant or extremely significant improvement. Vegetation degradation driven by anthropogenic activities was unevenly distributed and mostly limited to specific types. The “others” category experienced the greatest anthropogenic disturbance, with a total degradation proportion of 35.25% (including a 17.25% extremely significant decrease). CV followed, with a total degradation proportion of 13.04%, which was likely associated with urban expansion and agricultural restructuring. In contrast, human-induced degradation for natural vegetation types was relatively limited, consistently accounting for less than 5.11% of their respective areas.

Relative contributions of climate change and anthropogenic activities to changes in the NDVI for different vegetation types

On the basis of the simulation scenarios described in Table 2, the relative contributions of climatic factors and anthropogenic activities to vegetation dynamics in the Taihang Mountains were quantitatively assessed (Fig. 8). Overall, climatic factors accounted for approximately 13.84% of the NDVI variability, whereas anthropogenic activities strongly dominated vegetation dynamics, explaining approximately 86.16% of the observed changes. In vegetation improvement areas, the influence of climate change was limited, contributing only 13.76% to the increase in the NDVI. Most of these areas (78.25%) exhibited climate contribution rates between 0% and 20% (Fig. 8a). Regions where climate contributions exceeded 60% were rare (5.70%) and were located mainly in the eastern marginal zone of the Taihang Mountains. In contrast, anthropogenic activities played a decisive role in vegetation improvement (Fig. 8b), with an average contribution rate of 86.24%. Areas where human activity contributions exceeded 80% accounted for 78.25% of the improvement area, whereas regions with contributions less than 20% represented only 3.96%. In areas with vegetation degradation, the contribution of climate change increased slightly but remained secondary to anthropogenic influences. Climate change explained 14.87% of the decrease in the NDVI, with most degraded areas (79.52%) showing climate contribution rates less than 20% (Fig. 8c). Areas with high climate contributions (> 80%) were scarce, accounting for only 9.41% of the degraded area. In comparison, human activities were the dominant driver of vegetation degradation, contributing 85.13% overall. The areas where human activity contributions exceeded 60% accounted for 85.09% of the degraded regions (Fig. 8d), which were primarily distributed in low-elevation zones of the eastern Taihang Mountains and around urban areas with intensive human activity. Only 11.60% of the degraded area exhibited human contribution rates less than 40%.

Across all vegetation types, the contribution of anthropogenic activities to the improvement in the NDVI far exceeded that of climate change (Fig. 9a). Although the “others” category exhibited the lowest contribution of human activities to the improvement in the NDVI (73.44%), this anthropogenic factor still accounted for more than 80.85% across all the specific natural and CV types. The contribution of anthropogenic activities to most natural vegetation types exceeded 90%, reaching as high as 99.03% for CF. In contrast, the contribution of climate change to the improvement in the NDVI was relatively limited, ranging from 0.97% to 26.56%, with the lowest value observed in the CF category and the highest in the “others” category. In the vegetation degradation area (Fig. 9b), the influence of climate change became more pronounced; however, anthropogenic activities remained the dominant driver of the decrease in the NDVI across all the affected vegetation types. Notably, CF experienced no degradation pixels under the current land cover classification, further highlighting the absolute effectiveness of anthropogenic afforestation efforts. For the remaining vegetation types, the contribution of climate change to vegetation degradation ranged from 13.12% to 29.91%, with the lowest contribution occurring for CV and the highest for BF. Similarly, human activities accounted for more than 70.09% of the NDVI degradation for all the affected vegetation types, with the highest contribution observed for CV (86.88%) and the lowest for BF (70.09%). Overall, these results clearly demonstrate that anthropogenic activities are the primary drivers of both NDVI improvement and degradation across different vegetation types in the Taihang Mountains, whereas climate change plays a secondary but nonnegligible role.

Discussion

Spatiotemporal changes in the NDVI

From 2000 to 2024, vegetation cover in the Taihang Mountains showed a consistent increasing trend, which aligns with findings from previous studies27,29. The lowest recorded NDVI value during this period was 0.360 in 2001, a year marked by minimal rainfall and severe drought30. These arid conditions significantly restricted vegetation growth, leading to a pronounced decline in the NDVI. The NDVI subsequently showed a fluctuating but overall upward trend, reaching a peak of 0.469 by 2024. In recent decades, the sustained implementation of large-scale ecological projects—notably the Taihang Mountains Afforestation Program and the Taihang Mountains Ecological Restoration Project30,40–42—has been instrumental in driving the continued improvement in the NDVI across the region.

Spatially, the NDVI exhibited a pattern of higher values in the south, lower values in the northwest, and an intermixing of high and low values in the central region27, indicating pronounced spatial heterogeneity. The areas with high NDVI values in the southern and northeastern parts of the Taihang Mountains closely correspond to key areas for the continuous implementation of ecological restoration projects in the Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei Plain and the Taihang Mountains43,44. Conversely, the low-NDVI belt along the Zhangjiakou–Datong–Xinzhou line in the northwest reflects the vulnerability of vegetation within semiarid climate zones45. Future optimization of the Taihang Mountains Afforestation Project and the Taihang Mountains Ecological Restoration Project should prioritize steep slopes and ecologically fragile areas where vegetation cover remains low.

Notably, despite significant vegetation increasing over the past 25 years, predictions based on the Hurst index suggest that 56.97% of the Taihang Mountains are likely to experience a degradation trend. This trend is particularly pronounced in areas with high levels of anthropogenic activity, highlighting the uncertainty surrounding future vegetation changes driven by anthropogenic activities46. This suggests that recent greening does not necessarily guarantee long-term stability. Therefore, relying solely on historical NDVI trends may lead to the overestimation of long-term ecosystem resilience if persistence characteristics are not explicitly considered. Similar predictions of large-scale future vegetation degradation have been reported on the Tibetan Plateau47 and in Northeast China48. These findings collectively underscore that while vegetation management and ecological restoration efforts are advancing, strengthening the monitoring and protection of areas exhibiting degradation is crucial.

Impact mechanisms of climate change and anthropogenic activities on the NDVI

In this study, the relative influences of climatic factors (temperature, precipitation, and solar radiation) and anthropogenic activities on vegetation change were quantitatively assessed. On the basis of the current residual analysis framework, anthropogenic activities strongly dominated the variation in the NDVI, accounting for 86.16% of the variance, versus 13.84% for climatic factors. Human activities were the principal driver in both improving and degrading regions. Numerous studies have indicated that the influence of anthropogenic activities on vegetation is increasing38,49,50. In this study, approximately 90.73% of the Taihang Mountains showed an increasing NDVI trend influenced by anthropogenic activities, with highly significant increases dominating. This directly reflects the notable effectiveness of decades-long initiatives, such as afforestation projects51, the Grain-for-Green Program52, the Three-North Shelter Forest Program53, and the Beijing–Tianjin Sandstorm Source Control Project54. Large-scale ecological engineering was the main anthropogenic activity with the most significant impact on vegetation change in the Taihang Mountains and was a key driver of vegetation restoration.

Although human activities have become the primary driver of vegetation restoration, urbanization and resource development can inhibit vegetation growth. In the Taihang Mountains, 7.10% of the area showed a decreasing trend in vegetation. Among these areas, 85.09% were attributed to human activities, with a contribution rate exceeding 60%, and were distributed mainly in the eastern low-altitude zones and the peripheries of central and western cities. These areas are characterized by high population density and rapid urbanization55, where urban expansion has led to vegetation degradation.

Synergistic and antagonistic effects between climate and anthropogenic activities

Although residual analysis can effectively quantify the individual contributions of climate change and human activities, the complex interactions between these factors cannot be fully disentangled by this method. The vegetation dynamics in the Taihang Mountains were essentially determined by the coupled interactions of these two major drivers. In most areas, particularly in mid-elevation mountainous regions, anthropogenic activities and climate change strongly synergistic (positive coupling). Large-scale ecological restoration projects (e.g., afforestation projects and the Grain-for-Green Program), combined with the weak warming and wetting trend of recent decades56, have jointly promoted a significant increase in the NDVI. This synergistic interaction has driven vegetation greening to an extent markedly beyond that expected from the independent effect of either factor alone30,57.

In contrast, the low-elevation urban corridors in the eastern foothills (e.g., the Beijing–Shijiazhuang–Anyang–Jiaozuo belt) were dominated by an antagonistic effect (negative tug-of-war effect). In these areas, although climatic conditions such as rising temperatures may weakly favour vegetation growth, this positive effect was completely offset or overwhelmed by intense and rapid negative anthropogenic activities (e.g., urban expansion and encroachment by built-up land)58. Specifically, for the CV and “others” categories, the proportions of areas degraded by human disturbance reached 13.04% and 35.25%, respectively, indicating that the negative pressure from anthropogenic activities completely outweighed the positive benefits of climate change. Therefore, targeted urban growth management should be implemented in the eastern low-elevation corridor to curb further vegetation degradation caused by urban sprawl59.

Different responses of various vegetation types to influencing factors

Although the NDVI increased across all vegetation categories, both the magnitude of change and the dominant driving factors differed markedly among vegetation types. CF exhibited the most consistent and strongest improvement, with 100% of its area showing a human-induced increasing trend. This was attributed mainly to the widespread use of conifers (e.g., Pinus tabuliformis and Platycladus orientalis) as the primary afforestation species in the Taihang Mountains60, together with their long-term protection and management through ecological restoration projects. Furthermore, coniferous forests predominantly occur at relatively high elevations, where human disturbance is limited. Therefore, ecological restoration should be continued and strengthened in high-elevation coniferous forest zones. However, they were the most severely impacted by climatic factors, a vulnerability that was associated with their high-elevation distribution and heightened sensitivity to climatic variability61. Meadow and BF also exhibited high proportions of climate-driven reductions, suggesting their relatively low climatic resilience, whereas the “others” category was more vulnerable to negative human impacts—a pattern that aligns with the general trend of vegetation changes across different vegetation types in China62. In addition, the CV and “others” categories exhibited higher levels of degradation, likely reflecting their fragmented spatial distribution, frequent land use transitions, and lower resistance to external disturbance63.

Human activities were the primary driver of vegetation recovery across all vegetation types. These findings underscore the effectiveness of large-scale ecological restoration and land use management in mountainous regions. Nevertheless, human activities were also responsible for more than 70.09% of the degradation observed in each vegetation type. Vegetation degradation was concentrated mainly in low-elevation areas and along urban corridors on the eastern flank of the Taihang Mountains, where urban expansion and infrastructure development exert strong localized pressure64. This spatial pattern illustrates the dual influence of anthropogenic activities on vegetation dynamics, promoting large-scale ecological recovery while simultaneously driving localized degradation65.

Overall, the divergent responses among vegetation types suggest that the inherent resilience of ecosystems in the Taihang Mountains also plays an important role in shaping regional vegetation dynamics. Ecological pressures associated with land use change62 and urbanization66,67 therefore remain significant, highlighting the importance of balancing regional ecological restoration with targeted mitigation of localized human impacts.

Limitations and future research

In this study, the long-term trends, spatial patterns, and separate effects and contributions of climate change versus anthropogenic activities on vegetation in the Taihang Mountains were analysed primarily using remote sensing data. However, few insufficiencies currently exist. First, many factors influence vegetation growth. With respect to climatic factors, in this study, temperature, precipitation, and solar radiation were considered, but the effects of other elements, such as wind speed, relative humidity, and soil moisture, were neglected. Future research should integrate other critical climatic variables, such as the effects of CO2, topographic factors, and soil properties, to increase the reliability of vegetation change assessments68. Furthermore, the residual term may still contain unmeasured natural variability. Therefore, accurately disentangling the contribution of anthropogenic activities to vegetation recovery remains methodologically challenging30. Second, in terms of human activities, this study did not precisely quantify specific anthropogenic influences, such as land use, urban expansion, ecological restoration, and industrial disturbance. Third, the resampling of meteorological data may indeed smooth out the complex microclimatic differences in the Taihang Mountains. Because the residual method attributes all vegetation changes not explained by climate to anthropogenic disturbance, this could lead to a slight overestimation of the contribution rate of anthropogenic activities in some topographically complex pixels. Finally, this study did not explore the nonlinear interactions and feedback mechanisms between the NDVI and individual driving factors. Future research should employ methods such as random forest models, decision tree models, and explainable artificial intelligence methods69 to further clarify the driving factors of vegetation coverage and their complex relationships, thereby achieving a deeper mechanistic understanding.

Conclusions

We analysed vegetation cover changes in the Taihang Mountains from 2000 to 2024 and quantified the influence and relative contributions of climate change and anthropogenic activities to vegetation dynamics. The results revealed that during the past 25 years, vegetation coverage in the study area increased significantly (p < 0.01) at a rate of 0.0036 per year. High NDVI values were primarily found in the southern regions, whereas low values were concentrated in the northwest. Over the 25 years, the vast majority (92.90%) of the Taihang Mountains exhibited an overall increasing NDVI trend, whereas 7.10% showed a degrading trend. Future trend predictions indicate that 43.04% of the area will show an improvement trend, whereas 56.97% will show a degradation trend, challenging assumptions of long-term stability based on recent greening.

Human activities have become the primary driver of vegetation cover change in the Taihang Mountains, explaining 86.16% of the observed variation in the NDVI. Specifically, restoration efforts, such as afforestation, have substantially increased vegetation coverage across extensive areas of the region. In contrast, urbanization and related land use changes have reduced vegetation cover, particularly in the eastern low-elevation zones and in peri-urban areas of the central and western Taihang Mountains.

Different vegetation types showed distinct responses to the driving factors. CF exhibited the strongest human-driven improvement and the highest climatic sensitivity, whereas the CV and the “others” categories were most prone to human-induced degradation because of their fragmented landscapes and lower resilience.
