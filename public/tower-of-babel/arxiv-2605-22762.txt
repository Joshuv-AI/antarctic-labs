Abstract
Every transitive cellular automaton (CA) is sensitive to initial conditions. We study this implication in the more general context of non-uniform cellular automata (NUCA) with finitely many different local update rules assigned to cells. We construct a two-dimensional NUCA that is minimal – and hence transitive – but that is not sensitive to initial conditions. The construction is based on an odometer NUCA on { 0 , 1 , 2 } ℕ \{0,1,2\}^{\mathbb{N}} which is nearly uniform in the sense that only the first cell uses a different local rule. Then we show that if the assignment of local rules in the cells is recurrent then transitivity implies sensitivity.



1
Introduction

Classical cellular automata (CA) are homogeneous both in space and time. All cells use the same local update rule, and the same rule is used at all discrete time steps. A non-uniform cellular automaton (NUCA) is a generalization where different cells may use different update rules. However, in our formalism, there are only finitely many different rules available, and the assignment of rules – called the rule distribution – does not change over time. Non-uniformity greatly changes possible behaviors of the system. While every CA has temporally periodic points, there are NUCAs – even reversible equicontinuous ones – that do not have any temporally periodic points
[
3
]
. Also the classical Garden of Eden theorem
[
5
,
7
]
of cellular automata that links injectivity and surjectivity does not hold for all NUCAs. However, if the local rule distribution is uniformly recurrent, or recurrent in the one-dimensional case, the Garden of Eden theorem holds
[
8
]
. A one-dimensional CA is either sensitive or almost equicontinuous
[
4
]
, but this dichotomy breaks for one-dimensional NUCA
[
9
]
.

In this work we investigate the relationship of transitivity and sensitivity in NUCA. If a uniform cellular automaton is transitive then it is also sensitive
[
2
]
. We show that this implication holds also among NUCA with a recurrent rule distribution. On the other hand, we construct a two-dimensional NUCA (with a non-recurrent rule distribution) that is minimal – and hence transitive – but equicontinuous – and hence not sensitive. In the construction we use a one-way cellular automaton with an odometer behavior.

Finally, we give definitions for a few dynamical properties. These are direct translations of
the corresponding standard properties on general discrete time topological dynamical systems to our setting. We start with sensitivity and equicontinuity.

Definition 7

Let
\Sigma

,

Equicontinuous systems are stable while sensitive ones are unstable. Next we define several variants of transitivity. We give the definitions for general dynamical systems.

Definition 8

Let

\displaystyle A_{U,V}=\{t\in\mathbb{N}\ |\ F^{t}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset\}.

•

•

•

for any non-empty open

In the case

for arbitrary cylinders.

Definition 9

Let
S
be a compact metric space and
F:S\rightarrow S

For any discrete time dynamical system, we have the trivial relations that a mixing system is weakly mixing, syndetically transitive and totally transitive, and a system is transitive if it is minimal, weakly mixing, strongly transitive, syndetically transitive, or totally transitive. In uniform CA, transitivity, total transitivity, weakly mixing and syndetic transitivity are mutually equivalent
[
6
]
. It is an open problem whether all transitive or strongly transitive uniform CA are mixing.

The following example NUCA is strongly transitive (and hence transitive) but not totally transitive or weakly mixing (and hence not mixing). It is also syndetically transitive.

Example 1

Let
\Sigma=\{0,1\}
and
N=\{-1,0,1\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{R}=\{f_{\rightarrow},f_{\leftarrow},g\}
be a set of local rules
\Sigma^{N}\rightarrow\Sigma
where

\begin{array}[]{rcll}f_{\rightarrow}(p)&=&p(-1)&\mbox{(the right shift)}\\
f_{\leftarrow}(p)&=&p(1)&\mbox{(the left shift)}\\
g(p)&=&p(0)\oplus 1&\mbox{(the toggle)}\end{array}

where
p\in\Sigma^{N}
and
\oplus
is the modulo 2 sum. Let
\theta\in\mathcal{R}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be a rule distribution where

\displaystyle\theta(x)=\begin{cases}f_{\rightarrow},&\text{if }x<0,\\
g,&\text{if }x=0,\\
f_{\leftarrow},&\text{if }x>0,\end{cases}

where
x\in\mathbb{Z}
. The NUCA
H_{\theta}
is strongly transitive (and hence transitive), but neither totally transitive nor weakly mixing (and hence not mixing).

First to show that
H_{\theta}
is strongly transitive. Let
c,e\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}}
be any two configurations and
D\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
a finite domain. To prove strong transitivity we show that there is
e^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Cyl}(c,D)
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
H_{\theta}^{t}(e^{\prime})=e
. Let
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
be even if
c(0)=e(0)
and odd if
c(0)\neq e(0)
, and such that
D\subseteq[-r,r]
. Let
e^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Cyl}(c,[-r,r])\subseteq\mathrm{Cyl}(c,D)
be the configuration such that

e^{\prime}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e(x+r),&\mbox{ for $x<-r$,}\\
c(x),&\mbox{ for $x\in[-r,r]$,}\\
e(x-r),&\mbox{ for $x>r$.}\end{array}\right.

For all
x<0
we have
H_{\theta}^{r}(e^{\prime})(x)=e^{\prime}(x-r)=e(x)
. Similarly, for all

x>0
we have
H_{\theta}^{r}(e^{\prime})(x)=e^{\prime}(x+r)=e(x)
. And because
H_{\theta}
toggles

times the bit at cell
0
, we also have
H_{\theta}^{r}(e^{\prime})(0)=e(0)
. So

as required.

The NUCA

is not weakly mixing, because for any configurations

such that
c(0)=e(0)
, and any

,

. It is not totally transitive, because
H_{\theta}^{2}
is not transitive. This is because for any

,
H_{\theta}^{2}(c)(0)=c(0)\oplus 1\oplus 1=c(0)
. If we have configurations

such that

and hence

Theorem 3.1

5
Recurrent rule distributions

We have seen that there are transitive NUCAs that are not necessarily sensitive. However, if the rule distribution is recurrent then transitivity implies sensitivity.

Theorem 5.1

Let
\theta\in\mathcal{R}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be a recurrent rule distribution such that
H_{\theta}:\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rightarrow\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is transitive. Then
H_{\theta}
is sensitive.

Proof

Suppose
H_{\theta}
is not sensitive. Let us first prove that, for some
n>0
, the traces of all configurations at cell
\vec{0}
have period
n
.

Because
H_{\theta}
is not sensitive, there is some
D\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
c\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
such that for any
e\in\mathrm{Cyl}(c,D)
and
t\geq 0
,
H_{\theta}^{t}(e)(\vec{0})=H_{\theta}^{t}(c)(\vec{0})
. Because
H_{\theta}
is transitive, there is some
e\in\mathrm{Cyl}(c,D)
and
n>0
such that
H_{\theta}^{n}(e)\in\mathrm{Cyl}(c,D)
. Then for any
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle H_{\theta}^{t}(c)(\vec{0})=H_{\theta}^{t}(H_{\theta}^{n}(e))(\vec{0})=H_{\theta}^{n+t}(e)(\vec{0})=H_{\theta}^{n+t}(c)(\vec{0}),

meaning that the trace
T=(H_{\theta}^{t}(c)(\vec{0}))_{t\geq 0}
of
c
at cell
\vec{0}
has period
n
.

Consider then an arbitrary configuration
e\in\Sigma^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, and denote

a_{t}=H_{\theta}^{t}(e)(\vec{0})
for all
t\geq 0
.
Let
r>0
be such that the rules in
\mathcal{R}
have at most radius
r
. Obviously for any
e^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Cyl}(e,[-nr,nr]^{d})
, we have
H_{\theta}^{t}(e^{\prime})(\vec{0})=a_{t}
when
0\leq t\leq n
. Because
H_{\theta}
is transitive, there is some
k\geq 0
such that

H_{\theta}^{k}(\mathrm{Cyl}(c,D))\cap\mathrm{Cyl}(e,[-nr,nr]^{d})\neq\emptyset
. Then
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
is part of the trace
T
starting at time
k

a_{t}=T_{k+t}
when
0\leq t\leq n
. Because
T
has period
n
, we have
a_{0}=a_{n}
. As
e
was arbitrary, it follows that the trace
(H_{\theta}^{t}(e)(\vec{0}))_{t\geq 0}
of every configuration
e
at cell
\vec{0}
has period
n
.

6
Conclusions

In uniform cellular automata, transitivity implies sensitivity. Additionally, transitivity, syndetic transitivity, total transitivity and weak mixing are mutually equivalent, and both mixing and strong transitivity imply transitivity. However, it is open whether transitivity or strong transitivity implies mixing.
A simple counter-example (Example
1
) shows that there are strongly transitive (and hence transitive) non-uniform cellular automata, which are not mixing, weakly mixing, or totally transitive.

We’ve shown that if a NUCA is defined by a recurrent rule distribution, it is sensitive if it is transitive. We’ve also shown an example of a minimal (and hence transitive), non-sensitive NUCA on
\mathbb{N}
(as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
), and a minimal, non-sensitive two-dimensional NUCA, which can easily be extended to higher dimensions. It remains open whether there exists a transitive and non-sensitive NUCA on
\mathbb{Z}
. It is also open whether all transitive NUCA are syndetically transitive.
