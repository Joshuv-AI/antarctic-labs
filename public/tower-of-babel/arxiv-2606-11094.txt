Edge-regular graphs with non-negative curvature have polynomial growth
Guy Blachar, Hervé Pajot, Justin Salez

Abstract
A long-standing conjecture in the emerging discrete Bakry-Émery theory asserts that bounded-degree graphs satisfying CD ⁡ ( 0 , ∞ ) \mathrm{CD}(0,\infty) have polynomial growth. In the present paper, we prove this conjecture for all edge-regular graphs, and even obtain a volume doubling estimate with a constant that depends only on the degree. This is made possible thanks to the discovery of a surprising self-improvement phenomenon, which seems of independent interest: any edge-regular graph satisfying CD ⁡ ( κ , ∞ ) \mathrm{CD}(\kappa,\infty) for some κ ∈ ℝ \kappa\in\mathbb{R} must in fact satisfy CD ⁡ ( κ , n ) \mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n) for some explicit, universal and optimal dimension parameter n n .

Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a locally finite, connected, simple, undirected graph, and let
\Delta
denote its Laplacian operator, which acts on any function
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
as follows:

\displaystyle\forall x\in V,\qquad\Delta f(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in N_{x}}\left(f(y)-f(x)\right),

(1)

where
N_{x}:=\{y\in V\colon\{x,y\}\in E\}
is the set of neighbors of
x
. Following
[
1
,
26
,
13
]
, we define the carré du champ operator
\Gamma_{1}
and its iterated version
\Gamma_{2}
inductively via

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}(f,g)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta\Gamma_{k-1}(f,g)-\Gamma_{k-1}(f,\Delta g)-\Gamma_{k-1}(g,\Delta f)\right),

(2)

for all
f,g\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
, with
\Gamma_{0}(f,g):=fg
. Given two parameters
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in(0,\infty]
, we then say that
G
satisfies the
Curvature-Dimension
condition
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n)
if

\displaystyle\forall f\in\mathbb{R}^{V},\quad\Gamma_{2}(f,f)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\kappa\,\Gamma_{1}(f,f)+\frac{(\Delta f)^{2}}{n}.

(3)

As observed by Bakry and Émery in their seminal work
[
1
]
, this pointwise inequality holds when
\Delta
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator of a
n-
dimensional Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature at least
\kappa
, and it turns out to have a number of remarkable analytical, geometric and probabilistic consequences for the associated diffusion. We refer the unfamiliar reader to the excellent textbook
[
2
]
for an introduction to this beautiful theory, and to
[
23
,
24
,
20
]
for recent connections with the cutoff phenomenon.

In recent years, considerable efforts have been made to develop a discrete analogue of the Bakry-Émery theory, where manifolds are replaced by graphs
[
26
,
13
,
4
,
11
,
15
,
14
,
16
,
7
,
5
,
12
,
10
,
25
]
. Unfortunately, several important pieces are still missing, due to the notorious failure of the chain rule for differentiating composed functions on discrete spaces. In particular, the following fundamental relation between curvature and volume is still lacking, and constitutes one of the most frustrating gaps between the Riemannian Bakry-Émery theory and its emerging discrete counterpart. Throughout the paper, we write
{\mathrm{dist}}(\cdot,\cdot)
for the usual graph distance, and
B(x,r)
for the corresponding open ball of radius
r
around
x
:

\displaystyle B(x,r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{y\in V\colon{\mathrm{dist}}(x,y)<r\right\}.

(4)

Conjecture 1

(Non-negative curvature implies polynomial growth)
.

If
G
has bounded degrees and satisfies
\mathrm{CD}(0,\infty)
, then
G
has polynomial growth. More precisely,

\displaystyle\forall x\in V,\quad\forall r\in\mathbb{N},\quad\#B(x,r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r^{D},

(5)

where the constant
D<\infty
depends only on the maximum degree.

The degree restriction is of course necessary here, since choosing
r=2
in (
5
) shows that
D
is at least logarithmic in the maximum degree.
Conjecture
1
has received a lot of attention
[
8
,
18
,
21
]
, and several results have been establish in its direction
[
3
,
22
,
17
,
9
]
. In particular, a major breakthrough was recently obtained by Hutchcroft and Münch
[
9
]
, who proved that bounded-degree graphs with non-negative curvature in the (different) sense of Ollivier
[
19
]
have sub-exponential growth. Another remarkable result in the direction of Conjecture
1
is the fact that polynomial growth is indeed guaranteed when the assumption
\mathrm{CD}(0,\infty)
is strengthened to its finite-dimensional refinement
\mathrm{CD}(0,n)
, for any
n<\infty
. More precisely, the following
Volume Doubling
estimate was recently obtained by Münch
[
18
]
for finite graphs and by Pajot and Russ
[
21
]
in the infinite case (see also
[
3
]
for a closely related, wearker result).

Theorem 1

(
\mathrm{CD}(0,n)
implies Volume Doubling, c.f.
[
18
,
21
]
)
.

If
G
has bounded degrees and satisfies
\mathrm{CD}(0,n)
for some
n<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\forall x\in V,\quad\forall r\in\mathbb{N},\quad\#B(x,2r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\#B(x,r),

(6)

where the constant
C<\infty
depends only on
n
and the maximum degree.

Note that this volume doubling estimate can readily be iterated to yield a polynomial growth estimate of the form (
5
), with
D=1+\log_{2}C
. Unfortunately, the constant
C
obtained in
[
18
,
21
]
tends to infinity with
n
, and new ideas seem to be needed to reach Conjecture
1
.

In the present paper, we establish Conjecture
1
for all edge-regular graphs. Let us here recall that
G
is
edge-regular
if there exist two integers
d,\tau\geq 0
such that any vertex has exactly
d
neighbors, and any two adjacent vertices have exactly
\tau
common neighbors:

\displaystyle\forall x\in V,\quad\#N_{x}\ =\ d,

and

\displaystyle\forall\{x,y\}\in E,\quad\#(N_{x}\cap N_{y})\ =\ \tau.

(7)

For example, any triangle-free regular graph is edge-regular, as well as any Cayley graph generated by a conjugacy class. Our main contribution is the surprising discovery that an edge-regular graph satisfying
\mathrm{CD}(0,\infty)
must actually satisfy the stronger property
\mathrm{CD}(0,d)
, where
d
is the degree. Our result is in fact substantially stronger: for any value of the curvature parameter
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, we determine the optimal value of the dimension parameter
n
such that
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,\infty)
is equivalent to
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n)
, simultaneously for all graphs satisfying (
7
).

Theorem 2

(Self-improvement of the Bakry-Émery condition on edge-regular graphs)
.

Fix
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and two integers
d,\tau\geq 0
, and suppose that
G
satisfies
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,\infty)
and (
7
). Then,

1.

2\kappa\leq 4+\tau
;

2.

\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n)
holds for
n:=4d/(4+\tau-2\kappa)
.

3.

\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n^{\prime})
fails for any
n^{\prime}<n
.

In particular, any edge-regular graph satisfying
\mathrm{CD}(0,\infty)
automatically satisfies
\mathrm{CD}(0,d)
where
d
is the degree, as promised. Combining this with Theorem
1
, we deduce that Conjecture
1
holds, in a strong sense, for all edge-regular graphs.

Corollary 1

(Volume doubling for edge-regular graphs with non-negative curvature)
.

If
G
is edge-regular and satisfies
\mathrm{CD}(0,\infty)
, then the Volume Doubling property (
6
) holds with a constant
C
that depends only on the degree. In particular,
G
satisfies Conjecture
1
.

Whether the edge-regularity assumption in Theorem
2
can be relaxed is a natural question, with direct implications for Conjecture
1
. We note that at least
some
restrictions are necessary. Indeed, in Section
3
, we will construct a
7-
vertex graph satisfying
\mathrm{CD}(0,\infty)
but not
\mathrm{CD}(0,n)
for any
n<\infty
.
We were not able to produce a regular graph with those properties, and we therefore propose the following natural strengthening of Theorem
2
.

Conjecture 2
.

Any
d-
regular graph satisfying
\mathrm{CD}(0,\infty)
satisfies
\mathrm{CD}(0,d)
.

2
Proof of the main result

We will naturally say that the
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n)
condition holds at a vertex
o\in V
if

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f,f)(o)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\kappa\,\Gamma_{1}(f,f)(o)+\frac{(\Delta f(o))^{2}}{n},

(8)

for all
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
. Our proof of Theorem
2
relies on the construction of
height functions
.

Definition 1

(Height functions)
.

Given two parameters
\kappa,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and a vertex
o\in V
, we say that
g\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)-
height function at
o
if
\Delta g(o)=1
and for all
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f,g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\,\Gamma_{1}(f,g)(o)+\lambda\,\Delta f(o).

The interest of this mysterious definition is contained in the following result, which shows that height functions systematically provide an optimal self-improvement in the local Bakry-Émery criterion. As the reader will see, the proof does not use the specific form of the discrete Laplacian (
1
): it applies to any Markov generator
\Delta
on any state space. The converse part will not be used here, but it guarantees that there is no intrinsic limitation in using height functions as a general tool to establish curvature-dimension estimates.

Lemma 1

(Height functions yield optimal Bakry-Émery self-improvement)
.

If
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,\infty)
holds at
o
and if there exists a
(\kappa,\lambda)-
height function at
o
, then

1.

\lambda\geq 0
;

2.

\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n)
holds at
o
, with
n:=1/\lambda
.

3.

\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n^{\prime})
fails at
o
, for any
n^{\prime}<n
.

Conversely, if
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n)
holds at
o
for some
n<\infty
and if
V
has at least two points, then there exists a
(\kappa,\lambda)-
height function at
o
for some
\lambda\geq 1/n
.

Proof.

The fact that
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,\infty)
holds at
o
ensures that the bilinear symmetric form

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,g)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f,g)(o)-\kappa\,\Gamma_{1}(f,g)(o),

(9)

is positive semi-definite. Thus, it satisfies
\mathcal{Q}(g,g)\geq 0
and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{2}(f,g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,f)\mathcal{Q}(g,g),

(10)

for all
f,g\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
. On the other hand, if
g
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)-
height function at
o
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(g,g)\ =\ \lambda,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,g)\ =\ \lambda\,\Delta f(o),

for any
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
. Thus, we must have
\lambda\geq 0
, and (
10
) becomes

\displaystyle\lambda\,\left(\Delta f(o)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,f).

(11)

But this precisely means that
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n)
holds at
o
, with
n:=1/\lambda
. Moreover, this value is optimal, because (
11
) is an equality when
f=g
. Conversely, if
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n)
holds at
o
for some
n<\infty
, then the linear form
f\mapsto\Delta f(o)
is continuous w.r.t. the semi-norm induced by the positive semi-definite bilinear symmetric form (
9
), so the Riesz representation theorem ensures the existence of
g\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
such that

\displaystyle\forall f\in\mathbb{R}^{V},\quad\Delta f(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,g).

(12)

Note that we must then have
\mathcal{Q}(g,g)>0
, as otherwise the map
f\mapsto\Delta f(o)
would be identically zero, contradicting the fact that
G
is connected with at least two points. Thus, we can safely divide
g
by
\mathcal{Q}(g,g)
to obtain a
(\kappa,\lambda)-
height function, where
\lambda=1/\mathcal{Q}(g,g)
. But then, our assumption that
\mathrm{CD}(\kappa,n)
holds at
o
forces
n\geq 1/\lambda
, by Item 2 in the lemma.
∎

In order to apply the above lemma, we now need to construct height functions. This is precisely the aim of the next lemma, in which the edge-regularity assumption is crucially used.
When combined together, Lemmas
1
and
2
readily imply Theorem
2
.

Lemma 2

(Existence of height functions)
.

If
G
satisfies the edge-regularity property (
7
), then for every
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and every
o\in V
, there is a
(\kappa,\lambda)-
height function at
o
, with

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{4+\tau-2\kappa}{4d}.

(13)

Proof.

Given
o\in V
, we will show that the function
g\colon x\mapsto{\mathrm{dist}}(o,x)
satisfies

\displaystyle(\Delta g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d,

(14)

\displaystyle 2\Gamma_{1}(f,g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta f(o),

(15)

\displaystyle 4\Gamma_{2}(f,g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4+\tau)\Delta f(o),

(16)

for all
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
. Clearly, the normalized function
g/d
is then a
(\kappa,\lambda)-
height function, for any
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda
as in (
13
). Now, the first two claims are immediate: we have

\displaystyle\Delta g(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in N_{o}}\left(g(x)-g(o)\right)\ =\ d,

(17)

\displaystyle 2\Gamma_{1}(f,g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in N_{o}}\left(f(x)-f(o)\right)\left(g(x)-g(o)\right)\ =\ \Delta f(o),

(18)

for every
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
, because
g(x)-g(o)=1
for all
x\in N_{o}
. To prove the third claim, we will now explicitate each of the three terms in the definition of
4\Gamma_{2}(f,g)(o)
:

\displaystyle 4\Gamma_{2}(f,g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Delta\Gamma_{1}(f,g)(o)-2\Gamma_{1}(f,\Delta g)(o)-2\Gamma_{1}(\Delta f,g)(o).

(19)

The third term is readily obtained by replacing
f
with
\Delta f
in (
18
):

\displaystyle 2\Gamma_{1}(\Delta f,g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}f(o).

(20)

We now fix
x\in N_{o}
and
y\in N_{x}
, and observe that
g(y)-g(x)
is
-1
if
y=o
,
0
if
y\in N_{x}\cap N_{o}
, and
+1
in the remaining cases. In view of our edge-regularity assumption, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Delta g(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in N_{x}}\left(g(y)-g(x)\right)\ =\ d-2-\tau.

Recalling that
\Delta g(o)=d
, we can now compute the second term in (
19
):

\displaystyle 2\Gamma_{1}(f,\Delta g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in N_{o}}\left(f(x)-f(o)\right)\left(\Delta g(x)-\Delta g(o)\right)\ =\ -(\tau+2)\Delta f(o).

(21)

Finally, in order to compute the first term in (
19
), we fix again
x\in N_{o}
and use the above discussion about the possible values of
g(y)-g(x)
to write

\displaystyle 2\Gamma_{1}(f,g)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in N_{x}}\left(f(y)-f(x)\right)\left(g(y)-g(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta f(x)+2\left(f(x)-f(o)\right)+\sum_{y\in N_{x}\cap N_{o}}\left(f(x)-f(y)\right).

Substracting (
18
) and summing over all
x\in N_{o}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle 2\Delta\Gamma_{1}(f,g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}f(o)+2\Delta f(o)+\sum_{x\in N_{o}}\sum_{y\in N_{x}\cap N_{o}}\left(f(x)-f(y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}f(o)+2\Delta f(o),

where we have crucially observed that the function
(x,y)\mapsto f(x)-f(y)
is anti-symmetric, and that the double sum runs over all pairs
(x,y)
in
N_{o}^{2}\cap E
, which is a symmetric set. Having explicited each term in (
19
), we can now add them up and simplify to obtain

\displaystyle 4\Gamma_{2}(f,g)(o)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\tau+4)\Delta f(o),

(22)

as desired.
∎
