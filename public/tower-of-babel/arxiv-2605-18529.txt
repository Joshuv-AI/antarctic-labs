AMR-SD: Asymmetric Meta-Reflective Self-Distillation for Token-Level Credit Assignment
Zhenlin Wei, Pu Jian, Yingzhuo Deng, Xiaohan Wang, Jiajun Chai, Zhexin Hu, Wei Lin, Shanbin Zhang, Guojun Yin

Abstract
The alignment of Large Language Models (LLMs) for complex reasoning heavily relies on Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). However, standard algorithms like GRPO apply sequence-level rewards uniformly to all tokens, creating a severe credit-assignment bottleneck. While on-policy self-distillation attempts to resolve this by conditioning a self-teacher on privileged contexts, direct exposure to raw oracle solutions often induces over-conditioned teacher distributions, implicit answer leakage, and late-stage training collapse. To overcome these limitations, we propose Asymmetric Meta-Reflective Self-Distillation (AMR-SD). Instead of conditioning directly on raw reference traces, AMR-SD inserts a reflection bottleneck: it compresses diagnostic signals—from verifier outcomes, peer rollouts, or reference feedback—into concise, self-generated Socratic hints and critiques. Furthermore, we introduce Causal Information Gain (CIG) with an asymmetric, ReLU-gated threshold to translate these reflections into sparse, highly precise token-level advantage modulations. Combined with temporal annealing, this mechanism preserves the base environmental reward while filtering out distributional noise. Experiments across scientific, mathematical, and tool-use benchmarks demonstrate that AMR-SD significantly outperforms existing baselines, achieving robust long-horizon stability and successfully preventing late-stage collapse.

Introduction

The alignment of Large Language Models (LLMs) for complex reasoning heavily relies on Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)
28
;
21
. Standard policy gradient algorithms, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
4
;
15
, efficiently estimate advantages from sparse sequence-level outcomes. However, GRPO applies a uniform advantage scalar to all tokens within a rollout. This creates a severe credit-assignment bottleneck: critical logical deductions and superficial stylistic choices receive identical reward signals, hampering the optimization of reasoning tasks
26
;
10
;
23
;
8
.

To resolve this credit-assignment bottleneck, offline Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) offers dense supervision but suffers from severe train-test distribution shifts. To bridge the gap between RL’s on-policy exploration and SFT’s dense supervision, the community turned to On-Policy Distillation (OPD)
1
. While highly effective
22
;
19
, maintaining an external teacher incurs prohibitive computational overhead. Consequently, On-Policy Self-Distillation emerged, utilizing the model itself as the teacher conditioned on privileged information (e.g., ground-truth solutions) (Figure
1
)
30
;
5
;
16
;
29
. However, directly forcing a conditionally independent student to match a conditionally dependent teacher introduces information asymmetry. The student blindly imitates confident reasoning without accessing the underlying context, leading to privileged information leakage, degraded intrinsic reflective capabilities, and late-stage training collapse
6
;
9
.

Recent advancements attempt to mitigate this by anchoring optimization to environment rewards for continuous magnitude adjustment
25
;
7
. Unfortunately, a subtle limitation remains: because the privileged context narrows the teacher’s output distribution onto specific oracle tokens, the teacher’s probabilities for most student-generated tokens are systematically lower than the student’s own. This causes the continuous multipliers to be predominantly less than 1, dampening the primary reward signal. Furthermore, the privileged information here is often simply the directly provided ground truth; directly relying on it during training is clearly inefficient. Consequently, such global distribution alignment inevitably allows privileged information to implicitly leak into the parameters, inducing late-stage training collapse.

To fully unlock self-distillation without these drawbacks, we propose the Asymmetric Meta-Reflective Self-Distillation (AMR-SD) framework, which retains the reward-anchoring principle of
25
while replacing continuous
scaling with an asymmetric, ReLU-gated threshold (
\tau
), powered by a novel metric: Causal Information Gain (CIG). To mitigate direct oracle-solution leakage, AMR-SD operates as a reflection-mediated self-training loop. Instead of exposing the teacher to raw ground-truth solutions during token rescoring, we enforce a Meta-Reflection phase: the model generates an encouraging
<hint>
for successful trajectories or a targeted
<critique>
for failed trajectories, using verifier-approved peer rollouts when available, which then serve as the conditioning context for the teacher.

Crucially, to overcome the signal dampening inherent in continuous scaling, CIG introduces an asymmetric, threshold-gated filtering mechanism. Rather than diluting the reward by modulating every token, this mechanism acts as a strict gatekeeper: it preserves the baseline environmental reward intact by default, triggering sparse, targeted adjustments only when the Socratic teacher identifies a highly confident divergence from the student. This approach effectively filters out trivial distributional noise, delivering precise token-level credit assignment without compromising the overall strength of the base reinforcement signal.

We emphasize that AMR-SD does not entirely discard privileged information (e.g., verifier outcomes, peer rollouts, or reference answers). Instead, it prevents the token-rescoring teacher from directly seeing raw oracle traces. Diagnostic sources are first compressed into concise natural-language reflections, acting as a low-bandwidth bottleneck. This mitigates over-conditioned teacher distributions and answer leakage while preserving essential token-level guidance for credit assignment.

Our main contributions are threefold:

•

We introduce Meta-Reflection, prompting the model to generate Socratic self-teaching targets (
hint
/
critique
) from verifier outcomes, on-policy peer rollouts, or reference-assisted diagnostic signals when available. This reflection-mediated paradigm does not remove privileged information, but introduces a low-bandwidth bottleneck between diagnostic sources and token-level rescoring, thereby reducing direct oracle-trace conditioning and improving calibration of token-level credit signals.

•

We design CIG as a threshold-gated (ReLU) advantage adjustment mechanism. Integrated with temporal annealing, it concentrates dense supervision during early learning and smoothly shifts to unadulterated environmental rewards, ensuring rapid early alignment and long-horizon stability.

•

Building upon these mechanisms, we propose the AMR-SD framework. Extensive experiments across scientific, mathematical, and tool-use benchmarks demonstrate that AMR-SD outperforms existing baselines, achieving robust long-horizon stability and successfully preventing the late-stage training collapse.

3
Method

We introduce the AMR-SD framework. Unlike standard sequence-level RL which broadcasts a uniform advantage to all tokens, AMR-SD resolves the spatial credit-assignment bottleneck by dynamically mapping the sequence-level GRPO advantage into a dense, state-action specific tensor.

Formally, we model the language generation process as a MDP defined by the tuple
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{R})
. Let the state
s_{t}=(x,y_{<t})\in\mathcal{S}
denote the prompt and the generated prefix, and the action
a_{t}=y_{t}\in\mathcal{A}
represent the current token. To construct a precise token-level advantage
\widehat{A}_{i,t}
without suffering from the information leakage inherent in traditional self-distillation, our framework operates in three sequential phases: Socratic Rescoring via Meta-Reflection, CIG quantification, and Asymmetric Modulation.The full procedure is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

3.1
Socratic Rescoring via Meta-Reflection

To construct a dense reward signal without human annotation, we formalize a Meta-Reflection mechanism that transitions the stop-gradient policy
\pi_{\theta_{\text{sg}}}
into a conditional Socratic evaluator. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the latent space of natural language reflections. We define a discrete conditional mapping function
f_{\text{reflect}}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}^{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{C}
governed by the signum of the empirical advantage:

\begin{split}c_{i}&=f_{\text{reflect}}(x,y_{i},\text{sgn}(A_{i}))\\
&=\mathbb{I}_{\{A_{i}\geq 0\}}\cdot c_{\text{hint}}+\mathbb{I}_{\{A_{i}<0\}}\cdot c_{\text{critique}}\end{split}

(1)

where
\mathbb{I}_{\{\cdot\}}
denotes the indicator function. The hint reflection
c_{\text{hint}}
extracts latent reasoning trajectories for successful rollouts, while the critique reflection
c_{\text{critique}}
isolates logical or syntactic divergences in failed ones.

Crucially, the Meta-Reflection phase is executed in-situ by the current student policy
\pi_{\theta}
itself. By generating hints and critiques as intermediate Socratic reflections, AMR-SD does not expose the rescoring teacher to raw oracle answers or full ground-truth reasoning traces. Instead, outcome information is first compressed into concise, structured natural-language guidance. This lower-bandwidth conditioning mitigates the over-conditioning and answer-leakage risks of directly conditioning the teacher on oracle solutions, while still providing token-level guidance for credit assignment.

The teacher model subsequently evaluates the trajectory via forced decoding over the augmented context vectors
[x\oplus c_{i}\oplus y_{i}]
. This operation yields a conditional state-transition probability distribution, defined as
\pi_{\theta_{\text{sg}}}(a_{t}\mid s_{t},c_{i})
. By injecting
c_{i}
, we effectively break the standard autoregressive Markov assumption, allowing the teacher to retrospectively assess action
a_{t}
given the terminal trajectory outcome encoded in
c_{i}
.

3.2
Causal Information Gain (CIG)

To quantify the granular contribution of each action, we introduce CIG. From an information-theoretic perspective, CIG measures the pointwise log-likelihood ratio between the context-aware Socratic teacher and the student policy. For an action
a_{t}
at state
s_{t}
, the raw CIG is computed as:

\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\text{CIG}}=\log\left(\frac{\pi_{\theta_{\text{sg}}}(a_{t}\mid s_{t},c_{i})}{\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid s_{t})}\right)

(2)

A strictly positive
\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\text{CIG}}
indicates that the transition
(s_{t},a_{t})
is assigned a low probability by the student but a high likelihood by the informed teacher, signaling a knowledge deficit. Conversely,
\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\text{CIG}}<0
implies student over-confidence relative to the Socratic baseline.

To guarantee bounded Lipschitz continuity in the subsequent gradient updates and mitigate numerical instability caused by the heavy-tailed nature of KL-divergence, we apply a symmetric clamping operator to the information gain:

\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{\text{CIG}}=\operatorname{clip}(\mathcal{I}_{t}^{\text{CIG}},-\kappa,\kappa)

(3)

where
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
defines the maximum allowed informational deviation. Based on our empirical observations of the CIG distribution (detailed in Appendix
E
), we constrain this bound to
\kappa=5
to mitigate numerical instability while preserving meaningful signals.

3.3
Asymmetric Token-Level Advantage Modulation

We integrate the localized CIG with the global trajectory objective through a non-linear, asymmetric modulation scalar
\Delta_{t}
. The asymmetry is mathematically parameterized to decouple the gradient flows for positive and negative rollouts, utilizing a noise-filtering threshold
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
:

\begin{split}\Delta_{t}=\;&\mathbb{I}_{\{A_{i}\geq 0\}}\cdot\lambda_{\text{eff}}\max(0,\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{\text{CIG}}-\tau)\\
&+\mathbb{I}_{\{A_{i}<0\}}\cdot\gamma_{\text{eff}}\max(0,-\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{\text{CIG}}-\tau)\end{split}

(4)

This formulation ensures orthogonal gradient scaling:

•

Reward Amplification:
When
A_{i}\geq 0
,
\Delta_{t}
monotonically increases only for actions where the student underperformed the teacher (
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{\text{CIG}}>\tau
).

•

Penalty Amplification:
When
A_{i}<0
,
\Delta_{t}
monotonically increases only for actions where the student was erroneously over-confident (
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{t}^{\text{CIG}}<-\tau
).

The refined, token-level advantage tensor is thus expressed as a multiplicative modulation of the original baseline:

\widehat{A}_{i,t}=A_{i}\cdot\left(1+\Delta_{t}\right)

(5)

Temporal Annealing Schedule

In late training stages, self-distillation encounters two bottlenecks. First, reflection quality degrades: hints become redundant for internalized reasoning, and residual errors become too complex for accurate critiques. Second, the teacher reaches its intrinsic capability ceiling, losing the ability to provide meaningful CIG signals to an already highly-optimized student. To prevent overfitting to these depleted signals, a linear temporal decay smoothly shifts the optimization entirely to the pure environmental reward:

\{\lambda_{\text{eff}},\gamma_{\text{eff}}\}=\{\lambda,\gamma\}\cdot\max\left(0,1-\frac{t_{\text{global}}}{T_{\text{decay}}}\right)

(6)

where
t_{\text{global}}
is current optimization step.

3.4
Optimization Objective

The student policy parameters
\theta
are optimized by maximizing a modified PPO clipped surrogate objective. We substitute the standard trajectory advantage with our dense estimator
\widehat{A}_{i,t}
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{AMR-SD}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},y\sim\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{T_{i}}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{clip}}(\theta,t)\Bigg]

(7)

where the clipped surrogate term is defined as:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\text{clip}}(\theta,t)=\min\Big(&\rho_{t}(\theta)\widehat{A}_{i,t},\\
&\text{clip}(\rho_{t}(\theta),1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)\widehat{A}_{i,t}\Big)\end{split}

(8)

and the importance sampling ratio is
\rho_{t}(\theta)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid s_{t})}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_{t}\mid s_{t})}
. The gradient
\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{AMR-SD}}(\theta)
now incorporates both global trajectory optimality and precise token-level informational corrections.

Scientific Reasoning and Tool Use.

We evaluate AMR-SD on the SciKnowEval benchmark across four scientific disciplines (Chemistry, Physics, Biology, and Materials Science) as well as a Tool Use task, using two backbone models: Qwen3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Table
1
). Across both backbones, all training methods substantially improve over the untrained baselines, confirming that reinforcement learning provides a consistent benefit for scientific reasoning. At final convergence (step 75), AMR-SD achieves the strongest or near-strongest performance in the majority of categories. Most significantly, AMR-SD attains the highest Biology scores on both backbones by a substantial margin: 63.7 on Qwen3-8B and 66.4 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, surpassing GRPO by approximately 3.2 and 18.2 percentage points respectively, and exceeding RLSD by roughly 7.2 and 11.8 percentage points. On Physics and Materials Science, AMR-SD similarly reaches competitive or best-in-class scores at step 75—79.7 on Physics and 80.3 on Materials for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the highest figures among all methods in both categories. These results suggest that the token-level credit assignment provided by the CIG mechanism enables more reliable generalisation across diverse scientific domains compared to trajectory-level or coarser reward signals.

Convergence Dynamics.

A notable pattern across both backbones is the distinct convergence profile of AMR-SD relative to competing methods (Figure
2
). At the early checkpoint, AMR-SD frequently lags behind baseline methods that aggressively exploit early reward signals. For instance, on the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct Tool Use task, AMR-SD scores 61.0 at step 15, trailing both GRPO (61.6) and RLSD (63.3). Similarly, on Qwen3-8B Chemistry, AMR-SD (65.0) lags noticeably behind SDPO (69.2) and RLSD (66.3). However, by step 75, these early gaps are closed or reversed, with AMR-SD achieving the best or near-best scores in most categories on both backbones. By contrast, RLSD exhibits early gains that subsequently plateau or regress; its Biology score on Qwen3-8B improves only to 56.5 at step 75—well below AMR-SD’s 63.7. SDPO demonstrates the most pronounced instability, suffering severe performance degradation on both backbones and being excluded entirely from the step-75 Tool Use evaluation due to severe policy collapse. These dynamics indicate that AMR-SD’s fine-grained credit assignment trades off faster initial convergence for more stable and ultimately superior long-run optimization.

Mathematical Reasoning.

To assess generalisation beyond scientific knowledge tasks, we further evaluate AMR-SD on four mathematical competition benchmarks—AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, and HMMT—using Qwen3-8B in thinking mode (Table
2
). AMR-SD achieves the best performance on every individual benchmark and the highest average score of 62.7, compared to 60.3 for GRPO (+2.4 points) and 57.2 for RLSD (+5.5 points). The gains are particularly pronounced on the most challenging benchmarks: 69.6 on AIME24 (vs. 66.7 for GRPO), 55.2 on AIME25 (vs. 53.1), and 34.6 on HMMT (vs. 31.7). Notably, RLSD shows constrained optimization efficiency on harder problems, scoring 27.6 on HMMT and 47.3 on AIME25. This indicates that directly using raw ground-truth solutions as privileged information introduces a distributional shift that makes the resulting reward signal insufficiently discriminative for complex multi-step reasoning.

Furthermore, we provide deeper analyses of optimization stability and generation dynamics in Appendices
C
and
F
. As shown, while standard continuous self-distillation methods like RLSD exhibit late-stage performance fluctuations and compressed reflection behavior due to direct answer exposure, AMR-SD maintains a stable, monotonically improving reward signal. Crucially, AMR-SD improves reasoning efficiency by reducing unnecessary verbosity while successfully preserving critical cognitive explorations. This confirms that introducing a reflection bottleneck successfully mitigates the vulnerabilities inherent in direct answer conditioning, effectively translating training stability to superior out-of-domain accuracy.

4.3
Analysis: Quality of Meta-Reflections

The efficacy of AMR-SD hinges on the quality of self-generated reflections, as they directly calibrate the CIG-based advantage modulation. A precise reflection concentrates the learning signal on semantically informative tokens, whereas a vague or factually incorrect one risks propagating miscalibrated supervision.

Qualitative analysis of sampled reflections from SciKnowEval and DAPO-Math-17k rollouts reveals two consistent patterns. First,
hints for successful trajectories
effectively distill latent core principles rather than merely repeating the answer path. Second,
critiques for failed trajectories
demonstrate precise fault localization, diagnosing root causes and reframing them in a positive, prospective manner. This "positive-framing" is crucial; as the critique is prepended to the teacher’s forward pass, it provides constructive guidance that enables the model to resolve the underlying reasoning divergence. Detailed representative examples of these Socratic targets are provided in Appendix
B
.

Effect of Meta-Reflection.

In this ablation variant, we replace the model’s self-generated meta-reflection with the ground-truth solution directly as the teacher’s conditioning context. Performance drops from 74.3 to 70.5, a reduction of 3.8 points. This result reveals a counterintuitive finding: injecting privileged ground-truth information as the conditioning signal is strictly inferior to the model’s own Socratic self-reflection. The underlying cause lies in the information asymmetry problem: when the teacher is conditioned on the ground-truth answer, its token probability distribution presupposes information unavailable to the student, creating a fundamental distributional gap that inflates CIG estimates and induces miscalibrated advantage modulation. By contrast, self-generated hints and critiques share the same information boundary as the student policy, yielding better-calibrated CIG signals and more precise gradient interventions.

Effect of the ReLU Threshold
\tau

Removing the margin
\tau

The threshold enforces sparsity: without it, low-magnitude CIG signals that correspond to distributional noise rather than genuine knowledge deficits are allowed to modulate the advantage, diluting gradient quality. Further ablating the ReLU gate itself reduces accuracy to 71.3. In this setting, the mechanism loses its asymmetric directional filtering. Without the gate, “counter-directional” signals—such as positive CIG in a failed rollout or negative CIG in a successful one—can freely offset the intended advantage modulation, partially undermining the primary reward signal. These two ablations validate that gate and margin are necessary for robust, noise-resistant, and directionally-aware credit assignment.

Effect of Temporal Annealing.

Disabling the decay schedule reduces performance to 70.6. In late training stages, as the student policy becomes highly optimized, its residual errors become increasingly complex. This not only degrades self-reflection quality but also exhausts the teacher model’s inherent capacity to provide reliable token-level guidance. Without annealing, persistent distillation forces the policy to learn from these noisy, depleted signals. Temporal annealing mitigates this by smoothly shifting the optimization focus entirely to the pure environmental reward.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced AMR-SD to resolve the token-level credit-assignment bottleneck in RLVR. To overcome the privileged information leakage and signal dampening inherent in standard self-distillation, AMR-SD employs an autonomous Meta-Reflection phase to generate Socratic hints and critiques. We formalized CIG with an asymmetric, ReLU-gated threshold to translate these reflections into sparse, precise token-level interventions, strictly preserving the base environmental reward. Extensive experiments across scientific (SciKnowEval), mathematical (AIME, AMC, HMMT), and tool-use (ToolAlpaca) benchmarks demonstrate that AMR-SD significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines. By effectively filtering distributional noise and utilizing temporal annealing, our framework guarantees long-horizon stability and avoids late-stage training collapse, providing a highly robust, scalable solution for aligning large language models on complex reasoning and agentic tasks.

Limitations

Despite the strong empirical results, AMR-SD has two notable limitations.

Dependence on Reflection Quality.

The effectiveness of the CIG mechanism is fundamentally contingent on the quality of the
self-generated meta-reflections. When the base model lacks sufficient introspective capability,
the produced hints and critiques tend to be vague or factually miscalibrated, propagating
corrupted supervision signals through the CIG modulation and ultimately degrading performance.
This manifests most clearly in mathematical reasoning tasks: on weaker or non-thinking-mode
models such as Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B with
enable_thinking=False
, the
reflection quality is insufficient to yield reliable CIG signals, and the method fails to
outperform the GRPO baseline. AMR-SD is therefore most effective when deployed on models
with strong chain-of-thought or self-evaluation capabilities.

Inference Overhead from Meta-Reflection Generation.

The meta-reflection phase introduces an additional autoregressive generation step per trajectory
during training rollouts. Although this overhead is bounded by the reflection budget (capped at
4096 tokens for SciKnowEval and 16384 tokens for math), it nonetheless increases the wall-clock
time per training iteration relative to standard GRPO or RLSD. However, this computational
overhead is highly manageable in practice. Empirically, our method generally maintains a training
speed of at least 80% (e.g., in the mathematical reasoning task, during the first 50 steps before method degeneration, GRPO and RLSD took 18 hours, whereas AMR-SD took 22 hours) compared to standard GRPO. For severely resource-constrained
settings, this latency may still be a practical concern, and future work could explore
lightweight reflection distillation or early-exit strategies to further reduce this cost.

\lambda>\gamma
) to balance penalties and bonuses.

n=50{,}000

When the teacher model conditions on the meta-reflection—a hint or critique unavailable to the student—its output distribution is recalibrated: probability mass shifts toward alternative continuations, and by the softmax normalization constraint, the probability assigned to the token actually produced by the student decreases. Appendix F
Analysis of Reflection Capabilities and Reasoning Efficiency

Motivation.

While our Asymmetric Meta-Reflective Self-Distillation (AMR-SD) framework significantly improves accuracy and reasoning efficiency, a critical concern in On-Policy Distillation is whether the dense supervision alters the model’s intrinsic ability to explore and reflect. Standard self-distillation often forces the student to mimic an oracle-conditioned teacher. When this teacher is directly exposed to ground-truth answers, it can induce over-alignment and restrict natural trial-and-error, resulting in more short-sighted generation.
