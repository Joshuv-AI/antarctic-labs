Superposition of shock waves of the generalized BBM equation
Alexey Samokhin

Abstract
. The generalized BBM studied in this paper contains an additional dissipative term. Thus instead of solitons for the classic BBM there exists a lot of travelling shock wave solutions. The rules of their interactions or superposition is of high importance. The paper gives a detailed description of the two-parameter family of travelling wave solutions and proves their stability using a conservation law. Based on these results, effective rules of superposition are obtained. Moreover these rules are applicable not exclusively to the travelling wave solutions of BBM, but also to a wider class of shock waves, in particular discontinuous. Characteristic examples are illustrated by numerically worked out graphs.



Keywords:
generalized BBM equation, travelling waves, stability, shock waves, superposition.

MSC:
35Q53, 35B36.

Introduction

The Benjamin-Bona-Mahony equation (BBM) is of the form

u_{t}+u_{x}+uu_{x}-\lambda u_{xxt}=0.

(1)

It is
also known as the regularized long-wave equation.
It first appeared (with
\lambda=1
) in
[
1
]
, as a modification of the Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV).
long wavelength in liquids, acoustic-gravity waves in compressible
fluids, hydromagnetics waves in cold plasma and acoustic waves in anharmonic crystals, etc.

The generalized BBM (gBBM) studied in this paper has one additional
dissipation term:

u_{t}+u_{x}+uu_{x}-\lambda u_{xxt}-\varepsilon u_{xx}=0.

(2)

Here
\lambda>0
,
\varepsilon>0
are constants connected to dispersion
and dissipation.

Instead of solitary waves characteristic for classical BBM (
1
), the combination of dissipation and dispersion produce continuous monotonic or oscillatory traveling waves. Throughout this paper they are called travelling shock waves (as well as ”real” shocks, i.e., with discontinuities). They are the subject of this paper.

There is a considerable number of publication in the field. The following citations are the most relevant.
In

1

[
3

a superposition of the KdV-B travelling waves is discussed.
The review
[
4

deals with modelling Rieman-type shocks for

u_{t}=f(u)_{x}
type equations using conservation laws,
with modified KdV (mKdV) and KdV-B as a main examples. In
[
5

travelling wave solutions and conservation laws for the KdV-BBM equations are explored.

2.
Stability of shock waves

n
There is a lot of solutions of (
2
) with the boundary condition
H,h
: just solve (
2
)
for the initialvalue/boundary problem

\{u(x,0)=v(x),u(-\infty,t)=H,u(+\infty,t)=h\},

where
v(x)
is not a solution of (
6
).

For the next argument we put the
w weak restrictions on
v(x)
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\exists\;v(x)|_{-\infty}=H\;\wedge\;\exists\;v(x)|_{+\infty}=h)\bigwedge(\forall n>0:\;\frac{d^{n}u(x)}{dx^{n}}|_{\pm\infty}=0).\\[8.53581pt]
\exists\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{x}^{2}(x)\,dx<\infty.\end{array}\right.

Denote by
S_{H,h}
the class of equation (
2
)
solutions obtained with such a
v(x)
as the initial condition. Note that
T_{H,h}\in S_{H,h}
.

Theorem 1

(The stability property of travelling shock
T_{H,h}:
)
.

Every solution from
S_{H,h}
tends to
T_{H,h}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

As it is well known, e.g.
[
2
]
, the equation BBM (
1
) has the
momentum
conservation law, namely
u^{2}
. That means that for all rapidly decreasing at infinity solutions
u(x,t)
of (
1
)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}\;dx=2\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{t}\;dx=-2\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}u(u_{x}+uu_{x}-\lambda u_{xxt})\;dx=0

holds.

Not so for the equation gBBM (
2
). There the
momentum
conservation law
u^{2}
decays:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}dx=-2\left[\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}u(u_{x}+uu_{x}-\lambda u_{xxt})\;dx+\varepsilon\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{xx}dx\right];\\[8.53581pt]
\mbox{so }\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}dx=2\varepsilon\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}uu_{xx}dx=-2\varepsilon\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}^{2}dx<0.\end{array}

Thus a positive
momentum
decays (or dissipates) monotonically to zero while
u_{x}\neq 0
. And, inevitably,
u\rightarrow 0
— to its boundary condition.

Let
T_{H,h}(x,t)
be a travelling shock wave with
H,h
as the boundary conditions, and a perturbation
v(x)
of
T_{H,h}(x,0)
with the same boundary conditions.
Solving
(
2
) for initial value/boundary problem
\{\delta|_{x=\pm\infty}=0;\delta(x,0)=T_{H,h}(x,0)-v(x)\}

results in the solution
\delta(x,t)
rapidly decreasing at infinity.

As it was stated above, the
momentum
of
\delta
decays and

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\delta(x,t)=0.

It means that the initial difference between
T_{H,h}(x,0)
and
v(x)
vanishes.
∎

Superposition of shocks

Corollary 1

(Superposition rule.)
.

T_{h_{1},h_{1}}+T_{h_{1},h_{1}}\rightarrow T_{H_{1}+H_{2},h_{1}+h_{2}}

(14)

Proof.

Consider two shock waves:
T_{H_{1},h_{1}}

and

The sum of these expressions is not, of course, a solution to the nonlinear equation gBBM (

5.
Discussion

The gBBM
differs from the classical BBM by an additional dissipation term.

As a result, instead of the BBM solitons appears a class of travelling shock wave solutions — solutions with fixed values at infinity and all derivatives rapidly decrease there. The way of interaction (superposition) of such solutions is of high
practical importance.

In this paper a detailed description of the two-parameter family of the gBBM shock wave solutions is obtained and their stability is
proved using the
momentum
conservation law. Based on these results, effective rules of superposition are obtained. Moreover these rules are applicable not exclusively to the travelling
wave solutions of gBBM, but also to a much wider class of shock waves.

This study fortify the usefulness of the selective decay approach
to study of diffusion/dissipanive equations.

The characteristic examples are illustrated by numerically worked out graphs; Maple 21 standard packages: DEtools, PDEtools and Plots were used in this study.
