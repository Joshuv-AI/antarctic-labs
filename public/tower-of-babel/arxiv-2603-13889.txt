A note on the invariants of the L L -functions
J.KACZOROWSKI and A.PERELLI

Abstract




Abstract.
We explain the exact meaning of a statement we made in a previous paper on invariants, namely that a complex-valued function of the data of the functional equation of an
L
-function is an invariant if and only if it is stable under the multiplication and factorial formulae. To this end, we show that every invariant has a so-called rational extension, having some desired invariance properties. The existence of such an extension, which enables to express formally the above heuristic concept, is not apparent and its construction is the main novelty of the paper.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010):
11M41

Keywords:
Selberg class; gamma factors; invariants.

1.
Introduction

Every element
F
of the extended Selberg class, denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
, satisfies a Riemann-type functional equation of the form

\gamma(s)F(s)=\omega\overline{\gamma}(1-s)\overline{F}(1-s),

where
\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
|\omega|=1
and

\gamma(s)=Q^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j})

(1.1)

with
Q>0
,
\lambda_{j}>0
and
\Re(\mu_{j})\geq 0
for
j=1,\ldots,r
. Here
\Gamma(s)
denotes the familiar Euler gamma function and
\overline{f}(s)=\overline{f(\overline{s})}
. The numbers and vectors

\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}),\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})

(1.2)

are called the data of the functional equation. It is well-known that the
\gamma
-factor
\gamma(s)
, as a function of a complex variable
s
, is uniquely determined by
F
up to a non-zero multiplicative constant; see Conrey-Ghosh
[
1
]
. Nevertheless, the functional equation of a given
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
may have different forms with different data sets. This is due to the functional equations satisfied by the Euler function, in particular, the factorial formula

s\Gamma(s)=\Gamma(s+1)

and the Legendre-Gauss multiplication formula

\Gamma(s)=m^{s-1/2}(2\pi)^{(1-m)/2}\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+k}{m}\right).

This leads to the notion of invariant, namely a function

I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})

depending on the data (
1.2
) whose value depends only on
F
, independently of the particular form of the
\gamma
-factor.

In
[
3
]
, we proved that any
\gamma
-factor of
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
can be transformed to a complex multiple of any other
\gamma
-factor of
F
by repeated applications of the above two formulae. As a consequence, we sometimes used the following heuristic assertion:

\begin{split}&\text{{\it$I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})$ is an invariant if and only if it is}}\\
&\hskip-14.22636pt\text{{\it stable under the multiplication and factorial formulae,}}\end{split}

(1.3)

where “stable” meant, generically, “suitably invariant”. The authors were then frequently asked about the precise meaning of this assertion. While the meaning of stability under the multiplication formula is obvious and was rigorously defined in
[
3
]
, the problem is with the application of the factorial formula. Indeed, applying such a formula to
\Gamma(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1})
in (
1.1
) changes
\gamma(s)
to

(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1}-1)\Gamma(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1}-1)Q^{s}\prod_{j=2}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j}),

which is no longer of the form (
1.1
). In general, repeated applications of the factorial formula transform a
\gamma
-factor into

R(s)\gamma(s),

(1.4)

where
R(s)
is a rational function and
\gamma(s)
is of the form (
1.1
). Formally, for such expressions,
I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
is not defined; hence the meaning of stability is not clear. Of course the problem disappears if the factorial formula is not used in transforming a
\gamma
-factor to a multiple of another
\gamma
-factor. According to Theorem 1 in
[
3
]
, this holds when the
\gamma
-class number
h_{F}
of
F
is
1
(meaning that in (
1.1
) we have
\lambda_{i}/\penalty\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
for
i,j=1,\ldots,r
) or if
F
is reduced (meaning that
0\leq\Re(\mu_{j})<1
for
j=1,\ldots,r
). Another instance where an application of the factorial formula does not create the above-mentioned problems occurs when
I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
depends only on
\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda}
and
\Im(\boldsymbol{\mu}):=(\Im(\mu_{1}),\ldots,\Im(\mu_{r}))
. Indeed, applications of the factorial formula may change the real parts of the
\mu_{j}
’s only, so in such cases the appearance of the rational factor in (
1.4
) can be ignored. For instance, the factorial formula can be avoided, or the presence of the rational factor can be ignored, in the case of the following well-known invariants: the degree

d_{F}=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j},

the conductor

q_{F}=(2\pi)^{d_{F}}Q^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{2\lambda_{j}}

and the root number

\omega_{F}=\omega\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{-2i\Im(\mu_{j})}.

In contrast, there are problems in the case of the important
H
-invariants, defined in
[
4
]
for every integer
n\geq 0
as

H_{F}(n)=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{B_{n}(\mu_{j})}{\lambda_{j}^{n-1}},

(1.5)

where
B_{n}(x)
denotes the
n
-th Bernoulli polynomial. Checking the stability of
H_{F}(n)
under the multiplication formula is not difficult, see e.g. Section 3.3 of
[
2
]
. We only remark that it is performed using the following well-known property of the Bernoulli polynomials

B_{n}(mx)=m^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}B_{n}(x+\frac{j}{m})\hskip 42.67912pt(n\geq 0,\ m\geq 1).

However, the treatment of the stability under the factorial formula in
[
2
]
is not entirely satisfactory, as it treats a special case where the formula is applied to a pair of factors
\Gamma(\lambda s+\mu)
and
\Gamma(\lambda^{\prime}s+\mu^{\prime})
satisfying the extra consistency condition

\frac{\mu-1}{\lambda}=\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\lambda^{\prime}}.

It is not evident that the algorithm transforming a
\gamma
-factor to another can be arranged so that applications of the factorial formula are always performed in the above way. Hence this needs further explanation, otherwise the argument cannot be regarded as general. We stress that these comments apply only to the method of proof used in
[
2
]
to check the stability of
H_{F}(n)
under multiplication and factorial formulae. Indeed, there are other proofs of the fact that the
H
-invariants are actually invariants, based on different principles; see
[
4
]
.

To clarify the situation with
H_{F}(n)
, and with the general case as well, we introduce the following definitions. Let
R(s)
denote a generic not identically vanishing rational function,
\gamma(s)
be a
\gamma
-factor as in (
1.1
) and
I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
be a function of the data of
\gamma(s)
. Let now
I^{*}(R,\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
be a complex-valued function depending on the parameters involved in
R(s)
and on the data of
\gamma(s)
. In other words, we may think of
I^{*}
as a function of the data of a product of type (
1.4
). We say that
I^{*}
is a
rational extension
of
I
if for every
constant
rational function
R
we have

I^{*}(R,\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})=I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}).

An application of the multiplication formula transforms
R(s)\gamma(s)
to
R(s)c\gamma^{\prime}(s)
, with certain
c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
\gamma^{\prime}(s)
of type (
1.1
), while the factorial formula transforms it to
R^{\prime}(s)\gamma^{\prime}(s)
, with a rational function
R^{\prime}(s)\not\equiv 0
and
\gamma^{\prime}(s)
again of type (
1.1
). Hence in both cases
R(s)\gamma(s)
is transformed to a product of a similar form. Accordingly, we say that
I^{*}
is
stable
under the multiplication and factorial formulae if, with obvious notation,

I^{*}(R,\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})=I^{*}(R,\omega\overline{c}/c,Q^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\mu^{\prime}})

(1.6)

and

I^{*}(R,\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})=I^{*}(R^{\prime},\omega,Q^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\mu^{\prime}}),

(1.7)

respectively. We can finally state the precise version of the heuristic statement (
1.3
).

Theorem.
A function
I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
of the data is an invariant if and only if it has a rational extension which is stable under the multiplication and factorial formulae.

The proof contains an explicit construction of the rational extension, and this is the main novelty of the paper.

2.
Rational extension of
H
-invariants

For a product of type (
1.4
), say

R(s)\gamma(s)=\kappa\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{l}(s-\alpha_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(s-\beta_{j})}Q^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j}),

(2.1)

for
n\geq 1
we write synthetically

H^{*}(n)=H^{*}(n;R\gamma):=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{B_{n}(\mu_{j})}{\lambda_{j}^{n-1}}+(-1)^{n}2n\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}^{n-1}-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}^{n-1}\right)

(2.2)

with the usual convention that empty products equal
1
and empty sums equal
0
. It is obvious that
H^{*}(n)
is a rational extension of the
H
-invariants
H_{F}(n)
defined in (
1.5
).

Let us check now that
H^{*}(n)
is stable under the multiplication and factorial formulae. Since application of the former does not change
R(s)
, in this case the stability of
H^{*}(n)
is checked exactly as for
H_{F}(n)
, see Section 3.3 of
[
2
]
. The factorial formula can be applied to a single
\Gamma
-factor in (
1.1
) in two ways, which in
[
3
]
we called “expanding” and “contracting”. Without losing generality we focus on
\Gamma(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1})
. The expanding process changes it to
(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1}-1)\Gamma(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1}-1)
, whereas the contracting process to
(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1})^{-1}\Gamma(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1}+1)
. Accordingly,
R(s)\gamma(s)
in (
2.1
) is transformed to
R^{\prime}(s)\gamma^{\prime}(s)
, where

R^{\prime}(s)=\begin{cases}(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1}-1)R(s)&\text{in the expanding case}\\
(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1})^{-1}R(s)&\text{in the contracting case}\end{cases}

and

\gamma^{\prime}(s)=\begin{cases}\Gamma(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1}-1)\prod_{j=2}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j})&\text{in the expanding case}\\
\Gamma(\lambda_{1}s+\mu_{1}+1)\prod_{j=2}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j})&\text{in the contracting case}.\end{cases}

Hence in the expanding case we have

\begin{split}H^{*}(n,R^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime})&=2\frac{B_{n}(\mu_{1}-1)}{\lambda_{1}^{n-1}}+2\sum_{j=2}^{r}\frac{B_{n}(\mu_{j})}{\lambda_{j}^{n-1}}\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt+(-1)^{n}2n\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}^{n-1}-\frac{(1-\mu_{1})^{n-1}}{\lambda_{1}^{n-1}}-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\alpha_{j}^{n-1}\right)\\
&=H^{*}(n,R\gamma)+\frac{2}{\lambda_{1}^{n-1}}(B_{n}(\mu_{1}-1)-B_{n}(\mu_{1})+n(\mu_{1}-1)^{n-1})\\
&=H^{*}(n,R\gamma),\end{split}

where in the last step we applied the well known functional equation

B_{n}(x+1)=B_{n}(x)+nx^{n-1}.

Since the contracting case can be treated exactly in the same way, the stability of
H^{*}(n)
under the factorial formula follows.

3.
Proof of the Theorem

Suppose that
\gamma(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s)
are two
\gamma
-factors of
F
and
I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
is a function of the data with rational extension
I^{*}(R,\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
stable under the multiplication and factorial formulae. Then
\gamma(s)
can be transformed by means of repeated applications of such formulae to
c\gamma^{\prime}(s)
, and the
\omega
-datum of
\gamma^{\prime}(s)
is therefore
\omega^{\prime}=\omega\overline{c}/c
. Moreover we have

I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})=I^{*}(1,\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})

and, after repeated application of the two formulae, thanks to (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) also

I^{*}(1,\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})=I^{*}(c,\omega^{\prime},Q^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\mu^{\prime}}).

But
I^{*}(c,\omega^{\prime},Q^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\mu^{\prime}})=I(\omega^{\prime},Q^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\mu^{\prime}})
, thus
I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})=I(\omega^{\prime},Q^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\lambda^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\mu^{\prime}})
and hence
I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
is an invariant.

Viceversa, suppose that
I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
is an invariant. Thus its values are independent of the particular choice of
\gamma(s)
and
\omega
in the functional equation of
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
. On the other hand, we know that the invariants
\omega_{F},q_{F}
and
H_{F}(n)
,
n\geq 0
, determine the functional equation of
F
; see Theorem 1 of
[
4
]
. Thus

I(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})=\tilde{I(}\omega_{F},q_{F},(H_{F}(n))_{n=0}^{\infty})

for a certain function
\tilde{I}
. Given
(\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})
and a rational function
R(s)
we write

\gamma_{Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}(s)=Q^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j})

and

I^{*}(R,\omega,Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})=\tilde{I}\left(\omega\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{-2i\Im(\mu_{j})},(2\pi)^{d_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}Q^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{2\lambda_{j}},(H^{*}(n,R\gamma_{Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}})_{n=0}^{\infty}\right),

where
d_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}
and
H^{*}(n,R\gamma_{Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}})
is defined by (
2.2
) for
n\geq 1
, while for
n=0
we put
H^{*}(0,R\gamma_{Q,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}):=d_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
. This is obviously a rational extension of
I
, and is stable under the multiplication and factorial formulae thanks to the known invariance properties of
\omega_{F}
,
q_{F}
and
H^{*}(n)
. The proof is now complete, and an algorithm for the construction of a rational extension is also provided. ∎
