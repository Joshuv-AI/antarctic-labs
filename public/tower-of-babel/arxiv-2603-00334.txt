Lower Bounds for the Pfaffian Number of Graphs
Enrique Junchaya, Cláudio Leonardo Lucchesi, Alberto Alexandre Assis Miranda

Abstract
The number of perfect matchings of a k k -pfaffian graph can be counted by computing a linear combination of the pfaffians of k k matrices. The pfaffian number of a graph G G is the smallest integer k k such that G G is k k -pfaffian. We present the first known lower bounds for the pfaffian number of graphs. As an intermediate step, we prove an upper bound for the rank of two matrices related to their Khatri-Rao product, a result of independent relevance. One of the consequences of these results is the existence of graphs whose pfaffian numbers are arbitrarily large.

Introduction

Counting perfect matchings of a graph is a fundamental problem with
practical applications. Its main applications are in
chemistry
[
6
,
7
]
, statistical physics
[
2
,
10
]
and quantum mechanics
[
11
]
.
Examples of some of such applications can be found in the book by Lovász and
Plummer
[
14
, Chapter 8]
. On the other hand, this problem is

#
p
-complete
[
20
]
, which suggests that there are no
efficient algorithms to solve it in general. In that sense, it is
interesting to study graph classes for which efficient algorithms for
counting perfect matchings are known. One of the most studied such
classes is the class of pfaffian graphs.

Unless otherwise stated, assume that the graphs in this work are simple.
Let
G
be a graph and let
D
be an orientation of
G
.
Let
(1,2,\dots,2k)
be an enumeration of the vertices in
G
.
Let
M
be a perfect matching of
G
.
The
permutation
of
M
in
D
, denoted by
\pi_{D}(M)
, is

\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3&4&\dots&2k-1&2k\\
u_{1}&v_{1}&u_{2}&v_{2}&\dots&u_{k}&v_{k}\end{pmatrix},

where, for each
i
,
1\leq i\leq k
,
u_{i}v_{i}
is an arc of
M

oriented from
u_{i}
to
v_{i}
in
D
. The
sign
of
M
in
D
,
denoted by
\textnormal{sign}_{D}(M)
, is the sign of the permutation
\pi_{D}(M)
.
Notice that
\textnormal{sign}_{D}(M)
is independent of the order in which the
edges of
M
are listed in
\pi_{D}(M)
. The orientation
D
is a

pfaffian orientation
if and only if all the perfect matchings
of
G
have the same sign in
D
. A
pfaffian graph
is a graph
that admits a pfaffian orientation.

Denote the
skew-symmetric adjacency matrix of
D
by
\mathbf{A_{D}}:=(a_{ij})
,
defined as follows:

a_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1,&\text{if $ij$ is an arc of }D,\\
-1,&\text{if $ji$ is an arc of }D,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{array}\right.

The
pfaffian
of a
skew-symmetric matrix
\mathbf{A}
is a polynomial in the entries of
the matrix and is denoted by
\textnormal{Pf}(\mathbf{A})
. If the
orientation
D
is pfaffian, then
|\textnormal{Pf}(\mathbf{A_{D}})|
is equal to
the number of perfect matchings of
G
. It turns out that, for every
skew-symmetric matrix, the square of its pfaffian is equal to its
determinant. Hence, if
D
is pfaffian then it is possible to
efficiently compute the number of perfect matchings of
G
.

The reader can find a detailed account of the main results of the
theory of pfaffian graphs in the book by Lucchesi and Murty
[
15
, Part
III]
.

Of course, not every graph is pfaffian. The graph
K_{3,3}
is the
smallest non-pfaffian graph. Hence, we might consider a
generalization of pfaffian graphs. Let
k
be a positive integer. A

k
-orientation
of
G
is a vector
\mathbf{D}=(D_{1},D_{2},\dots,D_{k})
of
k
orientations of
G
. The
sign
of a perfect
matching
M
in
\mathbf{D}
is the vector

\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(M):=(\textnormal{sign}_{D_{1}}(M),\textnormal{sign}_{D_{2}}(M),\dots,\textnormal{sign}_{D_{k}}(M)).

Denote by
\mathcal{M}(G)
the set of perfect matchings of
G
. The

signature matrix
of
\mathbf{D}
is the
matrix
\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}
of
|\mathcal{M}(G)|
rows such that
every one of them is the sign in
\mathbf{D}
of a different perfect
matching of
G
. The
k
-orientation
\mathbf{D}
is
pfaffian

if the system

\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{1}

(1)

is solvable.
A vector that satisfies (
1
) is a
solution
of
\mathbf{D}
.
A graph that admits a pfaffian
k
-orientation is
k
-pfaffian
.
Notice that relabeling the vertices of
G
either changes the sign of all of its perfect matchings in
D
or does not change the sign of any of its perfect matchings in
D
.
Therefore, the property of a graph being
k
-pfaffian is independent of the enumeration of its vertices.
It is known, as we will see later, that every graph admits a
k
-pfaffian orientation for some integer
k
.
The
pfaffian number
of
G
, denoted by
\textnormal{pf}(G)
, is the smallest integer
k
such that
G
is
k
-pfaffian.
In particular, the pfaffian number of a pfaffian graph is 1.

Given a
k
-pfaffian orientation
\mathbf{D}
of
G
with solution
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, the number of perfect matchings of
G
can be computed by

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\textnormal{Pf}(\mathbf{A_{D_{i}}}).

Kasteleyn
[
8
]
, in 1961, introduced pfaffian orientations for solving problems in statistical physics.
Later, in 1963, he proved that every planar graph is pfaffian
[
9
]
.
In his paper of 1963, he also claimed, without proof, that is possible to count the number of perfect matchings of a graph embeddable in a surface of genus
g
using
4^{g}
pfaffians.
This claim was formally proved in the end of the 20th century by Gallucio and Loebl
[
5
]
and, independently, by Tesler
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Every graph embeddable in an orientable surface of genus
g
is
4^{g}
-pfaffian.
∎

Tesler also extended this result for non-orientable surfaces.
In 2009, Norine
[
18
]
proved that not every positive integer is the pfaffian number of a graph:

Theorem 2

(Norine,
[
18
]
)
.

Every
3
-pfaffian graph is pfaffian and every
5
-pfaffian graph is
4
-pfaffian.
∎

In particular, the pfaffian number of
K_{3,3}
, embeddable in the
torus, is four. Due to this and other results, Norine conjectured that
the pfaffian numbers are always a power of four. However, in 2011,
Miranda and Lucchesi
[
17
]
showed a counterexample to that
conjecture: a graph with pfaffian number six.
Norine
[
18
]
also characterized graphs with pfaffian number four. More
recently, in 2021, Costa Moço, Miranda and Nunes da Silva
[
4
]

characterized graphs with pfaffian number six. These two
characterizations are similar but it is not known if they can be
generalized.

Preliminaries and Notation

Let
G
be a graph. The graph
G
is
matchable
if it admits a
perfect matching. An edge of
G
is
matchable
if it is
contained in some perfect matching of
G
. A graph
G
is

matching covered
if it is connected, has at least one edge and
all of its edges are matchable. The study of
k
-pfaffian graphs can
be naturally reduced to matching covered graphs. A subgraph
H
of

G
is

if
H
and
G-V(H)
are matchable.

Let
D
and
D^{\prime}
be orientations of the same graph
G
. Denote by
D\mathbin{\triangle}D^{\prime}
the set of edges of
G
that have different orientations
in
D
and in
D^{\prime}
. The next result follows easily from the
definition of sign of a perfect matching.

Proposition 3
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
D
and
D^{\prime}
be orientations of

G
. Let

be a perfect matching of
G
. Then,

\textnormal{sign}_{D}(M)\cdot\textnormal{sign}_{D^{\prime}}(M)=(-1)^{|M\cap(D\mathbin{\triangle}D^{\prime})|}.

Let
T
be a trail of
G
. An arc of
D
is a
forward arc

of
T
if it is traversed from tail to head. The set of forward arcs
of
T
is denoted by
\textnormal{fw}_{D}(T)
. Let
Q
be a conformal cycle of
G
.
The
sign
of

in
D
, denoted by
\textnormal{sign}_{D}(Q)
, is
1
if

|\textnormal{fw}_{D}(Q)|
is odd and
-1
otherwise. As

is conformal then it
is even, hence the sign of

in
D
does not depend on the direction
of its traversal. The following basic result appears in the book by
Lovász and Plummer
[
14
, Chapter 8]
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
D
be an orientation of
G
. Let

M
and
N
be perfect matchings of
G
. Then,

\textnormal{sign}_{D}(M)\cdot\textnormal{sign}_{D}(N)=\prod_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\textnormal{sign}_{D}(Q),

where
\mathcal{C}
is the set of all
(M,N)
-alternating
cycles. ∎

Similarity of Orientations

Let

be a set of vertices of
G
. The

with

X

We denote by

if

is similar to

for every

. The next

.

Proposition 5
.

Let

be an orientation of a graph

, let

be a perfect

G
and let

be a cut of

.
Let

\textnormal{sign}_{D}(M)\cdot\textnormal{sign}_{D^{\prime}}(M)=(-1)^{|X|}.

Let

and

-orientations.
If

is pfaffian with solution

, then

Let

and

be two similar

Cuts and Contractions

Let
X
be a set of vertices of
G
. Denote by
G/X
the graph
obtained from
G
by the contraction of the vertices in the set
X
to
a single vertex and removing the resulting loops. Let
C:=\partial(X)
. The
C
-contractions
of
G
are the graphs
G/X

and
G/\overline{X}
.

Let
C
be a cut of a matching covered graph

. The cut

is

separating
if both

-contractions
of
G
are matching
covered. The cut
C
is

if
|M\cap C|=1
for every
perfect matching
M
of
G
. The following characterization of
separating cuts appears in the book by Lucchesi and
Murty
[
15
, Chapter 4]
.

Theorem 7
.

A cut
C
of a matching covered graph
G
is separating if and
only if each edge of
G
lies in a perfect matching that
contains precisely one edge in
C
. ∎

It is then easy to see that every tight cut is separating. A cut is

trivial
if there is a single vertex in one of its shores.
Notice that every trivial cut is also tight. A matching covered graph
free of non-trivial tight cuts is a
brick
if it is
non-bipartite and is a

if it is bipartite. A
tight
cut decomposition
of a graph is a collection of bricks and braces
obtained by recursively applying a
C
-contraction of a non-trivial
tight cut

whenever possible. In 1987, Lovász
[
13
]
proved
that every tight cut decomposition of a graph is, in a certain way,

Theorem 8

(Lovász,
[
13
]
)
.

Every application of the tight cut decomposition to a matching
covered graph

produces the same set of bricks and braces,
up to multiple edges. ∎

Lemma 9

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a matrix with

The

of two vectors

obtained by position-wise product. Namely,
\mathbf{u}\odot\mathbf{v}=(u_{i}\cdot v_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be
a set of vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Hadamard product of all the vectors in

\mathcal{S}
is denoted by
\prod_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{v}
.

We denote the
i
-th row of a matrix
\mathbf{A}
by
\mathbf{A}_{i}
.
The

of two matrices

and
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}\times n}
, denoted by
\mathbf{A}\ast\mathbf{B}
, is a matrix obtained
by the Hadamard product of pairs of rows from
\mathbf{A}
and

\mathbf{B}
. Formally, the product
\mathbf{A}\ast\mathbf{B}
is
the matrix
\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{1}m_{2})\times n}
such that

\mathbf{C}_{(i-1)\cdot m_{2}+j}=\mathbf{A}_{i}\odot\mathbf{B}_{j}

for every
i
,
1\leq i\leq m_{1}
, and every

,
1\leq j\leq m_{2}
.

It turns out that the products defined above are useful for describing

-orientations.

-orientations.
Let

be a

. The

\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(Q):=(\textnormal{sign}_{D_{1}}(Q),\textnormal{sign}_{D_{2}}(Q),\dots,\textnormal{sign}_{D_{k}}(Q)).

Proposition 10
.

Let

\mathbf{D}
be a
k

of

. Let

\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(M)\odot\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(N)=\prod_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(Q),

Outline of the Paper

In Section
2
, we review a recently developed
method for counting perfect matchings: the symbolic pfaffian method.
The known results of lower bounds for this method are similar to those
obtained in this paper for the pfaffian number. We discuss the
similarities of the symbolic pfaffian method and the
k
-pfaffian
method. Also, we show some implications of the lower bounds for the
symbolic pfaffian method to the pfaffian numbers. In
Section
3
, we present a lower bound for
the pfaffian number of a graph related to the pfaffian numbers of its
conformal subgraphs. As an intermediate step, we prove an upper bound
for the rank of two matrices related to their Khatri-Rao product, a
result of independent relevance. Then, we conclude that the pfaffian
number is unbounded by showing an infinite family of graphs with
increasing pfaffian number. In Section
4
, we
show that the pfaffian number of a graph is at least the pfaffian
number of its separating cut contractions. Also,
we discuss the relation of this result with previous results on
pfaffian graphs. Finally, in Section
5
, we
conclude with some final remarks about these new results.

Related Work: The Symbolic Pfaffian Method

In 2000, Tesler
[
19
]
proved the assertion of Kasteleyn that implies that graphs embeddable in orientable sufaces of genus
g
are
4^{g}
-pfaffian.
The prove by Tesler, as he noticed, also implied that the number of perfect matchings of graphs embeddable in surfaces of genus
g
can be computed via evaluation of the pfaffian of a matrix with symbolic entries.
The symbolic pfaffian method for counting perfect matchings was then formally described in 2017, by Babenko and
Vyalyi
[
1
]
, for bipartite graphs. Then, in 2021,
Vyalyi
[
21
]
generalized this method for arbitrary graphs.
Some of the notation below differs from the notation of Babenko and Vyalyi for the sake of
comparison with the
k
-pfaffian method.

Let
G
be a graph and let
d
be an integer.
Consider the vector space
\textnormal{GF}(2)^{d}
formed by the binary vectors of dimension
d
.
Denote the sum in this vector space by
\oplus
.
A
d
-symbolic function

\tau\colon E(G)\rightarrow\textnormal{GF}(2)^{d}
of
G
is a function defined in the edges of
G
and that extends to perfect matchings by
\tau(M)=\bigoplus_{e\in M}\tau(e)
for every
M\in\mathcal{M}(G)
.
A
d
-symbolic orientation
of
G
is a pair

(D,\tau)
, where
D
is an orientation of
G
and
\tau
is a

d
-symbolic function of
G
. A
d
-symbolic orientation is

pfaffian
if, for every pair of perfect matchings
M
and
N
of
G
,

\textnormal{sign}_{D}(M)\neq\textnormal{sign}_{D}(N)\implies\tau(M)\neq\tau(N).

Every graph
G
admits a pfaffian
|E(G)|
-symbolic orientation.
Indeed, a
|E(G)|
-symbolic
function which maps every edge to a different vector in the standard basis implies that
\tau(M)\neq\tau(N)
for every two different perfect matchings
M
and
N
of
G
.
The

symbolic pfaffian number
of
G
, denoted by
\textnormal{spf}(G)
, is the
smallest integer
d
such that
G
admits a pfaffian
d
-symbolic
orientation. In particular, the symbolic pfaffian number of a
pfaffian graph is 0.

Given a pfaffian
d
-symbolic orientation
(D,\tau)
of
G
, we can
count the number of perfect matchings of
G
with a parameterized
algorithm. Let
\mathcal{R}_{d}=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{d}]/(t_{i}^{2}-1)
be a polynomial ring in
d
variables with
exponents modulo 2. Associate to every edge of
G
a monomial in

\mathcal{R}_{d}
with coefficient 1 and exponents corresponding to the
entries of
\tau(e)
. Let
\boldsymbol{\tau}{(D)}
be the skew-symmetric
matrix obtained by multiplying the nonzero entries of
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{D}}

by the monomials associated with the corresponding edges. The number of
perfect matchings of
G
is equal to the sum of the absolute values of
the coefficients of
\textnormal{Pf}(\boldsymbol{\tau}{(D)})
.
Notice, however, that determinant-based algorithms for computing the pfaffian are not suitable for matrices over
R_{d}
.
Nevertheless, there are efficient combinatorial algorithms for
computing the pfaffian directly and without using divisions
[
16
]
.
As the intermediate operations of such algorithms
are executed over polynomials with at most
2^{d}
terms, the complexity
of this method is
\mathcal{O}(2^{d}\mathrm{poly}(n))
.

Babenko and Vyalyi
[
1
]
also extended this method for a
polynomial ring without restrictions in the exponents of their
variables. However, the most interesting results are for the case
described here, with exponents modulo 2. Also, Vyalyi
[
21
]

considered a generalization of this method that uses more than one
symbolic orientation. The results obtained for that generalization
hold, in particular, for the version described here.

In 2021, Vyalyi
[
21
]
obtained lower bounds for the symbolic
pfaffian number of arbitrary graphs. Let
\textnormal{Pf}^{*}(G)
be the greatest
absolute value of
\textnormal{Pf}(\mathbf{A_{D}})
among all orientations
D

of
G
.

Theorem 11

(Vyalyi,
[
21
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then,

\textnormal{spf}(G)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\log_{2}\dfrac{|\mathcal{M}(G)|}{\textnormal{Pf}^{*}(G)}.

Corollary 12

(Vyalyi,
[
21
]
)
.

\textnormal{spf}(K_{2n})=\Omega(n\lg n)
∎

We noticed that we can build a pfaffian
d
-symbolic orientation of a
graph given a pfaffian
(d+1)
-orientation. This implies that the
pfaffian number of a graph is lower bounded by its symbolic pfaffian
number as we prove below.

Theorem 13
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then,
\textnormal{pf}(G)\geq\textnormal{spf}(G)+1
.

Proof.

Let
k:=\textnormal{pf}(G)
.
If
k=1
, then
G
is pfaffian,
\textnormal{spf}(G)=0
and the theorem holds trivially.
Suppose then that
G
is non-pfaffian and hence
k>1
.
Let
\mathbf{D}
be a
k
-pfaffian
orientation of
G
. Let
\tau
be a
(k-1)
-symbolic
function of
G
defined by

\tau(e)_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1,&\text{if }e\in D_{i}\mathbin{\triangle}D_{k}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{array}\right.

for every edge
e\in E(G)
and
i
,
1\leq i\leq k-1
. Then,
by definition, for every perfect matching
M
of
G
, we have
that
\tau(M)_{i}\equiv|M\cap(D_{i}\mathbin{\triangle}D_{k})|\pmod{2}
.
We will show that the
(k-1)
-symbolic orientation
(D_{k},\tau)
is pfaffian.

Since
G
is not pfaffian, there are perfect matchings of
G
, say
M
and
N
, with different signs in
D_{k}
.
Assume, by contradiction, that
\tau(M)=\tau(N)
. Then, by the construction of
\tau
, we have that

|M\cap(D_{i}\mathbin{\triangle}D_{k})|\equiv|N\cap(D_{i}\mathbin{\triangle}D_{k})|\pmod{2},

for every
i
,
1\leq i\leq k-1
. Therefore, by
Proposition
3
, we obtain that

\textnormal{sign}_{D_{i}}(M)\cdot\textnormal{sign}_{D_{k}}(M)=\textnormal{sign}_{D_{i}}(N)\cdot\textnormal{sign}_{D_{k}}(N).

Given that the signs of
M
and
N
in
D_{k}
are different, we
know that
\textnormal{sign}_{D_{i}}(M)\neq\textnormal{sign}_{D_{i}}(N)
. We conclude
that the signs of
M
and
N
are different in all the
orientations of
\mathbf{D}
. However, this implies that the
equation
\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{1}
has no
solution because
\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(M)+\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(N)=0
.
This contradicts that
\mathbf{D}
is pfaffian.
∎

Applying Corollary
12
, we obtain the
following lower bound for the pfaffian number.

Corollary 14
.

\textnormal{pf}(K_{2n})=\Omega(n\lg n)
∎

Vyalyi
[
21
]
also proved lower bounds for a family of sparse
graphs. From the results of Vyalyi together with
Theorem
13
, we conclude that the pfaffian number is
unbounded. In the next section, we show a different proof of that
fact that does not depend on the lower bounds for the symbolic
pfaffian method.

3
The Pfaffian Number and Conformal Subgraphs

In this section, we prove a lower bound for the pfaffian number of a
graph that depends on the pfaffian number of its conformal subgraphs.
This lower bound is a direct consequence of a new result on the
Khatri-Rao product. In that sense, we start this section describing
the relation between the Khatri-Rao product and the restriction of a

k
-orientation to conformal subgraphs.

Proposition 15
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a
k
-orientation of a matching covered graph

G
. Let
H^{\prime}
be a non-spanning conformal subgraph of
G
and
let
H^{\prime\prime}:=G-V(H^{\prime})
. Let
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{D}^{\prime\prime}

be the restrictions of
\mathbf{D}
to
H^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime\prime}
,
respectively. Then, the matrix
\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}^{\prime}}\ast\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}^{\prime\prime}}
is formed by a subset of the rows of

\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}
.

Proof.

Let
M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}
be perfect matchings of
H^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime\prime}
,
respectively.
Adjust the enumeration of the vertices of
G
such that the
first
2|M^{\prime}|
vertices are the vertices in
H^{\prime}
.
For every
i
,
1\leq i\leq k
, adjust the order of the edges listed in
the permutation
\pi_{D_{i}}(M^{\prime}\cup M^{\prime\prime})
such that all the
edges in
M^{\prime}
are listed first. Hence, it is clear that the
number of inversions of
\pi_{D_{i}}(M^{\prime}\cup M^{\prime\prime})
is equal to
the sum of the number of inversions of
\pi_{D^{\prime}_{i}}(M^{\prime})

and of
\pi_{D^{\prime\prime}_{i}}(M^{\prime\prime})
. Thus, we obtain that

\textnormal{sign}_{D^{\prime}_{i}}(M^{\prime})\cdot\textnormal{sign}_{D^{\prime\prime}_{i}}(M^{\prime\prime})=\textnormal{sign}_{D_{i}}(M^{\prime}\cup M^{\prime\prime}).

Therefore,
\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})\odot\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}^{\prime\prime}}(M^{\prime\prime})=\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(M^{\prime}\cup M^{\prime\prime})
holds by the definition of the Hadamard product.
Given that
M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime\prime}
are arbitrary perfect matchings
and by the definition of the Khatri-Rao product, we conclude
that every row of the matrix
\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D^{\prime}}}\ast\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D^{\prime\prime}}}
is a row of

\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}
.
∎

Now we will prove an upper bound for the sum of the ranks of two
particular matrices using the Khatri-Rao product. We will use the
following straightforward result.

Lemma 16
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}},\dots,\mathbf{v_{n}}\}
be a set of linearly independent vectors in

\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
\mathbf{u}
be a vector in

\mathbb{R}^{k}
with no zero entries. The set
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\{\mathbf{u}\odot\mathbf{v_{i}}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
is
linearly independent. ∎

Theorem 17
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times n}
and
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}\times n}
be matrices with the same
number of columns. If there is a vector
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
without
zero entries that satisfies
(\mathbf{A}\ast\mathbf{B})\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\mathbf{1}
, then

\textnormal{rank}(\mathbf{A})+\textnormal{rank}(\mathbf{B})-1\leq n.

Proof.

Suppose that such a vector
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
exists. Then, by the
definition of the Khatri-Rao product, we have that

(\mathbf{A}_{i}\odot\mathbf{B}_{j})\boldsymbol{\alpha}=1
, for
every
i
,
1\leq i\leq m_{1}
, and every
j
,
1\leq j\leq m_{2}
.
Notice that, after transposing
\mathbf{B}_{j}
to match dimensions, we
can apply the associativity between the Hadamard
product and the matrix product, yielding
\mathbf{A}_{i}(\mathbf{B}^{T}_{j}\odot\boldsymbol{\alpha})=1
. Therefore,
the vector set
\{\mathbf{B}^{T}_{j}\odot\boldsymbol{\alpha}\}_{1\leq j\leq m_{2}}
is a
set of solutions to the system
\mathbf{Ax}=\mathbf{1}
.
Since the vector
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
does not contain zeroes and due
to Lemma
16
, we obtain that

\mathbf{Ax}=\mathbf{1}
has at least
\textnormal{rank}(\mathbf{B})

linearly independent solutions. By
Lemma
9
, we conclude that

\textnormal{rank}(\mathbf{A})\leq n-\textnormal{rank}(\mathbf{B})+1
.
∎

The theorem above has direct consequences for the pfaffian number of
graphs.

Corollary 18
.

Let
G
be a matching covered graph. Let
H^{\prime}
be a non-spanning
conformal subgraph of
G
and let
H^{\prime\prime}:=G-V(H^{\prime})
. Let

\mathbf{D}
be a pfaffian
\textnormal{pf}(G)
-orientation of
G
. Let

\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{D}^{\prime\prime}
be the restrictions of

\mathbf{D}
to
H^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. Then,

\textnormal{rank}(\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}^{\prime}})+\textnormal{rank}(\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D^{\prime\prime}}})-1\leq\textnormal{pf}(G).

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
be a solution of
\mathbf{D}
. By
Proposition
15
, we
know that
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is a solution to the
system
(\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D^{\prime}}}\ast\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D^{\prime\prime}}})\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{1}
. Notice that
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
has no
zeroes; otherwise, we could discard the orientations of

\mathbf{D}
corresponding to the zero entries of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}

and obtain a pfaffian
k
-orientation of
G
with
k<\textnormal{pf}(G)
, a contradiction. Thus, this corollary follows from
Theorem
17
.
∎

The obtained lower bound for the pfaffian number can also be written
using the pfaffian numbers of the subgraphs by noticing that the
rank of a signature matrix of a pfaffian
k
-orientation is lower bounded by the pfaffian
number of the underlying graph.

Proposition 19
.

Let
\mathbf{D}
be a pfaffian
k
-orientation of a graph
G
.
Then,

\textnormal{pf}(G)\leq\textnormal{rank}(\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{A}:=\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}
. Since
\mathbf{D}

is pfaffian, the vector
\mathbf{1}
is a linear combination
of the columns of
\mathbf{A}
. Let
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}
be a

\textnormal{rank}(\mathbf{A})
-orientation formed by
\textnormal{rank}(\mathbf{A})

orientations of
\mathbf{D}
corresponding to linearly
independent columns of
\mathbf{A}
. Clearly,
\mathbf{D}^{\prime}

is pfaffian. Therefore,
\textnormal{rank}(\mathbf{A})
cannot be less
than
\textnormal{pf}(G)
.
∎

Corollary 20
.

Let
G
be a matching covered graph. Let
H^{\prime}
be a non-spanning
conformal subgraph of
G
and let
H^{\prime\prime}:=G-V(H^{\prime})
. Then,

\textnormal{pf}(H^{\prime})+\textnormal{pf}(H^{\prime\prime})-1\leq\textnormal{pf}(G).

To apply this lower bound, we need a non-spanning subgraph. However,
the restriction of a pfaffian
k
-orientation of a graph
G
to a spanning
subgraph of
G
clearly remains pfaffian.
This observation allows us to extend the lower bound to arbitrary conformal subgraphs, yielding the following corollary.

Corollary 21
.

Let
G
be a matching covered graph. If
H
is a conformal subgraph
of
G
, then the inequality
\textnormal{pf}(G)\geq\textnormal{pf}(H)
holds. ∎

The lower bound in Corollary
20
is
sharp. Indeed, consider the graph
K_{4,4}
. Let
H
be a conformal
subgraph of
K_{4,4}
isomorphic to
K_{3,3}
. The graph
K_{4,4}-V(H)
is formed by a single edge and, hence, is pfaffian. Then, given
that
\textnormal{pf}(K_{3,3})=4
and by
Corollary
20
, we obtain that

\textnormal{pf}(K_{4,4})\geq 4
. Moreover, since
K_{4,4}
is embeddable in the
torus and by Theorem
1
, we conclude that

\textnormal{pf}(K_{4,4})=4
This example is a particular case of the following
corollary.

Corollary 22
.

Let
G
be a matching covered graph. Let
H
be a non-spanning
conformal subgraph of
G
. If
\textnormal{pf}(G)=\textnormal{pf}(H)
, then the
graph
G-V(H)
is pfaffian. ∎

We do not know if the lower bound in
Corollary
20
remains sharp if we only
consider non-pfaffian conformal subgraphs.

3.1
Unboundedness of the Pfaffian Number

The lower bound from Corollary
20

implies that the pfaffian number of graphs is unbounded. Indeed, we
only need to consider a graph with many disjoint non-pfaffian
conformal subgraphs. For example, we can consider the family of
bipartite complete graphs
K_{3n,3n}
. A graph in this family
contains
n
disjoint copies of
K_{3,3}
. Hence, applying
Corollary
20
recursively, we obtain
that
\textnormal{pf}(K_{3n,3n})\geq n\cdot\textnormal{pf}(K_{3,3})-(n-1)=3n+1
. This
graph has
9n^{2}
edges and, therefore, the pfaffian number of the
bipartite complete graphs grows at least as fast as the square root of
their number of edges.

We also constructed an infinite family of sparse matchings covered
graphs in which the pfaffian number grows at least linearly with the
number of edges. Then, we noticed that Vyalyi
[
21
]
also
constructed a similar family to prove lower bounds for the symbolic
pfaffian method in sparse graphs. We will focus on the Vyalyi family
of graphs because these graphs are also cubic. We will show that
the pfaffian numbers of the graphs in this family grow linearly using
Corollary
20
.

n
copies of

the interior vertices of one
of the ears of length three in

p_{i}
and

the interior vertices of the other ear of length three in

.
The graph

4
The Pfaffian Number and Separating Cuts

In 1991, Little and Rendl
[
12
]
reduced the problem of
deciding if a graph is pfaffian to deciding if the graphs in its tight
cut decomposition are pfaffian. Their result can be stated as
follows.

Theorem 23

(Little and Rendl,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
G
be a matching covered graph and let
C
be a tight cut
of
G
. The graph
G
is pfaffian if and only if both

C
-contractions of
G
are pfaffian. ∎

The sufficient condition of the theorem above does not hold if the cut is separating but not tight.
As an example, consider a cut
C
of the Petersen graph whose shores are the
5
-cycles.
The cut
C
is separating and both
C
-contractions are pfaffian because they are planar.
However, the Petersen graph is not pfaffian.
In contrast, the necessary condition of Theorem
23
still holds for non-tight separating cuts.

Theorem 24

(Little and Rendl,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
G
be a matching covered graph and let
C
be a
separating cut of
G
. If one of the
C
-contractions of
G

is not pfaffian, then
G
is not pfaffian. ∎

In 2012, Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty
[
3
]
proved a stronger version of the theorem above.

Theorem 25

(Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty,
[
3
]
)
.

Let
C
be a separating cut of a matching covered graph
G
and let
D_{0}
be a pfaffian orientation of
G
.
Then
G
has an orientation
D
similar to
D_{0}
such that both
C
-contractions of
D
are pfaffian.
∎

We prove a generalization of the previous theorem to
k
-orientations

.

Let

Proposition 30

Let

If

is a vertex of degree two in
G
with neighbours
u
and
w
.
Let
H
be the graph obtained from
G
by bicontraction of
v
.
We will show that
\textnormal{pf}(G)=\textnormal{pf}(H)
.

By Corollary
28
, we know that
\textnormal{pf}(G)\geq\textnormal{pf}(H)
.
Therefore, it suffices to prove that
\textnormal{pf}(H)\geq\textnormal{pf}(G)
.
Let
\mathbf{D_{H}}
be a
\textnormal{pf}(H)
-orientation of
H
.
Extend
\mathbf{D_{H}}
to a
\textnormal{pf}(H)
-orientation
\mathbf{D}
of
G
such that
(u,v,w)
is an oriented path.
Let
Q
be a conformal cycle of
G
that contains the vertex
v
and let
Q_{H}
be the bicontraction of
v
from
Q
.
By definition of sign of cycles, we have that
\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(Q)=\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D_{H}}}(Q_{H})
.
This implies that the sets of all signs of conformal cycles of
\mathbf{D_{H}}
and of
\mathbf{D}
are equal.
Let
M
be a perfect matching of
G
that contains
uv
and let
M_{H}:=M-uv
.
Let
\mathbf{v}:=\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(M)\odot\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D_{H}}}(M_{H})
be a vector.
It is easy to see that, for every perfect matching
N
of
G
, there is a perfect matching
N_{H}
of
H
, such that

\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D}}(N)=\textnormal{{sign}}_{\mathbf{D_{H}}}(N_{H})\odot\mathbf{v}.

This implies that
\mathbf{D}
is pfaffian.
The proposition follows by induction.
∎

5
Conclusions

We proved lower bounds for the pfaffian number of graphs.
Lower bounds for a closely related method, namely the symbolic pfaffian method, were already known.
We established a relation between the pfaffian number and the symbolic pfaffian number: for a graph
G
, the relation
\textnormal{pf}(G)\geq\textnormal{spf}(G)+1
holds.
However, the condition for the existence of a pfaffian
k
-orientation is more complicated than the condition for the existence of a pfaffian
k
-symbolic orientation.
In that sense, we believe that the growth rate of the pfaffian number might be much faster than the growth rate of the symbolic pfaffian number.
In fact, the growth rate of the pfaffian number might be exponential for complete graphs.
In that regard, we conjecture that the lower bound of Corollary
20
is not sharp if both conformal subgraphs are not pfaffian.

Conjecture 31
.

Let
G
be a matchable graph.
Let
H^{\prime}
be a non-spanning conformal subgraph of
G
and let
H^{\prime\prime}:=G-V(H^{\prime})
.
If
H^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime\prime}
are not pfaffian, then

\textnormal{pf}(H^{\prime})+\textnormal{pf}(H^{\prime\prime})-1<\textnormal{pf}(G).

We also proved that the pfaffian number of a graph does not
increase after a separating cut contraction.
In particular, a bicontraction preserves the pfaffian number.
It is an interesting open problem to characterize separating cuts
whose contractions preserve the pfaffian number.
