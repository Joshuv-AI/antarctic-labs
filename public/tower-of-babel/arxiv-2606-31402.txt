On the trilinear and Ginzburg-Rallis models
Xinrui Wang

Abstract
. Let k k be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic zero. We give sufficient conditions under which the Ginzburg-Rallis models of the induced representations of GL 6 ​ ( k ) {\mathrm{GL}}_{6}(k) from a parabolic subgroup of type [ 2 3 ] [2^{3}] are isomorphic to the trilinear models of the inducing data. We also give nonvanishing criterion for these trilinear models and Ginzburg-Rallis models.

1.
Introduction and the main results

This paper is devoted to the study of local Ginzburg-Rallis models for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{6}
, which is the Whittaker induction of the trilinear models for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}^{3}
in the framework of the
relative Langlands program (
[
BZSV24
]
).

We first recall the definition of the Ginzburg-Rallis model. Let
k
be a local field of characteristic zero with normalized absolute value
\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert_{k}
, and let
G:={\mathrm{GL}}_{6}(k)
. For a positive integer
n
, let
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(k)
be the space of
n\times n
matrices over
k
. The Ginzburg-Rallis subgroup of
G
is

H:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}g&gX&gZ\\
&g&gY\\
&&g\end{bmatrix}\,\middle|\,g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k),X,Y,Z\in{\mathrm{M}}_{2}(k)\right\}=H_{0}\ltimes N,

where
H_{0}:=\{\operatorname{diag}(g,g,g)\mid g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)\}
is the image of the diagonal embedding of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)
into
G
, and

N:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1_{2}&X&Z\\
&1_{2}&Y\\
&&1_{2}\end{bmatrix}\,\middle|\,X,Y,Z\in{\mathrm{M}}_{2}(k)\right\}.

Here and henceforth,
1_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix.

Fix a character
\eta:k^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and a nontrivial additive character
\psi:k\to\mathbb{C}
. Then we have the following character

\varphi_{H}:H\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times},\quad h=\begin{bmatrix}g&gX&gZ\\
&g&gY\\
&&g\end{bmatrix}\mapsto\eta_{H}(h)\psi_{H}(h):=\eta(\det g)\psi({\mathrm{tr}}\,X+{\mathrm{tr}}\,Y).

Let
\pi
be an admissible smooth representation of
G
, which is of Casselman-Wallach type if
k
is archimedean. The Ginzburg-Rallis model of
\pi
is the space

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}\left(\pi,\varphi_{H}\right),

which is at most one-dimensional if
\pi
is irreducible by
[
JSZ11
,
JLX25
]
. In the case that
\pi
is tempered, it is studied in
[
W19
]
using local trace formula.

From now on we assume that
k
is non-archimedean. In this paper we will study the Ginzburg-Rallis models of certain induced representations of
G
. Let
P
be the following block upper triangular
parabolic subgroup of
G
of type
[2^{3}]
:

P=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}g_{1}&X&Z\\
&g_{2}&Y\\
&&g_{3}\end{bmatrix}\,\middle|\,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k),X,Y,Z\in{\mathrm{M}}_{2}(k)\right\}=MN,

where
M=\{\operatorname{diag}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\mid g_{i}\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k),i=1,2,3\}
.

For a topological group
A
, let
\delta_{A}
denote its modular character. One computes that for
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)
and
X,Y,Z\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(k)
,

\delta_{P}\left(\begin{bmatrix}g_{1}&X&Z\\
&g_{2}&Y\\
&&g_{3}\end{bmatrix}\right)=\left\lvert\det\left(g_{1}g_{3}^{-1}\right)\right\rvert_{k}^{4}.

Let
\tau_{i},1\leq i\leq 3
be irreducible admissible smooth representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)
, and
\tau:=\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}\otimes\tau_{3}

which is a representation of
M
. Extend
\tau
to a representation of
P
trivially across
N
and also denote it by
\tau
. Let
V_{\tau}
be the representation space of
\tau
. Consider the induced representation

\pi_{\tau}:={\mathrm{Ind}}^{G}_{P}(\tau)\quad\text{(normalized smooth induction)},

consisting of all locally constant functions
f:G\rightarrow V_{\tau}
satisfying

f(pg)=\delta_{P}^{1/2}(p)\tau(p)f(g),\quad\text{for all }p\in P,g\in G.

We will study the Ginzburg-Rallis model
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}\left(\pi_{\tau},\varphi_{H}\right)
and compare it with the following trilinear model (see
[
P90
]
)

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H_{0}}(\tau,\eta_{H}).

Our results will be complementary to the works mentioned above.

Let us recall the classification of irreducible admissible smooth representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k)
(see
[
BH06
, 9.11]
). For a positive integer
n
, denote by
B_{n}
the upper triangular Borel subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(k)
. An irreducible admissible smooth representation
\sigma
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)
is one of the following:

•

\sigma
is supercuspidal.

•

\sigma={\mathrm{Ind}}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)}_{B_{2}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})
(normalized smooth induction), where
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are characters of
k^{\times}
with
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}\neq\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert_{k}^{\pm 1}
.

•

\sigma
is the unique irreducible quotient of the representation

{\mathrm{Ind}}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)}_{B_{2}}\left(\chi\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert_{k}^{-1/2}\otimes\chi\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert_{k}^{1/2}\right)=(\chi\circ\det)\otimes{\mathrm{Ind}}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)}_{B_{2}}\delta_{B_{2}}^{-1/2}

for some character
\chi
of
k^{\times}
, which is called a Steinberg representation and denoted by
\mathrm{St}(\chi)
.

•

\sigma=\chi\circ\det
is the one-dimensional subrepresentation of the representation
{\mathrm{Ind}}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)}_{B_{2}}\left(\chi\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert_{k}^{-1/2}\otimes\chi\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert_{k}^{1/2}\right)
for some character
\chi
of
k^{\times}
.

Here,
\delta_{B_{2}}=\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert_{k}\otimes\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert_{k}^{-1}
is the modular character of
B_{2}
. Infinitely dimensional irreducible smooth representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)
that are isomorphic to the representations of the form
{\mathrm{Ind}}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)}_{B_{2}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})
are called principal series representations and others are essential discrete series representations.

Let
\omega_{\tau_{i}}
denote the central character of the representation
\tau_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
. For a smooth representation
V
of finite length of a totally disconnected group, we denote by
V_{\mathrm{ss}}
the semi-simplification of
V
, i.e., if
V
admits a filtration
0=V_{0}\subseteq V_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V_{n}=V
with each
V_{i+1}/V_{i}
irreducible, then
V_{\mathrm{ss}}:=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}V_{i+1}/V_{i}
. Let

T_{2}=\{\operatorname{diag}(x_{1},x_{2})\mid x_{1},x_{2}\in k^{\times}\}\text{ and }N_{2}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{bmatrix}\,\middle|\,x\in k\right\}.

For each smooth representation
V
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)
, define its Jacquet module by

J(V):=V/{\mathrm{Span}}_{\mathbb{C}}\{n\cdot v-v\mid n\in N_{2},v\in V\}.

It naturally carries a
T_{2}
-representation structure.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
and
\tau_{3}
be irreducible admissible smooth representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)
. A representation
\tau=\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}\otimes\tau_{3}
of
M
is called

if for each
i=1,2,3
and
j,l\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}
with

,

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{T_{2}}\left(J(\tau_{i})_{\mathrm{ss}},(\eta\circ\det)\otimes\left(\omega_{\tau_{j}}^{-1}\otimes\omega_{\tau_{l}}^{-1}\right)\right)=0.

The first main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\tau=\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}\otimes\tau_{3}
be a good representation of
M
. Then it holds that

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}\left(\pi_{\tau},\varphi_{H}\right)\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H_{0}}\left(\tau,\eta_{H}\right).

Remark 1.3
.

For tempered
\tau
, the statement was proved in
[
W19
]
without requiring
\tau
to be good.

We will prove the following criterion for good representations.

Proposition 1.4
.

A representation
\tau=\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}\otimes\tau_{3}
of
M
is good if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(G1)

For each
i=1,2,3
, if
\tau_{i}=\chi_{i}\circ\det
for some character
\chi_{i}
of
k^{\times}
, then

\chi_{i}\otimes\chi_{i}\neq(\eta\circ\det)\otimes\left(\omega_{\tau_{j}}^{-1}\otimes\omega_{\tau_{l}}^{-1}\right)

where
j,l\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}
with
j<l
;

(G2)

For each
i=1,2,3
, if
\tau_{i}=\mathrm{St}\left(\chi_{i}\right)
for some character
\chi_{i}
of
k^{\times}
, then

\chi_{i}\otimes\chi_{i}\neq(\eta\circ\det)\otimes\left(\omega_{\tau_{j}}^{-1}\otimes\omega_{\tau_{l}}^{-1}\right)\otimes\delta_{B_{2}}^{-1},

where
j,l\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}
with
j<l
;

(G3)

For each
i=1,2,3
, if
\tau_{i}=\mathrm{Ind}_{B_{2}}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k)}(\chi_{ij}\otimes\chi_{il})
for some characters
\chi_{ij}
and
\chi_{il}
of
k^{\times}
, where
j,l\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}
with

, then

\chi_{ij}\otimes\chi_{il}\neq(\eta\circ\det)\otimes\left(\omega_{\tau_{j}}^{-1}\otimes\omega_{\tau_{l}}^{-1}\right)\otimes\delta_{B_{2}}^{-1/2}

and

\chi_{ij}\otimes\chi_{il}\neq(\eta\circ\det)\otimes\left(\omega_{\tau_{l}}^{-1}\otimes\omega_{\tau_{j}}^{-1}\right)\otimes\delta_{B_{2}}^{1/2}.

4.1.
Preliminaries

•

Let

and Proposition
4.4
. Choose
\Sigma_{F/k}
to be a character of
F^{\times}
extending the character
\varsigma_{F/k}
of
k^{\times}
. Then by (
13
), the restriction of the character
\mu_{ij}\Sigma_{F/k}
to
k^{\times}
is trivial for each
i,j=0,1
:

(\mu_{ij}\Sigma_{F/k})|_{k^{\times}}=(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3})|_{k^{\times}}\varsigma_{F/k}\eta^{-2}=1.

Applying Proposition
4.4
gives that

\varepsilon\left(\mu_{ij}\Sigma_{F/k},\psi_{F}\right)=c_{\alpha}\mu_{ij}(\alpha)\Sigma_{F/k}(\alpha),\quad i,j=0,1,

where
\alpha\in F
has trace zero over
k
and
c_{\alpha}
is a constant independent of
\mu_{ij}
and
\Sigma_{F/k}
. By Proposition
4.3
and the hypothesis on conductors, we obtain

\varepsilon\left(\mu_{ij},\psi_{F}\right)=\Sigma_{F/k}(y_{ij})\varepsilon\left(\mu_{ij}\Sigma_{F/k},\psi_{F}\right),

where for each
i,j=0,1
,
y_{ij}\in F
satisfies

\left(\mu_{ij}\Sigma_{F/k}\right)(1+x)=\psi_{F}(y_{ij}x)

for all
x\in F
with
v(x)\geq\mathrm{cond}\left(\mu_{ij}\Sigma_{F/k}\right)/2
. Therefore,

\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{\varepsilon\left(\mu_{00},\psi_{F}\right)\varepsilon\left(\mu_{11},\psi_{F}\right)}{\varepsilon\left(\mu_{01},\psi_{F}\right)\varepsilon\left(\mu_{10},\psi_{F}\right)}=\frac{\mu_{00}\mu_{11}(\alpha)\Sigma_{F/k}(y_{00}y_{11})}{\mu_{10}\mu_{01}(\alpha)\Sigma_{F/k}(y_{10}y_{01})}=\Sigma_{F/k}\left(\frac{y_{00}\cdot y_{11}}{y_{10}\cdot y_{01}}\right).

∎
