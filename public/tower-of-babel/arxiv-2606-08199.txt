Nimish A. Shah, Pengyu Yang

Abstract
. We study Birkhoff genericity for a simple uniformly expanding diagonal flow on SL n + 1 ​ ( ℝ ) / SL n + 1 ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z}) , with initial points restricted to affine subspaces of the expanding horospherical orbit through the identity coset. We prove that almost every point on such an affine subspace is Birkhoff generic, except possibly in two situations: either the defining matrix of the affine subspace has Diophantine exponent at least n n , or the affine subspace is arbitrarily well approximable by affine subspaces of dimension r − 1 r-1 defined over a real number field of degree m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , with n + 1 = m ​ r n+1=mr . As applications, we obtain Dirichlet non-improvability and logarithmic density results for almost every point on these affine subspaces.

1.
Introduction

Let
X=G/\Gamma
be a finite-volume homogeneous space and let

\{a_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a one-parameter diagonal subgroup of
G
.
A point
x\in X
is said to be Birkhoff generic for the
\{a_{t}\}
-action with respect to the

G
-invariant probability measure
\mu_{X}
if

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(a_{t}x)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{X},\qquad\forall f\in C_{c}(X).

Suppose that the
a_{t}
-action is ergodic with respect to
\mu_{X}
. By the Birkhoff ergodic theorem,
\mu_{X}
-almost every point is Birkhoff generic. Let
P_{a}^{-}=\{g\in G:\lim_{t\to\infty}a_{t}ga_{-t}\text{ exists in }G\}
denote the stable subgroup for
\{a_{t}\}
. Then if
x
is generic then
gx
is also generic for any
g\in P_{a}^{-}
. Let
U_{a}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{g\in G:\lim_{t\to\infty}a_{-t}ga_{t}\to e\}
denote the expanding horospherical subgroup for
\{a_{t}\}
. Then
P_{a}^{-}U_{a}^{+}
is open in
G
. Therefore, for any
x\in X
, we have that
gx
is generic for almost every
g\in U_{a}^{+}
with respect to the Haar measure on
U_{a}^{+}
. We are interested in finding out when the same conclusion holds for other measures on
U_{a}^{+}
, for example smooth measures on proper submanifolds of
U_{a}^{+}
.

The above question was motivated by another class of similar results, where one shows that translates of certain smooth measures on a submanifold of a horosphere by a diagonal action get equidistributed in the limit
[
9
,
18
,
23
]
. These type of results are like mixing of piece of a low-dimensional submanifold under translates. While in this article we are interested in ergodic averages along the diagonal orbit from various points of the submanifold.

Shi
[
20
]
carefully studied this problem for subgroup action of semisimple groups
H
on
G/\Gamma
, and established Birkhoff genericity for points on orbits of horospheres of
H
for actions of
\mathbb{R}
-diagonalizable subgroups. For points on horospheres in the case of actions on the space of unimodular lattices, Kleinbock, Shi, and Weiss
[
11
]
have also obtained precise error terms for ergodic averages. See also
[
6
,
8
,
24
]
for progress on different cases of this problem.

More recently, a strong result was proved by Solan and Wieser
[
21
]
who established Birkhoff genericity for non-degenerate curves on horospheres of simple uniformly expanding diagonal group actions on the space of unimodular lattices.

In this paper we consider the Birkhoff genericity question for points on affine subspaces of the expanding horospherical subgroup. Our work complements the results in
[
20
,
11
,
5
,
21
]
and we refer the readers to the references therein for other applications, history and background in a more general context.

1.1.
Main results

Let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{R})
,
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{Z})
,
X=G/\Gamma
, the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
\mu_{X}
denote the
G
-invariant probability measure on
X
, and
x_{0}=e\Gamma/\Gamma\in X
. Let

t\mapsto a_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{n}{n+1}t}&\\
&e^{-\frac{1}{n+1}t}I_{n}\end{pmatrix}

denote the uniformly expanding simple diagonal one-parameter subgroup. And let

U_{a}^{+}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&x_{1}&\cdots&x_{n}\\
&1&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&1\\
\end{pmatrix}:(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\},

denote the expanding horospherical subgroup of
G
with respect to
a_{1}
. From now on we will identify
U^{+}_{a}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
denote the basis of
U_{a}^{+}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (proper) affine subspace of
U_{a}^{+}
of dimension
d<n
. To parametrize
\mathcal{A}
, by permuting the coordinates, without loss of generality we may assume that the projection of
\mathcal{A}
to the coordinate plane spanned by
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
is bijective. Then, there exists a unique matrix
A\in\Mat_{d+1,n-d}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{L}_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{(\mathbf{x},A\widetilde{\mathbf{x}})\colon\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}\text{, where $\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}=(1,\mathbf{x})$ for all $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$.}

Here
A
is called the matrix defining
\mathcal{A}
. Let

(1.2)

u_{A}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{d+1}&A\\
&I_{n-d}\\
\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }x_{A}=u_{A}x_{0}.

For
A\in\Mat_{d+1,n-d}(\mathbb{R})
, we define the Diophantine exponent
\omega(A)
of
A
to be the supremum of
\omega>0
such that the inequality
\left\lVert A\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{p}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert^{-\omega}
has infinitely many nonzero integral solutions
(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
. By Dirichlet’s approximation theorem,
\omega(A)\geq\frac{n-d}{d+1}
for every
A
, and the equality holds for almost every
A
.

We shall prove the following point-wise non-escape of mass result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\omega(A)<n
. Then for almost every
u\in\mathcal{A}
, every weak-
\ast
limit point of
\{\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}a_{t}u\delta_{x_{0}}\mathrm{d}t\}_{T\to\infty}
is a probability measure on
X
.

Remark 1.1
.

We note that the condition on the Diophantine exponent is optimal in the following sense. Suppose
\omega(A)>n
. Then by the argument in the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 6.5]
, see also
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, escape of mass occurs for every
u\in\mathcal{A}
. More precisely, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
T_{i}\to\infty
such that for every compact subset
K
of
X
and every
u\in\mathcal{A}
, the Lebesgue measure of the set
\{0\leq t\leq T_{i}\colon a_{t}ux_{0}\in K\}
is at most
(1-\varepsilon_{0})T_{i}
for all
i\gg 1
.

In the critical case of
\omega(A)=n
, we are not able to draw any conclusions.

In this article, a real number field is defined to be a subfield of
\mathbb{R}
which is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
; it is not necessarily totally real. Let
\operatorname{Gr}(k_{1},k_{2})
denote the Grassmannian variety parametrizing
k_{1}
-dimensional linear subspaces of a
k_{2}
-dimensional vector space.

Definition 1.1

(
\mathbb{F}
-Liouville subspaces)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a real number field and
k\leq l<n
are positive integers. We say a
k
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
is
(\mathbb{F},l)
-
Liouville
if for every
\kappa\geq 1
, there exists an
l
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
which is defined over
\mathbb{F}
, such that

(1.3)

d(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T})\leq H_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{T})^{-\kappa}.

Here
d(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}):=\max_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\min_{y\in\mathcal{T}}d(x,y)
, where
d(x,y)
is the angle between the lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
that
x
and
y
parametrize.
The height function
H_{\mathcal{L}}
is an exponential Weil height function
H_{\mathcal{L}}:\operatorname{Gr}(l+1,n+1)(\mathbb{F})\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
associated with the ample generator
[\mathcal{L}]
of the Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Gr}(l+1,n+1))\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Note that the height function is determined by
[\mathcal{L}]
up to multiplication by a constant, but changing the constants does not affect our definition of Liouville-ness.

We say an affine subspace of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
is
(\mathbb{F},l)
-Liouville if its projective closure in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{k;\mathbb{F},l}
denote the set of
(\mathbb{F},l)
-Liouville subspaces in
\operatorname{Gr}(k+1,n+1)
. Then
Definition

1.1
gives natural coverings of
\mathcal{L}_{k;\mathbb{F},l}
by thin neighborhoods of
\mathbb{F}
-Schubert cycles in
\operatorname{Gr}(k+1,n+1)
defined by the condition
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{T}
. These Schubert cycles are of dimension
(k+1)(l-k)
. From this one can show that the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{L}_{k;\mathbb{F},l}
is
(k+1)(l-k)
, which is strictly smaller than the dimension of
\operatorname{Gr}(k+1,n+1)
. Since this fact is not used in this article, we leave it to the reader to verify the details.

The following is the main result of this article.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\omega(A)<n
. Then one of the following possibilities holds:

(1)

For almost every
u\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(a_{t}ux_{0})\mathrm{d}t=\int_{X}f\mathrm{d}\mu_{X},\quad\forall f\in C_{c}(X).

(2)

There exist a real number field
\mathbb{F}
of degree
m\geq 2
and a positive integer
r\geq d+1
such that

•

rm=n+1
,

•

\mathcal{A}
is
(\mathbb{F},r-1)
-Liouville.

In particular, if
n+1
is prime, then possibility (2) does not occur.

Remark 1.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is contained in a
(r-1)
-dimensional affine subspace defined over
\mathbb{F}
such that
rm=n+1
; see
4.5
. Then by
[
16
, Proof of Lemma 6.6]
, there exists a proper closed subset
\mathcal{K}
of
X
such that
a_{t}\mathcal{A}x_{0}\subset\mathcal{K}
for all
t\geq 0
. So for every
u\in\mathcal{A}
, the sequence of probability measures
\frac{1}{T_{i}}\int_{0}^{T_{i}}a_{t}u\delta_{x_{0}}\mathrm{d}t
do not equidistribute in
X
for any sequence
T_{i}\to\infty
.

1.1.1.
Geometric formulation

Let
W
be a linear subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
. As done in the ‘geometric formulation’ given in the introduction of

In the above notation, we have that

]
.

.

G
whose pushforward on

1.2.
Dirichlet non-improvability on subspaces

Initial motivation for our study comes from the Diophantine approximation. Denote by
\|\cdot\|
the supremum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
n\geq 1
(unless specified otherwise, all the norms on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be taken to be the supremum norm). Let
0<\delta\leq 1
. Following Davenport and Schmidt
[
3
]
, let
\mathrm{DI}(\delta)
denote the set of vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for all large
T\geq 1
,

(1.4)

\exists(p,\mathbf{q})\in\mathbb{Z}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\text{ such that }\begin{cases}\left\lvert\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}+p\right\rvert\leq\delta T^{-n}\\
\|\mathbf{q}\|\leq\delta T.\end{cases}

Similarly, let
\mathrm{DI}^{\prime}(\delta)
denote the set of vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for all large
T\geq 1
,

(1.5)

\exists(\mathbf{p},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times(\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})\text{ such that }\begin{cases}\|q\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{p}\|\leq\delta T^{-1}\\
\left\lvert q\right\rvert\leq\delta T^{n}.\end{cases}

By Dirichlet’s approximation theorem,
\mathrm{DI}(1)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathrm{DI}^{\prime}(1)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
\mathrm{DI}=\cup_{0<\delta<1}\mathrm{DI}(\delta)
and
\mathrm{DI}^{\prime}=\cup_{0<\delta<1}\mathrm{DI}^{\prime}(\delta)
, denote the sets of Dirichlet’s improvable vectors. Davenport and Schmidt
[
3
]
proved that the sets
\mathrm{DI}
and
\mathrm{DI}^{\prime}
are Lebesgue null in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see also
[
12
]
. By the arguments as in
[

,
19
]
involving Dani-correspondence, it is straightforward to deduce the following result from
1.2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a proper

-dimensional affine subspace of

that does not satisfy the condition in possibility (

The result weakens the conditions for Dirichlet non-improvability in
[

for the case of

,

and

is even (for

in its statement).

Following

we formulate a density result on improvability of Dirichlet’s approximation on affine subspaces of

. For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathbf{x}}(\delta)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\{1,\ldots,N\}:\\
\text{\eqref{eq:lf} holds for $T$}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{T},

There exist continuous

1.3.
Strategy of the proof and organization of the paper

The general strategy of the proof in this article is based on extending the techniques developed in
[
20
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
15
]
. In
Section

2
we describe and recall properties of the Margulis height function on
G/\Gamma
as defined in
[
1
]
for the action of
H_{d}=\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R})
. We study the exponential growth rate of the height function along trajectories of a diagonal group
\{b_{t}\}
commuting with
H_{d}
. Using the relation between the growth rate and Diophantine exponent obtained in
[
15
]
, we define a modified height function with contraction property for the
\{a_{t}\}
-action. This allows us to prove
1.1
in view of the methods developed in
[
20
]
. In
Section

3
, we recall a result inspired by
[
2
]
from
[
20
]
to obtain unipotent invariance of the limiting measures for almost all ergodic averages. These limiting measures are invariant under the simple diagonal group
\{a_{t}\}
by definition. These observations allow one to use measure rigidity theorems of Ratner
[
14
]
as done by Einsiedler and Shi
[
5
]
. They show that if such a limit measure is not
G
-invariant, then it must be positive on certain algebraic subvariety of
G
projected to
G/\Gamma
; called singular manifolds. To analyze how the ergodic averages assigns mass to the singular manifold, we carefully construct a height function to measure ‘distance’ from the singular submanifold. We ensure that this function satisfies nice properties, in particular it satisfies the contraction property with respect to the
a_{t}
-action. The contraction property is related to exponential growth of a new height function along the
b_{t}
-trajectories. This construction of the height function given in
Section

3
is a novel part of this article. In
Section

4
we interpret the high exponential growth rate in terms of the
\mathbb{F}
-Liouville property.

2.1.
Notation

Let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{R})
and

H_{d}=\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R})&\\
&I_{n-d}\end{pmatrix}
, where
I_{k}
denotes the
k\times k
-identity matrix. Consider the one-parameter diagonal subgroups of
G
defined by the following: For all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle a_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{n}{n+1}t}&\\
&e^{-\frac{1}{n+1}t}I_{n}\end{pmatrix},

(2.2)

\displaystyle b_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{n-d}{(d+1)(n+1)}t}I_{d+1}&\\
&e^{-\frac{1}{n+1}t}I_{n-d}\end{pmatrix}\in Z_{G}(H_{d})\text{, and }

(2.3)

\displaystyle c_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{d}{d+1}t}&&\\
&e^{-\frac{1}{d+1}t}I_{d}&\\
&&I_{n-d}\end{pmatrix}\in H_{d}.

Then

Let

&I_{d}\\
&&I_{n-d}\end{pmatrix}\in U=U_{a}^{+}\cap H_{d},\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Given

A=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\end{pmatrix}\text{, where $A_{1}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{1,n-d}(\mathbb{R})$ and $A_{2}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{d,n-d}(\mathbb{R})$.}

Let

\mathcal{A}=\{(s,\tilde{s}A)\colon\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}

&I_{d}&-A_{2}\\

&I_{d}\\
&&I_{n-d}\end{pmatrix}=z_{A}u(s)u_{A}\in\mathcal{A}\subset U_{a}^{+}.

2.2.
Height function with respect to the point at infinity

Restricting the standard action of
G
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
to
H_{d}
, we have the following decomposition of
H_{d}
-modules

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=V_{0}^{\perp}\bigoplus V_{0},

where
V_{0}^{\perp}
is the
\mathbb{R}
-span of
\{e_{0},\dots,e_{d}\}
and
V_{0}
is the
\mathbb{R}
-span of
\{e_{d+1},\dots,e_{n}\}
. Here,
V_{0}^{\perp}
is the standard representation of
H_{d}\cong\operatorname{SL}_{d+1}(\mathbb{R})
and
H_{d}
acts trivially on
V_{0}
.
For
0\leq k\leq n+1
, taking the
k
-exterior products we get

(2.8)

\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=\bigoplus_{i=\max\{0,k-(n-d)\}}^{\min\{d+1,k\}}\bigwedge^{i}(V_{0}^{\perp})\bigotimes\bigwedge^{k-i}V_{0},

because if
\bigwedge^{i}(V_{0}^{\perp})\neq 0
, then
0\leq i\leq d+1
, and if
\bigwedge^{k-i}V_{0}\neq 0
, then
0\leq k-i\leq n-d

We recall the construction of a Margulis height function

These results are needed only in

Proof.

The arguments given here are based on

In view of the definitions (
