Monotone quantities on 3 3 -manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature
Jiannan Chen, Haiping Fu 1.Department of Mathematics, Nanchang University, Nanchang 330031, Jiangxi, China.

Abstract
In this paper, we derive monotone quantities for harmonic functions on asymptotically flat 3-manifolds with simple topology and nonnegative scalar curvature. These monotone quantities are constant on spatial Schwarzschild manifolds outside rotationally symmetric spheres. To derive monotone quantities, our method is different from the ODE analysis in Xia-Yin-Zhou [ 32 ] and Mazurowski-Yao [ 22 ] , we follow the strategy developed in Miao [ 23 ] . As applications, we recover and generalize some geometric inequalities and mass-capacity inequalities in Miao [ 23 ] and Oronzio [ 27 ] . Furthermore, we obtain the integral identities for the mass-capacity ratio which is parallel to the results in Miao [ 23 ] .

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}f(u)\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\zeta}=

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of critical values of
u
in
[t_{1},t_{2}]
. Let
W
denote an open set of
[t_{1},t_{2}]
such that
W
contains
\mathcal{C}
. Let
D
be the complement of
W
in
[t_{1},t_{2}]
.

On
u^{-1}(D)
,
f(u)\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}
is integrable. By coarea formula,

\int_{u^{-1}(D)}f(u)\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}=\int_{D}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}f(u)\frac{\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}}{|\nabla u|}.

By Stern’s formula
[
31
]
,

\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}}[|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+(R_{g}-2K_{\Sigma_{t}})|\nabla u|^{2}],

where
K_{\Sigma_{t}}
is the Gauss curvature of
\Sigma_{t}
. Hence,

\int_{u^{-1}(D)}f(u)\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}\geq\int_{D}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{f(u)}{|\nabla u|}\frac{[|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+(R_{g}-2K_{\Sigma_{t}})|\nabla u|^{2}]}{2\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}}.

(2.8)

With
W
fixed, letting
\epsilon\to 0
in (
2.8
) and using Gauss-Bonnet theorem, we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits\inf_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{u^{-1}(D)}f(u)\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{D}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}f(u)\frac{[|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+(R_{g}-2K_{\Sigma_{t}})|\nabla u|^{2}]}{2|\nabla u|^{2}}

(2.9)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}f(t)\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+R_{g}\Big)-2\pi\chi(\Sigma_{t})\Big].

To estimate the integral on
u^{-1}(W)
, we note that

\displaystyle\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}

\displaystyle=div\Big[\frac{\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}}{2\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}}\Big[|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}|\nabla|\nabla u||^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}}\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla u,\nabla u).

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{u^{-1}(W)}f(u)\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\geq-\max_{\Omega}|\mathrm{Ric}|\int_{u^{-1}(W)}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}}f(u)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\geq-\max_{\Omega}|\mathrm{Ric}|\int_{u^{-1}(W)}|\nabla u|f(u)

\displaystyle=-\max_{\Omega}|\mathrm{Ric}|\int_{W}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}f(u).

Combining (
2.9
) with (
2.10
), we obtain

\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}f(u)\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}\geq\int_{D}f(t)\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+R_{g}\Big)-2\pi\chi(\Sigma_{t})\Big]-\max_{\Omega}|\mathrm{Ric}|\int_{W}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}f(u).

Since
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|f(u)<\infty
, we can choose
W
, such that the measure of
W
is arbitrarly small. Then one has

\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}f(u)\Delta\sqrt{|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon}\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}f(t)\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+R_{g}\Big)-2\pi\chi(\Sigma_{t})\Big].

(2.11)

From (
2.4
), (
2.6
), (
2.7
) and (
2.11
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\Omega,g)
be a compact, Riemannian 3-manifold with boundary
\partial\Omega
. Suppose

has two connected components

and

are constants with

and the level sets

\mathcal{D}_{k}(t)=\frac{4\pi(1-t)}{k\omega(t)}+\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|H-\frac{4\omega(t)-k}{(1-t)\omega(t)^{3}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}

(3.1)

is nonincreasing in
t

denotes a regular value of

and

is the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{t}
with respect to the unit normal
\nu=|\nabla u|^{-1}\nabla u
.

Proof.

Notice that
R_{g}\geq 0
and
\Sigma_{t}
are connected. By Lemma
2.2
, at a regular value

, we have

\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|H\Big)^{\prime}\leq 4\pi-\frac{3}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H^{2},

(3.2)

where we have used the Gauss-Bonnet theorem. Hence, at a regular value
t
, we compute

\displaystyle-\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\Big[4\pi+\frac{3(2\omega(t)-k)^{2}}{(1-t)^{2}\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{6\omega(t)-3k}{(1-t)\omega(t)}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|H-\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|H\Big)^{\prime}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\Big[\frac{3(2\omega(t)-k)^{2}}{(1-t)^{2}\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{6\omega(t)-3k}{(1-t)\omega(t)}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|H+\frac{3}{4}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}H^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{3}{4}\Big(H-\frac{2(2\omega-k)}{(1-u)\omega}|\nabla u|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

In general, let
t_{1}<t_{2}
be two regular values of
u
. On
\Omega_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}=\{x\in\Omega\,|\,t_{1}\leq u\leq t_{2}\}
, taking

in Lemma
2.4
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega(t_{1})^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}|\nabla u|H-\frac{1}{\omega(t_{2})^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}|\nabla u|H

(3.3)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+R_{g}+\frac{4(k-1)}{\omega(u)}|\nabla u|H\Big)-2\pi\chi(\Sigma_{t})\Big].

Let
\mathbb{II}
denote the second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{t}
with respect to
\nu
. Let
X,Y
be the vectors tangent to
\Sigma_{t}
. Along
\Sigma_{t}
, we have

\nabla^{2}u(X,Y)=|\nabla u|\mathbb{II}(X,Y),\,\,\,\nabla^{2}u(X,\nu)=X(|\nabla u|),\,\,\,\nabla^{2}u(\nu,\nu)=-H|\nabla u|.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{II}|^{2}+2\frac{|\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+H^{2}

\displaystyle=|\overset{\circ}{\mathbb{II}}|^{2}+2\frac{|\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+\frac{3}{2}H^{2},

where
\overset{\circ}{\mathbb{II}}
denotes the traceless part of

By (

) and (

), one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega(t_{1})^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}|\nabla u|H-\frac{1}{\omega(t_{2})^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}|\nabla u|H

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\overset{\circ}{\mathbb{II}}|^{2}+\frac{|\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}H^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{g}+\frac{2(k-1)}{\omega(u)}|\nabla u|H\Big)-2\pi\chi(\Sigma_{t})\Big].

Now, we replace term

Thus, we can rewrite (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega(t_{1})^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}|\nabla u|H-\frac{1}{\omega(t_{2})^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}|\nabla u|H

(3.6)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\overset{\circ}{\mathbb{II}}|^{2}+\frac{|\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}\Big(H-\frac{2(2\omega-k)}{(1-u)\omega}|\nabla u|\Big)^{2}

Under the assumption that
\Sigma_{t}
is connected, we get

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{k}\Big(\frac{1-t_{1}}{\omega(t_{1})}-\frac{1-t_{2}}{\omega(t_{2})}\Big)+\frac{1}{\omega(t_{1})^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}|\nabla u|H-\frac{1}{\omega(t_{2})^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}|\nabla u|H

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\overset{\circ}{\mathbb{II}}|^{2}+\frac{|\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}\Big(H-\frac{2(2\omega-k)}{(1-u)\omega}|\nabla u|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\omega-k}{(1-u)\omega}|\nabla u|H-\frac{3(2\omega-k)^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}\omega^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}R_{g}\Big).

On the other hand, taking
f(u)=\frac{4\omega(u)-k}{(1-t)\omega(u)^{3}}
in Lemma
2.3
gives

\displaystyle\frac{4\omega(t_{2})-k}{(1-t_{2})\omega(t_{2})^{3}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{2}}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{4\omega(t_{1})-k}{(1-t_{1})\omega(t_{1})^{3}}\int_{\Sigma_{t_{1}}}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{3(2\omega-k)^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}\omega^{4}}|\nabla u|^{3}-\int_{\{|\nabla u|\neq 0\}}\frac{4\omega-k}{(1-u)\omega^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}H

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Big[\frac{3(2\omega-k)^{2}}{(1-u)^{2}\omega^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{4\omega-k}{(1-u)\omega}|\nabla u|H\Big].

It follows from (
3.7
) and (

) that

\mathcal{D}_{k}(t_{1})-\mathcal{D}_{k}(t_{2})\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\overset{\circ}{\mathbb{II}}|^{2}+\frac{|\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}\Big(H-\frac{2(2\omega-k)}{(1-u)\omega}|\nabla u|\Big)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}R_{g}\Big).

(3.9)

Since
R_{g}\geq 0
, we conclude that

. This completes the proof of Lemma

∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

where

, and equality holds if and only if

is isometric to
(M_{m,r_{0}},g_{m})
of mass
\mathfrak{m}=2r_{0}(k-1)

Proof.

By (

) and Hölder’s inequality,

Let

k(4-k)z^{2}-k\sqrt{16\pi W}z-4\pi\leq 0.

Solving this equation, we obtain

(4.2)

On the other hand, by (

(4.3)

Now, (

.
∎

. By the condition (

Proof of Theorem 1.6
.
Observe that

\displaystyle B(\nu,\nu)=-H|\nabla u|+\frac{2(2\omega-k)|\nabla u|^{2}}{(1-u)\omega},

\displaystyle B(\nu,\cdot)|_{\Sigma_{t}}=\langle\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|,\cdot\rangle,

\displaystyle B(\cdot,\cdot)|_{\Sigma_{t}}=|\nabla u|\Big(\mathbb{II}-\frac{(2\omega-k)|\nabla u|^{2}}{(1-u)\omega}\gamma_{t}\Big),

where
\gamma_{t}
is the induced metric on
\Sigma_{t}
. Hence, we have

|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla^{2}u-\Psi_{u}|^{2}=2|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla^{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u||^{2}+|\overset{\circ}{\mathbb{II}}|^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\Big(H-\frac{2(2\omega-k)}{(1-u)\omega}|\nabla u|\Big)^{2}.

(5.1)

Recall that

\mathcal{D}_{k}(t)=\frac{4\pi(1-t)}{k\omega(t)}+\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|H-\frac{4\omega(t)-k}{(1-t)\omega(t)^{3}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

By (
3.12
) and (
5.1
), we get

\mathcal{D}_{k}(t)\geq\int_{t}^{1}\frac{1}{\omega(s)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{|\nabla^{2}u-\Psi_{u}|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+R_{g}\Big]ds=\int_{t}^{1}\psi_{k}(s)ds,

where

\psi_{k}(t)=\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{|\nabla^{2}u-\Psi_{u}|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+R_{g}\Big].

Hence, given two regular values
t_{1}<t_{2}
, taking
f(u)=\frac{k^{2}}{(1-u)^{3}\omega(u)}
in Lemma
2.3
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k}(t_{2})-\mathcal{B}_{k}(t_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{k^{2}}{(1-t)^{2}}\Big[\frac{4\pi}{k}-\frac{4\omega(t)-k}{(1-t)^{2}\omega(t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{(1-t)\omega(t)}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|\nabla u|H\Big]dt

(5.2)

\displaystyle=k^{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\omega(t)}{(1-t)^{3}}\mathcal{D}_{k}(t)dt

\displaystyle\geq k^{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\omega(t)}{(1-t)^{3}}\Big(\int_{t}^{1}\psi_{k}(s)ds\Big)dt.

Taking
t_{2}\to 1
and
t_{1}\to 0
in (
5.2
), we obtain

4\pi k\Big(\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}_{\Sigma}^{-1}-\frac{2(k-1)}{k}\Big)-\mathcal{B}_{k}(0)\geq k^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(t)}{(1-t)^{3}}\Big(\int_{t}^{1}\psi_{k}(s)ds\Big)dt.

Integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(t)}{(1-t)^{3}}\Big(\int_{t}^{1}\psi_{k}(s)ds\Big)dt=

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{2\omega(t)-k}{2(1-t)^{2}}\int_{t}^{1}\psi_{k}(s)ds\Big]\Big|_{t=0}^{t=1}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2\omega(t)-k}{2(1-t)^{2}}\psi_{k}(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to 1}\frac{2\omega(t)-k}{2(1-t)^{2}}\int_{t}^{1}\psi_{k}(s)ds-\frac{2-k}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{k}(s)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2\omega(t)-k}{2(1-t)^{2}}\psi_{k}(t)dt.

We claim that

\lim\limits_{t\to 1}\frac{2\omega(t)-k}{2(1-t)^{2}}\int_{t}^{1}\psi_{k}(s)ds=0.

(5.3)

Indeed, by Lemma
2.1
, we have the following

\int_{t}^{1}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla u|^{-2}|\nabla^{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla u||^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\overset{\circ}{\mathbb{II}}|^{2}=O((1-t)^{1+2\tau})

and

\int_{t}^{1}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}|R_{g}|=o((1-t)^{2}).

Moreover, we have

\int_{t}^{1}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}\Big(H-\frac{2(2\omega-k)}{(1-u)\omega}|\nabla u|\Big)^{2}=O((1-t)^{1+2\tau}).

Hence, (
5.3
) holds.

Now, we find that

\displaystyle 4\pi k\Big(\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}_{\Sigma}^{-1}-\frac{2(k-1)}{k}\Big)-\mathcal{B}_{k}(0)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Big[\frac{2\omega(t)-k}{(1-t)^{2}}+k-2\Big]\psi_{k}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\omega(t)^{2}}\Big[\frac{2\omega(t)-k}{(1-t)^{2}}+k-2\Big]\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Big[\frac{|\nabla^{2}u-\Phi_{u}|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+R_{g}\Big]dt

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{2}}{4}\int_{M}\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}\Big[\frac{2\omega-k}{(1-u)^{2}}+k-2\Big]\Big[\frac{|\nabla^{2}u-\Phi_{u}|^{2}}{|\nabla u|}+R_{g}|\nabla u|\Big].

This proves (
1.24
).

By Proposition
3.2
,
\mathcal{F}_{k}(t)
is is monotone nondecreasing in
t
. Similarly, we have

\mathcal{F}_{k}(t_{2})-\mathcal{F}_{k}(t_{1})\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\Big(\frac{\omega(t)}{1-t}\Big)^{2}\psi_{k}(t)dt.

Letting
t_{2}\to 1
and
t_{1}\to 0
, we get

\displaystyle 8\pi(\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{c}_{\Sigma}^{-1}-\frac{2(k-1)}{k})-\mathcal{F}_{k}(0)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{\omega(t)}{1-t}\Big)^{2}\psi_{k}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2(1-t)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Big[\frac{|\nabla^{2}u-\Psi_{u}|^{2}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}+R_{g}\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\frac{1}{2(1-u)^{2}}\Big[\frac{|\nabla^{2}u-\Psi_{u}|^{2}}{|\nabla u|}+R_{g}|\nabla u|\Big].

This proves (
1.25
).
