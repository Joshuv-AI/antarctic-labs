Chiral Transport in Metric-Affine Geometries
Miguel Á. Vázquez-Mozo

Abstract




Departamento de Física Fundamental, Universidad de Salamanca

Plaza de la Merced s/n,
37008 Salamanca, Spain

Abstract

Anomalous
transport in equilibrium fermionic fluids chirally coupled to background Weyl-type nonmetricity
is studied. A formal descent analysis is
carried out in which the dependence of the
anomaly polynomial on the nonmetricity tensor is encoded in a Weyl
invariant four-form. The constitutive relation of the axial-vector current
is evaluated from the equilibrium partition function obtained using transgression techniques,
showing
the existence of nonmetricity-mediated chiral separation effects driven
by the fluid’s vorticity and the Weyl magnetic field. A second nonminimal coupling
of fermionic matter to metric-affine geometries proposed in the literature is also discussed.

6
,
the constitutive relation for the chiral coupling of ref.
[
20
]

can be obtained from the one for the model proposed in
[
17
]

by an appropriate replacement of the Weyl gauge field.
,
\mathcal{Q}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{ab}=0
. Nevertheless,
for the time being
we are going to be more general and use the general
invariant (
3.4
) to write the anomaly polynomial

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{6}(\mathcal{F}_{A},I^{Q}_{4})=c_{W}\mathcal{F}_{A}\wedge I^{Q}_{4},

(4.1)

where
\mathcal{F}_{A}=d\mathcal{A}
is the field strength of the external gauge field coupling to the
axial-vector fermion current
and
c_{W}
is a
pure number to be determined from the details of the coupling.
This anomaly polynomial is invariant under both axial-vector gauge
transformations
\delta_{\gamma}\mathcal{A}=d\gamma
, with
\gamma
an arbitrary
zero-form, and Weyl transformations, that leave the gauge field
\mathcal{A}
unchanged
[see the discussion after eq. (
3.4
)].
The associated Chern-Simons five-form

\displaystyle\omega^{0}_{5}(\mathcal{A},I^{Q}_{4})=c_{W}\mathcal{A}\wedge I^{Q}_{4},

(4.2)

leads to the following expression of the consistent anomaly for the axial-vector current coupling to
\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\delta_{\gamma}\omega^{0}_{5}

\displaystyle=d\omega^{1}_{4}\hskip 28.45274pt\Longrightarrow\hskip 28.45274pt\omega_{4}^{1}=c_{W}\gamma I^{Q}_{4}.

(4.3)

In writing the Chern-Simons form (
4.2
), we have
chosen to preserve its invariance under Weyl transformations, at the expense of
axial-vector gauge invariance. In fact, we can shift
from one invariance to the other by adding to
\omega^{0}_{5}
an exact differential

\displaystyle\omega^{0}_{5}(a)\equiv\omega^{0}_{5}-{ac_{W}\over 2}d\big(\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{W}_{3}\big),

(4.4)

with
a
a real parameter. Indeed,
for
a=-2
the corresponding Chern-Simons form
\omega^{0}_{5}(-2)=c_{W}\mathcal{F}_{A}\wedge\mathcal{W}_{3}

is invariant under
axial-vector gauge transformations but not under Weyl rescalings (
3.2
).
The associated consistent anomaly for general
a
is given by

\displaystyle\omega^{1}_{4}(a)={(2+a)c_{W}\over 2}I^{Q}_{4},

(4.5)

and vanishes when
a=-2
. Defining the Chern-Simons nonlocal effective action

\displaystyle\Gamma(a)_{\rm CS}=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{5}}\omega^{0}_{5}(a),

(4.6)

where
\mathcal{M}_{5}
is an Euclidean five-dimensional manifold
whose boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}_{5}
is identified with the compatified four-dimensional spacetime, the
integrated consistent
anomaly is obtained from its gauge variation

\displaystyle\delta_{\gamma}\Gamma(a)_{\rm CS}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{5}}\omega^{1}_{4}(a)

\displaystyle={(2+a)c_{W}\over 2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{5}}\gamma I^{Q}_{4}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\equiv-\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{5}}\gamma d\langle\star J_{5}\rangle_{\rm cons},

where
\star
indicates the four-dimensional Hodge dual.

The dependence of the consistent anomaly on the local counterterm
parameter
a
is not shared by the covariant form of the anomaly.
To find the covariant current,
we need to compute first the Bardeen-Zumino (BZ) term
[
43
]
,
given by (see, e.g.,
[
22
,
24
]
)

\displaystyle\langle\star J_{5}\rangle_{\rm BZ}

\displaystyle\equiv-{\delta\over\delta\mathcal{F}_{A}}\Gamma(a)_{\rm CS}

\displaystyle={ac_{W}\over 2}\mathcal{W}_{3}.

(4.8)

Adding this expression to the one of the consistent
current,
\langle\star J_{5}\rangle_{\rm cons}=-{c_{W}(a+2)\over 2}\mathcal{W}_{3}
, obtained
from the boundary variation of the effective action under
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}+\delta\mathcal{A}
,
we get the covariant current

\displaystyle\langle\star J_{5}\rangle_{\rm cov}

\displaystyle=-c_{W}\mathcal{W}_{3}.

(4.9)

This is independent
of
a
, as it is also the case for the covariant anomaly

\displaystyle d\langle\star J_{5}\rangle_{\rm cov}=-c_{W}I^{Q}_{4}.

(4.10)

Since for applications to chiral transport we are
interested
in the constitutive relation of the
covariant axial-vector current
[
44
]
, we set
a=0
from now on.

5
The chiral current constitutive relation

We are now ready to address the problem of nonmetricity-induced transport of chiral charge
in a fermion fluid at thermal equilibrium.
The equilibrium partition function will be computed from the Chern-Simons five-form (
4.2
)
by using the transgression
methods presented, e.g., in refs.
[
22
,
24
,
25
]
, where the reader can
find full details.
We will eventually particularize our expressions to the “pure Weyl” case,
\mathcal{Q}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{ab}=0
,
in order to apply them to the models proposed in refs.
[
17
,
20
]
.

We consider a one-parameter family of connections (
0\leq t\leq 1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}-t\mu_{5}u,

\displaystyle(\omega_{t})^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}-t\mu^{a}_{\,\,\,b}u,

(5.1)

where
u\equiv u_{\mu}dx^{\mu}
is the fluid’s four-velocity one-form
and
\mu_{5}
and
\mu_{ab}
are the chiral and spin chemical potentials, respectively.
The associated field strength, curvature, and nonmetricity have the following expressions

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}

\displaystyle=d\widehat{\mathcal{A}}-2t\mu_{5}\omega+tu\big(d+\mathfrak{a}\big)\mu_{5}\omega,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}_{t})^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}-2t\mu^{a}_{\,\,\,b}\omega+tu\wedge\big(\widehat{D}+\mathfrak{a}\big)\mu^{a}_{\,\,\,b},

(5.2)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{t})_{ab}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{ab}-t(\mu_{Q})_{ab}u,

where

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{R}}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle\equiv d\widehat{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}+\widehat{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,c}\wedge\widehat{\omega}^{c}_{\,\,\,b},

\displaystyle\widehat{D}\mu^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle\equiv d\mu^{a}_{\,\,\,b}+\widehat{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,c}\mu^{c}_{\,\,\,b}-\mu^{a}_{\,\,\,c}\widehat{\omega}^{c}_{\,\,\,b},

(5.3)

while
\mathfrak{a}\equiv u\rfloor du
and
\omega\equiv{1\over 2}(du+u\wedge\mathfrak{a})

are, respectively, the fluid’s acceleration and
vorticity.
The definition of the nonmetricity one-form (
2.1
) leads to the relations

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{ab}

\displaystyle=2\widehat{\omega}_{(ab)},

\displaystyle(\mu_{Q})_{ab}

\displaystyle=2\mu_{(ab)}.

(5.4)

To evaluate the Chern-Simons form,
we apply the Mañes-Stora-Zumino generalized transgression formula
[
45
]

(see Appendix A of ref.
[
24
]
for
a quick overview)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial T}{\ell_{t}^{p}\over p!}\mathscr{Q}=\int_{T}{\ell_{t}^{p+1}\over(p+1)!}d\mathscr{Q}+(-1)^{p+q}d\int_{T}{\ell_{t}^{p+1}\over(p+1)!}\mathscr{Q},

(5.5)

where
\mathscr{Q}
is a polynomial depending on the family of connections and their
curvatures, and
\ell_{t}
is an even operator
replacing the exterior differential
d
by the odd operator

\displaystyle d_{t}=dt{d\over dt}.

(5.6)

In addition,
p+1
is the number of parameters in the family of connections and
q
is the order
of the polynomial
\mathscr{Q}
in the operator
d_{t}
.
We use eq. (
5.5
) with

\displaystyle\mathscr{Q}

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}^{5}(\mathcal{A}_{t},I^{Q}_{4,t}),

\displaystyle d\mathscr{Q}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{6}(\mathcal{A}_{t},I^{Q}_{4,t}),

(5.7)

where
I^{Q}_{4,t}=d\mathcal{W}_{3,t}
and
\mathcal{W}_{3,t}={1\over 2}(\mathcal{Q}^{a}_{\,\,\,b})_{t}\wedge(\mathcal{R}^{b}_{\,\,\,a})_{t}
.
Considering that in our case
T=[0,1]
and
p=q=0
, the transgression
five-form can be written as

\displaystyle\omega_{5}^{0}(\mathcal{A}_{t=1},I^{Q}_{4,t=1})-\omega_{5}^{0}(\mathcal{A}_{t=0},I^{Q}_{4,t=0})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{t}\mathcal{P}_{6}(\mathcal{A}_{t},I^{Q}_{4,t})+d\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{t}\omega_{5}^{0}(\mathcal{A}_{t},I^{Q}_{4,t}).

(5.8)

At equilibrium we have a general stationary background
whose line element takes the form
ds^{2}=u\otimes u+g_{ij}(\mathbf{x})dx^{i}\otimes dx^{j}

and all hatted quantities become purely magnetic, i.e., transverse to
u
, a fact
indicate by boldface fonts. We thus write

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t=1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{A}-u\mu_{5},

\displaystyle(\omega_{t=1})^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}-\mu^{a}_{\,\,\,b}u,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t=1}

\displaystyle=d\boldsymbol{A}-2\mu_{5}\omega+u\big(d+\mathfrak{a}\big)\mu_{5}

\displaystyle\equiv\boldsymbol{B}_{A}+u\wedge E_{A},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}_{t=1})^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle=d\boldsymbol{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}+\boldsymbol{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,c}\wedge\boldsymbol{\omega}^{c}_{\,\,\,b}-2\mu^{a}_{\,\,\,b}\omega+u\wedge\big(\boldsymbol{D}+\mathfrak{a}\big)\mu^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\equiv\boldsymbol{B}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}+u\wedge E^{a}_{\,\,\,b},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{t=1})_{ab}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{Q}_{ab}-(\mu_{Q})_{ab}u,

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{Q}_{t=1})_{ab}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{Q}_{ab}-2(\mu_{Q})_{ab}\omega+u\wedge\Big[\big(\boldsymbol{D}+\mathfrak{a}\big)(\mu_{Q})_{ab}+\boldsymbol{Q}_{cb}\mu^{c}_{\,\,\,a}+\boldsymbol{Q}_{ac}\mu^{c}_{\,\,\,b}\Big]

\displaystyle=2\boldsymbol{B}_{(ab)}+2u\wedge E_{(ab)},

where all quantities are time-independent
and
\boldsymbol{D}
is the exterior covariant differential associated to the magnetic part of the
connection,
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}
.
Moreover, since
\omega_{5}^{0}(\mathcal{A}_{t=0},I^{Q}_{4,t=0})
is a maximal form with no
component along
u
, it vanishes. The equilibrium partition function is then
obtained by integrating eq. (
5.8
)
over the five-dimensional bulk Euclidean space
\mathcal{M}_{5}

\displaystyle W_{\rm eq}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{5}}\omega_{5}^{0}(\mathcal{A}_{t=1},I^{Q}_{4,t=1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{5}}\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{t}\mathcal{P}_{6}(\mathcal{A}_{t},I^{Q}_{4,t})+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{5}}\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{t}\omega_{5}^{0}(\mathcal{A}_{t},I^{Q}_{4,t})

(5.10)

\displaystyle\equiv W_{\rm bulk}+W_{\rm bdy}.

A rather lengthy computation of the terms in the second line renders
the following expressions for the bulk and boundary components of the partition function

\displaystyle W_{\rm bulk}

\displaystyle=-{c_{W}\over 2}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{5}}u\wedge\Bigg\{\mu_{5}\bigg[2\boldsymbol{B}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!^{(ab)}\wedge\boldsymbol{B}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{(ab)}+4(\,\mu\hskip-14.51074pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-9.10509pt}{$\longrightarrow$}}\!_{Q})^{ab}\boldsymbol{B}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{(ab)}\wedge\omega

and

\displaystyle W_{\rm bdy}

\displaystyle={c_{W}\over 2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{5}}\,u\wedge\Big[2(\,\mu\hskip-14.51074pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-9.10509pt}{$\longrightarrow$}}\!_{Q})^{ab}\boldsymbol{B}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{(ab)}-\mu_{[ab]}\,\boldsymbol{Q}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!^{ac}\wedge\,\boldsymbol{Q}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{c}^{\,\,\,b}+(\,\mu\hskip-14.51074pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-9.10509pt}{$\longrightarrow$}}\!_{Q})^{ab}(\,\mu\hskip-14.51074pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-9.10509pt}{$\longrightarrow$}}\!_{Q})_{ab}\omega\Big]\wedge\boldsymbol{A}

given by

(5.14)

together with

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{B}_{Q}

\displaystyle\equiv{1\over 2}\boldsymbol{B}^{a}_{\,\,\,a}=d\boldsymbol{Q}-2\mu_{Q}\omega.

(5.15)

The covariant axial-vector current is obtained from the boundary contribution of
the functional variation of bulk partition function

\boldsymbol{A}

\displaystyle\langle\star J_{5}\rangle_{\rm cov}

A computation of the consistent current from the boundary partition function (

shows that

,
which is consistent with the expression of

The covariant anomaly is then evaluated to give

\displaystyle d\langle\star J_{5}\rangle_{\rm cov}

\displaystyle=-{c_{W}\over 2}u\wedge\Big[4E\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!^{(ab)}\wedge\boldsymbol{B}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{(ab)}+2(\,\mu\hskip-14.51074pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-9.10509pt}{$\longrightarrow$}}\!_{Q})_{ac}\,\,\boldsymbol{Q}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!^{a}_{\,\,\,b}\wedge\boldsymbol{B}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!^{[cb]}+\,\boldsymbol{Q}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!^{ac}\wedge E\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{[ab]}\wedge\,\boldsymbol{Q}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{\,\,\,c}^{b}\Big]

(5.18)

in
[

]

however, we need to set the shear nonmetricity component to zero,
\,\boldsymbol{Q}\hskip-16.78702pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{ab}=(\,\mu\hskip-14.51074pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-9.10509pt}{$\longrightarrow$}}\!_{Q})_{ab}=0

6
Discussion

We have investigated the consequences of background nonmetricity for the transport
of chiral charge in fermionic fluids at equilibrium.
Our main result is the existence of magnetic and vortical nonmetricity-driven
chiral separation effects that, in view of the results of refs.
[
17
,
18
]
,
might be of physical relevance
for the context of Weyl semimetals with point defects.
Both effects
disappear for static geometries with
\mathcal{Q}_{0}=0
(see the related
discussion in ref.
[
17
]
). Our analysis here, however,
considers stationary backgrounds in which
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, and hence
\mu_{Q}
,
is generically nonzero.

In the previous section, we presented the analysis of the model
proposed in
[
17
]
, where the
axial-vector fermionic current couples the Weyl gauge field. The
nonminimal chiral coupling of
ref.
[
20
]
, on the other hand, also involves the
second gauge field
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{a}
, defined in eq. (
2.5
),
as well as the edge torsion
\mathcal{T}_{a}
.
A comparison between the actions (
2.19
)
and (
2.15
) shows that the
results for
the latter model can be found from our analysis
by replacing
\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow{1\over 2}\beta\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}\theta^{a}

[see eq. (
2.16
)]. In particular, the corresponding constitutive relation for the
chiral current is obtained from (
5.21
) by substituting

\displaystyle\mu_{Q}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\beta\over 4}\big(2\mu_{\mathcal{T}}+\mu_{\mathcal{\widehat{Q}}}-4\mu_{Q}\big),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{B}_{Q}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\beta\over 4}\big(2\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathcal{T}}+\boldsymbol{B}_{\widehat{Q}}-4\boldsymbol{B}_{Q}\big),

(6.1)

where the chemical potentials
\mu_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\mu_{\widehat{Q}}
are defined by the
electric-magnetic decompositions of the screw torsion
\mathcal{T}\equiv\mathcal{T}_{a}\theta^{a}

and the second nonmetricity gauge field
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle\equiv\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}-\mu_{\mathcal{T}}u,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle\equiv\widehat{\boldsymbol{Q}}-\mu_{\widehat{Q}}u,

(6.2)

while
\boldsymbol{B}_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\boldsymbol{B}_{\widehat{Q}}
are the
magnetic components of their corresponding field strengths [cf. eq. (
5.15
)].
Interestingly, in this case the two chiral separation effects survive
in the limit of vanishing nonmetricity, when they are driven by
the background’s edge torsion
\mathcal{T}
alone. This is in addition to chiral transport effects
resulting from the minimal chiral coupling of microscopic fermions
to the screw torsion gauge field
\mathcal{S}_{a}
, that were investigated in
[
25
]
.

Appendix A
Some identities in metric-affine geometry

In what follows, we summarize basic identities in
metric-affine geometry
relevant for our discussion (full details can be found, e.g.,
in
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
). Applying the
general expression of the covariant
differential (
2.2
) to
the definition of the nonmetricity tensor in
eq. (
2.1
), we are led to the so-called
zeroth Bianchi identity

\displaystyle D\mathcal{Q}_{ab}=2\mathcal{R}_{(ab)},

(A.1)

where, to compute the right-hand side, we used

\displaystyle D^{2}\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{a_{1}}_{\,\,\,\,c}\wedge\mathfrak{T}^{c\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}+\ldots+\mathcal{R}^{a_{p}}_{\,\,\,\,c}\wedge\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots c}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptc\ldots b_{q}}\wedge\mathcal{R}^{c}_{\,\,\,b_{1}}-\ldots-\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots c}\wedge\mathcal{R}^{c}_{\,\,\,\,b_{q}}.

(A.2)

The first and second Bianchi identities, respectively involving torsion
and curvature, retain their standard form

\displaystyle DT^{a}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}\wedge\theta^{b},

\displaystyle D\mathcal{R}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle=0,

(A.3)

where
T^{a}\equiv D\theta^{a}
is the torsion
two-form. It is important, however, to keep in mind that in the presence of
nonmetricity
we should be wary when lowering and rising indices inside covariant
derivatives, since
extra terms might arise.
For example, from
D\eta_{ab}=-\mathcal{Q}_{ab}
we find
D\delta^{a}_{b}=0
,
while by lowering the contravariant index
the second Bianchi identity in the second line of eq. (
A.3
) we arrive
at
D\mathcal{R}_{ab}=-\mathcal{Q}_{ac}\wedge\mathcal{R}^{c}_{\,\,\,b}
.

The zeroth Bianchi identity (
A.1
) can be recast in terms of the
trace and the shear components of
\mathcal{Q}_{ab}

introduced in eq. (
2.3
).
The Weyl one-form,
\mathcal{Q}={1\over 4}\mathcal{Q}^{a}_{\,\,\,a}
,
satisfies

\displaystyle d\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\mathcal{R}^{a}_{\,\,\,a},

(A.4)

therefore playing the role of a gauge potential
for the trace of the curvature two-form.
As for the shear nonmetricity tensor, its Bianchi identity reads

\displaystyle D\mathcal{Q}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{ab}+\mathcal{Q}\wedge\mathcal{Q}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{ab}=2\mathcal{R}\hskip-15.07993pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\longrightarrow$}}\!_{(ab)},

(A.5)

where we have used the notation defined in (
2.4
).

The
spin connection components
\omega^{a}_{\,\,\,b}\equiv\omega_{c\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}\theta^{c}

can be split
as
[
14
,
15
,
16
]

\displaystyle\omega^{\,\,\,b}_{a\,\,\,c}

\displaystyle=\accentset{\circ}{\omega}^{\,\,\,b}_{a\,\,\,c}+K^{\,\,\,b}_{a\,\,\,c}+L^{\,\,\,b}_{a\,\,\,c},

(A.6)

where
\accentset{\circ}{\omega}^{b}_{\,\,\,c}=\accentset{\circ}{\omega}^{\,\,\,b}_{a\,\,\,c}\theta^{a}
is
the Levi-Civita spin connection,
while
K^{\,\,\,b}_{a\,\,\,c}
and
L^{\,\,\,b}_{a\,\,\,c}
are the contorsion
and disformation tensors, respectively.
In terms of the torsion and nonmetricity components

\displaystyle T^{a}

\displaystyle\equiv{1\over 2}T_{bc}^{\,\,\,\,\,a}\theta^{b}\wedge\theta^{c},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{Q}_{c\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}\theta^{c},

(A.7)

these two tensors are explicitly given by

\displaystyle K_{c\,\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\big(T^{a}_{\,\,\,\,cb}+T^{a}_{\,\,\,\,bc}-T_{bc}^{\,\,\,\,a}\big),

\displaystyle L_{c\,\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{c\,\,\,\,b}^{\,\,\,a}+\mathcal{Q}_{b\,\,\,\,c}^{\,\,\,a}-\mathcal{Q}_{\,\,\,bc}^{a}\big).

(A.8)

Taking into account that
T_{bca}=T_{[bc]a}

and
\mathcal{Q}_{cab}=\mathcal{Q}_{c(ab)}
, we find

\displaystyle K_{cab}

\displaystyle=K_{c[ab]},

\displaystyle L_{c(ab)}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\mathcal{Q}_{cab},

(A.9)

\displaystyle L_{c[ab]}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{abc}-\mathcal{Q}_{bca}\big).

On the other hand, from
the antisymmetry of the Levi-Civita connection,
\accentset{\circ}{\omega}_{ab}=\accentset{\circ}{\omega}_{[ab]}
,
we obtain the following
expressions

\displaystyle\omega_{c(ab)}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\mathcal{Q}_{cab},

\displaystyle\omega_{c[ab]}

\displaystyle=\accentset{\circ}{\omega}_{cab}+K_{cab}+{1\over 2}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{abc}-\mathcal{Q}_{bca}\big).

(A.10)

In particular, the metric-compatible connection

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}_{bc}\equiv\omega_{[bc]},

(A.11)

has nonvanishing torsion given by (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{a}

\displaystyle=T^{a}-{1\over 2}\mathcal{Q}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}\wedge\theta^{b}.

(A.12)

Under generalized
metric rescalings (
B.1
)
and (
B.3
),
\widetilde{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

transforms as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\omega}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}+(1-\Lambda)\delta^{a}_{b}d\sigma,

(A.13)

being therefore Weyl invariant (
\Lambda=1
). Notice that
despite satisfying
\widetilde{D}\eta_{ab}=0
,
the connection
\widetilde{\omega}_{ab}

still has a dependence
on the components of the original nonmetricity one-form
\mathcal{Q}_{ab}
, as
is seen from the second identity in eq. (
A.10
).
Let us mention, incidentally, that the nonmetricity-dependent
term in (
A.12
) does not contribute to the torsional Chern-Simons
form
[
46
,
47
,
25
]
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}\equiv\theta_{a}\widetilde{T}^{a}=\theta_{a}T^{a}\equiv\mathcal{H}
, and therefore neither
to the corresponding Nieh-Yan invariant,
\widetilde{N}\equiv d\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}=T_{a}\wedge T^{a}-\theta^{a}\wedge\mathcal{R}_{ab}\wedge\theta^{b}\equiv N
.

Appendix B
Generalized metric rescalings

In this Appendix we review the implementation of generalized metric rescalings.
We consider the family of transformations
(cf.
[
14
]
)

\displaystyle\theta^{a}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow e^{\Lambda\sigma}\theta^{a},

\displaystyle\eta_{ab}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow e^{2(1-\Lambda)\sigma}\eta_{ab},

(B.1)

\displaystyle\omega^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\omega^{a}_{\,\,\,b}+(\Xi-\Lambda)\delta^{a}_{b}d\sigma,

with
\Lambda
and
\Xi
real parameters, while
the condition
e_{b}\rfloor\theta^{a}=\delta^{a}_{b}
implies

\displaystyle e_{a}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow e^{-\Lambda\sigma}e_{a}.

(B.2)

The corresponding transformation of the
torsion, nonmetricity, and curvature forms read

\displaystyle T^{a}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow e^{\Lambda\sigma}\big(T^{a}+\Xi d\sigma\wedge\theta^{a}\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}+2\big(\Xi-1\big)\delta^{a}_{b}d\sigma,

(B.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{a}_{\,\,\,b},

while for the trace and traceless part of the nonmetricity tensor we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}+2\big(\Xi-1\big)d\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!^{a}_{\,\,\,b}.

(B.4)

Switching to spacetime indices, the transformations (
B.1
)
realize metric rescalings combined with
shifts in the linear connection, torsion, and nonmetricity

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow e^{2\sigma}g_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda}+\Xi\partial_{\mu}\sigma\delta^{\lambda}_{\nu},

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda}+2\Xi\partial_{[\mu}\sigma\delta^{\lambda}_{\nu]},

(B.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda}+2\big(\Xi-1\big)\partial_{\mu}\sigma\delta^{\lambda}_{\nu}.

The two nonmetricity vector fields introduced in
eqs. (
2.3
) and (
2.5
)
undergo the same gauge transformation

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}+2\big(\Xi-1\big)\partial_{\mu}\sigma,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mu}+2\big(\Xi-1\big)\partial_{\mu}\sigma.

(B.6)

In the language of ref.
[
48
]
,
\Xi=0
and
\Xi=1
correspond
to conformal transformations and frame rescalings, respectively.
Following e.g.
[
49
,
50
]
, we refer
to the case
\Lambda=1
,
\Xi=0

as Weyl transformations.
“Pure gauge” projective
transformations
[
48
,
51
,
52
,
20
]

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow g_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda}+\partial_{\mu}\sigma^{\prime}\delta^{\lambda}_{\nu},

(B.7)

are retrieved from previous expressions by setting
\sigma=\Xi^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}
and
taking the limit
\Xi\rightarrow\infty
.

Given a general
r
-form tensor of type
(p,q)

of weight
w
under (
B.1
)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}\longrightarrow e^{w\sigma}\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}},

(B.8)

a new covariant derivative can be defined for
\Xi\neq 1

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}\equiv D\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}+{(\Xi-\Lambda)(p-q)+w\over 2(1-\Xi)}\mathcal{Q}\wedge\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}},

(B.9)

satisfying

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}\longrightarrow e^{w\sigma}\mathscr{D}\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}.

(B.10)

Particularizing to
Weyl transformations (
\Lambda=1
,
\Xi=0
), the previous definition simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}\equiv D\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}}+{w-p+q\over 2}\mathcal{Q}\wedge\mathfrak{T}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}_{\hskip 22.76228ptb_{1}\ldots b_{q}},

(B.11)

in terms of which the torsion and shear nonmetricity are written as

\displaystyle T^{a}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{D}e^{a},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{ab}

\displaystyle=-\mathscr{D}\eta_{ab},

(B.12)

while the three Bianchi identities (
A.5
)
and (
A.3
) take the form

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\mathcal{Q}\hskip-14.22636pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$}}\!_{ab}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{R}\hskip-15.07993pt\rotatebox{45.0}{\raisebox{-7.11317pt}{$\longrightarrow$}}\!_{(ab)},

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}T^{a}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}\wedge e^{b},

(B.13)

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\mathcal{R}^{a}_{\,\,\,b}

\displaystyle=0.

Being expressed in terms of the covariant derivative
defined in (
B.11
),
the previous expressions are manifestly Weyl covariant.
