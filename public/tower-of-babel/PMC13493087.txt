LncRNA RAD51-AS1 Alleviates Oxidative Stress and Inflammation in Diabetic Nephropathy by Adsorbing miR-154-5p
Authors: Mengmeng Pu, Yijun Chen, Dewei Ouyang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDiabetic nephropathy (DN) is one of the severe complications of diabetes. The specific roles and mechanisms of lncRNAs in DN remain to be further elucidated. This study aimed to investigate whether lncRNA RAD51-AS1 participates in oxidative stress and inflammatory responses in DN by regulating the miR-154-5p/MPC2 axis, and to elucidate its potential molecular mechanisms.MethodsThis study included 74 patients with DN, 104 patients with type 2 diabetes alone, and 80 healthy controls, assessing their clinical indexes and gene expression. DN models were established using HK-2 cells treated with high-glucose (HG). Cell proliferation and apoptosis were assessed using CCK-8 assay and flow cytometry. Oxidative stress indicators and inflammatory factors were measured by ELISA. The targeting relationship was validated using dual-luciferase reporter assay.ResultsClinical studies indicated that serum RAD51-AS1 levels were downregulated in DN patients, and it was positively correlated with estimated glomerular filtration rate while negatively correlated with blood urea nitrogen, serum creatinine, and urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio. Overexpression of RAD51-AS1 significantly inhibited HG-induced oxidative stress and inflammatory responses in HK-2 cells, manifested as enhanced cell proliferation, suppressed apoptosis, reduced levels of MDA, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α, and increased SOD activity. Furthermore, overexpression of miR-154-5p reversed protective effect of RAD51-AS1 on cell injury. In addition, as an endogenous competitive RNA, RAD51-AS1 adsorbed miR-154-5p, thereby releasing its suppression of the target gene MPC2.ConclusionRAD51-AS1 is downregulated in DN, and it regulates MPC2 by adsorbing miR-154-5p, thereby participating in oxidative stress and inflammatory responses in DN. This study elucidated a novel mechanism of RAD51-AS1/miR-154-5p/MPC2 axis in DN pathogenesis, providing potential theoretical basis and new targets for therapy of DN.

Introduction

As a complication of diabetes, diabetic nephropathy (DN) has emerged as a leading cause of end-stage renal disease worldwide [1]. The pathology is characterized by persistent proteinuria, progressive decline in glomerular filtration rate, and tubulointerstitial fibrosis [2]. Although factors such as hyperglycemia and hemodynamic abnormalities have been implicated in DN, its precise pathogenesis remains incompletely understood [3, 4]. Recently, increasing evidence indicates that beyond metabolic disorders, oxidative stress and chronic inflammation take center stage in renal tissue damage in DN [5]. The excessive production of reactive oxygen species and pro-inflammatory factors induced by a high-glucose (HG) environment, which directly lead to dysfunction and apoptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells, ultimately accelerating DN [6]. Therefore, it is especially important to explore the molecular mechanisms that induce the production of oxidation products and inflammatory factors in DN.

In exploring the molecular mechanisms of DN, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are increasingly emerging as pivotal nodes regulating complex disease networks [7]. Although lncRNAs do not encode proteins, they finely regulate gene expression through multiple ways, including epigenetic regulation, transcription, and post-transcriptional modifications [8–10]. In DN, multiple lncRNAs have been confirmed to be expressed abnormally and participate in disease progression by affecting biological processes such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, fibrosis, and inflammation [11]. For example, LINC00707 exerts a pro-damaging role in DN by regulating the miR-223-3p/FKBP5 axis to affect podocyte proliferation, apoptosis, and inflammatory responses [12]. LncRNA 4930556M19Rik inhibits HG-induced podocyte apoptosis, fibrosis, and inflammation by adsorbing miR-27a-3p and upregulating TIMP3, thereby exerting a protective effect [13]. As an essential mechanism of action, the competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) hypothesis reveals that lncRNAs competitively deregulate miRNA on their target genes, thereby forming intricate regulatory networks [14].

This study focused on RAD51-AS1, which has been limitedly researched and mainly concentrated in cancer fields. Chen et al. [15] demonstrated that RAD51-AS1 is downregulated in prostate cancer cells and exerts antitumor effects via the HNRNPC/RARA/ZYX axis. Li et al. [16] revealed that RAD51-AS1 suppresses proliferation, migration, and glycolysis in colorectal cancer cells by sponging miR-29b-3p and miR-29c-3p and regulating NDRG2. Additionally, bioinformatics suggested that its expression levels were markedly dysregulated in DN patients’ peripheral blood, indicating that it may act as a major player in DN pathological process [17]. However, its precise function and underlying molecular mechanisms in DN, especially whether it participates in regulation as a ceRNA, remain unclear. Based on bioinformatics predictions, we noted that miR-154-5p is a potential target of RAD51-AS1. Research has revealed that miR-154-5p is upregulated in DN rats, inhibits proliferation of glomerular mesangial cells, and engages in the fibrotic process [18]. Further analysis identified mitochondrial pyruvate carrier 2 (MPC2) as a downstream target of miR-154-5p. MPC2 plays a vital function in cellular energy metabolism and oxidative homeostasis [19]. Additionally, MPC1 and MPC2 exhibited reduced expression levels in DN patients and showed remarkable correlation with renal function indicators [20]. However, whether its role in DN is regulated by RAD51-AS1/miR-154-5p axis remains to be explored.

We conducted clinical sample studies and in vitro cell experiments to explore expression patterns, biological functions, and molecular mechanisms of RAD51-AS1 in DN. It aimed to provide a new theoretical perspective for understanding DN pathogenesis and providing a theoretical basis for its diagnostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets.

Methods

Clinical Cases

A total of 178 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) admitted to our hospital between 2022 and 2024 were selected as study subjects. They were diagnosed according to the American Diabetes Association criteria [21]. They were divided into two groups based on whether they had concurrent DN. DM group (n = 104) were T2DM patients without nephropathy. DN group (n = 74), they met the diagnostic criteria of urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) ≥30 mg/g or estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <60 mL/min/1.73 m2. The exclusion criteria were as follows: patients with type 1 diabetes, gestational diabetes, or other special types of diabetes. Patients with concomitant chronic kidney disease caused by non-diabetic factors. Patients with autoimmune diseases, malignant tumors, cardiovascular diseases, or hematological disorders. Patients who have recently used glucocorticoids or immunosuppressants. Additionally, 80 volunteers who underwent physical examinations at our hospital during the same period were included as the healthy control group.

Basic information for all participants was collected using standardized case report forms. Fasting venous blood samples were collected on the morning of admission or physical examination. A portion of whole blood samples were used for point-of-care glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) testing. The remaining blood samples were centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 15 min. The supernatant was collected and stored at −80°C for subsequent biochemical parameter and gene expression analysis. Simultaneously, about 10 mL of midstream urine collected in the morning was obtained. After centrifugation, the supernatant was collected and stored at −80°C for UACR testing.

Cell Culture and HG Induction

Human renal tubular epithelial cell line HK-2 was purchased from ATCC. Cells were cultured in DMEM/F-12 medium containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator. When cells reached 80%–90% fusion, they were digested with trypsin. Cells in the logarithmic growth phase were used for subsequent experiments.

To simulate the glucose metabolism environment within DN, this study employed high concentration glucose treatment of HK-2 cells to establish an in vitro model. For normal glucose (control) group, cells were cultured in complete medium containing 5.5 mm glucose. For HG group, cells were cultured in complete medium containing 30 mm glucose. Cells in each group were treated in their corresponding medium for 48 h, then collected and used for subsequent transfection and detection.

Cell Transfection

RAD51-AS1 and MPC2 overexpression plasmid and its negative control were designed and constructed by GenePharma (Shanghai, China), named oe-RAD51-AS1, oe-NC, pcDNA3.1-MPC2, and pcDNA3.1. miR-154-5p mimic and the negative controls (mimic NC) were synthesized by Ribobio (Guangzhou, China). Lipofectamine™ 3000 (L3000001, Invitrogen, USA) was used as the transfection reagent. HK-2 cells in logarithmic growth phase were seeded into cell culture plate and transfected when the fusion rate reached 60%–70%. Six h after transfection, the medium was replaced. After 48 h, transfection efficiency was verified, and other experimental assays were conducted.

RT-qPCR Assay

Cells were lysed using Trizol reagent (15596026CN, Thermo Fisher, USA), and total RNA was extracted following the protocol. Reverse transcription was performed by Reverse AID First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (K1622, Thermo Fisher, USA). Amplification and detection were conducted by SYBR Green Quantitative PCR Kit (RR047A, Takara, Japan) on QuantStudio instrument. Reaction conditions were pre-denaturation at 95°C for 30 s, followed by denaturation at 95°C for 5 s and annealing at 60°C for 30 s, repeated for 40 cycles. GAPDH served as the reference gene for normalizing RAD51-AS1 and MPC2 expressions, and U6 for miR-154-5p. Relative expressions were calculated by 2−ΔΔCt method. All primers used in this study were synthesized by Sangon (Shanghai, China). The primer sequences were as follows: RAD51-AS1, forward 5′-TCGCAGCTGGACTATTAGCG-3′ and reverse 5′-ATGCGAGTAGGAGGCTCAGA-3′; miR-154-5p, forward 5′-GCCGAGUAGGUUAUCCGUGU-3′ and reverse 5′-CTCAACTGGTGTCGTGGA-3′; MPC2, forward 5′-CCCCACCGTGTCTTAATGTC-3′ and reverse 5′-CACAGTGGACTGAGCTGTGC-3′; GAPDH, forward, 5′-CATCAACGGGAAGCCCATC-3′ and reverse, 5′-CTCGTGGTTCACACCCATC-3′; U6, forward, 5′-CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3′ and reverse, 5′-AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT-3′.

CCK8 Assay

HK-2 cells in logarithmic growth phase were trypsinized and resuspended. Cells were seeded at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well in a 96-well plate. They were placed in an incubator at 37°C and 5% CO2 for 24 h. Subsequently, CCK-8 assays (C0037, Beyotime, China) were performed at 0 h, 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h. Under dark conditions, 10 μL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 2 h. The absorbance of each well was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Flow Cytometry

HK-2 cells in logarithmic growth phase were seeded into 6-well plates and digested with trypsin without EDTA. All cell precipitates were collected by centrifuging at 1,000 rpm for 5 min. Spare cells were obtained by resuspending and washing cells twice with pre-cooled PBS. The procedure was carried out with the Annexin V-FITC kit (556547, BD, USA) and all steps were performed following the instructions and under dark conditions. Detection was conducted using a flow cytometer, and data was analyzed with FlowJo software.

Oxidative Stress and Inflammatory Factor Assays

Cells from each group were gently washed twice with pre-cooled PBS before collection. Cell suspensions were thoroughly lysed on ice, then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4°C to obtain cell lysates. Malondialdehyde (MDA, A003-1-2) content and superoxide dismutase (SOD, A001-3-2) activity were measured using commercial detection kits from the corresponding Nanjing Jiancheng Biotechnology Institute.

The culture supernatants from each cell group were collected and centrifuged at 4°C and 3,000 rpm for 10 min to remove cell debris. The supernatants were taken directly for the determination of inflammatory cytokine concentrations. The concentration of pro-inflammatory factors was detected by ELISA kits (ml058059, ml077385, ml058097) from Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay

Bioinformatics software lncRNASNP2 and miRDB were used to predict the binding sites of 3‘UTR regions of RAD51-AS1 and MPC2 with miR-154-5p. Accordingly, RAD51-AS1 and MPC2 fragments containing the wild-type binding sites were designed and cloned into the pmirGLO vector to construct the RAD51-AS1-WT and MPC2-WT plasmids. The key complementary bases to miR-154-5p in the binding sites were mutated to construct mutant reporter plasmids RAD51-AS1-MUT and MPC2-MUT. When cell fusion reached 70–80%, co-transfection with miR-154-5p mimic/inhibitor was performed. After 48 h, cell lysates were collected and analyzed by dual luciferase reporter assay kit (DL101-01, Vazyme, China).

Statistical Analysis

All data in the study were processed by SPSS 26.0, and figures were generated by GraphPad Prism 9.0. All experiments were independently repeated at least 3 times. Quantitative data are expressed as mean ± SD. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Independent samples t tests were used for comparisons between two groups, while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed for multiple groups, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for pairwise comparisons between groups. For the gender analysis among three groups, the χ2 test was employed. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was employed to analyze the diagnostic performance of RAD51-AS1 in DN. Pearson correlation analysis was employed to examine the linear relationship between RAD51-AS1 levels and renal function indicators. The raw data and original gel images supporting this study have been submitted as online supplementary Files 1, 2 (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000552931).

RAD51-AS1 Has a Diagnostic Value for DN

In serum samples of study subjects, RAD51-AS1 levels were significantly reduced in DM patients, with the lowest levels observed in DN patients, demonstrating a significant difference (Fig. 1a). ROC analysis indicated that RAD51-AS1 demonstrated an AUC of 0.809 for distinguishing DM patients from healthy controls (sensitivity = 73.6%, specificity = 73.8%, cut-off = 0.815, 95% CI = 0.753–0.864, p < 0.001, Fig. 1b). In addition, RAD51-AS1 demonstrated a higher capacity to distinguish DN from DM patients, with an AUC of 0.870, a sensitivity of 85.1%, and a specificity of 75.0% (cut-off = 0.645, 95% CI = 0.817–0.924, p < 0.001, Fig. 1c).

RAD51-AS1 has a diagnostic value for DN. a Serum RAD51-AS1 levels in healthy controls, DM, and DN groups. ROC curve analysis of RAD51-AS1’s ability to distinguish DM patients from healthy controls (b) and DN from DM patients (c). ***p < 0.001.

Correlation between RAD51-AS1 and Renal Function Indicators

The correlation between serum RAD51-AS1 levels and renal function indicators in DN patients was further analyzed, with results shown in Figures 2a–d. RAD51-AS1 levels exhibited significant negative correlations with BUN (r = −0.720, p < 0.001), Scr (r = −0.714, p < 0.001), and UACR (r = −0.633, p < 0.001), and positive correlations with eGFR (r = 0.609, p < 0.001).

Correlation between RAD51-AS1 and renal function indicators. Correlation of RAD51-AS1 with BUN (a), eGFR (b), Scr (c), and UACR (d) in DN patients.

Overexpression of RAD51-AS1 Alleviates Oxidative Stress and Inflammation in HK-2 Cells

Transfection of oe-RAD51-AS1 greatly increased RAD51-AS1 expression in HK-2 cells (Fig. 3a). HG treatment decreased RAD51-AS1 levels in cells, while transfection with oe-RAD51-AS1 restored its expression (Fig. 3b). HG induced proliferation inhibition and apoptosis acceleration in HK-2 cells, while overexpression of RAD51-AS1 reversed these effects (Fig. 3c, d). Additionally, HG resulted in raised MDA levels, decreased SOD activity, and elevated proinflammatory factor concentrations in cells, whereas overexpression of RAD51-AS1 attenuated these trends (Fig. 3e–g).

Overexpression of RAD51-AS1 alleviates oxidative stress and inflammation in HK-2 cells. a Effect of transfection of oe-RAD51-AS1 on RAD51-AS1 expression. Effects of HG treatment and RAD51-AS1 overexpression on RAD51-AS1 expression (b), cell proliferation (c), apoptosis (d), MDA levels (e), SOD activity (f), and proinflammatory factor concentrations (g). Cell experiment data were obtained from three independent experiments. *p < 0.05, ***p < 0.001.

RAD51-AS1 Adsorbs miR-154-5p

The binding sites between RAD51-AS1 and miR-154-5p were predicted using lncRNASNP2 (Fig. 4a). Co-transfection of RAD51-AS1-WT and miR-154-5p mimic decreased the luciferase activity in cells, and co-transfection of miR-154-5p inhibitor increased the activity, while co-transfection of RAD51-AS1-MUT with this did not show significant difference (Fig. 4b). Furthermore, in serum samples from study subjects, miR-154-5p levels were elevated in DM patients and even higher in DN patients (Fig. 4c). In DN patients’ serum, miR-154-5p presented a significant negative correlation with RAD51-AS1 (Fig. 4d). Additionally, HG treatment caused an elevation in miR-154-5p levels in HK-2 cells, while overexpression of RAD51-AS1 reversed the levels (Fig. 4e).

Upregulation of miR-154-5p Counteracts the Protection of RAD51-AS1 Overexpression in HK-2 Cells

Under HG conditions, overexpression of RAD51-AS1 reduced miR-154-5p levels, whereas co-transfection of oe-RAD51-AS1 and miR-154-5p mimic restored its expression (Fig. 5a). Compared to overexpressing RAD51-AS1, co-upregulating both RAD51-AS1 and miR-154-5p inhibited cell proliferation and accelerated apoptosis (Fig. 5b, c). In addition, co-transfection of oe-RAD51-AS1 and miR-154-5p mimic reversed the protective effect of RAD51-AS1 overexpression on cells, aggravating oxidative stress and inflammatory responses (Fig. 5d–f).

MPC2 Is a Target of miR-154-5p

Binding sites between miR-154-5p and MPC2 were predicted using miRDB (Fig. 6a). MPC2-WT co-transfection with miR-154-5p mimic reduced luciferase activity, while co-transfection with miR-154-5p inhibitor showed the opposite trend. MPC2-MUT co-transfection with either miR-154-5p mimic or inhibitor showed no significant difference in the activity (Fig. 6b). Additionally, MPC2 levels were reduced in DM patients’ serum, with even lower levels observed in DN patients (Fig. 6c). MPC2 levels were negatively correlated with miR-154-5p (r = −0.777, p < 0.001) and positively correlated with RAD51-AS1 (r = 0.748, p < 0.001) in DN patients (Fig. 6d, e). Furthermore, in HK-2 cells, HG treatment reduced MPC2 levels, and overexpression of RAD51-AS1 increased its expression, while co-transfection of oe-RAD51-AS1 and miR-154-5p mimic reversed its expression (Fig. 6f).

Correlation of MPC2 with miR-154-5p (d) and RAD51-AS1 (e) in DN patients. f Effects of HG treatment, transfection of oe-RAD51-AS1 and co-transfection of oe-RAD51-AS1 and miR-154-5p mimic on MPC2 levels. miR-154-5p Regulates Oxidative Stress and Inflammatory Responses in HK-2 Cells by Targeting MPC2

As shown in Figure 7a, compared with the HG group, transfection with miR-154-5p mimic significantly reduced MPC2 expression, whereas transfection with pcDNA3.1-MPC2 reversed this effect. In addition, transfection of miR-154-5p mimic on HG basis inhibited cell proliferation, accelerated cell apoptosis, and promoted oxidative stress and inflammatory response, while these trends were partially reversed after co-transfection of miR-154-5p mimic and pcDNA3.1-MPC2 (Fig. 7b–f).

Discussion

The pathological progression of DN is complex and multifaceted. Oxidative stress and chronic inflammation driven by a HG environment are widely recognized as factors contributing to decline in glomerular filtration function and tubulointerstitial fibrosis [22]. However, the regulatory networks of these pathological events remain largely unknown and require further exploration. This study systematically revealed the ceRNA regulatory pathway of RAD51-AS1, which exerts a crucial role in DN process by targeting miR-154-5p to modulate MPC2 expression.

In clinical samples, RAD51-AS1 levels were lower in DN patients compared to those with T2DM alone and healthy individuals. More importantly, its decreased expression showed a statistically negative correlation with renal injury markers such as BUN, UACR, and Scr, whereas it exhibited a positive correlation with the renal function indicator eGFR. These findings are consistent with previous studies on roles of SNHG14 and ANRIL in DN [23, 24]. Notably, there was no significant difference in diabetes duration between patients in the DM and DN groups in this study. This result reinforces the value of RAD51-AS1 as an independent diagnostic marker. ROC curve analysis revealed that the AUC for distinguishing DN from DM patients was 0.870, which further confirmed its diagnostic efficacy. This discovery implied that decreased RAD51-AS1 expression may be closely associated with DN development and progression, making it a potential biomarker.

To explore its biological functions in depth, we established a HG-induced HK-2 cell model. This model is widely used in in vitro studies of DN. In a study by Kuang et al. [25] the DANCR/miR-214-5p/KLF5 axis was investigated for its effect on renal tubular injury in DN through this model. In our research, restoring RAD51-AS1 expression effectively alleviated HG-induced cell injury. Specifically, it promoted cell proliferation, inhibited apoptosis, and significantly improved oxidative stress and inflammatory conditions. These results confirmed that RAD51-AS1 is an essential protective factor for renal tubular epithelial cells in response to hyperglycemic injury.

At the mechanism levels, ceRNA models provide compelling explanations for lncRNA functions [26]. lncRNA has been extensively reported as a ceRNA in DN. For instance, TUG1 suppresses HG-induced cell fibrosis by targeting miR-21 and regulating TIMP3 [27]. Wang et al. indicated that HOTAIR promotes HG-induced cell proliferation, fibrosis, and oxidative stress by adsorbing miR-147a and activating the WNT2B signaling pathway, thereby accelerating DN progression [28]. We confirmed that RAD51-AS1 directly binds to miR-154-5p, thereby relieving its suppression of downstream target genes. Our rescue assay offered direct evidence. Overexpression of miR-154-5p dramatically diminished the protective effect mediated by RAD51-AS1, indicating that miR-154-5p is a key factor in DN progression.

This study further identified MPC2 as a functional target of miR-154-5p in DN. MPC2 is a critical component of the mitochondrial pyruvate carrier, which is central to cellular energy metabolism and oxidative homeostasis [29, 30]. Research has revealed that SIRT3 regulates mitochondrial function by acetylating MPC2, resulting in mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis in podocytes [31]. In this study, rescue assays further validated that miR-154-5p overexpression aggravated cell injury, while overexpression of MPC2 partially reversed the injury induced by miR-154-5p mimic. This result not only confirms that MPC2 is a functional target of miR-154-5p but also provides evidence for the protective role of MPC2 in mitochondrial metabolic regulation. We hypothesized that upregulation of MPC2 expression may enhance mitochondrial function by regulating mitochondrial pyruvate metabolism, thereby reducing oxidative stress production and indirectly inhibiting related inflammatory signaling pathways, ultimately alleviating DN injury. This provided new associations and insights into the mechanisms of metabolic disorders with oxidative stress and inflammation in DN.

This study also has several limitations. First, the protective role of RAD51-AS1/miR-154-5p/MPC2 axis has yet to be conclusively validated in DN animal models. Second, the molecular pathways by which MPC2 affect oxidative stress and inflammatory signaling networks require further investigation. Finally, whether RAD51-AS1 participates in DN regulation through mechanisms other than miR-154-5p also remains future exploration.

In conclusion, the findings of this study enriched our understanding of the ceRNA regulatory network in DN pathogenesis. Furthermore, it established the crucial role of RAD51-AS1/miR-154-5p/MPC2 as a novel signaling axis in DN progression. It is expected to become a novel biomarker for assessing DN and a target for therapy.
