A Transverse Averaging Operator and Cohomology of Quotients by Non-closed Subgroups
Yi Lin

Abstract
. In this article, we introduce a transverse averaging operator for basic forms on a Riemannian foliation equipped with an isometric transverse Lie algebra action, under the assumption that the leaf closure space is compact. Unlike the classical averaging operator in equivariant geometry, which is defined by integration over a compact Lie group, our operator is built purely from infinitesimal transverse data and does not require any global group action. We show that it sends every closed basic form to an invariant basic form representing the same basic cohomology class. As a main application, we compute the diffeological de Rham cohomology of the homogeneous space G / H G/H , where G G is a connected Lie group, not necessarily compact, and H H is a connected Lie subgroup, not necessarily closed. Let 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} and 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} be the Lie algebras of G G and H H , respectively. Assuming that 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is of compact type and that G / H ¯ G/\overline{H} is compact, we prove that H d ​ R ∙ ​ ( G / H ) ≅ H ∙ ​ ( 𝔤 , 𝔥 ) . H^{\bullet}_{dR}(G/H)\cong H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}). If, in addition, 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} is an ideal in 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , then under the weaker assumption that G / H ¯ G/\overline{H} is compact, we obtain H d ​ R ∙ ​ ( G / H ) ≅ H ∙ ​ ( 𝔤 / 𝔥 ) , H^{\bullet}_{dR}(G/H)\cong H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}), without requiring 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} to be of compact type.

1.
Introduction

Reinhart
[
15
]
introduced the notion of
basic cohomology

as a cohomology theory for the leaf space of a foliation. It has
proved to be a powerful and flexible tool in the study of Riemannian
foliations. Killing foliations form an important class of Riemannian
foliations and are known to possess a type of internal symmetry
given by the transverse action of their structural Lie algebras. In
order to study this symmetry, Goertsches and Töben
[
5
]

introduced the notion of isometric transverse Lie algebra actions on
Riemannian foliations and the associated equivariant basic cohomology,
and proved, among other things, a Borel-type localization theorem for
the transverse actions of structural Lie algebras on Killing foliations.
Lin and Sjamaar
[
10
]
subsequently studied isometric transverse Lie algebra actions
on general Riemannian foliations, established several fundamental properties of such actions,
and generalized the Borel-type localization theorem to this broader setting.
More recently, Lin and Wang
[
11
]
extensively developed equivariant Morse–Bott theory for
isometric transverse Lie algebra actions on general Riemannian foliations.

A key tool in classical equivariant geometry is the averaging operator over a compact
Lie group, which allows one to replace arbitrary closed differential forms by invariant ones
without changing cohomology classes. Historically, this averaging principle plays an
important role in the classical study of homogeneous spaces. Building on earlier work of
Cartan, Chevalley and Eilenberg identified the de Rham cohomology of
G/H
with the
relative Lie algebra cohomology
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
when
G
is
compact and connected and
H\subset G
is closed and connected. In the study of
isometric transverse Lie algebra actions, however, there is in general no compact Lie
group acting on the foliated manifold
M
, and the classical compact-group averaging
mechanism is therefore unavailable. The main technical contribution of this paper is the
construction of a transverse analogue of this averaging operator for isometric transverse
Lie algebra actions on Riemannian foliations. In contrast with the classical averaging
operator, our construction is built purely from infinitesimal transverse data and does
not require any global group action on the underlying manifold
M
.

The main difficulty lies in the absence of a global compact group action on

M
. Our approach combines several ingredients from the theory of Riemannian
foliations, notably Molino’s structural theory, the framework for isometric transverse
Lie algebra actions developed by Lin and Sjamaar, Sergiescu’s
transverse integration, and basic Poincaré duality. Following
[
10
]
, we
pass to the transverse orthonormal frame bundle

Let

,

.

2.
Transverse geometric structures on foliations

This section reviews the transverse geometric and algebraic background used in
the sequel. After fixing notation for transverse vector fields and basic forms,
we recall transverse Riemannian metrics, transverse Killing vector fields, and
the Cartan model for equivariant basic cohomology. We also review relative
Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology, which will be used in the proof of
Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on a smooth manifold
M
, and let
T\mathcal{F}
be the tangent bundle of the foliation.
Throughout this paper we denote by

\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})\subset\mathfrak{X}(M)
the subspace of vector fields tangent to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.
We say that a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is
foliate
, if
[X,Y]\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})
for all
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})
.
We will denote by

\mathfrak{R}(\mathcal{F})
the space of foliate vector fields on
(M,\mathcal{F})
.
Clearly we have that

\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})\subset\mathfrak{R}(\mathcal{F})
.
In this context, a
transverse vector field
is an equivalence class in the quotient space
\mathfrak{R}(\mathcal{F})/\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})
. The space of transverse vector fields, denoted by
\mathfrak{X}(M,\mathcal{F})
, forms a Lie algebra with a Lie bracket inherited from the natural one on
\mathfrak{R}(\mathcal{F})
.
The space of
basic forms
on
M
is

\Omega^{\bullet}(M,\mathcal{F}):=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{\bullet}(M)\mid\iota_{X}\alpha=\mathcal{L}_{X}\alpha=0,\ \forall X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})\}.

Since
d
preserves basic forms,
(\Omega^{\bullet}(M,\mathcal{F}),d)
is a subcomplex
of the de Rham complex. Its cohomology
H^{\bullet}(M,\mathcal{F})
is called the

basic cohomology
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
transverse Riemannian metric
on a foliation
(M,\mathcal{F})
is a Riemannian metric
g
on the normal bundle
TM/T\mathcal{F}
of the foliation, such that
\mathcal{L}_{X}g=0
,
for
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{F})
.
We say that
(\mathcal{F},g)
is a Riemannian foliation if there exists a transverse Riemannian metric
g
on
(M,\mathcal{F})
.

Definition 2.2
.

A Riemannian metric
g_{TM}
on a foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F})
is

bundle-like
if for every open set
U\subset M
and every pair of
foliate vector fields
v,w
on
U
that are orthogonal to the leaves,
the function
g_{TM}(v,w)
is basic on
U
.

A bundle-like Riemannian metric
g_{TM}
on
(M,\mathcal{F})
induces a transverse
Riemannian metric
g
by identifying
N\mathcal{F}
with the

g_{TM}
-orthogonal complement of
T\mathcal{F}
and restricting
g_{TM}
to

N\mathcal{F}
. Conversely, every transverse Riemannian metric arises in this way
from a bundle-like Riemannian metric. We say that a Riemannian foliation

(M,\mathcal{F},g)
is
complete
if there exists a complete bundle-like
Riemannian metric on
M
inducing
g
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a Riemannian foliation with transverse metric
g
, and let
\overline{X}\in\mathfrak{X}(M,\mathcal{F})
be a transverse vector field represented by a foliate vector field
X
. Define

\mathcal{L}_{\overline{X}}g:=\mathcal{L}_{X}g
. This is well defined, i.e. independent of the choice of representative
X
. We say that
\overline{X}
is
transversely Killing
if
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{X}}g=0
. If
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
are transversely Killing, then the Cartan identities imply that
[\overline{X},\overline{Y}]
is again transversely Killing. Thus the space of transversely Killing vector fields, denoted by
\mathrm{iso}(M,\mathcal{F})
, is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{X}(M,\mathcal{F})
, called the
transverse Killing Lie algebra
.

2.1.
Transverse Lie algebra action and the equivariant basic cohomology

Definition 2.3
.

A

of a Lie algebra

is a Lie algebra homomorphism

(2.1)

\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M,\mathcal{F}).

When the foliation

under consideration is Riemannian with a given transverse Riemannian metric, a transverse action of

\iota(\xi)\alpha:=\iota_{\xi_{M}}\alpha,\qquad\mathcal{L}(\xi)\alpha:=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{M}}\alpha.

2.2.
Relative Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a
Lie subalgebra. Recall that the
Chevalley–Eilenberg complex
of
\mathfrak{g}
is

(C_{CE}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}),d_{CE}):=(\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*},d_{CE}),

where the differential

d_{CE}:\Lambda^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\>\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 4.49588pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 4.49588pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.99176pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces}}}}\>\Lambda^{k+1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}

of

, denoted

.

For

, let
\iota_{X}
denote contraction by

\operatorname{ad}^{*}(X)
denote the coadjoint action of

(\operatorname{ad}^{*}(X)\alpha)(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k})=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha(Y_{1},\dots,[X,Y_{i}],\dots,Y_{k}),

and

\operatorname{ad}^{*}(X)=d_{CE}\iota_{X}+\iota_{X}d_{CE}.

Definition 2.4
.

The relative Chevalley–Eilenberg complex of the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})

is the subcomplex

C_{CE}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}):=\{\alpha\in\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\mid\iota_{X}\alpha=0,\ \theta(X)\alpha=0,\ \forall X\in\mathfrak{h}\}.

C_{CE}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is preserved by
d_{CE}
.
The cohomology of the cochain complex
(C_{CE}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}),d_{CE})

is called the relative Lie algebra cohomology of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
and is
denoted by
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.

Clearly, if
\mathfrak{h}=0
, then

C_{CE}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})=C_{CE}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
and

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})=H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Lemma 2.5

If
\mathfrak{h}

\mathfrak{g}
, then the quotient map

q:\mathfrak{g}\>\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.55554pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 4.49588pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 4.49588pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.99176pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces}}}}\>\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
induces a natural isomorphism of

Consequently,

The following result is also standard. We include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra of compact type, i.e.,

\mathfrak{g}
admits a bi-invariant metric. Then the
inclusion

\iota:\bigl((\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathrm{ad}^{*}},d_{CE}\bigr)\hookrightarrow(\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*},d_{CE})

is a quasi-isomorphism. In particular, the Lie algebra
cohomology
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
is computed by the sub-complex
of
\mathrm{ad}^{*}
-invariant forms.

Proof.

Write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{ss}
,
where
\mathfrak{z}
is the center and
\mathfrak{g}_{ss}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is compact semisimple. By the
Künneth formula for Lie algebra cohomology,

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})\cong H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{z})\otimes H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{ss}).

Since
\mathfrak{z}
is abelian,
d_{CE}=0
on

\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{z}^{*}

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{z})=\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{z}^{*}
. Since
\mathfrak{z}
acts trivially
under
\mathrm{ad}^{*}
, we have

(\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathrm{ad}^{*}}\cong\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{z}^{*}\otimes(\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}_{ss}^{*})^{\mathrm{ad}^{*}(\mathfrak{g}_{ss})}.

It therefore suffices to show that the inclusion

(\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}_{ss}^{*})^{\mathrm{ad}^{*}}\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}_{ss}^{*}
is a quasi-isomorphism, which follows from Bourbaki
[
3
, Ch. IX, §6, no. 5, Th. 2 and Cor. 1]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
(M,\mathcal{F})
is a Lie-
\mathfrak{g}
foliation. Then the following statements hold true.

a)

d\Phi(\gamma)=\Phi(d\gamma)
;

b)

For every
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and every
\gamma\in\Lambda^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, one has

\mathcal{L}_{\rho(\xi)}\Phi(\gamma)=-\Phi\bigl(\mathrm{ad}^{*}(\xi)\gamma\bigr).

In particular, if
\gamma\in\left(\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)^{\mathrm{ad}^{*}}
, then

\Phi(\gamma)\in\Omega^{\bullet}(M,\mathcal{F})^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

a)

It suffices to show that for every
\gamma\in\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,

d\Phi(\gamma)=\Phi(d_{CE}\gamma).

Both sides are
(k+1)
-forms on

\bar{X}_{i}=\rho(e_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq q
, corresponding to a basis

\{e_{1},\dots,e_{q}\}
of

, form a basis of the transverse tangent
space at every point, it suffices to verify the identity on ordered tuples

(\bar{X}_{i_{0}},\dots,\bar{X}_{i_{k}})
of such vector fields. For these vectors,

\alpha(\bar{X}_{i})=e_{i}
is constant, and

\alpha([\bar{X}_{i},\bar{X}_{j}])=[e_{i},e_{j}].

Therefore

\displaystyle d(\gamma\circ\alpha)(\bar{X}_{i_{0}},\dots,\bar{X}_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s<t\leq k}(-1)^{s+t}(\gamma\circ\alpha)([\bar{X}_{i_{s}},\bar{X}_{i_{t}}],\bar{X}_{i_{0}},\dots,\widehat{\bar{X}_{i_{s}}},\dots,\widehat{\bar{X}_{i_{t}}},\dots,\bar{X}_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=(d_{CE}\gamma)(e_{i_{0}},\dots,e_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=((d_{CE}\gamma)\circ\alpha)(\bar{X}_{i_{0}},\dots,\bar{X}_{i_{k}}).

This proves part (a).

b)

Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
, and let

be the corresponding transverse vector field. We claim that

\mathcal{L}_{\rho(\xi)}\Phi(\gamma)=-\Phi\bigl(\operatorname{ad}^{*}(\xi)\gamma\bigr)

) and Cartan’s formula

\mathcal{L}_{X}\alpha=-\operatorname{ad}(\xi)\circ\alpha

Since
\Phi
is defined multiplicatively from
\alpha

\mathcal{L}_{X}\Phi(\gamma)=-\Phi\bigl(\operatorname{ad}^{*}(\xi)\gamma\bigr)

In particular, if
\gamma\in\left(\Lambda^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\right)^{\mathrm{ad}^{*}}

for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Therefore
\Phi(\gamma)\in\Omega^{k}(M,\mathcal{F})^{\mathfrak{g}}
. This proves part (b).

∎

Historically, the conclusion of Theorem

well known in the case where a complete Lie foliation

on a manifold

. By

where

A second case, perhaps less well known and apparently not recorded in the

is semisimple of compact type. Then

integrates to a compact simply connected Lie group

, and

is a compact and connected subgroup of

The main novelty of Theorem

lies in the general compact
type case, where

may possess a nontrivial center.

Example 5.4
.

We show that the compact-type hypothesis in

1.3
is essential, even for the point foliation.

Let

and let
\Gamma\subset L
be a torsion-free cocompact lattice. Set

and let
\mathcal{F}
be the point foliation on

. Then
(M,\mathcal{F})
is a Lie

-foliation, where

Indeed, the right Maurer–Cartan form on

is the point

The leaf closure space of

Since

6.
The de Rham cohomology of
G/H

Throughout this section, we use
L
and
R
to denote left and right group
actions, respectively. We write
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
for the Lie
algebras of a Lie group
G
and its Lie subgroup

, respectively, and we let

\mathcal{F}
denote the foliation on
G
whose leaves are the orbits of the
right action of
H
on
G
. The main results of this section are
Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
. A theorem of Héctor, Macías-Virgós,
and Sanmartín-Carbón
[

, Theorem 3.5]
identifies the basic cohomology of a foliation
with the diffeological de Rham cohomology of its leaf space. In the present
setting, the leaf space is precisely the diffeological quotient

. Thus the
problem is reduced to one in basic cohomology. The key new ingredient is the
transverse averaging mechanism developed earlier. We begin with a technical lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
H
be a connected Lie subgroup of a Lie group

. Then restriction to
the identity induces a canonical isomorphism of cochain complexes

\Omega^{\bullet}(G,\mathcal{F})^{L(G)}\cong C^{\bullet}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}).

Proof.

Restriction to the identity induces the standard isomorphism of cochain complexes

\Omega^{\bullet}(G)^{L(G)}\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\qquad\omega\mapsto\omega_{e},

under which the de Rham differential corresponds to the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential.

Since the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are the right
H

T_{g}\mathcal{F}=(dL_{g})_{e}(\mathfrak{h}).

Equivalently, for each

\widetilde{X}(g):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}g\exp(tX)=(dL_{g})_{e}X.

Thus
\mathcal{F}
is generated by the vector fields

.
Hence a left-invariant form

\iota_{\widetilde{X}}\omega=0,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{X}}\omega=0\quad\text{for all }X\in\mathfrak{h}.

Therefore

Suppose that

Proof.

Let

We are now ready to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

Note that the leaf space of the foliation

By Lemma

Therefore Theorem

Combining these two identifications yields

∎

The following two examples serve complementary purposes. The first is a
counterexample showing that the compactness hypothesis in
Theorem

is sharp: without this condition, the conclusion may
fail even in simple cases. The second is a concrete positive example
showing that the theorem applies to connected Lie subgroups that are
neither closed nor dense, even when

is
nonabelian and

is noncompact. Throughout, we write

for the diffeological de Rham cohomology
of

.

Example 6.4
.

We present an example showing that compactness of the
quotient
G/\overline{H}
is essential. Let

G=\mathbb{R}\times SU(2)
and
H=SU(2)
. Then
G
is connected,

H
is a connected closed normal Lie subgroup, and

\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)
,

\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{su}(2)
, so

\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathbb{R}
, which is of compact type.
However,
\overline{H}=H
and
G/H\cong\mathbb{R}
, so

G/\overline{H}
is noncompact.

Since
G/H\cong\mathbb{R}
, we have

H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(G/H)=0
, whereas

H^{1}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})\cong\mathbb{R}
. Thus

H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{dR}}(G/H)\not\cong H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
.

Example 6.5
.

We give a simple and representative example showing that
Theorem
1.5
applies to connected Lie subgroups that are
neither closed nor dense, even when
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
is
nonabelian and

is noncompact.

Let

Appendix A
De Rham cohomology of a diffeological space

This appendix collects the diffeological background used in the paper; for
general background on diffeology, we refer the reader to
[
7
]
. For the
main body, only the result of Hector, Macías-Virgós, and
Sanmartín-Carbón

]
is needed, together with a minimal amount of

and Theorem

give an alternative foliation-theoretic proof of the Clark–Ziegler theorem,

Theorem
A.10
establishes an equivariant version of that result: for any
regular foliation
(M,\mathcal{F})
, there is a canonical isomorphism of cochain

is equipped with the quotient diffeology

with one based on Haefliger’s cocycle definition, which makes equivariance
transparent in the presence of a foliation-preserving action of a Lie group

identifies the diffeological de Rham
cohomology of a quotient

forms on

.
