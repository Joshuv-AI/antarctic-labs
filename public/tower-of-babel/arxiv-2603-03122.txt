Complicial simple-minded collections
Marvin Plogmann

Abstract
. We consider the problem of characterizing derived endomorphism algebras of simple objects in length categories up to quasi-isomorphism. We give such a characterization for module categories, abelian categories, exact categories, as well as, for certain differential graded analogues of them. It turns out that the property of being d d -complicial ( d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 ), in the sense of Lurie, of the involved simple-minded collections plays a central role. We also explain how this characterization can be interpreted as a coherent generation property for any minimal A ∞ A_{\infty} -model of the derived endomorphism algebra. Along the way, we propose a notion of length exact differential graded categories and explain how they relate to length abelian d d -truncated differential graded categories, generalizing results of Enomoto.

Introduction

Let
k
be a field. It is well-known that a module category or, more generally an abelian (or exact) length category
\mathcal{A}
can be recovered from the derived endomorphism algebra of the simple objects in the sense that

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{A})\simeq\mathrm{per}(\REnd_{\mathcal{A}}(S)),

where
S
is the direct sum of all simple objects. Moreover, one can describe the image of
\mathcal{A}
under the above functor as the twisted modules over
\REnd_{\mathcal{A}}(S)
. This motivates the question of characterizing differential graded (=dg) endomorphism algebras of simple objects in such length categories.

The study of such correspondences is known as Koszul duality. Koszul duality originated in the study of Koszul algebras, which appear in representation theory of algebras, Lie theory, algebraic geometry, and other areas of mathematics, see for example
[
BGS88
,
BGS96
]
. By considering derived endomorphism algebras (instead of only their cohomology) Koszul duality has been generalized to dg and
A_{\infty}
-algebras, see
[
Kel94
,
Lef03
,
LPWZ08
,
Avr13
,
Van15
,
Fus24
]
. Koszul duality is also central in the one-to-one correspondence between silting collections and simple-minded collections, see
[
KY14
,
SY19
,
Zha23
,
Fus25
,
Bon26
]
.

For a locally finite (cohomologically) connective dg algebra

is finite-dimensional for all

and

Dually, for a locally finite coconnective dg algebra

1.1.
Context and relation to other work

Recently, there has been growing interest in extended module categories of finite-dimensional algebras or, more generally, extended hearts of
t
-structures in (algebraic) triangulated categories.

In the case of a finite-dimensional algebra
\Lambda
, the tilting theory of the extended module category
\mathcal{H}_{{\Lambda}}^{{d}}
was studied in
[
Gup24
,
Zho24
,
GZ25
]
. In
[
Gup24
, Thm. 4.1]
, a bijection between
(d+1)
-term silting complexes, certain torsion pairs in
\mathcal{H}_{{\Lambda}}^{{d}}
and certain cotorsion pairs in
\mathrm{K}^{[-d,0]}(\mathrm{proj}(\Lambda))
is proven. In
[
Zho24
, Thm. 3.12]
it is proven that
\mathcal{H}_{{\Lambda}}^{{d}}
has almost-split conflations and the former bijections are extended to include basic
\tau
-tilting pairs in
\mathcal{H}_{{\Lambda}}^{{d}}
in
[
Zho24
, Thm. 4.7]
. Finally, in
[
GZ25
, Thm. 7.3, Thm 7.11]
, the bijections have been further extended to include
(d+1)
-term simple-minded collections in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\Mod\Lambda)
as well as left and right-finite semibricks and wide subcategories in
\mathcal{H}_{{\Lambda}}^{{d}}
. In particular,
Theorem

6.7
can be seen as a generalization of the bijections between semibricks and wide subcategories in
\mathcal{H}_{{\Lambda}}^{{d}}
, which corresponds to the case
n=1
.

The extended hearts of proper connective dg algebras
A
were first studied in
[
Moc25
]
, where it is proven that
\mathcal{H}_{{A}}^{{d}}
is an abelian
d
-truncated dg category (see
[
Moc25
, Thm. 3.44]
). In
Theorem

7.13
we prove that any locally-finite,
\Ext
-finite, length abelian
d
-truncated dg category
\mathcal{A}
with finitely many simples and enough projectives is of this form. In
[
MP25
]
it was proven that
\mathcal{H}_{{A}}^{{d}}
has almost split conflations and satisfies the first Brauer–Thrall Conjecture. Finally, in
[
Moc26
]
a higher-dimensional version of the Auslander correspondence for proper connective dg algebras is proven. All these results indicate that
\mathcal{H}_{{A}}^{{d}}
seems to behave very similarly to the module category of a finite-dimensional algebra.

In
[
Moc25
, Thm. 3.44]
it is shown that extended hearts of
t
-structures on pretriangulated dg categories are
d
-truncated dg categories. In
Theorem

5.4
we show that, analogous to the case
d=1
, all abelian
d
-truncated dg categories are of this form. We also remark that, in
[
WZ25
]
, part of the tilting theory for
\mathcal{H}_{{\Lambda}}^{{d}}
is generalized to extended hearts of silting
t
-structures on (not necessarily algebraic) triangulated categories.

This article continues this line of research but focuses on the foundational aspects of the relationship between length exact
d
-categories, length abelian
d
-truncated dg categories and extended hearts of proper connective dg algebras as well as their relationship to Koszul duality.

. Unless mentioned otherwise, all categories are assumed to be
k

[

,
Kel06
]

\mathcal{A}
we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{dg}(\mathcal{A})
its
derived dg category
and by

{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}\coloneqq H^{0}(\mathcal{D}_{dg}(\mathcal{A}))

its
derived category
. For an object

we denote by

{{{A}}^{\wedge}}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}(-,A)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})

the corresponding representable dg
\mathcal{A}
-module. We write

{\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A})}\coloneqq\thick(\{{{A}}^{\wedge}\colon A\in\mathcal{A}\})\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})

for the full subcategory of
perfect dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules
and

{\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(\mathcal{A})}\coloneqq\left\{M\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}):\dim_{k}H^{*}(M)<\infty\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})

for the
perfectly valued derived category of
\mathcal{A}
. We denote by
{\mathrm{per}_{dg}(\mathcal{A})}
and
{\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}_{dg}(\mathcal{A})}
the canonical dg enhancements of
\mathrm{per}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(\mathcal{A})
, respectively. For a dg category
\mathcal{A}
, we denote by
{\tau^{\leq 0}\mathcal{A}}
its

, that is the dg category with
\mathrm{ob}(\tau^{\leq 0}\mathcal{A})\coloneqq\mathrm{ob}(\mathcal{A})
and, for

the dg vector space of morphisms

where

is in degree

. We call
\mathcal{A}

connective

is concentrated in non-positive degrees and

is concentrated in non-negative degrees. Observe that

and all

proper
.

2.2.
t
-Structures, simple-minded collections and silting collections

We fix a Krull–Schmidt triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
. In this section, we recall several different descriptions of the bounded
t
-structures associated to simple-minded and silting collections in
\mathcal{D}
. We first introduce some notation.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be subcategories of a category
\mathcal{C}
. We write
\mathcal{A}\perp\mathcal{B}
if
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)=0
for all
a\in\mathcal{A},b\in\mathcal{B}
.

For a collection of objects
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{D}
, we write

{\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{>0}}}=\{D\in\mathcal{D}:\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(C,D[m])\;\forall m>0,C\in\mathcal{C}\}

and define
\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{<0}}
,
\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\geq 0}}
,
\mathcal{C}^{\perp_{\leq 0}}
analogously.

The following notion was introduced in
[
BBDG18
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
t
-structure
on a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is a pair
t=\left(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}\right)
called
aisle
and
coaisle
of (strict) full subcategories such that

•

\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-1]\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
,

•

\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\perp(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-1])
,

•

for all
X\in\mathcal{D}
there exists a triangle (called
t
-decomposition
of
X
)

\mathrm{t}^{<0}X\to X\to\mathrm{t}^{\geq 0}X\to\mathrm{t}^{<0}X[1]

with
\mathrm{t}^{<0}X\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[1]
and
\mathrm{t}^{\geq 0}X\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
.

The full subcategory

\heartsuit\coloneqq\heartsuit_{t}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}

is called the
heart
of
t
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we write

\mathcal{D}^{\leq n}:=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[-n],\quad\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}:=\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-n],\quad\mathcal{D}^{<n}:=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[-(n-1)],\quad\mathcal{D}^{>n}:=\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-(n+1)].

We call
t

bounded
if

\mathcal{D}=(\cup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}^{\leq n})\cap(\cup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}).

Recall that
\heartsuit_{t}
is an abelian category. Moreover,
\mathrm{t}^{<0}
and
\mathrm{t}^{\geq 0}
or more generally

{\mathrm{t}^{\leq n}}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\leq n},\quad{\mathrm{t}^{\geq n}}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}

can be defined as the adjoints
\iota^{\leq n}\dashv\mathrm{t}^{\leq n}
and
\mathrm{t}^{\geq n}\dashv\iota^{\geq n}
to the inclusions

\iota^{\leq n}:\mathcal{D}^{\leq n}\to\mathcal{D}\text{ and }\iota^{\geq n}:\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}\to\mathcal{D}\text{ respectively.}

In this paper we are interested in bounded
t
-structures arising from simple-minded and silting collections. We recall the necessary definitions. The following definition goes back to
[
Ric02
]
, see also
[
KY14
, Def. 3.2]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
simple-minded collection
is a collection of objects
\mathbb{L}
in
\mathcal{D}
such that

•

no object of
\mathbb{L}
is isomorphic to zero,

•

any non-zero morphism between two objects of
\mathbb{L}
is an isomorphism,

•

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(L,L^{\prime}[n])=0
for all
L,L^{\prime}\in\mathbb{L}
,
n<0
and

•

\mathcal{D}=\thick(\mathbb{L})
.

In
[
Sch20
, Thm. 4.4]
it is shown that every simple-minded collection
\mathbb{L}
in
\mathcal{D}
gives rise to a bounded
t
-structure
t_{\mathbb{L}}=(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
, where
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
(resp.
D^{\geq 0}
) denotes the full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
consisting of objects that have a
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
-
\mathbb{L}
-filtration
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
d_{1}\geq\dots\geq d_{m}\geq 0
(resp.
0\geq d_{1}\geq\dots\geq d_{m}
), that is there exist
L_{1},\ldots,L_{m}\in\mathbb{L}
and a sequence

0=M_{0}\overset{f_{0}}{\longrightarrow}M_{1}\overset{f_{1}}{\longrightarrow}\cdots\overset{f_{m-2}}{\longrightarrow}M_{m-1}\overset{f_{m-1}}{\longrightarrow}M_{m}=M

in
\mathcal{D}
such that there exist triangles

M_{i-1}\overset{f_{i-1}}{\longrightarrow}M_{i}\overset{g_{i}}{\longrightarrow}L_{i}[d_{i}]\longrightarrow M_{i-1}[1]\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq m.

The heart
\heartsuit_{\mathbb{L}}
of this
t
-structure is a
length abelian category
, that is every object admits a finite composition series and the simple objects of
\heartsuit_{\mathbb{L}}
are precisely the objects of
\mathbb{L}
. Moreover, every bounded
t
-structure on
\mathcal{D}
with length heart arises in this way, by taking
\mathbb{L}
to be the collection of simple objects in the heart (see for example
[
Sch20
, Thm. 4.6]
).

Definition 2.3

(
[
PV18
, Def. 4.1]
)
.

A
silting collection
is a collection of objects
\mathbb{P}
in
\mathcal{D}
such that

•

\mathbb{P}=\add(\mathbb{P})
, where
\add(\mathbb{P})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
is the smallest subcategory closed under direct sums and direct summands,

•

t_{\mathbb{P}}
, defined by
(\mathbb{P}^{\perp_{>0}},\mathbb{P}^{\perp_{<0}})
is a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{D}
, called the
silting
t
-structure
associated to
\mathbb{P}
,

•

and
\mathbb{P}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{\perp_{>0}}
.

An object
P\in\mathcal{D}
is called a
silting object
if
\mathbb{P}\coloneqq\add(P)
is a silting collection and
\mathcal{D}=\thick(\mathbb{P})
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
A
be a proper connective dg algebra. The
standard
t
-structure
t_{\mathrm{st}}
on
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)
is defined by

\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\leq 0}\coloneqq\left\{M:H^{i}(M)=0\;\;\forall i\geq 0\right\}\text{ and }\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\geq 0}\coloneqq\left\{M:H^{i}(M)=0\;\;\forall i\leq 0\right\}.

Then
(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}(A)^{\geq 0})
is a silting
t
-structure for the silting collection
\add(A)
. Moreover,
A
is a silting object in
\mathrm{per}(A)
.

2.3.
A_{\infty}
-algebras

In this section we recall some basic definitions and facts on
A_{\infty}
-algebras and
A_{\infty}
-categories. We refer the reader to
[
Kel01
,
Kel02
,
Lef03
]
for details. In the following, we avoid the use of explicit signs appearing in the theory of
A_{\infty}
-categories. Although very important for the rigorous foundation of the theory, they are immaterial for our purposes. We remark, however, that there are several different sign conventions for the
A_{\infty}
-relations below. For an overview of the different conventions and their relations among each other we refer the reader to
[
Pol24
]
. The following definition is originally due to Stasheff
[
Sta63
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

An
A_{\infty}
-category

\mathcal{A}
over
k
is the datum of

•

a class of objects
\mathrm{obj}(\mathcal{A})
,

•

for all
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
, a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
{\mathcal{A}}(A,B)
,

•

for all
n\geq 1
and all
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
a graded
k
-linear map

m_{n}:{\mathcal{A}}(a_{n-1},a_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{A}}(a_{n-2},a_{n-1})\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathcal{A}}(a_{0},a_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}(a_{0},a_{n})

of degree
2-n

such that for all
n\geq 1
and all
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have the identity

\sum_{r+s+t=n}\pm m_{r+1+t}(\id^{\otimes r}\otimes m_{s}\otimes\id^{\otimes t})=0,

of maps

{\mathcal{A}}(a_{n-1},a_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{A}}(a_{n-2},a_{n-1})\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathcal{A}}(a_{0},a_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}(a_{0},a_{n}).

An
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}
is called
minimal
if
m_{1}=0
. An
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}
is
strictly unital
if for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
there exists an element
1_{a}
of degree
0
such that for all
f,g
it holds

m_{1}(1_{a})=0,\quad m_{2}(1_{a},f)=f,\quad m_{2}(g,1_{a})=g

as well as for
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
, the product
m_{n}(f_{n},\ldots,f_{1})
vanishes if one of the
f_{i}
equals
1_{a}
.

Remark 2.6
.

Observe that there are different notions of unitality of
A_{\infty}
-categories in the literature. However, as discussed in
[
Sei08
, Ch. I.2]
, it turns out that they are equivalent in a suitable sense.

Remark 2.7
.

We identify a
dg category
with an
A_{\infty}
-category with
m_{n}=0
for
n\geq 3
. An
A_{\infty}
-algebra
is given by the datum of
A\coloneqq{\mathcal{A}}(\star,\star)
, where
\mathcal{A}
is an
A_{\infty}
-category with one object
\star
. Explicitly, an
A_{\infty}
-algebra over
k
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space

endowed with a family of graded
k
-linear maps

m_{n}:A^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow A,\quad n\geq 1

of degree
2-n
, such that the following relations hold: For
n\geq 1
,

\sum_{r+s+t=n}\pm m_{r+1+t}(\id_{A}^{\otimes r}\otimes m_{s}\otimes\id_{A}^{\otimes t})=0,

where
r,t\geq 0,s\geq 1
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
be a pair of
A_{\infty}
-categories. An
A_{\infty}
-functor

\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is the datum of

•

a map
\mathcal{F}_{0}:\mathrm{obj}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathrm{obj}(\mathcal{B})
,

•

for all
n\geq 1
and all
a_{0},\ldots a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
a graded
k
-linear map

\mathcal{F}_{n}:\mathcal{A}(a_{n-1},a_{n})\otimes\mathcal{A}(a_{n-2},a_{n-1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{A}(a_{0},a_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(a_{0}),\mathcal{F}_{0}(a_{n}))

of degree
1-n

such that for all
n\geq 1
and all
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have the identity

\sum_{r+s+t=n}\pm\mathcal{F}_{r+1+t}(\id^{\otimes r}\otimes m^{\mathcal{A}}_{s}\otimes\id^{\otimes t})=\sum_{u=1}^{n}\sum_{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{r}=n}\pm m^{\mathcal{B}}_{u}(\mathcal{F}_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{F}_{i_{r}})

of maps

\mathcal{A}(a_{n-1},a_{n})\otimes\mathcal{A}(a_{n-2},a_{n-1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{A}(a_{0},a_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(a_{0}),\mathcal{F}_{0}(a_{n})).

The
A_{\infty}
-functor
\mathcal{F}
is a

if for all

the chain map

\mathcal{F}_{1}\colon\mathcal{A}(a_{0},a_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(a_{0}),\mathcal{F}_{0}(a_{1}))

is a quasi-isomorphism. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
strict
if
\mathcal{F}_{n}=0
for all

. By specializing to the case where

and

have a single object, we obtain the notion of a
(strict) morphism of
A_{\infty}
-algebras
and of

A_{\infty}
-algebras
.

A
minimal model
of an
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}
is given by the datum of the structure of a minimal
A_{\infty}
-category
(H^{*}(\mathcal{A}),m_{2},m_{3},\ldots)
on the cohomology

of
\mathcal{A}
together with a quasi-equivalence of
A_{\infty}
-categories

\mathcal{F}:(H^{*}(\mathcal{A}),m_{2},m_{3},\ldots)\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}\quad\text{such that}\quad[\mathcal{F}_{1}(f)]=f\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathcal{A},f\in H^{*}(\mathcal{A})(a,b).

Theorem 2.9

(Homotopy Transfer Theorem
[
Kad82
]
)
.

Every dg category admits a minimal model. Moreover, this model is unique up to (non unique)
A_{\infty}
-isomorphism.

.

Every minimal

Definition 2.25

(
[
GNP21
, Def. 4.1., Def. 4.5]
,
[
GNP23
, Sec. 7.1]
)
.

A
contravariant connected sequence of functors
is a pair
(F,\epsilon)
where

is a sequence of right
\mathfrak{C}
-modules and a collection of morphisms
\epsilon=\epsilon^{n}_{\delta}:F^{n}(A)\to F^{n+1}(C)
for any
\mathbb{E}
-extension
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
and
n\geq 0
, which is natural with respect to morphisms of
\mathbb{E}
-extensions. Such a pair is a
right
\delta
-functor
if for any
\mathbb{E}
-extension
\delta
, with
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[A\overset{x}{\to}B\overset{y}{\to}C]

\cdots\overset{\epsilon^{n-1}_{\delta}}{\longrightarrow}F^{n}(C)\overset{y_{*}}{\longrightarrow}F^{n}(B)\overset{x_{*}}{\longrightarrow}F^{n}(A)\overset{\epsilon^{n}_{\delta}}{\longrightarrow}F^{n+1}(C)\longrightarrow\cdots,

is exact. A
morphism of right
\delta
-functors

(F,\epsilon)\to(\tilde{F},\tilde{\epsilon})
is a family of morphisms
\theta^{n}:F^{n}\to\tilde{F}^{n}
in
\Mod_{\mathfrak{C}}
which is compatible with the connecting homomorphism, i.e. for each
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
and

, the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{n}(A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{n+1}(C)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tilde{F}^{n}(A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tilde{F}^{n+1}(C)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon^{n}_{\delta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta^{n}(A)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta^{n+1}(C)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\epsilon}^{n}_{\delta}}

Composition of morphisms of right
\delta
-functors is defined in the obvious way.
Covariant connected sequences of functors

(F,\eta)
and
left
\delta
-functors
are defined dually.

A

\mathfrak{C}
-
\mathfrak{C}
-bimodules and

and

\mathbb{E}
-extensions. It is called a
\delta
-functor
, if for any
\mathbb{E}
-extension
\delta
, with

\cdots\overset{\epsilon^{n-1}_{\delta}}{\longrightarrow}F^{n}(C,-)\overset{F^{n}(y,-)}{\longrightarrow}F^{n}(B,-)\overset{F^{n}(x,-)}{\longrightarrow}F^{n}(A,-)\overset{\epsilon^{n}_{\delta}}{\longrightarrow}F^{n+1}(C,-)\to\cdots,

\cdots\overset{\eta^{n-1}_{\delta}}{\longrightarrow}F^{n}(-,A)\overset{F^{n}(-,x)}{\longrightarrow}F^{n}(-,B)\overset{F^{n}(-,y)}{\longrightarrow}F^{n}(-,C)\overset{\eta^{n}_{\delta}}{\longrightarrow}F^{n+1}(-,A)\to\cdots,

are exact in
\Mod_{\mathfrak{C}}
and
{}_{\mathfrak{C}}\Mod
, respectively. A
morphism of
\delta
-functors

(F,\epsilon)\to(\tilde{F},\tilde{\epsilon})
is a family of
\mathfrak{C}
-
\mathfrak{C}
-bimodule morphisms

\delta

Example 2.26

(
[

)

Example 2.27

(
[
GNP21
, Def. 3.1.]
)
.

Define
{\mathbb{E}^{0}}=\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}}

{\mathbb{E}^{1}}:=\mathbb{E}

3.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pretriangulated dg category and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{C}

and an exact dg functor

We call this functor the

3.1.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}

•

-truncated with respect to the induced exact structure. For an wide

We obtain the following generalization of

, Thm. 2.5]
. A variant of the proof we present here was explained in the case

by Bodzenta at the Hausdorff Trimester Program: Symplectic Geometry and Representation Theory at the Hausdorff Center for Mathematics in Bonn in 2017; we were, however, not able to find it in the literature.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a length exact
d
-category and

. Then the assignments
\mathbb{L}\mapsto\Filt_{n}(\mathbb{L})
and
\mathcal{W}\mapsto\mathfrak{S}({\mathcal{W}}^{\mathrm{discr.}})
define mutually inverse bijections between the following two classes.

(1)

The class of
n
-semibricks in
\mathcal{E}
.

(2)

The class of length wide
n
-subcategories of
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{L}
be a
n
-semibrick in
\mathcal{E}
. We prove that
\mathcal{W}\coloneqq\Filt_{n}(\mathbb{L})
is a wide
n
-subcategory and
\mathfrak{S}({\mathcal{W}}^{\mathrm{discr.}})=\mathbb{L}
. First observe that
\mathcal{W}=\Filt_{n}(\mathbb{L})\subseteq\mathcal{E}
is clearly an extension closed exact dg subcategory of
\mathcal{E}
.

We claim that
\mathbb{L}
defines a simple-minded collection in
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{dg}(\mathcal{W})
and for the corresponding
t
-structure
t
on
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{dg}(\mathcal{W})
, the extended heart
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{dg}(\mathcal{W})^{(-n,0]}
can be identified with
\mathcal{W}
via the universal embedding. From this claim we get that
\mathcal{W}
is an abelian
n
-category by
[
Moc25
, Thm. 3.44]
, since it is an extended heart of a
t
-structure. Moreover, under this identification the simple objects in
{\mathcal{W}}^{\mathrm{discr.}}=\heartsuit_{t}
are the objects of
\mathbb{L}
by
[

, Thm. 4.4]
. Hence, it is enough to prove the above claim.

We first observe that since
\mathbb{L}
is a
n
-semibrick, for
L,L^{\prime}\in\mathbb{L}
,

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{W})}(L,L^{\prime})\cong H^{0}(\mathcal{W})(L,L^{\prime})=H^{0}(\mathcal{E})(L,L^{\prime})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }L\neq L^{\prime},\\
\text{ division algebra}&\text{ if }L=L^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Moreover, for all

At last,

and thus

Finally, we need to show that

, the simple objects in

Since

.

Assume now

is a length wide
n
-subcategory. Then
\mathcal{W}
is a length abelian
n
-truncated dg category and

Finally, since

∎

Example 6.10
.

We have seen in

that every abelian
d
-truncated dg category is
d
-filtered. This is not the case for exact
d
-truncated dg categories. One possible explanation for this defect is that exact
m
-truncated dg categories are exact
n
-truncated dg categories for every
m\geq n
, while this is never the case for an abelian
m
-truncated dg category that is not the
0
category.

Consider for example any exact category
\mathcal{E}
. We can view
\mathcal{E}
as an exact
1
-category. Then

{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{discr.}}[0,\ldots,1-1]={\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{discr.}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}

and hence
\mathcal{E}
is
1
-filtered. However,
\mathcal{E}
is an exact
d
-truncated dg category for any

, but for

since

.

7.
Length abelian
d
-truncated dg categories with enough projectives

In what follows,
\mathcal{A}
denotes a length abelian
d
-truncated dg category with finitely many simples
\mathfrak{S}\coloneqq\mathfrak{S}({\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{discr.}})
, and
H^{0}(\mathcal{A})
its homotopy category with its canonical extriangulated structure. By
6.9
, we can and we will identify
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{dg}(\mathcal{A})^{(-d,0]}\eqqcolon\mathcal{H}_{{\mathcal{A}}}^{{d}}
.

Recall from section
3
that we call an object
P\in\mathcal{A}

projective
if
P
is projective in the extriangulated category
H^{0}(\mathcal{A})
, that is
\mathbb{E}(P,-)=0
. We denote by
{\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
the subcategory of projective objects in in
\mathcal{A}
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
has enough projectives
, if the extriangulated category

has enough projectives, that is for all
X\in H^{0}(\mathcal{A})
there exists
P\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})
and a deflation

P\twoheadrightarrow X.

We say that
P\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})
is a projective generator if for all
X\in\mathcal{A}
there exists a deflation of the form
P^{n}\twoheadrightarrow X
for some

.

The goal of this chapter is to prove an analogue of the classical statement that a length abelian category with finitely many simples is equivalent to the module category of a finite-dimensional algebra if and only if it has enough projectives if and only if it has globally bounded Loewy-length (see
[
Gab62
,
Gab73
]
).

The following theorem from
[
Fus24
]
characterizes pretriangulated dg categories that are quasi-equivalent to
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}_{dg}(A)
for a connective, locally-finite dg algebra
A
. We explain how to restrict to proper connective dg algebras in order to characterize length abelian
d
-truncated dg categories with enough projectives.

Theorem 7.1

(
[
Fus24
, Thm. F]
)
.

A locally-finite pretriangulated dg category
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{fd}}_{dg}(A)
for some locally-finite connective dg algebra
A
if and only if there is a bounded
t
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
with length heart which has a projective generator.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a locally-finite pretriangulated dg category. Assume

is a finite

-complicial simple-minded collection and define

The implication

Let

.

Consider

and

. Then, since

∎

Assume now

Proof.

We first observe that once we show that we get a well-defined bijection of

and

proves that
F
is well defined. In order to prove that

is well-defined we consider
\mathcal{E}
as in

and

Remark 8.3
.

In the context of
Theorem

8.2
, it would be interesting to replace the “admissible Schur” condition by a condition that is intrinsic to the dg category
E
. One difficulty arises as follows: for any finite-dimensional algebra
\Lambda
,
\add(\Lambda)\subseteq\Mod(\Lambda)
is a (split-)exact category with simple objects the indecomposable direct summands of
\Lambda
and it holds
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\add(\Lambda))(\mathbb{L},\mathbb{L})\cong\Lambda
. This means there are no restrictions on
H^{0}(E)
(in this case no morphism is admissible). Thus, any such condition on
E
could not only refer to
H^{0}(E)
.
