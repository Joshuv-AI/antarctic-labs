Covariant field with unique mass and spin 3 2 \frac{3}{2}
Ion I. Cotăescu

Abstract
We present the explicit theory of eight-dimensional massive covariant fields with single spin 3 2 \frac{3}{2} transforming according to the representation ( 3 2 , 0 ) ⊕ ( 0 , 3 2 ) (\frac{3}{2},0)\oplus(0,\frac{3}{2}) of the group S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) SL(2,\mathbb{C}) . This is done starting with the reducible representation ( 1 , 0 ) ⊗ ( 1 2 , 0 ) (1,0)\otimes(\frac{1}{2},0) instead of the irreducible one ( 1 , 1 2 ) = ( 1 , 0 ) ⊗ ( 0 , 1 2 ) (1,\frac{1}{2})=(1,0)\otimes(0,\frac{1}{2}) we meet in Rarita-Schwinger or Joss-Weinberg frameworks. The resulting 12 12 -component covariant field transforming according to the representation [ ( 1 , 0 ) ⊗ ( 1 2 , 0 ) ] ⊕ [ ( 0 , 1 ) ⊗ ( 0 , 1 2 ) ] [(1,0)\otimes(\frac{1}{2},0)]\oplus[(0,1)\otimes(0,\frac{1}{2})] is maximally reducible, up to subspaces of irreducible representations of the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) group. Consequently, after building the theory in direct product basis of the representation ( 1 , 0 ) ⊗ ( 1 2 , 0 ) (1,0)\otimes(\frac{1}{2},0) , the sector of spin half can be separated revealing thus the genuine ( 3 2 , 0 ) ⊕ ( 0 , 3 2 ) (\frac{3}{2},0)\oplus(0,\frac{3}{2}) field. In this manner the theory of massive field of single spin 3 2 \frac{3}{2} can be developed naturally from the field equation and associated matrices, Lagrangian formalism and inner product up to closed expressions of orthonormal mode spinors. 2.1
Finite-dimensional representations of the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})

For fields with half integer spins one considers, in addition, the universal covering group of the Poincaré one,
\bar{P}^{\uparrow}_{+}={T}(4)\,\circledS\,SL(2,\mathbb{C})

\lambda(\omega)=\exp\left(-\frac{i}{2}\omega^{\alpha\beta}s_{\alpha\beta}\right)\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})\,,

(1)

\omega^{\alpha\beta}=-\omega^{\beta\alpha}

Lie algebra, re-denoted often as

s_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}s^{jk}\,,\quad k_{i}=s_{i0}\,,\quad(i,j,k...=1,2,3)\,,

(2)

when one needs to study separately the rotations

or the Lorentz boosts

We remind the reader that the operators

and

form two independent

algebras such that any irreducible representation
(j_{a},j_{b})
of the

{\cal V}_{j_{a}}\otimes{\cal V}_{j_{b}}
formed as direct products between spaces of unitary irreducible representations,
(j_{a})
and
(j_{b})
, of the
su(2)
algebras
\{a_{i}\}
and respectively
\{b_{i}\}

[
15
,
16
]
. A special role play the single spin irreducible representations,
\tau_{s}=(s,0)
and their conjugated,
\bar{\tau}_{s}=(0,s)
, whose generators

\displaystyle\tau_{s}(b_{i})=0~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~~\tau_{s}(s_{i})=I_{i}^{s}\,,~~~\tau_{s}(k_{i})=-iI_{i}^{s}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\tau_{s}(a_{i})=0~~

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle~~\bar{\tau}_{s}(s_{i})=I_{i}^{s}\,,~~~\bar{\tau}_{s}(k_{i})=iI_{i}^{s}\,,

(4)

can be expressed in terms of generators
I^{s}_{i}
of unitary irreducible representations
(s)
of the
SU(2)
group. Once we have such irreducible representations we can construct any representation
\tau=(j_{1},j_{2})
as the direct product
\tau=\tau_{j_{1}}\otimes\tau_{j_{2}}
.
Given an irreducible representation
\tau=(j_{1},j_{2})
we say that
\bar{\tau}=(j_{2},j_{1})
is its conjugated or simply adjoint representation. When
j_{1}=j_{2}
the irreducible representation
\tau=\bar{\tau}
is selfadjoint. For
j_{1}\not=j_{2}
the irreducible representations
\tau
and
\bar{\tau}
are not equivalent being related as

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}(s_{i})=\tau(s_{i})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}(r)=\tau(r)\,,~~~~\forall r\in SU(2)

(5)

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}(k_{i})=-\tau(k_{i})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}(l)=\tau(l^{-1})\,,~\forall l\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})/SU(2).

(6)

Moreover, these representations have the Hermitian properties

\displaystyle\tau(s_{i})^{+}=\tau(s_{i})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\tau(r)^{+}=\tau(r^{-1})\,,~~~~\forall r\in SU(2)

(7)

\displaystyle\tau(k_{i})^{+}=-\tau(k_{i})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\tau(l)^{+}=\tau(l)\,,~\forall l\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})/SU(2).

(8)

that can be gathered in compact form,

\tau(\lambda)^{+}=\bar{\tau}(\lambda^{-1})\,,\quad\forall\lambda\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})\,.

(9)

As the irreducible representations
\tau
and
\bar{\tau}
have the same spin content,

\mu_{2}=j_{2},j_{2}-1,...-j_{2}\end{array}\,,

(10)

or the canonical rotation basis whose vectors

|\tau,s,\sigma\rangle=\sum_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}=\sigma}|j_{1},\mu_{1}\rangle\otimes|j_{2},\mu_{2}\rangle\langle j_{1},\mu_{1};j_{2},\mu_{2}|s,\sigma\rangle\,,

(11)

are derived with the help of the Clebsh-Gordan coefficients
[
15
]
. In what follows we say simply that this is the

The vector irreducible representation
\tau_{V}=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
defines the Lorentz group
{L}_{+}^{\uparrow}
for which we use the traditional notations with capital symbols,

2.2
Covariant fields with half integer spin

In this framework the covariant fields with spin,
\psi:M\to{\cal V}_{\rho}

carrying reducible finite-dimensional representations

The space

\rho_{L}
and
\rho_{R}
such that the covariant fields can be represented as

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\psi_{L}(x)+\psi_{R}(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{L}(x)\\
\phi_{R}(x)\end{array}\right)\,,~~~\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{L}\in{\cal V}_{L}\,,\\
\phi_{R}\in{\cal V}_{R}\,.\end{array}

These fields
\psi
transform under isometries according to the covariant representation
{T}\,:\,(\lambda,a)\to{T}_{\lambda,a}\in{\rm Aut}({\cal V}_{\rho})
of the group
\tilde{P}^{\uparrow}_{+}
which has the action
[
15
]

\displaystyle({T}_{\lambda,a}\psi)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\lambda)\psi\left(\Lambda(\lambda)^{-1}(x-a)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\rho_{L}(\lambda)&0\\
0&\rho_{R}(\lambda)\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{L}(\Lambda(\lambda)^{-1}(x-a))\\
\phi_{R}(\Lambda(\lambda)^{-1}(x-a))\end{array}\right)\,.

Working with many kind of matrices it is useful to denote by
{\mathfrak{M}}(n)
the algebra of
n\times n
-matrices with complex-valued matrix elements. Taking

we have

\rho_{L},\,\rho_{R}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(n)~~\Rightarrow~~\rho=\rho_{L}\oplus\rho_{R}\,\in\,{\mathfrak{M}}(2n)\,,

(23)

avoiding redundant explanations. In this lay out any matrix is formed by chiral blocks

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{L}&M_{LR}\\
M_{RL}&M_{R}\end{array}\right)\,\in{\mathfrak{M}}(2n)\quad M_{L},M_{LR},...\in{\mathfrak{M}}(n)\,,

(24)

which may be considered separately.

The diagonal blocks of
\rho(\lambda)
have the remarkable property

\rho_{L/R}^{+}(\lambda)=\rho_{R/L}(\lambda^{-1})\,,

(25)

resulted from the rule (
9
) of adjoint irreducible representations.
Therefore, the scalar

is invariant under transformations (
2.2
). This expression can be put in compact form as
\overline{\psi}\psi
defining the adjoint
\overline{\psi}=\psi^{+}\gamma
of
\psi
with the help of the new matrix

\gamma=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&{\bf 1}\\
{\bf 1}&0\end{array}\right)\in{\mathfrak{M}}(2n)\,,\quad{\bf 1}=1_{n\times n}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(n)\,,

(26)

which play the role of metric operator. We say that the matrix

is the adjoint of the matrix
\mu
which means that a selfadjoint matrix satisfies

. As we may write

\overline{\rho}(\lambda)=\gamma\rho^{+}(\lambda)\gamma=\rho(\lambda^{-1})\,,\quad\overline{\rho}(s_{\mu\nu})=\rho(s_{\mu\nu})\,,

(27)

we say that the representations
\rho
are pseudo-unitary having selfadjoint generators.
In addition, we consider the identity matrix

of the rep
\rho

and the chiral projection operators defined as

0&{\bf 1}\end{array}\right)\,,~~P_{L/R}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1_{\rho}\mp\gamma^{5}\right)\,.

Obviously, we have

and

3.2
Lagrangian formalism and inner products

The field equations of our approach are expected to be of the first order for
s=\frac{1}{2}
and third one for
s=\frac{3}{2}
, having the general form

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\frac{1}{2}}(i\partial_{\mu})\psi_{\frac{1}{2}}-m\,\psi_{\frac{1}{2}}=0\,,~~

where
\hat{\gamma}^{\mu}
and

supposed to be selfadjoint,

and
\overline{\hat{\gamma}^{\mu\nu\sigma}}=\hat{\gamma}^{\mu\nu\sigma}

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(\lambda^{-1})\hat{\gamma}^{\mu}\hat{\rho}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\mu\,\cdot}_{\cdot\,\alpha}(\lambda)\hat{\gamma}^{\alpha}\,,

(46)

The spaces of solutions of the above equations have to be equipped with inner products that can be defined only in the context of the Lagrangian formalism.

In what follows we discuss this formalism separately for the first and third order equations omitting for simplicity the spin indices of the corresponding fields. We start with the field
\psi=\psi_{\frac{1}{2}}
whose first order equation (
43

(49)

similar to that of Dirac’s theory.

For
s=\frac{3}{2}

5
Concluding remarks

We presented the explicit theory of massive covariant fields of spin
\frac{3}{2}
tanks to our new method based on maximally reducible representations. These fields satisfy equations of third order coming from a more complicated Lagrangian formalism where we defined the new inner product (
57
) which behaves remarkably in normalizing eight-dimensional mode spinors. Another specific feature of this approach is the mass condition (
130
) which has only one real valued solution,
\bar{p}p-m^{2}=0
, such that it seems there are no dipole or multi-pole ghosts states as in theories leading to mass conditions of the form
\left(\bar{p}p-m^{2}\right)^{n}=0

[
20
,
21
]
. We may conclude thus that our approach works properly at least as a free field theory.

However, this does not guarantee a good behaviour in interaction. We have already the example of the Rarita-Swinger equation
[
22
]
which gives suprluminal states in external electromagnetic field
[
23
]
. For testing how our field interacts with an external electromagnetic field we must introduce a suitable coupling.
Observing that here the minimal coupling is inappropriate as generating non-renormable terms, we have to consider the interaction Lagrangian density
\propto j^{\mu}A_{\mu}
given by the current density (
56
) which contains first and second order derivatives. This could lead to a new phenomenology we hope to be free of inconsistencies and anomalies. Interesting couplings with tensor fields
are expected in Minkowski space-time but especially in general relativity where the
flat condition
\eta_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\sigma}=0
may select special couplings with the gravitational field.

Finally, let us discuss how the method of maximally reducible representations can be generalized to any set of fields with half integer spin, from
\frac{1}{2}
up to the maximal spin
s_{\rm m}=\frac{1}{2}+k
where
k
is an arbitrary integer. We define first the chiral representations

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1,0)^{\otimes k}\otimes(\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}},0)=(s_{\rm m},0)\oplus(s_{\rm m}-1,0)\oplus\cdots\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,1)^{\otimes k}\otimes(0,\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}})=(0,s_{\rm m})\oplus(0,s_{\rm m}-1)\oplus\cdots\,,

(176)

of dimension
n=2\times 3^{k}
, formed as Kronecker products of simple representations presented in Appendix A. In subspaces
{\cal V}_{L}
and
{\cal V}_{R}
we may introduce the common direct product basis
\{|\epsilon_{i}\rangle,i=1,2,\cdots n\}
with a convenient enumeration. In this basis the matrices
M\in{\mathfrak{M}}(n)
of chiral sectors have matrix elements
M_{ij}=\langle i|M|j\rangle
. The next step is to change this basis with the canonical basis of the representation
(s_{\rm m})\oplus\cdots(s)_{1}\oplus(s)_{2}\oplus\cdots(s)_{n_{s}}\oplus\cdots
, resulted after decomposing the chiral representations as in Eq. (
176
). Each representation
(s)
is supposed to have the multiplicity
n_{s}
which means that a supplemental index,
\iota_{s}
, is needed for distinguish among the representations with same spin
s
.
Furthermore, projection operators
{\mathfrak{P}}_{\iota_{s},s}:{\mathfrak{M}}(n)\to{\mathfrak{M}}(2s+1)
separating spin sectors can be defined such that any matrix
M\in{\mathfrak{M}}(n)
having the elements
M_{ij}
be projected to the matrix
M^{\iota_{s},s}={\mathfrak{P}}_{\iota_{s},s}(M)
with the matrix elements

M^{\iota_{s},s}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}=\sum_{i,j}\langle\iota_{s},s,\sigma|i\rangle\langle i|M|j\rangle\langle j|\iota_{s},s,\sigma^{\prime}\rangle\,.

(177)

Using these projection operators, the entire framework can be split in independent sectors governed by representations
(s,0)\oplus(0,s)
with
\frac{1}{2}\leq s\leq s_{\rm m}
. As there are no major difficulties in applying this method we hope to obtain the closed formulas of all the quantities we need for writing down the Feynman rules for any spin.

Appendx A: Building boosts

The transformations
l_{\bf p}\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})/SU(2)
are parametrized as

l_{\bf p}=e^{-i\kappa^{i}k_{i}}\,,\quad\kappa^{i}=\frac{p^{i}}{|{\bf p}|}{\rm tanh}^{-1}\frac{|{\bf p}|}{E_{p}}\,,\quad~~l^{-1}_{\bf p}=l_{-{\bf p}}\,,

(A.1)

giving rise to the Lorentz boosts
\rho_{V}(l_{\bf p})=\Lambda(l_{\bf p})
having the matrix elments,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{0\cdot}_{\cdot 0}(l_{\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{E_{p}}{m},\quad\Lambda^{0\cdot}_{\cdot i}(l_{\bf p})=\Lambda^{i\cdot}_{\cdot 0}(l_{\bf p})=\frac{p^{i}}{m},

(A.2)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{i\cdot}_{\cdot j}(l_{\bf p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}+\frac{p^{i}p^{j}}{m(E_{p}+m)},~~

(A.3)

which transforms the representative momentum
\mathring{p}=(m,0,0,0)
into the desired momentum
p=(E_{p},{\bf p})=\Lambda(l_{\bf p})\,\mathring{p}
.

The representation
\tau_{\frac{1}{2}}=(\frac{1}{2},0)
is the left-handed part of Dirac’s one such that we may write

\tau_{\frac{1}{2}}(\l_{\bf p})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m(E_{p}+m)}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{-}+m&-p_{-}\\
-p_{+}&E_{+}+m\end{array}\right)\,,

(A.4)

where we used null momentum components on mass-shell,

E_{\pm}=E_{p}\pm p^{3}\,,\quad p_{\pm}=p^{1}\pm ip^{2}\Rightarrow\,,\bar{p}p=E_{+}E_{-}-p_{+}p_{-}\,.

(A.5)

Note that off mass-shell we must consider
p^{0}_{\pm}=p^{0}\pm p^{3}
instead of
E_{\pm}
.

The boost generators of
\tau_{1}=(1,0)
,
\tau_{1}(k_{i})=-iI^{1}_{i}
depend on the generators
I^{1}_{i}
of representation
(1)
of the
SU(2)
group whose matrices in canonical basis read

I^{1}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
1&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{array}\right)\,,\quad I^{1}_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&-i&0\\
i&0&-i\\
0&i&0\end{array}\right)\,,\quad I^{1}_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\end{array}\right)\,,

(A.6)

Using these generators we can calculate

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(l_{\bf p})=e^{-\kappa^{i}I^{1}_{i}}=\frac{1}{2m(E_{p}+m)}

\displaystyle\times\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}(E_{-}+m)^{2}&-\sqrt{2}(E_{-}+m)p_{-}&(p_{-})^{2}\\
-\sqrt{2}(E_{-}+m)p_{+}&E_{+}E_{-}+mE_{p}&-\sqrt{2}(E_{+}+m)p_{-}\\
(p_{+})^{2}&-\sqrt{2}(E_{+}+m)p_{+}&(E_{+}+m)^{2}\end{array}\right),

in terms of null components defined above.

With these elements we construct the matrices we need

\hat{\rho}_{L}(l_{\bf p})=\hat{\rho}_{R}(l_{\bf p})^{-1}=\tau_{1}(l_{\bf p})\otimes\tau_{\frac{1}{2}}(\l_{\bf p})\,,

(A.11)

using the Kronecker product in indicated order. The results are presented in Eqs. (
79
) and (
80
). Finally we verify that these matrices are Hermitian,
\hat{\rho}_{L/R}^{+}=\hat{\rho}_{L/R}
, and are maximally reducible,

\hat{\rho}_{L/R}(l_{\bf p})=\hat{\rho}_{L/R}(l_{\bf p})\pi_{\frac{3}{2}}+\hat{\rho}_{L/R}(l_{\bf p})\pi_{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

(A.12)

acting separately on the subspaces of spin
\frac{3}{2}
and
\frac{1}{2}
.
