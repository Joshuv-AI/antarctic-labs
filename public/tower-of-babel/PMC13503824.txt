Research on a belt roller fault diagnosis method based on adaptive band-time KAN
Authors: Zhiyong Yang, Guangxu Luo, Guilin Hu, Jun Wang, Hongyu Zhang, Fucheng Yang, Lei Li, Wenhan Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBelt conveyor idlers frequently fail under high-load harsh conditions, causing system shutdowns. Existing deep learning-based fault diagnosis methods suffer from insufficient frequency resolution and poor dynamic adaptability. To address this, this paper proposes a fault diagnosis framework based on adaptive frequency-band KAN: First, an adaptive frequency-band Mel filter bank designed based on fault mechanisms enhances resolution in critical fault frequency bands through non-uniform frequency-axis remapping and third-order peak detection. Second, a temporal convolutional network is integrated to expand the receptive field and capture cross-period features. A Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN) layer is introduced to dynamically analyze nonlinear coupling relationships in the frequency domain using learnable B-spline basis functions. This model achieves synergistic optimization of feature resolution enhancement and dynamic modeling, significantly improving diagnostic accuracy and cross-condition generalization capability for roller faults. Under actual conveyor roller operating conditions, fault prediction accuracy reaches 81.25%, fully validating the model’s adaptability to real-world industrial scenarios.Keywords: Idler, Fault diagnosis, Deep learning, Mel spectrogramSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In heavy industrial sectors such as coal mining, mining operations, and port logistics, belt conveyors serve as the core arteries for material transportation. Their operational stability directly impacts production safety and economic efficiency. As critical load-bearing components of the conveying system, idlers endure prolonged exposure to high loads, intense impacts, and corrosive environmental conditions. This leads to frequent failures including bearing damage, roller eccentricity, and insufficient lubrication. Research indicates that idler failures are a primary cause of belt conveyor system downtime. In mining, port, and power generation sectors, such failures can halt entire production lines, resulting in substantial economic losses1. Abnormal vibration serves as the primary manifestation of idler malfunction; noticeable changes in vibration signals occur when idler bearings deteriorate or the idler becomes eccentric2. However, traditional roller fault diagnosis primarily relies on vibration signal analysis and manual inspections, employing signal processing techniques like short-time Fourier transforms for fault detection3. These methods perform poorly when handling non-stationary mechanical vibration signals. The high-noise environment in industrial settings often masks early-stage fault signals. Furthermore, they require extensive expert experience for feature selection and fault judgment, resulting in low automation levels and high false alarm rates4,5.

In recent years, deep learning technology has demonstrated tremendous potential in the field of mechanical fault diagnosis. Convolutional neural networks can automatically extract deep features from vibration signals, eliminating the cumbersome manual feature engineering required by traditional methods. Research indicates that deep learning-based fault diagnosis methods can achieve high diagnostic accuracy in bearing fault detection6,7. At the feature extraction level, the Mel-spectrogram, as a time-frequency analysis tool, exhibits unique advantages in mechanical fault diagnosis. It effectively captures the time-frequency characteristics of vibration signals and outperforms traditional MFCC features in pump equipment fault detection8. Mel-frequency mapping technology converts linear Hertz frequencies into nonlinear Mel frequencies, better adapting to the changing fault characteristics of mechanical equipment under different operating conditions9. Deep learning methods possess advantages such as automatic feature learning, end-to-end learning, and strong generalization capabilities, demonstrating good adaptability under various operating conditions10.

Despite significant advancements in deep learning technology for fault diagnosis, numerous technical bottlenecks and limitations persist in practical applications for roller fault detection. At the feature extraction level, the design of traditional Mel filter banks still suffers from insufficient resolution in critical mechanical fault frequency bands, particularly when processing narrowband fault features. This limits their ability to effectively capture the heterogeneous frequency domain patterns of diverse roller faults. At the model architecture level, while CNNs can effectively capture the spatial-temporal correlations in spectrograms, their fixed convolutional kernels struggle to adaptively adjust frequency band weights, and pooling operations lead to the loss of low-frequency fault details11. RNNs and their variants can model temporal dependencies, but their recurrent structures suffer from low training efficiency and difficulty in capturing long-term dependencies12. Furthermore, existing approaches face challenges including severe data imbalance, poor adaptability to varying operating conditions, limitations of single-feature extraction methods in comprehensively capturing fault information, and the difficulty of balancing real-time requirements with diagnostic accuracy13–16. A more fundamental limitation lies in the use of fixed activation functions across existing models. Their static nonlinear mapping capabilities fail to adapt to the complex and variable response patterns of fault features in the frequency domain, resulting in insufficient feature decoupling.

Distinct from a simple combination of existing techniques, the core contributions of this research are manifested at four synergistic levels.

1. A fault-mechanism-driven adaptive frequency band remapping method. To avoid masking mechanical fault features by fixed or uniform frequency partitioning, we design a nonlinear frequency-axis remapping function and a dynamic bandwidth adjustment mechanism based on the inherent frequency characteristics of faults such as idler bearing damage and eccentricity. This method achieves targeted resolution enhancement in critical narrowband fault frequency bands, effectively remedying the insufficient frequency resolution of traditional Mel-spectrograms in mechanical fault diagnosis.

Based on the above principles, this paper constructs the Adaptive Frequency-band Temporal KAN (AFB-TKAN) fault diagnosis framework. First, a Gaussian weighting function is constructed based on the failure mechanisms of idlers to design an adaptive frequency-band Mel filter bank. Through nonlinear frequency-axis remapping, scale stretching is achieved in key fault frequency bands, increasing local filter density, while a third-order peak detection algorithm is employed for dynamic delineation of fault frequency bands. Subsequently, the optimized Mel spectrogram is fed into the fusion architecture. The TCN is utilized to capture cross-cycle impact evolution features, and simultaneously, the KAN layer is introduced to dynamically analyze the nonlinear coupling relationships among frequency-domain features. This establishes a closed-loop optimization from feature enhancement to dynamic decoupling, ultimately leading to a significant improvement in the accuracy and generalization capability of fault diagnosis.

Adaptive mel filter

Traditional Mel filter banks are optimized based on human auditory perception, with their central logarithmic frequency distribution following the Fletcher-Munson response curve. Consequently, they exhibit severely inadequate resolution in the frequency bands characteristic of mechanical faults17. To address this issue, this paper develops an adaptive frequency-band Mel spectrogram based on roller failure mechanisms, aiming to achieve precise enhancement and analysis of the characteristic frequencies of roller damage.

Mechanism of roller failure and frequency band analysis

Different damage types in belt conveyor idlers exhibit distinct frequency domain characteristics in vibration signals, stemming from fundamental differences in their physical failure mechanisms: localized damage generates transient impacts that excite the system’s inherent vibration modes, while continuous wear causes shifts in steady-state vibration energy distribution18. Therefore, accurately capturing changes in energy distribution within specific frequency bands is essential for achieving fault diagnosis.

Optimization of mel filter bank

Calculations based on fault mechanisms indicate that the core characteristic frequency bands of roller faults are primarily distributed in the low-frequency region. Therefore, filter design should prioritize enhancing characteristic resolution in the low-frequency range. However, traditional Mel filter banks exhibit uniform distribution across Mel scales from 0 to 8 kHz, rendering them unsuitable for adapting to the narrowband frequency domain characteristics of roller faults19. To address this issue, this paper proposes an adaptive Mel filter bank based on fault feature frequency bands. By increasing filter density in critical frequency bands, it enhances the resolution of fault features while reducing redundant coverage in non-critical bands, thereby more efficiently focusing on fault-sensitive information.

Parameter settings and implementation details

To ensure the reproducibility of our method, the key implementation parameters of the adaptive Mel filter bank are detailed as follows.

Total number of filters (N): Set to 20, consistent with the number in traditional Mel filter banks to ensure fair comparison, while providing sufficient degrees of freedom for frequency band reallocation.

Peak detection parameters: In the third-order peak detection algorithm, the positions of the first three (k = 3) most significant non-rotational frequency harmonic peaks in the power spectrum are selected to define the core fault frequency band . The peak significance threshold is set to twice the global average power spectrum amplitude to filter out noise-induced pseudo-peaks.

Weighting function hyperparameter: The resolution enhancement factor γ in the Gaussian weighting function is empirically set to 2.0 to form a sufficiently steep weight distribution within the fault frequency band . The band center μ is automatically taken as .

Bandwidth compression factor (α): In the dynamic bandwidth adjustment mechanism, the compression factor α is set to 0.7. This means that within the core fault frequency band , the bandwidth of the filters is compressed to 70% of the original design bandwidth, thereby increasing the frequency resolution by approximately 1.43 times. Specifically, the selection of hyperparameters γ and α was determined through sensitivity analysis. A grid search was conducted within the ranges of γ ∈ [1.0,3.0] and α ∈ [0.5,0.9] . The results indicated that the energy concentration in the fault frequency band reached its peak when γ = 2.0 ; conversely, an excessively large γ resulted in an overly narrow frequency band, leading to the loss of edge features. For α , a value of 0.7 achieved the optimal balance between frequency resolution and noise immunity. Consequently, γ and α were fixed at 2.0 and 0.7, respectively, in this study to ensure the robustness of feature extraction.

The selection of the third-order peak detection is based on a trade-off between the physical characteristics of idler vibration signals and computational efficiency. According to impact vibration theory, the impulse response generated by an idler fault typically contains the fundamental frequency and its first two harmonics. In the frequency domain, this manifests as three clusters of concentrated energy centered around the fault characteristic frequency. While selecting a higher order could capture more harmonic details, it would introduce substantial high-frequency noise and increase computational complexity. Conversely, choosing a lower-order parameter might lead to the omission of critical fault modulation sidebands. Therefore, setting k=3 represents the optimal solution for balancing feature integrity with noise immunity.

TKAN fault diagnosis network model architecture

The fault diagnosis network architecture proposed in this paper centers on the deep integration of Time-Convolutional Networks20 and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN)21, with the network model architecture illustrated in Fig. 4. This model takes Mel-spectrograms as input. Through the temporal feature extraction module, it captures long-range temporal dependencies and local frequency-domain correlations. The nonlinear feature enhancement module amplifies low-frequency fault details, and the fault classification module ultimately outputs diagnostic results. This hierarchical, progressive feature processing workflow achieves precise capture and adaptive modeling of low-frequency narrowband features in Mel-spectrograms of roller faults.

TKAN fault diagnosis network model partial architecture.

Traditional convolutional feature extraction suffers from fixed, limited receptive fields that struggle to capture long-range dependencies, while dimensionality reduction operations result in loss of low-frequency details. To address this, the temporal feature extraction module in this model employs dilated convolutions with residual connections. This significantly expands the receptive field without increasing parameters while preserving spatio-temporal resolution, as shown in Fig. 4a. However, the extracted coarse-grained features still exhibit coupling between low-frequency fault characteristics and high-frequency noise, with complex nonlinear distributions. Traditional convolution enhancement methods struggle to adaptively adjust frequency-band weights due to fixed kernel weights. Therefore, a nonlinear feature enhancement module employs a two-layer convolution structure (as shown in Fig. 4b). This leverages the local receptive field advantage of standard convolutions to strengthen low-frequency details, suppress high-frequency noise, and enhance feature discriminability.

To address the limitation of traditional activation functions in modeling the complex nonlinear patterns of fault features, the Fault Classification Module (FCM) employs the Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem 22. It replaces fixed activation functions with learnable B-spline basisfunctions to construct a KAN layer, enabling adaptive nonlinear transformation. This ultimately outputs multi-class classification probabilities to accomplish fault type identification. The structure of its KAN layer is shown in Fig. 5.

Data description

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed AFB-TKAN-based fault diagnosis model, a roller failure experiment was conducted on the No. 1 belt conveyor at Nanlu Tianyu Coal Mine. The selected roller and bearing parameters are shown in Table 1.

Experimental parameters.

The experiment involved laying optical fibers on the roller bridge structure, with fiber terminals connected to an industrial control computer to monitor phase changes in the fibers caused by vibrations. Signal acquisition software received these phase changes, which were then converted into real-time vibration signals through phase demodulation. These signals provided the raw experimental data. The entire vibration signal acquisition process is illustrated in Fig. 6.

Vibration signal acquisition process.

Vibration signals from normal idlers were collected as baseline data, followed by sequential installation and testing of various faulty components. For each fault type, vibration signals were captured under identical belt speed conditions, with each condition recorded continuously for 150 minutes. To enhance data analyzability, the raw vibration signals were segmented into 5-second intervals. Each operating condition yielded 1,800 valid samples, which were divided into training, validation, and test sets in a 10:4:4 ratio.

Experimental results

To validate the effectiveness of this model in roller fault diagnosis tasks, 1,000 samples were randomly selected from all samples in each fault category to form the training set. Subsequently, 400 samples were randomly selected from the remaining samples to form the validation set, with the rest serving as the test set. The confusion matrix of the AFB-TKAN fault diagnosis model’s prediction results obtained from the experiment is shown in Fig. 7. where “A, B, C, D, E, F” correspond to normal, inner ring failure, outer ring failure, rolling element failure, roller eccentricity, and insufficient lubrication, respectively.

Fault diagnosis model prediction results.

The confusion matrix data indicates that the AFB-TKAN model can perform fault classification prediction in the roller fault diagnosis task, demonstrating strong classification capabilities on both the training and test sets. The average accuracy on the training set is 0.869, while on the test set it is 0.812. The model exhibits stable generalization performance with no significant overfitting observed. Regarding fault type discrimination, normal samples achieve the highest recognition accuracy (0.932 on the training set and 0.895 on the test set), indicating significant feature differences between normal and fault states. while identification accuracy for insufficient lubrication (training set: 0.825, test set: 0.758) and roller eccentricity (training set: 0.846, test set: 0.772) was relatively lower, reflecting the higher complexity of fault features in these two categories.

Although the AFB-TKAN model demonstrates impressive inference speed (50.9 ms) on the workstation, actual industrial edge deployment typically involves strict resource constraints. To evaluate the model’s applicability in resource-constrained environments, we conducted a quantitative analysis. The model contains 85.6K parameters, and after INT8 quantization, the model size can be compressed to approximately 171 KB, which fully satisfies the storage limits of most industrial gateways. However, the computation of B-spline basis functions in the KAN layer involves table lookup and interpolation operations, which have a higher computational density than standard multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations. On low-end MCUs, this may lead to increased inference latency. Therefore, while the model theoretically possesses deployment potential, for practical implementation, it is recommended to perform operator-level optimization of the KAN layer for specific edge chip architectures, or to employ knowledge distillation techniques to distill the nonlinear feature mappings into a lightweight CNN.

Conclusion

Computational efficiency and real-time feasibility analysis

Limitations in generalization under variable working conditions

Although the proposed AFB-TKAN model demonstrates excellent performance under fixed working conditions, we must honestly discuss its limitations under variable conditions. The characteristic frequency of mechanical faults is linearly positively correlated with the rotational speed, whereas the adaptive Mel filter bank designed in this paper is based on the fault characteristic frequency under a specific rotational speed. When the belt speed changes drastically, the fault characteristic frequency band shifts, which may lead to a mismatch between the preset center frequencies of the filters and the actual fault frequencies. Although the nonlinear fitting capability of the KAN layer can compensate for the feature distortion caused by this frequency shift to a certain extent, the physical limitations of the filter bank objectively exist. Future work will introduce a rotational speed adaptive mapping mechanism to dynamically adjust the stretching region of the Mel axis according to the real-time rotational frequency, thereby addressing this generalization bottleneck.

Physical essence and design trade-offs of accuracy

Regarding the accuracy of 0.812 on the test set, we need to conduct a more in-depth critical discussion from the perspective of physical mechanisms. This value is not merely a model defect but is determined by the physical nature of the fault signals. In the open-pit coal mine environment, idler fault signals are not only modulated by bearing damage but also interfered with by broadband noise such as belt tension fluctuations and material impact. The model converging at 0.812 indicates that under this signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), the model has extracted distinguishable feature boundaries, and the remaining errors mainly stem from the unpredictability of random noise. Furthermore, the confusion matrix shows a high misclassification rate for "insufficient lubrication." This is essentially because the change in vibration energy caused by the lubrication state is continuous rather than a discrete step change, which increases the ambiguity of the classification boundary.

Parameter sensitivity and robustness

Although the hyperparameters in the AFB module were optimized via grid search, we acknowledge that these parameters may exhibit sensitivity during cross-equipment applications. Ablation studies indicate that when α deviates from the optimal value of 0.7 by more than ±0.2, the model accuracy drops by over 5%. This demonstrates that the model performance is highly dependent on the design of the feature extractor. To address this issue, we introduced Dropout regularization in the KAN layer, which mitigates output fluctuations caused by minor perturbations in feature input to a certain extent and enhances the internal robustness of the model.

Conclusion

This study addresses the dual challenges of insufficient resolution in traditional Mel spectrograms and limited nonlinear expressive capabilities in deep learning models for roller fault diagnosis by proposing an Adaptive Frequency Band Time-Convolutional KAN Framework (AFB-TKAN). At the feature extraction level, it innovatively designs an adaptive frequency band Mel filter bank. Through fault mechanism-driven nonlinear frequency axis remapping and a third-order peak detection algorithm, it achieves resolution enhancement in critical frequency bands (e.g., bearing damage, roller eccentricity) while sparsifying non-critical bands, significantly improving the separability of narrowband fault features. At the model architecture level, it integrates a temporal convolutional network with a Kolmogorov-Arnold network. The temporal convolutional network employs dilated convolution to expand the receptive field, precisely capturing cross-cycle impact evolution features. The KAN utilizes learnable B-spline basis functions to replace fixed activation functions, adaptively decoupling the nonlinear coupling patterns of fault features in the frequency domain. Experimental validation demonstrates that this model achieves a diagnostic accuracy of 81.25%. Compared with mainstream fault detection models, AFB-TKAN exhibits higher overall precision in fault diagnosis and recognition, providing a novel approach for fault diagnosis tasks in conveyor belt idlers.
