Well-posedness of the heat equation in domains with topological transitions The author M.O. was supported in part by the U.S. National Science Foundation under award DMS-2408978.
Maxim A. Olshanskii, Arnold Reusken

Abstract
We analyze a linear parabolic equation with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions posed in domains whose evolution may involve topological transitions. The domains are described as sublevel sets of a smooth space–time level set function, allowing for transitions such as domain splitting and merging and the creation or vanishing of islands and holes. We introduce anisotropic space–time function spaces that extend the classical Bochner spaces used in cylindrical domains and establish key functional-analytic properties of these spaces, including the density of compactly supported smooth functions. This framework enables the application of the Babuška–Banach theorem, yielding existence, uniqueness, and a priori estimates for weak solutions. The analysis applies to domain evolutions generated by level set functions with isolated nondegenerate critical points, which correspond to the generic topology changes classified by Morse theory in two and three spatial dimensions.

Introduction

Phenomena and processes involving changes in topology are commonly observed in nature and engineering applications. Examples include drop breakage, membrane fusion, bubble coalescence, cell division, and phase transitions. Mathematical models of these and related phenomena may involve partial differential equations posed in domains undergoing topological transitions. In this paper, we address the question of well-posedness for a linear parabolic equation posed in a time-dependent domain that may change its topology in the course of evolution.

Well-posedness of parabolic equations in stationary domains is well understood
[
8
,
17
]
. There is also a substantial body of literature devoted to parabolic problems in non-cylindrical space–time domains; see, for example,
[
13
,
1
,
6
,
16
,
5
,
11
,
10
,
15
,
4
,
9
]
. Topics that are addressed include well-posedness under low regularity assumptions on the space-time domain or the (diffusion) coefficients in the equation (e.g.,
[
5
]
), particular non-standard boundary conditions (e.g.,
[
9
]
), or an analysis of nonlinear (e.g., monotone) parabolic type problems, e.g.,
[
15
]
. However, the well-posedness of PDEs posed in domains that undergo topological changes during their evolution appears to be essentially unexplored in the existing literature.

To address this apparent gap, we consider a linear parabolic PDE with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions posed in a domain whose evolution can be described by a smooth level set function depending on space and time. If one restricts to the generic case where the level set function has an isolated nondegenerated critical point (cf. Section
2
for definitions) a complete classification of the possible topological changes is known from the literature. In spatial dimension two, for example, the only scenarios that can occur are creation/vanishing of an island, domain merging and splitting or the creation/vanishing of a hole. In this paper we study all scenarios that can occur in spatial dimensions two and three. For most of these scenarios, we prove that a natural weak formulation of the problem is well posed.

The approach taken in this paper extends the standard analysis for cylindrical domains, where a weak solution is defined in the Bochner space
L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
with a time derivative in
L^{2}([0,T];H^{-1}(\Omega))
. Corresponding extensions to non-cylindrical space–time domains defined by a smooth evolution of a spatial domain
\Omega(t)
can be found in
[
13
,
4
,
3
]
. Such a smooth evolution is typically defined through the existence of a smooth (in space and time) diffeomorphism
\Phi_{t}
from a reference domain onto
\Omega(t)
. The existence of a smooth (or even continuous) family of diffeomorphisms
\Phi_{t}
excludes any change in topology.

We introduce the spaces
H_{0}
and
W
, which are analogues of the space
L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
and its subspace consisting of functions whose time derivative belongs to
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))
. These are anisotropic function spaces defined on the space–time domain.
Instead of relying on a global smooth transformation to a cylindrical space–time domain, we exploit geometric information near the topological singularity to analyze these spaces. We establish key functional-analytic properties of these spaces, including the density of compactly supported smooth functions, which is far from obvious in the presence of topological singularities. This enables us to apply the general Babuška–Banach framework to prove the existence and uniqueness of a weak solution, which satisfies the corresponding
a priori
estimate and solves the original PDE in the sense of distributions. Moreover, any distributional solution to the original problem is our weak solution.

We note that topological transitions—for example, the merging of two connected components of
\Omega(t)
—may occur in various ways, and the present analysis covers only scenarios described by smooth level set functions with isolated nondegenerated critical points; see Section
2
. All these scenarios can be classified and our analysis covers all these, except that of the creation or vanishing of a hole in a 2D domain and a void inside a 3D domain.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section

3

and

are also studied there. Section
4

Domain evolution

For
t\in[t_{0},T]
, consider a time-dependent bounded domain
\Omega(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d=2,3

\Gamma(t)=\partial\Omega(t)
.

We assume that there exists a
critical time

t_{c}\in[t_{0},T]
such that the evolution of
\Omega(t)
is smooth before and after
t_{c}
in the following sense:
Let
\mathcal{I}_{-}:=(0,t_{c})
,
\mathcal{I}_{+}:=(t_{c},T)
, we assume that
there exist smooth bounded domains
\Omega_{\pm}^{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, (each consisting of a finite number of connected components), and diffeomorphisms

\Phi_{\pm}(t):\Omega_{\pm}^{0}\to\Omega(t),\quad t\in\mathcal{I}_{\pm},~~\text{such that}~~\Phi_{\pm}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega_{\pm}^{0}}\times\mathcal{I}_{\pm};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1)

Since continuous transformations preserve the topology,
t_{c}
is the moment where topology of
\Omega(t)
may change. There can be various scenarios of topological transitions which fall into this general description and analysis may heavily depend on further assumptions on the evolution of
\Omega(t)
in the vicinity of the critical time.
In this study we restrict to a class of domains characterized as the subzero levels of a globally smooth level set function
\phi
:

\forall~t\in[t_{0},T]:~~\Omega(t)=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}~|~\phi(x,t)<0\,\},\quad\Gamma(t)=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}~|~\phi(x,t)=0\,\},

(2)

and

\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[t_{0},T]).

(3)

The analysis below can be extended to less smooth
\phi
. Here, however, we do not focus on minimal regularity assumptions for the level set function
\phi
.

For the space–time domain and its ‘spatial’ boundary we use the notations:

\mathcal{Q}=\bigcup\limits_{t\in(t_{0},T)}\Omega(t)\times\{t\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\quad\Gamma_{\!\mathcal{Q}}:=\bigcup\limits_{t\in(t_{0},T)}\Gamma(t)\times\{t\}.

(4)

If
|\nabla\phi(x,t)|\geq c_{0}>0
for all
x\in\Gamma(t)
and
t\in[t_{0},T]
, then, by the implicit function theorem,
\Gamma(t)
does not undergo topological changes.
To allow for topological changes, we assume an isolated
critical point

(x_{c},t_{c})\in\Gamma_{\!\mathcal{Q}}
, i.e.,

\nabla\phi(x_{c},t_{c})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla\phi(x,t)\neq 0\quad\text{for all }~(x,t)\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}\setminus(x_{c},t_{c}).

(5)

Remark 2.1
.

For
\Omega(t)
given by (
2
), (
3
), and (
5

\Phi_{\pm}(y,t)=x(t)

x_{t}=V(x,t),\quad t\in\mathcal{I}_{\pm},\quad x(0)=y\in\Omega_{\pm}^{0},\quad\text{and}~~V=\frac{\mp\phi_{t}\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\phi^{2}}.

(6)

Here we take
\Omega_{-}^{0}=\Omega(t_{0})
,
\Omega_{+}^{0}=\Omega(T)
.
Note that for
x\in\Gamma(t)
,
(x,t)\neq(x_{c},t_{c})
, the normal velocity of the domain boundary is

V_{\Gamma}=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\frac{1}{|\nabla\phi|}\mathbf{n},

(7)

where
\mathbf{n}=\frac{\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|}
is the outward pointing normal vector. Therefore, at an isolated critical point the normal velocity is unbounded if
\phi_{t}(0,0)\neq 0

The critical point is called

if

\det\big(\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{c},t_{c})\big)\neq 0.

(8)

In this case, certain topological transitions can be classified via Morse theory

Lemma 1
.

Consider a nondegenerate critical point
(x_{c},t_{c})
on the zero level of
\phi
. Without loss of generality, assume
(x_{c},t_{c})=(0,0)
. Then there exists a neighborhood
\widehat{X}=X\times(-\delta,\delta)
of
(0,0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and a map
\psi:\,\widehat{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that:
\psi(0,0)=0
,
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{X})
,
\psi(\cdot,t)
is a diffeomorphism from
X
onto
\psi(X,t)
for each
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
. Furthermore, the following normal form holds:

\phi\big(\psi(x,t),t\big)=-\sum_{i=1}^{q}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=q+1}^{d}x_{i}^{2}+v(t),

(9)

with
0\leq q\leq d
and
v:(-\delta,\delta)\to\mathbb{R}
a smooth map such that
v(0)=0
,
v^{\prime}(0)=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(0,0)
.

If
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(0,0)\neq 0
, then due to the inverse function theorem
there exists
0<\hat{\delta}\leq\delta
sufficiently small such that for
t\in(-\hat{\delta},\hat{\delta})
there is a smooth inverse map
v^{-1}
, i.e.,
v^{-1}(v(t))=t
for
t\in(-\hat{\delta},\hat{\delta})
. Using the smooth diffeomorphism

\Psi(x,t)=(\psi(x,v^{-1}(t)),v^{-1}(t))\in C^{\infty}(\widehat{X}\times(-\hat{\delta},\hat{\delta})),

(10)

we obtain new coordinates in which the level set function takes the form

\phi\circ\Psi=-\sum_{i=1}^{q}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=q+1}^{d}x_{i}^{2}+\text{s}\,t,\qquad\text{s}=\text{sign}\big(\tfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(0,0)\big).

(11)

2.1
A classification result

Further we are interested in critical points satisfying

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(x_{c},t_{c})\neq 0.

(12)

Using
\psi(x_{c},t_{c})=x_{c}
and
\nabla\phi(x_{c},t_{c})=0
we compute

\begin{split}\nabla^{2}\phi\big(\psi(x_{c},t_{c}),t_{c}\big)&=[\nabla\psi(x_{c},t_{c})]^{T}\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{c},t_{c})\nabla\psi(x_{c},t_{c})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}(x_{c},t_{c})\nabla^{2}\psi_{i}(x_{c},t_{c})\\
&=[\nabla\psi(x_{c},t_{c})]^{T}\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{c},t_{c})\nabla\psi(x_{c},t_{c}).\end{split}

Since
\nabla\psi(x_{c},t_{c})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is non-singular, the Sylvester law of inertia and (
9
) give

\text{Inertia}\big(\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{c},t_{c})\big)=\{d-q,q,0\}.

(13)

Recalling that the evolving domains are characterized by
\phi<0
, thanks to the observation in (
13
) and the normal form (

as follows. Let the spectrum of

\lambda_{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{d},\quad\lambda_{i}\in\sigma\big(\nabla^{2}\phi(x_{c},t_{c})\big).

For nondegenerate critical points all eigenvalues are non-zero and we obtain the following

2.2
Uniform Poincaré and trace inequalities

For the time dependent domains
\Omega(t)
given by the subzero levels of a smooth
\phi
with an isolated nondegenerate critical point satisfying (
12
), there are uniform in
t
bounds on the best constant in the Poincaré and trace inequalities. This is made precise in this section. For
t\in[-1,1]\setminus\{0\}
the domain
\Omega(t)
has a smooth boundary and the
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega(t))
subspace of
H^{1}(\Omega(t))
functions that have zero trace on
\partial\Omega(t)
is defined in the usual way.

Lemma 2
.

There is a constant
C
such that for all
t\in[-1,1]\setminus\{0\}
the following holds:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\Omega(t)}\

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{\Omega(t)}\quad\text{for all}~~u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega(t)).

(15)

Proof.

Recall that we assumed the domains
\Omega(t)
,
t\neq t_{c}
, to be smooth and bounded. It is clear that for all scenarios there is a constant
a
such that
\Omega(t)\subset[-a,a]^{d}
for all
t\in[-1,1]
. We now apply a standard argument for all regular times
t\neq 0
. Take
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega(t))
,
t\neq 0
, and extend it by zero outside
\Omega(t)
. The extended function
\bar{u}
is from
H^{1}_{0}([-a,a]^{d})
and we have thanks to Poincaré inequality in the cube:

\|u\|_{\Omega(t)}=\|\bar{u}\|_{[-a,a]^{d}}\leq\sqrt{2}a\|\nabla\bar{u}\|_{[-a,a]^{d}}=\sqrt{2}a\|\nabla u\|_{\Omega(t)}

which proves the result (
15
).
∎

Lemma 3
.

For the cases 2a),3a) there is a constant
C
such that the following trace estimate holds

\|u\|_{\partial\Omega(t)}^{2}\leq C\big(|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{\Omega(t)}^{2}+|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega(t))}^{2}\big)\quad\text{for all}~~u\in C^{1}(\Omega(t))

(16)

and for all
t\in[-1,0)
(vanishing island case) or or all

(creation of an island case).

For the other classification cases there is a constant
C
such that for all
t\in[-1,1]\setminus\{0\}
the following trace estimate holds

\|u\|_{\partial\Omega(t)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega(t))}\quad\text{for all}~~u\in C^{1}(\Omega(t)).

(17)

Proof.

For
|t|\geq\delta
, with a given
\delta>0
, there is a diffeomorphism between
\Omega(t)
and
\Omega(-1)
or
\Omega(1)
.
For the domains
\Omega(-1)

the standard trace inequality holds, and thus we obtain an estimate as in (
17

t
provided
|t|\geq\delta
. For
|t|\leq\delta
and
\delta
sufficiently small, we can use the normal form of
\phi
and classification
1
.

Cases 2a), 2c), 3a), 3d).
For the cases 2a) and 3a) we have a domain
\Omega(t)
that is a ball with radius equal to
|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Now use the standard trace inequality for the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a scaling argument. This then yields the result (
16
). The cases 2c) and 3d) are straightforward.

Cases 2b), 3b), 3c).

For the analysis of these cases we use a different technique.
For
\varepsilon>0
we define
\Box_{\varepsilon}:=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,|x_{i}|\leq\varepsilon,~1\leq i\leq d\,\}
. For

we will derive a uniform bound for

in terms of
\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega(t)\cap\Box_{\tilde{\varepsilon}})}

. To this end, we consider a subdivision of the (local) boundary

into a finite number of boundary subdomains. Assume

is one of such subdomains and assume a

, such that

, defines local coordinates with

, independent of

. Furthermore, assume that the following two conditions are satisfied, where

\displaystyle\inf_{\Gamma_{+}(t)}|w^{T}n|\geq c_{0}>0

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all}~t\in[-\delta,\delta],

(18)

\displaystyle S_{\alpha_{0}}(t):=\{\xi+\alpha w\,|\,\xi\in\Gamma_{+}(t),~\alpha\in[0,\alpha_{0}]\,\}\subset\Omega(t)\cap\Box_{\varepsilon+\alpha_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all}~t\in[-\delta,\delta],

(19)

with a constant

\begin{split}\int_{\Gamma_{+}(t)}u^{2}\,ds&\leq c_{1}\int_{\Gamma_{+}(t)}\int_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}u^{2}+(w\cdot\nabla u)^{2}\ d\alpha\,d\xi\\
&\leq c_{2}\int_{S_{\alpha_{0}}}u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq c_{2}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega(t)\cap\Box_{\varepsilon+\alpha_{0}})}^{2},\end{split}

with constants

Case 2b).
We consider this case with
\phi_{t}(0,0)>0

. The result for other parts of

and with arguments as in (

|n(x)^{T}w|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}

3
Model problem and space-time function spaces

We assume that an evolving domain
\Omega(t)
,
t\in[-1,1]
, as specified in Section
2
is given and consider the heat equation as a model problem:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\Delta u=f\quad\text{on}~~\Omega(t),~~t\in(-1,1],

(23)

with initial condition
u(x,-1)=u_{0}(x)
for
x\in\Omega(-1)
(except for the classification case of an island creation). We assume homogeneous Dirichlet’s boundary condition

u=0\quad\text{on}~~\partial\Omega(t),~~t\in(-1,1].

(24)

A sufficiently regular solution to (
23
)– (
24
) satisfies the integral identity

\left(u_{t},v\right)_{\mathcal{Q}}+\left(\nabla u,\nabla v\right)_{\mathcal{Q}}=\left(f,v\right)_{\mathcal{Q}}

(25)

for any sufficiently smooth
v
vanishing on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. The integral equality suggests functions from
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
with space gradients in
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
to form an appropriate test space and to consider solutions
u
from the same space, for which time derivative
u_{t}
exists in a suitable weak sense.

We introduce such spaces below and derive relevant properties. In section
5
we use these spaces to show well-posedness of a more general parabolic problem.

3.1
Function spaces

For the space–time domain
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
let
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})
be the space of infinitely differentiable functions with compact support in
\mathcal{Q}
.
Consider a space consisting of functions from
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
for which the weak first partial derivatives with respect to the space variables exist in
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
:

H:=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})~|~\partial_{i}u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Q}),~1\leq i\leq d\,\right\}.

(26)

Endowed with the scalar product

(u,v)_{H}=(u,v)_{\mathcal{Q}}+(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{\mathcal{Q}},\quad u,v\in H,

this is a Hilbert space. In the literature we did not find a result showing that functions from this anisotropic Sobolev space can be approximated by functions that are smooth up to the boundary of
\mathcal{Q}
. The next theorem states that the expected density result indeed holds.

Theorem 4
.

The space
C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{Q}})
is dense in
H
.

Proof.

The arguments used in standard references, e.g.
[
2
,
8
]
, for proving density of globally smooth functions in the standard Sobolev space
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
also apply to this anisotropic case. Details are given in Appendix
A
.
∎

Recall that
V_{\Gamma}
is the normal velocity of
\Gamma(t)
, cf. (
7
). Then, for any
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{Q}})
, it holds that

\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}}(1+|V_{\Gamma}|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}u^{2}\,dq=\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{\Gamma(t)}u^{2}\,ds\,dt.

(27)

To define a trace on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}
for functions from
H
, we introduce the weight function

\omega=\left\{\begin{split}|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|V_{\Gamma}|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\quad\text{for cases 2a), 3a),}\\
(1+|V_{\Gamma}|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\quad\text{for other cases}.\end{split}\right.

The corresponding weighted
L^{2}
-space is denoted by
L^{2}_{\omega}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}})
.
From the definition of
\omega
and the uniform trace inequalities (
16
)–(
17
) it follows that

\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\omega}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}})}\leq C\|u\|_{H}\quad\text{for all}~u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{Q}})

(28)

holds. Owing to the density result in Theorem
4
and a standard continuity argument, the trace operator
u|{\Gamma\mathcal{Q}}:,H\to L^{2}_{\omega}(\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}})
is well defined.

We now can define the space of functions from
H
vanishing on the space boundary of
Q
in the sense of their traces,

H_{0}=\{u\in H\,:\,u|_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}}=0\}.

Theorem 5
.

The space
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{Q})
is dense in
H_{0}
.

Proof.

For a given
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, define
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup\limits_{t\in(-1,-\varepsilon)\cup(\varepsilon,1)}\hskip-8.61108pt\Omega(t)\times\{t\}
. For an arbitrary
u\in H_{0}
define
u_{\varepsilon}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u&\text{in }\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon},\\
0&\text{in }\mathcal{Q}\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}.\end{array}\right.

We have
u_{\varepsilon}\in H_{0}
and
\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H}\to 0
for
\varepsilon\to 0
. For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
the domain
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}
is the union of two subdomains
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
, each corresponding to a smooth evolution of
\Omega(t)
, cf. (
1
). For example,
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{+}=\bigcup_{t\in(\varepsilon,1)}\Omega(t)\times\{t\}
, where
\Omega(t)=\Phi^{+}(t)(\Omega(1))
and
\Phi^{+}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega(1)}\times[\varepsilon,1])
. By standard arguments based on cutting off and mollifying, see, e.g.
[
13
]
, one shows that there is a function
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon})
such that
\|u_{\varepsilon}-\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}-\psi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\leq\varepsilon
. Extending
\psi_{\varepsilon}
by zero to
\mathcal{Q}
and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields by the triangle inequality
\|u-\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{H}\to 0
and so proves the theorem. ∎

Besides the (test) space
H_{0}
we also need a suitable solution space containing elements from
H_{0}
that have a well-defined weak time derivative.
For
u\in H_{0}
, following a standard approach we consider
\partial_{t}u
as a distribution:

\langle\partial_{t}u,\xi\rangle:=-\int_{\mathcal{Q}}u\,\partial_{t}\xi\,dq,\quad\text{for}~\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{Q}).

We restrict to
u\in H_{0}
for which

\|\partial_{t}u\|_{H^{-1}}:=\sup_{\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Q)}\frac{\langle\partial_{t}u,\xi\rangle}{\|\xi\|_{H}}<\infty

holds. In this case,
\partial_{t}u
is uniquely extended to a linear bounded functional on
H_{0}
.

As the solution space for our problem we propose

W=\left\{u\in H_{0}\,|\,\partial_{t}u\in H_{0}^{-1}\right\},\quad\text{with}~\|u\|_{W}=\left(\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(29)

In the next section we shall see that smooth functions vanishing on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}
are dense in
W
for certain classes of the classification from Section
2.1
. We also show a Lions-Magenes type result for
W
.

Concerning the rather technical proof of Lemma

The crucial point in the analysis is to show that

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}}=0.

On the domain

, where

On the domain

For the latter analysis, a one-dimensional Hardy estimate, presented in the next lemma, is useful.

Lemma 6
.

Take
0\leq a<b
and
u\in H^{1}(a,b)
with
u(b)=0
. Then for
p\geq 0
, the estimate

\int_{a}^{b}\left(\frac{u}{b-x}\right)^{2}x^{p}\,dx\leq C_{p}\int_{a}^{b}(u^{\prime})^{2}x^{p}\,dx

(32)

holds with a constant
C_{p}<\infty
independent of
a,b
and
u
.

Proof.

By the change of the variables
x=bx^{\prime}
, we see that (
32
)
is equivalent to

\int_{a}^{1}\left(\frac{u}{1-x}\right)^{2}x^{p}\,dx\leq C_{p}\int_{a}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}x^{p}\,dx.

(33)

for
u\in H^{1}(a,1)
with
u(1)=0
and
a\in[0,1)
.
For
p=0
this a standard Hardy estimate. Take
p\geq 0
, let
\alpha=\max\{\tfrac{1}{2},a\}
.
For the integral over
[\alpha,1]
we use the Hardy inequality on that interval and obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\alpha}^{1}\left(\frac{u}{1-x}\right)^{2}x^{p}\,dx&\leq\int_{\alpha}^{1}\left(\frac{u}{1-x}\right)^{2}\,dx\leq\tilde{c}_{H}\int_{\alpha}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dx\\
&\leq\tilde{c}_{H}2^{p}\int_{\alpha}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}x^{p}\,dx\leq\tilde{c}_{H}2^{p}\int_{a}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}x^{p}\,dx.\end{split}

(34)

If
a<\tfrac{1}{2}
, we have another integral over
(a,\tfrac{1}{2})
. We first note

\int_{a}^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{u}{1-x}\right)^{2}x^{p}\,dx\leq 4\int_{a}^{1}u^{2}x^{p}\,dx

(35)

and also

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{1}u^{2}x^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{a^{p+1}}{p+1}u^{2}(a)-\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{a}^{1}uu^{\prime}x^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{p+1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}x^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{a}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}x^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{1}u^{2}x^{p}\,dx+\frac{2}{(p+1)^{2}}\int_{a}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}x^{p}\,dx.

Shifting the integral of
u^{2}x^{p}
to the left
and using
this in (
35
) completes the proof of (
33
).
∎

Lemma 7
.

Consider a space-time domain with critical points as in classification
1
, except for the cases 2c) and 3d).
For
u\in H_{0}
we have
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H}=0
.

Proof.

Take
u\in H_{0}
.
By the triangle inequality, it holds

\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H}\leq\|(1-{\theta}_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}}+\|(1-{\theta}_{\varepsilon})\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{Q}}+\|(\nabla{\theta}_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}}.

(36)

We use the notation
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}:=\text{supp}(1-\theta_{\varepsilon})
.
For the first term in (
36
), we have
\|(1-{\theta}_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}}\leq\|u\|_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}}\to 0
for
\varepsilon\to 0
, since

if

and
\mbox{meas}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon})\to 0
, cf. (

\varepsilon
. For the third term we note
\|(\nabla{\theta}_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}}=\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}
. In the rest of the proof we show that
\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}\to 0
for
\varepsilon\to 0
.

A finite cover.
We introduce a finite cover of
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}
with cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with side length
2\delta
. The value of
\delta
will be chosen sufficiently small, but independent of
\varepsilon
. This cover is denoted by
\{\mathcal{O}_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq N}
,

,
\mathcal{O}_{i}=\{\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}~|~\|y-a^{(i)}\|_{\infty}<\delta\,\}
, with centers
a^{(0)}:=(0,0)
,
a^{(i)}\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}
,
1\leq i\leq N
, and
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}\subset\cup_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathcal{O}_{i}
. We choose this cover such that
(0,0)\notin\overline{\cup_{1\leq i\leq N}\mathcal{O}_{i}}
and
|\nabla\phi|>0
on
\overline{\cup_{1\leq i\leq N}\mathcal{O}_{i}}
. Note that

\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{O}_{0}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}.

(37)

We can make use of the density of smooth functions (Theorem
5
) as follows. Consider a term
\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}
,
0\leq i\leq N
. Assume that there is a constant

\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{U_{\varepsilon}}\quad\text{for all}~u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})

(38)

holds for a domain
U_{\varepsilon}
with
(\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon})\subset U_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{Q}
with
{\rm meas}(U_{\varepsilon})\to 0
for
\varepsilon\to 0
. For the given

we take a sequence
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q})
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}=u
in

\displaystyle\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}(u-u_{k})\|_{\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}+\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}\leq c\varepsilon^{-1}\|u-u_{k}\|_{H}+C\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{U_{\varepsilon}}

Letting

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}=0

Estimate (

.

In the neighborhood

,

, with coordinate

Degenerating island.
We consider the creation of an island scenario. The case of a vanishing island is then also covered by replacing

in

\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{O}_{0}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}u^{2}\,dq=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}}|x|^{2}u^{2}\,dq.

\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}}|x|^{2}u^{2}\,dq\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{S^{d-1}}\int_{[t-3\varepsilon]_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\sqrt{t}}r^{2}u^{2}r^{d-1}\,dr\,ds\,dt.

Domains merging and splitting.

We now turn to the case of domain merging or splitting. We consider the 2D case, i.e.,
\phi(x,t)=-x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+t
(domain splitting, while domain merging is covered by changing
t\to-t
).
As above we have
\mathcal{O}_{0}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}\subset\{\,(x,t)\in\mathcal{O}_{0}\,|\,-3\varepsilon\leq\phi(x,t)\leq 0\,\}=:\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}
.

We first consider
t\in[0,\delta]
and note that
-3\varepsilon\leq\phi(x,t)\leq 0
iff
x_{2}^{2}+t\leq x_{1}^{2}\leq x_{2}^{2}+t+3\varepsilon
. We parameterize the domain
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(t\geq 0)
over
x_{2}\in[-\delta,\delta]
and due to symmetry we have

\begin{split}\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(t\geq 0)}^{2}&\leq c\varepsilon^{-2}\||x|u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(t\geq 0)}^{2}\\
&=2c\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\int_{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}}^{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t+3\varepsilon}}u^{2}|x|^{2}\,dx_{1}\,dx_{2}\,dt.\end{split}

(43)

Rewriting the inner integral yields

\begin{split}&\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}}^{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t+3\varepsilon}}u^{2}|x|^{2}\,dx_{1}\\
&=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}}^{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t+3\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{u}{x_{1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}}\right)^{2}\big(x_{1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}\big)^{2}|x|^{2}\,dx_{1}.\end{split}

(44)

For
\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}\leq x_{1}\leq\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t+3\varepsilon}
we have
|x_{1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}|\leq\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t+3\varepsilon}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}
and so

\big(x_{1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}\big)^{2}|x|^{2}\leq 9\varepsilon^{2}\frac{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}{\big(\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t+3\varepsilon}+\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}\big)^{2}}\leq 9\varepsilon^{2}.

Noting that
u|_{x_{1}=\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}}=0
and using this in (
44
) together with the Hardy inequality (
32
) we obtain

\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}}^{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t+3\varepsilon}}u^{2}|x|^{2}\,dx_{1}\leq 9C_{0}\int_{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t}}^{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+t+3\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{2}\,dx_{1}.

(45)

Substituting this in (
43
) yields

\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(t\geq 0)}^{2}\leq c\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(t\geq 0)}^{2},

(46)

with a suitable constant
c
independent of
\varepsilon
. For
t\in[-\delta,0]
we use very similar arguments, but parameterize over
x_{1}
, noting that
3\varepsilon\leq\phi(x,t)\leq 0
iff
x_{1}^{2}-t-3\varepsilon\leq x_{2}^{2}\leq x_{1}^{2}-t
, we obtain

\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(t\leq 0)}^{2}\leq c\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{-\delta}^{0}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\int_{[x_{1}^{2}-t-3\varepsilon]_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-t}}u^{2}|x|^{2}\,dx_{2}\,dx_{1}\,dt.

(47)

For the inner integral we have

\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{[x_{1}^{2}-t-3\varepsilon]_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-t}}u^{2}|x|^{2}\,dx_{2}=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{[x_{1}^{2}-t-3\varepsilon]_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-t}}\left|\frac{u}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-t}-x_{2}}\right|^{2}\big(\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-t}-x_{2}\big)^{2}|x|^{2}\,dx_{2},

(48)

and for
[x_{1}^{2}-t-3\varepsilon]_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq x_{2}\leq\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-t}
we have

\big(\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-t}-x_{2}\big)^{2}|x|^{2}\leq 9\varepsilon^{2}\frac{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}{\big(\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-t}+[x_{1}^{2}-t-3\varepsilon]_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}}\leq 18\varepsilon^{2}.

(49)

By the same arguments as above we obtain
\|(\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon})u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(t\leq 0)}^{2}\leq c\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}^{c}\cap(t\leq 0)}^{2}
, with
c
independent of
\varepsilon
. Combining this with the result (
46
) we conclude that (
38
) holds for
i=0
. The case of domain merging or splitting for
d=3
can be handled in a very similar way, cf. Appendix
B
.

Remark 4.1
.

We explain why the cases 2c) and 3d) are excluded from the statement of Lemma

.
The triangle inequality (

should vanish for

and
\varepsilon\to 0

Let

d=3
. We use polar coordinates

around the critical point

The spatial slices of

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\big(\|\nabla\theta_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)|\big)\subset S(t):=\{\,x~|~t+\varepsilon\leq r^{2}\leq t+3\varepsilon\,\}.

Note that this support has a uniform width
\sim\varepsilon
is space-time, but that the width of the spatial slices depends on
\varepsilon
and

Without loss of generality we may assume that

is a space–time box. Then for the term at the left-hand side of (

In

(50)

In the proof of Lemma

the key result (

\left\|\frac{r}{\varepsilon}u\right\|_{L^{2}(S(t))}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(S_{\rm ex}(t))},

with

Take

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{r}{\varepsilon}u\right\|_{L^{2}(S(t))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\xi^{-2}\int_{\sqrt{1+\xi}}^{\sqrt{1+3\xi}}y^{3}\ln^{2}y\,dy\,d\psi\sim\xi^{-2}\big(\xi^{2}\ln^{2}\xi)=\ln^{2}\xi\quad\text{for}~\xi\to\infty,

Lemma 9
.

Consider a space-time domain with a critical point as in classification
1
, except for the cases 2c) and 3d).
For
u\in W
, there exists a sequence
(w_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset W
with
\text{\rm dist}\big(\text{\rm supp}(w_{n}),\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}\big)>0
for all
n
and
w_{n}\to u
in
W
.

Proof.

Take
u\in W
and consider the cut off with
\theta_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
30
). We note that
\theta_{\varepsilon}u\in W
, since
\theta_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{Q}})
. Let
\varepsilon_{n}=\varepsilon_{0}2^{-n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small. Define
u_{n}:=\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}u
.
Lemma
7
implies
u_{n}\to u
in
H
.
By definition, we have for any
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{Q})
:

\begin{split}\langle\partial_{t}u_{n},\xi\rangle&=-\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}u\,\partial_{t}\xi\,dq=-\int_{\mathcal{Q}}u\,\partial_{t}(\xi\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}})\,dq+\int_{\mathcal{Q}}u\,\xi\,\partial_{t}\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dq\\
&=\langle\partial_{t}u,\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\xi\rangle+\int_{\mathcal{Q}}u\,\xi\,\partial_{t}\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dq.\end{split}

(53)

Note that

\left|\langle\partial_{t}u,\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\xi-\xi\rangle\right|\leq\|\partial_{t}u\|_{H^{-1}}\|\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\xi-\xi\|_{H}\leq\|u\|_{W}\|\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\xi-\xi\|_{H}\to 0\quad\text{for}~n\to\infty,

where we used Lemma
7
to claim
\|\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\xi-\xi\|_{H}\to 0
. Furthermore, with the result in Lemma
8
we obtain

\left|\int_{\mathcal{Q}}u\,\xi\,\partial_{t}\theta_{\varepsilon_{n}}\,dq\right|=\left|\int_{\mathcal{Q}}u\,\xi\,h_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{\prime}\partial_{t}\phi\,dq\right|\leq c\,\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon_{n}}}|u\,\xi|\,dq\leq\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}\|u\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon_{n}}}\to 0~\text{for}~n\to\infty.

Thus we conclude

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\partial_{t}u_{n}-\partial_{t}u,\xi\rangle=0\quad\text{for all}~\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{Q}).

Using this and the density of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{Q})
in
H_{0}
we get weak convergence
\partial_{t}u_{n}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}u
in
H_{0}^{-1}
. By the Banach–Saks theorem there exists a subsequence of
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, which we also denote by
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, such that the Cesaro means of
\{(\partial_{t}u_{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
converge strongly in
H_{0}^{-1}
, i.e,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k}\partial_{t}u_{n}=\partial_{t}u\quad\text{strongly in}~H_{0}^{-1}.

Define
w_{k}:=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{k}u_{n}
. From
u_{n}\to u
in
H
it follows that
w_{k}\to u
in
H
. We also have
\partial_{t}w_{k}\to\partial_{t}u
in
H_{0}^{-1}
. Hence
w_{k}\to u
in
W
. From
\text{\rm dist}\big(\text{\rm supp}(u_{n}),\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}\big)>0
for all
n
it follows that
\text{\rm dist}\big(\text{\rm supp}(w_{k}),\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}\big)>0
for all
k
.

∎

Lemma 10
.

Consider a space-time domain with critical points as in classification
1
, except for the cases 2c) and 3d). The subspace of functions from
C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{Q}})
vanishing on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}
is dense in
W
.

Proof.

From Lemma
9
it follows that it is sufficient to consider
u\in W
for which
\epsilon:=\text{dist}(\text{supp}(u),\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}})>0
holds and to show that
u
can be approximated arbitrary well by smooth functions in
\|\cdot\|_{W}
.
Since
\Omega(t)
is bounded for all
t\in[-1,1]
, there is a ball
\widehat{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a space–time cylinder
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}=\widehat{B}\times[-1,1]\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
such that
\mathcal{Q}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}
. For functions defined on
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}
we introduce the standard Bochner space

\widehat{W}=\{u\in L^{2}([-1,1],H^{1}_{0}(\widehat{B}))\,|\,\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}([-1,1],H^{-1}(\widehat{B}))\},

with the corresponding norm.
For
u\in W
denote by
\bar{u}
its extension by zero to
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}
.
Since
u\in H_{0}
it is straightforward to see that
\bar{u}\in L^{2}([-1,1],H^{1}_{0}(\widehat{B}))

and

\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}([-1,1],H^{1}_{0}(\widehat{B}))}=\|u\|_{H}.

Since
\varepsilon=\text{dist}(\text{supp}(u),\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}})>0
, there is a cut-off function
\kappa_{\varepsilon}=\kappa_{\varepsilon}(u)\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}})
, such that
\text{supp}(\kappa_{\varepsilon})\subset\overline{\mathcal{Q}}
,
\text{dist}(\text{supp}(\kappa_{\varepsilon}),\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}})\geq\varepsilon/2
and

\kappa_{\varepsilon}=1
on
\text{supp}(u)
.
For
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{Q}})
we have

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\bar{u},\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}}\bar{u}\,\partial_{t}\xi\,dq=-\int_{\mathcal{Q}}u\,\partial_{t}(\kappa_{\varepsilon}\xi)\,dq=\langle\partial_{t}u,\kappa_{\varepsilon}\xi\rangle.

Using this,
\partial_{t}u\in H_{0}^{-1}
and
\|\kappa_{\varepsilon}\xi\|_{H}\leq c(\varepsilon)\|\xi\|_{L^{2}([-1,1],H^{1}_{0}(\widehat{B}))}
it follows that
\bar{u}\in\widehat{W}
. One easily checks that
\|u\|_{W}\leq\|\bar{u}\|_{\widehat{W}}
holds. By a standard result for the cylindrical space-time domain, we have that for any
\delta>0
there exists
\bar{u}_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}})
such that

\|\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\delta}\|_{\widehat{W}}\leq\delta.

(54)

Define
u_{\delta}=\kappa_{\varepsilon}\bar{u}_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}})
. Note that
\text{supp}(u_{\delta})\subset\overline{\mathcal{Q}}
holds and
u_{\delta}
vanishes on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}
. For
g\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}})
and
w\in\widehat{W}
we have
\|gw\|_{\widehat{W}}\leq c_{g}\|w\|_{\widehat{W}}
, for a suitable constant
c_{g}
independent of
w
. Using this and triangle inequalities we obtain, for suitable
c_{\varepsilon}=c_{\varepsilon}(u)>0
,

\begin{split}\|u-u_{\delta}\|_{W}&\leq\|\bar{u}-u_{\delta}\|_{\widehat{W}}\leq\|\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\delta}\|_{\widehat{W}}+\|\bar{u}_{\delta}-u_{\delta}\|_{\widehat{W}}\leq\delta+\|\bar{u}_{\delta}-u_{\delta}\|_{\widehat{W}}\\
&=\delta+\|(1-\kappa_{\varepsilon})\bar{u}_{\delta}\|_{\widehat{W}}=\delta+\|(1-\kappa_{\varepsilon})(\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\delta})\|_{\widehat{W}}\leq\delta+c_{\varepsilon}\|\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\delta}\|_{\widehat{W}}\\
&\leq(1+c_{\varepsilon})\delta.\end{split}

For given
u
and
\epsilon>0
we take
\delta>0
such that
(1+c_{\varepsilon}(u))\delta<\epsilon
and for the corresponding
\bar{u}_{\delta}
as in (
54
) we take
u_{\delta}=\kappa_{\varepsilon}\bar{u}_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{Q}})
, which vanishes on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{Q}}
and satisfies
\|u-u_{\delta}\|_{W}\leq\epsilon
. This completes the proof.
∎

(56)

holds. Consider the cases 2b), 3b) or 3c), i.e.

The continuity property is straightforward:

Lemma 12
.

The inf-sup inequality

\inf_{0\neq u\in W_{0}}~\sup_{0\neq v\in{H_{0}}}\frac{\langle u_{t},v\rangle+a(u,v)}{\|u\|_{W}\|v\|_{H}}\geq c_{s}

(66)

holds with some
c_{s}>0
.

Proof.

Take
u\in W_{0}
and let
u_{\gamma}=e^{-\gamma t}u\in H_{0}
, with some
\gamma>0
. From (
59
) and
u(t_{0})=0
, we infer

\langle u_{t},u_{\gamma}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\langle(u_{\gamma})_{t},u\rangle+\langle u_{t},u_{\gamma}\rangle+\gamma(u,u_{\gamma})_{Q}\right)\geq\tfrac{\gamma}{2}(u,u_{\gamma})_{Q}.

From this and the Gårding condition we get

\langle u_{t},u_{\gamma}\rangle+a(u,u_{\gamma})\geq c_{0}\|u\|_{H}^{2},\quad c_{0}=c_{0}(\gamma)>0,

(67)

if
\gamma=2c_{1}
.
This establishes the control of
\|u\|_{H}
on the right-hand side of the inf-sup inequality. We also need control of
\|u_{t}\|_{H^{-1}}
to bound the full norm
\|u\|_{W}
.

By Riesz’ representation theorem, there is a unique
z\in H_{0}
such that
\langle u_{t},v\rangle=(z,v)_{H}
for all
v\in H_{0}
, and
\|z\|_{H}=\|u_{t}\|_{H^{-1}}
holds. Thus we obtain

\langle u_{t},z\rangle=(z,z)_{H}=\|u_{t}\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}.

Therefore, we get

\langle u_{t},z\rangle+a(u,z)=\|z\|_{H}^{2}+a(u,z)\geq\|z\|_{H}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}C_{a}^{2}\|u\|_{H}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\|z\|_{H}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}C_{a}^{2}\|u\|_{H}^{2}.

(68)

This establishes control of
\|u_{t}\|_{H^{-1}}
at the expense of the
H
-norm, which is controlled in (
67
).
Letting
v=z+\mu u_{\gamma}\in H_{0}
with some sufficiently large parameter
\mu\geq 1
, we have the estimate

\|v\|_{H}\leq\|z\|_{H}+\mu\|u_{\gamma}\|_{H}\leq\|u_{t}\|_{H^{-1}}+\mu\|u\|_{H}\leq\mu\sqrt{2}\|u\|_{W}.

(69)

Taking
\mu:=\frac{1+C_{a}^{2}}{2c_{0}}
, we conclude from (
67
), (
68
) and (
69
) that

\langle u_{t},v\rangle+a(u,v)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}+(\mu c_{0}-\tfrac{1}{2}C_{a}^{2})\|u\|_{H}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|u\|_{W}^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\tfrac{1}{\mu}\|u\|_{W}\|v\|_{H}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 13
.

If
\langle u_{t},v\rangle+a(u,v)=0

and all

, then

.

Assume there is

such that
\langle u_{t},v\rangle=-a(u,v)
for all

. For all

we have by definition

\displaystyle\langle v_{t},u\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(t)}vu_{t}\,dx\,dt=-\langle u_{t},v\rangle=a(u,v).

(70)

Since the functional
u\to a(u,v)
is in
H_{0}^{-1}
, we conclude
v_{t}\in H_{0}^{-1}
, and thus
v\in W
holds. From (
70
) and the density result in Theorem
5
it follows that

\langle v_{t},u\rangle=a(u,v)\quad\text{ for all}~~u\in H_{0}.

(71)

Combining this with
\langle u_{t},v\rangle=-a(u,v)
for all

and using (

0=\langle v_{t},u\rangle+\langle u_{t},v\rangle=\int_{\Omega(T)}u(x,T)v(x,T)\,dx\quad\text{for all}~~u\in W_{0}.

This implies that

\Omega(T)

u=v_{\gamma}=e^{-\gamma t}v

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle v_{t},v_{\gamma}\rangle-a(v_{\gamma},v)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\langle v_{t},v_{\gamma}\rangle+\langle(v_{\gamma})_{t},v\rangle+\gamma(v,v_{\gamma})_{Q})-a(v_{\gamma},v)
