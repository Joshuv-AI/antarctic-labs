Allergy burden score and lung function impairment in children with asthma: a dose–response cross-sectional study
Authors: Junlin Zhao, Zhiyuan Wang, Yanan Wang, Qianqian Dai, Shengmeng Qu, Liang Ru
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Children with asthma commonly present with multiple allergic comorbidities; however, the quantitative dose–response relationship between cumulative allergic burden and lung function has not been established in pediatric populations. This retrospective cross-sectional study included 264 treatment-naive children with asthma aged 4–16 years. An allergy burden score (ABS) was constructed using a cumulative disease count strategy incorporating allergic rhinitis, atopic dermatitis, food allergy, drug allergy, and family history of allergic disease. Multivariable linear regression and restricted cubic spline (RCS) analyses assessed dose–response relationships between ABS and FEV1% predicted. Bootstrap-based statistical decomposition examined the contribution of type 2 inflammatory markers, and logistic regression assessed the odds of lung function abnormality. In the fully adjusted model, each 1-point increase in ABS was associated with a 3.03% decrease in FEV1% predicted (95% CI: − 3.77 to − 2.29; P < 0.001). RCS analysis indicated significant nonlinearity (P for nonlinearity < 0.001), with accelerated decrease at ABS ≥ 3. Type 2 inflammatory markers accounted for only 2.7% of the statistically decomposed total association. Each 1-point increase in ABS was associated with 43% higher odds of lung function abnormality (adjusted OR = 1.43, 95% CI: 1.07–1.94).Conclusion: A dose–response association exists between cumulative allergic comorbidity burden and lung function impairment in treatment-naive children with asthma. Peripheral type 2 inflammatory markers explain only a small proportion of this association, suggesting additional contributing mechanisms. The ABS may warrant further evaluation as a simple clinical indicator for identifying children with greater lung function impairment, pending prospective multicenter validation.Trial registration: Not applicable. As a retrospective cross-sectional study, this research analyzed existing medical records without prospective assignment of interventions by the research team, and therefore does not meet the WHO criteria for clinical trial registration. Introduction

Bronchial asthma is one of the most common chronic respiratory diseases in children, with a steadily increasing global prevalence that imposes a substantial burden on public health and healthcare systems [1]. Notably, children with asthma frequently exhibit comorbid allergic rhinitis, atopic dermatitis, and food allergy, among other allergic conditions. This pattern of “allergic multimorbidity” has been proposed as a distinct clinical phenotype [2]. Epidemiologic studies have shown that the prevalence of allergic comorbidities in children with asthma is substantially higher than that in the general population [3]. Furthermore, multiple allergic diseases often occur concurrently [4]. Based on these comorbidity patterns, it has been hypothesized that these diseases may share common pathophysiological mechanisms via shared type 2 immune-inflammatory pathways [2, 5].

Prior studies have documented the independent adverse effects of individual allergic comorbidities on asthma outcomes, including accelerated lung function decline associated with allergic rhinitis [6], and the role of atopic dermatitis in the allergic march [7, 8]. However, the cumulative association between multiple coexisting allergic comorbidities and lung function has not been systematically quantified in pediatric populations. Although the cumulative disease count strategy has been applied broadly in multimorbidity research [9], its application in pediatric allergic asthma remains underexplored, and the statistical contribution of type 2 inflammatory markers to this association has not been quantified [10].

We therefore developed an Allergy Burden Score (ABS) in 264 treatment-naive children with asthma aged 4–16 years to: (1) evaluate the dose–response relationship between cumulative allergic burden and lung function; (2) quantify the statistical contribution of type 2 inflammatory markers; and (3) assess the association between ABS and lung function abnormality.

Methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective cross-sectional study reviewed medical records of children with asthma aged 4–16 years at the Pediatric Center of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University (May 2022–October 2025). Inclusion criteria were: age 4–16 years, GINA 2022-confirmed asthma diagnosis [11], and treatment-naive status. The age range was selected because 4 years represents the minimum age for reliable spirometry per ATS/ERS preschool standards [12], while 16 years captures the full pediatric spectrum. Exclusion criteria were: chronic pulmonary disease, inborn errors of metabolism, congenital heart disease, epilepsy or intellectual disability, and incomplete records. Preterm birth was retained as a covariate rather than an exclusion criterion to statistically adjust for its potential confounding effect while preserving sample size; the proportion of preterm births did not differ significantly across ABS groups after pairwise correction. Asthma severity was not formally classified because all participants were treatment-naive at enrollment, and GINA-based severity grading requires assessment of treatment response over time [11]. All values were expressed as percent predicted using Global Lung Function Initiative (GLI) 2012 equations [13], and pre-specified subgroup analyses by age group assessed the consistency of findings. The participant selection process is illustrated in Fig. 1. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University (No. K202509-54). Allergy burden score and group classification

An ABS was constructed using a cumulative disease count approach [2]. One point was assigned for each of five conditions: allergic rhinitis [14], atopic dermatitis [15], food allergy [16], drug allergy (clinically confirmed history of drug hypersensitivity reaction), and family history of allergic disease, yielding a total score of 0–5. Diagnoses were established from medical records verified by attending physicians according to internationally accepted criteria. Family allergy history was included as a surrogate marker for hereditary atopic predisposition [4]. Participants were classified into low (0–1), moderate (2–3), and high (4–5) burden groups (Supplementary Table S1). Equal weights were assigned to reflect the cumulative additive contribution of multiple allergic conditions [17].

Clinical data collection

Demographic data (age, sex, BMI), perinatal factors (delivery mode, gestational age, birth weight, breastfeeding duration), and environmental exposures (passive smoking, respiratory infection frequency) were extracted from electronic medical records using a standardized protocol. Preterm birth was defined as gestational age < 37 weeks.

Testing methods and instruments

Spirometry was performed using the MasterScreen system (JAEGER, Germany) at the initial diagnostic visit, prior to any controller therapy. All analyzed values were obtained before bronchodilator administration; bronchodilator reversibility testing was subsequently performed for diagnostic confirmation per GINA 2022 criteria [11], but only pre-bronchodilator values were analyzed. Testing followed ATS/ERS guidelines: the 2007 preschool standards for ages 4–6 [12], which incorporate age-appropriate acceptability and repeatability criteria for younger children, and the 2019 guidelines for ages 7–16 [18]. Parameters included forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV₁), forced vital capacity (FVC), FEV₁/FVC, and maximal mid-expiratory flow (MMEF), expressed as percent predicted using GLI 2012 equations [13]. FeNO was measured by chemiluminescence analyzer (Sunvou-CA2122; Sunvou Medical Electronics, Wuxi, China) per ATS/ERS recommendations. Peripheral blood eosinophil percentage and serum total IgE were determined by automated hematology analyzer and chemiluminescence immunoassay, respectively. Data entry employed double-entry verification with discrepancies resolved by a third senior researcher.

Statistical analysis

General methods

Descriptive statistics and between-group comparisons were performed using SPSS 29.0. Continuous variables were compared using one-way ANOVA, Welch ANOVA, or Kruskal–Wallis tests; categorical variables using chi-square or Fisher exact tests. Post hoc comparisons used Bonferroni-corrected tests.

Multivariable linear regression analysis

Multivariable linear regression assessed the association between ABS (continuous, 0–5) and spirometric parameters. Covariates were selected a priori based on established associations with both allergic burden and lung function: age and sex as determinants of lung growth [13]; BMI as a correlate of airway obstruction [11]; passive smoking [19] and respiratory infection frequency [20] as environmental factors associated with reduced lung function; and perinatal factors (delivery mode, preterm birth, breastfeeding duration) given their documented associations with atopic sensitization and early lung development [21]. Four sequential models were fitted: crude; Model 1 (age, sex); Model 2 (+ BMI, passive smoking, infection frequency); Model 3 (fully adjusted). Trend tests used burden group as an ordinal variable.

Dose–response relationship

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression with three knots (10th, 50th, 90th percentiles) evaluated nonlinearity, adjusting for Model 3 covariates. Nonlinearity was tested by likelihood ratio test.

Exploratory statistical decomposition analysis

This analysis was explicitly exploratory and was not intended to establish causal mediation; rather, it aimed to statistically quantify the proportion of the ABS–lung function association that could be accounted for by measurable type 2 inflammatory markers. Exploratory statistical decomposition employed a counterfactual-based framework to examine whether type 2 inflammatory markers (total IgE, FeNO, eosinophil percentage) statistically contributed to the ABS–lung function association. Indirect contributions were estimated using the product-of-coefficients method with bias-corrected bootstrap 95% CIs (1,000 iterations). A parsimonious adjustment strategy (age and sex only) was adopted to minimize overfitting given the sample size (N = 264). Sequential ignorability was assumed; residual confounding cannot be excluded. Accordingly, the directional structure adopted in this decomposition reflects a pre-specified analytical framework and should not be interpreted as evidence of a directional or causal pathway. Analyses were performed using the mediation package in R (version 4.5.0), applied here for its statistical decomposition functionality rather than for causal mediation inference.

Logistic regression analysis

Logistic regression assessed ABS–lung function abnormality (FEV₁% < 80%) associations, modeling ABS as both continuous and categorical variables with the same covariates as Model 3. The Cochran–Armitage trend test examined dose–response across groups.

Subgroup and sensitivity

Prespecified subgroup analyses stratified participants by age (< 6, 6–12, > 12 years) and sex; interaction terms (ABS × age group; ABS × sex) were tested via likelihood ratio tests (threshold: P < 0.10). Restricted cubic spline curves and forest plots were generated; no correction for multiple comparisons was applied. Two sensitivity analyses were performed: (1) excluding extreme FEV₁% values (< 50% or > 110%) under Model 1, and (2) substituting MMEF% as the primary outcome under Model 3. Model diagnostics included VIF assessment and Q-Q plots; where non-normality was detected, robustness was confirmed by the central limit theorem [22].

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 264 children (mean age 8.80 ± 2.88 years; 62.1% male) were classified into low- (n = 46), moderate- (n = 166), and high-burden (n = 52) groups. Demographic characteristics were comparable across groups (Supplementary Table S1). The prevalence of all five ABS components increased progressively across burden groups (all P < 0.001). Serum total IgE increased across groups (P = 0.003), while FeNO was lower in the high-burden group (P = 0.002). All spirometric parameters differed significantly across groups (all P < 0.001; Supplementary Tables S2–S3).

Dose–response relationship between allergy burden score and lung function

In the fully adjusted model, each 1-point increase in ABS was associated with decreases of 3.03% in FEV₁% predicted (95% CI: –3.77 to –2.29), 1.28% in MMEF%, 2.00% in FVC%, and 1.18% in FEV₁/FVC (all P < 0.001). Effect estimates were slightly attenuated after sequential covariate adjustment but remained significant. Trend tests confirmed dose–response gradients across burden groups (all P for trend < 0.001; Table 1). RCS analysis demonstrated significant nonlinearity (P < 0.001; Fig. 2A), with a moderate decrease at scores 0–2 and accelerated decrease at scores ≥ 3, suggesting a threshold effect. The box plot (Fig. 2B) illustrated the distribution of FEV1% across the three burden groups. Model diagnostics confirmed absence of multicollinearity (maximum VIF = 2.11) and acceptable residual distributions (Supplementary Table S4 and Fig. S1). In the decomposition model adjusted for age and sex (total association: β = –3.59), indirect contributions through total IgE, FeNO, and eosinophil percentage were small and individually non-significant (Supplementary Table S5; Fig. 3). The combined indirect contribution was statistically significant (β = –0.10, 95% CI: –0.15 to –0.04), accounting for 2.7% of the total association. The residual direct association was β = –3.49 (95% CI: –4.13 to –2.85), representing 97.3% of the total. Odds of lung function abnormality

Logistic regression analysis (Table 2) showed that each 1-point increase in the allergy burden score was associated with a 43% higher odds of lung function abnormality (adjusted OR = 1.43, 95% CI: 1.07 to 1.94, P = 0.018). In categorical analysis, the high-burden group showed 2.67-fold higher odds compared with the low-burden group (adjusted OR = 2.67, 95% CI: 0.84–9.61, P = 0.108), which did not reach significance, likely due to limited sample size (n = 52). The Cochran–Armitage trend test was significant (P = 0.043). The prevalence of lung function abnormality increased from 73.9% (low-burden) to 75.9% (moderate) and 90.4% (high-burden). Subgroup and sensitivity

Subgroup analyses confirmed significant inverse associations across all age and sex strata (all P < 0.05; Fig. 4A), with non-significant interactions (age: P = 0.58; sex: P = 0.41; Fig. 4B and C). Sensitivity analyses confirmed robustness: the association persisted after excluding extreme FEV₁% values (β = –3.21, P < 0.001), and MMEF% as an alternative outcome yielded a consistent pattern (Supplementary Table S7 and Fig. S3A). Discussion

This study identified a dose–response association between cumulative allergic comorbidity burden and lung function impairment in treatment-naive children with asthma. A nonlinear threshold effect was observed at ABS ≥ 3, peripheral type 2 inflammatory markers accounted for only 2.7% of the total association, and these findings were consistent across age and sex subgroups.

The observed negative association between cumulative allergic burden and lung function is consistent with prior epidemiologic evidence linking allergic multimorbidity to worse asthma outcomes [1, 3, 4]. Building on the cumulative disease count strategy established in multimorbidity research [23], the present study was motivated by the observation that the existing literature has focused predominantly on individual comorbidity pairs [24, 25] or descriptive prevalence data [24], without providing quantitative association data between cumulative comorbidities and lung function. Scelo et al. [17] reported that a greater number of comorbidities was associated with worse clinical outcomes in adults with severe asthma; Licari et al. [26] emphasized comorbidity management as a modifiable factor in pediatric severe asthma but did not quantify the cumulative burden–lung function relationship. The present study extends this line of inquiry by applying RCS analysis to characterize a nonlinear pattern, with an accelerated decline observed at ABS ≥ 3, suggesting a potential threshold beyond which the association may intensify, although prospective confirmation is needed. Kalb et al. [27] proposed that shared epithelial barrier dysfunction, rather than simple sequential allergic progression, may represent the mechanism underlying allergic co-occurrence, which could partly account for this cumulative pattern.

Exploratory statistical decomposition analysis revealed that type 2 inflammatory markers collectively accounted for only 2.7% of the total association, with no individual marker reaching significance independently. This finding is consistent with observations from the Global Allergy and Asthma European Network (GA2LEN) cohort [28], in which patients with asthma multimorbidity exhibited more severe symptoms without corresponding elevations in type 2 markers. Paradoxically, FeNO levels were lower in the high-burden group, contrary to the anticipated positive association with allergic comorbidity burden. Several factors may explain this observation. First, FeNO predominantly reflects airway eosinophilic inflammation rather than systemic allergic burden [29, 30], and technical factors such as nasal symptoms affecting expiratory maneuver quality cannot be excluded [31, 32]. Second, the high prevalence of atopic dermatitis (90.4%) in the high-burden group, given the substantial type 2 inflammatory infiltration within the skin [33, 34] and the capacity for inflammatory cell migration across tissues [35], may suggest a redistribution pattern of immune responses between the skin and airways. Third, the wide interquartile range of FeNO indicates substantial within-group heterogeneity, with some children potentially exhibiting a predominantly non-eosinophilic airway inflammatory phenotype [36].

Airway structural remodeling driven by epithelial barrier dysfunction may represent a contributing mechanism. Epithelial damage can trigger alarmin release [thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP), interleukin (IL)−25, IL-33], promoting smooth muscle hyperplasia, basement membrane thickening, and collagen deposition [37–40], and these processes may be amplified when cumulative allergic comorbidities simultaneously compromise multiple epithelial barriers across skin, nasal mucosa, and gastrointestinal tract [35, 41]. Although the IL-4/IL-13-mediated type 2 pathway promotes IgE production, eosinophilic infiltration, and mucus hypersecretion [5], the finding that peripheral type 2 markers accounted for only 2.7% suggests that these tissue-level processes may not be fully captured by circulating biomarkers. Additional mechanisms, including neuroimmune interactions and epigenetic modifications [42, 43], may also contribute but were not assessed in this study.

The high prevalence of lung function abnormality in the high-burden group (90.4%) and the significant Cochran–Armitage trend (P = 0.043) support the potential clinical relevance of cumulative allergy burden assessment, although categorical comparisons did not reach significance, likely reflecting limited statistical power (n = 52). GINA guidelines recommend evaluating comorbidity status [44], yet a simple quantitative indicator for cumulative allergic burden is lacking. Our findings provide preliminary observational evidence for incorporating cumulative allergy burden into comprehensive phenotype assessment [36], and suggest that children with ABS ≥ 3 may warrant closer lung function monitoring; however, the clinical utility of the ABS and the applicability of this threshold require confirmation through prospective cohort studies. Subgroup analyses confirmed consistent inverse associations across all age and sex strata, with non-significant interactions; effect estimates were numerically largest in preschool children, supporting robustness in this age group despite inherent spirometric challenges in younger children. Sensitivity analyses corroborated these findings: the association persisted after excluding extreme FEV₁% values, and MMEF% as an alternative outcome yielded a consistent nonlinear pattern. The correlation matrix revealed moderate negative correlations between ABS and lung function parameters, negligible associations with demographic variables, and weak inter-correlations among inflammatory markers.

This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional, single-center design precludes causal inference. All lung function measurements were obtained at a single time point during the initial diagnostic visit; observed differences across ABS groups reflect between-group variation rather than within-individual longitudinal decline. The modest sample size (N = 264, with 52 high-burden participants) limited statistical power for certain subgroup analyses; prospective multicenter validation is warranted. Second, the statistical decomposition analysis was exploratory and constrained by the cross-sectional design, which precludes establishing temporal precedence between inflammatory markers and lung function; results should be interpreted as statistical associations rather than causal pathways, and reverse or bidirectional associations among these variables cannot be excluded. The parsimonious adjustment strategy (age and sex only) was adopted to minimize overfitting but may have introduced residual confounding, as BMI and passive smoking could affect both inflammatory markers and lung function, potentially biasing indirect contribution estimates in either direction. Third, equal ABS component weights may not reflect differential contributions of individual comorbidities, and diagnoses relied on medical records rather than prospective standardized assessments, potentially introducing misclassification bias. Fourth, unmeasured confounders—including allergen exposure intensity, air pollution, and socioeconomic factors—could not be assessed in this retrospective analysis. Additionally, the applicability of GLI 2012 reference equations to the multi-ethnic population of Xinjiang warrants cautious interpretation. Fifth, preterm birth (12.9% of participants, with no significant between-group differences) was retained as a covariate rather than an exclusion criterion. Asthma severity was not formally classified, as GINA-based grading requires longitudinal treatment response assessment; the homogeneous treatment-naive status minimizes therapy-related confounding but precludes severity stratification. The overall prevalence of FEV₁% < 80% was 78.4%, suggesting at least moderate obstruction in most participants. Future prospective studies incorporating longitudinal severity assessment are warranted. Additionally, statistical power was insufficient for certain analyses, and the generalizability of the results requires verification in multicenter studies.

Conclusions

This study identified a dose–response association between cumulative allergic comorbidity burden and lung function impairment in treatment-naive children with asthma. Type 2 inflammatory markers accounted for only a small proportion of this association, suggesting involvement of additional mechanisms. The ABS may warrant further evaluation as a simple clinical indicator for identifying children with greater lung function impairment, particularly those with three or more allergic conditions. Prospective multicenter validation is needed.
