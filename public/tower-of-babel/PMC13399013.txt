Letter to the Editor in response to the article “Association between thyroid dysfunction and cognitive impairment in type 2 diabetes mellitus: A cross‐sectional study”
Authors: Qun Fang
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

I read with great interest the cross‐sectional study by Zheng et al 1 . published in Journal of Diabetes Investigation, which concluded hypothyroidism independently raises cognitive impairment risk among patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). Although the well‐designed grouping and dose–response analysis strengthen its clinical implication, one critical methodological limitation deserves academic discussion.

The authors stratified thyroid function solely by serum TSH and FT4 without testing thyroid peroxidase antibody (TPOAb) and thyroglobulin antibody (TgAb), the core biomarkers defining autoimmune thyroid disease, the leading cause of hypothyroidism worldwide. Autoimmune inflammation not only induces thyroid hormone insufficiency but also triggers systemic low‐grade inflammation, a well‐documented mediator of neuronal injury and cognitive decline in diabetic population 2 . Unmeasured thyroid autoantibodies cannot distinguish autoimmune hypothyroidism from nonautoimmune hypothyroidism, making it unclear whether elevated cognitive risk originates from thyroid hormone deficiency or chronic autoimmune inflammation.

Moreover, existing cohort evidence has verified isolated positive thyroid autoantibodies with normal TSH/FT4 also worsen diabetic cognitive function, which the present research cannot identify and adjust as confounding factor 3 . The omission of autoantibody indicators may overestimate the independent effect of elevated TSH on cognitive impairment.

We acknowledge the study's valuable clinical suggestion to screen thyroid function routinely for T2DM patients. Supplementary thyroid autoantibody detection in future related research will refine risk stratification and clarify underlying pathogenic pathways.
