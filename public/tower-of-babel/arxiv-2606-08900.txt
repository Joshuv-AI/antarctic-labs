2
Coadjoint orbits of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})

This section describes the elliptic coadjoint orbits
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
of
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
, their identification with the Poincaré
disk and the upper half-plane, and the geometric quantization (with half-form
correction) that produces the holomorphic discrete series

\mathcal{D}^{+}_{2\mu+1}
of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

On
\mathfrak{g}
we use the invariant pseudo-Euclidean form

(X,Y):=-2\tr(XY)
and the basis

v_{1}=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad v_{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad v_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

with
(v_{1},v_{1})=(v_{2},v_{2})=-1
and
(v_{0},v_{0})=+1
.
Pairing with
v_{i}
yields the coordinate function
x_{i}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,
so this identifies
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\cong\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
.

For
\mu\geq 0
set

\mathcal{O}_{\mu}\;:=\;\bigl\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{1,2}\ \big|\ x_{0}^{2}-x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}=\mu^{2},\;x_{0}>0\,\bigr\}.

For
\mu=0
this is the future light cone (regular nilpotent orbit); for
\mu>0

it is the upper sheet of a two-sheeted hyperboloid (elliptic orbit).

The Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau symplectic form on
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}

(see
[
4
, Ch. 1]
for the general construction) is

\omega\;=\;\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{2}}{x_{0}}.

This is the
opposite
sign convention from
[
8
]
, where

\omega_{\text{MMX}}=\mathrm{d}x_{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{1}/x_{0}=-\omega
;
our choice here ensures that
\omega
pulls back to a positive multiple of the
hyperbolic area form on
\mathbb{H}
(see below).

2.1
Identification of
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
(
\mu>0
) with the upper half-plane
and geometric quantization

For
\mu>0
, let
G:=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
act on
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
via the coadjoint action and let

K:=\mathrm{SO}(2)\subset G
be the stabilizer of
(\mu,0,0)\in\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
.
The
G
-equivariant identification of
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}\cong G/K
with the
Poincaré disk
\mathbb{D}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w|<1\}
sends a point

(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
to

w\;=\;\frac{x_{1}+\mathrm{i}\,x_{2}}{x_{0}+\mu}\;\in\;\mathbb{D},

mapping the apex
(\mu,0,0)
to the origin and intertwining the coadjoint
action with the
G
-action on
\mathbb{D}
.
(Equivalently, this is the stereographic projection from
(-\mu,0,0)
,
rescaled by
1/\mu
so that the image is the
unit
disk rather than
a disk of radius
\mu
.)
Composing with the Cayley map

z\;=\;\mathrm{i}\,\frac{1+w}{1-w},

the standard biholomorphism from
\mathbb{D}
to the upper
half-plane
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Im z>0\}
, gives an identification

\mathcal{O}_{\mu}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\mathbb{H}
.

Under the composite identification
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}\cong\mathbb{H}
, the
Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau form becomes

\omega\;\longmapsto\;\omega_{\mu}:=\mu\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y}{y^{2}},\qquad z=x+\mathrm{i}y\in\mathbb{H}.

That is, the KKS form on
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
is identified with
\mu
times the standard

\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-invariant area form on
\mathbb{H}
.

Equip
\mathbb{H}
with the symplectic form
\omega_{\mu}

and the Kähler polarization given by its complex structure.
The prequantum line bundle
L_{\mu}\to\mathbb{H}
is the Hermitian line bundle
with curvature
\omega_{\mu}
. Trivializing sections as holomorphic functions
on
\mathbb{H}
, the
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
-action carries Möbius weight
2\mu
:

(g\cdot f)(z)=(cz+d)^{-2\mu}\,f\!\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right),\qquad g^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},

and the natural
L^{2}
-condition on sections of
L_{\mu}
gives the
weighted Bergman space
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{H}}_{2\mu}

(squared norm
\|f\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{H}}|f|^{2}\,y^{2\mu-2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y
).
The canonical bundle
\mathcal{K}=T^{*(1,0)}\mathbb{H}
of this polarization has sections
g(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

with weight
2
(since
\mathrm{d}((az+b)/(cz+d))=(cz+d)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}z
), so the half-form bundle
\mathcal{K}^{1/2}

carries weight
1
.
Geometric quantization with the half-form (metaplectic) correction
(see
[
17
,
19
]
for textbook
treatments) replaces
L_{\mu}
by
L_{\mu}\otimes\mathcal{K}^{1/2}
, shifting
the Möbius weight from
2\mu
to
2\mu+1
. The resulting representation is the

holomorphic discrete series representation

\mathcal{D}_{2\mu+1}^{+}
of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
(made precise in Section
4.2

below), with Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{H}}_{2\mu+1}\;=\;\Bigl\{\,f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\;\Big|\;\int_{\mathbb{H}}|f(z)|^{2}\,y^{2\mu-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty\Bigr\},

and the
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
-action is the weight-
(2\mu+1)
Möbius action lifted to
the universal cover.
Equivalently, in the Poincaré disk model
\mathbb{D}
(via the Cayley map

w=(z-\mathrm{i})/(z+\mathrm{i})
), the Hilbert space is

\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{D}}_{2\mu+1}\;=\;\Bigl\{\,g\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D})\;\Big|\;\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g(w)|^{2}\,(1-|w|^{2})^{2\mu-1}\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}v<\infty\Bigr\},\qquad w=u+\mathrm{i}v.

As
\mu\to 0^{+}
, the elliptic orbits
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
degenerate to the regular nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}
and the symplectic form

\omega_{\mu}
degenerates to
0
. On the quantum side, the holomorphic discrete series
representations
\mathcal{D}_{2\mu+1}^{+}
correspondingly degenerate to the

limit of holomorphic discrete series

\mathcal{D}_{1}^{+}
. This provides a natural

quantization of the nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}
: one first quantizes the nearby elliptic orbits
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}

to obtain the holomorphic discrete series, then passes to the limit
\mu\to 0
.

3
Classical symmetry regularization: the maps

\iota_{\pm}:\Sigma_{\pm}\to\mathcal{O}_{\pm}

We now recall the two embeddings of Ma–Meng–Xiao
[
8
]
. Both are symplectic embeddings with dense open image in their respective target orbits. Throughout
this section,
\mathcal{O}_{-}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{O}_{+}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mu/2}
, and
H
denotes
the classical Hamiltonian (
1
).

3.1
The negative-energy embedding

\iota_{-}:\Sigma_{-}\to\mathcal{O}_{-}

Define first
\tilde{\iota}_{-}:\Sigma_{-}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
by

\xi_{0}\;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{-2H}},\qquad\xi_{1}\;=\;-\,\frac{1+2Hq}{\sqrt{-2H}},\qquad\xi_{2}\;=\;qp,

(2)

a diffeomorphism onto the dense open set

U^{-}_{\mu}=\{(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{O}_{\mu}\colon x_{0}+x_{1}\neq 0\}

(equal to all of
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
when
\mu>0
). Then applying an elliptic twist
\tau_{-}
by

\varphi=\xi_{2}/\xi_{0}=\sqrt{-2H}\,qp
gives:

\iota_{-}\;=\;\bigl(\xi_{0},\;\xi_{1}\cos\varphi-\xi_{2}\sin\varphi,\;\xi_{1}\sin\varphi+\xi_{2}\cos\varphi\bigr).

(3)

The map
\iota_{-}
satisfies
\iota_{-}^{*}\omega_{\text{MMX}}=\mathrm{d}p\wedge\mathrm{d}q
(symplectic
embedding,
[
8
, Prop. 1]
); equivalently,

\iota_{-}^{*}\omega=-\mathrm{d}p\wedge\mathrm{d}q=\mathrm{d}q\wedge\mathrm{d}p
in our sign convention.
The map is one-to-one because of a lemma of Ligon–Schaaf
[
7
]

(proof given also in
[
8
]
):
\theta=A\sin(\theta+\gamma)
has a unique real
solution when
|A|\leq 1
. It is in fact an embedding onto a dense open
subset of
\mathcal{O}_{-}
, and pulls back the orbit Hamiltonian
h_{-}:=1/(-2x_{0}^{2})

to
H
:

\boxed{\;\iota_{-}^{*}h_{-}\;=\;H.\;}

(4)

Since the energy surfaces
\{h_{-}=E\}
are compact subsets of
\mathcal{O}_{-}
, the
flow is complete on
\mathcal{O}_{-}

even when
\mu=0
, where the original
Kepler flow may blow up in finite time. This is the dynamical completion; it is also a symmetry completion, since
\mathcal{O}_{-}
carries the full
G
-action.

3.2
The positive-energy embedding

\iota_{+}:\Sigma_{+}\to\mathcal{O}_{+}

In the positive case,
\iota_{+}
is the composition of three maps. First,
define
\tilde{\iota}_{+}\colon\Sigma_{+}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
by

\xi_{0}\;=\;\frac{1+2Hq}{\sqrt{2H}},\qquad\xi_{1}\;=\;qp,\qquad\xi_{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2H}}.

(5)

Then applying a hyperbolic twist
\tau_{+}
by
\varphi=\xi_{1}/\xi_{2}=\sqrt{2H}\,qp
gives:

\tau_{+}\tilde{\iota}_{+}\;=\;\bigl(\xi_{0}\cosh\varphi-\xi_{1}\sinh\varphi,\;-\xi_{0}\sinh\varphi+\xi_{1}\cosh\varphi,\;\xi_{2}\bigr).

(6)

Finally, apply the squaring map

\pi\colon(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\;\longmapsto\;\Bigl(\tfrac{x_{0}}{2},\;\tfrac{x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}}{2\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}},\;\tfrac{x_{1}x_{2}}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\Bigr).

(7)

The composition
\iota_{+}=\pi\,\tau_{+}\tilde{\iota}_{+}
lands in

\mathcal{O}_{+}=\mathcal{O}_{\mu/2}
, is symplectic (with respect to

\omega_{\text{MMX}}
), and is a smooth embedding with dense
open image (a proper subset of
\mathcal{O}_{+}
). The orbit Hamiltonian

h_{+}\;=\;\frac{1/4}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-x_{1}\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}

(whose denominator vanishes on the ray
\{x_{2}=0,\,x_{1}>0\}
, the
locus excluded from the image of
\iota_{+}
)
is pulled back to
H
:

\boxed{\;\iota_{+}^{*}h_{+}\;=\;H.\;}

(8)

This is the dynamical completion (see
[
8
]
for the detailed argument); it is also a symmetry completion, since
\mathcal{O}_{+}
carries the full
G
-action.

The role of the squaring map
\pi\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mu/2}
is essential here: without it, the image
of
\tau_{+}\tilde{\iota}_{+}
would fill only one half of
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}

(the
x_{2}>0
half), which is far from being an open dense subset.
The squaring map
\pi
, a
2{:}1
ramified cover, identifies the two halves
so that the composite
\iota_{+}
has dense open image in
\mathcal{O}_{\mu/2}
. Writing
z=x_{1}+\mathrm{i}x_{2}
, the
last two components of
\pi(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})
combine to
z^{2}/(2|z|)
; thus
the orbit parameter is halved from
\mu
to
\mu/2
, and the Casimir

x_{0}^{2}-x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}
drops from
\mu^{2}
to
\mu^{2}/4

as a side effect of the squaring.
This halving of the orbit parameter (
\mu\mapsto\mu/2
) on the positive
energy side will reappear at the quantum level as the parameter shift

\kappa\mapsto(\kappa+1)/2
.

3.3
Why “S-duality” is the right name

As emphasized in
[
8
]
, the term “regularization map” is a misnomer
when
\mu>0
:
\Sigma_{\pm}
is already dynamically complete and
\iota_{-}

is a diffeomorphism onto
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}
, so no (generalized) Kepler motion needs to be regularized.
A more accurate name is
S-duality
, in the sense familiar from
gauge and string theory: a change of variables that exchanges a
description in which a symmetry is hidden for one in which it is
manifest. As explained in
[
8
]
, the maps
\iota_{\pm}
share
two hallmarks of S-duality: (i) they turn hidden symmetries into manifest
ones, and (ii) they are implemented by a Fourier-type transform between
conjugate variables. The dynamical completion that occurs at
\mu=0
is
then a byproduct, not the defining feature, of the S-duality map.

From the quantum vantage point this is even clearer: quantization of
a symplectic manifold
M
and of a dense open submanifold
M^{\prime}\subset M

yields the
same
Hilbert space (the quantum states are insensitive to
sets of measure zero). Whatever dynamical incompleteness
M^{\prime}
may suffer is
therefore invisible to the quantization, and the name “regularization” — in
the sense of Moser
[
14
]
and Ligon–Schaaf
[
7
]
—
is unmistakably a misnomer from the quantum point of view.

4.1
The universal cover

Since

,

F=v_{2}-v_{0}
. We also write
h:=\mathrm{i}(F-E)=-2\mathrm{i}v_{0}
for the elliptic Cartan
generator and

e_{\pm}=\tfrac{1}{2}(H\pm\mathrm{i}(E+F)),\qquad[h,e_{\pm}]=\pm 2e_{\pm},

(9)

so
e_{+}
raises and
e_{-}
lowers the
\widetilde{K}
-type.
All four of the following models will be used below; standard
references for the equivalences and intertwiners between them are

[
3
,
16
]
. In what follows,
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
(no superscript)
denotes the abstract Hilbert space of
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
;

\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{Kir}}_{\kappa}
,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{Wh}}_{\kappa}
,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\kappa}
,

\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{D}}_{\kappa}
are its concrete realizations in the four models below.
The two half-line models (i)–(ii) are valid for all
\kappa>0
; the two
Bergman models (iii)–(iv) are concrete
L^{2}
-spaces only for
\kappa>1
;
for
\kappa\leq 1
they are defined by reproducing-kernel completion
(see the remark after model (iv) below).

(i) Half-line (Kirillov) model.

Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{Kir}}_{\kappa}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{>0},\,t^{\kappa-1}\,\mathrm{d}t)
. The Lie
algebra acts by

\pi_{\kappa}(H)=2t\partial_{t}+\kappa,\qquad\pi_{\kappa}(E)=\mathrm{i}t,\qquad\pi_{\kappa}(F)=\mathrm{i}\bigl(t\partial_{t}^{2}+\kappa\partial_{t}\bigr).

The (normalized) lowest-weight vector is
\phi_{0}(t)=\sqrt{2^{\kappa}/\Gamma(\kappa)}\,e^{-t}
(annihilated by
e_{-}
),
and applying the raising operator
e_{+}
produces the orthonormal basis

\phi_{n}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{n!\,2^{\kappa}}{\Gamma(n+\kappa)}}\;L_{n}^{(\kappa-1)}(2t)\,e^{-t},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,

of
\widetilde{K}
-type
\kappa+2n
. Here
L_{n}^{(\alpha)}
denotes the generalized
(associated) Laguerre polynomial of degree
n
and parameter
\alpha
, i.e. the
orthogonal polynomials on
(0,\infty)
with weight
x^{\alpha}e^{-x}

(see
[
15
, Ch. 18]
); they appear here because successive applications of
the raising operator
e_{+}
to the Gaussian-type lowest-weight vector

\phi_{0}\propto e^{-t}
generate precisely
L_{n}^{(\kappa-1)}(2t)\,e^{-t}
.

(ii) Half-line (Whittaker) model.

Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{Wh}}_{\kappa}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{>0},\,t^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}t)
, obtained
from (i) by the unitary conjugation
f\mapsto t^{\kappa/2}f
. Writing

\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(X):=t^{\kappa/2}\,\pi_{\kappa}(X)\,t^{-\kappa/2}
for the
conjugated representation, the Lie algebra acts by

\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(H)=2t\partial_{t},\qquad\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(E)=\mathrm{i}t,\qquad\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(F)=\mathrm{i}\!\left(t\partial_{t}^{2}-\frac{\kappa(\kappa-2)}{4t}\right).

The elliptic Cartan
generator
h=\mathrm{i}(F-E)
becomes a Schrödinger-type operator

\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(h)\;=\;-t\partial_{t}^{2}\;+\;\frac{\kappa(\kappa-2)}{4t}\;+\;t.

(10)

Its complete orthonormal eigenbasis
\{\hat{\phi}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, with
\hat{\phi}_{n}:=t^{\kappa/2}\phi_{n}
the image of the Kirillov basis under conjugation by
t^{\kappa/2}
, satisfies

\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(h)\,\hat{\phi}_{n}\;=\;(\kappa+2n)\,\hat{\phi}_{n},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

(11)

(iii) Bergman (upper-half-plane) model.

Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{H}}_{\kappa}
of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{H}=\{z=x+\mathrm{i}y:y>0\}

with
\|f\|^{2}_{\kappa}=\int_{\mathbb{H}}|f|^{2}\,y^{\kappa-2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty
, with
the cocycle action

(\pi_{\kappa}(g)f)(z)=(cz+d)^{-\kappa}f\!\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right),\qquad g^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

For non-integer
\kappa
, single-valuedness of
(cz+d)^{-\kappa}
requires lifting
to
\widetilde{G}
.

(iv) Bergman (Poincaré disk) model.

Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{D}}_{\kappa}
of holomorphic functions on the unit disk

\mathbb{D}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w|<1\}
with

\|f\|^{2}_{\mathbb{D},\kappa}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(w)|^{2}(1-|w|^{2})^{\kappa-2}\,\mathrm{d}^{2}w<\infty
.
The Cayley transform
w=(z-\mathrm{i})/(z+\mathrm{i})
identifies
\mathbb{H}
with
\mathbb{D}
and intertwines
(iii) with (iv); under this identification the Möbius action becomes

(\pi_{\kappa}(g)f)(w)=(\bar{\beta}w+\bar{\alpha})^{-\kappa}\,f\!\left(\frac{\alpha w+\beta}{\bar{\beta}w+\bar{\alpha}}\right),\qquad g^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\bar{\beta}&\bar{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SU}(1,1),

with
|\alpha|^{2}-|\beta|^{2}=1
. In this model the elliptic Cartan generator
h

is diagonalized by the monomial basis
\{w^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, with
w^{n}
of

\widetilde{K}
-type
\kappa+2n
; the lowest-weight vector is the constant function
1
.

Strictly, the weights
(1-|w|^{2})^{\kappa-2}
in (iv) and
y^{\kappa-2}
in (iii)
are locally integrable only for
\kappa>1
; for
0<\kappa\leq 1
both Bergman models
are understood as reproducing-kernel completions, obtained by analytic
continuation in
\kappa

[
16
]
.

5
The 1D generalized Coulomb problems

Throughout this and the remaining sections we use the
representation-theoretic parameter
\kappa>0
. For
\kappa\geq 1
it is
related to the classical magnetic charge
\mu\geq 0
by

\kappa=2\mu+1
; the range
0<\kappa<1
has no classical counterpart
but is perfectly meaningful on the quantum side, since
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}

and
H_{\kappa}
are well-defined for every
\kappa>0
.
In terms of
\kappa
, the Casimir element (in the normalization of Section
4.2
) acts on
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}

by the scalar
\Omega=\kappa(\kappa-2)/4
,
and the classical squaring
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mu/2}
(for
\mu\geq 0
)
corresponds to the representation-parameter shift

\kappa\mapsto(\kappa+1)/2
on the quantum side.

Since quantization is not a functor, the classical picture of
Sections
2
and
3
serves as a guide rather than a recipe. Following

[
10
]
, the 1D generalized Coulomb problems are introduced
directly from the unitary lowest-weight representations, via the TKK algebra.
The
universal Kepler problem
of
[
10
]
is an abstract
object attached to a Euclidean Jordan algebra
V
, encoded by its
TKK algebra. A
suitable
Poisson realization
of this TKK algebra produces a (classical) generalized Kepler problem;
likewise, a
suitable
operator realization of this TKK algebra
produces a generalized quantum Kepler problem — i.e. a generalized
Coulomb problem. For the simplest Jordan algebra
V=\mathbb{R}
the conformal algebra is

\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
, and the suitable operator realizations of the
TKK algebra are precisely the unitary lowest-weight
representations
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
. Each
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
yields a
Hamiltonian in the following
\kappa>0
family:

\boxed{\;H_{\kappa}\;=\;-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}q^{2}}\;+\;\frac{\kappa(\kappa-2)}{8q^{2}}\;-\;\frac{1}{q}.\;}

(12)

The inverse-square coefficient
\kappa(\kappa-2)/8
equals
\Omega/2
; it is
repulsive for
\kappa>2
, vanishes at
\kappa=0,2
, and is
attractive for
0<\kappa<2
.

Viewed as a symmetric operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{>0},\mathrm{d}q)
with domain

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
,
(
12
) is essentially self-adjoint only for
\kappa\geq 3
.
For
0<\kappa<3
the two Frobenius solutions of
H_{\kappa}\psi=E\psi
at
q=0
, behaving as

q^{\kappa/2}
and
q^{1-\kappa/2}
, are both square-integrable near the
origin, so a choice of boundary condition is needed. For

1\leq\kappa<3
, the canonical choice is the
Friedrichs extension

(equivalently, requiring
\psi(q)=O(q^{\kappa/2})
as
q\to 0^{+}
);
for
0<\kappa<1
, it is the
Krein (anti-Friedrichs) extension

(equivalently, requiring
\psi(q)=O(q^{1-\kappa/2})
as
q\to 0^{+}
).
In both cases, the chosen extension is the unique one whose bound-state
subspace carries
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
representation-theoretically.
Henceforth
H_{\kappa}
denotes this
canonical self-adjoint operator.

The assignment
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}\longleftrightarrow H_{\kappa}
is a bijection

\boxed{\;\begin{gathered}\bigl\{\text{unitary lowest-weight representations of }\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})\bigr\}\\
\updownarrow\;{1{:}1}\\
\bigl\{\text{1D generalized Coulomb problems}\bigr\}.\end{gathered}\;}

5.1
Spectral decomposition

H_{\kappa}
has the standard Coulomb-type spectral structure:

\sigma(H_{\kappa})\;=\;\{E_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\;\cup\;[0,\infty),\qquad E_{n}=-\frac{1/2}{(n+\kappa/2)^{2}}\nearrow 0.

Correspondingly,

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{>0},\mathrm{d}q)\;=\;\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}\,\oplus\,\widehat{\Sigma}_{+},

where
\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}
(resp.
\widehat{\Sigma}_{+}
) is the closed subspace on which

H_{\kappa}
is negative-definite (resp. positive-definite). These are the natural
quantum counterparts of the classical
\Sigma_{\pm}=\{\pm H>0\}
.
We now derive the explicit (generalized) eigenfunctions of
H_{\kappa}
that span each subspace.

The Kummer equation

The
Kummer equation
(or confluent hypergeometric equation) is

z\,w^{\prime\prime}(z)\;+\;(b-z)\,w^{\prime}(z)\;-\;a\,w(z)\;=\;0,

(13)

with parameters
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
. The solution regular at the origin is

Kummer’s function of the first kind
,

{}_{1}F_{1}(a;\,b;\,z)\;=\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}}{(b)_{n}}\,\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

(14)

where
(a)_{n}=\Gamma(a+n)/\Gamma(a)
is the Pochhammer symbol
(see
[
15
, Ch. 13]
and
[
18
]
).

Scattering eigenfunctions (continuous spectrum)

The generalized eigenvalue equation
H_{\kappa}\psi=\frac{k^{2}}{2}\psi
at positive
energy
E=k^{2}/2>0
reads

\psi^{\prime\prime}(q)\;+\;\left(k^{2}\;+\;\frac{2}{q}\;-\;\frac{\kappa(\kappa-2)/4}{q^{2}}\right)\psi(q)\;=\;0.

(15)

Substituting
\psi(q)=q^{\kappa/2}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}kq}\,f(2\mathrm{i}kq)
and writing

z=2\mathrm{i}kq
, a direct calculation reduces (
15
) to the
Kummer equation (
13
) with parameters

a\;=\;\frac{\kappa}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{k},\qquad b\;=\;\kappa.

The solution regular at the origin is
f(z)={}_{1}F_{1}(a;\,b;\,z)
.
The normalized scattering generalized eigenfunction of
H_{\kappa}
is

\psi_{E}(q)\;=\;\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}(k)\,q^{\kappa/2}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}kq}\;{}_{1}F_{1}\!\bigl(\tfrac{\kappa}{2}+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{k};\,\kappa;\,2\mathrm{i}kq\bigr),

(16)

with the Coulomb normalization constant

\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}(k)\;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,(2k)^{\kappa/2}\,e^{\pi/(2k)}\,\frac{\bigl|\Gamma(\tfrac{\kappa}{2}+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{k})\bigr|}{\Gamma(\kappa)}.

(17)

These satisfy
[
15
, Ch. 33]

\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\psi_{E(k)}(q)}\,\psi_{E(k^{\prime})}(q)\,\mathrm{d}q=\delta(k-k^{\prime})
, and the
scattering subspace

\widehat{\Sigma}_{+}
is
the closed span of
\{\psi_{E}\}_{E>0}
.

Bound-state eigenfunctions (discrete spectrum)

The eigenvalue equation
H_{\kappa}\psi=-\frac{1}{2N_{n}^{2}}\psi
at negative
energy
E_{n}=-1/(2N_{n}^{2})
, with
N_{n}:=n+\kappa/2
, is obtained from
the same ODE (
15
) by the substitution
k\mapsto-\mathrm{i}/N_{n}
.
Applying the same substitution to the scattering ansatz gives

\psi(q)=q^{\kappa/2}\,e^{-q/N_{n}}\,f(2q/N_{n})
; writing

z=2q/N_{n}
, one arrives at the Kummer equation (
13
)
with

a\;=\;-n,\qquad b\;=\;\kappa.

Since
a=-n
is a non-positive integer, the Kummer function terminates and
yields a generalized
Laguerre polynomial
:

L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(z)\;=\;\binom{n+\alpha}{n}\;{}_{1}F_{1}(-n;\;\alpha+1;\;z),\qquad n=0,1,2,\dotsc

(18)

The normalized bound-state eigenfunction is therefore

\psi_{n}(q)\;=\;\mathcal{N}_{n}\,q^{\kappa/2}\,e^{-q/N_{n}}\,L_{n}^{(\kappa-1)}(2q/N_{n}),\qquad\mathcal{N}_{n}\;=\;\frac{1}{N_{n}}\Bigl(\frac{2}{N_{n}}\Bigr)^{\!\kappa/2}\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\Gamma(n+\kappa)}},

(19)

where the normalization follows from the Laguerre orthogonality relation
[
15
, Ch. 18]

\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{\alpha}\,e^{-z}\,L_{m}^{(\alpha)}(z)\,L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\;=\;\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{n!}\,\delta_{mn},\qquad\alpha>-1.

(20)

The
bound subspace

\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}
is spanned by the orthonormal
set
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

6
Quantum symmetry regularization

The classical regularization reviewed in Section
3
suggests its quantum analog:
for each sign
\pm
, a unitary isomorphism

\hat{\iota}_{\pm}\colon\widehat{\Sigma}_{\pm}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}

under which the positive operator
|2H_{\kappa}|^{-1/2}
corresponds to
the action of an element in the complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
on the target
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}
.
Since quantization is not a functor, there is a priori no guarantee that such quantum analogues exist, nor is it clear what the relevant Lie algebra elements should be.
This question—which we call
quantum (symmetry) regularization
—can be posed independently of the classical picture.
Nevertheless, the classical picture sharpens the question: it suggests that the targets should be

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{-}=\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{+}=\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\kappa+1)/2}
, an identification we adopt throughout this paper.

With the facts reviewed in Section
4.3
and Section
5.1
, this question admits an explicit answer:

Theorem 1

(Quantum regularization)
.

Realize both targets
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}
on the common Whittaker half-line Hilbert space

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{>0},t^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}t)
, equipped respectively with the representations

\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}
for
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{-}=\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
and
\hat{\pi}_{(\kappa+1)/2}

for
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{+}=\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\kappa+1)/2}
. Let
E,F
denote the standard

\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
-basis elements of Section
4.3
.

(i) Negative-energy case.

Set
h=\mathrm{i}(F-E)
. The map
\psi_{n}\mapsto\hat{\phi}_{n}
, where

\{\psi_{n}\}
is the bound-state eigenbasis of
H_{\kappa}
on
\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}

and
\{\hat{\phi}_{n}\}
is the orthonormal
\widetilde{K}
-eigenbasis
of (
11
), extends by linearity and continuity to a unitary
isomorphism

\hat{\iota}_{-}\colon\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{-}

satisfying
\hat{\iota}_{-}^{-1}\,\hat{h}_{-}\,\hat{\iota}_{-}=H_{\kappa}
, where

\hat{h}_{-}:=-1/\bigl(2(\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(h/2))^{2}\bigr)
.

(ii) Positive-energy case.

Let
\psi_{\lambda}:=\lambda^{-1}\,\psi_{E}\big|_{E=1/(2\lambda^{2})}
denote the
scattering generalized eigenfunction (
16
) reparametrized by

\lambda=(2E)^{-1/2}>0
, so that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\psi_{\lambda}(q)}\,\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}(q)\,\mathrm{d}q=\delta(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})
. The map
\psi_{\lambda}\mapsto\sqrt{t}\,\delta(t-\lambda)

extends to a unitary isomorphism

\hat{\iota}_{+}\colon\widehat{\Sigma}_{+}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{+}

satisfying
\hat{\iota}_{+}^{-1}\,\hat{h}_{+}\,\hat{\iota}_{+}=H_{\kappa}
, where

\hat{h}_{+}:=1/\bigl(2(-\mathrm{i}\hat{\pi}_{(\kappa+1)/2}(E))^{2}\bigr)
.

Proof.

The argument in both cases rests on the same principle: two self-adjoint
operators on separable Hilbert spaces that have the same spectral type
(i.e. the same spectrum with the same multiplicity) are unitarily
equivalent, and matching (generalized) eigenfunctions with equal
eigenvalues defines the intertwining unitary isomorphism.

(i)

On
\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}
, the operator

\hat{N}_{-}=(-2H_{\kappa})^{-1/2}
is positive and self-adjoint with
purely discrete, simple spectrum

\{n+\kappa/2\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and orthonormal eigenbasis

\{\psi_{n}\}
.
On
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{-}=\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
(Whittaker model), the elliptic Cartan
generator
\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(h/2)
is self-adjoint with the same purely
discrete, simple spectrum
\{n+\kappa/2\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
orthonormal eigenbasis
\{\hat{\phi}_{n}\}

(cf. (
11
)).
Since both eigenbases are complete orthonormal systems indexed by the
same eigenvalues, the map
\psi_{n}\mapsto\hat{\phi}_{n}
extends by
linearity and continuity to a unitary isomorphism

\hat{\iota}_{-}\colon\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{-}

satisfying
\hat{\iota}_{-}^{-1}\,\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(h/2)\,\hat{\iota}_{-}=\hat{N}_{-}
.
Squaring and taking reciprocals gives

\hat{\iota}_{-}^{-1}\,\hat{h}_{-}\,\hat{\iota}_{-}=-1/(2\hat{N}_{-}^{2})=H_{\kappa}
, where

\hat{h}_{-}=-1/\bigl(2(\hat{\pi}_{\kappa}(h/2))^{2}\bigr)
.

(ii)

On
\widehat{\Sigma}_{+}
, the operator

\hat{N}_{+}=(2H_{\kappa})^{-1/2}
is positive and self-adjoint with
purely continuous, simple spectrum
(0,\infty)
and Dirac-normalized
generalized eigenfunctions
\{\psi_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
.
On
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{+}=\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\kappa+1)/2}
(Whittaker model), the self-adjoint
companion
-\mathrm{i}\hat{\pi}_{(\kappa+1)/2}(E)
acts as
multiplication by
t>0
, hence also has purely continuous, simple
spectrum
(0,\infty)
with Dirac-normalized generalized eigenfunctions

\{\sqrt{t}\,\delta(t-\lambda)\}_{\lambda>0}
.
Since both operators have the same spectral type, matching generalized
eigenfunctions at each
\lambda
defines the unitary isomorphism

\hat{\iota}_{+}\colon\widehat{\Sigma}_{+}\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{+}

satisfying
\hat{\iota}_{+}^{-1}\,(-\mathrm{i}\hat{\pi}_{(\kappa+1)/2}(E))\,\hat{\iota}_{+}=\hat{N}_{+}
.
Squaring and taking reciprocals gives

\hat{\iota}_{+}^{-1}\,\hat{h}_{+}\,\hat{\iota}_{+}=1/(2\hat{N}_{+}^{2})=H_{\kappa}
, where

\hat{h}_{+}=1/\bigl(2(-\mathrm{i}\hat{\pi}_{(\kappa+1)/2}(E))^{2}\bigr)
.
∎

This theorem is the quantum counterpart of Theorems 1 and 2
of
[
8
]
. The classical statements that
\iota_{\pm}
are
symplectic embeddings with
open dense
image are sharpened, on
quantization, to the statement that
\hat{\iota}_{\pm}
are unitary
isomorphisms with image equal to
all
of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}
.
Equivalently, the one-parameter unitary group

e^{-\mathrm{i}\tau H_{\kappa}}
(
\tau
denoting time) on
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\pm}

is conjugate, via
\hat{\iota}_{\pm}
, to

e^{-\mathrm{i}\tau\hat{h}_{\pm}}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}
.

7
Concluding remarks

On the classical side, Ma–Meng–Xiao
[
8
]
realize each generalized
Kepler phase-space half
\Sigma_{\pm}
as a dense open part of a
coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\pm}
of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
, and rename “regularization” as

S-duality
: an explicit change of variables that turns a hidden
symmetry into a manifest one, with the same flavor as the Fourier transform
between conjugate variables.

Quantizing this picture produces a strict quantum analog

\hat{\iota}_{\pm}\colon\widehat{\Sigma}_{\pm}\to\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\pm}

(Theorem
1
):
\hat{\iota}_{-}
identifies the
bound-state subspace with
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
, while
\hat{\iota}_{+}

identifies the scattering subspace with
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\kappa+1)/2}
. Their pullback identities are the precise quantum
counterparts of the classical pullbacks
\iota_{\pm}^{*}h_{\pm}=H
.

Two structural lessons stand out.

(a) Quantum S-duality parallels the classical decomposition.

Just as classically
\Sigma
splits into
\Sigma_{-}\sqcup\Sigma_{+}
,
the quantum Hilbert space splits into
\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}\oplus\widehat{\Sigma}_{+}
,
and the two pieces are mapped to
different
unitary
representations:

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{-}=\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
is the unitary lowest-weight representation onto
which
\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}
(where
H_{\kappa}
has discrete spectrum) is mapped;

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{+}=\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\kappa+1)/2}
is the unitary lowest-weight representation
onto which
\widehat{\Sigma}_{+}
(where
H_{\kappa}
has continuous spectrum) is mapped.
The distinction between scattering and bound is purely
representation-theoretic: it lies in the choice of Lie algebra
element—a nilpotent generator (
-\mathrm{i}E
) for the positive-energy case
and an elliptic generator (
h/2
) for the negative-energy case.

(b) Quantum construction is easier than classical.

The bulk of the work lies in formulating the problem in the right
framework and assembling the requisite background from representation
theory, special functions, and spectral theory.
Once this is in place, the actual construction of

\hat{\iota}_{\pm}
becomes transparent—it reduces to matching
complete families of (generalized) eigenfunctions, with no
hard techniques involved.
The classical construction of
\iota_{\pm}
, by contrast, is substantially harder.

The present analysis fits into a lineage that begins with
Fock
[
2
]
, who, at each fixed negative energy level of the
3D hydrogen atom, used momentum-space stereographic projection to

S^{3}
to exhibit concretely the hidden
\mathrm{SO}(4)
symmetry of that energy
eigenspace. Bander and
Itzykson
[
1
]
subsequently
unified these per-level
\mathrm{SO}(n+1)
actions (for the Coulomb problem in dimension
n\geq 2
) into a single irreducible
representation
D_{-}
of the larger group
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(n+1,1)
, organizing all
bound levels into one Hilbert space. Indeed, they construct a unitary isomorphism

\hat{\iota}_{-}\colon\widehat{\Sigma}_{-}\to L^{2}(S^{n})
, where
L^{2}(S^{n})
realizes the
representation
D_{-}
. Incidentally, the representation
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\kappa}
can be realized as a closed subspace of
L^{2}(S^{1})
only when
\kappa=1
, in which case it is the Hardy space (the non-negative Fourier modes)
[
5
]
. This strongly suggests that the 1D analogue of the hydrogen atom is the system with Hamiltonian
H_{1}
(i.e.
\kappa=1
, equivalently
\mu=0
).

A natural next step is to repeat this analysis for the quantum
MICZ-Kepler problems and their higher-dimensional
analogs
[
9
]
. In
[
13
]
, Meng and Zhang have already constructed the analogue of
\hat{\iota}_{-}
, for which the target is realized as the space of
L^{2}
sections of an appropriate vector bundle over the punctured Euclidean space of dimension
n
; it remains to work out the story for the scattering side, for which the group
\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(n,2)
plays the role of
\mathrm{Spin}_{0}(n+1,1)
, following the guidance of the classical regularization results in
[
11
]
.
