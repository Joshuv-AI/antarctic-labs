Pseudocomplementation in rings of continuous functions
Guram Bezhanishvili, Marcus Tressl

Abstract
. We study rings of real-valued continuous functions in terms of pseudocomplementation conditions on various lattices attached to their prime spectrum. We fully characterize pseudocomplementation in all cases and have an almost complete characterization of relative pseudocomplementation.

Primary: 54C30, 06D15 Secondary: 54G05, 54G10, 18F70

1.
Introduction

The ring
C(T)
of continuous real-valued functions on a topological space
T
is one of the most studied objects in topology
[
GiJe60
]
. It is a classic result of Gel’fand and Kolmogorov
[
GK91
, Theorem IV’]
based on earlier work of Stone
[
Sto37
]
that the Stone-Čech compactification of a completely regular space
X
can be described as the maximal spectrum of
C(T)
and of its subring
C^{*}(T)
of bounded functions. This has generated a comprehensive study of maximal spectra of the rings
C(T)
, as well as their minimal spectra, see for instance
[
GiJe60
,
HW04
]
. However, the complicated structure of full prime spectra
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T)
and
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C^{*}(T)
(as for instance in
[
Sch97
,
Tre07
]
) is investigated to a lesser degree.

We are contributing to this study in the following way. By Stone duality, prime spectra of rings are equivalently described by the lattice of their compact and open subsets. We aim to provide characterizations of variants of pseudocomplementation properties of these lattices and their order duals in terms of algebraic properties of
C(T)
and topological properties of
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T)
. For instance, we have complete characterizations for pseudocomplementation and an almost complete characterization of relative pseudocomplementation. In order to carry these out, we recall and add to the knowledge of pseudocomplementation in lattice theory. This is done in sections
3
and
4
, which we describe now.

The study of pseudocomplementation and relative pseudocomplementation has been a mainstream topic in lattice theory
[
BaDw74
,
Grä11
]
, which has important consequences in algebraic logic as such structures serve as algebraic models of various non-classical logics
[
RS63
,
Ras74
]
. In particular, there is a well-developed duality theory for such structures, which utilizes Stone duality
[
Sto37
]
and Priestley duality
[
Pri70
]
for bounded distributive lattices, the two being two sides of the same coin
[
Cor75
]
.

From Hochster’s characterization of prime spectra of commutative rings
[
Hoc69
]
, it follows that spectra of both bounded distributive lattices and of commutative rings describe the same class of topological spaces, the so-called
spectral spaces
(see
[
DST19
]
for a detailed account).

There are various characterizations of when a bounded distributive lattice
L
is pseudocomplemented or a Stone algebra using either the spectral space of
L
or its sister Priestley space (see
[
Grä63
,
ChGr69
,
Pri74
,
Pri75
]
), as well as when it is a Heyting algebra or a coHeyting algebra (see
[
Esa74
,
Esa19
,
BBGK10
,
DST19
]
).

In this paper we utilize the above results, as well as the structure of
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T)
, to obtain several characterizations of when the lattice
\mathop{\mathring{{\mathcal{K}}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T))
of compact open sets of
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T)
is pseudocomplemented or a coHeyting algebra. Our main results include the following characterizations:

Let
T
be a completely regular space. Then

•

\mathop{\mathring{{\mathcal{K}}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T))
is pseudocomplemented iff
X
is basically disconnected iff
\mathop{\mathring{{\mathcal{K}}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T))
is a Stone algebra (Theorem
5.6
). As a consequence we also show that
\mathop{\mathring{{\mathcal{K}}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T))
is pseudocomplemented iff
\mathop{\mathring{{\mathcal{K}}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C^{*}(T))
is pseudocomplemented (Corollary
5.7
)

•

If
T
is metrizable, then
\mathop{\mathring{{\mathcal{K}}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T))
is a Heyting algebra iff
X
is discrete (Corollary
5.9
).

•

In Theorem
5.2
we show that the order dual of
\mathop{\mathring{{\mathcal{K}}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T))
is pseudocomplemented iff it is a Heyting algebra iff it is a Stone algebra iff
X
is a P-space. Furthermore, all these conditions on
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T)
are equivalent to their formulations for the subspace of z-prime ideals
\mathop{\operatorname{z\text{-}Spec}}\nolimits C(T)
(see
2.4
for definitions).

On the way we will also see how known results fit into the topic of pseudocomplemntation. For instance, on the ring theoretic side we reframe known characterizations of basically disconnected spaces (
5.6
) and Baer rings (
3.7
); on the lattice theoretic side, Theorem
3.5
combines and extends various known characterizations of Stone algebras (see
3.6
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this preliminary section we briefly recall Stone and Priestley dualities for bounded distributive lattices. The former gives rise to spectral spaces, while the latter gives rise to Priestley spaces; we briefly discuss the Cornish isomorphism between the two categories. We also recall prime spectra of commutative rings. Our notation mostly follows that in
[
DST19
]
; for the reader interested in the frame theoretic approach to the matter we refer to

2.1

Spectral Spaces

The

of bounded distributive lattices states that each such lattice

is the space of maximal ideals of

The morphisms in the category

of spectral spaces are

2.2
.

Priestley spaces
A
Priestley space
is a Boolean space

equipped with a partial order
\leq
that satisfies the
Priestley separation axiom

x\not\leq y\Longrightarrow\exists D\mbox{ clopen down-set}:x\notin D\mbox{ and }y\in D.

The
Priestley representation
then states that each bounded distributive lattice

is isomorphic to the lattice of clopen down-sets of some Priestley space, which is unique up to an order-homeomorphism. In particular, if
X
is the spectral space of
L
, then
(X_{\mathrm{con}},\,{\mathchoice{{\hbox{\pxsyadisplay\char 32\relax}}}{{\hbox{\pxsyanormal\char 32\relax}}}{{\hbox{\pxsyascript\char 32\relax}}}{{\hbox{\pxsyascriptscript\char 32\relax}}}}\,)
is the Priestley space of
L
, where
X_{\mathrm{con}}
is the patch space of

and
↝
is the specialization order.

The morphisms in the category
Priestley
of Priestley spaces are continuous and order preserving maps and Priestley duality says that
Priestley
is anti-equivalent to the category of bounded distributive lattices with bounded lattice morphisms.

As is evident, there is a close connection between spectral spaces and Priestley spaces. Indeed, each spectral space

gives rise to the Priestley space

]

]
and

(2)

(3)

Pseudocomplementation in bounded distributive lattices

3.4

Normal spectral spaces and retraction maps
Normal spectral spaces are discussed in detail in

X^{\max}\hookrightarrow X
possesses a retraction

that preserves specialization.

In such a space

, the map

that sends

to the unique closed point in

is a continuous, closed, and proper retraction of the embedding
X^{\max}\hookrightarrow X

[

, Proposition 3, p. 230]

[3]
[3]

[3]

A ring whose spectrum is normal is called a

(aka a

Now note that for a subset

is closed, then

is closed as well.

For an arbitrary spectral space

5.6 Theorem
.

Let
T
be a completely regular space. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits(C(T))
is a PC-space.

(2)

\mathop{\mathring{{\mathcal{K}}}}\nolimits(\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits C(T))
is a Stone algebra.

(3)

\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits(C(T))^{\min}
is patch closed and
T
is an
F
-space, i.e.
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits(C(T))
is stranded.
[7]
[7]

[7]

See
[
GiJe60
, Theorem in 14.25]
for more information on F-spaces.

(4)

T
is basically disconnected.

(5)

The poset
C(T)
is Dedekind
\sigma
-complete.

(6)

For every
f>0
in
C(T)
, the interval
[0,f]
is pseudocomplemented.

Remark.
The equivalence of the last four conditions is not new and is recorded here to showcase the topological impact of the first two conditions.

Proof.

1
\Leftrightarrow
2
\Leftrightarrow

. The equivalence

and

have the same minimal spectrum and one of them is stranded iff the other one is. However, our assertion

Open problem.
We do not have a characterization of those completely regular spaces
T
for which
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits(C(T))
is an Esakia space. In
5.9
we have seen that metric spaces have this property only if they are discrete, but we have no example of an infinite compact Hausdorff space
T
such that
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits(C(T))
is an Esakia space. In particular, we do not know whether
\mathop{\operatorname{Spec}}\nolimits(C(\beta\mathds{N}))
is Esakia.

We also do not have a general characterization of when
\mathop{\operatorname{z\text{-}Spec}}\nolimits(C(T))
is Esakia (equivalently:
\mathop{\operatorname{Coz}}\nolimits(T)
is a Heyting algebra). This does happen frequently, e.g. when all open sets of
T
are cozero sets, like in perfectly normal spaces, but we do not have a full characterization.
