On the structure of the sandpile identity element on Sierpiński gasket graphs
Robin Kaiser, Ecaterina Sava-Huss, Julia Überbacher

Abstract
We consider the identity of the abelian sandpile group of finite approximation graphs of the Sierpiński gasket, and we show that the second-order term in the scaling limit converges to the path distance to the nearest corner on the Sierpiński gasket. The proof relies on a decomposition of the identity of the sandpile group into the sum of a constant function and the Laplacian of the graph distance on the approximating graphs.



Keywords:
Abelian sandpiles, sandpile group, identity sandpile, Sierpiński gasket, Green’s function, scaling limit, second-order limit.

31E05, 60J10, 60J45, 05C81.

Introduction

Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld
[
2
,
3
]
introduced the sandpile automaton on lattices as a system that displays self-organized criticality: it evolves into a critical state without the fine-tuning of external parameters. Dhar
[
8
]
generalized this model to arbitrary finite graphs and noticed that the dynamics of the model are abelian, which led him to refer to it as the abelian sandpile model. Because of its relevance in physics and its interesting connections to many different fields in mathematics, the abelian sandpile model has since attracted the interest of physicists and mathematicians alike.

For a finite graph
G=(V\cup\{s\},E)
with a designated sink vertex
s
, a sandpile configuration is a collection of indistinguishable particles on the vertices of
G
. We call a vertex stable, if the number of particles is smaller than its degree, and unstable otherwise. The unstable vertices can be legally toppled by sending one particle to each neighboring vertex. During a toppling, if particles are sent to the sink, they are lost forever. By using these toppling dynamics, one can define the abelian sandpile Markov chain on the space of stable sandpile configurations in the following manner. At each time step, a particle is added at a uniformly random location, and subsequently, the configuration is stabilized by performing all possible legal topplings.

Research on the abelian sandpile model spans topics ranging from the limit shape in the single-source model — where all particles are initially placed on a single vertex and the stabilized configuration is analyzed
[
22
,
20
,
10
]
— through the dynamics of the sandpile Markov chain, including its mixing behaviour and stationary distribution
[
15
,
11
,
14
]
, to the infinite-volume limit of the stationary distribution and the size of avalanches at criticality
[
12
,
1
,
5
,
9
]
.

The set of recurrent configurations for the sandpile Markov chain forms an abelian group — also known as the sandpile group or critical group — which is the focus of this note. In particular, we study the identity element of the sandpile group (shortly
sandpile identity
) on Sierpiński gasket approximation graphs. The sandpile identity has been examined on various graphs; for example,
[
19
]
analyzes it on rectangular grids under certain size constraints, and
[
21
]
investigates the identity on elliptical subsets of the square lattice. Notably, the conjecture of Dhar et al.
[
7
]
concerning a central square of vertices with two particles each in the identity on square boxes in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
remains open.

On the Sierpiński gasket, recent work has characterized and analyzed the identity element of the sandpile group. In particular,
[
6
]
describes its structure on Sierpiński gasket graphs under various boundary conditions, and
[
16
]
gives a recursive characterization of the identity on the approximating graphs.
In this paper, we focus on the scaling limit of the sandpile identity on the Sierpiński gasket, previously studied in
[
17
]
. That work shows that the piecewise-constant extensions of the identities on the approximation graphs converge in the weak* sense to a constant function on the gasket. However, the structure of the identity is lost under the weak* topology. Here, we propose a different notion of limit by convolving the sandpile identity with the stopped Green’s function. This approach retains the identity’s structure and addresses a question raised in
[
17
]
. We now present our main result. Formal definitions of the objects in the statement are deferred to Section

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathsf{id}_{n}
be the identity of the sandpile group of the

of the Sierpiński gasket

the Green function of the simple random walk on

be the Green function on the Sierpiński gasket

.

(I1)

The function

grows asymptotically as

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{5^{n}}g_{n}*\mathsf{id}_{n}^{\blacktriangle}(x)=8\int_{SG}G(x,y)d\mu(y).

The second order term of

Preliminaries

Abelian Sandpiles.
For a comprehensive overview of the abelian sandpile model, see the survey
[
13
]
. Let
G=(V\cup\{s\},E)
be a finite, connected, undirected graph with a distinguished vertex
s
(the sink). For
v\in V\cup\{s\}
, let
{\rm deg}_{G}(v)
denote its degree in
G
, i.e., the number of its neighbors; when the graph is clear from context, we write
{\rm deg}(v)
. For vertices
v,w\in V
, we write
v\sim w
if they are adjacent in
G
(connected by an edge).

A sandpile on
G
is a function
\sigma:V\to\mathbb{N}
that assigns to each vertex
v\in V
the number of particles at
v
. A vertex
v
is unstable if
\sigma(v)\geq{\rm deg}(v)
; otherwise it is stable. The configuration
\sigma
is called stable if it is stable at every vertex. For each vertex
v\in V
, define a toppling
T_{v}
at
v
by removing
{\rm deg}(v)
particles from
v
and sending one particle along each incident edge. Writing
a_{vw}
for the number of edges between
v
and
w
, the toppling redistributes particles as follows:

(T_{v}\sigma)(u)=\begin{cases}\sigma(u)-{\rm deg}(v),&u=v,\\
\sigma(u)+a_{vu},&u\sim v,\\
\sigma(u),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

A toppling is legal if it results in a configuration with all nonnegative entries, i.e., if the toppled vertex
v
is unstable in
\sigma
. Any particles sent to the sink are permanently removed from the system. We view
\sigma
as a vector indexed by
V
, with each entry recording the number of particles at the corresponding vertex. With this identification, toppling operations can be expressed using the graph Laplacian of
G
. The Laplacian
\Delta\in{\rm Mat}_{|V\cup\{s\}|\times|V\cup\{s\}|}(\mathbb{Z})
is the matrix with entries

\Delta_{vw}=\begin{cases}-a_{vw},&v\neq w,\ v\sim w,\\
{\rm deg}(v),&v=w,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
\Delta^{\prime}
denote the reduced Laplacian of
G
with respect to
s
, obtained by restricting
\Delta
to
V\times V
(i.e., deleting the row and column corresponding to the sink). Then a toppling at
v
is given by

T_{v}\sigma=\sigma-\Delta^{\prime}\delta_{v},

where
\delta_{v}
is the vector with a
1
at index
v
and
0
elsewhere. This formulation immediately shows that topplings commute (the model is abelian):

T_{v}T_{w}\sigma=T_{w}T_{v}\sigma\quad\text{for all }v,w\in V.

The stabilization
\sigma^{\circ}
of a sandpile
\sigma
is the unique stable configuration obtained by performing all legal topplings. This is well defined: there exists a sequence of vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
— unique up to permutation — such that all topplings are legal and the resulting configuration
\sigma^{\circ}=T_{v_{n}}\cdots T_{v_{1}}\sigma
is stable. See
[
13
]
for details.

Sandpile Markov chain and the sandpile group.
Given sandpiles and stabilization, one defines a Markov chain on the set of stable configurations of
G
, known as the abelian sandpile Markov chain, as follows.
Let
(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be i.i.d. uniformly distributed on
V
, and start from any stable configuration
\sigma_{0}
. Define the Markov chain
(\sigma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by

\sigma_{n+1}=(\sigma_{n}+\delta_{X_{n}})^{\circ},

i.e., at time-step
n
, given
\sigma_{n}
, we add one particle to a uniformly chosen vertex in
G
and then we stabilize
\sigma_{n}+\delta_{X_{n}}
. The chain
(\sigma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is called
the abelian sandpile Markov chain
, and its set of recurrent states forms an abelian group denoted
\mathcal{R}_{G}
, where the group operation

\oplus:\mathcal{R}_{G}\times\mathcal{R}_{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{G}
is the pointwise addition followed by stabilization
\sigma\oplus\omega=(\sigma+\omega)^{\circ}
. Since
\mathcal{R}_{G}
is an abelian group, it has a neutral element under
\oplus
: a recurrent configuration
\mathsf{id}_{G}
, called the sandpile identity of
G
.

Green function on
SG_{n}
.
Let
(X_{t}^{n})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
denote the simple random walk on

p(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4},\quad\text{if }x\sim_{n}y,\,x\neq u_{i}\\

Piecewise constant continuation to

Green function on
SG
.
On the Sierpiński gasket

4
Scaling limit of the sandpile identity

We prove the three parts of Theorem
1.1
separately, beginning with
(I2)
. This case follows immediately from the decomposition of the sandpile identity established in Proposition
3.1
.

Let
d_{SG}(x,y)
denote the path distance between points
x,y\in SG
. Write

d(x)=\inf\{\,d_{SG}(x,u_{1}),\,d_{SG}(x,u_{2}),\,d_{SG}(x,u_{3})\,\}.

Proposition 4.1
.

It holds

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\Bigl(g_{n}*\mathsf{id}_{n}(x)-\frac{8}{3}h_{n}(x)\Bigr)^{\blacktriangle}=-\frac{1}{3}d(x).

Proof.

In view of Proposition
3.1

g_{n}*\mathsf{id}_{n}(x)-\frac{8}{3}h_{n}(x)=-\frac{1}{3}d_{n}(x),

for all
x\in V_{SG_{n}}
, from which the claim follows by using

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\Bigl(g_{n}*\mathsf{id}_{n}(x)-\frac{8}{3}h_{n}(x)\Bigr)^{\blacktriangle}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{3\cdot 2^{n}}d_{n}(x)^{\blacktriangle}=-\frac{1}{3}d(x).

∎

We now address statement
(I3)
of Theorem
1.1
. The first step is to establish that the functions
h_{n}
provide a good approximation to the integral of the Green function
G
over the Sierpiński gasket. This is proved in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For the Green function
G
on the Sierpiński gasket
SG
and the discrete Green function
g_{n}
on the
n
-th approximation graph
SG_{n}
, the following holds for all
x\in V_{*}
:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Biggl|\,3\cdot 5^{n}\int_{SG}G(x,y)\,d\mu(y)\;-\;\sum_{y\in V_{SG_{n}}}g_{n}(x,y)\,\Biggr|<c_{*}

for some constant
c_{*}>0
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and partition
SG
into balls
B(y,2^{-n-1})
centered at
y\in SG_{n}
. Let
x\in V_{*}
be arbitrary. If
x\notin B(y,2^{-n-1})
, then by the harmonicity of
G(x,\cdot)
on
B(y,2^{-n-1})
,

3^{n+1}\int_{B(y,2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)\,d\mu(z)=G(x,y).

Since
x\in V_{*}
, for all sufficiently large
n
we have
x\in SG_{n}
. By Lemma
2.1
and the ball decomposition,

\displaystyle 3^{n+1}\int_{SG}G(x,z)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in SG_{n}}3^{n+1}\int_{B(y,2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=3^{n+1}\int_{B(x,2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)d\mu(z)+\sum_{y\in SG_{n}\backslash\{x\}}3^{n+1}\int_{B(y,2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=3^{n+1}\int_{B(x,2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)d\mu(z)+\sum_{y\in V_{SG_{n}}\backslash\{x\}}G(x,y)

\displaystyle=3^{n+1}\int_{B(x,2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)d\mu(z)+\bigg(\frac{3}{5}\bigg)^{n}\sum_{y\in V_{SG_{n}}\backslash\{x\}}g_{n}(x,y),

which implies

3\cdot 5^{n}\int_{SG}G(x,z)d\mu(z)-\sum_{y\in V_{SG_{n}}}g_{n}(x,y)=3\cdot 5^{n}\int_{B(x,2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)d\mu(z)-g_{n}(x,x).

On the other hand

g_{n}(x,x)=3^{n+1}\int_{B(x,2^{-n-1})}g_{n}(x,x)d\mu(z)=3\cdot 5^{n}\int_{B(x,2^{-n-1})}G(x,x)d\mu(z).

The Green function on
SG
is in the domain of the energy form, and this gives

\big|G(x,z)-G(x,x)\bigr|\leq c\Bigl(\frac{3}{5}\Bigr)^{n},

for
z\in B(x,2^{-n-1})
, where
c>0
is a constant (see
[
23
, Chapter 1.6]
). Combining the above

\Bigl|3\cdot 5^{n}\int_{SG}G(x,z)d\mu(z)-\sum_{y\in V_{SG_{n}}}g_{n}(x,y)\Bigr|<c_{*}

for some constant
c_{*}>0
.
∎

We now prove
(I3)
in Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For all
x\in V_{*}
it holds

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\biggl(g_{n}*\mathsf{id}_{n}(x)-8\cdot 5^{n}\int_{SG}G(x,y)d\mu(y)\biggr)=-\frac{1}{3}d(x).

Proof.

Let
x\in V_{*}
, then by Proposition
3.1

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\biggl(g_{n}*\mathsf{id}_{n}(x)-

\displaystyle 8\cdot 5^{n}\int_{SG}G(x,y)d\mu(y)\biggr)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3\cdot 2^{n}}d_{n}(x)+\frac{8}{3\cdot 2^{n}}\bigg(h_{n}(x)-3\cdot 5^{n}\int_{SG}G(x,y)d\mu(y)\bigg).

Lemma
4.1
gives

\frac{8}{3\cdot 2^{n}}\bigg|h_{n}(x)-3\cdot 5^{n}\int_{SG}G(x,y)d\mu(y)\bigg|\leq c_{*}\frac{8}{3\cdot 2^{n}}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0,

which together with
\lim_{n}\frac{d_{n}(x)}{2^{n}}=d(x)
yield the claim.
∎

In order to prove

in Theorem

Lemma 4.2

For all
x\in SG

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{5^{n}}h_{n}^{\blacktriangle}(x)=3\int_{SG}G(x,y)d\mu(y).

4.1

the claim, so we consider the case

\displaystyle\biggl|h_{n}(x_{n})-\bigg(\frac{5}{3}\bigg)^{n}\sum_{y\in V_{SG_{n}}}G(x,y)\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{5}{3}\bigg)^{n}\sum_{y\in V_{SG_{n}}}\bigl|G(x_{n},y)-G(x,y)\bigr|\leq c,

for some constant
c>0
, and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3^{n}}\sum_{y\in SG_{n}}G(x,y)=3\int_{SG\backslash B(x_{n},2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)d\mu(z)+\frac{1}{3^{n}}G(x,x_{n}).

Moreover

\displaystyle\biggl|\frac{1}{3^{n}}G(x_{n},x)-3\int_{B(x_{n},2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)d\mu(z)\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{B(x_{n},2^{-n-1})}\bigl|G(x_{n},x)-G(x,z)\bigr|d\mu(z)<c\frac{1}{5^{n}}.

All together, we get

\displaystyle\biggl|\frac{1}{5^{n}}h_{n}^{\blacktriangle}(x)-3\int_{SG}G(x,z)d\mu(z)\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{5^{n}}\biggl|h_{n}(x_{n})-\Big(\frac{5}{3}\Big)^{n}\sum_{y\in V_{SG_{n}}}G(x,y)\biggr|

\displaystyle+\biggl|\Big(\frac{1}{3}\Big)^{n}\sum_{y\in V_{SG_{n}}}G(x,y)-3\int_{SG}G(x,z)d\mu(z)\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{5^{n}}+\biggl|\frac{1}{3^{n}}G(x_{n},x)-3\int_{B(x_{n},2^{-n-1})}G(x,z)d\mu(z)\biggr|\leq\frac{2c}{5^{n}},

from which the claim follows as
n\to\infty
.
∎

The proof of
(I1)
in Theorem
1.1
is now straightforward.

Proposition 4.3
.

The function
g_{n}*\mathsf{id}_{n}
grows asymptotically as
5^{n}
, and for all
x\in SG
it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{5^{n}}g_{n}*\mathsf{id}_{n}^{\blacktriangle}(x)=8\int_{SG}G(x,y)d\mu(y).

Proof.

Recall Proposition
3.1
.
Since
d_{n}
is the graph distance to the closest corner in
SG_{n}
, it grows asymptotically as
2^{n}
, which implies

\frac{1}{5^{n}}d_{n}^{\blacktriangle}(x)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0
,
for all
x\in SG
. This together with Lemma
4.2
shows

\frac{1}{5^{n}}h_{n}^{\blacktriangle}(x)\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}3\int_{SG}G(x,y)d\mu(y),

from which the claim now follows.
∎

Remark 1
.

In this work, we examined the sequence of identity elements in the sandpile group on the Sierpiński gasket approximation graphs and showed that, after smoothing via convolution with the Green operator, the identity admits both first- and second-order scaling limits. The first-order limit is the integral of the Green function on the Sierpiński gasket, while the second-order limit is the distance to the gasket’s corner vertices.
An intriguing question is whether the emergence of the distance function in the Sierpiński gasket case is incidental and fails for other graph sequences, or whether a decomposition analogous to Proposition
3.1
also exists more broadly. Identifying such a structure for sandpile identities on more general graphs would allow to analyze scaling limits beyond the Sierpiński gasket, across a wider range of state spaces.
