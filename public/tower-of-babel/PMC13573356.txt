Data analysis tool for identifying multidimensional health profiles associated with frailty in older adults
Authors: Alondra Sánchez-Salinas, Anilu Franco-Arcega, Manuel A Ojeda-Misses, Lilian E Bosques-Brugada, Christian J Flores-Pérez, Aldo Márquez-Grajales
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundFrailty in older adults is a multidimensional condition associated with physical, psychological, and sociodemographic factors that may affect functionality and quality of life during aging. The analysis of these factors often requires the management of heterogeneous information, which can represent a challenge for specialists during evaluation and follow-up processes. In this context, computational tools and data-driven approaches may support the organization and exploratory analysis of multidimensional health information. This study presents the development of a web-based computational system designed to support specialists in the collection, management, and analysis of health-related data from older adults in the State of Hidalgo, México.MethodA web platform was developed to register and organize sociodemographic, psychological, and physical information from older adults. The system integrated questionnaires, physical measurements, statistical analysis tools, and computational intelligence techniques within a single environment. Data from 210 participants were collected and analyzed using exploratory statistical methods, feature selection techniques, and K-means clustering to identify patterns associated with functional and health-related characteristics. Internal validation metrics, including the Silhouette coefficient, Davies-Bouldin Index, and Calinski-Harabasz Index, were used to support cluster evaluation.ResultsThe proposed system facilitated the structured storage, visualization, and exploratory analysis of multidimensional health information. The clustering analysis identified two differentiated groups characterized by distinct physical and psychological profiles. Differences were observed in variables such as muscular strength, calf circumference, body mass index, gratitude, and functional self-perception. The interpretation of the obtained profiles considered the demographic composition of the groups, particularly the predominance of women in one cluster, which may influence physical measurements associated with aging. Additionally, the usability assessment conducted with specialists using the SUS instrument indicated an acceptable level of usability for exploratory and research support purposes.ConclusionsThe results suggest that integrating computational intelligence and statistical analysis techniques into a unified platform may support the exploratory assessment of multidimensional factors related to aging and frailty in older adults. The proposed system does not aim to establish clinical diagnoses, but rather to provide specialists with an additional resource for organizing and interpreting complex health-related information. This approach may contribute to the development of data-driven tools to support gerontological research and comprehensive evaluation processes in older populations.Trial registrationNot applicable.Keywords: Clustering analysis, Clinical decision support system, Medical informatics, Web-based health system, Older adults, Frailty

Introduction

The rapid growth of the older adult population represents a significant challenge for healthcare systems, particularly in the comprehensive assessment of health status, which involves complex interactions among personal information, sociodemographic data, physical data, and diagnostic information based on the patient’s results factors. In this context, it is essential to develop strategies that allow early identification of vulnerability conditions in order to improve prevention and care.

Frailty in older adults is a geriatric syndrome characterized by a decrease in physiological reserve and resistance to stressors, which increases vulnerability to adverse health events. According to the frailty phenotype proposed by Fried et al. [1], this condition is characterized by unintentional weight loss, exhaustion, muscle weakness, slow walking speed, and low physical activity. Frailty is associated with disability, hospitalization, and mortality, as well as deterioration in psychological well-being and increased depressive symptoms. In this sense, frailty represents one of several relevant conditions that can be considered when analyzing the overall health status of older adults.

In addition to physical conditions, mental health factors such as emotional state, social support, and the presence of depressive and anxious symptoms also play an important role in the overall health of older adults. These factors may interact with physical health conditions, influencing their progression and outcomes. However, integrating these multidimensional aspects into a unified analytical framework remains a dafy.

New technologies have addressed numerous contemporary problems, particularly through disciplines such as Computational Intelligence, which includes Data Mining, Deep Learning, Machine Learning (ML), and Pattern Recognition [2]. These approaches have enabled the development of computer systems that support decision-making processes by analyzing data to generate, estimate, evaluate, and systematically compare alternatives [3, 4]. In the healthcare field, such systems have been applied to the pre-diagnosis of chronic diseases [5], detection of heart diseases [6], and the analysis of mental health-related outcomes, including suicidal behavior [7].

Despite these advances, many existing approaches focus on specific conditions, limiting their ability to capture the multidimensional nature of health in older adults. In particular, there is a need for tools that allow specialists to collect and analyze heterogeneous data in an integrated manner, facilitating a more comprehensive understanding of patient conditions.

In this context, this work addresses a relevant social and clinical problem by proposing the development of a computational tool that allows specialists to collect and analyze multidimensional information from older adults. The system integrates variables related to physical health, mental health, and sociodemographic conditions, supporting the identification of patterns and profiles within the collected data.

Although the system was initially designed to explore mental health risk factors, including suicidal ideation, its application in a population of older adults attending gerontological centers showed low variability in these indicators. However, the analysis revealed the presence of differentiated health profiles, which were interpreted by specialists as reflecting different levels of vulnerability, potentially associated with frailty.

Therefore, the objective of this study is to develop and evaluate a computational tool capable of identifying multidimensional health profiles in older adults through data analysis techniques, and to explore how these profiles may reflect different levels of vulnerability, including conditions potentially associated with frailty.

In this context, the study seeks to answer how multidimensional data analysis can support the identification of health profiles in older adults, and whether these profiles reflect differences in vulnerability associated with conditions such as frailty.

Methods

Study design

This study follows an observational and exploratory design, combined with the development and application of a computational system. The research focuses on the analysis of multidimensional data collected from older adults in order to identify patterns and profiles within the population.

Participants and data collection

The study included a sample of 210 older adults who attended gerontological centers in Mexico. Participants voluntarily agreed to provide their information and signed an informed consent form prior to their inclusion in the study.

Data were collected through the developed system, which integrates multiple instruments and questionnaires designed to capture information related to physical health, psychological status, and sociodemographic characteristics.

Variables and instruments

The system initially collected a total of 96 variables, including physical, psychological, and sociodemographic factors. These variables were obtained through structured questionnaires and validated instruments related to mental health, emotional state, and self-perception, all of which were designed for older adults and are described in Section “Specialist specifications”.

To improve the interpretability and consistency of the analysis, the dataset was reduced to 44 variables prior to the clustering process. This reduction was performed by retaining the total scores of the psychological evaluation instruments while excluding the individual item responses associated with each questionnaire. In this way, the analysis focused on the overall instrument outcomes rather than on each individual question, reducing dimensionality and avoiding redundancy among highly related variables.

Data analysis

Descriptive statistical analyses were performed to characterize the study population. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of the variables and to determine whether parametric or non-parametric statistical tests were appropriate.

To explore relationships between variables, correlation analyses and graphical methods such as scatter plots and box plots were used.

To identify patterns within the data, a clustering approach based on the K-Means algorithm was applied. This method groups individuals into clusters according to similarity in their characteristics, allowing the identification of distinct health profiles.

The optimal number of clusters was initially explored using the elbow method, which evaluates the reduction of intra-cluster variance as the number of clusters increases. Additionally, internal validation metrics were considered to support the selection of the final number of clusters.

Finally, differences between the identified groups were analyzed using appropriate statistical tests depending on the distribution of the data.

Usability evaluation

The usability of the system was evaluated using the System Usability Scale (SUS) [17], a standardized instrument for assessing perceived usability. A group of gerontology specialists participated in this evaluation. The results of this assessment are presented in Section “System validation”.

Development

Software development methodology

This section describes the design and development of the computational system used to support data collection and analysis in this study.

To carry out this project, an agile approach combining Kaizen and Kanban principles was adopted. Kaizen supports continuous improvement [18], while Kanban facilitates workflow management and task tracking [18, 19]. This combination enabled iterative development and timely adjustments throughout the project.

The resulting methodology is agile and comprises three main phases, each with its own sub-phases, as shown in Fig. 1. Next, each one is detailed.

Proposed Kaizen-Kanban methodology (own elaboration)

Planning phase

During this phase, various preliminary activities were carried out before development to avoid delays and errors, and to identify the needs the system must satisfy. This phase, in turn, contains three subphases that support the execution of the planning.

Additionally, a series of tasks was developed to support the process and serve as a guide for visualizing a draft of how the system should look and work, including creating use case diagrams, entity-relationship diagrams, and wireframes.

The tasks to achieve the desired goal were identified, including system design, development, integration of data analysis tools, and validation.

Requirements analysis

The system was developed to help health science specialists (gerontology and psychology) improve previously manual processes. Therefore, a requirements analysis was conducted with the specialists, during which it was determined which essential features the system should have, and a list of activities was created to meet those requirements.

Data collection

Based on studies conducted by specialists, the collected data must be properly processed for subsequent analysis, which involves integration, selection, cleaning, and transformation.

Execute phase

This phase is particularly related to system development, as it includes two subphases, each with a list of activities to be completed. In the Pending section, activities that have not yet been completed are listed, and in the In-Progress subphase, activities that are currently in progress are listed. This categorization ensures that the Project Manager can monitor the development team’s time spent on activities and promptly address any issues that may affect the schedule.

Test phase

In this phase, as the name suggests, the system’s sections are tested both separately and together. It is divided into three subphases: (i) validation, during which functionality tests are performed on the system section; if the results are positive, the development activity is considered complete; otherwise, it is sent to the adjustment section. (ii) adjust, where the functions that failed the validation subphase are carefully reviewed, and the necessary changes are made to test them again. Finally, (iii) the system’s validated features are listed. Once all activities in this section are complete, the system is ready.

The integration of the system’s main components, including databases and files, was implemented on the Microsoft Azure platform, which provides a wide range of cloud computing services.

The system consists of several interfaces through which the user can connect to the database. One of the main interfaces is the login screen, shown in Fig. 6a. To access all the system’s processes, authentication is required.

Case study: Multidimensional health profiles associated with frailty identification in older age group in the state of Hidalgo, México

Using the developed system, data were collected from a sample of 210 older adults from several municipalities in the state of Hidalgo, including Pachuca de Soto, Tula de Allende, Mineral de la Reforma, and Zempoala.

Information related to sociodemographic, physical, and psychological characteristics was obtained. A total of 96 variables were initially collected, as described in Section “Specialist specifications”. After preprocessing and feature selection, 44 variables were retained for analysis. In the case of psychological instruments, only the overall scores were considered, excluding individual item responses to reduce dimensionality and improve interpretability.

Results

Group 0 is characterized by a lower average age and higher values in calf circumference and muscular strength, suggesting better preservation of physical capacity. These differences are statistically significant (p < 0.05), reinforcing the distinction between groups.

Discussion

The results obtained in this study suggest that the proposed computational system can support the organization and exploratory analysis of multidimensional information related to older adults. Through the integration of sociodemographic, physical, and psychological variables, the platform facilitated the identification of differentiated profiles associated with functional status and potential vulnerability conditions.

The clustering process revealed two groups with distinct characteristics. However, the interpretation of these groups requires caution, particularly regarding physical variables such as body mass index (BMI), calf circumference, and muscular strength. Although Group 1 presented lower average values in these indicators, this group was composed predominantly of women (94.4%), while Group 0 showed a more balanced sex distribution. Therefore, differences in muscular strength and body composition should not be interpreted directly as indicators of deterioration or pathological conditions, since these measures are naturally influenced by biological sex differences and age-related physiological changes.

In this sense, the lower average values observed in calf circumference, muscular strength, and BMI in Group 1 may be partially explained by the predominance of female participants in this cluster rather than by the presence of severe frailty-related impairments. Sex-related physiological differences influence body composition and grip strength measurements in older populations, since women commonly present lower average muscular strength and muscle mass than men. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted within the demographic composition of the groups and not exclusively as indicators of functional decline [37].

Also, Group 1 showed higher scores in variables related to body perception, functional self-perception, and gratitude. These findings may reflect a more favorable psychological adaptation to aging in women and suggest that subjective well-being does not always correspond directly to physical performance measures. This observation is consistent with studies indicating that psychological and emotional resources can contribute positively to the perception of health and functionality during aging [38].

On the other hand, Group 0 showed greater muscular strength and calf circumference, which are commonly associated with better preservation of physical function. Nevertheless, this group also presented higher BMI values, suggesting that physical robustness alone may not fully represent a balanced health condition in older adults. Consequently, the interpretation performed by the specialists considered the combined interaction of physical and psychological variables instead of relying on isolated indicators.

Compared with previous computational systems designed for older adult support, the proposed platform emphasizes the integration of sociodemographic, physical, and psychological variables within a unified exploratory analysis environment. Previous approaches [39] have focused primarily on semantic nutritional recommendation systems for older adults, while other proposals have incorporated machine learning techniques for emotional self-regulation and mental health support in aging populations [40]. In contrast, the present work prioritizes the exploratory identification of multidimensional health profiles associated with frailty-related conditions.

Additionally, previous studies have frequently emphasized predictive performance or recommendation generation, whereas the proposed system incorporates clustering techniques, variable selection strategies, and visual exploratory analysis tools intended to support interpretability and specialist-oriented analysis. This approach may facilitate the practical exploration of heterogeneous aging-related conditions within gerontological research contexts.

Other studies have explored the integration of explainable artificial intelligence and clinical decision support systems in geriatric contexts. One of these studies developed a clinical decision support system focused on nutrition-related geriatric syndromes, including malnutrition, dehydration, dysphagia, and eating disorders associated with dementia. Their proposal incorporated explainable artificial intelligence techniques and fuzzy logic to support diagnostic assessment and was validated against expert geriatricians in hospital environments [41].

Unlike approaches centered on direct diagnostic support, the present work focused on the exploratory identification of multidimensional health profiles associated with frailty-related characteristics in older adults. Rather than generating automated diagnostic decisions, the proposed system emphasizes data organization, exploratory clustering analysis, variable selection, and interpretability-oriented analysis to support specialists during gerontological assessment processes. Additionally, while previous systems have primarily focused on specific geriatric syndromes in clinical settings, this study explored heterogeneous multidimensional variables in community-dwelling older adults from the Mexican population.

These differences suggest that the proposed platform may complement existing geriatric decision support systems by providing an exploratory framework for analyzing multidimensional health information associated with aging, functionality, and potential vulnerability conditions.

An important contribution of this work lies in the integration of computational intelligence techniques into a system specifically designed to support specialists during data collection and exploratory analysis. This proposal emphasizes interpretability and practical usability within gerontological research contexts. The incorporation of statistical tools, clustering techniques, and variable selection methods within a single platform facilitates the analysis process and may reduce the complexity associated with handling large volumes of heterogeneous information.

Additionally, the usability evaluation obtained through the SUS instrument yielded an acceptable score, suggesting that the platform can be incorporated into research and clinical support activities without requiring advanced technical expertise from users. This aspect is particularly relevant in multidisciplinary environments where health specialists interact with technological systems.

Despite these contributions, this study presents some limitations. First, the sample was obtained from older adults participating in gerontological and health-related activities, which may introduce a selection bias toward individuals with greater interest in maintaining their well-being. Second, the clustering results correspond to exploratory groupings generated from the available data and should not be interpreted as clinically validated diagnostic categories. Finally, the relatively low Silhouette values indicate that the separation between groups is moderate, suggesting that aging-related conditions may present overlapping characteristics that are difficult to separate completely using unsupervised methods.

Although the usability evaluation based on the SUS instrument yielded acceptable results, the assessment was conducted with a limited number of specialists involved in the project. Therefore, the usability findings should be interpreted as a preliminary evaluation of the platform rather than as a definitive validation of user experience in broader clinical environments. Future studies should incorporate larger and more diverse groups of users to obtain a more comprehensive assessment of system usability and acceptance, where users can provide feedback on the design and the tools that could be included.

Future work may include the incorporation of longitudinal analyses, larger and more heterogeneous populations, and supervised learning approaches to evaluate predictive capabilities related to frailty progression and functional decline. Furthermore, integrating explainable artificial intelligence techniques could improve the interpretability of computational results for specialists in gerontology and mental health.

Conclusions

This work presented the development and exploratory evaluation of a computational system designed to support the collection, organization, and analysis of multidimensional information related to older adults. By integrating sociodemographic, physical, and psychological variables into a single platform, the proposed system facilitated the identification of differentiated profiles associated with functional characteristics and aging conditions within the studied population.

The application of clustering and feature selection techniques allowed the identification of two groups with distinct characteristics. The obtained profiles reflected differences not only in physical variables such as muscular strength, calf circumference, and BMI, but also in psychological dimensions related to body perception, functional self-perception, and gratitude. However, the interpretation of these findings must consider the demographic composition of the groups, particularly the predominance of women in one cluster, since sex-related physiological differences may influence body composition and muscular strength measurements.

The results obtained suggest that the integration of computational intelligence techniques into gerontological research may provide useful support for the exploratory analysis of complex datasets. In this sense, the proposed system does not aim to replace clinical judgment or establish diagnostic classifications, but rather to provide specialists with an additional tool that facilitates data interpretation and supports comprehensive evaluation processes.

From a technological perspective, the platform demonstrated the feasibility of integrating statistical analysis, clustering methods, and usability-oriented interfaces into a single environment adapted to the needs of specialists working with older populations. Furthermore, the usability assessment performed through the SUS instrument indicated an acceptable level of user acceptance, suggesting the potential applicability of the system in research and professional practice contexts, although broader evaluations are still required.

The findings of this work also highlight the importance of combining physical and psychological variables when analyzing aging-related conditions. The observed profiles suggest that favorable psychological perceptions may coexist with physical changes commonly associated with aging, reinforcing the need for multidimensional approaches in gerontological assessment.

Finally, this study contributes to the growing field of computational tools applied to healthy and active aging. Future work may focus on incorporating larger and more diverse populations, longitudinal analyses, and explainable artificial intelligence approaches to improve the interpretability and practical applicability of the generated results.
