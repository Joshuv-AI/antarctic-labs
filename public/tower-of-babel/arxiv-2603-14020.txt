Entropy Maximization and Weak Gibbsianity of Quasi-Free Fermionic States
Vojkan Jakšić, Claude-Alain Pillet, Anna Szczepanek

Abstract




Abstract.

In their 1972 study of
approach to equilibrium,
Lanford and Robinson
showed that gauge-invariant quasi-free states of lattice fermions maximize
entropy among all translation-invariant states with a fixed two-point function,
and suggested that the maximizer is unique. In subsequent work on this topic,
the uniqueness question re-emerged, together with the problem of whether such
quasi-free states are weak Gibbs states. We provide a positive answer to both
questions within a class of states whose momentum-space two-point function

\widehat{C}
satisfies
0<\widehat{C}(k)<1
and belongs to the Wiener algebra
of the Brillouin zone. The proof reveals that both the entropy maximization
principle and weak Gibbsianity follow directly from the thermodynamic formalism
for lattice fermions.

Introduction

In their 1972 study of approach to equilibrium of free fermionic
systems
[
LR
]
, Lanford and Robinson proved, under natural
thermodynamic assumptions, that among all translation-invariant states with
fixed two-point function, the specific entropy is maximized by gauge-invariant
quasi-free states. They further suggested that this maximizer should be unique;
see Proposition 1a and the end of Section A in
[
LR
]
.

We establish this conjecture for systems on the lattice
{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}
, under a
natural regularity assumption on their two-point function. We also prove the
related conjecture that these maximizers are weak Gibbs states. Both
results follow directly from the thermodynamic formalism for lattice fermions
developed by Araki–Moriya
[
AM
]
and from the perspective on weak
Gibbsianity introduced in
[
JPT
,
JPST
]
. These
observations fit naturally into the research program initiated in the latter
works. Since the arguments are conceptually simple and may be of independent
interest, we present them here in a self-contained form.

Lattice Fermions

2.1
Quasi-Free States

We describe gauge-invariant quasi-free states of the CAR algebra over
an arbitrary Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{h}}
.

\mathop{\mathrm{CAR}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})
is the
C^{\ast}
-algebra generated by the creation/annihilation
operators
a^{\ast}(f)/a(f)
associated to elements
f\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and satisfying
the anti-commutation relations

\{a(f),a^{\ast}(g)\}=\langle f,g\rangle,\qquad\{a(f),a(g)\}=0,

for all
f,g\in{\mathfrak{h}}
. Let
h
be a (possibly unbounded) self-adjoint operator on
{\mathfrak{h}}
.
The maps

\tau_{h}^{t}(a(f))=a(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}th}f)

uniquely extend to a strongly continuous group
\left(\tau_{h}^{t}\right)_{t\in{\mathbbm{R}}}

of
\ast
-automorphisms of
\mathop{\mathrm{CAR}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})
, the group of
Bogoliubov automorphisms

generated by
h
. It describes the Heisenberg dynamics of a system of non-interacting
fermions with one-particle Hamiltonian
h
.

More generally, a
C^{\ast}
-dynamics
on
\mathop{\mathrm{CAR}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})
is a strongly continuous group

\alpha=\left(\alpha^{t}\right)_{t\in{\mathbbm{R}}}
of
\ast
-automorphisms. A state
\omega
on

\mathop{\mathrm{CAR}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})
is said to be
KMS for

\alpha

at inverse temperature

\beta\neq 0
whenever, for any
A,B\in\mathop{\mathrm{CAR}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})
, there exists a function
F_{A,B}

which is analytic in the strip
\{z\in{\mathbbm{C}}\mid\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}\nolimits(\beta)z\in]0,|\beta|[\}
and continuous
on its closure such that, for t
\in{\mathbbm{R}}
,

F_{A,B}(t)=\omega(A\alpha^{t}(B)),\qquad F_{A,B}(t+\mathrm{i}\beta)=\omega(\alpha^{t}(B)A).

The
gauge group
of
\mathop{\mathrm{CAR}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})
is the group of Bogoliubov automorphisms
generated by the unit
\mathbbm{1}
,
i.e.,
\tau_{\mathbbm{1}}^{\theta}(a(f))=a(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}f)
. A state

\omega
on
\mathop{\mathrm{CAR}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})
is
gauge-invariant
whenever

\omega\circ\tau_{\mathbbm{1}}^{\theta}=\omega
for all
\theta\in{\mathbbm{R}}
.

A gauge-invariant state
\omega
with covariance operator
C
is
quasi-free
iff,
for all integers
n,m
and any
f_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,

\omega(a^{\ast}(f_{1})\cdots a^{\ast}(f_{n})a(g_{m})\cdots a(g_{1}))=\delta_{nm}\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits\left(\left[\langle g_{i},Cf_{j}\rangle\right]_{i,j\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\right).

Any covariance operator
C
determines a unique gauge-invariant quasi-free
state
\omega_{C}
by the same formula. This state is faithful iff

\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(C)\cap\{0,1\}=\emptyset
.
4
4

4

\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(C)
denotes the spectrum of
C

A faithful gauge-invariant quasi-free state with the covariance
C
is a KMS-state
at
\beta=1
for the group of Bogoliubov automorphisms generated by
h=\log((\mathbbm{1}-C)C^{-1})
.
Conversely,

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
h
be a self-adjoint operator on
{\mathfrak{h}}
. The gauge-invariant quasi-free state with covariance

C=\left(\mathbbm{1}+\mathrm{e}^{h}\right)^{-1}

is the unique
\beta=1
KMS-state for
\tau_{h}
on
\mathop{\mathrm{CAR}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})
.

For the proof of this result, see
[
BR

2.2
Thermodynamic Formalism

The thermodynamic formalism of lattice quantum spin systems, developed in the
1960s and 1970s, is discussed in detail in the classical
monographs
[
Isr
,
BR
,
Sim
]
. Its extension to lattice fermions,
placing them on the same footing as spin systems, was developed in the major
work
[

from
[

1.3

As in the introduction, we set
{\mathfrak{h}}=\ell^{2}({\mathbbm{Z}}^{d})
and
{\mathfrak{A}}=\mathop{\mathrm{CAR}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})

{\mathcal{F}}
denotes the set of all finite subsets of
{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}

|X|
the cardinality
of

{\mathfrak{A}}
. For
A\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, let
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(A)
be
the smallest
X\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that
A\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{X}
.

We denote by
\Theta
the unique involutive
\ast
-automorphism of
{\mathfrak{A}}
satisfying

\Theta(a_{x})=-a_{x},

for all
x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}
. The lattice
{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}
acts naturally on
{\mathfrak{A}}
as a group

\varphi\coloneq\left(\varphi^{x}\right)_{x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}
of
\ast
-automorphisms,

\varphi^{x}
mapping
{\mathfrak{A}}_{X}
onto
{\mathfrak{A}}_{X+x}
. A state
\omega

{\mathfrak{A}}
is

translation-invariant
whenever
\omega\circ\varphi^{x}=\omega
for all
x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}
,
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{I}}
denotes the set of all translation-invariant states on
{\mathfrak{A}}
.

The von Neumann entropy of the restriction to a box
\Lambda
of the state

\omega\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{A}})
is

S(\omega_{\Lambda}):=-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\omega_{\Lambda}\log\omega_{\Lambda}).

The limit

s(\omega)\coloneq\lim_{\Lambda}\frac{S(\omega_{\Lambda})}{|\Lambda|}

exists and takes values in
[0,\log 2]
. The number
s(\omega)
is the

specific entropy
of the state
\omega\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{A}})
. The entropy map

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{I}}({\mathfrak{A}})\ni\omega\mapsto s(\omega)

is affine and upper semicontinuous. For the proof of these results,
see
[
AM
, Section 10.2]
.

An

is a collection
\Phi=\left(\Phi(X)\right)_{X\in{\mathcal{F}}}

\Phi(X)
is a self-adjoint element of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{X}
. An interaction

\Theta(\Phi(X))=\Phi(X)

X\in{\mathcal{F}}
,

We will only consider even translation covariant interactions satisfying

\|\Phi\|\coloneq\sum_{X\ni 0}\|\Phi(X)\|<\infty.

(2.1)

The set
{\mathcal{B}}

2.1
) is a Banach space.
In
[
AM
]

2.1
),
and we will comment on this point in Section

.
The

\Phi\in{\mathcal{B}}

E(\Phi)\coloneq\sum_{X\ni 0}\frac{\Phi(X)}{|X|},

To

The following result was established in

Let

(i)

For any

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

\Phi\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Moreover, if
A\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\mathrm{loc}}
with
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(A)=X_{1}
, one has

\delta_{\Phi}(A)=\sum_{X\cap X_{1}\neq\emptyset}\mathrm{i}[\Phi(X),A]=\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}\sum_{y_{1}\in X_{1}}\mathrm{i}[\Phi(\{x_{1},y_{1}\}),A]=\sum_{x_{1}\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}A^{(1)}(x_{1})

where
A^{(1)}(x_{1})\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(A^{(1)}(x_{1}))=X_{1}\cup\{x_{1}\}\eqcolon X_{2}
.
Iterating, we get

\delta_{\Phi}^{n}(A)=\sum_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}\sum_{y_{n}\in X_{n}}\cdots\sum_{y_{1}\in X_{1}}\mathrm{i}[\Phi(\{x_{n},y_{n}\}),\mathrm{i}[\cdots,\mathrm{i}[\Phi(\{x_{1},y_{1}\}),A]\cdots]],

where
X_{k+1}\coloneq X_{1}\cup\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
.
Since
\|\mathrm{i}[\Phi(\{x,y\}),B]\|\leq 2|h(x,y)|\|B\|
, and for any

,

\sum_{x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}\sum_{y\in Y}|h(x,y)|\leq\sum_{x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}\sum_{y\in Y}|h(x-y,0)|=|Y|\VERT h\VERT,

(2.3)

we get the estimate

\|\delta_{\Phi}^{n}(A)\|\leq\sum_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}\sum_{y_{n}\in X_{n}}\cdots\sum_{y_{1}\in X_{1}}2^{n}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}|h(x_{k}-y_{k},0)|\right)\|A\|\leq 2^{n}\frac{(|X_{1}|+n-1)!}{(|X_{1}|-1)!}\VERT h\VERT^{n}\|A\|.

It follows that, for small enough
t\in{\mathbbm{R}}
,

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\|\delta_{\Phi}^{n}(A)\|\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{|X_{1}|+n-1}{|X_{1}|-1}\left(2t\VERT h\VERT\right)^{n}\|A\|\leq 2^{|X_{1}|-1}\|A\|\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(4t\VERT h\VERT\right)^{n}<\infty,

(2.4)

and we conclude that Condition
(i)
of Theorem
2.3

is satisfied.

(ii)
By Part

2.3
,
the interaction
\Phi

\alpha_{\Phi}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\Lambda}a^{\#}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}th_{\Lambda}}f_{1})\cdots a^{\#}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}th_{\Lambda}}f_{n})

3
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Define a density matrix in
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\Lambda}
by

\nu_{\Lambda}\coloneq\mathrm{e}^{-H_{\Lambda}(\Phi)-P_{\Lambda}(\Phi)},

(3.1)

and observe that the relative entropy of the pair
(\omega_{\Lambda},\nu_{\Lambda})
is

\mathop{\mathrm{Ent}}\nolimits(\omega_{\Lambda}|\nu_{\Lambda})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\omega_{\Lambda}(\log\omega_{\Lambda}-\log\nu_{\Lambda}))\geq 0,

(3.2)

with equality iff
\omega_{\Lambda}=\nu_{\Lambda}
. Since

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\omega_{\Lambda}\log\nu_{\Lambda})=-\omega_{\Lambda}(H_{\Lambda}(\Phi))-P_{\Lambda}(\Phi),

(3.3)

dividing (
3.2
) by
|\Lambda|
and taking the thermodynamic limit, we deduce
from (
3.3
) that

{\mathcal{e}}_{\Phi}(\omega)+P(\Phi)\geq s(\omega).

(3.4)

Since
{\mathcal{e}}_{\Phi}(\omega)={\mathcal{e}}_{\Phi}(\omega_{C})
, and
\omega_{C}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{eq}}(\Phi)
, it follows
from (
3.4
) that

s(\omega_{C})\geq s(\omega).

This proves the Lanford-Robinson Theorem
1.1
under our assumptions on
C
, but
we get more. If
s(\omega)=s(\omega_{C})
, then
e_{\Phi}(\omega)=e_{\Phi}(\omega_{C})
implies

s(\omega)=e_{\Phi}(\omega)+P(\Phi),

and so
\omega\in{\cal S}_{\mathrm{eq}}(\Phi)
. It then follows from Proposition
2.5
(iii)
that
\omega=\omega_{C}
, and Theorem
1.3
is proven.

3.1
Remarks

1.
As mentioned above, the Araki–Moriya thermodynamic formalism
applies to a class of interactions more general than those satisfying
(
2.1
). It is, however, unclear to what extent these broader
assumptions on
\Phi
would allow one to relax our regularity
condition on
C
. It seems likely that any such extension would require a
technical argument and lead only to a modest generalization.

2.
The proof of Theorem
1.3
is perhaps
deceptively simple; it ultimately relies on deep structural results from the
thermodynamic formalism of lattice fermions. It would be interesting to know
whether a more direct and self-contained proof of this uniqueness result can be
found.

3.
Our regularity assumption excludes
singular covariance operators, such as those with discontinuous symbol
\widehat{C}
. Although
the entropy maximization principle itself is expected to hold more generally,
extending the uniqueness result to such singular quasi-free states would likely
require a different strategy, independent of the thermodynamic formalism.

On the other hand, from the thermodynamic perspective, our regularity assumption
is very natural, since it is equivalent to requiring that the associated
interaction
\Phi
belongs to the class of so-called
small
(or

physical
) interactions
[
Isr
,
BR
]
. The well-known
pathologies that arise with interactions outside this class are discussed
in
[
Isr
,
BR
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Introducing the decoupled one-particle Hamiltonian

{h_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{dec}}}\coloneq h_{\Lambda}\oplus h_{\Lambda^{c}},

we note that

{h_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{dec}}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}h(x,y)&if $(x,y)\in(\Lambda\times\Lambda)\cup(\Lambda^{c}\times\Lambda^{c})$;\\[4.0pt]
0&otherwise,\end{cases}

and hence, invoking the estimate (
2.3
), we deduce that for any
Y\in{\mathcal{F}}

\sum_{x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}\sum_{y\in Y}|{h_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{dec}}}(x,y)|\leq\sum_{x\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{d}}\sum_{y\in Y}|h(x,y)|=|Y|\VERT h\VERT.

(4.1)

Denote by
{\Phi_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{dec}}}
the interaction of the form (
1.2
), with

replaced by
{h_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{dec}}}
,

{\Phi_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{dec}}}(X)=\begin{cases}\Phi(X)&if $X\subset\Lambda$ or $X\subset\Lambda^{c};$\\[4.0pt]

applies.

replaced by

We infer that any element of

\alpha_{{\Phi_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{dec}}}}=\tau_{{h_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{dec}}}}

In particular

The dynamics

. Setting
