Bivariate incomplete-Bessel kernels for the first nonlinear Vlasov–Maxwell response
Roberto Ricci \aff 1 \corresp

Abstract
The weakly nonlinear response of a homogeneous magnetised plasma is usually written as a double cyclotron-harmonic expansion. This representation is explicit, but the repeated use of the Jacobi–Anger expansion produces long sums of Bessel functions and nested resonance denominators. In the linear problem we recently obtained an alternative formulation by keeping the Larmor phase unexpanded and by evaluating the characteristic integral in terms of the newly introduced incomplete-Bessel function G μ ​ ( z , ψ ) G_{\mu}(z,\psi) . In this article we apply the same idea directly to the first nonlinear Vlasov–Maxwell equation. The nonlinear source contains the linear response of an inner mode. When this linear response is written in incomplete-Bessel form, the outer characteristic integral produces a bivariate orbit-resolvent G μ , ν ( r ) ​ ( z , ψ , w , χ ) G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}(z,\psi;w,\chi) . This function emerges therefore as the natural orbit integral generated by the first nonlinear characteristic problem. We derive the nonlinear distribution function in terms of these bivariate functions, collect the identities needed for the current projection, recover an extension of the classical double-harmonic Liu–Tripathi formula by expansion, and indicate how the nonlinear susceptibility tensor is obtained from bivariate angular contractions.



keywords
nonlinear Vlasov-Maxwell equation, bivariate orbit-resolvents, nonlinear susceptibility

1
Introduction

The kinetic theory of nonlinear wave coupling in magnetised plasmas is a classical problem. The standard weakly nonlinear treatment expands the Vlasov–Maxwell system about a homogeneous magnetised equilibrium and expresses the response in cyclotron harmonics. This gives the familiar picture of resonant mode coupling, but the explicit formulas rapidly become cumbersome when electromagnetic waves propagate at arbitrary angle with respect to the background magnetic field. In the first nonlinear correction one already encounters two coupled harmonic indices: one from the outer characteristic integration of the output mode and one from the inner linear response entering the nonlinear source. This is the algebraic origin of the complicated double sums appearing in Liu–Tripathi and similar calculations
(
4
;
10
;
12
)
.

In the linear problem, the same cyclotron structure can be organised in a different way. Instead of expanding the Larmor phase at the beginning of the calculation, one keeps the phase along the unperturbed orbit and evaluates the characteristic integral directly. This produces the incomplete-Bessel function
G_{\mu}(z,\psi)
, which replaces the cyclotron harmonic sum before the susceptibility tensor is projected
(
8
)
. This idea is closely related to the sum-rule approach of Newberger and subsequent developments
(
5
;
1
;
11
;
6
;
3
;
7
)
.

The purpose of the present paper is to show that the first nonlinear problem admits an exactly analogous organisation. The first nonlinear equation has the same left-hand side as the linear equation; only the source term changes. More precisely, the nonlinear source is the Lorentz force of one mode acting on the linear response of another mode. If the inner linear response is written in its incomplete-Bessel form, then the outer characteristic integral acts on a shifted
G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}
-response. The resulting object is a bivariate incomplete-Bessel function

G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}^{(r)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right).

The integer
r
records the finite harmonic shifts generated by transverse velocity factors and gyrophase derivatives in the nonlinear force.

The terminology “bivariate incomplete-Bessel function” is used in the structural sense:
G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}
is the bivariate orbit-resolvent generated by applying a second incomplete-Bessel-type characteristic transform to the one-variable incomplete-Bessel kernel
G_{\nu}
. It is not meant to imply that the function is a classical two-variable Bessel function in the sense of the standard theory of multivariable Bessel functions.

This changes the exposition of the nonlinear calculation. The bivariate function appears directly from the nonlinear characteristic solution, exactly as
G_{\mu}
appears directly from the linear characteristic solution. The double-harmonic formula is then recovered by expanding the bivariate function. In this sense the traditional harmonic expression becomes a validation of the orbit formulation rather than its starting point.

The article is organised as follows. We first state the perturbative Vlasov–Maxwell problem and introduce the notation for the cyclotron orbit. We recall the linear solution in incomplete-Bessel form, using the integral definition of
G_{\mu}
as primary. We then take the nonlinear characteristic formula from the standard calculation and rewrite its source with compact differential operators. Inserting the
G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}
-form of the linear response produces the bivariate incomplete-Bessel function. We derive the explicit nonlinear distribution function in this notation, study the main properties of the bivariate functions, derive independently the traditional double-harmonic formula and recover the same formula by expansion. We finally describe the current projection leading to the nonlinear susceptibility tensor, by providing an explicit compact formula.

2
Perturbative Vlasov–Maxwell problem and orbit notation

We consider a homogeneous plasma in a uniform background magnetic field

\mathbf{B}_{0}=B_{0}\mathbf{b},

where
\mathbf{b}
is a fixed unit vector, assumed along the
z
direction of an otherwise arbitrarily oriented Cartesian frame. For one species, the Vlasov equation is

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{x}}F+\frac{e}{m}\left(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\right)\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{v}}F=0

.
Here
F
is the species distribution function,
e
is the signed charge and
m
denotes the particle mass. We write

\mathbf{B}={\mathbf{B}_{0}+\mathbf{B}^{(1)}+\mathbf{B}^{(2)}+\cdots,\qquad\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}^{(1)}+\mathbf{E}^{(2)}+\cdots}

and

F=f_{0}+f^{(1)}+f^{(2)}+\cdots.

The equilibrium distribution
f_{0}=f_{0}(v_{\perp},v_{\parallel})
is assumed gyrotropic.
Then the linear problem is described by

\left[\partial_{t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{x}}+\frac{e}{m}(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}_{0})\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{v}}\right]f^{(1)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v},t)=-\frac{e}{m}\left[\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}_{0}\right]\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}(v_{\perp},v_{\parallel})

The first non linear equation considered in this work has the same structure, apart from the source term:

\left[\partial_{t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{x}}+\frac{e}{m}(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}_{0})\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{v}}\right]f^{(2)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v},t)=-\frac{e}{m}\left[\mathbf{E}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x},t)+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x},t)\right]\cdot\partial_{\mathbf{v}}f^{(1)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v},t)

.
At all perturbative levels, the left-hand side is the same unperturbed cyclotron operator.
After Laplace–Fourier transforming, the equations can be solved by using the method of characteristics.

We adopt the following conventions. The unperturbed signed cyclotron frequency is

\omega\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{eB_{0}}{m}.

For a Laplace–Fourier mode
(\mathbf{k},\Omega)
, the characteristic parameters are

z_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}={\frac{k_{\perp}v_{\perp}}{\omega},\qquad\mu_{\mathbf{k},\Omega}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{k_{\parallel}v_{\parallel}-\Omega}{\omega},\qquad\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\phi-\phi_{\mathbf{k}}}

.
In the following, we set
\Omega=\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}
, where
\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}=\omega_{\mathbf{k}}+\operatorname{i}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}
denotes a generally complex solution of the linear dispersion equation, assumed one-branched for simplicity.

Along the unperturbed orbit we use

\lambda={\omega\tau,\qquad\psi_{\mathbf{k}}(\lambda)=\psi_{\mathbf{k}}+\lambda}

.
The linear and nonlinear responses at mode
\mathbf{k}
have the same external Larmor dressing,

f_{\mathbf{k}}^{(s)}(\mathbf{v})=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k}}^{(s)}(\mathbf{v}),

where
s=1,2,\ldots
. This factorisation is a consequence of the common cyclotron characteristic operator. It is the basic reason why the incomplete-Bessel construction can be iterated from the linear to the first nonlinear response.

We drop for simplicity the superscript from
\mathbf{E}^{(1)}
and use circular transverse components:

E_{\mathbf{k},1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}={\frac{E_{\mathbf{k},x}-\operatorname{i}E_{\mathbf{k},y}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad E_{\mathbf{k},-1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\frac{E_{\mathbf{k},x}+\operatorname{i}E_{\mathbf{k},y}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad E_{\mathbf{k},0}=E_{\mathbf{k},z}}

.
The two velocity-space differential operators used below are

\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{k}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\partial_{v_{\perp}}+\frac{k_{\parallel}}{\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}}\left(v_{\perp}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}-v_{\parallel}\partial_{v_{\perp}}\right),

and

\mathcal{G}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=v_{\parallel}\partial_{v_{\perp}}-v_{\perp}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}

.

For the first nonlinear response we impose the resonance condition

\mathbf{k}={\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q},\qquad\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}=\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}+\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}}

.

The prime on sums over
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
denotes the standard exclusion of zero modes.

3
The linear solution in incomplete-Bessel form

The one-variable incomplete-Bessel function introduced in
(
8
)
is primarily defined as the orbit integral

G_{\mu}(z,\psi)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z\sin(\psi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu\lambda}\operatorname{d}\lambda,

with the usual causal prescription understood in the oscillatory integral. This function is
2\pi
-periodic in the variable
\psi
. Its harmonic representation is obtained by using the Jacobi–Anger formula
(
2
, Sec. 10.12, Eqs. 10.12.1–10.12.2)
to expand the Larmor phase:

G_{\mu}(z,\psi)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{J_{n}(z)\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}n\psi}}{n+\mu}.

This is the linear prototype: one cyclotron characteristic integral acting on one Larmor phase produces one cyclotron denominator.

The linear characteristic solution can be written as

f_{\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{v})=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{q}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{v})

,
where

\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}=\frac{\operatorname{i}e}{m\omega}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{I}}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)X_{\mathbf{q},a}(v_{\perp},v_{\parallel})

.
Here
\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{-1,0,1\}
and

X_{\mathbf{q},1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left(\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{q}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{q},1}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{q}}+\frac{q_{\perp}E_{\mathbf{q},0}}{2\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}}\mathcal{G}\right)f_{0}

X_{\mathbf{q},-1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left(\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{q}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{q},-1}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{q}}+\frac{q_{\perp}E_{\mathbf{q},0}}{2\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}}\mathcal{G}\right)f_{0}

and

X_{\mathbf{q},0}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=E_{\mathbf{q},0}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}f_{0}.

The shifted factors
G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-1}
,
G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}+1}
, and
G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}
are the result of the linear orbit integral and multiply the corresponding velocity-space source terms.

The standard harmonic coefficients are recovered by expanding the shifted incomplete-Bessel factors. The familiar coefficient form is

f_{\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{v})=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{q}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\sum_{N=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}N\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{q},N}^{(1)}(v_{\perp},v_{\parallel}),

with

\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{q},N}^{(1)}=\operatorname{i}\frac{e}{m}\frac{1}{N\omega-(\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}-q_{\parallel}v_{\parallel})}\left[\left(J_{N+1}(z_{\mathbf{q}})\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{q}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{q},1}+J_{N-1}(z_{\mathbf{q}})\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{q}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{q},-1}\right)\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{q}}+J_{N}(z_{\mathbf{q}})E_{\mathbf{q},0}\left(\partial_{v_{\parallel}}+\frac{N\omega}{v_{\perp}\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}}\mathcal{G}\right)\right]f_{0}.

4
The nonlinear characteristic formula

The first nonlinear perturbation satisfies the same characteristic equation as the linear perturbation. For a triad
\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}
, the nonlinear source is the field of the mode
\mathbf{p}
acting on the linear response of the mode
\mathbf{q}
. The characteristic solution obtained in the standard calculation is

f_{\mathbf{k}}^{(2)}(\mathbf{v})=-\frac{e}{m\omega}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}{}^{\prime}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}{}^{\prime}\delta_{\mathbf{q},\,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{p}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{k}}(\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu_{\mathbf{k}}\lambda}\left.\left\{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\psi_{\mathbf{k}}(\lambda)}\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},1}+\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\psi_{\mathbf{k}}(\lambda)}\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},-1}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},0}\right\}f_{\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}\right|_{\mathbf{v}(\lambda)}\operatorname{d}\lambda

.
The differential blocks are independent of
\lambda
. Their explicit form is:

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{p}})}\left[\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{p},1}\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{p}}-\operatorname{i}\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{p}}\omega}{v_{\perp}}\partial_{\phi}\right)+E_{\mathbf{p},0}\frac{p_{\perp}}{2}\left(\mathcal{G}+\operatorname{i}\frac{v_{\parallel}}{v_{\perp}}\partial_{\phi}\right)\right]

;

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},-1}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{p}})}\left[\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{p},-1}\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{p}}+\operatorname{i}\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{p}}\omega}{v_{\perp}}\partial_{\phi}\right)+E_{\mathbf{p},0}\frac{p_{\perp}}{2}\left(\mathcal{G}-\operatorname{i}\frac{v_{\parallel}}{v_{\perp}}\partial_{\phi}\right)\right]

;

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},0}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{i}p_{\perp}\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}E_{\mathbf{p},-1}-\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}E_{\mathbf{p},1}}{\sqrt{2}}\partial_{\phi}+E_{\mathbf{p},0}\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}

.
These differential blocks act on the full linear response
f_{\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}
and the resulting expression is evaluated at
\mathbf{v}(\lambda)
.
In particular,
v_{\perp}
,
v_{\parallel}
,
z_{\mathbf{q}}
, and
\mu_{\mathbf{q}}
do not change; only the inner gyrophase acquires a dependence on
\lambda
through

\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\mapsto{\psi_{\mathbf{q}}(\lambda)=\psi_{\mathbf{q}}+\lambda}

.

5
Bivariate incomplete-Bessel functions

For later convenience, we anticipate the definition of the bivariate incomplete-Bessel function. A more detailed analysis of this function and the investigation of its main properties is deferred to
section

7
.

Definition 1
(Bivariate incomplete-Bessel function)

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z\sin(\psi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}w\sin(\chi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}r\lambda}G_{\nu}\left(w,\chi+\lambda\right)\operatorname{d}\lambda

(1)

.

This function emerges as the natural orbit-resolvent kernel associated with the first nonlinear cyclotron response. The first triplet of variables
(z,\psi,\mu)
belongs to the outer output mode. The second triplet
(w,\chi,\nu)
belongs to the inner linear response. The exponential factor with positive sign is the inner Larmor factor already present in the full linear distribution. Therefore
G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}
is the exact orbit integral produced when the outer characteristic acts on a full inner incomplete-Bessel response.

The elementary replacement repeated used in the following is

\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{k}}\sin(\psi_{\mathbf{k}}+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{q}}\sin(\psi_{\mathbf{q}}+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu_{\mathbf{k}}\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}r\lambda}G_{\nu_{\mathbf{q}}}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}+\lambda\right)\operatorname{d}\lambda=-\operatorname{i}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\nu_{\mathbf{q}}}^{(r)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

.
This identity is the nonlinear analogue of the linear characteristic evaluation. It contains the full inner Larmor factor and therefore also accounts for the phase-gradient terms produced when the nonlinear velocity-space derivatives act on the inner linear response.

6
Nonlinear distribution in bivariate form

We write the full inner linear response as

f_{\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}=\frac{\operatorname{i}e}{m\omega}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{I}}K_{\mathbf{q},a}X_{\mathbf{q},a}

,
where

K_{\mathbf{q},a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{q}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{q}}+\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

.
Then the characteristic formula for the nonlinear response can be written in the compact form

f_{\mathbf{k}}^{(2)}(\mathbf{v})=-\frac{e^{2}}{m^{2}\omega^{2}}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}{}^{\prime}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}{}^{\prime}\delta_{\mathbf{q},\,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{p}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}}\cdot\\
\operatorname{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{k}}\sin(\psi_{\mathbf{k}}+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu_{\mathbf{k}}\lambda}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{I}}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{I}}\left.\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},b}\left(K_{\mathbf{q},a}X_{\mathbf{q},a}\right)\right|_{\mathbf{v}(\lambda)}\operatorname{d}\lambda

,
where

\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{-1,0,1\}

.
The index
a\in\mathcal{I}
labels the inner linear block, while the index
b\in\mathcal{I}
labels the outer gyrophase block generated by the nonlinear force.

The operator acting on the inner block is

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},b}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{p}})}\Bigg\{E_{\mathbf{p},b}\left[b^{2}\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{p}}-\operatorname{i}b\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{p}}\omega}{v_{\perp}}\partial_{\phi}\right)+(1-b^{2})\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}\right]+\sum_{c=\pm 1}E_{\mathbf{p},b+c}\frac{1-bc}{2}\left[b^{2}\frac{p_{\perp}}{2}\left(\mathcal{G}-\operatorname{i}c\frac{v_{\parallel}}{v_{\perp}}\partial_{\phi}\right)-(1-b^{2})\operatorname{i}p_{\perp}c\sqrt{2}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}c\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}\partial_{\phi}\right]\Bigg\}

.

Equivalently, by regrouping according to velocity derivatives,

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},b}=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{p}})}\left[C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\phi)}\partial_{\phi}+C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\perp)}\partial_{v_{\perp}}+C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\parallel)}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}\right]

(2)

,
where

C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\phi)}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\operatorname{i}b^{3}E_{\mathbf{p},b}\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\mu_{\mathbf{p}}\omega}{v_{\perp}}+\sum_{c=\pm 1}E_{\mathbf{p},b+c}\frac{1-bc}{2}\left[-\operatorname{i}cb^{2}\frac{p_{\perp}v_{\parallel}}{2v_{\perp}}-(1-b^{2})\operatorname{i}p_{\perp}c\sqrt{2}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}c\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}\right]

,

C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\perp)}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=b^{2}E_{\mathbf{p},b}\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}-p_{\parallel}v_{\parallel}\right)+\sum_{c=\pm 1}E_{\mathbf{p},b+c}\frac{1-bc}{2}b^{2}\frac{p_{\perp}v_{\parallel}}{2}

,
and

C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\parallel)}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=E_{\mathbf{p},b}\left[b^{2}\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}p_{\parallel}v_{\perp}+(1-b^{2})\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\right]-\sum_{c=\pm 1}E_{\mathbf{p},b+c}\frac{1-bc}{2}b^{2}\frac{p_{\perp}v_{\perp}}{2}

.
Values of
E_{\mathbf{p},j}
with
j\notin\mathcal{I}
are understood to vanish.

The product rule gives

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},b}\left(K_{\mathbf{q},a}X_{\mathbf{q},a}\right)=\left(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},b}K_{\mathbf{q},a}\right)X_{\mathbf{q},a}+K_{\mathbf{q},a}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},b}X_{\mathbf{q},a}\right)

(3)

.
Since

{z_{\mathbf{q}}=\frac{q_{\perp}v_{\perp}}{\omega},\qquad\psi_{\mathbf{q}}=\phi-\phi_{\mathbf{q}},\qquad\mu_{\mathbf{q}}=\frac{q_{\parallel}v_{\parallel}-\Omega_{\mathbf{q}}}{\omega}}

,
one has

\partial_{\phi}K_{\mathbf{q},a}=\partial_{\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}K_{\mathbf{q},a}

,

\partial_{v_{\perp}}K_{\mathbf{q},a}=\frac{q_{\perp}}{\omega}\partial_{z_{\mathbf{q}}}K_{\mathbf{q},a}

,
and

\partial_{v_{\parallel}}K_{\mathbf{q},a}=\frac{q_{\parallel}}{\omega}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{q}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{q}}+\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\partial_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

.
Along the orbit

\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\mapsto\psi_{\mathbf{q}}+\lambda

,
whereas
z_{\mathbf{q}}
,
\mu_{\mathbf{q}}
, and
X_{\mathbf{q},a}
are constant. Hence, grouping together the
\lambda
-dependent terms and using the definition of
G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}
in
eq.

1
, we obtain the replacement rules

K_{\mathbf{q},a}\mapsto\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

,

\partial_{\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}K_{\mathbf{q},a}\mapsto\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\partial_{\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}+\operatorname{i}a\right)G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

,

\partial_{z_{\mathbf{q}}}K_{\mathbf{q},a}\mapsto\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\partial_{z_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

,
and

\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{q}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{q}}+\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\partial_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)\mapsto\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\left.\partial_{\nu}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\nu}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)\right|_{\nu=\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}

.
Therefore, by introducing the shortcut notations

\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},a,b}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

,

\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},a,b}^{(\phi)}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left(\partial_{\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}+\operatorname{i}a\right)G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

,

\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},a,b}^{(\perp)}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\partial_{z_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

,
and

\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},a,b}^{(\parallel)}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left.\partial_{\nu}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\nu}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)\right|_{\nu=\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}

.
the reduced nonlinear distribution can be eventually written as

\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k}}^{(2)}=-\frac{e^{2}}{m^{2}\omega^{2}}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}{}^{\prime}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}{}^{\prime}\delta_{\mathbf{q},\,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{p}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{I}}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{I}}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{p}})}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\cdot\\
\Bigg[\left(C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\phi)}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},a,b}^{(\phi)}+C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\perp)}\frac{q_{\perp}}{\omega}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},a,b}^{(\perp)}+C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\parallel)}\frac{q_{\parallel}}{\omega}\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},a,b}^{(\parallel)}\right)X_{\mathbf{q},a}+\mathbb{G}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},a,b}\left[C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\perp)}\partial_{v_{\perp}}+C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\parallel)}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}\right]X_{\mathbf{q},a}\Bigg]

(4)

.
Only the bivariate orders
a+b
occur. Since
a,b\in\{-1,0,1\}
, these orders range from
-2
to
2
.

7
Properties of the bivariate functions

We now collect the identities needed to manipulate the nonlinear response and to compare the bivariate characteristic representation with the calssical double-harmonic formula. The bivariate incomplete-Bessel function is defined by

G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z\sin(\psi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}w\sin(\chi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}r\lambda}G_{\nu}\left(w,\chi+\lambda\right)\operatorname{d}\lambda

.
The same causal prescription used for the one-variable incomplete-Bessel function is understood.

It is useful to introduce the dressed inner factor

B_{\nu}(w,\chi)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}w\sin\chi}G_{\nu}(w,\chi)

(5)

.
Its Fourier expansion is

B_{\nu}(w,\chi)=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}T_{\ell}(w,\nu)\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\ell\chi}

(6)

,
where

T_{\ell}(w,\nu)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{J_{n}(w)J_{n-\ell}(w)}{n+\nu}

is called Turkin’s function in
(
5
)
.

Proposition 1
(Superposition of one-variable incomplete-Bessel functions)

The bivariate function admits the representation

G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}T_{\ell}(w,\nu)\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\ell\chi}G_{\mu+\ell-r}\left(z,\psi\right)

(7)

.

Proof 7.1
.

Use the Fourier expansion of
B_{\nu}
at
\chi+\lambda
in the defining integral. The remaining
\lambda
-integral is exactly
G_{\mu+\ell-r}(z,\psi)
.

Proposition 7.2

(Harmonic expansion)
.

The bivariate function has the double expansion

G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)=\sum_{N=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{J_{N}(z)T_{\ell}(w,\nu)\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}N\psi}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\ell\chi}}{N+\ell+\mu-r}

(8)

.

Proof 7.3
.

This follows from the superposition formula and from

G_{\eta}(z,\psi)=\sum_{N=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{J_{N}(z)\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}N\psi}}{N+\eta}

.

Proposition 7.4

(Outer resolvent identity)
.

The bivariate function satisfies

\left(\operatorname{i}\partial_{\psi}+\operatorname{i}\partial_{\chi}+\mu-r\right)G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)=\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z\sin\psi}B_{\nu}(w,\chi)

.

Proof 7.5
.

In the harmonic expansion the operator multiplies the term
(N,\ell)
by
N+\ell+\mu-r
, cancelling the denominator.

Proposition 7.6

(Outer Bessel equation)
.

The bivariate function satisfies the homogeneous Bessel equation in the outer variables:

\left[z^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+z\partial_{z}+z^{2}+\partial_{\psi}^{2}\right]G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)=0

.

Proof 7.7
.

Each outer harmonic is proportional to
J_{N}(z)\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}N\psi}
, which satisfies the corresponding Bessel equation.

Proposition 7.8

(Outer differential recurrence)
.

The derivative with respect to the outer Larmor radius satisfies

\partial_{z}G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\psi}G_{\mu+1,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)-\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\psi}G_{\mu-1,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)\right]

.

Proof 7.9
.

Use the superposition formula and the corresponding one-variable recurrence.

Proposition 7.10

(Inner Turkin recurrence)
.

The contracted inner coefficients satisfy

(\nu+\ell)T_{\ell}(w,\nu)+\frac{w}{2}\left[T_{\ell-1}(w,\nu)+T_{\ell+1}(w,\nu)\right]=\delta_{\ell,0}

.
Consequently,

\left(\operatorname{i}\partial_{\chi}+\nu\right)G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)+\frac{w}{2}\left[\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\chi}G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r+1)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)+\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\chi}G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r-1)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)\right]=G_{\mu-r}(z,\psi)

.

Proof 7.11
.

The dressed factor satisfies

\left(\operatorname{i}\partial_{\chi}+\nu+w\cos\chi\right)B_{\nu}(w,\chi)=1

.
Comparing Fourier coefficients gives the recurrence for
T_{\ell}
. The bivariate identity follows by multiplying by
\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\ell\chi}G_{\mu+\ell-r}(z,\psi)
and summing over
\ell
.

Proposition 7.12

(Phase insertions)
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\operatorname{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z\sin(\psi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}w\sin(\chi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}r\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}s(\chi+\lambda)}G_{\nu}\left(w,\chi+\lambda\right)\operatorname{d}\lambda=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}s\chi}G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r+s)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)

.

Proof 7.13
.

The factor
\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}s(\chi+\lambda)}
gives
\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}s\chi}
and shifts
r
to
r+s
.

Proposition 7.14

(Inner derivative rules)
.

The derivatives with respect to the inner variables act on the full dressed inner orbit factor:

\partial_{w}G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}=\operatorname{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z\sin(\psi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}r\lambda}\partial_{w}\left[\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}w\sin(\chi+\lambda)}G_{\nu}\left(w,\chi+\lambda\right)\right]\operatorname{d}\lambda

,

\partial_{\chi}G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}=\operatorname{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z\sin(\psi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}r\lambda}\partial_{\chi}\left[\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}w\sin(\chi+\lambda)}G_{\nu}\left(w,\chi+\lambda\right)\right]\operatorname{d}\lambda

,
and

\partial_{\nu}G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}=\operatorname{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z\sin(\psi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}w\sin(\chi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\mu\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}r\lambda}\partial_{\nu}G_{\nu}\left(w,\chi+\lambda\right)\operatorname{d}\lambda

.

Proposition 7.15

(Double-characteristic representation)
.

The bivariate function can also be written as

G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}z\sin(\psi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}w\sin(\chi+\lambda)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}w\sin(\chi+\lambda+\sigma)}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}(\mu-r)\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\nu\sigma}\operatorname{d}\sigma\operatorname{d}\lambda

.

Proof 7.16
.

Insert the one-variable characteristic representation of
G_{\nu}
into the definition of
G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}
.

Proposition 7.17

(Degenerate one-orbit limit)
.

At zero inner Larmor radius one has

G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;0,\chi\right)=\frac{1}{\nu}G_{\mu-r}(z,\psi)

.

Proof 7.18
.

For
w=0
, the inner dressed factor reduces to
1/\nu
, and the defining integral becomes the one-variable incomplete-Bessel function with detuning
\mu-r
.

8
Derivation of the double-harmonic formula

We provide in the following a classical derivation of the double-harmonic formula for the nonlinear response, obtained by Fourier expanding the cyclic gyrophase-depending components before performing the orbit integral. The result is a generalisation of the classical formula of Liu–Tripathi
(
4
)
and will be used in the following for a consistency check of the correctness of the bivariate representation.

We start from the harmonic form of the inner linear response
(
8
)
,

f_{\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{v})=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{q}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\sum_{M=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}M\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{q},M}^{(1)}

.
Equivalently, for each elementary block
K_{\mathbf{q},a}X_{\mathbf{q},a}
, one may
write

K_{\mathbf{q},a}X_{\mathbf{q},a}=\sum_{M=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}M\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}K_{\mathbf{q},a,M}X_{\mathbf{q},a}

.
Since
\partial_{\phi}=\partial_{\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}
on the inner response, the
action of the operator
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},b}
in the form of
eq.

2
on the
M
-th inner harmonic is

\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{p},b}\left(\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}M\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}K_{\mathbf{q},a,M}X_{\mathbf{q},a}\right)=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{p}})}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}M\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\left[-\operatorname{i}MC_{\mathbf{p},c}^{(\phi)}+C_{\mathbf{p},c}^{(\perp)}\partial_{v_{\perp}}+C_{\mathbf{p},c}^{(\parallel)}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}\right]\left(K_{\mathbf{q},a,M}X_{\mathbf{q},a}\right)

.
This is the essential reduction. The gyrophase derivative in the bivariate
formula becomes multiplication by
-\operatorname{i}M
in the harmonic formula.

Along the outer orbit,

\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\mapsto\psi_{\mathbf{q}}+\lambda

,
and the outer block labelled by
b
contributes the phase

\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\lambda}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}M(\psi_{\mathbf{q}}+\lambda)}=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}M\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}(M-b)\lambda}

.
The remaining outer characteristic integral therefore selects the harmonic
denominator with index
N=M-b
, or equivalently the Bessel factor

J_{N-M+b}(z_{\mathbf{p}})
in the standard notation. Thus the three values of

b
give the three blocks:

\displaystyle b=1

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow J_{N-M+1}(z_{\mathbf{p}})

\displaystyle b=-1

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow J_{N-M-1}(z_{\mathbf{p}})

\displaystyle b=0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow J_{N-M}(z_{\mathbf{p}})

It remains only to evaluate the three coefficient combinations. For
b=1

one has

-\operatorname{i}MC_{\mathbf{p},1}^{(\phi)}+C_{\mathbf{p},1}^{(\perp)}\partial_{v_{\perp}}+C_{\mathbf{p},1}^{(\parallel)}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}=\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{p},1}\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{p}}-M\frac{\omega\mu_{\mathbf{p}}}{v_{\perp}}\right)+\frac{p_{\perp}E_{\mathbf{p},0}}{2}\left(\mathcal{G}+M\frac{v_{\parallel}}{v_{\perp}}\right)

.
For
b=-1
,

-\operatorname{i}MC_{\mathbf{p},-1}^{(\phi)}+C_{\mathbf{p},-1}^{(\perp)}\partial_{v_{\perp}}+C_{\mathbf{p},-1}^{(\parallel)}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}=\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{p},-1}\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{p}}+M\frac{\omega\mu_{\mathbf{p}}}{v_{\perp}}\right)+\frac{p_{\perp}E_{\mathbf{p},0}}{2}\left(\mathcal{G}-M\frac{v_{\parallel}}{v_{\perp}}\right)

.
Finally, for
b=0
,

-\operatorname{i}MC_{\mathbf{p},0}^{(\phi)}+C_{\mathbf{p},0}^{(\perp)}\partial_{v_{\perp}}+C_{\mathbf{p},0}^{(\parallel)}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}=M\frac{p_{\perp}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}E_{\mathbf{p},-1}-\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}E_{\mathbf{p},1}\right)+\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}E_{\mathbf{p},0}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}

.
Combining these three cases gives

f_{\mathbf{k}}^{(2)}(\mathbf{v})=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\sum_{N=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}N\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k},N}^{(2)}

,
with

\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k},N}^{(2)}=\operatorname{i}\frac{q}{m}\frac{1}{N\omega-(\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}-k_{\parallel}v_{\parallel})}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}{}^{\prime}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}{}^{\prime}\delta_{\mathbf{q},\,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{p}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}}\cdot\\
\sum_{M=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\left[N\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-(N-M)\phi_{\mathbf{p}}-M\phi_{\mathbf{q}}\right]}\mathcal{L}_{NM}^{(\mathbf{p})}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{q},M}^{(1)}

,
where

\mathcal{L}_{NM}^{(\mathbf{p})}=\mathcal{L}_{NM,1}^{(\mathbf{p})}+\mathcal{L}_{NM,-1}^{(\mathbf{p})}+\mathcal{L}_{NM,0}^{(\mathbf{p})}

,
and

\mathcal{L}_{NM,1}^{(\mathbf{p})}=J_{N-M+1}(z_{\mathbf{p}})\left[\frac{\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{p},1}\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{p}}-M\frac{\omega\mu_{\mathbf{p}}}{v_{\perp}}\right)+\frac{p_{\perp}E_{\mathbf{p},0}}{2}\left(\mathcal{G}+M\frac{v_{\parallel}}{v_{\perp}}\right)\right]

,

\mathcal{L}_{NM,-1}^{(\mathbf{p})}=J_{N-M-1}(z_{\mathbf{p}})\left[\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}}{\sqrt{2}}E_{\mathbf{p},-1}\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{p}}+M\frac{\omega\mu_{\mathbf{p}}}{v_{\perp}}\right)+\frac{p_{\perp}E_{\mathbf{p},0}}{2}\left(\mathcal{G}-M\frac{v_{\parallel}}{v_{\perp}}\right)\right]

,

\mathcal{L}_{NM,0}^{(\mathbf{p})}=J_{N-M}(z_{\mathbf{p}})\left[\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}E_{\mathbf{p},0}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}+M\frac{p_{\perp}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}E_{\mathbf{p},-1}-\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}\phi_{\mathbf{p}}}E_{\mathbf{p},1}\right)\right]

.
This is the classical double-harmonic formula. In the next section we show that this formula can be directly obtained from
eq.

4
using the properties of the bivariate incomplete Bessel functions discussed in
section

7
.

9
Recovery of the traditional double-harmonic formula

We now verify that the bivariate response reproduces the double-harmonic expression. The point of the verification is to start from
eq.

4
, use only the identities of
section

7
, and recover the formula independently obtained in
section

8
.

We first use the superposition property of the bivariate function,
eq.

7
. With

\nu=\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a
,
r=a+b
, and
\ell=m+a
, it gives

\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}m\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}T_{m+a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a\right)G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\right)

.
The differentiated kernels are expanded in the same way:

\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\left(\partial_{\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}+\operatorname{i}a\right)G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}m\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\left(-\operatorname{i}m\right)T_{m+a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a\right)G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\right)

,

\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\partial_{z_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}m\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\partial_{z_{\mathbf{q}}}T_{m+a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a\right)G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\right)

,
and

\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}a\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\left.\partial_{\nu}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\nu}^{(a+b)}\right|_{\nu=\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}m\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\partial_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}T_{m+a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a\right)G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\right)

.
Thus the sum over
a
in
eq.

4
reconstructs
the
m
-th harmonic of the full inner linear response. More explicitly, if

Y_{\mathbf{q},m}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{I}}T_{m+a}\left(z_{\mathbf{q}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a\right)X_{\mathbf{q},a}

,
then

f_{\mathbf{q},m}^{(1)}=\frac{\operatorname{i}e}{m\omega}Y_{\mathbf{q},m}

.
The differentiated bivariate blocks reconstruct the corresponding derivatives
of
Y_{\mathbf{q},m}
. Consequently the bracket in

eq.

4
becomes

G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\right)\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(m)}Y_{\mathbf{q},m}

,
where

\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(m)}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=-\operatorname{i}mC_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\phi)}+C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\perp)}\partial_{v_{\perp}}+C_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(\parallel)}\partial_{v_{\parallel}}

.
Using
Y_{\mathbf{q},m}=m\omega f_{\mathbf{q},m}^{(1)}/(\operatorname{i}e)
, the reduced bivariate
response becomes

\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k}}^{(2)}=\frac{\operatorname{i}e}{m\omega}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}{}^{\prime}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}{}^{\prime}\delta_{\mathbf{q},\,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{p}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}}\cdot\\
\sum_{b\in\mathcal{I}}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{p}})}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}m\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\right)\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(m)}f_{\mathbf{q},m}^{(1)}

.
Since

\psi_{\mathbf{q}}=\psi_{\mathbf{k}}+\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{q}}

,
the phase factor satisfies

\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}m\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}=\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}(m-b)\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}m(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{q}})}

.
We now expand the remaining one-variable incomplete-Bessel function:

G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\right)=\sum_{P=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}P\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\frac{J_{P}(z_{\mathbf{k}})}{P+\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b}

.
Setting

N\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=P+m-b

,
or equivalently
P=N-m+b
, gives

P+\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b=N+\mu_{\mathbf{k}}

.
Therefore

\frac{1}{P+\mu_{\mathbf{k}}+m-b}=\frac{\omega}{N\omega-\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}-k_{\parallel}v_{\parallel}\right)}

.
At this stage the bivariate formula gives the intermediate harmonic form

\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k},N}^{(2)}=\operatorname{i}\frac{e}{m}\frac{1}{N\omega-\left(\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}-k_{\parallel}v_{\parallel}\right)}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}{}^{\prime}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}{}^{\prime}\delta_{\mathbf{q},\,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{p}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}}\cdot\\
\sum_{b\in\mathcal{I}}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{N-m+b}(z_{\mathbf{k}})\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}m(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{q}})}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{p}})}\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(m)}f_{\mathbf{q},m}^{(1)}

.
This formula is already the harmonic expansion of the bivariate response.
It remains only to rewrite the full inner harmonic
f_{\mathbf{q},m}^{(1)}
in
terms of the reduced inner harmonic coefficients used in the traditional
formula. By definition,

f_{\mathbf{q}}^{(1)}=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{q}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\sum_{M=-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}M\psi_{\mathbf{q}}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{q},M}^{(1)}

,
and therefore

f_{\mathbf{q},m}^{(1)}=\sum_{M=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{M-m}(z_{\mathbf{q}})\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{q},M}^{(1)}

.
The differential operator
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{p},b}^{(m)}
acts on the product

J_{M-m}(z_{\mathbf{q}})\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{q},M}^{(1)}
. The only identities needed to
move the derivatives through the Bessel factor are

\Omega_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{p}}J_{M-m}(z_{\mathbf{q}})=-\mu_{\mathbf{p}}q_{\perp}J^{\prime}_{M-m}(z_{\mathbf{q}})

,

\mathcal{G}J_{M-m}(z_{\mathbf{q}})=\frac{v_{\parallel}q_{\perp}}{\omega}J^{\prime}_{M-m}(z_{\mathbf{q}})

,
and

J^{\prime}_{s}(z_{\mathbf{q}})=\frac{1}{2}\left[J_{s-1}(z_{\mathbf{q}})-J_{s+1}(z_{\mathbf{q}})\right]

.
After these elementary reductions, all terms contain sums of the form

\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{N-m+b}(z_{\mathbf{k}})J_{M-m}(z_{\mathbf{q}})\operatorname{e}^{-\operatorname{i}m(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{q}})}\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}b(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{p}})}

.
The remaining sum over the intermediate harmonic
m
is evaluated by
Graf’s addition theorem. With the phase convention used here and

\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}
, this theorem may be written as

\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}l(\phi_{\mathbf{p}}-\phi_{\mathbf{k}})}J_{l}(z_{\mathbf{p}})=\sum_{h=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{l+h}(z_{\mathbf{k}})J_{h}(z_{\mathbf{q}})\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}h(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{q}})}

,
see, for instance,

Projected kernels and nonlinear susceptibility

The bivariate representation obtained above gives a compact expression for the second-order distribution function. The nonlinear susceptibility tensor is obtained by projecting this distribution on the velocity moments entering the current. In this section we perform this projection without returning to the double-harmonic expansion. The result is a susceptibility written directly in terms of projected bivariate kernels. This form keeps the orbit structure of the nonlinear response explicit and isolates the angular contractions into a small number of reusable special-function blocks.

The second-order nonlinear plasma susceptibility is the third-rank tensor implicitly defined by the relation

11
Outlook toward higher orders

The factorisation

f_{\mathbf{k}}^{(2)}=\operatorname{e}^{\operatorname{i}z_{\mathbf{k}}\sin\psi_{\mathbf{k}}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k}}^{(2)}

has the same form as the linear factorisation. This is not accidental. In the weakly nonlinear hierarchy considered here, the left-hand characteristic operator is the same at every perturbative order; only the source term changes. Consequently, once the source at a given order has been written in terms of orbit kernels, the next order is obtained by applying the same characteristic integral once more.

The linear response is governed by the one-variable incomplete-Bessel kernel

G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}}}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\right)

.
The first nonlinear response is governed by the bivariate kernel

G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}}-a}^{(a+b)}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)

,
which couples the outer characteristic of the output mode
\mathbf{k}
to the inner linear orbit associated with the mode
\mathbf{q}
. At the next perturbative step, the nonlinear source contains the first nonlinear response itself. Applying the outer characteristic integral to this source therefore produces a trivariate kernel, schematically of the form

G_{\mu_{\mathbf{k}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}},\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{2}}}^{(\mathbf{r})}\left(z_{\mathbf{k}},\psi_{\mathbf{k}};z_{\mathbf{q}_{1}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}_{1}};z_{\mathbf{q}_{2}},\psi_{\mathbf{q}_{2}}\right)

,
with discrete shifts
\mathbf{r}
determined by the circular and parallel polarisation blocks appearing in the nested nonlinear source.

More generally, within the fixed-characteristic perturbative hierarchy, the
n
-th order distribution is expected to be expressible in terms of
n
-variate orbit kernels. These kernels are obtained recursively by adding one characteristic time integral for each nonlinear interaction. Their harmonic expansions reproduce the usual multiple cyclotron-harmonic sums, while their projected versions provide the angular contractions needed for the corresponding higher-order susceptibility tensors.

This recursive statement has the standard limitation of weakly nonlinear response theory. It assumes that the nonlinear corrections remain on the right-hand side of the Vlasov equation, so that the unperturbed cyclotron characteristics are not modified. If nonlinear orbit corrections, trapping, ponderomotive drifts, or self-consistent changes of the guiding-centre motion are promoted to the left-hand side, then the characteristic operator itself changes and the simple incomplete-Bessel recursion is deformed.

Thus the present construction should be understood as a compact orbit-kernel reformulation of the fixed-characteristic perturbative hierarchy. In this setting the first nonlinear susceptibility is generated by projected bivariate kernels, and higher-order susceptibilities should be generated by projected multivariate kernels. Whether these higher-rank projected kernels admit closed forms as compact as those obtained here depends on the corresponding angular-contraction calculus.

12
Conclusion

We have reorganised the first nonlinear Vlasov–Maxwell response around the characteristic structure of the problem. The linear response is obtained by applying one cyclotron orbit integral to one Larmor phase, producing the incomplete-Bessel function
G_{\mu}
. The first nonlinear response is obtained by applying an outer cyclotron orbit integral to an inner
G_{\mu_{\mathbf{q}}}
-resolved linear response. This produces the bivariate incomplete-Bessel function

G_{\mu,\nu}^{(r)}\left(z,\psi;w,\chi\right)

.
The bivariate function is therefore not an auxiliary notation, but the natural orbit-resolvent kernel associated with the first nonlinear cyclotron response.

The resulting nonlinear distribution function is explicit and preserves all physical factors of the original calculation through compact differential blocks. Expanding the bivariate function recovers a generalisation of the traditional double-harmonic Liu–Tripathi expression, while keeping it unexpanded gives a direct route to the nonlinear susceptibility tensor through projected bivariate angular contractions. In this sense the bivariate formulation is equivalent to the harmonic representation, but reorganises it around the geometry of the characteristic flow.

The projected kernels introduced above are also relevant for numerical implementation. In the traditional formulation one has to handle double cyclotron-harmonic sums, together with several shifted Bessel factors and derivative-generated recurrence terms. In the present formulation these structures are absorbed into a small family of projected kernels,

{\mathcal{K}_{N}^{(r,a)},\qquad\partial_{\Delta}\mathcal{K}_{N}^{(r,a)},\qquad\partial_{z_{\mathbf{q}}}\mathcal{K}_{N}^{(r,a)},\qquad\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{K}_{N}^{(r,a)}}

.
These kernels may be evaluated either from their angular-integral definition, from their single-sum coefficient representation in terms of the contracted coefficients
T_{\ell}
, or from the original characteristic integral when this is more stable. This flexibility is expected to be advantageous in regimes where direct double-harmonic summation is slowly convergent, strongly oscillatory, or numerically delicate because of cancellations among shifted harmonics.

The numerical advantage should not be interpreted as a universal reduction of computational cost. Its usefulness depends on the values of the Larmor radii, detunings, damping prescription, and truncation strategy. Nevertheless, the bivariate formulation offers a more structured computational target: instead of repeatedly summing and differentiating long harmonic expressions, one can tabulate projected kernels and their derivatives, reuse them across tensor components, and exploit their recurrence, resolvent, and projection identities. This suggests a practical route toward efficient evaluation of nonlinear susceptibilities in parameter regimes where the harmonic representation is algebraically unwieldy.

The main advantage of the formulation is therefore both structural and potentially computational. Structurally, it identifies the special-function object underlying the first nonlinear cyclotron response. Computationally, it replaces the unorganised double-harmonic expansion by a small set of orbit-projected kernels whose analytic properties can be studied independently and then used systematically in the susceptibility tensor.
