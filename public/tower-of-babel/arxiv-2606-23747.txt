1.
Introduction

The study of Toeplitz and Hankel operators has become a major area of interest for several mathematicians from the last few decades due to their rich structural properties and numerous applications in function theory, control theory, quantum physics, signal processing, time series analysis, and so on. A systematic study of Toeplitz operators was initiated after the seminal paper of Brown and Halmos
[
2
]
, where they obtained the algebraic characterization of Toeplitz operators on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. On the other hand, the celebrated Nehari’s theorem
[
18
]
characterized the bounded Hankel operators in terms of bounded symbols. Peller’s monograph
[
19
]
is a valuable source in the development of the theory of Hankel operators and their applications. Inspired by these developments, several mathematicians extended the theory of Toeplitz and Hankel operators in the polydisc setting. Recently, Maji, Sarkar, and Sarkar
[
15
]
extended the Brown-Halmos type characterization in the polydisc setting. Cotlar and Sadosky
[
3
]
initiated the systematic study of Hankel operators in the polydisc. Gu
[
12
]
established the characterization results of Hankel operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and also studied their structural properties and their relation with the Toeplitz operators. For further results regarding Toeplitz and Hankel operators on the polydisc, one may refer to
[
7
,
11
]
.

The class of Toeplitz + Hankel operators has also emerged as a distinguishable class of operators that has been studied for many years. The invertibility and Fredholmness of Toeplitz + Hankel operators are studied extensively by many mathematicians
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
8
]
. In addition, their structural and algebraic properties on scalar as well as vector-valued Hardy spaces have been explored in several works (see
[
9
,
14
,
20
]
and the references within). Recently, Das, Das, and Sarkar
[
4
]
characterized the Toeplitz + Hankel operators on the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
. In this article, we are concerned with the generalization of the notion of Toeplitz + Hankel operators in the polydisc setting.
More precisely, we provide a complete classification of Toeplitz + Hankel operators on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as follows:

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert space and
A\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A=
Toeplitz + Hankel.

(2)

For all
j=1,2,\dots,n
,
T_{z_{j}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}-A
is a Hankel operator.

(3)

For all
j=1,2,\dots,n
,
T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}}
is a Toeplitz operator.

(4)

For all
j,k=1,2,\dots,n
, we have

AT_{z_{j}}+T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{k}}=T_{z_{j}}^{*}A+T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}T_{z_{k}}.

Another important aspect of this paper is the study of paired operators. For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, let
P
and
Q
be orthogonal projections on
\mathcal{H}
such that
P+Q=I_{\mathcal{H}}
. Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. The
paired operator

S_{X,Y}
on
\mathcal{H}
is defined by

S_{X,Y}=XP+YQ.

Denoting
P_{+}=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}
, the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
onto
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
P_{-}=I_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}-P_{+}
. Clearly,
P_{+}
and
P_{-}
are
complementary projections
, i.e.,
P_{+}+P_{-}=I_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}
. We define the paired operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

For
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, the paired operator
S_{\varphi,\psi}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is defined as

S_{\varphi,\psi}=M_{\varphi}P_{+}+M_{\psi}P_{-},

where
M_{\varphi}
and
M_{\psi}
are the Laurent operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

These paired operators are dilations of Toeplitz operators and are also referred to as
singular integral operators
by several authors. The structural and algebraic properties of
S_{\varphi,\psi}
have been studied extensively over the years (see
[
13
,
16
]
). In
[
4
]
, an algebraic characterization of paired operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
is obtained. So our next goal is to give a generalized version of this characterization on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. We obtain a characterization result for paired operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
as follows:

Theorem B
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
is a paired operator.

(2)

A=M_{z_{j}}^{*}AM_{z_{j}}P_{+}+M_{z_{j}}AM_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}
for all
j=1,\dots,n
.

The remainder of the article is outlined as follows: In Section
2
, we give preliminary definitions, notations, and basic results that are required for subsequent sections. Section
3
is devoted to classifying the Toeplitz + Hankel operators on the
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space over the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Finally, the characterization results for paired operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
have been presented in Section
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

All Hilbert spaces considered in this paper are complex and separable. Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
be the open unit disc in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
and the unit circle
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
be the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
. For
n\geq 1
, we denote by
\mathbb{D}^{n}
the open unit polydisc in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\mathbb{T}^{n}
the distinguished boundary of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
, denote by
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, the space of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that

\|f\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left\|f(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{n})\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}\,d\mu<\infty,

where
d\mu
is the normalised Lebegue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

The
\mathcal{E}
-valued Hardy space

H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
over the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is the Hilbert space of all
\mathcal{E}
-valued holomorphic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that

\|f\|^{2}=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left\|f(r\zeta_{1},\dots,r\zeta_{n})\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}\,d\mu\right)<\infty.

It is well-known that
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is isometrically isomorphic to the closed subspace of
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
which consists of all such functions whose negative Fourier coefficients are zero and this characterization can be proved by means of radial limits, see
[
17
]
for complete details. Let
(T_{z_{1}},\ldots,T_{z_{n}})
be the
n
-tuple of shift operators, where

(2.1)

T_{z_{i}}f=z_{i}f,\quad(f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})),

for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. For
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and the multi-index
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we write
\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}
. Furthermore, we denote
L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
as the Banach algebra of all measurable functions
\Phi:\mathbb{T}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
such that

\|\Phi\|_{\infty}=\underset{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}}{\text{ess\,sup}}\|\Phi(\bm{z})\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}<\infty,

where
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
is the space of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{E}
. For each
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, the multiplication operator or
Laurent operator

M_{\Phi}:L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is defined as

M_{\Phi}f=\Phi f,\quad(f\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})).

Moreover, the
Toeplitz operator

T_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is defined as

T_{\Phi}f=P_{+}M_{\Phi}f,\quad(f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})),

where
P_{+}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
onto
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Putting
\Phi=z_{i}I_{\mathcal{E}}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},
we obtain the corresponding shift operator
T_{z_{i}}
as defined in (
2.1
). Similarly, the
Hankel operator

H_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
is defined by

H_{\Phi}f=P_{+}M_{\Phi}Jf,\quad(f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})),

where
J
is defined on
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
as

(Jf)(\bm{z})=f(\bar{\bm{z}}),\quad(f\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\bm{z}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}),

where
\bar{\bm{z}}=(\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n})
.
J
is a unitary operator and

J^{*}=J,\quad J^{2}=I,\quad J(I-P)=PJ.

The following results will be useful in the sequel. The Brown-Halmos type characterization for Toeplitz operators on the polydisc setting is as follows (see
[
15
]
,
[
21
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

A bounded operator
T
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a Toeplitz operator if and only if

T_{z_{i}}^{*}TT_{z_{i}}=T\quad\mbox{for all~~~}i=1,\ldots,n.

Similarly, the algebraic characterization for Hankel operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is given in
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
. The characterization is true and can be proved verbatim for Hankel operators on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
:

Theorem 2.2
.

A bounded operator
T
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a Hankel operator if and only if

T_{z_{i}}^{*}T=TT_{z_{i}}\quad\mbox{for all~~~}i=1,\ldots,n.

We also write
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as the Banach algebra of bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. By means of radial limits, we also identify
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as the space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. We call a function
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is
inner
if

|\theta(\bm{z})|=1,\quad\text{a.e. }\bm{z}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

3.
Toeplitz + Hankel operators on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

The goal of this section is to characterize the Toeplitz + Hankel operators on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. We need the following lemma to prove the main theorem of this section. Note that the one-variable version of the following lemma is given in
[
4
,
9
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert space and let
\Phi:\mathbb{T}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
be a measurable function. Suppose there exist
M>0
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that

\|\Phi(\bm{z})(1-z_{i}^{2m})\|_{\infty}\leq M

for all
m\geq 1
and
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
a.e. Then
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

We are now ready to prove the main theorem of the section.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A=
Toeplitz + Hankel.

(2)

For all
j=1,2,\dots,n
,
T_{z_{j}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}-A
is a Hankel operators.

(3)

For all
j=1,2,\dots,n
,
T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}}
is a Toeplitz operators.

(4)

For all
j,k=1,2,\dots,n
, we have

AT_{z_{j}}+T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{k}}=T_{z_{j}}^{*}A+T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}T_{z_{k}}.

Proof.

We first establish the implication
(1)\implies(4)
. Let

A=T_{\Phi}+H_{\Psi}

for some
\Phi,\Psi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. For all
j,k=1,2,\dots,n
, we have

\displaystyle AT_{z_{j}}+T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=(T_{\Phi}+H_{\Psi})T_{z_{j}}+T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{z_{k}}^{*}(T_{\Phi}+H_{\Psi})T_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}T_{z_{j}}+H_{\Psi}T_{z_{j}}+T_{z_{j}}^{*}(T_{z_{k}}^{*}T_{\Phi}T_{z_{k}})+T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{z_{k}}^{*}H_{\Psi}T_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}T_{z_{j}}+T_{z_{j}}^{*}H_{\Psi}+T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}+T_{z_{k}}^{*}H_{\Psi}T_{z_{j}}T_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=T_{z_{j}}^{*}(T_{\Phi}+H_{\Psi})+T_{\Phi}T_{z_{j}}+T_{z_{k}}^{*}H_{\Psi}T_{z_{j}}T_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=T_{z_{j}}^{*}A+T_{z_{k}}^{*}(T_{\Phi}+H_{\Psi})T_{z_{j}}T_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=T_{z_{j}}^{*}A+T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}T_{z_{k}}.

Conversely, assume that (4) holds true. That is,

AT_{z_{j}}+T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{k}}=T_{z_{j}}^{*}A+T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}T_{z_{k}},\quad\text{for all }1\leq j,k\leq n.

It follows from the above identity that

T_{z_{k}}^{*}(T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}})T_{z_{k}}=T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}},\quad\text{for all }1\leq j,k\leq n,

which implies that
T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}}
is a Toeplitz operator for all
j=1,2,\dots,n
. Therefore, for all
j=1,2,\dots,n
, there exists
\Phi_{j}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that

(3.1)

T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}}=T_{\Phi_{j}},\quad(j=1,2,\dots,n).

Multiplying (
3.1
) by
T_{z_{j}}
and
T_{z_{j}}^{*}
from left and right, respectively. We obtain

T_{z_{j}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}-AT_{z_{j}}^{2}=T_{z_{j}\Phi_{j}}

and

T_{z_{j}}^{*2}A-T_{z_{j}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}=T_{\overline{z_{j}}\Phi_{j}}.

Addition of the above two identites yields

T_{z_{j}}^{*2}A-AT_{z_{j}}^{2}=T_{(z_{j}+\overline{z_{j}})\Phi_{j}}=T_{\left(\frac{z_{j}^{2}-\overline{z_{j}}^{2}}{z_{j}-\overline{z_{j}}}\right)\Phi_{j}}.

For each variable
z_{j}
, we follow the same line of arguments as that of Theorem 2.2 in
[
4
]
and obtain

T_{z_{j}}^{*m}A-AT_{z_{j}}^{m}=T_{\left(\frac{z_{j}^{m}-\overline{z_{j}}^{m}}{z_{j}-\overline{z_{j}}}\right)\Phi_{j}},\quad(m\geq 1).

Now we know that

\left\|\frac{z_{j}^{m}-\overline{z_{j}}^{m}}{z_{j}-\overline{z_{j}}}\Phi_{j}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|T_{\left(\frac{z_{j}^{m}-\overline{z_{j}}^{m}}{z_{j}-\overline{z_{j}}}\right)\Phi_{j}}\right\|=\|T_{z_{j}}^{*m}A-AT_{z_{j}}^{m}\|\leq 2\|A\|.

Applying Lemma
3.1
, we get

\frac{\Phi_{j}}{\overline{z_{j}}-z_{j}}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\quad(j=1,2,\dots,n).

Claim:
We prove the following for all
j,k=1,2,\dots,n
:

\frac{\Phi_{j}}{\overline{z_{j}}-z_{j}}=\frac{\Phi_{k}}{\overline{z_{k}}-z_{k}},

i.e.,

\Phi_{j}(\overline{z_{k}}-z_{k})=\Phi_{k}(\overline{z_{j}}-z_{j}),

i.e.,

T_{\Phi_{j}(\overline{z_{k}}-z_{k})}=T_{\Phi_{k}(\overline{z_{j}}-z_{j})},

i.e.,

T_{z_{k}}^{*}T_{\Phi_{j}}-T_{\Phi_{j}}T_{z_{k}}=T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi_{k}}-T_{\Phi_{k}}T_{z_{j}}.

Proof of claim:
Using (
3.1
), we have the following:

\displaystyle T_{z_{k}}^{*}T_{\Phi_{j}}-T_{\Phi_{j}}T_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=T_{z_{k}}^{*}(T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}})-(T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}})T_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=T_{z_{k}}^{*}T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}-T_{z_{j}}^{*}AT_{z_{k}}+AT_{z_{j}}T_{z_{k}}

\displaystyle=(T_{z_{k}}^{*}T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-T_{z_{j}}^{*}AT_{z_{k}})-(T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{j}}-AT_{z_{j}}T_{z_{k}})

\displaystyle=T_{z_{j}}^{*}(T_{z_{k}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{k}})-(T_{z_{k}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{k}})T_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi_{k}}-T_{\Phi_{k}}T_{z_{j}}

for all
j,k=1,2,\dots,n
. This proves our claim. In that case, let
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
be such that

\Phi=\frac{\Phi_{j}}{\overline{z_{j}}-z_{j}},\quad(j=1,2,\dots,n).

That is,

\Phi_{j}=\Phi(\overline{z_{j}}-z_{j}),\quad(j=1,2,\dots,n),

which implies

T_{\Phi_{j}}=T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}-T_{\Phi}T_{z_{j}},\quad(1,2,\dots,n).

Again using (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=T_{z_{j}}^{*}T_{\Phi}-T_{\Phi}T_{z_{j}},\quad(j=1,2,\dots,n)

\displaystyle T_{z_{j}}^{*}(A-T_{\Phi})

\displaystyle=(A-T_{\Phi})T_{z_{j}},\quad(j=1,2,\dots,n).

Consequently,
A-T_{\Phi}
is a Hankel operator. That is, there exists
\Psi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that

A=T_{\Phi}+H_{\Psi}.

This establishes the equivalence of (1) and (4). Moreover, the equivalences
(2)\iff(4)
and
(3)\iff(4)
follows immediately once we rewrite the condition (4) as follows:

T_{z_{j}}^{*}(T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{k}}-A)=(T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{k}}-A)T_{z_{j}},\quad(j,k=1,2,\dots,n).

which is same as saying that
T_{z_{k}}^{*}AT_{z_{k}}-A
is a Hankel operator for all
k=1,2,\dots,n
. Similarly, we also rewrite condition (4) as follows:

T_{z_{k}}^{*}(T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}})T_{z_{k}}=T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}},\quad(j,k=1,2,\dots,n).

which is equivalent to saying
T_{z_{j}}^{*}A-AT_{z_{j}}
is a Toeplitz operator for all
j=1,2,\dots,n
.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

4.
Paired operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})

The goal of this section is to classify paired operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Recall that
\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}:\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
is the standard orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. We call the elements of
\text{span}\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}:\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
as the trigonometric polynomials in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and denote this linear space by
\mathbb{P}
. Similarly, we call the members of
\text{span}\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}:\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\}
and
\text{span}\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}:\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus Z_{+}^{n}\}
as the polynomials in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
, respectively, and denote

\mathbb{P}_{+}=\text{span}\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}:\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}_{-}=\text{span}\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}:\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\}.

It now follows that

(4.1)

\overline{\mathbb{P}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\quad\overline{\mathbb{P}}_{+}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\mathbb{P}}_{-}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}.

We also denote
P_{+}=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}
, the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
onto
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and write
P_{-}=I_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}-P_{+}
. Therefore

P_{-}=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}.

From the fact that
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is invariant under
M_{z_{j}}
for all
j=1,\dots,n
, we observe that

(4.2)

P_{+}M_{z_{j}}P_{+}=M_{z_{j}}P_{+},\quad\text{or}\quad P_{-}M_{z_{j}}P_{+}=0,

for all
j=1,\dots,n
.

The following lemma is crucial in establishing the main theorem of this section.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
satisfies

\|\varphi p\|_{2}\leq C\|p\|_{2}

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
and some
C>0
. Then
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
is a paired operator.

(2)

A=M_{z_{j}}^{*}AM_{z_{j}}P_{+}+M_{z_{j}}AM_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}
for all
j=1,\dots,n
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
A
is a paired operator, i.e.,

A=S_{\varphi,\psi}=M_{\varphi}P_{+}+M_{\psi}P_{-}

for some
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. In view of (
4.2
), we note that

\displaystyle M_{z_{j}}^{*}AM_{z_{j}}P_{+}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}^{*}S_{\varphi,\psi}M_{z_{j}}P_{+}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}^{*}(M_{\varphi}P_{+}+M_{\psi}P_{-})M_{z_{j}}P_{+}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}^{*}M_{\varphi}P_{+}M_{z_{j}}P_{+}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}^{*}M_{\varphi}M_{z_{j}}P_{+}

\displaystyle=M_{\varphi}P_{+}.

By (
4.2
), we also have
P_{+}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}=0
, and hence

\displaystyle M_{z_{j}}AM_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}S_{\varphi,\psi}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}(M_{\varphi}P_{+}+M_{\psi}P_{-})M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}M_{\psi}P_{-}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}

\displaystyle=M_{z_{j}}M_{\psi}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}

\displaystyle=M_{\psi}P_{-}.

Evidently, we have

A=S_{\varphi,\psi}=M_{z_{j}}^{*}S_{\varphi,\psi}M_{z_{j}}P_{+}+M_{z_{j}}S_{\varphi,\psi}M_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-},\quad(j=1,\dots,n).

Thus,
(1)\implies(2)
is established.

Conversely, assume that condition (2) holds. That is,

(4.3)

A=M_{z_{j}}^{*}AM_{z_{j}}P_{+}+M_{z_{j}}AM_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-},\quad(j=1,\dots,n).

Multiplying the above identity by
P_{+}
from left, yields

AP_{+}=M_{z_{j}}^{*}AM_{z_{j}}P_{+},\quad(j=1,\dots,n).

which implies

(4.4)

AM_{z_{j}}P_{+}=M_{z_{j}}AP_{+},\quad(j=1,\dots,n).

Moreover, for all
(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
we have

(4.5)

P_{+}M_{z_{1}}^{m_{1}}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{m_{n}}P_{+}=M_{z_{1}}^{m_{1}}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{m_{n}}P_{+}.

Note that the identities in (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) yields, for
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
k_{1}\geq 1
, that

\displaystyle A(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle=AM_{z_{1}}^{k_{1}}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{k_{n}}P_{+}(1)

\displaystyle=AM_{z_{1}}(M_{z_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{k_{n}}P_{+})(1)

\displaystyle=AM_{z_{1}}(P_{+}M_{z_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{k_{n}}P_{+})(1)

\displaystyle=(AM_{z_{1}}P_{+})M_{z_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{k_{n}}P_{+}(1)

\displaystyle=(M_{z_{1}}AP_{+})M_{z_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{k_{n}}P_{+}(1)

\displaystyle=z_{1}A(P_{+}M_{z_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{k_{n}}P_{+})(1)

\displaystyle=z_{1}AM_{z_{1}}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{k_{n}}P_{+}(1).

Hence, the repeated application of identities in (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) yields, for
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, that

(4.6)

A(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}})=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}A(1)=\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}A(1).

Now we set

\varphi=A(1)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

Therefore, (
4.6
) implies that

Aq=\varphi q,\quad\text{for all }q\in\mathbb{P}_{+}.

Assume that
p\in\mathbb{P}
is arbitrary, then we can choose a
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
such that
\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}p\in\mathbb{P}_{+}
. It now follows that

\|\varphi p\|_{2}=\|\varphi\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}p\|_{2}=\|A(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}p)\|_{2}\leq\|A\|\|p\|_{2}

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Applying Lemma
4.1
, we immediately obtain
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. For
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, it follows from the facts recorded in (
4.1
) that there exists a sequence
(q_{m})_{m\geq 1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{+}
such that

q_{m}\to f\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\|Af-\varphi f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|Af-Aq_{m}\|_{2}+\|Aq_{m}-\varphi f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|\|f-q_{m}\|_{2}+\|\varphi q_{m}-\varphi f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\|A\|+\|\varphi\|_{\infty})\|q_{m}-f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow 0

Hence we obtain

Af=\varphi f,\quad\text{for all }f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),

or,

(4.7)

AP_{+}=M_{\varphi}P_{+}.

Again, we observe from (
4.3
) that
AP_{-}=M_{z_{j}}AM_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}
, i.e.,

(4.8)

AM_{z_{j}}^{*}P_{-}=M_{z_{j}}^{*}AP_{-},\quad(j=1,\dots,n).

Furthermore, we note that for all
(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
,

(4.9)

P_{-}M_{z_{1}}^{*m_{1}}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{*m_{n}}P_{-}=M_{z_{1}}^{*m_{1}}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{*m_{n}}P_{-}.

For each
j=1,\dots,n
, applying the above identities in (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) repeatedly, yields for all
k_{j}\geq 1
that

\displaystyle A(\overline{z_{j}}^{k_{j}})

\displaystyle=AM_{z_{j}}^{*k_{j}-1}P_{-}(\overline{z_{j}})

\displaystyle=\overline{z_{j}}^{k_{j}}(z_{j}A(\overline{z_{j}})).

Set

\psi_{j}=z_{j}A(\overline{z_{j}})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\quad(j=1,\dots,n).

We now claim the following:

(a)

\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}=\dots=\psi_{n}(=\psi,\text{ say})
.

(b)

Ap=\psi p
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}_{-}
.

(c)

\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

It follows from (
4.3
) that

A(\overline{z_{j}})=0+M_{z_{k}}AM_{z_{k}}^{*}P_{-}(\overline{z_{j}})=M_{z_{k}}AM_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{z_{j}}^{*}(1),\quad(j,k=1,\dots,n),

so that

z_{j}A(\overline{z_{j}})=M_{z_{j}}M_{z_{k}}AM_{z_{k}}^{*}M_{z_{j}}^{*}(1),\quad(j,k=1,\dots,n).

Therefore,

z_{j}A(\overline{z_{j}})=z_{k}A(\overline{z_{k}}),\quad\text{or}\quad\psi_{j}=\psi_{k},\quad(j,k=1,\dots,n).

This proves claim (a). Thereby, we have

A(\overline{z_{j}}^{k_{j}})=\psi\overline{z_{j}}^{k_{j}},\quad(k_{j}\geq 1,j=1,\dots,n).

To establish the claim (b), let us suppose now
\emptyset\neq E\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
and consider the following set

\mathcal{S}_{E}=\{\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:k_{j}<0\text{ if }j\in E\text{ and }k_{j}\geq 0\text{ if }j\notin E\}.

Clearly,

\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}=\bigcup_{\emptyset\neq E\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}}\mathcal{S}_{E}.

Fix an arbitrary
\emptyset\neq E\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathcal{S}_{E}
. Then
using (
4.3
), we observe the following interesting fact that

A(z_{j}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}})=z_{j}A(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}),\quad\text{provided}\quad z_{j}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{P}_{-}.

Now from the repeated application of (
4.3
) and the fact above, we obtain

\displaystyle A(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle=A\left(\prod_{j\notin E}z_{j}^{k_{j}}\,\prod_{j\in E}z_{j}^{k_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j\notin E}z_{j}^{k_{j}}\right)A\left(\prod_{j\in E}z_{j}^{k_{j}}\right).

Moreover, if
\bm{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})
be such that
m_{j}\leq 0
for all
j=1,\dots,n
. Then for each
j
, we have

A(\overline{z_{j}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}})=AM_{z_{1}}^{*l_{1}}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{*l_{n}}P_{-}(\overline{z_{j}}),\quad\text{where}\quad(l_{j}=-m_{j},\,\forall j=1,\dots,n).

Once again, the repeated application of the identities in (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) reduces the above identity as follows

\displaystyle A(\overline{z_{j}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}})

\displaystyle=M_{z_{1}}^{*l_{1}}\cdots M_{z_{n}}^{*l_{n}}AP_{-}(\overline{z_{j}})

\displaystyle=\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}A(\overline{z_{j}})

\displaystyle=\overline{z_{j}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}}(z_{j}A(\overline{z_{j}}))

\displaystyle=(\overline{z_{j}}\bm{z}^{\bm{m}})\psi.

Consequently, we deduce that

A\left(\prod_{j\in E}z_{j}^{k_{j}}\right)=\left(\prod_{j\in E}z_{j}^{k_{j}}\right)\psi.

Hence

A(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}})=\left(\prod_{j\notin E}z_{j}^{k_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j\in E}z_{j}^{k_{j}}\right)\psi=\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\psi

for all
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{E}
and
\emptyset\neq E\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
. Thus,

A(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}})=\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\psi,\quad(\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}).

It now immediately follows that

Ap=\psi p,\quad(p\in\mathbb{P}_{-}).

This proves our claim (b). Again, we assume that
p\in\mathbb{P}
is arbitrary, then we can choose a
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
with
k_{j}\leq 0
for all
j=1,\dots,n
such that
\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}p\in\mathbb{P}_{-}
so that

\|\psi p\|_{2}=\|\psi\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}p\|_{2}=\|A(\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}p)\|_{2}\leq\|A\|\|p\|_{2}

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Again, the application of Lemma
4.1
immediately yields
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and the claim (c) is proved. Finally, for
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
, (
4.1
) implies that there exists a sequence
(p_{m})_{m\geq 1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{-}
such that

p_{m}\to f\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\|Af-\psi f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|Af-Ap_{m}\|_{2}+\|Ap_{m}-\psi f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|\|f-p_{m}\|_{2}+\|\psi p_{m}-\psi f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\|A\|+\|\psi\|_{\infty})\|p_{m}-f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow 0

Hence we obtain

Af=\psi f\quad\text{for all }f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp},

or,

(4.10)

AP_{-}=M_{\psi}P_{-}.

Finally, combining (
4.7
) and (
4.10
), we obtain

A=M_{\varphi}P_{+}+M_{\psi}P_{-}=S_{\varphi,\psi}

for some
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and this establishes the implication
(2)\implies(1)
.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

We now turn our attention to generalize the concept of
\theta
-paired operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Recently, the characterization of
\theta
-paired operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
was given in
[
4
]
. Recall that for a non-constant inner function
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
,
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a nonzero closed subspace of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
which is invariant under
T_{z_{i}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Therefore,

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})=\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\oplus K_{\theta},

where
K_{\theta}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\ominus\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
be a non-constant inner function. The
\theta
-paired operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
with symbols
\varphi,\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is defined as

S_{\varphi,\psi}^{\theta}=T_{\varphi}P_{\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}+T_{\psi}P_{K_{\theta}}.

We now state the characterization result for
\theta
-paired operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. The proof follows along the same lines as in the case of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
in
[
4
]
, so we omit it.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
be a non-constant inner function. A bounded operator
X
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a
\theta
-paired operator if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is invariant under
X
.

(2)

There exist a non-zero
f_{0}\in K_{\theta}
and
v\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
such that

Xf_{0}=vf_{0}

and

[X,T_{z_{i}}]=(X-T_{v})P_{\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}T_{z_{i}}P_{k_{\theta}}

for all
i=1,\ldots,n.
