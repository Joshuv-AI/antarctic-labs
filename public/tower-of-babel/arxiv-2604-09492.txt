Dynamic Ranked List Truncation for Reranking Pipelines via LLM-generated Reference-Documents
Nilanjan Sinhababu, Soumedhik Bharati, Debasis Ganguly, Pabitra Mitra

Abstract
. Large Language Models (LLM) have been widely used in reranking. Computational overhead and large context lengths remain a challenging issue for LLM rerankers. Efficient reranking usually involves selecting a subset of the ranked list from the first stage, known as ranked list truncation (RLT). The truncated list is processed further by a reranker. For LLM rerankers, the ranked list is often partitioned and processed sequentially in batches to reduce the context length. Both these steps involve hyperparameters and topic-agnostic heuristics. Recently, LLMs have been shown to be effective for relevance judgment. Equivalently, we propose that LLMs can be used to generate reference documents that can act as a pivot between relevant and non-relevant documents in a ranked list. We propose methods to use these generated reference documents for RLT as well as for efficient listwise reranking. While reranking, we process the ranked list in either parallel batches of non-overlapping windows or overlapping windows with adaptive strides, improving the existing fixed stride setup. The generated reference documents are also shown to improve existing efficient listwise reranking frameworks. Experiments on TREC Deep Learning benchmarks show that our approach outperforms existing RLT-based approaches. In-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks demonstrate that our proposed methods accelerate LLM-based listwise reranking by up to 66% compared to existing approaches. This work not only establishes a practical paradigm for efficient LLM-based reranking but also provides insight into the capability of LLMs to generate semantically controlled documents using relevance signals.



1.
Introduction

Retrieval and reranking pipelines are increasingly being adopted in search systems. Managing the efficiency-effectiveness trade-off for multi-stage retrieval pipelines poses a significant challenge due to the use of effective but computationally expensive reranking models
(
Ma et al. 2023
;
Wu et al. 2021
;
Lien et al. 2019a
)
. Reranking strategies vary significantly across models and can be broadly categorized into pointwise, pairwise, and listwise setups
(
Pradeep et al. 2021a
;
Qin et al. 2024
;
Sinhababu et al. 2024
;
Sinhababu et al. 2025
;
Sun et al. 2023
)
.

Typically, in a pointwise or pairwise setup, the first-stage retrieve candidate list is pruned before reranking
(
Nogueira and Cho 2019
)
. This method of pruning before reranking in a typical “
retrieve then rerank
” setup is known as ranked list truncation (RLT) and has been widely adopted in reranking pipelines
(
Lien et al. 2019b
;
Meng et al. 2024
)
. However, recent literature shows that a fixed cut-off or score-based heuristic method is often suboptimal because they ignore query-specific relevance distributions and reranking gains
(
Bahri et al. 2020
)
. Most existing approaches view truncation as a selection over the retrieved list, without exploiting richer semantic structure
(
Gao et al. 2022
)
. Additionally, there are instances where reranking a larger ranked list without truncation is beneficial to facilitate performance gains, particularly for listwise LLM rerankers
(
Sun et al. 2023
;
Pradeep et al. 2023b
)
. However, the improvement in retrieval performance comes at the expense of added reranking time, which harms the overall responsiveness of retrieval pipelines, particularly in real-time applications
(
Pradeep et al. 2021b
)
.

Recent studies indicate that LLMs demonstrate strong capabilities in understanding semantics and query-document relationships
(
Thomas et al. 2024
;
Faggioli et al. 2023b
)
. In particular, LLMs have been successfully utilised for zero-shot, few-shot, and instruction-tuned relevance judgment, surpassing even human annotators in terms of consistency and completeness
(
Faggioli et al. 2023b
)
. However, while research has extensively explored the capability of LLMs for relevance judgment, their potential to generate semantically controlled reference-documents within retrieval pipelines remains largely unexplored
(
Shen et al. 2025
)
.

Our work investigates the complementary role of LLMs in multi-stage retrieval, utilizing them to generate a moderately relevant reference-document for a given query and then leveraging this document as a semantic pivot to distinguish between highly relevant and non-relevant documents in a ranked list. The idea of leveraging a semantic pivot enables designing a new RLT approach for efficient reranking in pointwise and pairwise setups that is both adaptive and model-agnostic, thereby improving efficiency without requiring explicit model training or parameter tuning. In listwise reranking, for which LLMs are now commonly used, the ranked list is often partitioned into overlapping windows and processed sequentially in batches to reduce the context length, known as the sliding window
(
Sinhababu et al. 2025
;
Parry et al. 2024
)
. Using the generated pivot, we propose methods that can process the ranked list in either parallel batches of non-overlapping windows or overlapping windows with adaptive strides, improving the existing fixed stride setup. We further investigate the application of this semantic pivot in existing methods that require partitioning to facilitate efficient listwise reranking.

Our Contributions.

The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

(1)

LLM-generated semantic reference document:
We leverage the generation capability of LLMs to generate a semantically controlled pivot-document that acts as a
pivot
distinguishing relevant from non-relevant documents within a ranked list.

(2)

Adaptive truncation via pivot placement:
We propose PSI-Rank to utilize the generated pivot for RLT in pointwise and pairwise setups, wherein the retriever itself determines the pivot’s position in the ranked list. This approach requires only a single model inference call for scoring the pivot, thereby improving reranking efficiency without exhaustive scoring.

(3)

Parallel listwise reranking through document propagation:
In listwise reranking, we incorporate the semantic pivot to enable document propagation across non-overlapping windows (SNOW), facilitating parallel batch processing and substantially reducing inference latency.

(4)

Adaptive stride mechanism for sliding windows:
We extend the conventional sliding window strategy by introducing an
adaptive stride
mechanism (VS-Sliding). The pivot dynamically estimates relevance density across reranking windows to balance contextual completeness and computational efficiency.

(5)

Enhanced anchor for TD-Part framework:
We adapt the TD-Part algorithm for efficient listwise reranking and demonstrate that substituting the conventional top-document anchor with our LLM-generated pivot yields improved relevance consistency and ranking stability (GPTD-Part).

Our experiments across in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks demonstrate that our truncation mechanism outperforms existing RLT-based approaches, while requiring no task-specific training or sensitive hyperparameter tuning. Furthermore, our method accelerates LLM-based list-wise reranking by up to 66% relative to strong baselines, while maintaining, and in many cases improving, retrieval effectiveness. We highlight not only the practical significance of using LLM-generated documents to improve RLT but also offer insight into their robustness, model dependence, and application in multi-stage retrieval pipelines. The repository containing environment details and implementations of our proposed methods is shared for research and development

Related Work

Ranked List Truncation

Traditionally, RLT has been formulated as a cascade ranking problem. These methods determine the optimal cut-off depth for a ranked list before reranking to balance effectiveness against efficiency. Lien et al.
(
Lien et al. 2019b
)
explored efficient top-
k
processing by dynamically pruning candidates unlikely to affect the final evaluation metric.

For neural retrieval, RLT has evolved from heuristic score-based thresholding to learned models. Bahri et al.
(
Bahri et al. 2020
)
introduced
Choppy
, a transformer-based model that predicts query-specific cut-offs by analyzing the score distribution of the top retrieved documents. Similarly, Wu et al.
(
Wu et al. 2021
)
proposed
AttnCut
, which utilizes an attention mechanism to model the dependencies between document scores and the optimal truncation point. More recently, Meng et al.
(
Meng et al. 2024
)
investigated RLT specifically for reranking pipelines, demonstrating that fixed-depth reranking is often suboptimal and that dynamic truncation can significantly improve the efficiency of LLM-based rerankers.

LLM-based reranking

The recent developments in LLM capabilities and scale, especially large context windows, have led to the emergence of zero-shot listwise reranking with generative ranking models
(
Tang et al. 2024
;
Gangi Reddy et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2024
;
Sun et al. 2023
)
. Listwise objectives better capture inter-document dependencies than pointwise or pairwise methods or traditional cross-encoders
(
Sun et al. 2023
)
. Distilled LLMs, trained via permutation distillation on teacher rankings from zero-shot prompts, outperform much larger supervised cross-encoders like monoT5
(
Sun et al. 2023
;
Pradeep et al. 2023a
;
Pradeep et al. 2023b
)

Despite these gains, inference efficiency remains the main challenge while deploying listwise LLM rankers at scale. Recent approaches, such as FIRST and its variants, limit generation to a single identifier token, thereby improving retrieval efficiency
(
Gangi Reddy et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2025
)
. Li et al.
(
Li et al. 2025
)
use documents from the ranked list as references for efficient zeroshot LLM reranking. Parry et al.
(
Parry et al. 2024
)
proposed Top-Down Partitioning (TDPart), which replaces window traversal with a pivot-guided selection that can be reranked in parallel.

LLMs for Judgment and Generation

The capabilities of LLMs extend beyond ranking to relevance judgment and data generation. Thomas et al.
(
Thomas et al. 2024
)
and Faggioli et al.
(
Faggioli et al. 2023b
)
have benchmarked LLMs extensively as automated relevance judges. Upadhyay et al. propose UMBRELA for relevance judgments with zeroshot and fewshot prompting. Meng et al.
(
Meng et al. 2026
)
employ reranking frameworks for improved relevance assessment. These frameworks demonstrate the LLM’s intrinsic understanding of query-document relevance relationships
(
Upadhyay et al. 2024
)
.

Consequently, LLMs have been successfully employed to generate synthetic training data to enable high performance dense retrieval with minimal examples
(
Shen et al. 2025
;
Dai et al. 2022
)
. Approaches like
HyDE

(
Gao et al. 2022
)
and
Query2Doc

(
Wang et al. 2023
)
generate hypothetical documents to improve retrieval via zeroshot expansion. Ruiyang et al.
(
Ren et al. 2025
)
use finetuned LLMs to generate global scores for the documents in the ranked list, improving listwise reranking efficiency.

3.
Reference-Document Generation and its Utility

In this section, we propose that an LLM can generate a moderately relevant answer (
reference-document
) to a query. This reference-document, based on its ranking position, can serve as a pivot in a ranked list to separate relevant from non-relevant documents.

Motivation

Prior work has shown that LLMs have been successfully utilized to generate relevance judgments for query document pairs
(
Rahmani et al. 2024
;
Upadhyay et al. 2024
;
Faggioli et al. 2023a
)
. LLMs serve as effective judges for relevance in IR collections, often demonstrating high correlation with human annotators
(
Rahmani et al. 2024
;
Faggioli et al. 2023a
)
. This implies that LLMs can understand graded relevance between query-document pairs when provided with a relevance scale similar to that of human assessors
(
Upadhyay et al. 2024
)
. However, the focus of existing strategies remains solely on LLMs as judges, rather than generating documents with a specified relevance label.

Crucially, we experiment on the capability of LLMs to generate documents of a particular relevance label. We acknowledge that the capability of LLMs in document generation controlled with varying levels of relevance can be useful. In particular, we are focused on generating documents with moderate relevance, as these can serve as a controlling pivot between highly relevant and less relevant documents.

3.1.
Generative Pointwise Reranking

We propose
Ranked List
P
artitioning using
S
ingle
I
nference
(
PSI-Rank
), which uses the same retriever to score the pivot document, that retrieves the ranked list as shown in Figure
3
. This requires only a single additional inference (scoring the pivot), resulting in negligible inference cost for positioning the pivot within the ranked list. Documents that are scored higher than the pivot are considered more promising candidates and, therefore, only those documents are reranked. We obtain the final reranked list by appending the remaining documents to the reranked list.

Formally, we consider
\theta(Q)_{m}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
, where
\theta
is the initial ranker that retrieves top-
m
documents in a multi-stage retrieval pipeline. Following which, a reranker
\phi
reranks the top-
n
candidates from the initial ranked list
\theta(Q)_{m}
, producing a reordered sub-list
\phi(Q,\theta(Q)_{m})_{n}
, where
m\geq n
. PSI-Rank employs two complementary truncation strategies, depending on the availability of calibration data (i.e., topics from a similar collection).

Dynamic Cutoff (Per-Query Hard Boundary)

Our method
PSI-Rank
Dyn
controls the semantic anchoring of
D^{*}
to establish precise, query-adaptive truncation without requiring any parametric supervision compulsory in existing dynamic RLT methods
(
Bahri et al. 2020
)
. We score
D^{*}
using
\theta(Q,D^{*})
such that the current ranked list now becomes
\{D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D^{*},\ldots,D_{m-1},D_{m}\}
. We partition the ranked list into
D^{+}=\{D_{i}\in\theta(Q)_{m}\mid\theta(Q,D_{i})>\theta(Q,D^{*})\}
and
D^{-}=\{D_{i}\in\theta(Q)_{m}\mid\theta(Q,D_{i})<\theta(Q,D^{*})\}
. Then we rerank
D^{+}
using
Z=\phi(Q,D^{+})_{n}
, where
n=|D^{+}|
defines the dynamic reranking depth. The final ranked list is constructed by concatenating the reranked list with the remaining ranked list:
\mathcal{R}=Z\oplus D^{-}
.

Average Cutoff (Query Set Statistics)

We calculate cutoff from a calibration query set by calculating the mean pivot score
\bar{\theta}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\theta(Q,D^{*})
, where
\mathcal{C}
denotes the calibration queries. In contrast to the dynamic mechanism, which can be influenced by query-specific bias, our method
PSI-Rank
Avg
relies on a global instance
\bar{\theta}
that remains fixed across all test queries
Q\notin\mathcal{C}
. This mitigates per-query outliers in pivot scoring, yielding consistent reranking depths across diverse queries. This provides statistical stability at the cost of query adaptivity. The ranked list is then partitioned using
\bar{\theta}
into
D^{+}=\{D_{i}\in\theta(Q)_{m}\mid\theta(Q,D_{i})>\bar{\theta}\}
and
D^{-}=\{D_{i}\in\theta(Q)_{m}\mid\theta(Q,D_{i})<\bar{\theta}\}
, followed by reranking
Z=\phi(Q,D^{+})_{n}
, where
n=|D^{+}|
. We yield the final list as
\mathcal{R}=Z\oplus D^{-}
.

This dual-mechanism design ensures PSI-Rank remains retriever-agnostic, requiring only a single-inference pivot generation per query, and eliminates the need for learned thresholds, embedding models, or query-specific hyperparameter tuning, unlike prior RLT approaches
(
Wu et al. 2021
;
Meng et al. 2024
)
.

RLT using Pivot

LLM-based reranking is typically performed using pointwise, pairwise, or listwise strategies, depending on the model and type of task
(
Sinhababu et al. 2025
;
Sinhababu et al. 2024
;
Qin et al. 2024
)
. Among these reranking strategies, the listwise approach is generally preferred due to the fewer inferences and contextual comparisons required
(
Sinhababu et al. 2025
;
Sun et al. 2023
)
.

Background

Listwise ranking is implemented using either a single window or a sliding window approach. However, existing literature suggests that a single window approach can rerank approximately 20 documents from the first stage, limiting its applicability when more documents need to be reranked
(
Sinhababu et al. 2025
;
Parry et al. 2024
)
. In contrast, the sliding window can rerank a larger number of documents by overlapping windows that slide across the entire document span. The use of overlaps ensures that potentially relevant documents are propagated across adjacent windows, allowing documents initially positioned at lower ranks to be promoted toward the top positions. Since this method lets the model focus on smaller windows iteratively, it enables reranking without exceeding model context limits. However, the drawback of this strategy is the need for
\approx n/S
serial inferences, where
n
is the number of documents to be reranked and
S
is the stride.

Reranking using Non-Overlapping Window

Our proposed solution is to consider multiple single windows that contain non-overlapping sets of documents, i.e., implementing a sliding window with a stride equal to the window size
(S=w)
. We name our reranking method as
S
hared
N
on-
O
verlapping
W
indow
(
SNOW
). Under this strategy, rankings are generated independently for each localized window. However, the absence of overlapping documents between windows restricts the possibility of repositioning relevant documents from one window to another, thereby limiting the overall effectiveness. Therefore, to make this approach functional, it is essential to avoid this restriction and allow for the propagation of documents across adjacent windows.

SNOW employs the same pivot document
D^{*}
generated by PSI-Rank as a
shared semantic reference point
across all non-overlapping windows. Unlike traditional sliding window approaches that rely on overlapping documents for propagation, SNOW injects
D^{*}
into each window independently. This design exploits the pivot’s query-specific relevance boundary, thereby enabling document propagation without sequential dependencies. Documents are promoted or demoted based on their ranking relative to
D^{*}
within their respective windows as shown in Figure
3
. Critically, this requires no additional pivot generation or retrieval scoring beyond the single inference already performed in PSI-Rank, making it computationally efficient while maintaining semantic consistency across all parallel reranking operations.

Formally, the ranked list from the first stage ranker is partitioned into
K=\lfloor m/w\rfloor
non-overlapping windows. Considering
j=[1,\dots,K]
windows, each batch is
{W}_{j}=\{D_{(j-1)w+1},\ldots,D_{jw}\}
. For each window,
D^{*}
is appended to form
W^{\prime}_{j}=W_{j}\cup\{D^{*}\}
, followed by listwise reranking using
\phi(Q,W^{\prime}_{j})\rightarrow D^{+}_{j}>\{D^{*}\}>D^{-}_{j}
, where
D^{+}_{j}=\{D_{i}\in W_{j}\mid\phi(Q,D_{i})>\phi(Q,D^{*})\}
and
D^{-}_{j}=\{D_{i}\in W_{j}\mid\phi(Q,D_{i})<\phi(Q,D^{*})\}
denotes the subset of documents ranked higher than the pivot, leveraging pivot promotion to reveal query-adaptive local relevance boundaries. Global high-priority (
D^{+}=D^{+}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus D^{+}_{K}
) and low-priority (
D^{-}=D^{-}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus D^{-}_{K}
) lists are concatenated to preserve cross-window ordering via shared pivot semantics, before a final single-window pass
\mathcal{R}=\phi(Q,(D^{+}\oplus D^{-}))_{w}
captures global promotions within context limits.

Stride Adaptation in Sliding Window

Here, we present another application of the generated pivot in a sliding window setup. We observe that the standard sliding window technique uses a fixed stride value over the entire ranked list. However, in a reranking setup, the density of actual relevant documents at the end of the list is relatively lower compared to the initial portion. This is because reranking is performed on a list generated by a first-stage ranker. With this, we introduce
V
ariable
S
tride-
Sliding
Window
(
VS-Sliding
) reranking, a framework that enhances the existing sliding window mechanism by allowing for an adaptable stride as shown in Figure
3
.

Given the initial ranked list
\theta(Q)_{m}
, VS-Sliding processes list from the end using adaptive stride
S_{t}
initialized as
S_{1}=S_{\text{max}}
. Here,
S_{\text{max}}
is the maximum stride, which is by default equal to the window size
w
(which is a hyper-parameter in standard sliding window based approaches), i.e.,
S_{max}=w
. Starting at position
p_{1}=m
, each window
W_{t}=\{D_{p_{t}-w+1},\ldots,D_{p_{t}}\}
undergoes PSI-Rank
Dyn
with listwise reranker
\phi(Q,W_{t}\cup\{D^{*}\})\rightarrow D^{+}_{j}>\{D^{*}\}>D^{-}_{j}
where
D^{+}_{j}=\{D_{i}\in W_{t}\mid\phi(Q,D_{i})>\phi(Q,D^{*})\}
. The next position advances as
p_{t+1}=p_{t}-S_{t+1}
where
S_{t+1}=\max(1,\min(S_{\text{max}},W-|D^{+}_{j}|))
, continuing until
p_{t}\leq W
. This density-adaptive mechanism accelerates through sparse tail regions (low
|D^{+}_{j}|
) by skipping non-relevant documents, while decelerating in relevant-dense regions (high
|D^{+}_{j}|
).

Generative Top-Down Partitioning

Top-Down Partitioning (
TDPart
)
(
Parry et al. 2024
)
improves reranking efficiency by recursively partitioning the search space against a pivot element selected from the top-

G

P

D

Unlike TDPart, GPTD-Part injects

k_{p}=\text{rank}_{\theta}(D^{*}\mid\theta(Q)_{m})
using the first-stage retriever
\theta

initializes the cutoff boundary. Subsequently, the listwise reranker
\phi

W_{\text{top}}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{w},D^{*}\}
and deeper partitions, anchoring all comparisons to
D^{*}
. Formally, for any candidate within
W

D^{+}
as
D^{+}=\{D_{i}\in W\mid\phi(Q,D_{i})>\phi(Q,D^{*})\}
. This ensures that document promotion is governed by a query-specific, semantically grounded threshold rather than arbitrary retrieval artifacts. GPTD-Part is resilient against retrieval noise while retaining the parallel efficiency of top-down traversal. We have outlined the GPTD-Part method in Figure
3
.

4.
Evaluation

In this section, we provide empirical evidence and experimental details to demonstrate the utility of the generated pivot in RLT and LLM-based listwise rerankers. The effectiveness of our proposed approach depends on several critical assumptions, which we systematically investigate through three research questions.

4.1.
Research Questions

The fundamental hypothesis of our approach is that LLM-generated pivots can serve as an adaptive threshold between relevant and non-relevant documents. Unlike fixed thresholds that remain constant across queries, our pivots should theoretically adjust based on query complexity, domain characteristics, and the distributional properties of retrieved candidates. This question examines whether our generated pivots genuinely exhibit threshold-like behavior, creating meaningful boundaries in the ranking space, or merely act as arbitrary reference points. Understanding this property is essential for our adaptive RLT, making our first research question:

RQ1:

Does the generated pivot function as a dynamic threshold for RLT in pointwise and pairwise reranking setups?

Even if pivots function as dynamic thresholds, their utility depends on whether they translate into measurable ranking improvements. Listwise reranking with LLMs faces challenges due to significantly higher inference overhead compared to other reranking methods. We investigate whether pivot-guided comparisons help LLMs make more accurate relative judgments by providing stable reference points throughout the ranking process. This question directly addresses the practical value of our generated reference-document and therefore makes our second research question:

RQ2:

Does the generated pivot effectively improve LLM listwise reranking performance and efficiency?

The quality of generated pivots likely depends on the generating LLM’s understanding of relevance, domain knowledge, and text generation capabilities. If ranking performance varies substantially based on which model generates the pivot, this suggests that pivot generation itself is a critical bottleneck that requires careful model selection. Conversely, if diverse LLMs produce functionally equivalent pivots, it indicates that RLT is robust to variations in pivot quality. This question addresses whether investing in stronger models specifically for pivot generation is necessary or whether lightweight models are sufficient, making our third research question:

RQ3:

Does the ranking effectiveness significantly change with pivots generated by different LLMs?

4.3.
Evaluation Measures

To assess the performance of the proposed approach against the baselines, three key metrics that capture both the effectiveness and efficiency of the ranking pipelines are employed.

•

Mean Average Precision (MAP):
We report the mean average precision (MAP) at cut-off 100 (MAP@100) with the binary relevance threshold set to 2 for DL-19 and DL-2020 topics only.

•

Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG):
We use nDCG as the standard measure because it accounts for a document’s relevance and its position in the ranked list. We report this metric for both in-domain and out-of-domain evaluation
(
Thakur et al. 2021
)
.

•

Inferences Per-Query (IPQ):
Since the initial ranked list is truncated before reranking in pointwise and pairwise setups, we report IPQ by dividing the total inferences by the number of queries in the test set
1
. A method with a lower IPQ is considered better when overall performance is not significantly reduced.

•

Speed-Up (SU):

To quantify efficiency gains in listwise reranking setups, we report the proportional gains in run-time relative to the sliding window baseline. SU values greater than 1 indicate an improvement in efficiency. We include the time required to generate the pivot in the execution time for methods that use it.

5.
Conclusion

Our empirical analysis confirms that LLM-generated pivot documents function as effective semantic thresholds for adaptive ranked list truncation (RLT) in both pointwise and pairwise reranking pipelines, achieving competitive MAP and nDCG@10 scores with 35-66% inference reductions across in-domain and out-of-domain collections. Our proposed pivot-guided listwise reranking methods (SNOW, VS-Sliding, and GPTD-Part) consistently outperform baselines across test collections while showing stability across multiple LLMs (RankZephyr, RankVicuna, RankGPT). Our methods in listwise setups achieved the significant efficiency gain while conserving or improving performance. Pivot generation proves robust across different models, exhibiting very low variance. These findings establish LLM-generated pivots as stable anchors that enhance multi-stage reranking efficiency and stability without model training, solely by leveraging LLMs’ generative capabilities.
