Theta correspondence and the Borisov-Gunnells relations
Romain Branchereau

Abstract
We consider a geometric theta correspondence from the first homology of a modular curve, to modular forms of weight 2 2 . Using Stevens’ description of the homology, we find that this map sends modular symbols to product of weight one Eisenstein series, modular caps to weight 2 2 Eisenstein series, and hyperbolic cycles to diagonal restrictions of Hilbert-Eisenstein series. We use it to revisit work of Borisov and Gunnells, and explain its connection to a theorem of Li. In particular, we give a geometric proof of certain relations between Eisenstein series.



1.  Introduction

Let
Y_{1}(N)
be the modular curve with
N\geq 4
. In previous work
[
8
]
, we combined ideas due to Bergeron-Charollois-Garcia
[
2
,
3
]
and Kudla-Millson
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
to construct a closed differential form

\displaystyle{E}(z,\tau)\in\Omega^{1}(Y_{1}(N))\otimes C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})

(1.1)

that transforms in the variable
\tau
as a modular form of weight
2
for
\Gamma_{1}(N)
. The key feature of this construction is that it induces a theta lift

\displaystyle{E}\colon H_{1}(Y_{1}(N);\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow M_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N)),\qquad\pazocal{Z}\longrightarrow\int_{\pazocal{Z}}{E}(z,\tau)

(1.2)

to holomorphic modular forms, whose Fourier coefficients are Poincaré duals of linear combinations of modular symbols. More precisely, we have the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T_{n}\{0,\infty\}
be the Hecke translate of the modular symbol
\{0,\infty\}
, and
\mathrm{PD}(T_{n}\{0,\infty\})
its Poincaré dual in
H^{1}(Y_{1}(N);\mathbb{Z})
. In cohomology, the differential form
{E}
has a Fourier expansion

\displaystyle[{E}]=-\frac{1}{2i\pi}d\log(g_{0,1})-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{PD}(T_{n}\{0,\infty\})q^{n}\in H^{1}(Y_{1}(N);\mathbb{Q})\otimes M_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N)),\qquad q=e^{2i\pi\tau},

(1.3)

where
g_{0,1}
is a Siegel unit.

For an integer
r
, we define the Eisenstein series of weight
k

\displaystyle G_{r}^{(k)}(\tau)\coloneqq N\frac{(k-1)!}{(-2i\pi)^{k}}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(mN\tau+n)^{k}|mN\tau+n|^{2s}}e\left(-\frac{rn}{N}\right)

(1.4)

by analytic continuation. We will also need the Eisenstein series

\displaystyle\widehat{G}_{r}^{(k)}(\tau)\coloneqq\frac{(k-1)!}{(-2i\pi)^{k}}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
m\equiv r\mod{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m\tau+n)^{k}|m\tau+n|^{2s}}.

(1.5)

Both define holomorphic Eisenstein series in
E_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
, except in some cases when weight
k=2
. To get a holomorphic Eisenstein series of weight
2
, we set for
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle H_{p,q}^{(2)}\coloneqq G^{(2)}_{q}-\delta_{q0}\widehat{G}^{(2)}_{p}\in E_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))

(1.6)

for two integers
p,q
.
By passing from homology to cohomology, the map (
1.2
) restricts to the map considered by Borisov-Gunnells in
[
5
, Theorem. 4.11]
. Let
S^{\textrm{new}}_{2,\mathrm{rk}=0}(\Gamma)\subseteq S_{2}(\Gamma)
be the subspace spanned by rank
0
newforms, and
\pazocal{H}^{(2)}\subset E_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
be the subspace spanned by the forms
G^{(2)}_{q}(\tau)
with
q\not\equiv 0\mod{N}
, and the forms
H^{(2)}_{p,0}
with
p\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

Corollary 1.1.1
.

Let
\mathrm{Im}({E})
be the image of the theta lift (
1.2
). We have the inclusion

\displaystyle\pazocal{H}^{(2)}\oplus S^{\textrm{new}}_{2,\mathrm{rk}=0}(\Gamma_{1}(N))\subseteq\mathrm{Im}({E}),

(1.7)

which is an equality when
N
is prime.

Two complementary results in the literature describe spanning sets of
S^{\textrm{new}}_{2,\mathrm{rk}=0}(\Gamma)
. For
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}(N)
, Li proves in
[
21
]
that this space is spanned by diagonal restrictions of certain Hilbert-Eisenstein series. On the other hand, Borisov-Gunnells show in
[
5
]
that it can also be spanned by Eisenstein series of weight
2
, and products of weight
1
Eisenstein series.

The first goal of this paper is to explain how both results arise naturally from the above theta lift, by evaluating it on appropriate generators of the homology. The second goal is to use this theta lift to obtain relations among Eisenstein series. The main novelty lies in the use of the theta correspondence, which moreover admits a natural generalization to a correspondence from the
(n-1)
-st homology of a symmetric space of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(n)
to modular forms of weight
n
, leading to corresponding generalizations of the results obtained here.

1.1. Evaluation on hyperbolic matrices and Li’s result

Every class in
H_{1}(Y_{1}(N);\mathbb{Z})
is represented by a cycle

\displaystyle\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma}\coloneqq\{z_{0},\gamma z_{0}\},\qquad\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}(N).

(1.8)

As explained in
[
8
]
in the case of the modular curve
Y_{0}(p)
, we can recover Li’s result by evaluating the theta lift on cycles
\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma}
, with
\gamma
a hyperbolic matrix. This argument extends naturally to
\Gamma_{1}(N)
.

Let
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
be a real quadratic field, with ring of integers
{O}_{F}
. Let
\mathfrak{f}\subset F
be a lattice of rank
2
, and
{O}\subset{O}_{F}
be the order that preserves this lattice. The subgroup of units
U\coloneqq{O}^{\times,+}\cap(1+N\mathfrak{f})\subset{O}^{\times,+}
preserves the coset
1+N\mathfrak{f}
. We define the Hilbert-Eisenstein series

\displaystyle E_{1,\mathfrak{f}}^{(k)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\coloneqq(-1)^{k}\frac{D^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{(2i\pi)^{2k}}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{(m,n)\in(1+N\mathfrak{f})\times N\mathfrak{f}/U}\frac{(yy^{\prime})^{s}}{\mathrm{N}(m\tau+n)^{k}|\mathrm{N}(m\tau+n)|^{2s}},

(1.9)

of parallel weight
k
, for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}(N\mathfrak{f})\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}({O})
. The second part of the following theorem result can be compared to
[
21
, Theorem. 1.2]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
be a primitive hyperbolic matrix in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
, let
D\coloneqq\mathrm{tr}(\gamma)^{2}-4
, and
\mathfrak{f}=\mathbb{Z}+\nu\mathbb{Z}
with
\nu=\frac{1}{2c}(a-d-\sqrt{D})
. Then

\displaystyle{E}(\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma})=E_{1,\mathfrak{f}}^{(1)}\left(\tau,\tau\right).

(1.10)

Hence, every form in
\pazocal{H}^{(2)}\oplus S^{\textrm{new}}_{2,\mathrm{rk}=0}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
is a linear combination of such modular forms.

1.2. Modular caps, modular symbols and the result of Borisov-Gunnells

The main result of this paper is to recover the results of Borisov-Gunnells from the theta lift.

We use Stevens’
[
25
]
description of the homology of the modular curve. Let
\overline{Y_{1}(N)}^{\textrm{BS}}
be the Borel-Serre compactification of
Y_{1}(N)
. From the long exact sequence in homology, one can write

\displaystyle H_{1}(Y_{1}(N);\mathbb{Z})\simeq H_{1}(\overline{Y_{1}(N)}^{\textrm{BS}};\mathbb{Z})\simeq\pazocal{C}(\mathbb{Z})\oplus\pazocal{M}\pazocal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}),

(1.11)

where the space
\pazocal{C}(\mathbb{Z})
is spanned by modular caps
\pazocal{C}_{r}
(a loop around the cusp
r
), and
\pazocal{M}\pazocal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
is spanned by modular symbols of degree
0
. Thus, it suffices to evaluate the periods of
{E}(z,\tau)
over modular caps and modular symbols.

Theorem 1.3
.

1.

Let
\pazocal{C}_{r}
be the modular cap around the cusp
r=\gamma_{r}\infty
for some matrix
\gamma_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The period of
{E}(z,\tau)
over
\pazocal{C}_{r}
is
H^{(2)}_{d,-c}(\tau)
.

2.

Let
\pazocal{M}=\gamma\{0,\infty\}
be the unimodular symbol with
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The period of
{E}(z,\tau)
over
\pazocal{M}
is
-G_{d}^{(1)}(\tau)G_{c}^{(1)}(\tau)
.

Remark 1.1
.

The theta lift (
1.2
) is similar to a cocycle constructed in
[
2
, Theorem. 5]
or
[
3
, Théorème p. 2.10]
, without the geometric interpretation of the Fourier coefficients. The construction in
loc. cit.
also uses the Mathai-Quillen formalism
[
22
]
, as well as previous work of Charollois-Sczech
[
12
]
. The cocycle is given by a product of weight one Eisenstein series, so that the case
N=2
of
[
3
, Théorème p. 2.10]
is analogous to the second part of Theorem
1.3
. A result similar to ours has also been obtained by Xu
[
27
]
, where the cocycle of Bergeron-Charollois-Garcia is computed explictly in terms of Kronecker-Eisenstein series of weights
1
and
2
, using the Bruhat decomposition. Comparable results in the setting of
\mathrm{SL}_{N}({O}_{K})
over an imaginary quadratic field can be found in
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
. Finally, the connection to the work of Borisov-Gunnells was already suggested in
[
2
, Example p. 8]
and
[
3
, Remark p. 30]
.

Next, we would like to write any cycle
\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma}
as a linear combination of modular caps and unimodular symbols, with respect to the splitting (
1.11
). First, if
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
, then it is easy to see that
\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma}
can be pushed to the cusp and it is equal to
n\pazocal{C}_{\infty}
. The same can be done for any parabolic matrix, after translation.

We represent
\mathbb{H}
in the Poincaré disc model. The polygon

For hyperbolic matrices, this can be achieved by adapting the continued fraction algorithm explained in

. Suppose that
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
with

. Consider the continued fraction expansion of

\displaystyle\frac{a}{c}=b_{0}-\cfrac{1}{b_{1}-\cfrac{1}{\cdots-\cfrac{1}{b_{n-1}-\cfrac{1}{b_{n}}}}}

(1.12)

with

and convergents
\frac{p_{0}}{q_{0}}=\frac{b_{0}}{1},\frac{p_{1}}{q_{1}},\dots,\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}=\frac{a}{c}
. Set

and

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}-p_{k}&p_{k-1}\\

(1.13)

As shown in Figure

whose boundary is

plus a linear combination of modular caps and unimodular symbols. It follows that we can write the cycle in homology as

\displaystyle\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma}=\left[(b_{0}+bq_{n-1}-p_{n-1}d)\pazocal{C}_{\infty}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}b_{k+1}\pazocal{C}_{\gamma_{k}\infty}\right]\oplus\sum_{k=0}^{n}\gamma_{k}\{0,\infty\}\in\pazocal{C}(\mathbb{Z})\oplus\pazocal{M}\pazocal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}).

(1.14)

By combining this with the previous result, we deduce the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
is a hyperbolic matrix in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
, then

\displaystyle{E}(\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma})=(b_{0}-p_{n-1}d+bq_{n-1})H_{1,0}^{(2)}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}b_{k+1}H^{(2)}_{q_{k-1},q_{k}}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{q_{k}}^{(1)}G_{q_{k-1}}^{(1)}.

(1.15)

If we denote by
\pazocal{H}^{(1,1)}
the span of the products
G^{(1)}_{a}G^{(1)}_{b}
, then it follows from Corollary
1.1.1
that

\displaystyle\pazocal{H}^{(2)}\oplus S^{\textrm{new}}_{2,\mathrm{rk}=0}(\Gamma_{1}(N))\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{\pazocal{H}^{(2)},\pazocal{H}^{(1,1)}\}

(1.16)

Remark 1.2
.

The second part of this theorem can be compared with
[
5
, Theorem. 4.11]
. Note that the result in
loc. cit.
is stronger, as it proves an equality.

Remark 1.3
.

There have been several results on spanning modular forms by Eisenstein series, extending and generalizing the work of Borisov-Gunnells; e.g. by Dickson-Neururer
[
14
]
, Xue
[
28
]
, or Raum-Xia
[
24
]
.

1.3. Relation between Eisenstein series

Finally, we can use the theta lift to deduce relations between Eisenstein series. Let
\pazocal{T}\subset\mathbb{H}
be a hyperbolic triangle whose sides are three unimodular symbols and closed by three modular caps, as in Figure
2
. The image of the boundary
\partial\pazocal{T}
is trivial in
H_{1}(Y_{1}(N);\mathbb{Z})
, so that
{E}(\partial\pazocal{T})=0
. The boundary
\partial\pazocal{T}
is a linear combination of unimodular symbols and modular caps, whose image have been computed in Theorem
1.3
. We deduce the following result, similar to
[
5
, Proposition. 3.7]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
a,b,c
be three coprime integers that satisfy
a+b+c\equiv 0\mod N
and such that
a,b,c\neq 0\mod N
. We have

\displaystyle G_{a}^{(1)}G_{b}^{(1)}+G_{b}^{(1)}G_{c}^{(1)}+G_{c}^{(1)}G_{a}^{(1)}=G_{a}^{(2)}+G_{b}^{(2)}+G_{c}^{(2)}.

(1.17)

Remark 1.4
.

Note that the proof of Theorem
1.5
generalizes to the case of
d
Eisenstein series of weight
2
, and
d
products of two Eisenstein series of weight
1
; see Corollary
4.7.1
. We conclude by mentioning several further results in the literature concerning relations among Eisenstein series, such as the recent work of Brunault
[
11
]
, Brunault-Zudilin
[
9
]
, Khuri-Makdisi–Raji
[
15
]
, or Zhang
[
29
]
.

2.  Preliminaries on the modular curve

2.1. Homology classes

Let
Y\coloneqq\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
be the modular curve for some congruence subgroup
\Gamma
that will be
\Gamma(N)
or
\Gamma_{1}(N)
with
N\geq 4
.
By Hurewicz’s theorem, the group homorphism
\Gamma\longrightarrow H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
that sends
\gamma
to the cycle
\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma}\coloneqq\{z_{0},\gamma z_{0}\}
is independent of the choice of the basepoint
z_{0}
and surjective. The trace of a matrix
\gamma
in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
is congruent to
2
modulo
N
. In particular, if
N\geq 4
, then
|\mathrm{tr}(\gamma)|\geq 2
.

Definition 2.1
.

The matrix is parabolic if
|\mathrm{tr}(\gamma)|=2
, in which case it stabilizes a cusp in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. The matrix is hyperbolic if
|\mathrm{tr}(\gamma)|>2
, and in that case it stabilizes two real quadratic points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\smallsetminus\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. Moreover, it is primitive in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
if it cannot be written as a nontrivial power
\gamma=\gamma_{1}^{m}
of another hyperbolic matrix
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{1}(N)
.

2.2. Borel-Serre compactification

We follow Stevens
[
25
]
. Let
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
be the boundary of
\mathbb{H}
and let

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{H}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{r\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})}B_{r}

(2.1)

be the Borel-Serre completion, obtained by gluing horocycles
B_{r}=\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\smallsetminus\{r\}
at the cusp
r
. At
\infty
, the gluing is done such that a sequence
z_{n}=x_{n}+iy_{n}
converges to
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\smallsetminus\{\infty\}\simeq\mathbb{R}
if
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}=\alpha
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}y_{n}=\infty
. At a cusp
r=\gamma^{-1}\infty
, a sequence
z_{n}
converges to
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\smallsetminus\{r\}
if
\gamma z_{n}
converges to
\gamma\alpha
.

The group
\Gamma
acts on
\bigsqcup_{r\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})}B_{r}
by sending

to
\gamma z\in B_{\gamma z}

\displaystyle\overline{Y}^{\textrm{BS}}\coloneqq\Gamma\backslash\overline{\mathbb{H}}

(2.2)

be the Borel-Serre compactification of

\displaystyle\overline{Y}^{\textrm{BS}}=Y\cup\bigsqcup_{r\in C_{N}}\pazocal{C}_{r}

\Gamma

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}z_{n}=\alpha\in B_{r}
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{r}(z_{n})=\pi_{r}(\alpha).

(2.4)

Given two distinct points

, let
\{r_{1},r_{2}\}\in Z_{1}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})
be the geodesic oriented from

to
r_{2}
. If

are in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, we call it a

. Its boundary in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
is

\displaystyle\partial\{r_{1},r_{2}\}=\pi_{r_{2}}(r_{1})-\pi_{r_{1}}(r_{2}).

(2.5)

By abuse of notation, we will also denote by
\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
its image in
Y
, which represents a
1
-cycle

\displaystyle\{r_{1},r_{2}\}\in H_{1}(\overline{Y}^{\textrm{BS}},\partial\overline{Y}^{\textrm{BS}};\mathbb{Z}).

(2.6)

Definition 2.2
.

A modular symbol
\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
is unimodular if there is a matrix
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\{\alpha,\beta\}=\gamma\{0,\infty\}
.

Let us now take complex coefficients. The integration of
1
-cycle on a

. The constant term is

\displaystyle a_{0}(E^{(1)}_{p,q})=\begin{cases}\ \frac{1}{2}\frac{1+\zeta^{q}_{N}}{1-\zeta_{N}^{q}}&\textrm{if}\ p=0,\ q\neq 0\\[14.22636pt]
\ -B_{1}\left(\left\{\frac{p}{N}\right\}\right)&\textrm{if}\ p\neq 0\end{cases}

(2.71)

in weight
k=1
, and

\displaystyle a_{0}(E^{(2)}_{p,q})=-\frac{1}{2}B_{2}\left(\left\{\frac{p}{N}\right\}\right)

(2.72)

in weight
k=2
, where
B_{k}(t)
is the Bernoulli polynomial and
\{t\}
denotes the fractional part of

.

In Theorem
1.5
we stated that

\displaystyle G_{a}^{(1)}G_{b}^{(1)}+G_{b}^{(1)}G_{c}^{(1)}+G_{c}^{(1)}G_{a}^{(1)}=G_{a}^{(2)}+G_{b}^{(2)}+G_{c}^{(2)}

(2.73)

for three integers

that satisfy
a+b+c\equiv 0\mod N
and such that
a,b,c\neq 0\mod N
. We can verify this relation by comparing the constant terms on both sides. Since

, the two Eisenstein series have the same constant term

\displaystyle a_{0}(G_{r}^{(2)})

\displaystyle=a_{0}(E^{(2)}_{r,0})=-\frac{1}{2}B_{2}\left(\left\{\frac{r}{N}\right\}\right),

(2.74)

\displaystyle a_{0}(G_{a}^{(1)}G_{b}^{(1)})

\displaystyle=B_{1}\left(\left\{\frac{a}{N}\right\}\right)B_{1}\left(\left\{\frac{b}{N}\right\}\right).

(2.75)

Finally, the condition
a+b+c\equiv 0\mod N
implies that the numbers
x=\{\frac{a}{N}\}
,
y=\{\frac{b}{N}\}
and
z=\{\frac{c}{N}\}
satisfy
x+y+z=1
or
2
. Finally, taking the constant term of (
2.73
) is consistent with the following relation between Bernoulli polynomials.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
x,y,z
be three real numbers such that
x+y+z=1
or
2
. Then

\displaystyle B_{1}(x)B_{1}(y)+B_{1}(y)B_{1}(z)+B_{1}(z)B_{1}(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(B_{2}(x)+B_{2}(y)+B_{2}(z)\right).

(2.76)

Proof.

We have
B_{1}(t)=t-\frac{1}{2}
and
B_{2}(t)=t^{2}-t+\frac{1}{6}=B_{1}(t)^{2}-\frac{1}{12}
. For three real numbers
x,y,z
, we have

\displaystyle\left(B_{1}(x)+B_{1}(y)+B_{1}(z)\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle B_{2}(x)+B_{2}(y)+B_{2}(z)+\frac{1}{4}

(2.77)

\displaystyle+2\left(B_{1}(x)B_{1}(y)+B_{1}(y)B_{1}(z)+B_{1}(z)B_{1}(x)\right).

(2.78)

Thus, if
x+y+z=1
or
2
, then
B_{1}(x)+B_{1}(y)+B_{1}(z)=\pm\frac{1}{2}
and the equality follows.

3.6. Fourier expansion of the theta lift

Under Poincaré-Lefschetz duality

\displaystyle H_{1}(\overline{Y}^{\textrm{BS}},\partial\overline{Y}^{\textrm{BS}};\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z}),

(3.55)

the cycle
\pazocal{Z}_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\coloneqq\pazocal{Z}_{n}(L_{\mathbf{x}_{0}})
has a Poincaré dual

\displaystyle\mathrm{PD}(\pazocal{Z}_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{0}))\in H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z}),

(3.56)

which is characterized by the property that for any closed form

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\eta\wedge\mathrm{PD}(\pazocal{Z}_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{0}))=\int_{\pazocal{Z}_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{0})}\eta.

(3.57)

If
\eta{Z}
represents the Poincaré dual in

of a cycle
\pazocal{Z}\in H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
, then

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\eta{Z}\wedge\mathrm{PD}(\pazocal{Z}_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{0}))=\langle\pazocal{Z},\pazocal{Z}_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\rangle

(3.58)

is the intersection number between the two cycles.

Theorem 3.6
.

(3.60)

Proof.

[
8

Lemma 3.7
.

The forms
\mathrm{Re}(E^{(2)}_{p,q}(z)dz)
and
E^{(2)}_{p,q}(z)dz
are cohomologous.

Proof.

We show that
\mathrm{Im}(E^{(2)}_{p,q}(z)dz)
is exact, using the same idea as in
[
3
, Lemma. 9.6]
. The weight
0
Kronecker-Eisenstein series (with
(p,q)\neq(0,0)
)

\displaystyle\pazocal{K}_{0}\left(s,z,0,\frac{pz+q}{N}\right)=\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\pi^{s}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{v^{s}}{|mz+n|^{2s}}e\left(\frac{mq-np}{N}\right)

(3.75)

satisfies Kronecker’s second limit formula
[
19
, p. 276]

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}\pazocal{K}_{0}\left(s,z,0,\frac{pz+q}{N}\right)=-2\pi\log|g_{p,q}(z)|

(3.76)

where
g_{p,q}(z)
is the Siegel unit. Moreover, from

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\frac{v^{s}}{|mz+n|^{2s}}=-is\frac{v^{s-1}}{(mz+n)^{2}|mz+n|^{2s-2}}

(3.77)

we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\pazocal{K}_{0}\left(s,z,0,\frac{pz+q}{N}\right)=4is\frac{\pi^{s+1}}{\Gamma(1+s)}\pazocal{K}_{2}\left(1+s,z,0,\frac{pz+q}{N}\right)

(3.78)

for
\mathrm{Re}(s)\gg 0
. Since
(p,q)\neq(0,0)
, the functions admit an analytic continuation to the entire plane and at
s=1
we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2i\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\log|g_{p,q}(z)|=E^{(2)}_{p,q}(z).

(3.79)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2i\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}\log|g_{p,q}(z)|=-\overline{E^{(2)}_{p,q}(z)},

(3.80)

from which we conclude that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2i\pi}d\log|g_{p,q}(z)|=E^{(2)}_{p,q}(z)dz-\overline{E^{(2)}_{p,q}(z)dz}=2i\mathrm{Im}\left(E^{(2)}_{p,q}(z)dz\right).

(3.81)

∎

4.  Integral over modular caps and modular symbols

The next goal will be to compute the image of modular caps and modular symbols under this theta lift. Using the isomorphism
H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\simeq\pazocal{C}(\mathbb{Z})\oplus\pazocal{M}\pazocal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
, we can then express the lift of a cycle
\pazocal{Z}
as a linear combination of lifts of caps and modulars symbols.

4.1. Constant term and integral over modular caps

We start by computing the constant term of
{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau)
, in the boundary component
\pazocal{C}_{\infty}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau)=H^{(2)}_{p,q}(\tau)du,\qquad\qquad(z=u+iv).

(4.1)

Proof.

We compute the limit as
v\rightarrow\infty
of each of the components

\displaystyle{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau,s)_{(d_{1},d_{2})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta_{p,q}(z,t,\tau)_{(d_{1},d_{2})}t^{2s}\frac{dt}{t}.

(4.2)

We start with the
(2,0)
-component. From (
3.113
) we write

\displaystyle\Theta_{p,q}(z,t,\tau)_{(2,0)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{v^{2}t^{2}}{Ny^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{v}}\overline{(n_{1}\tau+n_{2})}^{2}e\left(u\langle m,n\rangle-pn_{2}\right)e^{-\pi vt^{2}\frac{|n_{1}\tau+n_{2}|^{2}}{y}-\pi\frac{v}{t^{2}}\frac{|m_{2}\tau-m_{1}|^{2}}{y}},

(4.3)

where the sum is over
\mathbf{v}=\begin{bmatrix}m_{1}&n_{1}\\
m_{2}&n_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{\ast}_{p,q}
. We split the sum over regular vectors
\begin{bmatrix}m_{1}&n_{1}\\
m_{2}&n_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{\ast}_{p,q}
, for which
m,n\neq 0
, and singular vectors, for which
m=0
or
n=0
. For two real numbers
a,b>0
and a complex number
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
, we define the Bessel function by

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(a^{2}t+b^{2}/t)}t^{\nu}\frac{dt}{t}.

(4.4)

After integrating the theta series from
0
to
\infty
, we find that

\displaystyle{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau,s)_{(2,0)}=\delta_{q0}A(\tau,v,s)+\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}B^{(d)}(\tau,v,s)e(ud),

(4.5)

where for
d\in\mathbb{Z}
the regular term is

\displaystyle B^{(d)}(\tau,v,s)=-\frac{v^{2}}{2Ny^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{bmatrix}m_{1}&n_{1}\\
m_{2}&n_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{\ast}_{p,q}\\
\langle m,n\rangle=d\\
m,n\neq 0\end{subarray}}\overline{(n_{1}\tau+n_{2})}^{2}e(-pn_{2})K_{1+s}\left(\pi v\frac{|n_{1}\tau+n_{2}|}{y},\pi v\frac{|m_{2}\tau-m_{1}|}{y}\right).

(4.6)

The regular term is rapidly decreasing for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
, so that

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau,s)_{(2,0)}=\delta_{q0}A(\tau,v,s).

(4.7)

Since
n_{1}\tau+n_{2}=0
if
(n_{1},n_{2})=(0,0)
, the only singular vectors that contribute to
A(\tau,v,s)
are the vectors
\begin{bmatrix}m_{1}&n_{1}\\
m_{2}&n_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in L_{p,q}^{\ast}
with
(m_{1},m_{2})=(0,0)
. This is only possible if
q=0
(otherwise
A=0
), in which case we have is

\displaystyle A(\tau,v,s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\frac{v^{1-s}\Gamma\left(1+s\right)}{2N\pi^{1+s}y^{1-s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{2}\in\frac{1}{N}{\mathbb{Z}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{n_{1}\tau+n_{2}}}{\left(n_{1}\tau+n_{2}\right)\left|n_{1}\tau+n_{2}\right|^{2s}}e(-pn_{2})

(4.8)

\displaystyle=-\frac{v^{1-s}\Gamma\left(1+s\right)}{2N^{1-2s}\pi^{1+s}y^{1-s}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\overline{n_{1}N\tau+n_{2}}}{\left(n_{1}N\tau+n_{2}\right)\left|n_{1}N\tau+n_{2}\right|^{2s}}e\left(-\frac{pn_{2}}{N}\right).

(4.9)

At
s=0
, it follows from the functional equation that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}A(\tau,v,s)=2vE^{(1,1)}_{p,0}(N\tau)=2v\widehat{E}^{(2)}_{p,0}(N\tau)=2v\widehat{G}^{(2)}_{p}(\tau)

(4.10)

and we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau,s)_{(2,0)}=\delta_{q0}\widehat{G}^{(2)}_{p}du.

(4.11)

The computation for the
(0,2)
-component is analogous and we find that

\displaystyle{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau,s)_{(0,2)}=\widetilde{A}(\tau,v,s)+\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{B}^{(d)}(\tau,v,s)e(ud),

(4.12)

where the sum over the regular terms is rapidly decreasing, and

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}(\tau,v,s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\frac{v^{1+s}}{2N\pi^{1-s}y^{1+s}}\Gamma\left(1-s\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2}\in q+N\mathbb{Z}\\
m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{m_{2}\tau-m_{1}}}{\left(m_{2}\tau-m_{1}\right)\left|m_{2}\tau-m_{1}\right|^{-2s}}.

(4.13)

At
s=0
it is equal to
\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{A}(\tau,v,s)=2vG^{(2)}_{q}(\tau)
and it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau,s)_{(0,2)}=G^{(2)}_{q}(\tau).

(4.14)

Finally, for the component
(1,1)
there are no singular contributions, since the term
(n_{1}\tau+n_{2})(m_{2}\tau-m_{1})
forces the vanishing of the series whenever
m=0
or
n=0
. Thus, the sum is rapidly decreasing as
v\rightarrow\infty
and

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau,s)_{(1,1)}=0.

(4.15)

∎

Proposition 4.2

(Integral over modular caps)
.

Let
\pazocal{C}_{r}
be the closed modular cap at the cusp
r=[m:n]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. Write
r=\gamma_{r}\infty
for some matrix
\gamma_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}m&i\\
n&j\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\pazocal{C}_{r}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=H_{j,-n}^{(2)}(\tau).

(4.16)

Proof.

Since
\int_{\pazocal{C}_{\infty}}du=1
and the integral of
{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau)
along the modular cap
\pazocal{C}_{r}
at the cusp
r
is given by the constant term (in
z
) of
{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau)
at the cusp
r
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\pazocal{C}_{\infty}}{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau)=H^{(2)}_{p,q}(\tau).

(4.17)

From the equivariance of
{E}^{(p,q)}
in (
3.84
), it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{r}\pazocal{C}_{\infty}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=\int_{\pazocal{C}_{\infty}}\gamma_{r}^{\ast}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=\int_{\pazocal{C}_{\infty}}{E}_{j,-n}(z,\tau)=H_{j,-n}^{(2)}(\tau),

(4.18)

since
\gamma_{r}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}j\\
-n\end{pmatrix}
.
∎

4.2. Integral over unimodular symbols

It will be enough to compute the integral of
{E}^{(p,q)}
along the modular symbol
\{0,\infty\}
, the computation over a unimodular symbol
\gamma\{0,\infty\}
follows from translation. Moreover, only the
(1,1)
-component (which is the
dv
-component) contributes to the sum, so that we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{0,\infty\}}{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{E}_{p,q}(iv,\tau)_{(1,1)}\frac{dv}{v}.

(4.19)

From the Poisson summation (
3.113
) it follows that the restriction of the theta series along the modular symbol
\{0,\infty\}
is

\displaystyle\Theta_{p,q}(iv,t,\tau)_{(1,1)}

(4.20)

\displaystyle=-\frac{v^{2}}{Ny^{2}}\sum_{\begin{bmatrix}m_{1}&n_{1}\\
m_{2}&n_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in L_{p,q}^{\ast}}\overline{(n_{1}\tau+n_{2})}\overline{(m_{2}\tau-m_{1})}e\left(-pn_{2}\right)e^{-\pi vt^{2}\frac{|n_{1}\tau+n_{2}|^{2}}{y}-\pi\frac{v}{t^{2}}\frac{|m_{2}\tau-m_{1}|^{2}}{y}}.

(4.21)

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\{0,\infty\}}{E}_{p,q}(iv,\tau,s)_{(1,1)}\frac{dv}{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta_{p,q}(iv,t,\tau)_{(1,1)}t^{2s}\frac{dv}{v}\frac{dt}{t}

(4.22)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{Ny^{2}}\sum_{\begin{bmatrix}m_{1}&n_{1}\\
m_{2}&n_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in L_{p,q}^{\ast}}\overline{(n_{1}\tau+n_{2})}\overline{(m_{2}\tau-m_{1})}e\left(-pn_{2}\right)

(4.23)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\pi vt^{2}\frac{|n_{1}\tau+n_{2}|^{2}}{y}-\pi\frac{v}{t^{2}}\frac{|m_{2}\tau-m_{1}|^{2}}{y}}v^{2}t^{2s}\frac{dv}{v}\frac{dt}{t}.

(4.24)

Setting
a=vt^{2}
and
b=\frac{v}{t^{2}}
gives

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4Ny^{2}}\sum_{\begin{bmatrix}m_{1}&n_{1}\\
m_{2}&n_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in L_{p,q}^{\ast}}\overline{(n_{1}\tau+n_{2})}\overline{(m_{2}\tau-m_{1})}e\left(-pn_{2}\right)

(4.25)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\pi a\frac{|n_{1}\tau+n_{2}|^{2}}{y}-\pi b\frac{|m_{2}\tau-m_{1}|^{2}}{y}}a^{1+\frac{s}{2}}b^{1-\frac{s}{2}}\frac{da}{a}\frac{db}{b}

(4.26)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{s}{2}\right)}{4N\pi^{2}}\sum_{\begin{bmatrix}m_{1}&n_{1}\\
m_{2}&n_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}\\
q+N\mathbb{Z}&\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}\end{bmatrix}}\frac{1}{(m_{2}\tau-m_{1})|m_{2}\tau-m_{1}|^{-\frac{s}{2}}}\frac{e\left(-pn_{2}\right)}{(n_{1}\tau+n_{2})|n_{1}\tau+n_{2}|^{\frac{s}{2}}}.

(4.27)

By the functional equation, we have
\widehat{E}^{(1)}_{r,0}(N\tau)=E^{(1)}_{r,0}(N\tau)
where

\displaystyle\widehat{E}^{(1)}_{r,0}(N\tau)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2i\pi}\ \lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
m\equiv p\mod{N}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m\tau+n)|m\tau+n|^{2s}},

(4.28)

\displaystyle E^{(1)}_{r,0}(N\tau)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2i\pi}\ \lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(mN\tau+n)|mN\tau+n|^{2s}}e\left(-\frac{np}{N}\right).

(4.29)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{0,\infty\}}{E}_{p,q}(z,\tau)=\widehat{E}^{(1)}_{q,0}(N\tau)E^{(1)}_{p,0}(N\tau)=\widehat{E}^{(1)}_{q,0}(N\tau)\widehat{E}^{(1)}_{p,0}(N\tau)=G^{(1)}_{q}(\tau)G^{(1)}_{p}(\tau).

(4.30)

Proposition 4.3

(Integral over unimodular symbols)
.

Let
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The image of the unimodular symbol
\pazocal{M}=\gamma\{0,\infty\}
is

\displaystyle\int_{\pazocal{M}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=-G_{d}^{(1)}(\tau)G_{c}^{(1)}(\tau).

(4.31)

Proof.

From the equivariance of
\varphi
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma\{0,\infty\}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=\int_{\{0,\infty\}}\gamma^{\ast}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=\int_{\{0,\infty\}}{E}_{d,-c}(z,\tau),

(4.32)

where

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}d\\
-c\end{pmatrix}.

(4.33)

The result follows from the previous computation.
∎

4.3. Image of the theta lift

We can now determine the image of the theta lift

\displaystyle{E}\colon H_{1}(Y_{1}(N);\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow M_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N)).

(4.34)

If
\gamma
is hyperbolic, let
[b_{0},\dots,b_{n}]
be the continued fraction expansion of
\frac{a}{c}=\gamma\infty
as in (
2.17
). Let
\frac{p_{0}}{q_{0}},\dots,\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}
be the convergents, and
m=p_{n-1}d-bq_{n-1}
. We set
(p_{-1},q_{-1})\coloneqq(1,0)
.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
\gamma
is a parabolic matrix in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
stabilizing the cusp
r=[m:n]
, then

\displaystyle{E}(\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma})=b(\gamma)H_{j,-n}^{(2)},

(4.35)

where
i,j
are integers such that
mj-in=1
. If
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
is a hyperbolic matrix in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
, then

\displaystyle{E}(\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma})=(b_{0}+bq_{n-1}-p_{n-1}d)H_{1,0}^{(2)}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}b_{k+1}H^{(2)}_{q_{k-1},q_{k}}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{q_{k}}^{(1)}G_{q_{k-1}}^{(1)}.

(4.36)

Proof.

Recall from Theorem
2.3
that we can write the cycle as

\displaystyle\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma}=\begin{cases}\ b(\gamma)\pazocal{C}_{r}&\ \ \textrm{if}\ \gamma\ \textrm{is parabolic},\\[10.00002pt]
\ (b_{0}+bq_{n-1}-p_{n-1}d)\pazocal{C}_{\infty}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}b_{k+1}\pazocal{C}_{\gamma_{k}\infty}\oplus\sum_{k=0}^{n}\gamma_{k}\{0,\infty\}&\ \ \textrm{if}\ \gamma\ \textrm{is hyperbolic}\end{cases}

(4.37)

under the isomorphism
H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\simeq\pazocal{C}(\mathbb{Z})\oplus\pazocal{M}\pazocal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
. The integral along the unimodular symbol
\gamma_{k}\{0,\infty\}
is

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{k}\{0,\infty\}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=-G_{q_{k}}^{(1)}(\tau)G_{q_{k-1}}^{(1)}(\tau)

(4.38)

by Proposition
4.3
, where
\gamma_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}-p_{k}&p_{k-1}\\
-q_{k}&q_{k-1}\end{pmatrix}
.
If
\gamma_{r}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is a matrix such that
[m:n]=r=\gamma_{r}\infty
, then
\gamma_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}m&i\\
n&j\end{pmatrix}
for some integers
i,j
such that
mj-in=1
. By Proposition
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\pazocal{C}_{r}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=H_{j,-n}^{(2)}(\tau).

(4.39)

In particular, the integral along the modular cap
\pazocal{C}_{\gamma_{k}\infty}
is

\displaystyle\int_{\pazocal{C}_{\gamma_{k}\infty}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=H^{(2)}_{q_{k-1},q_{k}}(\tau).

(4.40)

For
\gamma_{r}=\mathbf{1}
, the integral along
\pazocal{C}_{\infty}
is

\displaystyle\int_{\pazocal{C}_{\infty}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=H_{1,0}^{(2)}(\tau).

(4.41)

∎

Let
\pazocal{H}^{(2)}\subset E_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
be the subspace spanned by the forms
H^{(2)}_{p,q}(\tau)=G^{(2)}_{q}(\tau)
with
q\not\equiv 0\mod{N}
, and the forms
H^{(2)}_{p,0}
with
p\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Let
\pazocal{H}^{(1,1)}\subset M_{2}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
be the subspace spanned by all the products
G^{(1)}_{p}G^{(1)}_{q}
.

Corollary 4.4.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\pazocal{H}^{(2)}\oplus S_{2,\mathrm{rk}=0}^{\textrm{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(N))\subseteq\mathrm{Im}({E})\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{\pazocal{H}^{(2)},\pazocal{H}^{(1,1)}\}.

(4.42)

Proof.

First, note that the forms
H^{(2)}_{1,0}
and
H^{(2)}_{q_{k-1},q_{k}}
that appear in the previous theorem are all in
\pazocal{H}^{(2)}
,since
q_{k-1},q_{k}
are coprime integers This proves
\mathrm{Im}({E})\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{\pazocal{H}^{(2)},\pazocal{H}^{(1,1)}\}
.

Now suppose that
H^{(2)}_{p,q}\in\pazocal{H}^{(2)}
. If
q\equiv 0\mod N
, then
(p,q)
are coprime. By Proposition
4.2
we can then find a modular cap such that its image is
H^{(2)}_{p,q}
. If
q\not\equiv 0\mod N
, then
H^{(2)}_{p,q}
does not depend on
p
and
H^{(2)}_{p,q}=H^{(2)}_{1,q}
, which reduces it to the first case. Finally, the inclusion of the rank
0
forms was proved in Theorem
3.9
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

In view of
[
5
, Theorem. 4.11]
, it seems reasonable to expect that these inclusions are also equalities, when

is prime. The first equality is Theorem
3.9
, but it is not completely clear whether
\mathrm{Im}({E})=\mathrm{span}\{\pazocal{H}^{(2)},\pazocal{H}^{(1,1)}\}
holds. While a single product

is the image of a unimodular symbol, one would need to write it as the image of a cycle, so a linear combination of modular caps and modular symbols of degree

.

4.4. Diagonal restrictions of Eisenstein series

Recall that a matrix is hyperbolic if
|\mathrm{tr}(\gamma)|>2
.

Proposition 4.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}({E})=\mathrm{span}\{{E}(\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma})\ |\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{1}(N)\ \textrm{hyperbolic}\}.

(4.43)

Proof.

It follows from the fact that any parabolic matrix can be written as a product of hyperbolic matrices. More precisely, if
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&t-a\end{pmatrix}
is a hyperbolic matrix with
|t|=|\mathrm{tr}(\gamma)|>2
, then we write

\displaystyle\gamma=\gamma\gamma_{1}\gamma_{1}^{-1},\qquad\gamma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1+m^{2}&m\\
m&1\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{1}(N)

(4.44)

where
m
is any integer divisible by
N
. We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\gamma\gamma_{1})=t+(b+c)m+am^{2}.

(4.45)

For
m
large enough, both
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma\gamma_{1}
are hyperbolic, and

\displaystyle\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma}=\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma\gamma_{1}}-\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma_{1}}

(4.46)

in homology. Thus,
{E}(\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma})={E}(\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma\gamma_{1}})-{E}(\pazocal{Z}_{\gamma_{1}})
is a linear combination of images of hyperbolic matrices.
∎

Let
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
be a primitive hyperbolic matrix in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
, and
F=\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)
the field generated by
\gamma
. We have

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\delta_{0}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0\\
0&\epsilon^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\delta_{0},

(4.47)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{0}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&\nu\\
1&\nu^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})^{+},\quad\textrm{with}\ \ \nu=\frac{\epsilon-d}{c},\quad\textrm{and}\ \ \epsilon=\frac{a+d+\sqrt{D}}{2}.

(4.48)

The matrix
\delta_{0}
induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}(\epsilon)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}),\quad\delta_{0}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\mu&0\\
0&\mu^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\delta_{0}\longmapsto\mu,

(4.49)

with
D=\mathrm{tr}(\gamma)^{2}-4
. Note that
D
is never a square. The linear map
\delta_{0}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
F
-equivariant, where it acts on the left as a matrix in
F=\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, and on the right
F\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\epsilon)
acts by
\nu\cdot(x,y)=(\nu x,\nu^{\prime}y)
. The lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is sent to
\delta_{0}\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\sigma(\mathfrak{f})=\left\{(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ |\ \mu\in\mathfrak{f}\right\}
, where
\mathfrak{f}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}+\nu\mathbb{Z}
. Let
{O}\subseteq F
be the multiplier ring that preserves
\mathfrak{f}
, which is an order in
{O}_{F}
. It corresponds to the stabilizer in
\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)
of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, so that
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\cap\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)\simeq{O}^{\times,+}
. On the other hand,
\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)\cap\Gamma_{1}(N)
is the stabilizer of the coset
\begin{pmatrix}1+N\mathbb{Z}\\
N\mathbb{Z}\end{pmatrix}
. It corresponds to the stabilizer
U\coloneqq{O}^{\times,+}\cap(1+N\mathfrak{f})\subset{O}^{\times,+}
of the coset
1+N\mathfrak{f}
.
By Dirichlet’s unit theorem, the units
{O}^{\times,+}=\epsilon_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}
are generated by a fundamental unit
\epsilon_{0}
, which is an eigenvalue of the primitive hyperbolic matrix

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=\delta_{0}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{0}&0\\
0&\epsilon^{\prime}_{0}\end{pmatrix}\delta_{0}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

(4.50)

Since
U
is a subgroup of
{O}^{\times,+}\simeq\gamma_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, it is generated by some power
\gamma_{1}\coloneqq\gamma_{0}^{m}\in\Gamma_{1}(N)
, for some
m\geq 1
, and with eigenvalue
\epsilon_{1}
. Since
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}(\gamma)\simeq\Gamma_{1}(N)\simeq U=\gamma_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have that
\gamma=\gamma_{1}^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
. If
\gamma
is primitive in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
(in the sense that it cannot be written as a power of another hyperbolic matrix in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
), then
k=1
and
\gamma=\gamma_{1}
.

The computation of the integral is similar to the computation in
[
8
]
. Since the integral along
\{z_{0},\gamma z_{0}\}
does not depend on the basepoint, we pick
z_{0}=g^{-1}_{0}i
. Moreover, the segment
\{i,\epsilon^{2}i\}
is contained in
\{0,\infty\}
, only the
dv
-component contributes. We have

\displaystyle\int_{\{z_{0},\gamma z_{0}\}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=\int_{1}^{\epsilon^{2}}(\delta_{0}^{-1})^{\ast}{E}_{1,0}(iv,\tau)_{(1,1)}\frac{dv}{v}.

(4.51)

The form
\varphi
satisfies the equivariance (
3.23
)

\displaystyle g^{\ast}\varphi(z,t,\mathbf{v})=\varphi(z,t,\rho_{g^{-1}}\mathbf{v}),

4.5. Linear relations between Eisenstein series

In this last section, we show how to recover linear relations between the Eisenstein series.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}
be three integers coprime to
N
that satisfy
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}\equiv 0\mod N
. Then

\displaystyle G_{n_{1}}^{(1)}G_{n_{2}}^{(1)}+G_{n_{2}}^{(1)}G_{n_{3}}^{(1)}+G_{n_{3}}^{(1)}G_{n_{1}}^{(1)}=G_{n_{1}}^{(2)}+G_{n_{2}}^{(2)}+G_{n_{3}}^{(2)}.

(4.65)

Proof.

The Eisenstein series only depend on the residue of
n_{i}\mod{N}
, so we can replace
n_{i}
by
n_{i}-k_{i}N
. First, we can pick
k_{2}
such that
n_{2}-k_{2}N
and
n_{1}
are coprime integers: since
n_{1}
and
N
are coprime, there exists an integer
k_{2}
such that
k_{2}\equiv(n_{2}-1)N^{-1}\mod{n_{1}}
, and thus
n_{2}-1\equiv k_{2}N\mod n_{1}
. It follows that
\gcd(n_{1},n_{2}-k_{2}N)=1
. Moreover, by assumption, we have
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=k_{3}N
for some
k_{3}
. By replacing
n_{3}
with
n_{3}^{\prime}=n_{3}-k_{3}N
, we can assume that the
n_{i}^{\prime}s
are such that

\displaystyle n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=0

(4.66)

and
n_{1},n_{2}
coprime.

Since
n_{1},n_{2}
are coprime, we can find
m_{1},m_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{12}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}m_{2}&m_{1}\\
-n_{2}&-n_{1}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

(4.67)

Setting
m_{3}\coloneqq-m_{1}-m_{2}
, we find that the matrices

\displaystyle\gamma_{23}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}m_{3}&m_{2}\\
-n_{3}&-n_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\gamma_{31}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}&m_{3}\\
-n_{1}&-n_{3}\end{pmatrix},

(4.68)

are also in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\pazocal{M}_{ij}\coloneqq\gamma_{ij}\{0,\infty\}
be the corresponding unimodular symbols then

\displaystyle\pazocal{M}_{12}=\left\{r_{1},r_{2}\right\},\quad\pazocal{M}_{23}=\left\{r_{2},r_{3}\right\},\quad\pazocal{M}_{31}=\left\{r_{3},r_{1}\right\}.

(4.69)

with
r_{i}=-\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}
. Let
\pazocal{T}
be the triangle having the oriented modular symbols as sides, and closed by adding the modular caps

\displaystyle\pazocal{C}_{1}=[r_{3},r_{2}]_{r_{1}},\quad\pazocal{C}_{2}=[r_{1},r_{3}]_{r_{2}},\quad\pazocal{C}_{3}=[r_{2},r_{1}]_{r_{3}};

(4.70)

see Figure
5

\displaystyle\pazocal{M}_{12}+\pazocal{M}_{23}+\pazocal{M}_{31}+\pazocal{C}_{1}+\pazocal{C}_{2}+\pazocal{C}_{3}=\partial\pazocal{T}=0\in H_{1}(\overline{Y_{1}(N)}^{\textrm{BS}};\mathbb{Z}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\pazocal{M}_{12}+\pazocal{M}_{23}+\pazocal{M}_{31}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau)=-\int_{\pazocal{C}_{1}+\pazocal{C}_{2}+\pazocal{C}_{3}}{E}_{1,0}(z,\tau).
