Methodological concerns regarding the prediction model for ADL decline in older adults with diabetes
Authors: Xueting Wang, Ying Zhao
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

We read with great interest the study by Inoguchi et al., 1 demonstrating that higher HNA% and lower serum bilirubin independently predict ADL decline in older adults with type 2 diabetes, offering promising redox‐based biomarkers for risk stratification. However, we have several methodological concerns that potentially threaten the validity of the conclusions.

First, the study did not account for competing risks, particularly death. In elderly cohorts with diabetes, mortality is a substantial competing event that precludes the occurrence of ADL disability. Standard Cox regression may overestimate the cumulative incidence of the outcome when the competing risk is present. 2 The authors reported 28 disability events among 101 participants, but did not specify how many deaths occurred during follow‐up or whether death was treated as a censoring event. If participants who died were censored, the hazard ratios for HNA% and bilirubin would be biased upward. We suggest reanalyzing the data using a Fine‐Gray subdistribution hazard model to obtain unbiased estimates of the risk of ADL decline in the presence of death as a competing risk.

Second, the internal validation procedure may overestimate model performance due to data‐driven model selection. The authors built multiple candidate models and chose Model 4 based on the lowest AIC and highest C‐index. This selective reporting, though common, can inflate apparent predictive performance because the model was optimized on the same dataset used for evaluation. Although bootstrap resampling was applied, it was performed after model selection; thus, optimism‐correction was not fully accounted for. 3 A more rigorous approach would involve prespecifying the primary model in a statistical analysis plan or using cross‐validation to guide model selection before internal validation.

In summary, while the findings are novel and clinically relevant, addressing the competing risk of death and using a prespecified modeling strategy are essential to confirm the robustness and clinical utility of HNA% and bilirubin for predicting functional decline in older adults with diabetes.
