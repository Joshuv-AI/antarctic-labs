Abstract

1.
Introduction and main results

In recent years, nonlocal operators have attracted considerable attention in analysis and partial differential equations. Among these, the fractional Laplacian has emerged as a central object. Let
s\in(0,1)
be fixed, then the fractional Laplacian can be defined formally through its integral representation

(1.1)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)

\displaystyle=c_{N,s}\,\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy=c_{N,s}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy,

where
c_{N,s}
is a normalization constant given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle c_{N,s}=\frac{4^{s}\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}+s\right)}{\Gamma(2-s)\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}s(1-s).

The expression (
1.1
) is in particular well-defined for
u\in C^{2}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}_{s}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, where
L^{1}_{s}
is a weighted
L^{1}
-space defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle L^{1}_{s}(\mathds{R}^{N}):=\Bigl\{u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathds{R}^{N})\,\,:\,\,\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|}{\left(1+|x|\right)^{N+2s}}\,dx<\infty\Bigr\}

endowed with the norm

(1.4)

\displaystyle u\mapsto\|u\|_{L^{1}_{s}(\mathds{R}^{N})}:=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|}{\left(1+|x|\right)^{N+2s}}\,dx.

We emphasize that (
1.3
), (
1.4
) can be defined for any
s\in\mathds{R}
and we shall also use this space, in particular, for
s=0
.

In the present paper, we are concerned with functions
u\in L^{1}_{s}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, which are
s
-harmonic in some bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
with
C^{2}
boundary, i.e., which satisfy

(1.5)

(-\Delta)^{s}u=0\qquad\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega.

Here, equation (
1.5
) is to be understood in distributional sense, i.e.,

(1.6)

\int_{\Omega}u(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\,dx=0\qquad\text{for all $\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)$.}

It is well known that (
1.6
) implies
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and, in particular, that (
1.5
) holds pointwisely in
\Omega
(see e.g.
[
25
, Chapter I.6]
,
[
3
]
or
[
19
, Theorem 12.17]
and the references in there). While most of the literature treats
s
as a fixed parameter in
(0,1)
, it is natural to ask how the property of being
s
-harmonic depends on the parameter
s
. In particular, it is an interesting question of what happens to (families of)
s
-harmonic function
u_{s}
in the limiting regimes
s\to 1^{-}
and
s\to 0^{+}
. Here, the first limit corresponds to the case where the fractional Laplacian converges to the classical Laplace operator, and the
small-order
limit,
s\to 0^{+}
, corresponds to the case where
(-\Delta)^{s}
tends to the identity.

In this paper, we will be concerned with the second limit. We start our discussion with a first observation regarding the flatness of uniformly bounded
s
-harmonic functions
u_{s}
in the limit
s\to 0^{+}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain. Also, let
u_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})
,
s\in(0,s_{0}]
be
s
-harmonic in
\Omega
and such that

(1.7)

\|u_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}\leq C\qquad\text{for all $s\in(0,s_{0}]$ with a constant $C>0$.}

Then we have that

\inf_{c\in\mathds{R}}|u_{s}-c|\to 0\qquad\text{as $s\to 0^{+}$ locally uniformly in $\Omega$.}

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on an explicit local Poisson representation formula for
s
-harmonic functions in balls. More precisely, if
u\in L^{1}_{s}(\mathds{R}^{N})
is
s
-harmonic in
B_{r}(x)
for some
x\in\mathds{R}^{N}
,
r>0
and bounded in a neighborhood of
\overline{B_{r}(x)}
, then we have

(1.8)

u(x)=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(x)}P_{s,r}(x,y)u(y)\,dy\qquad\text{for $x\in B_{r}(x)$}

with the associated Poisson kernel

\displaystyle P_{s,r}(x,y)=\frac{\gamma_{N,s}}{|x-y|^{N}}\left(\frac{r^{2}-|x|^{2}}{|y|^{2}-r^{2}}\right)^{s},\qquad\text{where}\quad\gamma_{N,s}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)}.

Using this representation, the convergence for
s\to 1^{-}
of
s
-harmonic functions in
\Omega
to
classical harmonic
functions has been discussed in
[
20
, Theorem 5.80]
.
If we assume in addition that
u
is nonnegative, we also have the following
s
-independent version of Harnack’s inequality:

(1.9)

2^{-4N-2}\leq\frac{u(x_{1})}{u(x_{2})}\leq 2^{4N+2}\qquad\text{for all $x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{r/4}(x)$.}

This inequality is well-known to experts and follows easily from (
1.8
). For the convenience of the reader, we include the short proof in the appendix.

While Theorem
1.1
implies that (uniformly bounded)
s
-harmonic functions become flatter and flatter in
\Omega
as
s\to 0^{+}
, it clearly does not imply that
u_{s}
converges locally to a fixed constant in
\Omega
. To analyze the shape of
u_{s}
for
s
near zero in greater detail, we now restrict our attention to
s
-harmonic functions
u_{s}
satisfying
u_{s}\equiv g
in
\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
for a
fixed
(s-independent) function
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
. Hence we consider the family of nonhomogeneous Dirichlet problems

(1.10)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u=0\,\,\,&\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega\\
u=g\,\,\,&\text{in}\,\,\,\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain with
C^{2}
boundary,
s\in(0,1)
, and
N\geq 2
. For each fixed
s
, this problem has a unique solution
u_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, which can again be represented in terms of the associated Possion kernel as

(1.11)

u_{s}=\mathbb{P}_{s}g:=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}P_{s}(\cdot,y)g(y)\,dy\,\chi_{\Omega}+g\,\chi_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega},

see e. g.
[
6
]
. In the following, we analyze the solution map

s\mapsto u_{s}

and study the behavior of
u_{s}
as
s\to 0
with respect to the exterior data
g
. In particular, our study is motivated by the following questions:

Question 1.

Under what conditions on the data
g
does the family
(u_{s})_{s}
converge in
\Omega
as
s\to 0^{+}
?

Question 2.

Is the solution map
s\mapsto u_{s}
differentiable at
s=0
under appropriate assumptions on the exterior data
g
? If so, can it be expressed in terms of the logarithmic Laplacian?

Question 3.

How does the dependence on
s
behave for general
s\in(0,1)
, and are there some monotonicity properties with respect to
s
?

Our main results answer these questions. For Question 1, we establish conditions on the exterior data
g
ensuring almost uniform convergence of
u_{s}
as
s\to 0^{+}
. We will see that, in particular, if the spherical averages of
g
converge to a constant at infinity, then the solutions
u_{s}
converge to this constant inside
\Omega
as
s\to 0^{+}
. Moreover, we shall identify a first order term in the expansion of
u_{s}
as
s\to 0^{+}
. Before we state the main theorem concerning Question 1 and Question 2, we recall the following definition from
[
23
]
.

Definition 1.2

(Almost uniform convergence)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
be an open bounded set, and let
u_{n}:\Omega\to\mathds{R}
be a sequence of functions. We say that
(u_{n})_{n}
converges
almost uniformly in
\Omega
to a function
u
if

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\delta^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}(u_{n}-u)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0\,\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,\varepsilon>0,

where, here and in the following, we put

\delta_{\Omega}(x)=\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathds{R}^{N}$,}

so
\delta_{\Omega}:\mathds{R}^{N}\to\mathds{R}
is the boundary distance function.

We introduce some standard notation. Let
B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
resp.
S_{r}:=S_{r}(0)
denote the ball and sphere of radius
r>0
centered at zero and let
\omega_{N-1}
denote the
(N-1)
-dimensional volume of the unit sphere
S_{N-1}
. Moreover, for a function
g\in L^{1}(S_{r})
, we let

\fint_{S_{r}}g(y)\,d\sigma(y):=\frac{1}{r^{N-1}\omega_{N-1}}\int_{S_{r}}g(y)\,d\sigma(y)

denote the average integral of
g
over
S_{r}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N\geq 2
, let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
be an open bounded set with
C^{2}
-boundary, and let
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega).

Moreover, let

(1.12)

\tilde{g}(r):=\fint_{S_{r}}g(y)\,d\sigma(y),

which is well-defined for a. e.
r>0
with
S_{r}\subset\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
.

(i)

If
c_{g}:=\lim\limits_{r\to+\infty}\tilde{g}(r)
exists, then
u_{s}\to c_{g}
almost uniformly in
\Omega
as
s\to 0^{+}.

(ii)

If
c_{g}:=\lim\limits_{r\to+\infty}\tilde{g}(r)
exists and

(1.13)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{|\tilde{g}(r)-c_{g}|}{r}dr<\infty,

then we have

(1.14)

u_{s}=c_{g}+s\Bigl(L_{g}+o(1)\Bigr)\qquad\text{in $\Omega$,}

where

(1.15)

L_{g}(x)=c_{N}P.V.\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{g(y)-c_{g}}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy\qquad\text{for $x\in\Omega$}

with
c_{N}=\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}=\frac{2}{\omega_{N-1}}
and
o(1)\to 0
almost uniformly in
\Omega
as
s\to 0^{+}
.

Remark 1.4
.

(i) We clarify that the principle value integral in (
1.15
) is concerned with the behavior at infinity, i.e.,

(1.16)

L_{g}(x)=c_{N}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{B_{r}\setminus\Omega}\frac{g(y)-c_{g}}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega$.}

Under the more restrictive assumption that
g-c_{g}
extends to a function in
L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, this integral also exists in Lebesgue sense. In contrast, the weaker assumption (
1.13
) allows large oscillations of
g
in the spherical variable. For a proof of the fact that
the limit in (
1.16
) exists in the general case of functions
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
satisfying (
1.13
), see Lemma
5.1
below.

(ii) The function
L_{g}
can be seen as the restriction of
-L_{\Delta}(g1_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}-c_{g})
to
\Omega
, where
L_{\Delta}
denotes the logarithmic Laplacian. The operator
L_{\Delta}
has been introduced in
[
8
]
, and it appears in various contexts where the zero order limit of fractional problems is considered, see for instance
[
18
,
8
,
12
,
2
,
21
,
15
,
17
,
7
,
22
,
9
,
10
]
. For the definition and properties of logarithmic Laplacian, see Section
2
below.

(iii) The first order expansion (
1.14
) shows that pointwisely we have

L_{g}=\frac{d}{ds}\Bigl|_{s=0}u_{s}\qquad\text{in $\Omega$.}

In the case where
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
is nonnegative and
c_{g}=0
, we deduce from (
1.9
) and
Theorem

1.3
(ii) that
for every ball
B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega
we have

2^{-4N-2}\leq\frac{L_{\Delta}(1_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}g)(x_{1})}{L_{\Delta}(1_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}g)(x_{2})}\leq 2^{4N+2}\qquad\text{for all $x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{r/4}(x)$.}

By approximation, this also holds for nonnegative functions
g\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\cap L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.3
, we derive the following limiting Harnack inequality.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
be an open bounded set with
C^{2}
-boundary, let
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
satisfy
c_{g}=\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\tilde{g}(r)=0
with
\tilde{g}
defined in (
1.12
), and let
u_{s}
denote the solutions of (
1.10
) for

. Then we have

(1.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{u_{s}(x)}{s}\leq\Bigl(\frac{\diam(\Omega)}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}\Bigr)^{N}\Bigg(\,\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{u_{s}(y)}{s}+c_{N}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{g^{-}(z)}{|y-z|^{N}}\,dz\Bigg)\quad\text{for $x,y\in\Omega$.}

We emphasize that if
g\geq 0,
then (
1.17
) reduces to

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{u_{s}(x)}{s}\leq\Bigl(\frac{\diam(\Omega)}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}\Bigr)^{N}\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{u_{s}(y)}{s}\quad\text{for all $x,y\in\Omega$.}

Next, in view of Question 1 we also show that, if
g
is as in
Theorem

1.3
but
\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\tilde{g}(r)
does not exist, then
u_{s}
may not converge as
s\to 0^{+}
. The precise statement of our result is the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Omega:=B_{1}(0)\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
be the unit ball. Then there exists
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
with the property that the unique solutions
u_{s}
of (
1.10
) satisfy

(1.18)

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}u_{s}(x)=1,\quad\liminf_{s\to 0^{+}}u_{s}(x)=0\qquad\qquad\text{for some $x\in\Omega$.}

With the result above, we close the discussion of Question 1 and Question 2. In our next main result, we give an answer to Question 3 by showing that, under rather weak assumptions on
g
, the solution map
(0,1)\to L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})
,
s\mapsto u_{s}
for the family of problems (
1.10
) is of class
C^{1}
. Moreover, we derive an explicit formula for its derivative involving the logarithmic Laplacian. The result can be seen as an analogue to
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
, which is concerned with the corresponding family of
s
-dependent Poisson problems with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions.

To state the result, we first recall the definition of the
complementary Poisson kernel
of order
s\in(0,1)
from
[
24
]
, which is given by

(1.19)

P_{s}^{c}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathds{R},\qquad P_{s}^{c}(x,z)=c_{N}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{P_{s}(z,y)}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy.

We also need the fractional-logarithmic operator, very recently introduced by Chen, Chen, and Hauer in
[
11
]
, given by

(1.20)

[(-\Delta)^{s+\Log}u](x)=c_{N,s}\,\mathcal{\PV}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\bigl(-2\ln|x-y|\bigr)\,dy+b_{N,s}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)

with
b_{n,s}=\frac{d}{ds}c_{N,s}
. Note that, for
x\in\mathds{R}^{N}
,
[(-\Delta)^{s+\Log}u](x)
is well-defined for functions
u\in L^{1}_{s-{\varepsilon}}(\mathds{R}^{N})
which are of class
C^{2s+{\varepsilon}}
in a neighborhood of

for some
{\varepsilon}>0
. For functions
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, Chen, Chen, and Hauer in
[
11
]
proved the pointwise identity

(1.21)

(-\Delta)^{s+\Log}u=L_{\Delta}(-\Delta)^{s}u=(-\Delta)^{s}L_{\Delta}u=\frac{d}{ds}[(-\Delta)^{s}u].

In fact, this identity also holds if
u\in C^{\alpha}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N})
for some
\alpha>2s
. For the reader’s convenience, we supply an elementary proof of this in Appendix
D
. Finally, we fix

(1.22)

U\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
as an open neighbourhood of
\Omega
, so that
\overline{\Omega}\subset U
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
be an open bounded domain with
C^{2}
-boundary. Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap C^{\alpha}(U)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
where
U
is given by (
1.22
), and let
u_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})
be the unique solution of (
1.10
) for

(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})\to L^{\infty}(\Omega),\qquad s\mapsto u_{s}

is of class

, and its derivative
\partial_{s}u_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to

is given by the unique solution of the fractional Dirichlet problem

(1.23)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}(\partial_{s}u_{s})&={{\mathbb{P}}_{s}}^{\!\!c}\Bigl((-\Delta)^{s}g\big|_{\Omega}\Bigr)+L_{\Delta}\bigl(1_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{s}g\bigr)-(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{Log}}g&&\qquad\text{in $\Omega$,}\\
\partial_{s}u_{s}&=0&&\qquad\text{in $\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega$.}\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, if
(-\Delta)^{s}g\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
for some
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
, then the right hand side of (
1.23
) can be written as

(1.24)

{{\mathbb{P}}_{s}}^{\!\!c}\Bigl((-\Delta)^{s}g\big|_{\Omega}\Bigr)-L_{\Delta}\bigl(1_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}(-\Delta)^{s}g\bigr)\qquad\text{in $\Omega$.}

Remark 1.8
.

(i) The assumption
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap C^{\alpha}(U)
implies that
(-\Delta)^{s}
is defined as a distribution on
\mathds{R}^{N}
and in pointwise sense as a
C^{\alpha-2s}
-function on
\Omega
for
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
. These definitions are consistent, see Lemma
7.3
below. In particular,
L_{\Delta}\bigl(1_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{s}g\bigr)
is well-defined as a function in
\Omega
. Moreover, the extra assumption
(-\Delta)^{s}g\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
guarantees that also
L_{\Delta}\bigl(1_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}(-\Delta)^{s}g\bigr)
is well-defined as a function in
\Omega
.

(ii) It is remarkable that, while the family of solutions
u_{s}
of (
1.10
) and its derivative
\partial_{s}u_{s}
only depend on the values of
g
in
\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, it seems more convenient to express the derivative in terms of an extension of

\mathds{R}^{N}

) and (

).

As a corollary of Theorem
1.7
and the positivity of the complementary Poisson kernel
P_{s}^{\,c}
we can derive a sufficient criterion for the pointwise monotonicity of the solution family
u_{s}
in
s
.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
N\geq 2
and let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
be an open bounded domain with
C^{2}
-boundary. Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap C^{\alpha}(U)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1],
where
U
is given by (
1.22
), and let
u_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})
be the unique solution of (
1.10
) for

. Moreover, suppose that

(-\Delta)^{s}g\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})\qquad\text{for all $s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})$.}

If
(-\Delta)^{s}g\geq 0\,\,(\leq 0)
in
\mathds{R}^{N}
for

, then the map

(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})\to\mathds{R},\qquad s\mapsto u_{s}(x)

.

Remark 1.10
.

At first glance, the assumption on the sign of
(-\Delta)^{s}g
may appear nontrivial, but there is a large class of functions

for which

has a fixed sign for all
s\in(0,1)
. Indeed, let

, let

be nonnegative and consider the Newtonian potential

of

g=F_{1}\ast f\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap C^{2+\beta}(\mathds{R}^{N})
. Here, for

, we let

F_{s}:\mathds{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty],\quad F_{s}(z)=\kappa_{N,s}|z|^{2s-N}.

denote the Riesz kernel, cf.

. By the semigroup property of convolution with Riesz kernels, we then have

g=F_{s}\ast f_{s}\qquad\text{for all $s\in(0,1)$ with $f_{s}:=F_{1-s}\ast f$}

and thus
(-\Delta)^{s}g=f_{s}\geq 0
in
\mathds{R}^{N}
for all

. Hence, for this class of functions

, Corollary
1.9
applies and yields that

(0,1)\to\mathds{R},\qquad s\mapsto u_{s}(x)

is increasing for every
x\in\Omega
. We emphazise that this conclusion does not depend on the shape of
\Omega
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some basic material that is needed for our purposes. We begin with the logarithmic Laplacian and the associated function spaces. Then, we recall some aspects of fractional operators, including fundamental solutions, Green and Poisson operators, and representation formulas.

2.1.
The logarithmic Laplacian and function spaces

The logarithmic Laplacian is a weakly singular Fourier integral operator associated with the symbol
2\log|\cdot|.
As mentioned earlier, the operator
L_{\Delta}
appears in the study of
(-\Delta)^{s}u
in the limit
s\to 0
. Given a function
u\in C^{\alpha}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N})
for some
\alpha>0,

L_{\Delta}u(x)
can be uniquely defined by the asymptotic expansion.

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)+sL_{\Delta}u(x)+o(s),

where
o(s)
denotes the small
o
that satisfies
\frac{o(s)}{s}\to 0
as
s\to 0.
So,
L_{\Delta}u
appears as the linear correction term in the small-order regime of the fractional Laplacian. The operator admits an explicit (weakly singular) integral representation
L_{\Delta}u(x)
, which, for
u\in C^{\alpha}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N})
,
\alpha>0
, and
x\in\mathds{R}^{N}
, is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle L_{\Delta}u(x)=c_{N}\text{P.V.}\int_{B_{1}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy-c_{N}\int_{B^{c}_{1}(x)}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy+\rho_{N}u(x).

Here,
B_{r}(x)
is the ball centered at
x
with radius
r>0
and

\displaystyle c_{N}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}=\frac{2}{\omega_{N-1}},\quad\rho_{N}=2\log 2+\psi(\frac{N}{2})-\gamma,

where as in the introduction
\omega_{N-1}
denotes the
(N-1)
-dimensional volume of the unit sphere in
\mathds{R}^{N}
,
\gamma=-\Gamma^{\prime}(1)
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, and
\psi=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}
is the Digamma function.

To extend the operator beyond smooth, compactly supported functions, one works with a weighted space
L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
as in (
1.3
) with
s=0
and the corresponding norm. Then, for a function
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, the logarithmic Laplacian of
u
,
L_{\Delta}u
, is well-defined in the distributional sense as

\displaystyle(L_{\Delta}u)\phi=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}u(x)L_{\Delta}\phi(x)\,dx\quad\text{for}\quad\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N}).

In the following, we use also the corresponding functional analytic framework for the logarithmic Laplacian. For this we define the space

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}(\mathds{R}^{N})=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{N})\,:\,\iint_{\{x,y\in\mathds{R}^{N}\,:\,|x-y|\leq 1\}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dx\,dy<\infty\right\}

and, for
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
open and bounded, we consider

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{0}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in\mathbb{H}(\mathds{R}^{N})\,:\,u\equiv 0\quad\text{on}\quad\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\right\}.

The space
\mathbb{H}_{0}(\Omega)
corresponds to the Dirichlet problem for
L_{\Delta}
in
\Omega
and is a Hilbert space with the inner product

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{0}(\Omega)}=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\iint_{x,y\in\mathds{R}^{N},|x-y|\leq 1}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dx\,dy.

We denote the induced norm by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}(\Omega)}
. We recall, that
\mathbb{H}_{0}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded into
L^{2}(\Omega)
, where the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
is identified as the space of functions in
L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{N})
with
u\equiv 0
on
\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, see
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Furthermore, the bilinear form naturally associated with
L_{\Delta}
is well-defined on
\mathbb{H}_{0}(\Omega)
and given by

\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{H}_{0}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{H}_{0}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\langle u,v\rangle_{L}=\langle u,v\rangle-c_{N}\iint_{x,y\in\mathds{R}^{N},|x-y|\geq 1}\frac{u(x)v(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dx\,dy+\rho_{N}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}u(x)v(x)\,dx.

We note that the logarithmic Laplacian
L_{\Delta}u(x)
is also well-defined by (
2.1
) if
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
is only Dini continuous at
x
, see
[
8
, Proposition 2.2]
. In this case, we also have the domain dependent representation

(2.2)

L_{\Delta}u(x)=c_{N}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy-c_{N}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy+\left(h_{\Omega}(x)+\rho_{N}\right)u(x)

for
x\in\Omega
, where the function
h_{\Omega}
is given by

(2.3)

h_{\Omega}:\Omega\to\mathds{R},\qquad h_{\Omega}(x)=c_{N}\left(\int_{B_{1}(x)\setminus\Omega}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}-\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{1}(x)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N}}\right).

2.2.
Solution maps and related operators

To prepare for the analysis of boundary value problems, we now recall some basic tools from the theory of
(-\Delta)^{s}
similar to
[
23
]
. Recall that, for
s\in(0,1)
, the fundamental solution of
(-\Delta)^{s}
is given by
F_{s}:\mathds{R}^{N}\to\mathds{R}
, where
1
1

1

Recall, that
N\geq 2
throughout our work

F_{s}(z)=\kappa_{N,s}|z|^{2s-N},\quad\kappa_{N,s}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}-s)}{4^{s}\pi^{N/2}\Gamma(s)}=\frac{s\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}-s)}{4^{s}\pi^{N/2}\Gamma(1+s)}.

We note that

(2.4)

\frac{\kappa_{N,s}}{s}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}-s)}{4^{s}\pi^{N/2}\Gamma(1+s)}\to c_{N}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\pi^{N/2}}\qquad\text{as $s\to 0^{+}$.}

The convolution with the fundamental solution is usually called the

f\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{s}f:=F_{s}*f.

By the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (see
[

,
26

{\mathbb{F}}_{s}:L^{r}(\mathds{R}^{N})\to L^{p(r,s)}(\mathds{R}^{N})
for

(2.5)

r\in(1,\frac{N}{2s})\qquad\text{and}\qquad p(r,s):=\frac{rN}{N-2sr}.

Given some exterior data
g
, the following holds for
s
-harmonic functions
u
in
\Omega
, which equal

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

, which is bounded in a relative neighborhood of

. Then there exists a unique function

which is
s
-harmonic in
\Omega

, and satisfies

\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

For

P_{s}:\Omega\times(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\to\mathds{R}
is defined as

(2.6)

\displaystyle P_{s}(x,z):=c_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{G_{s}(x,y)}{|y-z|^{N+2s}}\,dy\qquad\text{for $x\in\Omega$, $z\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}$.}

Here,
G_{s}:\mathds{R}^{2N}_{*}\to\mathds{R}^{N}
denotes the Green kernel associated to
(-\Delta)^{s}
in
\Omega
, with

\mathds{R}^{2N}_{*}:=\mathds{R}^{N}\times\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\{(x,x)\>:\>x\in\mathds{R}^{N}\}.

The Green kernel has the property that, for every
g\in H^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
, the function
x\mapsto\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}P_{s}(x,z)g(z)dz

is the unique
s
-harmonic extension of
g
on
\Omega
, which is bounded in a neighborhood of

, see e.g.
[

]
.

Next, we provide some preliminary convergence results, as
s\to 0^{+}
, related to homogeneous and nonhomogeneous fractional Dirichlet problems involving the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
. We start by fixing some notation.

For

we define the Green operator

(2.7)

{\mathbb{G}}_{s}\>:\>L^{r}(\Omega)\to L^{p(r,s)}(\mathds{R}^{N}),\quad[{\mathbb{G}}_{s}f](x)=\int_{\Omega}G_{s}(x,y)f(y)\,dy\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathds{R}^{N}$,}

where

is as in (
2.5
). We emphasize that
{\mathbb{G}}_{s}
can also be defined on
L^{r}(\Omega)
for any

Analogously, for

, we can define associated to the fractional Poisson kernel

in (

) the Poisson operator, cf. (

),

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{P}}_{s}}\>

\displaystyle:\>L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\cap C^{\alpha}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\to C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle\qquad[{{\mathbb{P}}_{s}}h](x)=\Bigl(\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}P_{s}(x,y)h(y)\,dy\Bigr)1_{\Omega}(x)+h(x)1_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}(x)\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathds{R}^{N}$.}

Finally, to relate the Poisson kernel, resp. the Poisson operator, with the fundamental solution, resp. the Riesz operator, we define a function
H_{s}:\mathds{R}^{2N}_{*}\to\mathds{R}
, associated with

, as follows: For fixed

H_{s}(x,\cdot):\mathds{R}^{N}\to\mathds{R}

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}_{y}H_{s}(x,\cdot)&=0&&\text{in $\Omega$,}\\
H_{s}(x,\cdot)&=F_{s}(x-\,\cdot)&&\text{on $\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega$.}\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, if

on

By the maximum principle, we then have

0\leq H_{s}(x,y)\leq F(x-y)\qquad\text{for $x,y\in\mathds{R}^{2N}_{*}$.}

Consequently, for

, we can define an operator

{\mathbb{H}}_{s}:L^{r}(\Omega)\to L^{p(r,s)}(\mathds{R}^{N}),\qquad[{\mathbb{H}}_{s}f](x)=\int_{\Omega}H_{s}(x,y)f(y)\,dy\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathds{R}^{N}$.}

Recall that the general Poisson kernel representation formula for
s
-harmonic functions in

Under the assumptions of Lemma

Hence, as an immediate consequence of Lemma

&H_{s}(x,\cdot)&&\text{for $x\in\Omega$.}\end{aligned}\right.

Using the fact that

2.3.
A further preliminary estimate

In the following, we note the simple estimate

|a^{-\alpha}-b^{-\alpha}|\leq\alpha|a-b|(a^{-\alpha-1}+b^{-\alpha-1})\qquad\text{for $a,b,\alpha>0$,}

which implies that

(2.9)

\Bigl||y|^{-\alpha}-|z|^{-\alpha}\Bigr|\leq\alpha|y-z|\bigl(|y|^{-\alpha-1}-|z|^{-\alpha-1}\bigr)\qquad\text{for $y,z\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}$.}

As a consequence, for
0<r<R<\infty
and

we have

\displaystyle\Bigl||y|^{-N}-|z-y|^{-N}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq N|z|\Bigl(|y|^{-N-1}+|z-y|^{-N-1}\Bigr)\leq\frac{Nr}{R}|y|\Bigl(|y|^{-N-1}+(|y|-|z|)^{-N-1}\Bigr)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Nr}{R}|y|\Bigl(|y|^{-N-1}+\bigl(|y|-\frac{r}{R}|y|\bigr)^{-N-1}\Bigr)=C_{r,R}|y|^{-N}

with
C_{r,R}:=\frac{Nr}{R}\Bigl(1+(1-\frac{r}{R})^{-N-1}\Bigr)
.

3.
Flatness of bounded
s
-harmonic functions for small

The purpose of this section is to give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We start with the following comparison estimate for
s
-harmonic functions in balls.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
0<r<1
,
R\geq 2,

x\in\mathds{R}^{N}
, and let
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})
be
s
-harmonic in
B_{r}(x)
. Then we have

|u(z)-u(x)|\leq m_{s,r,R}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}\qquad\text{for $z\in B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)$}

with
m_{s,r,R}:=\Bigl(s2^{N+2}\Bigl(1+\ln(\frac{R}{2r})\Bigr)+\frac{r^{2s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\Bigl(\bigl(1-\bigl(\frac{3}{4}\bigr)^{s}\bigr)+C_{r,R}\Bigr)
.

Proof.

By translation and relabeling we may assume
x=0
and fix
R\geq 2
. The Poisson kernel representation (
1.8
) then yields, for
z\in B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
,

u(z)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\frac{(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}u(y)}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}|z-y|^{N}}\,dy.

Consequently,

|u(z)-u(0)|\leq A_{z}+B_{z}

with

A_{z}:=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}}\frac{u(y)}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}}\Bigl|\frac{(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}}{|z-y|^{N}}-\frac{r^{2s}}{|y|^{N}}\Bigr|\,dy,

and

B_{z}:=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{u(y)}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}}\Bigl|\frac{(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}}{|z-y|^{N}}-\frac{r^{2s}}{|y|^{N}}\Bigr|\,dy.

We note that, since
|y-z|\geq\frac{|y|}{2}
for
z\in B_{\frac{r}{2}}
,
y\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{z}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}}(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{-s}\Bigl|\frac{(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}}{|z-y|^{N}}-\frac{r^{2s}}{|y|^{N}}\Bigr|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}}(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{-s}\Bigl(\frac{r^{2s}}{|z-y|^{N}}+\frac{r^{2s}}{|y|^{N}}\Bigr)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2^{N}+1)\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}r^{2s}}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}}(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{-s}|y|^{-N}\,dy

where, since
\frac{R}{r}>2
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{r}}(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{-s}|y|^{-N}\,dy

\displaystyle=\omega_{N-1}\int_{r}^{R}(\tau^{2}-r)^{-s}\tau^{-1}\,d\tau=\omega_{N-1}r^{-2s}\int_{1}^{R/r}(t^{2}-1)^{-s}t^{-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{N-1}r^{-2s}\Bigl(\int_{1}^{2}(t-1)^{-s}(t+1)^{-s}\,dt+\int_{2}^{R/r}t^{-1-2s}\,dt\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{N-1}r^{-2s}\Bigl(\frac{1}{1-s}+\frac{1}{2s}\Big(2^{-2s}-\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{-2s}\Bigr).

Since
(0,1)\ni s\mapsto\frac{1}{2s}\Big(2^{-2s}-\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{-2s}\Bigr)
is decreasing, we have

\frac{1}{2s}\Big(2^{-2s}-\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{-2s}\Bigr)\leq\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2s}\Big(2^{-2s}-\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{-2s}\Bigr)=\ln(\frac{R}{2r})

and therefore we obtain

\displaystyle A_{z}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2^{N}+1)\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}\omega_{N-1}}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\Bigl(\frac{1}{1-s}+\ln(\frac{R}{2r})\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq s\frac{2(2^{N}+1)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}}{\Gamma(2-s)\Gamma(1+s)}\Bigl(1+\ln(\frac{R}{2r})\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq s2^{N+2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}\Bigl(1+\ln(\frac{R}{2r})\Bigr).

Moreover, since
|y|^{2}-r^{2}\geq(1-\frac{r}{R})|y|^{2}
for
y\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}
, we estimate

\displaystyle B_{z}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{-s}\Bigl|\frac{(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}}{|z-y|^{N}}-\frac{r^{2s}}{|y|^{N}}\Bigr|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}(1-\frac{r}{R})^{-s}}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|y|^{-2s}\Bigl|\frac{(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}}{|z-y|^{N}}-\frac{r^{2s}}{|y|^{N}}\Bigr|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}(1-\frac{r}{R})^{-s}}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|y|^{-2s}\Bigl(\frac{|(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}-r^{2s}|}{|y|^{N}}+(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}\Bigl||z-y|^{-N}-|y|^{-N}\Bigr|\Bigr)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}(1-\frac{r}{R})^{-s}}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\times

\displaystyle\left[\bigl(r^{2s}-(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}\bigr)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|y|^{-(N+2s)}\,dy+r^{2s}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|y|^{-2s}\Bigl||z-y|^{-N}-|y|^{-N}\Bigr|\Bigr)\,dy\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{crR estimate}}{\leq}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}(1-\frac{r}{R})^{-s}}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\Bigl(\bigl(r^{2s}-(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}\bigr)+r^{2s}C_{r,R}\Bigr)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|y|^{-(N+2s)}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R^{-2s}}{s}\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}(1-\frac{r}{R})^{-s}}{\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\Bigl(\bigl(r^{2s}-(r^{2}-|z|^{2})^{s}\bigr)+r^{2s}C_{r,R}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R^{-s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}(R-r)^{-s}}{s\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\Bigl(\bigl(r^{2s}-\bigl(\frac{3r^{2}}{4}\bigr)^{s}\bigr)+r^{2s}C_{r,R}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r^{2s}R^{-s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}(R-r)^{-s}}{s\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\Bigl(\bigl(1-\bigl(\frac{3}{4}\bigr)^{s}\bigr)+C_{r,R}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r^{2s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\Bigl(\bigl(1-\bigl(\frac{3}{4}\bigr)^{s}\bigr)+C_{r,R}\Bigr),

where we have used in the last step that
R\geq 2
and
R-r\geq 1
by assumption. Combining the above estimates of
A_{z}
and
B_{z},
we finish the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
s_{0},r\in(0,1)
,
x\in\mathds{R}^{N}
, and let
u_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})
be
s
-harmonic in
B_{r}(x)
for

. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
s_{\varepsilon}\in(0,s_{0})
, independent of

and
s_{0}
, with

|u(z)-u(x)|\leq\varepsilon\qquad\text{for $z\in B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)$, $s\in(0,s_{\varepsilon})$.}

Proof.

Let

Lemma 4.1

The following Theorem can be seen as a generalization of Theorem
1.3
(i).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
be an open bounded domain with
C^{2}
-boundary, and let

Moreover, let

\tilde{g}(r):=\fint_{S_{r}}g(y)\,d\sigma(y),

which is well-defined for a. e.

(4.2)

\int_{M_{R}^{\delta}}|\tilde{g}(r)|dr=o(R)\quad\text{as $R\to\infty$}\qquad\text{for every $\delta>0$,}

where

u_{s}\to 0
almost uniformly in
\Omega
as
s\to 0^{+}.

Proof.

For any
x\in\Omega,
the solution to (
1.10

\displaystyle u_{s}(x)=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}P_{s}(x,y)g(y)\,dy=\int_{B_{r_{\Omega}}(x)\setminus\Omega}P_{s}(x,y)g(y)\,dy+\int_{B^{c}_{r_{\Omega}}(x)}P_{s}(x,y)g(y)\,dy=\mathrm{I}(x)+\mathrm{II}(x)

where
r_{\Omega}:=\text{diam}\,\Omega+1.
We estimate each term below.

Estimate of
\mathrm{I}(x)
:
Denote
K=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\;:\;\delta_{\Omega}(x)<r_{\Omega}\}.
We use
[
13
, Theorem 2.10]
and (
4.1
) of Lemma
4.1
in the following estimate for
x\in\Omega
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}(x)=\int_{B_{r_{\Omega}}(x)\setminus\Omega}P_{s}(x,y)g(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq Cs(1-s)\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{s}\int_{B_{r_{\Omega}}(x)\setminus\Omega}\frac{g(y)}{\delta_{\Omega}(y)^{s}(1+r^{-1}_{0}\delta_{\Omega}(y))^{s}|x-y|^{N}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq Cs(1-s)\delta^{s}_{\Omega}(x)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\int_{B_{r_{\Omega}}(x)\setminus\Omega}\delta_{\Omega}(y)^{-s}|x-y|^{-N}\,dy

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Lemma \ref{int:la}}}{\leq}Cs\delta^{s}_{\Omega}(x)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\left(1+\delta^{-s}_{\Omega}(x)\min\left\{\frac{1}{s},1+|\log\delta_{\Omega}(x)|\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq sC(\Omega,\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(K)})\left(1+|\log\delta_{\Omega}(x)|\right)=sC_{x}.

Moreover, the RHS goes to zero as
s\to 0^{+}
almost uniformly in
\Omega
. Next, we estimate the second integral.

Estimate of
\mathrm{II}(x)
:
To estimate
\mathrm{II}(x),
we use the assumptions on
g
and
Theorem

B.2
, which yields the limit

(4.3)

\lim_{|y|\to\infty}\frac{|y|^{2s+N}P_{s}(x,y)}{c_{N,s}}=\int_{\Omega}G_{s}(x,y)\,dy=:\mathcal{K}_{s}(x)\geq 0\qquad\text{uniformly in $s\in(0,1)$ and $x\in\Omega$.}

Note that
\mathcal{K}_{s}
is the unique solution of the fractional torsion problem

(-\Delta)^{s}\mathcal{K}_{s}=1\quad\text{in $\Omega$,}\qquad\mathcal{K}_{s}=0\quad\text{in $\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega$,}

and it satisfies
\mathcal{K}_{s}(x)\to\mathcal{K}_{0}(x)=1_{\Omega}>0
as
s\to 0^{+}
almost uniformly in
\Omega
, see for instance
[
23
, Remark 3.8]
.
The asymptotics (
4.3
) allow to write the fractional Poisson kernel as

\displaystyle P_{s}(x,y)=s\cdot\frac{c_{N,s}}{s}\mathcal{K}_{s}(x)|y|^{-(2s+N)}+s\,o\bigl(|y|^{-(2s+N)}\bigr)

where
o(|y|^{-(2s+N)})\to 0
as
|y|\to\infty
uniformly in
s\in(0,1)
.
Employing this in
\mathrm{II}(x)
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{c}_{r_{\Omega}}(x)}P_{s}(x,y)g(y)\,dy

(4.4)

\displaystyle=c_{N,s}\mathcal{K}_{s}(x)\int_{B^{c}_{r_{\Omega}}(x)}|y|^{-(2s+N)}g(y)\,dy+s\int_{B^{c}_{r_{\Omega}}(x)}o\bigl(|y|^{-(2s+N)}\bigr)g(y)\,dy.

Now using the assumption of
g,
we estimate the first integral, which then also gives an estimate for the second integral. For
k\in\mathbb{N},r>0
and
\delta>0

\displaystyle M^{\delta}_{r}(k):=\left\{r\in[2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r]\,:\,|\tilde{g}(r)|\geq\delta\right\},

\displaystyle N^{\delta}_{r}(k):=\left\{r\in[2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r]\,:\,|\tilde{g}(r)|<\delta\right\}.

Moreover, we split the integral in the following way.

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{B^{c}_{r_{\Omega}}(x)}|y|^{-(2s+N)}g(y)\,dy\Bigr|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k}r_{\Omega}}^{2^{k+1}r_{\Omega}}r^{N-1}\Bigl|\fint_{S_{r}}r^{-(2s+N)}g(y)\,d\sigma(y)\Bigr|dr

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k}r_{\Omega}}^{2^{k+1}r_{\Omega}}r^{-(1+2s)}|\tilde{g}(r)|\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\int_{M^{\delta}_{r_{\Omega}}(k)}r^{-(1+2s)}|\tilde{g}(r)|\,dr+\delta\int_{N^{\delta}_{r_{\Omega}}(k)}r^{-(1+2s)}\,dr\Bigr),

where

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{N^{\delta}_{r_{\Omega}}(k)}r^{-(1+2s)}\,dr\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k}r_{\Omega}}^{2^{k+1}r_{\Omega}}r^{-(1+2s)}\,dr=\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-(1+2s)}dr=\frac{r^{-2s}_{\Omega}}{2s},

and, for every

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{M^{\delta}_{r_{\Omega}}(k)}r^{-(1+2s)}|\tilde{g}(r)|\,dr\leq\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}\int_{M^{\delta}_{r_{\Omega}}(k)}r^{-(1+2s)}|\tilde{g}(r)|\,dr+\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{M^{\delta}_{r_{\Omega}}(k)}r^{-(1+2s)}|\tilde{g}(r)|\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(k_{0},g)+C(k_{0})\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}(2^{k}r_{\Omega})^{-2s}=C_{1}(k_{0},g)+\frac{C_{2}(k_{0})2^{-2sk_{0}}r_{\Omega}^{-2s}}{1-2^{-2s}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(k_{0},g)+\frac{C_{2}(k_{0})}{1-2^{-2s}}

with

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle c_{N,s}\mathcal{K}_{s}(x)\Bigl|\int_{B^{c}_{r_{\Omega}}(x)}|y|^{-(2s+N)}g(y)\,dy\Bigr|\leq c_{N,s}\mathcal{K}_{s}(x)\Bigl(\delta\frac{r^{-2s}_{\Omega}}{2s}+C_{1}(k_{0},g)+\frac{C_{2}(k_{0})}{1-2^{-2s}}\Bigr)

where
\frac{c_{N,s}}{s}\to c_{N}
(
see the explicit form of
c_{N,s}
in (
1.2
)
),
\frac{s}{1-2^{-2s}}\to\frac{1}{\log 2}
, and

almost uniformly in
\Omega
. Consequently

\limsup_{s\to 0^{+}}\Bigl(c_{N,s}\mathcal{K}_{s}(x)\Bigl|\int_{B^{c}_{r_{\Omega}}(x)}|y|^{-(2s+N)}g(y)\,dy\Bigr|\Bigr)\leq\delta\frac{c_{N}}{2}+\frac{C_{2}(k_{0})}{\log 2}

almost uniformly in
x\in\Omega
. Since this holds for every
\delta>0
,
k_{0}\geq 1
and

as

by assumption (

), we have

almost uniformly in
x\in\Omega
. As mentioned above, the same estimate also gives that the second integral of (
4
) converges to
0
uniformly in
x\in\Omega
as
s\to 0^{+}
. Consequently,
\mathrm{II}(x)\to 0
as
s\to 0^{+}
almost uniformly in
x\in\Omega
. Since the same holds for
\mathrm{I}(x)
by the previous estimate, we conclude that

almost uniformly in
\Omega
as

as claimed.
∎

We may now complete the

\Bigl(\frac{1}{s}-\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\Bigr)\Phi_{s,y}(x)=\Bigl(\frac{c_{N,s}}{s}-c_{N,s}\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\Bigr)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\to\frac{c_{N}}{|x-y|^{N}}\qquad\text{as $s\to 0^{+}$}

uniformly in
x\in\Omega\setminus N_{\beta}
,
y\in B_{r}\setminus\Omega_{\beta}
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

If
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
, then for
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}>0
and
r>\tilde{r}_{\Omega}
we have

\int_{B_{r}\setminus\Omega_{\beta_{1}}}\frac{P_{s}(\cdot,y)}{s}g(y)\,dy\to\int_{B_{r}\setminus\Omega_{\beta_{1}}}\frac{c_{N}g(y)}{|\cdot-y|^{N}}\,dy\qquad\text{as $s\to 0^{+}$ uniformly in $\Omega\setminus N_{\beta_{0}}$.}

Proof.

Lemma
5.2
with
\beta=\min\{\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\}
yields that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{P_{s}(x,y)}{s}g(y)=\frac{c_{N}g(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}\qquad\text{uniformly in $x\in\Omega\setminus N_{\beta}$, $y\in B_{r}\setminus\Omega_{\beta}$.}

whereas
\Omega\setminus N_{\beta_{0}}\subset\Omega\setminus N_{\beta}
and
B_{r}\setminus\Omega_{\beta_{1}}\subset B_{r}\setminus\Omega_{\beta}
. Hence the claim follows.
∎

Moreover, we have the following.

Lemma 5.4
.

If
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)
satisfies (
1.13
) with
c_{g}=0
, then for every
{\varepsilon}>0
we have

(5.13)

\delta_{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}(x)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\frac{P_{s}(x,y)g(y)}{s}dy\to 0\qquad\text{as $r\to\infty$ uniformly in $s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})$, $x\in\Omega$.}

Proof.

Let
r>\tilde{r}_{\Omega}.
Then we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\frac{P_{s}(x,y)g(y)}{s}\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\left(\frac{P_{s}(x,y)}{s}-c_{N,s}\left(\frac{1}{s}-\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\right)|y|^{-N-2s}\right)g(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}c_{N,s}\left(\frac{1}{s}-\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\right)|y|^{-N-2s}g(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq||g||_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r})}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\left|\frac{P_{s}(x,y)}{s}-c_{N,s}\left(\frac{1}{s}-\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\right)|y|^{-N-2s}\right|\,dy

\displaystyle+\omega_{N-1}\left|\frac{c_{N,s}}{s}-c_{N,s}\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\right|\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{|\tilde{g}(\tau)|}{\tau^{1+2s}}\,d\tau.

To estimate the second integral above, we note that, since
\frac{c_{N,s}}{s}\to c_{N}
as
s\to 0^{+},
using the assumption (
1.13
) with
c_{g}=0,
we get

\displaystyle\delta^{{\varepsilon}}_{\Omega}(x)\left|\frac{c_{N,s}}{s}-c_{N,s}\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\right|\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{|\tilde{g}(\tau)|}{\tau}\,d\tau\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad r\to\infty

uniformly in
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
x\in\Omega
, and for fixed every
{\varepsilon}>0
, since
|\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)|=|h_{\Omega}+\rho_{N}|\leq C(1+|\ln(\delta_{\Omega}(x)|)
by the explicit representation of
h_{\Omega}
in (
2.3
).

In the remainder of the proof we discuss the convergence of the first integral. For this, we use the following two inequalities. The first one is,

\displaystyle\Bigl||y|^{-N-2s}-|x-y|^{-N-2s}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq(N+2s)|x|\Bigl(|y|^{-N-2s-1}+|x-y|^{-N-2s-1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq 2(N+2s)\tilde{r}_{\Omega}\delta_{\Omega}^{-N-2s-1}(y)\qquad\text{for $x\in\Omega$, $y\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega$,}

and the second one is,

(5.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\delta^{-N-\tau}_{\Omega}(y)\,dy\leq\frac{2^{N+\tau}}{\tau}\omega_{N-1}\frac{1}{r^{\tau}}\quad\text{ for $r>2\tilde{r}_{\Omega}$ and $\tau>0$.}

To see the latter inequality, note that for
|y|\geq r>2\tilde{r}_{\Omega}>r_{\Omega}
, we have
\delta_{\Omega}(y)\geq|y|-r_{\Omega}\geq\frac{|y|}{2}.
Therefore, we conclude

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\delta_{\Omega}^{-N-\tau}(y)dy\leq 2^{N+\tau}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}|y|^{-N-\tau}dy=2^{N+\tau}\omega_{N-1}\frac{r^{-\tau}}{\tau},

as stated in (
5.14
). With this we estimate, for
r>2\tilde{r}_{\Omega}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\Bigl|\frac{P_{s}(x,y)}{s}-c_{N,s}\Bigl(\frac{1}{s}-\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\Bigr)|y|^{-N-2s}\Bigr||g(y)|dy

(5.15)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\Bigl|\frac{P_{s}(x,y)}{s}-c_{N,s}\Bigl(\frac{1}{s}-\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\Bigr)|x-y|^{-N-2s}\Bigr|dy

\displaystyle+2(N+2s)c_{N,s}\tilde{r}_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{1}{s}-\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\Bigr)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\delta^{-N-2s-1}_{\Omega}(y)dy,

where

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\delta_{\Omega}^{-N-2s-1}(y)dy\leq 2^{N+2s+1}\omega_{N-1}\frac{r^{-2s-1}}{2s+1}\leq\frac{2^{N+4}\omega_{N-1}}{r}\to 0\qquad\text{for $r\to\infty$}

uniformly in
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Since
c_{N,s}=O(s)
and
\delta_{\Omega}^{{\varepsilon}}(x)\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)
is bounded in
\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle 2(N+2s)c_{N,s}\tilde{r}_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{1}{s}-\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\Bigr)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\delta^{-N-2s-1}_{\Omega}(y)dy\to 0\quad\text{as $r\to\infty$}

uniformly for
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and almost uniformly in
x\in\Omega
. Thus, it remains to discuss the convergence of (
5.15
). For this, note that, for
r>2\tilde{r}_{\Omega}
, using (
5
), (
5.14
), and

c_{N,s}\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+1)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}\quad\text{for $s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})$},

we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\Bigl|\frac{P_{s}(x,y)}{s}-c_{N,s}\Bigl(\frac{1}{s}-\tilde{h}_{\Omega}(x)\Bigr)|x-y|^{-N-2s}\Bigr|dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{sc_{N,s}C}{\varepsilon\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\delta^{-N-2s}_{\Omega}(y)dy+r_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{2sC(N+2s)}{\varepsilon\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{\varepsilon}}+4\omega_{N-1}c_{N,s}\Bigr)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}}\delta_{\Omega}^{-N-2s-1}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{sc_{N,s}C}{\varepsilon\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{\varepsilon}}\frac{2^{N+2s}}{2s}\omega_{N-1}r^{-2s}+r_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{2sC(N+2s)}{\varepsilon\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{\varepsilon}}+4\omega_{N-1}c_{N,s}\Bigr)2^{N+2s+1}\frac{r^{-2s-1}}{2s+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{N}C}{\varepsilon\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{\varepsilon}}\omega_{N-1}\bigl(c_{N,s}r^{-2s}\bigr)+r_{\Omega}\Bigl(\frac{C(N+1)}{\varepsilon\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{\varepsilon}}+4\omega_{N-1}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+1)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}\Bigr)2^{N+3}\frac{1}{r}.

Since

c_{N,s}r^{-2s}\to 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{r}\to 0\qquad\text{as $r\to\infty$ uniformly in $s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})$,}

we conclude the proof of (
5.13
).
∎

For this we let

\displaystyle\left|\frac{P_{s}(x,y)g(y)}{s}\right|\leq C(1-s)\frac{|g(y)|}{\delta^{s}_{\Omega}(y)|x-y|^{N}}

A counterexample

In this section, we construct the counterexample that yields the proof of Theorem

For this, let

Lemma 7.1

We also need the following extension of

.

Lemma 7.2

Let
f\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
with

(7.1)

\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\Bigl\|\frac{(-\Delta)^{\sigma}f-f}{\sigma}-\loglap f\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N})
be a function with

and
\phi\equiv 1
in an open neighborhood
\tilde{U}\subset U

f\phi\in C^{\gamma}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N})
. Hence, using
[

, Theorem 1.1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(-\Delta)^{\sigma}-\bm{I}}{\sigma}\right)f\phi\to L_{\Delta}f\phi\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,\,\sigma\to 0\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}).

Now, to show

(7.2)

\left(\frac{(-\Delta)^{\sigma}-\bm{I}}{\sigma}\right)f(1-\phi)\to L_{\Delta}f(1-\phi)\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,\,\sigma\to 0\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,L^{\infty}(\Omega),

note that for
x\in\Omega,
we have

\left[\left(\frac{(-\Delta)^{\sigma}-\bm{I}}{\sigma}\right)f(1-\phi)\right](x)=\left[\left(\frac{(-\Delta)^{\sigma}}{\sigma}\right)f(1-\phi)\right](x)=\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\tilde{U}}\frac{f(\phi-1)(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2\sigma}}\,dy

and

\left[L_{\Delta}f(1-\phi)\right](x)=c_{N}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega_{0}}\frac{f(\phi-1)(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}\,dy.

Hence,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\left|\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{(-\Delta)^{\sigma}-\bm{I}}{\sigma}\Bigr)f(1-\phi)\Bigr](x)-\left[L_{\Delta}f(1-\phi)\right](x)\right|\leq\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\tilde{U}}\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma|x-y|^{N+2\sigma}}-\frac{c_{N}}{|x-y|^{N}}\Bigr||f(y)|\,dy.

Next, we let
{\varepsilon}>0
and set
B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
for
r>0
. Since
f\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, we may choose
R>1
sufficiently large with
\Omega\subset\tilde{U}\subset B_{R/2}
and such that

(7.4)

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{N}}\,dy\leq{\varepsilon}.

We note that,

|x-y|\geq|y|-|x|\geq|y|-\frac{R}{2}\geq\frac{|y|}{2}\qquad\text{for $x\in\Omega$, $y\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}$},

and therefore from (
7.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma|x-y|^{N+2\sigma}}-\frac{c_{N}}{|x-y|^{N}}\Bigr||f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{N}}\,dy\>\Bigl\|\frac{c_{N}(1+|\cdot|)^{N}}{|x-(\cdot)|^{N}}\Bigl(\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma c_{N}}|x-(\cdot)|^{-2\sigma}-1\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq{\varepsilon}\Bigl\|2^{N}c_{N}\frac{(1+|\cdot|)^{N}}{|\cdot|^{N}}\Bigl(\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{c_{N}\sigma}2^{2\sigma}|\cdot|^{-2\sigma}+1\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}\leq{\varepsilon}4^{N}c_{N}\Bigl(\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma c_{N}}4^{\sigma}+1\Bigr)\qquad\text{for $x\in\Omega$,}

which, since
\frac{4^{\sigma}c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma c_{N}}\to 1
as
\sigma\to 0^{+}
, implies that

(7.5)

\limsup_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma|x-y|^{N+2\sigma}}-\frac{c_{N}}{|x-y|^{N}}\Bigr||f(y)|\,dy\leq{\varepsilon}2^{2N+1}c_{N}.

Moreover, since
\tau:=\text{dist}(\Omega,\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\tilde{U})>0
, we have

\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma c_{N}}|x-y|^{-2\sigma}\to 1\qquad\text{as $\sigma\to 0^{+}$ uniformly in $x\in\Omega$, $y\in B_{R}\setminus\tilde{U},$}

and also

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus\tilde{U}}\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma|x-y|^{N+2\sigma}}-\frac{c_{N}}{|x-y|^{N}}\Bigr||f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{N}}\,dy\Bigl\|\frac{c_{N}(1+|\cdot|)^{N}}{|x-(\cdot)|^{N}}\Bigl(\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{c_{N}\sigma}|x-(\cdot)|^{-2\sigma}-1\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\setminus\tilde{U})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{N}(R+1)^{N}}{\tau^{N}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{N}}\,dy\Bigl\|\Bigl(\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma c_{N}}|x-(\cdot)|^{-2\sigma}-1\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\setminus\tilde{U})},

which implies that

(7.6)

\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{B_{R}\setminus\tilde{U}}\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma|x-y|^{N+2\sigma}}-\frac{c_{N}}{|x-y|^{N}}\Bigr||f(y)|\,dy=0.

Combining (
7.5
) and (
7.6
) gives

\limsup_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\tilde{U}}\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma|x-y|^{N+2\sigma}}-\frac{c_{N}}{|x-y|^{N}}\Bigr||f(y)|\,dy\leq{\varepsilon}2^{2N+1}c_{N}

for every
{\varepsilon}>0
and therefore

\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\tilde{U}}\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,\sigma}}{\sigma|x-y|^{N+2\sigma}}-\frac{c_{N}}{|x-y|^{N}}\Bigr||f(y)|\,dy=0.

Consequently, (
7.2
) follows by (
7.3
). This complete the proof of (
7.1
).
∎

Next, we need the following observation.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
0<s<\frac{\alpha}{2}<1
and
g\in L^{1}_{s}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.
Then we have

(7.7)

\int_{\Omega}[(-\Delta)^{s}g]\phi\,dx=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\,dx\qquad\text{for all $\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)$.}

We note that this lemma guarantees that the pointwise principle value definition of
(-\Delta)^{s}g
in
\Omega
is consistent with the definition of
(-\Delta)^{s}g
as a distribution on
\mathds{R}^{N}
via

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}[(-\Delta)^{s}g]\phi\,dx:=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\,dx\qquad\text{for all $\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N})$.}

Proof.

For
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
we have, with
K:=\supp\phi\subset\subset\Omega
, by the Fubini theorem and a change of variable,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\,dx=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(x)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{2\phi(x)-\phi(x+z)-\phi(x-z)}{|z|^{N+2s}}dzdx

\displaystyle\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(x)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{2\phi(x)-\phi(x+z)-\phi(x-z)}{|z|^{N+2s}}dxdz

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|z|^{N+2s}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}2\phi(x)g(x)dx-\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(x)\phi(x+z)\bigr)dx-\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\bigl(g(x)\phi(x-z)\bigr)dx\Bigr)dz

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|z|^{N+2s}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}2\phi(x)g(x)dx-\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(x-z)\phi(x)\bigr)dx-\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\bigl(g(x+z)\phi(x)\bigr)dx\Bigr)dz

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\Bigl(\int_{K}\phi(x)\frac{2g(x)-g(x+z)-g(x-z)}{|z|^{N+2s}}dx\Bigr)dz

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}\int_{K}\phi(x)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{2g(x)-g(x+z)-g(x-z)}{|z|^{N+2s}}dz=\int_{\Omega}\phi(-\Delta)^{s}g\,dx.

∎

With the help of Lemma
7.3
, we may now establish the following local version of the semigroup property for fractional Laplacians.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
0<t\leq\frac{s}{2}<s<\frac{\alpha}{2}<1
, and let
g\in L^{1}_{t}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
satisfy
(-\Delta)^{s-t}g\in L^{1}_{t}(\mathds{R}^{N})
. Then we have

(7.8)

(-\Delta)^{s}g=(-\Delta)^{t}(-\Delta)^{s-t}g\qquad\text{in $\Omega$.}

Proof.

We start by noting that, by the assumption
(-\Delta)^{s-t}g\in L^{1}_{t}(\mathds{R}^{N})
and since
(-\Delta)^{s-t}g\in C^{\alpha-2(s-t)}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, both sides of (
7.8
)
are well-defined in pointwise sense in
\Omega
. It then suffices to show that

(7.9)

\int_{\Omega}[(-\Delta)^{s}g]\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}[(-\Delta)^{s-t}(-\Delta)^{t}g]\phi\,dx\qquad\text{for all $\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)$.}

By (
7.7
) and since
L^{1}_{t}(\mathds{R}^{N})\subset L^{1}_{s}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, we have

(7.10)

\int_{\Omega}[(-\Delta)^{s}g]\phi\,dx=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(-\Delta)^{s}\phi\,dx=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(-\Delta)^{s-t}\psi\,dx

with
\psi:=(-\Delta)^{t}\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N},(1+|x|)^{N+2t}dx).

Moreover, we note that for any multiindex
\alpha
we have
\partial^{\alpha}\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, and

\partial^{\alpha}\psi=(-\Delta)^{s}[\partial^{\alpha}\phi]\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N},(1+|x|)^{N+2t}dx).

In particular,
\psi
is contained in the space
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{t}(\mathds{R}^{N})
of
C^{2}
-functions
\mathds{R}^{N}\to\mathds{R}
satisfying that

\partial^{\alpha}\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N},(1+|x|)^{N+2t}dx)
for
|\alpha|\leq 2
. We endow this space with the norm

\|\psi\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{t}}:=\sup_{|\alpha|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N},(1+|x|)^{N+2t}dx)}

Since
t\leq\frac{s}{2}\leq s-t
,
(-\Delta)^{s-t}
defines a continuous linear operator

{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{t}(\mathds{R}^{N})\to L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N},(1+|x|)^{N+2t}dx),

see e.g.
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
. Using that
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N})
is dense in the space
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{t}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, we may choose a sequence
\psi_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N})
with
\|\psi_{n}-\phi\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{t}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, which, since
g,(-\Delta)^{s-t}g\in L^{1}_{t}(\mathds{R}^{N})
by assumption, implies that

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(-\Delta)^{s-t}\psi\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}g(-\Delta)^{s-t}\psi_{n}\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}[(-\Delta)^{s-t}g]\psi_{n}\,dx=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}[(-\Delta)^{s-t}g]\psi\,dx.

Combining this with (
7.10
) gives

\int_{\Omega}[(-\Delta)^{s}g]\phi\,dx=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}[(-\Delta)^{s-t}g]\psi\,dx=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}[(-\Delta)^{s-t}g](-\Delta)^{t}\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}[(-\Delta)^{t}(-\Delta)^{s-t}g]\phi\,dx,

where we have used (
7.7
) and the fact that
(-\Delta)^{s-t}g\in L^{1}_{s}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap C^{\alpha-2(s-t)}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
in the final step. Hence, (
7.9
) holds.
∎

We now begin with the main part of the proof of Theorem
1.7
. For this, from now on let

(7.11)

g\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap C^{\alpha}(U)\quad\text{for some $\alpha\in(0,1]$,}

where
U
is any open neighborhood of
\Omega
as fixed in (
1.22
). As a consequence, for
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
,
(-\Delta)^{s}g
is defined as a distribution on
\mathds{R}^{N}
and pointwisely as a function in
C^{\alpha-2s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)
, cf. Lemma
7.3
.
Throughout the proof of Theorem
1.7
, we will need to consider different cases distinguished by properties of

(7.12)

f_{s}:=(-\Delta)^{s}g.

As a rather direct consequence of Lemma
7.2
and Proposition
7.4
we have the following.

Lemma 7.5
.

Suppose that

(7.13)

f_{s}=(-\Delta)^{s}g\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})

for some
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
. Then we have

\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\Bigl\|\frac{f_{s+\sigma}-f_{s}}{\sigma}-\loglap f_{s}\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0.

Proof.

The assumption (
7.13
) implies that
f_{s}\in L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathds{R}^{N})
for every
\sigma>0
, and therefore Proposition
7.4
gives

\frac{f_{s+\sigma}-f_{s}}{\sigma}=\frac{(-\Delta)^{\sigma}-\bm{I}}{\sigma}f_{s}\qquad\text{for $\sigma\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2}-s)$.}

Since
f_{s}\in C^{\alpha-2s}(\Omega)\cap L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, the claim now follows from Lemma
7.2
.
∎

Next, we add the following complement of Lemma
7.5
under a different additional assumption on
g
.

Lemma 7.6
.

Suppose that

(7.14)

g\equiv 0
in a neighborhood
U
of
\Omega
,

and let
f_{s}
be given by (
7.12
) for
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
. Then we have

\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\Bigl\|\frac{f_{s+\sigma}-f_{s}}{\sigma}-(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{Log}}g\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0\qquad\text{for $s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})$}

with the fractional-logarithmic operator
(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{Log}}
defined in (
1.20
). Moreover, the map

(7.15)

(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})\to L^{\infty}(\Omega),\qquad s\mapsto(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{Log}}g

is continuous.

Proof.

A direct computation gives

\Bigl[\frac{f_{s+\sigma}-f_{s}}{\sigma}-(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{Log}}g\Bigr](x)=\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus U}\ell_{\sigma}(x,y)g(y)\,dy\qquad\text{for $x\in\Omega$}

with

\displaystyle\ell_{\sigma}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma}\Bigl(\frac{c_{N,s+\sigma}}{|x-y|^{N+2s+2\sigma}}-\frac{c_{N,s}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\Bigr)-\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\Bigl(b_{N,s}-c_{N,s}\ln|x-y|\Bigr)

\displaystyle=|x-y|^{-N-2s}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\sigma}\Bigl(\frac{c_{N,s+\sigma}}{|x-y|^{2\sigma}}-c_{N,s}\Bigr)-\Bigl(b_{N,s}-c_{N,s}\ln|x-y|\Bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=|x-y|^{-N-2s}\Bigl[\ell_{\sigma}^{1}(x,y)+\ell_{\sigma}^{2}(x,y)\Bigr]

with

\ell_{\sigma}^{1}(x,y)=\frac{c_{N,s+\sigma}-c_{N,s}}{\sigma}|x-y|^{-2\sigma}-b_{N,s}

and

\ell_{\sigma}^{2}(x,y)=c_{N,s}\biggl[\frac{1}{\sigma}\Bigl(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2\sigma}}-1\Bigr)+\ln|x-y|\biggr].

Consequently,

(7.16)

\Bigl|\frac{f_{s+\sigma}-f_{s}}{\sigma}-(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{Log}}g\Bigr|(x)\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus N)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus U}|\ell_{\sigma}(x,y)|\,dy.

Next, we let
{\varepsilon}>0
, and we choose
R>1
sufficiently large with
\Omega\subset U\subset B_{R/2}
and

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|y|^{-N-\frac{s}{2}}\,dy<{\varepsilon}.

Then we have
|x-y|\geq|y|-|x|\geq\frac{|y|}{2}
for
x\in\Omega
,
y\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}
and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x-y|^{-N-2s}|\ell^{1}_{\sigma}(x,y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,s+\sigma}-c_{N,s}}{\sigma}\Bigr|+|b_{N,s}|\Bigr)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x-y|^{-N-2s}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,s+\sigma}-c_{N,s}}{\sigma}\Bigr|+|b_{N,s}|\Bigr)2^{N+2s}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|y|^{-N-2s}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\Bigl|\frac{c_{N,s+\sigma}-c_{N,s}}{\sigma}\Bigr|+|b_{N,s}|\Bigr)2^{N+2s}\leq\kappa_{1}(N,s){\varepsilon}

for all
\sigma\in[0,1-s]
,
x\in\Omega
with a constant
\kappa_{1}(N,s)>0
. Moreover, using the estimate (see
[
23
, Lemma 2.1]
)

\Bigl|\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2\sigma}}-1\Bigr|\leq\frac{2\sigma}{s}\Bigl(|x-y|^{-2\sigma-s}+|x-y|^{s}\Bigr)\leq\frac{4\sigma}{s}|x-y|^{s}\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega$, $y\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}$,}

we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x-y|^{-N-2s}|\ell^{2}_{\sigma}(x,y)|\,dy\leq\frac{4c_{N,s}}{s}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|x-y|^{-N-s}(1+\ln|x-y|)dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4c_{N,s}2^{N+s}}{s}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|y|^{-N-s}(1+\ln(R+|y|))dy\leq\kappa_{2}(N,s)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|y|^{-N-\frac{s}{2}}dy\leq\kappa_{2}(N,s){\varepsilon}

for all
x\in\Omega
with a constant
\kappa_{2}(N,s)>0
.
Consequently,

(7.17)

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|\ell_{\sigma}(x,y)|\,dy\leq(\kappa_{1}(N,s)+\kappa_{2}(N,s)){\varepsilon}\qquad\text{for all $\sigma\in[0,1-s]$, $x\in\Omega$.}

In addition, since with
\tau:=\text{dist}(\Omega,\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus U)>0
we have

\tau\leq|x-y|\leq 2R\qquad\text{for $x\in\Omega$, $y\in B_{R}\setminus U$,}

we have

\ell_{\sigma}^{1}(x,y)\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\ell_{\sigma}^{2}(x,y)\to 0\qquad\text{as $\sigma\to 0^{+}$ uniformly in $x\in\Omega$, $y\in B_{R}\setminus U$,}

which implies that

(7.18)

\int_{B_{R}\setminus U}|\ell_{\sigma}(x,y)|\,dy\to 0\qquad\text{as $\sigma\to 0^{+}$ uniformly in $x\in\Omega$.}

Combining (
7.17
) and (
7.18
) yields

\limsup_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus N}|\ell_{\sigma}(x,y)|\,dy\leq(\kappa_{1}(N,s)+\kappa_{2}(N,s)){\varepsilon}\qquad\text{for all ${\varepsilon}>0$}

and therefore

(7.19)

\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus U}|\ell_{\sigma}(x,y)|\,dy=0.

The claim
7.6
now follows by combining (
7.16
) and (
7.19
).

The continuity of the map in (
7.15
) follows by very similar estimates, so we skip the details.
∎

Next, let
u_{s}
denote the unique solution of (
1.10
) for
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
. To study the differentiability of the maps
s\mapsto u_{s}
, we can equivalently consider the function

(7.20)

v_{s}:=g-u_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})

for
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
, which is the unique solution of the problem

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}v_{s}=f_{s}\,\,\,

\displaystyle\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega,

\displaystyle v_{s}=0\,\,\,

\displaystyle\text{in}\,\,\,\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

Hence, we have
v_{s}={\mathbb{G}}_{s}f_{s}
with the Green operator
{\mathbb{G}}
defined in (
2.7
), and we can apply the results from
[
24
]

to the map
s\mapsto v_{s}
in the following.

Proposition 7.7
.

Suppose that (
7.13
) holds for some
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
. Then we have

(7.21)

\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\frac{{\mathbb{G}}_{s+\sigma}f_{s+\sigma}-{\mathbb{G}}_{s}f_{s}}{\sigma}={\mathbb{G}}_{s}(w_{s}-{{\mathbb{P}}_{s}}^{\!\!c}f_{s})\qquad\text{in $L^{\infty}(\Omega)$}

with

(7.22)

w_{s}=L_{\Delta}(1_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}f_{s})\qquad\text{in $\Omega$.}

Proof.

To prove (
7.21
), we note that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\frac{{\mathbb{G}}_{s+\sigma}f_{s+\sigma}-{\mathbb{G}}_{s}f_{s}}{\sigma}-{\mathbb{G}}_{s}(w_{s}-{{\mathbb{P}}_{s}}^{\!\!c}f_{s})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\Bigl\|\frac{{\mathbb{G}}_{s+\sigma}f_{s+\sigma}-{\mathbb{G}}_{s}f_{s}}{\sigma}-{\mathbb{G}}_{s}(L_{\Delta}f_{s}+{\mathbb{D}}f_{s})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\|\frac{({\mathbb{G}}_{s+\sigma}-{\mathbb{G}}_{s})f_{s}}{\sigma}-{\mathbb{G}}_{s}{\mathbb{D}}_{s}f_{s}\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\Bigl\|{\mathbb{G}}_{s+\sigma}\left(\frac{f_{s+\sigma}-f_{s}}{\sigma}\right)-{\mathbb{G}}_{s}L_{\Delta}f_{s}\Bigl\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}:=\mathrm{I}_{s}+\mathrm{II}_{s}

with

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}_{s}f_{s}=-L_{\Delta}1_{\Omega}f_{s}-{{\mathbb{P}}_{s}}^{\!\!c}f_{s}.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{f_{s+\sigma}-f_{s}}{\sigma}\to L_{\Delta}f_{s}\qquad\text{as $\sigma\to 0^{+}$ uniformly in $\Omega$}

by Lemma
7.5
, it follows from
Lemma

7.1
that

(7.23)

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}_{s}=\Bigl\|{\mathbb{G}}_{s+\sigma}\left(\frac{f_{s+\sigma}-f_{s}}{\sigma}\right)-{\mathbb{G}}_{s}L_{\Delta}f_{s}\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to 0\,\,\,\text{as }\,\,\,\sigma\to 0.

On the other hand, by
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
and since
f_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
by the assumptions on
g
, we have

(7.24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{s}=\Bigl\|\frac{({\mathbb{G}}_{s+\sigma}-{\mathbb{G}}_{s})f_{s}}{\sigma}-{\mathbb{D}}_{s}f_{s}\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to 0\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,\,\sigma\to 0.

Combining (
7.23
)-(
7.24
), we conclude (
7.21
), as claimed.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

In the special case where

(7.25)

g\in C^{\alpha}_{c}(\mathds{R}^{N}),

we have

f_{s}=(-\Delta)^{s}g\in C^{\alpha-2s}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{N})\quad\text{for $s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})$,}

so (
7.13
) holds for all
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
. Moreover, using (
1.21
), we find that the function
w_{s}
in (
7.22
) can be written as

w_{s}=L_{\Delta}\bigl(1_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}f_{s}\bigr)=L_{\Delta}f_{s}-L_{\Delta}\bigl(1_{\Omega}f_{s}\bigr)=(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{Log}}g-L_{\Delta}\bigl(1_{\Omega}f_{s}\bigr)\qquad\text{in $\Omega$}.

In the next proposition, we show that the same holds under assumption (
7.14
) for
g
.

Proposition 7.9
.

Assume
g
is as in (
7.14
) and
f_{s}
as in (
7.12
). Then for
s\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{2})
we have

(7.26)

\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\frac{{\mathbb{G}}_{s+\sigma}f_{s+\sigma}-{\mathbb{G}}_{s}f_{s}}{\sigma}={\mathbb{G}}_{s}(w_{s}-{{\mathbb{P}}_{s}}^{\!\!c}f_{s})\qquad\text{in $L^{\infty}(\Omega)$}

with

w_{s}=(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{Log}}g-L_{\Delta}\bigl(1_{\Omega}f_{s}\bigr)\qquad\text{in $\Omega$.}

Proof.

Following the proof of Proposition
7.7
line by line and using Lemma
7.6
in place of Lemma
7.5

Appendix A
A simple uniform Harnack inequality

Here we give a short proof of the Harnack inequality stated in (
1.9
).

Lemma A.1

(Harnack inequality – proof of (
1.9
))
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
open, and let
u\in L^{1}_{s}(\mathds{R}^{N})
be a nontrivial, nonnegative function such that
(-\Delta)^{s}u=0
in
\Omega
. Then, given
x\in\Omega
,
r>0
such that
B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega
we have

2^{-4N-2}\leq\frac{u(x_{1})}{u(x_{2})}\leq 2^{4N+2}\quad\text{for all $x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{r/4}(x)$.}

Proof.

Under the assumptions, it follows that
u|_{\Omega}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
(after passing to a suitable representative of
u
). Hence,
u
is locally bounded and thus, in particular,

(-\Delta)^{s}u=0\quad\text{in $B=B_{r/2}(x)$}

and
u
is bounded in a neighborhood of
B
. Then it follows that for any
z\in B
we have

u(z)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(s)}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B}\frac{(\frac{r^{2}}{4}-|z-x|^{2})^{s}u(y)}{(|y-x|^{2}-\frac{r^{2}}{4})^{s}|z-y|^{N}}\,dy.

If
|z-x|\leq\frac{r}{4}
,
y\in\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B
it is not hard to see that

\frac{|x-y|^{N}}{|z-y|^{N}}\leq 2^{N}\Big(1+\frac{|x-z|^{N}}{|z-y|^{N}}\Big)\leq 2^{N}\big(1+2^{N}\big)<8^{N}

and

\frac{|x-y|^{N}}{|z-y|^{N}}\geq\Big(\frac{|z-y|-|x-z|}{|z-y|}\Big)^{N}=(1-\frac{1}{2})^{N}=2^{-N}.

Thus

\displaystyle 2^{-N}\frac{3^{s}r^{2s}}{16^{s}|x-y|^{N}}\leq\frac{(\frac{r^{2}}{4}-|z-x|^{2})^{s}}{|z-y|^{N}}\leq 8^{N}\frac{r^{2s}}{4^{s}|x-y|^{N}}

which entails

\frac{3^{s}}{2^{N}4^{s}}u(x)\leq u(z)\leq 8^{N}u(x)\quad\text{for all $z\in B_{r/4}(x)$.}

Hence, for
x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{r/4}(x)
we have

\frac{u(x_{1})}{u(x_{2})}\leq\frac{8^{N}2^{N}4^{s}}{3^{s}}\leq 2^{4N+2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{u(x_{1})}{u(x_{2})}\geq\frac{3^{s}}{2^{N}4^{s}8^{N}}\geq 2^{-4N-2}

as claimed.
∎

\displaystyle\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)-(-\Delta)^{t}u(x)}{s-t}\quad\text{as}\quad t\to s.

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)-(-\Delta)^{t}u(x)}{s-t}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{N,s}-c_{N,t}}{s-t}\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(x+z)}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz+c_{N,t}\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\left(u(x)-u(x+z)\right)\frac{|z|^{-(N+2s)}-|z|^{-(N+2t)}}{s-t}\,dz.

First, we show that

(D.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\,x\mapsto\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\left(u(x)-u(x+z)\right)\left(\frac{|z|^{-(N+2s)}-|z|^{-(N+2t)}}{s-t}+\frac{2\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\right)\,dz\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to s.

We note that

F_{t}(|z|):=\frac{|z|^{-(N+2s)}-|z|^{-(N+2t)}}{s-t}+\frac{2\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to s,

and since
u\in C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U),
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon<|z|<1}\left(u(x)-u(x+z)\right)F_{t}(|z|)\,dz\leq\int_{\varepsilon<|z|<1}|z|^{\alpha}F_{t}(|z|)\,dz.

An application of the mean value theorem guarantees that there exists a
\xi\in(t,s)
such that

\displaystyle|F_{t}(|z|)|\leq G_{s}(|z|)=2|\ln|z||\left(|z|^{-(N+2s)}+|z|^{-(N+2\xi)}\right).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon<|z|<1}|z|^{\alpha}|F_{t}(|z|)|\leq 2\int_{\varepsilon<|z|<1}|z|^{\alpha-\kappa-N-2s}+|z|^{\alpha-\kappa-N-2\xi}\,dz<\infty\quad\text{for every small}\quad\varepsilon,\kappa>0,

since
\xi<s<\frac{\alpha}{2}.
Thus using Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in V}\text{P.V}\int_{|z|<1}\left(u(x)-u(x+z)\right)\left(\frac{|z|^{-(N+2s)}-|z|^{-(N+2t)}}{s-t}+\frac{2\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\right)\,dz\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to s.

On the other hand, for
|z|\geq 1,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\geq 1}\left(u(x)-u(x+z)\right)F_{t}(|z|)\,dz\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}|\ln|z||\left(|z|^{-(N+2s)}+|z|^{-(N+2\xi)}\right)\,dz<\infty.

Hence, again by dominated convergence, we conclude

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{|z|\geq 1}\left(u(x)-u(x+z)\right)\left(\frac{|z|^{-(N+2s)}-|z|^{-(N+2t)}}{s-t}+\frac{2\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\right)\,dz\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to s.

Combining these two convergences above, we have (
D.2
). Finally, since by definition

\displaystyle\frac{c_{N,s}-c_{N,t}}{s-t}\to b_{N,s}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,c_{N,t}\to c_{N,s}\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,\,t\to s,

we complete the proof the first statement.

The second statement (
D.1
) is a direct consequence of
[
11
, Theorem 1.1 (iv)]
since
C^{\alpha}(\mathds{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})\subset\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(\mathds{R}^{N})
, where
\mathcal{Z}(\mathds{R}^{N})
denotes the Lizorkin space on
\mathds{R}^{N}.

∎

We prove the regularity of the fractional logarithmic Laplacian in the following result, which might be of independent interest.

Lemma D.2
.

Let
0<s<1
,
\Omega,U\subset\mathds{R}^{N}
open with
\overline{\Omega}\subset U
, and
u\in C^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)\cap L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
with
\alpha>2s
. Then
(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{L}og}u\in C^{\alpha-\kappa-2s}(\Omega)
for any
\kappa>0.

Proof.

Let
d=\text{dist}(\Omega,\partial U)
and set
\varepsilon=|x_{1}-x_{2}|.
Since
3
3

3

Indeed, the statement is also true for any
s>0
, but one has to be more careful due to the higher order finite differences in the numerator. As it is not relevant for our analysis, we use the present formulation for the reader’s convenience.

s<\frac{1}{2},
we recall
(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{L}og}u
without the principal value,

\displaystyle[(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{L}og}u](x)

\displaystyle=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(x+z)}{|z|^{N+2s}}\bigl(-2\ln|z|\bigr)\,dy+b_{N,s}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)

\displaystyle=I^{s}_{\mathrm{L}og}u(x)+b_{N,s}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)

Note that the second part of the above expression is
(-\Delta)^{s}
and it is known that
(-\Delta)^{s}u\in C^{\alpha-2s}(\Omega).
Hence, we shall only consider the first part, which involves a logarithmic contribution in the kernel. For any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega,
we estimate

\displaystyle|I^{s}_{\mathrm{L}og}u(x_{1})-I^{s}_{\mathrm{L}og}u(x_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{N,s}\Bigg|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}}\frac{\left[(u(x_{1})-u(x_{1}+z))-(u(x_{2})-u(x_{2}+z))\right]\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{N,s}\Bigg|\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}...\,dz\Bigg|+\Bigg|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)}...\,dz\Bigg|=2c_{N,s}(\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}).

Estimate of
\mathrm{I}:

Using
u\in C^{\alpha}(U),
we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{|[(u(x_{1})-u(x_{1}+z))-(u(x_{2})-u(x_{2}+z))]\ln|z||}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\leq[u]_{0,\alpha}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{|z|^{\alpha}|\ln|z||}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq c(\kappa)[u]_{0,\alpha}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\rho^{N-1}\rho^{\alpha-\kappa-N-2s}\,d\rho=\frac{c(\kappa)[u]_{0,\alpha}}{\alpha-\kappa-2s}\varepsilon^{\alpha-\kappa-2s}=\frac{c(\kappa,[u]_{0,\alpha})}{\alpha-\kappa-2s}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha-\kappa-2s}.

Estimate of
\mathrm{II}:

Here we can split the integral as

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{[(u(x_{1})-u(x_{1}+z))-(u(x_{2})-u(x_{2}+z))]\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq|(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{|\ln|z||}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz+\Bigg|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{(u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{2}+z))\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\Bigg|.

The first integral can be estimated again using the Hölder continuity of
u,
and we obtain

\displaystyle|(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{|\ln|z||}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\leq[u]_{0,\alpha}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}|\ln\rho|\rho^{N-1}\rho^{-N-2s}\,d\rho.

We note that

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}|\ln\rho|\rho^{-1-2s}\,d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}(-\ln\rho)\rho^{-1-2s}\,d\rho+\int_{1}^{\infty}(\ln\rho)\rho^{-1-2s}\,d\rho

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-2s}\left[\frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{2s}-\frac{1}{4s^{2}}\right]+\frac{1}{2s^{2}}\leq\varepsilon^{-2s}\left[\frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{2s}+\frac{1}{4s^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-\kappa-2s}\left(\frac{1}{2s}+\frac{1}{4s^{2}}\right).

Using this in the above estimate, we get

\displaystyle|(u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{|\ln|z||}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\leq c(s,[u]_{0,\alpha})|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha-\kappa-2s}.

We use a change of variable to estimate the second integral of
\mathrm{II}.

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{[u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{2}+z)]\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\varepsilon<|z|\leq\frac{d}{2}}\frac{|u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{2}+z)||\ln|z||}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz+\bigg|\int_{|z|>\frac{d}{2}}\frac{[u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{2}+z)]\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\bigg|.

Note that since
|z|\leq\frac{d}{2},
for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega,
we have that
x_{i}+z\in U,

i=1,2.
Therefore, using the Hölder continuity of
u,
we derive

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon<|z|\leq\frac{d}{2}}\frac{|u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{2}+z)||\ln|z||}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\leq c(s,[u]_{0,\alpha})|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha-\kappa-2s}.

Next, using change of variable in the second integral, we get

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{|z|>\frac{d}{2}}\frac{[u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{2}+z)]\ln|z|}{|z|^{N+2s}}\,dz\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{|x_{1}-y|>\frac{d}{2}}\frac{u(y)\ln|x_{1}-y|}{|x_{1}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy-\int_{|x_{2}-y|>\frac{d}{2}}\frac{u(y)\ln|x_{2}-y|}{|x_{2}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\bigg|.

Let us define the sets

\displaystyle D_{1}:=\left\{y\;:\;|x_{1}-y|>\frac{d}{2},|x_{2}-y|>\frac{d}{2}\right\},

\displaystyle D_{2}:=\left\{y\;:\;|x_{1}-y|>\frac{d}{2},|x_{2}-y|\leq\frac{d}{2}\right\},

\displaystyle D_{3}:=\left\{y\;:\;|x_{1}-y|\leq\frac{d}{2},|x_{2}-y|>\frac{d}{2}\right\},

and write the above integral in the following form.

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{|x_{1}-y|>\frac{d}{2}}\frac{u(y)\ln|x_{1}-y|}{|x_{1}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy-\int_{|x_{2}-y|>\frac{d}{2}}\frac{u(y)\ln|x_{2}-y|}{|x_{2}-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy\bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\int_{D_{1}}u(y)(\ln|x_{1}-y||x_{1}-y|^{-N-2s}-\ln|x_{2}-y||x_{2}-y|^{-N-2s})\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{D_{2}}u(y)\ln|x_{1}-y||x_{1}-y|^{-N-2s}\,dy-\int_{D_{3}}u(y)\ln|x_{2}-y||x_{2}-y|^{-N-2s}\,dy\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}\int_{D_{1}}\Big|\ln|x_{1}-y||x_{1}-y|^{-N-2s}-\ln|x_{2}-y||x_{2}-y|^{-N-2s}\Big|\,dy

\displaystyle+||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}\int_{D_{2}}|\ln|x_{1}-y|||x_{1}-y|^{-N-2s}\,dy+||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})}\int_{D_{3}}|\ln|x_{2}-y|||x_{2}-y|^{-N-2s}\,dy.

We consider the function
h:(d/2,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}

h(\rho)=(\ln\rho)\rho^{-m}\quad\text{for}\quad m\geq 1.

Using mean value theorem, for any
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in(d/2,\infty),
we have

\displaystyle\left|h(\rho_{1})-h(\rho_{2})\right|\leq\max_{\xi\in[\rho_{1},\rho_{2}]}\left|\xi^{-m-1}(1-m\ln\xi)\right||\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}|.

Since, we have

\displaystyle\int_{d/2}^{\infty}\rho^{-2(s+1)}|\ln\rho|\,d\rho<\infty,

in particular, taking
m=N+2s,

we estimate the first term as

\displaystyle\int_{D_{1}}\Big|\ln|x_{1}-y||x_{1}-y|^{-N-2s}-\ln|x_{2}-y||x_{2}-y|^{-N-2s}\Big|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|\Bigg[\int_{D_{1}}\left||x_{1}-y|^{-(N+2s)-1}\left(1-(N+2s)\ln|x_{1}-y|\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\left||x_{2}-y|^{-(N+2s)-1}\left(1-(N+2s)\ln|x_{2}-y|\right)\right|\,dy\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c(d/2,N,s)|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

To estimate the second and third integral, we first observe that for
y\in D_{2},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{2}<|x_{1}-y|\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|+\frac{d}{2}.

This implies
D_{2}\subset\left\{y:\frac{d}{2}<|x_{1}-y|\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|+\frac{d}{2}\right\}:=\widetilde{D}_{2}.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{D_{2}}|\ln|x_{1}-y|||x_{1}-y|^{-N-2s}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widetilde{D}_{2}}|\ln|x_{1}-y|||x_{1}-y|^{-N-2s}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{d/2}^{|x_{1}-x_{2}|+d/2}|\ln\rho|\rho^{-1-2s}\,d\rho

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\ln(2/d),\ln(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+d/2)\right\}(\frac{d}{2})^{-(1+2s)}|x_{1}-x_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\ln(2/d),\ln(\text{diam}\,\Omega+d/2)\right\}(\frac{d}{2})^{-(1+2s)}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha-\kappa-2s}.

Similarly, we note that

D_{3}\subset\left\{y:\frac{d}{2}<|x_{2}-y|\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|+\frac{d}{2}\right\}:=\widetilde{D}_{3}

and proceeding as before, we get the same bound.

\displaystyle\int_{D_{3}}|\ln|x_{2}-y|||x_{2}-y|^{-N-2s}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widetilde{D}_{3}}|\ln|x_{2}-y|||x_{2}-y|^{-N-2s}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\ln(2/d),\ln(\text{diam}\,\Omega+d/2)\right\}(\frac{d}{2})^{-(1+2s)}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha-\kappa-2s}.

Combining all the estimates above, for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\Omega,
we obtain

\displaystyle|(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{L}og}u(x_{1})-(-\Delta)^{s+\mathrm{L}og}u(x_{2})|\leq C(\kappa,d,s,N,||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{N})},[u]_{0,\alpha})|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha-\kappa-2s}.

This completes the proof.
