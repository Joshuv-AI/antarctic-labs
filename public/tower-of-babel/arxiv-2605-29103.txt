Cohomological support varieties for monomial ideals
Kara Fagerstrom, Julianne Faur, Benjamin Katz, Kesavan Mohana Sundaram, Stephen Stern, Ryan Watson

Abstract
. Let R R be a local or positively graded ring with a regular presentation R ≅ Q / I R\cong Q/I where I I is a monomial ideal generated by n n elements on a regular sequence. In [ 9 ] the authors classify the cohomological support varieties 𝒱 R ​ ( R ) {\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R) for n ⩽ 5 n\leqslant 5 . In this paper we extend their results to classify the varieties that can occur as 𝒱 R ​ ( R ) {\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R) for n = 6 n=6 . Moreover, we provide two families of rings, one realizing cohomological support varieties of unbounded codimension, the other realizing an unbounded number of components. Finally, we answer a question of [ 12 ] about the varieties that occur as 𝒱 R ​ ( R ) {\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R) where I I is given by the edge ideal of a cycle.

Introduction

Inspired by Quillen’s geometric approach to modular representation theory
[
19
]
, Avramov imported the theory of support varieties to commutative algebra to study local complete intersection rings
[
5
]
.
This has now expanded to include all noetherian local rings through the work of various authors
[
14
,
10
,
17
,
18
]
.
Most notably for this paper, Pollitz developed the theory of cohomological support varieties over Koszul complexes which generalizes the theory of Avramov.

Suppose that
R
is a local or positively graded ring. Then
R
is said to admit a minimal regular presentation, if one of the following holds:

•

\widehat{R}=Q/I
with
(Q,\mathfrak{m},k)
a regular local ring and
I\subset\mathfrak{m}^{2}
in the local case, or

•

R=Q/I
where
Q
is a positively graded polynomial algebra over a field
k
and
I
is an ideal generated by homogeneous forms of degree at least 2 in the graded case.

Assume that
R
has a minimal regular presentation given by
Q/I
, where
I
is minimally generated by
n
elements. Let
E
be the Koszul complex on a minimal generating set of
I
. The ring of cohomological operators
\mathcal{S}=k[\chi_{1},\dotsc,\chi_{n}]
is a polynomial ring over
k
where each
\chi_{i}
is a variable of cohomological degree
2
. For any finitely generated
R
-module
M
, the ring
\mathcal{S}
acts on
{\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(M,k)
, making
{\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(M,k)
into a graded
\mathcal{S}
-module. There are many different ways to define this action, but one is explained as follows. The ring
\mathcal{S}
is a graded
k
-subalgebra of
{\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(k,k)
, and the left action of
{\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(k,k)
on
{\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(M,k)
defines an action of
\mathcal{S}
on
{\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(M,k)
(see
[
1
, Section 2]
). The specific details of how
\mathcal{S}
acts on this
{\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(M,N)
are not needed for this paper, so we refer the interested reader to
[
17
, 3.2.6]
. Due to our conditions on
Q
, it turns out that
{\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(M,k)
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{S}
module
[
17
, 3.2.5]
.

The cohomological support variety of a finitely generated
R
module
M
, is defined to be

{\mathcal{V}}_{R}(M):={\operatorname{Supp}}_{\mathcal{S}}\left({\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(M,k)\right).

As
{\operatorname{Ext}}_{E}(M,k)
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{S}
module, we can and will view
{\mathcal{V}}_{R}(M)
as a conical affine variety (i.e. an affine cone), living in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
which we identify with
\mathfrak{m}{\operatorname{Spec}}(\mathcal{S})
.
Geometric properties of these varieties encode homological information of both the ring and the module. For example, they see some of the structure of the lattice of thick subcategories in the derived category of
R

[
20
,
17
]
.

When the ring is a complete intersection, the theory of support varieties is generally well-understood and has been studied extensively
[
5
,
2
,
6
,
4
,
11
]
. However, moving away from the complete intersection case, their behavior becomes much more mysterious. In
[
18
]
, the author asks the following question known as the
realization problem
for cohomological support varieties:

Question 1
.

Given a noetherian local ring
R
, what conical affine varieties in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
can be realized as the support variety of some finitely generated
R
-module?

This problem is solved when the ring is a complete intersection
[
6
,
4
]
, with the answer being that every conical affine variety is realizable. However, when the ring is not a complete intersection, much less is known. There are bounds on the dimensions of the varieties that can occur
[
8
,
9
]
, and certain varieties can be guaranteed to be realizable over certain rings in
[
18
,
7
,
22
,
12
]
. Beyond these results, little is known in general. In fact, it is unknown what varieties can arise as
{\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R)
. Some results in this direction include the following. When
R
is a complete intersection,
{\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R)=\{0\}

[
5
]
, and when
R
is a Golod ring of codepth at least two,
{\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R)
is all of
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}

[
8
]
. Moreover, the possible support varieties arising from
{\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R)
when
R
has small codepth is contained in
[
18
]
. In
[
9
]
the authors classify all varieties that can be realized as
{\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R)
when
R
is defined by a monomial ideal generated by five elements on a regular sequence of
Q
. These are exactly linear subspaces with dimension not 1 and the union of two hyperplanes. In
[
12
]
, the author computes the support varieties of equigenerated monomial ideals with six generators on a regular sequence of
Q
when the residue field is
\mathbb{Q}
. In this paper, we extend the results of
[
9
]
and
[
12
]
to all rings defined by six monomial relations with no restrictions on the residue field.

Theorem A
.

Let
R
be a local or positively graded ring with minimal regular presentation given by
Q/I
where
I
is a monomial ideal generated by six elements on a regular sequence of
Q
. Then up to a reordering of the monomial generators of
I
, the varieties realized as
{\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R)
are listed below:

•

a coordinate subspace of
\mathbb{A}^{6}_{k}
with dimension not equal to
1

•

V(\chi_{1},\;\chi_{4}\chi_{6})

•

V(\chi_{4}\chi_{6})

•

V(\chi_{4}\chi_{6},\;\chi_{5}\chi_{6})

•

V(\chi_{2}\chi_{4}\chi_{6})

•

V(\chi_{1}\chi_{3}\chi_{5}+\chi_{2}\chi_{4}\chi_{6})
.

When dealing with rings defined by monomial ideals, one has to go all the way to five generators to get a ring whose support variety is not a linear subspace
[
9
]
. The only such variety is given by
V(\chi_{1}\chi_{5})
which is a union of hyperplanes which can be realized by
R=k\llbracket x,y,z,w\rrbracket/I
where

. Our result shows that one need only go up to six generators to attain a non-linear hypersurface, namely

.
Expanding beyond this variety, we obtain the following theorem which answers a question of
[
12
]
, about the support variety of
R
where

is defined by the edge ideal of a cycle.

Theorem B
.

Let
R=k[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]/(x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},\dotsc,x_{n}x_{1})
, the ring defined by the edge ideal of an
n
-cycle. Then

{\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R)=\begin{cases}V(\chi_{1}\chi_{3}\ldots\chi_{n-1}+\chi_{2}\chi_{4}\ldots\chi_{n})&\text{if}\ n\equiv 2\pmod{4}\\
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}&otherwise.\end{cases}

Note that specializing to the case

gives the non-linear hypersurface mentioned above.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
1
we go over the necessary background information for computing support varieties over rings defined by monomial ideals. The general method of computing support varieties in this paper is taken from

]

. In Section

n

1.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we assume that
R
is either a local ring or a positively graded ring with residue field
k
, admitting a minimal regular presentation
Q/I
. Moreover, we assume the ideal
I
is minimally generated by the ordered list of monomials
\bm{f}=f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
on a regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
of
Q
. That is, each
f_{i}
is a product of powers of the
x_{j}
.
Our object of interest is the support variety
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}:={\mathcal{V}}_{R}(R)
.
In this setting,
[
9
]
furnishes a combinatorial construction of
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}
in terms of two graphs that are determined by
\bm{f}
, the GCD graph
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
and the Taylor graph
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}
.
Moreover, for a fixed number of generators, only finitely many
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
and
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}
can be realized, hence only finitely many support varieties can occur.

Definition 1.1
.

The
GCD graph
of
\bm{f}
is the simple graph
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
with vertex set
[n]=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
such that there is an edge
\{f_{i},f_{j}\}
if and only if
\gcd(f_{i},f_{j})
is not a unit. For a set of vertices
\sigma\subseteq[n]
we denote the
neighborhood
of
\sigma
as
N_{\bm{f}}(\sigma)=\left\{{f_{i}}\;:\;{\{f_{i},f_{j}\}\in\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}},\;j\in\sigma}\right\}.

Example 1.2
.

Consider two monomial ideals generated by

\bm{f}_{1}=(bce,bd,cd,ae)\ \text{and}\ \bm{f}_{2}=(de,ae,be,cd).

In both cases,
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
is a triangle on
\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}
along with the edge
\{f_{1},f_{4}\}
.
Hence,
N_{\bm{f}}(f_{1})=\{f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\}
.
This also illustrates that a fixed
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
can have multiple monomial ideals attaining it.

A priori, for a given
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
there are infinitely many monomial ideals to consider; our first step is to reduce to considering a finite number of ideals. The first part of this reduction is given by
[
9
, Remark 6.18]
, which argues that
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
and
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}
(and hence
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}
) are preserved by polarization. Thus we always assume that
\bm{f}
is a list of square-free monomials. Notably
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}
depends only on
I
and hence is not sensitive to the choice of the
Q
-regular sequence over which our monomials are defined. Thus, the second step of our reduction is to show that, up to a choice of regular sequence, there are only finitely many square-free monomial ideals with
n
generators.
Moving forward, we adopt the convention that

\bm{f}
is an ordered list of
n
square-free monomials, none of which divide another.
Hence
[n]:=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
is the set of minimal generators of a square-free monomial ideal.

Definition 1.3
.

Given a fixed list of square-free monomials
\bm{f}
, for a regular element
x
, the
type

\tau(x)
is the subset of minimal generators that
x
divides. That is, we identify
\tau(x)=\left\{{f_{i}}\;:\;{x\mid f_{i}}\right\}\subseteq[n]
.

The image of this function appears as the construction of certain hypergraphs in
[
16
,
15
]
where it is used to compute the arithmetic rank and regularity of square-free monomial ideals.
Towards the second step of our reduction, we will consider ideals for which
\tau
is injective.
Suppose for some
i\neq j
that
\tau(x_{i})=\tau(x_{j})
, then
\bm{f}
can be realized on a regular sequence of length
r-1
by removing both
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
along with adding in their product
x_{i}x_{j}
. Repeating until
\tau
is injective, we have that
r\leq 2^{n}
as well as a more insightful naming of our regular sequence:
x_{\sigma}=\tau^{-1}(\sigma)
.
Note that, when appropriate, we will abbreviate a set
\{f_{i_{1}},\ldots,f_{i_{s}}\}\subseteq[n]
with the concatenation
i_{1}\cdots i_{s}
, for example
x_{123}
refers to
x_{\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}}
. Note further that
\bm{f}
may be determined by the image of
\tau
by setting
f_{i}=\prod_{i\in\sigma\subseteq im(\tau)}x_{\sigma}
.

Example 1.4
.

Consider the ideal generated by
\bm{f}=(abcd,abce,cde)
then
\tau(a)=\tau(b)=\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
,
\tau(c)=\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}
,
\tau(d)=\{f_{1},f_{3}\}
, and
\tau(e)=\{f_{2},f_{3}\}
. Then
\bm{f}
can be realized on the regular sequence
x_{12}=ab,\;x_{123}=c,\;x_{13}=d,\;x_{23}=e
. Hence,
\bm{f}=(x_{12}x_{123}x_{13},\;x_{12}x_{123}x_{23},\;x_{123}x_{13}x_{23})
.

The possible image sets of
\tau
are the elements of
2^{2^{[n]}\smallsetminus\emptyset}
, allowing us to reduce to a finite number of square-free monomial ideals for fixed
n
. Moving forward, we assume the regular sequence consists only of regular elements that appear in
\bm{f}
and that no distinct regular elements have the same type. Moreover, we assume that each regular element is is decorated as
x_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,x_{\sigma_{r}}
where
\sigma_{i}=\tau(x_{\sigma_{i}})
.
When our regular elements are labeled as such, we refer to them as
variables
.
We have lost no generality in these assumptions as any list of square-free monomials
\bm{f}
can be realized in this manner by adjusting the regular sequence appropriately.
Let us denote the set of variables used by
\bm{f}
to be
X_{\bm{f}}
which is in correspondence with
\im(\tau)
.
Note that
X_{\bm{f}}
and
\bm{f}
determine one another, hence the square-free monomial ideals we consider are in correspondence with elements of
2^{2^{[n]}\smallsetminus\emptyset}
.

Example 1.5
.

The two ideals of Example
1.4
are determined by the variables

X_{\bm{f}_{1}}=\{x_{4},x_{12},x_{13},x_{23},x_{14}\}\text{ and }X_{\bm{f}_{2}}=\{x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{14},x_{123}\},

so that

\bm{f}_{1}=(bce,bd,cd,ae)=(x_{12}x_{13}x_{14},\;x_{12}x_{23},x_{13}x_{23},\;x_{4}x_{14})

and

\bm{f}_{2}=(de,ae,be,cd)=(x_{14}x_{123},\;x_{2}x_{123},\;x_{3}x_{123},\;x_{4}x_{14}).

Definition 1.6
.

We call the variable
x_{\sigma}

present
if
x_{\sigma}\in X_{\bm{f}}
and
absent
if
x_{\sigma}\notin X_{\bm{f}}
.

Remark 1.7
.

We can express conditions like
f_{3}\mid f_{24}
(where
f_{24}=\lcm(f_{2},f_{4}
)) from
[
9
, Theorem 6.16]
in terms of which variables are present and absent. In the two graphs given in that theorem,
f_{3}\mid f_{24}
is equivalent to the absence of the variable
x_{3}
. For larger graphs, we will classify support varieties based on the absence of several variables.

Let us now introduce some combinatorial objects that will be helpful to stratify the square-free monomial ideals we will consider.

Definition 1.8
.

An
abstract simplicial complex
with vertex set
[n]
is a nonempty subset
K\subseteq 2^{[n]}
with the property that
\sigma\in K
and
\theta\subset\sigma
implies
\theta\in K
.
Elements of
K
are called
faces
and maximal faces are called
facets
.
The dimension of a nonempty face is one less than its cardinality.
A
simple graph
is a simplicial complex with facets of dimension at most 1, where the 0-dimensional faces are called vertices and the 1-dimensional faces are called edges.
The
1-skeleton
of
K
is the simple graph given by removing the faces of
K
with dimension more than 1.
The GCD graph
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
can be identified with 1-skeleton of the smallest simplicial complex containing the labels of the variables
X_{\bm{f}}
.

Definition 1.9
.

A
complete graph
is a simple graph with every possible edge.
If
G
is a simple graph and
W
is a subset of its vertices, then the
induced subgraph
of
G
on
W
is the graph with vertex set
W
and edges of
G
that use vertices in
W
.
We call an induced, complete subgraph a
clique
.

Remark 1.10
.

Note that every induced subgraph of a clique is also a clique. Hence, the set of all cliques of a simple graph forms a simplicial complex, which we call the
clique complex
. We denote the clique complex of
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
by
K_{\bm{f}}
.
Note that if
i,j\in\sigma
, then the presence of
x_{\sigma}
witnesses the edge
\{i,j\}
in
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
.
In particular,
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
is the 1-skeleton of
K_{\bm{f}}.

Moreover, we always have that the labels of variables in
X_{\bm{f}}
must be elements of
K_{\bm{f}}
.
It follows that the
\bm{f}
satisfying
\mathtt{G}=\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
arise from selecting certain subsets of
2^{K}
if
K
is the clique complex of
\mathtt{G}
.

We now prepare some lemmas that relate
X_{\bm{f}}
with the combinatorics of
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
, in particular we identify features of a GCD graph that force certain variables to be present.

Lemma 1.11
.

If
\{f_{i},f_{j}\}
is an edge in
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
that is not contained in a triangle, then
x_{ij}
is present. Moreover, if
f_{\ell}
is a vertex with degree 1 then
x_{\ell}
is present.

Proof.

Any edge of
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
must be witnessed by some
x_{\sigma}
where
\sigma
contains the edge. Any clique larger than an edge containing
\{f_{i},f_{j}\}
would contain a triangle with
\{f_{i},f_{j}\}
as an edge. Thus,
x_{ij}
is the only possible variable that can witness this edge in
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}.
If
f_{\ell}
has degree 1 with neighbor
f_{m}
, the only variables that can appear in
f_{\ell}
are
x_{\ell}
and
x_{\ell m}
. By the first part of this lemma, it must be that
x_{\ell m}
is present. However, it cannot be that
f_{\ell}|f_{m}
and only the presence of
x_{\ell}
can prevent this. Thus
f_{\ell}=x_{\ell}x_{\ell m}.

∎

Corollary 1.12
.

If
\mathtt{G}
is a triangle-free graph with
\ell
vertices of degree 1, then up to a choice of regular sequence there are exactly
2^{n-\ell}
monomial ideals
\bm{f}
realizing
\mathtt{G}=\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
.

Lemma 1.13
.

Suppose
N_{\bm{f}}(f_{\ell})=\{f_{i},f_{j}\}
, for distinct
f_{i},f_{j},f_{\ell}\in[n]
. Then at least one of the following holds:

•

x_{i\ell}
and
x_{j\ell}
are present;

•

x_{\ell}
and
x_{ij\ell}
are present.

Proof.

By symmetry, it suffices to show that if
x_{i\ell}
is not present, then
x_{\ell}
and
x_{ij\ell}
are present. By assumption, the only other variable that could witness the edge
\{f_{i},f_{\ell}\}
in
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
is
x_{ij\ell}
which thus must be present. On the other hand, the only other variable that ensures
f_{\ell}\nmid f_{j}
is
x_{\ell}
, which must then be present.
∎

Let us now consider the Taylor graph which is instrumental in our computation of support varieties.
This graph encodes a
2^{n}\times 2^{n}
matrix that we use to compute the stable homology of an eventually 2-periodic complex.
In turn this homology is used to compute the support variety of
R
.
We give a brief description of this process.

Recall that
R
has a regular presentation
(Q,\mathfrak{m},k)/I
where
I
is minimally generated by
n
monomials
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{n}
.
Let
E=\Kos^{Q}(f_{1},\dotsc,f_{n})
, and let
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}f_{i}
for some
a_{i}\in k
. Then we have that
(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n})\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}\iff{\operatorname{pdim}}_{Q/(f)}(R)=\infty

[
18
, 6.2.4]
.
One can resolve
R
over
Q/(f)
using the Taylor resolution of
R
over
Q
along with the dg
E
-module structure on the Taylor resolution that is dictated by its own differential graded algebra structure.
This construction can be found in
[
9
, Section 2]
, and is also explained in higher generality in
[
23
, Chapter 4]
. Let us call this resolution
G
. The complex
G
eventually becomes
2
-periodic and so the finiteness of the projective dimension of
R
over
Q/(f)
can be detected by the differential in the
2
-periodic part of
G
. The edges of the Taylor graph encode the
2
-periodic differentials of
G\otimes k
and so the Taylor graph can be used to detect exactly which points are in the support variety.

For
\sigma\subseteq[n]
set
f_{\sigma}=\lcm(\sigma)
and for
f_{i}\notin\sigma
set
\sign(\sigma,i)=(-1)^{p}
for
p=\lvert\left\{{f_{j}\in\sigma}\;:\;{j<i}\right\}\rvert
.

Definition 1.14
.

The
Taylor graph
of
\bm{f}
is the weighted directed graph
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}
defined as follows.

•

Vertices: one vertex
v_{\sigma}
for each subset
\sigma\subseteq[n]
.

•

Edges: for each
\sigma\subseteq[n]
and
f_{i}\in[n]\smallsetminus\sigma
:

–

if
f_{i}\;|\;f_{\sigma}
, there is a
differential edge

v_{\sigma\cup\{f_{i}\}}\xrightarrow{\mathtt{d}_{\sigma,i}}v_{\sigma}
with weight
\mathtt{d}_{\sigma,i}=\sign(\sigma,i)
,

–

if
f_{i}\notin N_{\bm{f}}(\sigma)
, there is a
homotopy edge

v_{\sigma}\xrightarrow{\mathtt{h}_{\sigma,i}}v_{\sigma\cup\{f_{i}\}}
with weight
\mathtt{h}_{\sigma,i}=\sign(\sigma,i)\cdot\chi_{i}.

Note that homotopy edges depend only on
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}
.
If the symbols
\mathtt{d}_{\sigma,i},\mathtt{h}_{\sigma,i}
correspond to edges that do not exist in
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}
we take their value/weight to be 0. When referring to an edge that is unknown to be a homotopy edge or differential edge, we will use the symbol
\mathtt{e}_{i}
to denote an edge that either adds or removes
f_{i}
.
Similarly, if
\sigma
is unknown or obvious we will use the symbols
\mathtt{d}_{i}
and
\mathtt{h}_{i}
accordingly.
We use
\mathtt{e}_{i}^{s}
to refer to the source vertex of
\mathtt{e}_{i}
, and we denote its target vertex by
\mathtt{e}_{i}^{t}
.

For any point
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
, we consider a linear transformation
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}(a):k^{2^{n}}\rightarrow k^{2^{n}}
obtained by picking the vertices of
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}
as a basis, substituting
\chi_{i}=a_{i}
, and sending

(1)

v_{\sigma}\mapsto\sum_{f_{i}\notin\sigma}\mathtt{h}_{\sigma,i}v_{\sigma\cup\{f_{i}\}}+\sum_{f_{i}\in\sigma}\mathtt{d}_{\sigma\smallsetminus\{f_{i}\},i}v_{\sigma\smallsetminus\{f_{i}\}}.

This choice of basis realizes
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}(a)
as a sparse,
2^{n}\times 2^{n}
matrix with entries in
k
. We choose the convention that the columns of
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}(a)

Proposition 1.17
.

If
\mathtt{d}_{\sigma,i}
is an edge of

If

Proposition 1.20
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let

Proposition 2.3
.

If
v

\chi_{i_{1}},\ldots,\chi_{i_{s}}

Take

and every coefficient appearing in

[

Lemma 3.2
.

In general, the edge ideal construction associates to any simple graph a square-free monomial ideal with degree 2 generators.
In particular–and in contrast to GCD graphs–the vertices of the simple graph correspond to variables of a polynomial ring and the generators of the edge ideal are the products of each of the variables corresponding to edges.
For

let
C_{n}
denote the
n
-cycle, the simple graph with
n
vertices and
n
edges given by a regular
n
-gon. These graphs have the remarkable property that they are the GCD graphs of their own edge ideals. In particular, ideals with GCD graph

forms a family of ideals which contains the edge ideal of
C_{n}
.

.

∎

Lemma 5.13
.

If
\bm{f}
generates the edge ideal of a
(4m+2)
-cycle, then each vertex of
G(m-1,\emptyset)
is a coordinate color class of
\mathtt{A}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
and moreover, for every edge of
\mathtt{A}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
there is an edge between the color classes of its vertices in
G(m-1,\emptyset)
.

Proof.

Since
M
is an
O
-determined,
E
-perfect matching, we have that every edge in
\mathtt{A}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
has a weight
\mathtt{e}_{i}^{-1}\mathtt{e}_{j}
where
i
is even and
j
is odd. The containment of
M_{b}
inside
H_{\ell}
or
D_{\ell}
is determined by the location of the first
\tt 00
or
\tt 11
in appearing in
b
.
It follows that any edge with weight
\mathtt{e}_{i}^{-1}\mathtt{e}_{j}
in
\mathtt{A}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
and source in
H_{\ell}
or
D_{\ell}
must have that
j\leq\ell+1
in order to change the type color.
Moreover, edges in
\mathtt{A}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
between coordinate colors
M_{b}
and
M_{b^{\prime}}
must have that
b
and
b^{\prime}
differ in exactly one entry.
It suffices to show that the edges of
G(m-1,\emptyset)
are exactly those satisfying the above criteria.

By construction it is clear that every edge of
G(m-1,\emptyset)
is between color classes differing by one coordinate.
It is also easy to see that edges of
G(m-1,\emptyset)
involve changing a coordinate at or before the first repetition.
The result follows by showing every vertex
M_{b}
with type color
H_{i}
or
D_{i}
has degree
i/2+1
.
We observe this is true for the initial left and right endpoints
M_{{\tt 1}\odot({\tt 01})^{\odot 2m}}
and
M_{{\tt 0}\odot({\tt 10})^{\odot 2m}}
as each recursive call adds two edges with the exception of the base case adding just one edge.
For other vertices, consider the first appearance (largest prefix length
p
) of a given vertex.
This vertex must have type color in
\{H_{4p+2},D_{4p+2},H_{4p+4},D_{4p+4}\}
and be an internal vertex (i.e. not a left/right endpoint) in the depiction of
G(p,s)
, whereas in all subsequent recursive calls, the vertex is a left or right endpoint.
The subsequent recursive calls result in adding
2p+1
edges.
Notably vertices with type color with subscript
4p+2
have 1 edge coming from the first appearance, and type color with subscript
4p+4
have 2 such edges.
In both cases, this gives the desired degrees.
∎

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},\ldots,x_{n}x_{1})
, the ring defined by the edge ideal of an n-cycle. Then

{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}=\begin{cases}V(\chi_{1}\chi_{3}\ldots\chi_{n-1}+\chi_{2}\chi_{4}\ldots\chi_{n})&\text{if}\ n\equiv{2\pmod{4}}\\
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}&otherwise.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Lemma
5.4
, it suffices to just consider the case when
n=4m+2
for
m\geq 1
.
We closely follow the same strategy outlined in Example
5.6
.
Our first goal is to classify the
M
-walks on the Taylor graph that act as the identity.
If
\mathtt{h}_{i}^{-1}
appears in such an
M
-walk, it must be that
\mathtt{d}_{i}^{-1}
also appears.
Note that this forces
\mathtt{h}_{1}
and
\mathtt{d}_{1}
to be traversed on the walk as the coordinate colors contained in
H_{i}
and
D_{i}
differ in the first coordinate.
In turn,
\mathtt{h}_{n}
and
\mathtt{d}_{n}
must also be traversed.
Notably both
H_{n}
and
D_{n}
consist of exactly one coordinate color each given by
M_{{\tt 0\odot(10)}^{\text{\tiny$\odot 2m$}}}
and
M_{{\tt 1\odot(01)}^{\text{\tiny$\odot 2m$}}}
respectively.
We have argued that any cycle of
\mathtt{A}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
must pass through these two color classes, hence we consider paths between them.

For any
G(p,s)
, we wish to show that each vertex of
\mathtt{A}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
with coordinate color
M_{r\odot{\tt 10}\odot s}
(the left endpoint) has exactly one vertex of color
M_{\tilde{r}\odot{\tt 01}\odot s}
(the right endpoint) reachable by a (unique) path contained in
G(p,s)
.
The argument is by induction on
p
, and the claim is obvious for
p=-1
.
Note that
r
and
\tilde{r}
do not contain
{\tt 00}
or
{\tt 11}
as substrings and hence every vertex drawn has type color
H_{i}
or
D_{i}
with
i\geq 4p+2.

Moreover, the type color of the endpoints
M_{r\odot{\tt 10}\odot s}
and
M_{\tilde{r}\odot{\tt 01}\odot s}
depends on
s
but the index of this type color is larger than
4p+4.

Hence, the type colors depicted in Definition
5.11
are correct.
It follows that if
f_{4p+2}
is not in a vertex in the left endpoint of
G(p,s)
then
G(p-1,{\tt 00}\odot s)
cannot be traversed.
Similarly,
if
f_{4p+2}
is in a vertex in the left endpoint of
G(p,s)
then
G(p-1,{\tt 10}\odot s)
cannot be traversed.
Furthermore, whether or not
f_{4p+2}
is in a vertex in
M_{r\odot{\tt 11}\odot s}^{D_{4p+4}}
excludes one of
G(p-1,{\tt 11}\odot s)
or
G(p-1,{\tt 01}\odot s)
from being traversed.
Moreover, if a vertex in the left endpoint does not contain
f_{4p+4}
, then no path contained in
G(p,s)
starting from this vertex can land at
M_{\tilde{r}\odot{\tt 00}\odot s}^{H_{4p+4}}
.
Hence, if there is an edge from a vertex in
\mathtt{A}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
with coordinate color given by the left endpoint to a vertex with coordinate color
M_{r\odot{\tt 11}\odot s}^{D_{4p+4}}
, it must be traversed.
This gives uniqueness of a path, should one exist.
We note that in all four cases of
f_{4p+2}
and
f_{4p+4}
being present or not in a vertex of the left endpoint, a path to a left endpoint of some
G(p-1,-)
exists.
Applying the inductive hypothesis yields our desired path.
Note that this
M
-walk results in taking the complement of the prefix.

Applying this to
G(m-1,\emptyset)
gives that every vertex of
D_{n}
can be reached by a (unique) path from a unique vertex in
H_{n}
and that these vertices are complements.
By symmetry, the same is true when swapping the roles of
H_{n}
and
D_{n}
.
Hence we have shown there are exactly
\lvert H_{n}\rvert=2^{2m}
cycles in
\mathtt{A}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
and they are all disjoint.
Further, each cycle traverses each even
\mathtt{d}_{i}^{-1}
and
\mathtt{h}_{i}^{-1}
along with each odd
\mathtt{d}_{j}
and
\mathtt{h}_{j}
exactly once.
The argument that each cycle used in a cycle decomposition multiplies the product of the diagonal of
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}^{M}
by
{\chi^{O}}/{\chi^{E}}
is the exact same as in Example
5.6
.
It follows that
\chi^{E}(\chi^{O}+\chi^{E})\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\bm{f}}
.
Hence by symmetry
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}\subseteq V(\chi^{O}+\chi^{E})
.

For the other containment, we note that the
M
-walk

({\tt 0011})^{\odot m}\odot{\tt 00}=\mathtt{h}_{n}^{-1}\mathtt{d}_{n-1}\cdots\mathtt{d}_{4}^{-1}\mathtt{h}_{3}\mathtt{h}_{2}^{-1}\mathtt{d}_{1}\mathtt{d}_{n}^{-1}\mathtt{h}_{n-1}\cdots\mathtt{h}_{4}^{-1}\mathtt{d}_{3}\mathtt{d}_{2}^{-1}\mathtt{h}_{1}\left(({\tt 0011})^{\odot m}\odot{\tt 00}\right)

traverses every edge of a connected component of the Taylor graph. When
a\in V(\chi^{O}+\chi^{E})
the corresponding block of
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}(a)
admits a cycle that is not a boundary.
Consequently, we conclude that
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}=V(\chi^{O}+~\chi^{E}).

∎

Corollary 5.15
.

If
\mathtt{G}_{\bm{f}}=C_{n}
and
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}\neq\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}
, then
n=4m+2
(for some
m
) and, up to relabeling,
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}
is either
V(\chi^{O}+\chi^{E})
or
V(\chi^{E})
.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.5
and Theorem
5.14
we need only consider the case
n=4m+2
and at least one singleton variable is present.
Recall that by Proposition
2.15
we have that
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}
is a subgraph of the Taylor graph for the edge ideal of
C_{n}
.
We first consider when
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are present with different parity.
Note that this removes differential edges of different parity in the connected component of
\mathtt{T}_{\bm{f}}
containing
({\tt 0011})^{\odot m}\odot{\tt 00}
.
This results in the existence of at least one walk satisfying the hypothesis of Lemma
5.3
, hence
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}=\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}
. Thus, we may assume that all
x_{i}
present have the same parity, and by relabeling, we may further assume that they are all odd.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{1}
is present, so no
\mathtt{d}_{1}
exists.
Thus there are no cycles in the auxiliary graph.
We remark that the
E
-perfect matching
M
remains and is now triangular, hence by Proposition
2.12
,
V_{\bm{f}}\subseteq V(\chi^{E})
.
For the other containment, we have that for every
0\leq s\leq m
, the vertex
\tt(0110)^{\odot s}\odot 01\odot(0011)^{\odot m-s}
is a homotopy sink for
\chi_{4s+2}
and that
\tt(1100)^{\odot s}\odot 01\odot(1001)^{\odot m-s}
is a homotopy source for
\chi_{4s}
.
Thus every even indexed
V(\chi_{i})
is contained in
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}
and hence we have
{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{f}}=V(\chi^{E})
.
∎
