Moment bounds and exclusion processes on random Delaunay triangulations with conductances
Alessandra Faggionato, Cristina Tagliaferri

Abstract
. We consider the Voronoi tessellation associated to a stationary simple point process on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with finite and positive intensity. We introduce the Delaunay triangulation as its dual graph, i.e. the graph with vertex set given by the point process and with edges between vertices whose Voronoi cells share a ( d − 1 ) (d-1) –dimensional face. We also attach to each edge a random weight, called conductance. We provide sufficient conditions ensuring the integrability w.r.t. the Palm distribution of several quantities as weighted degrees and associated moments. These integrability properties are crucial in applications, as they allow to apply existing results on random walks, resistor networks and the symmetric simple exclusion processes with random conductances (cf. [ 1 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 ] ). For the latter, while the moment bounds ensure its well definiteness and several properties, the same does not hold when the jump rates are not symmetric, i.e. for a generic simple exclusion process. In this case, by using a criterion from [ 13 ] , we recover construction and properties of the simple exclusion process whenever the simple point process has finite range of dependence and the conductances are uniformly upper bounded. This last result relies on a suitable analysis of Bernoulli bond percolation on the Delaunay triangulation inspired by [ 2 , 3 ] . All the above results remain valid if the simple point process is stationary with respect to integer translations. Keywords : Voronoi tessellation, Delaunay triangulation, conductance field, Palm distribution, moment bounds, bond percolation on Delaunay triangulation. AMS 2010 Subject Classification : 60G55, 60K37, 35B27.

1.
Introduction

Random structures generated by point processes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
provide a natural and versatile framework for modeling disordered media and spatially inhomogeneous systems. Among these, the Delaunay triangulation associated with a simple point process (i.e. a locally finite configuration of points) plays a particularly important role, as it encodes the geometric adjacency relations induced by the corresponding Voronoi tessellation. When random weights (or
conductances
) are assigned to the edges of the Delaunay triangulation, one obtains a weighted random graph that serves as a fundamental model for studying stochastic processes in random environments.

The aim of this paper is to investigate quantitative properties of such weighted Delaunay graphs, with a particular focus on moment bounds and integrability conditions for key functionals of the system. These functionals include, for instance, weighted degrees and sums of conductances around a typical point, as well as higher-order quantities involving spatial displacements. Establishing suitable integrability properties for some of these objects w.r.t. the Palm distribution (as
\sum_{x:x\sim 0}|x|^{\zeta}
,
\lambda_{0}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
\big({\rm deg}_{{\rm DT}(\xi)}(0)\big)^{p}
,
\mu^{\omega}(0)^{p}
,
\nu^{\omega}(0)^{p}
introduced in Section
4
) is a crucial step in the analysis of several probabilistic models on the Delaunay triangulation, including random walks, electrical resistor networks, and interacting particle systems such as the symmetric simple exclusion process, all among random conductances (cf.
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
).

A central difficulty in this setting stems from the interplay between geometric randomness and probabilistic dependence. The structure of the Delaunay triangulation is highly sensitive to the configuration of points, and even local modifications of the point process may induce long-range changes in the graph. This makes it challenging to control quantities such as the degree of a vertex or the spatial range of its neighbors. To overcome this difficulties, inspired by
[
23
,
26
]
we develop an estimate procedure based on the so-called
fundamental region
. For previous moment bounds we refer to
[
23
, Section 11]
(where the assumptions are much stronger than ours) and
[
26
]
for the Poisson point process.

Our analysis is carried out in the general setting of simple point processes that are stationary and ergodic, with finite and positive intensity. We consider a broad class of models, ranging from processes with finite range of dependence to more general situations where correlations may decay slowly or are not explicitly controlled. In addition, we treat point processes exhibiting positive association, which allows us to exploit monotonicity properties in the derivation of moment bounds. To give a flavor of applications, in Section
7
we discuss Poisson point processes, determinantal point processes, and Gibbsian point processes arising in statistical mechanics.

In addition to moment estimates, we also investigate Bernoulli bond percolation on the
Delaunay triangulation. In particular, we provide conditions under which the resulting random graph has only finite connected components, a property that is relevant for the graphical construction of exclusion processes with possibly non-symmetric jump rates and for the derivation of some fundamental properties (cf.
[
13
]
). This analysis is restricted to the case of bounded conductances and simple point processes with finite-range dependence.
To perform this analysis we use the “
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
–process" approach developed in
[
2
,
3
]
for Poisson point processes and we extend it to the case of finite range of dependence.

2.3.
Voronoi tessellations and Delaunay triangulations

Given
\xi\in\mathcal{N}
and
x\in\xi
, the Voronoi cell with nucleus
x
is given by

{\rm Vor}(x|\xi)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,:\,|y-x|\leq|y-z|\;\forall z\in\xi\}\,.

The Voronoi tessellation associated with
\xi
is the collection of the Voronoi cells
{\rm Vor}(x|\xi)
,
x\in\xi
(see Figure
1
).
Since it is a countable intersection of closed half-spaces, each Voronoi cell is closed and convex.
Moreover, if
x\not=y
in
\xi
, then the cells
{\rm Vor}(x|\xi)
and
{\rm Vor}(y|\xi)
either share a
(d-1)
-dimensional face or they do not share any
(d-1)
–dimensional region.

Definition 2.1

(Delaunay triangulation
{\rm DT}(\xi)
)
.

Given
\xi\in\mathcal{N}
we define the
Delaunay triangulation
associated to
\xi
as
the graph
{\rm DT}(\xi)
with vertices the points of
\xi
and edges given by the pairs
\{x,y\}
with
x\not=y
in
\xi
such that
{\rm Vor}(x|\xi)
and
{\rm Vor}(y|\xi)
share a
(d-1)
–dimensional face (see Figure
1
).
We write
\mathcal{E}_{\rm DT}(\xi)
for the edges of
{\rm DT}(\xi)
.
Given
x,y\in\xi
, we write
x\sim y
(understanding the dependence on
\xi
) whenever
\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{E}_{\rm DT}(\xi)
.

Figure 1.
Voronoi tessellation and Delaunay triangulation associated to the set
\xi
, whose points are given by the black dots (
d=2
).

We stress that in computational geometry the notion of Delaunay triangulation is slightly different and can refer to a complex, whose 0-skeleton and 1-skeleton correspond to the above
{\rm DT}(\xi)
. See e.g.
[
17
, Section 10.3]
. For us the Delaunay triangulation is just the graph dual to the Voronoi tessellation. We also point out that, although
in Figure
1
the edges of the Delaunay triangulation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
bound triangles, this is not always true for
d=2
(take for example
\xi={\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
). In general,
the
Delaunay triangulation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is the
1
–skeleton of a tessellation made by
d
-simplices when
points of
\xi
are in
general quadratic position
(cf.
[
17
, Chapter 10]
and
[
21
]
for definitions and results).
We refer to
[
25
]
for sufficient conditions on simple point processes ensuring that a.s. points of
\xi
are in general quadratic position (an example is given by the homogeneous Poisson point process).

We recall (cf.
[
18
, Definition 1.5]
) that a

polyhedron
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is defined as the intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces, while
a
convex polytope
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is defined as the convex hull of finitely many points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. It is known that bounded polyhedra coincide with convex polytopes (cf.
[
18
, Theorems 1.20 and 1.22]
). As shown below, for our simple point processes a.s. the Voronoi cells will be convex polytopes. Therefore it is natural to introduce the following subset
\mathcal{N}_{\rm pol}\subset\mathcal{N}
:

Definition 2.2

(Set
\mathcal{N}_{\rm pol}
)
.

We denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\rm pol}
the family of
\xi\in\mathcal{N}
such that the Voronoi cell

is a convex polytope (equivalently, a bounded polyhedron) for all

.

One can prove that
\mathcal{N}_{\rm pol}
belongs to
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N})
. Note that, according to the above definition,
\emptyset\in\mathcal{N}_{\rm pol}
.
In Appendix
A.1
we prove the following criterion ensuring that

, where the orthant

is defined as follows:

Q_{\sigma}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,:\,\sigma_{i}x_{i}>0\text{ for }1\leq i\leq d\}\,,\qquad\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{d}\,.

Lemma 2.3
.

If

satisfies

2.4.
Delaunay triangulation in a random environment with a conductance field

We consider a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})

{\rm E}[\cdot]

\mathcal{P}

We assume that the additive group

(endowed with the Euclidean metric) acts on

(\theta_{x})_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}

(P1)

\theta_{0}=\mathds{1}

(P2)

\theta_{x}\circ\theta_{y}=\theta_{x+y}

(P3)

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\Omega\ni(x,\omega)\mapsto\theta_{x}\omega\in\Omega

Definition 2.4

(Simple point process
\omega\mapsto\hat{\omega}

We fix a simple point process

.
\mathcal{P}
is called

\mathcal{P}(A)\in\{0,1\}

. Strictly speaking, stationarity, translation invariance and ergodicity are w.r.t. the action
(\theta_{x})_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
on
\Omega
(but the action will be understood in what follows).
The same definitions hold when replacing
\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P},\theta_{x}
by
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}),{\mathbb{P}},\tau_{x}
respectively (recall (
2
)).
If
{\mathbb{P}}
is stationary, then the

of the SPP (or of
{\mathbb{P}}
) is denoted by

m:={\rm E}\bigl[\hat{\omega}([0,1]^{d})\bigr]={\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\xi([0,1]^{d})\bigr]\,.

(3)

We recall the so-called

{\mathbb{P}}\text{ stationary }\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;{\mathbb{P}}(\xi=\emptyset\text{ or }\sharp\xi=\infty)=1\,.

(4)

As proved in Appendix

, an important consequence of the stationarity of

is the following:

Lemma 2.5
.

If
{\mathbb{P}}
is stationary, then
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{N}_{\rm pol})=1
.

We now introduce the last ingredient of our model:

Definition 2.6

We assume to have a measurable map (called

As will be made clear from our applications,

For several results we will take the following assumptions (they will be stated explicitly):

Main Assumptions

The following holds:

(A1)

The probability measure
\mathcal{P}

(A2)

The simple point process

))

(A3)

For some translation invariant measurable set

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta_{x}\omega}=\tau_{x}\hat{\omega}\qquad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle c_{y-x,z-x}(\theta_{x}\omega)=c_{y,z}(\omega)\qquad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,\;\forall y\sim z\in{\rm DT}(\hat{\omega})\,.

(6)

Suppose for the moment just that

Palm distributions
\mathcal{P}_{0}
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}

In this section, we assume (A1), (A2) and (A3).

Definition 3.1

(Palm distribution
\mathcal{P}_{0}
)
.

The Palm distribution
\mathcal{P}_{0}

and the simple point process

\Omega
concentrated on
\Omega_{0}:=\{\omega\in\Omega\,:\,0\in\hat{\omega}\}

\mathcal{P}_{0}(A)=\frac{1}{m}\int_{\Omega}d\mathcal{P}(\omega)\int_{[0,1]^{d}}d\hat{\omega}(x)\mathds{1}_{A}(\theta_{x}\omega)\qquad\forall A\in\mathcal{F}\,.

(7)

The expectation associated to
\mathcal{P}_{0}

We recall that

is naturally identified with the atomic measure
\sum_{x\in\hat{\omega}}\delta_{x}

We refer the interested reader to

[

Since by Lemma

). We recall that
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
is the probability measure on
\mathcal{N}
concentrated on
\mathcal{N}_{0}:=\{\xi\in\mathcal{N}\,:\,0\in\xi\}
such that

{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(A)=\frac{1}{m}\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\int_{[0,1]^{d}}d\xi(x)\mathds{1}_{A}(\tau_{x}\xi)\qquad\forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N})\,.

(8)

Given
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N})
and setting
B:=\{\omega\in\Omega\,:\,\hat{\omega}\in A\}
, for all
\omega\in\Omega_{*}
we have
\mathds{1}_{A}(\tau_{x}\hat{\omega})=\mathds{1}_{B}(\theta_{x}\omega)
since
\widehat{\theta_{x}\omega}=\tau_{x}\hat{\omega}
by (

{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(A)=\mathcal{P}_{0}(\hat{\omega}\in A)\qquad\forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N})\,.

In what follows, we will use a generalization of (

[

): for each measurable function

it holds

\int_{\mathcal{N}_{0}}d{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}dxf(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{m}\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}d\xi(x)f(x,\tau_{x}\xi)\,.

(9)

For completeness, although not used below, we recall that a similar formula holds for
\mathcal{P}
(see

).

In this section, we make Assumptions (A1), (A2) and (A3).
We recall that the Palm distribution

(10)

A fundamental condition in order to apply the results in

for

As in
[

2
2

2

Although

or subgraphs.

\mu^{\omega}(0):=\lambda_{0}(\omega)=\sum_{x:x\sim 0}c_{0,x}(\omega)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\nu^{\omega}(0):=\sum_{x:x\sim 0}\frac{1}{c_{0,x}(\omega)}\,.

Several results in
[

require that
\mu^{\omega}(0)\in L^{p}(\mathcal{P}_{0})
and

When providing sufficient conditions for

: one in which the SPP has a finite range of dependence, and a generic case in which no information on the decay of correlations is available. We point out that the methods used in the proofs could be further optimized for situations exhibiting an intermediate form of correlation decay. In addition to the above extreme two cases, we also treat the case in which the SPP has positive association.
To state our results, given

4.2.
Integrability of
\sum_{x:x\sim 0}|x|^{\zeta}
,
\lambda_{0}
,
\lambda_{2}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{P}_{0}

To state our results, we first recall two definitions concerning SPPs.

Definition 4.2

(SPP with finite range of dependence)
.

A probability measure
{\rm P}
on
\mathcal{N}
has
finite range of dependence
if there exists
L>0

such that, for any Borel sets
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
having Euclidean distance at least
L
, the random variables
\xi\cap A
and
\xi\cap B
defined on the probability space
(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}),{\rm P})
are independent. In this case, we say that the range of dependence is smaller than
L
.

Definition 4.3

(SPP with negative/positive association)
.

A probability measure
{\rm P}
on
\mathcal{N}
has
positive association
if for any
n,k\geq 0
, any weakly increasing functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
g:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and any family of pairwise disjoint bounded Borel sets
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{n}
,
B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{k}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, it holds

{\rm Cov}\Big(\,f\big(\xi(A_{1}),\xi(A_{2}),\dots,\xi(A_{n})\big)\,,\,g\big(\xi(B_{1}),\xi(B_{2}),\dots,\xi(B_{k})\big)\,\Big)\geq 0\,,

(15)

whenever the two random variables appearing in the above covariance has finite second moment w.r.t.
{\rm P}
.
The probability measure
{\rm P}
is said to have
negative association
if the same holds with
\leq
instead of
\geq
in (
15
).

Remark 4.4
.

{\rm E}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\xi(A_{i}))\Big]\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\rm E}\Big[f_{i}(\xi(A_{i}))\Big]

whenever
{\rm P}
has negative association,
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
are pairwise disjoint bounded Borel sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
are bounded functions all weakly increasing or all weakly decreasing. Take for example
n=3
and
f_{i}
decreasing. Then
{\mathbb{E}}[f_{1}f_{2}f_{3}]={\mathbb{E}}[(-f_{1}f_{2})(-f_{3})]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[-f_{1}f_{2}]{\mathbb{E}}[-f_{3}]={\mathbb{E}}[f_{1}f_{2}]{\mathbb{E}}[f_{3}]
by (
15
), since
-f_{1}f_{2}
and
-f_{3}
are weakly increasing (a further iteration allows to conclude).

We now introduce a condition concerning the so–called

relevant for our results. To this aim,
recall the definition of
B_{r}(x)
,
\Lambda_{r}
and
\Lambda_{r}(x)
given in (

Definition 4.5

(Condition

Given

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\xi(\Lambda_{\ell})=0\right)\leq\kappa\,\ell^{-\alpha}\qquad\forall\ell\geq 1\,,

If

We generalize the above result in the next proposition. Given

We set

Suppose that

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a measurable function. Suppose that under

\nu_{|x-y|}

and such that

\mathcal{P}_{0}
–a.a.
\omega
it holds

Let
{\mathbb{P}}
be the law of the SPP. Without loss, we can take as space

the set
\{(\xi,\Theta)\,:\,\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{\rm pol},\;\Theta\in(0,+\infty)^{\mathcal{E}_{\rm DT}(\xi)}\}

and

if

. Finally we

\mathcal{P}

\xi

4.3.
Integrability of

\big({\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)\big)^{p}
,

\mu^{\omega}(0)^{p}
and
\nu^{\omega}(0)^{p}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{P}_{0}

Fixed
p\in[1,+\infty)
, below we will provide conditions ensuring that
{\rm E}_{0}\left[{\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)^{p}\right]<+\infty
. Let us now show how this moment bound is related to the integrability of
\mu^{\omega}(0)^{p}
and
\nu^{\omega}(0)^{p}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{P}_{0}
.

Let
p_{*}
be the exponent conjugate to
p
(i.e.
p^{-1}+p_{*}^{-1}=1
). By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\mu^{\omega}(0)=\sum_{x:x\sim 0}c_{0,x}(\omega)\leq\Big(\sum_{x:x\sim 0}c_{0,x}(\omega)^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big({\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{*}}}\,.

Hence we have (also by conditioning w.r.t.
\hat{\omega}
)

\begin{split}{\rm E}_{0}\big[\mu^{\omega}(0)^{p}\big]&\leq{\rm E}_{0}\Big[\Big(\sum_{x:x\sim 0}c_{0,x}(\omega)^{p}\Big){\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)^{\frac{p}{p_{*}}}\Big]\\
&\leq{\rm E}_{0}\Big[\Big(\sum_{x:x\sim 0}{\rm E}_{0}[c_{0,x}(\omega)^{p}|\hat{\omega}]\Big){\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)^{\frac{p}{p_{*}}}\Big]\,.\end{split}

(22)

Suppose now that for some constant
C<+\infty
it holds that, for
\mathcal{P}_{0}
–a.a.
\omega
,

\sup_{x:x\sim 0}{\rm E}_{0}[c_{0,x}(\omega)^{p}|\hat{\omega}]\leq C
. Then, using that
1+p/p_{*}=p
, we get

{\rm E}_{0}\left[\mu^{\omega}(0)^{p}\right]\leq C{\rm E}_{0}\left[{\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)^{p}\right]\,.

Similarly we get
that
\sup_{x:x\sim 0}{\rm E}_{0}\left[1/c_{0,x}(\omega)^{p}\,\big|\,\hat{\omega}\right]\leq C
implies that
{\rm E}_{0}\left[\nu^{\omega}(0)^{p}\right]\leq C{\rm E}_{0}\left[{\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)^{p}\right]
. Note that in this case it must be
c_{0,x}(\omega)>0
for all
x\sim 0
.

It remains to ensure that
{\rm E}_{0}\left[{\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)^{p}\right]={\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[{\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\xi)}(0)^{p}\right]<+\infty
. This task is carried out in Section
11
. Our final result is the following:

Theorem 4.13
.

Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
. In addition to our main assumptions (A1), (A2), (A3), we assume
that at least one of the following cases holds:

(C1)

\rho_{\gamma}<+\infty
for some
\gamma>p+1
and Condition
C(\alpha)
holds for some

\alpha>\frac{dp\gamma}{\gamma-1-p}
;

(C2)

\rho_{1+p}<+\infty
and
{\mathbb{P}}
has finite range of dependence;

(C3)

\rho_{1+p}<+\infty
,
{\mathbb{P}}
has positive association and Condition
C(\alpha)
holds for some
\alpha>dp
.

Then
{\rm E}_{0}\left[{\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)^{p}\right]<+\infty
and the following implications hold:

(a)

If
\mathcal{P}_{0}
–a.s.

\sup_{x:x\sim 0}{\rm E}_{0}\bigl[c_{0,x}(\omega)^{p}\,|\,\hat{\omega}\big]\leq C

for some
C\in[0,\infty)
, then
{\rm E}_{0}\left[\mu^{\omega}(0)^{p}\right]<+\infty
.

(b)

If
\mathcal{P}_{0}
–a.s.

\sup_{x:x\sim 0}{\rm E}_{0}\left[\frac{1}{c_{0,x}(\omega)^{p}}\Large{|}\hat{\omega}\right]\leq C

for some
C\in[0,\infty)
, then
{\rm E}_{0}\left[\nu^{\omega}(0)^{p}\right]<+\infty
.

Proof.

Due to the discussion before the theorem, it is enough to prove that
{\rm E}_{0}\left[{\rm deg}_{\rm DT(\hat{\omega})}(0)^{p}\right]<+\infty
. This bound is an immediate consequence of Proposition
11.1
and Corollary
11.2
.
∎

Note that a sufficient condition implying the validity of the the hypotheses in the above implications (a) and (b) is provided by Proposition
4.12
with
\varphi(t):=t^{p}
and
\varphi(t):=t^{-p}
, respectively.

7.
Some examples

There are several popular classes of simple point processes. We just give a flavor of applications, discussing in what follows void probabilities and boundedness of

in particular for determinantal and Gibbsian point processes.

Some examples of stationary ergodic point processes with finite range of dependence are the homogeneous Poisson point processes, the Matérn cluster processes and the Matérn hardcore processes discussed in

[

. Due to Proposition
4.6
for all these SPPs the void probability decays exponentially in the volume and in particular
{\mathbb{P}}(\{\emptyset\})=0
. For the homogeneous PPP it is known that

for all

7.1.
Determinantal point processes

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a locally trace–class self–adjoint operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},dx)
with
0\leq\mathcal{K}\leq\mathds{1}
. It is always possible
to associate with

a kernel

such that

for any

bounded. Then the law

of the determinantal SPP associated with

of pairwise disjoint bounded Borel sets, where

\rho_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}):={\rm det}\bigl(K(x_{i},x_{j})\bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\,.

In particular, the intensity is given by

. The determinantal SPP becomes stationary if

Since determinantal SPPs have negative association, we have that the void probability on a box

decays at least as

, as

(cf. Proposition

). As a consequence

.
Finally we point out that

for all

due to
[

. In particular also

is finite.

{\mathbb{P}}(\xi(\Lambda)=0)=\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\eta)\big(\Theta_{\Lambda}^{\eta})^{-1}\,.

(38)

We recall that the potential
V
is called of

if there exists

such that

for any finite point configuration
\eta
whose diameter is greater than
L
. When this property holds for some

, we say that the range is smaller than

. We point out that in this case
{\mathbb{P}}
does not necessarily have finite range of dependence in the sense of Definition
4.2
. Indeed, suppose that
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
are bounded Borel subsets with distance at least
L
. Then, by the DLR equation,

{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi_{A})g(\xi_{B})]=\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\eta)\int_{\mathcal{N}_{A\cup B}}d\mu_{A\cup B}^{\eta}(d\sigma)\,f(\sigma_{A})g(\sigma_{B})\,.

(39)

Since
\sigma(A\cup B)=\sigma(A)+\sigma(B)
and
H_{A\cup B}(\sigma|\eta)=H_{A}(\sigma_{A}|\eta)+H_{B}(\sigma_{B}|\eta)
(in the last identity we used the assumption that
V
has range smaller than
L
) we conclude that

d\mu_{A\cup B}^{\eta}(\sigma)
factorizes as

d\mu_{A\cup B}^{\eta}(\sigma)=d\mu_{A}^{\eta}(\sigma_{A})d\mu_{B}^{\eta}(\sigma_{B})
with
\sigma=\sigma_{A}\cup\sigma_{B}
. Hence for (
39
) we can just say that

{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi_{A})g(\xi_{B})]=\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\eta)\int_{\mathcal{N}_{A}}d\mu_{A}^{\eta}(\sigma)\int_{\mathcal{N}_{B}}d\mu_{B}^{\eta}(\sigma^{\prime})f(\sigma)g(\sigma^{\prime})\,.

(40)

For later use we point out that, reasoning as above we have that, given bounded sets
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{n}\in\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with reciprocal distance at least
L
, it holds

d\mu_{\Lambda}^{\eta}(\sigma)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}d\mu^{\eta}_{A_{k}}(\sigma_{A_{k}})\;\;\text{ where }\Lambda=\cup_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}\,,\;\;\sigma=\sigma_{A_{1}}\cup\sigma_{A_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup\sigma_{A_{n}}\,.

(41)

Proposition 7.2
.

If the Hamiltonian is associated to a finite range potential and the one particle potential is lower bounded by a constant, then
{\mathbb{P}}(\xi(\Lambda_{\ell})=0)\leq e^{-c\ell^{d}}
for some
c>0
and for
\ell
large enough. In particular, the void probability satisfies condition
C(\alpha)
for any
\alpha>0
.

Remark 7.3
.

If the potential is translation invariant, i.e.
V(A)=V(A+x)
for all
A\in\mathcal{N}_{f}
and all

, then trivially the one particle potential
V(\{a\})
is a constant independent from

.

Proof.

Suppose that
V(\eta)=0
if
{\rm diam}(\eta)>L
.
We consider in
\Lambda_{\ell}
a maximal family of boxes
\{B_{w}\,:\,w\in\mathcal{W}\}
with side length
4L
and with reciprocal distance at least
L
. Then, for
\ell
large enough,
|\mathcal{W}|\geq c\ell^{d}
for a suitable constant

Using the DLR equation with

and using the factorization formula
(

\begin{split}{\mathbb{P}}(\xi(\Lambda_{\ell})=0)&\leq{\mathbb{P}}(\xi(\Lambda)=0)=\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\eta)\mu^{\eta}_{\Lambda}(\sigma(\Lambda)=0)\\
&=\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\eta)\prod_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\mu^{\eta}_{B_{w}}(\sigma(B_{w})=0)\leq\kappa^{c\ell^{d}}\end{split}

(42)

where

\kappa:=\inf_{\zeta}\mu^{\zeta}_{\Delta}(\{\emptyset\})=\left(\sup_{\zeta}\Theta^{\zeta}_{\Delta}\right)^{-1}\qquad\Delta:=\Lambda_{2L}=[-2L,2L]\,.

We note that

Setting
\Delta^{\prime}:=\Lambda_{L/2}

\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{N}

We call

\int_{\mathcal{A}}d{\rm P}_{\Delta}(\sigma)e^{\mathcal{L}(\Delta)}z^{\sigma(\Delta)}e^{-\beta H_{\Delta}(\sigma|\zeta)}\geq ze^{\mathcal{L}(\Delta)-\beta C}{\rm P}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{A})=ze^{-\beta C}\mathcal{L}(\Delta^{\prime})\,.

Hence

If the potential is not of finite range, one can anyway give reasonable sufficient conditions leading to Condition
C(\alpha)
. For simplicity we treat the case of pair potentials:

Proposition 7.4
.

Suppose the Hamiltonian
satisfies (

(i)

and

Moreover in Case (i), for some

Proof.

Given
\ell>0
we want to estimate the void probability
{\mathbb{P}}(\xi(\Lambda_{\ell})=0)
. Below constants as

can change from line to line and do not depend on the relevant parameters as
\ell

We first take Item (i) where

.

Due to (
35

{\mathbb{E}}[e^{\xi(\Lambda)}]\leq e^{c\mathcal{L}(\Lambda)}

Since

{\mathbb{P}}(\cup_{r=\ell}^{\infty}E_{r})\leq\sum_{r=\ell}^{\infty}e^{-\mathcal{L}(A_{r})}\leq\sum_{r=\ell}^{\infty}e^{-C_{0}r^{d-1}}\leq C_{1}e^{-C_{0}\ell^{d-1}}\,.

Take now a maximal family of disjoint boxes

\begin{split}H_{\Lambda}(\sigma|\eta)&=\sum_{x\in\sigma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\sigma\cup\eta_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}\\

8.
Moments of the number of points in a box

In this section we just assume that
{\mathbb{P}}
is the law of a stationary simple point process on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with finite and positive intensity
m={\mathbb{E}}[\xi([0,1]^{d})]
.
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{0}
denote the Palm distribution associated to
{\mathbb{P}}
and the corresponding expectation, respectively. Our aim here is to analyze the finite moment condition
\rho_{\gamma}:={\mathbb{E}}[\xi([0,1]^{d})^{\gamma}]<+\infty
.

Lemma 8.1
.

\forall\gamma\geq 1
and
\forall L\in{\mathbb{N}}
it holds
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi([0,L]^{d})^{\gamma}]\leq L^{d\gamma}\rho_{\gamma}
.

Proof.

The claim is trivial for
L=0
. Let
L>0
. If
\gamma=1
, the claim follows from the stationarity of
{\mathbb{P}}
and indeed it holds
{\mathbb{E}}[\xi([0,L]^{d})]=L^{d}\rho_{1}
. Let us take
\gamma>1
. We use Hölder’s inequality with conjugate exponents
p:=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}
and
q:=\gamma
. Then, using also the
stationarity of
{\mathbb{P}}
, we get

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[\>\xi([0,L]^{d})^{\gamma}\>]&={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{z\in[0,L-1]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\xi(z+[0,1]^{d})\Big)^{\gamma}\>\Big]\\
\leq&{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{z\in[0,L-1]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}1^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\Big)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\cdot\gamma}\Big(\sum_{z\in[0,L-1]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\xi(z+[0,1]^{d})^{\gamma}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}\cdot\gamma}\Big]\\
=&L^{d(\gamma-1)}\sum_{z\in[0,L-1]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}{\mathbb{E}}[\>\xi(z+[0,1]^{d})^{\gamma}]=L^{d\gamma}{\mathbb{E}}[\>\xi([0,1]^{d})^{\gamma}]\,.\qed\end{split}

Lemma 8.2
.

For any
\gamma>0
,
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, it holds

{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\>\xi(\Lambda_{L}(z))^{\gamma}]\leq\frac{1}{m}(2L+2)^{d\gamma}\rho_{1+\gamma}\,.

Proof.

Campbell’s formula (
9
) with
f(x,\xi):=\mathds{1}_{[0,1]^{d}}(x)\xi(\Lambda_{L}(z))^{\gamma}
implies that

{\mathbb{E}}_{0}[\>\xi(\Lambda_{L}(z))^{\gamma}]=\frac{1}{m}\>\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}d\xi(x)f(x,\tau_{x}\xi)\\
=\frac{1}{m}\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\sum_{x\in\xi\cap[0,1]^{d}}\xi(x+\Lambda_{L}(z))^{\gamma}\leq\frac{1}{m}{\mathbb{E}}[\>\xi([0,1]^{d})\xi(\Lambda_{L+1}(z))^{\gamma}]\,.

(46)

We now apply Hölder’s inequality with conjugate exponents
p=(1+\gamma)/\gamma
and
q=1+\gamma
, the stationarity of
{\mathbb{P}}
and afterwards Lemma
8.1
. Since
q=\gamma p=1+\gamma
, we get

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[\>\xi([0,1]^{d})\xi(\Lambda_{L+1}(z))^{\gamma}]&\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\>\xi([0,1]^{d})^{q}]^{1/q}{\mathbb{E}}[\>\xi(\Lambda_{L+1})^{\gamma p}]^{1/p}\\
&\leq\rho_{q}^{1/q}(2L+2)^{d\gamma}\rho_{\gamma p}^{1/p}=\rho_{1+\gamma}(2L+2)^{d\gamma}\,.\end{split}

(47)

By combining (
46
) and (
47
) we get the desired estimate.
∎

The following proposition clarifies the relation between the moments of the number of points in a given box w.r.t.
{\mathbb{P}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{0}
:

Lemma 8.3
.

Given
\gamma>0
the following facts are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[\xi\left(\Lambda_{L}(z)\right)^{\gamma}\right]<\infty
for any
L>0
and
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
;

(ii)

\rho_{1+\gamma}<\infty
.

Proof.

(ii) implies (i) by Lemma
8.2
. Let us show that (i) implies (ii).
We
apply Campbell’s formula (
9
) with
f(x,\xi):=\mathds{1}_{\Lambda_{1/2}}(x)\xi\left(\Lambda_{1}\right)^{\gamma}
, getting

m{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\left[\xi\left(\Lambda_{1}\right)^{\gamma}\right]=\int_{\mathcal{N}}d{\mathbb{P}}(\xi)\sum_{x\in\xi\cap\Lambda_{1/2}}\xi\left(x+\Lambda_{1}\right)^{\gamma}\geq{\mathbb{E}}[\xi(\Lambda_{1/2})^{1+\gamma}]=\rho_{1+\gamma}\,.

As (i) implies that the above l.h.s. is bounded, we get (ii).
∎

Lemma 9.1
.

Let

and

. Then the points in

adjacent to

Lemma 9.2
.

If

Without loss of generality we take

. We set

-(right)).
