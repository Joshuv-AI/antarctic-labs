Research on factors influencing teaching staff’s continued use intention of AIGC tools: based on structural equation modeling and artificial neural networks
Authors: Wenbiao Xiao, Dongmei Xia, Ju Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrevious studies have applied models such as UTAUT to investigate users’ intentions to adopt new technological tools. However, these studies have primarily focused on initial usage intentions, paying limited attention to continuance intention. To address this gap and provide a more comprehensive understanding of HE teaching staff’s continuance intention toward AIGC tools, this study innovatively integrates ECM and UTAUT to develop a novel framework for examining this continuance intention. Using a combined approach of Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Model (PLS-SEM) and Artificial Neural Network (ANN) models, data from 310 HE teaching staff in China were analyzed. The results show that: (1) perceived usefulness, satisfaction, effort expectancy, and performance expectancy have significant direct effects on teaching staff’s continued intention to use; (2) social influence and perceived risk do not have significant effects on continued intention; (3) perceived usefulness and expectation confirmation have significant positive effects on continued intention through satisfaction: The ANN analysis further indicates that the relative importance of the predictors of continuance intention, in descending order, is as follows: performance expectancy, satisfaction, expectation confirmation, effort expectancy, and perceived usefulness; (4) the ANN analysis further indicates that the relative importance of the predictors of continuance intention, in descending order, is as follows: performance expectancy, satisfaction, expectation confirmation, effort expectancy, and perceived usefulness.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58611-x.Keywords: HE teaching staff, Artificial Intelligence Generated Content, Continued usage intention, ECM model, UTAUT modelSubject terms: Information systems and information technology; Mathematics and computing; Psychology; Psychology; Science, technology and society

Introduction

In recent years, with the rapid development of artificial intelligence technology, Artificial Intelligence Generated Content (AIGC) has been widely used in people’s daily work and life. AIGC has played a significant role in reshaping the educational ecosystem and driving innovation in educational methods1,80. As the main facilitators of teaching activities, teaching staff’s continued willingness to use AIGC tools directly affects the empowering impact of these tools in educational activities2. However, despite the increasing integration of AIGC into higher education, limited attention has been paid to understanding the mechanisms and relative importance of factors influencing teaching staff’s continued intention to use these tools.

Notably, teaching staff’s willingness to adopt AIGC tools differs significantly across various stages of adoption. Although many teachers demonstrate strong enthusiasm during the initial adoption phase, their intention to continue using these tools tends to diminish over time76. Existing research mostly focuses on factors that influence users’ initial intention to use AIGC tools, such as perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use3, with insufficient attention paid to “continued usage intention.” This gap is especially pronounced when it comes to HE teaching staff, for whom studies on the influencing mechanisms of sustained usage remain relatively scarce.

Previous research on users’ intentions to use technologies or tools has primarily been based on theoretical frameworks such as the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), and Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)4,56,. However, these frameworks mainly address users’ initial usage intentions and are relatively weak in explaining continued usage intentions. To better understand continued usage intention, this study combines the Expectation Confirmation Model of Information Systems Continuance (ECM) with the UTAUT model to further advance a comprehensive theoretical framework for examining the mechanisms underlying the continued use of AIGC tools in education. This approach will help expand existing technology acceptance frameworks and provide practical strategies for school administrators and technology developers to enhance HE teaching staff’s sustained usage intention for AIGC tools.

this study aims to address the following issues: (1) Based on the UTAUT and ECM models, identify the main factors that may influence HE teaching staff’s sustained usage intention of AIGC tools; (2) On this basis, propose a model of factors influencing HE teaching staff’s continuance intention toward AIGC tools according to previous studies; (3) Using empirical data, verify and refine the model of factors influencing HE teaching staff’s sustained usage intention of AIGC tools to clarify the mechanisms among the main influencing factors;(4) Based on artificial neural network results, rank the main influencing factors according to their impact.

This study follows a systematic research procedure consisting of six stages (as shown in Fig. 1). First, the literature review focuses on three major themes: The attitudes of HE teaching staff Towards AIGC Tools, research on the user’s intention to use new technology tools based on the ECM Model, and research on users’ willingness to adopt new technological tools based on the UTAUT model. Second, an integrated ECM–UTAUT framework was developed to construct the theoretical model of this study, and the corresponding research hypotheses were proposed. Third, questionnaire survey data were collected from HE teaching staff in China. Fourth, SmartPLS 4 was employed to conduct PLS-SEM analysis, assessing the measurement model and evaluating the structural model to test the proposed hypotheses. Fifth, based on the PLS-SEM results, an artificial neural network (ANN) model was developed using the ANN module in SPSS 26.0 for predictive validation and ranking the relative importance of the predictors. Finally, the empirical findings were discussed in depth, and theoretical as well as practical implications were drawn.

Literature review and theoretical hypothesis

Literature review

The attitudes of HE teaching staff towards AIGC tools

In the field of higher education, the empowering potential of AIGC tools has sparked widespread academic discussion7. Their impact extends beyond merely assisting with individual tasks,they have the potential to fundamentally reshape teaching relationships, strategies, and even alter the role of HE teaching staff82. Compared to primary and secondary school teachers, HE teaching staff exhibit a higher tendency to use AIGC tools, applying them extensively in both teaching and research practices3.

Research indicates that many HE teaching staff hold a positive view of AIGC tools. For example, a survey conducted by Xia and Beech8found that 63.6% of HE teaching staff expressed satisfaction with AI-generated content, while 87% were willing to recommend AIGC tools to their peers. Furthermore, Kung et al9. emphasized that these tools can significantly enhance teaching and research efficiency in higher education. However, other studies present contrasting conclusions. Iqbal et al10. conducted interviews and found that many HE teaching staff harbor negative attitudes toward AIGC tools, mainly concerned with potential issues of cheating, plagiarism, and academic integrity. Similarly, Shata11, using a mixed-methods approach, argued that the resistance to AIGC tools among HE teaching staff is not only due to doubts about the practicality and ease of use of the technology but also closely linked to considerations of identity, values, and ethics.

A growing body of research reveals that HE teaching staff hold a complex and ambivalent attitude toward AIGC tools. While they acknowledge the potential of these tools to improve classroom teaching efficiency, facilitate personalized teaching, and enhance research, they also express concerns about the potential negative impacts12,79. Chinese HE teaching staff often face numerous challenges when using generative artificial intelligence tools, including curriculum adaptation, assessment transformation, student engagement with AIGC tools, ethical concerns, professional development, and shifts in teaching paradigms83–86.

As a key participant in the development of artificial intelligence, China is actively promoting the deep integration of AI technologies into the education sector13. In Chinese higher education, university faculty members are tasked with both teaching and research responsibilities, while also facing heavy workloads related to curriculum innovation and routine administrative duties14,15. In this context, AIGC, with its powerful information processing and content generation capabilities, holds significant potential to enhance faculty efficiency in teaching, research, and other professional tasks. However, the realization of this empowering potential depends critically on sustained use. It is important to note that faculty willingness to use AIGC tools is not static. Previous research on Chinese faculty’s adoption of AIGC has largely focused on initial usage intentions, leaving the determinants of continued use underexplored16. Therefore, this study examines university faculty members’ sustained usage intentions toward generative AI and the factors influencing such behavior, aiming to address this research gap and deepen our understanding of the underlying mechanisms that drive continuous engagement with AIGC in higher education.

Research on the user’s intention to use new technology tools based on the ECM model

The Expectation Confirmation Model (ECM), also known as the Information Systems Continuance Model, was proposed by scholar Bhattacherjee17. Its core lies in explaining how users, after using a technology, are influenced by expectation confirmation and satisfaction in their intention to continue using it. The model includes three key variables: Perceived Usefulness, Expectation Confirmation, and Satisfaction. These variables together explain users’ continuance intention to use a technology. Multiple empirical studies have validated the effectiveness of the ECM. For example, in a study on teaching staff using online learning tools, the ECM was employed to predict users’ intention to continue using new technologies18. Additionally, research on mobile learning indicates that the ECM can effectively predict university students’ continuance intention to use mobile learning19. ECM is well suited to explaining users’ continuance intention toward AIGC tools because it effectively captures users’ actual experiences and dynamic psychological changes during interactions with AIGC technologies20,21. However, the explanatory power of the ECM remains somewhat limited, as it does not adequately account for several important factors that may influence continuance intention, such as social influence and effort expectancy22.

Research on users’ willingness to adopt new technological tools based on the UTAUT model

The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), also known as the Unified Model of Technology Acceptance, was proposed by Venkatesh et al23. Its goal is to explain users’ willingness and behavior in adopting new technologies or tools by integrating multiple technology acceptance theories. The model consists of four core variables: performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions. These variables are collectively used to explain users’ behavioral intentions. Currently, the UTAUT model has been widely applied in the field of education to understand teaching staff’s willingness to use new technologies. For example, a study on the flipped classroom found that the UTAUT model can effectively predict teaching staff’s willingness to use the flipped classroom81. Another study also found that the UTAUT model has high explanatory power, accounting for nearly 70% of the variance in teaching staff’s willingness to use digital technologies24. Numerous studies have demonstrated that the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) model exhibits strong applicability and explanatory power across a wide range of domains and contexts25,26. However, the model primarily focuses on users’ initial behavioral intentions and is therefore limited in its ability to explain continued usage intentions, particularly in the context of AIGC tools. This limitation is especially evident when examining user behavior within the Chinese context27.

Building on the preceding discussion, it is evident that both the ECM and UTAUT provide valuable yet complementary perspectives for understanding users’ continuance intention toward emerging technologies. The ECM focuses on post-adoption evaluations, emphasizing expectation confirmation, perceived usefulness, and satisfaction, whereas the UTAUT highlights the roles of performance expectancy, social influence, and other determinants in shaping individuals’ initial intentions to use technology. Integrating these two models thus allows for a more comprehensive examination of the cognitive, technological, and social factors influencing users’ continuance intention. In addition, the potential risks associated with AIGC use warrant careful consideration. Perceived risk has been widely recognized as a critical psychological factor affecting both the adoption and continuance of emerging technologies28. Compared with other psychological constructs, perceived risk more directly captures individuals’ overall assessment of the potential negative consequences of technology use2930,75. While traditional technology acceptance models primarily emphasize the positive benefits of adoption, they often overlook the possible adverse outcomes of technology use28. In the context of AIGC, the uncertainty and potential biases inherent in generated content may raise concerns regarding content accuracy, privacy, and ethical implications31–33. Consequently, although users may recognize the functional value and convenience offered by AIGC tools, they may simultaneously perceive various associated risks. Prior studies indicate that such perceived risks can negatively affect users’ intentions to adopt and continue using AI-based systems34.

Overall, relying solely on the ECM or UTAUT is insufficient to fully explain individuals’ continuance intention toward emerging technologies. The ECM primarily addresses post-adoption cognitive evaluations, while the UTAUT focuses on initial technology adoption intentions; neither adequately accounts for the potential inhibitory effects of perceived risk. Therefore, this study integrates the ECM, UTAUT, and perceived risk into a unified research framework to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the factors influencing university teachers’ continuance intention to use AIGC tools.

Theoretical hypothesis

This study integrates the UTAUT and ECM models to form a comprehensive theoretical framework, enabling the models to complement each other and offering a more systematic and thorough examination of HE teaching staff’s continued intention to use AIGC tools.

Specifically, In this study, teaching staff’s continuance intention to use AIGC tools serves as the outcome variable in the theoretical model. Continuance intention refers to teaching staff’s willingness to persist in using their chosen AIGC tools for activities such as learning and work. When considering the factors that influence HE teaching staff’s continuance intention to use AIGC tools, this study largely retains the key elements from the ECM and UTAUT models, while incorporating certain adjustments to better reflect contemporary characteristics and ensure the model’s practical relevance.

Assumptions based on the ECM model

Currently, empirical studies based on the ECM model have explored the interrelationships between perceived usefulness, expectation confirmation, satisfaction, and continuance intention35. In the context of this study, expectation confirmation refers to the degree to which teaching staff evaluate whether their expectations before using AIGC tools have been met after actual use. Alnaser et al36. found in their research on the application of artificial intelligence in digital banking that if users’ expectations are met, it will significantly improve their satisfaction with AI digital banking. This result is consistent with the findings of Tian et al27., who pointed out that when Chinese graduate students use AI, if their actual experience meets (or exceeds) their expectations, it can enhance their satisfaction with the AI tool. Therefore, expectation confirmation is an antecedent variable affecting user satisfaction. Based on this, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H1: Teaching staff’s expectation confirmation of AIGC tools has a positive effect on satisfaction.

Perceived Usefulness refers to the extent to which teaching staff, after using AIGC tools, perceive that these tools can meet their needs and improve their teaching abilities and work efficiency. Yang and Jiang37, in their study on learners’ continued use intention of online education platforms, found that if learners believe the platform can improve their learning efficiency and outcomes, they are more likely to continue using it. Li and Phongsatha38conducted research on the usage behavior of junior high school students with blended learning systems and found that improvements in students’ performance and learning outcomes brought by the system not only generate positive emotions but also foster the psychological tendency to continue using the system. Rekha et al39. found that after using MOOC platforms, improvements in students’ learning outcomes not only enhanced their evaluation of MOOCs but also encouraged continued use of MOOCs for learning. Thus, perceived usefulness is an antecedent variable influencing user satisfaction and the intention to continue usage.

Based on this, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H2: Teaching staff’s perceived usefulness of AIGC tools has a positive impact on their satisfaction.

H3: Teaching staff’s perceived usefulness of AIGC tools has a positive impact on their intention to continue usage.

Satisfaction refers to teaching staff’s overall evaluation of their experience and the effectiveness of using AIGC tools. Examining the direct or indirect impact of satisfaction on usage intention has attracted significant scholarly attention. In a study exploring consumers’ continued use intention of AI chatbots, Jia et al40. found that consumers are more willing to keep using the service when they are satisfied with the high-quality, accurate, and useful information provided by the chatbot, and when they gain positive emotional experiences during use. Tam et al41. indicated that the greater the match between users’ actual experience and their expectations of a mobile application, the higher their evaluation of the app, which in turn increases their intention to continue using it. Meanwhile, Yang and Jiang37 demonstrated that when users believe that an online education platform can effectively enhance their learning efficiency and outcomes, their satisfaction increases, thereby raising the likelihood of continued use. Further, Zhang and Wu42 revealed that for patients using online medical platforms, expectation confirmation and perceived value have a positive effect on satisfaction, ultimately influencing their intention to keep using the platform. In summary, satisfaction may be a mediating variable between expectation confirmation and perceived usefulness on continued use intention. Therefore, satisfaction is not only a direct influence on continued usage, but may also mediate the relationship between expectation confirmation, perceived usefulness, and continued usage intention.

Based on this, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H4: Teaching staff’s satisfaction with AIGC tools has a positive impact on continued usage intention;

H5: Teaching staff’s expectation confirmation of AIGC tools positively influences continued usage intention through satisfaction;

H6: Teaching staff’s perceived usefulness of AIGC tools positively influences continued usage intention through satisfaction;

Hypotheses based on the UTAUT model

A large body of empirical research has used the UTAUT model to explore people’s willingness to adopt new technologies (or tools). For example, studies on teaching staff’s use of information and communication technology have revealed the combined effects of effort expectancy, social influence, and performance expectancy on their intention to continue use43. In the context of this study, Performance Expectancy refers to teaching staff’s expectation that using AIGC tools can enhance their work performance. Unlike Perceived Usefulness, this concept emphasizes expectation rather than the perception after actual use. Research by Nikolopoulou et al44. focused on Generation Z’s acceptance of mobile devices and found that the higher the individual’s performance expectancy, the more likely they are to develop an intention to use the device. Similarly, Strzelecki45 pointed out that if students believe ChatGPT can significantly enhance their learning effectiveness, they are more likely to develop an intention to use the tool. Thus, performance expectancy is a key variable influencing users’ continued intention to use. Based on this, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H7: Teaching staff’ performance expectancy regarding AIGC tools has a positive influence on their intention to continue using them.

Effort Expectancy refers to the extent to which teaching staff perceive the use of AIGC tools as easy or difficult. Chen et al46. investigated students’ use of online course platforms and found that the easier it was for students to operate the platform, the stronger their intention to continue using it. According to research by Sleiman et al47., the easier users perceive mobile payments to be, the more likely they are to continue using this payment method. Similarly, Ates & Polat48 found that if teaching staff perceive humanoid robots as easy to use, they are more willing to adopt this technology in their teaching. These findings suggest that the less effort users believe is required to use a technology or service, the higher their intention to continue using it. Based on this, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H8: Teaching staff’ effort expectancy of AIGC tools has a positive impact on their intention to continue using them.

Social influence refers to teaching staff’s perceptions of the attitudes held by important people around them (such as colleagues and students) regarding their use of AIGC tools. Sohn and Kwon49 found that when individuals perceive important others in their social network (such as close friends and family) as open or positive toward emerging technologies, they are more likely to exhibit positive behavioral intentions. Similarly78. found that if people around users (such as family members, friends, or colleagues) use and recommend QR code mobile payment services, users are more likely to continue using these services. In the same vein, Biloš and Budimir50 found in their study of Generation Z that when groups perceive their social circles as approving or even encouraging the use of ChatGPT, they show a significantly stronger intention to use it. Therefore, it can be inferred that social influence may affect continued use intention. Based on this, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H9: Social influence has a positive effect on teaching staff’s continued use intention of AIGC tools.

In addition, some scholars have explored the relationship between perceived risk and the intention to continue using a technology. Perceived risk refers to teaching staff’s concerns about the potential risks associated with AI applications, particularly regarding privacy, ethics, and autonomy. Magsayo51investigated college students’ use of mobile devices and pointed out that safety concerns are an important factor influencing their ongoing usage. Jangir et al22. further found that even if users are satisfied with the quality of financial technology services, a high level of perceived risk may still cause them to stop using those services. Similarly, Lim et al52. demonstrated that the higher users’ perceived risk regarding e-wallets, the less likely they are to continue using them. Based on this, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H10: Teaching staff’ perceived risk of AIGC tools negatively affects their intention to continue using them.

Based on the above hypotheses, this study identifies performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, perceived usefulness, expectation confirmation, satisfaction, and intention to continue using as core variables. Taking into account the characteristics of generative AI application scenarios, the perceived risk variable is incorporated to design a model of factors influencing teaching staff’s intention to continue using generative AI tools (as shown in Fig. 2).

Research design

Research methods

Teaching staff’s continued willingness to use AIGC tools may be influenced by multiple factors simultaneously, with complex interrelations and potentially nonlinear effects. Therefore, to enhance the rigor of this research, a combined approach using structural equation modeling and artificial neural networks is adopted.

In the results section of this study, SPSS 26.0 is first used to perform descriptive analysis, common method bias testing, group differences analysis, and correlation analysis among variables in the hypothesized model based on the collected data, laying the foundation for further investigation.

Next, SmartPLS4 is employed to establish a Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Model (PLS-SEM) to analyze the reliability and validity of the measurement model, as well as the path relationships and effects among variables. The reasons for using PLS-SEM in this research are twofold: (1) PLS-SEM is better suited for exploratory work and constructing complex multivariate models53,and (2) PLS-SEM is applicable to small-sample studies and does not require the sample data to follow a normal distribution54.

Finally, based on the results of the structural equation modeling, the artificial neural network module in SPSS 26.0 is used to build an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) model. This model is used to explain the nonlinear relationships among variables identified in the prior structural equation model and to assess the importance of each variable55, thereby increasing the accuracy of predicting teaching staff’s continued willingness to use AIGC tools.

Data collection and evaluation

Data collection

This cross-sectional study employed a simple random sampling approach to recruit HE teaching staff in China who had prior experience using AIGC tools. Data were collected through an online questionnaire administered between February 1 and March 1, 2025. Before accessing the survey, potential participants were provided with an introductory statement outlining the study objectives, research procedures, and ethical considerations. Participants were informed that their participation was entirely voluntary and that they could withdraw from the survey at any time without providing a reason. Electronic informed consent was obtained prior to participation, and only those who agreed to participate were allowed to proceed to the questionnaire. To ensure the eligibility of respondents, two screening questions were included at the beginning of the survey: (1) “Are you currently a university teacher?” and (2) “Have you ever used AI-generated content (AIGC) tools (e.g., Doubao, DeepSeek, ChatGPT, or Kimi)?” Only respondents who answered “Yes” to both questions were permitted to continue to the main survey. In addition, measures were implemented to prevent duplicate submissions from the same IP address, thereby enhancing data quality.

A total of 342 questionnaires were collected in this study. After excluding invalid responses, including questionnaires completed in an unreasonably short time and those in which respondents selected the same option for all items, 310 valid questionnaires were retained, yielding an effective response rate of 90.64%. Regarding gender, the sample comprised 147 male teachers (47.4%) and 163 female teachers (52.6%). In terms of academic discipline, 144 participants (46.5%) were from the humanities and social sciences, 133 (42.9%) from the natural sciences, and 33 (10.6%) from the arts and physical education. With respect to geographic location, 145 respondents (46.8%) were employed in eastern China (including Northeast China), 93 (30.0%) in central China, and 72 (23.2%) in western China. The age distribution showed that 97 respondents (31.3%) were aged 35 years or younger, 123 (39.7%) were between 36 and 45 years old, and 90 (29.0%) were aged 46 years or above. Regarding institutional type, 46 respondents (14.8%) were employed at Double First-Class universities, 162 (52.3%) at regular undergraduate institutions, and 102 (32.9%) at junior colleges. In terms of academic rank, 61 participants (19.7%) held junior professional titles, 158 (51.0%) held intermediate titles, and 91 (29.3%) held senior or above professional titles.

Sample size evaluation

To ensure the robustness of the research sample, Barclay et al56. recommend that the minimum sample size for a study should be at least 10 times the largest number of structural model paths or 10 times the largest number of items for any measurement model. In this study, the maximum number of paths in the structural model is 6, and the maximum number of items for a single measurement variable is 5, so the minimum required sample size is 60 or 50. The valid sample size in this study is 310, which is greater than 60, and therefore meets the sample size criterion for using PLS-SEM as proposed by Barclay D W et al.

Questionnaire design and variable measurement

The first section of the questionnaire examines whether respondents have used AIGC or similar technologies and collects their demographic information (e.g., gender). The second part consists of scale-based questions covering eight variables: performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, perceived usefulness, perceived risk, expectancy confirmation, satisfaction, and continued use intention. Each item for these variables is scored using a five-point Likert scale (Table 1). To ensure the validity of the measurement instrument, all items for the latent constructs were adapted from well-established studies and appropriately refined to reflect the practical context of HE teaching staff. During the questionnaire development process, two scholars specializing in English and two scholars in educational management were invited to translate and review the instrument, thereby ensuring consistency between the Chinese and English versions.

Results

Common method bias test

Since all participants used self-reporting, there may be a potential issue of common method bias. Therefore, to address this problem, we adopted corresponding procedural and statistical methods to ensure that common method bias would not affect the research results61,77.

In terms of procedural methods, we explicitly informed respondents at the beginning of the questionnaire that there were no right or wrong answers, and that their identities would remain confidential, encouraging them to answer honestly62. In addition, we used multiple scales in this study61, with all core variables measured using a five-point Likert scale. Variables such as gender and teaching subject were measured using nominal items.

In terms of statistical methodology, this study first employed Harman’s single-factor test to assess common method bias (CMB). The analysis revealed that the six factors extracted from the construct data collectively accounted for 70.10% of the total variance, with the variance explained by any single factor ranging from 4.36% to 36.89%, all below the commonly accepted 40% threshold. These results indicate that the Harman single-factor test was successfully passed62.Second, all measurement indicators were transformed into single-item constructs using SmartPLS 4, thereby rendering all primary constructs as second-order constructs (as shown in Fig. 3), and a common factor analysis was conducted Table 2. The findings demonstrated that most method factor loadings were either non-significant or exhibited small or negative values (as shown in Table 2), further suggesting that common method bias was not a concern. Finally, the variance inflation factor (VIF) test was performed to provide additional evidence that common method bias had been adequately controlled. No VIF values exceeded the threshold of 5 in the PLS output (as shown in Table 3), indicating that serious common method bias was absent in the data63. In summary, these results collectively confirm that the present study is not affected by substantial common method bias.

Construction and analysis of artificial neural network

While structural equation modeling (SEM) can effectively explain multivariate linear relationships among variables, it cannot assess nonlinear relationships between variables, nor provide a ranking of the importance of each variable’s influence on the outcome. Additionally, there are compensatory issues among variables. Therefore, to address the limitations of SEM, this analysis introduces an artificial neural network model (ANN) for further exploration. The ANN model can test nonlinear relationships among variables and determine the relative importance of each variable to the dependent variable55.

Construction of artificial neural network

This study uses the multi-layer perceptron (MLP) method in neural network to construct an artificial neural network (ANN) model. Artificial neural network is a computational model that simulates the working mechanism of human brain neurons. It is currently widely used in pattern recognition, predictive analysis and research on complex nonlinear problems. Specifically, based on the hypothesis test results of the above PLS-SEM, referring to the literature of Chen & Wu70 the multi-layer perceptron (MLP) method in neural network is used to construct two artificial neural network (ANN) models, Model A and Model B (Fig. 5 and Fig. 6). The input layer of Model A includes perceived usefulness and expectation confirmation, and the output layer is satisfaction; the input layer of Model B includes perceived usefulness, expectation confirmation, satisfaction, effort expectation, and performance expectation, and the input layer is continued use intention. The configurations for both models were identical. Specifically, the hidden layer was configured with a single layer, and the number of neurons was determined automatically. The hyperbolic tangent function was selected as the activation function for the hidden layer. For the output layer, an identity activation function was employed. The scaled conjugate gradient algorithm was used as the optimization method. These settings were chosen to balance computational efficiency and predictive accuracy while maintaining consistency with prior studies using multilayer perceptron networks.

Discussion and conclusion

Discussion

This study systematically examines the key factors influencing the continued use Intention of AIGC tools among HE teaching staff in China. By integrating the ECM and UTAUT models, the study focuses on six core variables—perceived usefulness, expectation confirmation, satisfaction, performance expectancy, effort expectancy, perceived risk, and social influence—and investigates their mechanisms in shaping continued use intention. The PLS-SEM approach is employed to identify the structural relationships among variables, whereas the ANN model is used to evaluate and rank the relative importance of these factors.

Correlation analysis indicates that all six variables are significantly associated with the continued use Intention of AIGC tools. Further PLS-SEM results demonstrate that perceived usefulness not only directly affects continued use intention but also indirectly influences it through the mediating role of satisfaction, similar to expectation confirmation. These findings are consistent with prior research, confirming that perceived usefulness and expectation confirmation are important antecedents of continued use intention and supporting the applicability of the ECM model71,72. However, the relatively low f2 value of perceived usefulness suggests that its direct explanatory power is limited. For HE teaching staff, merely perceiving the functional value of AIGC tools is insufficient to strongly drive continued use intention. In the context of the increasing diffusion of AIGC in China, HE teaching staff have generally acknowledged its functional benefits; thus, “usefulness” is no longer the decisive factor. Instead, sustained usage is more strongly determined by the overall user experience, particularly satisfaction derived from actual use. Therefore, perceived usefulness primarily influences continued use intention indirectly through satisfaction, while its direct effect remains relatively weak.

Notably, the PLS-SEM results reveal that perceived risk and social influence do not have significant effects on the continued use Intention of AIGC tools among HE teaching staff, which is inconsistent with prior studies73,74. This finding suggests that, in the context of Chinese higher education, continued usage decisions are not significantly constrained by risk concerns or external social pressures. On the one hand, with strong policy support and rapid advancements in AI technologies, AIGC tools have become increasingly prevalent in Chinese universities, leading to a relatively high level of psychological acceptance among HE teaching staff. Compared with potential risks, such as privacy and information accuracy, teachers place greater emphasis on the practical value that these tools bring to their work. As long as AIGC tools can effectively support their professional tasks, risk-related concerns are unlikely to become decisive barriers to continued use. On the other hand, HE teaching staff, as a highly knowledgeable and professional group, tend to exhibit strong autonomy in decision-making regarding technology adoption. Furthermore, access to AIGC-related knowledge and usage experience through diverse channels—such as online platforms, academic communities, and institutional training—reduces reliance on peer influence. Consequently, social influence does not play a significant role in shaping continued use intention.

Importantly, the ANN results indicate that the relative importance of predictors of continued use intention, in descending order, is performance expectancy, satisfaction, expectation confirmation, effort expectancy, and perceived usefulness. Among these, performance expectancy emerges as the most critical predictor, with an average relative importance coefficient of 0.240 and a normalized importance of 100%. This finding suggests that, compared with other factors, HE teaching staff are more concerned with whether AIGC tools can effectively enhance their work performance when deciding whether to continue using them. In China, HE teaching staff often undertake multiple responsibilities, including teaching, research, and social service, resulting in high workload pressure and complex task demands. In this context, their adoption of new technologies is strongly goal-oriented, with a focus on whether such tools can deliver sustained practical benefits, such as improving teaching efficiency, optimizing research processes, and supporting course design and academic writing. In other words, the primary driving force behind the continued use of AIGC tools lies in their perceived work value and practical effectiveness, which is consistent with the logic of technology adoption among organizational employees, where productivity and task performance are key considerations.

Conclusion

Theoretical contribution

This study innovatively integrates the ECM and UTAUT models to develop a comprehensive theoretical framework aimed at explaining the influence of multiple factors on HE teaching staff’s continued intention to use AIGC tools, thereby extending the application of ECM within the educational domain. By combining PLS-SEM and ANN analyses, the research not only elucidates the mechanisms through which variables such as performance expectancy, satisfaction, perceived usefulness, and confirmation of expectations affect continuance intention but also identifies the key determinants of teaching staff’s continued intention and their relative importance. The findings indicate that performance expectancy is the most critical factor, followed by satisfaction (serving as a mediating variable), and subsequently expectation confirmation, effort expectancy, and perceived usefulness. Overall, the results suggest that HE teaching staff’s continuance intention is primarily driven by performance-oriented considerations, while satisfaction plays a pivotal mediating role in the effects of perceived usefulness and confirmation of expectations on continuance intention.

Practical implications

This study offers actionable insights for educational administrators and technology developers, supporting the development of strategies to promote the continued use Intention of AIGC tools among HE teaching staff. Firstly, higher education institutions should establish regularized training and technical support mechanisms when promoting the adoption of AIGC tools. Initiatives such as case sharing, operational guidance, hands-on workshops, and online consultation can enhance HE teaching staff’s competence and satisfaction, thereby strengthening their intention to continue using these tools. Secondly, at the policy level, educational authorities can guide the appropriate use of AIGC tools by incorporating performance evaluations and teaching and research incentives. These measures can improve both the frequency and standardization of AIGC tool use in teaching and research activities. In addition, developing and refining AIGC usage guidelines can enhance HE teaching staff’s expectancy that using these tools will improve their work performance. Finally, AIGC tool developers should prioritize optimizing functionality and interface design to improve usability and practical value. By analyzing HE teaching staff’s historical usage data, developers may also design customized modules or plugins that align with teachers’ work habits and needs. Such tailored features enable HE teaching staff to anticipate performance gains prior to use and validate these expectations during use, thereby increasing satisfaction and reinforcing the continued use Intention of AIGC tools.

Research limitations

This study employed a cross-sectional research design, and therefore could not capture temporal changes in HE teaching staff’s willingness to adopt generative AI tools. Although simple random sampling was applied and the sample was relatively balanced with respect to key characteristics such as gender, disciplinary field, age, and academic rank, unobserved sampling biases may still be present. Accordingly, the generalization and interpretation of the findings should be made with caution. Future research could employ longitudinal designs or increase the sample size to further assess the robustness of these results.
