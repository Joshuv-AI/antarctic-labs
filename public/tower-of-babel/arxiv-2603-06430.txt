Countable models of weakly quasi-o-minimal theories II
Slavko Moconja, Predrag Tanović

Abstract
. We confirm Martin’s conjecture for a broad subclass of weakly quasi-o-minimal theories.



1.
Introduction and preliminaries

This paper continues
[
15
]
and
[
16

In
[
15

, the case of binary such theories was resolved, and in
[
16

we started the ”Non-structure” part in the general case, that is, searching for conditions that imply the existence of the maximum number of countable models. There, we found one such condition: the existence of a definable family of semiintervals of

Theorem 1
.

Let
T
be a countable, weakly quasi-o-minimal theory. Suppose that at least one of the following two conditions holds.

(1)

T
does not have simple semiintervals;

(2)

There exists a non-convex type
p\in S_{1}(T)
.

Then
I(T,\aleph_{0})=2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

The simplicity of semiintervals is equivalent to the nonexistence of shifts in small weakly quasi-o-minimal theories. However, it is a strong technical property that guaranties the exceptional simplicity of the relatively definable binary subsets of the locus of a complete 1-type. Together with the convexity of types, it will suffice to develop the ”Structure” part of the classification theory for almost
\aleph_{0}
-categorical weakly quasi-o-minimal theories, for which we verify Martin’s conjecture, a strengthening of Vaught’s conjecture.

Theorem 2
.

Martin’s conjecture holds for almost
\aleph_{0}
-categorical weakly quasi-o-minimal theories.

In Section

(R)

For all

Martin’s conjecture holds for weakly quasi-o-minimal theories with an additional feature: binary, rosy, quasi-o-minimal, or has finite convexity rank.

Proof.

over

2)

2.2.
Simplicity of semiintervals and binary forking dependence

In this subsection, in Proposition
2.24
, we prove that the set

measures quite accurately the amount of forking dependence of

and

are weakly o-minimal and at least one of them has simple semiintervals. This looks like a very weak form of 1-basedness, but the intuition behind this is that the simplicity of semiintervals implies that the binary forking dependence is measured by elements of a certain meet-tree, as evidenced in Corollary
2.22
.

Notation 2.19
.

For any

we will use the following notation:

•

\mathcal{W}_{A}(D)=\{a\in D\mid\mbox{$\mathrm{tp}(a/A)$ is weakly o-minimal}\}
;

•

\mathcal{W}_{A}^{ss}(D)=\{a\in\mathcal{W}_{A}(D)\mid\mbox{$\mathrm{tp}(a/A)$ has simple semiintervals}\}
;

•

\mathcal{T}_{A}(D)=\{X\subset\mathfrak{C}^{eq}\mid\mbox{$X=\mathrm{dcl^{eq}}(AX)\subseteq\mathrm{dcl^{eq}}(Aa)$ for some $a\in\mathcal{W}_{A}(D)$}\}
;

•

m_{A}(a,b)=\mathrm{dcl^{eq}}(Aa)\cap\mathrm{dcl^{eq}}(Ab)
.

If
A=\emptyset
, we omit stressing it in the index.

In Corollary
2.22
we will prove that

is a meet-tree with

its meet operation, which will be used in the proof of Proposition
2.24
.

Remark 2.20
.

(a) Note that
A\subseteq B
implies
\mathcal{W}_{A}(D)\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{B}(D)
: if
a\in\mathcal{W}_{A}(D)
, then

as any extension of a weakly o-minimal type is weakly o-minimal.

(b) In general,

does not imply

: It is possible that

has simple semiintervals but

does not, as we know from Example

.

(c)

is a natural identification of elements of

with elements of

.

3.
Existence of shifts vs simplicity of semiintervals

Unlike in the definable case studied in
[
16
, Section 6]
, the notion of a shift is inappropriate for families of
\mathbf{p}
-semiintervals: if
\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{p}}
is such a family, then there is no reason why the power
(S_{a}^{\mathbf{p}})^{2}=\{x\in p(\mathfrak{C})\mid(\exists t\models p)(t\in S_{a}\land x\in S_{t})\}
should be relatively definable at all.
In this section, we compare the following two conditions for a weakly quasi-o-minimal
T
:

(NS)

T
does not have simple semiintervals;

(SH)

T
admits a definable family of semiintervals of
(\mathfrak{C},<)
that contains a shift.

The main technical result of this section is Proposition
3.6
in which we prove that every
\mathbf{p}
-semiinterval that is not
\mathcal{E}_{p}
-determined can be slightly enlarged, so that the new
\mathbf{p}
-semiinterval is induced by a shift of some monotone definable family of semiintervals. As an immediate corollary we see that (NS) implies (SH).
Example
3.8
shows that the converse is not always true, but in Theorem
3.7
(b), we prove the converse assuming, in addition, that
T
is small or weakly o-minimal.
As corollaries, we obtain that every weakly quasi-o-minimal theory with few countable models has simple semiintervals, and that every convex weakly o-minimal type in a theory with few countable models has hereditarily simple semiintervals.

This is a rephrased version of

5.3.
Definability vs triviality of convex types

The notions of left-/right-definability of convex so-pairs were introduced in
[
15
]
; here, we need them only in the context of weakly o-minimal pairs.

Definition 5.11
.

A convex weakly o-minimal pair
\mathbf{p}=(p,<)
is left-definable (right-definable) if the type
\mathbf{p}_{l}
(
\mathbf{p}_{r}
) is definable.

The definability of convex weakly o-minimal pairs is related to the definability of final parts of
(p(\mathfrak{C}),<)
and to nonisolation properties of nonforking extensions of
p
as follows:

Remark 5.12
.

Suppose that
p\in S(A)
is a weakly o-minimal type such that
p(\mathfrak{C})
is a convex subset of
(D,<)
, an
A
-definable linear order.

(1)

By Lemma 7.2 of
[
15
]
, the pair
\mathbf{p}=(p,<)
is left-definable if and only if some initial part of
p(\mathfrak{C})
is a definable set if and only if the set
p^{-}(\mathfrak{C})=\{x\in D\mid x<p(\mathfrak{C})\}
is
A
-definable. Similarly for right-definable.

(2)

For all
B\supseteq A
,
\mathbf{p}=(p,<)
is left-definable if and only if
((\mathbf{p}_{l})_{\restriction B},<)
is left-definable, and
\mathbf{p}=(p,<)
is right-definable if and only if
((\mathbf{p}_{r})_{\restriction B},<)
is right-definable.

(3)

If
\mathbf{p}
is not left-definable, then the type
\mathbf{p}_{l}
is finitely satisfied in
p^{-}(\mathfrak{C})
. Indeed, if
\phi(x)\in\mathbf{p}_{l}
, then
\phi(\mathfrak{C})
contains an initial part of
p(\mathfrak{C})
, so since no initial part of
p(\mathfrak{C})
is definable,
\phi(x)
is realized in
p^{-}(\mathfrak{C})
. Note that in this case, the type
p
, as well as the type
(\mathbf{p}_{l})_{\restriction B}
(where
B\supseteq A
), is nonisolated.

(4)

If
p
is simple and
\mathbf{p}
is of kind (x,y) (
\mathrm{x,y}\in\{\mathrm{d,nd}\}
), as defined below, then
((\mathbf{p}_{r})_{\restriction Aa},<)
is of kind (d,y) and
((\mathbf{p}_{l})_{\restriction B},<)
is of kind (x,d) for all
a\models p
.

There are four kinds of convex weakly o-minimal
pairs
depending on whether their ends are definable or not. We will label them by (d,d), (d,nd), (nd,d), and (nd,nd); for example, (d,nd) labels the pairs that are left-definable but not right-definable. There are three kinds of convex weakly o-minimal
types
.

•

p
is of kind (d,d) if both global nonforking extensions of
p
are definable; that is, if every (equivalently, some) weakly o-minimal pair
(p,<_{p})
over
A
is of kind (d,d). Analogously for:
p
is of kind (nd,nd).

•

p
is of
mixed kind
if exactly one of its global nonforking extensions is definable; in that case, every (equivalently, some) weakly o-minimal pair
(p,<_{p})
over
A
is of kind (d,nd) or (nd,d).

Remark 5.13
.

(a) A weakly o-minimal convex type
p
is of kind (d,d) if and only if it is isolated:
By Remark
5.12
,
p
is of kind (d,d) if and only if the locus
p(\mathfrak{C})
has some initial part and some final part definable, which is easily seen to be equivalent to the definability of
p(\mathfrak{C})
. Finally,
p(\mathfrak{C})
is definable if and only if
p
is isolated.

(b) Every definable, nonalgebraic, weakly o-minimal type over a model is of mixed kind.

(c) In the literature, complete 1-types in (weakly) o-minimal theories of kind (nd,nd) are also called cuts or irrational types, while those of mixed kind are called noncuts, rational cuts or quasi-rational types.

Fact 5.14
.

[
15
, Lemma 7.17]

Directly non-orthogonal convex weakly o-minimal pairs are of the same kind.

A consequence of this fact is that all convex weakly o-minimal types from the same
\not\perp\hskip-3.99994pt^{\mathit{w}}
-class are of the same kind. In different terminology, this was proved for complete 1-types in o-minimal theories by Marker in
[
14
]
, and in weakly o-minimal theories by Baizhanov in
[
5
]
.

In
[

we proved that all definable complete 1-types over a dense o-minimal structure are trivial. Now, we generalize this result.

6.
Martin’s conjecture

Martin’s conjecture is a strengthening of Vaught’s conjecture. Assume that
T
is small and let
L_{1}(T)
be the smallest countable fragment of
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
that contains all first-order formulae and formulae
\bigwedge_{\phi(x)\in p}\phi(x)
for all
p\in S(T)
. For each countable
M\models T
, let
T_{1}(M)
denote the
L_{1}(T)
-theory of
M
.

Martin’s Conjecture.
If
I(T,\aleph_{0})<2^{\aleph_{0}}
then
T_{1}(M)
is
\aleph_{0}
-categorical for all countable
M\models T
.

Note that the quantifier rank of any
L_{1}(T)
-formula is below
\omega+\omega
, so Martin’s conjecture predicts that, assuming
I(T,\aleph_{0})<2^{\aleph_{0}}
, the Scott ranks of countable models of
T
are at most
\omega+\omega
; this is known to imply Vaught’s conjecture.

Definition 6.1
.

A complete first-order theory
T
is almost
\aleph_{0}
-categorical if for all
n
and all types
p_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,p_{n}(x_{n})\in S_{1}(T)
the type
\bigcup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}p_{i}(x_{i})
has finitely many extensions in
S_{n}(T)
.

Almost
\aleph_{0}
-categorical theories were introduced by Ikeda, Pillay, and Tsuboi in
[
8
]
. The following holds in any almost
\aleph_{0}
-categorical theory:

•

Let
A\subseteq B
be finite. Then every complete type over
A
has finitely many extensions over
B
.

•

Every complete type over a finite domain that extends an isolated type is also isolated.

•

The almost
\aleph_{0}
-categoricity is preserved by naming finitely many parameters.

These facts will be used silently in the proof of Theorem
2
, where we also need the following folklore fact and its consequence.

Fact 6.2

(Any countable
T
)
.

If there exists a sequence of finite tuples
(a_{n})_{n<\omega}
such that:

–

\mathrm{tp}(a_{n})
is non-isolated for all

, and

–

\mathrm{tp}(a_{n})\perp\hskip-3.99994pt^{\mathit{w}}\mathrm{tp}(\bar{a}_{<n})
for all
n<\omega
,

then
I(\aleph_{0},T)=2^{\aleph_{0}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that a sequence
(a_{n})_{n<\omega}
satisfies the two properties. Recall that if
a,b,c
are tuples such that
\mathrm{tp}(a)\perp\hskip-3.99994pt^{\mathit{w}}\mathrm{tp}(b)
and
\mathrm{tp}(ab)\perp\hskip-3.99994pt^{\mathit{w}}\mathrm{tp}(c)
, then
\mathrm{tp}(a)\perp\hskip-3.99994pt^{\mathit{w}}\mathrm{tp}(bc)
(for if
a\equiv a^{\prime}
, then
ab\equiv a^{\prime}b
as
\mathrm{tp}(a)\perp\hskip-3.99994pt^{\mathit{w}}\mathrm{tp}(b)
, and let

be such that

; since
