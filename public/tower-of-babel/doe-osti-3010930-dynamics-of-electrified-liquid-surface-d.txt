Final report for DE-SC0023214 (9/1/2022-8/31/2025)
 Dynamics of electrified liquid surface during plasma-liquid interaction

 PI: Ken Hara

The research goal of this Department of Energy (DOE) Office of Fusion Energy Science (OFES)
project was to investigate the electrified liquid surface dynamics using a shallow water model.
The project was an extension of the Princeton Collaborative Research Facility (PCRF) project to
work with Dr. Mikhail Shneider at Princeton University.

Final Technical Progress

The PI asked for a 12-month no-cost extension. This final report is to summarize the activities
from the start date (September 1, 2022) to the final day (August 31, 2025). During this period,
we have achieved a few important milestones.

• Development of the shallow water model to describe the dynamics of liquid surface

Plasma liquid interactions play an important role for various applications, including plasma
agriculture, water treatment, material processing, and nuclear fusion. The plasma liquid interface
can move dynamically based on the interactions between the electric field, surface tension,
gravitational force, and surface reactions, such as evaporation, desorption, and chemical
reactions, as shown in Fig. 1.

 Figure 1: The forces exerted on electrified liquid surface

With multiple protrusions in electrospray or for liquid cathodes, the electrified liquid surface has
been observed to be dynamic in nature. This is potentially due to how the electric field is coupled
with the liquid surface, i.e., the liquid surface profile determines the electric field structure
through the Poisson equation and the electric field profile determines the dynamics of liquid
surface through the Maxwell stress tensor. In addition, there are self-organization pattens

observed when a gaseous plasma interacts with a liquid. This indicates that the liquid surface is
affected by or could affect the self-organization patterns.

Investigating dynamics of the liquid surface typically requires three-dimensional (3D) fluid
simulations, such as the ones that use the volume-of-fluid (VOF) method. A large-scale 3D
simulation might be computationally costly, particularly when studying the coupled dynamics of
the liquid surface and electromagnetic fields. Here, in this project, we have focused on the
development of a shallow water model that tracks the liquid surface height as a function of time
and space. For instance, for 3D liquid dynamics, the vertical depth needs to be tracked as a
function of the 2D transverse directions, which enables saving computational costs of the liquid
motion. The key assumptions for the shallow water model to be valid are (i) long wavelength,
i.e., the wavelength of the surface wave must be much larger than the liquid depth, and (ii) the
vertical liquid velocity is negligible. The latter means that the flow circulation inside the bulk
liquid is negligible.

• Investigation of Tonks-Frenkel instability of electrified liquid surface

The most basic instability of liquid surface is characterized by the so-called Tonks-Frenkel
instability, which provides a critical electric field above which the electrified liquid surface
becomes unstable. This electric field is a function of the surface tension and gravitational
acceleration.

We have studied the Tonks-Frenkel instability in two papers. First is in Liu et al. Phys. Fluids
2023. We have used a simplified electric field model, i.e., not solving the Laplace/Poisson
equation on discretized grids. Figure 3 shows the two cases where the electric field is below and
above the critical electric field, showing the damped and unstable liquid surface, respectively.

 Figure 3: Tonks-Frenkel instability study in Liu et al. Phys. Fluids 2023.

Then, using the self-consistent electrified shallow water model with the WLSQ interpolation
method, we repeated the study. Figure 4 shows the Tonks-Frenkel instability study by Suzuki et
al. in Physics of Plasmas 2025. Without the WLSQ method, we observed that the coupled
simulation often is numerical unstable.

Figure 4: Tonks-Frenkel instability study in Suzuki et al. Phys. Plasmas 2025. The color contour
 shows the electrostatic potential and the black solid line corresponds to the liquid surface.

Impact

The developed method is potentially useful for the liquid surface dynamics in plasma-liquid
interactions. For instance, if an electrode is immersed in the liquid, the plasma dynamics can be
affected by the liquid properties, such as conductivity, viscosity, surface tension, and liquid
temperature. The shallow water model provides a computational cheap option to simulate the
liquid surface dynamics.
