Comment on “accuracy of machine learning models for mitral regurgitation severity assessment: A systematic review and meta-analysis"
Authors: Sharyu Ikhar, Yogesh Nagargoje, Ruchita Shrivastava
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the systematic review and meta-analysis by Eini et al. [1]. Evaluating machine learning models for mitral regurgitation severity assessment.

Reference standard heterogeneity and its diagnostic consequences

The pooled area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.97 is impressive; however, the subgroup analysis identifying divergent specificity between quantitative Doppler-based reference standards and expert or algorithmic classification exposes a consequential problem. When the reference standard lacks objectivity, as in cardiologist-graded labels without core laboratory adjudication, a model trained and tested against that standard inherits its variability. They used structured field extraction and natural language processing of clinical reports as surrogate ground truth; these labels carry inherent interobserver noise that propagates into performance metrics. Treating these measurements equivalently to studies using proximal is velocity surface area and vena contract measurements against the American Society of Echocardiography criteria inflates the apparent diagnostic precision [2].

Frame-level versus clinical classification

The superior performance of frame-based models over video-based architectures warrants clinical scrutiny. Frame-level classification optimizes image-level pattern recognition rather than the integrative multiparametric judgment governing clinical severity grading. A model that identifies severe regurgitation from three systolic frames solves a fundamentally different problem from one that integrates colour Doppler jet behaviour across cardiac cycles and respiratory variations. Pooling these architectures into a single diagnostic accuracy estimate conflates the computational performance with the clinical decision support capacity [3].

The authors' moderate GRADE certainty rating was appropriate. Prospective validation against adjudicated multiparametric standards with prespecified operating thresholds remains a necessary step before clinical deployment.
