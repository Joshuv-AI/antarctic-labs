Vaping-associated acute respiratory distress syndrome managed with VV-ECMO: a case report
Authors: G F Habtar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractElectronic cigarettes (e-cigarettes) have been increasingly promoted as safer alternatives to conventional smoking and as smoking cessation tools. However, emerging evidence has linked e-cigarette use to serious pulmonary complications, including e-cigarette or vaping product associated lung injury (EVALI). Severe cases may progress to acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), requiring advanced respiratory support. We report the case of a previously healthy 25-year-old female who developed severe vaping-associated ARDS refractory to conventional mechanical ventilation and was successfully managed with veno-venous extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VV-ECMO). This case highlights the potential severity of vaping-related lung injury and emphasizes the role of ECMO as a life-saving intervention in selected patients.Keywords: critical care medicine, respiratory disorders

Introduction

Electronic cigarettes (e-cigarettes) are battery-powered devices that generate inhalable aerosols by heating liquids that contain nicotine and various chemical additives. Inhalation of this aerosol, commonly referred to as vaping [1, 2], has gained widespread popularity over the past decade, particularly among young adults [3]. Although initially marketed as a safer alternative to combustible cigarettes, increasing evidence suggests that e-cigarette use is associated with significant adverse respiratory effects [4–6].

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) defines e-cigarette or vaping product use– associated lung injury (EVALI) as a diagnosis of exclusion, given the absence of definitive diagnostic criteria. Clinical judgment, supported by imaging findings and the exclusion of infectious or alternative etiologies, remains essential for diagnosis. The clinical spectrum of EVALI ranges from mild respiratory symptoms to fulminant respiratory failure. Common manifestations include cough, dyspnea, chest discomfort, fever, fatigue, and gastrointestinal symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea [4–6].

Among the most severe presentations, vaping-induced Acute Respiratory Distress syndrome (ARDS) has emerged as a critical condition, characterized by diffuse alveolar injury and profound hypoxemia [7]. The pathophysiology is thought to involve inflammatory responses and oxidative stress induced by inhaled toxic compounds [8]. In cases where conventional ventilatory strategies fail, veno-venous extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VV-ECMO) may provide effective respiratory support while allowing lung recovery [9, 10]. We present a case of severe vaping-associated ARDS that was successfully treated with VV-ECMO.

Case report

A 25-year-old female with no known medical comorbidities presented to the emergency department with a fever, cough, and progressive shortness of breath. She had no history of allergies, chronic medication use, or illicit drug use, and denied recent viral or bacterial infections or contact with sick individuals. She reported the current use of electronic cigarettes.

The initial evaluation resulted in a presumptive diagnosis of community-acquired pneumonia, and empirical antibiotic therapy was initiated. Despite treatment, her respiratory symptoms worsened over the following two days, culminating in acute respiratory failure requiring endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation. She was subsequently admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU).

A comprehensive infectious workup included a multiplex respiratory viral PCR panel covering adenovirus, seasonal coronaviruses (229E, HKU1, NL63, OC43), SARS-CoV-2, human metapneumovirus, rhinovirus/enterovirus, influenza A and B (including subtypes), parainfluenza viruses (types 1–4), respiratory syncytial virus, and bocavirus. In addition, testing for atypical bacterial pathogens (including Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Legionella pneumophila, and Bordetella pertussis) was performed. Standard bacterial and fungal cultures from BAL were also obtained. All infectious workup results were negative. The patient developed progressive hypoxemia despite maximal conventional ventilatory support, which was consistent with severe ARDS (Fig. 1). Given the refractory nature of her respiratory failure, veno-venous ECMO was initiated.

The patient remained on ECMO support for 12 days while receiving lung-protective ventilation strategies and high-dose systemic corticosteroids. Chest radiography and computed tomography (CT) performed during ICU admission demonstrated diffuse bilateral pulmonary infiltrates consistent with ARDS. Over time, her respiratory status improved, allowing for successful extubation and decannulation from ECMO. She was discharged in stable condition and was followed up in the outpatient clinic. A follow-up CT scan performed three months after discharge demonstrated marked radiological improvement (Fig. 2).

Discussion

This case illustrates the severe manifestation of vaping-associated lung injury progressing to ARDS in a young, previously healthy individual. EVALI remains a diagnostic challenge owing to its nonspecific clinical presentation and the absence of definitive diagnostic criteria. A high index of suspicion is required, particularly for patients with recent vaping exposure and unexplained respiratory failure.

The pathogenesis of vaping-induced lung injury is complex and multifactorial, and involves inflammatory activation, oxidative stress, and direct epithelial toxicity from inhaled substances. Severe cases may overwhelm the capacity of conventional mechanical ventilation, thus necessitating advanced support modalities.

VV-ECMO has emerged as a valuable rescue therapy for severe ARDS, providing adequate gas exchange while minimizing ventilator-induced lung injury and allowing time for pulmonary recovery. The favorable outcome in this case supports the early consideration of ECMO in carefully selected patients with vaping-associated ARDS who fail standard management.

Conclusion

Vaping-associated lung injury can result in life-threatening ARDS, even in young and previously healthy individuals. Early recognition of EVALI and prompt escalation of care are crucial to prevent adverse outcomes. VV-ECMO is an effective life-saving intervention in cases of refractory respiratory failure secondary to vaping-induced ARDS. Increased awareness of the potential severity of e-cigarette-related lung injuries is essential for timely diagnosis and management.
