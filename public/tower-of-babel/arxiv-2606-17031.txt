Effective Intrinsic Ergodicity for renewal-type potentials on S S -gap shifts
Farhod Haydarov, Shirali Kadyrov, Mirmukhsin Makhmudov, Khudoyor Mamayusupov

Abstract
. We establish effective intrinsic ergodicity for renewal-type potentials on one-sided S S -gap shifts. Inducing on the one-symbol cylinder [ 1 ] [1] reduces the system to a full shift over the alphabet S S , where the induced potential becomes a one-symbol potential and the equilibrium measure is Bernoulli. The associated renewal equation has a unique solution P P , and under the condition P > ϕ ⁡ ( 0 ∞ ) P>\phi(0^{\infty}) (automatic when S S is infinite), we show that P P is the topological pressure and that the potential admits a unique equilibrium state μ ϕ \mu_{\phi} . Our main result is an effective intrinsic ergodicity estimate: invariant measures whose free energy is within Δ \Delta of the pressure are O ⁡ ( Δ ) O(\sqrt{\Delta}) -close to μ ϕ \mu_{\phi} when tested against Hölder observables. As an application, every finite-word cylinder of positive μ ϕ \mu_{\phi} -measure yields a uniform pressure gap for the set of orbits avoiding that cylinder, leading in the entropy case to strict entropy and Hausdorff-dimension gaps.

1.
Introduction

The variational principle is one of the central facts in thermodynamic formalism. For a continuous map
T:X\to X
on a compact metric space and a continuous potential
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
, it identifies the topological pressure with

P_{\mathrm{top}}(X,T,\phi)=\sup\left\{h_{\mu}(T)+\int\phi\,\,d\mu:\mu\text{ is }T\text{-invariant}\right\}.

A measure attaining this supremum is called an equilibrium state. Classical results of Bowen, Ruelle, Walters and others give uniqueness and statistical properties for many uniformly hyperbolic systems and shifts of finite type
[
5
,
19
,
24
,
25
,
15
]
. A parallel operator-theoretic approach, based on transfer operators and spectral stability, is also central in the classical theory
[
3
,
14
,
10
,
4
]
.

A natural problem is to understand what remains true beyond the finite-state Markov setting. For countable Markov shifts, Sarig developed a thermodynamic formalism that extends many features of the classical theory
[
22
,
23
]
. For compact symbolic systems beyond specification, Climenhaga and Thompson proved uniqueness results using orbit decompositions and pressure estimates
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. In particular,
S
-gap shifts are a standard family of compact symbolic systems which need not be shifts of finite type.

This paper concerns a quantitative version of uniqueness. Suppose that
\mu_{\phi}
is the unique equilibrium state. If another invariant measure
\mu
has free energy close to the pressure, must
\mu
be close to
\mu_{\phi}
? Effective versions of this question go back to work of Kadyrov for measures of maximal entropy on finite-state shifts
[
13
]
. Related estimates for countable Markov shifts were proved by Rühr and by Rühr–Sarig
[
21
,
20
]
. Recently, Pollicott gave an analogue for expanding interval maps, including beta-transformations
[
18
]
. The aim here is to prove such an estimate for a compact symbolic system which is naturally coded by a countable renewal shift.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
be nonempty. The one-sided
S
-gap shift
X_{S}\subset\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
consists of all sequences for which the number of zeros between two successive symbols
1
belongs to
S
. No mixing assumption is needed in the main theorem. The cylinder

E=[1]=\{x\in X_{S}:x_{0}=1\}

has a simple first-return structure. If
x\in E
returns to
E
, then the return time is
n+1
for some
n\in S
, and the block read by the orbit is
10^{n}
. Thus inducing on
E
gives the full shift

Y_{S}=S^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}

with return-time function
\tau(y)=y_{0}+1
.

We study Hölder potentials
\phi
on
X_{S}
whose induced potential depends only on the current return block. More precisely, if

\Phi_{\phi}(y):=\sum_{j=0}^{\tau(y)-1}\phi(\sigma^{j}\pi(y)),

where
\pi(y)=10^{y_{0}}10^{y_{1}}10^{y_{2}}\cdots
, then
\phi
is called renewal-type if

\Phi_{\phi}(y)=\psi(y_{0})

for some function
\psi:S\to\mathbb{R}
. The induced equilibrium problem is then one-dimensional. The pressure parameter
P
is determined by the renewal equation

(1)

\sum_{n\in S}e^{\psi(n)-P(n+1)}=1.

We prove below that this equation has a unique real solution. The induced equilibrium measure is the Bernoulli measure with weights

p_{n}=e^{\psi(n)-P(n+1)}.

The passage from the induced system back to
X_{S}
is not formal. First, a bounded Hölder observable
f
on
X_{S}
induces to

F_{f}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\tau(y)-1}f(\sigma^{j}\pi(y)),

which is generally unbounded because
\tau
is unbounded. We handle this by writing

F_{f}=f(0^{\infty})\tau+G_{f},

where
G_{f}
is bounded and Hölder. Second, the inducing formulas do not apply to an arbitrary invariant measure as soon as
\mu([1])>0
. The reason is that

\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\sigma^{-j}[1]=X_{S}\setminus\{0^{\infty}\}.

Thus a measure may have both a positive atom at
0^{\infty}
and positive mass on
[1]
. Before inducing, one must split off the atom at
0^{\infty}
. This correction is built into the proof below.

We now state the main theorem in a form that does not require topological mixing. Fix
\theta\in(0,1)
. For
x,y\in X_{S}
, set

d_{\theta}(x,y)=\theta^{t(x,y)},\qquad t(x,y)=\min\{k\geq 0:x_{k}\neq y_{k}\},

with the convention
d_{\theta}(x,x)=0
. Let
C^{\theta}(X_{S})
be the Banach space of bounded Hölder functions with norm

\left\|f\right\|_{\theta}=\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}+|f|_{\theta},\qquad|f|_{\theta}=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d_{\theta}(x,y)}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
be nonempty, and let
X_{S}
be the one-sided
S
-gap shift. Let
\phi\in C^{\theta}(X_{S})
be a renewal-type potential with induced one-symbol potential
\psi:S\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
P
be the unique real solution of (
1
). If
S
is finite, assume the pressure separation condition

P>\phi(0^{\infty}).

If
S
is infinite, no additional pressure assumption is needed. Under these hypotheses,

P=P_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S},\sigma,\phi),

and
\phi
has a unique equilibrium state
\mu_{\phi}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
S,\phi
and
\theta
, such that for every
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measure
\mu
on
X_{S}
and every
f\in C^{\theta}(X_{S})
,

\left|\int f\,\,d\mu-\int f\,\,d\mu_{\phi}\right|\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{\theta}\sqrt{P-\left(h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\mu\right)}.

The estimate in
theorem

1.1
has the same square-root form as the inequalities appearing in effective intrinsic ergodicity. For shifts of finite type and the measure of maximal entropy, such estimates were proved by Kadyrov
[
13
]
. Rühr and Rühr–Sarig developed related estimates in countable-state settings
[
21
,
20
]
. Pollicott’s recent note proves an analogue for expanding interval maps and beta-transformations
[
18
]
. The present paper differs from those results in that the system
X_{S}
is compact, but the natural proof passes through a countable induced renewal shift and then returns to
X_{S}
.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section

2
introduces
S
-gap shifts, the renewal coding, the lift from the induced shift, and the atom-splitting lemma.
Section

3
defines renewal-type potentials and proves the exponential tail estimate used later.
Section

4
constructs the equilibrium state and proves uniqueness.
Section

5
proves the square-root estimate on the induced renewal shift.
Section

6
transfers the estimate back to
X_{S}
and proves
theorem

1.1
.
Section

7
gives the cylinder-avoidance application.

2.
The renewal coding and the inducing formulas

2.1.
The
S
-gap shift

Let
S\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
be nonempty. The one-sided
S
-gap shift
X_{S}
is the set of all
x=(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that whenever two consecutive symbols
1
occur in
x
, the number of zeros between them belongs to
S
. The point

0^{\infty}=000\cdots

always belongs to
X_{S}
. We write
\sigma:X_{S}\to X_{S}
for the left shift and
\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
for the set of
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measures on
X_{S}
.

For reference, topological mixing of an
S
-gap shift is characterized by

\gcd\{n+1:n\in S\}=1,

with the usual non-degeneracy assumptions; see, for example,
[
16
,
9
]
. This characterization is not used below; the theorem holds without assuming mixing.

Let

E=[1]=\{x\in X_{S}:x_{0}=1\}.

Then

(2)

\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\sigma^{-j}E=X_{S}\setminus\{0^{\infty}\}.

Indeed, a point belongs to the union on the left precisely when it has at least one symbol
1
.

2.2.
The induced renewal shift

Let

Y_{S}=S^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}

with the left shift, again denoted by
\sigma
. Define

\pi:Y_{S}\to E,\qquad\pi(y)=10^{y_{0}}10^{y_{1}}10^{y_{2}}\cdots,

and define the return-time function

\tau(y)=y_{0}+1.

On points of
E
which return to
E
infinitely often, the first return map to
E
is conjugate to
(Y_{S},\sigma)
through
\pi
.

If
\nu\in\M_{\sigma}(Y_{S})
and

m_{\nu}:=\int\tau\,\,d\nu<\infty,

we define its lift
L(\nu)\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
by

(3)

\int f\,\,dL(\nu)=\frac{1}{m_{\nu}}\int\sum_{j=0}^{\tau(y)-1}f(\sigma^{j}\pi(y))\,\,d\nu(y)

for every bounded measurable
f:X_{S}\to\mathbb{R}
. This is the standard tower construction. It gives a
\sigma
-invariant probability measure on
X_{S}
, and it gives no mass to
0^{\infty}
.

2.3.
Inducing measures on
[1]

The following formulation is the one used in the paper. The condition
\mu(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
is essential.

Lemma 2.1

(Kac–Abramov formulas in the present setting)
.

Let
\mu\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
satisfy
\mu(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
. Then
\mu(E)>0
. Let
\nu\in\M_{\sigma}(Y_{S})
be the measure obtained by normalizing
\mu|_{E}
and transporting it to
Y_{S}
through
\pi^{-1}
. Then

m:=\int\tau\,\,d\nu=\frac{1}{\mu(E)},

h_{\mu}(\sigma)=\frac{h_{\nu}(\sigma)}{m},

and, for every bounded measurable
f:X_{S}\to\mathbb{R}
,

(4)

\int f\,\,d\mu=\frac{1}{m}\int F_{f}\,\,d\nu,\qquad F_{f}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\tau(y)-1}f(\sigma^{j}\pi(y)).

Conversely, if
\nu\in\M_{\sigma}(Y_{S})
and
\int\tau\,\,d\nu<\infty
, then
L(\nu)
satisfies these formulas with
m=\int\tau\,\,d\nu
.

Proof.

Since
\mu(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
, (
2
) gives

1=\mu\left(\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\sigma^{-j}E\right).

If
\mu(E)=0
, invariance gives
\mu(\sigma^{-j}E)=0
for every
j
, a contradiction. Hence
\mu(E)>0
. Poincaré recurrence implies that the normalized measure on
E
is supported on points returning to
E
infinitely often. Equivalently, the points with only finitely many symbols
1
have zero
\mu
-measure, since they eventually enter
0^{\infty}
and
\mu(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
. The first return map is then identified with
(Y_{S},\sigma)
by
\pi
. Kac’s formula, Abramov’s entropy formula and the tower integral identity give the displayed formulas; see
[
12
,
2
,
25
,
1
]
. The converse is the standard tower construction.∎

2.4.
Splitting off the fixed point

For arbitrary invariant measures one must first remove the possible atom at
0^{\infty}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Atom splitting)
.

Let
\mu\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
, and set

a:=\mu(\{0^{\infty}\}),\qquad\beta:=1-a.

If
\beta>0
, define

\widetilde{\mu}:=\frac{\mu-a\delta_{0^{\infty}}}{\beta}.

Then
\widetilde{\mu}\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
,
\widetilde{\mu}(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
, and

\mu=a\delta_{0^{\infty}}+\beta\widetilde{\mu}.

Moreover, for every continuous potential
\phi
,

h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\mu=\beta\left(h_{\widetilde{\mu}}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\widetilde{\mu}\right)+a\phi(0^{\infty}).

Consequently, if
P\in\mathbb{R}
and

\Delta_{\phi}(\eta):=P-\left(h_{\eta}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\eta\right),

then

(5)

\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)=a\bigl(P-\phi(0^{\infty})\bigr)+\beta\Delta_{\phi}(\widetilde{\mu})

whenever
\beta>0
.

Proof.

The point
0^{\infty}
is fixed, so removing its atom and renormalizing gives an invariant probability measure when
\beta>0
. The displayed decomposition of the free energy follows from the affinity of measure-theoretic entropy on invariant probability measures and from the linearity of the integral; see
[
25
]
. The formula for the defect is obtained by subtracting the free energy from
P
.∎

3.
Renewal-type potentials

We use the same symbolic metric on
Y_{S}
:

d_{\theta}(y,z)=\theta^{t(y,z)},\qquad t(y,z)=\min\{k\geq 0:y_{k}\neq z_{k}\},

with
d_{\theta}(y,y)=0
. The space
C^{\theta}(Y_{S})
consists of bounded Hölder functions with the corresponding norm.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\theta}(X_{S})
. The induced potential of
\phi
is

\Phi_{\phi}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\tau(y)-1}\phi(\sigma^{j}\pi(y)).

We say that
\phi
is
renewal-type
if there is a function
\psi:S\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\Phi_{\phi}(y)=\psi(y_{0})\qquad(y\in Y_{S}).

In this case
\psi
is called the induced one-symbol potential.

Equivalently, the total weight accumulated along a return block
10^{n}
depends only on
n
. The following examples illustrate the condition and also show that it is a genuine restriction.

3.1.
Examples and nonexamples

Example 3.2
.

The following potentials are typical.

(i)

The zero potential
\phi\equiv 0
is renewal-type with
\psi(n)=0
for all
n\in S
.

(ii)

More generally, suppose that
\phi\in C^{\theta}(X_{S})
and that, for each
n\in S
, there is a constant
c_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\phi(\sigma^{j}x)=c_{n}\qquad\text{for every }x\in[10^{n}1].

Then
\Phi_{\phi}(y)=c_{y_{0}}
, so
\phi
is renewal-type with
\psi(n)=c_{n}
.

(iii)

Fix
0<\rho<\theta
, and define

\phi_{\rho}(x)=\begin{cases}\rho^{n},&x\in[10^{n}1]\text{ for some }n\in S,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Equivalently,
\phi_{\rho}=\sum_{n\in S}\rho^{n}\mathbf{1}_{[10^{n}1]}
, since the cylinders
[10^{n}1]
are disjoint. If
t(x,z)=m\geq 1
and the values of
\phi_{\rho}
at
x
and
z
differ, then any nonzero value involved is at most
\rho^{m-1}
. Hence

|\phi_{\rho}(x)-\phi_{\rho}(z)|\leq 2\rho^{m-1}\leq\frac{2}{\rho}\,\theta^{m},

and
\phi_{\rho}\in C^{\theta}(X_{S})
. Along a first return block, only the initial point begins with
1
, so

\Phi_{\phi_{\rho}}(y)=\phi_{\rho}(\pi(y))=\rho^{y_{0}}.

Thus
\phi_{\rho}
is renewal-type with
\psi(n)=\rho^{n}
.

(iv)

The renewal-type condition is not automatic. Assume that
0\in S
and
S\cap\mathbb{N}\neq\emptyset
. Then
\phi=\mathbf{1}_{[010]}
is not renewal-type. Indeed, for
\pi(y)=10^{y_{0}}10^{y_{1}}\cdots
, the word
010
occurs during the first return block exactly when
y_{0}\geq 1
and
y_{1}\geq 1
. Thus

\Phi_{\phi}(y)=\mathbf{1}_{\{y_{0}\geq 1,\,y_{1}\geq 1\}},

which depends on
y_{1}
as well as
y_{0}
.

The next estimate is the source of the exponential tail of the induced equilibrium distribution.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\theta}(X_{S})
be renewal-type with induced potential
\psi:S\to\mathbb{R}
. Then, for every
n\in S
,

(6)

\left|\psi(n)-(n+1)\phi(0^{\infty})\right|\leq\frac{|\phi|_{\theta}}{1-\theta}.

Consequently, if
P>\phi(0^{\infty})
and

p_{n}=e^{\psi(n)-P(n+1)},

then there is
\eta>0
such that

(7)

\sum_{n\in S}p_{n}e^{\eta(n+1)}<\infty.

In particular,
\sum_{n\in S}(n+1)p_{n}<\infty
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in S
and choose
y\in Y_{S}
with
y_{0}=n
. Since
\phi
is renewal-type,

\psi(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\phi(\sigma^{j}\pi(y)).

For
j=0
, the distance from
\pi(y)
to
0^{\infty}
is at most
1
. For
1\leq j\leq n
, the point
\sigma^{j}\pi(y)
begins with
n+1-j
zeros before the next
1
, hence

d_{\theta}(\sigma^{j}\pi(y),0^{\infty})\leq\theta^{n+1-j}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left|\psi(n)-(n+1)\phi(0^{\infty})\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\phi|_{\theta}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta^{n+1-j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\phi|_{\theta}}{1-\theta}.

If
\eta=(P-\phi(0^{\infty}))/2
, then (
6
) gives

p_{n}e^{\eta(n+1)}\leq\exp\left(\frac{|\phi|_{\theta}}{1-\theta}\right)e^{-\eta(n+1)}.

The right-hand side is summable over
S\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
, proving (
7
).∎

Lemma 3.4

(The renewal equation)
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\theta}(X_{S})
be renewal-type with induced one-symbol potential
\psi:S\to\mathbb{R}
. The equation

(8)

\sum_{n\in S}e^{\psi(n)-P(n+1)}=1

has a unique real solution
P
. If
S
is infinite, then this solution satisfies

P>\phi(0^{\infty}).

Proof.

Set

Z(t)=\sum_{n\in S}e^{\psi(n)-t(n+1)}.

For finite
S
, the function
Z
is continuous and strictly decreasing on
\mathbb{R}
, with
Z(t)\to\infty
as
t\to-\infty
and
Z(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Hence there is a unique
P\in\mathbb{R}
with
Z(P)=1
.

Assume now that
S
is infinite. Let
B=|\phi|_{\theta}/(1-\theta)
. By
section

3.1
,

e^{-B}e^{-(t-\phi(0^{\infty}))(n+1)}\leq e^{\psi(n)-t(n+1)}\leq e^{B}e^{-(t-\phi(0^{\infty}))(n+1)}.

Thus
Z(t)<\infty
for
t>\phi(0^{\infty})
, while
Z(t)=\infty
for
t\leq\phi(0^{\infty})
. On
(\phi(0^{\infty}),\infty)
, the function
Z
is continuous and strictly decreasing. Also
Z(t)\to\infty
as
t\downarrow\phi(0^{\infty})
, and
Z(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Hence there is a unique solution of
Z(P)=1
, and it satisfies
P>\phi(0^{\infty})
.∎

Let
P
denote the unique solution of (
8
). Throughout the rest of the paper we assume the pressure separation condition

(9)

P>\phi(0^{\infty}).

By
section

3.1
, this condition is automatic when
S
is infinite. Define

(10)

p_{n}=e^{\psi(n)-P(n+1)},\qquad n\in S,

and let
\nu_{\phi}
be the Bernoulli measure on
Y_{S}
with one-coordinate distribution
p=(p_{n})_{n\in S}
. By
section

3.1
,

\bar{\tau}_{\phi}:=\int\tau\,\,d\nu_{\phi}=\sum_{n\in S}(n+1)p_{n}<\infty.

Thus the lifted measure

(11)

\mu_{\phi}:=L(\nu_{\phi})

is well defined.

4.
The equilibrium state

We begin with a countable-alphabet entropy estimate. For a probability vector
q=(q_{n})_{n\in S}
, write

H(q)=-\sum_{n\in S}q_{n}\log q_{n}

with the convention
0\log 0=0
, and write

D(q\|p)=\sum_{n\in S}q_{n}\log\frac{q_{n}}{p_{n}}

for relative entropy.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
q=(q_{n})_{n\in S}
be a probability vector such that

\sum_{n\in S}q_{n}(n+1)<\infty.

Then
H(q)<\infty
,
\sum_{n}q_{n}|\psi(n)|<\infty
, and

(12)

D(q\|p)=-H(q)-\sum_{n\in S}q_{n}\psi(n)+P\sum_{n\in S}q_{n}(n+1).

In particular,

(13)

H(q)+\sum_{n\in S}q_{n}\psi(n)\leq P\sum_{n\in S}q_{n}(n+1),

with equality if and only if
q=p
.

Proof.

The finite first moment implies finite entropy by comparison with an exponential distribution. Indeed, for any
a>0
, the partition function
Z_{a}=\sum_{n\in S}e^{-a(n+1)}
is finite, and the Gibbs inequality gives

H(q)\leq a\sum_{n\in S}q_{n}(n+1)+\log Z_{a}<\infty.

Since
\phi
is bounded and
\psi(n)
is a sum of
n+1
values of
\phi
,

|\psi(n)|\leq(n+1)\left\|\phi\right\|_{\infty},

so
\sum_{n}q_{n}|\psi(n)|<\infty
. Finally,
\log p_{n}=\psi(n)-P(n+1)
, and therefore

D(q\|p)=\sum_{n}q_{n}\log q_{n}-\sum_{n}q_{n}\log p_{n},

which is exactly (
12
). The inequality and equality case are the standard properties of relative entropy.∎

Proposition 4.2
.

The measure
\mu_{\phi}=L(\nu_{\phi})
is the unique equilibrium state of
\phi
on
X_{S}
, and

P_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S},\sigma,\phi)=P.

Proof.

First consider an invariant measure
\eta\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
with
\eta(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
. Let
\nu\in\M_{\sigma}(Y_{S})
be its induced measure, and put

m=\int\tau\,\,d\nu.

By
section

2.3
,

h_{\eta}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\eta=\frac{1}{m}\left(h_{\nu}(\sigma)+\int\psi(y_{0})\,\,d\nu\right).

Let
q
be the one-coordinate marginal of
\nu
. Then
\sum_{n}q_{n}(n+1)=m<\infty
. Since entropy rate is bounded above by one-symbol entropy,

h_{\nu}(\sigma)\leq H(q),

and
section

4
gives

H(q)+\int\psi(y_{0})\,\,d\nu\leq Pm.

Therefore

(14)

h_{\eta}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\eta\leq P.

Now let
\mu\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
be arbitrary. If
\mu=\delta_{0^{\infty}}
, then

h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\mu=\phi(0^{\infty})<P.

Otherwise, apply
section

2.4
. Writing
\mu=a\delta_{0^{\infty}}+\beta\widetilde{\mu}
,
\beta>0
, we have

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\mu

\displaystyle=a\phi(0^{\infty})+\beta\left(h_{\widetilde{\mu}}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\widetilde{\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\leq a\phi(0^{\infty})+\beta P\leq P.

Thus every invariant measure has free energy at most
P
.

For the lifted Bernoulli measure
\mu_{\phi}=L(\nu_{\phi})
,
section

2.3
applies and

h_{\nu_{\phi}}(\sigma)=H(p).

Using
\log p_{n}=\psi(n)-P(n+1)
, we get

H(p)+\sum_{n\in S}p_{n}\psi(n)=P\sum_{n\in S}p_{n}(n+1)=P\bar{\tau}_{\phi}.

Hence

h_{\mu_{\phi}}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\mu_{\phi}=P.

The variational principle now gives
P_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S},\sigma,\phi)=P
, and
\mu_{\phi}
is an equilibrium state.

It remains to prove uniqueness. Let
\mu
be an equilibrium state, and set
a=\mu(\{0^{\infty}\})
. The atom-splitting formula and
P>\phi(0^{\infty})
force

, so
\mu(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
. Let
\nu
be the induced measure and let
q
be its one-coordinate marginal. Equality in the preceding upper bound implies both

and

We spell out the last consequence. Since

, the entropy rate of the stationary coordinate process satisfies

h_{\nu}(\sigma)=\lim_{k\to\infty}H_{\nu}(y_{k}\mid y_{0},\ldots,y_{k-1}),

and the conditional entropies form a non-increasing sequence bounded above by

; see
[

11
]
. If the limit is

, then every conditional entropy equals
H(q)
. Hence
y_{k}
is independent of
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{k-1})
for every

is the Bernoulli measure with marginal

, i.e.
\nu=\nu_{\phi}
. Therefore

5.
The pressure-gap estimate on the induced shift

For
\nu\in\M_{\sigma}(Y_{S})
with
\int\tau\,\,d\nu<\infty
, define the induced pressure defect by

(15)

\Delta_{Y}(\nu)=P\int\tau\,\,d\nu-h_{\nu}(\sigma)-\int\psi(y_{0})\,\,d\nu.

By the proof of
section

4
,
\Delta_{Y}(\nu)\geq 0
.

We use the convention

\TV(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\in S}|q_{n}-p_{n}|.

Pinsker’s inequality says

\TV(q,p)\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}D(q\|p)}.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every
H\in C^{\theta}(Y_{S})
and every
\nu\in\M_{\sigma}(Y_{S})
with
\int\tau\,\,d\nu<\infty
,

\left|\int H\,\,d\nu-\int H\,\,d\nu_{\phi}\right|\leq\frac{|H|_{\theta}}{\sqrt{2}(1-\theta)}\sqrt{\Delta_{Y}(\nu)}.

Proof.

For
n\geq 0
, define

(T_{n}H)(y)=\int H(y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1},z_{0},z_{1},\ldots)\,\,d\nu_{\phi}(z),

where for
n=0
the list
y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1}
is empty. Thus
T_{0}H=\int H\,\,d\nu_{\phi}
. Since two points which agree in their first
n
coordinates are at distance at most
\theta^{n}
, we have
T_{n}H\to H
uniformly. Hence

\int H\,\,d\nu-\int H\,\,d\nu_{\phi}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int(T_{n+1}H-T_{n}H)\,\,d\nu,

and the series is absolutely convergent.

Fix
n\geq 0
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{n}
be the sigma-algebra generated by
y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1}
, with
\mathcal{F}_{0}
trivial. Let
Q_{n}(\cdot\mid y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})
be a regular conditional distribution of
y_{n}
with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{n}
. For a prefix
a=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})
and
b\in S
, put

\Phi_{n}(a,b)=\int H(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1},b,z_{0},z_{1},\ldots)\,\,d\nu_{\phi}(z).

Then

\displaystyle\int(T_{n+1}H-T_{n}H)\,\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int\sum_{b\in S}\Phi_{n}(y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1},b)

\displaystyle\cdot\bigl(Q_{n}(b\mid y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})-p_{b}\bigr)\,\,d\nu.

For each fixed prefix
a
, the function
b\mapsto\Phi_{n}(a,b)
has oscillation at most
|H|_{\theta}\theta^{n}
. Since
Q_{n}(\cdot\mid a)-p
has total mass zero,

\left|\sum_{b\in S}\Phi_{n}(a,b)\bigl(Q_{n}(b\mid a)-p_{b}\bigr)\right|\leq|H|_{\theta}\theta^{n}\TV(Q_{n}(\cdot\mid a),p).

Pinsker’s inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz therefore give

\left|\int(T_{n+1}H-T_{n}H)\,\,d\nu\right|\leq\frac{|H|_{\theta}\theta^{n}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\int D(Q_{n}(\cdot\mid y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})\|p)\,\,d\nu\right)^{1/2}.

It remains to bound the averaged conditional relative entropy. Since
\int\tau\,\,d\nu<\infty
, the conditional first moment of
Q_{n}(\cdot\mid y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})
is finite for
\nu
-almost every prefix. Applying
section

4
conditionally and integrating gives

\displaystyle\int D(Q_{n}(\cdot\mid y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})\|p)\,\,d\nu

\displaystyle=P\int\tau(y_{n})\,\,d\nu-H_{\nu}(y_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n})

\displaystyle-\int\psi(y_{n})\,\,d\nu.

By stationarity,
\int\tau(y_{n})\,\,d\nu=\int\tau\,\,d\nu
and
\int\psi(y_{n})\,\,d\nu=\int\psi(y_{0})\,\,d\nu
. Also

H_{\nu}(y_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n})\geq h_{\nu}(\sigma),

because the conditional entropies decrease to the entropy rate; see
[
25
,
11
]
. Therefore

\int D(Q_{n}(\cdot\mid y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})\|p)\,\,d\nu\leq\Delta_{Y}(\nu).

Consequently,

\left|\int(T_{n+1}H-T_{n}H)\,\,d\nu\right|\leq\frac{|H|_{\theta}\theta^{n}}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\Delta_{Y}(\nu)}.

Summing over
n\geq 0
proves the theorem.∎

6.1.
The bounded remainder of an induced observable

For

, set

Lemma 6.1
.

There is a constant
C_{\theta}>0
, depending only on
\theta
, such that for every

,

\left\|G_{f}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\theta}|f|_{\theta},\qquad|G_{f}|_{\theta}\leq C_{\theta}|f|_{\theta}.

In particular,
\left\|G_{f}\right\|_{\theta}\leq C_{\theta}\left\|f\right\|_{\theta}
.

Proof.

Let
r=\tau(y)=y_{0}+1
. The point
\pi(y)
may differ from

at the first coordinate, and for
1\leq j\leq r-1
, the point
\sigma^{j}\pi(y)
begins with
r-j
zeros. Hence

|G_{f}(y)|\leq|f|_{\theta}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\theta^{r-j}\right)\leq\frac{|f|_{\theta}}{1-\theta}.

Now let
y,z\in Y_{S}
and suppose that
t(y,z)=n\geq 1
. Then
y_{i}=z_{i}
for
0\leq i<n
. In particular
\tau(y)=\tau(z)
, and
\pi(y)
and
\pi(z)
agree for at least

L_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(y_{i}+1)\geq n

coordinates. For

,

|f(\sigma^{j}\pi(y))-f(\sigma^{j}\pi(z))|\leq|f|_{\theta}\theta^{L_{n}-j}.

Since
L_{n}\geq\tau(y)+n-1
, summing in
j
yields

|G_{f}(y)-G_{f}(z)|\leq\frac{|f|_{\theta}}{1-\theta}\theta^{n}.

The case

follows from the sup norm bound.∎

If

be its induced measure and put

6.3.
Controlling the return-time average

We need one elementary estimate on the original system. Let

B_{\phi}=\frac{|\phi|_{\theta}}{1-\theta}.

Lemma 6.3
.

For every
\mu\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
,

\int\phi\,\,d\mu\leq\phi(0^{\infty})+B_{\phi}\mu([1]).

Proof.

Let
N(x)=\min\{k\geq 0:x_{k}=1\}
, with
N(0^{\infty})=\infty
. Then

d_{\theta}(x,0^{\infty})\leq\theta^{N(x)},

where
\theta^{\infty}=0
. Hence

\phi(x)\leq\phi(0^{\infty})+|\phi|_{\theta}\theta^{N(x)}.

Since
\{N=k\}\subset\{x_{k}=1\}=\sigma^{-k}[1]
, invariance gives

\int\theta^{N(x)}\,\,d\mu(x)\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\theta^{k}\mu(\sigma^{-k}[1])=\frac{\mu([1])}{1-\theta}.

The result follows.∎

Lemma 6.4
.

There are constants
\delta_{0}>0
,
C_{\tau}>0
, and
M_{0}>0
, depending only on
S,\phi
and
\theta
, with the following property. If
\mu\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
,
\mu(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
, and
\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)\leq\delta_{0}
, then for the induced measure
\nu
of
\mu
,

\left|\int\tau\,\,d\nu-\bar{\tau}_{\phi}\right|\leq C_{\tau}\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}

and

\int\tau\,\,d\nu\leq M_{0}.

Proof.

Write

m=\int\tau\,\,d\nu,\qquad p_{\mu}=\mu([1]).

Since
\mu(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
, Kac’s formula gives
m=1/p_{\mu}
.

We first show that
m
is uniformly bounded when the pressure defect is small. Let

\gamma=P-\phi(0^{\infty})>0.

For a stationary binary process with
\mu([1])=p_{\mu}
,

h_{\mu}(\sigma)\leq H_{\mathrm{bin}}(p_{\mu}),

where
H_{\mathrm{bin}}(u)=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u)
. By
section

6.3
,

h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\mu\leq\phi(0^{\infty})+H_{\mathrm{bin}}(p_{\mu})+B_{\phi}p_{\mu}.

Choose
p_{*}>0
so small that

H_{\mathrm{bin}}(u)+B_{\phi}u<\frac{\gamma}{2}\qquad(0\leq u<p_{*}).

If
\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)\leq\gamma/2
, then the free energy is at least
\phi(0^{\infty})+\gamma/2
, so necessarily
p_{\mu}\geq p_{*}
. Thus

(18)

m\leq M_{*}:=p_{*}^{-1}

whenever
\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)\leq\gamma/2
.

Let
q
be the one-coordinate marginal of
\nu
. Then

m=\sum_{n\in S}q_{n}(n+1).

Since
H(q)\geq h_{\nu}(\sigma)
,
section

4
and
section

6.2
give

(19)

D(q\|p)\leq\Delta_{Y}(\nu)=m\Delta_{\phi}(\mu).

If
\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)\leq\gamma/2
, then (
18
) gives

(20)

D(q\|p)\leq M_{*}\Delta_{\phi}(\mu).

It remains to convert relative entropy into control of the first moment. By
section

3.1
, there is
\eta>0
such that
\sum_{n}p_{n}e^{\eta(n+1)}<\infty
. Define

M(t)=\sum_{n\in S}p_{n}e^{t((n+1)-\bar{\tau}_{\phi})},\qquad\Lambda(t)=\log M(t).

Then
M(t)<\infty
for
|t|\leq\eta/2
, and
M
is twice continuously differentiable on this interval. Since
M(0)=1
and
M^{\prime}(0)=0
, there are
t_{0}>0
and
A>0
, depending only on
S,\phi,\theta
, such that

(21)

\Lambda(t)\leq At^{2}\qquad(|t|\leq t_{0}).

For
|t|\leq t_{0}
, set

p_{n}^{(t)}=e^{-\Lambda(t)}p_{n}e^{t((n+1)-\bar{\tau}_{\phi})}.

Then
(p_{n}^{(t)})
is a probability vector. Since
D(q\|p^{(t)})\geq 0
,

t(m-\bar{\tau}_{\phi})\leq D(q\|p)+\Lambda(t)

for
0<t\leq t_{0}
, and applying the same inequality with
-t
gives

|m-\bar{\tau}_{\phi}|\leq\frac{D(q\|p)}{t}+At\qquad(0<t\leq t_{0}).

Choose

\delta_{0}=\min\left\{\frac{\gamma}{2},\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{M_{*}}\right\}.

If
\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)\leq\delta_{0}
, then (
20
) gives
D(q\|p)\leq t_{0}^{2}
. Taking
t=\sqrt{D(q\|p)}
when
D(q\|p)>0
, and arguing trivially when
D(q\|p)=0
, we obtain

|m-\bar{\tau}_{\phi}|\leq(1+A)\sqrt{D(q\|p)}\leq(1+A)\sqrt{M_{*}}\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}.

This proves the first estimate. The second estimate follows from (
18
); set
M_{0}=M_{*}
.∎

6.4.
The estimate for measures without the fixed-point atom

Proposition 6.5
.

There is a constant
C_{0}>0
, depending only on
S,\phi
and
\theta
, such that for every
\mu\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
satisfying
\mu(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
and every
f\in C^{\theta}(X_{S})
,

\left|\int f\,\,d\mu-\int f\,\,d\mu_{\phi}\right|\leq C_{0}\left\|f\right\|_{\theta}\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}.

Proof.

Let
\delta_{0},C_{\tau},M_{0}
be as in
section

6.3
. If
\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)\geq\delta_{0}
, then

\left|\int f\,\,d\mu-\int f\,\,d\mu_{\phi}\right|\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\leq 2\delta_{0}^{-1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{\theta}\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}.

Assume now that
\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)<\delta_{0}
. Let
\nu
be the induced measure of
\mu
, and set
m=\int\tau\,\,d\nu
. By (
16
) and (
17
),

\left|\int f\,\,d\mu-\int f\,\,d\mu_{\phi}\right|\leq\left|\frac{1}{m}\int G_{f}\,\,d\nu-\frac{1}{\bar{\tau}_{\phi}}\int G_{f}\,\,d\nu_{\phi}\right|.

Thus

\left|\int f\,\,d\mu-\int f\,\,d\mu_{\phi}\right|\leq\frac{1}{m}\left|\int G_{f}\,\,d\nu-\int G_{f}\,\,d\nu_{\phi}\right|+\left\|G_{f}\right\|_{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{\bar{\tau}_{\phi}}\right|.

Since
m\geq 1
,
theorems

5.1
,
6.2
,
6.1
and
6.3
give

\displaystyle\left|\int G_{f}\,\,d\nu-\int G_{f}\,\,d\nu_{\phi}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|G_{f}|_{\theta}}{\sqrt{2}(1-\theta)}\sqrt{\Delta_{Y}(\nu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|G_{f}|_{\theta}}{\sqrt{2}(1-\theta)}\sqrt{m\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left\|f\right\|_{\theta}\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)},

where
m\leq M_{0}
has been used in the last line. Also,

\left|\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{\bar{\tau}_{\phi}}\right|=\frac{|m-\bar{\tau}_{\phi}|}{m\bar{\tau}_{\phi}}\leq\frac{C_{\tau}}{\bar{\tau}_{\phi}}\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}.

Together with
\left\|G_{f}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\theta}\left\|f\right\|_{\theta}

6.5.
Proof of the main theorem

Proof of
theorem

1.1
.

The pressure identity and uniqueness of
\mu_{\phi}
were proved in
section

4
. It remains only to prove the effective estimate for an arbitrary invariant measure
\mu
.

Set

a=\mu(\{0^{\infty}\}),\qquad\beta=1-a,\qquad\gamma=P-\phi(0^{\infty})>0.

If
\beta=0
, then
\mu=\delta_{0^{\infty}}
and
\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)=\gamma
, so

\left|\int f\,\,d\mu-\int f\,\,d\mu_{\phi}\right|\leq 2\gamma^{-1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{\theta}\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}.

Assume
\beta>0
, and let
\widetilde{\mu}
be as in
section

2.4
. Then
\widetilde{\mu}(\{0^{\infty}\})=0
, and
section

6.4
gives

\left|\int f\,\,d\widetilde{\mu}-\int f\,\,d\mu_{\phi}\right|\leq C_{0}\left\|f\right\|_{\theta}\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\widetilde{\mu})}.

By (
5
),

\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)=a\gamma+\beta\Delta_{\phi}(\widetilde{\mu}).

Therefore

a\leq\frac{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}{\gamma},\qquad\beta\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\widetilde{\mu})}=\sqrt{\beta(\beta\Delta_{\phi}(\widetilde{\mu}))}\leq\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\int f\,\,d\mu-\int f\,\,d\mu_{\phi}\right|

7.
Cylinder-avoiding sets and dimension

Throughout this section we assume the hypotheses of
theorem

1.1
. In particular
P=P_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S},\sigma,\phi)
, and
\mu_{\phi}
denotes the unique equilibrium state.

Let
C=[w]\subset X_{S}
be a cylinder determined by an admissible word
w
of length
\ell\geq 1
. Define

\mathcal{E}_{C}=\{x\in X_{S}:\sigma^{k}x\notin C\text{ for every }k\geq 0\}.

This is a compact forward-invariant set. If
\mu\in\M_{\sigma}(X_{S})
and
\mu(\mathcal{E}_{C})=1
, then
\mu(C)=0
.

Lemma 7.1
.

The cylinder indicator
\mathbf{1}_{C}
belongs to
C^{\theta}(X_{S})
, and

\left\|\mathbf{1}_{C}\right\|_{\theta}\leq 1+\theta^{-(\ell-1)}.

Proof.

The sup norm is
1
. If
\mathbf{1}_{C}(x)\neq\mathbf{1}_{C}(y)
, then
x
and
y
differ in one of the first
\ell
coordinates. Hence
d_{\theta}(x,y)\geq\theta^{\ell-1}
, and the Hölder seminorm is at most
\theta^{-(\ell-1)}
.∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Assume
\mu_{\phi}(C)>0
. Then every invariant measure
\mu
supported on
\mathcal{E}_{C}
satisfies

P-\left(h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi\,\,d\mu\right)\geq\kappa_{C},

where, if
C_{\mathrm{eff}}
denotes the constant from
theorem

1.1
,

\kappa_{C}=\frac{\mu_{\phi}(C)^{2}}{C_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{1}_{C}\right\|_{\theta}^{2}}.

Consequently,

P_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{E}_{C},\sigma,\phi)\leq P-\kappa_{C},

where the pressure on
\mathcal{E}_{C}
is computed using invariant measures supported on
\mathcal{E}_{C}
.

Proof.

If
\mu(\mathcal{E}_{C})=1
, then
\mu(C)=0
. Applying
theorem

1.1
to
f=\mathbf{1}_{C}
, we get

\mu_{\phi}(C)=\left|\int\mathbf{1}_{C}\,\,d\mu-\int\mathbf{1}_{C}\,\,d\mu_{\phi}\right|\leq C_{\mathrm{eff}}\left\|\mathbf{1}_{C}\right\|_{\theta}\sqrt{\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)}.

This gives the lower bound on
\Delta_{\phi}(\mu)
. Taking the supremum over all invariant measures supported on
\mathcal{E}_{C}
gives the pressure estimate.∎

In the entropy case
\phi=0
, the pressure separation condition is
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})>0
. Under this assumption
\mu_{0}:=\mu_{\phi}
is the unique measure of maximal entropy. Writing
h=h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})
,
section

7
gives

(22)

h_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{E}_{C})\leq h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})-\kappa_{C}

whenever
\mu_{0}(C)>0
and
\mathcal{E}_{C}
is nonempty. If
\mathcal{E}_{C}
is empty, the pressure and entropy estimates are trivial; in the Hausdorff-dimension statement below we use a slightly smaller positive gap to cover both cases without relying on a convention for the entropy of the empty set.

For the dimension estimate we use cylinder-covering entropy. This is the symbolic form of a standard entropy-dimension estimate; see, for example, Pesin’s text on dimension theory
[
17
]
. For compact
Z\subset X_{S}
, let
N_{n}(Z)
be the least number of length-
n
cylinders needed to cover
Z
, and set

h_{\mathrm{cyl}}(Z)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log N_{n}(Z).

For compact shift-invariant subsets of a one-sided subshift, this agrees with the usual topological entropy.

Lemma 7.3
.

For every compact
Z\subset X_{S}
,

\dim_{H}(Z,d_{\theta})\leq\frac{h_{\mathrm{cyl}}(Z)}{-\log\theta}.

Proof.

Let
h_{Z}=h_{\mathrm{cyl}}(Z)
, and choose
s>h_{Z}/(-\log\theta)
. For every small
\varepsilon>0
and all large
n
, the set
Z
can be covered by at most
\exp(n(h_{Z}+\varepsilon))
cylinders of length
n
. Each such cylinder has diameter at most
\theta^{n}
. Thus the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff content is bounded by

\exp(n(h_{Z}+\varepsilon))\theta^{ns}.

Choose
\varepsilon>0
so that
h_{Z}+\varepsilon+s\log\theta<0
, and let
n\to\infty
. This gives
\mathcal{H}^{s}(Z)=0
.∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Assume
\phi=0
and
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})>0
. Let
C\subset X_{S}
be a cylinder with
\mu_{0}(C)>0
, and set

\widehat{\kappa}_{C}=\min\left\{\kappa_{C},\frac{1}{2}h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})\right\}.

Then
\widehat{\kappa}_{C}>0
and

\dim_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{C},d_{\theta})\leq\frac{h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})-\widehat{\kappa}_{C}}{-\log\theta}<\frac{h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})}{-\log\theta}.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{E}_{C}=\emptyset
, then
\dim_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{C},d_{\theta})=0
, and the displayed bound is immediate because
\widehat{\kappa}_{C}\leq h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})/2
. If
\mathcal{E}_{C}
is nonempty, then it is compact and shift-invariant, so
h_{\mathrm{cyl}}(\mathcal{E}_{C})=h_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{E}_{C})
. By (

),

h_{\mathrm{cyl}}(\mathcal{E}_{C})\leq h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})-\kappa_{C}\leq h_{\mathrm{top}}(X_{S})-\widehat{\kappa}_{C}.

The claim follows from
section

7
.∎

8.
Final remarks

The main result gives an effective intrinsic ergodicity for renewal-type potentials on one-sided
S
-gap shifts. The estimate is proved on the original compact symbolic system, not only on the induced countable renewal shift. This distinction is important: the passage from the induced system back to
X_{S}
changes both observables and pressure defects.

The proof has two main ingredients. First, the square-root pressure-gap inequality on the induced renewal full shift is obtained directly from conditional relative entropy and a telescoping decomposition of Hölder observables. In the renewal-type setting, the induced potential is one-symbol and the induced equilibrium measure is Bernoulli, so this part of the argument does not require spectral theory or the general theory of strongly positively recurrent countable Markov shifts.

Second, the transfer from the induced system to
X_{S}
requires more than a formal lifting argument. If
f\in C^{\theta}(X_{S})
, then the induced observable

F_{f}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\tau(y)-1}f(\sigma^{j}\pi(y))

is generally unbounded because of the roof term. The decomposition

F_{f}=f(0^{\infty})\tau+G_{f}

separates this unbounded part from a bounded Hölder remainder. The return-time average is then controlled by a pressure-gap estimate, using the exponential tail of the induced Bernoulli weights. The atom-splitting step is also essential: Kac–Abramov formulas are applied only after removing the possible fixed-point atom at
0^{\infty}
.

The cylinder-avoidance application shows one concrete consequence of the effective estimate. If an orbit avoids a fixed cylinder of positive equilibrium measure, then the corresponding exceptional set has a uniform pressure drop. In the entropy case this gives a strict entropy drop and hence a strict Hausdorff-dimension upper bound in the symbolic metric.

Two natural questions remain. One is whether the renewal-type assumption can be replaced by a broader class of Hölder potentials for which the induced equilibrium measure is no longer Bernoulli. Another is whether the same atom-splitting and return-time control can be adapted to other coded symbolic systems with a renewal-type inducing scheme.
