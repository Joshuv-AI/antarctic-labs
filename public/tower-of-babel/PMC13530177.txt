A novel approach for forecasting algal bloom: long short-term memory artificial neural network with exponential smoothing feedback optimized by the arithmetic mean algorithm
Authors: Hakan Isık, Erol Egrioglu, Eren Bas, Buse Eraslan Akkan, Tamer Akkan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractArtificial intelligence-based methods have gained increasing attention for sustainable environmental monitoring due to their efficiency and low operational cost. In this study, a new long short-term memory with an exponential smoothing method is proposed, and an arithmetic mean optimization algorithm-based training strategy is introduced for the proposed neural networks. The resulting proposed neural network method is used to forecast chlorophyll-a concentrations, a key indicator of algal bloom dynamics, using satellite-derived time series data from 15 estuarine monitoring stations in the Black Sea region. To evaluate the effectiveness of this framework, comprehensive experiments are conducted by comparing the proposed neural network with several shallow and deep learning models. The results show that the proposed neural network model achieves superior forecasting performance in terms of root mean square error and mean absolute percentage error across the majority of the analysed time series. Furthermore, statistical significance analyses based on the Friedman and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm that the observed performance improvements are statistically significant. The proposed neural network model attains the best overall ranking with a mean rank of 1.06 and significantly outperforms all competing models under the Holm–Bonferroni correction. The findings indicate that the proposed neural network improves both forecasting accuracy and robustness, providing a reliable and effective framework for modelling chlorophyll-a dynamics. This approach offers a promising tool for the early detection and management of algal bloom events in marine ecosystems.Keywords: Artificial neural networks, Long short term memory, Arithmetic mean optimization algorithm, Forecasting, Exponential smoothing, Chlorophyll-aSubject terms: Climate sciences, Ecology, Ecology, Environmental sciences, Ocean sciences

Introduction

The development of marine pollution has extended throughout the history of human civilization, from its beginnings to the present day. However, seawater pollution has not been given much importance until it reaches a level that can create adverse consequences for ecosystems and organisms, known as the threshold level. The Black Sea ecosystem, which is characterized by high ecological sensitivity and significant socioeconomic importance, is under enormous pressure from various human activities in its surroundings. Recently, manifold phenomena of eutrophication and hypoxia have caused turbidity in the aquatic environment and the die-off of fauna1. Different regions have observed harmful algal blooms (HABs) and eutrophication, characterized by longer durations and varying toxicities2. HABs lead to serious socioecological, economic, food safety, and public health problems in countries where aquaculture, fishing, and tourism depend on marine ecosystem products and services3. This emerging threat poses a significant risk to public health, ecosystems, and the primary economy based on fishing4. To identify the eutrophication status, the content of chlorophyll a of algae in the water column was compared with other variables, including phosphorus and nitrogen content and transparency. For this reason, it’s crucial to continuously monitor the environmental and ecological indicators of the Black Sea. Monitoring the environmental and ecological parameters of the Black Sea is crucial for identifying and assessing adverse effects on the environment, as well as for implementing preservation and protection measures. In our research, we utilized the Copernicus Marine Environment Monitoring Service (CMEMS) as the sole online tool to obtain chlorophyll-a (Chl-a) concentration data for 365 days from satellite observations. The presence of numerous river outlets that affect the Black Sea ecosystem allows us to gather information about Chl-a in areas where they discharge into the sea. In this article, we combine artificial neural network modelling with remote sensing technology to enhance the ability to predict algal blooms and forecast the increase in Chl-a concentrations. This will offer an effective solution for managing algal blooms in a sustainable manner. Furthermore, it will remove the carbon footprint from laboratory analyses and propose a green, economical, and sustainable methodology.

Recent advances in artificial intelligence have facilitated the development of data-driven forecasting models capable of capturing nonlinear temporal dependencies in environmental time series. In particular, recurrent neural networks, such as long short-term memory (LSTM) models, have demonstrated strong performance in modelling complex temporal patterns. Although the connections formed between cells in deep neural networks based on LSTM cells, according to inter-cell time steps, constitute a feedback mechanism, it is known that feedback structures of the exponential smoothing type, which have a significantly different architecture, improve forecasting performance. In this study, a simple exponential smoothing feedback mechanism was integrated into the fully connected layer of the network, enabling the LSTM neural network to possess a feedback structure consistent with classical time series theory. The training of such deep models is often sensitive to initialization and may suffer from issues such as local minima and unstable convergence when conventional gradient-based optimization (GBO) algorithms are employed. For training the long short-term memory with exponential smoothing (LSTM-EXP) model, the arithmetic mean optimization algorithm (AMOA) was preferred over genetic algorithms and particle swarm optimization, as it requires fewer parameters and reduces the burden of parameter tuning. Consequently, training can be performed without the need for parameter tuning, such as determining inertia weights, social, and cognitive coefficients in particle swarm optimization; setting crossover and mutation rates along with the selection mechanism in genetic algorithms; or specifying the type of mutation operator and crossover rate in the differential evolution algorithm. Besides introducing a fundamentally new model, the proposed framework also focuses on enhancing training behaviour and evaluating its impact on forecasting performance. The study utilizes daily Chl-a concentration data obtained from fifteen estuarine monitoring stations in the Black Sea for the period 2021–2022.

Literature review

Chl-a concentration is a highly significant parameter commonly used in observing water quality in coastal waters5. Eutrophication is currently one of the most serious water quality issues in estuaries and coastal waters in many countries. Understanding and modeling the level of eutrophication (Chl-a) can contribute to the ecosystem management of estuaries6. Given the significant role of eutrophication, accurate prediction of the coastal ocean Chl-a concentrations is highly important for water quality monitoring7. Early detection of Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs) using data-driven models is promising for future intervention against HABs. However, ensuring model reliability requires high-frequency water quality observation data, which can be costly and challenging8. Phytoplankton productivity can be obtained from remote sensing data through satellite-based measurements of Chl-a, which is an indicator for productivity9. The satellite-based measurements of Chl-a concentrations significantly contribute to phytoplankton research10. For this purpose, the Copernicus Marine Environment Monitoring Service (CMEMS) and the Ocean State Report (OSR) provide an annual report on the current state of global oceans and European regional seas for decision-makers and practitioners. Additionally, they aim to raise public awareness about the state of marine environments and related changes11. The Copernicus Marine Environment Monitoring Service (CMEMS) demonstrates high performance in predicting and observing physical and biogeochemical developments in the Black Sea, both technically and scientifically12. In the last two decades, numerous studies have utilized satellite-derived Chl-a datasets to determine the temporal and spatial distributions of phytoplankton biomass13–16. To investigate how the COVID-19 quarantine might affect phytoplankton formation processes, variability in chlorophyll-a concentration in the northwest Arabian/Persian Gulf (Kuwait Gulf) was studied using the Sentinel OLCI remote sensing instrument between 2018 and 2020 and compared to the current situation15. Another study aimed to observe algal blooms detected through Copernicus’ S3 and S2 satellites with medium and high spatial resolutions and evaluate their capabilities in mapping the spread and location of HABs (Harmful Algal Blooms) during the COVID-19 quarantine period17.

It has been confirmed that machine learning models can model complex relationships in intricate structures and provide more reliable predictions for inferred parameters compared to traditional empirical models18. Predicting the behaviour of algae is difficult due to their complex physical, chemical, and biological reasons. Artificial neural network models are beneficial and efficient, especially for problems where describing the intricacies through numerical models is challenging19. ANN has been applied multiple times in the last twenty years for predicting algal blooms and chlorophyll dynamics20. Studies using the artificial neural network method can be found in the literature. In a study by Park et al.21, the usability of machine learning models was evaluated to examine their effect on predicting the alert level of algal blooms based on environmental variables such as nutrients and meteorological factors in a freshwater reservoir. To predict Chl-a concentration in the coastal ocean of Yangjiang, China, machine learning (ML) or deep learning (DL) models were used. The Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) model outperformed the DL model significantly in terms of stability and robustness7. In this study, instead of using numerous monitoring methods, the Environmental Fluid Dynamics Code (EFDC) was applied to extend single-point data obtained with a single device to the entire 249-hectare water area. Subsequently, LSTM was used to predict HABs in the entire water mass. Correlation analysis and principal component analysis were used to reduce data dimensions and improve model accuracy8. In another study, an ANN and genetic algorithm (GA)-based hybrid model was presented to assess the seasonal and spatial variability of chlorophyll-a concentration in coastal waters of Sinpho Bay using MODIS data22. Karul et al.23 developed a Chl-a model using both ANN and linear regression methods to predict chlorophyll-a levels in the Keban Dam reservoir in Türkiye. They concluded that if input and output parameters are not linearly related, the ANN model performs more efficiently compared to regression models.

Recknagel et al.24 applied a three-layer static ANN model to forecast algal blooms in different lakes. Soyupak and Chen25 developed a new fuzzy logic model to forecast spatially and temporally highly variable Chl-a concentrations in a very large and deep dam reservoir. Çamdevýren et al.26 examined the relationships between Chl-a and various biotic and abiotic water quality variables using principal component scores in multiple linear regression analysis (MLR) to forecast Chl-a levels in a reservoir. Wang et al.27 constructed an NARX neural network to forecast Chl-a concentration based on experimental observation data collected from Lake Taihu. Park et al.28 used an ANN and a Support Vector Machine (SVM) to forecast Chl-a concentration for early warning in Juam Reservoir and Yeongsan Reservoir. Chen et al.29 constructed an autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model to forecast daily Chl-a concentrations using data from Lake Taihu in China. Cho and Park30 compared the forecasting results of different neural network models in their study for forecasting of Chl-a concentration in the Geum River. According to the comparison results, the Merged-LSTM model (RMSE: 0.0459) was found to be the model with the best forecast accuracy. Jang et al.31 presented a hybrid model to forecast Chl-a concentration in unmeasured locations by combining existing simulation and deep learning-based forecasting methods. Kim et al.32 constructed a multivariate hidden Markov model (MHMM) that can evaluate the spatio-temporal relationship in chlorophyll-a concentration in the Nakdong River, South Korea. Shin et al.33 used common machine learning models such as Support Vector Regression, Bagging, Random Forest, Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Recurrent Neural Network, and LSTM to build a new model to forecast chlorophyll-a concentrations in the Nakdong River, Korea. Su et al.34 proposed a new machine learning model, Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), to forecast the time series Chl-a concentration in the coastal waters of Fujian. Gómez et al.35 presented a machine learning-based application for the forecast of the Chl-a component in the upper part of the water column at Mar Menor using Sentinel-2 data and Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, artificial neural networks, and deep learning algorithms. Cen et al.36, 10 years (2011–2020) of satellite-observed chlorophyll-a data and logarithmic data processed by two different preprocessing methods emphasized that the LSTM neural network can effectively forecast chlorophyll-a concentration in terms of forecasting accuracy. Busari et al.37 used random forest and artificial neural network methods to forecast Chl-a concentration in Boyd Millpond, a lake in northern South Carolina, using high-frequency water quality data. Zhang et al.38 proposed a GRU method based on PSO for Chl-a index forecasting. Yao et al.39 used a Convolutional LSTM Network (ConvLSTM) to forecast Chl-a over the Yellow Sea and Bohai Sea (YBS) in China. Tian et al.40 proposed a novel hybrid runoff forecasting model for nonlinear and nonstationary hydrological environments. The study combined optimized variational mode decomposition, an improved Arctic Puffin Optimization algorithm (APO), and temporal convolutional networks—gated recurrent units deep learning architecture, demonstrating that the proposed approach outperformed multiple benchmark and advanced forecasting models in runoff prediction tasks.

Recently, Transformer-based deep learning models have attracted considerable attention in time-series forecasting due to their ability to capture long-term temporal dependencies and model complex nonlinear patterns more effectively than traditional recurrent architectures. Bouhamed et al.41 proposed a Transformer-based deep probabilistic network for electric load forecasting. Caetano et al.42 compared different Transformer-based deep learning models for retail sales time series forecasting and investigated the impact of explanatory variables on forecasting performance. Moustati and Gherabi43 evaluated several Transformer-based models, including Vanilla Transformer, Autoformer, Informer, and Spacetimeformer, for energy consumption forecasting using smart meter data. Liu44 compared Transformer-based deep learning models and classical forecasting approaches for financial time-series forecasting using daily stock return data. Gawusu and Zhang45 evaluated Transformer-based deep learning architectures for multi-horizon power generation forecasting.

LSTM-EXP with AMOA-based training strategy

In conventional deep ANN implementations, model parameters are typically optimized using gradient-based algorithms such as Adam. However, these approaches may suffer from sensitivity to initialization, convergence instability, and the tendency to be trapped in local minima, particularly in complex nonlinear forecasting problems. To address these limitations, this study employs the AMOA as an alternative optimization mechanism for training LSTM-EXP networks. Besides introducing a new neural network architecture, LSTM-EXP, the proposed framework also focuses on improving the training dynamics by exploiting the global search capability of a metaheuristic optimization approach. Experimental setup

In this study, daily chlorophyll-a (Chl-a) concentration data obtained from fifteen estuarine monitoring stations in the Black Sea for the period 2021–2022 are used as time series inputs. Each station is analysed independently to ensure a comprehensive and location-specific evaluation of model performance. For each time series, the data are divided into training, validation, and test sets. Each hyperparameter configuration is run using the training data with 10 different random initializations, and the RMSE values for the validation data, which the network has not seen, are calculated, and the average of these values is calculated. The best hyperparameter configuration is selected based on the average validation error. The test data is used to compare the performance of different methods. The last 10 and 30 observations are reserved for validation, while the final 10 and 30 observations are used for testing. The remaining data are used for training. This consistent data partitioning strategy is applied across all models to ensure a fair comparison. To evaluate the forecasting performance, multiple neural network models are considered, including LSTM, GRU, SRNN, SMN, MLP, DNM-ANN, PS-ANN, and the proposed method. All models are implemented under identical experimental conditions, and their hyperparameters are selected using predefined search ranges given in Table 2.

The SRNN model ranked second with a mean rank of 2.2000, while GRU and LSTM-PSO demonstrated moderate forecasting capability. In contrast, the conventional approaches, including SMN, MLP, DNM, and PS, generally obtained weaker ranking performances.

Discussion

In this study, the performance of the proposed exponential smoothing-feedback long short-term memory (LSTM-EXP) model, designed to forecast Chl-a concentrations in the estuarine regions of the Black Sea, was comprehensively evaluated. The experimental results clearly demonstrate that the proposed LSTM-EXP model produced the lowest RMSE and MAPE values at all stations for both short and long forecast horizons. For example, on the ST-1 dataset, the LSTM-EXP model demonstrated significantly higher accuracy with a mean RMSE value of 0.0882 compared to traditional MLP (0.2554) and SRNN (0.1916) models. This demonstrates how effective the exponential smoothing mechanism integrated into the model is at capturing the complex and nonlinear patterns in the time series.

The AMOA used in training the model has eliminated the need for fine-tuning control parameters, unlike methods such as genetic algorithms or particle swarm optimization. This has reduced uncertainty during the training process and ensured that the model converges more stably without getting stuck in local minima. The very low standard deviation values demonstrated by the LSTM-EXP model in repeated tests (e.g., RMSE Std. = 0.0014 for ST-1) confirm that the proposed method not only offers high accuracy but also exhibits high consistency.

Statistical analysis using the Friedman and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirmed that the LSTM-EXP model outperformed all its competitors with an average ranking of 1.06, and that this performance difference was statistically significant. When compared to other studies in the literature, it is evident that using deep learning architectures alone is insufficient for Chl-a prediction; the combination of the feedback mechanisms and meta-heuristic optimization strategies presented in this study significantly improves prediction accuracy and robustness.

Conclusions

This study presents an innovative framework for estimating chlorophyll-a concentrations, which are of critical importance for sustainable monitoring and early warning systems in marine ecosystems. The proposed LSTM-EXP model, which combines an exponential smoothing structure inspired by classical time series theory with deep learning capabilities, has demonstrated superior performance at 15 different stations in the Black Sea. The key findings of the study can be summarized as follows.

The LSTM-EXP model demonstrated that it is a reliable tool for the early detection of algal blooms by achieving the lowest error metrics across all tested datasets. The use of the AMOA algorithm has simplified complex hyperparameter tuning processes and made the model’s training process more efficient. Comprehensive statistical analyses have shown that the superiority of the proposed method over its competitors is not due to chance and that it maintains its reliability even under different data characteristics. Processing satellite-derived data with this model offers an economical and environmentally friendly methodology for environmental monitoring by reducing the cost and carbon footprint of laboratory analyses. In conclusion, this developed approach provides strategic support to decision-makers for the protection of marine ecosystems and eutrophication management. In future studies, the goal is to adapt this model to other water quality parameters and further improve its performance using multivariate data inputs.
