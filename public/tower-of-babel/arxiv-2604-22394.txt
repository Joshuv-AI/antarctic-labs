Abstract
. We introduce multiplicative Ehresmann connections on surjective submersions of Lie groupoids, extending both the classical notion of Ehresmann connections on fibre bundles and the more recent notion of multiplicative connections on Lie groupoid extensions. We investigate the existence of such connections, showing that, in general, they may fail to exist even for proper Lie groupoids. In contrast, positive results hold for Morita submersions, uniform Lie groupoid fibrations, locally trivial families of Lie groupoids, and proper families of Lie groupoids. Our main results concern completeness. For Lie groupoid fibrations, we prove that the completeness of a multiplicative connection is governed by the induced connection on the kernel bundle and, under connectivity assumptions, by the base connection. For families of source-proper Lie groupoids, we prove the equivalence between local triviality and the existence of complete multiplicative Ehresmann connections.



1.
Introduction

In many areas of differential geometry, fibrations play a fundamental role—most notably vector bundles, principal bundles, covering spaces, Riemannian submersions, and symplectic fibrations. These classes of fibrations come with corresponding notions of connections, defined by certain compatibility conditions with the internal geometric or algebraic structure of the fibration. In
[
20
]
, the study of Lie groupoid extensions, i.e., surjective submersions of Lie groupoids covering the identity, and their corresponding notion of
multiplicative
Ehresmann connection was initiated. The authors introduced these connections in order to describe non-abelian differentiable gerbes over stacks and studied, among other things, the problems of existence and completeness. With the aim of producing local models in Poisson geometry
[
13
]
, the study of multiplicative Ehresmann connections was taken up again in
[
12
]
, where a strong existence result was obtained for proper groupoids and where the infinitesimal theory was developed. The cohomological obstruction theory at both the groupoid and algebroid levels was further developed in
[
14
]
. Multiplicative Ehresmann connections have found recent applications in the study of Yang–Mills theory on Lie groupoids
[
15
]
and in the construction of a Cartan model for the differentiable stack cohomology of Lie groupoids
[
19
]
.

The purpose of the present paper is to extend the theory of multiplicative Ehresmann connections beyond extensions to Lie groupoid morphisms

\pi\colon(\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M)\to(\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows N)

which are surjective submersions, but whose base map
\pi_{0}\colon{M}\to N
is not necessarily the identity. Also in this setting, multiplicative Ehresmann connections are defined by requiring their horizontal bundle to be a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subgroupoid of
T\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows TM

\Hor\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0},

where
\Hor_{0}
is an induced connection for the base map
\pi_{0}
. These objects simultaneously generalise classical Ehresmann connections on surjective submersions, linear connections on vector bundles, multiplicative connections on Lie groupoid extensions, and, in particular, principal connections, via the gauge groupoid.

Section
3
is devoted to the basic properties of multiplicative Ehresmann connections. We show that they are characterized by the property that the horizontal paths form a subgroupoid of the path groupoid of
\mathcal{G}
(Proposition
3.5
). Finally, we briefly review the infinitesimal notions of multiplicative connections.

In Section
4
and at the beginning of Section
6
, we turn to the existence problem for multiplicative Ehresmann connections. For extensions, properness of the total groupoid suffices to ensure existence
[
12
]
. The general case of Lie groupoid fibrations is much more restrictive. For example, the action morphism
\pi\colon H\ltimes M\to H
corresponding to a non-trivial action of a connected group
H\ {\rotatebox[origin]{-90.0}{$\circlearrowright$}}\ M
never admits a multiplicative connection (Corollary
4.3
). We obtain positive results for the following classes of groupoid morphisms (reviewed in Section
2.2
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Multiplicative Ehresmann connections always exist for:

(1)

Morita fibrations (Proposition
4.4
),

(2)

proper uniform Lie groupoid fibrations (Corollary

(3)

The main results of the paper are about the completeness of multiplicative Ehresmann connections. Theorem

[

(4)

Multiplicative, affine connections on a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows N
have been introduced and studied in
[

. These are the same as affine connections
\nabla
on
\mathcal{H}
which, when seen as an Ehresmann connection on the surjective Lie groupoid surjective submersion

For later use, let us remark that multiplicative Ehresmann connections lift infinitesimal groupoid automorphisms to infinitesimal groupoid automorphisms (for the basics on multiplicative vector fields on Lie groupoids, see
[
22
]
).

Proposition 3.3
.

A multiplicative horizontal lift
\hor\colon T\mathcal{H}\times_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{G}\to T\mathcal{G}
on
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
sends multiplicative vector fields to multiplicative vector fields

\hor\colon\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{mult}}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{mult}}(\mathcal{G}).

Proof.

A vector field
X
on
\mathcal{H}
is multiplicative precisely when
X\colon\mathcal{H}\to T\mathcal{H}
is a groupoid morphism. If this is the case, then so is the pullback section, i.e. the map

\widetilde{X}\colon\mathcal{G}\to T\mathcal{H}\times_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{G}\colon g\mapsto(X_{\pi(g)},g).

The vector field
\hor(X)
is obtained by composing this map with the
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-groupoid morphism
\hor\colon T\mathcal{H}\times_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{G}\to T\mathcal{G}
. Hence, the result.
∎

The above proposition does not have a converse and therefore does not fully characterise a multiplicative Ehresmann connection. The following example was constructed by Luca Vitagliano. It shows that, in general, the conclusion of Proposition
3.3
is not sufficient to ensure multiplicativity.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the surjective submersion
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to S^{1},\,(x,y)\mapsto e^{ix}
, and remark that this is a Lie group(oid) homomorphism with respect to the standard structures. Any multiplicative vector field on
S^{1}
must be the zero vector field. Therefore, any connection
\hor
on
\pi
will trivially induce a map

\hor\colon\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{mult}}(S^{1})\to\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{mult}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

s
As
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an abelian Lie group, the left and right trivialization of
T\mathbb{R}^{2}
coincide. Moreover, as a Lie group, it is isomorphic to the standard structure on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
via the left/right trivialization. In this global chart, the vertical bundle is given by:

\ker T\pi=\mathrm{span}\{(x,y,a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\mid a=0\}

Next, consider a distribution, given in the global chart as:

\Hor=\{(x,y,a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\mid b=x^{2}a\}.

Fibrewise, meaning for fixed
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\Hor
is not a subgroup of

as for any

.

3.2.
Infinitesimal pictures

In the following subsections, we briefly discuss the possible counterparts of the notion of multiplicative connection in the infinitesimal setting. We will not go into more detail in this paper.

3.2.1.
Lie algebroid submersions

Given a Lie algebroid map
\Pi\colon A\to B
, which is a surjective submersion, the notion of an
infinitesimal multiplicative Ehresmann connection (IMEC)
is defined as a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-algebroid splitting
TA=\ker\Pi\oplus E_{\Hor}
, where
E_{\Hor}\Rightarrow\Hor_{0}
is a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subalgebroid of
TA\Rightarrow TM
. Given a MEC
\Hor\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0}
for a Lie groupoid surjective submersion
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
, the Lie functor in the
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
setting
[
3
]
yields an IMEC
E_{\Hor}=\mathrm{Lie}(\Hor)
for the induced Lie algebroid surjective submersion
\Pi\colon A\to B
, where
A=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{G})
and
B=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{H})
. Conversely, if
\mathcal{G}
is source-simply connected, then any IMEC
E_{\Hor}
for
\Pi
integrates to a MEC for
\pi
. This result follows from
[
3
]
*Section 4.3. In the case of extensions, IMECs were studied in
[
12
]
.

3.2.2.
Multiplicative foliations

Instead of a Lie groupoid surjective submersion, we could consider a multiplicative foliation
\mathcal{D}
on a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
, which replaces
\ker T\pi
. The corresponding notion of a MEC is simply a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-groupoid complement
T\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{D}\oplus\Hor
. For extensions, this point of view was very useful in
[
12
]
in showing that the existence of MECs is Morita invariant.

As shown in
[
18
]
, a multiplicative (simple) foliation
\mathcal{D}\subset T\mathcal{G}
does not necessarily induce a (Lie) groupoid structure on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{D}
. Indeed assume that
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}_{0}
are both simple, so that quotient maps
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{D}
and
\pi_{0}\colon M\to M/\mathcal{D}_{0}
are surjective submersions. While the source, target, unit, and inverse map are well-defined on the quotients, the multiplication may fail to descend. Under the additional assumptions:

(1)

the map

\mathcal{G}\to M{}_{\pi_{0}}\times_{\overline{\boldsymbol{s}}}\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{D},\qquad g\mapsto(\boldsymbol{s}(g),\pi(g))

is surjective, and

(2)

if
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
and
h\sim 1_{\boldsymbol{s}(h)}
in
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{D}
then
hg\sim g
in
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{D}
,

it was shown in
[
18
]
that
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{D}\rightrightarrows M/\mathcal{D}_{0}
inherits a groupoid structure. In this case,
\pi
is a Lie groupoid fibration (see Theorem
A.1
).

3.2.3.
Infinitesimal ideal systems

To combine the settings discussed above, consider a Lie algebroid
A\Rightarrow M
together with
infinitesimally multiplicative foliation
(IMF), i.e. an involutive subbundle
E_{\mathcal{D}}\subset TA
which is a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subalgebroid
E_{\mathcal{D}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{0}
. These objects were studied in
[
17
]
, where they are also described in terms of the more explicit data of an
infinitesimal ideal system
. Infinitesimally multiplicative foliations generalise the previous two settings:

•

if
\Pi\colon A\to B
is a Lie algebroid submersion, then
E_{\mathcal{D}}:=\ker T\Pi
is an IMF;

•

if
A=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{G})
, and
\mathcal{D}\subset T\mathcal{G}
is a multiplicative foliation, then
E_{\mathcal{D}}:=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{D})
is an IMF. Moreover, if
\mathcal{G}
is source-simply connected, then any IMF arises from a unique multiplicative foliation on
\mathcal{G}

[
17
]
*Theorem 4.8.

An IMEC
E_{\Hor}
in this setting is a vector bundle complement
TA=E_{\mathcal{D}}\oplus E_{\Hor}
which is a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subalgebroid
E_{\Hor}\Rightarrow\Hor_{0}
.

Remark
.

At the infinitesimal level, there also arise notions of Lie algebroid fibrations and uniform Lie algebroid fibrations, see
[
24
]
*Section 4.4. In conjunction with Appendix
A
, we notice, however, that at the infinitesimal level, both a Lie groupoid surjective submersion and Lie groupoid fibration define a Lie algebroid fibration. Somewhat analogously, uniform Lie groupoid fibrations descend to uniform Lie algebroid fibrations.

\vartriangle

4.
Existence

In this section, we discuss the problem of the existence of multiplicative Ehresmann connections in various contexts. Surjective submersions admit Ehresmann connections simply because any vector subbundle admits a complement. However, in the multiplicative setting, the existence problem is much more subtle, as
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subgroupoids do not always admit multiplicative complements.

4.1.
Extensions

Consider the special case of an extension, i.e. a Lie groupoid surjective submersion
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
covering
\pi_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{M}\colon M\to M
. In this case, the existence problem was well-researched already in
[
20
]
, where a cohomological theory was developed, and an obstruction class was identified. In
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
the problem was studied in the more general framework of
bundles of ideals
on the
\mathcal{G}
, which correspond to the notion of multiplicative foliations
\mathcal{D}\subset T\mathcal{G}
from Section
3.2.2
with trivial base
\mathcal{D}_{0}=0_{M}
, and also in the setting of
infinitesimal bundles of ideals
in Lie algebroids, which correspond to Section
3.2.3
for
\mathcal{D}_{0}=0_{M}
. In particular,
[
14
]
introduces a cohomological obstruction theory, both for the global level (Section 5.1.1), as well as for the infinitesimal level (Section 5.1.2). Existence was proven in
[
12
]
*Theorem 4.2 a bundle of ideals on a proper Lie groupoid; we recall the result in the particular case of extensions.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

Any Lie groupoid extension
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
, defined on a proper Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, admits a multiplicative Ehresmann connection.

This result does not extend directly to general Lie groupoid fibrations. A class of counterexamples is presented in the next subsection.

4.2.
Action morphisms

We discuss the existence of MECs for action morphisms. This case will provide examples showing that properness is not a sufficient condition for the existence of MECs.

Consider a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows N
with an action
a:\mathcal{H}{}_{\boldsymbol{s}}\times_{\pi_{0}}M\to M
on a surjective submersion
\pi_{0}:M\to N
. This yields the action groupoid
\mathcal{H}\ltimes M:=\mathcal{H}{}_{\boldsymbol{s}}\times_{\pi_{0}}M\rightrightarrows M
, and the action morphism

\pi:=\mathrm{pr}_{1}:\mathcal{H}\ltimes M\to\mathcal{H}.

(4)

Up to isomorphism, any action morphism is of this type.

Proposition 4.2
.

Any MEC
\Hor\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0}
for (
4
) is determined by its base connection via the equality

\Hor=T\mathcal{H}{}_{T\boldsymbol{s}}\times_{T\pi_{0}}\Hor_{0}\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0}.

(5)

Conversely, given an Ehresmann connection
\Hor_{0}\subset TM
for
\pi_{0}
, the above formula defines a MEC precisely when it is
T\mathcal{H}
-invariant:

Ta(T\mathcal{H}{}_{T\boldsymbol{s}}\times_{T\pi_{0}}\Hor_{0})\subset\Hor_{0}.

In particular, such a connection satisfies
\rho(\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{H})\times_{N}M))\subset\Hor_{0}
, where
\rho
is denotes the infinitesimal action of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{H})
on
\pi_{0}\colon M\to N
.

Proof.

The tangent of the action groupoid is also a tangent groupoid:

T(\mathcal{H}\ltimes M)=T\mathcal{H}\ltimes TM.

Therefore, if
\Hor\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0}
is a MEC, then
\Hor\subset T\mathcal{H}{}_{T\boldsymbol{s}}\times_{T\pi_{0}}\Hor_{0}
. Since the two bundles have the same rank, (
5
) follows.

Conversely, (
5
) defines a subgroupoid precisely when
\Hor_{0}
is
T\mathcal{H}
-invariant.

The last inclusion follows from
T\mathcal{H}
-invariance

\rho(\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{H})\times_{N}M)=Ta(\mathrm{Lie}(\mathcal{H}){}_{T\boldsymbol{s}}\times_{T\pi_{0}}0_{M})\subset\Hor_{0}.\qed

For the particular case of groups, this implies the following.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
H
be a connected Lie group acting on a manifold
M
. The Lie groupoid fibration

\pi:=\mathrm{pr}_{1}\colon H\ltimes M\to H,

admits a multiplicative Ehresmann connection only if the action is trivial.

Proof.

Note that
\Hor_{0}=0_{M}
, because it is a connection on
\pi_{0}\colon M\to\{*\}
. The proposition implies that the infinitesimal action of
\mathrm{Lie}(H)
on
M
is trivial. Since
H
is connected, so is the action of
H
on
M
.
∎

We conclude that any non-trivial action of a compact, connected Lie group on a compact manifold yields an example of a compact Lie groupoid fibration that does not admit a MEC.

4.3.
Morita fibrations

For this class of maps, we have a general existence result.

Proposition 4.4
.

Any Morita fibration
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
admits a MEC. More precisely, for any Ehresmann connection
\Hor_{0}
for the base map
\pi_{0}:M\to N
, there is a unique MEC
\Hor
for
\pi
with base
\Hor_{0}
.

Proof.

The base map
\pi_{0}:M\to N
is a surjective submersion, and we may assume that
\mathcal{G}=\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{H}
and
\pi=\mathrm{pr}\colon\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
,
(x,h,y)\mapsto h
.

Let
\Hor_{0}\subset TM
be an Ehresmann connection for
\pi_{0}
. Note that

\overline{\pi}_{0}:=T\pi_{0}|_{\Hor_{0}}\colon\Hor_{0}\to TN

is a vector bundle map and a surjective submersion. Pulling back
T\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows TN
along this map, we obtain the pullback
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-groupoid

\Hor:=\overline{\pi}_{0}^{*}T\mathcal{H}=\Hor_{0}{}_{\overline{\pi}_{0}}\times_{T\boldsymbol{t}}T\mathcal{H}{}_{T\boldsymbol{s}}\times_{\overline{\pi}_{0}}\Hor_{0}\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0},

which is canonically a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subgroupoid of
T(\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{H})
and defines a MEC for
\mathrm{pr}
. Uniqueness follows because any MEC with base
\Hor_{0}
would be a subset of
\Hor
.
∎

4.4.
Uniform Lie groupoid fibrations

For uniform Lie groupoid fibrations (recall Definition
2.1
), the existence problem can be reduced to the case of extensions.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a uniform Lie groupoid fibration. If the induced map
\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{H}
admits a MEC, then so does
\pi
.

Proof.

We can factor
\pi=\mathrm{pr}\circ\pi^{*}
, where
\mathrm{pr}:\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a Morita fibration. By Proposition
4.4
,
\mathrm{pr}
admits a MEC. The result follows from the lemma below.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

If the Lie groupoid surjective submersions
\pi^{1}\colon\mathcal{G}^{1}\to\mathcal{G}^{2}
and
\pi^{2}\colon\mathcal{G}^{2}\to\mathcal{G}^{3}
admit MECs, then so does their composition
\pi^{2}\circ\pi^{1}\colon\mathcal{G}^{1}\to\mathcal{G}^{3}
.

Proof.

Consider MECs
\Hor^{1}\rightrightarrows\Hor^{1}_{0}
for
\pi^{1}
and
\Hor^{2}\rightrightarrows\Hor^{2}_{0}
for
\pi^{2}
. Then

\Hor:=\Hor^{1}\cap(T\pi^{1})^{-1}(\Hor^{2})\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0}:=\Hor^{1}_{0}\cap(T\pi^{1}_{0})^{-1}(\Hor^{2}_{0})

is a MEC for
\pi^{1}\circ\pi^{2}
. Indeed, that
\Hor
and
\Hor_{0}
are Ehresmann connections is a general fact about Ehresmann connections for compositions of submersions. Multiplicativity follows because
T\pi^{1}
is a groupoid map.
∎

Proposition
4.5
together with Theorem
4.1
yield the following.

Corollary 4.7
.

Any uniform Lie groupoid fibration
\pi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
, defined on a proper Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, admits a multiplicative Ehresmann connection.

5.
Completeness and the connection on the kernel

We turn to completeness of multiplicative Ehresmann connections. We prove two completeness criteria. The first generalises
[
12
]
*Proposition 2.17 to fibrations and is in terms of the connections induced on the kernel, while the second is in terms of the connection induced on the base.

We discuss first the connection induced on the kernel. Given a Lie groupoid surjective submersion
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
, its kernel

\mathcal{K}:=\ker(\pi)=\pi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{u}(N))\rightrightarrows M

is a family of Lie groupoids with projection denoted

\underline{\pi}:=\boldsymbol{s}\circ\pi|_{\mathrm{\mathcal{K}}}\colon\mathcal{K}\to N.

First, we note that MECs are inherited by kernels (for extensions, this was remarked in
[
20
]
*Section 4.1 and in
[
12
]
*Section 2.4).

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a Lie groupoid surjective submersion. Given a MEC
\Hor\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0}
for
\pi
, we have that

\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}:=\Hor\cap T\mathcal{K}\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0}

is a MEC on the kernel
\underline{\pi}\colon\mathcal{K}\to N
.

Proof.

We use the following general fact. Let
f:A\to B
be a surjective submersion,
B^{\prime}\subset B
an embedded submanifold, and
A^{\prime}:=f^{-1}(B^{\prime})
. Then
A^{\prime}
is an embedded submanifold, and
f^{\prime}:=f|_{A^{\prime}}\colon A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
is a surjective submersion. Moreover, if
\Hor
is an Ehresmann connection for
f
, then
\Hor^{\prime}:=\Hor\cap TA^{\prime}
is an Ehresmann connection for
f^{\prime}
with corresponding horizontal lift given by restriction:

\hor^{\prime}=\hor|_{TB^{\prime}\times_{B^{\prime}}A^{\prime}}\colon TB^{\prime}\times_{B^{\prime}}A^{\prime}\subset TB\times_{B}A\to\Hor^{\prime}=\Hor\cap\ TA^{\prime}.

Using this fact for
f:=\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
and
B^{\prime}:=\boldsymbol{u}(N)
, we obtain that
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
defined above is a connection for
\pi^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{K}\to\boldsymbol{u}(N)
(which is the map
\underline{\pi}
from the statement, under the identification
N\cong\boldsymbol{u}(N)
). Moreover, the horizontal lift restricts to a groupoid isomorphism:

\hor^{\mathcal{K}}\colon T\boldsymbol{u}(N)\times_{\boldsymbol{u}(N)}\mathcal{K}\to\Hor^{\mathcal{K}},

where
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
—as the intersection of two groupoids—is set-theoretically a groupoid. Using that
\hor
is a groupoid map covering
\hor_{0}
, we have that:

T\boldsymbol{s}(\hor(T\boldsymbol{u}(v),k))=\hor_{0}(v,\boldsymbol{s}(k)),\quad\textrm{for all}\quad v\in T_{x}N,\ k\in\pi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{u}(x)).

Since
\hor_{0}(\cdot,\boldsymbol{s}(k))\colon T_{x}N\to(\Hor_{0})_{\boldsymbol{s}(k)}
is a linear isomorphism, the equality shows that also
T\boldsymbol{s}\colon(\Hor^{\mathcal{K}})_{k}\to(\Hor_{0})_{s(k)}
is surjective. In particular, the base of
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
is
\Hor_{0}
. That
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0}
is indeed a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subgroupoid of
T\mathcal{K}
follows now from Proposition
B.1
.
∎

The following result generalises
[
12
]
*Proposition 2.17 to fibrations.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a Lie groupoid fibration with kernel bundle
\underline{\pi}\colon\mathcal{K}:=\ker(\pi)\to N
. A multiplicative Ehresmann connection
\Hor
for
\pi
is complete if and only if the induced multiplicative Ehresmann connection
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
on
\underline{\pi}
is complete.

Proof.

Assume that
\Hor
is a complete MEC for
\pi
. Then for any
\delta\in C^{\infty}(I,N)
and any
k\in\pi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{u}(\delta(0)))\subset\mathcal{K}
, the
\Hor
-horizontal lift
\tau^{t}_{\boldsymbol{u}(\delta)}(k)
is defined on
I
. Since
\pi\circ\tau_{\boldsymbol{u}(\delta)}(k)=\boldsymbol{u}(\delta)
, it follows that
\tau^{t}_{\boldsymbol{u}(\delta)}(k)\in\mathcal{K}
. Thus
\frac{d}{dt}\tau^{t}_{\boldsymbol{u}(\delta)}(k)\in T\mathcal{K}\cap\Hor=\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
. So
\tau^{t}_{\boldsymbol{u}(\delta)}(k)
is also the
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
-horizontal lift of
\boldsymbol{u}(\delta)
. This shows that
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
is complete.

Conversely, assume that
\Hor
is MEC for
\pi
such that
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
is complete. Note that
\Hor
and
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
induce the same connection
\Hor_{0}
on
\pi_{0}\colon M\to N
; simply because

\Hor_{0}=T(\boldsymbol{u}(M))\cap\Hor\subset T\mathcal{K}\cap\Hor=\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}.

By Proposition
3.6
completeness of
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
implies completeness of
\Hor_{0}
.

Consider a smooth path
\gamma\in C^{\infty}(I,\mathcal{H})
pick
g\in\pi^{-1}(\gamma(0))
. Assume
\gamma
cannot be lifted completely at
g
, and consider its maximal lift

\tau^{t}_{\gamma}(g)\colon[0,{t_{\text{m}}})\to\mathcal{G}.

By completeness of
\Hor_{0}
, the
\Hor_{0}
-lift of
\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)
at
\boldsymbol{s}(g)
is defined on
I
, and by Proposition
3.5
, for
t\in[0,{t_{\text{m}}})
it is given by

\tau^{t}_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}(\boldsymbol{s}(g))=\boldsymbol{s}(\tau^{t}_{\gamma}(g)).

In particular, we can consider
\tau^{{t_{\text{m}}}}_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}(\boldsymbol{s}(g))\in M
. Since
\pi
is a Lie groupoid fibration, there exists
g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
such that:

\pi(g^{\prime})=\gamma({t_{\text{m}}})\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\boldsymbol{s}(g^{\prime})=\tau^{{t_{\text{m}}}}_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}(\boldsymbol{s}(g)).

Next, consider the
\Hor
-lift of
\gamma
which at
t={t_{\text{m}}}
gives
g^{\prime}
:

\tau^{\cdot,{t_{\text{m}}}}_{\gamma}(g^{\prime}):({t_{\text{m}}}-\delta,{t_{\text{m}}}+\delta)\to\mathcal{G},\qquad t\mapsto\tau^{t,{t_{\text{m}}}}_{\gamma}(g^{\prime}),

which is well-defined for some
0<\delta<\mathrm{min}({t_{\text{m}}},1-{t_{\text{m}}})
. Using Proposition
3.5
, this satisfies for all
t\in({t_{\text{m}}}-\delta,{t_{\text{m}}}+\delta)

\boldsymbol{s}\circ\tau^{t,{t_{\text{m}}}}_{\gamma}(g^{\prime})=\tau_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}^{t,{t_{\text{m}}}}(\boldsymbol{s}(g^{\prime}))=\tau^{t,{t_{\text{m}}}}_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}\big(\tau^{{t_{\text{m}}}}_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}(\boldsymbol{s}(g))\big)=\tau^{t}_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}(\boldsymbol{s}(g)),

where the last equality uses the multiplicativity property of parallel transport. We conclude that the following path is well-defined

\eta\colon({t_{\text{m}}}-\delta,{t_{\text{m}}})\to\mathcal{G},\qquad\eta(t):=\tau_{\gamma}^{t}(g)\cdot\big(\tau_{\gamma}^{t,{t_{\text{m}}}}(g^{\prime})\big)^{-1}.

Proposition
3.5
implies that
\eta
is a horizontal path. It covers

\pi(\eta(t))=\pi(\tau_{\gamma}^{t}(g))\cdot\pi(\tau_{\gamma}^{t,{t_{\text{m}}}}(g^{\prime}))^{-1}=\gamma(t)\cdot\gamma^{-1}(t)=\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{t}(\gamma))(t).

Fix
t_{0}\in({t_{\text{m}}}-\delta,{t_{\text{m}}})
and denote
k_{0}:=\eta(t_{0})
. By completeness of
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
, the
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
-horizontal lift of
\boldsymbol{u}\circ\boldsymbol{t}(\gamma)
, which at
t=t_{0}
equals
k_{0}
is defined on
I

\tau^{t,t_{0}}_{\boldsymbol{u}\circ\boldsymbol{t}(\gamma)}(k_{0})\colon I\to\mathcal{K}.

By the uniqueness of horizontal lifts, we have that:

\eta(t)=\tau^{t,t_{0}}_{\boldsymbol{u}\circ\boldsymbol{t}(\gamma)}(k_{0}),\qquad\mbox{for }t\in({t_{\text{m}}}-\delta,{t_{\text{m}}}).

This implies smoothness of the path

\zeta\colon[0,{t_{\text{m}}}+\delta)\to\mathcal{G},\qquad t\mapsto\begin{cases}\tau^{t}_{\gamma}(g)&\mbox{if }t\in[0,{t_{\text{m}}}),\\
\tau^{t,t_{0}}_{\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{t}(\gamma))}(k_{0})\cdot\tau^{t,{t_{\text{m}}}}_{\gamma}(g^{\prime})&\mbox{if }t\in({t_{\text{m}}}-\delta,{t_{\text{m}}}+\delta).\end{cases}

The fact that
\zeta
is horizontal and covers
\gamma
contradicts the maximality of
{t_{\text{m}}}
.

We conclude that
\Hor
is complete as well.
∎

In fact, we have proven a stronger result: the implication

\Hor\textrm{ is complete }\implies\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}\textrm{ is complete }

does not use the assumption that
\pi
is a Lie groupoid fibration. However, if the base is source-connected, existence of a complete MEC already implies that we are dealing with a Lie groupoid fibration, as the following result shows.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a Lie groupoid surjective submersion which admits a complete MEC. If
\mathcal{H}
is source-connected, then
\pi
is a Lie groupoid fibration.

Proof.

By Theorem
A.1
it suffices to show that, for any
x\in M
,
\pi
restricts to a surjection between
\boldsymbol{s}
-fibres
\pi\colon\boldsymbol{s}^{-1}(x)\to\boldsymbol{s}^{-1}(\pi_{0}(x))
. Take some
h\in\boldsymbol{s}^{-1}(\pi_{0}(x))
. By assumption, there is a smooth path
\gamma\colon I\to\boldsymbol{s}^{-1}(\pi_{0}(x))
such that
\gamma(0)=\boldsymbol{u}(\pi_{0}(x))
and
\gamma(1)=h
. By completeness,
t\mapsto\tau_{\gamma}^{t}(\boldsymbol{u}(x))
is defined on
I
. Proposition
3.5
yields
\boldsymbol{s}\circ\tau_{\gamma}^{t}(\boldsymbol{u}(x))=\tau_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}^{t}(x)=x
. Thus
g:=\tau_{\gamma}^{1}(\boldsymbol{u}(x))\in\boldsymbol{s}^{-1}(x)
and satisfies
\pi(g)=h
.
∎

For later use, let us note the following consequence of the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a Lie groupoid surjective submersion which admits a complete MEC. Then
\pi_{0}\colon M\to N
admits a complete Ehresmann connection.

The assumption that
\pi
is a fibration was used for the proof of the implication

\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}\textrm{ is complete }\implies\Hor\textrm{ is complete}.

The next example shows that the fibration assumption was indeed necessary.

Example 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=N\times\Gamma\rightrightarrows N
be a constant family of groups, where
\Gamma
is any discrete, non-trivial group. Fix
x_{0}\in N
and consider the open subgroupoid
\mathcal{H}^{*}:=\mathcal{H}\backslash(\{x_{0}\}\times\Gamma\backslash\{e_{\Gamma}\})\rightrightarrows N
. Consider the disjoint union Lie groupoid

\big(\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows N\sqcup N\big):=\big(\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows N\big)\,\sqcup\,\big(\mathcal{H}^{*}\rightrightarrows N\big).

We have an induced Lie groupoid surjective submersion
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
, which is the identity and inclusion on the respective factors. The map
\pi
is not a Lie groupoid fibration, as, for
g\neq e_{\Gamma}
, the arrow
(x_{0},g)
cannot be lifted over the copy of
x_{0}
in the second factor.

The map
\pi
is a local diffeomorphism, and the unique connection is multiplicative. However,
\pi
is not a covering space, as the point
(x_{0},e_{\Gamma})\in\mathcal{H}
is not evenly covered, and so the connection is not complete.

The kernel bundle is simply the unit groupoid
\mathcal{K}=N\sqcup N
and the projection
\underline{\pi}\colon\mathcal{K}\to N
is a covering space. So it admits the path-lifting property and thus the induced connection on the kernel bundle is complete.

\vartriangle

Next, using the fact that the unit component of a Lie groupoid is generated by an arbitrary open neighbourhood of the units, we show that if the kernel is a source-connected Lie groupoid, then the completeness problem can be reduced further to the base connection, providing a partial converse to Corollary
5.4
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a Lie groupoid fibration and suppose that the kernel is source-connected. A multiplicative Ehresmann connection
\Hor
for
\pi
is complete if and only if the induced connection
\Hor_{0}
on the base submersion
\pi_{0}
is complete.

For this, we will use the following:

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
X
be a multiplicative vector field on
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
, which covers
X_{M}
on
M
. If
\mathcal{G}
is source-connected and
X_{M}
is complete, then so is
X
.

Proof.

The set
U
where
\phi_{X}^{1}
(
:=
the flow of
X
at time
1
) is defined, is an open subset of
\mathcal{G}
. Since
X
is multiplicative,
X
is tangent to the zero-section
M
. Thus, its restriction to
M
is the complete vector field
X_{M}
and
M\subset U
. Multiplicativity implies that
U
is a subgroupoid. The only open subgroupoid of a source-connected Lie groupoid that contains the units is the whole groupoid (see, for example
[
5
]
*Proposition 13.7). Hence
X
is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.6
.

By Theorem
5.2
, it suffices to show that completeness of
\Hor_{0}
implies that of
\Hor^{\mathcal{K}}
. This is equivalent to the horizontal lift to
\mathcal{K}
of any complete vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
be complete. Since
X
is a multiplicative vector field on the unit groupoid, by Proposition
3.3

\hor^{\mathcal{K}}(X)
is multiplicative, and it covers
\hor_{0}(X)
, which is complete because
\Hor_{0}
is. Hence, the previous lemma yields the result.
∎

The example below shows that source-connectedness is a necessary condition.

Example 5.8
.

Consider a Lie groupoid, similar to the one in Example
5.5
:

\mathcal{G}=(M\times\Gamma)\backslash(\{x_{0}\}\times\Gamma\backslash\{e_{\Gamma}\})\rightrightarrows M

where
\Gamma
is any discrete, non-trivial group and
x_{0}\in M
. Then
\mathcal{G}
is a bundle of Lie groups with projection
\pi=\mathrm{pr}_{1}\colon\mathcal{G}\to M
. Notice that
\pi
is a local diffeomorphism, and, therefore, it admits only a unique connection
\Hor=0_{\mathcal{G}}
, which is not complete as
\pi
is not a covering map, but it is clearly multiplicative. Nevertheless, the base connection
\Hor_{0}=0_{M}
on
\pi_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
is clearly complete.

\vartriangle

For Morita fibrations, by using also Proposition
4.4
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.9
.

A Morita fibration
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
admits a complete MEC if and only if the base map
\pi_{0}\colon M\to N
admits a complete Ehresmann connection.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.4
, we know that the base Ehresmann connection of a complete MEC is complete as well.

Conversely, any Morita fibration is isomorphic to pullback
\mathrm{pr}:\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
,
(x,h,y)\mapsto h
. By Proposition
4.4
, any connection
\Hor_{0}
for
\pi_{0}
gives a unique MEC
\Hor
for
\mathrm{pr}
. The explicit description of this correspondence from the proof of Proposition
4.4
implies that the
\Hor
-horizontal lift of
\gamma\colon I\to\mathcal{H}
is given by

t\mapsto\tau_{\gamma}^{t}(x,\gamma(0),y)=(\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}(\gamma)}^{t}(x),\gamma(t),\tau^{t}_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}(y)),

for any
x\in\pi_{0}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{t}(\gamma)(0))
,
y\in\pi_{0}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{t}(\gamma)(0))
, where
\tau_{\boldsymbol{t}(\gamma)}
and
\tau_{\boldsymbol{s}(\gamma)}
are
\Hor_{0}
-horizontal lifts. Thus, if
\Hor_{0}
is complete, then so is
\Hor
.
∎

Next, for uniform Lie groupoid fibrations, we obtain a variant of Proposition
4.5
in the complete case, with the same strategy of proof.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a uniform Lie groupoid fibration. If
\pi_{0}\colon M\to N
admits a complete Ehresmann connection and
\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{H}
admits a complete MEC then
\pi
admits a complete MEC.

The existence and completeness of multiplicative Ehresmann connections in the case of Lie groupoid extensions have been characterised in

]
.

6.
Families of Lie groupoids

A
family of Lie groupoids
is a Lie groupoid surjective submersion

\pi\colon(\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M)\to N,

where
N
is a manifold regarded as a unit groupoid. This yields indeed a family of Lie groupoids indexed by
y\in N
:

\mathcal{G}_{y}:=\pi^{-1}(y)\rightrightarrows\pi_{0}^{-1}(y),

where, as usually,
\pi_{0}\colon M\to N
denotes the base map of
\pi
.

In this section, we discuss in detail multiplicative Ehresmann connections for families, with focus on the existence and completeness problems.

We begin with a useful description of MECs in this setting.

Lemma 6.1
.

An Ehresmann connection for a family of Lie groupoids is multiplicative if and only if the horizontal vector fields coincide with the multiplicative vector fields.

Proof.

Assume that
\Hor\rightrightarrows\Hor_{0}
is a MEC for
\pi\colon(\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M)\to N
. Then every vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
is a multiplicative vector field of a unit groupoid. Therefore, Proposition
3.3
implies that
\hor(X)
is multiplicative.

Conversely, let
\Hor
be an Ehresmann connection for
\pi\colon(\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M)\to N
such that
X_{\mathcal{G}}:=\hor(X)
is multiplicative, for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(N)
. In particular,
X_{\mathcal{G}}
is tangent to the unit section
M
; we denote the induced vector field on
M
by
X_{M}
. This shows that along
M
,
\Hor|_{M}\subset TM
, and so it defines an Ehresmann connection
\Hor_{0}:=\Hor|_{M}
for
\pi_{0}
with horizontal lift
X\mapsto X_{M}
. Since the groupoid morphism
X_{\mathcal{G}}\colon\mathcal{G}\to T\mathcal{G}
covers
X_{M}\colon M\to TM
, the following compatibility relations hold:

T\boldsymbol{s}\circ X_{\mathcal{G}}=X_{M}\circ\boldsymbol{s}\quad\textrm{and}\quad T\boldsymbol{t}\circ X_{\mathcal{G}}=X_{M}\circ\boldsymbol{t}.

This implies that
T\boldsymbol{s}(\Hor)\subset\Hor_{0}
and
T\boldsymbol{t}(\Hor)\subset\Hor_{0}
. Consider now a composable pair
(v,w)
in
\Hor_{g}\times\Hor_{h}
. Denote
x:=\pi(g)=\pi(h)
and
u_{0}:=T_{g}\pi(v)=T_{h}\pi(w)
. We extend
u_{0}
to a vector field
X
on
N
. Horizontality implies that
v=X_{\mathcal{G}}|_{g}
and
w=X_{\mathcal{G}}|_{h}
. Since
v^{\mathcal{G}}
is multiplicative, it follows that

T_{(g,h)}\boldsymbol{m}(u,v)=T_{(g,h)}\boldsymbol{m}(X_{\mathcal{G}}|_{g},X_{\mathcal{G}}|_{h})=X_{\mathcal{G}}|_{gh}\in\Hor_{gh}.

This shows that
\Hor
is closed under multiplication. That
\Hor
is also closed under inversion follows from
T\iota(X_{\mathcal{G}})=X_{\mathcal{G}}\circ\iota
.
∎

6.1.
Existence

The following existence result will be refined later on to incorporate completeness as well.

Proposition 6.3
.

Any locally trivial family of Lie groupoids admits a MEC.

Proof.

Let
\{(U^{\alpha},\psi^{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
be a trivialising cover. We use the local trivialization to obtain for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
a MEC on
\pi^{-1}(U^{\alpha})
with horizontal lift

\hor^{\alpha}\colon\pi^{*}TU^{\alpha}\to T\pi^{-1}(U^{\alpha}),\qquad(x,u)\mapsto(T_{x}\psi^{\alpha})^{-1}(u,0).

Consider a partition of unity
\{\chi^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
on
N
subordinate to the cover
\{U^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
. The pullbacks
\{\pi^{*}\chi^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
define a biinvariant partition of unity on
\mathcal{G}
which is subordinate to
\{\pi^{-1}(U^{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
. We glue the horizontal lifts
\hor^{\alpha}
using this partition of unity to obtain
\hor:=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\pi^{*}\chi^{\alpha}\cdot\hor^{\alpha}
, a horizontal lift corresponding to a multiplicative Ehresmann connection for
\pi
.
∎

Any family of Lie groupoids
\pi\colon(\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M)\to N
with
\pi
proper is locally trivial (see
[
6
]
*Theorem 7.8 and
[
10
]
*Theorem 5.0.7); therefore, it admits a MEC. The techniques developed in
[
6
]
imply an even stronger version of this result.

Theorem 6.4
.

Any family of Lie groupoids
\pi\colon(\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M)\to N
, where
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
is a proper Lie groupoid, admits a MEC.

When
N
is an interval, the result was proven in
[
6
]
*Lemma 6.5 using the notion of deformation cohomology, which is developed in that paper, together with explicit homotopy operators for the deformation cohomology of proper Lie groupoids. The same proof strategy applies in our case. For the convenience of the reader, we present a self-contained proof that avoids the explicit use of deformation cohomology and instead extracts only the minimal tools from
[
6
]
. Nevertheless,
[
6
]
remains indispensable for a deeper understanding of the phenomenon.

As in
[
9
,
6
]
, we will use that on a proper Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
one can build linear averaging operators along the
\boldsymbol{t}
-fibres:

C^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{t}^{-1}(x))\to\mathbb{R},\qquad f\mapsto\int_{\boldsymbol{t}^{-1}(x)}f(h)dh\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{not.}}}{{=}}\int_{x}f(h)dh,

(6)

which are normalized:
\int_{x}dh=1
, left-invariant:
\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}f(gh)dh=\int_{\boldsymbol{t}(g)}f(h)dh
, and smooth: if
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{G})
then
x\mapsto\int_{x}f
is in
C^{\infty}(M)
. These can be used to make vector fields multiplicative.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
be a proper Lie groupoid with averaging operators along the
\boldsymbol{t}
-fibres (
6
). Let
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G})
be an
\boldsymbol{s}
-projectable vector field. Then

g\mapsto\widehat{X}_{g}:=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{gh},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h})\big)dh\in T_{g}\mathcal{G},

defines a multiplicative vector field
\widehat{X}
on
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

That
X
is
\boldsymbol{s}
-projectable is crucial for
T\boldsymbol{m}(X_{gh},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h}))
to be defined. Let us check the multiplicativity conditions now.

First, we calculate
\widehat{X}
along the unit section using the inverse axiom on
T\mathcal{G}

\widehat{X}_{\boldsymbol{u}(x)}=\int_{x}T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{h},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h})\big)dh=\int_{x}T\boldsymbol{t}(X_{h})dh=:V_{x}\in T_{x}M.

Hence
\widehat{X}\circ u=V
, where
V\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is a vector field.

Next, we note that
\widehat{X}
is
\boldsymbol{s}
-projectable to
V

T\boldsymbol{s}(\widehat{X}_{g})=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}T\boldsymbol{s}\circ T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{gh},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h})\big)dh=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}T\boldsymbol{t}(X_{h})dh=V_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}.

Using left-invariance, we obtain that
\widehat{X}
is also
\boldsymbol{t}
-projectable to
V

\displaystyle T\boldsymbol{t}(\widehat{X}_{g})

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}T\boldsymbol{t}\circ T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{gh},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h})\big)dh=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}T\boldsymbol{t}(X_{gh})dh

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{t}(g)}T\boldsymbol{t}(X_{h})dh=V_{\boldsymbol{t}(g)}

To show multiplicativity, note that
\boldsymbol{s}
- and
\boldsymbol{t}
-projectability of
\widehat{X}
imply that, for any composable pair
(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
, the pair
(\widehat{X}_{g_{1}},\widehat{X}_{g_{2}})
is also composable, i.e. it belongs to
(T\mathcal{G})^{(2)}
. Using this and left-invariance, we obtain

\displaystyle T\boldsymbol{m}(\widehat{X}_{g_{1}},\widehat{X}_{g_{2}})

\displaystyle=T\boldsymbol{m}\Big(\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g_{1})}T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{g_{1}h},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h})\big)dh,\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g_{2})}T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{g_{2}h},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h})\big)dh\Big)

\displaystyle=T\boldsymbol{m}\Big(\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g_{1})}T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{g_{1}h},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h})\big)dh,\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g_{1})}T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{h},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{g_{2}^{-1}h})\big)dh\Big),

and using linearity of
T\boldsymbol{m}
, we can further write

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g_{1})}T\boldsymbol{m}\Big(T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{g_{1}h},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h})\big),T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{h},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{g_{2}^{-1}h})\big)\Big)dh

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g_{1})}T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{g_{1}h},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{g_{2}^{-1}h})\big)dh

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g_{2})}T\boldsymbol{m}\big(X_{g_{1}g_{2}h},T\boldsymbol{i}(X_{h})\big)dh=\widehat{X}_{g_{1}g_{2}}.

Compatibility with inversion follows from the other axioms.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.4
.

We start with an Ehresmann connection
\Hor_{0}
on
\pi_{0}\colon M\to N
with horizontal lift
\hor_{0}\colon\mathfrak{X}(N)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Consider a second Ehresmann connection
\Hor_{\boldsymbol{s}}
for the submersion
\boldsymbol{s}\colon\mathcal{G}\to M
with horizontal lift
\hor_{\boldsymbol{s}}\colon\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G})
. For
\pi
we obtain an induced Ehresmann connection
\widetilde{\Hor}:=\Hor_{\boldsymbol{s}}\cap T\pi^{-1}(\Hor_{0})
with horizontal lift
\widetilde{\hor}=\hor_{\boldsymbol{s}}\circ\hor_{0}\colon\mathfrak{X}(N)\to\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{G})
. Note that
\widetilde{\hor}(X)
is
\boldsymbol{s}
-projectable to
\hor_{0}(X)
. We use the previous lemma to make the vector field
\widetilde{\hor}(X)
multiplicative, hence we define:

\hor\colon\mathfrak{X}(N)\to\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{mult}}(\mathcal{G}),\qquad\hor(X):=\widehat{\widetilde{\hor}(X)}.

Since
\pi\circ\boldsymbol{m}(g,h)=\pi(g)
, we have that

\displaystyle T_{g}\pi(\hor(X))

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}T\pi\circ T\boldsymbol{m}\big(\widetilde{\hor}(X)_{gh},T\boldsymbol{i}(\widetilde{\hor}(X)_{h})\big)dh

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}T\pi(\widetilde{\hor}(X)_{gh})dh=\int_{\boldsymbol{s}(g)}X_{x}dh=X_{x},

where
x=\pi(g)=\pi(gh)\in N
. Hence
\hor(X)
is
\pi
-projectable to
X
. As the fibres of
\pi
are
\mathcal{G}
-invariant, the construction implies that
X\mapsto\hor(X)
is
C^{\infty}(N)
-linear, and so it corresponds to the lift of an Ehresmann connection
\Hor
for
\pi
. Lemma
6.1
implies that
\Hor
is multiplicative.
∎

Remark

In

, the condition that

be a proper (or even source-proper) Lie groupoid does not imply local triviality of the family. The example, which relies on earlier work of Palais and Richardson, consists of a smooth family of actions

\mathcal{G}=(G\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathbb{R}

6.4
still yields a MEC for this family.

6.2.
Completeness

We recall Theorem
5.6
in the case of families.

Corollary 6.6
.

For a source-connected family of Lie groupoids
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to N
, a multiplicative Ehresmann connection
\Hor
is complete if and only if the induced connection
\Hor_{0}
on the base is complete.

6.3.
Existence of complete connections

A surjective submersion
\pi_{0}\colon M\to N
is a locally trivial fibration if and only if it admits a complete Ehresmann connection. Somewhat surprisingly, this “classical” result was only recently given a correct proof by del Hoyo
[
11
]
.

In this subsection, we discuss the relation between the existence of complete multiplicative connections and local triviality of a family of Lie groupoids, in the sense of Definition
6.2
. Clearly, if a family admits a complete MEC, then its parallel transport yields local groupoid trivializations. Conversely, if
\pi
is a locally trivial family, then, as seen in Proposition
6.3
, it admits a MEC—which is, however, not guaranteed to be complete.

The main result of this section, which generalizes
[
11
]
, is the following criterion for the existence of complete connections on locally trivial bundles.

Theorem 6.7
.

A locally trivial family of Lie groupoids whose typical fibre is a source-proper Lie groupoid admits a complete MEC.

The proof mimics and extends the techniques developed in
[
11
]
. First, we give a geometric criterion for completeness of connections.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
p\colon A\to B
be a locally trivial fibre bundle with typical fibre
F
, endowed with an Ehresmann connection
\Hor
. If there exists a trivialising cover

\{\psi^{\alpha}\colon p^{-1}(U^{\alpha})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.68887pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{\smash{$\sim$}}}U^{\alpha}\times F\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}

and, for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
, a closed subset
S^{\alpha}\subset F
such that:

(1)

T\psi^{\alpha}(\Hor)|_{U^{\alpha}\times S^{\alpha}}=TU^{\alpha}\times 0_{S^{\alpha}}
and

(2)

the connected components of
F\backslash S^{\alpha}
are precompact,

then
\Hor
is complete.

Proof.

Assume first that
A=B\times F
. Consider a connection
\Hor
for the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{1}\colon B\times F\to B
, and a closed subset
S\subset F
satisfying the above conditions. Let
\eta\colon I\to B
be a curve. For any
x\in F
, the horizontal lift of
\eta
starting at
(\eta(0),x)
has the form
\tau^{t}_{\eta}(\eta(0),x)=(\eta(t),\xi(x,t))
. Note that
x\mapsto\xi(x,t)
is the flow of the time dependent vector field
X(\cdot,t)
on
F
defined by the relation

\hor_{(\eta(t),x)}\big(\tfrac{d}{dt}\eta(t)\big)=\big(\tfrac{d}{dt}\eta(t),X(x,t)\big).

To show completeness of
\Hor
, we need to show that, for any
x\in F
, the flow line
t\mapsto\xi(x,t)
of
X
is defined on
I
. This follows as we are in either of two cases:

•

If
x\in S
, then by (1)
X(x,t)=0
for all
t\in I
, so
t\mapsto\xi(x,t)=x
is the flow line through
x
, which is defined on
I
.

•

If
x\notin S
, then
\xi(x,t)
stays within the connected component of
x
in
F\backslash S
, denoted
\operatorname{Conn}(F\backslash S,x)
. Indeed, otherwise there is
t_{0}>0
such that
\xi(x,t_{0})=x_{0}\in S
, and so
t\mapsto x_{0}
is a integral curve of
v
that coincides with
\xi(x,t)
at time
t=t_{0}
, therefore they must be equal, which yields the contradiction:
x=x_{0}\in S
. Hence,
\xi(x,t)
must stay within the compact set
\overline{\operatorname{Conn}(F\backslash S,x)}
, which implies that it is defined on the whole of
I
.

The general case of a locally trivial fibre bundle follows by using the above argument along a finite cover of a path by local trivialisations.
∎

Appendix A
Lie groupoid fibrations

Lie groupoid fibrations (recall Definition
2.1
) were studied before; for the non-smooth setting see
[
2
,
4
]
, and for the smooth setting see
[

,

]
. In this appendix, we provide simpler criteria for a functor to be a Lie groupoid fibration. We apply these to show that conclude that uniform Lie groupoid fibrations are indeed Lie groupoid fibrations.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
\pi
be a Lie groupoid morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
with base map
\pi_{0}\colon M\to N
, and denote

\pi^{!}:=\pi\times\boldsymbol{s}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}{}_{s}\times_{\pi_{0}}M.

Any of the following conditions is equivalent to
\pi
being a Lie groupoid fibration.

(1)

The maps
\pi_{0}
and
\pi^{!}
are surjective submersions.

(2)

The map
\pi
is a surjective submersion and the map
\pi^{!}
is surjective.

(3)

The map
\pi
is a surjective submersion, and it restricts to a surjective map between source-fibres, i.e.,
\pi\colon\boldsymbol{s}^{-1}(x)\to\boldsymbol{s}^{-1}(\pi_{0}(x))
is surjective for any
x\in M
.

In the proof, we will use some auxiliary results.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
f\colon X\to Y
is a surjective submersion. Then for any smooth map
g\colon Z\to Y
, the base change map
\widehat{f}\colon Z{}_{g}\times_{f}X\to Z
is a surjective submersion.

Proof.

Let
z\in Z
, and let
y:=g(z)
. Since
f
is surjective, there exists
x\in X
such that
f(x)=y
. In other words
(z,x)\in Z{}_{g}\times_{f}X
and
\widehat{f}(z,x)=z
. So
\widehat{f}
is surjective. Moreover, since
f
is a submersion, for any
x\in f^{-1}(y)
we find a smooth local section
\sigma:U\to X
of
f
, i.e.,
f\circ\sigma=\mathrm{id}_{U}
, such that
\sigma(y)=x
. Then the map

\widehat{\sigma}\colon g^{-1}(U)\to Z{}_{g}\times_{f}X,\qquad w\mapsto(w,\sigma(g(w)))

is a smooth local section of
\widehat{f}
with
\widehat{\sigma}(z)=(z,x)
. Hence
\widehat{f}
is a submersion.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Consider a commutative diagram of vector spaces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Suppose that
F
is surjective and the restriction
G\colon\ker F\to\ker f
is surjective, then
G\times F\colon U\to X{}_{f}\times_{g}Y
is surjective.

Proof.

Take some
(x,y)\in X{}_{f}\times_{g}Y
and notice that by surjectivity of
F
we can find

such that
F(u_{1})=y
. It follows that

f(x-G(u_{1}))=f(x)-f(G(u_{1}))=f(x)-g(F(u_{1}))=f(x)-g(y)=0.

Therefore,

for which

F(u_{1}+u_{2})=F(u_{1})=y,\quad G(u_{1}+u_{2})=x\implies G\times F(u)=(x,y).

Therefore,

is surjective.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

Consider a commutative diagram of sets

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The map

is surjective.

(2)

For all

Proof.

(

)
\implies
(

): Suppose

is surjective and pick some arbitrary

and

. Therefore, the restriction

(

(

Corollary A.5
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a Lie groupoid fibration and
\phi\colon\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{H}
be a Lie groupoid morphism. Then
\widehat{\pi}:\mathcal{K}{}_{\phi}\times_{\pi}\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{K}
is also a Lie groupoid fibration.

Proof.

Lemma
A.2
shows that
\widehat{\pi}
is a surjective submersion. By Theorem
A.1
(c), we need only show that
\widehat{\pi}
is surjective between source fibres. Denote the bases of the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
,
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
by
M
,
N
and
K
, respectively. Pick some
(x,y)\in K{}_{\phi_{0}}\times_{\pi_{0}}M
and
k\in\boldsymbol{s}^{-1}(x)\subset\mathcal{K}
. Note that

\boldsymbol{s}(\phi(k))=\phi_{0}(\boldsymbol{s}(k))=\phi_{0}(x)=\pi_{0}(y).

So,
(\phi(k),y)\in\mathcal{H}{}_{\boldsymbol{s}}\times_{\pi_{0}}M
. As
\pi
is a Lie groupoid fibration, there exists some
g\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\pi^{!}(g)=(\pi(g),\boldsymbol{s}(g))=(\phi(k),y)
. So
(k,g)\in\mathcal{K}{}_{\phi}\times_{\pi}\mathcal{G}
,
\widehat{\pi}(k,g)=k
and
\boldsymbol{s}((k,g))=(x,y)
. This shows that
\widehat{\pi}
is surjective between source-fibres.
∎

Next, we show that the name
uniform Lie groupoid fibration
is indeed justified.

Corollary A.6
.

Any uniform Lie groupoid fibration is a Lie groupoid fibration.

Proof.

Suppose that
\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is a uniform Lie groupoid fibration. Note that the map
\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{H}
is a Lie groupoid extension, and thus, in particular, a Lie groupoid fibration. Additionally, as
\pi_{0}\times\pi_{0}\colon M\times M\to N\times N
is a Lie groupoid fibration, by Corollary

, so is the base-change
\widehat{\pi_{0}\times\pi_{0}}\colon\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
. This implies that
\pi=\widehat{\pi_{0}\times\pi_{0}}\circ\pi^{*}
is also a Lie groupoid fibration.
∎

Appendix B
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subgroupoids

The following statement characterizes
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subgroupoids as subgroupoids that are vector bundles, and has been used several times in the paper. The result generalizes the first part of
[
18
]
*Lemma 3.5, and is proven similarly.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
(\Gamma,E\mathbin{\mkern-2.0mu/\mkern-6.0mu/\mkern-2.0mu}\mathcal{G},M)
be a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-groupoid. A vector subbundle
S\subset\Gamma
over
\mathcal{G}
which is a subgroupoid is a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-subgroupoid of
\Gamma
with base
S|_{M}\cap E
.

Remark
.

In the statement, we allow the vector bundle
S|_{M}\cap E
to have possibly different ranks over different connected components of
M
. In fact, from the proof one can easily see that the rank of
S|_{M}\cap E
will be the same over two connected components
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
that are related by an arrow
g\in\mathcal{G}
with
\boldsymbol{s}(g)\in M_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{t}(g)\in M_{2}
.

\vartriangle

In the following auxiliary result, we also allow
V_{1}\cap V_{2}
to have different ranks over different connected components.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
V
be a vector bundle and
V_{1},V_{2}\subset V
vector subbundles, all over the connected manifold
M
. Then
V_{1}\cap V_{2}
is a vector subbundle if and only if each
v\in V_{1}\cap V_{2}
can be extended to a smooth local section of
V
with values in
V_{1}\cap V_{2}
.

Proof.

As
V_{1}\cap V_{2}
is the kernel of the vector bundle map
V_{1}\to V/V_{2}
, it is a vector subbundle if and only if it has locally constant rank. As a kernel of a vector bundle map, its rank can only drop locally. The property that any
v\in V_{1}\cap V_{2}
can be extended to a local section implies that the rank can only grow locally, and thus, it implies that
V_{1}\cap V_{2}
is a vector subbundle. The converse is obvious.
∎

Proof of Proposition
B.1
.

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1:
S|_{M}\cap E
is a vector subbundle of
E
.

Here we identify
E
with the unit section
E\equiv\boldsymbol{u}(E)\subset\Gamma
, which is a vector subbundle over the unit section of
\mathcal{G}
, which we also identify as
M\equiv\boldsymbol{u}(M)\subset\mathcal{G}
. So
S|_{M}
and
E
are vector subbundles of
\Gamma|_{M}
. We check the criterion in Lemma
B.2
for each connected component of
M
. Let
e\in(S\cap E)_{x}
. Pick a local section of
S|_{M}
,
\sigma\colon U\subset M\to S|_{M}
, such that
\sigma(x)=e
. Then
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}\circ\sigma\colon U\to E
is a smooth section of
E
with values in
S|_{M}\cap E
such that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}\circ\sigma(x)=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}(e)=e
.

Step 2:
S|_{M}\cap C
is a vector bundle, where
C:=\ker\widetilde{s}|_{M}
is the core of
\Gamma
.

Notice that
\Gamma|_{M}=C\oplus E
, with projection to
E
given by
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}\colon\Gamma|_{M}\to E
and inclusion of
E
given by
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\colon E\to\Gamma|_{M}
. Since
S
is a groupoid, it is invariant under these two maps, which shows that
S|_{M}=(S|_{M}\cap C)\oplus(S|_{M}\cap E)
. Using Step 1, it follows that
S|_{M}\cap C
has locally constant rank, which implies the claim.

Step 3:
S\cap\ker\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}
is a vector subbundle.

We first note that
\ker\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}
is canonically isomorphic to
\boldsymbol{t}^{*}C
via the morphism:

j\colon\boldsymbol{t}^{*}C\to\ker\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}},\qquad(c,g)\mapsto c\cdot 0_{g},\quad c\in C_{\boldsymbol{t}(g)};

in other words, right translation by
0_{g}\in\Gamma_{g}
, denoted as
r_{0_{g}}\colon C_{\boldsymbol{t}(g)}\to\ker\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}_{g}
, is a linear isomorphism. Now as
S
is a subgroupoid of
\Gamma
, this map restricts to an isomorphism
r_{0_{g}}\colon S_{\boldsymbol{t}(g)}\cap C_{\boldsymbol{t}(g)}\to S_{g}\cap\ker\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}_{g}
. So
S\cap\ker\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}
has locally constant rank. By Lemma
B.2
, it follows that it is a vector subbundle.

Step 4:
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}\colon S\to S|_{M}\cap E
is a surjective submersion.

First, the previous steps imply that the map is fibrewise surjective:

\mathrm{rank}(S|_{M}\cap E)=\mathrm{rank}(S|_{M})-\mathrm{rank}(S|_{M}\cap C)=\mathrm{rank}(S)-\mathrm{rank}(S\cap\ker\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}})=\mathrm{rank}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{s}}|_{S}).

Since the map covers the surjective submersion
\boldsymbol{s}
, it is a surjective submersion as well.

Step 5: Conclusion.

We can conclude that
S\rightrightarrows S|_{M}\cap E
is a Lie groupoid. Being contained in
\Gamma\rightrightarrows E
, it is compatible with the vector bundle structure, so as to be a
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{B}
-groupoid.
∎
