Edge partitions into induced- 2 ​ K 2 2K_{2} -free bipartite graphs
András London

Abstract
. We study edge partitions of a bipartite graph into induced- 2 ​ K 2 2K_{2} -free bipartite graphs, i.e. into Ferrers (chain) graphs. We define fp ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{fp}(G) as the minimum number of parts in such a partition. We prove general lower and upper bounds in terms of induced matchings and Dilworth widths of neighborhood posets. We compute the parameter exactly for paths and even cycles, and we exhibit separations showing that the induced-matching lower bound and the width upper bound can both be far from tight. We also record a simple host-induced conflict-graph lower bound, present a 0 0 – 1 1 matrix viewpoint, and add some complexity remarks.

Related Work

Ferrers graphs appear throughout the literature under the names

and

[

They admit the familiar forbidden induced subgraph characterization, namely the

Two nearby lines of work should be distinguished from the present paper.
First,

[

[

Second,

]

Edge partitions into constrained bipartite subgraphs have also been studied recently in a broader “template class” framework; see

Finally, we should mention Gera et al. works

3.
Definitions

All graphs are finite and simple. Let
G=(U,V,E)
be bipartite with color classes
U
and
V
. All subgraphs considered below are spanning on the same bipartition
(U,V)
; only the edge set is partitioned (isolated vertices are allowed in parts).
We write
2K_{2}
for the graph on four vertices consisting of two disjoint edges.

Definition 1
.

A bipartite graph is a
Ferrers graph
(chain graph) if it contains no induced copy of
2K_{2}
.
Equivalently, the family (of neighborhoods)
\{N(u):u\in U\}
(or symmetrically
\{N(v):v\in V\}
) is totally ordered by inclusion.

Proposition 1
.

A Ferrers graph has at most one connected component containing an edge.
Equivalently, if a bipartite graph contains two vertex-disjoint edges in distinct connected components,
then it contains an induced
2K_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
e=uv
and
f=u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
be edges in different connected components.
There are no edges between
\{u,v\}
and
\{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\}
, hence the subgraph induced by

\{u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\}
consists of exactly the two edges
e
and
f
, i.e. an induced
2K_{2}
.
Therefore a Ferrers graph (which is induced-
2K_{2}
-free) cannot contain edges in two distinct components.
∎

Definition 2
.

For a bipartite graph
G
, the
Ferrers partition number

\operatorname{fp}(G)
is the minimum
k
such that

E(G)
can be partitioned into a disjoint union of
k
edge sets, each inducing a Ferrers graph on the same bipartition, i.e.

E=E_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}E_{k},

where each
G_{i}=(U,V,E_{i})
is a Ferrers graph.

This parameter reacts sharply to induced
2K_{2}
obstructions, and its behavior differs from cover/intersection models.
Even for very classical families (paths, cycles, crown graphs) one sees both tightness and large gaps.

Remark 3
.

Győrffy et al.
[
7
]
define
\chi^{\prime}_{H}(G)
as the minimum size of an edge partition of
G
into bipartite graphs avoiding
H
as an induced subgraph.
For bipartite
G
and
H=2K_{2}
we have
\operatorname{fp}(G)=\chi^{\prime}_{2K_{2}}(G)
.

Definition 4
.

Given graph
G=(V,E)
, a set
M\subseteq E(G)
is an
induced matching
if
M
is a matching and for any two distinct edges
uv,u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in M
there are no edges of
G
among

\{u,u^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\}
except
uv
and
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
. In particular, in a bipartite graph this means

uv^{\prime}\notin E(G)
and
u^{\prime}v\notin E(G)
.

Definition 5
.

Given graph
G=(V,E)
, the
induced matching number
is the maximum size of an induced matching in
G
.

4.
General Bounds

4.1.
A lower bound via induced matchings

Lemma 6
.

For every bipartite graph
G
,

\operatorname{fp}(G)\geq\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(G).

Proof.

A Ferrers graph contains no induced
2K_{2}
(Prop.
1
), hence contains at most one edge from any induced matching.
In an edge partition into
k
Ferrers graphs, each part contains at most one induced-matching edge, so
\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(G)\leq k
.
∎

4.2.
An upper bound via Dilworth chain decompositions

For
u\in U
, let
N(u)\subseteq V
be its neighborhood.
Let
\mathcal{N}_{U}=\{N(u):u\in U\}
partially ordered by inclusion, and write
0pt(\mathcal{P}_{U})
for the maximum size of an antichain of the corresponding neighborhood-inclusion poset (multiplicities do not affect width) on vertices of
U
(and
V
, resp.) defined by
u\preceq u^{\prime}
iff
N(u)\subseteq N(u^{\prime})
.

Theorem 7
.

For every bipartite graph
G=(U,V,E)
,

\operatorname{fp}(G)\leq\min\big(0pt(\mathcal{P}_{U}),\,0pt(\mathcal{P}_{V})\big).

Proof.

By Dilworth’s theorem
[
16
]
,
\mathcal{N}_{U}
can be partitioned into
w=0pt(\mathcal{P}_{U})
chains.
This yields a partition
U=U_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}U_{w}
such that within each
U_{i}
the neighborhoods are totally ordered by inclusion.
Let
E_{i}
be the edges incident to
U_{i}
; then
(U,V,E_{i})
is Ferrers and
E=\dot{\cup}_{i}E_{i}
.
Thus
\operatorname{fp}(G)\leq w
.
The same argument applies on
V
, giving the stated minimum.
∎

5.
Exact values and bounds for some families

Throughout,
P_{n}
denotes the
n
-vertex path and
C_{n}
the
n
-vertex cycle.

Lemma 8
.

Any Ferrers subgraph of a path or a cycle contains at most
3
edges.

Proof.

Any subgraph of a path or cycle has maximum degree at most
2
, hence every connected component is a path.
If a Ferrers subgraph contains a component isomorphic to
P_{5}
, then its two end edges are vertex-disjoint and there is no edge between their endpoints inside the subgraph; hence they induce a
2K_{2}
, a contradiction. Thus each nontrivial component has at most
3
edges. Finally, by Prop.
1
a Ferrers graph has at most one nontrivial component, so the entire Ferrers subgraph has at most
3
edges.
∎

5.1.
Paths

Theorem 9
.

For the path
P_{n}
on
n\geq 2
vertices,

\operatorname{fp}(P_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\right\rceil\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(P_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\right\rceil.

In particular,
\operatorname{fp}(P_{n})=\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(P_{n})
for all
n
.

Proof.

Let
m=n-1
be the number of edges of
P_{n}
.
Partition the edges into consecutive blocks of
3
edges (and a final block of size
1
or
2
if needed).
Each block induces
P_{2}
,
P_{3}
, or
P_{4}
, hence is Ferrers, so
\operatorname{fp}(P_{n})\leq\lceil m/3\rceil
.
By Lemma
8
, every Ferrers subgraph of
P_{n}
has at most
3
edges, so
\operatorname{fp}(P_{n})\geq\lceil m/3\rceil
.

For the induced matching number, selecting every third edge along the path gives an induced matching of size
\lceil m/3\rceil
, and no induced matching can use more than one edge among any
3
consecutive edges, so
\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(P_{n})=\lceil m/3\rceil
.
∎

5.2.
Even cycles

Theorem 10
.

For the cycle
C_{n}
on
n\geq 4
vertices,

\operatorname{fp}(C_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{n}{3}\right\rceil\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(C_{n})=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\right\rfloor.

In particular, for even cycles
C_{2t}
(which are bipartite),

\operatorname{fp}(C_{2t})\in\Big\{\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(C_{2t}),\,\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(C_{2t})+1\Big\}.

Proof.

The cycle
C_{n}
has
n
edges.
Partition the edges into consecutive blocks of
3
around the cycle (with a final block of size
1
or
2
if needed).
Each block induces a path on at most
4
vertices, hence is Ferrers, so
\operatorname{fp}(C_{n})\leq\lceil n/3\rceil
.
Lemma
8
gives the reverse inequality.

For
\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(C_{n})
, selecting every third edge yields an induced matching of size
\lfloor n/3\rfloor
.
Conversely, an induced matching cannot contain two edges within the same triple of consecutive edges, so it has size at most
\lfloor n/3\rfloor
.
∎

5.3.
Ladders

Let
L_{n}=P_{n}\square P_{2}
denote the ladder graph on
2n
vertices.
We label the vertices in the two rails by
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
(top) and
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
(bottom),
and the edges are the rails
t_{i}t_{i+1}
,
b_{i}b_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, together with the rungs
t_{i}b_{i}
for

1\leq i\leq n
.

Theorem 11
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(L_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Lower bound.

The set of rungs
\{t_{i}b_{i}:i\ \text{odd}\}
is a matching of size
\lceil n/2\rceil
.
If
i
and
j
are distinct odd indices, then
|i-j|\geq 2
, hence there is no edge of
L_{n}
between the vertex pairs

\{t_{i},b_{i}\}
and
\{t_{j},b_{j}\}
(all edges of
L_{n}
lie within a column or between adjacent columns).
Thus these rungs form an induced matching, and
\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(L_{n})\geq\lceil n/2\rceil
.

Upper bound.

For an edge
e
of
L_{n}
, define its
start column

s(e)
by

s(t_{i}b_{i})=i,\qquad s(t_{i}t_{i+1})=i,\qquad s(b_{i}b_{i+1})=i.

We claim that if
M
is an induced matching in
L_{n}
, then the start columns
\{s(e):e\in M\}
differ by at least
2
:
there do not exist distinct
e,f\in M
with
s(f)=s(e)+1
.

Indeed, fix
i
and suppose
s(e)=i
and
s(f)=i+1
.
Every edge with start column
i
contains at least one of
t_{i},b_{i},t_{i+1},b_{i+1}
, and every edge with start column
i+1

contains at least one of
t_{i+1},b_{i+1},t_{i+2},b_{i+2}
.
In all possible cases, there is an edge of
L_{n}
between an endpoint of
e
and an endpoint of
f
:
either the two edges share a vertex, or one uses
t_{i+1}
and the other uses
b_{i+1}
(joined by the rung
t_{i+1}b_{i+1}
),
or one uses
t_{i+1}
and the other uses
t_{i+2}
(joined by
t_{i+1}t_{i+2}
), or one uses
b_{i+1}
and the other uses
b_{i+2}

(joined by
b_{i+1}b_{i+2}
).
Any such adjacency between endpoints is forbidden in an induced matching, so
s(f)\neq s(e)+1
.

Consequently, the set
\{s(e):e\in M\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
has pairwise gaps at least
2
, hence has size at most
\lceil n/2\rceil
.
Therefore
\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(L_{n})\leq\lceil n/2\rceil
.
∎

Corollary 12
.

For all
n\geq 1
,

\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil\leq\operatorname{fp}(L_{n})\leq n.

Proof.

Lower bound immediately follows form Theorem
11
and Lemma
6
. It is easy to see that the
Dilworth-width of the corresponding poset is
n
and apply Theorem
7
.
∎

Remark 13
.

For the ladder graph, is easy to see that the lower bound is the truth. Consider the ladder of 8 vertices. Two letter “P” graph put together in a right way covers the graph, and they are Ferrers. We leave the rest to the Reader.

6.
Additivity and Separations

6.1.
Additivity over disjoint unions

Lemma 14
.

Let
G
and
H
be vertex-disjoint graphs, each with at least one edge, and let
G\dot{\cup}H
denote their disjoint union.
Then

\operatorname{fp}(G\dot{\cup}H)=\operatorname{fp}(G)+\operatorname{fp}(H),\qquad\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(G\dot{\cup}H)=\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(G)+\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(H).

Proof.

Induced matchings are additive over disjoint unions.

For
\operatorname{fp}
, note that if a Ferrers graph contains edges from two different components of
G\dot{\cup}H
, then it contains two vertex-disjoint edges with no edges between their endpoints (since the host has no such edges), hence an induced
2K_{2}
, impossible.
Thus every Ferrers part in a partition of
G\dot{\cup}H
lies entirely within one component, and the minimum number of parts is the sum.
∎

6.2.
A growing gap between
\operatorname{fp}
and
\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}

Corollary 15
.

Let
F_{t}
be the disjoint union of
t
copies of
C_{8}
.
Then

\operatorname{fp}(F_{t})-\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(F_{t})=t.

Proof.

By Theorem
10
,
\operatorname{fp}(C_{8})=\lceil 8/3\rceil=3
and
\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(C_{8})=\lfloor 8/3\rfloor=2
.
Apply Lemma
14
.
∎

6.3.
A separation for the Dilworth-width upper bound

Definition 16
.

The
crown graph

H_{n}
(
n\geq 3
) is obtained from
K_{n,n}
by deleting a perfect matching:

U=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
,
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
, and
u_{i}v_{j}\in E(H_{n})
iff
i\neq j
.

Theorem 17
.

For the crown graph
H_{n}
with
n\geq 3
,

\operatorname{fp}(H_{n})=2,\qquad 0pt(\mathcal{P}_{U})=0pt(\mathcal{P}_{V})=n.

In particular, the Dilworth-width bound in Theorem
7
can be off by a factor
\Theta(n)
.

Proof.

Order
U
and
V
by indices.
Partition
E(H_{n})
into

E^{+}:=\{u_{i}v_{j}:i<j\},\qquad E^{-}:=\{u_{i}v_{j}:i>j\}.

Then
E(H_{n})=E^{+}\dot{\cup}E^{-}
.

In
G^{+}=(U,V,E^{+})
, we have

N_{G^{+}}(u_{1})\supseteq N_{G^{+}}(u_{2})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq N_{G^{+}}(u_{n})=\varnothing
,
so
G^{+}
is Ferrers; similarly
G^{-}
is Ferrers.
Thus
\operatorname{fp}(H_{n})\leq 2
.
Since
H_{n}
contains an induced
2K_{2}
(e.g. edges
u_{1}v_{2}
and
u_{2}v_{1}
), it is not Ferrers, hence
\operatorname{fp}(H_{n})\geq 2
.

Finally,
N(u_{i})=V\setminus\{v_{i}\}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, and these
n
sets are pairwise incomparable, hence form an antichain of size
n
.
So
0pt(\mathcal{P}_{U})=n
, and it holds symmetrically on
V
.
∎

Theorem 18
.

Let
G=K_{m,n}\setminus M
, where
M
is a matching of size
t
in
K_{m,n}
.
Then

\operatorname{fp}(G)=\begin{cases}1,&t\leq 1,\\
2,&t\geq 2.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
t=0
, then
G=K_{m,n}
is Ferrers, so
\operatorname{fp}(G)=1
.
If
t=1
, say the missing edge is
u_{1}v_{1}
, then all neighborhoods on the
U
-side are comparable:

N(u_{1})=V\setminus\{v_{1}\}\subseteq V=N(u_{i})
for
i\geq 2
, hence
G
is Ferrers and
\operatorname{fp}(G)=1
.

Assume now that
t\geq 2
.
After relabeling, we may assume the deleted matching is

M=\{u_{i}v_{i}:i=1,\dots,t\}
with
U=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
and
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
.
Then
N(u_{i})=V\setminus\{v_{i}\}
for
i=1,\dots,t
, and these neighborhoods are pairwise incomparable for
t\geq 2
.
In particular,
G
is not Ferrers, so
\operatorname{fp}(G)\geq 2
.

We show
\operatorname{fp}(G)\leq 2
by constructing a partition of
E(G)
into two Ferrers subgraphs.
Let
V_{0}=\{v_{t+1},\dots,v_{n}\}
and
U_{0}=\{u_{t+1},\dots,u_{m}\}
denote the vertices not incident to the deleted matching.
Define

E^{+}:=\{u_{i}v_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq t\}\ \cup\ \{u_{i}v:1\leq i\leq t,\ v\in V_{0}\},

and let
E^{-}:=E(G)\setminus E^{+}
.
Clearly
E(G)=E^{+}\dot{\cup}E^{-}
.

In
G^{+}=(U,V,E^{+})
, the
U
-side neighborhoods satisfy

N_{G^{+}}(u_{1})\supseteq N_{G^{+}}(u_{2})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq N_{G^{+}}(u_{t})\supseteq N_{G^{+}}(u_{t+1})=\cdots=N_{G^{+}}(u_{m})=\varnothing,

so
G^{+}
is Ferrers.

For
G^{-}=(U,V,E^{-})
, consider neighborhoods on the
V
-side.
For
1\leq j\leq t
we have

N_{G^{-}}(v_{j})=\{u_{i}:1\leq i\leq t,\ i>j\}\ \cup\ U_{0},

while for
v\in V_{0}
we have
N_{G^{-}}(v)=U_{0}
.
Hence

N_{G^{-}}(v_{1})\supseteq N_{G^{-}}(v_{2})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq N_{G^{-}}(v_{t})=U_{0}=N_{G^{-}}(v)\ \ (v\in V_{0}),

so
G^{-}
is Ferrers.
Thus
\operatorname{fp}(G)\leq 2
, and together with
\operatorname{fp}(G)\geq 2
we obtain
\operatorname{fp}(G)=2
for
t\geq 2
.
∎

7.
A host-induced
2K_{2}
conflict graph lower bound

The obstruction
2K_{2}
can be viewed from two perspectives: it may be induced
inside a part
, or it may be forced by missing cross-edges already in the host graph.
The latter yields a simple and useful necessary condition.

Definition 19
.

Let
G=(U,V,E)
be bipartite. The
host-induced
2K_{2}
conflict graph

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{host}}(G)
is the graph with vertex set
E
.
Two distinct edges
e=uv
and
f=u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
(with
u,u^{\prime}\in U
and
v,v^{\prime}\in V
) are adjacent if

u\neq u^{\prime}
,
v\neq v^{\prime}
, and the cross-edges are
absent in the host
:

uv^{\prime}\notin E
and
u^{\prime}v\notin E
.

Proposition 2
.

For every bipartite graph
G
,

\chi\big(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{host}}(G)\big)\leq\operatorname{fp}(G).

Proof.

Suppose
E=E_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}E_{k}
and each
G_{i}=(U,V,E_{i})
is Ferrers.
If two edges
e,f\in E
are adjacent in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{host}}(G)
, then the cross-edges
uv^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime}v
do not exist in
G
, hence they do not exist in any subgraph
G_{i}
.
Therefore
e
and
f
cannot lie in the same Ferrers part
E_{i}
, since they would form an induced
2K_{2}
inside
G_{i}
.
Thus each
E_{i}
is an independent set of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{host}}(G)
, and so
k\geq\chi(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{host}}(G))
.
∎

Remark 20
.

In general,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{host}}(G)
only captures
forced

2K_{2}
obstructions.
It does
not
characterize feasibility of a Ferrers partition: for
G=K_{2,2}
, the set of two opposite edges is independent in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{host}}(G)
,
yet it induces a
2K_{2}
as a subgraph and hence is not Ferrers.

7.1.
A worked example: the crown graph
H_{4}

Let
H_{4}
be the crown graph on
U=\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}\}
and
V=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
, i.e.
H_{4}=K_{4,4}\setminus\{u_{i}v_{i}:i\in[4]\}
.
Its bipartite adjacency matrix (rows indexed by
U
, columns by
V
) is
J-I
:

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1&1\\
1&0&1&1\\
1&1&0&1\\
1&1&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Define
E^{+}:=\{u_{i}v_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq 4\}
and

E^{-}:=\{u_{i}v_{j}:1\leq j<i\leq 4\}
.
Then
E(H_{4})=E^{+}\dot{\cup}E^{-}
, and both
G^{+}=(U,V,E^{+})
and
G^{-}=(U,V,E^{-})
are Ferrers:
in
G^{+}
the
U
-side neighborhoods satisfy

N(u_{1})\supseteq N(u_{2})\supseteq N(u_{3})\supseteq N(u_{4})
,
while in
G^{-}
the
V
-side neighborhoods satisfy

N(v_{1})\supseteq N(v_{2})\supseteq N(v_{3})\supseteq N(v_{4})
.
Hence
\operatorname{fp}(H_{4})\leq 2
, and since
H_{4}
is not Ferrers we have
\operatorname{fp}(H_{4})=2
.
Moreover, in this example the lower bound from Proposition
2
is tight:
\chi(\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{host}}(H_{4}))=2
.

8.
Matrix Viewpoint

Let
A\in\{0,1\}^{m\times n}
be the bipartite adjacency matrix of
G
.
A Ferrers graph corresponds to a matrix that can be permuted so that each row is a (possibly empty) prefix of ones.
This gives a compact matrix restatement of
\operatorname{fp}(G)
.

Remark 21
.

\operatorname{fp}(G)
is the minimum
k
such that the
1
-entries of
A
can be partitioned into
k
disjoint supports, each support forming a Ferrers
0
–
1
matrix (allowing independent row/column permutations for each part).

For the crown graph
H_{n}
, the matrix is
J-I
(all ones except the diagonal).
The decomposition
E(H_{n})=E^{+}\dot{\cup}E^{-}
from Theorem
17
is simply the split of
J-I
into its strictly upper- and strictly lower-triangular parts (after ordering rows/columns by index), and each part is Ferrers.
This example is a useful reminder that neighborhood-width can be a coarse proxy for
\operatorname{fp}
.

Theorem
18
can be phrased neatly in matrix terms.
If
A
is the
m\times n
all-ones matrix with zeros at the positions of a matching (i.e. the zero pattern is a partial permutation matrix), then the
1
-entries of
A
can be partitioned into two Ferrers shapes unless there is at most one zero, in which case
A
itself is Ferrers.

9.
Computational complexity

9.1.
Decision problem.

We formalize the natural decision version of
\operatorname{fp}
.

Problem 22

(
Ferrers-
k
-Partition
)
.

Instance: A bipartite graph
G=(U,V,E)
and an integer
k\geq 1
.
Question: Does there exist a partition
E=E_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}E_{k}
such that each

G_{i}=(U,V,E_{i})
is Ferrers (equivalently, an induced-
2K_{2}
-free bipartite) graph?

Proposition 3
.

Ferrers-
k
-Partition
belongs to
\operatorname{NP}
.

Proof.

A certificate is a map
c:E\to[k]
defining
E_{i}=\{e\in E:c(e)=i\}
.
Given
E_{i}
, we can test in polynomial time whether
G_{i}
is a chain graph/Ferrers graph,
for instance by attempting to produce a nested-neighborhood ordering on one side and rejecting otherwise.
Moreover, chain graphs admit linear-time
certifying
recognition algorithms
[
8
]
.
∎

Problem
22
with
k=1
is exactly recognition of chain graphs (Ferrers graphs), which is solvable in linear time with certificates
[
8
]
.

9.2.
Relation to the template-partition framework.

According to Győrffy et al. for bipartite host graphs
G
and
H=2K_{2}
, the
H
-free bipartite graphs are precisely Ferrers (chain) graphs,
and therefore

\operatorname{fp}(G)=\chi^{\prime}_{2K_{2}}(G).

Their standing conventions exclude empty template graphs and isolated vertices inside templates;
this does not affect the optimum value, since empty parts can be dropped and isolated vertices removed without changing the edge partition.
[
7
]

9.3.
Hardness evidence from neighboring problems.

We do not attempt a full complexity classification for
\operatorname{fp}
here; instead we record a few related NP-hardness
results indicating that chain-structure constraints can be algorithmically rigid.
(These do
not
by themselves imply NP-hardness of computing
\operatorname{fp}(G)
.)

•

Chain-structure completion is hard.

The
chain graph sandwich problem
(deciding whether one can choose a graph between mandatory/optional edges
that is a chain graph) is NP-complete.
[
9
]

•

The cover analogue becomes hard for
k\geq 3
.

In the
cover
version (edges may be covered more than once),
the
k
-
chain subgraph cover
problem is NP-complete for
k\geq 3
and polynomial-time solvable for
k\leq 2
;
see, e.g., Takaoka for a clear statement and references.
[
10
]

•

Induced matchings are hard already on bipartite graphs.

Computing a maximum induced matching is NP-complete for bipartite graphs.
[
13
]

Since induced matchings provide universal lower bounds on
\operatorname{fp}
, this suggests that obtaining tight lower-bound
certificates may be difficult in general instances.

10.
Open Problems

The bounds
\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(G)\leq\operatorname{fp}(G)\leq\min\{0pt(\mathcal{P}_{U}),0pt(\mathcal{P}_{V})\}
are simple, but the examples already show there is room between them.
Paths satisfy
\operatorname{fp}=\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}
, even cycles can have a gap of
1
, and by disjoint unions of cycles the gap
\operatorname{fp}-\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}
can grow arbitrarily.

Problem 23
.

Compute
\operatorname{fp}(G)
for further natural bipartite families (grids, convex bipartite graphs, for instance), and identify graph-theoretic conditions forcing
\operatorname{fp}(G)=\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(G)
.

Problem 24
.

How large can
\operatorname{fp}(G)
be as a function of
\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(G)
under constraints such as bounded maximum degree or given edge density?

Problem 25
.

Is it true that if
\operatorname{fp}(G)=\nu_{\operatorname{ind}}(G)
, then
G
admits an optimal Ferrers partition in which every part is a forest of diameter at most
3
(equivalently, a bistar or
P_{4}
)?

Problem 26
.

From algorithmic perspective, a concrete next step is to determine the complexity of
\operatorname{fp}(G)\leq 2
for bipartite
G
. More broadly, it would be interesting to study approximation and parameterized aspects of
\operatorname{fp}(G)
, and to identify additional graph classes where
\operatorname{fp}(G)
is computable in polynomial time.
