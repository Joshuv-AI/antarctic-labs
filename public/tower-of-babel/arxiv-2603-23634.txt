Testing Dark Energy with Black Hole Ringdown
Laurens Smulders, Johannes Noller, Sergi Sirera

Abstract
We show that dynamical dark energy theories can imprint 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) {\cal O}(1) modifications on the quasi-normal mode (QNM) spectrum characterising black hole ringdown. The time dependence of dynamical dark energy naturally gives rise to cosmological ‘hair’ around a black hole. Taking the cubic Galileon as a concrete example – which admits the only known stable solution of this kind – we parametrically connect the cosmological and black hole regimes, derive the induced QNM shifts and forecast the resulting dark energy constraints. We find that the dark energy field profile can be constrained with an accuracy of up to 10 − 2 10^{-2} for LVK and 10 − 4 10^{-4} for LISA.

. All combined, this made it seem questionable whether black hole ringdown would ever provide an insightful laboratory for testing dark energy. However, using the recently identified stable, time-dependent and dynamical dark energy driven black hole solution in
92
, we have here shown how to parametrically connect cosmological and strong field limits of this solution. This implies the dark energy field can imprint observable deviations onto the QNM spectrum, shifting frequencies by several tens of percent, and hence opening the door for using ringdown measurements as a novel tool for constraining dark energy. Going forward, more accurate modelling (e.g. better merger modelling to extract more accurate mass estimates, extending the QNM calculation to spinning black hole remnants, performing full parameter estimations) and larger SNR detections with upcoming surveys (such as LISA and ET/CE) are set to make this a precision tool for investigating the underlying nature of dark energy.

Appendix A
Equations of motion

In order to find numerical solutions, we consider the equations of motion in the quasi-stationary case. A small time dependence is induced by a non-zero radial component of the Noether current due to the shift-symmetry:

\displaystyle r^{2}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}J^{r}=\alpha_{BH}M_{\rm Pl}r_{s},

(15)

where
\alpha_{BH}
is a constant controlling the time dependence and the full covariant form of the shift-symmetry current is given by

\displaystyle J^{\mu}=2c_{2}\partial^{\mu}\varphi+2\frac{c_{3}}{\Lambda_{3}^{3}}\partial^{\mu}\varphi\Box\varphi-\frac{c_{3}}{\Lambda_{3}^{3}}\partial^{\mu}\left(\partial\varphi\right)^{2}.

(16)

To numerically solve the equations of motion, it is convenient to express them in dimensionless form. To this end we define the following dimensionless parameters:

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{c_{3}}{c_{2}}\frac{q}{\Lambda_{3}^{3}r_{s}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv-2c_{2}\frac{q^{2}r_{s}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{4}

\displaystyle\equiv\alpha_{BH}\frac{M_{\rm Pl}}{c_{2}qr_{s}}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{r}{r_{s}}.

(17)

In terms of these parameters, the quasi-stationary equations of motion are

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(x^{4}h)^{\prime}\frac{f}{h}\Xi^{2}+2x^{4}h\Xi-\alpha_{1}x^{4}h^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{4}x^{2}h\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}x^{2}\left[1-\frac{f}{h}\Xi^{2}\right]+2xfh^{\prime}+2h(f-1)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\left[1-\frac{f}{h}\Xi^{2}\right]\left[\alpha_{2}x^{2}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\left(x^{2}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\Xi\right)^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle-2xh^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0,

(18)

where primes indicate derivatives with respect to
x
. Note that the full equations of motion also include time-dependent terms, but this time dependence is suppressed
92
.

Finally, the dimensionless parameters
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
can be expressed in terms of the physical parameters
q/q_{0}
,
H
and
r_{s}
used in the main text as

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{3Hr_{s}}\frac{q}{q_{0}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}=6\left(\frac{q}{q_{0}}\right)^{2}\left(Hr_{s}\right)^{2},

(19)

while
\alpha_{4}
must satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{4}\sqrt{\beta}}{\alpha_{1}}=-1+\epsilon,\quad\text{where}\quad|\epsilon|\lower 3.22916pt\hbox{$\sim$}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\raise 1.1625pt\hbox{$<$}}(Hr_{s})^{2/3}

(20)

for stability. Note that due to the smallness of
Hr_{s}
this effectively fixes
\alpha_{4}=-\alpha_{1}/\sqrt{\beta}
, which we have used in the main text. For more details on the derivation of these equations of motion and the stability requirement see
92

Appendix B
Regge-Wheeler gauge

In the Regge-Wheeler gauge, the perturbations have the forms

\displaystyle r_{s}^{2}\delta g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left[h(t,r)dt^{2}H_{\ell m}^{(0)}(t,r)+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(t,r)}H_{\ell m}^{(2)}(t,r)\right]Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi),

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left[2dtdrH_{\ell m}^{(1)}(t,r)-2r_{s}dtd\theta\frac{1}{\sin\theta}h_{\ell m}^{(0)}(t,r)\partial_{\phi}+2r_{s}dtd\phi\sin\theta h_{\ell m}^{(0)}(t,r)\partial_{\theta}\right]Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi),

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left[r^{2}d\Omega^{2}K_{\ell m}(t,r)-2r_{s}drd\theta\frac{1}{\sin\theta}h_{\ell m}^{(1)}(t,r)\partial_{\phi}+2r_{s}drd\phi\sin\theta h_{\ell m}^{(1)}(t,r)\partial_{\theta}\right]Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi),

\displaystyle\delta\varphi

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\delta\varphi_{\ell m}(t,r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi).

(21)

Here,
h_{\ell m}^{(0)}
and
h_{\ell m}^{(1)}
are fields describing metric perturbations of odd parity, while
H_{\ell m}^{(0)}
,
H_{\ell m}^{(1)}
,
H_{\ell m}^{(2)}
and
K_{\ell m}
describe metric perturbations of even parity. The (even parity) scalar perturbations are (with a slight abuse of notation) described by the fields
\delta\varphi_{\ell m}
.
Y_{\ell m}
are the usual spherical harmonic functions.

The two spin-2 degrees of freedom are conveniently expressed in terms of the summary variables

\displaystyle w_{\ell m}

\displaystyle=r_{s}\dot{h}_{\ell m}^{(1)}-r_{s}h_{\ell m}^{\prime(0)}+\frac{2r_{s}}{r}h_{\ell m}^{(0)},

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell m}

\displaystyle=H_{\ell m}^{(2)}+\frac{(\ell+2)(\ell-1)r+3r_{s}}{2(r-r_{s})}K_{\ell m}-rK^{\prime}_{\ell m},

(22)

where dots and primes denote derivatives with respect to
t
and
r

w_{\ell m}
describes odd metric perturbations and
\psi_{\ell m}

Appendix C
Modified Regge-Wheeler and Zerilli equations

In this Appendix, we provide some more detail on the derivations of the modified Regge-Wheeler and Zerilli equations (
IV
). We will use the odd part of the quadratic action as an example, but the derivation for the even metric modes will be analogous, since the quadratic action has the same general form.

The odd part of the quadratic action (
IV
) has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{odd/even}}^{(2)}=\gamma_{1}(r)\dot{w}^{2}-\gamma_{2}(r)w^{\prime 2}-\gamma_{3}(r)w^{2},

(23)

where dots and primes indicate derivatives with respect to the coordinates
t
and
r
respectively. The equation of motion we find is then

\displaystyle\ddot{w}-\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}w^{\prime\prime}-\frac{\gamma_{2}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{1}}w^{\prime}+\frac{\gamma_{3}}{\gamma_{1}}w=0.

(24)

To get the equation in a Schrödinger-like form we define a tortoise coordinate
dr_{*}=dr/A(r)
, with
A(r)\propto\sqrt{\gamma_{2}/\gamma_{1}}
. This function is given in section
IV
. For both the even and odd modes we are able to choose the proportionality constant such that we obtain exactly the same tortoise function as in GR. We then have an equation of the form

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial_{t}^{2}}{\beta}-\partial_{*}^{2}\right)w-b_{1}\partial_{*}w-b_{2}w=0,

(25)

where
\partial_{*}=\partial/\partial r_{*}
. To remove the first order derivative term we define the Regge-Wheeler function
Q(t,r)=B(r)w(t,r)
. We find

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial_{t}^{2}}{\beta}-\partial_{*}^{2}\right)Q

\displaystyle=B\left(\frac{\partial_{t}^{2}}{\beta}-\partial_{*}^{2}\right)w-2\partial_{*}B\partial_{*}w-\partial_{*}^{2}Bw

\displaystyle=\left(Bb_{1}-2\partial_{*}B\right)\partial_{*}w+\left(Bb_{2}-\partial_{*}^{2}B\right)w.

(26)

We then require

\displaystyle Bb_{1}-2\partial_{*}B=0,

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle B(r)\propto\exp\int dr_{*}\frac{b_{1}(r)}{2}\propto\exp\int dx\frac{b_{1}(r)}{2A(r)},

(27)

which gives
B(r)\propto r
in the odd case. We then identify

\displaystyle B(r)V_{\ell}^{RW}(r)=\partial_{*}^{2}B-Bb_{2}=B\left(\frac{Ab_{1}^{\prime}}{2}+\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{4}-b_{2}\right),

(28)

which gives

\displaystyle V_{\ell}^{RW}(r)=\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)\left[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}-\frac{3r_{s}}{r^{3}}\right]

(29)

for the Regge-Wheeler potential. Similarly, we find for the Zerilli potential

\displaystyle V_{\ell}^{Z}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{r_{s}}{r}\right)\frac{8\lambda^{2}(\lambda+1)r^{3}+12\lambda^{2}r_{s}r^{2}+18\lambda r_{s}^{2}r+9r_{s}^{3}}{r^{3}(2\lambda r+3r_{s})^{2}},

(30)

where
\lambda=\left(l+2\right)\left(l-1\right)/2
. Note that these potentials are identical to those in GR.

Appendix D
Fisher information analysis

In order to derive forecasted constraints on
\beta
we have employed a simplified Fisher information analysis, where only the combination of parameters
M^{2}/\beta
is to be determined from the ringdown signal, while all other waveform parameters
(A,\phi^{+},...)
are assumed to be known. Details and explicit checks of these assumptions can be found in
26
.

Error estimates can be derived from the Fisher information matrix, defined as the noise-weighted inner product

\Gamma_{ab}=\left(\frac{\delta h}{\delta\theta^{a}}\Big|\frac{\delta h}{\delta\theta^{b}}\right),

(31)

with
h
being the characteristic ringdown waveform and
\theta^{a}
corresponding to the set of parameters to be determined.
Parameter errors can then be obtained by inverting the matrix (giving the covariance matrix
\Sigma
) and taking the square-root of the diagonal elements as

\sigma_{a}=\sqrt{\Sigma_{aa}}=\sqrt{\Gamma_{aa}^{-1}}.

(32)

In the main text, we have shown how
M
and
\beta
are completely degenerate parameters in the context of ringdown. Hence, with
M^{2}/\beta
being the only independent parameter combination in the ringdown, its error from the
1
-dimensional Fisher matrix corresponding to the
\ell=2
mode is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{M^{2}/\beta}\rho=\frac{f}{\sqrt{2}Qf^{\prime}}\approx 0.67\frac{M^{2}}{\beta}.

(33)

Assuming an independent mass measurement, e.g. as determined from the inspiral-merger, we have isolated the error on
\beta
by introducing a mass prior as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sigma_{\beta}}{\beta}\right)^{2}=\underbrace{\left(\frac{\sigma_{M^{2}/\beta}}{M^{2}/\beta}\right)^{2}}_{\text{Ringdown}}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{2\sigma_{M}}{M}\right)^{2}}_{\text{Prior}}.

(34)

This expression is illustrated in the main text Figure
3
, showing the achievable constraints on
\beta
for some given SNR and mass prior. Additionally, we illustrate in Figure
4
the degeneracy between
M
and
\beta
, and how it can be broken by a combination of sufficiently high SNR and precise mass prior, providing tight
\beta
constraints.

Below, we present an alternative derivation of the same expression. Treating
M
and
\beta
as separate parameters, the total Fisher matrix is constructed as the sum of the ringdown information and the independent mass prior as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{T}=\Gamma_{R}+\Gamma_{P},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{R}=\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{\beta\beta}&\Gamma_{\beta M}\\
\Gamma_{\beta M}&\Gamma_{MM}\end{pmatrix},\quad\Gamma_{P}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1/\sigma_{M}^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(35)

Inverting the total matrix
\Gamma_{T}
yields the error for
\beta
11
11

11

Note that in order to invert
\Gamma_{T}
we have used the fact that
\det(\Gamma_{R})=0
, due to the complete degeneracy between
\beta
and
M
.

\displaystyle\sigma_{\beta}^{2}=(\Gamma_{T}^{-1})_{\beta\beta}=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\beta\beta}}+\frac{\Gamma_{MM}\sigma_{M}^{2}}{\Gamma_{\beta\beta}},

(36)

The first term on the RHS represents the idealised
1
-dimensional error
\sigma_{\beta,1D}^{2}
, in the case where
M
is known with absolute precision (
\sigma_{M}=0
). This is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\beta,1D}\rho=\left|\frac{f}{\sqrt{2}Qf^{\prime}}\right|\approx 0.67\beta,

(37)

For the second term on the RHS of Eq. (

(38)
