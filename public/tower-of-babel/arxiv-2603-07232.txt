Introduction

We consider a connected simple graph

n

denotes the edge set. The adjacency matrix of graph
G

A(G)=a_{ij}
, is a square matrix with
a_{ij}=1
if the vertex
v_{i}
is adjacent to
v_{j}
, and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise. A graph is regular if each vertex has the same degree. If
M
represents the matrix associated with a graph
G

is termed
M

are integers. The
M

be the length of the shortest path between vertices
v_{i}

The

The complement of
G
, denoted as

, is the graph sharing the same vertex set as
G
, where two vertices are adjacent if and only if they are not adjacent in
G

and
G_{2}

G_{2}

by adding all possible edges from the vertices of
G_{1}
to the vertices of
G_{2}
. Conventionally,

denotes the complete graph,

represents the cycle and

is the graph obtained by the join
\overline{K_{m}}\nabla C_{n}

[
11

, therefore, the possible values of
a
and

corresponding to

are
a=1,~m=4
or
a=4,~m=1
or
a=2,~m2
or
a=1,~m=12
or
a=2,~m=6
or
a=3,~m=4
or
a=4,~m=3
or
a=6,~m=2
or
a=12,~m=1
. Thus, for

, the possible extended distance integral graphs
EGW(a,m,n)
are

EGW(1,4,6),EGW(2,2,6),EGW(4,1,6),EGW(1,12,6),EGW(2,6,6),\\
EGW(3,4,6)
,
EGW(4,3,6)
,
EGW(6,2,6)
,

.
∎

The following observation gives the distance spectrum of the graph
K_{p,p}\nabla C_{n}
.

Theorem 2.8
.

The distance spectrum of
K_{p,p}\nabla C_{n}
is

\displaystyle\Bigg\{-2^{2p-2},-2-2cos\left(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\right);k=1,2,\dots,n-1,

\displaystyle\frac{2n+3p-6\pm\sqrt{(2n+3p-6)^{2}-16pn+48p+16n-32}}{2}\Bigg\}

Fact 1.
An ordered triplet of positive integers
(a,b,c)
is called Pythagorean triplet if
a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}
. We note that
(3,4,5)
is the only Pythagorean triplet whose one component is
4
.

The following result gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the graph
K_{p,p}\nabla C_{n}
to be
D
-integral.

Theorem 2.9
.

The graph
K_{p,p}\nabla C_{n}
is
D
-integral if and only if (i)
n=3,\hskip 2.84526ptp=1
and (ii)
n=6,\hskip 2.84526ptp=4
.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.8
, the distance spectrum of the graph
K_{p,p}\nabla C_{n}
includes eigenvalues of
-2
of multiplicity
2p-2
, as well as
-2-2cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n})
, where
k
ranges from
1
to
n-1
. Additionally, the remaining two eigenvalues are

\displaystyle\frac{2n+3p-6\pm\sqrt{(2n+3p-6)^{2}-16pn+48p+16n-32}}{2}.

It is evident that
2n+3p-6\pm\sqrt{(2n+3p-6)^{2}-16pn+48p+16n-32}
will yield integer values if and only if
(2n+3p-6)^{2}-16pn+48p+16n-32
is a perfect square. Furthermore, it is clear that
2\cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n})
results in the integer values for any
1\leq k\leq n-1
if and only if
n
belongs to the set
\{3,4,6\}
.
Consequently, the graph
K_{p,p}\nabla C_{n}
is
D
-integral if and only if
(2n+3p-6)^{2}-16pn+48p+16n-32
is a perfect square and
n
belongs to the set
\{3,4,6\}
. Let
t=(2n+3p-6)^{2}-16pn+48p+16n-32=c^{2}
. Now, we discuss the following three cases of
n
.

Case 1.
Let
n=3
. Then, we have
t=(3p)^{2}+4^{2}=c^{2}
. Since each term on both sides is a perfect square and the left-hand side is the sum of two perfect square numbers with one number
4^{2}
, so according to Fact 1,
3p
should be equal to
3
, implying that
p
equals to
1
. Therefore, the only possible distance integral graph in this case is
K_{1,1}\nabla C_{3}
.

Case 2.
Let
n=4
. In this case, we have
t=(3p+2)^{2}-16p+32=c^{2}
, that is,
9p^{2}-4p+36=c^{2}
. This gives
p=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{4c^{2}-1040}}{18}
. We first check the values of
c
, for which
4c^{2}-1040
is a perfect square number. Assume that
4c^{2}-1040=q^{2}
. Then
(2c-q)(2c+q)=1040
. The different possibilities to write
1040
as a product of two factors are

\displaystyle 1040=1\times 1040=2\times 520=4\times 260=8\times 130=10\times 104

\displaystyle=20\times 52=40\times 26=16\times 65=80\times 13=5\times 208.

We eliminate the possibilities
1040=1\times 1040=2\times 520=8\times 130=40\times 26=16\times 65=80\times 13=10\times 104=5\times 208
, because none of these pairs has the sum a multiple of
4
. So we only consider two cases, namely
(2c-q)(2c+q)=1040=4\times 260
and
(2c-q)(2c+q)=1040=20\times 52
. For the case
(2c-q)(2c+q)=4\times 260
, we get
4c=264
, implying
c=66
and for the case
(2c-q)(2c+q)=20\times 52
, we have
c=18
. By substituting these values of
c
in
p=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{4c^{2}-1040}}{18}
, we get
p=\frac{20}{18}
and
p=\frac{132}{18}
, which is absurd. Thus, for
n=4
, there is no value of
p
for which
K_{p,p}\nabla C_{n}
is distance integral.

Case 3.
Let
n=6
. Here, we have
t=(3p+6)^{2}-48p+64=c^{2}
, that is,
9p^{2}-12p+100=c^{2}
. This gives
p=\frac{12\pm\sqrt{4c^{2}-3456}}{18}
. Continuing with the same approach as in Case 2, we find the only integer value of
p
is
4
. Therefore, the only possible
D
-integral graph in this case is
K_{4,4}\nabla C_{6}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we show that there is no dumbbell graph that is D-integral.

Lemma 2.10
.

[
10
]

The distance spectrum of dumbbell graph
\boldsymbol{DB}(W_{m,n})
consists of the set of eigenvalues

\displaystyle\Bigg\{-4^{m-1},0^{m-1},\frac{1}{2}[-(m+n+4)\pm\sqrt{(m+n+4)^{2}-16m}],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[(5m+5n-8)\pm\sqrt{25m^{2}+25n^{2}-14mn}],-2-2cos\Bigg(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\Bigg);k=2,\dots,n\Bigg\},

where each of the eigenvalues
-2-2cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n});k=2,3,\dots,n
is of multiplicity
2
.

Theorem 2.11
.

There are no
D
-integral dumbbell graphs.

Proof.
By Lemma
2.10
, the
D
-spectrum of the dumbbell graph
\boldsymbol{DB}(W_{m,n})
is given by

\displaystyle\Bigg\{-4^{m-1},0^{m-1},\frac{1}{2}[-(m+n+4)\pm\sqrt{(m+n+4)^{2}-16m}],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[(5m+5n-8)\pm\sqrt{25m^{2}+25n^{2}-14mn}],-2-2cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n});k=2,\dots,n\Bigg\},

where each of the eigenvalues
-2-2cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n});k=2,3,\dots,n
is of multiplicity
2
.

Since
-(m+n+4)
and
(m+n+4)^{2}-16m
are of the same parity and also
(5m+5n-8)
and
25m^{2}+25n^{2}-14mn
are of the same parity, therefore it suffices to find the values of
m
and
n
for which both
\frac{1}{2}[-(m+n+4)\pm\sqrt{(m+n+4)^{2}-16m}
and
\frac{1}{2}[(5m+5n-8)\pm\sqrt{25m^{2}+25n^{2}-14mn}]
are perfect square numbers. As
cos\frac{2k\pi}{n}
is an integer only if
n\in\{3,4,6\}
, therefore,
DB(W_{m.n})
is
D
-integral if and only if
\sqrt{(m+n+4)^{2}-16m}
and
\sqrt{25m^{2}+25n^{2}-14mn}
are integers, where
n\in\{3,4,6\}
. Assume that
t=(m+n+4)^{2}-16m=c^{2}
. We consider the following three cases.

Case 1
.
Let
n=3
. We have
t=(m+7)^{2}-16m=c^{2}
, that is,
48=c^{2}-(m-1)^{2}
which is equivalent to
48=(c-(m-1))(c+(m+1))
. As
48=1\times 48=2\times 24=3\times 16=6\times 8=4\times 12
, the sum of factors of
48
should be the multiple of
2
. Therefore, we consider only
(c-(m-1))(c+(m-1))=4\times 12
,
(c-(m-1))(c+(m-1))=2\times 24
and
(c-(m-1))(c+(m-1))=6\times 8
. This gives
c=8
,
c=13
and
c=7
. Substituting back the values of
c
, we get
m=2
,
m=5
and
m=12
as the only integer values of

. Now,
\sqrt{25m^{2}+25n^{2}-14mn}
is not a perfect square number when

and
n=3
. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{DB}(W_{m,n})
is not
D
-integral in this case.

Case 2
.

Let
n=4
. Then
t=(m+8)^{2}-16m=c^{2}
, that is,

. So the possible values of

and

\boldsymbol{DB}(W_{m,n})
is not
D
-integral in this case.

Case 3
.

For

. This gives,

. Proceeding similarly as in above cases, we observe that there exists no positive integral value of
