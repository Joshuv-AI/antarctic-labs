Predicting token prices in the blockchain market using neural networks
Authors: Ying Lu, Ziyu Cheng, Zhihao Zhang, Almetwally M Mostafa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCryptocurrency price prediction is challenging due to high volatility, non-stationarity, and complex interactions among diverse data sources. Existing models often fail to effectively integrate on-chain metrics, sentiment data, and technical indicators, while lacking adaptive attention mechanisms and uncertainty estimation. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a Multi-Mode Adaptive Attention Network (M2A2-Net), which combines Temporal Fusion Transformers, dynamic cross-attention, and probabilistic GRUs to model multimodal temporal dependencies. The framework incorporates 47 heterogeneous features, including blockchain metrics (e.g., hash rate, active addresses), sentiment embeddings, macroeconomic indicators, and technical patterns. A regime-adaptive attention mechanism dynamically adjusts feature importance based on market conditions, while Monte Carlo dropout provides uncertainty estimation. The model is evaluated on six major cryptocurrencies across multiple market phases spanning from early 2022 to the end of 2025. Results demonstrate competitive performance, achieving a mean absolute percentage error of 1.87% for Bitcoin and 2.34% for Ethereum, showing improvements over baseline models such as Transformers, LSTM-XGBoost, and BiGRU. Additionally, the probabilistic framework ensures reliable prediction intervals with 94.3% coverage. SHAP analysis highlights that sentiment and on-chain features dominate during volatile periods, while technical indicators are more influential in stable markets, confirming the model’s effectiveness and interpretability.Keywords: Cryptocurrency, Price prediction, Neural networks, Deep learning, Sentiment analysisSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The cryptocurrency market has rapidly grown over the past decade, becoming a complex financial ecosystem with a global market value exceeding several trillion dollars. This market engages millions of investors worldwide and is characterized by a lack of centralization, high price volatility, and sensitivity to various triggers such as blockchain developments and social sentiments1. High levels of precision in price prediction of cryptocurrencies have far reaching consequences on the optimization of portfolios, management of risk, algorithmic trading and even financial stability in general. However, given the hyper volatile nature of these markets such as days with sharp price fluctuations of more than 20% within hours of time, complex analytical structures are required which are able to incorporate different information flows and adjust to new market environments that change rapidly2. Growing market pressure has driven advanced Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) development, yet limitations remain. Early cryptocurrency forecasting relied on classical time-series models such as autoregressive-integrated moving-average (ARIMA) and autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity (GARCH) to capture historical price dynamics3.

These methods were helpful in fairly stable market dynamics, but from the very nature of the cryptocurrency market, their assumptions of linearity and stationarity made them insufficient to deal with the chaotic and nonlinear dynamics of such markets4,5. In addition, they could not predict the future as well because of their low capability to capture exogenous variables on an annual basis other than historical prices, in time of high volatilities or regime changing6. Although ensemble models improved prediction accuracy, their high computational complexity and limited interpretability remain significant challenges in cryptocurrency forecasting7.

The rapid expansion of digital finance is also evident in the emergence of central bank digital currencies, which alongside decentralized assets, are reshaping consumption patterns and investment behaviors8. Accurate forecasting in these complex environments is critical for advanced portfolio rebalancing strategies and systemic risk management9. The underlying blockchain infrastructure itself faces continuous challenges in scalability and security, necessitating advanced deterministic routing protocols for payment channel networks10 and robust consensus mechanisms like multi-leader Byzantine fault tolerance11.

The introduction of the DL models became the paradigmatic change in the cryptocurrency prices-prediction studies. The recurrent neural networks, specifically the long short-term memory (LSTM) networks and the gated recurrent units (GRU) were discovered as efficient instruments of predicting temporal patterns in sequential data of cryptocurrency prices12. Those architectures proved to be better with respect to the ability to capture time-scale patterns over long distances and to adapt to the non-stationary nature of digital-asset markets. Several researchers confirmed the usefulness of LSTM-based models in different cryptocurrencies in which significant progress is noted with respect to the accuracy of predictions in comparison with the conventional techniques13,14. Convolutional neural networks were another feature-extraction method examined by researchers to identify local patterns as well as hierarchy features in time-series data, which is known to be suitable in tagging numerous features15,16. Although such improvements were made, traditional DL models were severely restricted in that they took the task of input data through single-mode information as a norm, which repelled the integration of the highly diverse ecosystem of blockchain-specific metrics and live sentiment indicators which have a substantial impact on cryptocurrency valuations17,18.

Recent studies have paid more and more attention to the possibilities of attention mechanisms and transformer architectures in alleviating a part of the drawbacks of the previous methods of DL. Originally designed to work with natural language processing, transformer-based models have been used to make predictions of the prices of cryptocurrencies and they show significant ability to form long-term dependencies by building self-attention networks19,20. These models were strong at taking long sequence of time in processing and learn nonlinear relationships. Some articles examined how sentiment analysis provided by social-media sources can be integrated with price -prediction markets, and agreed that the impact of social-media sentiment on the behavior of cryptocurrency markets can be significant21,22. Beyond standard transformers, advanced topological and graph-based deep learning architectures, such as Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), have recently demonstrated exceptional capabilities in capturing spatial dependencies in blockchain anomaly detection23 and digital financial inclusion trends24.

Text-mining methods on Twitter-data and news about the financial domain have shown that sentiment characteristics boost performance of prediction, particularly in times of increased social-media exertion25,26. Another emerging trend in research involved using blockchain-based metrics in an on-chain format transaction volumes, addresses in active status, and network hash rates that are particularly attractive to the cognizance that blockchain metrics give basic insights into the health and adoption in cryptocurrency network27,28. Still, even with these progressive advancements, a comprehensive model that synergistically integrates multivariate data sources in addition to the application of adaptive mechanisms to modulate the level of feature importance in varying market regimes is still conspicuously missing in the literature29,30.

Existing cryptocurrency prediction studies suffer from fragmented feature usage, lacking a unified cross-modal architecture integrating on-chain, sentiment, macroeconomic, and technical indicators31. Existing crypto forecasting attention mechanisms use static feature weights, failing to adapt to shifting predictive importance across volatile, stable, bull, and bear market regimes32. Third, most of the published deep-learning cryptocurrency prediction models derive point estimates with no uncertainty on prediction, a key constraining factor to risk-sensitive trading choices and portfolio management in highly volatile markets33,34. These models are extremely limited in their practical use in financial situations in the real world where reliability of prediction is as important as the predictions themselves because of the lack of probabilistic frameworks that provide the calibrated confidence intervals.

The present-day paper proposes M2A2-Net, an efficient neural network model for predicting cryptocurrency prices. This approach solves various holes in the current material on digital currencies predictions. In this research, the neural network model was created in a multiple-modal fusion setting using 47 different investment attributes. Furthermore, the model generates probability intervals through stochastic gated repetitive stages and Monte Carlo’s drops.

There are three major contributions to the work. First, a novel temporal fusion transformer along with dynamic cross-attention layers and probabilistic gated recurrent units is introduced to improve the identification of heterogeneous time-series patterns, thereby capturing short-term and long-term temporal trends in an efficient manner. The second contribution is related to empirical validation of the proposed architecture, which demonstrates superior predictive performance over other state-of-the-art solutions such as separable transformers and LSTM/XGBoost ensembles by 29%. The average prediction coverage of 94.3% of the 90-percent confidence intervals suggests excellent calibration quality in real-world applications. Finally, the analysis of important features based on Shapley additive explanations shows that in volatile regimes, the most crucial factors include the on-chain network and sentiment trends, while technical features prevail during stable regimes.

The paper is structured as follows: Sect. "Theoretical framework and architecture of the multi-modal adaptive attention network" presents the proposed model architecture. Section “Methodology” details data, feature engineering, and methodology. Section "Empirical results and performance analysis" reports experimental results and comparisons. Section "Discussion and implications" discusses implications, limitations, and future work. Section “Conclusion” concludes with key contributions to cryptocurrency forecasting.

Training from theoretical foundations of the recent developments in attention-temporal modelling15,16, M2A2-Net also entails Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) multi-scale dependencies, dynamic multi-modality cross-attention, regime-adaptive attention, and Monte Carlo dropout-augmented probabilistic Gated Recurrent Unit to obtain calibrated uncertainty estimation.

M2A2-Net has an overall architecture that employs a hierarchical information processing pipeline that converts forty-seven raw heterogeneous features to probabilistic price predictions with reverse engineered confidence intervals. This entire pipeline is shown in Fig. 1, which starts with parallel feature-embedding models that transform the various input modalities into coherent (vector) features that could be then further processed by attention-based models. The architecture integrates specialized encoding for various feature types: continuous numerical features like historical prices and trading volumes are linearly projected into dense embeddings, while textual sentiment from social media and financial news is transformed through pre-trained language models. Ultimately, probabilistic GRU apply Monte Carlo dropout to yield final price predictions and intervals, providing investors with quantified confidence measures for risk management. The theoretical foundation of using stochastic simulations for financial risk and uncertainty quantification is well-established in recent computational finance literature35.

Avoidance of Look-Ahead Bias in Regime Labeling

The volatility thresholds used to generate regime labels (low, medium, and high volatility) were only calculated using the training dataset to maintain scientific rigor and avoid any kind of look-ahead bias. In particular, only the training period was used to determine the tertile limits of the empirical volatility distribution, which were then set. The validation and test datasets were then uniformly subjected to these precalculated thresholds for regime assignment. This method ensures that the training process is unaffected by future data from the validation or test periods, protecting the forecasting framework’s integrity and practicality.

The M2A2-Net training employs a composite loss function that optimizes prediction accuracy and uncertainty calibration. It processes sixty historic time steps for multi-horizon price predictions spanning one, three, and seven days. The architecture includes approximately 8.7 million trainable parameters across various components and requires about six hours of training on an NVIDIA V100 GPU using mixed-precision arithmetic.

To balance interpretability, computational efficiency, and adaptive learning performance, the volatility states were divided into three distinct regimes: low, medium, and high, based on the tertiles of the empirical volatility distribution. Because it avoids the over-fragmentation that could result from excessive regime partitioning, a three-state structure was chosen to adequately depict the significant shifts in the dynamics of the bitcoin market. Through allowing the model to learn specific feature-weighting patterns for distinct market conditions, discrete regime categorization, as opposed to continuous weighting techniques, improves training stability and increases interpretability of attention adaption. According to preliminary experiments, adding more volatility states increased model complexity but produced just a slight boost in performance.

Data collection

A chronological split of 70/15/15 in training/validation/testing datasets was employed to keep the temporal correlation and avoid the potential look-ahead bias in the financial series prediction. In detail, the first 70% of the observations (or roughly 1,023 trading days from January 2022 to October 2024) were selected for training purposes, while the following 15% (approximately 219 trading days from November 2024 to May 2025) were set aside for validation and hyperparameter tuning tasks. Meanwhile, the last 15% of the observations (or about 219 trading days from June 2025 to December 2025) served as the test dataset.

Methodology

The Multi-sensory Adaptive Attention Network needs to be empirically validated based on a strict experimental design involving a systemic feature engineering process, thorough implementation of the baseline model and strong evaluation procedures. This methodology section outlines all processes in an adequate detail to make them completely reproducible without violating the consistency with the existing specifications that were outlined by the architectures. The experimental design focuses on ecological validity by using realistic train test splits that maintain time order, a complete comparison of baseline against state-of-the-art algorithms and multidimensional evaluation measures to address quality prediction and quality of uncertainty calibration.

The range of six major cryptocurrencies, which were sample groups based on the diversity of market-capitalization and heterogeneity of blockchain-architectures, including Bitcoin (proof-of-work pioneer), Ethereum (smart-contract platform), Ripple (enterprise-oriented payment protocol), Litecoin (faster-Bitcoin derivative), Cardano (proof-of-stake research-driven platform), and Solana (high-throughput scalability-oriented chain) were used in data collection. Timely sentiment the sentiment being made on fine-tuned RoBERTa transformer models adapted to the financial inputs generated sentiment scores of compounds (from − 1 extremely bearish to + 1 extremely bullish) and a discrete polarity scale and aggregation of sentiment scores over daily, weekly, and monthly time periods to estimate sentiment momentum and volatility. These raw data streams were also processed to form the forty-seven heterogeneous features that were model inputs, which are grouped into four conceptual groups that aim at grasping the different aspects of dynamic cryptocurrencies markets.

The technical-indicator category contains fifteen indicators, among which are simple moving averages of seven, thirty, ninety and two-hundred-day; exponential moving averages with twelve and twenty-six-day decay parameters; relative strength index that is calculated over fourteen days to measure the momentum; moving-average convergence divergence that measures the trend strength by comparing the difference between exponential moving averages; Bollinger Bands (boundary, lower limits, widths) which are used to measure volatility envelopes in terms of price fluctuations; rate of change indicators of one day and seven On-chain-metrics category takes eleven features: hash rate, the seven-day growth rate of the same, active addresses and their variance to the ninety-day moving average, the number of transactions normalized to the blockchain capacity, the average transaction fee divided by the historical median, the SOPR showing the intensity of profit-taking, the NVT ratio that gauges the value of the network in relation to transaction activities, miner revenue that is broken down into block rewards and fee and network difficulty, along with its adjustment velocity.

The sentiment category has thirteen items such as compound sentiment scores aggregated along daily, weekly and monthly windows, sentiment volatility, or rolling standard deviation over seven days, sentiment momentum, which is a measure of how differentiating between the seven days average and the thirty days average, the bullish bearish ratio as the ratio of directional sentiment balance, social-volume measures that give the intensity of discussion normalized by historical baselines, news sentiment that is independently aggregated over professional financial journals, and cross correlation features that measure how Twitter and news sentiment are aligned. The category of macroeconomics has eight indicators based on them such as S&P 500 daily returns, percent change in price in gold, change in percentage price in the U.S. Dollar Index, ten-year Treasury yields, and changes, VIX index, correlation coefficients between equity and cryptocurrency returns over rolling thirty-day periods, and regime indicators that describe overall market risk-on and risk-off regimes. The experimental design applies a strict temporal train-validation-test split in chronological sequence in order to preclude look-ahead bias and provide viable performance on truly unseen future data as recommended by the best practices of financial time-series forecasting.

In the case of each cryptocurrency, the data sample is separated one after another with the initial seventy per cent portion (around 1,023 days between January 2022 and October 2024) of the observations being divided into the training set, the next fifteen per cent of observations (219 days between November 2024 and May 2025) as validation and hyper-parameter optimization, and the final fifteen per cent of observations (219 days between June 2025 and December 2025) held as an independent test sample to This division over time makes certain that the models are only trained based on past data and tested on future periods thus it is similar to actual life deployment of forecasting systems where the forecasting system is required to generalize to the market conditions that are not there during the training period. To avoid look-ahead bias, random sampling was not used, as financial time series data exhibits temporal dependence. Systematic grid search was used to optimize hyper-parameters, such as the number of encoder layers in the transformer (range: 4–8), the number of attention heads (4, 8, 16), the size of GRU hidden-dimensions (128, 256, 512), dropout rates (0.1, 0.2, 0.3), and learning rates (hyper-parameters were chosen by minimizing validation-set mean absolute percentage error. It was found that the best configuration used six layers of transformers, eight attention heads, 256-dimensional GRU hidden states, a 0.2 dropout rate, and a 1 × 10⁻⁴ learning rate. Training was also conducted on NVIDIA V100 GPUs with 32GB memory using the PyTorch 2.0 framework, with computational efficiency using mixed-precision training using automatic mixed-precision (AMP) and the tradeoff between gradient-estimates and memory by mini-batching 32 sequences each.

Baseline models were attentively chosen to reflect the up-to-date state-of-the-art methods in cryptocurrency-price prediction which will allow making a thorough comparative analysis of the benefits of M2A2-Net performance. The baseline ensemble incorporates standalone TFT, where regime-adaptive attention and probabilistic components are absent, therefore, simply being an ablation control which isolates the contribution of the proposed architectural innovations; Long Short-Term Memory network paired with XGBoost in two-stage hybrid architecture, the LSTM offers temporal features which are then inputted into the addressing phase (where gradient-boosting takes place), which represents recent successful methods reported across cryptocurrency-forecasting literature; bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit, sequences are processed in both forward and To make the baseline models fair in their comparison, the same train-validation-test splits, feature sets, and evaluation protocols were used to train all the baseline models to avoid any potential advantage of differential tuning. Baseline models were optimized using grid-search processes identical to the ones used to tune M2A2-Net to preclude the possibility of preferential tuning.

Measurements of evaluation were to be picked in order to give a complete measure of accuracy in predictions, quality of uncertainty-calibration, practical trading utility of the various forecasting horizons. The main measure of accuracy is the mean absolute percentage error, which gives realized in comparison with analytically relative to the real prices, and which makes it possible to compare the errors of prediction between the various cryptocurrencies that have significantly different scales of absolute prices. Root mean squared error and mean absolute error complement MAPE by providing a different way to look at the quality of the prediction where punishes prediction error on a quadratic scale andis resistant to outliers. In probabilistic evaluation of forecasting, a better measure to use is the probability of prediction-interval coverage, meaning what fraction of observed prices are within predicted interval bounds, and this is calculated as Coverage = (1/N)Σ[yi ∈ [Li, Ui]], where Li and Ui denote lower and upper interval bounds and is the indicator function; well-calibrated models achieve coverage rates approximating the nominal confidence level (95%). Uncertainty-estimate-Precision Prediction-interval is used to quantify and narrower intervals are used to produce more confident predictions at appropriate coverage levels. Pinball loss assesses the quality of probabilistic-forecasts in the quantiles by using asymmetric penalty functions. The Diebold-Mariano test is used to test statistically significant differences between the predictive accuracy of M2A2-Net and each of the base test models to construct confidence intervals to show whether the performance improvement is greater than the sampling error variance at the five-percent significance level. Importance feature analysis relies on the Shapley Additive Explanation, role computation of TreeSHAP algorithm, which computes the average influence of a particular feature on the predicted outputs of the model, on average, for the whole test set, and shows how the importance patterns differ between volatility states, where explainability helps overcome model trust and potential regulatory compliance issues in finance applications.

All DL models underwent training and evaluation processes conducted across five different independent experiments initialized from distinct random seeds. Metrics for model performances were calculated as an average of these repeated experiments, and standard deviation was tracked for each model training experiment, demonstrating the stability of the training process. As expected, our proposed M2A2-Net exhibited stable performances in all conducted experiments.

Volatility regimes were defined using tertiles of the empirical volatility distribution, resulting in three distinct states. This choice balances interpretability, computational efficiency, and the learning capacity of adaptive attention mechanisms. A three-regime structure effectively captures major shifts in Bitcoin market dynamics while avoiding over-segmentation caused by excessive regime partitions. Compared to continuous weight assignment methods, regime-based classification improves training stability and convergence, and enhances interpretability by enabling the model to learn distinct feature-weight patterns tailored to different market conditions.

To maintain computational feasibility, a restricted grid search method was employed for tuning the hyperparameters, instead of an exhaustive one. Even though the established search space (transformer layers: 4–8, attention heads: 4/8/16, GRU hidden size: 128/256/512, dropout: 0.1/0.2/0.3, learning rate: contains 405 different combinations, evaluation of all the combinations is infeasible considering the average time taken of around six hours for training each configuration. Hence, a total of 70 different configurations has been evaluated by intelligently sampling from the search space to sample from the lower, mid, and higher parameter ranges.

Efficiency was improved using early stopping using 15-epochs patience with validation MAPE-based hyperparameter tuning. The experiments were executed using mixed precision training on a single NVIDIA V100 GPU, where each experiment was run sequentially owing to computational constraints.

The cumulative advantage of the addition of architectural innovations is evidenced by progressive improvement of MAPE to 2.84 per cent (a reduction of 11.5 per cent), to 2.43 per cent (a further reduction of 14.4 per cent), and to 1.87 per cent (a further reduction of 23.0 per cent) as the most beneficial innovations are progressively incorporated in the model. The regime-adaptive attention mechanism plays the most important role at high-volatility times, and makes the MAPE reduction by 32.1% in turbulent market conditions versus a reduction of 15.3% in stable market conditions, thus supporting the hypothesis that the weights given to dynamic features are more valuable when the market is volatile. On the other hand, the probabilistic uncertainty quantification sub-component shows uniformity in value at all regimes, which lowers MAPE by an estimated 18per cent at all states of volatility and also enhances the calibration quality. The asymmetric benefit of multi-modal feature integration among various cryptocurrencies suggests that the asset gaining more accuracy on-chain data ecosystems (Bitcoin, Ethereum) can gain it more than the one with less comprehensive blockchain metrics (Solana, Cardano, which are relatively new to the market).

The Fig. 7 below represents visualized SHAP values under a three-pane visualization approach of the different regimes.

For the period of moderate-volatility transitional period, the analysis shows a relatively similar importance of both technical indicators (mean SHAP = 0.228) and sentiment features (mean SHAP = 0.197), although, on-chain metrics tend to prevail (mean SHAP = 0.215). Uncertainty quantification and prediction interval calibration

The M2A2-Net model facilitates effective uncertainty quantification using probabilistic forecasting, and its calibrated prediction intervals are presented in Fig. 8 using several visualizations.

Prediction interval calibration analysis.

The calibration curves show comparisons of theoretical probabilities and actual coverages of prediction intervals at various confidence intervals ranging from 50% to 99%. Data points lying on the diagonal line represent the best calibration results. In our case, the M2A2-Net model is close to the ideal calibration line at all confidence intervals, reaching a 95% empirical coverage rate of 94.3%, with the maximum calibration error of 2.1% occurring at the 99% confidence interval. Furthermore, box plots representing prediction interval widths under varying degrees of market volatility are shown in Fig. 8. Due to the Monte-Carlo dropout process used by M2A2-Net, intervals are narrower (median width: $847) during stable market times and broader ($3,421) in times of market turmoil, indicating uncertainty. Finally, sharpness calibration graphs measure the trade-off between the accuracy of prediction intervals and their reliability; thus, the M2A2-Net produces intervals that are 18.3% smaller than those of the baseline TFT method.

Multi-horizon forecasting performance

The evaluation of the predictive power of M2A2-Net with regard to different forecast periods (one up to seven days ahead) gives insight into the decline of the model’s effectiveness over time and demonstrates how it can be applied to several trading tactics. Figure 9 offers the results of the multi one horizon forecasting in the form of a sequence of line charts illustrating the change in mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) with increasing forecasting horizon, starting at t + 1 days up to t + 7 days of all the six cryptocurrencies being studied.

Multi-Horizon MAPE Performance.

The multi-horizon forecasting assessment provides information on the decrease of skills for prediction with a horizon from one to seven days. In the case of Bitcoin, the proposed M2A2-Net is quite stable and the MAPE is slowly improving over time, rising by 1.87% at 1 day, 2.34% at 3 days and 3.42% at 7 days. The standalone Transformer, however, shows a more significant degradation of performance, raising MAPE from 3.21% (1-day window) to 6.78% (7-day window), which is an 111% relative increase in prediction error. Likewise, the LSTM-XGBoost hybrid suffers a significant loss, increasing from 2.95% to 7.23% in the same forecasting horizon.

However, the prediction errors generated by M2A2-Net are generally smaller than those of the best-performing baseline, with the relative gain being around 49% compared to the best baseline when evaluating at the seven-day horizon for Bitcoin and around 44% for Ethereum. Such superior temporal stability offers evidence of the regime adaptive attention mechanism and probabilistic components that are able to extract longer term dependencies while ensuring calibrated uncertainty estimates. The forecasting gains of M2A2-Net are statistically significant for all horizons and cryptocurrencies (p < 0.01) according to the Diebold–Mariano tests. Moreover, Model Confidence Set analysis confirms that M2A2-Net is the only model in the 95% Confidence Set for Bitcoin and Ethereum and in the top two for other assets, thus substantiating its strong predictive performance over various time horizons.

Trading strategy performance and economic significance

Aside from accuracy in predicting stock prices, the practicality of using the M2A2-Net model is further explored through simulated trading approaches under practical conditions, such as 0.1% fees and size positions based on confidence levels. The simulated trading approach employing the M2A2-Net model yields annual Sharpe ratios of 2.34 for Bitcoin and 2.18 for Ethereum, which surpasses both the buy-and-hold strategy and all other baseline models. Additionally, the downside risks are minimized, as shown by maximum drawdowns of 18.3%, compared to 34.7% in the buy-and-hold approach and 24.6% in the best-performing baseline model. These findings suggest that the statistical forecasting advantages of M2A2-Net can translate into tangible trading value, although real-world constraints must still be considered.

The trading strategy evaluation employs a simplistic threshold strategy with an estimated processing cost of 0.1%, which does not effectively reflect real-world cryptocurrency trading conditions. In practice, the trade execution price and profits are influenced and reduced by high trading fees, bid-ask spreads, slippage, and market effect, especially in non-liquid alt-coin markets. The presented evaluation metrics, including the Sharpe ratio, can be interpreted as optimistic estimates due to the lack of modeling for these factors. In the future, research will incorporate realistic trade execution assumptions, including slippage and dynamic trading costs.

Discussion and implications

Theoretical implications

The empirical validation of M2A2 2Net has a few theoretical implications to the cryptocurrency forecasting and neural temporal modeling. One, the established excellence of regime-adaptive attention functions disputes the status quo of stable feature significance in financial time series. Our result supports the assertion that relevant predictive features show dynamic regime-dependence with sentiment and on-chain feature taking precedence during high-volatility and technical indicators prevailing in equilibrium periods. The integration of 47 heterogeneous features within the proposed architecture demonstrates the potential of synthesizing multi-modal financial data for cryptocurrency forecasting. This design of incorporating blockchain metrics, sentiment embeddings, macroeconomics, and technical indicators within the architecture highlights the effectiveness of cross-modal temporal learning for capturing the complexities in cryptocurrency prices. As opposed to the assumption underlying minimalistic feature selection strategies, multimodal integration does not affect but improves model performance in terms of accuracy. Furthermore, by using probability distributions to model uncertainty, the model guarantees that the uncertainty predictions are reliable, with a coverage of 94.3% for 95% prediction intervals.

Furthermore, sentiment analysis in financial markets must account for complex behavioral and ethical dimensions, as moral attitudes and social-adjustive behaviors significantly influence market dynamics and risk perceptions, even in unconventional asset classes36.

Practical applications

M2A2-Net framework provides practical benefits of a tangible practical nature in the different fields of cryptocurrency markets. When applied to algorithmic trading systems, the model shows relative improvements in accuracy compared to the evaluated baseline models, with promising economic returns in simulated trading scenarios, with a simulated trading strategy on the Bitcoin exchange earning a Sharpe ratio of 2.34 versus 1.12 on the same instrument with a basic buy-and-hold policy. Intrinsic identification of the market regimes becomes possible through regime adaptive attention in the absence of external sources of volatility information. The probabilistic nature of the output will help in determining position sizing from a risk perspective, while knowledge about feature importance can be used to inform investment decisions, particularly in times of volatility. However, integrating these advanced probabilistic forecasts into real-world trading requires overcoming behavioral inertia, as market participants often exhibit resistance to uprooting their embedded status quo and habitual decision-making patterns when adopting novel AI-driven tools37.

Limitations and future research directions

The proposed model M2A2-Net has proved its ability of strong predictive performance and well-calibrated uncertainty estimation; some limitations should be noted for a comprehensive understanding of the current boundaries of the model.

The first is the architectural complexity, with about 8.7 million trainable parameters and several attention mechanisms, which comes with high computational costs. The model is trained with a considerable amount of GPU memory and time, which could hinder immediate use in ultra-low latency, high-frequency trading applications. Future research will investigate model compression methods, such as Knowledge Distillation and Structural Pruning to improve computational efficiency without sacrificing prediction accuracy.

Secondly, the model is dependent on the quality and continuity of the multimodal data used. While newer or less liquid cryptocurrencies may have on-chain data sparsity, missing data is also handled using forward filling. Moreover, sentiment data from social media can be noisy, contain bots, and be sarcastic – something that may not be properly captured by the language models used. Subsequent updates might incorporate even more advanced NLP capabilities tailored for the crypto community, such as sophisticated slang recognition and filtering bot detection.

Third, the economic results of the simulated trading strategy were good, but the assumptions about how the strategy would be executed are made to be simple. This is a “no kill” simulation, meaning it does not accurately represent real world market friction, such as slippage, bid-ask spreads or market impact, especially under extreme market conditions or in less liquid altcoin markets. Therefore, the reported Sharpe ratios should be interpreted as best possible. Further developments will include the use of realistic order-book dynamics and dynamic transaction cost modelling.

Fourthly, the current framework treats each cryptocurrency separately and fails to take into account the cross-asset correlations and contagion effects that are common in the blockchain world. Moreover, the model is based on exogenous volatility thresholds for regime switching, not on endogenous regime switching. Future research will build upon this architecture and incorporates Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to capture spatial dependency in various tokens and will also investigate endogenous regime switching mechanisms.

Lastly, the training and validation periods (2022–2025) represent certain macroeconomic conditions and crypto market cycles. The success of the model’s ability to accommodate market shocks not seen before or dramatically varying macroeconomic circumstances has yet to be fully tested. The framework will continually be refined and revised with subsequent data collected after 2025, to robustly test its temporal stability and long-term strength.

Additionally, future research could explore complex game-theoretic models, such as symmetric quantum Stackelberg duopoly games with heterogeneous expectations38, to better capture the strategic, non-linear interactions between dominant market makers and retail traders. Finally, as models grow in complexity, addressing the hardware-level fault tolerance and bit-width accumulation issues in specialized AI accelerators will be crucial for deploying these massive networks in high-frequency trading environments39.

Conclusion

Accurate token price prediction is made possible by neural networks’ ability to efficiently record intricate blockchain market patterns. The method strengthens forecasting accuracy, facilitates well-informed decision-making, and increases flexibility in volatile and dynamic cryptocurrency contexts. Under extremely volatile and non-stationary market conditions, the proposed M2A2-Net shows exceptional ability to estimate cryptocurrency token prices. The model successfully captures intricate interdisciplinary interactions by combining 47 heterogeneous characteristics, such as on-chain measurements, sentiment attachments, macroeconomic indicators, and technical signals. The structure achieves lower prediction errors compared to Transformers (3.21%), LSTM-XGBoost (2.95%), and BiGRU (2.58%), with relative improvements ranging from approximately 28% to 44% depending on the cryptocurrency, achieving a MAPE of 1.87% for Bitcoin and 2.34% for Ethereum. Strong risk-aware decision-making is supported by its probabilistic design, which guarantees accurate variance estimate and provides 94.3% coverage within 95% prediction intervals. While SHAP research validates the supremacy of cross-chain activity and emotions during volatility and technical indicators during stability phases, regime-responsive attention improves reaction to market dynamics.
