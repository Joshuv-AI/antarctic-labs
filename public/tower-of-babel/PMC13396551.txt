Expression of concern: “Novel Melatonin, Estrogen, and Progesterone Hormone Therapy Demonstrates Anti-Cancer Actions in MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 Breast Cancer Cells”
Published: 2026

The Journal Editor and Sage Publications hereby issue an expression of concern for the following article:

Hasan M, Browne E, Guarinoni L, Darveau T, Hilton K, Witt-Enderby PA. Novel Melatonin, Estrogen, and Progesterone Hormone Therapy Demonstrates Anti-Cancer Actions in MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 Breast Cancer Cells. Breast Cancer: Basic and Clinical Research. 2020;14. doi:10.1177/1178223420924634

The Journal Editor and Sage were alerted to a discussion surrounding the article on PubPeer. The concerns are summarised below:

1. Table 1, the Western Blot images for pERK1/2 and Beta-actin appears highly similar for the experiments including: Vehicle/BIX02189/PD98059

2. Table 1, the Western Blot images for pERK1/2 and Beta-actin appears highly similar for the experiments including: MEMP HT/MEMP HT + BIX02189/MEMP HT + PD98059

3. Table 2, the Western Blot images for pERK1/2 and Beta-actin appears highly similar for the experiments including: BIX02189/MEMP HT/PD98059

4. Table 2, the Western Blot images for pERK1/2 and Beta-actin appears highly similar for the experiments including: MEMP HT + PD98059/MEMP HT + BIX02189/PD98059

Sage contacted the authors for comments and requested the uncropped, raw images underlying the experiments.

The authors explained that pERK1/2 and beta-actin are the same molecular weight range of 42-45kD causing them both to migrate to similar positions during SDS-PAGE. The authors noted that all primary antibodies were added together and then after washing, both secondary antibodies were added, incubated, washed and imaged at the same time in grayscale, red channel and green channel. Referring to the raw images, the authors noted that there are background patterns that distinguish the Western Blot images.

The authors provided the uncropped, raw images underlying the experiments.When queried about the placement of the molecular weight (MW) markers in the images, the authors explained that the orientation of blots may have been flipped when scanned by the LICOR Imaging machine. The blots were placed on the imaging system with MW markers on the left, largest proteins on top and the protein bands have a concave upward appearance. Because the LICOR Imaging system scans from bottom to top, the images become flipped with the MW markers still on the left but the smaller proteins now on top, larger proteins at the bottom and protein bands with a concave downward appearance.

For the published images, the blots were re-oriented so that the MW markers appeared to the left and largest proteins at the top.

The Journal Editor assessed the response and noted that it was plausible, however, the Journal Editor and Sage are unable to uphold the claims made by the authors in the article. The raw images are enclosed as Supplementary material for the readers’ attention.

This expression of concern will remain in place to alert the readership unless further evidence is provided to resolve the dispute.
