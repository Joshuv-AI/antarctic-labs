Long-term outcomes of patients receiving Dialysis and undergoing Transcatheter aortic valve replacement with contemporary balloon-expandable valves
Authors: Hidemasa Shitan, Vivek Patel, Kazuki Suruga, Yuchao Guo, Daniel Ng, Brittany Cole, Aakriti Gupta, Tarun Chakravarty, Wen Cheng, Yuito Okada, Hasan Jilaihawi, Mamoo Nakamura, Raj R. Makkar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLong-term clinical outcomes after transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) in patients with severe aortic stenosis who received dialysis remain poorly investigated. Therefore, we aimed to evaluate long-term outcomes after TAVR using a contemporary balloon-expandable valve (BEV) in patients who received dialysis compared with those who did not.MethodsIn this single-center study, 143 patients who received dialysis were identified among 3189 patients who underwent TAVR with a contemporary BEV between June 2015 and February 2024. After propensity score matching, long-term outcomes were compared between 139 patients who received dialysis and 139 patients who did not. Causes of death after TAVR and predictors of mortality in the dialysis group were also investigated.ResultsPatients who received dialysis had higher 5-year mortality (adjusted HR: 2.38; 95% CI: 1.40–4.04; P < 0.001) than those who did not. Most deaths in the dialysis group occurred within 3 years after TAVR, and over 50% of the deaths were noncardiac-related, with infection as the leading cause. No significant differences were observed between the two groups in 5-year heart failure rehospitalization (subdistribution HR [sHR]: 1.20; 95% CI: 0.56–2.54; P = 0.640), aortic valve reintervention (sHR: 2.47; 95% CI: 0.40–15.19; P = 0.330), or disabling stroke (sHR: 0.35; 95% CI: 0.07–1.70; P = 0.190). In the dialysis group, baseline moderate or severe tricuspid regurgitation was independently associated with all-cause mortality.ConclusionsPatients who received dialysis had higher 5-year mortality than those who did not, with an increase in noncardiac deaths, particularly due to infection.

Introduction

TAVR is now widely adopted for the treatment of aortic stenosis (AS), regardless of surgical risk [1]. Despite its growing use, patients receiving dialysis have been excluded from multiple landmark clinical trials, primarily due to their poor short-term prognosis and elevated procedural risk [2], [3]. Consequently, long-term clinical outcomes after transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) in this high-risk group remain scarce. AS is the most common valvular heart disease in patients receiving dialysis. From 2002 to 2022, the number of newly diagnosed end-stage renal disease cases in the United States increased from 99,956 to 131,194, indicating a 31.3% increase [4], [5]. Patients with AS who are receiving dialysis often present with multiple comorbidities and are considered at high risk for surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR). Hence, TAVR should be considered a primary treatment option to improve outcomes.

To date, only one retrospective study has directly compared long-term outcomes after TAVR between patients receiving dialysis and those not receiving dialysis, in which approximately 40% of patients received older-generation transcatheter heart valves (THVs) [6]. Previous studies reported a significant increase in mortality among patients receiving dialysis throughout the follow-up period, likely due to multiple underlying comorbidities and increased procedural complexity. THV technology has advanced over time, and outcomes following TAVR have improved accordingly [7], [8]. However, at present, the long-term clinical outcomes of patients with AS who are receiving dialysis, specifically those treated with contemporary THVs, have not been thoroughly investigated.

Therefore, we aimed to compare long-term outcomes, specifically mortality and its causes, between patients who received dialysis after TAVR with contemporary balloon-expandable valves (BEVs) and those who did not. In addition, predictors of mortality were investigated to identify high-risk factors in the dialysis population undergoing TAVR.

Methods

Study design and population

This retrospective, single-center observational study included consecutive patients who underwent TAVR at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center between June 2015 and February 2024. We screened 4565 patients and excluded those who underwent valve-in-valve TAVR (n = 399), had noncontrast multidetector computed tomography (CT) imaging (n = 351), lacked suitable CT images or had missing data for analysis (n = 68), underwent TAVR with THVs other than BEVs or with older-generation BEVs (SAPIEN XT) (n = 493), or underwent TAVR for pure aortic regurgitation (n = 65). After these exclusions, 3189 patients treated with contemporary BEVs (SAPIEN 3, SAPIEN 3 Ultra, and SAPIEN 3 Ultra RESILIA) were included in the final analysis. Patients were classified into two groups according to whether they received dialysis, including both hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis, at the time of TAVR. After propensity score matching, 139 patients each in the dialysis and nondialysis groups (1:1) were compared for 5-year mortality, heart failure (HF) rehospitalization, aortic valve reintervention, and disabling stroke (Fig. 1). TAVR eligibility was determined by the heart team, and valve type and size were selected based on annular area measured using CT and transesophageal echocardiogram. Transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE) assessments were performed at baseline, 30 days, 1 year, and then annually. Causes of death were reviewed by investigators using available medical records and classified as cardiovascular or non-cardiovascular according to Valve Academic Research Consortium-3 (VARC-3) criteria [9].Fig. 1Study flowchart. When the cause of death was uncertain or multifactorial, cases were discussed among three investigators until consensus was reached. Deaths with insufficient information for classification were categorized as unknown. Other non-cardiovascular deaths included multiorgan failure, trauma, or suicide. The Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Cedars-Sinai Medical Center (Los Angeles, CA, USA) approved this study protocol (IRB Protocol ID: STUDY00003840), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants before the procedure. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki.

Definitions and study outcomes

Clinical outcomes were defined according to the VARC-3 criteria [9]. Baseline patient characteristics, CT and echocardiographic findings, and follow-up data were retrospectively collected from the interventional cardiology laboratory database at our institution. We compared clinical outcomes between patients who received dialysis and those treated without dialysis. The primary outcome was 5-year all-cause mortality after TAVR. Secondary outcomes included 5-year HF rehospitalization, aortic valve reintervention, and disabling stroke.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as median and interquartile range, while categorical variables are presented as numbers and percentages. The two groups were compared using the Student's t-test, the Mann–Whitney U test, Fisher's exact test, or the Pearson chi-square test, as appropriate. Given potential baseline differences in clinical and echocardiographic characteristics between patients who received dialysis and those who did not, we performed one-to-one propensity score matching with a caliper of 0.1 using nearest-neighbor matching. We adjusted for age, sex, body surface area, hypertension, dyslipidemia, prior stroke, prior transient ischemic attack, diabetes mellitus, hemoglobin, albumin, peripheral artery disease, coronary artery disease, prior myocardial infarction, prior percutaneous coronary intervention, prior coronary artery bypass grafting, atrial fibrillation or flutter, prior pacemaker implantation, left ventricular ejection fraction, AV peak velocity, AV mean gradient, AVA, and femoral access. The balance of propensity score matching was evaluated using the standardized mean difference, with thresholds of <0.1 indicating excellent balance and < 0.2 indicating acceptable balance. The covariate balance before and after propensity score matching is presented in Supplementary Fig. 1. Given that some residual imbalances persisted after propensity score matching, IPTW analysis using the same 22 covariates was performed as a sensitivity analysis to assess the robustness of the results. Covariate balance after IPTW is presented in Supplementary Fig. 2. Cumulative survival rates were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression was used to assess intergroup differences in all-cause mortality. The associations between clinical variables and all-cause mortality were assessed using univariate Cox proportional hazard models. Clinical variables included in the multivariable Cox regression models were selected using a theory-driven strategy based on prior literature. Multivariable Cox regression models were used to evaluate adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for 5-year all-cause mortality among patients receiving dialysis. Given the high non-cardiovascular mortality in patients receiving dialysis, cause-specific deaths were analyzed as competing risks using the Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model. Cumulative incidence functions were estimated with cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular death treated as competing risks for each other. Furthermore, to address potential competing risk bias in secondary outcomes, cumulative incidence of heart failure rehospitalization, aortic valve reintervention, and disabling stroke were also estimated using the Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model, with death treated as the competing risk. Statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.5.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), and significance was set at a P value <0.05.

Results

Serum albumin and hemoglobin levels were lower in this group. The Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) risk score was also higher in this group, reflecting their overall greater surgical risk, partly due to dialysis. Clinical outcomes

The median follow-up duration in the matched cohort was 721 days (Q1–Q3: 330–1686 days). The median follow-up duration was 523 days (Q1–Q3: 196–1049 days) in the dialysis group and 1065 days (Q1–Q3: 477–1825 days) in the nondialysis group. In the matched cohort, 26 patients (9.4%) were lost to follow-up before 5 years, including 16 patients (11.5%) in the dialysis group and 10 patients (7.2%) in the nondialysis group. Patients who received dialysis showed a higher incidence of all-cause mortality than those treated without dialysis (adjusted HR: 2.38; 95% CI: 1.40–4.04; P < 0.001, Fig. 2A). Within the first 3 years after TAVR, mortality increased markedly among patients in the dialysis group, after which it relatively plateaued. No significant difference was observed in HF rehospitalization (subdistribution HR [sHR]: 1.20; 95% CI: 0.56–2.54; P = 0.640), aortic valve reintervention (sHR: 2.47; 95% CI: 0.40–15.19; P = 0.330), and disabling stroke (sHR: 0.35; 95% CI: 0.07–1.70; P = 0.190) between the two groups (Figs. 2B–D). In the sensitivity analysis using IPTW, consistent results were observed for all clinical outcomes throughout the follow-up period (Supplementary Table 1). Non-cardiovascular death was significantly higher in patients receiving dialysis (sHR: 9.58; 95% CI: 3.89–23.59; P < 0.001), whereas cardiovascular death did not differ significantly between the groups (sHR: 1.53; 95% CI: 0.84–2.80; P = 0.170) (Supplementary Fig. 3). When evaluating the cause of death within the groups, over 50% of mortality was due to noncardiac causes in the dialysis group, whereas the majority of deaths were cardiac-related in the nondialysis group (Fig. 3A and B). Moreover, in the dialysis group, infection was the most predominant cause of death (Fig. 3C). Detailed causes of infection-related deaths and the median time from TAVR to death are presented in Supplementary Table 2. Major clinical outcomes, including new permanent pacemaker implantation, are summarized in Supplementary Table 3. In multivariable Cox proportional hazards analysis, moderate or severe TR remained independently associated with all-cause mortality among patients receiving dialysis (HR: 1.99; 95% CI: 1.11–3.57; P = 0.021, Table 4). Fig. 4 presents the echocardiographic results from baseline to 5 years after TAVR. Complete echocardiographic follow-up data were available in 210 patients (75.5%) at 30 days (100 in the dialysis group and 110 in the nondialysis group), 125 patients (45.0%) at 1 year (50 and 75, respectively), 54 patients (19.4%) at 3 years (19 and 35, respectively), and 42 patients (15.1%) at 5 years (11 and 31, respectively). At baseline, no significant difference was observed in AV mean gradient or AVA between the two groups. Post-TAVR, AV mean gradient and AVA were similar between the groups throughout the follow-up period. During follow-up, patients who received dialysis showed a numerical decline in AVA and an increase in AV mean gradient compared with the nondialysis group. However, these differences were not statistically significant. Discussion

We investigated the long-term outcomes of patients receiving dialysis following treatment with contemporary BEVs. The major findings of this study are:1)The dialysis group had a higher 5-year mortality than the nondialysis group, with most deaths occurring within 3 years after TAVR.2)In the dialysis group, over 50% of the deaths were due to noncardiac causes, with infection being the most frequent cause.3)Moderate or severe TR was independently associated with all-cause mortality among patients receiving dialysis.4)No statistically significant difference was observed between the groups in valve performance after TAVR, evaluated by AV mean gradient and AVA within 5 years, though numerical worsening of these parameters was observed in the dialysis group after 3 years.

Historically, clinical outcomes in patients receiving dialysis have been poor. Szerlip et al. reported clinical outcomes in patients who received dialysis after TAVR from a large registry. However, the follow-up period was limited to 1 year [10], and only a few other studies have reported 5-year mortality outcomes in these patients [6], [11], [12]. These studies included a substantial proportion of older-generation THVs and reported 5-year mortality rates ranging from 75% to 90%. In contrast, our study demonstrated a 5-year mortality rate of 56.4% in patients who received dialysis, which was lower than that reported in prior studies. The better survival rate observed in this study may be attributed to several factors. First, TAVR techniques and procedural strategies have evolved, likely yielding better patient outcomes [7]. Second, we exclusively used contemporary BEVs, which may be associated with improved valve durability. Third, the patients who received dialysis in the current study were younger than those in prior studies [6], [11]. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to primarily evaluate 5-year mortality in patients receiving dialysis after TAVR exclusively with contemporary BEVs. Despite improved outcomes over time compared with prior studies, the dialysis group had significantly higher 5-year mortality than the nondialysis group, and a substantial proportion of patients who received dialysis died within 3 years after TAVR. This highlights the remaining significant impact of dialysis on long-term survival after TAVR, even with advancements in procedural techniques and improvements in device performance.

In the nondialysis group, cardiovascular death accounted for approximately 80% of overall mortality. In contrast, over 50% of overall mortality were non-cardiovascular deaths in patients who received dialysis, with infection as the leading cause. Notably, the majority of infection-related deaths occurred within 1 year after TAVR, suggesting that aggressive infection management during this critical period may contribute to improving long-term outcomes in dialysis patients undergoing TAVR.

In this study, baseline moderate or severe TR was independently associated with mortality in the dialysis group. Zahr et al. also reported that the severity of preprocedural TR was strongly associated with higher mortality after TAVR, including in patients receiving dialysis. Our findings suggest that baseline TR severity serves as an important prognostic marker in dialysis patients undergoing TAVR. Given its higher prevalence in this population, careful assessment of TR severity may help with risk stratification when considering TAVR in patients receiving dialysis.

To date, only a few studies have investigated valve hemodynamics after TAVR in patients receiving dialysis [13], [14]. Moreover, these studies include only short-term follow-up of 1–2 years after TAVR, and long-term data are still lacking. In the present study, no significant difference in echocardiographic valve hemodynamics was observed between the dialysis and nondialysis groups over the 5-year follow-up period using contemporary BEV. However, a gradual numerical increase in AV mean gradient and a decrease in AVA were observed in the dialysis group beginning at 3 years after TAVR, whereas they remained stable for 5 years in the nondialysis group, though these differences did not reach statistical significance. Whether this numerical trend reflects early valvular deterioration remains speculative due to the limited number of patients with longer-term follow-up data. However, similar patterns have been reported in dialysis patients after SAVR [15]. Longer-term follow-up in a larger cohort is needed to determine whether early valvular deterioration occurs after TAVR in patients receiving dialysis.

Limitations

This study has some limitations. First, it was a single-center, retrospective, observational study, which may have introduced selection bias in patient enrollment. Second, despite using propensity score matching, some residual imbalances persisted. To address this limitation, an IPTW approach was performed as a sensitivity analysis, and consistent results were observed. Third, the analysis was limited to patients treated with BEVs, and the findings may not be generalizable to those treated with self-expanding valves. Fourth, important clinical factors, including the etiology of renal disease, dialysis vintage, vascular access type, frailty, inflammatory markers, and prior history of serious infection, were not available in the dataset. Therefore, the potential influence of these dialysis-specific and vulnerability-related factors on long-term outcomes could not be fully assessed. Fifth, the secondary outcomes, including aortic valve reintervention and disabling stroke, had limited statistical power due to the relatively small number of events. Therefore, these outcomes should be interpreted with caution, as the results may have been underpowered to detect clinically meaningful differences. Sixth, although moderate or severe TR was associated with mortality among patients receiving dialysis, data regarding the underlying causes of TR, such as right ventricular function, pulmonary hypertension, and volume overload status, were not available. Finally, echocardiographic analyses of valve hemodynamics were limited to patients who survived and had available follow-up data, which may have introduced survivor bias.

Conclusion

Long-term mortality in patients receiving dialysis remained higher than in those treated without dialysis despite the use of contemporary BEVs. Most deaths occurred within 3 years after TAVR, with infection as the leading cause, and moderate or severe TR was independently associated with all-cause mortality in patients receiving dialysis. These findings suggest that greater emphasis on infection prevention and control may improve long-term outcomes in this high-risk population. Baseline TR severity may also serve as a useful prognostic marker to help with risk stratification when considering TAVR in patients receiving dialysis.
