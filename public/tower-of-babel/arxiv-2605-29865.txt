Abstract:

In this work, we introduce a new class of Leibniz algebras, named
quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebras, which generalizes the minimal
condition on ideals. Furthermore, we provide some characterizations
and give conditions under which a
quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebra is Artinian. Finally, within the
framework of Leibniz algebras, we establish a connection between
prime ideals and the quasi-Artinian structure.

2010 MSC:
17A32, 17A36, 17B40, 18B99.

Keywords:
Descending Chain Condition on Leibniz algebra,
semisimple Leibniz algebra, Prime Leibniz algebra, Prime ideals.

1
Introduction

The concept of Leibniz algebra originated in works published in 1965 by Bloh
[
5
]
, and did
not receive significant attention until it was independently rediscovered by Jean Louis Loday
[
17
]
. Essentially, A Leibniz algebra is a
vector space equipped with a binary operation which has the property of being a derivation of
itself. Consequently, Leibniz algebras extend the class of Lie algebras,
and much of the research on Leibniz algebras focuses on developping
analogues of results from Lie theory on Leibniz algebras.

This paper studies the Artinian conditions on Leibniz algebras. The concept of Artinian algebras plays an important role in the study of
infinite-dimensional algebraic structures. It originated in the foundational work of
E. Artin and E. Noether whose work on
chain conditions laid the groundwork for modern ring theory. In particular, Noether’s introduction
[
18
]

of the ascending chain condition initiated the study of finiteness
conditions in algebra and Artin’s examination
[
1
]
of descending chain conditions
led to tools useful in controlling the ideal structure of rings
[
2
,
6
,
13
,
12
,
16
]
.
These conditions have become central in extending
finiteness methods to more general algebraic systems ,
including Lie algebras, especially in the infinite-dimensional case
[
4
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
14
]
.

An Artinian Leibniz algebra is defined as a Leibniz algebra that satisfies the descending
chain condition on both left and right ideals. This provides a useful framework for understanding their structure, particularly in the infinite-dimensional case. As for many algebraic structures, this advantage can be extended to a broader class of Leibniz algebras by considering a suitable generalization of the Artinian condition. This motivates the introduction in this paper of the class of quasi-Artinian Leibniz
algebras, which generalizes both Artinian Leibniz algebras (
[
19
]
,
[
10
]
) and quasi-Artinian Lie algebras
[
15
]
, and is shown to include important classes of Leibniz algebras, such as solvable algebras. We investigate several results analogous to those known for quasi-Artinian Lie algebras.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the necessary
definitions and set the notations used throughout the paper.
In Section 3,
we introduce the notion of quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebra in the category of Leibniz algebras and provide some examples and characterizations of these algebras . Among the results obtained, we show that the class of quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebras is closed under
quotients but not under extensions.
In Section 4, we study the connection between the subclass of Leibniz algebras satisfying the minimal condition and
the subclass of Leibniz algebras satisfying the minimal condition on abelian ideals. Finally,
in section 5, we investigate infinite dimensional Leibniz algebras with chain condition(CC) on ideals of ideals.

2
Preliminaries on Leibniz algebras

We follow the notations and terminologies in
[
4
]
, and
some of the notions are equally found in
[
10
]
and

[
3
]
. For convenience, we recall some preliminaries.

Definition 2.1

A
(left) Leibniz algebra
is a vector space
{\mathfrak{g}}
equipped with a bilinear map
[-,-]:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
usually called
Leibniz bracket
, satisfying the left
Leibniz identity
:

[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+[y,[x,z]],

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Leibniz algebra of arbitrary dimension.
For a given ordinal
\alpha
,
\mathfrak{g}^{\left(\alpha\right)}
denotes
the
\alpha
-th terms of the (transfinite) derived series defined
inductively by:

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{\left(0\right)},\mathfrak{g}^{\left(\alpha+1\right)}=\left[\mathfrak{g}^{\left(\alpha\right)},\mathfrak{g}^{\left(\alpha\right)}\right]

and

\mathfrak{g}^{\left(\rho\right)}=\underset{\alpha<\rho}{\cap}\mathfrak{g}^{\left(\alpha\right)}

for every limit ordinal
\rho.

The right and the left centers of

\mathfrak{g}
are respectively defined as follows:

Z^{r}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:[y,x]=0,~\mbox{for each
element}~y\in\mathfrak{g}\},

Z^{l}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:[x,y]=0,~\mbox{for each
element}~y\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

Thus the center of
\mathfrak{g}
is given by:

Z_{\mathfrak{g}}=Z^{r}_{\mathfrak{g}}\cap Z^{l}_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Starting from the notion of the center of the Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, for a given ordinal
\alpha,
we denote by
\zeta_{\alpha}
,
the
\alpha
-th term of the transfinite upper central series of

\mathfrak{g}:

\zeta_{0}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\zeta_{1}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\zeta_{2}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\zeta_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})=\zeta_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
and define it inductively as follows:

\zeta_{0}(\mathfrak{g})=0,\zeta_{1}(\mathfrak{g})=Z_{\mathfrak{g}},

\zeta_{\alpha+1}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)/\zeta_{\alpha}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)=\zeta_{1}\left(\mathfrak{g}/\zeta_{\alpha}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)\right)
and
\zeta_{\rho}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)=\underset{\alpha<\rho}{\cup}\zeta_{\alpha}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right),

for every limit ordinal
\rho
. The last term,
\zeta_{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}),

of this series is called the upper hypercenter of
\mathfrak{g}
while the
hypercenter of
\mathfrak{g}
is
\zeta_{\ast}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)=\underset{\alpha}{\cup}\zeta_{\alpha}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right).
A Leibniz
algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be hypercentral if it coincides with its
hypercenter.

Denote respectively by
\leq
and
\vartriangleleft
the
relation of subalgebra and that of ideal of
\mathfrak{g},
and let

\mathfrak{X}
be the collection of Leibniz algebras up to
isomorphism, together with the
0
-dimensional Leibniz algebra.
Define the following classes:

\displaystyle{}{S}\mathfrak{X}

\displaystyle:=\{H:\exists\mathfrak{g}\in\mathfrak{X}~\mbox{and}~H\leq\mathfrak{g}\}.

\displaystyle{}{E}\mathfrak{X}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathfrak{g}:\exists\quad 0=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft\mathfrak{g}_{n}=\mathfrak{g},\quad\mbox{a finite series,}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{such that}\quad\mathfrak{g}_{i+1}/\mathfrak{g}_{i}\in\mathfrak{X},\quad\mbox{for}\quad 0\leq i\leq n-1\}.

\displaystyle{}{R}\mathfrak{X}

\displaystyle:=~\{\mathfrak{g}:\exists\quad\left(I_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\varLambda},\quad I_{\alpha}\vartriangleleft\mathfrak{g},\quad\mathfrak{g}/I_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{X}

\displaystyle\mbox{for all}\quad\alpha\in\varLambda\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underset{\alpha\in\varLambda}{\cap}I_{\alpha}=Leib(\mathfrak{g})\}.

\displaystyle{}\mathfrak{X}^{Q}

\displaystyle:=~\{\mathfrak{g}:\quad\forall I\vartriangleleft\mathfrak{g},\quad\mathfrak{g}/I\in\mathfrak{X}\}.

We say that a class
\mathfrak{X}
of Leibniz algebras is
E
-
closed

(respectively
R
-
closed
) if
{E}\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}

(respectively
{R}\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}
).

Throughout the remainder of this paper, we adopt the following notation for these classes of Leibniz
algebras:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}

\displaystyle:=~abelian.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}^{d}

\displaystyle:=~solvable~of~derived~length\leq d.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}

\displaystyle:=~nilpotent.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{c}

\displaystyle:=~nilpotent~of~class\leq c.

\displaystyle E\mathfrak{U}

\displaystyle:=~solvable.

\displaystyle\;{\L}\mathfrak{N}

\displaystyle:=~locally~nilpotent.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{3}

\displaystyle:=~Leibniz~algebra~\mathfrak{g}~such~that~\zeta_{\ast}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)=\mathfrak{g}.

Remark 2.2

1.

If
\mathfrak{R}
denotes the subclass of Leibniz algebras
\mathfrak{g}
whose
elements satisfy:
x\in\mathfrak{g}
implies
x=0
or

x\notin\left[x,\mathfrak{g}\right]\cup\left[\mathfrak{g},x\right]
. Then

{\L}\mathfrak{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}
.

2.

Moreover if one denotes by
\;\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft
the class
of Leibniz algebras together with the minimal condition, then we
have the following subclasses:

\displaystyle\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft{}_{E\mathfrak{U}}

\displaystyle:=~the~subclass~of~Leibniz~algebras~satisfying~the~minimal~condition

\displaystyle on~solvable~ideals.

\displaystyle\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft\mathfrak{U}

\displaystyle:=~the~subclass~of~Leibniz~algebras~satisfying~the~minimal~condition

\displaystyle on~abelian~ideals.

These two subclasses are related by:

\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft{}{E}\mathfrak{U}\subsetneq\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft\mathfrak{U}
since every abelian two-sided ideal is solvable, but the converse is not true.

3
Quasi-Artinian structure on Leibniz algebras.

3.1
Definitions and some characterizations

Hereafter, we extend the well-known notion of the minimal condition on ideals from Lie algebras to the category of Leibniz algebras, and we provide several characterizations of Leibniz algebras satisfying this condition.

Definition 3.1

A Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be a left (resp. right) quasi-Artinian if, for every descending chain of ideals

I_{1}\supseteq I_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq I_{t}\supseteq\cdots,

there exists
m_{l}~(\mbox{resp.}~m_{r})\in\mathbb{N}
such
that for all
n\in\mathbb{N},

\left[\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{l})},I_{m_{l}}\right]\subseteq I_{n}~(resp.\left[I_{m_{r}},\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{r})}\right]\subseteq I_{n}).

We say that a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is quasi-Artinian if it is both left and right quasi-Artinian.

Notations:

The class of left (resp. right) quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebras is denoted by

q\min\text{-}\lhd_{l}
(resp.
q\min\text{-}\lhd_{r}
), and the class of all quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebras is denoted by
q\min\text{-}\lhd
.

Hereafter, we present some observations and characterizations of quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebras.

Remark 3.2

1.

A Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be Artinian if any decreasing
sequence of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
terminates. Consequently any Artinian
Leibniz algebra is quasi-Artinian.

2.

Any finite dimensional Leibniz algebra is Artinian, and hence quasi-Artinian.

3.

Let
I
be a two-sided ideal of a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such
that
\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{I}
is Artinian, then
\mathfrak{g}
is quasi-Artinian.

4.

A Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is quasi-Artinian if and
only if for every descending chain
of ideals,

I_{1}\supseteq I_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq I_{t}\supseteq\cdots,

of
\mathfrak{g},
there exists positive integer
s
such that:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\left[\mathfrak{g}^{\left(s\right)},I_{s}\right]\subseteq\underset{i\geq 1}{\cap}I_{i}\\
\\
\left[I_{s},\mathfrak{g}^{\left(s\right)}\right]\subseteq\underset{i\geq 1}{\cap}I_{i}\end{array}\right.

Proposition 3.3

A Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is quasi-Artinian if and only if there exists
I
, a two-sided ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{g}/I
is solvable
and the sets of ideals
\{[\mathcal{H},I]:\mathcal{H}\lhd\mathfrak{g}\}
and
\{[I,\mathcal{H}]:\mathcal{H}\lhd\mathfrak{g}\}
satisfy the minimal condition.

Proof.
Assume that
\mathfrak{g}\in q\min\mbox{-}\lhd
and set
I=\mathfrak{g}^{(\omega)}
with
w
the smallest limit ordinal. Then
I
is a two-sided ideal of
\mathfrak{g}.

Since
(\mathfrak{g}^{(k)})_{k\geq 0}
is a decreasing sequence of ideals of
\mathfrak{g},
there exists
n<w
such that

\mathfrak{g}^{(n+1)}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}=I
.
So
(\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{I})^{(n+1)}=0,
and hence
\mathfrak{g}/I
is solvable.
Now consider the sequence of ideals
(\mathcal{H}_{i}\lhd\mathfrak{g})_{i\geq 1}
such that

[\mathcal{H}_{1},I]\supseteq[\mathcal{H}_{2},I]\supseteq\cdots\supseteq[\mathcal{H}_{n},I]\supseteq\cdots.

Since
\mathfrak{g}\in q\min\mbox{-}\lhd
, there exists an integer
r\geq 1
such that
[\mathfrak{g}^{(r)},[\mathcal{H}_{r},I]]\subseteq[\mathcal{H}_{i},I]
and

[[\mathcal{H}_{r},I],\mathfrak{g}^{(r)}]\subseteq[\mathcal{H}_{i},I]
, for all
i\geq 1
. Notice that
\omega
is a limit ordinal, thus
[\mathfrak{g}^{(\omega)},\mathfrak{g}^{(\omega)}]=I^{(1)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(\omega)}=I.
Hence for
n<\omega,

I=\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(\omega)}.
So if
m=\mbox{max}(\{n,r\}),
then

[\mathcal{H}_{m},I]=[\mathcal{H}_{m},[I,I]]\subseteq[I,[\mathcal{H}_{m},I]]=[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},[\mathcal{H}_{m},I]]\subseteq[\mathcal{H}_{i},I],
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}.
So the set
\{[\mathcal{H},I]:\mathcal{H}\lhd\mathfrak{g}\}
satisfies the minimal condition. Similarly, one shows that
\{[I,\mathcal{H}]:\mathcal{H}\lhd\mathfrak{g}\}
also satisfies the minimal condition.

Conversely, let
I
be a two-sided ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\mathfrak{g}/I
is solvable and consider the descending chain of ideals
of
\mathfrak{g}
:

\mathcal{J}_{1}\supseteq\mathcal{J}_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{J}_{n}\supseteq\cdots.

Then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}=I
and
the set of ideals:
\{[I,\mathcal{J}_{i}]:i\geq 1\}
has a
minimal element
[I,\mathcal{J}_{s}].
Set
m=\mbox{max}\{s,n\},

we have
[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},\mathcal{J}_{m}]\subseteq[I,\mathcal{J}_{s}]\subseteq[I,\mathcal{J}_{i}]\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{i}
, for all
i\geq 1.
That is
\mathfrak{g}
is a left
quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebra. Similarly, one shows that

\mathfrak{g}
is a right quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebra. Therefore

\mathfrak{g}
is a quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebra.

3.2
On Quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebras

The main goal of this subsection
is to establish the connection between some fundamental
properties of Leibniz algebras and the minimal condition of ideals
on Leibniz algebras.

Proposition 3.4

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Leibniz algebra, then the following statements
hold.

1.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is solvable, then it is quasi-Artinian.

2.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is hypercentral and quasi-Artinian, then
\mathfrak{g}
is solvable.

Proof.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Leibniz algebra.

To prove the first statement, suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is solvable. Then there exists
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{0})}=0
. For any descending chain of
ideals of
\mathfrak{g},

I_{0}\supseteq I_{1}\supseteq I_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq I_{t}\supseteq\cdots
, one has
[\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{0})},I_{m_{0}}]=[I_{m_{0}},\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{0})}]=0\in I_{n},
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
So
\mathfrak{g}
is a
quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebra.

To prove the second statement, let
\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(t)}\supseteq\cdots
be a descending chain
of ideals of a hypercentral Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Observe that
the transfinite hypercentrality of
\mathfrak{g}
implies

\bigcap_{\alpha<\omega}\mathfrak{g}^{(\alpha)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(\omega)}=0
with

\omega
, the smallest limit ordinal.
Moreover if
\mathfrak{g}
is quasi-Artinian, there
exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

g^{(m+1)}=[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}
for all

n\geq 0
. Therefore

\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(m+1)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(m+2)}=\cdots,

and thus
g^{(m+k)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(w)}=0
for ll
k\geq 1
. Hence
\mathfrak{g}
is
solvable.

Corollary 3.5

A Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is quasi-Artinian if one of the following
assertion holds:

1.

\mathfrak{g}
has a solvable ideal
H
such that
\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{H}
is solvable.

2.

\mathfrak{g}
has finitely many subalgebras
\{H_{i}:0\leq i\leq n\}
such that
0=H_{0}\vartriangleleft H_{1}\vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft H_{n}=\mathfrak{g}
and such that

\frac{H_{i+1}}{H_{i}}
is abelian for
0\leq i\leq n-1.

Proof.
On one hand, let
H
be a solvable two-sided ideal of a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{H}
is
solvable. There exists
0\neq n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}=H
and since
H
is solvable,
\mathfrak{g}
is also solvable
and the result follows by Proposition
3.4
.

On the other hand, assume that
\mathfrak{g}
has finitely many subalgebras
\{H_{i}:0\leq i\leq n\}
such that
0=H_{0}\vartriangleleft H_{1}\vartriangleleft\cdots\vartriangleleft H_{n}=\mathfrak{g}
and such that

\frac{H_{i+1}}{H_{i}}
is abelian for
0\leq i\leq n-1.
Then
H_{1}

is obviously solvable since
\frac{H_{1}}{H_{0}}
is abelian. Now assume
that
H_{k}
is solvable for
2\leq i\leq n-1.
Since

\frac{H_{n}}{H_{n-1}}
is abelian, then
\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}=H_{n-1}
and
by the induction hypothesis,
\mathfrak{g}
is solvable. The result follows again by Proposition
3.4
.

Theorem 3.6

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Leibniz algebra over an arbitrary field of characteristic different to two.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathfrak{g}
is quasi-Artinian,

(ii)

There exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every descending chain
of ideals
I_{1}\supseteq I_{2}\supseteq\cdots

of
\mathfrak{g}
, the descending chains of ideals
\left[\mathfrak{g}^{\left(m\right)},I_{1}\right]\supseteq\left[\mathfrak{g}^{\left(m\right)},I_{2}\right]\supseteq\cdots

and
\left[I_{1},\mathfrak{g}^{\left(m\right)}\right]\supseteq\left[I_{2},\mathfrak{g}^{\left(m\right)}\right]\supseteq\cdots

terminate.

(iii)

For any non-empty collection
\mathcal{C}
of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
, there
exists
I\in\mathcal{C}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left[\mathfrak{g}^{\left(m\right)},I\right]\subseteq J
and
\left[I,\mathfrak{g}^{\left(m\right)}\right]\subseteq J

for all
J\in\mathcal{C}
with
J\subseteq I
.

Proof.
To prove that

(i)\Rrightarrow(ii),

assume that
\mathfrak{g}\in q\min
-
\lhd
. Since
\mathfrak{g}\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(t)}\supseteq\cdots

is a descending chain of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
, there exists
s\in\mathbb{N}
such that
[\mathfrak{g}^{(s)},\mathfrak{g}^{(s)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(r)}

for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
. So,
\mathfrak{g}^{(s+1)}=[\mathfrak{g}^{(s)},\mathfrak{g}^{(s)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(r)}
for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence
\mathfrak{g}^{(s+1)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(s+k)}
for all
k\geq 1.

In addition,
I_{1}\supseteq I_{2}\supseteq I_{3}\supseteq\cdots
is a descending chain of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
, so there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}

such that
[\mathfrak{g}^{(t)},I_{t}]\subseteq I_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
p=\mbox{max}\{s+1,t\}\
;
one has
[\mathfrak{g}^{(p)},I_{t}]=[\mathfrak{g}^{(s+1)},I_{t}]\subseteq I_{k}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N},
thus
[\mathfrak{g}^{(s+1)},I_{t}]\subseteq[\mathfrak{g}^{(s+1)},I_{k}],
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}.
Taking
m_{1}=s+1,
we obtain
[\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{1})},I_{t}]\subseteq[\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{1})},I_{k}],
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}.
That is,
[\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{1})},I_{t}]=[\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{1})},I_{t+i}]
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}.
Henceforth the descending chain of ideals

\left[\mathfrak{g}^{\left(m_{1}\right)},I_{1}\right]\supseteq\left[\mathfrak{g}^{\left(m_{1}\right)},I_{2}\right]\supseteq\cdots

terminates. Similarly, we obtain
m_{2}
such that
[I_{1},\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{2})}]\supseteq[I_{2},\mathfrak{g}^{(m_{2})}]\supseteq\cdots
terminates.
Finally for
m=max\{m_{1},m_{2}\},
the above two sequences of
ideals terminate simultaneously.

To prove that
(ii)\Rrightarrow(iii),

let
\mathcal{C}
the collection of all non trivial ideals of
\mathfrak{g}.
Also, let
0\neq m\in\mathbb{N}
and
I_{0}\in\mathcal{C},
and
assume that
(iii)
does not hold. Then, there exists
I_{1}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
I_{0}\supseteq I_{1}

with
[I_{0},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]\supsetneq[I_{1},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]
or
[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},I_{0}]\supsetneq[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},I_{1}].

Similarly for
I_{1},
there exists
I_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
such that

I_{1}\supseteq I_{2}
and
[I_{1},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]\supsetneq[I_{2},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]
or
[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},I_{1}]\supsetneq[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},I_{2}].
So, we obtain progressively a non
stationary descending chain of ideals in
\mathcal{C},

I_{0}\supseteq I_{1}\supseteq I_{3}\cdots,
from which one can extract an infinite descending
subchain,
I^{\prime}_{0}\supseteq I^{\prime}_{1}\supseteq I^{\prime}_{2}\cdots,
with terms in
\mathcal{C}
such that one of the following descending chain

[I^{\prime}_{0},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]\supsetneq[I^{\prime}_{1},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]\supsetneq[I^{\prime}_{2},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]\supsetneq\cdots,

[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},I^{\prime}_{0}]\supsetneq[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},I^{\prime}_{1}]\supsetneq[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},]\supsetneq\cdots
does not terminate. Therefore
(ii)
does not
hold as well.

To prove that
(iii)\Rrightarrow(i),

let
I_{1}\supseteq I_{2}\supseteq I_{3}\supseteq\cdots
be a
descending chain of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}.
Set
\mathcal{C}=\{I_{s},s>0\}
the set whose elements are the terms of the above sequence.
From
(iii),
there exists
I_{s_{0}}\in\mathcal{C}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
[\mathfrak{g}^{(r)},I_{s_{0}}]\subseteq I_{s}
and

[I_{s_{0}},\mathfrak{g}^{(r)}]\subseteq I_{s},
for all
s\geq s_{0}.

Therefore
[\mathfrak{g}^{(r)},I_{s_{0}}]\subseteq I_{s}
and
[I_{s_{0}},\mathfrak{g}^{(r)}]\subseteq I_{s},
for all
s>0.
Considering
m=\mbox{max}\{r,s_{0}\}
, one has
[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},I_{m}]\subseteq I_{s}
and
[I_{m},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]\subseteq I_{s},
for all
s>0.
Hence

\mathfrak{g}
is a quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebra.

Remark 3.7

Using the above theorem, one shows that the subclass
q\min\mbox{-}\lhd
of quasi-Artinian
Leibniz algebra is not
E
-closed. Indeed, let
\mathcal{H}
be the infinitely
generated
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space defined by:

\mathcal{H}:=\langle\{x_{\alpha}:0\neq\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\}\rangle.

Endows
\mathcal{H}
with the abelian Leibniz algebra structure and
define the following vector space homomorphisms:

\mathcal{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{H},x_{\alpha}\longmapsto x_{\alpha+1};\mathcal{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{H},x_{\alpha}\longmapsto(\alpha-1)x_{\alpha-1};\mathcal{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{H},x_{\alpha}\longmapsto 2\alpha x_{\alpha}.

Set
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{K}
where
\mathcal{K}

is the vector space generated by
\{a,b,c\}.
Endows

\mathfrak{g}
with the Leibniz bracket by extending the bracket of
\mathcal{H}

as follows:
[x_{\alpha},v]=v(x_{\alpha}),

[v,x_{\alpha}]=0

and
[u,v]=u\circ v-v\circ u,
for
u,v\in\{a,b,c\}.

We
have the following facts:
\mathcal{H}\lhd\mathfrak{g},

(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}\cong\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathcal{H}}\in q\min\mbox{-}\lhd)
and thus
\mathfrak{g}\in E(q\min\mbox{-}\lhd).

Consider the descending sequence
(\mathcal{H}_{n})_{n>0}
of subspaces of

\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{H}_{n}=\langle\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{Q},\alpha<\frac{1}{n}\}\rangle.
Obviously

\mathcal{H}_{n}\lhd\mathfrak{g},
for
n>0
and for some positive integer
m,

\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}=\mathfrak{g}.
In addition, the descending sequence,

\{[\mathcal{H}_{n},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]=\mathcal{H}_{n}:n>0\},
of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}

does not satisfy the minimal condition. It follows from the above
theorem that
\mathfrak{g}\notin q\min\mbox{-}\lhd
and hence
q\min\mbox{-}\lhd
is not

E
-closed.

Proposition 3.8

The subclass of quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebras,
q\min\mbox{-}\lhd,
is
Q
-closed.

Proof.
Let
J
be an ideal of a quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and consider
\mathfrak{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\rightarrow}}\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{J}
the natural projection. Let
\bar{J_{1}}\supseteq\bar{J_{2}}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\bar{J_{r}}\supseteq\cdots

be a descending chain of ideals of
\bar{\mathfrak{g}}=\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{J}.
Then

\phi^{-1}(\bar{J_{1}})\supseteq\phi^{-1}(\bar{J_{2}})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\phi^{-1}(\bar{J_{r}})\supseteq\cdots
is a
descending chain of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Since
\mathfrak{g}
is quasi-Artinian, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}

such that
[\mathfrak{g}^{(m)},\phi^{-1}(\bar{J_{m}})]\subseteq\phi^{-1}(\bar{J_{n}})
and
[\phi^{-1}(\bar{J_{m}}),\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}]\subseteq\phi^{-1}(\bar{J_{n}})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Applying the map
\phi
to the previous inclusions leads us to the
following:
[(\phi(\mathfrak{g}))^{(m)},\bar{J_{m}}]\subseteq\bar{J_{n}}

and
[\bar{J_{m}},(\phi(\mathfrak{g}))^{(m)}]\subseteq\bar{J_{n}}
for
all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and thus
[\bar{\mathfrak{g}}^{(m)},\bar{J_{m}}]\subseteq\bar{J_{n}}

and
[\bar{J_{m}},\bar{\mathfrak{g}}^{(m)}]\subseteq\bar{J_{n}}
for all

Hence
q\min\mbox{-}\lhd
is
Q
-closed.

Theorem 3.9

Let
I
be an ideal of a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
\mathfrak{g}
is
quasi-Artinian if one of the following assertions holds:

1.

\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{I}
is quasi-Artinian and
I
is Artinian,

2.

I
is quasi-Artinian and
\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{I}
is solvable,

Proof.
To prove the first part, let
I
be an Artinian ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{I}
is quasi-Artinian and consider

J_{0}\supseteq J_{1}\supseteq J_{2}\supseteq\cdots
, a descending chain of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}.
Then
J_{0}\cap I\supseteq J_{1}\cap I\supseteq J_{2}\cap I\supseteq\cdots\supseteq J_{r}\cap I\supseteq\cdots
is a descending chain of

I
and
\frac{J_{0}+I}{I}\supseteq\frac{J_{1}+I}{I}\supseteq\frac{J_{2}+I}{I}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\frac{J_{r}+I}{I}\supseteq\cdots
is a descending chain of ideals
of
\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{I}

J_{s_{0}}\cap I
be its minimal element. On the

\mathfrak{g}/I

By taking

[J_{q},\mathfrak{g}^{(q)}]\subseteq J_{q}\subseteq J_{s_{0}-i},

J_{s_{0}+i}\cap J_{q}+J_{s_{0}+i}\cap I=J_{\mbox{max}\{s_{0}+i,q\}}+J_{s_{0}}\cap I=J_{\mbox{max}\{s_{0}+i,q\}},\quad\mbox{for all}\quad i>0,

[J_{q},\mathfrak{g}^{(q)}]\subseteq J_{s_{0}+i},

Therefore

[\mathfrak{g}^{(q)},J_{q}]\subseteq J_{n}

[J_{q},\mathfrak{g}^{(q)}]\subseteq J_{n},
for all

Hence
\mathfrak{g}

To prove the second part,
assume that

\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{I}

descending chain of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}.
So
J_{0}\cap I\supseteq J_{1}\cap I\supseteq J_{2}\cap I\supseteq\cdots
a descending

I

Observe that

[\mathfrak{g}^{(p+r+1)},J_{p+r+1}]\subseteq[\mathfrak{g}^{(p+r+1)},J_{p+r}]\subseteq[\mathfrak{g}^{(p+r)},[\mathfrak{g}^{(p+r)},J_{p+r}]]\subseteq[I^{(r)},I\cap J_{p+r}]\subseteq[I^{(r)},I\cap J_{r}]\subseteq I\cap J_{s}\subseteq J_{s},

Similarly, one can show that

Proposition 3.13

A finite direct sum of quasi-Artinian Leibniz algebras is

Proof.

Since

\min\mbox{-}\lhd\mathfrak{U}

Proposition 4.1

Over an arbitrary field of characteristic different to two,

\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft\subsetneq\left(\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft{E}\mathfrak{U}\right)^{Q}.

Proof.
Clearly,
\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft
is a subset of

\left(\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft{E}\mathfrak{U}\right)^{Q}.
So it
remains to show that this inclusion is strict. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a simple

be an infinite family of isomorphic
copy of
\mathcal{H}.
Set
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{i},
then
\mathfrak{g}

is an infinite dimensional Leibniz algebra. Considering the
decreasing sequence,
\{J_{s}=\oplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{H}_{i},s\geqslant 1\},

\mathfrak{g}\notin\min\mbox{-}\lhd

\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{I}\in\min\mbox{-}\lhd E\mathfrak{U}

Lemma 4.2

\left(\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft E\mathfrak{U}\right)^{Q}=\left(\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft\mathfrak{U}\right)^{Q}
.

Proof.
Observe that
\left(\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft E\mathfrak{U}\right)^{Q}\subset\left(\min\mbox{-}\vartriangleleft\mathfrak{U}\right)^{Q}

. For the reverse inclusion, It is enough to prove that

for all
s\geqslant 1
. We use induction on
s

this inclusion holds for
s=1
. Assume that
it holds for
s
and let
\mathfrak{g}\in(\min\mbox{-}\lhd\mathfrak{U})^{Q}.
Consider

I_{0}\supseteq I_{1}\supseteq I_{2}\supseteq\cdots

to be a decreasing chain of
\mathfrak{U}^{s+1}

\mathfrak{g}
.
For all

,

is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. In

. Hence

So

\min\mbox{-}\lhd=(\min\mbox{-}\lhd\mathfrak{U})^{Q}\cap(q\min\mbox{-}\lhd).

Definition 5.1

A Leibniz algebra

Proposition 5.2

\mathcal{K}
abelian, that is

Consider
the sequence,

of ideals of
\mathcal{K}

constructed inductively as follows:
\mathcal{K}_{0}=\langle\{e_{0}\}\rangle
is the ideal of
\mathcal{K}
spanned by

a

\mathcal{K}_{j+1}=\langle\mathcal{K}_{j}\cup\{e_{j+1}\}\rangle.
We have
\mathcal{K}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{j+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{K}.
Since
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
the maximal chain condition on ideal of ideals, then the above chain
terminates, and thus
\mathcal{K}
is of finite dimension. Suppose that the
result is established for all solvability indexes
\leq s
and let
us show it when this index is
s+1.
We have

\mathcal{K}^{(s+1)}=[\mathcal{K}^{(s)},\mathcal{K}^{(s)}]=0,
and so
\mathcal{K}^{(s)}
is a
solvable ideal. From the previous lines, it follows that

\mbox{dim}(\mathcal{K}^{(s)})<\infty.
So
\frac{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{K}^{(s)}}
is
a solvable ideal of the quotient Leibniz algebra

\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathcal{K}^{(s)}},
with solvability index
s.
In addition

\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathcal{K}^{(s)}}
also verifies the maximal chain
condition, so by the inductive
hypothesis,
\mbox{dim}(\frac{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{K}^{(s)}})<\infty,

Therefore
\mbox{dim}(\mathcal{K})<\infty.

Remark 5.3

1.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple Leibniz algebra, then
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathcal{A}\oplus Leib(\mathfrak{g}),
where
\mathcal{A}
is a semisimple Lie algebra and

Leib(\mathfrak{g})
is an
\mathcal{A}
-module.

2.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is an infinite dimensional Leibniz algebra over an
arbitrary field of characteristic different to two, together with the ascending or descending chain
condition, then
\mathfrak{g}
has a unique maximal solvable ideal
\mathcal{K}
and the
quotient algebra
\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathcal{K}}
is an infinite dimensional
semisimple Lie algebra.

Definition 5.4

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Leibniz algebra. The socle
S
of

\mathfrak{g}
is defined as follows:
S:=S_{1}+S_{2},
where
S_{1}
is a
sum of simple ideals of
\mathcal{A}
and
S_{2}
is a sum of simple

\mathcal{A}
-submodules of
Leib(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proposition 5.5

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Leibniz algebra
satisfying the descending chain condition. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is a direct
sum of a finite direct sum of simple ideals of
\mathcal{A}
and a direct
sum of simple

\mathcal{A}
-submodule of
Leib(\mathfrak{g}),
i.e. there exists
\{S_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
a finite set of simple ideals of
\mathcal{A}
such that
\mathfrak{g}=(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{i=n}S_{i})\oplus S_{2}

with
S_{2}=Leib(\mathfrak{g})
direct sum of simple
\mathcal{A}
-submodules of

.

Proof.
Since

]

Using the notions of prime ideal and prime algebra defined in
[
11
]
, we
investigate below the structure of Leibniz algebras with a certain
chain condition(CC) on subideals.

Definition 5.6

(i)

A Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be prime if
[h_{1},h_{2}]\subseteq Leib(\mathfrak{g})
for
h_{1},h_{2}
ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
h_{1}\subseteq Leib(\mathfrak{g})
or
h_{2}\subseteq Leib(\mathfrak{g})
.

(ii)

A proper ideal
K
of
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be prime if
[h_{1},h_{2}]\subseteq K
for
h_{1},h_{2}
ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
h_{1}\subseteq K
or
h_{2}\subseteq K
.

(iii)

A Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be semiprime if
[h,h]\subseteq Leib(\mathfrak{g})
for an ideal
h
of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
h\subseteq Leib(\mathfrak{g})
.

(iv)

A proper ideal
I
of
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be semiprime if
[h,h]\subseteq I
for an ideal
h
of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
h\subseteq I
.

(v)

An ideal
J
of
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be maximal if
J\subseteq I\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
for some ideal I of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
J=I
or
I=\mathfrak{g}
.

(vi)

Let
P
and
I
be two ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
P
is prime. P is said to be a minimal prime ideal belonging
to I, if
P\supset I
and there is no prime ideal
P_{1}
of
\mathfrak{g}

such that
I\subset P_{1}\subsetneq P.

(vii)

A prime radical of an ideal
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, denoted
Rad_{P}(\mathcal{H}),
is the intersection of all minimal prime
ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
belonging to
\mathcal{H}
. In particular the prime
radical of the zero ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
will be denoted
Rad_{P}(0):=Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Remark 5.7

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Leibniz algebra. One has the following assertions:

(i)

Rad_{P}(\mathcal{H})
is the intersection of all prime ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathcal{H}
(see
[
9
]
, Theorem 2.1.5).

(ii)

For any two ideals
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}
of
\mathfrak{g},

Rad_{P}(\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{K})=Rad_{P}(\mathcal{H})\cap Rad_{P}(\mathcal{K})
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{K}\subseteq Rad_{P}(\mathcal{H}),
then
Rad_{P}(\mathcal{H})=Rad_{P}(\mathcal{K}).

(iii)

For any ideal
\mathcal{H},
of

\mathfrak{g},

Rad_{P}(Rad_{P}(\mathcal{H})=Rad_{P}(\mathcal{H}).

(iv)

Rad_{P}(\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g})})=(0),
the zero
ideal of
\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g})}.

Proposition 5.8

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Leibniz algebra satisfying the maximal chain
condition on ideals. For any ideal
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathfrak{g},
there exists
only a finite number of prime ideals of
\mathfrak{g},

\{P_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\},
such that
Rad_{P}(\mathcal{H})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{i=m}P_{i}.

Proof.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Leibniz algebra satisfying the maximal chain
condition on ideals and
\mathcal{H}
be an arbitrary ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. If

\mathcal{H}
is a prime ideal, the result follows.

Assume that
\mathcal{H}

is not a prime ideal and that there is an infinite number of minimal
prime ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathcal{H}.
Since
\mathcal{H}
is non
prime, there exists two ideals
I,J
of
\mathfrak{g}
, such that
[I,J]\subseteq\mathcal{H}

with
I\nsubseteq\mathcal{H}
and
J\nsubseteq\mathcal{H}.
Observe that
[I+\mathcal{H},J+\mathcal{H}]\subseteq\mathcal{H}
and that neither
I+\mathcal{H}
nor
J+\mathcal{H}
is prime. Thus there is an infinite number of minimal prime
ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
belonging at least to one of the following ideals

I+\mathcal{H}
,
J+\mathcal{H}.
Assume that there is an infinite number of
minimal prime ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
belonging to
I+\mathcal{H},
by using
the same arguments we obtain an ideal
\mathcal{K}
such that
\mathcal{H}\subsetneq I+\mathcal{H}\subsetneq\mathcal{K}+I+\mathcal{H}.
So progressing
in the similar way, we show that there is an ascendant chain of
ideals,
\mathcal{H}\subsetneq I+\mathcal{H}\subsetneq\mathcal{K}+I+\mathcal{H}\subsetneq\cdots,
which does not terminates. This contradicts the
fact that
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the maximal chain condition.

Proposition 5.9

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple Leibniz algebra satisfying the maximal
condition on ideals, then
Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g})=\mbox{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Leibniz algebra satisfying the maximal
condition on ideals and consider
\mathfrak{I},
a solvable ideal of

\mathfrak{g}
with solvability index
n.
We have
\mathfrak{I}^{(n)}=0
and
therefore
\mathfrak{I}\subseteq P
for any
P,
minimal prime ideal of

\mathfrak{g}
. Hence the solvable Radical,
Rad(\mathfrak{g})
,
is an ideal of
Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g}).

Conversely assume that
Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g})
is not solvable and consider

\mathfrak{M}=\{\mathcal{K}\lhd\mathfrak{g}:Rad_{P}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{g})\nsubseteq\mathcal{K},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

\mathfrak{M}\neq\emptyset
since the zero ideal belong to

\mathfrak{M}.
Moreover
\mathfrak{M}
has a maximal element since

\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the maximal condition on ideals. Let
Q
be such
element and assume that
Q
is not prime , then there exists
A,B

two ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
[A,B]\subseteq Q
with

A\nsubseteq Q
and
B\nsubseteq Q.
But
Q+A\supseteq Q,

Q+B\supseteq Q
and
Q
maximal implies that there exists
r
and

s
such that
Rad_{P}^{(r)}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq Q+A
and

Rad_{P}^{(s)}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq Q+B.
Set
u=\mbox{max}\{r,s\}
,
then we have
Rad_{P}^{(u+1)}(\mathfrak{g})=[Rad_{P}^{(u)}(\mathfrak{g}),Rad_{P}^{(u)}(\mathfrak{g})]\subseteq[Q+A,Q+B]\subseteq Q.
This
contradicts the fact that
Q\in\mathfrak{M}.
Hence
Q
is prime
and
Rad_{P}^{(n)}(\mathfrak{g})\nsubseteq Q,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

implies that
Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g})\nsubseteq Q
which contradicts the
definition of
Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g}).
So
Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq Rad(\mathfrak{g}).

Finally since
\mathfrak{g}
is semi-simple we have
Leib(\mathfrak{g})=Rad(\mathfrak{g})=Rad_{P}(\mathfrak{g}).
