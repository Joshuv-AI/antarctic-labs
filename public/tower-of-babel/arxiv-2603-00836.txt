On a conjecture due to Kanade related to Nahm sums Journal: Indagationes Mathematicae
Cetin Hakimoglu-Brown

Abstract
Kanade explored the construction of modular companions to q q -series identities, using the asymptotics of Nahm sums, and Mizuno [Ramanujan J. 66 (2025), Paper No. 62, 31] recently obtained a generalization of Kanade’s asymptotic formula for symmetrizable Nahm sums. A related conjecture from Kanade concerning the dilogarithm function and related to the work of Kurşungöz on Andrews–Gordon-type series [Ann. Comb. 23 (2019), 835–888] has remained open. In this paper, we prove Kanade’s conjecture, through an application of dilogarithm identities due to Kirillov together with a dilogarithm ladder due to Lewin and Loxton. Inspired by Kanade’s result, we extend this to conjecture two new dilogarithm identities and associated rank-2 matrices.

Rogers’ five-term identity is such that

\operatorname{L}(x)+\operatorname{L}(y)=\operatorname{L}(xy)+\operatorname{L}\left({\frac{x(1-y)}{1-xy}}\right)+\operatorname{L}\left({\frac{y(1-x)}{1-xy}}\right).

There are a finite number of single-term identities, those being the classical results of Euler and Landen, found in introductory texts:

\operatorname{L}(1)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6},\ \operatorname{L}(-1)=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12},\ \operatorname{L}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{12},\\
\operatorname{L}\left(\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}\right)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{10},\ \operatorname{L}\left(\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{15}.

A notable two-term
\operatorname{Li}_{2}
-identity given by
15

u_{0}=\sqrt{2}-1
and

. The respective base equations

, the latter of which we explore for this paper. Bytsko alludes to the problem of

Higher-degree (e.g., quartic), two-term identities of type

The difficulty of finding two-term identities
subject to
all
the above criteria has to do with the difficulty of ensuring that the linear relations between the elements cancel
or combine in such a way as to leave only two terms. Bytsko’s list can be proven analytically, without asymptotic analysis, because all
his identities are ‘reachable’ within 1-3 iterations of Roger’s 5-term identity (under suitable substitutions), and then imposing
one of the elementary identities (e.g., Euler or Landen) or other substitutions. Hence, we are left with only two terms, those being
two-term identities with

as to allow non-unit
a_{1}
subject to the two-term restriction.

not evident how one would go about trying to narrow down the search space when trying many possible base equations. As an exception, Lewin

Because

does not appear to be any others of the general base equation

The conjecture due to
8

for

Proof.

Combining dilogarithm relations given by
10
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{L}(a^{2})

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{L}(y^{2})+2\operatorname{L}(y)-2\operatorname{L}(x^{2}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{L}(c^{2})

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{L}(y)+\operatorname{L}(z^{2})-2\operatorname{L}(y^{2}).

Using substituion, we then obtain

\displaystyle-\operatorname{L}(x^{3})+3\operatorname{L}(x^{2})+\operatorname{L}(x)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{L}(z^{2})+\operatorname{L}(y^{2}),

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{L}(a)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{L}(y^{2})+2\operatorname{L}(y),

\displaystyle-\operatorname{L}(z^{2})+2\operatorname{L}(z)

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{L}(h),

\displaystyle\operatorname{L}(y)+\operatorname{L}(a)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{L}(h)+\operatorname{L}(x)+\operatorname{L}(b).

Employing the 3-term Lewin–Loxton identity, we have

\operatorname{L}(x^{3})=3\operatorname{L}(x)+3\operatorname{L}(x^{2})

Here we have:

y=\frac{1}{1+x},\ z=\frac{x}{1+x},\ b=\frac{x^{2}}{x+1},\ h=\frac{x}{1+2x},\\
a=\frac{1}{x+2},\ c=\frac{1}{x(x+2)},\ x=\frac{1}{2}\sec\!\left(\frac{\pi}{9}\right).

An application of row elimination then gives us

3\operatorname{L}(y)=\operatorname{L}(b)
.
∎

Concluding remarks
