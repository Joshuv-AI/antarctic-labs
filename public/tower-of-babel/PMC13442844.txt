Dual Photoredox/Cobalt Catalysis Enabled Regiospecific Markovnikov Hydroboration of Unactivated Mono-, Di-, and Trisubstituted Alkenes
Authors: Xin-Yu Xu, Hong-Chao Liu, Yiliang Chen, Tao Zhang, Heng Cai, Qi Wang, Li Rao, Siyuan Tang, Yong-Min Liang, Xinya Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hydroboration of alkenes constitutes an important synthetic tool to assemble valuable alkylboron molecules. Although various protocols have been developed, the regiospecific Markovnikov reactions of unactivated alkenes, in particular for trisubstituted ones, remain formidable challenges. Here, we report a general method for the Markovnikov hydroboration of unactivated mono-, di- and trisubstituted alkenes enabled by dual photoredox/cobalt catalysis. The reaction proceeds via a photoinduced metal-hydride hydrogen atom transfer of unactivated alkenes followed by a versatile radical borylation process. This strategy is characterized by its Markovnikov selectivity, mild reaction conditions, high catalytic efficiency and broad substrate scope. This method demonstrates a reaction pattern that achieves the hydroboration of unactivated alkenes through the combination of reductive metal-hydride hydrogen atom transfer and radical borylation. Moreover, this catalytic system can also be applied to complex substrates derived from natural products. The mechanism presented is supported by control experiments and density functional theory calculations.

Introduction

Alkylboron compounds are not only recognized as one of the most versatile building blocks and important organic molecules in chemical reactions for their high reactivity and low toxicity1,2, but also prevalent structures in pharmaceuticals, biologically active molecules and advanced functional materials3,4. Therefore, the synthetic methods for preparing alkylboron compounds are of great importance and there is a growing demand for the development of efficient and practical methods to prepare these valuable compounds5–11. Among the methods that have been developed for the construction of alkylboron compounds, the hydroboration of alkenes is a powerful method owing to its atom economy, high cost-effectiveness and ready availability of alkenes12–14. Though the hydroboration of styrenes providing Markovnikov and anti-Markovnikov products has been well established15–19, the regioselective hydroboration of unactivated alkenes remains more challenging. Reported approaches for such a process were mainly limited to anti-Markovnikov regioselectivity20–22, reflecting the inherent reactivity of the traditional hydroboration of unactivated alkenes (Fig. 1a). To date, only a limited number of Markovnikov-selective hydroboration reactions of unactivated alkenes have been developed23–26, including outstanding work in enantioselectivity variants enabled by Rh-catalysts27 and Cu-catalysts23,24. However, these methods are largely limited to simple terminal alkenes, with relatively modest functional-group and substitutional diversity. Furthermore, the corresponding transformation of sterically more hindered and less reactive trisubstituted alkenes remains unexplored. Thus, it is of great significance to develop a general protocol for achieving such transformations, providing powerful methods to make structurally complex and valuable organoborons.Fig. 1Background of current work and reaction design.a Inherent anti-Markovnikov regioselectivity of alkenes hydroboration. b Limitations of reductive MHAT strategy for hydrofunctionalization of unactivated alkenes. c A general protocol for Markovnikov hydroboration of unactivated mono-, di-, and trisubstituted alkenes.

The metal-hydride hydrogen atom transfer (MHAT) strategy has been proven to be an effective approach for the hydrofunctionalization of unsaturated bonds, owing to its outstanding functional group tolerance, chemoselectivity, and regioselectivity28–37. In particular, the reductive MHAT strategy generates the key cobalt-hydride species (Co(III)-H) through the transfer of a proton and an electron with photocatalysts38–48, providing a more sustainable and milder alternative to traditional MHAT methods relying on stoichiometric oxidants and reductants49–52. What’s more, the reductive MHAT strategy is a highly selective and predictable technique that, via transfer of a H-atom to an unactivated olefin and consequent generation of the most electronically stabilized radical species, ultimately forms Markovnikov-selective hydrofunctionalization products. To date, excellent work has been done on reductive MHAT of unactivated alkenes in both radical trapping47,48 and radical-polar crossover reactions40–44,46, demonstrating its utility as a powerful strategy for the hydrofunctionalization of alkenes. However, the reductive MHAT strategy without additional stoichiometric reductants faces the limitation that the generated alkyl radicals or cations in reductive MHAT reactions have so far primarily been captured by nucleophilic reagents (e.g., halide ions, alkoxide ions, etc.) (Fig. 1b)40–44,46. This seriously limits the development of reductive MHAT reactions. Hence, it is essential to develop reaction patterns for reductive MHAT to diversify its use in organic synthesis.

We established our working hypothesis that a neutral diboron substrate could serve as the functional reagent to capture the active alkyl radicals generated from unactivated alkenes via a reductive MHAT process. This pathway could provide a general approach for the Markovnikov hydroboration of aliphatic alkenes, while overcoming the inherent reactivity of unactivated alkenes participating in reductive MHAT that mainly employs nucleophilic reagents as functional reagents.

Here, we show a photoinduced cobalt-catalyzed Markovnikov hydroboration of unactivated mono-, di- and trisubstituted alkenes using electroneutral bis(catecholato)diboron (B2cat2) as borylation reagent via the combination of reductive MHAT reactions and radical borylation process (Fig. 1c). This strategy is characterized by its mild reaction conditions, high catalytic efficiency, Markovnikov selectivity and broad substrate scope. What’s more, this method expands the synthetic toolbox for the hydrofunctionalization of unactivated alkenes via reductive MHAT.

Results

Optimization studies

We commenced our study by using trisubstituted aliphatic alkene 17a and B2cat2 as model substrates to investigate this reaction (Table 1). When this reaction was performed in the presence of 4CzIPN (1 mol%), Co-1 (1 mol%) and collidinium triflate (HX-I, 1.0 equiv.) in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) (2 mL) under blue LED irradiation at ambient temperature, the desired Markovnikov hydroboration product 17 was obtained in 66% isolated yield after 48 h (Table 1, entry 1). Note that due to the instability of the catechol boronic ester, the crude product was transesterified with pinacol and Et3N to give the alkyl boronic ester 17. Further investigation disclosed that the DMF was the pivotal solvent for accomplishing this reaction, as other solvents abolished reactivity (entry 2). Upon testing other cobalt-MHAT catalysts, we found that Co(TPP) afforded a 54% yield of the desired product whereas other cobalt-MHAT catalysts failed to trigger the reaction (entries 3, 4). Photocatalysts were also studied and the results demonstrated that though using Ir[dF(CF3)ppy]2(dtbbpy)PF6 as photocatalyst provided a 67% yield, 4CzIPN was the best one in view of the yield and the price of the photocatalysts (entries 1, 5, 6). To our delight, when 4CzIPN (0.5 mol%) and Co-1 (0.5 mol%) were employed as the catalytic system, the desired product was obtained in 69% yield (entry 7). We also found that the reaction conversion was nearly complete in 15 h, with the desired product obtained in 69% yield (entry 8). It’s worth noting that no desired product was obtained when using other acids as the hydrogen source (entry 9). The reaction did not proceed when other boron reagents (such as B2pin2 or B2(OH)4) were used as the boron source (entry 10). Control experiments indicated that the reaction could not yield the desired product in the absence of photocatalyst, cobalt-catalyst, acids, or light (entry 11). Substrate scope studies

With the optimized reaction conditions established, the scope of unactivated trisubstituted alkenes was first explored. As shown in Fig. 2, the challenging trisubstituted alkenes bearing various functional groups participated in the hydroboration reaction smoothly and delivered the desired products with quaternary carbons in moderate to good yields with Markovnikov selectivity. For example, ethers (1-8, 65%-89%), halides (6, 70%; 15, 77%), boron ester (4, 65%), nitrile (7, 67%), esters (9–12, 58%–79%; 17, 69%), sulfonamide (13, 80%), silicon ether (14, 81%), and amide (19, 82%) all proved to be compatible with the reaction conditions. Importantly, aryl rings with halogen substituents (2, 89%; 3, 83%), including F and Cl, were well tolerated under the standard reaction conditions, facilitating further modifications at the halogenated positions. Interestingly, the ketone survived well during the reaction, and the product 16 was isolated in 36% yield, thus highlighting the mild conditions of this reaction. To our delight, the trisubstituted alkenes with more steric bulk could also undergo the hydroboration reaction and generated the desired products in good yield with Markovnikov selectivity (18, 87%; 19, 82%). Pharmaceutically important heterocycles, such as indole, were also compatible and provided the desired product in 58% yield (11). Substrates derived from complex natural products were converted into the desired products with quaternary carbons smoothly (20–23, 61%-82%). What’s more, this reaction was also applicable to gram-scale preparation. The scope of unactivated monosubstituted alkenes was then investigated. To our delight, we found that a variety of monosubstituted alkenes underwent hydroboration reaction smoothly leading to the branched alkylboron compounds in moderate to satisfactory yields with excellent regioselectivity using 4CzIPN (0.25 mol%) and Co-1 (0.25 mol%) as the catalyst (Fig. 3A). Exclusive Markovnikov selectivity was observed in all cases. A wide range of functional groups was well tolerated in this reaction. Ethers (24-30, 64%-87%), halides (25, 75%; 26, 87%; 32, 82%; 35, 72%; 47, 59%), boron ester (27, 73%), nitrile (30, 68%), esters (31-38, 72%-89%; 45, 65%), siloxane (39, 61%), aryls (40, 85%; 46, 75%) and silanes (41, 61%) all proved to be compatible with the reaction, and gave the target products in moderate to good yields. Notably, simple olefins bearing either short or long alkyl chains were viable in this transformation, and the length of the chain had a limited impact on the efficiency of this reaction (42–44, 74%–89%). The disubstituted alkenes could also undergo the Markovnikov hydroboration reaction smoothly using 4CzIPN (0.25 mol%) and Co-1 (0.25 mol%) as the catalysts, and gave the Markovnikov hydroboration products in moderate to good yields (Fig. 3B). The 1,1-disubstituted open-chain olefins with a methyl group were transformed to the corresponding tertiary alkyl borates in moderate to excellent yields (48–57, 54%–86%). Methylene-substituted cycloalkanes underwent the Markovnikov hydroboration reaction smoothly, and gave the desired products in moderate to good yields, regardless of the size of the ring (58–60, 61%–81%). It is worth noting that this reaction could distinguish between a terminal alkene and an internal alkene. When the substrate contained both terminal alkene and internal alkene, only the terminal alkene participated in the Markovnikov hydroboration reaction; the retention of internal alkenes enabled the product to be further transformed (61, 54%). To our delight, 1,2-disubstituted alkene was also compatible under the reaction conditions, as exemplified by the synthesis of 62 in 74% yield with high d.r. value from the corresponding norbornene. Mechanistic studies

To gain more mechanistic information of this transformation, several mechanism investigation experiments were performed. Ring closure experiment was conducted using diethyl 2,2-diallylmalonate 69a as substrate, and the corresponding ring closure product 69 was obtained in 88% yield (Fig. 5a). Furthermore, the hydroboration reaction was completely inhibited when a stoichiometric amount of radical scavenger (TEMPO) was added under the standard conditions (Fig. 5b). Then, radical clock experiments were conducted. Using cyclopropane derivatives 70a and 71a as substrates afforded the corresponding ring-opening hydroboration products in good yields, respectively (70, 88%; 71, 80%) (Fig. 5c). All these mechanistic studies demonstrated that this reaction may proceed via a radical pathway. Stern-Volmer fluorescence quenching experiments were also conducted, and the results showed that both Co-1 and B2cat2 could quench the excited state of 4CzIPN, whereas alkene 5a and HX-I showed only a slight quenching ability under an excitation wavelength of 440 nm (Fig. 5d). The detailed UV-Vis absorption experiments and Stern-Volmer fluorescence quenching experiments are provided in S1-S5 and S6-S9 of the Supporting Information, respectively. Although the quenching rate constant of Co-1 is larger than that of B2cat2 (about 2 times), the concentration of B2cat2 in the reaction system is 600 or 1200 times that of Co-1. Therefore, we believe that it is B2cat2 that quenches the excited-state photocatalysts in the reaction.Fig. 5Mechanism study experiments.a Ring closure experiment. b Influence of radical scavenger. c Radical clock experiment. d Stern-Volmer fluorescence quenching experiments.

Based on the mechanistic study experiments and previous reports5,6,10,41,44,46,59–66, a plausible mechanism for this transformation was proposed as depicted in Fig. 6a. The reaction commences by single electron transfer (SET) from the DMF-ligated B2cat2 to photoexcited 4CzIPN*, generating the corresponding radical anion 4CzIPN.- and radical cation A. Cleavage of the B-B bond in A generates cation B and DMF-complexed radical C. Meanwhile, SET from radical anion 4CzIPN.- to Co(II) (D) generates the corresponding Co(I) (E) and regenerates 4CzIPN. The Co(III)-H species (F) is formed through protonation of Co(I) (E) with the 2,4,6-collidine·HOTf, and the Co(III)-H species (F) undergoes Markovnikov MHAT with unactivated alkenes, forming the Co(II) (D) and an alkyl radical (G). G then adds to B2cat2 to give adduct radical H, which is trapped by the solvent (DMF) to give intermediate I. The weak B-B one-electron σ-bond readily homolyzes to give the alkyl catechol boronic ester J along with K. The product J can also be generated from the radical-radical cross-coupling of DMF-complexed radical C and radical G. Radical K is oxidized by the 4CzIPN* to give L and radical anion 4CzIPN.-. Finally, J is converted to M by reacting with pinacol and triethylamine.Fig. 6A plausible mechanism.a Proposed mechanism.

To verify our proposed mechanism and further gain a deep understanding, DFT calculations of the intermediates and transition states were carried out (Fig. 7a). Computational details can be found in the Supporting Information, and the Cartesian coordinates are provided in the Source Data. Owing to the limitations of computational cost, the four tert-butyl groups on the benzene ring of the Co-1 catalyst were replaced with four methyl groups as a reasonable approximation. The reaction begins with N accepting an electron from the 4CzIPN anion. This step is predicted to be 15.2 kcal/mol endothermic and is likely accelerated under light irradiation. The negatively charged intermediate O accepts a proton from TfOH to form P, which passes through a transition state TS-P-Q to transfer the hydrogen atom to the alkene and leads to the formation of radical Q and the restoration of N. Q further reacts with B2cat2 and DMF to form product R and intermediate K, which releases an electron to 4CzIPN and bonds with the TfO- anion to form L. The rate-determining step barrier height from P to Q is predicted to be 25 kcal/mol, in accord with the ambient temperature conditions of the experiment. The reaction pathway involving DMF-ligated B2cat2 reducing photoexcited 4CzIPN*, as proposed in the reported literature, was also investigated. However, it is found to be less favorable compared to the pathway proposed in this work, suggesting that this process serves primarily as a trigger for the reaction. Discussion

In conclusion, we have developed a dual photoredox/cobalt catalytic strategy for the formal Markovnikov hydroboration of unactivated mono-, di-, and trisubstituted alkenes to construct alkylboron compounds. This strategy delivers alkylboron compounds under mild reaction conditions, without the addition of stoichiometric reductants or oxidants, and features high catalytic efficiency, Markovnikov selectivity, and a broad substrate scope. This modular protocol proceeds via photoinduced reductive MHAT of unactivated alkenes and a radical borylation process, overcoming the limitation that photoinduced reductive MHAT without additional stoichiometric reductants in hydrofunctionalization reactions of unactivated alkenes mainly employs nucleophiles as the functional reagents, and thus expands the synthetic toolbox for alkene hydrofunctionalization via reductive MHAT.

Methods

General procedure for the Markovnikov hydroboration of unactivated mono- and disubstituted alkenes

In an oven-dried 15 mL tube equipped with a stirring bar, unactivated terminal alkenes (0.8 mmol, 1.0 equiv.), B2cat2 (2.4 mmol, 3.0 equiv.), collidinium triflate (HX-I) (0.8 mmol, 1.0 equiv.), 4CzIPN (0.002 mmol, 0.25 mol%), and Co-1 (0.002 mmol, 0.25 mol%) were added. The tube was charged with nitrogen (repeated three times), then DMF (4 mL) was injected. The resulting suspension was stirred at room temperature and irradiated with blue LEDs (440 nm, 10 W) for 15 h. Then pinacol (4.0 mmol, 5.0 equiv.) and Et3N (5.6 mL) were added, and stirring was continued for 1 h. After completion, the resulting mixture was quenched with H2O (30 mL). Then the mixture was extracted with ethyl acetate (30 mL). The organic layer was washed with brine (30 mL × 2), dried over anhydrous Na2SO4, and concentrated in vacuum. The residue was purified by flash chromatography on silica gel using petroleum ether/ethyl acetate as the eluent to afford the desired products.

General procedure for the Markovnikov hydroboration of unactivated trisubstituted alkenes

In an oven-dried 15 mL tube equipped with a stirring bar, unactivated trisubstituted alkenes (0.4 mmol, 1.0 equiv.), B2cat2 (1.2 mmol, 3.0 equiv.), collidinium triflate (HX-I) (0.4 mmol, 1.0 equiv.), 4CzIPN (0.002 mmol, 0.5 mol%), and Co-1 (0.002 mmol, 0.5 mol%) were added. The tube was charged with nitrogen (repeated three times), then DMF (2 mL) was injected. The resulting suspension was stirred at room temperature and irradiated with blue LEDs (440 nm, 10 W) for 15 h. Then pinacol (2.0 mmol, 5.0 equiv.) and Et3N (2.8 mL) were added, and stirring was continued for 1 h. After completion, the resulting mixture was quenched with H2O (30 mL). Then the mixture was extracted with ethyl acetate (30 mL). The organic layer was washed with brine (30 mL × 2), dried over anhydrous Na2SO4, and concentrated under vacuum. The residue was purified by flash chromatography on silica gel using petroleum ether/ethyl acetate as the eluent, affording the desired products.
