It was observed by van Lint and Seidel

Definition 1.2

(Switching equivalence)
.

Given a graph
G
and a subset
U\subseteq V(G)
, the

of
G
with respect to
U
, denoted
G^{U}
, is the graph obtained from
G
by toggling adjacency between every vertex in
U
and every vertex in
V(G)\setminus U
. Two graphs
G
and
H
are
switching equivalent
if there exists
U\subseteq V(G)
such that
H
is isomorphic to
G^{U}
.

To describe most of the graphs with smallest Seidel eigenvalue at least
-1/\alpha^{*}
, we introduce the following notion. All subgraphs are induced throughout this paper.

Definition 1.3

(Switching closure)
.

Given a family
\mathcal{G}
of graphs, the
switching closure
, denoted
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle_{\pm}
, is the smallest class of graphs containing every member of

3.1
Hash function of graphs and Seidel degrees

A common routine in the above algorithm is to decide whether a graph

Suppose that we need to check whether a graph

is switching equivalent to any existing member of
set
. To efficiently detect switching equivalence, we maintain a hash table

of existing members of
set
using the following hash function.

Definition 3.6
.

Given a graph

k\in\mathbb{N}
, the

-th Seidel degree of

in
G
is the
(v,v)
-entry of the
k

Proposition 3.7
.

For every graph
G

U
of
G
, every vertex
v
of
G

-th Seidel degree of

Proof.

Define

to be the diagonal matrix on

by
D_{v,v}=1
if

and
D_{v,v}=-1
otherwise. The Seidel matrices of

and

are related by

. Finally, since

, whose diagonal entries agree with those in

∎

For a graph

This allows us to test whether

4.2
Positive semidefiniteness and rank

Testing whether
S(G)+I/\alpha^{*}\succeq 0
amounts to verifying that all the roots of
p_{G}(x-1/\alpha^{*})
are non-negative, where
p_{G}
is the characteristic polynomial of
S(G)
. We recall the following criterion, which is a special case of Descartes’ rule of signs: for
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, they are non-negative if and only if their elementary symmetric functions are all non-negative. Suppose that

p_{G}\left(x-1/\alpha^{*}\right)=c_{n}x^{n}+\dots+c_{0}.

We can conclude that
S(G)+I/\alpha^{*}\succeq 0
if and only if
(-1)^{n-k}c_{k}\geq 0
for every
k\in\left\{{0,\dots,n}\right\}
.

To compute the characteristic polynomial
p_{G}
of
S(G)
, we use the Faddeev–LeVerrier algorithm (see
[
7
, Chapter IV Section 5]
), throughout which only integers are involved. Computing the coefficients of
p_{G}(x-1/\alpha^{*})
can be done entirely within the integral domain
\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]
.

As a byproduct, the rank of
S(G)+I/\alpha^{*}
can be computed as
n-m
, where
m
is the multiplicity of
0
as a root of
p_{G}(x-1/\alpha^{*})
, that is,
m
is the index such that
c_{m}\neq 0
but
c_{i}=0
for all
i<m
.

Remark
.

One caveat in the implementation comes from the possibility that the integers involved in the Faddeev–LeVerrier algorithm as well as those in the coefficients of
p_{G}(x)
and
p_{G}(x-1/\alpha^{*})
can be quite large theoretically. Fortunately, in practice, the integers involved in the computation never exceed
3.42\times 10^{21}
in absolute value, and so signed 128-bit integers are sufficient for the computation mentioned in this subsection.

5.2
Finiteness of exceptional graphs

For general
\alpha\in(0,1)
, set
\lambda=(1-\alpha)/(2\alpha)
, and let
\mathcal{G}(\lambda)
be the set of connected graphs with spectral radius at most
\lambda
. A general construction of graphs
G
with smallest Seidel eigenvalue at least
-1/\alpha
is to take the vertex-disjoint union of graphs from
\mathcal{G}(\lambda)
. Indeed, such a graph
G
satisfies
\lambda I-A(G)\succeq 0
, which implies that
S(G)+I/\alpha=J+2(\lambda I-A(G))\succeq 0
. Taking the switching closure yields the family
\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda)\rangle_{\pm}
whose members all have smallest Seidel eigenvalue at least
-1/\alpha
. This leads to the following qualitative question.

Problem 5.3
.

For which
\alpha\in(0,1)
, are there only finitely many graphs outside
\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda)\rangle_{\pm}
with smallest Seidel eigenvalue at least
-1/\alpha
, where
\lambda=(1-\alpha)/(2\alpha)
?

We note that the answer to the above problem is negative when
\alpha=1/(4\sqrt{u^{2}+1}-1)
for any integer
u\geq 10^{5}
. In this case,
\lambda=2\sqrt{u^{2}+1}-1
is not the spectral radius of any graph, and so for any graph
G\in\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda)\rangle_{\pm}
on
n
vertices, the matrix
S(G)+I/\alpha
is positive definite, and so its rank is
n
. If there were only finitely many graphs outside
\langle\mathcal{G}(\lambda)\rangle_{\pm}
with smallest Seidel eigenvalue at least
-1/\alpha
, then we would conclude, by an argument similar to that in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, that
N_{\alpha}(d)=d
for sufficiently large
d
. However, Schildkraut
[
18
, Theorem 1.4]
proved that
N_{\alpha}(d)\geq d+\Omega(\log\log d)
.
