On the structure of fine Mordell-Weil groups over a ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension and its intermediate subextensions
Meng Fai Lim

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the structure of the fine Mordell-Weil groups over the intermediate subextensions of a given ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension F ∞ F_{\infty} of F F . Keywords and Phrases: Fine Mordell-Weil groups, ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 11G05, 11G35, 11R23.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we always let
p
denote an odd prime. Let
F
be a number field and
A
an abelian variety over
F
. The (
p
-primary) fine Selmer
group of
A
is a much studied object in Iwasawa theory (for instances, see

[
1
,
8
,
9
,
17
]
) and plays a central role in both the statement and proof of the Iwasawa main conjecture (see
[
4
,
5
,
13
]
). Analogous to the usual Selmer group, the fine Selmer group
R(A/F)
fits into the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(A/F)\longrightarrow R(A/F)\longrightarrow\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{n})\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{M}(A/F)
and
\mbox{Ж}(A/F)
denote the fine Mordell-Weil group and fine Tate-Shafarevich
group, respectively, as defined by Wuthrich
[
18
]
. These groups can be thought as the “fine”
counterpart of the usual Mordell-Weil group and Tate-Shafarevich group. In recent years, these fine objects have garnered significant research attention, as evidenced in the works
[
3
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
14
]
.

The goal of the paper is study the structure of the fine Mordell-Weil groups over the intermediate subextensions of a given
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}
of
F
. For each
n
, we let
F_{n}
be the unique subextension of
F_{\infty}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. The fine Mordell-Weil group
\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})
has a natural
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gal(F_{n}/F)]
-module structure. Denote by
\Gamma
the Galois group
\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
and write
\Lambda
for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
. Via the canonical surjection
\Lambda\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gal(F_{n}/F)]
, we may view
\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})
as a
\Lambda
-module structure. Recall that a homomorphism
M\longrightarrow N
of finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-modules is said to be a pseudo-isomorphism if its kernel and cokernel are finite. We denote this relation by
M\sim N
. Finally, let
\Phi_{j}
denote the
p^{j}
-th cyclotomic polynomial. We are now in a position to state our main result.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
A
be a
g
-dimensional abelian variety defined over a number field
F
and
F_{\infty}/F
a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Suppose further that every prime of
F
above
p
does not split completely in
F_{\infty}/F
. Then there exists a sequence of integers
\{s_{n}\}
such that the following pseudo-isomorphism holds:

\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}.

Furthermore, there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
, the integers
s_{n}
satisfy the following estimates

\max\{0,e_{n}-g|F:\mathbb{Q}|\}\leq s_{n}\leq\max\{0,e_{n}-1\},

where

e_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

We like to make a remark on the notation in the statement of the theorem. The
n
appearing the sequence
\{s_{n}\}
is consistent with the
n
in
F_{n}
, as in the
n
th-layer of the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}/F
, and how they fit into the asserted pseudo-isomorphism in the following form

\mathcal{M}(A/F_{0})^{\vee}\sim(\Lambda/\Phi_{0})^{\oplus s_{0}},\mathcal{M}(A/F_{1})^{\vee}\sim(\Lambda/\Phi_{0})^{\oplus s_{0}}\oplus(\Lambda/\Phi_{1})^{\oplus s_{1}}

and so on. In other words, roughly speaking,
\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n-1})^{\vee}
and
\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})^{\vee}
differs (up to pseudo-isomorphism) by a certain number of
\Lambda/\Phi_{n}
, and this number is given by
s_{n}
.

Results of such have previously been established for an elliptic curve over the cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
(see
[
7
]
) and certain
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of an imaginary quadratic field (see
[
10
]
). A key improvement over these earlier works, in addition to extending the setting to abelian varieties and more general
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions, is that our approach does not require the finiteness of the fine Tate-Shafarevich groups, a condition assumed in the aforementioned references.

A natural question is whether one can determine the precise values of
s_{n}
, rather than merely obtaining bounds. This turns out to be not easy; indeed, we can compute the exact values of

s_{n}
only for elliptic curves under certain conditions. We illustrate this by the next result.

Proposition 1.2

(Proposition
4.5
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
\mathbb{Q}
and
F
a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
such that
\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus m}
. Let
K_{i}
(
i=1,...,2^{m}-1
) be all the quadratic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
F
, and
E_{i}
the corresponding quadratic twist of
E
. We set
E_{0}=E
. Let
F_{n}
be the intermediate extension of
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. Then there exists a sequence of integers
\{s_{n}\}
such that the following pseudo-isomorphism

\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}.

holds for every
n
. Furthermore, the integers
s_{n}
satisfy the following equalities

s_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{2^{m}-1}\max\{0,e_{i,n}-1\},

where

e_{i,n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}(\mathbb{Q}_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}(\mathbb{Q}),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

For the proof, we will require to employ certain properties of Weil restriction (in the sense of Weil
[
15
]
) as developed in
[
12
]
. In the case when the elliptic curve has complex multiplication, we also have analogous results on obtaining precise values over certain non-cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions (see Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
).

It is then natural to ask whether our results can shed light on the structure of the fine Mordell-Weil group over a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. We address this question in the final section (see Section
6
), and this is the only place where the finiteness of fine Tate-Shafarevich groups will have to come into play in order for our results on intermediate subextensions to be able to transfer to the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.

We now give a brief overview of the paper. In Section
2
, we recall the definition of the fine Selmer groups, fine Mordell-Weil groups and fine Tate-Shafarevich groups.
Moving to Section
3
, we review relevant results on the variation of Mordell-Weil groups in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, with the discussion relying heavily on a result of Lee
[
6
]
. Section
4
is devoted to proving our main results (Theorem
4.1
) on the structure of the fine Mordell-Weil groups in the intermediate subextensions of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Subsequently, in Section
5
, we present our results on CM elliptic curves, where we can determine the precise structure of the fine Mordell-Weil groups. In Section
6
, which is the last section of the paper, we examine the implications of our result on the structure of the fine Mordell-Weil group over a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.

2
Fine objects over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension

In this section, we first review the definition of our fine objects. Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field
F
. Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
F
containing the primes above
p
, the bad reduction primes of
A
and the infinite primes. Denote by
F_{S}
the maximal algebraic extension of
F
that is unramified outside
S
. For every extension
\mathcal{L}
of
F
contained in
F_{S}
, we write
G_{S}(\mathcal{L})=\Gal(F_{S}/\mathcal{L})
, and let
S(\mathcal{L})
denote the set of primes of
\mathcal{L}
above
S
.

Let
L
be a finite extension of
F
contained in
F_{S}
. Following Coates, Sujatha and Wuthrich
[
1
,
17
]
, the fine Selmer group
R(A/L)
of
A
over
L
is defined by the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R(A/L)\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{S}(L),A[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S(L)}H^{1}(L_{v},A[p^{\infty}]).

Next, we introduce the fine Mordell-Weil group and fine Tate-Shafarevich group of Wuthrich
[
18
]
. To begin, the fine Mordell-Weil group
\mathcal{M}(A/L)
is defined by

\mathcal{M}(A/L)=\ker\Big(A(L)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S(L),v|p}A(L_{v})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\Big).

The fine Tate-Shafarevich group
\mbox{Ж}(A/L)
is then defined to be

\mbox{Ж}(A/L)=\mathrm{coker}\,\Big(\mathcal{M}(A/L)\longrightarrow R(A/L)\Big).

Consequently, we have the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(A/L)\longrightarrow R(A/L)\longrightarrow\mbox{Ж}(A/L)\longrightarrow 0,

(2.1)

where it can be shown that
\mbox{Ж}(A/L)
injects into
\sha(A/L)[p^{\infty}]
(see discussion in
[
18
, Section 2]
or
[
8
, Section 3]
).

For a given
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}
of
F
, we denote by
\Gamma
the Galois group of the extension
F_{\infty}/F
and let
\Lambda
denote the resulting Iwasawa algebra over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We in turn denote by
F_{n}
the fixed field of
\Gamma_{n}
, where
\Gamma_{n}
is the unique subgroup of
\Gamma
of index
p^{n}
. The fine Selmer group of
A
over
F_{\infty}
is then defined to be
R(A/F_{\infty})=\displaystyle\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{n}R(A/F_{n})
which comes naturally equipped with a
\Lambda
-module. The
\Lambda
-modules
\mathcal{M}(A/F_{\infty})
and
\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{\infty})
are defined via similar limiting processes.

3
Mordell-Weil growth in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of number fields and local fields

In this section, we recall certain results on the variation of Mordell-Weil groups in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}/F
. As before, we write
\Gamma:=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
. In what follows, all intermediate objects
A(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and their Pontryagin dual will be viewed as
\Gamma
-modules via the surjection
\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Gal(F_{n}/F)
without further mention. We begin with the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field
F
and
F_{\infty}/F
a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Define

e_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Then we have pseudo-isomorphism

\big(A(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus e_{j}}.

Proof.

For the verification of the proposition, we refer readers to
[
10
, Proposition 3.8]
. We emphasize that the proof rely heavily on the results of Lee
[
6
]
. Furthermore, it is important to highlight that the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group is not assumed in the statement or the proof of the proposition.
∎

For our subsequent discussion, we also require the following corresponding local result. The proof of which can be found in
[
10
, Proposition 3.9]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathcal{K}
.
Let
A
be a
g
-dimensional abelian variety defined over
\mathcal{K}
.
Then we have

\big(A(\mathcal{K}_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus g|\mathcal{K}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}|},

where
\mathcal{K}_{n}
is the subextension of
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}/\mathcal{K}
with
|\mathcal{K}_{n}:\mathcal{K}|=p^{n}
.

We now discuss a semi-local variant of the preceding result, which will be needed for the subsequent parts of the paper. Let
v
be a prime of
F
lying above
p
, and let
\Gamma_{v}
denote the decomposition group of
\Gamma:=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
at some fixed prime of
F_{\infty}
above
v
. We assume throughout that
|\Gamma:\Gamma_{v}|=p^{n_{v}}
with
n_{v}<\infty
. In other words, the prime
v
does not split completely in
F_{\infty}/F
. Fix a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
. We may then define
\gamma_{v}:=\gamma^{p^{n_{v}}}
, which is a topological generator of
\Gamma_{v}
. To distinguish between the two cases, we write
\Lambda(\Gamma)
(resp.,
\Lambda(\Gamma_{v})
) for the Iwasawa algebra of
\Gamma
(resp.,
\Gamma_{v}
). The cyclotomic polynomials over
\Lambda(\Gamma)
and
\Lambda(\Gamma_{v})
are then denoted by
\Phi_{j}(\gamma)
and
\Phi_{j}(\gamma_{v})
respectively. A straightforward verification shows that

\Phi_{j}(\gamma_{v})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi_{j+n_{v}}(\gamma),&\hbox{if $j\geq 1$;}\\
\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{n_{v}}\Phi_{k}(\gamma),&\hbox{if $j=0$.}\end{array}\right.

For
n>n_{v}
, we have

\Lambda(\Gamma)/\Phi_{n}(\gamma)=\Big(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p^{n_{v}}-1}\gamma^{i}\Lambda(\Gamma_{v})\Big)/\Phi_{n-n_{v}}(\gamma_{v})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p^{n_{v}}-1}\gamma^{i}\Big(\Lambda(\Gamma_{v})/\Phi_{n-n_{v}}(\gamma_{v})\Big)\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p^{n_{v}}-1}\Lambda(\Gamma_{v})/\Phi_{n-n_{v}}(\gamma_{v}).

When
n=n_{v}
, we have

\Lambda(\Gamma)/\prod_{k=0}^{n_{v}}\Phi_{k}(\gamma)=\Big(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p^{n_{v}}-1}\gamma^{i}\Lambda(\Gamma_{v})\Big)/\Phi_{0}(\gamma_{v})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p^{n_{v}}-1}\gamma^{i}\Big(\Lambda(\Gamma_{v})/\Phi_{0}(\gamma_{v})\Big)\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p^{n_{v}}-1}\Lambda(\Gamma_{v})/\Phi_{0}(\gamma_{v}).

Proposition 3.3
.

Under the notation as above, we have

\bigoplus_{w_{n}\mid v}\big(A(F_{n,w_{n}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda(\Gamma)/\Phi_{j}(\gamma))^{\oplus g|F_{n,v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}|}

for every
n\geq n_{v}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, one has

\bigoplus_{w_{n}\mid v}\big(A(F_{n,w_{n}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{w_{n}\mid v}\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n-n_{v}}(\Lambda(\Gamma_{v})/\Phi_{j}(\Gamma_{v}))^{\oplus g|F_{n,v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}|}.

By applying the formulas before this proposition, the latter can be seen to be isomorphic to

\left(\Lambda(\Gamma)/\prod_{k=0}^{n_{v}}\Phi_{k}(\gamma)\times\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n-n_{v}}\Lambda(\Gamma)/\Phi_{j+n_{v}}(\gamma)\right)^{\oplus g|F_{n,v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}|}.

The conclusion now follows.
∎

4
Main result

We can now in position to prove our main result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
be a
g
-dimensional abelian variety defined over a number field
F
and
F_{\infty}/F
a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Suppose further that every prime of
F
above
p
does not split completely in
F_{\infty}/F
. Then there exists a sequence of integers
\{s_{n}\}
such that the following pseudo-isomorphism holds:

\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}.

Furthermore, there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
, the integer
s_{n}
satisfies the following estimates

\max\{0,e_{n}-g|F:\mathbb{Q}|\}\leq s_{n}\leq\max\{0,e_{n}-1\},

where

e_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By definition, we have the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})\longrightarrow A(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\mid p}\bigoplus_{w_{n}\mid v}A(F_{n,w_{n}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

(4.1)

Taking Pontryagin dual, we obtain the exact sequence

\bigoplus_{v\mid p}\big(\bigoplus_{w_{n}\mid v}A(F_{n,w_{n}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\big(A(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0.

Taking Proposition
3.1
into account, the rightmost surjection implies that we have

\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}

for some integers
s_{j}
. For every prime
v
of
F
above
p
, we let
\Gamma_{v}
denote the decomposition group of
\Gamma:=\Gal(F_{\infty}/F)
at some fixed prime of
F_{\infty}
above
v
. We then set
n_{0}:=\max\{n_{v}\mid v|p\}
, where
p^{n_{v}}=|\Gamma:\Gamma_{v}|
.
Now, let
n
be an arbitrary integer
\geq n_{0}
. In view of Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
, by comparing the number of summands
\Lambda/\Phi_{n}
in the exact sequence, we obtain the lower bound of the theorem. For the upper bound, one simply note that if
e_{n}>0
, then a non-torsion point
P\in A(F_{n})-A(F_{n-1})
is sent to a non-torsion point in
A(F_{n,w})-A(F_{n-1,w})
for some
w
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

(a) The inspiration towards proving the preceding theorem comes from a result of Wuthrich
[
18
, Corollary 7.3]
, where he proves the following estimate

\max\{0,\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(F)-|F:\mathbb{Q}|\}\leq\corank_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathcal{M}(E/F)\leq\max\{0,\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E(F)-1\}

for an elliptic curve over a number field
F
. Our proof approach is essentially to consider an
\Lambda
-adic analogue of this result, with the underlying idea drawn from a work of Lei
[
7
]
.

(b) Results of such were established in
[
7
, Corollary 3.8]
and
[
10
]
(also see
[
3
]
for some results in this direction), but in a less general setting and under the additional assumption that the fine Tate-Shafarevich groups are finite.

(c) One might ask whether we can say anything if a prime above
p
is totally split in
F_{\infty}/F
. In this context, as can be seen from the proof, we still have the upper bound. However, we are not able to derive a lower bound, as this part of the proof relies on Proposition
3.3
which in turn requires that the prime does not split completely.

One can obtain a slightly better estimate than what was stated above. Namely, we have the following.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field
F
and
F_{\infty}/F
a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Assume that
A
is isogenous to a product of abelian varieties
\prod_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}
, where each
A_{i}
has dimension
g_{i}
. Suppose further that every prime of
F
above
p
does not split completely in
F_{\infty}/F
. Then there exists a sequence of integers
\{s_{n}\}
such that the following pseudo-isomorphism

\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}

holds for every
n
.
Furthermore, there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
, the integer
s_{n}
satisfies the following estimates

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\max\{0,e_{i,n}-g_{i}|F:\mathbb{Q}|\}\leq s_{n}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\max\{0,e_{i,n}-1\},

where

e_{i,n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}(F_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}(F_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}(F),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Under the assumption of the proposition, we have a pseudo-isomorphism

\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})\sim\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{M}(A_{i}/F_{n})

for every
n
. The conclusion of the proposition then follows from this and applying Theorem
4.1
to each
\mathcal{M}(A_{i}/F_{n})
.
∎

In particular, the above proposition implies that, in estimating the number of summands of
\Lambda/\Phi_{j}
, it suffices to treat the case of a simple abelian variety.

For the remainder of the section, we consider a base-changing context, where we can obtain a better estimate. Let
A
be a simple abelian variety defined over a number field
F
and
F_{\infty}
a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
. Suppose that
L
is a finite Galois extension of
F
such that
L\cap F_{\infty}=F
(this is a very mild condition; one can always replace
F
by a larger extension in
F_{\infty}
to ensure this). Denote by
L_{n}
(
0\leq n\leq\infty
) the compositum of
F_{n}
and
L
.
Since
L\cap F_{\infty}=F
, we have that
L_{n}\cap F_{\infty}=F_{n}
and so the Galois groups
\Gal(L_{n}/F_{n})
naturally identified with one another. We shall use
G
to denote these Galois groups. Let
B:=\mathrm{Res}^{L}_{F}(A)
, where
\mathrm{Res}^{L}_{F}(A)
is the Weil restriction of scalar of
A
from
L
to
F
(in the sense of
[
15
, §1.3]
). From the functoriality property of Weil restriction, we have
A(L_{n})=B(F_{n})
for every
n
.

The group ring
\mathbb{Q}[G]
is semisimple. Furthermore, there is a decomposition

\mathbb{Q}[G]=\bigoplus_{\rho}\mathbb{Q}[G]_{\rho}

of a direct sum of minimal two-sided
ideals indexed by the irreducible rational representations
\rho
of
G
. Here
\mathbb{Q}[G]_{\rho}
is the
\rho
-isotypic component of
\mathbb{Q}[G]
, i.e., the sum of all left ideals of
\mathbb{Q}[G]
isomorphic to
\rho
. Following
[
12
]
, we define the
\rho
-twist of
A
by

A_{\rho}:=(\mathbb{Q}[G]_{\rho}\cap\mathbb{Z}[G])\otimes A.

By
[
12
, Theorem 2.1(i)]
, this is an abelian variety with dimension
g(\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\rho)
. With these notation, we can now state the following result.

Proposition 4.4
.

Retain the above settings. Suppose further that every prime of
F
above
p
does not split completely in
F_{\infty}/F
. Then there exists a sequence of integers
\{s_{n}\}
such that we have

\mathcal{M}(A/L_{n})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}.

Furthermore, there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for every
n>n_{0}
, the integer
s_{n}
satisfies the following estimates

\sum_{\rho}\max\{0,e_{\rho,n}-g(\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\rho)|F:\mathbb{Q}|\}\leq s_{n}\leq\sum_{\rho}\max\{0,e_{\rho,n}-1\},

where

e_{\rho,n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A_{\rho}(F_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A_{\rho}(F_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A_{\rho}(F),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Recall that
A(L_{n})=B(F_{n})
by the functoriality of Weil restriction. On the other hand, it follows from
[
12
, Theorem 4.5]
that
B(F_{n})
is isogenous to
\bigoplus_{\rho}A_{\rho}(F_{n})
. Consequently, we have a pseudo-isomorphism

\mathcal{M}(A/L_{n})\sim\bigoplus_{\rho}\mathcal{M}(A_{\rho}/F_{n})

for every
n
. Applying Theorem
4.1
to each
\mathcal{M}(A_{\rho}/F_{n})
and subsequently summing up the estimates obtained, we obtain the conclusion of the proposition.
∎

We specialize to the context of an elliptic curve, where we can obtain precise values.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
\mathbb{Q}
and
F
a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
such that
\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus m}
. Let
K_{i}
(
i=1,...,2^{m}-1
) be all the quadratic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
F
, and
E_{i}
the corresponding quadratic twist of
E
. We set
E_{0}=E
. Let
F_{n}
be the intermediate extension of
F^{\mathrm{cyc}}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. Then there exists a sequence of integers
\{s_{n}\}
such that the following pseudo-isomorphism holds:

\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}}.

Furthermore, for every
n
, we have the following equalities

s_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{2^{m}-1}\max\{0,e_{i,n}-1\},

where

e_{i,n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}(\mathbb{Q}_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}(\mathbb{Q}_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}(\mathbb{Q}),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{cyc}}/\mathbb{Q}
is totally ramified at
p
and

is a
2
-extension, we see that

is totally ramified at every prime of
F
above

. Therefore, we may take
n_{0}=0
in Proposition
4.4
. Finally, note that in the context of the current proposition, the twist of
E
is precisely the corresponding quadratic twist of
E
.
∎

.

5
CM elliptic curves

In this section, we present some cases, where we can obtain precise values on the structure of the fine Mordell-Weil groups as in Proposition
4.5
.

Throughout this section,
K
will always denote an imaginary quadratic field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
K
with complex multiplication given by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. We shall always assume that our elliptic curve has good reduction at every primes above
p
.

We first consider the case that the prime
p
splits completely in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{p}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
the primes of
K
above
p
. Since the class group of
K
is finite, there exists an integer
h
such that
\mathfrak{p}^{h}=\pi\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{h}=\overline{\pi}\mathcal{O}_{K}
for some
\pi\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
. We then set

E[\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}]:=\cup_{n\geq 1}E[\pi^{n}]\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptE[\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}]:=\cup_{n\geq 1}E[\overline{\pi}^{n}]

which are cofree
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules of corank
1
. Moreover, there is a natural decomposition

E[p^{\infty}]=E[\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}]\oplus E[\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{\infty}]

of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)
-modules.

For a finite extension
L
of
K
, we define the fine
\mathfrak{p}
-Mordell-Weil group by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(E/L)=\ker\left(E(L)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w|\mathfrak{p}}E(L_{w})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\right),

where
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
is the ring of integers of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Note that since we are assuming that
p
splits in
K/\mathbb{Q}
, we have identifications
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\mathcal{O}_{K,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

The fine
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
-Mordell-Weil group
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(E/L)
is defined similarly. One can then check easily that
\mathcal{M}(E/L)=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(E/L)\oplus\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(E/L)
. Here the identification is taken over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
acts on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(E/L)
through the isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, while
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
acts on
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}(E/L)
via the isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{K,\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Retain setup as above. Let
K_{\infty}/K
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Let
F
be a Galois extension of
K
such that
\Gal(F/K)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus m}
. Let
L_{i}
(
i=1,...,2^{m}-1
) be all the quadratic extensions of
K
contained in
F
, and
E_{i}
the corresponding quadratic twist of
E
. We set
E_{0}=E
. Let
F_{n}
be the intermediate extension of
F_{\infty}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. Then there exists a sequence of integers
\{s_{n}\}
such that the following pseudo-isomorphism holds:

\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus 2s_{j}}.

Furthermore, there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for every
n>n_{0}
, the integer
s_{n}
satisfies the following estimates

s_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\max\{0,f_{i,n}-1\},

where

f_{i,n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}E_{i}(K_{n})-\rank_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}E_{i}(K_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}E_{i}(K),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

To begin with, we claim that for every
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}
of
K
, the primes of
K
above
p
do not split completely in
K_{\infty}/K
. Plainly, this is true if both primes
\mathfrak{p}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
are ramified in
K_{\infty}/K
. Suppose that that if one of them, say
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
, is unramified over
K_{\infty}/K
. Then
K_{\infty}
is precisely the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
unramified outside
\mathfrak{p}
. In this situation, it follows from
[
2
, Chap. Proposition 1.9(iii)]
that the prime
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
does not split in
K_{\infty}/K
. This establishes our claim.

Since the quadratic twist
E_{i}
of
E
also has complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, by the argument in Proposition
4.5
, it suffices to show the theorem for the elliptic curve
E
over
K_{\infty}
. By applying Pontryagin dual to

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(E/K_{n})\longrightarrow E(K_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w|\mathfrak{p}}E(K_{n,w})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}},

and subsequently tensoring the resulting sequence with
F_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, we obtain another exact sequence

\bigoplus_{w\mid\mathfrak{p}}\big(E(K_{n,w})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\big(E(K_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(E/K_{n})^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0.

By an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-analogue of Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
, we have

\big(E(K_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\big)^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}\big(\Lambda/\Phi_{j}\big)^{\oplus f_{n}}

and

\bigoplus_{w\mid\mathfrak{p}}\big(E(K_{n,w})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\big)^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}\Lambda/\Phi_{j}

for large enough
n
. On the other hand, if
f_{n}>0
, then a non-torsion point
P\in E(K_{n})-E(K_{n-1})
is sent to a non-torsion point in
E(K_{n,w})-E(K_{n-1,w})
for some
w
. Hence this yields the required conclusion of the proposition.
∎

We now prove the analog of the preceding proposition in the context when the prime
p
is inert in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. We shall write
\mathfrak{p}
for the unique prime of
K
above
p
. In this context, for every Galois extension
L
of
K
, both the fine Mordell-group
\mathcal{M}(E/L)
and Mordell-group
E(L)
carry a natural
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module structure. Moreover, for every prime
w
of
L
above
\mathfrak{p}
, the group
E(L_{w})
has a
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
-module structure.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
E
is an elliptic curve defined over an imaginary quadratic field
K
with complex multiplication given by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and that the prime
p
is inert in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Let
K_{\infty}/K
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Let
F
be a Galois extension of
K
such that
\Gal(F/K)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus m}
. Let
L_{i}
(
i=1,...,2^{m}-1
) be all the quadratic extensions of
K
contained in
F
, and
E_{i}
the corresponding quadratic twist of
E
. We set
E_{0}=E
. Let
F_{n}
be the intermediate extension of
F_{\infty}/F
such that
|F_{n}:F|=p^{n}
. Then there exists a sequence of integers
\{s_{n}\}
such that one has

\mathcal{M}(E/F_{n})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus 2s_{j}}.

Furthermore, there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
, the integer
s_{n}
satisfies the following estimates

s_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\max\{0,f_{i,n}-1\},

where

f_{i,n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}E_{i}(K_{n})-\rank_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}E_{i}(K_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}E_{i}(K),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

As before, it suffices to show the result over
K_{\infty}/K
. Since
p
is inert in
K/\mathbb{Q}
, there is only one prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
over
p
, and so it must ramify in
K_{\infty}/K
.

As seen in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 4.2]
, we have isomorphisms

E(K_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong E(K_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}

and

E(K_{n,w})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong E(K_{n,w})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}

for every prime
w
of
K_{n}
above
p
. Therefore, the defining sequence of the fine Mordell-Weil group can be rewritten as

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(E/K_{n})\longrightarrow E(K_{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w|p}E(K_{n,w})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}

which are viewed as
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}[\Gal(K_{n}/K)]
-modules. Furthermore, the quadratic extension
K_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is unramified, and so the cyclotomic polynomial
\Phi_{n}
remains irreducible over
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
. Hence, by applying the
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
-analogue of the results in Section
3
and following the argument of the preceding proposition, we can obtain the conclusion of the present proposition. ∎

.

6
Structure of fine Mordell-Weil groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions

In this final section, we say how our results have implications on the structure of the fine Mordell-Weil group over the full
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}
. Recall that by
[
6
, Proposition 2.1.2]
, there is a
\Lambda
-map

\big(A(F_{\infty})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\big)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{\oplus r}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}(\Lambda/\Phi_{n})^{\oplus a_{n}}\Big)

with finite kernel and cokernel. The proof of this result relies on the
fact that
A(F_{n})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is divisible. However, this latter fact does not always hold for the fine Mordell group
\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})
(see
[
18
, Section 7]
). Nevertheless, by the structure theory of finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-module, we still have a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
-homomorphism

\mathcal{M}(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{u}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\Lambda/\Phi_{c_{i}}^{b_{i}}\Big)\oplus N

(6.1)

with finite kernel and cokernel, where
u
is a non-negative integer,
c_{i}
and
b_{i}
are some positive integers (but we allow
r=0
), and
N
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module whose support does not contain any cyclotomic polynomials.

We can now state the main result of this section.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
A
be a
g
-dimensional abelian variety defined over
F
and let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
. Retain the notation in (
6.1
).
Suppose that
\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{n})
is finite for every
n
. Then there exists a
n_{0}
such that whenever
n\geq n_{0}
, we have the estimates

\max\{0,e_{n}-g|F:\mathbb{Q}|\}\leq u+\#\{c_{i}~|~c_{i}=n\}\leq\max\{0,e_{n}-1\},

where

e_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

For each
n
, we have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(A/F_{n})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{n}}
\textstyle{R(A/F_{n})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{n}}
\textstyle{\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{n})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{n}}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(A/F_{\infty})^{\Gamma_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R(A/F_{\infty})^{\Gamma_{n}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{\infty})^{\Gamma_{n}}}

with exact rows. By
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
, the map
\beta_{n}
has finite kernel and cokernel for every
n
. Combining this with the finiteness of
\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{n})
, we see that the map
\alpha_{n}
has finite kernel and cokernel for every
n
. Taking the latter into account, the conclusion of the proposition follows from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

There is a conjecture that asserts that the dual fine Selmer group
R(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module. To the best of the author’s knowledge, this conjecture, stated in such generality, first appears in the work Perrin-Riou
[
13
]
, and was later studied in greater depth by Wuthrich
[
16
,
17
]
. For further theoretical and numerical evidence in support of this conjecture, the reader is referred to
[
8
, Corollary 3.5 and Remark 5.2]
and
[
9
, Theorem 3.9 and Section 6]
.

Assuming the torsionness conjecture holds, we obtain the estimates

\max\{0,e_{n}-g|F:\mathbb{Q}|\}\leq\#\{c_{i}~|~c_{i}=n\}\leq\max\{0,e_{n}-1\}

for large enough
n
.

Remark 6.2
.

Of course, one can always work with a slightly weaker assumption, namely that
\mathcal{M}(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is torsion over
\Lambda
. (For some discussion on this latter torsionness, we refer readers to
[
11
]
.) However, we also note that if one is willing to assume the finiteness of
\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{n})
, then it is shown in
[
8
]
that
\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is torsion over
\Lambda
. Therefore, under the assumption of the finiteness of
\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{n})
, we see that
R(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is torsion over

if and only if
\mathcal{M}(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is so.

Finally, we can say more under the assumption that
A(F_{\infty})
is finitely generated.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
A
be a
g
-dimensional abelian variety defined over
F
and let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
.
Suppose that the following statements are valid.

(a)

\mbox{Ж}(A/F_{n})
is finite for every
n
.

(b)

A(F_{\infty})
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}
.

Then we have

\mathcal{M}(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}(\Lambda/\Phi_{j})^{\oplus s_{j}},

and there exists a
n_{0}
such that whenever
n\geq n_{0}
, we have the estimates

\max\{0,e_{n}-g|F:\mathbb{Q}|\}\leq s_{n}\leq\max\{0,e_{n}-1\},

where

e_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n})-\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F_{n-1})}{\phi(p^{n})},&\mbox{if $n\geq 1;$}\\
\\
\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}A(F),&\mbox{if $n=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Under the finite generation of
A(F_{\infty})
, it then follows from
[
6
, Theorem 2.1.2]
that

A(F_{\infty})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\big(\Lambda/\Phi_{n}\big)^{\oplus a_{n}}

for some integers
a_{n}
. Since
\mathcal{M}(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is a quotient of
A(F_{\infty})^{\vee}
, we also have

\mathcal{M}(A/F_{\infty})^{\vee}\sim\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\big(\Lambda/\Phi_{n}\big)^{\oplus s_{n}}

for some integers
s_{n}
. The conclusion now follows from this and the preceding proposition.
∎
