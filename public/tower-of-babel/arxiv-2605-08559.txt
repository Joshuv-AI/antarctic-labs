Structure-Preserving Reconstruction of Convex Lipschitz Functionals on Hilbert Spaces from Finite Samples
Anastasis Kratsios

Abstract
Convex functionals are ubiquitous in applied analysis, appearing as value functions, risk measures, super-hedging prices, and loss functionals in machine learning. In many applications, however, the functional is only observed through finitely many exact pointwise evaluations. We ask whether a convex functional on a separable Hilbert space H H can be reconstructed, up to arbitrary uniform accuracy, by an explicit formula which preserves convexity and Lipschitz regularity and is finitely computable. We answer this affirmatively. For every compact convex C ⊆ H C\subseteq H , every L L -Lipschitz convex functional ρ : C → ℝ \rho:C\to\mathbb{R} , and every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , we construct an explicit finite-sample reconstruction which is convex, L L -Lipschitz, and uniformly ε \varepsilon -accurate on C C . The construction uses only finitely many linear measurements ⟨ b , ⋅ ⟩ H \langle b,\cdot\rangle_{H} , with b b lying in a finite-dimensional subspace of H H , and is exactly implementable by a ReLU \operatorname{ReLU} -MLP. Building on this, we introduce convex neural functionals (CNFs), a structured trainable architecture class containing our reconstruction, whose every admissible parameter configuration is automatically convex and Lipschitz, providing a principled foundation for learning convex functionals from finite data.

Conclusion

We have shown that finite samples of an
L
-Lipschitz convex functional
\rho:C\to\mathbb{R}
on a compact convex subset of a separable Hilbert space admit a structure-preserving reconstruction theory. First, we constructed an explicit finite-sample formula which is convex,
L
-Lipschitz, and uniformly accurate on
C
(Theorem
3.1
). Then, we showed that this reconstruction is exactly computable by sparsely connected
\operatorname{ReLU}
-MLPs using only finitely many Hilbert-space measurements of the form
\langle b,\cdot\rangle_{H}
(Theorem
3.2
). Finally, we certified that this reconstruction belongs to the broader trainable class of convex neural functionals (CNFs), every admissible parameter configuration of which is automatically convex and Lipschitz (Theorem
3.3
). Our toy numerical experiments illustrate that CNFs can learn randomly generated convex
\operatorname{ReLU}
-MLP targets while preserving convexity throughout training.

It would be interesting to consider specialized applications of our general tools in risk management, where one seeks to learn a convex, or even coherent, risk measure from finitely many risk measurements at finitely many random variables, and in mathematical finance, where one seeks to learn a super-hedging price from finitely many contingent claims. On the theoretical side, we will extend the present Hilbert-space theory to the separable, and likely reflexive, Banach space setting with applications to spaces such as
L^{p}_{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{F})
over some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
for finite
1<p
.

5.1
Proof of Reconstruction Result (Theorem
3.1
)

We now state the technical version of our main result before moving to its proof.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
L>0
. Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space, let
C\subseteq H
be compact, convex, and with

D\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\operatorname{diam}(C)>0
.

For any convex,
L
-Lipschitz map
\rho:C\to\mathbb{R}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, set

\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{\varepsilon}{4L}
,
\alpha\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{\varepsilon}{16L}
, and
\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{\varepsilon}{8D}
.

Let
\mathbb{X}_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\subseteq C
be a
\delta
-net of
C
with

N=N_{\delta}(C)
. Then there exist
d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, a
d
-dimensional linear subspace

V_{d}\subseteq H
, and an
\eta
-net

p^{\eta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{p_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
of
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
such that the induced map

f_{\varepsilon,d}:H\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

f_{\varepsilon,d}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\max_{m=1,\dots,M}\,\Big\{\langle p_{m},x\rangle+\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p_{m},\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)\Big\}\mbox{ (for each }x\in H)

(5.1)

is convex,
L
-Lipschitz, and satisfies

\sup_{\xi\in C}\,\bigl|\rho(\xi)-f_{\varepsilon,d}(\xi)\bigr|<\varepsilon.

(5.2)

Moreover,

N=N_{\varepsilon/(4L)}(C)
,

d=d_{C}\,(\frac{\varepsilon}{16L})\leq N_{\varepsilon/(16L)}(C)
,
and
M\leq(1+\tfrac{16LD}{\varepsilon})^{d}
; where

d_{C}(\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\min\biggl\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\exists\,V_{k}\subseteq H\text{ linear subspace with }\dim(V_{k})=k\text{ and }\sup_{\xi\in C}\operatorname{dist}(\xi,V_{k})\leq\alpha\biggr\}

for any
\alpha>0
.

We prove our result in three steps. First, we obtain an exact but in-computable primal form. Next, we dualize and discretize it in a way which we preserve both convexity and Lipschitz regularity. Finally, we estimate the remaining complexity parameters
d
and
M
(as
N
is computed in the second step).

5.1.1
Step 1 - Infinitely Parameterized, Exact, Primal Form

In what follows, we denote the Fenchel (convex) dual of a function on
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
by
f^{\star}
; its convex envelope is thus
f^{\star\,\star}
. Note that, when
f
is convex then by the Fenchel duality theorem
f^{\star\,\star}=f
.
The following is a low-regularity version of the main result in
[
4
]
; which conveniently admits a simpler form in the finite data case.

Lemma 5.1

(Convexified Whitney-McShane Extension)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space, let
C\subseteq H
be non-empty and convex, and let

\rho:C\to\mathbb{R}
be convex and
L
-Lipschitz. Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and

\mathbb{X}_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\subseteq C
, and define the upper
McShane extension, cf.
[
12
]
, for every
x\in H
by

f_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}g_{N}^{\star\,\star}\mbox{ and }g_{N}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\big\{\rho(\xi_{n})+L\|x-\xi_{n}\|\big\}.

(5.3)

Then
f_{N}
is convex,
L
-Lipschitz, and interpolates the data:

f_{N}(\xi_{n})=\rho(\xi_{n})
for all
n=1,\dots,N
.

Moreover,
f_{N}
admits the semi-closed-form expression:

f_{N}(x)=\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\,\biggl\{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})+L\Big\|x-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\Big\|\biggr\}.

(5.4)

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

For each
n=1,\dots,N
abbreviate
y_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\rho(\xi_{n})
.
Since
\rho
is
L
-Lipschitz on
C
, for every
m,n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
,

\rho(\xi_{m})\leq\rho(\xi_{n})+L\|\xi_{m}-\xi_{n}\|.

Hence
g_{N}
interpolates the data:

g_{N}(\xi_{m})=\rho(\xi_{m})

for every
m=1,\dots,N
. Moreover,
g_{N}
is
L
-Lipschitz, as a finite
minimum of
L
-Lipschitz functions.
We construct a convex minorant of
g_{N}
which already interpolates the data.
Let
\Delta_{N}
denote the simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and define

R_{N}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\inf_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\Big\{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})+L\left\|x-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\right\|\Big\}

(5.5)

for each
x\in H
.

Note that
R_{N}
is
L
-Lipschitz, hence continuous. Indeed, for every

x,y\in H
and every
\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
,

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})+L\left\|x-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\right\|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})+L\left\|y-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\right\|+L\|x-y\|.

Taking the infimum over
\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
gives

R_{N}(x)\leq R_{N}(y)+L\|x-y\|
, and exchanging
x
and
y
gives

|R_{N}(x)-R_{N}(y)|\leq L\|x-y\|
.

The function inside the infimum in (
5.5
) is jointly convex in
(x,\lambda)
; hence

R_{N}
is convex. Taking
\lambda=e_{n}
implies that
R_{N}\leq g_{N}
, since for every
n=1,\dots,N
we have

R_{N}(x)\leq\rho(\xi_{n})+L\|x-\xi_{n}\|.

It remains to verify interpolation. Fix
m\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and

\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
. Since
C
is convex,

y_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\in C
.
By convexity of
\rho
,

\rho(y_{\lambda})\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})
.
By the
L
-Lipschitzness of
\rho
,

\rho(\xi_{m})\leq\rho(y_{\lambda})+L\|\xi_{m}-y_{\lambda}\|
.
Consequently,

\rho(\xi_{m})\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})+L\left\|\xi_{m}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\right\|.

Taking the infimum over
\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
gives

\rho(\xi_{m})\leq R_{N}(\xi_{m})
. The reverse inequality follows by taking

\lambda=e_{m}
. Thus, for each
m=1,\dots,N
we have that

R_{N}(\xi_{m})=\rho(\xi_{m})
.
Since
R_{N}
is continuous, convex, and
R_{N}\leq g_{N}
, while

f_{N}=g_{N}^{\star\star}
is the largest lower semicontinuous convex minorant of

g_{N}
, we have

R_{N}\leq f_{N}\leq g_{N}
.
Evaluating at
\xi_{m}
gives

\rho(\xi_{m})=R_{N}(\xi_{m})\leq f_{N}(\xi_{m})\leq g_{N}(\xi_{m})=\rho(\xi_{m}).

Therefore
f_{N}(\xi_{m})=\rho(\xi_{m})
for every
m=1,\dots,N
.

Finally, we show that
f_{N}
is
L
-Lipschitz. By the Fenchel-Moreau
representation,
f_{N}=g_{N}^{\star\star}
is the supremum of all continuous affine
minorants of
g_{N}
. Thus

f_{N}(x)=\sup\big\{\ell(x):\ell\text{ is continuous affine and }\ell\leq g_{N}\text{ on }H\big\}

where
\leq
is meant in the pointwise sense; i.e.
f\leq g
if
f(x)\leq g(x)
for all
x\in H
.
Let
\ell(x)=\langle p,x\rangle+b
be such a minorant. We claim that

\|p\|\leq L
. If
p=0
, this is immediate. Otherwise, set

u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}p/\|p\|
. Since
g_{N}
is
L
-Lipschitz, for every
x\in H
and

t>0
,

\ell(x)+t\|p\|=\ell(x+tu)\leq g_{N}(x+tu)\leq g_{N}(x)+Lt.

(5.6)

Hence

t(\|p\|-L)\leq g_{N}(x)-\ell(x)
.
Letting
t\to\infty
in (
5.6
) implies that
\|p\|\leq L
.
Now fix
x,y\in H
. For every continuous affine minorant

\ell(z)=\langle p,z\rangle+b
of
g_{N}
, the preceding argument gives

\|p\|\leq L
, and therefore

\ell(x)=\ell(y)+\langle p,x-y\rangle\leq\ell(y)+L\|x-y\|\leq f_{N}(y)+L\|x-y\|.

Taking the supremum over all continuous affine minorants
\ell\leq g_{N}
gives

f_{N}(x)\leq f_{N}(y)+L\|x-y\|
.
Exchanging
x
and
y
yields

|f_{N}(x)-f_{N}(y)|\leq L\|x-y\|
,
and hence
\operatorname{Lip}(f_{N})\leq L
.

For the last “formula” claim, since
\Delta_{N}
is compact and the objective
in (
5.5
) is continuous, the infimum in (
5.5
) is
attained. Hence

R_{N}(x)=\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\Big\{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})+L\left\|x-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\right\|\Big\}.

It remains to prove that
R_{N}
is the closed convex envelope of
g_{N}
. We already know that

R_{N}
is convex, continuous, and
R_{N}\leq g_{N}
; hence

R_{N}\leq g_{N}^{\star\star}
.
Conversely, fix
x\in H
and
\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
. Set

y_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}
. For each
n=1,\dots,N
, define

z_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\xi_{n}+x-y_{\lambda}
; then
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}z_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}+x-y_{\lambda}=x
.
Moreover,

g_{N}(z_{n})\leq\rho(\xi_{n})+L\|z_{n}-\xi_{n}\|=\rho(\xi_{n})+L\|x-y_{\lambda}\|
.
Therefore, by convexity of
g_{N}^{\star\star}
and since
g_{N}^{\star\star}\leq g_{N}
,

\displaystyle g_{N}^{\star\star}(x)

\displaystyle=g_{N}^{\star\star}\biggl(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}z_{n}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}g_{N}^{\star\star}(z_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}g_{N}(z_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})+L\left\|x-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\right\|.

Taking the infimum over
\lambda\in\Delta_{N}
gives

g_{N}^{\star\star}(x)\leq R_{N}(x)
.
Thus
g_{N}^{\star\star}=R_{N}
, which proves (
5.4
).
∎

The only trouble with the formula in (
5.4
) is that the
pointwise
minimum on the “final layer” is not genuinely explicit; namely the following must be
exactly
computed

\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\,\biggl\{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}y_{n}+L\Big\|x-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\Big\|\biggr\}.

(5.7)

over pairs of paired datapoints
\{(\xi_{n},y_{n})\}_{n=1}^{N}
. The idea, is instead to relax the problem by
entropic
penalizing the “objective function”
F_{N}:H\times\Delta_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
defined implicitly in (
5.4
) for each
(x,\lambda)\in H\times\Delta_{N}
by

F_{N}(x,\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})+L\Big\|x-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\Big\|.

(5.8)

5.1.2
Step 2 - Discretization While Preserving Convexity and Lipschitzness

A naïve approach is to discretize the
N
-simplex
\Delta_{N}
which
F_{N}
is optimized over (pointwise in
x
); however, since any such discretization is non-convex then the pointwise minimum can fail to be convex. Instead, we will discretize
F_{N}
by first considering its dual formulation of (
5.7
).

Lemma 5.2

(Dual form of
f_{N}
)
.

In the setting of Lemma
5.1
, for
every
x\in H
,
f_{N}
is given by

f_{N}(x)=\sup_{\|p\|\leq L}\Big\{\langle p,x\rangle+\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p,\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)\Big\}.

(5.9)

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Using the Hilbert-space dual representation of the norm (by identifying
H\cong H^{\star}
through the Riesz representation theorem) for each
z\in H
, we have that

L\|z\|=\sup_{\|p\|\leq L}\langle p,z\rangle
; whence we deduce that

\displaystyle f_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\,\sup_{\|p\|\leq L}\,\Big\{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\rho(\xi_{n})+\left\langle p,x-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\xi_{n}\right\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle=\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\sup_{\|p\|\leq L}\Big\{\langle p,x\rangle+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p,\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)\Big\}.

For fixed
x
, define

\Phi_{x}(\lambda,p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\langle p,x\rangle+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p,\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)
.
The map
\Phi_{x}
is affine in
\lambda
and affine in
p
. Moreover,

\Delta_{N}
is compact and convex, while
\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
is weakly compact
and convex, since
H
is reflexive. Since
p\mapsto\Phi_{x}(\lambda,p)
is
weakly continuous for every
\lambda
, Sion’s minimax theorem (cf.
[
51
, Theorem 4.2’]
) implies that

\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\sup_{\|p\|\leq L}\Phi_{x}(\lambda,p)=\sup_{\|p\|\leq L}\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\Phi_{x}(\lambda,p)
.
Therefore,
f_{N}
in (
5.4
) can be re-expressed as

f_{N}(x)=\sup_{\|p\|\leq L}\Big\{\langle p,x\rangle+\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p,\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)\Big\}

(5.10)

Finally, since a linear functional over the simplex attains its minimum at an
extreme point,

\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{n}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p,\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)=\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p,\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)

(5.11)

substituting (
5.11
) into (
5.10
) yields (
5.9
).
∎

The advantage of the dual form is that one can discretize the set of
p\in H
being optimized over in (
5.9
), namely the closed Hilbert ball
\overline{B}(0,L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{p\in H:\,\|p\|\leq L\}
of radius
L\geq 0
, into an
\eta
-net of a finite-dimensional sub-ball thereof; which is possible since finite dimensional balls are compact while infinite-dimensional ones are not (by the Hahn-Banach theorem).
The reason why this discretization will not break convexity, unlike discretizing
\Delta_{N}
directly in the primal form (
5.4
), is that (
5.9
) expresses
f_{N}
as the supremum of affine hyperplanes; and the point-wise
any
supremum of (even possibly finitely) affine hyperplanes is still a convex function.

Lemma 5.3

(Discretized dual form of
f_{N}
)
.

In the setting of Lemma
5.1
, for every non-empty compact subset
K\subseteq H
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
d\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, a
d
-dimensional linear subspace
V_{d}\subseteq H
,
\eta>0
, and a finite
\eta
-net
p^{\eta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{p_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
of
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
such that the map
f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}:H\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\max_{m=1,\dots,M}\,\Big\{\langle p_{m},x\rangle+\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p_{m},\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)\Big\},\qquad x\in H,

(5.12)

satisfies

\sup_{x\in K}\bigl|f_{N}(x)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)\bigr|<\varepsilon.

(5.13)

Moreover,
f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}
is
L
-Lipschitz and convex on all of
H
.

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

For each
x\in H
and
p\in\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
, set

\Phi_{x}(p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\langle p,x\rangle+\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p,\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)=\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})+\langle p,x-\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr).

By Lemma
5.2
,
f_{N}(x)=\sup_{\|p\|\leq L}\Phi_{x}(p)
.

Choose
\alpha>0
such that
L\alpha<\varepsilon/2
. Since
K
is compact, there exist
z_{1},\dots,z_{J}\in K
such that

K\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}B_{H}(z_{j},\alpha)
.
Set

V_{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\operatorname{span}\{z_{1},\dots,z_{J},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N}\}
,
and let
d\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\dim(V_{d})
. Let
P_{d}:H\to V_{d}
denote the orthogonal projection. Since
\xi_{n}\in V_{d}
for each
n=1,\dots,N
, and since
\sup_{x\in K}\operatorname{dist}(x,V_{d})\leq\alpha
, we have

\alpha_{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\sup_{x\in K}\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|(I-P_{d})(x-\xi_{n})\|\leq\alpha
.
For
p\in\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
, set
q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}P_{d}p
. Then
q\in V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
and, for every
x\in K
,

|\Phi_{x}(p)-\Phi_{x}(q)|\leq\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|\langle p-q,x-\xi_{n}\rangle|=\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|\langle p,(I-P_{d})(x-\xi_{n})\rangle|\leq L\alpha_{d}\leq L\alpha<\varepsilon/2.

Defining
f_{N}^{V_{d}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\sup_{p\in V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)}\Phi_{x}(p)
, we obtain
0\leq f_{N}(x)-f_{N}^{V_{d}}(x)<\varepsilon/2
for every
x\in K
; where the lower bound holds since
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)\subseteq\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
implies
f_{N}^{V_{d}}(x)\leq f_{N}(x)
.
It remains to replace
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
by a finite net to deduce the approximation claim. Since
V_{d}
is finite-dimensional,
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
is compact. Let
R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\sup_{x\in K}\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|x-\xi_{n}\|<\infty
. Choose
\eta>0
such that
\eta R<\varepsilon/2
, with arbitrary
\eta>0
if
R=0
. Let
p^{\eta}=\{p_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
be an
\eta
-net of
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
. Then, for every
p\in V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
, there exists
m\in\{1,\dots,M\}
such that
\|p-p_{m}\|\leq\eta
. Hence, for every
x\in K
we have that

|\Phi_{x}(p)-\Phi_{x}(p_{m})|\leq\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|\langle p-p_{m},x-\xi_{n}\rangle|\leq\eta R
.
Taking suprema over
p\in V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
gives, for every
x\in K
,

0\leq f_{N}^{V_{d}}(x)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)\leq\eta R<\varepsilon/2.

(5.14)

Combining the preceding finite-dimensional approximation estimate with (
5.14
) implies that, for every
x\in K
we have

0\leq f_{N}(x)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)\leq f_{N}(x)-f_{N}^{V_{d}}(x)+f_{N}^{V_{d}}(x)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)<\varepsilon
; implying (
5.13
).

It remains to prove convexity and Lipschitzness. For each
m\in\{1,\dots,M\}
, set
a_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p_{m},\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)
. Then
f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)=\max_{1\leq m\leq M}\{\langle p_{m},x\rangle+a_{m}\}
. For any
x,y\in H
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
, let
m_{\lambda}\in\{1,\dots,M\}
be a maximizer such that

f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)=\langle p_{m_{\lambda}},\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\rangle+a_{m_{\lambda}}
.
Then, by affinity of
H\ni z\mapsto\langle p_{m_{\lambda}},z\rangle+a_{m_{\lambda}}\in\mathbb{R}
we have that

\displaystyle f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)

\displaystyle=\lambda\bigl(\langle p_{m_{\lambda}},x\rangle+a_{m_{\lambda}}\bigr)+(1-\lambda)\bigl(\langle p_{m_{\lambda}},y\rangle+a_{m_{\lambda}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)+(1-\lambda)f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(y).

Thus
f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}
is convex on
H
.
Moreover, since
p_{m}\in V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
, we have
\|p_{m}\|\leq L
for every
m
. Thus, for every
x,y\in H
, we have that

f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(y)\leq\max_{1\leq m\leq M}\bigl(\langle p_{m},x\rangle+a_{m}-\langle p_{m},y\rangle-a_{m}\bigr)\leq\max_{1\leq m\leq M}|\langle p_{m},x-y\rangle|\leq L\|x-y\|.

Interchanging
x
and
y
gives
f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(y)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)\leq L\|x-y\|
. Hence
|f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(y)|\leq L\|x-y\|
, and so
f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}
is
L
-Lipschitz on
H
.
∎

It remains to relate our construction back to the original function being reconstructed from sample data; which we now demonstrate.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
(Pt. 1).

Let
f_{N}
be the convexified Whitney-McShane extension from Lemma
5.1
. By that lemma,
f_{N}
interpolates the data, i.e.
f_{N}(\xi_{n})=\rho(\xi_{n})
for every
n=1,\dots,N
.
Applying Lemma
5.3
to the compact set
K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\mathbb{X}_{N}
, implies that: there exist
d\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta>0
, and a finite
\eta
-net
p^{\eta}=\{p_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
of
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}(0,L)
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{X}_{N}}\bigl|f_{N}(x)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)\bigr|<\varepsilon.

(5.15)

Since
f_{N}(\xi_{n})=\rho(\xi_{n})
, (
5.15
) implies

\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\bigl|\rho(\xi_{n})-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\xi_{n})\bigr|=\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\bigl|f_{N}(\xi_{n})-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\xi_{n})\bigr|<\varepsilon.

(5.16)

Now fix
\xi\in C
. Since
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
is a
\delta
-net of
C
then, there exists
n_{\xi}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
\|\xi-\xi_{n_{\xi}}\|\leq\delta
. Using the
L
-Lipschitzness of
\rho
and of
f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}
, together with (
5.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bigl|\rho(\xi)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\xi)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl|\rho(\xi)-\rho(\xi_{n_{\xi}})\bigr|+\bigl|\rho(\xi_{n_{\xi}})-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\xi_{n_{\xi}})\bigr|+\bigl|f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\xi_{n_{\xi}})-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\xi)\bigr|

(5.17)

\displaystyle<L\|\xi-\xi_{n_{\xi}}\|+\varepsilon+L\|\xi-\xi_{n_{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2L\delta+\varepsilon.

Taking the supremum over
\xi\in C
gives (
3.2
). Finally, Lemma
5.3
also implies that
f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}
is convex and
L
-Lipschitz on all of
H
.
Without loss of generality, replace
\varepsilon
by
\varepsilon/2
and set
\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varepsilon/(4L)
throughout, so that
2L\delta+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon
.
∎

5.1.3
Step 3 - Estimating The Complexity of the Formula

It remains to estimate the complexity of our reconstruction formula; i.e. the quantities
d
and
M
(as
N
was already computed). For this, we only require the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4

(Finiteness and estimates for the finite-dimensional dual discretization)
.

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space, let
C\subseteq H
be non-empty and compact, and let

D\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\operatorname{diam}(C)
. For
\alpha>0
, define the effective linear dimension of
C
at scale
\alpha
by

d_{C}(\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\min\biggl\{\dim(V):V\subseteq H\text{ is a finite-dimensional linear subspace and }\sup_{\xi\in C}\operatorname{dist}(\xi,V)\leq\alpha\biggr\}.

Then
d_{C}(\alpha)\leq N_{\alpha}(C)<\infty
.

Let
L>0
, let
\mathbb{X}_{N}=\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\subseteq C
, and fix a finite-dimensional subspace

V_{d}\subseteq H
satisfying

\sup_{\xi\in C}\operatorname{dist}(\xi,V_{d})\leq\alpha.

Then, for every
\eta>0
, the ball
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
admits a finite
\eta
-net

p^{\eta}=\{p_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
satisfying

M\leq\biggl(1+\frac{2L}{\eta}\biggr)^{d}.

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

If
d=0
, then
V_{d}=\{0\}
and
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)=\{0\}
. Thus one may take

M=1
, and the bound

M\leq(1+2L/\eta)^{d}

holds since both sides equal
1
. We may therefore assume that
d\geq 1
.
We first prove that
d_{C}(\alpha)
is finite. Since
C
is compact, it is totally bounded. Hence, for every
\alpha>0
, there exist points

c_{1},\dots,c_{J}\in C
such that

C\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}B(c_{j},\alpha)
and
J=N_{\alpha}(C)
.
Set
V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\operatorname{span}\{c_{1},\dots,c_{J}\}
. Then
\dim(V)\leq J
, and for every
\xi\in C
there exists
j\in\{1,\dots,J\}
such that

\|\xi-c_{j}\|\leq\alpha
. Since
c_{j}\in V
, we obtain

\operatorname{dist}(\xi,V)\leq\|\xi-c_{j}\|\leq\alpha
.
Taking the supremum over
\xi\in C
gives

\sup_{\xi\in C}\operatorname{dist}(\xi,V)\leq\alpha
.
Therefore
d_{C}(\alpha)\leq\dim(V)\leq N_{\alpha}(C)<\infty
.

It remains to prove the estimate on
M
. Since
V_{d}
is
d
-dimensional Hilbert, it is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with its Euclidean norm. Let
p^{\eta}=\{p_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
be a maximal
\eta
-separated subset of

V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
. By maximality,
p^{\eta}
is an
\eta
-net of

V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
. Since the balls

\{B_{V_{d}}\bigl(p_{m},\eta/2\bigr)\}_{m=1}^{M}
are pairwise disjoint, and since they are all contained in

B_{V_{d}}(0,L+\eta/2)
, then, upon comparing
d
-dimensional Euclidean volumes gives

M\,\operatorname{vol}_{d}\bigl(B_{V_{d}}(0,\eta/2)\bigr)\leq\operatorname{vol}_{d}\bigl(B_{V_{d}}(0,L+\eta/2)\bigr).

Since Euclidean ball volumes scale like
r^{d}
, this yields

M\leq(\frac{L+\eta/2}{\eta/2})^{d}=(1+\frac{2L}{\eta})^{d}
.
∎

5.1.4
Completing the Proof

We may now complete the proof of our main approximate-reconstruction formula.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
(Continued).

It remains to justify that the parameters
d
and
M
can be chosen with the stated bounds. Set

\alpha\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varepsilon/(16L)

and choose

d\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}d_{C}(\alpha)
.
By Lemma
5.4
, there exists a
d
-dimensional linear
subspace
V_{d}\subseteq H
satisfying

\sup_{\xi\in C}\operatorname{dist}(\xi,V_{d})\leq\alpha,

and

d=d_{C}\biggl(\frac{\varepsilon}{16L}\biggr)\leq N_{\varepsilon/(16L)}(C)<\infty
.
Next set

\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\varepsilon/(8D)
.
Again by Lemma
5.4
, the ball

V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
admits an
\eta
-net

p^{\eta}=\{p_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}
satisfying

M\leq\biggl(1+\frac{2L}{\eta}\biggr)^{d}=\biggl(1+\frac{16LD}{\varepsilon}\biggr)^{d}
.
We now verify that this choice of
V_{d}
and
p^{\eta}
gives the required reconstruction error. For
x\in H
and
p\in\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
, set

\Phi_{x}(p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\langle p,x\rangle+\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(\rho(\xi_{n})-\langle p,\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)
.
By Lemma
5.2
,

f_{N}(x)=\sup_{\|p\|\leq L}\Phi_{x}(p)
.
Let
P_{d}:H\to V_{d}
denote the orthogonal projection. For every
x\in C
and

p\in\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
, we have
P_{d}p\in V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
and
|\Phi_{x}(p)-\Phi_{x}(P_{d}p)|
can be controlled from above by
2L\alpha
since

\displaystyle|\Phi_{x}(p)-\Phi_{x}(P_{d}p)|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl|\langle p-P_{d}p,x-\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl|\langle p-P_{d}p,(I-P_{d})(x-\xi_{n})\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq L\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\|(I-P_{d})(x-\xi_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq L\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(\operatorname{dist}(x,V_{d})+\operatorname{dist}(\xi_{n},V_{d})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq 2L\alpha.

Consequently, if

f_{N}^{V_{d}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\sup_{p\in V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)}\Phi_{x}(p)
,
then

0\leq f_{N}(x)-f_{N}^{V_{d}}(x)\leq 2L\alpha
for each
x\in C
.
Next, since
p^{\eta}
is an
\eta
-net of
V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
, for every

p\in V_{d}\cap\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)
there exists
m\in\{1,\dots,M\}
such that

\|p-p_{m}\|\leq\eta
. Since
x,\xi_{n}\in C
, we have
\|x-\xi_{n}\|\leq D
, and hence

\displaystyle|\Phi_{x}(p)-\Phi_{x}(p_{m})|\leq\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl|\langle p-p_{m},x-\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr|\leq\eta D.

Thus,
0\leq f_{N}^{V_{d}}(x)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)\leq\eta D
for each
x\in C
.
Consequently, we deduce that

0\leq f_{N}(x)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(x)\leq 2L\alpha+\eta D=\frac{\varepsilon}{8}+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}=\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Therefore,

\sup_{\xi\in C}|f_{N}(\xi)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\xi)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
.

Finally, since
\mathbb{X}_{N}
is a
\delta
-net of
C
, for each
\xi\in C
there exists

n_{\xi}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
\|\xi-\xi_{n_{\xi}}\|\leq\delta
. Since
f_{N}
interpolates the data and both
\rho
and
f_{N}
are
L
-Lipschitz,

\displaystyle|\rho(\xi)-f_{N}(\xi)|\leq|\rho(\xi)-\rho(\xi_{n_{\xi}})|+|\rho(\xi_{n_{\xi}})-f_{N}(\xi_{n_{\xi}})|+|f_{N}(\xi_{n_{\xi}})-f_{N}(\xi)|\leq 2L\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence, we compute that

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in C}|\rho(\xi)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\xi)|\leq\sup_{\xi\in C}|\rho(\xi)-f_{N}(\xi)|+\sup_{\xi\in C}|f_{N}(\xi)-f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}(\xi)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}<\varepsilon.

Lastly, it is enough to set

N=N_{\delta}(C)=N_{\varepsilon/(4L)}(C),
and the bounds on
d
and
M
were proved above. Since
f_{N}^{p^{\eta}:V_{d}}
is a finite maximum of affine maps whose slopes have norm at most
L
, it is convex and
L
-Lipschitz on
H
.
∎

5.2
Proof of Neural Network Computation Result (Theorem
3.2
)

We now prove Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
f_{N}
be the convexified Whitney-McShane extension from
Lemma
5.1
. Then
f_{N}
interpolates
the data:

f_{N}(\xi_{n})=\rho(\xi_{n})=y_{n}
for each
n=1,\dots,N
.
Apply Lemma
5.3
to the compact set

K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N}\}
with accuracy
\varepsilon/2
. Then there exist

M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and directions
\{p_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}\subseteq\overline{B}_{H}(0,L)

such that the functional

H\ni x\mapsto\max_{1\leq m\leq M}\Bigg\{\langle p_{m},x\rangle+\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(y_{n}-\langle p_{m},\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)\Bigg\}

satisfies

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl|f_{N}(\xi_{n})-f_{\varepsilon,d}(\xi_{n})\bigr|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.
Since
f_{N}(\xi_{n})=\rho(\xi_{n})
, this gives

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\,\bigl|\rho(\xi_{n})-f_{\varepsilon,d}(\xi_{n})\bigr|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.
By
[
46
, Lemma 5.11]
, for every
q\geq 2
there exists
a
\operatorname{ReLU}
-MLP realizing the minimum of
q
inputs with size at most

16q
, width at most
3q
, and depth at most
\lceil\log_{2}(q)\rceil
. The same
lemma gives a
\operatorname{ReLU}
-MLP realizing the maximum with the same
complexity bounds. Applying this with
q=N
gives
\Psi_{N}
, and applying it
with
q=M
gives
\Phi_{M}
. If
N=1
or
M=1
, the corresponding map is the
identity and the claim is trivial.
Therefore, for every
m=1,\dots,M
, we know that

\Psi_{N}\Big(\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}\bigl(y_{n}-\langle p_{m},\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)\Big)=\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(y_{n}-\langle p_{m},\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr),

and hence, for every
x\in H
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{M}\Big(\bigoplus_{m=1}^{M}\left[\langle p_{m},x\rangle+\Psi_{N}\Big(\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}\bigl(y_{n}-\langle p_{m},\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)\Big)\right]\Big)=\max_{1\leq m\leq M}\Bigg\{\langle p_{m},x\rangle+\min_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigl(y_{n}-\langle p_{m},\xi_{n}\rangle\bigr)\Bigg\}.

This proves the asserted neural-network representation.
Finally, assume that
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
contains an

-net of

C
. Fix
\xi\in C
and choose
n_{\xi}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that

. Since both

are

\displaystyle|\rho(\xi)-f_{\varepsilon,d}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq|\rho(\xi)-\rho(\xi_{n_{\xi}})|+|\rho(\xi_{n_{\xi}})-f_{\varepsilon,d}(\xi_{n_{\xi}})|+|f_{\varepsilon,d}(\xi_{n_{\xi}})-f_{\varepsilon,d}(\xi)|

∎

5.3
Proof of Convexity and Lipschitzness Certificate (Theorem
3.3
)

We use the following little lemma.

Lemma 5.5

(Positive-weight PReLU-max networks are convex, monotone, and Lipschitz)
.

Let
T:\mathbb{R}^{d_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\displaystyle T(u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}{\bf A}^{(L-1)}z^{(L-1)}(u)+b^{(L-1)},

\displaystyle z^{(\ell+1)}(u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\max_{\Pi^{(\ell)}}\operatorname{PReLU}^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{\alpha}^{(\ell)}}\bigl({\bf A}^{(\ell)}z^{(\ell)}(u)+b^{(\ell)}\bigr),\qquad\mbox{ for: }\qquad\ell=0,\dots,L-2,

\displaystyle z^{(0)}(u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}u.

Assume that
{\bf A}^{(\ell)}
has nonnegative entries for every

\ell=0,\dots,L-2
, that
\mathbf{\alpha}^{(\ell)}\in[0,1]^{d_{\ell+1}^{\prime}}
for every

\ell=0,\dots,L-2
, and that
{\bf A}^{(L-1)}
is a nonnegative row vector.
Then
T
is convex and coordinate-wise non-decreasing. Moreover,

\operatorname{Lip}(T)\leq\|{\bf A}^{(L-1)}\|_{2\to 2}\,\big(\prod_{\ell=0}^{L-2}\|{\bf A}^{(\ell)}\|_{2\to 2}\big)
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

The input layer of the CNF may be written as

x^{(0)}=P(x)+q
,
where
q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}(q_{1},\dots,q_{M})\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
and
P:H\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
is the bounded linear map

P(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\bigoplus_{m=1}^{M}\langle p_{m},x\rangle_{H}
.
Let
T:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the finite-dimensional PReLU-max network obtained from the hidden and output layers of Definition
2.2
. Then

\Phi(x)=T(P(x)+q)
.
Now, by Lemma
5.5
,
T
is convex. Since
x\mapsto P(x)+q
is affine, convexity is preserved under this pre-composition. Hence, for every
x,y\in H
and
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\Phi(tx+(1-t)y)

\displaystyle=T(P(tx+(1-t)y)+q)

\displaystyle=T(t(P(x)+q)+(1-t)(P(y)+q))

\displaystyle\leq tT(P(x)+q)+(1-t)T(P(y)+q)

\displaystyle=t\Phi(x)+(1-t)\Phi(y).

Thus
\Phi
is convex.
To prove Lipschitzness, note that by Lemma
5.5
,

\operatorname{Lip}(T)\leq\|{\bf A}^{(L-1)}\|_{2\to 2}\Big(\prod_{\ell=0}^{L-2}\|{\bf A}^{(\ell)}\|_{2\to 2}\Big)
.
Therefore, for every
x,y\in H
,

\displaystyle|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)|

\displaystyle=|T(P(x)+q)-T(P(y)+q)|

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}(T)\|P(x)-P(y)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}(T)\|P\|_{H\to\ell_{2}^{M}}\|x-y\|_{H}.

Hence

\operatorname{Lip}(\Phi)\leq\|{\bf A}^{(L-1)}\|_{2\to 2}\Big(\prod_{\ell=0}^{L-2}\|{\bf A}^{(\ell)}\|_{2\to 2}\Big)\|P\|_{H\to\ell_{2}^{M}}
.
Finally, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for every
x\in H
we have

\|P(x)\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}|\langle p_{m},x\rangle_{H}|^{2}\leq\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|p_{m}\|_{H}^{2}\Big)\|x\|_{H}^{2}
.
Thus

\|P\|_{H\to\ell_{2}^{M}}\leq\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|p_{m}\|_{H}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}
,
which gives the following explicit Lipschitz bound

\operatorname{Lip}(\Phi)\leq\|{\bf A}^{(L-1)}\|_{2\to 2}\Big(\prod_{\ell=0}^{L-2}\|{\bf A}^{(\ell)}\|_{2\to 2}\Big)\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|p_{m}\|_{H}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

It remains to prove the exact computability claim. Let
f_{\varepsilon,d}
be as in Theorem
3.2
, and set

V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\operatorname{span}\{p_{1},\dots,p_{M}\}
.
For each
m=1,\dots,M
, define

q_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\Psi_{N}\Big(\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}\bigl(y_{n}-\langle p_{m},\xi_{n}\rangle_{H}\bigr)\Big)
.
Then the CNF input layer is exactly

x^{(0)}_{m}=\langle p_{m},x\rangle_{H}+q_{m}
.
Since
\operatorname{PReLU}_{1}
is the identity map, the max layer appearing in (
3.3
) is obtained by taking

L=2
,
d_{0}=M
,
d_{1}=1
,
d_{1}^{\prime}=M
,
{\bf A}^{(0)}=I_{M}
,
b^{(0)}=0
,
\mathbf{\alpha}^{(0)}=\mathbf{1}_{M}
, and
\Pi^{(0)}=\{[M]_{+}\}
.
Then

x^{(1)}=\max_{m\in[M]_{+}}x^{(0)}_{m}
.
Taking finally
{\bf A}^{(1)}=(1)
and
b^{(1)}=0
gives exactly
f_{\varepsilon,d}
.
This proves the claim.
∎
