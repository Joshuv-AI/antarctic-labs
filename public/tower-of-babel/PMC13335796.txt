Left atrial appendage thrombus after 2 days of postoperative atrial fibrillation
Authors: Hisato Takagi
Published: 2026

Summary

Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) for an 84-year-old woman with severe chest pain demonstrated type-A acute aortic dissection and no left-atrial-appendage thrombus (LAAT). The patient successfully underwent urgent ascending aortic replacement. Continuous electrocardiographic-telemetry monitoring confirmed sinus bradycardia until postoperative day (POD) 5. Atrial fibrillation (AF) emerged suddenly on POD 6 and continued for 2 days without anticoagulation. On POD 8, CECT revealed LAAT. CECT-determined LAAT (with preoperative confirmation of no LAAT) within a short term like the present case (definitively 8 days determined by the CT interval and probably 2 days inferred from the clinical evidence of AF occurrence and continuance) has never been reported.

Case description

An 84-year-old woman with gastroesophageal reflux disease and no hypertension complained of severe chest pain, and Stanford type-A acute aortic dissection (Figure 1A, asterisk) was diagnosed on CECT. The CT demonstrated no LAAT (Figure 1A, arrow). The patient successfully underwent urgent ascending aortic replacement. Continuous electrocardiographic-telemetry monitoring confirmed sinus bradycardia (50–60 beat/min) until POD 5. On POD 6, however, AF emerged suddenly. Although amiodarone of 150 mg was intravenously injected without attempting electrical cardioversion on the same day, restoration of sinus rhythm was unable to be achieved. Despite oral administration of amiodarone of 200 mg/day, AF continued for 2 days. No anticoagulant agent was initially prescribed. On POD 8 (2 days after the AF onset and 8 days after the initial CT determining no LAAT [Figure 1A, arrow]), postoperative follow-up CECT revealed LAAT (Figure 1B, arrow). Oral administration of edoxaban of 30 mg/day was initiated immediately after the diagnosis of LAAT (and has been continued ever since then). Because of falling into pre-shock state (blood pressure of 88/72 mm Hg and heart rate of 97 beat/min), electrical cardioversion was urgently performed despite not confirming resolution of LAAT one hour after administrating edoxaban, and sinus rhythm was restored. Although sinus rhythm was maintained afterward without thromboembolic events, LAAT was not resolved at discharge.

No LAAT (arrow) before the surgery for Stanford type-a acute aortic dissection (asterisk) (A) and LAAT (arrow) on POD 8 (2 days after the AF onset) (B).

Though we initially used no oral anticoagulant because of the limited evidence for prevention of ischaemic stroke in postoperative AF and the association with a high bleeding risk soon after cardiac surgery,1 a meta-analysis of observational cohort studies suggests a possible protective impact of oral anticoagulants in postoperative AF for all-cause mortality.2 Although the incidence of new-onset AF after cardiac surgery is approximately 25%,2,3 emerging LAAT in this setting has been little known. Merely one study4 of 362 patients undergoing transoesophageal echocardiography-guided cardioversion for atrial arrhythmias (AF, 69.9%) within 30 days (median, 6 days; interquartile range, 5–9 days) after cardiac surgery detected LAAT in 13 cases (3.6%). It is unclear in the study, however, whether or not the atrial arrhythmias newly developed postoperatively and LAAT was absent preoperatively. CECT-determined LAAT (with preoperative confirmation of no LAAT) within a short term like the present case (definitively 8 days determined by the CT interval and probably 2 days inferred from the clinical evidence of occurrence and continuance of AF) has never been reported. Astonishingly, a case with formation of LAAT during cardiopulmonary bypass despite full heparinization and adequate activated clotting time has been known, and the possible cause may be heparin-induced thrombocytopenia or surgical trauma to the appendage.5 In general, however, contact of the blood with the extracorporeal circuit and re-transfusion of thrombogenic blood aspirated from the pericardium are considered to lead to increased thrombogenicity.
