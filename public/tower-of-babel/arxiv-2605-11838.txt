Gradient Clipping Beyond Vector Norms: A Spectral Approach for Matrix-Valued Parameters
Alexander Yukhimchuk 1, Mladen Kolar 1,2, Martin Takáč 1, Sayantan Choudhury 1

Abstract
Gradient clipping is a standard safeguard for training neural networks under noisy, heavy-tailed stochastic gradients; yet, most clipping rules treat all parameters as vectors and ignore the matrix structure of modern architectures. We show empirically that data outliers often amplify only a small number of leading singular values in layer-wise gradient matrices, while the rest of the spectrum remains largely unchanged. Motivated by this phenomenon, we propose spectral clipping , which stabilizes training by clamping singular values that exceed a threshold while preserving the singular directions. This framework generalizes classical gradient norm clipping and can be easily integrated into existing optimizers. We provide a convergence analysis for non-convex optimization with spectrally clipped SGD, yielding the optimal 𝒪 ⁡ ( K 2 − 2 ​ α / 3 ​ α − 2 ) {\mathcal{O}}\left(K^{\nicefrac{{2-2\alpha}}{{3\alpha-2}}}\right) rate for heavy-tailed noise. To minimize hyperparameter tuning, we introduce layer-wise adaptive thresholds based on moving averages or sliding-window quantiles of the top singular values. Finally, we develop efficient implementations that clip only the top r r singular values via randomized truncated SVD, avoiding full decompositions for large layers. We demonstrate competitive performance across synthetic heavy-tailed settings and neural network training tasks.

1.1
Background and Related Works

Most deep learning tasks reduce to solving the minimization problem

\min_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f({\bm{x}})

and
SGD

[
53
]
is the most common approach for solving it. The update rule of
SGD
is given by

{\bm{x}}_{k+1}={\bm{x}}_{k}-\eta_{k}{\bm{g}}_{k}
,
where
\eta_{k}>0
is the step size and
{\bm{g}}_{k}
is an unbiased estimator of the gradient of
f

{\bm{x}}_{k}

\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{g}}_{k}\mid{\bm{x}}_{k}\right]=\nabla f({\bm{x}}_{k})

[
20

18

\text{clip}({\bm{g}}_{k},\tau):=\min\left\{1,\frac{\tau}{\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|}\right\}{\bm{g}}_{k}

[
34
]
,

which yields the update

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{k}-\eta_{k}\text{clip}({\bm{g}}_{k},\tau).

(1)

Here
\tau>0
is the clipping threshold, which ensures that
\|\text{clip}({\bm{g}}_{k},\tau)\|\leq\tau
for all

Gradient clipping was established as a practical remedy for exploding gradients in recurrent neural networks
[
41
,
46
]
and remains a standard stabilization tool in modern training pipelines. Beyond this classical motivation, a recent theoretical literature has clarified when clipping helps and what its limitations are.
34

67
,
51

49

(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness and instantiates this viewpoint in
Clipped Scion
. Additional related works about heavy tailed gradient noise and spectral computation could be found in Appendix
A

2
Gradient Clipping beyond Vector Norms

In this section, we first conduct an experiment in which we artificially add noise and check the influence of this noise on the spectrum of gradient matrices. Based on our observations in this experiment, we suggest a novel clipping technique in the second part. Finally, we show that our method is more general than the norm clipping method discussed in (
1
).

2.1
Singular Values of Gradient Matrices

Again, all the works mentioned above treat the gradients as vectors, while each layer of a neural network has a matrix structure, and their corresponding gradients are also matrices. To gain a deeper understanding, we study the gradients of these layer-wise gradients. Here, we consider the pretrained GPT-2 model
p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid\cdot)

[
52
]
. This model is already trained, and given a sequence of tokens
s_{[1:t-1]}
, it assigns a high probability to a
good
token; i.e.,
p_{\theta}(s_{t}\mid s_{[1:t-1]})
is high if
s_{t}
is good, and a lower probability to a
bad
token; i.e.,
p_{\theta}(s_{t}\mid s_{[1:t-1]})
is low if
s_{t}
is a bad token
[
61
]
. Now we implement backpropagation on
p_{\theta}
to compute the gradients of the parameters for both the good and bad sets of tokens.

In Figure
1
, we plot the distribution of the largest singular values of gradient matrices computed from mini-batches containing different numbers of bad tokens. When all tokens in the mini-batch are good, the singular values of the gradient matrix remain small. As the number of bad tokens in the mini-batch increases, the top singular values increase significantly.

We perform this analysis for each layer of GPT-2 and observe similar trends across all layers (see Appendix
G
for additional details). These observations lead us to consider a clipping technique that truncates the singular values of gradient matrices in order to address noise in modern deep learning.

2.2
Spectral Clipping of Gradient Matrices

Motivated by the empirical behavior observed in the previous section, we treat model parameters as matrices and clip the singular values of the corresponding stochastic gradient matrices. To elaborate on our technique, we consider the matrix-parameter optimization problem

\textstyle\min_{{\bm{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}f({\bm{X}}),

(2)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
. To solve this problem, we propose the update rule

\textstyle{\bm{X}}_{k+1}={\bm{X}}_{k}-\eta_{k}{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{G}}_{k}).

(3)

where
{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau_{k}}
is an operator that clamps the singular values of
{\bm{G}}_{k}
to a threshold
\tau_{k}>0
. For example, if the SVD of the gradient matrix is

{\bm{G}}_{k}={\bm{U}}_{k}\text{diag}(\bm{\sigma}_{k}){\bm{V}}_{k}^{\top}

(where
d\coloneqq\min\{m,n\},{\bm{U}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d},{\bm{V}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
contain the singular values) then
{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{G}}_{k}):={\bm{U}}_{k}\text{diag}(\clamp_{\tau}(\bm{\sigma}_{k})){\bm{V}}_{k}^{\top},
where the coordinates of the vector
\clamp_{\tau}(\bm{\sigma}_{k})
are given by

\textstyle\clamp_{\tau_{k}}(\bm{\sigma}_{k})[i]=\begin{cases}\bm{\sigma}_{k}[i]&\text{if }\bm{\sigma}_{k}[i]\leq\tau_{k}\\
\tau_{k}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4)

We call this technique spectral clipping. The operator
{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau_{k}}
leaves the singular directions
{\bm{U}}_{k},{\bm{V}}_{k}
unchanged and ensures
\sigma_{\max}({\mathcal{C}}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{G}}_{k}))\leq\tau_{k}
and
\|{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{d}\tau_{k}
.
As we observe, the top singular values of the gradient matrices spike in the presence of noise; this clipping technique can help in practice.

2.3
Gradient Norm Clipping as a Special
Case

We next show that standard gradient norm clipping is a special case of spectral clipping. Norm clipping treats parameters as vectors and updates using (
1
). Observe that any
vector
{\bm{g}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be viewed as a matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times 1}
, and its SVD is
{\bm{g}}_{k}={\bm{U}}_{k}\text{diag}(\bm{\sigma}_{k}){\bm{V}}_{k}^{\top}
, with
{\bm{U}}_{k}=\frac{{\bm{g}}_{k}}{\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\bm{\sigma}_{k}=\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|\in\mathbb{R}
, and
{\bm{V}}_{k}=1\in\mathbb{R}
. Then from (
4
), we have
\text{clamp}_{\tau_{k}}\left(\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|\right)=\min\{\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|,\tau_{k}\}
and thus
{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{g}}_{k})=\frac{{\bm{g}}_{k}}{\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|}\min\left\{\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|,\tau_{k}\right\}
. Plugging this into (
3
) gives

\textstyle{\bm{x}}_{k+1}={\bm{x}}_{k}-\eta_{k}{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{g}}_{k})={\bm{x}}_{k}-\eta_{k}\frac{{\bm{g}}_{k}}{\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|}\min\left\{\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|,\tau_{k}\right\}={\bm{x}}_{k}-\eta_{k}\min\left\{1,\frac{\tau_{k}}{\|{\bm{g}}_{k}\|}\right\}{\bm{g}}_{k},

which coincides with the norm-clipping update (
1
) (with
\tau_{k}=\tau
). Therefore, spectral clipping generalizes gradient norm clipping, and
any analysis of our method captures that of gradient norm clipping as a special case
.

3
Convergence Guarantees

In this section, we establish convergence guarantees for
SGD
with spectral clipping (
3
). For this section, we consider only the constant spectral clipping threshold, i.e.,
\tau_{k}=\tau
in (
3
). We consider the matrix optimization problem (
2
), and we make some standard assumptions on the function
f
and the stochastic gradient matrix
{\bm{G}}_{k}
.

3.1
.

We assume
f
is
L
-smooth i.e. for all
{\bm{X}},{\bm{Y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
we have

f({\bm{X}})\leq f({\bm{Y}})+\langle\nabla f({\bm{Y}}),{\bm{X}}-{\bm{Y}}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|{\bm{X}}-{\bm{Y}}\|_{F}^{2}.

This is a standard assumption in the optimization literature for the analysis of optimization algorithms
[
44
,
19
,
34
]
.
Moreover, we assume that the stochastic gradient estimates satisfy the bounded
\alpha
-moment assumption
[
68
,
28
,
11
,
12
]
, also called
p
-BCM.

3.2
.

We assume the gradient
{\bm{G}}_{k}
computed at each iteration
k
is unbiased i.e.
\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{G}}_{k}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]=\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})
and has bounded
\alpha
-moment i.e.

\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{\alpha}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\leq\sigma^{\alpha}

for some
\alpha\in(1,2]
and
\sigma>0
.

For
\alpha=2
, Assumption
3.2
reduces to the classical bounded-variance assumption
[
36
,
14
]
, and it is extensively used in optimization to study different stochastic algorithms.
However, the bounded variance assumption can be restrictive in modern applications. Many recent empirical studies suggest that the stochastic gradient noise in image classification
[
58
,
5
]
, large language models
[
68
,
2
]
, and reinforcement
[
17
]
tasks follows a heavy-tailed distribution. Assumption
3.2
accommodates such heavy-tailed behavior with
\alpha<2

[
68
,
43
]
. Under this assumption, we can show that
\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\leq 2\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+2^{4-\alpha}d^{1-\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}
(see Lemma
C.1
, Appendix
C
) for
d=\min\{m,n\}
and use this to prove the following bound on the iterates of
SGD
with spectral clipping (
3
).

3.3
.

Suppose Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
hold, and let
f_{*}\coloneqq\inf_{{\bm{X}}}f({\bm{X}})>-\infty
. Then for any
K\geq 1
,
SGD
with spectral clipping (
3
) and a constant step size
\eta_{k}=\eta:=\frac{1}{4L\sqrt{K}}
satisfies

\textstyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{16L(f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*})}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{C_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{8\sqrt{K}}+\frac{2}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Delta_{k}

(5)

where
\Delta_{k}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}\right]
and
C_{\alpha,d}:=2^{4-\alpha}d^{1-\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}
with
d=\min\{m,n\}
.

The first term in (
5
) is the standard optimization error for
L
-smooth objectives. The second term depends on
\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}
and captures the effect of stochastic noise after clipping. For
\alpha<2
, this term reflects how clipping controls the contribution of heavy-tailed gradient noise to the convergence rate. The last term is dependent on the clipping threshold
\tau
and measures the bias introduced by the nonlinear operator
\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{k}}
. Using conditional unbiasedness, we can rewrite the bias as
\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}]=\mathbb{E}[{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}]
,
so
\Delta_{k}
is small whenever clipping is rarely active (i.e.,
\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{G}}_{k})\approx{\bm{G}}_{k}
with high probability). A similar phenomenon of bias is observed in
SGD
with gradient norm clipping
[
34
]
. When clipping is disabled by taking
\tau_{k}=\infty
, we have
\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{k}}({\bm{G}}_{k})={\bm{G}}_{k}
and hence
\Delta_{k}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{G}}_{k}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}\right]=0
.

For
\alpha=2
, note that the second term on the right hand side of (
5
) becomes independent of the clipping threshold
\tau
. Since clipping can introduce bias through
\Delta_{k}
but does not improve the variance dependent second term of (
5
) when
\alpha=2
, the best bound is obtained by disabling clipping (
\tau_{k}=\infty
), thereby recovering the optimal
\mathcal{O}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{\sqrt{K}}})
convergence rate for non-convex minimization with
SGD

[
53
,
18
]
. However, when gradients have heavy-tailed noise, i.e.,
\alpha<2
, we need clipping to control both the second and third terms of (
5
) and achieve a better rate of convergence. We provide a detailed proof of Theorem
3.3
in Appendix
C.1
.

The bias due to a finite clipping threshold in (
5
), i.e.,
\frac{2}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Delta_{k}
, is unavoidable. However, we can choose the threshold
\tau
depending on the noise level
\sigma
to prove convergence.

3.4
.

Suppose Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
hold, and let
f_{*}\coloneqq\inf_{{\bm{X}}}f({\bm{X}})>-\infty
. Then, for any
K\geq 1
,
SGD
with spectral clipping (
3
), a constant step size
\eta_{k}=\eta\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{4dL}}
and clipping threshold
\tau\geq\max\{2,8\sigma\}
satisfy the following

\textstyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\phi\left(\|\nabla f({\bm{X}})\|_{F}\right)\right]\leq\frac{4(f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*})}{\eta K}+32\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}+8\eta dL\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}

(6)

where
\phi(t):=\min\{t,t^{2}\}
and
d=\min\{m,n\}
. Moreover, when the step size
\eta\leq\min\left\{\nicefrac{{1}}{{4dL}},\nicefrac{{1}}{{\tau^{\alpha}L}}\right\}
and the threshold
\tau\geq\max\left\{2,8\sigma,\sigma K^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3\alpha-2}}}\right\}
, we have

\textstyle\hskip-5.69054pt\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\phi\left(\|\nabla f({\bm{X}})\|_{F}\right)\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(K^{\frac{-2\alpha+2}{3\alpha-2}}\right).

(7)

Theorem
3.4
establishes the convergence of
SGD
with spectral clipping to a stationary point for a non-convex problem under Assumption
3.2
. Here we use
\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\min\{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F},\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\}\right]
as the potential function to measure convergence.
51
,
68
,
67
also uses the same potential function for the analysis of stochastic clipping algorithms. When the gradients are small, i.e.,
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\varepsilon\ll 1
, then
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\ll\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}
and
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}
are the dominant terms. Therefore, in this particularly relevant regime, (
7
) yields a convergence rate of
\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]={\mathcal{O}}\left(K^{\nicefrac{{-2\alpha+2}}{{3\alpha-2}}}\right)
. In particular, for
\alpha=2
, the right hand side of (
7
) becomes
{\mathcal{O}}\left(K^{\nicefrac{{-1}}{{2}}}\right)
, matching the optimal convergence rate for
SGD

[
36
,
18
]
.

Finally,
68
(Theorem 6) proves that the rate

\mathcal{O}\left(K^{\nicefrac{{-2\alpha+2}}{{3\alpha-2}}}\right)
is optimal for gradient norm clipping
under their setting. Since norm clipping is a special case of our spectral clipping strategy,
this provides evidence that the rate in (
7
) is tight for our clipping scheme as well.

4
Adaptive Clipping Strategies

As discussed in Theorem
3.3
, clipping introduces bias; therefore, we need to choose the threshold
\tau
to handle the noise
\sigma
and the bias
\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Delta_{k}
. In Theorem
3.4
, we show that one can choose
\tau
depending on the noise level
\sigma
to ensure convergence. However, in practice, we do not have any knowledge of
\sigma
. Moreover,
68
shows that the gradient noise distribution changes during the course of training. Therefore, we propose adaptive strategies for choosing the clipping thresholds. We describe our strategies below.

EMA of Singular Values.

The clipping threshold
\tau_{k}
can be chosen using an exponential moving average of the top singular values of the gradient matrix
{\bm{G}}_{k}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\hat{\tau}_{k-1}+(1-\theta)\sigma_{\max}({\bm{G}}_{k}),

\displaystyle\tau_{k}

\displaystyle=

/

^

τ

k

(1 -
θ

k+1

)

(8)

for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. In all our experiments, we use
\theta=0.9
, which places more weight on previous estimates and less on the current gradient. As discussed in the previous section, the top singular values of gradient matrices increase in the presence of noise. Therefore, when there is sudden noise in the gradients, it will not significantly distort our clipping threshold. Similar adaptive ideas were proposed by
[
68
]
for a norm based clipping method.

Quantile of Singular Values.

We also propose a quantile-based adaptive threshold computed over a sliding window of
w
top singular values, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tau_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{q}({\mathcal{S}}_{k-1}),

(9)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{S}}_{k-1}\setminus\sigma_{\max}\left({\bm{G}}_{k-w-1}\right)\right)\cup\sigma_{\max}\left({\bm{G}}_{k}\right)

(10)

for some
q\in(0,1]
. Here
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
is the set of the last
w
top singular values, and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{q}
denotes the quantile operator at level
q\in[0,1]
. Thus, (
9
) sets the clipping threshold based on the
q
-th quantile of the sliding window set
{\mathcal{S}}_{k-1}
, and (
10
) updates the window by discarding the outdated singular value
\sigma_{\max}\left({\bm{G}}_{k-w-1}\right)
and adding the new one
\sigma_{\max}\left({\bm{G}}_{k}\right)
. We then use this threshold
\tau_{k}
at the
k
-th iteration to clip the raw gradient matrix
{\bm{G}}_{k}
with operator
{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau_{k}}
. Note that our EMA based clipping strategy can still be sensitive to occasional extremely large singular values. However, this quantile-based rule can mitigate this issue by selecting the threshold based on the empirical distribution of the top singular values. In experiments, we use
q=0.85
and
w=100
.

In Figure
5(a)
(Appendix
E
), we compare spectral clipping with different choices of threshold
\tau_{k}
for the trace regression problem
[
31
]

\displaystyle\textstyle\min_{{\bm{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}f({\bm{X}}):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle{\bm{A}}_{i},{\bm{X}}\right\rangle-{\bm{b}}_{i}\right)^{2}

(11)

and plot the average gradient bias

5.3
Layer-wise Adaptive Clipping

In deep neural networks, different layers operate at different scales. Thus, using a single clipping threshold
\tau_{k}
at iteration
k
for the entire network can be suboptimal
[
9
,
68
]
. This can result in one of two scenarios: (1) over-clipping layers with small singular values, which results in slow optimization, or (2) under-clipping layers with large singular values, thus harming the stability of the algorithm.

To address this, we propose a layer-wise adaptive spectral clipping scheme in which each layer maintains its own adaptive clipping threshold. Let
{\bm{G}}_{k}^{(\ell)}
denote the gradient of layer
\ell\in\{1,\cdots,L\}
at iteration
k
. Then, for each layer
\ell
, we have an EMA-based clipping threshold
\tau_{k}^{(\ell)}
given by
\hat{\tau}_{k}^{(\ell)}=\theta\hat{\tau}_{k-1}^{(\ell)}+(1-\theta)\sigma_{\max}\left({\bm{G}}_{k}^{(\ell)}\right),\tau_{k}^{(\ell)}=\nicefrac{{\hat{\tau}^{(\ell)}_{k}}}{{(1-\theta^{k+1})}}

or a quantile-based clipping threshold given by
\tau_{k}^{(\ell)}={\mathcal{Q}}_{q}\left({\mathcal{S}}^{(\ell)}_{k-1}\right)
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}^{(\ell)}=\left({\mathcal{S}}^{(\ell)}_{k-1}\setminus\sigma_{\max}\left({\bm{G}}^{(\ell)}_{k-w-1}\right)\right)\cup\sigma_{\max}\left({\bm{G}}^{(\ell)}_{k}\right)
. These per-layer thresholds
\{\tau_{k}^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
are then used for spectral clipping of layer-wise gradient matrices
\{{\bm{G}}_{k}^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
using Algorithm
2
.

This layer-wise clipping scheme can have several practical advantages. First, it adapts the clipping threshold to each layer of the neural network, allowing aggressive clipping in layers with large singular values while avoiding unnecessary clipping in layers with small singular values. Secondly, the EMA-based and quantile-based updates of the clipping threshold can adapt during training (e.g., phase transitions), thus automatically increasing or decreasing
\tau_{k}^{(\ell)}
as the spectrum of
{\bm{G}}_{k}^{(\ell)}
evolves.

Appendix A
Further Related Works

Heavy-Tailed Gradient Noise.

A growing body of empirical evidence suggests that stochastic gradient noise in modern deep learning is often heavy-tailed
[
58
,
68
]
rather than in settings captured by the classical bounded variance assumption (Assumption
3.2
with
\alpha=2
). This observation has motivated clipping-based algorithms, adaptive clipping rules, and related normalization analyses under bounded
\alpha
-moment or high-probability assumptions
[
19
,
68
,
28
,
11
]
. In particular,
68
proposed
ACClip
, an adaptive coordinate-wise clipping rule based on moving-average thresholds, and
11
showed that clipping can substantially improve
AdaGrad
and
Adam
-type methods in heavy-tailed regimes.

Matrix Structure and Spectral Computations.

For a matrix
{\bm{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with rank
r
, the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
{\bm{A}}
is given by
{\bm{U}}\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\sigma}){\bm{V}}^{\top}
, where
{\bm{U}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
and
{\bm{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
have orthonormal columns and
\bm{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{r}
contains the singular values. The SVD plays an important role in many applications
[
69
]
.
Recent optimizer design has increasingly sought to preserve matrix structure rather than flatten parameters into vectors. Matrix-aware preconditioners such as
Shampoo
and
SOAP
use tensor structure or eigenspace-adapted second moments to construct richer updates
[
22
,
65
]
. Orthogonalization or norm-based methods such as
Muon
,
Scion
, and
ASGO
also operate directly on matrix-valued parameters, but they modify the update direction at every step via orthogonalization, norm-constrained LMOs, or structured preconditioning
[
29
,
48
,
4
,
55
]
.
The computation of the full decomposition is expensive and requires
{\mathcal{O}}(mn^{2})
work. Over the years, researchers have developed several techniques to reduce this cost for specific applications.
Randomized low-rank decompositions provide a standard route to scalable partial SVD computation
[
23
]
, and related low-rank ideas have recently been used in optimization systems such as GaLore
[
70
]
. Orthogonalization-based optimizers such as
Muon
typically avoid exact decompositions via Newton-Schulz
[
27
,
29
]
, while PolarExpress
[
3
]
develops faster GPU-friendly polar decomposition routines for this setting.
In this work, we utilize randomized truncated SVD
[
23
]
to clamp the singular values of the gradient matrix beyond a certain threshold.

\tau&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Using this notation, the update rule can be written as

\displaystyle{\bm{X}}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bm{X}}_{k}-\eta_{k}\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k}).

Then, from the smoothness property of the function
f
, we have

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})+\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\bm{X}}_{k+1}-{\bm{X}}_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|{\bm{X}}_{k+1}-{\bm{X}}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\bm{G}}_{k}\right\rangle-\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})-{\bm{G}}_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}.

Then, taking the expectation conditioned on
{\bm{X}}_{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right].

From the Lemma, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+C_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}

where
C_{\alpha,d}:=2^{4-\alpha}d^{1-\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}
. Thus, the above inequality provides us

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\left(2\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+C_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left(1-2\eta_{k}L\right)\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}LC_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{2}.

Now, let us define
\Delta_{k}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}\right]
. Then, by taking the total expectations on both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k})\right]-\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left(1-2\eta_{k}L\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\Delta_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}LC_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{2}.

Then, rearranging the terms, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left(1-2\eta_{k}L\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\right]+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\Delta_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}LC_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{2}.

Now, if we choose
\eta_{k}\leq\frac{1}{4L}
for all
k
, then we can lower bound the left hand side with
\frac{\eta_{k}}{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left(1-2\eta_{k}L\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{k}}{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\right]+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\Delta_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}LC_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{2}.

Now, summing up both sides for
k=0,1,\cdots K-1
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\frac{\eta_{k}}{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{0})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{K})\right]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\eta_{k}\Delta_{k}+\frac{LC_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\eta_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\eta_{k}\Delta_{k}+\frac{LC_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\eta_{k}^{2}.

The last line follows from
\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{K})\right]\geq f_{*}
. For constant step size
\eta_{k}=\eta
, we obtain.

\displaystyle\frac{\eta}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*}+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Delta_{k}+\frac{\eta^{2}LC_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}K}{2}.

Now divide both sides by
\frac{\eta K}{4}
to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4(f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*})}{\eta K}+\frac{2}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Delta_{k}+\frac{\eta LC_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{2}.

Therefore, by lower bounding the left hand side, we obtain

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4(f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*})}{\eta K}+\frac{2}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Delta_{k}+\frac{\eta LC_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{2}.

For
\eta=\frac{1}{4L\sqrt{K}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{16L(f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*})}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{2}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Delta_{k}+\frac{C_{\alpha,d}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}}{8\sqrt{K}}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

\tau&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Using this notation, the update rule can be written as

\displaystyle{\bm{X}}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bm{X}}_{k}-\eta_{k}\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k}).

Then, from the smoothness property of the function
f

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})+\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\bm{X}}_{k+1}-{\bm{X}}_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|{\bm{X}}_{k+1}-{\bm{X}}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\bm{G}}_{k}\right\rangle-\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})-{\bm{G}}_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}.

Then, taking the expectation conditioned on
{\bm{X}}_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right].

For further analysis, we will consider two cases: either
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}
or
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}
.

Case 1.
Suppose
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}
. Then we have the following bound

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}\leq\tau\right\}+\|{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}\leq\tau\right\}+d\tau^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+2\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}\leq\tau\right\}+d\tau^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+2\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\nicefrac{{3\tau}}{{2}}\right\}+d\tau^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+2(\nicefrac{{3\tau}}{{2}})^{2-\alpha}\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{\alpha}+d\tau^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}

Then, taking the expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+2(\nicefrac{{3\tau}}{{2}})^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}+d\tau^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

Moreover, for
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}
, we also have
\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}\leq\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\right\}
. Thus, using Markov’s Inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr[\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr[\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{\alpha}>(\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}})^{\alpha}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{\alpha}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]}{(\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}})^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\alpha}\tau^{-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}.

Thus, combining the bounds, we have shown

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+C_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}

where
C_{\alpha,1}:=2^{\alpha}d+3\cdot(2/3)^{\alpha-1}\leq 8d
. Similarly, we want to bound
\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{E}\left[{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\right\|_{F}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\right\|_{F}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}\leq\tau\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\right\|_{F}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\right\|_{F}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\right\|_{F}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\right\|_{F}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]^{2}

where the last inequality follows from the observation that, for
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}

\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\|_{F}>\tau\right\}\leq\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\right\}
. Then

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}\right\|_{F}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\right\|_{F}+\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\right)\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt4\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\right\|_{F}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]^{2}

where the last line follows from
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}
Then we apply Holder’s Inequality to get

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\right\|_{F}\mathbf{1}\left\{\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\right\}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{\alpha}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}\cdot\left(\Pr[\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}]\right)^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\left(\sigma^{\alpha}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}\cdot\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{\alpha}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]}{(\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}})^{\alpha}}\right)^{\nicefrac{{\alpha-1}}{{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\sigma\cdot\left(\frac{2\sigma}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha-1}.

Thus, by combining the bounds, we obtain.

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}

where
C_{\alpha,2}:=2^{2\alpha}\leq 16
. Hence, for the
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}
case, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left(1-2\eta_{k}L\right)\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}C_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}.

Then for
\eta_{k}=\eta\leq\frac{1}{4L}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{4}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}C_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{4}\min\left\{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F},\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right\}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}C_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}.

Case 2.
Now, consider
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}
. Similar to the previous case, we also have the following bound

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})+\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\bm{X}}_{k+1}-{\bm{X}}_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|{\bm{X}}_{k+1}-{\bm{X}}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}\|\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\left\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathcal{C}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\right\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau^{2}}{2}

Here, we take the expectation conditioned on
{\bm{X}}_{k}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle

\displaystyle-\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})-{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\rangle+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle

\displaystyle+\eta_{k}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\|\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})-{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]\|_{F}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle

\displaystyle+\eta_{k}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}({\bm{G}}_{k})-{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\|_{F}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle

\displaystyle+\eta_{k}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle

\displaystyle+\eta_{k}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\bm{G}}_{k}-\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{\alpha}\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle+\eta_{k}\sigma\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau^{2}}{2}.

where the second-to-last inequality follows from Jensen’s Inequality with
\alpha\in(1,2]
. Now we want to lower bound the term
\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle
when
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}
. Note that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{i}\cdot\min\left\{\sigma_{i},\tau\right\}

where
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
denotes the singular values of
\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})
. Now we consider two cases:

Case 1.

\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}<\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\tau
. In this case, as
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}\leq\tau
, all the singular values of
\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})
are less than
\tau
. Therefore

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}.

Case 2.

\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\tau
. In this case, For each singular value
\sigma_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\min\left\{\sigma_{i},\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{i}\tau}{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{i}\cdot\min\left\{\sigma_{i},\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}.

Therefore, we have shown
\langle\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\tau}(\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k}))\rangle\geq\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}
when
\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}>\nicefrac{{\tau}}{{2}}
. Finally, combining the bounds, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}\tau}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}+\eta_{k}\sigma\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}\tau}{2}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}+\eta_{k}\sigma\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}+\eta_{k}^{2}dL\tau\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left(\tau(1-2\eta_{k}dL)-2\sigma\right)\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}.

Here, we choose
\eta_{k}\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{4dL}}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}-2\sigma\right)\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}.

For
\tau\geq 8\sigma
, we can further bound it as shown below

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}\tau}{8}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}\tau}{8}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}C_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}.

For
\tau\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f({\bm{X}}_{k+1})\mid{\bm{X}}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{4}\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}C_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f({\bm{X}}_{k})-\frac{\eta_{k}}{4}\min\left\{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F},\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right\}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\frac{\eta_{k}^{2}L}{2}C_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}.

Finally, taking the total expectation, rearranging, summing up the inequality for
k=0,1,\cdots,K-1
, and dividing by
K
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\eta}{4K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\min\left\{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F},\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*}}{K}+\frac{\eta}{2}C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}C_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}.

Dividing both sides by
\nicefrac{{\eta}}{{4}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\min\left\{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F},\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4(f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*})}{\eta K}+2C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\eta LC_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}.

This implies

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\min\left\{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F},\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4(f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*})}{\eta K}+2C_{\alpha,2}\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\eta LC_{\alpha,1}\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4(f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*})}{\eta K}+32\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+8\eta dL\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}.

This completes the proof of the first part of the Theorem. Now, for the second part, consider
\eta\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{L\tau^{\alpha}}}
, which implies

\displaystyle 32\tau^{-2(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{2\alpha}+8\eta dL\tau^{2-\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 32d\frac{\sigma^{2\alpha}+\sigma^{\alpha}}{\tau^{2\alpha-2}}.

Thus, the above bound reduces to

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\min\left\{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F},\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4(f({\bm{X}}_{0})-f_{*})}{\eta K}+32d\frac{\sigma^{2\alpha}+\sigma^{\alpha}}{\tau^{2\alpha-2}}.

Here, we choose
\tau\geq\sigma K^{\frac{1}{3\alpha-2}}
to obtain

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\min\left\{\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F},\|\nabla f({\bm{X}}_{k})\|_{F}^{2}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(K^{\frac{-2\alpha+2}{3\alpha-2}}\right).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎
