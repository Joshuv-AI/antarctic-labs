1.
Introduction

The study of independent sets and their generalisations is a popular topic in graph theory. It is motivated by connections to combinatorics, statistical physics, and chemical graph theory. Given a graph
G
, an
independent vertex subset
is a set of vertices, no two of which are adjacent in
G
. The number of such sets in
G
, denoted by
\sigma_{0}(G)
, is often known as the Merrifield-Simmons index of
G
. It has been extensively studied since its introduction by Merrifield and Simmons
[
5
]
in the context of molecular structure analysis. This invariant, together with related quantities such as the Hosoya index and graph energy, has generated a rich body of extremal and structural results; see the survey
[
7
]
.

A natural and recent extension of independent sets is the notion of
k
-nearly independent vertex subsets
, where a subset of vertices induces exactly
k
edges. This generalisation provides more tools for the analysis of graph structure. The systematic study of
k
-nearly independent subsets was recently advanced (see
[
1
]
) by two of the authors of this article, who introduced and investigated the parameter
\sigma_{1}(G)
, the number of
1
-nearly independent vertex subsets, establishing foundational recursive relations and extremal bounds. Further developments were made in
[
3
]
, exploring the average size of such subsets and highlighting their combinatorial significance. This was followed by the Nordhaus-Gaddum-type results in
[
2
]
.

Beyond enumeration, comparative studies of different invariants have emerged as a powerful tool for understanding the interplay between different structural parameters. Classical examples include correlations (see
[
6
]
) between parameters and a ratio
[
4
]
. Such ratios often reveal more refined structural information than the single invariants themselves, and they have been used to characterise extremal graphs, compare graph classes, and study asymptotic behaviour. Motivated by these developments, we consider in this paper the ratio

Q(G)=\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\,.

We establish sharp lower and upper bounds, as well as identifying extremal graphs under various structural constraints. Our approach combines recursive techniques, decomposition methods, and inequalities derived from classical results on
\sigma_{0}(G)
and
\sigma_{1}(G)
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we start our preparation with preliminary results, including key recursive relations for
\sigma_{0}(G)
and
\sigma_{1}(G)
, as well as known extremal properties that will be used throughout the paper. In Section
3
, we provide lower bounds for the ratio
Q(G)
, first for connected graphs and then for more general graph classes, with particular attention to the role of maximum degree and graph decomposition. Section
4
is devoted to upper bounds, where we derive sharp inequalities for forests and trees using inductive techniques and refined structural lemmas. These results are further improved through alternative formulations that yield tighter bounds.

2.
Preliminary Results

In this section, we present some preliminary results that will be helpful in proving our main results.

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph,
v
a vertex of
G
, and
S
a set of vertices or edges of
G
. We denote by
G-S
the subgraph obtained by removing from
G
all elements of
S
along with their incident edges. We simply write
G-v
instead of
G-\{v\}
.

We represent by
N_{G}(v)=\{u:uv\in E(G)\}
the (open) neighbourhood of
v
in
G
and by
N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
the closed neighbourhood of
v
in
G
. As usual, the star and the path with
n
vertices are denoted by
S_{n}
and
P_{n}
respectively. For two vertex disjoint graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, we denote by
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
the graph with the vertex set
V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and edge set
E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})
, while we simply write
mG
instead of
G\cup G\cup\cdots\cup G
(
m
copies of
G
). By
\overline{G}
, we mean the complement of
G
, i.e. the graph with vertex and edge sets
V(G)
and
\{uv:~uv\notin E(G)\}
, respectively. We write
\overline{K_{n}}
for
nP_{1}
. The number of vertices (resp. edges) of
G
is referred to as the order (resp. size) of
G
.

The following well-known recursive relation will be used often.

Lemma 1

(
[
1
,
5
]
)
.

For any vertex
v
in a graph
G
we have

\sigma_{0}(G)=\sigma_{0}(G-v)+\sigma_{0}(G-N_{G}[v])\,,

and

\sigma_{1}(G)=\sigma_{1}(G-v)+\sigma_{1}(G-N_{G}[v])+\sum_{u\in N_{G}(v)}\sigma_{0}(G-N_{G}[v]-N_{G}[u]).

The next two results stated here as lemmas can be found in
[
1
,
7
]
.

Lemma 2

(
[
1
]
)
.

If
G
is a connected graph of order
n
, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G)\geq\sigma_{1}(S_{n})=n-1,

with equality if and only if
G=S_{n}
.

Lemma 3

(
[
7
]
)
.

If
G
is a connected graph of order
n
, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(G)\leq\sigma_{0}(S_{n})=2^{n-1}+1,

with equality if and only if
G=S_{n}
.

Lemma 4

(
[
7
]
)
.

If
G=\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}G_{i}
, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(G)=\sigma_{0}\left(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{r}G_{i}\right)=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{0}(G_{i}).

Lemma 5

(
[
1
]
)
.

If
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G)=\sigma_{1}(G_{1})\sigma_{0}(G_{2})+\sigma_{1}(G_{2})\sigma_{0}(G_{1}).

Lemmas
4
and
5
lead to the following lemma on the ratio
\sigma_{1}/\sigma_{0}
.

Lemma 6

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two vertex disjoint graphs. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{1}\cup G_{2})}=\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{1})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{1})}+\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{2})}\,.

In the next section, we present several lower bounds for
Q
, beginning with straightforward ones and progressing to more interesting ones.

3.
Lower bounds

The following theorem is trivial, we skip the proof.

Theorem 1
.

If
G
is a general graph of order
n
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}(\overline{K_{n}})}{\sigma_{0}(\overline{K_{n}})}=\frac{0}{2^{n}}=0\,,

with equality if and only if
G=\overline{K_{n}}
.

One might think from Theorem
1
that removing an edge from a graph
G can possibly reduce the ratio
\sigma_{1}/\sigma_{0}
, however this is not always the case. In fact if we consider the path
P_{4}
on
4
vertices, and
uv
its middle edge, then
P_{4}-uv=2K_{2}
and

\frac{\sigma_{1}(P_{4})}{\sigma_{0}(P_{4})}=\frac{5}{8}<\frac{6}{9}=\frac{\sigma_{1}(2K_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(2K_{2})}\,.

We now aim to determine the connected graph of order
n
that has the smallest
Q
.

Theorem 2
.

If
G
is a connected graph of order
n
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{n})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{n})}=\frac{n-1}{2^{n-1}+1}\,,

with equality if and only if
G=S_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be any connected graph of order
n
. By Lemma
2
we have
\sigma_{1}(G)\geq\sigma_{1}(S_{n})
, and by Lemma
3
we have
\sigma_{0}(S_{n})\geq\sigma_{0}(G)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{n})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{n})}=\frac{n-1}{2^{n-1}+1}\,.

To prove the characterisation of the equality case, suppose that
G
is not
S_{n}
and that
G
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}=\frac{n-1}{2^{n-1}+1}.

By Lemma
2
,
\sigma_{1}(G)\geq n
and by Lemma
3
,
2^{n-1}\geq\sigma_{0}(G)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{n}{2^{n-1}}\,.

However,

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2^{n-1}}-\frac{n-1}{2^{n-1}+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n}{2^{n-1}}+\frac{1}{2^{n-1}+1}\right)-\frac{n}{2^{n-1}+1}

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{n}{2^{n-1}+1}+\frac{1}{2^{n-1}+1}\right)-\frac{n}{2^{n-1}+1}

\displaystyle>0,

a contradiction. This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

Since
S_{n}
is a tree, we immediately have the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

If
T
is a tree of order
n
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(T)}{\sigma_{0}(T)}\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{n})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{n})}\,,

with equality if and only if
T=S_{n}
.

Our next result extends Theorem
2
to general (possibly disconnected) graphs. Before we get to it, we need to separately study the case where the maximum degree is
1
.

Proposition 1
.

If
G
is a graph with maximum degree
\Delta(G)=1
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{1}{3},

with equality if and only if
G=K_{2}\cup\overline{K_{n-2}}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph with maximum degree
\Delta(G)=1
. Then
G\cong(tK_{2})\cup\overline{K_{n-2t}}
for some integer
t\geq 1
. Thus, by Lemmas
4
and
5
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(G)=\sigma_{1}(tK_{2}\cup\overline{K_{n-2t}})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}(tK_{2})\sigma_{0}(\overline{K_{n-2t}})+\sigma_{0}(tK_{2})\sigma_{1}(\overline{K_{n-2t}})

\displaystyle=t(3)^{t-1}\cdot 2^{n-2t}+0\cdot 3^{t}=t(3)^{t-1}\cdot 2^{n-2t}\,,

and also

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(G)=\sigma_{0}(tK_{2}\cup\overline{K_{n-2t}})=\sigma_{0}(tK_{2})\sigma_{0}(\overline{K_{n-2t}})=3^{t}\cdot 2^{n-2t}\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}=\frac{\sigma_{1}(tK_{2}\cup\overline{K_{n-2t}})}{\sigma_{0}(tK_{2}\cup\overline{K_{n-2t}})}=\frac{t\cdot 3^{t-1}\cdot 2^{n-2t}}{3^{t}\cdot 2^{n-2t}}=\frac{t}{3}\geq\frac{1}{3}

for all
t\geq 1
. Equality holds if and only if
t=1
, that is,
G=K_{2}\cup\overline{K_{n-2}}
.
∎

We treat empty sums as
0
. Following Theorem
1
, we now obtain the
n
-vertex graph with the second-smallest value of
Q
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\geq 4
and
G\neq\overline{K_{n}}
be a graph of order
n
. It holds that

\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{n})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{n})}\,.

Proof.

Define
f(j)=(j-1)/(2^{j-1}+1)=Q(S_{j})
. Denote by
c_{2}
the number of connected components of
G
that have
2
vertices, and let
G_{1},\dots,G_{c_{3}}
be the connected components of
G
that have more than
2
vertices, with
n_{1},\dots,n_{c_{3}}
their respective orders. Note that isolated vertices do not affect the value of
Q
, meaning that
Q(H)=Q(H-w)
if
w
is an isolated vertex in a graph
H
.

By Lemma
6
alongside Theorems
1
and
2
, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{1}(c_{2}K_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(c_{2}K_{2})}+\sum_{i=1}^{c_{3}}\frac{\sigma_{1}(G_{i})}{\sigma_{0}(G_{i})}\geq c_{2}\frac{\sigma_{1}(K_{2})}{\sigma_{0}(K_{2})}+\sum_{i=1}^{c_{3}}\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{n_{i}})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{n_{i}})}=c_{2}f(2)+\sum_{i=1}^{c_{3}}f(n_{i})\,.

Note that

f(1)=0<f(2)=\frac{1}{3}<f(3)=\frac{2}{5}>f(4)=\frac{3}{9}=f(2)

and that

f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2^{x-1}+1-\ln(2)2^{x-1}(x-1)}{(2^{x-1}+1)^{2}}=\frac{2^{x-1}(1-\ln(2)(x-1))+1}{(2^{x-1}+1)^{2}}<0\text{ for all real }x\geq 4\,,

as
1-\ln(2)(x-1)<-1
for
x\geq 4
. So
f(i)
, restricted to integers
i
, decreases from
i=3
.

If
c_{3}\neq 0
, then by (
1
),

\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{c_{3}}f(n_{i})\geq f\left(n_{1}\right)\geq f(n)=\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{n})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{n})}\,.

Otherwise,
c_{3}=0
. With
G\neq\overline{K_{n}}
, the graph
G
is of maximum degree
1
. By Proposition
1
, we have

\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{1}{3}=f(4)\geq f(n)=\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{n})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{n})}\,,

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

It is striking that the
n
-vertex graphs with smallest value of
Q
has maximum degree
0
, while the one with the second-smallest has maximum degree
n-1
.

To end this section, we provide a generalisation of Proposition
1
for arbitrary maximum degree
\Delta
.

Theorem 4
.

If
G
is a graph with maximum degree
\Delta
, then

\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{3},\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{\Delta+1})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{\Delta+1})}\right\}\,.

Equality happens if and only if
G
is
S_{\Delta+1}\cup\overline{K_{|V(G)|-\Delta-1}}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the order
n\geq\Delta+1
of
G
. For
n=\Delta+1
,
G
is connected. We apply Theorem
2
and obtain

\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{n})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{n})}\,,

as desired. Suppose that the statement holds whenever
\Delta+1\leq n<k
for some integer
k
. Now assume that
n=k
. By Lemma
6
, we can take the ratio
Q
of one connected component of
G
as a lower bound. Hence, we can assume that
G
is connected.

Let
v
be a vertex of degree
\Delta
in
G
. If
G-v
has no edge, then
G
is a star and the theorem holds trivially. Otherwise, if an edge is also present in
G-N_{G}[v]
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)+\sigma_{1}(G-N_{G}[v])+\sum_{x\in N_{G}(v)}\sigma_{0}(G-N_{G}[x]-N_{G}[v])}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)+\sigma_{0}(G-N_{G}[v])}

\displaystyle>\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)+\sigma_{1}(G-N_{G}[v])}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)+\sigma_{0}(G-N_{G}[v])}=\rho\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)}+(1-\rho)\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-N_{G}[v])}{\sigma_{0}(G-N_{G}[v])}

with
\rho=\sigma_{0}(G-v)/(\sigma_{0}(G-v)+\sigma_{0}(G-N_{G}[v]))
. Let
\Delta_{1}
an
\Delta_{2}
be the maximum degrees of
G-v
and
G-N_{G}[v]
, respectively. The induction hypothesis implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}

\displaystyle>\min\left\{\frac{1}{3},\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{\Delta_{1}+1})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{\Delta_{1}+1})},\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{\Delta_{2}+1})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{\Delta_{2}+1})}\right\}\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{3},\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{\Delta+1})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{\Delta+1})}\right\}\,,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
f(j)=(j-1)/(2^{j-1}+1)=Q(S_{j})
, restricted to integers
j
, decreases from
j=3
(see the proof of Theorem
3
).

Now, we assume that
G-N_{G}[v]
has no edge. Since

|V(G-N_{G}[x]-N_{G}[v])|\geq n-2\Delta

by the choice of vertex
v
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)+\sum_{x\in N_{G}(v)}\sigma_{0}(G-N_{G}[x]-N_{G}[v])}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)+\sigma_{0}(G-N_{G}[v])}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)+\Delta 2^{n-2\Delta}}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)+2^{n-(\Delta+1)}}=\rho\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)}+(1-\rho)\frac{\Delta 2^{n-2\Delta}}{2^{n-(\Delta+1)}}

\displaystyle=\rho\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)}+(1-\rho)\frac{\Delta}{2^{\Delta-1}}>\rho\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)}+(1-\rho)\frac{\Delta}{2^{\Delta}+1}

with
\rho=\sigma_{0}(G-v)/(\sigma_{0}(G-v)+2^{n-(\Delta+1)})
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}>\rho\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)}+(1-\rho)\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{\Delta+1})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{\Delta+1})}.

With
\Delta_{1}
being the maximum degree in
G-v
(so
\Delta_{1}\leq\Delta
), using the induction assumption, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(G)}{\sigma_{0}(G)}

\displaystyle>\rho\frac{\sigma_{1}(G-v)}{\sigma_{0}(G-v)}+(1-\rho)\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{\Delta+1})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{\Delta+1})}\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{3},\frac{\sigma_{1}(S_{\Delta+1})}{\sigma_{0}(S_{\Delta+1})}\right\}\,.

Our arguments also show that the case of equality can occur only if
G-v
has no edge, i.e.
G
is a star on
1+\Delta(G)
vertices. Since isolated vertices do not affect the value of
Q
, the equality case is proved.
∎

In the next section, we focus on finding upper bounds on
Q
.

4.
Upper bounds

We now aim to prove the following sharp upper bound.

Theorem 5
.

Let
F
be a forest of order
n\geq 1
. We have

Q(F)\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(n-1\right)\,.

The equality is reached for
F\in\{P_{1},P_{2}\}
.

Before we prove the theorem, let us show two lemmas.

Lemma 7
.

If
T
is a tree of order
n\geq 2
and
v
a leaf of
T
, then it holds that

\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}\leq 1-\frac{1}{2^{n-2}+1}\,.

Proof.

Let
v
be a leaf attached to
u
in an
n
-vertex tree
T
. We have

(2)

\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}=1-\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)-\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}\,.

Moreover,
\sigma_{0}(T-v)=\sigma_{0}\left(T-v-u\right)+\sigma_{0}\left(T-v-N_{T}[u]\right)
. Thus

\sigma_{0}(T-v)-\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)=\sigma_{0}\left(T-v-N_{T}[u]\right)=\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[u]\right)\geq 1\,.

Identity (
2
) implies that

\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}\leq 1-\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}\,.

Since
T-v
is a tree,

\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)\leq\sigma_{0}\left(S_{n-1}\right)=1+2^{n-2}\,.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 8
.

If
T
is a tree and
v
a leaf of
T
, then it holds that

Q(T)\leq\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}Q(T-v)+1+Q\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}{1+\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}}\,.

Proof.

From

\sigma_{1}(T)=\sigma_{1}\left(T-v\right)+\sigma_{1}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)+\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[u]\right)\,,

where
u
is the vertex adjacent to
v
in
T
, together with

\sigma_{0}(T)=\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)+\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)\,,

we have

Q(T)=\frac{\sigma_{1}(T)}{\sigma_{0}(T)}=\frac{\sigma_{0}(T-v)Q(T-v)+\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)Q\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)+\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[u]\right)}{\sigma_{0}(T-v)+\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}.

The graph
T-N_{T}[u]
is a subgraph of
T-N_{T}[v]
, so
\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[u]\right)\leq\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)
. This shows that

Q(T)\leq\frac{\sigma_{0}(T-v)Q(T-v)+\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)Q\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)+\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}{\sigma_{0}(T-v)+\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}\,,

thus proving the lemma, upon dividing through by
\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)
. ∎

We can now prove Theorem
5
.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

We proceed by induction on the order of the forest
F
. Let
n=|F|:=|V(F)|
. If
n=1
, then
F=P_{1}
,
Q(F)=Q(P_{1})=0
, and
0=\frac{1}{3}(1-1)
. If
n=2
, then
F=P_{2}
or
F=P_{1}\cup P_{1}
. We have

Q(P_{2})=\frac{1}{3}
, while
\frac{1}{3}\left(2-1\right)=\frac{1}{3}
. Also

Q(P_{1}\cup P_{1})=0<\frac{1}{3}\left(2-1\right)
.

Assume that the theorem holds for all forests with order less than or equal to
n-1
, for some
n\geq 3
. Let
F
be a forest of order
n
. If
F
is not connected , then
F=\cup_{i=1}^{m}T_{i}
, for some
m\geq 2
. In this case,

Q(F)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Q(T_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{3}\left(|T_{i}|-1\right)=\frac{1}{3}|T|-\frac{1}{3}m<\frac{1}{3}|T|-\frac{1}{3}\,.

So, assume
F
is a tree
T
.
Set
a:=\frac{1}{3}
. By Lemma
8
together with the induction hypothesis, we get

\displaystyle Q(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}Q(T-v)+1+Q\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}{1+\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}(a(n-2))+1+a(n-3)}{1+\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}}\,.

Using Lemma
7
, that is

\frac{\sigma_{0}\left(T-v\right)}{\sigma_{0}\left(T-N_{T}[v]\right)}\geq\frac{1}{\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{2^{n-2}+1}}=\frac{2^{n-2}+1}{2^{n-2}}\,,

together with the fact that the function
x\mapsto(Ax+B)/(1+x)
is decreasing if
A<B
, we obtain

Q(T)\leq\frac{\displaystyle\frac{2^{n-2}+1}{2^{n-2}}\left(a(n-2)\right)+1+a(n-3)}{1+\displaystyle\frac{2^{n-2}+1}{2^{n-2}}}\,.

Set
c_{n}:=(2^{n-2}+1)/2^{n-2}
. We have

Q(T)\leq\frac{c_{n}\left(an-a-a\right)+an-2a-a+1}{1+c_{n}}.

We finish the proof by showing that

c_{n}\left(an-a-a\right)+an-2a-a+1\leq\left(1+c_{n}\right)\left(an-a\right)\,.

This simplifies to

\displaystyle ac_{n}+2a-1\geq 0\,.

Since
c_{n}\geq 1
for all positive integers
n
, it suffices to show that
a(1)+2a-1\geq 0
, that is
a\geq\frac{1}{3}
,
which is indeed true.
∎

Now we improve on the upper bound, by using a different approach.

Lemma 9
.

Let
v
be a leaf of a tree
T
attached to a vertex
u
. We have

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(T)

\displaystyle=2\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[v])+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[u])\,,

\displaystyle Q(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[v])}{\sigma_{0}(T)}\cdot\frac{1}{2}(Q(T-v)+Q(T-N_{T}[v]))+\frac{\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[u])}{\sigma_{0}(T)}(1+Q(T-v))\,.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, we have
\sigma_{0}(T)=\sigma_{0}(T-v)+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[v])
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(T-v)=\sigma_{0}((T-v)-u)+\sigma_{0}((T-v)-N_{T}[u])=\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[v])+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[u])\,.

Thus
\sigma_{0}(T)=2\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[v])+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[u])
. Using Lemma
1
for
\sigma_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(T)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}(T-v)+\sigma_{1}(T-N_{T}[v])+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[u])

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}(T-v)Q(T-v)+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[v])Q(T-N_{T}[v])+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[u])

\displaystyle=\big(\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[v])+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[u])\big)Q(T-v)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[v])Q(T-N_{T}[v])+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[u])

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[v])\big(Q(T-v)+Q(T-N_{T}[v])\big)+\sigma_{0}(T-N_{T}[u])(1+Q(T-v))\,.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Our final theorem reads as follows.

Theorem 6
.

Let
F
be a forest of order
n\geq 1
. We have the following:

\displaystyle Q(F)\leq\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{1}{6}\,.

Proof.

The theorem clearly holds for
n\leq 2
(with equality for
n=2
). Consider a forest
F
of order
n\geq 3
. For simplicity, set
a:=1/4
and
c:=1/6
. If
F
is not connected, then let
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{m}
, for some integer
m\geq 2
, denote all its connected components, with orders
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m}
, respectively. Since

Q(F)=Q(T_{1})+Q(T_{2})+\cdots+Q(T_{m})\,,

we have

\displaystyle Q(F)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}(a\cdot n_{j}-c)=a\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{i}\right)-m\cdot c=a\cdot n-m\cdot c

by the induction hypothesis. Since
m\geq 2
,

Q(F)\leq a\cdot n-2c<a\cdot n-c\,.

If
F
is connected, then we fix a leaf
v
attached to a vertex
u
in
F
, and invoke Lemma
9
to obtain

\displaystyle Q(F)=\rho\cdot\frac{1}{2}(Q(F-v)+Q(F-N_{F}[v]))+(1-\rho)(1+Q(F-v))

with

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{2\sigma_{0}(F-N_{F}[v])}{2\sigma_{0}(F-N_{F}[v])+\sigma_{0}(F-N_{F}[u])}\,.

The induction hypothesis yields

\displaystyle Q(F)\leq

\displaystyle~\rho\cdot\frac{1}{2}\big(a(n-1)-c)+a(n-2)-c\big)+(1-\rho)(1+a(n-1)-c)

\displaystyle=\rho\big(a(n-1)-c-\frac{1}{2}a\big)+(1-\rho)(1+a(n-1)-c)

\displaystyle=a\cdot n-c+1-a-\rho\left(1+\frac{1}{2}a\right)\,.

Thus, it suffices to prove that

1-a-\rho\left(1+\frac{1}{2}a\right)\leq 0\,,

or equivalently (using
a=1/4
),

\frac{2d}{2d+1}=\rho\geq\frac{1-a}{1+a/2}=\frac{2-2a}{2+a}

with

d=\frac{\sigma_{0}(F-N_{F}[v])}{\sigma_{0}(F-N_{F}[u])}\,.

In other words,

d\geq\frac{1-a}{3a}=1\,,

which is indeed true, since
F-N_{F}[u]
is a subgraph of
F-N_{F}[v]
. The inductive argument and thus the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎
