THE POULTRYMAN
7
Friday, January 7, 1966
New broiler
house to have
108,000 birds
SEAFORD, Del.—A broiler house,
which is one-eighth of a mile long,
3 stories high and has a capacity
of 108,000 birds, is being built here
by Allen’s Hatchery.
Designed to cope with the prob-
lems of the hatchery’s broiler grow-
out operation, the house will be
cared for by one man and his
family.
Warren Allen of Seaford, a part-
ner in the hatchery, said the pres-
ent house was decided upon after
he and his 2 brothers obtained ex-
cellent results from a 41,000 ca-
pacity 3-decker house built 18
months ago.
The new house is big and modern
in every way. It is 660 feet long,
40 feet wide, and will house 38,000
broilers on each floor, 2000 per pen.
The house is mechanically venti-
lated with overhead ducts^ boasts
automatic feeders and a freight
elevator and has 3 sets of stairs.
Clearance on the floor is 9 feet
so a spreader truck can drive
through. This means easy cleaning
Allen pointed out. ’
Manure from the second and third
floors will be pushed by shovels and
brooms to trap doors in the floor
so it can fall directly to the spreader
truck.
On the first floor, manure will be
picked up with a front loader on
a tractor and carried to the end
of the building where it can be
loaded on the spreader truck.
Allen noted 2 other advantages of
the house:
1. Triple deckers have solid floors
of concrete and wood which are
level. Water troughs can be ad-
justed right down to the litter. The
Allens do not use gallon water jars
to start their flocks.
2. There is a significant saving in
fuel costs because of the insulating
effect of the second and third floors.
