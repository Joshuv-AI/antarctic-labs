Abstract
. We study random Schrödinger operators on closed Riemannian manifolds with Anderson-type potentials. We prove high-probability spectral inclusion bounds showing that eigenvalues remain close to those of the Laplacian, with deviations controlled by a norm of the potential coefficients. Compared with deterministic bounds, this yields a square-root cancellation gain. The proof is based on a general principle showing that randomisation improves operator norm bounds for multiplier-type operators, which we formulate in both discrete and continuous settings.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Random Schrödinger operators

On a closed (i.e., compact, boundaryless) Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
d\geq 2
, we consider the random Schrödinger operator

(1.1)

\displaystyle H_{\omega,\lambda}=-\Delta_{g}+V_{\omega,\lambda}\quad\text{on }L^{2}(M),

where
V_{\omega,\lambda}
is an Anderson-type potential built from bumps at scale
\lambda^{-1}
.
More precisely, we assume that

(1.2)

\displaystyle V_{\omega,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N(\lambda)}\omega_{j}v_{j}(\lambda)\psi_{j}(x;\lambda),\quad x\in M,

where the functions
\psi_{j}(\cdot;\lambda)
are uniformly bounded and have bounded overlap, with spatial support at scale
\lambda^{-1}
,
v_{j}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{C}
are deterministic coefficients, the random variables
\omega_{j}
are i.i.d. symmetric Bernoulli (Rademacher) or centred normalised Gaussians,
\lambda\geq 2
is a large parameter (the inverse randomisation length), and
N(\lambda)\leq C\lambda^{d}
.

Since
M
is compact, the spectrum of
-\Delta_{g}
is discrete

\spec(-\Delta_{g})=\{\lambda_{k}^{2}\}_{k=0}^{\infty},\qquad 0=\lambda_{0}^{2}\leq\lambda_{1}^{2}\leq\cdots,\qquad\lambda_{k}^{2}\to\infty.

Our first main result gives a high-probability bound on the location of the eigenvalues of
H_{\omega,\lambda}
, up to energy
\lambda^{2}
,
in terms of the
\ell^{2q}
-norm of
v(\lambda)=(v_{j}(\lambda))_{j=1}^{N(\lambda)}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
N\geq 1
. Then there exists a constant
C_{N}
such that for every
d/2\leq q\leq\infty
, every
\lambda\geq 2
, and every
K\geq 1
, with probability at least
1-\exp(-K^{2})
,
we have

\{z\in\spec(H_{\omega,\lambda}):|z|\leq\lambda^{2},\,\dist(z,\spec(-\Delta_{g}))\geq\lambda^{-N}\}\subset\bigcup_{\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda}D(\lambda_{k}^{2},C_{N}Kr_{k})\cup\Omega,

where

(1.3)

\displaystyle r_{k}:=(1+\lambda_{k})^{\frac{d(q+1)}{2q}-\mu(q)}\,\lambda^{-\frac{d(q+1)}{2q}}(\log\lambda)^{11/2}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Omega:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|^{1/2}(1+|z|)^{-\frac{d(q+1)}{4q}+\frac{\mu(q)}{2}}\leq C_{N}K\,\lambda^{-\frac{d(q+1)}{2q}}(\log\lambda)^{11/2}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}}\},

and

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mu(q):=\begin{cases}1,&1\leq q\leq\frac{d+1}{2},\\
\frac{d+1}{4q}+\frac{1}{2},&\frac{d+1}{2}\leq q\leq\infty.\end{cases}

We postpone further remarks concerning Theorem
1.1
to the end of the introduction, including a comparison with the deterministic results of
[
Cue26
]
.

1.2.
Multiplier-type operators

The main new ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is an abstract operator-theoretic principle showing that randomisation improves norm bounds for multiplier-type operators of the form
T_{1}^{*}VT_{2}
, where
V
is a multiplication operator and
T_{1},T_{2}
are structured linear operators, for instance spectral projectors, resolvents, or Fourier extension operators.

Random multiplier estimates play a crucial role in the work of Schlag–Shubin–Wolff
[
SSW02
]
and Bourgain
[
Bou02
,
Bou03
]
on weakly disordered random Schrödinger operators on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. In Schlag–Shubin–Wolff, the relevant bounds are obtained by exploiting geometric considerations involving curvature. Bourgain introduced a different approach, based on a general entropy bound, which works in arbitrary dimension and does not rely on curvature. On the other hand, Bourgain’s method makes essential use of the Rademacher or Gaussian distribution of the random variables
\omega_{j}
.

Our contribution is a general abstract framework inspired by Bourgain’s approach. An instance of such a principle for operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
was established in
[
CM25
]
. In the present paper, we develop this idea in a substantially broader form.

We formulate the abstract results in two settings. The first is a discrete model, where a function on a finite set is randomised over the elements of a partition. The second is a continuous analogue on Ahlfors regular metric measure spaces, where the randomisation is performed on Voronoi cells associated with a finite set. These results are of independent interest and form the abstract core of the paper.

Theorem 1.2

(Discrete abstract estimate)
.

For
i=1,2
, let
\mathcal{H}_{i}
be complex separable Hilbert spaces,
E
a nonempty finite set, and consider bounded linear operators

S_{i}:\mathcal{H}_{i}\to\ell^{\infty}(E),

which we also view as operators into
\ell^{p^{\prime}}(E)
since
E
is finite.
Let
\{E_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a partition of
E
, that is,

E=\bigcup_{j\in J}E_{j},\qquad E_{j}\cap E_{k}=\emptyset\quad\text{for }j\neq k.

Let
v\in\ell^{\infty}(E)
, set
v_{j}:=v\mathbf{1}_{E_{j}}
, and let
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be i.i.d. symmetric Bernoulli or centred normalised Gaussian random variables. Define

(1.6)

\displaystyle v_{\omega}(x)=\omega_{j}v(x),\qquad x\in E_{j}.

Let
2\leq q\leq\infty
and
1\leq p\leq 2\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty
satisfy
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
. Then

(1.7)

\displaystyle\!\!\mathbf{E}\|S_{1}^{*}v_{\omega}S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}}\lesssim(\log\#E)^{\frac{5}{2}}\|S_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\ell^{p^{\prime}}(E)}\|S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\ell^{\infty}(E)}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\|v_{j}\|_{\ell^{p}(E_{j})}^{2q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2q}},

with the obvious modification for
q=\infty
.

Here and below, we use the notation
A\lesssim B
for functions
A,B\geq 0
to indicate that there is a constant
c
such that
A\leq cB
. If
c
depends on a parameter
\tau
, we may write
A\lesssim_{\tau}B
, but the dependence on
d,M
is usually ignored in the notation.

Remark 1.3
.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\|v_{j}\|_{\ell^{p}(E_{j})}^{2q}\Big)^{1/2q}\leq\sup_{j\in J}(\#E_{j})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2q}}\|v\|_{\ell^{2q}(E)}.

In particular, if all the
E_{j}
are singletons, then the right-hand side is just
\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}(E)}
.

We now state a continuous version of Theorem
1.2
. All technical notions appearing in the statement are defined in Section
3.2
. A precise and stronger statement is given in Theorem
3.16
.

Theorem 1.4

(Continuous abstract estimate)
.

Let
X
be an Ahlfors
d
-regular metric measure space, and let

T_{i}:\mathcal{H}_{i}\to L^{\infty}(X)\cap L^{p^{\prime}}(X),\qquad i=1,2,

be bounded linear operators, where
\mathcal{H}_{i}
are complex separable Hilbert spaces. Let
\Lambda
be a finite, maximal
r
-separated subset of
X
, for some
r>0
, and let
V_{\omega}
be the randomisation, by i.i.d. symmetric Bernoulli or centred normalised Gaussian random variables, of a measurable complex-valued function
V\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X)
on the Voronoi cells associated with
\Lambda
. Assume moreover that
X
,
\Lambda
, and the ranges of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
satisfy the uniform reparametrisation and local constancy hypotheses stated in Section
3.2
. Then, whenever
2\leq q\leq\infty
and
1\leq p\leq 2\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty
satisfy
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|T_{1}^{*}V_{\omega}T_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{d/p}(\log\#\Lambda)^{5/2}\|T_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to L^{p^{\prime}}(X)}\|T_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to L^{\infty}(X)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big(\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,2r))}^{2q}\Big)^{1/2q},

with the obvious modification for
q=\infty
.

In the compact-manifold setting, there are two principal choices of
T_{1},T_{2}
for which this theorem becomes effective.

(i) Spectral projectors.

Let

P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}},\qquad\Pi_{\lambda}=\mathbf{1}(P\in[\lambda,\lambda+1]).

The range of
\Pi_{\lambda}
is locally constant at wavelength scale
r=\lambda^{-1}
, and Sogge’s spectral cluster bounds give

\|\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d-1}{2(d+1)}}.

Inserting these estimates into Theorem
1.4
yields the randomised spectral cluster estimate

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\Pi_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega,\lambda}\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}(\log\lambda)^{5/2}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{d+1}},

see Proposition
4.1
.
This is the Riemannian analogue of the random Fourier extension estimate
[
CM25
, Section 6]
. By sub-Gaussian tail bounds (see Lemma
2.2
), it follows that with probability at least
1-\exp(-K^{2})
,

\left|\int_{M}|\Pi_{\lambda}u|^{2}V_{\omega,\lambda}\mathrm{d}v_{g}\right|\lesssim K\lambda^{-1}(\log\lambda)^{5/2}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{d+1}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2},

while the deterministic bound holds only with the
{\ell^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}
norm, without the logarithm.

Whereas
L^{2}\to L^{p^{\prime}}
bounds measure the size of level sets of
\Pi_{\lambda}u
, weighted expressions of the form

\int_{M}|\Pi_{\lambda}u|^{2}V\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}

provide geometric information about the shape of level sets. In the present random setting, however,
V_{\omega,\lambda}
is signed, so this geometric interpretation is less direct.

In the Euclidean setting, weighted Fourier restriction theory is concerned with inequalities of the form

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|E_{S}f|^{2}w\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(w)\|f\|_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}

for deterministic nonnegative weights
w
. We refer, for example, to
[
BGNO25
,
CIW24
]
for further background and results.

(ii) Half-resolvents.

We will prove deterministic estimates for the square root of the resolvent,

\bigl\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}}(M)}\lesssim\max(d(z)^{-1/2},\langle z\rangle^{-1/4})\log\langle z\rangle^{1/2}\langle z\rangle^{\frac{d-1}{4(d+1)}},

see Proposition
4.3
.
Combining these bounds with Theorem
1.4
yields a probabilistic estimate for the Birman-Schwinger operator

\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)=|-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}V_{\omega,\lambda}(-\Delta_{g}-z)^{-1/2},

see Proposition
4.6
. The spectral inclusion in Theorem
1.2
is then obtained by combining this estimate with the Birman–Schwinger principle.

1.3.
Further applications

The compact-manifold Schrödinger problem considered in this paper is only one instance of a more general mechanism that separates the probabilistic from the analytic input.
As an outlook, we briefly indicate a few problems in which we expect this mechanism to apply.

(i)

Random Schrödinger operators in other geometric settings.

The argument is not specific to compact boundaryless manifolds. It should extend to compact manifolds with boundary, as well as to noncompact spaces such as asymptotically conic or hyperbolic manifolds. The appropriate deterministic spectral cluster and resolvent bounds may be found, e.g., in
[
SS94
,
SS07
,
CH18
,
GHK20
]
.
Another direction would be to consider random operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with trapping background potentials or magnetic fields, see, e.g.,
[
KT05
,
KR07
]
.

In all of these settings, the length of the spectral cluster window and the proximity of
z
to the spectrum of the unperturbed operator in the half-resolvent estimates, both of which play an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, are expected to shrink compared to the compact manifold setting. One then also expects the
r_{k}
in Theorem
1.2
to shrink. This improvement could also be explored for the torus, where shrinking spectral cluster bounds are well-studied, see, e.g.,
[
GRM22
,
Pez25
]
.

Once the location of individual eigenvalues is well understood, one could then investigate their distribution via Lieb–Thirring type inequalities; this was done in the Euclidean setting in
[
CM23
]
.

(ii)

Existence and completeness of a.s. wave operators in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Ionescu and Schlag
[
IS06
]
proved the existence and completeness of wave operators for Schrödinger operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for a large class of deterministic potentials. In particular, their results apply when
V\in L^{\frac{d+1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. When the potential is random, we expect an improvement to
L^{d+1-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This would be an analogue of Bourgain’s result
[
Bou03
]
for
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
;
the first author and Schippa
[
CS22
]
also sketched the argument for
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. A related question is whether the results of Ionescu–Jerison
[
IJ03
]
and Koch–Tataru
[
KT06
]
on the absence of embedded eigenvalues for Schrödinger operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be strengthened under randomisation. Finally, it would be interesting to investigate the
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators for random potentials.

(iii)

Discrete models.

The discrete abstract estimate, Theorem
1.2
, is designed for applications to lattice and graph operators. In particular, it is compatible with spectral projectors and resolvents for discrete Schrödinger operators, and may therefore be useful in weak-disorder problems on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and on more general finite-range graph models. This direction is close in spirit to the work of Schlag–Shubin–Wolff and Bourgain discussed above.

1.4.
Comparison of Theorem
1.1
with deterministic results

In
[
Cue26
]
, the first author proved

(1.8)

\displaystyle\spec(-\Delta_{g}+V)\subset\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}D(\lambda_{k}^{2},C\tilde{r}_{k}^{\rm det})\cup\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|z|)^{-\sigma(q)}\leq C\|V\|_{L^{q}(M)}\},

where
d/2<q<\infty
,

\tilde{r}_{k}^{\rm det}:=\|V\|_{L^{q}(M)}(1+\lambda_{k})^{2\sigma(q)},

and
\sigma(q)
is given by (
4.5
). Specializing to Anderson-type potentials (
1.2
) with
\omega_{j}=1
and applying this with
q
replaced by
2q
, it follows that

\{z\in\spec(H_{\omega,\lambda}):|z|\leq\lambda^{2}\}\subset\bigcup_{\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda}D(\lambda_{k}^{2},CKR_{k}^{\prime})\cup\Omega^{\rm det},

where
d/2<2q<\infty
and

r_{k}^{\rm det}:=(1+\lambda_{k})^{2\sigma(2q)}\lambda^{-\frac{d}{2q}}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}},

\Omega^{\rm det}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|z|)^{-\sigma(2q)}\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{d}{2q}}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}}\}.

For
\lambda_{k}\ll\lambda
, one has
\frac{r_{k}}{r_{k}^{\rm det}}\ll 1
; more precisely,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\frac{r_{k}}{r_{k}^{\rm det}}\leq\begin{cases}(1+\lambda_{k})^{d/2}\lambda^{-d/2}(\log\lambda)^{11/2},&1\leq q\leq\frac{d+1}{4},\\[5.69054pt]
(1+\lambda_{k})^{\frac{d-2}{2}+\frac{d+1}{4q}}\lambda^{-d/2}(\log\lambda)^{11/2},&\frac{d+1}{4}\leq q\leq\frac{d+1}{2},\\[5.69054pt]
(1+\lambda_{k})^{(d-1)/2}\lambda^{-d/2}(\log\lambda)^{11/2},&\frac{d+1}{2}\leq q\leq\infty.\end{cases}

We now compare
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\rm det}
. From Theorem
1.1
, it follows that for
z\in\Omega
and
|z|\leq\lambda^{2-\varepsilon}
.

|z|^{1/2}(1+|z|)^{-\sigma(2q)}\leq C_{N}K\lambda^{-\frac{d}{2q}-c\varepsilon}(\ln\lambda)^{11/2}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}}.

1.5.
Further remarks on Theorem
1.1

We also make the following remarks on Theorem
1.1
.

(1)

For comparison of the compact manifold bounds with the Euclidean bounds of Frank
[
Fra11
]
, we refer to
[
Cue26
, Section 4]
.

(2)

The trivial bound

\spec(-\Delta_{g}+V)\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\dist(z,\spec(-\Delta_{g}))\leq\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\}

implies that
\spec(H_{\omega,\lambda})
lies in the
\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
-neighbourhood of
\{\lambda_{k}^{2}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. Since
\ell^{q}\subset\ell^{\infty}
, this gives the asymptotically (as
k\to\infty
) weaker bound
r_{k}\leq\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{q}}
.

(3)

The deterministic bounds of
[
Cue26
]
are sharp in both the low-energy and high-energy regimes. The former follows from a scaling argument when
M
is a torus, while the latter follows from the optimality of Sogge’s spectral cluster bounds together with an operator-valued Rouché theorem (Gohberg–Sigal theory) when
M
is the sphere, or more generally a Zoll manifold. It would be interesting to know whether Theorem
1.1
is sharp up to the logarithmic loss. Due to the probabilistic nature of the bound, this seems to be a difficult question.

(4)

For additional background on deterministic and random eigenvalue bounds for Schrödinger operators with complex potentials, we refer to the survey
[
Ste26
]
, which also discusses fractional Laplacians.

(5)

Theorem
1.1
remains nontrivial even when
v_{j}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}
, so that
H_{\omega,\lambda}
is self-adjoint; in this case, it gives a high-probability improvement over the trivial
L^{\infty}
spectral inclusion discussed in (3).

2.
Tools from probability theory

We collect some tools from probability theory that were used in
[
CM25
]
, and that will also be important here to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
.

2.1.
Sub-Gaussian random variables and tail bounds

We recall that a (complex) scalar random variable
X
is called
sub-Gaussian
if it has finite sub-Gaussian norm,

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}=\inf\{t>0:\,\mathbf{E}\exp(|X|^{2}/t^{2})\leq 2\}<\infty.

We will use the following properties of sub-Gaussian (e.g., centred normalised Gaussian or symmetric Bernoulli) random variables; see, e.g.,
[
Ver18
, Proposition 2.6.1 and Exercise 2.5.10]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
CM25
, Proposition 21]
)
.

Assume that
(X_{j})_{j=1}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, is a finite collection of i.i.d. mean-zero sub-Gaussian random variables.

(i)

Then
\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}
is also sub-Gaussian and

\displaystyle\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|X_{j}\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}.

(ii)

We have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\max_{j\leq N}|X_{j}|\lesssim\sqrt{\log N}\max_{j\leq N}\|X_{j}\|_{\psi_{2}}.

We call (
2.1
) Dudley’s inequality, cf.
[
Ver18
, Section 8.1]
.

We now consider tail bounds for (infinite-dimensional)
vector-valued
normalised Gaussian or Bernoulli random variables
X
which have finite
\psi_{2}
-norm. We have
(\mathbf{E}\|X\|^{p})^{1/p}\asymp(\mathbf{E}\|X\|^{q})^{1/q}
for all
p,q>0
(cf.
[
LT91
, Corollary 3.2 and Theorem 4.7]
), which, combined with
[
LT91
, (3.5), (4.12)]
implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\|X\|>t)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{ct^{2}}{(\mathbf{E}\|X\|)^{2}}\right)

for some
c>0
. Thus, we have the following estimate.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
CM25
, Lemma 22]
)
.

Let
X
be a vector-valued Gaussian or Bernoulli random variable.
If
\mathbf{E}\|X\|\leq C
, then

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\|X\|>KC)\leq\exp(-cK^{2})

for any
K>0
.

We are not aware of generalisations of this lemma to more general sub-Gaussian vector-valued random variables.

2.2.
Entropy bounds

We state an abstract version of the chaining argument
[
CM25
, Corollary 14]
, whose proof was inspired by that of Bourgain
[
Bou02
]
. The proof is the same.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space,
E
a nonempty finite set and

S:\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{\infty}(E)
a linear operator.
For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist sets
\mathcal{F}_{k}\subset\ell^{\infty}(E)
with the following properties.

(a)

\log\#\mathcal{F}_{k}\lesssim 4^{k}\log\#E
.

(b)

For every
\xi\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(E)}\lesssim 2^{-k}\|S\|_{\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{\infty}(E)}.

(c)

For each
g\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 1
there is a representation

(2.4)

\displaystyle Sg=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\xi_{k}\quad\text{for some}\quad\xi_{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}.

Moreover, each
\xi_{k}
lies in the image of the unit ball in
\mathcal{H}
under
S
.

3.
Abstract results

In this section, we first prove the discrete abstract estimate (Theorem
1.2
). We then introduce the appropriate setup for the continuous abstract estimate (Theorem
1.4
) and prove a rigorous version of it (Theorem
3.16
). The results of this section form the technical core of the paper and are of independent interest.

3.1.
Discrete model

We now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

1. We prove this for
q<\infty
; the case
q=\infty
is analogous. We assume first that
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}
are finite-dimensional.
We apply Proposition
2.3
to both
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
and denote the resulting sets by
\mathcal{F}_{1,k}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}
, respectively. Then

\displaystyle\|S_{1}^{*}v_{\omega}S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}}=\sup_{\|g_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}=\|g_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=1}|\langle S_{1}g_{1},v_{\omega}S_{2}g_{2}\rangle|\leq\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\max_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}}|\langle\xi_{1},v_{\omega}\xi_{2}\rangle|,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the inner product in
\ell^{2}(E)
.
By monotonicity of the expectation,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|S_{1}^{*}v_{\omega}S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}}\leq\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbf{E}\max_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}}|\langle\xi_{1},v_{\omega}\xi_{2}\rangle|.

Note that, by definition of
v_{\omega}
in (
1.6
) and Proposition
2.1
(i), we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\langle\xi_{1},v_{\omega}\xi_{2}\rangle\|_{\psi_{2}}=\Big\|\sum_{j\in J}\omega_{j}\langle\xi_{1},v\xi_{2}\rangle_{\ell^{2}(E_{j})}\Big\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{j\in J}|\langle\xi_{1},v_{j}\xi_{2}\rangle_{\ell^{2}(E_{j})}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

where we have also used
\sup_{j\in J}\|\omega_{j}\|_{\psi_{2}}<\infty
.
By Proposition
2.3
(a), the cardinality
N
of the set
\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}
satisfies

(3.2)

\displaystyle\log N=\log\#(\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell})\lesssim(4^{k}+4^{\ell})\,\log\#E.

By Dudley’s inequality (
2.1
),
(
3.1
), (
3.2
), disjointness of the sets
E_{j}
, and Hölder’s inequality for sums, (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\max_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}}|\langle\xi_{1},v_{\omega}\xi_{2}\rangle|\lesssim\sqrt{\log N}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}|\langle\xi_{1},v_{j}\xi_{2}\rangle_{\ell^{2}(E_{j})}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}}\sqrt{\log N}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\|\xi_{1}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(E_{j})}^{2}\|v\xi_{2}\|_{\ell^{p}(E_{j})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}}\sqrt{\log N}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\|\xi_{1}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(E_{j})}^{2}\|\xi_{2}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(E_{j})}^{2}\|v\|_{\ell^{p}(E_{j})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}}\sqrt{\log N}\|\xi_{1}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(E)}\|\xi_{2}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(E)}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\|v\|_{\ell^{p}(E_{j})}^{2q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\log\#E}(2^{k}+2^{\ell})2^{-\ell}\|S_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\ell^{p^{\prime}}(E)}\|S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\ell^{\infty}(E)}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\|v\|_{\ell^{p}(E_{j})}^{2q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2q}}.

Interchanging the roles of
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
, we can replace
(2^{k}+2^{\ell})2^{-\ell}
by
(2^{k}+2^{\ell})2^{-k}
, so that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\max_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}}|\langle\xi_{1},v_{\omega}\xi_{2}\rangle|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sqrt{\log\#E}\|S_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\ell^{p^{\prime}}(E)}\|S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\ell^{\infty}(E)}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\|v\|_{\ell^{p}(E_{j})}^{2q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2q}}=:A.

Combining this with the trivial bound

\displaystyle\max_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{F}_{1,k}\times\mathcal{F}_{2,\ell}}|\langle\xi_{1},v_{\omega}\xi_{2}\rangle|\lesssim 2^{-k-\ell}\|S_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\ell^{\infty}(E)}\|S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\ell^{\infty}(E)}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{1}(E)}=:2^{-k-\ell}B,

we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|S_{1}^{*}v_{\omega}S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\min(A,2^{-k-\ell}B)\lesssim A(1+(\log B/A)^{2}).

By Hölder’s inequality and the inclusion
\ell^{s}(E)\subset\ell^{r}(E)
, for
s\leq r
, we have

\displaystyle B\leq\|S_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\ell^{p^{\prime}}(E)}\|S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\ell^{\infty}(E)}(\#E)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\|v\|_{\ell^{p}(E_{j})}^{2q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2q}}

so that
\log(B/A)\leq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\log\#E
.
This concludes the proof for the case where
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}
are finite-dimensional.

2. In the case where
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}
are infinite-dimensional and separable, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{i}^{(1)}\subset\mathcal{H}_{i}^{(2)}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{H}_{i},\quad i=1,2,

be an increasing sequence of finite-dimensional subspaces whose union is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Let
P_{i}^{(k)}
be the orthogonal projections onto
\mathcal{H}_{i}^{(k)}
. For each
\omega=\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we set

\displaystyle N_{k}(\omega):=\|P_{1}^{(k)}S_{1}^{*}v_{\omega}S_{2}P_{2}^{(k)}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(k)}}.

For fixed
\omega
, the numbers
N_{k}(\omega)
are nondecreasing in
k
and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}N_{k}(\omega)=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}N_{k}(\omega)=\|S_{1}^{*}v_{\omega}S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}}.

By the monotone convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|S_{1}^{*}v_{\omega}S_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbf{E}N_{k}(\omega).

Since
\mathbf{E}N_{k}(\omega)
is uniformly bounded in
k
by the right-hand side of (
1.7
), the general case is proved.
∎

3.2.
Continuous model

We now introduce the setup for the continuous model. The main result of this subsection is Theorem
3.16
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a metric measure space and
d>0
. We say that
X
is
Ahlfors
d
-regular
if
\mu
is a Radon measure and there is
C_{A}>0
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle C_{A}^{-1}r^{d}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq C_{A}r^{d}

for all balls
B(x,r)\subset X
with
r\in(0,1)
.
We say
X
is
upper/lower Ahlfors
d
-regular
if the upper/lower bound in (
3.3
) holds.

Clearly, if
X
is upper Ahlfors
d
-regular, then for all
p\in[1,\infty]
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r))}\leq C_{A}^{1/p}r^{d/p}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,r))}.

Note furthermore that if
X
is Ahlfors
d
-regular, then
X
is
\sigma
-finite.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a metric measure space and
r>0
.
We say that a collection of measurable functions
\mathcal{F}
on
X
satisfies the
local constancy property at scale
r
if there exists a constant
C_{\rm loc}
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
and all balls
B\subset X
with radii at most
2r
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C_{\rm loc}\int_{X}|f|w_{B}\mathrm{d}\mu,

where the weights
w_{B}
are given by

\displaystyle w_{B}(x):=\mu(B)^{-1}(1+r^{-1}\dist(x,B))^{-100d}.

Remark 3.3
.

(1) By definition, if
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the local constancy property at scale
r
, then the same is true for all scales
\leq r
.

(2) As an example, for
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\rho
the Euclidean metric, and
\mu
the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, the family of functions
\{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,|\,\exists a\in\mathbb{R}^{d},b>0:\,\supp\hat{f}\subseteq B_{a}(b/r)\}
satisfies the local constancy property at scale
r
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space and
r>0
. We call
\Lambda\subset X
an
r
-separated set
if

\displaystyle\rho(x,x^{\prime})>r\quad\mbox{for all distinct }x,x^{\prime}\in\Lambda.

We call
\Lambda

maximal
r
-separated
if
\Lambda
is
r
-separated and if for every
x\in X\setminus\Lambda
there exists
x^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
\rho(x,x^{\prime})\leq r
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be a metric measure space and
r>0
.
We say that a collection of measurable functions
\mathcal{F}
on
X
satisfies the
weak local constancy property at scale
r
if for every
s\in[1,\infty]
there exists a constant
C_{s}
such that for all
r
-separated sets
\Lambda\subset X
and
f\in\mathcal{F}
, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{x\in\Lambda}|f(x)|^{s}\Big)^{1/s}\leq C_{s}r^{-d/s}\Big(\int_{X}|f|^{s}\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{1/s}.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
X
be Ahlfors
d
-regular,
r>0
, and
\Lambda\subset X
be an
r
-separated set. Let
T:\mathcal{H}\to L^{p}(X)
be a bounded linear operator for some
p\in[1,\infty]
. Assume
\mathop{\rm Ran}T
has the weak local constancy property at scale
r
. Then for
S_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{p}(\Lambda)
defined by
S_{\Lambda}f(x):=Tf(x)
,
x\in\Lambda
, we have

\displaystyle\|S_{\Lambda}\|_{\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{p}(\Lambda)}\leq C_{p}r^{-d/p}\|T\|_{\mathcal{H}\to L^{p}(X)}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the definition.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

The local constancy property (at scale
r
) implies the weak local constancy property (at scale
r
).

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}
has the local constancy property at scale
r
.
Let
\Lambda\subset X
be an
r
-separated set and
f\in\mathcal{F}
. By (
3.5
) and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Lambda}|f(x)|^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,r))}^{s}\leq C_{\rm loc}^{s}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\Big(\int_{X}|f|w_{B(x,r)}\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm loc}^{s}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\Big(\int_{X}|f|^{s}w_{B(x,r)}\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)\Big(\int_{X}w_{B(x,r)}\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm loc}^{s}(C_{A}2^{2d+2})^{s-1}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\int_{X}|f|^{s}w_{B(x,r)}\mathrm{d}\mu,

where we have also used both the upper and lower bounds in (
3.3
) to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{X}w_{B}\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq r^{-d}\big(\mu(\{\dist(y,B)\leq r\})+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-100dk}\mu(\{r2^{k}\leq\dist(y,B)\leq r2^{k+1}\})\big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\big(2^{d}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+2^{k+1})^{d}2^{-100dk}\big)\leq 2^{2d+2}C_{A}.

Since Ahlfors regularity implies
\sigma
-finiteness of
X
, Tonelli’s theorem and a similar calculation as above yield

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\int_{X}|f|^{s}w_{B(x,r)}\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{X}|f|^{s}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}w_{B(x,r)}\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq r^{-d}\int_{X}|f|^{s}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\big(\mathbf{1}_{B(x,2r)}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-100dk}\mathbf{1}_{B(x,2^{k+2}r)}\big)\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}^{2}\big(5^{d}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-100dk}2^{(k+4)d}\big)r^{-d}\int_{X}|f|^{s}\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq 2^{4d+2}C_{A}^{2}r^{-d}\int_{X}|f|^{s}\mathrm{d}\mu,

from which (
3.6
) follows. In the penultimate inequality, we have used that for
M\geq 1
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\mathbf{1}_{B(x,Mr)}\leq C_{A}^{2}(2M+1)^{d}.

Indeed, for fixed
y\in X
, consider the set

\displaystyle\Lambda_{y}:=\{x\in\Lambda:y\in B(x,Mr)\}.

Since
\Lambda
is
r
-separated, the balls
\{B(x,r/2)\}_{x\in\Lambda}
are disjoint. By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\bigcup_{x\in\Lambda_{y}}B(x,r/2)\subset B(y,(M+1/2)r),

so that, by (
3.3
) (both upper and lower bounds),

\displaystyle C_{A}^{-1}2^{-d}r^{d}\#\Lambda_{y}\leq\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{y}}\mu(B(x,r/2))\leq\mu(B(y,(M+1/2)r))\leq C_{A}(M+1/2)^{d}r^{d}.

Since
y
was arbitrary, this proves (
3.7
).
∎

The following development merely uses the weak local constancy property. In applications, the local constancy property can be verified rather directly; see Appendix
A
below in the case of the range of the spectral projector for
-\Delta_{g}
.

Definition 3.8
.

Given a maximal
r
-separated subset
\Lambda
of a metric space
(X,\rho)
, we denote by
\mathcal{V}_{x}
the
Voronoi cell
associated to
x\in\Lambda
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{x}:=\{y\in X:\rho(x,y)<\rho(x^{\prime},y)\mbox{ for all }x^{\prime}\in\Lambda\setminus\{x\}\}.

All Voronoi cells are mutually disjoint and, if
X
is Ahlfors
d
-regular,
\{\mathcal{V}_{x}\}_{x\in\Lambda}
covers
X
up to a set of measure zero. Thus, for any integrable function
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}f\mathrm{d}\mu=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\int_{\mathcal{V}_{x}}f\mathrm{d}\mu.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space,
r>0
, and
\Lambda\subseteq X
be a maximal
r
-separated subset. Then
B(x,r/2)\subset\mathcal{V}_{x}\subset B(x,2r)
for all
x\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

B(x,r/2)\subset\mathcal{V}_{x}
: Let
y\in B(x,r/2)
and
x^{\prime}\in\Lambda\setminus\{x\}
. Then

\displaystyle\rho(x^{\prime},y)\geq\rho(x^{\prime},x)-\rho(x,y)\geq r-\frac{r}{2}=\frac{r}{2},

which implies
\rho(x,y)<r/2\leq\rho(x^{\prime},y)
and hence
y\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
.

\mathcal{V}_{x}\subset B(x,2r)
: Let
y\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
y\neq x
, otherwise there is nothing to prove. Assume for contradiction that
\rho(x,y)\geq 2r
. Since
\Lambda
is an
r
-net (see e.g.
[
Ver18
, Lemma 4.2.6]
), there exists
x^{\prime}\in\Lambda\setminus\{x\}
such that
\rho(x^{\prime},y)\leq r
. But this implies

\displaystyle r\geq\rho(x^{\prime},y)>\rho(x,y)\geq 2r,

a contradiction.
∎

Definition 3.10
.

Let
(X,\rho,\mu)
be Ahlfors
d
-regular,
r>0
, and
let
\Lambda\subset X
be an
r
-separated set.

(1)

We say that the pair
(X,\Lambda)
satisfies the
uniform reparametrisation property at scale
r

if there exists a finite measure space
(Y,\nu)
with
\nu(Y)\leq C_{Y}r^{d}
and, for each
x\in\Lambda
, a measurable bijection

\varphi_{x}:X\supset B(x,2r)\to Y
with measurable inverse,
such that the pushforward measures
\mu_{x}:=(\varphi_{x})_{*}(\mu|_{B(x,2r)})

are absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
, and the Radon–Nikodym derivatives

w_{x}=\frac{d\mu_{x}}{d\nu}
satisfy the uniform bounds

(3.9)

\displaystyle 0\leq w_{x}(y)\leq C_{\rm RN}\qquad\text{for all $x\in\Lambda$ and $\nu$-a.e.\ $y\in Y$}.

(2)

We say that
(X,\Lambda)
satisfies the
strong uniform reparametrisation property at scale
r
if there exists a separable metric measure space
(Y,\delta,\nu)
with finite Ahlfors
d
-regular measure
\nu
satisfying
C_{Y}^{-1}r^{d}\leq\nu(Y)\leq C_{Y}r^{d}
and, for each
x\in\Lambda
, a bi-measurable bijection
\varphi_{x}:X\supset B(x,2r)\to Y
such that

\varphi_{x}^{-1}
is Lipschitz with uniform Lipschitz bounds in
x
, i.e., there exists a constant
C_{L}
such that

\displaystyle\rho(\varphi_{x}^{-1}(y),\varphi_{x}^{-1}(z))\leq C_{L}\delta(y,z)\quad\mbox{for all }y,z\in Y.

(3)

We say that
X
satisfies the
(strong) uniform reparametrisation property at scales
\geq r
if for every
r^{\prime}\geq r
and every
r^{\prime}
-separated set
\Lambda\subset X
, the pair
(X,\Lambda)
satisfies the (strong) uniform reparametrisation property at scale
r^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.11
.

(1) In the uniform reparametrisation property, the upper bound

\nu(Y)\leq C_{Y}r^{d}
is in fact complemented by a lower bound of
the same order. Indeed, by Ahlfors
d
-regularity of
X
, for each

x\in\Lambda
,

\mu(B(x,2r))\geq C_{A}^{-1}(2r)^{d},

while the Radon–Nikodym bound gives

\mu(B(x,2r))=\int_{Y}w_{x}\,d\nu\leq C_{\rm RN}\nu(Y).

Hence

\nu(Y)\geq 2^{d}C_{A}^{-1}C_{\rm RN}^{-1}r^{d}.

(2) The uniform reparametrisation property is a purely
measure-theoretic condition: it provides a common measurable model for
the balls
B(x,2r)
, together with uniform control of the associated
Radon–Nikodym densities. The strong uniform reparametrisation property is a strengthening, in which the common model is a
metric measure space and the Radon–Nikodym condition is replaced by a uniform
Lipschitz condition. The main abstract theorem in this subsection (Theorem
3.16
) only uses the weaker assumption. However, in most applications,
X
will satisfy the stronger assumption (see Remark
3.12
).
Moreover, the strong assumption is usually easier to verify. This is the reason we included it in the above definition.

Remark 3.12

(Examples)
.

The strong uniform reparametrisation properties are satisfied in a number of
standard situations.

(1)

Let
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\rho
be the Euclidean metric, and
\mu
be the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue
measure
\mathcal{L}^{d}
. For any
r>0
and any
r
-separated set

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one may take

Y=B(0,2r),\qquad\nu=\mathcal{L}^{d}|_{B(0,2r)},\qquad\varphi_{x}(y)=y-x.

Then
\varphi_{x}:B(x,2r)\to Y
is an isometry and

(\varphi_{x})_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{d}|_{B(x,2r)})=\nu
. Thus
w_{x}\equiv 1
,
and the strong uniform reparametrisation property holds at all
scales. The same argument applies to finite-dimensional normed
spaces with Lebesgue measure.

(2)

More generally, if
X
is a Carnot group equipped with a
homogeneous distance and Haar measure, then left translation gives
the required parametrisations. Namely, for
Y=B(e,2r)
and

\varphi_{x}(y)=x^{-1}y,

the maps
\varphi_{x}:B(x,2r)\to Y
are isometries and preserve Haar
measure. Hence again
w_{x}\equiv 1
, and the strong uniform reparametrisation property holds at all scales.

(3)

Let
M
be a (not necessarily closed)
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold of
bounded geometry, equipped with its geodesic distance and Riemannian
volume measure. At sufficiently small scales, one may use normal
coordinates centred at
x
to show that
M
satisfies the strong uniform reparametrisation property locally. In particular, any closed manifold
M
with smooth Riemannian metric (as considered in this article) satisfies the strong uniform reparametrisation property (see the proof of Proposition
4.1
for details).

Lemma 3.13
.

The strong uniform reparametrisation property implies the uniform reparametrisation property.

Proof.

1. By the Lipschitz assumption, for any ball
B(z,t)
in
Y
, we see

\varphi_{x}^{-1}(B(z,t))\subset B(\varphi_{x}^{-1}(z),C_{L}t)
. Thus, by definition of the pushforward measure, the Ahlfors regularity of
\mu
and lower Ahlfors regularity of
\nu
,

\displaystyle\mu_{x}(B(z,t))=\mu(\varphi_{x}^{-1}(B(z,t)))\leq\mu(B(\varphi_{x}^{-1}(z),C_{L}t))\leq C_{A}(C_{L}t)^{d}\leq C_{A}C_{Y}^{2}C_{L}^{d}\nu(B(z,t)).

2. Let
U\subset Y
be an open set, and for each
y\in U
choose
\delta_{y}\in(0,1)
such that
B(y,\delta_{y})\subset U
. By the
5r
-covering theorem (see, e.g.,
[
Mat95
, Theorem 2.1]
), there exists a finite or countable sequence of disjoint balls
B_{i}=B(y_{i},\delta_{y_{i}})
such that the collection
\{5B_{i}\}
covers
U
. Thus, by the first step,

\displaystyle\mu_{x}(U)\leq\sum_{i}\mu_{x}(5B_{i})\leq C_{A}C_{Y}^{2}C_{L}^{d}\sum_{i}\nu(5B_{i})\leq C_{A}^{2}C_{Y}^{2}(5C_{L})^{d}\sum_{i}\nu(B_{i})\leq C_{A}^{2}C_{Y}^{2}(5C_{L})^{d}\nu(U).

3. Let
E\subset Y
be a measurable set with
\nu(E)=0
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. By outer regularity of
\nu
, there exists an open set
U\subset Y
such that
E\subset U
and
\nu(U)\leq\varepsilon
. By the second step, it follows that
\mu_{x}(U)\leq C_{A}^{2}C_{Y}^{2}(5C_{L})^{d}\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, we have
\mu_{x}(E)=0
, so that
\mu_{x}\ll\nu
.

4. By the Radon-Nikodym theorem and the estimate in the first step, for any ball
B\subset Y
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{x}(B)=\int_{B}w_{x}\mathrm{d}\nu\leq C_{A}C_{Y}^{2}C_{L}^{d}\nu(B)\implies\int_{B}(w_{x}-C_{A}C_{Y}^{2}C_{L}^{d})\mathrm{d}\nu\leq 0.

Consider the sets

\displaystyle E:=\{y\in Y:w_{x}-C_{A}C_{Y}^{2}C_{L}^{d}>0\},\quad E_{n}:=\{y\in Y:w_{x}-C_{A}C_{Y}^{2}C_{L}^{d}\geq 1/n\},

and
E_{n,k}:=E_{n}\cap B(y_{0},k)
for some fixed
y_{0}\in Y
. Then
E_{n,k}\nearrow_{k\to\infty}E_{n}
and
E_{n}\nearrow_{n\to\infty}E
. Since

\displaystyle 0\leq\nu(E_{n,k})\leq n\int_{B(y_{0},k)}(w_{x}-C_{A}C_{Y}^{2}C_{L}^{d})\mathrm{d}\nu\leq 0,

it follows that
\nu(E_{n})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\nu(E_{n,k})=0
and
\nu(E)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu(E_{n})=0
. Hence,
w_{x}\leq C_{A}C_{Y}^{2}C_{L}^{d}
almost everywhere.
∎

Lemma 3.14
.

Assume that
(X,\Lambda)
satisfies the uniform reparametrisation property at scale
r
. Then for every
\mu
-integrable function
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\int_{B(x,2r)}f\,d\mu=\int_{Y}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}f\circ\varphi_{x}^{-1}\,w_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

Proof.

This follows from the change-of-variables formula, the Radon–Nikodym theorem and Fubini’s theorem. More precisely, by change-of-variables (definition of pushforward) and the Radon–Nikodym theorem, we have the identity

\int_{B(x,2r)}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{Y}f\circ\varphi_{x}^{-1}\,w_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

To justify Fubini’s theorem, we apply the same argument to
|f|
, but in the reverse direction,

\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\int_{Y}|f|\circ\varphi_{x}^{-1}\,w_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\nu=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\int_{B(x,2r)}|f|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq C_{A}^{2}5^{d}\|f\|_{L^{1}(X)},

where we used the finite overlap bound (
3.7
).
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

Assume that
(X,\Lambda)
satisfies the uniform reparametrisation property at scale
r
and that
\Lambda
is maximal
r
-separated. Then for every
\mu
-integrable function
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{X}f\mathrm{d}\mu=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\int_{\mathcal{V}_{x}}f\mathrm{d}\mu=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\int_{B(x,2r)}f\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{V}_{x}}\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{Y}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}(f\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{V}_{x}})\circ\varphi_{x}^{-1}\,w_{x}\,d\nu.

Proof.

The first equality follows from maximality, the second from

Theorem 3.16
.

3.3.
A sufficient condition for the weak local constancy property

4.
Compact manifolds

We start this section with an application of Theorem
3.16
to a dual form of Sogge’s spectral projection bounds. The result will not be used directly to prove Theorem
1.1
but is perhaps of independent interest. We then turn to resolvent estimates, which are similar to those in
[
Cue26
]
, but for the square root of the resolvent instead of the full resolvent. We then use these estimates, together with Theorem
3.16
, to prove a Birman–Schwinger bound involving Anderson-type potentials. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is then a straightforward consequence of this bound.

4.1.
Spectral projections

Consider the spectral projection operators

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\lambda}:=\mathbf{1}\left(P\in[\lambda,\lambda+1]\right):L^{2}(M)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(M)

where
P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
. By Sogge’s bounds
[
Sog88
]
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\nu(p^{\prime})}.

where

(4.3)

\displaystyle\nu(p^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}\frac{d-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})\quad&2\leq p^{\prime}\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1},\\
d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})-\frac{1}{2}\quad&\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty.\end{cases}

Here and in what follows, we always assume that
1\leq p\leq 2\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty
,
Given
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we also adopt the convention that
p
is defined by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}.

More explicitly, this means that

p=\frac{2q}{q+1},\quad p^{\prime}=\frac{2q}{q-1}.

We also set

q_{c}:=\frac{d+1}{2}.

Using this identification in (
4.3
), we can write

(4.5)

\displaystyle\nu(p^{\prime})=\sigma(q):=\begin{cases}\frac{d}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}\quad&1\leq q\leq q_{c},\\
\frac{d-1}{4q}\quad&q_{c}\leq q\leq\infty.\end{cases}

Then, by (
4.2
) and Hölder’s inequality,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\lambda}^{*}V\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{2\sigma(q)}\|V\|_{L^{q}(M)},\quad V\in L^{q}(M).

Conversely, (
4.6
) implies (
4.2
) by duality.

The following proposition yields an improvement over (
4.6
) for random potentials.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\geq 2
and

r\leq\min\Big(\frac{\mathrm{inj}(M)}{4},\lambda^{-1}\Big)\leq R\leq\diam(M).

Assume that
V\in L^{1}_{\rm{\rm loc}}(M)
and that its support has diameter at most
R
. Let
\Lambda\subset M
be a maximal
r
-separated set and let
\{\mathcal{V}_{x}\}_{x\in\Lambda}
be the associated Voronoi cells. Let

\displaystyle V_{\omega}(y):=\omega_{x}V(y)\quad\mbox{for }x\in\Lambda,y\in\mathcal{V}_{x},

where
\omega_{j}
are i.i.d. symmetric Bernoulli or centred normalised Gaussian random variables. Then, for
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\Pi_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-\mu(q)}(\log R/r)^{5/2}\Big(\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,2r))}^{2q}\Big)^{1/(2q)},

with the obvious modification for
q=\infty
. Here,
\mu(q)
is given by (
1.5
)
In particular, for any
K\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim K\lambda^{-\mu(q)}(\log R/r)^{5/2}\Big(\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,2r))}^{2q}\Big)^{1/(2q)}

with probability at least
1-\exp(-K^{2})
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from Theorem
3.16
(1) together with (
4.2
) by taking

•

X=M
with Riemannian volume measure and the metric space structure induced by the Riemannian metric,

•

Y=B(0,2r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure and
\varphi_{x}:=\exp_{x}^{-1}
,

•

\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{2}=L^{2}(M)
and
T_{1}=T_{2}=\Pi_{\lambda}
,

and observing that, due to (
4.4
), (
4.5
),

-d/p+\nu(p^{\prime})+\nu(\infty)=-\mu(q).

By definition of the injectivity radius,

\exp_{x}:B(0,\mathrm{inj}(M))\to B(x,\mathrm{inj}(M))

is a diffeomorphism for every
x\in M
. Since
4r\leq\mathrm{inj}(M)
, it follows by compactness of
M
that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in M}\|\mathrm{d}\exp_{x}^{-1}|_{B(x,2r)}\|<\infty.

Hence, by Lemma
3.13
, Definition
3.10
is satisfied. The assumption (
3.6
) is verified in Lemma
A.4
.
The second statement follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The class of potentials covered by Proposition
4.1
contains randomisations of any fixed
V
with finite
\ell^{2q}L^{\infty}
norm (as in (
3.12
)), not just Anderson-type potentials of the form (
1.2
). In the latter case, taking
r=\lambda^{-1}
,
R=\diam(M)
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\Pi_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{-\mu(q)}(\log\lambda)^{5/2}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}}.

In comparison, e.g. in the Rademacher case, the deterministic bound (
4.6
) yields

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{2\sigma(2q)-\frac{d}{2q}}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}}.

Since

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mu(q)+2\sigma(2q)-\frac{d}{2q}=\begin{cases}0,&1\leq q\leq\dfrac{q_{c}}{2},\\
1-\frac{q_{c}}{2q},&\dfrac{q_{c}}{2}\leq q\leq q_{c},\\
\frac{1}{2},&q_{c}\leq q\leq\infty,\end{cases}

it follows that
-\mu(q)<2\sigma(2q)-\frac{d}{2q}
for
q>q_{c}/2
. Hence, the probabilistic bound improves upon the deterministic one in this range.

4.2.
Half-resolvent estimates

We prove the following deterministic bounds for a larger range of exponents than needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let

(4.8)

\displaystyle d(z):=\dist(z,\spec(-\Delta_{g})),\quad\langle z\rangle:=2+|z|,\quad\delta(z):=\frac{\min\{d(z),|z|^{1/2}\}}{\log\langle z\rangle}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Delta_{g}
be the Laplace–Beltrami operator on a
d
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. Let
2\leq p^{\prime}\leq 2d/(d-2)
if
d\geq 3
and
2\leq p^{\prime}<\infty
if
d=2
.
Then for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\spec(-\Delta_{g})
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(M)}\lesssim\delta(z)^{-1/2}\langle z\rangle^{\frac{\nu(p^{\prime})}{2}}.

The implicit constant depends only on
(M,g)
and
d,p
, but not on
z
.

Proof.

1. The same argument as in
[
Cue26
, Lemma 2.3]
yields

\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(M)}\lesssim d(z)^{-1/2},\quad\re z\leq 1,

which is better than (
4.9
).

2. It remains to consider the case
\re z>1
.
Let
\Sigma:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\im z|\geq\re z\}
. Then for
z\in\Sigma
, we have

\Big\|\frac{(-\Delta_{g}+|z|)^{1/2}}{|-\Delta_{g}-z|^{1/2}}\Big\|\lesssim 1.

Thus, by Sobolev embedding,

\displaystyle\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\|(-\Delta_{g}+|z|)^{-1/2}\frac{(-\Delta_{g}+|z|)^{1/2}}{|-\Delta_{g}-z|^{1/2}}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|z|^{\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\|\frac{(-\Delta_{g}+|z|)^{1/2}}{|-\Delta_{g}-z|^{1/2}}f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim|z|^{\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}

for
z\in\Sigma
, which is again better than (
4.9
) for
\re z>1
. A similar argument shows

\displaystyle\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}\mathbf{1}(-\Delta_{g}\notin[\re z/2,2\re z])\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|z|^{\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}.

3. It remains to estimate the half resolvent, spectrally localised to
[\re z/2,2\re z]
, for
\re z>\max(1,|\im z|)
. Let
f\in\mathop{\rm Ran}\mathbf{1}(-\Delta_{g}\in[\re z/2,2\re z])
. We first observe that

\displaystyle|-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-\frac{1}{2}}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2\sqrt{\re z}]}\Pi_{k}\Upsilon_{k,z}f,

where

\Upsilon_{k,z}:=\sum_{\lambda_{j}^{2}\in[k^{2},(k+1)^{2})\cap[\re z/2,2\re z]}\Big(\frac{1}{|\lambda_{j}^{2}-z|}\Big)^{1/2}E_{j},

and
E_{j}
is the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace of
-\Delta_{g}
corresponding to
\lambda_{j}^{2}
.
Let
k_{0}:=\left\lceil{\sqrt{\re z}}\right\rceil
.
By the spectral theorem, we have the
L^{2}(M)
-operator norm estimates

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|\Upsilon_{k,z}\|^{2}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{d(z)},\quad&\mbox{for }|k-k_{0}|\leq 10,\\
\frac{5}{\sqrt{\re z}|k-k_{0}|},\quad&\mbox{for }|k-k_{0}|>10.\end{cases}

Indeed, for
|k-k_{0}|>10
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2\sqrt{\re z}]
and
\re z\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|\lambda_{j}^{2}-z|

\displaystyle\geq|k^{2}-\re z|-(2k+1)\geq\sqrt{\re z}|k-k_{0}|-2(\sqrt{\re z}+k+1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{5}\sqrt{\re z}|k-k_{0}|.

By orthogonality and Sogge’s spectral cluster bounds (
4.2
), it follows that

\displaystyle\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2\sqrt{\re z}]}\|\Upsilon_{k,z}\|^{2}\|\Pi_{k}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\sqrt{\re z})^{2\nu(p^{\prime})}\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2\sqrt{\re z}]}\|\Upsilon_{k,z}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\sqrt{\re z})^{2\nu(p^{\prime})}\Big(\frac{1}{d(z)}+\frac{\log\sqrt{\re z}}{\sqrt{\re z}}\Big)\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{2}.

By duality (using that estimates are invariant under
z\to\overline{z}
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}\mathbf{1}(-\Delta_{g}\in[\re z/2,2\re z])\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{d(z)}+\frac{\log|z|}{|z|^{1/2}}\Big)^{1/2}|z|^{\frac{\nu(p^{\prime})}{2}}

for
\re z>\max(1,|\im z|)
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

We used
p^{\prime}\leq 2d/(d-2)
(or
p^{\prime}<\infty
if
d=2
) only in Step 2. The estimates in Steps 1 and 3 hold for
2\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty
.

We can also draw the following consequence from the above proof.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
and
\lambda\geq 2
. Let
\chi_{-}
and
\chi
be smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}
supported on the intervals
(-\infty,4)
and
(1/4,4)
, respectively. Then for all
\lambda\geq 2
,

and all

,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}\chi_{-}(P/\lambda)\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta(z)^{-1/2}\langle z\rangle^{\frac{\nu(p^{\prime})}{2}},

(4.12)

\displaystyle\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}\chi(P/2^{k})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p^{\prime}}-1)}\quad\mbox{for }|z|\leq 2^{2k-5}.

By the spectral theorem,

\Big\|\frac{\chi(P/2^{k})(-\Delta_{g}+2^{2k})^{1/2}}{|-\Delta_{g}-z|^{1/2}}\Big\|\leq\sup_{2^{k-2}<\tau<2^{k+2}}\left|\frac{\tau^{2}+2^{2k}}{\tau^{2}-z}\right|^{1/2}\lesssim 1\quad\mbox{for }|z|\leq 2^{2k-5}.

\displaystyle\||-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-1/2}\chi(P/2^{k})f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}

The bound (
4.11
) follows from (

) and Remark
4.4
.

4.3.

Let us consider the Birman–Schwinger operator

\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z):=|-\Delta_{g}-z|^{-\frac{1}{2}}V_{\omega,\lambda}(-\Delta_{g}-z)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\spec(-\Delta_{g}).

where

is of Anderson-type, as in (

). More precisely, we assume that

(4.13)

\displaystyle V_{\omega,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N(\lambda)}\omega_{j}v_{j}(\lambda)\psi_{j}(x;\lambda),\quad x\in M,

where
\psi_{j}(\cdot;\lambda)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M;[0,1])

, with bounded overlap

\sup_{x\in M}\sum_{j=1}^{N(\lambda)}\mathbf{1}_{E_{j}(\lambda)}(x)\leq C.

The coefficients
v_{j}(\lambda)
are complex numbers,
\omega_{j}
are i.i.d. symmetric Bernoulli or centred normalised Gaussian random variables, and
\lambda\geq 2
is a large parameter.

The following is the main result of this subsection.

.

Let

be as in (

,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)\|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{d(q+1)}{2q}}\,\delta(z)^{-1}\,\langle z\rangle^{\frac{d(q+1)}{4q}-\frac{\mu(q)}{2}}(\log\lambda)^{5/2}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}},

), respectively.

, and

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(E_{0}(z,q,\lambda,K))\geq 1-\exp(-K^{2})

(4.16)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)\|\lesssim K\lambda^{-\frac{d(q+1)}{2q}}\,\delta(z)^{-1}\,\langle z\rangle^{\frac{d(q+1)}{4q}-\frac{\mu(q)}{2}}(\log\lambda)^{5/2}\|v(\lambda)\|_{\ell^{2q}}.

4.4.
Discretisation and union bound

We cannot directly apply Proposition
4.6
to prove Theorem
1.1
.
The problem is that the events on which the corresponding inequalities hold depend on the spectral parameter
z
.
Since
z
ranges over a continuum, one cannot directly apply a union bound over all
z
. To overcome this, we will introduce a discretisation scheme.

To carry out the discretisation, let

(4.24)

\displaystyle E(z,q,\lambda,K)

\displaystyle:=\{\omega:\|\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)\|\lesssim KA_{\lambda}B(z)^{-1}\},

where
A_{\lambda}
,
B(z)
were defined in (
4.17
), (
4.18
), respectively.
In what follows, we will abbreviate
E(z,q,\lambda,K)
by
E(z)
.
The result of Proposition
4.6
can then be stated as

(4.25)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(E(z))\geq 1-\exp(-K^{2}).

The aim of this section is to prove the following proposition, which is a uniform version of the above bound. For technical reasons, we need to restrict
z
to the complement of an arbitrarily small neighborhood of
\spec(-\Delta_{g})
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
N\geq 1
be fixed. Then there exists a constant
C_{N}>0
such that
for every
K\geq 1
,

(4.26)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\Bigl(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|z|\leq\lambda^{2}\\
d(z)\geq\lambda^{-N}\end{subarray}}B(z)\,\|\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)\|\leq C_{N}K\,A_{\lambda}(\log\lambda)^{2}\Bigr)\geq 1-\exp(-K^{2}).

As a preliminary step, we record the following simple Lipschitz bound for the Birman-Schwinger operator.

Lemma 4.12
.

For any
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\spec(-\Delta_{g})
with
d(z),d(z^{\prime})\geq\rho>0
, we have

\|\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)-\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z^{\prime})\|\lesssim|z-z^{\prime}|\,\rho^{-2}\,\|V_{\omega,\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}.

Proof.

Set

R(w):=(-\Delta_{g}-w)^{-1/2},\qquad w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\spec(-\Delta_{g}).

Then

\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(w)=|R(w)|V_{\omega,\lambda}R(w),

and hence

\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)-\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z^{\prime})=\bigl(|R(z)|-|R(z^{\prime})|\bigr)V_{\omega,\lambda}R(z)+|R(z^{\prime})|V_{\omega,\lambda}\bigl(R(z)-R(z^{\prime})\bigr).

Therefore, taking the operator norm,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)-\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|R(z)-R(z^{\prime})\|\,\|V_{\omega,\lambda}\|\,\|R(z)\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\|R(z^{\prime})\|\,\|V_{\omega,\lambda}\|\,\|R(z)-R(z^{\prime})\|,

where
\|V_{\omega,\lambda}\|=\|V_{\omega,\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}
.
It remains to estimate
\|R(w)\|
and
\|R(z)-R(z^{\prime})\|
.

By the spectral theorem,

(4.27)

\displaystyle\|R(w)\|=\sup_{\mu\in\spec(-\Delta_{g})}|\mu-w|^{-1/2}=d(w)^{-1/2}.

For the difference, define for
\mu\in\spec(-\Delta_{g})

f_{w}(\mu):=|\mu-w|^{-1/2}.

Then

\|R(z)-R(z^{\prime})\|=\sup_{\mu\in\spec(-\Delta_{g})}|f_{z}(\mu)-f_{z^{\prime}}(\mu)|.

Using

\displaystyle\bigl||a|^{-1/2}-|b|^{-1/2}\bigr|

\displaystyle=\frac{\bigl||b|^{1/2}-|a|^{1/2}\bigr|}{|a|^{1/2}|b|^{1/2}}=\frac{|\,|b|-|a|\,|}{|a|^{1/2}|b|^{1/2}(|a|^{1/2}+|b|^{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|a-b|}{|a|^{1/2}|b|^{1/2}(|a|^{1/2}+|b|^{1/2})},

with
a=\mu-z
and
b=\mu-z^{\prime}
, we obtain

|f_{z}(\mu)-f_{z^{\prime}}(\mu)|\leq\frac{|z-z^{\prime}|}{|\mu-z|^{1/2}|\mu-z^{\prime}|^{1/2}\bigl(|\mu-z|^{1/2}+|\mu-z^{\prime}|^{1/2}\bigr)}.

Since

\frac{1}{x^{1/2}y^{1/2}(x^{1/2}+y^{1/2})}\leq\min(x^{-1/2}y^{-1},x^{-1}y^{-1/2})\leq x^{-3/2}+y^{-3/2},

for all
x,y>0
, it follows that

|f_{z}(\mu)-f_{z^{\prime}}(\mu)|\lesssim|z-z^{\prime}|\bigl(|\mu-z|^{-3/2}+|\mu-z^{\prime}|^{-3/2}\bigr).

Taking the supremum over
\mu\in\spec(-\Delta_{g})
yields

\|R(z)-R(z^{\prime})\|\lesssim|z-z^{\prime}|\bigl(d(z)^{-3/2}+d(z^{\prime})^{-3/2}\bigr).

Substituting these bounds into the previous estimate gives

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)-\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim|z-z^{\prime}|\,\|V_{\omega,\lambda}\|\bigl(d(z)^{-3/2}+d(z^{\prime})^{-3/2}\bigr)\bigl(d(z)^{-1/2}+d(z^{\prime})^{-1/2}\bigr).

If
d(z),d(z^{\prime})\geq\rho
, then

\|\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)-\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z^{\prime})\|\lesssim|z-z^{\prime}|\,\rho^{-2}\,\|V_{\omega,\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We decompose the set
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\spec(-\Delta_{g}):|z|\leq\lambda^{2}\}

according to the size of
B(z)
. More precisely, for
j\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\spec(-\Delta_{g}):|z|\leq\lambda^{2},\ 2^{j}\leq B(z)<2^{j+1}\Bigr\}.

Then,

\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\spec(-\Delta_{g}):|z|\leq\lambda^{2}\}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{j}

and on each

4.24

(4.28)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\lambda}(z)\|\lesssim K\,2^{-j}A_{\lambda},\qquad z\in\Omega_{j},\quad\omega\in E(z).

Lemma A.1

Proof.
