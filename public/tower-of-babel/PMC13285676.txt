Dataset on the transcriptomic responses of ‘Fuji Hubrax’ apples to deep seawater application
Authors: Bimpe Suliyat Azeez, Se-Jin Oh, Jong-Kuk Na
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Deep seawater (DSW) has attracted increasing interest as a mineral-rich resource for agricultural applications due to its stable composition and high concentrations of dissolved inorganic nutrients. Mineral-based treatments derived from DSW have the potential to influence plant growth, physiological performances, and stress responses [1]. Despite these growing interests, limited information is available on their impact on gene expression and associated biological pathways in apple, although such information would be crucial for optimized utilization of DSW as a natural fertilizer. ‘Fuji Hubrax’, a widely cultivated and economically important apple cultivar [2], was used to examine the transcriptional response associated with DSW application in apple (Malus domestica Borkh.). Peel tissues from control (HB-NT) and deep seawater treated samples (HB-DSW) of ‘Fuji Hubrax’ were subjected to paired-end RNA sequencing using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Sequencing generated 28.5 million reads for HB-NT, and 25.0 million reads for HB-DSW, corresponding to 2.9 Gb and 2.5 Gb of raw data, respectively. After quality filtering, 2.8 Gb and 2.5 Gb of clean reads were obtained. GC content was 47.17% (HB-NT) and 47.0% (HB-DSW), and the Q20 quality score exceeded 99% for both sequencing data. Approximately 95% of reads from the individual sample were mapped to the reference genome. In DSW-treated vs. control samples, the total number of upregulated and downregulated genes was 2274 and 1517, respectively. The sequencing datasets are publicly available in the NCBI BioProject database under the accession number www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/query.fcgi?cmd=search&db=pdb&doptcmdl=genbank&term=PRJNA1434662. These datasets provide transcriptional profiles of ‘Fuji Hubrax’ apple peel in response to deep seawater treatment, establishing a reference framework for subsequent genetic improvement and cross-study comparisons in this species.

Background

Apples (Malus domestica Borkh.) serve as an important model for investigating fruit quality and treatment-induced transcriptional changes due to the availability of genomic resources and well-annotated reference genomes. In 2025, South Korea produced 447,953 tons of apples, covering an area of 33,212 hectare [8]. ‘Fuji’ apples account for approximately two-thirds of the national cultivation area, making it the most economically important apple cultivar in Korea [2]. ‘Fuji Hubrax’ (commercially known as Fuji ‘Kiku® Fubrax’) [9] is a red-colored natural bud mutation of the original ‘Fuji’ cultivar, retaining its sweetness and firmness while exhibiting improve and more uniform skin coloration, thereby representing a commercially valuable clonal selection. DSW, characterized by low temperature, cleanliness, and high mineral content, has garnered growing interest for agricultural and horticultural applications [1]. Although DSW has been explored for its potential to modulate physiological and molecular responses in fruit crops, transcriptomic data elucidating DSW responses in ‘Fuji Hubrax’ apple remain limited despite the advantages of RNA sequencing in identifying genes and their pathways associated with DSW treatment. The dataset presented here addresses this gap by providing comprehensive transcriptomic profiles of DSW-treated ‘Fuji Hubrax’ apples to support downstream analyses on mineral-based treatment in this species.

Data Description

RNA sequencing data were obtained from fresh, healthy fruit peel of ‘Fuji Hubrax’ apple in replicates for each of the treatments (DSW) and Control (NT). The raw data of both DSW and NT transcriptomes were deposited to the Sequence Read Archive (SRA) database of NCBI BioProject under the accession of PRJNA1434662. The SRA accession numbers for these data are SRX32436505 and SRX32436506, respectively. Table 1 summarizes the raw and pre-processed filtered reads for each sample. The filtered reads are then mapped to a known reference genome using the HISAT2 program [5]. After read mapping, transcript assembly was performed using the StringTie program [6]. Using these values, differentially expressed genes (DEG) analysis was performed using edgeR [7]. 3791 genes were extracted that satisfied the conditions of FC ≥ 2 or FC ≤ 0.5 and exactTest raw p-value<0.05 in the samples comparison. Experimental Design, Materials, and Methods

Cultivation environment, experimental layout, and treatment application

The experiment was conducted during the 2025 growing seasons at Jinbu-ryeong Apple Farm (100–1 Heulli-gil, Ganseong-eup, Goseong County, Gangwon-do, Republic of Korea; Latitude: 38°15′44.65″N; Longitude: 128°22′26.37″E), covering a total cultivation area of 0.66 hectares. The site is located in the northeast highlands of Gangwon-do province, characterized by temperatures approximately 4.16 °C lower than the surrounding plains, with recent spring maximum of 17.3–19.6 °C and summer averages from 23.3 to 24.9 °C, providing favorable conditions for apple cultivation. The soil consists of well-drained loam to sandy loam with a pH of approximately 6.0, an effective soil depth of 50–100 cm, and gentle slopes (2–7%) conducive to root development and frost avoidance. To optimize light interception and yield per unit area, the trees were trained using a tall, slender spindle (fusiform) system with 1 m spacing between trees and 4 m between rows. DSW was applied as a 5% diluted foliar spray five times during the growing season: at the pre-flowering, flowering, fruiting, pre-rainy season, and ripening stages. The non-treatment trees were simultaneously sprayed with tap water at each of these stages as well. The DSW solution had a pH of 7.5 and an electrical conductivity (EC) of 54.4 mS/m and contained elevated mineral concentrations, including Ca, Mg, K, Na, B, Si, and Zn.

Tissue sampling, total RNA extraction, and purification

Freshly harvested, visibly healthy apple fruits of HB-NT and HB-DSW were collected in triplicate from each group on October 21, 2025, manually peeled using a fruit peeler, flash-frozen, and kept at −80 °C for subsequent RNA extraction. Total RNA was extracted from the peel tissues and their biological replicates, using the GeneAll Ribospin Seed/Fruit reagent (GeneAll Biotechnology Co., Ltd, Seoul, South Korea) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA integrity was initially evaluated by electrophoresis on 1% RNase-free agarose gel to assess potential degradation or contamination. The purity was determined using the NanoPhotometer® spectrophotometer (IMPLEN, CA, USA), and RNA concentration was quantified with the Quan-it™ RiboGreen RNA Assay Kit (Invitrogen, cat. #R11490). Overall RNA integrity was further assessed using the TapeStation RNA ScreenTape system (Agilent Technologies, #5067–5576).

Data processing and library construction

RNA samples were pooled within each experimental group (HB-NT and HB-DSW) in equal proportions. For each group, 1 µg of total RNA was used for mRNA isolation and second-strand cDNA synthesis using the Illumina TruSeq Stranded mRNA Library Preparation Kit (Illumina, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA; #RS-122–2101). cDNA library preparation was done using quality-controlled RNA samples, library quantification was performed using the KAPA Library Quantification Kit (Kapa Biosystems; #KK4854), and size distribution was assessed with the TapeStation D1000 ScreenTape system (Agilent Technologies; #5067–5582). These libraries were then sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA).

Differentially expressed genes

Clean reads were aligned to the Malus domestica reference genome GDT2T_hap1 (NCBI RefSeq assembly accession: GCF_042453785.1) using HISAT2 (v2.1.0) [5]. The genome sequence and corresponding gene annotation files were obtained from the NCBI Genome Assembly and NCBI RefSeq databases (RefSeq release 2024–09–27), respectively. Alignment files in SAM format were subsequently sorted and indexed using SAMtools (v1.9) [10]. Transcript assembly and abundance estimation were performed with StringTie (v2.1.3b) [6]. Differential gene expression analysis was conducted using the edgeR package (v3.40.2) [7], with statistical significance assessed through the exactTest function. Fold changes and p-values were calculated from the exactTest results, and genes meeting the thresholds of |fold change| ≥ 2 and raw p-value < 0.05 were considered differentially expressed. In the comparison between HB-DSW and HB-NT samples, a total of 2274 genes were upregulated, and 1517 genes were downregulated.

Gene Ontology (GO) analysis

Gene Ontology analysis was performed on differentially expressed genes using g: Profiler (2019 update; Ensembl release e94-based) [11] on three GO categories: Molecular Function (MF), Biological Function (BF), and Cellular Component (CC), using reference databases from the GO consortium and multispecies annotation from UniProt.
