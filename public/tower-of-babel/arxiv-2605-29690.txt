Abstract
. We investigate critical polyharmonic equations of the following type: L ​ u = | u | 2 ♯ − 2 ​ u in ​ Ω Lu=|u|^{2^{\sharp}-2}u\quad\text{ in }\Omega with Dirichlet boundary conditions, in a smooth bounded domain Ω \Omega of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . Here L L is an elliptic differential operator of even integer order 2 ≤ 2 ​ k < n 2\leq 2k<n whose leading order term is ( − Δ ) k (-\Delta)^{k} and 2 ♯ = 2 ​ n n − 2 ​ k 2^{\sharp}=\frac{2n}{n-2k} is the critical Sobolev exponent. Our main result establishes, in large dimensions, uniform a priori bounds on bounded-energy solutions of this problem under a coercivity assumption of sorts on the lower-order terms of L L . Our results are sharp, at least when k = 1 k=1 . Our approach uses asymptotic analysis techniques and in the course of the proof we obtain in particular a new global pointwise description of bounded-energy blowing-up solutions for this problem, which is of independent interest.

Introduction

1.1.
Statement of the results

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 3
be a smooth bounded domain, and let
k
be an integer satisfying
1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}
. Throughout this paper
L
will denote an elliptic differential operator of the following form:

(1.1)

Lu=(-\Delta)^{k}u+\sum_{l=0}^{p}(-1)^{l}\nabla^{l}\big(A_{l}(\nabla^{l}u\,)\big)

for a smooth function
u
in
\Omega
, where
p\in\{0,\cdots,k-1\}
denotes a fixed integer and, for
0\leq l\leq p
,
A_{l}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
is a symmetric
(2l,0)
-tensor. In (
1.1
) we write classically
\nabla^{l}u=\big(\partial^{i_{1}\,\ldots\,i_{l}}u\big)_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{l}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}
for the
(0,l)
-tensor of the partial derivatives of
u
of order
l\geq 1
. In the particular case
k=1
,
L
as in (
1.1
) simply writes as
L=-\Delta+A_{0}
for some
A_{0}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. We investigate critical polyharmonic equations for
L
with Dirichlet boundary conditions in
\Omega
:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Lu=|u|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u&&\text{in }\Omega\\
&|\nabla^{l}u|=0&&\text{for }0\leq l\leq k-1\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
{2^{\sharp}}=\frac{2n}{n-2k}
is the critical exponent for Sobolev embeddings of
H^{k}(\Omega)
into Lebesgue spaces. Problem (
1.2
) has attracted uninterrupted attention since the seminal paper of Brézis–Nirenberg
[
BN83
]
in the case
k=1
and has originated a vast literature. For a suitable choice of constant coefficients
A_{l}
, (
1.2
) includes the case of the Brézis-Nirenberg problem
L=-\Delta-\lambda
for
\lambda\geq 0
(when
k=1
) and its higher-order counterparts. For operators
L
with general non-constant coefficients, existence and multiplicity results for (
1.2
) are in
[
HV94
]
(
k=1
) and
[
GWZ11a
]
(
k\geq 2
). Existence and multiplicity results for the Brézis-Nirenberg problem when
k=1
are in
[
CAP25
,
CFS84
,
CSS86
,
CZ15
,
CW05
,
DS02
,
RW09b
,
SZ10
,
TYZ22
]
and the references therein, and for its higher-order counterparts they have been obtained in
[
BGAP96
,
DW99
,
EFJ90
]
(
k=2
) and
[
Gru95
,
PS90
]
(
k\geq 3
).

We investigate in this paper compactness and
a priori
bounds results for energy-bounded solutions of (
1.2
). We let

(1.3)

\mathcal{A}_{p}=\Big\{\mathbb{A}=(A_{0},\cdots,A_{p}),A_{l}\in C^{l}(\overline{\Omega})\text{ if }l\geq 1,A_{0}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})\Big\},

where each
A_{l}
is a symmetric
(2l,0)
tensor, that we endow with the norm

\|A\|_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}=\sum_{l=1}^{p}\|A_{l}\|_{C^{l}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|A_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})}.

If
\mathbb{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
,
L
is as in (
1.1
) and
\Lambda>0
is fixed we define

\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{A},\Lambda}=\Big\{u\in H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)\text{ solution of }\eqref{eq:lmmain}\text{ with }\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{k}u|^{2}\,dx\leq\Lambda\Big\}.

Our main result states that, under a non-vanishing assumption on the tensor
A_{p}
of highest order, energy-bounded solutions of (
1.2
) possess
a priori
bounds in
C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})
that are uniform under perturbations of the coefficients of
L
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 3
and let
1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}
and
0\leq p\leq k-1
be integers. Let
\mathbb{A}_{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
be fixed,
\mathbb{A}_{*}=(A_{0},\cdots,A_{p})
, and let
L
be as in (
1.1
). We assume that
n>6k-4p
and that
\mathbb{A}_{*}
satisfies condition (
2.6
) below. Let
\Lambda>0
. Then there exist positive real numbers
\delta=\delta(n,\Lambda,\mathbb{A}_{*})
and
C=C(n,\Lambda,\mathbb{A}_{*})
such that

\sup_{\|\mathbb{A}_{*}-\mathbb{A}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}<\delta}\,\,\sup_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{A},\Lambda}}\|u\|_{C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C.

An important consequence of Theorem
1.1
, which follows from standard elliptic theory, is that the set of energy-bounded solutions of (
1.2
) for a fixed
L
is compact in strong topologies:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 3
and let
1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}
and
0\leq p\leq k-1
be integers. Let
\mathbb{A}_{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
be fixed,
\mathbb{A}_{*}=(A_{0},\cdots,A_{p})
, and let
L
be as in (
1.1
). We assume that
n>6k-4p
and that
\mathbb{A}_{*}
satisfies condition (
2.6
) below. Let
\Lambda>0
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{A}_{*},\Lambda}
is a compact subset of
C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})
.

We point out that we do not assume
\ker L=\{0\}
in Theorem
1.1
, but solely that (
2.6
) is satisfied. Our main assumption, condition (
2.6
), is defined and discussed in detail in subsection
2.1
below. It should be understood as a weak coercivity of sorts for
A_{p}
and states that, at each point
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, the quadratic form associated to
A_{p}(x_{0})
defines a norm on the space of limiting bubbling profiles for (
1.2
). Condition (
2.6
) is easy to verify and is satisfied for a large class of differential operators, including polyharmonic Brézis–Nirenberg operators such as

L=(-\Delta)^{k}+\lambda(-\Delta)^{p},

where
0\leq p\leq k-1
and
\lambda\neq 0
. When
p=0
this is the so-called Pucci–Serrin problem
[
PS90
]
, for which nonexistence of solutions when
\lambda>0
and
\Omega
is starshaped was proven in
[
PS86
]
. We do not restrict to starshaped domains in the following so we will not restrict to the case
\lambda\leq 0
. For polyharmonic Brézis–Nirenberg problems, Theorem
1.1
reformulates as follows:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 3
and let
1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}
and
0\leq p\leq k-1
be integers. Let
\lambda\neq 0
and
E>0
be fixed. We assume that
n>6k-4p
. There exist positive real numbers
\delta=\delta(\lambda,E)
and
C=C(\lambda,E)
such that for any
|\mu-\lambda|<\delta
and for any solution

of

(1.4)

(-\Delta)^{k}u+\mu(-\Delta)^{p}u=|u|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u\quad\text{ in }\Omega

satisfying
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{k}u|^{2}dx\leq E
, we have

\|u\|_{C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C.

When
k\geq 2
, condition (
2.6
) allows us to apply Theorem
1.1
to operators
L
which are not isotropic. We refer to Remark
2.3
below for an illustration.

When
k=1
we have
p=0
and
L=-\Delta+A_{0}
: the condition

simply becomes
n\geq 7
and (

) amounts to saying that
A_{0}
never vanishes in

(see subsection

and

is a nonnegative constant, equation (

) is the celebrated Brézis-Nirenberg problem and when

, Theorem

answers several open questions related to the existence of blowing-up solutions for this problem — by which we mean families of solutions which are unbounded in
C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. If first shows that the only value of

at which blow-up may occur when

is
\lambda=0
, and thus answers negatively
[
LVWW25b
, Open Question 6]
. As an immediate consequence, and still when

, it shows in particular that bubble-tower solutions may only appear as
\lambda\to 0
, which proves Conjecture 1 in
[
LVWW25b
]
; it also shows, still when

, that boundary concentration may only arise as

, partially answering
[
LVWW25b
, Open Question 8]
.

For a general

, Theorem

generalises the only previous compactness results on domains for (

), proven when

in
[
DS02
]
, which only covered the case where

was a negative constant: in particular, even when

is not constant or positive in

. As such, it is the euclidean counterpart of similar results previously proven on closed manifolds
[
Dru03
,
PV22c
]
. When

, the assumptions of Theorem

are sharp. First, (

) is necessary: families of solutions of (

which blow-up as

have for instance been constructed when

in
[

,

,
PV22a
,
Pre22
,

. Second, the dimensional restriction

sign-changing families of solutions of (

which blow-up as

approaches some

[
LVWW25a
,
LVWW25b
]
. As a remark, the solutions in

1.2.
Strategy of proof and organisation of the paper

Theorem
1.1
was previously proven for
k=1
and when
L=-\Delta+A_{0}
and
A_{0}
is a negative constant in
[
DS02
]
. The proof of
[
DS02
]
relied on the observation that, if
u
solves (
1.2
),
|u|
is a weak subsolution to a close variant of (
1.2
), and used the latter to prove integral bounds for
u
on annuli corresponding to scales where the solution may concentrate. When
k\geq 2
this approach trivially fails since the absolute value
|u|
of a function
u\in H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
is not in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
in general, and
(-\Delta)^{k}|u|
does not easily relate to
(-\Delta)^{k}u
in a weak sense. In this paper we thus adopt a new and radically different approach than
[
DS02
]
, which allows us to treat simultaneously the case of every
k\geq 1
. Our proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on asymptotic analysis techniques. Equation (
1.2
) has a critical nonlinearity on its right-hand side, and this implies that its solutions are likely to
blow-up
. By this we mean that they may not be compact in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
despite having bounded energy or, equivalently, they may be unbounded in
C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. We prove Theorem
1.1
by precisely showing, by a contradiction argument, that no blow-up may occur when
n>6k-4p
and (
2.6
) holds.

This approach requires us to first prove a very accurate description of the behavior of energy-bounded blowing-up sequences of (perturbations of) (
1.2
), which is of independent interest. This description is the subject of Theorem
5.1
below: it focuses most of our efforts in this work and is the main novelty of this paper. Theorem
5.1
should be understood as a quantitative version of classical
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
compactness results
à la
Struwe
[
Str84
]
(see
[
Maz17
]
for the polyharmonic case): it improves classical energy decompositions into global pointwise estimates, where the difference between blowing-up solutions and the bubbling profiles that they develop — which we call a
bubble-tree
(see Section
3
for precise definitions) — is precisely estimated in strong spaces. The estimates that we prove are valid in
\overline{\Omega}
and thus include the boundary, and an important feature of Theorem
5.1
is that it holds true regardless of the bubbling configuration: we do not assume anything on the bubbling profiles that may appear in the blow-up
1
1

1

There is an infinite number of them since our solutions may change sign, and so may the bubbling profiles
and we allow for bubbles at the boundary, whose existence is not ruled out yet when
k\geq 2
. Theorem
5.1
is the first instance of pointwise estimates up to the boundary for solutions of equations like (
1.2
) in a general bounded domain, and it builds upon very recent developments in the asymptotic analysis of sign-changing solutions for polyharmonic elliptic equations, that can be found in
[
Car25
,
CR25
,
Pre24
,
Rob25b
,
Rob25c
]
. The final part of the proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a polyharmonic Pohozaev identity, and the contradiction is obtained by analysing the contribution of lower-order terms in
L
, which are explicit to first-order due to the assumption (
2.6
). Estimating each term in the Pohozaev identity requires precise
pointwise
estimates on the solutions, and this is the reason we prove Theorem
5.1
.

The organisation of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we investigate some properties of the bubbling profiles that arise in the blow-up and which solve a model equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. We prove sharp pointwise decay for these bubbles as well as for solutions of their linearised equations. Section
3
introduces the notion of bubble-tree and analyses the interactions between the different bubbling profiles arising during the blow-up. Sections
4
and
5
are devoted to the proof of Theorem
5.1
. Section
4
is the core of the analysis of this paper and we prove in it a quantitative invertibility result for the linearisation of (
1.2
) around a fixed bubble-tree configuration. In Section
5
we then apply this result to prove Theorem
5.1
by a nonlinear perturbative argument. Section
6
is devoted to an analysis of the Pohozaev identity and concludes the proof of our main theorem. Finally the Appendix contains several technical results that are used throughout this paper, in particular a folklore result for Struwe-type decompositions whose proof for sign-changing solutions we could not find in the literature.

2.
Preliminary results

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any smooth domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 3
, not necessarily bounded. We define
D^{k,2}(U)
as the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
under the following norm

(2.1)

\left\|u\right\|_{D^{k,2}(U)}^{2}:=\int_{U}|\nabla^{k}u|^{2}\,dx.

When
U
is a bounded and smooth domain, (
2.1
) coincides with the classical
H^{k}_{0}(U)
norm and we will denote it by
\|\cdot\|_{H^{k}_{0}(U)}
. We endow the Hilbert spaces
D^{k,2}(U)
(and
H^{k}_{0}(U)
when
U
is bounded) with the scalar product induced by (
2.1
). We recall the following definition:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain with smooth boundary,
0\leq p\leq k-1
be an integer and let
(A_{l})_{0\leq l\leq p}
be continuous tensors in
\overline{U}
. Let
L
be defined as in (
1.1
) and let
u\in D^{k,2}(U)
. We say that
u
is a weak solution of (
1.2
) if

\int_{U}\big\langle(-\Delta)^{k/2}u,(-\Delta)^{k/2}\varphi\big\rangle\,dx+\sum_{l=0}^{p}\int_{U}A_{l}(\nabla^{l}u,\nabla^{l}\varphi)\,dx=\int_{U}|u|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u\varphi\,dx

for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
, where we have let
(-\Delta)^{k/2}=\nabla(-\Delta)^{m}
when
k=2m+1
is odd and where
\big\langle(-\Delta)^{k/2}u,(-\Delta)^{k/2}\varphi\big\rangle
denotes the (pointwise) scalar product in
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
depending on the parity of
k
.

Throughout the rest of this paper, the only unbounded example of of open sets that we will investigate will be
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, that we define as

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{x_{1}>0\}.

2.1.
Limiting equations and the compactness condition (
2.6
)

We study in this subsection two equations that will arise as limiting problems in the analysis of the blow-up behavior of (
1.2
). They are:

(2.2)

(-\Delta)^{k}u=|u|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad u\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

which will correspond to
interior
bubbling solutions, and

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{k}u&=|u|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\\
|\nabla^{l}u|&=0\quad\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\text{ for }l=0,\ldots,k-1\\
\end{aligned}\right.,\quad u\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

which will correspond to
boundary
bubbling solutions. We recall that
2^{\sharp}=\frac{2n}{n-2k}
. Solutions to (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) are defined in a weak sense as in Definition
2.1
and regularity theory for critical elliptic polyharmonic equations (see e.g.
[
Maz16
]
) shows that every solution to (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) is of class
C^{2k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(resp.
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
). Concerning (
2.2
), positive solutions are classified and are, up to scaling and translations, the so-called
standard bubble

(2.4)

B(x)=\big(1+a_{n,k}|x|^{2}\big)^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}},

where
a_{n,k}=\Big(\Pi_{l=-k}^{k-1}(n+2l)\Big)^{-1/k}
, as proven in
[
WX99
]
. The function
B
achieves the optimal constant for the critical Sobolev embedding
D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{2^{\sharp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By contrast, any sign-changing solution
u
of (
2.2
) satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2^{\sharp}}\,dx>2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|B|^{2^{\sharp}}\,dx
(see
[
GWZ11b
, Lemma 5]
for the large inequality and
[
HPP25
, Lemma A.1]
for the strict one) and there is a plethora of sign-changing solutions with arbitrarily large energy: see e.g.
[
Din86
,
dPMPP11
,
dPMPP13
,
MM21
,
MMW19
]
for the case
k=1

[
BSW04
,
GLW13
]
when
k\geq 2
. Concerning (
2.3
), if follows from the classical Pohozaev identity
[
Pz65
]
that no non-zero solutions exist when
k=1
, and it was proven in
[
RW09a
, Theorem 4]
that when
k\geq 2
no non-zero bounded non-negative solutions exist. The question of whether non-trivial sign-changing solutions of (

) exist for

remains open to this day.

) and (

Let

Here we see

(2p,0)

(2.6)

\displaystyle\text{ Either}\quad\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{A}}\subseteq(0,+\infty)\quad\text{ or }\quad\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{A}}\subseteq(-\infty,0).

Thus we assume that, for any

and any non-zero finite-energy solution
u
of (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), all the integrals
I_{\mathbb{A}}(x_{0},u)
are non-zero and have the same sign. As a first result we observe that (
2.6
) is satisfied for equations of Brézis-Nirenberg type and deduce Corollary
1.3
:

Proof of Corollary
1.3
assuming Theorem
1.1
.

Let
0\leq p\leq k-1
and
\lambda\neq 0
be fixed. For
\mu\neq 0
we let
A_{p,\mu}=\mu(\cdot,\cdot)
, where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the pointwise scalar product on
p
-symmetric tensors, and we let
\mathbb{A}_{\mu}=(0,\cdots,0,A_{p,\mu})\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
. For any
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
and any non-zero
u
solving (
2.2
) or (
2.3
) we have
I_{\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}}(x_{0},u)=\lambda\int_{U}|\nabla^{p}u|^{2}\,dx
, where
U=\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, and thus
\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}
satisfies (
2.6
). Theorem
1.1
thus applies to
L
associated to
\mathbb{A}_{\lambda}
as in (
1.1
). Integration by parts in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
shows that

L=(-\Delta)^{k}+\lambda(-\Delta)^{p},

and Corollary
1.3
now follows from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Condition (
2.6
) allows
L
to have anisotropic differential terms of lower order, and Theorem
1.1
does not only apply to operators like (
1.4
). For simplicity we illustrate this when
k=2
and
p=1
, but this example easily generalises to any order
k\geq 2
and
0\leq p\leq k-1
. Let
1\leq d<n
be fixed and let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{d}
be positive functions in
\overline{\Omega}
. Let, for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

A_{1}(x)=\text{Diag}(a_{1}(x),\cdots,a_{d}(x),0,\cdots,0),

let
A_{0}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be any function and let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{0},A_{1})\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Then
L=(-\Delta)^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}\big(a_{i}\partial_{i}\big)+A_{0}
and, for any
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
we have

I_{\mathbb{A}}(x_{0},u)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}(x_{0})\int_{U}(\partial_{i}u)^{2}\,dx

where
U=\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
depending on whether
u
solves (
2.2
) or (
2.3
). The latter is positive and
\mathbb{A}
thus satisfies (
2.6
). The same argument applies to the case where all the
a_{i}
are negative in
\overline{\Omega}
.

2.2.
Proof of Proposition
2.2

We prove Proposition
2.2
in what follows. The strategy of proof for the full space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is the same: we first transform (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) into critical equations on spaces of compact closure via a well-chosen conformal map, we then obtain global bounds for solutions on the compact space, and we translate back these bounds into decay on the original space. We will need some definitions in the half-space case
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Following the arguments in
[
RW09a
]
, we define a variant of the Kelvin transform that sends the unit ball to the upper half space. In the following we will let
B=B(0,1)
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We let

\Phi:\left\{\begin{aligned} B&\to\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\\
y&\mapsto\frac{y+e_{1}}{|y+e_{1}|^{2}}-\frac{e_{1}}{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

It is easily seen that
\Phi
is smooth and bijective and sends
\partial B\backslash\{-e_{1}\}
to
\{x_{1}=0\}=\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. For
y\in B
, straightforward computations show that
D\Phi(y)=\frac{1}{|y+e_{1}|^{2}}\big(Id-2M)

where
M=\frac{1}{|y+e_{1}|^{2}}(y+e_{1}){}^{t}(y+e_{1})
is a rank-one matrix. Thus

|\det D\Phi(y)|=|y+e_{1}|^{-2n}

and, if
\xi
denotes the euclidean metric both in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
B
, and for
y\in B
, we have
\Phi^{*}\xi(y)=\frac{1}{|y+e_{1}|^{4}}\xi
. For
u\in L^{2^{\sharp}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
we define its Cayley transform as follows:

(2.7)

u^{*}(y)=|\det D\Phi(y)|^{\frac{n-2k}{2n}}\,u\big(\Phi(y)\big)=|y+e_{1}|^{2k-n}\,u\big(\Phi(y)\big)\quad\text{ a.e. }y\in B.

A simple change of variables shows that
\Phi
leaves the critical norm invariant:

(2.8)

\int_{B}{|u^{*}(y)|^{2^{\sharp}}}dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u(x)|^{2^{\sharp}}\,dx.

If
U
denotes either the unit ball
B
or the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
we write
G_{U}\in L^{1}_{loc}(U\times U)
for the Green’s function of the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{k}u=f&\text{in }U\\
|\nabla^{l}u|=0&\text{on }\partial U,\quad\text{for }l=0,\ldots,k-1\end{cases}.

The arguments in
[
RW09a
, Section 3]
show that
G_{B}
and
G_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
are positive and are respectively given by:

\displaystyle G_{B}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{k,n}|x-y|^{2k-n}\int_{1}^{\big(1+\psi(x,y)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}}(t^{2}-1)^{k-1}t^{1-n}dt\quad\text{ for }\penalty\ x\neq y\text{ in }B,

\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{k,n}|x-y|^{2k-n}\int_{1}^{\big(1+\psi_{\infty}(x,y)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}}(t^{2}-1)^{k-1}t^{1-n}dt\quad\text{ for }\penalty\ x\neq y\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where
\kappa_{k,n}>0
is some numerical constant depending only on
n
and
k
and where

\psi(x,y)=\frac{(1-|x|^{2})(1-|y|^{2})}{|x-y|^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\psi_{\infty}(x,y)=\frac{4x_{1}y_{1}}{|x-y|^{2}}.

These formulas can also be derived from the formulas in
[
GGS10
, Formula (4.7)]
or
[
Bog05
]
. Direct computations using the expression of
\Phi
show that

(2.9)

\big|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\big|=\frac{|x-y|}{|x+e_{1}||y+e_{1}|}\quad\text{ for }x,y\in B,

and hence that

\psi_{\infty}\big(\Phi(x),\Phi(y)\big)=\psi(x,y)\quad\text{ for }x,y\in B.

The latter, together with the expression of
G_{B}
and
G_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
and again with (
2.9
), shows that for any
x\neq y\in B
the following holds:

(2.10)

G_{B}(x,y)=|x+e_{1}|^{2k-n}|y+e_{1}|^{2k-n}G_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(\Phi(x),\Phi(y)).

Let
v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Since
v
is supported away from
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, it is easily seen that its Cayley transform satisfies
v^{*}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B)
. A representation formula for
v^{*}
then shows that for any
x\in B
,

\displaystyle v^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}G_{B}(x,y)(-\Delta)^{k}v^{*}(y)dy

\displaystyle=|x+e_{1}|^{2k-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}G_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\big(\Phi(x),z\big)\big|\Phi^{-1}(z)+e_{1}\big|^{n+2k}\big[(-\Delta)^{k}v^{*}\big]\big(\Phi^{-1}(z)\big)dz,

where the last equality follows from (
2.10
) and a change of variables. Identifying with the definition of
v^{*}
then yields

\displaystyle v\big(\Phi(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}G_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\big(\Phi(x),z\big)\big|\Phi^{-1}(z)+e_{1}\big|^{n+2k}\big[(-\Delta)^{k}v^{*}\big]\big(\Phi^{-1}(z)\big)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}G_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\big(\Phi(x),z\big)(-\Delta)^{k}v(z)dz,

where the last equality is simply a representation formula for
v
. Since this equality is true for every
x\in B
we obtain that for any
y\in B
and any
v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
,

(2.11)

(-\Delta)^{k}v^{*}(y)=\frac{1}{\big|y+e_{1}\big|^{n+2k}}(-\Delta)^{k}v\big(\Phi(y)\big).

A consequence of (
2.11
) is that the
D^{k,2}
norm is also preserved via
\Phi
: integrating (
2.11
) by parts against
v^{*}
and using the expression of
v^{*}
indeed yields

(2.12)

\int_{B}\big|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}v^{*}(y)\big|^{2}dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\big|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}v(x)\big|^{2}dx.

We are now in position to prove Proposition
2.2
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

We first consider the entire space case. We adapt the proof in
[
Pre24
]
for the case
k=1
. Let
u\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a solution of (
2.2
). Let, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
U(x)^{\frac{4}{n-2k}}=4(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{S}^{n}\backslash\{N\}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the stereographic projection from the North pole and let, for
y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\tilde{u}(y)=\frac{u}{U}(\pi(y))
. If
g_{0}
denotes the round metric on the sphere it is well-known that
(\pi^{-1})^{*}g_{0}(x)=U(x)^{\frac{4}{n-2k}}\xi
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We recall that
(-\Delta)^{k}
is the Graham–Jenne–Mason–Sparling operator
[
GJMS92
]
for the euclidean metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and we denote by
P_{g_{0}}
the GJMS operator of order
2k
on the round sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0})
. The conformal invariance of
P_{g_{0}}
shows that, for any
y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\tilde{u}
satisfies

(2.13)

P_{g_{0}}\tilde{u}(y)=U(\pi(y))^{1-{2^{\sharp}}}(-\triangle)^{k}u(\pi(y))=|\tilde{u}(y)|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\tilde{u}(y)\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{S}^{n}.

P_{g_{0}}
is explicit and writes as
P_{g_{0}}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Big(\Delta_{g_{0}}+\frac{(n+2j-2)(n-2j)}{4}\Big)

(see e.g.
[
FG12
, Proposition 7.9]
) and is thus coercive in
H^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. A consequence of the first equality in (
2.13
) and of the assumption
u\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is that
\tilde{u}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. With (
2.13
) we may thus apply the regularity theory of
[
Maz16
, Proposition 8.3]
and we obtain that
\tilde{u}\in C^{2k}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Since, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
u(x)=U(x)\tilde{u}(\pi^{-1}(x))
, we obtain that
|u(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{2k-n}
for some numerical constant
C
which does not depend on
x
. This proves Proposition
2.2
in the case
l=0
. The proof for
1\leq l\leq 2k
now follows from local elliptic estimates in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We now consider the half-space case. Let
u\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
be a weak solution of (
2.3
) and let
u^{*}
be its Cayley transform. By Sobolev’s inequality,
u\in L^{{2^{\sharp}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and hence (
2.8
) and (
2.12
) show that
u^{*}\in H^{k}(B)
. It is a simple observation that
u^{*}\in H^{k}_{0}(B)
: this follows from the definition of
H^{k}_{0}(B)
as the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(B)
, the fact that if
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
then
\varphi^{*}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B)
, and (
2.12
). A change of variable using the weak formulation of (
2.3
) and the properties of
\Phi
shows, by using (
2.11
), that
u^{*}
satisfies weakly

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{k}u^{*}=|u^{*}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u^{*}&\text{in }B\\
|\nabla^{l}u^{*}|=0&\text{for }l=0,\ldots,k-1,\quad\text{on }\partial B\end{cases}

in the sense of Definition
2.1
. Standard elliptic regularity theory then shows that
u^{*}\in C^{2k}(\overline{B})
and that there exists
C>0
such that

(2.14)

\|\nabla^{l}u^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq C

for every
l=0,\ldots 2k
. Going back to the definition of
u^{*}
we have, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,

(2.15)

u(x)=\big|\Phi^{-1}(x)+e_{1}\big|^{n-2k}u^{*}\big(\Phi^{-1}(x)\big).

Using the definition of
\Phi
it is easily seen that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
we have

\Phi^{-1}(x)=\frac{x+\frac{e_{1}}{2}}{\big|x+\frac{e_{1}}{2}\big|^{2}}-e_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\big|\Phi^{-1}(x)+e_{1}\big|=\frac{1}{\big|x+\frac{e_{1}}{2}\big|}\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|}

for some numerical constant
C
that does not depend on
x
. With (
2.14
) we thus get

|u(x)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+|x|)^{n-2k}},

which proves Proposition
2.2
in the case
l=0
. The proof for
1\leq l\leq 2k
now follows from (
2.14
), (
2.15
) and the chain rule, since the explicit expression of
\Phi^{-1}
shows that
|\nabla^{l}\Phi^{-1}(x)|\leq\frac{C_{l}}{(1+|x|)^{l+1}}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.
∎

2.3.
Estimates on solutions of linearised equations

In our analysis we will also investigate the linearisations of (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) at a non-zero solution. If
v\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a solution of (
2.2
) we let

(2.16)

\mathcal{K}_{v}=\{h\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,:\,(-\Delta)^{k}h=({2^{\sharp}}-1)|v|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}h\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\}.

Similarly, if
v\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is a solution of (
2.3
) we let

(2.17)

\mathcal{K}_{v}=\{h\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\,:\,(-\Delta)^{k}h=({2^{\sharp}}-1)|v|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}h\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\}.

We prove the following optimal decay for elements in
\mathcal{K}_{v}
:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
v
be a solution of (
2.2
) (resp. (
2.3
)) and let
Z\in\mathcal{K}_{v}
, where
\mathcal{K}_{v}
is as in (
2.16
) (resp. (
2.17
)). There exists
C>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(resp.
x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
) and for any
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
,

|\nabla^{l}Z(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{2k-n-l}.

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines and uses the same notations than the proof of Proposition
2.2
and we only sketch the main steps.

Consider first consider the entire space case. Let
Z\in\mathcal{K}_{v}
and let, for
y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\tilde{Z}(y)=\frac{Z}{U}(\pi(y))
. Then
\tilde{Z}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
and the conformal invariance of
P_{g_{0}}
shows that, for any
y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\tilde{Z}
satisfies

P_{g_{0}}\tilde{Z}(y)=a(y)\tilde{Z}(y)\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{S}^{n},\quad\text{ where }\quad a(y)=(2^{\sharp}-1)\Big(\frac{v(\pi(y))}{U(\pi(y))}\Big)^{2^{\sharp}-2}.

By Proposition
2.2
,
a\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
, so that by standard elliptic theory
\tilde{Z}\in C^{2k-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. We conclude as in the proof of Proposition
2.2
.

We now consider the half-space case. Let
v\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
be a weak solution of (
2.3
), let
v^{*}
be its Cayley transform and let
Z\in\mathcal{K}_{v}
. As before,
Z^{*}\in H^{k}_{0}(B)
, and a change of variable using the weak formulation of (
2.3
) and the properties of
\Phi
shows, by using (
2.11
), that
Z^{*}
satisfies weakly

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{k}Z^{*}=(2^{\sharp}-1)|u^{*}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}Z^{*}&\text{in }B\\
|\nabla^{l}Z^{*}|=0&\text{for }l=0,\ldots,k-1,\quad\text{on }\partial B\end{cases}.

As proven in (
2.14
) we have
u^{*}\in L^{\infty}(B)
and hence standard elliptic theory shows that
Z^{*}\in C^{2k-1}(\overline{B})
. We again conclude as in the proof of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

3.
Bubble-tree configurations

In this section we define the bubbling profiles that we will use in this work and we introduce the notion of bubble-tree.

3.1.
Bubbling profiles and weak
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
compactness

Until the end of this paper
\Omega
will denote a smooth bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 3
. Smoothness ensures the existence of
r_{0}>0
and, for any
x\in\partial\Omega
, of an open set
U_{x}
containing
x
and of a diffeomorphism
\sigma_{x}:B(0,r_{0})\to U_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\sigma_{x}(0)=x
,
(x,z)\mapsto\sigma_{x}(z)
is smooth from
\partial\Omega\times B(0,r_{0})
to
\overline{\Omega}
, and

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}\big(B(0,r_{0})\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\big)=U_{x}\cap\Omega,\quad

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}\big(B(0,r_{0})\cap\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=U_{x}\cap\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle d\sigma_{x}(0):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ is an isometry, }\quad

\displaystyle\text{ and }d\sigma_{x}(0)[-e_{1}]=\nu(x)

where
\nu(x)
is the outer unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
at
x
. Throughout this paper we will let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a cut-off function, such that
\chi\equiv 1
in
B(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\chi\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,1)
. Following
[
Maz17
, Definition 2.1]
we define

Definition 3.1

(Interior bubble)
.

We call
interior bubble
a triple
[(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v]
, where
(\mu_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is a sequence of positive numbers converging to
0
as
\alpha\to+\infty
,
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is a convergent sequence of points in
\Omega
such that
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{dist}(x_{\alpha},\partial\Omega)}{\mu_{\alpha}}=+\infty
, and
v\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a non-zero solution of (
2.2
). If
[(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v]
is an interior bubble we define, for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

V_{\alpha}(x)=\chi\Bigg(\frac{x-x_{\alpha}}{\operatorname{dist}(x_{\alpha},\partial\Omega)}\Bigg)\mu_{\alpha}^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}v\Big(\frac{x-x_{\alpha}}{\mu_{\alpha}}\Big).

We will sometimes refer to
V_{\alpha}
as the bubble.

Definition 3.2

(boundary bubble)
.

We call
boundary bubble
a triple
[(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v]
, where
(\mu_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is a sequence of positive numbers converging to
0
as
\alpha\to+\infty
,
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is a convergent sequence of points in
\partial\Omega
and
v\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is a non-zero solution of (
2.3
). If
[(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v]
is a boundary bubble we define, for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

V_{\alpha}(x)=\chi\big(\sigma_{x_{\alpha}}^{-1}(x)\big)\mu_{\alpha}^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}v\Big(\frac{\sigma_{x_{\alpha}}^{-1}(x)}{\mu_{\alpha}}\Big).

We will also sometimes refer to
V_{\alpha}
as the bubble.

We could equivalently define a boundary bubble as a triple
[(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v]
, where
\operatorname{dist}(x_{\alpha},\partial\Omega)=O(\mu_{\alpha})
and
v\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
solves (
2.3
). Up to replacing
x_{\alpha}
by its projection onto
\partial\Omega
(which is uniquely defined since
\mu_{\alpha}\to 0
), however, we may assume with no loss of generality that
x_{\alpha}\in\partial\Omega
for all
\alpha
, which justifies Definition
3.2
. Remark that for an interior bubble as in Definition
3.1
it is perfectly possible that
\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}x_{\alpha}\in\partial\Omega
. Direct computations show that in either case we have
V_{\alpha}\in H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
,
V_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
and, as
\alpha\to+\infty
,

\|V_{\alpha}\|_{H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)}=\begin{cases}\left\|v\right\|_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\smallo(1)&\text{for an interior bubble,}\\
\left\|v\right\|_{D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}+\smallo(1)&\text{for a boundary bubble. }\\
\end{cases}

Throughout this paper we will investigate sequences of solutions of perturbations of (
1.2
), where the operator
L
given by (
1.1
) is replaced by a sequence of differential operators
(L_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
. The perturbations of
L
that we will consider are as follows: if
0\leq p\leq k-1
is as in (
1.1
) we will let

(3.1)

L_{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{k}+\sum_{l=0}^{p}\nabla^{l}(-1)^{l}\big(A_{l,\alpha}(\nabla^{l}\,\cdot\,)\big),

where, for
l=0,\ldots,p
,
A_{l,\alpha}
is a smooth symmetric
(2l,0)
-tensor such that
A_{l,\alpha}\to A_{l}
in
C^{l}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\alpha\to\infty
. We will investigate the blow-up behavior of energy-bounded sequences of solutions
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
that satisfy the following critical polyharmonic equations with Dirichlet boundary conditions:

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &L_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}=|u_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u_{\alpha}&&\text{in }\Omega\\
&|\nabla^{l}u_{\alpha}|=0&&\text{for }l=0,\ldots,k-1,\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of solutions of (
3.2
). We assume that
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is bounded in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
, that is
\limsup_{\alpha\to+\infty}\|u_{\alpha}\|_{H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)}<+\infty
, and that it blows-up as
\alpha\to+\infty
, that is
\|u_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=+\infty
. By (
3.1
) such a sequence is a Palais-Smale sequence for the functional over
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
defined by

(3.3)

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{k/2}u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{p}\int_{\Omega}A_{l}(\nabla^{l}u,\nabla^{l}u)\,dx-\frac{1}{{2^{\sharp}}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{{2^{\sharp}}}\,dx

for which (
1.2
) is the associated Euler-Lagrange equation. We may thus apply the global compactness result of
[
Maz17
, Theorem 1.2]
, that generalizes the celebrated result of
[
Str84
]
for
k=1
: it shows that there exist
u_{0}\in H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
a solution to (
1.2
), an integer
N\geq 1
and
N
bubbles
V^{i}_{\alpha}
defined as in Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
by their triples
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
,
i=1,\ldots,N
such that up to a subsequence we have

(3.4)

u_{\alpha}=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}V^{i}_{\alpha}+\smallo(1)\qquad\text{in }H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)

and

(3.5)

I(u_{\alpha})=I(u_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{N}I_{0}(v^{i})+\smallo(1),

where we have let

(3.6)

I_{0}(v^{i})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(-\Delta)^{k/2}v^{i}|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{{2^{\sharp}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v^{i}|^{{2^{\sharp}}}\,dx\\
&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|(-\Delta)^{k/2}v^{i}|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{{2^{\sharp}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|v^{i}|^{{2^{\sharp}}}\,dx\end{aligned}\right.

depending on whether
v^{i}
is an interior or a boundary bubble. Moreover, the sequences
(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
satisfy the fundamental structure relation

(3.7)

\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{ij}=\frac{|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}+\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}+\frac{\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\to\infty\quad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty,

for all
i\neq j
in
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Relation (
3.7
) implies that the bubbles
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
do not interact at first-order in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
. Relation (
3.7
) has been known for positive solutions of equations like (
3.2
) for a long time, where it appeared e.g.
[
BC88
]
(see also
[
Heb14
, Chapter 3]
for a proof). For
sign-changing
solutions of equations like (
3.2
), relation (
3.7
) has also been known to hold true for a long time (it is e.g. mentioned in
[
Str08
, Chapter III, Remark 3.2]
in the case
k=1
) and is a folklore-type result but, to the best of our knowledge, no proof seems to have appeared in the literature, not even in the case
k=1
. For the sake of completeness we provide a simple proof of (
3.7
) in Appendix
B
below.

3.2.
Bubble-tree configurations

To prove Theorem
1.1
we will show that, for sequences
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of energy-bounded solutions of (
3.2
), the decomposition (
3.4
) can be improved into strong global pointwise bounds in
\overline{\Omega}
. In order to do this we will analyse the pointwise behavior of any configuration that may arise in (
3.4
). Let
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
be a solution to (
1.2
),
N\geq 1
be an integer and let
N
bubbles
V^{i}_{\alpha}
, defined as in Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
. We allow indistinctly interior and boundary bubbles, but we assume that (
3.7
) is satisfied. We let

\mathcal{K}_{0}=\{h\in H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)\,:\,Lh=({2^{\sharp}}-1)|u_{0}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}h\text{ in }\Omega\},

where
L
is as in (
1.1
). Since
L
is elliptic
\mathcal{K}_{0}
is finite-dimensional: we let
d_{0}=\dim\mathcal{K}_{0}
and we let
Z^{01},\ldots,Z^{0d_{0}}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{K}_{0}
in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
with respect to the
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
scalar product (
2.1
). For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
we represent the bubble
V^{i}_{\alpha}
by its triple
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
.

•

If
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is an interior bubble we let
\mathcal{K}_{v^{i}}
be given by (
2.16
),

d_{i}=\dim\mathcal{K}_{v^{i}}
, we let
Z^{i1},\ldots,Z^{id_{i}}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{K}_{v^{i}}
in
D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and we define, for
j=1,\ldots d_{i}
and for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

Z^{ij}_{\alpha}(x)=\chi\Big(\frac{x-x^{i}_{\alpha}}{\operatorname{dist}(x^{i}_{\alpha},\partial\Omega)}\Big)(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}Z^{ij}\Big(\frac{x-x^{i}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\Big),

where
\chi
is as in definition
3.1
.

•

If
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is a boundary bubble we let
\mathcal{K}_{v^{i}}
be given by (
2.17
),

d_{i}=\dim\mathcal{K}_{v^{i}}
, we let
Z^{i1},\ldots,Z^{id_{i}}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{K}_{v^{i}}
in
D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and we define, for
j=1,\ldots d_{i}
and for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

Z^{ij}_{\alpha}(x)=\chi\big(\sigma_{x^{i}_{\alpha}}^{-1}(x)\big)(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}Z^{ij}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{x^{i}_{\alpha}}^{-1}(x)}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\Big),

where
\chi
and
\sigma_{x^{i}_{\alpha}}
are as in definition
3.2
.

Simple computations show that
Z^{ij}_{\alpha}\in H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
and

(3.8)

\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{k}Z^{i_{1}j_{1}}_{\alpha}Z^{i_{2}j_{2}}_{\alpha}\,dx=\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}}\delta_{j_{1}j_{2}}+\smallo(1)

as
\alpha\to\infty
, for
i_{1},i_{2}\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
,
j_{1}\in\{1,\ldots,d_{i_{1}}\},\,j_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,d_{i_{2}}\}
. For
1\leq i\leq N
we define
\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha}=\operatorname{span}\left\{Z^{ij}_{\alpha},\,j=1,\ldots,d_{i}\right\}\subset H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
. For simplicity we will write
Z^{0j}_{\alpha}=Z^{0j}
even if
Z^{0j}
does not depend on
\alpha
. We define

(3.9)

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{K}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha}.

This space will act as an approximate kernel for the linearised equation of (
3.2
) at a bubbling solution. We may now introduce the definition of a bubble-tree configuration that we will use in this paper:

Definition 3.3

(Bubble-tree configuration)
.

Let
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
be a solution of (
1.2
), let
N\geq 1
be an integer and let
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
,
i=1,\ldots,N
be
N
bubbles as in Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
, satisfying (
3.7
). For
0\leq i\leq N
and
1\leq j\leq d_{i}
, where
d_{i}=\dim\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha}
, let
(\nu^{ij}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be any sequence of real numbers such that
\nu^{ij}_{\alpha}\to 0
. For
\alpha\geq 1
we define

(3.10)

\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}V^{i}_{\alpha}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots N\\
j=1,\ldots d_{i}\end{subarray}}\nu^{ij}_{\alpha}Z^{ij}_{\alpha}.

We say that
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
is a
bubble-tree
defined by
u_{0}
and
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, and
\nu_{\alpha}^{ij}
,
0\leq i\leq N
,
1\leq j\leq d_{i}
.

Clearly,
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\limsup_{\alpha\to+\infty}\|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}\|_{H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)}<+\infty
. Up to a small perturbation in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
along
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
,
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
is modeled on the first-order terms that arise in the Struwe decomposition (
3.4
). We will often compare each bubble
V^{i}_{\alpha}
with its associated positive bubble, that we define as follows. We let, for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle B^{0}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=1\quad\text{ and, for every }1\leq i\leq N,

\displaystyle B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}{(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{2}+a_{n,k}|x-x^{i}_{\alpha}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\mu^{i}_{\alpha}+|x-x^{i}_{\alpha}|

where
a_{n,k}
is as in (
2.4
). We define
B^{0}_{\alpha}(x)\equiv 1
in (
3.11
) for simplicity but also to highlight that we will consider, in our analysis, the weak limit
u_{0}
as the zero-th bubbling profile. Note that
u_{0}
may be everywhere zero in Definition
3.3
, but we still define
B^{0}_{\alpha}\equiv 1
in that case nonetheless. We introduce the following notation: until the end of the paper, the inequality

F_{\alpha}\lesssim G_{\alpha},

that we will use for real numbers or functions, will mean that there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
\alpha
such that
F_{\alpha}\leq CG_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\geq 1
. It is easily seen with (
3.11
) that

(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{2k-n}\lesssim B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{2k-n}\quad\text{ for all }x\in\overline{\Omega}.

By the definition of
V^{i}_{\alpha}
, and by (
2.5
), we get that for
0\leq l\leq 2k
, for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

(3.12)

|\nabla^{l}V^{i}_{\alpha}(x)|\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x).

Concerning the kernel elements, it follows from elliptic theory that for
j=1,\ldots,d_{0}
,
Z^{0j}\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})
. Similarly, it follows from Lemma
2.4
and from direct computations using the expression of
Z^{ij}_{\alpha}
that for
i=1,\ldots,N
,
j=1,\ldots,d_{i}
, and for
0\leq l\leq 2k
and
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, we have

(3.13)

|\nabla^{l}Z^{ij}_{\alpha}(x)|\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x).\\

Combining the previous estimates gives, for
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
and all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

(3.14)

|\nabla^{l}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}(x)|\lesssim 1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x).

The constant in (
3.14
) depends on
n,k,u_{0},(v^{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
.

3.3.
Radii of influence and bubble-tree computations

Throughout this subsection we let
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
be a bubble-tree configuration as given by Definition
3.3
. The bubbles
V^{i}_{\alpha}
,
1\leq i\leq N
, that appear in
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
may concentrate at different speeds and may take very different pointwise values in
\overline{\Omega}
. In this subsection we decompose
\overline{\Omega}
in regions where each bubble
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is dominant at a pointwise level. This information will be crucial to prove sharp global pointwise estimates in Theorem
5.1
below by an inductive approach. We rely for this on the notion of
radius of influence
, which was originally introduced in
[
Dru03
]
for positive bubbles (see also
[
Heb14
, Chapter 8.2]
). As observed in
[
Pre24
]
, the notion of radius of influence remains just as useful to obtain pointwise estimates for sign-changing bubbles.

Up to passing to a subsequence for
(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
, and up to renumbering the bubbles in
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
, we can assume that

(3.15)

\mu^{N}_{\alpha}\leq\ldots\leq\mu^{1}_{\alpha}<1

for all
\alpha\geq 1
. Let
i\in\{1,\ldots N\}
be fixed. We define

(3.16)

\mathcal{A}_{i}=\{j\neq i\,:\,\mu^{i}_{\alpha}=\bigO(\mu^{j}_{\alpha})\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{A}_{i}^{c}=\{j\neq i\,:\,\mu^{j}_{\alpha}=\smallo(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})\}.

Indices in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
refer to bubbles
V_{\alpha}^{j}
,
j\neq i
, which concentrate at a speed slower than or comparable to
V^{i}_{\alpha}
, while indices in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
refer to bubbles
V_{\alpha}^{j}
,
j\neq i
, which concentrate strictly faster than
V^{i}_{\alpha}
. We will often refer to these bubble, respectively, as to
lower
and
higher
bubbles than
V^{i}_{\alpha}
. Note that
\Omega
is a bounded open set here, so the speed of concentration for bubbles is solely measured in terms of the size of
\mu_{\alpha}^{i}
, and not by
\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{ij}
in (
3.7
) (as was e.g. the case in
[
DSW25
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). Remark that by (
3.15
), we have
\{1,\ldots,i-1\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{i}
for
i>1
, and
\mathcal{A}_{i}^{c}\subseteq\{i+1,\ldots,N\}
for
i<N
.

Let
j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
, so that
\mu^{i}_{\alpha}=\bigO(\mu^{j}_{\alpha})
and
V^{j}_{\alpha}
is lower than
V^{i}_{\alpha}
. We define
s^{ij}_{\alpha}
as follows:

•

If
\mu^{i}_{\alpha}=o(\mu^{j}_{\alpha})
, we define

\big(s^{ij}_{\alpha}\big)^{2}=\frac{1}{a_{n,k}}\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}\Big((\mu^{j}_{\alpha})^{2}+a_{n,k}|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|^{2}\Big)

•

If
\mu^{j}_{\alpha}=\bigO(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})
, that is if we have in addition
i\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
, we also have
\frac{|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\to\infty
by (
3.7
). In this case we let
m_{ij}=\limsup\limits_{\alpha\to\infty}\Big(\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}+\frac{\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\Big)\in(0,+\infty)
, and we define

\big(s^{ij}_{\alpha}\big)^{2}=\frac{1}{4a_{n,k}}\frac{1}{m_{ij}}\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}\Big((\mu^{j}_{\alpha})^{2}+a_{n,k}|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|^{2}\Big),

where
a_{n,k}
is as in (
2.4
). It follows from the definition of
B^{i}_{\alpha}
in (
3.11
) that
B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\geq B^{j}_{\alpha}(x^{i}_{\alpha})
for every
x\in B(x^{i}_{\alpha},s^{ij}_{\alpha})
, whenever
j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
, and this is the reason why we include
\frac{1}{a_{n,k}}
in our definition of
s^{ij}_{\alpha}
. Moreover, a simple computation shows that if
j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
, for any
0\leq l\leq 2k
and
x\in B(x^{i}_{\alpha},s^{ij}_{\alpha})
we have

(3.17)

\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim\frac{(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}}{(s^{ij}_{\alpha})^{n-2k+l}}\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x).

Specialising (
3.17
) to
l=0
shows in particular that
s^{ij}_{\alpha}
measures the radius around
x^{i}_{\alpha}
up to which
B^{i}_{\alpha}
is pointwise larger than
B^{j}_{\alpha}
. In the special case of
comparable
bubbles where
j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
i\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
, ie when
\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\lesssim\mu^{j}_{\alpha}\lesssim\mu^{i}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\geq 1
, the definition of
s^{ij}_{\alpha}
shows that
s^{ij}_{\alpha}+s^{ji}_{\alpha}\leq\frac{|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|}{2}
for
\alpha
big enough, so that

B(x^{i}_{\alpha},s^{ij}_{\alpha})\cap B(x^{j}_{\alpha},s^{ji}_{\alpha})={\text{\Large\o}}.

This is the motivation for the additional normalisation factor
\frac{1}{4m_{ij}}
when
i\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
1\leq i\leq N
. We define the radius of influence of
V^{i}_{\alpha}
as

(3.18)

r^{i}_{\alpha}=\min\left\{\min_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}s^{ij}_{\alpha},\sqrt{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\right\}.

In view of (
3.17
)
r^{i}_{\alpha}
should be understood as the largest radius of a ball centered at
x^{i}_{\alpha}
inside which the positive bubble
B^{i}_{\alpha}
is
pointwise
larger than all the other lower positive bubbles. This also includes the non-concentrated zero-th bubble
B^{0}_{\alpha}\equiv 1
since, as can be easily checked,
B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\geq(1+\smallo(1))a_{n,k}^{k-\frac{n}{2}}
when
|x-x^{i}_{\alpha}|\leq\sqrt{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}
. It follows from (
3.7
) and the definition of
s^{ij}_{\alpha}
that
\frac{s^{ij}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\gtrsim\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{ij}
as
\alpha\to+\infty
for every
j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
. As a consequence, and for any
1\leq i\leq N
,

(3.19)

\frac{r^{i}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\gtrsim\min\Bigg(\min_{1\leq i\leq N}(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{-\frac{1}{2}},\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots N\\
j\neq i\,:\,\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\lesssim\mu^{j}_{\alpha}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{ij}\Bigg)\to+\infty

as
\alpha\to+\infty
. We showed that in
B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha})
,
B^{i}_{\alpha}
pointwise dominates the positive lower bubbles. But inside
B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha})
there can still be higher bubbles interacting with
B^{i}_{\alpha}
. They are described by the following set of indices:

(3.20)

\mathcal{B}_{i}=\{j\neq i\,:\,\mu^{j}_{\alpha}=o(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})\text{ and }|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|\leq 2r^{i}_{\alpha}\}.

The following result shows that, as long as pointwise estimates on derivatives of the bubble-tree
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
are considered in
B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha})
, one only has to take into account the
i
-th bubble and higher bubbles belonging to
\mathcal{B}_{i}
:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
1\leq i\leq N
and
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
be fixed. For any
x\in B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha})
we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x).

Proof.

Let
j\neq i
. If
j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
and since
r^{i}_{\alpha}\leq s^{ij}_{\alpha}
by definition we have
\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)
for any
x\in B(x^{i}_{\alpha}r^{i}_{\alpha})
by (
3.17
). If
j\not\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
then
\mu^{j}_{\alpha}=\smallo(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})
as
\alpha\to+\infty
and two cases may occur: either
j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
as in (
3.20
) or
j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}^{c}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{i}
. In the latter case we have
\mu^{j}_{\alpha}=o(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})
and
|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|>2r^{i}_{\alpha}
. Therefore, we have
|x-x^{j}_{\alpha}|\geq r^{i}_{\alpha}
for all
x\in B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha})
, so that

\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\leq C\left(\frac{\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\frac{(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}}{(r^{i}_{\alpha})^{n-2k+l}}=\smallo\big(\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\big)

as
\alpha\to+\infty
, where we again used (
3.17
). Finally, since
r^{i}_{\alpha}\lesssim\sqrt{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}
we have
1\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)
for all
x\in B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha})
. This proves (
3.21
).
∎

A consequence of (
3.21
) and of the definition of
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
in Definition
3.3
is that, for any
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
, we have

(3.22)

|\nabla^{l}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}(x)|\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\quad\text{ for }x\in B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha}).

Let now
j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
be fixed. It follows easily from the definition of
s_{\alpha}^{ji}
and from the property
\mu^{j}_{\alpha}=\smallo(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})
that there exists
C>0
such that if
B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\geq\frac{1}{C}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)
then
|x-x^{j}_{\alpha}|\leq Cs_{\alpha}^{ji}
. As a consequence,
B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\leq CB^{i}_{\alpha}(x)
when
|x-x^{j}_{\alpha}|\geq Cs_{\alpha}^{ji}
. Since
\mu^{j}_{\alpha}=\smallo(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})
we have
s_{\alpha}^{ji}=\smallo(|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|)
by definition of
s_{\alpha}^{ji}
, and thus direct computations give

B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\leq CB^{i}_{\alpha}(x^{j}_{\alpha})=(1+\smallo(1))CB^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\quad\text{ for }s_{\alpha}^{ji}\leq|x-x^{j}_{\alpha}|\leq Cs_{\alpha}^{ji}.

Specialising (
3.22
) to
l=0
with the latter shows in particular that

(3.23)

|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}(x)|\lesssim B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\quad\text{ for }x\in B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha})\backslash\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}B(x^{j}_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}^{ji}).

Estimate (
3.23
) shows that in the region
B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha})\backslash\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}B(x^{j}_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}^{ji})
the
i
-th positive bubble is
pointwise
dominant in the bubble-tree
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
. Partitioning
\Omega
in regions where each positive bubble
B^{i}_{\alpha},1\leq i\leq N
, is dominant at the pointwise level will provide us with a top-bottom inductive scheme to prove the pointwise estimates of Theorem
5.1
below. As it turns out, though, (
3.23
) is not enough for our purposes, since we will need precise estimates on all the derivatives of
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
up to order
2k-1
. In the following we modify the definition of
r^{i}_{\alpha}
in order to control the sum over
\mathcal{B}_{i}
in (
3.22
) solely by the
i
-th bubble term:

Definition 3.6
.

Let
1\leq i\leq N
and let
j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
. We define

(3.24)

\rho^{ji}_{\alpha}=2\left(\frac{\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{n-2k}{2(n-1)}}\big(|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{i}_{\alpha}|+\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\big).

It is simple to check that for any
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
we have

(3.25)

(\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x))^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\gtrsim(\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x))^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\quad\text{ for every }\quad x\in B(x^{i}_{\alpha},\rho^{ji}_{\alpha}),

that there exists
C>0
such that

\text{ if }\quad(\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x))^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\geq\frac{1}{C}(\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x))^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\quad\text{ then }\quad|x-x^{j}_{\alpha}|\leq C\rho^{ji}_{\alpha}

and that

(3.26)

(\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x))^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim(\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x))^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\quad\text{ when }|x-x^{j}_{\alpha}|\geq\rho^{ji}_{\alpha}.

By analogy with
s^{ij}_{\alpha}
above,
\rho^{ji}_{\alpha}
is thus defined as the maximum distance from
x^{j}_{\alpha}
up to which
(\theta^{j}_{\alpha})^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}
is pointwise larger than
(\theta^{i}_{\alpha})^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}
. Since
|\nabla^{l}B^{j}_{\alpha}|
is of the order of
(\theta^{j}_{\alpha})^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}
this should be understood as the maximum distance from
x^{j}_{\alpha}
up to which
|\nabla^{l}B^{j}_{\alpha}|
dominates
|\nabla^{l}B^{i}_{\alpha}|
pointwise.

By definition of
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, for every
j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
we have
|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|\leq 2r^{i}_{\alpha}
and hence, since
\mu^{j}_{\alpha}=\smallo(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})
,
\rho^{ji}_{\alpha}=o(r^{i}_{\alpha})
. Since
\frac{n-2k}{2(n-1)}<\frac{1}{2}
we also easily have
s^{ji}_{\alpha}=o(\rho^{ji}_{\alpha})
. Moreover, when
j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
is such that
\frac{|x^{j}_{\alpha}-x^{i}_{\alpha}|}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}=\bigO(1)
, we get that
\rho^{ji}_{\alpha}=o(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})
. For a fixed
1\leq i\leq N
we now define

(3.27)

\Omega^{i}_{\alpha}=\Omega\cap\Big(B(x^{i}_{\alpha},r^{i}_{\alpha})\setminus\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}B(x^{j}_{\alpha},\rho^{ji}_{\alpha})\Big).

We have the following Lemma:

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. For any
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
and for any
x\in\overline{\Omega^{i}_{\alpha}}
we have

1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x).

A consequence of Lemma
3.7
and of (
3.22
) is that, for any
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
and any
x\in\overline{\Omega^{i}_{\alpha}}
, we have

|\nabla^{l}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}(x)|\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x).

Lemma
3.7
shows that
\Omega^{i}_{\alpha}
can be understood as the region in
\Omega
where
V^{i}_{\alpha}
, and all its derivatives up to order
2k-1
, are pointwise dominant in the bubble-tree
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
. We will sometimes refer to it as the
region of influence of the
i
-th bubble
V^{i}_{\alpha}
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the definitions of
r^{i}_{\alpha}
and
\Omega^{i}_{\alpha}
. If
j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
, (
3.26
) shows that

\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)

for all

. The result then follows from (
3.21
).
∎

Remark 3.8

It is possible to give a subtler definition of

We conclude this subsection by illustrating how the notion of radius of influence allows us to precisely estimate the pointwise interactions between the different bubbling profiles:

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
1\leq i\leq N
be fixed, let
B^{i}_{\alpha}
be as in (

) and let
\Omega^{i}_{\alpha}
be as in (

). We have:

\displaystyle(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n+2k}{2}}\Bigg|\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s=0,\ldots,N\\
r\neq s\end{subarray}}(B^{r}_{\alpha})^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{s}_{\alpha}\Bigg|\Bigg|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega^{i}_{\alpha}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1,\ldots N\\

.

This lemma will be crucially used in the next section: the key point here is that the right-hand side goes to

and is solely estimated (albeit not in a sharp way) in terms of the parameters that define the bubble-tree

.

Proof.

Let

be fixed. Using Lemma

, we have

(3.28)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s=0,\ldots,N\\
r\neq s\end{subarray}}B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{s}_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=0,\ldots,N\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=0,\ldots,N\\
r\neq i\end{subarray}}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}.

Let
r\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\backslash\{i\}
be fixed. We estimate the r.h.s of (

) by distinguishing several cases that depend on the position of the
r
-th bubble with respect to the
i
-th bubble.

Assume first that

. Then, by (

) we have

. Using (

) we then obtain

\displaystyle(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n+2k}{2}}\Big[B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)+B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}{s^{ir}_{\alpha}}\right)^{\min(n-2k,4k)}\lesssim\Big(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1,\ldots N\\
j\neq i\,:\,\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\lesssim\mu^{j}_{\alpha}\end{subarray}}(\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{ij})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{\min(n-2k,4k)},

where we used the definition of
s^{ir}_{\alpha}
and (
3.7
) to write that
\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}{s^{ir}_{\alpha}}\lesssim(\varepsilon^{ir}_{\alpha})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for

. In particular, the right-hand goes to
0
as

by (

).

Assume now that
r\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
. Then, by definition of

, we have
B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)\lesssim\frac{(\mu^{r}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}}{(\rho^{ri}_{\alpha})^{n-2k}}
for all

, so that

\displaystyle(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n+2k}{2}}\Big[B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)+B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\mu^{r}_{\alpha}}{\rho^{ri}_{\alpha}}\right)^{\min(2k,\frac{n-2k}{2})}\lesssim\Big(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots N\\

where we used (

\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\mu^{r}_{\alpha}}{\rho^{ri}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu^{r}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{2k-1}{n-1}}
for

by definition of

Assume now that

and

and

\displaystyle(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n+2k}{2}}\Big[B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)+B^{r}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)\Big]

4.
Sharp weighted estimates for linearised equations at a bubble-tree

This section contains the core of the analysis of the paper. We prove a uniform invertibility result in appropriate weighted spaces for a linearisation of (
3.2
) at any fixed bubble-tree configuration. We keep the notations of Sections
2
and
3
. Throughout this section we fix a bubble-tree
(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
given by

\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}V^{i}_{\alpha}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots N\\
j=1,\ldots d_{i}\end{subarray}}\nu^{ij}_{\alpha}Z^{ij}_{\alpha},

where
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
solves (
1.2
),
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
,
i=1,\ldots,N
are
N
bubbles

as in Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
and satisfying (
3.7
),
(\nu^{ij}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
,
0\leq i\leq N
,
1\leq j\leq d_{i}
, are sequences of real numbers converging to
0
and
d_{i}=\dim\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha}
is as in (
3.9
). For
\alpha\geq 1
and
y\in\overline{\Omega}
we define

(4.1)

\Psi_{\alpha}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{2-2k}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0,\ldots,N\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\big(B^{j}_{\alpha}(y)\big)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y),

where
B^{i}_{\alpha}
and
\theta^{i}_{\alpha}
are given by (
3.11
). Let
\eta>0
be a positive real number. We introduce the following weighted norms:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\ast}

\displaystyle=\max_{y\in\overline{\Omega}}\sum_{l=0}^{2k-1}\frac{|\nabla^{l}\varphi(y)|}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)}\quad\text{ for }\varphi\in C^{2k-1}(\overline{\Omega}),

\displaystyle=\max_{y\in\overline{\Omega}}\frac{|R(y)|}{\Psi_{\alpha}(y)+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{N}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}}\quad\text{ for }R\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}).

These norms depend on
\alpha
, but for simplicity we simply denote them by

. We let
L_{\alpha}
be as in (
3.1
). The main result of this section is as follows:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be defined as in (
3.10
). There exist a constant
C_{0}>0
and a sequence
(\eta_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of positive numbers converging to zero as

such that the following holds: for any

and for any
\alpha
large enough, there exists a unique function
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\perp}
that solves

(4.3)

L_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha}-({2^{\sharp}}-1)|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\varphi_{\alpha}=R+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots N\\
j=1,\ldots d_{i}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{ij}_{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{k}Z^{ij}_{\alpha}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

for some real numbers
(\lambda^{ij}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
,
i=0,\ldots,N
,
j=1,\ldots,d_{i}
. Moreover,
\varphi_{\alpha}\in C^{2k-1}(\overline{\Omega})
and satisfies

(4.4)

\|\varphi_{\alpha}\|_{\ast}\leq C_{0}\eta_{\alpha}\|R\|_{\ast\ast,\eta_{\alpha}}.

Recall that
Z^{ij}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
are given by (
3.9
), and that the orthogonal is taken with respect to the
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
scalar product given by (
2.1
).

Some remarks are in order. The operator
\tilde{L}_{\alpha}=L_{\alpha}-({2^{\sharp}}-1)|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}
is, formally, the linearisation of (
3.2
) at
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
– even though
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
is in general only an approximate solution of (
3.2
). Theorem
4.1
should thus be understood as a uniform invertibility result for the linearised version of (
3.2
) at a general bubble-tree
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
: it provides sharp pointwise bounds, in weighted norms adapted to the configuration of the bubble-tree (i.e. norms that capture the leading order of size of
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
at each scale), for solutions of the linearised equation (
4.3
). Theorem
4.1
does not assume that
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
originates from a Struwe-type decomposition like (
3.4
):
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
could be any bubble-tree and Theorem
4.1
is formally decorrelated from (
3.2
). Theorem
4.1
shows that the only obstruction to the uniform (in
\alpha
) invertibility of
\tilde{L}_{\alpha}
is the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
, which simply consists of the contributions of the kernels of each element
u_{0},V^{1}_{\alpha},\ldots,V^{N}_{\alpha}
appearing in the bubble-tree. This fact has long been known at the energy level (see for instance Proposition
4.2
below), but the real novelty – and the main analytical difficulty – in Theorem
4.1
is to prove that uniform invertibility (
4.4
) holds true in the weighted norms given by (
4.2
). In particular, Theorem
4.1
remains true for
any
bubble-tree configuration
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
, regardless of the choice of
u_{0}
and the bubbles
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
, provided they satisfy (
3.7
). The constant
C_{0}
and the sequence
(\eta_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in (
4.4
) only depend on
n,k
,
\Omega
,
\|A_{l,\alpha}-A_{l}\|_{C^{l}(\overline{\Omega})}
, and
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
(see (
4.8
) below). The weighted norms (
4.2
) that appear in (
4.4
) are dictated by the bubble-tree structure. Simple computations show indeed that
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
satisfies

\big|L_{\alpha}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}-|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}|^{2^{\sharp}-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}\big|\lesssim\Psi_{\alpha}+\eta_{\alpha}\sum_{i=0}^{N}(B^{i}_{\alpha})^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}\quad\text{ in }\Omega

for some sequence
\eta_{\alpha}\to 0
of positive numbers (we will prove this in (
5.5
) below). The choice of the

norm is therefore motivated by the setting of Section

to prove global pointwise estimates for solutions of (

). The

norm is, in turn, imposed by

: for a fixed

canonically appears when integrating a Green’s function against

(see Lemma

j=1,\ldots d_{i}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{ij}_{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{k}Z^{ij}_{\alpha}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

This had already been observed in
[

]
in the case

\left\lvert\int_{\big(\hat{\Omega}^{m}_{\alpha}\cap\bigcap_{x^{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}B(x^{i},\frac{1}{R})\big)}\langle\nabla^{k}\psi_{\alpha},\nabla^{k}Z^{mj}\rangle\,dy\right\rvert\lesssim R^{-n}+\smallo(1)

as
\alpha\to+\infty
, where the constants in
\lesssim
do not depend on
R
. Combining these two estimates with (
4.37
), and using (
4.42
), (
4.43
) therefore shows that

\left\lvert\int_{\big(\hat{\Omega}^{m}_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{x^{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}B(x^{i},\frac{1}{R})\big)\cap B(0,R)}\langle\nabla^{k}\psi_{\alpha},\nabla^{k}Z^{mj}\rangle\,dy\right\rvert\lesssim\smallo(1)+R^{-n}

for all
\alpha\geq 1
and for
R\geq 1
. Since
\psi_{\alpha}
converges to
\psi_{\infty}
in
C^{2k-1}_{loc}(\overline{\Omega_{\infty}^{m}}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{m})
, the previous estimate passes to the limit as
\alpha\to+\infty
and we get

\left\lvert\int_{\big(\Omega_{\infty}^{m}\setminus\bigcup_{x^{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}B(x^{i},\frac{1}{R})\big)\cap B(0,R)}\langle\nabla^{k}\psi_{\infty},\nabla^{k}Z^{mj}\rangle\,dy\right\rvert\lesssim R^{-n}.

We may now let
R\to+\infty
: using (
4.51
) and dominated convergence we obtain (
4.57
). Now, (
4.57
) shows that
\psi_{\infty}\in\mathcal{K}_{v^{m}}^{\perp}
, where the orthogonal is taken with respect to the
D^{k,2}(\Omega_{\infty}^{m})
scalar product (
2.1
): together with (
4.56
) it thus shows that
\psi_{\infty}\equiv 0
in
\Omega_{\infty}^{m}
, which proves (
4.55
).

Step 3: the contradiction.

For each
\alpha\geq 1
, we let
z_{\alpha}\in\overline{\Omega^{m}_{\alpha}}
be a point where
y\mapsto\frac{|\varphi_{\alpha}(y)|}{\theta^{m}_{\alpha}(y)B^{m}_{\alpha}(y)}
reaches its maximum in
\overline{\Omega^{m}_{\alpha}}
, which is by (
4.24
) equal to
\Phi^{m}_{\alpha}
. We apply (
4.30
) for
l=0
at
z_{\alpha}
: together with Lemma
3.7
and by the contradiction assumption (
4.43
) we obtain that

(4.58)

\Phi^{m}_{\alpha}=\frac{|\varphi_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})|}{\theta^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})B^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})}\lesssim\frac{\mu^{m}_{\alpha}}{\theta^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})}\Phi^{m}_{\alpha}+\frac{(\mu^{m}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}}{(r^{m}_{\alpha})^{n-2k}}\frac{\|\varphi_{\alpha}\|_{\ast}}{\theta^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})B^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})}.

Since
z_{\alpha}\in\overline{\Omega^{m}_{\alpha}}\subseteq\overline{B(x^{m}_{\alpha},r^{m}_{\alpha})}
we have
|z_{\alpha}-x^{m}_{\alpha}|\leq r^{m}_{\alpha}
and thus
\theta^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})B^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})\gtrsim\frac{(\mu^{m}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}}{(r^{m}_{\alpha})^{n-2k-1}}
. We thus obtain

\frac{(\mu^{m}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}}{(r^{m}_{\alpha})^{n-2k}}\frac{\|\varphi_{\alpha}\|_{\ast}}{\theta^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})B^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})}\lesssim\frac{\|\varphi_{\alpha}\|_{\ast}}{r^{m}_{\alpha}}=\frac{\mu^{m}_{\alpha}}{r^{m}_{\alpha}}\frac{\|\varphi_{\alpha}\|_{\ast}}{\mu^{m}_{\alpha}}=\smallo(\Phi^{m}_{\alpha})

as
\alpha\to+\infty
, where in the last equality we again used (
4.43
), together with (
4.42
). With the latter (
4.58
) becomes, after dividing by
\Phi^{m}_{\alpha}
,

(4.59)

1\lesssim\frac{\mu^{m}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{m}_{\alpha}+|z_{\alpha}-x^{m}_{\alpha}|}+\smallo(1),

which implies that
|z_{a}-x^{m}_{\alpha}|=\bigO(\mu^{m}_{\alpha})
as
\alpha\to+\infty
. As a technical remark, we mention that the term
\theta^{m}_{\alpha}
in the definition of
\Phi^{m}_{\alpha}
in (
4.24
) was precisely added to obtain decay in (
4.59
) as
|z_{\alpha}-x^{m}_{\alpha}|
becomes larger than
\mu^{m}_{\alpha}
. There is no particular reason to choose precisely
\theta^{m}_{\alpha}
however, and it is easily seen from (
4.24
) that we may choose any weight
(\theta^{m}_{\alpha})^{\kappa}
in the definition of
\Phi^{m}_{\alpha}
provided
0<\kappa<\min(2,2k,n-2k)
. Let now, for any
\alpha\geq 1
,

\hat{z}_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{z_{\alpha}-x^{m}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{m}_{\alpha}}&\text{ if }V^{m}_{\alpha}\text{ is an interior bubble }\\
&\frac{1}{\mu^{m}_{\alpha}}\sigma_{x^{m}_{\alpha}}^{-1}(z_{\alpha})&\text{ if }V^{m}_{\alpha}\text{ is a boundary bubble }\end{aligned}\right\}\in\overline{\hat{\Omega}^{m}_{\alpha}}.

Then
|\hat{z}_{\alpha}|=\bigO(1)
and, up to a subsequence, we may let
\hat{z}_{\infty}=\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\hat{z}_{\alpha}\in\Omega_{\infty}^{m}
. By (
3.11
), (
4.35
) and by definition of
\psi_{\alpha}
we have

1=\frac{1}{\Phi^{m}_{\alpha}}\frac{|\varphi_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})|}{\theta^{m}_{\alpha}(z_{\alpha})B^{m}_{\alpha}(y)}\lesssim\left\lvert\psi_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\alpha})\right\rvert\big(1+|\hat{z}_{\alpha}|\big)^{n-2k-1},

so that

(4.60)

\liminf_{\alpha\to\infty}\left\lvert\psi_{\alpha}(\hat{z}_{\alpha})\right\rvert>0.

Assume first that
\hat{z}_{\infty}\in\overline{\Omega_{\infty}^{m}}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{m}
: then, since
\psi_{\alpha}
converges to
\psi_{\infty}
in
C^{2k-1}_{loc}(\overline{\Omega_{\infty}^{m}}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{m})
, we get that
|\psi_{\infty}(\hat{z}_{\infty})|>0
, which is a contradiction with (
4.55
). Assume now that
\hat{z}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
, that is
\frac{|z_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|}{\mu^{m}_{\alpha}}\to 0
for some
j\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
. By (
4.60
), and thanks to the bound on the derivatives of
\psi_{\alpha}
in (
4.44
), we can let
(\hat{w}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be another sequence of points in
\overline{\hat{\Omega}^{m}_{\alpha}}
which converges to some point
w_{\infty}\in\overline{\Omega_{\infty}^{m}}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{m}
arbitrarily close to
\hat{z}_{\infty}
and which still satisfies
\liminf_{\alpha\to\infty}|\psi_{\alpha}(\hat{w}_{\alpha})|>0
. Using again the
C^{2k-1}_{loc}(\overline{\hat{\Omega}^{m}_{\alpha}}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{m})
convergence we again obtain
|\psi_{\infty}(w_{\infty})|>0
, which again contradicts (
4.55
). This concludes the proof of Proposition
4.11
.
∎

As we already explained, Proposition
4.11
implies Proposition
4.7
which in turn implies Theorem
4.1
. The proof of Theorem
4.1
is thus complete.

5.
Global pointwise estimates for the blow-up

In this section we prove global pointwise estimates for blowing-up solutions of (
3.2
) with bounded energy. We obtain a sharp asymptotic description of the blow-up which will be the main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The main result of this section is as follows (we keep the notations from Sections
2
,
3
and
4
):

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth bounded domain and
k\geq 1
an integer such that
n>2k
. Let
(L_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of polyharmonic operators as in (
3.1
) and let
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of solutions of (
3.2
) which satisfies
\limsup_{\alpha\to+\infty}\|u_{\alpha}\|_{H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)}<+\infty
. Up to passing to a subsequence, there exists
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})
solving (
1.2
), there exists an integer
N\geq 0
and there exist
N
bubbles
(V^{i}_{\alpha})_{1\leq i\leq N}
, defined by triplets
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
as in definitions
3.1
and
3.2
and satisfying (
3.7
), such that for any
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
,

(5.1)

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\left|\left|\frac{\displaystyle\nabla^{l}\Big(u_{\alpha}-u_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V^{i}_{\alpha}\Big)}{\displaystyle 1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\theta^{i}_{\alpha})^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}}\right|\right|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})}=0.

The functions
B^{i}_{\alpha}
and
\theta^{i}_{\alpha}
, for
0\leq i\leq N
, are as in (
3.11
). In the statement of Theorem
5.1

u_{0}
is the weak limit of
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
and, if
N=0
,
u_{\alpha}
strongly converges to
u_{0}
in
C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})
. An equivalent formulation of (
5.1
) is that there is a sequence of positive numbers
(\varepsilon_{\alpha})
with
\varepsilon_{\alpha}\to 0
as
\alpha\to+\infty
such that for any
\alpha\geq 1

\Big|\nabla^{l}u_{\alpha}(x)-\nabla^{l}u_{0}(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla^{l}V^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\Big|\leq\varepsilon_{\alpha}\Big(1+\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\Big)\quad\text{ for any }x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Estimates such as (
5.1
) have been the subject of intense investigation in the last twenty years, especially because of their importance in the proof of compactness results for equations like (
3.2
). The novelty of Theorem
5.1
is that it holds true in the general polyharmonic setting
k\geq 1
, for sign-changing solutions, and that the estimates in (
5.1
) are true uniformly up to
\partial\Omega
. This last fact is new even when
k=1
. Theorem
5.1
holds true under very general assumptions: we do not assume anything but (
3.7
) on the bubbles
(V^{i}_{\alpha})_{\{1\leq i\leq N\}}
: in particular the profiles
v^{i}
need not be non-degenerate in the sense of
[
DKM16
]
. When
k\geq 2
we allow boundary bubbles to arise in (
5.1
) (although their existence is still unknown) and we allow interior concentration points
x^{i}_{\alpha}
to converge to
\partial\Omega
as
\alpha\to+\infty
, at any speed. When
k=1
estimates like (
5.1
) were first obtained for positive solutions of critical Yamabe-type equations on compact manifolds without boundary in
[
LZ99
,
Dru03
,
Dru04
,
Heb14
]
and for positive solutions of equations like (
3.2
) in domains of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
[
DL10
,
KL24
,
KL
,
Li95
,
Li96
]
. For sign-changing solutions the analogue of Theorem
5.1
, still when
k=1
, was first obtained on closed manifolds in
[
Pre24
]
, and in domains of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for solutions of least-energy in
[
CAP26
,
CAP25
]
. For
k\geq 2
and sign-changing solutions, an analogue of Theorem
5.1
was obtained in
[
Rob25b
,
Rob25c
]
, again on closed manifolds. All these results were applied to obtain compactness results, for positive solutions
[
Dru03
,
Li95
,
Li96
]
and for sign-changing ones
[
PR25
,
PV22b
,
PV22c
,
PV24
]
.

We briefly describe the strategy of proof of Theorem
5.1
. Recall that by
[
Maz17
, Theorem 1.2]
(see (
3.4
)) a sequence of solutions
u_{\alpha}
as in Theorem
5.1
splits as

u_{\alpha}=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}V^{i}_{\alpha}+\smallo(1)\qquad\text{in }H^{k}_{0}(\Omega).

Theorem
5.1
therefore states that this energy decomposition can be improved into global pointwise estimates: it can be seen as a quantitative version of Struwe decomposition in strong spaces. To prove it we will show that
u_{\alpha}
is uniquely obtained as a perturbation of the bubble-tree
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}

via
a nonlinear fixed-point argument in
(C^{2k-1}(\overline{\Omega}),\|\cdot\|_{*})
. We prove this by linearising and using the sharp linear estimates of Theorem
4.1
. We will then show that
u_{\alpha}
inherits the estimates of Theorem
4.1
, which will yield (
5.1
). Here again we follow the strategy of proof of
[
Pre24
]
.

5.1.
Nonlinear procedure

The first result that we state claims that any bubble-tree as in (
3.10
) can be perturbed in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
into a solution of (
3.2
) up to an obstruction term in
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
:

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
be a solution of (
1.2
), let
N\geq 1
be an integer and let
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
,
i=1,\ldots,N
be
N
bubbles
V^{i}_{\alpha}
defined as in Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
, satisfying (
3.7
). For
0\leq i\leq N
and
1\leq j\leq d_{i}
, where
d_{i}=\dim\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha}
, let
(\nu^{ij}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be any sequence of real numbers such that
\nu^{ij}_{\alpha}\to 0
, and define
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
as in (
3.10
). There exists
\delta>0
such that, up to a subsequence, the following holds: there exists a unique function

\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\perp}\cap\{\varphi\in H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)\,:\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\delta\}

that satisfies

(5.2)

\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\perp}}\Big(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha}+(-\Delta)^{-k}\Big[\sum_{l=0}^{p}(-1)^{l}\,\nabla^{l}\big(A_{l,\alpha}(\nabla^{l}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha}))\big)\\
-\big|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha}\big|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\big(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha}\big)\Big]\Big)=0,

Recall that
\Pi_{\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\perp}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\perp}
in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
for the scalar product (
2.1
), that
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is defined in (
3.9
), and that
(-\Delta)^{-k}
is as in Remark
4.3
. The proof of Proposition
5.2
is standard and follows from a
verbatim
adaptation of the arguments in
[
EPV14
]
and
[
RV14
]
for
k=1
and
[
Car25
]
(with
N=1
) for
k\geq 2
. Proposition
5.2
provides us with a local uniqueness result in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
around
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
for solutions of (
3.2
). In the next result, which is the main one in this section, we prove that
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
can also be uniquely perturbed into a solution of (
3.2
) in
(C^{2k-1}(\overline{\Omega}),\|\cdot\|_{*})
, where
\|\cdot\,\|_{\ast}
is as in (
4.2
), up to an obstruction in
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})\cap H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
be a solution of (
1.2
), let
N\geq 1
be an integer and let
[(x^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},v^{i}]
,
i=1,\ldots,N
be
N
bubbles
V^{i}_{\alpha}
defined as in Definitions
3.1
and
3.2
, satisfying (
3.7
). For
0\leq i\leq N
and
1\leq j\leq d_{i}
, where
d_{i}=\dim\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha}
, let
(\nu^{ij}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be any sequence of real numbers such that
\nu^{ij}_{\alpha}\to 0
, and define
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
as in (
3.10
). There exists a sequence
\eta_{\alpha}\to 0
such that, up to passing to a subsequence in
\alpha
, the following holds: there is a unique function

\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\perp}\cap\Big\{\varphi\in C^{2k-1}(\overline{\Omega})\,:\,\|\varphi_{\alpha}\|_{\ast}\leq\eta_{\alpha}\Big\},

which satisfies

(5.3)

L_{\alpha}\big(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha}\big)=\big|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha}\big|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\big(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha}\big)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots N\\
j=1,\ldots d_{i}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{ij}_{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{k}Z^{ij}_{\alpha}\quad\text{ in }\Omega

for some
(\lambda^{ij}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
,
i=0,\ldots N
,
j=1,\ldots,d_{i}
, uniquely determined by
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
. Moreover, this solution satisfies

(5.4)

\|\varphi_{\alpha}\|_{H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)}\to 0\qquad\text{as }\alpha\to\infty.

Since
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\subset H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{\alpha}
satisfies Dirichlet boundary conditions. We prove Proposition
5.3
by adapting the proofs in
[
Pre24
, Proposition 4.2]
(for
k=1
) and
[
Car25
, Proposition 4.11]
(for a single bubble and
k\geq 2
).

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. We define

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=L_{\alpha}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}-|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle N_{\alpha}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\big|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi\big|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\big(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi\big)-|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}-({2^{\sharp}}-1)|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\varphi.

Since
u_{0}
satisfies (
1.2
) we have
u_{0}\in C^{2k}(\overline{\Omega})
by standard elliptic theory. Straightforward computations using (
3.1
) then show that

(5.5)

\displaystyle|E_{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\theta^{i}_{\alpha})^{2-2k}B^{i}_{\alpha}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0,\ldots,N\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}(B^{j}_{\alpha})^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{i}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\Big(\nu_{\alpha}+\sum_{l=0}^{p}\|A_{l,\alpha}-A_{l}\|_{C^{l}(\overline{\Omega})}\Big)\sum_{i=0}^{N}(B^{i}_{\alpha})^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}

for all
y\in\overline{\Omega}
, where we have let
\nu_{\alpha}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots,N\\
j=1,\ldots,d_{i}\end{subarray}}|\nu_{\alpha}^{ij}|
.
Hence, there exists
C_{1}>0
independent of
\alpha
such that

(5.6)

\|E_{\alpha}\|_{\ast\ast,\eta_{\alpha}}\leq C_{1},

where
\eta_{\alpha}
is the sequence defined in (
4.8
) and where
\|\cdot\,\|_{\ast\ast,\eta_{\alpha}}
is as in (
4.2
). We let, for each
\alpha\geq 1
,

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\perp}\cap\Big\{\varphi\in C^{2k-1}(\overline{\Omega})\,:\,\|\varphi\|_{\ast}\leq 2C_{0}C_{1}\eta_{\alpha}\Big\},

where
C_{0}>0
is given by Theorem
4.1
and
C_{1}>0
by (
5.6
). It is easily seen that, for all
\alpha
,
(\mathcal{C}_{\alpha},\|\cdot\|_{\ast})
is a non-empty complete metric space.
For
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
and
\alpha\geq 1
we also define

R_{\alpha}(\varphi)=-E_{\alpha}+N_{\alpha}(\varphi),

and we let
T_{\alpha}(\varphi)\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\perp}\cap C^{2k-1}(\overline{\Omega})
be the unique solution, given by Proposition
4.2
, to

(5.7)

L_{\alpha}\big(T_{\alpha}(\varphi)\big)-({2^{\sharp}}-1)|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}T_{\alpha}(\varphi)=R_{\alpha}(\varphi)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots N\\
j=1,\ldots d_{i}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{ij}_{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{k}Z^{ij}_{\alpha},

where the real numbers
(\lambda^{ij}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
,
i=0,\ldots,N
,
j=1,\ldots,d_{i}
are uniquely given by Proposition
4.2
. We will now show that
T_{\alpha}:(\mathcal{C}_{\alpha},\|\cdot\|_{\ast})\to(\mathcal{C}_{\alpha},\|\cdot\|_{\ast})
is a contraction provided
\alpha
is large enough.

Step 1:
T_{\alpha}
stabilizes
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
. We have, for all
y\in\overline{\Omega}
,

|N_{\alpha}(\varphi)(y)|\lesssim|\varphi(y)|^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}+\sum_{i=0}^{N}\big(B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)\big)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2-\tau}|\varphi(y)|^{1+\tau}

for some
0<\tau<\min\{1,{2^{\sharp}}-2\}
. By definition of
\|\cdot\|_{*}
, since
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
and since
\eta_{\alpha}\to 0
as
\alpha\to+\infty
, the previous inequality ensures that we have

\displaystyle|N_{\alpha}(\varphi)(y)|\lesssim\eta_{\alpha}^{1+\tau}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\big(B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)\big)^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}

for any
y\in\overline{\Omega}
, for
\alpha
large enough. Together with (
5.6
) this shows that there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

(5.8)

\|R_{\alpha}(\varphi)\|_{\ast\ast,\eta_{\alpha}}\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\eta_{\alpha}^{\tau}.

We may now apply Theorem
4.1
to equation (
5.7
): we obtain that, up to a subsequence,

\|T_{\alpha}(\varphi)\|_{\ast}\leq C_{0}\eta_{\alpha}\big(C_{1}+C_{2}\eta_{\alpha}^{\tau}\big).

Since
\eta_{\alpha}\to 0
, we have
C_{2}\eta_{\alpha}^{\tau}<C_{1}
for
\alpha
large enough, and hence
T_{\alpha}(\varphi)\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
.

Step 2:
T_{\alpha}
is a contraction in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
.

We now let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
. By linearity,
T_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1})-T_{\alpha}(\varphi_{2})
satisfies

(5.9)

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}\big(T_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1})-T_{\alpha}(\varphi_{2})\big)-({2^{\sharp}}-1)|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\big(T_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1})-T_{\alpha}(\varphi_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(N_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1})-N_{\alpha}(\varphi_{2})\big)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots N\\
j=1,\ldots d_{i}\end{subarray}}\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{ij}(-\Delta)^{k}Z^{ij}_{\alpha}

for some real numbers
\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{ij}
,
i=0,\ldots,N
,
j=1,\ldots,d_{i}
. Direct computations and the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
give

\displaystyle\big|N_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1})-N_{\alpha}(\varphi_{2})\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}|\sum_{i=0}^{N}(B^{i}_{\alpha})^{{2^{\sharp}}-2-\tau}\big(|\varphi_{1}|^{\tau}+|\varphi_{2}|^{\tau}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{\ast}\eta_{\alpha}^{\tau}\sum_{i=0}^{N}(B^{i}_{\alpha})^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}

in
\overline{\Omega}
. It thus follows that there exists
C_{3}>0
such that
\|N_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1})-N_{\alpha}(\varphi_{2})\|_{\ast\ast,\eta_{\alpha}}\leq C_{3}\eta_{\alpha}^{\tau-1}\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{\ast}
, and therefore Theorem
4.1
applied to (
5.9
) shows that

\|T_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1})-T_{\alpha}(\varphi_{2})\|_{\ast}\leq C_{0}C_{3}\eta_{\alpha}^{\tau}\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{\ast}.

Since
\eta_{\alpha}\to 0
, we have
C_{0}C_{3}\eta_{\alpha}^{\tau}\leq\frac{1}{2}
provided
\alpha
is large enough, and
T_{\alpha}
is a contraction in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
.

Conclusion:

We can now apply Banach’s fixed point Theorem to
T_{\alpha}
in
(\mathcal{C}_{\alpha},\|\cdot\|_{\ast})
: we obtain that there exists a unique
\varphi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
such that
T_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})=\varphi_{\alpha}
. With (
5.7
),
\varphi_{\alpha}
then solves

L_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha}-({2^{\sharp}}-1)|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\varphi_{\alpha}=R_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots N\\

5.3.
Generalisations of Theorem
5.1

The strategy of proof of Theorem
5.1
was initially developed in
[
Pre24
]
for critical second-order equations on closed manifolds without boundary and we generalise it in this paper to polyharmonic critical equations on domains of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. The strategy of proof that we follow here is flexible and allows for further generalisations. If one decides to complement the equation
Lu=|u|^{2^{\sharp}-2}u
in
\Omega
(or its perturbations
L_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}=|u_{\alpha}|^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}u_{\alpha}
) with boundary conditions that are not Dirichlet — Navier or Steklov, for instance —, one can still expect to prove global pointwise estimates on blowing-up solutions and to obtain an analogue of Theorem
5.1
to this new setting. The final argument in the proof of Theorem
5.1
(the nonlinear procedure of Proposition
5.3
) is indeed very general and reduces the proof of Theorem
5.1
to proving the linear invertibility of Theorem
4.1
. A careful inspection of the proof of Theorem
4.1
shows that it relies on the following three fundamental ingredients:

•

The validity of a Struwe-type decomposition such as (
3.4
) for energy-bounded sequences of solutions to the problem, measured with respect to a Hilbertian norm

•

Sharp pointwise decay for the interior and boundary bubbles that may arise in the analysis, that is for the finite-energy solutions of the limiting problems (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) (Proposition
2.2
in this paper)

•

Pointwise estimates on the Green’s function of the operator
L-(2^{\sharp}-1)|u_{0}|^{2^{\sharp}-2}
with the chosen boundary conditions (Dirichlet for us here, see e.g. the proof of (
4.14
))

Provided each of the three points above is satisfied for the new boundary condition considered, it is likely that a step-by-step adaptation of the proof of Theorem
4.1
will yield a similar result in this new setting. Verifying each one of these three points will of course require an
ad hoc
analysis. Similarly, since the proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a Pohozaev argument, it is likely that it will adapt to other boundary conditions to yield new compactness results for different boundary conditions.
Struwe-type decompositions, for instance, are known for biharmonic problems (
k=2
) with Navier boundary conditions by
[
GGS10
, Lemma 7.74]
, and it is therefore likely that Theorem
1.1
possesses a close analogue to the Navier setting, at least when
k=2
. We do not pursue this further in this paper.

We state in this section a polyharmonic version of the celebrated Pohozaev identity

Proposition A.1

(Proposition A.1 in
[

]
)
.

Let

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle+\left(\frac{n-2k}{2}-\frac{n}{p}\right)\int_{\Omega}f\,|u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}(x-\xi)^{i}\partial_{i}f\,|u|^{p}\,dx.

(A.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{k}(\Omega;u)=\mathcal{R}_{k}(\Omega;u)

\displaystyle+\frac{n-2k}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{[k/2]-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\bigg(\partial_{\nu}((-\Delta)^{i}u)\,(-\Delta)^{k-i-1}u\,-(-\Delta)^{i}u\penalty\ \partial_{\nu}((-\Delta)^{k-i-1}u)\bigg)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{[k/2]-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\bigg[\partial_{\nu}((-\Delta)^{i}((x-\xi)^{a}\partial_{a}u))\,(-\Delta)^{k-i-1}u\,

\displaystyle-(-\Delta)^{i}((x-\xi)^{a}\partial_{a}u)\,\partial_{\nu}((-\Delta)^{k-i-1}u)\bigg]\,d\sigma,

where we have let

\mathcal{R}_{k}(\Omega;u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(x-\xi,\nu)\big|(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}u\big|^{2}d\sigma\quad\text{ if }k\text{ is even }\\

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(\Omega;u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(x-\xi,\nu)(-\Delta)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,d\sigma

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\bigg[(-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\,\partial_{\nu}((x-\xi)^{a}\partial_{a}((-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u))\,

\displaystyle-\big((x-\xi)^{a}\partial_{a}((-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u)\big)\partial_{\nu}\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}u\big)\bigg]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\text{ if }k\text{ is odd. }

. Indeed, if
\mu^{i}_{\alpha}=\smallo(\mu^{j}_{\alpha})
or
\mu^{j}_{\alpha}=\smallo(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})
we obviously have
\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{ij}\to+\infty
by definition. And if
\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\lesssim\mu^{j}_{\alpha}\lesssim\mu^{i}_{\alpha}
, and since
x^{j}_{\alpha}\in\partial\Omega
, we have
|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|\geq\operatorname{dist}(x^{i}_{\alpha},\partial\Omega)>>\mu^{i}_{\alpha}
, hence again
\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{ij}\to+\infty
.

We will prove that
E(i)=\{i\}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, which will prove that (
B.3
) holds. If
j\in E(i)
we let
\mu_{j,i}
and
z_{j,i}
be as in (
B.4
). In the case where
V^{i}_{\alpha}
and
V^{j}_{\alpha}
are both boundary bubbles we have
z_{j,i}\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. We let

v_{\mu_{j,i},z_{j,i}}(x)=(\mu_{j,i})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}v^{j}\big(\mu_{j,i}x+z_{j,i}),

which is defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
depending on the nature of
V^{i}_{\alpha}
. We then have the following result:

Lemma B.1
.

We let

U_{i}=\sum_{j\in E(i)}v_{\mu_{j,i},z_{j,i}},

which is defined in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
depending on the nature of
V^{i}_{\alpha}
.
Then:

•

If
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is an interior bubble,
U_{i}
is a solution of (
2.2
).

•

If
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is a boundary bubble,
U_{i}
is a solution of (
2.3
).

Proof of Lemma
B.1
.

Assume first that
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is an interior bubble. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let, for all
x\in\Omega
,

\varphi_{i,\alpha}(x)=(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{x-x^{i}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\Big).

It is easily seen that
(\varphi_{i,\alpha})_{\alpha}
is bounded in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
and that
\varphi_{i,\alpha}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
u_{\alpha}
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
I
we have

(-\Delta)^{k}u_{\alpha}+\sum_{l=0}^{p}(-1)^{l}\nabla^{l}\big(A_{l}(\nabla^{l}\,u_{\alpha}\,)\big)-|u_{\alpha}|^{2^{\sharp}-2}u_{\alpha}=\smallo(1)\quad\textrm{ in }H^{-k}(\Omega),

and integrating the latter against
\varphi_{i,\alpha}
gives, together with (
B.5
),

(B.6)

\int_{\Omega}\Big\langle\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}V^{j}_{\alpha},(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\varphi_{i,\alpha}\Big\rangle dy=\int_{\Omega}f\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}V^{j}_{\alpha}\Big)\varphi_{i,\alpha}dy+\smallo(1).

First, direct computations show that if
j\not\in E(i)
then
\int_{\Omega}\langle(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}V^{j}_{\alpha},(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\varphi_{i,\alpha}\rangle dv_{g}=\smallo(1)
. Then, it is easily checked that
(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}V^{j}_{\alpha}(x^{i}_{\alpha}+\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\cdot)\to v_{\mu_{j,i},z_{j,i}}
in
D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
\alpha\to+\infty
. Combining these two elements yields, by a simple change of variables,

(B.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big\langle\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}V^{j}_{\alpha},(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\varphi_{i,\alpha}\Big\rangle dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big\langle\sum_{j\in E(i)}(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}V^{j}_{\alpha},(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\varphi_{i,\alpha}\Big\rangle dy+\smallo(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\langle(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}U_{i},(-\Delta)^{\frac{k}{2}}\varphi\Big\rangle dy+\smallo(1)

as
\alpha\to+\infty
. Independently we have, using (
3.12
),

\displaystyle\Big|f\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}V^{j}_{\alpha}\Big)-f\Big(\sum_{j\in E(i)}V^{j}_{\alpha}\Big)\Big|\lesssim\Big(\sum_{j\not\in E(i)}B^{j}_{\alpha}\Big)^{2^{\sharp}-1}+\Big(\sum_{j\in E(i)}B^{j}_{\alpha}\Big)^{2^{\sharp}-2}\Big(\sum_{j\not\in E(i)}B^{j}_{\alpha}\Big).

When
j\not\in E(i)
, direct computations show that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(B^{j}_{\alpha})^{2^{\sharp}-1}|\varphi_{i,\alpha}|dv_{g}=\smallo(1)\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sum_{k\in E(i)}B^{k}_{\alpha}\Big)^{2^{\sharp}-2}(B^{j}_{\alpha})|\varphi_{i,\alpha}|dv_{g}=\smallo(1)

as
\alpha\to+\infty
, and Hölder’s inequality and a change of variables thus show that

(B.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}V^{j}_{\alpha}\Big)\varphi_{i,\alpha}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f\Big(\sum_{j\in E(i)}V^{j}_{\alpha}\Big)\varphi_{i,\alpha}dy+\smallo(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(U_{i})\varphi dy+\smallo(1).

Combining (
B.7
) and (
B.8
) in (
B.6
) and passing to the limit shows that
U_{i}
is a weak, and hence a strong, solution of (
2.2
). This proves Lemma
B.1
if
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is an interior bubble. The proof when
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is a boundary bubble is identical by defining
\varphi_{i,\alpha}(x)=\mu_{\alpha}^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}\varphi\Big(\frac{\sigma_{x_{\alpha}}^{-1}(x)}{\mu_{\alpha}}\Big)
and since
U_{i}\in D^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.
∎

We may now conclude the proof of (
B.3
). Let
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. If
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is an interior bubble we let

U_{i,\alpha}(x)=\chi\Bigg(\frac{x-x^{i}_{\alpha}}{\operatorname{dist}(x^{i}_{\alpha},\partial\Omega)}\Bigg)(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}U_{i}\Big(\frac{x-x^{i}_{\alpha}}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\Big),

while if
V^{i}_{\alpha}
is a boundary bubble we let

U_{i,\alpha}(x)=\chi\big(\sigma_{x^{i}_{\alpha}}^{-1}(x)\big)(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{-\frac{n-2k}{2}}U_{i}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{x^{i}_{\alpha}}^{-1}(x)}{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}}\Big).

It is easily seen that
U_{i,\alpha}=\sum_{j\in E(i)}V^{j}_{\alpha}(x)+\smallo(1)
in
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
, and the initial Struwe decomposition (
B.5
) thus becomes

u_{\alpha}=u_{0}+U_{i,\alpha}+\sum_{j\not\in E(i)}V^{j}_{\alpha}+\smallo(1)\quad\textrm{ in }H^{k}_{0}(\Omega).

Since
U_{i}
is a solution of (
2.2
), this contradicts the minimality of
N
in the case where
\#E(i)\geq 2
, regardless of whether
U_{i}\equiv 0
or not. Hence
E(i)=\{i\}
. This holds true for any
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and proves (
B.3
).
∎

Appendix C
Technical results

C.1.
Yet another version of Giraud’s Lemma

We state a version of Giraud’s lemma that we frequently used in this paper:

Lemma C.1

(Giraud’s Lemma)
.

Let
\overline{\Omega}
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 3
. Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>0
satisfying
\beta+\gamma<n
and let
0<\mu<1
. Let
X\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})
,
Y\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{(x,x)\,:\,x\in\overline{\Omega}\})
be such that

\displaystyle|X(x,y)|\leq(\mu+|x-y|)^{\gamma-n}\quad\text{ and }\quad|Y(x,y)|\leq|x-y|^{\beta-n}

for all
x\neq y\in\overline{\Omega}
. Define
Z(x,y)=\int_{\Omega}X(x,z)Y(z,y)\,dz
. Then
Z\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})
and there exists
C>0
such that

|Z(x,y)|\leq C\begin{cases}\mu^{\gamma}(\mu+|x-y|)^{\beta-n}&\text{if }\gamma<0\\
(\mu+|x-y|)^{\beta-n}\left(1+\left\lvert\log\Big(\frac{\mu+|x-y|}{\mu}\Big)\right\rvert\right)&\text{if }\gamma=0\\
(\mu+|x-y|)^{\beta+\gamma-n}&\text{if }\gamma>0\end{cases}.

The proof of Lemma
C.1
easily follows from an adaptation of the proof of classical Giraud-type inequalities. See for instance the arguments in
[
Heb14
, Lemma 7.5]
or
[
CR25
, Lemma 7.1]
.

C.2.
Some integral estimates

In this subsection we use the notations of Sections
2
and
4
. We state several bubble-tree estimates. They are all adaptations of well-known results in the case
k=1
but we gather them here for clarity.

Lemma C.2
.

Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. We have, for any
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
,

\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{2k-n-l}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(y)B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}\,dy\lesssim\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)

as
\alpha\to+\infty
, uniformly in
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

This is a direct application of Lemma
C.1
.
∎

Lemma C.3
.

Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. We have, for any
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
,

(C.1)

\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{2k-n-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\,dy=\smallo\big(1+\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\big)

as
\alpha\to+\infty
, uniformly in
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

A direct computation using Lemma
C.1
shows that for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}

\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{2k-n-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}\,dy\lesssim\begin{cases}(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)&\text{when $n<4k$}\\
(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{2k-1}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{1-2k-l}&\text{when $n=4k$}\\
(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{2k}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-2k-l}&\text{when $n>4k$}\end{cases}.

When
n<4k
(
C.1
) thus follows. Assume now that
n>4k
. We have

\displaystyle(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{2k}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-2k-l}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{3k-\frac{n}{2}}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{n-4k}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{k}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)&\text{ if }\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\leq(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{k-\frac{l}{2}}&\text{ if }\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\geq(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{aligned}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)),

which proves (
C.1
). The case
n=4k
is similar.
∎

Lemma C.4
.

Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
. Let
(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of positive numbers such that
M_{\alpha}\geq 1
. Then for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

\int_{\Omega\setminus B(x^{i}_{\alpha},M_{\alpha}\mu^{i}_{\alpha})}|x-y|^{2k-n-l}\big(B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)\big)^{{2^{\sharp}}-1}\,dy\lesssim\frac{1}{M_{\alpha}^{2k}}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x).

Proof.

The proof is an adaptation of
[
Heb14
, Lemma 7.6]
for the case
k=1
.
∎

Lemma C.5
.

Let
i\neq j\in\{1,\ldots N\}
. There exists a sequence
\varepsilon_{\alpha}\to 0
as in (
4.9
) such that

(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}(\mu^{j}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{k-n}\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(y)^{k-n}\,dx\leq\varepsilon_{\alpha}.

Let
p\in\{0,\cdots,k-1\}
and assume that
n>4k-2p
. Then

(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}(\mu^{j}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{2k-p-n}\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(y)^{2k-p-n}\,dx\leq\varepsilon_{\alpha}\big(\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\mu^{j}_{\alpha}\big)^{k-p}.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from an adaptation of the proof of estimate (A.6) in
[
Pre24
, Proposition A.1]
for the case
k=1
. We prove the second one. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\mu^{j}_{\alpha}\leq\mu^{i}_{\alpha}
. Since
\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(y)\geq|x^{j}_{\alpha}-y|
and since
n>2(2k-p)
we may apply Lemma
C.1

to get

\displaystyle(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}(\mu^{j}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{2k-p-n}\theta^{j}_{\alpha}(y)^{2k-p-n}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}(\mu^{j}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2}}}{(\mu^{i}_{\alpha}+|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|)^{n-4k+2p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\mu^{j}_{\alpha}}{(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{2}+|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-4k+2p}{2}}\big(\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\mu^{j}_{\alpha}\big)^{k-p}\lesssim\big(\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{ij}\big)^{-\frac{n-4k+2p}{2}}\big(\mu^{i}_{\alpha}\mu^{j}_{\alpha}\big)^{k-p},

and the result follows from (
3.7
).
∎

C.3.
Proof of Lemma
4.5

We finally give a proof of Lemma
4.5
:

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

By (
3.11
) we have
B^{0}_{\alpha}\equiv 1
. Using (
4.1
) we thus have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{2-2k}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)+\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}\Big(B^{j}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}+B^{j}_{\alpha}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1,\ldots,N\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}B^{j}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y).

We let
0\leq l\leq 2k-1
be fixed and integrate each term separately against
|x-y|^{2k-n-l}
. Let
i\in\{1,\ldots N\}
. First, a direct application of Lemma
C.1
shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{2k-n-l}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(y)^{2-2k}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{2}\log\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\big)\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\smallo\big(1+\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\big)

uniformly for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Since
\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{2}\log\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\lesssim\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)
, the last equality follows again from the following observation:

\displaystyle\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2(n-2k+l)}}\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)&\text{ if }\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\leq(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2(n-2k+l)}}\\
&(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2(n-2k+l)}}&\text{ if }\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\geq(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2(n-2k+l)}}\end{aligned}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mu^{i}_{\alpha})^{\frac{n-2k}{2(n-2k+l)}}(1+\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)).

Similarly, arguing as in the proof of Lemma
C.3
and using Lemma
C.1
we get that

\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{2k-n-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)\,dy=\smallo\big(1+\theta^{i}_{\alpha}(x)^{-l}B^{i}_{\alpha}(x)\big)

on
\overline{\Omega}
, and the term involving
\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}(B^{j}_{\alpha}(y))^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}
in
\Psi_{\alpha}
is estimated using (
C.1
). Thus we only need to estimate

\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{2k-n-l}B^{j}_{\alpha}(y)^{{2^{\sharp}}-2}B^{i}_{\alpha}(y)\,dy

for all
i\neq j
in
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. The conclusion then follows from considering separately every possible configuration of
\mu^{i}_{\alpha},\mu^{j}_{\alpha},|x^{i}_{\alpha}-x^{j}_{\alpha}|
satisfying (
3.7
), by a straightforward adaptation of the proof for the case
k=1
in
[
Pre24
, Proposition A.1]
.
∎
