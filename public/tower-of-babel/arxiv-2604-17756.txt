Polarization, Maximal Concurrence, and Pure States in High-Energy Collisions
Yu-Xuan Liu, Luo-Ting He, Bo-Wen Xiao

Abstract
We establish a quantitative relation between local spin polarization and quantum entanglement in two-qubit systems by deriving an upper bound on the concurrence at fixed local polarization, showing that increasing polarization constrains the maximum achievable entanglement. We further demonstrate that this bound is saturated by pure states in certain cases. As a concrete physical application, we consider the parity-violating process e + ​ e − → Z 0 → q ​ q ¯ e^{+}e^{-}\to Z^{0}\to q\bar{q} , which generates final-state spin polarization. We show that the maximal concurrence is attained in specific kinematic regions and is significantly reduced relative to the unpolarized case. These results establish a general, process-independent framework connecting local polarization, maximal entanglement, and the role of pure states.

Introduction

The spin degree of freedom of quarks and antiquarks produced in high-energy collisions
encodes rich information about the underlying dynamics of both perturbative and
nonperturbative QCD, as well as electroweak interactions. Understanding how polarization
arises in quark-antiquark pair production is not only central to the physics of spin
in QCD, but also provides a natural arena for exploring quantum correlations, especially the entanglement between the spin states of the produced pair. In this work, we explore the relation that connects the polarization of the
quark-antiquark pair to the maximal concurrence, a measure of spin entanglement,
and apply it to several physically relevant production mechanisms. Quark polarization in the final state can originate from a variety of sources,
which we briefly survey below to introduce the generality of this study.

1.

Polarized deep inelastic scattering:
When a polarized lepton scatters off a polarized nucleon, the struck quark inherits
a longitudinal polarization governed by the helicity parton distribution functions

\Delta q(x,Q^{2})
. The polarization transfer from the initial-state nucleon to the
final-state quark has been extensively studied in polarized deep inelastic scattering
(DIS) experiments, providing the primary means of extracting the spin structure of
the nucleon
EuropeanMuon:1987isl
,
HERMES:2006jyl
.

2.

Parity-violating processes:
The chiral nature of the electroweak interaction provides a clean mechanism for producing polarized quarks even from unpolarized initial states. In
e^{+}e^{-}

annihilation through a
Z^{0}
boson
ALEPH:2005ab
, the produced quark-antiquark pair acquires
a net polarization determined by the weak mixing angle and the
quark electroweak quantum numbers.

3.

Spin-orbit correlations:

In unpolarized hadron collisions, where neither initial-state polarization nor parity violation is present, the fragmentation process itself can generate polarizations through final-state interactions that couple spin to the production-plane geometry, as evidenced by the large transverse polarization of
\Lambda
hyperons observed in unpolarized
pp

. The underlying mechanism for the self-polarization of
\Lambda

4.

Global polarization in heavy-ion collisions:

In non-central heavy-ion collisions, the large orbital angular momentum of the
colliding system generates a vortical quark-gluon plasma. Through spin-orbit
coupling in the medium, quarks and antiquarks acquire a net polarization aligned
with the system’s angular momentum

observed via the polarization of

Formalism for two-Qubit system

A general two-qubit system can be represented as a
4\times 4
density matrix, written as

\rho=\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{4}+B_{i}^{+}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}+B_{j}^{-}\,\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{j}+C_{ij}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}\right),

(1)

where
B_{i}^{+}=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho(\sigma_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})\right]
and
B_{j}^{-}=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{j})\right]
are the Bloch vector of each qubit representing the individual spin polarizations, and
C_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho(\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j})\right]
is the correlation matrix characterizing the spin correlations between the qubits.

The degree of mixedness of a quantum state is quantified by its purity,
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{2})
.
For a pure state,
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{2})=1
, whereas for the maximally mixed state

\rho=\tfrac{1}{4}\mathbbm{1}_{4\times 4}
, the purity is
\tfrac{1}{4}
.

Concurrence
\mathcal{C}
provides a quantitative measure of the entanglement. For any two-qubit system, the concurrence is given by
Wootters:1997id
:

\mathcal{C}(\rho)=\max\{0,\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}\},

(2)

where
{\lambda_{i}}
are the eigenvalues of the auxiliary matrix,
\mathcal{R}=\sqrt{\,\sqrt{\rho}\,\tilde{\rho}\,\sqrt{\rho}\,}
, ordered as
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}\geq\lambda_{4}
. The concurrence ranges from 0 to 1, with larger values indicating a higher degree of entanglement. The
\tilde{\rho}
here is spin-flipped state of density matrix
\rho
which is defined as

\tilde{\rho}=(\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})\,\rho^{\ast}\,(\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y}).

(3)

For pure two-qubit states, the concurrence can be equivalently expressed in a simpler way, which is the so-called I-concurrence
Rungta:2001zcj
defined as:

\mathcal{C}(\rho)=\mathcal{C}_{I}(\rho)=\sqrt{\,2\bigl(1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{A}^{\,2}\bigr)}=\sqrt{\,2\bigl(1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{B}^{\,2}\bigr)}.

(4)

where
\rho_{A}=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}(\rho)
and
\rho_{B}=\operatorname{Tr}_{A}(\rho)
are the reduced density matrices.

An important and widely used subclass of two-qubit states is the X state, whose density matrix has nonzero entries only on the main diagonal and anti-diagonal:

\rho_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{11}&0&0&\rho_{14}\\
0&\rho_{22}&\rho_{23}&0\\
0&\rho_{23}^{\ast}&\rho_{33}&0\\
\rho_{14}^{\ast}&0&0&\rho_{44}\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

For
\rho_{X}
to be a physical density matrix, it must satisfy the following conditions:

\rho_{ii}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\rho_{ii}\geq 0\ (i=1,2,3,4),\quad\sum_{i=1}^{4}\rho_{ii}=1,

(6)

and

|\rho_{14}|^{2}\leq\rho_{11}\rho_{44},\qquad|\rho_{23}|^{2}\leq\rho_{22}\rho_{33},

(7)

which ensure the positivity of the density matrix
Ali:2010unc
. For this class, the concurrence takes the compact form
Wang:2006fru

\mathcal{C}(\rho_{X})=2\max\!\left\{0,\,|\rho_{14}|-\sqrt{\rho_{22}\rho_{33}},\,|\rho_{23}|-\sqrt{\rho_{11}\rho_{44}}\right\}.

(8)

3
Maximal concurrence limited by polarization

In a two-qubit system, polarization can affect the entanglement and limit the maximal concurrence attainable by the system. Here we derive an analytic relation between the magnitudes of the two local polarizations
(\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|
and
\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|)
, and the maximal concurrence.

We focus on the magnitudes of the polarizations rather than their directions, since local unitary transformations preserve both the concurrence and the magnitudes of the local polarizations. Therefore, without loss of generality, we can use local unitary transformations to rotate the Bloch vectors to the
z
-direction. We write:

\vec{B}^{\,+}=(0,0,a),\qquad\vec{B}^{\,-}=(0,0,b),

(9)

where
a=\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|
,
b=\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|
, and
0\leq a,b\leq 1
.

To determine the maximal concurrence, we restrict our optimization to

X
states, a choice motivated by the observation that maximum
concurrence often occurs at such configurations in physical systems
Munro:2001foh
.
We first show analytically that the maximal concurrence bound is
saturated by the
X
states discussed below. We then validate this
restriction through exhaustive numerical simulations over all possible
density matrices without imposing the
X
-state constraint, confirming
that no density matrix exceeds this bound, thereby justifying our analytical focus on the
X
-state subclass.

For the
X
state in Eq. (
5
), the local polarizations satisfy

a=(\rho_{11}+\rho_{22})-(\rho_{33}+\rho_{44}),\quad b=(\rho_{11}+\rho_{33})-(\rho_{22}+\rho_{44}).

(10)

Since one degree of freedom remains in the diagonal entries after fixing
a
and
b
, we introduce an additional real parameter
c
to parametrize them:

c=\rho_{11}-\rho_{22}-\rho_{33}+\rho_{44}.

(11)

Then the diagonal entries can be written as

\displaystyle\rho_{11}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+a+b+c}{4},

\displaystyle\rho_{22}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+a-b-c}{4},

(12)

\displaystyle\rho_{33}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-a+b-c}{4},

\displaystyle\rho_{44}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-a-b+c}{4}.

(13)

Combining Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
), we obtain
\mathcal{C}^{(X)}\leq 2\left|\sqrt{\rho_{11}\rho_{44}}-\sqrt{\rho_{22}\rho_{33}}\right|
. The upper bound is saturated when
\rho_{14}
and
\rho_{23}
saturate the positivity constraints, and the maximal concurrence can then be written as a function of
c
:

\mathcal{C}_{\max}^{(X)}(c)=2\left|\sqrt{\rho_{11}\rho_{44}}-\sqrt{\rho_{22}\rho_{33}}\right|.

(14)

Using the above parameterization, one finds

\displaystyle\rho_{11}\rho_{44}=\frac{(1+c)^{2}-(a+b)^{2}}{16},\quad\quad\rho_{22}\rho_{33}=\frac{(1-c)^{2}-(a-b)^{2}}{16},

(15)

and therefore

\mathcal{C}_{\max}^{(X)}(c)=\frac{1}{2}\left|\sqrt{(1+c)^{2}-(a+b)^{2}}-\sqrt{(1-c)^{2}-(a-b)^{2}}\right|.

(16)

From the diagonal positivity condition, the allowed interval is

a+b-1\leq c\leq 1-|a-b|.

(17)

Since
\sqrt{\rho_{11}\rho_{44}}
increases with
c
and
\sqrt{\rho_{22}\rho_{33}}
decreases with
c
, the maximum is attained at one of the endpoints in Eq. (
17
). After comparing the values of
\mathcal{C}
at the two endpoints, we find that the maximum is achieved at
c=1-|a-b|
, with the value

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\max}^{(X)}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(1-\max\{a,b\})(1+\min\{a,b\})}

(18)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(1-\max\{\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|,\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|\})(1+\min\{\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|,\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|\})}

Therefore, within the subclass of
X
states, the maximal concurrence at fixed local polarizations is given by Eq. (
18
). The corresponding optimal density matrices are listed in
A
and are consistent with the optimal density matrix for fixed reduced density matrices (marginals) obtained in Ref.
Baio:2019cgb

Several comments on the above results are in order.

1.

Eq. (
18
) demonstrates that polarization reduces the maximum achievable concurrence. In the asymmetric case where
\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|\neq\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|
, the maximum concurrence is jointly determined by both polarization magnitudes, with stronger polarization in either subsystem suppressing entanglement.

2.

It is also worth noting that Eq. (
18
) provides a more stringent upper bound on the concurrence than that of Ref.
Zhang:2008ebu
,
Barr:2024djo
, which gives
\mathcal{C}[\rho]\leq\sqrt{1-\max\{\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|^{2},\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|^{2}\}}
. While this bound is controlled solely by the larger of the two polarization magnitudes
\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|
and
\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|
, Eq. (
18
) depends on both simultaneously, and yields a strictly tighter upper bound whenever
\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|\neq\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|
. In the special case of a pure state, one has
\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|=\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|
, and both bounds coincide.

3.

With the analytic expression for the maximal concurrence at fixed local polarizations in hand, we now consider two special cases in which the maximal entanglement states are pure.

(a)

The total polarization
B
is fixed, where
B=\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|^{2}+\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|^{2}
.

(b)

The two fixed polarization magnitudes are equal, i.e.,
\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|^{2}=\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|^{2}
.

The maximal concurrence of both cases is
\mathcal{C}_{\max}=\sqrt{1-\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|^{2}}=\sqrt{1-\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|^{2}}=\sqrt{1-\frac{B}{2}}
, and the derivations of above statements are given in
B
. For the first case, one can also find
\mathcal{C}_{\text{max}}^{2}=1-|a-b|-ab
from Eq. (
18
). Maximizing
\mathcal{C}_{\max}
subject to the constraint
a^{2}+b^{2}=B

yields
a=b
, which gives
\mathcal{C}_{\max}=\sqrt{1-\frac{B}{2}}
. These results indicate that the maximal concurrence is achieved by density matrices that correspond to pure states.

4.

The physical interpretation of the above result can be understood as follows: this tension between polarization and entanglement reflects a fundamental feature of quantum mechanics. Polarization represents a definite alignment of each quark spin along a preferred direction, effectively encoding spin information locally within the individual subsystems, whereas entanglement encodes nonlocal correlations between the pair that manifest only upon measurement and cannot be attributed to either particle alone. As the polarization of the quark or antiquark increases, a larger share of the spin information is stored locally in each particle, leaving less room for the nonlocal quantum correlations that constitute entanglement. In the extreme limit of full polarization, the spin state of each particle is individually predetermined, and the pair necessarily factorizes into a product state with vanishing entanglement. Furthermore, since the concurrence of a mixed state is bounded by its purity
Munro:2001foh
, it is natural to understand that the maximum is obtained precisely when the density matrix is a rank-1 pure-state projector
\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
under certain physical constraints.

In general, the local polarizations satisfy
\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|\neq\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|
. Such configurations can be realized, for example, by preparing polarized initial states
Cheng:2025zaw
, to which the above results directly apply. In the following, we consider a concrete example,
e^{+}e^{-}\to Z^{0}\to q\bar{q}
, where
\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|=\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|
.

Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we establish a quantitative relation between local spin polarization and quantum entanglement in two-qubit systems, and derive an exact analytical expression for the maximal concurrence at fixed polarization magnitudes. In particular, the states that maximize the concurrence are pure states in certain cases, revealing a direct connection between polarization, entanglement, and pure states. Physically, polarization encodes spin information locally within each subsystem, thereby reducing the available nonlocal quantum correlations. Moreover, when the polarization magnitudes of the two subsystems are unequal, the analytic bound becomes more restrictive, further limiting the attainable concurrence.

We illustrate these general results using the parity-violating process
e^{+}e^{-}\to Z^{0}\to q\bar{q}
. We find that the concurrence reaches its upper bound at specific kinematic configurations, namely in the ultra-relativistic limit with a perpendicular scattering angle
\theta=\pi/2
. The corresponding maximal concurrence is strictly smaller than unity, with values
C_{\max}\simeq 0.744
for up-type quarks and
C_{\max}\simeq 0.353
for down-type quarks. At these points, the final-state density matrix is a pure state with nonvanishing longitudinal polarization, consistent with the general analysis.

Appendix B
Maximum Concurrence with certain polarizations

Using
\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{i})=0
,

\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j})=2\delta_{ij}
and
\operatorname{Tr}(A\otimes B)=\operatorname{Tr}(A)\operatorname{Tr}(B)
, we obtain

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{2})=\frac{1}{4}\bigl(1+\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|^{2}+\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|^{2}+T\bigr)=\frac{1}{4}\bigl(1+B+T\bigr),

(27)

where
T=\sum_{i,j}\|C_{ij}\|^{2}
.

The action of the spin-flip operation yields

\tilde{\rho}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{4}-B_{i}^{+}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}-B_{j}^{-}\,\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{j}+C_{ij}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}\right).

(28)

Introduce the shorthand

X=\mathbbm{1}_{4}+C_{ij}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j},\qquad Y=B_{i}^{+}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}+B_{j}^{-}\,\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{j}.

(29)

Then

\rho=\frac{1}{4}(X+Y),\qquad\tilde{\rho}=\frac{1}{4}(X-Y),

(30)

and therefore

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\tilde{\rho})=\frac{1}{16}\operatorname{Tr}((X+Y)(X-Y))=\frac{1}{16}(\operatorname{Tr}(X^{2})-\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{2}))),

(31)

where the cross terms cancel. Taking the trace gives

\operatorname{Tr}(X^{2})=4+C_{ij}C_{kl}\,(4\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl})\ =4+4\|C_{ij}\|^{2}=4(1+T),

(32)

and

\operatorname{Tr}(Y^{2})=B_{i}^{+}B_{k}^{+}\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[(\sigma_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})(\sigma_{k}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})\big]+B_{j}^{-}B_{l}^{-}\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{j})(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{l})\big]=4\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|^{2}+4\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|^{2}=4B.

(33)

Substituting these results into Eq. (
31
) yields

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\tilde{\rho})=\frac{1}{4}\bigl(1-B+T\bigr).

(34)

Using Eqs. (
27
) and (
34
), whose derivations are given above, we have

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\tilde{\rho})=\frac{1}{4}(1-B+T)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{2})-\frac{B}{2}\leq 1-\frac{B}{2}.

(35)

with equality holding if and only if
\rho
is a pure state, i.e.,

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{2})=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho)=1
.

From the definition of concurrence,

\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\tilde{\rho})=\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2}+\lambda_{4}^{2}\geq(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4})^{2}=\mathcal{C}^{2}.

(36)

The equality can be achieved when
\rho
is a pure state,
since
\operatorname{rank}(\rho)=1
implies
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R})=1

by the rank inequality.

Combining the two inequalities together with their equality conditions gives

\mathcal{C}^{2}\leq 1-\frac{B}{2},

(37)

with equality if and only if
\rho
is pure.

For the first case, the total polarization
B
is fixed, so
\mathcal{C}_{\max}^{2}=1-\frac{B}{2}
. The equality condition then implies that the maximizing state is pure, and the pure-state condition gives
\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|^{2}=\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|^{2}
.

For the second case, Eq. (
18
) gives
\mathcal{C}_{\max}^{2}=1-\|\vec{B}^{\,+}\|^{2}=1-\|\vec{B}^{\,-}\|^{2}
, which forces the equality condition in Eq. (
37
). Hence the maximizing state is pure.

Appendix C
Full expression of final-state density matrix

This appendix collects the explicit coefficients of the final-state density matrix used in the
e^{-}e^{+}\to q\bar{q}
example discussed in the main text.

In the helicity basis
\{\hat{\mathbf{n}},\hat{\mathbf{r}},\hat{\mathbf{k}}\}
, the two-qubit spin density matrix is written as

\rho_{f}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{4}+B_{i}^{+}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}+B_{j}^{-}\,\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\sigma_{j}+C_{ij}\,\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{j}\right).

(38)

For compactness, define the common denominator

\mathcal{D}\equiv\;8\,c_{A}^{e}c_{V}^{e}c_{A}^{q}c_{V}^{q}\,z\,u+({c_{A}^{e}}^{2}+{c_{V}^{e}}^{2})\!\left[(c_{A}^{q})^{2}(1+z^{2})u^{2}+(c_{V}^{q})^{2}\!\left(2+(-1+z^{2})u^{2}\right)\right],

The non-vanishing correlation coefficients are

\big(C_{ij}\big)=\begin{pmatrix}C_{11}&0&0\\[4.0pt]
0&C_{22}&C_{23}\\[4.0pt]
0&C_{23}&C_{33}\end{pmatrix},

(39)

with

\displaystyle C_{11}

\displaystyle=\frac{\big[(c_{A}^{e})^{2}+(c_{V}^{e})^{2}\big]\big[(c_{A}^{q})^{2}-(c_{V}^{q})^{2}\big](1-z^{2})u^{2}}{\mathcal{D}},

(40)

\displaystyle C_{22}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\big[(c_{A}^{e})^{2}+(c_{V}^{e})^{2}\big](1-z^{2})\Big[(c_{A}^{q})^{2}u^{2}+(c_{V}^{q})^{2}(-2+u^{2})\Big]}{\mathcal{D}},

(41)

\displaystyle C_{23}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\,c_{V}^{q}\Big[\big((c_{A}^{e})^{2}+(c_{V}^{e})^{2}\big)c_{V}^{q}\,z+2\,c_{A}^{e}c_{V}^{e}c_{A}^{q}\,u\Big]\sqrt{(1-z^{2})(1-u^{2})}}{\mathcal{D}},

(42)

\displaystyle C_{33}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\,c_{A}^{e}c_{V}^{e}c_{A}^{q}c_{V}^{q}\,z\,u+({c_{A}^{e}}^{2}+{c_{V}^{e}}^{2})\!\left[(c_{A}^{q})^{2}(1+z^{2})u^{2}+(c_{V}^{q})^{2}\!\left(u^{2}-z^{2}(-2+u^{2})\right)\right]}{\mathcal{D}}.

(43)

The Bloch vectors (polarizations) are identical for the quark and antiquark,

\vec{B}^{\,+}=\vec{B}^{\,-}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\[4.0pt]
B_{2}\\[4.0pt]
B_{3}\end{pmatrix},

(44)

with

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\,c_{V}^{q}\Big[2\,c_{A}^{e}c_{V}^{e}c_{V}^{q}+\big((c_{A}^{e})^{2}+(c_{V}^{e})^{2}\big)c_{A}^{q}zu\Big]\sqrt{(1-z^{2})(1-u^{2})}}{\mathcal{D}},

(45)

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\Big[\big((c_{A}^{e})^{2}+(c_{V}^{e})^{2}\big)c_{A}^{q}c_{V}^{q}(1+z^{2})u+2\,c_{A}^{e}c_{V}^{e}z\big((c_{V}^{q})^{2}+(c_{A}^{q})^{2}u^{2}\big)\Big]}{\mathcal{D}}.

At the special point
u=1
and
z=0
, where the concurrence reaches its maximum in the main text, the density matrix simplifies to

\rho_{f}^{opt}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{(c_{A}^{q}-c_{V}^{q})^{2}}{2\left[(c_{A}^{q})^{2}+(c_{V}^{q})^{2}\right]}&0&0&\dfrac{(c_{A}^{q}-c_{V}^{q})(c_{A}^{q}+c_{V}^{q})}{2\left[(c_{A}^{q})^{2}+(c_{V}^{q})^{2}\right]}\\
0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0\\

(46)
