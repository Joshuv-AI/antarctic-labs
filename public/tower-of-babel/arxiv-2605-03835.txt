Birational Classification of Orbifold Compactified Jacobians
Jeremy Feusi and Sam Molcho

Abstract
. We study the equivariant orbifold birational classification problem for families of toroidal compactifications of a group G G over a toroidal base, in the cases where G G is an algebraic torus or a semiabelian scheme. The classification is reduced to the problem of finding the minimal orbifold toroidal compactifications of G G in the world of logarithmic geometry, which is shown to be a combinatorial problem. We solve the problem for families of algebraic tori, Jacobians of families of nodal curves, and semiabelian schemes with abelian generic fiber. The general semiabelian case is reduced to an open conjecture. These results generalize and geometrically interpret recent results of [ 22 ] .



1.
Introduction

The birational classification of algebraic varieties is one of the central
problems of algebraic geometry. The analogous problem for orbifolds is much
less studied, but exhibits several novel phenomena. From the outset, the definition
is interesting: it is an observation of Kresch and Tschinkel

[
18
]
that to call two DM stacks
\mathcal{X}
and

\mathcal{Y}
birationally equivalent, one should not simply require the
existence of a birational map between them – a definition which would imply
for example that every orbifold is birational to a variety. Rather, one should
say that two Deligne-Mumford stacks
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are
birationally equivalent if there exists a correspondence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

with
\mathcal{Z}
a DM stack and with the maps
p,q
proper, birational and

representable
. We remark here that this definition is not equivalent to
the traditional one even when restricted to varieties, although it is
so if we demand that the varieties in question are proper.

The problem of birational classification of orbifolds is strictly
harder than that of algebraic varieties, and thus a complete classification is
currently out of reach. It is therefore natural to study the
classification problem upon restriction to subcategories (full or not) of the
category of orbifolds or DM stacks for which the classical problem is completely understood. For example, one may try to study the
birational classes of orbifolds
\mathcal{X}
“lifting” a single given birational
class, i.e., the birational classification of orbifolds
\mathcal{X}
whose
underlying good moduli space
X
has a fixed birational type as an algebraic
variety. Alternatively, one may impose dimension constraints, group actions, et cetera.

Recently, Schmitt
[
22
]
solved an instance of this problem, namely the
birational classification problem in the category of toric orbifolds with
respect to toric morphisms. The corresponding problem for varieties is trivial
here – the only birational invariant of a complete toric variety is its
dimension – but the orbifold problem remains interesting, with infinitely many
distinct orbifold birational classes. Using the correspondence between toric
orbifolds and stacky fans (see for example

[
3
,
7
,
9
,
10
]
),
Schmitt gave a bijection between toric birational equivalence classes of toric
orbifolds and certain combinatorial objects which he called sublattice
colorings.

Our goal in this paper is twofold. On the one hand, we aim to explain the
geometric meaning of Schmitt’s result and place it in what is, in our view,
its natural conceptual framework: Schmitt’s sublattice colorings are precisely the fans of a generalized class of geometric objects which, while not traditional objects of algebraic geometry, arise naturally in logarithmic geometry. On the other hand, using this framework, we
can extend the result to other equivariant geometries – namely compactified semiabelian schemes.
Both of those goals are achieved by studying this problem using the language of logarithmic geometry.

This is in contrast with the standard minimal model program where, for example,
the minimal toric surfaces are the Hirzebruch surfaces or

The Néron mapping property for more general log abelian varieties

Assuming this conjecture, the results of this paper generalize to arbitrary families of semiabelian varieties

1.2.
Organization of the paper.

The goal of sections
2
,
3
and
5
is to describe the tropicalization and geometric realization functors defined above. Many of the technical results required for this are currently missing from the literature in the necessary generality and are therefore developed within these sections. The expert comfortable with the foundations of tropicalization maps in logarithmic geometry may wish to skip some of the more technical results in these sections. In Section
2
we introduce the precise definition of a
logarithmic compactification and prove basic results, which require some
technical machinery related to stacks over the category of log schemes.
In Sections
3
and
5
,
we establish the connection between the combinatorial and the logarithmic
category, which requires us to generalize the notion of an Artin fan

[
21
]
to our setting and prove several
generalizations of the results of
[
1
]
.

and
a,b\in N_{\sigma}
. We say
a\leq b
if
b-a\in\sigma
. Similarly,
if
a,b\in M_{\sigma}
, we say
a\leq b
if
b-a\in\sigma^{\vee}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(\sigma_{0},N_{0})\in\RPC
and let
N
be a lattice. Fix a symmetric bilinear map
Q:M\times M\to M_{0}
.

(1)

Q
is called positive-definite if for every
m\in M\setminus\{0\}
we have
Q(m,m)\in\sigma_{0}^{\vee}\setminus\{0\}
.

(2)

If
\tau\to\sigma_{0}
is a morphism of RPCs, we denote the composition of
Q
with
M_{0}\to M_{\tau}
by
Q_{\tau}
.

(3)

We write
Q_{\mathrm{hom}}:M\to\Hom(M,M_{0})
for the morphism defined by
m\mapsto Q(m,\ast)
.

(4)

Observe that
\Hom(M,M_{0})\cong\Hom(N_{0},N)
by dualizing. We write
Q_{\mathrm{hom},N}:M\to\Hom(N_{0},N)
for the morphism obtained
by composing
Q_{\mathrm{hom}}
with this isomorphism.

(5)

Write
\underline{M}
for the constant sheaf of groups on
\RPC
with fiber
M
. We denote by

\Hom(\underline{M},\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}})^{(M)}
the subsheaf of
\Hom(\underline{M},\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}})
on
\RPC/\sigma_{0}

which to
\tau\to\sigma_{0}
assigns the set of
\phi\in\Hom(M,M_{\tau})
such that for every
m\in M
there exist

m_{1},m_{2}\in M
with:

Q_{\tau}(m_{1},m)\leq\phi(m)\leq Q_{\tau}(m_{2},m).

The following property of positive-definite symmetric bilinear forms will be key to many properties of tropical abelian varieties:

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix a symmetric positive-definite bilinear form
Q
over
\sigma\in\RPC
.
Let
m\in M
and
n\in N_{\sigma}\cap\sigma
. If
n(Q(m,m))=0
then
n(Q(m,m^{\prime}))=0
for all
m^{\prime}\in M
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists
m^{\prime}\in M
such that
n(Q(m,m^{\prime}))\neq 0
. Replacing
m^{\prime}
with
-m^{\prime}
if necessary, we can assume
that
n(Q(m,m^{\prime}))<0
. By the positive-definiteness of
Q
, we have for each
\lambda>0
that
n(Q(\lambda m+m^{\prime},\lambda m+m^{\prime}))\geq 0
.
Expanding this expression, we get:

0\leq 2\lambda n(Q(m,m^{\prime}))+n(Q(m^{\prime},m^{\prime})).

Letting
\lambda\to\infty
yields a contradiction.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(\sigma_{0},N_{\sigma_{0}})\in\RPC
and
Q:M\times M\to M_{\sigma_{0}}
a symmetric positive-definite bilinear form. We
call the sheaf

\Hom(\underline{M},\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}})^{(M)}/Q_{\mathrm{hom}}(\underline{M})

on
\RPC/\sigma_{0}

a family of principally polarized tropical abelian varieties. The group structure on
\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}}
induces the structure of a group object
over
\sigma_{0}
on
\Hom(\underline{M},\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}})^{(M)}/Q_{\mathrm{hom}}(\underline{M})
.

A morphism
X\to Y
of stacks over
\RPC
is called a family of
principally polarized tropical abelian varieties if
X
is a group object over
Y
and
for every
\tau\in\RPC
we have that
X\times_{Y}\tau\to\tau
is a family of tropical
abelian varieties in the previous sense.

Remark 3.6
.

All families of tropical abelian varieties in this article will
be principally polarized. We will therefore drop the prefix
“principally polarized” in what follows. The results carry over similarly to more general
non-principally polarized tropical abelian varieties. In this case, the lattice
M
and its dual
N

can no longer be canonically identified and more care is required in the definition of a tropical abelian
variety (see
[
14
]
for details).
We restrict to the principally polarized case to avoid these additional complications.

The above definition of a tropical abelian variety matches the definition in
[
14
]
.
Below we give an alternative description of the
\tau
-points of a tropical abelian variety over an RPC which
is more similar to the way the functor of points of a cone complex is defined. This will be used later when
we study the combinatorial structure of partial log compactifications.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\sigma_{0}\in\RPC
and
Q:M\times M\to M_{\sigma_{0}}
be a symmetric positive-definite bilinear form.
We define
(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)}\subseteq N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N
to be the subset of all tuples
(n,n^{\prime})

such that
n\in\sigma_{0}
and
n^{\prime}
lies in the intersection of
N
and the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of
Q_{\mathrm{hom},N}(M)(n)\subseteq N
.

Lemma 3.8
.

In the above situation,
m\in M
acts on
(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)}
by

m\cdot(n,n^{\prime}):=(n,n^{\prime}+Q_{\mathrm{hom},N}(m)(n)).

We denote the action of
m\in M
on
(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)}
by
T_{m}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that if
(n,n^{\prime})\in(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)}
then also
(n,n^{\prime}+Q_{\mathrm{hom},N}(m)(n))\in(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)}
.
This is clear since
Q_{\mathrm{hom},N}(m)(n)
obviously lies in the span of
Q_{\mathrm{hom},N}(M)(n)
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\tau\to\sigma_{0}
be an RPC over
\sigma_{0}
in the situation above. Write
\Hom(\tau,(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)})

for the set of homomorphisms
N_{\tau}\to N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N
over
N_{\tau}\to N_{\sigma_{0}}
such that the image
of
N_{\tau}\cap\tau
lies in
(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)}
. By the preceding lemma, there is an action of
M

on
\Hom(\tau,(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)})
by translation. With this action, we have:

\Hom(\tau,\Hom(\underline{M},\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}})^{(M)}/Q_{\mathrm{hom}}(\underline{M}))\cong\Hom(\tau,(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)})/M.

Proof.

We first show that a morphism
\tau\to\Hom(\underline{M},\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}})/Q_{\mathrm{hom}}(\underline{M})
is equivalent to the
datum of a morphism
N_{\tau}\to(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)
modulo the action of
M
. By definition, a morphism

\tau\to\Hom(\underline{M},\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}})/Q_{\mathrm{hom}}(\underline{M})
is the datum of an equivalence class

[\phi]\in\Hom(M,M_{\tau})/Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom}}(\underline{M})
. We obtain a morphism
\phi^{\vee}:N_{\tau}\to N
and taking
the product with
f:N_{\tau}\to N_{\sigma_{0}}
gives a morphism
N_{\tau}\to N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N
. Clearly, this construction
is a bijection. Note that
\phi^{\prime}\in[\phi]
if and only if

\phi^{\prime}-\phi=Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom}}(m)
for some
m\in M
. This is equivalent to
{\phi^{\prime}}^{\vee}-\phi^{\vee}=Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom},N}(m)
,
so the quotients coincide.

Finally, it remains to show that
\phi\in\Hom(M,M_{\tau})^{(M)}
if and only if
f\times\phi^{\vee}
sends
\tau\cap N_{\tau}

to
(N_{\sigma_{0}}\times N)^{(M)}
. First assume that
\phi\in\Hom(M,M_{\tau})^{(M)}
. Assume for a contradiction
that there exists
n\in\tau\cap N_{\tau}

such that
\phi^{\vee}(n)
does not lie in the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of
Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom}}(M)(n)
. Then there exists
m\in M
such that

m(\phi^{\vee}(n))>0
and
m|_{Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom},N}(M)(n)}=0
, viewing
m
as a map
N\to\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
m_{1}\in M
be arbitrary. Then
n(Q_{\tau}(m_{1},m))=0<n(\phi(m))
. It follows
that
Q_{\tau}(m_{1},m)-\phi(m)\notin\tau^{\vee}
which contradicts the definition of
\Hom(M,M_{\tau})^{(M)}
.

The converse is more involved: Assume that for each
n\in N_{\tau}\cap\tau
, we have that

\phi^{\vee}(n)
lies in the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of
Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom}}(M)(n)

and let
m\in M
be arbitrary. We must show that there exist
m_{1},m_{2}\in M
such that

Q_{\tau}(m_{1},m)\leq\phi(m)\leq Q_{\tau}(m_{2},m).

Note that
\phi(m)\geq Q_{\tau}(m_{1},m)\Leftrightarrow Q_{\tau}(-m_{1},m)\geq\phi(-m)
. So it suffices to construct
m_{2}

such that
\phi(m)\leq Q_{\tau}(m_{2},m)
. This inequality is equivalent to
m(\phi^{\vee}(n))\leq m(Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom},N}(m_{2})(n))

for all
n\in\tau
. Note that the locus of
n
such that this inequality holds is convex, so it suffices to find
m_{2}

such that the inequality holds on each ray
\rho
of
\tau
. Since
\phi^{\vee}(n)
lies in the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of
Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom},N}(M)(n)
,
we can find for each
\rho
some
m_{2,\rho}\in M
such that
m(\phi^{\vee}(n_{\rho}))\leq m(Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom},N}(m_{2,\rho})(n_{\rho}))

for any
n_{\rho}\in\rho
. Setting
m_{2}:=\sum_{\rho}m_{2,\rho}
, it suffices to show that we may choose
m_{2,\rho}
such that
m(Q_{\tau,\mathrm{hom},N}(m_{2,\rho})(n))\geq 0

for any
n\in\tau
. In other words, we must show that we may choose
m_{2,\rho}\in Q_{\tau}(m,\ast)^{-1}(\tau^{\vee})
. For
this, note that since
Q_{\tau}(m,m)\geq 0
by assumption, we may replace
m_{2,\rho}
by
m_{2,\rho}+\lambda m
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
.
So we reduce to showing that
Q_{\tau}(m,m_{2,\rho}+\lambda m)\in\tau^{\vee}
for
\lambda
sufficiently large. Let
n\in\tau

be a primitive element of a ray of
\tau
. Then:

n(Q_{\tau}(m,m_{2,\rho}+\lambda m))=n(Q_{\tau}(m,m_{2,\rho}))+\lambda n(Q_{\tau}(m,m)).

If
n(Q_{\tau}(m,m))\neq 0
then it must be positive by positive semi-definiteness of
Q_{\tau}
, so choosing
\lambda

large enough, we may assume that
n(Q_{\tau}(m,m_{2,\rho}+\lambda m))\geq 0
. Otherwise, by Lemma
3.4
,
we know that
n(Q_{\tau}(m,m_{2,\rho}+\lambda m))=0
. Since there are only finitely many rays, we may choose
\lambda
large
enough to work for all
n
. Since any element of
\tau
is a positive
\mathbb{R}
-linear combination of primitive elements of rays in
\tau

it then follows that
n(Q_{\tau}(m,m_{2,\rho}+\lambda m))\geq 0
for all
n\in\tau
, so
Q_{\tau}(m,m_{2,\rho}+\lambda m)\in\tau^{\vee}

as required.
∎

Definition 3.10
.

A split family of tropical semiabelian varieties is a product of a family of tropical abelian varieties and a family
of tropical tori.

Remark 3.11
.

In general, one could consider more general extensions of tropical abelian varieties by tropical tori.
In this case one would need to add additional restrictions similar to the bounded monodromy condition
in
[
19
]
to ensure that the extensions of log abelian varieties by log tori
tropicalize. We will not need this, since any extension of a log abelian variety by a log torus
which comes from an extension by an algebraic torus tropicalizes to the trivial extension.

Although we will not make use of this perspective, we remark that split families of tropical semiabelian varieties
are piecewise linear spaces (or piecewise linear complexes) in the sense of
[
17
]
.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
X\to Z
be a split family of tropical semiabelian varieties. A partial tropical compactification of
X\to Z
is a
monomorphism
Y\to X
representable by finite cone stacks such that there exists a section

Z\to Y
over the zero-section
Z\to X
. Morphisms of partial tropical compactifications are morphisms over
X
.
We call
Y\to X
a tropical compactification of
X\to Z
if
Y\to X
is surjective, i.e., if the base-change
over any
\sigma\in\RPC
is surjective.

We explain what it means to be a finite cone stack:

Definition 3.13
.

A cone stack
C
is called finite if there are only finitely many objects in
C
up to isomorphism and
if there are only finitely many morphisms in
C
up to 2-isomorphism.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
X\to Z
be a split family of tropical semiabelian varieties.
Any morphism
Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
of partial tropical compactifications of
X\to Z
is a monomorphism of stacks
representable by finite cone stacks.

Proof.

Since the composition
Y_{1}\to Y_{2}\to X
is a monomorphism by assumption, it follows that
Y_{1}\to Y_{2}

is also a monomorphism. Let
\sigma\to Y_{2}
be a morphism from an RPC
\sigma
. Then

Y_{1}\times_{Y_{2}}\sigma\cong Y_{1}\times_{X}\sigma
since
Y_{2}\to X
is a monomorphism.
It follows that
Y_{1}\times_{Y_{2}}\sigma
is a finite cone stack, since
Y_{1}\to X
is representable
by finite cone stacks.
∎

3.1.
The tropical realization functor

To obtain a tropical correspondence theorem we must translate split families
of tropical semiabelian varieties into stacks over the category of
fs log schemes. As explained in

[
4
]
, there is a standard
procedure for doing this. For convenience, we recall the key points of this procedure
below.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stack over
\RPC
and let
M\in\ShpMon^{\mathrm{op}}
be an
arbitrary sharp saturated and integral monoid. We define:

\Hom(M,\overline{\mathcal{C}}):=\mathop{colim}\displaylimits_{Q\to M}\Hom_{\RPC}(Q^{\vee},\mathcal{C})

where the 2-colimit is over the category of morphisms

Q\to M
in
\ShpMon
for an arbitrary toric monoid
Q
.

Remark 3.16
.

In the above definition we are using the equivalence of categories
between
\RPC
and the opposite category of the category of
toric monoids. It is clear that the above definition defines a stack for the chaotic topology (i.e., the Grothendieck topology generated
by the class of isomorphisms) on
\ShpMon^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Definition 3.17
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stack over
\RPC
. We define a
prestack
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
on
\LogSch/\Spec(K)
by:

\Hom(X,\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=\Hom(\overline{M}_{X}(X),\overline{\mathcal{C}}),

where
K
denotes the ground field (which we have assumed to
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero).
Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
be the strict étale stackification of

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
.
We call
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
the geometric realization
of
\mathcal{C}
. The functor sending
\mathcal{C}

to
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
is called the geometric realization
functor.

Example 3.18
.

If
\mathcal{C}=\sigma
is an RPC, then
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}=[X_{\sigma}/T]
, where
X_{\sigma}

is the affine toric variety with cone
\sigma
and dense open torus
T
. The canonical quotient
map
X_{\sigma}\to[X_{\sigma}/T]
is an Artin fan for
X_{\sigma}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}=\mathcal{A}_{X_{\sigma}}
.
We call
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
an
Artin cone
.
More generally, if
\Sigma
is a fan in some lattice, then
\mathcal{A}_{\Sigma}=[X_{\Sigma}/T]
for the toric
variety
X_{\Sigma}
with fan
\Sigma
and dense open torus
T
.

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
\sigma\in\RPC
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{\prime}

represents the functor
X\mapsto\Hom_{\ShpMon}(\sigma^{\vee}\cap M_{\sigma},\overline{M}_{X}(X)).

Proof.

First observe that since
X\mapsto\overline{M}_{X}(X)
is a sheaf, the
functor
X\mapsto\Hom_{\ShpMon}(\sigma^{\vee}\cap M_{\sigma},\overline{M}_{X}(X))

is indeed a stack.
Hence it suffices to show that
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{\prime}
represents
this functor. This is a direct computation:

\displaystyle\Hom(X,\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathop{colim}\displaylimits_{Q\to\overline{M}_{X}(X)}\Hom_{\ShpMon}(Q^{\vee},\sigma\cap N_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=\mathop{colim}\displaylimits_{Q\to\overline{M}_{X}(X)}\Hom_{\ShpMon}(\sigma^{\vee}\cap M_{\sigma},Q)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\ShpMon}(\sigma^{\vee}\cap M_{\sigma},\overline{M}_{X}(X)).

∎

Lemma 3.20
.

With notation as above, the functor
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}

is a stack for the strict smooth topology.

Proof.

Let
Y
be an fs log scheme and
f:X\to Y
a strict smooth cover. Let
\xi\in\Hom(X,\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}})
be equipped
with a descent datum

\alpha:\pi_{1}^{*}\xi\to\pi_{2}^{*}\xi

on
X\times_{Y}X
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a stack in the strict étale topology, effectiveness
of this descent datum is strict étale local on
Y
. Since the underlying morphism of schemes of
f

is smooth, étale locally on
Y
it admits a section. Hence after replacing
Y
by a strict étale cover,
we may assume that
f
admits a section
s:Y\to X
. Since
f
is strict, the section
s
is strict as well.

Set
\eta:=s^{*}\xi\in\Hom(Y,\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}})
. We claim that
\eta
descends
\xi
.
Let

\Delta=(s\circ f,id_{X}):X\to X\times_{Y}X.

Pulling back
\alpha
along
\Delta
, we obtain an isomorphism

\beta:=\Delta^{*}(\alpha):f^{*}\eta=(s\circ f)^{*}\xi\to\xi.

It remains to check that
\beta
is compatible with the descent datum on
\xi
.
Let

\delta=(s\circ f\circ\pi_{1},\pi_{1},\pi_{2}):X\times_{Y}X\to X\times_{Y}X\times_{Y}X.

Pulling back the cocycle condition

\pi_{23}^{*}(\alpha)\circ\pi_{12}^{*}(\alpha)=\pi_{13}^{*}(\alpha)

along
\delta
, we get

\alpha\circ\pi_{1}^{*}(\beta)=\pi_{2}^{*}(\beta),

which is exactly the compatibility condition. Thus
(\eta,\beta)
is a descent of
(\xi,\alpha)
,
showing that objects descend.

It remains to show that morphisms descend. Let
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\Hom(Y,\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}})
and let

\psi:f^{*}\eta_{1}\to f^{*}\eta_{2}

be a morphism such that

\pi_{2}^{*}(\psi)=\pi_{1}^{*}(\psi)

as morphisms
\pi_{1}^{*}f^{*}\eta_{1}=\pi_{2}^{*}f^{*}\eta_{1}\to\pi_{1}^{*}f^{*}\eta_{2}=\pi_{2}^{*}f^{*}\eta_{2}
on
X\times_{Y}X
.
Set

\overline{\psi}:=s^{*}(\psi):\eta_{1}\to\eta_{2}.

We claim that
f^{*}(\overline{\psi})=\psi
. Let

\Delta=(s\circ f,id_{X}):X\to X\times_{Y}X.

Pulling back the equality
\pi_{2}^{*}(\psi)=\pi_{1}^{*}(\psi)
along
\Delta
, we get

\psi=f^{*}s^{*}(\psi)=f^{*}(\overline{\psi}),

as required. This proves existence of descended morphisms.

For uniqueness, let
\theta,\theta^{\prime}:\eta_{1}\to\eta_{2}
be morphisms such that

f^{*}(\theta)=f^{*}(\theta^{\prime})
. Pulling back along the section
s
, we obtain

\theta=s^{*}f^{*}(\theta)=s^{*}f^{*}(\theta^{\prime})=\theta^{\prime}.

Hence morphisms descend uniquely. Therefore
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}

is a stack for the strict smooth topology.
∎

For later use, we record the following useful lemma.

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
Z
be an fs log scheme and
\mathcal{C}
a stack over
\RPC
. Let
Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}

be a morphism. Then strict étale locally on
Z

there exists a strict morphism
Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
and a morphism
\sigma\to\mathcal{C}

such that
Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
factors as
Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

Since the statement is strict étale local, we may assume that
Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
is represented by
a morphism
Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
of prestacks.
By
[
19
, Proposition 2.2.2.5]
, we may
assume that
Z
is atomic. Let
z\in Z
lie in the closed stratum. The stalk
\overline{M}_{Z,z}
is a sharp fs monoid, hence toric
and
\overline{M}_{Z}(Z)\to\overline{M}_{Z,z}
is an isomorphism. Let
\sigma\in\RPC
be the cone such that

\sigma^{\vee}\cap M_{\sigma}\cong\overline{M}_{Z,z}
. The morphism
Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}

corresponds to a morphism
\overline{M}_{Z}(Z)\to\mathcal{C}
, hence a morphism
\sigma\to\mathcal{C}
. The isomorphism

\overline{M}_{Z}(Z)\cong\sigma^{\vee}\cap M_{\sigma}
induces a morphism
Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
and the composition

Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
is equal to the given morphism by construction.
∎

Definition 3.22
.

We define
\mathcal{A}^{1}:=(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{Z})
. Note
that
\mathcal{A}^{1}
is a monoid object in
\RPC
via the map

(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{Z})\times(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{Z})\to(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{Z})
sending

to

.

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
X
be a stack on
\LogSch/\Spec(K)
. Then

\Hom_{\mathrm{St}}(X,\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{A}^{1}})
is a sharp integral saturated monoid,
where
\Hom_{\mathrm{St}}
denotes morphisms of stacks.

Proof.

Note that since
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}
is fibered in
setoids by Lemma
3.19
, the Hom-object is indeed
a set. We equip it with the monoid structure induced by
the monoid structure on
\mathcal{A}^{1}
. Note that the submonoid
of units of
(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{Z})
is represented by
the zero-cone
0
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is the terminal object
in the category of stacks, the monoid is sharp. To see that
it is saturated, note that for any
Z\in\LogSch/\Spec(K)
,
we have
\Hom(Z,\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{A}^{1}})=\overline{M}_{Z}(Z)

by Lemma
3.19
. Hence there is a natural monomorphism

j:\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}\to\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}}
sending
a section of
\overline{M}_{Z}(Z)
to its image in

\overline{M}^{\mathrm{gp}}_{Z}(Z)
. Let

\phi:X\to\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}}
be a morphism and assume
there exists
\psi:X\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}
such
that the composition
j\circ\psi
is equal to
n\cdot\phi
.
Let
f:Z\to X
be an arbitrary morphism from a log scheme
Z
.
Then
\phi\circ f
corresponds to a section

s
of
\overline{M}_{Z}^{\mathrm{gp}}(Z)
and the statement that
j\circ\psi=n\cdot\phi

is equivalent to the statement that
n\cdot s\in\overline{M}_{Z}(Z)
.
Since
\overline{M}_{Z}(Z)
is saturated, it follows that

s\in\overline{M}_{Z}(Z)
and hence we obtain a lift

Z\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}
. This construction is natural
since the lift is unique, as
j
is a monomorphism. We hence
obtain a lift
X\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{A}^{1}}
of
\phi
.

Finally, integrality follows from the fact that

\Hom_{\mathrm{St}}(X,\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{A}^{1}})\to\Hom_{\mathrm{St}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{trop}})

is an injection into a group.
∎

We extend
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
to a stack on the category of stacks over
\Spec(K)
as follows:

Definition 3.24
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be as above.
We define
a prestack

Corollary 3.28
.

The functor
\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}
preserves fiber products.

Proof.

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{3}}

.

is an isomorphism, where

.

, we may assume that

\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{3}
for

to the datum of a map

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}\times_{\mathcal{C}_{3}}\mathcal{C}_{2}}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\times_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}_{3}}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}

Proposition 3.32

Let
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
be a monomorphism of stacks over

representable by finite cone stacks.
Then
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{X}}
is a log étale monomorphism representable by
log DM stacks of finite presentation. Moreover:

The morphism

is representable by log
algebraic spaces if and only if for every morphism
\sigma\to\mathcal{X}
and every

the induced morphism

surjects onto

.

(2)

If the base-change of

over any cone is non-empty, The morphism
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{X}}
is proper if and only if

\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
is surjective (i.e., for every
\sigma\to\mathcal{X}
, the morphism

For the proof we require the following definition and lemma:

Definition 3.33

(
[
10
, Definition 2.2.3]
)
.

Let
\sigma
be an RPC. A stacky fan in
\sigma

is a finite set of RPCs
\Delta
contained in
\sigma
and finite-index
sublattices
N_{\tau}\subseteq N_{\sigma}\cap\Span(\tau)
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\tau_{1}\cap\tau_{2}
is a common face of
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\Delta
.

(2)

If
\tau\in\Delta
and
\tau^{\prime}
is a face of
\tau
, then
\tau^{\prime}\in\Delta
.

(3)

If
\tau_{1}\to\tau_{2}
is a face inclusion in
\Delta
then
N_{\tau_{2}}\cap\Span\tau_{1}=N_{\tau_{1}}
.

A stacky fan defines a cone complex (also denoted by
\Delta
)
obtained by gluing the cones
(\tau,N_{\tau})
along the face inclusions in
\Delta
.

Lemma 3.34
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}\to\sigma
be a monomorphism from a finite cone stack
\mathcal{Y}
to an RPC
\sigma
. Then
\mathcal{Y}

is isomorphic to a stacky fan in
\sigma
.

Proof.

First observe that since
\sigma
is fibered in setoids and
\phi:\mathcal{Y}\to\sigma
is a monomorphism, the stack
\mathcal{Y}

is fibered in setoids, i.e.,
\mathcal{Y}
is a cone space. Let
\Delta:=\{\phi(\tau)|\tau\in\mathcal{Y}\}
. The set
\Delta

is clearly finite and closed under taking faces. Since

\mathcal{Y}\to\sigma
is a monomorphism, for each
\tau\in\Delta
there is a unique cone
\phi^{-1}(\tau)\in\mathcal{Y}

such that
\phi^{-1}(\tau)\to\tau
is an isomorphism.
Let
N_{\tau}
be the image of
N_{\phi^{-1}(\tau)}
under
\phi
. Since
\phi
is a morphism of cone spaces, we have

N_{\tau}\subseteq N_{\sigma}\cap\Span(\tau)
. Moreover,
N_{\tau}
spans
\Span(\tau)
as an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
and hence
N_{\tau}
is of finite index in
N_{\sigma}\cap\Span(\tau)
. We show that
\Delta
satisfies the properties in
the statement of the lemma. Let
\tau:=\tau_{1}\cap\tau_{2}
for
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\Delta
and let

N_{\tau}^{\prime}:=N_{\tau_{1}}\cap N_{\tau_{2}}\cap\Span(\tau).

The pair
(\tau,N_{\tau}^{\prime})
is an RPC and the inclusions
\tau\to\tau_{i}
lift to morphisms

\phi_{i}:(\tau,N_{\tau}^{\prime})\to\phi^{-1}(\tau_{i})\to\mathcal{Y}
. Note that the compositions of the
\phi_{i}
with
the projection
\mathcal{Y}\to\sigma
are equal. Hence since
\mathcal{Y}\to\sigma
is a monomorphism, the two morphisms

\phi_{i}
are equal. If the image of
\phi_{1}
intersects the interior of
\phi^{-1}(\tau_{1})
this is only possible if there is a face
morphism
\phi^{-1}(\tau_{1})\to\phi^{-1}(\tau_{2})
in
\mathcal{Y}
so
\tau=\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{1}
is a face of
\tau_{2}
. If not,
let
\phi^{-1}(\tau_{1}^{\prime})\subseteq\phi^{-1}(\tau_{1})
be a proper face such that the image of
\phi_{1}
factors through
\phi^{-1}(\tau_{1}^{\prime})
.
Then
\tau=\tau_{1}^{\prime}\cap\tau_{2}
and by induction on the dimension of
\tau_{1}
it follows that (1) is satisfied.

For (2), notice that if
\tau^{\prime}
is a face of
\tau\in\Delta
, then
\tau^{\prime}
is the image of some face of
\phi^{-1}(\tau)
,
so
\tau^{\prime}\in\Delta
. It remains to show point (3): Let
\tau_{1}\to\tau_{2}
be a face morphism in
\Delta
. The argument
above for the case
\tau=\tau_{1}
shows that this lifts to a face morphism
\phi^{-1}(\tau_{1})\to\phi^{-1}(\tau_{2})
. In particular,

N_{\phi^{-1}(\tau_{1})}=N_{\phi^{-1}(\tau_{2})}\cap\Span(\tau_{1})
and finally
N_{\tau_{1}}=N_{\tau_{2}}\cap\Span(\tau_{1})
.

We now show that
\mathcal{Y}\cong\Delta
. First note that
\mathcal{Y}\to\sigma
lifts to a monomorphism
\mathcal{Y}\to\Delta

by construction of
\Delta
. Hence it suffices to show that every morphism
f:\tau\to\Delta
for
\tau
an RPC lifts uniquely to
a morphism
\tau\to\mathcal{Y}
. Fix such a morphism. Then
f(\tau)\subseteq\tau^{\prime}
for some
\tau^{\prime}\in\Delta
. Since
f(N_{\tau})\subseteq N_{\tau^{\prime}}
,
the morphism
f
lifts to a morphism
\tau\to\phi^{-1}(\tau^{\prime})
. Composing with
\phi^{-1}(\tau^{\prime})\to\mathcal{Y}
gives a lift
of
f
to
\mathcal{Y}
. Uniqueness of this lift is clear since
\mathcal{Y}\to\Delta
is a monomorphism.
∎

is proper if and
only if

∎

Using this result, we can give a combinatorial description of when a
combinatorial partial log compactification is a log compactification of

Lemma 3.35

Let notation be as in Definition

A partial tropical compactification

is a tropical compactification if
and only if

is proper.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.32
(2) we know that
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
is a tropical compactification if and only
if
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{X}}
is proper (non-emptyness of the base-change over any cone is clear by restricting to the origin, corresponding to the dense open subset of

with trivial log structure). Note that since

is log smooth, the morphism

is a strict smooth cover. By

∎

Proof of Proposition

3.2.
The correspondence theorem

The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem:

Combine Theorem

The following is a technical lemma which allows us to tropicalize log étale monomorphisms:

Lemma 3.40
.

Let
X
be a log smooth log DM stack with a global chart and

a log étale monomorphism representable

By
[

\mathcal{A}_{Y}
for

\mathcal{A}_{Y}=\mathcal{A}_{\Sigma}
.

U_{i}\to X
(resp.
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\to X

\tau_{i}\to\tau

(resp.
\tau_{i,j}\to\tau

(resp.
\tau_{i,j}

is surjective. Note that

[

, Tag 025G]

to

under these lifts. Since

is log smooth, the fiber of

Definition 4.1

Let

The results of the previous sections reduce the problem of classifying log compactifications

Definition 4.2

Remark 4.4

It is clear that birational equivalence is an equivalence relation since subdivisions

Lemma 4.5

By symmetry, it suffices to assume that there exists a subdivision

Proposition 4.18

Definition 4.19

Proposition 4.22
.

Let

be a minimal fan and
\Delta
be as in the definition of a minimal fan. Then

\Sigma_{S}\cong M_{\Sigma_{\Delta}}
. In particular,
\Sigma_{S}
is minimal.

Proof.

First note that for each
\tau\in\Delta
, we have
\tau\cap\tilde{N}_{\tau}\subseteq S
,
so we get a morphism
\Sigma_{\Delta}\to\Sigma_{S}
. We first show that this morphism is represented
by subdivisions: Let
\tau\to\Sigma_{S}
be an arbitrary morphism. Since
\Sigma_{S}\to A\times T_{N^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{trop}}

is a monomorphism, we have

\tau\times_{\Sigma_{S}}\Sigma_{\Delta}\cong\tau\times_{A\times T_{N^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{trop}}}\Sigma_{\Delta}.

In particular, the latter is representable by finite cone complexes by our previous results.
Pick a lift
\tau\to N_{\sigma}\times N\times N^{\prime}
. By
construction, this cone complex is the union of the preimages of
(\tau^{\prime},\tilde{N}_{\tau^{\prime}})

under this lift, for
\tau^{\prime}\in\Delta
. Since
\tau\to A\times T_{N^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
factors through

\Sigma_{S}
, the preimage of
\tau^{\prime}\cap\tilde{N}_{\tau^{\prime}}
is equal to the restriction
of
\tau\cap N_{\tau}
to the preimage of
\tau^{\prime}
. Since
\tau^{\prime}
spans
\tilde{N}_{\tau^{\prime}}
, it follows that
the preimage of
\tilde{N}_{\tau^{\prime}}
is the intersection of

with the span of the preimage of

\tau^{\prime}
. It remains to show that the preimages cover all of
\tau
. Indeed,
if not, we find some

not contained in the preimage (since the preimage
is a finite union of rational polyhedral cones). But

must be mapped to some element of

which is contained
in some

, a contradiction. It follows that
\tau\times_{\Sigma_{S}}\Sigma_{\Delta}

is a subdivision, which hence defines a map
\tau\to M_{\Sigma_{\Delta}}
. This construction is

\Sigma_{S}\to M_{\Sigma_{\Delta}}
. To construct an inverse,
assume we are given a morphism
\tau\to M_{\Sigma_{\Delta}}
corresponding to a subdivision

\Sigma\to\tau
and a morphism
\Sigma\to\Sigma_{\Delta}
. For each cone
\tau^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\Delta}

and any lift

\tau^{\prime}\to N_{\sigma,\mathbb{R}}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime},

we know that
\tau^{\prime}\cap N_{\tau^{\prime}}\subseteq S
.
Since the
\tau^{\prime}
cover all of
\tau
and the lattices
N_{\tau^{\prime}}
agree with the restriction of
N_{\tau}
,
it follows that
\tau\cap N_{\tau}
maps to
S
for any lift of
\tau\to A\times T_{N^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
.
Hence we obtain a map
\tau\to\Sigma_{S}
. This construction is clearly inverse to the previous

∎

Corollary 4.23
.

In the above situation the following statements hold:

(1)

\Sigma_{S}
for some minimal fan

.

(2)

If

We start with point (1).
By Theorem

for

is a partial tropical compactification. Let

be as in Proposition

,
so

is a subdivision. Then

is a subdivision. We claim that

is a finite cone complex. Indeed, the projection

The above corollary gives a concrete combinatorial description for the set of birational equivalence classes of generalized
partial tropical compactifications over a rational polyhedral cone
\sigma
. We complete the classification in general
by means of the following result:

Proposition 4.24
.

Let
X\to Z
be a split family of tropical semiabelian varieties and let
Y\to X
be a generalized partial tropical compactification.
Then
Y
is minimal if and only if for every morphism
\sigma\to Z
from an RPC
\sigma
, the base-change

Y\times_{Z}\sigma\to X\times_{Z}\sigma
is minimal.

Proof.

Assume first that
Y
is minimal and let
Y_{1}
be birationally equivalent to
Y\times_{Z}\sigma
. Let
Y_{2}
be
a subdivision of
Y_{1}
such that there exists a morphism
Y_{2}\to Y\times_{Z}\sigma
. Composing with the projection
onto the first factor gives a morphism
Y_{2}\to Y
which descends to a morphism
Y_{1}\to Y
by minimality of
Y
.
Together with the structure morphism
Y_{1}\to\sigma
, this defines a morphism
Y_{1}\to Y\times_{Z}\sigma
showing
that
Y\times_{Z}\sigma
is minimal.

Conversely, assume that

Y\times_{Z}\sigma\to X\times_{Z}\sigma

is minimal for each
\sigma\in\RPC
. Let

Y_{1}\to X
be a morphism and
Y_{2}\to Y_{1}
a subdivision such that there exists a morphism

Y_{2}\to Y
. We must construct functorial maps
\Hom(\sigma,Y_{1})\to\Hom(\sigma,Y)
. Let
\sigma\to Y_{1}

be a morphism and write
\Sigma:=\sigma\times_{Y_{1}}Y_{2}
. This is a subdivision of
\sigma
by assumption.
The projection onto the first factor and
\Sigma\to Y_{2}\to Y
define a morphism
\Sigma\to Y\times_{Z}\sigma

over
\sigma\to X\times_{Z}\sigma
. By minimality of
Y\times_{Z}\sigma
we obtain a morphism
\sigma\to Y\times_{Z}\sigma
.
Projecting onto the first factor we obtain a morphism
\sigma\to Y
as required. Functoriality is clear,
so we obtain a morphism

. To see that this morphism is unique, note that any morphism
\sigma\to Y

can be uniquely factored as
\sigma\to Y\times_{Z}\sigma\to Y

∎

This result means that for a split family of tropical semiabelian varieties over a cone complex, the datum of a minimal
generalized partial tropical compactification is equivalent to the datum of a minimal fan over each cone compatible with face inclusions.
We end this section with the following result for complete minimal fans.

Proposition 4.25

A minimal fan is complete if and only

is birationally equivalent to a tropical compactification of

.

Let

be a minimal fan and write

\Delta
. If

is birationally equivalent to a tropical compactification of

is complete. Conversely, if

is complete, then

must be complete, so by Lemma

is a tropical compactification. Since

∎

5.
Translation to the geometric setting

In the following sections we will apply the results of the previous section
to solve the birational classification problem for specific classes
of log semiabelian varieties. For this, we must translate the combinatorial results to
the geometric setting. We fix a log semiabelian variety

over an irreducible log smooth

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
be a generalized partial tropical compactification. Then
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}\times_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{X}}}X\to X

is a generalized partial log compactification of

Proof.

Pick a subdivision
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{Y}
such that
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
is a partial tropical compactification.
We need only show that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is proper and representable by
log algebraic spaces. Consider a morphism
W\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}
from
a log scheme

. By Lemma
3.21
, strict étale locally on

we have a

for some RPC

. By Corollary
3.28
,
it holds that
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}\times_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}\cong\mathcal{A}_{\sigma\times_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}

By assumption,

is a proper morphism of toric varieties. In particular, the pullback to

is a proper morphism of log schemes.
∎

Our next goal is to prove that the inclusion of generalized partial log compactifications does not change the notion

be a

\mathcal{A}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}

\sigma^{\prime}
in
\Sigma
.
Note that the unique maximal point in
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
maps to the maximal point in
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
and since
the fiber of
T\to\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
over this point is non-empty, it follows that there exists
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
over
t
with

\overline{M}_{T^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\cong\sigma^{\prime\vee}\cap M_{\sigma^{\prime}}
. Let
U\to T^{\prime}
be a strict étale cover such that the morphism

T^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is represented by a morphism
U\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}
. Let
u\in U
be a point
over
t^{\prime}
. The composition of the morphism
\overline{M}_{U,u}\to\overline{M}_{U}(U)
in

induced by the restriction

gives a morphism

. The fact that

is a lift of

implies that the composition of

By Theorem

, the morphisms
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{X}}

and
\mathcal{A}_{\Sigma}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Y}}

If a birational equivalence class of generalized log compactifications contains a combinatorial partial log compactification

6.
Birational classification of compactified Jacobians

We use the results of the previous section to classify all compactified Jacobians over log smooth bases

of log curves over

with a section

. Denote by

the dense open subset
on which

has trivial log structure. In particular,

is a family of smooth curves over

Definition 6.1
.

A partially compactified Jacobian is a separated log smooth morphism

of
log DM stacks of finite presentation, together with an action

.

(2)

The unit section

extends to a section

.

.

Definition 6.2
.

A morphism of partially compactified Jacobians is a

J_{C}
-equivariant morphism over

preserving the extension of the unit section.

Remark 6.3
.

A semi-stable degeneration of
J_{C}|_{U}
is an example of a proper
vertical log smooth morphism.

To be able to apply our results so far, we must construct a morphism from

to a log abelian variety. This is based on the following result by Holmes et al.:

(Theorem 1.7 in

The logarithmic Jacobian

.

Let

be a partially compactified Jacobian. Then

admits a unique

-equivariant log étale monomorphism

Since

Proof of Corollary

Note that over

to

For equivariance, consider the

defined as follows:

f_{1}
is the composition of the group action morphism

It remains to show that

We must show that

To see that

Since

Proposition 7.7
.

The map which sends
X
to

defines an equivalence
of categories between the category of complete toric orbifold bundles
over
S
and the category of log compactifications
of

Proof.

Note that the unit section
S\to T
composed with the inclusion

gives a lift of the unit section
S\to T_{\mathrm{log}}
to

X
. Hence by the preceding lemma, we know that when
X
is a
toric orbifold bundle, then
X\to T_{\mathrm{log}}
is a partial log compactification.
If
X
is complete, then
X\to S
satisfies the valuative criterion for properness.
Since
\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathrm{log}}
satisfies the valuative criterion for properness,
so does
T_{\mathrm{log}}\to S
. It follows that
X\to T_{\mathrm{log}}
satisfies the valuative
criterion for properness. Note that
X
is of finite presentation
since this is strict étale local on

and toric varieties are of finite presentation. Moreover, the diagonal
of
T_{\mathrm{log}}
is representable by morphisms of finite presentation by Lemma

, so it follows that
X\to T_{\mathrm{log}}
is proper,
i.e.,

is a log compactification.

Conversely, assume that

is a log compactification such
that
X
is representable by a log DM stack. We obtain
a morphism
T\to X
by composing the lift
S\to X
of the
unit section with the group action

T\times X\to X.

Note that the composition
T\to X\to T_{\mathrm{log}}
is the canonical inclusion

T\to T_{\mathrm{log}}
and hence in particular a monomorphism. Moreover,

T\to X
is the base-change of the lift of the zero-section

over

. Since both

are log étale
over

shows that

is log étale, so by base-change

is log étale. Since
T
is equipped with the trivial

is a strict étale
monomorphism, hence an open immersion
[
23
, Tag 025G]
.

Assume we are given a morphism
X_{1}\to X_{2}
of toric orbifold bundles.
Then the composition

X_{1}\to X_{2}\to T_{\mathrm{log}}

is a
T
-equivariant
log étale monomorphism which preserves the embedding
of
T
. Hence by the uniqueness in Lemma
7.6
,
it follows that
X_{1}\to X_{2}
is a morphism over

showing
that the functor is fully faithful.

It remains to show that strict étale locally on
S
we have

X\cong S\times X_{\Delta}
for some complete toric DM stack

.
By Theorem
3.36
, there exists an Artin fan

such that there exists a tropical

and a tropical compactification

Since
S
has trivial log structure, the Artin fan

is a point modulo a finite group. Hence after replacing

with an étale cover, we may assume that
\mathcal{A}_{S}
is a point
with trivial log structure. In this case, we have that
\mathcal{T}\cong T_{N}^{\mathrm{trop}}

is a log compactification, the map

is surjective by Lemma

is
isomorphic to a stacky fan. Let

is in particular normal. Hence the underlying DM stack of

By equivariance, the log structure on

Since the image of

Proof of Corollary
7.12
.

By Theorem
7.11

we get a bijection between the set of birational equivalence classes of toric
DM stacks and the set of subsets

S\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
of the form

S=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Delta}\sigma\cap N_{\sigma},

for some complete stacky fan
\Delta
. We claim that the
datum of
S
is equivalent to the datum of a sublattice
coloring of
N
. Indeed, given a sublattice coloring

(C_{N^{\prime}})_{N^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}}
, we obtain a set as above
by letting:

S:=\bigcup_{N^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}}C_{N^{\prime}}\cap N^{\prime}.

Conversely, given
S
, define for each finite-index
sublattice
N^{\prime}\subseteq N
a set
C_{N^{\prime}}
by letting

C_{N^{\prime}}
be the union of all maximal dimensional rational
polyhedral cones
\sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\sigma\cap N^{\prime}=\sigma\cap S
. We claim that this is a
sublattice coloring. For this, observe that

C_{N^{\prime}}\supseteq\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Delta:N_{\sigma}=N^{\prime}}\sigma.

It suffices to show that these two sets are equal.
Let
\sigma\subseteq C_{N^{\prime}}
be a maximal dimensional RPC
such that
\sigma\cap N^{\prime}=\sigma\cap S
. Assume that

\sigma
is not contained in the locus
\bigcup_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Delta:N_{\sigma^{\prime}}=N^{\prime}}\sigma^{\prime}
.
Hence there exists
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Delta
maximal such that

\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}
intersects the relative interior of
\sigma

and
N_{\sigma^{\prime}}\neq N^{\prime}
. In particular,
\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}
is maximal dimensional,
i.e.,
\Span(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime})=N_{\mathbb{R}}
. By the preceding lemma, we have

N_{\sigma^{\prime}}=(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}\cap N_{\sigma^{\prime}})^{\mathrm{gp}}=(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}\cap S)^{\mathrm{gp}}=(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}\cap N^{\prime})^{\mathrm{gp}}=N^{\prime},

a contradiction. Hence equality holds, showing that

is a union of finitely many rational polyhedral cones.
It is clear that there are only finitely many non-empty
