Lean-certified four-point HRT results for three lattice points and one off-lattice point
Vignon Oussa

Abstract
. We record a Lean-certified theorem package for the four-point Heil–Ramanathan–Topiwala configuration Λ = { 0 , a , b , ν } ⊂ ℝ 2 , L 0 = ℤ ​ a + ℤ ​ b , ν = r ​ a + s ​ b , \Lambda=\{0,a,b,\nu\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad L_{0}=\mathbb{Z}a+\mathbb{Z}b,\qquad\nu=ra+sb, with a a and b b linearly independent. The principal certified theorem states that if | σ ⁡ ( a , b ) | > 1 |\sigma(a,b)|>1 and 1 , r , s 1,r,s are linearly independent over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , then for every nonzero f ∈ L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) the four vectors f , π ⁡ ( a ) ​ f , π ⁡ ( b ) ​ f , π ⁡ ( ν ) ​ f f,\qquad\pi(a)f,\qquad\pi(b)f,\qquad\pi(\nu)f are linearly independent. A second certified theorem treats the rational-coordinate case r , s ∈ ℚ r,s\in\mathbb{Q} , where the configuration lies in a finer full-rank lattice and linear independence follows from Linnell’s theorem. The paper is written in standard mathematical prose. An appendix records the precise Lean certification ledger and the explicit analytic inputs used by the formal development and a download link is provided.

1.
Introduction

Let

T_{x}f(t)=f(t-x),\qquad M_{\omega}f(t)=e^{2\pi i\omega t}f(t),\qquad\pi(x,\omega)=M_{\omega}T_{x},

so that
\pi
denotes the usual projective Schrödinger representation on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The Heil–Ramanathan–Topiwala (HRT) conjecture asserts that for every nonzero
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and every finite set of distinct points
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the family
\{\pi(\lambda)f:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
is linearly independent
[
3
]
.

In this paper we consider the four-point configuration

\Lambda=\{0,a,b,\nu\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad L_{0}=\mathbb{Z}a+\mathbb{Z}b,\qquad\nu=ra+sb,

where
a
and
b
are linearly independent. Our aim is to present, in conventional mathematical language, the theorem package that has been certified in Lean 4.

The certified content of the paper is as follows.

We write

\sigma((x,\omega),(y,\eta))=x\eta-y\omega

for the standard symplectic form on

\operatorname{covol}(L_{0})=|\sigma(a,b)|.

This is the quantity that enters the large-covolume hypothesis in the main theorem.

2.
Statement of the main theorem

Theorem 2.1

(Dense large-covolume case)
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be linearly independent, let
\nu=ra+sb
, and let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be nonzero. Assume that

|\sigma(a,b)|>1\qquad\text{and}\qquad 1,r,s\ \text{are linearly independent over }\mathbb{Q}.

Then the four vectors

f,\qquad\pi(a)f,\qquad\pi(b)f,\qquad\pi(\nu)f

are linearly independent over
\mathbb{C}
.

This is the principal certified theorem in the Lean package. In the formal files it appears as
hrt_dense_large_covolume
; the equivalent finite-family linear-independence formulation appears as
hrt_dense_large_covolume_lindep
. Those names are recorded in Appendix
A
.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

We begin with the lattice-cyclic subspace generated by
f
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

V=\overline{\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\pi(\ell)f:\ell\in L_{0}\}}.

We call
V
the lattice-cyclic subspace of
f
associated with
L_{0}
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Assume, for contradiction, that there exist coefficients
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\mathbb{C}
, not all zero, such that

(1)

c_{0}f+c_{1}\pi(a)f+c_{2}\pi(b)f+c_{3}\pi(\nu)f=0.

If
c_{3}=0
, then (
1
) is a nontrivial linear dependence among the three vectors
f
,
\pi(a)f
, and
\pi(b)f
. Since
0
,
a
, and
b
are distinct and
a,b
are linearly independent, this contradicts the three-point HRT theorem
[
3
]
. Hence
c_{3}\neq 0
.

Because
0,a,b\in L_{0}
, we have

f\in V,\qquad\pi(a)f\in V,\qquad\pi(b)f\in V.

By rearranging (
1
) we obtain

\pi(\nu)f=-c_{3}^{-1}\bigl(c_{0}f+c_{1}\pi(a)f+c_{2}\pi(b)f\bigr)\in V.

Next we claim that
\pi(\nu)V\subseteq V
. It is enough to verify this on the generators
\pi(\ell)f
with
\ell\in L_{0}
. By projective commutation there exists, for each
\ell\in L_{0}
, a nonzero scalar
\kappa(\nu,\ell)
such that

\pi(\nu)\pi(\ell)f=\kappa(\nu,\ell)\,\pi(\ell)\pi(\nu)f.

Since
\pi(\nu)f\in V
and
V
is invariant under every lattice shift
\pi(\ell)
, the right-hand side belongs to
V
. Therefore the left-hand side belongs to
V
as well. By linearity and closure,
\pi(\nu)V\subseteq V
.

It follows that

\pi(\ell+n\nu)V\subseteq V\qquad\text{for all }\ell\in L_{0}\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Because
1,r,s
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, the forward semigroup
L_{0}+\mathbb{N}\nu
is dense in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by Kronecker’s theorem. Let
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
h\in V
. Choose a sequence
z_{k}\in L_{0}+\mathbb{N}\nu
with
z_{k}\to z
. Then
\pi(z_{k})h\in V
for every
k
, and strong continuity of
z\mapsto\pi(z)
implies that
\pi(z_{k})h\to\pi(z)h
. Since
V
is closed, we obtain
\pi(z)h\in V
. Thus

\pi(z)V\subseteq V\qquad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Now
V
is nonzero because
f\in V
and
f\neq 0
. Irreducibility of the Schrödinger representation therefore forces

V=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

On the other hand,
V
is the closed span of the lattice Gabor orbit associated with
L_{0}
. Since

\operatorname{covol}(L_{0})=|\sigma(a,b)|>1,

standard density theory implies that this lattice Gabor orbit is incomplete in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

[
2
,
5
]
. Hence
V\neq L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, a contradiction.

Therefore no nontrivial relation (
1
) can exist, and the four vectors in the statement of the theorem are linearly independent.
∎

4.
The rational-coordinate case

Theorem 4.1

(Rational-coordinate case)
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be linearly independent, let
\nu=ra+sb
, and suppose that
r,s\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then the configuration

\Lambda=\{0,a,b,\nu\}

is contained in a finer full-rank lattice. Consequently, for every nonzero
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the family
\{\pi(\lambda)f:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
is linearly independent.

Proof.

Choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
Nr,Ns\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\nu\in\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}a+\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}b.

Since
0
,
a
, and
b
also lie in the same lattice, we have

\Lambda\subset\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}a+\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}b.

This is a full-rank lattice because
a
and
b
are linearly independent. Linnell’s theorem therefore implies that
\{\pi(\lambda)f:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
is linearly independent for every nonzero
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

[
4
]
.
∎

The formal Lean name of Theorem
4.1
is
hrt_finite_relative_orbit
.

A.5.
Brief note on scope

The present paper proves and records the two certified theorems above. A separate normalization leading to a Bekka-type fiber equation is stored in the formal project, but it is not used in the proof of either theorem stated in the body of the paper.
