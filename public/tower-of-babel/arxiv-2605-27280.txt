On the Faithful Projective Representations of Finite Groups and their Minimal Dimension
Sumana Hatui, Poonam Nayak

Abstract
. The first part of this article is devoted to characterizing the cocycles α \alpha of a finite group G G that give rise to faithful projective representations of G G . We prove that a p p -group G G admits a faithful irreducible projective representation if and only if the cohomology class [ α ] [\alpha] does not lie in the image of the inflation map inf : H 2 ​ ( G / N , ℂ × ) ⟶ H 2 ​ ( G , ℂ × ) \inf:\mathrm{H}^{2}\!\left(G/N,\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}\!\left(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right) for any non-trivial central subgroup N N of G G . In the case where [ α ] ∈ Im ⁡ ( inf ) [\alpha]\in\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{inf}) , we determine a criterion such that a direct sum of irreducible α \alpha -representations is faithful. We conclude this part by describing the behaviour of cocycles α \alpha that yield faithful irreducible representations for direct products of groups. In the second part, we introduce the notion of the projective embedding degree of a finite group G G , defined as the smallest integer n n such that G G embeds into PGL n ​ ( ℂ ) \mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}) ; equivalently, it is the smallest n n such that G G has a faithful complex projective representation of degree n n . We also define the analogous notion of the irreducible projective embedding degree of G G . These invariants have been investigated for several classes of groups, including direct products of groups, finite abelian groups, extra-special p p -groups, Heisenberg groups, and groups of order p 3 p^{3} , p 4 p^{4} (for primes p p ), and p 5 p^{5} (for p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 ).

1.
Introduction

A complex projective representation of a finite group
G
is a homomorphism
\rho\colon G\to\mathrm{PGL}(V)
, where
V
is a finite-dimensional complex vector space. Equivalently, we say a map
\rho:G\to GL(V)
is a projective representation of
G
if there is a map
\alpha\colon G\times G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that

\rho(g)\rho(h)=\alpha(g,h)\rho(gh),g,h\in G\text{ and }\rho(1)=1_{V}.

Then
\alpha
becomes a cocycle of
G
, and we refer
\rho
as an
\alpha
-representation.
Projective representations are a generalization of ordinary representations. The equivalence classes of projective representations of
G
are classified by the elements of the second
cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, known as the Schur multiplier of
G
. It is also denoted by
M(G)
.

A fundamental and long-standing problem in the representation theory of finite groups
G
is to determine the smallest positive integer
n
for which
G
embeds into
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
To address this problem, the embedding degree
\delta(G)
and irreducible embedding degree
\delta_{irr}(G)
of a group
G
were introduced, which are defined as follows. The embedding degree (or the representation dimension),
\delta(G)
, is the minimal dimension of a faithful complex ordinary representation of
G
, and the irreducible embedding degree
\delta_{irr}(G)
is the minimal dimension of a faithful irreducible complex ordinary representation of
G
.
The representation dimension has been studied extensively for finite
p
-groups (see
[
2
,
6
,
26
]
) as well as for finite groups of Lie type (
[
29
,
28
]
).
While questions of faithfulness, irreducibility, and minimal dimension for ordinary representations of finite groups have been extensively studied in
[
8
,
32
,
36
,
37
]
, analogous questions for projective representations have received comparatively little attention.
Existing work on projective representations has
primarily focused on faithfulness and irreducibility
[
3
,
11
,
17
,
33
]
, with further results obtained for specific families of groups, such as metabelian groups
[
34
]
, and finite classical groups in
[
27
,
38
]
.

These earlier studies naturally lead to the problem of determining the smallest positive integer
n
for which a finite group
G
embeds into
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
; equivalently, of deciding when
G
admits a faithful (possibly irreducible) projective representation, and what the minimal possible dimension of such a representation is.
Let
Z^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
denote the set of cocycles of
G
over
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Recall that if
N\lhd G
, the inflation map is defined as follows. For a given
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G/N,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, define
\alpha^{\prime}:G\times G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by
\alpha^{\prime}(x,y)=\alpha(xN,yN)
. Then the assignment
[\alpha]\mapsto[\alpha^{\prime}]
induces a homomorphism
\text{inf}:\text{H}^{2}(G/N,\mathbb{C}^{\times})\to\text{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, called the inflation map.

Our first result appears in Theorem
3.1
, where we show that a finite
p
-group
G
admits a faithful irreducible
\alpha
-representation if and only if the cohomology class

[\alpha]
does not lie in the image of any inflation homomorphism

\mathrm{inf}\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}\!\left(G/N,\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}\!\left(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right),

where
N
ranges over all non-trivial central subgroups of
G
. As a corollary, we obtain corresponding results for non-capable groups (see Corollary
3.2
). Next, in Theorem
3.3
, we determine a criterion when a direct sum of irreducible
\alpha
-representations gives rise to a faithful
\alpha
-representation for
[\alpha]\in\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{inf})
. Furthermore, for direct products of groups
G
with
Z(G)\subseteq[G,G]
, we provide an explicit description of the cocycles
\alpha
that give rise to faithful irreducible
\alpha
-representations in Theorem
3.7
.

Since every finite nilpotent group is isomorphic to the direct product of its Sylow
p
-subgroups, it follows from Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.6
that the study of faithful irreducible projective representations of finite nilpotent groups reduces to the corresponding problem for
p
-groups. Therefore, we restrict our attention to
p
-groups.

For an
\alpha
-representation
\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
of
G
, we define the kernel of
\rho
by
\ker\rho=\{g\in G\mid\rho(g)=\lambda I_{V}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
}. If
\ker\rho=1
, then we say that
\rho
is a faithful projective representation. Note that every finite group admits a faithful complex projective representation, which follows
from the fact that the
\alpha
-regular representation of
G
is always faithful (see
[
22
, Chapter 7, Section 1]
).
Building on this observation, we introduce numerical invariants that capture the smallest possible dimensions of faithful projective representations. We define the
projective embedding degree

\tau(G)
of
G
to be the smallest positive integer
n
for which
G
embeds into
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
Equivalently,

\tau(G):=\min\{\deg(\rho):\rho\text{ is a faithful complex projective representation of }G\},

where
\deg(\rho)
denotes the degree of
\rho
.
The
irreducible projective embedding degree

\tau_{irr}(G)
of
G
is defined as follows:

\tau_{irr}(G):=\min\{\deg(\rho):\rho\text{ is a faithful irreducible complex projective representation of }G\}.

We note that
\tau_{irr}(G)
need not exist for every group
G
. However, whenever it exists, one has
\tau(G)\leq\tau_{irr}(G).

In Section
4
, we establish sufficient conditions under which two groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
satisfy

\tau(G_{1})=\tau(G_{2})

(see Theorem
4.4
). We also study the invariants
\tau(G)
and
\tau_{irr}(G)
for several important classes of finite groups, including direct products of groups, finite abelian groups, extra-special
p
-groups, and Heisenberg groups.
In Section
5
, we present several examples of groups
G
and compute
\tau(G)
and
\tau_{irr}(G)
. We also carry out these computations for non-abelian groups of orders
p^{3}
and
p^{4}
, where
p
is a prime, as well as for groups of order
p^{5}
with
p\geq 5
; see Section
6
. The determination of these invariants is more intricate and requires a number of non-trivial arguments. The proofs make essential use of auxiliary lemmas and techniques developed in Sections
2
,
3
,
4
, and
6
.

Remark 1.1
.

One might expect that there is not much significant difference between
\tau(G)
and
\delta(G)
; however, this is not the case. Our computations show that there is a large class of groups
G
for which
\tau(G)
and
\delta(G)
differ substantially, for example, extra-special
p
-groups, Heisenberg groups, and groups of order
p^{5}
in
\Phi_{7}
. (See Section
4.3
, Theorem
6.8
and
[
26
, Theorem 3.1]
.)

Let us fix some notation here. For
x,y\in G
, the commutator
x^{-1}y^{-1}xy
is denoted by
[x,y]
. The subgroups
G^{\prime}
and
Z(G)
refer to the commutator subgroup and the centre of
G
, respectively. For
x\in G
,
C_{G}(x)
denotes the centralizer of
x
in
G
. The one-dimensional trivial ordinary representation of
G
is denoted by
1_{G}
. We write
cd(G)=\{\deg(\rho)\mid\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)\}
, where
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
denotes the set of all linearly inequivalent irreducible ordinary representations of
G
. If
\psi
is an ordinary representation of
G
, then we write
K_{\psi}=\{x\in G\mid\psi(x)=I\}
. For
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
,
\mathrm{Irr}^{\alpha}(G)
denotes the set of all irreducible
\alpha
-representations of
G
up to linear equivalence. Furthermore, we say that a cocycle
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
is non-trivial if
[\alpha]\neq[1]
.

Throughout this paper, we restrict our attention to finite groups and complex representations.
By the tensor product
G_{1}\otimes G_{2}
of two groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, we always mean the abelian tensor product, i.e.,
G_{1}/G_{1}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}G_{2}/G_{2}^{\prime}
.

Unless stated otherwise, we say
\rho:G\to GL(V)
is faithful on
G
if it is faithful as a projective representation of
G
.

Preliminaries

Definition 2.1

(
\alpha

For a cocycle
\alpha
of
G
, an element

is called an
\alpha
-regular element of

\alpha(g,x)=\alpha(x,g)
for all

. Further, we say that

\alpha
-regular element of

x\in Z(G)
and

is an
\alpha
-regular element of

Lemma 2.2

(
[
22
]
, Chapter 3, Lemma 1.1)
.

Suppose
\alpha_{i}\in Z^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
,
i=1,2
.
Let
\rho_{1}
be an
\alpha_{1}
-representation. If
[\alpha_{1}]=[\alpha_{2}]
, then there exists an
\alpha_{2}
-representation
\rho_{2}
which is projectively equivalent to
\rho_{1}
. In particular, if
\alpha_{1}
is a coboundary, then
\rho_{1}
is projectively equivalent to an ordinary representation.

The next result follows immediately.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
be projective representations that are projectively equivalent. If
\rho_{1}
is faithful, then
\rho_{2}
is also faithful.

In view of the above results, throughout this paper, we consider projective representations associated with a fixed cocycle representative of each cohomology class. In particular, if
\alpha
is a coboundary, then we restrict our attention to ordinary representations (
\alpha=1
).

Lemma 2.4
.

(1)

If
G
is a non-abelian group, then the direct sum of one-dimensional ordinary representations can never be a faithful projective representation.

(2)

A faithful irreducible ordinary representation of
G
is a faithful irreducible projective representation if and only if
Z(G)=1
.

(3)

If
Z(G)=1
, then
\tau(G)\leq\delta(G)
.
Furthermore, if
Z(G)=1
and
M(G)=1
, then
\delta(G)=\tau(G)

and

\tau_{irr}(G)=\delta_{irr}(G)
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose
\rho=\oplus_{k=1}^{n}\rho_{i}
, where
\rho_{i}s
are one-dimensional ordinary representations of
G
.
For
x\in G^{\prime}
,
\rho_{i}(x)=1
and so

\rho(x)=I_{n\times n}
. Hence,
\rho
cannot be faithful.

(2) Let
\psi
be a faithful irreducible ordinary representation of
G
. Then, by
[
18
, Chapter 2, Lemma 2.27, (f)]
,
\{g
|
\psi(g)=\lambda I
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\}=Z(G)
.

(3) Let
\psi
be a faithful ordinary representation of
G
. Suppose
\psi(a)=\lambda I
for some
\lambda

\in

\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
a\in G
. Then,
\psi(a)\psi(g)=\psi(g)\psi(a)

\forall g\in G
, which implies
\psi(a^{-1}g^{-1}ag)=I
. Since
\psi
is a faithful ordinary representation,
[a,g]=1
for all
g\in G
.
Hence,
a

\in

Z(G)
and
\psi
is a faithful projective representation. Thus,
\tau(G)\leq\delta(G)
. Furthermore, if
Z(G)=1
and
M(G)=1
, the result is obtained using
(2)
and the argument above.

Corollary 2.14

Let

If

\rho|_{A}

Our first main result provides a cohomological characterization of the existence of faithful irreducible projective representations.

Theorem 3.1

A p-group

\in

4.2.

In this section, we determine

Recall that the rank of a group

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian
p
-group of rank
n
with
|G|>1
. Suppose
\rho=\oplus_{i=1}^{r}\rho_{i}
, where
\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
. If
1\leq r\leq n
, then
\rho
is not a faithful projective representation of
G
.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward for
n=1
. So, we assume
n>1
. First, we consider
G=(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. Let
e_{k}=\underbrace{(0,0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0)}_{\text{$1$ in the $k{\text{th}}$ position}}\in G
for
1\leq k\leq n
.

We assume that
r=n
.
Suppose
\rho
is a faithful projective representation of
G
. Then, for each
k
, there exists
j_{lk}\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
(
1\leq l\leq n
) such that all
j_{lk}
’s are not equal and

\rho(e_{k})=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}e^{\frac{2\pi ij_{1k}}{p}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&e^{\frac{2\pi ij_{nk}}{p}}\end{array}\right]

is a diagonal matrix. Hence,

\rho\left(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\right)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}e^{\frac{2\pi i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}m_{k}j_{1k}}{p}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&e^{\frac{2\pi i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}m_{k}j_{nk}}{p}}\end{array}\right].

Since
\rho
is also a faithful ordinary representation,
\rho\left(m_{1},m_{2}\ldots,m_{n}\right)=I
implies that
\left(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\right)=(0,0,\ldots,0)
, which says

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{11}&\cdots&j_{1n}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
j_{n1}&\cdots&j_{nn}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
{x}_{n}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{array}\right]

has only a trivial solution in
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Then, the matrix
A=(j_{lk})_{n\times n}
is invertible,
and for
b=[1,1,\cdots,1]^{\top}
,
Ax=b
has a unique solution
c=A^{-1}b,
where
c\neq 0
.
Let
c^{\top}=\left[c_{1},c_{2},\ldots.,c_{n}\right]
,

c_{i}

\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
.
Then,
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}j_{ik}c_{k}=1
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Therefore,

\rho\left(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n}\right)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}e\frac{2\pi i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}j_{1k}}{p}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&e\frac{2\pi i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}j_{nk}}{p}\end{array}\right]=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p}}I_{n\times n},

which contradicts our assumption. Hence,
\rho
cannot be faithful.

If
r<n
, let
\psi=\rho\oplus(\oplus_{i=1}^{n-r}1_{G}
). If
\rho
is faithful as projective, then
\psi
is also faithful as projective, which gives a contradiction to Case 1. Hence, the result holds if
G
is elementary abelian.
Now, let
G
be any abelian group of rank
n
and
H=(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{n}\subseteq G
. If
\rho
is faithful on
G
, then it is also faithful on
H
, which is not possible. This completes the proof.

∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let

be a finite abelian
p
-group of rank

and

. Then,

Proof.

Since
\delta(G)=n
, by Lemma
4.1
, we obtain that
\tau(G)\leq n+1
.
Let
\rho
be a faithful
\alpha
-representation of

of degree

, where

If

\rho=\oplus_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{i}

. Since

are one-dimensional,

is a coboundary. Therefore, we can assume that

is an ordinary representation. However, by Lemma

,

∎

We first recall the following part from

\alpha^{\prime}(x,y)={\alpha(x,y)}{\alpha(y,x)^{-1}}

\in G

is independent of the choice of cocycle representative in

. Suppose that

has order

. Let
\omega
be a primitive

th root of unity. Then, for all

, we can write

We define the alternating

over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
representing
[\alpha]
. Then, the map

is an isomorphism from
\mathrm{H^{2}}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
onto the group
R(p^{k},n)
of all alternating
n\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
, under matrix addition.
Let
x=x_{1}^{b_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{b_{n}}\in G
. Then,
x
is an
\alpha
-regular element if and only if
C(\alpha)b=0
, where
b^{\top}=[b_{1},...,b_{n}]
.
Furthermore, if
k=1
, then
\operatorname{rank}\big(C(\alpha)\big)=2r
,
where
p^{r}
is the common degree of all irreducible
\alpha
-representations of
G
.

Remark 4.12
.

Note that
|\operatorname{ker}\big(C(\alpha)\big)|=|G_{0}|
.

In the following result, we determine

and

Theorem 4.13
.

Let

be an extra-special

does not exist and

\tau(G)=\begin{cases}2p^{n/2},&\text{if n is even }\\

By
[
22

-groups are central products of their subgroups of order

. Hence, by Lemma
2.18
and Corollary

does not exist.

From the proof of Lemma
2.18
, it follows that
\mathrm{inf}:\mathrm{H}^{2}(G/Z(G),\mathbb{C}^{\times})\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
is surjective. Let

Recall the transgression map

\mathrm{tra}:\mathrm{Hom}(Z(G),\mathbb{C}^{\times})\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(G/Z(G),\mathbb{C}^{\times})

from
[

. Let
\omega
be a primitive

th root of unity. Then

for some

.
Hence,

-rI_{n\times n}&0\end{bmatrix}
.
It is easy to see that
\text{rank}\big(C(\mathrm{tra}(\chi)\big)=2n

.

Let
\tau(G)=m
and
\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}^{\alpha}(G)

\alpha
-representation of

3.3

\tilde{\rho}_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}^{\beta\mathrm{tra}(\chi_{i})}\big(G/Z(G)\big)

.
Furthermore, we can assume that

for

Since

∎

Theorem 4.14

\psi(\bar{z})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\chi(\bar{z})\chi(z_{2})^{-p^{r}}&&&\text{\huge 0}\\

\Gamma_{i}=\operatorname{Ind}^{G^{*}}_{{I_{G^{*}}(\chi_{i})}}(\psi_{i})

\tilde{\rho}_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}^{\beta\mathrm{tra}(\chi_{i})}\big(G/Z(G)\big)

5.
Examples

In this section, we discuss several examples illustrating
\tau(G)
and
\tau_{irr}(G)
for certain classes of groups

, particularly metacyclic groups, finite simple groups and symmetric groups.
We begin by recalling the following facts, which will be useful in discussing the examples.

(1)

If

or

, then
\delta(G)=2
by
[
26

, where

and

denote the Dihedral group of order

and the Quaternion group of order

(2)

If

or

, then
\delta(G)=\delta_{irr}(G)=n-1
(
[
26
, Example 2.4, 2.5, 2.6]
).

(3)

For

,

by
[
22
, Chapter 4, Theorem 8.3(i)]
.

(4)

For
[\alpha]\neq 1
, every
\alpha
-representation has even degree by
[
25
, Chapter 5, Theorem 5.3]
.

(5)

A metacyclic
p
-group

,

, has a faithful irreducible projective representation if and only if
G
is of symmetric type, i.e.,

can be written as a semidirect product of two isomorphic cyclic subgroups (see
[
34
, Theorem 5.2]
).

Example 5.1.

By
[

Theorem 6.2
.

Let

be a non-abelian
p
-group with cyclic centre. If

or

.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
B_{G}=\{A.G\mid A\cong Z(A.G)\text{ and }\delta_{irr}(A.G)\text{ exists}\}
. Then
\tau_{irr}(G)
exists if and only if

, in which case
\tau_{irr}(G)=\min\{\delta_{irr}(A.G)\mid A.G\in B_{G}\}
.

.

Proof.

Let

be a faithful projective representation of
G
and
\rho^{*}
be a lift of
\rho
in
G^{*}
. Suppose

. It is easy to see that

induces a faithful ordinary representation of

.
Suppose

exists. By Theorem

,
B_{G}\neq\emptyset
. Let

. Since

is cyclic due to

, Theorem 2.32(a)]
. Since

and

, we have

. Hence,

.

H
be a covering of

of order

. By Theorem

\tau_{irr}(G)
exists.

, it follows that
