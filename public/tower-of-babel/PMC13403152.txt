Modification of the existing maximum residue levels for acequinocyl in celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarb
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Marta Szot, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the applicant Agro‐Kanesho submitted a request to the competent national authority in Germany to modify the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for the active substance acequinocyl in celeries, Florence fennels and rhubarbs. The data submitted in support of the request was found to be sufficient to derive MRL proposals for all crops under consideration. Adequate analytical methods for enforcement are available to control the residues of acequinocyl in plant matrices under consideration at the validated limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg mg/kg. Although uncertainties remain due to the data gaps identified in the review of existing EU MRLs according to Article 12 of the above‐mentioned Regulation, the indicative exposure calculation did not present a risk to consumer's health. Based on these calculations, EFSA concluded that the proposed use of acequinocyl on celery, Florence fennel and rhubarb is acceptable with regard to consumer exposure.

SUMMARY

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, Agro‐Kanesho submitted an application to the competent national authority in Germany (evaluating Member State, EMS) to modify the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for the active substance acequinocyl in celeries, Florence fennels and rhubarbs.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) Central Submission System on 13 September 2024. The appointed EMS Germany assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 7 May 2025. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 26 May 2025 to 16 June 2026. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the EMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) on 9 February 2026. To accommodate for the intended NEU use of acequinocyl, the EMS proposed to raise the existing MRLs from the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg to 3 mg/kg for celeries, Florence fennels and rhubarbs.

On 2 March 2026 the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

EFSA identified data gaps which needed further clarifications and requested the EMS to address them. On 26 May 2026 the EMS submitted the requested information in a revised evaluation report, which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

Based on the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC, the data evaluated under previous MRL assessments, including the review of the existing MRLs for acequinocyl and the additional data provided by the EMS in the framework of this application, the following conclusions are derived.

The metabolism of acequinocyl following foliar and soil application was investigated in crops belonging to the group of fruit crops. Based on the available data, the plant residue definition for monitoring and risk assessment, limited to the fruit crop group, honey and to hops only, was set as ‘acequinocyl’.

With the present MRL application, the applicant submitted a new metabolism study on hops to address the metabolic pathway of acequinocyl in leafy crops. The radioactivity was determined in dried hop cones (the inflorescence) only; no data in leaves are available. The major radioactive fraction in dried hop cones was identified as parent acequinocyl with minor fractions identified as acequinocyl‐OH (or AKM‐05 or R1) and phthalic acid. The study did not indicate that the metabolism in hop cones proceeds in a different way than in fruit crops.

Although hops are one of the representatives of leafy crop group, the submitted metabolism study only investigated dried hop cones and did not provide information on residue composition in leaves, furthermore it used a single radiolabel position. Therefore, EFSA is of the opinion that the submitted study alone may not be sufficient to fully characterise acequinocyl metabolism across the entire leafy crop group as proposed by the EMS. However, for the intended uses of celery, Florence fennel and rhubarbs, which are classified within the leafy crop group, but whose edible parts are primarily the stalks (celery and rhubarb) and the enlarged leaf‐base pseudobulb (Florence fennel), the metabolism of acequinocyl can be considered investigated and further data are not required at this stage. The existing risk assessment and enforcement residue definition ‘acequinocyl’ can be applied to these three stem crops, but this conclusion, as well as any broader extrapolation of the residue definitions and the potential need for additional metabolism studies on leafy crops, should be further considered in the context of the renewal of the approval of the active substance.

Studies investigating the nature or magnitude of acequinocyl in rotational crops are not required, due to low persistence of acequinocyl in soil.

Specific studies investigating the nature and magnitude of acequinocyl residues in processed commodities are not required, as the individual contribution of crops under consideration to the total theoretical maximum daily intake (TMDI) is below the trigger value of 10% of the acceptable daily intake (ADI). Nevertheless, a standard hydrolysis study was assessed in a previous EFSA opinion. Acequinocyl showed to be stable under pasteurisation conditions and progressively degraded to acequinocyl‐OH and AKM −18 under processing conditions representative of baking, brewing and boiling, and sterilisation. The study was conducted with a single radiolabel position and part of the radioactivity observed under baking/brewing/boiling conditions was not fully characterised. Considering the deficiencies observed in the hydrolysis studies, the data gap regarding the nature of residues in processed commodities identified in the MRL review for fruit crops and hops is still pending and should be addressed in the framework of the Art.12 confirmatory data assessment or in the context of the renewal of the approval of the active substance.

Sufficiently validated analytical methods based on high performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS/MS) are available to quantify residues in high‐water (celery, fennel) and high‐acid (rhubarb) content commodities according to the enforcement residue definition with the validated LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

The available residue trials are sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 3 mg/kg in celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarbs in support of the intended NEU use.

Residues of acequinocyl in commodities of animal origin were not assessed since the commodities under consideration in this MRL application are normally not fed to livestock.

The toxicological profile of acequinocyl was assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC and the data were sufficient to derive an ADI of 0.023 mg/kg bw per day and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.08 mg/kg bw.

The consumer risk assessment was performed with revision 3.1 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo). This exposure calculation includes also commodities for which the MRL review confirmatory data gaps have so far not been addressed and therefore this exposure calculation shall be considered indicative. The chronic consumer exposure performed in a most recent (not yet endorsed) EFSA reasoned opinion on the modification of existing MRLs for acequinocyl in cane fruits and other small fruits and berries, was updated with the median risk assessment values as derived from celery trials for the crops under consideration. The short‐term exposure assessment was performed for the crops of interest and calculations were based on the highest residue (HR) derived from supervised field trials.

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the ARfD for any of the commodities assessed in this application and accounted for 47% of the ARfD for celeries and rhubarbs (both for childrens' diets) and for 23% of the ARfD for Florence fennels (adult's diets). The highest estimated long‐term dietary intake was 9% of the ADI (NL toddler diet).

Although uncertainties remain due to the data gaps identified in the MRL review, this indicative exposure calculation did not identify a risk to consumer's health. Based on these calculations, EFSA concluded that the proposed use of acequinocyl on celery, Florence fennel and rhubarb is acceptable with regard to consumer exposure.

As the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval of acequinocyl in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 is not started yet but it is expected to supposed to start soon, and it will also assess the Art.12 confirmatory data, the conclusions reported in this reasoned opinion may need to be reconsidered in the light of the outcome of the peer review and the Art.12 confirmatory data.

ASSESSMENT

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) received an application from Agro‐Kanesho (Kabushiki Kaisha European Branch) to modify the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for acequinocyl in celeries, Florence fennels and rhubarbs. The detailed description of the intended NEU use of acequinocyl, which is the basis for the current MRL application, is reported in Appendix A.

The chemical structures of the active substance and its main metabolites are reported in Appendix E.

Acequinocyl was evaluated in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC 1 in conjunction with Regulation (EU) No 188/2011 2 with the Netherlands designated as rapporteur Member State (RMS) for the representative use as field and greenhouse foliar applications on ornamentals, apples and pears. The draft assessment report (DAR) prepared by the RMS has been peer reviewed by EFSA (EFSA, 2013). Acequinocyl was approved 3 for the use as an acaricide on 1 September 2014. The approval of acequinocyl expires on 15 November 2026 and the renewal of approval process is expected to initiate soon.

The European Union (EU) MRLs for acequinocyl are established in Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. 4 Since the EU pesticides peer review, EFSA has issued a number of reasoned opinions on the modification of MRLs for acequinocyl, including the review of existing MRLs according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (MRL review) in 2020. Following the MRL review, EFSA finalised four opinions on acequinocyl, respectively in sweet peppers/bell peppers (EFSA, 2022), strawberries (EFSA, 2025a), various crops (EFSA, 2025b) and small berries (EFSA, 2026). The proposals from these reasoned opinions have been considered in recent MRL regulations, 5 apart from those from the latest opinions in various crops and small berries (EFSA, 2025b, 2026). EFSA included the conclusions of these latter opinions in the present assessment. The assessment of Article 12 confirmatory data will be undertaken together with the peer review on the renewal of approval.

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

The metabolism of acequinocyl has been investigated in fruit crops, following foliar (apples, aubergines and oranges) and soil (aubergines) treatment, and assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review and the MRL review (EFSA, 2013, 2020).

In the studies conducted with apples and aubergines (both foliar and soil), acequinocyl was radiolabelled in the phenyl ring or the dodecyl chain of the molecule, while in oranges, it was labelled only in the phenyl ring. Results from the different studies were comparable.

In investigated crops the metabolic pathway was seen to be similar and proceeds through the hydrolysis of the acetic acid ester to the metabolite acequinocyl‐OH (named as R1 in the peer review and also as AKM‐05), followed by the opening of the naphthalenedione ring leading to the metabolite AKM − 18 and further hydrolysis of the dodecyl aliphatic chain, resulting in the formation of phthalic acid (EFSA, 2013).

Acequinocyl was the major component identified in the total radioactive residues (TRR) of all the studies, accounting for 28% (0.06 mg eq/kg) to 41% TRR (0.11 mg eq/kg) in apple and orange fruits 30 days after treatment (DAT), and for 55% TRR (0.01 mg eq/kg) in aubergines, 14 DAT. The other metabolites identified, namely acequinocyl‐OH (metabolite R1/AKM‐05), AKM − 18 and phthalic acid represented less than 10% of the TRR. In leaves, at harvest, acequinocyl was also the most prominent component identified, representing up to 58% TRR (1.14 mg eq/kg) (EFSA, 2020).

In the MRL review, an authorised use of acequinocyl on hops (leafy crop) was reported. No additional metabolism information was requested as it was concluded that the nature of residues in hops is addressed by the available metabolism data in fruit crop leaves, where a similar metabolic fate as in fruits was observed (EFSA, 2020).

With the present MRL application, the applicant submitted a new metabolism study on hops which was assessed by the EMS (Germany, 2026). The study was carried out in 2021 and is Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) compliant. Hops, grown under protected outdoor conditions, were treated once by foliar application with acequinocyl labelled in phenyl ring at an application rate of 328 g/ha. The growth stage of hops at the time of the application was BBCH 75, corresponding to cone development half‐way. Hop cone (the inflorescence) samples were taken 21 days (BBCH 89) after the treatment (DAT); leave samples were taken 5 (BBCH 75) and 21 days (BBCH 89) after application but, apart from determination of total radioactivity in the sample of 5 day pre‐harvest interval (PHI), they were not analysed further. Collected cones were cured for 5 days at room temperature.

The total radioactive residue (TRR) in the hop sample was determined by initial combustion, and after further analysis (extraction with acidified acetonitrile, acidified acetonitrile/water mixtures and water). The total radioactive residues in dried cones accounted for 26.99 mg eq/kg 21 DAT and in leaves for 27 mg eq/kg 5 DAT. The major radioactive fraction in dried hop cones was identified as parent acequinocyl with 84% of the TRR (21.4 mg/kg). Two of the minor fractions were identified as the acequinocyl‐ OH (AKM‐05/R1) at 2.3% TRR (0.58 mg/kg) and phthalic acid at 1% TRR (0.25 mg/kg). Six additional minor radioactive components, none of which was present above 1.7% TRR (or above 0.44 mg/kg) were detected. Overall, 87.3% of the TRR was identified.

The extraction efficiency of the metabolism study (for dried hop cones) was tested for two separate extraction methods: method A using acetonitrile/water (60/40, v/v) mixture and method B, using acidified acetonitrile. Samples were analysed using HPLC–MS/MS. Method B demonstrated the highest extraction efficiency with 93.5% of total (acequinocyl and AKM‐05) extracted while, according to method A, the extraction rate reached for these residues was 75.1%.

The metabolism study did not identify in dried cones any components that have not been identified previously in the metabolism studies with fruit crops. The metabolic pathway involves deacetylation of the parent forming acequinocyl‐OH which is further metabolised, including the loss of the dodecyl chain and the opening of the quinone ring. These findings in dried cones are in line with the available data in fruit crop leaves where acequinocyl was the predominant residue at the PHI investigated (14 days in aubergines and 30 days in apples and oranges). For the intended use on celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarbs, the PHI interval is shorter (3 days) than the PHI interval (21 day) investigated in the metabolism study with hops. At shorter PHIs acequinocyl is expected to be metabolised to a lesser extent, thus remaining the main residue.

It is noted that in the hop metabolism study acequinocyl was radiolabelled at the phenyl ring and a second labelling position (e.g. the dodecyl label) was not investigated. Upon EFSA's request, the EMS justifies – and it is indeed confirmed by data – that in the metabolism studies with apples and aubergines where both radiolabelling positions were investigated, all identified metabolites still contained both the phenyl ring and at least one part of the annealed naphthoquinone containing the dodecyl position. Therefore, the EMS is of the opinion that radiolabelling at dodecyl position would not provide additional information and the use of only the phenyl‐label alone is sufficient to elucidate metabolic pathway in hops.

EFSA notes that the absence of significant differences between the radiolabelling positions has only been demonstrated in the fruit crop group (the only metabolism group previously investigated). While a similar behaviour may reasonably be expected in other crop groups, no experimental data on other crop groups are currently available to substantiate this assumption. In this context, it is noted that, in the livestock metabolism study conducted in goats, acequinocyl was radiolabelled in the phenyl ring only and EFSA considered the available information to be sufficient (EFSA, 2020).

On the basis of available studies in fruit crops and hops, the EMS concluded that the main residue in investigated crop groups is acequinocyl and proposes to extrapolate this conclusion to the entire leafy crop category. Although, according to applicable guidance document, hop is one of representative crops of the leafy crop category and the stem vegetables under assessment belong to this group (European Commission, 1997a), the metabolism study was analysing for residues only in dried hop cones (the inflorescence), while no information is available on residue composition in leaves. Therefore, EFSA is of the opinion that data on dried hop cones alone cannot be considered fully sufficient to conclude on the metabolism of acequinocyl across the entire leafy crop group.

For the intended uses on celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarbs, whose edible parts are primarily the stalks (celery and rhubarb) and the enlarged leaf‐base pseudobulb (Florence fennel), EFSA concludes that based on the residue behaviour observed in fruit leaves and dried hop cones, the metabolism of acequinocyl can be considered investigated and that no further data are required at this stage. Nevertheless, the representativeness of the submitted hop metabolism study to address the metabolism of acequinocyl in the whole leafy crop category, should be further considered in the broader context of the renewal of the approval of the active substance.

Nature of residues in rotational crops

Celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarbs can be grown in rotation with other crops. However, the field DT90 reported in the soil degradation studies evaluated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review was 21 and 24 h for parent acequinocyl and metabolite acequinocyl‐OH, respectively. For metabolite AKM − 18 only laboratory data were available, indicating a DT90 of 12 days (EFSA, 2013). Therefore, considering low persistence of acequinocyl in soil, studies investigating the nature of acequinocyl in rotational crops are not required.

Nature of residues in processed commodities

Specific studies to assess the impact of processing on the nature of acequinocyl residues (standard hydrolysis studies) and the magnitude of residues (processing studies with celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarb) are not necessary as the individual total theoretical maximum daily intake (TMDI) of these raw commodities amounts to less than 10% of the ADI (European Commission, 1997d).

In the framework of the previous MRL application on sweet peppers/bell peppers, a standard hydrolysis study was provided and assessed (EFSA, 2022). The results of this study indicated that acequinocyl was stable under pasteurisation conditions and remained the predominant residue under the other hydrolysis conditions tested. The main degradation products under baking/brewing/boiling and sterilisation conditions were acequinocyl‐OH (AKM‐05/R1) and AKM − 18, both occurring at levels above 10% AR. These compounds were also observed in primary crop metabolism. The study was conducted with a single radiolabel position and part of the radioactivity observed under baking/brewing/boiling conditions was not fully characterised. While a study with a second radiolabel would be useful to fully elucidate the potential degradation of the side chain under hydrolysis conditions and to confirm the currently available findings, EFSA considered the hydrolysis study provisionally sufficient for the assessment of the residues in processed pepper and strawberry products. However, the data gap identified in the MRL review regarding the nature of residues in processed commodities was maintained (EFSA, 2022, 2025a) and shall be considered in the framework of the of Art.12 confirmatory data assessment or in the context of the renewal of the approval of the active substance.

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

The availability of analytical enforcement methods for the determination of acequinocyl residues in plant commodities was investigated in the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2013) and in the MRL review (EFSA, 2020).

Overall, a hyphenated analytical method based on HPLC–MS/MS was validated for the determination of parent acequinocyl and metabolite acequinocyl‐OH 10 in high‐acid and high‐water content commodities, with an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg, for each analyte (EFSA, 2013). Additionally, an HPLC‐MS/MS method for parent acequinocyl in high‐acid and dry commodities was made available in the MRL review with the reported LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. In high‐oil commodities the validation was not successful. For the determination of parent acequinocyl and metabolite acequinocyl‐OH in hops (difficult matrix), a validated HPLC‐MS/MS method is available with an LOQ of 0.1 mg/kg for each analyte (EFSA, 2020).

A sufficiently validated quick, easy, cheap, effective, rugged, and safe (QuEChERS) method for enforcement using HPLC–MS/MS with two mass transitions was submitted within the previous MRL application on acequinocyl in strawberries (EFSA, 2025a). The method allows quantification of residues in high‐water, high‐acid matrices at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg and in hops at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg. Extraction performed with acetonitrile was equivalent to the extraction used in the fruit crop metabolism study, where > 70% of the TRR was extracted. Extraction efficiency was proven by cross‐validation in high‐water and high‐acid matrices (EFSA, 2025a).

For the crops under consideration, which belong to the high‐water (celeries and Florence fennel) and high‐acid (rhubarbs) matrix group, EFSA concludes that sufficiently validated analytical methods are available for the determination of acequinocyl residues at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

Storage stability of residues in plants

The storage stability of acequinocyl and acequinocyl‐OH residues in high‐water content (apples) and high‐acid content (mandarins, oranges, strawberries) matrices has been investigated in the EU pesticides peer review and in the recent EFSA assessments (EFSA, 2013, 2020, 2025a). It was concluded that both compounds are stable in high‐water content matrices for 18 months and in high‐acid content matrices for 12 months when stored deep frozen at −18°C. An overview of the available storage stability data is presented in Table B.1.1.2 in the Appendix.

Proposed residue definitions

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in the metabolism studies, the toxicological significance of acequinocyl and its metabolites, the following residue definitions were proposed by the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2013) and confirmed in the MRL review (EFSA, 2020): residue definition for risk assessment (raw agricultural commodity (RAC)): acequinocylresidue definition for enforcement (RAC): acequinocyl

residue definition for risk assessment (raw agricultural commodity (RAC)): acequinocyl

residue definition for risk assessment (raw agricultural commodity (RAC)): acequinocyl

residue definition for enforcement (RAC): acequinocyl

residue definition for enforcement (RAC): acequinocyl

The residue definition for enforcement set in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 is identical with the above‐mentioned enforcement residue definition.

The enforcement and risk assessment residue definitions are limited to fruit crops and applicable also to hops (EFSA, 2020) and honey (EFSA, 2026). Based on the new metabolism study submitted under the present assessment (see Section 1.1.1), EFSA confirms their applicability on hops and concludes that the existing risk assessment and enforcement residue definitions can be extended to the stem crops under consideration (celeries, fennel, rhubarbs).

This conclusion as well as any broader extrapolation of the residue definitions and the potential need for additional metabolism studies on leafy crops, should be further considered in the broader context of the renewal of the approval of the active substance. Depending on the outcome of the renewal of approval process, new metabolism studies on leafy crop might be required.

For processed commodities, the residue definition for enforcement and risk assessment was tentatively set as ‘acequinocyl and acequinocyl‐OH, expressed as acequinocyl’ (EFSA, 2022). A final conclusion on the residue definitions for processed products should be reached in the framework of the Art.12 confirmatory data process or the renewal of the active substance, taking into account the deficiencies reported for the hydrolysis studies (see Section 1.1.3).

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

In support of the intended NEU use requested with the present MRL application, the applicant provided data from 4 residue trials on celeries which were performed under field conditions in three various locations in Germany in 2023. Considering that all crops under consideration are minor crops, the lack of having trials carried out over two growing seasons is considered minor deviation and accepted. All submitted residue trials were GAP compliant.

In line with applicable data requirements, half of residue trials were designed as decline trials with samples taken on day 0 and 1, 3 and 7 days after the treatment. The decline trials confirm that the highest acequinocyl residues are expected at the intended PHI of 3 days.

No residues were found in untreated control samples. Residues of acequinocyl in treated celery samples were within the range of 0.24–1.0 mg/kg.

The samples were analysed for parent acequinocyl using the QuEChERS method with HPLC–MS/MS where residue extraction was performed using acetonitrile and buffer salt mixture. The extract was subsequently diluted with acetonitrile/water (8/2, v/v) containing 0.2% formic acid prior to analysis. It is noted that the QuEChERS method using acetonitrile extraction is used for enforcement purposes and the extraction efficiency of that method has been cross‐validated with the metabolism study on fruit crops where acetonitrile was used as extraction agent (EFSA, 2025a). For the present MRL application, the EMS did not assess whether the extraction efficiency of the method used to analyse residue trial samples is demonstrated and the impact of formic acid that was added during extraction, remains unclear and adds uncertainty, while not affecting the outcome of the assessment. The final conclusion of the extraction efficiency of enforcement method still should be reconsidered in the framework of the peer review on the renewal of approval.

Validation data were provided and confirm that method of analysis is sufficiently validated and it fits for purpose (Germany, 2026). The validated LOQ is 0.01 mg/kg. Prior to analysis, the samples were stored frozen for 7–10 months. Considering that in high‐water content matrices acequinocyl is stable for up to 18 months, the provided residue trial data are considered valid.

The applicant proposes to extrapolate the residue data from celeries to Florence fennel and rhubarbs and such extrapolation is considered acceptable according to EU guidance (European Commission, 2023b).

The submitted data are sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 3 mg/kg for acequinocyl in celeries Florence fennel and rhubarbs in support of the intended NEU use.

Magnitude of residues in rotational crops

Considering the low persistence of acequinocyl in soil, residues in rotational crops are not expected to exceed 0.01 mg/kg.

Magnitude of residues in processed commodities

Specific processing studies with the crops under consideration have not been submitted. The EMS noted that the individual contribution of residues in celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarbs is significantly below 10% of the ADI and processing studies are therefore not required (European Commission, 1997d).

This argument is acceptable according to the applicable data requirements and therefore further processing studies were not required for the crops under consideration.

Proposed MRLs

The available data are considered sufficient to derive MRL proposals as well as risk assessment values for celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarbs. In Section 3 EFSA assessed whether residues in these crops resulting from the intended uses are likely to pose a consumer health risk.

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK AND FISH

Not relevant as crops under consideration are not used for feed purposes.

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA performed a dietary risk assessment using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018, 2019). This exposure assessment model contains food consumption data for different sub‐groups of the EU population and allows the acute and chronic exposure assessment to be performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016).

The health based guidance values (HBGVs) for acequinocyl used in the risk assessment (i.e. (ARfD) 0.08 mg/kg body weight (bw) and acceptable daily intake (ADI) 0.023 mg/kg bw per day) were derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (European Commission, 2014).

Short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment

The short‐term exposure assessment was performed for the commodities assessed in this application. The calculations were based on the highest residue (HR) derived from supervised field trials on celeries, extrapolated to Florence fennel and rhubarbs. The complete list of input values can be found in Appendix D.1.

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the ARfD for any of the commodities assessed in this application and accounted for 47% of the ARfD for celeries and rhubarbs (both for childrens' diets) and for 23% of the ARfD for Florence fennels (adults' diets).

Long‐term (chronic) dietary risk assessment

In the framework of the MRL review a comprehensive long‐term exposure assessment was performed, taking into account the existing uses at EU level which were subsequently updated by an application on peppers and a fast‐track procedure on kaki/Japanese persimmons (EFSA, 2020, 2022; European Commission, 2023a, 2023b, 2023c, 11 ). EFSA notes that the exposure calculation took into consideration data from the latest EFSA reasoned opinions (EFSA, 2025b, 2026) despite that MRLs evaluated therein have so far not been formally implemented in Regulation.

EFSA now updated the latest consumer exposure calculation, performed within the assessment of the MRL application on modification of the existing MRLs in cane fruits and other small berries (EFSA, 2026, not implemented yet) with the relevant median residue (STMR) value derived from the supervised residue trials on celeries, extrapolated to Florence fennel and rhubarbs, submitted in support of this MRL application. The contributions of commodities where no GAP was reported in the framework of the MRL review or in the subsequent EFSA outputs were not included in the calculation. This exposure calculation includes also commodities for which the MRL review confirmatory data gaps have so far not been addressed and therefore this exposure calculation shall be considered indicative. The input values used in the exposure calculations are summarised in Appendix D.1.

The highest estimated long‐term dietary intake was 9% of the ADI (NL toddler diet). The highest calculated contribution of residues in the crops under consideration to the overall long‐term exposure was from celeries, accounting for 1.08% of the ADI (GEMS/Food G11).

Although uncertainties remain due to the data gaps identified in the review of existing MRLs (EFSA, 2020), this indicative exposure calculation did not indicate a risk to consumer's health. Based on these calculations, EFSA concluded that the proposed use of acequinocyl on celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarbs is acceptable regarding consumer exposure.

For further details on the exposure calculations, a screenshot of the Report sheet of the PRIMo is presented in Appendix C.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The data submitted in support of this MRL application were found to be sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 3 mg/kg for celeries, Florence fennel and rhubarbs.

Although uncertainties remain due to the data gaps identified in the MRL review, based on the indicative risk assessment results, EFSA concluded that the proposed use of acequinocyl on the crops under consideration will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the HBGVs and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health.

As the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval of acequinocyl in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 is supposed to start soon and will also assess the Art.12 confirmatory data for acequinocyl, the conclusions reported in this reasoned opinion may need to be reconsidered in the light of the outcome of the peer review and the Art.12 confirmatory data review.

The MRL recommendations are summarised in Appendix B.4.
