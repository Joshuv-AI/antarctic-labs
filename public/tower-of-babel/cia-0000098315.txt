1. It has been learned foe Reapers: a California based
monthly magazine, is planning to publish in its March issue
an expose of CIA involvement with the*National Student Asso-
ciation and with a number of foundations which are either
Agency owned organizations or cooperate extensively with the
Agency. The Ramparts article will also probably mention CIA
involvement with a number of other domestic private voluntary
organizations, as well as with two or more international
organizations in the youth & student field. Although the _
article will probably contain some false information, it will
almost certainly accurately identify a number of ostensibly
private foundations as funding channels used by the Agency
in covert operations, and name a number of Agency employees
who have been involved with clandestine youth and student

operations.

3. CIA has cooperated with and subsidized a number of
domestic and international voluntary organizations in the
youth and student field since the early 1950's. Assistance
was given because the activities of these organizations
served to counter the influence and propaganda of communist
front groups in the youth and student field. Communist sub-
versive activity was mainly directed at the youth of Asia,
Africa and Latin America, and it was felt that in order to
effectively present the democratic point of view in these
areas it was necessary to work through non-governmental channels.

; 4, CIA assistance to these pro-Western organizations was
given at the request of the responsible officers of the groups
involved. Their cooperation with CIA has always been wholly
voluntary, and no effort was ever made by CIA to improperly
influence their policy or their domestic activities. The
subsidies they received from CIA were used only in international
activities, and only in cases where assistance was necessary
if the organizations were to be able to counter effectively
the efforts of the international communist front groups.

5. It is considered likely that the Ramparts article will
be picked up by respected domestic newspapers, such as the
New York Times and the Washington Post, and that there will
be an extended public controversy over CIA involvement with
private voluntary organizations and foundations. The CIA
will probably be accused of improperly interfering in domestic
affairs, and of manipulating and endangering innocent young
people. The Administration will probably come under attack
for permitting this kind of activity. It is probable that
the State Department and the White House will be asked to
make statements about both CIA involvement with private
voluntary organizations and about the use of private founda-
tions as channels for clandestine funds.

6. The following people have been briefed on the problem
posed by the Ramparts article:

Ambassador Kohler and Mr. Hughes of the State
Department

Vice-President Humphrey and members of his staff
Mr. Cater and Mr. McPherson of the White House staff
Undersecretary Vance of the Defense Department

Senator Fulbright

7. CIA is in consultation with the current officers of
the National Student Association as to the kind of statement
they should issue before or after the appearance of the
article. At present, it appears to CIA that the detail and
accuracy of the article will be such that they will be obliged
to make a partial admission, affirming that they have had
occasional contact with the Agency and that they have received
funds they knew to have originated with the Agency. Of course,
the final decision on the National Student Association's
statement will be made independently by e organization's

officers.
