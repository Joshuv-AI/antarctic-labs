No-Go Theorem for Singularity Resolution
Zhen-Xiao Zhang 1, Chen Lan 2, Yan-Gang Miao 1

Abstract
We prove a No-Go theorem for singularity resolution in gravitational collapse: within any analytic gravitational theory, including general relativity and all theories with polynomial actions, quantum corrections introduced solely as effective matter sources are insufficient to halt singularities. This rules out singularity resolution via effective energy density in a broad class of quantum gravity approaches, including asymptotic safety and noncommutative geometry, which inevitably yield finite-time singularities or geodesic incompleteness. Resolution strictly requires either non-analytic modifications to the gravitational action, or a vanishing effective energy density at high densities (as realized in loop quantum gravity’s Planck stars). The theorem is proved via an intrinsic f ⁡ ( ℚ ) f(\mathbb{Q}) gravity framework, extended universally to general relativity, f ⁡ ( ℝ ) f(\mathbb{R}) , and f ⁡ ( 𝕋 ) f(\mathbb{T}) through the geometrical trinity—with regularity criteria and junction conditions grounded in non-metricity, free of standard GR tools.



I
Introduction

The fate of matter under gravitational collapse is one of the deepest open questions in theoretical physics. General Relativity (GR) predicts that collapse inevitably produces spacetime singularities
12
, regions where predictability breaks down and classical gravity fails. The leading resolution strategy, shared by virtually all quantum gravity (QG) approaches, is to introduce quantum corrections as an effective energy density
\epsilon_{\text{eff}}
, an equivalent matter source that suppresses collapse at high densities
1
. This underlies asymptotic safety
6
, loop quantum gravity (LQG)
11
;
22
, and noncommutative geometry
10
;
16
;
9
, as well as a broad class of regular black hole models
15
. Despite their diversity, these approaches share a common structure: they modify the matter sector while leaving the analytic structure of the gravitational action intact. By matching boundary conditions on the collapsing matter’s surface, these models enable matching to a static exterior geometry (often invoking generalizations of Birkhoff’s theorem
5
;
18
;
21
). However, existing efforts rely on phenomenological, model-specific analyses, requiring case-by-case verification of regularity. Whether the shared strategy of modifying the matter sector within an analytic gravitational theory can ever suffice for genuine singularity resolution has remained an open question.

We address this within a unified framework provided by the “geometrical trinity” of gravity. Replacing GR’s curved manifold with one where curvature vanishes but torsion is present yields the teleparallel equivalent of GR (TEGR); a manifold with vanishing curvature and torsion but non-metricity
3
;
13
(
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}
, where
\nabla
denotes the general affine connection) yields the symmetric teleparallel equivalent of GR (STEGR). GR, TEGR, and STEGR are equivalent classical gravity descriptions whose extensions,
f(\mathbb{R})
,
f(\mathbb{T})
, and
f(\mathbb{Q})
gravity
14
;
24
, offer complementary mathematical perspectives on collapse. We exploit
f(\mathbb{Q})
gravity as a particularly clean arena: its flat-spacetime formulation eliminates curvature entirely, enabling a fully intrinsic collapse framework free of GR-derived tools. The No-Go theorem we prove, however, extends universally to GR,
f(\mathbb{R})
, and
f(\mathbb{T})
via the geometrical trinity.

However, as extensions of GR,
f(\mathbb{T})
and
f(\mathbb{Q})
lack many GR-established methods, especially in nascent
f(\mathbb{Q})
gravity. Studies often borrow GR concepts (e.g., Riemann and extrinsic curvatures via the Levi-Civita connection) as auxiliaries, despite
f(\mathbb{Q})
’s distinct non-metric-compatible connection. This introduces rigor issues, as discussing curvature in
f(\mathbb{Q})
’s flat spacetime is inappropriate.

The implications of our result are broad and model-independent. For asymptotic safety, our theorem implies that renormalization-group-improved black hole spacetimes
6
;
5
cannot be rendered truly regular without modifying the polynomial structure of the gravitational action. For noncommutative geometry, the same conclusion applies. For regular black hole model-building, our result provides a no-go criterion that bypasses case-by-case analysis: any proposed regular black hole sourced by an effective QG density within an analytic theory must, upon closer inspection, harbor either a scalar singularity or geodesic incompleteness. The theorem thus transforms singularity resolution from a model-dependent question into a matter of algebraic structure.

In this Letter, leveraging
f(\mathbb{Q})
’s advantages for collapse analysis, we establish a purely intrinsic
f(\mathbb{Q})
-based framework, including regularity criteria and junction conditions grounded in non-metricity. These recover GR results when
f(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{Q}
. Using this, we discuss QG contributions via
\epsilon_{\text{eff}}
to dust collapse, encompassing most QG theories. We prove a No-Go theorem: in analytic theories with polynomial actions (including GR),
\epsilon_{\text{eff}}
modifications alone fail to resolve singularities, leading to finite-time singularities or geodesic incompleteness in asymptotic collapse. Resolution requires non-analytic, non-polynomial action changes or solutions with vanishing effective energy density (as in LQG). We illustrate with a quadratic
f(\mathbb{Q})
model under asymptotic safety. Via the geometrical trinity, our results extend to GR,
f(\mathbb{R})
, and
f(\mathbb{T})
. This suggests many QG approaches, like asymptotic safety and noncommutative geometry, cannot eliminate singularities without fundamental ultraviolet restructuring.

II.1
Intrinsic regularity criteria

Assessing black hole regularity in
f(\mathbb{Q})
gravity poses a non-trivial challenge, as the theory’s non-Riemannian structure—with vanishing curvature and torsion—renders direct application of GR criteria ineffective. In GR, regularity requires finite curvature invariants (e.g., Kretschmann scalar
K=R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
) and geodesic completeness. However, in
f(\mathbb{Q})
, the Riemann tensor is identically zero, making such invariants trivially vanishing and unsuitable for detecting pathologies like divergences in tidal forces or metric deformations. The physical motivation is transparent: these invariants measure tidal distortion rates and metric deformation, playing the role of curvature invariants in GR. As we prove in the SM, geometric finiteness of
\mathbb{Q}
and
\nabla Q
invariants implies finiteness of the Kretschmann scalar, recovering the standard GR criterion in the appropriate limit.

To address this, we propose an intrinsic framework grounded in
f(\mathbb{Q})

Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}
and its covariant derivatives. A black hole solution is intrinsically regular if both conditions hold:

1.

Geometric Finiteness
: All independent scalar invariants constructed from
Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}
and

\mathbb{Q}

\overline{Q}_{\alpha}\overline{Q}^{\alpha}

\nabla_{\beta}Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}\nabla^{\beta}Q^{\alpha\mu\nu}

III
Dynamical obstruction and No-Go theorem

The strategy of our proof is as follows. The most general scenario in which QG contributes an effective matter source is that of homogeneous dust collapse, modeled by a contracting FLRW interior matched to a static exterior. In this setting, the entire collapse dynamics is encoded in a single function: the scale factor
a(t)
. The modified Friedmann equation in
f(\mathbb{Q})
gravity then takes the form of a master equation
\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{Q})=\epsilon_{\rm eff}(a)
, which separates the gravitational response
\mathcal{J}
from the quantum-corrected matter source
\epsilon_{\rm eff}
. Singularity resolution requires either (i) a bounce, meaning
\dot{a}=0
at some finite
a_{\rm min}>0
, or (ii) asymptotic non-collapse, meaning
a(t)\to{\rm const}>0
. We show that for any analytic
f(\mathbb{Q})
(including GR), both possibilities are obstructed: case (i) is forbidden by the algebraic identity
\mathcal{J}(0)=0
, and case (ii) always leads to geodesic incompleteness. This constitutes the No-Go theorem.

To rigorously analyze the singularity resolution, we adopt the standard Oppenheimer-Snyder approach, which models the interior of a collapsing homogeneous dust cloud using a contracting Friedmann-Lema\̂mathrm{i}tre-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric. This naturally dictates that the collapse dynamics are governed by the Friedmann equations. Rewriting the Friedmann equation in
f(\mathbb{Q})
gravity
14
separates gravity from matter, yielding the master equation for collapse dynamics with scale factor
a(t)

\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{Q})=\epsilon_{\text{eff}}(a),

(3)

where the dynamical response is

\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{Q})\equiv\mathbb{Q}f^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})-\frac{1}{2}f(\mathbb{Q}),

(4)

and
\epsilon_{\text{eff}}(a)
is the effective energy density from collapsing dust. In coincident gauge,
\mathbb{Q}=6H^{2}\geq 0
, with Hubble parameter
H=\dot{a}/a
.

We analyze collapse, focusing on singularity-free scenarios in finite time (requiring detailed asymptotic checks). Two possibilities emerge:

IV.2
Junction to the Exterior Geometry

It is worth noting that, the static solution from the perspective of an external observer can be obtained through our defined junction conditions:

M(R)=\frac{R^{3}\left[\sqrt{\frac{24\alpha}{\xi}\ln\left(\frac{3m_{0}\xi r_{b}^{3}}{R^{3}}+1\right)+1}-1\right]}{72\alpha},

Although the matching procedure formally yields a static exterior metric for a collapsing dust cloud that cannot reach a singularity within a finite time, this solution masks a fundamental pathology. The singularity is merely pushed to infinite coordinate time. Given the divergence of internal geometric scalars and the incompleteness of geodesics, the resulting spacetime fails to meet the criteria for a regular black hole.

This finding clarifies an apparent tension with Ref.
5

Our analysis shows that while the exterior metric is indeed well-behaved, and the junction conditions are formally satisfied, the interior spacetime remains singular: the non-metricity scalar diverges and null geodesics are incomplete. The regular black hole of Ref.
5

V
Conclusion

In this Letter, we establish a rigorous, intrinsic framework in
f(\mathbb{Q})
gravity for analyzing gravitational collapse, avoiding GR-derived tools by defining regularity criteria and junction conditions based on non-metricity and momentum conservation. This self-consistent approach recovers GR limits and enables generalized studies of singularity resolution.

Applying it, we derive a No-Go theorem: in analytic gravitational theories with polynomial actions (including GR), quantum corrections via effective energy density
\epsilon_{\text{eff}}
alone fail to resolve singularities. The dynamical response vanishes at zero expansion (
\mathcal{J}(0)=0
), obstructing bounces and leading to finite-time singularities or asymptotic collapse with divergent invariants and geodesic incompleteness—persisting even if masked for external observers.

Nonsingular resolution requires a non-singular bounce
4
, achievable only through (i) non-analytic, non-polynomial action modifications at ultraviolet scales, or (ii)
\epsilon_{\text{eff}}
vanishing at high densities (as in LQG’s Planck stars). These constraints guide future regular black hole constructions, with implications for evaporating black holes and potential observables like modified Hawking radiation.

Our results call to mind a known limitation in constructing regular black holes via Non-Linear Electrodynamics (NLED). While actions of regular metrics can be mathematically obtained by reverse-engineered NLED Lagrangians (the Ayon-Beato and Garcia approach
2
), physically realistic models like the Euler-Heisenberg theory generally fail to resolve singularities
20
. Similarly, we find here that QG theories providing an effective matter source do not automatically guarantee regularity; in fact, under analytic gravity, they inevitably lead to singularities. This reinforces the conclusion that resolving the singularity problem requires more than just a modified source. It requires a fundamental change in the gravitational sector itself
7
.

In terms of future prospects, while our current analysis establishes the fundamental dynamical constraints under spherical symmetry, realistic astrophysical collapse inevitably involves angular momentum. Extending this intrinsic framework to axially symmetric spacetimes is crucial for confronting theoretical predictions with upcoming observational data, such as black hole shadows
23
and gravitational wave ringdowns
8
. However, this extension presents significant theoretical challenges. In particular, formulating the intrinsic junction conditions for rotating collapse in
f(\mathbb{Q})
gravity is considerably more intricate than in the static case. The matching of the generalized momentum flux across a rotating boundary involves complex non-diagonal components of the non-metricity tensor, requiring a rigorous generalization of the matching procedure developed here. Addressing these complexities constitutes a primary direction for future research.

Via the geometrical trinity, our results extend universally to
f(\mathbb{R})
,
f(\mathbb{T})
, and GR, mandating a paradigm shift in QG phenomenology toward fundamental non-analytic frameworks or vanishing
\epsilon_{\text{eff}}
models. Investigating non-polynomial actions offers promising directions. More broadly, our No-Go theorem reframes the singularity problem as a question about the analytic structure of gravity rather than the choice of matter source. The widespread practice of constructing regular black holes by sourcing GR with a carefully chosen
\epsilon_{\rm eff}
, whether from QG, nonlinear electrodynamics, or phenomenological models, cannot produce genuine regularity within analytic theories. The singular core is at best displaced to infinite coordinate time, remaining present as geodesic incompleteness. True resolution demands a departure from perturbative, analytic gravity: precisely the kind of non-perturbative ultraviolet completion that approaches such as LQG aim to provide. Our results thus supply a sharp theoretical criterion for distinguishing genuine from apparent singularity resolution, with direct relevance to black hole phenomenology in the era of gravitational-wave and Event Horizon Telescope observations.

Since
L\sim Q
, it follows that

\nabla L\sim\nabla Q,\qquad L\cdot L\sim Q\cdot Q.

(11)

Consequently, the Riemann tensor can be schematically expressed as

\mathring{R}\sim\mathcal{A}(\nabla Q)+\mathcal{B}(Q\cdot Q),

(12)

where
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
denote linear operators involving only the metric and index contractions.
The Kretschmann scalar is defined as

K=\mathring{R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathring{R}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.

(13)

Substituting the schematic structure of
\mathring{R}
yields

K\sim(\nabla Q+Q\cdot Q)\cdot(\nabla Q+Q\cdot Q).

(14)

Therefore, the Kretschmann scalar consists of a linear combination of the following three types of contributions:

1.

(\nabla Q)\cdot(\nabla Q)
, representing quadratic invariants of
\nabla Q
;

2.

(Q\cdot Q)\cdot(Q\cdot Q)
, representing quartic invariants of
Q
;

3.

(\nabla Q)\cdot(Q\cdot Q)
, representing mixed contractions between
\nabla Q
and
Q\cdot Q
.

According to our definition of geometric finiteness, the complete scalar bases constructed from both
Q
and
\nabla Q
remain finite. This condition guarantees that all higher-order contractions built from these quantities are finite as well. In particular, the first two classes of terms are manifestly finite. Moreover, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, if the norms of
\nabla Q
and
Q\cdot Q
are finite, then their cross contraction
(\nabla Q)\cdot(Q\cdot Q)
must also remain finite. Consequently, every contribution entering the Kretschmann scalar is finite, and therefore the Kretschmann scalar
K
itself is finite.

In this work we argue that physical matter follows the geodesics associated with the Levi-Civita connection. In other words, the motion of physical particles is completely determined by the spacetime metric. For this reason, the convergence or divergence of affine parameters along Levi-Civita geodesics is adopted in this Letter as the criterion for black hole regularity.

The choice of physical trajectories is not dictated solely by the geometric structure of the gravitational sector, but rather by how matter couples to gravity. In the standard model and in the dust collapse model considered here, the coupling follows the principle of minimal coupling. According to this principle, matter fields interact with the geometry only through the metric and do not couple directly to the affine connection. The matter action therefore takes the form

S_{m}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}_{m}(g_{\mu\nu},\psi_{m},\partial\psi_{m}).

(17)

Consequently, when deriving the equations of motion for particles from the matter action, the affine connection does not appear explicitly. For a classical point particle the action is

S_{p}=-m\int\mathrm{d}\tau\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}}.

(18)

Since this action has the same form as in GR, varying the worldline
x^{\mu}(\tau)
leads directly to the metric geodesic equation involving the Levi-Civita connection.

For continuous matter fields, variation with respect to the metric defines the energy-momentum tensor
T^{\mu\nu}
. The Levi-Civita derivative
\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}
is the unique metric-compatible covariant derivative satisfying

\mathring{\nabla}_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}=0.

(19)

Only with this derivative operator can the standard conservation law

\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0

(20)

be obtained from diffeomorphism invariance of the matter action. This conservation law determines the motion of matter. For example, for dust with

T^{\mu\nu}=\rho u^{\mu}u^{\nu},

(21)

one obtains directly

u^{\mu}\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}u^{\nu}=0,

(22)

which is precisely the Levi-Civita geodesic equation.

For completeness we outline the derivation. Consider the variation generated by a diffeomorphism with vector field
\xi^{\mu}
,

\delta_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu}.

(23)

The variation of the matter action is

\delta_{\xi}S_{m}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\frac{\delta S_{m}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\delta_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}T^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu}.

(24)

The Lie derivative of the metric is independent of the choice of connection,

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu}=\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}+g_{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\alpha}+g_{\alpha\nu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\alpha}.

(25)

To extract a conservation law we rewrite this expression using a covariant derivative.
If the Levi-Civita derivative is used,

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu}=\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\mathring{\nabla}_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}.

(26)

The variation becomes

\delta_{\xi}S_{m}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}T^{\mu\nu}\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}.

(27)

Integrating by parts gives

\delta_{\xi}S_{m}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\left[\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g}T^{\mu\nu}\xi_{\nu})-\xi_{\nu}\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g}T^{\mu\nu})\right].

(28)

\delta_{\xi}S_{m}=-\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\xi_{\nu}(\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}).

(29)

Requiring the variation to vanish for arbitrary

yields

\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0.

V
Proof that the affine parameter integral for null geodesics is finite in the saturated source of Scenario 1

We prove that the affine parameter for null geodesics remains finite when the source saturates in Scenario 1.

Assume that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\dot{a}(t)}{a(t)}=-H_{0},\qquad H_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{Q}_{0}}{6}}>0.

(51)

The affine parameter along a radial null geodesic satisfies

\lambda\propto\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(52)

We show that this integral converges.

From the definition of the limit, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a time
T>t_{0}
such that for all
t>T
,

\left|\frac{\dot{a}(t)}{a(t)}+H_{0}\right|<\epsilon,

(53)

which implies

-H_{0}-\epsilon<\frac{\dot{a}(t)}{a(t)}<-H_{0}+\epsilon.

(54)

Choosing
\epsilon=H_{0}/2
, we obtain

\frac{\dot{a}(t)}{a(t)}<-\frac{H_{0}}{2},\qquad t>T.

(55)

Integrating this inequality from
T
to
t
gives

\int_{T}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left(\ln a(\tau)\right)\mathrm{d}\tau<\int_{T}^{t}-\frac{H_{0}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(56)

which yields

\ln a(t)-\ln a(T)<-\frac{H_{0}}{2}(t-T).

(57)

Exponentiating both sides gives the bound

a(t)<a(T)\exp\!\left(\frac{H_{0}}{2}T\right)\exp\!\left(-\frac{H_{0}}{2}t\right).

(58)

Defining

C=a(T)\exp\!\left(\frac{H_{0}}{2}T\right),

(59)

we obtain

0<a(t)<C\exp\!\left(-\frac{H_{0}}{2}t\right),\qquad\forall\,t>T,

(60)

where
0<C<\infty
.
The affine parameter can now be written as

\lambda\propto\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{t_{0}}^{T}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{T}^{\infty}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(61)

The first term is finite because
a(t)
is finite on the compact interval
[t_{0},T]
,

\int_{t_{0}}^{T}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

(62)

For the second term, using the exponential bound above,

\int_{T}^{\infty}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\int_{T}^{\infty}C\exp\!\left(-\frac{H_{0}}{2}t\right)\mathrm{d}t=\frac{2C}{H_{0}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{H_{0}}{2}T\right)<\infty.

(63)

Therefore,

\lambda\propto\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

(64)

which completes the proof.

VI
Proof that bounce solutions satisfy the regularity criteria

Let
a(t)
be a bounce solution of the modified Friedmann dynamics.
A bounce configuration implies that the scale factor admits a strictly positive lower bound,

a(t)\geq a_{\min}>0,\qquad\forall\,t\in(-\infty,\infty).

(65)

We now verify that such solutions satisfy the two regularity criteria introduced in the main text.

VI.0.1
Geometric Finiteness

In the coincident gauge of the FLRW metric, the non-metricity scalar reads

\mathbb{Q}=6H^{2}=6\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}.

(66)

Since
a(t)
never approaches zero and the evolution is assumed to be smooth, the derivative
\dot{a}(t)
remains finite for solutions generated by the master equation

\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{Q})=\epsilon_{\text{eff}}(a),

where
\epsilon_{\text{eff}}(a)
is finite for
a\geq a_{\min}
. Consequently, the scalar
\mathbb{Q}
is finite for all cosmic times.

Higher-order invariants constructed from
\mathbb{Q}
and its covariant derivatives involve terms of the schematic form
\ddot{a}/a
and
(\dot{a}/a)^{2}
. The condition
a(t)\neq 0
guarantees that these denominators never vanish. Together with the smoothness of the solution, this ensures that all algebraic and differential invariants constructed from
\mathbb{Q}
remain finite. Hence the spacetime satisfies the geometric finiteness criterion.

VI.0.2
Geodesic Completeness

Consider the affine parameter
\lambda
along a radial null geodesic. In FLRW spacetime the relation between the affine parameter and the coordinate time satisfies
\mathrm{d}\lambda\propto a(t)\mathrm{d}t
. Integrating from an initial time
t_{0}
to
t
yields

\lambda(t)\propto\int_{t_{0}}^{t}a(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(67)

Using the lower bound
a(t)\geq a_{\min}
we obtain

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}a(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\geq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}a_{\min}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=a_{\min}(t-t_{0}).

(68)

Taking the limit
t\to\infty
and noting that
a_{\min}>0
, one finds
\lambda(t)\to\infty
. Therefore null geodesics can be extended to arbitrarily large affine parameter. An analogous argument applies to timelike geodesics. Hence all physical observers can extend their worldlines indefinitely, implying geodesic completeness.

We therefore conclude that the existence of a strictly positive lower bound
a_{\min}
is sufficient to ensure both intrinsic regularity criteria. In particular, any bounce solution generated by the non-perturbative dynamics considered here corresponds to a physically regular spacetime.
