From AdS Propagators to Celestial Propagators arXiv: -
Pongwit Srisangyingcharoen

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate how AdS scalar propagators are represented in the celestial basis. Starting from the standard bulk-to-boundary propagator in Euclidean AdS space, we express the propagator in a Schwinger parametrization and construct the corresponding boundary-to-boundary propagator. We then transform the resulting propagators to the celestial basis using conformal primary wavefunctions for both massless and massive scalar fields. For the massless case, the celestial propagator reduces to an effectively two-dimensional boundary-to-boundary object on the celestial sphere dependent on the AdS/CFT conformal dimension Δ \Delta . For the massive case, the celestial propagator exhibits a nontrivial kernel involving modified Bessel functions, closely resembling the momentum-space radial structure of AdS bulk-to-boundary propagators. The results suggest a structural translation from AdS propagators and celestial propagators.

An interesting aspect of celestial holography is that conformal primary wavefunctions themselves resemble bulk-to-boundary propagators. Motivated by these observations, in this work we investigate how scalar propagators in AdS space are represented after transforming to the celestial basis. In particular, we focus on Euclidean AdS bulk-to-boundary propagators written in a Schwinger parametrization and examine their corresponding celestial representations for both massless and massive scalar fields. Starting from the standard AdS boundary-to-boundary propagator constructed by gluing two bulk-to-boundary propagators, we perform the celestial transform using conformal primary wavefunctions and study the resulting structures on the celestial sphere.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review scalar bulk-to-boundary propagators in Euclidean AdS space and rewrite them using Schwinger parametrization. In Section 4, we derive the celestial representation of the massless AdS boundary-to-boundary propagator and identify its effective two-dimensional structure on the celestial sphere. In Section 5, we extend the analysis to the massive case and obtain the corresponding celestial propagator. Finally, Section 6 contains conclusions.

2
AdS Scalar propagator

In this section, we would like to exhibit the bulk-to-boundary propagators in
\text{AdS}_{d+1}
in a Schwinger representation for a massive scalar field. It is convenient to work in the Poincaré coordinates of Euclidean
\text{AdS}_{d+1}
,

ds^{2}=L^{2}\frac{d\mathbf{z}^{2}+dz_{0}^{2}}{z_{0}^{2}}

(1)

where
L
is called the AdS curvature radius. Let us consider the massive scalar action

S[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{d}\mathbf{z}dz_{0}\sqrt{g}\left(g^{MN}\partial_{M}\phi(z)\partial_{N}\phi(z)+m^{2}\phi^{2}(z)\right)

(2)

where
\phi(z)=\phi(z_{0},\mathbf{z})
. A bulk-to-boundary propagator
K(z_{0},\mathbf{z};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})
can be obtained from the wave equation

\left(z_{0}^{d+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\left(z_{0}^{-d+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\right)+z_{0}^{2}\Box-m^{2}L^{2}\right)K=0.

(3)

where
\Box
is
d
-dimensional Laplacian operator in direction
\mathbf{z}
. It is well-known that the expression for bulk-to-boundary propagator takes the form

K_{\Delta}(z_{0},\mathbf{z};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})=\frac{\Gamma(\Delta)}{\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(\Delta-\frac{d}{2})}\left(\frac{z_{0}}{z_{0}^{2}+(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{\prime})^{2}}\right)^{\Delta}

(4)

where
\Delta=\frac{1}{2}\left(d+\sqrt{d^{2}+4m^{2}L^{2}}\right)

32
. The AdS bulk field
\phi(z)
is related to the boundary data by

\phi(z_{0},\mathbf{z})=\int d^{d}\vec{x}\ K_{\Delta}(z_{0},\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x})\phi_{0}(\mathbf{x}).

(5)

Using a Schwinger parametrization, one obtains

K(z_{0},\mathbf{z};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{z_{0}^{\Delta}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(\Delta-\frac{d}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\ \rho^{\Delta-1}e^{-\rho}e^{-\frac{(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{\prime})^{2}}{z_{0}^{2}}\rho}.

(6)

We can then construct a boundary-to-boundary propagator through

\displaystyle K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}\int d^{d}z\ K_{\Delta}(t,\mathbf{z};\mathbf{y})K_{\Delta}(t,\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x})

(7)

which can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi^{d}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\Gamma(\Delta-d/2))^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta+\frac{d}{2}+1}}\int d^{d}z\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\rho_{1}^{\Delta-1}\rho_{2}^{\Delta-1}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x})^{2}\frac{\rho_{1}}{t}}e^{-(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{y})^{2}\frac{\rho_{2}}{t}}

(8)

where
t=z_{0}^{2}
.

Alternatively, one may utilize the heat kernel,

\langle x|e^{\tau\Box}|y\rangle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}e^{-(x-y)^{2}/4\tau}

(9)

to rewrite

K_{\Delta}(t,\mathbf{z};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})=\frac{t^{(d-\Delta)/2}}{\Gamma(\Delta-\frac{d}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\ \rho^{\Delta-\frac{d}{2}-1}e^{-\rho}\langle\mathbf{z}|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho}\Box}|\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\rangle.

(10)

This gives

\displaystyle K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(\Gamma(\Delta-d/2))^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta+1-\frac{d}{2}}}\int d^{d}z\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\times\ (\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\Delta-\frac{d}{2}-1}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}\langle\mathbf{y}|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho_{2}}\Box}|\mathbf{z}\rangle\langle\mathbf{z}|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho_{1}}\Box}|\mathbf{x}\rangle.

(11)

The expression describes the standard boundary-to-boundary propagator constructed from two identical bulk-to-boundary propagators of weight
\Delta
. In this paper, we examine how such an AdS propagator is perceived in the 2D celestial space. Therefore, throughout the paper, we work in
d=4
dimensions.

3
Conformal primary wavefunctions and celestial amplitudes

In a two-dimensional celestial CFT, celestial amplitudes are scattering amplitudes expanded on the basis of conformal primary wavefunctions
18
;
20
rather than in the usual plane-wave basis. A conformal primary wavefunction
\phi_{h_{i}}^{\pm}(\mathbb{z}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i})
with the conformal dimension
h_{i}
can be thought of as a bulk-to-boundary propagator in
\mathbb{R}^{3,1}
that maps four-dimensional Minkowski spacetime
\mathbf{x}_{i}
to a boundary point
\mathbb{z}_{i}=(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
on the celestial sphere.

Consider a
4D
scattering amplitude of
n
-incoming and
m-
outgoing particles,
A_{n+m}(\mathbf{x}_{i})
. The corresponding celestial amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{n+m}(h_{i},z_{i})
is defined through

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n+m}(h_{i},\mathbb{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{n+m}d^{4}x_{i}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\phi^{+}_{h_{j}}(\mathbb{z}_{j},\mathbf{x}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\phi^{-}_{h_{j}}(\mathbb{z}_{k},\mathbf{x}_{k})\right)\,A_{n+m}(\mathbf{x}_{i}).

(12)

The plus and minus signs of the conformal primary wavefunctions denote incoming and outgoing scattering waves, respectively. Some may write the conformal dimensions
h_{j}=1+i\lambda_{j}
,
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
.

The four-momentum
k^{\mu}
of a scattering particle is parametrized according to its mass. For a massless particle,

\displaystyle\mathbf{k}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\omega\mathbf{q}^{\mu}(\mathbb{z})

\displaystyle=\omega_{j}\bigl(1+|z|^{2},\,z+\bar{z},\,-i(z-\bar{z}),\,1-|z|^{2}\bigr),

(13)

where
\omega_{j}
is the angular frequency associated with the energy of the external particle and
\mathbf{q}^{\mu}_{j}
is a null vector pointing toward a point
\mathbb{z}=(z,\bar{z})
on the celestial sphere. For a massive particle with mass
m
, the momentum is

\displaystyle\mathbf{k}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=m\mathbf{p}^{\mu}(y,\mathbb{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{2y}\bigl(1+y^{2}+|z|^{2},z+\bar{z},-i(z-\bar{z}),1-y^{2}-|z|^{2}\bigr)

(14)

where
\mathbf{p}^{\mu}
represents a unit vector directed toward a point on the three-dimensional upper unit hyperboloid
H_{3}
with
y
denoting the radial distance into the bulk of
H_{3}
. The complex coordinates
\mathbb{z}=(z,\bar{z})
identify the location on the celestial sphere where the particle’s trajectory intersects the boundary.

The primary conformal wavefunctions for massless and massive scalars are

\displaystyle\phi^{\pm}_{h}(\mathbb{z},\mathbf{x}^{\mu})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\omega^{h-1}e^{\pm i\omega\mathbf{q}(\mathbb{z})\cdot\mathbf{x}}

(15)

\displaystyle\phi^{\pm}_{h}(\mathbb{z},\mathbf{x}^{\mu})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}\int d^{2}wG_{h}(y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z})e^{\pm im\mathbf{p}(y,\mathbb{w})\cdot\mathbf{x}}

(16)

respectively.
G_{h}(y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z})
is the
4D
-to-
2D
bulk-to-boundary propagator in Minkowski spacetime whose expression is

G_{h}(\mathbf{{p}}(y,\mathbb{w});\mathbf{q}(\mathbb{z}))=\frac{1}{(-\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{q})^{h}}=\left(\frac{y}{y^{2}+|w-z|^{2}}\right)^{h}.

(17)

The conformal primary wavefunctions also admit a shadow
transformation analogous to that in ordinary conformal field
theory
6
. In a two-dimensional conformal field theory,
a primary operator with conformal dimension
h
is related to
its shadow operator with conformal dimension
2-h
. For the
celestial conformal primary wavefunctions, the shadow trans
formation acts as an integral transform on the celestial sphere,

\widetilde{\phi}^{\pm}_{h}(\mathbb{z},\mathbf{x})=\frac{\Gamma(h)}{\pi\Gamma(h-1)}\int d^{2}w\frac{\phi^{\pm}_{h}(\mathbb{w},\mathbf{x})}{|z-w|^{2(2-h)}}.

(18)

For scalar conformal primary wavefunctions, the shadow
transform produces another conformal primary wavefunction
with shadow conformal dimension

h\rightarrow 2-h.

(19)

Accordingly, the shadow transform of the conformal
primary wavefunction (15) is

\widetilde{\phi}^{\pm}_{h}(\mathbb{z},\mathbf{x})=\phi^{\pm}_{2-h}(\mathbb{z},\mathbf{x})

(20)

for both scalar massless and massive cases.

Beyond the standard Mellin transform over the energy variables,
alternative integral representations of celestial amplitudes have
also been explored. Recently, a Mellin-like transformation acting
directly on the celestial coordinates
(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
was introduced for celestial massless amplitudes
34
. Inspired
by closed-string worldsheet integrals, the transformation maps

(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
to a new set of complex variables

(s_{i},\bar{s}_{i})
and leads to constraints associated with global
conformal invariance.

4
From Massless boundary-to-boundary propagator to celestial propagator

Since the boundary-to-boundary propagator
K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is in a flat four-dimensional space, we can map this to the celestial sphere. Let first consider a massless scalar field with AdS weight
\Delta
. According to (
12
), the celestial propagator for a massless particle reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{2}\,\omega_{1}^{h_{1}-1}\omega_{2}^{h_{2}-1}

\displaystyle\times\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\ e^{i\omega_{1}\mathbf{q}_{1}(\mathbb{z}_{1})\cdot\mathbf{x}-i\omega_{2}\mathbf{q}_{2}(\mathbb{z}_{2})\cdot\mathbf{y}}.

(21)

The second line of the above equation is simply the Fourier transform of the propagator
K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
, by which we will refer to it as
K(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
. Performing Gaussian integration, one obtains

\displaystyle K(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{4}}{2}\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{1}\mathbf{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\mathbf{q}_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\times\rho_{1}^{\Delta-3}\rho_{2}^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-\omega_{1}^{2}q_{1}^{2}\frac{t}{4\rho_{1}}}e^{-\omega_{2}^{2}q_{2}^{2}\frac{t}{4\rho_{2}}}.

(22)

The conservation of momentum together with their massless representations (
13
) allows us to write

\displaystyle\omega_{1}^{2}q_{1}^{2}=\omega_{2}^{2}q_{2}^{2}=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\mathbf{q}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{2}=2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}|z_{12}|^{2}

(23)

where
z_{12}=z_{1}-z_{2}
. Note that we work in the mostly
minus Minkowski signature. Accordingly, the expression (
22
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{4}}{2}

\displaystyle\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{1}\mathbf{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\mathbf{q}_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\times\rho_{1}^{\Delta-3}\rho_{2}^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}{2}|z_{12}|^{2}t\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}.

(24)

The Mellin transformation then converts the momentum space propagator to the corresponding two-dimensional celestial one through

\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{2}\,\omega_{1}^{h_{2}-1}\omega_{2}^{h_{2}-1}K(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}).

(25)

Integration over
\omega_{i}
is not straightforward as the integrating variables appear inside the Dirac delta function. To this extent, we decompose the delta function as

\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\omega}\,\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\,\delta^{(2)}(z-z^{\prime})\,\delta(k^{2})\,\Theta(k^{0}).

(26)

More detail is shown in the appendix
A
. We may ignore the step function as
k^{0}
is always non-negative.

First let us naively use the fact that
z_{1}=z_{2}
and
\bar{z}_{1}=\bar{z}_{2}
via the constraint
\delta^{(2)}(z_{12})
. This greatly simplifies the celestial propagator as
\exp{\left(-\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}{2}|z_{12}|^{2}t\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)\right)}=1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{4}}{2}\delta(k^{2}_{1})\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{2}\,\omega_{1}^{h_{1}-2}\omega_{2}^{h_{2}-1}\delta(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})

\displaystyle\times\delta^{(2)}(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{4}}{2}(\Gamma(\Delta-2))^{2}\delta^{(3)}(0)\Lambda^{\Delta-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\omega^{h_{1}+h_{2}-3}.

(27)

The integral over the Schwinger parameter produces a UV divergence, which we regulate by introducing a cutoff, resulting in an overall factor
\Lambda^{2}
. In addition, the factor
\delta(k^{2})
is also divergent on-shell providing a prefactor
\delta^{(3)}(0)
which is an IR divergence. The
\omega
-integral provides a constraint

\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\omega^{h_{1}+h_{2}-3}=2\pi\delta(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})

(28)

where
h_{j}=1+1\lambda_{j}
. This recovers a known condition for celestial amplitudes. Accordingly, up to an overall divergent normalization factor, the celestial propagator behaves

\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\sim\delta(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}).

(29)

While the propagator (
29
) yields known conditions, its current form is trivial and lacks explicit dependence on the celestial coordinates
z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}
. Therefore, we re-examine the propagator of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{4}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{2}\,\omega_{1}^{h_{2}-1}\omega_{2}^{h_{2}-1}

\displaystyle\times\delta^{4}(\omega_{1}\mathbf{q}_{1}-\omega_{2}\mathbf{q}_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\times\rho_{1}^{\Delta-3}\rho_{2}^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}{2}|z_{12}|^{2}t\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}.

(30)

However, we rewrite the Dirac delta function as

\displaystyle\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}_{1}-\mathbf{k}_{2})=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}k\,\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}_{1}-\mathbf{k})\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\omega d^{2}z\,\frac{1}{\omega}\delta(\omega_{1}-\omega)\delta(\omega_{2}-\omega)

\displaystyle\times\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z)\delta(k^{2})\Theta(k^{0}).

(31)

Accordingly, the massless celestial propagator becomes

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{4}}{2}\delta(k^{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}\int d^{2}z\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\omega_{1}^{h_{1}-2}\omega_{2}^{h_{2}-1}\frac{1}{\omega}\delta(\omega_{1}-\omega)\delta(\omega_{2}-\omega)

\displaystyle\times\rho_{1}^{\Delta-3}\rho_{2}^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-\frac{\omega_{1}\omega}{2}|z_{1}-z|^{2}t\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}}e^{-\frac{\omega_{2}\omega}{2}|z_{2}-z|^{2}t\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}}.

(32)

Performing the integration over
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
, one obtains the Gaussian integral over
\omega
as

\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\omega^{h_{1}+h_{2}-3}e^{-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}t\left(|z_{1}-z|^{2}\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+|z_{2}-z|^{2}\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}.

(33)

We can then use a Gaussian integral formula

\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x^{n}e^{-ax^{2}}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}{2a^{\frac{n+1}{2}}},

(34)

to obtain

t^{1-\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}-1\right)\left(\frac{|z_{1}-z|^{2}}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{|z_{2}-z|^{2}}{\rho_{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}}.

(35)

Substitute this into (
32
). It yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\frac{(2\pi)^{4}}{2}\delta(k^{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}t^{1-\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Gamma\left(\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}-1\right)\int d^{2}z\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z)

\displaystyle\times\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}\rho_{1}^{\Delta-3}\rho_{2}^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}\left(\frac{|z_{1}-z|^{2}}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{|z_{2}-z|^{2}}{\rho_{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}}.

(36)

To evaluate the
\rho
-integral in the last line, we change the variables
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
to

\rho_{1}=ux|z_{1}-z|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{2}=u(1-x)|z_{2}-z|^{2}

(37)

with
u\in(0,\infty)
and
u\in(0,1)
. Under the transformation, the measure reads

d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}=u|z_{1}-z|^{2}|z_{2}-z|^{2}dudx.

(38)

This turns the
\rho-
integral to

\displaystyle|z_{1}-z|^{2\Delta-4}|z_{2}-z|^{2\Delta-4}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{0}^{1}dx\,u^{\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}+2\Delta-6}x^{\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}+\Delta-4}

\displaystyle\times(1-x)^{\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}+\Delta-4}e^{-u\left(x|z_{1}-z|^{2}+(1-x)|z_{2}-z|^{2}\right)}.

(39)

We then integrate over
u
to have

\displaystyle|z_{1}-z|^{2\Delta-4}|z_{2}-z|^{2\Delta-4}\Gamma\left(\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}+2\Delta-5\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{x^{\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}+\Delta-4}(1-x)^{\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}+\Delta-4}}{\left(x|z_{1}-z|^{2}+(1-x)|z_{2}-z|^{2}\right)^{\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}+2\Delta-5}}.

(40)

Upon applying the conservation of celestial conformal weights
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0
or equivalently
h_{1}+h_{2}=2
, the above
x-
integration matches with the Feynman parametrization which is

\frac{1}{A^{\alpha_{1}}B^{\alpha_{2}}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\Gamma(\alpha_{2})}\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{x^{\alpha_{1}-1}(1-x)^{\alpha_{2}-1}}{(xA+(1-x)B)^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}}.

(41)

The expression (
40
) then becomes

\left(\Gamma(\Delta-2)\right)^{2}\frac{1}{|z_{1}-z|^{2-\Delta}}\frac{1}{|z_{2}-z|^{2-\Delta}}.

(42)

Consequently, the massless celestial propagator is

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\int d^{2}z\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma(\Delta-2)}{|z_{1}-z|^{2-\Delta}}\frac{\Gamma(\Delta-2)}{|z_{2}-z|^{2-\Delta}}

(43)

where all divergent factors were absorbed into the prefactor
\mathcal{N}
. Since the celestial propagator is now dependent on
\Delta
, we may call
\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
by
\mathcal{K}(\Delta,z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
. We successfully translate an AdS propagator to the corresponding celestial propagator, where AdS data were captured by parameter
\Delta
.

Although we could integrate out the AdS bulk radius coordinate
t
, leaving it untouched allows us to relate the celestial two-point function with a bulk-to-boundary propagator in AdS space, we rewrite the above expression as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\Delta,z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{3}}\int d^{2}z\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}(t;\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z})\,\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}(t;\mathbb{z}_{2},\mathbb{z})

(44)

where

\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}(t;z_{1},z)=\frac{\Gamma(\Delta-2)}{|z_{1}-z|^{2-\Delta}t^{\frac{\Delta}{2}-2}}

(45)

We may interpret the new propagator
\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}(t;\mathbb{z}_{1},\mathbb{z})
as a scaling celestial propagator that correlates two points
z
and
z_{1}
on the celestial sphere. The propagator is
characterized by a scaling dependence on the AdS bulk
parameter
t
. The expression (
44
) resembles the structure of the AdS boundary-to-boundary propagator (
7
) where two AdS bulk-to-boundary propagators are glued together through a bulk integration. This similarity suggests a correspondence between the two constructions. In particular, the AdS bulk-to-boundary propagator
K_{\Delta}(t,\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x})
can be related to the celestial scaling two-point function
\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}(t;\mathbb{z},\mathbb{z}_{i})
, namely

K_{\Delta}(t,\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x})\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}(t;\mathbb{z},\mathbb{z}_{i}).

(46)

It is also important to emphasize that the celestial coordinates
z,\bar{z}
are not related to the four-dimensional position coordinates
\mathbf{x}^{\mu}
. Instead, they parametrize null momenta via a Fourier transformation.

5
From Massive boundary-to-boundary propagator to celestial propagator

Similar to the previous section, we begin with the boundary-to-boundary propagator
K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and map it to the corresponding celestial propagator using (
12
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy_{1}}{y_{1}^{3}}\int d^{2}w_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy_{2}}{y_{2}^{3}}\int d^{2}w_{2}

\displaystyle\times G_{h_{1}}(y_{1},\mathbb{w}_{1};\mathbb{z}_{1})G_{h_{2}}(y_{2},\mathbb{w}_{2};\mathbb{z}_{2})K(y_{i},w_{i},\bar{w}_{i})

(47)

where

K(y_{i},w_{i},\bar{w}_{i})=\int d^{4}xd^{4}y\ K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\ e^{im\mathbf{p}_{1}(y_{1},\mathbb{w}_{1})\cdot\mathbf{x}-im\mathbf{p}_{2}(y_{2},\mathbb{w}_{2})\cdot\mathbf{y}}.

(48)

First, we examine the propagator in momentum space
K(y_{i},w_{i},\bar{w}_{i})
. By performing the Gaussian integral, we obtain

\displaystyle K(y_{i},w_{i},\bar{w}_{i})=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{4}}{2}\delta^{4}(m\mathbf{p}_{1}-m\mathbf{p}_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\times(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-m^{2}p_{1}^{2}\frac{t}{4\rho_{1}}}e^{-m^{2}p_{2}^{2}\frac{t}{4\rho_{2}}}.

(49)

Naively, if we apply the on-shell relations
p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}=1
, the whole integration (
49
) would be independent of
(y_{i},w_{i},\bar{w}_{i})
. Therefore, we may write

K(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=f(\Delta)\delta^{4}(\mathbf{p}_{1}-\mathbf{p}_{2})

(50)

for some function
f(\Delta)
that depends only on
\Delta
.
Substituting into (
47
), it yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle f(\Delta)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy_{1}}{y_{1}^{3}}\int d^{2}w_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy_{2}}{y_{2}^{3}}\int d^{2}w_{2}

\displaystyle\times 4y_{1}^{3}\delta(y_{1}-y_{2})\,\delta^{(2)}(w_{12})\delta(p^{2}-1)\Theta(p^{0})

\displaystyle\times G_{h_{1}}(y_{1},\vec{w}_{1};\vec{z}_{1})G_{h_{2}}(y_{2},\vec{w}_{2};\vec{z}_{2})

(51)

where the Dirac delta function
\delta^{4}(\mathbf{p}_{1}-\mathbf{p}_{2})
was decomposed. More details on decomposition can be found in
A
. Upon reparameterizing the bulk-to-boundary propagator
G_{h_{i}}(y_{i},\mathbb{w}_{i};\mathbb{z}_{i})
as

\frac{1}{y_{i}^{h_{i}}\Gamma(h_{i})}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha_{i}\,\alpha^{h_{i}-1}e^{-\alpha_{i}}e^{-\frac{|w_{i}-z_{i}|^{2}}{y_{i}^{2}}\alpha_{i}}

(52)

and integrating over the variables
y_{2}
and
w_{2}
, one finds

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle C^{\prime}f(\Delta)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}y^{-(h_{1}+h_{2})}\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha_{1}d\alpha_{2}\,e^{-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}

\displaystyle\times\alpha_{1}^{h_{1}-1}\alpha_{2}^{h_{2}-1}\left(\int d^{2}w\,e^{-\frac{1}{y^{2}}\left(|w-z_{1}|^{2}\alpha_{1}+|w-z_{2}|^{2}\alpha_{2}\right)}\right).

(53)

Take into account the Gaussian integral inside the parentheses,
by which we can rewrite its exponent
\alpha_{1}|w-z_{1}|^{2}+\alpha_{2}|w-z_{2}|^{2}
as

(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\left|w-\frac{\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\right|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\left|z_{12}\right|^{2}.

(54)

Performing the two-dimensional Gaussian integral, it yields

\displaystyle\int d^{2}w\,e^{-\frac{1}{y^{2}}\left(|w-z_{1}|^{2}\alpha_{1}+|w-z_{2}|^{2}\alpha_{2}\right)}=\frac{\pi y^{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}e^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\frac{|z_{12}|^{2}}{y^{2}}}.

(55)

Put the above result into (
53
). It yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\pi C^{\prime}f(\Delta)\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha_{1}d\alpha_{2}\frac{\alpha_{1}^{h_{1}-1}\alpha_{2}^{h_{2}-1}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}e^{-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\,y^{-(h_{1}+h_{2})-1}e^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\frac{|z_{12}|^{2}}{y^{2}}}.

(56)

One can turn the
y
-integral in the second line into the form (
34
) by changing
y\rightarrow\frac{1}{y}
. Accordingly, the above equation takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}C^{\prime}f(\Delta)\Gamma\left(\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}\right)\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{h_{1}+h_{2}}}

\displaystyle\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha_{1}d\alpha_{2}\frac{\alpha_{1}^{\frac{h_{1}-h_{2}}{2}-1}\alpha_{2}^{-\frac{h_{1}-h_{2}}{2}-1}}{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{1-\frac{h_{1}+h_{2}}{2}}}e^{-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}.

(57)

What remains is to evaluate the
\alpha-
integral in the second line.

To continue, we change the variables
\alpha_{1}=xu
and
\alpha_{2}=x(1-u)
with
x\in(0,\infty)
and
u\in(0,1)
. Hence, we can write the integration with respect to the variable
u
as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}du\,u^{\frac{h_{1}-h_{2}}{2}-1}(1-u)^{-\frac{h_{1}-h_{2}}{2}-1}

(58)

which is equal to

2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dv\,e^{v(h_{1}-h_{2})}=4\pi\delta(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}).

(59)

This is the conservation of conformal dimensions for massive two point correlations.

Despite the desirable result, the expression still lacks a structure resembling bulk-to-boundary propagators. To this extent, let us reconsider the form of the propagator (
49
) but instead of using
p_{i}^{2}=1
, we use the conservation of momentum to rewrite

p_{1}^{2}=p_{2}^{2}=\mathbf{p}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{2}=\frac{|w_{12}|^{2}+y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}{2y_{1}y_{2}}

(60)

where
w_{12}=w_{1}-w_{2}
. Consequently, the celestial propagator (
47
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle N\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy_{1}}{y_{1}^{3}}\int d^{2}w_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy_{2}}{y_{2}^{3}}\int d^{2}w_{2}

\displaystyle y_{1}^{3}\delta(y_{1}-y_{2})\,\delta^{(2)}(w_{12})G_{h_{1}}(y_{1},\mathbb{w}_{1};\mathbb{z}_{1})

\displaystyle\times G_{h_{2}}(y_{2},\mathbb{w}_{2};\mathbb{z}_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\times(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-m^{2}\frac{|w_{12}|^{2}+y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}{2y_{1}y_{2}}t\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}

(61)

with some constant
N
. Integrating over
y_{2}
and
w_{2}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle N\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}\int d^{2}w\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{y}{y^{2}+|w-z_{1}|^{2}}\right)^{h_{1}}\left(\frac{y}{y^{2}+|w-z_{2}|^{2}}\right)^{h_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}\,(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-m^{2}t\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}.

(62)

The
\rho-
integral matches the standard integral representation of the modified Bessel function of the second kind
10
(See eq.3.471.9 thereof) which is

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\nu-1}e^{-\frac{\beta}{x}-\gamma x}dx=2\left(\frac{\beta}{\gamma}\right)^{\nu/2}K_{\nu}(2\sqrt{\beta\gamma}).

(63)

Consequently, the massive celestial propagator takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle N\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}\int d^{2}w\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t}\,\left(2m^{(\Delta-2)}K_{\Delta-2}(2m\sqrt{t})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{y}{y^{2}+|w-z_{1}|^{2}}\right)^{h_{1}}\left(\frac{y}{y^{2}+|w-z_{2}|^{2}}\right)^{h_{2}}.

(64)

Therefore, we may introduce a celestial bulk-to-boundary propagator

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\Delta}^{m}(h,t;y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z})=\left(\frac{y}{y^{2}+|w-z|^{2}}\right)^{h}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{\Delta}{2}-2}}\left(2(m\sqrt{t})^{\Delta-2}K_{\Delta-2}(2m\sqrt{t})\right)

(65)

such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=

\displaystyle N\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{3}}\int d^{2}w

\displaystyle\times\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\Delta}^{m}(h_{1},t;y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z}_{1})\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\Delta}^{m}(h_{2},t;y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z}_{2}).

(66)

Note that when the mass
m
approaches zero, the factor

2(m\sqrt{t})^{\Delta-2}K_{\Delta-2}(2m\sqrt{t})\rightarrow\Gamma(\Delta-2)

(67)

giving the same scaling factor as in the massless propagator (
45
).

An appearance of the modified Bessel function of the second kind
K_{\Delta-2}(2m\sqrt{t})
inside the celestial bulk-to-boundary propagator suggests a remnant of the momentum-space AdS bulk-to-boundary propagator after transforming to the celestial basis.

Frankly speaking, the object
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\Delta}^{m}(h,t;y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z})
is not particularly interesting, since the celestial variables
(h,y,w,z)
are completely decoupled from the AdS variables
(\Delta,t)
. In fact, one can further integrate out the AdS parameter
t
, yielding a fully factorized expression whose remaining dependence is only through
\Delta
.

Nevertheless, directly comparing the two celestial propagators
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\Delta}^{m}(h,t;y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z})
and
\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}(t;\mathbb{w},\mathbb{z})
(massive and massless cases respectively) is somewhat subtle due to the different gluing integration measures appearing in the massive and massless cases. In the massless case, the gluing structure involves

\int d^{2}z\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z)

whereas the massive case involves the bulk measure

\int\frac{dy}{y^{3}}d^{2}z.

The absence of delta-function constraints in the massive case causes the AdS components in
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\Delta}^{m}(h,t;y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z})
such as
(\Delta,t)
to remain separated from the celestial variables
(h,y,z)
as explicitly shown in (
65
). This contrasts with the massless case, where the AdS data, particularly
\Delta
, becomes directly encoded in the celestial structure through factors of the form

\frac{1}{|z-z_{1}|^{2-\Delta}|z-z_{2}|^{2-\Delta}}.

To deal with this subtlety, we observe that the Dirac delta functions in the measure can be recovered by considering the asymptotic behavior of the bulk-to-boundary propagator

G_{h}(y,\mathbb{w},\mathbb{z})=\left(\frac{y}{y^{2}+|w-z|^{2}}\right)^{h}

(68)

which behaves as a Dirac delta function (up to normalization) in the boundary limit
y\rightarrow 0^{+}
. In this limit, the propagator viewed as a function of
\mathbb{w}
vanishes everywhere except for a sharp peak at
\mathbb{w}=\mathbb{z}
. Mathematically,

\lim_{y\rightarrow 0^{+}}G_{h}(y,\mathbb{w},\mathbb{z})\sim\delta^{(2)}(w-z).

(69)

To be precise, we can do a near-boundary expansion of the bulk-to-boundary propagator as

\displaystyle G_{h}(y,\mathbb{w},\mathbb{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{h-1}y^{2-h}\delta^{(2)}(w-z)

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi}{4(h-1)(h-2)}y^{4-h}\partial^{2}\delta^{(2)}(w-z)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(y^{6-h}\partial^{4}\delta^{(2)}(w-z)\right).

(70)

More details are provided in
B
.

Now let’s evaluate the first few terms applying the expansion (
70
) to the massive celestial propagator (
61
) which yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=N\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}\int d^{2}w_{1}\int d^{2}w_{2}\,\delta^{(2)}(w_{12})

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\frac{\pi^{2}}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{2}-1)}y^{4-h_{1}-h_{2}}\delta^{(2)}(w_{1}-z_{1})\delta^{(2)}(w_{2}-z_{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi^{2}y^{6-h_{1}-h_{2}}}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{2}-1)(h_{2}-2)}\delta^{(2)}(w_{1}-z_{1})\partial^{2}_{w_{2}}\delta^{(2)}(w_{2}-z_{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi^{2}y^{6-h_{1}-h_{2}}}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{2}-1)(h_{1}-2)}\partial^{2}_{w_{1}}\delta^{(2)}(w_{1}-z_{1})\delta^{(2)}(w_{2}-z_{2})+\ldots\bigg]

\displaystyle\times\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-m^{2}\left(\frac{|w_{12}|^{2}}{2y^{2}}+1\right)t\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}.

(71)

Integrate over
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
by which it turns
w_{1}=z_{1}
and
w_{2}=z_{2}
. One obtains

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\frac{N\pi^{2}}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{2}-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}y^{4-h_{1}-h_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}\Bigg[\delta^{(2)}(z_{12})e^{-m^{2}\left(\frac{|z_{12}|^{2}}{2y^{2}}+1\right)t\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle+y^{2}\frac{1}{h_{1}-2}\partial^{2}_{z_{1}}\left(\delta^{(2)}(z_{12})e^{-m^{2}\left(\frac{|z_{12}|^{2}}{2y^{2}}+1\right)t\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle+y^{2}\frac{1}{h_{2}-2}\partial^{2}_{z_{2}}\left(\delta^{(2)}(z_{12})e^{-m^{2}\left(\frac{|z_{12}|^{2}}{2y^{2}}+1\right)t\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}\right)+\ldots\Bigg].

(72)

We may identify this series as

\mathcal{K}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=0\\
n=\text{even}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}_{n}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})

(73)

where
n
indicates the order of expansion. For simplicity, we content ourselves to demonstrate only for the first expanding term, i.e.
\mathcal{K}_{0}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
. By writing

\delta^{(2)}(z_{12})=\int d^{2}z\,\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z),

(74)

\mathcal{K}_{0}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{N\pi^{2}}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{2}-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}y^{4-h_{1}-h_{2}}\int d^{2}z\,\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}\iint_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\Delta-3}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-m^{2}\left(\frac{|z_{1}-z|^{2}}{2y^{2}}+1\right)\frac{t}{\rho_{1}}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-m^{2}\left(\frac{|z_{2}-z|^{2}}{2y^{2}}+1\right)\frac{t}{\rho_{2}}}.

(75)

According to (
63
), integrating out
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{N\pi^{2}}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{2}-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}y^{4-h_{1}-h_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta-1}}

\displaystyle\int d^{2}z\,\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z)

\displaystyle\times 2\left(\left(\frac{m^{2}}{2y^{2}}(|z_{1}-z|^{2}+2y^{2})t\right)^{\frac{\Delta}{2}-1}K_{\Delta-2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}m}{y}\sqrt{\left(|z_{1}-z|^{2}+2y^{2}\right)t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times 2\left(\left(\frac{m^{2}}{2y^{2}}(|z_{2}-z|^{2}+2y^{2})t\right)^{\frac{\Delta}{2}-1}K_{\Delta-2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}m}{y}\sqrt{\left(|z_{2}-z|^{2}+2y^{2}\right)t}\right)\right).

(76)

We then rescale
y\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}y
by which we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{0}

\displaystyle(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=\frac{N\pi^{2}\sqrt{2}^{h_{1}+h_{2}-2}}{(h_{1}-1)(h_{2}-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dy}{y^{3}}y^{4-h_{1}-h_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{3}}

\displaystyle\int d^{2}z\,\delta^{(2)}(z_{1}-z)\delta^{(2)}(z_{2}-z)\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}^{m}(t;y,\mathbb{z};\mathbb{z}_{1})\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}^{m}(t;y,\mathbb{z};\mathbb{z}_{2})

(77)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\Delta}^{m}

\displaystyle(t;y,\mathbb{z};\mathbb{z}_{1})=G_{1-\frac{\Delta}{2}}(y,\mathbb{z};\mathbb{z}_{1})\frac{1}{t^{\frac{\Delta}{2}-2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(2\left(m\sqrt{\frac{t}{y}}\right)^{\Delta-2}K_{\Delta-2}\left(2m\sqrt{\frac{t}{y}}G_{-1/2}(y,\mathbb{z};\mathbb{z}_{1})\right)\right).

(78)

Remember that
G_{h}(y,\mathbb{z};\mathbb{z}^{\prime})
is a Minkowski bulk-to-boundary propagator defined in (
17
). Investigation on higher-order terms
\mathcal{K}_{n}(h_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
for
n\geq 2
involves
n
-times derivative of Dirac delta functions which are subject to future study.

Writing the celestial two-point correlator in this way allows us to translate an AdS bulk-to-boundary propagator to a celestial bulk-to-boundary propagator as

K_{\Delta}(t,\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x})\rightarrow\mathcal{K}^{m}_{\Delta}(t;y,\mathbb{z},\mathbb{z}^{\prime}).

(79)

The resulting celestial propagators exhibit qualitatively different structures in the massless (
45
) and massive (
78
) cases. In the massless case, the celestial propagator effectively reduces to a two-dimensional boundary-to-boundary object defined entirely on the celestial sphere, with the AdS bulk parameter
t
entering only as an overall scaling parameter. By contrast, the massive celestial propagator retains an additional hyperbolic momentum-space structure
y,w,\bar{w}
leading to a celestial bulk-to-boundary propagator. Also, the information on the previous AdS space was stored in the constant
\Delta
.

6
Conclusions

In this work, we studied how scalar propagators in AdS space are represented after transforming to the celestial basis. Beginning with the Euclidean AdS bulk-to-boundary propagator written in a Schwinger representation, we constructed the corresponding boundary-to-boundary propagator and subsequently mapped it to celestial space using conformal primary wavefunctions.

For the massless case, the resulting celestial propagator effectively reduces to a two-dimensional boundary-to-boundary object on the celestial sphere (
43
). Interestingly, the celestial two-point function captures the AdS data via the parameter
\Delta
. We also express the celestial propagator analogous to the form of AdS/CFT boundary-to-boundary propagator. This allows us to structurally translate an AdS bulk-to-boundary propagator into a scaling boundary-to-boundary celestial propagator (
46
).

For the massive case, the celestial propagator exhibits a richer structure. In particular, the propagator naturally contains the modified Bessel function of the second kind, which resembles the radial dependence of momentum-space AdS bulk-to-boundary propagators. This suggests that the celestial transformation preserves part of the radial propagation structure of the original AdS theory. An AdS bulk-to-boundary propagator is structurally translated to a celestial bulk-to-boundary propagator (
79
).

The resulting expressions reveal a structural similarity between AdS gluing representations and celestial propagators which construct direct translations from AdS propagators, particularly bulk-to-boundary propagators, to celestial propagators.

Appendix A
Decomposition of Dirac delta function

We begin by decomposing the four-dimensional Dirac delta function
\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
for massless particles. We start from the normalization condition

\int d^{4}\mathbf{k}\,\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=1.

(80)

We assume that the delta function admits the factorized form:

\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=G(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\delta(k^{2})\,\Theta(k^{0}),

(81)

where
\delta(k^{2})\Theta(k^{0})
restricts the integration to the future light cone.

Using the standard identity,

\int d^{4}\mathbf{k}\,\delta(k^{2})\Theta(k^{0})\,f(\mathbf{k})=\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{2|\vec{k}|}\,f(k^{0}=|\vec{k}|,\vec{k}),

(82)

we obtain

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}\mathbf{k}\,\delta(k^{2})\Theta(k^{0})\,G(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{2|\vec{k}|}\,G(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime}).

(83)

Next, we parametrize the spatial momentum
\vec{k}
in terms of
(\omega,z,\bar{z})
as

\vec{k}=\omega\bigl(z+\bar{z},\,-i(z-\bar{z}),\,1-|z|^{2}\bigr),

(84)

which implies

|\vec{k}|=\omega(1+|z|^{2}).

(85)

The corresponding Jacobian for this change of variables is

\displaystyle d^{3}\vec{k}

\displaystyle=\left|\det\frac{\partial(k^{1},k^{2},k^{3})}{\partial(\omega,z,\bar{z})}\right|d\omega\,dz\,d\bar{z}

\displaystyle=2\omega^{2}(1+|z|^{2})\,d\omega\,dz\,d\bar{z}.

(86)

Substituting into Eq. (
83
), we find that the function
G(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
must take the form

G(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\omega}\,\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\,\delta^{(2)}(z-z^{\prime}).

(87)

Thus, the four-dimensional delta function for the massless case decomposes as

\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\omega}\,\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\,\delta^{(2)}(z-z^{\prime})\,\delta(k^{2})\,\Theta(k^{0}).

(88)

For a particle of mass
m
, we parametrize the four-momentum as

\mathbf{k}=m\,\mathbf{p}(y,z,\bar{z}),

(89)

where
\mathbf{p}
satisfies
p^{2}=1
and is given by

\mathbf{p}=\frac{1}{2y}\left(1+|z|^{2}+y^{2},\;z+\bar{z},\;-i(z-\bar{z}),\;1-|z|^{2}-y^{2}\right).

(90)

The magnitude of the spatial momentum is then

|\vec{p}|=\frac{1}{2y}\sqrt{(1+y^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}-4y^{2}}.

(91)

The Lorentz-invariant phase space measure can be written as

d^{4}\mathbf{k}\,\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})\theta(k^{0})=m^{2}\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}}{2\sqrt{|\vec{p}|^{2}+1}}.

(92)

By calculating the Jacobian for the transformation to
(y,z,\bar{z})
coordinates, we obtain

\displaystyle d^{3}\vec{p}

\displaystyle=\left|\det\frac{\partial(p^{1},p^{2},p^{3})}{\partial(y,z,\bar{z})}\right|dy\,dz\,d\bar{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+y^{2}+|z|^{2})}{4y^{4}}\,dy\,dz\,d\bar{z}.

(93)

Consequently, the measure simplifies to

\frac{d^{3}\vec{\hat{p}}}{2\sqrt{|\vec{p}|^{2}+1}}=\frac{1}{4y^{3}}\,dy\,dz\,d\bar{z}.

(94)

Following the same logic as the massless case, we define

\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=G(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})\,\Theta(k^{0}).

(95)

To satisfy the normalization condition,
G(k-k^{\prime})
must satisfy

G(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\frac{4y^{3}}{m^{2}}\,\delta(y-y^{\prime})\,\delta^{(2)}(z-z^{\prime}).

(96)

We conclude that the massive four-dimensional delta function decomposes as

\delta^{(4)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\frac{4y^{3}}{m^{4}}\delta(y-y^{\prime})\,\delta^{(2)}(z-z^{\prime})\delta(p^{2}-1)\Theta(p^{0}).

(97)

Appendix B
Near-boundary expansion for a bulk-to-boundary propagator

As stated in (
69
), a bulk-to-boundary propagator
G_{h}(y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z})
behaves like a Dirac delta function near the celestial boundary. To make the statement precise, we examine the action of
the propagator on a smooth test function
f(\vec{w})
,

I(y,\mathbb{z})=\int d^{2}w\,G_{h}(y,\mathbb{w},\mathbb{z})f(\mathbb{w}).

(98)

Using

G_{h}(y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z})=\left(\frac{y}{y^{2}+|w-z|^{2}}\right)^{h},

(99)

we shift the integration variable according to

\mathbb{w}=\mathbb{z}+y\mathbb{u}.

(100)

Therefore,

I(y,\mathbb{z})=y^{2-h}\int d^{2}u\frac{f(\mathbb{z}+y\mathbb{u})}{(1+|u|^{2})^{h}}.

(101)

Expanding the test function around
\vec{z}
gives

\displaystyle f(\mathbb{z}+y\mathbb{u})

\displaystyle=f(\mathbb{z})+yu_{i}\partial_{i}f(\mathbb{z})+\frac{y^{2}}{2}u_{i}u_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f(\mathbb{z})+\mathcal{O}(y^{3}).

(102)

Substituting into the integral, we obtain

\displaystyle I(y,\mathbb{z})

\displaystyle=y^{2-h}f(\mathbb{z})\int d^{2}u\frac{1}{(1+|u|^{2})^{h}}

(103)

\displaystyle+y^{3-h}\partial_{i}f(\mathbb{z})\int d^{2}u\frac{u_{i}}{(1+|u|^{2})^{h}}

\displaystyle+\frac{y^{4-h}}{2}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f(\mathbb{z})\int d^{2}u\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{(1+|u|^{2})^{h}}+\cdots.

The linear term vanishes by rotational symmetry,

\int d^{2}u\frac{u_{i}}{(1+|u|^{2})^{h}}=0.

(104)

The zeroth-order integral evaluates to

\int d^{2}u\frac{1}{(1+|u|^{2})^{h}}=\frac{\pi}{h-1},

(105)

while rotational invariance implies

\int d^{2}u\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{(1+|u|^{2})^{h}}=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2}\int d^{2}u\frac{|u|^{2}}{(1+|u|^{2})^{h}}=\frac{\pi\delta_{ij}}{2(h-1)(h-2)}.

(106)

Hence,

\displaystyle I(y,\mathbb{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{h-1}y^{2-h}f(\mathbb{z})

(107)

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi}{4(h-1)(h-2)}y^{4-h}\partial^{2}f(\mathbb{z})+\mathcal{O}(y^{6-h}).

Using the defining property of derivatives of the Dirac delta
function,

\int d^{2}w\,\partial^{n}\delta^{(2)}(\vec{w}-\vec{z})f(\mathbb{w})=(-1)^{n}\partial^{n}f(\mathbb{z}),

(108)

we conclude that the bulk-to-boundary propagator admits the
distributional expansion

\displaystyle G_{h}(y,\mathbb{w};\mathbb{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{h-1}y^{2-h}\delta^{(2)}(w-z)

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi}{4(h-1)(h-2)}y^{4-h}\partial^{2}\delta^{(2)}(w-z)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(y^{6-h}\partial^{4}\delta^{(2)}(w-z)\right).

(109)
