Naringin sensitizes nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells to paclitaxel by inducing AKR1C3 expression
Authors: Zhenhe Huang, Yumei Qiu, Fangchu Liu, Xiaoli Chen, Xintao Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate whether naringin enhances the chemosensitivity of nasopharyngeal carcinoma–derived CNE2 cells to paclitaxel and identify potential molecular mediators.MethodsCNE2 cells were treated with naringin alone or in combination with paclitaxel, cisplatin, or 5-fluorouracil. Cell viability, proliferation, and migration were assessed using cell counting kit-8 and Transwell assays. Transcriptomic profiling followed by bioinformatic analysis of Gene Expression Omnibus datasets (GSE53819, GSE12452, and GSE102349) was performed to identify nasopharyngeal carcinoma prognosis–related genes. AKR1C3 overexpression was established via lentiviral transduction, and pharmacological inhibition was performed using ASP9521. mRNA and protein expression were validated using reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction and Western blot analysis.ResultsNaringin (160 μM) demonstrated a trend toward reducing the half-maximal inhibitory concentration of paclitaxel from 10.52 to 8.04 nM; however, it did not significantly alter sensitivity to cisplatin or 5-fluorouracil. Combined treatment with 2 nM paclitaxel and 160 μM naringin synergistically suppressed CNE2 proliferation and migration compared with that using either agent alone (p < 0.05). Bioinformatic analysis revealed that high AKR1C3 expression was correlated with improved survival in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma (p < 0.05), whereas high PAIP1, PRKDC, PTPRR, and COL12A1 expressions were correlated with poorer outcomes. Reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction confirmed that both naringin and paclitaxel upregulated AKR1C3 mRNA, with the combination producing the strongest effect. Gain-of-function studies demonstrated that AKR1C3 overexpression significantly enhanced paclitaxel sensitivity, with half-maximal inhibitory concentration values decreasing from 13.63 to 6.994 nM in CNE2 cells and from 8.534 to 4.668 nM in CNE1 cells. Furthermore, the specific AKR1C3 inhibitor, ASP9521, significantly attenuated the synergistic anti-proliferative and anti-migratory effects of paclitaxel + naringin in CNE2 cells, confirming that naringin enhances chemosensitivity to paclitaxel by upregulating AKR1C3 expression.ConclusionsNaringin sensitizes CNE2 cells to paclitaxel, potentially via AKR1C3 upregulation. This flavonoid may represent a low-toxicity adjunct to enhance the efficacy of paclitaxel in nasopharyngeal carcinoma.Keywords: Nasopharyngeal carcinoma, naringin, paclitaxel, chemosensitization, AKR1C3

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) exhibits a distinct geographic distribution, with the highest incidence rates recorded in Southern China and Southeast Asia. 1 Although concurrent chemoradiotherapy remains the standard of care for locally advanced disease, approximately 10% of patients respond poorly to induction chemotherapy, primarily due to acquired or intrinsic chemoresistance.2,3 The taxane family—including paclitaxel (PTX) and docetaxel—forms a cornerstone of NPC treatment; however, resistance mechanisms continue to undermine clinical outcomes. 4

One emerging contributor to chemoresistance is the aldo-keto reductase (AKR) superfamily. Specifically, AKR1C3 has been implicated in drug metabolism and resistance across multiple malignancies, including hepatocellular and lung carcinomas.5–7 However, its role in NPC—particularly in the context of taxane sensitivity—remains poorly characterized.

Naringin (Nar), a flavanone glycoside abundant in citrus fruits, has attracted attention owing to its pleiotropic bioactivities. In addition to its well-documented anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidant effects, Nar demonstrates anti-proliferative activity against liver, colorectal, breast, and cervical cancer cells.8–16 Notably, recent studies have highlighted Nar's capacity to maintain bone homeostasis and prevent steroid-induced osteonecrosis through the modulation of oxidative stress pathways.17,18 These findings underscore the compound's favorable safety profile and its potential as an adjunctive anticancer agent. Critically, Nar has been shown to sensitize prostate cancer cells to PTX, raising the question of whether similar chemosensitization occurs in NPC. 19

Given the structural and functional similarities between prostate and head/neck malignancies in terms of taxane responsiveness, we hypothesized that Nar potentiates PTX activity in NPC cells. Using the CNE2 cell line—a well-characterized model of undifferentiated NPC—we aimed to achieve the following objectives: (a) determine whether Nar enhances the anti-proliferative and anti-migratory effects of PTX; (b) identify NPC prognosis–related genes through integrated transcriptomic and bioinformatic analysis; and (c) validate whether AKR1C3 mediates this chemosensitizing effect.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

The human NPC cell lines of CNE2 (RRID: CVCL_6889) and CNE1 (RRID: CVCL_6888) were obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Cells were maintained in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI)-1640 medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% carbon dioxide (CO2). All experiments were performed with cells between passages 5 and 15. Cells were confirmed to be mycoplasma-free using a mycoplasma detection kit (TransDetect® polymerase chain reaction (PCR) Mycoplasma Detection Kit) and authenticated using short tandem repeat (STR) profiling.

Lentiviral production and cell infection

The overexpression control vector (OE-NC; no-load virus control) and AKR1C3 overexpression vector (the vector overexpressing the aldo-keto reductase family 1 member C3 gene) (OE-AKR1C3, transcript No. NM 003739) were purchased from Wuhan Paivi Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Wuhan, China). To establish stable AKR1C3-overexpressing cell lines, lentiviruses were generated by co-transfecting HEK-293T cells with the packaging plasmid pSPAX2 (Addgene), the envelope plasmid pMD2.G (Addgene), and the OE-AKR1C3–encoding lentiviral vector using a polyethylenimine transfection reagent (Polysciences, USA). Viral supernatants were harvested at 48 h and 72 h post-transfection, pooled, and filtered. CNE2 and CNE1 cells were transduced with the pooled virus in the presence of 4 μg/mL polybrene (GlpBio; USA). Stable transductants were selected with puromycin (2 μg/mL; GlpBio; USA) for 7 days, and AKR1C3 overexpression was subsequently validated at both transcriptional and translational levels using reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) and Western blot analysis, respectively.

Drugs and reagents

Nar (purity ≥95%) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, Inc.; PTX and cisplatin (CDDP) were sourced from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd; and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) was obtained from MedChemExpress LLC. Nar was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) to prepare a 160-mM stock solution and was stored at −20°C. 19 PTX, CDDP, and 5-FU were prepared according to the manufacturer's instructions. In this study, the AKR1C3 inhibitor ASP9521 was dissolved in DMSO to a final concentration of 10 nM, a concentration approximating its reported half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC₅₀) value of 11 nM for human AKR1C3. 20 The final concentration of DMSO in culture medium did not exceed 0.1% (v/v), and vehicle controls contained equivalent DMSO concentrations.

Cell viability and IC50 determination

Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 3 × 103 cells/well and allowed to adhere overnight. Cells were then treated with serial dilutions of PTX (1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 nM), CDDP (1.75, 3.5, 7, 14, and 28 μM), or 5-FU (2.5, 5, 10, 20, 40 μM) either alone or in combination with 160 μM Nar. After 48 h of treatment, 10 μL of cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) solution (GlpbiO) was added to each well containing 100 μL of culture medium (final concentration 10% v/v), followed by incubation at 37°C for 2 h. 21 Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Aosun, Hangzhou). IC50 values were calculated by nonlinear regression using a four-parameter logistic model ((inhibitor) vs. normalized response—variable slope) in GraphPad Prism 9.5. Each experiment was performed in triplicate with five technical replicates per condition.

Proliferation assay

Cells were seeded as described above and treated with the following: (a) control (vehicle); (b) 2 nM PTX; (c) 160 μM Nar; or (d) 2 nM PTX + 160 μM Nar. Absorbance was measured at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-treatment. Proliferation inhibition rates were calculated as follows: ((optical density (OD) control − OD treatment)/OD control) × 100%.

Migration assay

Cells were serum-starved overnight, harvested, and resuspended in serum-free RPMI-1640 at 1 × 105 cells/mL. A 200-μL aliquot of cell suspension was seeded into the upper chamber of Transwell inserts (8.0 μm pore size, Corning) without Matrigel coating. The lower chamber contained 600 μL of complete medium (10% FBS) as a chemoattractant. After 48 h incubation, non-migrated cells on the upper surface were removed using a cotton swab. Migrated cells on the lower surface were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min, rinsed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and air-dried. Five random fields per insert were imaged using an upright light microscope (Zeiss), and migrated cells were counted manually. Each condition was tested in triplicate.

Transcriptome sequencing and analysis

CNE2 cells were treated with 160 μM Nar or vehicle control for 48 h (n = 3 biological replicates per group). Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). Library preparation, sequencing, and initial data processing were performed by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Gene expression levels were quantified as fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using DESeq2 with a threshold of |log2FC| ≥1.5 and false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed using the ‘clusterProfiler R’ package. Visualization was conducted in Cytoscape v3.7.2.

Bioinformatic analysis of public datasets

Three NPC-related gene expression datasets (GSE53819, GSE12452, and GSE102349) were downloaded from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). These datasets were selected based on the following criteria: (a) inclusion of NPC and adjacent non-tumor tissues; (b) availability of raw or normalized expression data; and (c) sample size ≥30. Differential expression analysis between tumor and normal tissues was performed using the linear models for microarray data (LIMMA) package with a threshold of |log2FC| ≥1 and FDR <0.05. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was conducted using the GSE102349 dataset, with patients stratified by the median expression of each candidate gene. Survival curves were compared using the log-rank test.

RT-qPCR

Cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 5 × 104 cells/well and treated as described above. After 48 h, total RNA was extracted using the EasyPure® Fast Cell RNA Kit (TransGen Biotech). RNA (1 μg) was reverse-transcribed using EasyScript® RT/RI Enzyme Mix (TransGen Biotech) according to the manufacturer's protocol. qPCR was performed in a 20-μL reaction volume containing 10 μL PerfectStart Green qPCR SuperMix (TransGen Biotech), 0.4 μM each primer, and 2 μL cDNA. Amplification was conducted on a CFX96TM Real-Time PCR (Bio-Rad) system using the following protocol: 95°C for 30 s; 40 cycles of 95°C for 5 s, 55°C for 15 s, and 72°C for 10 s. A melt curve analysis (65°C–95°C, 0.5°C increments) was performed to verify single-product amplification. Primer sequences are listed in Table 1. Relative mRNA expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method with glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) as the internal reference. All reactions were performed in triplicate.

Western blot analysis

Cells were seeded as described above and treated with the following: (a) control (vehicle); (b) 2 nM PTX; (c) 2 nM PTX + 160 μM Nar; or (d) 2 nM PTX + 160 μM Nar + 10 nM ASP9521. After 48 h, total proteins were extracted using Radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer supplemented with a protease inhibitor cocktail (GlpBio, USA). Protein concentrations were determined using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit (Pierce). Equal amounts of protein (30 μg per sample) were separated by 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS–PAGE) and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore; USA). Membranes were blocked using 5% nonfat milk at room temperature for 1 h, followed by overnight incubation at 4°C with primary antibodies against AKR1C3 (Cat No. 11194-1-AP, the ration of dilution of 1:2000) and β-actin (Cat No. 66009-1-lg; dilution ration, 1:5000) (both from Proteintech; Wuhan, China). After washing, membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)–conjugated secondary antibody (Proteintech; Wuhan, China) for 2 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using the SuperSignal chemiluminescence kit (Proteintech; Wuhan, China). Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software, with β-actin serving as the loading control.

Statistical analyses

Data are presented as mean ± SEM. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post hoc test. Comparisons between two groups were performed using Student's t-test (two-tailed). Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.5. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Impact of Nar in conjunction with varying concentrations of PTX, 5-FU, and CDDP on the viability of CNE2 cells

The CCK-8 assay was employed to evaluate the effects of PTX, 5-FU, and CDDP on the viability of CNE2 cells (Figure 1(a) to (c)). The IC50 values for PTX, 5-FU, and CDDP in CNE2 cells were 10.52 nM (95% confidence interval (CI): 8.91–12.43), 37.64 μM (95% CI: 32.18–44.02), and 15.12 μM (95% CI: 12.87–17.76), respectively. Co-administration with 160 μM Nar reduced the IC50 of PTX to 8.04 nM (95% CI: 6.77–9.55), representing a 23.6% reduction. No significant reduction was observed for 5-FU (36.74 μM) or CDDP (14.40 μM).

Effect of Nar combined with PTX on the proliferation capability of CNE2 cells

In this study, the IC50 value of PTX for CNE2 cells was determined to be 10.52 nM. Consequently, a concentration of 2 nM PTX was selected for subsequent experiments. Compared with the control group, the proliferation of CNE2 cells was significantly inhibited following treatment with 2 nM PTX and 160 μM Nar for 48 h and 72 h, respectively (p < 0.05). Notably, when 2 nM PTX was combined with 160 μM Nar, the inhibitory effect on CNE2 cell proliferation was more pronounced and exhibited a time-dependent trend (p < 0.05). Furthermore, at the 72 h time point, the use of PTX alone (29.31%) versus PTX + Nar (34.95%) demonstrated a 5.64% absolute increase, representing a modest but statistically significant (p < 0.05) enhancement (Figure 1(d)). These results imply that the co-administration of 2 nM PTX with 160 μM Nar enhances the suppression of CNE2 cell proliferation, indicating that Nar may augment the sensitivity of CNE2 cells to PTX.

Effect of the combination of Nar and PTX on the migration capability of CNE2 cells

Results of the Transwell assay are illustrated in Figure 1(e) and (f). Compared with the control group, the groups treated with Nar (160 μM), PTX (2 nM), and a combination of PTX (2 nM) and Nar (160 μM) exhibited significant inhibition of CNE2 cell migration (p < 0.05). Furthermore, the combined treatment with PTX (2 nM) and Nar (160 μM) produced a significantly greater inhibitory effect on CNE2 cell migration than the PTX (2 nM) group alone (p < 0.05). These findings suggest that Nar enhances the inhibitory impact of PTX on the migratory behavior of CNE2 cells.

Transcriptome results

To identify pathways potentially modulated by Nar, we performed bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) on Nar-treated CNE2 cells (Figure 2). Compared with controls, 1996 genes were upregulated and 2892 were downregulated (|log2FC| ≥ 1.5, FDR < 0.05). KEGG enrichment analysis implicated several cancer-related pathways, including cell cycle (ko04110), ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis (ko04120), and focal adhesion (ko04510). However, direct overlap between Nar-regulated DEGs and the six NPC prognosis–related genes identified bioinformatically was limited, suggesting that the effect of Nar on these candidates is indirect or context-dependent.

Results of bioinformatics analysis

The GSE53819 dataset comprises 18 NPC tissue samples and 18 adjacent normal tissue samples, whereas the GSE12452 dataset includes 31 NPC tissue samples and 10 adjacent normal tissue samples. As illustrated in Figure 3(a), the heatmap generated based on the dataset GSE53819 highlights the variations in gene expression across different samples. Red denotes high expression levels, blue signifies low expression levels, and the color intensity is directly proportional to the expression level. Principal component analysis (PCA) conducted on this dataset revealed that the cumulative variance contribution rate of the first two principal components reached 26.4%. The boundaries between the two groups of samples were distinct, with sample points within the same group being relatively concentrated, indicating minimal intra-group variability (Figure 3(b)). In total, 1938 DEGs were identified from this dataset, including 774 significantly upregulated genes and 1164 significantly downregulated genes (Figure 3(c)). The heatmap and PCA plot derived from the GSE12452 dataset demonstrated greater heterogeneity among samples. In total, 845 DEGs were detected, comprising 339 significantly upregulated genes and 506 significantly downregulated genes (Figure 3(d) to (f)).

As illustrated in Figure 4, within the GSE53819 (Figure 4(a)) and GSE12452 (Figure 4(b)) datasets, compared with that in normal tissues, the genes PAIP1, PRKDC, PTPRR, and COL12A1 exhibited significant upregulation in NPC tissues, whereas the genes NQO1 and AKR1C3 demonstrated marked downregulation. These differences were statistically significant (p < 0.05). The GSE102349 dataset encompassed whole-genome transcriptomic data from 113 untreated undifferentiated NPC tumors. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was employed to evaluate the influence of six core genes on patient survival outcomes. The findings revealed that lower expression levels of PAIP1, PRKDC, PTPRR, and COL12A1 were associated with significantly improved survival rates (p < 0.05); higher expression levels of AKR1C3 correlated with enhanced survival and better prognostic outcomes (p < 0.05); and no statistically significant correlation was observed between NQO1 expression and patient survival (p > 0.05) (Figure 5). Collectively, these results indicate that reducing the expression of PAIP1, PRKDC, PTPRR, and COL12A1 and simultaneously increasing the expression of AKR1C3, may contribute positively to the survival and prognosis of patients with NPC.

RT-qPCR verification results

We selected 6 DEGs (4 up and 2 down) for RT-qPCR validation, as illustrated in Figure 6. Compared with the control group, the mRNA levels of PAIP1 and NQO1 were significantly downregulated in the PTX group (p < 0.05), whereas the mRNA levels of PRKDC, PTPRR, COL12A1, and AKR1C3 were significantly upregulated (p < 0.05). In the Nar group, the mRNA levels of PAIP1, NQO1, and AKR1C3 were significantly upregulated (p < 0.05), whereas no significant differences were observed in the mRNA levels of PRKDC, PTPRR, and COL12A1. In the PTX + Nar group, the mRNA levels of PAIP1, PRKDC, PTPRR, COL12A1, and AKR1C3 were significantly upregulated (p < 0.05), whereas the mRNA level of NQO1 was significantly downregulated (p < 0.05). Compared with the PTX group, all mRNA levels of PAIP1, PRKDC, PTPRR, NQO1, COL12A1, and AKR1C3 were significantly upregulated in the PTX + Nar group (p < 0.05). Notably, both PTX and Nar significantly increased the mRNA level of AKR1C3 (p < 0.05) (Figure 6(f)), with the most pronounced effect observed when PTX and Nar were used in combination (p < 0.05), which is consistent with the findings from bioinformatics analysis.

Results of functional intervention studies

To investigate whether Nar enhances PTX sensitivity in CNE2 cells through AKR1C3 upregulation, CNE2 and CNE1 cell lines with stable AKR1C3 overexpression were established via lentiviral transduction. Compared with the scramble control and OE-NC groups, OE-AKR1C3 significantly upregulated the AKR1C3 mRNA expression in both cell lines (p < 0.05, Figure 7(a)). Western blot analysis further confirmed the successful overexpression of AKR1C3 protein in OE-AKR1C3–transduced CNE2 and CNE1 cells (Figure 7(b)), collectively verifying the successful generation of AKR1C3-overexpressing stable cell lines. Subsequently, these stable cell lines were employed to assess the PTX sensitivity of AKR1C3-overexpressing cells. The IC50 values of PTX were significantly reduced in AKR1C3-overexpressing cells compared with those in the OE-NC controls (Figure 7(c)), decreasing from 13.63 nM (95% CI: 10.97–18.28) to 6.994 nM (95% CI: 6.587–7.437) in CNE2 cells and from 8.534 nM (95% CI: 7.675–9.568) to 4.668 nM (95% CI: 4.253–5.128) in CNE1 cells. These results collectively demonstrate that AKR1C3 upregulation markedly enhances the sensitivity of CNE2 cells to PTX. Furthermore, at both 48 and 72 h, PTX and PTX + Nar treatments significantly inhibited CNE2 cell proliferation compared with control, and this effect was markedly enhanced by AKR1C3 overexpression. Notably, at 72 h, the combination of PTX and Nar exhibited the most pronounced inhibitory effect on proliferation in AKR1C3-overexpressing cells (Figure 7(d)). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that AKR1C3 overexpression sensitizes CNE2 cells to PTX, and Nar further potentiates this antiproliferative effect, suggesting a synergistic role of Nar and AKR1C3 upregulation in enhancing chemosensitivity to PTX.

To investigate the functional role of AKR1C3, we used its specific inhibitor ASP9521, and the results are presented in Figure 8. Compared with the control group, PTX treatment alone showed a trend toward increased AKR1C3 protein expression, whereas the combination of PTX and Nar led to a statistically significant upregulation (p < 0.05). Conversely, upon addition of ASP9521, AKR1C3 protein expression was significantly reduced (p < 0.05). The mRNA expression levels of AKR1C3 were consistent with the protein expression across all groups, confirming that ASP9521 effectively inhibits AKR1C3 expression. In the proliferation assay, no significant differences were observed among the treatment groups at 24 h. However, at 48 and 72 h, there was significant inhibition of CNE2 cell proliferation in all treatment groups compared with that in the control group (p < 0.05). Moreover, the PTX + Nar combination exhibited a significantly stronger antiproliferative effect than PTX alone (p < 0.05). Notably, this enhanced inhibitory effect was significantly attenuated upon the addition of ASP9521. In the migration assay, both PTX alone and in combination with Nar significantly suppressed CNE2 cell migration compared with the control (p < 0.05). The PTX + Nar combination showed a significantly greater inhibitory effect on cell migration than PTX alone (p < 0.05). Importantly, this anti-migratory effect was markedly weakened when ASP9521 was added. Our results demonstrate that Nar combined with PTX exerts antiproliferative and anti-migratory effects on CNE2 cells by upregulating AKR1C3 expression.

Discussion

Nar has emerged as a promising chemosensitizing agent across multiple malignancies, with demonstrated synergy with PTX in prostate cancer and emerging evidence of bone-protective effects relevant to cancer therapy.17–19 In this study, we extend these findings to NPC, demonstrating that Nar potentiates the antiproliferative and anti-migratory effects of PTX in CNE2 cells. Notably, this effect was specific to PTX as Nar did not significantly alter sensitivity to CDDP or 5-FU. This drug-class selectivity suggests that Nar interferes with taxane-specific resistance mechanisms rather than broadly modulating general chemosensitivity.

The principal molecular finding of this study—that Nar upregulates AKR1C3 mRNA and AKR1C3 protein expression and that this upregulation is enhanced by co-treatment with PTX—presents an apparent paradox. AKR1C3 is conventionally viewed as a mediator of chemoresistance, with high expression associated with poor prognosis in hepatocellular, oral squamous, and esophageal carcinomas.6,22,23 Conversely, in endometrial cancer, higher AKR1C3 expression is associated with improved survival, and our bioinformatic analysis of NPC cohorts similarly associated high AKR1C3 expression with better patient outcomes. 24 These discordant findings underscore a growing recognition that AKR1C3's role in cancer is highly context-dependent, influenced by tissue-specific metabolism, substrate availability, and co-expressed AKR family members.

This raises the question: How would AKR1C3 upregulation sensitize CNE2 cells to PTX? Several non-mutually exclusive mechanisms warrant consideration. First, AKR1C3 catalyzes the reduction of prostaglandins and steroids, potentially altering the inflammatory microenvironment in ways that modulate drug responsiveness. 5 Second, AKR1C3 has been shown to influence PARP1 stability and DNA repair capacity in hepatocellular carcinoma; whether similar mechanisms operate in NPC remains unexplored. 25

In this study, we established AKR1C3-overexpressing CNE2 and CNE1 stable cell lines via lentiviral transduction to investigate the functional role of AKR1C3 in Nar-mediated chemosensitization to PTX. Successful overexpression was confirmed at both mRNA and protein levels using RT-qPCR and Western blot analysis. Functional assays revealed that AKR1C3 overexpression significantly enhanced PTX sensitivity, with IC₅₀ values decreasing from 13.63 to 6.994 nM in CNE2 cells and from 8.534 to 4.668 nM in CNE1 cells, representing reductions of approximately 49% and 45%, respectively. These findings demonstrate that AKR1C3 upregulation alone is sufficient to sensitize NPC cells to PTX, underscoring its critical role in modulating chemosensitivity. Furthermore, proliferation assays showed that AKR1C3 overexpression markedly potentiated the antiproliferative effect of PTX at both 48 and 72 h, an effect that was further amplified by Nar co-treatment, with the most pronounced inhibition observed at 72 h in AKR1C3-overexpressing cells.

To functionally validate the role of AKR1C3 in Nar-mediated chemosensitization, we employed the specific inhibitor ASP9521. Our results showed that PTX alone induced a trend toward increased AKR1C3 protein expression, whereas the PTX–Nar combination led to a statistically significant upregulation. Importantly, ASP9521 effectively reduced AKR1C3 mRNA and AKR1C3 protein expressions. Functional assays revealed that at 48 and 72 h, the combination exhibited significantly stronger antiproliferative effects than PTX alone, and this enhanced effect was markedly attenuated by ASP9521 treatment. Similarly, the combination showed greater inhibition of cell migration, which was also reversed by AKR1C3 inhibition. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that Nar potentiates the antiproliferative and anti-migratory effects of PTX in CNE2 cells at least in part through AKR1C3 upregulation.

Collectively, these bidirectional loss- and gain-of-function data establish AKR1C3 as a critical mediator of Nar-enhanced chemosensitivity in NPC. Although the precise downstream effectors remain to be elucidated, our findings identify AKR1C3 as a potential biomarker for PTX response and support the further exploration of Nar as a chemosensitizing adjuvant in NPC therapy.

Conclusion

Nar selectively enhances the sensitivity of CNE2 cells to PTX, but not to CDDP or 5-FU, indicating a specific synergistic interaction between Nar and PTX in NPC. Functional assays confirmed that this combination synergistically suppresses NPC cell proliferation and migration. Mechanistically, we identified AKR1C3 as a critical mediator of this chemosensitizing effect. Gain-of-function studies showed that AKR1C3 overexpression significantly reduced PTX IC₅₀ values, whereas pharmacological inhibition with ASP9521 effectively abrogated the synergistic antiproliferative and anti-migratory effects, confirming that Nar acts at least in part through AKR1C3 upregulation. Notably, bioinformatic analysis revealed that high AKR1C3 expression correlates with favorable prognosis in patients with NPC, underscoring its clinical relevance as a potential prognostic biomarker. Although the precise downstream effectors remain to be fully elucidated, our findings suggest that targeting AKR1C3 or combining Nar with PTX represents a promising therapeutic strategy to improve outcomes in NPC.
