Deformation rigidity for ℤ / 2 \mathbb{Z}/2 eigensections
Andriy Haydys Université libre de Bruxelles Siqi He Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science Willem Andriaan Salm Université libre de Bruxelles

Abstract
. We prove a rigidity result for certain critical ℤ / 2 \mathbb{Z}/2 eigensections of the Laplacian on S 2 S^{2} associated to a flat real line bundle determined by a branch–point configuration. More precisely, we show that every minimal non-degenerate critical eigensection is deformation rigid: any sufficiently small deformation of the configuration that still admits a critical eigensection must come from an SO ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{SO}(3) -rotation. This generalizes the rigidity phenomenon previously discovered in symmetric examples of Taubes–Wu.



1.
Introduction

Recently, various types of
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic sections attracted a noticeable attention due to their appearance in a number of seemingly unrelated geometric problems. For example, sequences of degenerating flat
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
connections and diverging sequences of solutions of the generalized Seiberg–Witten equations

[
7
,
13
,
14
,
16
]
are related to
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic forms and spinors. Certain coassociative fibrations of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifolds in the adiabatic limit yield
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic functions
[
2
]
. Infinitesimal deformations of branched special Lagrangian submanifolds are modelled on
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic forms
[
9
]
.
All this provides a strong motivation for studying
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic sections among which
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic functions are perhaps the simplest objects to study.

A better understanding of regularity of
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic functions/forms/spinors is necessary to make a progress in the above mentioned problems such as compactifications of various gauge–theoretic moduli spaces of interest.
Indeed, the technique currently available allows us to deal effectively with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
harmonic sections whose branching locus is smooth.
While some progress has been achieved recently in dealing with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
harmonic sections with mildly singular branching loci
[
5
]
, our understanding of regularity of branching sets at present is insufficient for intended applications.
This motivates our studies of certain
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigensections of the Laplacian on the 2-sphere since they are models for singularities of
\mathbb{Z}/2
harmonic functions on 3-manifolds.

To explain some details, let
\mathbf{p}
be an unordered set of
2n
distinct points on
S^{2}
.
There is a unique flat real Euclidean line bundle
{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathbf{p}}\to S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{p}
with monodromy
-1
along each small circle encircling any point in
\mathbf{p}
.
We then obtain the Laplace operator acting on sections of
{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathbf{p}}
in the standard way. Then
u\in\Gamma({\mathcal{I}}_{\mathbf{p}})
is said to be an eigensection of the Laplacian (or a
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigenfunction) if there exists
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\Delta u=\lambda u\qquad\text{holds on }S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{p}.

We are mainly interested in the so called
critical
eigensections, which are solutions
u
satisfying the following property: near each
p\in\mathbf{p}
we have
|u(x)|\leq C\mathrm{dist}(x,p)^{3/2}
.
We refer to Section
2
for some background material and further details.

Critical eigensections were first introduced by Taubes in his foundational work on the zero sets of

\mathbb{Z}/2
-harmonic spinors
[
15
]
, albeit the terminology we use appeared later
[
1
]
. In Taubes’ work
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigensections
on
S^{2}
arise as local models governing the tangent behaviour near singular points.
This perspective was subsequently developed in a systematic way by Taubes–Wu

[
11
,
12
]
, who carried out a detailed analytic and
topological study of the associated eigenvalue problem, established the structure of
critical eigensections, and exhibited explicit families of singularity models on the sphere.
These results provide a robust framework in which critical
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigensections serve as
the fundamental local models for degenerations of
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
harmonic sections.

All critical eigensections necessarily lie in eigenspaces of dimension at least four,
a consequence of the
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-symmetry of the Laplacian and the multiplicity
phenomena described in
[
11
,
12
]
.
Motivated by this observation, we call a critical eigensection
minimal
if the
corresponding eigenspace has dimension exactly four.
The basic examples of minimal
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigensections are the Taubes–Wu
tetrahedral eigensections
[
11
]
; it is proved in

[
1
]
that these tetrahedrally symmetric solutions are
non-degenerate and deformation rigid.
Within the class of minimal eigensections, the
non-degenerate
ones—characterised by a natural
non-vanishing condition on their leading local coefficients—play a particularly
important role and appear in several geometric and topological contexts

[
3
,
8
,
10
]
.
One can think of critical, non-degenerate, minimal eigensections, which are the main players below, as ‘generic’ among critical eigensections, albeit we do not make an attempt to justify this claim.

The goal of this article is to understand the deformation theory of such eigensections.
Given a configuration
\mathbf{p}
and a critical, non-degenerate, minimal eigensection
f
,
we ask whether
f
persists under small deformations of
\mathbf{p}
, or whether it is rigid up
to the natural
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-action.
Our main result, whose proof can be found on Page
3
below, shows that the latter holds.

Theorem 1.1
.

A minimal non-degenerate critical
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
eigenfunction
f\in\Gamma\big(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}}\big)
is deformation rigid.

To explain, the deformation rigidity means that for any sufficiently small deformation
\tilde{\mathbf{p}}
of
\mathbf{p}
that still admits
a nearby critical eigensection
\tilde{f}
there exists some
R\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
such that
\tilde{\mathbf{p}}=R\,\mathbf{p}
and
\tilde{f}=f\circ R
.
This establishes a general rigidity phenomenon extending the symmetric tetrahedral models
of Taubes–Wu, and it complements recent work on the existence and structure of critical

\mathbb{Z}/2
eigenvalues

[
1
,
6
]
and recent applications
[
4
]
.

2.
Background on
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigensections

In this section we recall the basic setup for
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigensections on the sphere, which have been studied and developed in
[
15
,
11
,
12
]
.

2.1.
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigensections

Let
\mathcal{C}_{2n}
be the space of unordered configurations of
2n
distinct points on
(S^{2},g_{0})
with
g_{0}
being the round metric.
For
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{C}_{2n}
, let
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}}\to S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{p}
be the unique flat real Euclidean line bundle with monodromy
-1
around any small loop linking a single point of
\mathbf{p}
.
Let

\Delta:\Gamma(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}})\longrightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}})

denote the Laplace operator associated with the flat connection on
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}}
.

Let
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}
be the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{p};\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}})
with respect to the
energy norm

\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}}^{2}\;:=\;\int_{S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{p}}|df|^{2}.

A section
f\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}
is called a
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigensection at
\mathbf{p}
if

\Delta f\;=\;\lambda f

for some eigenvalue
\lambda\geq 0
, where the above equality is understood in the sense of distributions on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{p}
.
For a fixed eigenvalue
\lambda
, denote by

V_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})\;:=\;\{\,f\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}\;\mid\;\Delta f=\lambda f\,\}

the corresponding eigenspace and write
{\mathrm{mul}\,}\lambda:=\dim V_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})
.
Notice that we have the orthogonal decomposition

\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}=\oplus_{\lambda}V_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p}).

2.2.
Local expansions and non-degenerate eigenfunctions

Given
p\in\mathbf{p}
, let
z=re^{i\theta}
denote the complex coordinate centred at
p
obtained from the stereographic projection from
-p
.
In this coordinate the Laplace operator can be written as

\Delta=\Bigl(\frac{r^{2}+1}{2}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\,\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\Bigr).

Think of sections of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}}
on a punctured disc
0<|z|<\rho
as ‘functions’ changing the sign if one goes along a circle centred at the origin just like
\Re\sqrt{z}
does.
Then any eigensection
f\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}
admits a convergent asymptotic expansion

(1)

f(z)\sim\Re\!\left(a_{p}\,z^{1/2}+b_{p}\,z^{3/2}\right)+\mathcal{O}(r^{5/2}),

where
a_{p}
and
b_{p}
are complex numbers, well defined up to an overall sign.

Let
\pi:\Sigma_{\mathbf{p}}\to S^{2}
denote the double branched cover with branch locus
\mathbf{p}
.
If
f
is an eigensection, then its pullback

\tilde{f}:=\pi^{*}f
is an
odd
eigenfunction of the conical Laplacian of
\pi^{*}g_{S^{2}}
, where the pulled-back metric has cone angle
4\pi
at each branch point.
Moreover, near a branch point one may choose a local coordinate
w
on
\Sigma_{\mathbf{p}}
such that the covering map is given by
\pi(w)=w^{2}=z
.
A choice of such local coordinates on
\Sigma_{\mathbf{p}}
fixes the signs of the coefficients
a_{p}
and
b_{p}
.
Throughout the remainder of this article, we make this choice and, hence, consider
a_{p}
and
b_{p}
as well-defined complex numbers.
Notice that for any configuration
\tilde{\mathbf{p}}
sufficiently close to
\mathbf{p}
, the corresponding coordinate
\tilde{w}
on
\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathbf{p}}}
is canonically defined.

It is convenient to choose an order on
\mathbf{p}
so that we can write
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{2n})
.
For any finite sum
f\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}
of eigensections we have well-defined coefficients
a_{p_{i}}
and
b_{p_{i}}
as in (
1
).
We say that the vector

\mathrm{Tr}(f):=(a_{p_{1}},\ldots,a_{p_{2n}})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}

encoding the lowest order terms is
the trace
of
f
.

Definition 2.1
.

An eigensection
f
is said to be
critical
if
\mathrm{Tr}(f)=0
.
An eigenspace
V_{\lambda}
(or the eigenvalue
\lambda
) is called
critical
if it contains a critical eigensection.
Furthermore, a critical eigensection
f
is said to be
non-degenerate
, if
b_{p_{i}}\neq 0
for all
i
.

2.3.
Deformations of
\mathbb{Z}/2
eigenvalues

For a fixed configuration
\mathbf{p}
, the spectrum of the Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}
is discrete. However, understanding the dependence of this spectrum on
\mathbf{p}
is crucial for the proof of our main result.
We begin with the following.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
12
, Proposition 2.5, Proposition 2.6]

Fix some
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{C}_{2n}
and let
f\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}
be an eigensection of the Laplacian corresponding to an eigenvalue
\lambda(\mathbf{p})
. For
p\in\mathbf{p}
, denote by
a_{p}(f)
the leading coefficient of
f
near
p
as above.

If
\mathrm{mul}\,\lambda(\mathbf{p})=1
, then there is a neighbourhood
U
of
\mathbf{p}
in
\mathcal{C}_{2n}
, such that there is a smooth function
\lambda(-)
defined on
U
with the following properties:

(1)

The value of
\lambda{(-)}
at
\mathbf{p}
is
\lambda(\mathbf{p})
;

(2)

If
\mathbf{q}
is contained in
U
, then
\lambda{(\mathbf{q})}
is an eigenvalue of the Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{q}}
with
\mathrm{mul}\,\lambda{(\mathbf{q})}=1
;

(3)

If
\mathbf{v}
is a tangent vector of
\mathcal{C}_{2n}
at
\mathbf{p}
, then the directional derivative of
\lambda{(-)}
along
\mathbf{v}
is
\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{p\in\mathbf{p}_{f}}\Re\,(v(p)a_{p}(f)^{2})
, where
f
denotes an eigensection corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda(\mathbf{p})
with
\|f\|_{L^{2}}=1
.

If
N:=\mathrm{mul}\,\lambda(\mathbf{p})>1
, then there exist a neighbourhood
U
of
\mathbf{p}
and a set of
N
continuous functions
\mu_{i}(-)(i=1,\cdots,N)
defined on
U
with the following properties:

(1)

\mu_{i}(\mathbf{p})=\lambda(\mathbf{p})
for
i=1,\cdots,N
;

(2)

If
\mathbf{q}\in U
, then each
\mu_{i}(\mathbf{q})
is an eigenvalue of the Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{q}}
;

(3)

For each tangent vector
\mathbf{v}
at
\mathbf{p}
consider the symmetric bilinear form on the eigenspace
V_{\lambda(\mathbf{p})}
defined by

(2)

\displaystyle B_{\mathbf{v}}(f,f^{\prime}):=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{p\in\mathbf{p}_{f}\cap\mathbf{p}_{f^{\prime}}}\Re\,\big(v(p)a_{p}(f)a_{p}(f^{\prime})\big).

Denote by
\eta_{1}(\mathbf{v}),\cdots,\eta_{N}(\mathbf{v})
the eigenvalues of this bilinear form and chose an
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis
\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{N}\}
of
V_{\lambda(\mathbf{p})}
such that
B_{\mathbf{v}}(f_{i},f_{j})=\eta_{i}(\mathbf{v})\delta_{ij}
. If
\mathbf{p}(t)
is a smooth path of configurations such that
\mathbf{p}(0)=\mathbf{p}
and
\dot{\mathbf{p}}(0)=\mathbf{v}
, then for
t
small, there exist
L^{2}
normalized eigensections
f_{i,t}
with eigenvalues
\mu_{i}(\mathbf{p}(t))
such that

(3)

f_{i,t}=f_{i}+\mathcal{O}(t),\qquad\mu_{i}(t)=\lambda(\mathbf{p})+t\eta_{i}(\mathbf{v})+o(t).

Consider
S^{2}
as a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and denote by
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
its standard coordinates.
The vector fields on
S^{2}
given by

(4)

\begin{split}L_{1}=x_{2}\,\partial_{x_{3}}-x_{3}\,\partial_{x_{2}},\qquad L_{2}=x_{3}\,\partial_{x_{1}}-x_{1}\,\partial_{x_{3}},\qquad L_{3}=x_{1}\,\partial_{x_{2}}-x_{2}\,\partial_{x_{1}}\end{split}

represent the infinitesimal generators of the
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-action.
Using the flat structure on
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}}
, each
L_{i}
acts on sections and defines a linear map

L_{i}:\Gamma(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}})\to\Gamma(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}}),

satisfying
[\Delta,L_{i}]=0
.
Therefore, if
f\in V_{\lambda}
is a critical eigensection, then
L_{i}f
is also an eigensection corresponding to the same eigenvalue
\lambda
so that a critical eigenvalue is never simple.
More precisely, we have the following result:

Proposition 2.3

(
[
1
, Proposition 4.5, Theorem 1.5]
,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{C}_{2n}
with
2n\geq 2
, and let
f\in V_{\lambda}
be a critical eigensection.
Then the eigensections

f,\;L_{1}f,\;L_{2}f,\;L_{3}f

are linearly independent.
In particular,
\mathrm{mul}\,V_{\lambda}\geq 4
.

This proposition motivates the following.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a critical eigensection
f\in V_{\lambda}
is
minimal
if
\dim V_{\lambda}=4
.

By
[
1
, Section 5.3]
, Taubes–Wu tetrahedral eigensections found in
[
11
]
are minimal.

3.
Deformation rigidity

In this section, we will prove that critical eigensections are deformation-rigid under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
.

First observe that the standard action of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
on
S^{2}
yields an action on
\mathcal{C}_{2n}
. For
R\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
, we therefore obtain a map
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbf{p}}\to\mathbb{H}_{R\cdot\mathbf{p}}
,
(R\cdot f)(x)=f(R^{-1}x)
, which maps each
V_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})
isomorphically onto
V_{\lambda}(R\cdot\mathbf{p})
.

Definition 3.1
.

A critical eigensection
f_{0}\in V_{\lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{p}_{0})
is called
deformation rigid
if there exist a neighbourhood
U\subset\mathcal{C}_{2n}
of
\mathbf{p}_{0}
and a constant
\epsilon
with the following property: If for some
\mathbf{p}\in U
there exists a critical eigensection
f\in V_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})
with
\lambda\in(\lambda_{0}-\epsilon,\lambda_{0}+\epsilon)
, then there exists
R\in SO(3)
such that
R\cdot\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}_{0}
and
R\cdot f=f_{0}
.

Let
\mathbf{p}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{2n}
be a configuration with
2n\geq 4
and let
f_{0}\in V_{\lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{p}_{0})
be a critical eigensection of unit
L^{2}
-norm.
In what follows, we study the deformation rigidity of
f_{0}
when
f_{0}
is non-degenerate and minimal.

Remark
.

If
\mathbf{p}_{0}
is the Taubes–Wu tetrahedral configuration and
V_{\lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{p}_{0})
is a critical eigenspace, then according to
[
1
, Section 5]
there exists a critical eigensection
f\in V_{\lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{p}_{0})
such that

V_{\lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{p}_{0})=\mathrm{span}\{f,L_{1}f,L_{2}f,L_{3}f\}.

Moreover, it is proved in
[
1
]
that every Taubes–Wu tetrahedral eigensection is non-degenerate and rigid.

Given
\mathbf{p}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{2n}
, we choose an order on
\mathbf{p}_{0}
as in the preceding section and write

\mathbf{p}_{0}=(p_{1,0},\ldots,p_{2n,0}).

Let
U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
be a neighbourhood of
\mathbf{p}_{0}
consisting of small disjoint geodesic balls centred at each
p_{j,0}
.
For any
\mathbf{p}\in U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
, choose a diffeomorphism

\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}:S^{2}\to S^{2}

depending smoothly on
\mathbf{p}
, such that
\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}(\mathbf{p}_{0})=\mathbf{p}
,
\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}(U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}})\subset U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
and
\varphi_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}=\mathrm{Id}
.
Notice that any
p\in U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
inherits an order so that we can write
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{2n})
, where
p_{i}
lies in the geodesic ball centred at
p_{i,0}
.

Since
2n>2
, there exist two points—without loss of generality
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
—such that
p_{1}\neq-p_{2}
. We can choose a unique rotation
R\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
so that
R\cdot p_{1}=(0,0,1)

(the north pole) and
R\cdot p_{2}\in\{y=0,\ x>0\}
.
This yields in particular that the
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-action on
U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
is free, and hence
\mathcal{C}_{2n}/\mathrm{SO}(3)

is a smooth manifold near
[\mathbf{p}_{0}]
.

By Proposition
2.2
, there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that for each
\mathbf{p}\in U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
the vector space

V(\mathbf{p}):=\bigoplus_{|\lambda-\lambda_{0}|<\epsilon}V_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})

is of dimension
4
.
Moreover, this yields a trivial rank
4
vector bundle over
U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
.

For each
\mathbf{p}\in U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
, elements of
\varphi_{\mathbf{p}}^{*}V(\mathbf{p})
are sections of
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
.
Define the following linear functionals

(5)

\chi_{j}:V(\mathbf{p})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\chi_{j}(f):=\langle L_{j}f_{0},\ \varphi_{\mathbf{p}}^{*}f\rangle_{L^{2}}

and set

L:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{3}\ker\chi_{j}.

Recalling that the minimality of
f_{0}
implies linear independence of
L_{1}f_{0},L_{2}f_{0},L_{3}f_{0}
, we obtain that
\chi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3})\colon V(p)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
has full rank at
\mathbf{p}_{0}
and therefore also for all
\mathbf{p}
sufficiently close to
\mathbf{p}_{0}
.
We conclude that
L
is a real line bundle over
U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
such that
f_{0}\in L_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
.

Since
L
inherits an Euclidean scalar product, there is a unique section
\mathbf{p}\mapsto f_{\mathbf{p}}\in L|_{\mathbf{p}}
satisfying

\|f_{\mathbf{p}}\|_{L^{2}}=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}=f_{0}

so that the map

(6)

\hat{\Theta}\colon U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}\subset(S^{2})^{2n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2n},\qquad\hat{\Theta}(\mathbf{p}):=\mathrm{Tr}(f_{\mathbf{p}})

is well-defined.
Write
\mathrm{Tr}(f_{\mathbf{p}})=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{2n})
, where
a_{i}=a_{i}(\mathbf{p})
is the leading coefficient of
f_{\mathbf{p}}
at
p_{i}
. Observe that the construction implies that
\hat{\Theta}
is
\mathrm{SO}(3)
-invariant in the following sense: if
\mathbf{p}\in U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
and
R\in SO(3)
is such that
R\cdot\mathbf{p}\in U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
, then
\hat{\Theta}(R\cdot\mathbf{p})=\hat{\Theta}(\mathbf{p})
.

Let
H_{1}
denote the infinitesimal action of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
at
\mathbf{p}_{0}
, i.e.,

(7)

H_{1}:=\mathrm{span}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(g_{t}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{0})\;:\;g_{t}\in SO(3)\right\}\subset T_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}(S^{2})^{2n}.

Clearly, we have an isomorphism

T_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}\big(U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}/SO(3)\big)\cong T_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}\big(S^{2\,}\big)^{2n}/H_{1}.

Also, define

H_{2}(\mathbf{p}):=\mathrm{span}\big\{\mathrm{Tr}(f)\mid f\in L_{\mathbf{p}}^{\perp}\subset V(\mathbf{p})\big\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2n},

and note that for any
R\in SO(3)
we have
H_{2}(R\cdot\mathbf{p})=H_{2}(\mathbf{p})
. In particular, we have

(8)

H_{2}(\mathbf{p}_{0})=\mathrm{span}\{\mathrm{Tr}(L_{1}f_{0}),\,\mathrm{Tr}(L_{2}f_{0}),\,\mathrm{Tr}(L_{3}f_{0})\}.

A direct verification yields
\dim H_{2}(\mathbf{p}_{0})=3
and Proposition
2.2
implies that
\dim H_{2}(\mathbf{p})=3
for all
\mathbf{p}
sufficiently close to
\mathbf{p}_{0}
.
Thus, we obtain a real vector bundle
\mathcal{H}
over
U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}/SO(3)
defined by
\mathcal{H}|_{[\mathbf{p}]}:=H_{2}(\mathbf{p})
.
By construction,
\mathcal{H}
is a subbundle of the product bundle
\mathcal{E}:=U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}/SO(3)\times\mathbb{C}^{2n}
and we denote by
\mathcal{Q}
the quotient bundle:

\mathcal{Q}:=\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{H}.

Observe that the product connection on
\mathcal{E}
induces a connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{Q}
.

Next we define a section

\Theta:U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}/SO(3)\to\mathcal{Q}\qquad\text{by setting}\qquad\Theta([\mathbf{p}]):=[\hat{\Theta}(\mathbf{p})].

In particular, we have
\Theta([\mathbf{p}_{0}])=0
. Let

(9)

\nabla\Theta_{[\mathbf{p}_{0}]}:\;T_{[\mathbf{p}_{0}]}(U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}/SO(3))\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{[\mathbf{p}_{0}]}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2n}/H_{2}(\mathbf{p}_{0})

denote the covariant derivative of
\Theta
at
\mathbf{p}_{0}
.

Let
\mathbf{p}_{t}=(p_{1,t},\ldots,p_{2n,t})
be a unique geodesic connecting
\mathbf{p}_{0}
and
\mathbf{p}
.
Denote by
\mathbf{v}
its tangent vector, i.e.,

\mathbf{v}:=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\mathbf{p}_{t}\in T_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}(S^{2})^{2n}.

Proposition 3.2
.

The derivative of
\Theta
at
\mathbf{p}_{0}
in the direction of
\mathbf{v}
is

(10)

\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}\Theta_{[\mathbf{p}_{0}]}=\frac{3}{2}\,\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{v}\pmod{H_{2}(\mathbf{p}_{0})}.

Proof.

We compute the derivative of
\hat{\Theta}
in the direction of
\mathbf{v}
first. Rewriting
\varphi_{t}:=\varphi_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}
for convenience, we obtain a family of diffeomorphisms satisfying
\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{p}_{0})=\mathbf{p}_{t}
. We thus wish to compute

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\mathrm{Tr}(f_{t}),\qquad\text{where }\quad f_{t}:=\varphi_{t}^{*}(f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}})\in\Gamma(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}).

Recall that
f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}
is a section of rank
4
vector bundle
V\to U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
spanned by eigensections and therefore can be written as
f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}=\sum_{j=1}^{4}f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}^{j}
, where each component
f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}^{j}
satisfies

(11)

\Delta_{g_{0}}f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}^{j}=\lambda_{t}^{j}f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}^{j}.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
f_{0}^{1}=f_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}=f_{0}
is the initial critical
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
eigenfunction and
f_{0}^{j}=0
for
j=2,3,4
.

Pulling (
11
) back by
\varphi_{t}
, we obtain

(12)

\Delta_{\varphi_{t}^{*}g_{0}}(\varphi_{t}^{*}f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}^{j})=\lambda_{t}^{j}(\varphi_{t}^{*}f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}^{j})\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\Delta_{g_{t}}f_{t}^{j}=\lambda_{t}f_{t}^{j},

where we denote
g_{t}:=\varphi_{t}^{*}g_{0}
and
f_{t}^{j}:=\varphi_{t}^{*}f_{\mathbf{p}_{t}}^{j}
.
By
[
3
, Section 5.1]
, the derivative of the family
\Delta_{t}
can be written in the following form:

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\Delta_{g_{t}}h=\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}(\Delta_{g_{0}}h)-\Delta_{g_{0}}(\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}h)\qquad\text{for any section }h.

Since
\lambda_{t}
is differentiable at
t=0
by Proposition
2.2
, differentiating (
12
) at
t=0
yields

\Delta_{g_{0}}(\dot{f}_{0}^{j}-\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}^{j})=\lambda_{0}(\dot{f}_{0}^{j}-\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}^{j})+\dot{\lambda}_{0}^{j}\,f_{0}^{j}.

Notice that the value of
\lambda_{t}^{j}
at
t=0
does not depend on
j
.

Furthermore, near each singular point we have the expansion
f_{0}\sim|z|^{3/2}
, and hence

\dot{f}_{0}-\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}\sim|z|^{1/2}.

Integrating by parts, we obtain

\int_{S^{2}}\langle(\Delta-\lambda_{0})(\dot{f}_{0}-\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}),\,f_{0}\rangle=\int_{S^{2}}\langle(\dot{f}_{0}-\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}),\,(\Delta-\lambda_{0})f_{0}\rangle=0.

Since the left hand side of the above equality equals
\dot{\lambda}_{0}^{1}\|f_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
and
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=1
, it follows that
\dot{\lambda}_{0}^{1}=0
. Therefore, we obtain that the equation

\Delta(\dot{f}_{0}^{j}-\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}^{j})=\lambda_{0}(\dot{f}_{0}^{j}-\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}^{j})

holds for any
j=1,2,3,4
, which yields
\dot{f_{0}}-\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}\in V_{\lambda_{0}}
.
Since
\mathrm{Tr}(h)\in H_{2}
for all
h\in V_{\lambda_{0}}
, we conclude

\mathrm{Tr}(\dot{f_{0}}-\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0})\in H_{2}.

Furthermore, if
z_{j}
is the local coordinate centred at
p_{j,0}
, then the expansion of
f_{0}
near
p_{j,0}
takes the form

f_{0}\sim\Re\!\left(b_{j}\,z_{j}^{3/2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\big(|z_{j}|^{5/2}\big),

so that we obtain a vector
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{2n})
with all components being non-zero.

For a tangent vector
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{2n})
, in a neighbourhood of
p_{j,0}
we have

\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}=\Re\!\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\,b_{j}\cdot v_{j}\,z_{j}^{1/2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\big(|z_{j}|^{3/2}\big).

Therefore,

\mathrm{Tr}(\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0})=\tfrac{3}{2}\,\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{v}:=\tfrac{3}{2}\big(b_{1}\cdot v_{1},\ldots,b_{2n}\cdot v_{2n}\big).

Consequently,

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\mathrm{Tr}(f_{t})=\mathrm{Tr}(\dot{f}_{0})\equiv\mathrm{Tr}\big(\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{0}\big)=\tfrac{3}{2}\,\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{v}\quad\pmod{H_{2}}.

We conclude that the covariant derivative of
\Theta
is given by (
10
) as claimed.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

\Theta
is transverse to the zero section.

Proof.

To prove that (
9
) is an isomorphism, first observe that the linear map

\Gamma:T_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}(S^{2})^{2n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2n},\qquad\Gamma(\mathbf{v}):=\frac{3}{2}\,\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{v}

is an isomorphism because
f_{0}
is non-degenerate. Furthermore, the geometric origin
of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
implies that the variation of the trace along a
rotational vector field corresponds exactly to the trace of the
rotationally generated eigensection; explicitly, we have

\Gamma(H_{1})=H_{2}
.
Therefore,
\Gamma
descends to a well-defined isomorphism of the quotient
spaces.
It remains to notice that this induced map between quotient spaces coincides with
\nabla\Theta_{[\mathbf{p}_{0}]}
by Proposition
3.2
.
∎

We can now prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Corollary
3.3
, shrinking
U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}
if
necessary, we may assume that the image of
\Theta([\mathbf{p}])
in

\mathbb{C}^{2n}/H_{2}(\mathbf{p})
is non-zero for all

\mathbf{p}\in U_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}\setminus\{\mathbf{p}_{0}\}
.

Furthermore, recall that we have the splitting
V({\mathbf{p}})=L_{\mathbf{p}}\oplus L_{\mathbf{p}}^{\perp}
satisfying the following properties:

\mathrm{Ker}\,\mathrm{Tr}|_{V(\mathbf{p}_{0})}=L_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}=\mathbb{R}\,f_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{Tr}|_{L_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}^{\perp}}\colon L_{\mathbf{p}_{0}}^{\perp}\to H_{2}(\mathbf{p}_{0})\ \text{is an isomorphism}.

Therefore, for any

f=f_{1}+f_{2}\in L_{\mathbf{p}}\oplus L_{\mathbf{p}}^{\perp}=V({\mathbf{p}})
, with

f_{1}\in L_{\mathbf{p}}
and
f_{2}\in L_{\mathbf{p}}^{\perp}
, we have

\mathrm{Tr}(f_{1})\neq 0\mod{H_{2}(\mathbf{p})}.

Since
\mathrm{Tr}(f_{2})\in H_{2}(\mathbf{p})
, we conclude that
\mathrm{Tr}(f)\neq 0
.
Thus no non-zero eigensection in
V({\mathbf{p}})
has vanishing trace, and
therefore
f_{0}
is deformation rigid.
∎
