Every Feedforward Neural Network Definable in an o-Minimal Structure Has Finite Sample Complexity
Anastasis Kratsios, Gregory Cousins, Haitz Sáez de Ocáriz Borde, Bum Jun Kim, Simone Brugiapaglia

Abstract
We show that, in a precise sense, a broad class of feedforward neural networks learn (have finite sample complexity) in the PAC model: every fixed finite feedforward architecture whose layers are definable in an o-minimal structure has finite sample complexity in the agnostic PAC setting, even with unbounded parameters. This covers standard fixed-size MLPs, CNNs, GNNs, and transformers with fixed sequence length, together with the operations and layers typically used in such architectures, including linear projections, residual connections, attention mechanisms, pooling layers, normalization layers, and admissible positional encodings. Hence, distribution-free learnability for modern non-recurrent architectures is not an exceptional property of particular activations or architecture-specific VC arguments, but a consequence of tame feedforward computation. Our results reposition finite-sample PAC learnability as a baseline rather than a differentiator: they shift the focus of architectural comparison toward inductive biases, symmetries and geometric priors, scalability, and optimization behaviour.



1
Introduction

Modern neural architectures appear strikingly diverse, ranging from classical multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) to graph neural networks (GNNs) and contemporary transformers. Each major architectural innovation introduces new layers, symmetries, and “basic computational units,” and has, in turn, sparked a corresponding line of architecture-specific statistical theory proving that the resulting model class can learn. Examples include guarantees for piecewise-linear MLPs
[
12
]
, message-passing GNNs
[
36
,
65
]
, and transformer architectures
[
32
,
82
,
59
]
.

From the viewpoint of computation, however, these architectures share a common form. At inference time, they are finite feedforward programs: directed acyclic computational graphs whose nodes execute elementary parametrized operations; cf. circuit-complexity theory
[
48
,
49
,
62
]
. This observation suggests a simple question.

Do all standard fixed finite feedforward neural networks have finite sample complexity?

We answer this question
affirmatively
for the broad class of feedforward architectures whose primitive operations are definable in an o-minimal structure. In simple terms:
modern artificial neural networks are mathematically well-behaved enough to be theoretically learnable.
More precisely, we prove that every fixed finite feedforward architecture assembled from definable layers/gates and arranged in a directed acyclic computational graph has finite agnostic PAC sample complexity for both classification (Theorem
3.1
) and regression (Theorem
3.2
). This holds even when their parameter space is unbounded.

Throughout the paper, a “fixed network” or “fixed architecture” means that the computational graph, input and output dimensions, widths, depths, sequence lengths, and the number and placement of trainable parameters are fixed. The numerical values of the parameters themselves are not fixed; they are allowed to vary over their prescribed definable parameter spaces (free to vary during training).

We verify that the definability assumption is mild by checking it for the standard fixed finite building blocks of MLPs, CNNs, GNNs, and transformers, including common activations, convolution, attention, normalization, pooling, and admissible positional encodings.
Here, learnability is statistical and distribution-free: finite uniform-convergence, equivalently finite agnostic PAC sample complexity via ERM. We do not claim efficient ERM or any optimization guarantee.

Takeaways.

Our results complement the ongoing work in the foundations of statistical learning theory seeking to characterize the exact conditions under which PAC learning is possible by uncovering sharp learning-theoretic dimensions; see, e.g.,
[

,

]
.
Here, we demonstrate that most architectures satisfy the well-known necessary conditions of finite fat-shattering and finite VC dimension, whose sufficiency dates back at least to

]
.
Thus, our
theoretical takeaway
is a simple, virtually always guaranteed

result under a unified framework, relying on clarified connections between fat-shattering dimension

[

]
, particularly model theory
[

,
25
,

]
.

The
practical takeaway
is that finite sample complexity is too coarse to distinguish standard feedforward architectures. Once a model is fixed, finite, feedforward, and built from definable primitives, distribution-free learnability follows automatically. Thus, model selection should be guided by finer criteria: problem-specific inductive bias, symmetry and geometric priors, as studied in geometric deep learning

Background and Preliminaries

One of the most important bridges between mathematical logic and learning theory is shattering. In PAC learning, finite VC dimension is the fundamental combinatorial certificate for distribution-free learnability
[
16
,
4
]
; in model theory, the same phenomenon appears as the independence property for uniformly definable families
[
56
,
20
]
. Early neural-network theory already exploited this connection: tame real geometry was used to prove finiteness results for sigmoidal networks
[
64
]
, polynomial VC bounds for Pfaffian networks
[
51
]
, and general bounds for real-parametrized concept classes generated by elementary operations
[
39
]
; see also
[
11
]
. Our work revisits this logic-learning bridge in the language of modern architectures: a fixed feedforward network is a finite composition of parametrized gates, so joint definability of the gates propagates to the whole model, while o-minimal tameness rules out unbounded shattering. Thus, model theory supplies a clean statistical certificate: definable feedforward computation has finite sample complexity.

Background in Mathematical Logic: O-Minimality

We now review the background required to formulate our results. Standard learning-theoretic notions used later, such as VC dimension and pseudo-dimension, are recalled in Appendix

Definition 2.2

(O-Minimal Structure Expanding the Real Field)

A

(ii)

.

(iii)

If

(iv)

The elements of

-definable subsets

is a finite union of points and intervals
.

Example 1

(Semi-Algebraic + (Global) Exponential)
.

The real exponential field
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}(\mathbb{R},<,+,\cdot,0,1,\exp)
is o-minimal by
[

]
.

More broadly, we may add in all restricted analytic functions.

Example 2

(Restricted Analytic Functions + (Global) Exponentials)
.

The structure
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp}
, obtained by adding all restricted analytic functions and the global exponential map, is also o-minimal and contains the standard analytic operations used in softmax-type layers by
[
86
]
.

Furthermore, we may add in Pfaffian functions and still preserve o-minimality. Briefly, Pfaffian functions form a robust class of tame functions: their graphs may bend, grow, and interact non-linearly, but cannot oscillate with unrestricted complexity (cf. Appendix
A.1
for a precise definition of Pfaffianity).
The implication of learnability for neural networks with Pfaffian activation functions was considered in
[
51
]
and more recently for GNNs in
[
30
]
.

Example 3

(Pfaffian Closures)
.

The Pfaffian closure of an o-minimal structure is obtained by adjoining the tame solution sets of definable Pfaffian differential systems. More precisely, the Pfaffian closure
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S})
of any o-minimal expansion
\mathcal{S}
of the real field is again o-minimal, and contains the Pfaffian functions generated from
\mathcal{S}
-definable
C^{1}
data
[
75
]
.

A function is well-behaved, in the o-minimal sense, if its graph is definable in the ambient structure.

Definition 2.3

(Definable Map)
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
A map

f:A\to\mathbb{R}^{p}

is called
definable
if its graph

\Gamma_{f}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\;\{(x,f(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{n+p}:x\in A\}

is a definable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
.

In our examples section, Section
4
, we show that most neural networks are definable in the o-minimal structures above.
We do so by verifying that the standard components used in deep learning are definable therein, and then using the fact that compositions of definable maps are again definable.
Definable functions enjoy several closure properties: their linear combinations, coordinate-wise concatenations, and compositions are all again definable in
\mathcal{S}
; cf.
[
25
, Chapter 1.3]
.

The core of this work is to show that, if a model family is

Intuition of Proofs: What Definability Does.

The main results are derived in three steps. First, Proposition
2.5
shows that any
\mathbb{G}
-neural network is jointly definable in its parameters and inputs; whence the composite map

((x,y),\theta)\mapsto I(I_{(0,\infty)}\circ f_{\theta}(x)=y)

(resp.
((x,y),\theta)\mapsto\ell(f_{\theta}(x),y)
in the regression case)
is jointly definable in
(x,y,\theta)
. Next, we use this to show that this composite map has a bounded number of osculations at any scale, implying that
I_{(0,\infty)}\circ f_{\theta}
(resp.
f_{\theta}
) cannot (resp. fat-)shatter arbitrarily many points at any scale; whence the family must have a finite VC (resp. fat-shattering) dimension as the parameter
\theta

Finally, we use classical results of
[
84
,
16
]
(resp.
[
2
]
) to deduce finite sample-complexity bounds in the classification (resp. regression) case.

Scope and Limitations.

Our result is qualitative and statistical. It applies to each fixed finite feedforward architecture, with fixed input dimension, fixed depth, fixed width, fixed graph size, and fixed sequence length. It does not provide rate-sharp constants (which are necessarily architecture-specific), efficient ERM algorithms, optimization guarantees, or bounds uniform over growing model size.
This excludes
genuinely

recurrent architectures
, such as RNNs
[
33
,
45
]
, and recurrent/looped transformers
[
28
,
37
]
, which are covered in our theory only by unrolling their iterates up to a fixed
time horizon
.
Thus, the result should be read as a universal finite-sample-complexity certificate for fixed tame feedforward computation, not as a complete theory of trainability or scaling.

4.1
Neural-Network Building Blocks

Affine and Polynomial Maps

The affine functions core to classical MLPs (linear layers) and CNNs (convolutional kernels) are
quadratic polynomial
functions jointly of their inputs and parameters, i.e.,
(A,b,x)\mapsto Ax+b
,
are among the simplest examples of definable maps; more generally, the same is
true of polynomial maps, which cover polynomial feature maps (e.g.,

x\mapsto(x_{i}x_{j})_{i,j}
or all monomials up to a fixed degree) and polynomial
activations (e.g.,
t\mapsto t^{2}
or
t\mapsto t^{3}
). Since the graph of a polynomial map is defined by polynomial equations, polynomial maps are semi-algebraic and hence definable in the real field.

Proposition 4.1

(Definability of Polynomial Maps)
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, and let

P:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}

be a polynomial map. Then
P
is semi-algebraic. In particular,
P
is definable in the real field
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,<)
, and hence in every o-minimal expansion of the real field.

Corollary 4.2

(Definability of Linear Layers in MLPs)
.

Fix
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. The affine layer
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m},L(x,A,b)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}Ax+b,
is a polynomial map jointly in
(x,A,b)
. In particular, it is semi-algebraic and definable in the real field
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,<)
, and hence in every o-minimal expansion of the real field.

Corollary 4.3

(Definability of Convolutional Layers in CNNs)
.

Fix
n,m,p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Let

T:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

be the linear map that sends a vector of convolutional kernel parameters to the corresponding Toeplitz-type (or doubly block Toeplitz) matrix. The layer
\operatorname{Conv}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m},\operatorname{Conv}(x,\theta,b)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}T(\theta)x+b,

is a polynomial map jointly in
(x,\theta,b)
. It is semi-algebraic and definable in the real field
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,<)
, and in every o-minimal expansion of the real field.

5
Conclusion

We showed that distribution-free generalization is a universal consequence of tame feedforward computation. More precisely, every fixed finite feedforward architecture (numerical values of the parameters themselves are not fixed: they are trainable) whose layers are definable in an o-minimal structure has finite sample complexity in the agnostic PAC setting for classification (Theorem
3.1
) and for bounded definable regression losses (Theorem
3.2
), even when its parameters are unbounded. This covers the standard non-recurrent architectures used in modern deep learning, including MLPs, CNNs, finite-size GNNs, and transformers, to name a few. The main conceptual consequence is that PAC learnability is
not
a
fragile property
requiring architecture-specific VC arguments, nor is it tied to particular activations or parameter bounds. Instead, it follows automatically from the definable structure shared by essentially all finite feedforward models used in practice. This shifts the role of generalization theory: for modern non-recurrent architectures, finite sample complexity should be viewed as a baseline property, not as the feature that distinguishes one architecture from another.

The Practical Takeaway.

Accordingly, the meaningful differences between architectures must be sought elsewhere: in their inductive biases, symmetries and geometric prior, optimization geometry, scalability, and computational efficiency. Therefore o-minimality provides a unifying language for separating what is generic about deep learning from what is genuinely architecture-specific.

A.1
Pfaffian Functions: A Smooth Tame Class

Pfaffian functions, introduced by Khovanskii in his theory of fewnomials
[
53
]
, form a robust class of real-analytic functions whose derivatives satisfy triangular polynomial differential equations. They provide a smooth analogue of semi-algebraic functions: their format controls their algebraic-differential complexity, while the main result of
[
75
]
ensures that adjoining Pfaffian solutions to an o-minimal structure preserves o-minimality. Thus Pfaffian functions are both analytically regular and model-theoretically tame.

Definition A.1

(Pfaffian Chain)
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty open domain. A Pfaffian chain of length
q
and degree
D
on
U
is a sequence of real-analytic functions

f_{1},\dots,f_{q}:U\to\mathbb{R}

such that, for every
1\leq i\leq q
and every
1\leq j\leq n
, there exists a polynomial

P_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{i}]

of degree at most
D
satisfying

\frac{\partial f_{i}(\bar{x})}{\partial x_{j}}=P_{i,j}\big(\bar{x},f_{1}(\bar{x}),\dots,f_{i}(\bar{x})\big),\qquad\bar{x}\in U.

Definition A.2

(Pfaffian Function of Format
(q,D,d)
)
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty open domain. A function
g:U\to\mathbb{R}
is Pfaffian of format
(q,D,d)
on
U
if there exist a Pfaffian chain

f_{1},\dots,f_{q}:U\to\mathbb{R}

of length (or order)
q
and degree
D
, and a polynomial

Q\in\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{q}]

of degree at most
d
, such that

g(\bar{x})=Q\big(\bar{x},f_{1}(\bar{x}),\dots,f_{q}(\bar{x})\big),\qquad\bar{x}\in U.

By convention, if
F:U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then
F
is Pfaffian of format at most
(q,D,d)
on
U
if each coordinate function
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
is Pfaffian of format at most
(q,D,d)
on
U
.

In particular, Pfaffian functions are definable in an o-minimal expansion of the real field. Since real-analyticity is part of the definition of a Pfaffian chain, every Pfaffian function is real-analytic, and hence
C^{\infty}
, on its domain.

For illustrative purposes, following
[
35
, Example 2.3]
, we record several elementary examples of Pfaffian functions.

Example 4

(Elementary functions)
.

The functions
e^{\beta\cdot}
, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\log(|\cdot|)
,
\arctan(\cdot)
, and every real-analytic branch of an algebraic function are Pfaffian on their domains of definition. In particular,
x\mapsto x^{\alpha}
, for rational
\alpha
, is Pfaffian on any interval on which it is real-valued and real-analytic.

Example 5

(Sinusoidal functions on bounded domains)
.

The function
t\mapsto\sin(t)
is not Pfaffian on all of
\mathbb{R}
, but its restriction to any bounded interval is Pfaffian. Consequently, the three-variable function

(x,w,b)\mapsto\sin(wx+b)

is Pfaffian on every bounded open domain
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Indeed, set

u(x,w,b)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}wx+b.

Since
U
is bounded,
u(U)
is bounded. Choose
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
large enough so that
u(U)/k
is contained in an interval of length strictly smaller than
2\pi
. Then there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{u(U)}{k}-c\subset(-\pi,\pi).

Define, for
(x,w,b)\in U
,

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,w,b)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\tan\Big(\frac{u(x,w,b)/k-c}{2}\Big),

\displaystyle f_{2}(x,w,b)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{1+f_{1}(x,w,b)^{2}}.

The functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are real-analytic on
U
. Moreover, for each
z\in\{x,w,b\}
,

\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial z}=\frac{1}{2k}\big(1+f_{1}^{2}\big)\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\in\mathbb{R}[x,w,b,f_{1}],

and

\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial z}=-2f_{1}f_{2}^{2}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial z}=-\frac{1}{k}f_{1}f_{2}^{2}\big(1+f_{1}^{2}\big)\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\in\mathbb{R}[x,w,b,f_{1},f_{2}].

Thus
(f_{1},f_{2})
is a Pfaffian chain on
U
; for instance, it has length
2
and degree at most
6
.

Now let

\theta(x,w,b)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}u(x,w,b)/k-c.

By the half-angle identities,

\sin(\theta)=2f_{1}f_{2},\qquad\cos(\theta)=(1-f_{1}^{2})f_{2}.

Therefore, by the angle-sum formula,

\sin\Big(\frac{u(x,w,b)}{k}\Big)=2\cos(c)f_{1}f_{2}+\sin(c)(1-f_{1}^{2})f_{2},

and

\cos\Big(\frac{u(x,w,b)}{k}\Big)=\cos(c)(1-f_{1}^{2})f_{2}-2\sin(c)f_{1}f_{2}.

Finally, if
U_{k-1}
denotes the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind, then

\sin(u(x,w,b))=\sin\Big(k\,\frac{u(x,w,b)}{k}\Big)=\sin\Big(\frac{u(x,w,b)}{k}\Big)U_{k-1}\Big(\cos\Big(\frac{u(x,w,b)}{k}\Big)\Big).

Since both
\sin(u/k)
and
\cos(u/k)
are polynomial expressions in
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
of degree at most
3
, it follows that
\sin(wx+b)
is Pfaffian on
U
, with format at most
(2,6,3k)
.

As discussed in
[
53
, page 111]
, Pfaffian functions are closed under sums, products, derivatives, composition, and reciprocals of nonvanishing Pfaffian functions. They are also stable under solving suitable Pfaffian equations. Quantitative bounds on the order and degree of Pfaffian functions constructed in this way are available; see, for instance,
[
35
, Lemmas 2.4 and 2.5]
.

A.2
Learning Theory

Consider a uniform class of scalar-valued functions

\mathcal{F}=\{f(\bar{x},\bar{\theta}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{1},\bar{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\}.

We say that a set
A=\{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{d}\}
is
pseudo-shattered
by
\mathcal{F}
if and only if there exists a set of real numbers
\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}\}
such that for every subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
, there is
\bar{\theta}_{A^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that

\bar{a}_{i}\in A^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow f(\bar{a}_{i},\bar{\theta}_{A^{\prime}})>r_{i}.

The
pseudo-dimension
of
\mathcal{F}
,
\pdim(\mathcal{F})
, is the size of the largest set that can be pseudo-shattered by
\mathcal{F}
. If we allow non-scalar functions,

\mathcal{F}=\{f(\bar{x},\bar{\theta}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m},\bar{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\},

we will define the pseudo-dimension to be the component-wise minimum pseudo-dimension, and so we will restrict our attention to scalar-valued functions.

The pseudo-dimension is given with a strict inequality, but the bi-implication in this definition still gives the same encoding of the characteristic function of each subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
, perhaps with a little less “wiggle room”. This motivates the notion of fat-shattering dimension of
[
52
]
, defined as follows.

Fix a tolerance
\gamma>0
. We say that a set
A=\{\bar{a}_{1},\ldots,\bar{a}_{d}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

is
\gamma
-fat shattered
by
\mathcal{F}
if and only if there exists a set of
real numbers
\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}\}
such that for every subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
,
there exists
\bar{\theta}_{A^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that, for every

i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,

\bar{a}_{i}\in A^{\prime}\mbox{ implies that: }f(\bar{a}_{i},\bar{\theta}_{A^{\prime}})\geq r_{i}+\gamma\qquad\text{{and}}\qquad\bar{a}_{i}\notin A^{\prime}\mbox{ implies that: }f(\bar{a}_{i},\bar{\theta}_{A^{\prime}})\leq r_{i}-\gamma.

The
\gamma
-fat shattering dimension
of
\mathcal{F}
is

\mathrm{fat}_{\gamma}(\mathcal{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\sup\Big\{\,d\in\mathbb{N}\ :\ \exists A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},\ |A|=d,\ A\ \text{is $\gamma$-fat shattered by }\mathcal{F}\Big\},

with the convention that
\mathrm{fat}_{\gamma}(\mathcal{F})=\infty
if sets of arbitrarily
large size are
\gamma
-fat shattered by
\mathcal{F}
.

A.2.1
Reducing to the VC Dimension

We will work in
\mathbb{R}
equipped with the usual language of ordered rings, with a symbol “
<
” for strict inequality.
Consider a uniform family of scalar-valued functions

\mathcal{F}=\{f(\bar{x},\bar{\theta}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{1},\bar{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\}.

Then
\pdim(\mathcal{F})\geq V
if and only if there is a set
A=\{(\bar{a}_{1},r_{1}),\ldots,(\bar{a}_{V},r_{V})\}
such that for any subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
, there is a tuple
\bar{\theta}_{A^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
that encodes the characteristic function of
A^{\prime}
:

(\bar{a}_{i},r_{i})\in A^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow f(\bar{a}_{i},\bar{\theta}_{A^{\prime}})-r_{i}>0.

In other words, the pseudo-dimension of
\mathcal{F}
is at least
V
if and only if the VC dimension of the class of uniformly definable sets

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}=\{f(\bar{x},\bar{\theta})-z>0:\bar{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\}

is at least
V
. Thus, we may bound the pseudo-dimension of
\mathcal{F}
by bounding the size
V
of a set that is shattered by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

B.2
Proofs of General o-Minimal Bounds

The proof does not proceed through the usual metric-complexity route. Our main technical tool is a uniform cell decomposition over the full parameterization, exploiting the o-minimal tameness, or fixed-format Pfaffian regularity, of the parametrized learning rule. Consequently, we do not need to truncate the parameter space to
[-M,M]^{p}
, estimate covering numbers or Rademacher complexities there, and then control how these estimates propagate through the parameter-to-realization map. That approach is poorly matched to unbounded parameter spaces, since the metric size of
[-M,M]^{p}
diverges as
M\to\infty
. The definable route captures a different phenomenon: uniformly over all parameters in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the model family cannot oscillate arbitrarily often. Equivalently, its realizable sign patterns admit a finite uniform cell decomposition. This is the geometric mechanism behind finite sample complexity with unbounded parameters.

Lemma B.2

(Jointly-Definable Families of Classifiers Have Tame Pre-Images)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be non-empty and definable, and let

f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times X\to\mathbb{R}
be definable (in the same o-minimal setting).
Then there exists a constant
K\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that, for every
\theta\in X
,
the set

\big(I_{(0,\infty)}\circ f(\cdot,\theta)\big)^{-1}[\{0\}]=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ f(x,\theta)\leq 0\}

has at most
K
connected components.

Proof of Lemma
B.2
.

Define the jointly-definable classifier

g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times X\to\{0,1\},\qquad g(x,\theta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}I_{(0,\infty)}(f(x,\theta)).

Since
(0,\infty)
is definable and definable maps are closed under composition
(cf.
[
25
, Exercise 1.11]
), the map
g
is definable; hence so is

A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}g^{-1}[\{0\}]=\{(x,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times X:\ f(x,\theta)\leq 0\}.

Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times X\to X
denote the coordinate projection
(x,\theta)\mapsto\theta
.
Apply the cell decomposition theorem to
A

compatible with

\pi

(cf.
[
25
, Theorem 2.10]
) to obtain finitely many pairwise disjoint definable cells

C_{1},\dots,C_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times X
such that

A=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{N}C_{j},

and such that for each
j
and each
\theta\in X
the fibre

(C_{j})_{\theta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ (x,\theta)\in C_{j}\}

is either empty or a definable cell in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Now, fix
\theta\in X
and set

A_{\theta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ (x,\theta)\in A\}=\big(I_{(0,\infty)}\circ f(\cdot,\theta)\big)^{-1}[\{0\}].

Then

A_{\theta}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{N}(C_{j})_{\theta}
; whence, by
[
25
, Proposition 2.5]
, every (non-empty) cell is definably homeomorphic to some Euclidean space,
hence connected. Therefore each
(C_{j})_{\theta}
is either empty or connected, and so
A_{\theta}
has at most
N

connected components. Taking
K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}N
completes the proof.
∎

Now, using our uniform cell decomposition, we are able to infer a VC dimension bound on the class of classifiers.

Lemma B.3

(Uniform Cell Decomposition Implies VC Dimension Bounds)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, let
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
both be non-empty and definable, and let

f:Z\times X\to\mathbb{R}
be definable.
Define the family of classifiers

\mathcal{F}^{\star}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\Big\{Z\ni x\mapsto I_{(0,\infty)}\big(f(x,\theta)\big)\ :\ \theta\in X\Big\}.

Then,
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}^{\star})<\infty
.
More precisely, there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and every

N
-point set
S\subseteq Z
,

\big|\mathcal{F}^{\star}|_{S}\big|\leq C\,N^{m}.

(B.1)

Consequently,

\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}^{\star})<\infty
.

Proof of Lemma
B.3
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and let
S=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subseteq Z
be an arbitrary
N
-point set. For each

i=1,\dots,N
, define the definable subset of parameter space

X_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{\theta\in X:\ f(x_{i},\theta)>0\}.

A labeling of
S
induced by
\mathcal{F}^{\star}
is determined by the membership pattern of
\theta
in the
sets
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
. Equivalently, the number of distinct labelings induced on
S
is bounded by the number of
non-empty atoms of the Boolean algebra generated by
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
.

By uniform cell decomposition for definable families
[
25
, Theorem 2.10]
, there exists a
constant
C>0
, depending only on the definable family induced by
f
and on
X
, such that this Boolean algebra has
at most
CN^{m}
non-empty atoms, uniformly over
N
and over the choice of
S\subseteq Z
. Therefore

\big|\mathcal{F}^{\star}|_{S}\big|\leq CN^{m}.

If
S
is shattered by
\mathcal{F}^{\star}
, then
\big|\mathcal{F}^{\star}|_{S}\big|=2^{N}
. Hence

2^{N}\leq CN^{m}.

Since the exponential function eventually dominates every polynomial, this inequality can hold for only finitely
many
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Thus

\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}^{\star})\leq\max\{N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}:\ 2^{N}\leq CN^{m}\}<\infty
.
∎

Remark B.4

(The One-Dimensional Refinement)
.

In the special case where
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, the preceding conclusion admits a sharper elementary bound.
Indeed, suppose that there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that, for every
\theta\in X
, the set

\{x\in Z:\ f(x,\theta)\leq 0\}

has at most
K
connected components. Then

\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}^{\star})\leq 2K.

To see this, define the associated set system

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\Big\{\{x\in Z:\ f(x,\theta)\leq 0\}\ :\ \theta\in X\Big\}.

Since
x\mapsto I_{(0,\infty)}\big(f(x,\theta)\big)=1
if and only if

x\notin\{y\in Z:\ f(y,\theta)\leq 0\}
, the classes
\mathcal{F}^{\star}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
induce the same
labelings on every finite sample up to flipping
0
and
1
. Hence

\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}^{\star})=\operatorname{VCdim}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}).

Now let
S=\{x_{1}<\cdots<x_{2K+1}\}\subseteq Z
. Since every set

\{x\in Z:\ f(x,\theta)\leq 0\}
has at most
K
connected components, and since connected components in

\mathbb{R}
are intervals, its trace on
S
is a union of at most
K
blocks of consecutive points. Therefore it
cannot realize the alternating subset

\{x_{1},x_{3},x_{5},\dots,x_{2K+1}\},

which consists of
K+1
singleton blocks. Thus no
(2K+1)
-point subset of
Z
is shattered, and consequently

\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}^{\star})\leq 2K
.

This now allows us to obtain classifier bounds via the core result in VC theory.
We propose and prove the following more general formulation of Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem B.5
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be non-empty and definable, and let

be definable (in the same o-minimal setting). Set

\mathcal{F}^{\star}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\big\{I_{(0,\infty)}\circ f(\cdot,\theta)\ :\ \theta\in X\big\},

so that, for each
\theta\in X
,

\big(I_{(0,\infty)}\circ f(\cdot,\theta)\big)^{-1}[\{0\}]=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ f(x,\theta)\leq 0\}.

Let
K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}^{\star})<\infty
, and let
\ell
denote the
0
–

loss.
Then, there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that: for every Borel probability measure

\mathbb{P}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0,1\}
, every sequence of i.i.d. samples

with law
\mathbb{P}
, every error
\varepsilon>0
, and every failure

, the following holds with probability at least

B.5
.

By Lemma

, applied with

, the class of classifiers

\mathcal{F}^{\star}
has finite VC dimension, which we denote by

.
Applying the fundamental theorem of PAC-learning for binary classifiers, as formulated in
[

yields the conclusion.
∎

In a similar vein, pseudo-dimension bounds can be obtained from uniform cell decompositions of the type in Lemma

Indeed, a standard lifting argument applied to the hypothesis class reduces the pseudo-dimension estimate to a VC dimension bound for an associated class of subgraphs. Once this reduction is in place, the proof is immediate: we invoke the preceding lemma that converts uniform cell decompositions into VC bounds, and then apply the main generalization theorem of

Lemma B.6

(Standard Lifting Trick)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, let
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be non-empty, and let

f:Z\times X\to\mathbb{R}
.
Define the real-valued class

\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\Big\{\,x\mapsto f(x,\theta)\ :\ \theta\in X\,\Big\}.

For each
\gamma\geq 0
, define the lifted classifier class on
Z\times\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\Big\{(x,r)\mapsto I_{(0,\infty)}\big(f(x,\theta)-r-\gamma\big)\ :\ \theta\in X\Big\}.

Then, for every
\gamma>0
, we have

\operatorname{fat}_{\gamma}(\mathcal{F})\leq\operatorname{Pdim}(\mathcal{F})\leq\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
.

Proof of Lemma
B.6
.

For
\gamma\geq 0
and
\theta\in X
, define

h_{\theta}^{(\gamma)}:Z\times\mathbb{R}\to\{0,1\}

by

h_{\theta}^{(\gamma)}(x,r)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}I_{(0,\infty)}\big(f(x,\theta)-r-\gamma\big),

so that
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}=\{h_{\theta}^{(\gamma)}:\theta\in X\}
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. By definition,
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\}\subseteq Z
is pseudo-shattered by
\mathcal{F}
if and only if there exist

r_{1},\dots,r_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for every

, one can choose
\theta\in X
with

I_{(0,\infty)}\big(f(x_{i},\theta)-r_{i}\big)=b_{i}

for each
i=1,\dots,d
.
Setting
z_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}(x_{i},r_{i})\in Z\times\mathbb{R}
, this implies that, for every
b\in\{0,1\}^{d}
, there exists
\theta\in X
such that

for each

. Hence

is shattered by
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Taking suprema over

yields

\operatorname{Pdim}(\mathcal{F})\leq\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
.

Now fix
\gamma>0
and suppose that

is
\gamma
-fat shattered by
\mathcal{F}
, witnessed by

. Thus, for every labeling
b\in\{0,1\}^{d}
, one can choose
\theta\in X
so that, for each

,

Since

for each

. Hence

is pseudo-shattered by
\mathcal{F}
with witnesses

. Therefore

, and taking suprema over all such

.

Remark B.8

(Lipschitz Losses May Destroy Definability)
.

Now, if we only knew that the loss function
\ell
was Lipschitz, without assuming definability in an o-minimal structure, then there would be no guarantee that the pseudo-dimension of the composite class
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}
would remain finite even if that of
\mathcal{F}
is finite. For instance, if
n=m=1
,
\mathcal{F}=\{ax:\,a\in[0,2\pi]\}
and
\ell(y_{1},y_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\tfrac{\sin(y_{1})+1}{2}
, then
\mathcal{F}
has finite pseudo-dimension, but the composite class need not have finite pseudo-dimension.

Remark B.9
.

Note also that we did not pass from the fat-shattering dimension to covering number bounds, e.g., using a result such as
[
10
, Theorem 2]
, which may unnecessarily loosen our bounds.

We now prove the regression variant of Theorem
3.2
for definable regressors.

Theorem B.10

(Definability Implies Learning Is Possible—Regression Case)
.

Let
m,n,p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
be non-empty and definable,
let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times X\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be definable, and let
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,1]
be definable.
Set

\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\{x\mapsto f(x,\theta):\theta\in X\}.

Then there exist constants
C,K>0
such that, for any Borel probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, any i.i.d. random variables

(X_{1},Y_{1}),\dots,(X_{N},Y_{N})
with law
\mathbb{P}
, any accuracy parameter
\varepsilon>0
, and any failure probability
0<\delta\leq 1
, the following bound holds with probability at least
1-\delta
:

\sup_{g\in\mathcal{F}}\,\biggl|\mathbb{E}_{(X,Y)\sim\mathbb{P}}\big[\ell(g(X),Y)\big]-\frac{1}{N}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,\ell(g(X_{i}),Y_{i})\biggr|\leq\varepsilon

provided that

N\geq C\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(K\ln^{2}\left(\frac{K}{\varepsilon}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\right)\right)
.

Proof of Theorem
B.10
.

Since
f
is definable, the identity map
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is definable,
\ell
is definable, and the products and compositions of definable functions are definable, cf.
[
25
, Section 1.3]
,
the map

f_{\ell}:\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\times X\to[0,1]
sending any
(x,y)
to
f_{\ell}((x,y),\theta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}\ell(f(x,\theta),y)

is definable.
Thus, the conclusion follows from Lemma
B.3
combined with Lemma
B.7
.
∎

B.3
Proofs regarding Definable Neural Network Building Blocks and Architectures

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Write

P=(P_{1},\dots,P_{m})
,
where each
P_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a polynomial. Then

\operatorname{graph}(P)=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}:y_{j}-P_{j}(x)=0,\,j=1,\dots,m\Big\}.

Hence
\operatorname{graph}(P)
is algebraic, and therefore semi-algebraic. Thus
P
is semi-algebraic, so it is definable in
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,<)
, and consequently in every o-minimal expansion of the real field.

Finally, polynomials are Pfaffian functions relative to the empty Pfaffian chain. Therefore each coordinate
P_{j}
is Pfaffian, and hence
P
is Pfaffian.
∎

Proof of Corollary
4.2
.

For each output coordinate
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, we have

L_{i}(x,A,b)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}x_{j}+b_{i}.

This is a polynomial function of the coordinates of
(x,A,b)
. Hence
L
is a polynomial map. The conclusion follows from Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
4.3
.

For each output coordinate
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, we have

\operatorname{Conv}(x,\theta,b)_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}T(\theta)_{ij}x_{j}+b_{i}.

Since
T:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is linear, each entry
T(\theta)_{ij}
is a linear function of
\theta
. Hence each term
T(\theta)_{ij}x_{j}
is polynomial in the coordinates of
(x,\theta,b)
. Therefore each output coordinate is polynomial in
(x,\theta,b)
, and so
\operatorname{Conv}
is a polynomial map.

The conclusion follows from Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
4.4
.

Write

F=(F_{1},\dots,F_{n})\qquad\text{and}\qquad G=(G_{1},\dots,G_{n}),

where each
F_{i},G_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a polynomial function.

The residual block

R(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}x+F(x)

has coordinates

R_{i}(x)=x_{i}+F_{i}(x),\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

Since sums of polynomial functions are polynomial, each coordinate
R_{i}
is polynomial. Hence
R
is a polynomial map.

Similarly, the gated residual block

H(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def.}}}{{=}}x+G(x)\odot F(x)

has coordinates

H_{i}(x)=x_{i}+G_{i}(x)F_{i}(x),\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

Since products and sums of polynomial functions are polynomial, each coordinate
H_{i}
is polynomial. Hence
H
is also a polynomial map.

Therefore both residual and gated residual polynomial blocks are polynomial maps. By Proposition
4.1
, they are semi-algebraic and definable in the real field.
∎

Proof of Proposition

Remark B.15

(Why boundedness is necessary)
.

The boundedness assumption is essential. The global functions
\sin:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\cos:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are not definable in any o-minimal expansion of the real field, since their zero sets have infinitely many connected components. Thus Fourier positional encodings are definable only when positions are fixed finite objects, or when the continuous position domain is bounded. If the frequencies are also treated as trainable parameters, then those frequency parameters must likewise range over a bounded definable set; otherwise the map
(t,\omega)\mapsto\sin(2\pi\langle\omega,t\rangle)
contains global sine as a slice.
