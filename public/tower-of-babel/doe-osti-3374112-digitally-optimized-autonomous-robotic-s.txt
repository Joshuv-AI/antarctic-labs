Project Plan

Primary outputs of the proposal support risk reduction associated with monitoring, inspection and
mapping of Hanford tank underground pits and includes:
1. A pit mock-up demonstration of Idaho National Laboratory’s (INL) Autonomous Pit Exploration
 System (APES) technology, which has two configurations:
 A. The Autonomous Robotic Arm, which is a proposed collaboration with Florida International
 University (FIU) and has capabilities to visually inspect, monitor, map and conduct simple tool
 manipulation tasks in the tank pits, and
 B. The robotic crawler configuration, which has capabilities to deploy to the bottom of the pits
 and conduct closer and bottom-up visual inspections.
2. An Implementation Plan that identifies Hanford site-wide application of the INL remote
 robotics technologies to enhance the performance of the tank farm systems and the
 characterization of the waste they contain to mitigate risk and optimize the overall waste mission.
3. A first-of-a-kind environmental digital twin will be produced. Digital twins to date require permanently
 installed sensors in order to produce asset specific predictions. This project will use the intermittent
 signals during inspections from deployed sensors on robotic systems to produce the data sets used
 by AI/ML to enable predictions of issues on tanks.

 A Mobile Approach to Autonomy
• Ease of use of the system is a primary
  design concern. The first step was
  embracing a primary platform that most
  operators are familiar with, a passenger
  car. (F150 Lightening)

• Will integrate robotic platforms into a
  single system. EV system allows
  powering of robotic systems without the
  use of diesel generators. Additionally,
  can keep operators comfortable in the
  cabin.

• Will also house the digital twin, robotic
  autonomy, and data acquisition systems.

 Tackling Challenges of Physics-Informed Robotics Simulation

• Simulating articulation motion, however,
  requires exhaustive tuning as small
  changes in the robot's properties can
  drastically alter its behavior.

• Model outputs can rely also on over-
  simplified physical assumptions that might
  not reflect real world's noise, creating a
  gap between simulated and real
  performance
