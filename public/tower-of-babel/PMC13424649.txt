Prognostic Factors in Colorectal Cancer Patients with Synchronous Peritoneal Metastasis Undergoing Macroscopic Complete Resection: A Multicenter Prospective Observational Study
Authors: Takatoshi Matsuyama, Hideyuki Ishida, Hirotoshi Kobayashi, Kenjiro Kotake, Masayasu Kawasaki, Yukihide Kanemitsu, Yusuke Kinugasa, Hideki Ueno, Kotaro Maeda, Takeshi Suto, Kenichi Sugihara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objectives: Colorectal cancer (CRC) with synchronous peritoneal metastasis is considered to have a worse prognosis. Although macroscopic complete resection is associated with a favorable prognosis, the prognostic determinants in this population remain poorly characterized. Therefore, it is crucial to identify prognostic factors following macroscopic complete resection to determine optimal care strategies that improve patient outcomes and to predict prognosis. Methods: In a multi-institute cohort study, we prospectively assessed clinicopathological factors that affect prognosis in 36 patients with CRC and synchronous peritoneal metastasis who underwent macroscopic complete resection. Results: 26 of 36 patients (72%) developed recurrence during follow-up, with a median of 34 months. Peritoneum was the most common site of recurrence. In survival analysis, patients with low preoperative serum CA19-9 levels had a favorable overall survival compared with those with high preoperative serum CA19-9 levels (HR: 0.42, 95% CI: 0.15-1.21, P=0.044). Also, patients with right-sided CRC had more favorable overall survival than those with left-sided CRC (HR: 0.44, 95% CI: 0.18-1.06, P=0.047). Furthermore, right-sided CRC with low preoperative serum CA19-9 levels was associated with significantly better overall survival than those with high preoperative serum CA19-9 levels or left-sided CRC (HR: 0.17, 95% CI: 0.07-0.41, P<0.001). Conclusions: In this prospective multi-institutional cohort of CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis undergoing macroscopic complete resection, recurrence was frequent, predominantly involving the peritoneum. Integrating tumor biology (sidedness) and serum biomarkers (CA19-9) may enhance prognostic stratification and guide individualized management strategies in this high-risk population.

INTRODUCTION

Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the most common malignancies worldwide and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality[1]. Despite improvements in screening programs for early detection, approximately 5% of patients present with peritoneal metastasis at the time of initial diagnosis[2,3]. CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis are considered to have a worse prognosis compared to hematological metastasis, such as metastasis to the liver or lung[4].

Improving the prognosis of patients with peritoneal metastasis from CRC, cytoreductive surgery combined with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) has been widely adopted following early randomized controlled studies reporting favorable long-term outcomes compared to systemic chemotherapy[5]. However, recent high-quality randomized controlled trials, most notably the PRODIGE-7 trial, demonstrated no additional survival benefit from oxaliplatin-based HIPEC when added to macroscopic complete resection[6]. In addition, a recent study revealed that CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis benefit from macroscopic complete resection without HIPEC in terms of long-term prognosis[7]. As a result, the therapeutic focus has shifted from HIPEC itself toward the prognostic significance of achieving macroscopic complete resection. Nevertheless, the prognostic determinants in this specific population remain poorly characterized, and optimal selection criteria for surgical intervention are yet to be established. Thus far, several retrospective studies have addressed this issue; however, the evidence remains limited by potential selection and information biases inherent to retrospective designs[8,9].

Therefore, this multicenter, prospective, observational study aimed to investigate prognostic factors associated with survival outcomes in patients with CRC who underwent macroscopic complete resection for synchronous peritoneal metastasis. A better understanding of these determinants may help refine the selection of potential candidates who may benefit from extended surgical management and improve prognostic stratification of this challenging subgroup of CRC patients.

METHODS

Patients

We included 150 CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis who were enrolled from 2012 to 2016 at 28 participating institutes of the Japanese Society for Cancer of the Colon and Rectum. Of these patients, we assessed 36 patients who underwent macroscopic complete resection for CRC with peritoneal metastasis. Macroscopic complete resection was defined as the absence of any visible residual tumor after systematic intraoperative inspection of the entire abdominal cavity. Specifically, the peritoneal cavity was evaluated according to a 13-region classification based on the Peritoneal Cancer Index (PCI): the central region, right upper abdomen, epigastrium, left upper abdomen, left lateral abdomen, left lower abdomen, pelvis, right lower abdomen, right lateral abdomen, proximal jejunum, distal jejunum, proximal ileum, and distal ileum. No patients in this study underwent HIPEC. Two patients underwent preoperative chemotherapy. No patients received immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy during the course of their disease. Figure 1A shows the flowchart for this study. Clinical data were obtained from a database created by prospectively collecting patient information at each institute. The ethical committees of the Japanese Society for Cancer of the Colon and Rectum approved the study protocol. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before enrollment.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 10.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA) and Medcalc Statistical Software version 23.4.5 (MedCalc Software, Ostend, Belgium). The outcomes were compared using Pearson's chi-square test or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables. The Kaplan-Meier analysis and log-rank test were performed to estimate and compare overall survival (OS) rates. We assessed the hazard ratio (HR) for the OS using univariate Cox regression analysis. Cox's proportional hazards model was used to identify independent prognostic factors that determine patient survival. Due to the limited number of events, a stepwise selection procedure was applied in the multivariate Cox proportional hazards model. Candidate variables were selected based on established clinical relevance and prior literature. Given the limited the events per variable, the multivariable analysis was regarded as exploratory. The cutoff value for preoperative serum CEA and CA19-9 was predefined as 5 U/mL and 37 U/mL, respectively, based on the upper limit of the institutional reference range. All P values were 2-sided, and analysis items with P values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

The incidence of recurrence after macroscopic complete resection in CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis

The patients' clinical information in this study is listed in Table 1. Twenty-six of the 36 patients (72%) developed recurrence in their follow-up periods, with a median of 34 months. No significant difference in clinicopathological factors was observed between the recurrence group and the non-recurrence group. Histopathological confirmation of resected peritoneal metastases was obtained in 35 of 36 patients, while pathological data were unavailable in one case. Compared with patients who underwent macroscopic incomplete resection, those who achieved macroscopic complete resection showed more favorable clinical and tumor-related characteristics, including female sex, PCI ≤4, absence of ascites, and lower serum levels of CEA and CA19-9 (Supplementary Table 1).

The pattern of recurrence after macroscopic complete resection in CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis

Figure 1B illustrates the first site of recurrence after macroscopic complete resection for CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis. Among 37 sites in twenty-six patients, we found that the peritoneum was the most common site of recurrence (17 patients: 65.4%), followed by the liver (5 patients: 19.2%), lymph node (4 patients: 15.4%), lung (3 patients: 11.5%), ovary (2 patients: 7,7%), skin (2 patients: 7.7%), spleen (2 patients: 7.7%), bone (1 patient: 3.8%), and anastomosis (1 patient: 3.8%), respectively.

Exploring prognostic factors of OS in CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis after macroscopic complete resection

We performed univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards analyses to identify independent prognostic factors for OS in CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis after macroscopic complete resection. Univariate analysis revealed that tumor sidedness and preoperative serum CA19-9 levels had borderline associations with OS (left-sided; HR: 2.36, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.99-5.64, P=0.05, high preoperative serum CA19-9 levels; HR: 2.55, 95% CI: 1.03-6.30, P=0.04, respectively) (Table 2). Multivariate analysis revealed that tumor sidedness, preoperative serum CA19-9 levels, and gender were independent factors for predicting OS (left-sided; HR: 3.16, 95% CI: 1.21-8.26, P=0.02, high preoperative serum CA19-9 levels; HR: 2.85, 95% CI: 1.09-7.43, P=0.03, male; HR: 2.74, 95% CI: 1.02-7.38, P=0.046, respectively) (Table 2).

Survival analysis of CRC patients who underwent macroscopic complete resection for synchronous peritoneal metastasis

We next conducted Kaplan-Meier analyses to compare the OS curves of each prognostic factor in CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis after macroscopic complete resection. Kaplan-Meier analyses revealed that patients with low preoperative serum CA19-9 levels had a favorable OS compared with those with high preoperative serum CA19-9 levels (HR: 0.42, 95% CI: 0.15-1.21, P=0.04) (Figure 2A). Also, patients with right-sided CRC showed favorable OS than those with left-sided CRC (HR: 0.44, 95% CI: 0.18-1.06, P=0.047) (Figure 2B). There was no statistically significant difference in OS according to gender.

Kaplan-Meier curve showing overall survival (A) according to preoperative serum CA19-9 levels, (B) according to the primary tumor sidedness, (C) according to the combination of preoperative serum CA19-9 levels and the primary tumor sidedness.

Then, to enhance prognostic stratification in CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis after macroscopic complete resection, we further conducted additional analysis by combining tumor sidedness with preoperative serum CA19-9 levels. As a result, right-sided CRC with low preoperative serum CA19-9 levels was associated with significantly better OS than those with high preoperative serum CA19-9 levels or left-sided CRC (HR: 0.17, 95% CI: 0.07-0.41, P<0.01) (Figure 2C). When comparing the OS between patients who underwent macroscopic complete resection with high preoperative serum CA19-9 levels or left-sided CRC and those who underwent macroscopic incomplete resection, there was no difference between the two groups (P=0.14) (Figure 2C).

DISCUSSION

In this study, we assessed clinicopathological factors that affect prognosis after macroscopic complete resection in a prospective, multicenter cohort of CRC patients with synchronous peritoneal metastasis. Our results demonstrated that preoperative serum CA19-9 levels and the sidedness of the primary tumor were independent prognostic factors for OS. In addition, the combination analysis of these two factors showed that patients with high preoperative serum CA19-9 levels or left-sided CRC had a significantly worse prognosis compared to those with low preoperative serum CA19-9 levels and right-sided CRC. Thus far, studies have demonstrated that curative resection, even without HIPEC, for synchronous peritoneal metastases from CRC has led to better outcomes, yielding 5-year OS rates of 23-36%[10,11]. However, as the overall prognosis remains poor even after curative resection, it is necessary to identify subgroups that may lead to further improvement in prognostic outcomes. From our results, selected patients, such as those with right-sided CRC and low preoperative serum CA19-9 levels, might derive greater prognostic benefit from macroscopic complete resection for synchronous peritoneal metastasis. In addition, as patients with high preoperative serum CA19-9 levels or left-sided CRC following macroscopic complete resection demonstrated OS comparable to those who received macroscopic incomplete resection, careful consideration should be given to undergoing macroscopic complete resection in these high-risk patients, particularly when the anticipated high surgical invasiveness or patients present with multiple comorbidities that may increase perioperative morbidity.

CA19-9 is a sialylated Lewis antigen that serves as a mucin-associated glycoprotein marker and is frequently elevated in tumors with abundant mucin production[12]. In CRC, mucinous adenocarcinoma and signet-ring cell carcinoma exhibit higher rates of serum CA19-9 elevation than non-mucinous histologies[13]. Thus far, current NCCN Guidelines recommend CEA as the standard biomarker and do not include CA19-9 in routine assessment. However, multiple studies have demonstrated that elevated serum CA19-9 level is an independent, worse prognostic factor in CRC with peritoneal metastasis[9,14]. The findings of the present study, in which preoperative serum CA19-9 levels were identified as a significant prognostic factor after macroscopic complete resection for synchronous peritoneal metastases, are therefore concordant with prior evidence and reinforce the clinical rationale for its measurement. Taken together, these data indicate that preoperative serum CA19-9 levels, while not currently included in the recommended biomarker list of the NCCN Guidelines, might provide meaningful prognostic information in patients with colorectal peritoneal metastasis and improve risk stratification. Consequently, routine assessment of preoperative serum CA19-9 levels may be of particular benefit in this subset, in which patterns of peritoneal dissemination may not be reliably amenable to CEA-based approaches alone.

Right-sided CRC is well known to exhibit a higher prevalence of mucinous adenocarcinoma and peritoneal metastasis compared with left-sided CRC, reflecting different molecular backgrounds and patterns of metastatic spread[15,16]. These characteristics may partly explain the more aggressive biological behavior and poorer overall prognosis generally reported for right-sided disease[16]. In our cohort, however, patients with right-sided CRC who had low preoperative serum CA19-9 levels demonstrated significantly better OS. One possible hypothesis is that CA19-9 may reflect, at least in part, tumor biology related to mucinous differentiation and peritoneal tumor burden within the spectrum of right-sided tumors. Accordingly, the right-sided CRC with low preoperative serum CA19-9 levels might represent a biologically less aggressive subgroup that features limited mucinous components or peritoneal spread. However, as histological subtype was not identified as an prognostic factor in this study, any biological interpretation should be considered exploratory. Further studies with detailed evaluation of mucinous proportions and peritoneal disease burden are warranted to validate this association.

Given that peritoneal recurrence was the predominant pattern after macroscopic complete resection in this study, intensive postoperative surveillance may warrant a high index of suspicion for early peritoneal relapse, with particular attention to subtle findings such as small-volume ascites or newly emerging peritoneal nodules. Clinically, progression of peritoneal disease frequently leads to bowel stenosis or obstruction, which can preclude the administration of systemic chemotherapy. Meanwhile, advances in systemic therapy for unresectable advanced CRC have expanded the availability and cumulative use of active agents and have been associated with prolonged OS[17]. From this perspective, earlier detection and timely initiation of treatment may help preserve treatment feasibility and maximize cumulative exposure to effective systemic therapies.

In this study, PCI did not remain an independent prognostic factor in the multivariable model. According to our results, PCI was significantly low in patients who achieved macroscopic complete resection compared to those who underwent incomplete resection, indicating its relevance as a marker of tumor burden and resectability. However, among patients who successfully achieved macroscopic complete resection, the prognostic impact of PCI was thought to be attenuated. This observation may suggest that when PCI is relatively low enough to permit complete macroscopic resection, its subsequent influence on survival outcomes becomes limited. Nevertheless, because of the limited sample size and number of events in this cohort, these findings should be interpreted with caution and require confirmation in larger prospective studies.

Adequate inspection of the small intestine and entire abdomen is essential for accurate assessment of peritoneal disease, and that limited bowel manipulation in minimally invasive procedures may theoretically affect diagnostic accuracy. To address this concern, we provide survival curves for patients who achieved macroscopic complete resection stratified by surgical approach (Supplementary Figure 1). No statistically significant difference in OS was observed (P = 0.15), and outcomes were not inferior in the laparoscopic group.

Although this study was a multicenter, prospective, observational study, it had several limitations. The number of patients in this study remained small, despite being collected from 28 center hospitals in Japan, resulting in a limited number of outcome events. Accordingly, although a stepwise multivariable Cox model was used to mitigate overfitting in the context of a limited number of events, the stability of the model may remain uncertain. Therefore, the multivariable findings should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of independent prognostic factors. On the other hand, it is worth noting that most prior studies have spanned long observational periods, during which advances in chemotherapy and surgical management may have confounded survival outcomes. In the present study, the inclusion period was limited to five years, thereby minimizing the potential influence of temporal changes in treatment modalities on patient prognosis. In addition, because this cohort consisted exclusively of Japanese patients treated at specialized centers, the generalizability of our findings to broader clinical settings may be limited. Therefore, a population-based or nationwide study and a well-planned, large, prospective, multicenter study in the future should validate our results. Another limitation of the present study is the absence of molecular profiles, such as MSI status, including but not limited to KRAS/BRAF variants. Specific molecular biological differences in CRC-which not only influence choice and efficacy of systemic chemotherapy but also prognosis-were not fully studied in this study. As such, unassessed molecular heterogeneity may have influenced treatment strategies and survival outcomes, and hence the findings of the current analysis. Furthermore, although we found greater survival benefit in right-sided CRC for the patients treated with macroscopic complete resection for peritoneal metastases in our study, prior study indicated that right-sided CRC is associated with a poorer prognosis in the setting of hepatic metastases[18]. These apparently conflicting results may indicate that prognostic effect of tumor sidedness depend on metatatic site. Therefore, this generalizability should be approached as the biological behavior of right-sided tumours is not consistently the same for all metastatic sites.

In conclusion, this multi-institutional prospective observational study suggests that right-sided CRC and low preoperative serum CA19-9 levels may be associated with more favorable OS among patients undergoing macroscopic complete resection for synchronous peritoneal metastasis. Given the exploratory nature of the study and the limited sample size, these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating and require validation in larger prospective cohorts. Nevertheless, the combined assessment of tumor sidedness and CA19-9 levels may contribute to improved prognostic stratification and could potentially inform patient selection for aggressive macroscopic complete resection in this high-risk population.
