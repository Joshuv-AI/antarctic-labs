(Quasi-)affineness of perverse character varieties
Enrico Lampetti, Michele Pernice

Abstract
. We show that perverse character varieties are (quasi-)affine. We do this in a purely stack-theoretic fashion, by exhibiting enough sections of the structure sheaf.



1.
Introduction

Given a finitely presented group
\Gamma
, the character variety
\mathrm{Char}(\Gamma)
is an affine scheme parametrizing finite-dimensional semisimple representations of
\Gamma
. The first systematic treatment of character varieties is given by Lubotzky–Magid
[
LM85
]
, but they already appeared in the work of Weil
[
Wei60a
,
Wei60b
]
, Artin
[
Art69
]
and Procesi
[
Pro75
]
.
A modern treatment can be found in the work of Sikora
[
Sik12
]
. They have been widely studied in the literature and present a rich geometry.
A central example is the case
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(X,x)
, where
X
is a topological space with finitely presented fundamental group. For instance, in the case of
X
an (open) Riemann surfaces, character varieties give examples of (Poisson, respectively) symplectic varieties
[
Gol84
]
.

The so-called monodromy equivalence allows one to interpret
\mathrm{Loc}(X)^{\heartsuit}\coloneqq\mathrm{Char}(\pi_{1}(X,x))
as an algebraic object parametrizing locally constant sheaves on
X
.
Locally constant sheaves and their moduli arise in many areas of geometry and physics.
Moreover, locally constant sheaves are related to vector bundles with connections via the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence
[
Del70
]
and to Higgs bundles via the non-abelian Hodge correspondence
[
Sim92
]
.

In order to present our main result, we need to take a more intrinsic point of view on character varieties.
For any
X
as above, we can construct the stack of locally constant sheaves, or character stack
\mathbf{Loc}(X)^{\heartsuit}
. The character stack carries more information than the character variety: indeed, while the character variety parametrizes semisimple locally constant sheaves, the stack
\mathbf{Loc}(X)^{\heartsuit}
parametrizes all locally constant sheaves or, equivalently, all representations of the fundamental group, and its underlying topological space parametrizes conjugacy classes
[\rho]
of representations of the fundamental group of
X
. Notice that each point has a unique closed point in its closure and such closed point corresponds to the conjugacy class
[\rho^{ss}]
of its semisimplification, i.e. the associated graded to any Jordan–Holder filtration of
\rho
.
The class
[\rho^{ss}]
can be also seen as a point of the character variety
\Loc(X)
and we get a well defined morphism

\displaystyle q\colon\mathbf{Loc}

\displaystyle(X)^{\heartsuit}\to\Loc(X)^{\heartsuit}

\displaystyle[\rho]\mapsto[\rho^{ss}]\ .

The morphism
q
defined above is an example of good moduli space, which is the most natural generalization of good quotients in the language of algebraic stacks (see
Section

2
for a more detailed discussion).

The stack
\mathbf{Loc}(X)^{\heartsuit}
introduced above is an open substack of a bigger stack
\mathbf{Loc}(X)
, where the latter parametrizes complexes of sheaves on
X
with locally constant cohomologies.
Notice that complexes of sheaves with locally constant cohomologies are a particular example of complexes of sheaves with constructible cohomology and, indeed, the theory generalizes in the constructible setting.
If
P
is a stratification of
X
verifying some mild hypothesis, Haine-Porta-Teyssier in
[
HPT26
]
constructed a (derived) stack
\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)
parametrizing complexes of sheaves with
P
-constructible cohomology.
Inside this stack, one can find open substacks parametrizing perverse sheaves
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{Perv}_{P}(X)
, depending on a perversity function
\mathfrak{p}\colon P\to\mathbb{Z}
.
We will refer to these as
perverse character stacks
.

An axiomatic background for perverse sheaves has been introduced in the influential paper
[
BBDG18
]
.
The most fundamental and studied examples of perverse sheaves are the one associated to the zero perversity (where one recovers the abelian category of constructible sheaves) and the middle perversity (related to the theory
\mathcal{D}
-modules via a far-reaching generalization of the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence mentioned above
[
Kas84
,
Meb84
]
).
In both cases, generalizing the aforementioned results for classical character stacks, perverse character stacks admit shifted Poisson structures
[
CL26b
]
and good moduli spaces
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)

[
Lam25a
]
.
We will refer to these good moduli spaces as
perverse character varieties
.
Its points are in bijection with semisimple perverse sheaves.
In this paper we are concerned with another feature of perverse character varieties, that is, their affineness.
This leads to the main result of this paper:

Main Theorem

(
Corollary

5.2.6
)
.

Perverse character varieties are quasi-affine, that is, the canonical morphism

{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)\to\Spec(\Gamma({}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X),\mathcal{O}_{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)}))

is a quasi-compact open immersion.

We want to stress that, although we only prove the quasi-affineness of the good moduli space, the techniques used are completely independent of GIT constructions and thus they fall in the territory of the “Beyond GIT” moduli theory: see
[
AHL26
]
for a thorough presentation of this more recent intrinsic approach to moduli theory.

The main contribution to the literature is to give an effective way of constructing global sections of the structural sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}Perv_{P}(X)}
. Informally, to any loop
\gamma
contained in a stratum
i_{p}\colon X_{p}\hookrightarrow X
, we associate the global section that evaluated on a semisimple perverse sheaf
F
gives back the trace of the monodromy associated to
\gamma
on the locally constant sheaf
i_{p}^{\ast}F
.
We show that such global sections are enough to separate points, see
Theorem

5.2.5
.
Both schemes appearing in the Main Theorem above admit natural derived enhancement and we show that the result holds in the derived setting.
Notice that by Lurie’s
[
Lur18
, Proposition 2.4.2.3]
, the derived and underived statements are actually equivalent.
Since our methods, naturally provide sections of the structural sheaf of the derived enhancement of
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)
, we decided to work directly in the framework of derived algebraic geometry.
All our results on quasi-affineness holds with the same proof in classical algebraic geometry.

In what remains, we explain informally the construction of these global sections using Hochschild homology.
The stack
\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)
is an example of moduli of objects
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
in a stable compactly generated
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
introduced by Toën-Vaquié
[
TV07
]
, and revisited by Antieau-Gepner
[
AG14
]
.
More precisely, for
k
a commutative ring and
\mathcal{C}=\Cons_{P}(X;\Mod_{k})
, there is an equivalence of derived stacks over
k

\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)\simeq\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}\ .

For any stable compactly generated
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, one can consider its Hochschild homology
\HH(\mathcal{C})
.
Hochschild homology can be used to construct
k
-forms on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
: see for example
[
BD21
]
, where a relation between Calabi–Yau structures on
\mathcal{C}
and shifted symplectic forms on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is established.
In the special case of
n=0
, there is a canonically defined morphism

\HO_{\mathcal{C}}\colon\HH(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}})\ .

Hence taking
H^{0}
on both sides and setting
\mathcal{C}=\Cons_{P}(X;\Mod_{k})
, we have that Hochschild homology
\HH(\Cons_{P}(X;\Mod_{k}))
gives rise to global sections of the derived stack
\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)
.
Following
[
CL26a
]
, this map can be explicitely described: in
Lemma

4.3.1
we describe
\HH(\Cons_{P}(X;\Mod_{k}))
and in
Corollary

4.3.2
we describe the morphism
\HO_{\mathcal{C}}
is this setting.
Restricting to the open substack
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{Perv}_{P}(X)
, this gives a map to global sections of the
perverse character stack.
Since by definition the morphism to the good moduli space
q\colon{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{Perv}_{P}(X)\to{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)
induces an equivalence
\mathcal{O}_{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)}\simeq q_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{Perv}_{P}(X)}
, we have that global sections of the structural sheaf of
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{Perv}_{P}(X)
agree with global sections of the structural sheaf of
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)
.

2.
Good moduli spaces

In this section, we recall the definition of good moduli spaces and their main properties. Good moduli spaces for algebraic stacks were introduced by Alper in
[
Alp13
]
and have played a prominent role in moduli theory as generalizations of Mumford’s good GIT quotients
[
MFK94
]
and Abramovich–Olsson–Vistoli’s tame stacks
[
AOV08
]
.
If a good moduli space
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
exists, then it is unique, as
q
is universal for maps from
\mathcal{X}
to algebraic spaces (
[
Alp13
, Theorem 6.6]
).
Similarly to the Keel–Mori Theorem
[
KM97
]
, a fundamental result of Alper–Halpern-Leistner–Heinloth
[
AHLH23
, Theorem A]
provides an intrinsic way to show that an algebraic stack admits a good moduli space.
The existence of a good moduli space for an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
has many pleasant consequences.
It allows for example to give a local presentation of
\mathcal{X}
by quotient stacks and prove the compact generation of
\QCoh(\mathcal{X})
(
[
AHR25
]
).
Also, the existence of good moduli spaces can be exploited to construct BPS Lie algebras and unlock the study of the cohomology of
\mathcal{X}
via cohomological Hall algebras, see e.g.
[
DHM22
,
Dav24
,
Hen24
,
BDN+25
,
HK25
]
.

Definition 2.0.1

(
[
Alp13
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
be a qcqs morphism over an algebraic space
S
with
\mathcal{X}
an algebraic stack and
X
an algebraic space.
We say that
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
is a good moduli space if the following properties are satisfied:

(1)

the functor
q_{\ast}\colon\QCoh(\mathcal{X})\to\QCoh(X)
is
t
-exact;

(2)

the canonical morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to q_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
is an equivalence.

For completeness, we recall some of the main properties of good moduli spaces:

Theorem 2.0.2

(
[
Alp13
, Proposition 4.5 & Theorem 4.16 & Theorem 6.6]
)
.

Let
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
be a good moduli space.
Then

(1)

The functor
q^{\ast}\colon\QCoh(X)^{\heartsuit}\to\QCoh(\mathcal{X})^{\heartsuit}
is fully faithful;

(2)

the map
q
is universally closed and surjective;

(3)

if
Z_{1},Z_{2}
are closed substacks of
\mathcal{X}
, then

\mathrm{Im}(Z_{1})\cap\mathrm{Im}(Z_{2})=\mathrm{Im}(Z_{1}\cap Z_{2})

where the intersections and images are scheme-theoretic.

(4)

for
\kappa
an algebraically closed field over
k
, the
\kappa
-points of
X
are
\kappa
-points of
\mathcal{X}
up to closure equivalence (see
[
Alp13
, Theorem 4.16-(iv)]
for a precise statement);

(5)

if
\mathcal{X}
is reduced (resp., quasi-
compact, connected, irreducible), then
X
is also.
If
\mathcal{X}
is locally noetherian
and normal, then
X
is also.

(6)

if
\mathcal{X}
is locally noetherian, then
X
is locally noetherian and
q_{\ast}\colon\QCoh(\mathcal{X})^{\heartsuit}\to\QCoh(X)^{\heartsuit}
preserves coherence;

(7)

if
k
is excellent and
\mathcal{X}
is of finite type, then
X
is of finite type;

(8)

if
\mathcal{X}
is locally noetherian, then
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
is universal for maps to algebraic spaces.

Remark 2.0.3
.

The work of Alper–Halpern-Leistner–Heinloth
[
AHLH23
]
shows that in order to prove that an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
admits a good moduli space, it is enough to check intrinsic properties of
\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 2.0.4
.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow X
be a moduli space morphism with
X
an algebraic space and let
x\in\mathcal{X}
be a point. We say that
x
is polystable if
x
is closed in
\pi^{-1}(\pi(x))
.

Remark 2.0.5
.

The previous definition is inspired by the definition of polystable vector bundles (and more recently
K
-polystable Fano varieties). Indeed, if
\mathcal{X}
is locally of finite type over some algebraic space
S
and admits a good moduli space, a point
x
of
\mathcal{X}
is polystable if and only if there are no non-trivial
\Theta
-degenerations of
x
, namely morphisms
\Theta\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
which maps the generic point of
\Theta
to
x
. This follows from
[
AHLH23
, Lemma 3.24]
.

If
\mathcal{X}
does not admit a good moduli space, the more general definition of
N
-closed point can be given using the notion of
N
-specializations. This will appear in a future work of David Rydh on topological moduli spaces.

Finally, let us recall that a notion of derived good moduli spaces has been introduced in
[
AHPS23
]
.
The following is the main result of that paper:

Theorem 2.0.6

(
[
AHPS23
, Theorem 2.12]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a geometric derived stack.

(1)

If
t_{0}\mathcal{X}
admits a good moduli space
t_{0}q\colon t_{0}\mathcal{X}\to t_{0}X
, then
\mathcal{X}
admits a derived good moduli space
q^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
such that
t_{0}q^{\prime}\simeq t_{0}q
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{X}
admits a derived good moduli space
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
, then
t_{0}q
is a good moduli space for
t_{0}X
.

Remark 2.0.7
.

When applying
Theorem

2.0.6
one should take some care in general, as the notion of good moduli space in
[
AHPS23
]
is stronger then the one of the one in
[
Alp13
,
AHLH23
]
.
However, the two notions agree under mild conditions (
[
AHPS23
, Proposition 2.5-(iii)]
) which will always be verified in the cases of interest for us.

2.1.
Quasi-affineness of good moduli spaces

By definition, a good moduli space is only an algebraic space.
In this paragraph we provide a simple criterion (
Corollary

2.1.6
) for a good moduli space to be a quasi-affine scheme.

Definition 2.1.1

(
[
Sta25
,
Tag 01P6
]
&
[
Lur18
, Definition 2.4.1.1]
)
.

A (derived) scheme
X
is called quasi-affine if it is quasi-compact and isomorphic to an open subscheme of a (derived) affine scheme.

For a derived scheme
X
, the property of being quasi-affine can be checked on it classical truncation.
Indeed as a special case of
[
Lur18
, Proposition 2.4.2.3]
we have the following

Theorem 2.1.2
.

Let
X
be a derived algebraic space.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is quasi-affine;

(2)

t_{0}X
is quasi-affine.

Definition 2.1.3
.

Let
Y
be an algebraic stack and
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
.
We say that a line bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(Y)
separates
y_{1}
from
y_{2}
if there exists
s\in\mathrm{H}^{0}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{L})
such that
s_{y_{1}}\neq 0
and
s_{y_{2}}=0
.
We say that
\mathcal{L}
separates points if it separates
y_{1}
from
y_{2}
for each
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
.

The following results are well know to the experts, but we include a proof for completeness

Lemma 2.1.4
.

Let
Y
be a separated algebraic space locally of finite type over a scheme
S
.
Assume that the structural sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
separates points.
Then

f\colon Y\to\Spec(\mathrm{H}^{0}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}))

is locally quasi-finite.

Given
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
and a global section
s\in\mathrm{H}^{0}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
be a section separating
y_{1}
from
y_{2}
, we have that the pullback of
X
and
\Spec(\mathrm{H}^{0}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})_{s})
along
\Spec(\mathrm{H}^{0}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}))
contains
y_{1}
but not
y_{2}
.
Hence the fibers
f
are finite and the result follows by
[
Sta25
,
Tag 06RW
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.1.5
.

Let
Y
be a derived quasi-compact separated algebraic space locally of finite type over
k
.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

Y
is a quasi-affine;

(2)

the canonical morphism
f\colon Y\to\Spec(\tau_{\geq 0}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}))
is representable by a quasi-compact open immersion;

(3)

Y
is quasi-compact and the structural sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
separate points of
Y
.

The equivalence between
(1)
and
(2)
is
[
Lur18
, Proposition 2.4.1.3]
and the fact that
Y
is connective (see also
[
AHPS23
, Lemma 1.5]
).
Clearly
(2)
implies
(3)
.
We now show that
(3)
implies
(2)
.

Since
Y\to\Spec(k)
is of locally of finite type, then
f
is locally of finite type.
Since being locally quasi-finite is a property defined on the classical truncation of
Y
(
[
Lur18
, Definition 3.3.1.1]
), if
(3)
holds then
f
is locally quasi-finite by
Lemma

2.1.4
.
Moreover since
X
is quasi-compact and separated and
\Spec(\tau_{\geq 0}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}))
is affine, we have that
f
is quasi-compact and separated.
The result then follows by Zariski’s Main Theorem
[
Lur18
, Theorem 3.3.0.2]
.
∎

We will use the following criterion for quasi-affineness of a good moduli space.

Corollary 2.1.6
.

Let
q\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
be a good moduli space over
k
with
X
separated and of finite type over
k
.
Then
X
is quasi-affine if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
separates polystable points.

The points of
X
are in bijection with the polystable points of
\mathcal{X}
by
Theorem

2.0.2
-
(4)
.
Since
q_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
by definition of
q
, it follows that
\mathcal{O}_{X}
separates points if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
separates polystable points.
The claim then follows by
Lemma

2.1.5
.
∎

3.
Moduli of flat objects

In this Section we recall the main definition of moduli of (flat) objects and the main results needed in the rest of the paper.

3.1.
Families of objects

In this paragraph we recall properties of the tensor product of presentable
\infty
-categories, which is used to define families of objects.
We also discuss some finiteness properties one can impose on compactly generated presentable categories.

Recollection 3.1.1

(
[
Lur17
, Section 4.8]
)
.

For
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{k}
, we can consider their tensor product

\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{R}}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathcal{D})

where the right hand side is the
\infty
-category of
k
-linear functors commuting with limits.
This tensor product endows
\Pr^{\mathrm{L}}_{k}
with a symmetric monoidal structure which restricts to
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
.
When
\mathcal{C}
is compactly generated, there is a canonical equivalence

\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op},\mathcal{D})

where the right hand side is the
\infty
-category of exact
k
-linear functors.

Remark 3.1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}\in\Pr_{k}^{\mathrm{L}}
.
Then an adjunction

f\colon\mathcal{D}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{E}\colon g

induces an adjunction

\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}f\colon\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{D}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{E}\colon\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}g.

The functor
\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}g
corresponds to

g\circ-\colon\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{R}}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathcal{E})\to C\otimes_{k}\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{R}}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathcal{D})

under the equivalences of
3.1.1
.
Its left adjoint
\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}f
has no easy description in general.
Notice indeed that post-composition with
f
do not preserve functors that commutes with limits.
Nevertheless, if
\mathcal{C}
is compactly generated one can see that the adjunction
\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}f\dashv\Id_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes_{k}g
corresponds to

f\circ-\colon\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op},\mathcal{D})\leftrightarrows\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op},\mathcal{E})\colon g\circ-

under the equivalences of
3.1.1
.

Notation 3.1.3
.

In the setting of

3.1.2
, we denote the functors

.

In order to introduce finiteness conditions on presentable categories, let us give the following:

Lemma 3.1.10

(

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}

\mathcal{C}

with dual given by

We will need the following finiteness condition:

Definition 3.1.11

(

smooth in the sense of

In this paragraph we introduce the moduli of objects of an

.
This construction firstly appeared in

]

[

Recollection 3.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
.
Consider the presheaf

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{C}}\colon

\displaystyle\dAff_{k}^{op}\to\mathrm{Spc}

\displaystyle\Spec(A)\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{A}^{\simeq}.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is dualizable (

), the functor
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}-
commutes with limits.
Since

satisfies faithfully flat descent (

, Corollary D.6.3.3]

defines a derived stack over

.
The stack
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{C}}
is too big and has no chance to be representable.
The derived moduli of objects of

is defined as the presheaf

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}\colon

\displaystyle\dAff_{k}^{op}\to\mathrm{Spc}

\displaystyle\Spec(A)\to\mathcal{C}_{A}^{pp}

where
\mathcal{C}_{A}^{pp}\subset\mathcal{C}_{A}
is the maximal groupoid spanned by pseudo-perfect families over
A
in the sense of
Definition

3.1.9
.
Since
\Perf(-)
satisfies hyper-descent (
[
Lur18
, Proposition 2.8.4.2-(10)]
), the sub-presheaf

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{C}}

is a derived substack by
Remark

3.1.2
.

The main theorem of
[
TV07
]
translates as:

Theorem 3.2.2

(
[
AG14
, Theorem 5.8 & Corollary 5.9]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
be of finite type.
Then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a locally geometric derived stack locally of finite presentation over
k
.
Furthermore, the tangent complex at a point
x\colon\Spec(A)\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
corresponding to a pseudo-perfect family of objects
M_{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
is given by

x^{\ast}\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}}\simeq\Hom(M_{x},M_{x})[1].

Remark 3.2.3

(Functoriality of
\mathcal{M}_{(-)}
)
.

Let

in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
between finite type categories and let

be its right adjoint.
Assume that

is itself a left adjoint, so that

preserve compact objects.
Then we have an induced map on moduli of objects

g_{(-)}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}

given by
g_{A}
on each
\Spec(A)\in\dAff_{k}
. Indeed this map corresponds to the pre-compoosition with

under the equivalences
\mathcal{D}_{A}\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}((\mathcal{D}^{\omega})^{op},\Mod_{A})
and
\mathcal{C}_{A}\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{st}}_{k}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op},\Mod_{A})
.

3.2.1.
Moduli of flat objects

In this paper we are mainly interested in moduli of objects in abelian categories.
If
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
is equipped with a
t
-structure
\tau
, we can introduce a substack

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{[0,0]}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}

whose classical truncation parametrizes families of objects in the heart
\tau
.
Under suitable assumption on
\tau
, the stack
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{[0,0]}
is an open substack of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
, so that it is also locally geometric and locally of finite presentation when
\mathcal{C}
is of finite type.

Definition 3.2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
equipped with a
t
-structure
\tau
.
We say that

(1)

\tau
is
\omega
-accessible if the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is stable under filtered colimits;

(2)

\tau
is left-complete if
\bigcap\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}\simeq 0
;

(3)

\tau
is right-complete if
\bigcap\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}\simeq 0
;

(4)

\tau
is non-degenerate if it is both right-complete and left-complete.

(5)

\tau
is admissible if it is
\omega
-accessible and non-degenerate.

Recollection 3.2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
.
Then a morphism
f\colon\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)
in
\dAff_{k}
corresponding to a map
A\to B
induces an adjunction

f_{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}\colon\mathcal{C}_{A}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{C}_{B}\colon f_{\mathcal{C},\ast}

where
f_{\mathcal{C},\ast}
is the forgetful functor.
Denote by
p\colon\Spec(A)\to\Spec(k)
be the structural morphism.
As explained in
[
DPS25
, Section II.2.5.1]
, given an
\omega
-accessible right-complete
t
-structure
\tau
on
\mathcal{C}
, we get an induced
\omega
-accessible right-complete
t
-structure
\tau_{A}
on
\mathcal{C}_{A}
for any
Spec(A)\in\dAff_{k}
by declaring that
F\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
is (co)connective if
p_{{}_{\mathcal{C}},\ast}(F)\in\mathcal{C}
is (co)connective.
Moreover
\tau_{A}
is non-degenerate if
\tau
is.

For
X\in\dSt_{k}
, we can also define an induced
t
-structure
\tau_{X}
on
\mathcal{C}_{X}
whose connective part is defined by descent.
That is,
F\in(\mathcal{C}_{X})_{\geq 0}
if and only if
f^{\ast}F\in(\mathcal{C}_{A})_{\geq 0}
for any
f\colon\Spec(A)\to X
.

Definition 3.2.6

(
[
DPS25
, Definition II.2.44]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
equipped with an
\omega
-admissible right-complete
t
-structure
\tau
and
\Spec(A)\in\dAff_{k}
.
We say that a family
F\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
is
\tau
-flat over
\Spec(A)
(or
\tau_{A}
-flat) if for every
M\in\Mod_{A}^{\heartsuit}
one has
F\otimes_{A}M\in\mathcal{C}_{A}^{\heartsuit}
.

Remark 3.2.7
.

In the setting of
Definition

3.2.6
, when
A
is discrete, a
\tau
-flat family
F
over
A
lies in
\mathcal{C}_{A}^{\heartsuit}
.
Indeed in this case one has
A\in\Mod_{A}^{\heartsuit}
, so that
F\simeq F\otimes_{A}A\in\mathcal{C}_{A}^{\heartsuit}
.
The converse holds when
A
is a field.

The key property for a
t
-structure to define an open substack of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is linked to the following definition:

Definition 3.2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\Pr_{k}^{L,\omega}
equipped with an admissible
t
-structure
\tau
.
Let
\textsf{S}\in\dAff_{k}
and let
F\in\mathcal{C}_{S}
.
The flat locus of
F
is the functor

\Phi_{F}\colon(\dAff_{k})^{op}_{/_{S}}\to\Spc

\displaystyle(T\xrightarrow{f}\textsf{S})\mapsto\left\{\begin{aligned} &\ast\ \ \text{if $f^{\ast}(F)$ is $\tau$-flat relative to $T$,}\\
&\emptyset\ \ \ \ \text{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Definition 3.2.9

(
[
DPS25
, Definition II.2.48]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\Pr_{k}^{L,\omega}
equipped with an admissible
t
-structure
\tau
.
We say that
\tau
universally satisfies openness of flatness if for every
S\in\dAff_{k}
and every
F\in\mathcal{C}_{S}
the map
\Phi_{F}\to S
is representable by an open immersion.

Proposition 3.2.10

(
[
DPS25
, Proposition II.2.56]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\Pr_{k}^{L,\omega}
be of finite type equipped with an admissible
t
-structure
\tau
.
Then
\tau
universally satisfies openness of flatness if and only if then the structural morphism

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{[0,0]}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}

is representable by a Zariski open immersion.
In particular, in this case
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{[0,0]}
is a locally geometric derived stack, locally of finite presentation over

.

Notation 3.2.11
.

In the setting of

3.2.10
, we set
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\heartsuit}\coloneqq t_{0}\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{[0,0]}
to be the classical truncation of the derived stack

A priori,

only guarantees that

and its truncation

have an open exhaustion by (derived) algebraic stacks.
When

Hence

is

Then
[

implies the following

\mathcal{C}\in\Pr_{k}^{L,\omega}
be of finite type equipped with an admissible

If

3.3.
Hochschild homology and global sections

We now introduce one the main tools of this paper, that is, Hochschild homology of
k
-linear stable
\infty
-categories.
This is a generalization of Hochschild homology as classically defined in commutative algebra and scheme theory, and recovers it when one consider the stable category
\Mod_{A}
for
A\in\mathrm{CAlg}_{k}
.
We thank Marc Hoyois for clarification about the content of this section.

Recall from
Lemma

3.1.10
that for any
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
, there are duality data (unique up to contractible choices)

\ev_{\mathcal{C}}\colon\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\vee}\leftrightarrows\Mod_{k}\colon\coev_{\mathcal{C}}\ ,

where we freely use that Lurie’s tensor product is symmetric.
With this in hand, we can give the following

Definition 3.3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{k}
dualizable and
(\ev_{\mathcal{C}},\coev_{\mathcal{C}})
a duality datum for
\mathcal{C}
.
Then the Hochschild homology of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as the trace of
id_{\mathcal{C}}
, that is, it is the image of
k
through the composition

\Mod_{k}\xrightarrow{\coev_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{C}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\ev_{\mathcal{C}}}\Mod_{k}\ .

Hochschild homology of a category
\mathcal{C}
provides a way to construct global section of the structure sheaf of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma 3.3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{k}
be of finite type.
There is a canonical map

\HO_{\mathcal{C}}\colon\HH(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}})\ .

This is well known
[
BD21
,
BCS24
,
CL26a
]
, but we include few details for the sake of completeness.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is of finite type, we obtain a universal family

F_{univ}\colon(\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op}\to\Perf(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})

corresponding to
id_{\mathcal{M}_{C}}
.
Since
Lemma

3.1.10
gives us that
\mathrm{Ind}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op})\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
, we get a natural morphism

\HH(\mathcal{C})\simeq\HH(\mathcal{C}^{\vee})\to\HH(\mathrm{Ind}(\Perf(\mathcal{M}_{C})))\ .

Since the trace on simplicial commutative algebras define a natural transformation
tr\colon\HH(-)\to\mathcal{O}
from Hochschild homology to global sections, we obtain the desired map as the composition

\HO_{\mathcal{C}}\colon\HH(\mathcal{C})\simeq\HH(\mathcal{C}^{\vee})\xrightarrow{F_{univ}}\HH(\mathrm{Ind}(\Perf(\mathcal{M}_{C})))\xrightarrow{tr}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}})\ .

Following
[
BCS24
]
, the above map can also be obtained as the composition

\HH(\mathcal{C})\simeq\HH(\mathrm{Ind}((\mathcal{C}^{\omega})^{op}))\to\lim_{\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\to\Perf(B)}\HH(B)\to\lim_{\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\to\Perf(B)}B\simeq\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}})\ ,

where
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\to\Perf(B)
corresponds to a point
\Spec(B)\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
by definition of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

Remark 3.3.3
.

Following
[
CL26a
, Section 4.1]
, we have chosen the notation
\HO
in
Lemma

3.3.2
as a mnemonic for "from Hochschild to

".

Remark 3.3.4
.

Notice that in the references
[

,

]
, in the morphism of

there is no appearance of

4.
Moduli of constructible and perverse sheaves

In this paragraph we introduce constructible and perverse sheaves, as well as their moduli. The role of perverse sheaves is central in the study of singularities and they naturally generalize local systems.
Moreover they are linked to the study of differential equation with regular singularities (i.e., simple poles) via the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (
[
Kas84
,
Meb84
]

Perverse sheaves originated form the theory of

-module on a complex manifold (
[
Kas84
,
KS90
]
).
An axiomatic background has been introduced in the influential paper
[
BBDG18
]

We claim no originality here, possibly except
Section

4.3
.
Every statement claimed in this paragraph holds true in much wider generality, for which we refer to
[
PT22
,
HPT24
,
HPT26
]
.
We choose to work under more restrictive hypothesis so that we can avoid introducing many technical notions, and in any case this is enough for our scope.

(2)

\dim_{\kappa}(F_{q})_{x}=\dim_{\kappa}(G_{q})_{x}
for each for any
x\in X_{q}
.

If
q
is not a maximal element of the list
I\coloneqq\{q\}\cup P_{G}\subset P
, then
F
and
G
lie in different connected components.
Indeed, if it is not the case, consider a maximal element of
q^{\prime}\in I
and consider an element
x_{q^{\prime}}\in X_{q^{\prime}}
.
Then we get

\chi(F)(x_{q^{\prime}})=0\neq\dim_{\kappa}G_{q^{\prime}}=\chi(G)({x_{q^{\prime}}})\ .

Hence
q
must be a maximal element of
I
by
Lemma

5.1.6
.
If
q\notin I
, by replacing the roles of
F
and
G
above one sees that
x
and
y
do not lie in the same connected component.
This proves
(1)
.
Item
(2)
follows since
q
is maximal and if
x,y
lie in the same connected component, then by
Lemma

5.1.6
we must have

\dim_{\kappa}(F_{q})_{x}=\chi(F)(x_{q})=\chi(G)({x_{q}})=\dim_{\kappa}(G_{q})_{x}\ .

This proves
(2)
.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main result:

Theorem 5.2.5
.

Under the assumptions of
Lemma

5.2.4
, let
\kappa
be of characteristic zero.
Assume that
\Pi_{\infty}(X_{p})
is a compact object in
\Cat_{\infty}
for each
p\in P
.
Then the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{t_{0}\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)}
separates points representing semisimple perverse sheaves of
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P,\omega}(X;\Mod_{\kappa})
.
More precisely, such points can be separated by global sections in the image of the morphism

\HO_{X}\colon\HH_{0}(\Cons_{P}(X;\Mod_{k}))\to\mathrm{H}^{0}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)})\ .

Let
x_{F},x_{G}\colon\Spec(\kappa)\to\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)
parametrizing semisimple perverse sheaves
F,G\in{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P,\omega}(X;\Mod_{\kappa})
.
By
4.1.19
, there exist semisimple local systems of finite rank
F_{p},G_{p}\in Loc_{\omega}(X_{p};\Mod_{\kappa})^{\heartsuit}
such that

F\simeq\bigoplus_{p\in P}(i_{\leq p})_{\ast}(j_{p})_{!\ast}F_{p}\ ,\qquad G\simeq\bigoplus_{p\in P}(i_{\leq p})_{\ast}(j_{p})_{!\ast}G_{p}\ .

Following the notation of
Lemma

5.2.4
, let
P_{F}
(respectively
P_{G}
) be the subset
\,\{p\in P\,|F_{p}\neq 0\}\subset P
(respectively
\,\{p\in P\,|G_{p}\neq 0\}\subset P
) and let
n
(respectively
m
) be the cardinality of
P_{F}
(respectively
P_{G}
). We proceed by induction on
n
.

Suppose that
n=1
, and thus
P_{F}=\{q\}
.
By
Lemma

5.2.4
, we have that
q\in P_{G}
is a maximal element of
P_{G}
. We also have that
F_{q}
and
G_{q}
are semisimple locally constant sheaves of the same rank. If
F_{q}
and
G_{q}
are not isomorphic in
\Loc_{\omega}(X_{q};\Mod_{\kappa})^{\heartsuit}
, then we are done by
Corollary

4.3.2
and
Corollary

5.2.3
.
Indeed by
[
HPT24
, Theorem 5.1.7-(5)]
and
[
Lam25a
, Theorem 4.1.22]
, the assumptions of
Corollary

5.2.3
are satisfied.
Hence the points corresponding to
F_{q}
and
G_{q}
can be separated in
\mathbf{Loc}(X_{q})
by global sections in the image of

\HO_{X_{q}}\colon\HH_{0}(\Loc(X_{q});\Mod_{k})\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{Loc}(X_{q}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{Loc}(X_{q})})\,.

By the splitting provided by
Corollary

4.3.2
, it follows that
x_{F},y_{F}\in\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)
can be separated by sections in the image of
\HO_{X}
.
Hence we are reduced to consider the case
F_{q}\simeq G_{q}
.
We analyze separately the cases
m=1
and
m>1
.
If
m=1
, then
F\simeq G
and thus the points
x
and
y
agree.
In this case there is nothing to prove.
If
m>1
, since the traces of
Corollary

4.3.2
are additive in direct sums (more generally, in short exact sequences), then
x
and
y
lie in different connected components by
Lemma

5.1.6
.
To see this, choose a maximal
q^{\prime}\in P_{G}\setminus\{q\}
and consider a point of
x^{\prime}\in X_{q^{\prime}}
.
Since the Euler-Poincaré index is additive in short exact sequences, we get

\displaystyle\chi(F)(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\chi((i_{\leq q})_{\ast}(j_{q})_{!\ast}F_{q})(x^{\prime})=\chi((i_{\leq q})_{\ast}(j_{q})_{!\ast}G_{q})(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\neq\chi((i_{\leq q})_{\ast}(j_{q})_{!\ast}G_{q})(x^{\prime})+\dim_{\kappa}(G_{{q^{\prime}}})_{x^{\prime}}=\chi(G)(x^{\prime})\ .

Hence if
m>1
there is nothing to prove.
We now proceed by induction, supposing the result is true for
n-1
.
Let
q\in P_{F}
be a maximal element. Again by
Lemma

5.2.4
, we have that
q\in P_{G}
and that
q
is a maximal element of
P_{G}
.
Arguing as above, we reduce to treat the case
F_{q}\simeq G_{q}
.
Since the traces of
Corollary

4.3.2
are additive in direct sums (more generally, in short exact sequences), we have that the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{t_{0}{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{Perv}_{P}(X)}
separates
x,y
if and only if it separates the point representing

F^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{p\in P\setminus\{q\}}(i_{\leq p})_{\ast}(j_{p})_{!\ast}F^{\prime}_{p}\ ,\qquad G^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{p\in P\setminus\{q\}}(i_{\leq p})_{\ast}(j_{p)_{!\ast}}G_{p}^{\prime}\ ,

where
F^{\prime}_{p}
(respectively
G^{\prime}_{p}
) is set to be equal to
F_{p}
(respectively
G_{p}
) if
p\neq q
, whereas we set
F^{\prime}_{p}=0
(respectively
G^{\prime}_{q}=0
).
The result now follows by our inductive hypothesis, since
P_{F^{\prime}}=P_{F}\setminus\{q\}
.
∎

Corollary 5.2.6
.

Let
(X,P)
be a complex Whitney stratified space and let
\mathfrak{p}\colon X\to\mathbb{Z}
be the middle perversity.
Let
k
be a noetherian ring containing
\mathbb{Q}
.
Then the perverse character variety
t_{0}{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)
is a disjoint union of quasi-affine scheme over
k
.

By
Theorem

5.1.1
the polystable points of
t_{0}{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)
are the semisimple perverse sheaves.
By
Theorem

4.2.2
and
Proposition

4.1.14
, the assumptions of
Theorem

5.2.5
are satisfied.
It then follows directly from the definition of good moduli space and
Theorem

2.0.2
-
(4)
that
\mathcal{O}_{t_{0}{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)}
separates points.
The claim follows by applying
Corollary

2.1.6
to each connected components of
t_{0}{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\Perv_{P}(X)
, which is possible thanks to
Theorem

5.1.9
.
∎

In
Corollary

5.2.6
we have shown the quasi-affineness in the important case of the middle perversity.
Our results are completely general and apply to any perversity as long as the corresponding perverse character variety exists.
Another example is given by the trivial perversity introduced in

4.1.16
, whose associated perverse
t
-structure is the standard

-structure, yielding a stack
\mathbf{Cons}_{P}(X)^{[0,0]}
parametrizing constructible sheaves with flat stalks (see

and
5.1.11
).
Recall the notions of (local) categorical compactness for a stratified space
(X,P)
from
[
PT22
, Definition 2.2.1]
).
