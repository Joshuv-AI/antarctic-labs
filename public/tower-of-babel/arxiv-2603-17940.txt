On the logarithmic coefficients of Ma-Minda type convex functions
Md Firoz Ali, Lokenath Thakur

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate three specific subclasses of Ma-Minda type convex functions: namely, convex functions of order α \alpha , Janowski convex functions, and Robertson functions of normalized analytic functions defined in the open unit disk. For these classes, we establish logarithmic coefficient inequalities concerning both individual coefficient estimates and weighted series. The results presented here correct some earlier erroneous results and extend several previously known ones.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the class of analytic functions defined in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. We define
\mathcal{A}
as the subclass of
\mathcal{H}
consisting of functions normalized by the power series expansion

(1.1)

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},

and let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{A}
be the class of functions that are univalent (one-to-one) in
\mathbb{D}
.
The study of coefficient bounds for
\mathcal{S}
is a cornerstone of geometric function theory. In
1916
, Bieberbach
[
7
]
demonstrated that for any
f\in\mathcal{S}
, the second coefficient satisfies
|a_{2}|\leq 2
, with equality occurring if and only if
f
is a rotation of the Koebe function
k(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}
. This observation led to the famous Bieberbach conjecture
|a_{n}|\leq n
for all
n\geq 3
, with the Koebe function again serving as the extremal case.

Central to the eventual resolution of the Bieberbach conjecture are the logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}
, defined by

(1.2)

F_{f}(z)=\log\frac{f(z)}{z}=2\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}z^{n}.

The importance of these coefficients was first recognized by Bazilević
[
5
]
. In subsequent work, Bazilević
[
6
]
studied the Dirichlet-type series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|\gamma_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}
, which relates to the area of the image of the disk
|z|<r<1
under the mapping

\frac{1}{2}F_{f}(z)
for
f\in\mathcal{S}
. A pivotal result in this domain is the de Branges inequality (resolving the earlier Milin conjecture) which asserts that if
f\in\mathcal{S}
and the coefficients
\gamma_{n}
are defined as in (
1.2
), then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k+1)|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{n-k+1}{k},\quad n=1,2,\cdots,

with equality holding precisely for Koebe function or one of its rotations (see
[
8
]
). This inequality was instrumental in de Branges’s proof of the long-standing Bieberbach conjecture. Furthermore, it inspired various subsequent inequalities, such as the well-known bound (see
[
9
]
)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

Along similar lines, Roth
[
24
]
established a sharp inequality for functions
f\in\mathcal{S}
:

(1.3)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n^{2}},\quad\lambda_{n}=\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{2}.

This inequality has become a source of many new inequalities for the logarithmic coefficients of univalent functions (see
[
18
]
). Roth’s inequality (
1.3
) was subsequently extended by Ponnusamy and Sugawa
[
19
]
, who established a family of sharp inequalities for logarithmic coefficients using convex sequences as weights.

While the average behavior of logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
has been studied extensively, (see
[
2
,
9
]
), precise upper bounds on
|\gamma_{n}|
for functions in the class
\mathcal{S}
remain scarce. The Koebe function
k(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}
, whose logarithmic coefficients are given by
\gamma_{n}=1/n
, typically serves as the extremal function for many problems in
\mathcal{S}
. This might lead to the conjecture that
|\gamma_{n}|\leq 1/n
holds generally for functions in
\mathcal{S}
. However, this conjecture fails dramatically, not only in precise values but also in terms of asymptotic behavior.
However, one of the reasons the logarithmic coefficients have received more attention is because, although the sharp bound

\displaystyle|\gamma_{1}|\leq 1\quad\mathrm{and}\quad|\gamma_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1+2e^{-2}\right)

for the class
\mathcal{S}
is known, the problem of determining the sharp bounds for
|\gamma_{n}|
when
n\geq 3
remains an open challenge.

For
f,g\in\mathcal{H}
, we say
f
is subordinate to
g
, denoted
f\prec g
, if there exists a function
\omega:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
\omega(0)=0
such that
f(z)=g(\omega(z))
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. If
g
is univalent, then
f\prec g
if and only if
f(0)=g(0)
and
f(\mathbb{D})\subset g(\mathbb{D})
.
For
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
with
\varphi(0)=1
, let the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
be defined by

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\right\},~\mathrm{and}~~\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\right\},

respectively. The classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
were introduced by Ma and Minda
[
13
]
under the additional hypotheses that
\varphi
is univalent with a positive real part in
\mathbb{D}
, and that
\varphi(\mathbb{D})
is symmetric with respect to the real axis and starlike with respect to
\varphi(0)=1
, with
\varphi^{\prime}(0)>0
. The classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
are called the Ma-Minda classes of starlike and convex functions, respectively. It is important to note that
f\in\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
if and only if
zf^{\prime}(z)\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
.

For different choices of
\varphi
, the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
reduce to several well-known classes which were earlier introduced and studied for their geometric and analytic properties. For instance, if
\varphi(z)=(1+z)/(1-z)
, then
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=:\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=:\mathcal{C}
, representing the usual classes of starlike and convex functions, respectively.
If
\varphi(z)=(1+(1-2\alpha)z)/(1-z)
with
0\leq\alpha<1
, then
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=:\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=:\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
, which are the well-known classes of starlike and convex functions of order
\alpha
, introduced in
[
22
]
. Similarly, if
\varphi(z)=(1+Az)/(1+Bz)
with
-1\leq B<A\leq 1
, then
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=:\mathcal{S}^{*}(A,B)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=:\mathcal{C}(A,B)
, denoting the classes of Janowski starlike and Janowski convex functions introduced in
[
11
,
12
]
.
If
\varphi=(1+(c-1)z)/(1-z)
with
0<c\leq 3
, then
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
reduces to the class
\mathcal{F}(c)
(see
[
18
]
). For
c\in(0,2]
, if we take
\alpha=1-c/2
, the family
\mathcal{F}(c)
coincides with the class
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
of convex functions of order
\alpha
. For
c=3
, the class
\mathcal{F}(3)
has received considerable attention in recent years (see
[
16
]
). Functions in
\mathcal{F}(3)
, are known to be convex in one direction, and are hence univalent and close-to-convex, but they are not necessarily starlike in
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
28
]
).
Furthermore, if
\varphi=(1+e^{-2i\alpha}z)/(1-z)
with
|\alpha|<\pi/2
, then
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
reduces to the Robertson class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
, introduced by Robertson
[
21
]
. Unlike convex or starlike functions, members of
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
are not necessarily univalent. Robertson
[
21
]
himself noted that univalence in
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
is not guaranteed for all
\alpha
. Indeed, it is now well known that functions in
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
are univalent if
0<\cos\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

In this article, we investigate inequalities concerning the logarithmic coefficients of functions belonging to the classes
\mathcal{F}(c)
,
\mathcal{C}(A,B)
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
. Our primary objective is to establish sharp upper bounds for these coefficients, thereby extending several results currently existing in the literature. Furthermore, we identify certain inaccuracies in previous studies and provide corrected versions of those results. The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we provide the formal statements of our main theorems, detailing the specific coefficient bounds obtained for each class. In Section
3
, we present the necessary auxiliary lemmas that serve as the foundation for our subsequent analysis. Finally, the proofs of the main theorems are developed in Section
4
.

2.
Main Results

2.1.
The class
\mathcal{F}(c)

Recall that a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}(c)
,
c\in(0,3]
if
f\in\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
with
\varphi=(1+(c-1)z)/(1-z)
. In other words, a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is in
\mathcal{F}(c)
if and only if

\operatorname{Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>1-\frac{c}{2}.

For a wide range of extremal problems within the class
\mathcal{F}(c)
, the function

(2.1)

\displaystyle f_{c}(z)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(1-z)^{1-c}-1}{c-1},&~~~c\neq 1,\\
\displaystyle-\log(1-z),&~~~c=1.\end{cases}

often serves as the extremal function. With the help of the function
f_{c}
, we define a new function
\psi(z)
as given below:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\psi(z)=\frac{zf^{\prime}_{c}(z)}{f_{c}(z)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(c-1)z}{1-z}\cdot\frac{1}{1-(1-z)^{c-1}},&c\neq 1,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{-z}{(1-z)\log(1-z)},&c=1.\end{cases}

This function will play an important role in the present article.

Recently, Cho et al.
[
3
]
studied the logarithmic coefficients for functions in the class
\mathcal{F}(c)
and proved the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

[
3
, Theorem 2.1 ]

For
f\in\mathcal{F}(c)
with
c\in(0,0.656]\cup\{2\}
, the logarithmic coefficients of
f
satisfies the inequality

(2.3)

|\gamma_{n}|\leq\frac{|D_{n}|}{2n},\quad n=1,2,3,\dots,

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|D_{n}|^{2}}{n^{2}},

where
D_{n}
are the Taylor coefficients of
\psi(z)
given by (
2.2
). Further, both the inequalities are sharp.

The proof of Theorem
A
(specially the estimate (
2.3
)) completely relies on the convexity of
\psi(z)
defined by (
2.2
). The authors of
[
3
]
claimed to verify the convexity of
\psi(z)
pictorially (see
[
3
, Fig. 1]
) using
Maple
™
software by showing that

(2.4)

\operatorname{Re}\Psi(z)=\operatorname{Re}\left(1+\frac{z\psi^{\prime\prime}(z)}{\psi^{\prime}(z)}\right)>0\quad\text{for}~z\in\mathbb{D}.

However, this claim is incorrect. Indeed, first we prove that the function
\psi(z)
is not convex for
c\in(1/2,2)
.

Proposition 1
.

The function
\psi(z)
defined by (
2.2
) is not convex when
c\in(1/2,2)
.

On the other hand, by numerical computation, we demonstrate that
\psi(z)
defined by (
2.2
) fails to be convex for several values of
c
in
(0,1/2]
. To show that
\psi(z)
is not convex for a certain value of
c\in(0,1/2]
, we show that

\operatorname{Re}\Psi(e^{i\theta})=\operatorname{Re}\left(1+\frac{e^{i\theta}\psi^{\prime\prime}(e^{i\theta})}{\psi^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})}\right)<0

for some values of
z=e^{i\theta}
,
0\leq\theta<2\pi
on the unit circle
|z|=1
.

, we demonstrate that

is not convex for selected values of

fails to be convex. This suggests that

established sharp estimates for

when

.

Let

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|D_{n}|^{2},

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{t}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)^{t}}{n^{2}}|D_{n}|^{2}\quad\text{for }t\leq 2,

where the

We note that if we take
\alpha=1-c/2
, then the class
\mathcal{F}(c)
for

\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
of convex functions of order

, where
\alpha\in[0,1)
.

2.2.
The class
\mathcal{C}(A,B)

For
-1\leq B<A\leq 1
, the class
\mathcal{C}(A,B)
of
Janowski convex functions
consists of functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying the subordination relation

1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}.

It is an easy exercise to see that the function
K_{A,B}
defined by

(2.5)

\displaystyle K_{A,B}(z)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}\left((1+Bz)^{A/B}-1\right),&A\neq 0,\;B\neq 0,\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1}{B}\log(1+Bz),&A=0,\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}\left(e^{Az}-1\right),&B=0,\end{cases}

is a function in
\mathcal{C}(A,B)
. Using the function, we define another function
\psi_{A,B}
as

(2.6)

\displaystyle\psi_{A,B}=\frac{zK^{\prime}_{A,B}(z)}{K_{A,B}(z)}=:1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}z^{n}.

It is worth noting that Cho et al.
[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
studied the logarithmic coefficients for the class
\mathcal{C}(0,B)
where
B\in[-0.99,0)
, establishing results analogous to Theorem
A
.

Theorem C
.

[
3
, Theorem 2.2]

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}[0,B]
for
-0.99\leq B<0
. Then, the logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
of
f
satisfy the inequalities

|\gamma_{n}|\leq\frac{|E_{n}|}{2n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|E_{n}|^{2}}{n^{2}},

where
E_{n}
are the Taylor coefficients of
\psi_{0,B}
defined by (
2.6
) and the inequalities are sharp.

The proof of Theorem
C
relies on the convexity of
\psi_{0,B}(z)
defined in (
2.6
). The authors claimed to verify this property pictorially (see
[
3
, Fig. 2]
) using
Maple
™
. From the first inequality of Theorem
C
, it follows that

|\gamma_{1}|\leq\frac{|B|}{4},\quad|\gamma_{2}|\leq\frac{5|B|^{2}}{48},\quad|\gamma_{3}|\leq\frac{|B|^{3}}{16}

for functions in the class
\mathcal{C}[0,B]
with
-0.99\leq B<0
. However, these estimates are not universally correct, as we demonstrate in Theorem
2.2
. This suggests that the underlying claim regarding the convexity of
\psi_{0,B}(z)
is incorrect.

\mathcal{C}(A,B)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}(A,B)
with
-1\leq B<A\leq 1
. Then the logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
of
f(z)
satisfy

\displaystyle|\gamma_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A-B}{4},

\displaystyle|\gamma_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{A-B}{12}&~~\text{for}~|A-5B|\leq 4,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{A-B}{48}|A-5B|&~~\text{for}~|A-5B|>4,\end{cases}

\displaystyle|\gamma_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{A-B}{24},&~~\text{for}~(\mu,\nu)\in D_{1}\cup D_{2},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{A-B}{48}|B(A-3B)|,&~~\text{for}~(\mu,\nu)\in D_{6},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{A-B}{72\sqrt{3}}\cdot\frac{\left(|A-5B|+2\right)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{|A-5B|+2+3B^{2}-AB}}&~~\text{for}~(\mu,\nu)\in D_{8}\cup D_{9},\end{cases}

where
\mu=\frac{1}{2}(A-5B)
and

and the sets

are defined as in Lemma

On the other hand, Allu and Sharma
[

proved logarithmic coefficient inequalities similar to Theorem
B
for functions in the classes
\mathcal{C}(0,B)
with
B\in[-0.99,0)
and
\mathcal{C}(A,0)
with
A\in(0,1]
.
In the next theorem, we prove logarithmic coefficient inequalities similar to Theorem
B
for functions in the class
\mathcal{C}(A,B)
with

, which generalize the previous results of Allu and Sharma
[

.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}(A,B)
with
-1\leq B<A\leq 1
. Then the logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
of
f
satisfy the inequalities

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|E_{n}|^{2},

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{t}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)^{t}}{n^{2}}|E_{n}|^{2}\quad\text{for }t\leq 2,

where the coefficients
E_{n}
are defined as in (

). Moreover, the first inequality is sharp.

2.3.
The class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}

For
-\pi/2<\alpha<\pi/2
, the class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
, sometimes known as the Robertson class, is defined as follows

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}=:\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\operatorname{Re}\left\{e^{i\alpha}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\right\}>0\right\}.

For the class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
, we obtain the following counterpart of Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}~(|\alpha|<\pi/2)
, then the logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
of
f
satisfy the inequalities

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|F_{n}|^{2},

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{t}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)^{t}}{n^{2}}|F_{n}|^{2}\quad\text{for }t\leq 2,

where the
F_{n}
are Taylor coefficients of the function

\psi_{2}(z)=\frac{Az}{(1-z)(1-(1-z)^{A})}=:1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}z^{n},

with
A=e^{-2i\alpha}
. Moreover, the first inequality is sharp.

In the next theorem, we obtain sharp estimates of the initial logarithmic coefficients for functions in the class
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
for
|\alpha|<\pi/2
, then the logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
of
f(z)
satisfy

\displaystyle|\gamma_{1}|\leq\frac{\cos\alpha}{2},\quad|\gamma_{2}|\leq\frac{\cos\alpha}{6}\sqrt{4+5\cos^{2}\alpha},\quad|\gamma_{3}|\leq\frac{\cos\alpha}{3}\sqrt{1+3\cos^{2}\alpha}.

All the estimates are sharp.

3.
Auxiliary Results

In this section, we list down some results which are known in the literature and are useful in the proof of our results.
Within the class
\mathcal{H}
, consider the subclass
\mathcal{B}
consisting of functions
\omega
satisfying
|\omega(z)|<1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and its subclass
\mathcal{B}_{0}
where
\omega(0)=0
. Any
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
admits a Taylor series of the form

(3.1)

\displaystyle\omega(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}.

With the class
\mathcal{B}_{0}
, a closely related class is the class
\mathcal{P}
. It consists of analytic functions
p(z)
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
satisfying

p(0)=1,\qquad\operatorname{Re}p(z)>0\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{D}.

Such functions are called
Carathéodory functions
. They appear naturally in the study of univalent functions.

In
1981
, Prokhorov et al.
[

z\left(\log\left(\frac{K_{A,B}(z)}{z}\right)\right)^{\prime}=\frac{zK^{\prime}_{A,B}(z)}{K_{A,B}(z)}-1=\psi_{A,B}-1

which leads to

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2n\gamma_{n}(K_{A,B})z^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}z^{n}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
then

1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1+e^{-2i\alpha}z}{1-z}=:\varphi_{2}(z).

Set
g(z)=zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)
, which is a non-vanishing analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
with
g(0)=1
. Then, from the aforementioned subordination, we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle g(z)+\frac{zg^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}=1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\varphi_{2}(z).

Assume that
\psi_{2}
satisfies the differential equation

(4.15)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(z)+\frac{z\psi_{2}^{\prime}(z)}{\psi_{2}(z)}=\varphi_{2}(z).

Since
\alpha\in(-\pi/2,~\pi/2)
, the function
\varphi_{2}
has a positive real part in
\mathbb{D}
. By Lemma
3.5
, the solution
\psi_{2}
that satisfies
\operatorname{Re}\psi_{2}>0
in
\mathbb{D}
is given by

\psi_{2}(z)=\frac{Az}{(1-z)(1-(1-z)^{A})},~~~A=e^{-2i\alpha}.

Furthermore, using Lemma
3.4
, we get
\psi_{2}
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. From relations (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) along with Lemma
3.4
, we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle g(z)\prec\psi_{2}(z)\quad\text{i.e.}\quad\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\psi_{2}(z)\quad\text{i.e.}~~~\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi_{2}),

Since
\psi_{2}(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}z^{n}
, it follows from (
4.16
) and Lemma
3.3
that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|F_{n}|^{2}

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{t}|\gamma_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)^{t}}{n^{2}}|F_{n}|^{2}\quad\text{for }t\leq 2.

The inequality is sharp for the function
h_{1}(z)=\left[(1-z)^{-A}-1\right]\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
, because

z\left(\log\left(\frac{h_{1}(z)}{z}\right)\right)^{\prime}=\frac{zh_{1}^{\prime}(z)}{h_{1}(z)}-1=\psi_{2}(z)-1

yielding

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2n\gamma_{n}(h_{1})z^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}z^{n}.

This completes the proof.
∎

2.5
.

1.1
) . Then
