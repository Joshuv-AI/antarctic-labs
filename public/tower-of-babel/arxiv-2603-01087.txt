Solutions to autonomous partial difference equations via the third and sixth Painlevé equations and the Garnier system in two variables
Nobutaka Nakazono

Abstract
. In this paper, we show that integrable autonomous partial difference equations admit special solutions described by the non-autonomous ordinary difference equations arising from the Bäcklund transformations of the third and sixth Painlevé equations and the Garnier system in two variables. Remarkably, although the equations themselves are autonomous, their special solutions are governed by non-autonomous ordinary difference equations.

Introduction

Integrable partial difference equations (P
\Delta
Es) on lattices play an important role in mathematical physics, appearing in soliton theory and discrete differential geometry.
Among them, integrable two-dimensional P
\Delta
Es admitting consistency properties form a fundamental class.
See,
e.g.
,
[
17
,
5
]
and references therein.

It is natural that special solutions of non-autonomous integrable P
\Delta
Es are described in terms of non-autonomous integrable ordinary difference equations (O
\Delta

e.g.
,
[
36
,
13
,

,
14
,

,

,

,

).
By contrast, the fact that special solutions of autonomous integrable P
\Delta

\Delta

e.g.
,
[
22
,
32
,
38
]
).
The mechanisms by which non-autonomous structures emerge at the level of special solutions of autonomous P
\Delta
Es therefore remain not yet fully understood.

In this paper, we show that the following five autonomous two-dimensional P
\Delta
Es admit special solutions described in terms of the non-autonomous O
\Delta
Es arising from the Bäcklund transformations of the third and sixth Painlevé equations and the Garnier system in two variables.
Remarkably, although the equations themselves are autonomous, their special solutions are governed by non-autonomous O
\Delta
Es.

[

]

(1.1)

A P

The lattice sine-Gordon (lsG) equation

\dfrac{~u_{l+1,m+1}~}{u_{l,m}}=\left(\dfrac{\alpha-\beta\,u_{l+1,m}}{\beta-\alpha\,u_{l+1,m}}\right)\left(\dfrac{\beta-\alpha\,u_{l,m+1}}{\alpha-\beta\,u_{l,m+1}}\right).

Remark 1.1
.

The
Q1_{\delta=1}
equation (
1.2
) and the lsG equation (
1.4
) each have exactly one essential parameter.
Indeed, by setting

Similarly, by setting

Remark 1.2
.

The HV equation (
1.3
) can be obtained from the

Indeed, substituting

\alpha=\epsilon(\epsilon+1),\quad\beta=\epsilon^{2},\quad u_{l,m}=\epsilon\left(2\epsilon U_{l,m}-(\epsilon+1)l-\epsilon\,m\right),

(1.6)

into Equation (

The construction of the special solutions in this paper does not rely on any known reduction procedure.
In a related setting, special solutions of P
\Delta

(hereafter referred to as dP solutions)

The special solutions obtained in this paper can also be regarded as belonging to this class in this sense.
However, since our approach treats difference equations as Bäcklund transformations of differential Painlevé equations and Garnier systems, and places emphasis not only on Painlevé equations but also on Garnier systems, we do not explicitly use the terminology of dP solutions in what follows.

Painlevé equations and Garnier systems

In the early 20th century, to find a new class of special functions, Painlevé
[
50
,
51
,
52
]
and Gambier
[
11
]
classified all the ordinary differential equations of the type

y^{\prime\prime}=F(y^{\prime},y,t),

(1.11)

where
y=y(t)
,
{}^{\prime}=d/dt
, and
F
is a function meromorphic in
t\in\mathbb{C}
and rational in
y
and
y^{\prime}
,
with the Painlevé property (solutions do not have movable singularities other than poles).
As a result, they obtained six new equations that are collectively referred to as Painlevé equations.
The Painlevé equations are numbered beginning with one: P
I
,
\dots
, P
VI
,
and starting from the sixth Painlevé equation, we can, through appropriate limiting processes, obtain other Painlevé equations:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}{\rm P}_{\rm VI}~(D_{4}^{(1)})&\to&{\rm P}_{\rm V}~(A_{3}^{(1)})&\to&{\rm P}_{\rm III}~(2A_{1}^{(1)})&\\
&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\\
&&{\rm P}_{\rm IV}~(A_{2}^{(1)})&\to&{\rm P}_{\rm II}~(A_{1}^{(1)})&\to&{\rm P}_{\rm I}\end{array}

It is well known that the Painlevé equations, except for P
I
, have Bäcklund transformations, which collectively form (extended) affine Weyl groups.
The symbols inside the parentheses of the diagram above indicate the types of affine Weyl groups.
(See
[
42
,
46
,
45
,
44
,
43
,
41
,
54
,
26
]
for the details.)
Note that P
VI
was found by Fuchs
[
10
]
through his study of linear differential equations, prior to the work of Painlevé
et al.

Garnier systems are multivariable generalizations of P
VI
, which were derived by Garnier
[
12
]
using the method developed by Fuchs
[
10
]
.
At present, as with the Painleve equations, Garnier systems have been studied from various perspectives, including Bäcklund transformations
[
27
,
28
,
55
,
58
,
57
]
, special solutions
[
47
,
58
,
59
,
62
]
, and applications to discrete holomorphic functions
[
23
]
.
See,
e.g.
,
[
56
]
for details on Garnier systems.

In this paper, we focus in particular on P
III
, P
VI
, and the Garnier system in two variables.

2.1.
The third Painlevé equation

Let
t\in\mathbb{C}
be the independent variable,
p=p(t)\in\mathbb{C}
and
q=q(t)\in\mathbb{C}
dependent variables, and
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
parameters.
P
III
is equivalent to the Hamiltonian system:

\dfrac{dq}{dt}=\dfrac{\partial H_{\rm III}}{\partial p},\quad\dfrac{dp}{dt}=-\dfrac{\partial H_{\rm III}}{\partial q},

(2.1)

with the Hamiltonian
[
26
,
46
]
:

H_{\rm III}=H(p,q,t\,;a_{1},a_{2})=\dfrac{1}{t}\left(p(p-1)q^{2}+(a_{1}+a_{2})pq+tp-a_{2}q\right).

(2.2)

We define the birational transformations
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
by their actions on
\{a_{1},a_{2},t,p,q\}
as follows:

\displaystyle T_{1}:(a_{1},a_{2},t)\mapsto(a_{1}+1,a_{2},t),

(2.3a)

\displaystyle T_{2}:(a_{1},a_{2},t)\mapsto(a_{1},a_{2}+1,t),

(2.3b)

\displaystyle T_{1}(p)=-\dfrac{tp}{T_{1}(q)^{2}}-\dfrac{a_{2}}{T_{1}(q)},\quad T_{1}(q)=\dfrac{t(p-1)}{a_{1}+(p-1)q},

(2.3c)

\displaystyle T_{2}(p)=\dfrac{t(p-1)}{T_{2}(q)^{2}}-\dfrac{a_{1}}{T_{2}(q)}+1,\quad T_{2}(q)=-\dfrac{tp}{pq+a_{2}}.

(2.3d)

By direct computation, these transformations satisfy the following relations as actions on
\{a_{1},a_{2},t,p,q\}
:

T_{1}\,\dfrac{d}{dt}=\dfrac{d}{dt}\,T_{1},\quad T_{2}\,\dfrac{d}{dt}=\dfrac{d}{dt}\,T_{2},\quad T_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}T_{1}.

(2.4)

Hence, the transformations
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are mutually commuting Bäcklund transformations of P
III
(
2.1
).
Accordingly, we define

and

and define

,

, and

by

\displaystyle p^{(k_{1},k_{2})}={T_{1}}^{k_{1}}{T_{2}}^{k_{2}}(p),\quad q^{(k_{1},k_{2})}={T_{1}}^{k_{1}}{T_{2}}^{k_{2}}(q),

(2.6a)

\displaystyle H_{\rm III}^{(k_{1},k_{2})}=H\left(p^{(k_{1},k_{2})},q^{(k_{1},k_{2})},t\,;a_{1}^{(k_{1})},a_{2}^{(k_{2})}\right),

(2.6b)

so that

\dfrac{dq^{(k_{1},k_{2})}}{dt}=\dfrac{\partial H_{\rm III}^{(k_{1},k_{2})}}{\partial p^{(k_{1},k_{2})}},\quad\dfrac{dp^{(k_{1},k_{2})}}{dt}=-\dfrac{\partial H_{\rm III}^{(k_{1},k_{2})}}{\partial q^{(k_{1},k_{2})}},

is again equivalent to P

the O

\displaystyle p^{(k_{1}+1,k_{2})}=-\dfrac{tp^{(k_{1},k_{2})}}{{q^{(k_{1}+1,k_{2})}}^{2}}-\dfrac{a_{2}^{(k_{2})}}{q^{(k_{1}+1,k_{2})}},

Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have shown that Equations (

It is noteworthy that although Equations (

\Delta

In the paper
[

]
, it was shown that special solutions of a variation of the dKdV equation:

However, since the bilinear difference equation satisfied by the tau function associated with Equation (

[

]
), it is understandable that special solutions expressed in such non-autonomous O
\Delta

On the other hand, the autonomous P
\Delta

)–(
1.5
) are autonomous also from the viewpoint of tau functions, and nevertheless possess special solutions expressed by non-autonomous O
\Delta

In this sense, they provide more mysterious examples.
However, similar phenomena have already been reported in several works, where special solutions are regarded not as the dependent variables of Painlevé equations or the Garnier system themselves, but as their tau functions.
For example, see the work by Nijhoff
et al.

[
38
]
and its extension by Joshi
et al.

[

]
.
In the former, a special solution of the autonomous version of the lattice Schwarzian KdV equation
[

is constructed by the tau function of P

Before concluding this section, let us briefly explain our future work.
In the paper
[

]
, it was shown that the autonomous P
\Delta

\Delta

What, then, about cases other than

-Painlevé equations?
As mentioned in the Introduction, noting that the special solutions given in this paper can also be called dP solutions, we have shown that P

Although this paper mainly deals with autonomous P
