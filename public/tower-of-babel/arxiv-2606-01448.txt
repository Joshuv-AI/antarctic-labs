Convergence to global equilibrium for the semiconductor Boltzmann equation
Gayrat Toshpulatov

Abstract
The Boltzmann equation describing the transport of electrons in semiconductor devices with an external electrostatic potential is considered when the spatial variable is in a torus and the wave vector is in the Brillouin zone. We prove the exponential time decay of solutions towards the global equilibrium in a weighted L 2 L^{2} space. Our result holds for wide classes of energy functions of electrons and external electrostatic potentials, and the estimates on the rate of convergence are explicit and constructive. We remove the close-to-equilibrium assumption on the initial datum and the parabolic band approximation assumption that were required in previous works. The technique is based on the construction of a suitable highly non-linear Lyapunov functional by modifying the relative entropy. The analysis benefits from uniform bounds for the solution in terms of the global equilibrium.



Keywords:
Semi-classical kinetic equations, Boltzmann equation for semiconductors, convergence to equilibrium, Fermi-Dirac distribution, hypocoercivity, Lyapunov functional.

Introduction

In semiconductor devices, electrical currents originate from the transport of electrons and holes. In semi-classical kinetic description, the statistical evolution of electrons can be described by
the following spatially inhomogeneous Boltzmann equation
[
32
,
26
,
28
]

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(t,x,k)+\frac{1}{\hbar}\nabla_{k}{\mathcal{E}}(k)\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,k)-\frac{q}{\hbar}\nabla_{x}V(x)\cdot\nabla_{k}f(t,x,k)=Q(f)(t,x,k)\\
\displaystyle f(0,x,k)=f_{0}(x,k).\end{cases}

Here the variables
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
stand for time and position, respectively. In practice,
d
is actually equal to two or three, however, in this paper,
d
can be any natural number. The variable
k
stands for wave vector and it belongs to the
Brillouin zone

B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
associated with the underlying crystal lattice. The Brillouin zone
B
is the elementary cell of the dual lattice and it will be identified with the
d
-dimensional torus corresponding to the periodicity of the lattice. We have the property of
B
that
k\in B
implies
-k\in B.
The constants
\hbar
and
q
are respectively the Planck constant and the positive elementary charge. The function
\mathcal{E}(k)
describes the energy of electrons. The expression of
\mathcal{E}(k)
is determined by acceptable solutions of the Schrödinger equation for the actual crystal potential, which varies markedly from solid to another. The function
V(x)
describes a given external electrostatic potential. In general, the electrostatic potential is deduced from the distribution function through the Coulomb interaction, but for sake of simplicity, we shall assume here that it is given and does not depend on time
t.
The unknown
f(t,x,k)\geq 0
describes the phase space distribution function of particles. The operator
Q
describes the interactions with the particles and the device, and it can be written as

\displaystyle Q(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\big(s(x,k^{\prime},k)f^{\prime}(1-f)-s(x,k,k^{\prime})f(1-f^{\prime})\big)dk^{\prime},

where
f=f(t,x,k)
and
f^{\prime}=f(t,x,k^{\prime}).
The function
s(x,k^{\prime},k)
is non-negative and presents the probability for a particle to change its wave vector
k
into another
k^{\prime}
during interaction at the position
x
. We assume that the
principle of detailed balance
holds, according to which

s(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{\frac{\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})}{K_{B}T}}=s(x,k^{\prime},k)e^{\frac{\mathcal{E}(k)}{K_{B}T}},

where
K_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant and
T
is the temperature of crystal. This relation is a sufficient condition for the Fermi-Dirac distributions to be in the null space of
Q
. We denote

\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}s(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{\frac{\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})}{K_{B}T}}.

Then the principle of detailed balance condition provides that
\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})=\sigma(x,k^{\prime},k)
.

The wave
vector
k
is usually taken in a bounded set, the Brillouin zone
B
, which is the most physically
relevant case. However, when the particles under consideration have energies close to an extremum of an energy band, then the
parabolic band approximation

[
32
,
26
]
is used, which consists in taking

\mathcal{E}(k)=\frac{\hbar}{2m}|k|^{2}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,B=\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
m
is the effective mass of the particle.
To include the parabolic band approximation case, in this paper,
B
is a bounded set (which is identified with the torus corresponding to the periodicity of the lattice) or
\mathbb{R}^{d}.
If
B=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\mathcal{E}(k)
is a quadratic polynomial as above.

For simplicity, we set all physical constants to unity:
\hbar=q=K_{B}=T=m=1.
This is not a restriction since one can check that our results hold without this condition. Therefore, we shall consider the normalized equation

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(t,x,k)+\nabla_{k}{\mathcal{E}}(k)\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,k)-\nabla_{x}V(x)\cdot\nabla_{k}f(t,x,k)=Q(f)(t,x,k)\\
\displaystyle f(0,x,k)=f_{0}(x,k).\end{cases}

(1)

with

Q(f)=\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})\big(e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}(1-f)f^{\prime}-e^{-\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})}(1-f^{\prime})f\big)dk^{\prime}.

Equation (
1
) has several properties following standard physical considerations.
The factors
(1-f)
and
(1-f^{\prime})
appearing in
Q
take into account the Pauli exclusion principle. The distribution function is supposed to satisfy

0\leq f\leq 1.

Whenever
f(t,x,k)
is a (well-behaved) solution of (
1
), one has
global conservation of mass

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{B}f(t,x,k)dkdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{B}f_{0}(x,k)dkdx,\,\,\,\,\forall\,t\geq 0.

Equation (
1
) is
dissipative
in the sense that the following entropy functional decreases under the time-evolution of the solution
f
: For functions
f
satisfying
0<f<1
(
f
is not necessarily the solution), we define the
entropy functional

\mathrm{H}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\big(f\log{f}+(1-f)\log{(1-f)+f(\mathcal{E}(k)+V(x))}\big)dkdx.

(2)

If we have the existence of a solution
f\in(0,1)
which is regular enough, one can check (see (
4.1
) below), for all
t>0,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}(f(t))=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F-F^{\prime})(\log{F}-\log{F^{\prime}})dk^{\prime}dkdx,

(3)

where
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(k),

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})
,
F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{f(t,x,k)}{e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)-V(x)}(1-f(t,x,k))},
and
F^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{f(t,x,k^{\prime})}{e^{-\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})-V(x)}(1-f(t,x,k^{\prime}))}.

Since the function
s\mapsto\log{s}
is increasing for
s>0
, we have

(F-F^{\prime})(\log{F}-\log{F^{\prime}})\geq 0,

hence

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}(f(t))\leq 0,\,\,\,\forall\,t>0.

This means that
\mathrm{H}(f(t))
decreases under the time-evolution of the solution
f
. Moreover, the entropy dissipation functional (the integral on the right hand side of (
3
)) vanishes only if
F=F^{\prime}
. This means that
F
does not depend on
k
and so

f(t,x,k)=\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}(k)+V(x)-\mu(t,x)}}

(4)

for some function
\mu(t,x).
Hence, any function
f
in the form of (
4
) is a
local equilibrium
for (
1
). For such
f
both left and right hand sides of (
1
) vanish if
\mu
is constant. Hence, the unique
global equilibrium
for (
1
) is

f_{\infty}(x,k)=\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}(k)+V(x)-\mu_{\infty}}},

(5)

where
\mu_{\infty}>0
is determined by mass conservation

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}f_{0}(x,k)dkdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}f_{\infty}(x,k)dkdx.

The function
f_{\infty}(x,k)
is called the Fermi-Dirac distribution.

On the basis of the decay of
\mathrm{H}(f(t)),
one can conjecture that
f(t)
converges to the global equilibrium
f_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty.
Thus, it is a fundamental problem to prove (or disprove) this convergence and to estimate the convergence rate. Our goal is to prove this convergence and to derive explicit and constructive estimates for convergence rate.

Proving decay of solutions to equilibrium is one of the fundamental problems for kinetic equations. For spatially homogeneous kinetic equations it is well-known that explicit estimates on convergence to equilibrium can be obtained by the direct study of the relative entropy and its dissipation functional. With the help of so-called Sobolev inequalities one can obtain a Grönwall inequality for the relative entropy, which implies convergence of the solution to the global equilibrium. Unfortunately, this idea can not be used for spatially inhomogeneous kinetic equations. The reason is that the collision operator acts only on the velocity variable, which causes to loose information about
x-
direction in the dissipation functional. To deal with this problem, in recent years, many new methods, so called hypocoercivity methods, are introduced to study the long-time behavior of spatially inhomogeneous kinetic equations.
The challenge of hypocoercivity is to understand the interplay between the collision operator that provides dissipativity in the velocity variable and the transport one which is conservative, in order to obtain global dissipativity for the whole problem. There are several hypocoercivity methods: see
[
17
,
44
,
39
]
for the entropy-entropy dissipation method,
[
36
,
25
,
44
,
1
,
4
,

]
for the

-hypocoercivity method,
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
for the

-hypocoercivity method, and
[

for some other methods.

Concerning the long time behavior of the semiconductor Boltzmann equation (
1
), there are results only in the case of the parabolic band approximation (i.e.,
\mathcal{E}(k)=|k|^{2}
and
B=\mathbb{R}^{d}
) and without the electrostatic potential (i.e.,
V(x)
is identically zero): Neumann and Schmeiser
[
39
]
obtained a polynomial rate of convergence to equilibrium. Their proof is based on the entropy-entropy dissipation method and uniform regularity bounds on the solution. Using the
H^{1}
-hypocoercivity method and assuming that the initial data is close to equilibrium (i.e.,
f_{0}-f_{\infty}
is sufficiently small) in a Sobolev space, an exponential decay result was obtained in
[
36
]
. Recently, Chen, Liu, and Wan
[
14
]
considered the semiconductor Boltzmann equation with the diffusive reflection boundary condition. They proved the exponential convergence of the solution to the global equilibrium in
L^{\infty}
when the initial data is close to equilibrium.
Pirner and the author
[
40
]
proved that when initial data is bounded in terms of the equilibrium, then the solution converges to the equilibrium exponentially.

As mentioned above, the long time behavior of solutions has been studied in the case of the parabolic band approximation and without the electrostatic potential. In this paper, we shall improve these previous results. We consider the most physically relevant case by letting the wave
vector
k
be in a bounded set, the Brillouin zone
B
. The energy function
\mathcal{E}(k)
can be non-quadratic, and we only require that the matrix
\int_{B}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{E}(k)}{\partial k^{2}}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}dk
is positive definite. We assume the electrostatic potential
V
is a given smooth function. We do not require the close-to-equilibrium assumption on the initial data. We prove exponential decay of the solution to the global equilibrium. Our results also include the parabolic band approximation case.
The proof is based on the construction of a suitable Lyapunov functional for (
1
). This functional has the following form

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(f(t))\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}

\displaystyle\left(f(t,x,k)\log\frac{f(t,x,k)}{f_{\infty}(x,k)}+(1-f(t,x,k))\log\frac{1-f(t,x,k)}{1-f_{\infty}(x,k)}\right)dkdx

\displaystyle+\delta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{k}\mathcal{E}(k)\log\left(\frac{f(t,x,k)}{1-f(t,x,k)}\right)e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}dkdx,

(6)

where
\delta>0
is a small constant and the function
\phi
is the solution of the elliptic equation

-\mathrm{div}_{x}(A\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x))=\int_{B}\big(f(t,x,k)-f_{\infty}(x,k)\big)dk,\,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\phi(t,x)dx=0,\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\,t\geq 0

with

A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{B}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{E}(k)}{\partial k^{2}}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}dk\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.

One can check that the first term on the right hand side of (
1
) is equal to the
relative entropy

\mathrm{H}(f|f_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{H}(f)-\mathrm{H}(f_{\infty}).

Hence, one can consider
\mathrm{E}(f)
as a modification of the relative entropy. The second term in (
1
) is chosen so that, if the initial data is bounded in terms of the equilibrium (see (
7
) below), then this functional is equivalent to the square of the weighted
L^{2}
-norm

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\big(f(t,x,k)-f_{\infty}(x,k)\big)^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}(k)}dxdk

and it satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{E}(f(t))\leq-2\lambda\mathrm{E}(f(t)),\,\,\,\forall\,t>0

for some
\lambda>0
(see the proof of Theorem
2.1
). This inequality lets us conclude that the solution
f(t,x,v)
converges to the equilibrium
f_{\infty}(x,k)
exponentially in the above
L^{2}
-norm.

Compared to previous hypercoercivity methods, the Lyapunov functional
\mathrm{E}(f)
seems new and can be useful to study long time behavior of other semi-classical kinetic equations. The results of this work provide an extension to the results of
[
36
,
39
,
14
,
40
]
because we do not require the parabolic band approximation and close-to-equilibrium assumptions, and the electrostatic potential does not have to be identically zero.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we state the main results. In Section 3, we establish existence of a unique distributional solution. In Sections 4 and 5, we study the relative entropy and its dissipation, and prove some estimates involving a non-linear projection operator. In section 6, we construct a Lyapunov functional and present the proof of our main result.

Main results

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:B\to\mathbb{R},

V:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\sigma:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B\times B\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions.
Assume that there exist constants
\sigma_{+}>0,

\mu_{1}>0,
and
\mu_{2}>0
such that

0\leq\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})\leq\sigma_{+},\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\,\,\forall\,k,k^{\prime}\in B,

and

\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}(k)+V(x)-\mu_{1}}}\leq f_{0}(x,k)\leq\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}(k)+V(x)-\mu_{2}}},\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\forall\,k\in B.

Then there is a unique distributional solution
f\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B))
to (
1
) satisfying the same bounds

\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}(k)+V(x)-\mu_{1}}}\leq f(t,x,k)\leq\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}(k)+V(x)-\mu_{2}}}

(7)

for all
t\geq 0,

x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},
and
k\in B.
Moreover, assume that the matrix

\int_{B}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{E}(k)}{\partial k^{2}}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}dk\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

is positive definite, and there is a constant
\sigma_{-}>0
such that

\sigma_{-}\leq\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime}),\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\,\,\forall\,k,k^{\prime}\in B.

Then there exist explicitly computable constants
\lambda>0
and
c>0
such that the solution
f
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\big(f(t,x,k)-f_{\infty}(x,k)\big)^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}(k)}dkdx\leq ce^{-2\lambda t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\big(f_{0}(x,k)-f_{\infty}(x,k)\big)^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}(k)}dkdx

(8)

for all
t\geq 0.

Note that (
8
) shows that
f(t,x,k)
converges exponentially to
f_{\infty}(x,k)
in the weighted
L^{2}
-norm

\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\big(f(t,x,k)-f_{\infty}(x,k)\big)^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}(k)}dxdk}.

If the Brillouin zone
B
is bounded, then
e^{\mathcal{E}}
is bounded from below and above, hence this weighted
L^{2}
-norm is equivalent to the usual
L^{2}
-norm.

We will show in the proof of Lemma
6.1
that the relative entropy
\mathrm{H}(f(t)|f_{\infty})
is equivalent to the square of the above weighted
L^{2}
-norm. Therefore, (
8
) is equivalent to

\mathrm{H}(f(t)|f_{\infty})\leq\tilde{c}e^{-2\lambda t}\mathrm{H}(f_{0}|f_{\infty}),\,\,\,\,\forall\,t\geq 0,

for some constant
\tilde{c}>0
.

As mentioned above, in Theorem
2.1

B
is a bounded set (which is identified with the torus corresponding to the periodicity of the lattice) or
\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The case
B=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{E}(k)=|k|^{2}
corresponds to the parabolic band approximation. In this case we can compute

\int_{B}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{E}(k)}{\partial k^{2}}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}dk=\left(\frac{1}{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|k|^{2}e^{-|k|^{2}}dk\right)\mathrm{Id}=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{2}\mathrm{Id},

where
\mathrm{Id}
denotes the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Since this matrix is positive definite, Theorem
2.1
also holds for the parabolic band approximation case. One can check that the examples of
\mathcal{E}(k)
in
[

, Appendix A]
also satisfy this condition.

There are more general models than Equation (

): As done in previous works

,

,

,

3
Existence and uniqueness

In this section we prove the existence part of Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
2.1
, there is a unique distributional solution
f\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B))
to (
1
) satisfying the bounds (
7
).

Proof.

We first define the following operators

\mathcal{A}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})f^{\prime}dk^{\prime},

\mathcal{B}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})(1-f^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}dk^{\prime}.

With these notations
f
is a solution if it satisfies

\partial_{t}f+\nabla_{k}{\mathcal{E}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{k}f+[\mathcal{A}(f)+\mathcal{B}(f)]f=\mathcal{A}(f).

We define the set

\mathcal{V}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\big\{f\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)):\,\,f\,\,\text{satisfies}\,\,\eqref{b1}\big\}.

For a given
f\in\mathcal{V}
, let
g
be the solution of the linear transport equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}g+\nabla_{k}{\mathcal{E}}\cdot\nabla_{x}g-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{k}g+[\mathcal{A}(f)+\mathcal{B}(f)]g=\mathcal{A}(f)\\
g(0,x,k)=f_{0}(x,k).\end{cases}

We then define the map
\Gamma(f)=g.
Obviously, a fixed point of
\Gamma
correspond to a solution of the equation.
We show that
\Gamma
maps
\mathcal{V}
to itself.
Let
r\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}g-\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}r+\nabla_{k}{\mathcal{E}}\cdot\nabla_{x}r-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{k}r+[\mathcal{A}(f)+\mathcal{B}(f)]r=\left(1-\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}\right)\mathcal{A}(f)-\frac{\mathcal{B}(f)}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}.

Since
\mathcal{A}(f)
and
\mathcal{B}(f)
are respectively increasing and decreasing functions of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(f)-\frac{\mathcal{B}(f)}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}\right)\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}\right)-\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}\right)}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}

\displaystyle=Q\left(\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}\right)=0.

This estimate shows

\partial_{t}r+\nabla_{k}{\mathcal{E}}\cdot\nabla_{x}r-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{k}r+[\mathcal{A}(f)+\mathcal{B}(f)]r\geq 0.

Since
r(0,x,k)\geq 0
, we conclude
r(t,x,k)\geq 0.
This means
g\geq\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{1}}}
. Similar arguments as above imply
g\leq\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{2}}}
and so
g\in\mathcal{V}.

Next, we show that
\Gamma
is a contraction for sufficiently small time
intervals.
Let
f
and
h
be in
\mathcal{V}.
We define
g\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gamma(f)
and
u\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Gamma(h).

Then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(g-u)+\nabla_{k}{\mathcal{E}}\cdot\nabla_{x}(g-u)

\displaystyle-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{k}(g-u)+[\mathcal{A}(f)+\mathcal{B}(f)](g-u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-u)\mathcal{A}(f-h)-u\mathcal{B}(f-h).

We multiply this equation by
\mathrm{sign}(g-u)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|g-u|+\nabla_{k}{\mathcal{E}}\cdot\nabla_{x}|g-u|

\displaystyle-\nabla_{x}V\cdot\nabla_{k}|g-u|+[\mathcal{A}(f)+\mathcal{B}(f)]|g-u|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{sign}(g-u)[(1-u)\mathcal{A}(f-h)-u\mathcal{B}(f-h)].

We integrate and use
\mathcal{A}(f)+\mathcal{B}(f)\geq 0

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}||g-u||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)}\leq||(1-u)A(f-h)-uB(f-h)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)}.

Using
0<u<1
we estimate

||(1-u)\mathcal{A}(f-h)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)}\leq||\mathcal{A}(f-h)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)}\leq\sigma_{+}\left(\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}dk\right)||f-h||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)}.

Since
u\in\mathcal{V}
and
u\leq\frac{1}{1+e^{\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}+\mathcal{E}-\mu_{2}}},
we have

||u\mathcal{B}(f-h)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)}\leq\sigma_{+}e^{-\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}}\left(\int_{B}\frac{dk}{1+e^{\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}+\mathcal{E}(k)-\mu_{2}}}\right)||f-h||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)}.

This leads

\displaystyle||\Gamma(f)(t)-\Gamma(h)(t)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{B}\sigma_{+}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}dk+\int_{B}\frac{\sigma_{+}e^{-\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}}dk}{1+e^{\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}+\mathcal{E}(k)-\mu_{2}}}\right]\int_{0}^{t}||f(s)-h(s)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)}ds.

Then the fixed point arguments provide the existence of a unique solution.
∎

4
Relative Entropy

In this section we show that the relative entropy decays, and we get bounds on the collision operator with respect to the dissipation functional.

For non-negative function
f
we define the relative entropy

\mathrm{H}(f|f_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\left(f\log\frac{f}{f_{\infty}}+(1-f)\log\frac{1-f}{1-f_{\infty}}\right)dkdx.

We note, if
f
and
f_{\infty}
have the same mass, then

\mathrm{H}(f|f_{\infty})=\mathrm{H}(f)-\mathrm{H}(f_{\infty}).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:B\to\mathbb{R},

V:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\sigma:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B\times B\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions. Let
f
be in
W^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B)
for all
T>0
and
0<f<1.
If
f
is a solution of (
1
), then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}(f(t)|f_{\infty})=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}

\displaystyle\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})\times

\displaystyle\times(F-F^{\prime})(\log F-\log F^{\prime})dk^{\prime}dkdx\leq 0,

(9)

where
F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{f}{(1-f)e^{-\mathcal{E}-V}}.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}(f(t)|f_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\left(\partial_{t}f\log\frac{f}{1-f}-\partial_{t}f\log\frac{f_{\infty}}{1-f_{\infty}}\right)dxdk

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\left(\partial_{t}f\log\frac{f}{(1-f)}+\partial_{t}f(\mathcal{E}+V-\mu_{\infty})\right)dxdk.

The conservation of mass implies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}f\mu_{\infty}dxdk=0.

Integrating by parts one can easily show

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\left(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{E}(k)\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{x}V(x)\cdot\nabla_{k}f\right)(\mathcal{E}(k)+V(x)))dkdx=0.

We can write

\nabla_{x}f\log\frac{f}{1-f}=\nabla_{x}\left(f\log{f}+(1-f)\log{(1-f)}\right)

and

\nabla_{k}f\log\frac{f}{(1-f)}=\nabla_{k}\left(f\log{f}+(1-f)\log{(1-f)}\right).

These equations and integration by parts show

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\left(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{E}(k)\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{x}V(x)\cdot\nabla_{k}f\right)\log\frac{f}{1-f}dkdx=0.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}(f(t)|f_{\infty})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}Q(f)\log\frac{f}{(1-f)e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)-V(x)}}dxdk.

By recalling
F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{f}{(1-f)e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)-V(x)}}
we compute

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}Q(f)\log{F}dxdk

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\left(\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F^{\prime}-F)\log Fdk^{\prime}\right)dkdx.

In this equation we change the order of the integrals with respect to
k^{\prime}
and
k
, and use
\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})=\sigma(x,k^{\prime},k)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}Q(f)\log{F}dxdk

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\left(\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F-F^{\prime})\log F^{\prime}dk^{\prime}\right)dkdx.

We add the last two equations and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B}Q(f)\log{F}dxdk

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F-F^{\prime})(\log F-\log F^{\prime})dk^{\prime}dkdx\leq 0.

∎

We define the dissipation function

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F-F^{\prime})(\log F-\log F^{\prime})dk^{\prime}dkdx.

With this notation we can write (
4.1
) as

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}(f(t)|f_{\infty})=-\mathcal{D}(f(t))\leq 0.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:B\to\mathbb{R},

V:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\sigma:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B\times B\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions. Assume that there exists a constant
\sigma_{+}>0
such that

0\leq\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})\leq\sigma_{+},\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\,\,\forall\,k,k^{\prime}\in B.

Then, for functions
f
satisfying (
7
), there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}|Q(f)|^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dkdx\leq C_{1}\mathcal{D}(f).

(10)

Proof.

The Hölder inequality provides

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}|Q(f)|^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}+V}dkdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\left(\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F^{\prime}-F)dk^{\prime}\right)^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}+V}dkdx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\left(\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})dk^{\prime}\right)\times

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\times\left(\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F^{\prime}-F)^{2}dk^{\prime}\right)e^{\mathcal{E}+V}dkdx.

Since
\sigma(x,k^{\prime},k)\leq\sigma_{+},

1-f\leq 1,
and
1-f^{\prime}\leq 1,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}|Q(f)|^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}+V}dkdx

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{+}\left(\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})}dk^{\prime}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime})e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F^{\prime}-F)^{2}dk^{\prime}dkdx

\displaystyle=2\sigma_{+}\left(\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})}dk^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{D}(f).

This proves the claimed estimate with
C_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}2\sigma_{+}e^{-\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}}\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})}dk^{\prime}
.
∎

5
Projection operator

We denote the macroscopic densities

\rho_{\infty}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{B}f_{\infty}(x,k)dk

and

\rho(t,x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{B}f(t,x,k)dk.

We define a nonlinear projection operator

\Pi f(t,x,k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{1+e^{\mathcal{E}(k)+V(x)-\mu(x,t)}},

(11)

where
\mu(t,x)
is chosen by the condition

\int_{B}\Pi f(t,x,k)dk=\rho(t,x).

In the following we prove some estimates involving the projection operator
\Pi.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:B\to\mathbb{R}
and
V:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions.
Then, for functions
f
satisfying (
7
), there are constant
{C}_{2}>0,

{C}_{3}>0,
and
{C}_{4}>0
such that

{C}_{2}(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}\leq(\rho-\rho_{\infty})^{2}e^{-\mathcal{E}}\leq{C}_{3}(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}.

(12)

and

{C}_{4}(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}\leq({\mu}-{\mu_{\infty}})(\rho-\rho_{\infty})e^{-\mathcal{E}}.

(13)

Proof.

By the definition of
\Pi
we have

\rho-\rho_{\infty}=\int_{B}(\Pi f-f_{\infty})dk=(e^{\mu}-e^{\mu_{\infty}})e^{-V}\int_{B}(1-\Pi f)(1-f_{\infty})e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk

and

e^{\mu}-e^{\mu_{\infty}}=\frac{\Pi f-f_{\infty}}{(1-\Pi f)(1-f_{\infty})e^{-\mathcal{E}-V}}.

These equations imply

(\rho-\rho_{\infty})^{2}=\frac{(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{2\mathcal{E}}}{(1-\Pi f)^{2}(1-f_{\infty})^{2}}\left(\int_{B}(1-\Pi f)(1-f_{\infty})e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk\right)^{2}

(14)

and

\displaystyle(\rho-\rho_{\infty})(e^{\mu}-e^{\mu_{\infty}})

\displaystyle=\frac{(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{2\mathcal{E}}e^{V}}{(1-\Pi f)^{2}(1-f_{\infty})^{2}}\int_{B}(1-\Pi f)(1-f_{\infty})e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{2\mathcal{E}}e^{\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}}}{(1-\Pi f)^{2}(1-f_{\infty})^{2}}\int_{B}(1-\Pi f)(1-f_{\infty})e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk.

(15)

Because of the bound (
7
) we can estimate

0<\frac{e^{\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-\mu_{2}}}{1+e^{\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-\mu_{2}}}\leq 1-f<1.

The same bounds also hold for
1-\Pi f
and
1-f_{\infty}.
In particular,
\int_{B}(1-\Pi f)(1-f_{\infty})e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk
is bounded from below and above by positive constants. These bounds and (
14
) show that (
12
) holds.

Next, (
5
) shows that
\rho-\rho_{\infty}
and
e^{\mu}-e^{\mu_{\infty}}
have the same sign. Since
e^{\mu}-e^{\mu_{\infty}}
and
\mu-\mu_{\infty}
have the same sign, the mean value theorem and (
7
) provide

(\rho-\rho_{\infty})(e^{\mu}-e^{\mu_{\infty}})\leq e^{\mu_{2}}(\rho-\rho_{\infty})({\mu}-{\mu_{\infty}}).

The last two estimates show

(\rho-\rho_{\infty})({\mu}-{\mu_{\infty}})\geq\frac{(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{2\mathcal{E}}e^{\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-\mu_{2}}}{(1-\Pi f)^{2}(1-f_{\infty})^{2}}\int_{B}(1-\Pi f)(1-f_{\infty})e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk.

Since
1-\Pi f,

1-f_{\infty},
and
\int_{B}(1-\Pi f)(1-f_{\infty})e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk
are bounded from below and above by positive constants, (
13
) also holds.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:B\to\mathbb{R},

V:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\sigma:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times B\times B\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions. Assume there is a constant
\sigma_{-}>0
such that

\sigma_{-}\leq\sigma(x,k,k^{\prime}),\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\,\,\forall\,k,k^{\prime}\in B.

Then, for functions
f
satisfying (
7
), there exists a constant
C_{5}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(f)\geq C_{5}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{{B}}(f-\Pi f)^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dkdx.

(16)

Proof.

The mean value theorem implies that there exists
\theta\in[0,1]
such that

(F-F^{\prime})(\log F-\log F^{\prime})=\frac{(F-F^{\prime})^{2}}{\theta F+(1-\theta)F^{\prime}}.

By (
7
) we have
\theta F+(1-\theta)F^{\prime}\leq e^{\mu_{2}}
and so

(F-F^{\prime})(\log F-\log F^{\prime})\geq e^{-\mu_{2}}(F-F^{\prime})^{2}.

This estimate and the lower bound on
\sigma
imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(f)\geq\frac{\sigma_{-}e^{-\mu_{2}}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F-F^{\prime})^{2}dk^{\prime}dkdx.

(17)

We have

\displaystyle(F-F^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle=(F-e^{\mu}+e^{\mu}-F^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{f-\Pi f}{e^{-\mathcal{E}-V}(1-f)(1-\Pi f)}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{f^{\prime}-\Pi f^{\prime}}{e^{-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f^{\prime})(1-\Pi f^{\prime})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,-\frac{2(f-\Pi f)(f^{\prime}-\Pi f^{\prime})}{e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-2V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(1-\Pi f)(1-\Pi f^{\prime})}.

This equation let us write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F-F^{\prime})^{2}dk^{\prime}dkdx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(1-\Pi f)(1-\Pi f^{\prime})(F-F^{\prime})^{2}dk^{\prime}dkdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}e^{\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}+V}(1-f^{\prime})(1-\Pi f^{\prime})\frac{(f-\Pi f)^{2}}{(1-f)(1-\Pi f)}dk^{\prime}dkdx

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{E}^{\prime}+V}(1-f)(1-\Pi f)\frac{(f^{\prime}-\Pi f^{\prime})^{2}}{(1-f^{\prime})(1-\Pi f^{\prime})}dk^{\prime}dkdx

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,-2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{V}\int_{B}\int_{B}(f-\Pi f)(f^{\prime}-\Pi f^{\prime})dk^{\prime}dkdx

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(1-f^{\prime})(1-\Pi f^{\prime})dk^{\prime}\right)\left(\int_{B}\frac{(f-\Pi f)^{2}}{(1-f)(1-\Pi f)}e^{\mathcal{E}+V}dk\right)dx

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,-2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\int_{B}(f-\Pi f)dk\right)^{2}dx.

According to the definition of
\Pi f,
the last integral is zero. Moreover, because of the bound (
7
) we can estimate

0<\frac{e^{\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-\mu_{2}}}{1+e^{\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-\mu_{2}}}\leq 1-f<1.

The same bounds also hold for
\Pi f.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-V}(1-f)(1-f^{\prime})(F-F^{\prime})^{2}dk^{\prime}dkdx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2e^{2\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+2\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-2\mu_{2}}}{(1+e^{\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-\mu_{2}})^{2}}\left(\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}dk^{\prime}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}(f-\Pi f)^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}+V}dxdk.

This equation and (
17
) imply

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(f)\geq\frac{\sigma_{-}e^{2\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+3\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-3\mu_{2}}}{(1+e^{\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-\mu_{2}})^{2}}\left(\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}dk^{\prime}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}(f-\Pi f)^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dxdk.

Hence, (
16
) holds with
C_{5}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\sigma_{-}e^{2\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+3\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-3\mu_{2}}}{(1+e^{\min_{B}\{\mathcal{E}\}+\min_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\{V\}-\mu_{2}})^{2}}\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k^{\prime})}dk^{\prime}.

∎

6
Lyapunov functional

We recall

\rho_{\infty}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{B}f_{\infty}(x,k)dk

and

\rho(t,x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{B}f(t,x,k)dk.

We define a vector function

\displaystyle J(t,x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{B}\nabla_{k}\mathcal{E}(k)\log\left(\frac{f(t,x,k)}{1-f(t,x,k)}\right)e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}dk\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and a constant matrix

A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{B}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{E}(k)}{\partial k^{2}}e^{-\mathcal{E}(k)}dk\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.

We consider the elliptic equation

-\mathrm{div}_{x}(A\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x))=\rho(t,x)-\rho_{\infty},\,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\phi\,dx=0,\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\,t\geq 0.

Since
\rho(t,\cdot)-\rho_{\infty}
has zero mass for every
t\geq 0
and
A
is positive definite, the classical elliptic regularity results (see, for example,
[
20
]
) show that there exists a unique solution
\phi
satisfying

||\phi(t,\cdot)||_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq C_{R}||\rho(t,\cdot)-\rho_{\infty}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,t\geq 0,

(18)

where the constant
C_{R}>0
is independent of
t
.
Then we define a modified entropy functional

\mathrm{E}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{H}(f|f_{\infty})+\delta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot Jdx,

where
\delta>0
will be chosen later.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}:B\to\mathbb{R}
and
V:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions.
If
\delta>0
is small enough, for functions
f
satisfying (
7
), there are constants
{C}_{6}>0
and
\mathcal{C}_{7}>0
such that

{C}_{6}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dkdx\leq\mathrm{E}(f)\leq{C}_{7}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}(f-f_{\infty})e^{\mathcal{E}}dkdx.

(19)

Proof.

The function

\displaystyle f\mapsto f\log\frac{f}{f_{\infty}}+(1-f)\log\frac{1-f}{1-f_{\infty}}

and its derivative with respect to
f
vanish at
f=f_{\infty}
. This fact and the mean value theorem provide

\displaystyle f\log\frac{f}{f_{\infty}}+(1-f)\log\frac{1-f}{1-f_{\infty}}=\frac{(f-f_{\infty})^{2}}{2g(1-g)},

where the function
g
can be written as
g\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\theta f+(1-\theta)f_{\infty}
for some function
\theta(t,x,k)\in[0,1].
In particular,
g
satisfies (
7
). As a consequence
\frac{e^{-\mathcal{E}}}{g(1-g)}
is bounded from below and above by positive constants. This implies
\mathrm{H}(f|f_{\infty})
is equivalent to

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dkdx.

Next, we estimate
\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot Jdx\right|
using the Hölder inequality

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot Jdx\right|\leq||\nabla_{x}\phi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}||J||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

(
18
) shows that
||\nabla_{x}\phi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
is bounded (up to a constant) by
||\rho-\rho_{\infty}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.
Moreover, the Hölder inequality provides

||\rho-\rho_{\infty}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq\sqrt{\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dkdx}.

Integrating by parts one can show

\displaystyle\int_{B}\nabla_{k}\mathcal{E}\log\frac{f_{\infty}}{1-f_{\infty}}\,e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk=-\int_{B}\nabla_{k}\mathcal{E}(\mu_{\infty}-\mathcal{E}-V)e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk=0.

This let us write

\displaystyle||J(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\int_{B}\nabla_{k}\mathcal{E}\left(\log\frac{f}{1-f}-\log\frac{f_{\infty}}{1-f_{\infty}}\right)e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk\right|^{2}dx}.

By the mean value theorem there is a function
\tilde{\theta}(t,x,k)\in[0,1]
such that for
h=\tilde{\theta}f+(1-\tilde{\theta})f_{\infty}

\log\frac{f}{1-f}-\log\frac{f_{\infty}}{1-f_{\infty}}=\frac{f-f_{\infty}}{h(1-h)}.

(
7
) implies
\frac{e^{-\mathcal{E}}}{h(1-h)}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\int_{B}\nabla_{k}\mathcal{E}(f-f_{\infty})dk\right|^{2}dx}.

The Hölder inequality implies

\displaystyle||J(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\int_{B}|\nabla_{k}\mathcal{E}|^{2}e^{-\mathcal{E}}dk\right)\left(\int_{B}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dk\right)dx}

These estimates show that there is a constant

such that

Therefore, we have

\mathrm{H}(f|f_{\infty})-\delta\tilde{C}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dkdx\leq\mathrm{E}(f)\leq\mathrm{H}(f|f_{\infty})+\delta\tilde{C}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dkdx.

We have proved that

\mathrm{H}(f|f_{\infty})
is equivalent to
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{B}(f-f_{\infty})^{2}e^{\mathcal{E}}dkdx.
Hence, if
\delta
is small enough, the claimed estimate (
19
) holds.
