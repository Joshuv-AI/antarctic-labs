 1. Executive Summary

Opportunities exist for realizing transformative advances in productivity and reductions in energy
footprint through ubiquitous sensing in manufacturing environments. Enhanced Preparation for
Intelligent Cybermanufacturing Systems (EPICS) is a 21-month (4 academic semesters, plus one
summer) experience for graduate students that focuses on scaling the knowledge, understanding
and leadership skills in the cyber manufacturing area. Masters students (8/year, 32 total) complete
2-year projects on industrially-driven project topics, rotating to internships in summer semester to
work on scoping and implementation at project partners. Students complete academic training in
embedded systems, process modeling, data science, and cloud-based systems design. Their
projects are targeted toward sensor retrofit, process monitoring, root cause analysis, and sensor
fusion.

 2. Project Goals and Objectives

The EPICS program focuses on the complete development of leaders in the digital manufacturing
sector. To that end the primary outcome of this project is to develop a process that trains and
prepares the EPICS Fellows for careers as industry leaders leveraging digital manufacturing, while
embracing and leveraging the rapidly ever-changing landscape of global manufacturing operations
from an integrated technical, business, personnel and economic perspective.

The EPICS program is an intense 21-month (4 academic semesters, plus one summer) experience
for graduate students that focuses on scaling the knowledge, understanding and leadership skills
in the cyber manufacturing area. The outcomes of these trainings will yield transformative
advances in productivity and reductions in energy footprint through highly interleaved additive
and subtractive processing via sensors, big data and their integration using commercially available
cloud/fog computing platforms.

The program is designed to achieve the following objectives:
 1. First, train a next generation of engineers with tools to fully utilize the technology, business
 and economic models that are emerging from digital manufacturing opportunities.
 2. Second, develop and deploy a digital manufacturing curriculum at a variety of academic
 levels.
 3. Third, transition next generation engineers to industrial partners by providing research
 projects to industry partners via summer internships that continue past the summer as the
 EPICS Fellows’ thesis projects.

 3. Background and Motivation
The traineeship program is focused on meeting the needs for trained graduate students in the area
of smart manufacturing as it is applied to next generation integrated manufacturing systems, such
as hybrid additive and subtractive manufacturing systems. While large-scale OEM facilities are
presently integrating additive processing into conventional manufacturing workflows, the added
dimensionality requires significantly more complex process engineering to realize optimality from
a manufacturing systems perspective. Realizing this optimality can be accelerated via integrating
large data sets generated by on-board manufacturing systems sensors or fog compute based sensor
platforms that provide information pertaining to process operations.

To address these problems, the next generation of manufacturing process engineers will need
extensive training in new cross-disciplinary domains that include embedded process measurement,
process modeling, prognostics/diagnostics, data science and cloud-based systems design and
execution. The goal of the project is to provide cohorts of graduate students with new targeted
coursework and novel hands-on laboratory experiences in advanced manufacturing processes,
cybermanufacturing systems, digital thread, statistical process control, information modeling, data
analytics, project management, and entrepreneurship. Furthermore, the trainees will learn not only
by doing in their class, laboratories, research projects and internships, but will also learn by
teaching.

 5

Current and prior grants to the project team members by a portfolio of federal and private sources
have funded research to develop the underlying technologies for distributed sensing and control,
high performance computing and process design, machine diagnostics and prognostics, and
machine sensing. These ongoing/previously funded efforts have resulted in advancement of
technologies to relatively high technology readiness levels (e.g., TRL7+) and now new training
programs are needed to realize their utilization to solve industrial problems of critical interest to
the nation’s economy.

A strong history of collaboration between GT and industrial partners was leveraged during the
course of this project. The technical focus area of the traineeship program (e.g., digital
manufacturing) is of critical importance to future systems designs for industrial manufacturing
operations. The close proximity of these companies enabled the success of traineeship activities.
The program successfully brought together a US DoE laboratory (ORNL) and several non-profit
organizations to support the EPICS students through direct advising and mentoring, as well as
internship opportunities. The partnership also integrated major non-profit consortia and public-
private partnerships for mentorship and internship opportunities, including NCMS, NCDMM,
SME, AMT, DMDII, ASME, America Makes, and IACMI.

 4. EPICS Cohort Program

EPICS Training Curriculum and Training Fellows
The EPICS Cohort training curriculum began with an intensive basic skill set training period
during the first several weeks of the program, where a combination of GT personnel and program
partners (National Instruments, Siemens, and Autodesk) provided professional training in a variety
of areas including CNC programming, materials processing, real-time data acquisition and control,
and technical communication. Table 1 provides the details of specific training elements provided
by each industry partner.

 Table 1: Lead Institutions for EPICS Training Elements
 Company Technical Area
 Siemens CNC Programming (828D / 840D Controller)
 NI Real-Time Data Acquisition and Control (LabVIEW Real-Time)
 Autodesk CAD/CAM Systems Design and Simulation (Fusion 360)
 GA Tech Manual Machining/Shop Safety/EHS Protocol
 GA Tech Technical Communication

Each component was instructed following a train-the-trainer module. Subsequently, during their
first two semesters, the trainees became Training Fellows themselves in a second-year
undergraduate design/build course (ME2110) operated by GT’s Mechanical Engineering
department. In this role, they worked with GT faculty, staff, and EPICS partners to train
undergraduate students adhering to the philosophy that in teaching one masters a skillset.

ME 2110 is the Mechanical Engineering (ME) second-year design/build course, where all students
are trained in manual machining, CNC programming, real-time control and signal processing,
basic design rules and tools, teaming dynamics and written and oral communication.

 6

Approximately 800 students per year are trained in this course. Each student participates in 2 hours
of lecture and 3 hours of studio per week. In the studio component, a maximum of 20 students
work in teams of 3 or 4 to build an autonomous system that competes at the end of the semester
on a track against 3 other teams. The students program an NI myRIO, which is a dual-processor
ARM/FPGA, to operate their systems autonomously. All sensors and actuators are supplied to the
students, and the students are allowed to spend a maximum of $100 on other elements of their
system (materials are prorated as necessary and taken into account in a formal bill of materials,
BOM).

Each of the EPICS Training Fellows taught one section of ME 2110 with a faculty member for
two semesters, honing their basic skills in the aforementioned areas. During one of those two
semesters, the School of Mechanical Engineering supported the EPICS Fellows as a Graduate
Teaching Assistant, which provided further training in communicating and mentoring to the EPICS
Fellows. As mentors in ME 2110, the EPICS Fellows had a significant opportunity to learn from
the participating ME 2110 faculty, as well as to closely mentor a group of 20 students in all of the
areas previously mentioned.

Course Work
All EPICS Fellows completed standard required course work for an MS in Mechanical
Engineering at GT. Several courses were required, including graduate courses in Manufacturing
(ME 6222), a new Cyber/Digital Manufacturing course (ME 6XXX), Mechatronics (ME 6405),
and a required Mathematics course. The students were allowed to choose either a Computer
Graphics (CS 6491) (a CS course that was developed in conjunction with the School of Computer
Science) or a Computer Aided Engineering Course (CAE) course (ME 6104), and two other
flexible technical electives. All students successfully completed the formal coursework.

Summer Internship
All EPICS Fellows completed an internship working with either industry, a national laboratory, a
Manufacturing USA institute, or another team outside of GT between the first and second years of
the program. There were more external partners identified in the proposal phase of this project
than available EPICS Fellows, and therefore there was significant competition to hire the EPICS
Fellows. Furthermore, many of the same partner companies hired EPICS fellows at the conclusion
of their academic studies. A summary of internship locations and current career path for each
EPICS Fellow is listed later in this report.

Theses
All EPICS Fellows performed research and wrote an M.S. thesis as part of their experience and
their M.S. degree requirements. The basis for each thesis was developed during the summer
internship and was completed during the final two terms (3 and 4) at GT.

 8

Primary thesis mentors were recruited from the Mechanical Engineering program, with M.S.
committee members selected across Mechanical Engineering, Electrical and Computer
Engineering, Industrial and Systems Engineering, Bioengineering, Chemical Engineering, and the
College of Computing.

The GT team, having had significant experience working with industry on proprietary projects,
worked with the partners to identify appropriate research projects and internships that provided
both the necessary value proposition for the partner and the needed technical content for the EPICS
Fellows’ theses. It was understood that much of the work conducted by the EPICS Fellows could
be proprietary in nature. However, many of the theses were submitted as academic conference and
journal papers with full support of the industry partners. A list of publications is included at the
end of this report.

External Conference Participation
To provide the students with a broad perspective on digital manufacturing, all EPICS Fellows
participated in conferences during their tenure. The two conferences are GT’s including the
Internet of Things for Manufacturing (IoTfM) held on GT’s campus annually in November (see:
http://manufacturing.gatech.edu/internet-things-manufacturing-iotfm-workshop-2017), and the
combined ASME/SME Manufacturing Science and Engineering Conference / North American
Manufacturing Research Conference.

Their first year, they attended these conferences as participants with the objective to understand
the technical and interactive offerings of the two conferences, and to learn. During their second
year, they worked with IoTfM staff to develop sessions, discussions and engage keynote and
plenary speakers. They will help set the agenda for two of the most exciting sets of discussion for
the Industrial Internet of Things / Industry 4.0 / Smart Manufacturing topics.

 5. EPICS Fellows
Through the duration of this program 32 EPICS fellows were accepted and participated across four
cohorts. Each Fellow completed two years of study, a summer internship, and earned their Master’s
degree. A non-exhaustive list of companies and organizations who hosted internships between
2019 and 2023 includes Autodesk, Delta Tech Ops, Caterpillar, Georgia Pacific, Ford, Sandia
National Laboratory, Oak Ridge National Laboratory, Boeing, RTX, Mazak, Tesla, and Apple.
The support of more than 10 Fortune 500 companies illustrates the program’s sustained
engagement with major industry partners and national laboratories. Across all cohorts, fellows
represent a wide range of engineering and applied science disciplines, include mechanical,
electrical, industrial, chemical, materials, bioengineering, and computing, highlighting the EPICS
program’s interdisciplinary reach. All MS Thesis documents are listed with a summary in
Appendix A.

After completing their EPICS experience, fellows progressed into impactful roles in academia,
advanced manufacturing, R&D organizations, and technology companies. Together, the four
cohorts reflect not only the breadth of technical skill cultivated through the program but also the

strong professional outcomes and industry integration that characterize the EPICS fellowship
model.

 6. Project Tasks

The below consists of a task-by-task summary of activities completed in this project.

Task 1 - Traineeship Curriculum Design and Implementation
Task Summary: The objective of this task is development and execution of the traineeship
curriculum and its implementation in the EPICS program to achieve the objectives of initiating the
training for the incoming cohort of EPICS trainees. EPICS trainees will receive formal training for
smart manufacturing skills and mentoring undergraduate ME2110 students on cybermanufacturing
topics.

Task Accomplishments: The team successfully developed and implemented the traineeship
curriculum. The team transitioned the course curricular modules in FY23Q2 to ME2110 as part of
the training of students for facility usage at Georgia Tech in terms of computer aided
manufacturing techniques. All task components were completed.

Task 2 - Research Project Definition and Execution
Task Summary: The objective of this task is recruiting students for EPICS cohorts, establishing
research projects for EPICS fellow, formal project definition and scoping of thesis topics, and
execution of those projects.

Task Accomplishments: All EPICS Cohorts successfully completed the requirements of this task.
Thesis projects were developed with industry partners, comprehensively investigated, and
presented by the EPICS fellows. All EPICS fellows successfully presented their Thesis projects
and graduated the program. All task components were completed.

Task 3 – Program Evaluation
Task Summary: The objective of this task is development of program evaluation instruments to
establish effectiveness of the EPICS program using quantitative data.

Task Accomplishments: Survey instruments and survey results were used in conjunction with the
program sustainability model to explore effective design of traineeship activities beyond the DOE
program support. All EPICS Fellows were evaluated by instructors and by students in the ME2110
course, who were instructed by the EPICS fellows. Each evaluation conformed to GT’s standards
for data anonymity and privacy through the CIOS instrument, an evaluation tool that is
implemented campus-wide for all GT faculty, instructors, and TAs. A final EPICS Post-Program
evaluation was conducted by the PIs at the conclusion of this project. All EPICS Fellows were
invited to respond. A summary of results from EPICS Fellows evaluations and EPICS Post-
Program evaluations are presented in this report. Detailed results are included in the Appendix B
of this report. All task components were completed.

Task 4 - Program Sustainability Model
Task Summary: The objective of this task is development of a sustainability model beyond the
DoE funding for the EPICS program. Financial models and technical road mapping are the major
activities.

Task Accomplishments: The project team leaders solicited industrial participants for pilot program
of masters-level training/teaching/research related to ME2110. The ME2110 curriculum changes
propelled by the EPICS program have been fully adopted by GT’s Mechanical Engineering
Department. Many aspects of the advanced manufacturing training modules developed as part of
the EPICS program are now sustainable modules offered in the core curriculum of ME2110. This
task is on track for completion at the end of the project. All task components were completed.

Task 5 - Project Management
Task Summary: The objective of this task is to facilitate reporting and project management.

Task Accomplishments: Engagement with project partners occurred in project review meetings.
All quarterly reports, financial reports, and final reports are complete with the approval of this
report. All task components are completed.

Across all semesters shown (Fall 2018 through Spring 2022), student evaluation scores remained
consistently strong, with most average ratings falling between 4.4 and 4.9 across categories such
as oral and written communication, explanation clarity, preparedness, classroom management, and
overall effectiveness. Early semesters, particularly Fall 2018 and Spring 2019, show higher
response rates around 69 - 75% providing a comprehensive snapshot of student perceptions. These
semesters also feature some of the highest average ratings with several categories reaching 4.8 or
above, indicating extremely positive student experiences.

Even as response rates declined in later semesters, notably during Fall 2020 and Fall 2021 which
aligns with pandemic-related instructional disruptions, the ratings themselves remained stable,
demonstrating consistent teaching quality despite external challenges.

 13

Specific strengths of the EPICS Fellows emerge across all terms, including approachability,
respect for students, and preparedness consistently earn some of the highest ratings, reflecting
strong rapport and instructional reliability. Stimulated subject matter interest and engaged students
show slightly more fluctuation but generally remain above 4.3, indicating effective motivation and
interaction. By Spring 2022, response rates increased again to 66%, and evaluation averages
returned to levels comparable to pre-2020 results. Overall, the data reflects sustained excellence
in instruction, resilience through shifting learning environments, and student satisfaction across
multiple years.

The EPICS Post-Program survey results reflect a consistently positive experience among
participants in the EPICS program. The vast majority of respondents reported being either satisfied
or very satisfied, with very few (a maximum of 2 students) expressing dissatisfaction in the EPICS
program. Likewise, students overwhelmingly agreed that the program provided valuable
opportunities to apply cyber/digital manufacturing concepts in real-world contexts. These
responses indicate that the program not only met but exceeded participant expectations in
providing meaningful, practice-oriented learning experiences.

In terms of skill development, respondents reported substantial growth across a range of technical
and professional competencies, including cloud/web systems, software development, technical
communication, data acquisition and control, materials processing, and CNC programming. Many
students rated themselves at proficient or expert levels by the end of the program.

Confidence levels in applying these skills followed a similar pattern, with most participants feeling
moderately to extremely confident in their ability to use them in academic or professional
environments. This alignment between skill development and confidence suggests that the
program effectively equips students with applicable, industry-relevant capabilities.
