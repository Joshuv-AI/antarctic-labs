Optimizing Proof-Search via Linearization for Gödel-Löb Logic with Tree-Hypersequents
Tim S. Lyon Omar Y. A. A. Taher

Abstract
We answer a question posed by Poggiolesi concerning a syntactic decidability proof for 𝖦𝖫 \mathsf{GL} in the tree-hypersequent system 𝖢𝖲𝖦𝖫 \mathsf{CSGL} , and resolve a challenge identified by Maggesi and Perini Brogi, who sought a 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{PSPACE} proof-search algorithm for 𝖦𝖫 \mathsf{GL} in expressive sequent-based formalisms. We work with a notational variant of 𝖢𝖲𝖦𝖫 \mathsf{CSGL} formulated in terms of (labeled) tree sequents. Our answer is complexity-optimal: we present a proof-search algorithm that decides the (in)validity of formulae and runs in 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{PSPACE} , matching the known 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{PSPACE} -completeness of 𝖦𝖫 \mathsf{GL} . To achieve this, we introduce a linearization method , which constructs only a single branch of a derivation and of a tree sequent at a time, avoiding the exponential blowup typical of naive proof-search in sequent formalisms. We show how to systematically combine fragments of tree sequents generated during proof-search to extract finite counter-models, which serves as a theoretical device for establishing the correctness of the algorithm when proof-search fails. Finally, we show that every valid formula admits a proof consisting solely of line sequents, which correspond to linear nested sequents. This establishes a connection between depth-first proof-search and linear nested sequent calculi. Our results not only answer the aforementioned questions, but also provide new insights into proof-search and correctness arguments in tree sequent systems for modal logics.

Introduction

Provability logics are a class of modal logics in which the modal operator
\Box
is interpreted as “it is provable that” with respect to a given arithmetical theory. Among these, Gödel–Löb logic (
\mathsf{GL}
) is particularly prominent. It originates in the work of Löb, who identified a set of conditions satisfied by the provability predicate of Peano Arithmetic (PA), leading to both axiomatic and semantic characterizations of
\mathsf{GL}
. The logic can be axiomatized as an extension of the basic modal logic
\mathsf{K}
by adding
Löb’s axiom

\Box(\Box\varphi\rightarrow\varphi)\rightarrow\Box\varphi
and is sound and complete with respect to transitive and conversely well-founded relational models
[
26
]
. In a landmark result, Solovay
[
29
]
showed that
\mathsf{GL}
precisely captures the provability logic of PA, in the sense that it proves exactly the modal principles that PA can establish about its own provability predicate.

The logic
\mathsf{GL}
has a well-developed structural proof theory and admits a number of cut-free sequent-style calculi. Sequent systems in the style of Gentzen were first provided by Sambin and Valentini in the early 1980s
[
24
,
25
]
; see also Avron
[
2
]
. Since then, a variety of alternative systems have been introduced, either by enriching the underlying sequent structure or by generalizing the notion of proof itself
[
15
,
18
,
21
,
27
,
28
]
. These systems have proven useful for studying (meta-)logical properties of
\mathsf{GL}
, including cut-elimination
[
18
]
and constructive Lyndon interpolation
[
27
]
.

In this paper, we study proof-search in the
tree-hypersequent system

\mathsf{CSGL}
introduced by Poggiolesi
[
21
]
. Tree-hypersequents are trees of Gentzen sequents and are more traditionally known as
nested sequents
. This formalism is also known to be equivalent to the formalism of
tree sequents
, which use labeled sequent notation (cf.
[
7
,

). (NB. In this paper, we take tree-hypersequents, nested sequents, and tree sequents to be synonymous with each other as all formalisms are notational variants of one another.) The formalism was introduced independently by Kashima
[

]
and Bull
[
4
]
with further influential works provided by Brünnler
[
3
]
and Poggiolesi
[
22

. Such systems arose out of a call for cut-free sequent-style systems for logics not known to possess a cut-free Gentzen system, such as the tense logic
\mathsf{K_{t}}
and the modal logic
\mathsf{S5}
. Such systems exhibit fundamental admissibility and invertibility properties, making them well-suited for automated reasoning tasks
[
16
,
13
]
. For a comprehensive survey on nested (and equivalent) sequent systems, see Lellmann and Poggiolesi
[

In the concluding section of Poggiolesi
[

]
, the author raises the question of how decidability for

might be established using the system
\mathsf{CSGL}

. In this paper, we answer both of these questions: we present a proof-search algorithm for
\mathsf{GL}
based on
\mathsf{CSGL}
that runs in
\mathsf{PSPACE}
, thereby achieving complexity-optimality. Since proofs between
\mathsf{CSGL}
and
\mathsf{G3KGL}
are inter-translatable in
\mathsf{PTIME}

[
7
,
14
]
, our approach is also applicable to
\mathsf{G3KGL}
and can be used to resolve the
\mathsf{PSPACE}
goal for labeled sequent-based proof-search.

Our investigation of these problems led to several technical and conceptual insights, which we outline below. We note that we work with a syntactic variant of Poggiolesi’s
\mathsf{CSGL}
in this paper that uses
tree sequents

[
8
]
since it simplifies much of our work and definitions. Our main contributions are as follows:

\bullet

First, naive proof-search algorithms for nested sequent systems are typically at least exponential in both time and space. This is because derivations in such systems are “trees of trees,” which can produce exponentially large structures. Since
\mathsf{GL}
is
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete (see
[
5

), one should expect the existence of a proof-search algorithm in
\mathsf{CSGL}
that runs in
\mathsf{PSPACE}
. To address this, we show how to formulate a proof-search algorithm that constructs only a single branch of a derivation and of a tree sequent at a time. We refer to this method here as
linearization
. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first proof-search algorithm of this kind for a tree (i.e., nested) sequent system, and the technique appears adaptable to similar systems as well.

\bullet

Second, because our linearization method generates only single branches of tree sequents at a time, the usual methods for obtaining counter-models from failed proof-search (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
30
]
) are obstructed. Each branch of a tree sequent represents only a fragment of a counter-model, which complicates proving correctness of the algorithm. To address this, we show how all such fragments can be systematically combined when proof-search fails to define a finite counter-model of the input, which constitutes a further contribution.

\bullet

Third, as a corollary to our proof-search algorithm, we find that every valid formula has a proof in
\mathsf{CSGL}
in which every tree sequent is actually a
line
. It is known that such line sequents are notational variants of
linear nested sequents
, introduced by Lellmann
[
12

\mathsf{LNGL}
for
\mathsf{GL}

[
15

\bullet

Sambin and Valentini

\mathsf{GL}
within the Gentzen sequent formalism and labeled sequent formalism, respectively.

[

implemented a tableau algorithm for

; however, no complexity analysis was provided. We believe their algorithm is likely

However, neither paper established complexity bounds, and neither procedure appears to run in

by Maggesi and Perini Brogi
[

\mathsf{EXPSPACE}

is not inherent to such formalisms but can be overcome with suitable proof-search techniques.

The efficiency of our algorithm relies on two key ideas: forgoing the support of counter-model extraction and the use of disjunctive branching. Counter-model extraction is an algorithm design choice that supports the output of a counter-model witnessing the invalidity of the input, in the same way that proofs can be output to witness the validity of the input. The existing proof-search procedures mentioned above all output counter-models when proof-search fails, which causes an exponential blowup since counter-models can be of exponential size. The key to achieving the
\mathsf{PSPACE}
upper bound is thus the following trade-off: our algorithm forgoes direct counter-model extraction in exchange for reduced space consumption. Our linearization method avoids
the exponential blowup by outputting only a Boolean verdict;
counter-model extraction is no longer immediate and instead serves as a
theoretical device
for proving correctness when proof-search fails.

The second idea is the concept of
disjunctive branching
, which was motivated by the work of Mints
[
19
]
. While Mints employs (essentially) hypersequents to perform disjunctive branching, we take an
alternative approach and use a
‘disjunctive inference rule’
whose conclusion is provable whenever at least one premise is provable. This allows us to further reduce the space needed to carry out proof-search. We note that our use of disjunctive inference rules is closely related
to the disjunctive (or ‘existential’) rules employed in tableau
algorithms (cf.
[
1
]
). However, our approach is distinct in that we perform disjunctive branching at two levels: at the level of branches in a derivation, which is common practice for reducing complexity, but also at the level of tree sequents, which appears to be a new idea.

4
Complexity-Optimal Proof-Search

We provide an answer to a question posed by Poggiolesi
[
21
, p. 610]
concerning a syntactic decision procedure for
\mathsf{GL}
using
\mathsf{CSGL}
. More
precisely, we present a proof-search algorithm that decides the
(in)validity of formulae in
\mathsf{PSPACE}
, thereby matching the known
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-completeness of
\mathsf{GL}
. Since
\mathsf{CSGL}
and

\mathsf{G3KGL}
are
\mathsf{PTIME}
-equivalent
[
7
,
14
]
, our
method is adaptable to labeled sequent-based proof-search as well, and thus resolves the open problem of Maggesi and Perini
Brogi
[
17
, Section 7]
.

Several insights emerge from the design and analysis of this algorithm. First, in order to ensure the
\mathsf{PSPACE}
upper-bound, we develop a ‘depth-first’ proof-search procedure that generates only a single branch of a derivation at a time, as well as a single branch of the underlying tree sequents. While this strategy significantly reduces space consumption, it complicates the proof of correctness. In particular, demonstrating the existence of a counter-model when proof-search fails becomes difficult because the algorithm only produces partial fragments of a counter-model in each branch. We show how these fragments can nevertheless be combined to yield a genuine counter-model for the input sequent when proof-search fails.

Second, we observe that proof-search in
\mathsf{CSGL}
terminates
automatically
. In particular, the diagonal formula occurring in the
\Box\mathsf{R}
rule provides a natural bound on the depth of tree sequents generated during proof-search, eliminating the need for loop checking, despite the presence of transitivity, viz., the
\mathsf{4L}
rule. A similar observation was made for proof-search with Gentzen sequent calculi
[
24
]
and full labeled sequent calculi
[
20
]
.

Finally, when the algorithm succeeds and produces a proof, each tree sequent appearing in the derivation is in fact a line sequent (cf.
[
10
]
), as a direct consequence of the depth-first nature of the search. We argue that the resulting derivations can be viewed as linear nested sequent (LNS) proofs, which are variants of those in the recently introduced LNS calculus
\mathsf{LNGL}

[
15
]
. This demonstrates that LNS systems can be extracted from depth-first proof-search procedures for tree (i.e., nested) sequent calculi.

We now turn to the description of our proof-search algorithm. To detect termination, we introduce two syntactic properties of tree sequents. Intuitively, a tree sequent is said to be

if
\Box\mathsf{R}
is the only rule that can be applied non-redundantly (to a leaf), whereas a tree sequent is
stable
if no rule is non-redundantly applicable (to a leaf). Throughout this section, when we say that a rule is
applicable

Theorem 4.9
.

For any
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}
,
\mathtt{prove}(\vdash x:\varphi)
terminates in
\mathsf{PSPACE}
.

Proof.

Let
N:=|\mathsf{sufo}(\varphi)|
, that is,
N
is the number of subformulae of
\varphi
. Recall that every computation tree generated by
\mathtt{prove}
is line-like and end-active. We first show that for every line sequent of the form
xRy_{1},\ldots,y_{n{-}1}Ry_{n},\Gamma\vdash\Delta
generated during the computation of
\mathtt{prove}(\vdash x:\varphi)
, we have
n\leq N
. In other words, the length (i.e., number of relational atoms) of any line sequent is bounded by
N
.

For a contradiction, suppose the opposite, i.e., a line sequent
xRy_{1},\ldots,y_{n{-}1}Ry_{n},\Gamma\vdash\Delta
was generated by
\mathtt{prove}(\vdash x:\varphi)
such that
n>N
. Let
\Box\psi_{1},\ldots,\Box\psi_{k}\in\mathsf{sufo}(\varphi)
be all
\Box
-subformulae of
\varphi
. By the definition of a subformula, it must be the case that
k\leq N
. Observe that each relational atom was introduced via a
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
application with a principal formula
\Box\psi_{i}
, and so, there must exist
n
applications of
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
along the branch
\mathcal{B}
of the computation tree from the input
\vdash x:\varphi
to
xRy_{1},\ldots,y_{n{-}1}Ry_{n},\Gamma\vdash\Delta
. By the pigeonhole principle, it follows that a subformula
\Box\psi_{\ell}
was principal
twice
along the branch
\mathcal{B}
, i.e., for some
i<j\in[n]
, we have
y_{i}:\Box\psi_{\ell}
and
y_{j}:\Box\psi_{\ell}
occurring as principal in
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
applications. That is to say, the branch
\mathcal{B}
is of the following form:

xRy_{1},\ldots,y_{n{-}1}Ry_{n},\Gamma\vdash\Delta

\vdots

\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}

xRy_{1},\ldots,y_{i{-}1}Ry_{i},\ldots,y_{j{-}1}Ry_{j},\Sigma,y_{i+1}:\Box\psi_{\ell}\vdash\Pi,y_{j}:\Box\psi_{\ell}

\vdots

xRy_{1},\ldots,y_{i{-}1}Ry_{i},y_{i}Ry_{i{+}1},\Gamma^{\prime},y_{i+1}:\Box\psi_{\ell}\vdash\Delta^{\prime},y_{i+1}:\psi_{\ell}

\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}

xRy_{1},\ldots,y_{i{-}1}Ry_{i},\Gamma^{\prime}\vdash\Delta^{\prime},y_{i}:\Box\psi_{\ell}

\vdots

\vdash x:\varphi

Observe that
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
is only applied to a saturated sequent. Therefore, it must be the case that
y_{j}:\Box\psi_{\ell}\in\Sigma
since
i<j
and by the
(\Box\mathsf{4L})
condition. However, this implies that

xRy_{1},\ldots,y_{i{-}1}Ry_{i},\ldots,y_{j{-}1}Ry_{j},\Sigma,y_{i+1}:\Box\psi_{\ell}\vdash\Pi,y_{j}:\Box\psi_{\ell}

is an instance of
\mathsf{id_{2}}
, meaning,
\mathtt{prove}
would have halted and output
\mathtt{true}
rather than apply
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
bottom-up, giving a contradiction. Therefore,
n\leq N
, i.e., every line sequent has its length bounded by
N
.

Let us define the
size
of a sequent
T=\mathcal{T},\Gamma\vdash\Delta
to be
\mathsf{s}(T)=|\mathcal{T}|+|\Gamma|+|\Delta|
. It is not difficult to show that each bottom-up rule application in the algorithm strictly increases the size of a line sequent along its branch of the corresponding computation tree. The maximum size of any line sequent generated during proof-search is bounded as follows: there are at most
N
relational atoms that can occur in a line sequent as established above. It follows that there are at most
N{+}1
labels that can occur in a line sequent, each contributing at most
N
labeled formulae to the antecedent and at most
N
labeled formulae to the consequent. Hence,
\mathsf{s}(T)\leq N+2N(N{+}1)=2N^{2}+3N
for any line sequent
T
generated during proof-search. Since the input
\vdash x:\varphi
has size
1
and each rule application along a branch increases size by at least
1
, the number of rule applications along any branch is at most
2N^{2}+3N-1=\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
. The algorithm
\mathsf{prove}
is a recursive procedure that explores branches of a computation tree in a depth-first manner, meaning, the total space consumed at any point during execution is
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})\times\mathcal{O}(N^{2})=\mathcal{O}(N^{4})
due to the maximum size of line sequents and maximum length of branches (along with minor bookkeeping overhead). Since
N\leq|\varphi|
, the algorithm runs in space polynomial in
|\varphi|
.
∎

A Note on Linear Nested Sequents.

A
linear nested sequent (LNS)
is an expression of the form
X_{1}\vdash Y_{1}\sslash\cdots\sslash X_{n}\vdash Y_{n}
such that for each
i\in[n]
,
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
are finite sets of formulae from
\mathscr{L}
. We use
\mathcal{A}
to denote linear nested sequents. It is well known that line sequents are syntactic variants of LNSs and that the two kinds of expressions are mutually translatable (see
[
10
]
).

Recently, a linear nested sequent calculus
\mathsf{LNGL}
for
\mathsf{GL}
was introduced in
[
15
]
, formulated over the signature
\{\neg,\lor,\Box\}
. By standard definitional translations, this calculus can be straightforwardly adapted to our present signature
\{\bot,\rightarrow,\Box\}
. From this perspective, the results of the present section may be viewed as providing a variant of
\mathsf{LNGL}
. Indeed, our analysis shows that whenever a formula
\varphi
is valid, it admits a line-like, end-active proof, which can be transformed into an LNS proof by adapting the translation from line sequents to LNSs described in
[
10
]
.

More broadly, our results shed light on the connection between depth-first proof-search and linear nested sequent calculi. In particular, they suggest that sound and complete LNS systems can be
extracted
from depth-first proof-search procedures formulated in tree (i.e., nested) sequent calculi. For completeness, we have included the LNS calculus obtained from our proof-search algorithm in Figure
5
. We remark that our algorithm can be viewed as performing proof-search in this LNS system.

Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we answered the question posed by
Poggiolesi
[
21
]
concerning a syntactic decidability
proof in the tree-hypersequent calculus
\mathsf{CSGL}
,
and achieved the
\mathsf{PSPACE}
goal identified by Maggesi and Perini Brogi
[
17
]
for proof search in expressive sequent formalisms for
\mathsf{GL}
. Our work shows how to reduce the complexity of proof-search in expressive sequent formalisms, where complexity is usually non-optimal (cf.
[
17
]
). To ensure complexity-optimality, we developed a
linearization method
for proof-search, which constructs a computation tree of the input formula. We showed how line-like, end-active proofs and counter-models can be extracted from these computation trees when proof-search succeeds or fails, respectively. We note that our algorithm
\mathtt{prove}
itself outputs only a Boolean value; counter-model extraction from computation trees serves as a theoretical device for establishing the correctness of the algorithm when proof-search fails (Theorem
4.8
).

For future work, it would be interesting to investigate how our proof-search method can be adapted to decide other modal logics. Such an adaptation could also yield new LNS systems for these logics, which would be worth studying proof-theoretically, as such systems often enjoy desirable properties such as admissibility and invertibility of rules while producing more compact proofs
[
12
,
15
]
. Furthermore, one could study the properties of the LNS system for
\mathsf{GL}
(and the analogous system in
[
15
]
) in greater detail, including admissibility of structural rules, invertibility of rules, and syntactic cut admissibility.

T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
must be line sequents. We now define the model
\mathbf{M}=(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{R},\mathbf{V})
such that (1)
\mathbf{W}:=\mathsf{Lab}(T^{*})
, (2)
(y,z)\in\mathbf{R}

iff
there exist
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathsf{Lab}(T^{*})
such that
yRu_{1},\ldots,u_{n}Rz\in\mathcal{T}
, and (3)
y\in\mathbf{V}(p)

iff

y:p\in\Gamma
. We now prove that
\mathbf{M}
is indeed a model.

First, since
\vdash x:\varphi
was the input to proof-search, we know that
x\in\mathbf{W}
, and so,
\mathbf{W}\neq\emptyset
. Second, by construction, we know that
\mathcal{T}
is a finite tree, meaning,
\mathbf{R}
is a finite transitively-closed tree. Hence,
\mathbf{R}
is both transitive and conversely-wellfounded. Last, observe that
\mathbf{V}
is well-defined.

To finish the proof, we prove the following two claims by a mutual induction on the length of
\varphi
and
\psi
, for all
y\in\mathsf{Lab}(T^{*})
: (i) if
y:\varphi\in\Gamma
, then
\mathbf{M},y\models\varphi
and (ii) if
y:\psi\in\Delta
, then
\mathbf{M},y\not\models\psi
.

\varphi=p.

If
y:p\in\Gamma
, then by the definition of
\mathbf{V}
, we know that
y\in\mathbf{V}(p)
, and so,
\mathbf{M},y\models p
.

\psi=p.

Let
y:p\in\Delta
. Assume for a contradiction that
y:p\in\Gamma
as well. Then, it must be the case that for some
i\neq j\in[n]
,
y:p\in\Gamma_{i}
and
y:p\in\Delta_{j}
. Observe that if
i=j
, then
T_{i}
would not be saturated, contradicting our assumption that
T_{i}
is stable.
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
must occur along different branches of
(\mathsf{V}^{\prime},\mathsf{E}^{\prime})
as they are distinct leaves. Let
T
be the closest common ancestor to
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
. Since
(\mathsf{V}^{\prime},\mathsf{E}^{\prime})
is a tree, such an ancestor must exist, and by what was said above, it must be the conclusion of a
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
application. All labels shared by
T_{i}
and
T_{j}
must occur in
T
by construction because after
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
is applied, all labels introduced will be fresh and pairwise distinct; consequently,
y\in\mathsf{Lab}(T)
. By the definition of
\mathtt{prove}
, we know that all rules in
(\mathsf{V}^{\prime},\mathsf{E}^{\prime})
will be end-active, meaning, after
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
is applied bottom-up to
T
,
y:p
cannot be introduced along the branch to
T_{i}
or
T_{j}
. Therefore,
y:p
must occur in the antecedent and consequent of
T
, which contradicts our assumption that
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
was applied bottom-up to
T
;
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
is only applied to saturated sequents and in this case
T
would not satisfy condition (
\mathsf{id}
). It follows that
y:p\not\in\Gamma
, meaning
y\not\in\mathbf{V}(p)
, and so,
\mathbf{M},y\not\models p
.

\varphi=\bot.

Observe that
y:\bot\not\in\Gamma
since then some
T_{i}
would not be saturated, contrary to our assumption. Hence, claim (i) vacuously holds.

\psi=\bot.

If
y:\bot\in\Delta
, then claim (ii) vacuously holds because
\mathbf{M},y\not\models\bot
by definition.

\varphi=\chi\rightarrow\theta.

If
y:\chi\rightarrow\theta\in\Gamma
, then there exists some
T_{i}
with
y:\chi\rightarrow\theta\in\Gamma_{i}
. Since
T_{i}
is saturated, we know that either
y:\chi\in\Delta_{i}\subseteq\Delta
or
y:\theta\in\Gamma_{i}\subseteq\Gamma
. By IH, either
\mathbf{M},y\not\models\chi
or
\mathbf{M},y\models\theta
. Either way,
\mathbf{M},y\models\chi\rightarrow\theta
.

\psi=\chi\rightarrow\theta.

If
y:\chi\rightarrow\theta\in\Delta
, then there exists some
T_{i}
with
y:\chi\rightarrow\theta\in\Delta_{i}
. Since
T_{i}
is saturated, we know that
y:\chi\in\Gamma_{i}\subseteq\Gamma
and
y:\theta\in\Delta_{i}\subseteq\Delta
. By IH,
\mathbf{M},y\models\chi
and
\mathbf{M},y\not\models\theta
. Hence,
\mathbf{M},y\not\models\chi\rightarrow\theta
.

\varphi=\Box\chi.

Suppose
y:\Box\chi\in\Gamma
. Let
z\in\mathbf{W}
with
(y,z)\in\mathbf{R}
. Then, there exist
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathsf{Lab}(T^{*})
such that
yRu_{1},\ldots,u_{n}Rz\in\mathcal{T}
by definition. Since
\mathcal{T}
is the composition of
n
line sequents, we know that some
i\in[n]
exists such that
yRu_{1},\ldots,u_{n}Rz\in\mathcal{T}_{i}
for
T_{i}=\mathcal{T}_{i},\Gamma_{i}\vdash\Delta_{i}
. As
T_{i}
is saturated, we know that
z\in\chi\in\Gamma_{i}\subseteq\Gamma
. By IH,
\mathbf{M},z\models\chi
, meaning,
\mathbf{M},y\models\Box\chi
since
z
was arbitrary.

\psi=\Box\chi.

Suppose
y:\Box\chi\in\Delta
. Then, there exists some stable line sequent
T_{i}=\mathcal{T}_{i},\Gamma_{i}\vdash\Delta_{i}
such that
y:\Box\chi\in\Delta_{i}
. Since
T_{i}
is stable and
y:\Box\chi\in\Delta_{i}
, it cannot be the case that
y
is a leaf; otherwise,
T_{i}
would not be stable. Hence, there must exist a
z\in\mathsf{Lab}(T^{*})
such that
yRz\in\mathcal{T}_{i}
. Let us consider the
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
application on the path from
T_{i}
to the root of
(\mathsf{V}^{\prime},\mathsf{E}^{\prime})
which introduced
yRz
with
z
fresh, and let
T_{i}^{\prime}=\mathcal{T}_{i}^{\prime},\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}\vdash\Delta_{i}^{\prime}
be the conclusion of
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
. Since
y:\Box\chi\in\Delta_{i}
, by inspection of the rules applied during proof-search, one will find that
y:\Box\chi\in\Delta_{i}^{\prime}
. Furthermore, observe that
\mathsf{L}(T_{i}^{\prime})=\mathtt{false}
, meaning, every premise of the
\Box_{\mathsf{DB}}
application will be labeled with
\mathtt{false}
as well. Thus, there will exist some premise
T_{j}^{\prime}=\mathcal{T}_{j}^{\prime},\Gamma_{j}^{\prime}\vdash\Delta_{j}^{\prime}
such that
yRu\in\mathcal{T}_{j}^{\prime}
and
u:\chi\in\Delta_{j}^{\prime}
. By the definition of
(\mathsf{V}^{\prime},\mathsf{E}^{\prime})
, there will exist a stable tree sequent
T_{j}=\mathcal{T}_{j},\Gamma_{j}\vdash\Delta_{j}
that is a leaf in
(\mathsf{V}^{\prime},\mathsf{E}^{\prime})
above
T_{j}^{\prime}
such that
yRu\in\mathcal{T}_{j}
and
u:\chi\in\Delta_{j}
. Consequently,
yRu\in\mathcal{T}
and
u:\chi\in\Delta
, so by the definition of
\mathbf{M}
and IH, we know that there exists a
u\in\mathbf{W}
such that
(y,u)\in\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{M},u\not\models\chi
. This implies that
\mathbf{M},y\not\models\Box\chi
.

This concludes the proof.
∎
