Kocher or Piehler technique of aortic root enlargement, or when originality is nothing but judicious imitation
Authors: Jerome Soquet
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

Claiming the primacy of a surgical procedure can be a perilous move. In their recent publication, Kocher and colleagues1 report a self-named technique of posterior aortic root enlargement. The authors should be commended for providing a detailed method and practical illustrations of a useful and reproducible procedure. However, attention should be paid to an article published in 1983 by Piehler and colleagues2 from the Mayo Clinic. The latter authors reported a technique of aortic root enlargement in the noncoronary sinus and clearly stated that “further enlargement of the aortic root could be accomplished by extending the incision laterally and medially for several millimeters parallel to the mitral valve annulus.” This is not dissimilar to the inverted T-extension “alongside the hinge line of the anterior mitral valve leaflet” described by Kocher and colleagues. Because Piehler and colleagues did not illustrate or promote their technique, it is believed that Kocher and colleagues were genuinely unaware of the original publication.

Factually, the type of implanted device does not change the appellation of a surgical maneuver whatsoever: a Nicks root enlargement remains a Nicks root enlargement whether the patch is made of bovine pericardium or Dacron. The fact that Kocher and colleagues use a portion of Dacron-made Valsalva graft instead of autologous pericardium as per Piehler and colleagues does not warrant the creation of a newly labeled procedure.

Consequently, unless such a technique was reported earlier than September 1983 by other authors, the originality of the procedure, which consists of adding an inverted T-incision at the level of the basal ring to an aortic root enlargement in the noncoronary sinus, should be attributed to Piehler and colleagues.
