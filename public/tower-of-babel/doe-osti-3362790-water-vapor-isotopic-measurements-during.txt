1.0 Summary
 CAPE-K_ISO deployed a Picarro L-2130 water vapor isotope analyzer to the Kennaook/Cape Grim
 Baseline Air Pollution Station in northwest Tasmania as a guest instrument within the U.S. Department of
 Energy Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility’s Cloud and Precipitation Experiment
 at Kennaook (CAPE-K). The project was led by Joseph Galewsky at the University of New Mexico and
 supported the second ARM Mobile Facility (AMF2) deployment by adding continuous measurements of
 water vapor mixing ratio, delta-18O, delta-D, and deuterium excess in marine boundary-layer air.

 The ARM project period for CAPE-K_ISO was 15 April 2024 through 15 September 2025. The cleaned
 1-minute water isotope product used in this report begins on 2024-06-11 06:01 UTC and extends through
 2025-09-15 07:05 UTC within that project window; the source file continues through 15 October 2025
 because final calibration and demobilization checks were also retained for calibration analysis. In total,
 the project-window analysis uses 631,917 cleaned 1-minute observations and 441 UTC days with at least
 12 hours of data.

 The scientific goal was to use water isotopes as tracers of Southern Ocean moisture sources, transport
 pathways, cloud microphysical processing, and local versus advected moisture contributions. These
 measurements complement CAPE-K's emphasis on seasonal low-cloud, precipitation, aerosol, and
 radiation processes at a site where long-term aerosol and trace-gas observations already exist but
 vertically resolved cloud and precipitation measurements had been sparse.

 The principal technical result is that the calibration data do not support an explicit
 water-vapor-concentration correction. After accounting for standard-specific offsets, the common H2 O
 slopes are statistically indistinguishable from zero for both delta-18O and delta-D. The final processing
 therefore removed calibration periods and adjacent transition outliers, applied a linear Vienna Standard
 Mean Ocean Water-Standard Light Antarctic Precipitation (VSMOW-SLAP) scale calibration from stable
 known-standard runs, computed deuterium excess, and removed a small number of conservative
 corrected-isotope outliers.

The February 2025 jug runs established the operating procedure and tested the instrument response over
several H2 O levels. On 20 March 2025, three sealed standards (USGS45, USGS47, and USGS RSIL
W-64444) were measured in sequence. The October 2025 wrap-up calibrations repeated the jug tests and
the three sealed standards; these post-period checks were used to strengthen the final calibration fit and to
evaluate stability near demobilization.

The concentration-dependence test used 31 stable plateau points spanning about 5,590 to 12,344 ppmv
across Hawaii water, South Pole surface snow, USGS45, USGS47, and W-64444. The preferred model
used standard-specific intercepts and a common H2O slope. The fitted slopes were 0.0077 +/- 0.0374 per
mil per 1000 ppmv for delta-18O and 0.633 +/- 0.546 per mil per 1000 ppmv for delta-D; both 95%
confidence intervals include zero. Thus, no concentration-dependent isotope correction was applied.

2.2 VSMOW-SLAP Calibration
The final VSMOW-SLAP calibration used known-standard runs with stable_minutes >= 10, retaining
nine stable calibration points. The South Pole jug plateaus were useful for the concentration-dependence
check but were excluded from the final scale fit by this objective duration filter. The centered calibration
model was true = a0 + b(measured - x0), fit separately for delta-18O and delta-D.

For delta-18O, x0 = -18.7417 per mil, a0 = -17.3089 per mil, b = 1.009152, RMSE = 0.115 per mil, and
R2 = 0.999965. The equivalent uncentered form is true = 1.60433 + 1.009152 x measured. For delta-D,
x0 = -138.8220 per mil, a0 = -128.4491 per mil, b = 0.996120, RMSE = 2.17 per mil, and R2 = 0.999804;
the equivalent uncentered form is true = 9.83429 + 0.996120 x measured. The final mean propagated
uncertainties are 0.125 per mil for d18O, 2.325 per mil for dD, and 2.532 per mil.

2.3 One-Minute Time Series
The corrected 1-minute record shows a coherent seasonal cycle superposed on strong synoptic variability.
Water vapor mixing ratio increased from winter 2024 values near 8,900-10,000 ppmv to summer/early-
autumn 2025 monthly means near 14,400 ppmv, then declined toward about 10,300 ppmv in winter 2025.
Delta-D was most depleted during the cool season, with monthly means near -101 to -105 per mil in
June-July and more enriched values near -86 to -88 per mil in January-March 2025. D-excess was lowest
during the warm season, near 12-15 per mil, and higher during May-July 2025, near 18-19 per mil.

Within the project-window 1-minute data, H2 O ranges from 5,013 ppmv on 7 October 2024 to
26,424 ppmv on 3 February 2025. Delta-D ranges from -181.6 per mil on 24 June 2025 to -46.6 per mil
on 19 March 2025. D-excess ranges from -3.2 per mil on 3 February 2025 to 33.3 per mil on 27 August
2025. Daily means are less extreme: H2 O spans 6,584 to 21,490 ppmv, delta-D spans -130.4
to -70.7 per mil, and d-excess spans 2.62 to 28.83 per mil.

2.4 Dominant Time Scales
The time-scale analysis indicates that synoptic and intraseasonal variability are central to the data set. For
isotope variables and d-excess, the largest variance fractions occur in the 3-10 day band, with an
additional important 10-30 day contribution. Water vapor also carries a large seasonal component at
periods longer than 250 days. Variability below one day is present, but it accounts for much less of the
total variance than the multi-day and seasonal bands.

 These results point to the importance of multi-day air-mass history, frontal passages, and seasonal
 boundary-layer humidity changes for interpreting Cape-K water vapor isotope variability. Future work
 should combine the isotope record with ARM cloud radar, lidar, surface meteorology, precipitation,
 aerosol, and trajectory products to separate local cloud-processing signals from advected Southern Ocean
 moisture-source signatures and to test isotope-enabled model representations of marine boundary-layer
 clouds.

 No peer-reviewed publications from CAPE-K_ISO have been submitted as of May 2026. The calibrated

4.0 Lessons Learned
 The analyzer was straightforward for site staff to maintain remotely, and the campaign benefited from
 clear operator notes during calibration periods. For future deployments, the strongest improvement would
 be to schedule known-standard runs earlier and at regular intervals, with at least 20-30 minutes of stable
 plateau data per standard and an intentional set of H2 O concentration steps. This would make scale
 calibration and concentration-dependence checks more robust while the instrument is still in routine
 operation.
