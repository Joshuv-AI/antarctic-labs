Some hybrid matrix triangle inequalities
Jean-Christophe Bourin, Eun-Young Lee

Abstract




Abstract.
A recent result due to Teng Zhang compares the sum of
m
matrices and the sum of their quadratic symmetric moduli:

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right\|\leq\sqrt{2}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\|

for every unitarily invariant norm. Here
|A|_{\mathrm{qsym}}
is the quadratic mean of
|A|
and
|A^{*}|
. We derive operator and eigenvalue refinements of Zhang’s inequality from a new polar decomposition for the quadratic symmetric modulus. For instance,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}+V|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}V^{*}\right)\right\}

for some unitary matrix
V
. We also establish the polar decomposition for the maximal modulus associated with Olson’s order, and derive, as in the quadratic case, a series of estimates.

Keywords.
Matrix inequalities, log-majorization, unitary orbits, symmetric modulus, Olson’s spectral order.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.
15A42, 15A60, 47A30, 47A60.

1
Introduction

In
[
10
]
, Zhang initiated a systematic study of matrix triangle inequalities involving different operator moduli. We refer to such estimates as hybrid triangle inequalities. This paper aims to refine one of Zhang’s theorems, stated in the abstract, and thereby contribute to the emerging theory of symmetrized moduli. While Zhang’s theorem is formulated in terms of weak majorization, our main results provide operator inequalities involving unitary orbits. Consequently, they yield refined eigenvalue and norm estimates.

We denote by
\mathbb{M}_{d}
the space of complex
d\times d
matrices and
\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
its cone of positive semidefinite matrices. For
A\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
, we write
|A|=(A^{*}A)^{1/2}
for its absolute value (or right modulus). If
A\in\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
, we denote by

\lambda_{1}^{\downarrow}(A)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}^{\downarrow}(A)
its eigenvalues arranged in nonincreasing order. For
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
, the weak majorization, or submajorization, relation
A\prec_{w}B
means that

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(B),\qquad k=1,\ldots,d.

(1.1)

We adhere to the convention that, for
j>d
and
A\in\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
,
\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A):=0
.
A basic property of symmetric norms (i.e., unitarily invariant ones) on
\mathbb{M}_{d}
is Fan’s dominance principle: if
A,B\in\mathbb{M}^{+}_{d}
, then

\|A\|\leq\|B\|\quad{\mathrm{for\ all\ symmetric\ norms}}\iff|A|\prec_{w}|B|.

We refer to
[
4
]
for a background and details on majorization, symmetric norms, and the matrix geometric mean.

For a matrix
Z\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
, its left and right moduli
|Z^{*}|
and
|Z|
are the positive parts in the polar decompositions

Z=|Z^{*}|U=|Z^{*}|^{1/2}U|Z|^{1/2}=U|Z|.

These moduli occur in a number of important operator inequalities. Since neither modulus enjoys an intrinsic preference, it is natural to consider symmetrized versions obtained by averaging them.
The symmetric modulus and the quadratic symmetric modulus of
Z\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
are respectively defined as

|Z|_{\mathrm{sym}}:=\frac{|Z|+|Z^{*}|}{2},\qquad|Z|_{\mathrm{qsym}}:=\sqrt{\frac{|Z|^{2}+|Z^{*}|^{2}}{2}}.

Note that

|Z|_{\mathrm{qsym}}=\sqrt{|{\mathrm{Re\,}}Z|^{2}+|{\mathrm{Im\,}}Z|^{2}}

and

|Z|_{\mathrm{sym}}\leq|Z|_{\mathrm{qsym}}

(1.2)

by operator concavity of
\sqrt{t}
. The quadratic symmetric modulus enjoys remarkably strong subadditivity properties. A striking result of Zhang
[
9
, Theorem 1.9]
states that:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then, for some unitaries
U,V\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
,

|A+B|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\leq U|A|_{\mathrm{qsym}}U^{*}+V|B|_{\mathrm{qsym}}V^{*}.

This is the symmetrized companion of the celebrated Thompson’s inequality
[
8
]
. Theorem
1.1

was motivated by the following special case
[
1
]
:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
Z\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then, for some unitaries
U,V\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
,

|Z|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\leq U|{\mathrm{Re\,}}Z|U^{*}+V|{\mathrm{Im\,}}Z|V^{*}.

Here (
1.2
) shows that the left-hand side may be replaced by
|Z|_{\mathrm{sym}}
.
Despite this observation Theorem
1.1
does not hold for the symmetric modulus
|\cdot|_{\mathrm{sym}}
. Two matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{3}
are given in
[
10
]
with

\lambda_{1}^{\downarrow}(|A+B|_{\mathrm{sym}})=\sqrt{2}\left\{\lambda_{1}^{\downarrow}(|A|_{\mathrm{sym}})+\lambda_{1}^{\downarrow}(|B|_{\mathrm{sym}})\right\}.

Here, in dimension
d\geq 3
, the constant
\sqrt{2}
is optimal. Indeed, a result of
[
2
]
states a submajorization form of the triangle inequality as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}\right|_{\mathrm{sym}}\prec_{w}\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|X_{k}\right|_{\mathrm{sym}}.

In his impressive work
[
10
]
, Zhang got the idea to study triangle inequalities between different types of moduli. In particular, he observed the following hybrid version of Theorem
1.3
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}\right|\prec_{w}\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|X_{k}\right|_{\mathrm{qsym}}.

Here both the usual modulus and the quadratic symmetric modulus appear simultaneously. The constant
\sqrt{2}
is optimal in any dimension
d\geq 2
. Theorem
1.4
is remarkable if one keeps in mind the well known submajorization
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
:

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}\right|\prec_{w}\sqrt{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|X_{k}\right|

Hence replacing the usual modulus in the right-hand side by the quadratic symmetric modulus yields the pleasant constant
\sqrt{2}
, regardless of the dimension. The constant
\sqrt{m}
is optimal whenever
m\leq d
. If
m>d
, then the multiplicative constant
\sqrt{m}
can be replaced by the optimal one
\sqrt{d}
, see
[
10
, Theorem 1.7]
.

The purpose of Section 2 is to refine Theorem
1.4
at the operator level. Our main tool is a polar decomposition associated with the quadratic symmetric modulus. It yields unitary-orbit inequalities strengthening Zhang’s submajorization estimate and leads to several eigenvalue consequences. We also establish, in Section 3, a polar decomposition for the maximal modulus associated with Olson’s spectral order. This allows us to obtain estimates for the maximal modulus similar to those of Section 2 for the quadratic modulus.

2
Unitary orbits and log-majorization

The following lemma may be regarded as a polar decomposition for the quadratic symmetric modulus.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then there exists a contraction
C\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
such that

A=\sqrt{2}|A|_{\mathrm{qsym}}^{1/2}C|A|_{\mathrm{qsym}}^{1/2}.

Proof.

By a limit argument we may suppose invertibility of
A
. It then suffices to show that

C=(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/4}A(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/4}

is a contraction. Note that the polar decomposition
A=|A^{*}|^{1/2}U|A|^{1/2}
gives

C=(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/4}\left(|A^{*}|^{1/2}U|A|^{1/2}\right)(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/4}.

Thus
C=XY
where

X=(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/4}|A^{*}|^{1/2},\qquad Y=U|A|^{1/2}(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/4}.

Hence
\|C\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\|X\|_{\infty}^{2}\|Y\|_{\infty}^{2}=\|X^{*}X\|_{\infty}\|YY^{*}\|_{\infty}
, that is

\|C\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\||A^{*}|^{1/2}(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/2}|A^{*}|^{1/2}\|_{\infty}\||A|^{1/2}(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/2}|A|^{1/2}\|_{\infty}.

Now, since
t\mapsto t^{-1/2}
is operator decreasing, we have

|A^{*}|^{1/2}(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/2}|A^{*}|^{1/2}\leq|A^{*}|^{1/2}(|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/2}|A^{*}|^{1/2}=I,

and

|A|^{1/2}(|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2})^{-1/2}|A|^{1/2}\leq|A|^{1/2}(|A|^{2})^{-1/2}|A|^{1/2}=I.

The last three inequalities together give

\|C\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 1.

∎

Now we give a hybrid comparison between the usual modulus and the quadratic symmetric modulus with the help of the geometric mean
\#
on
\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
. The result is strongly influenced by Zhang’s Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\leq\sqrt{2}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}\#V\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}V^{*}

for some unitary matrix
V\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, for every
A\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
, the block matrix

\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2}}&A\\
A^{*}&\sqrt{|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2}}\end{bmatrix}

is positive semidefinite. Hence so is the sum of
m
such matrices,

\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{k}\sqrt{|A_{k}|^{2}+|A_{k}^{*}|^{2}}&\sum_{k}A_{k}\\
\sum_{k}A_{k}^{*}&\sum_{k}\sqrt{|A_{k}|^{2}+|A_{k}^{*}|^{2}}\end{bmatrix}

where each sum runs over
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Now consider the polar decomposition

\sum_{k}A_{k}=U|\sum_{k}A_{k}|

and perform the unitary congruence

\begin{bmatrix}U^{*}&0\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{k}\sqrt{|A_{k}|^{2}+|A_{k}^{*}|^{2}}&\sum_{k}A_{k}\\
\sum_{k}A_{k}^{*}&\sum_{k}\sqrt{|A_{k}|^{2}+|A_{k}^{*}|^{2}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}U&0\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}

to get the positive semidefinite block-matrix:

\begin{bmatrix}U^{*}\left\{\sum_{k}\sqrt{|A_{k}|^{2}+|A_{k}^{*}|^{2}}\right\}U&\left|\sum_{k}A_{k}\right|\\
\left|\sum_{k}A_{k}\right|&\sum_{k}\sqrt{|A_{k}|^{2}+|A_{k}^{*}|^{2}}\end{bmatrix}

By the maximal characterization of the geometric mean,

\left|\sum_{k}A_{k}\right|\leq\left(U^{*}\left\{\sum_{k}\sqrt{|A_{k}|^{2}+|A_{k}^{*}|^{2}}\right\}U\right)\#\left\{\sum_{k}\sqrt{|A_{k}|^{2}+|A_{k}^{*}|^{2}}\right\}

Using
X\#Y=Y\#X
and setting
V=U^{*}
completes the proof.
∎

From the matrix AGM inequality we obtain:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}+V|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}V^{*}\right)

for some unitary matrix
V\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
.

Corollary
2.3
clearly implies Zhang’s Theorem
1.4
. Besides the series of eigenvalue inequalities of the form (
1.1
), Corollary
2.3
adds some new ones, by application of the classical Weyl inequalities:

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then, for all
i,j=0,1,\ldots
,

\lambda_{1+i+j}^{\downarrow}\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\right)\leq\frac{\lambda_{1+i}^{\downarrow}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right)+\lambda_{1+j}^{\downarrow}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right)}{\sqrt{2}}.

Another consequence of Theorem
2.2
is a weak log-majorization
\prec_{\mathrm{wlog}}
on
\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
given in the next corollary. Recall that, for
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
, we write
A\prec_{\mathrm{wlog}}B
to mean that the following
d
inequalities hold:

\prod_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A)\leq\prod_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(B),\qquad p=1,\ldots,d.

It is well known that
A\prec_{\mathrm{wlog}}B\Rightarrow A\prec_{w}B
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\prec_{\mathrm{wlog}}\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}.

Proof.

We have

\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}\#V\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}V^{*}=\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}^{1/2}\!\!W\!\cdot\!V\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}^{1/2}\!\!\!V^{*}

for some unitary
W\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Using Theorem
2.2
, we infer

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\leq\sqrt{2}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}^{1/2}\!\!W\!\cdot\!V\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}^{1/2}\!\!\!V^{*}.

(2.1)

This combined with Horn’s multiplicative inequalities for singular values yields

\prod_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\right)\leq\prod_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}\left(\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right)

for all
p=1,\ldots,d
. This is exactly our weak log-majorization relation
\prec_{\mathrm{wlog}}
.
∎

From another classical singular value inequality of Weyl, we obtain from (
2.1
):

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then, for all
i,j=0,1,\ldots
,

\lambda_{1+i+j}^{\downarrow 2}\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\right)\leq\,2\,\lambda_{1+i}^{\downarrow}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right)\lambda_{1+j}^{\downarrow}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right).

Note that Corollary
2.4
follows from Corollary
2.6
by applying the AGM inequality. A nice special case from either Corollary
2.3
or Corollary
2.6
is:

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then, for all
j=0,1,\ldots
,

\lambda_{1+2j}^{\downarrow}\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\right)\leq\sqrt{2}\,\lambda_{1+j}^{\downarrow}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right).

Next we turn to averages over a unitary orbit. It has recently been shown
[
3
]
that any weak majorization relation
A\prec_{w}B
in
\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
is equivalent to an inequality with an average over a unitary orbit:

A\leq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}U_{k}BU_{k}^{*}

for some unitary matrices
U_{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Therefore, Theorem
1.4
is equivalent to:

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\{X_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then, there exist unitary matrices
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
such that

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}\right|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|X_{k}\right|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}V_{i}^{*}.

That Theorem
2.8
implies Theorem
1.4
is immediate. The converse follows from the orbit-average
characterization quoted above. Going back to Corollary

2.3
, we see that we can actually choose an average with only two elements in the unitary orbits of
\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|X_{k}\right|_{\mathrm{qsym}}
,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}\right|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|X_{k}\right|_{\mathrm{qsym}}+V\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|X_{k}\right|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\right\}V^{*}\right)

for some unitary matrix
V\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
.

3
Related results for the maximal modulus

We have focused on a hybrid inequality involving the usual modulus and its quadratic symmetrized version. Hybrid inequalities with the symmetric modulus and the quadratic one are also interesting; for instance
[
3
, Corollary 2.12]

states:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
Z\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then there exist unitary matrices
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
such that

|Z|_{\mathrm{qsym}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}|Z|_{\mathrm{sym}}V_{i}^{*}.

The constant
\sqrt{2}
cannot be diminished.

A simpler relation between the symmetric modulus and the quadratic symmetric modulus is established in the next proposition. It improves (
1.2
).
By operator convexity of
t^{2}
,

\left(\frac{|Z|+|Z^{*}|}{2}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{|Z|^{2}+|Z^{*}|^{2}}{2}

equivalently

|Z|^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}\leq|Z|^{2}_{\mathrm{qsym}}.

(3.1)

This inequality implies (
1.2
) as
\sqrt{t}
is operator monotone. The following proposition highlights the geometric significance of (
3.1
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
Z\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
and let
{\mathcal{U}}
be the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Then,

|Z|_{\mathrm{sym}}({\mathcal{U}})\subset|Z|_{\mathrm{qsym}}({\mathcal{U}}).

Proof.

Thanks to (
3.1
) it suffices to show that, for
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
, we have the equivalence

A^{2}\leq B^{2}\iff A({\mathcal{U}})\subset B({\mathcal{U}}).

Since each side ensures that the range of
A
is included in the range of
B
, restricting our operators to the range of
B
if necessary, we may assume that
B
is invertible. Then

A^{2}\leq B^{2}\iff B^{-1}A^{2}B^{-1}\leq I\iff\|B^{-1}A\|_{\infty}\leq 1\iff B^{-1}A({\mathcal{U}})\subset{\mathcal{U}},

equivalently,
A({\mathcal{U}})\subset B({\mathcal{U}})
.
∎

The symmetric modulus
|Z|_{\mathrm{sym}}
is the
\ell^{1}
-mean of
|Z^{*}|
and
|Z|
, and
the quadratic symmetric modulus
|Z|_{\mathrm{qsym}}
is their
\ell^{2}
-mean.

There exists another interesting symmetrized modulus, which corresponds to a
\ell^{\infty}
-mean. This is the maximal modulus

|Z|_{\vee}
which corresponds to the maximum of
|Z|
and
|Z^{*}|
for a natural order, Olson’s order
[
7
]
. This order endows the positive matrices with a lattice structure. We may define the maximal modulus of
Z\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
with Kato’s formula
[
5
]
,

|Z|_{\vee}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}(|Z|^{n}+|Z^{*}|^{n})^{1/n}.

A concise treatment of the Olson order, and the proof of this formula is given in
[
3
]
.
We think that these three symmetric moduli are especially important ones.
Our next result may be regarded as a polar decomposition for the maximal modulus.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then, for some contraction
K\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
,

A=|A|_{\vee}^{1/2}K|A|_{\vee}^{1/2}.

Proof.

By restricting to the range of
|A|+|A^{*}|
if necessary, we may assume that
|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n}
is invertible for each integer
n>0
. It then suffices to show that

C_{n}=(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/2n}A(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/2n}

is a contraction. Indeed
(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/2n}\to|A|_{\vee}^{-1/2}
as
n\to\infty
, and we may extract from
\{C_{n}\}
a subsequence converging to a contraction
K
. The proof is then similar to that of Lemma
2.1
. The polar decomposition
A=|A^{*}|^{1/2}U|A|^{1/2}
gives

C_{n}=(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/2n}\left(|A^{*}|^{1/2}U|A|^{1/2}\right)(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/2n}.

Thus, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
\|XY\|_{\infty}\leq\|X^{*}X\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|YY^{*}\|_{\infty}^{1/2}
applied to

X=(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/2n}|A^{*}|^{1/2},\qquad Y=U|A|^{1/2}(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/2n},

we obtain

\|C_{n}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\||A^{*}|^{1/2}(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/n}|A^{*}|^{1/2}\|_{\infty}\||A|^{1/2}(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/n}|A|^{1/2}\|_{\infty}.

Now, since
t\mapsto t^{-1/n}
is operator decreasing, we have

|A^{*}|^{1/2}(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/n}|A^{*}|^{1/2}\leq|A^{*}|^{1/2}(|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/n}|A^{*}|^{1/2}=I,

and

|A|^{1/2}(|A|^{n}+|A^{*}|^{n})^{-1/n}|A|^{1/2}\leq|A|^{1/2}(|A|^{n})^{-1/n}|A|^{1/2}=I.

The last three inequalities together give

\|C_{n}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 1.

∎

Having at our disposal Lemma
3.3
we see that the block matrix

\begin{bmatrix}|A|_{\vee}&A\\
A^{*}&|A|_{\vee}\end{bmatrix}

is positive semidefinite. Arguing as in the quadratic symmetric case we thus obtain the following results:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\leq\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\vee}\right\}\#V\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\vee}\right\}V^{*}

for some unitary matrix
V\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\leq\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(|A_{k}|_{\vee}+V|A_{k}|_{\vee}V^{*}\right)}{2}

for some unitary matrix
V\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\{A_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Then,

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}\right|\prec_{\mathrm{wlog}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}|A_{k}|_{\vee}.

4
Concluding remarks

This note is a small contribution to the study of symmetric moduli. We believe that this line of research will shed further insight on matrix inequalities and decompositions. In fact, there exists already a series of impressive results, in particular in Zhang’s papers
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
. We may also mention our contributions
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
, in which several results and questions are proposed.

These symmetric moduli are natural representatives in
\mathbb{M}_{d}^{+}
for matrices in
\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Results such as Zhang’s Theorem
1.1
or the maximal polar decomposition Lemma
3.3
invite further investigation.
