Approximate Simulation-Based Verification of Compatibility of the Friedkin–Johnsen Model with Binary Observations*
Yu Xing, Aneesh Raghavan, Michael T. Schaub, Karl H. Johansson

Abstract
We consider a verification problem for opinion dynamics based on binary observations. The opinion dynamics is governed by a Friedkin-Johnsen (FJ) model, where only a sequence of binary outputs is available instead of the agents’ continuous opinions. At every time-step we observe a binarized output for each agent depending on whether the opinion exceeds a fixed threshold. The objective is to verify whether an FJ model with a given set of stubbornness parameters and initial opinions can generate the observed binary outputs up to a small error. The FJ model is formulated as a transition system, and an approximate simulation relation of two transition systems is defined in terms of the proximity of their opinion trajectories and output sequences. We then construct a finite set of abstract FJ models by simplifying the influence matrix and discretizing the stubbornness parameters and the initial opinions. It is shown that the abstraction approximately simulates any concrete FJ model with continuous parameters and initial opinions, and is itself approximately simulated by some concrete FJ model. These results ensure that consistency verification can be performed over the finite abstraction. Specifically, by checking whether an abstract model satisfies the observation constraints, we can conclude whether the corresponding family of concrete FJ models is consistent with the binary observations. Finally, numerical experiments are presented to illustrate the proposed verification framework.

I-B

Contribution

In this paper we consider a verification problem for the FJ model with binary observations.
It is assumed that agents generate binarized outputs at each time step. That is, their continuous opinions cannot be observed.
The problem of interest is to determine whether there exists an FJ model, with a given set of parameters, that can generate the observed binary observations.

The FJ model is remodeled as a transition system on a finite set by discretizing the system parameters and initial conditions.
Subsequently the model identification problem is formulated as a verification problem using temporal logic, and an approximate simulation relation is introduced suited for the discontinuous quantization function.
Unlike existing approaches, the proposed approximate simulation relation introduced in this paper incorporates the proximity of both opinions and outputs.
It is shown that the constructed abstraction can approximately simulate the original FJ dynamics when the discretization is sufficiently fine (Theorem
1
).
As a consequence, the abstraction yields a necessary condition for consistency with observations (Proposition
2
):
Violation of the specification by the abstraction guarantees violation by the original system, whereas satisfaction by the abstraction indicates potential consistency.

Unlike prior work that investigates verification of global behaviors in swarm systems
[
3
,
9
,
10
]
, the current work shifts the focus to reasoning about system parameters based on observed behaviors, providing a novel approach to understanding complex dynamics from limited data.
By leveraging logical specifications, the proposed method can incorporate missing or partial data as constraints and allows individual information, providing a flexible framework for reasoning about multi-agent systems.

Preliminaries

II-A

The Friedkin–Johnsen Model

We study a parameter reasoning problem in opinion dynamics over social networks, where
n
agents exchange opinions about a topic.
The opinion of agent
i
at time
t
is denoted by
x_{i}(t)
, and the vector
x(t)\in\mathcal{I}^{n}
collects the opinions of all agents.
The opinion formation process is modeled by the FJ model
[
7
]

x(t+1)=(I-\Lambda)Wx(t)+\Lambda x(0),\text{ for }t\in\mathbb{N},

(1)

where
\Lambda=\textup{diag}(\lambda)\in\mathcal{I}^{n\times n}
is a diagonal matrix with the diagonal
\lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{I}
representing the stubbornness of agent
i
,
and
W\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
is the influence weight matrix.
Note that the above model can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}x(t+1)\\
z(t+1)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}(I-\Lambda)W&\Lambda\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x(t)\\
z(t)\end{bmatrix},\;z(0)=x(0).

(2)

In practice, real-valued opinions are often not observable, due to coarse measurements or privacy restrictions.
Instead, quantized observations may be available.
For example, if an agent has an opinion beyond a threshold
\gamma
, she may report a value of
1
, and otherwise
0
(a common choice of
\gamma
is
\gamma=0.5
).
Overall we thus observe a binary sequence
y(t)
:

\displaystyle y(t)=\mathcal{Q}(x(t)),\quad~t\in\mathbb{N},

(3)

where
\mathcal{Q}
is the quantization function such that
y_{i}(t)=1
if
x_{i}(t)\geq\gamma
and
y_{i}(t)=0
otherwise.

II-B

Transition Systems

Discrete-time dynamical systems can be formulated as transition systems
[
1
,
20
]
. In our definition, we omit the set of inputs, since the FJ model does not have inputs.

Definition
1
(Transition System)

A transition system is a tuple
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{X},X_{0},\theta,\to,Y,H)
, where

•

\mathcal{X}
is a set of states,

•

X_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is a set of initial states,

•

\theta\in\Theta
contains parameters of the system, where
\Theta
is the parameter set,

•

\to\subseteq\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
is a transition relation depending on
\theta
,

•

Y
is a set of outputs, and

•

H\colon\mathcal{X}\to Y
is an output map.

We use
\mathcal{T}(X_{0},\theta)
to emphasize the dependence on parameters and initial states.
If
X_{0}
is a singleton, i.e.,
X_{0}=\{x_{\textup{init}}\}
, we denote the system by
\mathcal{T}(x_{\textup{init}},\theta)
.

The FJ model with binary observations (
2
)–(
3
) can then be regarded as a transition system
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)
, where
\check{x}_{\textup{init}}\coloneqq\textup{dup}(x(0))
.
Specifically, the parameters are given by
\theta=(\lambda,W)\in\Theta=\mathcal{I}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}_{n}
.
The set of states is
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{2n}

and the set of initial states is
X_{0}=\{\check{x}_{\textup{init}}\}\subseteq\textup{dup}(\mathcal{I}^{n})
.
The transition relation
\to
is defined such that
(\check{x},\check{x}^{+})\in\to
if and only if

\displaystyle\check{x}^{+}=\begin{bmatrix}(I-\Lambda)W&\Lambda\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}\check{x}.

Finally, the set of outputs is
Y=\{0,1\}^{n}
, and the output map is
H(\check{x})=\mathcal{Q}(\check{x}_{1:n})
.
The previously defined transition system
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)
with a singleton initial state possesses a unique path (defined in
[
1
]
)
\sigma=(\check{x}(0),\check{x}(1),\dots)
satisfying that
\check{x}(0)=\check{x}_{\textup{init}}
and
\check{x}(t)=[x(t)^{\top}~x(0)^{\top}]^{\top}
.

II-C

Approximate Simulation

We introduce the following normalized Hamming distance as metric for binary signals
x,y\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\|x-y\|_{\textup{H}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-y_{i}|.

For two transition systems defined by the FJ model, we define the following approximate simulation relation, which implies that, for the two systems, both states and binary observations are close.

Definition
2
(Approximate Simulation Relation)

Consider two transition systems
\mathcal{T}_{a}=(\mathcal{X}_{a},X_{a0},\to_{a},\theta_{a},\linebreak Y,H)
and
\mathcal{T}_{b}=(\mathcal{X}_{b},X_{b0},\to_{b},\theta_{b},Y,H)
, where
\mathcal{X}_{a},\mathcal{X}_{b}

\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
X_{a0},X_{b0}\subseteq\textup{dup}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
Y=\{0,1\}^{n}
is equipped with the normalized Hamming distance. Let
\delta\geq 0
be a nonnegative real number. A relation
R\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{a}\times\mathcal{X}_{b}
is an
\delta
-approximate simulation relation from
\mathcal{T}_{a}
to
\mathcal{T}_{b}
if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

for every
\check{x}^{a0}\in X_{a0}
, there exists
\check{x}^{b0}\in X_{b0}
with
(\check{x}^{a0},\check{x}^{b0})\in R
,

•

for every
(\check{x}^{a},\check{x}^{b})\in R
, it holds that
\|\check{x}^{a}_{1:n}-\check{x}^{b}_{1:n}\|\leq\delta\sqrt{n}
and
\|H(\check{x}^{a})-H(\check{x}^{b})\|_{\textup{H}}\leq\delta
, and

•

for every
(\check{x}^{a},\check{x}^{b})\in R
, it holds that
\check{x}^{a}\to_{a}(\check{x}^{a})^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{T}_{a}
implies the existence of
\check{x}^{b}\to_{b}(\check{x}^{b})^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{T}_{b}
such that
((\check{x}^{a})^{\prime},(\check{x}^{b})^{\prime})\in R
.

We say that
\mathcal{T}_{a}
is
\delta
-approximately simulated by
\mathcal{T}_{b}
or that
\mathcal{T}_{b}

\delta
-approximately simulates
\mathcal{T}_{a}
, denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{a}\preceq_{\delta}\mathcal{T}_{b}
.

Remark
1

The preceding definition introduces stronger conditions than the classic approximate simulation
[
20
]
, which only assumes output proximity.
However, in our system, a small output error
\|H(\check{x}^{a})-H(\check{x}^{b})\|_{\textup{H}}
does not guarantee
x^{a}
and
x^{b}
are close.
On the other hand, due to the discontinuity of the quantization function
\mathcal{Q}
, two nearby states
x^{a}
and
x^{b}
may produce entirely different outputs.

II-D

Specification and Problem Statement

We are interested in verifying whether a transition system with certain parameters is consistent with an observed sequence of binary outputs.
To formally specify temporal output properties of the transition system
\mathcal{T}
, we use signal temporal logic (STL)
[
16
]
.
An STL formula
\varphi
consists of a finite set of predicates
\mu
, a set of logical operators, and a set of temporal operators.
The syntax of STL can be described as:

\displaystyle\varphi::=true\;|\;\mu\;|\;\neg\varphi\;|\;\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2},

where
\neg
is the negation operator,
\wedge
is the conjunction operator, and
\mu
is a predicate defined through a predicate function
g_{\mu}\colon Y\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\mu\coloneqq\begin{cases}true,&\textup{ if }g_{\mu}(y)\geq 0,\\
false,&\textup{ if }g_{\mu}(y)<0,\end{cases}\quad\textup{ for }y\in Y.

The disjunction operator can be then defined as
\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2}=\neg(\neg\varphi_{1}\wedge\neg\varphi_{2})
.
For a trajectory
\mathbf{y}=\{y(0),~y(1),~\dots\}
and time
t\geq 0
, we say
\mathbf{y}
satisfies
\mu
at
t
, if
g_{\mu}(y(t))\geq 0
, i.e.,
(\mathbf{y},t)\models\mu\textup{ iff }g_{\mu}(y(t))\geq 0
.
Inductively,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{y},t)\models\neg\varphi

iff

\displaystyle\neg((\mathbf{y},t)\models\varphi),

\displaystyle(\mathbf{y},t)\models\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}

iff

\displaystyle(\mathbf{y},t)\models\varphi_{1}\wedge(\mathbf{y},t)\models\varphi_{2}.

The property that the output sequence is compatible with the observed ones
\{\tilde{y}(t),0\leq t\leq T\}
is encoded by a sequence of predicates
\Phi^{\kappa}=\{\varphi^{\kappa}(t),0\leq t\leq T\}
with
g_{\varphi^{\kappa}(t)}(y)\coloneqq\kappa-\|y-\tilde{y}(t)\|_{\textup{H}}
, where
\kappa\geq 0
.
For the output sequence
\mathbf{y}=\{y(t),0\leq t\leq T\}
of a transition system
\mathcal{T}(\check{x},\theta)
, define
\mathbf{y}\models\Phi^{\kappa}
iff
(\mathbf{y},t)\models\varphi^{\kappa}(t)
for all
0\leq t\leq T
.
Since
\mathcal{T}(\check{x},\theta)
admits only a unique path, we say
\mathcal{T}(\check{x},\theta)\models\Phi^{\kappa}
if
\mathbf{y}\models\Phi^{\kappa}
, where
\mathbf{y}
corresponds to its unique output sequence.
The following problem is studied in this paper.

Problem.
Given a sequence of binary observations from the FJ model
\{\tilde{y}(0),\dots,\tilde{y}(T)\}
, a set of initial states
X_{0}
, a set of parameters
\Theta_{0}
, an estimate of the influence matrix
W
, and a nonnegative real number
\kappa\geq 0
, verify whether there exists
(\check{x},\theta)\in X_{0}\times\Theta_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\check{x},\theta)\models\Phi^{\kappa}.

The satisfaction of
\Phi^{\kappa}
by
\mathcal{T}(\check{x},\theta)
means that the binary output
y(t)
is consistent with the observed
\tilde{y}(t)
up to an error
\kappa
for all
t\leq T
, where
\kappa
represents the tolerance level. When
\kappa=0
, an exact match is required, whereas a small
\kappa>0
provides robustness in the verification.
We construct abstractions that approximately simulate the original system (Theorem
1
), and develop a verification framework through abstraction (Proposition
2
).

Main Results

In this section, we provide conditions under which the original transition system

In the following assumptions, we introduce

Assumption
1

For the abstraction
\mathcal{T}^{\textup{ab}}
, let

be the set of parameters, and

, where

(ii)

The stubbornness of each agent in the abstraction

\mathcal{L}(d_{\lambda})\coloneqq\{0,\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}},\dots,\frac{d_{\lambda}-1}{d_{\lambda}},1\}
, i.e.,
\Theta^{\textup{ab}}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{L}(d_{\lambda})^{n}
, and
\lambda^{\textup{ab}}\in\Theta^{\textup{ab}}_{\lambda}

(iii)

The set of weight matrix

with

such that

\displaystyle\|W^{\textup{ab}}-W\|\leq\varepsilon_{w},

(4)

where

is the weight matrix of the original system.

Remark
2

We discretize the stubbornness parameters and initial states.
We also assume that the network structure can be observed, subject to certain noise.
When the network is generated from a random graph model, the normalized adjacency matrices are known to concentrate around averaged versions

]
, which is a property described by (

For the transition system

, recall that
\check{x}_{\textup{init}}=\textup{dup}(x(0))

The next lemma indicates the existence of an abstraction, whose parameters and initial states are close to those of the original system, such that the states of the two systems remain close.

Lemma
1

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds, and the following conditions hold for
\theta^{\textup{ab}}\in\Theta^{\textup{ab}}
and
\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}}\in\mathcal{X}^{\textup{ab}}_{0}

\displaystyle\|x_{\textup{init}}-x^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}}\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2d_{x}},

(5)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda-\Lambda^{\textup{ab}}\|\leq\frac{1}{2d_{\lambda}},

(6)

where
x_{\textup{init}}=[\check{x}_{\textup{init}}]_{1:n}
,
x^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}}=[\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}}]_{1:n}
,
\Lambda=\textup{diag}(\lambda)
and
\Lambda^{\textup{ab}}=\textup{diag}(\lambda^{\textup{ab}})
.
Then the paths
\{\check{x}(t)\}
and
\{\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}(t)\}
of
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)
and
\mathcal{T}^{\textup{ab}}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})
satisfy that, for

,

\displaystyle\|x(t+1)-x^{\textup{ab}}(t+1)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-\Lambda)W\|\|x(t)-x^{\textup{ab}}(t)\|+\sqrt{n}\varepsilon_{x},

(7)

where
x(t)=\check{x}_{[1:n]}(t)
,
x^{\textup{ab}}(t)=\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{[1:n]}(t)
, and

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{x}=\frac{\|W\|+1}{2d_{\lambda}}+\frac{1}{2d_{x}}+\varepsilon_{w}.

(8)

If it further holds that
\|(I-\Lambda)W\|<1
, then

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\|x(t)-x^{\textup{ab}}(t)\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{x}\sqrt{n}}{1-\|(I-\Lambda)W\|}.

Proof:

It follows from (
5
) and (
6
) that

\displaystyle\|\Lambda-\Lambda^{\textup{ab}}\|\leq\frac{1}{2d_{\lambda}},

\displaystyle\|(I-\Lambda)W-(I-\Lambda^{\textup{ab}})W^{\textup{ab}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-\Lambda^{\textup{ab}})(W-W^{\textup{ab}})\|+\|(\Lambda-\Lambda^{\textup{ab}})W\|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{w}+\frac{\|W\|}{2d_{\lambda}}.

Note that
x_{\textup{init}}=x(0)
,
x^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}}=x^{\textup{ab}}(0)
, and

\displaystyle\|x(t+1)-x^{\textup{ab}}(t+1)\|

\displaystyle=\|(I-\Lambda)Wx(t)+\Lambda x(0)

\displaystyle\quad\ -(I-\Lambda^{\textup{ab}})W^{\textup{ab}}x^{\textup{ab}}(t)-\Lambda^{\textup{ab}}x^{\textup{ab}}(0)\|

\displaystyle=\|(I-\Lambda)W(x(t)-x^{\textup{ab}}(t))

\displaystyle\quad\ +((I-\Lambda)W-(I-\Lambda^{\textup{ab}})W^{\textup{ab}})x^{\textup{ab}}(t)

\displaystyle\quad\ +(\Lambda-\Lambda^{\textup{ab}})x(0)+\Lambda^{\textup{ab}}(x(0)-x^{\textup{ab}}(0))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-\Lambda)W\|\|x(t)-x^{\textup{ab}}(t)\|+\bigg(\frac{\|W\|}{2d_{\lambda}}+\varepsilon_{w}\bigg)\|x^{\textup{ab}}(t)\|

\displaystyle\quad\ +\frac{1}{2d_{\lambda}}\|x(0)\|+\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2d_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-\Lambda)W\|\|x(t)-x^{\textup{ab}}(t)\|

\displaystyle\quad\ +\sqrt{n}\bigg(\frac{\|W\|+1}{2d_{\lambda}}+\frac{1}{2d_{x}}+\varepsilon_{w}\bigg).

The conclusion follows from induction.
∎

Remark
3

\varepsilon_{x}

is a contraction.
This value vanishes as

and

grows and

When the error

is small enough, at each time

are close to each other.

As noted in Remark
1
, the discontinuity in the quantization function complicates the characterization of the relation between opinions and their binary outputs.
To obtain the approximate simulation relation, we introduce the following index set of a vector
x
whose entries are close to the quantization threshold
\gamma
.
For a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define
\mathcal{V}(x,\eta)\coloneqq\{i\in\mathcal{V}:|x_{i}-\gamma|\leq\eta\}
as the set of indices
i
such that the absolute difference between
x_{i}
and
\gamma
is less than
\eta
.
Since the
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)
has only a unique path, hereafter we restrict its state space to the set of states visited along this path, denoted by
\mathcal{X}_{\textup{res}}
.
The following technical assumption ensures that only a few agents have opinions close to the threshold
\gamma
for the given time period of observation.
Relaxing this assumption is left to future work.

Assumption
2

Consider the transition system
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)
with an initial state
\check{x}_{\textup{init}}\in\textup{dup}(\mathcal{I}^{n})
and parameters
\theta\in\mathcal{I}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}_{n}
.
Assume that there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that
|\mathcal{V}(\check{x}_{1:n},\sqrt{2\delta})|\leq\delta n/2
for all
\check{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{\textup{res}}
.

The following theorem shows that the original system can be approximately simulated by the abstraction in Lemma
1
.

Theorem
1

Consider the transition system
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)
and an abstraction
\mathcal{T}^{\textup{ab}}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})
defined in Lemma
1
.
Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
hold.
If the conditions below hold

\displaystyle d_{x}\geq 1/2\delta,

(9)

\displaystyle\|(I-\Lambda)W\|<1,

(10)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{x}\leq(1-\|(I-\Lambda)W\|)\delta,

(11)

then
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\preceq_{\delta}\mathcal{T}^{\textup{ab}}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})
, where
\varepsilon_{x}
is given in (
8
).

Proof:

For
(\check{x},\check{x}^{\textup{ab}})\in R
with
\|x-x^{\textup{ab}}\|\leq\delta\sqrt{n}
,
\check{x}\to\check{x}^{+}
, and
\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}\to^{\textup{ab}}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}})^{+}
, it follows from (
7
) that

\displaystyle\|x^{+}-(x^{\textup{ab}})^{+}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-\Lambda)W\|\delta\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{n}\varepsilon_{x}\leq\delta\sqrt{n}.

This bound ensures that

\displaystyle\|x^{+}|_{(\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{2\delta}))^{\textup{c}}}-(x^{\textup{ab}})^{+}|_{(\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{2\delta}))^{\textup{c}}}\|\leq\|x^{+}-(x^{\textup{ab}})^{+}\|\leq\delta\sqrt{n},

where for a vector
x
and an index set
\mathcal{S}
,
x|_{\mathcal{S}}
represents the restriction of the vector to the entries whose indices are in
\mathcal{S}
, and for a set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
we denote
\mathcal{S}^{\textup{c}}=\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{S}
.
This upper bound implies that, except for
\delta n/2
agents in
(\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{2\delta}))^{\textup{c}}
, every agent
i
satisfies that
|x^{+}_{i}-(x^{\textup{ab}})^{+}_{i}|<\sqrt{2\delta}
.
From Assumption
2
, each agent
i\in(\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{2\delta}))^{\textup{c}}
(which has cardinality at least
(1-\delta/2)n
) satisfy that
(x^{+}_{i}-\gamma)((x^{\textup{ab}})^{+}_{i}-\gamma)>0
.
Hence
|y^{+}_{i}-(y^{\textup{ab}})^{+}_{i}|=0
for at least
(1-\delta)n
agents, meaning
\|y^{+}-(y^{\textup{ab}})^{+}\|_{\textup{H}}\leq\delta
.

For
\check{x}_{\textup{init}}\in X_{0}
, it follows from Lemma
1
that
\|x^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}}-x_{\textup{init}}\|\leq\sqrt{n}/(2d_{x})\leq\delta\sqrt{n}
. A similar argument implies that
\|H(\check{x}_{\textup{init}})-H(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}})\|_{\textup{H}}\leq\delta
, and hence
(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}})\in R
.
∎

Note that (
9
) provides an upper bound for
\delta
and (
11
) provides a lower bound.
Finer abstractions and a smaller
\|(I-\Lambda)W\|
increase the admissible range of
\delta
.

The assumptions in Theorem
1
restrict the set of systems we can reason about.
Define

\Pi_{1}\coloneqq\{(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\in\textup{dup}(\mathcal{I}^{n})\times(\mathcal{I}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}_{n}):\|(I-\Lambda)W\|<1\}

as the set of parameters satisfying condition (
10
).
Define

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}(\delta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\in\textup{dup}(\mathcal{I}^{n})\times(\mathcal{I}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}_{n}):

\displaystyle\qquad\ \mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\textup{ satisfies Assumption~\ref{asmp:prop}}\}.

Given
\delta>0
, each abstract configuration of an initial state and parameters
(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})
can represent a set of original configurations:

\displaystyle\Pi_{3}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}},\delta)\coloneqq\{(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\in\textup{dup}(\mathcal{I}^{n})\times(\mathcal{I}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}_{n}):

\displaystyle\quad\ \exists 1/(2\delta)\leq d_{x}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq d_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{N},\varepsilon_{w}\geq 0\textup{ satisfying}~\eqref{eq:apprx_condition}

\displaystyle\quad\ \textup{ such that conditions }\eqref{eq:asmp_W}\textup{--}\eqref{eq:asmp_lambda}\textup{ hold}\}.

Denote
\Pi(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}},\delta)=\Pi_{1}\cap\Pi_{2}(\delta)\cap\Pi_{3}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}},\delta)
, and we have the following consequence of the
\delta
-approximate simulation relation given in Theorem
1
.

Proposition
1

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds.
Then for
(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})\in\mathcal{X}^{\textup{ab}}_{0}\times\Theta^{\textup{ab}}
, any
\delta>0
such that
\Pi(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}},\delta)
is nonempty, and any
\kappa\geq 0
,
\mathcal{T}^{\textup{ab}}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})\not\models\Phi^{\kappa+\delta}
implies that
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\not\models\Phi^{\kappa}
for all
(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\in\Pi(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}},\delta)
.

Proof:

Suppose that there exists
(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\in\Pi(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}},\delta)
such that
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\models\Phi^{\kappa}
. The satisfaction relation implies that
\|y(t)-\tilde{y}(t)\|_{\textup{H}}\leq\kappa
,
\forall 0\leq t\leq T
.
The definition of
\Pi(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}},\delta)
and Assumption
1
indicate that
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\preceq_{\delta}\mathcal{T}^{\textup{ab}}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})
, which means that
\|y(t)-y^{\textup{ab}}(t)\|_{\textup{H}}\leq\delta
,
\forall 0\leq t\leq T
.
Hence by the triangle inequality,
\|y^{\textup{ab}}(t)-\tilde{y}(t)\|_{\textup{H}}\leq\delta+\kappa
,
\forall 0\leq t\leq T
, leading to a contradiction.
∎

The previous result makes it possible to test whether the output sequence of the transition system with a given set of parameters is consistent with observations.
In particular, to check whether the parameters and initial states in a given set are consistent with observations, we can verify
\Phi^{\kappa+\delta}
for abstractions derived from an abstract configuration set that covers the original configuration set.

Remark
4

The value of
\delta
depends on both Assumption
2
and the condition (
11
).
As noted in Remark
3
,
\varepsilon_{x}
can be made small when
d_{\lambda}
and
d_{x}
are sufficiently large and
\varepsilon_{w}
is small.
This ensures the existence of
\delta
such that (
11
) holds, and thus that the set
\Pi_{3}
is nonempty.
The existence of
\delta
and hence the nonemptiness of
\Pi
rely on the initial state and the parameters of the FJ model.
Deriving explicit conditions for
\delta
remains future work.

Proposition
1
implies the first part of the following result, whereas the second part follows from an argument similar to the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proposition
2

Let
W\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
be a fixed influence matrix
W
, and let
W^{\textup{ab}}
be an estimate of
W
such that
\|W-W^{\textup{ab}}\|\leq\varepsilon_{w}
with
\varepsilon_{w}\geq 0
.
Consider
\kappa\geq\delta>0
and a set of state-parameter configurations
\Pi_{*}\subseteq(\textup{dup}(\mathcal{I}^{n})\times(\mathcal{I}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}_{n}))\cap\Pi_{1}\cap\Pi_{2}(\delta)
.
Assume
1/(2\delta)\leq d_{x}\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq d_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
11
), and
0\leq\varepsilon_{w}<(1-\inf_{\Pi_{*}}\|(I-\Lambda)W\|)\delta
.
Then

(i)

\mathcal{T}^{\textup{ab}}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})\not\models\Phi^{\kappa+\delta}
for all
(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})\in[\Pi_{*}]_{(d_{x},d_{\lambda})}
,
implies
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\not\models\Phi^{\kappa}
,
\forall(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\in\Pi_{*}
,
where

\displaystyle[\Pi_{*}]_{(d_{x},d_{\lambda})}\coloneqq\{(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})\in\mathcal{X}^{\textup{ab}}_{0}\times(\mathcal{L}(d_{\lambda})^{n}\times\{W^{\textup{ab}}\})\colon

\displaystyle\ (\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\Lambda^{\textup{ab}})\textup{ satisfies}~\eqref{eq:asmp_x0}\textup{--}\eqref{eq:asmp_lambda}\textup{ for some }(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\in\Pi_{*}\}.

(ii)

If there exists
(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})\in[\Pi_{*}]_{(d_{x},d_{\lambda})}
satisfying Assumption
2
and
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}^{\textup{ab}}_{\textup{init}},\theta^{\textup{ab}})\models\Phi^{\kappa-\delta}
, then there exists
(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\in\Pi_{*}
such that
\mathcal{T}(\check{x}_{\textup{init}},\theta)\models\Phi^{\kappa}
.

From Proposition
2
, it follows that the abstraction yields a necessary condition for consistency with observations.
Violation of the specification by the abstraction guarantees violation by the original system, but satisfaction by the abstraction indicates potential consistency.
Therefore the verification problem introduced in Section
II-D
can be addressed by verifying whether the output sequence of the abstraction is consistent with the binary observations
\{\tilde{y}(0),\dots,\tilde{y}(T)\}
.

IV
Numerical Experiments

In this section we present numerical experiments to illustrate the obtained results.
To address the verification problem, we use the SMT solver Z3 (version 4.15.3), as all the constraints can be expressed in algebraic form.

Consider the FJ model (
1

3

n=40

The initial opinion
x_{i}(0)

\lambda_{i}

i

\mathcal{I}

The adjacency matrix of the network is generated from a stochastic block model, where the agents are divided into two equal-sized communities.
Two agents are connected with probability
0.3
if they are in the same community, and with probability
0.1
otherwise.
The influence matrix
W
is obtained by row-normalizing the adjacency matrix.
We construct abstractions under Assumption
1

d_{x}
and the number of abstract stubbornness is

As shown in Fig.

\max_{1\leq t\leq T}\|x(t)-x^{\textup{ab}}(t)\|

V
Conclusion

We considered a verification problem for the FJ model with binary observations.
We constructed finite abstractions of the FJ model and showed that these abstractions can approximately simulate the original FJ dynamics.
Future work includes optimizing the verification implementation and applying the framework to datasets.
