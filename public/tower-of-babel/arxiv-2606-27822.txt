Abstract
Supernova remnants (SNRs) strongly influence the physical and chemical properties of the molecular clouds (MCs) with which they interact. We carried out a high-resolution observation toward W28F, a chemically rich MC interacting with SNR W28, with the Atacama Compact Array (ACA) in Band 7. Significant emission ( > 10 ​ σ >10\sigma ) of CO, CH 3 ​ OH \rm CH_{3}OH , p p - H 2 ​ CO \rm H_{2}CO , SiO and SO is detected. We reveal the clumpy structures of the shocked MC, with different spatial distributions between CH 3 ​ OH \rm CH_{3}OH and SiO. We select six molecular clumps to conduct spectral decomposition and non-local-thermodynamic-equilibrium analysis with the CH 3 ​ OH \rm CH_{3}OH and p p - H 2 ​ CO \rm H_{2}CO lines. The best-fit results show a H 2 \rm H_{2} density of n H 2 ∼ 1 n_{\rm H_{2}}\sim 1 – 3 × 10 5 ​ cm − 3 3\times 10^{5}\rm\ cm^{-3} and a gas temperature of T gas ∼ 50 T_{\rm gas}\sim 50 –170 K in most of the fitted components. The H 2 \rm H_{2} density and gas temperature show a clear anti-correlation across different regions, with the thermal pressure consistent with that of the adjacent X-ray-emitting hot plasma. This is consistent with the picture that the SNR shocks propagate into multi-phase gas, with a pressure balance existing between different phases. We propose that the high abundance ratio between E- CH 3 ​ OH \rm CH_{3}OH and A- CH 3 ​ OH \rm CH_{3}OH ( > 0.9 >0.9 ) suggests extra gas-phase processes to enhance this ratio, such as proton exchange with H 3 + \rm H_{3}^{+} and HCO + \rm HCO^{+} . The chemical segregation between CH 3 ​ OH \rm CH_{3}OH and SiO, in both the spatial and spectral regime, can be explained by the fact that CH 3 ​ OH \rm CH_{3}OH traces slow shocks while SiO traces fast shocks.



I
Introduction

Supernova remnants (SNRs) exert strong influence on the physical and chemical properties of the molecular clouds (MCs) with which they interact, regulating star formation and galaxy evolution
(
55
;
72
, e.g.,)
.
So far, in our Galaxy where the SNR-MC interaction can be spatially resolved, tens of SNRs have been found to be interacting with adjacent MCs
(
52
;
108
, e.g.,)
.
The physical parameters of these MCs can be significantly altered by the heating and compression effect of shocks
(
25
;
24
)
.
The X-ray emission from the SNRs and the cosmic-rays (CRs) accelerated by the SNR shocks are also important heating source of MCs
(
64
)
.
Molecular line observations which are able to estimate the physical parameters of the shocked MCs have confirmed the physical feedback of SNRs
(
95
;
77
;
1
;
43
;
23
)
.

SNRs can also change the chemical properties, including molecular abundances and abundance ratios, of the associated MCs.
So far, a series of chemical effects related to SNR-MC interaction have been found. They include (1) formation of SiO due to shock sputtering of dust grains
(
95
;
68
;
20
;
19
)
, (2) release of dust mantle species to the gas phase (e.g.,
\rm NH_{3}

(
37
)
and
\rm H_{2}CO

(
63
)
), (3) enriched
\rm H_{3}^{+}
molecules and reduced deuteration of ionized species such as
\rm HCO^{+}
due to CR ionization
(
50
;
14
;
96
;
51

\rm He^{+}
ionized by CRs
(

and

(

due to both shock and CRs
(

90

In dense star-forming regions, molecular species whose abundances are sensitive to shock interaction are regarded as shock tracers and used to study the properties of shock wave.
These species, including SiO
(

)
,
\rm CH_{3}OH

(
6
)
, HNCO
(
82
)

They have only been detected in a limited number of MCs interacting with SNRs — IC443
(
95
)
, W28
(
68
;
63
)
, and W44
(
18
;
20
)

By taking the advantage of these shock tracers, we can study the properties of the shocked MCs without the confusion of unrelated velocity components in the line of sight.

IV.3
The E/A ratio of
\rm CH_{3}OH

The
\rm CH_{3}OH
molecule has two spin isomers, E-
\rm CH_{3}OH
and A-
\rm CH_{3}OH
.
The radiative or collisional transition between E-
\rm CH_{3}OH
and A-
\rm CH_{3}OH
is forbidden, and they are thus often treated as two separate species
(
101
)
.
Since the conversion between the two isomers requires a long timescale, the E/A ratio of
\rm CH_{3}OH
is expected to be fixed ever since it is formed.

The ground state of E-
\rm CH_{3}OH
is 7.9 K higher than that of A-
\rm CH_{3}OH
.
Therefore, if the formation environment (provided that it is in thermal equilibrium) of
\rm CH_{3}OH
is at a low temperature, we expect that the E/A ratio of
\rm CH_{3}OH
to be
<1
.
On the contrary, if the formation environment is warm enough
(
101
,
\gtrsim 37
K,)
, the E/A ratio can be
\sim 1
.
An important formation mechanism of
\rm CH_{3}OH
is successive hydrogenation of CO ice
(
99
;
28
, i.e.,
\rm CH_{3}O/CH_{2}OH+H
,)
or reaction
\rm CH_{3}O+H_{2}CO

(
86
)
on dust mantle at typical temperature of quiescent dark MCs (
\sim 15
K), followed by desorption to the gas phase, such as thermal desorption
(
9
)
, shock sputtering
(
6
)
and other various processes
(
98
, e.g.,)
.
If this is the case,
\rm E/A<1
is expected because
\rm CH_{3}OH
forms on cold (
\sim 15
K) dust mantles.
Such values have been found in cold dense MCs and protostellar outflows where the formation of gas-phase
\rm CH_{3}OH
is expected to follow this route
(
65
;
45
, e.g.,)
.
However, it is also common to find E/A close to or even higher than 1
(
75
)
.

As shown in Table
3
, the
\rm CH_{3}OH
E/A ratio in the six regions are all higher than 0.9, and in some cases higher than 1, e.g. regions 1 and 6, as well as the low
V_{\rm LSR}
components of regions 4 and 5.
If the gas-phase transformation between E-
\rm CH_{3}OH
and A-
\rm CH_{3}OH
can be neglected,
\rm E/A=0.9
will require a formation temperature of
>16
K
(
101
)
.
At this temperature,
\rm CH_{3}OH
still forms efficiently on dust mantle.
However, it is not clear whether methanol formation on dust grain surfaces alone can explain even higher E/A ratios that we detect (
>0.9
) since it would require higher dust temperatures.
The preshock MC appears in
\rm{}^{12}CO
self-absorption in W28F (see Figure
5
and Section
III.3
), which indicates that the preshock gas is at very low temperature (at least lower than the brightness temperature of the
\rm{}^{12}CO
3-2, for instance,
\sim 16
K for regions 1, 2, 5, and 6).
Simulations proposed that in shocked MCs,
\rm CH_{3}OH
forms mainly via desorption from dust grains instead of gas-phase reactions
(
48
, e.g.,)
.
Therefore, additional mechanisms are required to explain the high E/A ratio.

101
proposed that proton exchange reactions with
\rm H_{3}^{+}
and
\rm HCO^{+}
can equilibrate the E/A ratio at another characteristic temperature.
Since enhanced CR ionization rates have been found in W28
(
96
;
51
;
90
)
, it is possible that
\rm H_{3}^{+}
, a direct product of CR ionization, plays a role in raising the E/A ratio.
Such mechanism has been used to explain the high E/A in the central molecular zone of NGC 253
(
47
)
where enhanced CR ionization rates are also found
(
39
;
44
)
.
In addition, the assumptions and uncertainties in the non-LTE analysis, as well as some excitation effects
(
40
)
, may also lead to the enhanced E/A ratio that we infer from observation.
We note that the enhanced E/A ratio is expected to be the result of an interplay of multiple processes, which cannot be readily distinguished based on the current observation.

IV.4
Chemical segregation between
\rm CH_{3}OH
and SiO

Both SiO and
\rm CH_{3}OH
have been regarded as shock tracers.
SiO is believed to form via the sputtering of dust grain cores in fast shocks (
\gtrsim 25

\rm km\ s^{-1}
), followed by gas phase reactions of
\rm Si+O_{2}/OH

(
84
;
13
;
38
)
.
In addition to sputtering, processes such as grain-grain collision
(
13
)
, grain vaporization
(
36
)
, and desorption of ice mantle with pre-existing SiO
(
67
)
, can also contribute to the total yield of gas-phase SiO when the shock velocity is lower
(
13
;
53
,
\gtrsim 15
–20
\rm km\ s^{-1}
,)
.
But in general, more SiO is expected to be produced in MCs subject to faster shocks because they can release more Si atoms to the gas phase from dust grains
(
31
;
38
;
41
)
.
On the other hand,
\rm CH_{3}OH
mainly resides in dust mantles and are desorbed to the gas phase in shocked MCs (see Section
IV.3
).
Simulations found that gas-phase chemistry can produce a
\rm CH_{3}OH
abundance up to
\sim 10^{-8}
, while the inclusion of mantle desorption will raise this value to
\sim 10^{-7}
–
10^{-6}

(
8
;
53
)
.
The sputtering and evaporation of dust grain mantles requires much slower shocks than the sputtering of grain cores to produce SiO.
A shock with a velocity of
\sim 10

\rm km\ s^{-1}
is already fast enough to enhance the
\rm CH_{3}OH
abundance compared with quiescent MCs
(
53
;
49
)
.
In fast shocks, however, some studies found
\rm CH_{3}OH
can be destroyed during the sputtering or by the following gas-phase reactions
(
54
;
87
)
, while some found high
\rm CH_{3}OH
abundance even after the passage of a shock of
\sim 40
–50
\rm km\ s^{-1}

(
53
;
49
)
.

Our observation reveals the chemical segregation between
\rm CH_{3}OH
and SiO in both the spatial and spectral points of view.
Their spatial distribution is different, as shown in Figure
2
and
4
, with
\rm CH_{3}OH
peaking at region 3 and SiO peaking at regions 2 and 5.
Details about the different spatial and spectral distribution between
\rm CH_{3}OH
and SiO have been presented in Section
III.1
and
III.2
.
The estimation of the column density of SiO further confirm the enhanced
N({\rm SiO})/N({\rm CH_{3}OH})
abundance ratio in region 2 and the low
V_{\rm LSR}
component of region 5 (see Table
4
).
In addition, the
N({\rm SiO})/N({\rm CH_{3}OH})
in the low
V_{\rm LSR}
components are always higher than the values in the high
V_{\rm LSR}
components of regions 3–5, where the line widths are smaller and
\rm H_{2}
densities are higher.

All of these observational findings suggest the scenario that
\rm CH_{3}OH
formation on dust grain surface followed by desorption in low-velocity shocks should be considered as the dominant formation mechanism of gas-phase
\rm CH_{3}OH
, while SiO traces the fast shocks.
This scenario can explain both the spatial and spectral features of the
\rm CH_{3}OH
and SiO emission.

The smaller densities and the larger line widths in the low
V_{\rm LSR}
components, where the
N({\rm SiO})/N({\rm CH_{3}OH})
abundance ratios are high, can both be attributed to faster shocks.
When an SNR blast wave propagates into a MC with density fluctuation, the cloud shocks in the more diffuse clouds is expected to be faster than those in the denser clouds, and can drive stronger disturbance which gives rise to larger line widths
(
80
;
78
)
.
Toward region 2, i.e. the SiO peak, the blueshift of the SiO line can also be explained by faster shocks pushing the SiO line farther away from the systemic velocity of W28.
We note that the single Gaussian fitting of the line profile at region 2 actually underestimates the extent of this blueshift because the line profile of SiO deviates from a standard Gaussian profile (see Figure
2
).
Enhanced line ratio between SiO and
\rm CH_{3}OH
at line wings has also been observed frequently in protostellar outflows, where SiO emission is often found to be enhanced in the high velocity components
(
54
;
89
;
17
)
.

Typical values of
N({\rm SiO})/N({\rm CH_{3}OH})
in Galactic protostellar outflows fall within
\sim 3\times 10^{-4}
–
4\times 10^{-2}

(
30
;
32
;
54
;
88
;
83
;
17
, e.g.,)
.
In comparison, we detect
N({\rm SiO})/N({\rm CH_{3}OH})\gtrsim 0.1
toward three regions (components), i.e. region 2 (0.44), region 6 (0.23), and the low
V_{\rm LSR}
component of region 5 (0.17), higher than typical values in protostellar outflows.
Such high value of
N({\rm SiO})/N({\rm CH_{3}OH})
has been observed in the extremely high-velocity component of protostars
(
89
, e.g.,)
.
This further suggests that the segregation between
\rm CH_{3}OH
and SiO in W28F is due to different shock velocities.
However, we should still bear in mind that here the SiO column density is estimated based on the assumption that it traces the same gas as
\rm CH_{3}OH
, which is likely not the case.
Since the molecular gas traced by SiO can be subject to faster shock than that traced by
\rm CH_{3}OH
, SiO may reside in clumps with higher temperature and lower density than those traced by
\rm CH_{3}OH
.
In this case, the column density of SiO we obtain may be overestimated.
We also note that obtaining the abundances of
\rm CH_{3}OH
and SiO relative to
\rm H_{2}
and comparing them to typical values in other environments would be a good way to study their origin in W28F.
However, we cannot determine
N(\rm H_{2})
toward W28F since the CO lines are hard to decompose, and even if the column density of CO is estimated, the abundance of CO could be significantly lower than the typical interstellar values due to shock destruction
(
107
, e.g.,)
.

IV.5
Comparison between
\rm CH_{3}OH
and other species

As shown in Figures
3
,
6
, and
7
, the spatial distribution and spectral features of
\rm CH_{3}OH
and
p
-
\rm H_{2}CO
lines are rather similar.
This suggests that the chemistry of these two molecular species is similar to each other, both formed on ice and desorbed by the shock wave
(
21
;
35
;
11
)
.
SO, however, peaks at region 3 but also show clumpy structures at regions 2 and 5.
The
N({\rm SO})/N({\rm CH_{3}OH})
is highest at region 2 (
\approx 1.4
), and drops to 0.2–0.6 in several other components.
The SO distribution is in an intermediate manner between
\rm CH_{3}OH
and SiO suggests that it traces the intermediate velocity shocks.
Some studies also proposed the similar opinion
(
17
, e.g.,)
.
Indeed, SO is a species widely found in both cold quiescent MCs and shocks with low and high velocities
(
92
;
29
;
10
)
.
Enhancement of SO abundance in shocked MCs could be due to the desorption of S-bearing species, such as atomic S and
\rm H_{2}S
, from the dust mantle, followed by gas-phase reaction with O,
\rm O_{2}
and OH
(
73
;
92
)
.

The column densities of CO in this study are not estimated due to their complex line profiles.
Figure
5
shows that the line profiles of
\rm{}^{13}CO
and A-
\rm CH_{3}OH
are similar in the red wings but not in the blue wings.
According to our discussion in Section
IV.4
, the red wing is likely due to the low-velocity shock, while the blue wing is associated with the high-velocity shock that produces SiO.
In shocked MCs, the abundance of CO is not expected to be enhanced or reduced significantly compared to its canonical value without depletion (
\sim 10^{-4}
) in quiescent MC
(
26
)
.
The similar line profiles of
\rm{}^{13}CO
and A-
\rm CH_{3}OH
in the red wings suggest their similar kinematics under the shock interaction, although they may trace different parts of the MC due to their different critical densities.
However, in fast shocks (
\gtrsim 25

\rm km\ s^{-1}
), the CO molecules could be dissociated
(
107
)
.
The different line profiles between
\rm{}^{13}CO
and A-
\rm CH_{3}OH
in the blue wings could thus be due to their different chemistry in fast shocks.
Alternatively, it could also arise from their different dependency on the drastic density enhancement by the fast shock, provided their distinct critical densities.

V
Conclusion

We present new ACA observation of W28F, an MC interacting with SNR W28 featuring the detection of five 1720 MHz OH masers and two 36 GHz
\rm CH_{3}OH
masers.
We detect CO isotopes,
\rm CH_{3}OH
,
p
-
\rm H_{2}CO
, SO,
\rm{}^{34}SO
, SiO, and OCS lines.
Our main findings are summarized as follows.

1. Clumpy structures of shocked MCs are revealed by our observation, with masers distributed roughly at their outer edge.
All
\rm CH_{3}OH
and
p
-
\rm H_{2}CO
lines show similar spatial distribution, different from that of SO and SiO.

2. We choose six shocked clumps from the velocity channel maps of A-
\rm CH_{3}OH
and SiO and conduct (multi-)Gaussian spectral decomposition as well as non-LTE radiative transfer analysis in these regions.
The brightest
\rm CH_{3}OH
and
p
-
\rm H_{2}CO
lines always show consistent spectral line profiles, while the line centers of the SiO line sometimes deviate from their
\rm CH_{3}OH
counterparts.
The best-fit
\rm H_{2}
density and gas temperature in most of the fitted components fall in 1–
3\times 10^{5}\rm\ cm^{-3}
and 50–160 K, respectively.
We find an anti-correlation between
n_{\rm H_{2}}
and
T_{\rm gas}
, from which we estimate an average thermal pressure of
\sim 2\times 10^{7}\rm\ cm^{-3}\ K
, which is consistent with the thermal pressure of the X-ray emitting hot plasma close to W28F.
This is consistent with the picture that the SNR shocks propagate into multi-phase gas, with a pressure balance existing between different phases.

3. The E/A ratios of
\rm CH_{3}OH
in all the fitted components are
\gtrsim 0.9
, which suggests some extra processes of
\rm CH_{3}OH
in the gas phase, such as proton exchange with
\rm H_{3}^{+}
and
\rm HCO^{+}
.
The chemical segregation between
\rm CH_{3}OH
and SiO in both spatial distribution and spectral line profile is likely because
\rm CH_{3}OH
traces slower shocks (
\sim 10

\rm km\ s^{-1}
) than SiO does (
\gtrsim 25

\rm km\ s^{-1}
).
The chemistry of
\rm CH_{3}OH
and
p
-
\rm H_{2}CO
is expected to be similar because their behavior in W28F is similar.
SO is likely to trace intermediate velocity shocks.
