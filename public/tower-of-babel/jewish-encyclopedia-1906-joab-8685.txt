The name "Joab" has been especially frequent since the tenth century in the Roman family of Anaw, within which it is characteristic of the branch that calls itself "Beth-el," "Min ha-Keneset," or "De Synagoga" (See Jew. Encyc. i. 566, s.v. Anaw ). In the Anaw family the name occurs as follows:

(1) Joab, great-great-grandfather of Nathan, the author of the 'Aruk (second half of 10th cent.). (2) Joab b. Solomon b. Abraham (1166), great-great-grandson of No. 1. (3) Joab, grandfather of Paola, descendant of No. 1. (4) Joab b. Benjamin b. Solomon (1280-1304), grammarian and Biblical exegete in Rome. (5) Joab b. Solomon b. Shabbethai b. Mattithiah, father of Menahem, in Rome (about 1340). In the fourteenth century a synagogue of R. Joab in Rome is mentioned. The following is a genealogical table of the Joab family:

I. Most of the persons named in the genealogical table are known merely as copiers of Hebrew manuscripts. Only the following are more important:

Whether he is identical with the religious poet attacked by Immanuel b. Solomon can not be determined. Zunz mentions three poets of this name.
