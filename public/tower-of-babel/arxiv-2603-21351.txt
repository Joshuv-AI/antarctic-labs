Functions of pairs of commuting self-adjoint operators under relatively bounded perturbations
A.B. Aleksandrov and V.V. Peller

Abstract
. We study the behaviour of functions of pairs of commuting self-adjoint operators under perturbations by relatively bounded operators. We obtain analogs of our earlier results for functions of a single self-adjoint operator under relatively bounded perturbations. The main tool is double operator integrals

Introduction

This paper can be considered as a sequel of our earlier papers
[
Pe
]
,
[
APPS
]

[

]
. We study the behaviour of functions of normal operators (or, functions of pairs of commuting self-adjoint operators under perturbations.

Let
N
and
M
be (not necessarily bounded) normal operators with the same domain. We are going to consider
N
as an initial operator and
M
is a perturbed operator. The operator
M-N
will be interpreted as the perturbation. Recall that
M-N
is said to be a
relatively bounded perturbation of

N
if
{\rm Dom}(M)\subset{\rm Dom}(N)
and
the following inequality hods

\|(M-N)x\|\leq\operatorname{const}\big(\|Nx\|+\|x\|),\quad x\in{\rm Dom}(M),

where
{\rm Dom}(N)
stands for the domain of
N
.

It turns out that it is more natural to consider the assumption that
\operatorname{Re}M-\operatorname{Re}N
is a relatively bounded perturbation of
\operatorname{Re}N

and
\operatorname{Im}M-\operatorname{Im}N
is a relatively bounded perturbation of
\operatorname{Im}N
. It is easy to see that this assumption implies that
M-N
is a relatively bounded perturbation of
N
. The converse, however, is false. Indeed, let
A
be an unbounded self-adjoint operator. Put

N={\rm i}A\quad\mbox{and}\quad M=A+{\rm i}A.

Clearly,
M-N
is a relatively bounded perturbation of
N
. On the other hand,
\operatorname{Re}M-\operatorname{Re}N=A
and
\operatorname{Re}M-\operatorname{Re}N=A
and, clearly,

\operatorname{Re}M-\operatorname{Re}N
is not a relatively bounded perturbation of
\operatorname{Re}N={\boldsymbol{0}}
.

Thus, we are going to consider the case when both
\operatorname{Re}M-\operatorname{Re}N
is a relatively bounded perturbation of
\operatorname{Re}N
and
\operatorname{Im}M-\operatorname{Im}N
is a relatively bounded perturbation of
\operatorname{Im}N
. Passing to the real and imaginary parts, we arrive at the situation when we deal with an unperturbed pair
(A_{1},A_{2})
and a perturbed pair
(B_{1},B_{2})
of commuting self-adjoint operators.
Note that the most natural way to define the notion of commuting self-adjoint operators is to say that
two self-adjoint operators commute if their spectral measures commute
.

Our assumption that
B_{1}-A_{1}
is a relatively bounded perturbation of
A_{1}
and
B_{2}-A_{2}
is a relatively bounded perturbation of
A_{2}
.
It is well known (see e.g.
[
AP3
]
) that this assumption is equivalent to the fact that

(A_{1},A_{2})
and
(B_{1},B_{2})
are pairs of commuting self-adjoint operators such that
the operators

\displaystyle(B_{1}-A_{1})(A_{1}+{\rm i}I)^{-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(B_{2}-A_{2})(A_{2}+{\rm i}I)^{-1}\quad\mbox{are bounded}.

(1.1)

2.
Double operator integrals and Schur multipliers

In this section we give a brief introduction to double operator integrals and Schur multipliers. We refer the reader to

[
BSh1
]
,
[
BSh2
]
,
[
AP1
]
,
[
Pe2
]
and
[
Pe3
]
for more detailed information.

Double operator integrals are expressions of the form

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathscr{X}}\times{\mathscr{Y}}}\Phi(x,y)\,{\rm d}E_{1}(x)Q\,{\rm d}E_{2}(y).

(2.1)

Here
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are spectral measures on Hilbert space,
\Phi
is a bounded measurable function and

Q
is a bounded linear operator on Hilbert space. Such double operator integrals appeared first in the paper
[
DK
]
by Yu.L. Daletskii and S.G. Krein. Later M.Sh. Birman and M.S. Solomyak in
[
BSh1
]
created a rigorous beautiful theory of double operator integrals. They started with the case when
Q
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator and defined the double operator integral in (
2.1
) as

\left(\int_{{\mathscr{X}}\times{\mathscr{Y}}}\Phi\,{\rm d}{\rm E}\right)Q,

where
{\rm E}
is the spectral measure on the Hilbert Schmidt class
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}
, which is defined on the measurable rectangles by

{\rm E}(\Lambda\times\Delta)Q=E_{1}(\Lambda)QE_{2}(\Delta),\quad Q\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}.

It follows from the definition that in this case the double operator integral in (
2.1
) belongs to
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}
and

\left\|\iint\Phi(x,y)\,{\rm d}E_{1}(x)Q\,{\rm d}E_{2}(y)\right\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}}\leq\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Q\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{2}}.

A bounded measurable function
\Phi
on
{\mathscr{X}}\times{\mathscr{Y}}
is called a
Schur multiplier (of the trace class
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1}
) with respect to the spectral measures
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
if

Q\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\iint\Phi\,{\rm d}E_{1}Q\,{\rm d}E_{2}\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1}.

We denote the class of Schur multipliers of
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{1}
with respect to spectral measures
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
by
\mathfrak{M}_{E_{1},E_{2}}
.

If
\Phi\in\mathfrak{M}_{E_{2},E_{1}}
, we can define by duality the double operator integral in (
2.1
) for arbitrary bounded linear operator
Q
and the transformer

Q\mapsto\iint\Phi\,{\rm d}E_{1}Q\,{\rm d}E_{2}

becomes a bounded linear operator in the operator norm.

In this paper we consider the situation when the spectral measures
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are defined on the Borel subsets of the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
the class of
Borel Schur multipliers
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
i.e. the class of of Borel functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
that are Schur multipliers with respect to arbitrary spectral measures on the Borel subsets of the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

We proceed now to the definition of the Haagerup tensor product of subspaces of
L^{\infty}
spaces.

Suppose that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are spectral measures. The
Haagerup tensor product

L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{1}}}\otimes_{\rm h}L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{2}}}
of
L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{1}}}
and
L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{2}}}
is, by definition, the class of functions
\Phi

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
of the form

\displaystyle\Phi(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\varphi_{n}(x)\psi_{n}(y),\quad\mbox{for}\quad\varphi_{n}\in L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{1}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi_{n}\in L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{2}}}

(2.2)

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\in L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{1}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{n\geq 0}|\psi_{n}|^{2}\in L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{2}}}.

(2.3)

The norm of
\Phi
in
L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{1}}}\otimes_{\rm h}L^{\infty}_{E_{A_{2}}}
is the infimum of the expression

\left\|\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}|\varphi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\left\|\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}|\psi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

over all representations of
\Phi
in the form of (
2.2
).

There are various descriptions of the space
\mathfrak{M}_{E_{1},E_{2}}
of Schur multipliers, see
[
Pe
]
,
[
AP1
]
and
[
AP2
]
. We mention the following one given in
[
Pe
]
:
\Phi\in\mathfrak{M}_{E_{1},E_{2}}
if and only

\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{E_{1}}\otimes_{\rm h}L^{\infty}_{E_{2}}
. Note that the implication

\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{E_{1}}\otimes_{\rm h}L^{\infty}_{E_{2}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Phi\in\mathfrak{M}_{E_{1},E_{2}}

was obtained earlier in
[
BSh2
]
.

Note also that in
[
AP2
]
it was shown that

\|\Phi\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{E_{1},E_{2}}}=\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{E_{1}}\otimes_{\rm h}L^{\infty}_{E_{2}}},\quad\Phi\in\mathfrak{M}_{E_{1},E_{2}}.

\boldsymbol{B_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

In this section we briefly introduce the Besov space
B_{\infty,1}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. More detailed information about Besov spaces
in more general situations can be found in
[

]
,
[
Pee
]

]

Let
w
be an infinitely differentiable function on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle w\geq 0,\quad\operatorname{supp}w\subset\left[1/2,2\right],\quad\mbox{and}\quad w(t)=1-w\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\in[1,2].

(3.1)

We define the functions
W_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

\big({\mathscr{F}}W_{n}\big)(x)=w\left(\frac{\|x\|}{2^{n}}\right),\quad n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2}),\quad\|x\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\Big(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

where
{\mathscr{F}}
is
Fourier transform
defined on
L^{1}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
by

\big({\mathscr{F}}f\big)(t_{1},t_{2})=\!\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)e^{-{\rm i}(x_{1}t_{1}+x_{2}t_{2})}\,{\rm d}x_{1}\,{\rm d}x_{2},\!\quad

Clearly,

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathscr{F}}W_{n})(t)=1,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}.

With each tempered distribution
f
in
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
we associate the sequence

\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
,

\displaystyle f_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f*W_{n}.

(3.2)

The formal series
\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{n}
, being a Littlewood–Payley type expansion of
f
, does not necessarily converges to
f
.
We start with the definition of the (homogeneous) Besov class
\dot{B}^{1}_{\infty,1}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
,

s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
0<p,\,q\leq\infty
, as the space of distributions

f
of class
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
(i.e., tempered distributions) such that

\displaystyle\{2^{n}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}),\quad\|f\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\big\|\{2^{n}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|_{\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}})}.

(3.3)

In accordance with this definition,
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
contains all polynomials; next,
\|f\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}=0
for an arbitrary polynomial
f
. Moreover, the distribution
f
is determined by the sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}

uniquely up to a polynomial. It is easy to see that the series

\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}
converges in
1
1

1

The space

{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is equipped with the weak topology

\sigma\big({\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\big)
.

{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
However, the series
\sum_{n<0}f_{n}
can diverge in general. Nevertheless, it can be shown that the series

\displaystyle\sum_{n<0}\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{n<0}\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{2}}

(3.4)

converge uniformly in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Now we can define the modified (homogeneous) Besov space
B^{s}_{p,q}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\big)
. We say that a distribution
f

belongs to the Besov class
B^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(
3.3
) is satisfied and

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{2}}\quad

in the space
{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
. Now the function
f
is uniquely determined by the sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
up to a constant. Moreover, a polynomial
g
belongs to

B^{1}_{\infty,1}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
if and only if it is a constant.

4.
The main result

The key role in this paper will be played by the integral formula given in the following theorem.
We are going to use the notation

x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Also, for pairs of commuting self-adjoint operators
(A_{1},A_{2})
and
(B_{1},B_{2})
, we use the notation
E_{A}
for the joint spectral measure of the pair
(A_{1},A_{2})
and
E_{B}
for the joint spectral measure of the pair
(B_{1},B_{2})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
(A_{1},A_{2})
and
(B_{1},B_{2})
are pairs of commuting self-adjoint operators satisfying (
1.1
).
Suppose that
f
is a continuous function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that the functions

(x,y)\mapsto\frac{f(x_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},x_{2})}{y_{2}-x_{2}}(x_{2}+{\rm i}),\quad(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

and

(x,y)\mapsto\frac{f(y_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},y_{2})}{y_{1}-x_{1}}(x_{1}+{\rm i}),\quad(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

belong to the space
\mathfrak{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
of Borel Schur multipliers.

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle(B_{1},B_{2})-f(A_{1},A_{2})

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{f(x_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},x_{2})}{y_{2}-x_{2}}(x_{2}+{\rm i})\,{\rm d}E_{B}(y_{1},y_{2})(B_{2}-A_{2})(A_{2}+{\rm i}I)^{-1}\,{\rm d}E_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{f(y_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},y_{2})}{y_{1}-x_{1}}(x_{1}+{\rm i})\,{\rm d}E_{B}(y_{1},y_{2})(B_{1}-A_{1})(A_{1}+{\rm i}I)^{-1}\,{\rm d}E_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})

(4.1)

First, we establish several auxiliary facts.
For a function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we put

f_{t}(s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(t,s)\quad\mbox{and}\quad f^{s}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(t,s).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
be a function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Suppose that

\displaystyle|(f(x_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},x_{2}))(x_{2}+{\rm i})|\leq C|y_{2}-x_{2}|

(4.2)

and

\displaystyle|f(y_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},y_{2}))(x_{1}+{\rm i})|\leq C|y_{1}-x_{1}|

(4.3)

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Then
f
is bounded.

Proof.
Condition (
4.2
) can be rewritten as follows

|f_{t}(s_{1})-f_{t}(s_{2})|\leq C\frac{|s_{1}-s_{2}|}{\sqrt{s_{1}^{2}+1}}

for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Substituting
s_{2}=0
we get
|f(t,s)-f(t,0)|\leq C

for all
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

In the same way we have
|f(t,s)-f(0,s)|\leq C
for all
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
It remains to note that

|f(t,s)-f(0,0)|\leq|f(t,s)-f(t,0)|+|f(t,0)-f(0,0)|\leq 2C

and
|f(t,s)|\leq 2C+|f(0,0)|
for all
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
\blacksquare

We also need the following assertion.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f
be a function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Suppose that

\displaystyle|f(x_{1},y_{2})(y_{2}+{\rm i})-f(x_{1},x_{2})(x_{2}+{\rm i})|\leq C|y_{2}-x_{2}|

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle|f(y_{1},y_{2})(y_{1}+{\rm i})-f(x_{1},y_{2})(x_{1}+{\rm i})|\leq C|y_{1}-x_{1}|

(4.5)

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

Then
f
is bounded.

Proof.
Condition (
4.4
) can be rewritten as follows

|f_{t}(s_{1})(s_{1}+{\rm i})-f_{t}(s_{2})(s_{2}+{\rm i})|\leq C|s_{1}-s_{2}|

for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Substituting
s_{2}=t=0
we get

|f_{0}(s)(s+{\rm i})-f(0,0){\rm i}|\leq C|s|

for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence,
f_{0}
is a bonded function. Let
|f_{0}|\leq C_{1}
.

Condition (
4.5
) can be rewritten as follows

|f^{s}(t_{1})(t_{1}+{\rm i})-f^{s}(t_{2})(t_{2}+{\rm i})|\leq C|t_{1}-t_{2}|

for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Hence,

|f(t,s)(t+{\rm i})-f(0,s){\rm i}|\leq C|t|

for all
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

It remains to observe that

|f(t,s)|\leq\frac{C|t|+C_{1}}{|t+{\rm i}|}.\quad\blacksquare

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.
Assume first that the self-adjoint operators in question are bounded. Obviously, the divided differences

(x,y)\mapsto\frac{f(x_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},x_{2})}{y_{2}-x_{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(x,y)\mapsto\quad\frac{f(y_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},y_{2})}{y_{1}-x_{1}}

are Schur multipliers with respect to
E_{A}
and
E_{B}
.

We have

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{f(x_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},x_{2})}{y_{2}-x_{2}}(x_{2}+{\rm i})\,{\rm d}E_{B}(y_{1},y_{2})(B_{2}-A_{2})(A_{2}+{\rm i}I)^{-1}\,{\rm d}E_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})\\[5.69046pt]
=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{f(x_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},x_{2})}{y_{2}-x_{2}}\,{\rm d}E_{B}(y_{1},y_{2})(B_{2}-A_{2})\,{\rm d}E_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})

Clearly, the function

(x,y)\mapsto f(x_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},x_{2})

must also be a Schur multipliers with respect to
E_{A}
and
E_{B}
.

By Lemma
4.2
, the function
f
must be bounded, and so the function

(x,y)\mapsto f(x_{1},x_{2})
, being independent of
y
, must be in
\mathfrak{M}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})

which in turn implies that the function
(x,y)\mapsto f(x_{1},y_{2})
must be a Schur multipliers with respect to
E_{A}
and
E_{B}
.

Thus,

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{f(x_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},x_{2})}{y_{2}-x_{2}}\,{\rm d}E_{B}(y_{1},y_{2})(B_{2}-A_{2})\,{\rm d}E_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})\\[5.69046pt]
=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(f(x_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},x_{2}))\,{\rm d}E_{B}(y_{1},y_{2})\,{\rm d}E_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})\\[5.69046pt]
=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(x_{1},y_{2})\,{\rm d}E_{B}(y_{1},y_{2})\,{\rm d}E_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})-f(A_{1},A_{2}).

Similarly,

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{f(y_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},y_{2})}{y_{1}-x_{1}}(x_{1}+{\rm i})\,{\rm d}E_{B}(y_{1},y_{2})(B_{1}-A_{1})(A_{1}+{\rm i}I)^{-1}\,{\rm d}E_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})\\[5.69046pt]
=f(B_{1},B_{2})-\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(x_{1},y_{2})\,{\rm d}E_{B}(y_{1},y_{2})\,{\rm d}E_{A}(x_{1},x_{2}).

This proves formula (
4.1
) in the case of bounded operators. The reduction to the case of possibly unbounded operators is more or less standard, see, e.g. the proof of Theorem 5.2 in
[
APPS
]
.

Indeed, clearly, the double operator integrals on the right-hand side of (
4.1
) determine bounded linear operators. Multiply them by the spectral projections

P_{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}E_{B}(\{(y_{1},y_{2}):~|y_{1}|\leq M~\;\mbox{and}~\;|y_{2}|\leq M\}

on the left and by the spectral projections

Q_{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}E_{B}(\{(x_{1},x_{2}):~|x_{1}|\leq M~\;\mbox{and}~\;|x_{2}|\leq M\}

on the right. Obviously, the resulting sequence converges to the right-hand side of (
4.1
) in the strong operator topology. It remains to observe that
formula (
4.1
) holds if we replace the pairs
(A_{1},A_{2})
and
(B_{1},B_{2})
with the pairs

(Q_{M}A_{1},Q_{M}A_{2})
and
(P_{M}B_{1},P_{M}B_{2})
of bounded commuting self-adjoint operators
and proceed to the limit as
M\to\infty
in the strong operator tipology.
\blacksquare

Corollary 4.5
.

Under the hypotheses of Lemma

the function

(x,y)\mapsto\frac{f(y_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},y_{2})}{y_{1}-x_{1}}(x_{1}+{\rm i})

Corollary 4.6
.

Under the hypotheses of Lemma

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(A_{1},A_{2})
and

be pairs of commuting self-adjoint operators satisfying
(
1.1
).
Suppose that
f

such that the functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}

g_{1}(s,t)=f(s,t)(s+i)\quad\mbox{and}\quad g_{2}(s,t)=f(s,t)(t+i)

. Then the functions

(x,y)\mapsto\frac{f(y_{1},y_{2})-f(x_{1},y_{2})}{y_{1}-x_{1}}(x_{1}+{\rm i})

and

are Borel Schur multipliers on

and

\big\|f(B_{1},B_{2})-f(A_{1},A_{2})\big\|\leq\operatorname{const}\big(\|g_{1}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{1}}+\|g_{2}\|_{B_{\infty,1}^{1}}\big)\\
\times\max\Big\{\big\|(B_{1}-A_{1})(A_{1}+{\rm i}I)^{-1}\big\|,\big\|(B_{2}-A_{2})(A_{2}+{\rm i}I)^{-1}\big\|\Big\}.

(4.6)
