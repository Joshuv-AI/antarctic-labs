1. Project Overview
Adapting routine techniques and technologies of various scientific fields is a commonly
used approach in safeguards, e.g., satellite imagery, plombs, etc. However, the
International Atomic Energy Agency’s (IAEA) safeguards system is not too widely known
in these fields. Moreover, even in the nuclear field, knowledge about IAEA safeguards
could be more widespread and that would help reduce the recruitment struggles of the
IAEA especially in very specialized technical areas. Additionally, at LANL, several
divisions and groups have been involved in IAEA safeguards relevant missions, and the
collaboration could be significantly improved among them which could lead to
strengthening LANL’s role in IAEA safeguards.

The Nuclear Engineering and Nonproliferation (NEN) Division has a long history of
providing training courses on various topics including non-destructive analysis (NDA) for
safeguards inspectors. The Trace Analytical Chemistry Team of the Nuclear and
Radiochemistry Group (C-NR) of the Chemistry Division is part of the Network of
Analytical Laboratories (NWAL) for the bulk analysis of environmental samples. The
team has been working on increasing LANL’s role in environmental sampling and
destructive analysis (DA) training. To help this effort, utilizing NEN’s experience in
training development and improving the level of collaboration, in FY25 and FY26, the
NEN and C divisions cooperated in developing a safeguards workshop that provided
early- and mid-career subject matter experts (SMEs) an opportunity to better
understand the role of NDA and DA measurements in the implementation of IAEA
safeguards and LANL’s role and capabilities to address current challenges in
safeguards implementation.
In FY25, this workshop targeted mainly colleagues of NEN-1 and C-NR to raise
awareness and build collaboration between the two groups on current safeguards
implementation issues and future opportunities. In FY26, the workshop targeted a wider
audience with safeguards relevant skills across the LANL complex and outside of LANL.

3. Workshop Agenda
The Workshop agenda (Figure 1) had three large topic areas.
The first half day started with a brief safeguards history that was also beneficial for
participants even with safeguards experience. Then routine NDA and DA techniques
used by the IAEA for safeguards purposes were covered.
The second half day focused on safeguards related NDA R&D (research and
development) projects, i.e., INCC6 Neutron NDA Software Implementation in DAIL for
LANL Plutonium Facility Waste Measurements, Microcalorimetry for Safeguards and
Japan NDA Systems at RRP and JMOX. The second section was concluded by the
Non-destructive Analysis Laboratory Tour at TA66.
The third half day introduced the participants to LANL’s NWAL Bulk Analysis Program
then discussed two DA R&D projects: From Soil to Sky: An Overview of Environmental
Sampling Applications and R&D and LG-SIMS particle isotope analysis for nuclear
safeguards. Finally, the third section and the Workshop were concluded by the
Destructive Analysis Laboratory Tour.

4. Participants
The maximum number of participants was set to 20 because that was the maximum
number of people the laboratory tours could accommodate.
For advertising the Workshop, several flyers have been prepared for LANL targeting
different groups of potential participants and we also reached out to Sandia National
Laboratory (SNL). Figure 2 shows an example of the LANL flyer.

 Figure 2. The NDA-DA Safeguards Workshop flyer

Two participants from SNL attended the Workshop and there were 18 participants from
LANL. The LANL participants were from seven different groups:
  NEN-1: Safeguards Science & Technology Group
  C-NR: Nuclear and Radiochemistry Group
  LEFFF/MST-8: Low-Enriched Fuel Fabrication Facility / Structure / Property
 Relations Group
  C-CDE: Chemical Diagnostics and Engineering Group
  C-IIAC: Inorganic, Isotope, and Actinide Chemistry Group
  C-PCS: Physical Chemistry and Applied Spectroscopy Group
  NMCA, LAMCAS: Nuclear Material Control and Accountability, Los Alamos
 Material Control & Accountability System

A large portion of the participants had experience either in the NDA or the DA field. All of
them had longer experience in these fields than in safeguards. A few participants didn’t
have NDA or DA experience, at all. Thirteen participants didn’t have any safeguards
experience. We can conclude that our outreach was successful and we attracted the
right audience.

5. Feedback
A survey was distributed at the end of the workshop. We received 16 surveys from the
participants.
The questions of the survey were as follows:
  Lecturers: (subject knowledge, delivery,). We welcome your specific comments
 on the lecturers for this course.
  How was the workshop specifically beneficial to your professional development
 and job needs?
  What was most useful about this workshop?
  What additional subject would you like to see included?
  What improvements in the format or scheduling of the workshop would you
 recommend?
  Would you recommend this workshop to your colleagues? Why or why not?
  Additional Comments

The lectures and lecturers received overwhelmingly positive feedback. Several
participants pointed out their knowledge and enthusiasm towards safeguards.
Some areas to improve:

  A few lectures were a few minutes longer than the length according to the
 agenda.
  Some lectures were a little monotone due to the too much information to be
 shared.
  Some lectures repeated general safeguards information.

For some participants, the workshop was directly related to their current position:
“It applies directly to my position as we set up LEFFF, the importance of safeguards as
we start to fabricate new fuel types.”
“It has given me a better understanding of how IAEA goes about inspections. This will
be very useful in proposing future research to support safeguards activities.”
“It gives me a better understanding of DA & how to better suit NA-241 needs.”

For others, even though it wasn’t directly relevant to their day-to-day work, the
benefitted from the workshop:
“It was fascinatory and opened up the door to safeguards as a career interest.”
“I’m now much more interested in detectors than before.”

The participants pointed out technically each segment of the workshop when they listed
the most useful part from “exposure to NDA techniques”, through “learning about the
different R&D projects” to the lab tours.

For additional subjects, the participants suggested a few great ideas: (1) inviting a guest
inspector, (2) a lecture with policy background and (3) demonstration of NDA detectors
in use.

Most participants were satisfied with the workshop. They mentioned the 1.5-day length
was optimal and the schedule was appropriate. There were a few constructive
suggestions: “sending out Outlook calendar invites as additional communication”, “not
going over breaktimes” and “more hands-on activities, e.g. handling detectors and
taking swipes”.

All participants that filled out the survey would recommend the workshop for various
reasons. Here are the most relevant ones:

“It shows how much they care about us learning new info.”
“Especially to newer staff who will be supporting safeguards research.”
“Distinct from much of SNL work but very important for IAEA mission.”
“I personally learned a lot and a lot of the talks were very engaging and well organized.”
“If they’re interested in rad detectors, because it really goes over so much of the
practical used for detectors.”
“Very informational intro to safeguards.”

6. Improvements for Potential Future Workshops
We believe that the length of the workshop is optimal for the information we want to
share, however two participants could not stay for the entire workshop. If people from
outside of LANL are attending the workshop, the 1.5-day setup is optimal. If participants
are only from LANL, probably three half day setup is better.
We would like to add a plenary discussion inviting colleagues with a career in
safeguards including senior colleagues with potentially decade(s) of safeguards
experience and also staff who spent part of their career in other field(s). During this
session, the invitees could tell their story about how they chose safeguards as a career
path.
We could also investigate whether there is a colleague at LANL who used an IAEA
safeguards inspector and invite him/her for a story telling lecture. Alternatively, the
participants really enjoyed Steve Balsley’s stories about how environmental sampling
was implemented for safeguards.
