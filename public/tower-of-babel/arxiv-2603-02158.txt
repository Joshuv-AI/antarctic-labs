Crossover from generalized to conventional hydrodynamics in nearly integrable systems under relaxation time approximation
Saikat Santra, Maciej Łebek, Miłosz Panfil

Abstract
Upon breaking the integrability, the equations of generalized hydrodynamics (GHD) are supplemented by a Boltzmann collision term. Such terms are typically complicated and stem from a perturbative treatment of integrability-breaking terms in the hamiltonian. In our work, we study a simplified version of the collision operator in a form of relaxation time approximation familiar from kinetic theory. We explicitly compute transport coefficients which characterize the Navier-Stokes (NS) hydrodynamic regime emerging at large space-time scales. We also thoroughly study the crossover between GHD and NS hydrodynamic descriptions, identifying relevant characteristic space-time scales for the transition. In particular, we show how the emergence of NS hydrodynamics is visible in dynamics of conserved and non-conserved charge densities, and in hydrodynamic two-point functions.



I
Introduction

Understanding the out-of-equilibrium behavior of interacting many-body quantum systems remains one of the most profound and active areas of research in theoretical physics. Generic isolated systems are expected to thermalize under non-equilibrium dynamics
Sengupta et al. 2004
;
Berges et al. 2004
;
Rigol et al. 2008
;
Abanin et al. 2019
. However, certain class of complex systems have been identified that appear to avoid thermalization even after long times due to their integrable structure. To describe the hydrodynamics of such systems, a theoretical framework known as generalized hydrodynamics (GHD) has been developed

;
Castro-Alvaredo et al. 2016
;
De Nardis et al. 2018
;
Doyon 2020
;
Bastianello et al. 2022
;
Doyon et al. 2025
. It captures the essential characteristics of integrable dynamics, namely the existence of infinitely many conservation laws and the presence of stable, ballistically propagating quasiparticles. This framework has been successful in explaining various phenomena, such as the lack of thermalization observed in the well-known Newton’s cradle experiment
Kinoshita et al. 2006
;
Tang et al. 2018
, the derivation of exact expressions for Drude weights
Doyon and Spohn 2017a
;
Ilievski and De Nardis 2017a
;
Ilievski and De Nardis 2017b
;
Bulchandani et al. 2018
and the accurate description of anomalous transport in strongly interacting spin chains
Ljubotina et al. 2017
;
Sánchez et al. 2018
;
Ilievski et al. 2018
;
Gopalakrishnan and Vasseur 2019
;
Ljubotina et al. 2019
;
De Nardis et al. 2019
, to name a few. Recent developments
Kinoshita et al. 2006
;
Tang et al. 2018
;
Schemmer et al. 2019
;
Wilson et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2020
;
Møller et al. 2021
;
Cataldini et al. 2022
;
Li et al. 2023
in cold atomic systems, particularly in controlling and manipulating the interactions have fuelled the progress of GHD which by now has undergone several numerical and experimental verifications
Doyon et al. 2017
;
Doyon and Spohn 2017b
;
Biagetti et al. 2024
;
Malvania et al. 2021
;
Horvath et al. 2025
;
Zeng et al. 2026
;
Dubois et al. 2026
;
Dubois et al. 2024
;
Charnay et al. 2026
.

Integrable systems are fine-tuned and not exactly realized in experiments. Therefore, it is important to study nearly integrable systems where the integrability is weakly broken due to, for instance, additional interactions present among the particles. In such scenario a system is described by a Hamiltonian consisting of an integrable part,
\hat{H}_{0}
and a weak non-integrable perturbation
\lambda\hat{V}
, where
\lambda
is a small parameter controlling the strength of the integrability breaking. If the perturbation
\hat{V}
describes an additional, long-range interactions between the particles and thus destroys all but three of the conserved quantities of the underlying integrable model, it has been shown that such systems eventually thermalize at times
t\approx\mathcal{O}[(a\lambda)^{-2}]

Biagetti et al. 2025
where
a
is the parameter controlling strength of integrable interactions. At large space-time scales, the effect of integrability breaking can be incorporated as corrections to the original GHD of the underlying integrable model
Møller et al. 2021
;
Cataldini et al. 2022
;
Caux et al. 2019
;
Friedman et al. 2020
;
Bouchoule et al. 2020
;
Bastianello et al. 2020
;
Durnin et al. 2021
;
Lopez-Piqueres et al. 2021
;
Lopez-Piqueres et al. 2022
;
Lopez-Piqueres and Vasseur 2023
;
Panfil et al. 2023
;
Łebek et al. 2024
. Specifically, in the weak integrability breaking regime, the quasiparticle distribution
\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda,x,t)
evolves according to the GHD equation modified with a collision integral
\mathcal{I}[\rho_{\rm p}]
, given by
Friedman et al. 2020
;
Durnin et al. 2021

\partial_{t}\rho_{\rm p}+\partial_{x}\left(v\rho_{\rm p}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\left(\mathfrak{D}\partial_{x}\rho_{\rm p}\right)+\mathcal{I}[\rho_{\rm p}],

(1)

where
v
is the effective velocity and
\mathfrak{D}
represents the diffusion kernel
Bertini et al. 2016
;
De Nardis et al. 2018
;
De Nardis et al. 2018
. Evaluating the collision integral
\mathcal{I}[\rho_{\rm p}]
involves, either applying Fermi’s Golden Rule to the matrix elements of the integrability-breaking term
Friedman et al. 2020
;
Durnin et al. 2021
or using the gBBGKY hierarchy
Biagetti et al. 2025
.

In this work, rather than computing the collision integral directly from a given integrability breaking perturbation of the system’s Hamiltonian, we adopt an approximate form under the relaxation time approximation (RTA), as introduced in the GHD context in
Lopez-Piqueres et al. 2021
:

\mathcal{I}_{\rm RTA}[\rho_{\rm p}]=\frac{\rho^{\rm th}_{\rm p}-\rho_{\rm p}}{\tau}.

(2)

The fundamental assumption of RTA is that the quasiparticle distribution relaxes over a single characteristic timescale
\tau
.
Remarkably, this assumption has been found to work well in reproducing the numerical time evolution in nearly-integrable XXZ model
Lopez-Piqueres et al. 2021
.

Moreover, the RTA preserves the standard (conventional) conservation laws, making it particularly well-suited for studying hydrodynamic behavior. Consequently, one can argue that the RTA is capable of capturing the essential features of the crossover from generalized to conventional hydrodynamics—one of the central phenomena explored in this work.

It is expected that at short timescales (
t\ll\tau
), the effect of the collision integral on the dynamics is negligible and the system evolves according to GHD. In contrast, at long timescales (
t\gg\tau
), the dynamics is completely described by conventional hydrodynamics where only the conserved quantities respected by the collision integral play a role. If the collision integral destroys all conserved quantities except for the particle number, momentum and energy, the long-time dynamics is governed by the universal Navier-Stokes (NS) equations which are nothing but the continuity equations for the density, momentum and the energy fields
Balescu 1975
;
Résibois and De Leener 1977
;
Dorfman et al. 2021
;
Łebek and Panfil 2025
;
Łebek and Panfil 2025
.

In this work we present a detailed analysis of the crossover between the GHD and the conventional hydrodynamics for nearly integrable quantum gases within the RTA. We start with evaluating the transport coefficients for the Navier-Stokes equations. This amounts to solving certain integral equations that depend on the collision integral. For the RTA collision integral, as we show, they admit analytical solutions and are expressed in terms of the Drude weights and other thermodynamic quantities computed for integrable theory. This is discussed in Section
III
and provides us with precise predictions of the conventional hydrodynamics. In the following Section
IV
, we consider the full GHD dynamics and show that the conventional hydrodynamics emerges at late times and identify relevant space and time scales for the crossover. Here, we focus on a linear regime where initially homogeneous system is perturbed locally in space. This gives us an analytical control on the dynamics from which the emergence of the conventional hydrodynamics can be derived and understood. This allows us also
to identify the relevant degrees of freedom which separate into the three standard conservation laws and the decaying modes. In the final Section
V
, we apply our framework to study of the hydrodynamic correlation functions, in which a similar crossover between and GHD and conventional hydrodynamics is identified. Some of the technical detail of our work are to be found in the Appendices.

II
Preliminaries

We provide here a short overview of GHD and introduce ingredients needed in the later parts. Nearly integrable systems under the RTA can be described by GHD-Boltzmann equation of the form
Friedman et al. 2020
;
Durnin et al. 2021
;
Panfil et al. 2023

\partial_{t}\rho_{\rm p}+\partial_{x}\left(v_{\rho}\rho_{\rm p}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\left(\mathfrak{D}_{\rho}\partial_{x}\rho_{\rm p}\right)+\mathcal{I}_{\rm RTA}[\rho_{\rm p}].

(3)

The subscript
\rho
in the effective velocity
v_{\rho}
and the diffusion kernel
\mathfrak{D}_{\rho}
indicates that both are functionals of the quasiparticle distribution
\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda,x,t)
and therefore depend on space and time. However, for notational simplicity, we have omitted the subscript
\rho
in (
1
) and will continue to do so hereafter. We consider Galilean-invariant models of particles with unit mass. While in this work we focus on particles with fermionic statistics having in mind the Lieb-Liniger model, all the results can be easily generalized to arbitrary statistics by the means of changing statistical factor defined below and adjusting the expression for the free energy in terms of
\rho_{\rm p}
. For such generalization, we refer to
Doyon 2020
. In what follows we introduce the necessary ingredients of Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz (TBA) and GHD, which are needed to understand the structure of GHD-Boltzmann equation.

Setting the right-hand side of (
3
) to zero corresponds to Euler GHD which is time-reversible and describes purely ballistic transport. For a quasiparticle with rapidity
\lambda
, the effective velocity
v(\lambda,x,t)
refers to its group velocity, renormalized due to interactions with other quasiparticles inside the local state at position
x
and time
t
. It can be obtained by solving the following linear integral equation
Castro-Alvaredo et al. 2016
;
Doyon 2020
:

\displaystyle v(\lambda)=\lambda+2\pi\int{\rm d}\lambda^{\prime}\mathcal{T}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda^{\prime})\left[v(\lambda^{\prime})-v(\lambda)\right].

(4)

The two-body scattering kernel
\mathcal{T}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})
in (
4
) encodes the scattering shift due to a collision between two quasiparticles with rapidity
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
. The function
\mathcal{T}(\lambda)
explicitly depends on the underlying microscopic interactions and is therefore model-dependent. In the case of the Lieb-Liniger model,
\mathcal{T}
takes a particularly simple form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{2c}{c^{2}+\lambda^{2}},

(5)

where
c
denotes the strength of the
\delta
-function interaction potential
Lieb and Liniger 1963
;
Yang and Yang 1969
.

In addition to the Euler-scale GHD, a diffusion term appears in
the large scale dynamics of interacting integrable systems
De Nardis et al. 2018
;
Gopalakrishnan et al. 2018
;
Nardis et al. 2019
. In such systems, diffusion originates from scatterings among quasiparticles, a feature absent in non-interacting integrable systems
Spohn 2018
. This diffusion term provides subleading corrections to the Euler-scale GHD and is responsible for positive entropy production as well as diffusive relaxation mechanisms
De Nardis et al. 2018
;
Panfil and Pawełczyk 2019
. The diffusion operator in (
3
) acts as
\partial_{x}\left[\mathfrak{D}\partial_{x}\rho_{\rm p}\right](\theta)=\int{\rm d}\alpha\partial_{x}\left[\mathfrak{D}(\theta,\alpha)\partial_{x}\rho_{\rm p}(\alpha)\right]
and
the diffusion kernel
\mathfrak{D}
is given by

\mathfrak{D}=(1-{n}\mathcal{T})^{-1}\rho^{-1}_{\rm tot}\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}\rho^{-1}_{\rm tot}(1-{n}\mathcal{T}),

(6)

with
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}(\lambda,\mu)=\delta(\lambda-\mu)w(\lambda)-W(\lambda,\mu)
. In our notation,
{n}(\lambda)
denotes the occupation function which is related to the rapidity distribution
\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda)
through
\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda)={n}(\lambda)\rho_{\rm tot}(\lambda)
,
where
\rho_{\rm tot}
is the total density of states. It satisfies the following integral equation
Doyon 2020

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm tot}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi}+\int{\rm d}\lambda^{\prime}\mathcal{T}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}){n}(\lambda^{\prime})\rho_{\rm tot}(\lambda^{\prime}).

(7)

The two auxiliary functions appearing in
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
are

\displaystyle W(\lambda,\mu)=\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda)f(\lambda)[\mathcal{T}^{\rm dr}(\lambda,\mu)]^{2}|v(\lambda)-v(\mu)|,

(8)

and
w(\mu)=\int{\rm d}\lambda W(\lambda,\mu)
.
The dressing operation applied to any function
g(\lambda)
is defined as

\displaystyle g^{\rm dr}(\lambda)=g(\lambda)+\int{\rm d}\lambda^{\prime}\mathcal{T}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}){n}(\lambda^{\prime})g^{\rm dr}(\lambda^{\prime}).

(9)

At this point, let us comment on the recent refinements
Hübner et al. 2025
in the theory of GHD at the diffusive scale. The picture of a single equation with diffusion kernel (
6
) is not correct in the presence of long-range correlations
Doyon et al. 2023
. Such correlations are dynamically generated in inhomogenous interacting integrable systems and one has to solve a system of two equations (for one- and two-point function) in order to properly describe the dynamics. In our case, we will always work in the linear regime, where the single equation with diffusion kernel
\mathfrak{D}
is valid
Doyon et al. 2023
.

Lastly, we discus the Boltzmann kernel in RTA. The specific form of the collision integral (
2
) ensures that the system locally thermalizes to the Gibbs state over relaxation timescale
\tau
. The state
\rho^{\rm th}_{\rm p}
in (
2
) is a nonlinear functional of
\rho_{\rm p}
, determined by requiring that its conserved charges

{q}^{\rm th}_{n}(x,t)=\int{\rm d}\lambda{h}_{n}(\lambda)\rho^{\rm th}_{\rm p}[\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda,x,t)],\,\,n=0,1,2,

(10)

locally coincide with those of the state
\rho_{\rm p}
. By

{h}_{n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}/n!,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

(11)

we denote single particle eigenvalues of the
ultralocal charges
.
In order to define thermal states we introduce more objects of TBA. In general, pseudoenergy
\epsilon(\lambda)
fulfills the following integral equation

\epsilon(\lambda)=\epsilon_{0}(\lambda)-\int{\rm d}\lambda^{\prime}\mathcal{T}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})\log\left(1+e^{-\epsilon(\lambda^{\prime})}\right),

(12)

where the bare pseudoenergy is given by

\epsilon_{0}(\lambda)=\sum_{n}\beta_{n}{h}_{n}(\lambda).

(13)

A thermal boosted state is characterized by the first three chemical potentials,
\beta_{0}
,
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
while the remaining (
n\geq 3
) are zero.
From the pseudoenergy we can get the occupation function as
{n}(\lambda)=1/(1+e^{\epsilon(\lambda)})
, which is then used to solve the integral equation (
7
) for
\rho_{\rm tot}
.
Once
\rho^{\rm th}_{\rm p}(\lambda)
is known, the expectation values of the conserved charges
{q}^{\rm th}_{n}(x,t)
can be computed using (
10
). For a state defined by the distribution
\rho_{\rm p}
, the corresponding local thermal state is specified by the chemical potentials
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
which are adjusted so that the charges
{q}^{\rm th}_{n}(x,t)
coincide with
{q}_{n}(x,t)
obtained from
\rho_{\rm p}
.

How the variation in chemical potentials changes the charges is encoded in the charge-susceptibility matrix
\mathcal{C}
, with kernel given as
Doyon 2020
;
Doyon and Spohn 2017a

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=(1-{n}\mathcal{T})^{-1}\rho_{\rm p}f(1-{n}\mathcal{T}).

(14)

Its matrix elements, in the basis of the ultralocal charges, are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{mn}=-\frac{\delta{q}_{m}}{\delta\beta_{n}}=\int{\rm d}\lambda\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda)f(\lambda){h}^{\rm dr}_{m}(\lambda){h}^{\rm dr}_{n}(\lambda).

(15)

We will also use the bra–ket notation for the matrix elements which makes the basis apparent,

\mathcal{C}_{mn}=\langle{h}_{m}|\mathcal{C}|{h}_{n}\rangle.

(16)

This is useful for introducing the hydrodynamic scalar product
\left(f|g\right)\equiv\langle f|\mathcal{C}|g\rangle
and the special basis
\{h_{n}\}
of
orthonormal charges
which diagonalize the hydrodynamic scalar product
(h_{m}|h_{n})=\delta_{mn}
. They are constructed via the Gram-Schmidt (GS) orthonormalization of the ultralocal charges
\{{h}_{n}(\lambda)\}
.
Similar to the charge-susceptibility matrix
\mathcal{C}
, one can define an analogous matrix
\mathcal{B}
that characterizes the response of the associated currents
\{{j}_{m}\}
to changes in the chemical potentials
\{\beta_{n}\}
. The matrix
\mathcal{B}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=(1-{n}\mathcal{T})^{-1}\rho_{\rm p}fv(1-{n}\mathcal{T}),

(17)

with elements in the basis of ultralocal charges

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{mn}=-\frac{\delta{j}_{m}}{\delta\beta_{n}}=\int{\rm d}\lambda\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda)f(\lambda)v(\lambda){h}^{\rm dr}_{m}(\lambda){h}^{\rm dr}_{n}(\lambda).

(18)

Finally, we are interested in the linearized GHD dynamics where
\rho_{\rm p}=\rho_{\rm p}^{\rm th}+\delta\rho_{\rm p}
. Here
\rho_{\rm p}^{\rm th}
is a homogeneous thermal state and
\delta\rho_{\rm p}
is a small perturbation of quasiparticle density on top of it. Linearizing Eq. (
1
) about the thermal state
\rho^{\rm th}_{\rm p}
, one gets

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta\rho_{\rm p}+\mathcal{A}_{\rm th}\partial_{x}\delta\rho_{\rm p}=\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{D}_{\rm th}\partial^{2}_{x}\delta\rho_{\rm p}+\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{I}}{\delta\rho_{\rm p}}\right)_{\rm th}\delta\rho_{\rm p},

(19)

where the operator
\mathcal{A}=(1-{n}\mathcal{T})^{-1}v_{\rho}(1-{n}\mathcal{T})

Doyon and Spohn 2017a
and the subscript ‘th’ indicates that these quantities are evaluated in the homogenous thermal state
\rho_{\rm p}^{\rm th}
.
It is convenient to express the resulting dynamics in terms of perturbations to the orthonormal charges,
\delta q_{n}(x,t)=\int{\rm d}\lambda h_{n}(\lambda)\delta\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda,x,t)
.
The equations are
Łebek and Panfil 2025

\partial_{t}\delta q_{n}+\sum_{m}A_{nm}\partial_{x}\delta q_{m}=\sum_{m}\mathcal{D}_{nm}\partial^{2}_{x}\delta q_{m}-\sum_{m}\Gamma_{nm}\delta q_{m}.

(20)

The matrices
A
,
\mathcal{D}
and
\Gamma
have the following elements,

\displaystyle A_{nm}=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}\lambda\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda)f(\lambda)v(\lambda)h^{\rm dr}_{n}(\lambda)h^{\rm dr}_{m}(\lambda),

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{nm}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}\lambda{\rm d}\mu h^{\rm dr}_{n}(\lambda)\rho^{-1}_{\rm tot}(\lambda)\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{\rm th}(\lambda,\mu),

(22)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times\rho^{-1}_{\rm tot}(\mu)\rho_{\rm p}(\mu)f(\mu)h^{\rm dr}_{m}(\mu)

(23)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{nm}=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}\lambda{\rm d}\mu h_{n}(\lambda)\Gamma(\lambda,\mu)h_{m}(\mu),

(24)

where the collisional operator
\Gamma
is obtained from the functional derivative of the collision integral with respect to the quasiparticle density and takes the form

\displaystyle\Gamma=-\frac{\delta\mathcal{I}}{\delta\rho_{\rm p}}\mathcal{C}.

(25)

As a last ingredient we introduce Drude weights which measures the ballistic, dissipationless part of transport in a system
Doyon and Spohn 2017a
. The matrix elements are given by (note that these are matrix elements in orthonormal basis)

D_{mn}=\int{\rm d}\lambda\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda)f(\lambda)v^{2}(\lambda)h_{m}^{\rm dr}(\lambda)h_{n}^{\rm dr}(\lambda).

(26)

We now turn to the analysis of transport coefficients in nearly integrable systems within the RTA.

III
Navier-Stokes equations and transport coefficients

The first three conserved charges
(n=0,1,2)
in the Galilean invariant model correspond to particle number, momentum and energy. If the collision integral respects these three conservation laws i.e.,

\int{\rm d}\lambda{h}_{n}(\lambda)\mathcal{I}(\rho_{\rm p})=0,\qquad n=0,1,2,

(27)

and breaks all the other then at large space-time scales the dynamics can be described by the Navier-Stokes equations
Łebek and Panfil 2025
;
Łebek and Panfil 2025
;
Balescu 1975
;
Résibois and De Leener 1977
;
Dorfman et al. 2021
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}(\varrho u),\quad\partial_{t}(\varrho u)=-\partial_{x}(\varrho u^{2}+\mathcal{P}),

(28)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho e)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}(u\varrho e+\mathcal{J})-\mathcal{P}\partial_{x}u.

The hydrodynamic fields are related to the local densities through the following relations
Łebek and Panfil 2025
:

\varrho={q}_{0},\qquad u=\frac{{q}_{1}}{{q}_{0}},\qquad e=\frac{{q}_{2}}{{q}_{0}}-\frac{{q}_{1}^{2}}{2{q}_{0}^{2}}.

(29)

The dynamic pressure
\mathcal{P}
and heat current
\mathcal{J}
are determined by the Newton’s law for a viscous fluid (with shear viscosity absent in one spatial dimension) and the Fourier’s law for the heat current:

\mathcal{P}=P-\zeta\partial_{x}u,\qquad\mathcal{J}=-\kappa\partial_{x}T.

(30)

Here,
P
denotes the standard thermodynamic pressure,
T
the local temperature and the two transport coefficients are viscosity
\zeta
and thermal conductivity
\kappa
. They receive additive contributions which can be identified as one coming from the GHD diffusion, hence due to integrable interactions, and the other from the collision integral, hence due to integrability breaking interactions,

\displaystyle\zeta=\zeta_{\mathfrak{D}}+\zeta_{\mathcal{I}},\qquad\kappa=\kappa_{\mathfrak{D}}+\kappa_{\mathcal{I}}.

(31)

In Ref.
Łebek and Panfil 2025
, the contributions
\zeta_{\mathfrak{D}}
and
\kappa_{\mathfrak{D}}
, which arise from the GHD diffusion and are independent of the details of the collision integral, have been thoroughly investigated. They have the following forms

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathfrak{D}}=\frac{1}{2T}\langle{h}_{1}|\mathfrak{D}\mathcal{C}|{h}_{1}\rangle,\qquad\kappa_{\mathfrak{D}}=\frac{1}{2T^{2}}\langle{h}_{2}|\mathfrak{D}\mathcal{C}|{h}_{2}\rangle.

(32)

On the other hand, the contributions
\kappa_{\mathcal{I}}
and
\zeta_{\mathcal{I}}
are due to the collision integral and depend on their precise structure. They capture dissipative effects of quasiparticle scattering and are given by

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{I}}=\langle{h}_{1}|\mathcal{B}|\phi_{\zeta}\rangle,\qquad\kappa_{\mathcal{I}}=\langle{h}_{2}|\mathcal{B}|\phi_{\kappa}\rangle.

(33)

The functions
\phi_{\zeta}
and
\phi_{\kappa}
are determined by integral equations involving the linearized collision operator

\displaystyle\Gamma|\phi_{\zeta,\kappa}\rangle=|\eta_{\zeta,\kappa}\rangle,

(34)

together with the relations
\langle h_{n}|\mathcal{C}|\phi_{\zeta,\kappa}\rangle=0
for
n=0,1,2
. The source terms
\eta_{\zeta,\kappa}
are given by
Łebek and Panfil 2025

\displaystyle\eta_{\zeta}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\varrho}{T}}\left[\vphantom{\left(h_{0}+\sqrt{\frac{c_{P}-c_{V}}{c_{V}}}h_{2}\right)}\mathcal{B}h_{1}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varrho\varkappa_{T}}}\mathcal{C}\left(h_{0}+\sqrt{\frac{c_{P}-c_{V}}{c_{V}}}h_{2}\right)\right],

(35)

\displaystyle\eta_{\kappa}=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\varrho c_{V}}}{T}\mathcal{B}\left(h_{2}-\sqrt{\frac{c_{P}-c_{V}}{c_{V}}}h_{0}\right).

(36)

Here,
\varkappa_{T}
represents the isothermal compressibility of the system while the specific heats at constant volume and constant pressure are denoted by
c_{V}
and
c_{P}
, respectively. All thermodynamic quantities are computed for the integrable model using TBA. The
\Gamma
operator can be diagonalised (it is real and symmetric),
\Gamma|\gamma_{\alpha}\rangle=\gamma_{\alpha}|\gamma_{\alpha}\rangle
and the solution to the equation (
34
) is

|\phi_{\zeta,\kappa}\rangle=\sum_{\gamma_{\beta}\neq 0}\gamma_{\beta}^{-1}\langle g_{\beta}|\eta_{\zeta,\kappa}\rangle|g_{\beta}\rangle.

(37)

As we shown in Appendix
A
, it is the orthonormal basis
\{h_{n}\}
that diagonalizes the RTA
\Gamma
operator. Consequently, it has three zero eigenvalues, corresponding to the three collision invariants, while all the other eigenvalues are equal
\tau^{-1}
, a feature specific to the RTA collision integral. This gives

|\phi_{\zeta,\kappa}\rangle=\tau\sum_{n\neq 0,1,2}\langle h_{n}|\eta_{\zeta,\kappa}\rangle|h_{n}\rangle,

(38)

with the relations
\langle h_{n}|\mathcal{C}|\phi_{\zeta,\kappa}\rangle=0
for
n=0,1,2
fulfilled. The formulas for the transport coefficients are then

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{n\neq 0,1,2}\langle{h}_{1}|\mathcal{B}|h_{n}\rangle\langle h_{n}|\eta_{\zeta}\rangle,

(39)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{n\neq 0,1,2}\langle{h}_{2}|\mathcal{B}|h_{n}\rangle\langle h_{n}|\eta_{\kappa}\rangle.

(40)

IV
GHD-NS crossover

In this section, we investigate how different quantities—both conserved and non-conserved evolve in a nearly integrable systems within the RTA framework. Specifically, we consider the following scenario: a small amount of charge, such as particles or energy, is deposited at a specific location and we observe how it spreads across the system over time.
Since transport phenomena are typically described by small deviations from local equilibrium, it is sufficient to consider linear perturbations around a homogeneous thermal background. This approximation allows for a systematic study of how local disturbances propagate and relax over time.

We begin by discussing the space-time evolution of the conserved charges as governed by the NS equations. Subsequently, we address both the conserved and non-conserved charges using the GHD-Boltzmann equation.

To set up the stage, we start with recalling the basic facts about Navier-Stokes equations in the linear regime, which will serve us as a reference point in the study of GHD-Boltzmann dynamics.

IV.1
Navier-Stokes hydrodynamics in linear regime

In what follows it will be convenient to change the degrees of freedom in NS equations (
28
) using the thermodynamic identity
Résibois and De
Leener 1977

\displaystyle\delta T=\frac{1}{c_{V}}\delta e-\frac{1}{c_{V}\varrho^{2}}\left[P-T\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_{\varrho}\right]\delta\varrho.

(44)

Our motivation here is to solve NS equations in the linear regime by considering perturbations around homogeneous thermal state.
The relevant equations are
Résibois and De Leener 1977

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta\varrho

\displaystyle=-\varrho\partial_{x}\delta u,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta u

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varrho}\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial\varrho}\right)_{T}\partial_{x}\delta\varrho-\frac{1}{\varrho}\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_{\varrho}\partial_{x}\delta T+\frac{\zeta}{\varrho}\partial^{2}_{x}\delta u,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta T

\displaystyle=-\frac{T}{\varrho c_{V}}\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_{\varrho}\partial_{x}\delta u+\frac{\kappa}{\varrho c_{V}}\partial^{2}_{x}\delta T.

(45)

They can be solved with Fourier-Laplace transform

\delta\varrho(x,t)=\int{\rm d}k\,e^{ikx-\Lambda^{\rm NS}(k)t}\delta\tilde{\varrho}(k,0),

(46)

and similarly for
\delta u,\delta T
, which turns them into
k
-dependent
3\times 3
eigenproblem.
To make the associated matrix symmetric, it is convenient to change the degrees of freedom to
\delta q_{0},\,\delta q_{1},\,\delta q_{2}
defined as follows
Łebek and Panfil 2025

\delta\varrho=\varrho\sqrt{T\varkappa_{T}}\delta q_{0},\;\;\;\delta u=\sqrt{T}{\varrho}\delta q_{1},\;\;\;\delta T=\frac{T}{\sqrt{\varrho c_{V}}}\delta q_{2}.

(47)

The eigenvalue problem then reads

\mathcal{M}^{\rm NS}_{nm}(k)\delta\tilde{q}_{m}(k)=\Lambda^{\rm NS}(k)\delta\tilde{q}_{n}(k),\quad n=0,1,2,

(48)

with summation over
m=0,1,2
is implied and with
\mathcal{M}^{\rm NS}(k)
given by

\mathcal{M}^{\rm NS}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varrho\varkappa_{T}}}k&0\\
i\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varrho\varkappa_{T}}}k&\frac{\zeta}{\varrho}k^{2}&i\sqrt{\frac{T}{\varrho^{2}c_{V}}}\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_{\varrho}k\\
0&i\sqrt{\frac{T}{\varrho^{2}c_{V}}}\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_{\varrho}k&\frac{\kappa}{\varrho c_{V}}k^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(49)

Let us denote by
R^{\rm NS}(k)
the matrix which diagonalizes the problem: (
R^{\rm NS})^{-1}(k)\mathcal{M}^{\rm NS}(k)R^{\rm NS}(k)=\Lambda^{\rm NS}(k)
.
We find two sound modes and a heat mode, with dispersion relations for small
k
given by

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\rm NS}_{\pm}(k)=

\displaystyle\pm iv_{s}k+\frac{1}{2\varrho}\left[\left(\frac{1}{c_{V}}-\frac{1}{c_{P}}\right)\kappa+\zeta\right]k^{2}+\mathcal{O}(k^{3}),

(50)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\rm NS}_{\rm th}(k)=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{\varrho c_{P}}k^{2}+\mathcal{O}(k^{3}),

where
v_{s}
denotes the speed of sound and can be derived from the thermodynamics of the system
Résibois and De Leener 1977
;
Łebek and Panfil 2025
. The corresponding eigenstates (sound modes and heat mode) are encoded in the matrix
R^{\rm NS}(k)
in
k\to 0
limit:

R^{\rm NS}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{c_{V}}{c_{P}}}&-1&\sqrt{\frac{c_{P}-c_{V}}{c_{P}}}\\
-\sqrt{\frac{c_{P}-c_{V}}{c_{P}}}&0&\sqrt{\frac{c_{V}}{c_{P}}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{c_{V}}{c_{P}}}&1&\sqrt{\frac{c_{P}-c_{V}}{c_{P}}}\end{pmatrix}.

(51)

Lastly, let us write the explicit dynamics of the fields
\delta\tilde{q}_{n}
. It is given by the following expression

\delta\tilde{q}_{n}(k,t)=\sum_{\sigma,p}(R^{\rm NS})^{-1}_{n\sigma}(k)e^{-\Lambda^{\rm NS}_{\sigma}(k)t}R^{\rm NS}_{\sigma p}(k)\delta\tilde{q}_{p}(k,0),

(52)

where the index
\sigma
runs over the eigenmmodes
\sigma=+,-,{\rm th}
, while the indices
n
and
p
run over
0,1,2,
labelling the charge perturbations.

IV.3
Dynamics of small perturbations

Let us now introduce the initial state into the discussion and analyze the main features of the dynamics given by (
55
). The vector
R_{mp}(k)\delta\tilde{q}_{p}(k,0)
, which enters the expression is a decomposition of the initial state in the eigenbasis of
\mathcal{M}(k)
. As we shall see, the emergence of the Navier-Stokes equations is driven by an interplay of two mechanisms. The first one is a
homogenisation
, namely the decay in time of large momentum modes with
k>k_{c}
. This occurs in the dynamics of all the charges. The second mechanism, that we call
thermalisation
, is a decay of charges broken by the collision integral and is important for the dynamics of the small momentum modes. To simplify the picture, we will first look at the dynamics of initial states which are localized in the momentum space such that
\{\delta\tilde{q}_{p}(k,0)\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}}=0
for
|k|
of order
k_{c}
and larger. After that we will look into dynamics of a generic initial state.

IV.3.1
Initial states with
k_{\rm ini}\ll k_{c}

For such large scale states we are deep on the left of crossover point in Fig.
2

(58)

and non-conserved

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{q}_{n}(k,t)\approx\sum_{\sigma,p}(R^{\rm NS})^{-1}_{n\sigma}(k)e^{-\Lambda^{\rm NS}_{\sigma}(k)t}R^{\rm NS}_{\sigma p}(k)\delta\tilde{q}_{p}(k,0).

(59)

Note that (

Therefore, it is clear that we recover NS hydrodynamics in GHD-Boltzmann equation.

Next, we consider the

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{q}_{n>2}(k,t)\approx e^{-t/\tau}\delta\tilde{q}_{n}(k,0).

(60)

IV.3.2
Generic initial states

We now move to the discussion of more general states, which are not necessarily localized in the region
|k|\ll k_{c}
and involve higher Fourier components. The components with

decay on timescales given by GHD dispersion relations. Even though such modes are typically thought of as long-lived, we have to be aware that this is the statement for asymptotically small

The dynamics (
59

k=0

V
Hydrodynamic correlation functions

In this section, we supplement the discussion of the dynamics by considering the problem of hydrodynamic correlation functions
De Nardis et al. 2022
. The central object of interest is the connected correlator of local charges

S_{o_{1},o_{2}}(x,t)=\langle o_{1}(x,t)o_{2}(0,0)\rangle_{c},

(61)

where
o_{1,2}
are densities of some local charges
O_{1,2}.
The initial value of correlation is given by
S_{o_{1},o_{2}}(x,0)=\delta(x)\langle f_{o_{1}}|\mathcal{C}|f_{o_{2}}\rangle

62

The correlator of local fields can be accessed through the standard reasoning of linear response and in our setup we will build on the solution (
55
) in order to get it. For convenience, let us consider the following expectation value

o(x)=\int{\rm d}\lambda f_{o}(\lambda)\rho_{\rm p}(x,\lambda)

(62)

giving the associated single particle eigenvalue. We assume that this quantity can be represented in the orthonormalized basis meaning that

f_{o}(\lambda)=\sum_{n}(h_{n}|f_{o})h_{n}(\lambda)\equiv\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}^{o}h_{n}(\lambda),

(63)

S_{o_{1},o_{2}}(x,t)=\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{i}^{o_{1}}\alpha_{j}^{o_{2}}S_{q_{i},q_{j}}(x,t).

In what follows, we will assume that the hydrodynamic state is described by space-dependent local GGE with density matrix given by

\rho_{\rm GGE}(t)\propto e^{-\int{\rm d}y\sum_{n}\beta_{n}(y,t)q_{n}(y,t)}.

(65)

For such states, the hydrodynamic correlation function can be computed as
Doyon 2018
;
Doyon 2020

S_{q_{i},q_{j}}(x,t)=-\frac{\delta q_{i}(x,t)}{\delta\beta_{j}(0,0)}.

(66)

To evaluate this, we can use the expression for charge dynamics given by
(
55
)
.
Using the definition of susceptibility matrix and the fact the we work in the basis, which is orthonormal with respect to inner produced induced by it, we get the following

S_{q_{i},q_{j}}(x,t)=\int{\rm d}k\,e^{ikx}\sum_{m}R^{-1}_{im}(k)e^{-\Lambda_{m}(k)t}R_{mj}(k).

(67)

Correlator

V.1
Regime

For small times

S_{q_{i},q_{j}}^{\rm GHD,E}(x,t)=\int{\rm d}\lambda\delta[x-v(\lambda)t]\rho_{\rm p}(\lambda)f(\lambda)h_{i}^{\rm dr}(\lambda)h_{j}^{\rm dr}(\lambda).

(68)

In principle, one can compute also the GHD correlation function on diffusive scale. However, it provides only a subleading corrections while introducing technical problems related to computing the exponential of diffusion operator
\mathfrak{D}
. In Fig.
4
we see that indeed in the small time regime the dynamics given by GHD-Boltzmann equation (

At such short timescales, effects of integrability breaking are still not visible, therefore there are no qualitative differences between correlators involving conserved (non-conserved) charges. The difference is visible at late times, to which we now turn.

V.2
Regime

At late times the integral in (

In fact, it approaches the well-known correlator of conventional fluid predicted by fluctuating hydrodynamics. There the hydrodynamic fields are random Gaussian variables with mean fixed by the deterministic dynamics and the variance controlled by the fluctuation-dissipation relation. Since dissipative processes are controlled by the transport coefficients, they set also the strength of the fluctuations. Thus, the transport coefficient are required to actually set up the fluctuating hydrodynamics. For the full exposition see
Spohn 2014
;
Mendl and Spohn 2014
or
Łebek and Panfil 2025
. Here we write for reference the (normalized) density-density correlator

\displaystyle S_{q_{0},q_{0}}^{\rm NS}

\displaystyle(x,t)=\frac{c_{P}-c_{V}}{c_{P}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi{D}_{\rm th}t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4{D}_{\rm th}t}}+

(69)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{V}}{2c_{P}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi{D}_{\pm}t}}\left(e^{-\frac{(x-v_{s}t)^{2}}{4{D}_{\pm}t}}+e^{\frac{{-(x+v_{s}t)^{2}}}{{4{D}_{\pm}t}}}\right).

Obviously a similar expressions can be obtained for

for

As discussed in
Łebek and Panfil 2025
, the diffusive form of correlators, such as (
69

This is the prediction of nonlinear fluctuating hydrodynamics, which incorporates nonlinear terms in the currents of NS equations and emphasizes their impact on correlation functions, which becomes dominant on large scales. In the present context, the nonlinearities become relevant at times of order
O(\tau^{2}/\tau_{\rm mic})
, where
\tau_{\rm mic}
is characteristic microscopic timescale of integrable system. Since GHD Boltzmann equation implicitly assumes
\tau\gg\tau_{\rm mic}
in order for quasiparticle description to hold, there is a parametrically large window, on which linear fluctuating hydrodynamics is expected to provide the correct description.

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we have addressed two aspects of hydrodynamics under weak integrability breaking. Firstly, we have presented a computation of transport coefficients: viscosity and thermal conductivity in relaxation time approximation for collision integral. Such approximation is motivated by the typically complicated structure of collision integrals stemming from FGR or gBBGKY. The complexity of such collision terms is due to the fact that three particle collisions are the leading thermalization processes in 1D Galilean systems, as opposed to two-particle scatterings in higher dimensions. Moreover, when the collision integral is not known, RTA can still be useful with timescale
\tau
treated as a phenomenological parameter. The transport coefficients obtained within the RTA can be expressed entirely in terms of the TBA thermodynamics and the Drude weights of the underlying integrable model.

Secondly, we took a close look at the crossover between GHD of infinite conserved charges and NS theory with particle number, momentum and energy being the only conserved quantities. The control parameters of this crossover are space and time scales, which we identified in this work as
k_{c}
defined in (
57
) and characteristic RTA timescale
\tau
. In the case of RTA,
\tau^{-1}
equals the gap of
\mathcal{I}_{\rm RTA}
. The explicit solution of dynamics in the linear regime allowed us to understand the behavior of charge densities. Depending on whether the considered charge is conserved by the perturbation or not, we observed a different dynamics. In the late time regime
t\gg\tau
the conserved charge densities obey NS dynamics and non-conserved quantities decay exponentially, irrespectively of the initial state. We also characterized the influence of initial state on the dynamics and described the process of homogenisation understood here as a decay of the modes, which are not governed by NS hydrodynamics.

Lastly, we looked at hydrodynamic two-point functions of charge densities. For small times, GHD behavior was found, whereas in the late time regime for conserved charges we found results consistent with linear fluctuating hydrodynamics build upon the NS equations. On the other hand, correlators involving non-conserved charges decay exponentially on timescale set by
\tau
. We also commented on the role of current nonlinearities for the correlation functions of conserved quantities. The effect of such terms is expelled to large space-time scales due to the proximity to integrable model assumed in this work.

Generalizing our RTA computations to other sets of conserved charges is a natural direction for future research. This was done already for the case of Galilean-invariant system with only particle number conservation in
Lisiak et al. 2026
. Thanks to its simplicity, the RTA collision integral can be also useful to study dynamics in non-linear Boltzmann equation, possibly with noise. Such studies could be useful to quantify the role of nonlinearities on various observables at the largest space-time scales.

Appendix A
Computation of the matrix elements
\Gamma_{nm}

In this section, within the relaxation time approximation, we present the computation of the eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenstates of the operator
\Gamma
appearing in (
25
) with elements given in (
24
). We first compute the functional derivative of the collision integral [given by (
2
)] with respect to the rapidity distribution,

\frac{\delta\mathcal{I}_{\rm RTA}[\rho_{\rm p}](\lambda)}{\delta\rho_{\rm p}(\mu)}=-\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\delta(\lambda-\mu)-\frac{\delta\rho^{\rm th}_{\rm p}(\lambda)}{\delta\rho_{\rm p}(\mu)}\right).

(70)

To compute the functional derivative of the thermal distribution
\rho^{\rm th}_{\rm p}
, we decompose it through variations of the charges:

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\rho^{\rm th}_{\rm p}(\lambda)}{\delta\rho_{\rm p}(\mu)}=\sum_{m,n=0}^{2}\underbrace{\frac{\delta\rho^{\rm th}_{\rm p}(\lambda)}{\delta\beta_{n}}}_{-\mathcal{C}{h}_{n}(\lambda)}\underbrace{\frac{\delta\beta_{n}}{\delta{q}_{m}}}_{-\mathcal{C}^{-1}_{nm}}\underbrace{\frac{\delta{q}_{m}}{\delta\rho_{\rm p}(\mu)}}_{{h}_{m}(\mu)},

(71)

where the derivatives are known (as indicated by the underbraces), see
Łebek and Panfil 2025
. Using this result, (
71
) simplifies

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{I}_{\rm RTA}[\rho_{\rm p}](\lambda)}{\delta\rho_{\rm p}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\left[\sum_{m,n=0}^{2}(\mathcal{C}{h}_{n})(\lambda)(\mathcal{C}^{-1})_{nm}{h}_{m}(\mu)-\delta(\lambda-\mu)\right].

The orthonormal charges are defined with respect to the hydrodynamic scalar product involving
\mathcal{C}
. This implies

\sum_{m,n=0}^{2}{h}_{n}(\lambda)(\mathcal{C}^{-1})_{nm}{h}_{m}(\mu)=\sum_{m=0}^{2}h_{m}(\lambda)h_{m}(\mu).

(72)

Thus, in the bra–ket notation the
\Gamma
operator in (
25
) can be written as

\Gamma=\frac{1}{\tau}\left(1-\sum_{m=0,1,2}\mathcal{C}|h_{m}\rangle\langle h_{m}|\right)\mathcal{C}.

(73)

It is clearly diagonal in the orthonormal basis with eigenvalue
0
for
n=0,1,2
and eigenvalue
\tau^{-1}
for
n>2
.

Appendix C
Limiting values of
\omega^{\rm coll}_{1}

It is important to emphasize that the Lieb-Liniger model in the
c\to 0
limit maps to free bosons while in the
c\to\infty
limit it maps to free fermions. In this section, we explicitly show that
\omega_{1}^{\rm coll}
as given in (

) vanishes both in the
c\to 0
and
c\to\infty
limit. Note that (
42
) can also be written as

\displaystyle\omega^{\rm coll}_{1}

\displaystyle=\tau\left[D_{11}-v^{2}_{s}\right],

(78)

since
v^{2}_{s}=A^{2}_{10}+A^{2}_{21}
where
v_{s}
denotes the speed of sound and can be expressed in terms of the known matrices:

\displaystyle v_{s}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\mathcal{B}_{10}}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{2}_{10}\mathcal{C}_{22}+\mathcal{B}^{2}_{21}\mathcal{C}_{00}-2\mathcal{B}_{10}\mathcal{B}_{20}\mathcal{C}_{21}}{\mathcal{C}_{22}\mathcal{C}_{00}-\mathcal{C}^{2}_{20}}}.

(79)

The matrix elements
\mathcal{B}_{mn}
and
\mathcal{C}_{mn}
can be found from (
15
) and (
18
), respectively.
The matrix element
D_{11}
in (
78
) denotes the momentum-momentum Drude weight given in (
26
).
Now, we show that
D_{11}
is exactly equal to
v^{2}_{s}
both in the
c\to 0
and
c\to\infty
limits. We start with the
c\to\infty
limit.

C.1
c\to\infty
limit

The scattering kernel,
\mathcal{T}
in (
5
) vanishes in the
c\to\infty
limit. Consequently, any dressed function
g^{\rm dr}(\lambda)
in (
9
), can be replaced by their original counterparts
g(\lambda)
. As a result all the matrix elements
\mathcal{B}_{mn}
and
D_{mn}
can be expressed in terms of the charge-susceptibility matrix
\mathcal{C}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{10}=2\mathcal{C}_{20},\;\mathcal{B}_{21}=2\mathcal{C}_{22}~\text{and}~D_{11}=2\frac{\mathcal{C}_{22}}{\mathcal{C}_{20}}.

(80)

We compute
v^{2}_{s}
from (
79
) using the relations in (
80
)

\displaystyle v^{2}_{s}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mathcal{C}_{20}}\frac{4\mathcal{C}^{2}_{20}\mathcal{C}_{22}+4\mathcal{C}^{2}_{22}\mathcal{C}_{00}-8\mathcal{C}^{2}_{20}\mathcal{C}_{22}}{\mathcal{C}_{22}\mathcal{C}_{00}-\mathcal{C}^{2}_{20}}=2\frac{\mathcal{C}_{22}}{\mathcal{C}_{20}}=D_{11}.

(81)

Therefore, the collisional component of the viscosity vanishes exactly in the limit
c\to\infty

C.2

In the

\displaystyle\epsilon(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}-\mu-\log(1+e^{-\epsilon(\lambda)}),

\displaystyle\epsilon(\lambda)=\log\left(e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}-\mu}-1\right).

(84)

The charge susceptibility matrix elements [in (

The statistical factor
