Internal structures in the category of right-preordered groups
Ony Aubril

Abstract
We show that the category of (right-)preordered groups is a quasivariety of universal algebras by giving explicit axioms. We then look at lattices of effective equivalence relations, which turn out to be similar to the lattices of equivalence relations in the category of groups. We study internal structures in the category of right-preordered groups, and we especially consider the class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} of Schreier split epimorphisms. The category of right-preordered groups turns out to be action representable when we restrict our attention to split epimorphisms in 𝒮 \mathcal{S} . Relatively to this class of split epimorphisms, we define the notion of 𝒮 − \mathcal{S}\mathchar 45\relax precrossed modules, and then of 𝒮 − \mathcal{S}\mathchar 45\relax crossed modules that correspond exactly to Schreier internal reflexive graphs and Schreier internal categories, respectively. Lastly, we characterize groupoids among Schreier internal categories and give some examples.

The quasivariety of (right-)preordered groups

2.1
Algebraic theory of right-preordered groups

Without giving an explicit axiomatization, M.M. Clementino and A. Montoli proved that both categories
PreOGrp
and
RPreOGrp
are finitary quasivarieties of universal algebra (
[
8
]
, theorem A.2 and remark A.3.). We give an example of explicit axiomatization.

First, we focus on right-preordered groups. To axiomatize this quasivariety, we consider a right-preordered group as a set
G\times P_{G}
, together with a monoid structure
((0_{G},0_{P_{G}}),+)
. To reflect the cartesian product of the set, we ask the set of operations to contain two projections
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
to respectively
G\times\{0_{P_{G}}\}
and
\{0_{G}\}\times P_{G}
. Both
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
are monoid morphisms, so we recover a monoid structure on both
\mathfrak{p}_{0}(G\times P_{G})
and
\mathfrak{p}_{1}(G\times P_{G})
. To get a group structure over
\mathfrak{p}_{0}(G\times P_{G})
we add an operation
-
that acts as the inverse operation over
\mathfrak{p}_{0}(G\times P_{G})
and is "somehow orthogonal" to
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
. Lastly, we ask for an injection
\mathfrak{i}
from
\mathfrak{p}_{1}(G\times P_{G})
to
\mathfrak{p}_{0}(G\times P_{G})
that is also a monoid morphism. Asking
\mathfrak{i}
to be injective is actually the only quasi-identity that we get in this axiomatization.

More formally, we give the following generalized equational Horn theory. The signature
\Sigma
of the theory of this quasivariety consists of one function of arity zero that we will write
0
, four unary operations
\mathfrak{i},\mathfrak{p}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{1},-
and one binary operation

. We will write

for

,
a-b
for
a+-b
,
a_{i}
for
\mathfrak{p}_{i}(a)

(i=1,2)
. We have the following axioms :

(M)

(0,+)
is the usual theory of monoids.
\mathfrak{p}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{i}
are monoid morphisms (
f(a)+f(b)=f(a+b)
,
f(0)=0
for
f\in\{\mathfrak{p}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{i}
}).

(P1)

a=a_{0}+a_{1}=a_{1}+a_{0}
.

(P2)

-(a_{1})=(\mathfrak{i}(a))_{1}=(a_{1})_{0}=(a_{0})_{1}=0
.

(P3)

\mathfrak{i}(a_{1})=(\mathfrak{i}(a))_{0}
.

(G)

.

(Inj)

\quad\mathfrak{i}(a)=\mathfrak{i}(b)\Rightarrow a_{1}=b_{1}
.

This set of quasi-equations will be written

The category of models of this theory in the category

will be written

2.2
Algebraic theory for preordered groups

The category of preordered groups is not a subquasivariety of the category of right-preordered groups, because the category of preordered groups is not closed under subobjects in the category of right-preordered groups. To get an example, just consider the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
over four elements. It is a preordered group if we consider the positive cone
\mathfrak{A}_{4}
, the alternating group over four elements.
\mathfrak{A}_{4}
is a normal subgroup thus a submonoid closed under
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
-conjugation. With this group we have a subobject whose group is still
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
but with positive cone
P=\{\text{Id},(12)(34)\}
. It is a submonoid since it even is a subgroup of order 2 of
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
, but it is not closed under conjugation in
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
because
(13)(12)(34)(13)=(32)(14)\not\in P
.

As a consequence, the axiomatization of preordered groups can not be done by adding identities and quasi-identities to the theory of right-preordered groups. We can still describe its theory by adding a unary operation expressed by
\rhd
that is "right-orthogonal" to
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
and such that
\mathfrak{i}\rhd
is the conjugation of
\mathfrak{i}\mathfrak{p}_{1}
by
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
. Once again formally this is two new axioms :

(C1)

\mathfrak{i}\rhd(a)=a_{0}+\mathfrak{i}(a_{1})-a_{0}
.

(C2)

(\rhd(a))_{0}=0
.

We write
\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma\cup\{\rhd\}
and
H^{\prime}=H\cup\{\textbf{(C1)},\textbf{(C2)}\}
so that the category of models in the category
Set
for all of the axioms of
(\Sigma,H)\mathchar 45\relax\textbf{Alg}
together with the operation
\rhd
and the axioms
(C1)
and
(C2)
will be written
(\Sigma^{\prime},H^{\prime})\mathchar 45\relax\textbf{Alg}
. If
X
is a model of
(\Sigma^{\prime},H^{\prime})\mathchar 45\relax\textbf{Alg}
, we will write
\rhd^{X}
to denote this new operation for
X
.

Corollary 2.2.1.

The category
(\Sigma^{\prime},H^{\prime})\mathchar 45\relax\textbf{Alg}
is equivalent to the category
PreOGrp
.

Proof.

We only need to check that

be objects in

\displaystyle=f(a_{0}+^{X}\mathfrak{i}^{X}(a_{1})-^{X}a_{0})

Given a right-preordered group

\displaystyle(N,N\cap P_{G})\lor(M,M\cap P_{G})=(N\cdot M,(N\cdot M)\cap P_{G})

\displaystyle(N,N\cap P_{G})\land(M,M\cap P_{G})=(N\cap M,N\cap M\cap P_{G}).

It was shown in

Consider the bijection

\displaystyle(N,P_{G}\cap N)\in\textbf{Norm}\left(G,P_{G}\right)\longleftrightarrow N\in\textbf{Norm}\left(G\right)

One direction is induced by the forgetful functor

and we get the inverse by taking the pullback of the inclusion of

5.1.4

We will describe in a similar way Schreier split extensions in the category

. Given a right-preordered group, we define

\cdot

,

\cdot

.

\bullet

is a morphism in

:

\cdot

,

\cdot

.

.

The next step will be to identify which reflexive graphs actually underlie an internal category in the category

Grp
