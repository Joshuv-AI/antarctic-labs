Cumulative Natural Direct and Indirect Effects for Causal Mediation Analysis
Yuta Kawakami, Jin Tian

Abstract
Causal mediation analysis provides a fundamental framework for quantifying the contributions of different pathways from a treatment X X to an outcome Y Y through a mediator. The natural direct and indirect effects (NDE and NIE) are widely used to decompose the total effect. In this paper, we observe that NDE and NIE can give rise to paradoxical interpretations due to their failure to satisfy two desirable properties of interpretable causal effects: skew-symmetry and additivity. To address these limitations, we introduce new measures of direct and indirect effects for continuous treatments, termed the cumulative natural direct and indirect effects (CNDE and CNIE), constructed by decomposing local causal effects 𝔼 ⁡ [ ∂ x Y x ] \mathbb{E}[\partial_{x}Y_{x}] into local direct and indirect effects. CNDE and CNIE yield a decomposition of the total effect that preserves both skew-symmetry and additivity. We further extend this framework to ordinal treatments by defining discrete analogues of the cumulative effects over ordered treatment levels that preserve these structural properties. We establish decomposition and identification results for the proposed measures under standard causal assumptions. We illustrate their behavior, in comparison with NDE and NIE, using canonical linear mediation models with interaction and a real-world dataset.

The total effect (TE),

satisfies two fundamental properties:

\displaystyle\text{\bf Skew-symmetry:}\quad\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)=-\text{TE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y),

(1)

\displaystyle\text{\bf Additivity:}\quad\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)=\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)+\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y),

In contrast, the NDE and NIE do not, in general, satisfy skew-symmetry or additivity. As a result, their decomposition of the total effect can exhibit unintuitive behavior. For example, reversing the treatment contrast may alter the relative contributions of direct and indirect effects, and decompositions over sub-intervals may not align with the decomposition over the full interval. Such phenomena can lead to what we term
paradoxical interpretations
in mediation analysis (see Section
3

To address these issues, in this paper, we introduce new measures of direct and indirect effects that preserve both skew-symmetry and additivity. We begin with the case of a continuous treatment
X

\mathbb{E}[\partial_{x}Y_{x}]

cumulative natural direct and indirect effects
(CNDE and CNIE). These measures yield a pathwise decomposition of the total effect that satisfies both skew-symmetry and additivity.

11

37

38

We further extend our framework to ordinal treatments. In this setting, we construct discrete analogues of the cumulative effects by aggregating local increments along ordered treatment levels. We consider a skew-symmetric version of NDE and NIE and propose direct and indirect effect formulations that additionally satisfy additivity.

For each type of proposed measures, we establish decomposition results showing that the total effect can be expressed as the sum of the proposed direct and indirect effects, and we provide identification results under standard causal assumptions.
We illustrate the behavior of these measures, in comparison with NDE and NIE, using canonical linear mediation models with interaction. Finally, we demonstrate their practical relevance through an application to a real-world dataset.

3
Mediation Paradox

In this section, we examine how the lack of skew-symmetry and additivity in the NDE and NIE can lead to paradoxical interpretations in mediation analysis.

As an illustrative example, we consider assessing the effect of cigarette smoking on heart disease mediated through hypertension
(
9
)
.
We let the amount of cigarettes per day be the treatment (
X
), a person’s blood pressure be the mediator (
M
), and the degree of heart disease be the outcome (
Y
).
Researchers try to answer the following question:

“Through which mechanism does smoking increase a person’s risk of heart disease?"

Specifically, researchers may want to know whether smoking cigarettes raises the risk of heart disease by raising a person’s blood pressure or whether smoking cigarettes raises the risk of heart disease directly.
Causal mediation analyses have empirically answered such questions by decomposing the total effect into the direct and indirect effects.

First, we illustrate how the lack of symmetry in NDE and NIE provokes paradoxes.
We consider
\mathcal{M}_{2}
in Example
2
. Suppose
\beta=0
,
\alpha=1
,
\gamma=0
, and
\delta=1
. Then
TE can be decomposed by Eq. (
12
) in two ways using NDE and NIE, obtained by flipping the treatment values
1
and
0
, as shown below:

\displaystyle\underbrace{1}_{\text{TE}(1,0;Y)}=\underbrace{0}_{\text{NDE}(1,0;Y)}-\underbrace{(-1)}_{\text{NIE}(0,1;Y)},\underbrace{-1}_{\text{TE}(0,1;Y)}=\underbrace{-1}_{\text{NDE}(0,1;Y)}-\underbrace{0}_{\text{NIE}(1,0;Y)}.

(13)

This represents a paradoxical situation because the decomposition of
\text{TE}(1,0;Y)
implies there is no direct effect, while the decomposition of
\text{TE}(0,1;Y)
implies there is no indirect effect.
Just by flipping the values of treatments
1
and
0
, the result of causal mediation analysis is completely overturned.
This could be unacceptable for most researchers and provoke severe problems in the interpretation of the results of the mediation analysis.

For the cigarette example, the above results state that

“The effect of increasing cigarettes on heart disease is completely via the effect of increasing blood pressure;
on the other hand, the effect of reducing cigarettes on heart disease is not via the effect of reducing blood pressure."

This seems to be a contradiction.

In general, because NDE and NIE do not satisfy skew-symmetry, the proportions of the direct and indirect effects in the total effect—NIE/TE is called the “proportion mediated” in
(
31
)
—will change when the reference point is flipped, that is,

\displaystyle\frac{\text{NDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}{\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}

\displaystyle\neq\frac{\text{NDE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}{\text{TE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)},\ \frac{\text{NIE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}{\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}\neq\frac{\text{NIE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}{\text{TE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}

(14)

This lack of invariance may lead to contradictory interpretations in mediation analysis.

Next, we explain how the lack of additivity also induces paradoxical situations.

Example 3
.

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\{\beta(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+\alpha\delta(x^{\prime\prime 2}-x^{\prime 2})x^{\prime}\}}_{\text{NDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}-\underbrace{\{\alpha\gamma(x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\alpha\delta x^{\prime\prime 2}(x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})\}}_{\text{NIE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}.

Now supposing

\alpha=1

\text{TE}(1,-1;Y)

\displaystyle\underbrace{2}_{\text{TE}(1,-1;Y)}=\underbrace{0}_{\text{NDE}(1,-1;Y)}-\underbrace{(-2)}_{\text{NIE}(-1,1;Y)}.

Meanwhile,
\text{TE}(1,0;Y)
and

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\underbrace{1}_{\text{TE}(1,0;Y)}=\underbrace{0}_{\text{NDE}(1,0;Y)}-\underbrace{(-1)}_{\text{NIE}(0,1;Y)},\underbrace{1}_{\text{TE}(0,-1;Y)}=\underbrace{1}_{\text{NDE}(0,-1;Y)}-\underbrace{0}_{\text{NIE}(-1,0;Y)}.

(18)

The above results appear paradoxical:
\text{TE}(1,0;Y)
is decomposed entirely into indirect effects, and
\text{TE}(0,-1;Y)
is decomposed entirely into direct effects; whereas their sum,
\text{TE}(1,-1;Y)

For the cigarette example, letting
X=0
be a reference of the amount of cigarettes, the above results state that

“The effect of increasing cigarette consumption by one unit above the reference level is entirely mediated through increased blood pressure. In contrast, the effect of increasing cigarette consumption by one unit from below the reference level to the reference level is entirely direct and not mediated through blood pressure. However, the effect of increasing cigarette consumption by two units across the reference level is again entirely mediated through increased blood pressure."

This interpretation also appears contradictory, as the mediation mechanism changes dramatically depending on how the treatment contrast is defined.

These paradoxes could be unacceptable for most researchers, and it would be desirable to avoid such paradoxical decompositions of TE into NDE and NIE.
In this paper, we propose new measures of direct and indirect effects that avoid these paradoxes.

Under SCM
\mathcal{M}_{N}
and Assumption
2
, we define

1.

Local natural direct effect (LNDE):

\text{LNDE}(x;Y)\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\partial_{x^{*}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{x^{*},{M}_{x}}]|_{x^{*}=x}

2.

Local natural indirect effect (LNIE):

\text{LNIE}(x;Y)\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\partial_{x^{*}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{x,{M}_{x^{*}}}]|_{x^{*}=x}

3.

Local controlled direct effect (LCDE):

\text{LCDE}(x,m;Y)\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\partial_{x}\mathbb{E}[Y_{x,m}]

for any
x\in\Omega_{X}
and
m\in\Omega_{M}
.

In Def.
4
,\partial_{x^{*}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{x^{*},{M}_{x}}]
represents

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}[Y_{x^{*}+h,{M}_{x}}]-\mathbb{E}[Y_{x^{*},{M}_{x}}]}{h}
and
\partial_{x^{*}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{x,{M}_{x^{*}}}]
represents
\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}[Y_{x,{M}_{x^{*}+h}}]-\mathbb{E}[Y_{x,{M}_{x^{*}}}]}{h}
.
LNDE is the slope of the expectation of the counterfactual
Y_{x^{*},{M}_{x}}
w.r.t.
x^{*}
at
x^{*}=x
, which represents the infinitesimal effect of the treatment at
X=x
while holding the mediator
M
at its natural level under
X=x
.
LNIE is the slope of the expectation of the counterfactual
Y_{x,{M}_{x^{*}}}
w.r.t.
x^{*}
at
x^{*}=x
, which represents the influence of the treatment at
X=x
through
M_{x}
, while keeping the treatment itself fixed to
X=x
.
LCDE is the slope of the expectation of the counterfactual
Y_{x,m}
w.r.t.
x
.
It depends on
m
and does not have a parallel indirect effect.

We have the following important relationship that the LTE is additively decomposed into LNDE and LNIE:

Lemma 1
.

[Decomposition of LTE by LNDE and LNIE]

Under SCM
\mathcal{M}_{N}
and Assumption
2
,

\text{LTE}(x;Y)=\text{LNDE}(x;Y)+\text{LNIE}(x;Y)

(19)

for any
x\in\Omega_{X}
.

Next, we study the identifiability of LNDE and LNIE.
They can be identified through identifying
\mathbb{E}[Y_{x,{M}_{x^{*}}}]
via Proposition
1
. Once
\mathbb{E}[Y_{x,{M}_{x^{*}}}]
is identified, LNDE and LNIE are given by
\partial_{x^{*}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{x^{*},{M}_{x}}]|_{x^{*}=x}
and
\partial_{x^{*}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{x,{M}_{x^{*}}}]|_{x^{*}=x}
, respectively.
We next provide explicit expressions to facilitate estimation.

Theorem 1
.

[Identification of LNDE and LNIE]

Under SCM
\mathcal{M}_{N}
and Assumptions
1
and
2
,
if the following regularity condition holds: either
(i)
\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x,M=m]
is bounded and
M
has bounded support; or
(ii)
\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x,M=m]
grows at most polynomially in
m
, and the exogenous error
\varepsilon_{M}
in
\mathcal{M}_{N}
has a continuously differentiable density
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(m)
is integrable and
\mathfrak{p}(m)\to 0

as
|m|\to\infty
faster than any polynomial (e.g., Gaussian),
then LNDE and LNIE are identifiable for any
x\in\Omega_{X}
by

\displaystyle\text{LNDE}(x;Y)=\int_{\Omega_{M}}\partial_{x}\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x,M=m]\mathfrak{p}(M=m|X=x)d{m},

(20)

\displaystyle\text{LNIE}(x;Y)=\int_{\Omega_{M}}\partial_{m}\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x,M=m]\partial_{x}\mathbb{P}(M>m|X=x)dm.

(21)

The regularity condition in Theorem
1
ensures the validity of the integration-by-parts argument used in the proof.

4.2
Decomposition of TE via decomposing LTE

Next, we introduce new measures of direct and indirect effects that satisfy skew-symmetry and additivity through LNDE and LNIE.

Definition 5

(CNDE and CNIE)
.

Under SCM
\mathcal{M}_{N}
and Assumption
2
,
we define the cumulative natural direct and indirect effects (CNDE and CNIE) over the interval
[x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}]
as the integral of the corresponding local effects:

\displaystyle\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\int_{x^{\prime}}^{x^{\prime\prime}}\text{LNDE}(x;Y)dx,

(22)

\displaystyle\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\int_{x^{\prime}}^{x^{\prime\prime}}\text{LNIE}(x;Y)dx

for any
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in\Omega_{X}
.

CNDE and CNIE capture the accumulated direct and indirect effects along the treatment path from
x^{\prime}
to
x^{\prime\prime}
, respectively.
Note the distinction from NDE and NIE, which, under Assumption
2
, can be expressed as

\displaystyle\text{NDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{x^{\prime}}^{x^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{x^{*}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{{x^{*}},M_{x^{\prime}}}]d{x^{*}}\right),

(23)

\displaystyle\text{NIE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{x^{\prime}}^{x^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{x^{*}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{x^{\prime},M_{x^{*}}}]dx^{*}\right).

(24)

The key distinction lies in how the effects are accumulated along the interval. The CNDE and CNIE integrate
local infinitesimal effects
evaluated along the treatment path, so that both the treatment level and the reference mediator value evolve with
x
. This yields a pathwise decomposition that aggregates infinitesimal direct and indirect effects as the treatment moves from
x^{\prime}
to
x^{\prime\prime}
.
In contrast, the NDE and NIE are constructed by integrating derivatives while holding one argument fixed at an endpoint (either
x^{\prime}
or
x^{\prime\prime}
), so the accumulation is taken
relative to a fixed reference regime
rather than along the evolving path.

CNDE and CNIE satisfy both skew-symmetry and additivity:

Theorem 2
.

[Skew-symmetry and additivity of CNDE and CNIE]

Under SCM
\mathcal{M}_{N}
and Assumption
2
, CNDE and CNIE satisfy both skew-symmetry and additivity.

We can easily extend additivity for arbitrary numbers of treatments, e.g.,

\displaystyle\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime\prime};Y)+\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)+\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

(25)

\displaystyle\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime\prime};Y)+\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)+\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

for any
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}\in\Omega_{X}
.

Furthermore, we have the following decomposition theorem:

Theorem 3
.

[Decomposition of TE into CNDE and CNIE]

Under SCM
\mathcal{M}_{N}
and Assumption
2
,

\displaystyle\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)-\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)

(26)

\displaystyle=\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)+\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

(27)

for any
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in\Omega_{X}
.

We note that the additive decomposition of TE into NDE and NIE only holds for the linear no-interaction model. In contrast, the additive decomposition of TE into CNDE and CNIE in Eq. (
27
) holds for any nonparametric model.
In addition, since CNDE and CNIE satisfy skew-symmetry, the proportion of direct
or indirect effect in the total effect does not change when
x^{\prime\prime}
and
x^{\prime}
are flipped

\displaystyle\frac{\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}{\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}=\frac{\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}{\text{TE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)},\ \ \frac{\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}{\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}=\frac{\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}{\text{TE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}

(28)

Finally, by Theorem
1
, we can identify CNDE and CNIE as follows.

Theorem 4
.

[Identification of CNDE and CNIE]

Under SCM
\mathcal{M}_{N}
and Assumptions
1
and
2
,
for any
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in\Omega_{X}
,
CNDE and CNIE are identifiable by

\displaystyle\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)=\int_{x^{\prime}}^{x^{\prime\prime}}\int_{\Omega_{M}}\partial_{x}\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x,M=m]\mathfrak{p}(M=m|X=x)d{m}dx,

(29)

\displaystyle\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)=\int_{x^{\prime}}^{x^{\prime\prime}}\int_{\Omega_{M}}\partial_{m}\mathbb{E}[Y|X=x,M=m]\partial_{x}\mathbb{P}(M>m|X=x)dmdx.

(30)

Remark: Covariate Adjustment.

Assumption
1
may be overly restrictive for observational studies. In Appendix
B
, we provide identification results that incorporate observed confounders
W
.

4.3
Revisiting Popular Mediation Models

Next, we revisit the three specific models and illustrate how TE is decomposed into CNDE and CNIE.

Example 1’
.

Under the linear SCM
\mathcal{M}_{1}
in (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\text{LTE}(x;Y)=\beta+\alpha\gamma,\ \ \text{LNDE}(x;Y)=\beta,\ \ \text{LNIE}(x;Y)=\alpha\gamma.

(31)

TE is decomposed into CNDE and CNIE as below:

\displaystyle\underbrace{(\beta+\alpha\gamma)(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})}_{\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}=\underbrace{\beta(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})}_{\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}-\underbrace{\alpha\gamma(x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})}_{\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}.

(32)

We have that CNDE and CNIE coincide with NDE and NIE, respectively.

Example 2’
.

Consider the SCM
\mathcal{M}_{2}
in (
11
).
We have

\displaystyle\text{LTE}(x;Y)=\beta+\alpha\gamma+2\alpha\delta x,\ \text{LNDE}(x;Y)=\beta+\alpha\delta x,\ \text{LNIE}(x;Y)=\alpha\gamma+\alpha\delta x.

(33)

The decomposition of TE into CNDE and CNIE becomes

\displaystyle\underbrace{(\beta+\alpha\gamma)(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+\alpha\delta(x^{\prime\prime 2}-x^{\prime 2})}_{\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}

(34)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\{\beta(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+0.5\alpha\delta(x^{\prime\prime 2}-x^{\prime 2})\}}_{\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}-\underbrace{\{\alpha\gamma(x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+0.5\alpha\delta(x^{\prime 2}-x^{\prime\prime 2})\}}_{\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\{\beta(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+0.5\alpha\delta(x^{\prime\prime 2}-x^{\prime 2})\}}_{\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}+\underbrace{\{\alpha\gamma(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+0.5\alpha\delta(x^{\prime\prime 2}-x^{\prime 2})\}}_{\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}.

This decomposition differs from the corresponding decomposition into NDE and NIE in Eq. (
12
).
Concretely, supposing
\beta=0
,
\alpha=1
,
\gamma=0
, and
\delta=1
, then
\text{TE}(1,0;Y)
is decomposed by

\displaystyle\underbrace{1}_{\text{TE}(1,0;Y)}=\underbrace{0.5}_{\text{CNDE}(1,0;Y)}-\underbrace{(-0.5)}_{\text{CNIE}(0,1;Y)}=\underbrace{0.5}_{\text{CNDE}(1,0;Y)}+\underbrace{0.5}_{\text{CNIE}(1,0;Y)}.

(35)

Meanwhile,
\text{TE}(0,1;Y)
is decomposed by

\displaystyle\underbrace{-1}_{\text{TE}(0,1;Y)}=\underbrace{-0.5}_{\text{CNDE}(0,1;Y)}-\underbrace{0.5}_{\text{CNIE}(1,0;Y)}=\underbrace{-0.5}_{\text{CNDE}(0,1;Y)}+\underbrace{(-0.5)}_{\text{CNIE}(0,1;Y)}.

(36)

For the cigarette example, the above results say that

“Half of the effect of increasing cigarette consumption by one unit from the reference level is via the effect of increasing blood pressure; also, similarly, half of the effect of decreasing cigarette consumption by one unit from the reference level is via decreasing blood pressure."

In contrast to the conventional decomposition into NDE and NIE in Eq. (
13
), the decomposition into CNDE and CNIE does not exhibit paradoxical phenomena.

Example 3’
.

Consider the SCM
\mathcal{M}_{3}
in (
15
).
We have

\displaystyle\text{LTE}(x;Y)=\beta+\alpha\gamma+3\alpha\delta x^{2},\ \text{LNDE}(x;Y)=\beta+2\alpha\delta x^{2},\ \text{LNIE}(x;Y)=\alpha\gamma+\alpha\delta x^{2}.

(37)

The decomposition of TE into CNDE and CNIE becomes

\displaystyle\underbrace{(\beta+\alpha\gamma)(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+\alpha\delta(x^{\prime\prime 3}-x^{\prime 3})}_{\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}

(38)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left\{\beta(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+\frac{2}{3}\alpha\delta(x^{\prime\prime 3}-x^{\prime 3})\right\}}_{\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}-\underbrace{\left\{\alpha\gamma(x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime})+\frac{1}{3}\alpha\delta(x^{\prime 3}-x^{\prime\prime 3})\right\}}_{\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left\{\beta(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+\frac{2}{3}\alpha\delta(x^{\prime\prime 3}-x^{\prime 3})\right\}}_{\text{CNDE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}+\underbrace{\left\{\alpha\gamma(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime})+\frac{1}{3}\alpha\delta(x^{\prime\prime 3}-x^{\prime 3})\right\}}_{\text{CNIE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)}.

This decomposition differs from the corresponding decomposition into NDE and NIE in Eq. (
16
).
Concretely, supposing
\beta=0
,
\alpha=1
,
\gamma=0
, and
\delta=1
, then

\text{TE}(1,-1;Y)
is decomposed by

\displaystyle\underbrace{2}_{\text{TE}(1,-1;Y)}=\underbrace{4/3}_{\text{CNDE}(1,-1;Y)}-\underbrace{(-2/3)}_{\text{CNIE}(-1,1;Y)}=\underbrace{4/3}_{\text{CNDE}(1,-1;Y)}+\underbrace{2/3}_{\text{CNIE}(1,-1;Y)}.

(39)

Meanwhile,
\text{TE}(1,0;Y)
and
\text{TE}(0,-1;Y)
are decomposed by

\displaystyle\underbrace{1}_{\text{TE}(1,0;Y)}=\underbrace{{2}/{3}}_{\text{CNDE}(1,0;Y)}-\underbrace{\left(-{1}/{3}\right)}_{\text{CNIE}(0,1;Y)}=\underbrace{{2}/{3}}_{\text{CNDE}(1,0;Y)}+\underbrace{{1}/{3}}_{\text{CNIE}(1,0;Y)},

(40)

\displaystyle\underbrace{1}_{\text{TE}(0,-1;Y)}=\underbrace{{2}/{3}}_{\text{CNDE}(0,-1;Y)}-\underbrace{\left(-{1}/{3}\right)}_{\text{CNIE}(-1,0;Y)}=\underbrace{{2}/{3}}_{\text{CNDE}(0,-1;Y)}+\underbrace{{1}/{3}}_{\text{CNIE}(0,-1;Y)}.

In contrast to the decomposition into NDE and NIE in Eqs. (
17
) and (
18
), the decomposition into CNDE and CNIE does not exhibit paradoxical phenomena as they satisfy additivity:

\displaystyle\text{CNDE}(1,-1;Y)=\text{CNDE}(1,0;Y)+\text{CNDE}(0,-1;Y),

(41)

\displaystyle\text{CNIE}(1,-1;Y)=\text{CNIE}(1,0;Y)+\text{CNIE}(0,-1;Y).

(42)

For the cigarette example, the decompositions in (
39
) and (
40
) imply that

“One-third of the effect of each of the three treatment contrasts, an increase of one unit above the reference level, an increase of one unit from below the reference level to the reference level, and an increase of two units across the reference level, is mediated through increased blood pressure, while the remaining two-thirds operates directly.”

We observe that our proposed decomposition of TE into CNDE and CNIE avoids the paradoxes exhibited by the decomposition into NDE and NIE discussed in Section
3
.

5
Direct and Indirect Effects for Ordinal Treatment

When the treatment
X
is a discrete variable, our definitions of CNDE and CNIE are not applicable because the derivative is not defined in such cases. The natural counterparts of CNDE and CNIE can be defined using finite differences rather than integrals.

5.1
CNDE and CNIE for ordinal treatment

If
X
is ordinal taking values in
\{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{K}\}
, the analogue of CNDE/CNIE can be expressed as sums of local increments along a sequence connecting
x_{i}
to
x_{j}
. We define the counterparts of CNDE and CNIE for the ordinal
X
, denoted by CNDE-O and CNIE-O, as follows:

Definition 6

(CNDE and CNIE for ordinal
X
)
.

Let
X
be an ordinal taking values in
\Omega_{X}=\{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{K}\}
.
We define CNDE and CNIE for the ordinal
X
, CNDE-O and CNIE-O, as follows:

\displaystyle\text{CNDE-O}(x_{j},x_{i};Y)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\begin{cases}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\text{NDE}(x_{k+1},x_{k};Y)&\text{if }i<j\\
\sum_{k=j}^{i-1}\text{NDE}(x_{k},x_{k+1};Y)&\text{if }i>j\\
\end{cases},

(43)

\displaystyle\text{CNIE-O}(x_{j},x_{i};Y)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\begin{cases}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\text{NIE}(x_{k+1},x_{k};Y)&\text{if }i<j\\
\sum_{k=j}^{i-1}\text{NIE}(x_{k},x_{k+1};Y)&\text{if }i>j\\
\end{cases},

(44)

for any
x_{i},x_{j}\in\Omega_{X}
.

Thus, the discrete analogue replaces integration of local derivatives with summation of finite-step contrasts. In the case of
|i-j|=1
, this reduces to a single-step decomposition, making CNDE-O/CNIE-O coincide with NDE/NIE.

CNDE-O and CNIE-O do not satisfy skew-symmetry because NDE and NIE do not. CNDE-O and CNIE-O satisfy a restricted form of additivity as stated in the following:

Theorem 5
.

[Additivity of CNDE-O and CNIE-O]
Under SCM
\mathcal{M}_{N}
,
CNDE-O and CNIE-O satisfy:
if
x^{\prime}<x^{\prime\prime}<x^{\prime\prime\prime}
or
x^{\prime}>x^{\prime\prime}>x^{\prime\prime\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\text{CNDE-O}(x^{\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\text{CNDE-O}(x^{\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)+\text{CNDE-O}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y),

(45)

\displaystyle\text{CNIE-O}(x^{\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\text{CNIE-O}(x^{\prime\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)+\text{CNIE-O}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y).

(46)

We have the following decomposition theorem:

Theorem 6
.

[Decomposition of TE into CNDE-O and CNIE-O]
Under SCM
\mathcal{M}_{N}
,

\displaystyle\text{TE}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\text{CNDE-O}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)-\text{CNIE-O}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y).

(47)

Next, we revisit the three models
\mathcal{M}_{1}
,
\mathcal{M}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{3}
.

Example 1”
.

Under
\mathcal{M}_{1}
in (
8
), for
x^{\prime}=x_{i}
and

with

\text{NDE}(x_{k+1},x_{k};Y)=\beta(x_{k+1}-x_{k})

\displaystyle\text{CNDE-O}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\beta(x_{k+1}-x_{k})=\beta(x_{j}-x_{i})=\beta(x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}),

(48)

\displaystyle\text{CNIE-O}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\alpha\gamma(x_{k}-x_{k+1})=\alpha\gamma(x_{i}-x_{j})=\alpha\gamma(x^{\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}).

They coincide with NDE/CNDE and NIE/CNIE respectively.

\displaystyle\underbrace{2}_{\text{TE}(1,-1;Y)}=\underbrace{1}_{\text{CNDE-O}(1,-1;Y)}-\underbrace{(-1)}_{\text{CNIE-O}(-1,1;Y)},

5.2

These decompositions are coherent across intervals: the direct and indirect components of the effect of increasing cigarette consumption from 20 to 40 cigarettes per day on heart rate are equal to the corresponding sums of the 20–30 and 30–40 components.

7
Conclusions

We show that the standard decomposition of the TE into NDE and NIE can give rise to paradoxical interpretations. These paradoxes stem from the failure of NDE and NIE to satisfy two fundamental properties of interpretable causal effects—skew-symmetry and additivity—and can complicate the interpretation of mediation analysis, particularly when comparing effects across treatment contrasts or aggregating effects over multiple levels. Despite their importance, these issues have received limited attention in the literature, possibly because earlier approaches, such as the regression-based framework of
3
, do not exhibit such behavior under linear models.

To address these limitations, we proposed a new decomposition of the TE based on cumulative natural direct and indirect effects that preserves skew-symmetry and additivity. This formulation provides a coherent and intuitive interpretation of direct and indirect effects across treatment levels and avoids the paradoxes associated with NDE and NIE.
Our results contribute to the conceptual foundations of mediation analysis by emphasizing the importance of structural properties in effect decomposition. The proposed framework offers a principled alternative for settings with continuous or ordinal treatments, where path-based interpretations are natural and desirable.

The identification results in the paper rely on the sequential ignorability assumption
(
13
;
14
)
. In settings where this assumption is not justified
(
6
;
19
)
, sensitivity analysis plays a crucial role in assessing the robustness of conclusions to unmeasured confounding
(
14
;
6
)
.
Furthermore, instrumental-variable, proximal-variable, and difference-in-differences approaches can facilitate identification in the presence of unmeasured confounding
(
24
;
7
;
12
)
.
Extending these approaches to our proposed measures is an important avenue for future research.
Another important extension concerns settings with multiple mediators, which are common in applied studies
(
5
;
36
;
39
)
. Developing cumulative direct and indirect effect measures in the presence of multiple mediators, as well as studying their identification and interpretation, is an interesting direction for future work.
Efficient estimation is another important topic, particularly in settings with limited sample sizes.

28
developed semiparametric theory for causal mediation analysis, while
10
proposed generalized weighting estimators. Developing efficient estimators for our proposed measures is an important direction for future research.

We hope that the proposed framework will provide a useful and interpretable alternative for mediation analysis and stimulate further research on structurally coherent decompositions of causal effects.
