Mod p p Monodromy of Cyclic Covers of the Projective Line
Stepan Nesterov

Abstract
In this paper, we prove a big monodromy theorem for the monodromy of cyclic coverings of projective line for cohomology with 𝐅 p \mathbf{F}_{p} -coefficients. This is a direct generalization of the results of Achter and Pries, where such a theorem is proved for cyclic coverings of degree 2 2 and 3 3 . Instead of generalizing their methods, we adapt the proof of the analogous theorem for integral cohomology. In our subsequent work, we will apply this theorem to construct in infinitely many cases Galois extensions of 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} with Galois group PSL ⁡ ( n , q ) \PSL(n,q) and PSU ⁡ ( n , q ) \PSU(n,q) , where q q can be an arbitrarilty large prime power.

Introduction

Over
\mathbb{C}
, cyclic branched coverings of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
can be explicitly constructed as smooth proper models of affine curves given by equations of the form
y^{l}=\prod_{i=0}^{n}(x-a_{i})^{k_{i}}
for some distinct scalars
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}

k_{i}\in\{1,2,\ldots,l-1\}
. Let us denote such a covering by
X_{\bar{a},\bar{k}}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
. The tuple
(k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
will be called the
monodromy vector
and fixed throughout the argument. The set of all tuples
(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
of distinct points of
\mathbb{C}
will be called
the configuration space
and denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{n}
. It is clear that all the cyclic branched coverings of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
with the monodromy vector
\bar{k}
can be considered as fibers of a universal family
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{k}}\to\mathcal{C}_{n}
. It is well-known that the fundamental group of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is canonically isomorphic to the Artin’s pure braid group
P_{n+1}
of pure braids on
n+1
strands, and so we obtain a representation of
P_{n+1}
on the cohomology group of any fiber of the universal cyclic branched covering.

The case of the cohomology group with integral coeffients was studied in
[
6
]

If

on

is a finite index subgroup in
\U_{n-1}(\mathbf{Z}[\cos\frac{2\pi}{l}])
.

We will modify this result to work for the cohomology group with mod
p
coefficients instead. For the convenience of reader, let us recall what the possible behaviour of the reduction mof

If

Our approach is inspired by an analogous theorem from

Our ultimate motivation for this project lies in its applications in inverse Galois theory. The connection between big monodromy theorems and big Galois image theorems is well-known, and the latter is also a well-studied topic (see
[

Definition 4.1
.

Let

be any group,

be any commutative ring, and

be a representation of

as a direct sum of free
R
-modules setwise preserved by

is primitive if it is not induced from a finite index subgroup of

.

Lemma 3
.

Under the assumptions of the proposition

, the Gassner representation
\bar{g}_{n}(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n+1})
is primitive.

Proof.

Let

be a direct sum decomposition preserved by

. First, I claim that any transvection in the image of the Gassner representation acts on the set

trivially. Without loss of generality, assume that

is a transvection with direction

\widetilde{T}V_{1}\subseteq V_{2}

, then in particular,

. But this implies that

is an element of even order, while in reality its order is

– the characteristic of

Now pick one of the spaces in this decomposition, say

, does not contain the vector

from proposition

Under the assumptions of the proposition

, the image of

Proof.

Let us say that an isotropic vector

are in the image of

preserves the set of good isotropic vectors. By proposition
6

\bar{g}_{n}(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n+1})
is irreducible, the

is irreducible, for then

Let

be a proper nonzero

. I claim that

is either contained in

or is orthogonal to it. Indeed, assume that

with direction

Given that

minimal

are
