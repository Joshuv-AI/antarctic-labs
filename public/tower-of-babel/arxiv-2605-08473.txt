Characterization of weights for the variable fractional maximal operator and weighted inequalities for variable fractional rough operators Thanks: The authors are partially supported by CONICET and SECYT-UNC
Rodrigo M. Pastrana, M. Silvina Riveros, Raúl E. Vidal

Abstract
. We characterize the class of weights related to the boundedness of variable fractional maximal operator M β ⁡ ( ⋅ ) , r ⁡ ( ⋅ ) M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)} on variable Lebesgue spaces. This extend previously known results, including those corresponding to the fractional operator M β ⁡ ( ⋅ ) , 1 M_{\beta(\cdot),1} . In addition, we introduce a class of kernels K K satisfying a new variable Hörmander-type condition H β ⁡ ( ⋅ ) , r ⁡ ( ⋅ ) H_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)} . For the fractional operator T β ⁡ ( ⋅ ) T_{\beta(\cdot)} given by a kernel in H β ⁡ ( ⋅ ) , r ⁡ ( ⋅ ) H_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)} , we prove a Coifman-Fefferman inequality and weighted inequalities in variable Lebesgue space. Finally, we provide examples of kernels in this variable Hörmander class.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
r(\cdot),\beta(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. For a function
f\in L^{r(\cdot)}_{\text{loc}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, the fractional maximal operator
M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
is defined by

(1.1)

M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\beta(\cdot)}\frac{\|f\chi_{Q}\|_{r(\cdot)}}{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{r(\cdot)}}.

where the supreme is taken over all cubes
Q
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes.

When
\beta(\cdot)\equiv\infty
we simply write
M_{r(\cdot)}
instead of
M_{\infty,r(\cdot)}
. If
\beta(\cdot)\equiv\infty
and
r(\cdot)\equiv 1
, we obtain the classical Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator,
M
. If
\beta(\cdot)\equiv n/\alpha
with
0\leq\alpha<n
, then
M_{\beta(\cdot),1}
reduces to the fractional maximal operator
M_{n/\alpha,1}
, usually denoted by
M_{\alpha}
in the literature. If
r(\cdot)\equiv r\geq 1
then the operator
M_{r(\cdot)}
reduces to the maximal operator
M_{r}
, defined by
M_{r}(f)=\left(M(|f|^{r})\right)^{1/r}
. If
\beta(\cdot)\equiv n/\alpha
with
0\leq\alpha<n
and
r(\cdot)\equiv r\geq 1
, we obtain the maximal operator
M_{n/\alpha,r}
, defined by

M_{n/\alpha,r}(f)(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}|Q|^{\alpha/n}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f|^{r}\right)^{1/r},

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes.

The variable fractional maximal operator
M_{r(\cdot)}
was introduced by Diening, Harjulehto, Hästö, and Růžička in
[
9
]
. The general case of
M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
was later defined by Melchiori and Pradolini in
[
16
]
.

In
[
9
]
and
[
16
]
, the boundedness of the maximal operators
M_{r(\cdot)}
and
M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
, respectively, was proved in variable Lebesgue spaces for exponents
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{log}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
(see the definition in Section
2
).

Theorem 1.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
p(\cdot),r(\cdot),s(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{log}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
p(\cdot)=r(\cdot)s(\cdot)
and
s^{-}>1
. Then

M_{r(\cdot)}:L^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\hookrightarrow L^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

And for the general case,

Theorem 1.2

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
p(\cdot),q(\cdot),r(\cdot),s(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{log}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
{p(\cdot)}\leq{q(\cdot)}\leq q^{+}<\infty
,
{p(\cdot)}={r(\cdot)}{s(\cdot)}
and
s^{-}>1
. If
{\beta(\cdot)}
is the exponent defined by
1/p(\cdot)-1/q(\cdot)=1/\beta(\cdot)
, then

M_{{\beta(\cdot)},{r(\cdot)}}:L^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\hookrightarrow L^{q(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

Note that these works did not consider weighted estimates.

By a weight
\omega
we mean a locally integrable non-negative function. The classical Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
consists of all weights
\omega
such that

\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\omega\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\omega^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes. The class
A_{\infty}
is defined as
A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{p\geq 1}A_{p}
.
In the variable context it is said that
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}
if there exists a constant
C
such that

\|\omega\chi_{Q}\|_{p(\cdot)}\|\omega^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}\leq C|Q|

uniformly for all cubes
Q
. Note that
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
if and only if
\omega^{p}\in A_{p}
.

Remark 1.3
.

We use the notation
\mathcal{A}
or
\mathbb{A}
(defined below) for the variable classes of weights and
A
for the classical Muckenhoupt class for constant exponents (see
[
19
,
18
]
for the properties of
A_{p}
).

The boundedness of the maximal operator
M
on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces was studied in
[
4
]
by Cruz-Uribe, Diening, and Hästö, and in
[
7
]
by Cruz-Uribe, Fiorenza, and Neugebauer. They proved that if
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{log}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
with
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\infty
, then there exists a constant
C
such that

\|(Mf)\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq C\|f\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}

if and only if
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}
.

In the proof carried out in
[
4
]
, the boundedness of the maximal operator
M_{r(\cdot)}
was used as a key tool. We will follow the same approach, using the boundedness of the maximal operator
M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
to establish the boundedness of
M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
in weighted variable Lebesgue spaces.

If
0\leq\alpha<n
and
r(\cdot)\equiv r
, Bernardis, Dalmasso, and Pradolini defined in
[
2
]
the class of weights
\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot),q(\cdot)}
for exponents
p(\cdot),q(\cdot)
satisfying
1/q(\cdot)=1/p(\cdot)-\alpha/n
with
p^{+}<n/\alpha
as:
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot),q(\cdot)}
if there exists a constant
C
such that

\|\chi_{Q}\omega\|_{q(\cdot)}\|\chi_{Q}\omega^{-1}\|_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}\leq C|Q|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}

uniformly for all cubes
Q
. They showed that this class characterizes the boundedness of the maximal operator
M_{n/\alpha,r}
on variable Lebesgue spaces:

Theorem 1.4

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<n
, let
w
be a weight, and let
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{log}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
be such that

1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<n/\alpha
and

1/q(\cdot)=1/p(\cdot)-\alpha/n
. Let
r
such that

1\leq r<p^{-}
.
Then

\|(M_{n/\alpha,r}f)\omega\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq C\|f\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}

if and only if

w^{r}\in\mathcal{A}_{\frac{p(\cdot)}{r},\frac{q(\cdot)}{r}}
.

In
[
17
]
, Melchori, Pradolini, and Ramos generalized this class without restrictions on the exponents. We say that
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot),q(\cdot)}
if

\|\chi_{Q}\omega\|_{q(\cdot)}\|\chi_{Q}\omega^{-1}\|_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}\lesssim\|\chi_{Q}\|_{q(\cdot)}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}

uniformly for all cubes
Q
. They proved that for exponents
p(\cdot),q(\cdot),\beta(\cdot)
satisfying
1/q(\cdot)=1/p(\cdot)-1/\beta(\cdot)
, if
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot),q(\cdot)}
then there exists a constant
C
such that

\|(T_{\beta(\cdot),1,\mathcal{S}}f)\omega\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq C\|f\omega\|_{p(\cdot)},

where
\mathcal{S}
is a sparse family (see the definition in Section
3
) and
T_{\beta(\cdot),1,\mathcal{S}}
is the sparse operator defined by

T_{\beta(\cdot),1,\mathcal{S}}f=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\beta(\cdot)}\|f\chi_{Q}\|_{1}\|\chi_{Q}\|^{-1}_{1}.

In this work we introduce a new class of weights,
\mathbb{A}_{p(\cdot),q(\cdot)}
, that generalizes the class
\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}
and coincides with the class
\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot),q(\cdot)}
when the exponents belong to
\mathcal{P}^{\text{log}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
(see Section
2
). Under appropriate conditions on the exponents, these weights characterize the boundedness of the fractional maximal operator
M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
. This will be established below.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
p(\cdot),r(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, such that
r(\cdot)\leq p(\cdot)
, and let
q(\cdot)
be defined by
1/r(\cdot)=1/p(\cdot)+1/q(\cdot)
. We say that a weight
\omega
belongs to the class
\mathbb{A}_{p{(\cdot)},r(\cdot)}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\chi_{Q}\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}\|\chi_{Q}\omega^{-1}\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq C\|\chi_{Q}\|_{r(\cdot)}

for all cubes
Q
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
p(\cdot),q(\cdot),r(\cdot),s(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
p(\cdot)\leq q(\cdot)\leq q^{+}<\infty
and
p(\cdot)=r(\cdot)s(\cdot)
with
s^{-}>1
. Let
\beta(\cdot)
be the exponent defined by
1/p(\cdot)-1/q(\cdot)=1/\beta(\cdot)
. Then exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|(M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}f)\omega\right\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq C\|f\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}

if and only if

\omega\in\mathbb{A}_{q(\cdot),\frac{\beta(\cdot)r(\cdot)}{\beta(\cdot)-r(\cdot)}}
.

Remark 1.7
.

Observe that we are considering possible the case
\beta(\cdot)=\infty
. We use the convention
\frac{r(\cdot)}{\infty}=0
when
r(\cdot)
is finite. Note that the expression
\frac{\beta(\cdot)r(\cdot)}{\beta(\cdot)-r(\cdot)}
can be rewritten as
\frac{r(\cdot)}{1-\frac{r(\cdot)}{\beta(\cdot)}}
. Throughout the rest of the paper we assume this convention.

If
p(\cdot)=q(\cdot)
, or equivalently
\beta(\cdot)\equiv\infty
, then we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
{p(\cdot)},{r(\cdot)},s(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{log}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
p^{+}<\infty
,
{p(\cdot)}={r(\cdot)}{s(\cdot)}
with
s^{-}>1
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|(M_{r(\cdot)}f)\omega\right\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq C\|f\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}

if and only if

\omega\in\mathbb{A}_{{p(\cdot)},{r(\cdot)}}
.

For the case
r(\cdot)\equiv 1
we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
{p(\cdot)},{q(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{log}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
1<p^{-}\leq{p(\cdot)}\leq{q(\cdot)}\leq q^{+}<\infty
and
{\beta(\cdot)}
be the exponent defined by
1/p(\cdot)-1/q(\cdot)=1/\beta(\cdot)
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|(M_{{\beta(\cdot)},1}f)\omega\right\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq C\|f\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}

if and only if

\omega\in\mathbb{A}_{{q(\cdot)},\beta^{\prime}(\cdot)}
.

Let
T
be an integral operator of the type

(1.2)

Tf(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}K(x,y)f(y)dy

where
K\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:x=y\}\right)
.

Let
K
be a kernel of the form
K(x,y)=\bar{K}(x-y)
, and
T
be bounded on some
L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Kurtz and Wheeden implicitly proved in
[
13
]
that if
K
satisfies the smoothness condition
H_{r}
with
1<r<\infty
(see the precise definition in
[
15
]
or Definition
1.10
with
r(\cdot)\equiv r_{0}
and
\beta(\cdot)\equiv\infty
), then
T
is controlled by the maximal operator
M_{r^{\prime}}
. This is

(1.3)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|Tf|^{p}\omega\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\left(M_{r^{\prime}}f\right)^{p}\omega

for
0<p<\infty
and
\omega\in A_{\infty}
, whenever the left-side is finite.

In
[
15
]
, Martell, Pérez and Trujillo-González showed that this estimate is sharp in the sense that, for
s<r^{\prime}
, there exists a kernel
K\in H_{r}
such that the operator induced by
K
is not controlled by
M_{s}
as in (1.3).

In
[
14
]
, Lorente, Riveros and de la Torre proved that if
K
satisfies the smoothness condition
H_{\mathscr{A}}
, then
T
is controlled by the maximal operator
M_{\bar{\mathscr{A}}}
, where
A
is a convex Young function,
\bar{\mathscr{A}}
denotes its complementary function, and
M_{\mathscr{A}}
is defined through the Luxemburg norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{A}}
in the corresponding Orlicz space (see
[
20
]
). When
\mathscr{A}(t)=t^{r}
, the condition
H_{\mathscr{A}}
reduces to the condition
H_{r}
. In
[
3
]
, Bernardis, Lorente and Riveros replaced the boundedness on
L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
hypothesis by a size condition
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,\mathscr{A}}
and the condition
H_{\mathscr{A}}
by
H_{\alpha,\mathscr{A}}
, under which the operator
T
is controlled by the fractional maximal operator
M_{n/\alpha,\bar{\mathscr{A}}}
. Following these ideas we introduce a variable Hörmander-type condition and a variable size condition.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, we say that a kernel

K\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:x=y\}\right)

satisfy the

L^{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
-Hörmander-1 condition, and we denote
K\in H_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),1}
, if

\underset{Q}{\sup}\underset{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}{\sup}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{m}\ell(Q))^{n}}{\|\chi_{2^{m}Q}\|_{\beta(\cdot)}}\frac{\left\|\left[K(x,\cdot)-K(z,\cdot)\right]\chi_{2^{m}Q\setminus 2^{m-1}Q}(\cdot)\right\|_{r(\cdot)}}{\left\|\chi_{2^{m}Q}\right\|_{r(\cdot)}}<\infty.

Analogously, we say that
K
satisfy the

L^{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
-Hörmander-2 condition, and we denote
K\in H_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),2}
, if

\underset{Q}{\sup}\underset{x,z\in\frac{1}{2}Q}{\sup}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{m}\ell(Q))^{n}}{\|\chi_{2^{m}Q}\|_{\beta(\cdot)}}\frac{\left\|\left[K(\cdot,x)-K(\cdot,z)\right]\chi_{2^{m}Q\setminus 2^{m-1}Q}(\cdot)\right\|_{r(\cdot)}}{\left\|\chi_{2^{m}Q}\right\|_{r(\cdot)}}<\infty.

where supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes.

We define
H_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}=H_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),1}\cap H_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),2}
. If
\beta(\cdot)\equiv\infty
, we simply write
H_{r(\cdot),i}
instead of
H_{\infty,r(\cdot),i}
, and
H_{r(\cdot)}
instead of
H_{\infty,r(\cdot)}
. If
K\in H_{r(\cdot),i}
, we say that
K
satisfies the
L^{r(\cdot)}
-Hörmander-
i
condition. If
K\in H_{r(\cdot)}
, we say that
K
satisfies the
L^{r(\cdot)}
-Hörmander condition.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
{\beta(\cdot)},{r(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. We say that the kernel
K
satisfy the size condition
S_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),1}
, and we write
K\in S_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),1}
, if there exists a constant
C
such that

\sup_{x\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\frac{\left\|k(x,\cdot)\chi_{2Q\setminus Q}(\cdot)\right\|_{r(\cdot)}}{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{r(\cdot)}}\leq C\frac{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\beta(\cdot)}}{|Q|}

uniformly for all cubes
Q
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes.

Analogously, we say that the kernel
K
satisfy the size condition
S_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),2}
, and we write
K\in S_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),2}
, if there exists a constant
C
such that

\sup_{y\in\frac{1}{2}Q}\frac{\left\|k(\cdot,y)\chi_{2Q\setminus Q}(\cdot)\right\|_{r(\cdot)}}{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{r(\cdot)}}\leq C\frac{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\beta(\cdot)}}{|Q|}

uniformly for all cubes
Q
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes. We define
S_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}=S_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),1}\cap S_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),2}
.

When
\mathscr{A}(t)=t^{r_{0}}
, the classes
S_{\beta(\cdot),r_{0}}
are a generalization of the classes
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,\mathscr{A}}
defined in
[
3

in the context of variable Lebesgue spaces.

The classes
H_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),i}
and
S_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),i}
are decreasing in the exponent

. Moreover, the inclusions

(1.4)

H_{\infty,i}\subset H_{q(\cdot),i}\subset H_{p(\cdot),i}\subset\cdots\subset H_{1,i}

are strict, as we will see with the examples of kernels presented in Section

Throughout,
T
denotes an operator with kernel
K\in H_{r(\cdot)}
. When the kernel
K

H_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),1}\cap S_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),2}

We prove that an integral operator of the type (
1.2
) satisfying these conditions is controlled by the maximal operator
M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
. In other words, we prove a Coifman-Fefferman inequality for these operators:

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
r(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, and let
T
be an integral operator bounded on some
L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
with
1<p_{0}<\infty
, whose kernel
K
belongs to
H_{r(\cdot)}
. Then

a)

For any
p
with
0<p<\infty
, and any

, there exists a constant
C
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|Tf|^{p}w\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\left(M_{r^{\prime}(\cdot)}f\right)^{p}w

whenever the left-hand side is finite.

b)

If
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}^{\text{log}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
with
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\infty
, and
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}
then there exists a constant C such that

\|(Tf)\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq C\|(M_{r^{\prime}(\cdot)}f)\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}

whenever the left-hand side is finite.

The sharp maximal function
M^{\sharp}
is defined as

M^{\sharp}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)-a|dy.

The weighted Bounded Mean Oscillation space,
BMO_{\omega}
is defined by

BMO_{\omega}=\{f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}):\omega M^{\sharp}f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\},

The seminorm on this space is given by

\|f\|_{*,\omega}=\sup_{Q}\|\chi_{Q}\omega\|_{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\left(f(x)-\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(y)dy\right)dx\right].

Is easy to see that
\|f\|_{*,\omega}\approx\left\|\omega M^{\sharp}f\right\|_{\infty}
(For details, see
[
12
]

2.1.
Variable Lebesgue space

Next, we give some definitions and state some basic results on variable Lebesgue spaces.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
the set of exponents consisting of all measurable functions
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to[1,\infty]
. For
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
, we single out the following sets:

\displaystyle({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{p(\cdot)}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:p(x)=\infty\right\}

\displaystyle({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{p(\cdot)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:p(x)=1\right\}

\displaystyle({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{p(\cdot)}_{*}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:1<p(x)<\infty\right\}.

For a measurable
E\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, denote by
p^{+}_{E}=\text{esssup}_{E}\ p(\cdot)
and
p^{-}=\text{essinf}_{E}\ p(\cdot)
. If
E={\mathbb{R}^{n}}
we simply write
p^{+}
and
p^{-}
respectively. The conjugated exponent
p^{\prime}(\cdot)
is defined as usually, by,

1/p(\cdot)+1/p^{\prime}(\cdot)=1
.
The variable Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is defined as the set of all measurable functions on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
such that, for some
\lambda>0
, the modular convex function satisfy

\rho_{p(\cdot)}\left(\frac{f}{\lambda}\right)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{p(\cdot)}_{\infty}}\left|\frac{f(x)}{\lambda}\right|^{p(x)}dx+\left\|f\chi_{({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{p(\cdot)}_{\infty}}\right\|_{\infty}<\infty.

This is a Banach space with norm

(2.1)

\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

and generalized the clasical Lebesgue space, if
p(\cdot)
is constantly
p_{0}
then
L^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})=L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
\|\cdot\|_{p(\cdot)}=\|\cdot\|_{p_{0}}
.

Given a weight
\omega
(a non-negative measurable function), the weighted variable Lebesgue space,
L^{p(\cdot)}(\omega)
, is defined as the set of all measurable functions
f
such that
f\omega\in L^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. The norm in this space is given by
\|f\omega\|_{p(\cdot)}
.

(
[
8
]
)

Given

with

, and a weight

The following properties hold:

3.

In this section we characterize the weights for the maximal fractional operator
M_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot)}
. Before proving Theorem
1.6
, we establish some auxiliary results.
Let
p(\cdot),{r(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
{r(\cdot)}\leq{p(\cdot)}

{q(\cdot)}
be the exponent defined by

\frac{1}{r(\cdot)}=\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}+\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}.

For

we define

(3.1)

\|f\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}=\underset{\|g\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq 1}{\sup}\|fg\|_{r(\cdot)}.

It is not hard to see that

defines a norm on

. Moreover, under appropriate conditions on the exponents
{p(\cdot)}
and
{r(\cdot)}
, the norms
\|\cdot\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}
and

are equivalent.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{p(\cdot)},{q(\cdot)},{r(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that

\frac{1}{r(x)}=\frac{1}{p(x)}+\frac{1}{q(x)}.

If either
r(\cdot)<p(\cdot)<\infty
or
r(\cdot)\leq p(\cdot)<\infty
with

, then

\|f\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}=\underset{\|g\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq 1}{\sup}\|fg\|_{r(\cdot)}\approx\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}.

The proof can be found in Section
5
.
In the particular case
r(\cdot)\equiv 1
, this coincides with the conjugate norm (see
[
9
,
5
]
).

The class introduced in
1.5
is closely related with the boundedness of the average operator
{\bf A}_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),Q}
defined by

{\bf A}_{\beta(\cdot),r(\cdot),Q}f=\chi_{Q}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\beta(\cdot)}\frac{\|f\chi_{Q}\|_{r(\cdot)}}{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{r(\cdot)}},

as shown below.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\omega
be a weight. Let
p(\cdot),q(\cdot),\beta(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that

and

(1)

Let

such that either
{r(\cdot)}<{p(\cdot)}

uniformly for all cubes

(2)

Let

{r(\cdot)}\leq{p(\cdot)}

Note that

\frac{1}{{p(\cdot)}}-\frac{1}{{q(\cdot)}}=\frac{1}{{\beta(\cdot)}}\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad\frac{1}{{q(\cdot)}}+\frac{{p(\cdot)}-{r(\cdot)}}{{p(\cdot)}{r(\cdot)}}=\frac{{\beta(\cdot)}-{r(\cdot)}}{{\beta(\cdot)}{r(\cdot)}}.

0&\text{if}&f(x)=0.\end{array}\right.

Then

\rho_{q(\cdot)}(g)=\int_{\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{q(\cdot)}_{\infty}}}\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)^{p(x)}dx\leq\rho_{p(\cdot)}\left(\frac{f\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{q(\cdot)}_{\infty}}}{\lambda}\right)=1,

which implies that
\|g\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq 1
. Therefore, by the hypothesis, definition of
\|\cdot\|^{*}_{p(\cdot)}
and Proposition
2.2

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\left\|f\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}\right\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}\geq\left\|f\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}g\right\|_{r(\cdot)}\geq\rho_{r(\cdot)}\left(f\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}g\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}|f(x)|^{r(x)}\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{p(x)-r(x)}{r(x)}r(x)}dx\geq\lambda\rho_{p(\cdot)}\left(\frac{f\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}}{\lambda}\right)=\lambda,

which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
{p(\cdot)},{r(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
r(\cdot)\leq p(\cdot)<\infty
with
r^{+}<\infty
. Let
{q(\cdot)}
by the exponent defined by
1/r(\cdot)=1/p(\cdot)+1/q(\cdot)
. If
\left\|f\right\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}\leq 1
and
\rho_{p(\cdot)}\left(f\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}\right)<\infty
, then
\rho_{p(\cdot)}\left(k_{q(\cdot)}^{r^{+}}f\right)\leq 1
, where

k_{q(\cdot)}^{-1}=\left\|\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\chi_{({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}\right\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
,
\rho_{p(\cdot)}\left(f\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}\right)\leq 1
. Therefore

(5.1)

\rho_{p(\cdot)}\left(f\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}\right)\leq\left\|\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{q(\cdot)}_{\infty}}\right\|_{\infty}.

Define

g(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}k_{q(\cdot)}|f(x)|^{\frac{p(x)-r(x)}{r(x)}}&si&q(x)<\infty,\\
\\
k_{q(\cdot)}&si&q(x)=\infty.\end{array}\right.

By
(\ref{A1})
,

\displaystyle\rho_{q(\cdot)}\left(\frac{g}{k_{q(\cdot)}}\right)\leq\left\|\chi_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\chi_{({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{q(\cdot)}_{\infty}}\right\|_{\infty}=k_{q(\cdot)}^{-1}.

Since
k_{q(\cdot)}\leq 1
, Proposition
2.2
implies that
\|g\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq 1
.

Finally, again by Proposition
2.2
,

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\|f\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}\geq\|fg\|_{r(\cdot)}\geq\rho_{r(\cdot)}(fg)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{q(\cdot)}_{\infty}}k_{r(\cdot)}^{r(x)}|f(x)|^{r(x)}|f(x)|^{\frac{p(x)-r(x)}{r(x)}r(x)}dx+\int_{({\mathbb{R}^{n}})_{\infty}^{q(\cdot)}}k_{q(\cdot)}^{r(x)}|f(x)|^{r(x)}dx

\displaystyle\geq k_{q(\cdot)}^{r^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p(x)}=k^{r^{+}}_{q(\cdot)}\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)\geq\rho_{p(\cdot)}(k^{r^{+}}_{q(\cdot)}f).

∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

By Hölder’s inequality
2.3
,

\|f\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}=\underset{\|g\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq 1}{\sup}\|fg\|_{r(\cdot)}\lesssim\underset{\|g\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq 1}{\sup}\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}\|g\|_{q(\cdot)}\leq\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}.

On the other hand, since
\||f|\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}=\|f\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}
, without loss of generality we can take
f\geq 0
. Moreover, by homogeneity of the norm
\|\cdot\|^{*}_{p(\cdot)}
, is enough to consider
\|f\|^{*}_{p(\cdot)}=1
.

Let
B_{k}(0)
be the ball centered at the origin with radius
k
. We define
E_{k}=B_{k}(0)\cap\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:p(x)<k\right\}
, and
f_{k}(x)=\min\left\{f(x),k\right\}\chi_{E_{k}}(x)
. Therefore
f_{k}\nearrow f
, as
k\to\infty
,
\|f_{k}\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}\leq\|f\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}=1
, and
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f_{k})<\infty
.

Case 1:
If
r(\cdot)<p(\cdot)<\infty
, result
({\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{q(\cdot)}_{\infty}=\emptyset
. Applying Lemma
5.1
to
f_{k}
, we obtain
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f_{k})\leq 1
. Therefore, by Fatou’s lemma,
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)\leq 1
, and consequently

\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq 1=\|f\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}.

Case 2:
If
r(\cdot)\leq p(\cdot)<\infty
and
r^{+}<\infty
. By Lemma
5.2
,

\rho_{p(\cdot)}\left(k_{q(\cdot)}^{r^{+}}f_{k}\right)\leq 1
. Therefore, by Fatou’s lemma,

\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq k_{q(\cdot)}^{-r^{+}}=k_{q(\cdot)}^{-r^{+}}\|f\|^{\ast}_{p(\cdot)}.

∎

Now we prove the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition.

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

The proof of (
1
) and (
2
) are an direct adaptation of the proofs of the Lemmas 3.9 and 3.13 of
[
5
]
. For (
3
), let
a_{0}>1
be a constant to be determined later. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a>a_{0}
let

\Omega_{k}=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:M^{\mathcal{D}}_{{\beta(\cdot)},{r(\cdot)}}f(x)>a^{k}\right\}=\bigcup_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}_{k}}Q_{j}^{k}

where
\{Q_{j}^{k}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}_{k}}
are the cubes given by
(
1
)
for
\lambda=a^{k}
. Therefore

(5.2)

a^{k}<\|\chi_{Q_{j}^{k}}\|_{\beta(\cdot)}\frac{\|f\chi_{Q_{j}^{k}}\|_{r(\cdot)}}{\|\chi_{Q_{j}^{k}}\|_{r(\cdot)}}\leq D^{2}_{r(\cdot)}a^{k}.

We define
E_{j}^{k}=Q_{j}^{k}\setminus\Omega_{k}
, then the sets
E_{j}^{k}
are pairwise disjoint for each
k
. Moreover the sets
E_{j}^{k}
are pairwise disjoints for all

and
j
. In fact, if
k>l
, and

\displaystyle x\in E_{j}^{k}\cap E_{i}^{l}=(Q_{j}^{k}\cap Q_{i}^{l})\setminus\Omega_{l}.

Then

a^{k}<M^{\mathcal{D}}_{{\beta(\cdot)},{r(\cdot)}}f(x)\leq a^{l}\leq a^{k}
which is absurd. We prove that the family
\{Q_{j}^{k}\}_{j}
is sparse, for this we most estimate

\frac{|E_{j}^{k}|}{|Q_{j}^{k}|}=\frac{|Q_{j}^{k}\setminus\Omega_{1+k}|}{|Q_{j}^{k}|}=\frac{|Q_{j}^{k}\setminus(Q_{j}^{k}\cap\Omega_{k+1})|}{|Q_{j}^{k}|}=1-\frac{|Q_{j}^{k}\cap\Omega_{k+1}|}{|Q_{j}^{k}|}.

By the definition of
\Omega_{k}

2.3

\displaystyle|Q_{j}^{k}\cap\Omega_{k+1}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q_{i}^{k+1}\subset Q_{j}^{k}}\left|Q^{k+1}_{i}\right|\leq C\sum_{Q_{i}^{k+1}\subset Q_{j}^{k}}\left\|\chi_{Q^{k+1}_{i}}\right\|_{r(\cdot)}\left\|\chi_{Q^{k+1}_{i}}\right\|_{r^{\prime}(\cdot)}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{Q_{i}^{k+1}\subset Q_{j}^{k}}a^{-(k+1)}\left\|\chi_{Q^{k+1}_{i}}\right\|_{\beta(\cdot)}\left\|f\chi_{Q^{k+1}_{i}}\right\|_{r(\cdot)}\left\|\chi_{Q^{k+1}_{i}}\right\|_{r^{\prime}(\cdot)}

\displaystyle\leq Ca^{-(k+1)}\left\|\chi_{Q^{k}_{j}}\right\|_{\beta(\cdot)}\sum_{Q_{i}^{k+1}\subset Q_{j}^{k}}\left\|f\chi_{Q_{j}^{k}}\chi_{Q^{k+1}_{i}}\right\|_{r(\cdot)}\left\|\chi_{Q^{k}_{j}}\chi_{Q^{k+1}_{i}}\right\|_{r^{\prime}(\cdot)}
