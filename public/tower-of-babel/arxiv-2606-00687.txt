A topological classification of generating functions
Sylvain Courte, Vivek Shende

Abstract
From a generating function for a Legendrian in a 1 1 -jet bundle, we may extract the following topological information: (1) a trivialization of the stable Gauss map, (2) the sheaf of sub-level-set stable cohomotopies, and (3) an identification of the microlocalization of the latter with the J-homomorphism image of the former. Here we show that in fact (1), (2), (3) completely classify generating functions up to the classical equivalence relations of stabilization and fiberwise diffeomorphism.

Introduction

The use of generating functions to present Lagrangian submanifolds appeared already in the works of Hamilton on classical mechanics and of Jacobi on geometric optics. Closer to modern times, this method was profitably employed by Arnol’d
[
4
]
and Hörmander
[
23
]
, for purposes of singularity theory and analysis, respectively.

Let us recall the basic idea. If
M
is a manifold and
f:M\to\mathbf{R}
is a function, then the graph of
df
is a Lagrangian in
T^{*}M
. More generally, if
\pi:E\to M
is any map (for us, a trivial bundle with fiber
\mathbf{R}^{n}
or an open set thereof), then the conormal to the graph of
\pi
is a Lagrangian correspondence, which can be used to transform Lagrangians in
T^{*}E
to Lagrangian immersions in
T^{*}M
. In particular, given
f:E\to\mathbf{R}
, we may obtain in this way, from the graph of
df
, an immersed Lagrangian
L_{f}\subset T^{*}M
(under a suitable transversality condition), parameterized by
\pi
-fiberwise critical points of
f
. In fact, such a Lagrangian is necessarily exact, i.e. the image of a Legendrian in the 1-jet bundle
J^{1}M
. To see this, transform the conormal to the graph of
f
in
T^{*}(E\times\mathbf{R})
by the correspondence derived as above from
\pi\times\mathrm{id}:E\times\mathbf{R}\to M\times\mathbf{R}
; the result is a conical Lagrangian in
T^{*}(M\times\mathbf{R})
. Fixing the value of the cotangent vector in the
\mathbf{R}
direction gives the desired Legendrian
\Lambda_{f}\subset J^{1}M
.

There is a classical equivalence relation among generating functions, generated by pullback by
\pi
-fiberwise diffeomorphism, and fiberwise sum with a quadratic form. It will be most natural for us to restrict to constant (over
M
) quadratic forms of symmetric signature
(k,k)
.
We are interested in classifying generating function presentations of a given Legendrian
\Lambda
, up to this equivalence. In fact this will lead
us to investigate the homotopy type of a space, denoted
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
, of all generating functions for
\Lambda
, the connected components
of which will correspond to equivalence classes.

There is a classification, due to Giroux
[
18
]
and Latour
[
32
]
,
of the germs of equivalence classes of generating functions along their critical loci (we refer to such germs as
semi-local generating functions
). Recall the stable Gauss map
\gamma:\Lambda\to U/O
, defined by comparing along
\Lambda
the polarizations given by the tangent to
\Lambda
and the cotangent fiber.
A semi-local generating function provides a null-homotopy of this map: the tangent to the graph of
df
provides a lift of the stable Gauss map to a contractible space of graphical Lagrangians. We will define a suitable space (simplicial set), denoted
\mu\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
,
of all semi-local generating functions for
\Lambda
(see Definition
2.27
). The result of Giroux and Latour can be phrased as
a homotopy equivalence between this space and the (possibly empty) space of all null-homotopies of the stable Gauss map.
Note that this classification implies that semi-local generating functions cannot detect the subtle global phenomena of symplectic topology, e.g. nonsqueezing, the expected lower bounds on Lagrangian intersections, etc.

Global generating functions, now required to be defined on all of
E=M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
and to be appropriately “tame at infinity, fiberwise for
\pi:E\to M
” (a notion whose properties we develop in Appendix
A
), do detect such global phenomena, as shown in the works of Chaperon
[
7
]
, Laudenbach and Sikorav
[
33
]
, Sikorav
[
46
]
, Chekanov
[
9
]
, Eliashberg and Gromov
[
12
]
, and Viterbo
[
50
]
, among many others. It follows that their classification must be richer than that of semi-local generating functions. Yet, in the only examples where the complete classification was previously known – the zero section of a jet bundle
[
50
,
48
]
and the 1-dimensional Legendrian unknot
[
27
]
– each (equivalence class of) generating function is determined by its underlying semi-local generating function.

We may obtain additional invariants of global generating functions from the topology of fiberwise sublevel sets of
f
. Such invariants have been considered since
[
50
]
; we record them using the formalism of sheaf theory. As we consider generating functions equivalent if they may be related by stabilization, only the stable homotopy types of said sublevel sets are invariant; to capture these, we work with sheaves valued in the category
\mathrm{Sp}
of spectra.

\mathbf{S}
be the sphere spectrum, and let
\mathbf{S}_{f\leq z}
be the constant sheaf on the region not below the graph of
f
in

. There is a sheaf

whose stalk at

, shifted by

To any sheaf

More generally, we regard a sheaf whose microsupport similarly characterizes a given Legendrian

Generating sheaves have many applications in symplectic topology, and in particular can be used in place of a generating function when one does not or is not known to exist

The classification problem for generating functions is thus reduced to two parts: classifying generating sheaves (which we will not discuss here, or said differently, we regard the sheaf itself as a topological invariant of the function) and determining when a generating sheaf arises from a generating function.
Let us mention some obvious obstructions. One typically restricts attention to generating functions
f
for which the fiberwise critical locus is transversely cut out and maps diffeomorphically onto
\Lambda
; the corresponding property of
\mathbf{s}(f)
is that
\mathbf{s}(f)
is a ‘simple’ sheaf in the sense of
[
29
, Def. 7.5.4]
. The notion of simple sheaf captures the idea that, as you cross the microsupport, the dimension of the space of sections changes by one.
In fact, this one-dimensional-change forms a sort of local system along the microsupport, the study of which will bring us again into contact with trivializations of the stable Gauss map. We recall the formalism for describing this local system.

Given a manifold
N
(we take
N=M\times\mathbf{R}
), there is a sheaf of categories defined on
T^{*}N
by sheafifying the presheaf:

\mu\mathrm{Sh}^{pre}(U):=\mathrm{Sh}(N)/\{F\,|\,ss(F)\cap U=\emptyset\}

When
N=M\times\mathbf{R}
, we write also
\mu\mathrm{Sh}
for the pullback of this sheaf of categories to
J^{1}M
. For
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
, we write
\mu\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}
for the subsheaf of full subcategories of objects supported on
\Lambda
. It is the pushforward of a sheaf of categories on
\Lambda
. The natural map
\mathrm{Sh}(M)\to\mu\mathrm{Sh}(J^{1}M)
carries a sheaf microsupported in
\Lambda
to a global section of
\mu\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}
.

If we are working with sheaves with coefficients in some symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, there is a canonical group homomorphism
[
37
, Sec. 11]
:

\mu:U/O\to B\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{C}).

Composing with the stable Gauss map
\Lambda\to U/O
gives
\mu_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\to B\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{C})
, or in other words, a
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{C})
local system over
\Lambda
, which we also denote
\mu_{\Lambda}
.
Then

\mu\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}=\mu_{\Lambda}\times^{\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{C})}\mathcal{C}

and as such, an invertible global section of
\mu\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}
provides a trivialization of
\mu_{\Lambda}
and

\mu\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}
. A sheaf
F
on
M\times\mathbf{R}
with
ss(F)|_{J^{1}M}=\Lambda
tautologically provides a section of
\mu\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}
; invertibility of the section is equivalent to the condition that
F
is simple in the sense of Kashiwara and Schapira. In summary, a simple sheaf microsupported in
\Lambda
provides a null-homotopy of
\mu_{\Lambda}
. In the case where
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Sp}
, Jin further identified (see
[
26
]
) the map
U/O\to B\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{C})
as (a delooping of) the J-homomorphism

U/O\simeq B(\mathbf{Z}\times BO)\to B(\mathbf{Z}\times BG)\simeq B\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{C})
(recall
BO
is the classifying space of stable vector bundles
while
BG
is the classifying space of stable spherical fibrations and the
J
-homomorphism maps
a vector bundle to the spherical fibration obtained by one point-compactifying each fiber).

In case
F=\mathbf{s}(f)
, then the null-homotopy of
\mu_{\Lambda}
furnished by
F
is
the composition of
\mu
with the null-homotopy of the stable Gauss map for
f
(e.g. consider both via the symplectic reduction from the graph of
f
).
That is, we have a commutative diagram

Here, we write

for the space (simplicial set) of all tame generating functions for

(we pass to colimit under stabilization by a fixed quadratic form, the

)
and

We write

Our main result is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a manifold and
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
a closed Legendrian submanifold.
The diagram (

) is a homotopy pullback square.

This means that we have a homotopy equivalence

\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}\simeq\mu\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}\times_{\mu\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda}(M;\mathbf{S})^{*}}\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}

) thus agree; so we see that lifts of a sheaf to a generating function
are classified by lifts of the induced null-homotopy of
\mu_{\Lambda}
to a null-homotopy of the stable Gauss map of
\Lambda
.

There is a natural ring map from the sphere spectrum
\mathbf{S}
to the integers
\mathbf{Z}
; tensoring over it carries sheaves over
\mathbf{S}
to sheaves over
\mathbf{Z}
.
This leads to the commutative square:

2
Generating functions

2.1
Tame generating functions

We will say that a function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is
fiberwise tame
if there is a closed set
K\subset M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
and a smooth function
a:M\to(0,+\infty)

such that the projection
K\to M
is proper and the maps

\{(x,v)\in M\times\mathbf{R}^{n},|f(x,v)|\geq a(x)\}\to\{(x,z)\in M\times\mathbf{R},|z|\geq a(x)\},

and

\{(x,v)\in M\times\mathbf{R}^{n},|f(x,v)|\leq a(x)\}\setminus K\to\{(x,z)\in M\times\mathbf{R},|z|\leq a(x)\}

are fibrations. Examples include:

•

(Quadratic at infinity):
f(x,v)=Q(v)+\epsilon(x,v)
where
Q
is a non-degenerate quadratic form and
\epsilon
has proper support over
M
,

•

(Linear at infinity):
f(x,v,w)=w+\epsilon(x,v,w)
where
\epsilon
has proper support over
M
.

We develop some elementary properties of fiberwise tameness in Appendix
A
.

Definition 2.1

(Generating functions)
.

A
generating function over
M
is a function

f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

for some
n\in\mathbf{N}
such that

•

f
is
fiberwise tame
with respect to the projection
M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to M
,

•

the map
M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n}
given by
(x,v)\mapsto\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(x,v)
is transverse to
0
.

The subset

\Sigma_{f}=\{(x,v)\in M\times\mathbf{R}^{n},\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(x,v)=0\}

is called the
singular set
of
f
(with respect to the projection
M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to M
),
it is a submanifold of codimension
n
.
If
M
has boundary and corners (this will be the case when we will take product by a simplex to define the simplicial set
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
), we require the same properties for the restriction of
f
to each stratum
M_{i}\subset\partial M
of any codimension.

The following property is well-known.

Proposition 2.2
.

The map
i_{f}:\Sigma_{f}\to J^{1}M
given by

i_{f}(x,v)=(x,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,v),f(x,v))

is a Legendrian immersion.

Definition 2.3

(Excellent generating functions)
.

A generating function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is
excellent
if the map
i_{f}
is an embedding. In this case we say that

i_{f}(\Sigma_{f})
is generated by
f
, and sometimes denote
i_{f}(\Sigma_{f})=\Lambda_{f}
.
If
M
has boundary and corners, we require the same property for the restriction of
f
to each stratum
M_{i}\subset M
of any codimension.

It can be proven using Thom’s transversality theorem that a generic fiberwise tame function

M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is an excellent generating function.

Example 2.4
.

If
M
is a single point,
f:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is excellent if and only if it is a Morse function with distinct critical values.
If
M=[0,1]
,
(f_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is excellent if and only if the restrictions to
\{0\}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
\{1\}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
(0,1)\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
are excellent. This says in particular that the Cerf diagram of the path
(f_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
(projection of
i_{f}(\Sigma_{f})
onto
J^{0}M=M\times\mathbf{R}
)
is the front of a Legendrian submanifold transverse to
t=0
and
t=1
.

Remark 2.5
.

There is a more general notion of generating functions where we replace
M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to M
by any fibration
p:E\to M

and require
f
to be fiberwise tame with respect to
p
. For the comparison with sheaves to be simpler, we stick in this paper
to the case where
p
is a trivial bundle with
\mathbf{R}^{n}
-fibers.

Definition 2.6

(Stabilization)
.

We fix once and for all a non-degenerate quadratic form
h
on
\mathbf{R}^{2}
of signature
0
(e.g.
h(u,v)=uv
), and denote
h^{k}:\mathbf{R}^{2k}\to\mathbf{R}
with
k\geq 0

the direct sum of
k
copies of
h
. Given a generating function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
, the function

\sigma^{k}f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}^{2k}\to\mathbf{R}

defined by

\sigma^{k}f(x;u,v)=f(x;u)+h^{k}(v)

is called the
k
-fold stabilization
of
f
.

The
k
-fold stabilization of
f
is also fiberwise tame (e.g. per Lemma
A.7
) and satisfies the transversality condition of Definition
2.1
,
hence it is a generating function over
M
. The singular set is

\Sigma_{\sigma^{k}f}=\Sigma_{f}\times\{0\}

and the generated Legendrian immersion is unchanged: for all
(x;u)\in\Sigma_{f}
,

i_{\sigma^{k}f}(x,u,0)=i_{f}(x,u).

Moreover,
f
is excellent if and only if
\sigma^{k}f
is excellent.

Definition 2.7

(Equivalence)
.

We say that two generating functions
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
and
g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{m}\to\mathbf{R}

are
equivalent
if there exists a diffeomorphism
\psi:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to M\times\mathbf{R}^{m}

fibered over
M
such that
g\circ\psi=f
(note that this implies
n=m
).

Definition 2.8

(Stable equivalence)
.

We say that two generating functions
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
and
g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{m}\to\mathbf{R}
are
stably equivalent
if there exists
k,l\in\mathbf{N}
such that
\sigma^{k}f

and
\sigma^{l}g
are equivalent (note that this implies
n-m
is even).
We sometimes write isomorphic to mean stably equivalent.

Stably equivalent excellent generating functions generate the same Legendrian submanifold.

The following celebrated result shows that generating function persist under Legendrian isotopy.

Theorem 2.9

(Chekanov, Chaperon-Théret)
.

Let
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
be an excellent generating function for a Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda
.
Let
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be a compactly supported Legendrian isotopy starting at
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda
.
Then there exists an excellent generating function
f:M\times[0,1]\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}^{2k}\to\mathbf{R}
over
M\times[0,1]

such that
f_{0}=\sigma^{k}f
and for each
t\in[0,1]
, the restriction
f_{t}
to
M\times\{t\}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}^{2k}
is an excellent generating function for
\Lambda_{t}
.

2.2
Semi-local generating functions

Definition 2.10

(semi-local generating function, version 1)
.

A
semi-local generating function
over
M
is an open set
U\lx@nobreakspace\subset M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}

and a function
f:U\to\mathbf{R}
(not necessarily tame) such that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}:U\to\mathbf{R}^{n}

is transverse to
0
and
\Sigma_{f}=(\frac{\partial f}{\partial v})^{-1}(0)\to M
is proper.
It is excellent if
i_{f}:\Sigma_{f}\to J^{1}M
is an embedding.

The germ of
f
near
\Sigma_{f}
can always be extended to a fiberwise tame function
\tilde{f}:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

in order to obtain a generating function such that
\Lambda_{f}=\Lambda_{\tilde{f}}
near
\Lambda_{f}
. Indeed
we may take a generic function
\tilde{f}
which satisfies
\tilde{f}(x,v)=\|v\|^{2}
at infinity and

\tilde{f}(x,v)=f(x,v)
near
\Sigma_{f}
.
This suggests the following variant, which we take as our official definition.

Definition 2.11

(semi-local generating function, version 2)
.

A
semi-local generating function
over
M
is a couple
(f,\Sigma)
where
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

is a generating function (hence fiberwise tame) and
\Sigma\subset\Sigma_{f}
is a closed and open subset.
It is excellent if
i_{f}:\Sigma\to J^{1}M
is an embedding.

Given such
(f,\Sigma)
, there is an open set
U\subset M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that
\Sigma_{f}\cap U=\Sigma

and the restriction of
f
to
U
is a semi-local generating function in the sense of Definition
2.10
.

Definition 2.12
.

Two semi-local generating functions
(f,\Sigma),(g,\Sigma^{\prime})
are
semi-locally equivalent
if there exists
a fibered diffeomorphism
\psi:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that
\psi(\Sigma)=\Sigma^{\prime}

and
g\circ\psi=f
near
\Sigma
.

In particular, this equivalence relation identifies semi-local generating functions with their germs
along their singular set and the two versions above agree at the level of equivalence classes.

Definition 2.13
.

Two semi-local generating functions
(f,\Sigma)
and
(g,\Sigma^{\prime})
over
M
are
stably semi-locally equivalent

if
(\sigma^{k}f,\Sigma)
is semi-locally equivalent to
(\sigma^{l}g,\Sigma^{\prime})
for some
k,l\in\mathbf{N}
. We sometimes write
isomorphic to mean semi-locally stably equivalent.

In fact, if the fibered diffeomorphism
\psi
in Definition

is only defined between neighborhoods

and

of
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
respectively (that would be the natural equivalence relation following Definition

),
then, after stabilization, it can be extended to a global fibered diffeomorphism of

.
Indeed the restriction of

to

is fibered isotopic to the inclusion provided

is large enough
and moreover we can arrange the framing by further stabilization so that

is fibered isotopic
to the identity map in a tubular neighborhood of

.

Of course a genuine generating function

can be considered as a semi-local generating function
(f,\Sigma)
with
\Sigma=\Sigma_{f}

and stably equivalent generating functions induce semi-locally stably equivalent semi-local generating functions.

Recall that any compact Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
(or more generally a compact Lagrangian immersion

L\to T^{*}M
) has a well-defined (up to homotopy)
stable Lagrangian Gauss map

\gamma_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\to U/O

which records how the tangent spaces
T_{x}\Lambda
(stably) turn with respect to the vertical distribution (fibers of
J^{1}M\to J^{0}M
).
Giroux and Latour observed that this map is null-homotopic if
\Lambda
admits a semi-local generating function,
and proved the converse. They moreover showed that semi-local generating functions for
\Lambda
can be classified up to stable semi-local equivalence
by homotopy classes of null-homotopies of
\gamma_{\Lambda}
. The latter set can be identified (non-canonically) with

[L,\mathbf{Z}\times BO]
in view of the Bott periodicity homotopy equivalence
\Omega(U/O)\simeq\mathbf{Z}\times BO
.

Theorem 2.14

(Giroux
[
18
]
, Latour
[
32
]
)
.

Let

be a manifold and
\Lambda\subset J^{1}(M)
a closed Legendrian submanifold.

1.

\Lambda
admits an excellent semi-local generating function if and only if the stable Gauss map

\Lambda\to U/O
is null-homotopic.

2.

There is a free and transitive action of the group of (unbased) homotopy classes
of maps
[\Lambda,\mathbf{Z}\times BO]
on the set of stable semi-local equivalence classes of excellent semi-local generating
functions for
\Lambda
.

A class in
[L,\mathbf{Z}\times BO]
acts by stabilizing a generating function
f:U\to\mathbf{R}

(where

is a tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma_{f}\simeq L
) by a fiberwise non-degenerate quadratic form
q:U\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

(the
\mathbf{Z}
-factor corresponding to the fiberwise signature of

and the

-factor corresponding to the fiberwise negative eigenspace of

).

Proposition 2.22
.

The group
\mathcal{G}(M)
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathrm{Map}(M,O)
where

is the infinite orthogonal group.

Proof.

This follows from Alexander’s trick: any fibered diffeomorphism

can be linearized by the formula
f(x,tv)/t

and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(x,0)v
for

.
∎

We are interested in the homotopy type of
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
. The connected components correspond
to the usual equivalence classes under stabilizations and fibered diffeomorphisms.

Proposition 2.23
.

Let
M
be a manifold and
\Lambda
a closed Legendrian submanifold of
J^{1}(M)
.
Two generating functions
f_{0},f_{1}\in\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}_{0}
are stably equivalent if and only if they lie
in the same connected component of
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
is a Kan complex,
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are in the same connected component if and only if there
is an edge joining them, namely a generating function
f:M\times\lx@nobreakspace[0,1]\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

such that
f(x,0,v)=f_{0}(x,v)
and
f(x,1,v)=f_{1}(x,v)
.
If we have such a function
f=(f_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
, we apply Moser’s trick and look for a fibered isotopy
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}

generated by a vector field
X_{t}=a_{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial v}
such that
f_{t}\circ\varphi_{t}=f_{0}
. This translates into the equation

\frac{df_{t}}{dt}+a_{t}\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial v}=0.

Outside of a compact set of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
this can be solved using the tameness property (see Proposition
A.9
).
Since each
f_{t}
generates the same Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda
, the singular sets
\Sigma_{f_{t}}

move by a fibered isotopy (explicitly
\Sigma_{f_{t}}=i_{f_{t}}^{-1}\circ i_{f_{0}}(\Sigma_{f_{0}}))
. By extending this fibered isotopy
we are reduced to the case where
\Sigma_{f_{t}}=\Sigma_{f_{0}}
for all
t
. Now the function
\frac{df_{t}}{dt}
vanishes
along
\Sigma_{f_{0}}\times[0,1]
because all
f_{t}
generate the same Legendrian submanifold, and
\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial v}

vanish transversely along
\Sigma_{f_{0}}
, so by Hadamard’s division lemma we can find the required vector field
X_{t}
.

Conversely if
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are equivalent, atfer stabilization there is a fibered diffeomorphism
\varphi_{1}

such that
f_{1}\circ\varphi_{1}=f_{0}
. If
\varphi_{1}
is isotopic to the identity map, namely if there is an edge

\varphi_{t}
in
\mathcal{G}(M)_{1}
joining
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{id}
, we can set
f_{t}=f_{0}\circ\varphi_{t}^{-1}

to obtain an edge in
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
joining
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
. We claim that
\varphi_{1}
can be made isotopic to the identity
by stabilization as follows. First, we can assume that
\varphi_{1}(0)=0
and then the Alexander trick:

\varphi_{t}(x,v)=\frac{\varphi_{1}(x,tv)}{t}

allows us to reduce
\varphi_{1}
to its differential at the origin
\varphi_{0}(x,v)=d_{(x,0)}\varphi_{1}(0,v)
.
Now there is a smooth family of linear automorphism
(B_{x})_{x\in M}:\mathbf{R}^{2k}\to\mathbf{R}^{2k}
preserving the quadratic form

h^{k}
and such that the family
(\varphi_{0})_{x}\oplus B_{x}
is homotopic to the constant family equal to the identity map of
\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}^{2k}
.
Hence
\sigma^{k}f_{0}
and
\sigma^{k}f_{1}
are equivalent by the fibered diffeomorphism
\varphi_{1}\oplus B
which is isotopic
to the identity as desired.
∎

There are natural (simplicial) maps

\mathrm{GF}(M)\to\mathcal{L}(M)\quad\text{ and }\mathrm{GF}(M)^{*}\to\mathcal{L}(M)^{*}

which map an excellent generating function to the Legendrian submanifold that it generates.
The persistence of generating functions under Legendrian isotopy translates into the following fibration statement.

Theorem 2.24

(Chekanov, Chaperon-Théret)
.

Let
M
be a manifold without boundary. The map
\mathrm{GF}(M)^{*}\to\mathcal{L}(M)^{*}
is a Kan fibration onto its image.
In particular, if
\Lambda\subset J^{1}(M\times[0,1])
is
a compact Legendrian cobordism which is the trace of a Legendrian isotopy
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
,
then restriction at
t=0
induces a homotopy equivalence

\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M\times[0,1])\to\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda_{0}}(M).

The map
\mathrm{GF}(M)^{*}\to\mathcal{L}(M)^{*}
is typically not surjective: its image consists of Legendrian submanifolds
that admit tame generating functions, and e.g. any stabilized Legendrian does not.
The fiber over a given
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M

is
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
, its homotopy type is thus invariant under Legendrian isotopy
of
\Lambda
.

Remark 2.25
.

Generating functions in general do not extend over Legendrian cobordisms that are not induced by a Legendrian isotopy
and hence
\mathrm{GF}(M)\to\mathcal{L}(M)
is not a fibration.

Two elements

Proposition 2.30
.

Let
M
be a manifold and
\Lambda
the empty Legendrian submanifold of
J^{1}(M)
. Then
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
is contractible.

Proof.

The group
\mathcal{G}(M)
acts transitively on
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
. Indeed for any function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}

generating the empty Legendrian,

is fiberwise tame without any fiberwise critical points, so
(p,f):M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to M\times\mathbf{R}
is a fibration. Its fibers are manifolds which become diffeomorphic to
\mathbf{R}^{n}
after taking a product by
\mathbf{R}
, and hence after stabilization they are diffeomorphic to

\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
and we obtain that

is stably equivalent to the projection
M\times\mathbf{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
.

The stabilizer consists of fibered diffeomorphisms of
M\times\mathbf{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbf{R}

which preserve the projection to
\mathbf{R}
. This subgroup is stably homotopy equivalent to the whole
\mathcal{G}(M)
as the inclusion
O(n-1)\to O(n)

becomes an equivalence in the colimit

. Hence we obtain the contractibility of
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
.
∎

3.1
Recollections on sheaves

For a topological space
X
and stable presentable symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, we write
\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathcal{C})
for sheaves in
X
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}
.
The basic operations of sheaf theory function well in this context
[
52
]
, as do, if
\mathcal{C}
is compactly generated, the microlocal considerations
[
42
]
. When
\mathcal{C}
is clear from context or fixed and irrelevant, we omit it from the notation.

For the present article, the main relevant case is when
\mathcal{C}
is the
(\infty-)
category of spectra
Sp
. There are various explicit descriptions of this category; modern foundations are given via an abstract characterization in
[
36
, Chap. 1]
(making precise the notion that a spectrum is a generalized homology theory). Let us recall some main properties. There is a map from the category of pointed spaces
\mathcal{S}
, sometimes denoted
\Sigma^{\infty}:\mathcal{S}\to Sp
. We omit this notation: if we write a pointed space when a spectrum is expected, we mean to apply this map; for an unpointed space, we mean to first add a disjoint basepoint, and then apply this map. The shift is given by
X[-1]:=S^{1}\wedge X
; if we write
\mathbf{S}
for the zero sphere (with some chosen basepoint), then
\mathbf{S}[-n]=S^{n}
.
For
X\to Y
, the mapping cone provides an exact triangle
X\to Y\to\mathrm{Cone}(X\to Y)\xrightarrow{[-1]}
; in particular, when
X\subset Y
, then
X\to Y\to Y/X\xrightarrow{[-1]}
. When
X\subset Y\subset Z
, one has (from the octahedron)
Y/X\to Z/X\to Z/Y\xrightarrow{[-1]}
.
There is a symmetric monoidal structure which descends from the smash product of pointed spaces, and has unit given by the zero-sphere
\mathbf{S}
; and there is an internal Hom. Whenever we write Hom of spectra, we mean the internal Hom.

For the constant sheaf of sphere spectra
\mathbf{S}_{X}
on a space
X
, one has, essentially by definition,
\Gamma(X,\mathbf{S}_{X})=\Hom(X,\mathbf{S})
. More generally, for a map
f:X\to Y
and open set
U\subset Y
,
one has

(f_{*}\mathbf{S}_{X})(U)=\Hom(f^{-1}(U),\mathbf{S})
.
This dualization is the price of using sheaves rather than cosheaves, but loses no information if
X
is a finite CW complex. Indeed, these are well known to be dualizable,

X\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom(\Hom(X,\mathbf{S}),\mathbf{S})
, as follows from the fact that, by definition, a finite CW complex admits a finite filtration whose associated graded pieces are pointed spheres.

We recall the notion of microsupport: for a sheaf
F\in\mathrm{Sh}(M)
, there is a conical co-isotropic locus
ss(F)\subset T^{*}M
measuring the failure of propagation of sections, as developped in
[
29
]
. For conic subsets
\Lambda\subset T^{*}M
, we write
\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(M)
for the full subcategory of sheaves whose microsupport is contained in
\Lambda
. Similarly, for
\Lambda\subset S^{*}M
, we write
\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(M)
for the sheaves
F
such that
ss(F)\cap S^{*}M\subset\Lambda
.

The following crucial result is the sheaf counterpart of Theorem
2.9
:

Theorem 3.1

(Guillermou-Kashiwara-Schapira
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda\subset S^{*}(M\times[0,1])
be a compact Legendrian cobordism that is the trace of
a Legendrian isotopy
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
.

Let
F_{0}\in\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda_{0}}(M;\mathbf{k})
. Then there is
F\in\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(M\times[0,1])
and an isomorphism
F|_{M\times\{0\}}\simeq F_{0}
.
Moreover, if we have
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(M\times[0,1])
and an isomorphism
u_{0}:F|_{M\times\{0\}}\to F^{\prime}|_{M\times\{0\}}

then the isomorphism extends over
M\times[0,1]
.

To deal with Legendrian submanifolds of the jet bundle
J^{1}M
, we embed
J^{1}M
as the open subset of
S^{*}(M\times\mathbf{R})

consisting of covectors which are positive on the
\mathbf{R}
-factor.
In this context it is often convenient, following Tamarkin
[
47
,
20
]
, to consider the category

\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M):=\mathrm{Sh}(M\times\mathbf{R})/\mathrm{Sh}_{T^{*}M\times T^{-}\mathbf{R}}(M\times\mathbf{R}),

where
T^{-}\mathbf{R}
is the locus of nonpositive covectors. This quotient admits an adjoint embedding it as the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Sh}(M\times\mathbf{R})
on objects with non-negative microsupport and vanishing stalks at
M\times-\infty
.

3.2
Microsheaves

Let us recall the notion of microlocalization. Following
[
29
, Chap. 6]
, consider the following
presheaf of (
\mathcal{C}
-linear) categories on
T^{*}M
:

\mu\mathrm{Sh}^{pre}(U):=\mathrm{Sh}(M)/\{F\,|\,ss(F)\subset T^{*}M\setminus U\}.

We write
\mu\mathrm{Sh}
for its sheafification. For
\Lambda\subset T^{*}M
or
\Lambda\subset S^{*}M
, one has the corresponding subsheaf of full subcategories

\mu\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}
on objects (micro)supported in
\Lambda
; this sheaf of categories is (the pushforward of a sheaf) supported on
\Lambda
.

It is immediate from the definition that there is a natural map

\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(M)\to\mu\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(\Lambda)
. This map is not generally surjective.

It is shown already in
[
29
]
that at a smooth Lagrangian/Legendrian point
p\in\Lambda
, there is a non-canonical isomorphism of
(\mu\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda})_{p}
with the coefficient category
\mathcal{C}

3.3
Spaces of sheaves

Let us recall that we may form a topological space from an ordinary (not
\infty
) category, called its nerve, as follows: the objects become 0-simplices, the 1-morphisms become 1-simplices, compositions become 2-simplices, etc. For
(\infty,1)
-categories (i.e. all categories we consider here), the description is simpler: such a category is by definition a simplicial set with certain properties; the nerve is just the underlying simplicial set. Given an
(\infty,1)
-category
\mathcal{C}
, we write
\mathcal{C}^{*}
for the
(\infty,0)
-category obtained by forgetting all non-invertible morphisms, and understand it as the homotopy type of a space.

When working with categories of sheaves, microsheaves, etc., it is often convenient to use a more internal description. For specificity let us give it for
\mathrm{Sh}(M)^{*}
: the
n
-simplices are sheaves on
\Delta^{n}\times M
whose microsupport is contained in
T^{*}_{0}(\Delta^{n})\times T^{*}M
, which hence are locally constant in the
\Delta
direction. Thus 0-simplices are just sheaves on
M
; and from a 1-simplex, you can reconstruct a morphism by parallel transport in the
\Delta^{1}=[0,1]
direction.

This identification allows many theorems which are originally stated for e.g. isomorphism classes of sheaves to be promoted to assertions about the homotopy type
\mathrm{Sh}(M)^{*}
by simply applying the original theorem as stated to all
M\times\Delta^{n}
. (See e.g.
[
35
]
for discussions of a similar nature.)
In particular, we have the following:

Theorem 3.5
.

(Space level version of
[
26
]
.)
Let
\Lambda
be a closed Legendrian submanifold of
J^{1}(M)
and
\gamma_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\to U/O
its stable Lagrangian Gauss map.
The space
\mu\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
is non-empty if and only if the composition
J\circ\gamma_{\Lambda}:L\to B(\mathbf{Z}\times BG)
is null-homotopic.
If non-empty the space
\mu\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
is a torsor over the space
\mathrm{Map}(L,\mathbf{Z}\times BG)
.

Theorem 3.6
.

(Space level version of
[
19
]
.)
The map
\mathrm{Sh}(M)^{*}\to\mathcal{L}(M)^{*}
is a Kan fibration. In particular, if

a compact Legendrian cobordism which is the trace of a Legendrian isotopy

\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(M\times[0,1])^{*}\to\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda_{0}}(M)^{*}.

4
The doubling trick

4.1
Doubling and reduction

Consider the function
D:\mathbf{R}^{2}\to\mathbf{R}
defined by

D(t,w)=w^{3}-3tw=w(w^{2}-3t)

It has a single critical point at
t=0,w=0
of index
1
and with critical value
0
.
In fact it is globaly conjugate to the quadratic form
h(u,v)=uv
via the diffeomorphism of
\mathbf{R}^{2}

given by

u=w^{2}-3t,v=w.

In particular,
D
is a tame function.

Now let us think of
D
as a family of functions
(D_{t})_{t\in\mathbf{R}}:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
. We observe that
\frac{\partial D}{\partial w}(t,w)=3(w^{2}-t)
vanishes transversely
along the parabola
\Sigma_{D}=\{w^{2}=t\}
. So
D_{t}
has no critical points for
t<0

and two critical points for
t>0
with critical values
\pm 2t^{3/2}
. Denote
\Lambda_{D}\subset J^{1}(\mathbf{R})

the Legendrian submanifold generated by
D
. In order for
D
to be a genuine generating function for
\Lambda_{D}

we need to check the tameness condition.

Lemma 4.1
.

D
is a fiberwise tame function (with respect to the projection
p:(t,w)\mapsto t
).

Proof.

The definition of fiberwise tameness is readily checked with any function
a:\mathbf{R}\to(0,+\infty)
such that
a(t)>2t^{3/2}
for
t>0
and
A=\{(t,w),|D(t,w)|\leq a(t)\}
.
∎

Given a Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset J^{1}(M)
, we can form a product Legendrian

\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}\subset J^{1}(M\times\mathbf{R})
whose reduction under the projection
M\times\mathbf{R}\to M

is
\Lambda
and whose reduction at slices
\{t=t_{0}>0\}
are
2
-copies of
\Lambda
(namely
\Lambda
shifted by
\pm 2t_{0}^{3/2}
).

Given a generating function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
for
\Lambda\subset J^{1}(M)
, the function
g:M\times\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
given by

g(x,t;u,w)=f(x;u)+D(t,w)

is fiberwise tame (with respect to the projection to
M\times\mathbf{R}
) per Lemma
A.7
, and generates
\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}\subset J^{1}(M\times\mathbf{R})
.
This defines a (simplicial) map

\mathcal{D}:\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}\to\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}.

Conversely if we have a generating function
g:M\times\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
for
\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}\subset J^{1}(M\times\mathbf{R})
, we get a generating function
f

for
\Lambda
by reduction (i.e. viewing the
t
-coordinate as an auxiliary variable):

f(x;t,u)=g(x,t;u).

The tameness condition for
f
follows from Lemma
A.6
applied to the fibration
q:M\times\mathbf{R}\to M
given by
q(x,t)=x
.
Indeed the properness assumptions are met: the singular set of
g
properly maps to
M\times\mathbf{R}
(under the map
(x,f)
) and the singular set of
f
is the intersection of the singular set of
g
with the slice
\{t=0\}
and hence properly maps to
M
.

So we have a (simplicial) map

\mathcal{R}:\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}\to\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}.

The composition
\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{D}
transforms a generating function
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
for
\Lambda
into another one
F:M\times\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}
given by

F(x;t,u,w)=f(x;u)+D(t,w)

which is equivalent to the stabilization
\sigma f
since
D
is conjugate to the quadratic form
h=uv
. Hence we have:

Proposition 4.2
.

The composition
\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{D}:\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}\to\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
is homotopic to the identity map.

The above discussion is equally valid for semi-local generating functions, so we also obtain:

Proposition 4.3
.

The composition
\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{D}:\mu\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}\to\mu\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
is homotopic to the identity map.

4.2
Rotation

We want to show that the other composition
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{R}
is also homotopic to the identity. In view of Proposition
4.2

it is equivalent to show
\mathcal{R}
is a homotopy equivalence.
For this purpose we use a rotation of

of angle

in order to map the cusped Legendrian
\{z=\pm 2t^{3}/2\}

\{z=3t^{3}\}

the corresponding

For

If

(x,s,v)\mapsto(x,s)

f(x,s,v)+ut
is tame with respect to the projection
(x,s,v,u,t)\mapsto(x,s,u,t)

by Lemma

and then
h
is fiberwise tame with respect to
(x,t,v,s)\mapsto(x,t,s)
by Lemma
A.5
using pullback along the diagonal

.
Finally if
f

\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}
, then

is fiberwise tame with respect to
(x,t,v,s)\mapsto(x,t)
by Lemma
A.6

(the properness conditions are met) and generates

Hence we obtain a map

\mathcal{A}:\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}\to\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{C}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}.

Similarly we define

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}:\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{C}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}\to\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}

by the formula

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(h)(x,t,v,s)=f(x,s,v)-st.

Proposition 4.4
.

The compositions
\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{A}
are homotopic to the identity. In particular,
\mathcal{A}
is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

Given a function
f(x,t;v)\in\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}
, the function
f^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{A}(f)
writes:

f^{\prime}(x,t;v,s,u)=f(x,s,v)+su-ut.

We make a first change of variables
s^{\prime}=s-t
and use Hadamard’s division Lemma to write

f(x,s^{\prime}+t,v)=f(x,t,v)+s^{\prime}g(x,t,v,s^{\prime})
for some smooth function
g
, so that

f^{\prime\prime}(x,t;v,s^{\prime},u)=f(x,t,v)+s^{\prime}g(x,t,v,s^{\prime})+s^{\prime}u.

Then we set
u^{\prime}=u+g(x,t,v,s^{\prime})
and obtain

f^{\prime\prime\prime}(x,t;v,s^{\prime},u^{\prime})=f(x,t,v)+s^{\prime}u^{\prime}

which is a stabilization of
f
. We can carry out all steps by a continuous
deformation from
f^{\prime}
to
f^{\prime\prime\prime}
, and moreover this works parametrically, so
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{A}

is homotopic to the identity. The proof works verbatim for
\mathcal{A}\circ\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.
∎

Next we define

\mathcal{I}_{0}:\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{C}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}\to\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}

Proposition 4.5

We have
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{I}_{0}\circ\mathcal{A}

Proof.

It is a direct computation:

\mathcal{I}_{0}\circ\mathcal{A}(f)(x,s,v)=\mathcal{A}(f)(x,0,s,v)=f(x,s,v)+0\times s=f(x,s,v).

∎

.

The map
\mathcal{I}_{0}

Proof.

Since
\Lambda_{C}
is graphical over
\mathbf{R}
, all functions
(x,v)\mapsto f(x,t,v)-C(t)
generate the same Legendrian
\Lambda

and thus

can be identified with the space of maps from
\mathbf{R}
to
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
, and hence

∎

is (multi-)graphical over
\mathbf{R}
, and hence a semi-local generating function defined
for
t<\epsilon
with
\epsilon>0
can be extended to the whole
\mathbf{R}
in a homotopically unique way. So setting

\mathrm{GF}^{\leq 0}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}:=\colim_{\epsilon>0}\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times(-\infty,\epsilon))^{*},

and similarly for the semi-local version with
\mathrm{GF}
replaced by
\mu\mathrm{GF}
,
we have:

Proposition 4.9
.

The restriction map
\mu\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}\to\mu\mathrm{GF}^{\leq 0}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}
is a homotopy equivalence.

Note that for a given closed Legendrian
\Lambda
, the above colimit is actually achieved for some small enough
\epsilon>0
.

4.3
From semi-local to global via doubling

We now turn to the main result of this section which allows to pass from semi-local generating functions
for a Legendrian
\Lambda
to genuine generating functions for
\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}
over
M\times(-\infty,\epsilon)
for small
\epsilon>0
.

Theorem 4.10
.

The map
\mathrm{GF}^{\leq 0}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}\to\mu\mathrm{GF}^{\leq 0}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}
is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

We first consider the case where
\Lambda
is empty. In this case, the right hand side is reduced to a single point so we need to prove
that the left hand side is contractible.
Since
\Lambda
is empty, the restriction map at
t=0
(or at any
t=t_{0}
for
t_{0}\leq 0
)
\mathrm{GF}^{\leq 0}_{\emptyset}(M\times\mathbf{R})\to\mathrm{GF}_{\emptyset}(M)

is a homotopy equivalence. The result now follows from Proposition
2.30
.

Another extreme case is when
M
is a single point and
\Lambda
consists of finitely many points
z_{1},\dots,z_{k}
in
\mathbf{R}=J^{1}(*)
.
Let
\phi\in\mu\mathrm{GF}^{\leq 0}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(\mathbf{R})^{*}
, this is a germ of function
\phi:\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}

near finitely many points
(v_{1},0),\dots,(v_{k},0)
. For each of these points, we pick an embedding
\psi_{i}:D^{n-1}\times[z_{i}-a,z_{i}+a]\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\to\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}
whose image is contained in the domain of definition of
\phi
(
a>0
and
\epsilon>0
can be taken arbitrarily small) and satisfies:

\psi_{i}(0,z_{i},0)=(v_{i},0)
,
\psi_{i}(x,z,t)=(j(x,z,t),t)
,
\phi(\psi_{i}(x,z,-\epsilon))=z
,
\phi(\psi_{i}(x,z_{i}\pm a,t))=z_{i}\pm a

and
\phi(\psi_{i}(x,z,t))=z
for
|x|=1
. Denote
K_{i}
the image of
\psi_{i}
.
We need to prove that the space of functions
f:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
which coincide with
\phi
on each
K_{i}
is highly connected (thus contractible in the limit
n\to\infty
).
We claim that restriction at
t=-\epsilon
yields a homotopy equivalence between this space and the space of functions

f:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
which have no critical points and agree with
\phi
on
K_{i}\cap\{t=-\epsilon\}
, namely
f(\psi(x,z,-\epsilon))=z
.
Indeed by a suitable retraction of
\{t\leq\epsilon\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}
onto
\{t\leq-\epsilon\}\cup\cup_{i}K_{i}
, we can deform
such functions until they coincide on
\{t\leq\epsilon\}
provided they already coincide in
\{t\leq-\epsilon\}\cup\cup_{i}K_{i}
.
Finally we need to argue that the space
F
of functions
\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
without critical points which coincide with the projection
D^{n-1}\times[z_{i}-a,z_{i}+a]\to[z_{i}-a,z_{i}+a]
in finitely many such disjoint cylinders embedded in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, is highly connected when
n
goes to infinity.
We claim first that this space is equivalent to the space of functions without critical points
f
such that
\nabla f(v_{i})=dz
.
Indeed, if
\nabla f(v_{i})=dz
, we can deform slightly
f
so that
f=z
near
v_{i}
(the center of
K_{i}
), then by dilating
K_{i}
we can arrange
f=z

on the whole
K_{i}
. From the space
E
of all functions
\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
without critical points, the collection of
\nabla f(v_{i})
thus defines a map to
(\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})^{k}\simeq(S^{n-1})^{k}
whose fiber is homotopy equivalent to the space
F
.
The space
(S^{n-1})^{k}
is
(n-2)
-connected and
E
is also highly connected (see Proposition
2.30
), therefore
F
is highly connected.

We now turn to the general case. We consider the fiber over some semi-local generating function
\phi
for
\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}
,
namely the space of generating functions
f:M\times\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
for
\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}
over
M\times(-\infty,\epsilon)

which agree with
\phi
near
\Sigma_{\phi}=\Sigma_{f}
. Denote
\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma_{\phi}\cap\{t=0\}
and
\phi_{0}
the restriction of
\phi
to
\Sigma_{0}
.
We pick a tubular neighborhood
U\times[-a,a]
of
\Sigma_{0}
(denote
r:U\to\Sigma_{0}
the corresponding projection) and
an embedding
\psi:U\times[-a,a]\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\to M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\times\mathbf{R}
such that

\psi
is the identity on
\Sigma_{0}\times\{0\}
,
\psi(u,z,t)=(*,*,t)
,
f(\psi(u,z,-\epsilon))=\phi_{0}(r(u))+z
and
f(\psi(u,z,t))=\phi_{0}(r(u))+z
for
u\in\partial U
.
As in the previous case, the function
f
is then determined up to contractible choice by its restriction to
t=-\epsilon
. The gradient
\nabla f

along
\Sigma_{0}
defines a map from the space of functions
f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
fiberwise without critical points to
the space
\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma_{0},S^{n-1})
, both of which are highly connected, with fiber homotopy equivalent to the space we are considering, so the claim follows.
∎

4.4
Doubling for sheaves

We turn to the sheaf setting, where the doubling trick was first employed by Guillermou in
[
21
]
(see also the exposition in
[
37
, Sec. 7]
).

The construction is guided by the fact that it should be compatible with the map
\mathbf{s}
from generating function to sheaves.
Let
D:\mathbf{R}^{2}\to\mathbf{R}
,
D(t,w)=w^{3}-3tw
be the function from Subsection
4.1
. As there, we regard it as a generating function over the
t
line, so we have some
\mathbf{s}(D)\in\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(\mathbf{R})
. After embedding
\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(\mathbf{R}_{t})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sh}(\mathbf{R}_{t}\times\mathbf{R}_{z})
,
\mathbf{s}(D)
is a sheaf on
\mathbf{R}^{2}
with positive microsupport in the
z
direction.

Recall that, in general, there is a product
\star\,:\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M)\times\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(N)\to\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M\times N)
given, after the embeddings
\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M)\to\mathrm{Sh}(M\times\mathbf{R})
etc, by external tensor product in the
M,N
factors and convolution in the
\mathbf{R}
factors.

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}:\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M)

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M\times\mathbf{R})

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\mapsto F\star\mathbf{s}(D)

The standard estimate on pushforwards of microsupport shows that, as for generating functions,
ss(F\star\mathbf{s}(D))\subset ss(F)\times\Lambda_{D}
.
So
\mathcal{D}
restricts to a map

\mathcal{D}:\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}\to\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}.

We define

\mathcal{R}:\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M\times\mathbf{R}_{t})\to\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M)
as pushforward along the
\mathbf{R}_{t}
factor. The following are straightforward 6-functor manipulations:

Lemma 4.11
.

\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{D}=1

To show that
\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{R}
is also the identity, we can use the rotation operator
\mathcal{A}

as we did for generating functions. Consider the function
A:\mathbf{R}^{2}\to\mathbf{R}
defined by
A(s,t)=st

and
\mathbf{s}(a)\in\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R})
(it is a quantization of the rotation of angle
\pi/2
of
\mathbf{R}^{2}
).
This kernel acts on
\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M\times\mathbf{R})
and defines a map

\mathcal{A}:\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}\to\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{C}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}

Using the inverse rotation, we see that
\mathcal{A}
is an equivalence.

Finally we have the restriction at
t=0
operator
\mathcal{I}_{0}:\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M\times\mathbf{R})\to\mathrm{Sh}^{+}(M)
.

Lemma 4.12
.

We have

\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{I}_{0}.

Moreover
\mathcal{I}_{0}
induces an equivalence
\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{C}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}\to\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}

since
\Lambda_{C}
is graphical over
\mathbf{R}
.
So we obtain

Theorem 4.13
.

The maps
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{D}
are inverse homotopy equivalences between
\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}(M\times\mathbf{R})^{*}
and
\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
.

Lemma 4.14
.

The map
\mathbf{s}
intertwines the
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{D}
for generating functions with those for sheaves.

Proof.

For
\mathcal{D}
, this is a special case of Lemma
3.13
. For
\mathcal{R}
, this follows from functoriality of pushforward.
∎

Finally, the sheaf counterpart of Theorem
4.10
is the following result of Guillermou.

Theorem 4.15
.

(
[
21
]
, see also
[
37
]
for this formulation)
Let
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
be a Legendrian. Then for all sufficiently small

and any

, the composition of the following maps is an equivalence:

\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D}}^{+}(M\times(-\infty,\epsilon))^{*}\to\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda_{\pm\delta}}(M\times\delta)^{*}\to\mu\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}.

Here, the first map is the restriction to the slice over

, and
\Lambda_{\pm\delta}
are the two copies of

in the symplectic reduction of

along this slice. The second map is the microlocalization along one of these copies.

Legendrian submanifolds without Reeb chords

At this point, we note the following application, which does not require

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\Lambda\subset J^{1}M
be a closed Legendrian which is contact isotopic to a Legendrian submanifold which projects injectively to

.
The maps
\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}\to\mu\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M)^{*}
and

Proof.

The proof is identical in the generating function or sheaf setting, we write it for generating functions.

.
The family
(\Lambda\times\Lambda_{D})_{t}
for

4.8
,

:

∎

Remark 4.18

Combined with the semi-local classification of generating functions from Giroux-Latour (Theorem

5
The crossing problem

5.1
The main statement

For
0\leq k\leq m
, consider the following functions on
\mathbf{R}^{m}
:

z_{k,m}^{+}(x)=1+x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{m-k}^{2}-x_{m-k+1}^{2}-\dots-x_{m}^{2}

and

z_{k,m}^{-}(x)=-z_{k,m}^{+}(x).

Note that
z_{k,m}^{+}-z_{k,m}^{-}
has a single critical point, which is non-degenerate of index
k
.
We write
\Lambda_{k,m}\subset J^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{m})
for the Legendrian given by the union of the jets of the above functions (its front projection is the union of their graphs).
We denote the positive half line as
\mathbf{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
, and write
\Lambda_{k+1,m+1}^{+}
for restriction of
\Lambda_{k+1,m+1}
to the half space
J^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{m}\times\mathbf{R}_{+})
.
Note that slicing
\Lambda_{k+1,m+1}^{+}
by the
x_{m+1}
-coordinate defines a Legendrian regular homotopy (i.e. a path of Legendrian immersions) with a single crossing (self-intersection)
corresponding to the single Reeb chord of
\Lambda_{k,m}
.

Restriction at
x_{m+1}=0
defines maps

\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda_{k+1,m+1}^{+}}(\mathbf{R}^{m+1}_{+})\to\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda_{k,m}}(\mathbf{R}^{m})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda_{k+1,m+1}^{+}}(\mathbf{R}^{m+1}_{+})\to\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda_{k,m}}(\mathbf{R}^{m})

which are compatible with the map
\mathbf{s}
from generating functions to sheaves. The crossing problem refers
to the problem of extending an object (generating function or sheaf) past the crossing, i.e. from
\mathbf{R}^{m}
to
\mathbf{R}^{m+1}_{+}
.
We will define obstructions valued in stable homotopy groups of spheres, the main observation is that
these obstructions are precisely the same on both side (if sheaves are considered over
\mathbf{S}
). In other words we have the following result which is the main goal of this section:

Theorem 5.1
.

The commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{GF}_{L_{k+1,m+1}^{+}}(\mathbf{R}^{m}\times\mathbf{R}_{+})^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda_{k,m}}(\mathbf{R}^{m})^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{L_{k+1,m+1}^{+}}(\mathbf{R}^{m}\times\mathbf{R}_{+};\mathbf{S})^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sh}_{\Lambda_{k,m}}^{+}(\mathbf{R}^{m};\mathbf{S})^{*}.}

(5.2)

is a homotopy pullback square. The fibers of the horizontal maps over an object giving Maslov indices
j
and
i

to
z^{+}_{k,m}
and
z^{-}_{k,m}
respectively is either empty (if some obstruction in the stable homotopy group
\pi_{k+j-i-1}^{S}
is non-vanishing),
or homotopy equivalent to

\Omega^{k+j-i+\infty}S^{\infty}
(if the obstruction vanishes).

For
f_{0}\in\mathrm{GF}_{L_{k,m}}(\mathbf{R}^{m})^{*}
and
F_{0}\in\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{L_{k,m}}(\mathbf{R}^{m})^{*}
, let us write
\mathcal{E}(f_{0})
(respectively
\mathcal{E}(F_{0})
)
for the fiber of the top (resp. bottom) horizontal map in (
5.2
). Theorem
5.1

implies that the map

\mathcal{E}(f_{0})\to\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{s}(f_{0}))

(5.3)

is a homotopy equivalence; in particular,
one is non-empty if and only the other one is non-empty.

Example 5.4
.

In the case where
k=0
and
j=i
, Theorem
5.1
says that the extension is always possible since
\pi^{S}_{k+j-i-1}=\pi^{S}_{-1}=0

and the space of extensions (fiber of horizontal maps in (
5.2
)) is

\Omega^{\infty}S^{\infty}

whose
\pi_{0}
is
\mathbf{Z}
: this corresponds to an algebraic count of handleslides.

Example 5.5
.

When
k+j\leq i
, the obstruction lies in

\pi^{S}_{\leq-1}=0
, so the extension is always possible. This recovers the well-known fact that we can always reorder critical points according to their index (and even
in
k
-parametric families if the index difference is at least
k
).

The theorem is a model of the singular moment in a Legendrian regular homotopy. In fact, when combined with the homotopy lifting property for generating functions and sheaves, this model case allows us to deduce the corresponding result
for any Legendrian regular homotopy:

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\Lambda\subset J^{1}(M\times[0,1])
be a compact Legendrian cobordism that is the trace of a Legendrian regular
homotopy
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
with
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
embedded. Then restriction at
t=0
defines a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M\times[0,1])^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda_{0}}(M)^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda}(M\times[0,1];\mathbf{S})^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sh}^{+}_{\Lambda_{0}}(M;\mathbf{S})^{*}.}

(5.7)

which is a homotopy pullback square.

Proof.

We can perturb the Legendrian regular homotopy relative to
t=0
and
t=1
so that it has finitely
many transverse self-crossings arising at distinct parameters
0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{l}<1
. Moreover we can assume
that at each self-intersection, the crossing arises along two branches of
\Lambda_{t}
which are smooth graphs
and no other branches of the front of
\Lambda_{t}
passes through the crossing point. Hence each crossing of
\Lambda_{t_{i}}

for
i=i,\dots,l
can be modeled after the regular homotopy
(\Lambda_{k+1,m+1}^{+})_{x_{m+1}}
for some
integers
k
. Away from a small ball around the crossing point,
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
is a Legendrian isotopy so
Theorem
2.24
and Theorem
3.6
apply. Hence, for small
\epsilon>0
, the fiber of

\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M\times[0,t_{i+1}+\epsilon])\to\mathrm{GF}_{\Lambda}(M\times[0,t_{i}+\epsilon])
is isomorphic to
the corresponding fiber in the model case of
\Lambda_{k+1,m+1}^{+}
, and the result follows by applying
Theorem
5.1
several times.
∎

The remainder of this section gives the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Reduction to surjectivity on

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(r^{l}F)\to\Omega^{l}\mathcal{E}(F_{0})

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

;

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}\mathcal{E}(r^{l}f)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{l}\mathcal{E}(f_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}\mathcal{E}(r^{l}\mathbf{s}(f))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{l}\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{s}(f_{0})).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

(5.12)

Hence Theorem

follows if we can show that
\mathcal{E}(f_{0})\to\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{s}(f_{0}))
is an isomorphism
on
\pi_{0}
(for any

). In fact, injectivity on
\pi_{0}
also follows
from surjectivity on
\pi_{0}
using the following small variation
on the above construction.

Given

.

For any

, the crossing obstruction map

is an isomorphism on

.

Proof.

As argued in the previous subsection, it is enough to show surjectivity on
\pi_{0}
.

The basic point is that
we want to show that certain ascending and descending spheres can be disjoined after an isotopy. Essentially by definition,

\alpha
measures the obstruction for doing this up to stable homotopy, and the stable homotopy can be realized by an isotopy after sufficient stabilization of the generating function. We now explain in more detail.

It is instructive to consider first the case
m=0
. Then

is just a single Morse function, and we are discussing the problem of varying

so that the critical values of two critical points cross.
A necessary and sufficient condition for crossing the critical values is the existence
of a descending sphere of
p
which is disjoint from an ascending sphere of
q
in some intermediate level set
\{f=b\}
, see e.g.
[
6
,
22
]
.
Now the descending sphere

is homotopic to a sphere disjoint from the ascending sphere of

if and only if the composition
S^{j-1}\to\{f=b\}\to\{f\leq b\}
factors up to homotopy through a map
S^{j-1}\to\{f\leq a\}\hookrightarrow\{f\leq b\}
. By construction of
\alpha
, this is true up to stable homotopy iff
\alpha(f)=0
. This stable homotopy can be realized by an actual homotopy if the pair
(\{f\leq b\},\{f\leq a\})
and
\{f\leq a\}
are sufficiently connected. We claim that this can be achieved by sufficient negative stabilization of
f
. Indeed, this is obvious for
(\{f\leq b\},\{f\leq a\})
, as the Morse indices are all shifted upwards. As for
\{f\leq a\}
, observe that the contractible space
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is obtained from
\{f\leq a\}
by attaching cells of index greater than say
l\geq 0
, so
\{f\leq a\}
is at least
(l-2)
-connected, and similarly
\{f-t^{2}\leq a\}
will be at least
(l-1)
-connected.
3
3

3

This is the only place in this article where we use that

is defined on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
as opposed to an arbitrary manifold.

Finally, a homotopy of submanifolds can be realized by an isotopy if the codimension is sufficiently large; we raise the codimenion by positive stabilization.
All in all, vanishing of
\alpha
implies we may cross the critical values after sufficient stabilization. Moreover by construction
the null-homotopy
\alpha(f_{+})
of
\alpha(f)
induced by the constructed extension
f_{+}
is homotopic to
the null-homotopy of
\alpha(f)
that we have used to construct

.

Let us consider the general case. Consider the descending sphere
\phi:S^{j+k-1}\to\{(x,v),f(x,v)=b(x)\}
.
The intersection

is a

; on the complement of this sphere, the map

fibers as

.
Similarly the ascending sphere of the lower critical point is described as a family

. For

,

and

The crossing is possible if and only if there is a disjunction isotopy, namely an isotopy of

which makes it disjoint from
\psi_{x}
and which is fixed for

[
24
, Theorem 4.11]
.

As before,
\alpha(f)
is the obstruction to stable-homotopy-disjoining the ascending and descending spheres

and
\psi

Because of the fibration structure, the existence of such a disjoining homotopy implies the existence of a fiberwise such homotopy. Indeed, let

. Let

be the witness to the homotopy disjoining, with

the original

(along which

is a fibration, which we may therefore lift along

to a fiberwise homotopy-disjoining

(i.e. so that

disjoins

from

).

Finally, as before, by stabilization we may raise the connectivity of the relevant ambient spaces and the codimension of the embedding, hence ensure that the homotopy is realized by an isotopy.
∎

Proof of Theorem
B
.

In the regular homotopy
(\Lambda_{t})_{t>0}
obtained from the doubling trick (see Section
4
),
the crossings are in one-to-one correspondence with the Reeb chords of the original Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda
.
Moreover for a Reeb chord of degree
k
, the corresponding crossing obstruction
\alpha
(see Section
5
)
is valued in
\pi_{k-1}^{S}
and the space of possible crossings is either empty (if
\alpha\neq 0
) or has
\pi_{0}
equal to
\pi_{k}^{S}
.

If
\Lambda
has no Reeb chords of degree larger than
1
, the crossing obstructions are valued in
\pi_{l}^{S}
with

which is the trivial group or
\mathbf{Z}
for

. In the case

, the obstruction is detected by homology with
\mathbf{Z}
-coefficients
and hence, by Theorem

, always vanishes if a sheaf over
\mathbf{Z}
is given. Hence we can always lift from
\mathbf{Z}
to
\mathbf{S}
in this case, i.e.
the map (

) is
0
-connected.

If further
\Lambda
has no Reeb chords of positive degree, then the space of extensions past each crossing is connected
or has
\pi_{0}
equal to
\pi_{0}^{S}
which again is detected by homology with
\mathbf{Z}
-coefficients and hence the lift from
\mathbf{Z}
to
\mathbf{S}
is unique.
More precisely the space of extensions in the case of integer coefficients has

isomorphic to
\mathbf{Z}

and all higher homotopy groups are zero (obstructions are valued in the trivial group

,

Similarly if all Reeb chords have degree at most

Example 7.4

Remark 7.9

The uniqueness of generating functions (up to shift) for the

Theorem 7.16

Let
\Lambda

For any Legendrian link

Proof.

Because of the factorization

We now recall the construction from
[

.

We try and construct a generating function

We now explain how to construct this lifting

At each birth, we simply introduce a new pair of critical points in a small box (

At each crossing of equal Maslov potential, there are two possible extensions of the sheaf

Appendix A
Fiberwise tame functions

Here we collect some properties of fiberwise tame functions.
Recall the following standard definition.

Definition A.1
.

A map of manifolds
f:X\to Y
is a
fibration
if for each
y\in Y
there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
y

and a diffeomorphism
\varphi:f^{-1}(U)\to U\times f^{-1}(y)
such that
f=\pr_{1}\circ\varphi
.

The image of a fibration is open and closed, but we do not require it to be all of
Y
.

Definition A.2
.

A function
f:E\to\mathbf{R}
is
fiberwise tame
with respect to a fibration
p:E\to M
if there is a closed set
K\subset E
and a smooth function
a:M\to(0,+\infty)

such that the projection
K\to M
is proper and the maps

\{e\in E,|f(e|\geq a(p(e))\}\to\{(x,z)\in M\times\mathbf{R},|z|\geq a(x)\},

and

\{e\in E,|f(e|\leq a(p(e))\}\setminus K\to\{(x,z)\in M\times\mathbf{R},|z|\leq a(x)\}

are fibrations.

We denote
\Sigma_{(p,f)}
the set of critical points of
(p,f):E\to M\times\mathbf{R}
or, equivalently, the set of fiberwise critical points of
f
.

Remark A.3
.

The definition appears (at least with
M
a single point) in
[
12
]

under the name "fibration at infinity".

Remark A.4
.

Some properties:

•

The notion of fiberwise tameness is invariant by left composition with fibered diffeomorphism of
M\times\mathbf{R}
and by right composition with fibered diffeomorphisms of
E
.

•

The set
\Sigma_{(p,f)}
maps properly to
M
.

•

If
p:E\to M
is proper any function
f:E\to\mathbf{R}
is fiberwise tame with respect to
p
.

•

The map
(p,f):E\to M\times\mathbf{R}
is a fibration if and only if
f
is fiberwise tame with respect to
p
and
\Sigma_{(p,f)}
is empty.

•

The subset
K
can be taken to be a smooth codimension
0
submanifold with boundary fibered over
M
.

The notion of fiberwise tameness is clearly invariant under pullback:

Lemma A.5
.

If
M^{\prime}\to M
is any map and
f:E\to\mathbf{R}
a fiberwise tame functions with respect to a fibration
p:E\to M
, then
f
restricted to

E\times_{M}M^{\prime}
is also fiberwise tame with respect to the fibration
E\times_{M}M^{\prime}\to M^{\prime}
.

Concerning pushforward, we have the following lemma.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
q:M\to M^{\prime}
be a fibration,
f:E\to\mathbf{R}
be a fiberwise tame function with respect a fibration
p:E\to M
and
p^{\prime}=q\circ p
.
If
\Sigma_{(p,f)}\to M^{\prime}\times\mathbf{R}
and
\Sigma_{(p^{\prime},f)}\to M^{\prime}
are proper then
f
is also fiberwise tame with respect to the fibration
p^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We prove it in the case where
M^{\prime}
is a single point. We have to find a compact codimension
0
submanifold
K\subset M
and
a>0
such that
\{|f|\leq a\}\setminus K\to[-a,a]
and
\{|f|\geq a\}\to\mathbf{R}\setminus(-a,a)
are fibrations.

The fiberwise tameness provides
b:M\to(0,+\infty)
and a codimension
0
subbundle
A\subset E
of
E\to M
such that
\{|f|\leq b\}\setminus A\to\{|z|\leq b\}\subset M\times\mathbf{R}
and
\{|f|\geq b\}\to\{|z|\geq b\}\subset M\times\mathbf{R}
are fibrations.

Since
\Sigma_{(p^{\prime},f)}
is proper over
M^{\prime}
we may find a codimension
0
submanifold
N\subset M
which properly maps to
M^{\prime}
and such that
p(\Sigma_{f})\subset\mathrm{int}(N)
.
Up to increasing
b
on
N
we may assume that it is equal to
a\circ q
on
N
for some function
a:M^{\prime}\to(0,+\infty)
and
b\geq a\circ q
on
M
. We set
K=A\cap p^{-1}(N)
and now check that
f
satisfies the tameness condition with
a
and
K
.

We already know that
\{|f|\leq b\}\setminus A\to\{(m,z)\in M\times\mathbf{R},|z|\leq a(m)\}
is a fibration, we need to take care of the region
A\setminus K
. By assumption
\Sigma_{(p,f)}\to M^{\prime}\times\mathbf{R}
is proper, so
\Sigma_{(p,f)}\cap\{|f|\leq a\}
is contained in
p^{-1}(N^{\prime})
for some codimension
0
submanifold
N
’ containing
N
in its interior and properly mapping to
M^{\prime}
. Above the region
M\setminus N^{\prime}
, the function
f
has no fiberwise critical points on the subbundle
A
, therefore
A\cap p^{-1}(M\setminus N^{\prime})
is fibered over
\{(m^{\prime},z)\in M^{\prime}\times\mathbf{R},|z|\leq a(m^{\prime})\}
. Over the region
N^{\prime}\setminus N
,
the function
f
may have
p
-fiberwise critical points on the subbundle
A
but by assumption it has no
p^{\prime}
-fiberwise critical points, so by properness of
A\cap p^{-1}(N^{\prime}\setminus\mathrm{int}(N))
over
M^{\prime}
, the latter region is also fibered over its image. It follows that
\{|f|\leq a\}\setminus K\to\{(m^{\prime},z)\in M^{\prime}\times\mathbf{R},|z|\leq a(m^{\prime})\}
is a fibration.

We now pick
c>a
and show that
\{a\leq f\leq c\}\to\{(m^{\prime},z),a(m^{\prime})\leq z\leq c(m^{\prime})\}
is a fibration. We already know that
\{b\geq f\leq c\}
is fibered over
\{b\leq z\leq c\}\subset M\times\mathbf{R}
. Since
\Sigma_{p,f}\to M^{\prime}\times\mathbf{R}
is proper, we may find a codimension
0
submanifold
N^{\prime}
of
M
which properly maps to
M^{\prime}
and such that
\Sigma_{p,f}\cap\{a\leq f\leq c\}
is contained in
p^{-1}(N^{\prime})
. Above
M\setminus N^{\prime}
the function
f
has no
p
-fiberwise critical points on the subbundle
A\cap\{a\leq f\leq b\}
so it is a fibration there. Above the region
N^{\prime}
the function
f
has no
p^{\prime}
-fiberwise critical points on
A\cap\{a\leq f\leq c\}
so it is also a fibration there. It follows that
\{a\leq f\leq c\}\to\{(m^{\prime},z),a(m^{\prime})\leq z\leq c(m^{\prime})\}
.
∎

The preceding lemma can be typically used with the map
q
from
M
to a point to guarantee that a fiberwise tame function is also tame as a function of all variables.

Another useful fact is the stability of fiberwise tame functions under addition:

Lemma A.7
.

Let
p:E\to M
and
p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to M^{\prime}
be fibrations and
f:E\to\mathbf{R}
,
f^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to\mathbf{R}
be fiberwise tame functions (with respect to
p
and
p^{\prime}
respectively).
Then the sum
f+f^{\prime}:E\times E^{\prime}\to\mathbf{R}
is also fiberwise tame with respect to the projection
p\times p^{\prime}:E\times E^{\prime}\to M\times M^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By assumption we have,
a,a^{\prime}>0
and proper subbundles
A
and
A^{\prime}
of
E
and
E^{\prime}
respectively
such that
\{|f|\leq a\}\setminus A\to\{|z|\leq a\}
,
\{|f^{\prime}|\leq a^{\prime}\}\setminus A^{\prime}\to\{|z|\leq a^{\prime}\}
,
\{|f|\geq a\}\to\{|z|\geq a\}
and
\{|f^{\prime}|\geq a\}\to\{|z|\geq a^{\prime}\}
are fibrations.
We pick complete fiberwise gradient vector files
X
and
X^{\prime}
for
f
and
f^{\prime}
respectively which
are adapted to
A
and
A
’, namely
X
points inward transversely along
A\cap\{z=-a\}
, outward along
A\cap\{z=a\}
and is tangent to
\partial A
in the remaining parts of
\partial A
.
All fiberwise critical points of
f+f^{\prime}
are contained in
A\times A^{\prime}
, and we may therefore saturate
A\times A^{\prime}
along the flow of
X+X^{\prime}
to obtain a subbundle
B\subset\{|f|\leq a+a^{\prime}\}\subset E\times E^{\prime}

with boundary decomposed in three parts lying respectively in
\{f+f^{\prime}=a+a^{\prime}\}
,
\{f+f^{\prime}=-a-a^{\prime}\}

and the remaining part fibered over
[-a-a^{\prime},a+a^{\prime}]
. Using the flow of
X+X^{\prime}
, we can then check that
f+f^{\prime}
satisfies the fiberwise tameness condition with
B
and
a+a^{\prime}
.
∎

Corollary A.8
.

Let
p:E\to M
and
p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to M
be fibrations and
f:E\to\mathbf{R}
,
f^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to\mathbf{R}
be fiberwise tame functions (with respect to
p
and
p^{\prime}
respectively).
Then the fibered sum
f+f^{\prime}:E\times_{M}E^{\prime}\to\mathbf{R}
is also fiberwise tame with respect to the induced projection
E\times_{M}E^{\prime}\to M
.

Proof.

It follows directly from Lemma
A.7
and Lemma
A.5
using pullback along the diagonal map
M\to M\times M
.
∎

Finally we record the following result which says that a
1
-parameter deformation of a (fiberwise) tame function is equivalent to a compactly supported deformation.

Proposition A.9
.

Let
p:E\to M
be a fibration and
f:E\times[0,1]\to\mathbf{R}
a fiberwise tame function with respect to
p\times\mathrm{id}:E\times[0,1]\to M\times[0,1]
,
then there exists a closed subset
A\subset E
such that
A\to M
is proper and a diffeomorphism
\varphi:E\times[0,1]\to E\times[0,1]

fibered over
M\times[0,1]
such that
f\circ\varphi(x,t)=f(x,0)
for
x\not\in A
.

Proof.

We write
f(e,t)=f_{t}(e)
for
(e,t)\in E\times[0,1]
.
Pick a subbundle
A
of
E
which properly maps to
M\times[0,1]
and
a:M\times[0,1]\to(0,\infty)
as in the definition of fiberwise tameness. We may assume that
a
is independent of
t
by compactness of
[0,1]
. Using the fibration properties from the definition of fiberwise tameness we can find a vector field
Y_{t}=(X_{t},1)
on
E\times[0,1]
which can be integrated up to time
1
and such that
dp(X_{t})=0
everywhere and
df_{t}(X_{t})+\frac{df_{t}}{dt}=0
away from
A
. The time-dependent vector field generates an isotopy
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
defined by
\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{id}
and
\frac{d\varphi_{t}}{dt}=X_{t}\circ\varphi_{t}
. Integrating this equation we find
f_{t}\circ\varphi_{t}=f_{0}
away from some closed set proper over
M
, so the fibered diffeomorphism of
E\times[0,1]
defined by
(x,t)\mapsto(\varphi_{t}(x),t)
has the desired properties.
∎
