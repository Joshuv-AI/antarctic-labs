Association between thyroid hormone and pre-sarcopenic obesity in euthyroid population: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Tingting Hu, Yiting Xu, Xiaoya Li, Yunfeng Xiao, Yufei Wang, Yuqian Bao, Xiaojing Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical Abstract AbstractObjectiveWe aimed to explore the association between thyroid indicators and pre-sarcopenic obesity, pre-sarcopenia, and visceral obesity in a community-based euthyroid cohort.MethodsA total of 1,280 euthyroid residents (42.3% men) aged over 40 years were recruited in 2013–2014 and re-examined in 2015–2016. Thyroid indicators were analyzed. Visceral fat area was quantified using magnetic resonance imaging, and skeletal muscle mass was calculated using a bioelectrical impedance analyzer. Pre-sarcopenic obesity was defined as the coexistence of pre-sarcopenia and visceral obesity.ResultsDuring a mean follow-up of 2.1 years, 127 (14.8%), 156 (21.3%), and 120 (20.1%) participants developed new-onset pre-sarcopenic obesity, pre-sarcopenia, and visceral obesity, respectively. Compared with those with the lowest tertile of baseline serum fT3 levels, participants with the highest tertile had an elevated risk of pre-sarcopenic obesity (risk ratio (RR): 1.82, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.20–2.76) and pre-sarcopenia (RR: 1.49, 95% CI: 1.04–2.15). Moreover, a 1 standard deviation increase in serum fT3 levels over 2.1 years was positively associated with incident pre-sarcopenic obesity (RR: 1.40, 95% CI: 1.28–1.53) and pre-sarcopenia (RR: 1.29, 95% CI: 1.18–1.40), respectively.ConclusionElevated serum fT3 levels were associated with increased risks of pre-sarcopenic obesity and pre-sarcopenia in euthyroid Chinese participants.

Introduction

The global population is aging rapidly. According to the World Social Report 2023, the number of people aged over 65 years was approximately 761 million in 2021 and is predicted to reach 1.6 billion by 2050 (1). It is generally accepted that age-related muscle loss is commonly accompanied by an absolute or relative increase in fat mass; therefore, the concept of ‘sarcopenic obesity’ was proposed in 2000 (2). It is defined as the coexistence of low muscle mass/function and excess adiposity. ‘Pre-sarcopenic obesity’, an early stage of sarcopenic obesity, is defined as skeletal muscle loss coupled with obesity. Sarcopenic obesity is a growing concern because it often develops and progresses asymptomatically in the early stage until adverse outcomes (falls, fractures, physical disability, mortality, etc.) occur (3, 4). Thus, attention should not only be paid to sarcopenic obesity among older adults but also to pre-sarcopenic obesity among younger individuals. However, the pathophysiological mechanisms of pre-sarcopenic obesity remain unclear.

In 2024, an oral selective agonist for thyroid hormone receptor β was approved by the Food and Drug Administration, which was the world’s first drug for the treatment of fatty liver diseases. This advance has increased interest in thyroid hormone pathways in metabolic disease. Although the association between overt thyroid diseases and metabolic disorders is well established (5, 6), the association in the euthyroid individuals, which accounts for about 85% of the total population, remains unclear. Free triiodothyronine (fT3), the bioactive form of thyroid hormone, is transformed from free thyroxine (fT4). Although TSH is the primary screening marker for thyroid dysfunction, an increasing number of studies have reported that fT3 may better reflect peripheral thyroid hormone activity and often shows strong associations with metabolic phenotypes in euthyroid individuals (7, 8). Serum fT3 levels in the normal range have been demonstrated to be positively associated with body mass index (BMI) (7), waist circumference (9), and visceral fat area (VFA) (10). One cross-sectional study including 6,974 individuals aged ≥ 50 years revealed that serum fT3 levels within the normal range were negatively associated with muscle mass (11). Another cross-sectional study involving 941 euthyroid men aged 25–45 years reported an inverse association between fT3 and muscle cross-sectional area (12). Taken together, fT3 may reflect the interplay between skeletal muscle and adiposity. However, few studies have explored the association between thyroid indicators and the risk of pre-sarcopenic obesity cross-sectionally or longitudinally.

The definition of pre-sarcopenic obesity is continuously evolving as new findings challenge current understanding. One of the primary concerns in defining pre-sarcopenic obesity is that although BMI is widely used, stable or increasing BMI due to adipose accumulation may mask the coincident loss of skeletal muscle mass, potentially resulting in the underdiagnosis of a subset of patients. Furthermore, a recent study compared the prevalence and diagnostic agreement of sarcopenic obesity using BMI, waist circumference, body fat percentage, and VFA (13). Among these indicators, VFA had the highest sensitivity for diagnosing sarcopenic obesity and had relatively good diagnostic agreement with other indicators.

Therefore, we used magnetic resonance imaging to precisely assess VFA and conducted cross-sectional and longitudinal analyses to explore the association between thyroid indicators and pre-sarcopenic obesity in a community-based euthyroid Chinese population.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This study is part of a project designed to investigate risk factors and the progression of metabolic diseases, which represent a major disease burden in China. A total of 2,433 participants aged 18–80 years were recruited mainly through posters in local community health centers or through the acquaintances of study participants. All participants underwent baseline examinations between 2013 and 2014. In 2015–2016, participants were invited for a re-examination with a mean follow-up of 2.1 ± 0.2 years (14). For the present analyses, we excluded those with a validated history of malignant tumors (n = 27), severe liver or kidney dysfunction (n = 23), and a history of cardiovascular or cerebrovascular diseases (n = 43); those undergoing treatment with steroids, thyroxine, or antithyroid medications (n = 43); those with fT3, fT4, or thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) levels beyond the reference ranges (n = 394); those aged < 40 years (n = 27); those with missing data on VFA or skeletal muscle mass (n = 158); and those lost to follow-up (n = 438). There were no significant differences in age, the proportion of sex, BMI, VFA, thyroid hormones, or metabolic profiles between participants included and lost to follow-up (all P > 0.05). Finally, a total of 1,280 participants were included in the study (Fig. 1).

Flow chart of study participants.

The present study is in accordance with the guidelines stated in the Declaration of Helsinki, and all procedures involving participants were approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. All participants signed informed consent before participation.

Clinical data collection

At baseline and follow-up, clinical data were collected by trained physicians using standardized protocols, as previously described (14). Standardized questionnaires were used to collect demographic characteristics, lifestyle habits, and health-related information. Current smoking was defined as smoking ≥ 1 cigarette per day for at least 6 months (15). Alcohol consumption was defined as drinking ≥ 20 g per day for at least 6 months (16). Red meat intake was evaluated by questionnaires on habitual dietary intake during the previous 12 months. Questionnaires were validated in the China National Nutrition and Health Survey in 2002 (17). According to the International Physical Activity Questionnaire 2010, physical activity level was classified as light, moderate, and high (18).

Anthropometric and laboratory measurements

Height, body weight, and blood pressure were measured using standardized methods. BMI was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters. Hypertension was defined according to the 2020 guidelines of the International Society of Hypertension (19).

After an overnight fasting for ≥ 10 h, venous blood samples were collected for complete blood count, glucose, insulin, hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), lipid profiles, and C-reactive protein (CRP) levels. Diabetes was diagnosed according to the 2025 American Diabetes Association guidelines (20). Dyslipidemia was identified according to the 2023 Chinese guidelines for lipid management (21). The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration equation (22).

Serum fT3, fT4, and TSH levels were measured by electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Germany, cobas e601 analyzer). The reference ranges for fT3, fT4, and TSH were 3.10–6.80 pmol/L, 12.00–22.00 pmol/L, and 0.27–4.20 mIU/L, respectively. The intra-assay coefficients of variation for fT3, fT4, and TSH were <7.0%, <5.0%, and <3.0%, respectively. The inter-assay coefficients of variation for fT3, fT4, and TSH were <8.0%, <7.0%, and <8.0%, respectively (23). Indices of resistance to thyroid hormones were also calculated, including thyroid feedback quantile-based index (TFQI), TSH index (TSHI), and TSH resistance index (TT4RI). TFQI was calculated as cdf fT4 − (1 − cdf TSH) (24). TSHI was calculated as log TSH (mIU/L) + 0.345 × fT4 (pmol/L) (25). TT4RI was obtained by multiplying fT4 levels by the TSH levels (26). Changes in thyroid indicators were defined as the value at the second examination − the value at the first examination.

Diagnosis of pre-sarcopenia, visceral obesity, and pre-sarcopenic obesity

Body composition was evaluated using a bioelectrical impedance analyzer (BIA; TBF-418B; Tanita Corp., Japan). BIA is a simple, safe, and non-invasive method with high accuracy compared with magnetic resonance imaging in Asian population (27), which has been recommended by the European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism (ESPEN) and the European Association for the Study of Obesity (EASO) consensus for community-based population (28). Skeletal muscle mass was calculated using the BIA equation of Janssen et al.: skeletal muscle mass (kg) = [(height2/BIA-resistance × 0.401) + (sex × 3.825) + (age × −0.071)] + 5.102, where height is in cm; BIA-resistance is in ohms; sex is coded as men = 1 and women = 0; and age is in years (29). Skeletal muscle mass was standardized by weight in kilograms (skeletal muscle mass/weight×100%) and converted to skeletal muscle index (SMI). Pre-sarcopenia was defined using externally derived, sex-specific SMI thresholds (<37.0% in men and < 27.6% in women), corresponding to 1 standard deviation (SD) below the mean of a healthy young adult reference population, as recommended by the ESPEN/EASO consensus (28, 30).

Subcutaneous fat area (SFA) and VFA were quantified by magnetic resonance imaging (Archiva 3.0 T; Philips Medical Systems, Netherlands), and the mean values were calculated using an image analysis software (Slice-O-Matic, version 4.2; Tomovision Inc., Canada). Visceral obesity was defined as VFA ≥ 80 cm2 (31).

Pre-sarcopenic obesity was defined as a combination of pre-sarcopenia and visceral obesity according to the ESPEN/EASO consensus (28). Considering that fat distribution significantly influences metabolic diseases, we replicated the main analyses using the VFA/SFA ratio to define pre-sarcopenic obesity. The median value of the VFA/SFA ratio (0.47) was used as the cutoff (32).

Statistical analysis

The total population was divided into tertiles according to fT3, fT4, and TSH levels, respectively: for fT3, tertile 1: ≤4.66 pmol/L, tertile 2: 4.67–5.14 pmol/L, tertile 3: ≥5.15 pmol/L; for fT4, tertile 1: ≤15.56 pmol/L, tertile 2: 15.57–17.13 pmol/L, tertile 3: ≥17.14 pmol/L; and for TSH, tertile 1: ≤1.77 mU/L, tertile 2: 1.78–2.56 mU/L, tertile 3: ≥2.57 mU/L.

First, normality testing was conducted for all variables. Normally distributed variables are presented as mean ± SD, while skewed variables are expressed as median (25th percentile–75th percentile). Categorical variables were described as number (proportion). Second, baseline characteristics were compared using Student’s t-test, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, or the chi-square test, as appropriate. Third, a multivariable linear regression analysis was used to assess the correlation of thyroid indicators (fT3, fT4, TSH, TFQI, TSHI, and TT4RI) with SMI and VFA, respectively. Fourth, multivariable logistic regression analysis was applied to evaluate the association of fT3, fT4, and TSH levels with pre-sarcopenic obesity and its defining components in a cross-sectional analysis. As the incidence of pre-sarcopenic obesity and its defining components was > 10%, the risk ratio (RR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) were estimated using a modified Poisson regression model. Models were adjusted for age, sex, smoking status, alcohol drinking status, physical activity, red meat intake, eGFR, and CRP (fT3, fT4, and TSH were mutually adjusted). Association of serum fT3 levels with pre-sarcopenic obesity and pre-sarcopenia according to different subgroups (age (<65 or ≥65 years) and sex) was further explored. Potential mediators (fasting plasma glucose, 2-h plasma glucose, HbA1c, total cholesterol, triglyceride, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure) were examined using mediation analysis. Fifth, restricted cubic splines were used to characterize the relationship between changes in fT3 and incident pre-sarcopenic obesity and its defining components. Sixth, a sensitivity analysis was performed using the VFA/SFA ratio as a criterion for pre-sarcopenic obesity. All statistical analysis was conducted using R software, version 4.0.3. Two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline clinical characteristics

A total of 1,280 euthyroid participants (542 men and 738 women) with a median age of 59 years (range: 54–63 years) were included in this study. The median BMI was 24.0 (22.1–25.9) kg/m2, the median VFA was 83.0 (58.7–113) cm2, and the median SFA was 174 (131–219) cm2. The mean SMI was 37.6 ± 3.4% in men and 28.4 ± 2.8% in women. Table 1 shows the participants’ characteristics according to their baseline pre-sarcopenic obesity status. Participants with pre-sarcopenic obesity were older and had higher BMI, VFA, SFA, fT3, and CRP, and the proportion of diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia patients were higher (all P < 0.05). However, they presented lower levels of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol and eGFR (both P < 0.05). There were no differences in total cholesterol, fT4, TSH, TFQI, TSHI, and TT4RI levels; red meat intake; physical activity levels; and the proportion of smokers or alcohol use (all P > 0.05).

Cross-sectional association of thyroid indicators with pre-sarcopenic obesity and its defining components

The prevalence of pre-sarcopenic obesity, pre-sarcopenia, and visceral obesity was 32.7, 42.7, and 53.3%, respectively. After adjusting for age and sex, the multivariate linear regression analysis showed that serum fT3 levels were negatively correlated with SMI (Table 2, standardized β = −0.946, P < 0.001). No significant associations were observed between other thyroid indicators (fT4, TSH, TFQI, TSHI, and TT4RI) and SMI (all P > 0.05). Similarly, serum fT3 levels were positively correlated with VFA (standardized β = 11.37, P < 0.001).

The multivariate logistic regression model was applied to further explore the association of thyroid indicators with pre-sarcopenic obesity and its defining components. After adjusting for age, sex, smoking status, alcohol drinking status, physical activity, red meat intake, CRP, eGFR, fT4, and TSH, the risk of pre-sarcopenic obesity increased across the fT3 tertiles (Fig. 2A). The corresponding ORs of pre-sarcopenic obesity across serum fT3 level tertiles were 1.00, 1.50 (95% CI: 1.09–2.06), and 1.57 (95% CI: 1.12–2.22), respectively. A 1 SD increase in fT3 was associated with an elevated risk of pre-sarcopenic obesity (OR: 1.17, 95% CI: 1.02–1.35). Regarding its defining components, compared with the lowest tertile of fT3, the median and the highest tertile of fT3 were associated with higher risks of pre-sarcopenia (tertile 2 – OR: 1.65, 95% CI: 1.23–2.23; tertile 3 – OR: 1.77, 95% CI: 1.28–2.44) and visceral obesity (tertile 2 – OR: 1.42, 95% CI: 1.06–1.92; tertile 3 – OR: 1.94, 95% CI: 1.40–2.69). Neither serum fT4 nor TSH levels were associated with pre-sarcopenic obesity or its defining components (Fig. 2A, all P > 0.05). This association pattern was consistent when the VFA/SFA ratio was used to define pre-sarcopenic obesity (Fig. 2B). Moreover, no significant associations of the resistance-to-thyroid-hormone indices (TFQI, TSHI, and TT4RI) with the risk of pre-sarcopenic obesity, pre-sarcopenia, and visceral obesity were observed (Table 3).

Longitudinal association of thyroid indicators with incident pre-sarcopenic obesity and its defining components

During a mean follow-up of 2.1 years, 127 (14.8%), 156 (21.3%), and 120 (20.1%) participants developed pre-sarcopenic obesity, pre-sarcopenia, and visceral obesity, respectively. Compared with those in the first tertile of serum fT3 levels, participants in the highest tertile of serum fT3 levels presented higher risks of pre-sarcopenic obesity (Fig. 3A, RR: 1.82, 95% CI: 1.20–2.76) and pre-sarcopenia (Fig. 3A, RR: 1.49, 95% CI: 1.04–2.15), respectively. However, the association between serum fT3 levels and visceral obesity disappeared (Fig. 3A, P > 0.05). We replicated this analysis by applying the VFA/SFA ratio to define pre-sarcopenic obesity. All results were consistent with the prior analysis (Fig. 3B). Moreover, the associations of serum fT3 levels with pre-sarcopenic obesity and pre-sarcopenia were consistent across age or sex subgroups (Table 4, both P for interaction > 0.05). When using VFA/SFA to define pre-sarcopenic obesity, similar results were observed (Table 4, both P for interaction > 0.05). No significant association between the resistance-to-thyroid-hormone indices and the incidence of pre-sarcopenic obesity, pre-sarcopenia, and visceral obesity were found (Table 5).

Mediation analysis

The effects of serum fT3 on the incidence of pre-sarcopenic obesity were partially mediated through triglyceride and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, with a mediation proportion of 4.16 (0.06–12.4)% and 13.1 (5.30–27.6)%, respectively, when using VFA to define pre-sarcopenic obesity (Table 6). When VFA/SFA was used to define pre-sarcopenic obesity, the effect of serum fT3 on pre-sarcopenic obesity was partially mediated through high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (7.25 (0.10–19.3)%). Regarding the association between serum fT3 level and the incidence of pre-sarcopenia, no significant mediation effects were found (Table 6).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to explore the association of thyroid indicators with pre-sarcopenic obesity and its defining components in a euthyroid cohort. Our cross-sectional analyses revealed that serum fT3 levels were independently and positively associated with pre-sarcopenic obesity, pre-sarcopenia, and visceral obesity. After 2.1 years of follow-up, the association between baseline serum fT3 levels and incident pre-sarcopenic obesity and pre-sarcopenia remained significant. In addition, elevated serum fT3 levels over 2.1 years were linearly associated with a higher risk of incident pre-sarcopenic obesity and pre-sarcopenia, respectively.

The world population is aging rapidly. Given that pre-sarcopenic obesity is an age-related disease and associated with many metabolic disorders, poor quality of life or even an increased risk of mortality (3), elucidating its pathophysiological mechanisms is a strategic focus that can lead to substantial improvements in public health and promoting healthy aging. Thyroid hormones are essential for skeletal muscle metabolism (33). Our previous studies supported the association between serum fT3 levels and fat distribution (23). On this basis, we aimed to further explore the association between serum fT3 levels and pre-sarcopenic obesity.

According to the ESPEN/EASO consensus, pre-sarcopenic obesity is characterized by a combination of high body fat and low muscle mass. However, BMI may not accurately capture specific shifts between muscle and adipose tissue, and individuals may present a stable or increasing BMI while gaining fat and losing muscle mass (34). In addition, Asian populations have a genetic predisposition to accumulate abdominal fat. Therefore, indicators such as VFA are prioritized when defining pre-sarcopenic obesity (35). Here, we used magnetic resonance imaging to accurately measure VFA. For the first time, we found that the highest tertile of serum fT3 levels was associated with a higher risk of pre-sarcopenic obesity compared with the lowest tertile of fT3 in both cross-sectional and longitudinal analyses. Furthermore, using the VFA/SFA ratio to define pre-sarcopenic obesity supported the robustness of our primary findings.

Although few studies have examined the association between thyroid indicators and pre-sarcopenic obesity, several cross-sectional studies reported inconsistent findings regarding the association between thyroid hormone and pre-sarcopenia. Consistent with our results, a study of 6,974 Brazilian euthyroid individuals aged ≥ 50 years demonstrated that serum fT3 levels were associated with higher odds of pre-sarcopenia (11). Inconsistent with our data, another study found that there were no significant differences in fT3 levels among 94 elderly participants (mean age: 77.6 years) with or without pre-sarcopenia (36). These discrepancies may reflect differences in the study design, age distribution, and the standardized method for skeletal muscle mass. The study mentioned above was cross-sectional, which may introduce reverse causation. Moreover, that study included individuals aged ≥ 65 years, while our study had 17.0% individuals aged ≥ 65 years. The skeletal muscle mass in that study was standardized by height, whereas the skeletal muscle mass in our study was standardized by weight. Furthermore, a cohort study of 198,069 Korean adults reported that serum fT3 levels were positively associated with incident pre-sarcopenia in a dose–response manner during a median follow-up of 3.1 years (37). Our study supports this finding and extends to show that increases in serum fT3 levels over 2.1 years were positively associated with new-onset pre-sarcopenia, which was independent of baseline serum fT3 levels. Taken together, these findings support an association between serum fT3 and pre-sarcopenia within the euthyroid range, although its utility for risk prediction, screening, or intervention requires further evaluation in future studies.

Aging is one of the major risk factors for pre-sarcopenic obesity and pre-sarcopenia. We therefore performed stratified analysis by age. Our findings showed no significant interaction between age and serum fT3 levels in relation to pre-sarcopenic obesity or pre-sarcopenia. It is suggested that the associations of serum fT3 levels with pre-sarcopenic obesity and pre-sarcopenia persist as age increases.

In euthyroid populations, circulating thyroid hormones appear to play a more sensitive role in metabolic regulation than is captured by TSH alone. TSH primarily reflects central pituitary feedback and is strongly buffered within the reference range, whereas fT3 may better reflect peripheral thyroid hormone availability and adaptive metabolic responses. Accumulating evidence suggests that higher serum fT3 levels within the normal range are associated with body composition (23), insulin resistance (38), and metabolic disorders (11), all of which are relevant to the development of pre-sarcopenic obesity. Moreover, diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia need careful consideration in model adjustment, as they may arise downstream of thyroid hormone activity and adiposity and therefore lie on the causal pathway linking serum fT3 to pre-sarcopenic obesity. In line with this interpretation, mediation analyses indicated that high-density lipoprotein cholesterol partially mediated the association between serum fT3 and incident pre-sarcopenic obesity, supporting a role of lipid-related metabolic pathways in this relationship.

Several mechanisms may help to explain the association between fT3 and pre-sarcopenic obesity. Chronic low-grade inflammation may impair metabolic processes in adipose tissue and skeletal muscle, thereby contributing to the progression of pre-sarcopenic obesity. Elevated fT3 could elevate inflammation via phosphoinositide 3-phosphate kinase (PI3K), protein kinase B (AKT), and mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs), which may contribute to fat accumulation and muscle atrophy (39, 40). In addition, both type II and type III iodothyronine deiodinases are highly expressed in skeletal muscle and act as key local determinants of intracellular T3 availability (41, 42). A recent study demonstrated that under condition of muscle injury, type II deiodinase in muscle-resident fibro-adipogenic progenitors (FAPs) could enhance local intracellular T3 production, leading to increased intracellular T3 signaling, which promoted adipogenic differentiation and contributed to intramuscular fat accumulation in adult male mice (43). Previous evidence suggests that serum T3 levels are generally correlated with tissue T3 content and thyroid hormone signaling across multiple organs (including skeletal muscle) (44). In this context, serum fT3 levels may provide indirect information about systemic thyroid hormone status, and the observed association between serum fT3 and pre-sarcopenic obesity in our study may partly reflect underlying alterations in thyroid hormone metabolism that are also relevant at the tissue level. Further studies integrating serum fT3 with tissue-specific assessments are warranted to better elucidate the complex interplay between systemic thyroid function, local hormone activation, and muscle-adipose tissue remodeling.

The strengths of our study include the prospective study design, the community-based setting, and the comprehensive collection of lifestyle variables, which allowed us to account for potential confounders and reduce reverse causality. However, our study has several limitations. First, our study only included one center, as such the generalizability of the findings to other ethnic groups is uncertain and the external validation is of great importance. Second, skeletal muscle mass was detected by BIA instead of dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry or magnetic resonance imaging. However, the correlation between BIA and magnetic resonance imaging for predicting muscle mass was 0.93 in Asian population, as previously reported (27). Third, measures of muscle strength and physical performance would have strengthened this study but were not available in our cohort. Fourth, given the relatively short follow-up period (median: 2.1 years), causal inference is limited. Early or subclinical changes in muscle mass, physical activity, and energy expenditure may precede the measured outcome and influence peripheral thyroid hormone conversion and circulating fT3 levels. Therefore, we cannot exclude that higher serum fT3 levels reflect underlying changes in body composition or metabolic status rather than being upstream of them. Future studies with longer duration are needed to further clarify the association between serum fT3 levels and pre-sarcopenic obesity. Fifth, participant attrition during follow-up may lead to selection bias, although baseline characteristics between those included and lost to follow-up showed no significant differences. Sixth, the deviation of the pre-sarcopenia cutoffs used in this study is uncertain. Finally, the use of weight-adjusted skeletal muscle index couples the assessment of muscle mass with adiposity and may partially capture variations in body fat mass.

Our findings indicate that elevated serum fT3 levels were associated with a higher risk of pre-sarcopenic obesity and pre-sarcopenia in a euthyroid Chinese population.
