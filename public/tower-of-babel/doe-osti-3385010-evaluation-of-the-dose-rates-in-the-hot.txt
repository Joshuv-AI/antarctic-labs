 vii

 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This analysis evaluates the radiation dose rates during beam-on operation in the Hot Process Vault (HPV)
at Oak Ridge National Laboratory’s Second Target Station (STS). The HPV houses many utility systems
like the primary cooling loop pumps, drain tanks, polishing loops, and valve manifolds. While the HPV
itself is a restricted area during beam-on operation, some of the areas adjacent to the HPV will be
accessible and should be shielded to dose rates <0.25 mrem/h. In this work, we determine the thicknesses
required for the HPV walls, ceiling, and doors, to provide adequate shielding. Additionally, we evaluate the
dose rates for a detailed design of the downstream doors and the dose rates through some of the planned
penetrations through the HPV walls.
This evaluation accounts for neutron and gamma emissions from beam-on-target (BOT) operations and
decay radiation from activated water in pipes. Computational tools such as MCNP6.2 (for particle
transport) and ADVANTG3.2 (for weight-window generation) have been employed to ensure accurate dose
rate predictions in a reasonable computational time.
Dose rates in the HPV are dominated by the contribution of the gamma source of the activated water and
range from 200 to 20,000 mrem/h. The BOT contribution inside the HPV accounts for only 0.1 to 20
mrem/h, but the highly penetrating neutrons play a significant role in sizing the carbon steel doors and the
concrete walls in the area close to the bunker.
The HPV wall and ceiling should be at least 68 cm concrete. Additional concrete or a section of
high-density concrete is needed in the corner between the bunker and the HPV to decrease the BOT dose.
The carbon steel doors between the HPV and the area east of the HPV need to be at least 16 cm thick. With
detailed models of the doors that include realistic gaps, it has been confirmed that the doors are sufficient to
keep dose rates below 0.25 mrem/h in the adjacent accessible areas. Finally, the evaluated penetrations and
hatch are not expected to cause radiation safety concerns.

 viii

 1 SCOPE

In this analysis, we calculate the dose rates in the Hot Process Vault (HPV) during beam-on operation at the
Second Target Station (STS). Specifically, this study seeks to determine the required thickness of walls,
ceilings, and doors of the HPV to maintain dose rates below 0.25 mrem/h in adjacent accessible areas.
Additionally, analyses of dose rates originating from some of the penetrations through the HPV walls are
presented.
During operation, two radiation sources need to be considered: 1) neutron radiation from spallation
reactions in the target area; 2) gamma radiation from the activated water in the cooling loops located inside
the HPV. In this analysis, we evaluate these sources separately.
Parts of this analysis will be presented at the 2026 ANS Annual conference.

 4 METHODOLOGY AND MODELS

4.1 GEOMETRY
The HPV is located downstream of the STS bunker, starting at approximately 12 meter and ending at
approximately 35 meter downstream of the spallation target. Vertically, the HPV extends from
approximately 2 meter below to 7 meter above the proton beam elevation. Figures 1 and 2 shows a
horizontal and vertical cross-section through the geometry. High-energy protons travel in the X-direction
and create spallation reactions when they interact with the target, which is mostly consisting of tungsten
and located near the origin of this model. High-energy neutrons from the spallation reactions are
subsequently moderated by interactions with the surrounding structures, especially in the two moderators,
consisting of a para-hydrogen moderator, water pre-moderator, and beryllium reflector. From these
moderators, beamlines transport the neutrons to the instruments. This model considers the bunker which
includes 18 beamlines from both moderators as well as the core vessel drain line pointing15 degrees to the
north at the Y = -100 cm elevation, see Figure 3. The monolith in this model consists of mostly regular
density concrete, except in the section Y= -129 cm and Y = 110 cm, where high-density (HD) concrete is
used.
The walls of the HPV (regular density concrete) and the doors to the accessible areas (carbon steel) have
been thickened in the model to ensure that the 0.25 mrem/h dose rate contour is captured, which is used to
determine the minimum shielding thickness. There are three doors that separate the HPV and the
accessible areas east of the HPV (X > 3500 cm): two on the first level as seen on Figure 1, and a third on
the second level, see Figure 4 (right). There are two 10-cm thick carbon steel doors separating the bunker
and het HPV: one on the first level (Figure 1), and one the second level (Figure 4 (left)).

4.2 GEOMETRY UPDATE
In this geometry, the full design of the HPV was updated to the latest version at the time of the analysis and
converted using GEOUNED 1.5.1 [2]. Figures 5 and 6 give an overview of this geometry. The most
significant differences are listed below.
 • The doors separating the HPV with the accessible areas downstream are now modeled in detail,
 including streaming gaps and realistic geometry features, see Figure 7.
 • The internal wall (concrete) on the second floor is no longer present. This has a large impact on the
 sizing of the south door on the second level. The model of the new geometry has been manually
 adjusted to increase the thickness to 18 cm, consistent with the two first-level doors to compensate
 for the elimination of the wall.
 • The opening for the door between the bunker and the HPV is ≈10 cm less wide and ≈10 cm further
 back (≈10 cm thicker wall) than the initial geometry. This will cause the amount of neutrons
 streaming through this opening to be smaller.
 • The second door between the bunker and HPV that was previously visible at elevation Y = 200 cm is
 now at a higher elevation, visible in the Y = 500 cm plot.
 • The wall between the bunker and the HPV has a slightly different thickness in the updated model and
 is dependent on the height. This will impact the dose rates originating from the neutrons in the
 beam-on-target (BOT) simulation. The door itself remains ≈10 cm thick and is sometimes voided in
 the simulations.

4.3 BEAM ON TARGET (BOT)
The beam-on-target (BOT) simulation evaluates the dose rates from neutrons and photons that originate
from interaction with the proton source. The 700 kW source of 1.3 GeV protons has a detailed description,
generated by Wouter de Wet, based on data from the accelerator systems group. The source starts before
the Proton Beam Window (PBW) at X = 309.4 cm and has a beam footprint of 90 cm2 on the target
surface. The particle transport calculations are performed with MCNP 6.2 and include the transport of
neutrons, photons, protons, muons, pions, deuterons, tritons, helions, and alphas [3]. High-energy physics
models (CEM) are used for proton interactions >1 MeV and neutron interactions >20 MeV. Cross-section
from ENDF7.1 are used for lower energies [4]. Photonuclear physics are included. From the bunker,
particles can enter the HPV or the instrument halls. For this simulation, the particles entering the
instrument halls are of no interest and, therefore, killed. The resulting dose rates from neutrons and
photons are tallied using an FMESH, with flux-to-dose-rate conversion factors taken from [5]. The total
dose rate from the BOT contribution consists of the sum of the neutron and photon contribution. The
former provides the dominant dose rate contribution.
Weight-windows generated by ADVANTG 3.2 are used to achieve convergence within a reasonable
computational time [6]. For the ADVANTG simulation, a surrogate neutron source is used that is
constructed from the neutron flux tallied in an FMESH overlaying the target geometry from a simulation
with a proton source, as demonstrated in Miller [7]. The ADVANTG mesh does not fully resolve the
beamlines. However, as neutrons in the beam lines are primarily of low energy, they are not expected to
contribute significantly to the dose rates in the HPV.

4.4 ACTIVATED WATER
A second radiation source in the HPV originates from the activated water in the cooling loops. There are
two cooling loops: loop 1 with water that cools the target and loop 2, which is the most highly activated,
with water that cools the moderator reflector assembly, core vessel, and other components. Each pipe in the
loop is modeled as a separate volume that considers the approximate decay time based on the flow rates
[8]. This results in a source description with >500 cells with decay times from 253 sec to 301 sec. The
decay times are chosen conservatively, taking the smallest decay time per considered volume. The total
source is separated into six SDEF’s for six different MCNP simulations, for which the resulting dose rates
are summed. The gamma transport simulations are performed using MCNP6.2 with weight-windows
generated by ADVANTG3.2.
Figure 9 shows the two water loops (left) and gives an impression of the source by plotting the FMESH
that tallies only the particle source flux in MCNP6.2 (right). Figure 10 shows the spectrum from the SDEF
with the largest contribution to the dose rates from Loop 2. The intensity of this source is 5 × 106
γ/sec/cm3 . The dose rates are primarily driven by the 511 keV gammas from 11 C (20 minute halflife) and
others, and the 2.2 MeV gammas from 14 O (70 second halflife). The 6 MeV gammas from 16 N (8 second
halflife), that are dominant for shorter decay times have a low contribution. The irradiation times for the
water in the Moderator Reflector Assembly are 5.45 sec and 3.47 sec for the lower and upper premoderator,
respectively, and1.63 sec and 1.65 sec for the lower and upper reflector. The irradiation time for the water
in the core vessel is 1.9 sec.

5.1 SIZING THE HPV WALLS AND DOORS
Figures 15, 16, and 17 show the dose rates from the BOT, activated water, and their sum for a plan view
through the geometry at beam elevation (Y = 0 cm), a plan view at Y = 500 cm. and a vertical
cross-section view at Z = 0 cm. These simulations make use of the initial model, see section 4.1. The
neutrons from the BOT simulation result in dose rates ranging from 20 mrem/h close to the door to the
bunker to 0.1 mrem/h further downstream in the HPV. The decay gammas from the activated water result in
dose rates from 200 mrem/h further away from the pipes up to 20,000 mrem/h close to the hottest pipes.
While the BOT dose rates are orders of magnitude lower than the gamma dose rates from water, they need
to be considered when sizing the wall and door thicknesses as neutrons can penetrate deeply into materials.

Figure 18 shows a three-dimensional plot of the 0.25 mrem/h contour colored by the fraction of the total

 6 CONCLUSION

This analysis determined the shielding requirements for the Hot Process Vault by assessing radiation dose
rates from both beam-on-target contributions and activated water gamma rays. While gamma radiation
from activated water dominates the dose rates within the vault, penetrating neutrons influence the design of
nearby carbon steel doors and concrete walls. The findings ensure that accessible adjacent areas maintain
dose rates below 0.25 mrem/h and provide a detailed basis for the design of downstream doors and wall
penetrations. Table 3 summarizes the required and actual thicknesses of the walls and doors of the HVP.
