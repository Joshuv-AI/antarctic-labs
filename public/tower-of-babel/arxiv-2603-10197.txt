Regge’s Inferno
Zohar Komargodski, Alessio Miscioscia, Fedor K. Popov

Abstract
We study large-spin operators in conformal field theories (CFTs) in spacetime dimensions d > 2 d>2 by placing the theory on appropriate pp-wave backgrounds. We show that these geometries admit Heisenberg-group symmetries, and that these symmetries, combined with locality of quantum fields on such spacetimes, impose strong constraints on the asymptotic spectrum in the large-spin limit. The pp-wave backgrounds probe both the small-twist regime, corresponding to the Regge or light-cone bootstrap, and a strongly coupled regime of large twist. Finally, we demonstrate that causality (or the requirement that the energy be bounded from below) leads to a new unitarity bound in 3 + 1 3+1 dimensions.

From Small to Large Twist

CFTs are generally strongly coupled and relatively little is known about them. However, in various limit such as large charge or large spin, there are interesting results about the spectrum of operators. We concentrate on large spin operators, which is a universal sub-sector.

7

For simplicity consider 2+1 dimensional theories and define the usual twist

\tau=\Delta-J_{z}\penalty\ ,\quad J_{z}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}

(19)

with
\Delta
the scaling dimension and
J_{z}
the spin of the operator with respect to rotations around the

\displaystyle\Delta\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}&J=0\\
1&J=1/2\\
J+1&J\geq 1\end{array}\right.

In particular, since
|J_{z}|\leq J
, we see that the twist (
19

\tau\geq\frac{1}{2}

We can ask what are the operators with the lowest possible values of
\tau

It turns out that operators with finite

J_{z}
are weakly interacting and furnish a Fock space, see
Komargodski:2012ek

Fitzpatrick:2012yx

For brevity, let
\partial_{\pm}=\partial_{x}\pm i\partial_{y}
denote a derivative with
\Delta=1
and
J_{z}=\pm 1
.
Consider any primary operator
\cal O
with
J_{z}=J_{0}
and scaling dimension
\Delta=\Delta_{0}
and consider general multi-twist operators of the form

\partial_{+}^{m_{1}}{\cal O}\cdots\partial_{+}^{m_{n}}{\cal O}\penalty\ .

(20)

Classically these operators have
\Delta=n\Delta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
and angular momentum
J_{z}=nJ_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
and hence twist
\tau=n\tau_{0}
.

Usually composite operators in CFT receive large quantum corrections, and naive estimation fails. For example, the dimension of a composite operator is not equal to the sum of the dimensions of its constituents.
However, for
m_{i}\gg n
it turns out that the composite operators behave almost classically and the twist just adds up due to the constituents ,“partons”, i.e.
\tau=n\tau_{0}
is correct to a good approximation.
The deviations from the classical predictions scale as
\sim m_{i}^{-1}
; we will be more precise about this below.

Due to this Fock space of weakly-coupled composite operators, the partition function

Z=\sum e^{-\beta(\Delta-J_{z})}

(21)

diverges. Indeed, the divergence is due to the fact that choosing arbitrary large
\{m_{i}\}
does not affect the twist spectrum to the leading order.
8
8

8

This is of course a similar divergence to the one we encounter in flat space QFT if we try to calculate

\sum e^{-\beta(H-P)}\penalty\ ,

(22)

which diverges from highly-boosted multi-particle states, though the details are quite different.

This divergence can be regularized by adding
e^{-\beta\epsilon J_{z}}
to the partition function, with
\epsilon>0
. Thus we consider the regularized partition function

Z=\sum e^{-\beta(\Delta-J_{z})-\beta\epsilon J_{z}}\penalty\ .

(23)

At
\beta\gg 1
the partition function contains information about the Fock states (
20
). As we increase the temperature, states with larger and larger twist become important and the Fock space picture breaks down at sufficiently large twist. For Fock states as in (
20
)
the corrections to the leading order formula take the following form
\Delta=n\Delta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}+\delta\Delta
with
\delta\Delta\sim{n^{2}\over m_{i}}\sim{n^{3}\over J_{z}}
where we assumed that typically
m_{i}\sim J_{z}/n
. Therefore
\tau-n\tau_{0}\sim{n^{3}\over J_{z}}
. The corrections overwhelm the classical prediction for
n^{2}/J_{z}\sim 1
, which means that when

\tau\sim\sqrt{J}_{z}

(24)

the weakly-coupled partons picture breaks down.
9
9

9

This counting is essentially identical to the argument regarding when the charged Fock space breaks down in

\delta\Delta\sim n^{3}/J_{z}
, we have estimated the sum over pairs contributes
n^{2}
and the typical inter-parton distance being
J_{z}/n
. This is too naive as it ignores the rotation of the partons. The correct calculation, which leads to the same conclusion, is presented in

;
Kravchuk:2024wmv

This will imply that at temperatures
\beta\lesssim 1

(65)

Therefore the partition function is

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\epsilon\,\beta\,m^{2}\,\sinh\frac{\beta m}{2}}\ .

(66)

Let us now compute the spectrum of a free conformal scalar field in this space-time. An early paper showing that scalar fields are well behaved in such spacetimes is
Gibbons:1975jb
. We write the equation of motion for the scalar field and determine the corresponding normal modes and their spectrum. The equation of motion is

\displaystyle(1+y^{2})\,\partial_{x}^{2}\phi+\frac{1}{4}\partial_{y}^{2}\phi+\,\partial_{t}\partial_{x}\phi=0.

(67)

Substituting the plane-wave ansatz
\phi=e^{ikx}\,\varphi(t,y)
with
k>0
, we find

\displaystyle i\,\partial_{t}\varphi=-\frac{1}{4k}\,\partial_{y}^{2}\varphi+\Bigl(1+y^{2}\Bigr)k\,\varphi,

(68)

where
k>0
.
This is a Schrödinger equation for a particle of mass
2k
in a harmonic oscillator potential with frequency
\omega=1
. Stability requires
k>0
. The corresponding eigenmodes have energies

\displaystyle E_{n,k}=k+\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right).

(69)

From this we can easily compute the thermal free energy of our space.
We obtain for the single particle states in the pp-wave geometry

Z_{1}=V\int_{0}^{\infty}{dk\over 2\pi}\sum_{n}e^{-\beta(k+n+\frac{1}{2})}={V\over 4\pi}{1\over\beta\sinh(\beta/2)}

(70)

Now identifying the volume of the pp-wave with

V=\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon}
, we obtain via the Plethystic exponential

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\epsilon\,\beta}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2m^{2}\,\sinh\frac{m\beta}{2}}\penalty\ ,

(71)

which matches the result from direct operator counting (
66
). The identification
V=\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon}
between the pp wave volume and the operator counting problem is model independent.

We can also study the case of
H=\Delta-(1-\epsilon)J_{\star}
in
d
dimensions, where
J_{\star}
is the spin conjugate to one of the angles. (So we only take one angular momentum to be large, in the
\epsilon\ll 1
limit.)

The treatment of this problem is via the geometry (
61
). The results are analogous to the three-dimensional case, but now, instead of one harmonic oscillator, we have
d-2
of them. To show this, let us write the equation of motion for a free conformal scalar in
d
dimensions,

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{t}\partial_{x}+\left(1+\sum^{d-2}_{i=1}x_{i}^{2}\right)\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{d-2}\right]\phi=0

(72)

Using the ansatz
\phi=e^{ikx}\prod\limits^{d-2}_{i=1}\psi_{i}(x_{i})
, we see that this equation decouples and

\displaystyle E=k+\sum^{d-2}_{i=1}\left(n_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\right),\qquad\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\epsilon\beta}\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\frac{1}{m^{2}\left(2\sinh\frac{m\beta}{2}\right)^{d-2}}\ .

(73)

A quick consistency check that this indeed agrees with the direction calculation for a scalar field, we use the large temperature expansion to get

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{\zeta(d)}{\beta^{d-1}\epsilon}=-\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\beta}{\epsilon\Gamma(d/2)}F_{d}(\beta),

(74)

where
F_{d}(\beta)=-\frac{\Gamma(d/2)\zeta(d)}{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\beta^{d}}
is the free energy of a massless scalar field in
d
dimensions in flat space-time.

4.2
Free fermion in 2+1 d

The spectrum of single particle excitations of a free Dirac fermion on
S^{2}\times S^{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}=l\penalty\ ,\quad SU(2):\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)\oplus\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)\ .

(75)

We can compute in the
\epsilon\ll 1
limit

\displaystyle Z_{\rm sp}=2\sum^{\infty}_{l=1}e^{-\beta\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\frac{\sinh\left(\beta(1-\epsilon)l\right)}{\sinh\frac{\beta(1-\epsilon)}{2}}=\frac{1}{\beta\epsilon\sinh\frac{\beta}{2}}

(76)

And using the plethystic exponent for fermions we get

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{m}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m^{2}\beta\sinh\frac{m\beta}{2}}\ .

(77)

Now let us show that the same free energy could be obtained by considering a theory of a free Dirac fermion in the pp-wave background. For that we need to study the spectrum of the Dirac operator in this background.
We have that

\not{D}\psi=\left(\gamma^{t}(\partial_{t}+(2+2y^{2})\partial_{x})+\gamma^{x}\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\gamma^{y}\partial_{y}\right)\psi=0\penalty\ .

(78)

For simplicity we square this operator to get

\not{D}^{2}\psi=\left(-2\partial_{t}\partial_{x}-2(1+y^{2})\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{y}^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{2}y}{2}\gamma^{+}\gamma^{2}\partial_{x}\right)\psi=0\ ,

(79)

note that
\gamma^{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\gamma^{0}+\gamma^{1}\right)
is nilpotent, and hence from the structure of this equation we expect that the solution should have the following form

\psi=\gamma^{+}\chi(y)e^{i\omega t+ikx}\ ,

(80)

from which

\left[\omega k+(1+y^{2})k^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\partial_{y}^{2}\right]\chi(y)=0\ .

(81)

Therefore the spectrum of this operator is just the same as in the previous sub-section

E_{n,k}=k+n+\frac{1}{2}\ ,

(82)

positivity of energy imposes
k>0
. Using the plethystic exponent for the fermions we can get that the free energy in this case is

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\epsilon\beta m^{2}\sinh\frac{m\beta}{2}}\ .

(83)

Once again the result perfectly matches the operator counting.

We see that the spectrum of the free scalar and free fermion coincide. As we mentioned in the introduction, this should be attributed to having supercharges commuting with the twist generator.

4.3
Free scalar field in 3+1 d

Now we will study various free conformal theories in 3+1 d. And we will start with the free conformal scalar. The spectrum of single particle excitations in this case is

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}=1+l,\quad SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}:\left(\frac{l}{2},\frac{l}{2}\right),

(84)

and from that we can find that the partition function with respect to the Hamiltonian
H=\Delta-2J_{R}+(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2})J_{R}+(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2})J_{L}
. Then we can find that the free energy to be, in the
\epsilon_{1,2}\ll 1
limit

\displaystyle Z_{\rm sp}=\sum^{\infty}_{l=0}e^{-\beta(l+1)}\frac{\sinh\left[\frac{1}{2}(l+1)\beta(2-\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2})\right]}{\sinh\left[\frac{1}{2}\beta\left(2-\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}\right)\right]}\frac{\sinh\left[\frac{1}{2}(l+1)\beta(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2})\right]}{\sinh\left[\frac{1}{2}\beta(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2})\right]}=\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\beta^{2}\sinh\beta}+\ldots,

And from that we find that the free energy is

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\beta^{2}}\sum_{m}\frac{1}{m^{3}\sinh m\beta}

(85)

Let us try to match to the computation in the pp wave background. For that we just need to find a spectrum of free conformal scalar field in background defined in (
55
) by just solving the equations of motions of a scalar field

\left(-2\partial_{t}\partial_{v}+(4u^{2}+1)\,\partial_{v}^{2}-4u\,\partial_{v}\partial_{y}+\partial_{u}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}\right)\phi=0.

(86)

Substituting the ansatz
\phi=\varphi(u)e^{ipv+iky+i\omega t^{\prime}}
we again get just a Schrödinger equation for a free particle in a external constant magnetic field

i\partial_{t^{\prime}}\varphi=\left[\frac{p}{2}-\frac{\partial_{u}^{2}}{2p}+2p\left(u-\frac{k}{2p}\right)^{2}\right]\varphi.

(87)

The spectrum is

E_{n}=\frac{p}{2}+2n+1\ ,

(88)

where
p>0
.
Let us emphasize that the energy eigenvalues are independent of
k
. This feature follows from the
\mathbb{H}_{3}
symmetry: states with different values of
k
are related by the action of
\mathbb{H}_{3}
, and therefore share the same energy. The label
k
thus parametrizes the degeneracy associated with the Heisenberg group.

From the energy spectrum we construct the partition function
in the same way as we did in the 2+1 dimensional case, to find

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{L_{v}L_{y}L_{u}}{\pi^{2}\beta^{2}}\sum_{m}\frac{1}{m^{3}\sinh m\beta}\penalty\ .

(89)

Now using that
L_{y}L_{u}L_{v}=V=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}
. From that we find that

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\beta^{2}}\sum_{m}\frac{1}{m^{3}\sinh m\beta}\ .

(90)

4.4
Free Fermion in 3+1 d

We now consider a free massless Dirac fermion in four dimensions. To simplify the computation in this case we will set
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon
. The spectrum of single–particle excitations on
S^{3}\times S^{1}
is well known. The eigenvalues of the Dirac Hamiltonian are

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}=n+\frac{3}{2},\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots

(91)

and the corresponding
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
quantum numbers are

\displaystyle SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}:\left(\frac{n+1}{2},\frac{n}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}\right).

(92)

Using this spectrum, the single–particle partition function with a chemical potential
2\beta(1-\epsilon)
for
J_{L}^{z}
is

\displaystyle Z_{\rm sp}(\beta,\epsilon)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta\left(n+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\frac{(n+1)\sinh\!\left[(n+2)\beta(1-\epsilon)\right]+(n+2)\sinh\!\left[n\beta(1-\epsilon)\right]}{\sinh\!\left[\beta(1-\epsilon)\right]}.

(93)

In the limit
\epsilon\to 0
this behaves as

\displaystyle Z_{\rm sp}(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}\beta^{2}\sinh\frac{\beta}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{-1}).

(94)

The full fermionic free energy is then obtained by the standard plethystic exponent

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{2\beta^{2}m^{3}\sinh\frac{m\beta}{2}}.

(95)

The Dirac equation written explicitly in the coordinates

(t,v,u,y)
of the pp wave defined in (
54
) is

\displaystyle\Bigg[\gamma^{+}\left(\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{v}\right)+\gamma^{-}\partial_{v}+\gamma^{1}\partial_{u}+\gamma^{2}\left(\partial_{y}-2u\partial_{v}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{+12}\Bigg]\Psi=0

(96)

Using the ansatz

\displaystyle\psi(t,v,y,u)=e^{-i\omega t-ipv+iky}\,\Psi(u)

(97)

we arrive at the following equation

\displaystyle\Bigg[-i\gamma^{+}\left(\omega-\frac{p}{2}\right)-i\gamma^{-}p+\gamma^{1}\partial_{u}+i\gamma^{2}\left(k+2up\right)+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{+12}\Bigg]\Psi=0,

(98)

using that
\Psi=\Psi_{+}+\Psi_{-}
with
\gamma^{+}\Psi_{+}=\gamma^{-}\Psi_{-}=0
we get the following equation

\displaystyle\Psi_{+}=-\frac{(\gamma^{1}\partial_{u}+i\gamma^{2}(k+2up))}{2ip}\gamma^{+}\Psi_{-}

(99)

Substiting it back into the above equation we find

\displaystyle\omega\Psi_{-}=\frac{p}{2}\Psi_{-}+\left[-\frac{\partial_{u}^{2}}{2p}+2p\left(u+\frac{k}{2p}\right)^{2}\right]\Psi_{-}+\frac{i}{2}\gamma^{12}\Psi_{-}\,,

(100)

and from that we find that the spectrum is

\displaystyle E=\frac{p}{2}+2n+1+\frac{s}{2},\ .

(101)

Supercharges have
\Delta=1/2
,
J_{1}=1/2
,
J_{2}=\pm 1/2
and therefore they do not commute with the Hamiltonian
\Delta-J_{1}-J_{2}
.
where
s=\pm 1
is the eigenvalue of
\gamma^{12}
.
As in the free scalar case, we observe that the energy eigenvalues do not depend on the parameter
k
. This is a direct manifestation of the
\mathbb{H}_{3}
symmetry.
And now we can find that

\displaystyle Z_{\rm sp}=L_{y}L_{v}\sum_{n,s}\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}\,\int^{2pL_{u}}_{0}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\,e^{-\beta p/2-\beta(2n+1)-\beta s/2}=\frac{V}{\pi^{2}\beta^{2}\sinh\frac{\beta}{2}}\ .

(102)

The free energy is

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{m}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2m^{3}\beta^{2}\sinh\frac{m\beta}{2}}\penalty\ ,

(103)

in agreement with the explicit operator counting.

4.5
Free Maxwell theory in 3+1 d

Another free theory that could be studied explicitly is a
U(1)
gauge theory in
3+1
d. Again for simplicity we will consider that
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon
. The spectrum of single particle excitations of this theory on
S^{3}\times S^{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}=l+1,SU(2)\times SU(2):\left(\frac{l+1}{2},\frac{l-1}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{l-1}{2},\frac{l+1}{2}\right)

(104)

From this we can find

\displaystyle Z_{\rm sp}(\beta,\epsilon)=\sum_{l}e^{-\beta(l+1)}\frac{l\sinh\left[\left(l+2\right)\beta(1-\epsilon)\right]+(l+2)\sinh\left[l\beta(1-\epsilon)\right]}{\sinh\beta(1-\epsilon)}=\frac{\coth\beta}{\beta^{2}\epsilon^{2}}+\ldots

And we can compute the following free-energy in this limit to be

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum\frac{\coth n\beta}{n^{3}\beta^{2}}+\ldots

(105)

Now using the metric of pp-wave we can compute the free energy of Maxwell field in that background. For that we write Maxwell’s equations in the background (
54
). In the lightcone gauge
A_{v}=0
and choosing the ansatz
A_{\mu}=a_{\mu}(y,t)e^{ipv+iky}
we get the following independent equations

\displaystyle pa_{t}+(2pu-k)a_{y}+i\partial_{y}a_{u}=0,

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}a_{u}=\frac{p}{2}a_{u}-\frac{\partial_{y}^{2}}{2p}a_{u}+2p\left(u-\frac{k}{2p}\right)^{2}a_{u}+ia_{y},

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}a_{y}=\frac{p}{2}a_{y}-\frac{\partial_{y}^{2}}{2p}a_{y}+2p\left(u-\frac{k}{2p}\right)^{2}a_{y}-ia_{u},

(106)

Introducing
a_{\pm}=a_{u}\pm ia_{y}
we get that the first equation determines
a_{t}
through
a_{y}
and
a_{u}
while the second equation gives the following Schrodinger problem

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}a_{\pm}=\frac{p}{2}a_{\pm}-\frac{\partial_{y}^{2}}{2p}a_{\pm}+2p\left(u-\frac{k}{2p}\right)^{2}a_{\pm}\pm a_{\pm}

(107)

Giving the spectrum

\displaystyle E_{\pm,n,p}=\frac{p}{2}+\left(2n+1\right)\pm 1,\quad Z_{\rm sp}=\sum_{n,s}L_{y}L_{v}\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}\int\limits^{2pL_{u}}_{0}\frac{dk}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}p-(2n+1\pm s)\beta}=\frac{2V\coth\beta}{\pi^{2}\epsilon^{2}\beta^{2}}

\displaystyle\log Z=\frac{2V}{\pi^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{m}\frac{\coth m\beta}{m^{2}\beta^{2}}

(108)

again taking into account that we have Landau levels we can compute the free energy

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{m}\frac{\coth m\beta}{\beta^{2}m^{3}}\ .

(109)

Once again, let us emphasize that—as in the free scalar and free fermion cases—the
k
-independence of the spectrum is a manifestation of the
\mathbb{H}_{3}
symmetry.

4.6
Wilson–Fisher fixed point in
d=4-\varepsilon
dimensions

In this section we will consider the example of an interacting theory, where we can compute the corresponding free energy for
\tau=\Delta-J_{12}
as a function of temperature
\beta
. For that we will consider usual Wilson-Fisher theory
Wilson:1971dc
in
d=4-\varepsilon
dimensions in the background of a pp wave geometry corresponding to fast rotation in one plane

\displaystyle S=\int d^{d}x\,\sqrt{g}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^{4}\right]\ ,\qquad ds^{2}=-\Bigl(1+\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}x_{i}^{2}\Bigr)dt^{2}+\,dx\,dt+\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}dx_{i}^{2}\ ,

(110)

where we have also used that the Ricci scalar vanishes
R=0
. At leading order in
\varepsilon\equiv 4-d
, the contribution to the free energy comes from two sources: the dependence of the free theory on the dimension, and the first-order correction in perturbation theory in
\lambda
(equivalently, in
\varepsilon
), since the fixed point is located at
Wilson:1971dc
;
Henriksson:2022rnm

\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{(4\pi)^{2}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})\ .

(111)

Using that

\displaystyle-\log\operatorname{tr}e^{-\beta H_{0}-\beta V}=F_{0}+\beta\langle V\rangle_{\beta,0}+\ldots,

(112)

we see that the first correction in perturbation theory is

\displaystyle\delta F=\frac{\lambda\beta}{32}\int d^{d-1}x\langle{\phi^{2}\rangle}^{2}_{T},

(113)

where we used that in the leading order in
\varepsilon
our theory would have correlators that could be computed using Wick’s theorem.

\displaystyle\langle{\phi^{2}\rangle}_{T}=\sum_{n_{i}}\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}\frac{1}{p}\frac{\prod^{d-2}_{i=1}\psi_{n_{i}}^{2}(x_{i})}{e^{\beta\left(p+\sum\limits^{d-2}_{i=1}\left(n_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)}-1}=\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-\beta mp}}{p}\prod\limits^{d-2}_{i=1}\left(\psi^{2}_{n_{i}}(x_{i})e^{-m\beta(n_{i}+\frac{1}{2})}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\int\frac{dp}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{e^{-p\beta m-2pr^{2}\tanh\left(\frac{m\beta}{2}\right)}}{\sinh m\beta}=\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}\sinh m\beta}\frac{1}{m\beta+r^{2}\tanh\frac{m\beta}{2}}\approx\frac{1}{12\,(1+r^{2})\beta^{2}}

(114)

Then the answer for the first correction to the partition function in the
\varepsilon
expansion is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \log Z(\beta,\epsilon)&=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{m}\frac{1}{m^{2}\beta\left(2\sinh\frac{m\beta}{2}\right)^{d-2}}\\
&\quad+\frac{\varepsilon}{12\epsilon}\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sinh n\beta\sinh m\beta}\left[\frac{\ln\left(\frac{n\tanh\frac{m\beta}{2}}{m\tanh\frac{n\beta}{2}}\right)}{n\tanh\frac{m\beta}{2}-m\tanh\frac{n\beta}{2}}\right]\end{aligned}

Note that, as in the free-field examples, the function
F(\beta)
is analytic for any value of
\beta
. Therefore, we conclude that at first order in
\varepsilon
there is no phase transition in the twist spectrum at large spin.

Let us see how this function behaves for small
\beta

\displaystyle\log Z=\frac{\zeta(4-\varepsilon)}{\beta^{3-\epsilon}\epsilon}+\frac{\pi^{4}\varepsilon}{216\beta^{3}\epsilon}=-\beta F_{4-\varepsilon}(\beta)V_{4-\varepsilon},

(115)

where
F_{4-\varepsilon}
is the free energy of WF theory in
d=4-\varepsilon
dimensions at inverse temperature
\beta
. And the low temperature behavior of this function yields

\displaystyle\log Z=\frac{1}{2^{d-2}\beta\epsilon}e^{-\beta\frac{d-2}{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3\epsilon}e^{-2\beta},

(116)

we see that the leading asymptotics did not change, implying that
\tau_{\rm min}
does not get corrections in the first order of
\epsilon
expansion. This is as expected.

The same computation can be carried out for the
\mathrm{O}(N)
model in the
\varepsilon
-expansion by considering
N
scalar fields and evaluating the theory at the
\mathrm{O}(N)
fixed point, where
Henriksson:2022rnm

\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{(4\pi)^{2}}=\frac{3\varepsilon}{N+8}+\mathrm{O}(\varepsilon^{2})\,.

That yields

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \log Z(\beta,\epsilon)&=\frac{N}{\epsilon}\sum_{m}\frac{1}{m^{2}\beta\left(2\sinh\frac{m\beta}{2}\right)^{d-2}}\\
&\quad+\frac{\varepsilon N(N+2)}{4\epsilon(N+8)}\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sinh n\beta\sinh m\beta}\left[\frac{\ln\left(\frac{n\tanh\frac{m\beta}{2}}{m\tanh\frac{n\beta}{2}}\right)}{n\tanh\frac{m\beta}{2}-m\tanh\frac{n\beta}{2}}\right]\end{aligned}

One again finds that

remains analytic for any value of
\beta
and for any
N
.

Appendix A
Conformal Algebra and Symmetries of pp-waves

Consider the Brinkmann pp-wave metric:

ds^{2}=-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}x_{i}^{2}\,dt^{2}+dt\,dx+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}dx_{i}^{2},

To reveakl the conformal structure of this spacetime, we introduce the following coordinate transformations:

u=\tan t,\qquad v=r^{2}\tan t-x,\qquad y_{i}=\frac{x_{i}}{\cos t}

Under this coordinate transformation, the metric takes a manifestly conformally flat form:

ds^{2}=\cos^{2}t\,(-\,du\,dv+dy_{i}^{\,2})

(117)

Because the metric is conformally equivalent to the flat metric, its conformal Killing algebra is maximal and isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}(d+1,2)
. We can construct an explicit basis for this algebra by pulling back the standard conformal generators of the flat metric.
To express these generators in the original
(t,x,x_{i})
coordinates, we will use that

\displaystyle\partial_{u}

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}t\,\partial_{t}-\sin t\cos t\,x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}+r^{2}\cos 2t\,\partial_{x}

\displaystyle\partial_{v}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{i}}

\displaystyle=\cos t\,\partial_{x_{i}}+2x_{i}\sin t\,\partial_{x}

Using these relations, the full
(d+2)(d+3)/2
-dimensional Conformal Killing Vector (CKV) basis—with
u,v,y_{i}
understood as functions of
(t,x,x_{i})
—can be written as follows:

\star

the usual
translations
take the form

\displaystyle P_{u}=-i\partial_{u},\quad P_{v}=-i\partial_{v},\quad P_{i}=-i\partial_{y_{i}}\ ,

(118)

Because of our choice of coordinates we must have
-iP_{u,v}\geq 0
.

\star

Rotations
and
Boosts

\displaystyle M_{ij}=y_{i}\partial_{y_{j}}-y_{j}\partial_{y_{i}}=x_{i}\partial_{x_{j}}-x_{j}\partial_{x_{i}},

\displaystyle M_{uv}=\frac{1}{2}u\partial_{u}-\frac{1}{2}v\partial_{v},\qquad M_{ui}=-\frac{1}{2}v\partial_{y_{i}}-y_{i}\partial_{u},\qquad M_{vi}=-\frac{1}{2}u\partial_{y_{i}}-y_{i}\partial_{v}\ ,

(119)

\star

Dilation

\displaystyle D=u\partial_{u}+v\partial_{v}+y_{i}\partial_{y_{i}}\ ,

(120)

\star

and finally
Special Conformal Transformations
:

\displaystyle K_{u}=iv\,D+i(-uv+y^{2})\partial_{u},\quad K_{v}=iu\,D+i(-uv+y^{2})\partial_{v}

\displaystyle K_{i}=-2iy_{i}\,D+i(-uv+y^{2})\partial_{y_{i}}\ .

(121)

These vector fields satisfy the standard
\mathfrak{so}(d+1,2)
commutation relations. Let us note the time generator in this basis is

\displaystyle H=P_{u}-K_{v}

(122)

Now we can do a similarity transformation

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}(P_{u}+K_{v})}He^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}(P_{u}+K_{v})}=\left(D+2M_{uv}\right)\geq 0

(123)

that is following from a standard unitary bound, this Hamiltonian has an infinite degeneracy. For instance,

\displaystyle\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\quad\partial_{+}^{m}\partial_{z}^{n}\phi,\quad H=n+\frac{1}{2},

(124)

leads to the infinite degeneracy. We can remove this degeneracy by changing the Hamiltonian or changing our metric. Thus, in the case of the metric (
61
) we have to make a transformation
x\to x+t
that enforces the Hamiltonian to be

\displaystyle H=P_{u}+P_{v}-K_{v},

(125)

that is again positive since
P_{v}\geq 0
. The other way to see it is to write an overlap of two states
|\Psi_{f}\rangle=\int d^{d}xf(x)\mathcal{O}(x)|0\rangle
and see that unitarity demands that particular combination to be positive
Mack:1975je
. We can make the same transformation to get

\displaystyle H=\Delta+2M_{uv}+P_{v},

(126)

because
[P_{v},P_{u}]=[P_{v},K_{v}]=0
. And we see that
H
