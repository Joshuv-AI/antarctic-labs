Optical communication network signal analysis and cyber security modelling by frequency modulation with machine learning algorithm
Authors: Ramesh Kumar Ayyasamy, Baddepaka Prasad, Chinnasamy Ponnusamy, Srikanth Cherukuvada, N V S M Reddy, Sk Khaja Shareef, Michael Melese Woldeyohannis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMachine learning (ML) methods are commonly used in optical communication networks to enhance bandwidth analysis through efficient resource management and performance monitoring, and to bolster cybersecurity through threat detection and proactive failure management. This research proposes a novel technique for bandwidth analysis of optical communication network signals, with cybersecurity modelling using frequency modulation and machine learning. Here, the optical communication network signal bandwidth is analysed using a dynamic frequency-modulated support vector machine algorithm. Network cybersecurity analysis is carried out using blockchain-reinforced adversarial Bayesian neural networks (NNs). Experimental analysis is based on signal-based cybersecurity modelling and bandwidth analysis. The proposed technique achieves more accurate bandwidth estimation with an error of 3%, a throughput of 97 Gbps, and a spectral efficiency of 4.82 b/s/Hz. By ensuring secure and efficient optical network operation, it achieves 98% attack-detection accuracy with 1.5% false positives in terms of security.Keywords: Optical communication network, Bandwidth analysis, Cyber security, Frequency modulation, Machine learning techniquesSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Optical communication methods have become the foundation of contemporary digital infrastructure as demand for high-speed, high-capacity communication continues to rise globally, driven by cloud computing, AI, 5G, virtual reality, and IoT. However, as these methods’ operational scale, performance demands, and complexity grow, they are beginning to exceed the capacities of conventional analytical and rule-based design techniques. ML has emerged as a transformative tool in this setting, enabling data-driven solutions that identify hidden patterns, optimise performance across the optical communication stack, and adapt to changing conditions. ML techniques, such as NNs and generative methods, are used for non-linear compensation, channel equalisation, and signal reconstruction in long-haul coherent optical systems, where signal integrity is threatened by fibre nonlinearities and polarisation effects over hundreds of kilometres1. For example, DL methods trained on simulated or real-world data can outperform digital backpropagation or traditional Volterra filters, offering greater flexibility and accuracy. ML is increasingly used to compensate for combined linear and non-linear impairments in short-reach and direct-detection systems, such as intensity-modulated direct-detection (IM/DD) lines commonly found in data centres. For instance, in low-cost hardware implementations like silicon photonic transceivers, vertical cavity surface emitting lasers (VCSELs), directly modulated lasers (DMLs), NNs, and support vector machines (SVMs) are demonstrated to improve equalisation, bit error rate (BER) performance, and adapt to non-idealities2. Optical access systems and passive optical networks (PONs) require high bandwidth at low cost with low latency and excellent dependability. In this case, machine learning is also used to construct intelligent receivers, improving system performance and robustness. The computational complexity of analytical/numerical computations for Optical Communication (OC) methods/networks is a primary motivation for combining ML with OC3,4. An example of the Machine as a body that draws its breath away from its entry5. Optical networks are susceptible to numerous attacks and eavesdropping due to specific vulnerabilities. Eavesdropping and attacks on optical networks are mostly thought to occur at the physical layer, in contrast to data-level attacks or data leakage on the Internet6. Physical-layer flaws in optical networks have attracted greater attention from researchers in recent years. Threats to optical networks at the physical layer typically divide into three categories: (1) eavesdropping; (2) masquerade attacks, which allow unauthorised users to access optical nodes covertly; and (3) jamming attacks, which stop optical networks from offering services. Optical-layer security techniques are necessary because packet-level security measures are inadequate to address threats to the optical layer. Although machine learning has advanced significantly, its applications in OC are still in their infancy. ML for OC uses WDM, OFDM, Spatial Division Multiplexing (SDM), and various modulations, such as Differential Phase Shift Keying (DPSK), Amplitude Phase Shift Keying (APSK), PAM, and On-Off Keying (OOK), as well as QAM7. ML for OC literature also examines a wide range of OC system/network topics, including performance monitoring, phase modulation, fault detection, synchronisation, fibre nonlinearity equalisation, wavelength assignment, blocking probability calculation, network performance evaluation, dynamic bandwidth allocation, bit error rate prediction, quality of transmission calculation, network optimisation, lightpath request, constellation shaping, virtual network slicing, adaptive equaliser, knowledge. Additionally, a variety of ML algorithms are employed, including SVM, ANN, DNN, CNN, KNN, Random Forest, Regression, k-means, PCA, Q-Learning, and Deep Reinforcement Learning. Due to the transparency of optical networks, it is challenging to detect and locate physical-layer attacks because they may spread across numerous nodes or links, making localisation difficult. Since attacks cannot be detected at the data level and traffic is not processed electronically in transparent optical networks, detecting attacks depends on precise, trustworthy physical-layer monitoring, which further complicates matters. However, by providing unauthorised signals in place of or in addition to authorised signals, masquerade attackers can enter the network, making it challenging to identify the attacks using traditional techniques. The proposal for secure communications over a public host channel highlights the potential for malevolent users to use these channels for attacks. Signal insertion attack detection and localisation techniques are therefore necessary8. Unlike existing studies that independently investigate machine-learning-based bandwidth estimation or intrusion detection in optical networks, this work introduces a unified learning framework that jointly addresses physical-layer signal bandwidth analysis and cybersecurity modelling. The proposed approach uniquely leverages frequency-domain signal characteristics extracted from optical transmissions to simultaneously guide adaptive bandwidth estimation and security-aware decision-making. By coupling dynamic frequency-modulated learning with intelligent cybersecurity, the proposed framework enables attack-aware spectrum utilisation and security-informed bandwidth optimisation, both of which have not been explored in prior optical communication literature.

This paper is organised as follows: Sect. 1 introduces the problem motivation and background, while Sect. 2 reviews related work and compares existing intrusion detection methods. Section 3 presents the proposed method and its methodology, followed by experimental results and performance evaluation in Sect. 4. Final Sect. 5 concludes with remarks on future directions.

Literature survey

ML for bandwidth estimation in optical communication networks

Recent surveys have comprehensively examined the application of artificial intelligence and machine learning in optical communication (OC) networks. Musumeci et al.4 presented an overview of ML techniques used in optical communications and networking. Tiwari et al.9 presented ML for OC to provide a network-layer perspective, initially reviewing physical-layer applications such as quality-of-transmission (QoT) estimation, fibre nonlinearity mitigation, and optical performance monitoring (OPM), followed by planning and operational aspects of transport and access networks. Similarly, Pointurier10 discussed the sources of inaccuracy in estimating QoT and proposed a taxonomy for ML-aided QoT estimation. Seok et al.11 investigated ML-based failure management in optical networks, demonstrating improved fault-detection accuracy and enhanced network resilience. Furdek et al.3 investigated the challenges affecting the performance of ML-based approaches for detecting and localizing optical-layer attacks and incorporating them into Network Management Systems (NMSs).

Signal processing techniques are used for bandwidth evaluation and spectrum analysis in optical methods. FFT-based thresholding methods are among the earliest, in which bandwidth occupancy is determined by applying fixed or adaptive thresholds to the frequency-domain power spectrum. Although computationally efficient, FFT-based techniques are susceptible to noise, spectral leakage, and non-stationary signal conditions, limiting their effectiveness in dynamic as well as elastic optical environments12.

To improve robustness, wavelet transform–based techniques combined with ML classifiers are explored. Wavelet-domain feature extraction enables multi-resolution analysis of optical signals by capturing both temporal and spectral characteristics. When integrated with SVM, Wavelet–SVM methods outperform pure FFT-based techniques; their performance depends strongly on the choice of wavelet basis and handcrafted features, limiting adaptability across varying modulation formats and channel conditions13.

ANNs are applied for bandwidth and performance evaluation by learning nonlinear mappings from extracted signal features and bandwidth metrics. ANN-based methods achieve higher prediction accuracy than traditional methods but often require large, labelled datasets and careful hyperparameter tuning to avoid overfitting. Fixed ANN architectures may struggle to adapt to rapid spectral variations in elastic or dynamically reconfigured optical networks14.

Kalman filter–assisted ML methods are proposed for adaptive bandwidth tracking, particularly in scenarios with temporal correlation. By combining state-space evaluation with learning-based prediction, Kalman–ML methods improve tracking stability under gradual changes in bandwidth. Their significance degrades in the presence of abrupt spectral changes or attack-induced anomalies, where the underlying methods’ assumptions no longer hold15.

Metaheuristic optimisation methods, such as particle swarm optimisation (PSO), are used to improve SVM-based bandwidth estimation by optimising kernel parameters. PSO-optimised SVM methods achieve improved convergence and accuracy compared to standard SVMs, but they introduce additional computational overhead and remain sensitive to noisy or adversarial spectral inputs.

DL–based spectrum analysis techniques gained attention. CNNs are utilised to automatically extract discriminative features from spectral representations, which are subsequently classified using ensemble learners such as random forests. CNN–RF methods significantly reduce reliance on handcrafted features and achieve higher estimation accuracy; however, they demand substantial computational resources and may exhibit limited interpretability and generalisation in low-SNR or attack-prone environments16.

ML for optical physical-layer security

Beyond bandwidth estimation, ML is increasingly applied to physical-layer security challenges in optical networks. Due to the transparent nature of optical transmission, physical-layer attacks such as jamming, fibre tapping, and masquerade signal injection are challenging to detect using traditional packet-level security methods. Accurate detection and localisation of such attacks rely heavily on physical-layer monitoring and intelligent signal analysis.

QoT prediction and anomaly detection using RF, NN, and SVMs are explored in demonstrating ML’s capacity to identify performance degradation caused by impairments or malicious activities17. In addition, Lian et al.18 conducted a comprehensive review of ML-based OPM techniques, including signal quality monitoring, impairment detection, fault localisation, and bit rate–modulation format identification (BR-MFI), enabling proactive detection of abnormal optical behaviour.

A focused review of ML for coherent optical-OFDM systems, where learning-based methods such as linear prediction, Volterra-series methods, and NNs are systematically analysed and compared with conventional digital signal processing techniques19. The techniques exhibit greater resilience to fibre nonlinearities and channel impairments, enabling them to detect subtle physical-layer attacks. The combination of MLPs and RBFNNs provides practical solutions for both fibre nonlinearities and the detection of unusual signal patterns. ANN-based nonlinear equalisation achieves a 2 dB improvement in Q-factor over Volterra-based methods in long-haul coherent systems20. In comparison, RBFNN-based equalisation results in an 8.7 dB improvement in Q-factor and better transmission performance21. The results demonstrate how machine learning methods can effectively model complex physical-layer disturbances indicative of unauthorised access attempts.

Blockchain-based intrusion detection and network security

Blockchain technology offers a trustworthy solution that requires secure network protection and enhances detection methods through machine learning. The initial research efforts focused on basic cybersecurity applications, while recent studies investigated ways to implement blockchain technology for secure network monitoring, intrusion detection, and access management. Adibnia et al.22 presented a comprehensive survey demonstrating how machine learning-based security system management operates in optical networks. Machine learning technology helps build better network protection systems and creates more efficient methods for handling failures. ML-based routing optimisation in software-defined networks (SDNs) is widely studied, with surveys that categorise supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning methods for adaptive routing decisions23. A comprehensive overview of routing and resource allocation (RRA) is performed in optical networks using ML that covers routing and wavelength allocation (RWA) in WDM networks, routing and spectrum allocation (RSA) in elastic optical networks (EONs), and routing, core, and spectrum allocation (RCSA) in spatial division multiplexing (SDM) methods24. This study further discusses ML-based solutions for QoT estimation, traffic prediction, and crosstalk mitigation.

Recent studies demonstrate the transformative potential of ML in real-world optical network operations, with practical case studies illustrating applications such as predictive maintenance, virtual topology management, capacity optimisation, and optical spectral analysis25,26. Existing blockchain-based methods primarily focus on generic network security and lack tight integration with physical-layer optical signal intelligence, motivating the need for blockchain-enabled intrusion detection methods that leverage ML-driven physical-layer features for secure, adaptive optical network operation.

Problem formulation

Despite significant advances in ML–driven optical communication methods, existing methods treat bandwidth evaluation and physical-layer security as independent issues, relying on static spectral features or isolated intrusion-detection methods. Classical signal processing techniques are not robust to noise and dynamic traffic conditions. At the same time, ML-based bandwidth evaluators often lack adaptability to abrupt spectral variations and adversarial signal behaviour. Optical IDS typically operates unaware of real-time bandwidth dynamics and relies on centralised architectures that are vulnerable to data tampering and delayed responses. The absence of uncertainty-aware detection and of secure, distributed telemetry limits the reliability of these systems in detecting stealthy or zero-day attacks. The core issue addressed in this work is the design of a unified, signal-driven learning framework that can accurately evaluate optical bandwidth under dynamic conditions while simultaneously detecting and mitigating physical-layer cyber threats, with minimal latency, scalable operation, and verifiable security.

Proposed system model

Our proposed system begins with the acquisition of optical signals from an optical communication network, where signal processing and feature extraction are performed through filtering, demodulation, and spectrum analysis to obtain meaningful signal characteristics, as shown in Fig. 1. These extracted features are simultaneously fed into 2 coordinated modules: the adaptive bandwidth analysis module and the cybersecurity modeling module. Along the bandwidth analysis path, a dynamic frequency-modulated Support Vector Machine learns frequency variations and signal patterns to estimate available bandwidth and optimize utilization accurately. In parallel, the cybersecurity module employs a blockchain-enabled reinforcement-adversarial Bayesian neural network, where network activities are securely logged in a distributed ledger, reinforcement learning adapts to evolving threats, and Bayesian inference improves attack-detection reliability. The outputs of both modules are integrated in a performance optimization layer, resulting in optimized bandwidth utilization and secured network operation. Overall, the architecture ensures enhanced network performance by jointly improving communication efficiency and cyber resilience in optical communication networks.

Feature extraction

In the proposed framework, signal processing and feature extraction are carried out through a structured sequence of filtering, demodulation, and spectrum analysis to convert raw optical signals into meaningful learning features. Initially, optical signals are passed through noise-reduction and bandpass filtering to suppress channel noise, dispersion, and interference. The filtered signals are then demodulated to recover the baseband information and extract modulation-dependent parameters such as amplitude, phase, and instantaneous frequency. Subsequently, spectrum analysis is performed using frequency-domain transformations to estimate bandwidth occupancy, power spectral density, and frequency variations. From this stage, discriminative features, including spectral width, signal-to-noise ratio, frequency deviation, and energy distribution, are extracted and forwarded to the bandwidth estimation and cybersecurity learning modules for further analysis.

Results and performance evaluations

All reported experimental results are obtained by averaging outcomes across five independent runs with different random initializations. Performance metrics are reported as mean values, and standard deviations were observed to remain within ± 1.2% for accuracy-related measures, confirming statistical consistency of results.

Results and performance evaluation of the proposed optical communication network framework are conducted to validate the effectiveness of the Dynamic Frequency Modulation–based SVM for bandwidth signal analysis, as well as the Blockchain Reinforcement Adversarial Bayesian Neural Network for cybersecurity modeling. The researchers evaluate their proposed method through extensive testing, comparing it to various advanced techniques and measuring system performance using specific key metrics. The evaluation highlights numerical improvements achieved by the proposed model in both communication efficiency and security resilience, demonstrating its suitability for next-generation high-speed, as well as secure, optical networks.

Computational complexity and scalability analysis

The computational complexity of the proposed framework is analyzed with respect to the number of signal samples, N, and the number of network nodes, M. The DFM-SVM training complexity is O(N²) in the worst case. In contrast, inference complexity is O(N_s), where N_s denotes the number of support vectors. Training is performed offline, and real-time bandwidth estimation remains computationally efficient.

The BR-ABNN security method has a time complexity of O(E × L × H), where E denotes the number of training epochs, L denotes the number of layers, and H denotes the number of neurons per layer. Blockchain operations incur linear overhead O(M) with respect to the number of participating nodes, as block validation is executed asynchronously.

Memory usage scales linearly with both feature dimensionality and the number of stored blockchain transactions. Experimental latency estimation validates stable performance up to 40 nodes, demonstrating that the proposed approach scales well for medium-scale optical networks.

Discussion

The results of the current experimental research demonstrate that the proposed integrated framework combining DFM-SVM for bandwidth signal analysis and BR-ABNN for cybersecurity modeling is an effective solution. For Bandwidth Signal Analysis, the proposed DFM-SVM produces an estimation error of less than 3.1%, which is 29.4% lower than the best existing solution. The throughput of the proposed DFM-SVM system is 97 Gbps, and its spectral efficiency is 4.83 b/s/Hz, both of which demonstrate improved spectral utilization. The end-to-end latency of the proposed DFM-SVM system is at least 2.2 ms, indicating that a DFM-SVM solution can achieve faster signal processing and adapt to changing network conditions. The robustness analysis conducted as part of this study shows that the estimation error will be less than 2.4% at high SNR levels, providing evidence that DFM-SVM solutions will be stable in noisy environments. In Cyber Security Modeling, the proposed BR-ABNN achieved a detection rate of 98%, surpassing those of the RL-DQN and GNN-based intrusion detection systems. The false-positive rate of the proposed BR-ABNN is 1.6%, reducing unwarranted alerts and improving overall operational reliability. The detection latency of the BR-ABNN was 10.8 ms, indicating that incorporating blockchain into the system will not incur any additional hardware overhead. When evaluated across increasing levels of attack intensity, the BR-ABNN’s successful detection rate remained above 95%, indicating its ability to withstand adversaries.

The results demonstrate the superiority of the proposed approach through overall parametric comparisons, achieving a normalized performance score of 94% for the proposed method, versus 88% for RL-DQN and 85% for CNN + RF. These numerical improvements further illustrate that by jointly optimizing bandwidth efficiency and cybersecurity, significantly better performance can be achieved when compared to treating them separately as independent issues.

Limitations and future scope

The framework under investigation in this research heavily relies on simulated optical network data. This means it may not be directly applicable to real-world heterogeneous deployments. The use of blockchain will add latency to applications that require ultra-low latency or are subject to extreme traffic loads. Additionally, the scale of the network will increase computational overhead; therefore, it is necessary to consider future hardware acceleration and consensus methods that require minimal resources (i.e., lightweight). Future work will validate the proposed framework using real-time experimental test beds and diverse fiber types. The goal of these future studies would be to improve the practical relevance of the proposed framework. Investigating how quantum-safe blockchain methods can help reduce potential future risks posed by emerging cryptographic threats will also be addressed. Moreover, expanding the proposed methods to accommodate 5G/6G fronthaul and edge-enabled optical architecture will also be investigated. Finally, utilizing FPGA and/or photonic processors for hardware acceleration to reduce latency and energy consumption will also be studied further.

Conclusion

This study provided proof of an innovative bandwidth analysis approach based on signals and a cybersecurity framework for optical communications. The integration of dynamic frequency modulation (DFM) with ML via a support vector machine (SVM) yields the DFM-SVM, improving bandwidth estimation accuracy and increasing throughput and spectral efficiency. By leveraging blockchain technology to enable reinforcement learning and adversarial Bayesian learning for decentralized validation and intelligent attack detection, this new framework will also deliver a much higher level of security in optical networks. Experimental results show that this new framework produces substantially better results than any state-of-the-art technique across all measurements. Results confirm that the proposed method is robust, scalable, and suitable for next-generation high-speed optical networks that need both high performance and strong cyber resilience. Adaptive quantum-safe blockchain methods are incorporated to address future cryptographic threats. Additionally, extending the framework to support 5G/6G fronthaul and edge-based optical networks could further broaden its applicability. Hardware-level optimization using FPGAs or photonic processors is explored to minimize computational latency and power consumption.
