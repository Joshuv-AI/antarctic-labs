After-Action Report

Class # 17-02
CIA Special Services Branch Canine Unit
July 31 — November 17, 2017

Background — The foundation for the course was a based on
classical conditioning paired with a food reward methodology and a passive final

response that is indicated by the dog through a change in behavior when exposed to an

explosives odor.

Purpose/Objectives — The purpose of this course is to train canines that can safely and
effectively conduct a basic detection search of luggage, mail, post blast scenes, vehicles,
buildings, and open areas for explosives and weapons.

The objective of this course is to develop a new student and canine into an effective team
while emphasizing to the handler to think on his/her own, and to work independently.
The student is taught how to evaluate a situation that requires an explosive detection
capability and correctly employ his/her team for maximum effectiveness while
understanding the team’s limitations.

Prior to departing the CIA Canine Training Center, the student has a working knowledge
of how to conduct effective explosives and scent discrimination training, and is taught
how to maintain their assigned canine in the optimal physical condition at all times.

Attendance - 1 Frederick County Fire Marshall, 1 Fairfax County Police Officer and
CIA Police Officers

Customer Satisfaction Data — A copy of the Student Critiques has been forwarded to
the Chief, CIA Security Protective Service for review.

Training Assessment —The One dog from Puppies Behind Bars and five dogs from
Susquehanna Service dogs with the five handlers that were paired with them performed
very well (see below on the sixth). All the dogs are very confident and show no signs of
anxiety in any environment and grew in confidence as the weeks went on. The teams
graduated class with all the necessary training to prepare them for work in the field. We
are confident that the five dogs and handlers will have long careers as strong successful
teams. :
