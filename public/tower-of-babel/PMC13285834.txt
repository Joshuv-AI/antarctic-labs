Reply
Authors: Alexander I Papolos, Christopher F Barnett, Jacob C Jentzer
Published: 2026

We thank Dr Lotfi for the thoughtful comments on our recent publication and for highlighting important methodological considerations inherent to survey-based research.1 We agree with these limitations and have acknowledged them in the manuscript.

Cardiac intensive care units (CICUs) staffed by physicians trained and board-certified in both critical care medicine and cardiology were exceedingly rare as recently as 15 years ago but are now increasingly common across both academic and nonacademic centers. However, approaches to critical care cardiology (CCC) job structure and CICU organization remain highly variable. Our survey represents only the second study examining CICU staffing and care models and was designed to provide practical insights for cardiology leaders seeking to establish or expand CCC programs.1,2

We agree that representativeness is an important consideration, as the response rate was modest and respondents were predominantly from academic centers with Level 1 CICUs. Our intent, however, was to characterize the experiences of contemporary CCC practitioners, recognizing that many practice in academic settings.

As no validated survey instrument existed, we developed a novel tool for this study. To enhance inclusiveness, certain items permitted nonmutually exclusive responses, and our definition of high-intensity staffing was intentionally broad to reflect the diversity of real-world CICU organizational structures. We agree that the development of standardized definitions and institution-level analyses will be important in future investigations.

While our cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and assessment of temporal trends, our findings, when considered alongside prior surveys, suggest a directional shift in CICU organization, with increased incorporation of CCC-trained physicians and high-intensity care models. The observed associations between career satisfaction and work-life domains are noncausal and hypothesis-generating but may offer insights into workforce sustainability for leaders developing CCC programs and recruiting clinicians.

We agree that future studies incorporating longitudinal designs, standardized definitions, and linkage to patient-centered outcomes will be essential. Nevertheless, we believe our study provides meaningful contemporary insights and establishes a foundation for further investigation in critical care cardiology practice.
