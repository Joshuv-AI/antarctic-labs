DEGENERATION ORDER OF 3 × 3 3\times 3 NILPOTENT MATRIX TUPLES Thanks: Partially supported by the Hungarian National Research, Development and Innovation Office, NKFIH K 138828.
Mátyás Domokos, Botond Miklósi

Abstract
. The degeneration order of simultaneous similarity classes of 3 × 3 3\times 3 nilpotent matrix tuples is determined, and is shown to be given by rank conditions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14L24; Secondary 14L30, 16G20

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Basic definitions.

For positive integers
n\geq 2
,
m
,
denote by
(K^{n\times n})^{m}
the space of
m
-tuples of
n\times n
matrices
over an algebraically closed field
K
.
The general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
acts on

(K^{n\times n})^{m}
by simultaneous conjugation:
for
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
and
A=(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})

we have
g\cdot A=(gA_{1}g^{-1},\dots,gA_{m}g^{-1})
.
We denote by
\mathcal{O}(A)
the

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-orbit of
A
.
We say that the matrix tuples
A
and
B
are
similar

if
\mathcal{O}(A)=\mathcal{O}(B)
.
Write
\overline{\mathcal{O}(A)}
for the closure of
\mathcal{O}(A)
in
(K^{n\times n})^{m}

with respect to the Zariski topology.

We shall consider two preorders on
(K^{n\times n})^{m}
.
We write
A\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}B
and say that
A
degenerates to
B
if

B\in\overline{\mathcal{O}(A)}
(or equivalently,

\mathcal{O}(B)\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}(A)}
).
Clearly,
\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}
induces a partial ordering on the set of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-orbits in

(K^{n\times n})^{m}
, called the
degeneration order
.

Let us turn to the second preorder. Let

R:=K\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\rangle
be the free
associative
K
-algebra on
m
generators
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}

(with
1\in R
).
Given an
f\in R
and
A\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}

it makes sense to substitute in
f
the non-commuting variables
x_{i}
by
A_{i}
, and we write

f(A)
for the resulting
n\times n
matrix.
Moreover, given an arbitrary matrix

\varphi=(\varphi_{ij})_{i=1,\dots,k}^{j=1,\dots,l}\in R^{k\times l}
,
set
\varphi(A):=(\varphi_{ij}(A))_{i=1,\dots,k}^{j=1,\dots,l}
, which is a

kn\times ln
matrix over
K
, partitioned into
k\times l
blocks of size
n\times n

(and
\varphi_{ij}(A)
is the
n\times n
block in the
(i,j)
position).
Denote by
\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A))
the
rank
of the
kn\times ln
matrix
\varphi(A)

over
K
.
Now for
A,B\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}
we set

A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
if
\mathrm{rk}\varphi(A)\geq\mathrm{rk}\varphi(B)

for all possible matrices
\varphi
over
R
.
By theorems of Auslander
[
2
]
and Riedtmann
[
29
]

(see Section
2
),

A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
and
B\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}A
both hold if and only if

\mathcal{O}(A)=\mathcal{O}(B)
. So

\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}
also induces a partial ordering (denoted by
\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}
as well)
on the set of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-orbits in
(K^{n\times n})^{m}
.
For reasons explained in Section
2
,
this is called the
hom-order
.
We shall use the notation
A<_{\mathrm{hom}}B
if
A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
and

\mathcal{O}(A)\neq\mathcal{O}(B)
.

We have the following well known lemma:

Lemma 1.1
.

A\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}B
implies
A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
for
A,B\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}
.

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in R^{k\times l}
,
A\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}
, and
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
,

\varphi(g\cdot A)
belongs to the
\mathrm{GL}_{nk}(K)\times\mathrm{GL}_{nl}(K)
-orbit of

\varphi(A)
, hence the function
\mathrm{rk}\circ\varphi
is constant along the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-orbits in

(K^{n\times n})^{m}
. On the other hand, for a fixed non-negative integer
r
, the set

\{B\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}\mid\mathrm{rk}\varphi(B)\leq r\}
is the common zero locus of the determinants
of the minors of size
(r+1)\times(r+1)
in
\varphi(B)
, hence is Zariski closed. Thus if

B
belongs to the Zariski closure of the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-orbit of
A
, then

\mathrm{rk}\varphi(B)\leq\mathrm{rk}\varphi(A)
.
∎

1.2.
Motivation.

The study of orbit closures is motivated by several areas of mathematics.
For example, basic problems of Geometric Complexity Theory ask to decide if an orbit under a linear action of a reductive group (say the general linear group) is contained in the Zariski closure of another orbit,
see for example
[
22
]
. So it is a natural and challenging problem
to characterize the preorder
\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}
. A possible candidate for such a characterization is offered
by
\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}
(see
[
9
]
,
[
14
]
). However,
the converse of Lemma
1.1
does not hold in general,
as an example attributed to Carlson in
[
29
]
shows (see also
[
10
]
,
and Remark
1.3
below).
Note also that the problem of classifying the similarity classes in
(K^{n\times n})^{2}
for all
n

is
wild
in the sense of
[
11
]
, but some approaches to it are given in

[
7
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
3
]
.

On the other hand, there are some notable partial converses for Lemma
1.1

in the theory of finite dimensional modules over a finite dimensional associative algebra.
Note that for a fixed finite dimensional associative algebra
\Lambda
generated by
m
elements,
the space
(K^{n\times n})^{m}
contains a
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-invariant affine subvariety
denoted by
\mathrm{Rep}_{\Lambda}(n)
, such
that the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-orbits in
\mathrm{Rep}_{\Lambda}(n)
are in bijection with the isomorphism classes of

n
-dimensional
\Lambda
-modules. Under certain restrictions for
\Lambda
, it is true that the
restriction of
\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}
to
\mathrm{Rep}_{\Lambda}(n)
implies the restriction of
\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}
to

\mathrm{Rep}_{\Lambda}(n)
. This holds if
\Lambda
is a representation-finite algebra by

[
31
]
,
or when
\Lambda
is the path algebra of an extended Dynkin quiver
by
[
4
]
.

An exact module theoretic characterization of when
A\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}B
for

A,B\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\Lambda}(n)
was found in
[
29
]
,
[
32
]
.
The problem in a more geneal setup is considered in
[
12
]
.
See
[
30
]
and
[
28
]
for a survey on the topic.

1.3.
Content of the present paper.

The
nullcone

\mathcal{N}_{n,m}
of the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-variety

(K^{n\times n})^{m}
consists of the matrix tuples
A
such that the
Zariski closure of
\mathcal{O}(A)
contains the
zero tuple
\mathbf{0}
(i.e.
A\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}\mathbf{0}
).
It is also the common zero locus of all homogeneous
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-invariant polynomial functions
on
(K^{n\times n})^{m}
, so it is an affine
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-variety.
Note that the general notion of the nullcone for a representation of a reductive algebraic group plays
a distinguished role in geometric invariant theory
[
26
]
.

A central result in our paper is the following partial converse to
Lemma
1.1
:

Theorem 1.2
.

For
A,B\in\mathcal{N}_{3,m}
, we have that

A\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}B\iff A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B.

Remark 1.3
.

The analogues of Theorem
1.2
for
\mathcal{N}_{n,m}
with

n\geq 4
does not hold.
Carlson’s example
[
29
]
mentioned above provides explicit
pairs
A,B\in\mathcal{N}_{4,2}
with
A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
, such that

B\notin\overline{\mathcal{O}(A)}
(see also
[
10
]
).
So Theorem
1.2
shows that this example is in some sense minimal
possible.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we begin with Lemma
2.1
stating a module theoretic description
of the preorder
\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}
due to Riedtmann
[
29
]
; we present a new proof fitting to our notation.
Although
A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
for
A,B\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}
does not necessarily imply
B\in\overline{\mathcal{O}(A)}
in general, we show the weaker statement that
A<_{\mathrm{hom}}B
implies
\dim\mathcal{O}(B)<\dim\mathcal{O}(A)
;
this is the content of Lemma
2.2
,
proved by combining Lemma
2.1
with a result of Bongartz
[
6
]
.
Section
3
deals with the case of
2\times 2
matrices, and we prove in
Corollary
3.2
that
\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}
coincide on
(K^{2\times 2})^{m}
.
This is deduced from a more general result
dealing with modules having Jordan-Hölder composition length at most
2
.
Section
4
is devoted to a direct study of
\mathcal{N}_{3,2}
.
In Proposition
4.2
we classify
the similarity classes of
3\times 3
nilpotent matrix pairs,
in Proposition
4.3
we determine the degeneration order on them
(see Figure
1
),
and in Proposition
4.5
we point out that
the restrictions of
\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}
to
\mathcal{N}_{3,2}
coincide.
In Section
5
we deduce from Proposition
4.5

that
\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}
coincide on
\mathcal{N}_{3,m}
for all
m
, i.e.
Theorem
1.2
holds.
In Section
6
we review the Hesselink stratification of
\mathcal{N}_{3,2}
worked out in

[
24
]
, and describe how the
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(K)
-orbits listed in Proposition
4.2
are distributed among the Hesselink strata. In the final Section
7

we take into account the natural action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)

on
(K^{n\times n})^{2}
commuting with the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
-action.
We show that there are finitely many
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(K)\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)
-orbits
in
\mathcal{N}_{3,2}
and determine the degeneration order for the orbits
in Proposition
7.2
. In fact there is a
2
-dimensional subgroup

H
in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(K)
such that the number of

\mathrm{GL}_{3}(K)\times H

7.1
.

2.
Some module theory

For
A\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}
denote by
V_{A}
the
R
-module

V_{A}=K^{n}
with module structure
f\cdot v:=f(A)v
for

f\in R
and
v\in K^{n}
.
Clearly, the
R
-modules
V_{A}
and
V_{B}
are isomorphic
if and only if
\mathcal{O}(A)=\mathcal{O}(B)
.
The relation
\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}
is called the hom-preorder because of
Lemma
2.1
below. It is due to
Riedtmann
[
29
, Section 2.1, Lemma]
,
where it is stated and proved using the language of representations of quivers with admissible
relations.
Here we give a different proof, suited better to our notation.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
A,B\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
;

(ii)

\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,V_{A})\leq\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,V_{B})

holds for all
finite dimensional
R
-modules
X
.

(iii)

\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},X)\leq\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{B},X)

holds for all
finite dimensional
R
-modules
X
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
A matrix
\varphi\in R^{k\times l}
can be interpreted as the
R
-module
homomorphism
R^{k}\to R^{l}
,
[r_{1},\dots,r_{k}]\mapsto[r_{1},\dots,r_{k}]\cdot\varphi

(matrix multiplication on the right hand side).
Denote by
X
the cokernel of
\varphi:R^{k}\to R^{l}
.
Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(-,V_{A})
to the exact sequence

(1)

R^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}R^{l}\to X\to 0

we get the exact sequence

(2)

0\to\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,V_{A})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(R^{l},V_{A})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(R^{k},V_{A}).

Identify
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(R^{k},V_{A})
with
V_{A}^{k}
, in the standard way; that is,

(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in V_{A}^{k}
corresponds to the
R
-module homomorphism

(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}v_{i}
. Similarly, make the identification

\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(R^{l},V_{A})\cong V_{A}^{l}
. Then

\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\varphi,V_{A})
gets identified with the
K
-linear map

K^{nl}=V_{A}^{l}\to V_{A}^{k}=K^{nk}
given by
multiplication from the left by the matrix
\varphi(A)
.
It follows by (
2
) that

(3)

\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A))=\dim_{K}\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\varphi,V_{A}))=nl-\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,V_{A}).

Since any finite dimensional
R
-module
X
has a finite free resolution as in (
1
)
(see
[
25
, P. 458, Corollary]
),
(
3
) shows that (i) implies (ii).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Assume that (ii) holds for
A,B\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}
.
Take any matrix
\varphi\in R^{k\times l}
. Consider the exact sequence (
1
).
In particular,
X
denotes the cokernel of
\varphi
.
Formula (
3
) shows that

(4)

\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,V_{A})-\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,V_{B})=\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(B))-\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A)).

If
X
is finite dimensional, than we may conclude from (
4
) by assumption (i) that

0\geq\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(B))-\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A))
.

It remains to deal with the case when
X
is not finite dimensional.
The annihilator ideals
\mathrm{ann}_{R}(V_{A})
and
\mathrm{ann}_{R}(V_{B})

both have finite codimension (at most
n^{2}
) in
R
. Write
J
for the intersection of the two annihilators,
then
J
also has finite codimension in
R
. Set
\bar{X}:=X/JX
. Then
\bar{X}
is a finitely generated module
over the finite dimensional algebra
R/J
, hence
\bar{X}
is finite dimensional. Moreover, any
R
-module
homomorphism
X\to V_{A}
factors through
\bar{X}
, and similarly, any homomorphism
X\to V_{B}
factors through
\bar{X}
.
It follows that
\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,V_{A})=\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\bar{X},V_{A})

and
\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,V_{B})=\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\bar{X},V_{B})
.
Thus (
4
) yields

\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\bar{X},V_{A})-\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\bar{X},V_{B})=\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(B))-\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A)),

implying by (ii) the desired inequality
\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A))\geq\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(B))
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii):
For a
K
-vector space
X
write
X^{*}
for the dual space

\mathrm{Hom}_{K}(X,K)
; when
X
is a left
R
-module,
X^{*}
is naturally a right
R
-module.
Now let
X
be an arbitrary finite dimensional left
R
-module,
and take a free resolution

(5)

R^{l}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}R^{k}\to X^{*}\to 0.

This time the free right
R
-modules
R^{l}
,
R^{k}
consist of column vectors with entries from
R
,
and the map
\varphi
is multiplication from the left by

\varphi\in R^{k\times l}
.
Apply the functor
-\otimes_{R}V_{A}
to (
5
) to get the exact sequence

(6)

R^{l}\otimes_{R}V_{A}\to R^{k}\otimes_{R}V_{A}\to X^{*}\otimes_{R}V_{A}\to 0.

Note that we have the natural isomorphism

X^{*}\otimes_{R}V_{A}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},X)^{*}
.
Moreover,
R^{l}\otimes_{R}V_{A}\cong V_{A}^{l}=K^{nl}
,

R^{k}\otimes_{R}V_{A}\cong V_{A}^{k}=K^{nk}
as
K
-vector spaces, and
the map
\varphi\otimes_{R}\mathrm{id}_{V_{A}}
is identified with multiplication by the

nk\times nl
matrix
\varphi(A)
.
It follows by (
6
) that

(7)

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},X)=\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},X)^{*}=\dim_{K}(X^{*}\otimes_{R}V_{A})

\displaystyle=nk-\dim_{K}(\mathrm{Im}(\varphi\otimes_{R}\mathrm{id}_{V_{A}}))=nk-\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A)).

Thus
\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A))\geq\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(B))
implies

\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},X)\leq\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{B},X)
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Take an arbitrary matrix
\varphi\in R^{k\times l}
. Denote also by
\varphi
the right
R
-module homomorphism from

R^{l\times 1}\to R^{k\times 1}
given by multiplication from the left by
\varphi
, and let

Y
be the cokernel of
\varphi
, so we have the exact sequence

R^{l}\to R^{k}\to Y\to 0.

If
Y
is finite dimensional, then setting
X:=Y^{*}
, we conclude from
(
7
) that

\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},X)-\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{B},X)=\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(B))-\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A)),

and we are done.

Suppose finally that
Y
is not finite dimensional. For given
A
and
B
denote by
J
the intersection of the
of the annihilators ideals of
V_{A}
and
V_{B}
. Then
\bar{Y}:=Y/JY
is a finitely generated module over the finite dimensional algebra

R/J
, hence is finite dimensional over
K
,
Y\otimes_{R}V_{A}\cong\bar{Y}\otimes_{R}V_{A}
,

Y\otimes_{R}V_{B}\cong\bar{Y}\otimes_{R}V_{B}
.
Moreover, (
7
) implies that

\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(B))-\mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A))=\dim_{K}(\bar{Y}\otimes_{R}V_{A})-\dim_{K}(\bar{Y}\otimes_{R}V_{B})

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},X)^{*}-\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{B},X)^{*},

where
X=\bar{Y}^{*}
.
∎

Although the converse of Lemma
1.1
does not hold in general (i.e.

A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
does not imply
A\leq_{\mathrm{deg}}B
), the following weaker
statement holds, which is a consequence of Lemma
2.1
and a result of
Bongartz
[
6
]
:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A,B\in(K^{n\times n})^{m}
.
If
A<_{\mathrm{hom}}B
, then
\dim\mathcal{O}(A)>\dim\mathcal{O}(B)
.

Proof.

The dimension
\dim\mathcal{O}(A)
of the locally closed algebraic variety

\mathcal{O}(A)
equals
\dim\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)-\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)}(A)
.
The stabilizer
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)}(A)
is the group of units in the endomorphism algebra

\mathrm{End}_{R}(V_{A})
of the
R
-module
V_{A}
.
Thus we have
\dim\mathcal{O}(A)=n^{2}-\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},V_{A})
and

\dim\mathcal{O}(B)=n^{2}-\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{B},V_{B})
.
Consequently, we have

(8)

\dim\mathcal{O}(A)-\dim\mathcal{O}(B)=\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{B},V_{B})-\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},V_{A}).

By Lemma
2.1
,
A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
implies

(9)

\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},V_{A})\leq\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},V_{B})\leq\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{B},V_{B}).

Combining (
8
) and (
9
) we conclude that

A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
implies
\dim\mathcal{O}(A)\geq\dim\mathcal{O}(B)
.
Note finally that
[
6
, Lemma 1.2]
asserts that

A\leq_{\mathrm{hom}}B
and

\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{A},V_{A})=\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(V_{B},V_{B})

together imply
V_{A}\cong V_{B}
as
R
-modules (equivalently,

\mathcal{O}(A)=\mathcal{O}(B)
).
This finishes the proof of the implication
A<_{\mathrm{hom}}B\Rightarrow\dim\mathcal{O}(A)>\dim\mathcal{O}(B)
.
∎

3.
Modules with at most two composition factors

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
and
N
be
R
-modules,
\dim_{K}M=\dim_{K}N
.
Assume that
M

or
N
has
Jordan-Hölder composition length at most
2
, and

(10)

\text{for all submodules }X\text{ of }M\colon\quad\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,M)\leq\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,N).

Then
M\cong N
, or
M
has Jordan-Hölder composition length
2
, and
denoting by
S,T
the composition factors of
M
(
S\cong T
is allowed), we have

N\cong S\oplus T
.

Proof.

By Schur’s Lemma we have that for a finite dimensional simple
R
-module
X
and
for a finite dimensional
R
-module
V
,
\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,V)
equals
the multiplicity of
X
as a direct summand in the socle of
V
.
Hence applying (
10
) for the simple submodules
X
of
M
,
we get that
N
contains a submodule isomorphic to
\mathrm{Soc}(M)
.
In particular, if
M=\mathrm{Soc}(M)
is semisimple, then
\mathrm{Soc}(N)

contains a direct summand isomorphic to
M
, implying in turn
by
\dim_{K}M=\dim_{K}N
that
M\cong N
.
From now on we assume that
M
is not semisimple,
and
M\ncong N
.

Suppose that
M
has Jordan-Hölder composition length
2
.
Then
\mathrm{Soc}(M)\cong S
and

M/\mathrm{Soc}(M)\cong T
are simple
R
-modules.
As we explained above,
N
has a submodule isomorphic to
S
.
As
\dim_{K}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,M)>0
, there exists a non-zero
