1. Introduction
What is JUSTIFI?
JUSTIFI is a free, open-source software tool that helps industrial end users justify energy efficiency
projects by showing their full business value. It does this by identifying and quantifying Multiple
Benefits of Energy Efficiency Projects and linking them to Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) and Key
Performance Metrics (KPMs)—the metrics that facilities already track to measure success.

The result is a more complete picture of project value: capturing safety improvements, quality gains,
reduced downtime, increased throughput, and other operational and financial benefits.

Who Should Use This Tool?
JUSTIFI is designed for:
 • Energy assessors (e.g., IAC/ITAC students, consultants)
 • Industrial energy managers
 • Plant engineers and operators
 • Facility managers and supervisors
 • EH&S and finance staff
 • Anyone trying to justify a good project!
No prior experience with operational benefits is required—JUSTIFI walks you through each step.

How does JUSTIFI Help?
Energy projects are often undervalued because they’re evaluated solely on energy savings and simple
payback. JUSTIFI expands this narrow view by highlighting and quantifying additional business
impacts, leading to:
 • Reduced payback periods
 • Stronger financial arguments
 • Increased internal support for project implementation

This results in clearer, more compelling justification for energy investments.

3. Understanding Key Concepts
What Are Multiple Benefits?
Multiple Benefits are the additional positive outcomes of energy improvements that extend beyond
energy savings – usually relating to operations, but often higher strategic goals as well – such as better
customer or supplier relations, expanded sales lines, and environmental impacts. They are also called
operational, non-energy, auxiliary, ancillary, or co- benefits. These Benefits are often more valuable than
the energy savings themselves. Examples:

 Fewer defects Less scrap or waste Improved safety

 Faster cycle time Lower maintenance Better product
 costs quality

What’s the difference between KPIs and KPMs?
Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) Key Performance Metrics (KPMs)
KPIs represent broad business goals such as: KPMs are the measurable aspects beneath
 • Safety each KPI. Examples:
 • Quality • Injury rate
 • Throughput • Defect percentage
 • Operational Costs • Units per hour
 • Material Utilization • Cost per unit

They reflect what matters to leadership. JUSTIFI uses KPMs to convert benefits into
 quantifiable impacts.

Energy Efficiency Measures (EEMs)
EEMs are the actual improvement opportunities identified during an assessment (e.g., insulation, steam
trap repair, compressed air optimization). While JUSTIFI does not help quantify these, MEASUR, one of
DOE’s software platforms, can. For more information on MEASUR, visit: industrialresources.ornl.gov.

Where do Multiple Benefits Fit into the Energy Assessment Process?
Ideally, the search for operational improvements begins as you begin planning your energy assessment.
At this point, you begin speaking with the individuals at a facility to learn more about business
objectives, processes, and potential for improvement. As potential opportunities are developed and
outlined, conversations about additional impacts can be started, and notes entered into JUSTIFI. After
the project is better understood, then the operational improvements can be finalized in JUSTIFI and
included in any report outs.

5. Phase 1 — Pre-Visit Setup
Step 1: Creating a Company Using the Setup Wizard
Start by clicking either Setup Wizard on the home screen, or you can click + Add Company if you’re in
the “My Portfolio” view. Fill out the details of your company but note that “Company Name” is the only
required data.

Step 2: Add Stakeholders
Identifying stakeholders will help you recognize who
to reach out to for data, and who will care about the
results. Note that “First and Last Name” are the only
required fields. There is no limit to the number of
stakeholders that you can enter. Make sure to enter
the people who:
 • Could provide data or other insights
 • Operate equipment
 • Care about project outcomes
These stakeholders will connect to KPIs, KPMs,
systems, and EEMs that you identify.

Step 3: Create a Facility
You can continue and add a facility in the Wizard
setup, or you can add from the Company Dashboard
by selecting + Add Facility.
 • “Facility Name” is required
 • Adding utility information is recommended
 • Add optional notes to support later
 conversations

 14

Facility Questions are optional but can help guide conversations
that are needed to identify Benefits.

These are not interview questions — this is a place to put notes
from conversations where these topics were addressed.
Facility questions are high level questions to understand how the
plant looks at operations, approaches energy-efficiency projects,
and the potential for acquiring KPM data.

 Step 4: Select KPIs and KPMs
 JUSTIFI provides a short list of Common KPIs and
 associated KPMs to help get started. In this
 section, you can:
 • Select common KPIs
 • Choose which pre-defined KPMs are
 relevant to the facility
 • Add custom KPIs and KPMs
 • Enter data related to the KPM
 baseline values and costs
 • Connect KPMs to stakeholders
 • Review existing associated impacts Add custom KPIs that
 matter to your facility!

 While this section is presented very early, it is
 expected that it will need to be revisited as more
 conversations are had with site personnel and
 more data is gathered.

 Step 5: Set Baselines & Financial Definitions
 You will also use this screen to set baseline and
 financial data however you wish to track this
 information. This data can:

 • Set how expected improvements impact

 • Establish how you will measure the
 baseline (cost per unit or direct financial
 impact)

6. Phase 2 — Conducting an Assessment
Step 1: Data Collection Using the Setup Wizard
This feature walks you through entering data gathered
during and after an assessment. You can use this to enter:
 • High-level energy assessment details (energy use,
 energy savings, cost savings, implementation costs)
 • Energy efficiency opportunities (EEMs)
 • Impacts of Multiple Benefits on KPMs

Like all portions of JUSTIFI, you can always revisit the form
and enter in additional details later. Additionally, you can
import energy details from MEASUR via the template.

Step 2: Assessment Details Page
In the Details tab, you can review previously entered details
about a system assessment.
 • Add total implementation cost and savings details
 for all opportunities consolidated together.
 • Note that if opportunities overlap with each other,
 individual savings cannot be estimated and “as
 single project” should be selected.
 • You can also connect these assessments to
 stakeholders, equipment, and end uses.

Step 3: Adding EEMs
In the EEMs tab, you can add EEMs for any
projects that may have additional benefits. You
can either fill this in manually or import this
from MEASUR.
 • Select the utility that is impacted
 • Add savings and costs if you can
 estimate this for individual projects
 • Add notes to help with remembering
 details
 • Connect to equipment/end uses

 19

Step 4: Adding Multiple Benefits
In the Benefits tab, you can add Benefits to
your system assessment. You can either
search the built-in Multiple Benefits Database
or create your own Benefits. Make sure to add
data for each Benefit:
 • Assign KPIs/KPMs
 • Connect to an EEM and stakeholders
 • Enter impact magnitude
 • Enter financial impact if applicable;
 this can be using a different
 calculation method than the baseline.
 • Additional financial benefits, not
 connected to a KPM can also be
 added.

Step 5: Adding Incentives
In the same tab, you can add information about incentives, including rebates, grants, and tax incentives.
These contribute to cost reduction.

TIPS: Using the Multiple Benefits Database
JUSTIFI has a searchable database of common Benefits which you can use here, and this is also
viewable from the home screen. You can search by Keywords and also filter by specific KPIs and KPMs.
Note: KPIs and KPMs that are being tracked will be indicated by *.

Step 4: Assessment Reports
The Assessment Reports includes detailed figures and tables that illustrate the financial and KPM

to include in your report.

Appendix B: Glossary
Energy Efficiency Measure (EEMs) - The specific utility related projects with associated cost savings
that are being evaluated.

Industrial Assessment Center (IAC)/ Industrial Technology & Assessment Center (ITAC) -
An IAC/ITAC is a university-based program that provides free energy efficiency, waste reduction, and
productivity assessments to small and medium-sized manufacturers (SMMs), training engineering
students to help businesses cut costs and become more competitive. These teams identify savings in
areas like motors, lighting, steam, and processes, offering recommendations with cost/payback
estimates to improve energy use and output.

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) - A general measure used by an organization to track progress
toward goals.

Key Performance Metrics (KPMs) - The specific data points used to evaluate the KPIs.

Multiple Benefits - Also referred to as Co-benefits, Non-Energy Benefits, Auxiliary or Ancillary Benefits;
The additional operational and strategic benefits gained from completing an improvement targeting
another important business metric. JUSTIFI often refers to these as a “Benefit”.
