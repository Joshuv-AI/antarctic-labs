Distributions of Iwasawa λ \lambda -invariants of \symbf ​ Z p \symbf{Z}_{p} -towers over supersingular isogeny graphs
Taiga Adachi, Kosuke Mizuno, Ryosuke Murooka, Sohei Tateno

Abstract
A graph-theoretic analogue of Iwasawa theory, initiated by Gonet and Vallières, has attracted considerable interest in the study of Iwasawa invariants. On the other hand, for a pair of prime numbers ( r , ℓ ) (r,\ell) , one obtains a graph, called the supersingular ℓ \ell -isogeny graph (SIG), whose adjacency matrix has eigenvalues given by the ℓ \ell -th Fourier coefficients of the weight 2 Eisenstein series and newforms of level r r . In this paper, we fix prime numbers r r and p p , and let ℓ \ell vary over infinitely many primes. We then investigate the distribution of the Iwasawa λ \lambda -invariants of the constant \symbf ​ Z p \symbf{Z}_{p} -towers over the SIGs, thereby revealing connections among graph theory, Iwasawa theory, elliptic curves, and the Galois representations attached to newforms. At the end of this paper, we propose a conjecture concerning the Galois orbits of newforms.

whose zero element is
0_{E}
and an embedding into the projective plane
\symbf{P}^{2}
as a cubic curve.
Each elliptic curve has a
j
-invariant
, which is a
\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}
-valued invariant.
Since
r\geqq 5
, taking
j
-invariants gives a one-to-one correspondence between isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}
and the elements of
\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}
, so
\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}
can be seen as a moduli space of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves.

(2)

A
supersingular
elliptic curve
E
is an elliptic curve whose group of
r
-torsion points
E\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big(}\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big)}[r]
is trivial.
A supersingular curve
E
can be defined over
\symbf{F}_{r^{2}}
, that is,
E
is the base extension of a curve in
\symbf{P}^{2}
over
\symbf{F}_{r^{2}}
and
0_{E}
is an
\symbf{F}_{r^{2}}
-rational point, in other words, we can choose a defining polynomial of
E
whose coefficients are in
\symbf{F}_{r^{2}}
and the coordinates of
0_{E}
are in
\symbf{F}_{r^{2}}
.
The
j
-invariant of a supersingular elliptic curve is called the
supersingular
j
-invariant
.

(3)

An
isogeny

g
is a non-constant morphism
g\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
between elliptic curves which sends
0_{E_{1}}\mapsto 0_{E_{2}}
.
As isogenies are non-constant morphisms between complete curves, they are surjective and finite, and induce group homomorphisms
E_{1}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big(}\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big)}\to E_{2}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big(}\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big)}
.
For a positive number
n
, an
n
-isogeny
is an isogeny
g
whose kernel is of order
n
, or equivalently, it is a morphism of degree
n
.
Every
n
-isogeny
g\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
has a unique
n
-isogeny
\hat{g}\colon E_{2}\to E_{1}
, called the
dual isogeny
of
g
, such that the composite morphism
\hat{g}\circ g\colon E_{1}\to E_{1}
is the endomorphism
{{}}{[n]}
multiplying points by
n
.
It is important that for every isogeny
g
, the double dual of
g
is itself, that is,
(\hat{g})\,\hat{}\,=g
holds.

According to
[
30
, §III.4]
, for a prime number
\ell\neq r
, a (separable)
\ell
-isogeny
E_{1}\to E_{2}
is determined by a non-trivial subgroup of
E_{1}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big(}\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big)}[\ell]
up to automorphisms of
E_{2}
, and conversely, every non-trivial subgroup
\Phi
of
\ell
-torsion points induces a
\ell
-isogeny from
E_{1}
to a
quotient elliptic curve

E_{1}/\Phi
.
If
E_{1}
is supersingular, and separably isogenous to
E_{2}
, then
E_{2}
is also supersingular.
We can confirm this fact group theoretically as follows.

Proof..

If
E_{2}
had a non-zero
r
-torsion point, then
E_{1}
has a point
P
with
P\not\in\ker g
and
r\cdot P\in\ker g
for some
\ell
-isogeny
g\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
.
Since
\ker g
is of order
\ell
,
r\ell\cdot P=0_{E_{1}}
, and hence
\ell\cdot P=0_{E_{1}}
since
E_{1}
is supersingular.
However, as
r
and
\ell
are distinct primes, we can find integers
a
,
b
such that
P=(ar+b\ell)P\in\ker g
, which is a contradiction.
∎

3
Supersingular isogeny graphs

In this section, we describe some basic facts about supersingular isogeny graphs.
Goren and Love
[
11
]
is a useful reference for supersingular isogeny graphs and several related topics.

Fix a complete set
\{E_{0},E_{1},\dotsc,E_{h}\}
of representatives of the isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curves over
\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}
.

Definitions 3.1
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number distinct from
r
.

(1)

The
supersingular
\ell
-isogeny graph
\SI(r,\ell)
over
\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}
is the directed graph whose set of vertices is given by

\symbf{V}(\SI(r,\ell))\coloneq\big\{E_{0},E_{1},\dotsc,E_{h}\big\},

(3.1)

and for two vertices
E,E^{\prime}
, the set of directed edges from
E
to
E^{\prime}
is given by

\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))_{E,E^{\prime}}\coloneq\big\{\,\text{$\ell$-isogenies $E\to E^{\prime}$ up to automorphisms of $E^{\prime}$}\,\big\}.

(3.2)

For simplicity, we abbreviate supersingular
\ell
-isogeny graph to
\ell
-SIG
.

(2)

The
double supersingular
\ell
-isogeny graph
(abbreviated to
\ell
-DSIG
)
X^{(r,\ell)}
is the graph constructed as follows.
Its set of vertices
\symbf{V}(X^{(r,\ell)})\coloneq\symbf{V}(\SI(r,\ell))
.
Its set of edges is given, as a set, by the disjoint union
\symbf{E}(X^{(r,\ell)})\coloneq\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))\coprod\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))
.
We embed
\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))
into
\symbf{E}(X^{(r,\ell)})
as the first summand, and give edge directions in the same way as
\SI(r,\ell)
.
Then we consider the edges in the second summand as the corresponding inverse edges.
In this meaning, we can write the second summand as
\wideoverbar{\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))}
.
For an isogeny
g
and its dual isogeny
\hat{g}
, one should not confuse
(\hat{g})\,\bar{}
with
g
.

We summarize the basic properties of SIGs.

Number of vertices

From
[
30
, V.4.1c]
, the number of vertices
h+1
is nearly equal to
\lfloor r/12\rfloor
, where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
is the floor function.
In particular,
h+1=\lfloor r/12\rfloor
if
r\equiv 1\pmod{12}
.

Outdegree

The
outdegree at a vertex
E\in\symbf{V}(\SI(r,\ell))
is the number of edges of
\SI(r,\ell)
whose origin is
E
.
As stated in preliminaries, we know that the number of edges starting from
E
is the number of non-trivial subgroups of
\ell
-torsion points.
By
[
30
, Corollary 6.4]
,
E\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big(}\wideoverbar{\symbf{F}}_{r}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big)}[\ell]\cong(\symbf{Z}/\ell\symbf{Z})\times(\symbf{Z}/\ell\symbf{Z})
, and hence the outdegree of every vertex of
\SI(r,\ell)
is
\ell+1
.
In other words,
D(\SI(r,\ell))=(\ell+1)I_{h+1}
where
I_{h+1}
denotes the identity matrix of size
h+1
.

Connectedness

Since
\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))_{E,E^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset
implies
\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))_{E^{\prime}\!,E}\neq\emptyset
, if there exists a path from
E
to
E^{\prime\prime}
, then there exists a path from
E^{\prime\prime}
to
E
.
This property allows us to consider the decomposition of
\SI(r,\ell)
into its connected components.
It is non-trivial but known that
\SI(r,\ell)
is connected, that is,
\SI(r,\ell)
has exactly one connected component, and so is
X^{(r,\ell)}
.
We will see the connectedness in corollary
3.6
below using the theory of modular forms.

Symmetry

Since
(\hat{g})\,\hat{}\,=g
for every isogeny
g
, we have a bijective map

(\cdot)\,\hat{}\,\colon\{\,\text{$\ell$-isogenies from $E$ to $E^{\prime}$}\,\}\to\{\,\text{$\ell$-isogenies from $E^{\prime}$ to $E$}\,\}.

(3.3)

The set of edges from
E
to
E^{\prime}
is the set of equivalence classes of
\ell
-isogenies from
E
to
E^{\prime}
up to automorphisms of
E^{\prime}
.
The table in
[
30
, III.10.1]
shows that the group of automorphisms of an elliptic curve
E
is
\{[1],[-1]\}
unless its
j
-invariant is 0 or 1728.
Moreover, if
r\equiv 1\pmod{12}
, 0 and 1728 are not supersingular
j
-invariants, see
[
30
, V.4.4 and V.4.5]
for this fact.
Hence, if
r\equiv 1\pmod{12}
, the above bijective map (
3.3
) induces a bijective map
(\cdot)\,\hat{}\,\colon\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))_{E,E^{\prime}}\to\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))_{E^{\prime}\!,E}
, which leads us to the following important property in our results.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\wideoverbar{\SI}(r,\ell)
be the directed graph defined by reversing the origin and terminus of each edge of
\SI(r,\ell)
.
Then, if
r\equiv 1\pmod{12}
, one has the following.

(1)

The adjacency matrix
A(\SI(r,\ell))
is symmetric,

(2)

A\raisebox{0.5382pt}{\big(}\wideoverbar{\SI}(r,\ell)\raisebox{0.5382pt}{\big)}=A(\SI(r,\ell))
,

(3)

A(X^{(r,\ell)})=2A(\SI(r,\ell))
.

Proof..

For (1), by the above bijective map
(\cdot)\,\hat{}\,\colon\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))_{E,E^{\prime}}\to\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))_{E^{\prime}\!,E}
implies that

A(\SI(r,\ell))_{E,E^{\prime}}=|\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))_{E,E^{\prime}}|=|\symbf{E}(\SI(r,\ell))_{E^{\prime}\!,E}|=A(\SI(r,\ell))_{E^{\prime}\!,E}

for each
E
,
E^{\prime}
.
This means that
A(\SI(r,\ell))
is a symmetric matrix.
Then (2) follows from the equality
A(\wideoverbar{\SI}(r,\ell))=A(\SI(r,\ell))^{\symsf{T}}
, and (3) follows from the equality
A(X^{(r,\ell)})=A(\SI(r,\ell))+A(\wideoverbar{\SI}(r,\ell))
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

be a positive integer and

a finite directed graph such that

(a)

the outdegree of each vertex is

, and

(b)

if there is a path from
u
to
v
, then there is a path from
v
to
u
.

Then,
k
is an eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix
A(X)
, and the dimension of the eigenspace of real eigenvectors of

is equal to the number of the connected components of
X
.

Proof..

By condition (a), the vector
\symbf{1}
whose entries are all 1 is a

A(X)
.
Let
X_{1},\dotsc,X_{m}
be the connected components of
X
.
Since
A(X)_{u,v}\neq 0
implies
A(X)_{v,u}\neq 0
, reordering vertices of
X
, we can write

as the block diagonal matrix

A(X)=\begin{pmatrix}A(X_{1})&&\text{\Large$O$}\\
&\ddots&\\
\text{\Large$O$}&&A(X_{m})\end{pmatrix}.

So we have to show that, for each
i
, the eigenspace of real eigenvectors of eigenvalue

is of dimension 1, or equivalently, the eigenspace is
\symbf{R}\cdot\symbf{1}
.
Hence we may assume that
X
is a finite connected directed graph satisfying both (a) and (b), and we will show that
\big\{\,\symbf{x}\in\symbf{R}^{\symbf{V}(X)}:A(X)\symbf{x}=k\cdot\symbf{x}\,\big\}=\symbf{R}\cdot\symbf{1}
.
Take a vector
\symbf{x}\in\symbf{R}^{\symbf{V}(X)}
with
A(X)\symbf{x}=k\cdot\symbf{x}
.
Choose a vertex

such that
\symbf{x}_{w}\geqq\symbf{x}_{v}
for all

.
Then, for each positive integer

\sum_{v\in\symbf{V}(X)}A(X)^{n}_{w,v}\symbf{x}_{v}=k^{n}\symbf{x}_{w}
.
Moreover, since

, we have
\sum_{v\in\symbf{V}(X)}A(X)^{n}_{w,v}=k^{n}
.
Therefore, we obtain an equality

\sum_{v\in\symbf{V}(X)}A(X)^{n}_{w,v}(\symbf{x}_{w}-\symbf{x}_{v})=0

(3.12)

for each positive integer

Note that

is the number of paths of length

Now, by connectedness of

Thus, from (

for each

Indeed, for compatibility of terminus maps, we can establish

-isogeny and

.
The remaining conditions for being a morphism of graphs are straightforward.
∎

Proposition A.4

(cf.

20

)
.

\gamma\colon\symbf{E}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big(}Y^{(r,\ell)}_{n_{0}}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big)}\to\symbf{Z}_{p}

and multiplicative inverse on
\symbf{E}\big(\wideoverbar{\SI}(r,\ell,e_{p^{n_{0}}})\big)

-adic logarithm.

-copies of

, where

(2)

For each non-negative integer

, the isomorphism in Proposition

induces an isomorphism

Proof..

(1)
We will deal with the derived graph
X^{(r,\ell)}(\beta_{n})
.
Since
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p^{n_{0}}}
, two vertices
(E,a),(E^{\prime},a^{\prime})
with
a\neq a^{\prime}
are never connected by directed edges by definition of derived graph.
For each

, the full subgraph of
Y^{(r,\ell)}_{n_{0}}
consisting of the vertices with
a

X^{(r,\ell)}
.

(2)
First we make an identification

(\symbf{Z}/p^{n_{0}+n}\symbf{Z})^{\times}\cong(\symbf{Z}_{p}/p^{n_{0}+n}\symbf{Z}_{p})^{\times}\cong\symbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}/(1+p^{n_{0}+n}\symbf{Z}_{p}).

(1+p^{n_{0}}\symbf{Z}_{p})/(1+p^{n_{0}+n}\symbf{Z}_{p})

.
Then, the cover
Y^{(r,\ell)}_{n_{0}+n}/Y^{(r,\ell)}_{n_{0}}
is given by the voltage assignment
\delta_{n}\colon\symbf{E}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big(}Y^{(r,\ell)}_{n_{0}}\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\big)}\to(1+p^{n_{0}}\symbf{Z}_{p})/(1+p^{n_{0}+n}\symbf{Z}_{p})

for every edge

; see Lemma

below for the general statement.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{n_{0}}}\log_{p}\colon

\displaystyle 1+p^{n_{0}}\symbf{Z}_{p}\xrightarrow{\log_{p}}p^{n_{0}}\symbf{Z}_{p}\xrightarrow{p^{-n_{0}}\cdot(\cdot)}\symbf{Z}_{p},

(A.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{n_{0}}}\log_{p}\colon

(A.3)

\frac{1}{p^{n_{0}}}\log_{p}\colon(1+p^{n_{0}}\symbf{Z}_{p})/(1+p^{n_{0}+n}\symbf{Z}_{p})\similarrightarrow\symbf{Z}_{p}/p^{n}\symbf{Z}_{p},

Lemma A.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
X
a finite graph, and
\alpha\colon\symbf{E}(X)\to G
a voltage assignment.
We do not impose connectedness on derived graph
X(\alpha)
.
For an arbitrary subgroup
H\subset G
, consider the action of
H
on
X(\alpha)
by left multiplication on
G
.
Let
Z
be the quotient
H\backslash X(\alpha)
by the action
H\curvearrowright X(\alpha)
.
Fix a complete set
\{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}\}
of representatives of cosets
H\backslash G
.
Define the voltage assignment
\beta\colon\symbf{E}(Z)\to H
by
(e,Hg_{i})\mapsto g_{i}\alpha(e)g_{j}^{-1}
where
j
is the unique number such that
g_{i}\alpha(e)\in Hg_{j}
.
Then the cover
X(\alpha)/Z
is isomorphic to the cover
Z(\beta)/Z
.

Proof..

Recall that the graph
Z
is defined as follows.
The sets of vertices and edges are given by
\symbf{V}(Z)\coloneq\symbf{V}(X)\times(H\backslash G)
,
\symbf{E}(Z)\coloneq\symbf{E}(X)\times(H\backslash G)
respectively.
For an edge
(e,Hg)\in\symbf{E}(Z)
, its origin, terminus, and inverse edge are given by
o(e,Hg)\coloneq(o(e),Hg)
,
t(e,Hg)\coloneq\big(t(e),Hg\alpha(e)\big)
,
(e,Hg)\,\bar{}\,\coloneq\big(\bar{e},Hg\alpha(e)\big)
respectively.
Note that using fixed representatives
g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m}
of
H\backslash G
, we have
(e,Hg_{i})\,\bar{}\,=(\bar{e},Hg_{j})
if
g_{i}\alpha(e)\in Hg_{j}
.
This allows us to calculate
\beta(\bar{e},Hg_{j})=g_{j}\alpha(\bar{e})g_{i}^{-1}=\beta(e,Hg_{i})^{-1}
, which means that
\beta
is indeed a voltage assignment.
The derived graph
Z(\beta)
can be described as

\displaystyle\symbf{V}(Z(\beta))

\displaystyle=\{\,(v,Hg_{i},h):v\in\symbf{V}(X),\ i\in\{1,\dotsc,m\},\ h\in H\,\},

(A.4)

\displaystyle\symbf{E}(Z(\beta))

\displaystyle=\{\,(e,Hg_{i},h):e\in\symbf{E}(X),\ i\in\{1,\dotsc,m\},\ h\in H\,\},

(A.5)

as the sets of vertices and edges, and for each edge
(e,Hg_{i},h)
, if
g_{i}\alpha(e)\in Hg_{j}
, its origin, terminus, and inverse edge are

\displaystyle o(e,Hg_{i},h)

\displaystyle=(o(e),Hg_{i},h),

(A.6)

\displaystyle t(e,Hg_{i},h)

\displaystyle=\big(t(e),Hg_{j},hg_{i}\alpha(e)g_{j}^{-1}\big),

(A.7)

\displaystyle(e,Hg_{i},h)\,\bar{}\,

\displaystyle=\big(\bar{e},Hg_{j},hg_{i}\alpha(e)g_{j}^{-1}\big)

(A.8)

respectively.
By this description, we can verify that the bijective map

H\backslash G\times H\to G\,;\quad(Hg_{i},h)\mapsto hg_{i}

induces an isomorphism
Z(\beta)\similarrightarrow X(\alpha)
.
∎
