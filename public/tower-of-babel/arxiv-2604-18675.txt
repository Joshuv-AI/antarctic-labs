All-order fluctuating hydrodynamics of the SYK lattice
Marta Bucca, Akash Jain, Márk Mezei, Alexey Milekhin

Abstract
The SYK model has played an important role in recent developments in many-body quantum chaos. We study a spatially local generalisation of it: the SYK lattice. Starting from the nonlinear action of pseudo-Goldstone bosons that dominate its dynamics at low temperatures, in the long wavelength limit we reorganise this action as the effective field theory for fluctuating hydrodynamics, thereby showing how the hydrodynamic degrees of freedom embed into the microscopic description of the model. We compute the hydrodynamic effective action to high orders in the derivative expansion and determine all the corresponding transport coefficients. Hence this work derives hydrodynamics from the microscopic description of a strongly coupled quantum many-body system.

Long wavelength expansion

In this section, we extend the non-local Euclidean effective action (

3.2
Energy diffusion

Every term in the effective action (

\begin{split}\partial_{t}T=D\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial_{x}^{2}T+{(\partial_{x}T)^{2}\over T}}}\right)+O(\lambda^{2})\,.\end{split}

(24)

From the thermodynamics of the Schwarzian, we know that the energy density

at leading order in

(25)

We will discuss this is detail in Section

Fluctuations around the classical hydrodynamic regime are small in our theory, as they are suppressed by

4.5
Local action with background fields

In the discussion above, we coupled the non-local effective action of the SYK chain to background fields. We varied it with respect to the background fields to obtain the energy currents on the individual Lorentzian folds and afterwards implemented the derivative expansion on them.

5
Continuous symmetries and Noether currents

In this section we discuss various continuous symmetries of our model and the ensuing Noether currents. We will see that the energy currents in Section
4
are Noether currents corresponding to average and difference time translations on the Schwinger–Keldysh contour. A curiosity is that one of them is realised as an internal symmetry, while the other as a spacetime symmetry. Hence the average energy current will arise as a Noether current for a global shift symmetry, while the difference energy current as the time component of the energy-momentum tensor.

The space component of the energy-momentum tensor is also conserved, but vanishes in the classical limit
Q=0
, similarly to the difference energy current; we do not know what role it plays in the dynamics of the theory. Finally, the variables that we are working with have an
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})^{2M}
gauge redundancy, which is a property of the microscopic Schwarzian chain action (
5
) we started from. This gauge redundancy is different than the gauge symmetries of gauge theories in that it is global in time but local in space, hence the Noether method produces a nonzero gauge current for it. Another important finding will be that only one generator of the
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
is compatible with the derivative expansion.

5.1
Noether currents for higher derivative theories

The effective theory contains higher derivatives, hence we need a higher derivative Noether formula to compute the conserved currents. Consider a field theory with dynamical fields
\phi^{A}
. For an infinitesimal symmetry transformation,
\phi^{A}\to\phi^{A}+\delta\phi^{A}
, the Lagrangian may change by a total derivative

\begin{split}\delta\mathcal{L}=\partial_{\mu}K^{\mu}\,.\end{split}

(71)

Then the associated Noether current is

\begin{split}j^{\mu}=-K^{\mu}+\sum_{A}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\,\partial_{a_{1}\dots a_{k}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\partial\mathcal{L}\over\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial_{\mu a_{1}\dots a_{n}}\phi^{A}}}\right)}}}\right)\partial_{a_{k+1}\dots a_{n}}\delta\phi^{A}\,.\end{split}

(72)

The energy-momentum tensor of a manifestly space and time translation invariant Lagrangian is accordingly given as

\begin{split}\tau^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\nu}=\sum_{A}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\,\partial_{a_{1}\dots a_{k}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\partial\mathcal{L}\over\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial_{\mu a_{1}\dots a_{n}}\phi^{A}}}\right)}}}\right)\partial_{a_{k+1}\dots a_{n}\nu}\phi^{A}-\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}\,.\end{split}

(73)

There is one subtlety in applying these formulas: they treat the derivatives
\partial_{\mu\nu\dots}
and
\partial_{\nu\mu\dots}
as distinct objects, and we have to compensate this by combinatorial factors.
21
21

21

This situation may be familiar from general relativity, where we have to take variational derivatives with respect to a symmetric tensor field.
For the special case of
1+1
dimensions, it is also useful to rewrite

\begin{split}j^{\mu}&=-K^{\mu}+\sum_{A}\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}w_{\mu,k,\ell,m,n}\,(-1)^{k+\ell}\,\partial_{t}^{k}\partial_{x}^{\ell}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\partial\mathcal{L}\over\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}\phi^{A}}}\right)}}}\right)\partial_{t}^{m-k}\partial_{x}^{n-\ell}\delta\phi^{A}\,,\\
w_{\mu,k,\ell,m,n}&\equiv{{k+\ell\choose k}\,{m+n-k-\ell\choose m-k}\over{m+n+1\choose m+\delta_{\mu,t}}}\,,\end{split}

(74)

where the numerator of the weight
w_{\mu,k,\ell,m,n}
counts the number of
\partial_{a_{1}\dots a_{k+\ell}}
and
\partial_{a_{k+\ell+1}\dots a_{m+n}}
combinations that are identical, while the denominator counts the identical
\partial_{\mu a_{1}\dots a_{m+n}}
combinations.
22
22

22

The weights can be fractional and are symmetric under the exchange of
t,x
despite appearances.
These formulas are implemented in the accompanying Mathematica file for the symmetries discussed below.

5.2
Average and difference time translations

Let us begin with the time translation symmetry of our theory. The original time translation symmetry of the SYK chain gets doubled on the SK closed-time contour, acting independently on the forward and backward Lorentzian folds. Correspondingly, the SK EFT features two independently conserved energy currents as discussed in Section
4
. As discussed there, the average of these currents is interpreted as the classical energy current, while the difference as its stochastic/quantum noise partner.

Our microscopic SYK chain model (
2
) is invariant under time translations. When the theory is put on the SK closed-time contour, this results in independent time translation symmetries on the forward and backward folds respectively, i.e.

\displaystyle t_{\pm}\to t_{\pm}-\xi_{\pm},

(75)

where
\xi_{\pm}
are constant parameters and
t_{\pm}\to t_{\pm}/\lambda^{2}
are understood as rescaled time coordinates. The symmetries act on the reparametrisation fields as
F^{\pm}(t,x)\to F^{\pm}(t+\xi_{\pm},x)
. It is useful to decompose the two symmetries into average and difference combinations
\xi_{\pm}=\xi\pm\lambda^{2}\xi_{q}/2
. In terms of the average and difference fields
F(t,x)
and
Q(t,x)
, the average time translations simply act as overall time translations

\displaystyle F(t,x)\to F(t+\xi,x)\,,\qquad Q(t,x)\to Q(t+\xi,x)\,.

(76)

The action of the difference time translations is quite non-trivial in this basis, but simplifies in the infinitesimal limit as

\begin{split}F(t,x)&\to F(t,x)+\frac{\lambda^{4}\xi_{q}}{4}\partial_{t}Q(t,x)+O(\xi_{q}^{2})\,,\\
Q(t,x)&\to Q(t,x)+\xi_{q}\partial_{t}F(t,x)+O(\xi_{q}^{2})\,.\end{split}

(77)

Note that these infinitesimal transformations rules are valid non-perturbatively in
\lambda
. One may explicitly check that the symmetries (
76
) and (
77
) are respected by our effective action (
21
) at leading order in
\lambda
, while the checks at subleading orders in
\lambda
have been performed in the supplementary Mathematica notebook.

As it turns out, the Noether current associated with difference time translations is the average energy current across the two Lorentzian folds. We have verified this by plugging in the transformation rule (
77
) into (
74
) and found that the corresponding Noether current
j^{\mu}
is equal to
{\cal E}^{\mu}
from (
60
).
In the classical limit, i.e. when the noise field
Q
is set to zero, this reproduces the standard energy conservation equation.

We observe that at leading order in
\lambda
, the combination
Q/\partial_{t}F
transforms as a Goldstone field, i.e. with a simple shift, under difference time translations:

\displaystyle\frac{Q}{\partial_{t}F}

\displaystyle\to\frac{Q}{\partial_{t}F}+\xi_{q}-\frac{\lambda^{4}\xi_{q}}{4}\frac{Q\partial_{t}^{2}Q}{(\partial_{t}F)^{2}}+O(\xi_{q}^{2})\,.

(78)

We can infer just from this that the difference time translations are spontaneously broken in our theory. This is a general feature of SK EFT for hydrodynamics, known as strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking
21
;
93
;
2
. Herein, the difference combination of a global symmetry of the theory on the closed-time contour is spontaneously broken while the average one remains intact.

A more intuitive way to demonstrate this symmetry breaking in our theory is to consider the thermal saddle point configuration
F^{\pm}={t\over\beta}
, which under an average time translation acquires a shift
F^{\pm}\to{t\over\beta}-{\xi\over\beta}
. As discussed below in Section
5.4
, space-dependent but time-independent shifts are gauged in our theory, hence a compensating gauge transformation can cancel this shift and show that the thermal state is invariant under average time translations. This is not true for the difference time translation, which is broken by the thermal state.

In fact, we can correct the combination in (
78
) order-by-order in
\lambda
to arrive at the nonlinear Goldstone field that appears in the SK EFT framework:

\begin{split}\chi_{a}=-\frac{Q}{\partial_{t}F}-\frac{\lambda^{4}}{8}\frac{Q^{2}}{(\partial_{t}F)^{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\partial_{t}^{2}Q}{\partial_{t}F}-\frac{Q\partial_{t}^{3}F}{3(\partial_{t}F)^{2}}}}\right)+{O}(\lambda^{8})\,,\end{split}

(79)

such that

\displaystyle\chi_{a}\to\chi_{a}-\xi_{q}.

(80)

We will discuss the connection of our
F
and
Q
variables with those in the conventional SK EFT framework in detail in Section
7
.

5.3
Energy-momentum tensor

The Noether current associated with average time translations is the time component of the energy-momentum tensor
\tau^{\mu}_{\,\,\,t}
. We have verified by explicit computation in the derivative expansion that

\begin{split}\tau^{\mu}_{\,\,\,t}={\cal E}_{q}^{\mu}+\text{(improvement term)}\,,\end{split}

(81)

where we use the notation from (
60
) for the difference energy current between the two folds.

{\cal E}_{q}^{\mu}
identically vanishes in the classical limit, nevertheless it is very important in the computation of correlation functions, e.g. the retarded correlator of energy currents is given by
G^{R,\mu\nu}=i\langle{\cal E}^{\mu}{\cal E}_{q}^{\nu}\rangle
.

We have also computed
\tau^{\mu}_{\,\,\,x}
and verified that it is conserved
\partial_{\mu}\tau^{\mu}_{\,\,\,x}=0
; see the Mathematica file. It identically vanishes in the classical limit similarly to
{\cal E}^{\mu}_{q}
. This had to be the case, as classical diffusion does not have conserved momentum. We are not aware of any use of this conserved current in the computation of correlation functions. It would be interesting to understand how it constrains the dynamics of noise in the system.

Note that it is a peculiar feature of our formalism that the canonical energy-momentum tensor
\tau^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\nu}
does not capture the classical energy current
{\cal E}^{\mu}
of the system, instead it contains two quantum currents.

5.4
Reparametrisation and
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
symmetry

A single SYK dot possesses an emergent
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
symmetry at low energy
76
, which is to be treated as a gauge symmetry for the
f_{x}
fields, because it leaves the fermion two-point function invariant. This gauge redundancy is different than the gauge symmetries of familiar gauge theories in that it is global in time. In our case,
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
can act independently on each dot. However, it must act the same way on the forward and backward folds of the SK contour, because the non-local part of the action couples them. In the continuum limit, the infinitesimal action of
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
on the reparametrisation fields
F^{\pm}
read as

F^{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\pm}\rightarrow F^{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\pm}+\delta_{\boldsymbol{0}}(x)+\delta_{\boldsymbol{+}}(x)\,e^{2\pi F^{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\pm}/\lambda^{2}}+\delta_{\boldsymbol{-}}(x)\,e^{-2\pi F^{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\pm}/\lambda^{2}}\,,

(82)

where
(\delta_{\boldsymbol{0}},\delta_{\boldsymbol{+}},\delta_{\boldsymbol{-}})
are independent parameters of the transformation on each lattice site.
23
23

23

Note that these
{\boldsymbol{+}}/{\boldsymbol{-}}
(in bold) are
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
indices rather than SK contour indices. We again used the red font for the latter to emphasise that.

One of the transformations,
\delta_{\boldsymbol{0}}
, simply shifts the classical variable
F=(F^{+}+F^{-})/2
by a constant and leaves the quantum variable
Q=\frac{F^{+}-F^{-}}{\lambda^{2}}
invariant. This symmetry is obvious in the hydrodynamic expansion, since
F
only enters via
\partial_{t}F
.

However, the other two components of the
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
symmetry are not manifest in the hydrodynamic expansion.
For example,
\delta_{\boldsymbol{+}}
acts as

\begin{split}\delta_{\boldsymbol{+}}:\quad&F\rightarrow F+\delta_{\boldsymbol{+}}\,e^{2\pi F/\lambda^{2}}\cosh\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\pi\,Q}}\right)\,,\\
&Q\rightarrow Q+{2\delta_{\boldsymbol{+}}\over\lambda^{2}}\,e^{2\pi F/\lambda^{2}}\sinh\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\pi\,Q}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(83)

These expressions have non-uniform
\lambda
scaling and hence spoil the grading. In particular, the shifts are exponentially large in the
\lambda
expansion. Around a fixed time,
t=t_{0}
, we could make the shift of
F
to be
O(\lambda^{0})
by rescaling as
\delta_{\boldsymbol{+}}(x)=\widetilde{\delta}_{\boldsymbol{+}}(x)\,e^{-2\pi F(t_{0},x)/\lambda^{2}}
, however the shift of
Q
would still be
O(\lambda^{-2})
at this time. Also, taking time derivatives of both sides shows another violation of grading, as the
\partial^{n}_{t}F
shifts by
O(\lambda^{-2n})
from the term when all derivatives act on the exponential.
24
24

24

In terms of unscaled time, the
n
th time derivative should scale as
\lambda^{2n}
, but its shift would be
O(\lambda^{0})
.

The same problem occurs for the
\delta_{\boldsymbol{-}}
generator:

\begin{split}\delta_{\boldsymbol{-}}:\quad&F\rightarrow F+\delta_{\boldsymbol{-}}\,e^{-2\pi F/\lambda^{2}}\cosh\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\pi\,Q}}\right)\,,\\
&Q\rightarrow Q-{2\delta_{\boldsymbol{-}}\over\lambda^{2}}\,e^{-2\pi F/\lambda^{2}}\sinh\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\pi\,Q}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(84)

We conclude that the
\delta_{\boldsymbol{\pm}}
symmetries cannot be manifest in the derivative expansion. It would be interesting to investigate whether truncating the action to a finite order in the derivative expansion leads to any pathology because of the broken gauge symmetry. We also note that
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
transformations (on a fourfold contour) are important in describing scrambling
76
;
18
;
19
.

The
\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
action is spatially local, but global in time, hence there is a corresponding Noether current. Since it is only the
\delta_{\boldsymbol{0}}(x)
transformation that is consistent with the derivative expansion, we focus on the current associated to it. The current vanishes in the classical limit, for
Q=0
. We did not find its explicit formula enlightening even to

6
Dynamical KMS symmetry

In this section we discuss the discrete KMS (Kubo-Martin-Schwinger) symmetry of our SK EFT. This symmetry emerges as the macroscopic incarnation of the microscopic time reversal symmetry. We further use the KMS symmetry to derive the entropy current for our theory.

The original SYK chain model is time reversal symmetric on the microscopic level. However, the effective action expressed in terms of the reparametrisation field is clearly not time reversal invariant. The mechanism for this violation is the same as in the classic Caldeira-Leggett
65
model: in order to arrive at (
5
) on the SK contour, we effectively integrated out a continuum of states at arbitrary low energies, leading to dissipation. Mathematically it is reflected in
i\epsilon
making
\sinh^{2}
a non-even function and hence the action not being invariant under:

\Theta:F^{\pm}(t)\rightarrow-F^{\pm}(-t)\,.

(86)

The Schwarzian part of the action is still invariant under this transformation. However, for the inter-site piece, the time reversal transformation (
86
) gets updated to the dynamical KMS symmetry transformation of the low-energy effective action.

6.1
KMS symmetry of thermal correlation functions

KMS symmetry is an elementary, but very consequential property of thermal correlators. By inserting operators on the SK contour on Figure
3
, we get a correlator with time-ordered and anti-time-ordered insertions:

\begin{split}\tr\bigg(\rho_\text{th} \,
\bar{\cal T} \{A(t_1) \dots A(t_K) \}\,
{\cal T} \{B(t_{K+1}) \dots B(t_L) \}\bigg)\,,\end{split}

(87)

where
\rho_{\text{th}}={\rm e}^{-\beta H}/Z
denotes the thermal density matrix, while
{\cal T}
and
\overline{\cal T}
denote time-ordering and anti-time-ordering operators.
A
and
B
may represent any operators of interest in the theory; in particular, energy density or flux. We have suppressed the spatial dependence of the operators for simplicity. Noting that the thermal density matrix
\rho_{\text{th}}
implements a translation in imaginary time,
\rho_{\text{th}}A(t)=A(t+i\tau)\rho_{\text{th}}
, and using cyclicity of trace, the same correlator can also be written as

\begin{split}&\tr\bigg(\rho_\text{th} \,
\bar{\cal T} \{A(t_1) \dots A(t_K) \}\,
{\cal T} \{B(t_{K+1}) \dots B(t_L) \}\bigg)\\
&=\tr\bigg(\rho_\text{th} \,
{\cal T} \{B(t_{K+1}) \dots B(t_L) \}\,
\bar{\cal T} \{A(t_1+i\beta) \dots A(t_K+i\beta) \}
\bigg)\\
&=\tr\bigg(\rho_\text{th}\,
{\cal T} \{B(t_{K+1}-i\beta/2) \dots B(t_L-i\beta/2) \}\,
\bar{\cal T} \{A(t_1+i\beta/2) \dots A(t_K+i\beta/2) \}
\bigg)\,,\end{split}

(88)

where in the third line we performed an imaginary time translation of all operators by
i\beta/2
. To constitute a symmetry, we need to bring the correlator to the original form, where time-ordered operators are inserted to the right of anti-time-ordered operators, which is not the case in the last line above. To bring it to the desired form, we perform a time reversal transformation. Since time-reversal is an anti-unitary transformation, it reverses the ordering of operator insertions. To wit,

\begin{split}\eqref{KMSStatement_derivation}&=\eta_{A}^{K}\eta_{B}^{L-K}\tr\bigg(\rho_\text{th} \,
\bar{\cal T} \{A(-t_K - i\beta/2) \dots A(-t_1- i\beta/2) \} \\
&\hspace{10em}
{\cal T} \{B(-t_{L}+i\beta/2) \dots B(-t_{K+1}+i\beta/2) \}
\bigg)\,,\end{split}

(89)

Here
\eta_{A,B}
are the eigenvalues of
A,B
under time reversal, e.g.
\Theta A(t)=\eta_{A}A(-t)
.
25
25

25

Time reversal is an anti-unitary symmetry and acts on correlation functions for complex time arguments as
\tr(\rho_{\text{th}} A(z_1)B(z_2))=\tr(\rho_{\text{th}} A(-z^*_1)B(-z^*_2))^{*}=\tr(\rho_{\text{th}}B(-z_2)A(-z_1))
.
This is known as the KMS symmetry of thermal correlation functions.

6.2
Dynamical KMS symmetry

The KMS symmetry (
89

\rho_{\text{th}}=e^{-\beta H}/Z

However, in real life, things are rarely in exact thermal equilibrium and there generically exists a small temperature gradient. Therefore, it was conjectured in

46

44

KMS symmetry, which reproduces the KMS symmetry (

In Appendix

\begin{split}&F^{\pm}(t,x)\rightarrow-F^{\pm}(-t,x)\pm\frac{i\lambda^{2}}{2}\,,\end{split}

(90a)

or equivalently in average-difference basis

\begin{split}&F(t,x)\rightarrow-F(-t,x)\,,\\
&Q(t,x)\rightarrow-Q(-t,x)+i\,.\end{split}

(90b)

The transformations (

Since difference time translations act quite non-trivially on

(91b)

where the trigonometric functions of derivatives implement the shifts in (

Note that unlike some

Further comments are given on this issue in Appendix

It is an interesting open question to write down the most general KMS symmetric effective action. Our theory is special in that all terms in the Lagrangian are of the form

\begin{split}(Q-i/2)^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}\,\partial_{x}^{m}\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[Q^{n}\,F^{p}}}\right]\,,\qquad k+l+n+p=\text{even}\,,\end{split}

6.3
Fluctuation-dissipation theorem

The relation between fluctuations and dissipation is a cornerstone result of statistical physics. It follows from the general structure of the SK EFT. Nevertheless, it is interesting to spell out its various manifestations in our concrete system.

Let us consider the system in thermal equilibrium and linearise around the saddle as in (
27
).
The fluctuation-dissipation theorem (FDT) relates the two point functions
\langle fq\rangle
(dissipation) and
\langle ff\rangle
(fluctuation). In Fourier-space, it states

\begin{split}\Big\langle f(\omega,k)f^{*}(\omega,k)\Big\rangle={\lambda^{2}}\coth\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\lambda^{2}\beta\omega\over 2}}}\right)\operatorname{Re}\Big\langle f(\omega,k)q^{*}(\omega,k)\Big\rangle\,.\end{split}

(93)

Note that

and similarly for

.
This follows by a straightforward application of the KMS symmetry (

\lambda^{2}\beta
, which results in the factor of
\lambda^{2}
in the argument of

above. Furthermore, the fields on the
\pm
folds of the closed-time contour are given as

, giving the overall
\lambda^{2}
factor in (

The linearised SK effective action in Fourier-space takes the schematic form

\begin{split}I_{\text{SK},2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega\,dk}{(2\pi)^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}{f^{*}(\omega,k)}&&{q^{*}(\omega,k)}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&&A(\omega,k)\\
{A^{*}(\omega,k)}&&B(\omega,k)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{f(\omega,k)}\\
{q(\omega,k)}\end{pmatrix}\,,\end{split}

(94)

for some coefficients
A(\omega,k)
and
B(\omega,k)
.
Note that the

From here we can obtain the correlation functions

\begin{split}\Big\langle f(\omega,k)f^{*}(\omega,k)\Big\rangle=\frac{-iB(\omega,k)}{|A(\omega,k)|^{2}},\qquad\Big\langle f(\omega,k)q^{*}(\omega,k)\Big\rangle=\frac{i}{A^{*}(\omega,k)}\,.\end{split}

(95)

Imposing the FDT, we find that

\begin{split}B(\omega,k)=-i\lambda^{2}\coth\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\lambda^{2}\beta\omega\over 2}}}\right)\operatorname{Im}A(\omega,k)\,.\end{split}

in the action.

6.4
Entropy current

An elegant consequence of the dynamical KMS symmetry is that it leads to an entropy current
{\cal S}^{\mu}
whose divergence is locally non-negative. This was proven in
46
in full generality in the SK EFT framework by using only the unitary properties of the SK effective action together with KMS symmetry.
Here we use a slightly different algorithm presented in
9
to extract the entropy current in our theory.

Two Lagrangians give rise to the same action if they differ by a total derivative. Our SK EFT has multiple symmetries. We can always make the Lagrangian exactly invariant under one symmetry, but under another symmetry transformation the Lagrangian may change by a total derivative as in (
71
). For the construction of the entropy current
\mathcal{S}^{\mu}
, we consider the SK Lagrangian
{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}(F,Q)
in the form (
21
), such that it is exactly invariant under the symmetry transformation (
90b
), without the need of total derivative terms. This transformation composes the KMS transformation with a difference time translation.

We know that the Lagrangian must take the form
{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}(F,Q)=-\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}Q+O(Q^{2})
, where
\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}
is some differential operator acting on
Q
. The operator
\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}
has information regarding the classical equations of motion. In fact,

\displaystyle\text{EoM}=\lambda\frac{\delta I_{\text{SK}}}{\delta Q}\bigg|_{Q=0}\quad\implies\quad-\lambda\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}Q=Q\,(\text{EoM})+\partial_{\mu}\big(\hat{\cal S}^{\mu}Q\big)\,,

(99)

where we have extracted the total derivative term left over from variational derivatives for some linear differential operator
\hat{\cal S}^{\mu}
acting on
Q
. The entropy current is given as
{\cal S}^{\mu}=\hat{\cal S}^{\mu}1
; provided that the SK Lagrangian is exactly invariant under (
90b
). Note that the entropy current is a classical object, which has not been generalised to include noise terms.

Following
46
, the local entropy production rate is given as

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}{\cal S}^{\mu}=-(\text{EoM})+\Delta,

(100a)

where

\displaystyle\Delta=\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dq\,\frac{\pi\cosh(\pi q)}{\sinh^{2}(\pi q)}\operatorname{Im}{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}(F,q)\,.

(100b)

Technically, (
100
) only applies to theories where
\operatorname{Im}{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}
is even under time reversal, which is true for our case.
We have presented a self-contained general proof of the entropy production rate in Appendix
E
.

We can use the algorithm above to read off the entropy current and entropy production rate for our theory. To leading order in
\lambda
, we find

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}^{\mu}=gN{2\pi^{3}\over 3}\begin{pmatrix}{1\over D}\,T\\[4.0pt]
-\partial_{x}T\end{pmatrix}+O(\lambda^{2})\,,\qquad\Delta=gN{2\pi^{3}\over 3}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial_{x}T}}\right)^{2}\over T}+O(\lambda^{2})\,.\end{split}

(101)

Note that the entropy density satisfies the first law of thermodynamics

\displaystyle d{\cal E}^{t}=Td{\cal S}^{t}\,,

(102)

understood to apply to leading order in derivatives.
One may verify that the entropy current satisfies the second law (
100
) upon using the equation of motion (
24
).
We have included the entropy current calculation to
O(\lambda^{4})
in our theory in the supplementary Mathematica notebook and have explicitly checked that (
100
) holds to that order.

After using the equations of motion, the total on-shell entropy production is given as

\displaystyle S_{\text{tot}}=\int dtdx\,\Delta=\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dq\,\frac{\pi\cosh(\pi q)}{\sinh^{2}(\pi q)}\operatorname{Im}I_{\text{SK}}[F,q]\,,

(103)

One of the unitarity conditions in SK EFTs is that
\operatorname{Im}I_{\text{SK}}\geq 0
for all field configurations. Since it is multiplied by a positive factor, it follows that
S_{\text{tot}}\geq 0
. However, hydrodynamics is built around the notion of local thermodynamic equilibrium. Therefore, we expect a stronger local version of the second law to hold in hydrodynamics, i.e.
\Delta\geq 0
at every point in spacetime. This would require that
\operatorname{Im}{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}\geq 0
locally, which is not guaranteed by
\operatorname{Im}I_{\text{SK}}\geq 0
. Note that this is true in our theory at leading order in
\lambda

101

Fortunately, the algorithm outlined above to extract the entropy current leaves room for improvements. In particular, we can improve the SK Lagrangian with total derivative terms that respect (
90b

\partial_{t}^{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q(1+iQ)/\partial_{t}F}}\right)

{\cal S}^{\mu}
and
\Delta
. We prove that these improvements can be tuned in such a way that
\operatorname{Im}{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}\geq 0
to any order in
\lambda

28
28

28

What we mean by this is the following: for a generic fluid configuration, this condition is satisfied by the leading order expression of
\Delta
at every spacetime point. However, it may happen that at a spacetime point

. By contrast, the Lagrange description is in terms of a coordinate field
X^{x}(t,y)
such that
u^{x}(t,X^{x}(t,y))=\partial_{t}X^{x}(t,y)
. Similarly in our case, the Euler description of energy diffusion is given in terms of the temperature field
T(t,x)
, which is expressed in the Lagrange-like description as
T(X(\sigma,x),x)^{-1}=\beta\partial_{\sigma}X(\sigma,x)
, where
\beta
is a reference inverse temperature.
We can think of the
X_{+}
and
X_{-}
fields as living on the forward and backward folds of the closed-time contour respectively. The theory features a pair of global time translation symmetries that act independently on the
X_{+}
and
X_{-}
fields, i.e.

\displaystyle X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)\to X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)-\xi_{\pm}.

(110)

We can introduce background fields for these symmetries,
e^{\pm}_{\mu}
from Section
4
. The respective covariant derivatives are given as

\begin{split}D_{\sigma}X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)&=e^{\pm}_{t}(X_{\pm}(\sigma,x),x)\,\partial_{\sigma}X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)\,,\\
D_{x}X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)&=e^{\pm}_{t}(X_{\pm}(\sigma,x),x)\,\partial_{x}X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)+e^{\pm}_{x}(X_{\pm}(\sigma,x),x)\,.\end{split}

(111)

(Note that
\sigma
here is not a spacetime index.) These objects are invariant (not just covariant) under time diffeomorphisms

\displaystyle X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)\to X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)-\xi_{\pm}\big(X_{\pm}(\sigma,x),x\big)\,.

(112)

higher derivatives, like
\partial_{\sigma}D_{\sigma}X_{\pm}
, are automatically invariant under time diffeomorphisms.

Worldvolume reparametrisation symmetry

The theory is required to be invariant under
space-dependent
time reparametrisations on the worldvolume

\displaystyle\sigma\to\sigma-a(x)\,,

(113)

which acts on the fields as:
X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)\to X_{\pm}(\sigma-a(x),x)
. Physically, this symmetry amounts to a freedom of arbitrarily relabelling the internal clock on the worldvolume without affecting the physical observables. The theory is also invariant under global spatial translation symmetries (and rotation symmetries in higher spatial dimensions) on the worldvolume, which act on the fields as usual.

Unitarity conditions

The SK effective action
I_{\text{SK}}[X_{+},X_{-}]
is generally complex. However, requiring that it emerges from a unitary microscopic theory results in three non-trivial unitarity conditions
71
:

\displaystyle I_{\text{SK}}[X,X]=0\,,\qquad I_{\text{SK}}[X_{-},X_{+}]=-I^{*}_{\text{SK}}[X_{+},X_{-}]\,,\qquad\operatorname{Im}I_{\text{SK}}[X_{+},X_{-}]\geq 0\,.

(114)

Broadly speaking, they arise from fundamental properties of density matrices in quantum mechanics:
\tr\rho=1
,
\rho^{*}=\rho
, and
0\leq\text{Eigenvalues}(\rho)\leq 1
respectively. The unitarity conditions naturally generalise in the presence of background fields, with
X_{\pm}
replaced with
\{X_{\pm},e^{\pm}_{\mu}\}
.

KMS symmetry

Lastly, to describe correlation functions in the thermal state, the SK EFT needs to be invariant under a dynamical KMS symmetry, which acts on the dynamical and background fields as

\begin{split}X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)&\to-X_{\pm}\big({-}\sigma\pm i\lambda^{2}\beta/2,x\big)\pm i\lambda^{2}\beta/2\,,\\
e^{\pm}_{t}(t,x)&\to e^{\pm}_{t}(-t\pm i\lambda^{2}\beta/2,x)\,,\\
e^{\pm}_{x}(t,x)&\to-e^{\pm}_{x}(-t\pm i\lambda^{2}\beta/2,x)\,.\end{split}

(115)

The factors of
\lambda
appear above because we are working in rescaled coordinates.
Note that the dynamical KMS symmetry leaves the equilibrium state
X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)=\sigma
invariant. It also leaves any time independent background field configurations invariant, up to an overall time-reversal flip. Similarly to
Section

6
, we can define a KMS shift symmetry by combining the KMS symmetry with a constant difference time translation. This acts as simply

\begin{split}X_{\pm}(\sigma,x)&\to-X_{\pm}\big({-}\sigma\pm i\lambda^{2}\beta/2,x\big)\,,\\
e^{\pm}_{t}(t,x)&\to e^{\pm}_{t}(-t,x)\,,\\
e^{\pm}_{x}(t,x)&\to-e^{\pm}_{x}(-t,x)\,.\end{split}

(116)

The dynamical KMS symmetry acts differently on the forward and backward folds of the Lorentzian contour, making it quite hard to implement during bottom-up constructions of SK EFTs. Generally, one is only able to implement the KMS symmetry in the
classical regime
, where the characteristic frequencies of fluctuations lie sufficiently below the quantum scale, i.e.
\lambda^{2}\omega\ll 1/\beta
.
33
33

33

Restoring the reduced Planck’s constant
\hbar
, this condition is
\lambda^{2}\omega\ll 1/(\hbar\beta)
.
We define the average/difference basis of fields
X_{\pm}=X\pm\lambda^{2}X_{a}/2
, on which the KMS shift symmetry on
X_{\pm}
simplifies in this regime:

\begin{split}X(\sigma,x)&\to-X(-\sigma,x)+O(\lambda^{4}\beta^{2})\,,\\
X_{a}(\sigma,x)&\to-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X_{a}+i\beta\partial_{\sigma}X}}\right)(-\sigma,x)+\beta\,O(\lambda^{4}\beta^{2})\,,\end{split}

(117)

which is easier to ensure in bottom-up constructions of SK EFTs. Note that, for the continuum hydrodynamic description to be valid, we have already assumed that
\lambda^{2}\omega\ll D/a^{2}

denotes the microscopic lattice spacing and recall that

By contrast, in this work we have derived the SK EFT for energy diffusion starting from the microscopic model of an SYK chain. Therefore, our theory respects the full quantum KMS symmetry, order-by-order in
\lambda

\lambda^{2}\omega\ll D/a^{2}\sim 1/\beta

We emphasise that the main virtue of the SK effective action derived in this work is that, through the chain of steps shown in Figure

8
Outlook

In this paper, we gave new arguments for the microscopic soft mode action (
5
) of the SYK lattice valid for energies much smaller than the coupling
J
.
From this starting point we obtained the nonlinear theory of fluctuating hydrodynamics (
21
) including higher derivative corrections. We have computed a few subleading corrections, but our formalism allows one to compute them algorithmically to arbitrary order relatively easily. We also proved the presence of the dynamical KMS symmetry conjectured to be present in any hydrodynamic theory, and we obtained the energy and entropy currents. We obtained a perfect match with the bottom-up construction of the Schwinger–Keldysh EFT of energy diffusion, after finding the appropriate mapping of the degrees of freedom.

The explicit all-order hydrodynamic action obtained in this paper can be used to address many interesting questions; let us list some of them here:

Additional charges

The only conserved quantity for the Majorana SYK lattice considered in this paper is the energy.
One obvious generalisation is the addition of extra charges, for example a U(1) charge. A single complex SYK dot with a U(1) charge has an extra “phase-reparametrisation” field in its low-energy effective action
50
. It would be interesting to find the counterpart of this theory for the SYK lattice and subsequently derive the associated SK EFT with energy and charge diffusion.

Quantum-complete SK EFT

We compared our results to the bottom-up approach to the SK EFT for energy diffusion at leading order in
\lambda
. As we discussed, a bottom-up SK EFT construction for higher derivative energy diffusion, consistent with fully quantum dynamical KMS symmetry, is missing in the literature. In light of our results, it will be interesting to revisit this question along the lines of
59
. In particular, we hope to find a closed-form non-perturbative version of our SK EFT valid at arbitrary
\lambda
, compatible with the non-perturbative energy diffusion equation (
52
) for our model. We expect that such an SK EFT will also manifest the
\SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})
symmetry of the SYK chain.

Stochastic transport coefficients

We have found a stochastic transport coefficient at
O(\lambda^{2})
in our theory, which belongs to a class of free parameters in SK EFTs that are only visible through fluctuations and do not enter the classical equations of motion
58
. In general, we expect many more such coefficients to appear in our theory at higher orders in
\lambda
. Not much is known about these coefficients at present, besides that they leave signatures in correlation functions via stochastic or quantum loop effects. Our theory provides one of the few non-equilibrium models where exact analytic calculations can be performed, which we hope to leverage to test and study these stochastic transport coefficients in more detail.

Entanglement entropy and OTOC

The action (
5
) is valid for any time contour. In this paper we considered the standard SK contour but it would be interesting to explore multi-fold time contours relevant for OTOC
22
and entanglement entropy (both conventional and entanglement “in time”
42
;
82
) which differ only in boundary conditions; the works
103
;
80
on Brownian SYK may provide important clues. Multi-fold contours also appear in the computation of spectral-form factor and their hydrodynamics has been addressed in
112
;
32
.
We refer to
88
for recent progress in the direction of the OTOC.

Gravity dual

A single low-temperature SYK dot has a dual gravitational description in terms of two-dimensional Jackiw–Teitelboim gravity
76
. It is an interesting question whether (
5
) can arise from higher-dimensional gravity.

Quantisation

The large
N
limit has allowed us to treat (
5
) semiclassically. It would be interesting to understand
1/N
corrections which become important when the temperature is of order
1/N
. The Schwarzian theory is exactly solvable
11
;
79
;
102
, with free-energy being one-loop exact, so the challenge comes from the non-local part. For two coupled SYK dots a step in this direction was taken in
86
. It was shown that keeping only the one-loop contribution to the density of states was not enough to match finite
N
numerical computation. This question is important for higher-dimensional black holes: the Schwarzian action can be seen as a spherical reduction of the Einstein action, whereas the non-local action comes from integrating out light matter
86
. It was recently discovered that classical effects from light matter fields near black hole horizons can lead to large (and sometimes singular) changes in the geometry and thermodynamics
56
. However, the fate of these effects on the quantum level at low temperatures is yet to be understood.

Generalisation to open quantum systems

Another interesting generalisation which should be accessible with our formalism is the case of open system dynamics, when the SYK lattice is coupled to a bath or undergoes a measurement process. The dynamics of the SYK reparametrisations under the presence of measurements has been previously investigated in
84
.

Experimental realisation

There are several proposals on the experimental realisation of an SYK dot
38
;
74
;
96
;
31
;
111
;
13
. One such proposal
31
, which was recently realised in a lab
7
, is through a graphene flake with an irregular boundary in an external magnetic field. There
0+1
dimensional SYK fermions are realised as electrons sitting on the lowest Landau level with disorder coming from the shape of the irregular boundary. It is a natural question whether the lattice model studied in this paper can be realised using known materials.

Appendix C
Comparison with the Brownian particle

It is instructive to compare and contrast our results to the paradigmatic SK EFT of a Brownian particle. We will follow the presentation of Section III. C. of
71
. In the large mass limit the SK EFT to leading order in derivatives is given by

\begin{split}L=-q(M\ddot{x}+kx+\nu\dot{x})+{i\over 2}{\sigma\over\hbar}q^{2}\,.\end{split}

(142)

The dynamical KMS transformation to leading order in derivatives is

\begin{split}{x}(t)&\to x(-t)\,,\\
{q}(t)&\to q(-t)-i\hbar\beta\dot{x}(-t)\,.\end{split}

(143)

We can make it more manifest that quantum fluctuations are small, by introducing
q=\hbar\widetilde{q}
, then the path integral becomes

\begin{split}Z=\int DxD\widetilde{q}\,e^{iS/\hbar}=\int DxD\widetilde{q}\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[i\int dt\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\widetilde{q}(M\ddot{x}+kx+\nu\dot{x})+{i\over 2}\sigma\widetilde{q}^{2}}}\right)}}\right]\,,\end{split}

(144)

and the KMS transformation of
\widetilde{q}
does not contain
\hbar
anymore:

\begin{split}{\widetilde{q}}(t)&\to\widetilde{q}(-t)-i\beta\dot{x}(-t)\,.\end{split}

(145)

The SK EFT only has KMS symmetry, if

\begin{split}\nu=\frac{1}{2}\beta\sigma\,.\end{split}

(146)

This relation is the Einstein relation of Brownian motion. To see this more explicitly, following
71
we integrate in a Hubbard-Stratonovich field using the relation

\begin{split}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[i\int dt\,{i\over 2}\sigma\widetilde{q}^{2}}}\right]=\int D\xi\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[i\int dt\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({i\over 2\sigma}\xi^{2}+\xi\widetilde{q}}}\right)}}\right]\,,\end{split}

(147)

and integrate out
\widetilde{q}
to obtain

\begin{split}Z=\int DxD\xi\,\delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\ddot{x}+kx+\nu\dot{x}-\xi}}\right)\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-\int dt\,{1\over 2\sigma}\xi^{2}}}\right]\,.\end{split}

(148)

This is just a Langevin equation (imposed as a functional delta function) with the Gaussian white noise

\begin{split}\langle\xi(t)\xi(t^{\prime})\rangle=\sigma\delta(t-t^{\prime})\,.\end{split}

(149)

Then (
146
) is indeed the Einstein relation connecting the noise to friction through the equipartition relation (or Boltzmann distribution for the energy of the Brownian particle).

We note in passing that the Hubbard-Stratonovich trick can be generalised to the case when we have non-Gaussian noise. We introduce
\xi
as a Lagrange multiplier together with another auxiliary field
\chi

\begin{split}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[i\int dt\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({i\over 2}\sigma\widetilde{q}^{2}+\lambda\widetilde{q}^{3}+\dots}}\right)}}\right]=\int D\xi D\chi\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[i\int dt\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\xi(\widetilde{q}-\chi)+{i\over 2}\sigma\chi^{2}+\lambda\chi^{3}+\dots}}\right)}}\right]\,.\end{split}

(150)

If
\lambda
and all implicit higher order terms are zero, we can integrate out
\chi
and obtain (
147
). If however, these are present, we proceed as in (
148
), and integrate out
q
to get a Langevin equation, but this time with a non-Gaussian noise distribution

\begin{split}P[\xi]=\int D\chi\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[i\int dt\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\xi\chi+{i\over 2}\sigma\chi^{2}+\lambda\chi^{3}+\dots}}\right)}}\right]\,.\end{split}

(151)

In large
N
systems in general and in the SYK chain in particular,
1/N
plays an analogous role to
\hbar
. Indeed, we introduced
\widetilde{Q}
that had
O(1)
fluctuations. In our system the KMS transformation (
91b
), the analogue of (
143
), does not include any power of
N
, but otherwise takes a very similar form

\begin{split}Q(t,x)\rightarrow-Q\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-t,x}}\right)-i\Big(\beta\partial_{t}F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-t,x}}\right)-1\Big)\,.\end{split}

(152)

If however, we aim to use
\widetilde{Q}
, then it transform by a large shift, mixing different orders in the
1/N
expansion:

\begin{split}\widetilde{Q}(t,x)\rightarrow-\widetilde{Q}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-t,x}}\right)-i\sqrt{N}\Big(\beta\partial_{t}F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-t,x}}\right)-1\Big)\,.\end{split}

(153)

Nevertheless, if
F=t/\beta+O(1/\sqrt{N})
, i.e. it is close to the global thermal state, then the natural
N
scaling of
Q=O(1/\sqrt{N})
is respected. Such an
F
would be natural in the case, when we turn on infinitesimal sources to compute thermal correlators. However, if we drive the state far from equilibrium with large sources,
F
can deviate from the thermal value by an arbitrary amount.

The dynamical KMS symmetry relates different coefficients to each other in the SK EFT, leading to fluctuation-dissipation relations and the Einstein relation discussed around eqs. (
93

53

146

Proof of the second law

In this appendix, we provide a proof of how the local second law of thermodynamics (

E.1
Derivation of the entropy production rate

Let us express the SK Lagrangian as

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}(F,Q)=\sum_{m=1}^{2R}(-i)^{m+1}\hat{\cal L}_{(m)}Q^{m},

(159)

where
\hat{\cal L}_{(m)}
are
F

Q^{m}

37
37

37

For example,
\hat{\cal L}_{(2)}AB=\hat{\cal D}_{1}A\,\hat{\cal D}_{2}B+\hat{\cal D}_{2}A\,\hat{\cal D}_{1}B
for some linear differential operators
\hat{\cal D}_{1,2}
and arbitrary functions
A
and
B
.
We will find it useful to split the operators into their time reversal even and odd parts, denoted
\hat{\cal L}_{(m)}^{e}
and
\hat{\cal L}_{(m)}^{o}
respectively.
Note that the expansion lacks a
O(Q^{0})
term due to the first condition in (
120
). For clarity, we have chosen to truncate the expansion at
O(Q^{2R})
, where
2R-1
denotes the maximal odd power of
Q
appearing in the Lagrangian working at a given order in derivatives. We can always take
R\to\infty
in the following discussion.

The symmetry (
90b
) can be expressed in terms of the Lagrangian as

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}\big(\Theta F,\Theta Q\big)\Big|_{t\to-t}

\displaystyle={\cal L}_{\text{SK}}(F,Q+i)\,.

(160)

Taking the imaginary part of this equation, we find

\begin{split}\operatorname{Im}\Big[{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}(\Theta F,\Theta Q)\Big]_{-t\to t}&=\frac{1}{2i}\Big[{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}\big(F,Q+i\big)-{\cal L}^{*}_{\text{SK}}\big(F,Q-i\big)\Big]\\
&=\frac{1}{2i}\Big[{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}\big(F,Q+i\big)+{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}\big(F,-Q+i\big)\Big]\,,\end{split}

(161)

where in the second step we used the second unitarity condition in (
120
). We can now Taylor expand the two terms in (
161
) in their noise arguments
\pm Q+i
. Recall from (
99
) that the

term in the expansion can be expressed in terms of the equations of motion and the entropy current operator

\displaystyle-\lambda\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}Q=Q\,(\text{EoM})+\partial_{\mu}\big(\hat{\cal S}^{\mu}Q\big)\,,

(162)

where
\hat{\cal S}^{\mu}
is understood as a linear differential operator acting on

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i}\Big[{-}\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}(Q+i)-\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}(-Q+i)\Big]=-\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}1=\frac{1}{\lambda}\text{EoM}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\partial_{\mu}{\cal S}^{\mu},

(163)

where
{\cal S}^{\mu}=\hat{\cal S}^{\mu}1
.
At next order, we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i}\Big[i\hat{\cal L}_{(2)}(Q+i)^{2}+i\hat{\cal L}_{(2)}(-Q+i)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\hat{\cal L}_{(2)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q^{2}-1^{2}}}\right)\,.

(164)

The complete expansion is given as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\Big[{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}(\Theta F,\Theta Q)\Big]_{-t\to t}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\text{EoM}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\partial_{\mu}{\cal S}^{\mu}-\sum_{m=2}^{2R}\hat{\cal L}_{(m)}\operatorname{Re}(1+iQ)^{m}.

(165)

Gaussian noise

Let us first analyse the simpler case of Gaussian noise, meaning that the Lagrangian is at most quadratic in noise fields. In this case

and we have

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{\text{Gaussian}}_{\text{SK}}(F,Q)=-\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}Q+i\hat{\cal L}_{(2)}Q^{2}.

(166)

Imposing the symmetry (

\displaystyle\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}^{e}Q=-\hat{\cal L}^{e}_{(2)}1Q,\qquad\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}^{o}1=0,\qquad\hat{\cal L}_{(2)}^{o}Q^{2}=0.

(167)

These conditions should be understood as being imposed for arbitrary
Q

The notation
\hat{\cal L}_{(2)}1Q
means that the bilinear operator
\hat{\cal L}_{(2)}
acts on 1 and
Q

\hat{\cal L}_{(1)}^{o}
acting on 1 vanishes. Importantly, these constraints leave the even part of the quadratic operator
\smash{\hat{\cal L}^{e}_{(2)}}
completely arbitrary. However, to agree with the non-negativity unitarity constraint in (

\displaystyle\hat{\cal L}^{e}_{(2)}Q^{2}\geq 0.

(168)

One may check that the our SK Lagrangian in (
21

\hat{\cal L}^{o}_{(1)}
and
\hat{\cal L}^{e}_{(2)}

In the Gaussian case, we may evaluate (

\displaystyle\Delta=\lambda\operatorname{Im}\Big[{\cal L}^{\text{Gaussian}}_{\text{SK}}(\Theta F,-1)\Big]_{-t\to t}=\lambda\operatorname{Im}{\cal L}^{\text{Gaussian}}_{\text{SK}}(F,1)=\lambda{\cal L}_{(2)}1^{2}.

(169)

This agrees with the entropy production formula in (
100

Non-Gaussian noise

Let us now turn our attention to the general case. Plugging the Lagrangian (

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{2R}

\displaystyle\Big(\hat{\cal L}_{(m)}^{e}-\hat{\cal L}_{(m)}^{o}\Big)(iQ)^{m}

E.2
Local positivity of entropy production

From the SK unitarity conditions (
120
), we know that
\operatorname{Im}I_{\text{SK}}[F,Q]\geq 0
for all field configurations. However, since the imaginary part
I_{\text{SK}}^{o}
flips sign for time reversed configurations, we must require that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}I^{e}_{\text{SK}}[F,Q]\geq\big|\operatorname{Im}I^{o}_{\text{SK}}[F,Q]\big|.

(178)

This condition guarantees that the total entropy production, integrated over space and time, is always non-negative. To wit,

\displaystyle S_{\text{tot}}=\int dtdx\,\Delta=\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dq\,\frac{\pi}{\sinh^{2}(\pi q)}\bigg(\cosh(\pi q)\operatorname{Im}I^{e}_{\text{SK}}[F,q]+\operatorname{Im}I^{o}_{\text{SK}}[F,q]\bigg)\,,

(179)

Since
\cosh(\pi q)\geq 1
for all
q
, (
178
) implies that
S_{\text{tot}}\geq 0
. This is essentially the generalisation of the argument presented near (
103
).

However, hydrodynamics is built around the notion of local thermodynamic equilibrium. Therefore, we expect a stronger local version of the second law to hold in hydrodynamics: that the on-shell divergence of the entropy current
\Delta
is non-negative at every point in spacetime. To achieve this, we note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}I_{\text{SK}}=\int dtdx\operatorname{Im}{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}\geq 0.

(180)

Provided that
\operatorname{Im}{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}
is smooth and falls of sufficiently fast at infinity, it follows that there must exist a smooth vector field
V^{\mu}
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}+\partial_{\mu}V^{\mu}\geq 0.

(181)

In other words, we can modify the Lagrangian with total derivative terms that leave the SK action invariant, i.e.

\displaystyle\widetilde{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}={\cal L}_{\text{SK}}+i\partial_{\mu}V^{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}U^{\mu},

(182)

such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\widetilde{\cal L}_{\text{SK}}(F,Q)\geq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Im}\widetilde{\cal L}^{e}_{\text{SK}}(F,Q)\geq\big|\operatorname{Im}\widetilde{\cal L}^{o}_{\text{SK}}(F,Q)\big|.

(183)

The vector
U^{\mu}
here is picked in such a way that the new Lagrangian continues to satisfy the symmetry constraint (
160
). We have explicitly checked the existence of such a
V^{\mu}
and
U^{\mu}
for our theory in the supplementary Mathematica notebook to
O(\lambda^{4})
.

We can now run the construction above to find the new entropy current
\widetilde{\cal S}^{\mu}
using (
99
) such that associated the entropy production rate
\widetilde{\Delta}
given by (
177
) is locally non-negative. One simply finds

\begin{split}\widetilde{\cal S}^{\mu}&={\cal S}^{\mu}+\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dq\,\frac{\pi}{\sinh^{2}(\pi q)}\bigg(\cosh(\pi q)V^{\mu e}(F,q)+V^{\mu o}(F,q)\bigg)\,.\end{split}

(184)

Here we have introduced a splitting
V^{\mu}=V^{\mu e}+V^{\mu o}
, such that
\partial_{\mu}V^{\mu e}
and
\partial_{\mu}V^{\mu o}
are even and odd under time reversal respectively. Note that this means
V^{te}
,
V^{xo}
are odd under time reversal, while
V^{to}
,
V^{xe}
are even. We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}=\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dq\,\frac{\pi}{\sinh^{2}(\pi q)}\bigg(\cosh(\pi q)\operatorname{Im}\widetilde{\cal L}^{e}_{\text{SK}}(F,q)+\operatorname{Im}\widetilde{\cal L}^{o}_{\text{SK}}(F,q)\bigg)\,,

(185)

which is locally non-negative due to (
183
).

Constructing
U^{\mu}

To complete the proof of local entropy production, we only need to convince ourselves that a
U^{\mu}
exists for any choice of
V^{\mu}
ensuring (
160
). We implement the expansion

\begin{split}V^{\mu}=\sum_{m=1}^{R}(-1)^{m+1}\hat{\cal V}^{\mu}_{(m)}Q^{2m},\qquad U^{\mu}=\sum_{m=1}^{R}(-1)^{m+1}\hat{\cal U}^{\mu}_{(m)}Q^{2m-1}\,.\end{split}

(186)

As before, we have introduced a truncation of the expansion parametrised by
R
. We will define it precisely below.
We introduce the even-odd splitting of operators,
\hat{\cal V}^{\mu}_{(m)}=\hat{\cal V}^{e\mu}_{(m)}+\hat{\cal V}^{o\mu}_{(m)}
, such that
\partial_{\mu}\hat{\cal V}^{e\mu}_{(m)}
and
\partial_{\mu}\hat{\cal V}^{o\mu}_{(m)}
are even and odd under time reversal respectively. We introduce a similar splitting for the operators
\hat{\cal U}^{\mu}_{(m)}
.

Plugging the decomposition (
186
) into the symmetry condition (
160
), we find the constraints akin to (
171
), i.e.

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{R}\hat{\cal U}_{(m)}1^{2m-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m=1}^{R}\hat{\cal V}_{(m)}1^{2m},

(187)

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\hat{\cal U}_{(m)}Q^{2m-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{r=m}^{R}{\textstyle\binom{2r}{2m-1}}\hat{\cal V}_{(r)}1^{2r-2m+1}Q^{2m-1}-\sum_{r=m+1}^{R}{\textstyle\binom{2r-1}{2m-1}}\hat{\cal U}_{(r)}1^{2r-2m}Q^{2m-1},

\displaystyle(1-\eta)\hat{\cal V}_{(m)}Q^{2m}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{r=m+1}^{R}{\textstyle\binom{2r-1}{2m}}\hat{\cal U}_{(r)}1^{2r-2m-1}Q^{2m}-\sum_{r=m+1}^{R}{\textstyle\binom{2r}{2m}}\hat{\cal V}_{(r)}1^{2r-2m}Q^{2m},

where
\eta=+1
in the even sector and
\eta=-1
in the odd sector. We have dropped the spacetime label
\mu
for clarity.
We have to solve these constraints to find
\hat{\cal U}_{(m)}
operators in terms of
\hat{\cal V}_{(m)}
operators. In particular, substituting
m\mapsto R
in the last condition in (
187
) for
\eta=-1
, we find a consistency condition on the
\hat{\cal V}_{(R)}
operator, i.e.

\displaystyle\hat{\cal V}^{o}_{(R)}Q^{2R}=0.

(188)

In other words, we can define
R
as the lowest number for which the operator
\hat{\cal V}^{o}_{(R)}
vanishes. In other words,
R
is the lowest number for which both the time reversal even part of
\hat{\cal V}^{t}_{(R)}
and the time reversal odd part of
\hat{\cal V}^{x}_{(R)}
vanishes.

In the trivial
R=1
case, we find the constraints

\displaystyle\hat{\cal U}^{o}_{(1)}1

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\hat{\cal U}_{(1)}^{e}Q=-\hat{\cal V}_{(1)}^{e}1Q,\qquad\hat{\cal V}^{o}_{(1)}Q^{2}=0,

(189)

which admit a solution

\hat{\cal U}_{(1)}Q=-\hat{\cal V}_{(1)}^{e}1Q.

(190)

Note that this is not the most general solution of the constraints (
189
). However, we only need to illustrate the existence of some
\hat{\cal U}^{\mu}_{(m)}
operators that solve the constraints and not necessarily find the most general ones. The constraints for
R=2
case are more interesting:

\begin{split}\hat{\cal U}_{(1)}^{e}Q&=-\hat{\cal V}_{(1)}^{e}1Q+\hat{\cal V}_{(2)}^{e}1^{3}Q,\\
\hat{\cal U}_{(2)}^{e}Q^{3}&=-2\hat{\cal V}_{(2)}^{e}1Q^{3},\\
\hat{\cal U}_{(2)}^{o}1Q^{2}&=-\frac{2}{3}\hat{\cal V}_{(1)}^{o}Q^{2},\\
\hat{\cal V}^{o}_{(2)}Q^{4}&=0,\end{split}

(191)

which has the solution

\begin{split}\hat{\cal U}_{(1)}Q&=-\hat{\cal V}_{(1)}^{e}1Q+\hat{\cal V}_{(2)}^{e}1^{3}Q-\frac{1}{3}\hat{\cal V}_{(1)}^{o}1Q,\\
\hat{\cal U}_{(2)}Q^{3}&=-2\hat{\cal V}_{(2)}^{e}1Q^{3}-\frac{2}{3}\Big(3Q\hat{\cal V}_{(1)}^{o}Q^{2}-3Q^{2}\hat{\cal V}_{(1)}^{o}1Q+Q^{3}\hat{\cal V}_{(1)}^{o}1^{2}\Big)\,.\end{split}

(192)

We have checked in the Mathematica file that this procedure to generate
U^{\mu}
can be extended to arbitrarily large values of
R
. It seems likely that there exists an explicit proof by induction, but we leave this exercise for future work.
