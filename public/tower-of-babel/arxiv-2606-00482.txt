On ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -extensions of real quadratic fields
Sosuke Sasaki

Abstract
. Let k k be a real quadratic field. Let k n k_{n} be the n n -th layer of the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -extension of k k , and 2 a n 2^{a_{n}} be the 2 2 -part of the class number of k n k_{n} . 1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a real quadratic field, and
consider the (cyclotomic)
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extension

(2)

k=k_{0}\subseteq k_{1}\subseteq k_{2}\subseteq\dots\subseteq k_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}k_{n}.

The field
k_{n}
is called the
n
-th layer of this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extension,
which is the unique intermediate field of
k_{\infty}/k
with degree
2^{n}
over
k
.
Let
A_{n}
be the
2
-Sylow subgroup of the ideal class group of
k_{n}
,
and let
2^{a_{n}}
be the order of
A_{n}
.
From Iwasawa’s class number formula
[
9
,
10
]
and
the Ferrero–Washington theorem
[
1
]
,
there exist a non-negative integer
\lambda_{2}(k)
and an integer
\nu_{2}(k)

such that for any sufficiently large integer
n
,

(3)

a_{n}=\lambda_{2}(k)n+\nu_{2}(k)

holds.
Under Greenberg’s conjecture
[
6
]
we have
\lambda_{2}(k)=0
,
i.e.,
a_{n}
is eventually constant.
However, even in the case of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extensions of real quadratic fields,
Greenberg’s conjecture remains unsolved.
In
[
4
]
,
it is shown that
\lambda_{2}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m}))=0
for all

1<m<10^{6}
.

Our aim in the present paper is to understand
the behavior of
a_{n}
not only for sufficiently large
n

but also for small
n
.

What kinds of integer sequences could there be for
(a_{0},a_{1},\dotsc)
?
Of course, not just any sequence will do;
there are many restrictions.
Iwasawa’s class number formula and the Ferrero–Washington theorem
are among such restrictions.
Here are other examples of well-known restrictions.
Let
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})
, where
m>1
is a square-free odd integer.
The class field theory yields that
the sequence
(a_{0},a_{1},\dotsc)
is non-decreasing.
Further, Fukuda’s theorem
[
2
]

states that if
a_{n}=a_{n+1}
, then
a_{i}=a_{n}
for all
i\geq n
.
Also, Kumakawa showed
(at an informal seminar; a paper is currently being prepared):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
, where
p
is a prime with
p\equiv 17\pmod{32}
.
If
a_{1}=1
and
a_{2}=3
, then
a_{3}=3

(and hence
a_{i}=3
for all
i\geq 2
from Fukuda’s theorem).

Similar to this theorem,
we wish to find many restrictions on the sequence
(a_{0},a_{1},\dotsc)

under certain conditions.
In this paper, we give a general method for this purpose.
For example,
we obtained the following results
(see
Theorems

6.5
and
6.6
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
, where
p
is a prime with
p\equiv 1\pmod{16}
.
Assume that the norm of the fundamental unit of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2p})
is
-1
.
If
a_{1}\geq 3
and
N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{8p})/k(\zeta_{8})}(1-\zeta_{8p})<0
,
then
a_{2}=a_{1}
(and hence

for all

.

Let

2.
Cyclotomic Units

In this section, we shall define cyclotomic units
denoted by
c_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
,
and summarize their fundamental properties.
In the case
m
is a prime,
their properties are summarized in
[
3
]
,
hence we extend them to the general

.
First, we define the following units.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 0
.

(1)

Let
c_{n}=1+\zeta_{2^{n+2}}+\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{-1}
.

(2)

Let

\eta_{n}=\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{-\varphi(m)s/4}N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}m})/k(\zeta_{2^{n+2}})}(1-\zeta_{2^{n+2}m})
,
where
s
is an integer with
ms\equiv 1\pmod{2^{n+2}}
,
and
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function.

Note that from the assumption on
m
,
we see that
\varphi(m)
is divisible by
4
.

It is well-known that
c_{n}
is a unit of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
The element
\eta_{n}
belongs to Sinnott’s circular unit group of
k(\zeta_{2^{n+2}})=k_{n}(\sqrt{-1})
,
but is actually a real number (see
[
4

).

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
n\geq 0
,
\eta_{n}
is a unit of
k_{n}
.

Next, we construct the following specific subgroups of
E_{n}
using
c_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
.

Definition 2.3
.

We define the subgroups

(4)

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle=\braket{c_{n},c_{n}^{\gamma},c_{n}^{\gamma^{2}},\dots,c_{n}^{\gamma^{2^{n-1}-1}}}\subseteq E_{\mathbb{B}_{n}},

(5)

\displaystyle H_{n}

\displaystyle=\braket{\eta_{n},\eta_{n}^{\gamma},\eta_{n}^{\gamma^{2}},\dots,\eta_{n}^{\gamma^{2^{n-1}-1}}}\subseteq E_{n}

.
Further, we put

(6)

E^{\prime}_{n}=E_{0}\times C_{1}\times H_{1}\times C_{2}\times H_{2}\times\dots\times C_{n}\times H_{n}\subseteq E_{n}

for

Using Sinnott’s formula
[

we can deduce the following relationship between

[

]
).

Theorem 2.4
.

For any

(7)

(E_{n}:E^{\prime}_{n})=\frac{h_{k_{n}}}{h_{k}}.

We want to know the
2
-part of this.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
n\geq 0
.

(1)

Let
E^{\prime\prime}_{n}
be the subgroup of

containing

so that

(2)

Let

and

We have

Corollary 2.6

(E^{\prime\prime}_{n}:V_{n})=2^{a_{n}-a_{n-1}}

Proof.

We obtain

Next we state the following formulae for the norms of

and
\eta_{n}

Proposition 2.7

For

(1)

N_{n,n-1}(c_{n})=-c_{n-1}

(2)

N_{n,n-1}(\eta_{n})=(-1)^{\varphi(m)/4}\eta_{n-1}

The norms to
\mathbb{B}_{n}
are somewhat difficult,
but we have the following explicit formula.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
n\geq 0
.
For a divisor
d
of
m
, let
r_{d}
be
a non-negative integer satisfying
d\equiv\pm 3^{r_{d}}\pmod{2^{n+3}}
.
Then we have

(8)

\eta_{n}^{1+\tau}=\prod_{d\mid m}c_{n}^{-\mu(d)(\gamma^{-1}+\gamma^{-2}+\dots+\gamma^{-r_{d}})},

where
\mu
is the Möbius function.

Proof.

Let
s,t
be integers with
ms+2^{n+3}t=1
.
We have

(9)

\displaystyle\eta_{n}^{1+\tau}

\displaystyle=N_{k_{n}/\mathbb{B}_{n}}\Bigl(\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{-\varphi(m)s/4}N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}m})/k(\zeta_{2^{n+2}})}(1-\zeta_{2^{n+2}m})\Bigr)

(10)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{-\varphi(m)s/2}N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}m})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}})}(1-\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{s}\zeta_{m}^{t})

(11)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{\varphi(m)s/2}N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}m})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}})}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{-s}-\zeta_{m}^{t})

(12)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{\varphi(m)s/2}\Phi_{m}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{-s}),

where
\Phi_{m}
is the
m
-th cyclotomic polynomial.
From the Möbius inversion formula, we know

(13)

\Phi_{m}(X)=\prod_{d\mid m}(X^{m/d}-1)^{\mu(d)},

and also its homogenization

(14)

Y^{\varphi(m)}\Phi_{m}(X/Y)=\prod_{d\mid m}(X^{m/d}-Y^{m/d})^{\mu(d)}.

Substituting
X=\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-s}
and
Y=\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{s}

into this yields

(15)

\eta_{n}^{1+\tau}=\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{\varphi(m)s/2}\Phi_{m}(\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{-s})=\prod_{d\mid m}(\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-ms/d}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{ms/d})^{\mu(d)}.

From the assumption on
r_{d}
,
we obtain
3^{r_{d}}\equiv(-2/d)d\pmod{2^{n+3}}

since
(-2/3)=1
,
where
(\,\cdot\,/\,\cdot\,)
is the Jacobi symbol.
Hence from
ms\equiv 1\pmod{2^{n+3}}
,

(16)

\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-ms/d}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{ms/d}=\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{-2}{d}(\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-1}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}})^{\gamma^{-r_{d}}},

where the domain of
\gamma
is extended as
\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{\gamma}=\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{3}
.
On the other hand,

(17)

(\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-1}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}})^{\gamma-1}=\frac{\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-3}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{3}}{\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-1}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}}}=1+\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{2}+\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-2}=c_{n}.

Therefore we obtain

(18)

c_{n}^{-(\gamma^{-1}+\gamma^{-2}+\dots+\gamma^{-r_{d}})}=(\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-1}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}})^{\gamma^{-r_{d}}-1}=\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{-2}{d}\frac{\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-ms/d}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{ms/d}}{\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-1}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}}},

and thus

(19)

\displaystyle\prod_{d\mid m}c_{n}^{-\mu(d)(\gamma^{-1}+\gamma^{-2}+\dots+\gamma^{-r_{d}})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{d\mid m}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{-2}{d}\left(\frac{\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-ms/d}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{ms/d}}{\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-1}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}}}\right)^{\mu(d)}

(20)

\displaystyle=\prod_{d\mid m}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{-2}{d}(\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{-ms/d}-\zeta_{2^{n+3}}^{ms/d})^{\mu(d)}

since
\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)=0
.
Hence, it suffices to show that
\prod_{d\mid m}(-2/d)=1
.
If
m
is prime, we have
m\equiv 1\ (8)
from our assumption,
which yields
(-2/m)=1
.
If
m
is composite,

(21)

\prod_{d\mid m}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{-2}{d}=\prod_{d\mid m}\prod_{p\mid d}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{-2}{p}=\prod_{p\mid m}\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{-2}{p}^{\sigma_{0}(m/p)}=1,

where
p
runs through the prime divisors
and
\sigma_{0}(m/p)
is the number of the divisors of
m/p

(equivalently, the number of

with
p\mid d\mid m
),
which is even.
∎

Next we state a formula for the signs of the conjugates of

Proposition 2.12
.

Let

be a non-negative integer,

be the integer with

\sgn c_{n}^{\gamma^{i}}=\begin{cases}-1&(2^{n+2}/3<d<2^{n+3}/3),\\

Proposition 2.13
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

E_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\cap E_{n}^{2}=E_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}^{2}
.

Proof.

Take a prime divisor
p
of
m
and
a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
above
p
.
Let

Then
\mathbb{B}_{n}\subsetneq\mathbb{B}_{n}(\sqrt{u})\subseteq k_{n}
,
hence
\mathbb{B}_{n}(\sqrt{u})=k_{n}
.
From the Kummer theory, we have

\sqrt{mu}\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
Hence the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic valuation of
m
must be even,
since
u
is a unit.
This contradicts that
m
is square-free and
\mathbb{B}_{n}/\mathbb{Q}
is unramified outside
2
.
∎

E_{n}

,
then such

.

(2)

If

.

(3)

If

, then

(4)

, then

(5)

(E_{n}\cap E_{n+1}^{2}:E_{n}^{2})=(E^{\prime\prime}_{n}\cap(E^{\prime\prime}_{n+1})^{2}:(E^{\prime\prime}_{n})^{2})

Proof.

Let
n<n^{\prime}
and let

.
Then

is a quadratic extension of

contained in

,
which must be

.
Hence

, and
such a unit

is unique up to

from the Kummer theory.
Thus we have (
1
) and (
3
).

It is well-known that

,
where
\beta_{n}=2-\zeta_{2^{n+2}}-\zeta_{2^{n+2}}^{-1}
.
Hence from the Kummer theory,
we have
\sqrt{\beta_{n}u}\in k_{n}
.
The ideal
(\beta_{n})

is the unique prime ideal of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
above
2
.
Take a prime ideal

of

above
(\beta_{n})
.
Since

is a unit and

,
the

must be even.
If

, this contradicts that

is unramified above

This yields (

).

Let

and let

which yields (

Finally, it is easy to show that

(E^{\prime\prime}_{n}\cap(E^{\prime\prime}_{n+1})^{2})\big/(E^{\prime\prime}_{n})^{2}\simeq(E_{n}\cap E_{n+1}^{2})\big/E_{n}^{2}

∎

3.
Iterative extensions of unit groups

Our goal is to construct
E^{\prime\prime}_{n}
by
iteratively extending
V_{n}
:

(43)

V_{n}=V_{n}^{(0)}\subseteq V_{n}^{(1)}\subseteq\dots\subseteq V_{n}^{(a_{n}-a_{n-1})}=E^{\prime\prime}_{n},

with
(V_{n}^{(j)}:V_{n}^{(j-1)})=2
for
1\leq j\leq a_{n}-a_{n-1}
.
In this iteration, we will also assume
that each
V_{n}^{(j)}
is
\gamma
-invariant.
It is possible from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V
be a subgroup of
E_{n}
containing
-1

and satisfying
V^{\gamma}=V
.
Assume that the index
(E_{n}:V)
is even.
Then there exists

v\in V\cap E_{n}^{2}
satisfying
v^{1+\gamma}\in V^{2}
and
v\notin V^{2}
.
For this
v
, we have
\braket{V,\sqrt{v}}^{\gamma}=\braket{V,\sqrt{v}}
.

Proof.

Take
u\in E_{n}\smallsetminus V
with
u^{2}\in V
.
If
u^{2}\in V^{2}
, then we have
u\in V
from
-1\in V
,
which is absurd.
Therefore
(V\cap E_{n}^{2})/V^{2}
is a non-trivial

\Gal(k_{n}/k)
-module of order
2
-power.
Hence there exists
v\bmod V^{2}\in(V\cap E_{n}^{2})/V^{2}

which is non-trivial and
\gamma
-invariant.
This means
v^{1-\gamma}\in V^{2}
and hence
v^{1+\gamma}\in V^{2}
.
Also we obtain

\sqrt{v}^{1-\gamma}=\pm\sqrt{v^{1-\gamma}}\in V
,
which shows

\braket{V,\sqrt{v}}^{\gamma}=\braket{V,\sqrt{v}}
.
∎

Also, each
V_{n}^{(j)}
is automatically
\tau
-invariant.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
V
be a subgroup of
E_{n}
containing
c_{n}

and satisfying
V^{\gamma}=V^{\tau}=V
.
Then for any
v\in V\cap E_{n}^{2}
, we have
v^{1+\tau}\in V^{2}
,
and also
\braket{V,\sqrt{v}}^{\tau}=\braket{V,\sqrt{v}}
.

Proof.

Put
u=\sqrt{v}^{1+\tau}\in E_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
.
The assumptions
c_{n}\in V
and
V^{\gamma}=V

yield that
V
contains

(44)

C=\braket{-1}\times C_{1}\times\dots\times C_{n},

the cyclotomic unit group of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
From
[
13
, Theorem 8.2]
,
we know
(E_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}:C)=h_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
.
Hence we have
u^{h_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}}\in C\subseteq V
.
Also
u^{2}=v^{1+\tau}\in V

since
V^{\tau}=V
.
It is well-known that
h_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
is odd,
hence
u\in V
, which means

v^{1+\tau}\in V^{2}
.
Also we have

\braket{V,\sqrt{v}}^{\tau}=\braket{V,\sqrt{v}}

similarly as the previous lemma.
∎

\displaystyle\equiv 1+X^{2^{n}}\pmod{2},

(72)

\displaystyle(1+X)^{2^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\equiv 1+X^{2^{n-1}}\pmod{2},

\displaystyle(1+X)^{2^{n}-j-1}f(X)

\displaystyle=1+X+\dots+X^{2^{n}-1}+(1+X^{2^{n}})g(X)+2h_{1}(X),

\displaystyle(1+X)^{2^{n-1}}

\displaystyle=1+X^{2^{n-1}}+2h_{2}(X),

with some

Therefore we have

\displaystyle v^{(1+\gamma)^{2^{n}-j-1}}

\displaystyle=u^{(1+\gamma^{2^{n-1}}+2h_{2}(\gamma))(1+\gamma)^{2^{n-1}-j-1}}c_{n}^{1+\gamma+\dots+\gamma^{2^{n}-1}}c_{n}^{(1+\gamma^{2^{n}})g(\gamma)+2h_{1}(\gamma)}

Since

,
which is a contradiction.
Hence we have
f\equiv 0\pmod{2}

and also

.
This proves the lemma.

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
n\geq 1
and assume

a_{n}-a_{n-1}<2^{n-1}
.
Then
a_{i}-a_{i-1}\geq a_{i+1}-a_{i}
for all
i\geq n
.

Proof.

It suffices to show
a_{n}-a_{n-1}\geq a_{n+1}-a_{n}
.
The sequence
(\delta_{n})_{n}
is non-increasing
from
Proposition

2.14
(
4
).
Hence we have

(98)

\widetilde{a}_{n}-\widetilde{a}_{n-1}\leq a_{n}-a_{n-1}<2^{n-1}.

From
Theorem

4.14
we obtain

\widetilde{a}_{n+1}-\widetilde{a}_{n}\leq\widetilde{a}_{n}-\widetilde{a}_{n-1}
, or equivalently,

(99)

a_{n+1}-a_{n}\leq a_{n}-a_{n-1}-\delta_{n+1}+2\delta_{n}-\delta_{n-1}.

If
\delta_{n+1}=\delta_{n}
, this yields
a_{n}-a_{n-1}\geq a_{n+1}-a_{n}
.
So we assume
\delta_{n-1}=\delta_{n}=1
and
\delta_{n+1}=0
,
and also assume that the above inequality is an equality.
Note that we have
a_{n}>a_{n-1}
from Fukuda’s theorem
[
2
]
.
Using
Theorem

4.14
for
j=\widetilde{a}_{n+1}-\widetilde{a}_{n}=a_{n+1}-a_{n}-1=a_{n}-a_{n-1}
,
we obtain

(100)

\displaystyle N_{n+1,n}(E^{\prime\prime}_{n+1}\smallsetminus V_{n+1}^{(a_{n+1}-a_{n}-1)})

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{V}_{n+1}^{(a_{n+1}-a_{n}-1)}\smallsetminus\widetilde{V}_{n+1}^{(a_{n+1}-a_{n}-2)})^{1+\gamma^{2^{n}}}

(101)

\displaystyle\subseteq\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(a_{n}-a_{n-1})}\smallsetminus\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(a_{n}-a_{n-1}-1)}=\widetilde{E}^{\prime\prime}_{n}\smallsetminus E^{\prime\prime}_{n},

which is a contradiction.
Hence the above inequality is strict,
which shows our claim.
∎

In
[

, Theorem 4.1]
,
they showed that

-extension of any number field,

As examples which satisfy the assumption of

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
n\geq 1
.
If
m=p
is a prime with
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{2^{n+2}}
,
then
a_{n}-a_{n-1}<2^{n-1}

(and hence

We can assume
n\geq 2

(and
p\equiv 1\pmod{8}
).
From
Proposition

2.8
,
we have

(102)

\eta_{n}^{1+\tau}=c_{n}^{\gamma^{-1}+\gamma^{-2}+\dots+\gamma^{-r_{p}}}=c_{n}^{g(\gamma)},

where
r_{p}
is the integer satisfying

0\leq r_{p}<2^{n+1}
and
p\equiv\pm 3^{r_{p}}\ (2^{n+3})
,
and

(103)

g(X)=X^{2^{n+1}-1}+X^{2^{n+1}-2}+\dots+X^{2^{n+1}-r_{p}}\in\mathbb{Z}[X].

We write

(104)

g(X)=(1+X^{2^{n-1}})h_{1}(X)+h_{0}(X)

with some
h_{0},h_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\deg h_{0}<2^{n-1}
.

Assume
a_{n}-a_{n-1}\geq 2^{n-1}
.
Then, from
Corollary

4.11
,
we can write

v_{n}^{(2^{n-1})}=uc_{n}^{f(\gamma)}\eta_{n}

with some
u\in E^{\prime\prime}_{n-1}
and
f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
with
\deg f<2^{n-1}
.
Hence,

(105)

(v_{n}^{(2^{n-1})})^{1+\tau}=u^{1+\tau}c_{n}^{2f(\gamma)+g(\gamma)}=u^{1+\tau}(-c_{n-1})^{h_{1}(\gamma)}c_{n}^{2f(\gamma)+h_{0}(\gamma)}

is square in
E^{\prime\prime}_{n}
.
Therefore
h_{0}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
must hold
from
Lemma

4.8
.
This yields
g(X)\equiv(1+X)^{2^{n-1}}h_{1}(X)\pmod{2}

and hence

(106)

X^{2^{n+1}-r_{p}}(1+X^{r_{p}})\equiv(1+X)g(X)\equiv(1+X)^{2^{n-1}+1}h_{1}(X)\pmod{2}.

Assume that

is not divisible by

,

be the

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle=(1+X^{r_{p}}+X^{2^{j}}-X^{r_{p}})\bmod 2

∎

5.

In

we explained the outline of our iteration,
without how to determine whether

The following theorem is our fundamental theorem

Theorem 5.1
.

If

\genfrac{(}{)}{}{}{k_{n+1}(\sqrt{p_1},\dots,\sqrt{p_t})/k_n}{\mathfrak{q}_i}\quad(i=1,...,t)

Remark that the equality of (

To prove the theorem, we show the following lemma.

Any conjugation of

and

Hence

∎

2^{(p-1)/4}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
,
then
a_{1}\geq 1
and
v_{1}^{(1)}=\lvert\eta_{1}\rvert
.
Moreover,
if
a_{1}=1
then
N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=1
,
and if
a_{1}\geq 2
then

v_{1}^{(j)}=\sqrt{v_{1}^{(j-1)}}
for
2\leq j<a_{1}
and

(135)

v_{1}^{(a_{1})}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1}-1)}}&(N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=1),\\
\varepsilon c_{1}\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1}-1)}}&(N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=-1).\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
B_{1}^{(0)}=(-1,\varepsilon,c_{1},\eta_{1})
in our iteration,
from
Corollary

5.3
we obtain that

a_{1}\geq 1
if and only if
there exist
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\in\{0,1\}
such that

(136)

v=(-1)^{x_{0}}\varepsilon^{x_{1}}c_{1}^{x_{2}}\eta_{1}

satisfies the conditions of
Corollary

5.3
.
Both

(137)

v^{1+\gamma}=\varepsilon^{2x_{1}}(-1)^{x_{2}}\eta_{1}^{1+\gamma},\quad v^{1+\tau}=(-1)^{x_{1}}c_{1}^{2x_{2}}\eta_{1}^{1+\tau}

are square in
V_{1}^{(0)}

if and only if
2^{(p-1)/4}\equiv(-1)^{x_{2}}\pmod{p}

and
(p-1)/8\equiv x_{1}\pmod{2}

from
Proposition

6.1
.
Under these conditions,
we have

(138)

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{1+\gamma}}^{1+\tau}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon^{x_{1}})^{1+\tau}=(-1)^{x_{1}},

(139)

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{1+\tau}}^{1+\gamma}

\displaystyle=(c_{1}^{x_{2}})^{1+\gamma}=(-1)^{x_{2}}.

Therefore, if
2^{(p-1)/4}\not\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/8}\pmod{p}
,
then
a_{1}=0
must hold
(this result is known in
[
11
]
).
In this case,
N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=1
also holds
from
Proposition

6.3
, since
h_{k^{\prime}}/2
is odd from
Proposition

6.2
.
Also, if
2^{(p-1)/4}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/8}\pmod{p}
,
then
a_{1}\geq 1
and

(140)

v_{1}^{(1)}=v=\begin{cases}\lvert\eta_{1}\rvert&(p\equiv 1\pmod{16}),\\
\lvert\varepsilon c_{1}\eta_{1}\rvert&(p\equiv 9\pmod{16}).\end{cases}

Assume
2^{(p-1)/4}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/8}\pmod{p}
.
From
Proposition

6.3
, we have

\eta_{1}=\pm(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{-h_{k^{\prime}}/4}
,
which yields

(-1)^{(p-1)/8}=\eta_{1}^{1+\tau}=N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{-h_{k^{\prime}}/4}
.
Hence if
p\equiv 9\pmod{16}
, then
a_{1}=1
and
N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=-1
.
Also, if
p\equiv 1\pmod{16}
and
a_{1}=1
, then
N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=1
.

Assume
2^{(p-1)/4}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/8}=1\pmod{p}

and
a_{1}\geq 2
.
Since

\eta_{1}=\pm(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{-h_{k^{\prime}}/4}
,
we must have
v_{1}^{(j)}=(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{-h_{k^{\prime}}/2^{j+1}}

for
1\leq j<a_{1}
.
Therefore we have

(141)

\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1}-1)}}^{1+\gamma}=\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1}-1)}}^{1+\tau}=N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})

since
h_{k^{\prime}}/2^{a_{1}+1}
is odd from
Proposition

6.2
.
Similarly as above,
we can show that
\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1}-1)}}
(resp.
\varepsilon c_{1}\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1}-1)}}
)
satisfies the conditions of
Corollary

5.3

if
N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=1
(resp.
N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=-1
).
∎

As applications of
Theorem

5.1
,
the following interesting properties of
a_{n}

are derived.

Theorem 6.5
.

Assume
p\equiv 1\pmod{16}
and
N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=-1
.
Then we have
a_{1}\geq 2
.
Moreover,

(1)

a_{2}>a_{1}\iff a_{1}\geq 3
when
(-1)^{(p-1)/16}\eta_{1}>0
,

(2)

a_{2}>a_{1}\iff a_{1}=2
when
(-1)^{(p-1)/16}\eta_{1}<0
.

Proof.

We have
a_{1}\geq 2
from
Proposition

6.4
.
Using
Corollaries

5.3
and
4.11
,
we obtain that

a_{2}>a_{1}
if and only if
there exist
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{5}\in\{0,1\}
such that

(142)

v=(-1)^{x_{0}}\varepsilon^{x_{1}}c_{1}^{x_{2}}\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}^{x_{3}}c_{2}^{x_{4}+x_{5}\gamma}\eta_{2}^{1+\gamma}

satisfies the conditions of
Corollary

5.3
.
From
Proposition

6.4
,
we have

v_{1}^{(a_{1})}=\varepsilon c_{1}(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{-h_{k^{\prime}}/2^{a_{1}+1}}
,
which yields
\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}^{1+\gamma}=\pm\varepsilon
and

\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}^{1+\tau}=\pm c_{1}
.
Hence we obtain

(143)

\displaystyle v^{1+\gamma}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2x_{1}}(-1)^{x_{2}}(\pm\varepsilon)^{x_{3}}(-c_{1})^{x_{5}}c_{2}^{x_{4}-x_{5}+(x_{4}+x_{5})\gamma}\eta_{1}\eta_{2}^{2\gamma},

(144)

\displaystyle v^{1+\tau}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{x_{1}}c_{1}^{2x_{2}}(\pm c_{1})^{x_{3}}c_{2}^{2x_{4}+2x_{5}\gamma}

using
Propositions

2.7
and
2.9
,
and they are square in
V_{2}^{(0)}

if and only if
x_{1}=x_{3}=x_{4}=x_{5}=0
and
(-1)^{x_{2}}\eta_{1}>0
.
Under these conditions,
we have

(145)

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{1+\gamma}}^{1+\tau}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\pm\sqrt{\lvert\eta_{1}\rvert}\eta_{2}^{\gamma}\Bigr)^{1+\tau}=((\varepsilon^{\prime})^{-h_{k^{\prime}}/8}\eta_{2}^{\gamma})^{1+\tau}=(-1)^{h_{k^{\prime}}/8+(p-1)/16},

(146)

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{1+\tau}}^{1+\gamma}

\displaystyle=(\pm c_{1}^{x_{2}})^{1+\gamma}=(-1)^{x_{2}}=\sgn\eta_{1}.

Hence
a_{2}>a_{1}
if and only if

(-1)^{2^{a_{1}-2}+(p-1)/16}\eta_{1}>0
.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

Assume
N_{k^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=1
.
If
a_{1}\geq 1
, then
a_{2}>a_{1}
.
Moreover, if
a_{1}\geq 2
and
(-1)^{(p-1)/16}\eta_{1}<0
,
then
a_{2}=a_{1}+1

(hence the
\lambda
-invariant is at most
1

from
Theorem

4.14
).

Proof.

From
Proposition

6.4
and our assumptions, we have

p\equiv 1\pmod{16}
and
(v_{1}^{(a_{1})})^{2^{a_{1}-1}}=\lvert\eta_{1}\rvert
.
Hence we obtain

(147)

\displaystyle\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}^{1+\gamma}

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{(v_{1}^{(a_{1})})^{1+\gamma}}=\pm 1,

(148)

\displaystyle\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}^{1+\tau}

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{(v_{1}^{(a_{1})})^{1+\tau}}=\pm 1.

If both are
1
, then
\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}
is square in
E_{1}

from
Theorem

5.1
, which is a contradiction.
Similarly, if both are
-1
, then
\varepsilon c_{1}\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}
is square in
E_{1}

from
Theorem

5.1
, which is also a contradiction.
Therefore they have different signs,
so we define
t\in\{0,1\}
to satisfy

(149)

\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}^{1+\tau}=-\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}^{1+\gamma}=(-1)^{t}.

Using
Corollaries

5.3
and
4.11
,
we obtain that

a_{2}>a_{1}
if and only if
there exist
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{5}\in\{0,1\}
such that

(150)

v=(-1)^{x_{0}}\varepsilon^{x_{1}}c_{1}^{x_{2}}\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}^{x_{3}}c_{2}^{x_{4}+x_{5}\gamma}\eta_{2}^{1+\gamma}

satisfies the conditions of
Corollary

5.3
.
Both

(151)

\displaystyle v^{1+\gamma}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2x_{1}}(-1)^{x_{2}}(-1)^{(t+1)x_{3}}(-c_{1})^{x_{5}}c_{2}^{x_{4}-x_{5}+(x_{4}+x_{5})\gamma}\eta_{1}\eta_{2}^{2\gamma},

(152)

\displaystyle v^{1+\tau}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{x_{1}}c_{1}^{2x_{2}}(-1)^{tx_{3}}c_{2}^{2x_{4}+2x_{5}\gamma}

are square in
V_{2}^{(0)}
if and only if
they are positive and
x_{4}=x_{5}=0
.
Under these conditions,
we have

(153)

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{1+\gamma}}^{1+\tau}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\pm\varepsilon^{x_{1}}\sqrt{\lvert\eta_{1}\rvert}\eta_{2}^{\gamma}\Bigr)^{1+\tau}=(-1)^{x_{1}+2^{a_{1}-1}t+(p-1)/16},

(154)

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{1+\tau}}^{1+\gamma}

\displaystyle=(\pm c_{1}^{x_{2}})^{1+\gamma}=(-1)^{x_{2}}.

Therefore,

a_{2}>a_{1}
if and only if
the system of linear equations

(155)

\displaystyle x_{2}+(t+1)x_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv s\pmod{2},

(156)

\displaystyle x_{1}+tx_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{2},

(157)

\displaystyle x_{1}+x_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv 2^{a_{1}-1}t+\frac{p-1}{16}\pmod{2}

has a solution
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\{0,1\}^{3}
,
where
\sgn\eta_{1}=(-1)^{s}
.
We can check that this always has a (unique) solution,
hence
a_{2}>a_{1}
holds.

In the case where
a_{1}\geq 2
and
(-1)^{(p-1)/16}\eta_{1}<0
,
the solution
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
of the above equations
must satisfy
x_{3}=1
.
Assume
a_{2}>a_{1}+1
.
Then we can write

(158)

v_{2}^{(2)}=\pm\varepsilon^{y_{1}}c_{1}^{y_{2}}\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}^{y_{3}}c_{2}^{y_{4}+y_{5}\gamma}\eta_{2}

with some
y_{1},\dots,y_{5}\in\{0,1\}
,
using
Corollary

4.11
.
Hence,

(159)

(v_{2}^{(2)})^{1+\gamma}=\pm\varepsilon^{2y_{1}}c_{1}^{y_{5}}c_{2}^{y_{4}-y_{5}+(y_{4}+y_{5})\gamma}\eta_{2}^{1+\gamma}

is square in
E_{2}
.
Also

v_{2}^{(1)}=v=\pm\varepsilon^{x_{1}}c_{1}^{x_{2}}\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}\eta_{2}^{1+\gamma}

is square in
E_{2}
,
hence so is

(160)

v/(v_{2}^{(2)})^{1+\gamma}=\pm\varepsilon^{x_{1}-2y_{1}}c_{1}^{x_{2}-y_{5}}\sqrt{v_{1}^{(a_{1})}}c_{2}^{-y_{4}+y_{5}-(y_{4}+y_{5})\gamma}.

This contradicts
Lemma

4.8
.
Therefore
a_{2}=a_{1}+1
holds.
∎

Although the above proofs are based on our iteration,
unlike
Algorithm

5.8
,

p
is not given specifically.
As in these proofs,
we can narrow down the candidates for
a_{n}

for an abstract
p
to some extent
under the certain conditions.
The following is a recursive algorithm
for executing this computationally.

Algorithm 6.7

(for abstract
p
)
.

Here
p
is not given specifically.
Let
n\geq 1
and let
b
be an integer satisfying
b\equiv 1\pmod{8}
.
Also
e_{ij}\in\{\pm 1\}
for
1\leq i\leq n,\,0\leq j\leq 2^{i-1}-1
are given.
This algorithm computes candidates for
(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

where
p
runs through primes satisfying
p\equiv b\pmod{2^{n+3}}
and

\sgn\eta_{i}^{\gamma^{j}}=e_{ij}
for all
i,j
.

(1)

Initialize
a_{0}\leftarrow 0,\,\dots,\,a_{n}\leftarrow 0
.

(2)

Initialize
G
and
T
using

Propositions

2.7
,
2.8
,
2.10
and
2.12
,
and using
e_{ij}
.

(3)

Let
A
be the
(2^{n+2}+1)\times 2^{n+1}
-matrix
formed by stacking
G
,
T
, and the top row of
(GT-TG)/2
vertically.
Find a non-trivial
\mathbf{v}=(x_{0},\dots,x_{2^{n+1}-1})\in\{0,1\}^{2^{n+1}}

which satisfies
A\mathbf{v}\equiv\mathbf{0}\pmod{2}

and the maximum index
r
with
x_{r}=1
is minimum.
If there is not such
\mathbf{v}
, add
(a_{0},\dots,a_{n})
to the list of candidates and
return to the caller.

(4)

Set
a_{i}\leftarrow a_{i}+1
for all
l\leq i\leq n
,
where
l
is the maximal integer satisfying
2^{l}\leq r
.

(5)

For
(s,s^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}^{2}
, do:

(a)

Transform
G
and
T
by elementary operations as in
Algorithm

5.8
.
Set the
(0,r)
-component of
G
to
s

and the
(0,r)
-component of
T
to
s^{\prime}
.

(b)

Recursively call (
3
) with these
G
,
T
, and
(a_{0},\dots,a_{n})
.

(6)

Return to the caller.

As a note on implementation,
proper use of lazy evaluations can avoid
a brute-force execution of all possibilities of
e_{ij}
.
