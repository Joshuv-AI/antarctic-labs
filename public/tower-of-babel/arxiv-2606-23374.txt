Population-based eigenstates of the SU(d) spin-exchange model for high-spin fermions in optical lattices Preprint: APS/123-QED
Hubert Dunikowski and Emilia Witkowska

Abstract
We investigate the SU ⁡ ( d ) \mathrm{SU}(d) spin exchange model describing ultra-cold fermionic atoms with spin s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 in a one-dimensional optical lattice. The model emerges from the Fermi-Hubbard model in the strongly interacting regime with one atom in each lattice site. The central result of this work is the systematic construction of SE eigenstates in terms of magnetic sub-level populations. This representation provides a natural description of high-spin fermionic systems, where the underlying SU ⁡ ( d ) \mathrm{SU}(d) symmetry gives rise to extensive degeneracies. We illustrate the usefulness of this framework for deriving effective Hamiltonians for a system weakly coupled to light through spin-orbit interactions, using a second-order Schrieffer–Wolff transformation projected onto the introduced population eigenbasis. These effective models provide a controlled description of the collective spin dynamics and capture the role of population redistribution among different collective spin length sectors induced by interactions. The agreement with the exact Fermi–Hubbard with light coupling dynamics confirms the consistency of the population eigenstates framework as a basis for describing high-spin quantum many-body systems.



I
Introduction

The realization of strongly correlated quantum matter with ultracold atoms has opened unprecedented opportunities for the controlled investigation of many-body phenomena ranging from quantum magnetism and superconductivity to nonequilibrium dynamics and quantum information processing
Bloch et al. 2008
;
Gross and Bloch 2017
. Among the most versatile platforms are fermionic alkaline-earth and alkaline-earth-like atoms, whose decoupled electronic and nuclear spin
s
degrees of freedom give rise to highly symmetric interactions that are largely independent of the nuclear-spin projection
Gorshkov et al. 2010
;
Cazalilla and Rey 2014
. As a consequence, these systems naturally realize models with
d=2s+1
magnetic sub-levels, enabling the exploration of many-body physics far beyond the conventional spin-
1/2
paradigm
Taie et al. 2012
;
Scazza et al. 2014
;
Pagano et al. 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2014
. Experimental implementations with isotopes such as
87
Sr and
173
Yb have already demonstrated long-lived quantum magnetism, spin-exchange interactions, and collective coherent dynamics in systems possessing up to ten internal spin components
Cappellini et al. 2014
;
Bromley et al. 2018
.

A particularly interesing regime is the strongly interacting limit of the Fermi–Hubbard model with one atom in each lattice site, where dynamics is governed by spin-exchange processes. For spin-
1/2
fermions, this limit yields the isotropic Heisenberg model
Auerbach 1994
, for which the energy levels have a specific, unique collective spin value.

The situation is fundamentally different for fermions with large (nuclear) spin
s\geq 1
. In this case, because of a simple permutation-like form of the dynamics, the effective Hamiltonian is described by the
\mathrm{SU}(d)
spin exchange (SE) model, whose symmetry and degeneracy structure become increasingly rich with growing
d

Gorshkov et al. 2010
;
Cazalilla and Rey 2014
. In particular, within a given energy sector of the SE model, various values of collective spin are allowed, in contrast to spin-
1/2
systems.

A major obstacle to the theoretical description of these phenomena is the lack of a complete and physically transparent characterization of the eigenstates of the
\mathrm{SU}(d)
SE Hamiltonian. While the symmetry properties of
\mathrm{SU}(d)
magnets have been extensively studied
Affleck 1985
;
Gorshkov et al. 2010
, explicit eigenstate constructions remain limited. In particular, a framework capable of simultaneously exploiting the extensive degeneracies of the SE model and preserving a direct connection to experimentally relevant observables has been missing.

In this work, we provide such a framework by systematically constructing the set of eigenstates of the
\mathrm{SU}(d)
SE Hamiltonian in terms of Population Eigenstates (PES).
Our construction exploits the fact that the SE Hamiltonian commutes with the collective population operators of magnetic sub-levels.
As a result, the natural basis for describing high-spin fermionic systems can be organized according to population configurations (not in terms of collective spin values).
We concentrate on the two classes of eigenstates. The first consists of Ground Population Eigenstates (GPES), which span the degenerate zero-energy manifold and are uniquely labeled by the sets magnetic sub-levels populations. The second consists of states caring momentum
q
, denoted
q
-wave Population Eigenstates (
q
-PES), which form the neighboring energy manifolds of GPES.
q
-PES are generated through collective spin-transfer operators carrying a well-defined quasi-momentum
q
. Additionally, we also construct the collective spin states from the populations eigenstates in order to show what are the allowed collective spin values for given energy sectors.

The utility of the PES framework is illustrated by effective light-induced Hamiltonians for high-spin fermions subject to spin-orbit coupling. Using a Schrieffer–Wolff transformation projected onto the PES manifolds, we obtain correct effective models that systematically account for the manifold degeneracies. The resulting GPES effective Hamiltonian accurately reproduces the dynamics of the full Fermi–Hubbard model with weak light coupling over experimentally relevant timescales. In particular, we were interested in the spin-spin correlations quantified by the spin squeezing parameter as well as the total spin dynamics. We show that the coherent transfer of population between sectors of different collective spin length takes place in time.

Our results reveal that the large degeneracy inherent to
\mathrm{SU}(d)
SE systems qualitatively modifies the generation of quantum spin-spin correlations in terms of spin squeezing. When coupled to light, the system, rather than evolving within a fixed collective-spin manifold, dynamically explores a broad family of spin-length sectors, giving rise to unconventional forms of spin squeezing and entanglement generation. The PES framework therefore provides both a complete eigenstate characterization of considered energy manifolds of the
\mathrm{SU}(d)
SE model and a practical theoretical foundation for understanding of quantum-enhanced spin-spin correlations in high-spin fermionic systems. More broadly, our work establishes a route toward effective descriptions of nonequilibrium dynamics in high-spin systems beyond the conventional spin-1/2 paradigm.

The model

II.1
The Fermi-Hubbard model

In the minimal treatment, the dynamics of
N
fermionic spin-full atoms in an optical lattice is given by the Fermi-Hubbard Hamiltonian
Cazalilla and Rey 2014
. It is derived in the Wannier eigenbasis when atom having spin
s
can be in one of
d=2s+1
internal magnetization states. The state of the single atom can be described by two integers: the number of the site
j
in which atom seats (from 1 to
N
) and magnetization of the atom
m
(from
-s
to
s
). Out of these states the Fock states for the system are constructed.

The Hamiltonian in the Hilbert space composed of these Fock states is constructed by taking the spin-preserving tunneling of atoms among neighbouring sites (
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{t}}
governed by
\mathrm{J}
), and interactions – the spin independent on-site repulsion (
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}
governed by
U
). This gives rise to the Fermi-Hubbard Hamiltonian

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{FH}}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{t}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}},

(1)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{t}}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{J}\sum\limits_{j}\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}(\hat{a}_{j+1,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j,m}+\text{h.c.}),

(2)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}

\displaystyle=\frac{U}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\hat{n}_{j}(\hat{n}_{j}-1)\,,

(3)

where
\hat{a}_{j,m}
(
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j,m}
) is an annihilation (creation) operator for atom in the
j
-th site and the
m
th magnetic sub-level, and where
\hat{n}_{j}
is a particle number operator for a
j
-th site:
\hat{n}_{j}=\sum_{m=-s}^{s}\hat{a}_{j,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j,m}
.

II.2
The Spin Exchange model

In the regime when repulsion strength is larger than tunneling (
U\gg\mathrm{J}
), with the number of atoms in the system
N=\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j}
equal to the number of sites of the lattice, the spectrum of FH Hamiltonian reveals a band structure.
The Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{FH}}
is then dominated by
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}
responsible for emergence of bands spaced by
U
(different bands correspond to states with different multi-atomic occupations of single sites) while
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{t}}
coupling between these bands can be treated as a perturbation.
The lowest-energy manifold consists of states with exactly one atom per lattice site (singlons). Since these states contain no doubly occupied sites, they are unaffected by contact interactions and therefore have zero interaction energy.

In the strongly interacting regime (
U\gg\mathrm{J}
), interband transitions are strongly suppressed, such that a state initially prepared in a given band evolves predominantly within that band.
For the singlon manifold, however, the projected Fermi-Hubbard Hamiltonian is trivial,

\hat{P}(\text{\small sing})\hat{H}_{\mathrm{FH}}\hat{P}(\text{\small sing})=0

The leading nontrivial contribution arises at second order in the tunneling Hamiltonian through virtual excitations to the dublon manifold (vide Fig.
1

\hat{H}_{\text{sing}}^{(\mathrm{t})}=\hat{P}(\text{\small sing})\,\hat{H}_{\mathrm{t}}\,\frac{\hat{P}(\text{\small dub})}{E_{\text{sing}}-E_{\text{dub}}}\,\hat{H}_{\mathrm{t}}\,\hat{P}(\text{\small sing})

(4)

where
(\hat{P}_{\rm sing})
and
(\hat{P}_{\rm dub})

E_{\text{sing}}=0
and
E_{\text{dub}}=U
.

The second order perturbation
\hat{H}_{\text{sing}}^{(\mathrm{t})}
(
4
) yields the Spin Exchange Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\text{SE}}
:

\hat{H}_{\text{SE}}=\underbrace{\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}}_{\mathrm{J_{SE}}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\Big(\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}-\hat{n}_{j,m}\hat{n}_{j+1,m^{\prime}}\Big),

(5)

where

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{j,m}

\displaystyle=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j,m}\hat{a}_{j,m}

(6)

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j}

\displaystyle=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j,m^{\prime}}\hat{a}_{j,m}

(7)

\hat{n}_{j,m}
are the number operator of atoms in the
j
-th lattice site and
m
magnetic quantum number, and the
S
-arrow operator
\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j}
is the spin changing operator between the two magnetic sub-levels from
m
to
m^{\prime}
at site
j
.
The
S
-arrow operators are useful building blocks for spin operators. Their properties are listed in appendix
A
.

The SE model consists of two parts, corresponding to two physical processes. One is an emergent nearest neighbour interaction coming from the process when the atom tunnels to the neighbouring site to feel the on-site repulsion and return back to its site of origin. The other is an emergent exchange of the spins between neighbouring sites (hance the name of the Hamiltonian) coming from a physical exchange of the atoms between neighbouring sites. It is important to notice, that these processes are forbidden when atoms of the neighbouring sites are in the same magnetization state, what comes from the Pauli exclusion principle making tunneling between these sites impossible (this is reflected by the
m\neq m^{\prime}
exclusion in the second summation sign in (
5
)).

The form of the SE Hamiltonian presented in (
5
) is the most natural one with terms directly corresponding to the physical processes behind the effective dynamics. It is however possible to rewrite the SE Hamiltonian in numerous forms seemingly different, yet in fact equivalent to the one presented in (
5
), see Appendix
C
for details.

The on-site spin operators
\hat{J}_{\sigma,j}
, can be expressed in terms of
S
-arrow operators as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-s}^{s}\!m\ \hat{S}_{j}^{m\rightarrow m},

(8)

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{+,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-s}^{s-1}\!\alpha_{s,m}\hat{S}_{j}^{m\rightarrow m+1},

(9)

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{-,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=-s}^{s-1}\!\alpha_{s,m}\ \hat{S}_{j}^{m+1\rightarrow m},

(10)

where

\alpha_{s,m}=\sqrt{(s-m)(s+m+1)},

(11)

and

\hat{J}_{x,j}=(\hat{J}_{+,j}+\hat{J}_{-,j})/2
,
\hat{J}_{y,j}=(\hat{J}_{+,j}-\hat{J}_{-,j})/(2i)
.
The collective components of spin operators are

\hat{J}_{\sigma}=\sum_{j}\hat{J}_{\sigma,j}
.
Therefore, the total spin
\hat{J}^{2}=\hat{J}^{2}_{x}+\hat{J}^{2}_{y}+\hat{J}^{2}_{z}
defines the spin states

\displaystyle\hat{J}^{2}\,|\Lambda,M\rangle

\displaystyle=\Lambda(\Lambda+1)\,|\Lambda,M\rangle,

(12)

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z}\,|\Lambda,M\rangle

\displaystyle=M\,|\Lambda,M\rangle.

(13)

It is worth noticing here that for
s\geq 1
the
S
-arrow operators can not be identified with the spin operators, and hence, the spin states can not be identified with the eigenstates of the collective
S
-arrow operators, as it is true for
s=\frac{1}{2}
.

It is also convenient to define the collective
S
-arrow operators:

\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j}.

(14)

They bring an atom from
m
to
m^{\prime}
magnetization no matter on which sit it seats, so they operate on global populations of atomic magnetization
\hat{n}_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{n}_{j,m}
rather than on a on-site populations.

III
PES: Population Eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{\textrm{S}E}

SE Hamiltonian (
5
) commutes with both collective
S
-arrows and collective spin operators

\displaystyle\big[\hat{H}_{\text{SE}},\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}\big]

\displaystyle=0

(15)

\displaystyle\Big[\hat{H}_{\text{SE}},\hat{\vec{J}}\Big]

\displaystyle=0,

(16)

therefore, its eigenbasis can be written in the form common with
S
-arrow and the collective spin
\hat{J}^{2}
operators (there is some degeneracy).
Since
\hat{H}_{\text{SE}}
commutes with the collective
S
-arrows, it preserves populations of atomic magnetization levels, because such a population is equal to a specific S-arrow
\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}_{j}=\hat{n}_{m}
. Therefore, it is expected that SE Hamiltonian posses eigenstates, that have specific overall populations of atomic magnetic sub-levels
n_{m}
.
We denote the population configuration of an eigenstate by
\vec{n}=(n_{s},n_{s-1},\ldots,n_{-s})
, where
n_{m}
is the population of the magnetic sub-level
m
.
The set of this populations
\vec{n}
provide an additional set of quantum numbers, allowing the eigenstates to be labeled as
|E,\vec{n}\rangle
. We refer to such states as
population eigenstates
(PESs).

In the context of solid state systems PES states would be rather unpractical and hard to encounter, since in experiments for these systems the observed quantity is a global magnetic response of the sample, hence the observed states are rather collective spin states. However in the context of modern cold-atom lattice experiments
Gas-Ferrer et al. 2026
;
Plassmann et al. 2026
, where populations of specific single-atom magnetic sub-levels are directly measured, the PES states comes as a very natural ones.

Although
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
commutes with both the S-arrow and collective-spin operators, the S-arrow operators generally do
not
commute with the collective-spin operators.
The collective S-arrow operators do not generally commute among themselves (
100
). An important exception is provided by the diagonal operators
\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}=n_{m}
, which correspond to magnetic sub-level populations and mutually commute. Consequently, one may construct a common eigenbasis of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
and the population operators
\hat{n}_{m}
(PES base), or a common eigenbasis of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
and the total spin operators (
|\Lambda,M\rangle
base).
However, no basis can simultaneously diagonalize
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
, all
\hat{n}_{m}
and collective spin operators. As a result, PESs are generally not eigenstates of the collective spin.

Although PESs are generally not eigenstates of the collective spin, they remain eigenstates of the collective magnetization operator
\hat{J}_{z}
:
\hat{J}_{z}\,|E,\vec{n}\rangle=\big(\sum_{m=-s}^{s}m\,n_{m}\big)\,|E,\vec{n}\rangle
.
The corresponding eigenvalue is determined solely by the populations of the magnetic sub-levels.

III.1
Ground population eigenstates (GPESs)

The SE manifold of zero energy
E=0
can be spanned by
Ground Population Eigenstates (GPES)
.
1
1

1

These states are ground states for
\mathrm{J_{SE}}<0
and actually the highest energy states for
\mathrm{J_{SE}}>0
.

All GPESs have zero energy,
E=0
, and are uniquely specified by the set of all collective populations of magnetic sub-levels
\vec{n}=(n_{s},n_{s-1},\ldots,n_{-s})
; that is, no two distinct GPESs share the same population configuration. We therefore omit the energy label and denote these states simply by
|\vec{n}\rangle
:

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}\,|\vec{n}\rangle=0\,|\vec{n}\rangle

(17)

here
n_{m}=\sum_{j}n_{j,m}
, where note that we have
n_{j,m}=0,1
.

The simplest GPES are
the sea states

|\mu\rangle
, the ones that have all atoms occupying the same magnetization sublevel
m=\mu
:

|\mu\rangle\equiv\Big|\vec{n}\!=\!(0,\ldots,\underbrace{N}_{n_{\mu}},\ldots,0)\Big\rangle=\bigotimes\limits_{j=1}^{N}|\mu\rangle_{j}.

(18)

Here,
|\mu\rangle_{j}
denotes the state of the atom at site
j
in the magnetic sub-level
m=\mu
. These states are eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
with
E=0
. Indeed, because all atoms occupy the same spin state, Pauli exclusion forbids spin-exchange processes, causing every term in (
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
) to annihilate (
|\mu\rangle
).

As the spin-exchange Hamiltonian commutes with the collective
S
-arrow operators,
\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}
(
15
), all GPESs can be generated from an arbitrary sea state
|\mu\rangle
through successive applications of the appropriate
S
-arrows operators,

|\vec{n}\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{\vec{n},\mu}^{-1/2}\prod_{m\in\vec{m}_{\mu}}\left(\hat{S}^{\mu\rightarrow m}\right)^{n_{m}}|\mu\rangle\,,

(19)

where
\vec{m}_{\mu}
denotes the set of magnetic sub-levels excluding
\mu
:

\vec{m}_{\mu}=(s,\ldots,{\mu+1},{\mu-1},\ldots,{-s}).

(20)

The normalization factor is

\mathcal{N}_{\vec{n},\mu}=\binom{N}{n_{-s},\ldots,n_{s}}\prod_{m\in\vec{m}_{\mu}}\big(n_{m}!\big)^{2},

(21)

which when using the multinomial coefficient,

\binom{N}{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{p}}=\frac{N!}{n_{1}!\ n_{2}!\ldots n_{p}!\ \Big(N-\sum_{i=1}^{p}n_{i}\Big)!}\,,

(22)

simplifies to

\mathcal{N}_{\vec{n},\mu}=N!\ n_{-s}!\ldots n_{\mu-1}!\ n_{\mu-1}!\ldots n_{s}!/n_{\mu}!.

(23)

It is particularly convenient to define GPES with respect to sea of maximally polarized state
|s\rangle
:

|\vec{n},s\rangle\equiv|\vec{n}\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{\vec{n}}^{-1/2}\prod_{m=-s}^{s-1}\left(\hat{S}^{s\rightarrow m}\right)^{n_{m}}|s\rangle,

(24)

with

\mathcal{N}_{\vec{n}}=\binom{N}{n_{-s},\ldots,n_{s-1}}\prod_{m=-s}^{s-1}\big(n_{m}!\big)^{2}.

(25)

Throughout the following, we adopt the convention that the sea index
\mu
is set to
s
whenever it is not explicitly specified; thus, the maximally polarized sea
\mu=s
serves as the default reference state.

The PES (
19
) obey orthogonality relation,

\langle\vec{n},\mu|\vec{n}^{\prime},\mu\rangle=\delta_{n_{s},n_{s}^{\prime}}\ldots\delta_{n_{-s},n_{-s}^{\prime}}\equiv\delta_{\vec{n},\vec{n}^{\prime}}.

(26)

The populations satisfy the constraint
\sum_{m}n_{m}=N
, corresponding to the fixed total number of atoms.

The total number of GPES is:

\#\{\text{GPES}\}=\overline{C}^{N}_{d}=\frac{(N+2s)!}{N!\,(2s)!}\,.

(27)

It is the
N
-element combination with repetitions of
d=2s+1
-element set: the number of ways in which one can choose magnetizations for
N
atoms from
d=2s+1
-element set of possible magnetizations.

III.2
q
-wave population eigenstates (
q
-PES)

We define the
q
-wave population eigenstate
(
q
-PES) as a PES characterized by a quasi-momentum
q
. Such states are obtained by acting on GPESs with the
q
-resolved S-arrow operator, which we will refer to as
q
-S-arrow
operator:

\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{q}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{\mathrm{i}q\lambda j}\,\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j}.

(28)

Here,
q
denotes the quasi-momentum and
\lambda
the lattice spacing between neighboring sites.
Under periodic boundary conditions (PBC), the allowed quasi-momenta are given by:

q=2\pi\frac{k}{N}\lambda^{-1},\quad k\in\{1,2,\ldots,N-1\}

(29)

The
q
-PES
|q,m_{q};\vec{n}\rangle
, is a population eigenstate in which the atoms transferred to the magnetic sub-level
m_{q}
carry quasi-momentum
q
, while the remaining populations are described by the vector
\vec{n}^{\prime}
. It is generated from a GPES by the action of the corresponding q-S-arrow operator in the following way:

|q,m_{q};\vec{n}^{\prime}\rangle=(\mathcal{Q}_{n_{m_{q}}}^{n_{m^{\prime}}})^{-1/2}\,\hat{S}^{{m^{\prime}}\rightarrow m_{q}}_{q}\,|\vec{n}\rangle.

(30)

The q-S-arrow operator acts on a PES analogously to a collective S-arrow operator: it transfers one atom from the magnetic sub-level
{m^{\prime}}
to the target sub-level
m_{q}
, thereby increasing
n_{m_{q}}
by one and decreasing
n_{m^{\prime}}
by one. Consequently, the population vector transforms as

\vec{n}^{\prime}=(n_{s},\ldots,n_{m^{\prime}}-1,\ldots,n_{m_{q}}\!+1,\ldots,n_{-s}).

(31)

The normalization factor associated with the action of the q-S-arrow operator is

\mathcal{Q}_{n_{m_{q}}}^{n_{m^{\prime}}}=\frac{n_{m^{\prime}}(N-n_{m_{q}}\!-1)}{N-1}.

(32)

The
q
-PES with a given
\vec{n}^{\prime}
can be also defined without involving relative GPES, by the direct generation from the sea:

|q,m_{q};\vec{n}^{\prime}\rangle=\mathcal{N}_{q,\vec{n}^{\prime}\!,\mu}^{-1/2}\!\prod_{m\in\vec{m}_{\mu}}\!\!\left(\hat{S}^{\mu\rightarrow m}\right)^{n_{m}^{\prime}\!-\delta_{m,m_{q}}}\hat{S}^{\mu\rightarrow m_{q}}_{q}\ |\mu\rangle,

(33)

where the normalization factor reads

\mathcal{N}_{q,\vec{n}^{\prime}\!,\mu}=\frac{N-n_{m_{q}}^{\prime}}{n_{m_{q}}^{\prime}(N-1)}\ \binom{N}{n_{-s}^{\prime},\ldots,n_{s}^{\prime}}\prod_{m\in\vec{m}_{\mu}}\big(n_{m}^{\prime}!\big)^{2}.

(34)

The q-PES defined by Eq. (
33
) is equivalent to the one introduced in Eq. (
30
). For a fixed choice of sea
\mu
, all q-PESs can be constructed except those with
m_{q}=\mu
, which require choosing a different reference sea.

There is one limitation for possible population configurations
\vec{n}^{\prime}
for
q
-PESsthey cannot correspond to a sea state, i.e., a configuration in which all atoms occupy a single magnetic sub-level, (
\vec{n}^{\prime}\neq(0,\ldots,N,\ldots,0)
).In this case, the phase factors entering the q-S-arrow construction lead to complete cancellation of all contributions, and the resulting state vanishes.

An energy of the
q
-PES is dependent only on the quasi-momentum
q
:

E_{q}=2\,\mathrm{J}_{\mathrm{SE}}\Big(\cos q\lambda-1\Big).

(35)

The
q
-PES are orthogonal in populations
\vec{n}
and in quasi-momenta
q
,

\langle q,m_{q};\vec{n}|q^{\prime}\!,m_{q};\vec{n}^{\prime}\rangle=\delta_{q,q^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\vec{n},\vec{n}^{\prime}}.

(36)

However,
q
-PESs corresponding to different magnetic sub-levels
m_{q}
carrying the quasi-momentum
q
are not, in general, orthogonal:

\langle q,m_{q};\vec{n}|q,m_{q}^{\prime};\vec{n}\rangle=\\[3.0pt]
=-\sqrt{\frac{n_{m_{q}}n_{m_{q}^{\prime}}}{(N-n_{m_{q}})(N-n_{m_{q}^{\prime}})}}(1-\delta_{m_{q},m_{q}^{\prime}})+\delta_{m_{q},m_{q}^{\prime}}.

(37)

The non-orthogonality of
q
-PESs implies that they do not constitute an orthogonal basis within the subspace of states with fixed
q
and
\vec{n}
. In fact, the set of
q
-PESs sharing the same
q
and
\vec{n}
, but differing in the magnetic sub-level
m_{q}
, forms an overcomplete representation of the subspace that it spans:

0=\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}\sqrt{n_{m}(N-n_{m})}\ \Big|q,m_{q}{=}m,\vec{n}\Big\rangle

(38)

Namely, every
q
-PES from this set can be expressed as a superposition of all other
q
-PES from the set:

\Big|q,m_{q}{=}\tilde{m},\vec{n}\Big\rangle=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{n_{\tilde{m}}(N-n_{\tilde{m}})}}\cdot\\
\cdot\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}(1-\delta_{m,\tilde{m}})\sqrt{n_{m}(N-n_{m})}\ \Big|q,m_{q}{=}m,\vec{n}\Big\rangle.

(39)

This demonstrates that removing any one state from this set is sufficient to obtain a complete basis. In the following, we choose to exclude the state with
m_{q}=\mu
, which is the most convenient choice for our construction.

Once the completeness criterion for
q
-PES is established, one can count the number of linearly independent
q
-PES for given
q
:

\#\{\text{q-PES}\}=\sum\limits_{k=2}^{d}\,\overline{C}^{N-k}_{k}\,C_{d}^{k}\,(k-1)=\\[3.0pt]
=d!\,(N-1)!\sum\limits_{k=2}^{d}\frac{1}{(k-2)!\,k!\,(N-k)!\,(d-k)!},

(40)

with
d=2s+1
.
For a given
\vec{n}
the number of linearly independent possible
q
-PES is equal the number of levels with non-zero populations
k
minus one for completeness (see the note under (
39
)):
k=\sum_{m=-s}^{s}\sgn n_{m}
. Values of
k
are integers from
2
to
d
(for
q
-PES at least two levels are occupied). Then, for given
k
there is
C_{d}^{k}
number of ways to choose
k
levels that are occupied out of total
d
number of levels. For a specific choice of occupied levels there are
\overline{C}^{N-k}_{k}
number of ways of distributing
N
atoms over
k
levels provided that each of
k
levels is occupied by at least one atom (where
\overline{C}
stands for combination with repetitions).

IV
Spin Eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}

All the PES states are the eigenstates of global
\hat{J}_{z}
with magnetization
M=\sum_{m=-s}^{s}m\,n_{m}
:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z}|\vec{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}m\,n_{m}\ \right)|\vec{n}\rangle

(41)

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z}|q,m_{q};\vec{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}m\,n_{m}\ \right)|q,m_{q};\vec{n}\rangle.

(42)

In general, PESs are not eigenstates of the collective-spin operator
\hat{J}^{2}
.
For GPESs, one finds:

\hat{J}_{+}|\vec{n}\rangle=\sum_{m=-s}^{s-1}\!\alpha_{s,m}\sqrt{n_{m}(n_{m+1}+1)}\ |\vec{n}_{m}\rangle.

(43)

Here,
\vec{n}_{m}
denotes the population vector obtained from
\vec{n}
by transferring one atom from the magnetic sub-level
m
to
m+1
,

\vec{n}_{m}=\big(n_{s},\ldots,n_{m+1}+1,n_{m}-1,\ldots,n_{-s}\big).

(44)

For q-PESs, the action of the collective spin raising operator gives

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{+}\big|q,m_{q};\vec{n}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\!\sum_{m=-s}^{s-1}\!F_{m}^{m_{q},\vec{n}}\,\big|q,m_{q};\vec{n}_{m}\big\rangle\ +

\displaystyle+\,f^{m_{q},\vec{n}}\,\big|q,m_{q}+1;\vec{n}_{m_{q}}\big\rangle

(45)

where the corresponding coefficients are:

\displaystyle F_{m}^{m_{q},\vec{n}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s,m}\sqrt{n_{m}(n_{m+1}+1)}\ \,\frac{n_{m}-\delta_{m_{q},m}}{n_{m}}\cdot

\displaystyle\phantom{=\ }\cdot\sqrt{\frac{n_{m_{q}}(N-n_{m_{q}}-\delta_{m_{q}-1,m}+\delta_{m_{q},m})}{(N-n_{m_{q}})(n_{m_{q}}+\delta_{m_{q}-1,m}-\delta_{m_{q},m})}},

(46)

\displaystyle f^{m_{q},\vec{n}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s,m_{q}}\sqrt{\frac{N-n_{m_{q}+1}-1}{N-n_{m_{q}}}}.

(47)

Since
\hat{J}^{2}=\hat{J}_{z}^{2}+\hat{J}_{z}+\hat{J}_{-}\hat{J}_{+}
, Eqs. (
43
) and (
45
) immediately imply that PESs are, in general, not eigenstates of the collective-spin operator
\hat{J}^{2}
. Indeed, the action of
\hat{J}_{+}
on a single PES produces a superposition of multiple PESs rather than a single state. This result is consistent with the more general observation discussed above: the collective S-arrow operators, including the population operators

\hat{n}
, do not commute with the collective-spin operators, preventing PESs from being simultaneous eigenstates of both sets of observables.

IV.1
General construction of total spin eigenstates

We denote simultaneous eigenstates of

\hat{H}_{\rm SE}
, total spin
\hat{J}^{2}
and
\hat{J}_{z}
by
|E,\Lambda,M\rangle
, where
\Lambda
and
M
are the collective-spin and collective-magnetization quantum numbers, respectively. These states satisfy

\hat{J}^{2}\,|\Lambda,M\rangle=\Lambda(\Lambda+1)\,|\Lambda,M\rangle.

(48)

For a given energy manifold (
E=0
or
E_{q}
) the spin state can be expressed in therms of PES states of energy
E
and such population sets
\vec{n}
which gives magnetization
M
:

|E,\Lambda,M\rangle=\sum\limits_{\vec{n}\in\{\vec{n}\}(M)}\!\sum\limits_{f}a_{\vec{n},f}\,|E,\vec{n},f\rangle,

(49)

where
f
is a superindex taking into account any further possible PES degeneration (for example for
q
-PES it could be
m_{q}
) and
\{\vec{n}\}(M)
is a set of all collective populations of magnetic sub-levels
\vec{n}
that give collective magnetization equal to
M
, namely
M=\sum_{m=-s}^{s}m\,n_{m}
.

Starting from any state
|E,\Lambda,M\rangle
, the remaining members of the same collective-spin multiplet can be generated through successive applications of the ladder operators
\hat{J}_{\pm}
. Consequently, it is sufficient to determine a single state for each multiplet. For our purposes, it is particularly convenient to consider the highest-weight state
|E,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle
, which has the maximal magnetization
M=\Lambda
. Such a state satisfies

\hat{J}_{+}|E,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle=0

(50)

and therefore provides a natural starting point for constructing the entire multiplet.

We refer to such a highest-weight state as the parent state of the multiplet. Expanding
|E,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle
in the PES basis according to Eq. (
49
), applying
\hat{J}_{+}
via Eq. (
43
), and collecting terms corresponding to the same
\vec{n}
and
f
we obtain

\hat{J}_{+}\,|E,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle=\sum\limits_{\vec{n}}\sum\limits_{f}C_{\vec{n},f}\,|E,\vec{n},f\rangle=0.

(51)

Because the chosen PES basis is linearly independent (a nontrivial requirement in the case of q-PESs), the highest-weight condition
\hat{J}_{+}\,|E,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle=0

implies

C_{\vec{n},f}=0,\quad\text{where}\quad\vec{n}\in\{\vec{n}\}(M{=}\Lambda{+}1)\,.

(52)

The coefficients
C_{\vec{n},f}
are linear combinations of the expansion coefficients
a_{\vec{n},f}
appearing in Eq. (
49
). Consequently, the above conditions define a system of linear equations for
a_{\vec{n},f}
, whose solutions determine the parent state
|E,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle
in the PES representation. Different choices of independent solutions generally lead to different families of collective-spin eigenstates.

To address this point, let us examine the structure of the parent state
|E,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle
in its PES decomposition as in Eq. (
49
). The population vectors
\vec{n}
entering the expansion are constrained only by two conditions: conservation of magnetization,
\sum_{m}m\,n_{m}=\Lambda
, and conservation of particle number,
\sum_{m}n_{m}=N
.
A natural question then arises: does the construction of the parent state
|E,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle
require PESs corresponding to all population configurations
\vec{n}
satisfying these constraints, or only a subset thereof?

The answer follows from the structure of the raising operator
\hat{J}_{+}
, crucial for calculation. Since
\hat{J}_{+}
is composed of operators
S^{m\to m+1}
), its action can only transfer population from lower to higher magnetic sub-levels. Consequently, if a PES
|E,\vec{n}_{\zeta},f\rangle
occupies only the
\zeta
highest magnetic sub-levels,

\vec{n}_{\zeta}=(\underbrace{n_{s},\ldots,n_{s-\zeta+1}}_{\zeta},0,\ldots,0),

(53)

the action of
\hat{J}_{+}
cannot populate any of the remaining sub-levels. Therefore, the subspace spanned by such states is closed under
\hat{J}_{+}
.
follows that, irrespective of the total number of magnetic sub-levels
d
, parent states may be constructed using only the
\zeta
highest sublevels, with
\zeta\leq d
. This naturally partitions the parent states into families labeled by
\zeta=1,2,\ldots,d
which we refer to as the parent-component number. The corresponding parent states take the form

|E,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta}=\sum\limits_{\vec{n}_{\zeta}\in\{\vec{n}\}(\Lambda)}\sum\limits_{f}a_{\vec{n}_{\zeta},f}\,|E,\vec{n}_{\zeta},f\rangle

(54)

Each value of
\zeta
defines a distinct family of collective-spin eigenstates characterized by the energy
E
, spin length
\Lambda
, and parent-component number
\zeta
.

In principle, up to
d
distinct families of states can be constructed, corresponding to the possible values of
\zeta=1,\ldots,d
. However, the existence of a family for a given
\zeta
is not guaranteed. Moreover, for a fixed
\zeta
, only specific values of the collective spin
\Lambda
may be allowed. For example, as shown below, the
\zeta=1
family contains only the Dicke states with
\Lambda=sN
.

The solution of the constraint equations (
52
) is determined primarily by the value of
\zeta
and is largely independent of the single-atom spin
s
. Therefore, the construction of collective-spin eigenstate families will be performed separately for each parent-component number
\zeta
and for each PES energy sector considered in this work (GPESs or
q
-PESs).

It is important to note that families corresponding to different values of
\zeta
are orthogonal. This follows from the orthogonality of their parent states: states with different
\zeta
are constructed from PESs belonging to distinct, non-overlapping population sectors, and PESs with different population vectors are orthogonal.

IV.2
Total spin eigenstates for
E=0

Let us first consider the construction of ground spin eigenstates, i.e., states composed of GPESs. Since all such states have zero energy,
E=0
, we omit the energy index in the notation, as for GPESs. Moreover, for a given population vector
\vec{n}
there is a unique GPES, so no additional index
f
is required. Therefore, the general parent state in the
E=0
sector takes the form:

|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta}=\sum\limits_{\vec{n}_{\zeta}\in\{\vec{n}\}(\Lambda)}a_{\vec{n}_{\zeta}}\,|\vec{n}_{\zeta}\rangle

(55)

\zeta=1
case:

The
\zeta=1
is a trivial case when the parent state is just a single GPES:

|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=1}=|\vec{n}_{\zeta=1}\rangle,\,\,{\rm with}\,\,\vec{n}_{\zeta=1}=(N,0,\cdots,0),

(56)

Since there is no degree of freedom left for
\zeta=1
there is only one possible value of
\Lambda
which is the maximal spin length of the system
\Lambda=sN
.

The family of states generated from
|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=1}=|\text{max}\rangle
parent state is called Dicke manifold:

|\Lambda,M\rangle_{\zeta=1}=\sqrt{\frac{(\Lambda+M)!}{(\Lambda-M)!(2\Lambda)!}}\hat{J}_{-}^{\Lambda-M}|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=1}.

(57)

The Dicke family exists for any elementary spin
s
.

It is also important to notice that there are only two specific GPES that are spin states alone. These are two sea states
|s\rangle=|\vec{n}{=}(N,0,\ldots,0)\rangle
and
|{-}s\rangle=|\vec{n}{=}(0,\ldots,0,N)\rangle
, that are respectively the maximally (
M=sN
) and the minimally (
M={-}sN
) polarized states of the system, hence in the context of spin states they are denoted as
|\text{max}\rangle
and
|\text{min}\rangle
respectively. The later is the parent for Dicke family of states
|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=1}=|s\rangle=|\text{max}\rangle

the former could also play a role of parent state of Dicke family if we were defining it from the minimal, not the maximal, magnetization
|\Lambda,{-}\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=1}=|{-}s\rangle=|\text{min}\rangle

\zeta=2
case:

It turns out that for
\zeta=2
no
E=0
energy spin state can be constructed for any
\Lambda
.

This is because for
\zeta=2
there is always only one GPES with the required magnetization
\Lambda
. Namely:

|\Lambda{=}sN{-}k,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=2}=a_{\vec{n}_{\zeta=2}}\Big|\vec{n}_{\zeta=2}{=}\big(N{-}k,k,0,\ldots,0\big)\Big\rangle

(58)

That only GPES is not a spin state on its own (despite
k=0
case, that is just the Dicke parent state, so
\zeta=1
family actually), what can be easily checked. This means, that the only way to satisfy
\hat{J}_{+}|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=2}=0
is setting
a_{\vec{n}_{\zeta=2}}=0
, what means, that
|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=2}
does not exist, and hence there are no families of spin states for
\zeta=2
parent population.

\zeta=3
case:

First non-trivial parent component number that allows for the constriction of some families is
\zeta=3
, when the three top magnetic sub-levels are populated,

\vec{n}_{\zeta=3}=(n_{s},n_{s-1},n_{s-2},0,\cdots,0)
.

Since every atom populating
n_{s-2}
lowers global magnetization by
2
with respect to the maximal
sN
, the consideration of
|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=3}
states is held differently for
\Lambda
lower than
sN
by even and odd number:

\displaystyle|\Lambda

\displaystyle\!=\!sN\!-\!2k,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=3}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k}a_{\vec{n}(p)}\,\big|\vec{n}(p)\!=\!(n_{s},2(k\!-\!p),p,0,\ldots)\big\rangle

(59)

\displaystyle|\Lambda

\displaystyle\!=\!sN\!-\!2k\!+\!1,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=3}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}a_{\vec{n}(p)}\,\big|\vec{n}(p)\!=\!(n_{s},2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!1,p,0,\ldots)\big\rangle

(60)

Where
k=1,2,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor
and
n_{s}
is determined from total particle number being fixed
n_{s}=N-n_{s-1}-n_{s-2}
: in (
59
) it is
n_{s}=N-2k+p
and in (
60
) it is
n_{s}=N-2k+p+1
.

Let us focus on the case with
\Lambda=sN-2k
, since later it will turn out that it is the only physically relevant case.

Acting on the (
59
) with
J_{+}
one obtains:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{+}

\displaystyle\,|\Lambda\!=\!sN\!-\!2k,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=3}=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}a_{\vec{n}(p)}\,\alpha_{s,s-1}\sqrt{2(k\!-\!p)(N\!-\!2k\!+\!p\!+\!1)}\,\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\cdot\big|\vec{n}\!=\!(n_{s}\!+\!1,2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!1,p,0,\ldots)\big\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{k}a_{\vec{n}(p)}\,\alpha_{s,s-2}\sqrt{p\big(2(k\!-\!p)\!+\!1\big)}\,\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\cdot\big|\vec{n}\!=\!(n_{s},2(k\!-\!p)\!+\!1,p\!-\!1,0,\ldots)\big\rangle

(61)

where
\alpha
is defined in (
11
). Gathering together terms with the same GPES we obtain:

\hat{J}_{+}\,|\Lambda\!=\!sN\!-\!2k,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=3}=\\
=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}C_{p}\,\big|\vec{n}\!=\!(n_{s},2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!1,p,0,\ldots)\big\rangle

(62)

with:

C_{p}=a_{\vec{n}(p)}\,\alpha_{s,s-1}\sqrt{2(k\!-\!p)(N\!-\!2k\!+\!p\!+\!1)}\ +\\
+\,a_{\vec{n}(p+1)}\,\alpha_{s,s-2}\sqrt{(p+1)\big(2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!1\big)}

(63)

Since
|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle
has to be annihilated by
\hat{J}_{+}
, all
C_{p}
have to be equal to zero, what gives a set of equations for
a_{\vec{n}}
coefficients in the form of recursion relation:

a_{\vec{n}(p)}=-\frac{\alpha_{s,s-2}}{\alpha_{s,s-1}}\sqrt{\frac{(p+1)\big(2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!1\big)}{2(k\!-\!p)(N\!-\!2k\!+\!p\!+\!1)}}\ a_{\vec{n}(p+1)}

(64)

and overall magnitude of
a_{\vec{n}}
are set by normalization of
|\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle
.

It remains to check if case described by eq. (
60
) gives some solution. The procedure is analogues to (
59
) case. First we act on (
60
) with
\hat{J}_{+}
and demand that it gives zero:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{+}

\displaystyle\,|\Lambda\!=\!sN\!-\!2k\!+\!1,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=3}=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}a_{\vec{n}(p)}\,\alpha_{s,s-1}\sqrt{[2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!1](N\!-\!2k\!+\!p\!+\!2)}\,\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\cdot\big|\vec{n}\!=\!(n_{s}\!+\!1,2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!2,p,0,\ldots)\big\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{k-1}a_{\vec{n}(p)}\,\alpha_{s,s-2}\sqrt{p\big(2(k\!-\!p)\big)}\,\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\cdot\big|\vec{n}\!=\!(n_{s},2(k\!-\!p),p\!-\!1,0,\ldots)\big\rangle

(65)

After gathering together terms with the same GPES:

\hat{J}_{+}\,|\Lambda\!=\!sN\!-\!2k\!+\!1,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=3}=\\
=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}C_{p}\,\big|\vec{n}\!=\!(n_{s}\!+\!1,2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!2,p,0,\ldots)\big\rangle

(66)

where:

\displaystyle C_{p}

\displaystyle=a_{\vec{n}(p)}\,\alpha_{s,s-1}\sqrt{[2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!1](N\!-\!2k\!+\!p\!+\!2)}\ +

\displaystyle\hskip 25.0pt+\,a_{\vec{n}(p+1)}\,\alpha_{s,s-2}\sqrt{(p+1)\big(2(k\!-\!p)\!-\!2\big)}

(67)

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0pt\text{for}\qquad p=0,1,\ldots,k\!-\!2

\displaystyle C_{k-1}

\displaystyle=a_{\vec{n}(k-1)}\,\alpha_{s,s-2}\sqrt{N-k+1}

(68)

The
\hat{J}_{+}|\Lambda\!=\!sN\!-\!2k\!+\!1,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=3}=0
condition is met only when all
C_{p}
coefficients are zero. In particular, setting
C_{k-1}=0
gives from (
68
), that
a_{\vec{n}(k-1)}
hast to be identically zero. If
a_{\vec{n}(k-1)}=0
then from (
67
) it follows that all other
a_{\vec{n}(p)}
coefficients have to be zero, so the
|\Lambda\!=\!sN\!-\!2k\!+\!1,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=3}
state does not exist.

Concluding the
\zeta=3
class states for
E=0
, it turns out that families of states exist only for
\Lambda=sN-2k
(with
k=1,2,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor
) while for all
\Lambda=sN-2k+1
the collective spin states do not exist in this
\zeta
class.

\zeta=4
case:

Authors also considered the case
\zeta=4
. Although the construction becomes significantly more computationally demanding, it remains feasible and yields collective-spin families with
\Lambda=sN-3k
and
\Lambda=sN-3k-2
, where
k
is a non-negative integer. No families are found for
\Lambda=sN-3k-1
. The resulting expressions for the coefficients

are, however, cumbersome and do not provide additional physical insight. Therefore, we do not present these explicit forms and restrict the discussion to the observation that the construction of such families is possible for the corresponding allowed values of
\Lambda
.

IV.3
Generalized Spin Wave states

Construction of spin states in the
E_{q}
manifold is also done according to section
IV.1
, heaving in mind that compared to the simple case of
E=0
manifold this time
m_{q}
plays the role of superindex
f
and also that one value of
m_{q}
has to be excluded to provide linear independence of
q
-PES involved (in our case we choose
m_{q}\neq s
):

|q,\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta}=\sum\limits_{\vec{n}_{\zeta}\in\{\vec{n}\}(\Lambda)}\ \sum\limits_{m_{q}=-s}^{s-1}a_{\vec{n}_{\zeta}}^{m_{q}}\,|q,m_{q},\vec{n}_{\zeta}\rangle

(69)

\zeta=1
case

does not exist, since there are not any
q
-PES states with only one magnetic sub-level populated (no sea
q
-PES).

\zeta=2
case:

Analogously to the
E=0
manifold, for the required magnetization
\Lambda
there is only one
\vec{n}_{\zeta=2}
, and since only two magnetic sub-levels are occupied, there is only one possible value of
m_{q}
, namely
m_{q}=s-1
:

|q,\Lambda{=}sN{-}k,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=2}\overset{?}{=}\\[3.0pt]
\overset{?}{=}\Big|q,m_{q}{=}s{-}1,\vec{n}_{\zeta=2}{=}\big(N{-}k,k,0,\ldots,0\big)\Big\rangle

(70)

However, this time for
k=1
(
\Lambda=sN{-}1
) case, such a
q
-PES is a spin state (what can be easily checked by acting with
\hat{J}_{+}
on it):

|q,\Lambda{=}sN{-}1,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=2}=\\[3.0pt]
=\Big|q,m_{q}{=}s{-}1,\vec{n}{=}\big(N{-}1,1,0,\ldots,0\big)\Big\rangle

(71)

The
|q,\Lambda{=}sN{-}1,\Lambda\rangle_{\zeta=2}
state is a parent state for a family of states called
Spin-Wave States
(SWS), which are known as eigenstates of the isotropic XXX Heisenberg model.

There are only two linearly independent
q
-PESs that are collective-spin eigenstates by themselves:
|q,\Lambda{=}sN{-}1,\Lambda\rangle=|q,m_{q}{=}s{-}1;\vec{n}{=}(N{-}1,1,0,\ldots,0)\rangle

|q,\Lambda{=}sN{-}1,{-}\Lambda\rangle=|q,m_{q}{=}{-}s;\vec{n}{=}(0,\ldots,0,1,N{-}1)\rangle
. Both states belong to the same SWS family. The first one is the parent state introduced above, constructed from the maximal-magnetization sector. The second can be interpreted as an alternative parent state if the family is defined starting from the minimal rather than maximal magnetization state.

For spin-
1/2
particles, the SWS family contains all possible spin eigenstates in the
E_{q}
sector, as
\zeta=2=d
.

\zeta=3

Two possible classes of
\Lambda

\Lambda=sN-2k
, other is
\Lambda=sN-2k-1
, where

\displaystyle\Big|q,

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{p=1}^{k}b_{\vec{n}_{\mathrm{even}}(p)}^{m_{q}=s-2}\Big|q,m_{q}{=}s{-}2,\vec{n}_{\mathrm{even}}(p)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle b_{\vec{n}_{\mathrm{even}}(p)}^{m_{q}=s-1}

where in equation (

and

IV.4
Spin eigenstate families: summary

The existence of spin eigenstates of the SE Hamiltonian has been demonstrated explicitly for several representative cases. A general construction procedure is provided in Sec.
IV.1
; however, since each pair of parameters
\zeta
and
\Lambda
requires a separate analysis, no universal closed-form solution can be obtained.

Moreover, even in the cases where the construction is possible, the resulting spin eigenstates expressed in the PES basis (and therefore in the Fock basis) have a highly involved structure, which limits their practical use in calculations. Their construction should therefore be viewed primarily as a proof of principle, confirming that the commutation relation between
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
and
\hat{\vec{J}}
allows the existence of common eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
,
\hat{J}^{2}
, and
\hat{J}_{z}
.

There is, however, an important lesson from finding spin eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
. Namely, the knowledge of possible values of collective spin
\Lambda
in specific energy manifolds.

For the
E=0
manifold and elementary spin
s=1/2
, the only collective-spin eigenstates belong to the Dicke manifold with the maximal spin value
\Lambda=sN
. In contrast, already for
s=1
, multiple families of spin eigenstates appear, with
\Lambda
ranging from the maximal value
\Lambda=sN
through values reduced by even integers,
\Lambda=sN-2k
, down to the lowest allowed values.

A similar behavior occurs in the
E_{q}
manifold. For
s=1/2
, the SWS family exhausts all spin eigenstates with energy
E_{q}
, corresponding to
\Lambda=sN-1
. For
s\geq 1
, however, the same energy manifold contains spin eigenstates with a broad range of collective-spin values, extending from
\Lambda=sN-1
to substantially lowest values
\Lambda=0
.

These differences have profound consequences for the spin dynamics generated by the SE Hamiltonian. In the
s=1/2
case, fixing the energy manifold also fixes the collective spin:
E=0
corresponds uniquely to the fully polarized state with
\Lambda=sN
, while
E_{q}
corresponds to
\Lambda=sN-1
. For
s\geq 1
, the same energy manifolds contain states with many different collective-spin lengths, fundamentally modifying spin dynamics.

This enhanced spin degeneracy for
s\geq 1
originates from the symmetry of the SE Hamiltonian in the space of atomic magnetic sub-levels. The Hamiltonian possesses an
\mathrm{SU}(d)
symmetry, reflecting its invariance under arbitrary transformations mixing the
d=2s+1
magnetic sub-levels. In contrast, the isotropic Heisenberg Hamiltonian possesses only global
\mathrm{SU}(2)
symmetry, corresponding to invariance under collective rotations of the spin degrees of freedom.

For
s=1/2
, the two symmetries coincide because
d=2
, and the spin-exchange Hamiltonian becomes equivalent to the Heisenberg model. This equivalence is broken for all
s\geq 1
, leading to qualitatively different many-body dynamics and spin properties.

V
Coupling to light

We concluded that the practical usage of spin eigenstates of SE Hamiltonian is rather limited. We would like to confirm the correctness of the PES framework by calculation of dynamics employing
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
, and hopefully presenting the influence of atomic spin length
s
on the collective spin dynamics.

In particular, we are interested in the spin squeezing dynamics in the system.
Spin-squeezed states are many-body states where fluctuations of a collective-spin component are reduced below the quantum noise level, with the reduction occurring at the expense of increased fluctuations in the conjugate component.
The dynamics starts with a coherent spin state which is obtained by applying a collective rotation to the maximally polarized state
|\mathrm{max}\rangle
,

|\vartheta,\varphi\rangle=e^{-\mathrm{i}\hat{J}_{z}\varphi}e^{-\mathrm{i}\hat{J}_{y}\vartheta}|\mathrm{max}\rangle.

(80)

Squeezing level is measured by the spin squeezing parameter
Wineland et al. 1992
;
Wineland et al. 1994
:

\xi^{2}=2sN\,\Delta J_{\perp,\mathrm{min}}^{2}/\langle\hat{J}\rangle^{2}.

(81)

It quantifies the minimal transverse collective-spin uncertainty,
\Delta J_{\perp,\mathrm{min}}
, relative to the mean collective spin length. Spin squeezing occurs when this parameter falls below unity, indicating fluctuations reduced below the coherent-spin-state limit.

It was shown in Ref.
Hernández Yanes et al. 2022
that for spin-
1/2
fermions in an optical lattice weakly coupled to light, via the spin-orbit interaction
\hat{V}{\mathrm{soc}}
, can generate scalable spin squeezing by effectively realizing the One-Axis Twisting (OAT) model. The latter is described by the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{OAT}}=\chi\hat{J}_{\sigma}^{2}
, which transforms an initial spin coherent state into a spin-squeezed state. In Ref.
Burba et al. 2024
, we showed how various couplings between the nuclear spin of alkaline-earth atoms and light can be engineered, including the spin-orbit coupling.

Here, we consider spin-orbit coupling in the form
Hernández Yanes et al. 2022
:

\hat{V}_{\text{soc}}=\frac{\Omega\hbar}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\bigg(e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda dj}\,S_{j,+}+e^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda dj}\,S_{j,-}\bigg).

(82)

It can be represented in terms of the
q
-S-arrow operators:

\hat{V}_{\text{soc}}=\frac{\hbar\Omega}{2}\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s-1}\alpha_{s,m}\Big(S_{q}^{m\rightarrow m+1}+S_{-q}^{m+1\rightarrow m}\Big).

(83)

This representation is particularly useful, as it explicitly shows that
\hat{V}_{\mathrm{soc}}
directly couples the GPES and
q
-PES sectors. Therefore, the PES framework provides a natural basis for performing perturbative calculations with
\hat{V}_{\mathrm{soc}}
as the perturbation.

The second-order Schrieffer–Wolff expression
Bravyi et al. 2011
for the effective Hamiltonian within the
E=0
manifold generated by
\hat{V}_{\mathrm{soc}}
is

\hat{H}_{\rm GPES}^{(\mathrm{soc})}=\hat{P}(\text{\small GPES})\,\hat{V}_{\text{soc}}\sum_{q\neq 0}\frac{\hat{P}(\text{\small$q$-PES})}{-E_{q}}\,\hat{V}_{\text{soc}}\,\hat{P}(\text{\small GPES}),

(84)

where
\hat{P}(\mathrm{GPES})
projects onto the GPES subspace and
\hat{P}(q\text{-PES})
onto the subspace of
q
-PESs with quasi-momentum
q
.

As shown in Eq. (
30
), the
q
-
S
-arrow operators generate
q
-PESs from GPESs according to

|q,m_{q};\vec{n}^{\prime}\rangle=(\mathcal{Q}_{n_{m_{q}}}^{n_{\mu}})^{-1/2}\,\hat{S}^{\mu\rightarrow m_{q}}_{q}\,|\vec{n}\rangle,

(85)

while the inverse process, mapping a
q
-PES back to the GPES manifold, is obtained through

(\mathcal{Q}_{n_{m_{q}}}^{n_{\mu}})^{-1/2}\,\hat{S}^{m_{q}\rightarrow\mu}_{-q}\,|q,m_{q};\vec{n}^{\prime}\rangle=|\vec{n}\rangle

(86)

The detailed derivation of the effective model generated by the light coupling is presented in Appendix
D
.

When
\phi\neq\pi
the effective model is

\hat{H}^{({\mathrm{soc}},\phi)}_{\rm GPES}=\chi_{\phi}\Bigg(\hat{J}^{2}-\hat{J}_{z}^{2}-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{m=-s}^{s-1}\alpha_{s,m}^{2}(\hat{n}_{m}+\hat{n}_{m+1})\Bigg),

(87)

with

\chi_{\phi}=\frac{\hbar^{2}\Omega^{2}}{4\mathrm{J_{SE}}(\cos\phi-1)(N-1)}.

(88)

The population-dependent contribution (the last term) breaks the conservation of the total collective spin. For
s=\frac{1}{2}

N

Hernández Yanes et al. 2022

VI
Summary and Conclusions

In this work, we have investigated the
\mathrm{SU}(d)
spin-exchange model describing strongly interacting ultra-cold fermions with large nuclear spin (
s\geq 1
) in optical lattices. Our primary result is the systematic construction of the complete eigenstate structure of the spin-exchange Hamiltonian in terms of PES within a two-energy manifold. Exploiting the conservation of the collective population operators, we identified the GPES spanning the degenerate zero-energy manifold and the
q
-PES forming the neighboring energy quasi-momentum manifolds. This construction provides a natural basis for the description of high-spin fermionic systems, directly connected to experimentally accessible spin-population observables.

The derived PES framework reveals qualitative differences between spin-
1/2
and higher-spin fermionic ultra-cold atoms weakly coupled to light. Whereas the zero energy dynamics of spin-
1/2
models remain confined to the maximal-spin Dicke manifold, the extensive degeneracies associated with the underlying
\mathrm{SU}(d)
symmetry allow systems with
s\geq 1
to dynamically explore multiple collective-spin sectors, since for given energy
E
a broad range of spin length values
\Lambda
is available. As a consequence, the collective spin length is generally not conserved, and descriptions restricted to a fixed-spin manifold fail to capture essential aspects of the many-body dynamics.

To demonstrate the usefulness of the PES basis, we derived effective light-induced Hamiltonians by performing a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation within the PES manifolds. The resulting effective models accurately reproduce the dynamics of the exact Fermi-Hubbard Hamiltonian over the parameter regimes considered in this work, including the coherent redistribution of population among sectors with different collective-spin length. In particular, they capture the mechanisms responsible for the breakdown of the conventional spin-1/2 description and the associated modifications of spin-squeezing dynamics.

Our results show that the large manifold degeneracies characteristic of
\mathrm{SU}(d)
spin-exchange systems fundamentally alter the generation of collective quantum correlations. Beyond establishing a complete eigenstate characterization of the spin-exchange model, the PES framework provides a practical route for deriving effective theories and analyzing nonequilibrium dynamics in high-spin fermionic systems. We believe that the framework proposed by us would be efficient for further exploration of quantum states and metrological protocols that rely on dynamics beyond fixed-spin manifolds, including forms of multipartite entanglement and squeezing.

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum_{j}\sum_{m=-s}^{s}\Big(\hat{a}_{j,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j+1,m}\hat{a}_{j+1,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j,m}+

\displaystyle\hskip 60.0pt+\hat{a}_{j+1,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j,m}\hat{a}_{j,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j+1,m}\Big)

(109)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{off-d}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum_{j}\sum_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\Big(\hat{a}_{j,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j+1,m}a_{j+1,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j,m^{\prime}}+

\displaystyle\hskip 60.0pt+\hat{a}_{j+1,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j,m}\hat{a}_{j,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j+1,m^{\prime}}\Big)

.

terms commutation of creation and annihilation operators of the same site and magnetization leads to emergence of additional identity operator

and grouped operators reproduce particle number operators

=\hat{n}_{j,m}(-\hat{n}_{j+1,m}+\hat{\mathbb{I}})+\hat{n}_{j+1,m}\Big(-\hat{n}_{j,m}+\hat{\mathbb{I}}\Big)

(111)

where RL and LR terms were merged by changing dummy indices. Since for singlons we have
1=\sum_{m,=-s}^{s}n_{j,m,}
for any
j
, we can further rewrite
m=m^{\prime}
terms using:

-\hat{n}_{j,m}+\hat{\mathbb{I}}=\Bigg(\sum\limits_{m^{\prime}=-s}^{m-1}+\!\sum\limits_{m^{\prime}=m+1}^{s}\Bigg)\hat{n}_{j,m^{\prime}}

(112)

Applying (
111
) modified according to (
112
) to (
109
) and changing dummy indices to merge terms of RL and LR origin, we obtain
\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}
in the form:

\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}=-\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\hat{n}_{j,m}\hat{n}_{j+1,m^{\prime}}

(113)

We see that
\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}
has a form of nearest neighbour interactions independent of magnetization, which, however do not occur if magnetizations are equal (what comes from Pauli exclusion).

For
m\neq m^{\prime}
terms, commuting creation-annihilation operators gives only several cancelling minus signs leading to:

\hat{a}_{j,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j+1,m}\hat{a}_{j+1,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j,m^{\prime}}+\hat{a}_{j+1,m}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j,m}\hat{a}_{j,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j+1,m^{\prime}}=\\[5.0pt]
=-\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}-\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}

(114)

Plugging (
114
) in to (
110
) and changing dummy indices to merge together terms of RL and LR origin, we obtain the
\hat{H}_{\text{off-d}}
:

\hat{H}_{\text{off-d}}=\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}

(115)

We see that
\hat{H}_{\text{off-d}}
is responsible for nearest neighbor spin exchange effective processes with strength independent of magnetization, , however do not occur if magnetizations are equal (what comes from Pauli exclusion).

An effective model obtained in this way is called the Spin Exchange effective Hamiltonian:

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{sing}}^{(\mathrm{t})}=\mathrm{J_{SE}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\Big(\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}-\hat{n}_{j,m}\hat{n}_{j+1,m^{\prime}}\Big)

(116)

denoted later by
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
(with
\mathrm{J_{SE}}=\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}
).

Appendix C
Alternative forms of SE Hamiltonian

The default for this work form of Spin Exchange Hamiltonian is given by (
5
). Here we present several equivalent forms of
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
that are used in literature.

Since
\hat{n}_{j,m}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j,m}\hat{a}_{j,m}=\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}_{j}
:

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}=\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\Big(-\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}+\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}\Big).

(117)

This form could be useful if one would like to work with the algebra of S-arrow operators (instead of creation-annihilation).

One can notice that:

\displaystyle\Big[-\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}+\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}\Big]_{m^{\prime}=m}

\displaystyle=-\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}_{j+1}+\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}_{j+1}=0

(118)

As such a term is identically zero, we can add it to
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}
in this way, getting rid of the exclusion
m\neq m^{\prime}
in the summation:

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}=\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M^{\prime}}\ \sum\limits_{m,m^{\prime}=-s}^{s}\!\!\!\Big(\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}\!{-}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}\Big)

(119)

or after restoring particle number operators in notation:

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SE}}=\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M^{\prime}}\ \sum\limits_{m,m^{\prime}=-s}^{s}\!\!\Big(\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}{-}\hat{n}_{j,m}\hat{n}_{j+1,m^{\prime}}\Big)

(120)

In the literature, one can also see another common way of expressing the spin exchange Hamiltonian, which looks simpler, however, turns out to be less practical for our application. It is obtained if (
111
) is left as it is, without applying (
112
). Then
\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}
reads:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j}\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}\Big(\hat{n}_{j,m}\big(-\hat{n}_{j+1,m}+\hat{\mathbb{I}}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt+\hat{n}_{j+1,m}\big(-\hat{n}_{j,m}+\hat{\mathbb{I}}\big)\Big)=

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j}\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}\bigg(2\,\hat{n}_{j,m}\hat{n}_{j+1,m}-\hat{n}_{j,m}-\hat{n}_{j+1,m}\bigg)

(121)

For singlons states (with one atom in each lattice site) we have:

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}n_{j,m}=N

(122)

since
\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}n_{j,m}=1
. What leads to
\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}
:

\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}=\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j}\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}\hat{n}_{j,m}\hat{n}_{j+1,m}-\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\,N

(123)

or with the use of S-arrow operators:

\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}=\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j}\sum\limits_{m=-s}^{s}\hat{S}_{j}^{m\rightarrow m}\hat{S}_{j+1}^{m\rightarrow m}-\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\,N

(124)

Then the total SE Hamiltonian reads:

\hat{H}_{\text{SE}}=\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\Bigg[\sum\limits_{j}\,\sum\limits_{m,m^{\prime}=-s}^{s}\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}-N\Bigg]

(125)

where terms in
\hat{H}_{\text{diag}}
and
\hat{H}_{\text{off-d}}
were complementary to restore summation over whole ranges of
m
and
m^{\prime}
. The global change of energy offset by
+\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}N
can be applied simplifying the SE Hamiltonian further:

\hat{H}_{\text{SE}}=\frac{2\mathrm{J}^{2}}{U}\sum\limits_{j}\,\sum\limits_{m,m^{\prime}=-s}^{s}\hat{S}^{m^{\prime}\rightarrow m}_{j}\hat{S}^{m\rightarrow m^{\prime}}_{j+1}

(126)

The (
126
) form of SE Hamiltonian, although compact, is not practical for calculations – for example, for states with all atoms occupying the same magnetic sub-level, the (
5
) clearly gives zero, which is a direct consequence of all dynamics being frozen by Pauli exclusion. Drawing the same conclusion from (
126
) form of the SE Hamiltonian is not so trivial.

The form favoured by us of SE Hamiltonian (
5
) also nicely exposes the physics of underlying physical processes that can happen between nearest neighbors giving a good intuition for the investigation of the relevant physics of the model.
