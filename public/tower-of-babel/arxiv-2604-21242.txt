Two New Extensions of Reider’s Theorem on Algebraic Surfaces
Aaron Bertram

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Walls and chambers for the moduli of semi-stable objects in the derived category of a complex projective manifold have been used to prove
“classical” theorems about the Brill-Noether theory of curves in surfaces
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
21
]

and about the degree and genus of curves in projective three-space
[
26
]
. Here we use them
to prove two extensions of Reider’s theorem on the positivity of adjoint linear series on a surface. We begin by recalling
Reider’s Theorem
[
29
]
.

Let
S
be a smooth complex projective surface.

Reider.
Let
D
be an ample (not necessarily effective) divisor on
S
. If

D^{2}>4\ \mbox{and}\ D\cdot C>C^{2}+1

for all curves
C\subset S
satisfying
C^{2}\leq 0
, then
|K_{S}+D|
is base point free, and if

D^{2}>9\ \mbox{and}\ D\cdot C>C^{2}+2

for all
C\subset S
with
C^{2}\leq 0
, then
|K_{S}+D|
is very ample.

One extension due to Beltrametti, Francia and Sommese
[
13
]
is the following. If

D^{2}>(k+1)^{2}\ \mbox{and}\ D\cdot C>C^{2}+k

for all
C\subset S
with
C^{2}\leq 0
, then
\mbox{H}^{1}(S,\omega_{S}(D)\otimes I_{Z})=0
for
Z\subset S
of length
\leq k
.
This is a natural generalization of Reider’s Theorem to the separation of
k
-jets.

Our first theorem is about the nef cone of the blow-up of
|K_{S}+D|^{\vee}
along
S
.

Theorem 7.1.
In the context of Reider’s Theorem, suppose that

D^{2}>9\ \mbox{and}\ D\cdot C>C^{2}+2\ \mbox{when}\ C^{2}\leq 0

so that
S\subset|K_{S}+D|^{\vee}
and let

Y:=\mbox{bl}_{S}(|K_{S}+D|^{\vee})
with exceptional divisor
E
.

If in addition:

(
a_{1}
)

then

is nef on
Y
.

(

)
D^{2}>16
and
D\cdot C>C^{2}+3
when

then
3H-E
is ample.

(
b_{1}
)
D^{2}\geq 25
and
D\cdot C>C^{2}+3
when
C^{2}=-1,0
, then
2H-E
is nef.

Note that
2H-E
cannot be ample on

due to the presence of secant lines. However, if

(
b_{2}
)
D^{2}>25
and
D\cdot C>C^{2}+4
when
C^{2}=-1,0
, then
|2H-E|
contracts:

(Y,\Sigma_{1}(S))\rightarrow(Y_{0},S^{[2]})

where
\Sigma_{1}(S)
is the bundle of secant lines contracting onto the Hilbert scheme.

Remark. The necessity of the additional inequality in (
b_{2}
) is seen with the Veronese:

{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{5}\ \mbox{associated to}\ D=5l

in which

on

is nef, but the secant variety is “defective.” Instead, in this case the contraction is a
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
-bundle over the dual projective plane

({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{\vee}
.

For motivation, consider the case of a projective curve
C
.

Riemann-Roch.
If
\deg(D)>k
, then
|K_{C}+D|
separates
k
-jets.

This has extensions to the
equations
cutting out
C
, starting with:

Castelnuovo.
If
\deg(D)>2
, the embedding of
C
is projectively normal.

Moreover, the homogeneous ideal
I_{C}
is generated by quadrics and cubics.

Saint-Donat.
If
\deg(D)>3
, then
I_{C}
is generated by quadrics.

Via the implications for a smooth subvariety
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and
Y=\mbox{bl}_{X}({\mathbb{P}}^{n})

I_{X}\ \mbox{is generated by homogeneous polynomials of degree $\leq d$}

\Downarrow

X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\ \mbox{is scheme-theoretically cut out by polynomials of degree $d$}

\Downarrow

\ \mbox{the divisor}\ dH-E\ \mbox{on the blow-up}\ Y:=\mbox{bl}_{X}({\mathbb{P}}^{n})\ \mbox{is nef}

one can view Theorem 7.1 as a weaker version of the curve results, suggesting that the ideal

I_{S}
might be generated in low degree when the conditions on
D^{2}
and
D\cdot C
are met.
What is missing is an understanding of the homogeneous rings:

\bigoplus\mbox{H}^{0}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(n(dH-E)))

and in particular whether or not they are generated in degree one.

As with Reider’s Theorem, a curve
C
for which
D\cdot C
violates the inequality in our theorem is directly
responsible for the
failure of
dH-E
to be nef (or ample). More interestingly,
if there are no such curves but
dH-E
still is not nef (or ample) then the failure is due to a special vector bundle
on
S
that comes into play with the failure of the inequality on
D^{2}
. On some surfaces, including

{\mathbb{P}}^{2},{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
,
K3
and abelian surfaces, there is detailed information about the (non)-existence of special vector bundles,
which is consistent with sharper results.

The proof follows a strategy outlined in Bayer-Macrì
[
11
]
, in which the Bridgeland semi-stability of families of
objects over a base (in this case
Y
) translates into the nefness of a
determinant
divisor class on
Y
.
We begin by constructing a Serre family
C_{|K_{S}+D|^{\vee}}
of objects
of the derived category
D^{b}(S)
parametrized by
|K_{S}+D|^{\vee}
which we pull back to the blow-up

Y
where an elementary modification refines it to the
Drinfeld family

B_{Y}
that we use to implement the strategy.
This is the higher dimensional analog of a construction of Drinfeld of a family
of rank two vector bundles on a curve
[
14
]
.
The bulk of the work is then to find a chamber in the stability manifold of
S
where the objects of
B_{Y}
are
Bridgeland semi-stable.

Our second extension of Reider’s Theorem concerns the Hilbert schemes
S^{[d]}

of ideal sheaves of colength
d
and may be seen as a generalization of the ampleness of
K_{S}+D
when

D^{2}>9
and
D\cdot C>C^{2}+2
for all curves
C
with
C^{2}<1
.

Theorem 7.2.
For all surfaces
S
, the divisor classes

(K_{S}+D)^{[d]}-E

are ample when

D
is ample,
D^{2}>9d
and
C\cdot D>C^{2}+2d
for all curves with
C^{2}<d
,
where
(K_{S}+D)^{[d]}
is the pull-back of the symmetrized divisor via the Hilbert-Chow morphism and

E\subset S^{[d]}
is the exceptional divisor of non-reduced schemes.

This, too, can be seen as the surface analogue of a well-known result for curves.

Curves.
The Hilbert scheme is the symmetric product
C_{d}
and divisor classes:

(K_{C}+D)_{d}-E

are ample when
\deg(D)>2d
. Indeed, in this case they are of the form:

(\deg(D)-2d)x_{d}+2\theta

where
\theta
is the pull-back via the Abel-Jacobi map of the (ample!) theta divisor on the
Jacobian
[
2
]
and
x_{d}
is a “unit” symmetrized divisor. This shows
the bound is sharp. Ampleness when
\deg(D)=2d
is contingent on the Abel-Jacobi map
being an embedding (or at least finite) which is in turn contingent on
C
not being
d
-gonal.

Although there is no analogue of the Jacobian for
0
-cycles on a general surface by a theorem of Mumford
[
28
]
, something similar
seems to be happening relative to Theorem 7.2. If
f:S\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is a finite map of degree
d
, then:

D=f^{*}(-K_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}})\ \mbox{satisfies}\ D^{2}=9d

and
(K_{S}+D)^{[d]}-E
is not ample. As in the curve case, it is trivial when restricted to the embedded
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\subset S^{[d]}
.
This is an interesting connection between the ampleness of such “boundary” divisors and the degree of irrationality of the surface (see
[
6
]
).

There are many computations in the literature of the nef cones of Hilbert schemes for special surfaces
[
5
,
30
,
15
,
9
,
15
]
,
as well as some more recent, much more intricate computations of the larger movable cones
[
1
,
18
,
22
]
.
The most general results on the nef cones of Hilbert schemes we are aware of are in
[
20
]
, which also employs the Bayer-Macrì strategy.
To our knowledge, the results in this paper are the first to apply to all surfaces and to condition ampleness on
Reider-type inequalities.

3.
Stability Conditions on a Surface

The Drinfeld family
W_{Y}
“refines” the rational map defined by
V_{|K_{C}+D|^{\vee}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ |K_{C}+D|^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{C}(2,-D)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

to the coarse moduli space of semi-stable rank two bundles
V
with
c_{1}(V)=-D
. There is one stability of
vector bundles on a curve
C
, based on the slope:

\mu(V)=\frac{\deg(V)}{\mbox{rk}(V)}

and a vector bundle
V
is semi-stable if

\mu(F)\leq\mu(V)
for all subbundles
F\subset V
.
When
X=S
, however, there are
families
of (Bridgeland) stability conditions.

We choose an ample polarization
H
on
S
, which we normalize so that
H^{2}=1
. When

D
is an ample
integral
divisor, we achieve this by setting

H=D/||D||
and
then there are two
H
-slope analogues of the slope of vector bundle on a curve.

Mumford Slope
is defined on the
torsion-free
sheaves
F
:

\mu_{H}(F)=\frac{c_{1}(F)\cdot H}{\mbox{rk}(F)}

Gieseker/Simpson Slope
is defined on
pure dimension one
torsion sheaves
\tau
:

\nu_{H}(\tau)=\frac{\mbox{ch}_{2}(\tau)}{c_{1}(\tau)\cdot H}

To hold on to more information, one uses instead the central charges:

Z_{\mu}(F)=(-c_{1}(F)\cdot H,\mbox{rk}(F)),\ \ Z_{\nu}(\tau)=(-\mbox{ch}_{2}(\tau),c_{1}(\tau)\cdot H)

underlying the Mumford and Gieseker slopes.
These are linear on the
K
-group and non-zero for non-zero sheaves
F
(and
\tau
), with

0<\arg(Z({\mathcal{E}}))<1
, where
\arg(re^{\pi i\theta}):=\theta

for sheaves
{\mathcal{E}}=F
or
\tau
.

Since arg
(Z)
imposes the same ordering as the slope, one makes the following
definition, enlarging the categories to include sheaves with
\arg(Z({\mathcal{E}}))=1
.

Definition.
An arbitrary torsion sheaf
\tau
on
S
is
H
-semistable if:

\arg(Z_{\nu}(\tau))\leq\arg(Z_{\nu}(\tau^{\prime}))\ \mbox{for all quotients}\ \tau\rightarrow\tau^{\prime}

A sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
with no torsion in dimension zero is
H
-semistable if:

\arg(Z_{\mu}(\mathcal{F}))\leq\arg(Z_{\mu}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))\ \mbox{for all quotients}\ \mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\prime}

and
H
-stable if the inequality is always strict and strictly
H
-semistable otherwise.

Note. A subsheaf of a coherent sheaf with no torsion in dimension zero also has no torsion in dimension zero, but to get a quotient
with the same property, one needs to saturate the subsheaf. This is the reason we switched to quotients.

The good properties of these slopes include:

Schur’s Lemma.
Stable sheaves are simple, i.e. End
(E)={\mathbb{C}}\cdot\mbox{id}_{E}
, and

\mbox{Hom}(E_{1},E_{2})=0\ \mbox{if $E_{1},E_{2}$ are semi-stable and}\ \arg(Z(E_{1}))>\arg(Z(E_{2}))

Finiteness of Filtrations.

(a) Semi-stables have finite Jordan-Holder filtrations with stable sub-quotients.

Note. In order to define this as an increasing filtration, one saturates the inclusions.

(b) Arbitrary sheaves (of each given type) have unique finite Harder-Narasimhan filtrations with semi-stable
sub-quotients with strictly decreasing slopes.

Projective Moduli.
Stability is an open condition on flat families, semi-stability is also open with a replacement property and
projective coarse moduli
spaces

{\mathcal{M}}_{S}(r,c_{1},d)\ \mbox{and}\ {\mathcal{M}}_{S}(0,D,d)

exist for each set of Chern character invariants of torsion-free/torsion sheaves.

Bogomolov Inequality.
Mumford semi-stable torsion-free sheaves satisfy

c_{1}(F)^{2}\geq 2\cdot\mbox{ch}_{2}(F)\cdot\mbox{rk}(F)

from which we get the
H
-Bogomolov inequality
, via the Hodge index thoerem:

(c_{1}(F)\cdot H)^{2}\geq 2\cdot\mbox{ch}_{2}(F)\cdot\mbox{rk}(F)

with equality if and only if the first is an equality and
c_{1}(F)=\lambda H
for some
\lambda
.

There are various ways to introduce geometric Bridgeland stability conditions on a polarized surface
as deformations of either of the “classical” stability conditions. We choose here to deform the Gieseker slope, i.e.
the associated central charge:

Z_{\nu}(\tau)=(-\mbox{ch}_{2}(\tau),\ c_{1}(\tau)\cdot H)

by introducing the rank of an
arbitrary
object
E
of
D^{b}(S)
via:

Z_{(x,y)}(E):=(-\mbox{ch}_{2}(E)+x\cdot\mbox{rk}(E),\ c_{1}(E)\cdot H+y\cdot\mbox{rk}(E))

or, equivalently, as a family of slopes:

\nu_{(x,y)}(E)=\frac{\mbox{ch}_{2}(E)-x\cdot\mbox{rk}(E)}{c_{1}(E)\cdot H+y\cdot\mbox{rk}(E)}

indexed by points of the
(x,y)
-plane.

A preliminary observation of these slopes gives:

(i) the slopes of sheaves
\tau
of pure dimension one do not vary with
(x,y)
.

(ii)
\arg(Z_{(x,y)}({\mathbb{C}}_{p}))=1
is maximal for all skyscraper sheaves
{\mathbb{C}}_{p}
and all

x_{0}<n
.
This is an upper bound for the slope of a wall. The lower bound is more subtle.

Preliminaries.
A
witness
for a wall
W
through
I({\mathcal{I}}_{Z})=(-n,0)
is a sequence:

0\rightarrow F\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}\rightarrow E\rightarrow 0

of semi-stable objects of the same
(x,y)
-slope, so that
W
passes through
I(F)
and, together with
\arg(z)=0
bounds a sector in which
all ideal sheaves
{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}
are stable.
We conclude as before that:

\bullet

F
is a torsion-free coherent sheaf with
c_{1}(F)\cdot H<0
.

\bullet

I(F)
is between
(-n,0)
and the parabola.

\bullet
if
r=\mbox{rk}(F)>1
, then
I(E)
is on the opposite side of the parabola and

-\mu(E)=\frac{c_{1}(E)\cdot H}{r-1}\ \mbox{and}\ -\mu(F)=\frac{c_{1}(E)\cdot H}{r}

so that in this case the
ratio
of the
y
-coordinates of
I(E)
and
I(F)
is
r/(r-1)
.

Proposition 5.1.
If the witness
F\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}
for a wall
W
has rank
r>1
, then

\frac{2}{3\sqrt{n}}\ \mbox{
is a lower bound for the slope of $W$}

Proof.
Let
y_{1},y_{2}
be the
y
-coordinates of
W
intersect the parabola. Then

-\mu(E)\leq y_{1}<y_{2}\leq-\mu(F)

and so their ratio satisfies

{y_{2}}/{y_{1}}\leq{r}/{(r-1)}
.
On the other hand, from:

y^{2}-\frac{2}{m}y+2n=0

we conclude that

y_{1}y_{2}=2n
and
y_{1}+y_{2}=\frac{2}{m}

which gives us:

\frac{2}{m}\leq\sqrt{2n}\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{r}{r-1}}+\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{r}}\right)

and a lower bound for
m
.
When
r=2
, this is the lowest lower bound, namely

m\geq\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}

Example.
When
n=1
and
S={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, the wall for
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}
is witnessed by:

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(-1)^{2}\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{p}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(-2)[1]\rightarrow 0

and indeed, this realizes the lower bound for higher rank walls, as

I(F)=\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)\ \mbox{and}\ I(E)=\left(2,2\right)\ \mbox{and}\ m=\frac{2}{3}

Next, we turn to the rank one walls for
S^{[n]}
. In general, a wall is witnessed by:

{\mathcal{I}}_{W}(-C)\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}

for a nonzero effective curve
C
through a subset of the points of
Z
.

But if there is such a wall then there is a wall of lower slope witnessed by:

{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-C)\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{Z^{\prime}}

for
Z^{\prime}\subset C
. So although witnesses of the first type do
exist, for the purpose of finding the
first
wall it suffices to consider only the walls of the latter type.

Proposition 5.2.
Suppose
H=D/||D|
for an integral divisor
D
such that:

D\cdot C>C^{2}+2n\ \mbox{for all}\ C\ \mbox{satisfying}\ -2n<C^{2}<2n

Then the slopes of all rank one walls are bounded below by
2/||D||
.

Proof.
The condition for a wall with witness
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-C)
is that:

I({\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-C))=\left(\frac{C^{2}}{2},C\cdot H\right)\ \mbox{be between $(-n,0)$ and the parabola}

The slope of the wall is then:

\frac{C\cdot H}{\frac{C^{2}}{2}+n}=\frac{2C\cdot H}{C^{2}+2n}=\frac{2C\cdot D}{||D||(C^{2}+2n)}>\frac{2}{||D||}

when
C\cdot D>C^{2}+2n
. This only needs to be checked
for
-2n<C^{2}<2n
since
(n,\sqrt{2n})
is the point of tangency of the line through
(-n,0)
and the parabola.

Putting the two Propositions together, we obtain:

Corollary 5.3.
When
D
is an ample divisor on
S
satisfying:

D\cdot D>9n\ \mbox{and}\ C\cdot D>C^{2}+2n\ \mbox{for all}\ C^{2}<n

then each
{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}
is stable up to and including the wall through
I({\mathcal{I}}_{Z})
with slope
2/||D||
.

Proof.
Except for the fact that it is sufficient to check
C^{2}<n
instead of
C^{2}<2n
, this is an immediate consequence of the two
Propositions.

But let

C^{2}=\alpha n\ \mbox{for}\ 0<\alpha<2
and suppose

D\cdot C\leq C^{2}+2n=(\alpha+2)n=\left(1+\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)C^{2}

Then it follows from the Hodge Index Theorem that when
1\leq\alpha<2
,

D^{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)D\cdot C\leq\left(1+\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)^{2}C^{2}=\frac{(\alpha+2)^{2}}{\alpha}n\leq 9n

So
D^{2}>9n
implies we need only check
D\cdot C>C^{2}+2n
for
\alpha<1
, i.e.
C^{2}<n
. ∎

We will need in §6 to know when each
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}
is stable along a hypothetical wall:

W(a)=\left(y=\frac{2}{||D||}x-\frac{2}{||D||}a\right)\cap\left(x>\frac{1}{2}y^{2}\right)\ \mbox{for}\ -2\leq a<-1

for the Drinfeld family.
Again, we can separate into higher rank and rank one.

(Higher Rank) It suffices to know that the hypothetical wall satisfies:

\frac{2}{||D||}\cdot\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{||D||}a\leq 1

to know it passes below the open line segment joining
(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
(2,2)
. This gives:

D^{2}>(1-2a)^{2}

and in particular,

D^{2}>16\ \mbox{when}\ a=-\frac{3}{2}\ \mbox{and}\ D^{2}>25\ \mbox{when}\ a=-2
.

(Rank One) Given that the hypothetical wall lies below
(\frac{1}{2},1)
with the inequalities above, it suffices to
test curves with
C^{2}=-1,0
, and to check that the line through

(-1,0)
and
I({\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-C))
passes through or above
(\frac{1}{2},1)
, i.e. that

m=\frac{C\cdot H}{\frac{1}{2}C^{2}+1}\geq-\frac{2a}{||D||}

when
I({\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-C))
is between
(-1,0)
and the parabola. That is,

C\cdot D\geq-a(C^{2}+2)\ \mbox{when}\ C^{2}=-1,0

Finally, the argument for
{\mathcal{I}}^{\vee}_{p}(-D)[1]
is completely analogous, and we have:

Corollary 5.4
(a) The stability sectors for
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}^{\vee}(-D)[1]
contain
W(-\frac{3}{2})
when

D^{2}>16\ \mbox{and}\ C\cdot D\geq\frac{3}{2}(C^{2}+2)\ \mbox{for}\ C^{2}=-1,0

(b) The stability sectors for
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}^{\vee}(-D)[1]
contain
W(-2)
when

D^{2}>25\ \mbox{and}\ C\cdot D\geq 2(C^{2}+2)\ \mbox{for}\ C^{2}=-1,0

Note. The
C^{2}=1
case is ruled out by the inequality on
D^{2}
.

6.
Stability Conditions and the Drinfeld Family

There is a “relative Serre” family
C_{|K_{S}+D-p-q|^{\vee}}
of extensions:

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{q}^{\vee}(-D)[1]\rightarrow C_{\epsilon}\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{p}\rightarrow 0

fibered over the product
S\times S
with fibers:

{\mathbb{P}}(\mbox{H}^{0}(S,\omega_{S}(D)\otimes{\mathcal{I}}_{p}\otimes{\mathcal{I}}_{q}))=|K_{S}+D-p-q|^{\vee}

This intersects the Drinfeld family along the exceptional divisor
E
, which embeds in the
relative Serre family via

{\mathbb{P}}(\mbox{H}^{0}(S,\omega_{S}(D)\otimes{\mathcal{I}}_{p}^{2}))\subset|K_{S}+D-p-p|^{\vee}

Thus, the objects of the Drinfeld family are all
(x,y)
-(semi)stable outside
W_{1}
if:

(i)
D^{2}>10
and
D\cdot C>C^{2}+2\ \mbox{for}\ C^{2}=-1,0
.

(ii) All objects
C_{\epsilon}
of the original Serre family for
\epsilon\not\in S
are
(x,y)
-(semi)stable.

(iii) All objects
C_{\epsilon}
of the relative Serre family are
(x,y)
-(semi)stable.

So we assume (i) and go to work on (ii) and (iii).

Original Serre.
If
C_{\epsilon}
is produced by the Serre family with
\epsilon\not\in S
, then:

(Higher Rank) We’ve already made this computation in §4. If

D^{2}>(-8a)\left(\frac{r^{2}}{r^{2}-1}\right)

then
W(a)
is not witnessed by a rank
r
(or more) sheaf, and in particular, if

D^{2}>(-a)\frac{32}{3}

then
W(a)
is not witnessed in rank
>1
.

(Rank One) The condition not to be witnessed by
{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}(-C)\subset C_{\epsilon}
is also done:

D\cdot C>C^{2}-2a\ \mbox{jumps when $a=-\frac{3}{2}$ and when $a=-2$}

Relative Serre
The genesis wall is
W_{1}
.
If
E\subset C_{\epsilon}
witnesses the next wall, with:

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{p}^{\vee}(-D)[1]\rightarrow C_{\epsilon}\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{q}\rightarrow 0

we would first like to conclude, as in §4, that

E\cap{\mathcal{I}}_{p}(-D)^{\vee}[1]=0

and therefore that

E\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{q}
is a torsion-free coherent sheaf.
From the cohomology sequence:

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-D)[1]\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{p}^{\vee}(-D)\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}_{p}\rightarrow 0

we see that

{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(E)\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-D)
and then as before that either

{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(E)=0\ \mbox{(and $E$ is a torsion-free sheaf), or}\ {\mathcal{H}}^{-1}(E)={\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-D)

In the latter case,
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(E)\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(C_{\epsilon})
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{-y}
, and from the sequence:

0\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}_{p}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(C_{\epsilon})\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{q}\rightarrow 0

it follows that either:

(i)
{\mathbb{C}}_{p}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(E)
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}^{\vee}(-D)[1]\subset E
or else

(ii)
{\mathbb{C}}_{p}\cap{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(E)=0
, in which case replacing
E
with
E^{\prime}=\ker(C_{\epsilon}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(C_{\epsilon})/{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(E))

results in a subobject of larger
(x,y)
-slope, and a contradiction.

We may draw a contradiction as in §4 to the alternative
I_{Z}^{\vee}(-D)[1]\subset E
when we use the additional
inequalities of Corollary 5.4 to conclude that
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}
is stable along the wall. Thus with these inequalities,
we may conclude that an
E
withnessing the wall is a torsion-free sheaf. At that point, the
argument proceeds exactly as with the original Serre family, and we obtain:

Proposition 6.1.
(a) if
D^{2}>16
and
D\cdot C>C^{2}+3
when
C^{2}=-1,0
then the stability region for the Drinfeld family contains the wall

W(-\frac{3}{2})
.

(b) if
D^{2}>25
and
D\cdot C>C^{2}+4
, the stability region contains the wall
W(-2)
.

7.
The Determinant Construction

The construction of Bayer-Macrì
[
11
]
associates a linear map:

\gamma_{(x,y)}:N_{1}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

to each family
F_{X}
of objects of
{\mathcal{A}}_{-y}
over a variety
X
. It is defined by:

\gamma_{(x,y)}(C):=-\mathfrak{Im}\left(\frac{Z_{(x,y)}(R\pi_{*}F_{C})}{Z_{(x,y)}(F_{p})}\right)

for
p\in C\subset X
and satisfies the following properties:

\bullet

\gamma_{(x,y)}(C)\geq 0
if
F_{C}
is a family of
(x,y)
-semistable objects, and among these

\bullet

\gamma_{(x,y)}(C)=0
only when the
(x,y)
-associated gradeds
\mbox{Ass}(F_{p})
do not vary.

\bullet
the ray spanned by
\gamma_{(x,y)}
is constant along walls for the invariants
\mbox{ch}(F_{p})
.

We now follow a strategy outlined by Bayer and Macrì to prove the Theorems.

Theorem 7.1.
The nef cone of the blow-up
Y
of
|K_{S}+D|^{\vee}
along
S

(i) contains
3H-E
when
D^{2}\geq 16
and
D\cdot C\geq C^{2}+3
for all
C^{2}=-1,0
.

(ii) contains
3H-E
in the
interior
when the inequalities are strict.

(iii) contains
2H-E
when
D^{2}\geq 25
and
D\cdot C\geq C^{2}+4
for
C^{2}=-1,0
.

(iv) contracts the variety of secant lines onto
S^{[2]}
when the inequalities are strict.

Proof.
We compute the determinant divisor class for the Drinfeld family:

(a) Let
l\subset|K_{S}+D|^{\vee}
be a line not meeting
S
(we assume codim
{}_{|K_{S}+D|^{\vee}}S\geq 2
).
Then via the restriction
C_{l}
of the Serre family, we obtain:

\gamma_{(x,y)}(l)=-\mathfrak{Im}\frac{Z_{(x,y)}(-1,2D,-D^{2})}{Z_{(x,y)}(0,D,-\frac{D^{2}}{2})}=(-a)\left(\frac{4||D||}{D^{2}(D^{2}+4)}\right)

for
(x,y)\in W(a)
, the wall through
(a,0)
with slope
2/||D||
.

(b) Let
l_{p}
be a line
l_{p}
in a fiber of
\pi:E\rightarrow S
. Then:

\gamma_{(x,y)}(l_{p})=-\mathfrak{Im}\frac{Z_{(x,y)}(-1,2D,-D^{2}+1)}{Z_{(x,y)}(0,D,-\frac{D^{2}}{2})}=-(a+1)\left(\frac{4||D||}{D^{2}(D^{2}+4)}\right)

from which we conclude that

\gamma_{(x,y)}\ \mbox{is proportional to}(-a)H+(-a+1)E
on
W(a)
, i.e. to

3H-E\ \mbox{along}\ W(-3/2)\ \mbox{and}\ 2H-E\ \mbox{along}\ W(-2)
.

Together with Proposition 6.1, this gives (i)-(iii).

For (iv), we consider the strictly semi-stable
objects of
Y
along the wall
W(-2)
. This is analogous to the locus
S\subset|K_{S}+D|^{\vee}
of strictly semi-stable
objects of the Serre family along the wall
W(-1)
. By Corollary 5.3 and 5.4, we may conclude that all of the objects
{\mathcal{I}}_{p},{\mathcal{I}}_{p}^{\vee}(-D)[1]
as well as

{\mathcal{I}}_{Z},{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}^{\vee}(-D)[1]
for
l(Z)=2
are stable along the wall, and then liftability of the ideal sheaf of
Z\in S^{[2]}
:

\begin{array}[]{cccccccccc}{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}\\
\downarrow&\searrow\\
C_{\epsilon}&\rightarrow&{\mathcal{O}}_{S}&\rightarrow&{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(-D)[1]&\rightarrow\\
\end{array}

flags the elements
C_{\epsilon}
(for
\epsilon\not\in S
) that are strictly semi-stable. In this case, however, the object
C_{\epsilon}
is not uniquely
determined by the liftability. Instead, all points of the secant (or tangent) line spanned by
Z
(other than points of
S
) admit the lift.

This is the non-singular complement
\Sigma_{1}(S)-S
of the variety of
secant/tangent lines to
S
, whose closure in
Y
gives the strictly semi-stable locus fibered over the moduli space
S^{[2]}

of
(x,y)
-stable objects of class
(1,0,-2)
(for
(x,y)\in W(-2)
).

Next, we turn our attention to the Hilbert schemes more generally.
Let
{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathcal{Z}}
be the family of ideal sheaves over
S^{[n]}
. In this case the walls are the line segments (in the stability region) through

(-n,0)
of slope
m>0
. We now compute the determinant divisor class (up to a scaling factor) along the wall
W(m)
defined by:

y=m(x+n)

(a) Fix
Z_{0}
a reduced divisor of length
n-1
and a curve
C\subset S-Z_{0}
and consider the family of reduced divisors
F_{C}
indexed by
C
given by:

F_{p}=\{p\cup Z_{0}\}
.
Then the family is an ideal in
C\times S
satisfying:

0\rightarrow F_{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{Z_{0}\times S}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta}\rightarrow 0

where
\Delta\subset C\times C
is the diagonal. The derived push-forward of the family satisfies:

\mbox{ch}(R\pi_{*}F_{C})=(1-g)\mbox{ch}({\mathcal{I}}_{Z_{0}})-\mbox{ch}({\mathcal{O}}_{C})=(1-g)(1,0,-n)+(0,-C,\frac{C^{2}}{2}+(1-g))

while of course

\mbox{ch}(F_{p})=(1,0,-n)
.
We may simplify using the adjunction formula:

(1-g)=-\frac{C\cdot(C+K_{S})}{2}

to obtain:

-\mathfrak{Im}\frac{Z_{(x,y)}(R\pi_{*}F_{C})}{Z_{(x,y)}(F_{p})}=\frac{y\cdot\frac{C\cdot K_{S}}{2}+(C\cdot H)(n+x)}{(n+x)^{2}+y^{2}}

and then substituting
y=m(x+n)
we further obtain:

\gamma_{(x,y)}(C)=\left(\frac{m}{2(n+x)(1+m^{2})}\right)\left(C\cdot(K_{S}+\frac{2D}{m||D||})\right)

(b) Next, let
Z_{1}
contain one point
p\in C
and
F_{C_{p}}
the resulting family, fitting in:

0\rightarrow F_{C_{p}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{Z_{1}\times S}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta}(-p)\rightarrow 0

This yields:

\gamma_{(x,y)}(C_{p})=\left(\frac{m}{2(n+x)(1+m^{2})}\right)\left(C\cdot(K_{S}+\frac{2D}{m||D||})-1\right)

and since
C_{p}\cdot E=1
because of the single point of the family with a non-reduced scheme, it follows that the determinant divisor class is proportional to

(K_{S}+D)^{[n]}-E

along the line of slope
m=2/||D||
. With Corollary 5.3, this gives Theorem 7.2.
