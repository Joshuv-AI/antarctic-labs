Convergence of the Yang-Mills flow on ALE gravitational instantons
Anuk Dayaprema, Alex Waldron

Abstract
. We prove a sharp convergence theorem for the Yang-Mills flow on an SU ⁡ ( r ) {\mathrm{SU}}(r) -bundle over a locally hyperKähler ALE 4-manifold. Our main result is a noncompact version of the “parabolic gap theorem” [ 7 , Theorem 1.1] previously established by the authors.



1.
Introduction and statement of main result

A time-dependent family of connections
A(t)
on a vector bundle
E
over a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is said to evolve by
Yang-Mills flow
if

(1.1)

\frac{{\partial}A}{{\partial}t}=-D_{A(t)}^{*}F_{A(t)}.

Here
F_{A(t)}
is the curvature of
A(t)
and
D_{A(t)}^{*}
is the formal adjoint of the covariant derivative.
The semi-parabolic equation (
1.1
) is the downward gradient-flow of the
Yang-Mills functional

\mathcal{YM}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|F_{A}|_{g}^{2}\,dV_{g},

which originated in physics and has become a fundamental object of study in differential geometry.

The goal of this paper is to extend the authors’ recent “parabolic gap theorem”
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
, valid over the 4-sphere, to a certain well-known family of noncompact spaces. Recall that an
elliptic
gap theorem states that any Yang-Mills connection with energy below a certain value is an instanton, i.e., has self-dual or anti-self-dual curvature and is a minimizer of
{\mathcal{YM}}(\cdot)
on the given bundle (see
[
7
, §1]
for a brief survey). A
parabolic
gap theorem states that any initial connection with energy below a certain value is deformed smoothly by (
1.1
) to an instanton. The latter is a stronger and more natural statement in that it applies to general connections rather than only to solutions of the Yang-Mills equation.

The well-known noncompact spaces in question are ALE locally hyperKähler 4-manifolds. These are complete, Ricci-flat 4-manifolds with vanishing self-dual Weyl tensor and maximal volume growth at infinity, having a single end modeled on the space
\left({\mathbb{R}}^{4}\setminus\{0\}\right)/\Gamma,
where
\Gamma\subset{\mathrm{SO}}(4)
is a finite subgroup. In the simply-connected case,
Kronheimer
[
13
,
12
]
gave a complete construction of all such
M,
identifying each one with a canonical hyperKähler structure on a resolution of the orbifold
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}/\Gamma.
Wright
[
30
]
later completed the classification by determining the possible finite quotients of the Kronheimer examples.
Note that a classification of complete 4D hyperKähler manifolds with less-than-maximal volume growth has also been achieved recently
[
6
,
4
,
5
,
24
]
, but in the present work we focus on the ALE case.

We have multiple motivations for proving parabolic gap theorems on gravitational instantons.

1.

The convergence problem for (
1.1
) on a complete noncompact space presents new analytic challenges as compared to the compact case. Indeed, the work of Gustaffson, Nakanishi, and Tsai
[
10
]
(see also Sire, Wei, Zheng, and Zhou
[
22
]
) demonstrates that, strictly speaking, the parabolic gap
fails
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
; there exist smooth
{\mathrm{SO}}(4)
-equivariant initial connections
H^{1}
-close to the standard instanton that fail to converge at infinite time under (
1.1
). We will see that this problem can be avoided by imposing a mild additional decay hypothesis on the self-dual curvature of the initial connection.

2.

A parabolic gap theorem on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
has already been established by Oh and Tataru
[
20
, Theorem 2.11]
for connections with Yang-Mills energy less than
8\pi^{2}
and zero topological charge (see Definition
2.1
).
Their result is used to construct a “caloric gauge,” an essential step in the proof of the Threshold Conjecture for the hyperbolic Yang-Mills equations
[
20
,
18
,
17
,
19
]
. Our main result generalizes that of Oh and Tataru to higher topological charge. In particular, our conclusion is that the flow (
1.1
) converges smoothly to an ASD instanton of the same charge as that of the initial connection, rather than to the flat connection. We expect that this will open the way to generalizations of Oh-Tataru’s work on the Threshold Conjecture.

3.

The parabolic gap question for (
1.1
) on a bundle over a gravitational instanton is closely analogous to the question of stability of the underlying space under Ricci flow, studied by
[
8
,
9
,
11
,
23
]
. In the case of (
1.1
), we are able to obtain convergence under a vastly weaker hypothesis on the initial data. In particular, we do not need to assume closeness to any ASD instanton.

4.

Last, we expect that our results will be useful in the future for studying the topology of moduli spaces of ASD instantons on ALE gravitational instantons
[
14
,
16
]
.

The following summarizes our main results:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an ALE gravitational instanton asymptotic to
\left({\mathbb{R}}^{4}\setminus\{0\}\right)/\Gamma,
oriented such that the self-dual Weyl curvature vanishes. Let
E\to M
be an
{\mathrm{SU}}(r)
-bundle and
A_{0}
a connection on
E
with
{\mathcal{YM}}(A_{0})<\infty
and

(1.2)

\|F^{+}_{A_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}<\frac{8\pi^{2}}{|\Gamma|}.

Let
A(t),

t\in\left[0,\infty\right),
be any finite-energy classical solution of the Yang-Mills flow with
A(0)=A_{0}.
Denote the self-dual part of the curvature of
A(t)
by
F^{+}(t).

(a) We have

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}=o\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)

and

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}(M)}\to 0

as
t\to\infty.

(b) Suppose further that
|F^{+}_{A_{0}}|\in L^{p}(M)
for some
1<p<2.

We then have

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{2}{p}}}\right),

and

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{L^{p}(M)}\to 0

as
t\to\infty.

(c) Under the above assumptions,
A(t)
converges in
C^{\infty}_{loc}\cap L^{q}
for each
q>\frac{2p}{2-p}
to an ASD instanton
A_{\infty}
on
E
with
\kappa(A_{\infty})=\kappa(A_{0}).

Note that in view of
[
28
]
or
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
, assuming a reasonable short-time existence theory on the given 4-manifold, we can always solve (
1.1
) classically on
\left[0,\infty\right).
A detailed treatment of short-time existence and well-posedness for (
1.1
) on complete manifolds is still lacking in the literature, and we plan to address this question separately.

2.
Sobolev constant and elliptic gap theorem

Let
(M,g)
be a complete, oriented, Ricci-flat, anti-self-dual, ALE 4-manifold. Note that we do not assume
M
is globally hyperKähler, or equivalently that
M
is simply-connected. According to Bando-Kasue-Nakajima
[
3
]
,
(M,g)
is in fact ALE of order
4,
i.e. there exists a compact subset
\Sigma\subset M,
a finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset{\mathrm{SO}}(4),
and diffeomorphism
\varphi:({\mathbb{R}}^{4}\setminus\{0\})/\Gamma\to M\setminus\Sigma
such that for each integer
k\geq 0,
we have as
r:=\operatorname{dist}_{g_{\operatorname{Euc}}}(x,0)\to\infty,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{\operatorname{Euc},k}(\varphi^{\ast}(g)-g_{\operatorname{Euc}})\right|_{g_{\operatorname{Euc}}}=O\left(r^{-4-k}\right).

Kronheimer’s classification
[
13
,
12
]
implies that
\Sigma
can be taken to be a set of measure zero, although we do not need this fact.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a connection on a unitary bundle
E\to M
with
L^{2}
curvature. The
topological charge
of
A
is

\kappa(A)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\operatorname{Tr}F_{A}\wedge F_{A}.

Note that

\kappa(A)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{M}\left(|F_{A}^{-}|^{2}-|F_{A}^{+}|^{2}\right)\,dV_{g},

so that
{\mathcal{YM}}(A)=4\pi^{2}|\kappa(A)|
if and only if
F_{A}=F_{A}^{-}
or
F_{A}=F_{A}^{+}
is self-dual or anti-self-dual, i.e. an instanton.

If
M
is compact then
\kappa
depends only on the topology of the bundle
E,
and takes integer values (equal to the second Chern class) if
c_{1}(E)=0
. If
M
is noncompact then
\kappa
is no longer independent of
A
or integer-valued, but connections with prescribed asymptotics have the same value mod
{\mathbb{Z}}.
For the case of ALE 4-manifolds, the value of
\kappa
is equivalent mod
{\mathbb{Z}}
to the Chern-Simons invariant of the induced flat connection over
S^{3}/\Gamma.
We shall not need these facts in the present paper.

The following Sobolev inequality, in the hyperKähler case, is originally due to Viaclovsky
[
25
, Theorem 1.3]
; for
M
not globally hyperKähler, we may cite the general result of Balogh-Kristaly
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
), which holds for complete manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and maximal volume growth.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
f\in\dot{H}^{1}(M),
we have

(2.1)

\|f\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq\frac{(6|\Gamma|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{8\pi}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Remark 2.3
.

Notice that this inequality is invariant under metric dilations
g\to\lambda g,
for constant
\lambda>0.
Furthermore, we crucially have that the constant in (
2.1
) is the
\dot{H}^{1}
Sobolev constant of
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}/\Gamma.

From this inequality we can deduce the following gap theorem using a version of a recent argument by Vieira
[
26
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

For a finite-energy Yang-Mills connection
A
on an
{\mathrm{SU}}(r)
-bundle
E\to M,
we have the implication

\|F_{A}^{+}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}<\frac{8\pi^{2}}{|\Gamma|}\quad\Rightarrow\quad F^{+}_{A}\equiv 0.

Proof.

Since
(M,g)
has vanishing scalar and self-dual Weyl curvatures, the Weitzenböck formula for
\Omega_{+}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\ast}\nabla|F_{A}^{+}|^{2}+|\nabla_{A}F_{A}^{+}|^{2}=\langle[F_{A}^{+},F_{A}^{+}],F_{A}^{+}\rangle.

Since
D_{A}^{\ast}F_{A}^{+}=D_{A}F_{A}^{+}=0,
the improved Kato inequality
[
21
, Lemma 3.1]
implies

\displaystyle 6|F^{+}|\left|\nabla|F_{A}^{+}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}=\frac{3}{2}|\nabla|F_{A}^{+}||^{2}\leq|\nabla_{A}F_{A}^{+}|^{2}.

For structure group
SU(r),
we have (see
[
7
, Remark 3.2 (4)]
)

\displaystyle|\langle[F_{A}^{+},F_{A}^{+}],F_{A}^{+}\rangle|\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}|F_{A}^{+}|^{3}.

Hence

\displaystyle 2|F_{A}^{+}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\nabla^{\ast}\nabla|F_{A}^{+}|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\ast}\nabla|F_{A}^{+}|^{2}+6|F_{A}^{+}|\left|\nabla|F_{A}^{+}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\ast}\nabla|F_{A}^{+}|^{2}+|\nabla_{A}F_{A}^{+}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}|F_{A}^{+}|^{3}.

We divide both sides by
2|F_{A}^{+}|,
integrate over
M,
and integrate by parts on the left-hand side to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla|F_{A}^{+}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\left\||F_{A}^{+}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{4}^{4}.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left\||F_{A}^{+}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{4}^{4}\leq\|F_{A}^{+}\|_{2}\|F_{A}^{+}\|_{4}^{2},

so if

\displaystyle\|F_{A}^{+}\|_{2}^{2}<\frac{8\pi^{2}}{|\Gamma|},

then

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla|F_{A}^{+}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}<\frac{8\pi}{(6|\Gamma|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|F_{A}^{+}\|_{4}^{2}.

This violates the Sobolev inequality (
2.1
) unless
F_{A}^{+}\equiv 0,
so the lemma holds.
∎

3.
Basic energy identities

In this section, we record the basic estimates used throughout the paper. Further details can be found in
[
7
,
27
]
. In the sequel,
A(t)
is a solution of (
1.1
) on
M.
We abbreviate

\displaystyle F=F(t)=F_{A(t)},

and similarly for
F^{+},D^{\ast}F,
and so on. We use
C
to denote a positive, universal constant that may increase in each appearance.

3.1.
Energy identities

We show that if
\mathcal{YM}(A(t))
is locally bounded in time, then in fact
\mathcal{YM}(A(t))
is nonincreasing. For these lemmas,
M
only needs to be a complete (oriented) Riemannian 4-manifold. First we recall the following local energy estimate.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M).
For any times
a<b,
we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left|\int\varphi^{2}(|F^{+}(b)|^{2}-|F^{+}(a)|^{2})+\int_{a}^{b}\hskip-3.00003pt\int\hskip-3.00003pt\varphi^{2}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\right|\leq 4\left(\int_{a}^{b}\hskip-5.0pt\int_{\text{supp}(\nabla\varphi)}\hskip-3.00003pt\varphi^{2}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\hskip-3.00003pt\int\hskip-3.00003pt|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|F^{+}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The inequality also holds with
F^{-}
replacing
F^{+}.

Proof.

Following the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 3.4]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\varphi^{2}|F^{+}|^{2}+\int\varphi^{2}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}=4\int\langle\varphi D^{\ast}F,\nabla\varphi\intprod F^{+}\rangle.

We integrate in time from
t=a
to
t=b.
By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{a}^{b}\int\langle\varphi D^{\ast}F,\nabla\varphi\intprod F^{+}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\text{supp}(\nabla\varphi)}\langle\varphi D^{\ast}F,\nabla\varphi\intprod F^{+}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\text{supp}(\nabla\varphi)}\varphi|D^{\ast}F||\nabla\varphi||F^{+}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\text{supp}(\nabla\varphi)}\varphi^{2}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\text{supp}(\nabla\varphi)}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|F^{+}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus (
3.1
) follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty).
Suppose
A(t)
is a solution of (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|F(0)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}|F|^{2}<\infty.

Then for
t\in[0,T],
we have the global energy identities

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|F^{\pm}(0)|^{2}=\int_{M}|F^{\pm}(t)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}

and the conservation of charge

(3.4)

\displaystyle\kappa(A(t))=\kappa(A(0)).

Proof.

It follows from the proof of Lemma
3.1
that for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
and
t\in(0,T],

\displaystyle\int\varphi^{2}|F(t)|^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\int\varphi^{2}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\leq\int\varphi^{2}|F(0)|^{2}+4\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int\varphi^{2}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|F|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By the Peter-Paul inequality,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int\varphi^{2}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|F|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int\varphi^{2}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|F|^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle\int\varphi^{2}|F(t)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int\varphi^{2}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\leq\int\varphi^{2}|F(0)|^{2}+4\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|F|^{2}.

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,1]
be a smooth cut-off function for
(-\infty,1]
supported in
(-\infty,2],
and define
\psi_{n}(x):=\psi(x/n).
Fix a point
p\in M
and denote the function
f:=\text{dist}_{g}(\cdot,p).
According to
[
1
, Theorem 1]
, there exists
\eta\in C^{\infty}(M)
with
\|\eta-f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and
\|\nabla\eta\|_{\infty}\leq 2.
We now replace
\varphi
with
\varphi_{n}:=\psi_{n}\circ\eta.
By completeness of
M,
the
\varphi_{n}
are compactly supported, and they converge to the constant function
1
locally uniformly with
\|\nabla\varphi_{n}\|_{\infty}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)
as
n\to\infty.
Since

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi_{n}|^{2}|F|^{2}\leq C|F|^{2},

and since we are assuming (
3.2
), the dominated convergence theorem implies

\displaystyle\int|F(t)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\leq\int|F(0)|^{2}.

In particular,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int|D^{\ast}F|^{2}<\infty.

The energy identities (
3.3
) now follow from (
3.1
) by dominated convergence, in view of (
3.5
).

We next show (
3.4
). We compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\varphi\text{Tr}(F\wedge F)

\displaystyle=-\int\varphi\text{Tr}(DD^{\ast}F\wedge F).

By the Bianchi identity
D_{A}F_{A}=0
and Stokes’ theorem, this last integral is equal to

\displaystyle-\int\varphi d\text{Tr}(D^{\ast}F\wedge F)=\int d\varphi\wedge\text{Tr}(D^{\ast}F\wedge F).

Hence

\displaystyle\left|\int\varphi\text{Tr}(F_{A(t)}\wedge F_{A(t)})-\int\varphi\text{Tr}(F_{A(0)}\wedge F_{A(0)})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla\varphi||D^{\ast}F||F|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int|\nabla\varphi|(|D^{\ast}F|^{2}+|F|^{2}).

In view of (
3.2
) and (
3.5
), (
3.4
) follows from dominated convergence as before.
∎

3.2.
{\varepsilon}
-regularity

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
p>1.
Let
x_{0}\in M
and
R>0,
and let
B_{R}(x_{0})
denote the metric ball of radius
R
about
x_{0}.
There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, depending only on
|\Gamma|,
and
C_{\ref{lemma:epsreg}}\geq 1,
depending only on
|\Gamma|
and
p,
such that the following holds. Suppose
A(t)
solves (
1.1
) on
B_{R}(x_{0})\times\left[T-R^{2},T\right]
with

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{T-R^{2}\leq t\leq T}\|F^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}.

We then have

(3.7)

\sup_{T-\frac{R^{2}}{4}\leq t\leq T}\|F^{+}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\frac{R}{2}}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)}\leq\frac{C_{\ref{lemma:epsreg}}}{R^{\frac{4}{p}}}\sup_{T-R^{2}\leq t\leq T}\|F^{+}(t)\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}.

Proof.

Note that if
\theta(t)
is a solution of (
1.1
) on the Riemannian manifold
(N,h),
then
\theta(\lambda^{-1}t)
is a solution on
(N,\lambda h)
for any
\lambda>0.
In particular, we may assume
A(t)
is defined on the ball of radius
1
about
x_{0}
with respect to the metric
h:=R^{-2}g
on
M,
for
t\in[-1,0].
Furthermore, since
M
is four-dimensional, the assumption (
3.6
) is preserved, and we have
F^{+_{g}}=F^{+_{h}}=F^{+}.

Denote

\displaystyle u:=|F^{+}|_{h}.

Since
(M,\lambda g)
has vanishing scalar and self-dual Weyl curvatures for any
\lambda>0
,
u
satisfies

(3.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta u\leq Cu^{2}.

Note that there is no linear term on the right-hand side, c.f.
[
27
, (2.1)]
. The standard Schoen-Uhlenbeck
{\varepsilon}
-regularity argument
[
27
, Prop. 2.3]
relies only on a scale-invariant Sobolev inequality (
2.1
), and therefore goes through in this situation. We provide the following brief alternative argument.

Define a function
\varphi
by

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{dist}_{h}(x,x_{0})\leq\frac{3}{4}\\
7-8\text{dist}_{h}(x,x_{0})&\frac{3}{4}\leq\text{dist}_{h}(x,x_{0})\leq\frac{7}{8}\\
0&\frac{7}{8}\leq\text{dist}_{h}(x,x_{0}).\end{cases}

We multiply (
3.8
) by
2\varphi^{2}u.
Since
M
is complete and
\varphi u
extends by zero to all of
M,
we may integrate by parts to obtain as in the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 19.1]

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\varphi^{2}u^{2}+2\int_{M}|\nabla(\varphi u)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}\varphi^{2}u^{3}+2\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}u^{2}.

We integrate in time and apply the Sobolev inequality (
2.1
) and Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}\varphi^{2}u^{2}(t)+\frac{16\pi}{(6|\Gamma|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{-1}^{t}\left(\int_{M}\varphi^{4}u^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\varphi^{2}u^{2}(0)+C\sup_{-1\leq s\leq t}\left(\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})}u^{2}(s)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{-1}^{t}\left(\int_{M}\varphi^{4}u^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+2\int_{-1}^{t}\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}u^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\left(1+\int_{-1}^{t}\left(\int_{M}\varphi^{4}u^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Thus if

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{8\pi}{C(6|\Gamma|)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

we deduce that for
t\in[-1,0]

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi}{(6|\Gamma|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{-1}^{t}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(x_{0})}u^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C.

In particular, there exists
s_{0}\in[-1,-\frac{3}{4}]
such that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(x_{0})}u^{4}(s_{0})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C|\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now let
\psi
be a cutoff for
B_{\frac{2}{3}}(x_{0})
supported in
B_{\frac{3}{4}}(x_{0}).

We multiply (
3.8
) by
3\psi^{2}u^{2}.
Shrinking
\varepsilon_{0},
we may proceed as before, integrating in time from
s_{0}
to
t,
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}\psi^{2}u^{3}(t)+\frac{8\pi}{(6|\Gamma|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{s_{0}}^{t}\left(\int_{M}\psi^{4}u^{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\psi^{2}u^{3}(s_{0})+3\int_{s_{0}}^{t}\int_{M}|\nabla\psi|^{2}u^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(x_{0})}u^{3}(s_{0})+C\int_{s_{0}}^{t}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(x_{0})}u^{3}.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(x_{0})}u^{3}\leq\left(\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(x_{0})}u^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(x_{0})}u^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

we deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{-\frac{3}{4}\leq t\leq 0}\int_{B_{\frac{2}{3}}(x_{0})}u^{3}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}|\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C.

Since the exponent
3
is bigger than
2,
we may apply standard parabolic Moser iteration (see
[
15
, Theorem 19.1]
) to obtain

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
E_{0},R>0,
and let
x_{0}\in M.

There exists a constant

, depending only on

and

satisfying

if

\left\|F^{+}\left(-(cR)^{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B_{R}(x_{0})\right)}<c,

then

(3.11)

\sup_{-\frac{1}{16}(cR)^{2}\leq t\leq 0}\|F^{+}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\frac{1}{4}}(x_{0})\right)}<\frac{1}{(cR)^{2}}.

Proof.

By rescaling, we may assume without loss of generality that

The assumption (
3.10
) then reads

(3.12)

\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})}|F^{+}(-c^{2})|^{2}<c^{2}.

Taking

in (

By the Peter-Paul inequality,

Lemma 5.1

Let

be a solution of (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta u\leq Cu^{2}

We multiply the inequality by

t\geq t_{0}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int u^{p}(t)+\frac{16\pi(p-1)}{p(6|\Gamma|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left(\int u^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\int u^{p}(t_{0}),

which in particular implies the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Fix
K\geq 0.
Let
A(t)
be a solution of (
1.1
) satisfying the hypotheses of Lemma
5.1
and satisfying

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{K}{t}

for
t\geq t_{0}.
There exists
C_{\ref{lemma:improveddecay}}>0,
depending only on
p,|\Gamma|,
and
K,
such that for
t\geq 2t_{0},

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\ref{lemma:improveddecay}}\|F^{+}(t_{0})\|_{p}t^{-\frac{2}{p}}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\|F^{+}(t)\|_{p}+t^{\frac{2}{p}}\|F^{+}(t)\|_{\infty}\right)=0.

Proof.

By (
5.3
),
u
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta u\leq\frac{CK}{t}u.

Thus by parabolic Moser iteration, there exists
C_{0}>0,
depending only on
p,|\Gamma|,
and
K,
such that for all
(x,t)\in M\times(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\sqrt{t}}(x)\right)}\leq C_{0}t^{-\frac{3}{p}}\left(\int_{t/2}^{t}\int_{B_{2\sqrt{t}}(x)}u^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{t/2}^{t}\int_{B_{2\sqrt{t}}(x)}u^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\text{Vol}(B_{2\sqrt{t}}(x))}\int_{t/2}^{t}\left(\int_{B_{2\sqrt{t}}(x)}u^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ct\int_{t/2}^{t}\left(\int_{B_{2\sqrt{t}}(x)}u^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\left(\int u^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|u(t_{0})\|_{p}^{p},

and thus we further have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{t/2}^{t}\left(\int_{B_{2\sqrt{t}}(x)}u^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0.

The
L^{\infty}
estimates of the lemma thus follow.

We next show that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{p}=0.
For
t\geq 2t_{0}
, we now have the differential inequality

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\Delta u\leq CC_{\ref{lemma:improveddecay}}t^{-\frac{2}{p}}u.

Hence the function

\displaystyle v:=e^{\frac{pCC_{\ref{lemma:improveddecay}}}{2-p}\left(t^{1-\frac{2}{p}}-(2t_{0})^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\right)}u

satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v-\Delta v\leq 0

and

\displaystyle v(2t_{0})=u(2t_{0}).

Let
\tilde{v}
denote the semigroup solution of the heat equation starting at time
2t_{0}
with initial data
u(2t_{0}).
Then by the maximum principle,
v(t)\leq\tilde{v}(t),
so we have

\displaystyle u(t)\leq e^{\frac{pCC_{\ref{lemma:improveddecay}}}{2-p}(2t_{0})^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}\tilde{v}(t).

We now use that the heat semigroup
H_{t}
on
(M,g)
satisfies for
1\leq q\leq r\leq\infty
and
t>0

(5.4)

\displaystyle\|H_{t}(f)\|_{r}\leq C_{|\Gamma|}t^{-2\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\|f\|_{q}.

To see this, note that we have

\displaystyle\|H_{t}(f)\|_{r}\leq\|f\|_{r}

since the heat semigroup is a contraction on
L^{r},
and we have

\displaystyle\|H_{t}(f)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{|\Gamma|}t^{-\frac{2}{q}}\sup_{t/2\leq s\leq t}\|H_{s}(f)\|_{q}\leq C_{|\Gamma|}t^{-\frac{2}{q}}\|f\|_{q}

by parabolic Moser iteration. The general bound (
5.4
) now follows by interpolation via Hölder’s inequality.

Fixing
x\in M
and letting
\varphi
be the indicator function for
B_{R}(x),
we thus have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|H_{t-2t_{0}}(\varphi\tilde{v}(2t_{0}))\|_{p}+\|H_{t-2t_{0}}((1-\varphi)\tilde{v}(2t_{0}))\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{|\Gamma|}(t-2t_{0})^{-2\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|\varphi\tilde{v}(2t_{0})\|_{1}+\|(1-\varphi)\tilde{v}(2t_{0})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{|\Gamma|}(t-2t_{0})^{-2\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}(CR^{4})^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|\tilde{v}(2t_{0})\|_{p}+\|(1-\varphi)\tilde{v}(2t_{0})\|_{p}.

Since
\tilde{v}(2t_{0})\in L^{p},
we may choose
R
large enough to make the second term small, and then choose
t
large enough, depending on
R,|\Gamma|,
and
\|\tilde{v}(2t_{0})\|_{p},
to make the first term small. We deduce that
\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{p}\to 0,
so the lemma follows.
∎

We now work towards showing that
\|F^{+}(t)\|_{2}
is small after a uniform time. First we show that
\|F^{+}(t)\|_{p}
remains locally bounded.

Lemma 5.3
.

Fix
E_{0},K\geq 0
and
p\in(1,2).
Suppose
A(t)
is a solution of (
1.1
) satisfying

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq E_{0}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{t}

for
t\geq 0.
Then

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|F^{+}(0)\|_{p}^{p}+C_{\ref{lemma:Lpcontrolbefore}}t^{2-p}

for
t\geq 0,
where
C_{\ref{lemma:Lpcontrolbefore}}>0
depends only on
p,E_{0},
and
K.

Proof.

By (
4.3
) and (
5.1
),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int u^{p}\leq pC\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq pC\left(\frac{K}{t}\right)^{p-1}E_{0}^{2}.

The desired estimate follows by integrating in time.
∎

Next we show that if the space-time
L^{2}
norm of
D^{\ast}F
is small, then
F^{+}
is scale-invariantly small in
L^{\infty},
not just bounded.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\varepsilon,t>0.
Suppose
A(t)
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
5.3
. There exist
\Omega\geq 1,
depending only on
\varepsilon
and
K,
and
\delta>0,
depending only on
\varepsilon,E_{0},
and
K,
such that the following holds. If

\displaystyle\int_{t/2}^{\Omega t}\int|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\leq\delta^{2},

then

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{t}.

Proof.

We may assume
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}.
Let
q\in M.
Then for
\Omega
large enough depending on
\varepsilon
and
K,
(
5.6
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\sqrt{t}}(q)}|F^{+}(\Omega t)|^{2}\leq\frac{K^{2}}{(\Omega t)^{2}}C\left(2\sqrt{t}\right)^{4}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4C^{2}}.

Let
\varphi
be the cutoff function

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{dist}(x,q)\leq\sqrt{t}\\
\frac{2\sqrt{t}-\text{dist}(x,q)}{\sqrt{t}}&\sqrt{t}\leq\text{dist}(x,q)\leq 2\sqrt{t}\\
0&2\sqrt{t}\leq\text{dist}(x,q).\end{cases}

Then by (
3.1
) with this choice of
\varphi,
we have for
\tau\in[t/2,\Omega t]

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\sqrt{t}}(q)}|F^{+}(\tau)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{2\sqrt{t}}(q)}|F^{+}(\Omega t)|^{2}+\int_{t/2}^{\tau}\hskip-1.99997pt\int|D^{\ast}F|^{2}+4\left(\int_{t/2}^{\tau}\hskip-1.99997pt\int|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{t/2}^{\tau}\hskip-1.99997pt\int|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|F^{+}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4C^{2}}+\delta^{2}+C\delta E_{0}\sqrt{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq(\varepsilon/C)^{2},

provided
\delta>0
is small enough depending on
\varepsilon,E_{0},
and
K.
Since
q\in M
was arbitrary, epsilon-regularity yields

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{t},

as desired.
∎

We now establish a uniform time after which
\|F^{+}(t)\|_{2}
is less than
\varepsilon_{p}.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose
A(t)
is a solution of (
1.1
) satisfying the hypotheses of Lemma
5.3
. There exists
t_{0}>0,
depending only on

and

such that
\|F^{+}(t_{0})\|_{2}\leq{\varepsilon}_{p}.

Proof.

Take
\Omega,\delta
from Lemma

such that the lemma holds with

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\min\left\{\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{p}}{\sqrt{\|u(0)\|_{p}^{p}+C_{\ref{lemma:Lpcontrolbefore}}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p}},1\right\}

By (

) and (

),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\leq E_{0}^{2},

so there exists an integer

\displaystyle i\in[0,E_{0}^{2}\delta^{-2}+1]

such that

\displaystyle\int_{(2\Omega)^{i}/2}^{\Omega(2\Omega)^{i}}\int_{M}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}\leq\delta^{2}.

Denoting

\displaystyle t_{0}:=(2\Omega)^{i}\geq 1,

we have by Lemma

that

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t_{0})\|_{p}^{\frac{p}{2}}\|u(t_{0})\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|u(0)\|_{p}^{p}+C_{\ref{lemma:Lpcontrolbefore}}t_{0}^{2-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{t_{0}}\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{p}.

The desired claim now follows from Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Finally, we have the following uniform estimate on the

.

6.
Convergence of
A(t)

Finally, we record the following convergence properties of
A(t)
under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
and the preceding section.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
A(t)
is a solution of (
1.1
) satisfying

\displaystyle\|F(0)\|_{2}+\|F^{+}(0)\|_{p}

\displaystyle<\infty,

\displaystyle\|F^{+}(0)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle<\frac{8\pi^{2}}{|\Gamma|}.

Then
A(t)
converges in
C_{\text{loc}}^{\infty}
to an ASD instanton
A_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty.
Moreover,
A(t)
converges to
A_{\infty}
in
L^{q}
for all
q\in(\frac{2p}{2-p},\infty],
and
F(t)
converges to
F_{A_{\infty}}=F_{A_{\infty}}^{-}
in
L^{2}.

Proof.

Since

(6.1)

\|F^{+}(t)\|_{\infty}\in L^{1}([1,\infty)),

it follows from
[
27
, Theorem 2.5]
that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[2,\infty)}\|F(t)\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Moreover, by (
3.1
) and Lemma
5.2
, we have for all
x\in M
that

\displaystyle\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{B_{1}(x)}|D^{\ast}F|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{2}(x)}|F^{+}(t-1)|^{2}+\int_{B_{2}(x)}|F^{+}(t)|^{2}+C\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{B_{2}(x)}|F^{+}|^{2}

\displaystyle=O\left(t^{-\frac{4}{p}}\right)

as
t\to\infty.
In view of the
L^{\infty}
bound on the full curvature (
6.2
), we have for each integer
k\geq 0
and for large
t
the derivative estimates of
[
27
, Prop. 3.2]

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}D^{\ast}F(t)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\sup_{x\in M}\|D^{\ast}F\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}(x)\times[t-1,t])}=O\left(t^{-\frac{2}{p}}\right),

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}F(t)\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Thus by
[
27
, Prop. 3.3]
,
A(t)
converges in
C_{\text{loc}}^{\infty}
to an ASD instanton
A_{\infty}.

Next we show global convergence of
A(t)
to
A_{\infty}
in
L^{q}.
It suffices to show that

\displaystyle\int_{N}^{\infty}\|D^{\ast}F\|_{q}<\infty.

By Hölder’s inequality and the first part of the proof,

\displaystyle\int_{N}^{\infty}\|D^{\ast}F\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{N}^{\infty}\|D^{\ast}F\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{q}}\|D^{\ast}F\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}n^{-\frac{2}{p}(1-\frac{2}{q})}\int_{n}^{n+1}\|D^{\ast}F\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}n^{-\frac{2}{p}(1-\frac{2}{q})}\left(\int_{n}^{n+1}\|D^{\ast}F\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

By (
3.1
), Lemma
5.1
, and Lemma
5.2
,

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}\|D^{\ast}F\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|F^{+}(n)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\|F^{+}(n)\|_{p}^{\frac{p}{2}}\|F^{+}(n)\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{p}{2}})^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F^{+}(n)\|_{\infty}^{2-p}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{2-\frac{4}{p}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{N}^{\infty}\|D^{\ast}F\|_{q}\lesssim\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}n^{\frac{2}{q}-\frac{2}{p}},

which is finite if
q>\frac{2p}{2-p},
as desired.

Finally we note that
F(t)
converges in
L^{2}
to
F_{A_{\infty}}.
Fix
x\in M.
Since

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus B_{R}(x)}|F(t_{2})-F(t_{1})|^{2}\leq 2\int_{M\setminus B_{R}(x)}|F(t_{2})|^{2}+|F(t_{1})|^{2},

and since
F(t)
converges to
F_{A_{\infty}}
in
C_{\text{loc}}^{\infty}
by the first part of the proof, it suffices to show that for any
\varepsilon>0,
there exists
R,T>0,
such that for
t\geq T

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus B_{R}(x)}|F(t)|^{2}\leq\varepsilon.

This follows from (
6.1
) and
[
27
, Theorem 2.5]
, where in the statement we replace
B_{\frac{R}{N}}(x)
with
M\setminus B_{NR}(x)
and
B_{R}(x)
with
M\setminus B_{R}(x),
and in the proof we replace the special cutoff function
\beta_{N,R}
with
1-\beta_{N,NR}.
Since the metric on
M
approaches Euclidean on the end sufficiently fast, the equivalent of
[
27
, Lemma 2.4]
holds for
1-\beta_{N,NR},
so the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 2.5]
goes through.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
c
.

The convergence statement is contained in the previous theorem. By Lemma
3.2
, we have
\kappa(A(t))\equiv\kappa(A_{0})
for all
t<\infty.
Since the curvature converges in

as
t\to\infty,
we also have
\lim_{t\to\infty}\kappa(A(t))=\kappa(A_{\infty}).
We conclude that
\kappa(A_{0})=\kappa(A_{\infty}).

∎
