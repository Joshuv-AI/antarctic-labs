Stochastically forced Navier-Stokes equations interacting with an elastic structure
Felix Brandt, Matthias Hieber, Arnab Roy

Abstract
. We prove global-in-time strong pathwise well-posedness for a stochastic fluid-structure interaction problem coupling a two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes fluid to a one-dimensional damped Kirchhoff plate. The coupling is imposed on a fixed interface through continuity of velocities and balance of normal stresses, and stochastic forcing, modeled by a cylindrical Wiener process, acts on both the fluid and structure equations. We split the problem into a linear stochastic part and a nonlinear deterministic remainder. The linear stochastic problem is treated by proving that the associated fluid-structure operator admits a bounded ℋ ∞ \mathcal{H}^{\infty} -calculus, yielding stochastic maximal regularity. This requires a decoupling procedure for the non-diagonal operator domain, and pressure estimates via suitable lifting constructions. The deterministic remainder is solved locally by quasilinear methods, and the resulting blow-up criterion is ruled out by higher-order a priori estimates. This is the first global-in-time strong pathwise well-posedness result for a stochastically forced Navier-Stokes system interacting with a deformable elastic structure.

1.
Introduction

This paper establishes a strong pathwise well-posedness theory for a stochastically forced Navier-Stokes system interacting with an elastic structure located at a part of the fluid boundary.
The main result is global-in-time existence and uniqueness for a two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes fluid coupled to a one-dimensional damped Kirchhoff plate subject to additive noise.
A central ingredient, and an independent contribution of the paper, is the investigation of the underlying fluid-structure operator.
The main analytical difficulties are the non-diagonal operator domain, and the non-local fluid pressure in the structure equation.
In order to decouple the non-diagonal domain of this operator, we invoke a Stokes lifting to obtain its bounded
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus, which is the basis for treating the stochastic convolution in UMD spaces.
In particular, the stochastic forcing is considered in the space of
\gamma
-radonifying operators.
For handling the pressure, we employ Neumann lifting arguments.
For the global-in-time existence, we establish a blow-up criterion.
The blow-up scenario is ruled out by a priori estimates that differ significantly from the classical Navier-Stokes case:
First, the contributions from the stochastic forcing have to be taken into account.
Second, the coupling with the elastic structure requires new elliptic estimates for the associated stationary Stokes problem.

We now describe the underlying deterministic problem.
For simplicity, and since this does not affect the analysis, all physical constants are normalized to one.
Let
T>0
and let
\mathcal{F}_{0}=(0,L)\times(-1,0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the rectangular fluid domain.
The fluid is assumed to be viscous, incompressible, and Newtonian.
Its velocity and pressure are denoted by
u\colon(0,T)\times\mathcal{F}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\pi\colon(0,T)\times\mathcal{F}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
, respectively.
The fluid equations are given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\mdiv\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(u,\pi)

\displaystyle=0,\kern 5.0pt\mdiv u=0,

\displaystyle\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times\mathcal{F}_{0},

where the fluid stress tensor is defined by
\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(u,\pi)=(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\top})-\pi I_{2}
.
The motion of the structure is described by a damped linear beam equation.
For the structure displacement
\eta\colon(0,T)\times(0,L)\to\mathbb{R}
, this equation reads

(1.2)

\partial_{tt}\eta+\partial_{xxxx}\eta+\partial_{xxxxt}\eta=\phi(u,\pi),\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times(0,L).

The right-hand side
\phi(u,\pi)
in (
1.2
) accounts for the continuity of normal stresses at the interface and is given by
\phi(u,\pi)=-\mathrm{e}_{2}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(u,\pi)\mathrm{e}_{2}
, where
\mathrm{e}_{2}=\binom{0}{1}
denotes the outer unit normal vector on the upper boundary of the fluid domain.

The system is complemented by coupling and boundary conditions.
The kinematic coupling condition expresses equality of the fluid and structure velocities at the interface and reads

(1.3)

u(t,x,0)=\partial_{t}\eta(t,x)\mathrm{e}_{2},\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0ptx\in(0,L).

Moreover, we assume periodicity in the horizontal variable
x
for the fluid, the pressure, and the structure.
Since the fluid is assumed to be at rest at the lower boundary, the boundary conditions are given by

\displaystyle(u,\pi)(t,0,y)

\displaystyle=(u,\pi)(t,L,y),\kern 5.0pt\eta(t,0)=\eta(t,L)

\displaystyle\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0pty\in(-1,0),

\displaystyle u(t,x,-1)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0ptx\in(0,L).

The incompressibility condition in (
1.1
) and the kinematic coupling condition (
1.3
) imply conservation of the spatial average of the beam displacement.
Indeed, invoking the boundary conditions of
u
, for all
t>0
, we get
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{L}\eta(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{L}u(t,x,0)\cdot\mathrm{e}_{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}\mdiv u\,\mathrm{d}(x,y)=0
, so the quantity
m\coloneqq\int_{0}^{L}\eta(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x
is constant in time.
Thus, if the initial displacement
\eta_{1}^{0}(x)=\eta(0,x)
satisfies
\int_{0}^{L}\eta_{1}^{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0
, then the same property is preserved by
\eta(t,\cdot)
for all
t\in(0,T)
.
This motivates the use of the space of functions with zero spatial average
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{m}}^{q}(0,L)
with associated projection
P_{\mathrm{m}}\colon\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,L)\to\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{m}}^{q}(0,L)
, defined by

(1.4)

\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{m}}^{q}(0,L)\coloneqq\left\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,L):\int_{0}^{L}f\,\mathrm{d}x=0\right\},\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptP_{\mathrm{m}}f\coloneqq f-\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}f\,\mathrm{d}x,\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptf\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(0,L).

After applying this projection to the damped beam equation, the deterministic FSI system underlying the present work can be written as

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\mdiv\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(u,\pi)&=0,\kern 5.0pt\mdiv u=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times\mathcal{F}_{0},\\
\partial_{tt}\eta+P_{\mathrm{m}}\partial_{xxxx}\eta+P_{\mathrm{m}}\partial_{xxxxt}\eta&=-P_{\mathrm{m}}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{2}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(u,\pi)\mathrm{e}_{2}\bigr),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times(0,L),\\
u(t,x,0)&=\partial_{t}\eta(t,x)\mathrm{e}_{2},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0ptx\in(0,L),\\
(u,\pi)(t,0,y)&=(u,\pi)(t,L,y),\kern 5.0pt\eta(t,0)=\eta(t,L),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0pty\in(-1,0),\\
u(t,x,-1)&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0ptx\in(0,L),\end{aligned}\right.

supplemented by suitable initial data
u_{0}(x)=u(0,x)
,
\eta_{1}^{0}(x)=\eta(0,x)
, and
\eta_{2}^{0}(x)=\partial_{t}\eta(0,x)
.

The analysis of
stochastic
FSI problems, which is motivated by real-world applications such as blood flow in vessels and the associated question whether FSI problems are robust under stochastic perturbations, is much more recent.
The first result in this direction appears to be due to Kuan and Čanić
[
36
]
, who studied a reduced linearly coupled model described by a stochastic viscous wave equation.
In
[
37
]
, the same authors considered a benchmark stochastic FSI problem coupling a 2D Stokes fluid to a 1D linearly elastic membrane.
Nonlinear stochastic FSI problems on moving domains were then studied by Tawri and Čanić
[
57
]
, who proved the existence of martingale solutions for a 2D Navier-Stokes fluid coupled to an elastic lateral wall.
Further developments include stochastic FSI models with nonzero longitudinal displacement
[
59
]
and Navier-slip boundary conditions
[
58
]
.
In three spatial dimensions, Breit, Mensah, and Moyo
[
13
]
obtained martingale solutions for an incompressible Navier-Stokes fluid interacting with a Koiter-type shell subject to transport noise.
All these works construct solutions in a
weak PDE sense
.
For an overview of deterministic and stochastic FSI problems, we also refer to the recent monograph
[
14
]
.

We now describe the stochastic problem.
Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space endowed with a filtration
\mathfrak{F}=(\mathfrak{F}_{t})_{t}
, and let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space.
The precise definition of an
\mathfrak{F}
-cylindrical Wiener process
W_{\mathcal{H}}\colon\mathrm{L}^{2}((0,\infty);\mathcal{H})\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
will be recalled in
Section 4
.
For an orthonormal basis
(e_{n})_{n}
of
\mathcal{H}
, the processes
\beta_{n}(t)\coloneqq W_{\mathcal{H}}(t)e_{n}
are standard
\mathfrak{F}
-Brownian motions, and one has the representation

(1.6)

W_{\mathcal{H}}(t)\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega),\kern 5.0pt\text{with}\kern 5.0ptW_{\mathcal{H}}(t)f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}(t)\langle f,e_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

The stochastic integral with respect to
W_{\mathcal{H}}
is defined for processes with values in spaces of
\gamma
-radonifying operators; see
[
63
]
and also
[
33
, Ch. 9]
.
The relevant spaces and assumptions will be specified in the context of the linear stochastic problem in
Section 4
.

For suitable stochastic forcing terms
f
and
g
, and for the fluid velocity, the fluid pressure, and the structure displacement being random variables
\tilde{u}\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathcal{F}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\tilde{\pi}\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathcal{F}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\tilde{\eta}\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\times(0,L)\to\mathbb{R}
, the linearly coupled stochastic FSI problem studied in this manuscript reads

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\tilde{u}+(\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{u}\,\mathrm{d}t-\mdiv\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(\tilde{u},\tilde{\pi})\,\mathrm{d}t&=f\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathcal{H}},\kern 5.0pt\mdiv\tilde{u}=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times\mathcal{F}_{0},\\
\mathrm{d}\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\bigl(P_{\mathrm{m}}\partial_{xxxx}\tilde{\eta}+P_{\mathrm{m}}\partial_{xxxxt}\tilde{\eta}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t&=-P_{\mathrm{m}}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{2}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(\tilde{u},\tilde{\pi})\mathrm{e}_{2}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad+g\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathcal{H}},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times(0,L),\\
\tilde{u}(t,x,0)&=\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}(t,x)\mathrm{e}_{2},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0ptx\in(0,L),\\
(\tilde{u},\tilde{\pi})(t,0,y)=(\tilde{u},\tilde{\pi})(t,L,y),\kern 5.0pt\tilde{\eta}(t,0)&=\tilde{\eta}(t,L),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0pty\in(-1,0),\\
\tilde{u}(t,x,-1)&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0ptx\in(0,L),\\
\tilde{u}(0,\cdot)&=\tilde{u}_{0}(\cdot),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{F}_{0},\\
\tilde{\eta}(0,\cdot)&=\tilde{\eta}_{1}^{0}(\cdot),\kern 5.0pt\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}(0,\cdot)=\tilde{\eta}_{2}^{0}(\cdot),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,L).\end{aligned}\right.

The paraphrased main result is as follows.
For the precise statement, see
Theorem 6.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Under the compatibility conditions and regularity assumptions on the data made precise in
Theorem 6.1
, the system (
1.7
) admits a unique, global, strong pathwise solution.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first global-in-time strong pathwise well-posedness result for a stochastically forced Navier-Stokes system coupled to a deformable elastic structure located at the fluid boundary.
In
[
12
]
, local strong well-posedness and blow-up criteria were obtained for a stochastic fluid-rigid body interaction problem.
The present manuscript continues this line of research, but the presence of an elastic boundary structure leads to a substantially different linear theory.
While maximal regularity for certain fluid-structure operators with elastic boundary dynamics has been established in deterministic settings, see for instance
[
44
]
, we prove here the boundedness of the
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus for the fluid-structure operator associated with the damped Kirchhoff plate model.
This operator-theoretic result is of independent interest and is the key input for stochastic maximal regularity.

The strategy for analyzing (
1.7
) is to split the solution into a linear stochastic part and a deterministic nonlinear remainder.
The linear stochastic problem is treated using the theory of stochastic maximal regularity developed by van Neerven, Veraar, and Weis
[
63
,
62
]
.
A substantial part of the paper is thus devoted to proving that the fluid-structure operator
A_{\mathrm{fs}}
, which encodes the Stokes dynamics, the damped plate dynamics, and the kinematic and dynamic coupling conditions, admits a bounded
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus, which paves the way for the stochastic maximal regularity in a second step.
The non-diagonal domain caused by the kinematic coupling condition is handled by a decoupling procedure, while the pressure contribution in the plate equation requires lifting constructions and estimates in negative Sobolev spaces.
The perturbation argument is carried out at the level of the bounded
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus, which is considerably more delicate than for sectoriality or maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity; see
[
45
]
.

The deterministic remainder is handled by quasilinear evolution methods
[
53
,
51
,
52
]
, which yield local well-posedness and a blow-up criterion.
The passage from local to global solutions is not a direct consequence of the standard two-dimensional Navier-Stokes energy inequality.
Indeed, the blow-up criterion is formulated in the interpolation space associated with
A_{\mathrm{fs}}
, and therefore requires uniform control of the fluid
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-norm together with high Sobolev norms of the plate.
We obtain these bounds by combining the basic energy identity with higher-order estimates, elliptic regularity for the coupled Stokes problem, and a time-local absorption argument for the nonlinear convection term.

The manuscript is organized as follows.

Section 2
addresses some preliminaries.
In
Section 3
, we prove the boundedness of the
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus of the fluid-structure operator in this setting.

Section 4
is dedicated to clarifying the stochastic setting and analyzing the linear stochastic problem by combining the bounded
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus of the fluid-structure operator with stochastic maximal regularity theory.
In
Section 5
, we use the solution constructed in
Section 4
to reduce the nonlinear stochastic problem to a deterministic non-autonomous one, and we prove local-in-time well-posedness together with blow-up criteria.
The purpose of
Section 6
is to establish the a priori estimates needed to rule out the blow-up scenario for the deterministic part of the solution, thereby proving global strong pathwise well-posedness of the stochastic FSI problem.
In the final
Section 7
, we discuss well-posedness results in the 3D/2D case.

2.
Preliminaries

The purpose of this section is to introduce some pieces of notation such as function spaces, and to present some auxiliary (deterministic) tools that are needed for the analysis carried out in this section.
This includes the analysis of the Stokes operator on a cylindrical domain as well as the structure operator associated with the present plate problem.

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathbb{P}u^{\prime}&=A_{0}\mathbb{P}(u-D_{\mathrm{fl}}\eta_{2})+\mathbb{P}f,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T),\\

where

denotes the modified trace to the upper boundary

Next, we introduce the so-called added mass operator defined by

.
The result below on the invertibility of

and its mapping properties will be important for the boundedness of the

-calculus of the fluid-structure operator.
Note that this seems to be the first result discussing the added mass operator on Sobolev spaces with negative regularity.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
s\in(\max\{-1,-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{q}\},1)
and
q\in(1,\infty)
.
Then

(1)

the added mass operator
M_{s}=\Id+\gamma_{\mathrm{m}}N_{1}
is an automorphism on
\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s,q}(0,L)
,

(2)

for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, it holds that
M_{s}^{-1}-\Id\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s,q}(0,L),\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s+1-\varepsilon,q}(0,L))
, and

(3)

the operator
M_{s}^{-1}-\Id
is compact on
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{m}}^{q}(0,L)
.

Proof.

From (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we get
N_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s,q}(0,L),\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s+1+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}-\varepsilon,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))
.
In particular,
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen sufficiently small such that
s+1+\frac{1}{q}-\varepsilon>\frac{1}{q}
.
Thus, the trace and then also
\gamma_{\mathrm{m}}
are well-defined on
\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s+1+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}-\varepsilon,q}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, and we obtain

(3.8)

\gamma_{\mathrm{m}}N_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s,q}(0,L),\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s+1-\varepsilon,q}(0,L)),

where
s+1-\varepsilon>0
.
In order to show the assertion of (a), it hence suffices to show that
\ker M_{s}=\{0\}
.
To this end, let
f\in\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s,q}(0,L)
, and suppose that
f\in\ker M_{s}
.
In particular, we find that
f=-\gamma_{\mathrm{m}}N_{1}f
.
Together with
s>-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{q}
and a choice of
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that
s-\varepsilon>-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{q}
as well as the resulting Sobolev embedding
\mathrm{W}^{s+1-\varepsilon,q}(0,L)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)
, this yields that
f\in\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{s+1-\varepsilon,q}(0,L)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}(0,L)
.
Therefore,
N_{1}f\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{m}}^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, and setting
g\coloneqq N_{1}f
, we test the identity
(\Id+\gamma_{\mathrm{m}}N_{1})f=0
by
f
, integrate by parts, and exploit the definition of
g=N_{1}f
to get

(3.9)

0=\int_{0}^{L}[(\Id+\gamma_{\mathrm{m}}N_{1})f]f\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{L}f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}|\nabla g|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}(x,y).

This implies that

and thus
\ker M_{s}=\{0\}
, completing the proof of (a).
Let us now invoke

M_{s}^{-1}-\Id=M_{s}^{-1}-M_{s}^{-1}(\Id+\gamma_{\mathrm{m}}N_{1})=-M_{s}^{-1}\gamma_{\mathrm{m}}N_{1}.

The assertion of (b) then follows from (
3.8
) together with (a), while for (c), we additionally use the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem for the compactness of the Sobolev embedding.
∎

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{P}u\\
\eta_{1}\\

\eta_{1}\\

-calculus of the fluid-structure operator

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first such result in the situation of a(n) (visco-)elastic structure located at a part of the fluid boundary, and this result is of independent interest.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
.
Then
-A_{\mathrm{fs}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathrm{X}_{0})
with
\phi_{-A_{\mathrm{fs}}}^{\infty}<\frac{\pi}{2}

The proof of

consists of two parts:
showing that the assertion of

is valid up to a shift, and discussing the spectral properties of

.
For the first part addressed in

, the main difficulty arises from the non-diagonal domain of

.
To overcome this, we use a decoupling approach based on a similarity transform.
We then decompose the decoupled operator matrix with diagonal domain into a main part handled by means of theory for block operator matrices and a perturbative part.

Proposition 3.7

Let

.
Then there is

such that

We first make precise the decoupling argument.

0&0&\Id\end{pmatrix}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptS^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\Id&0&\mathbb{P}D_{\mathrm{fl}}\\

\displaystyle\frac{13}{16}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{\tau}u(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{\tau\tau}\eta(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{xxt}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\partial_{\tau}u\,\mathrm{d}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{xx\tau}\eta(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau-\int_{0}^{L}\partial_{xx}\eta(t)\partial_{xxt}\eta(t)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}B_{U}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau+C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|u(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{4}+\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{4}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

where
\tilde{C}_{0}
depends only on the initial data
u_{0}
,
\eta_{1}^{0}
, and
\eta_{2}^{0}
.

The term

\int_{0}^{t}B_{U}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

is bounded for every
t\in(0,T)
due to
B_{U}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T)
as observed above, while the term

\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{xx\tau}\eta(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

is bounded by
C_{0}+C_{1}(T)
for every
t\in(0,T)
by virtue of
Subsection 6.1
.
Moreover, by Young’s inequality and
(
6.3
), for every
t\in(0,T)
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{L}\partial_{xx}\eta(t)\partial_{xxt}\eta(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{xx}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\partial_{xxt}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(C_{0}+C_{1}(T))+\frac{1}{4}\|\partial_{xxt}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}.

It remains to estimate the convective term. By Young’s inequality,

(6.18)

\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\partial_{\tau}u\,\mathrm{d}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|((u\cdot\nabla)u)(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{\tau}u(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

The idea is to absorb the term involving the time derivative into the left-hand side, and to handle the convective term with Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality, which requires controlling the Laplacian, so
Subsection 6.2
will be used.
We first handle the terms involving
U
.
To this end, we recall that
U\in\mathrm{L}^{4}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))
, and for
\theta\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{3})
, it follows from (
6.1
) that

U\in\mathrm{H}^{\theta}(0,T;\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{2(1-\theta)}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{4}(0,T;\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{1,3}(\mathcal{F}_{0})).

Hölder’s, and Young’s inequalities, the embedding
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{6}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, and Poincaré’s inequality imply

\displaystyle\|(U\cdot\nabla)u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2},

\displaystyle\|(u\cdot\nabla)U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\|\nabla U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+\|\nabla U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2},\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt

\displaystyle\|(U\cdot\nabla)U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\|U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\|\nabla U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+\|\nabla U\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}.

We set
f=-\partial_{t}u-((u+U)\cdot\nabla)(u+U)
.
Using the above estimates, we get

(6.19)

\|f(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\leq\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}+\|((u\cdot\nabla)u)(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}+C_{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+c_{U}(t),

where
c_{U}(t)\coloneqq C_{1}\bigl(\|U(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+\|\nabla U(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+\|\nabla U(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\bigr)
.
By
Subsection 6.2
applied to a Stokes system of the form (
6.10
) with right-hand side
f
and (
6.19
), we then obtain

(6.20)

\displaystyle\|\Delta u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(t)\Bigl(\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}+\|((u\cdot\nabla)u)(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}+\|\partial_{t}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,L)}+C_{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+c_{U}(t)\Bigr),

where
C^{\prime}(t)
is a nondecreasing function of time, bounded on bounded intervals.

By Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality in two dimensions,

\|(u\cdot\nabla)u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}.

Combining this with (
6.20
) and using
\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,L)}\leq C\|\partial_{xxt}\eta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}
, for
Y(t)=\|((u\cdot\nabla)u)(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}
, we get

Y(t)\leq\tilde{C}^{\prime}(t)A(t)\bigl(\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}+Y^{1/2}(t)+\|\partial_{xxt}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+c_{U}(t)\bigr),

where
\tilde{C}^{\prime}(t)
is again nondecreasing in
t
and bounded on
(0,T)
, and
A(t)=\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}
.
Applying Young’s inequality to the term
A(t)Y^{1/2}(t)
and absorbing
\frac{1}{2}Y(t)
into the left-hand side yields

(6.21)

\displaystyle\|((u\cdot\nabla)u)(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}^{\prime}(t)C_{3}\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{4}+\frac{1}{8}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{xxt}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{4}+c_{U}^{2}(t),

where
c_{U}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T)
, and hence
c_{U}^{2}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,T)
by the above arguments.

Using the basic energy estimate (
6.3
) to control
\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,t;\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0}))}^{2}
and
\|\partial_{xxt}\eta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,t;\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L))}^{2}
, this implies

(6.22)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\partial_{\tau}u\,\,\mathrm{d}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9}{16}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{\tau}u(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{4}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+C_{T},

where
C_{T}
depends on
C_{0}+C_{1}(T)
,
\tilde{C}^{\prime}(T)
, and the above norms of
U
.

Inserting the previous estimates into (
6.17
), and decreasing the positive constants on the left-hand side if necessary, gives, for every
t\in(0,T)
, the estimate

(6.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{\tau}u(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{\tau\tau}\eta(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\frac{1}{4}\|\partial_{xx\tau}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{0}+C_{T}+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{4}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

where by a slight abuse of notation, the constant
C_{T}
may be different from the estimate (
6.22
).

We close the estimate by absorption.
As a preparation, we define

F(a,b)\coloneqq\int_{a}^{b}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

The basic energy estimate yields that
F(a,b)<\infty
, and the absolute continuity of the integral implies that
F(a,b)\to 0
as
b-a\to 0
.

For
0\leq a<b\leq T
, define

X(a,b)\coloneqq\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\partial_{xxt}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{a}^{b}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{a}^{b}\|\partial_{tt}\eta(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

For suitable constants
D_{0}(a)
and
D_{1}(b)
, which is nondecreasing in
b
, (
6.23
) yields that

\displaystyle X(a,b)

\displaystyle\leq D_{0}(a)+D_{1}(b)+D_{1}(b)\int_{a}^{b}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq D_{0}(a)+D_{1}(b)+D_{1}(b)F(a,b)X(a,b).

Now, choosing
b>a
such that
D_{1}(b)F(a,b)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we get
X(a,b)\leq 2D_{0}(a)+2D_{1}(b)
.
Since
F(0,T)<\infty
, by absolute continuity of the integral we may
choose a finite partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=T

with

D_{1}(T)F(t_{j-1},t_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}\qquad\text{for every }j=1,\dots,N.

Applying the preceding estimate successively on each interval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, and using the already obtained endpoint bound as part of
the initial constant
D_{0}(t_{j-1})
, yields
X(0,T)<\infty
.
∎

Estimate for the structure displacement

The purpose of this subsection is to prove the bound for

in

\|\phi(u,\pi)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(0,L)}\leq C\bigl(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}+\|\partial_{t}\eta\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,L)}\bigr).

In the proposition below, we discuss the estimate of

Proposition 6.6
.

Let

be as specified in

Testing (

)

\int_{0}^{L}\partial_{tt}\eta\,\partial_{xxxx}\eta\,\mathrm{d}x+\|\partial_{xxxx}\eta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\partial_{xxxx}\eta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}=\int_{0}^{L}\phi(u,\pi)\partial_{xxxx}\eta\,\mathrm{d}x.

The first term on the left-hand side is estimated by Young’s inequality:

For the right-hand side, Young’s inequality as well as

applied to the case of a right-hand side

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\partial_{xxxx}\eta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{xxxx}\eta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{tt}\eta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+C\|\partial_{t}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+\bigl(C^{\prime}(t)C_{3}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}\bigr)\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{0})}^{4}

The right-hand side belongs to
\mathrm{L}^{1}(0,t)
for every

Therefore, integrating (

7.
Local-in-time well-posedness and blow-up criteria in the 3D/2D case

In this section, we elaborate on the local-in-time pathwise well-posedness of the 3D/2D linearly coupled stochastic FSI problem, and we also provide blow-up criteria in the end.

First, let us make precise the setting in the present case.
Here, the underlying domain will be
\mathcal{G}_{0}=(0,L)\times(0,L)\times(-1,0)
, and we still use
\tilde{u}
,
\tilde{\pi}
, and
\tilde{\eta}
to denote the fluid velocity and pressure as well as the plate displacement, respectively.
To shorten notation, we will also write
G=(0,L)\times(0,L)
in this case, so
\mathcal{G}_{0}=G\times(-1,0)
.
The space
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{m}}^{q}(G)
is defined in the same way as
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{m}}^{q}(0,L)
in (
1.4
), while by a slight abuse of notation, we will still denote the associated projection in the wake of (
1.4
) by
P_{\mathrm{m}}
.
Another relevant piece of notation will be
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}f\coloneqq\partial_{xx}f+\partial_{yy}f
, while in this case,
\nabla
and
\mdiv
represent the gradient and divergence in 3D in this case.
Moreover, we observe that the definition of periodic functions is analogous to the 2D/1D case.

With these preparations, and for the stochastic setting as specified around (
1.6
), the full stochastic problem is given by

(7.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\tilde{u}+(\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{u}\,\mathrm{d}t-\mdiv\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(\tilde{u},\tilde{\pi})\,\mathrm{d}t&=f\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathcal{H}},,\kern 5.0pt\mdiv\tilde{u}=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times\mathcal{G}_{0},\\
\mathrm{d}\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}+\bigl(P_{\mathrm{m}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}\tilde{\eta}+P_{\mathrm{m}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}\tilde{\eta}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t&=-P_{\mathrm{m}}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{3}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(\tilde{u},\tilde{\pi})\mathrm{e}_{3}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad+g\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathcal{H}},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times G,\\
\tilde{u}(t,x,y,0)&=\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}(t,x,y)\mathrm{e}_{3},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0pt(x,y)\in G,\\
\tilde{u}(t,x,y,-1)&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0pt(x,y)\in G,\\
\tilde{u}(0,\cdot)&=\tilde{u}_{0}(\cdot),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{G}_{0},\\
\tilde{\eta}(0,\cdot)&=\tilde{\eta}_{1}^{0}(\cdot),\kern 5.0pt\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}(0,\cdot)=\tilde{\eta}_{2}^{0}(\cdot),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0ptG,\end{aligned}\right.

and similarly as in (
1.7
), the problem is complemented by periodic boundary conditions at the lateral boundary of the domain.
In the above, the fluid velocity, the fluid pressure, and the structure displacement are again random variables, i.e.,

\tilde{u}\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathcal{G}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{3},\kern 5.0pt\tilde{\pi}\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathcal{G}_{0}\to\mathbb{R},\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\tilde{\eta}\colon(0,T)\times\Omega\times G\to\mathbb{R}.

Let us now elaborate on the associated linear stochastic problem and its solvability.
Using the same notation as in the 2D/1D case for the variables, and denoting by
\Delta_{s}^{2}
the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-realization of
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{2}
subject to periodic boundary conditions, the underlying linear stochastic problem is given by

(7.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}U-\mdiv\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(U,\Pi)\,\mathrm{d}t&=f\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathcal{H}},\kern 5.0pt\mdiv U=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times\mathcal{G}_{0},\\
\mathrm{d}H_{1}&=H_{2}\,\mathrm{d}t,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times G,\\
\mathrm{d}H_{2}+\bigl(P_{\mathrm{m}}\Delta_{s}^{2}H_{1}+P_{\mathrm{m}}\Delta_{s}^{2}H_{2}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t&=-P_{\mathrm{m}}\bigl(\mathrm{e}_{3}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(U,\Pi)\mathrm{e}_{3}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad+g\,\mathrm{d}W_{\mathcal{H}},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times G,\\
U(t,x,y,0)&=H_{2}(t,x,y)\mathrm{e}_{3},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0pt(x,y)\in G,\\
U(t,x,y,-1)&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0pt(x,y)\in G,\\
U(0,\cdot)&=U_{0}(\cdot),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{G}_{0},\\
H_{1}(0,\cdot)&=H_{1}^{0}(\cdot),\kern 5.0ptH_{2}(0,\cdot)=H_{2}^{0}(\cdot),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0ptG,\end{aligned}\right.

supplemented by periodic boundary conditions at the lateral boundary.
By adapting the approach from
Section 3
, we find that the corresponding linear deterministic problem can be reformulated in terms of a fluid-structure operator, and similar as in
Theorem 3.6
, it is possible to show the boundedness of its
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus.
This paves the way for the solvability of (
7.2
) in an analogous manner as in
Section 4
.
The regularity properties of this solution will be made precise in the statement of the theorem below.

Next, as in the 2D/1D case, it is possible to reduce the problem to a deterministic one, given by

(7.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\mdiv\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(u,\pi)&=-\bigl((u+U)\cdot\nabla\bigr)(u+U),\kern 5.0pt\mdiv u=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times\mathcal{G}_{0},\\
\partial_{t}\eta_{1}&=\eta_{2},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times G,\\
\partial_{t}\eta_{2}+P_{\mathrm{m}}\Delta_{s}^{2}\eta_{1}&=-P_{\mathrm{m}}\Delta_{s}^{2}\eta_{2}-P_{\mathrm{m}}(\mathrm{e}_{3}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{f}}(u,\pi)\mathrm{e}_{3}),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,T)\times G,\\
u(t,x,y,0)&=\eta_{2}(t,x,y)\mathrm{e}_{3},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0pt(x,y)\in G,\\
u(t,x,y,-1)&=0,&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,T),\kern 5.0pt(x,y)\in G,\\
u(0,\cdot)&=u_{0}(\cdot),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\mathcal{G}_{0},\\
\eta_{1}(0,\cdot)&=\eta_{1}^{0}(\cdot),\kern 5.0pt\eta_{2}(0,\cdot)=\eta_{2}^{0}(\cdot),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0ptG,\end{aligned}\right.

where periodic boundary conditions at the lateral boundary are taken into account.

Let us now state the local-in-time pathwise strong well-posedness in the present 3D/2D case.
For simplicity of the statement, we focus here on the situation of a Hilbert space for the deterministic part of the solution.
