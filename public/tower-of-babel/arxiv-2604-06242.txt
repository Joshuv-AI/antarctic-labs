On the Double Lambert Series Conjecture of Andrews–Dixit–Schultz–Yee Thanks: ORCID:0009-0001-7695-2599
Qianwen Fang

Abstract
. Andrews, Dixit, Schultz, and Yee conjecture the parity of a double Lambert series. In 2026, Amdeberhan, Andrews, and Ballantine offer some ideas that are pointing in the right direction for the proof. In this paper, we complete the rest of their proof.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B65, 11P81

1.
Introduction

In
[
2
]
, Andrews, Dixit, Schultz, and Yee proposed the following conjecture regarding the parity of a double Lambert series.

Conjecture 1.1
.

The following is an odd function of
q
,
|q|<1
:

\displaystyle Y(q):=\sum_{m,n\geqslant 1}\frac{(-q)^{2mn+m}}{(1+q^{m})(1-q^{2m-1})}.

As shown in
[
1
]
,
Y(q)
can be expressed as

(1.1)

\displaystyle Y(q)=-\sum_{k\geqslant 2}\frac{q^{k}}{1+q^{2k-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\frac{q^{n}}{1+q^{n}},

However, the authors were unable to complete the proof of Conjecture
1.1
using this representation. The difficulty in the final step may arise from the formulation in (
1.1
); instead, we consider the following form:

(1.2)

\displaystyle Y(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geqslant 1\\
k,l\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{m+k}q^{2mn+nk+2ml+m-l}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geqslant 1\\
k\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{m+k}q^{3m+k}}{(1-q^{2m-1})(1-q^{2m+k})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{m}q^{m}}{1-q^{2m-1}}\sum_{k=2m}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}.

Building on the approach in
[
1
, Theorem 5.7]
, we provide the remaining steps to complete the proof.

After this paper was written, we learned from George Andrews that Cui and Tang had also independently proven Conjecture
1.1
using a similar approach.

Throughout this paper, we adopt the standard notation for the
q
-Pochhammer symbol

(a;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n}).

Furthermore, for the product of multiple
q
-Pochhammer symbols, we use the following condensed notation:

(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k};q)_{\infty}:=(a_{1};q)_{\infty}(a_{2};q)_{\infty}\dots(a_{k};q)_{\infty}.

2.
Proof of Conjecture
1.1

We define the following auxiliary functions

\displaystyle Z(q):=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{m}q^{m}}{1-q^{2m-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{2m-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}},

\displaystyle A(q):=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{j+1}}{(1+q^{2i+1})(1+q^{2j+1})},

\displaystyle B(q):=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{i+2j+2}}{(1+q^{2i+1})(1+q^{2j+1})},

\displaystyle B_{1}(q):=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{q^{i+2j+2}}{(1+q^{2i+1})(1+q^{2j+1})},

\displaystyle D_{1}(q):=Y(q)+Z(q)=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{m}q^{m}}{1-q^{2m-1}}\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}},

and

\displaystyle D_{2}(q):=B(q)+B_{1}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\frac{q^{i}}{1+q^{2i+1}}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\frac{q^{2j+2}}{1+q^{2j+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{m}q^{m}}{1-q^{2m-1}}\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{2k}}.

The last equality can be verified in a similar fashion to (
1.2
). A direct calculation reveals that

\displaystyle Z(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geqslant 1\\
m,i,j\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{m+k+1}q^{3k+m+2mi+2ki+2kj-i-j-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,k\geqslant 1\\
i,j\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{m+k}q^{3k+m+2mi+2ki+2kj-i}

\displaystyle=A(q)+B(q).

Consequently, we obtain the relation

Y(q)=D_{1}(q)-D_{2}(q)-A(q)+B_{1}(q).

Conjecture
1.1
then follows from the two lemmas below.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have the following identity:

B_{1}(q)=A(-q).

Proof.

\displaystyle B_{1}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\geqslant 0}\frac{q^{i+3j+2}}{(1+q^{2i+2j+1})(1+q^{2j+1})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n,i,j\geqslant 0}(-1)^{m+n}q^{2im+2jm+2jn+n+i+m+3j+2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n,i,j\geqslant 0}(-1)^{i+j}(-q)^{2im+2jm+2jn+n+i+m+3j+2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geqslant 0}\frac{(-q)^{m+n+2}}{(1+(-q)^{2n+2m+3})(1+(-q)^{2m+1})}

\displaystyle=A(-q).

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The following relation holds:

D_{1}(q)-D_{2}(q)=q\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}^{4}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}q^{2k}}{1-q^{2k}}.

Proof.

Recall the following well-known identity from
q
-series theory (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Entry 29]
):

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{x^{n}}{1-yq^{n}}=\frac{(q,q,xy,q/xy;q)_{\infty}}{(x,q/x,y,q/y;q)_{\infty}},

Setting appropriate parameters, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{m}q^{m}}{1-q^{2m-1}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{m}q^{m}}{1-q^{2m-1}}=q\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}^{4}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}.

Furthermore, by observing the terms of
D_{1}(q)
and
D_{2}(q)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}-\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{k}q^{k}}{1-q^{2k}}=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}q^{2k}}{1-q^{2k}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Future work

The proof of Conjecture
1.1
would be greatly simplified if the following conjecture could be established through a more elementary method.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Y(q)=D_{2}(q)-D_{1}(q).
