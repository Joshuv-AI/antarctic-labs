Abstract
. In this work, we study local minimizers of elliptic functionals with strong absorption terms and unbounded, sign-changing sources. These problems naturally interpolate between two classical free boundary problems: Bernoulli-type (cavity) and obstacle-type. While previous studies have focused on bounded and strictly positive sources, we extend sharp regularity and nondegeneracy estimates to the unbounded, sign-changing setting, providing a comprehensive analysis of how the underlying nonlinearity interacts with minimal integrability assumptions on the source.

1.
Introduction

In this work we study nonnegative solutions of the semilinear elliptic equation

(1.1)

\mathrm{div}(A(x)\nabla u)=\gamma f(x)u^{\gamma-1}\quad\text{in }\{u>0\}\cap B_{1},

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
A(x)
is a uniformly elliptic matrix with measurable coefficients, and

f
belongs to the Lorentz-space
L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})
.
The nonlinearity
u^{\gamma-1}
introduces a singular absorption term as
u\to 0
; as a consequence,
solutions may develop a free boundary separating the positivity set
\{u>0\}
from the zero set
\{u=0\}
.

Equations of this type arise naturally in physical contexts such as combustion
models, flow through porous media, and chemical reaction processes. From a
mathematical perspective, problems of the form (
1.1
)
interpolate between two classical free boundary regimes. In the limiting case

\gamma=0
, the equation reduces to a cavity-type free boundary problem,
which is closely related to the Bernoulli problem studied by Alt and Caffarelli

[
1
]
. Conversely, when
\gamma=1
, the absorption term behaves
as the characteristic function
\chi_{\{u>0\}}
, and the problem becomes
one of obstacle type. In this sense, equation (
1.1
) provides a natural
bridge between these two fundamental and well-studied classes of free
boundary problems.

The classical model associated with (
1.1
), corresponding to the case
A\equiv I
and
f

positive and bounded, was introduced and studied by Alt and Phillips in
[
2
]
.
Under these assumptions, solutions are locally smooth, and the free boundary
\partial\{u>0\}

enjoys a rich and well-understood geometric structure.
Since then, a large body of work has extended this theory in various directions
(see, e.g.,
[
5
,
13
,
12
,
3
,
4
]
),
but invariably under strong structural assumptions on the source term
f
.
In contrast, many contemporary models involve highly irregular, unbounded,
or even sign-changing sources, for which the classical regularity theory no longer applies

[
6
,
9
,
8
]
.

Motivated by these considerations, we study (
1.1
) under the weaker assumption

f\in L^{p,\infty}(B_{1}),

with no sign restriction on
f
. The Lorentz-space condition identifies the critical integrability scale under which the singular absorption term remains controlled.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
we introduce the
mathematical framework and basic tools used throughout the text, and state the main results. In
Section
3
, we establish the existence and boundedness of
nonnegative weak solutions. Section
4
proves the local
Hölder regularity of minimizers, extending classical results to the
setting of Lorentz-space sources. Section
5

focuses on regularity estimates at the free boundary, yielding sharp growth
rates. Section
6
addresses nondegeneracy by providing
quantitative lower bounds near the free boundary. Finally, Section

7
illustrates these findings with a radial example
that demonstrates the sharpness of our estimates.

2.
Assumptions and Main results

In this section, we introduce the notation, assumptions, and functional framework used throughout the paper. And we state and comment the main results of this paper.

2.1.
Notation and assumptions

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
the
n
-dimensional
Euclidean space and consider an open domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a radius
r>0
,
we let
B_{r}(x_{0})
represent the open ball
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|<r\}
. When
x_{0}=0
, we simply write
B_{r}
.

We recall that for
p\geq 1
, a measurable function
f
belongs to the space
L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)
(also known as the weak
L^{p}
or Lorentz space) if

\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)}:=\sup_{t>0}\,t\,\big|\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>t\}\big|^{1/p}<\infty,

where
|E|
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of a measurable
set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. These spaces provide a natural framework for handling source terms with singular growth and arise frequently in the analysis of variational functionals with singular weights.

The variational problem:
We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(B_{1})
the class of symmetric matrix-valued functions

A:B_{1}\to\mathrm{Sym}(n)
that are uniformly elliptic, i.e.,

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle A(x)\,\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ and a.e. }x\in B_{1},

for some fixed constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty
.

In this paper, we consider minimizers of the functional

(2.1)

J_{A,f}(v):=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{2}\langle A(x)\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle+f(x)(v^{+})^{\gamma}\,dx,

where
A(x)\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(B_{1})
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
, and
f\in L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})
, for
p>\frac{n}{2}.
The minimum is taken over the admissible set

\mathbb{K}:=\left\{v\in W^{1,2}(B_{1}):v-g\in W_{0}^{1,2}(B_{1})\right\},

where
g\in H^{1}(B_{1})
is a given boundary datum. For simplicity, we shall write
J
instead of
J_{A,f}
whenever there is no ambiguity.

Throughout the paper, we will assume that the boundary data
g
satisfies

(2.2)

g\in C(\overline{B_{1}}),\quad g\geq 0,\;g\not\equiv 0,

so as to avoid technicalities related to traces and
representatives on
\partial B_{1}
, unless otherwise stated.

2.2.
Main Results

The novelty of our work lies in extending the classical theory of Alt and Phillips,
[
2
]
, to the setting of Lorentz-type sources, which includes singular potentials of the form
f(x)\sim|x-y_{0}|^{-n/p}
.
A fundamental question is whether the regularity of minimizers is preserved in this singular setting. While local Hölder continuity is well-established for bounded sources
f
, our first main result shows that this property persists for

f\in L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})
, establishing the necessary analytic foundation
for investigating the free boundary:

Theorem 1

(Local Hölder Regularity)
.

Let
u
be a local minimizer of (
2.1
). There exist universal constants
0<\beta\ll 1
and
C_{2}>0
, such that
u\in C^{0,\beta}(B_{1/2})
and

(2.3)

\|u\|_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{1/2})}\leq C_{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}.

A key observation is that local minimizers of
J_{A,f}
are weak solutions to the elliptic equation (
1.1
) in the positivity set
\{u>0\}
. Consequently, any universal regularity estimate for minimizers must respect the maximum regularity dictated by the singular source term
f\in L^{p,\infty}
and the nonlinearity exponent
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
Building on this, we obtain
sharp regularity estimates
at free boundary points, showing that solutions belong to the class
C^{\theta}
with the optimal exponent

\theta=\frac{2-\frac{n}{p}}{2-\gamma}.

This extends the classical Alt–Phillips regularity theory to the setting of unbounded, sign-changing sources (Section
5
). More precisely, we have the following result:

Theorem 2

(Regularity at the Free Boundary)
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative minimizer of (
2.1
) in
B_{1}
, and let
x_{0}\in\partial\{u>0\}\cap B_{1/2}
. Then,

u(x)\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}|x-x_{0}|^{\theta}\quad\text{for all }|x-x_{0}|<\rho_{*}/4,

where
\rho_{*}\in(0,1/8)
and
C>0
depend only on dimension, ellipticity constants,
\gamma
, and
p
.

Compared to the discontinuous regime corresponding to
\gamma=0
,
recently studied in
[
11
]
,
the singular nonlinearity
(u^{+})^{\gamma}
introduces new challenges in understanding how solutions detach from the free boundary. In particular, the nonlinearity prevents the direct use of linear barrier arguments. As a result, the growth of solutions near the free boundary depends on the local mass of the forcing term.

The following theorem addresses these difficulties, providing a quantitative lower bound on the solution near free boundary points where the source carries sufficient mass:

Theorem 3

(Nondegeneracy)
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative weak solution to (
1.1
). Suppose
f\geq C_{0}
for
C_{0}>0
in
B_{1}
, then for any
y_{0}\in\partial\{u>0\}
with
B_{2r}(y_{0})\in B_{1}
,

(2.4)

\sup_{\partial B_{r}(y_{0})}u\geq\tilde{c}r^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},

where
\tilde{c}>0
depends only on
n,\gamma,C_{0}
, and the ellipticity constants.

A key challenge arises at free boundary points
y_{0}\in\partial\{u>0\}
where the source term
f
blows up too rapidly. However, if the location of such a point is known, the assumptions on
f
in its vicinity can be
significantly relaxed. Specifically, we demonstrate that the concentration of mass near
y_{0}
compensates for the lack of global boundedness, allowing for a stronger non-degeneracy estimate near
y_{0}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative weak solution to (
1.1
). Suppose
y_{0}\in\partial\{u>0\}
is an interior free boundary point such that
B_{4r_{0}}(y_{0})\in B_{1}
. If
f
satisfies the following assumption in
B_{4r_{0}}(y_{0})
: There exist
\tau\in(1-4^{-n},1)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,8^{-n})
such that

(2.5)

\left|\{y\in B_{4r}(y_{0}):f(y)\geq r^{-n/p}\}\right|\geq\tau|B_{4r}(y_{0})|,\;\forall\;0<r<r_{0}

and

\int_{B_{r}(y_{0})}|f_{-}(x)|dx\leq\varepsilon r^{n-n/p}.

Then we have

(2.6)

\sup_{\partial B_{2r}(y_{0})}u\geq\tilde{c}r^{(2-n/p)/(2-\gamma)},\quad 0<r<r_{0}.

where
\tilde{c}>0
depending on
n
,
\gamma
,
p
and elliptic constants.

Remark 2.1
.

Theorem
3
assumes a positive lower bound on
f
and provides a non-degeneracy estimate around all interior free boundary points. Theorem
4
is based on the assumption of
f
near a known free boundary point and gives the sharp nondegeneracy index. Both theorems are more general than previous results.

Our results provide a comprehensive picture of the qualitative behavior of minimizers for the Alt–Phillips equation with singular or sign-changing sources, extending classical free boundary theory to the Lorentz-space setting. In particular, we show that several core features of the Alt–Phillips theory—such as local Hölder regularity, sharp growth near the free boundary, and nondegeneracy—persist far beyond the classical bounded setting, even in the presence of highly singular data and minimal regularity of the coefficients. We expect that these results will serve as a foundation for further developments in the regularity theory of singular free boundary problems, with potential applications in both mathematics and applied sciences.

2.3.
Auxiliarity results

In this subsection, we establish foundational results that are utilized repeatedly throughout the paper.

We begin with a classical embedding theorem for Lorentz spaces:

Lemma 1

(Weak embedding)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable set and
f\in L^{p,\infty}(E)
for some
p>0
. Then, for every
0<r<p
, we have

(2.7)

\|f\|_{L^{r}(E)}\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-r}\right)^{1/r}|E|^{\frac{p-r}{pr}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(E)}.

Proof.

The result follows from the layer-cake representation and the definition of the weak
L^{p}
norm:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{r}(E)}^{r}

\displaystyle=r\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{r-1}|\{x\in E:|f(x)|>t\}|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}rt^{r-1}|E|dt+r\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}t^{r-1}\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(E)}}{t}\right)^{p}\!dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{1}^{r}|E|+\frac{r}{p-r}\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}^{p}t_{1}^{r-p}

By choosing
t_{1}=\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}}/|E|^{1/p}
we obtain (
2.7
).
∎

The next estimate plays a central role in our arguments.

Lemma 2
.

Assume that
n\geq 2
,
p>\tfrac{n}{2}
, and
\gamma\in(0,2)
.
Let
E\subset B
be a measurable set, and let
f\in L^{p,\infty}(E)
.
Then for any
u\in H^{1}(B)
, the following estimate holds: For
n\geq 3
, there is a constant
C_{1}(n,p,\gamma)>0
such that

(2.8)

\left|\int_{E}f\,u^{\gamma}\,dx\right|\leq C_{1}\,\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(E)}\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(E)}^{\gamma}\;|E|^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}.

For
n=2
, given
\epsilon>0
, there is a constant
C_{2}(p,\gamma,\epsilon)>0
such that

(2.9)

|\int_{E}fu^{\gamma}dx|\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(E)}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{\gamma}|E|^{1-\frac{1}{p}-\epsilon}.

Proof.

Since
p>\frac{n}{2}
,

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{(n-2)\gamma}{2n}<1

for
\gamma\in(0,2)
.
Let
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
for
n>2
and
2^{*}
be a large constant for
n=2
. Then by Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
1
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E}f\,u^{\gamma}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{E}|f(x)|^{\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-\gamma}}dx\right)^{\frac{2^{*}-\gamma}{2^{*}}}\left(\int_{E}|u|^{2^{*}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(E)}\,\left(\int_{E}|u|^{2^{*}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2^{*}}}|E|^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2^{*}}-\frac{1}{p}}

Then for
n\geq 3
, there exists
C_{1}(p,n,\gamma)>0
such that

|\int_{E}fu^{\gamma}dx|\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(E)}\|u\|^{\gamma}_{H^{1}}|E|^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}.

For
n=2
choosing
2^{*}
as
2^{*}=\gamma/\epsilon
we have

|\int_{E}fu^{\gamma}dx|\leq C_{2}(p,\gamma,\epsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(E)}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{\gamma}|E|^{1-\frac{1}{p}-\epsilon}.

Here we further note that we choose
\epsilon>0
small to make
2^{*}>2
.
∎

To conclude this section, we recall some useful inequalities that will be used throughout the paper. For
a,b\geq 0
, the following hold:

a^{r}+b^{r}\leq(a+b)^{r}\leq 2^{r-1}(a^{r}+b^{r}),\quad r\geq 1,

and

2^{r-1}(a^{r}+b^{r})\leq(a+b)^{r}\leq a^{r}+b^{r},\quad r\in(0,1).

3.
Existence and boundedness

In this section, we establish the existence and boundedness of minimizers of (
2.1
).

Theorem 5

(Existence)
.

For a boundary data
g
satisfying (
2.2
), there exists a nonnegative minimizer

u\in H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})

of the functional (
2.1
).

Proof.

Combining (
2.8
), or (
2.9
) with ellipticity we have

\displaystyle J(v)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{1}}\frac{\lambda}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+fv^{\gamma}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{1}}\frac{\lambda}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}-|f|\left(|v-g|^{\gamma}+|g|^{\gamma}\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{1}}\frac{\lambda}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}dx-C\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\left(\|v-g\|_{H^{1}(B_{1})}^{\gamma}+\|g\|_{H^{1}(B_{1})}^{\gamma}\right)

Then by the standard Poincare inequality we have

J(v)\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}\int\limits_{B_{1}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx-C_{1}\left(\int\limits_{B_{1}}|\nabla v|^{2}\right)^{\gamma/2}-C_{2}.

where
C_{1}=C(n,\gamma,\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})})
and
C_{2}=C(\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})},g)
.
Applying Young’s inequality with
\varepsilon>0
to the last integral
in the previous expression, we obtain:

J(v)=(\frac{\lambda}{2}-\varepsilon)\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla v_{j}|^{2}dx-C(n,\gamma,\varepsilon,\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})},g).

We have verified that the functional
J
is coercive on
H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
. In particular,

\inf_{v\in H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})}J(v)>-\infty.

Let

m:=\inf\limits_{v\in\mathbb{K}}J(v),

and
\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{K}=H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
, a minimizing sequence. There exists
j_{0}\geq 1
such that

m\leq J(v_{j})<m+1,\quad\forall\,j\geq j_{0}.

Moreover, arguing as before

\displaystyle m+1>J(v_{j})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\frac{\lambda}{2}-\varepsilon)\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla v_{j}|^{2}dx-C(n,\gamma,\varepsilon,\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})},g).

Therefore, by taking
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we may conclude
that
\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
.

Thus, by the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem, there exists a function
v\in H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
, such that, passing to subsequence if necessary,

\displaystyle v_{j}\to v

\displaystyle\;\text{ weakly in }H^{1}(B),

\displaystyle v_{j}\to v

\displaystyle\;\text{strongly in }L^{q}(B)\text{ for all }q<2^{*},

\displaystyle v_{j}\to v

\displaystyle\;\text{a.e. in }B_{1}.

By the weak lower semicontinuity,

\int\limits_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{2}\langle A(x)\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle dx\leq\liminf\limits_{j\to\infty}\int\limits_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{2}\langle A(x)\nabla v_{j},\nabla v_{j}\rangle dx.

For the second term, let
1<r<p
be such that

\gamma r^{\prime}<2^{*},\quad\text{where }\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1.

By Hölder’s inequality, for any measurable set
E\subset B_{1}
we have

\int_{E}|f|\,|v_{j}|^{\gamma}\,dx\leq\|f\|_{L^{r}(E)}\,\|v_{j}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(B_{1})}=\|f\|_{L^{r}(E)}\,\|v_{j}\|_{L^{\gamma r^{\prime}}(B_{1})}^{\gamma}.

Since
\gamma r^{\prime}<2^{*}
, the sequence
\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
L^{\gamma r^{\prime}}(B_{1})
, and so there exists
C>0
independent of
j
such that

\int_{E}|f|\,|v_{j}|^{\gamma}\,dx\leq C\|f\|_{L^{r}(E)}.

By Lemma
1
, it follows that
\|fv_{j}^{\gamma}\|_{L^{1}(E)}\to 0
as
|E|\to 0
, uniformly in
j
.
This shows that the sequence
\{f\,v_{j}^{\gamma}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is
uniformly integrable
in
L^{1}(B_{1})
.

Moreover, since
v_{j}\to v
a.e., we have

f\,v_{j}^{\gamma}\to f\,v^{\gamma}\quad\text{a.e. in }B.

Hence, by Vitali’s convergence theorem, we conclude that

\int_{B}f\,v_{j}^{\gamma}\,dx\longrightarrow\int_{B}f\,v^{\gamma}\,dx.

Therefore,

J(v)\leq\liminf\limits_{j\to\infty}J(v_{j})=m,

and hence
v
is a minimum.
Moreover, since
g\geq 0
and
J(\max(u,0))\leq J(u)
, with equality if and only if
0\leq u
, can always be taken to be nonnegative.

∎

Theorem 6

(Boundedness of minimizers)
.

Let
u\in H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
be a nonnegative minimizer of (
2.1
), with
g
as in (
2.2
). Then
u
is uniformly bounded in
B_{1}
:

(3.1)

0\leq u\leq C\quad\text{a.e. in }B_{1},

where the constant
C>0
depends only on the dimension, the ellipticity constants,

\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}
, and
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})}
.

Proof.

For the upper bound in (
3.1
), let
u
be a minimizer of
J
over
H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
. Let
M_{0}=\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})}
and, for every
M\geq M_{0}
, define
u_{M}=\min(u,M)
. Since
u=g
on
\partial B_{1}
, we have
u_{M}\in H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})

E_{M}:=\{x\in B_{1}\ \big|\ u(x)>M\}.

It follows from the definition of
u_{M}
that

u_{M}=u\;\text{in}\;E_{M}^{c}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{M}=M\;\text{in}\;E_{M}.

By the minimality of
u

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle J(u_{M})-J(u)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{E_{M}}-\frac{1}{2}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle+f(x)[(M)^{\gamma}-u^{\gamma}]dx.

Hence,

\frac{\lambda}{2}\int\limits_{E_{M}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq\int\limits_{E_{M}}\frac{1}{2}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle dx\leq\int\limits_{E_{M}}|f||u-M|^{\gamma}dx,

|t^{\gamma}-s^{\gamma}|\leq|t-s|^{\gamma}
, for

.
Next, applying (

\int\limits_{E_{M}}|f||u-M|^{\gamma}dx\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(E_{M})}\|\nabla(u-M)\|_{L^{2}(E_{M})}^{\gamma}|E_{M}|^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}

Thus,

(3.2)

\int\limits_{E_{M}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq C|E_{M}|^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}\left(\frac{2-\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}=C|E_{M}|^{1-\frac{2}{n}+\delta}

where

is a positive number, since
p>\frac{n}{2}.
Therefore, by
[
10
, Chapter 2, Lemma 5.3]
,

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq\tilde{C}

for a constant
\tilde{C}>0
depending only on dimension ,
\delta
,
M_{0}
, the constant
C
from estimate (
3.2
) and
\|u\|_{L^{1}(E_{M_{0}})}
.
We now complete the proof by deriving an
L^{1}
estimate on
E_{M_{0}}
.
Since
(u-M_{0})_{+}\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{1})
, by Poincaré’s inequality and (
3.2
), we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{M_{0}}}|u|\;dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}(u-M_{0})_{+}\;dx+M_{0}|E_{M_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(u-M_{0})_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+M_{0}|E_{M_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(n)|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\|\nabla(u-M_{0})_{+}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+M_{0}|E_{M_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(n)|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(E_{M_{0}})}+M_{0}|E_{M_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C

where
C>0
depends only on the dimension, the ellipticity constants,

\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}
, and
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})}
.

∎

Remark 3.1

(Minimal assumptions on
g
)
.

We finish this section by remarking that the assumptions
g\in C(\overline{B_{1}})
,
g\geq 0
,
g\not\equiv 0

were imposed above for simplicity.
In fact, the results remain valid under weaker assumptions on
g
:

•

For existence of a minimizer, it is sufficient that

g\in H^{1/2}(\partial B_{1})
and
g\geq 0
(in the trace sense).

•

For the
L^{\infty}
boundedness of minimizers, it is enough that

g\in L^{\infty}(\partial B_{1})
and
g\geq 0
.

4.
Universal Hölder estimates

In this section, we establish local Hölder continuity for minimizers of
J_{A,f}
.
The main strategy relies on a multiscale analysis combining approximation and iterative oscillation control.

Since our interests are local, it will be convenient to work with local minimizers of (
2.1
). We start this section by recalling the definition of local minimum .

Definition 1
.

(
[
2
, Definition 1.4]
)
A nonnegative
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
is a local minimum relative to a subdomain
D\subset B_{1}
, if
J(u)\leq J(u+v)
for all
v\in H_{0}^{1}(D)
.

Note that if
u
is a minimizer of
J
over
H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
, then
u
is a local minimizer of
J
relative to any subdomain
D\subset B_{1}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u
be a local minimizer of
J_{A,f}
. Let
h
be the unique weak solution to

\left\{\begin{matrix}\mathrm{div}(A(x)\nabla h)=0&\text{in}&B_{R}(x_{0})\\
h=u&\text{in}&B_{1}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})\end{matrix}\right.

where
x_{0}\in B_{1/2}
, and
0<R<1/2
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, only depending upon
n,p,\gamma,\lambda,\Lambda
such that

(4.1)

\fint\limits_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-h|^{2}dx\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}R^{\sigma_{0}}

where
\sigma_{0}=\frac{2(2-n/p)}{2-\gamma}
if
n\geq 3
and
\sigma_{0}=\frac{2(2-2/p-\epsilon)}{2-\gamma}
if
n=2
.

Proof.

Since

J(u,B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq J(h,B_{R}(x_{0})),

we have

(4.2)

\int\limits_{B_{R}}\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle-\langle A(x)\nabla h,\nabla h\rangle dx\leq\int\limits_{B_{R}}f(x)[h^{\gamma}-u^{\gamma}]dx.

From the PDE satisfied by
h
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}\int\limits_{B_{R}}|\nabla(u-h)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{B_{R}}\langle A\nabla(u-h),\nabla(u-h)\rangle dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla(u-h)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla h\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\langle A(\nabla u-\nabla h+\nabla h,\nabla h\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{B_{R}}\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle-\langle A(x)\nabla h,\nabla h\rangle dx.

Moreover, since
p>\frac{n}{2}
, we set
r
to be
\frac{1}{r}=1-\frac{(n-2)\gamma}{2n}
if
n\geq 3
and
\frac{1}{r}=1-\epsilon
if
n=2
. Then applying (
2.8
) and (
2.9
)) we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{R}}f(x)[h^{\gamma}-u^{\gamma}]dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{R}}|f(x)|\,|h-u|^{\gamma}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(p,n,\gamma)\,\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{R})}\|u-h\|_{H^{1}(B_{R})}^{\gamma}\,|B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(p,n,\gamma)\,\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{R})}\|\nabla(u-h)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{\gamma}\,|B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}}.

where the last inequality follows from the Poincaré inequality.
Putting (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) together we get

\frac{\lambda}{2}\int\limits_{B_{R}}|\nabla(u-h)|^{2}dx\leq c(p,n,\gamma)\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{R})}\|\nabla(u-h)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{\gamma}|B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}}.

Therefore,

(4.5)

\|\nabla(u-h)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2-\gamma}\leq c(\lambda,n,p,\gamma)\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{R})}|B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}}.

Next, using
p>n/2
and
R<1
, we combine (
4.5
) and the Poincaré inequality once more to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|u-h|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(n)R^{2}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla(u-h)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{2}\;\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{R})}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}|B_{R}|^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{R})}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}R^{2+\frac{2n}{2-\gamma}(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p})}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{R})}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}R^{n+\sigma_{0}}.

∎

Before proceeding, we recall that solutions the homogeneous equation
\mathrm{div}(A(x)\nabla h)=0
, are Hölder continuous with estimates

(4.6)

\|h\|_{C^{0,\alpha_{0}}(B_{1/4})}\leq C_{*}\|h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})},

for some
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
and a constant
C_{*}>0
depending only on
n,\lambda,\Lambda
.

This estimate provides a first-scale control of minimizers of
J_{A,f}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exist universal constants
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
0<\rho<1/4
, depending only on
n,\lambda,\Lambda
, such that: if
u
be a local minimizer of
J_{A,f}
, with
\fint_{B_{1}}u^{2}dx\leq 1
,
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(B_{1})
, and
\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, then

(4.7)

\fint_{B_{\rho}}|u-\mu_{1}|^{2}dx\leq\rho^{\alpha},

for some universally bounded constant
|\mu_{1}|\leq C_{1}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
, and any

0<\alpha<\min\left\{2\alpha_{0},2\theta\right\}

Proof.

Let
h
be the harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{1/2}
. By Lemma
4.1
,

\int\limits_{B_{1/2}}|u-h|^{2}dx\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}.

where
C>0
depends only on
n,\lambda,\Lambda
,
p
and
\gamma
.
Thus,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u-h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{1}{2-\gamma}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon_{0}^{1/(2-\gamma)}+\sqrt{|B_{1}|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C+1)\sqrt{|B_{1}|}

if we choose
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\left(\sqrt{|B_{1}|}\right)^{2-\gamma}
.
Now set
\mu_{1}:=h(0)
.
By Lemma
4.1
, the Hölder continuity of
h
(see (
4.6
)), and (
4.8
),
we obtain, for
\rho<\tfrac{1}{4}
to be chosen later,

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{\rho}}|u-\mu_{1}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}}|u-h|^{2}\,dx+\fint_{B_{\rho}}|h-\mu_{1}|^{2}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}}|u-h|^{2}\,dx+C_{*}\|h\|_{C^{0,\alpha_{0}}(B_{1/2})}^{2}\rho^{2\alpha_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}}|u-h|^{2}\,dx+C_{*}\|h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}^{2}\rho^{2\alpha_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(C\,\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}\rho^{\sigma_{0}}+\tilde{C_{*}}(C+1)\rho^{2\alpha_{0}}\right),

for all
\rho\in(0,\tfrac{1}{4})
.
Next we choose
0<\rho<1/4
such that

C_{*}(C+1)\rho^{2\alpha_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\rho^{\alpha}

for
\alpha<2\alpha_{0}
. In the sequel, we choose
\varepsilon_{0}
even smaller such that

C\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}\rho^{\sigma_{0}}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\rho^{\alpha}.

Hence,

\fint_{B_{\rho}}|u-\mu_{1}|^{2}dx\leq\rho^{\alpha}.

∎

In what follows, we iterate the previous estimate to establish the continuity of
u
under a smallness condition on the source term
f
.

Proposition 4.3
.

There exist constants
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\rho\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, depending only on
n
, such that: for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
u
a local minimizer of
J_{A,f}
, with
\displaystyle\fint_{B_{1}}u^{2}dx\leq 1
,
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(B_{1})
, and
\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\leq\varepsilon
, there holds

(4.9)

|u(x)-u(0)|\leq C|x|^{\beta},\quad\forall|x|\,\leq\rho

for some
\beta\in(0,1)
,
C>0
, universal constants, depending additionally of
\varepsilon_{0}
,
\rho
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\rho>0
be the constants given by Proposition
4.2
. Define
u_{1}:B_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
, by

u_{1}(x)=\frac{u(\rho x)-\mu_{1}}{\rho^{\alpha/2}}.

Then, by the previous proposition
\fint_{B_{1}}u_{1}^{2}dx\leq 1
, and one can check that
u_{1}
is a local minimizer of

J_{1}(v,B_{1})=\int\limits_{B_{1}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\langle\tilde{A}(x)\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle+\tilde{f}(x)(v+\rho^{-\alpha/2}\mu_{1})_{+}^{\gamma}\right\}dx

where
\tilde{A}(x)=A(\rho x)
has the same ellipticity constants as
A
, and

\tilde{f}(x)=\rho^{2-\alpha(1-\gamma/2)}f(\rho x).

Indeed, for any
\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{1})
, with sufficiently small norm, we define

(4.10)

\displaystyle v(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}+\rho^{\alpha/2}(u_{1}(y/\rho)+\varphi(y/\rho)),\;\;\;y=\rho x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(y)+\rho^{\alpha/2}\varphi(y/\rho).

Then,
v-u\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{\rho})
and by the local minimality of
u
,

J(u,B_{\rho})\leq J(v,B_{\rho}).

After a change of variables,

J(u,B_{\rho})=\rho^{n+\alpha-2}J_{1}(u_{1},B_{1}),\;\;\text{and}\;\;J(v,B_{\rho})=\rho^{n+\alpha-2}J_{1}(u_{1}+\varphi,B_{1}).

Since
\tilde{f}(x)
satisfies

\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\leq\rho^{2-\alpha(1-\gamma/2)-n/p}\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})},

as long as
\alpha<\frac{2-n/p}{1-\gamma/2}
, we can apply Proposition
4.2
to find another constant
\tilde{\mu}
, with
|\tilde{\mu}|\leq C_{1}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
, such that

\fint_{B_{\rho}}|u_{1}-\tilde{\mu}|^{2}dx\leq\rho^{\alpha},

which translates back to
u
as

\fint_{B_{\rho^{2}}}|u-\mu_{2}|^{2}dx\leq\rho^{2\alpha}.

where
\mu_{2}=\mu_{1}+\rho^{\alpha/2}\tilde{\mu}
. Proceeding with the inductive argument, we conclude that

\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|u-\mu_{k}|^{2}dx\leq\rho^{k\alpha}.

where
\mu_{k}=\mu_{k-1}+\rho^{(k-1)\frac{\alpha}{2}}\tilde{\mu}_{k-1}
. It follows from universal bounds that

|\mu_{k}-\mu_{k-1}|\leq C_{1}\rho^{(k-1)\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Thus the above sequence is convergent. Let
\mu_{\infty}=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mu_{k}
. We estimate

|\mu_{\infty}-\mu_{k}|\leq\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}C_{1}\sum\limits_{i=k}^{N}\rho^{i(\alpha/2)}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{1-\rho^{\alpha/2}}\rho^{k(\alpha/2)}.

We conclude then

(4.11)

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|u-\mu_{\infty}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|u-\mu_{k}|^{2}dx+\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|u_{k}-\mu_{\infty}|^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[1+\left(\frac{C_{1}}{1-\rho^{\alpha/2}}\right)^{2}\right]\rho^{k\alpha}

Now, let
u_{B}=\fint_{B}udx
. Then

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|u-u_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|^{2}dx\leq 2\left(\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|u-\mu_{\infty}|^{2}dx+\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|\mu_{\infty}-u_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|^{2}dx\right)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|\mu_{\infty}-u_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}\left|\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}u-\mu_{\infty}dx\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|u-\mu_{\infty}|^{2}dx

by Jensen’s inequality. Finally,

\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|u-u_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|^{2}dx\leq 4\left[1+\left(\frac{C_{1}}{1-\rho^{\alpha/2}}\right)^{2}\right]\rho^{k\alpha}.

Thus, applying Campanato’s theorem,
[
7
, Theorem I.2]
, we conclude that
u
is Hölder continuous at the origin.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}
be the number given by Proposition
4.3
. Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
x_{0}\in B_{1/2}
. Define

\tilde{u}(y)=\kappa u(x_{0}+ry)

where

r=\min\left(\frac{dist(x_{0},\partial B_{1})}{10},\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\kappa^{2-\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}}\right)^{p/(2p-n)}\right)\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\kappa=\left(\frac{1}{\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u^{2}dx}\right)^{1/2}.

Hence
\tilde{u}
is a minimizer of
J_{\tilde{A},\tilde{f}}
, with
\tilde{A}(y)=A(x_{0}+ry)
, and

\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\leq k^{2-\gamma}r^{2-n/p}\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Moreover,

\fint_{B_{1}}\tilde{u}^{2}\,dx\leq 1.

Therefore, by Proposition
4.3

|\tilde{u}(y)-\tilde{u}(0)|\leq C|y|^{\beta},

whenever
|y|<\rho
. Translating this estimate back to
u
yields,

|u(x_{0}+ry)-u(x_{0})|\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}|y|^{\beta}.

This implies that
u
is Hölder continuous at
x_{0}
.
∎

As a consequence of the continuity of
u
, it follows that
\{u>0\}
is an open set, and we have the following

Corollary 1
.

Let
u
be a minimizer of

J(v)=\int_{B}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\langle A\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle+f(x)v_{+}^{\gamma}\Bigr)\,dx

over
v\in H_{g}^{1}(B)
. Then
u
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

(4.12)

\operatorname{div}(A(x)\nabla u)=\gamma f(x)u^{\gamma-1}\quad\text{in }B\cap\{u>0\},

in the weak sense.

Proof.

Since
u
is continuous,
\{u>0\}
is an open set. Let
U\subset B
be an open subset where
u>0
a.e., and let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(U)
. Because
u>0
on the support of
\varphi
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
|t|<\varepsilon
, the variation

u_{t}:=u+t\varphi

remains admissible (in particular,
u_{t}\geq 0
).

By minimality of
u
, the map
t\mapsto J(u+t\varphi)
has a critical point at
t=0
, yielding

0=\frac{d}{dt}J(u+t\varphi)\Big|_{t=0}=\int_{B}\bigl(\langle A\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle+\gamma f(x)u^{\gamma-1}\varphi\bigr)dx.

Since
\varphi
is supported in
U
, the integral reduces to
U
:

\int_{U}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle dx+\int_{U}\gamma f(x)u^{\gamma-1}\varphi dx=0.

This is precisely the weak formulation of (
4.12
) .
∎

5.
Regularity along the Free Boundary

In this section, our main goal is to describe the precise geometric behavior of a local minimizer near free boundary points. Roughly speaking, (weak) solutions to (
1.1
) should adjust their vanishing rate according to the singularity of the source term.

We will need the following Caccioppoli-type estimate:

Lemma 5.1
.

There is a constant
C>0
depending only on
n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,\gamma
, such that any minimizer of
J_{A,f}
over
H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
satisfies

(5.1)

\int\limits_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1})
such that
\xi\equiv 1
in
B_{1/2}
,
\xi\equiv 0
in
B_{1}\setminus B_{3/4}
and
|\nabla\xi|\leq C
. Thus
w=u(1-\xi^{2})\in H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
and by the minimality of
u
we have

J(u)\leq J(w),

which implies:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}}(2\xi-\xi^{2})\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}2u\xi(1-\xi^{2})\langle\nabla u,A\nabla\xi\rangle-2u^{2}\langle A\nabla\xi,\nabla\xi\rangle dx

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{1}}|f||\xi^{2}u|^{\gamma}dx

By Cauchy’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}2u\xi(1-\xi^{2})\langle\nabla u,A\nabla\xi\rangle dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\lambda}{4}\xi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{4}{\lambda}u^{2}|A\nabla\xi|^{2}dx.

Therefore, by ellipticity

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\lambda}{2}\xi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\lambda}{2}(2\xi^{2}-\xi^{4})|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}(2\xi-\xi^{2})\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\lambda}{4}\xi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{4}{\lambda}u^{2}|A\nabla\xi|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{1}}|f||\xi^{2}u|^{\gamma}dx

Next, since
\xi^{2}u\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{1})
then by (
2.8
), we estimate

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{1}}f|\xi^{2}u|^{\gamma}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n,p,\gamma)\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\|\xi^{2}u\|_{H^{1}(B_{1})}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\|\nabla(\xi^{2}u)\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(2-\gamma)\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}^{2/(2-\gamma)}}{2\varepsilon}+\frac{\gamma\varepsilon^{2/\gamma}}{2}\|\nabla(\xi^{2}u)\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{B_{1}}u^{2}dx+\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}\right)+\frac{\gamma\varepsilon^{2/\gamma}}{2}\int_{B_{1}}\xi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4}-\frac{\gamma\varepsilon^{2/\gamma}}{2}\right)\xi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}^{2/(2-\gamma)}\right)

Since
\xi=1
in
B_{1/2}
then taking
\varepsilon>0
so that
\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{4}-\frac{\gamma\varepsilon^{2/\gamma}}{2}\right)>0
, the result follows.

∎

We now present a key approximation result in the small-data regime.

Lemma 5.2

(Approximation)
.

Given
\delta>0
there exists a
\eta>0
depending only on
\delta
, dimension, ellipticity, p, and
\gamma
, such that if
u
is a local minimizer of
J_{A,f}
over
H_{g}^{1}(B_{1})
,
g\geq 0
, with
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(B_{1})
, and
\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\leq\eta
,
\fint_{B_{1}}u^{2}dx\leq 1
,
u(0)=0
then

\sup\limits_{B_{1/4}}u_{0}\leq\delta.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that there is an
\delta_{0}>0
and sequences
g_{k}\in H^{1}(B_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1})
,
f_{k}\in L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})
,
A_{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(B_{1})
, and
u_{k}\in H_{g_{k}}^{1}(B_{1})
such that

(1)

\fint_{B_{1}}u_{k}^{2}dx\leq 1
,
u_{k}(0)=0
;

(2)

\|f_{k}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{k}
;

(3)

u_{k}
is a local minimizer of

J_{k}(v)=\int\limits_{B_{1}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\langle A_{k}\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle+f_{k}(x)(v)_{+}^{\gamma}\right\}dx.

However,

(5.4)

\sup\limits_{B_{1/4}}u_{k}\geq\delta_{0}.

By the universal Hölder estimates obtained in the previous section,
u_{k}\to u_{\infty}
locally uniformly in
B_{1/2}
. By the Cacciopoli estimate
5.1
,
u_{k}
is bounded in
H_{g_{k}}^{1}(B_{1})
, and thus, up to a sequence,

u_{k}\to u_{\infty}\,\,\text{weakly in }\,\,H^{1}(B_{1/2}),\quad u_{k}\to u_{\infty}\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,L^{2}(B_{1/2}).

Moreover, by ellipticity, the sequence
(A_{k})
is uniformly bounded in
L_{loc}^{2}(B_{1/2},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}).
Hence, there exists a subsequence (still denoted
A_{k}
) such that
A_{k}\to A_{\infty}
weakly in
L_{loc}^{2}(B_{1/2},\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
to some
A_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(B_{1/2})
.

We want to verify that
u_{\infty}
is a weak solution to

(5.5)

\mathrm{div}(A_{\infty}\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in}\quad B_{1/2}.

Indeed, for any
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1/2})
, we have

J_{k}(u_{k})\leq J_{k}(u_{k}+v)

Thus,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{1/2}}2\langle A_{k}\nabla u_{k},\nabla v\rangle+\langle A_{k}\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle dx+\int\limits_{B_{1/2}}2f_{k}\left[(u_{k}+v)_{+}^{\gamma}-(u_{k})_{+}^{\gamma}\right]dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{I}_{k}+\text{II}_{k},

where

\text{I}_{k}=\int\limits_{B_{1/2}}2\langle A_{k}\nabla u_{k},\nabla v\rangle+\langle A_{k}\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle dx

and

\text{II}_{k}=\int\limits_{B_{1/2}}2f_{k}\left[(u_{k}+v)_{+}^{\gamma}-(u_{k})_{+}^{\gamma}\right]dx.

Once again, using (
1
),

|\text{II}_{k}|\leq C(n,p,\gamma)\|f_{k}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{\gamma}\to 0\,\,\text{as}\,\,k\to\infty.

The second term of
\text{I}_{k}
converges to
\int\limits_{B_{1/2}}\langle A_{\infty}\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle dx
by the weak convergency of
A_{k}

\displaystyle 0\quad+\quad\int\limits_{B_{1/2}}\langle A_{\infty}\nabla u_{\infty},\nabla v\rangle dx

, sufficiently large.

6.
Nondegeneracy

In this section, we establish Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Given
x_{0}\in\{u>0\}
, let
2r
be the distance from
x_{0}
to the free boundary. Suppose the point that
B_{4r}(x_{0})
touches the free boundary is
y_{0}
. Trivially
x_{0}\in B_{4r_{0}}(y_{0})
.

2r=|x_{0}-y_{0}|=dist(x_{0},\partial\{u>0\}).

Note that
u
solves

\mathrm{div}(A(x)\nabla u)=\gamma f(x)u^{\gamma-1}\quad\text{in}\quad B_{2r}(x_{0}),

Let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\frac{3}{2}r}(x_{0}))
with
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{r}
and
|\nabla\eta|\lesssim r^{-1}
.
Multiplying the equation for
u
by
\eta^{2}u^{1-\gamma}
and integrating, we have:

\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}\mathrm{div}(A(x)\nabla u)\,\eta^{2}u^{1-\gamma}\,dx=\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}\gamma f(x)\,\eta^{2}\,dx.

Clearly integration by parts yields the energy inequality:

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}f(x)\eta^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla(\eta^{2}u^{1-\gamma})\rangle dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}\eta^{2}(1-\gamma)u^{-\gamma}\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle|^{2}+2\eta u^{1-\gamma}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla\eta\rangle\,dx

Rewriting in terms of
w:=u^{(2-\gamma)/2}
yields,

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}f(x)\eta^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}\eta^{2}\frac{4(1-\gamma)}{(2-\gamma)^{2}}\langle A\nabla w,\nabla w\rangle+\frac{4}{(2-\gamma)}\eta w\langle A\nabla w,\nabla\eta\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{4(1-\gamma)\lambda}{(2-\gamma)^{2}}\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}\eta^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{2\Lambda}{(2-\gamma)}\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}(\delta^{2}w^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\eta^{2}|\nabla w|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\Lambda}{(2-\gamma)}\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}\delta^{2}w^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}dx

by choosing
\delta>0
so that

\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\frac{2\Lambda}{(2-\gamma)}=\frac{4(1-\gamma)\lambda}{(2-\gamma)^{2}}.

and using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality. Therefore,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}f(x)\eta^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\Lambda\delta^{2}}{(2-\gamma)}\int_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}w^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq c(\Lambda,\gamma)r^{-2}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}r}(x_{0})}w^{2}dx

\displaystyle=c(\Lambda,\gamma)r^{-2}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}r}(x_{0})}u^{2-\gamma}dx

Since
f\geq C_{0}
, we have

\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}r}(x_{0})}u^{2-\gamma}\geq C(\Lambda,\gamma,C_{0},n)r^{n+2}.

Therefore there exist

Notice that the distance from

to the free boundary is comparable to

\Box

Proof of Theorem

The difference of the proof mainly starts from (

7.
A radial example

In this final section, we construct a radial solution to illustrate
the sharpness of our regularity and non-degeneracy results. By
considering a source term with a calibrated singularity at the
origin, we demonstrate that the
L^{p,\infty}
scaling is the
critical threshold for maintaining the geometric properties of the
free boundary.

Let

u(x)=|x|^{\alpha},\qquad\alpha>0.

A direct computation shows that
u
satisfies

\Delta u=f(x)u^{\gamma-1},\quad\text{in}\quad\{u>0\}

with

f(x)=\alpha(n+\alpha-2)\,|x|^{\alpha(2-\gamma)-2}.

To analyze the integrability properties of
f
, we introduce the exponent

-\beta:=\alpha(2-\gamma)-2.

In this notation,

f(x)\simeq|x|^{-\beta}.

For each
t>0
, the distribution function of
f
can be computed explicitly:

\{f(x)>t\}=\{|x|^{-\beta}>t\}=\big\{|x|<t^{-1/\beta}\big\}.

Hence,

t\,\big|\{f(x)>t\}\big|^{1/p}=t\,|B_{t^{-1/\beta}}|^{1/p}=|B_{1}|^{1/p}\,t^{\,1-\tfrac{n}{\beta p}}.

From the characterization of weak
L^{p}
spaces, it follows that

f\in L^{p,\infty}(B_{1})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 1-\tfrac{n}{\beta p}=0,

that is,

\beta=\frac{n}{p}.

Recalling the definition of
\beta
, this condition is equivalent to

\alpha=\frac{2-n/p}{2-\gamma}.
