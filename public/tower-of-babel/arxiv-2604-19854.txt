1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph with
m
edges, and let
\rho(G)
denote the spectral radius of its adjacency matrix. A central problem in fixed-size spectral extremal graph theory asks for the largest possible value of
\rho(G)
under a forbidden-subgraph condition. In the language now standard in the subject, this is a Brualdi–Hoffman–Turan problem; see, for example, Liu and Wang
[
2
]
for a broad treatment and Rehman and Pirzada
[
4
]
for a short survey note.

In this paper we study the graph
H(4,3)
obtained by identifying one vertex of a
4
-cycle with one vertex of a triangle. The fixed-size
H(4,3)
-free problem fits into a family of recent results around the bowtie
H(3,3)
and its neighbors. Li, Lu, and Peng
[
1
]
determined the odd-size extremal graph for
H(3,3)
-free graphs. Pirzada and Rehman
[
3
]
proved the even-size extremal theorem for
\{H(3,3),H(4,3)\}
-free graphs, with the extremal value given by the quartic

p_{m}(x)=x^{4}-mx^{2}-(m-2)x+\frac{m}{2}-1.

Most recently, Zheng and Zhang
[
6
]
showed that the same quartic still governs the even-size problem when one forbids only
H(4,3)
, but their argument requires the global threshold
m\geq 38
.

Lowering that threshold matters for two reasons. First, in this circle of problems the extremal graph and extremal polynomial are often already known, so the natural next question is how far the structural proof can be pushed before new obstructions appear. Second, the even-size
H(4,3)
-free theorem of
[
6
]
sits between two sharper neighboring statements: the even
\{H(3,3),H(4,3)\}
-free theorem of Pirzada and Rehman
[
3
]
begins at
m\geq 10
, while the odd-size
H(4,3)
-free story has recently been pushed further by Wani, Pirzada, and Rehman
[
5
]
. Thus the numerical gap between
10
and
38
is mathematically meaningful rather than cosmetic.

Our contribution is a refinement of a single branch of the Zheng–Zhang proof. We keep their notation and their overall branch decomposition, but we sharpen the only configuration that genuinely forces the threshold
38
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be an
H(4,3)
-free graph of even size
m\geq 24
without isolated vertices. Then

\rho(G)\leq\rho^{\prime}(m),

where
\rho^{\prime}(m)
is the largest real root of

p_{m}(x)=x^{4}-mx^{2}-(m-2)x+\frac{m}{2}-1.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
G\cong S^{-}_{\frac{m+4}{2},2}
.

The proof is deliberately organized around the point where the threshold enters the original paper. The branch reduction from
[
6
, Claims 3.1–3.5]
shows that the only place where the published argument truly needs
m\geq 38
is the branch

G[A^{+}]\cong K_{4},\qquad e(A^{+},W)\leq 3.

Our new ingredient there is a local independence lemma for vertices of
W
adjacent to
A^{+}
. Once that lemma is in place, the cases
e(W)=0
and
e(W)=1
can be analyzed exactly; the cases
e(W)=2
and
e(W)=3
then follow from sharpened numerical inequalities. The case
e(W)=2
is the genuine bottleneck and is exactly where the threshold
24
comes from.

We also identify an explicit obstruction family.

Proposition 1.2
.

For even
m\in\{10,12,14,16\}
, the graph

T_{m}:=K_{1}\vee\bigl(K_{4}\cup(m-10)K_{1}\bigr)

is
H(4,3)
-free and satisfies
\rho(T_{m})>\rho^{\prime}(m)
. For every even
m\geq 18
, one has
\rho(T_{m})<\rho^{\prime}(m)
.

Thus no theorem of the same form can hold below
18
. We therefore expect the exact threshold to lie in the short interval
18\leq m_{\star}\leq 24
. Our computational appendix supports the conjecture that
m_{\star}=18
.

All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. For a graph

We adopt the neighborhood decomposition of Zheng and Zhang
[

Definition 2.1

For even

\rho(G)^{2}-\frac{3}{2}\rho(G)>L_{m}^{2}-\frac{3}{2}L_{m}=m-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{4m-5}-\frac{7}{4}.

The following lemma records the core inequalities from the proof of

(Zheng–Zhang)

In the above setting,

(3)

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=|N|+e(A^{+})+e(N,W)+e(W),

For explicit comparison families we shall repeatedly use the following elementary monotonicity lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(Decomposition comparison)
.

Let
f_{m}(x)
be the characteristic polynomial of an explicit family
F_{m}
, and suppose that

f_{m}(x)=q_{m}(x)p_{m}(x)+R_{m}(x),

where
p_{m}(x)=x^{4}-mx^{2}-(m-2)x+\frac{m}{2}-1
. Let
\alpha_{m}
be the Perron root of
F_{m}
. Assume there exists a lower bound
x_{0}=x_{0}(m)
such that
\rho^{\prime}(m)\geq x_{0}
and

(i)

q_{m}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\geq x_{0}
,

(ii)

R_{m}(x)>0
for all
x\geq x_{0}
.

Then
\alpha_{m}<\rho^{\prime}(m)
.

Proof.

Since
\rho^{\prime}(m)
is the largest real root of
p_{m}
, one has
p_{m}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\geq\rho^{\prime}(m)
. Hence for every
x\geq\rho^{\prime}(m)\geq x_{0}
,

f_{m}(x)=q_{m}(x)p_{m}(x)+R_{m}(x)>0

by (i) and (ii). Therefore
f_{m}
has no real root in
[\rho^{\prime}(m),\infty)
, so its Perron root
\alpha_{m}
satisfies
\alpha_{m}<\rho^{\prime}(m)
.
∎

When

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume

We now classify the admissible graphs in the case

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume
G[A^{+}]\cong K_{4}
and

. Then every admissible

-free graph is isomorphic to exactly one of the following three families:

(1)

(2)

(3)

the

: one endpoint of the unique edge of

, while the other endpoint is adjacent to one special vertex of

.

All remaining vertices of

are leaves adjacent only to

.

Proof.

Since

, the graph

is a single edge

, at most one of

, hence at most one of them may be adjacent to a vertex of

.

By

If neither endpoint meets

If exactly one endpoint meets

No other possibility survives the degree and compatibility constraints.
∎

Further directions

Conjecture 10.1
.

The sharp threshold in
theorem

1.1
is
18
.

By
proposition

5.1
, no theorem of the same form can hold below
18
. The remaining gap between the unconditional threshold
24
and the obstruction threshold
18
is therefore the only range in which the exact problem remains open.

The main obstacle is the
e(W)=2
branch in the
K_{4}
-case. Accordingly, the most immediate problem is to determine whether the estimates in
section

7
can be sharpened sufficiently to eliminate the residual cases
m=18,20,22
without recourse to computation.

More broadly, it would be interesting to understand whether the branch-reduction method used here can be adapted to other fixed-size spectral extremal problems in which the extremal graph and extremal polynomial are already known, but the proved threshold is not sharp. The present

-free problem suggests that, once the correct comparison graph has been identified, the main difficulty is often to isolate and analyze the unique threshold-bearing configuration.

Finally, it would be worthwhile to turn the finite residual search for

into a fully rigorous computer-assisted argument. Such a result would settle the exact threshold in the present problem and may also provide a useful template for related Brualdi–Hoffman–Turan problems of fixed size.
