Simultaneous false discovery rate control in location families
Zijun Gao, Wenjie Hu, Qingyuan Zhao

Abstract
When testing a number of statistical hypotheses using data from location families, it is often useful to control the false discovery rate (FDR) not just for hypotheses of the null values but also of other parameter values that are deemed practically insignificant. Here we consider FDR as a curve indexed by the location parameter and suggest a simple generalization of the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure that controls the FDR curve below any user-specified level. As a corollary of our main result, we show that the standard Benjamini-Hochberg procedure—designed to control the FDR at the null—also provides simultaneous control of the whole FDR curve for free. We further demonstrate the implications of our results and some practical considerations with a numerical example.



Keywords.

Benjamini-Hochberg procedure; FDR curve; Multiple testing

1
Introduction

The false discovery rate (FDR) has become a standard statistical error measure for
discovery in multiple hypothesis testing since the seminal work of

[
2
]
.
However, a p-value, or statistical significance, does not measure the
size of an effect or the importance of a result

[
11
]
. In light of this, the concept of FDR may seem
restricted as it only concerns false discoveries at the null value; in
practice, it may also be desirable to have low FDR for small or
“practically insignificant” values of the parameter. That is,
instead of just controlling the FDR at the null, it may be desirable
to control the whole “FDR curve”, a concept we will formally
introduce later.

In this article we document a “free lunch” theorem that shows that the standard
Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) procedure, when applied to control the FDR at
the null, can also control the whole FDR curve at a level that
depends on the nominal FDR at the null. This observation is
based on a simple generalization of the BH procedure that can be
applied to control the whole FDR curve below any user-specified
level. Next, we will introduce the setup and the main theoretical
results before demonstrating the practical implications using a real dataset.

2
Setup

Suppose we have independent, real-valued data
X_{i}\sim\mathbb{P}_{\theta_{i}},i=1,\dots,m
, from a location family
\{\mathbb{P}_{\theta}:\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
, where the distribution function of
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
is
given by
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(X\leq x)=F(x-\theta)
and
F
is continuous
and strictly increasing. We are interested in testing the one-sided hypotheses

H_{i,\theta}:\theta_{i}\geq\theta,\quad i=1,\dots,m,~\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

For example, if
\theta
represents a treatment effect on a risk
factor, we may be only interested in discovering
\theta_{i}
that is
sufficiently small (reduces the risk significantly). In this simple
setting, the p-value
for the hypothesis
H_{i,\theta}
is given by

P_{i,\theta}=F(X_{i}-\theta).

So the test that rejects

H_{i,\theta}
when
P_{i,\theta}\leq\alpha
has size
\alpha
.

Definition 2.1

(FDR curve)
.

The false discovery proportion curve for a rejection set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}
is given by

\displaystyle\text{FDP}_{\mathcal{S}}(\theta):=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\theta_{i}\geq\theta\}}}{\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}|\}},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

The FDR curve of a multiple testing procedure that rejects a random
subset
\mathcal{S}(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
is then defined as

\text{FDR}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[\text{FDP}_{\mathcal{S}(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})}(\theta)]
.

To test
H_{1,0},\dots,H_{m,0}
, the standard
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure computes

i^{*}=\max\left\{i:P_{(i),0}\leq iq/m,1\leq i\leq m\right\},

where
P_{(1),0}\leq\dotsc\leq P_{(m),0}
are the order
statistics of the p-values for the null hypotheses and
q
is a
user-specified value between 0 and
1
. The
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure then
rejects all hypotheses with p-values less than or equal to

P_{(i^{*})}
:

\mathcal{S}_{{\text{BH}}_{q}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})=\{i:P_{i,0}\leq P_{(i^{*})}\}.

It is not difficult to see that this is the largest subset

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}
such that
i\in\mathcal{S}
if
and only if
P_{i,0}\leq|\mathcal{S}|q/m
.
It has been shown that this standard
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure controls the FDR at
the null in the sense
that
\text{FDR}(0)\leq q
, or equivalently, controls the FDR
curve below a simple step function:

\text{FDR}(\theta)\leq q_{{\text{BH}}}(\theta):=q\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{\theta\geq 0\}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\theta<0\}},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

(1)

See
[
3
,
4
]
,
[
8
]
, and
[
6
]
for different
ways to prove this celebrated result.

3
Main Results

Our main methodological contribution is a generalized
{\text{BH}}_{q}

procedure for FDR curve control. Instead of considering
q\in\mathbb{R}
as a nominal FDR
level at the null, let
q:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a user-specified nominal
FDR curve.
For example, to control the FDR at a finite number of locations specified by
(\theta_{j},q_{j}),j=1,\dots,l
, it suffices to use the step function

q(\theta):=\inf_{\theta_{j}\leq\theta}q_{j},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R},\quad\text{and~}\inf_{j\in\emptyset}q_{j}=1.

Without loss of generality, we will assume
q
is
non-increasing—the target FDR can only become smaller as
\theta

increases, as
H_{i,\theta}
is stronger than
H_{i,\theta^{\prime}}
when
\theta>\theta^{\prime}
in our setup.

Consider the following generalized
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure:

1.

Compute the following target FDR curve-normalized “p-values” (can be larger than
1
):

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{i}=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}P_{i,\theta}/q(\theta),\quad i=1,\dots,m.

(2)

2.

Apply the original
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure to

\bar{P}_{1},\dots,\bar{P}_{m}
with
q=1
.

In other words, the discovery set
\mathcal{S}_{{\text{BH}}_{q}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
found by the generalized
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure is the largest subset

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}
such that
i\in\mathcal{S}
if
and only if
\bar{P}_{i}\leq|\mathcal{S}|/m
.
It is easy to see that this reduces to the original
{\text{BH}}_{q}

procedure when the step function
q_{{\text{BH}}}
in (
1
) is
used as the target FDR curve. For this reason, we will still refer to
this generalization as the
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure with the
understanding that
q
can now be a real-valued function.

Before stating our main result, let us introduce a modification to the
user-specified curve
q
:

q^{*}(\theta)=\inf_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{a\leq x-\theta^{\prime}\leq b}q(\theta^{\prime})\cdot\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-\theta^{\prime})},\quad\text{where}~a=F^{-1}(q(\theta^{\prime})/m)~\text{and}~b=F^{-1}(q(\theta^{\prime})).

(3)

Throughout,
F^{-1}(u)=\inf\{x:F(x)\geq u\}
denotes the generalized quantile,
with
F^{-1}(1)=+\infty
. It is easy to see that
q^{*}(\theta)\leq q(\theta)
is always true by
taking
\theta^{\prime}=\theta
in the definition above.

Remark 3.1
.

The choice of
(a,b)
ensures that
q(\theta^{\prime})/m\leq F(x-\theta^{\prime})\leq q(\theta^{\prime})
, so the supremum in (
3
) is taken over all values
of
x
that have a p-value against
\theta^{\prime}
in
[q(\theta^{\prime})/m,q(\theta^{\prime})]
, the maximum range of rejection threshold for the standard

{\text{BH}}_{q(\theta^{\prime})}
procedure.
If instead we simply use
a=-\infty
and
b=\infty
, it will typically be the case that
q^{*}(\theta)=q(\theta)
for all
\theta
. (The ratio
{F(x-\theta)}/{F(x-\theta^{\prime})}
typically converges to
1
as
x\to\infty
for
\theta>\theta^{\prime}
and to
\infty
as
x\to-\infty
for
\theta<\theta^{\prime}
.)

Theorem 3.1
.

The generalized
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure simultaneously controls
the FDR curve in the following sense

\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\text{FDR}(\theta)}{q^{*}(\theta)}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\text{FDP}(\theta)}{q^{*}(\theta)}\right]\leq 1.

(4)

So at any location
\theta
(possibly post hoc) we have

\text{FDR}(\theta)\leq q^{*}(\theta)
(almost surely).

Theorem

3.1
has two important practical implications:

1.

the standard

{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure (applied to null p-values only) may offer FDR control
at other locations for free,

2.

and the generalized
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure
may provide FDR curve control that is stronger than the user-specified
curve
q
.

The first implication is really just a special case of the second if
one takes the
viewpoint that the standard
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure corresponds to
using a simple step function
q_{{\text{BH}}}
in (
1
) as the
target FDR curve. In this case, it can be shown that

q_{{\text{BH}}}^{*}(0)=q_{{\text{BH}}}(0)
; see the remark after

Lemma

A.2
in the Appendix. Geometrically, this means
that
q^{*}_{{\text{BH}}}
is below
q_{{\text{BH}}}

but the two functions touch at
\theta=0
; see

Figure

1
below for an illustration.

The following result shows that the observation above is not a coincidence: under some mild conditions,
q^{*}
and

q
always touch at least one point.
We say the location family
F(x-\theta)
satisfies the
monotone ratio property
if

{F(x-\theta^{\prime})}/{F(x-\theta)}

is monotone in
x
for any
\theta
,
\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
.

(5)

This property ensures that the supremum in (
3
) is attained at one of the endpoints, that is
x-\theta^{\prime}=a
or
x-\theta^{\prime}=b
.
It holds for many common location families, including the Gaussian location model (i.e.
F=\Phi
is the CDF of the standard normal distribution); more generally, it holds whenever
F
admits a density
f
such that the ratio
f(x)/F(x)
is monotone in
x
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
F
is continuous, strictly
increasing, and has the monotone ratio
property (
5
). If
q
is decreasing,
right-continuous, bounded away from zero, and
q(\underline{\theta})=1
for some
\underline{\theta}>-\infty
, then there exists
\theta^{*}\in\mathbb{R}

such that
q^{*}(\theta^{*})=q(\theta^{*})
.

Proposition

3.1
shows that, even for general
q
functions, it is impossible that
q^{*}(\theta)<q(\theta)
for

all

\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, that is, the actual FDR control of the
generalized
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure cannot be uniformly better than the
nominal FDR control. See
Figures

1
and
3

for some examples.

An interesting question is how tight the inequalities in
(
4
) are. The following lower bound of the worst-case
FDR curve can be obtained by setting
\theta_{1}=\dots=\theta_{m}=\theta
.

Proposition 3.2

(Lower bound)
.

Suppose
F
is continuous, strictly increasing, and has the monotone ratio
property (
5
). Let
q^{*}
be the transformed
FDR curve for the standard
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure obtained by applying
(
3
) to (
1
). Then

\sup_{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{m}\in\mathbb{R}}\text{FDR}(\theta)\geq 1-e^{-q^{*}(\theta)}\quad\text{for all}~\theta<0.

When
\theta<0
and
q^{*}(\theta)
is small, the lower bound
1-e^{-q^{*}(\theta)}
above is close to
q^{*}(\theta)
, indicating that our
analysis is not too loose. This is further confirmed by the example below.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the Gaussian location family
X_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\theta_{i},1)
so
F=\Phi
.
Figure
1
shows that
q^{*}
touches
q_{{\text{BH}}}
at
\theta=0
, and is substantially smaller than
q_{{\text{BH}}}
for all
\theta\neq 0
displayed, suggesting that the BH procedure can provide much stronger control of the FDR curve than the simple step function
q_{{\text{BH}}}
.
We further add to the Figure a Monte Carlo estimate of the FDR
curve when
\theta_{1}=\dots=\theta_{m}=\theta
(blue dashed), which
lower bounds the worst-case FDR curve over all possible configurations
of
(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{m})
.

q(0)=0.05

(b)
q(0)=0.1

(c)
q(0)=0.2

4
Generalizations beyond a Single Location Family and a Real Data Example

In many applications, data for different hypotheses might not be generated from a single location family, but many results obtained above can be suitably generalized.
Here we consider the problem with independent
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
that satisfy

\mathbb{P}_{i,\theta_{i}}(X_{i}\leq x)=F_{i}(x-\theta_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,m
, where each
F_{i}
is continuous and strictly increasing.
The p-value for testing
H_{i,\theta}:\theta_{i}\geq\theta
is then
P_{i,\theta}=F_{i}(X_{i}-\theta)
,
and the FDR-curve normalized p-value
\bar{P}_{i}
remains as in (
2
).
We update the definition of
q^{*}
in (
3
) as

q^{*}(\theta):=\inf_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{i\in[m]}\sup_{a_{i}\leq x-\theta^{\prime}\leq b_{i}}q(\theta^{\prime})\cdot\frac{F_{i}(x-\theta)}{F_{i}(x-\theta^{\prime})},\quad a_{i}=F_{i}^{-1}\!\left({q(\theta^{\prime})/m}\right),~~b_{i}=F_{i}^{-1}\left(q(\theta^{\prime})\right),

(6)

where we further take the supremum over
F_{i}
and
[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}
.
When
F_{i}=F
for all
i
, this reduces to (
3
).
As before,
q^{*}(\theta)\leq q(\theta)
by taking
\theta^{\prime}=\theta
.

The FDR control result in
Theorem

3.1
continues to hold in this setting; see
Appendix

A
for the proof.
However,
Proposition

3.1
may not hold because of the additional supremum.
Nevertheless,
Proposition

4.1
below shows that when
q
is piecewise constant, there always exists a subset of the original constraints whose induced
q^{*}
curve still controls all prescribed FDR constraints and touches the target
q
curve at least once. We will further discuss this using a numerical example; see
Figure

2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
q
is piecewise constant (let
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}
denote the set of jump points), decreasing, and right-continuous.
For any subset
\Theta^{\prime}\subseteq\Theta
, let
q^{*}_{\Theta^{\prime}}
denote the curve obtained from (
6
) with the infimum restricted to
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta^{\prime}
. Then there exists a nonempty subset
\Theta^{\prime}\subseteq\Theta
such that

q^{*}_{\Theta^{\prime}}(\theta)\leq q(\theta)
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
q^{*}_{\Theta^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})=q(\theta^{\prime})
for some
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta^{\prime}
.

We provide a numerical illustration comparing formulating the FDR curve as a function of effect size (different
F_{i}
) vs. signal-to-noise ratio (shared
F_{i}=F
) using the benchmark breast cancer dataset in
[
9
]
.
This dataset contains expression measurements for
m=3,170
genes from hereditary breast tumors with BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations.
Suppose we are interested in finding genes with substantially higher BRCA2 expression than BRCA1 expression. We assume that the difference in mean log expression levels (base 2) for gene

1.

Effect sizes:

X_{i}

F_{i}(x)=\Phi(x/\hat{\sigma}_{i})
, which varies across genes through the estimated standard deviation
\hat{\sigma}_{i}
.

2.

Signal-to-noise ratio:
H_{i,\theta}:\theta_{i}=\mu_{i}/\sigma_{i}\geq\theta
, using test statistic
X_{i}/\hat{\sigma}_{i}
and a shared
F(x)=\Phi(x)
.

5
Further Discussion

The approach to simultaneous FDR control in this paper is based on FDR curve-normalization of p-values in (
2
). We refer the reader to
[
7
]
for a more general framework that can be applied to other selective compound decision problems (e.g. with hybrid error criteria combining errors made at different locations) beyond multiple hypothesis testing.

There is potential for adopting an empirical Bayes approach to select the FDR curve
q
using an estimated prior distribution of the location parameter. Heuristically, if
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{m}
are drawn independently from a distribution
\pi
, then the false discovery proportion curve for the rejection rule
\mathcal{S}=\{i:X_{i}\leq x\}
can be approximated by

\text{FDP}_{\mathcal{S}}(\theta)\to\mathbb{P}(\theta_{i}\geq\theta\mid X_{i}\leq x)=\frac{\int_{\theta}^{\infty}\pi(t)F(x-t)dt}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\pi(t)F(x-t)dt},\quad\text{as}~m\to\infty.

Thus, given an estimate of
\pi
by deconvolving the empirical
distribution of
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}

[
5
]
and
a desired number of rejections, one could use the formula above to
compute a target FDR curve. Further careful treatment would be needed
to address the double-dipping issue to guarantee FDR control.

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Here we prove an extended version of
Theorem

3.1
: the inequalities
in (
4
) still hold in the more general setting
described at the beginning of
Section

4
with
q^{*}
defined
in (
6
).

The first inequality in (
4
) directly follows from the definition
\text{FDR}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[\text{FDP}(\theta)]
. In proving the second inequality in (
4
) we will write the rejection
set
\mathcal{S}_{{\text{BH}}_{q}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
with the target FDR
curve
q
as
\mathcal{S}_{q}
. Using the assumption that
q
is
non-increasing, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\text{FDP}(\theta)}{q(\theta)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\theta_{i}\geq\theta,i\in\mathcal{S}_{q}\}}}{q(\theta)\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}_{q}|\}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{\theta_{i}\geq\theta,i\in\mathcal{S}_{q}\}}}{q(\theta)\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}_{q}|\}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{i\in\mathcal{S}_{q}\}}}{q(\theta_{i})\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}_{q}|\}}.

By adapting the standard “leave-one-out” argument
[
10
]
, it can be shown that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\text{FDP}(\theta)}{q(\theta)}\right]\leq 1.

The proof is deferred to Lemma
A.1
below. A more
general proof strategy can be found in
[
7
]
.

To complete the proof of
Theorem

3.1
, it suffices to show that

\mathcal{S}_{q}=\mathcal{S}_{q^{*}}
, that is, using the more
stringent FDR curve
q^{*}
as target always produces the same
rejection set as just using
q
. Let

\bar{P}^{*}_{i}=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}P_{i,\theta}/q^{*}(\theta),i=1,\dots,m,

be the normalized p-values by the FDR curve
q^{*}
. Since
q^{*}(\theta)\leq q(\theta)
, we immediately have
\bar{P}_{i}\leq\bar{P}^{\ast}_{i}
and
\mathcal{S}_{q^{*}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{q}
. To prove
\mathcal{S}_{q^{*}}\supseteq\mathcal{S}_{q}
, it suffices to show
\bar{P}_{i}^{*}\leq|\mathcal{S}_{q}|/m
for all
i\in\mathcal{S}_{q}
. Without loss of
generality, assume
|\mathcal{S}_{q}|>0
, so for any
i\in\mathcal{S}_{q}
, we have

F_{i}(X_{i}-\theta)/q(\theta)=P_{i,\theta}/q(\theta)\leq\bar{P}_{i}\leq|\mathcal{S}_{q}|/m\leq 1,\quad\text{for all}~\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

(7)

Therefore

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{F_{i}(X_{i}-\theta)}{\inf_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}\sup_{a_{j}\leq x-\theta^{\prime}\leq b_{j}}q(\theta^{\prime})\cdot\frac{F_{j}(x-\theta)}{F_{j}(x-\theta^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{F_{i}(X_{i}-\theta)}{\sup_{j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}\sup_{a_{j}\leq x-\theta^{\prime}\leq b_{j}}q(\theta^{\prime})\cdot\frac{F_{j}(x-\theta)}{F_{j}(x-\theta^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{F_{i}(\max\{X_{i},a_{i}+\theta^{\prime}\}-\theta)}{q(\theta^{\prime})\cdot\frac{F_{i}(\max\{X_{i},a_{i}+\theta^{\prime}\}-\theta)}{F_{i}(\max\{X_{i},a_{i}+\theta^{\prime}\}-\theta^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{F_{i}(\max\{X_{i},a_{i}+\theta^{\prime}\}-\theta^{\prime})}{q(\theta^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}}\max\left\{\frac{P_{i,\theta^{\prime}}}{q(\theta^{\prime})},\frac{1}{m}\right\}\leq|\mathcal{S}_{q}|/m.

The first inequality above follows from (
7
),
q(\theta)\leq 1
for all
\theta
, and the monotonicity of
F
. This
completes the proof.

Lemma A.1
.

For the generalized
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure, we have

\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{i\in\mathcal{S}_{q}\}}}{q(\theta_{i})\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}_{q}|\}}\right\}\leq\frac{1}{m},\text{~for~}i=1,\ldots,m.

Proof of
Lemma

A.1
.

Recall that the generalized
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure operates by applying the
standard BH procedure to the normalized

p

\bar{\bm{P}}=(\bar{P}_{1},\dots,\bar{P}_{m})
. Let
\bm{S}(\bar{\bm{P}})=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{m})\in\{0,1\}^{m}

selection indicator vector. Define the leave-one-out vector
\bar{\bm{P}}^{-i}
by removing
\bar{P}_{i}
. We construct a leave-one-out selected set
\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}
by manually setting the

\bm{S}(\bar{P}_{1},\dots,\bar{P}_{i-1},0,\bar{P}_{i+1},\dots,\bar{P}_{m})
.
A useful property of the standard BH procedure is that it is a
simple selection rule

[
1
,
7
]
: if hypothesis
i
is selected, changing
\bar{P}_{i}
to
0
(making it even more significant) will not alter the total number of rejections or the selection status of any other hypotheses.
This property guarantees that whenever
i\in\mathcal{S}_{q}
(i.e.,
S_{i}(\bar{\bm{P}})=1
), the selected set remains exactly the same, which implies
|\mathcal{S}_{q}|=|\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}|
.
Furthermore, the leave-one-out selected set
\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}
depends
only on
\bar{\bm{P}}^{-i}
, so
\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}\textup{ind}P_{i,\theta_{i}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{i\in\mathcal{S}_{q}\}}}{q(\theta_{i})\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}_{q}|\}}\right\}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{i\in\mathcal{S}_{q},P_{i,\theta_{i}}\leq q(\theta_{i})|\mathcal{S}_{q}|/m\}}}{q(\theta_{i})\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}_{q}|\}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{i\in\mathcal{S}_{q},P_{i,\theta_{i}}\leq q(\theta_{i})|\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}|/m\}}}{q(\theta_{i})\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}|\}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{P_{i,\theta_{i}}\leq q(\theta_{i})|\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}|/m\}}}{q(\theta_{i})\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}|\}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{q(\theta_{i})|\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}|/m}{q(\theta_{i})\max\{1,|\mathcal{S}_{q}^{-i}|\}}\right\}\leq\frac{1}{m}.

This completes the proof of
Lemma

A.1
.
∎

Next, we provide a proof of

. For a pair

, define

(8)

which generalizes the simple step function
q_{{\text{BH}}}
in (

\bar{q}^{*}_{t}(\theta)
be the corresponding

function obtained by the transformation in (

Lemma A.2
.

For all

\displaystyle\bar{q}^{*}_{t}(\theta)=\min\left\{\bar{q}_{t}(\theta),\,\sup_{a\leq x-t\leq b}\bar{q}_{t}(t)\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-t)}\right\}=\min\left\{1,\,\sup_{a\leq x-t\leq b}\bar{q}_{t}(t)\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-t)}\right\}.

In particular, if
\bar{q}_{t}(t)=1
, then
\bar{q}^{*}_{t}(\theta)=1
for all
\theta
.

The proof of
Lemma

A.2
is deferred. By taking
\theta=t

in

A.2
and using the definition of
\bar{q}_{t}(t)
, we
immediately find

\displaystyle\bar{q}^{*}_{t}(t)=q(t)\quad\text{for all}~t\in\mathbb{R}.

That is, the modified curve touches the original curve at
t
.

The following definition is useful in studying the relation between
q
and
q^{*}
.

Definition A.1
.

For any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and

, we write
(\theta_{1},q_{1})\preceq(\theta_{2},q_{2})
iff
\bar{q}_{\theta_{2}}^{*}(\theta_{1})\leq q_{1}
, and

\bar{q}_{\theta_{2}}^{*}(\theta_{1})<q_{1}
.
We write
(\theta_{1},q_{1})\sim(\theta_{2},q_{2})
iff
\bar{q}_{\theta_{1}}^{*}(\theta)=\bar{q}_{\theta_{2}}^{*}(\theta)
for all
\theta
.

Lemma A.3
.

If
F
satisfies the monotone ratio property (
5
), then
(\theta_{1},q_{1})\preceq(\theta_{2},q_{2})
is true if and only if

\displaystyle F^{-1}(q_{1})+\theta_{1}\geq F^{-1}(q_{2})+\theta_{2},~\text{and}~F^{-1}(q_{1}/m)+\theta_{1}\geq F^{-1}(q_{2}/m)+\theta_{2}.

(9)

Furthermore,
(\theta_{1},q_{1})\prec(\theta_{2},q_{2})
is true if and only if the inequalities in (

) hold and at least one of them is strict.

The proof of
Lemma

A.3
is also deferred. Next we prove

3.1
in the main text.

Proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

Denote
A(\theta)=\theta+F^{-1}(q(\theta)/m)
,
B(\theta)=\theta+F^{-1}(q(\theta))
, and
S(\theta)=A(\theta)+B(\theta)
.
As
q
is decreasing and
q(\underline{\theta})=1
, we only need to consider constraints corresponding to
\theta>\underline{\theta}
.
Since
q(\theta)
is bounded away from zero,
S(\theta)
is bounded below for
\theta>\underline{\theta}
and
S(\theta)\rightarrow+\infty
when
\theta\rightarrow+\infty
. Define
S_{*}:=\inf_{\theta}S(\theta)=\inf_{\theta>\underline{\theta}}S(\theta)>-\infty
.
Then there exists a
\theta^{*}
such that
S(\theta^{*})=S_{*}
. (This
is because there exists a bounded sequence
\{\theta_{n}\}
(not necessarily
with distinct terms) such that
S(\theta_{n})\to S_{*}
, and among them,
there further exists a subsequence
\{\theta_{n_{k}}\}
such that

(\theta_{n_{k}},q(\theta_{n_{k}}))
converges, and denote
\theta^{*}=\lim\theta_{n_{k}}
. As
F
and
F^{-1}
are continuous, and
q
is
right-continuous and decreasing,
S
is lower semi-continuous so
S(\theta^{*})=S_{*}
.)

We next show that
(\theta^{*},q(\theta^{*}))
cannot be strictly dominated in the sense of the relation
\prec
in
Definition

A.1
. Otherwise there exists
(s,q(s))\succ(\theta^{*},q(\theta^{*}))
. By
Lemma

A.3
, this implies that

s+a_{s}\leq\theta^{*}+a^{*},\quad s+b_{s}\leq\theta^{*}+b^{*},

where
a^{*}=F^{-1}(q(\theta^{*})/m)
,
b^{*}=F^{-1}(q(\theta^{*}))
,
a_{s}=F^{-1}(q(s)/m)
, and
b_{s}=F^{-1}(q(s))
, with at least one strict
inequality among the two. Summing up the two inequalities yields

\displaystyle 2s+a_{s}+b_{s}<2\theta^{*}+a^{*}+b^{*}=S_{*},

which contradicts the definition of
S_{*}
.

Below, we show that the
q^{*}
curve passes through
(\theta^{*},q(\theta^{*}))
. As noted in the main text,
q^{*}(\theta^{*})\leq q(\theta^{*})
by definition.
If
q^{*}(\theta^{*})<q(\theta^{*})
, we have, using the definition of
q^{*}
and the properties of the infimum,

\sup_{a\leq x-\theta^{\prime}\leq b}q(\theta^{\prime})\frac{F(x-\theta^{*})}{F(x-\theta^{\prime})}<q(\theta^{*})\quad\text{for some}~\theta^{\prime}.

Applying
Lemma

A.2
with
t=\theta^{\prime}
and

\theta=\theta^{*}
, we know that

\bar{q}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}(\theta^{*})\leq\sup_{a\leq x-\theta^{\prime}\leq b}q(\theta^{\prime})\frac{F(x-\theta^{*})}{F(x-\theta^{\prime})}.

Combining these inequalities yields
\bar{q}^{*}_{\theta^{\prime}}(\theta^{*})<q(\theta^{*})
, which contradicts the observation that
(\theta^{*},q(\theta^{*}))
cannot be dominated. Therefore,
q^{*}(\theta^{*})=q(\theta^{*})
.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

A.2
.

We consider two cases. For
\theta^{\prime}<t
, we have

\bar{q}_{t}(\theta^{\prime})=1
and
b=F^{-1}(1)=\infty
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{x-\theta^{\prime}\geq a}\bar{q}_{t}(\theta^{\prime})\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-\theta^{\prime})}=\sup_{x-\theta^{\prime}\geq a}\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-\theta^{\prime})}\geq\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-\theta^{\prime})}=1.

For
\theta^{\prime}\geq t
, we have
\bar{q}_{t}(\theta^{\prime})=q(t)
, and

\displaystyle\sup_{a\leq x-\theta^{\prime}\leq b}\bar{q}_{t}(\theta^{\prime})\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-\theta^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\leq x^{\prime}\leq b}\bar{q}_{t}(\theta^{\prime})\frac{F(x^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}-\theta)}{F(x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle(x^{\prime}:=x-\theta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{F^{-1}(q(t)/m)\leq x^{\prime}\leq F^{-1}(q(t))}q(t)\frac{F(x^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}-\theta)}{F(x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle(\bar{q}_{t}(\theta^{\prime})=q(t)\ \text{for}\ \theta^{\prime}\geq t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{F^{-1}(q(t)/m)\leq x^{\prime}\leq F^{-1}(q(t))}q(t)\frac{F(x^{\prime}+t-\theta)}{F(x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle(\theta^{\prime}\geq t,\ F\ \text{increasing, equality can be obtained at $\theta^{\prime}=t$.
})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\leq x-t\leq b}\bar{q}_{t}(t)\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-t)}

\displaystyle(x:=x^{\prime}+t).

This shows
\bar{q}_{t}^{*}(\theta)
is lower bounded by the expressions in

Lemma

A.2
. Now consider

\theta^{\prime}=\theta
, so

\displaystyle\sup_{a\leq x-\theta\leq b}\bar{q}_{t}(\theta)\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-\theta)}=\bar{q}_{t}(\theta)\leq 1.

This establishes the equality in
Lemma

A.2
.

For the special case
\bar{q}_{t}(t)=1
, we have
\bar{q}_{t}(t)=1
and
b=F^{-1}(q(t))=\infty
. Then, for
\theta\geq t
,
\sup_{a\leq x-t\leq b}\bar{q}_{t}(t)\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-t)}=1
; for
\theta<t
, we have
\sup_{a\leq x-t\leq b}\bar{q}_{t}(t)\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-t)}\geq 1
, and therefore
\min\left\{1,\sup_{a\leq x-t\leq b}\bar{q}_{t}(t)\frac{F(x-\theta)}{F(x-t)}\right\}=1
.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

3.2
.

Consider an arbitrary
\theta<0
. For
the standard
{\text{BH}}_{q}
procedure, the monotone ratio property ensures
that the supremum in the definition of
q^{*}
in (
3
) is
attained at the boundary
t=F^{-1}(q/m)
. Thus,
q^{*}(\theta)=qF(t-\theta)/F(t)
, or equivalently
F(t-\theta)=q^{*}(\theta)/m
.
Consider the parameter configuration
\theta_{1}=\cdots=\theta_{m}=\theta
. Under this configuration, the null hypothesis
H_{i,\theta}
(which is
\theta_{i}\geq\theta
) is true for all
i=1,\dots,m
. Therefore, any rejection is a false discovery, so

\displaystyle\text{FDR}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(|\mathcal{S}_{{\text{BH}}_{q}}|\geq 1)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}(X_{i}>t\text{ for all }i=1,\dots,m)

\displaystyle=1-\left(1-F(t-\theta)\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=1-\left(1-\frac{q^{*}(\theta)}{m}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\geq 1-e^{-q^{*}(\theta)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

A.3
.

Let
a_{1}=F^{-1}(q_{1}/m),b_{1}=F^{-1}(q_{1})
and
a_{2}=F^{-1}(q_{2}/m),b_{2}=F^{-1}(q_{2})
.
Then

\displaystyle(\theta_{1},q_{1})\preceq(\theta_{2},q_{2})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\sup_{a_{2}\leq x-\theta_{2}\leq b_{2}}q_{2}\frac{F(x-\theta_{1})}{F(x-\theta_{2})}\leq q_{1}

(
Lemma

A.2
,
q_{1}<1
)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle q_{2}\frac{F(\theta_{2}+a_{2}-\theta_{1})}{F(a_{2})}\leq q_{1},\quad q_{2}\frac{F(\theta_{2}+b_{2}-\theta_{1})}{F(b_{2})}\leq q_{1}

(Condition (
5
))

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle mF(\theta_{2}+a_{2}-\theta_{1})\leq q_{1},\quad F(\theta_{2}+b_{2}-\theta_{1})\leq q_{1}

(
F(a_{2})=q_{2}/m
,
F(b_{2})=q_{2}
)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle F(\theta_{2}+a_{2}-\theta_{1})\leq F(a_{1}),\quad F(\theta_{2}+b_{2}-\theta_{1})\leq F(b_{1})

(
F(a_{1})=q_{1}/m
,
F(b_{1})=q_{1}
)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\theta_{2}+a_{2}-\theta_{1}\leq a_{1},\quad\theta_{2}+b_{2}-\theta_{1}\leq b_{1}

(
F
is continuous and increasing)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\theta_{2}+a_{2}\leq\theta_{1}+a_{1},\quad\theta_{2}+b_{2}\leq\theta_{1}+b_{1}.

For
\prec
, we automatically have
\theta_{2}+a_{2}\leq\theta_{1}+a_{1}
,
\theta_{2}+b_{2}\leq\theta_{1}+b_{1}
.
We prove by negation that the two equalities can not hold
simultaneously.
Otherwise, suppose
\theta_{2}+a_{2}=\theta_{1}+a_{1}
and
\theta_{2}+b_{2}=\theta_{1}+b_{1}
.
By Lemma
A.2
and the monotone ratio property,

\displaystyle\bar{q}^{*}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\bar{q}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1}),\max\left\{q_{2}\frac{F(\theta_{2}+b_{2}-\theta_{1})}{q_{2}},q_{2}\frac{F(\theta_{2}+a_{2}-\theta_{1})}{q_{2}/m}\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\bar{q}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1}),\max\left\{F(b_{1}),mF(a_{1})\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\bar{q}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1}),q_{1}\right\}.

If
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
, then
\bar{q}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})=1\geq q_{1}
, so
\bar{q}^{*}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})=q_{1}
.
If
\theta_{1}\geq\theta_{2}
, then
\theta_{1}+a_{1}=\theta_{2}+a_{2}
implies
a_{1}\leq a_{2}
, so
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
. Thus
\bar{q}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})=q_{2}\geq q_{1}
, and again
\bar{q}^{*}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})=q_{1}
.
In both cases,
\bar{q}^{*}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})=q_{1}
, which
contradicts the assumption
(\theta_{1},q_{1})\prec(\theta_{2},q_{2})

(i.e.,
\bar{q}^{*}_{\theta_{2}}(\theta_{1})<q_{1}
).
∎

4.1

Now suppose the claim holds for any

\Theta_{K}^{\prime}

If

,
then it controls all
K+1
constraints and touches

at at least one point.
Otherwise, define

\Theta^{\prime}:=\Theta_{K}^{\prime}\cup\{\theta_{K+1}\}

Recall the definition of
q^{*}
in (

is obtained by taking the infimum over one additional

At the new point
