Integrating E-senses and volatile fingerprinting to decipher type-specific flavor signatures in four commercial Huangjiu
Authors: Zhenni Qu, Menghan Liu, Ying Zhang, Qiao Zhou, Juanjuan Zhu, Qian Zhou, Yiwen Ren, Peng Gao, Dianhua Shi, Yanpeng Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study established an integrated strategy to characterize the flavor profiles of four commercial Huangjiu types (dry, semi-dry, semi-sweet, and sweet). Electronic tongue (E-tongue) analysis showed that sweet Huangjiu exhibited the strongest sourness, while dry Huangjiu displayed intense aftertaste signals. Semi-dry and semi-sweet Huangjiu presented prominent umami and saltiness responses. Orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) of electronic nose (E-nose) data identified five key sensors that effectively distinguished the four types. Headspace gas chromatography-ion mobility spectrometry (HS-GC-IMS) detected 76 volatile compounds, among which semi-sweet Huangjiu contained the highest levels of alcohols, ketones, and aldehydes. A total of 31 compounds were identified as key discriminatory markers. Odor activity value (OAV) calculations indicated that (Z)-4-heptenal, (E,E)-2,4-nonadienal, and furanone derivatives were the dominant aroma contributors. Headspace solid-phase microextraction-gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC–MS) identified 60 volatile compounds, with semi-sweet Huangjiu exhibiting the greatest compositional diversity, including 12 unique components. Furthermore, 14 differential volatile components were identified as holistic markers to distinguish between the distinct regional and technological styles of Shaoxing-type and Jimo-type Huangjiu. This integrated sensory-chromatographic platform provides a reliable scientific basis for the quality evaluation, classification, and flavor monitoring of complex commercial Huangjiu products.

Introduction

Huangjiu, a representative traditional Chinese fermented alcoholic beverage, is recognized as one of the world's three oldest alcoholic drinks, alongside beer and wine, with a documented history spanning thousands of years (Jin et al., 2021). Manufactured from grains such as rice, millet, corn, and wheat, Huangjiu is traditionally produced through a multi-step process that includes soaking, steaming, fermentation, pressing, and sterilization. With an ethanol content typically ranging from 8% to 20% (v/v), Huangjiu is recognized for its smooth flavor profile and rich nutritional content, which has contributed to its widespread popularity among consumers across East Asia, where annual consumption exceeds two million kiloliters (Yang et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2024b). Based on sugar content, commercial Huangjiu is classified into four categories: dry (≤ 15 g/L), semi-dry (15–40 g/L), semi-sweet (40–100 g/L), and sweet (≥ 100 g/L). Beyond its sensory properties, Huangjiu has been reported to offer health benefits, such as alleviating cardiovascular symptoms and exerting anti-aging effects (Wang et al., 2019).

From a market perspective, semi-dry Huangjiu dominates the industry, accounting for approximately 45% to 50% of the national market share due to its balanced flavor profile and broad culinary versatility. In contrast, semi-sweet Huangjiu features moderate sweetness and distinct fruity notes, making it particularly favored among female consumers, who represent up to 60% of its target consumer group (Yang et al., 2021). Despite these well-recognized characteristics, systematic studies comparing the physicochemical properties, sensory profiles, taste characteristics, and volatile compositions among the four major commercial Huangjiu types remain limited. Therefore, the development of an integrated and efficient evaluation system for flavor-based classification and quality grading of Huangjiu is highly desirable.

Conventional flavor evaluation of Huangjiu heavily relies on human sensory panels, which are inherently subjective, difficult to reproduce, prone to sensory fatigue, and easily influenced by environmental factors (Martínez-García et al., 2021). As an alternative, electronic sensory technologies—including the E-tongue and E-nose—simulate the mammalian gustatory and olfactory systems. These intelligent analytical tools offer high efficiency, rapid response, excellent repeatability, and strong objectivity. Using taste-sensitive and gas-sensitive sensor arrays, they can capture the global taste and aroma fingerprints of alcoholic beverages, and have been widely applied in flavor profiling, brand differentiation (Yang et al., 2020), vintage identification, and quality grading (Hou et al., 2021). Drawing inspiration from the mammalian olfactory pathway, multi-channel sensor arrays combined with multivariate statistical methods (e.g., linear discriminant analysis) have become powerful approaches for discriminating structurally similar compounds in complex food matrices (Yu et al., 2025b).

Volatile compounds are among the most critical determinants of Huangjiu flavor quality. Significant differences in volatile profiles exist across Huangjiu types, and these differences directly determine product quality, sensory typicity, and market grade. To date, more than 900 volatile compounds have been identified in Huangjiu, mainly comprising alcohols, esters, phenols, organic acids, and aldehydes (Chen et al., 2018). HS-GC-IMS is a rapid and sensitive technique for analyzing volatile components in foods. In recent years, it has been increasingly applied to characterize the volatile profiles of alcoholic beverages (Su et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2021), tea, condiments, meat products, and traditional Chinese medicines. The characteristic fingerprint spectra generated by HS-GC-IMS effectively reveal trace-level compositional differences among sample groups. When combined with multivariate statistical analysis, HS-GC-IMS enables reliable identification of key volatiles responsible for sample discrimination (Cai et al., 2022). Similarly, HS-SPME-GC–MS is widely adopted due to its simple operation and high sensitivity, supporting applications in fermentation optimization (Briones-Labarca et al., 2017), geographical origin traceability, flavor characterization (Licen et al., 2021), and volatile compound profiling (Zhang et al., 2011).

In recent years, comprehensive volatile profiling—the systematic analysis of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in food matrices—has emerged as a robust strategy for investigating flavor formation, food authentication, and quality assessment (Li et al., 2023b; Parastar et al., 2025). This strategy typically integrates advanced platforms such as HS-GC-IMS and gas chromatography–mass spectrometry/olfactometry (GC–MS/O), coupled with chemometric tools for data processing and interpretation (Kaldeli et al., 2024). Unlike traditional targeted analysis, volatile profiling provides a more comprehensive overview of aroma composition by capturing both identified and uncharacterized compounds that collectively contribute to overall flavor perception (Majchrzak et al., 2020). When further combined with complementary electronic sensory technologies (E-nose and E-tongue), this multi-platform approach enables multi-dimensional characterization that bridges sensory perception and chemical signatures, offering deeper insights into the complex flavor profiles of food products (Li et al., 2026).

The primary objective of this study was to establish a systematic, multi-technique flavor profiling framework for Huangjiu. First, this work innovatively integrates intelligent sensory evaluation (E-tongue and E-nose) with HS-GC-IMS and HS-SPME-GC–MS, enabling comprehensive characterization from global sensory perception to detailed volatile composition. Second, it systematically discriminates among the four major commercial Huangjiu types classified by sugar content, identifying key sensor responses and characteristic volatile markers for practical product classification. Third, this study extends beyond simple compound identification by exploring correlations between specific sensory attributes and key volatile compounds. Finally, it reveals significant volatile compositional differences among representative regional commercial Huangjiu styles (e.g., Shaoxing-type vs. Jimo-type), providing a robust chemical foundation for the differentiation and quality control of complex commercial Huangjiu products.

Samples and preparation

Traditional Huangjiu is produced via a solid-state fermentation process using cereal grains (rice, millet, or wheat) as raw materials. Briefly, the raw grains are soaked, washed, and steamed to gelatinize the starch. After cooling, wheat Qu (a starter culture containing natural molds, yeasts, and bacteria) is mixed with the steamed grains to initiate simultaneous saccharification and fermentation (Li et al., 2024b; Tian et al., 2022). Spontaneous fermentation proceeds in sealed jars or tanks at controlled temperatures (15–28°C) for one to three months. Following fermentation, the mash is pressed to separate the liquid wine from the lees. The raw wine is then filtered, pasteurized, sealed in jars, and aged for several months to several years to develop its characteristic flavor profile. The final products are classified into dry, semi-dry, semi-sweet, and sweet types according to their sugar content. During fermentation and aging, complex microbial metabolism, enzymatic reactions, Maillard reactions, and esterification processes occur, all of which directly determine the formation of volatile and taste-active compounds (Yu et al., 2025a; Zhao et al., 2024a).

Ultrapure water was used throughout all experiments, and all chemical reagents were of analytical grade. A total of 52 commercial Huangjiu samples were used in this study, including 13 semi-dry, 13 semi-sweet, 13 dry, and 13 sweet samples, classified according to their labeled sugar content. Determination of total soluble solids (°Bx)

Total soluble solids (°Bx) were measured using a portable refractometer according to the recommendations from the International Commission for Uniform Methods of Sugar Analysis (ICUMSA). Before measurement, the refractometer was calibrated to zero with ultrapure water. Two to three drops of each Huangjiu sample were then applied to the detection prism, and the °Bx value was recorded. All measurements were performed in triplicate. This rapid refractometric method was employed specifically to verify the relative gradient of the pre-classified commercial categories.

E-tongue analysis

Taste profiles were recorded using an Insent E-tongue (Japan) equipped with eight cross-sensory electrodes for comprehensive taste characterization. Before analysis, the reference electrode was activated, and the instrument completed a full self-diagnosis to ensure stable and reliable signal output.

For each sample, 30 mL of Huangjiu was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min, and the clear supernatant was collected for analysis. The E-tongue probe was immersed in the supernatant, and signals were acquired for 120 s, followed by a 30 s cleaning cycle. Each sample was measured four times consecutively; the first cycle was discarded to eliminate unstable signals, and the subsequent three cycles were averaged to ensure repeatability. All measurements followed the manufacturer's standard operating protocol and previously published procedures (Toko, 2023; Wu et al., 2020; Wu & Toko, 2023).

The surfaces of the eight electrodes are coated with biomimetic lipid membranes that exhibit distinct responses to chemical substances based on their taste characteristics (Kobayashi et al., 2010a). These sensors generate electrical signals in response to sample components, which are then converted into digital signals, effectively simulating the function of taste buds on the human tongue (Fu et al., 2024). In this system, CA0 corresponds to sourness, C00 to bitterness and bitter aftertaste (aftertaste-B), AE1 to astringency and astringent aftertaste (aftertaste-A), AAE to umami and richness, and CT0 to saltiness. Aftertaste-A and aftertaste-B were calculated as the change in membrane potential by adsorption (CPA) values, representing the potential difference before and after sample adsorption and rinsing (Vr’ − Vr). Aftertaste-B corresponds to the persistent bitterness retained on the C00 membrane, while aftertaste-A corresponds to the lingering astringency on the AE1 membrane (Wu et al., 2020; Wu & Toko, 2023). For the Insent SA402B/TS-5000Z system, taste thresholds are calibrated to human perception. Specifically, the tasteless reference points are defined as −13 for sourness and − 6 for saltiness, whereas the threshold for the remaining six taste signals is 0 (Kobayashi et al., 2010b; Zhu et al., 2025). Values above these thresholds indicate detectable taste perception, which is consistent with the manufacturer's calibration and published sensor validations (Wang et al., 2024). The detection limit of the taste sensor for basic taste compounds ranges from 10 −4 to 10 −7 mol/L (Li et al., 2023a).

E-nose analysis

Volatile aroma profiles were analyzed using a PEN E-nose (Airsense, Germany) equipped with 10 metal oxide semiconductor (MOS) sensors (Buratti et al., 2018). Before detection, all Huangjiu samples were diluted with ultrapure water to an ethanol content of 2% (v/v) to reduce matrix interference. A 500 μL aliquot of the diluted sample was transferred into a 20 mL headspace vial, sealed tightly, and equilibrated at room temperature for 15 min.

Instrument parameters were set as follows: purge time = 120 s; zeroing time = 5 s; pre-sampling time = 5 s; sampling time = 120 s; injection flow rate = 400 mL/min. Each sample was analyzed in triplicate to ensure data accuracy. HS-GC-IMS analysis

Prior to analysis, Huangjiu samples were diluted to an ethanol content of 1% (v/v) with ultrapure water. Then, 10 μL of 2-octanol (0.5 mg/mL) was added as the internal standard. Volatile compounds were analyzed using a HS-GC-IMS system. Separation was performed on an MXT-WAX capillary column (30 m × 0.53 mm × 1 μm; Restek, USA) with nitrogen as the carrier gas. The column temperature was held constant at 60 °C.

Samples were incubated at 80 °C for 15 min (Liu et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2021), and 200 μL of the headspace was injected. Drift gas programming was set as follows: E1 = 75 mL/min; E2 = 2 mL/min (0–2 min), linear ramp to 10 mL/min (2–10 min), 100 mL/min (10–20 min), 150 mL/min (20–25 min), and held at 150 mL/min (25–30 min). All samples were analyzed in triplicate.

A series of C4–C9 ketones was used as external references to establish the retention index calibration. Volatile compounds were putatively identified by comparing experimental retention indices (RIs) and drift times with reference values in the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) RI database and the built-in IMS library.

Notably, identification by HS-GC-IMS relies on library matching and cannot fully distinguish structural isomers with similar retention and drift behavior. Therefore, annotations from HS-GC-IMS are considered tentative. Complementary techniques such as GC–MS or two-dimensional GC are recommended for unambiguous confirmation of isomers.

HS-SPME-GC–MS analysis

Volatile compounds were extracted and concentrated using headspace solid‑phase microextraction (HS-SPME). An 80 μm DVB/CAR/PDMS fiber (Thermo, USA) was used for extraction. Based on previous studies (Li et al., 2023c; Ye et al., 2022), the optimized conditions were as follows: In a 20 mL headspace vial, 5 mL of Huangjiu was mixed with 10 μL of the internal standard (2-octanol, 0.50 mg/mL). The vial was sealed and equilibrated at 60 °C for 15 min. The SPME fiber was conditioned at 270 °C for 60 min in the GC injector prior to extraction, then exposed to the sample headspace at 60 °C for 35 min for adsorption. After extraction, the fiber was immediately inserted into the GC injector and desorbed at 250 °C for 7 min. Each sample was analyzed in triplicate.

Volatile compounds were separated and identified using a Thermo Scientific Trace 1610 gas chromatograph coupled with a Thermo Scientific SQ9610 mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, USA). Chromatographic separation was performed on a TG-5 SILMS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm; Thermo Scientific). Helium (99.999%) was used as the carrier gas at a constant flow rate of 1.5 mL/min.

The oven temperature program was set as follows: held at 50 °C for 3 min, ramped at 5 °C/min to 80 °C (held for 5 min), 5 °C/min to 100 °C (held for 5 min), 5 °C/min to 150 °C (held for 5 min), 5 °C/min to 180 °C (held for 3 min), then 8 °C/min to 230 °C (held for 3 min). Mass spectrometry was operated in electron ionization (EI) mode at an ion source temperature of 230 °C, with a mass scan range of m/z 35–500.

Compounds were identified by matching mass spectra against the NIST 23 mass spectral library. Only compounds with a reverse match factor (RSI) > 800 were retained for further analysis (Qin et al., 2017; Stein, 2012). RIs were calculated using a C7–C40 n-alkane mixture analyzed under identical chromatographic conditions (Yu et al., 2024). Identities were further confirmed by comparing experimental RIs with literature values obtained on similar non-polar capillary columns. Quantification was performed using the internal standard method according to the following equation:Concentration (target VOC) = [Peak area (target VOC) × Mass (IS)] / [Peak area (IS) × Volume (sample)]

Calculation of OAV

OAVs were calculated to evaluate the aroma contribution of each volatile compound. The OAV was determined as: OAV = Cᵢ / Tᵢ, where Cᵢ is the quantified concentration of the target volatile compound (μg/kg), and Tᵢ is its odor threshold in water (μg/kg). Odor thresholds were primarily obtained from Odor Thresholds: Compilations of Odor Threshold Values in Air, Water and Other Media (2nd ed., Van Gemert, 2011) and supplemented with data from peer-reviewed publications (Yu et al., 2021). For compounds with multiple reported thresholds, the most recent and reliable value determined in aqueous media was used.

Statistical analysis

Differences in electronic tongue response values among four Huangjiu types were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by LSD post-hoc test (p < 0.05). Volatile compounds were identified using the built-in Library Search module, NIST database, and IMS library of the FlavourSpec® GC-IMS system. Fingerprint and topographic plots were generated using the Reporter and Gallery Plot plugins.

Results and discussion

Total soluble solids (°Bx) analysis

As sugar content serves as a key quality indicator during Huangjiu fermentation and commercial grading (Jin et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2023), the four types of Huangjiu exhibited significant differences in total °Bx (Fig. 2A), increasing in the following order: dry < semi-dry < semi-sweet < sweet. This trend is highly consistent with the official classification standards for commercial Huangjiu.Fig. 2Total soluble solids and E-tongue analysis of the four types of Huangjiu. Total soluble solids and E-tongue analysis of the four types of Huangjiu. (A) Distribution of °Bx in four Huangjiu types; (B) Radar plot of standardized E-tongue taste profiles; (C) Bar chart of E-tongue response values with statistical comparisons.

Specifically, dry Huangjiu displayed the lowest °Bx content with the narrowest distribution range, indicating high product uniformity within this category. Semi-dry Huangjiu showed slightly higher °Bx levels with a relatively consistent distribution. Semi-sweet Huangjiu exhibited further elevated °Bx content, along with slightly greater variability. In contrast, sweet Huangjiu exhibited the highest °Bx content and the widest data dispersion (p < 0.05), reflecting greater intrinsic variation among commercial sweet-type products. This clear gradient validates the pre-classified commercial grouping of the samples and provides a solid foundation for subsequent flavor differentiation.

Notably, sweetness perception in Huangjiu can be enhanced by aroma-taste interactions. For example, compounds such as furfural, phenylacetaldehyde, furfuryl alcohol, and furanones provide caramel-like notes and have been reported to enhance sweet sensory perception (Liu et al., 2021).

It should be noted that the refractometric measurement (°Bx) represents total soluble solids rather than absolute sugar content. In complex alcoholic matrices like Huangjiu, components such as ethanol and glycerol can independently influence the refractive index, potentially overestimating the true sugar concentration. While this rapid method is sufficient for confirming the broad relative gradient among the pre-classified commercial categories in this study, future quantitative analyses should employ standard titrimetric methods (e.g., Fehling or DNS methods per GB/T 13662) for precise sugar determination.

Analysis by E-nose and E-tongue

Ultrasensitive sensor-based characterization has become a powerful and reliable approach for evaluating food quality, enabling precise, objective, and reproducible signal acquisition (Du et al., 2025). E-nose and E-tongue technologies simulate human olfaction and taste perception, respectively, by generating electrical signals in response to volatile and non-volatile taste-active compounds. These tools are widely applied in food, cosmetics, and pharmaceutical research (Feng et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022).

The E-tongue results (Fig. 2B, C) revealed distinct response patterns among the four Huangjiu types, with different taste indicators exhibiting varying discriminatory power. The radar plot (Fig. 2B) shows that sweet Huangjiu exhibited the strongest sourness response, and dry Huangjiu showed high aftertaste signals, while semi-dry and semi-sweet Huangjiu displayed prominent umami and saltiness responses. Notably, the saltiness sensor of the E-tongue primarily reflects the overall ionic strength of the sample matrix (such as organic acid salts and mineral ions), rather than specifically responding to NaCl-equivalent salty perception. Therefore, these elevated responses likely reflect the complex ionic and amino acid compositions of these specific Huangjiu matrices (Li et al., 2024a; Moore et al., 2021).

As supported by the one-way ANOVA and post-hoc comparisons (Fig. 2C), sourness, saltiness, and richness were the most powerful indicators for distinguishing the four Huangjiu types, with all three showing significant gradient differences (p < 0.05). Notably, the umami values for all types were high and well above the taste threshold, consistent with the well-known contribution of amino acids to the characteristic umami taste of Huangjiu (Zhao et al., 2022). Bitterness in Huangjiu mainly arises from bitter metal ions (Mg2⁺, K⁺) and bitter amino acids including lysine, leucine, isoleucine, and histidine (Li et al., 2023a; Thiansilakul et al., 2007). Given that these samples represent commercially available products, the observed flavor differences among the Huangjiu types are driven by the combined effects of sugar content, specific raw materials, microbial starter cultures, and aging regimes, rather than being attributable to any single mechanism (Muñoz-Castells et al., 2025; Tao et al., 2014; Zhao et al., 2024b).

The response intensity of the E-nose sensors reflects both the composition and concentration of volatile odorants. As shown in Fig. 3A, the 10 sensors displayed similar response trends but significantly different signal intensities among the four types, indicating that the samples possessed characteristic and distinguishable volatile aroma profiles. An OPLS-DA was performed to quantify and visualize group differences. The model showed a good fit: R2X = 0.737, R2Y = 0.568, and Q2 = 0.517. The score plot (Fig. 3B) reveals a clear separation along the t[1] axis: sweet Huangjiu clustered on the left, dry Huangjiu in the lower-left, and semi-dry and semi-sweet Huangjiu on the right side of the plot. These results confirm that E-nose responses effectively discriminate the aroma profiles of different Huangjiu types. A 200-permutation test (Fig. 3C) confirmed the model was not overfitted: the intercepts of the R2 and Q2 regression lines were both <0, and the original model values were significantly higher than all randomized models, indicating high stability and reliability.Fig. 3E-nose response characteristics and OPLS-DA analysis of the four Huangjiu types. VIP analysis was utilized to identify key sensors contributing to the classification (VIP ≥ 1) (Wang et al., 2023), which included W3C, W6S, W5S, W5C, and W1S. Among these, W5S (nitrogen oxides) and W1S (short-chain alkanes) showed strong responses across all samples, suggesting that alkane formation and the generation of nitrogen oxides during brewing and storage are important processes contributing to the Huangjiu flavor (Zhang et al., 2024). In addition, W1W (sulfur compounds) had a VIP value close to 1 and showed high responses in most samples, indicating that Huangjiu is rich in sulfur-containing volatiles, which contribute significantly to its characteristic aroma (Huang et al., 2025).

Esters and alcohols were the most abundant classes of volatiles, with ethyl esters and butyl esters being the predominant ester types. Esters are mainly formed via non-enzymatic and microbial enzymatic esterification pathways (Solomando et al., 2021). They typically have low odor thresholds and pleasant fruity notes, making them major contributors to the Huangjiu aroma. Alcohols mainly originate from carbohydrate fermentation, methyl ketone reduction, amino acid metabolism, and lipid oxidation. They contribute to sweetness and aroma, and also act as precursors for ester synthesis. Phenethyl alcohol was identified as the primary higher alcohol in Huangjiu, providing floral notes (Wang et al., 2020). Aldehydes also contribute strongly to the overall aroma due to their low odor thresholds. They are mainly generated through lipid oxidation and the oxidative cleavage of unsaturated fatty acids.

Qualitative analysis of volatile compounds

Volatile compounds were putatively identified by comparing RIs and drift times with the NIST and IMS libraries embedded in the instrument software. A total of 76 volatile components (including monomers and dimers) were detected across all samples, consisting of 20 alcohols, 22 esters, 10 ketones, 9 aldehydes, 6 acids, 3 pyrazines, 2 terpenes, and 4 other compounds. Alcohols and esters together accounted for over half of the total volatiles concentrations (Table 3), confirming their role as dominant flavor components in Huangjiu, which is consistent with previous reports (Ye et al., 2022). Key compounds included isoamyl acetate and ethyl lactate, which impart pineapple and banana-like aromas (Feng et al., 2020). Butyl propanoate was the most abundant ester across all types, contributing to the characteristic fruity aroma of Huangjiu. Semi-sweet Huangjiu showed the highest levels of esters and ketones (Fig. 4C) and a more diverse and comprehensive volatile profile (Fig. S1). HS-GC-IMS shows higher sensitivity for low-boiling compounds than HS-SPME-GC–MS (Li et al., 2024c). Aldehydes, such as butyraldehyde, detected in this study were consistent with previous reports (Lan et al., 2025). Aldehydes contribute significantly to the Huangjiu flavor, mainly providing sweet and fruity notes. Octanal, for example, contributes citrus-like aromas (Cai et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2023). 2-Pentylfuran was most abundant in semi-sweet Huangjiu. Furan derivatives are typically associated with caramel-like odors and contribute to the mellow mouthfeel of Huangjiu (Liu et al., 2021).

The fingerprint gallery plot (Fig. S1) shows that semi-sweet Huangjiu possessed the most abundant and diverse volatile composition, with 11 unique components including 2-heptanol, 2-hexanol, (E)‑α‑ionone, 2-octanone, octanal, and isobornyl acetate. These may serve as potential marker compounds for characterizing commercial semi-sweet Huangjiu. Regions marked with yellow boxes correspond to volatile components that are present at low levels or absent in dry and semi-dry Huangjiu, whereas regions marked with white boxes represent compounds that are scarce or undetected in sweet Huangjiu. Moreover, clear differences were observed between the commercial Shaoxing and Jimo styles (Fig. 4D). A total of 14 differential compounds were identified, including (Z)-2-pentenol, geraniol, 2-heptanol, 2-hexanol, cinnamyl alcohol, 2-isopropyl-5-methylphenol, octanal, butanoic acid butyl ester, 2-nonanone, 2-octanone, (E)-α-ionone, isobornyl acetate, dimethyl trisulfide, and 2-ethyl-3-methylpyrazine. These differences reflect the combined effects of raw materials, starter cultures, and specific brewing techniques, forming a robust volatile signature for distinguishing these two representative commercial styles.

Notably, because this study evaluated commercially available Huangjiu products, variables such as sugar content, raw materials, geographical origin, fermentation technology, starter cultures, and aging regimes are naturally confounded. Therefore, the observed sensory and volatile differences should be interpreted as the holistic characteristics of distinct commercial types, rather than being solely attributable to any single factor.

Discriminative volatile markers and OAV analysis

An OPLS-DA was performed based on the 76 volatile compounds to identify key markers responsible for flavor differentiation. The 3D score plot (Fig. 5A) showed clear and tight clustering of the four groups, indicating excellent repeatability. Each point represents the mean of triplicate determinations, rather than the raw data of an individual sample. Model performance was excellent: R2X = 0.927, R2Y = 0.994, and Q2 = 0.983. A permutation test with 200 iterations (Fig. 5B) confirmed no overfitting, as the Q2 intercept was below zero. These findings are consistent with previously reported studies on Huangjiu (Bai et al., 2025; Xue et al., 2024). VIP values, odor thresholds, and OAVs of the key discriminative volatile compounds in four Huangjiu types.

Results showed that 23 compounds had an OAV ≥ 1 in at least one sample, indicating their role as key aroma-active compounds (OAVs for the remaining seven compounds could not be determined due to a lack of available odor thresholds). (Z)-4-Heptenal exhibited extremely high OAVs (4012.50–5537.50) across all types, making it the most dominant aroma contributor. Similarly, (E,E)-2,4-nonadienal showed very high OAVs (391.25–1365.00), with the highest value in sweet Huangjiu. Furanone derivatives also contributed strongly; for example, 5-ethyl-4-hydroxy-2-methyl-3(2H)-furanone yielded OAVs ranging from 153.00 to 320.50, and 3-hydroxy-4,5-dimethyl-2(5H)-furanone presented OAVs between 8.67 and 78.33, with much higher values in semi-sweet and sweet Huangjiu. 2-Phenylethanol showed higher OAVs in semi-dry, semi-sweet, and sweet Huangjiu (9.49–12.04) compared to dry Huangjiu (1.52). Other compounds with consistently high OAVs included butyl 2-methylbutyrate (44.94–72.18), 3-hepten-2-one (29.64–146.61), and 3-methylbutyl butanoate (41.33–94.67). These compounds collectively form the core aroma profile of Huangjiu.

In contrast, cyclopentanone showed an OAV < 1 in all samples, indicating that while it is chemically discriminatory (VIP > 1), it does not contribute independently to the overall sensory aroma.

To further explore the potential relationships between the identified volatile compounds and E-tongue taste attributes, an exploratory Pearson correlation analysis was conducted (Fig. S1, Supplementary Data). However, it must be explicitly acknowledged that this analysis is strictly exploratory in nature. Due to the high number of pairwise tests relative to the sample size, there is an inherent statistical risk of spurious associations (the multiple comparisons problem) as no false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied. Furthermore, because E-tongue signals represent instrumental responses rather than true human sensory evaluations, these correlations reflect mathematical chemometric associations rather than direct causal perceptual links. These preliminary findings are provided solely as supplementary observations, and future studies utilizing human sensory panels and larger cohorts are required to validate these exploratory instrumental correlations.

Volatile Compounds in Four Huangjiu Types (HS-SPME-GC–MS).

The numbers of volatile compounds detected in semi-dry, semi-sweet, sweet, and dry Huangjiu were 44, 54, 39, and 31, respectively (Fig. 6A), indicating that semi-dry and semi-sweet Huangjiu exhibited greater volatile diversity compared to the other two types. Further analysis revealed that 28 volatile compounds were common to all four Huangjiu types, serving as the basic flavor foundation. Notably, 12 compounds were exclusively detected in semi-sweet Huangjiu, demonstrating that this type exhibited the highest compositional complexity among the four types, which likely contributes to its unique flavor characteristics. Across all four Huangjiu types, esters and alcohols were the predominant volatile compound classes (Fig. 6B), consistent with the typical volatile profile of Huangjiu reported in previous studies (Feng et al., 2020). Specifically, semi-sweet Huangjiu exhibited the highest overall abundance of alcohols and aldehydes. This enrichment is likely driven by the specific fermentation and aging parameters typical of this commercial style. Biochemically, specific qu starter microbial communities and higher residual sugar concentrations can modulate yeast metabolism, thereby promoting the synthesis of higher alcohols. Subsequently, during the extended aging process characteristic of premium semi-sweet Huangjiu, these alcohols undergo slow, continuous oxidation and esterification, which ultimately contributes to the rich aldehyde and ester profiles observed.Fig. 6(A) Number of volatile compounds in four Huangjiu types; (B) Distribution of major volatile compound classes; (C) Venn diagram of volatile compounds from two techniques.Fig. 6

Benzaldehyde, a well-recognized key flavor component of Huangjiu, was detected in all four types, in agreement with previous reports (Feng et al., 2020). This compound imparts a pleasant almond-like aroma (Stijve et al., 2002) and plays a crucial role in harmonizing the overall flavor profile of Huangjiu by complementing other volatile notes. Previous studies report that the concentration of benzaldehyde increases with aging time, likely associated with the accumulation of aldehydes produced by the residual activity of yeast during the aging process (Feng et al., 2020). Another important aldehyde, 5-hydroxymethylfurfural, was also identified. This compound is primarily formed via Maillard reactions during processing and aging, and it plays a significant role in forming the characteristic aroma of Huangjiu, consistent with earlier studies (Asikin et al., 2016; Hodge, 1953; Liu et al., 2021). Esters are critical contributors to the fruity and floral notes of Huangjiu (Zhang et al., 2019), and are mainly formed through esterase-catalyzed esterification reactions between alcohols and carboxylic acids. These precursors are themselves products of the microbial metabolism of sugars and amino acids during the fermentation process (Bai et al., 2025). In addition, 1-pentanol and ethanol were detected by HS-SPME-GC–MS, aligning with previous studies that identified these two alcohols as important flavor-active substances in Huangjiu (Feng et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2022).

Acids represent another major class of flavor-active compounds, as they directly influence both the taste and aroma of the product. For instance, acetic acid and octanoic acid contribute characteristic sour notes, in line with reported findings (Feng et al., 2020). Low-molecular-weight acids, such as acetic acid, can be generated through the cleavage of monosaccharides or the oxidation of aldehydes during fermentation and aging (Vernin et al., 1992), as well as being derived from raw materials (Feng et al., 2020).

A comparative analysis revealed that 76 and 60 volatile compounds were identified by HS-GC-IMS and HS-SPME-GC–MS, respectively. Among these, 5 were common to both methods (Fig. 6C): ethanol, phenylethyl alcohol, 3-furanmethanol, ethyl lactate, and benzeneacetic acid ethyl ester. Phenylethyl alcohol, detected by both techniques, imparts floral and honey-like notes along with a sweet taste, contributing significantly to the flavor quality (Bai et al., 2025; Feng et al., 2020). Another shared compound, 3-furanmethanol, is associated with sweet, caramel-like, and slightly fruity notes (Liu et al., 2021), further enriching the flavor profile.

The two analytical techniques exhibited complementary strengths in volatile profiling. HS-GC-IMS enabled rapid fingerprinting and sensitive detection of low-boiling-point compounds, including many aldehydes and alcohols, while HS-SPME-GC–MS facilitated a more definitive identification of a broader range of volatile compounds, including certain structural isomers difficult to distinguish by HS-GC-IMS. Specifically, HS-SPME-GC–MS showed high sensitivity toward esters, alcohols, and acids. Conversely, some aldehydes and alcohols were exclusively detected by HS-GC-IMS. These complementary characteristics highlight the value of combining both methods for comprehensive volatile profiling of Huangjiu, as their simultaneous application provides a more complete and accurate representation of the volatile composition (Li et al., 2023d).

Conclusion

This study comprehensively characterized the sensory and volatile profiles of four commercial Huangjiu types. Given that sugar content, raw material, geographical origin, and manufacturing technology are inherently intertwined in commercial products, the observed differences reflect the combined signatures of multiple factors rather than any single variable.

Total soluble solids (°Bx) displayed a clear gradient among the four Huangjiu types (dry < semi-dry < semi-sweet < sweet). This pattern is highly consistent with the official commercial classification standards for Huangjiu, providing a solid foundation for subsequent flavor differentiation.

Analysis by the E-nose and E-tongue revealed significant sensory differences among the four types. The E-tongue results showed that sweet Huangjiu displayed the strongest sourness response, and dry Huangjiu exhibited high aftertaste signals, while semi-dry and semi-sweet Huangjiu showed prominent umami and saltiness responses. Statistical analysis further confirmed that sourness, saltiness, and richness were the most powerful taste indicators for distinguishing the four types of Huangjiu.

Volatile compound analysis using HS-GC-IMS and HS-SPME-GC–MS identified 76 and 60 volatile compounds, respectively. Esters and alcohols were the predominant classes in all samples. Specifically, semi-sweet Huangjiu possessed the highest abundance of these compounds. Benzaldehyde, which contributes a characteristic almond-like aroma, was detected in all four types and played an important role in harmonizing the overall flavor profile. Furthermore, a comparative analysis showed that 28 volatile compounds were shared by all four types, constituting the basic flavor foundation of Huangjiu. Twelve compounds were detected exclusively in semi-sweet Huangjiu, indicating that this type exhibited the highest compositional complexity.

Multivariate analyses (OPLS-DA and VIP) identified 31 key discriminatory markers, including (Z)-4-heptenal, (E,E)-2,4-nonadienal, and 5-ethyl-4-hydroxy-2-methyl-3(2H)-furanone, all of which exhibited high OAVs, confirming their crucial roles in the aroma profile of Huangjiu. Among them, (Z)-4-heptenal showed extremely high OAVs in all samples, emerging as the most dominant aroma contributor.

In summary, the results demonstrate that the flavor differences among commercial Huangjiu types are characterized by specific signatures of volatile components, especially esters, alcohols, aldehydes, and organic acids. By integrating electronic sensory technologies with advanced chromatographic profiling to elucidate the relationships between these flavor-related compounds and their sensory attributes, this study provides Huangjiu producers with objective, instrumental targets for rapid product grading, batch-to-batch quality control, and targeted process optimization. To further build upon this holistic commercial survey, future studies should employ controlled, single-variable fermentation experiments to decipher the precise biological mechanisms driving marker formation, and incorporate trained human sensory panels to translate these chemometric findings into standardized industry practices.
