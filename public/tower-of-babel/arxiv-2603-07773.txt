Revisiting colimits in \tpdf 𝐂𝐚𝐭 \mathbf{Cat} and homotopy category
Varinderjit Mann

Abstract
In this paper, we justify and make precise an elementary approach that establishes the existence of (co)limits in 𝐂𝐚𝐭 \mathbf{Cat} . This approach, while conceptually evident, has not been made fully explicit or systematically described in the literature. We first demonstrate an equivalence between the existence of the homotopy category functor h : 𝐬𝐒𝐞𝐭 → 𝐂𝐚𝐭 h:\mathbf{sSet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Cat} and the existence of a specific class of weighted colimits in 𝐂𝐚𝐭 \mathbf{Cat} . We then construct these weighted colimits explicitly by using certain properties of simplicial sets and the nerve functor. Consequentially, the embedding N : 𝐂𝐚𝐭 ↪ 𝐬𝐒𝐞𝐭 N:\mathbf{Cat}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{sSet} is reflective, and can be used to infer the (co)completeness of 𝐂𝐚𝐭 \mathbf{Cat} . Finally, we use this approach to reformulate the construction of coequalizers and localizations in 𝐂𝐚𝐭 \mathbf{Cat} .

2.1
Category of elements

Any function
f:X\rightarrow Y\in\mathbf{Set}
can be transformed into a discrete functor
\bar{f}:Y\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
by considering fibers over each point
y\in Y
. This transformation is completely reversible by bundling the codomain of
\bar{f}
using coproducts. In particular, there is a bijection between functions
f:X\rightarrow Y\in\mathbf{Set}
with fixed
Y\in\mathbf{Set}
and discrete functors
\bar{f}:Y\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
. A natural generalization is to ask whether arbitrary functors
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Cat}
or
F:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Cat}
can be bundled in some way. The
Grothendieck construction
answers this question in the positive. Our requirements only include functors of the form
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
or
F:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
. In this situation, the Grothendieck construction is also known as the
Category of elements
construction.

Definition 2.1

(Category of elements)
.

1.

For all
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
, the (covariant) category of elements
\el F
is a category defined by:

a)
\obj(\el F)

the collection
\{(*\xrightarrow{x}F(c),c):c\in\mathcal{C},x\in F(c)\}
,

b)
\mor(\el F)

any morphism
(*\xrightarrow{x}F(c),c)\rightarrow(*\xrightarrow{y}F(d),d)\in\el F
is given by a morphism
f:c\rightarrow d\in\mathcal{C}
that satisfy
F(f)(x)=y
.

2.

For all
F:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
, the (contravariant) category of elements
\el F
is a category defined by:

a)
\obj(\el F)

the collection
\{(*\xrightarrow{x}F(c),c):c\in\mathcal{C},x\in F(c)\}
,

b)
\mor(\el F)

any morphism
(*\xrightarrow{x}F(c),c)\rightarrow(*\xrightarrow{y}F(d),d)\in\el F
is given by a morphism
f:c\rightarrow d\in\mathcal{C}
that satisfy
F(f)(y)=x
.

Both of these
\el F
categories come with a canonical projection map
\el F\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
.

A routine check shows that both the constructions
\el
are functorial in the respective functor category.

Lemma 2.2

(
[

The covariant Grothendieck construction

\el:\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Cat}/\mathcal{C}

\el\alpha(c,x)=(c,\alpha_{c}(x))

\el:\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Cat}/\mathcal{C}

2.2
Weighted limits

Weighted limits and colimits generalize ordinary limits and colimits in a canonical way and provide simple formulas for plenty of constructions in category theory. In our case, they provide relevant formulas for computing
Kan extensions
. We mainly present the theory of weighted colimits and derive the theory of weighted limits using duality.

Definition 2.5

(Weighted colimit)
.

Let
W:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
and
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be two arbitrary functors. The weighted colimit
W\star F
, if it exists, is defined by the following natural isomorphism in
d\in\mathcal{D}
:

\mathcal{D}(W\star F,d)\cong\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}(W,\mathcal{D}(F(-),d))\cdpunct{.}

The functor
W
is called the
weight functor
for the weighted colimit
W\star F
.

Remark 2.6
.

The natural isomorphism is itself a part of the data that defines the weighted colimit
W\star F
. In particular, the image of the map
1_{W\star F}:W\star F\rightarrow W\star F
, say
\lambda_{W\star F}\in\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}(W,\mathcal{D}(F(-),W\star F))
is also necessarily a part of this data. In fact, the Yoneda lemma implies that a weighted colimit is completely specified by the pair
(W\star F,\lambda_{W\star F})
, which together are a
universal object

W\star F
and a
universal weighted cocone

\lambda_{W\star F}
.

This definition of weighted colimit is given in terms of a natural isomorphism. Hence, whenever all the weighted colimits exist, they extend to a
2
-ary functor
-\star-:\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}\times\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
using the Yoneda lemma. This
2
-ary functor is also cocontinuous in the weights argument (the left input). This again follows from the defining natural isomorphism.

Lemma 2.7
.

Whenever all the desired weighted colimits exist, there is a canonical way to assemble them into a
2
-ary functor
-\star-:\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}\times\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, which is cocontinuous in its weights argument. That is, the canonical comparison map
\Colim\limits_{i}(W_{i}\star F)\rightarrow(\Colim\limits_{i}W_{i})\star F
is an isomorphism.

The Yoneda embedding acts as a unit for the functor
-\star-
in two distinct ways which provide an important class of examples for weighted colimits.

The first form of unitality is where
\Yo
, as a whole, acts as the right unit and is demonstrated by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

For every functor
W:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
, there exists a natural isomorphism
W\star\Yo\cong W
.

Proof 2.9
.

The Yoneda lemma implies the existence of the following isomorphism natural in
W
and
X
:

\tikzcd{\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}(W,\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\Yo,X))}&{\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}}(W,X)}\arrow["\cong",from=1-1,to=1-3]\cdpunct{.}

This implies that
W
satisfies the universal property of
W\star\Yo
as desired.

The second unitality is symmetric and uses the representable functors
\Yo(c)=\mathcal{C}(-,c):\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
, taken once as a weight functor and once a functor whose weighted colimit is being computed. This is again directly verified using the Yoneda isomorphism and the cartesian closure of
\mathbf{Set}
.

Lemma 2.10

(Left unit)
.

For every functor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, there exists an isomorphism
\Yo(c)\star F\cong F(c)
, which is natural in both
c
and
F
.

Lemma 2.11

(Right unit)
.

For every functor
W:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
, there exists an isomorphism
W(c)\cong W\star\Yo(c)
, which is natural in both
c
and
W
.

We now briefly discuss the dual theory of weighted limits. The dual definition and properties of weighted limits are analogous to that of weighted colimits.

Definition 2.12

(Weighted limit)
.

Let
W:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
and
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be two arbitrary functors. The weighted limit
\{W,F\}
, if it exists, is defined by the following natural isomorphism in
d\in\mathcal{D}
:

\mathcal{D}(d,\{W,F\})\cong\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}}(W,\mathcal{D}(d,F(-)))\cdpunct{.}

Corollary 2.13
.

When all the weighted limits exist, they assemble into a
2
-ary functor
\{-,-\}:(\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}})^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, which is cocontinuous in the weights argument. That is, the canonical comparison map
\{\Colim\limits_{i}W_{i},F\}\rightarrow\Lim\limits_{i}\{W_{i},F\}
is an isomorphism.

However, weighted limits can be dualized in another evident way by declaring that the weighted limit of a functor
F
is equivalent to defining the weighted colimit of the opposite functor
F^{\mathrm{op}}
. This is directly checked by manipulating the defining natural isomorphism of weighted limits and colimits.

Lemma 2.14
.

A weighted limit of a functor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
with respect to a weight
W:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
is equivalently defined as the weighted colimit of
F^{\mathrm{op}}:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}
with respect to the same weight
W:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
. Let
-\star-:\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}}\times(\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}})^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}
and
\{-,-\}:(\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}})^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be the two defining functors. Then, there exists a natural isomorphism:

\left(-\star-\right)^{\mathrm{op}}\cong\{-,-\}:(\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}})^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}\cdpunct{.}

One of the most important example of weighted limits include the set of natural transformation between two functors
F,G:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
. In particular, there is a natural isomorphism
\mathbf{Set}(X,\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}}(F,G))\cong\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}}(F,\mathbf{Set}(X,G-))
(natural in all
F,G,X
) that exhibits
\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}}(F,G)
as the weighted limit of
G
with respect to
F
. This follows from cartesian closure of
\mathbf{Set}
applied pointwise at
c\in\mathcal{C}
.

The only substantial difference of weighted limits from weighted colimits is in the unitality property of
\Yo
. In this case,
\Yo
behaves as a unit only in the following sense.

Lemma 2.15

(left unit)
.

For every
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, there exists an isomorphism
\{\mathcal{C}(c,-),F\}\cong F(c)
, which is natural in both
F
and
c\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof 2.16
.

This is a restatement of the Yoneda lemma with the observation that the set of natural transformation can be expressed as a weighted limit.

Finally, the following examples illustrates how weighted limits and colimits generalize the ordinary ones. In essence, the weights dictate the
“shape”
of the cone point. Thus, we retrieve the usual definition of limits or colimits when we take the
“shape”
to be an actual single point.

Example 2.17

(Ordinary limits)
.

Let
\Delta*:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
be the constant functor at the terminal object
*\in\mathbf{Set}
. For every functor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, there exists a natural isomorphism
\{\Delta*,F\}\cong\Lim F
, whenever the desired limits exist.

Proof 2.18
.

By definition of ordinary limits using the category of cones, we have the natural isomorphism
\mathcal{D}(d,\lim F)\cong\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}(\Delta d,F(-))
. Now, we observe that
\mathbf{Set}^{\mathcal{C}}(\Delta*,\mathcal{D}(d,F(-)))\cong\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}(\Delta d,F(-))
by applying cartesian closure of
\mathbf{Set}
pointwise. Hence, we are done.

Example 2.19

(Ordinary colimits)
.

Let
\Delta*:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
be the constant functor at the terminal object
*\in\mathbf{Set}
. For every functor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, there exists a natural isomorphism
{\Delta*}\star F\cong\Colim F
, whenever the desired colimits exist.

Remark 2.20
.

On the surface, it may seem that weighted limits strictly generalize ordinary limits. However, it turns out that this is not true in
\mathbf{Set}
. There is another characterization of weighted limits using the Grothendieck construction on weights, which shows that weighted limits can be expressed completely using ordinary limits. We provide a complete description in
lemma

2.36
.

5.2
\tpdf
Coequalizers in
\mathbf{Cat}

While we have already provided a complete description of coproducts, we haven’t yet done the same for coequalizers. Indeed, we intentionally chose to avoid them until now because of their complexity and their nonessential role in our proof of cocompleteness. To further justify this choice, we now derive a complete description of them using the reflecting embedding
N:\mathbf{Cat}\rightarrow\mathbf{sSet}
.

Consider any two parallel arrow diagram
F,G:\mathcal{C}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{D}
in
\mathbf{Cat}
. The colimit of this diagram is defined as the coequalizer
\mathrm{coeq}(F,G)
. Using the nerve functor, we can reduce the problem to computing the coequalizer of the diagram
NF,NG:N\mathcal{C}\rightrightarrows N\mathcal{D}
(i.e.,
\mathrm{coeq}(NF,NG)
) and then computing its homotopy category. Although coequalizers in simplicial sets are not trivial either, they can still be computed levelwise as coequalizers in
\mathbf{Set}
, whose description is well known. Moreover, we only require this computation up to the
2
-simplices level as this data is sufficient to compute the homotopy category.

Our first goal is to compute the free category
F\mathrm{coeq}(NF,NG)
, which only depends on the two sets:
\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{0},NG_{0})
and
\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{1},NG_{1})
. Let
\pi_{0}:N\mathcal{D}_{0}\rightarrow\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{0},NG_{0})
be the canonical coequalizer map whose action on the elements is denoted as
x\mapsto[x]
. The key idea is that
\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{1},NG_{1})
can be computed locally through a collection of local coequalizers for all
[x],[y]\in\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{0},NG_{0})
.

Definition 5.5
.

For a given
X\in\mathbf{sSet}^{\leq 1}
and all
a,b\in X_{0}
, define an indexed family of sets

\{X_{1}(a,b)\}_{(a,b)\in X_{0}\times X_{0}}\in\mathbf{Set}

by partitioning the set
X_{1}
into the equivalences classes defined by the source and target pairs
(a,b)
. These sets are called the
local (1-simplices) sets
.

Lemma 5.6
.

The local coequalizer
coeq(NF_{1},NG_{1})([x],[y])
is computed by the following:

\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{1},NG_{1})([x],[y])\cong\left(\Coprod\limits_{(i,j)\in[x]\times[y]}N\mathcal{D}_{1}(i,j)\right)\Big/(NF_{1}\sim NG_{1})\cdpunct{.}

Proof 5.7
.

Both
NF
and
NG
are simplicial maps. Hence, they are compatible with the source, target, and identity maps. Consequentially, the coequalizer relations
NF_{\{i\}}(\sigma)\sim NG_{\{i\}}(\sigma)
for all
\sigma\in N\mathcal{C}_{\{i\}}
and
i\in\{0,1\}
are also stable under these three maps. As a result, the coequalizer equivalence relation generated by
NF_{1}\sim NG_{1}
is also compatible with
NF_{0}\sim NG_{0}
using the source, target, and identity maps. This provides the desired expression for the local coequalizer.

The free category
F\mathrm{coeq}(NF,NG)
is then the category formed by freely composing the arrows in the
1
-skeletal simplicial set
\mathrm{coeq}(NF,NG)_{\leq 1}
. Afterwards, we only need to identify the compositions corresponding to whenever there exist some
2
-simplex in
\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{2},GF_{2})
filing a particular
2
-sphere. However, the canonical coequalizer map
N\mathcal{D}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{2},GF_{2})
is a surjective map as it a coequalizer of sets. So, we only require existence the existence of some
2
-simplex in the preimage of this map.

Remark 5.8
.

In our construction of
hX
, the generators are given by declaring
gf\sim h
whenever there exists a
2
-simplex filling the
2
-sphere formed by
f,g,h
. In particular, this relation does not depend upon which
2
-simplex it is or how many such
2
-simplices there are. Hence, we only need to choose one
2
-simplex filling a given
2
-sphere.

Using this observation together with the description of
F\mathrm{coeq}(NF,NG)
, we obtain the following lemma providing a complete description of coequalizers in
\mathbf{Cat}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

The coequalizer
\mathrm{coeq}(F,G)
is described as follows:

objects

are given by the collection
\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{0},NG_{0})
,

morphisms

are given by the collection of homsets
F\mathrm{coeq}(NF,NG)([x],[y])
with objects
[x],[y]\in\mathrm{coeq}(NF_{0},NG_{0})
, subject to the relations
[g]\circ[f]=[h]
whenever there exists any corresponding representatives
f^{\prime},g^{\prime},h^{\prime}\in N\mathcal{D}_{1}
that are the boundary of a
2
-simplex in
N\mathcal{D}_{2}
.

The important takeaway is that verifying the universal property of above construction is highly intricate and cumbersome. In particular, our approach was designed to circumvent this difficulty.

5.3
Localization

Let
I
be the free isomorphism category and let
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be an arbitrary functor that satisfy
F(f)\in\mathrm{iso}(\mathcal{D})
for all
f\in\mathcal{C}
. Each commuting square

\tikzcd{\Coprod\limits_{f\in\mor\mathcal{C}}[1]}&\mathcal{C}\\
\\
{\Coprod\limits_{f\in\mor\mathcal{C}}I}\mathcal{D}\arrow[from=1-1,to=1-3]\arrow[hook^{\prime},from=1-1,to=3-1]\arrow["F",from=1-3,to=3-3]\arrow[from=3-1,to=3-3]

encapsulate such a functor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, which factors as
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathrm{iso}(\mathcal{D})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}
by definition. Conversely, each such functor that factors through the isomorphisms extends uniquely to such a square.

Thus, we can construct and analyze a universal such square by using pushouts. This pushout allows us to construct a non-trivial adjoint to the inclusion functor
\mathbf{Grpd}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Cat}
.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C})
be the pushout defined by the following square:

\tikzcd{\Coprod\limits_{f\in\mor\mathcal{C}}[1]}&\mathcal{C}\\
\\
{\Coprod\limits_{f\in\mor\mathcal{C}}I}{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C})}\arrow[from=1-1,to=1-3]\arrow[hook^{\prime},from=1-1,to=3-1]\arrow["{\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}}",from=1-3,to=3-3]\arrow[from=3-1,to=3-3]\arrow["\ulcorner"{anchor=center,pos=0.125,rotate=180},draw=none,from=3-3,to=1-1]\cdpunct[20pt]{.}

We call
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C})
the
total localization
of
\mathcal{C}
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
as the
localization map
of
\mathcal{C}
.

While computing
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C})
is difficult, we can utilize the nerve functor to provide a concrete description for it. To this end, we define
\mathcal{L}_{N}(N\mathcal{C})
to be the pushout given by the following diagram:

\tikzcd{\Coprod\limits_{f\in\mor\mathcal{C}}N[1]}&{N\mathcal{C}}\\
\\
{\Coprod\limits_{f\in\mor\mathcal{C}}NI}{\mathcal{L}_{N}(N\mathcal{C})}\arrow[from=1-1,to=1-3]\arrow[hook^{\prime},from=1-1,to=3-1]\arrow[from=1-3,to=3-3]\arrow[from=3-1,to=3-3]\arrow["\ulcorner"{anchor=center,pos=0.125,rotate=180},draw=none,from=3-3,to=1-1]\cdpunct[20pt]{.}

Now using that

preserve arbitrary coproducts, we obtain

.

Lemma 5.11

The map

\tikzcd&\bullet\bullet\bullet\\

\tikzcd&\bullet\bullet\bullet\bullet\bullet\\
\bullet\bullet\bullet\bullet\\
\bullet\bullet\\
\bullet\bullet\bullet\bullet\bullet\arrow[""{name=0,anchor=center,innersep=0},"{f^{-1}}"^{\prime},from=1-2,to=2-1]\arrow["f",from=1-5,to=1-6]\arrow["g",from=1-6,to=1-7]\arrow[""{name=1,anchor=center,innersep=0},"f"^{\prime},from=2-3,to=1-4]\arrow[""{name=2,anchor=center,innersep=0},"gf"^{\prime},from=2-5,to=2-7]\arrow["f"^{\prime},from=3-2,to=4-1]\arrow["f",from=3-2,to=4-3]\arrow[shiftleft=5,between={0}{0.6},equals,from=4-3,to=4-4]\arrow["1",from=4-4,to=4-4,loop,in=60,out=120,distance=5mm]\arrow["f",from=4-5,to=3-6]\arrow[shiftright=5,between={0}{0.6},equals,from=4-5,to=4-4]\arrow["f"^{\prime},from=4-7,to=3-6]\arrow[between={0.2}{0.8},equals,from=0,to=1]\arrow[between={0}{0.8},equals,from=1-6,to=2]\cdpunct[6pt]{.}

Proof 5.12
.

The bijection on objects of the functor
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C})
follows directly from the construction of pushouts. In particular, we are not modifying the
0
-simplices by the constructing of the pushout
\mathcal{L}_{N}
or by applying the functor
h
.

At the level of morphisms, we are freely adding an inverse arrow corresponding to each
f\in\mor\mathcal{C}
. We can label these newly added arrows as well as the already existing isomorphisms as
f^{-1}
for each such
f
. Note that if a map
f:x\rightarrow y\in\mathcal{C}
already has an inverse
f^{-1}:y\rightarrow x\in\mathcal{C}
then the uniqueness of inverses ensures that whatever new inverse we freely add for
f
is already identified with
f^{-1}
. As a consequence, at the free category level, the homsets will contain strings of maps generated by
f
and
f^{-1}
. For example,
f_{1}f_{2}^{-1}f_{1}f_{3}^{-1}
is one such possible string. At the level of homotopy category, we have to enforce the relations imposed by the original category
\mathcal{C}
, as well as impose that
ff^{-1}=1=f^{-1}f
for each
f
. This provides the desired identification with a zigzag by the depicting the maps
f^{-1}
in the string as a backward arrow in the zigzag.

The localization construction
\mathcal{L}(-)
is functorial in
\mathbf{Cat}
. In particular, it defines a functor
\mathcal{L}:\mathbf{Cat}\rightarrow\mathbf{Cat}
or
\mathbf{Cat}\rightarrow\mathbf{Grpd}

also extends to a natural transformation

by the universality of pushouts.

Lemma 5.13
.

There exists a triple of adjoints

\tikzcd\mathbf{Grpd}&\mathbf{Cat}\arrow[""{name=0,anchor=center,innersep=0},"i"{description},hook,from=1-1,to=1-5]\arrow[""{name=1,anchor=center,innersep=0},"\mathrm{iso}",shiftleft=3,curve={height=-18pt},from=1-5,to=1-1]\arrow[""{name=2,anchor=center,innersep=0},"\mathcal{L}"^{\prime},shiftright=3,curve={height=18pt},from=1-5,to=1-1]\arrow["\dashv"{anchor=center,rotate=-91},draw=none,from=0,to=1]\arrow["\dashv"{anchor=center,rotate=-89},draw=none,from=2,to=0]\cdpunct[0pt]{,}
