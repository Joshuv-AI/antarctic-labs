Nesterov Flow May Travel Infinitely Long to Converge to a Minimizer
Ernest K. Ryu

Abstract
Recent work has established that the trajectory of the Nesterov ODE, a the continuous-time model of Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method, exhibits point convergence towards a minimizer of a convex potential. A natural next question is whether this point convergence can be upgraded to rectifiability, namely whether the convergent orbit has finite path length. This work provides the answer in the negative by constructing a differentiable convex potential in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} for which the flow converges to its minimizer but still accumulates infinite path length. All proofs of this work are due entirely to an internal model at OpenAI.

2.2
Creation and conservation of angular momentum

Consider the angular momentum

J(t):=\det\bigl(X(t),\dot{X}(t)\bigr).

In the ODE
\ddot{X}(t)+\frac{3}{t}\dot{X}(t)+\nabla f_{\varepsilon,a}(X(t))=0
, the friction coefficient
3/t
dissipates angular momentum. However, the quantity
t^{3}J(t)
is conserved once the dynamics enters the radial region.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
t>0
,

t^{3}J(t)=\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}s^{3}X_{2}(s)\,\bigl(X_{1}(s)-a\bigr)_{+}\,\,\mathrm{d}s,

where we use the notation
X(t)=(X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
In particular, for all
t\geq T_{\mathrm{rad}}
,

t^{3}J(t)=\kappa_{\varepsilon},\qquad\kappa_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T_{\mathrm{rad}}}s^{3}X_{2}(s)(X_{1}(s)-a)_{+}\,\,\mathrm{d}s.

Proof.

Differentiating
J
, we get

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\det\bigl(\dot{X}(t),\dot{X}(t)\bigr)+\det\bigl(X(t),\ddot{X}(t)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\det\!\Big(X(t),-\frac{3}{t}\dot{X}(t)-\nabla f_{\varepsilon,a}(X(t))\Big).

Now the radial part
x\mapsto F(\|x\|)
contributes no torque, since its gradient is parallel to
x
. For the perturbation,

\nabla\bigl(\varepsilon\Psi_{a}(x_{1})\bigr)=\bigl(\varepsilon(x_{1}-a)_{+},0\bigr),

and therefore

\det\Bigl((x_{1},x_{2}),\nabla(\varepsilon\Psi_{a}(x_{1}))\Bigr)=-\varepsilon x_{2}(x_{1}-a)_{+}.

Hence

J^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{3}{t}J(t)+\varepsilon\,X_{2}(t)(X_{1}(t)-a)_{+}.

Multiplying by
t^{3}
, integrating, and using
J(0)=0
yields the first claim.
Since
(X_{1}(t)-a)_{+}=0
for
t\geq T_{\mathrm{rad}}
, the right-hand side becomes constant for
t\geq T_{\mathrm{rad}}
, proving the second claim.
∎

For a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0

\kappa_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T_{\mathrm{rad}}}s^{3}X_{2}(s)(X_{1}(s)-a)_{+}\,\,\mathrm{d}s>0.

(It can be shown that

2.3
Asymptotic dynamics and infinite path length

Let

r(t)=\|X(t)\|

for
t\geq 0
. By the convergence results of prior work,
r(t)\rightarrow 0
.
We first note a convenient lower bound on

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists
c_{F}>0
such that

F(r)\geq c_{F}\,\frac{r}{-\log r}\qquad\text{for all }0<r\leq e^{-2}.

Proof.

For
0<r\leq e^{-2}
, apply a change of varaibles for the integral defining

and get

F(r)=\int_{R}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-v}}{v}\,\,\mathrm{d}v,

Then,

F(r)\geq\int_{R}^{R+1}\frac{e^{-v}}{v}\,\,\mathrm{d}v\geq\frac{e^{-(R+1)}}{R+1}=\frac{r}{e(R+1)}\geq\frac{1}{2e}\,\frac{r}{R}=\frac{1}{2e}\,\frac{r}{-\log r}.

Next, we show the rate of

(1)

3
Further discussions

3.1
Rectifiability with quadratic potentials

Upon seeing the construction in Theorem
2.1
, one may wonder whether a simpler quadratic example could exhibit the same nonrectifiable behavior. In this section, we show that this cannot occur for pure quadratic potentials.

Let

q(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Qx,

where
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is symmetric positive semidefinite, and consider

\ddot{X}(t)+\frac{3}{t}\dot{X}(t)+QX(t)=0,\qquad t>0,\qquad X(0)=X_{0},\qquad\dot{X}(0)=0.

Let

Q=U^{\top}\Lambda U
, where
U
is orthogonal and
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})
with
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
. Writing

y(t)=UX(t),\qquad a=UX_{0},

we obtain the decoupled scalar equations

\ddot{y}_{i}(t)+\frac{3}{t}\dot{y}_{i}(t)+\lambda_{i}y_{i}(t)=0,\qquad y_{i}(0)=a_{i},\qquad\dot{y}_{i}(0)=0

for
i=1,\dots,d
.

Let
J_{\alpha}
denote the Bessel function of the first kind of order
\alpha
.
From the
[
26
, Section 3.2]
, we know that the solutions are given by

y_{i}(t)=\begin{cases}a_{i},&\lambda_{i}=0,\\[4.0pt]
\displaystyle 2a_{i}\,\frac{J_{1}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\,t)}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\,t},&\lambda_{i}>0.\end{cases}

In the case
\lambda_{i}>0
one also has

\dot{y}_{i}(t)=-\frac{2a_{i}}{t}J_{2}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\,t),\qquad t>0.

Then, we conclude the curve is rectifiable:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\dot{X}(t)\|\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\dot{y}(t)\|\,\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\dot{y}_{i}(t)\|\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\|a_{i}\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\|J_{2}(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}t)\|}{t}\,\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\|a_{i}\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\|J_{2}(s)\|}{s}\,\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty.

3.2
Gradient flow is radial, rectifiable, and reaches the minimizer in finite time

Upon seeing the pathological dynamics of Nesterov flow, one may wonder whether
gradient flow

\dot{X}=-\nabla f(X)
on the potential
f_{\varepsilon,a}
also exhibits similarly pathological behavior. In this section, we argue that it does not: gradient-flow trajectories have finite path length and reach the minimizer in
finite time
.

At a high level, the argument is as follows. Since gradient flow converges, the trajectory eventually enters the radial region. Once inside that region, the potential is radial, so the trajectory evolves along a straight ray toward the origin; this immediately yields rectifiability. The remaining dynamics reduce to a one-dimensional ODE for the radius, whose explicit analysis shows finite-time arrival at the minimizer.

This comparison highlights the point that while the momentum-driven Nesterov acceleration does improve worst-case function-value guarantees, but it may slow down convergence and cause extreme oscillatory behavior in some instances.
In other words, Nesterov flow may be worse than gradient flow for some functions.

3.3
Further pathological properties of the dynamics

In this section, we further analyze the dynamics induced by
f=f_{\varepsilon,a}
and obtain further insights on the worst-case behavior of Nesterov flow.

We first review the standard Lyapunov analysis for the critical Nesterov flow.

Lemma 3.1
.

Consider the setup of Theorem
2.1
.
The Lyapunov function

\mathcal{E}(t)=t^{2}f_{\varepsilon,a}(X(t))+\frac{1}{2}\|2X(t)+t\dot{X}(t)\|^{2}.

is nonincreasing on
[0,\infty)
. Consequently,

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}(0)=2\|X_{0}\|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

Proof.

Follows from the analysis of Theorem 3 of
[
26
]
.
∎

Next, we show that the function value
f(X(t))
actually matches the
\mathcal{O}(1/t^{2})
upper bound up to logarithmic factors. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first construction in which Nesterov flow exhibits an actual convergence rate slower than
\Theta(1/t^{3})
, which is the rate achieved by quadratic potentials
[
26
, Section 3.2]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

In the setup of Theorem
2.1
,
there exist constants
c_{0},C_{0},T_{0}>0
such that

\frac{c_{0}}{t^{2}\log t}\leq f(X(t))\leq\frac{C_{0}}{t^{2}}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq T_{0}.

Proof.

The upper bound follows immediately from prior work
[
26
, Theorem 3]
.
We next obtain a lower bound on
r(t)
. By Lemma
3.1
,

t\|\dot{X}(t)\|\leq\|2X(t)+t\dot{X}(t)\|+2\|X(t)\|\leq\sqrt{20a^{2}}+2\|X(t)\|.

Because
X(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, there exists a constant
C_{v}>0
such that

t\|\dot{X}(t)\|\leq C_{v}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

By (
1
),

\frac{\kappa}{t^{3}r(t)}=\|\dot{X}_{\perp}(t)\|\leq\|\dot{X}(t)\|\leq\frac{C_{v}}{t},\qquad\text{for all }t\geq T_{\mathrm{rad}}.

So,

r(t)\geq\frac{\kappa}{C_{v}\,t^{2}},\qquad\text{for all }t\geq T_{\mathrm{rad}}.

Since
r(t)\rightarrow 0
, we may choose
T_{0}
large enough that

r(t)\leq e^{-2}\quad\text{and}\quad t\geq\max\Bigl\{e,\,\frac{C_{v}}{\kappa},\,T_{\mathrm{rad}}\Bigr\}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq T_{0}.

Then with Lemma
2.4
, we conclude

f(X(t))\geq F(r(t))\geq c_{F}\,\frac{r(t)}{-\log r(t)}\geq c_{F}\,\frac{\kappa/(C_{v}t^{2})}{2\log t-\log(\kappa/C_{v})}\geq\frac{c_{F}\kappa}{3C_{v}\,t^{2}\log t}

for
t\geq T_{0}
.
This proves the lower inequality with
c_{0}=c_{F}\kappa/(3C_{v})
.
∎

Prior work on point convergence for Nesterov-type accelerated methods took the route of proving finiteness of integrals

\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,\big(f(X(t))-\min f\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty,\qquad\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,\|\dot{X}(t)\|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

By contrast,
[
18
,
12
]
established point convergence without such bounds, and suggested that these integrals may be infinite even when convergence holds. The following result provides closure to this question: point convergence does not require their finiteness. This also explains that the earlier attempts to prove point convergence via such estimates were pursuing an unviable path.

Theorem 3.3
.

In the setup of Theorem
2.1
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,f_{\varepsilon,a}(X(t))\,\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty,\qquad\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,\|\dot{X}(t)\|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
,

f_{\varepsilon,a}(X(t))\geq\frac{c_{0}}{t^{2}\log t}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq T_{0}.

Therefore

\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,f_{\varepsilon,a}(X(t))\,\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{T_{0}}^{\infty}t\,f_{\varepsilon,a}(X(t))\,\,\mathrm{d}t\geq c_{0}\int_{T_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t\log t}=\infty.

This proves the first claim.

For the second claim, define

\mathcal{K}(t):=t^{2}f(X(t))+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\|\dot{X}(t)\|^{2},\qquad t\geq 0.

With direct calculations, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2t\big(f(X(t))-\|\dot{X}(t)\|^{2}\big)+t^{2}\Bigl\langle\dot{X}(t),\ddot{X}(t)+\tfrac{3}{t}\dot{X}(t)+\nabla f(X(t))\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=2t\big(f(X(t))-\|\dot{X}(t)\|^{2}\big)\qquad\text{for all }t>0.

Hence, for all
R\geq T_{0}
,

\underbrace{\int_{T_{0}}^{R}t\,\|\dot{X}(t)\|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}t}_{\rightarrow\infty\text{ as }R\rightarrow\infty}=\underbrace{\int_{T_{0}}^{R}t\,f(X(t))\,\,\mathrm{d}t}_{\rightarrow\infty\text{ as }R\rightarrow\infty}+\underbrace{\frac{\mathcal{K}(T_{0})-\mathcal{K}(R)}{2}}_{\text{bounded as }R\rightarrow\infty}.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{K}
is bounded on
[T_{0},\infty)
. The quantity

\mathcal{E}(t)=t^{2}f(X(t))+\frac{1}{2}\|2X(t)+t\dot{X}(t)\|^{2}

is bounded by Lemma
3.1
,
X(t)\rightarrow 0
implies
X(t)
is bounded, so

\|t\dot{X}(t)\|
is bounded. Consequently,
\mathcal{K}
is bounded on
[T_{0},\infty)
.
∎

4
Conclusion

This work identifies a new pathological property of the momentum-driven Nesterov flow: trajectories may have infinite path length despite the point convergence. Together with the parallel line of work bounding the path length of gradient flow, this result highlights path length as a regularity property of interest in the analysis of optimization algorithms.
