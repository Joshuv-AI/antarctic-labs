Group-theoretical property of some integral non-degenerate fusion categories
Zhiqiang Yu

Abstract
We show that an integral non-degenerate fusion category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is group-theoretical if the Frobenius-Perron dimensions of its simple objects are either 1 or powers of a prime p p .



1
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathds{k}
of characteristic zero, we denote
\mathds{k}^{*}:=\mathds{k}\backslash{\{0}\}
.

A fusion category is a semisimple
\mathds{k}
-linear finite abelian tensor category. A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a pointed fusion category if it is tensor equivalent to
\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
, the category of
G
-graded finite-dimensional vector spaces over
\mathds{k}
, where
G
is a finite group and
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,\mathds{k}^{*})
is a
3
-cocycle, we note that the
3
-cocycle
\omega
gives the associativity constraint of
\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
. Recall that a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be group-theoretical if
\mathcal{C}
is (categorically) Morita equivalent to a pointed fusion category
\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
via an indecomposable
\mathcal{C}
-module category, see
[
5
,
8
]
for the precise definitions of module category and Morita equivalence of fusion categories. Equivalently, a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is group-theoretical if and only if there exists a braided tensor equivalence between their Drinfeld centers
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})\cong\mathcal{Z}(\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})

[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Since the property of being group-theoretical for fusion categories is invariant under taking Drinfeld centers and fusion subcategories, fusion subcategories of
\mathcal{Z}(\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
are always group-theoretical. In
[
13
]
, the authors studied explicitly fusion subcategories of
\mathcal{Z}(\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega})
in terms of subgroups of
G
, characters and cocycles. Moreover, it was shown that a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is group-theoretical if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is tensor equivalent to an equivariantization of some pointed fusion category
[
13
, Theorem 7.2]
, for the definition of equivariantization of fusion category see
[
4
]
for details.

A braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a fusion category equipped with a braiding

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\overset{\sim}{\to}Y\otimes X,~\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{C},

which satisfies the hexagon axioms, see
[
5
]
. For any fusion subcategory
\mathcal{D}
of a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, the centralizer
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{C}
is generated by objects
X\in\mathcal{C}
that centralize every object of
\mathcal{D}
. That is,

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is generated by objects
X\in\mathcal{C}
which satisfy

\displaystyle c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}=\text{id}_{X\otimes Y},~\forall Y\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D}),

where
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D})
is the set of equivalence classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{D}
. And we call the fusion subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
the Müger center of
\mathcal{C}
and it will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. We say a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is non-degenerate if its Müger center
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\text{Vec}
, where
Vec
is the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces over
\mathds{k}
.

It was proved in
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
that an integral non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is group-theoretical if all simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
have Frobenius-Perron dimensions
1
or
2
.
In this paper, we generalize the conclusion of
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
to an arbitrary prime
p
. More precisely, let
p
be a prime, if all simple objects of an integral non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{C}
have Frobenius-Perron dimensions
1
or powers of
p
, then
\mathcal{C}
is group-theoretical, see Theorem
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
. The proofs of Theorem
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
are based on
[
2
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
15
]
, it is also an analogue of that of
[
16
, Theorem 4.5]
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In section
2
, we recall some basic properties of fusion categories and braided fusion categories that we use throughout. In section
3
, we prove our main result (Theorem
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
) about the group-theoretical property of some integral non-degenerate fusion categories.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we will recall some important definitions and properties about fusion categories and braided fusion categories, we refer the reader to
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
9
]
for more details.

Given a
\mathds{k}
-linear finite semisimple abelian category
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
, the cardinal of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
is called the rank of
\mathcal{C}
and it will be denoted by
\text{rank}(\mathcal{C})
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category, then there exists a unique ring homomorphism FPdim(-) from the Grothendieck ring
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathds{k}
such that
\text{FPdim}(X)
is a positive algebraic integer for all objects
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})

[
6
, Theorem 8.6]
, and
\text{FPdim}(X)
is called the Frobenius-Perron dimension of object
X
. Moreover, we define the Frobenius-Perron dimension
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
by

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}.

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is integral if
\text{FPdim}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\forall X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
.

A simple object
X
of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is invertible if
X\otimes X^{*}=X^{*}\otimes X=I
, the unit object of
\mathcal{C}
, if and only if
\text{FPdim}(X)=1
. It is easy to see that the set of isomorphism classes of invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}
form a finite group, which will be denoted by
G(\mathcal{C})
below. A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is pointed if it is tensor equivalent to the fusion category
\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
of
G
-graded finite-dimensional vector spaces over
\mathds{k}

[
6
]
, where
G
is a finite group and
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,\mathds{k}^{*})
is a
3
-cocycle. We denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\text{pt}}
the maximal pointed fusion subcategory of any fusion category
\mathcal{C}
below, that is,
\mathcal{C}_{\text{pt}}
is the fusion subcategory generated by invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}
, so
G(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{pt}})
.

Let
G
be a finite group.
\mathcal{C}
is a
G
-graded fusion category, if
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
is a direct sum of abelian subcategories, and for arbitrary elements
g,h\in G
we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{g}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{gh},~(\mathcal{C}_{g})^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{g^{-1}}.

By definition, the trivial component
\mathcal{C}_{e}
is a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.
The
G
-grading of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
is faithful if
\mathcal{C}_{g}\neq 0
for all
g\in G
, in this special situation, we also say that
\mathcal{C}
is a
G
-extension of
\mathcal{C}_{e}
. Given a faithfully
G
-graded fusion category
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
,

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{g})=\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{e})~\text{ for any $g\in G$}

and
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=|G|\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{e})
by
[
6
, Proposition 8.20]
, where we denote

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{g}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{g})}\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
be the adjoint fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, that is,
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
is generated by simple objects
Y
such that
Y\subseteq X\otimes X^{*}
for some simple object
X\in\mathcal{C}
. And, for any fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, there is a faithful grading
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in U_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{C}_{g}
with
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
being the trivial component
[
9
, Corollary 3.7]
, this grading is called the universal grading of
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
U_{\mathcal{C}}
the universal grading group of
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, for any other faithful grading of
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
, there is a surjective group homomorphism from
U_{\mathcal{C}}
to
G
.

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is nilpotent, if there exists a natural number
n
such that
\mathcal{C}^{(n)}=\text{Vec}
, where
\mathcal{C}^{(0)}:=\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}:=\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
,
\mathcal{C}^{(m)}:=(\mathcal{C}^{(m-1)})_{\text{ad}}
, for all integers
m\geq 1

[
9
]
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{C}
is nilpotent if there is a sequence of fusion subcategories

\text{Vec}=\mathcal{C}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{n}=\mathcal{C}
and finite groups
G_{i}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is obtained as a
G_{i}
-extension of
\mathcal{C}_{i-1}
,
1\leq i\leq n
. If these finite groups
G_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) are cyclic groups, then we say that
\mathcal{C}
is a cyclically nilpotent fusion category, see
[
8
]
.

By definition, for any pointed fusion category
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}=\text{Vec}
, so pointed fusion categories are always nilpotent; and
[
6
, Theorem 8.28]
shows that fusion categories of prime power Frobenius-Perron dimensions are cyclically nilpotent. A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is weakly group-theoretical if it is (categorically) Morita equivalent to a nilpotent fusion category, furthermore, a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is called solvable if it is Morita equivalent to a cyclically nilpotent fusion category, see
[
5
,
8
]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided fusion category with a braiding
c
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}}
the braided fusion category with reverse braiding
c^{\text{rev}}
, where
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}}:=\mathcal{C}
as fusion category, and

\displaystyle c^{\text{rev}}_{X,Y}:=c_{Y,X}^{-1},~\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{rev}}.

A braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is symmetric if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}
; in addition, a symmetric fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is Tannakian if
\mathcal{C}\cong\text{Rep}(G)
, where
G
is a finite group, and the braiding
c
of
\text{Rep}(G)
is given as the ordinary reflection of linear vector spaces, i.e.,

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x,~x\in X,y\in Y,~\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{O}(\text{Rep}(G)).

When
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\text{Vec}
, we call
\mathcal{C}
a non-degenerate fusion category
[
4
]
. A braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is slightly degenerate if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\text{sVec}
, the symmetric fusion category of finite-dimensional super-vector spaces over
\mathds{k}
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category with
G
acted on
\mathcal{C}
by tensor auto-equivalences. Then there is a well-define fusion category
\mathcal{C}^{G}
, which is called the equivariantization of
\mathcal{C}
by
G

[
4
,
5
]
. Objects of
\mathcal{C}^{G}
are the
G
-equivariant objects
X
of
\mathcal{C}
, that is, there is a natural isomorphism
\mu_{g}:g(X)\overset{\sim}{\to}X
satisfying the following equation

\displaystyle\mu_{gh}\circ\nu_{g,h}^{X}=\mu_{g}\circ g(\mu_{h})

where
\nu_{g,h}
is the natural isomorphism associated to the
G
-action on
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, it is shown that
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}^{G})=\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})|G|

[
4
, Proposition 4.26]
, and the simple objects and fusion rules of
\mathcal{C}^{G}
were studied explicitly in
[
1
]
.

Given a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, let
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
be a maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the de-equivariantization
\mathcal{C}_{G}
admits a
G
-grading structure, making
\mathcal{C}_{G}
a
G
-crossed braided fusion category
[
4
,
5
,
10
]
. Moreover, the trivial component
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
of the
G
-grading of
\mathcal{C}_{G}
is a braided fusion subcategory, and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{G}:=(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})_{G}\cong\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
as braided fusion category
[
4
, Proposition 4.56]
. The braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is called the core of
\mathcal{C}

[
4
, Definition 5.8]
, it was shown that the core of a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is independent of the choice of the maximal Tannakian subcategory
\mathcal{E}

[
4
, Theorem 5.9]
.
See
[
4
, Sections 4
\&
5]
and the references therein for details.

Conversely, given a braided fusion category
\mathcal{D}
with a specific group homomorphism
\rho:G\to\text{Aut}_{\otimes}^{\text{br}}(\mathcal{D})
, where
\text{Aut}_{\otimes}^{\text{br}}(\mathcal{D})
is the group of braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{D}
. In order to obtain a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}\cong\mathcal{D}
as braided fusion category, one need to show the existence of a
G
-crossed braided fusion category
\mathcal{A}
such that
\mathcal{A}_{0}\cong\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is the trivial component of the
G
-grading of
\mathcal{A}
by
[
7
, Theorem 7.12]
. By using the Brauer-Picard groups and Picard groups of fusion categories developed in
[
7
]
, there are two obstructions
o_{3}(\rho)
and
o_{4}(\rho)
, which are characterized explicitly using cohomological data, to the existence of a desired braided
G
-crossed braided fusion category
\mathcal{A}
. Therefore, one have to show the vanishing of these two obstructions
o_{3}(\rho)
and
o_{4}(\rho)
, see
[
7
, Sections 7
\&
8]
for a more complete description.

In addition, if
\mathcal{C}
is a non-degenerate fusion category, then the above inverse procedure of taking the core of
\mathcal{C}
is called gauging, which is well-known and useful in physics, see
[
2
,
12
]
and the references therein, for example.

A finite abelian group
G
is called a metric group
(G,\eta)
if there is a non-degenerate quadratic form
\eta
on
G
. More precisely, there is a morphism
\eta:G\to\mathds{k}^{*}
satisfying
\eta(g^{-1})=\eta(g)
, and
\eta
defines a non-degenerate bi-character
\beta
on
G
, where

\displaystyle\beta(g,h):=\frac{\eta(gh)}{\eta(g)\eta(h)},~\forall g,h\in G.

It is well-known that there is a bijective correspondence between pointed non-degenerate fusion categories and metric groups,
see
[
4
, subsection 2.11]
. In this paper, we will use
\mathcal{C}(G,\eta)
to denote the non-degenerate pointed fusion category determined by the metric group
(G,\eta)
.

3
Main result

In this section, we always assume
p
is a prime. We prove that an integral non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is group-theoretical if all simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
have Frobenius-Perron dimensions
1
or
p
.

We begin with the following proposition. The proof of the proposition is easy, we include it for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an integral non-degenerate but not pointed fusion category. If all simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
have Frobenius-Perron dimensions
1
or powers of
p
, then
p|\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{E}
is an arbitrary maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

We first show that
\mathcal{C}
contain a Tannakian category
\text{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is not pointed,
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}\ncong\text{Vec}
. For any non-invertible simple object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
,
X\otimes X^{*}\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
by definition, notice that

\displaystyle X\otimes X^{*}=\oplus_{g\in G(X)}g\oplus\oplus_{Z}N_{X,X^{*}}^{Z}Z,

where
G(X)=\{g|g\otimes X=X,g\in G(\mathcal{C})\}
is a subgroup of
G(\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}})
and
Z
ranges over the set of isomorphism classes of non-invertible simple objects of
\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}}
. Since non-invertible simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
have Frobenius-Perron dimensions power of
p
,

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}=|G(X)|+pb,\text{where $b$ is a non-negative integer,}

we see
G(X)
is non-trivial
p
-group, so
p
divides
\text{FPdim}((\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}})_{\text{pt}})
.

Assume that
\text{FPdim}((\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}})_{\text{pt}})=p^{m}r
, since
(\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}})_{\text{pt}}
is a symmetric fusion subcategory by
[
9
, Corollary 6.8]
,
(\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}})_{\text{pt}}
contains a non-trivial Tannakian category if
p
is odd or
m\geq 2

[
4
, Corollary 2.50]
.
On the contrary, assume that
m=1
and
p=2
, and that
(\mathcal{C}_{\text{ad}})_{\text{pt}}
contains a symmetric fusion category
\mathcal{B}:=\text{sVec}=\langle g\rangle
, where the simple object
g
generates
\mathcal{B}
.
Then
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is a slightly degenerate fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}

[
4
, Corollary 3.11]
. Moreover,
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is not pointed, otherwise,
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cong\text{sVec}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}

[
8
, Proposition 2.6]
, where
\mathcal{D}
is a pointed non-degenerate fusion category. We then arrive to a contradiction, because
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
being an integral fusion category of Frobenius-Perron dimension
4
, so
\mathcal{C}
must be pointed.

By assumption,
g\subseteq X\otimes X^{*}
for all non-invertible simple objects
X
of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
, that is,
g
must fixes all non-invertible simple objects of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
, but this contradicts to
[
12
, Lemma 5.4]
. Thus,
\mathcal{C}
contains a Tannakian subcategory
\mathcal{E}_{1}\cong\text{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be an arbitrary maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
, then
p
divides
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{E})

[
6
, Proposition 8.15]
. Therefore,
p
divides the Frobenius-Perron dimension of any maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, as maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
have same the Frobenius-Perron dimension
[
4
, Theorem 5.9]
.
∎

Given two fusion subcategories
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}\vee\mathcal{B}
the fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
generated by
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
. More precisely,
\mathcal{A}\vee\mathcal{B}
is the smallest fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
that contains both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Given a faithfully
G
-graded fusion category
\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
, assume that the trivial component
\mathcal{C}_{e}
is pointed and
G(\mathcal{C}_{e})=N
. Then the
G
-grading structure of
\mathcal{C}
induces a natural group action of
N
on the set
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{g})
for all
g\in G
, i.e.,
(h,X)\mapsto h\otimes X
for all
h\in N
and
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{g})
. One can easily prove that the group
N
acts transitively on the sets
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C}_{g})
for all
g\in G
, see
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
for example. In particular, the simple objects of the component
\mathcal{C}_{g}
all have same the Frobenius-Perron dimension,
\forall g\in G
.

We first give our main theorem in a special situation. The proof of the following theorem is similar to that of
[
16
, Theorem 4.2]
. Recall that a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is weakly anisotropic if
\mathcal{C}
does not contain a non-trivial Tannakian fusion subcategory that is invariant under all braided auto-equivalences of
\mathcal{C}

[
4
, Definition 5.16]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an integral non-degenerate fusion category such that
\text{FPdim}(X)
is a power of
p
for all objects
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
. If all Tannakian subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
have Frobenius-Perron dimensions powers of
p
, then
\mathcal{C}
is nilpotent and group-theoretical.

Proof.

We assume that
\mathcal{C}
is not pointed below, otherwise there is nothing to prove. By Proposition
3.1
,
\mathcal{C}
contains a non-trivial Tannakian subcategory. Indeed, the existence of non-trivial Tannakian subcategory can also be deduced from
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
, as
\mathcal{C}
is always a solvable fusion category
[
14
, Theorem 7.2]
, by

[
15
, Theorem 3.1]

\mathcal{C}
contains a non-trivial Tannakian subcategory.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
be a maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, and
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{C}_{G}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{D}_{g}
with
\mathcal{D}_{e}=\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
being the trivial component of the
G
-grading. Then the core
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
of
\mathcal{C}
is a weakly anisotropic fusion category by
[
4
, Corollary 5.19]
, hence
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
says that
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is a pointed non-degenerate fusion category. Notice that the Frobenius-Perron dimensions of simple objects of
\mathcal{D}
also belong to the set
{\{p^{i}|i\geq 0}\}
by
[
4
, Proposition 4.26]
.

Assume
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=p^{m}q^{n_{1}}_{1}\cdots q^{n_{s}}_{s}
, where
q_{j}\neq p
are distinct primes and
n_{j}
are positive integers for all
j
,
1\leq j\leq s
. Since
|G|=p^{t}
for some positive integer
t
by assumption,
we deduce from
[
4
, Proposition 4.56]
that
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0})=p^{m-2t}q^{n_{1}}_{1}\cdots q^{n_{s}}_{s}
. Therefore, we have a braided tensor equivalence of non-degenerate pointed fusion categories

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}\cong\mathcal{C}(N,\varphi)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(H_{s},\eta_{s}),

where
(N,\varphi)
and
(H_{j},\eta_{j})
are metric groups of orders
p^{m-2t}
and
q^{n_{j}}_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq s
, respectively. Clearly, as
G
acts on
\mathcal{C}(N,\varphi)
and
\mathcal{C}(H_{j},\eta_{j})
as braided auto-equivalences, these fusion subcategories are stable under the action of
G
, therefore we obtain braided fusion subcategories
\mathcal{C}(N,\varphi)^{G}
and
\mathcal{C}(H_{j},\eta_{j})^{G}
of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cong(\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0})^{G}
, hence the generated fusion category
\mathcal{C}(N,\varphi)^{G}\vee\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})^{G}\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{C}(H_{s},\eta_{s})^{G}
is a braided fusion subcategories of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
.

By
[
4
, Proposition 4.26]
, for all
1\leq j\leq s
, we have

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}(N,\varphi)^{G})=|G||N|,~\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}(H_{j},\eta_{j})^{G})=|G||H_{j}|.

By
[
6
, Proposition 8.15]
, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{C}(N,\varphi)^{G}\vee\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})^{G}\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{C}(H_{s},\eta_{s})^{G}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
have the same Frobenius-Perron dimensions, so

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}(N,\varphi)^{G}\vee\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})^{G}\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{C}(H_{s},\eta_{s})^{G}.

The universal grading property implies that
\mathcal{D}_{\text{ad}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{e}

[
9
, Corollary 3.10]
, so
\mathcal{D}
is nilpotent. Then for an arbitrary element
g\in G
,
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
says that
q^{n_{1}}_{1}\cdots q^{n_{s}}_{s}
divides
\text{rank}(\mathcal{D}_{g})
. Meanwhile for any
g\in G
,
\text{rank}(\mathcal{D}_{g})=|\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D}_{e})^{g}|
by

[
10
, Lemma 10.7]
, where
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D}_{e})^{g}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D}_{e})
is the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{D}_{e}
that are fixed by the braided auto-equivalence
g
. Notice that
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D}_{e})^{g}
is an abelian group since
\mathcal{D}_{e}
is a pointed non-degenerate fusion category, thus
G
acts trivially on the non-degenerate the fusion category
\mathcal{C}(H_{j},\eta_{j})
for all
j
. Hence, for all
1\leq j\leq s
, since
(|H_{j}|,|G|)=1
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(H_{j},\eta_{j})^{G}\cong\mathcal{C}(H_{j},\eta_{j})\boxtimes\text{Rep}(G)

as braided fusion categories. Consequently, we have a braided tensor equivalence

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(H_{s},\eta_{s})

by
[
4
, Theorem 3.13]
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a non-degenerate fusion category of Frobenius-Perron dimension
p^{m}
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is nilpotent and group-theoretical by
[
3
, Theorem 6.10]
, as desired.
∎

In fact, Theorem
3.2
implies the following corollary:

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an integral non-degenerate weakly group-theoretical fusion category. Assume that
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
is a maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. If there is a prime
q
satisfies that
(q,|G|)=1
and
(q,\text{FPdim}(X))=1
for any simple object
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
, then we have a braided equivalence
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
, where
(H,\eta)
is a metric group of order
q^{m}
and
m
is the maximal non-negative integer such that
q^{m}
divides
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

(
sketched
) Let
m
be the maximal non-negative integer such that
q^{m}
divides
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
. If
m=0
, this is trivial; we assume
m>0
below.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is an integral weakly group-theoretical fusion category, we know that
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is a pointed non-degenerate fusion category
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
. Note that the Frobenius-Perron dimensions of the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
(and also for
\mathcal{C}_{G}
by
[
1
, Corollary 2.2]
) are coprime to
q
and
(q,|G|)=1
by assumption,
q^{m}
divides
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{D})
as
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{D})=\frac{\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{|G|^{2}}
by
[
4
, Proposition 4.56]
,
\mathcal{D}\cong\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)_{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
as braided fusion category, where
\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)
is a pointed non-degenerate fusion category of Frobenius-Perron dimension
q^{m}
. For the
G
-crossed braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}_{G}
,
we know that
G
acts trivially on
\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)
as braided auto-equivalences
[
10
, Lemma 10.7]
, a similar argument of Theorem
3.2
shows that
\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)^{G}\cong\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)\boxtimes\text{Rep}(G)
as braided fusion categories. Meanwhile
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)^{G}\vee(\mathcal{C}(H,\eta)_{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})^{G}
as braided fusion category,
then the conclusion follows by
[
4
, Theorem 3.13]
.
∎

We are now in a position to prove our main theorem about group-theoretical property of integral non-degenerate fusion categories, which strengthens the conclusion of

[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-degenerate fusion category. If
\text{FPdim}(X)\in{\{1,p}\}
for all simple objects
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
, then
\mathcal{C}
is a group-theoretical fusion category.

Proof.

We know that
\mathcal{C}
contains a non-trivial Tannakian subcategory by Proposition
3.1
. Let
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
be a maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, and
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{C}_{G}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{D}_{g}
with
\mathcal{D}_{e}=\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
being the trivial component. On the one hand, if
\mathcal{D}
is pointed, then
\mathcal{C}
is group-theoretical by
[
13
, Theorem 7.2]
. On the other hand, if all Tannakian subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
have Frobenius-Perron dimensions of powers of
p
, then this is exactly the result of Theorem
3.2
.

We assume that
\mathcal{D}
is not pointed and
|G|=p^{j}t
below, where
t>1
is an integer with
(p,t)=1
, we also have
j\geq 1
by Proposition
3.1
. The argument of Theorem
3.2
says
\mathcal{D}
is a nilpotent fusion category. Consequently, simple objects of
\mathcal{D}
also have Frobenius-Perron dimensions
1
or
p
by
[
1
, Corollary 2.2]
. Let
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=p^{m}n
with
(p,n)=1
, and
\mathcal{D}_{e}=\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(H_{2},\eta_{2})
, where
(H_{1},\eta_{1})
and
(H_{2},\eta_{2})
are metric groups of orders
p^{m-2j}
and
\frac{n}{t^{2}}
, respectively. Note that
m-2j\geq 2
, since for any non-invertible simple object
X
of
\mathcal{D}
, we have
X\otimes X^{*}\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{ad}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{e}
,
p^{2}|\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{ad}})|\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{D}_{e})

[
9
, Corollary 5.3]
.

Notice that the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 6.3]
(indeed, one just need to replace
2
by the prime
p
) shows that the universal grading group
U_{\mathcal{D}}
of
\mathcal{D}
is an abelian group. Meanwhile,
\mathcal{D}
is a
G
-crossed braided fusion category with a faithful
G
-grading
[
4
, Proposition 4.56]
, and it follows from
[
9
, Corollary 3.10]
that there is a surjective group homomorphism from
U_{\mathcal{D}}
to
G
, thus
G
is also an abelian group. Assume that
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
, where
|G_{1}|=p^{j}
and
|G_{2}|=t
, respectively.

As
G
is an abelian group, we deduce from
[
2
, Theorem 1, section 4]
that there exist a
G_{2}
-crossed braided fusion category
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{e}}
with trivial component
\mathcal{D}_{e}
and a
G_{1}
-crossed braided fusion category
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
with trivial component
\mathcal{B}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{e}}^{G_{2}}

such that we have a braided tensor equivalence
\mathcal{C}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{G_{1}}
. Since
\mathcal{D}_{e}=\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is a non-degenerate fusion category,
\mathcal{B}
is also a non-degenerate fusion category by

[
4
, Proposition 4.56]
. In addition, the same argument of Theorem
3.2
implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\supseteq\mathcal{D}_{e}^{G_{2}}=\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})^{G_{2}}\vee\mathcal{C}(H_{2},\eta_{2})^{G_{2}},

since simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
have Frobenius-Perron dimensions
1
or
p
, and
\mathcal{C}
is an equivariantization of
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
, simple objects of both
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mathcal{B}
have Frobenius-Perron dimensions
1
or
p
by
[
1
, Corollary 2.2]
. Therefore,
[
4
, Proposition 4.26]
and
[
10
, Lemma 10.7]
mean that
G_{2}
acts trivially on the pointed non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})
. Consequently,
\mathcal{B}\supseteq\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})\boxtimes\text{Rep}(G_{2})
, as orders of
G_{2}
and
H_{1}
are coprime, and
H^{i}(G_{2},H_{1})=0
for all non-negative integers
i
. So
[
4
, Theorem 3.13]
shows that
\mathcal{B}\cong\mathcal{C}(H_{1},\eta_{1})\boxtimes\mathcal{A}
for some non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{A}
, moreover, we have

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{A})=\frac{\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{B})}{|H_{1}|}=\frac{|G_{2}|^{2}\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{D}_{e})}{|H_{1}|}=n.

We claim that
\mathcal{A}
is pointed. In fact, note that
p\nmid\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{A})
and
\frac{\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{A})}{\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}}
is an algebraic integer for any simple object
X
of
\mathcal{A}

[
8
, Theorem 2.11]
, then the assumption on simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
implies that
\mathcal{A}
must be a pointed fusion category. Therefore,
\mathcal{B}
is a pointed non-degenerate fusion category. In the last, we consider the
G_{1}
-crossed braided fusion category
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
, whose trivial component
\mathcal{B}
is pointed. As we have done in Theorem
3.2
or Corollary
3.3
,

[
10
, Lemma 10.7]
also shows that
G_{1}
also acts trivially on the pointed non-degenerate fusion subcategory
\mathcal{A}
.

Note that
\mathcal{A}^{G_{1}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{G_{1}}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{G_{1}}\cong\mathcal{C}
,
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{A})=n
and
(n,|G_{1}|)=1
. Hence, the previous argument (same as that of Theorem
3.2
) also shows that
\mathcal{C}
contains
\mathcal{A}^{G_{1}}\cong\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\text{Rep}(G_{1})
as a braided fusion subcategory, which implies that
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
as braided fusion category by

[
4
, Theorem 3.13]
. Hence,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is a non-degenerate fusion category of Frobenius-Perron dimension
p^{m}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}
is nilpotent and group-theoretical by
[
3
, Corollary 6.8]
.
∎

Let
G
be a finite group. Recall that the Ito-Michler Theorem of finite groups states that
G
has an abelian normal
p
-subgroup if and only if
p
can’t divide
\dim_{\mathds{k}}(V)
for any irreducible representation
V
of
G
, see
[
11
, Theorem 5.4]
for details. With the help of the Ito-Michler Theorem, we can prove the following corollary by using the same method of Theorem
3.4
, which also strengthens the conclusion of Theorem
3.4
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
p
be an arbitrary prime. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an integral non-degenerate fusion category such that
\text{FPdim}(X)
is a power of
p
for all simple objects
X
of
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is a group-theoretical fusion category.

Proof.

(
sketched
) Assume
\mathcal{C}
is not a pointed fusion category, otherwise it is trivial. We know that
\mathcal{C}
contains a maximal non-trivial Tannakian subcategory
\mathcal{E}=\text{Rep}(G)
by Proposition
3.1
.
If
\mathcal{C}_{G}
is pointed, then
\mathcal{C}
is group-theoretical
[
13
, Theorem 7.2]
.
Assume
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{C}_{G}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{D}_{g}
is not pointed below. Note that
\mathcal{C}
is always weakly group-theoretical
[
15
, Proposition 5.3]
, same as Theorem
3.2
, we obtain that
\mathcal{D}_{e}
is a pointed non-degenerate fusion category
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
. As a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, the Frobenius-Perron dimensions of simple objects of
\mathcal{E}
are also powers of
p
, then the Ito-Michler Theorem of finite groups
[
11
, Theorem 5.4]
states that
G
is isomorphic to the semidirect product
G_{1}\rtimes G_{2}
, where
G_{1}
is an abelian group such that
(|G_{1}|,p)=1
and
G_{2}
is a
p
-group.

Notice that
[
2
, Theorem 1, section 4]
still works for semidirect product of finite groups, therefore there exist a
G_{1}
-crossed braided fusion category
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{e}}
with trivial component
\mathcal{D}_{e}
and a
G_{2}
-crossed braided fusion category
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
with trivial component
\mathcal{B}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{D}_{e}}^{G_{1}}

such that we have a braided tensor equivalence
\mathcal{C}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{G_{2}}
, then the rest of proof is same of that of Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Let
p
be a prime, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a slightly degenerate fusion category such that
\text{FPdim}(X)\in{\{p^{j}|j\geq 0}\}
for all simple objects
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is solvable by
[
14
, Theorem 7.2]
. In addition, we can assume that
\mathcal{C}
does not contain any non-trivial non-degenerate fusion subcategories. Otherwise,
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{B}
, where
\mathcal{B}
is non-degenerate and
\mathcal{D}
is slightly degenerate. Theorem
3.4
shows that
\mathcal{B}
is group-theoretical, hence we can replace
\mathcal{C}
by
\mathcal{D}
if
\mathcal{D}
is not pointed.

A non-degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{A}
is called a minimal extension of a slightly degenerate fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, if
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{A})=2\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})

[
12
, Conjecture 5.2]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a slightly degenerate fusion category such that
\text{FPdim}(X)\in{\{p^{j}|j\geq 0}\}
for all simple objects
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
, then
\mathcal{C}
admits a minimal extension
\mathcal{A}
by

[
16
, Theorem 3.5]
, which can also assumed to be an integral fusion category
by
[
16
, Remark 3.6]
. Indeed, one can show that there exists a minimal extension
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{G})

[
16
, Theorem 3.5]
, where
\text{Rep}(G)
is an arbitrary maximal Tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

As a faithful
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extension of
\mathcal{C}

[
5
, Proposition 3.5.3]
,
\mathcal{A}
is also a solvable fusion category by
[
8
, Proposition 4.5]
.
Since
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
is a slightly degenerate integral weakly anisotropic fusion category
[
4
, Corollary 5.19]
,
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}
must be pointed by
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
, hence
\text{sVec}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}^{\text{rev}}\cong\mathcal{C}_{G}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{G}^{0}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is a pointed non-degenerate fusion category,

[

, Theorem 1.3]
says that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\text{sVec})\boxtimes\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}^{\text{rev}}\cong\mathcal{Z}(\text{sVec})\boxtimes\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{D}^{\text{rev}})\cong\mathcal{Z}(\text{sVec}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}^{\text{rev}})\cong\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}_{G})_{G}^{0}\cong\mathcal{D}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{G}^{0},

thus,
\mathcal{A}_{G}^{0}\cong\mathcal{D}^{\text{rev}}\boxtimes\mathcal{Z}(\text{sVec})
