Quantitative Homogenization of a Cahn–Hilliard System with Source Term in Periodically Perforated Domains
Amartya Chakrabortty

Abstract
We study qualitative and quantitative homogenization for a Cahn–Hilliard system with a nonconservative source term in a periodically perforated domain. Using the periodic unfolding method, we derive uniform energy estimates and prove convergence to a homogenized Cahn–Hilliard system whose effective diffusion tensor is characterized by scalar Neumann cell problems on the pore cell. For the quantitative analysis, we construct first-order corrector approximations by means of a scale-splitting operator, so that the cell correctors are only required to belong to H per 1 ​ ( Y p ) H^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(Y_{p}) . Under H 2 H^{2} -regularity of the homogenized solution and well-prepared initial data, we obtain an order ε 1 / 2 \varepsilon^{1/2} corrector estimate: the corrected order-parameter error is controlled in L 2 ​ ( 0 , T , H 1 ​ ( Ω p ε ) ) L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})) , while the uncorrected order parameter is controlled in L 2 ​ ( 0 , T , L 2 ​ ( Ω p ε ) ) L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})) . This improves the rate ε 1 / 4 \varepsilon^{1/4} previously established for fourth-order phase-field equations in perforated media, and matches the natural rate for second-order elliptic problems in perforated domains. The rate reflects the boundary layer caused by incomplete cells near ∂ Ω \partial\Omega and improves to order ε \varepsilon on the flat torus 𝕋 d \mathbb{T}^{d} .



Keywords:

Cahn–Hilliard equation, source term, periodic homogenization, perforated domain, corrector estimates, convergence rates.

Introduction

The Cahn–Hilliard equation, introduced by Cahn and Hilliard
[
12
]

to model spinodal decomposition in binary alloys, is a fundamental diffuse-interface
model for phase separation and interfacial dynamics. Its applications range from
phase transitions in materials science
[
13
]
and two-phase
flows
[
35
]
to tumor growth
[
46
]
, image
inpainting
[
6
]
. In homogeneous domains the mathematical theory is
well-developed: the Ginzburg–Landau free energy is a Lyapunov functional, the
system is a gradient flow in
H^{-1}
, and well-posedness is classical for both
regular potentials
[
27
,
26
,
45
]

and singular ones
[
1
,
10
]
; see the
monograph
[
43
]
and the survey
[
58
]
.

In many physically relevant situations, however, phase separation occurs in a

porous medium
: a material with a periodic array of solid inclusions, the
fluid phases occupying the connected pore space. When the ratio
\varepsilon>0

between the pore scale and the macroscopic domain size is small, resolving the
microscopic geometry directly is computationally prohibitive. Homogenization then
provides effective macroscopic equations, and reveals how pore geometry enters
the macroscopic dynamics through an effective diffusion tensor determined by cell
problems on the reference pore cell. A further feature of practical importance is
the presence of
nonconservative source terms
: in porous electrodes,
reactive porous media, and biological tissues undergoing phase transitions, the
order parameter is subject to a volumetric source or sink rather than being
conserved. This paper addresses both the qualitative and quantitative homogenization
of a Cahn–Hilliard system with a monotone nonconservative source term in a
periodically perforated domain.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
denote the ratio between the pore scale and the macroscopic
domain size, and let
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

d\in\{2,3\}
, be the periodically perforated pore domain obtained by removing a
periodic array of solid inclusions from a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
; see
Section
2.1
for the precise construction. The microscopic unknowns
are the order parameter
\phi_{\varepsilon}
and the chemical potential
\mu_{\varepsilon}
,
solving

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}-\Delta\mu_{\varepsilon}+G(\phi_{\varepsilon})=0&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},\\[2.84526pt]
\mu_{\varepsilon}=-\Delta\phi_{\varepsilon}+F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},\\[2.84526pt]
\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\varepsilon}=\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\varepsilon}=0&\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},\\[2.84526pt]
\phi_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)=\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}&\text{in }\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

Here
F
is a general potential satisfying polynomial growth, coercivity, and
dissipativity conditions (Assumption
2.2
), with the classical double-well

F(s)=\tfrac{1}{4}(s^{2}-1)^{2}
as the model example, and
G
is a monotone
globally Lipschitz source term with
G(0)=0
and
G^{\prime}\geq c_{G}>0

(Assumption
2.3
). The homogeneous Neumann conditions are imposed on the
full boundary
\partial\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}=\partial\Omega\cup\Gamma_{s}^{\varepsilon}
,
encoding no-flux conditions through both the outer boundary
\partial\Omega
and
the pore walls
\Gamma_{s}^{\varepsilon}
.

The first main result, Theorem
3.7
, establishes that as

\varepsilon\to 0
the solutions
(\phi_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varepsilon})
converge,
up to subsequences, to a pair
(\phi,\mu)
solving the homogenized Cahn–Hilliard
system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\phi-\operatorname{div}({\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu)+G(\phi)=0&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
\mu=-\operatorname{div}({\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi)+F^{\prime}(\phi)&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi\cdot\mathbf{n}={\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu\cdot\mathbf{n}=0&\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\phi(0,\cdot)=\phi^{0}&\text{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

The effective diffusion tensor
{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}
is symmetric and uniformly
elliptic, determined by scalar Neumann cell problems on the pore cell
Y_{p}
;
see (
3.9
). The proof uses the periodic unfolding method adapted to
perforated domains
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
16
,
20
]
, uniform energy estimates,
and compactness.
A structural observation driving the analysis is that
G
, being monotone with

G(0)=0
, produces a dissipative contribution in the energy identity
(Lemma
2.8
): testing the chemical potential equation
with
G(\phi_{\varepsilon})
yields a positive gradient term, so the source term
reinforces rather than competes with the dissipation. As a result, the right-hand
side of the energy inequality is a fixed constant independent of
\varepsilon
,
and no Gronwall argument is needed. This stands in contrast to problems with a
general nonconservative forcing term, where the source would need to be controlled
by Gronwall at the cost of an exponential-in-time factor.

The second main result, Theorem
4.7
, is a quantitative
corrector estimate. Under an additional
H^{2}
-regularity assumption on the
homogenized solution (Assumption
4.1
) and well-prepared initial
data, we construct first-order corrector approximations

\Phi_{\varepsilon}=\phi+\varepsilon\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\,\chi_{i}\!\left(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),\quad\text{and}\quad M_{\varepsilon}=\mu+\varepsilon\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\mu)\,\chi_{i}\!\left(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),

where
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}
is the
Q_{1}
scale-splitting operator from
[
18
,
19
,
29
]
applied on the full domain
\Omega
, and

\chi_{i}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(Y_{p})
are the scalar pore-cell correctors solving
the Neumann cell problem (
3.7
). The estimate reads

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\left\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}-\left[\nabla\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\\
+\|\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}.

(1.1)

The three terms on the left measure: the uncorrected
L^{2}
-error in the order
parameter, the corrected gradient error in the order parameter, and the chemical
potential error in the dual norm
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})
.
The proof of Theorem
4.7
uses a negative-norm energy
method: the phase error equation is tested against
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
, where
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
is the Neumann
inverse Laplacian on
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
and
\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}

is the zero-mean part of the phase error. This produces an evolution equation
for the
H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}
-norm of the error (defined in Section
4.4

via the Neumann inverse Laplacian on
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
), which is then coupled with the
chemical potential error equation tested against
\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}

itself. The resulting system is closed by a Gronwall argument using the residual
estimate of Lemma
4.6
.
A gradient
estimate for
\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu
in
L^{2}
is not obtained; this is sharp
within the present variational framework, since controlling

\|\nabla(\mu_{\varepsilon}-M_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}}
would require testing the
chemical potential error equation with
\partial_{t}e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
, which is
not justified at the regularity level of the residuals; see
Remark
4.11
. On the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, where there
is no boundary layer of incomplete cells, the rate improves to

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
(Remark
4.10
).

Main contributions.

This paper makes two contributions to the homogenization theory of phase-field
equations in perforated media.

The first is qualitative. We derive the homogenized Cahn–Hilliard
system (
3.10
) with a nonconservative monotone source term via the
periodic unfolding method, treating
G
under Assumptions
2.2
–
2.3

without specializing to a particular double-well potential.

The second is quantitative. Under the additional regularity
Assumption
4.1
on the homogenized solution, we prove the corrector
estimate (
1.1
) of order
\varepsilon^{1/2}
. Specifically, the
uncorrected order-parameter error is controlled in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))
,
the corrected gradient error in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
, and the
chemical potential error in the dual norm
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})
.
This improves the rate
\varepsilon^{1/4}
of
[
49
]
— where the
error is measured in the uncorrected
H^{1}
-norm and no first-order corrector
is subtracted — and matches the natural rate for second-order elliptic problems
in perforated domains
[
48
]
.
Two methodological points drive the improvement.
First, the scale-splitting operator
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}
from
[
17
,
29
,
30
]
replaces the classical formal corrector expansion, allowing cell
correctors to stay in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(Y_{p})
without any
W^{1,\infty}
assumption and
reducing the required regularity of the macroscopic solution from
W^{k,\infty}

(
k\geq 4
) to
H^{2}
. Second, the consistency estimate
(Lemma
4.6
) is derived entirely in variational form, using the
weak no-flux condition on
\Gamma_{s}^{\varepsilon}
inherited from the cell problem;
this avoids the boundary-term contributions that cause the rate loss
in
[
49
]
. The
\varepsilon^{1/2}
rate on bounded domains comes from
the collar estimate for the boundary layer of incomplete cells near
\partial\Omega
,
not from the fourth-order structure. On the flat torus, where this layer is absent,
the rate improves to
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
.

2
The microscopic problem

2.1
Geometry of the periodic perforated domain

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
Let
Y:=(0,1)^{d}

be the reference periodic cell, and let
Y_{s}\subset Y
be a nonempty open set
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial Y_{s}
, representing the solid (impermeable)
inclusion in the reference cell. We assume the frame condition
\overline{Y_{s}}\subset Y
,
i.e. the solid inclusion is compactly contained in the reference cell and
does not touch
\partial Y
. We define the pore part of the cell by
Y_{p}:=Y\setminus\overline{Y_{s}}
.
Since
\overline{Y_{s}}
is closed and
\overline{Y_{s}}\subset Y
, the set
Y_{p}

is automatically open, and
\partial Y_{p}=\partial Y\,\cup\,\partial Y_{s}
,
where the two pieces are disjoint (since
\partial Y_{s}\cap\partial Y=\emptyset

by the frame condition). We assume in addition that
Y_{p}
is connected. The
porosity of the cell is

\theta_{p}:=|Y_{p}|\in(0,1).

Throughout,
H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}}}(Y_{p})
denotes the space of
Y
-periodic
H^{1}

functions on
Y_{p}
, and
H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p})
denotes its mean-zero
subspace
\{v\in H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}}}(Y_{p}):\int_{Y_{p}}v\,dy=0\}
. The cell
correctors belong to
H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p})
, with the mean-zero condition
inherited from the two-scale structure of the unfolding operator.

The perforated domain.

For
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, with a fixed
\varepsilon_{0}
, let

K_{\varepsilon}:=\bigl\{\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\;:\;\varepsilon(k+Y)\subset\Omega\,\bigr\}

be the set of indices of cells entirely contained in
\Omega
. By the frame
condition, the rescaled closed inclusions
\varepsilon(k+\overline{Y_{s}})
,

k\in K_{\varepsilon}
, are pairwise disjoint and each is compactly contained in

\varepsilon(k+Y)\subset\Omega
. We set

\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}:=\operatorname{int}\!\left(\bigcup_{k\in K_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon\bigl(k+\overline{Y_{s}}\bigr)\right),\qquad{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}:=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}}.

The internal oscillating (pore-wall) boundary is

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{s}:=\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}\cap\Omega=\bigcup_{k\in K_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon\bigl(k+\partial Y_{s}\bigr).

By the frame condition, every connected component of
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}

satisfies
\operatorname{dist}(\overline{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}},\partial\Omega)\geq c\,\varepsilon
for some
c>0
independent of
\varepsilon
; in
particular
{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
contains a fixed-width boundary layer
along
\partial\Omega
that is free of perforations, and

\partial{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}=\partial\Omega\,\cup\,\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{s},

with the two pieces disjoint. We denote by
{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}
the unit outward
normal vector field on
\partial{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
, i.e. the outward
normal to
\Omega
on
\partial\Omega
and the normal pointing from

{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
into
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{s}
.

Moreover, we set

{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}=\text{interior}\left\{\bigcup_{\kappa\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}(\kappa+\overline{Y})\right\},

where
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}=({\Omega}\setminus{\Omega}_{\varepsilon})
contains the part of the cells intersecting

\partial{\Omega}
; since
{\Omega}
is bounded with Lipschitz boundary,
|\Lambda_{\varepsilon}|\to 0
as

{\varepsilon}\to 0
.

Remark 2.1
.

The frame condition
\overline{Y_{s}}\subset Y
, together with the Lipschitz
boundary of
Y_{s}
and the connectivity of
Y_{p}
assumed above, is precisely
the hypothesis under which
{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
is connected, has a uniform Lipschitz
character (independent of
\varepsilon
), and admits a uniform extension
operator
P_{\varepsilon}\colon H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})\to H^{1}(\Omega)
with

\|P_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}),H^{1}(\Omega))}
bounded
independently of
\varepsilon
; see
[
2
,
21
,
47
]
.
This extension operator, combined with a contradiction argument
(assuming the Poincaré–Wirtinger constant blows up along a sequence

\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, normalizing, and extracting a compact subsequence via
the extension to reach a contradiction), yields a uniform
Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality on
{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
with constant independent
of
\varepsilon
; see
[
24
]
for details.
This inequality will be used in the uniform energy estimates of
Section
4
.

2.2
Problem description

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
. The unknowns are the order parameter

\phi_{\varepsilon}\colon(0,T)\times{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}\to\mathbb{R}
and
the chemical potential

\mu_{\varepsilon}\colon(0,T)\times{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}\to\mathbb{R}
, solving

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}-\Delta\mu_{\varepsilon}+G(\phi_{\varepsilon})=0,&\text{in }(0,T)\times{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p},\\[2.0pt]
\mu_{\varepsilon}=-\Delta\phi_{\varepsilon}+F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}),&\text{in }(0,T)\times{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p},\\[2.0pt]
\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}=\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}=0,&\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p},\\[2.0pt]
\phi_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)=\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0},&\text{in }{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

The homogeneous Neumann conditions are imposed on the whole boundary

\partial{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}=\partial\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{s}
: there is
no flux of
\phi_{\varepsilon}
or
\mu_{\varepsilon}
either through the outer
boundary
\partial\Omega
or through the pore walls
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}^{s}
.

Assumption 2.2

(General Potential)
.

The potential
F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies the polynomial growth
condition: there exists
C>0
such that

|F^{\prime}(s)|\leq C\bigl(1+|s|^{3}\bigr),\qquad|F^{\prime\prime}(s)|\leq C\bigl(1+|s|^{2}\bigr),\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover:

(i)

F
is bounded from below:
F(s)\geq-c_{3}
for some
c_{3}\geq 0
.

(ii)

(Coercivity) there exist
c_{0}>0
,
C_{0}\geq 0
such that

F(s)\geq c_{0}|s|^{4}-C_{0},\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

(iii)

(Dissipativity) there exist
c_{1}>0
,
c_{2}\geq 0
such that

s\,F^{\prime}(s)\geq c_{1}F(s)-c_{2},\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

The model example
F(s)=\tfrac{1}{4}(s^{2}-1)^{2}
,
F^{\prime}(s)=s^{3}-s
, satisfies all of
the above with
c_{1}=4,c_{2}=1
in (iii) (and
c_{0}=\tfrac{1}{4}
,
C_{0}=\tfrac{1}{4}
in
(ii)).

Assumption 2.3

(Source term)
.

The source term
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies

G(0)=0,\qquad 0<c_{G}\leq G^{\prime}(s)\leq C_{G}\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

Consequently
G
is globally Lipschitz with
|G(s)|\leq C_{G}|s|
, and

\bigl(G(a)-G(b)\bigr)(a-b)\geq c_{G}|a-b|^{2}\qquad\forall a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Assumption 2.4

(Initial data)
.

The initial data
\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}\in H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})
satisfy

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}\|_{H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C,

with
C
independent of
\varepsilon
. Moreover, there exists

\phi_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that the unfolded initial data converge
strongly in the sense of unfolding operator from Definition
3.1
,

\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0})\to\phi_{0}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega\times Y_{p}).

We use the standard notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime},H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}

for the duality pairing between
H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime}
and

H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})
, and
(\cdot,\cdot)
for the
L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})

inner product.

Definition 2.5

(Weak solution)
.

A pair

(\phi_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varepsilon})\in\Bigl[L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime})\Bigr]\times L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}))

is called a weak solution of (
2.1
) if

1.

for all
\zeta\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}))
,

\int_{0}^{T}\bigl\langle\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon},\zeta\bigr\rangle_{H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime},H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}G(\phi_{\varepsilon})\,\zeta\,dx\,dt=0;

(2.2)

2.

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and for all
\eta\in H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})
,

\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(t)\,\eta\,dx=\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx+\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,\eta\,dx;

(2.3)

3.

the initial condition is attained:

\phi_{\varepsilon}(0)=\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}\quad\text{in }L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}).

(2.4)

Remark 2.6
.

The space
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime})

embeds continuously into
C([0,T];L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}))
via the standard
interpolation lemma for evolution triples

H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})\hookrightarrow L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})\hookrightarrow H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime}
;
see
[
57
]
. Hence the initial condition (
2.4
)
is meaningful pointwise at
t=0
. Condition (
2.3
) is imposed
for a.e.
t
rather than in integrated form, since no time derivative
of
\mu_{\varepsilon}
appears in the system.

2.3
Structure of the source term

Two estimates for the source term
G
are derived below from Assumption
2.3
.

Lemma 2.7

(Structure of
G
)
.

Let
G
satisfy Assumption
2.3
. Then for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
:

(i)

c_{G}|s|\leq|G(s)|\leq C_{G}|s|
, and
G(s)
has the same sign as

s
; equivalently,

c_{G}\,s^{2}\leq G(s)\,s\leq C_{G}\,s^{2},\quad G(s)\,\mathrm{sgn}(s)\geq c_{G}|s|.

(ii)

Let
F
satisfy
Assumption
2.2
(i),(iii). Set
C_{3}:=c_{1}c_{3}+c_{2}\geq 0
. Then

G(s)\,F^{\prime}(s)\;\geq\;-C_{G}C_{3}\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

(iii)

If
v\in H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})
, then
G(v)\in H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})
, with

\nabla\bigl(G(v)\bigr)=G^{\prime}(v)\,\nabla v\qquad\text{a.e.\ in }{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}.

Moreover
\|G(v)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C_{G}\|v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}

and
\|\nabla G(v)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C_{G}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}
.

Proof.

(i)
Since
G(0)=0
and
c_{G}\leq G^{\prime}\leq C_{G}
, the fundamental theorem
gives
G(s)=\int_{0}^{s}G^{\prime}(r)\,dr
, so
c_{G}|s|\leq|G(s)|\leq C_{G}|s|
and

\mathrm{sgn}(G(s))=\mathrm{sgn}(s)
for all
s
. Hence

G(s)s=|G(s)|\,|s|\in[c_{G}s^{2},C_{G}s^{2}]
and

G(s)\,\mathrm{sgn}(s)=|G(s)|\geq c_{G}|s|
.

(ii)
By Assumption
2.2
(i),(iii),

sF^{\prime}(s)\geq c_{1}F(s)-c_{2}\geq-c_{1}c_{3}-c_{2}=-C_{3}
for all
s
.
Write
G(s)=\theta(s)s
where
\theta(s):=G(s)/s\in[c_{G},C_{G}]
for
s\neq 0

(by the mean-value theorem) and
\theta(0):=G^{\prime}(0)
. Then

G(s)F^{\prime}(s)=\theta(s)\,(sF^{\prime}(s)).

If
sF^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
, multiply the bound
sF^{\prime}(s)\geq-C_{3}
by
\theta(s)\geq c_{G}>0

to get
G(s)F^{\prime}(s)\geq-c_{G}C_{3}
. If
sF^{\prime}(s)<0
, since
\theta(s)\leq C_{G}

multiplying the negative quantity by the smaller factor gives the larger product,
so
G(s)F^{\prime}(s)\geq C_{G}(sF^{\prime}(s))\geq-C_{G}C_{3}
. Since
C_{G}\geq c_{G}
, both cases
give
G(s)F^{\prime}(s)\geq-C_{G}C_{3}
.

(iii)
The pointwise bound
|G(v)|\leq C_{G}|v|
gives

G(v)\in L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})
. The chain rule
\nabla(G(v))=G^{\prime}(v)\nabla v

holds in
H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})
by approximating
v
by smooth functions,
using
|G^{\prime}|\leq C_{G}
and dominated convergence, and invoking the closedness
of the weak gradient; see e.g.
[
26
]
. The stated

L^{2}
-bounds follow immediately from
|G^{\prime}|\leq C_{G}
.
∎

Lemma 2.8

(Dissipation produced by the source term)
.

Let
F
satisfy Assumption
2.2
and
G
satisfy Assumption
2.3
,
and let
(\phi_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of
(
2.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.5
. Then for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}G(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,\mu_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dx\;\geq\;c_{G}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}-C_{G}C_{3}\,|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|,

(2.5)

where
C_{3}:=c_{1}c_{3}+c_{2}\geq 0
is the constant from
Lemma
2.7
.

Proof.

Fix a.e.
t\in(0,T)
for which
\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\in H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})

and (
2.3
) holds.
By Lemma
2.7
(iii),
G(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\in H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})

with
\|G(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\|_{H^{1}}\leq C_{G}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}}
,
so
\eta=G(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))
is an admissible test function in
(
2.3
). Testing gives

\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(t)\,G(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dx=\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}G^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,G(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dx,

(2.6)

where we used
\nabla(G(\phi_{\varepsilon}))=G^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}

from Lemma
2.7
(iii) (no boundary term appears since the
weak formulation already encodes homogeneous Neumann conditions for
\phi_{\varepsilon}
).
By Assumption
2.3
,
G^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t,x))\geq c_{G}
a.e., so
the first term on the right satisfies

\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}G^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dx\geq c_{G}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

By Lemma
2.7
(ii),
G(s)F^{\prime}(s)\geq-C_{G}C_{3}
for all
s
, so
the second term satisfies

\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,G(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dx\geq-C_{G}C_{3}|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|
.
Substituting into (
2.6
) gives (
2.5
).
∎

2.4
The energy inequality

Recall the energy functional

E_{\varepsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}+\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}F(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dx.

Testing (
2.2
) with
\zeta=\mu_{\varepsilon}
and (
2.3
) with

\eta=\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}
and adding gives, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\frac{d}{dt}E_{\varepsilon}(t)+\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}+\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}G(\phi_{\varepsilon})\,\mu_{\varepsilon}\,dx=0.

(2.7)

Remark 2.9
.

For a weak solution in the sense of Definition
2.5
,
\eta=\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\in H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime}
is not a
priori an admissible test function in (
2.3
). Identity
(
2.7
) is therefore understood as holding for the Galerkin
approximations
(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{(n)},\mu_{\varepsilon}^{(n)})
used to
construct the weak solution (where
\eta=\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}

is finite-dimensional and hence admissible). The estimates below are derived
at the level of these approximations; passing to the limit
n\to\infty
,
weak lower semicontinuity of the norms
\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
,

\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
and of
\int F(\phi_{\varepsilon})
(by
Fatou’s lemma, using
F
bounded below) turns
(
2.7
) into the inequality (
2.8
) for
the weak solution
(\phi_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varepsilon})
itself.

Combining (
2.7
) with Lemma
2.8
and

|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|\leq|\Omega|
yields, with
C_{3}=c_{1}c_{3}+c_{2}
as in
Lemma
2.7
,

\frac{d}{dt}E_{\varepsilon}(t)+\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}+c_{G}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\;\leq\;C_{G}C_{3}\,|\Omega|.

(2.8)

As in the double-well case, the right-hand side of (
2.8
) is a
fixed constant independent of
{\varepsilon}
.

Bound on the initial energy.
By Assumption
2.4
,

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}\|_{H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C
. By
Assumption
2.2
,
|F^{\prime}(s)|\leq C(1+|s|^{3})
, so integrating gives the
growth bound
|F(s)|\leq C(1+|s|^{4})
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Together with
the uniform Sobolev embedding
H^{1}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})\hookrightarrow L^{4}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})
(Remark
2.1
), this gives

E_{\varepsilon}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}+\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}F(\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0})\,dx\leq C\bigl(1+\|\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}\|^{4}_{L^{4}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\bigr)\leq C,

with
C
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Integration in time.
Integrating (
2.8
) over

(0,t)
,
t\in[0,T]
, and using
E_{\varepsilon}(0)\leq C
,

E_{\varepsilon}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\,ds+c_{G}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\,ds\leq E_{\varepsilon}(0)+C_{G}C_{3}|\Omega|\,T\leq C(T).

(2.9)

From the energy to
H^{1}
and
L^{4}
bounds.
Unlike the
double-well case,
F
is here only bounded below
(Assumption
2.2
(i)), so
E_{\varepsilon}(t)\geq 0
no longer holds and

E_{\varepsilon}(t)
alone does not control
\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
. Instead,
by Assumption
2.2
(i),
F(s)\geq-c_{3}
, so

\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}F(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dx\geq-c_{3}|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|\geq-c_{3}|\Omega|,

and hence

\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq E_{\varepsilon}(t)+c_{3}|\Omega|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:energy-int}}}{{\leq}}C(T)+c_{3}|\Omega|=:C_{1}.

This gives, uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
and
\varepsilon
:

\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}\leq C_{1}.

(2.10)

For the
L^{4}
-bound, we now use the coercivity assumption

2.2
(ii),
F(s)\geq c_{0}|s|^{4}-C_{0}
, which gives

c_{0}\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)^{4}\,dx\leq\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}F(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dx+C_{0}|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|\leq E_{\varepsilon}(t)+C_{0}|\Omega|\leq C(T)+C_{0}|\Omega|.

Hence

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{4}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}\leq C_{2},

(2.11)

with
C_{2}
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Combining (
2.9
),
(
2.10
) and (
2.11
), there exists
C(T)>0
,
independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Bigl[\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}+\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{4}_{L^{4}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\Bigr]+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\,dt\leq C(T).

(2.12)

Lemma 2.10

(Uniform bound on the mean)
.

Let
\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t):=\dfrac{1}{|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|}\displaystyle\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx
.
Then there exists
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\|\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C.

(2.13)

Proof.

Taking the mean of equation (
2.1
)
1
over
{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
,
and using

\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\Delta\mu_{\varepsilon}\,dx=\int_{\partial{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}\,dS=0

(homogeneous Neumann condition on
\mu_{\varepsilon}
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t)+\overline{G(\phi_{\varepsilon})}(t)=0,\qquad\overline{G(\phi_{\varepsilon})}(t):=\frac{1}{|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|}\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}G(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t,x))\,dx.

Write
\overline{G(\phi_{\varepsilon})}=G(\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon})+r_{\varepsilon}
,
where

r_{\varepsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|}\int_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\bigl[G(\phi_{\varepsilon})-G(\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon})\bigr]\,dx,

so that

\frac{d}{dt}\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t)+G(\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t))=-r_{\varepsilon}(t).

(2.14)

Since
G
is Lipschitz with constant
C_{G}
and
\phi_{\varepsilon}-\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}

has zero mean, Cauchy–Schwarz and the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality on

{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
(with constant
C_{P}
independent of
\varepsilon
,
Remark
2.1
) give

|r_{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq\frac{C_{G}}{|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|^{1/2}}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq\frac{C_{G}C_{P}}{|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|^{1/2}}\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

(2.15)

Since
|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|\to\theta_{p}|\Omega|>0
, we have

|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{p}|\Omega|
for
\varepsilon
small
enough; together with (
2.10
) this gives

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|r_{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}:=C\,C_{1}.

By Lemma
2.7
(ii),
G(s)\mathrm{sgn}(s)\geq c_{G}|s|
for all

s\in\mathbb{R}
. Multiplying (
2.14
) by

\mathrm{sgn}(\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t))
gives, for a.e.
t
,

\frac{d}{dt}|\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq-c_{G}|\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t)|+|r_{\varepsilon}(t)|.

By Gronwall’s inequality, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

|\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq|\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(0)|\,e^{-c_{G}t}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c_{G}(t-s)}|r_{\varepsilon}(s)|\,ds\leq|\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\frac{C_{1}^{\prime}}{c_{G}}.

Finally, by Cauchy–Schwarz and Assumption
2.4
,

|\bar{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(0)|\leq\frac{1}{|{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}|^{1/2}}\|\phi_{\varepsilon}^{0}\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C.

Combining the last two displays gives (
2.13
) with

C=C+C_{1}^{\prime}/c_{G}
, independent of
\varepsilon
.
∎

2.5
Existence for fixed
\varepsilon

Theorem 2.11

(Existence of microscopic weak solutions)
.

Let Assumptions
2.2
,
2.3
, and
2.4
hold. Then, for
every
\varepsilon>0
, problem (
2.1
) admits a weak solution

(\phi^{\varepsilon},\mu^{\varepsilon})
in the sense of Definition
2.5
.

Sketch of the proof.

For fixed
\varepsilon>0
,
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
is a fixed bounded
Lipschitz domain and (
2.1
) is the classical Cahn–Hilliard
system with homogeneous Neumann conditions, regular potential
F

(Assumption
2.2
), and globally Lipschitz source term
G

(Assumption
2.3
). Existence of a global weak solution follows

of the Neumann Laplacian, derive the uniform bound
(
2.16
) at the Galerkin level (where
(
2.7
) holds exactly), and pass to the limit using

\ast

). The nonlinear terms

and

converge strongly
in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{4/3})
and

respectively, by the growth
bound on

, and the
L^{4}
-strong

[
27
,
26
,
45
]
for the full argument without the source

∎

2.6
Uniform estimates

Lemma 2.12

(Uniform energy estimate)
.

Let Assumptions
2.2
,
2.3
, and
2.4
hold, and let

(\phi^{\varepsilon},\mu^{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of (
2.1
) given by
Theorem
2.11
. Then there exists
C>0
, independent of

\varepsilon
, such that

\left\|\phi^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}+\left\|\mu^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}+\left\|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime})}\leq C.

(2.16)

Proof.

Step 1:
\phi^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))
.

The gradient bound (
2.10
) gives

\|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}\leq C_{1}
, and
Lemma
2.10
gives
\|\bar{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C
.
The Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality on
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
(Remark
2.1
) then yields

\|\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C_{P}\|\nabla\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}+|\bar{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\,|\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}|^{1/2}\leq C,

uniformly in
t
and
\varepsilon
. Together with (
2.10
) this
gives
\phi^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))
.

Step 2:
\mu^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))
.

The gradient bound
\|\nabla\mu^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}\leq C
is already
in (
2.12
). For the mean, taking
\eta=1
in (
2.3
)
gives
\bar{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t)=|\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}|^{-1}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}}F^{\prime}(\phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\,dx
. By
Assumption
2.2
and the uniform

-bound (

),

|\bar{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq C(1+\|\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{3}_{L^{4}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})})\leq C

uniformly in

and
\varepsilon
.
The Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality then gives

\|\mu^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))}\leq C
,
hence
\mu^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}))
.

Step 3:
\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime})
.

For

, equation (

) gives

\|\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime}}\leq\|\nabla\mu^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}+C_{G}\|\phi^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})}.

Squaring, integrating over

, and using Steps 1–2 gives

\partial_{t}\phi^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime})
, with norm bounded independently
of

. Combining Steps 1–3 gives (

).

3
Two-scale limit and homogenized system

3.1
Unfolding operator for a perforated domain

The main tool for homogenization in the perforated domain
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}

and for the derivation of the two-scale limit system is the periodic unfolding
operator adapted to perforated media, denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}
.
It was first introduced in
[
17
]
and further developed in

[
20
,
18
,
16
]
. For a detailed presentation we refer to

[
19
, Chapter 4]
.
We recall the definition of the periodic unfolding operators

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}
for functions defined
on
\Omega
and
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
, respectively.
For
x\in\Omega
, write

x=\varepsilon\left[\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right]+\varepsilon\left\{\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right\},

where
[\cdot]\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\{\cdot\}\in Y
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
Q_{T}=(0,T)\times\Omega
. For every measurable function

\psi
on
Q_{T}
the unfolding operator

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}:L^{1}(Q_{T})\to L^{1}((0,T){\times}\Omega\times Y)
is
defined by

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\psi)(t,x,y)\doteq\begin{cases}\psi\bigl(t,\varepsilon\bigl[\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigr]+\varepsilon y\bigr),&\text{for a.e. }(t,x,y)\in(0,T){\times}\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times Y,\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{for a.e. }(t,x,y)\in(0,T){\times}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\times Y.\end{cases}

For every measurable function
\psi
on
(0,T){\times}\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
the
perforated unfolding operator

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}:L^{1}((0,T){\times}\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})\to L^{1}((0,T){\times}\Omega\times Y_{p})
is defined by

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\psi)(t,x,y)\doteq\begin{cases}\psi\bigl(t,\varepsilon\bigl[\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\bigr]+\varepsilon y\bigr),&\text{for a.e. }(t,x,y)\in(0,T){\times}\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times Y_{p},\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{for a.e. }(t,x,y)\in(0,T){\times}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\times Y_{p}.\end{cases}

In particular, let
\psi
be a measurable function defined on

(0,T){\times}\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
, and let
\widetilde{\psi}
denote its zero-extension
to
(0,T){\times}\Omega
(i.e.
\widetilde{\psi}=\psi
on
(0,T){\times}\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}

and
\widetilde{\psi}=0
on
(0,T){\times}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
). Then

{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}^{\ast}(\psi)={\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}(\widetilde{\psi})_{|(0,T){\times}{\Omega}{\times}Y_{p}}.

Unfolding criterion for integrals in
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
(u.c.i.).

For every
\psi\in L^{1}((0,T){\times}\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
one has

\int_{Q_{T}\times Y_{p}}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\psi)(t,x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}(y,x,t)=\int_{(0,T){\times}{\Omega}_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\psi(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)-\int_{(0,T){\times}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}}\psi(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t).

Since
\Omega
has Lipschitz boundary, we have
|\Lambda_{\varepsilon}|\to 0
as

\varepsilon\to 0
. Hence (for
{\varepsilon}
independent
\psi
)

\left|\int_{(0,T){\times}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}}\psi(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)\right|\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

and therefore

\left|\int_{Q_{T}\times Y_{p}}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\psi)(t,x,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}(y,x,t)-\int_{(0,T){\times}{\Omega}_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\psi(t,x)\,{\mathrm{d}}(x,t)\right|\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(3.1)

If
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)
for some
p>1
, then

\left|\int_{(0,T)\times\Lambda_{\varepsilon}}\psi_{\varepsilon}\,dxdt\right|\leq|\Lambda_{\varepsilon}|^{1/p^{\prime}}\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\to 0.

(3.2)

Finally, the unfolding operators preserve products in the expected way: if

u,v:(0,T){\times}\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}
are measurable, then

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(u\,v)=\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(u)\,\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}(v)\qquad\text{a.e. in }(0,T){\times}\Omega\times Y_{p}.

If in addition

is continuous, then

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(H(u))=H\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(u)\bigr)\qquad\text{a.e. in }(0,T){\times}\Omega^{\varepsilon}\times Y_{p};

if moreover
H(0)=0
, this identity extends to a.e.
(0,T){\times}\Omega\times Y_{p}
,
since both sides vanish on
(0,T){\times}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\times Y_{p}
. This applies
in particular to
H=G
by Assumption
2.3
.

Compactness

Lemma 3.3

(Extension and consistency with the perforated unfolding operator)
.

Let

\phi_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}),\qquad\mu_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))

satisfy the uniform bounds (
2.16
). Then there exist

\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime}),\qquad\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),

with

\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}=\phi_{\varepsilon},\qquad\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}=\mu_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},

such that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl(\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}\Bigr),

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))},

with
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. Moreover, for a.e.

(t,x,y)\in(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\phi_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon})\big|_{(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon})\big|_{(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}},

and analogously,

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\mu_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon})\big|_{(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon})\big|_{(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}}.

Proof.

Set

V_{\varepsilon}:=H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}),\qquad V:=H^{1}(\Omega),\qquad H_{\varepsilon}:=L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}),\qquad H:=L^{2}(\Omega).

Let

J_{\varepsilon}:H_{\varepsilon}\to V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},\qquad J:H\to V^{\prime}

denote the canonical injections associated with the Gelfand triples

V_{\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow H_{\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},\qquad V\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}.

Let
P_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{L}(V_{\varepsilon},V)
be the uniform extension operator from
Remark
2.1
; see
[
2
,
47
]
. Define its
L^{2}
-adjoint

P_{\varepsilon}^{*}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})

by the identity

(P_{\varepsilon}v,\eta)_{H}=(v,P_{\varepsilon}^{*}\eta)_{H_{\varepsilon}}\qquad\forall v\in V_{\varepsilon},\ \eta\in V,

which holds by definition of the Hilbert-space adjoint. Since
P_{\varepsilon}

is constructed by reflection and local averaging near
\Gamma_{s}^{\varepsilon}

(see
[
2
]
), its adjoint
P_{\varepsilon}^{*}

maps
H^{1}(\Omega)
to
H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
with uniform bound

\|P_{\varepsilon}^{*}\eta\|_{V_{\varepsilon}}\leq C\|\eta\|_{V}\qquad\forall\eta\in V,

with
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. We set
P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}:=P_{\varepsilon}^{*}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

and define
F_{\varepsilon}:=(P_{\varepsilon,\sharp})^{\ast}:V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\to V^{\prime}

by

\langle F_{\varepsilon}\xi,\eta\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}:=\langle\xi,P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}\eta\rangle_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},V_{\varepsilon}}\qquad\forall\xi\in V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},\ \eta\in V.

Then

\|F_{\varepsilon}\xi\|_{V^{\prime}}\leq C\|\xi\|_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\qquad\forall\xi\in V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},

with
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.

For a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, define

\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t):=P_{\varepsilon}\phi_{\varepsilon}(t),\qquad\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}(t):=P_{\varepsilon}\mu_{\varepsilon}(t).

Since
P_{\varepsilon}
is bounded and linear, the maps

t\mapsto\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t)
and

t\mapsto\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}(t)
are Bochner measurable. Moreover,

\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,T),

and

\|\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,T).

Taking the essential supremum in time in the first estimate and integrating the second estimate
over
(0,T)
, we obtain

\|\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))},

and

\|\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C\|\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}.

We now identify the time derivative of the extended phase field. We claim that

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}=F_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,T;V^{\prime}).

Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T;V)
. Since
P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}
is independent of time,

P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T;V_{\varepsilon}),\qquad\partial_{t}(P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}\psi)=P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}(\partial_{t}\psi).

Using the definition of
F_{\varepsilon}
, the weak time-derivative identity for

\phi_{\varepsilon}
, and the defining relation of
P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle F_{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}(t),\psi(t)\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}(t),P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}\psi(t)\rangle_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},V_{\varepsilon}}\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t),\partial_{t}(P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}\psi)(t))_{H_{\varepsilon}}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t),P_{\varepsilon,\sharp}\partial_{t}\psi(t))_{H_{\varepsilon}}\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}(P_{\varepsilon}\phi_{\varepsilon}(t),\partial_{t}\psi(t))_{H}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(t),\partial_{t}\psi(t))_{H}\,dt.

Hence

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}=F_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,T;V^{\prime}).

Since

\|F_{\varepsilon}\xi\|_{V^{\prime}}\leq C\|\xi\|_{V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\qquad\forall\xi\in V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},

we get

\|\partial_{t}\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V^{\prime})}\leq C\|\partial_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})}.

Therefore

\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime}),

and the stated estimate for
\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}
follows.

It remains to prove the consistency with the perforated unfolding operator.
Since

\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}=\phi_{\varepsilon},\qquad\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}=\mu_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},

the locality of weak gradients gives the same identities for the gradients.
Hence, for
(t,x,y)\in(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times Y_{p}
, where

\varepsilon[x/\varepsilon]+\varepsilon y\in\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
, the definitions of

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}
yield

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\phi_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}),\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\mu_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}),

and the corresponding identities with
\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}
and

\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}
. On

(0,T)\times\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\times Y_{p}
, both sides vanish by definition.
Thus all four identities hold a.e. in
(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.5

(Two-scale limit system)
.

Let
(\phi,\mu,\phi_{1},\mu_{1})
be the limit fields from Lemma

Then, for all

\theta_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}\phi,\zeta_{0}\rangle\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Y_{p}}{\mathbf{D}}(\mu,\mu_{1})\cdot{\mathbf{D}}(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1})\,d(y,x,t)+\theta_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}G(\phi)\zeta_{0}\,d(x,t)=0,

Proof.

We construct a recovery sequence of test functions and pass to the limit in

Recovery sequence.
For smooth
\zeta_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)

\zeta^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))
is an admissible test function
in (
2.2
). Since
\zeta_{0}
is compactly supported in time,

\zeta^{\varepsilon}
vanishes near

and

. By the strong consistency
of the unfolding operator,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\partial_{t}\zeta^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\to\partial_{t}\zeta_{0}\quad

\displaystyle\text{strongly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}),

\displaystyle\to\zeta_{0}\quad

\displaystyle\text{strongly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}),

\displaystyle\to\nabla_{x}\zeta_{0}+\nabla_{y}\zeta_{1}\quad

An analogous recovery sequence
\eta^{\varepsilon}
is constructed for the chemical potential
equation using
\eta_{0}
and
\eta_{1}

Passage to the limit.
Insert
\zeta^{\varepsilon}
into (
2.2
) and

\eta^{\varepsilon}
into (

Since
\zeta^{\varepsilon}
vanishes at

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\to\phi

in

with the strong convergence

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}\zeta^{\varepsilon}\,dx\,dt\;\longrightarrow\;-|Y_{p}|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\partial_{t}\zeta_{0}\,dx\,dt=\theta_{p}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}\phi,\zeta_{0}\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}\,dt,

Apply the unfolding criterion (
3.1
)
to rewrite each remaining integral over

\Omega\times Y_{p}

Using the weak convergences of

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon})
and

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon})
from

It remains to identify the nonlinear terms.
Set

By compactness,

Since

is globally Lipschitz and

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(G(\phi_{\varepsilon}))=G(u_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}.

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(G(\phi_{\varepsilon}))\to G(\phi)\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}).

For the potential term, Assumption
2.2
implies

|F^{\prime}(a)-F^{\prime}(b)|\leq C(1+|a|^{2}+|b|^{2})|a-b|,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Since

F^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\to F^{\prime}(\phi)\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{6/5}(\Omega\times Y_{p})).

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}))=F^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}(0)\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda_{\varepsilon}}\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y_{p}.

\|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}))-F^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6/5}(\Omega\times Y_{p}))}\\
\leq\|F^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6/5}(\Omega\times Y_{p}))}+|F^{\prime}(0)|\,\|\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6/5}(\Omega\times Y_{p}))}.

The first term tends to zero by the preceding argument. The second term also
tends to zero because
|\Lambda_{\varepsilon}|\to 0
. Therefore,

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}))\to F^{\prime}(\phi)\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{6/5}(\Omega\times Y_{p})).

This convergence is sufficient to pass to the limit against smooth test
functions, since
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
for
d\leq 3
.
Therefore, passing to the limit in the unfolded variational formulation gives
(
3.5
)–(
3.6
) for smooth test functions.

A density argument extends the identities to the full test function spaces.
∎

3.4
Cell problems, correctors, and the homogenized tensor

Taking
\zeta_{0}=0
in (
3.5
) and
\eta_{0}=0
in (
3.6
)
and localizing to a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega
, the correctors
\phi_{1}
and
\mu_{1}

satisfy, for a.e.
(t,x)
,

\int_{Y_{p}}\!(\nabla_{x}\mu+\nabla_{y}\mu_{1})\cdot\nabla_{y}\zeta_{1}\,dy=0\quad\forall\,\zeta_{1}\in H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p}),

\int_{Y_{p}}\!(\nabla_{x}\phi+\nabla_{y}\phi_{1})\cdot\nabla_{y}\eta_{1}\,dy=0\quad\forall\,\eta_{1}\in H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p}).

These are scalar Neumann cell problems on
Y_{p}
. For
i=1,\dots,d
, let

\chi_{i}\in H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p})
be the unique solution of

\int_{Y_{p}}({\mathbf{e}}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{i})\cdot\nabla_{y}\psi\,dy=0\qquad\forall\,\psi\in H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p}),

(3.7)

or equivalently
-\Delta_{y}(y_{i}+\chi_{i})=0
in
Y_{p}
with no-flux condition

({\mathbf{e}}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{i})\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{y}=0
on
\partial Y_{s}
and
Y
-periodicity.
Existence and uniqueness follow from the Lax–Milgram theorem on
H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p})
.

Lemma 3.6

(Corrector representation)
.

The two-scale correctors satisfy

\phi_{1}(t,x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}\phi(t,x)\,\chi_{i}(y),\qquad\mu_{1}(t,x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}\mu(t,x)\,\chi_{i}(y).

(3.8)

Proof.

By linearity of the cell equation in the data
\nabla_{x}\phi
and
\nabla_{x}\mu
, the
functions
\sum_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\,\chi_{i}
and
\sum_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\mu\,\chi_{i}

satisfy the respective cell problems pointwise in
(t,x)
. Uniqueness in

H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p})
gives the result.
∎

The homogenized diffusion tensor is defined by

{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{ij}:=\frac{1}{|Y_{p}|}\int_{Y_{p}}({\mathbf{e}}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{i})\cdot(e_{j}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{j})\,dy.

(3.9)

Standard arguments show that
{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}
is symmetric and uniformly elliptic;
see e.g.
[
21
]
.

3.5
Homogenized system

Theorem 3.7

(Qualitative homogenization)
.

Let Assumptions
2.2
,
2.3
, and
2.4
hold. Then the microscopic
solutions
(\phi_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varepsilon})
converge, up to a subsequence, to a pair

(\phi,\mu)
satisfying the homogenized system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\phi-\operatorname{div}({\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu)+G(\phi)=0&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
\mu=-\operatorname{div}({\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi)+F^{\prime}(\phi)&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi\cdot{\mathbf{n}}={\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0&\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
\phi(0,x)=\phi_{0}(x)&\text{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.10)

Proof.

Substituting the corrector representation of Lemma
3.6
into
the two-scale system (
3.5
)–(
3.6
) and integrating out
the
y
-variable using the definition (
3.9
) of
{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}
, one obtains,
for all
\zeta_{0}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
,

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\partial_{t}\phi,\zeta_{0}\rangle\,dt+\int_{(0,T)\times\Omega}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu\cdot\nabla\zeta_{0}\,d(x,t)+\int_{(0,T)\times\Omega}G(\phi)\zeta_{0}\,d(x,t)=0,

\int_{(0,T)\times\Omega}\mu\,\eta_{0}\,d(x,t)=\int_{(0,T)\times\Omega}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\eta_{0}\,d(x,t)+\int_{(0,T)\times\Omega}F^{\prime}(\phi)\eta_{0}\,d(x,t),

for all
\eta_{0}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
. Here we used that integrals over

\Omega\times Y_{p}
of
y
-independent functions contribute a factor of
|Y_{p}|
, which
is divided through to be absorbed into
{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}
via (
3.9
). The boundary conditions follow
from the natural boundary conditions encoded in the test function space
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

The initial condition
\phi(0)=\phi^{0}
is identified as follows.
By Lemma
3.4
,
\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(0)=P_{\varepsilon}\phi^{0}_{\varepsilon}\to\phi(0)
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
By the unfolding criterion (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) and the strong convergence

\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{0}_{\varepsilon})\to\phi^{0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega\times Y_{p})

from Assumption
2.4
, for every
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\phi^{0}_{\varepsilon}\,\psi\,dx=\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega\times Y_{p}}\mathcal{T}^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}(\phi^{0}_{\varepsilon})\,\psi\,d(x,y)+\int_{\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\cap{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}\phi^{0}_{\varepsilon}\psi\,dx\;\longrightarrow\;\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{0}\,\psi\,dx,

so
P_{\varepsilon}\phi^{0}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\phi^{0}
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Since the strong limit is already
\phi(0)
, we conclude
\phi(0)=\phi^{0}
.
∎

4
Conditional Quantitative rates

4.1
Scale-splitting operator

We recall the scale-splitting operator used in the quantitative estimates.
The construction follows the fixed-domain scale-splitting operator of

[
19
, Section 1.6]
. Since the homogenized fields are defined on the
fixed macroscopic domain
\Omega
, we apply the scale-splitting operator to
functions on
\Omega
and then restrict the resulting functions to

{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
. Thus the operator itself is not intrinsic to the
perforated domain. The perforated geometry enters through the restriction to

{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
, the unfolding onto
\Omega\times Y_{p}
, and the
cell correctors defined on
Y_{p}
.

Full-cell averages.

Let
Y=(0,1)^{d}
. Since
\Omega
is Lipschitz, we fix a bounded extension
operator

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}:H^{m}(\Omega)\to H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad m=0,1,2,

where the value of
m
is chosen according to the regularity of the function
under consideration. For
v\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, define the local full-cell average by

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}v(\varepsilon\xi):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}|Y|}\int_{\varepsilon(\xi+Y)}v(z)\,dz.

The normalization is the usual average over the full cell

\varepsilon(\xi+Y)
.

The
Q_{1}
scale-splitting operator.

Let
\{q_{\kappa}\}_{\kappa\in\{0,1\}^{d}}
be the standard
Q_{1}
shape
functions on
Y
, namely

q_{\kappa}(y)=\prod_{\ell=1}^{d}y_{\ell}^{\kappa_{\ell}}(1-y_{\ell})^{1-\kappa_{\ell}},\qquad y\in Y.

For
v\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define

\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}v(x):=\sum_{\kappa\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}v\left(\varepsilon\left\lfloor\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor_{Y}+\varepsilon\kappa\right)q_{\kappa}\left(\left\{\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right\}_{Y}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For a function
v
defined on
\Omega
, we set

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}v:=\left.\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}v)\right|_{\Omega}.

When the function is used on the perforated domain, we use the same notation
for its restriction:

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}v:=\left.\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}v)\right|_{{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}}.

By construction,
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}v
is separately affine on each
full cell
\varepsilon(\xi+Y)
. It is important to note that this does not
mean that its gradient is constant on each cell. In dimensions
d\geq 2
, a

Q_{1}
-function generally has a gradient which still depends on the cell
variable.

Properties.

For
{\varepsilon}>0
, the scale-splitting operator

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}\,:\,H^{1}({\Omega})\to W^{1,\infty}({\Omega}),

satisfies the following estimates, with constants

C>0
that may depend on
\Omega
,

Y
,
d
, and the chosen extension operator, but not on
\varepsilon
.

(P1)

(Stability in
H^{1}
.)

For every
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\|\nabla\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|v-\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(P2)

(Unfolded convergence.)

If
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, then

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}v)\to v\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega\times Y_{p}),\quad\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(\nabla\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}v)\to\nabla v\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega\times Y_{p})^{d}.

(4.2)

(P3)

(Gradient product estimate.)
For every
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and every

\psi\in L^{2}_{{\mathrm{per}}}(Y)
,

\left\|\nabla{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(v)\,\psi\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\;\leq\;C\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

(4.3)

This is the gradient analogue of the product estimate for
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}

(Proposition 3.2 of
[
29
]
), and follows from the same cell-by-cell
argument. On each cell
\varepsilon(\xi+Y)
,
\nabla_{x}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(v)
is
expressed via the
Q_{1}
nodal formula in terms of differences of local averages

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}(v)
at adjacent nodes divided by
\varepsilon
; each
difference quotient is bounded by
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}
on the union of
neighbouring cells via the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality. Squaring,
multiplying by
|\psi(x/\varepsilon)|^{2}
, integrating over each cell, and
summing over
\xi
yields (
4.3
); see
[
29
, Theorem 3.4]

for the analogous computation. Cells intersecting
\partial\Omega
are handled
via
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(v)=\widetilde{{\mathcal{Q}}}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}v)
,
with the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality applied on neighbouring cells in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the bound absorbed into
C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

by boundedness of
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}:H^{1}(\Omega)\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}
will be applied to macroscopic
coefficients such as
\partial_{x_{i}}\phi
and
\partial_{x_{i}}\mu
. In the
corrector construction, the regularized coefficients

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)
and

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\mu)
are restricted to

{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
and multiplied by pore-cell correctors

\chi_{i}(x/\varepsilon)
. Thus the macroscopic regularization is performed on
the fixed domain
\Omega
, while the perforated geometry is encoded through
the restriction to
{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}_{p}
and the correctors on
Y_{p}
.

4.2
Corrector approximations

We now define the first-order corrector approximations used in the
quantitative estimates. From this point on, the rate result is conditional
on additional regularity of the homogenized solution. This regularity is not
a consequence of the weak compactness theory of Section
3
;
it is an additional assumption needed to justify the quantitative estimates.

Assumption 4.1

(Quantitative regularity assumption)
.

We assume that the homogenized solution satisfies

\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),\qquad\mu\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)).

(4.4)

In addition, we assume
\phi\in C([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega))
, so that

\phi(0)\in H^{2}(\Omega)
is well-defined and
Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi(0))

is meaningful. In particular,

\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),\qquad\partial_{x_{i}}\mu\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),\qquad i=1,\ldots,d.

The constants in the estimates below may depend on the norm of

(\phi,\mu)
in the spaces appearing in (
4.4
), but not
on
\varepsilon
.

Remark 4.2
.

Assumption
4.1
is an additional hypothesis on the
homogenized solution
(\phi,\mu)
of (
3.10
); it is

not
a consequence of the weak compactness theory of
Section
3
. Such assumptions are standard in
quantitative homogenization: see Bensoussan–Lions–Papanicolaou
[
5
]
,
Griso
[
29
,
30
]
, and Kenig–Lin–Shen
[
37
]
.
Establishing (
4.4
) rigorously would require compatible
initial data
\phi^{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, suitable geometry on
\Omega

(convexity or
C^{1,1}
boundary), and elliptic regularity for

-\operatorname{div}({\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\,\cdot)
, which depends
on the smoothness of
\partial Y_{s}
; see
[
19
,
21
]
.
We therefore take (
4.4
) as a standing hypothesis
throughout Section
4
.

Throughout this subsection, if a function is defined on
\Omega
, we use
the same notation for its ordinary restriction to
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
.
The scale-splitting operator is applied on the fixed domain

\Omega
, and the resulting functions are then restricted to

\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
.

Extension of the cell correctors.

Let
\chi_{i}\in H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p})
,
i=1,\ldots,d
, be the cell
correctors from Section
3.4
. Since
Y_{s}\Subset Y
and
Y_{p}

is Lipschitz, each
\chi_{i}
admits an
H^{1}
-extension to
Y
. We fix
periodic extensions, still denoted by
\chi_{i}
, such that

\chi_{i}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(Y),\qquad\chi_{i}|_{Y_{p}}\ \text{is the original pore-cell corrector}.

This convention makes
\chi_{i}(x/\varepsilon)
well defined also in the
boundary layer of incomplete cells. On complete pore cells, the extension
agrees with the original corrector on
Y_{p}
.

Product estimates.

Besides the gradient product estimate (
4.3
), we shall use the
standard scale-splitting product estimate form
[
29
, Proposition 3.2]

\left\|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}v\,\psi\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(Y)}

(4.5)

for every
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and every

\psi\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{per}}(Y)
. The constant
C
is independent of

\varepsilon
. This estimate is obtained by the same cell-by-cell argument
as (
4.3
).

Definition of the scalar correctors.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}
be the fixed-domain scale-splitting operator from
Section
4.1
. We define, for

(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\phi(t,x)+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)(t,x)\,\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),

(4.6)

\displaystyle M^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\mu(t,x)+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\mu)(t,x)\,\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right).

(4.7)

Note that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
is not required to satisfy any boundary condition
on
\partial\Omega
: in the variational framework employed here, the boundary
conditions on
\partial\Omega
enter through the homogenized conormal condition

{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0
, which is used in the integration-by-parts
argument of Lemma
4.5
.

Regularity of the scalar correctors.

Since
\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
, the stability
of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}
gives

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})).

Similarly,

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\mu)\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})).

The product rule gives, in the weak sense on
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
,

\nabla\!\left(\varepsilon{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)=\varepsilon\nabla{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)+{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right).

(4.8)

By (
4.3
),

\left\|\nabla{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\chi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

By (
4.5
),

\left\|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

Together with (
4.4
), this yields

\|\Phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))},\qquad\|M^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}.

(4.9)

Moreover, by (
4.5
),

\|\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))},\quad\|M^{\varepsilon}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}.

(4.10)

The time derivative of
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
is understood in the distributional
sense in time. Since
\partial_{t}\phi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
and

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}
is linear in the spatial variable,

\partial_{t}\Phi^{\varepsilon}=\partial_{t}\phi+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{t}\phi)\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})).

(4.11)

Furthermore,

\|\partial_{t}(\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\phi)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}.

(4.12)

Auxiliary Neumann-compatible fluxes.

For
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, define

\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\left({\mathbf{e}}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\quad\text{in }\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}.

(4.13)

For
w=\phi(t)
and
w=\mu(t)
, this flux belongs to

L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{d}
for a.e.
t
.
The no-flux property is understood in the following weak sense:
for every
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
,

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}J_{w}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\psi\,\mathrm{div}\,J_{w}^{\varepsilon}\,dx=0,

(4.14)

which follows from the cell boundary condition

(\mathbf{e}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{i})\cdot\mathbf{n}_{y}=0
on
\partial Y_{s}

in weak form (
3.7
), after rescaling to
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}

and summing over cells. Since
b_{i}=\mathbf{e}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\in L^{2}(Y_{p})^{d}

only, no pointwise normal trace is available; the identity
(
4.14
) is the correct variational substitute.

Lemma 4.3

(Approximation properties of the correctors)
.

Let
d\in\{2,3\}
. Under the regularity assumption
(
4.4
), we have

\|\phi-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))},

(4.15)

and, for each
i=1,\ldots,d
,

\|\partial_{x_{i}}\phi-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}.

(4.16)

Moreover,

\left\|\nabla\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\left[\nabla\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))},

(4.17)

and

\|\nabla\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}.

(4.18)

The corresponding estimates for
M^{\varepsilon}
are

\left\|\nabla M^{\varepsilon}-\left[\nabla\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\mu)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))},

(4.19)

and

\|\nabla M^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}.

(4.20)

Proof.

We prove the estimates for
\phi
. The proof for
\mu
is identical.

By the
L^{2}
-approximation property of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}
, applied on

\Omega
and then restricted to
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
, for a.e.

t\in(0,T)
,

\|\phi(t)-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

After squaring and integrating in time, this gives
(
4.15
). Applying the same estimate to

\partial_{x_{i}}\phi(t)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
yields

\|\partial_{x_{i}}\phi(t)-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Squaring and integrating in time gives (
4.16
).

From the definition of
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
, we have in the weak sense
on
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}

\nabla\Phi^{\varepsilon}=\nabla\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right).

The last term is estimated by the gradient product estimate
(
4.3
):

\left\|\nabla{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)(t)\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\chi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(Y)}.

Hence

\left\|\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))},

which proves (
4.17
).

It remains to compare with
\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
. Since

\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi){\mathbf{e}}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),

we combine (
4.17
) with
(
4.16
) and obtain

\|\nabla\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}.

This proves (
4.18
). The estimates
(
4.19
) and (
4.20
) follow in the same
way with
\mu
in place of
\phi
.
∎

4.3
Variational residual equations

The corrector approximations
(\Phi^{\varepsilon},M^{\varepsilon})
are not
expected to satisfy the microscopic equations in the strong sense, since
the cell correctors belong only to
H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}},0}(Y_{p})
.
We therefore define the residuals only through the weak formulations,
in the spirit of the variational unfolding error method.

For a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, define

\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}
by

\bigl\langle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t),\zeta\bigr\rangle:=\bigl\langle\partial_{t}\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t),\zeta\bigr\rangle+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\nabla M^{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}G(\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\zeta\,dx

(4.21)

for all
\zeta\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
. Similarly, define

\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}
by

\bigl\langle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t),\eta\bigr\rangle:=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}M^{\varepsilon}(t)\eta\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\eta\,dx

(4.22)

for all
\eta\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
.

The estimates of Section
4.2
imply

\Phi^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})),\qquad M^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})),

and

\partial_{t}\Phi^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})).

Hence

\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}),\qquad\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}).

Set

e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}:=\phi_{\varepsilon}-\Phi^{\varepsilon},\qquad e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}:=\mu_{\varepsilon}-M^{\varepsilon}.

Subtracting (
4.21
) from the microscopic weak formulation
gives, for all
\zeta\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
and a.e.

t\in(0,T)
,

\bigl\langle\partial_{t}e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t),\zeta\bigr\rangle+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\nabla e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(G(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))-G(\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\bigr)\zeta\,dx=-\bigl\langle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t),\zeta\bigr\rangle.

(4.23)

Likewise, subtracting (
4.22
) from the microscopic
chemical-potential identity gives, for all

\eta\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
and a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t)\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(t))-F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t))\bigr)\eta\,dx-\bigl\langle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t),\eta\bigr\rangle.

(4.24)

Lemma 4.4

(Porosity oscillation estimate)
.

Let
h\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
, and let

V_{\varepsilon}:=P_{\varepsilon}v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be a uniform extension of

v
, that is,

V_{\varepsilon}=v\quad\text{in }\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},\qquad\|V_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

Then

\left|\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}hv\,dx-\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega}hV_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\leq C\varepsilon\|h\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

(4.25)

Proof.

Since
V_{\varepsilon}=v
in
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}hv\,dx-\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega}hV_{\varepsilon}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}-\theta_{p}\bigr)hV_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

We split
\Omega=\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cup\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
, where

\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is the union of complete cells and

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}:=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

Set

\rho(y):=\mathbf{1}_{Y_{p}}(y)-\theta_{p}.

Then
\rho\in L^{\infty}(Y)
and

\int_{Y}\rho(y)\,dy=0.

For
k\in K_{\varepsilon}
, let

g(x):=h(x)V_{\varepsilon}(x).

By the zero-mean property of
\rho
and the Poincaré–Wirtinger
inequality in
W^{1,1}(Y)
, applied after rescaling to the cell

\varepsilon(k+Y)
, we obtain

\left|\int_{\varepsilon(k+Y)}\rho\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)g(x)\,dx\right|\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\varepsilon(k+Y)}|\nabla g(x)|\,dx.

Summing over
k\in K_{\varepsilon}
, using

|\nabla(hV_{\varepsilon})|\leq|\nabla h|\,|V_{\varepsilon}|+|h|\,|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}|,

and applying Cauchy’s inequality, gives

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}-\theta_{p}\bigr)hV_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(|\nabla h|\,|V_{\varepsilon}|+|h|\,|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|h\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|V_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(4.26)

Since
\Omega
is Lipschitz,
|\Lambda_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\varepsilon
.
Moreover, the standard collar estimate gives, for every

z\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\|z\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|z\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Lambda_{\varepsilon}}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}-\theta_{p}\bigr)hV_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon})}\|V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|h\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|V_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(4.27)

Combining (
4.26
) and
(
4.27
), and using the extension estimate for

V_{\varepsilon}=P_{\varepsilon}v
, gives (
4.25
).
∎

Lemma 4.5

(Elliptic consistency estimate)
.

Let
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)
. Define

\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\left({\mathbf{e}}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\quad\text{in }\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}.

Then, for every
v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
, with

V_{\varepsilon}:=P_{\varepsilon}v
, one has

\left|\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v\,dx-\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

(4.28)

Proof.

For
i=1,\ldots,d
, set

b_{i}(y):=\mathbf{e}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}(y)\in L^{2}(Y_{p})^{d}.

The cell problem (
3.7
) gives, in the weak sense,

\mathrm{div}_{y}\,b_{i}=0\quad\text{in }Y_{p},\qquad b_{i}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{y}=0\quad\text{on }\partial Y_{s},

and
b_{i}
is
Y
-periodic. By the definition (
3.9
) of

{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}
and the cell problem (
3.7
),

\int_{Y_{p}}b_{i}(y)\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{j}\,dy=\theta_{p}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}_{ij},\qquad i,j=1,\ldots,d.

(4.29)

Since
V_{\varepsilon}=v
on
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx
.
We split

R_{\varepsilon}:=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx-\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx=:R_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}+R_{\varepsilon}^{(2)},

where
R_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}
collects the contribution from the complete cells

\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
R_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}
collects the boundary layer

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}:=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

Step 1: Complete cells.

By the unfolding criterion (
3.1
) and the
L^{2}
-stability of

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx=\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w))(x,y)\,b_{i}(y)\cdot\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla V_{\varepsilon})(x,y)\,dy\,dx.

We replace
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w))
by

Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(x)
. By the standard unfolding approximation
estimate
[
29
, Equation (3.4)]
,

\|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w))-Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times Y)}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

where the last step uses the
H^{1}
-stability of
Q_{\varepsilon}

(property (
4.1
)) and
\partial_{x_{i}}w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Since
\|b_{i}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{p})}\leq C
and

\|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla V_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times Y_{p})}\leq C\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
the replacement error is bounded by

C\varepsilon\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

It remains to estimate

S_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(x)\,b_{i}(y)\cdot\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla V_{\varepsilon})(x,y)\,dy\,dx-\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

Apply Theorem 3.4 of Griso
[
29
]
to

V_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. There exists

\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}}}(Y;L^{2}(\Omega))
, equivalently

\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega;H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}}}(Y))
, such that

\displaystyle\bigl\|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla V_{\varepsilon})-\nabla_{x}V_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{y}\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}\bigr\|_{[L^{2}(Y;H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))]^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(4.30)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(Y;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We write

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla V_{\varepsilon})=\nabla_{x}V_{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{y}\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}+r^{\varepsilon},

where

\|r^{\varepsilon}\|_{[L^{2}(Y;H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))]^{d}}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Estimate of the
r^{\varepsilon}
-term.

Since
Q_{\varepsilon}
is
H^{1}
-stable and

\partial_{x_{i}}w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\in H^{1}(\Omega),\qquad\|Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

by property (
4.1
). Hence

Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)|_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

with the same bound. Since

r^{\varepsilon}\in[L^{2}(Y;H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))]^{d}
, the term involving

r^{\varepsilon}
is understood as the duality pairing

\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{Y_{p}}\left\langle r^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,y),Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(\cdot)b_{i}(y)\right\rangle_{[H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})]^{d},[H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})]^{d}}\,dy.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{Y_{p}}\left\langle r^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,y),Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(\cdot)b_{i}(y)\right\rangle_{[H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})]^{d},[H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})]^{d}}\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|r^{\varepsilon}\|_{[L^{2}(Y;H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))]^{d}}\,\|Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)b_{i}\|_{[L^{2}(Y_{p};H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))]^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|r^{\varepsilon}\|_{[L^{2}(Y;H^{-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))]^{d}}\,\|Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|b_{i}\|_{L^{2}(Y_{p})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Cancellation of the
\nabla_{y}\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}
-term.

Since
Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(x)
is independent of
y
, we have

\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y_{p}}Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(x)\,b_{i}(y)\cdot\nabla_{y}\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}(x,y)\,dy\,dx=\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(x)\left[\int_{Y_{p}}b_{i}(y)\cdot\nabla_{y}\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}(x,y)\,dy\right]dx.

By Fubini, for a.e.
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
the function

y\mapsto\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}(x,y)|_{Y_{p}}
belongs to

H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}}}(Y_{p})
. After subtracting its
Y_{p}
-mean, it is an
admissible test function in the cell problem (
3.7
). Since

b_{i}=e_{i}+\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}

satisfies

\int_{Y_{p}}b_{i}(y)\cdot\nabla_{y}\zeta(y)\,dy=0\qquad\text{for all }\zeta\in H^{1}_{{\mathrm{per}}}(Y_{p}),

we obtain

\int_{Y_{p}}b_{i}(y)\cdot\nabla_{y}\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}(x,y)\,dy=0\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Hence the
\nabla_{y}\hat{\psi}^{\varepsilon}
-contribution vanishes.

Identification of the
\nabla_{x}V_{\varepsilon}
-term.

The remaining contribution over the complete cells is

\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(x)\,b_{i}(y)\cdot\nabla_{x}V_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dy\,dx.

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(x)\,b_{i}(y)\cdot\nabla_{x}V_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)(x)\left[\int_{Y_{p}}b_{i}(y)\,dy\right]\cdot\nabla_{x}V_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx.

By the cell-average identity (
4.29
), this equals

\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}Q_{\varepsilon}(\nabla w)\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

Finally, using the approximation property (
4.1
)
3
,

\|Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)-\partial_{x_{i}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Therefore,

\left|\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\bigl(Q_{\varepsilon}(\nabla w)-\nabla w\bigr)\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\leq C\varepsilon\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Combining the complete-cell contributions gives

|R_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}|\leq C\varepsilon\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(4.31)

The boundary layer near

\partial\Omega
is accounted for by the
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
contribution in Step 2, which produces the

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{1/2})
collar estimate and is the source of the

\varepsilon^{1/2}
rate on bounded domains.

Step 2: Boundary layer.

We estimate

\|\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}
.
On each partial cell
\varepsilon(\xi+Y)
intersecting
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
,
a change of variables
x=\varepsilon\xi+\varepsilon y
gives

\int_{\varepsilon(\xi+Y)\cap\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}|\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|Q_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\varepsilon(\xi+Y))}\cdot\varepsilon^{d}\|b_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Y_{p})}.

Since
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)=\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(\partial_{x_{i}}w))

is
Q_{1}
on
\varepsilon(\xi+Y)
with nodal values given by averages of

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)
over neighbouring cells
(which may lie outside
\Omega
),
the inverse estimate for
Q_{1}
functions gives

\|\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|_{L^{\infty}(\varepsilon(\xi+Y))}\leq C\varepsilon^{-d/2}\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\varepsilon(\xi+Y)})},

where
\widetilde{\varepsilon(\xi+Y)}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes the union of

\varepsilon(\xi+Y)
and its
2^{d}
neighbours, which need not be subsets of

\Omega
. Substituting and summing over all partial cells
\xi
with

\varepsilon(\xi+Y)\cap\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset
, and using bounded
overlap of the neighbourhoods
\widetilde{\varepsilon(\xi+Y)}
,

\|\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|b_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Y_{p})}\sum_{\xi\,\mathrm{partial}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\varepsilon(\xi+Y)})}.

Applying the collar estimate to

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\sum_{\xi\,\mathrm{partial}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\varepsilon(\xi+Y)})}\leq C\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(\partial_{x_{i}}w)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Lambda_{2\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}w\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon\|w\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

where the last step uses boundedness of
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}:H^{2}(\Omega)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Taking square roots,

\|\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\left|\int_{\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

For the homogenized term on
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
, the collar estimate gives

\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
,
hence

\theta_{p}\left|\int_{\Lambda_{\varepsilon}}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Therefore

|R_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}|\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(4.32)

Step 3: Conclusion.

Combining (
4.31
) and (
4.32
), and
using
0<\varepsilon<1
so that
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}
,

|R_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})},

where the last step uses the uniform extension estimate

\|\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}
.
This proves (
4.28
).
∎

Lemma 4.6

(Variational residual estimate)
.

Assume that the homogenized solution satisfies (
4.4
).
Then there exists
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}+\|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}.

(4.33)

Proof.

We use the auxiliary fluxes
\mathcal{J}_{w}^{\varepsilon}
defined in
(
4.13
). The proof is entirely variational.

Define

a_{\phi}:=-{\mathrm{div}}({\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi),\qquad a_{\mu}:=-{\mathrm{div}}({\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu).

By the homogenized equations,

a_{\phi}=\mu-F^{\prime}(\phi),\qquad a_{\mu}=-\partial_{t}\phi-G(\phi).

(4.34)

Under (
4.4
), we have

a_{\phi}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)),\qquad a_{\mu}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)).

Indeed,
a_{\mu}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
follows from
\partial_{t}\phi\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))

and the Lipschitz continuity of
G
. Also,

a_{\phi}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
follows from
\mu\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))

and
F^{\prime}(\phi)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
.
The latter is a consequence of

\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
, the embedding

H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for
d\leq 3
, and
the growth assumption on
F^{\prime\prime}
.

Estimate of
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}
.

Let
\eta\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
, and set

H_{\varepsilon}:=P_{\varepsilon}\eta\in H^{1}(\Omega).

From (
4.22
), add and subtract

\mu
,
F^{\prime}(\phi)
, and
\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
:

\bigl\langle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t),\eta\bigr\rangle=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}(M^{\varepsilon}-\mu)\eta\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(F^{\prime}(\phi)-F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\eta\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(\nabla\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\bigr)\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\\
+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}(\mu-F^{\prime}(\phi))\eta\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx.

Using (
4.34
), the last line is

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}a_{\phi}\eta\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx.

We split this term as

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}a_{\phi}\eta\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\\
\left[\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}a_{\phi}\eta\,dx-\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega}a_{\phi}H_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right]+\left[\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla H_{\varepsilon}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\right].

Here we used the homogenized elliptic relation
a_{\phi}=-{\mathrm{div}}({\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi)

together with the homogeneous conormal boundary condition
{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega
,
which gives

\int_{\Omega}a_{\phi}H_{\varepsilon}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla H_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

By Lemma
4.4
, applied with

h=a_{\phi}(t)
, and by Lemma
4.5
,
applied with
w=\phi(t)
, we obtain, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\left|\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}(\mu-F^{\prime}(\phi))\eta\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\right|\leq C\varepsilon\|a_{\phi}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}+C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\\
\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\left(\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|a_{\phi}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

(4.35)

The remaining terms are of order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
in

H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}
. Indeed, by
(
4.10
),

\|M^{\varepsilon}(t)-\mu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\mu(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

and by Lemma
4.3
,

\|\nabla\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t)-\mathcal{J}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

For the nonlinear term, the growth condition on
F^{\prime\prime}
, the embedding

H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})

for
d\leq 3
, and the uniform
H^{1}
-bounds for

\phi
and
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
give

\|F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t))-F^{\prime}(\phi(t))\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}\leq C\|\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t)-\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

Using again (
4.10
), we get

\|F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t))-F^{\prime}(\phi(t))\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Consequently,

\|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\left(1+\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mu(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|a_{\phi}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right).

Squaring and integrating over
(0,T)
, and using
(
4.4
) together with

a_{\phi}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
, yields the estimate for

\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}
.

Estimate of
\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}
.

Let
\zeta\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
, and set

Z_{\varepsilon}:=P_{\varepsilon}\zeta\in H^{1}(\Omega).

From (
4.21
), add and subtract

\partial_{t}\phi
,
G(\phi)
, and

\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}
:

\bigl\langle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t),\zeta\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle\partial_{t}(\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\phi)(t),\zeta\bigr\rangle+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(G(\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t))-G(\phi(t))\bigr)\zeta\,dx\\
+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(\nabla M^{\varepsilon}(t)-\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t)\bigr)\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{t}\phi(t)+G(\phi(t)))\zeta\,dx.

Using (
4.34
), we have

\partial_{t}\phi+G(\phi)=-a_{\mu}.

Hence the last line is

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}a_{\mu}\zeta\,dx.

We split this term as

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}a_{\mu}\zeta\,dx=\left[\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx-\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu\cdot\nabla Z_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right]\\
+\left[\theta_{p}\int_{\Omega}a_{\mu}Z_{\varepsilon}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}a_{\mu}\zeta\,dx\right].

Here we used the homogenized elliptic relation
a_{\mu}=-{\mathrm{div}}({\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu)

together with the homogeneous conormal boundary condition
{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega
,
which gives

\int_{\Omega}a_{\mu}Z_{\varepsilon}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathrm{hom}}\nabla\mu\cdot\nabla Z_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

By Lemma
4.5
, applied with

w=\mu(t)
, and by Lemma
4.4
, applied
with
h=a_{\mu}(t)
, we obtain, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\left|\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{t}\phi+G(\phi))\zeta\,dx\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|\mu(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\zeta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}+C\varepsilon\|a_{\mu}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|\zeta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\\
\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\left(\|\mu(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|a_{\mu}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\|\zeta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

(4.36)

The gradient remainder satisfies

\|\nabla M^{\varepsilon}(t)-\mathcal{J}_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\mu(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

by Lemma
4.3
. The source term is controlled by
the Lipschitz continuity of
G
and (
4.10
):

\|G(\Phi^{\varepsilon}(t))-G(\phi(t))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Finally, (
4.12
) gives

\|\partial_{t}(\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\phi)(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}\leq C\varepsilon\|\partial_{t}\phi(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Combining these estimates gives

\|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\left(1+\|\mu(t)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|a_{\mu}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{t}\phi(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right).

Squaring and integrating over
(0,T)
, and using
(
4.4
) together with

a_{\mu}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
, yields the estimate for

\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}
.

Combining the estimates for
\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}
and

\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}
proves (
4.33
).
∎

4.4
Quantitative convergence rates

We now derive the quantitative estimate in the variational energy scale
associated with the Cahn–Hilliard structure. Since the residuals

\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}
are defined
only in dual spaces, we do not test the chemical-potential error equation
with
\partial_{t}e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
. Instead, we use the standard
Neumann inverse and work in a negative norm for the phase variable.

Let

m_{\varepsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{|\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}|}\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx,\qquad\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}:=e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}-m_{\varepsilon}.

For each
f\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}
with zero mean, let

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}f\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
be the solution
of

-\Delta\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}f=f\quad\text{in }\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},\qquad\nabla\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}f\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon},\qquad\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}f\,dx=0.

We define

\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}:=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}f|^{2}\,dx.

By the uniform Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality on
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}

(Remark
2.1
) and standard elliptic regularity for the Neumann
Laplacian on Lipschitz domains, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}
is
uniformly equivalent (in
\varepsilon
) to the standard
H^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p})^{\prime}
-norm
on zero-mean distributions; see for instance
[
24
]
.

Theorem 4.7

(Variational corrector estimate)
.

Let Assumptions
2.2
,
2.3
,
2.4
,
and
4.1
hold. Then there
exists
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1}_{\varepsilon})}+\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\varepsilon^{1/2}\Bigr).

(4.37)

If, in addition, we have well prepared initial data

\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2},

(4.38)

then

\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1}_{\varepsilon})}+\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}.

The constant
C
depends on the data,
T
, the cell geometry, and the
regularity norms in (
4.4
), but not on

\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the weak error identities
(
4.23
)–(
4.24
) and the
variational residual estimate of Lemma
4.6
. We suppress
the time variable in the notation.

Step 1: Negative-norm testing of the phase equation.

Take
\zeta=\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}

in (
4.23
). Since

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
has zero mean,
the mean part of
\partial_{t}e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
does not contribute.
To justify this identity, note that
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
is a bounded
self-adjoint operator on the zero-mean subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
,
and
t\mapsto\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t)
belongs to

L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))\cap H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})

with zero mean. For any
\tau\in(0,T)
, testing the distributional time
derivative against
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}

and using the symmetry of
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
gives

\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\langle\partial_{t}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(\tau)\|^{2}_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|^{2}_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}},

which is the integrated form of the identity below; see
e.g.
[
57
, Chapter III, Lemma 1.2]
.
Since the mean part of
\partial_{t}e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
pairs to zero against

\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
(which has zero mean),
we may replace
\partial_{t}e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
by

\partial_{t}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
on the left, giving

\left\langle\partial_{t}e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}.

Moreover,

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\nabla e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(G(\phi_{\varepsilon})-G(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx-\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\right\rangle.

(4.39)

Step 2: Testing the chemical-potential equation by the phase error.

Take
\eta=\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}

in (
4.24
). Since

\nabla\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}=\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx=\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx-\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\right\rangle.

(4.40)

Combining (
4.39
) and
(
4.40
) gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}=-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(G(\phi_{\varepsilon})-G(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\\
+\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\right\rangle.

(4.41)

Step 3: Estimates of the nonlinear terms.

The Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})

holds for
d\leq 3
with constant uniform in
\varepsilon
, by
Remark
2.1
. The uniform
H^{1}
-bounds

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C
and

\|\Phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C

follow from Lemma
2.12
and (
4.9
)
respectively.

The interpolation inequality for zero-mean functions on
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}

states: for
f\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
with
\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}f\,dx=0
,

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{1/2},

with
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. This follows from the uniform
Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality on
\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}
(Remark
2.1
)
and the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}
: indeed,

\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\langle f,f\rangle=\langle-\Delta\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}f,f\rangle=\|\nabla\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}=\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}},

where we used integration by parts and the definition of
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
.

Since
e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}=\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}+m_{\varepsilon}
,

\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}+C|m_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{1/2}+C|m_{\varepsilon}|.

Estimate of the
F^{\prime}
term.

Using
|F^{\prime}(a)-F^{\prime}(b)|\leq C(1+|a|^{2}+|b|^{2})|a-b|
, Hölder’s inequality
with exponents
(3,2,6)
in
d=3
,

\left|\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\leq C\|1+|\phi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\Phi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

By the uniform
H^{1}
-bounds and the embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{6}
,

\|1+|\phi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\Phi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C

and
\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}
,
both with constants uniform in
\varepsilon
. Substituting the bound on

\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
and applying Young’s inequality

ab\leq\delta a^{2}+C_{\delta}b^{2}
twice, we obtain for every
\delta>0
,

\left|\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\leq\delta\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+C_{\delta}|m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

(4.42)

Estimate of the
G
term.

Since
G
is globally Lipschitz with constant
C_{G}
,

\left|\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(G(\phi_{\varepsilon})-G(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\leq C_{G}\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

By the definition of
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
and the uniform
Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality,

\|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}

with
C
independent of
\varepsilon
. Substituting the bound on

\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
and applying Young’s inequality,
we obtain for every
\delta>0
,

\left|\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(G(\phi_{\varepsilon})-G(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\leq\delta\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+C_{\delta}|m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

(4.43)

Step 4: Estimates of the residual terms.

By the definition of
\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}
,

\|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}.

Therefore

\left|\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\right|\leq C\|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}^{2}+C\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}.

(4.44)

Moreover, since
\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
has zero mean, the
uniform Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality gives

\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

Hence

\left|\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon},\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\right|\leq\delta\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}^{2}.

(4.45)

Choosing
\delta>0
sufficiently small and combining
(
4.41
)–(
4.45
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+c\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}\leq C\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+C|m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+C\|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}^{2}+C\|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}^{2}.

(4.46)

Step 5: Control of the mean.

Taking
\zeta=1
in (
4.23
) gives

\frac{d}{dt}m_{\varepsilon}(t)+\frac{1}{|\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}|}\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(G(\phi_{\varepsilon})-G(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\,dx=-\frac{1}{|\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}|}\left\langle\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon},1\right\rangle.

Multiplying by
m_{\varepsilon}(t)
, using the Lipschitz continuity of

G
, the uniform lower bound on
|\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}|
, and
Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|m_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C|m_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}+C|m_{\varepsilon}(t)|\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}+C|m_{\varepsilon}(t)|\|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}.

Using again the interpolation inequality for

\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
, we infer that, for every

\delta>0
,

\frac{d}{dt}|m_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\leq C|m_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}+\delta\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}+C_{\delta}\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+C\|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}^{2}.

(4.47)

Step 6: Gronwall argument.

Adding (
4.46
) and (
4.47
), and
choosing
\delta>0
sufficiently small, yields

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+|m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)+c\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}^{2}\leq C\left(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+|m_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)+C\|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}^{2}+C\|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}^{2}.

Integrating over
(0,t)
and applying Gronwall’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1}_{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{2}+\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}^{2}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|^{2}+\|\mathcal{R}_{1}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}^{2}+\|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}^{2}\Bigr).

Finally, since

e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}=\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}+m_{\varepsilon}
, the
uniform Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality gives

\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}+C|m_{\varepsilon}(t)|.

Thus the previous estimate also controls

\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}
.
Taking square roots and using (
4.33
) proves
(
4.37
). The final
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{1/2})

statement follows immediately from (
4.38
).
∎

Corollary 4.8

(Uncorrected convergence rate)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.7
, one has

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\varepsilon^{1/2}\Bigr).

(4.48)

In particular, if (
4.38
) holds, then

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}.

(4.49)

Proof.

We first estimate the phase variable. By the triangle inequality,

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}.

The first term is controlled by Theorem
4.7
.
For the second term, using the definition of
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
and
the product estimate for the scale-splitting operator,

\|\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}.

Therefore,

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\varepsilon^{1/2}\Bigr).

We now estimate the chemical potential in the dual norm. By definition,

\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu=e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}+M^{\varepsilon}-\mu.

The corrector part satisfies

\|M^{\varepsilon}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}\leq C\varepsilon\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))},

and hence also

\|M^{\varepsilon}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))}.

It remains to control
e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}
in

L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})
. Let

\eta\in H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
. From
(
4.24
),

\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\eta\,dx-\bigl\langle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon},\eta\bigr\rangle.

The first term is bounded by

\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

For the nonlinear term, using the growth condition on
F^{\prime}
, the
uniform
H^{1}
-bounds on
\phi_{\varepsilon}
and
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
,
and the embedding

H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})

for
d\leq 3
, we get

\left|\int_{\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}}\bigl(F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}(\Phi^{\varepsilon})\bigr)\eta\,dx\right|\leq C\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}.

Consequently,

\|e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}\leq C\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}+C\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}+\|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}.

Integrating in time and using Theorem
4.7
and
Lemma
4.6
, we obtain

\|e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\varepsilon^{1/2}\Bigr).

Combining this estimate with the bound for
M^{\varepsilon}-\mu
gives
(
4.48
). The prepared-data conclusion
(
4.49
) follows immediately.
∎

Corollary 4.9

(Corrected gradient rate)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.7
, one has

\left\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}-\left[\nabla\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\varepsilon^{1/2}\Bigr).

(4.50)

In particular, if the initial mismatch satisfies (
4.38
), then

\left\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}-\left[\nabla\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}.

(4.51)

Proof.

By definition,

e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}=\phi_{\varepsilon}-\Phi^{\varepsilon}.

Hence Theorem
4.7
gives

\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\Phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\varepsilon^{1/2}\Bigr).

On the other hand, Lemma
4.3
yields

\left\|\nabla\Phi^{\varepsilon}-\left[\nabla\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))}.

The triangle inequality gives (
4.50
). The
prepared-data estimate (
4.51
)
follows from (
4.38
).
∎

Remark 4.10

(Periodic domains and improved rates)
.

If
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
is the flat torus, there is no boundary layer
of incomplete cells. In this case the variational consistency estimate
in Lemma
4.6
improves from order
\varepsilon^{1/2}
to
order
\varepsilon
. Hence the stability estimate of
Theorem
4.7
becomes

\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-1}_{\varepsilon})}+\|e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|m_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\varepsilon\Bigr).

In particular, using Lemma
4.3
, we also obtain
the corrected gradient estimate

\left\|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}-\left[\nabla\phi+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\varepsilon\Bigr).

(4.52)

Accordingly, the uncorrected estimate becomes

\|\phi_{\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))}+\|\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}\leq C\Bigl(\|\widetilde{e}_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}(0)\|_{H^{-1}_{\varepsilon}}+|m_{\varepsilon}(0)|+\varepsilon\Bigr).

If the initial mismatch is
O(\varepsilon)
in the natural negative
norm and in the mean, then the corrected gradient estimate
(
4.52
) and the uncorrected variational
estimate above are both of order
O(\varepsilon)
.

We do not claim an uncorrected rate for

\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\phi
. In general, the microscopic
gradient contains the oscillating first-order term

\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi)\nabla_{y}\chi_{i}\!\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),

which is of order one in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})
.
Thus the natural gradient estimate is the corrected one stated in
(
4.52
).

Remark 4.11

(On the absence of a gradient rate for the chemical potential)
.

The estimates above do not control
\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon}
at rate

\varepsilon^{1/2}
. The variational energy argument controls

e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}=\phi_{\varepsilon}-\Phi^{\varepsilon}
in

L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon}))
, but controls

e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}=\mu_{\varepsilon}-M^{\varepsilon}
only in the dual
space
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})
. Obtaining

\|\nabla e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
would require testing the
phase-error equation with
e_{\mu}^{\varepsilon}
, which in turn requires
testing the chemical-potential error equation with

\partial_{t}e_{\phi}^{\varepsilon}
. This is not justified at the regularity
level
\mathcal{R}_{2}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})
.
The dual estimate
\|\mu_{\varepsilon}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega_{p}^{\varepsilon})^{\prime})}

is therefore the natural conclusion of the present framework.

5
On the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
rate and future directions

The rate
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{1/2})
of Theorem
4.7

reflects the boundary layer of incomplete cells near
\partial\Omega
and is
sharp within the present variational framework. On a bounded Lipschitz domain
the expected rate is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, obtainable in principle by two
routes.

The first is the duality argument of Kenig–Lin–Shen
[
37
]

and Gu
[
32
]
: pair
e^{\varepsilon}_{\phi}
against the solution of a
backward Cahn–Hilliard system on
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{p}
and extract an extra

\varepsilon^{1/2}
via a boundary-strip estimate. The obstruction is that the
adjoint system is nonlinear and
\varepsilon
-dependent, so the full corrector
theory—including the analogue of Lemma
4.6
for the backward
problem—must be repeated.

The second is the operator-estimate approach of Birman–Suslina

[
7
,
8
,
9
]
: for linear
operators this yields
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
in the
L^{2}
-operator norm
without regularity assumptions on the coefficients; see
Suslina
[
56
,
55
]
. Extending it to the
nonlinear Cahn–Hilliard system requires treating
F^{\prime}(\phi_{\varepsilon})
and

G(\phi_{\varepsilon})
as lower-order perturbations, with
Theorem
4.7
as the input bound. Both directions are
left for future work.
