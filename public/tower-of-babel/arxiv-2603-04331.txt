Incompressible Limit for an Age-Structured Tumor Model
Maeve Wildes

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider an age-structured mechanical model for tumor growth. This model takes into account the life-cycle of tumor cells by including an age variable. The underlying process for tumor growth is the same as classical tumor models, where growth is driven by pressure-limited cell proliferation, and cell movement away from regions of high pressure. The main contribution of this paper is establishing the convergence of solutions of the age-structured model to a limit satisfying a Hele-Shaw free boundary problem. This limiting problem describes the geometric motion of the tumor as it grows according to a nonlinear Darcy’s law.



1.
Introduction

Mathematical models for tumor growth have been extensively studied
[
2
,
11
,
17
,
26
]
. These models complement
experimental findings and help further our understanding of cancer development and progression. Tumor
models can include various levels of complexity; in this paper we will focus on a simple model describing tumor growth as driven by two factors. The first is
cell proliferation (division), which is limited by internal pressure. The second is cell movement away from regions of high pressure, described by Darcy’s law
[
17
,
7
]
.

There are two commonly studied classes of tumor growth models. The first describes tumor evolution at the level of cell density. A classical model of this type was introduced in
[
6
]
, and has been widely studied since, e.g.,
[
12
,
26
,
24
]
. This model introduces the cell density function,
\rho(t,x),
which is governed by the porous media-type equation,

(1)

\partial_{t}\rho-\mathrm{div}\,_{x}(\rho\nabla p)=\rho\Phi(p),\qquad p=\frac{m}{m-1}\Big(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{M}}\Big)^{m-1},

where
p(t,x)
represents pressure. The growth rate,
\Phi(p)
, is a decreasing function of pressure, satisfying
\Phi(p_{M})=0
for some maximum pressure
p_{M}
, referred to as the
homeostatic pressure
, the pressure at which cell proliferation ceases
[
1
]
. Pressure is caused by cells competing for space: for
m\gg 1
, the pressure is high when cell density
\rho>\rho_{M}
, and low when
\rho<\rho_{M}
. For simplicity of notation, we will assume from now on that the maximum packing density is
\rho_{M}=1
.

The second class of tumor growth models describes the geometric growth of the solid tumor via a free boundary problem, rather than describing cell density
[
9
,
15
]
. In the seminal work of Perthame, Quirós, and Vázquez,
[
26
]
, a link between the cell density model and the free boundary model is established. The authors show that the asymptotic limit of (
1
) as
m\rightarrow\infty
yields a free boundary problem of Hele-Shaw type. This limit is referred to as an
incompressible limit
, referencing the increasing “stiffness” of the pressure as
m
increases.

1.1.
Age-structured mechanical model

In this work, our objective is to establish a link between the cell density model and a free boundary problem for an age-structured tumor model related to (
1
). In our previous work,
[
21
]
, we introduce an age-structured model for tumor growth, extending (
1
) to take into account the life cycle of tumor cells. We associate an age
\theta\geq 0
with each cell, representing the physiological age of the cell, i.e., how far along in the cell cycle it is. We consider a simplified cell cycle consisting of two phases: The first is interphase, where a cell grows and copies its genetic material, and the second is mitosis, where a cell splits into two daughter cells of age zero, and restarts the cycle.

We introduce the cell distribution function,
n(t,\theta,x),
which can be interpreted as the probability of finding a cell of age
\theta\geq 0
at position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and time
t\geq 0
. The evolution of this distribution function is described by the following boundary value problem:

(2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}n+r(p)\partial_{\theta}n-\text{div}_{x}(n\nabla_{x}p)=-r(p)\nu(\theta,p)n-\mu(\theta)n\qquad&t\geq 0,\;\theta\in(0,\infty),\;x\in\Omega,\\
n(t,0,x)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu(\theta,p)n(t,\theta,x)d\theta\qquad&t\geq 0,\;x\in\Omega,\\
n(0,\theta,x)=n_{0}(\theta,x)\qquad&\theta\geq 0,\;x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 1
. The volume density is given by:

\rho(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(\theta)n(t,\theta,x)d\theta,

where
V(\theta)
is the volume of a cell of age
\theta
, and the pressure is given by

p(t,x)=\frac{m}{m-1}\rho(t,x)^{m-1},

for a typical porous-media-type equation
[
29
]
. We assume that
\nu(\cdot,p)
and
r(p)
are decreasing, and

r(p)=0,\nu(\cdot,p)=0\text{ for all }p\geq p_{M},

for some
p_{M}>0,
as is natural for pressure-limited growth.

In (
2
), the growth term
r(p)
represents the rate of cell growth (“aging”). In this setting, age is not linear in time and does not simply represent the time since last mitosis, but represents how far along in the cell cycle a cell has progressed. It is assumed that a cell can only progress in its cell cycle if the pressure is not too high: when
p=p_{M},
the cell will not age and will be stuck in its current age until pressure decreases. The function
\nu(\theta,p)
is the rate of division of cells of age
\theta
under local pressure
p
. When a cell enters its mitotic phase, the cell disappears (the term
-r\nu n
) and is replaced by two cells of age
\theta=0
(the boundary condition in the second line of (
2
)). Cells will not divide when the pressure is too high, precisely, when
p=p_{M}.
The term
\mu(\theta)
represents the age-dependent cell death rate.

In
[
21
]
, it is shown that weak solutions of (
2
) exist, and the behaviour is analyzed numerically. These results are discussed further in Section
3
. The motivation behind studying this age-structured model is that cells at different phases of
their life cycle duplicate and die at different rates. Solutions encode the age
distribution of cells, providing insight into where within the tumor most proliferation occurs. Such knowledge of the tumor’s spatial structure can lead to more accurate predictions and help with therapy development
[
22
,
25
]
. Therapy development was one of the main motivations of this work: cells at different stages of their life-cycle respond differently to treatments, so understanding the age-structure helps in designing effective therapies to slow tumor growth.

The main contribution of this work is establishing convergence of solutions of (
2
), as
m\rightarrow\infty
, to a limit solving a Hele-Shaw-type free boundary problem. (The precise statements can be found in Section
4
).

1.2.
Motivations and Related Work

The model, (
2
), discussed in this paper is motivated by previous work on space-homogeneous age-structured models. The main result of this paper, the incompressible limit of (
2
), is motivated by previous work establishing the convergence of porous media-type tumor models to incompressible limits, e.g.,
[
26
,
11
,
24
,
20
,
10
]
.

Age-structured models for tumor growth.

Age-structured tumor models have been of interest for some time, motivated by the fact that cell proliferation and death rates depend on the age of the cell
[
13
,
16
,
19
]
. However, most previous models lack spatial dependence, and so do not consider the effect of pressure on proliferation rate or the effect of volume change as cells age. A simple model of this type, known as the growth-fragmentation equation, which is the space-homogenous version of (
2
), is given by,

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}n+\partial_{\theta}n=-\beta(\theta)n-\mu(\theta)n,\qquad&t>0,\theta>0,\\
n(t,0)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta(\theta)n(t,\theta)d\theta,\qquad&t>0,\\
n(0,\theta)=n_{0}(\theta)\qquad&\theta>0.\end{cases}

In
[
21
]
, we incorporate space-dependence into the age-structure model, combining the ideas of the cell density model and the growth-fragmentation model.

Incompressible limit.

The theory surrounding the convergence of porous media equations without a growth term, i.e.,
\partial_{t}\rho-\Delta\rho^{m}=0
, is well developed, and it is known that the limit as
m\rightarrow\infty
approaches the standard Hele-Shaw free boundary problem
[
3
,
14
]
. When
1<m<\infty,
the equation for
\rho
is of porous media-type, meaning the density spreads with a finite speed of propagation
[
29
]
. It thus seems natural that the asymptotic limit would satisfy a free-boundary problem.

In the work of Perthame, Quirós, and Vázquez,
[
26
]
, this result was extended to tumor models of the type (
1
), which are porous medium equations with a nonzero right hand side. The authors show that the solution
(\rho_{m},p_{m})
of the model (
1
) converges to an incompressible limit as
m\rightarrow\infty
, which solves a Hele-Shaw free-boundary problem. Precisely, the limit
(\rho_{\infty},p_{\infty})
satisfies the following
Hele-Shaw graph
:

(4)

p_{\infty}\in\begin{cases}0,\quad 0\leq\rho_{\infty}<1,\\
[0,\infty),\quad\rho_{\infty}=1,\end{cases}

and the following
complementarity formula
:

p_{\infty}(\Delta p_{\infty}+\Phi(p_{\infty})),\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),

in the sense that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\bigl(-|\nabla p_{\infty}|^{2}+p_{\infty}\Phi(p_{\infty})\bigr)-p\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla p\;dx=0,

for any test function
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
This gives rise to a free-boundary problem, where the region

\Omega(t):=\{x:p_{\infty}(t,x)>0\}

can be thought of as the “tumor”. The authors show that the normal velocity
V
at the boundary of the tumor
\Omega(t)
is given by

V=|\nabla p_{\infty}|,

following Darcy’s law. Further, the uniqueness of the limit
(\rho_{\infty},p_{\infty})
is shown by Hilbert’s uniqueness method
[
4
,
8
]
. Since then, the incompressible limit of porous media-type tumor growth models has been studied heavily, e.g.,
[
10
,
24
,
20
,
18
]
.

This work has been extended to cross-diffusion equations, i.e., systems that consider multiple cell populations. These systems can describe various interactions such as cancer cells with healthy cells, normal cells with self-destroying (autophagic) cells, or cells with varying phenotypes
[
5
,
11
,
23
,
27
]
.

In the more recent work of David,
[
11
]
, the author introduces a tumor growth model structured by
phenotypic traits
. The structured model is a cross-diffusion model describing an infinite number of interacting cell populations. The density,
n(t,y,x)
, of a cell with phenotype
y\in[0,1]
, is described by the following system:

(5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}n_{m}(t,y,x)-\nabla(n_{m}(t,y,x)\nabla p_{m}(t,x))=n_{m}(t,y,x)R(y,p_{m}(t,x)),\qquad(t,y,x)\in(0,T]\times[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\rho_{m}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}n_{m}(t,y,x)dy,\\
p_{m}(t,x)=\rho_{m}(t,x)^{m}.\end{cases}

This model is structured by a phenotypic trait which is assigned to each cell at birth and does not change over time. David shows that, under appropriate assumptions on
R
and the initial data, as
m\rightarrow\infty
, the solution
(n_{m},\rho_{m},p_{m})
of the model (
5
) approaches an incompressible limit
(n_{\infty},\rho_{\infty},p_{\infty})
, which solves a Hele-Shaw free-boundary problem. In particular,
(\rho_{\infty},p_{\infty})
satisfy the Hele-Shaw graph (
4
), and the pair
(n_{\infty},p_{\infty})
satisfies the following complementarity formula:

p_{\infty}\Bigl(\Delta p_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{1}n_{\infty}R(y,p_{\infty})\;dy\Bigr)=0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The structured model, (
5
), is a strong motivation for our current work. In this paper, we follow the outline of
[
11
]
to establish an incompressible limit of (
2
). The main point where our proof diverges from that of
[
11
]
is in establishing the strong convergence of
\nabla p_{m}
. In the age-structured case, the addition of the transport term in
\theta
means that
\theta
cannot be treated as a parameter, as
y
could. Another difference is that
\theta\in[0,\infty),
an unbounded domain, rather than
y\in[0,1].
These differences cause a challenge in proving strong convergence. The key to overcoming this obstacle is to show that
n_{m}
is compactly supported in
x
and
\theta
, uniformly in
m
. Additionally, this work extends
[
11
]
by incorporating an age-dependent volume, meaning
\rho
depends not only on the number of cells (
\rho=\int_{0}^{1}n\,dy
in (
5
)), but instead on the volume they take up (
\rho=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(\theta)n\,d\theta
in (
2
)).

Main Contribution.
In this paper, we follow the outline of
[
11
]
and
[
26
]
to show convergence of solutions to (
2
) to an incompressible limit, which satisfies Hele-Shaw free boundary problem and a complementarity formula.

1.3.
Definitions and notation

We will define the equation describing the evolution of the density,
\rho.

Multiplying (
2
) by
V(\theta)
and integrating in
\theta
yields the following equation for
\rho
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho-\text{div}(\rho\nabla p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}n\Bigl(r(p)[V^{\prime}(\theta)+(2V(0)-V(\theta))\nu(\theta,p)]-\mu(\theta)V(\theta)\Bigr)\;d\theta\qquad&t\in[0,T],\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(\theta)n_{0}(\theta,x)\,d\theta\qquad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

For simplicity of notation, we define

(6)

F(\theta,p):=r(p)[V^{\prime}(\theta)+(2V(0)-V(\theta))\nu(\theta,p)]-\mu(\theta)V(\theta),

so the equation for
\rho
becomes

(7)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho-\text{div}(\rho\nabla p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}n(t,\theta,x)F(\theta,p)\;d\theta\qquad&t\in[0,T],\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(\theta)n_{0}(\theta,x)\,d\theta\qquad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

From the assumptions on
r
and
\nu
, the function
F(\cdot,p)\leq 0
for
p\geq p_{M}
. We highlight two particular cases of
F(\theta,p)
, which have different biological meanings and result in very different dynamics.

Case 1: Consider cells that have constant volume, that is,
V(\theta)=V_{0}
for all
\theta\geq 0,
Then,

F(\theta,p)=V_{0}r(p)\nu(\theta,p)-\mu(\theta)V_{0},

and it is apparent that tumor growth is driven primarily by cell division. If we assume further that
\nu(\theta,p)=\nu(p),
and
\mu(\theta)=\mu_{0}
, the equation on
\rho
exactly recovers the original porous media tumor growth model, (
1
), with
\Phi(p)=r(p)\nu(p)V_{0}-\mu_{0}V_{0}.

Case 2: Consider cells with age-dependent volume, and assume that mitosis is volume-preserving, that is, the total size of two daughter cells is equal to the size of the parent cell. This can be done by imposing that

\nu(\theta,p)(2V(0)-V(\theta))=0,

for all
\theta\geq 0.
In this case,

F(\theta,p)=r(p)V^{\prime}(\theta)-\mu(\theta)V(\theta).

It can be seen that tumor growth is now driven by the increase in volume of individual cells prior to cell division.

Outline of the paper

2.
Assumptions and Main Results

Given
m>2
, we consider
n_{m}
, the weak solution of the following boundary value problem (see Theorem
3.1
for a precise definition of weak solution).

(8)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}n_{m}+r(p_{m})\partial_{\theta}n_{m}-\text{div}(n_{m}\nabla p_{m})=-r(p_{m})\nu(\theta,p_{m})n_{m}-\mu(\theta)n_{m}\qquad&t\in[0,T],\;\theta\geq 0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
n_{m}(t,0,x)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu(\theta,p_{m})n_{m}(t,\theta,x)\,d\theta\qquad&t\in[0,T],\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
n_{m}(0,\theta,x)=n_{0,m}(\theta,x)\qquad&\theta\geq 0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

where
p_{m}(t,x)=\frac{m}{m-1}\rho_{m}(t,x)^{m-1}.

We make the following assumptions on the various parameters of the problem:

(9)

0\leq\mu(\theta)\leq\mu_{max},\quad|\mu^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq C,

(10)

0\leq r(p)\leq r_{max},\quad r^{\prime}(p)\leq 0,\quad r(p_{M})=0,

(11)

\nu\in C^{1}([0,\infty)\times[0,p_{M}]),\text{ }0\leq\nu(\theta,p)\leq C,\text{ }\partial_{p}\nu(\theta,p)\leq 0,\text{ }|\partial_{\theta}\nu(\theta,p)|\leq C,\text{ }\nu(\theta,p_{M})=0,

and

(12)

V\in C^{2}([0,\infty)),\quad 0\leq V^{\prime}(\theta)\leq C,\quad|V^{\prime\prime}|\leq C,\quad\text{and}\quad V_{0}:=V(0)\leq V(\theta)\leq 2V_{0},

for constants
\mu_{max},r_{max},p_{M},V_{0}>0,
and various constants all called
C>0
.
Multiplying (
8
) by
V(\theta)
and integrating in
\theta
yields that
\rho_{m}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(\theta)n_{m}(t,\theta,x)\,d\theta
solves

(13)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{m}-\text{div}(\rho_{m}\nabla p_{m})=\int_{0}^{\infty}n_{m}F(\theta,p_{m})\;d\theta\qquad&t\in[0,T],\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\rho_{m}(0,x)=\rho_{0,m}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(\theta)n_{0,m}(\theta,x)\,d\theta\qquad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

where
F
is defined by (
6
).
We make the following assumptions on the initial data. Assume that

(14)

n_{0,m}\in L^{1}\cap L\log L((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad(|x|^{2}+\theta)n_{0,m}\in L^{1}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad 0\leq\rho_{0,m}\leq(\frac{m-1}{m}p_{M})^{\frac{1}{m-1}},

where the bounds are independent of
m
.
Assume there exists
n_{0}\in L^{1}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(15)

||n_{0,m}-n_{0}||_{L^{1}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\xrightarrow[m\rightarrow\infty]{}0.

In this paper, we show that, up to a subsequence, as
m\rightarrow\infty
,
n_{m}
,
\rho_{m}
,
p_{m}
converge to limits
n_{\infty}
,
\rho_{\infty}
, and
p_{\infty}
, solving the following equation:

(16)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}n_{\infty}+r(p_{\infty})\partial_{\theta}n_{\infty}-\text{div}(n_{\infty}\nabla p_{\infty})=-r(p_{\infty})\nu(\theta,p_{\infty})n_{\infty}-\mu(\theta)n_{\infty}\qquad\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T]\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
n_{\infty}(t,0,x)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu(\theta,p_{\infty})n_{\infty}(t,\theta,x)\,d\theta,\\
n_{\infty}(0,\theta,x)=n_{0}(\theta,x),\end{cases}

where

(17)

\rho_{\infty}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(\theta)n_{\infty}(t,\theta,x)d\theta,

and
\rho_{\infty},p_{\infty}
satisfy the Hele-Shaw condition,

(18)

p_{\infty}\in\begin{cases}0,\qquad&0\leq\rho_{\infty}<1,\\
[0,\infty),\qquad&\rho_{\infty}=1.\end{cases}

Further,
\rho_{\infty}
solves

(19)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{\infty}-\Delta p_{\infty}=\int_{0}^{\infty}n_{\infty}F(\theta,p_{\infty}))\,d\theta\qquad&\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\
\rho_{\infty}(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(\theta)n_{0}(\theta,x)\,d\theta.\end{cases}

We now state the two main convergence results.

Theorem 2.1

(Weak Free-Boundary Limit)
.

Let
(n_{m},p_{m},\rho_{m})
be a weak solution of (
8
), under the assumptions (
9
), (
10
), (
11
), and (
12
). Assume that the initial data satisfies (
14
)-(
15
). Further, assume that
n_{0,m}
is compactly supported in
x
and
\theta
, uniformly in
m
. Then, up to a subsequence, as
m\rightarrow\infty
,

converges strongly in

,
\rho_{m}
converges weakly in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
n_{m}
converges weakly-* in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};L^{1}([0,\infty))))
, for all

, to limits

)-(

).

Remark 2.2
.

In Theorem

,

converges weakly-* in

. This means that for any
\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};L^{\infty}([0,\infty))),

for some

Theorem 2.3

(Complementarity Formula)
.

The limits
p_{\infty}
,
n_{\infty}
satisfy the relation

(20)

p_{\infty}\Bigl(\Delta p_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{\infty}n_{\infty}\bigl(F(\theta,p_{\infty})-\mu(\theta)V(\theta)\bigr)\,d\theta\Bigr)=0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

These results will be proved in Section

using several lemmas that establish the convergence of

and

.

3.
Existence of solutions to the model

In this section, we recall the existence of solutions to the model (
2
), as proved in our previous work
[
21
]
. A slightly simplified model was considered:

(21)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}n+\partial_{\theta}n-\text{div}(n\nabla p)=-\nu(\theta,p)n\qquad&t\geq 0,\;\theta\in(0,\infty),\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
n(t,0,x)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu(\theta,p)n(t,\theta,x)d\theta\qquad&t\geq 0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
n(0,\theta,x)=n_{0}(\theta,x)\qquad&\theta\geq 0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

with
\rho(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V(\theta)n(t,\theta,x)d\theta,
and the same assumptions on
V
and
\nu
as stated in Section
1
. It is remarked in
[
21
]
that extending the existence results for (
21
) to the full model (
2
) is not difficult. We state below the main existence result:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
21
]
, Theorem 2.1)
.

For all
m>2
and for any initial condition
n_{0}(\theta,x)
such that

(22)

n_{0}\in L^{1}\cap L\log L((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad(|x|^{2}+\theta)n_{0}\in L^{1}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\rho_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

there exists a weak solution
n(t,\theta,x)
of (
21
).
The function
n(t,\theta,x)
is such that

n\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L\log L((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})),\quad(|x|^{2}+\theta)n\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})),

while the density
\rho(t,x)
and pressure
p(t,x)=\frac{m}{m-1}\rho(t,x)^{m-1}
are such that

\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\qquad\nabla p\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

and the equation (
21
) is satisfied in the following distributional sense:

(23)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}n(t,\theta,x)\big[-\partial_{t}\psi(t,\theta,x)-\partial_{\theta}\psi(t,\theta,x)+\nabla_{x}p(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi(t,\theta,x)+\nu(\theta,p(t,x))\psi(t,\theta,x)\big]\,d\theta\,dx\,dt\\
&\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}n_{0}(\theta,x)\psi(0,\theta,x)\,d\theta\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(t,0,x)2\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu(\theta,p(t,x))n(t,\theta,x)\,d\theta\,dx\,dt,\end{split}

for all
\psi\in\mathcal{D}([0,T)\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, the density
\rho(x,t)
solves

(24)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\rho-\mathrm{div}\,(\rho\nabla p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}V^{\prime}(\theta)n(t,\theta,x)\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu(\theta,p)(2V(0)-V(\theta))n(t,\theta,x)\,d\theta.\end{split}

In this paper, we use the same assumptions as stated in Theorem
3.1
, so as to ensure existence.

As noted in
[
21
]
, the uniqueness for cross-diffusion models such as (
2
) is
a difficult and largely open problem, and was not addressed in our previous work nor this current work.

In
[
21
]
, numerical approximations were performed to examine the behaviour of solutions to (
21
), especially to keep track of where in the tumor most of the cell proliferation takes place. Numerical simulations showed that most proliferation occured in an annulus near the outer edge of the tumor, and very little occurred on the inside of the tumor. It was observed that the inner part of the tumor was mostly very old cells, which can be interpreted as a “necrotic core”. These results are consistent with in-vitro experiments on cancer cells, which exhibit a necrotic core and a proliferating rim
[
6
,
28
,
25
,
22
]
.

These numerical findings highlight the importance of including the spatial dependence in age-structure models. Considering the spatial structure of tumors, that is, where in the tumor most
proliferation is occurring, is important when designing mathematical models because this knowledge can assist
with clinical forecasting and treatment development.

Lemma 4.2

Lemma 4.6

Remark 4.8

The limit functions

the weak-* convergences of

Remark that (

Using the convergences listed above, we are able to show that the left-hand side of equation (

Lemma 4.10

(
[

, Corollary 4.5)

The limit functions satisfy

for some

Proof.

The bounds on

and

follow from the

and
p_{m}
.

To show that

almost everywhere, consider

From Lemma
4.3
, we have that
\rho_{m},p_{m}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with weak

limits
\rho_{\infty},p_{\infty}

By the compensated compactness theorem (

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v_{m}\varphi\,dxdt=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}p_{m}\varphi\,dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\infty}p_{\infty}\varphi\,dxdt,

At the same time, because

Lemma 4.11

(
[
11
]
, Lemma 4.6)

Up to a subsequence, as

\nabla v_{m}\rightarrow\nabla v_{\infty}

Proof.

To show the strong convergence of
\nabla v_{m},

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla(v_{m}-v_{\infty})|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla v_{m}\cdot\nabla(v_{m}-v_{\infty})-\nabla v_{\infty}\cdot\nabla(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla v_{m}\cdot\nabla(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\,dxdt,

because
\nabla(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Testing (
29
) against

,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\rho_{m}(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla v_{m}\cdot\nabla(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\int_{0}^{\infty}n_{m}F(\theta,p_{m})\,d\theta dx.

Rearranging,

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla v_{m}\cdot\nabla(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\theta,p_{m})n_{m}\,d\theta dx

\displaystyle+\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}(-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\rho_{m}(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\,dxdt).

Notice that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{m}v_{m}=\frac{1}{m+1}\partial_{t}(\rho_{m}^{m+1}),

so

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}(-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\rho_{m}v_{m}\,dxdt)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}(-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{m+1}\rho_{m}^{m+1}\,dxdt)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{m}^{m+1}(0)-\rho_{m}^{m+1}(T)\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\rho_{m}v_{\infty}\,dxdt

(32)

\displaystyle+\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\theta,p_{\infty})n_{m}\,d\theta dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\rho_{\infty}v_{\infty}\,dxdt,

using the fact that of

Proof that (
31
) equals zero.
We have:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))n_{m}(v_{m}-v_{\infty})\,d\theta dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))n_{m}(p_{m}(\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1)+p_{m}-p_{\infty})\,d\theta dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))n_{m}(p_{m}(\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1))\,d\theta dxdt.

In the above calculation, the first line comes from the definition of
v_{m}
, and the fact that
p_{\infty}=v_{\infty}
. The second line comes from the fact that
F(\theta,p)
is nonincreasing in

so
(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))(p_{m}-p_{\infty})\leq 0.

Then, for a fixed
\varepsilon>0,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))n_{m}(p_{m}(\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1))\,d\theta dxdt

\displaystyle=\iint_{\rho_{m}\leq 1-\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))n_{m}\,d\theta(\rho_{m}^{m-1}(\rho_{m}-\frac{m}{m-1}))\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\iint_{\rho_{m}>1-\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))n_{m}\,d\theta(p_{m}(\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1))\,dxdt.

For the first term,

\displaystyle\iint_{\rho_{m}\leq 1-\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))n_{m}\,d\theta(\rho_{m}^{m-1}(\rho_{m}-\frac{m}{m-1}))\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\rho_{M}(1-\varepsilon)^{m-1}\iint_{\rho_{m}\leq 1-\varepsilon}\rho_{m}+\frac{m}{m-1}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C(T)(1-\varepsilon)^{(m-1)},

using the bound on

in the first inequality, and the

\rho_{m}
, and the compact support of
\rho_{m}
in the second inequality.
Also,

\displaystyle\iint_{\rho_{m}>1-\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))n_{m}\,d\theta(p_{m}(\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1))\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\sup_{\rho_{m}>1-\varepsilon}|\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1|,

bound on

.
To bound

\rho_{m}\leq e^{\frac{1}{m-1}\ln(p_{M})}\leq 1+\frac{1}{m-1}\ln(p_{M})+o(\frac{1}{m-1})
and so
\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1\leq o(\frac{1}{m-1}).

Also, when

\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1>-\varepsilon-\frac{1}{m}\rho_{M}.
Combining these bounds, for
\rho_{m}>1-\varepsilon,
it holds that

|\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1|\leq\max(\frac{1}{m}\rho_{M}+\varepsilon,o(\frac{1}{m-1})).

Taking

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(F(\theta,p_{m})-F(\theta,p_{\infty}))n_{m}(p_{m}(\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}-1))\,d\theta dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}C(T)(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{m-1}})^{m-1}+C(T)\max(\frac{1}{m}\rho_{M}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m-1}},o(\frac{1}{m-1}))=0.

Proof that (
32
) equals zero.
This will be done using an extended version of the proof of the Compensated Compactness Theorem found in Appendix A of
[

]
, and is the main difference between this Lemma and Lemma 4.6 in

For simplicity of notation, define

Recall that

weakly in that space. In what follows, we will use mollification to obtain a strong convergence, and show that the product

converges.
We have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(v_{m}-v_{\infty})F(\theta,p_{\infty})n_{m}d\theta dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{m}n_{m}d\theta dxdt.

Take

\psi

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{m}n_{m}d\theta dxdt

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}n_{m}(w_{m}-w_{m}*_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon})d\theta dxdt}_{\mathcal{A}_{m}}+

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(n_{m}-n_{m}*_{t}\zeta_{\sigma})(w_{m}*_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon})d\theta dxdt}_{\mathcal{B}_{m}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(n_{m}*_{t}\zeta_{\sigma})(w_{m}*_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon})d\theta dxdt}_{\mathcal{C}_{m}}.

We will show that

\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{m}n_{m}d\theta dxdt|\leq C(\varepsilon,\sigma),

where
\lim_{\varepsilon,\sigma\rightarrow 0}C(\varepsilon,\sigma)=0,

proving that (
32
) is zero.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{m}|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(n_{m}*_{t}\zeta_{\sigma})(w_{m}*_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon})d\theta dxdt|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}n_{m}w_{m}*_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon}*_{t}\zeta_{\sigma}d\theta dxdt|

\displaystyle\leq||w_{m}*_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon}*_{t}\zeta_{\sigma}||_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}||n_{m}||_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};L^{1}([0,\infty)))}.

4.6

Using the fact that

uniformly in

, it follows that

\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}|\mathcal{C}_{m}|=0.

Next, consider the term

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{m}|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}n_{m}(w_{m}-w_{m}*_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon})d\theta dxdt|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w_{m}(t,\theta,x)-w_{m}(t,\theta,x-\varepsilon y))\psi(y)dy\Bigr)n_{m}(t,\theta,x)d\theta dxdt|

using the fact that

and

for a constant

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{m}|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(n_{m}-n_{m}*_{t}\zeta_{\sigma})(w_{m}*_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon})d\theta dxdt|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\infty}(n_{m}(t)-n_{m}(t-\sigma\tau))\zeta(\tau)d\tau\Bigr)(w_{m}*_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon})d\theta dxdt|

using that

Proof that (
33
) equals zero.

Formally, this should hold because

\partial_{t}\rho_{\infty}=0.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\rho_{\infty}(t+\varepsilon,x)-\rho_{\infty}(t,x))v_{\infty}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\rho_{\infty}(t+\varepsilon,x)-1)v_{\infty}+(1-\rho_{\infty}(t,x))v_{\infty}\,dxdt\leq 0,

. Similarly,
and

\displaystyle 0

Lemma 4.13
.

Up to a subsequence,
\nabla p_{m}\rightarrow\nabla p_{\infty}
strongly in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

Assume

. First,

Because
\nabla v_{m}\rightarrow\nabla v_{\infty}
strongly in

it suffices to show that

Once this is shown, it will hold that
||\nabla p_{m}||_{L^{2}}\rightarrow||\nabla p_{\infty}||_{L^{2}}.
To see this, notice that
\frac{m}{m-2}\rho_{m}^{m-2}=\frac{m}{m-2}v_{m}^{\frac{m-2}{m}}\rightarrow p_{\infty}
, because
v_{m}
converges strongly to
v_{\infty}=p_{\infty}.
Since it already is known that
\nabla p_{m}\rightharpoonup\nabla p_{\infty}
weakly in

the weak convergence combined with the convergence in norm implies that
\nabla p_{m}\rightarrow\nabla p_{\infty}

To show the desired

|\frac{m}{m-2}\nabla\rho_{m}^{m-2}|^{2}=m^{2}\rho_{m}^{2m-6}|\nabla\rho_{m}|^{2}.

Then, using (

) with

Multiplying by

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{m}{m-2}\rho_{m}^{m-3}dx+\frac{m-3}{m-2}(m-4)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla(\frac{m}{m-2}\rho_{m}^{m-2})|^{2}\,dx\leq(m-3)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{m}{m-2}\rho_{m}^{m-3}\,dx.

Integrating in time,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla(\frac{m}{m-2}\rho_{m}^{m-2})|^{2}dxdt\leq\frac{m}{m-4}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{m}^{m-3}dxdt+\frac{m}{(m-3)(m-4)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{m}^{m-3}(0,x)\,dx.

Then, since

, and the compact support of solutions is uniform in
m
, the right hand side is bounded independent of
m
.
∎

Lemma 4.14
