Datopotamab deruxtecan versus chemotherapy in previously treated inoperable/metastatic hormone receptor-positive HER2-negative breast cancer: safety and patient-reported outcomes from the phase III TROPION-Breast01 study
Authors: HS Rugo, K Jhaveri, S Pernas, B Pistilli, S-A Im, M De Laurentiis, S Wang, N Martínez Jañez, G Borges, DW Cescon, M Hattori, Y-S Lu, E Hamilton, Q Zhang, J Tsurutani, K Kalinsky, PE Rubini Liedke, Z Nowecki, SM Tolaney, K Zhao, K Atuah, D Verma, B Xu, A Bardia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn TROPION-Breast01 (NCT05104866), datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) improved progression-free survival by blinded independent central review versus investigator’s choice of chemotherapy (ICC) in patients with inoperable/metastatic hormone receptor-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2—) breast cancer, who had disease progression on endocrine therapy and for whom endocrine therapy was unsuitable, and who had received 1-2 prior lines of chemotherapy in the inoperable/metastatic setting. We report detailed safety data and patient-reported outcomes (PROs) from the final analysis.MethodsPatients received Dato-DXd (6 mg/kg every 3 weeks) or ICC (eribulin/capecitabine/vinorelbine/gemcitabine). Mouthwash was advised (steroid mouthwash strongly recommended for Dato-DXd), and ophthalmologic assessments were carried out per protocol. Safety and time to deterioration (TTD) in global health status/quality of life (GHS/QoL), pain, and physical functioning, using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer QoL Questionnaire-Core 30, were secondary endpoints; exploratory PROs were also assessed.ResultsWith Dato-DXd (n = 360), nausea was the most common treatment-related adverse event (AE; 51.9%, grade ≥3: 1.4%). The AEs of special interest (grouped terms), treatment-related oral mucositis/stomatitis and ocular surface events, occurred in 57.2% (grade ≥3: 7.2%) and 43.9% (grade ≥3: 1.9%) of patients, respectively. Per protocol recommendations, most patients receiving Dato-DXd received anti-emetics (76.7%), prophylactic mouthwash (77.2%), or artificial tears (71.7%). Adjudicated drug-related interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis with Dato-DXd was mostly low-grade (3.9%; grade ≥3: 0.8%; one grade 5 event). Hematologic toxicity was the most notable feature of the ICC safety profile (n = 351). TTD in GHS/QoL, pain, and physical functioning was delayed with Dato-DXd versus ICC. Patient-reported symptomatic AEs were generally consistent with the clinician-reported AE profile in both arms.ConclusionSafety and PRO data from TROPION-Breast01 complement the improvements in efficacy demonstrated with Dato-DXd versus ICC, supporting Dato-DXd as a treatment option for patients with previously treated, inoperable/metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer.Key words: Dato-DXd, datopotamab deruxtecan, HR+/HER2– breast cancer, patient-reported outcomes, safety, TROPION-Breast01

Introduction

Datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) is a trophoblast cell-surface antigen 2 (TROP2)-directed antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) approved for the treatment of patients with unresectable or metastatic hormone receptor positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 negative (HR+/HER2—) breast cancer who have received prior endocrine-based therapy and chemotherapy for unresectable or metastatic disease.1, 2, 3 Approval was based on data from the phase III TROPION-Breast01 study, which assessed Dato-DXd compared with investigator’s choice of chemotherapy (ICC; eribulin, capecitabine, vinorelbine, or gemcitabine) in patients with inoperable or metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer, who had disease progression on endocrine therapy and for whom endocrine therapy was unsuitable, and who had received one or two previous lines of chemotherapy in the inoperable/metastatic setting.4,5 The dual primary end point of progression-free survival (PFS) by blinded independent central review (BICR) was met in TROPION-Breast01 [primary PFS data cut-off (DCO): 17 July 2023]; Dato-DXd demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement versus ICC, with a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.63 [95% confidence interval (CI) 0.52-0.76, P < 0.0001].4 At the prespecified final overall survival (OS) analysis (DCO: 24 July 2024), no statistically significant difference in the dual primary end point of OS was observed (HR 1.01, 95% CI 0.83-1.22), but an imbalance between arms in subsequent ADC treatment may have affected OS results.5

Dato-DXd demonstrated a manageable safety profile compared with ICC in TROPION-Breast01; of note, the rate of grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) in the Dato-DXd arm was less than half that in the ICC arm, and TRAEs led to fewer dose reductions and interruptions in patients who received Dato-DXd compared with those who received ICC.4,5 Furthermore, no new safety signals were identified with longer follow-up at the final OS DCO.5 Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) were secondary and exploratory endpoints in TROPION-Breast01. Initial PRO data from the primary PFS DCO complemented the efficacy and safety findings; notably, the secondary PRO endpoints of time to deterioration (TTD) in global health status/quality of life (GHS/QoL), pain, and physical functioning were delayed in the Dato-DXd arm compared with the ICC arm.6

Here, we report detailed safety data, including analyses of incidence, management, and outcomes of clinically relevant adverse events (AEs), as well as PRO data from the final OS analysis DCO of TROPION-Breast01.

Methods

Study design

Full details of the TROPION-Breast01 study design and methodology have been published previously.4,7 The study was carried out in accordance with the ethical principles set out in the Declaration of Helsinki and consistent with the International Conference on Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines and other applicable regulatory requirements. The protocol was approved by institutional review boards at each participating site, and all patients provided written informed consent.

Safety endpoints and assessments

Assessment of the safety and tolerability profile of Dato-DXd relative to ICC involved determination of type, incidence, severity, seriousness, and possible causal relation to study medication (as assessed by investigators) for AEs. Safety was evaluated from screening until completion of the follow-up period (35 days after the last dose of study drug, or until the start of any subsequent anti-cancer therapy), with the exception of protocol-prespecified AEs of special interest (AESIs), which were followed until resolution or the end of the study. AEs were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) and graded for severity by the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) version 5.0. Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were defined as any AE with onset or worsening that occurred on or after the first dose of study drug and within 35 days after the last dose of study drug or before the start of any subsequent anti-cancer therapy. TRAEs were those TEAEs where the possible AE causal relation to study medication was assessed as ‘yes’ by the reporting investigator. The following AESIs (grouped terms) for Dato-DXd were assessed in TROPION-Breast01: oral mucositis/stomatitis, mucosal inflammation other than oral mucositis/stomatitis, ocular surface events, infusion-related reactions, and interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis. An independent adjudication committee reviewed all cases of potential ILD/pneumonitis to confirm the diagnosis and determine whether events were related to the study drug. Methods for assessment of exposure-adjusted incidence rates (EAIRs) of AEs and details of prevention and management of AEs are provided in the Supplementary Methods, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330.

PRO endpoints and assessments

Secondary PRO endpoints were TTD in GHS/QoL, pain, and physical functioning, measured using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer 30-item core quality-of-life questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30). TTD and mean change from baseline in other patient-reported symptoms (fatigue, nausea/vomiting, appetite loss, dyspnea, insomnia, constipation, and diarrhea) and functioning (role, cognitive, emotional, and social) measured using the EORTC QLQ-C30 and the arm and breast symptoms scales from the EORTC 45-item quality-of-life questionnaire for breast cancer symptoms item list 116 (EORTC QLQ-BR45/IL116) were exploratory endpoints. Additional exploratory PRO endpoints included patient-reported symptomatic AEs, measured using the Patient-Reported Outcomes Version of the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (PRO-CTCAE) and EORTC IL117 (IL117), patient-reported treatment tolerability measured using the Patient’s Global Impression of Treatment Tolerability, and patient-reported health status measured using the EuroQoL 5-dimension 5-level health state utility index visual analog scale. Details of the frequency of PRO questionnaire completion are provided in the Supplementary Methods, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330.

Statistical analysis

Safety was analyzed in the safety analysis set, comprising all patients who had received at least one dose of study drug. All AEs were summarized descriptively by count (n) and percentage for each treatment group.

For PROs, scores for the EORTC QLQ-C30 were calculated according to the published scoring manual.8 A linear transformation was used to standardize raw scores and obtain final scores ranging from 0 to 100. Higher scores on the GHS/QoL and functioning scales indicate better health status and functioning, while higher scores for symptom scales indicate greater symptom severity. Scores for the EORTC IL116 were calculated in the same way as for the EORTC QLQ-C30. Meaningful change thresholds (MCTs) were derived for selected scales; clinically meaningful changes from baseline were defined by thresholds of a 16.6-point change for GHS/QoL and pain and a 13.3-point change for physical functioning.9 TTD was assessed in the intent-to-treat (ITT) population (comprising all randomly assigned patients) and defined in two ways as prespecified in the protocol: time to first deterioration, defined as the time from the date of randomization to the date of the first deterioration based on derived MCTs, was the primary analysis; time to confirmed deterioration, which required deterioration to be confirmed at a subsequent timepoint, was a sensitivity analysis. Further details of statistical methods for PROs, including for exploratory PRO endpoints, are provided in the Supplementary Methods, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330.

Results

Patients

In TROPION-Breast01, 732 patients were randomly assigned: the ITT population comprised 365 patients allocated to the Dato-DXd arm and 367 to the ICC arm; 5 and 16 patients, respectively, did not receive any treatment and the safety population therefore comprised 360 patients who received Dato-DXd and 351 who received ICC [207 (59.0%) eribulin, 75 (21.4%) capecitabine, 38 (10.8%) vinorelbine, and 31 (8.8%) gemcitabine]. Patient baseline demographics and clinical characteristics were reported previously.4

Safety

Overall summary

Safety data at the primary PFS and final OS DCOs were summarized previously.4,5 In brief, at the final analysis DCO, TRAEs were reported in 94.7% and 86.3% of patients in the Dato-DXd (n = 360) and ICC (n = 351) safety populations, respectively.5 The rate of grade ≥3 TRAEs with Dato-DXd was less than half that with ICC (22.2% versus 45.6%).5 In the Dato-DXd versus ICC arms, TRAEs led to dose interruptions in 15.8% versus 24.2% of patients, dose reductions in 24.2% versus 30.2%, and treatment discontinuation in 3.3% versus 2.6%.5 Data reported here are from the final OS DCO, at which time the median duration of treatment was 6.8 months (range 0.7-27.9) in the Dato-DXd arm compared with 4.1 months (range 0.2-27.9) in the ICC arm.

Most common TRAEs

The most common TRAEs reported in TROPION-Breast01 are detailed in Table 1. Nausea, stomatitis, and alopecia were the most frequent TRAEs in the Dato-DXd arm (51.9%, 51.4%, and 36.4% of patients, respectively, compared with 23.6%, 13.1%, and 21.4% in the ICC arm). The frequency of grade ≥3 treatment-related nausea was low (1.4% with Dato-DXd versus 0.6% with ICC), and treatment-related nausea led to dose reduction and/or interruption in 2.5% of patients in the Dato-DXd arm versus 1.1% in the ICC arm, while no patients discontinued treatment due to treatment-related nausea in either arm. In the Dato-DXd versus ICC arm, treatment-related stomatitis was grade ≥3 in 6.4% versus 2.6% of patients, led to dose reduction and/or interruption in 14.4% versus 2.3%, and led to discontinuation in 0.3% (n = 1) versus 0%. Treatment-related alopecia (for which the maximum CTCAE grade is grade 2) was grade 1/2 in 21.1%/15.3% of patients in the Dato-DXd arm and grade 1/2 in 10.8%/10.5% of patients in the ICC arm.

MedDRA preferred terms of nausea and vomiting were analyzed as a grouped term to provide additional details on these TRAEs: treatment-related nausea/vomiting was reported in 200 patients (55.6%) in the Dato-DXd arm (grade 1: 35.3%; grade 2: 17.8%; grade 3: 2.5%) and 87 patients (24.8%) in the ICC arm (grade 1: 17.1%; grade 2: 6.8%; grade 3: 0.9%). Among the 200 patients who experienced treatment-related nausea/vomiting in the Dato-DXd arm, onset of the first event was in cycle 1 in 139 patients (69.5%), cycle 2 in 27 patients (13.5%), cycle 3 in 13 patients (6.5%), and cycle 4 onward in 21 patients (10.5%) (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330). Among the 87 patients with nausea/vomiting in the ICC arm, the first event was in cycle 1 in 58 patients (66.7%), cycle 2 in 11 patients (12.6%), cycle 3 in 9 patients (10.3%), and cycle 4 onward in 9 patients (10.3%). Per protocol, prophylactic anti-emetic agents were highly recommended before infusion of Dato-DXd and on subsequent days as needed. Prophylactic anti-emetics were received by 76.7% of patients in the Dato-DXd arm and 49.6% in the ICC arm; two- and three-drug combinations were received by 42.2% and 10.6% of patients, respectively, in the Dato-DXd arm, and by 20.5% and 1.1% of patients, respectively, in the ICC arm. Glucocorticoids were received by 56.1% of patients in the Dato-DXd arm and 31.1% in the ICC arm.

Hematologic toxicity was the most notable feature of the safety profile in the ICC arm (Table 1). Neutropenia (grouped term comprising neutropenia and decreased neutrophil count) was the most common TRAE in the ICC arm, occurring in 43.3% of patients; it was grade ≥3 in 31.1% of patients, led to dose reduction and/or interruption in 30.5% of patients, and led to discontinuation in one patient (0.3%). Treatment-related anemia occurred in 19.9% of patients (grade ≥3 in 2.6%) and treatment-related leukopenia (grouped term) was reported in 17.4% of patients (grade ≥3 in 7.4%) in the ICC arm. Any grade treatment-related anemia, neutropenia, and leukopenia were reported in 12.2%, 11.7%, and 7.5% of patients, respectively, in the Dato-DXd arm. For neutropenia prevention and management, colony-stimulating factors were used during treatment in 83 patients (22.6%) in the ICC arm compared with 10 patients (2.7%) in the Dato-DXd arm.

Other notable TRAEs in the ICC arm included diarrhea, neuropathy (grouped term), and palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia syndrome (Table 1). Diarrhea was more frequent in the ICC arm compared with the Dato-DXd arm (12.5% versus 8.3%) and was associated with a higher rate of dose reduction and/or interruption (2.6% versus 0.3%). Neuropathy occurred in 14.2% of patients in the ICC arm (versus 4.4% in the Dato-DXd arm) and led to dose reduction in 2.8% of patients and discontinuation in 0.9%. Palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia syndrome was reported in 12.3% of patients in the ICC arm (versus 2.2% in the Dato-DXd arm) and led to dose reduction and/or interruption in 4.0% of patients.

AEs of special interest for Dato-DXd

Oral mucositis/stomatitis was the most common treatment-related AESI for Dato-DXd, occurring in 57.2% of patients (25.8% grade 1, 24.2% grade 2, 7.2% grade 3; Table 2), and leading to dose reduction in 13.9% of patients, dose interruption in 2.2%, and discontinuation in 0.3% (1 patient; Table 3). Median time to onset of treatment-related oral mucositis/stomatitis was 22 days; events were resolved/resolving in 178/206 patients at DCO, and median time to resolution (TTR) was 68.5 days (Table 3). The study protocol recommended the use of mouthwash for oral mucositis/stomatitis prophylaxis as part of an oral care plan in both treatment arms. In the Dato-DXd arm, 77.2% of patients received any prophylactic mouthwash as concomitant medication at any time during the treatment exposure period; 38.6% received steroid mouthwash, and 63.6% received nonsteroid mouthwash (patients could have received both steroid and nonsteroid mouthwash).

Treatment-related ocular surface events were reported in 43.9% of patients in the Dato-DXd arm (32.2% grade 1, 9.7% grade 2, 1.9% grade 3). The most commonly reported ocular surface events were dry eye (23.9% of patients), keratitis (grouped term; 15.8%), increased lacrimation (6.9%), and meibomian gland dysfunction (6.1%); grade 3 ocular surface events comprised dry eye (n = 3), keratitis (n = 3), and blurred vision (n = 1) (Table 2). Treatment-related ocular surface events led to Dato-DXd dose reduction in 1.9% of patients, dose interruption in 3.6%, and discontinuation in 0.8% (Table 3). Median time to onset of treatment-related ocular surface events was 69.5 days; events had resolved or were resolving in 79 of 158 patients at DCO, and median TTR was 98 days (Table 3). In six out of seven patients with grade 3 ocular surface events, the events were either resolved or were resolving at DCO, with the final patient having died before resolution of the event. The use of artificial tears was advised in the study protocol, and artificial tears were used by 258 patients (71.7%) in the Dato-DXd arm.

Adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis was reported in 14 patients (3.9%) in the Dato-DXd arm, comprising six grade 1 events, five grade 2 events, two grade 3 events, and one grade 5 event (Table 2); the grade 5 event was initially characterized by the investigator as grade 3 pneumonitis, with death attributed to disease progression, but was subsequently adjudicated as a grade 5 drug-related event. Adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis led to dose reduction in two patients, dose interruption in three patients, and discontinuation in six patients (Table 3). Median time to onset of adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis was 105.5 days, and events had recovered/resolved or were recovering/resolving in 6 of 14 patients at DCO, with median TTR of 45.5 days (Table 3). No adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis events were reported in the ICC arm.

Patient-reported outcomes

Questionnaire compliance and baseline scores

Compliance rates for all questionnaires were generally similar between the two treatment arms. Compliance with EORTC QLQ-C30 was 82.5% at baseline in both arms and declined but remained similar across both arms over time. Compliance with EORTC QLQ-C30 over time and for other PRO questionnaires and details of baseline scores are summarized in the Supplementary Results and Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330.

Time to deterioration

The secondary PRO endpoints of time to first deterioration and time to confirmed deterioration in GHS/QoL, pain, and physical functioning were all delayed in the Dato-DXd arm compared with the ICC arm. In all cases, median time to deterioration (TTD) was longer with Dato-DXd versus ICC, and HRs were <1 (Figure 1). TTD was longer in the Dato-DXd arm for the majority of other symptoms and functioning scales, except for nausea/vomiting, constipation, and breast symptoms (Figure 2). Change from baseline

Adjusted mean changes from baseline averaged over all visits for all other symptoms and functioning scales were similar between the two arms, except for constipation, which was numerically in favor of ICC compared with Dato-DXd [adjusted mean change from baseline: 12.22 (95% CI 9.84-14.61) for Dato-DXd and 4.95 (95% CI 2.16-7.73) for ICC], Supplementary Table S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330. Between-arm differences were not clinically meaningful according to derived thresholds for all scales.9

Patient-reported symptomatic AEs

Patient-reported symptomatic AEs were generally consistent with clinician-reported safety data (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330). Across most timepoints, a lower proportion of patients reported numbness/tingling, diarrhea, shortness of breath, and hand-foot syndrome with Dato-DXd compared with ICC, and a lower proportion of patients reported mouth/throat sores, nausea, vomiting, constipation, hair loss, and dry eyes with ICC versus Dato-DXd. Patients who reported any symptom of mouth/throat sores, numbness/tingling, or shortness of breath were asked how much the symptom interfered with their usual or daily activities. Data at weeks 12, 24, and 36 are shown in Supplementary Figure S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330; the level of interference with patients’ usual or daily activities was mainly ‘not at all’, ‘a little bit’, or ‘somewhat’ (78.5%-100%) as opposed to ‘quite a bit’ or ‘very much’ (0%-21.5%).

Patient-reported treatment tolerability

Patient-reported treatment tolerability was comparable between the Dato-DXd and ICC arms over time (Supplementary Figure S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330). At weeks 12 and 24, among patients who responded to the question ‘in the last 7 days, how bothered were you by the side effects of your cancer treatment?’ more patients responded ‘not at all’, ‘a little bit’, or ‘somewhat’ (in the Dato-DXd and ICC arms, respectively: 87.4% and 88.1% at week 12; 84.7% and 91.4% at week 24) than ‘quite a bit’ or ‘very much’ (in the Dato-DXd and ICC arms, respectively: 12.6% and 11.9% at week 12; 15.3% and 8.6% at week 24).

Patient-reported health status

Overall, patient-reported health status was similar between the two treatment arms (Supplementary Figure S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108330).

Discussion

In the TROPION-Breast01 study, Dato-DXd demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in PFS compared with ICC, along with a manageable safety profile.4,5 The rate of grade ≥3 TRAEs with Dato-DXd was less than half that with ICC, and fewer TRAEs led to dose interruption/reduction, indicating that Dato-DXd offers a manageable safety profile compared with ICC.4,5 The detailed analysis of the safety data from TROPION-Breast01 reported here provides further insight into key AEs and their management, while extensive data on PROs provide crucial information from the perspective of the patients.

Nausea was the most common TRAE with Dato-DXd in TROPION-Breast01 (51.9% of patients), but events were mostly grade 1/2, and the frequency of dose reduction and/or interruption due to nausea was very low. An analysis of nausea and vomiting as a grouped term showed that the majority of events occurred soon after initiation of study drug treatment (onset in cycle 1). Over three-quarters of patients received anti-emetics as concomitant medication. AESIs with Dato-DXd were typically low-grade and manageable using toxicity management guidelines. Treatment-related oral mucositis/stomatitis was reported in 57.2% of patients, but events were grade 1 (and therefore clinically mild/asymptomatic) in almost half of these patients, and a substantial proportion of events (178/206) were resolved or resolving at DCO. Approximately three-quarters of patients in the Dato-DXd arm received any prophylactic mouthwash, but of those patients, only half received steroid mouthwash (approximately one-third of all patients), despite strong recommendation in the study protocol. Treatment-related ocular surface events were reported in 43.9% of patients in the Dato-DXd arm; the majority of events were grade 1 (clinically mild/asymptomatic), and over half of the events were dry eye, with artificial tears being used in 71.7% of patients in the Dato-DXd arm. The frequent ophthalmologic assessments mandated throughout the study (which took place at screening, every three cycles from cycle 1 day 1 onward, as clinically indicated during the study, and at the end of treatment) are likely to have contributed to the rate of low-grade ocular surface events in both treatment arms, as illustrated by a higher incidence of ocular surface events in the ICC arm (12.0%) than has been observed historically.10 The rate of adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis in the Dato-DXd arm (3.9%) was consistent with rates reported previously with Dato-DXd in breast cancer,11 and lower than reported in lung cancer.12,13

Safety data from studies in HR+/HER2— breast cancer have illustrated that AE profiles vary considerably for different ADCs, even between TROP2-directed ADCs [Dato-DXd, sacituzumab govitecan (SG), and sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT)] or between ADCs with the same payload [Dato-DXd and trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd)].4,5,14, 15, 16, 17, 18 For example, among TROP2-directed ADCs, while treatment-related hematologic toxicity was uncommon with Dato-DXd in TROPION-Breast01, it has been shown to be a prominent feature of the safety profile for both SG and sac-TMT.14,16 Treatment-related diarrhea was common with SG (56.7%) but not with Dato-DXd (8.3%), nausea was frequent with both Dato-DXd (51.9%; grade ≥3 in 1.4%) and SG (55.2%; grade ≥3 in 1.1%), but less so with sac-TMT (22.0%), and stomatitis was common with Dato-DXd (51.4%; grade ≥3 in 6.4%) and sac-TMT (53.6%; grade ≥3 in 2.4%), but not with SG (<10%).14,16 Key differences between Dato-DXd and T-DXd safety profiles, potentially driven by the different targets, include higher rates of hematologic toxicities and some gastrointestinal disorders (nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea) with T-DXd compared with Dato-DXd, while rates of stomatitis and ocular surface events were higher with Dato-DXd versus T-DXd.4,5,17,18 Of note, adjudicated drug-related ILD/pneumonitis was less frequently reported with Dato-DXd (3.9% of patients; grade ≥3 in 0.8%) compared with T-DXd (11.3%-12.1% of patients; grade ≥3 in 1.4%-2.2%).17,18

Limitations in study design and data collection should be considered when interpreting data on prophylactic measures in TROPION-Breast01. The use of prophylaxis for the prevention of relevant AEs was recommended but not mandated throughout the duration of the study, and TROPION-Breast01 was not designed to evaluate or validate the effectiveness of prophylaxis. Limited data were captured on the use of mouthwash, artificial tears, and anti-emetics during the study, with only the number of patients who received these agents at any time during the treatment exposure period being provided. Moreover, data did not differentiate between those used for the purpose of preventing AEs and those used as secondary prophylaxis after the onset of AEs. Furthermore, the data did not capture adherence to the recommended administration method (for example, whether patients used steroid mouthwash 4x daily), thus limiting the ability to perform a robust assessment of, or draw conclusions on, the effectiveness of these prophylactic measures on oral mucositis/stomatitis, ocular surface events, or nausea/vomiting management.

PROs are an important component of oncology clinical trials, offering meaningful insights into patient experiences of symptoms, functioning, and QoL, complementing physician-reported safety data to provide a fuller picture of treatment tolerability, and contributing evidence to support drug approval.19, 20, 21, 22, 23 GHS/QoL, pain, and physical functioning were selected as core outcomes for the secondary PRO endpoints in TROPION-Breast01 due to their relevance to the treatment setting: GHS/QoL measures overall health-related QoL, which is important in evaluating the risk benefit of cancer therapies, especially in noncurative settings24; pain is a frequently reported disease symptom that can impact functioning and health-related QoL in participants with HR+/HER2— advanced breast cancer25; and physical functioning is a core PRO of cancer therapy that evaluates the impact of disease- and treatment-related symptoms on mobility and performing daily activities.22,26

TTD in GHS/QoL, pain, and physical functioning were all delayed with Dato-DXd compared with ICC in the analyses of both time to first deterioration and time to confirmed deterioration. The Kaplan‒Meier curves showed a separation between the arms in favor of Dato-DXd; this effect was sustained over time, and the proportion of patients without deterioration was higher in the Dato-DXd arm compared with the ICC arm at 3, 6, and 9 months and beyond. TTD in other prespecified patient-reported functioning and symptoms was also assessed (exploratory endpoints): the results were in favor of the Dato-DXd arm for the majority of functioning and symptom scales, with the exception of nausea/vomiting, constipation, and breast symptoms. Notably, TTD in role functioning (a core outcome to evaluate impact on ability to work and carry out daily, leisure, and social activities)22 and fatigue (a commonly reported symptom in patients with breast cancer)25 were both delayed in patients in the Dato-DXd arm compared with the ICC arm.

Patient-reported symptomatic AEs, measured by PRO-CTCAE and EORTC IL 117, were generally consistent with the AE profile reported by clinicians in both arms. Although symptomatic AEs captured by PRO-CTCAE and EORTC IL117 do not directly reflect investigator-assessed AEs, the symptoms of mouth/throat sores (reported in a lower proportion of patients with ICC versus Dato-DXd) and numbness/tingling (reported in a lower proportion of patients with Dato-DXd versus ICC) are of particular clinical interest: among the patients who reported these symptoms in both arms, the majority described limited interference with daily activities.

Health-related QoL with the TROP2-directed ADC, SG, was shown to be favorable compared with treatment of physician’s choice for patients with metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer in the phase III TROPiCs-02 study.27 TTD was delayed with SG for GHS/QoL, physical functioning, fatigue, emotional functioning, dyspnea, and insomnia, but was delayed with treatment of physician’s choice for diarrhea. For symptomatic TRAEs, more patients experienced worsening of diarrhea and hair loss with SG than with treatment of physician’s choice; for other symptomatic TRAEs, the percentages of patients with worsening were low in both arms. Comparisons between PROs for SG and Dato-DXd are confounded by differences in study designs, populations, and treatments. There is the potential for bias to be introduced in PRO assessments in studies with open-label designs, due to patients’ knowledge of their assigned treatment group.28 However, various reports have indicated that blinding has no significant impact on PRO results.28, 29, 30, 31

Conclusions

Dato-DXd demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in the dual primary end point of PFS by BICR compared with ICC in the TROPION-Breast01 study and is approved for use in several countries globally.2, 3, 4 The detailed analysis of the safety data from TROPION-Breast01 reported here provides further evidence that the safety profile of Dato-DXd was manageable, and AESIs with Dato-DXd were typically low-grade and manageable using toxicity management guidelines. The PRO results provide evidence from the patient’s perspective and complement the improvement in efficacy and manageable safety profile observed; TTD in GHS/QoL, pain, and physical functioning was delayed with Dato-DXd compared with ICC. The totality of data from TROPION-Breast01 supports Dato-DXd as a treatment option for patients with previously treated, inoperable/metastatic HR+/HER2— breast cancer.
