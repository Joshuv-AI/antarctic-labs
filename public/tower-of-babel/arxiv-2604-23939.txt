1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be an
n
-dimensional complete gradient Ricci soliton with the potential function
f
satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle\text{Ric}+\nabla^{2}f=\lambda g

for some constant
\lambda
, where
Ric
is the Ricci tensor of
g
and
\nabla^{2}f
denotes the Hessian of the potential function
f
.
The Ricci soliton is said to be shrinking, steady, or expanding accordingly as
\lambda
is positive, zero, or negative, respectively. By rescalling the metric
g
by a positive constant, we can assume
\lambda\in\{\frac{1}{2},0,-\frac{1}{2}\}
.

A gradient Ricci soliton is a self-similar solution to the Ricci flow which flows by diffeomorphism and homothety. The study of solitons has become increasingly important in both the study of the Ricci flow introduced by Hamilton
[
19
]
and metric measure theory. Solitons play a direct role as singularity dilations in the Ricci flow proof of uniformization. Due to the work of Perelman
[
30
]
, Ni-Wallach
[
28
]
, Cao-Chen-Zhu
[
8
]
, the classification of three dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton is complete. For more work on the classification of gradient Ricci soliton under various curvature condition, see
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
15
,
20
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
31
,
32
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
.

In this paper, we focus our attention on
n
-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature. Recall that in Petersen and Wylie’s paper
[
31
]
, a gradient Ricci soliton
(M,g)
is said to be rigid if it is isometric to a quotient
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, the product soliton of an Einstein manifold
\mathbb{N}
of positive scalar curvature with the Gaussian soliton
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Conversely, for the complete shrinking case, Prof. Huai-Dong Cao raised the following

Conjecture
:
Let
(M^{n},g,f)
,
n\geq 4
, be a complete
n
-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton. If
(M,g)
has constant scalar curvature, then it must be rigid,
i.e., a finite quotient of
\mathbb{N}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
for some Einstein manifold
\mathbb{N}
of positive scalar curvature.

Petersen and Wylie
[
31
]
proved that a complete gradient Ricci soliton is rigid if and only if it has
constant scalar curvature and is radially flat, that is, the sectional curvature
K(\cdot,\nabla f)=0
. Fernández-López and García-Río
[
18
]
obtained that the soliton is rigid if and only if the Ricci curvature has constant rank. They also derived the following results for complete
n
-dimensional gradient Ricci solitons (
1.1
) with constant scalar curvature
R
: (i) The possible value of
R
is
\{0,\lambda,\cdots,(n-1)\lambda,n\lambda\}
. (ii) If
R
takes the value
(n-1)\lambda
, then the soliton must be rigid. (iii) In the shrinking case, there is no any complete gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with
R=\lambda
.
(iv) Any
n
-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
R=(n-2)\lambda
has non-negative Ricci curvature.

Several years ago, Cheng and Zhou
[
17
]
confirmed Cao’s conjecture in dimension
n=4
.
Very recently, the authors gave a simple proof of Cheng-Zhou’s result in
[
33
]
. Later, the authors
[
21
]
finished the
5
-dimensional case when the scalar curvature is
\frac{3}{2}
. In this paper, we want to generalize this results to high dimension in some sense.

Our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g,f)
is an
n
-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=\frac{n-2}{2}
, if each level set of
f
has vanishing Weyl curvature, then it is isometric to a finite quotient of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
.

Remark
Theorem
1.1
can be seen a generalization of Cheng-Zhou’s result
[
17
]
in dimension four, since the level set of the potential function
f
has vanishing Weyl curvature automatically when
n=4
.

When the sectional curvature has sharp upper bounded, we also derive the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be an
n
-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with
R=\frac{k}{2}
. If it has nonnegative Ricci curvature and its sectional section has upper bound
\frac{1}{2(k-1)}
, then it is isometric to a finite quotient of
\mathbb{S}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
.

Remark
The above bound
\frac{1}{2(k-1)}
is sharp, because
S^{k}
has constant sectional curvature
\frac{1}{2(k-1)}
.

2.
Notations and basic formulas on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons

In this section, we recall the notations and basic formulas on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature. For details, we refer to
[
5
,
19
,
31
,
17
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete gradient shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying (
1.1
).
By scaling the metric
g
, one can normalize
\lambda
so that
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
. In this paper, we always assume
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
and the gradient shrinking Ricci soliton equation is as follows,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\text{Ric}+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2}g.

At first we recall some basic formulas which will be used throughout the paper:

(2.3)

\displaystyle dR=2Ric(\nabla f,\cdot),

(2.4)

\displaystyle R+\Delta f=\frac{n}{2},

(2.5)

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}=f,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}R=R-2|Ric|^{2},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}R_{ij}=R_{ij}-2R_{ikjl}R_{kl}.

where
\Delta_{f}=\Delta-\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\right\rangle
is the weighted Laplacian, and
\Delta_{f}
acting on the function
is self-adjoint on the space of square integrable functions with respect to the weighted measure
e^{-f}dv.
In general, the weighted Laplacian
\Delta_{f}
acting on tensors is given by
\Delta_{f}=\Delta-\nabla_{\nabla f}
.

Now we consider complete gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature
R
. In this case, the potential function
f
is isoparametric and the isoparametric property plays a very important role.
Concretely, the potential function
f
can be renormalized, by replacing
f-R
with
f
, so that
f:M\to[0,+\infty)
satisfies

(2.8)

|\nabla f|^{2}=f,

which implies that
f
is transnormal.
Recall (
2.4
)

\displaystyle\Delta f=\frac{n}{2}-R,

therefore the (nonconstant) renormalized
f
is an isoparametric function on
M
. From the potential function estimate (
),
f
is proper and unbounded.

For later application, we need to discuss the relation between barrier solution and distribution.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete manifold,
\nabla
be the gradient operator on
M
,

\Delta
be the Laplacian on
M
,
\Omega
be an open set of
M
and

F\in C^{\infty}(M)
. The
F
-Laplacian is defined by

\Delta_{F}:=\Delta-\nabla F\cdot\nabla.

We say that a continuous function
u\in C(\Omega)
satisfies
\Delta_{F}u\leq w

for some
w

in the barrier sense
, if for any fixed
x\in\Omega
,
there exists a smooth function
v
defined in a neighborhood
U(x)
of
x
,
such that
u(x)=v(x)
,
u(y)\leq v(y)
for any
y\in U(x)
and

\Delta_{F}v(x)\leq w(x).

We say that
u\in C(\Omega)
satisfies
\Delta_{F}u\leq w
on
\Omega

in the sense of distribution
, if

\int_{\Omega}u\Delta_{F}\phi\,e^{-F}\leq\int_{\Omega}w\phi\,e^{-F}

for any
\phi\geq 0
with
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

In the appendix of
[
34
]
, the authors proved the following Theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
34
]
)
.

If
u\in C(\Omega)
satisfies
\Delta_{F}u\leq w
for some
w

in the barrier sense, then it satisfies
\Delta_{F}u\leq w
on
\Omega
in
the sense of distribution.

Next we shall apply Theorem
2.1
to discuss some special cases for our
theorems in the preceding sections. We use the same notations as before. On
an
n
-dimensional shrinker
(M,g,f)
, let
u
be an
Lipschitz function and let
w
be an integrable function
with respect to measure
e^{-h}dv
. Here
h
is uniformly equivalent to the square of
distance function. For a sufficiently large

r
,
a
and
b
(
a<b
), set
D(r):=\{x\in M|h(x)\leq r\}
,

\Sigma(r):=\{x\in M|h(x)=r\}
and
D(a,b):=\{x\in M|a\leq h(x)\leq b\}
.
Then Theorem
2.1
implies that

Corollary 2.2

(
[
34
]
)
.

If
\Delta_{h}u\leq w
holds on
M\setminus D(r)
in the barrier sense, then it
holds on
M\setminus D(r)
in the sense of distribution.

Using Corollary
2.2
, we have the following useful proposition.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
34
]
)
.

If a continuous and Lipschitz function
u
satisfies
\Delta_{h}u\leq w

on
M\setminus D(r)
in the barrier sense, then

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma(r)}\langle\nabla u,\tfrac{\nabla h}{|\nabla h|}\rangle e^{-h}\leq\int_{M\setminus D(r)}w\,e^{-h}

holds for almost everywhere sufficiently large

.

Remark
. During the paper,
\int_{M\setminus D(a)}\Delta_{h}u\cdot e^{-h}dvol
is understood as
-\int_{\Sigma(a)}\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla h}{|\nabla h|}\rangle e^{-h}
, for more details, see
[
34
]
.

First, we recall the non-negativity of Ricci curvature for

Proposition 3.1

]

Let

be an

Proof.

Let
(M,g,f)
be an
n
-dimensional complete noncompact gradient shrinking Ricci soliton satisfying (
2.2
) with constant scalar curvature
R=\frac{n-2}{2}
, the authors proved that the Ricci curvature is non-negative in
[
18

(
2.3

.

∎

From Proposition
3.1
, throughout this paper we always denote the eigenvalues of Ricci curvature by

0=\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\lambda_{4}\leq\cdot\cdot\cdot\leq\lambda_{n}.

In the following, we calculate
\Delta_{f}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})
in the barrier sense, and have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be an
n
-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
\frac{n-2}{2}
. Then

(3.9)

\Delta_{f}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\leq(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})-2\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}K_{1\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}-2\sum_{\alpha=3}^{n}K_{2\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}

in the sense of barrier, where
K_{ij}
denotes the sectional curvature of the plane spanned by
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
, and
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the orthonormal eigenvectors corresponding to the Ricci-eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

Proof.

Actually, at
x
, because
R=\frac{n-2}{2}
,
Ric(\nabla f)=0
, so we choose
e_{1}=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}
, then extend
e_{1}
to an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},\cdot\cdot\cdot,e_{n}\}
such that
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are the eigenvectors of
Ric(x)
corresponding to eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Take parallel transport of
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
along all the geodesics from
x
, then in a neighborhood
B(x,\delta)
we get a smooth function
u(y)=Ric(y)(e_{1}(y),e_{1}(y))+Ric(y)(e_{2}(y),e_{2}(y))
satisfying
u(y)\geq\lambda_{1}(y)+\lambda_{2}(y)
and
u(x)=\lambda_{1}(x)+\lambda_{2}(x)
.
Thus, at
x
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}u(x)=

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\left(Ric(y)(e_{1}(y),e_{1}(y))+Ric(y)(e_{2}(y),e_{2}(y))\right)|_{y=x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{f}Ric)(e_{1},e_{1})(x)+(\Delta_{f}Ric)(e_{2},e_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})-2(\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{1i}\lambda_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{2i}\lambda_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})-2\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}K_{1\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}-2\sum_{\alpha=3}^{n}K_{2\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha},

this completes the proof.
∎

Next, we deal with the term
\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}K_{1\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}
. First we will give the following lemma for preparation.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be an
n
-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
\frac{n-2}{2}
. Then

(3.10)

K_{1\alpha}=\frac{\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\alpha}+\lambda_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})}{f}

for
\alpha=2,3,\cdot\cdot\cdot,n
.

Proof.

From the Ricci identity, we have

\displaystyle-R(\nabla f,e_{\alpha},\nabla f,e_{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\nabla_{\beta}f_{\alpha k}-\nabla_{k}f_{\alpha\beta}\right)f_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{\beta}R_{\alpha k}-\nabla_{k}R_{\alpha\beta}\right)f_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\beta}+\nabla_{\beta}(R_{\alpha k}f_{k})-R_{\alpha k}f_{k\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\beta}-R_{\alpha k}\left(\frac{1}{2}g_{k\beta}-R_{k\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\beta}-\left(\frac{1}{2}R_{\alpha\beta}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{\alpha k}R_{k\beta}\right),

where (
2.3
) was used in the third equality. Therefore, we see

(3.11)

R(e_{1},e_{\alpha},e_{1},e_{\beta})=\frac{\nabla_{\nabla f}R_{\alpha\beta}+\left(\frac{1}{2}R_{\alpha\beta}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}R_{\alpha k}R_{k\beta}\right)}{f}

due to
|\nabla f|^{2}=f
. (
3.10
) holds by setting
\beta=\alpha
in (
3.11
).
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be an
n
-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
\frac{n-2}{2}
. Then we have

\displaystyle-\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}K_{1\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{f}\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})=\frac{1}{f}\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}(\lambda_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}\lambda_{\alpha}.

Proof.

First, since the scalar curvature is constant, (
2.6
) implies

\displaystyle|Ric|^{2}=\frac{R}{2}=\frac{n-2}{4}.

This means
\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\lambda_{\alpha}=\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}=\frac{n-2}{2}
and
\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}=\lambda_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}^{2}=\frac{n-2}{4}
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\lambda_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})=0.

Recall (
3.10
), and we obtain

\displaystyle-\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}K_{1\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{f}\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\left[\nabla f\cdot\nabla\lambda_{\alpha}+\lambda_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})\right]\lambda_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{f}\left[\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}+\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{f}\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{f}\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\left[-\lambda_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f}\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\lambda_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\alpha})^{2}.

We have completed the proof of this Lemma.

Boundedness of Riemannian curvature

In this section, we prove the curvature is bounded, and the proof is similar to the argument in
[

]

Lemma 4.1

(
[
16
]
)
.

Given a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold with unbounded curvature, we can find a sequence of points
p_{j}
divergent to infinity such that for each positive integer
j
, we have
|Rm(p_{j})|\geq j
and

\displaystyle|Rm(x)|\leq 4|Rm(p_{j})|

for
x\in B(p_{j},\frac{j}{\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}})
.

The following backward pseudolocality Theorem will also be useful.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
23
]
)
.

For any
\alpha>0
, there is
\epsilon(n,\alpha)
such that the following holds.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
be a Ricci flow induced by a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton. Given
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times I
and
r>0
, if

\displaystyle|B_{t_{0}}(x_{0},r)|\geq\alpha r^{n},\quad|Rm|\leq(\alpha r)^{-2}\quad on\quad B_{t_{0}}(x_{0},r),

then

\displaystyle|Rm|\leq(\epsilon r)^{-2}\quad on\quad P(x_{0},t_{0};(1-\alpha)r,-(\epsilon r)^{2},0).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be an
n
-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
R=\frac{n-2}{2}
. Suppose each level set of
f
has vanishing weyl curvature, then its curvature is bounded.

Proof.

Suppose not, by Lemma
4.1
, then there exists a sequence of points
p_{j}
divergent to infinity such that for each positive integer
j
, we have
|Rm(p_{j})|\geq j
and

\displaystyle|Rm(x)|\leq 4|Rm(p_{j})|

for
x\in B(p_{j},\frac{j}{\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}})
.
By the
\kappa
-noncollapsed theorem in
[
22
]
and the scalar curvature is bounded for
(M,g)
, we get that
\text{Vol}(B(p_{i},1))
has a uniform positive lower bound
[
22
]
.

For the shrinking gradient Ricci soliton
(M,g,f)
,
the associated Ricci flow
(M,g(t),p_{j})
is defined on
(-\infty,0)
, where

g(t):=(-t)\phi^{*}_{t}g,\quad\frac{d\phi_{t}}{dt}=\frac{\nabla f}{-t}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{-1}=\mathrm{Id}.

Now we can use Theorem
4.2
with
r=|Rm|(p_{j})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
to derive that

\displaystyle|Rm|\leq(\epsilon r)^{-2}\quad on\quad B(p_{j},g(-1),\frac{j}{2}r)\times[-1-(\epsilon r)^{2},-1].

Then we can apply Hamilton’s compactness theorem to obtain that the rescaled manifolds
\left(B(p_{j},g,\frac{j}{\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}}),|Rm(p_{j})|g,p_{j}\right)
converge to a smooth complete Riemannian manifold
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty},p_{\infty})
with
|Rm(p_{\infty})|=1
which is Ricci flat because
(M^{n},g)
has bounded Ricci curvature and
|Rm(p_{j})|\rightarrow\infty
, moreover
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty},p_{\infty})
has Euclidean volume growth.

Since the integral curves of
f
passing through
p_{j}
are geodesics with respect to
(M,g)
, the geodesic segment of these curves contained in
B\left(p_{j},\frac{j}{\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}}\right)
will converge to a geodesic line in
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty})
, then Cheeger-Gromoll’s splitting theorem
[
13
]
implies that
M_{\infty}=\mathbb{R}\times N^{n-1}
, where
N^{n-1}
is Ricci flat and of Euclidean Volume growth.

Because
(\Sigma(s),g)
has second fundamental form

\displaystyle h=\frac{\frac{1}{2}g-Ric}{|\nabla f|},

which tends to zero as
f\rightarrow\infty
,
so the second fundamental form of
B\left(p_{j},g,\frac{j}{\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}}\right)\cap\Sigma(f(p_{j}))
with metric
|Rm(p_{j})|g
converges to zero. This implies the level set
B\left(p_{j},g,\frac{j}{\sqrt{|Rm(p_{j})|}}\right)\cap\Sigma(f(p_{j}))
with the induced rescaled metrics
|Rm(p_{j})|g
will converge to
N^{n-1}
.

Since each level set has vanishing Weyl curvature,

N^{n-1}
has vanishing Weyl curvature, hence it is flat. This contradicts the fact that
|Rm(p_{\infty})|=1
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be an
n
-dimensional shrinking gradient Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
R=\frac{n-2}{2}
. Suppose each level set of
f
has vanishing Weyl curvature, then
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\rightarrow 0
at infinity.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary, then there exists a sequence of
q_{j}
divergent to infinity with
(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})(q_{j})\geq\delta
for some
\delta>0
.

As
[
27
]
, define
f_{j}(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(q_{j})}{|\nabla f(q_{j})|}
. By Theorem
4.3
, we have the curvature is bounded, so the pointed manifolds
(M,g,q_{j})
converge in Cheeger-Gromov sense to
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty},q_{\infty})
. Since
|\nabla f_{j}(q_{j})|=1
,

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f_{j}=\frac{\nabla^{2}f}{|\nabla f(q_{j})|}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}g-Ric}{|\nabla f(q_{j})|}

tends to zero at infinity,
f_{j}
converges to a smooth function
f_{\infty}
with
|\nabla f_{\infty}|(q_{\infty})=1
and
\nabla^{2}f_{\infty}=0
. Hence
M_{\infty}=\mathbb{R}\times N^{n-1}
, where
N^{n-1}
is an
n-1
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with

and of constant scalar curvature
\frac{n-2}{2}
. Since each level set of
f
has vanishing Weyl curvature, then
N^{n-1}
also has vanishing weyl curvature.

Thanks to the classification of complete locally conformally flat manifolds
with nonnegative Ricci curvature by Zhu
[
38

and Carron-Herzlich
[

,

is
one of the following:

(1)
N
is non-flat and globally conformally equivalent to
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
;

(2)
N
is globally conformally equivalent to a space form of positive curvature;

(3)
N
is locally isometric to the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-2}

(4)
N
is isometric to a complete flat manifold.

If case (1) happens, then there is a positive function

such that

has constant scalar curvature
\frac{n-2}{2}
, where

\displaystyle\Delta u+\frac{n-3}{8}u^{\frac{n+1}{n-3}}=0\quad on\quad\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

By Caffarelli-Gidas-Spruck
[

]

[

is noncompact. If case (3) happens, it contradicts with
\lambda_{1}(g_{N^{n-1}})\geq\delta
. Case (4) can not happen, since
N
is nonflat.

In all, the Weyl curvature of

couldn’t be zero, contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

By Theorem
4.3
the curvature is bounded, so
|\nabla Ric|
is bounded, hence
|\frac{\nabla f\cdot\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{2}}{f}|\leq\delta(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})
, where
\delta>0
is sufficiently small. Then we can apply Proposition

to obtain that

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})-\frac{2}{n-3}\nabla\log f\cdot\nabla(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\leq-\frac{1}{n-3}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})

on

for some large

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}u\leq-\frac{1}{n-3}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}).

Integrating on

(5.20)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma(a)}\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla h}{|\nabla h|}\rangle e^{-h}=\int_{M\setminus D(a)}\Delta_{h}ue^{-h}\leq-\frac{1}{n-3}\int_{M\setminus D(a)}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})e^{-h}\leq 0,

notice that

Next it sufficies to prove

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(b)}\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla h}{|\nabla h|}\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(b)}\leq 0

for some

.

For this purpose, we consider the following one parameter family of diffeomorphisms,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}},\\
&F(x,a)=x\in\Sigma(a).\end{cases}

Then
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}f(F(x,s))=\langle\nabla f,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\rangle=1
,
and the advantage of
F
is that it maps level set of
f
to other level set, in particular
f(F(x,s))=s
for any

.

Suppose
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\cdot\cdot\cdot,x_{n-1}\}
are local coordinate chart of
\Sigma(a)
, on
\Sigma(s)
, let
g(s)(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}):=g(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}})
,
d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=\sqrt{det(g_{ij})}dx
, where
dx=dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge dx_{3}\wedge\cdot\cdot\cdot\wedge dx_{n-1}
.
Next we compute the derivatives of

.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\sqrt{det(g_{ij})}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2g^{ij}\langle\nabla_{\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial s},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{ij}\langle\nabla_{\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\nabla f|^{2}}g^{ij}\nabla^{2}f(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}})d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\nabla f|^{2}}g^{ij}\left(\frac{1}{2}g(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}})-Ric(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}})\right)d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\nabla f|^{2}}(\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n-2}{2})d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=\frac{1}{2s}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}.

Hence, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}\right)=\left(-\frac{1}{2}s^{-\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\frac{1}{2s}\right)d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}=0.

Next, define a function

\displaystyle I(s)=\int_{\Sigma(s)}u\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}.

and then we can compute the derivative of
I(s)
as follows:

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\int_{\Sigma(s)}u\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma(s)}\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\rangle\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}+\int_{\Sigma(s)}u\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma(s)}\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\rangle\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\int_{\Sigma(s)}\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla h}{|\nabla h|}\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(s)},

where
\frac{\nabla f}{\nabla f}=\frac{\nabla h}{|\nabla h|}
and
|\nabla f|=\sqrt{s}
were used in the last equality.

Moreover, since
I(s)
tends to zero as
s\rightarrow\infty
, there exists
b>a
such that
I^{\prime}(b)\leq 0
, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma(b)}\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla h}{|\nabla h|}\rangle d\sigma_{\Sigma(b)}\leq 0;

this finishes the proof of (
5.21
).

Combining (
5.20
) and (
5.21
), we derive that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0

on
M\setminus D(b)
for some
b
. This implies that

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=\cdot\cdot\cdot=\lambda_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{2}

on
M\setminus D(b)
.
Hence the function

\displaystyle G=tr(Ric^{3})-\frac{1}{2}|Ric|^{2},

is
0
on
M\setminus D(b)
.

Because
G
is an analytic function, it has to be zero, we obtain that
G\equiv 0
on
M
. Moreover, the equation
0=\Delta_{f}R=R-2|Ric|^{2}
implies that

\displaystyle G=

\displaystyle tr(Ric^{3})-|Ric|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i}-\frac{1}{2})^{2}\lambda_{i}=0.

Finally we get
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}\equiv 0
and
\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=\cdot\cdot\cdot=\lambda_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{2}
due to
Ric\geq 0
and the continuity of
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
. This implies the Ricci curvature has constant rank
n-2
. Therefore, the soliton is isometric to a finite quotient of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{N}^{n-2}
by
[
18
]
, where
\mathbb{N}^{n-2}
is an
(n-2)
-dimensional Einstein manifold. Because each level set has vanishing Weyl curvature, hence it it isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
.
We have completed the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

∎
