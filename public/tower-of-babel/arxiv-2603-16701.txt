Dyonic Einstein-Maxwell-scalar black holes: the cold, the hot and the plunge
Shun Chen, Xiao Yan Chew, Jutta Kunz

Abstract
We investigate dyonic nonlinearly scalarized black holes in Einstein-Maxwell-scalar theory. The domain of existence of scalarized dyonic black holes consists of three branches. The cold branch and the hot branch bifurcate at a minimal value of the charge, analogous to the purely electrically charged scalarized black holes. However, the presence of both charges allows for regular extremal black holes, leading to a third branch featuring a sudden plunge in Hawking temperature. In fact, the presence of both electromagnetic charges introduces a factor Δ ⁡ ( ϕ ) \Delta(\phi) in the source term of scalar field equations that vanishes when the coupling function f ⁡ ( ϕ ) f(\phi) equals the ratio of the charges for some value of the scalar field ϕ c \phi_{c} . The scalar field of extremal black holes assumes precisely this value at the horizon, ϕ H = ϕ c \phi_{H}=\phi_{c} . We demonstrate the plunge for the coupling function f ⁡ ( ϕ ) = exp ⁡ ( α ​ ϕ 3 ) f(\phi)=\exp(\alpha\phi^{3}) .

Model

The EMS family of models is defined by the following action (we set
c=G=4\pi\varepsilon_{0}=1
):

S=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(R-2\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi-f(\phi)\,F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right).

(1)

Here
R
is the Ricci scalar,

F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
is the Maxwell field strength tensor,
and
\phi
is the scalar field.
The coupling function
f(\phi)
governs the non-minimal coupling of
\phi
to the electromagnetic field.
After variation, we get the set of field equations

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}=2\left[\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\rho}\phi\,\partial^{\rho}\phi+f(\phi)\left(F_{\mu\rho}F_{\nu}{}^{\rho}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\right)\right],

(2)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}\,\partial^{\mu}\phi\right)=\frac{1}{4}\frac{df(\phi)}{d\phi}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma},

(3)

\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}\,f(\phi)\,F^{\mu\nu}\right)=0.

(4)

To obtain dyonic static spherically solutions we employ the following Ansatz for the metric

ds^{2}=-N(r)\,e^{-2\sigma(r)}dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{N(r)}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\varphi^{2}\right),

(5)

with

N(r)=1-\frac{m(r)}{r}.

(6)

The corresponding Ansatz for the Maxwell field of a dyonic black hole reads

A=V(r)dt+P\cos\theta d\varphi

(7)

with the electric potential
V(r)
and the magnetic charge
P
,
and the scalar field is given by
\phi(r)
.
The Maxwell equation (
4
) then yields

[
27
]

V^{\prime}(r)=\frac{e^{-\sigma}}{r^{2}}\frac{Q}{f(\phi)},

(8)

where
Q
is the electric charge.
With this Ansatz the resulting set of equations becomes

m^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}N(r)\,\phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\left(\frac{Q^{2}}{f(\phi)}+f(\phi)\,P^{2}\right),

(9)

\sigma^{\prime}(r)+r\,\phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}=0,

(10)

\left(e^{-\sigma(r)}r^{2}N(r)\,\phi^{\prime}(r)\right)^{\prime}+\frac{e^{-\sigma(r)}}{2r^{2}f(\phi)}\frac{df(\phi)}{d\phi}\left(\frac{Q^{2}}{f(\phi)}-f(\phi)\,P^{2}\right)=0.

(11)

The equations possess electro-magnetic duality
[
27
]

\{P\to Q,\ \ Q\to P\}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ f(\phi)\to 1/f(\phi)\ .

(12)

Taking both

positive and

limits their ratio to
P/Q\leq 1

Exact solutions

As noted before
[
27
]
, the Reissner-Nordström solution is a solution of the field equations for
f(\phi)=1

\phi=\phi_{c}

\displaystyle V(r)=-\frac{Q}{r}\ ,\qquad N(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{Q^{2}+P^{2}}{r^{2}}\ ,\qquad\sigma(r)=0\ ,

(13)

where
M
is the ADM mass of the dyonic black hole.
It remains a solution of the field equations if

\left.\frac{df(\phi)}{d\phi}\right|_{\phi_{c}}=0,

(14)

for
\phi(r)=\phi_{c}=0
, which presents the basic requisite of the phenomenon of scalarization of Reissner-Nordström black holes.

Another exact solution of the field equations is obtained, if eq. (
14
) holds in addition for a constant scalar field
\phi(r)=\phi_{c}\neq 0
.
The Einstein equations then reduce to

\displaystyle m^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\left(\frac{Q^{2}}{f(\phi_{c})}+f(\phi_{c})\,P^{2}\right),

(15)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\ ,

(16)

with solution

m(r)=M-\frac{1}{2r}\left(\frac{Q^{2}}{f(\phi_{c})}+f(\phi_{c})\,P^{2}\right)\ ,\ \ \ \sigma(r)=0\ .

(17)

The source term of the scalar field vanishes, however, as well, when the last factor vanishes,

\Delta(\phi)=\left(\frac{Q^{2}}{f(\phi)}-f(\phi)\,P^{2}\right)=0,

(18)

abbreviated by
\Delta(\phi)
.
Thus a further exact solution with a constant scalar field
\phi=\phi_{c}
is found when
\phi_{c}
is a solution of

\Delta(\phi_{c})=0\ \ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \ f(\phi_{c})=\frac{Q}{P}.

(19)

The Einstein equations then reduce to

\displaystyle m^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P\,Q}{r^{2}}\ ,

(20)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\ ,

(21)

with solution

m(r)=M-\frac{P\,Q}{r}\ ,\ \ \ \sigma(r)=0\ .

(22)

Thus, the extremal black hole satisfies

\displaystyle f(\phi_{H})=\frac{Q}{P}\ ,\ \ \ r_{H}=\sqrt{2PQ}\ .

(23)

In all these cases, the metric is asymptotically flat, but the scalar field
is non-vanishing in the asymptotic region.

4
Scalarized black holes

4.1
General considerations

The expansions at the horizon and at radial infinity for scalarized black holes have been presented in
[
27
]
.
Accordingly, a set of boundary conditions yielding non-extremal black holes that are regular on and outside their horizons, that are asymptotically flat, and possess a scalar field that is vanishing at infinity is given by

\displaystyle\left.m(r)\right|_{r_{H}}=\frac{r_{H}}{2}\ ,\ \ \ \left.\phi(r)\right|_{r_{H}}=\phi_{H}\ ,\ \ \

(24)

\displaystyle\left.\phi^{\prime}(r)\right|_{r_{H}}=\frac{1}{2r_{H}}\frac{df(\phi)}{d\phi}\bigg|_{\phi_{H}}\frac{\left(\frac{Q^{2}}{f(\phi_{H})}-f(\phi_{H})P^{2}\right)}{\left({Q^{2}}-r_{H}^{2}f(\phi_{H})+f(\phi_{H})^{2}P^{2}\right)}\ ,

(25)

\displaystyle\left.\sigma(r)\right|_{\infty}=0\ ,\ \ \ \left.V(r)\right|_{\infty}=0\ ,\ \ \ \left.\phi(r)\right|_{\infty}=0\ .

(26)

To be able to impose all boundary conditions, we introduce an auxiliary equation for the charge
Q
,
Q^{\prime}(r)=0

As shown in
[
27
]
, for extremal scalarized black holes the field equations imply

\displaystyle Q=\frac{r_{H}\sqrt{f(\phi_{H})}}{\sqrt{2}}\ ,\ \ \ P=\frac{r_{H}}{\sqrt{2f(\phi_{H})}}\ ,

(27)

and therefore

\displaystyle f(\phi_{H})=\frac{Q}{P}\ ,\ \ \ r_{H}=\sqrt{2PQ}\ ,

(28)

showing immediately the necessity of a finite magnetic charge

for a finite horizon area.
Moreover, we note that relations (

23

The asymptotics of the solutions determine their global charges.
The thermodynamics of the solutions is determined by their horizon properties.
The horizon area

and the Hawking temperature

are given by

A_{H}=4\pi r_{H}^{2}\ ,\ \ \ T_{H}=\frac{1}{4\pi}N^{\prime}(r_{H})e^{-\delta_{H}}.

(29)

Along with
[
27
]
we consider the dimensionless quantities

q=\frac{\sqrt{Q^{2}+P^{2}}}{M}\ ,\ \ \ \beta=\frac{P}{Q}\ ,\ \ \ a_{H}=\frac{A_{H}}{16\pi M^{2}}\ ,\ \ \ t_{H}=8\pi T_{H}M.

(30)

5
Conclusions

We have studied nonlinearly scalarized black holes in Einstein-Maxwell-scalar theory carrying both electric and magnetic charge.
In contrast to the solely electrically charged scalarized black holes, which possess a cold branch and a hot branch without a regular extremal limit, the dyonic scalarized black holes feature a regular extremal limit.
The extremal black holes satisfy the relation
f(\phi_{H})=Q/P
at their horizon.
This relation corresponds precisely to the relation necessary for the source term of the scalar field to vanish,
\Delta(\phi_{c})=0
for
\phi_{c}=\phi_{H}
.

To demonstrate the effect of the presence of both charges, we have chosen the coupling function
f(\phi)=\exp{(\alpha\phi^{3})}
.
For this coupling function we have solved the field equations for fixed values of the coupling constant
\alpha
and the charge ratio
\beta=P/Q
.
The resulting sets of solutions show a similar dependence of the horizon area on the charge as the singly charged solutions, except for their finite area endpoint formed by the extremal black hole.
The cold branch bifurcates with the hot branch at a minimal value of the charge.
However, when considering the temperature dependence, the hot branch ends suddenly, giving rise to a dramatic plunge to the extremal black hole.
In fact, the plunge is most dramatic when the scalar field at the horizon
\phi_{H}
is considered for small values of the charge ratio
\beta
.

The next steps will be to address the stability and quasinormal modes of these nonlinearly scalarized dyonic black holes, and to construct their rotating generalizations.
Since the electromagnetic charge of astrophysical black holes is thought to be very small (see e.g.,
[
47
,
47
,
48
,

,
51
]
),
the physical relevance of these solutions might reside in the dark sector.
