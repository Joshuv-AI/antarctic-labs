An efficient triple-domain YOLOv8 for real-time aluminum profile defect detection
Authors: Mohammad Rostami, Afsaneh Fatemi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Surface defect detection in aluminum profiles remains challenging due to complex textures, illumination variations, reflective noise, and subtle small-scale defects. Conventional YOLO-based detectors rely primarily on spatial-domain features and fail to exploit complementary frequency and gradient information, which is essential for detecting weak, texture-oriented, and elongated defects. This paper proposes Adaptive Triple-Domain YOLOv8, a real-time framework that integrates spatial, frequency (Discrete Wavelet Transform), and gradient (Gabor filter) features within a unified architecture. An adaptive attention-based fusion module dynamically integrates multi-domain features, enabling defect-specific discrimination while preserving high inference speed. Experiments on the Tianchi benchmark demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms spatial-domain, dual-domain, and recent detectors, achieving a mAP@0.5 of 96.7% and a mAP@0.5:0.95 of 73.2% at over 100 FPS. The method significantly improves the detection on low-contrast, texture-dominated, and elongated defects, providing a favorable balance between detection accuracy and real-time efficiency for industrial aluminum surface inspection.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58308-1.

Introduction

Surface defect detection is a cornerstone of industrial quality assurance, particularly in aluminum profile manufacturing, where small defects can compromise structural integrity, aesthetic quality, and end-user safety. Recent studies highlight that defects such as scratches, rolling marks, pits, cracks, oxidation, and oil stains remain among the most challenging categories to detect reliably under real production conditions1–7. Traditional manual inspection, reliant on human operators, suffers from subjectivity, visual fatigue, and limited throughput, rendering it inadequate for today’s high-speed, precision-driven production lines. The advent of deep learning has revolutionized this field by enabling fully automated, data-driven visual inspection systems8,9.

Among deep learning-based frameworks, the YOLO1 family has become the standard for real-time industrial applications due to its unified one-stage architecture and favorable speed-accuracy trade-off. From YOLOv310 to the current YOLOv811, continuous improvements in backbones, PAN-FPN necks, and detection heads have significantly enhanced multi-scale detection capabilities12–14. Recent adaptations for aluminum defect detection further demonstrate YOLOv8’s effectiveness in industrial scenarios, incorporating attention mechanisms, lightweight feature extractors, and enhanced context modeling to improve defect sensitivity4,15–24. Spatial-domain enhancements to YOLO-based detectors have also demonstrated improved performance. For example, YOLO-PDC25 integrates deformable convolutions into YOLOv7 to better model geometric variations in surface defects, while EMSPENet26, provides an efficient multi-scale perceptual enhancement network for aluminum defect detection. Similarly, TRIDENet21 introduces selective channel activation and a task-gated detection head to suppress noise and decouple regression and classification for lightweight defect detection, while primarily relying on multi-scale spatial feature fusion. Nevertheless, these methods operate solely in the spatial domain. Additional studies have introduced attention-enhanced CNN detectors, lightweight architectures, and transformer-inspired modules for industrial surface anomaly detection27–38, further demonstrating the rapid expansion of this research direction.

To overcome the limitations of purely spatial-domain features, researchers have increasingly explored complementary feature representations. Frequency-domain analysis based on wavelet transforms has proven particularly effective, as defects frequently appear as localized spectral irregularities. Studies combining DWT with YOLO variants have reported improved performance in steel39–41 and glass42 inspections. Similarly, DFRD-Net43 combines spatial convolution with learnable frequency-domain high-pass filtering in a dual-domain framework, but remains limited to spatial-frequency fusion. Concurrently, SDT-Net44 incorporates Sobel-based edge extraction within a YOLO-based detector to capture gradient information for steel defect detection, but relies on hierarchical edge pyramids rather than explicit frequency-domain analysis or adaptive multi-domain fusion. However, the application of these methods in aluminum profile inspection remains limited, and existing fusion strategies are typically fixed and unable to adapt to diverse defect patterns or integrate both frequency and gradient cues simultaneously.

In addition, gradient-domain features are highly sensitive to edge, line, and directional texture patterns, which are essential for detecting highly oriented defects such as scratches and rolling marks. Methods leveraging gradient-enhanced convolutional branches or Gabor filter-based feature extraction demonstrate significant improvements in preserving directional structures3,28,45,46. Yet, existing methods fail to fully exploit the complementary nature of spatial, frequency, and gradient cues within a unified detection architecture. This limitation leads to suboptimal performance in detecting low-contrast textures, repetitive patterns, and anisotropic defects, which are common challenges in aluminum profile inspection.

Existing wavelet-based approaches, such as SFC-YOLOv839 and Wavelet-FDA40, incorporate frequency-domain information into YOLO architectures but remain fundamentally dual-domain designs that rely on static concatenation or wavelet-attention mechanisms. These methods improve texture sensitivity but do not explicitly integrate directional gradient cues, limiting their ability to represent edge-oriented defect patterns. In contrast, the proposed model introduces a unified triple-domain framework that jointly exploits spatial, wavelet-frequency, and gradient (Gabor-based) cues through an adaptive attention-weighted fusion mechanism. The gradient domain is specifically designed to capture directional edge and orientation-aware features that are not explicitly modeled in prior dual-domain detectors, enabling a more comprehensive representation of surface defects across structural, textural, and edge characteristics.

To address these issues, the Efficient Triple-Domain YOLOv8 is introduced, integrating spatial, wavelet frequency, and Gabor gradient features through three parallel streams and an adaptive attention-based fusion module. This unified design enhances robustness across diverse defect types by leveraging the complementary strengths of the three domains. In this paper, the term “efficient” refers to a compact yet highly efficiency design that reduces model parameters, FLOPs, and training/inference time while maintaining higher detection accuracy than standard YOLOv8n and competitive performance with YOLOv8s, despite a moderate increase in parameters. Experimental results on the Tianchi benchmark show that the proposed model achieves notable improvements in detection precision and real-time efficiency, thereby advancing industrial surface defect inspection.

In this study, the task is formulated as an object detection problem in which the model predicts the spatial positions of defects using bounding boxes. The performance of the proposed method is evaluated using standard object detection metrics, including precision, recall, F1-score, and mean average precision (mAP). Proposed triple-domain adaptive YOLOv8 architecture

The proposed model adopts a triple-domain architecture for real-time detection of defects in aluminum profiles. After image preprocessing, three parallel processing streams operate simultaneously: (i) a spatial-domain stream based on the YOLOv8 backbone for extracting structural and contextual feature, (ii) a frequency-domain stream leveraging the discrete wavelet transform (DWT) to capture multi-resolution texture information, and (iii) a gradient-domain stream designed to extract directional edge and orientation-aware features using multi-scale Gabor filters. These complementary feature sets are integrated via an adaptive attention-based fusion mechanism, which dynamically learns the optimal weighting for each domain based on image content and defect features.

Unlike prior dual-domain detectors that combine spatial and frequency cues, the proposed architecture introduces an additional gradient-domain stream based on Gabor filtering to capture directional edge information. This stream complements both spatial-domain and wavelet-frequency-domain representations, while the adaptive attention-based fusion module dynamically balances the contributions of the three domains to produce a more discriminative representation for surface defects.

The fused multi-domain features are fed into the standard YOLOv8 neck and detection head to predict defect bounding boxes, class labels, and confidence scores. Figure 1 provides a schematic overview of the proposed triple-domain adaptive YOLOv8 architecture.

Compared with spatial-domain and dual-domain approaches, this design explicitly leverages complementary information across the spatial, frequency, and gradient domains, enabling more robust detection of low-contrast, texture-dominated, and elongated defects while preserving real-time inference efficiency.

Although the proposed architecture introduces three parallel processing streams, the additional components are intentionally designed to remain computationally efficient. Specifically, the wavelet and gradient branches employ shallow CNN structures with limited channel widths (32 → 64 → C/4), thereby introducing only a modest increase in model complexity. Moreover, both branches follow the same multi-scale hierarchy as the YOLOv8 backbone and primarily rely on simple resizing operations instead of deep convolutional stacks. The adaptive fusion mechanism is also implemented using a compact two-layer MLP with a hidden dimension of 128, adding only a minimal number of parameters to the overall network. Consequently, the proposed design enriches feature representation across spatial, frequency, and gradient domains while maintaining efficient computation. As demonstrated in the experimental evaluation, the resulting model maintains real-time inference capability while improving the robustness of defect detection.

Spatial-domain feature extraction with the YOLOv8 backbone

In the first processing stream of the proposed model, the standard YOLOv8 backbone is employed to extract spatial and structural features from the preprocessed input image (640 × 640 × 3). To preserve computational efficiency, the spatial-domain stream follows the original efficient design of the YOLOv8 backbone without introducing additional heavy blocks or channel expansions. The backbone begins with a stem consisting of two convolutional layers with a stride of 2, each followed by batch normalization and SiLU activation. This rapidly reduces the spatial dimensions while extracting preliminary features.

The backbone consists of four progressive stages, each comprising multiple C2f (Cross-Stage Partial with Feed-Forward) blocks and down-sampling convolutional layers. The core convolutional operation in these layers follows the sequence: convolution, batch normalization, and SiLU activation, formally expressed for layer l, as shown in Eq. Frequency-domain feature extraction via wavelet-CNN

In the second stream, a single-level two-dimensional discrete wavelet transform (2D-DWT) with the Haar basis is applied to the preprocessed grayscale image (640 × 640 × 1) to extract frequency-domain features. This stream is designed to remain computationally efficient, relying on a single-level wavelet decomposition and a shallow CNN with minimal channel width to avoid excessive computational overhead. A single-level 2D discrete wavelet transform (DWT) with the Haar basis is applied due to its low computational cost and suitability for real-time inference. Given a 640 × 640 input, it yields four 320 × 320 sub-bands: LL (low-frequency structural approximation), LH (horizontal details), HL (vertical details), and HH (diagonal details). The LL band captures low-frequency structure, while LH/HL/HH contain directional high-frequency details. This transformation is formally expressed as Eq. These characteristics are critical for detecting subtle, low-contrast, and directional defects such as parallel scratches, vertical cracks, and surface irregularities.

Each sub-band is processed by a lightweight CNN composed of two 3 × 3 convolutional layers with 32 and 64 channels respectively, followed by ReLU activation. A single-level wavelet decomposition is adopted to preserve sufficient spatial resolution for detecting small surface defects. Deeper decompositions progressively reduce the spatial size of the sub-bands (e.g., 160 × 160 or 80 × 80), which may lead to the loss of fine defect structures while increasing computational cost. Therefore, the single-level configuration provides an effective trade-off between frequency representation and spatial detail preservation.

After concatenation and channel alignment via a 1 × 1 convolution, the tensor (320 × 320×C) is resized using bilinear interpolation to generate three multi-scale features F3 (80 × 80×C), F4 (40 × 40×C), and F5 (20 × 20×C) aligned with YOLOv8 spatial levels P3, P4, P5. This multi-scale alignment ensures optimal correspondence between frequency-domain details at different resolutions and the corresponding spatial features.

Bilinear interpolation is adopted for feature alignment due to its computational efficiency and its widespread use in modern detection architectures such as FPN-based detectors and YOLO frameworks. Although interpolation does not introduce additional high-frequency information, it preserves the spatial structure of the feature maps while enabling efficient multi-scale alignment. Moreover, in the proposed architecture, high-frequency details are explicitly captured by the wavelet and gradient streams before the resizing stage, which mitigates potential information degradation during interpolation.

Gradient-domain feature extraction

In the proposed triple-domain framework, the gradient domain acts as the third parallel stream. To keep the overall model compact, the gradient domain employs a lightweight Gabor-CNN encoder, avoiding deep stacks and wide channels while still capturing rich orientation-sensitive features. The motivation for gradient-based features stems from the fact that many defects in aluminum profiles, such as narrow scratches, surface cracks, and rolling marks, exhibit directional structures and edge patterns. These characteristics are often weakly represented in RGB spatial features and may not be fully captured by frequency-domain features alone. Gradient features emphasize local intensity transitions and edge orientations while being inherently robust to illumination variations, making them effective for modeling fine-grained and oriented defects.

The input to this stream is the normalized grayscale image (640 640 × 1) obtained from the preprocessing stage. Grayscale conversion reduces computational overhead and directs focus exclusively toward structural variations, excluding chromatic information. To capture multi-scale and multi-directional gradient information, a bank of Gabor filters is employed. Gabor filters provide optimal localization in the spatial and frequency domains and are widely recognized for their effectiveness in modeling oriented textures and directional edges prevalent in industrial surface defects. Here, λ denotes the wavelength (scale), θ specifies the orientation, ψ is the phase offset, σ determines the Gaussian envelope width, and γ governs the spatial aspect ratio. In this model, four orientations (θ ∈ {0°, 45°, 90°, 135°}) and two wavelength scales (λ₁ = 4, λ₂ = 8) are used, generating a total of eight Gabor filters that respond to defects with diverse orientations and sizes.

In this implementation, the Gabor filter bank uses the following parameter configuration: wavelength λ ∈ {4, 8}, orientation θ ∈ {0°, 45°, 90°, 135°}, Gaussian envelope σ = 2.0, phase offset ψ = 0, and aspect ratio γ = 0.5. This configuration produces eight directional filters that capture defect structures at multiple orientations and scales. In this work, the Gabor filter parameters are intentionally kept fixed to maintain the efficient nature of the framework and provide stable low-level directional priors for edge and orientation representation. These fixed filters act as efficient handcrafted operators that capture fundamental gradient structures without introducing additional learnable parameters. The gradient branch is designed to complement rather than replace the learnable spatial and frequency streams. Moreover, using fixed filters avoids the extra computational cost of learnable orientation-aware filters, thereby preserving the efficiency required for real-time industrial defect detection.

Each Gabor filter produces a directional response map highlighting intensity transitions aligned with its orientation and scale. These maps are concatenated along the channel dimension to form multi-channel gradient features. The concatenated maps are formed as Eq. To suppress noise and extract higher-level abstractions, the concatenated tensor is processed by a lightweight convolutional neural network consisting of two 3 × 3 convolutional layers with ReLU activation, followed by a 1 × 1 convolution for channel compression. This lightweight design reduces network depth and width while maintaining effective directional feature extraction, thereby improving the overall computational efficiency of the model. (13).13\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{F}}_{{{\mathrm{grad}}}} = {\text{ C}}_{{{\mathrm{grad}}}} \left( {\mathrm{G}} \right)$$\end{document}

where Cgrad denotes the lightweight gradient CNN. Similar to the frequency domain, the CNN encoder in the gradient domain is deliberately kept shallow, consisting of only two convolutional layers followed by a 1 × 1 projection, ensuring minimal parameter overhead. To align with the multi-scale hierarchy of the YOLOv8 spatial stream (P3, P4, P5), the encoded gradient features undergo resolution-adaptive resizing / downsampling and 1 × 1 channel adjustment, producing three gradient feature maps G3, G4, and G5 at resolutions 80 × 80, 4 × 40, and 20 × 20, respectively. These maps match the spatial resolution and channel dimensionality of their spatial and frequency counterparts and are forwarded to the Adaptive Triple-Domain Fusion module.

The gradient branch initially preserves the full spatial resolution of the input image. After applying eight Gabor filters, the response maps are concatenated to form a tensor of size 640 × 640 × 8, which is then processed by a lightweight CNN encoder to produce a feature map of size 640 × 640×C. This feature map is subsequently resized to match the YOLOv8 detection scales (P3, P4, P5), generating three aligned representations of 80 × 80×C, 40 × 40×C, and 20 × 20×C. This step ensures dimensional compatibility with the spatial and frequency streams without introducing unnecessary intermediate upsampling.

By modeling directional edges and gradient information, the gradient stream significantly enhances the model’s sensitivity to elongated, oriented, and fine-grained defects that are challenging for spatial-domain or frequency-domain approaches. When integrated with spatial and frequency features through adaptive fusion, these gradient features enable a more comprehensive and discriminative characterization of aluminum surface defects under real-world industrial conditions. Adaptive triple-domain feature fusion

The proposed adaptive triple-domain fusion module integrates spatial-domain, frequency-domain, and gradient-domain features within a unified framework. It employs an attention-based weighting mechanism that dynamically determines the contribution of each domain based on image content and defect-specific characteristics, enabling discriminative and context-aware feature aggregation.

At each scale i ∈ {3, 4, 5}, the module receives three scale-aligned feature maps: the spatial-domain features Pi from the YOLOv8 backbone, frequency-domain features Fi from the Wavelet-CNN stream, and gradient-domain features Gi from the Gabor-CNN stream, corresponding to small, medium, and large defect patterns. To enable concatenation and weighted fusion, the frequency-domain and gradient-domain features are projected using 1 × 1 convolutions to match the channel dimension C of the spatial stream. The fusion process is performed independently at the three scales, producing the fused feature maps Ffused3, Ffused4, and Ffused5. A key advantage of this adaptive strategy is its ability to automatically identify the dominant feature domain for different defect types. For example, elongated and oriented defects, such as scratches, rolling marks, benefit from gradient-domain features; low-contrast or texture-dominated anomalies rely more heavily on frequency-domain cues; and defects with clear geometric boundaries are better captured by spatial-domain features.

Unlike conventional channel or spatial attention mechanisms, such as SE-Net and CBAM30,50, which operate within a single feature map, the proposed mechanism operates across domains and determines the relative importance of spatial-domain, frequency-domain, and gradient-domain features. The combination of GAP and a lightweight MLP provides an efficient global weighting mechanism for predicting domain-level importance coefficients with minimal computational overhead.

In contrast to earlier frequency-enhanced YOLO variants that use fixed or predetermined fusion rules, the proposed module adaptively learns domain-specific fusion weights, enabling the network to emphasize gradient cues for elongated defects and frequency-domain cues for subtle texture irregularities. This results in more flexible and context-aware feature integration.

Defect detection head

In the final stage of the proposed architecture, the enhanced multi-scale feature maps Pout3, Pout4, and Pout5 produced by the PAN-FPN neck, are fed into the YOLOv8 detection head. The proposed framework retains the original anchor-free detection mechanism of YOLOv8, which predicts object locations directly from feature map points without relying on predefined anchor boxes. This design simplifies the detection process and improves generalization while reducing anchor-related hyperparameters.

For each scale level i ∈ {3, 4, 5}, the feature map Pouti is first processed by a 3 × 3 convolutional layer and then passed to a decoupled head with to two parallel branches: a bounding-box regression branch and a classification branch.

Regression branch. The regression branch predicts the distances from each feature-map location to the four sides of the bounding box. For each grid location (x, y) on the feature map, the network outputs a distance vector d = (dl, dt, dr, db), where dl, dt, dr, and db denote the distances from the point to the left, top, right, and bottom sides of the predicted bounding box, respectively. Experimental results and analysis

This section presents a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed Adaptive Triple‑Domain YOLOv8 framework. We first describe the experimental setup, dataset, and evaluation metrics. Then, we conduct comparative experiments, ablation studies, and class‑wise performance analysis to validate the effectiveness and efficiency of our method.

Experimental data and conditions

The following subsections describe the hardware and software environment, the dataset used for training and evaluation, and the performance metrics adopted to evaluate the proposed model.

Experimental environment

All experiments were conducted using PyTorch 2.0 as the deep learning framework. Training and inference were performed on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU with CUDA 11.7 and an Intel Core i7-12700 K CPU running Ubuntu 20.04 LTS. Models were initialized with ImageNet-pretrained weights, and the AdamW optimizer was employed with an initial learning rate of 1 × 10⁻³, a weight decay of 5 × 10⁻⁴, and a cosine annealing learning rate scheduler. Input images were consistently resized to 640 × 640 pixels using the letterbox strategy. A batch size of 16 was applied throughout training, and each model was trained for 300 epochs. To enhance generalization and robustness, standard data augmentation techniques such as Mosaic augmentation, random scaling, horizontal flipping, and color jittering were incorporated during the training process.

During training and inference, the detection settings followed the standard YOLOv8 configuration. The confidence threshold was set to 0.25, and the Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) threshold was set to 0.50 to remove redundant bounding boxes. The maximum number of detections per image was limited to 300 following the standard YOLOv8 inference configuration. The training objective followed the default YOLOv8 loss formulation, which combines CIoU loss for bounding-box regression, Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss for classification, and Distribution Focal Loss (DFL) for improved bounding-box localization.

Dataset description

All experiments were conducted on the Tianchi Aluminum Profile Surface Defect Dataset, a widely used public benchmark specifically designed for evaluating surface inspection methods in aluminum profile manufacturing. This dataset consists of high-resolution grayscale industrial images captured under realistic production conditions, and includes a diverse range of defect categories, such as scratches, rolling marks, oxidation, oil stains, cracks, pits, and non-conductive regions51.

After preprocessing and data cleaning, a total of 10,500 images were used in this study. These images include various challenging conditions such as illumination variations, reflective surfaces, and complex background patterns commonly observed in industrial environments. The dataset provides a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating the robustness and generalization ability of the proposed method.

Dataset preparation and class distribution

The resulting dataset includes nine distinct defect categories: longitudinal scratch, transverse scratch, rolling mark, non-conductive, oil stain, oxidation, crack, pit, and edge damage. To ensure balanced evaluation, the dataset was divided on a class-wise basis into training, validation, and testing subsets with a fixed ratio of 70%, 15%, and 15%, respectively. A stratified splitting strategy was used to preserves balanced class distributions across all subsets. This approach ensures a fair evaluation of the model while reflecting the diversity and complexity of real industrial inspection scenarios.

Bounding boxes are annotated only for defective regions, while all remaining areas are treated as normal background during training. The dataset was split at the image level, ensuring that no images are shared between the training, validation, and testing subsets, thereby preventing any potential data leakage. Evaluation metrics

To thoroughly assess the detection performance, robustness, and computational efficiency of the proposed Adaptive Triple-Domain YOLOv8 framework, a comprehensive set of quantitative metrics was employed. These metrics assess detection precision, speed, and real-time suitability, enabling a comparison with existing approaches.

In this study, all evaluation metrics are defined in the context of object detection, where defect instances are evaluated using bounding boxes on aluminum profile surfaces. A predicted box is considered a true positive if its Intersection-over-Union (IoU) with the corresponding ground-truth box exceeds a specified threshold; otherwise, it is counted as a false positive, while unmatched ground-truth boxes are treated as false negatives. Precision, recall, and F1-score are computed based on these definitions. The mAP@0.5 metric is calculated at an IoU threshold of 0.50, and mAP@0.5:0.95 is obtained by averaging AP over IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95 with a step of 0.05. These criteria ensure a clear and consistent interpretation of the reported performance values.

Multi-domain comparative analysis

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed Adaptive Triple-Domain YOLOv8 framework, a series of comparative experiments were conducted by comparing it with carefully selected baseline models derived from the same architectural family. Specifically, the following four model variants were constructed and compared:

Baseline YOLOv8 (Spatial)

the standard YOLOv8 detector relying exclusively on spatial-domain image features.

Dual-Domain YOLOv8 (Spatial + Frequency)

an enhanced variant incorporating a wavelet-based frequency branch with dual-domain fusion, designed to isolate the impact of frequency-domain features.

Dual-Domain YOLOv8 (Spatial + Gradient)

a variant equipped with a Gabor-based gradient branch, allowing the evaluation of gradient-domain directional information.

Adaptive Triple-Domain YOLOv8 (Spatial + Frequency + Gradient)

the complete proposed model, which jointly integrates spatial, frequency, and gradient features through an adaptive attention-based fusion mechanism.

This systematic experimental design enables a clear investigation of how each additional domain contributes to detection accuracy while affecting inference speed and real-time efficiency. The results provide strong evidence that the explicit modeling of complementary spatial, frequency, and gradient features substantially enhances the detection of subtle, low-contrast, and elongated defects on aluminum surfaces while maintaining high computational efficiency suitable for industrial deployment.

Efficiency definition and computational characterization

In this work, the term efficient refers to a model that maintains strong detection accuracy with relatively low computational cost. This property is evaluated using three common efficiency indicators: the number of trainable parameters, computational cost measured by FLOPs, and the inference speed measured in FPS, which together reflect model compactness, processing cost, and real-time suitability.

The proposed Triple-Domain YOLOv8 contains 14.9 M parameters, requires 35.6 G FLOPs, and achieves an inference speed of 103 FPS. For comparison, Lightweight YOLOv818 uses fewer parameters (9.3 M) but achieves lower detection accuracy, whereas more complex models, such as MCH YOLOv1237 require 17.3 M parameters and 45.8 G FLOPs and operate at a lower inference speed of 97 FPS. This places the proposed model between compact and higher-capacity architectures, achieving a favorable trade-off between computational cost and detection accuracy.

These results, consistent with the comparisons reported later in Table 10, indicate that the proposed architecture achieves a balanced trade-off among model size, computational complexity, and detection accuracy. The ablation results demonstrate the complementary nature of the three feature domains. Adding the frequency domain (P + F) yields a 4.2% absolute improvement in mAP@0.5 over the spatial-only YOLOv8 baseline, indicating that wavelet-based spectral features enhance the detection of low-contrast and texture-oriented defects. A notable observation is the relatively lower performance of the F + G configuration compared with models that include spatial features. Although frequency-domain features effectively capture texture regularities and periodic patterns, and gradient-domain features emphasize edge orientation and local intensity transitions, these representations lack explicit spatial structural information. As a result, the detector receives insufficient cues for precise object localization.

Without spatial-domain features, the model suffers from degraded bounding-box regression and inaccurate object localization. In contrast, spatial features extracted from the backbone preserve the structural layout and positional context of surface defects. When spatial information is integrated with frequency or gradient representations (e.g., P + F or P + G), the detector benefits from both localization cues and complementary appearance descriptors, leading to significantly improved detection accuracy.

The proposed Triple-Domain (P + F+G) achieves the highest overall detection performance, with 96.7% mAP@0.5 and 73.2% mAP@0.5:0.95, while maintaining real-time throughput at 103 FPS. These findings demonstrate that the proposed adaptive attention-based fusion strategy effectively leverages the complementary strengths of spatial, frequency, and gradient representations, producing a more robust feature space for industrial defect detection.

Conclusion

This study has presented an Adaptive Triple-Domain YOLOv8 framework for real-time surface defect detection in aluminum profiles. The proposed architecture jointly exploits spatial, frequency, and gradient domains information by integrating standard convolutional features with the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) and multi-scale Gabor filters. This multi-domain representation enables the model to capture complementary characteristics of surface defects: spatial-domain features describe structures layouts, frequency-domain features highlight subtle texture irregularities, and gradient-domain features emphasize directional edges and patterns. An adaptive attention-based fusion module is employed to combine these heterogeneous features, helping the network distinguish diverse defect types under challenging industrial conditions such as illumination variation, reflections, and complex backgrounds.

Experimental evaluations on the Tianchi Aluminum Profile Surface Defect Dataset indicate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The model achieves 96.7% mAP@0.5 and 73.2% mAP@0.5:0.95, while maintaining real-time inference at 100 FPS. Ablation studies further confirm that each domain contributes to improved detection performance and that the adaptive fusion strategy plays an important role in balancing detection accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed design achieves a favorable trade-off between detection accuracy and computational cost, maintaining real-time performance while significantly improving detection precision, which is essential for deployment in real-world industrial inspection systems.

Despite these advantages, the current implementation uses grayscale inputs in the frequency and gradient branches, which may limit the exploitation of color cues for certain defects. Future work will explore color-aware wavelet and Gabor representations, learnable multi-scale fusion strategies, and the integration of additional sensing modalities such as polarization imaging. Another promising direction is channel-wise domain fusion, in which independent weights are assigned to each channel within each domain to enable more fine-grained feature reweighting. Overall, the proposed Adaptive Triple-Domain YOLOv8 provides an effective framework for aluminum surface inspection and offers a promising basis for extending multi-domain defect detection to other industrial materials.
