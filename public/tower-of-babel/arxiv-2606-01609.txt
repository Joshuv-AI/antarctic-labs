The Unitarity of Arthur Packets for Real Reductive Groups
Jeffrey Adams, Andrei Ionov, Lucas Mason-Brown, and David Vogan

Abstract
. Let G G be a connected reductive algebraic group defined over ℝ \mathbb{R} . In [ 5 , 6 ] , Arthur conjectured the existence of certain packets of irreducible admissible representations of G ⁡ ( ℝ ) G(\mathbb{R}) satisfying various remarkable properties. These packets were given a precise definition in [ 2 ] in terms of microlocal geometry on a space of Langlands parameters. A longstanding conjecture, originating in [ 5 ] , is that all Arthur packets consist of unitary representations. In this paper, we prove this conjecture in general. The main new idea is a ‘Jordan decomposition’ for Arthur packets: a canonical two-step process for realizing an arbitrary Arthur packet via real parabolic and cohomological induction from a unipotent Arthur packet for a certain Levi subgroup. This process is analogous to the decomposition of an element of a complex algebraic group as a (unique) commuting product of elliptic, hyperbolic, and unipotent parts. Using our Jordan decomposition, we reduce the question of unitarity to the case of unipotent Arthur packets, where the answer is already known (by work of Adams-Arancibia-Mezo, Adams-van Leeuwen-Miller-Vogan, Arthur, Barbasch, Barbasch-Ma-Sun-Zhu, and Davis-Mason-Brown). As an application of the same methods, we also give a proof of Jiang’s conjecture for real reductive groups, which gives an upper bound on the wavefront sets of the members of an Arthur packet in terms of the Barbasch-Vogan dual of the Arthur S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℂ ) SL_{2}(\mathbb{C}) .

Our strategy for proving Theorem
1.0.3
in general is to show that every Arthur packet can be obtained in a canonical fashion from a unipotent Arthur packet for a suitable Levi subgroup by means of several unitarity-preserving constructions, namely
real parabolic
and
cohomological induction
. The recipe for this induction is in some sense encoded in the structure of the Arthur parameter. Given an Arthur parameter
\psi:W_{\mathbb{R}}\times SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
, the image of
\mathbb{R}_{+}\subset W_{\mathbb{R}}
defines a Levi subgroup of
{}^{\vee}G

(1.0.3)

{}^{\vee}M_{1}:=Z_{{}^{\vee}G}(\psi(\mathbb{R}_{+})).

Since
\mathbb{R}_{+}
commutes with
S^{1}\subset W_{\mathbb{R}}
, the image of
S^{1}
under
\psi
belongs to
{}^{\vee}M_{1}
. The co-character
\psi|_{S^{1}}\colon S^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}M_{1}
defines a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading of
{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{1}

{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{1}:=\bigoplus{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{1,n},\qquad{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{1,n}:=\{X\in{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{1}\mid\Ad(\psi(z))X=z^{n}X,\quad\forall z\in S^{1}\},

and hence a parabolic subalgebra

(1.0.4)

{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{q}_{2}:={}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{2}\oplus{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{u}_{2},\qquad{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{2}:={}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{1,0}=Z_{{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{1}}(\psi(S^{1})),\qquad{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{u}_{2}:=\bigoplus_{n>0}{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{m}_{1,n}.

Let
{}^{\vee}M_{2}
denote the connected subgroup of
{}^{\vee}M_{1}
corresponding to

. Since

is a

The subgroups

2.5.
Parabolic induction

Let
G(\mathbb{R})
be a real form of
G
. Choose a maximal compact subgroup
K(\mathbb{R})\subset G(\mathbb{R})
and let
\theta
be the corresponding Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}
. Choose a parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathfrak{g}
with Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
. Let
L
(resp.
Q
) denote the connected subgroup of
G
corresponding to
\mathfrak{l}
(resp.
\mathfrak{q}
). Assume throughout this section that
\mathfrak{l}
is
\theta
-stable.

Let
2\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}
denote the determinant character of
\mathfrak{u}
:

2\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}(l)=\det(\Ad(l)|_{\mathfrak{u}}),\qquad l\in L.

Consider the fibered product
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\bullet)^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}}

The left vertical map
\widetilde{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}L

\widetilde{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a genuine character of
\widetilde{L}
, which we will denote by

. By the construction of

, there is a natural covering map

. By pullback along this covering map, we can regard
\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}
as a one-dimensional projective
(\mathfrak{l},L\cap K)
-module.

Dually, we can consider the algebraic co-character corresponding to the sum of roots in
\mathfrak{u}

(2.5.1)

(2\rho_{\mathfrak{u}})^{\vee}\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}L

and the order-2 element

(2.5.2)

z(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}):=(2\rho_{\mathfrak{u}})^{\vee}(-1)\in Z({}^{\vee}L)^{\theta_{Z}}

Note that

depends only on
\mathfrak{l}
(i.e., it is independent of the choice of
\mathfrak{q}
) and that the projective
(l,L\cap K)
-module
\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}
is of type

Now fix a finite-order element

also as an element of

via the natural inclusion

\mathcal{R}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\colon M^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}})}(\mathfrak{l},L\cap K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}M^{z}(\mathfrak{g},K).

This functor is built out of several ingredients:

•

Let
(\bullet)^{\#}\colon M^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}})}(\mathfrak{l},L\cap K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}M^{z}(\mathfrak{l},L\cap K)
denote the functor given by tensoring with

•

Let

•

Let

•

Let

denote the Bernstein functor, see
[

.

Now we define

\mathcal{R}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}:=\Pi\circ P\circ\mathcal{F}\circ(\bullet)^{\#}\colon M^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}})}(\mathfrak{l},L\cap K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}M^{z}(\mathfrak{g},K)

Note that
(\bullet)^{\#}
and

are exact and

and

are right exact. Thus,

is right exact. Since the categories

and

have enough projective objects, there are left-derived functors

Theorem 2.5.1

(
[
26
, Chapter 6.3]
)
.

The functors

preserve infinitesimal character and take finite-length modules to finite-length modules. Moreover,
L_{i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}=0
for

.

Thanks to Theorem

, we can define a group homomorphism

R^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\colon KM^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}})}_{fl}(\mathfrak{l},L\cap K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}KM^{z}_{fl}(\mathfrak{g},K),\qquad[\pi]\mapsto\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[L_{i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\pi)],

and hence a group homomorphism

R^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\colon K\Pi^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}})}(L(\mathbb{R}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}K\Pi^{z}(G(\mathbb{R})).

It will be convenient to define a version of parabolic induction in a setting which permits various real forms of
L

G

Let
G^{\Gamma}
be a weak extended group and let
L^{\Gamma}\subset G^{\Gamma}
be an extended Levi subgroup (this means that
L^{\Gamma}
is a weak extended group equipped with an injective
L

L^{\Gamma}\subset G^{\Gamma}
such that
L\subset G
is a Levi subgroup). Choose a parabolic subgroup

with Levi decomposition

, it is reasonable to say that

is ‘real’ or ‘
\theta
-stable’ with respect to the inclusion
L^{\Gamma}\subset G^{\Gamma}
. This is justified by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6.1
.

The following are equivalent:

(ia)

The involution
\sigma_{Z}
of
Z(L)
determined by
L^{\Gamma}
fixes the element
\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}\in Z(\mathfrak{l})
.

(iia)

There is a strong real form
\delta\in L^{\Gamma}\setminus L
with
\delta^{2}\in Z(G)
such that
\Ad(\delta)Q=Q
.

(iiia)

Every strong real form
\delta\in L^{\Gamma}\setminus L
with
\delta^{2}\in Z(G)
has
\Ad(\delta)Q=Q
.

Also, the following are equivalent:

(ib)

\sigma_{Z}(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}})=-\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}
.

(iib)

There is a strong real form
\delta\in L^{\Gamma}\setminus L
with
\delta^{2}\in Z(G)
such that
\Ad(\delta)Q=Q^{op}
.

(iiib)

Every strong real form
\delta\in L^{\Gamma}\setminus L
with
\delta^{2}\in Z(G)
has
\Ad(\delta)Q=Q^{op}
.

In the case of (ia)-(iiia), we say that
Q
is ‘real’. In the case of (ib)-(iiib), we say that
Q
is ‘

-stable’.

Proof.

We will prove the equivalence of conditions (ia)-(iiia). The equivalence of conditions (ib)-(iiib) is proved by a similar argument.

Let
\delta\in L^{\Gamma}\setminus L
be a strong real form of
L^{\Gamma}
with
\delta^{2}\in Z(G)
. Then
\Ad(\delta)Q
is a parabolic subgroup of
G
with Levi decomposition
L\Ad(\delta)U
. Clearly,
\rho_{\Ad(\delta)\mathfrak{u}}=\Ad(\delta)\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}=\sigma_{Z}\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}
. On the other hand, since
\Ad(\delta)Q
is a parabolic subgroup, it is determined by the element
\rho_{\Ad(\delta)\mathfrak{u}}
(it is the connected subgroup of
G
corresponding to the nonnegative weights for
\rho_{\Ad(\delta)\mathfrak{u}}
). So
\Ad(\delta)Q=Q
if and only if
\sigma_{Z}(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}})=\rho_{\mathfrak{u}}
. This proves the equivalence of (ia)-(iiia).
∎

0&else\end{cases}

can be regarded as a strong real form of

2.7.
The Langlands classification via standard limit characters

In this section, we will recall the classification of irreducible admissible representations in terms of
standard limit characters
of Cartan subgroups. This formulation of the Langlands classification does not involve the dual group.

Definition 2.7.1

(
[
2
, Definition 11.13]
)
.

Let
G^{\Gamma}
be a weak extended group, and let
G(\mathbb{R})
be a real form of
G
in the inner class defined by
G^{\Gamma}
. A
limit character for
G(\mathbb{R})
is a quadruple

\Lambda=(H(\mathbb{R}),\Lambda^{can},\Delta_{i\mathbb{R}}^{+},\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}^{+})

subject to the following conditions

(i)

H(\mathbb{R})
is the group of real points of a maximal torus
H
of
G
.

(ii)

\Lambda^{can}
is an irreducible projective representation
H(\mathbb{R})
.

(iii)

\Delta_{i\mathbb{R}}^{+}\subset\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a system of positive imaginary roots.

(iv)

\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\subset\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a system of positive real roots.

Suppose
z
is a finite-order element in
Z({}^{\vee}G)^{\theta_{Z}}
. We can regard
z
as an element of
({}^{\vee}H)^{\theta_{Z}}
via the natural inclusion
Z({}^{\vee}G)^{\theta_{Z}}\subseteq({}^{\vee}H)^{\theta_{Z}}
. We say that
\Lambda
is of
type
z
if
\Lambda^{can}
is of type
zz(\rho)
. There are various additional conditions which we may choose to impose on
\Lambda
:

(v)

We say that
\Lambda
is
standard
if

\langle d\Lambda^{can},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Delta_{i\mathbb{R}}^{+}.

(vi)

We say that
\Lambda
is
nonzero
if the following condition is satisfied: let
\alpha
be a simple root in
\Delta_{i\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that

\langle d\Lambda^{can},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=0.

Then
\alpha
is noncompact.

(vii)

We say that
\Lambda
is
final
if the following condition is satisfied: let
\alpha
be a real root such that

\langle d\Lambda^{can},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=0.

Then
\alpha
does not satisfy the parity condition for
\Lambda
(see
[
2
, Definition 11.10]
).

There is a notion of
equivalence
of limit characters for
G(\mathbb{R})
, see
[
2
, Definition 11.6]
. Write
L^{z}(G(\mathbb{R}))
for the set of equivalence classes of final nonzero standard type-
z
limit characters for
G(\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 2.7.2
.

Let
G^{\Gamma}
be a weak extended group. A
limit character for
G^{\Gamma}
is a pair
(\delta,\Lambda)
subject to the following conditions

(i)

\delta
is a strong real form of
G^{\Gamma}
.

(ii)

\Lambda
is a limit character for
G(\mathbb{R},\delta)
.

Two such pairs
(\delta,\Lambda)
and
(\delta^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})
are
equivalent
if there is an element
g\in G
such that
g\delta g^{-1}=\delta^{\prime}
and
\Ad(g)\Lambda
is equivalent to
\Lambda^{\prime}
. Suppose
z
is a finite-order element in
Z({}^{\vee}G)^{\theta_{Z}}
. We say that
(\delta,\Lambda)
is of type
z
(resp. final, resp. nonzero, resp. standard) if
\Lambda
is of type
z
(resp. final, resp. nonzero, resp. standard). Write
L^{z}(G^{\Gamma})
for the set of equivalence classes of final nonzero standard type-
z
limit characters for
G^{\Gamma}
.

The idea (made precise in Remark
2.7.4
below) is that a standard limit character is exactly what is needed to specify a cuspidal parabolic subgroup
Q(\mathbb{R})=MAN(\mathbb{R})
of
G(\mathbb{R})
, a limit of discrete series representation
\pi_{M}
of
M
, and a character
\nu
of
A
. An induced representation like
\Ind_{MAN(\mathbb{R})}^{G(\mathbb{R})}(\pi_{M}\otimes\nu\otimes 1)
is the starting point for the original Langlands classification. The
nonzero
condition is the same as the requirement that
\pi_{M}\neq 0
; the
final
condition eliminates representations like the reducible unitary principal series of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

We will now explain how to attach representations to standard limit characters. Suppose
\Lambda=(H(\mathbb{R}),\Lambda^{can},\Delta_{i\mathbb{R}}^{+},\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}^{+})
is a standard type-
z
limit character for
G(\mathbb{R})
. Choose a Cartan involution
\theta
of
G(\mathbb{R})
which preserves
H(\mathbb{R})
, and a system of positive roots
\Delta^{+}\subset\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
satisfying the following conditions

(1)

\Delta_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\subseteq\Delta^{+}
.

(2)

\Delta_{i\mathbb{R}}^{+}\subseteq\Delta^{+}
.

(3)

If
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
and
\langle d\Lambda^{can},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

\mathrm{Re}\langle\theta d\Lambda^{can}-d\Lambda^{can},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\geq 0.

(4)

\Delta^{+}\setminus\Delta_{i\mathbb{R}}^{+}
is
-\theta
-stable.

Let
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
be the Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to
\Delta^{+}
. Form the modules

L_{i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\Lambda^{can})\in M^{z}(\mathfrak{g},K),\qquad i\geq 0.

as in Section
2.5
. Then
L_{i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\Lambda^{can})=0
unless

(this is
[
4

; It follows easily from the transitivity of induction and Propositions
2.5.2
and
2.5.6
). The

attached to

is the type-
z
projective
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module

Let
\pi(\Lambda)
denote the co-socle of

, i.e. the largest completely reducible quotient of
M(\Lambda)
. We note that
M(\Lambda)
(and hence
\pi(\Lambda)
) is independent of the choice of

(this is
[
4
, Proposition 8.20]
). Here is one version of the Langlands classification of irreducible representations.

Theorem 2.7.3

(
[
2
, Theorem 11.14]
)
.

Let
G^{\Gamma}
be a weak extended group and let
G(\mathbb{R})
be a real form of
G
in the inner class defined by
G^{\Gamma}
. Suppose
z
is a finite-order element in
Z({}^{\vee}G)^{\theta_{Z}}
. Then the following are true:

(i)

If
\Lambda\in L^{z}(G(\mathbb{R}))
is a final nonzero type-

standard limit character, then the corresponding representation
\pi(\Lambda)
is irreducible.

(ii)

The map
\Lambda\mapsto\pi(\Lambda)
defines a bijection

\pi\colon L^{z}(G(\mathbb{R}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pi^{z}(G(\mathbb{R}))

from equivalence classes of final nonzero type-

projective representations of

.

(iii)

If
\Lambda
is a standard type-

such that

M(\Lambda)=\bigoplus_{\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathtt{final}(\Lambda)}M(\Lambda^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad\pi(\Lambda)=\bigoplus_{\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathtt{final}(\Lambda)}\pi(\Lambda^{\prime}).

(iv)

The bijections in (ii) induce a bijection

\pi\colon L^{z}(G^{\Gamma})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pi^{z}(G^{\Gamma}),\qquad\pi(\delta,\Lambda)=(\delta,\pi(\Lambda))

from equivalence classes of final nonzero type-
z
standard limit characters for
G
to equivalence classes of irreducible type-
z
projective representations of strong real forms of
G
.

Remark 2.7.4
.

(\mathfrak{g},K)

Definition 2.8.1

(
[
14
]

[
26

.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. A

Let

This allows us to define

2.9.
Action of the Weyl group

Choose a maximal torus
H\subset G
, and let
X=X^{*}(H)\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
denote the character lattice. For each
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we obtain a system of
\lambda
-integral roots

\Delta(\lambda)=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We denote by
W(\lambda)
the Weyl group of
\Delta(\lambda)
. Note that

W(\lambda)=\{w\in W\mid w\lambda-\lambda\text{ is a sum of roots in }\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\}.

We also define

\Delta_{\lambda}=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=0\},\qquad\Delta_{\geq 0}(\lambda)=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

Note that
\Delta_{\geq 0}(\lambda)
is a parabolic subsystem of
\Delta(\lambda)
with Levi subsystem
\Delta_{\lambda}
. We denote by
W_{\lambda}
the Weyl group of
\Delta_{\lambda}
.

If
\lambda
is regular, it belongs to a unique Weyl chamber. If
\lambda^{\prime}\in\lambda+X
is a (possibly singular) weight belonging to this Weyl chamber, then we get a natural positive system for
\Delta_{\lambda^{\prime}}
:

\Delta^{+}_{\lambda^{\prime}}=\{\alpha\in\Delta_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle>0\},

and hence a set of simple reflections in
W_{\lambda^{\prime}}
. Note that the positive system
\Delta^{+}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
and the associated simple reflections in
W_{\lambda^{\prime}}
depend on the choice of
\lambda
(more precisely, on its Weyl chamber), although this is not reflected in our notation.

Definition 2.9.1

(
[
26
, Definition 7.2.8]
)
.

Suppose
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is regular. For each
w\in W(\lambda)
, let

T^{\lambda}_{w}:=T(\lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}w^{-1}\lambda),

a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear automorphism of
KM^{z}_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g},K)
. The map
w\mapsto T^{\lambda}_{w}
defines a representation of
W(\lambda)
on
KM^{z}_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g},K)
, called
coherent continuation
(at
\lambda
).

Sometimes, when the base point is understood, we will suppress the superscript
\lambda
from the notation
T^{\lambda}_{w}
.

Proposition 2.9.2

(
[
26
, Proposition 7.3.7 and Corollary 7.3.19]
)
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Choose
\lambda_{r}\in\lambda+X
regular with
\lambda
contained in its (closed) Weyl chamber. Consider the operator

T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)\colon KM^{z}_{\lambda_{r}}(\mathfrak{g},K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}KM^{z}_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g},K).

(Definition
2.8.3
). Then the following are true:

(i)

If
\pi\in\Pi^{z}_{\lambda_{r}}(\mathfrak{g},K)
, then
T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)\pi
is either irreducible or
0
.

(ii)

T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)
induces a bijection

\{\pi\in\Pi^{z}_{\lambda_{r}}(\mathfrak{g},K)\mid T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)\pi\neq 0\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pi^{z}_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g},K).

(iii)

If
\pi\in\Pi^{z}_{\lambda_{r}}(\mathfrak{g},K)
, then
T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)\pi=0
if and only if there is a simple reflection
s\in W_{\lambda}
such that
T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s}\pi=-\pi
.

(iv)

Let
\pi\in\Pi^{z}_{\lambda_{r}}(\mathfrak{g},K)
and suppose that
T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s}\pi\neq-\pi
for some simple reflection
s\in W_{\lambda}
. Then
T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s}\pi=\pi+U_{s}\pi
, where
U_{s}\pi
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-linear combination of irreducibles
\pi^{\prime}
such that
T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s}\pi^{\prime}=-\pi^{\prime}
.

From (iii) and (iv) of Proposition
2.9.2
we deduce the following lemma.

Lemma 2.9.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Choose
\lambda_{r}\in\lambda+X
regular with
\lambda
contained in its (closed) Weyl chamber. Then for any simple reflection
s\in W_{\lambda}
, we have

\ker{(1+T_{s}^{\lambda_{r}})}\subset\ker{T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)}.

Proof.

Suppose
M\in KM^{z}_{\lambda_{r}}(\mathfrak{g},K)
lies in the kernel of
1+T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s}
. Write
M
as a linear combination of irreducibles:

M=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi^{z}_{\lambda_{r}}(\mathfrak{g},K)}c_{\pi}\pi,\qquad c_{\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then by Proposition
2.9.2
(iii) and (iv), we have

0=(1+T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s})M=\sum_{(1+T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s})\pi\neq 0}c_{\pi}(1+T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s})\pi=\sum_{(1+T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s})\pi\neq 0}c_{\pi}\pi+\sum_{(1+T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s})\pi\neq 0}c_{\pi}U_{s}(\pi),

where each
U_{s}(\pi)
is a linear combination of irreducibles
\pi^{\prime}
such that
(1+T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s})\pi^{\prime}=0
. It follows that

0=\sum_{(1+T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s})\pi\neq 0}c_{\pi}\pi+\text{(linear combination of other irreducibles)}.

Hence,
c_{\pi}=0
whenever
(1+T^{\lambda_{r}}_{s})\pi\neq 0
and therefore
T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)M=0
, as desired.
∎

We will also need the following elementary facts about coherent continuation.

Lemma 2.9.4
.

Let
\lambda_{r}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be regular and let
\lambda\in\lambda_{r}+X
. Then the following are true:

(i)

for any regular
\lambda_{r}^{\prime}\in\lambda_{r}+X
, we have

T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)=T(\lambda_{r}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)\circ T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda_{r}^{\prime});

(ii)

for any
w\in W(\lambda)
, we have

T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)=T(w\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}w\lambda).

Proof.

Given
M\in KM_{\lambda_{r}}^{z}(\mathfrak{g},K)
, let
\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}
denote the unique coherent family based on
\lambda_{r}+X
such that
\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}(\lambda_{r})=M
. Let
N=\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}(\lambda_{r}^{\prime})=T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda_{r}^{\prime})M
and let
\Theta^{\lambda^{\prime}_{r}}
denote the unique coherent family based on
\lambda_{r}^{\prime}+X=\lambda_{r}+X
such that
\Theta^{\lambda^{\prime}_{r}}(\lambda_{r}^{\prime})=N
. Since

\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}(\lambda^{\prime}_{r})=N=\Theta^{\lambda^{\prime}_{r}}(\lambda_{r}^{\prime})
, the uniqueness of coherent families implies that
\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}=\Theta^{\lambda^{\prime}_{r}}
. Thus

T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)M=\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}(\lambda)=\Theta^{\lambda^{\prime}_{r}}(\lambda)=T(\lambda_{r}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)N=T(\lambda_{r}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)T(\lambda_{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda_{r}^{\prime})M.

This proves (i).

For (ii), we argue similarly. For any
\lambda^{\prime}\in\lambda+X
, let
\Theta^{w\lambda_{r}}(\lambda^{\prime})=\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}(w^{-1}\lambda^{\prime})
. Since the weights of any finite-dimensional
G
-representation are
W(\lambda)
-invariant, the map
\Theta^{w\lambda_{r}}\colon\lambda+X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}KM^{z}(\mathfrak{g},K)
is a coherent family. On the other hand,
\Theta^{w\lambda_{r}}(w\lambda_{r})=\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}(\lambda_{r})=M
, so by definition
T(w\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}w\lambda)M=\Theta^{w\lambda_{r}}(w\lambda)=\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}(\lambda)
. It follows that

T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)M=\Theta^{\lambda_{r}}(\lambda)=T(w\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}w\lambda)M.

This proves (ii).
∎

L\subset G

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}

(\mathfrak{g},K)

\lambda+X^{*}(L)

\Theta_{L}\colon\lambda+X^{*}(L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}KM^{z}(\mathfrak{g},K)

which is the restriction to
\lambda+X^{*}(L)

.

The following is an analog of Theorem
2.8.2
.

Proposition 2.10.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and let
M\in KM_{\lambda}^{z}(\mathfrak{g},K)
. Then there is an
L

\Theta_{L}\colon\lambda+X^{*}(L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}KM^{z}(\mathfrak{g},K)

such that
\Theta_{L}(\lambda)=M
. If
\lambda

\mathfrak{l}
(Definition
2.5.3
), then
\Theta_{L}

Proof.

By Theorem
2.8.2
, there is a (classical) coherent family
\Theta\colon\lambda+X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}KM(\mathfrak{g},K)
such that
\Theta(\lambda)=M
. Its restriction to

is an

Now suppose that
\lambda

\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathfrak{g}
with Levi factor

. Choose any

and
\lambda_{r}\in\lambda+X
regular with
\lambda
in its chamber. For the uniqueness claim, it suffices to show that

\ker{T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda)}\subseteq\ker{T(\lambda_{r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\lambda^{\prime})}.

Write
W_{L,\lambda^{\prime}}=W_{L}\cap W_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, where

is the Weyl group of

is a Levi subgroup of

. Now choose an element

such that

in its (closed) Weyl chamber, and let

be the simple reflections in

(corresponding to the positive system determined by

By Proposition

is spanned by irreducibles

for a simple reflection

3.
The Langlands classification via geometric parameters

In this section, we will recall the ABV formulation of the Langlands classification and the definition of Arthur packets. We will also define in Section
3.5
some convolution functors on categories of sheaves on spaces of Langlands parameters. We will show in Section
3.6
that these functors are dual, in a certain precise sense, to coherent continuation.

3.1.
Geometric parameters

In this subsection, we will recall the definition of the ABV parameter space. It is helpful to introduce some general notation for reductive groups. Suppose
A
is a complex reductive group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
and let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}
be a semisimple element. For each
z\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\mathfrak{a}_{z}
denote the
z
-eigenspace of the operator
\ad(\lambda)
. Consider the nilpotent subalgebra

\mathfrak{n}(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}\mathfrak{a}_{n}\subset\mathfrak{a}.

The
canonical flat through
\lambda
is the affine subspace of
\mathfrak{a}
defined by

\mathcal{F}(\lambda)=\lambda+\mathfrak{n}(\lambda).

If
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{a}
is a semisimple
A
-orbit, we write

\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{O})=\{\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{O}\}.

It is easy to see that the set
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{O})
is a partition of
\mathbb{O}
. Moreover, the map
e:\mathfrak{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}A
given by
e(\lambda)=\exp(2\pi i\lambda)
is constant on canonical flats. So if
\Lambda
is a canonical flat, we can write
e(\Lambda)
without ambiguity.

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
be a weak
E
-group. A
geometric parameter
for
{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
is a pair
(y,\Lambda)
consisting of a semisimple element
y\in{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}-{}^{\vee}G
and a canonical flat
\Lambda\subset{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{g}
such that
y^{2}=e(\Lambda)
.

The set of geometric parameters for
{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
is denoted
X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})
. Note that
{}^{\vee}G
acts by conjugation on
X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})
. Two geometric parameters are said to be
equivalent
if they are conjugate under this action.

The relation between geometric and classical Langlands parameters (Definition
2.3.2
) is described in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1.2

(
[
2
, Corollary 5.9]
)
.

For any Langlands parameter
\varphi\in P({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})
, there is a (unique) semisimple element
\lambda=\lambda(\varphi)\in{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{g}
(called the ‘infinitesimal character’ of
\varphi
) such that

\varphi(\exp(t))=\exp(t\lambda+\bar{t}\Ad(\varphi(j))\lambda),\qquad t\in\mathbb{C}.

The
{}^{\vee}G
-equivariant map

P({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}),\qquad\varphi\mapsto(\exp(\pi i\lambda)\varphi(j),\mathcal{F}(\lambda)).

induces a bijection on
{}^{\vee}G
-orbits

\Phi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})=P({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})/{}^{\vee}G\xrightarrow{\sim}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})/{}^{\vee}G.

Next, we recall some facts from
[
2
, Section 6]
about the local geometry of
X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})
. Fix a semisimple
{}^{\vee}G
-orbit
\mathbb{O}\subset{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{g}
, and consider the sets

X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})=\{(y,\Lambda)\in X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})\mid\Lambda\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{O})\},\qquad Y(\mathbb{O})=\{y\in{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}-{}^{\vee}G\mid y^{2}\in e(\mathbb{O})\}.

There are
{}^{\vee}G
-equivariant surjections

(\bullet)^{2}\colon Y(\mathbb{O})\twoheadrightarrow e(\mathbb{O}),\qquad e\colon\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{O})\twoheadrightarrow e(\mathbb{O}),

and hence a
{}^{\vee}G
-equivariant isomorphism

X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})\simeq Y(\mathbb{O})\times_{e(\mathbb{O})}\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{O}).

By
[
2
, Proposition 6.13]
, the set
Y(\mathbb{O})
decomposes into finitely many
{}^{\vee}G
-orbits, denoted

Y_{1},\ Y_{2},...,\ Y_{k}\subset Y(\mathbb{O}).

(of course, the integer
k
and the sets
Y_{i}
depend on the choice of
\mathbb{O}
, even though this is not reflected in the notation). Fix one such
{}^{\vee}G
-orbit
Y_{i}\subset Y(\mathbb{O})
, and consider the subset

(3.1.1)

X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})=\{(y_{i},\Lambda)\in X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})\mid y_{i}\in Y_{i},\ \Lambda\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{O})\}\subset X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O}).

There is a
{}^{\vee}G
-equivariant surjection

(\bullet)^{2}\colon Y_{i}\twoheadrightarrow e(\mathbb{O}),

and hence a
{}^{\vee}G
-equivariant isomorphism

X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})\simeq Y_{i}\times_{e(\mathbb{O})}\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{O}).

Now choose an element
(y_{i},\Lambda)\in X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
. Define the following subgroups of
{}^{\vee}G
:

{}^{\vee}R=Z_{{}^{\vee}G}(e(\Lambda))^{0},\qquad{}^{\vee}K_{i}=Z_{{}^{\vee}R}(y_{i}),\qquad{}^{\vee}P=\mathrm{Stab}_{{}^{\vee}R}(\Lambda).

Note that
{}^{\vee}R
is (the identity component of) a pseudo-Levi subgroup of
{}^{\vee}G
and
{}^{\vee}K_{i}
(resp.
{}^{\vee}P
) is a symmetric (resp. parabolic) subgroup of
{}^{\vee}R
. Sometimes we will write

{}^{\vee}R={}^{\vee}R(\Lambda),\qquad{}^{\vee}K_{i}={}^{\vee}K(y_{i}),\qquad{}^{\vee}P={}^{\vee}P(\Lambda)

to indicate the dependence of these subgroups on the geometric parameter
(y_{i},\Lambda)
.

There are
{}^{\vee}G
-equivariant isomorphisms

Y\simeq{}^{\vee}G/{}^{\vee}K_{i},\qquad\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{O})\simeq{}^{\vee}G/{}^{\vee}P,\qquad e(\mathbb{O})\simeq{}^{\vee}G/{}^{\vee}R

and the maps
(\bullet)^{2}\colon Y_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}e(\mathbb{O})
and
e\colon\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{O})\twoheadrightarrow e(\mathbb{O})
correspond to the inclusions
{}^{\vee}K_{i}\subset{}^{\vee}R
and
{}^{\vee}P\subset{}^{\vee}R
. Thus

\displaystyle X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq({}^{\vee}G/{}^{\vee}K_{i})\times_{({}^{\vee}G/{}^{\vee}R)}({}^{\vee}G/{}^{\vee}P)

\displaystyle\simeq{}^{\vee}G\times_{{}^{\vee}K_{i}}({}^{\vee}R/{}^{\vee}P)

In particular, there is an equivalence of categories (restrition to the fiber)

(3.1.2)

D^{b}C^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}C^{{}^{\vee}K_{i}^{alg}}({}^{\vee}R/{}^{\vee}P)

3.2.
Complete geometric parameters

Let
{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
be a weak
E
-group. Fix a semisimple
{}^{\vee}G
-orbit
\mathbb{O}\subset{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{g}
and a
{}^{\vee}G
-orbit
Y_{i}\subset Y(\mathbb{O})
. Form the algebraic variety
X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
as in (
3.1.1
), and let
{}^{\vee}G^{alg}
denote the algebraic universal covering group of
{}^{\vee}G
, i.e., the projective limit of all finite coverings of
{}^{\vee}G
.

Definition 3.2.1

(
[
2
, Definition 7.1]
)
.

A
complete geometric parameter
for
({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
is a pair
(x,\tau)
consisting of a geometric parameter
x\in X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
and an irreducible representation
\tau
of the profinite group

A^{loc}_{x}:=\mathrm{Com}(\mathrm{Stab}_{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}(x)).

Note that
{}^{\vee}G^{alg}
acts by conjugation on the set of complete geometric parameters for
({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
. Two complete geometric parameters are said to be
equivalent
if they are conjugate under this action. Write
\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
for the set of equivalence classes of complete geometric parameters for
({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
. Note that an element of
\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
can be regarded as a pair
(S,\tau)
consisting of a
{}^{\vee}G
-orbit
S\subset X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
(i.e. an equivalence class of geometric parameters) and an irreducible
{}^{\vee}G^{alg}
-equivariant local system
\tau
on
S
.

Write

\displaystyle C^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})

\displaystyle=\text{category of ${}^{\vee}G^{alg}$-equivariant constructible sheaves on $X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})$}

\displaystyle P^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})

\displaystyle=\text{category of ${}^{\vee}G^{alg}$-equivariant perverse sheaves on $X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})$}

Denote by
K^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
the shared Grothendieck group of these two categories. To each complete geometric parameter
(S,\tau)\in\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
, we can associate two natural extensions:

\displaystyle\mu(S,\tau)

\displaystyle=j_{!}\tau\in C^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})

\displaystyle P(s,\tau)

\displaystyle=j_{!*}\tau[\dim(S)]\in P^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})

(here,
j
denotes the inclusion of
S
in its closure and
j_{!}
(resp.
j_{!*}
) denotes the extension-byzero (resp. intermediate extension)). For details, we refer the reader to the discussion following
[
2
, Theorem 7.9]
. The sets

\{\mu(S,\tau)\mid(S,\tau)\in\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})\},\qquad\{P(S,\tau)\mid(S,\tau)\in\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})\}

form two natural
\mathbb{Z}
-bases, in canonical bijection, for
K^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})
.

It is convenient to introduce some analogous notation for
X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})
:

\displaystyle C^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})

\displaystyle:=\bigoplus_{i}C^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})

\displaystyle P^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})

\displaystyle:=\bigoplus_{i}P^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})

\displaystyle K^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})

\displaystyle:=\bigoplus_{i}K^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})

\displaystyle\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})

\displaystyle:=\bigsqcup_{i}\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O},Y_{i})

3.3.
The Langlands classification via geometric parameters

In this section, we will recall the statement of the Langlands classification of irreducible representations, as formulated in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 3.3.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 10.4]
)
.

Let
(G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{W})
be an extended group for
G
and let
({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{S})
be a corresponding
E
-group with second invariant
z
. Fix an orbit
\mathbb{O}\subset{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{g}
of semisimple elements. Then there is a natural bijection

(3.3.1)

\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})\xrightarrow{\sim}L^{z}_{\mathbb{O}}(G^{\Gamma})

from complete geometric parameters to equivalence classes of nonzero final type-
z
standard limit characters. Composing with the bijection of Theorem
2.7.3
(iv), we obtain a further bijection

\pi\colon\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pi^{z}_{\mathbb{O}}(G^{\Gamma})

from complete geometric parameters to equivalence classes of irreducible type-
z
representations of strong real forms of
G
.

Remark 3.3.2
.

Let
\varphi\colon W_{\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
be a Langlands parameter (Definition
2.3.2
). Then the
L
-packet
corresponding to
\varphi
is the set of irreducible projective type-
z
representations

\Pi^{z}(G^{\Gamma})_{\varphi}:=\{\pi(x,\tau)\}

where
x
is the geometric parameter corresponding to
\varphi
(Proposition
3.1.2
) and
\tau
runs over all irreducible representations of
A_{x}^{loc}
.

For each complete geometric parameter
\xi\in\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})
, let
M(\xi)\in K\Pi^{z}_{\mathbb{O}}(G^{\Gamma})
denote the standard representation with Langlands quotient
\pi(\xi)
. Note that the set

\{M(\xi)\mid\xi\in\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})\}

is a basis for
K\Pi^{z}_{\mathbb{O}}(G^{\Gamma})
. Also define
d(\xi):=\dim(S)
, where
\xi=(S,\tau)
.

Theorem 3.3.3

(
[
2
, Theorem 1.24]
)
.

Let
(G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{W})
be an extended group for
G
and let
({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{S})
be a corresponding
E
-group with second invariant
z
. Fix an infinitesimal character
\mathbb{O}\subset{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{g}
. Then there is a unique perfect pairing

\langle-,-\rangle\colon K\Pi^{z}_{\mathbb{O}}(G^{\Gamma})\times KX^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{Z}

such that

\langle M(\xi),\mu(\xi^{\prime})\rangle=e(\xi)\delta_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}},\qquad\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})

and

\langle\pi(\xi),P(\xi^{\prime})\rangle=e(\xi)(-1)^{d(\xi)}\delta_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}},\qquad\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}).

Here
e(\xi)\in\{\pm 1\}
is the Kottwitz sign associated to the real form of
G
represented by
\pi(\xi)
, see
[
19
]
or
[
2
, Definition 15.8]
.

Since
\{M(\xi)\}
(resp.
\{\mu(\xi)\}
) is a basis for
K\Pi^{z}_{\mathbb{O}}(G^{\Gamma})
(resp.
K^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathbb{O})
), the uniqueness of the pairing defined by
one
of the two properties is obvious. The content of the theorem is that the pairings defined by both conditions coincide.

3.4.
Langlands functoriality

In this section, we will review the general formalism of Langlands functoriality, as developed in
[
2
]
. Suppose
(G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{W})
and
(H^{\Gamma},\mathcal{W}_{H})
are extended groups. Let
({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{S})
and
({}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma},\mathcal{S}_{H})
be corresponding
E
-groups with second invariants
z
and
z_{H}
, respectively. Suppose we are given an
L
-homomorphism
\epsilon\colon{}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
. Then there is a natural map

(3.4.1)

\epsilon\colon X({}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}),\qquad\epsilon(y_{H},\Lambda_{H})=(\epsilon(y),\epsilon(\Lambda_{H})).

Here,
\epsilon(\Lambda_{H})
denotes the unique canonical flat in
{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{g}
containing
d\epsilon(\Lambda_{H})
. This map is clearly
{}^{\vee}H
-equivariant. Now fix

\mathbb{O}_{H}=\text{semisimple ${}^{\vee}H$-orbit on ${}^{\vee}\mathfrak{h}$},\qquad Y_{H}=\text{semisimple ${}^{\vee}H$-conjugacy class in ${}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma}\setminus{}^{\vee}H$}

so that
Y_{H}^{2}=e(\mathbb{O}_{H})
. Let
\mathbb{O}={}^{\vee}G\cdot\mathbb{O}_{H}
and
Y:={}^{\vee}G\cdot Y_{H}
. Then (
3.4.1
) restricts to a closed
{}^{\vee}H
-equivariant morphism of algebraic varieties

(3.4.2)

\epsilon\colon X({}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma},Y_{H},\mathbb{O}_{H})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},Y,\mathbb{O}).

Choose an element
(y_{H},\Lambda_{H})\in X({}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma},Y_{H},\mathbb{O}_{H})
and let
(y,\Lambda)=\epsilon(y_{H},\Lambda_{H})\in X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},Y,\mathbb{O})
. Define

{}^{\vee}R_{H}=Z_{{}^{\vee}H}(e(\Lambda_{H}))^{0},\quad{}^{\vee}K_{H}=Z_{{}^{\vee}H}(y_{H}),\quad{}^{\vee}P_{H}=\mathrm{Stab}_{{}^{\vee}H}(\Lambda_{H})

and

{}^{\vee}R=Z_{{}^{\vee}G}(e(\Lambda))^{0},\quad{}^{\vee}K=Z_{{}^{\vee}G}(\epsilon(y_{H})),\quad{}^{\vee}P=\mathrm{Stab}_{{}^{\vee}G}(\Lambda).

As explained in Section
3.1
, there are natural identifications

X({}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma},Y_{H},\mathbb{O}_{H})\simeq{}^{\vee}H\times_{{}^{\vee}K_{H}}({}^{\vee}R_{H}/{}^{\vee}P_{H}),\qquad X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},Y,\mathbb{O})\simeq{}^{\vee}G\times_{{}^{\vee}K}({}^{\vee}R/{}^{\vee}P).

Under these identifications, (
3.4.2
) corresponds to the
{}^{\vee}H
-equivariant morphism

\epsilon\colon{}^{\vee}H\times_{{}^{\vee}K_{H}}({}^{\vee}R_{H}/{}^{\vee}P_{H})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}G\times_{{}^{\vee}K}({}^{\vee}R/{}^{\vee}P)

induced by the maps
\epsilon\colon{}^{\vee}H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}G
,
\epsilon\colon{}^{\vee}R_{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}R
, and so on.

Restriction along
\epsilon
defines a functor

\epsilon^{*}\colon D^{b}C^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},Y,\mathbb{O})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}D^{b}C^{{}^{\vee}H^{alg}}X({}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma},Y_{H},\mathbb{O}_{H})

of bounded derived categories of equivariant constructible sheaves, and hence a group homomorphism

\epsilon^{*}\colon K^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X(^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},Y,\mathbb{O})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}K^{{}^{\vee}H^{alg}}X(^{\vee}H^{\Gamma},Y_{H},\mathbb{O}_{H})

of Grothendieck groups. Assembling these homomorphisms over the various
Y_{H}
and
\mathbb{O}_{H}
, we get a group homomorphism

\epsilon^{*}\colon K^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X(^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}K^{{}^{\vee}H^{alg}}X(^{\vee}H^{\Gamma})

Taking transpose, we get a group homomorphism in the opposite direction

(3.4.3)

\epsilon_{*}\colon K^{{}^{\vee}H^{alg}}X(^{\vee}H^{\Gamma})^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}K^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X(^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})^{*}.

(here,
(\bullet)^{*}
stands for
\mathbb{Z}
-linear dual). Theorem
3.3.3
provides identifications

\overline{K}\Pi^{z_{H}}(H^{\Gamma})\simeq K^{{}^{\vee}H^{alg}}X(^{\vee}H^{\Gamma})^{*},\qquad\overline{K}\Pi^{z}(G^{\Gamma})\simeq K^{{}^{\vee}G^{alg}}X(^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})^{*}

of these dual spaces with formal virtual representations of strong real forms for
H
and
G
respectively. Under these identifications, the map (
3.4.3
) corresponds to a group homomorphism

(3.4.4)

\epsilon_{*}\colon\overline{K}\Pi^{z_{H}}(H^{\Gamma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\overline{K}\Pi^{z}(G^{\Gamma})

from formal virtual representations of strong real forms of
H
to formal virtual representations of strong real forms of
G
. This final map is called
Langlands functoriality
. It is easy to describe its behavior on standard representations.

If
S_{H}\subset X({}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma})
is a
{}^{\vee}H
-orbit (of geometric parameters for
H
) and
S={}^{\vee}G\cdot\epsilon(S_{H})
, there is a natural homomorphism of local component groups

A^{loc}_{\epsilon}\colon A^{loc}_{S_{H}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}A^{loc}_{S},

(notation from
3.2.1
). Thus, for each complete geometric parameter
(S_{H},\tau_{H})\in\Xi({}^{\vee}H^{\Gamma})
there is an associated subset
\epsilon_{*}(S_{H},\tau_{H})\subset\Xi({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma})
of complete geometric parameters for
G
, defined by

(3.4.5)

Let

Definition 3.5.1

{}^{\vee}G

Remark 3.5.2

If we have a fixed maximal torus

, then an equivalence class of translation data determines, and is determined by, a
W(G,H)
-orbit of pairs

in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}

Now fix a translation datum

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\vee}R/^{\vee}P_{r}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

Lemma 3.6.2
.

In the setting described in the beginning of this section (in particular, assuming conditions (GR1) and (GR2)), the following are true:

By Proposition

Theorem 3.6.3
.

In the setting described in the beginning of this section (in particular, assuming conditions (GR1) and (GR2)), the operators

(\Lambda,\Lambda_{r})
and
(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}_{r})
from
\mathbb{O}
to
\mathbb{O}_{r}
and
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}
to
\mathbb{O}^{\prime}_{r}

\mathrm{nil}(\mathfrak{a})\subset\mathrm{nil}(\mathfrak{b})
implies
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{a}
. In our case, this implies
{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{p}_{M_{1}}\subset{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{q}_{2}\cap{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{r}_{M_{1}}
and therefore
{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}\subset{}^{\vee}Q_{2}\cap{}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}
. The claim that
{}^{\vee}Q_{2}\cap{}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}={}^{\vee}R_{M_{2}}{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}
follows immediately.
∎

Now consider the
{}^{\vee}M_{1}
-invariant subset

\tilde{U}:={}^{\vee}M_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2}X({}^{\vee}M_{2}^{\Gamma},Y_{M_{2}},\mathbb{O}_{M_{2}})\subset X({}^{\vee}M_{1}^{\Gamma},Y_{M_{1}},\mathbb{O}_{M_{1}}).

Lemma 4.4.2
.

Suppose
\psi
is in the good range (Definition
4.1.2
). Then the following are true:

(i)

\epsilon_{2}
induces a
{}^{\vee}M_{1}
-equivariant isomorphism

{}^{\vee}M_{1}\times_{{}^{\vee}M_{2}}X({}^{\vee}M_{2}^{\Gamma},Y_{M_{2}},\mathbb{O}_{M_{2}})\xrightarrow{\sim}\widetilde{U}.

(ii)

\tilde{U}
is an open subset of
X({}^{\vee}M_{1}^{\Gamma},Y_{M_{1}},\mathbb{O}_{M_{1}})
.

Proof.

For (i), it is equivalent to show that the natural map

(4.4.3)

{}^{\vee}K_{M_{1}}\times_{{}^{\vee}K_{M_{2}}}{}^{\vee}R_{M_{2}}/{}^{\vee}P_{M_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}/{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}

is injective, i.e. that the stabilizer in
{}^{\vee}K_{M_{1}}
of each point in
{}^{\vee}R_{M_{2}}{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}/{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}\subset{}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}/{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}
belongs to
{}^{\vee}K_{M_{2}}
. But every such stabilizer is contained in

{}^{\vee}K_{M_{1}}\cap{}^{\vee}R_{M_{2}}{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}},

and by Lemma
4.4.1
, we have that
{}^{\vee}R_{M_{2}}{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}={}^{\vee}Q_{2}\cap{}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}
. So

{}^{\vee}K_{M_{1}}\cap{}^{\vee}R_{M_{2}}{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}={}^{\vee}K_{M_{1}}\cap{}^{\vee}Q_{2}\cap{}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}={}^{\vee}K_{M_{1}}\cap{}^{\vee}M_{2}\cap{}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}={}^{\vee}K_{M_{2}}.

This proves (i).

For (ii), we must show that the image of (
4.4.3
), namely
{}^{\vee}K_{M_{1}}{}^{\vee}R_{M_{2}}{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}/{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}
is an open subset of
{}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}/{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}
. By part (i), this subset is the image of an injective map from an irreducible variety, and hence locally closed and irreducible. So it suffices to show that

\dim({}^{\vee}K_{M_{1}}{}^{\vee}R_{M_{2}}{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}/{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}})=\dim({}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}/{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}).

This follows by comparing tangent spaces at the point
{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}/^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}
. The tangent space in
{}^{\vee}R_{M_{1}}/^{\vee}P_{1}
is
{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{r}_{M_{1}}/^{\vee}\mathfrak{p}_{1}
, whereas the tangent space in
{}^{\vee}K_{M_{1}}{}^{\vee}R_{M_{2}}{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}/{}^{\vee}P_{M_{1}}
is

\left({}^{\vee}\mathfrak{k}_{M_{1}}+^{\vee}\mathfrak{r}_{M_{2}}+^{\vee}\mathfrak{p}_{M_{1}}\right)/^{\vee}\mathfrak{p}_{M_{1}}=\left({}^{\vee}\mathfrak{k}_{M_{1}}+(^{\vee}\mathfrak{q}_{2}\cap^{\vee}\mathfrak{r}_{M_{1}})\right)/^{\vee}\mathfrak{p}_{M_{1}}.

Thus, it suffices to check

{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{k}_{M_{1}}+(^{\vee}\mathfrak{q}_{2}\cap^{\vee}\mathfrak{r}_{M_{1}})=^{\vee}\mathfrak{r}_{M_{1}}.

This follows from the fact that the parabolic
{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{q}_{2}\cap^{\vee}\mathfrak{r}_{M_{1}}\subset{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{r}_{M_{1}}
is ‘real’, i.e. taken to its opposite by the Cartan involution defining
{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{k}_{M_{1}}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4.3
.

Let
(G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{W})
be an extended group and let
({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{S})
be a corresponding
E
-group with second invariant
z
. Let
\psi\colon W_{\mathbb{R}}\times SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
be an Arthur parameter, and fix the notation of Definition
4.1.1
. Then the following are true:

(i)

\epsilon_{2}
induces an isomorphism of Arthur component groups

A_{\epsilon_{2}}^{mic}\colon A_{\psi_{2}}^{mic}\xrightarrow{\sim}A_{\psi_{1}}^{mic}

(ii)

For any
s\in A_{\psi_{2}}^{mic}
, there is an identity in
K\Pi^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}_{1}})}(M_{1}^{\Gamma})

\eta^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}(A^{mic}_{\epsilon_{2}}(s))=(\epsilon_{2})_{*}\eta^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}(s).

Proof.

In view of Lemma
4.4.2
, the proof is basically identical to that of Proposition
4.3.3
.
∎

4.5.
The Jordan Decomposition of an Arthur packet: good range case

In this section, we combine Theorem
4.2.3
(describing the relationship between the Langlands functoriality maps
(\epsilon_{1})_{*}
,
(\epsilon_{2})_{*}
and parabolic induction) with Propositions
4.3.3
and
4.4.3
(analyzing the behavior of the functoriality maps with respect to Arthur packets). The conclusion is summarized in the Corollary below.

Corollary 4.5.1

(Jordan Decomposition of a Good Range Arthur Packet)
.

Let
(G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{W})
be an extended group and let
({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{S})
be a corresponding
E
-group with second invariant
z
. Let
\psi\colon W_{\mathbb{R}}\times SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
be an Arthur parameter in the good range (Definition
4.1.2
) and fix the notation of Definition
4.1.1
. The image of
\psi
is contained in
{}^{\vee}M_{2}^{\Gamma}
; let
\psi_{2}\colon W_{\mathbb{R}}\times SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}M_{2}^{\Gamma}
denote the corresponding (unipotent) Arthur parameter. Then

(i)

\epsilon
induces an isomorphism of Arthur component groups

A_{\epsilon}^{mic}\colon A^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}\xrightarrow{\sim}A^{mic}_{\psi}.

(ii)

For any
s\in A_{\psi_{2}}^{mic}
, there is an identity in
K\Pi^{z}(G^{\Gamma})

\eta^{mic}_{\psi}(A^{mic}_{\epsilon}(s))=e(G^{\Gamma})R^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}R^{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}e(M_{2}^{\Gamma})\eta^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}(s)

Here,
R^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}
is (normalized) real parabolic induction,
R^{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}
is (normalized) cohomological induction in the good range, and
e(G^{\Gamma})
,
e(M_{2}^{\Gamma})
are the Kottwitz sign maps (see Theorem
4.2.3
).

Proof.

(i) is a consequence of Proposition
4.3.3
(i) and Proposition
4.4.3
(i). Let
s\in A^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}
. Then by Proposition
4.3.3
(ii) and Proposition
4.4.3
(ii), we have the character identity

\eta^{mic}_{\psi}(A_{\epsilon}(s))=\epsilon_{*}\eta^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}(s).

On the other hand, Theorem
4.2.3
implies that

(4.5.1)

\epsilon_{*}=(\epsilon_{1})_{*}(\epsilon_{2})_{*}=R^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}e(M_{1}^{\Gamma})R^{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}e(M_{2}^{\Gamma})\colon K\Pi^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}_{1}})z(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}_{2}})}(M_{2}^{\Gamma})^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}K\Pi^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}_{1}})}(G^{\Gamma})^{mic}_{\psi}.

By
[
19
, (6) of Corollary on p. 295]
,
e(M_{1}(\mathbb{R},\delta_{1}))=e(G(\mathbb{R},\delta_{1}))
for any strong real form
\delta_{1}
of
M_{1}
. Thus, we can rewrite
4.5.1
in the desired form.
∎

Remark 4.5.2
.

We note that under the good range hypothesis, a bit more is true: the map
R^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}R^{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}
takes irreducibles in
\Pi(M_{2}^{\Gamma})^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}
to irreducibles in
\Pi(G^{\Gamma})^{mic}_{\psi}
(up to signs), and this induces a bijection

\Pi(M_{2}^{\Gamma})^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pi(G^{\Gamma})^{mic}_{\psi}.

These statements follow easily from Lemmas
4.3.2
and
4.4.2
.

4.6.
The Jordan Decomposition of an Arthur packet: general case

In this section, we will extend Corollary
4.5.1
to general Arthur packets.

For a virtual representation
\eta\in K\Pi^{z}(G^{\Gamma})
, let
[\eta]\subset\Pi^{z}(G^{\Gamma})
denote the set of irreducible representations appearing (with nonzero multiplicity) in the formula for

.

Theorem 4.6.1

(Jordan Decomposition of a General Arthur Packet)
.

Let
(G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{W})
be an extended group and let
({}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma},\mathcal{S})
be a corresponding
E
-group with second invariant

. Let

. The image of

is contained in

; let

(i)

\epsilon
induces an isomorphism of Arthur component groups

(ii)

For any
s\in A^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}
, there is an identity in
K\Pi^{z}(G^{\Gamma})

\eta^{mic}_{\psi}(A^{mic}_{\epsilon}(s))=e(G^{\Gamma})R^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}R^{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}e(M_{2}^{\Gamma})\eta^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}(s)

Here,

There is an inclusion of subsets of

The map
R^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}R^{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}

Proof.

Let
\chi\colon M_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}

. Then
\chi
corresponds to an algebraic co-character
\chi^{\vee}\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}Z({}^{\vee}M_{2})

The Arthur packets and
s
-stable sums for
\psi_{2}^{N}
and

are related by the formulas

\Pi^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}_{1}})z(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}_{2}})}(M_{2}^{\Gamma})^{mic}_{\psi_{2}^{N}}=\Pi^{zz(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}_{1}})z(\rho_{\mathfrak{u}_{2}})}(M_{2}^{\Gamma})^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}\otimes\chi^{2N},\qquad\eta^{mic}_{\psi_{2}^{N}}(s)=\eta^{mic}_{\psi_{2}}(s)\otimes\chi^{2N}.

Define Arthur parameters
\psi_{1}^{N}\colon W_{\mathbb{R}}\times SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}M_{1}^{\Gamma}
and
\psi^{N}\colon W_{\mathbb{R}}\times SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
for

and
{}^{\vee}G^{\Gamma}
by composing with the inclusions

\psi_{1}^{N}:=\epsilon_{1}\circ\psi_{2}^{N},\qquad\psi^{N}:=\epsilon\circ\psi_{2}^{N}.

Let

denote the holomorphic part of the differential of

and let

denote the image of

under

\lambda_{f}:=\lambda(\varphi_{\psi})=\frac{1}{2}h+z_{f},\qquad\lambda_{g}:=\lambda(\varphi_{\psi^{N}})=\frac{1}{2}h+z_{g}.

(notation as in Proposition
3.1.2
). Now choose

, and

We proceed to proving (ii). Since

is in the good range, Corollary

(ii) implies that for
each

, there is an identity in

\eta^{mic}_{\psi^{N}}(A^{mic}_{\epsilon}(s))=e(G^{\Gamma})R^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}R^{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}}e(M_{2}^{\Gamma})\eta^{mic}_{\psi_{2}^{N}}(s).

Lemma 4.6.2

{}^{\vee}R={}^{\vee}R(y)

{}^{\vee}P_{f}={}^{\vee}P(\Lambda_{f})

(e,0)
at the point
(1{}^{\vee}P_{g},h{}^{\vee}P_{f})
, which is also contained in
SS(\iota_{!}{\mathcal{F}})
. By (
4.6.5
) and (
4.6.8
) this implies that
e=e+0\in(^{\vee}\mathfrak{u}_{g}\cap\Ad(h)^{\vee}\mathfrak{u}_{f})^{-^{\vee}\theta}
. Recall, Section
3.7
, that
e\in({}^{\vee}\mathfrak{u}_{g})^{-{}^{\vee}\theta}
. So this condition is equivalent to
e\in\Ad(h)^{\vee}\mathfrak{u}_{f}
. On the other hand, since
(1^{\vee}P_{g},h^{\vee}P_{f})\in\mathrm{Supp}(\iota_{!}{\mathcal{F}})
we must have that
h\in\overline{{}^{\vee}P_{g}{}^{\vee}P_{f}}
. In summary,

must belong to the set

\{h\in\overline{{}^{\vee}P_{g}{}^{\vee}P_{f}}\mid e\in\Ad(h){}^{\vee}\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}=\{h^{-1}\in\overline{{}^{\vee}P_{f}{}^{\vee}P_{g}}\mid\Ad(h^{-1})e\in{}^{\vee}\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}.

But Theorem
A.2.1
tells us that this set is equal to
{}^{\vee}P_{f}
. Thus,
(1^{\vee}P_{g},h^{\vee}P_{f})=(1{}^{\vee}P_{g},1{}^{\vee}P_{f})
, as asserted above.

Since
\Phi_{F\circ p_{g}}|_{\{1^{\vee}P_{g}\}\times^{\vee}R/^{\vee}P_{f}}(\iota_{!}{\mathcal{F}})
is a skyscraper sheaf supported at the point
(1{}^{\vee}P_{g},1{}^{\vee}P_{f})
, there is an isomorphism of vector spaces

(4.6.11)

(p_{g}|_{\{1^{\vee}P_{g}\}\times^{\vee}R/^{\vee}P_{f}})_{!}\Phi_{F\circ p_{g}}|_{\{1^{\vee}P_{g}\}\times^{\vee}R/^{\vee}P_{f}}(\iota_{!}\mathcal{F})\simeq\Phi_{F\circ p_{g}}(\iota_{!}\mathcal{F})_{(1^{\vee}P_{g},1^{\vee}P_{f})}.

But
\iota(1(^{\vee}P_{g}\cap^{\vee}P_{f}))=(1^{\vee}P_{g},1^{\vee}P_{f})
, so

(4.6.12)

\Phi_{F\circ p_{g}}(\iota_{!}\mathcal{F})_{(1^{\vee}P_{g},1^{\vee}P_{f})}\simeq\left(\Phi_{F\circ p_{g}\circ\iota}{\mathcal{F}}\right)_{1(^{\vee}P_{g}\cap^{\vee}P_{f})}.

Finally, using the compatibility of microlocalization with smooth pullbacks (
[
17
, Proposition 4.3.5]
) we have an isomorphism

(4.6.13)

\left(\Phi_{F\circ p_{g}\circ\iota}{\mathcal{F}}\right)_{1(^{\vee}P_{g}\cap^{\vee}P_{f})}\simeq\chi^{mic}_{(1^{\vee}P_{f},e)}(\mathcal{G}).

Now (
4.6.4
) follows by combining the isomorphisms (
4.6.9
),(
4.6.10
), (
4.6.11
), (
4.6.12
), and (
4.6.13
).

∎

This completes the proof of (ii). Since each irreducible in
\Pi^{z}(G^{\Gamma})^{mic}_{\psi}
appears in the linear combination
\eta^{mic}_{\psi}(1)
with nonzero multiplicity, (iii) follows from (ii) (after specializing to
s=1
). (iv) follows from (iii), together with Corollary

Proof.

Appendix A
A Lie theory calculation

A.1.
Recollections on prehomogeneous vector spaces

Recall that a
prehomogeneous vector space
(PVS) is a pair
(L,V)
consisting of a complex algebraic group
L
and a finite-dimensional rational
L
-representation
V
containing an open dense
L
-orbit
\mathbb{O}
.

Definition A.1.1
.

A
relative invariant
of
(L,V)
is a nonzero rational function
f
on
V
such that

f(l\cdot v)=\chi(l)f(v),\qquad\forall l\in L,\ v\in V,

for some character
\chi\colon L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

We note that a relative invariant is determined by its character, up to multiplication by
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
(
[
25
, Proposition 3]
). In particular, every relative invariant is a homogeneous function.

Let
S_{1},...,S_{n}
denote the codimension one irreducible components of
V\setminus\mathbb{O}
. Then each
S_{i}
is the zero set of an irreducible polynomial
f_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[V]
, unique up to nonzero scalar multiplication. We will need the following fundamental fact about relative invariants.

Proposition A.1.2

(
[
25
, Proposition 5]
)
.

The polynomials
f_{i}
are algebraically independent relative invariants of
(L,V)
. Moreover, every relative invariant
f
of
(L,V)
can be written in the form

f=c\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{m_{i}},

for
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
m_{1},...,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let us now consider a very special kind of PVS which arises in this paper and in other Lie theory contexts. Let
G
be a complex connected reductive algebraic group, and let
(e,f,h)
be an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple in
\mathfrak{g}
. The semisimple operator
\ad(h)
defines a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading of
\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{k},\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{k}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[h,X]=kX\}.

Let
G_{0}=Z_{G}(h)
, a Levi subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Clearly
\Ad(G_{0})
preserves
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. It is a theorem of Kostant (
[
18
]
) that
(G_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is a PVS with open dense orbit
\Ad(G_{0})e
. In fact, more is true.

Proposition A.1.3

(
[
24
, Proposition 11.2.9]
)
.

The complement
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\setminus\Ad(G_{0})e
is of pure codimension one, i.e. it is the union of its codimension one irreducible components.

Fix a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
\langle-,-\rangle
on
\mathfrak{g}
with the following property: for any maximal torus
T\subset G
,
\langle-,-\rangle
is positive definite on the co-character lattice
X_{*}(T)
. Note that if
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, then the Killing form has this property (and is the unique such form, up to positive real scaling on each simple factor).

Proposition A.1.4
.

Let
\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n}
be the characters of
G_{0}
corresponding to the irreducible polynomials
f_{1},...,f_{n}
defining the components of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}\setminus\Ad(G_{0})e
. Then there are nonnegative real numbers
c_{1},...,c_{n}
such that

\langle h,x\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}d\chi_{i}(x),\qquad x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}.

Proof.

We can easily reduce to the case when
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra and
\langle-,-\rangle
is the Killing form

\langle x,y\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(\ad(x)\circ\ad(y)).

We begin by constructing an explicit polynomial relative invariant
p
on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
which transforms according to
\langle h,-\rangle
. Choose a basis
\{e_{k}^{1},...,e^{l_{k}}_{k}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
(this choice of basis will not matter significantly, but is useful for definitions). For each
k\leq-1
, we define a matrix-valued polynomial
M_{k}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
by the formula

M_{k}(v)_{ij}=\langle\ad(v)^{-k}e_{k}^{i},e_{k}^{j}\rangle,\qquad v\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

Let
p_{k}
be the polynomial on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
defined by taking the determinant of
M_{k}

p_{k}(v)=\det(M_{k}(v)),\qquad v\in\mathfrak{g}_{2},

and let

p=\prod_{k\leq-1}p_{k}(v)^{-k}.

We claim that
p
is a relative invariant of
(G_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
and that the corresponding character
\tau\colon G_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
satisfies

d\tau(x)=\langle h,x\rangle,\qquad x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}.

For
g\in G
, let
C_{k}(g)
denote the matrix of
\Ad(g^{-1})|_{\mathfrak{g}_{k}}
with respect to the basis
\{e_{k}^{1},...,e^{l_{k}}_{k}\}
. Then for any
k\leq-1
and
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
we have

\displaystyle M_{k}(\Ad(g)v)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\langle\ad(\Ad(g)v)^{-k}e_{k}^{i},e_{k}^{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Ad(g)\ad(v)^{-k}\Ad(g)^{-1}e^{i}_{k},e^{j}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\ad(v)^{-k}\Ad(g)^{-1}e^{i}_{k},\Ad(g)^{-1}e^{j}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=(C_{k}(g)^{t}M_{k}(v)C_{k}(g))_{ij}

Hence,
M_{k}(\Ad(g)v)=C_{k}(g)^{t}M_{k}(v)C_{k}(g)
and so

\displaystyle p_{k}(\Ad(g)v)

\displaystyle=\det(M_{k}(\Ad(g)v))

\displaystyle=\det(C_{k}(g))^{2}p_{k}(v)

\displaystyle=\det(\Ad(g^{-2})|_{\mathfrak{g}_{k}})p_{k}(v)

This shows that
p_{k}
is a relative invariant of
(G_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
, corresponding to the character

\tau_{k}\colon G_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{C}^{\times},\qquad\tau_{k}(g)=\det(\Ad(g^{-2}))|_{\mathfrak{g}_{k}}.

It follows that
p
is a relative invariant, corresponding to the character

\tau\colon G_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{C}^{\times},\qquad\tau(g)=\prod_{k\leq-1}\tau_{k}(g)^{-k}.

Note that for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have the relation
\det(\Ad(g)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{k}})=\det(\Ad(g)^{-1}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{-k}})
. Thus,

\tau(g)=\prod_{k}\det(\Ad(g)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{k}})^{k},\qquad g\in G_{0}.

Taking differentials, we get

\displaystyle d\tau(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}k\mathrm{Tr}(\ad(X)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{k}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathrm{Tr}(\ad(h)\circ\ad(X)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{k}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}(\ad(h)\circ\ad(X))

\displaystyle=\langle h,X\rangle

Since
p
is a relative invariant, Proposition
A.1.2
implies that

p=c\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{m_{i}}

for
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
m_{1},...,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence

d\tau=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}d\chi_{i}.

Since
p
is a polynomial, we have
m_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
. This completes the proof.
∎

A.2.
Three parabolics

In this section, we will consider the following situation. Let
G
be a complex connected reductive algebraic group with Borel subgroup
B\subset G
and maximal torus
T\subset B
. Let
Q=LU
be a standard parabolic subgroup of
G
(in our application,
Q
will arise as the parabolic dual to our ‘inducing’ parabolic). Denote the roots for
T
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{l}
by
\Delta(\mathfrak{g})
,
\Delta(\mathfrak{l})
, and the positive roots by
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
,
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l})
. Let
(e,f,h)
be an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple in
\mathfrak{l}
with
h\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l})
. We will also assume that
(e,f,h)
is ‘even’ in
\mathfrak{l}
, in that

(i)

h
has even weights on
\mathfrak{l}

Choose
z_{f},z_{g}\in X_{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
such that

\alpha(z_{f})=\alpha(z_{g})=0,\qquad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{l})

(the subscripts ‘f’ and ‘g’ stand for ‘fair’ and ‘good’, respectively), and consider the elements

\lambda_{f}:=\frac{1}{2}h+z_{f},\qquad\lambda_{g}=\frac{1}{2}h+z_{g}

(in our application, these elements will arise as the infinitesimal characters of Arthur packets with a common
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
). We will impose the following additional conditions on
h
,
z_{f}
, and
z_{g}
:

(ii)

\lambda_{f}
and
\lambda_{g}
have integral weights on
\mathfrak{g}
.

(iii)

z_{f}
has nonnegative weights on
\mathfrak{u}
(this is the ‘weakly fair range’ assumption).

(iv)

\lambda_{g}
has strictly positive weights on
\mathfrak{u}
(this is the ‘good range’ assumption).

Because of condition (ii), the semisimple operators
\ad(\lambda_{f})
and
\ad(\lambda_{g})
define
\mathbb{Z}
-gradings of
\mathfrak{g}
, and hence parabolic subgroups

P_{f}=L_{f}U_{f},\qquad P_{g}=L_{g}U_{g}.

Here,
L_{f}
(resp.
L_{g}
) is the centralizer of
\lambda_{f}
(resp.
\lambda_{g}
), and
U_{f}
(resp.
U_{g}
) is the unipotent subgroup corresponding to the strictly positive (integral) weight spaces for
\lambda_{f}
(resp.
\lambda_{g}
). Because of condition (iv), we have inclusions

(A.2.1)

L_{g}\subseteq L,\qquad U\subseteq U_{g},\qquad P_{g}\subseteq Q.

Note that in general, there are no simple relations between
P_{f}
and
Q
, or between
P_{f}
and
P_{g}
, except that

P_{f}\cap L=P_{g}\cap L

(since
\lambda_{f}-\lambda_{g}
is central in
\mathfrak{l}
).

Consider the constructible subset
P_{f}P_{g}\subset G
and its Zariski closure
\overline{P_{f}P_{g}}
. The main result of this section is

Theorem A.2.1
.

In the setting described above, we have

\{g\in\overline{P_{f}P_{g}}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}=P_{f}.

The proof of Theorem
A.2.1
will occupy the remainder of this section. First, we will prove the corresponding statement for the ‘open’ Bruhat cell
P_{f}P_{g}\subset\overline{P_{f}P_{g}}
.

Proposition A.2.2
.

In the setting described above, we have

\{g\in P_{f}P_{g}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}=P_{f}.

Proof.

The proof has several steps.

Step 1.
Note that
[\lambda_{f},e]=[\frac{1}{2}h,e]+[z_{f},e]=e
. So
e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}
. The inclusion
P_{f}\subseteq\{g\in P_{f}P_{g}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}
follows immediately. It remains to prove the reverse inclusion
\{g\in P_{f}P_{g}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}\subseteq P_{f}
. Since
\{g\in P_{f}P_{g}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}
is stable under left-multiplication by
P_{f}
, it suffices to show that
\{g\in P_{g}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}\subseteq P_{f}
.

Step 2.
By the inclusions in (
A.2.1
), there is a decomposition

(A.2.2)

P_{g}=(L\cap P_{g})U.

Since
U\subset G
is unipotent subgroup of
G
, it is a product of its root groups (multiplied in any order). Hence

(A.2.3)

U=\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u})}U_{\alpha}=(\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{f})}U_{\alpha})(\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{u}_{f}^{op})}U_{\alpha})=(U\cap P_{f})(U\cap U_{f}^{op}).

Combining (
A.2.2
) and (
A.2.3
), we arrive at the decomposition

(A.2.4)

P_{g}=(L\cap P_{g})(U\cap P_{f})(U\cap U_{f}^{op}).

Since
\{g\in P_{g}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}
is stable under left-multiplication by
P_{g}\cap P_{f}
and
(L\cap P_{g})(U_{\cap}P_{f})\subset P_{g}\cap P_{f}
, it in fact suffices to show that

\{g\in U\cap U_{f}^{op}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}=\{1\}.

Step 3.
Suppose, for contradiction, that
g
is a nontrivial element in
U\cap U_{f}^{op}
such that
\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}
. Since
U\cap U_{f}^{op}
is a unipotent group, the exponential map
\exp\colon\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{u}_{f}^{op}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}U\cap U_{f}^{op}
is a bijection. In particular,
g=\exp(Y)
for some nonzero element
Y\in\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{u}_{f}^{op}
.

Let
t
denote the sum of the positive co-roots in
\mathfrak{u}
, so that
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{z}_{\mathfrak{g}}(t)
and
\mathfrak{u}
is the sum of the positive weight spaces for
t
. Consider the
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
-grading of
\mathfrak{g}
defined by the (commuting) semisimple operators
\ad(\lambda_{f})
,
\ad(h)
, and
\ad(t)
:

(A.2.5)

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{k,l,m}\mathfrak{g}_{k,l,m},\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{k,l,m}=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[\lambda_{f},\xi]=k\xi,\ [h,\xi]=l\xi,\ [t,\xi]=m\xi\}.

Consider the decomposition of
Y
with respect to this grading:

Y=\sum Y_{k,l,m},\qquad Y_{k,l,m}\in\mathfrak{g}_{k,l,m}.

Since
Y\in\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{u}_{f}^{op}
, we have that
k\leq-1
and
m\geq 1
whenever
Y_{k,l,m}\neq 0
. Let

k_{0}=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\mathfrak{g}_{k,l,m}\neq\{0\}\text{ for some }l,m\}.

Since
k_{0}+1\leq 0
and
\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}
, we have that

(A.2.6)

(\Ad(g)e)_{k_{0}+1,l,m}=0,\qquad\forall l,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

On the other hand

\Ad(g)e=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n!}\ad(Y)^{n}e=\sum_{k_{1},...,k_{n},l_{1},...,l_{n},m_{1},...,m_{n}}\frac{1}{n!}\ad(Y_{k_{1},l_{1},m_{1}})...\ad(Y_{k_{n},l_{n},m_{n}})e

(this sum is finite since
\ad(Y)
is nilpotent). Since
e\in\mathfrak{g}_{1,2,0}
, we have that

\ad(Y_{k_{1},l_{1},m_{1}})...\ad(Y_{k_{n},l_{n},m_{n}})e\in\mathfrak{g}_{1+\sum k_{i},2+\sum l_{i},\sum m_{i}}.

So

(A.2.7)

(\Ad(g)e)_{k_{0}+1,l,m}=\sum_{l,m\in\mathbb{Z}}[Y_{k_{0},l,m},e].

Combining (
A.2.6
) and (
A.2.7
), we deduce

[Y_{k_{0},l,m},e]=0,\qquad\forall l,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Choose
l,m\in\mathbb{Z}
so that
Y_{k_{0},l,m}\neq 0
(such
l
and
m
exist by the definition of
k_{0}
). Since
[Y_{k_{0},l,m},e]=0
and
[h,Y_{k_{0},l,m}]=lY_{k_{0},l,m}
, we have by the representation theory of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
that
l\geq 0
. On the other hand,
Y_{k_{0},l,m}
is a weight vector for
z_{0}
of weight
k_{0}-\frac{1}{2}l
:

[z_{0},Y_{k_{0},l,m}]=[\lambda_{f}-\frac{1}{2}h,Y_{k_{0},l,m}]=k_{0}Y_{k_{0},l,m}-\frac{1}{2}lY_{k_{0},l,m}=(k_{0}-\frac{1}{2}l)Y_{k_{0},l,m}.

Thus, since
Y_{k_{0},l,m}\in\mathfrak{u}
, our assumption on
z_{0}
implies that
k_{0}-\frac{1}{2}l\geq 0
. Hence
k_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}l\geq 0
. This is a contradiction.
∎

Next, we will show that there are no contributions to
\{g\in\overline{P_{f}P_{g}}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}
coming from the boundary
\partial P_{f}P_{g}=\overline{P_{f}P_{g}}\setminus P_{f}P_{g}
. Let
W=N_{G}(T)/T
denote the Weyl group of
G
. Let
\ell\colon W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
denote the length function and let
\leq
denote the Bruhat order. The following lemma gives an explicit description of
\partial P_{f}P_{g}
as a union of Bruhat cells.

Lemma A.2.3
.

Let
v\in W
denote the (unique) minimal-length element such that
v\lambda_{f}
is dominant, and let

W_{\partial}:=\{w\in W\mid vw<v\}.

Then

\partial P_{f}P_{g}=\bigcup_{w\in W_{\partial}}P_{f}\dot{w}P_{g},

where, for each
w\in W
,
\dot{w}
denotes a lift of
w
to
N_{G}(T)
.

Proof.

Since
v\lambda_{f}
is dominant, the parabolic subgroup
P_{f}^{\prime}:=\dot{w}P_{f}\dot{w}^{-1}
contains the Borel subgroup
B
. So by the Bruhat decomposition, we have, for elements
w_{1},w_{2}\in W

P_{f}^{\prime}\dot{w}_{1}P_{g}\nsubseteq\overline{P_{f}^{\prime}\dot{w}_{2}P_{g}}\iff w_{1}^{min}<w_{2}^{min},

where
w^{min}
denotes the (unique) minimal-length element in the double-coset
W_{f}^{\prime}wW_{g}
. Hence

\displaystyle P_{f}\dot{w}P_{g}\subseteq\partial P_{f}P_{g}

\displaystyle\iff\dot{v}P_{f}\dot{w}P_{g}\subseteq\partial(\dot{v}P_{f}P_{g})

\displaystyle\iff P_{f}^{\prime}\dot{vw}P_{g}\subseteq\partial(P_{f}^{\prime}\dot{v}P_{g})

\displaystyle\iff vw<v^{min}=v.

This completes the proof.
∎

For the next proposition, we will use the following notation. For any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, there is a unique decomposition

x=t+\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g})}x_{\alpha},\qquad t\in{\mathfrak{t}},\ x_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

We write

\mathrm{Supp}(x):=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g})\mid x_{\alpha}\neq 0\}.

Proposition A.2.4
.

In the setting described in the beginning of Section
A.2
, we have

\{g\in\partial P_{f}P_{g}\mid\Ad(g)e\in\mathfrak{u}_{f}\}=\emptyset.

Proof.

The proof has several steps.

Step 1.
By Lemma
A.2.3
, it suffices to show that

w\in W_{\partial}\implies\Ad(P_{f}\dot{w}P_{g})e\cap\mathfrak{u}_{f}=\emptyset.

Since
\mathfrak{u}_{f}
is preserved by
\Ad(P_{f})
, this is equivalent to the claim that

(A.2.8)

w\in W_{\partial}\implies\Ad(\dot{w}P_{g})e\cap\mathfrak{u}_{f}=\emptyset.

Suppose
\Ad(\dot{w}L_{g})e\cap\mathfrak{u}_{f}=\emptyset
for some
w\in W
and let
p\in P_{g}
be arbitrary. Write
p=ul
for
u\in U_{g}
and
l\in L_{g}
. Let
e^{\prime}=\Ad(l)e
, and
u=\exp(Y)
with
Y\in\mathfrak{u}_{g}
. Then

\Ad(\dot{w}p)e=\Ad(\dot{w})\Ad(u)e^{\prime}=\Ad(\dot{w})e^{\prime}+\Ad(\dot{w})\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n!}\ad(Y)^{n}e^{\prime}

Since
l
centralizes
\lambda_{g}
and
e
has
\lambda_{g}
-weight
1
,
e^{\prime}
must also have
\lambda_{g}
-weight
1
. On the other hand, since
Y\in\mathfrak{u}_{g}
, each term
\frac{1}{n!}\ad(Y)^{n}e^{\prime}
is a sum of weight vectors for
\lambda_{g}
of weight strictly greater than
1
. Thus,
\mathrm{Supp}(e^{\prime})
is disjoint from
\mathrm{Supp}(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n!}\ad(Y)^{n}e^{\prime})
. Since
\Ad(\dot{w})
permutes the root spaces, this implies that
\mathrm{Supp}(\Ad(\dot{w}e^{\prime})
is disjoint from
\mathrm{Supp}(Ad(\dot{w})\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n!}\ad(Y)^{n}e^{\prime})
. It follows that

\mathrm{Supp}(\Ad(\dot{w}p)e)=\mathrm{Supp}(\Ad(\dot{w})e^{\prime})\sqcup\mathrm{Supp}(Ad(\dot{w})\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n!}\ad(Y)^{n}e^{\prime}).

Since
\Ad(\dot{w}e^{\prime})\cap\mathfrak{u}_{f}=\emptyset
, we know that
\mathrm{Supp}(\Ad(\dot{w}e^{\prime}))
contains a root
\alpha
such that
\alpha(\lambda_{f})\leq 0
. It follows from the above that
\mathrm{Supp}(\Ad(\dot{w}p)e)
must also contain this root and hence that
\Ad(\dot{w}p)e\notin\mathfrak{u}_{f}
. So in fact (
A.2.8
) is equivalent to the implication

(A.2.9)

w\in W_{\partial}\implies\Ad(\dot{w}L_{g})e\cap\mathfrak{u}_{f}=\emptyset.

This is what we will prove in Steps 2-4.

Step 2.
Recall that if
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}
is integral and dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
and
w_{1}<w_{2}
, then
w_{1}\lambda-w_{2}\lambda
is a sum of positive co-roots for
\mathfrak{g}
. Applying this fact to the Weyl group elements
(vw)^{-1}<v^{-1}
and the integral dominant coweight
v\lambda_{f}\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, we see that
\gamma:=w^{-1}\lambda_{f}-\lambda_{f}
is a sum of positive co-roots for
\mathfrak{g}
(a nonzero sum, as
v
is of minimal-length and
vw<v
). Fix a positive-definite
W
-invariant symmetric bilinear form
\langle-,-\rangle
on
X_{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. Condition (iii) on
z_{f}
implies that
z_{f}
is dominant for
\mathfrak{g}
. So
\langle z_{f},\gamma\rangle\geq 0
. This implies that
\langle h,\gamma\rangle<0
by the following calculation:

\displaystyle\langle h,\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle\lambda_{f},\gamma\rangle-2\langle z_{f},\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle=(||\lambda_{f}+\gamma||^{2}-||\lambda_{f}||^{2}-||\gamma||^{2})-2\langle z_{f},\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle=(||w^{-1}\lambda_{f}||^{2}-||\lambda_{f}||^{2}-||\gamma||^{2})-2\langle z_{f},\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle=-||\gamma||^{2}-2\langle z_{f},\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle<0.

Step 3.
Now let
V
denote the
2
-eigenspace of
\ad(h)
on
\mathfrak{l}
. Recall from Section
A.1
that
(L_{g},V)
is a prehomogeneous vector space with open dense orbit
\Ad(L_{g})e
. Moreover, by Proposition
A.1.3
,
V\setminus\Ad(L_{g})e
is the union of its codimension one irreducible components
S_{1},...,S_{n}
. Let
f_{1},...,f_{n}
be irreducible polynomials defining these components. These are relative invariants by Proposition
A.1.2
. Denote the corresponding characters of
L_{g}
by
\chi_{1},...,\chi_{n}
. By Proposition
A.1.4
, there are nonnegative real numbers
c_{1},...,c_{n}
such that

\langle h,\gamma\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}d\chi_{i}(\gamma).

Since
\langle h,\gamma\rangle<0
, we must therefore have
d\chi_{i}(\gamma)<0
for some
\chi_{i}
. Substituting
w^{-1}\lambda_{f}-\lambda_{f}
for
\gamma
and
\frac{1}{2}h+z_{f}
for
\lambda_{f}
, we get the following inequality:

(A.2.10)

d\chi_{i}(w^{-1}\lambda_{f})<d\chi_{i}(\lambda_{f})=\frac{1}{2}d\chi_{i}(h)+d\chi_{i}(z_{f})=\frac{1}{2}d\chi_{i}(h).

Step 4.
Let
\Delta(V)=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{l})\mid\alpha(h)=2\}
, the set of roots in
V
, and choose a basis of nonzero root vectors
\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta(V)}
. Recall that
f_{i}
is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial on
V
. Let
d_{i}
denote its homogeneous degree. Then
f_{i}(x)
is of the following form:

f_{i}(x)=\sum_{|S|=d_{i}}c_{S}\prod_{\alpha\in S}x(\alpha),\qquad x=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta(V)}x(\alpha)E_{\alpha}.

where the sum runs over all order-
d_{i}
multisets of roots in
\Delta(V)=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{l})\mid\alpha(h)=2\}
and the coefficients
c_{S}
are complex numbers (not all
0
). Moreover, since
f_{i}
is a relative invariant with character
\chi_{i}
, we have

(A.2.11)

d\chi_{i}=\sum S,

for any order-
d_{i}
multiset
S\subset\Delta(V)
with
c_{S}\neq 0
.

Suppose that
x\in\Ad(L_{g})e
. Then in particular
f_{i}(x)\neq 0
. This means that there is an order-
d_{i}
multiset
S\subset\Delta(V)
such that
c_{S}\neq 0
and
\prod_{\alpha\in S}x(\alpha)\neq 0
. In particular
S\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(x)
and so

(A.2.12)

wS\subseteq w\mathrm{Supp}(x)=\mathrm{Supp}(\Ad(\dot{w})x).

Evaluating (
A.2.11
) at the element
h\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, we obtain the equation

d\chi_{i}(h)=(\sum S)(h)=2d_{i}.

Combining with (
A.2.10
), we get a strict inequality

\sum_{\alpha\in S}\alpha(w^{-1}\lambda_{f})<d_{i}.

Since the sum on the left consists of
d_{i}
terms, there must be some
\alpha\in S
such that
\alpha(w^{-1}\lambda_{f})<1
. Since
\lambda_{f}
, and hence
w^{-1}\lambda_{f}
, has integral weights on
\mathfrak{g}
, this implies that
\alpha(w^{-1}\lambda_{f})\leq 0
, i.e. that
w\alpha\notin\Delta(\mathfrak{u}_{f})
. But by (
A.2.12
),
w\alpha\in\mathrm{Supp}(\Ad(\dot{w})x)
. We conclude that
\Ad(\dot{w})x\notin\mathfrak{u}_{f}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, Theorem
A.2.1
follows immediately from Propositions
A.2.2
and
A.2.4
.
