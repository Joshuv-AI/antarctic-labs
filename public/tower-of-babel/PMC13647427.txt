Computational Study
of CO2 Capture by Metallic
Nanoparticles in Diethylenetriamine Solution

Marcus Vinícius F. Rodrigues, Lucas Lima Bezerra, Leonardo Paes da Silva, Norberto de Kássio Vieira Monteiro

Abstract

The high concentration of CO2 present in the
atmosphere
caused by human action is triggering a series of climate disorders
worldwide, such as an increase in global temperatures. Therefore,
it is necessary to develop new efficient and sustainable technologies
to remove excess CO2 gas from the atmosphere. Based on
this problem, this work aims to investigate the action of noble metal
nanoparticles contained in diethylenetriamine (DETA) on CO2 capture through computational simulations. First, platinum nanoparticles
(PtNPs) and palladium nanoparticles (PdNPs) are obtained by the CHARMM-GUI
server, and DETA, CO2, and H2O optimizations
are based on Density Functional Theory (DFT). Finally, from Molecular
Dynamics (MD), the configurations of Interaction Potential Energy
(IPE), Radial Distribution Function (RDF), Spatial Distribution Function
(SDF) and weak interactions analysis, Non-Covalent Interactions (NCI)
and Thermal Fluctuation index (TFI) are used to confirm the best system
for CO2 capture. The simulations indicate that PtNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 is the best system, showing the longest
stability in the root mean square deviation (RMSD) analysis, the lowest
IPE (−16003.61 kJ mol–1); the PtNP in this
system has a superior g(r) when
compared to the PdNP, and spatial distribution in SDF analysis and
the presence of strong interactions and stability between CO2 and DETA are demonstrated in aIGM and TFI images, respectively.
These results suggest future application of these materials in a practical
structure, like industrial plants.

Introduction

Nowadays, one of the biggest
challenges is the gradual increase
in the global temperature. The main cause of this thermal imbalance
is greenhouse gas emissions, which are intensified by anthropic actions
like consumption of fossil fuels and forest deforestation.
,
 The greenhouse effect is the heat retention from gases like methane
(CH4), water vapor (H2O), and carbon dioxide
(CO2). For this reason, the
life of various fauna and flora is possible because this effect allows
for a milder temperature in the atmosphere.
,
 However,
the increase in the concentration of these gases, primarily CO2, which is a product of the combustion of fossil fuels such
as oil derivatives and mineral coal, generates a deregulation in temperature
in the atmosphere. This increase was studied by the Mauna Loa Observatory,
which showed that the acceptable limit of CO2 in the atmosphere
(350 ppm) was exceeded in 2022 (421 ppm).
,

This fuel combustion occurs, for the most part, due to improvements
in world energy consumption since the energy matrices used are oil
and its derivatives (29.5%), mineral coal (27.2%), and natural gas
(23.6%), according to the International Energy Agency (IEA). Besides, the main fuels used in most common vehicles
are oil derivatives such as gasoline, diesel, natural gas, and aviation
fuel.
,
 Therefore, developing new greenhouse gas
capture technologies, especially for CO2, is necessary
to reduce global warming and alleviate the climate crisis.

In
Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS), the process involves four
categories: precombustion, chemical looping combustion, oxyfuel combustion,
and postcombustion.
−

 The latter is the most adaptable and recommended for studies on
generating gas sources. One option for
postcombustion CCS is amine-based solvents like diethylenetriamine
(DETA).
−

 This solvent presents low reactivity compared to
reference solvents like monoethanolamine (MEA), which has high volatility
and causes damage to industrial equipment.
,,
 Another aspect to consider is the CO2 absorption kinetics and the CO2 absorption capacity
of DETA, which is superior to MEA due to having more amine groups.
−

 Wu et al. studied DETA modified with
hydroxyalkyls for CO2 absorption. Their computational analysis
used the DFT method to determine the Gibbs free energy of initial,
transitional, and final states of the reactions using the software
Gaussian 16 with the B3LYP/6-311++(2d,p) configuration. The results
indicated that 2-hydroxypropyl-DETA presented the best results for
CO2 capture with a lower free energy barrier and a favorable
electrostatic potential (ESP).

Another material with good absorption
capacity for CCS is metallic
nanoparticles (MNPs), particularly noble metals.
−

 These metals have higher catalytic properties in
bimetallic alloys or modified surfaces applied in Hydrogen Evolution
Reaction (HER) and Oxygen Evolution Reaction (OER) in fuel cells or
electrocatalysts.
−

 Besides, these noble metals have applications in
other reactions, such as gold used as a catalyst in hydrogen production
in the water–gas shift process, beyond platinum, palladium, and rhodium present in automotive exhaust
catalysts. Li et al. studied the performance of Na-promoted (Pt–Na) Pt
nanoparticles supported in Al2O3 for CO2 capture and selective hydrogenation. The analysis of catalyst
tests confirmed the best adsorption for CO2 and selectivity
for CO formation at high temperatures (623.15 K) when compared to
Mg addition.

CO2 capture with MNPs requires solvents
for NP formation,
such as ionic liquids (IL)
,
 and deep eutectic solvents
(DES).
−

 These solvents stabilize the thermodynamic instability of MNPs,
avoiding aggregation and preserving their catalytic behavior.
,
 Although there are no reports indicating that DETA can serve as
a stabilizing agent, the presence of amine groups in both IL and DES
suggests its possible use as a stabilizing agent.

Torkzadeh
and Moosavi studied the
interaction between dicationic ionic liquids (DILs) and CO2. The DILs [Bis­(mim)­C5]­[NTf2]2,
[Bis­(mim)­C5-(C4)2]­[NTf2]2 and [N111-C5-mim]­[NTf2]2 were analyzed to verify the effect of cation symmetry
and chain length, and the analysis showed that ([Bis­(mim)­C5-(C4)2]­[NTf2]2) interacted
better with CO2. Shaikh et al. analyzed choline-based IL amino acids (glycine, alanine, serine,
and proline) and other anions (−COOH, −NH2, and −OH) with DMSO for CO2 capture with MD and
DFT analysis. Ghaed-Sharaf and Omidvar developed a study with green IL cholinium geranate and DES cholinium
geranate, through MD and DFT, which revealed higher interaction for
the cholinium geranate IL than the cholinium geranate DES. Li et al. analyzed metal-based ionic liquids (MBILs: [Bmim]­[Cl]
and [Bmim]­[FeCl4], [Bmim]2[ZnCl4],
[Bmim]2[CuCl4], and [Bmim]­[CrCl4])
to determine their mechanism of capture of CO2 and the
effect of metal in absorbing CO2 through MD. It was observed
that the highest interactions were between [Bmim]­[Cl] and [Bmim]2[ZnCl4] and CO2. Yang et al. used DFT to study the mechanism for CO2 capture and conversion into methane for photoreduction with Pd MNPs
supported on a TiO2 surface, showing that the presence
of MNPs improved the reaction performance. Liu et al. reported a study involving the use of an amino acid (l-Met) to form a CO2 hydrate from CO2 liquid,
a clathrate for CO2 capture in offshore structures. The
computational simulations indicated that l-Met showed a reduction
in energy barrier when compared with water and a preferential approximation
of CO2 on the hydrophobic amino acid part.

These
studies showed a direct relationship between amine-based
solvents and MNPs for CO2 capture; however, works involving
MNPs with amine solvents for CO2 capture through a computational
approach still remain poorly explored. Therefore, this work relates
the use of Pd and Pt MNPs (PdNPs and PtNPs, respectively) in a DETA
solution for CO2 capture through computational simulations.
Using MD, the goal was to understand the molecular interactions among
CO2, DETA, MNPs, and H2O and identify which
interactions are the strongest with CO2. The systems are
divided into MNP–DETA–H2O–CO2, MNP–CO2, and DETA–H2O–CO2 and compared to determine the best CO2 capture
system.

Computational Details

Molecular Structures

The structures
of DETA, CO2, and H2O were optimized using Density
Functional Theory (DFT) calculations, which were applied to determine
the lowest energy tridimensional structure using B3LYP
−

 as a hybrid functional with the basis set 6-311+G­(d,p), implemented in the Gaussian 09 software. The PdNPs and PtNPs were based on the Atilhan
and Aparicio study. The MNPs were spherical,
and the number of atoms was 201 for each. The diameters were 17.8
Å for PdNPs and 17.9 Å for PtNPs. First, the MNPs were built
in the CHARMM-GUI server in the Nanomaterial
Modeler module, and the topology files
were converted for the OPLS-AA force field by the TopolGen 1.1_dev script written by Lemkul.
 shows the structures of the components used in this work.

Molecular Dynamics

All simulations
were performed using the GROMACS software 2021.2 with the OPLS-AA force field, used in previous work involving gas capture.
,
 The CO2 parameters were obtained from Zhu et al., and water molecules were described by the TIP3P
method. The 36 DETA, 804 H2O, and 285 CO2 numbers were based on the experimental
study of Zhang et al., and the simulations
were performed in a cubic box with initial dimensions 12 × 12
× 12 nm3. The systems were divided into three groups:
MNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 (PtNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 and PdNP–DETA–H2O–CO2), MNP–CO2 (PtNP–CO2 and PdNP–CO2), and DETA–H2O–CO2.

The system’s geometry was optimized
using the steepest descent and the gradient
conjugate algorithms, with an energy
tolerance of 10 kJ mol–1, 105 steps,
and 10–4 nm step size. Then, the equilibration was
developed by using NVT and NPT ensembles for 400 ps using the LINCS
algorithm constraint, the Particle Mesh
Ewald (PME) for long-range interactions, and 1.0 nm as the cutoff radius for Coulombic and van der Waals
short-range interactions under the periodic boundary conditions (PBC).
The NVT ensemble was the first step with the V-rescale thermostat at 303.15 K for all systems, and the second
step, the NPT ensemble, utilized the C-rescale barostat with a pressure of 1.029 bar. Finally, the production
stage was realized with the Leap-Frog algorithm in 100 ns with the parameters of the equilibration stage.
Additionally, Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD) 1.9.3 was used to visualize MD results; each analysis was performed
in triplicate.

Weak Interactions Analysis

To identify
the weak interactions between MNPs and
DETA, H2O, and CO2, the average independent
gradient model (aIGM) was used through Multiwfn 3.8 software. This analysis used noncovalent interactions (NCIs),
which are illustrated as isosurfaces in selected regions to define
the weak intermolecular forces. The MNPs were selected to define isosurfaces
and classify interactions with DETA, H2O, and CO2 as follows: strong interactions are indicated by blue color, van
der Waals interactions by green color, and repulsive interactions
by red color. Thermal fluctuation interaction (TFI) analysis identified
the stability of interactions with highly stable ones represented
in blue, moderately stable ones represented in green, and weakly stable
ones indicated by red color (Figures S1–S3). Finally, the resulting images were generated by the VMD software.

Results and Discussion

Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD)

RMSD analysis was applied to systems to determine their equilibrium
in a range of 0 to 100 ns, represented in . This equilibrium indicates the stability
of the system’s components, and the reference selected in this
analysis was the entire system. In the PdNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 system (a), equilibrium was reported at approximately
40 ns, while in the PtNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 system (b) and PtNP–CO2 system (d) it was reached at 30 ns, in the PdNP–CO2 system (c) it was reached at 80 ns, and in the DETA–H2O–CO2 system (e) it was reached in 50 ns. The PtNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 system, b, reached equilibrium more quickly than
other systems, which indicated that it was the most stable. Furthermore,
the insertion of MNPs contributed to reducing RMSD values in a DETA–H2O–CO2 system, indicating good system stability.

Average IPE Values

presents the average IPE values
for the five systems to determine the best interaction with CO2 and, consequently, the best system for CO2 capture,
starting from the equilibrium time indicated by the RMSD results.
In MNP–CO2 systems, IPE average values were found
to be more negative for PtNPs (−4560.20 kJ mol–1) when compared to PdNPs (−3915.87 kJ mol–1). PtNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 demonstrated
the highest interactions with CO2 (−16003.61 kJ
mol–1) compared with PdNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 (−15867.81 kJ mol–1). Besides, a synergistic effect of MNPs when combined with DETA–H2O was observed, as shown by energy increase compared to the
DETA–H2O–CO2 interaction (−15194.25
kJ mol–1). These results suggest that the PtNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 system with lower IPE was the best
system for CO2 capture. Even though the IPE value of PtNPs
is lower than those of DETA and H2O, the relative contributions
of each component account for this disparity, and the MNPs can alter
the molecular distribution in the simulation box, thereby permitting
synergistic effects and modifying the capture behavior.

Conclusions

The simulations revealed
that the PdNP, PtNP, DETA, and H2O interact favorably with
CO2, with the PtNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 system exhibiting the strongest interaction.
For the PtNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 system, the RMSD graph indicates the most stable system, IPE values
exhibit lower energy, RDF analysis shows higher g(r), and SDF analysis illustrates the highest spatial
density. Furthermore, the aIGM images showed stronger interactions
between PtNPs and CO2 and DETA molecules around the MNPs,
indicating a possible stabilizing agent for CO2 capture,
and TFI analysis confirmed the highest stability interaction of PtNPs
with CO2, and the DETA molecules in the PtNP–DETA–H2O–CO2 system were more distributed than
those in the PdNP–DETA–H2O–CO2, showing a stabilization between PtNPs and DETA, which can
contribute to their synergetic effect.
