Introduction

Leadless pacemakers have been available since 2012, when the prototype, the Nanostim leadless pacemaker, was first introduced.1 Although this device represented a major advancement in pacing technology, it was later affected by a safety advisory that required explantation in many patients.2 Subsequent developments included the Micra Transcatheter Pacing System, which was first implanted in 2013,3 followed by the Aveir VR in 2020, which succeeded the Nanostim platform.4 Most recently, the dual-chamber Aveir DR system underwent its first implantation in 2022.5 These technologies have provided a relatively low-risk pacing option for patients at high risk of infection or with limited vascular access. We present a case of a complex retrieval of an Aveir AR in a hemodialysis patient who previously underwent implantation of an Aveir DR for sinus node dysfunction and subsequently reached the recommended replacement time for the Aveir AR portion (Figure 1).6Figure 1Aveir AR and VR showing the double helix in the AR: inner and outer coils. Case report

A 79-year-old man with end-stage renal disease, paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, prior aortic valve replacement for aortic stenosis (2015), and sinus node dysfunction who underwent a dual-chamber Aveir implantation in June 2022 as part of the IDE study presented in February 2026 with recommended replacement time for the Aveir AR. The atrial threshold was chronically elevated (1.5 V/0.5 ms or 1.25 V/0.8 ms), and implant-to-implant (i2i) communication was poor despite good proximity to the Aveir VR and being within 2 device lengths as recommended by the manufacturer (Figure 2). I2i is the beat-to-beat communication that occurs between paired implanted Aveir devices using low-energy, subthreshold pulses.7 Due to the higher threshold and poor i2i communication, the device was programmed as AAI only, and then later as AAI/VVI (backup), once this feature became available. In February 2026, he was seen in follow-up and found to have <3 months remaining on the battery; total dwell time for the device was 45.5 months. After full discussion regarding options, including transvenous pacing or programming to VVI only pacing mode, the patient opted to undergo explantation of the Aveir AR, followed by reimplantation in the same setting.Figure 2Chest radiograph (left: posteroanterior, right: lateral) demonstrating the original position of the Aveir DR system.

The patient was brought to the electrophysiology laboratory. The Aveir DR was unpaired, and the patient was programmed to VVI pacing at 40 beats per minute to provide pacing support during the procedure. The right femoral vein was used for access. 2 Perclose (Abbott Inc) sutures were placed initially to provide hemostasis at the conclusion of the procedure. Using ultrasound guidance, a 0.035-mm wire was placed into the right femoral vein. The vein was dilated using 16 Fr then 22 Fr dilatators, followed by placement of the introducer sheath. The Abbott retrieval catheter was prepared for insertion. The snare was hooked to the docking button successfully (Figure 3). The device was successfully docked onto the docking cap, and the protective sleeve was easily advanced over the device to cover half the device length (Figure 4). The rotation mechanism was then turned counterclockwise to begin explantation (Video 1). Initially, there was some resistance, but then the device rotated with a 1:1 response to the counterclockwise rotation. As the device continued to rotate, the helix was seen to begin extending and stretching. It was then realized that the helix had fractured, and a portion was left behind in the atrial myocardium (Figure 5). The device was brought out of the vasculature and inspected (Figure 6A and 6B). Endothelial overgrowth was noticed around the midportion of the device. The fractured helix was smooth and did not have a sharp edge. The patient remained hemodynamically stable throughout the procedure and had no sequelae. A pigtail catheter was inserted to perform an atriogram using 15 cc of contrast in both the right anterior oblique projection (30 degrees) and the left anterior oblique projection (30 degrees). A new Atrial Aveir AR EL was implanted using the newer technique of having a better injury and more contact with the atrial wall (Figure 7). The initial threshold was 4.0 V/0.4 milliseconds, impedance was 300 Ω, and sensing was <0.5 mV. The introducer sheath was removed, and hemostasis was immediately obtained with the Perclose. The following day, the atrial sensing was 0.5 mV, the threshold was 0.5 V/0.5 milliseconds, and the impedance was 270 Ω. The patient was paced at AAIR 70 beats per minute, with backup VVI at 65 beats per minute.Figure 7Chest radiograph (left: posteroanterior, right: lateral) demonstrating newly placed Aveir AR, remnant of the fractured helix, and the prior Aveir VR.

Discussion

This is a case of an Aveir AR implanted for just under 4 years in a patient on hemodialysis. During rotation to retrieve the device, the helix fractured, and a portion was left in the right atrium. The device was found to have endothelialization over at least 50% of the body. The nature of this endothelialization was a very thin fibrous layer that did not interfere with retrieval. Helix fracture during retrieval has not been previously reported. The manufacturer’s report found no abnormalities with device function. Visual inspection noted that the outer helix was forcefully twisted into the inner helix, with both lengths being shorter than expected, due to the fractured part that remained embedded in the atrial wall. No other abnormalities were reported, nor was there any further insight as to the cause of this fracture. Possible mechanisms for this occurrence include damage during the initial implantation that led to the fracture at retrieval, complete enveloping of the outer helix with endothelium, and fibrosis that resulted in a force that is larger than that exerted by the rotation of the Aveir AR. It is also possible that the longer dwell time contributed to the helix fracture. Importantly, there was no evidence of negative clinical sequelae for the patient.

In 2026, many of the patients who underwent leadless pacemaker implantation as part of the initial studies, or as part of routine clinical use, are now presenting with the need for a new device due to battery depletion. In the IDE study, the Aveir AR had higher thresholds and decreased battery longevity due to the use of i2i communication, as required by that study.5 Once the IDE study was completed and very few complications occurred with the atrial device (2 pericardial effusions), the technique for implanting was changed to permit greater forward pressure and better contact with atrial tissue at the time of deployment.6 The only reports of chronically implanted retrieval of dual-chamber pacemakers are in an ovine model.8 Few reports of Aveir AR retrieval have been chronically implanted, which offer different complexity than the ventricular counterpart due to the presence of a double helix at the point of anchoring with the myocardium (Figure 1).6 Ip et al9 reported on 10 patients who underwent explant with a mean time from implant being 23.5 ± 6.6 months (range 13.0–31.2 months), with no complications being reported. The LEADLESS II study chronicled the outcomes of 241 retrievals of the Nanostim device, with 231 of these occurring more than 6 weeks after implant. The mean time from implantation to retrieval was 3.2 ± 1.8 years (range 0.2–9.0 years). The success rate was not correlated with implant time. Main complications (n = 11) in this study included valve damage (n = 3), inability to release snare from docking button (n = 2), access site issues (n = 3), cardiac tamponade (n = 1), pericardial effusion (n = 1), and detached docking button (n = 1).

Conclusion

This is a case of a hemodialysis patient with a high risk of infection who underwent an Aveir DR requiring retrieval of the Aveir AR portion due to recommended replacement that was complicated by an outer helix fracture during retrieval. This complication has not been previously reported and will require further work to try to mitigate in future Aveir AR retrievals.
