The equivalence between timelike Ricci curvature and the timelike Brunn-Minkowski inequality on synthetic Lorentzian spaces
Osama Farooqui

Abstract
We introduce the strong q q -timelike Brunn-Minkowski condition 𝗌𝖳𝖡𝖬 q ​ ( K , N ) \mathsf{sTBM}_{q}(K,N) on synthetic Lorentzian spaces, for 0 < q < 1 0<q<1 . We show that, in the timelike q q -essentially non-branching setting, the q q -timelike curvature dimension condition 𝖳𝖢𝖣 q ​ ( K , N ) \mathsf{TCD}_{q}(K,N) is equivalent to 𝖳𝖡𝖬 q ​ ( K , N + ) \mathsf{TBM}_{q}(K,N^{+}) , and that the entropic q q -timelike curvature dimension condition 𝖳𝖢𝖣 q e ​ ( K , N ) \mathsf{TCD}_{q}^{e}(K,N) is equivalent to the reduced 𝗌𝖳𝖡𝖬 \mathsf{sTBM} condition, 𝗌𝖳𝖡𝖬 q ∗ ​ ( K , N ) \mathsf{sTBM}_{q}^{*}(K,N) . This extends, to a non-smooth setting, our earlier work in proving the equivalence between Ricci curvature and the Brunn-Minkowski inequality on C 2 C^{2} spacetimes.



1
Introduction

This report is concerned with the Brunn-Minkowski inequality on synthetic Lorentzian spaces. Lorentzian spaces carry a causal structure induced by the so-called ‘‘time separation function’’, in analogy with the metric structure induced by the distance function on complete Riemannian manifolds. Continuing the analogy, one might be interested in forgetting the smooth structure of the manifold and studying only the causal structure. Work in this direction is old indeed and has been revisited numerous times in the last 100 years, see
[
21
,
11
,
19
,
12
]
. The program is less intuitive than it’s Riemannian counterpart, in part due to the lack of topological data that can be extracted from the time separation function, and moreover due to recent interest in constructing spaces which are amenable to the tools of optimal transport. The first of these more modern definitions of ‘‘synthetic’’ Lorentzian spaces is Kunzinger–Sämann’s
Lorentzian length space

[
12
]
. Soon after, McCann introduced the notion of
metric spacetime

[
17
]
; he later helped refine this definition as part of the octet of Beran
et al.

[
4
]
. Within these two synthetic Lorentzian spaces, one can define the
\TCD
condition – a non-smooth analogue of uniform Ricci lower bounds in timelike directions on a Lorentzian manifold. The
\TCD
condition was first introduced in the smooth setting by McCann
[
16
]
, and independently, Mondino and Suhr
[
18
]
. Later Braun
[
7
]
introduced the
\TCD_{q}
condition on Lorentzian length spaces, following Cavalletti and Mondino’s
[
8
]
introduction of the ‘‘entropic’’
\TCD^{e}_{q}
condition. Beran
et al.
introduced a weaker condition,
\TMCP_{h}^{+}
, on metric spacetimes, but it’s fairly easy to see what the correct notion of
\TCD
would be on these spaces.

As we mentioned earlier, Lorentzian synthetic spaces are analogues of metric measure spaces. Similarly is
\TCD
an analogue of the
\CD
condition, introduced by Lott–Villani
[
13
]
, and independently, Sturm
[
23
,
22
]
. Roughly speaking,
\CD
is a convexity requirement of the entropy functional along a geodesic of probability measures. One of the strengths of the
\CD
formulation is the recovery of well-known geometric inequalities, notably the Brunn-Minkowski inequality, suitably modified to account for the lower Ricci bounds. Recall that the Brunn-Minkowski inequality is a concavity requirement of the
n
-th root of the volume of compact sets along a geodesic interpolation.

Recently Magnabosco, Portinale, and Rossi
[
14
]
proved that the Brunn-Minkowski inequality is sufficient to deduce the convexity of the entropy on weighted Riemannian manifolds. In
[
15
]
the same authors introduced the notion of the
strong Brunn-Minkowski inequality
, which requires concavity of the
n
-th root of the volume along a geodesic of probability measures, and showed that the condition is equivalent to
\CD(K,N)
in the essentially non-branching metric measure space setting.

In a previous paper
[
25
]
, we proved an equivalence between the timelike Brunn Minkowski inequality and timelike Ricci lower bounds on
C^{2}
-smooth weighted spacetimes. In this paper we introduce a
strong timelike Brunn-Minkowski inequality
, and reproduce the results of
[
15
]
in the synthetic Lorentzian setting. Our main results are
Theorem

3.8
and
Theorem

3.10
, which can be summarized as follows.

Theorem
:
Fix
0<q<1
. Let
X
be a timelike
q
-essentially non-branching globally hyperbolic synthetic spacetime with
\ell_{+}
continuous. Then
X\in\sTBM_{q}^{*}(K,N)
iff
X\in\TCD_{q}^{e}(K,N)
, and
X\in\sTBM_{q}(K,N^{+})
iff
X\in\TCD_{q}(K,N)
.

In section 2 we recall the definition of metric spacetimes, optimal transport on these spaces, and the curvature conditions
\TCD_{q}^{(e)}(K,N)
, and
\sTBM_{q}^{(*)}(K,N)
. We also discuss the notion of ‘‘simple measures’’, which are central to the proof, as they were in
[
15
]
; in that paper they are referred to as ‘‘step measures’’, whereas we opt for the term ‘‘simple measure’’ in analogy with simple functions. In section 3 we prove our main results. We begin by verifying that
\TCD
spaces indeed recover the
\sTBM
property. We then show that
\sTBM
spaces satisfy a weaker curvature condition,
\TMCP
, which simplifies our analysis significantly and allows us to use the arguments in
[
15
]
to prove our main results.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Lorentzian Synthetic Spaces

Let us reiterate that there is some freedom in choosing the spaces that we will work in. Our framework will be based on Beran
et al.
’s metric spacetimes. We want to include some additional properties within the construction of our spaces, and want as well an efficient name to refer to spaces with such properties; we opt for the somewhat generic label of ‘‘synthetic spacetime’’. We will endeavour to draw a connection between our synthetic spacetimes and the other examples highlighted in the introduction, whenever possible. Most of, if not all, of the following definitions are drawn from
[
17
]
.

2.1.1
Causal Spaces

Definition 2.1
.

A
causal space
is a pair
(X,\ell)
consisting of a set
X
, and a
time separation function

\ell:X\times X\to\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
, with the following properties

1.

(Causal reflexivity):

\ell(x,x)\geq 0\quad\forall x\in X
;

2.

(Reverse triangle inequality):

\ell(x,y)\geq\ell(x,z)+\ell(z,y)\quad\forall x,y,z\in X
,

with the convention that
-\infty+a=a+(-\infty)=-\infty
for all
a\in\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
. We often use the notation
\ell_{+}:=\max\{\ell,0\}
.

The time separation induces a binary relation, called the
causal order
. We say
x
is in the
causal past
of
y
, and write
x\leq y
, iff
\ell(x,y)\geq 0
. Similarly, we say
x
is in the
chronological past
of
y
, and write
x\ll y
iff
\ell(x,y)>0
.

We remark that if the relation
\leq
is antisymmetric, i.e
x\leq y
and
y\leq x
imply
x=y
, then
\leq
is a partial order, and
(X,\leq,\ll)
is a causal set in the sense of Kronheimer–Penrose
[
11
]
. Antisymmetry does not hold in our general setting, but is inherited under stronger assumptions on
(X,\ell)
.

We define the
causal set

X_{\leq}^{2}:=\{(x,y)\in X^{2}:x\leq y\},

and the
chronological set

X_{\ll}^{2}:=\{(x,y)\in X^{2}:x\ll y\}.

For each
x\in M
, we denote the
causal past
of
x
by
J^{-}(x):=\{y\in X:y\leq x\}
, and the
causal future
of
x
by
J^{+}(x):=\{y\in X:x\leq y\}
. Then for a set
A\subset X
, we define the causal future/past of
A
as

J^{\pm}(A):=\bigcup_{a\in A}J^{\pm}(a).

For two sets
A,B\subset X
, we define the
causal emerald
of
A
and
B
,
J(A,B)
, as

J(A,B):=J^{+}(A)\cap J^{-}(B).

One could also define similar constructions for the chronological relation, with similar notation; commonly, one replaces
J
with an
I
. Such sets will not be relevant to our discussion here.

Definition 2.2

(Paths and the age of a path)
.

A
path
is a map
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
. A path is
causal
if it is increasing in the causal order, i.e
s\leq t\implies\gamma_{s}\leq\gamma_{t}
. A causal path is
timelike

\ell(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{t})>0
for all
s\leq t
, and
null
if
\ell(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{t})=0
for all
s\leq t
. The
age
of a causal path is defined by

\age(\gamma):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell(\gamma_{t_{i-1}},\gamma_{t_{i}}):N\in\mathbb{N},0=t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq\dots\leq t_{N}=1\right\}.

Proposition 2.1

(Properties of
\age
)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a causal space. Then
\age
is additive under concatenation, and invariant under (continuous, strictly increasing) reparametrization.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
and
\sigma
be two causal paths such that
\gamma_{1}=\sigma_{0}
. Define the concatenation

\gamma\oplus\sigma:=\begin{cases}\gamma_{2t}&0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2};\\
\sigma_{2t-1}&\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 1.\end{cases}

Let
\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a partition of
[0,1]
. Let
t_{j}
be the largest element which is smaller than
1/2
. Then
\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{j}\cup\{1/2\}
and
\{1/2\}\cup\{t_{i}\}_{i=j+1}^{N}
are partitions of
[0,1/2]
and
[1/2,1]
, and

\displaystyle\age(\gamma)+\age(\sigma)\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j}\ell(\gamma_{2t_{i-1}},\gamma_{2t_{i}})+\ell(\gamma_{2t_{i}},\gamma_{1})\right)+\left(\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{2t_{j+1}-1})+\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}\ell(\sigma_{2t_{i-1}-1},\sigma_{2t_{i}-1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{j}\ell((\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{t_{i-1}},(\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{t_{i}})+\ell((\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{t_{j}},(\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{1/2})

\displaystyle+\ell((\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{1/2},(\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{t_{j+1}})+\sum_{i=j+1}^{N}\ell((\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{t_{i-1}},(\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{t_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell((\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{t_{i-1}},(\gamma\oplus\sigma)_{t_{i}}).

Taking infimum over all partitions
\{t_{i}\}
, we obtain

\age(\gamma)+\age(\sigma)\leq\age(\gamma\oplus\sigma).

Similarly, we may show that

\age(\gamma\oplus\sigma)\leq\age(\gamma)+\age(\sigma),

and thus we achieve equality. Now we show invariance under reparametrization. Let
\tilde{\gamma}
be a reparametrization of a causal path
\gamma
; that is,
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma\circ\phi
, where
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is a continuous and strictly increasing bijection.
If
\{t_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
is a partition of
[0,1]
, then so too is
\{\phi(t_{i})\}_{i=0}^{N}
,

\age(\gamma)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell(\gamma_{\phi(t_{i-1})},\gamma_{\phi(t_{i})})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{t_{i-1}},\tilde{\gamma}_{t_{i}}).

Taking infimums over
\{t_{i}\}
gives
\age(\gamma)\leq\age(\tilde{\gamma})
. Using the invertibility of
\phi
, we may similarly prove that
\age(\tilde{\gamma})\leq\age(\gamma)
.
∎

Definition 2.3

(Age maximizing and geodesic paths)
.

A causal path
\gamma
is
age-maximizing
if
\age(\gamma)=\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
. A causal path
\gamma
is a
geodesic path
if there is a reparametrization
\tilde{\gamma}
of
\gamma
such that
\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{s},\tilde{\gamma}_{t})=(t-s)\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{0},\tilde{\gamma}_{1})
for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
.

Clearly every geodesic path is age-maximizing.
2.2
below will give conditions under which the converse also holds.

2.1.2
Topology on Causal Spaces

Definition 2.4

(Topological and metric causal spaces)
.

A
topological causal space
is a triple
(X,\ell,\tau)
consisting of a causal space
(X,\ell)
and a topology
\tau
. When this topology is metrizable, we say that
X
is a
metrizable causal space
. A metrizable causal space is
Polish
if the topology can be generated by a complete and separable metric.

Definition 2.5

(Age-maximizing and geodesic curves)
.

On a topological causal space, a
curve
is a continuous path, i.e. a continuous map
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
. A continuous age-maximizing path is called an
age-maximizing curve
and a continuous geodesic path is called a
geodesic
.

Proposition 2.2

(Equivalence between age-maximizing and geodesic curves)
.

Let
(X,\ell,\tau)
be a metric spacetime, and suppose
\ell
is upper semicontinuous and does not attain
\infty
. Then every age-maximizing curve is a geodesic.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be an age-maximizing curve. Since
\ell_{+}
is finite,
\age(\gamma)=A<\infty
. If
M=0
, then
\gamma
is null, and thus must be a geodesic. So assume
A\neq 0
.

Define
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
via
\phi(t):=A^{-1}\age(\gamma|_{[0,t]})
. It is easy to show that
\phi
is strictly increasing. It is also continuous, since for
s<t
,
\phi(t)-\phi(s)=A^{-1}\age(\gamma|_{[s,t]})\leq A^{-1}\ell(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))
, and the map
(s,t)\mapsto\ell(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))
is upper semicontinuous and vanishes along the diagonal. Then
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma\circ\phi^{-1}
is a reparametrization of
\gamma
. Moreover we have

\age(\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,t]})=\age(\gamma|_{[0,\phi^{-1}(t)]})=A\phi(\phi^{-1}(t))=At=t\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}),

and therefore

\ell(\tilde{\gamma}_{s},\tilde{\gamma}_{t})=\age(\tilde{\gamma}|_{[s,t]})=(t-s)\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}).

∎

From now on we assume that every geodesic
\gamma
is parametrized such that
\ell(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{t})=(t-s)\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
. We now show that timelike geodesic paths can be made to be continuous under certain conditions.

A central condition is
global hyperbolicity
, which is somehow analogous to properness in metric spaces.

Definition 2.6

(Global hyperbolicity)
.

A topological causal space
(X,\ell,\tau)
is
globally hyperbolic
if for any two compact subsets
C_{1},C_{2}\subset X
, the causal emerald
J(C_{1},C_{2})
is compact.

This definition of global hyperbolicity excludes the standard condition of
non-totally imprisoning
which is usually paired with the compactness of diamonds. However, Minguzzi showed that the standard definition is equivalent to compactness of diamonds and causal closedness. In our setting, we inherit causal closedness from upper semicontinuity of the time separation, so we reserve the term ‘‘globally hyperbolic’’ for the compactness of emeralds only. This keeps us close to
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Continuity of timelike geodesic paths)
.

Let
(X,\ell,\tau)
be a topological causal space such that the following 4 properties hold:

1.

Every pair of causally related points can be joined by an age-maximizing curve;

2.

Every age-maximizing curve joining a pair of timelike related points is timelike;

3.

The time separation
\ell
is upper semi-continuous;

4.

The space
X
is globally hyperbolic.

Then every timelike geodesic path of finite age is continuous.

Remark:
Any topological causal space satisfying the property
2
is said to be
regular
. Notice that if we add the condition that
\ell
does not attain
\infty
, then by
2.2
, every age-maximizing curve is a geodesic, and so regularity is satisfied.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be a timelike geodesic path with finite age. Let
s\in(0,1]
. Then
J(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{s})
is compact, and
\gamma_{t}\in J(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{s})
for all
t\in(0,s)
. Let
\{t_{n}\}
be a sequence which increases to
s
. By compactness,
\gamma_{t_{n}}
admits some subsequential limit point
p\in J(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{s})
. Upper semicontinuity of
\ell
then confirms that
\ell(\gamma_{0},p)\geq\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{s})
and
\ell(p,\gamma_{s})\geq 0
.

We may find two age-maximizing curves
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
joining
\gamma_{0}
to
p
, and joining
p
to
\gamma_{s}
respectively. Concatenating these curves gives a curve
\sigma:=\sigma_{1}\oplus\sigma_{2}
such that

\age(\sigma)=\age(\sigma_{1})+\age(\sigma_{2})=\ell(\gamma_{0},p)+\ell(p,\gamma_{1})\geq\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{s}).

Since of course
\age(\sigma)\leq\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{s})
, we obtain equality in all above inequalities, which verifies that
\sigma
is an age maximizing curve joining
\gamma_{0}
to
\gamma_{1}
, and moreover gives that
\ell(\gamma_{0},p)=\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{s})
and
\ell(p,\gamma_{s})=0
. But this last equality violates regularity unless
p=\gamma_{s}
. Arbitrariness of the (sub)sequence
\{t_{n}\}
then shows that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to s^{-}}\gamma_{t}=\gamma_{s}
for all
s\in(0,1]
. A similar proof shows that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to s^{+}}\gamma_{t}=\gamma_{s}
for all
s\in[0,1)
. Thus
\gamma
is continuous.

∎

Definition 2.7

(Synthetic spacetime)
.

We say that a topological causal space
X=(X,\ell,\tau)
is a
synthetic spacetime
if the following hold:

1.

The topology
\tau
is Polish;

2.

The time separation
\ell
is upper semicontinuous and does not attain
+\infty
;

3.

(
Causally geodesic
) Every pair of causally related points can be joined by an causal geodesic curve.

Throughout this paper, we will be working in synthetic spacetimes which are globally hyperbolic and in which
\ell_{+}:=\max\{\ell,0\}
is continuous. However we would also like to restrict our definition to those properties which are inherited by the space of probability measures (see
Lemma

2.15
), and the two properties noted above are not in this class.

Remark:
(Comparison with other Lorentzian synthetic spaces). As we said, our definition of spacetimes are close to the ‘Polish metric spacetimes’ of
[
4
]
. For them, the basic working assumptions are Polishness of the topology, antisymmetry of the causal order and so called
forward completeness
: every sequence which is increasing and bounded above in the causal order attains a limit. For us, antisymmetry is guaranteed by the fact that
\ell
does not attain
+\infty
, and completeness (both forward and backward) will be implied by global hyperbolicity.

We are somewhat further away from the Lorentzian length spaces of Kunzinger and Sämann. There are some technical assumptions that stand in the way of drawing a general equivalence, but in light of
[
17
]
, we can say that our globally hyperbolic synthetic spacetime becomes their globally hyperbolic Lorentzian length space if one first fixes a metric
d
which generates the topology, and then adds lower semicontinuity of
\ell_{+}:=\max\{\ell,0\}
, and non-emptiness of the sets
I^{\pm}(x)
for all
x\in X
. One should also note that for them, curves are always
d
-Lipschitz continuous.

2.2
Optimal Transport on Lorentzian Synthetic Spaces

2.2.2
The Causal Structure on Probability Measures

We now define the causal structure on
\mathcal{P}(X)
. For
q\in(0,1)
, we define the
q
-Eckstein-Miller time separation
,
\ell_{q}
, as

\displaystyle\ell_{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}):=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})}\left(\int_{X\times X}\ell^{q}(x,y)d\pi(x,y)\right)^{1/q},

(2)

with the convention
(-\infty)^{q}=(-\infty)^{1/q}=-\infty

[
9
, Def. 3]
, who to our knowledge were the first to introduce a time separation of this form.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(X,\ell,\tau)
be a metrizable causal space. For each
q\in(0,1)
,
\ell_{q}
is a time separation.

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. Then
(\id,\id)_{\#}\mu
is an admissible plan, and therefore

\ell_{q}(\mu,\mu)\geq\left(\int\ell^{q}(x,x)d\mu(x)\right)^{1/q}\geq 0.

Now we prove the reverse triangle inequality. Let
\mu,\nu,\upsilon\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. We want to show that

\ell_{q}(\mu,\nu)\geq\ell_{q}(\mu,\upsilon)+\ell_{q}(\upsilon,\nu).

WLOG assume that
\ell_{q}(\mu,\upsilon)\geq 0
and
\ell_{q}(\upsilon,\nu)\geq 0
. In particular, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there must exist admissible couplings
\pi_{1}^{n}\in\Pi(\mu,\upsilon)
,
\pi_{2}^{n}\in\Pi(\upsilon,\nu)
such that

\ell_{q}(\mu,\upsilon)\leq\left(\int\ell^{q}(x,y)d\pi_{1}^{n}(x,y)\right)^{1/q}+\varepsilon;\quad\ell_{q}(\upsilon,\nu)\leq\left(\int\ell^{q}(x,y)d\pi_{2}^{n}(x,y)\right)^{1/q}+\varepsilon.

Disintegrate
\pi_{1}^{n}
along
p_{2}
, and
\pi_{2}^{n}
along
p_{1}
to get
d\pi_{1}^{n}:=(\pi_{1}^{n})_{x}d\eta(x)
and
d\pi_{2}^{n}:=(\pi_{2}^{n})_{x}d\eta(x)
, and define

\bar{\pi}:=\int(\pi_{1}^{n})_{x}\otimes(\pi_{2}^{n})_{x}d\eta(x)\in\mathcal{P}(X\times X\times X)

We define
\pi:=(p_{1,3})_{\#}\bar{\pi}
, where
p_{1,3}(x,y,z)=(x,z)
. One can check by applying the projection maps that
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
. From Minkowski’s reverse inequality, we then see that

\displaystyle\ell_{q}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int\ell^{q}(x,y)d\pi(x,y)\right)^{1/q}=\left(\int\ell^{q}(x,z)d\bar{\pi}(x,y,z)\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int\ell^{q}(x,y)d\bar{\pi}(x,y,z)\right)^{1/q}+\left(\int\ell^{q}(y,z)d\bar{\pi}(x,y,z)\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\ell_{q}(\mu,\upsilon)+\ell_{q}(\upsilon,\nu)-2\varepsilon.

As
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, we achieve the result.
∎

Thus for each
q\in(0,1)
,
(\mathcal{P}(X),\ell_{q})
is a metrizable causal space when equipped with the narrow topology. We will see later that when
X
is a compact synthetic spacetime,
\mathcal{P}(X)
is also a compact synthetic spacetime (
Lemma

2.15
).

attains the supremum in (

When

(Existence of optimal couplings,

A useful characterization of optimal couplings is that of cyclical monotonicity.

Definition 2.11

(Cyclical monotonicity)

Let

-cyclically monotone

Proposition 2.10

(Optimality and cyclical monotonicity,
[
8
, Prop. 2.8]
)
.

Let
(\mu,\nu)
be a dualizable pair of measures, and let

\ell_{q}
-cyclically monotone set contained in

Definition 2.12

(Chronology for measures)

…

Proposition 2.11

(Stability of optimal couplings for totally timelike pairs)
.

Let
(X,\ell,\tau,\mathfrak{m})
be a measured topological causal space with
\ell_{+}
continuous. Let
\{(\mu^{n}_{0},\mu^{n}_{1})\}
be a sequence of totally timelike pairs of measures which are concentrated on the same compact set
K\subset X_{\ll}^{2}
. Then
(\mu^{n}_{0},\mu^{n}_{1})
has a narrow subsequential limit
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, and any sequence
\pi^{n}\in\Opt_{q}(\mu_{0}^{n},\mu_{1}^{n})
has a further narrow subsequential limit
\pi\in\TOpt_{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
.

Proof.

Compactness of
K
implies tightness of
\{(\mu_{0}^{n},\mu_{1}^{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and so the set admits some subsequential limit
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
which is also concentrated on
K
. Moreover tightness implies that any sequence
\{\pi^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\pi^{n}\in\Opt_{q}(\mu_{0}^{n},\mu_{1}^{n})
has a further subsequential limit
\pi\in\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
. As each pair
(\mu_{0}^{n},\mu_{1}^{n})
is totally timelike, each
\pi^{n}
is concentrated on an
\ell_{+}^{q}
-cyclically monotone set contained in
X_{\ll}^{2}
, and since
\ell_{+}
is continuous, this property is inherited in the (subsequential) limit. Therefore
\pi
is concentrated on a
\ell_{+}^{q}
-cyclically monotone set and concentrated on
K\subset X_{\ll}^{2}
, and therefore by
2.10
is
q
-optimal.
∎

Proposition 2.12

(Restriction preserves optimality)
.

Suppose

-optimal between its marginals. For each

with

, the measure

defined by

is also timelike

-optimal between its marginals.

Proof.

Evidently the measure
\pi_{f}
is concentrated on the same set as
\pi
. As

is timelike and

-optimal, by
2.10
is concentrated on an
\ell_{q}
-cyclically monotone set

, and therefore so is

. By

,
\pi_{f}
is
q
-optimal between its marginals.
∎

2.2.3
Optimal Plans and Geodesics

Given
t\in[0,1]
, we define the
t
-evaluation map

e_{t}:C([0,1],X)\to X
given by
e_{t}(\gamma):=\gamma_{t}
. We also define the space
\Geo(X)\subset C([0,1],X)
to be the set of geodesics parametrized in
[0,1]
.

Definition 2.13

(Optimal plans and displacement geodesics)
.

A measure
\eta\in\mathcal{P}(\Geo(X))
is a
q
-optimal plan
between
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}(X)^{2}
if
(e_{0}\times e_{1})_{\#}\eta\in\Opt_{q}(\mu,\nu)
. The set of
q
-optimal plans from
\mu
to
\nu
is denoted
\OptGeo_{q}(\mu,\nu)
. A plan
\eta
is
timelike
if it is concentrated on

, and the set of timelike
q
-optimal plans is denoted
\TOptGeo_{q}(\mu,\nu)
. Note that if
\eta
is

-optimal plan then

.

Remark:
(On definitions) What we call an ‘‘optimal coupling’’, other authors call an ‘‘optimal plan’’, and what we call an ‘‘optimal plan’’, they call an
‘‘optimal dynamical plan
’’.

Proposition 2.13

(Existence of optimal plans and displacement geodesics,
[
4
, Thm. 2.43]
)
.

Let
(X,\ell,\tau)
be a compact synthetic spacetime. Then every
q
-optimal coupling is induced by a
q
-optimal plan, and every geodesic on

is a displacement geodesic.

Definition 2.14

(Timelike (
q
-essential) non-branching)

A set

is

if for each

, the restriction map

is injective on the set of timelike curves in

. We say that

is
timelike

-essentially non-branching
if every timelike optimal plan

is concentrated on a timelike non-branching set, for all

.

The non-branching assumption prevents optimal plans from ‘‘meeting’’ in a sense outlined by the following lemma. This results mirrors now classic results in the metric measure setting;
[
3
, Lem. 2.6]
, and
[
22
, Lem. 2.11]
. In the Lorentzian setting we point out

.

Definition 2.15

(Midpoint)
.

Fix
q\in(0,1)
. Let
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in(\mathcal{P}(X),\ell_{q})
, and let

. A measure
\nu\in(\mathcal{P}(X),\ell_{q})
is called a

-midpoint between

and

if

\ell_{q}(\mu_{0},\nu)=t\ell_{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1});\quad\ell_{q}(\nu,\mu_{1})=(1-t)\ell_{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

Lemma 2.14

(Midpoints between mutually singular measures remain mutually singular)
.

Suppose
(X,\ell,\tau,\mathfrak{m})
is a timelike
q
-essentially non-branching globally hyperbolic synthetic spacetime with
\ell_{+}
continuous. Fix
t\in[0,1)
. Let
\{(\mu_{0k},\mu_{1k})\}_{k=1}^{n}
be a finite collection of
q
-timelike pairs of measures in
\mathcal{P}_{c}(X)^{2}
, and let
\mu_{tk}
be a
t
-midpoint joining
\mu_{0k}
to
\mu_{1k}
. For
i=0,t,1
, let
\mu_{i}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\mu_{ik}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. Suppose
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is a
q
-timelike pair, and
\mu_{t}
is a
t
-midpoint between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
. If
\mu_{0k}\perp\mu_{0k^{\prime}}
for all
k\neq k^{\prime}
, then
\mu_{tk}\perp\mu_{tk^{\prime}}
for all
k\neq k^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Fix
t\in(0,1)
.
Let
Y:=J(\spt\mu_{0},\spt\mu_{1})
. From
Lemma

2.15
,
\mathcal{P}(Y)
is a compact synthetic spacetime, and WLOG we may restrict ourselves to
\mathcal{P}(Y)
as every relevant measure lives in this space. We have that
\mu_{t}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\mu_{t,k}
is a
t
-midpoint between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
. As
\mathcal{P}(Y)
is causally geodesic, for each
k=1,\dots,n
, there are geodesics
s\mapsto\mu^{-}_{s,k}
and
s\mapsto\mu^{+}_{s,k}
which join
\mu_{0,k}
to
\mu_{t,k}
, and
\mu_{t,k}
to
\mu_{1,k}
, respectively. Define
s\mapsto\mu_{s,k}
via

\mu_{s,k}:=\begin{cases}\mu_{s/t,k}^{-}&0\leq s\leq t;\\
\mu_{(s-t)/(1-t),k}^{+}&t\leq s\leq 1.\end{cases}

It’s a straightforward computation to show that
\mu_{t,k}
is a geodesic path joining
\mu_{0,k}
to
\mu_{1,k}
, which from
Lemma

2.3
and
Lemma

2.15
implies that
\mu_{t,k}
is a geodesic. Then
2.13
implies that
\mu_{t,k}
is induced by a timelike
q
-optimal plan
\eta_{k}
.

Since
\mu_{t}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\mu_{t,k}
is also a
t
-midpoint, by the same proof we have a geodesic
s\mapsto\mu_{s}
joining
\mu_{0}
to
\mu_{1}
which meets
\mu_{t}
at
s=t
, such that
\mu_{s}
is induced by the timelike
q
-optimal plan
\eta:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\eta_{k}
.

Now suppose there is
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
such that
\mu_{t,k_{1}}\not\perp\mu_{t,k_{2}}
. For
i=0,t,1
, we define the measures

\nu_{i}:=\frac{\mu_{i,k_{1}}+\mu_{i,k_{2}}}{2},

with associated timelike
q
-optimal plan

\zeta:=\frac{\eta_{k_{1}}+\eta_{k_{2}}}{2}.

Recall the restriction map
r_{s}^{t}:\Geo(Y)\to\Geo(Y)
(see
2.14
), and define

\displaystyle\zeta^{-}

\displaystyle:=(r_{0}^{t})_{\#}\zeta,

\displaystyle\zeta^{+}

\displaystyle:=(r_{t}^{1})_{\#}\zeta.

By disintegration with respect to the evaluation maps
e_{0},e_{1}:\Geo(Y)\to Y
, we obtain the families
\{\zeta^{\pm}_{x}\}_{x\in Y}
of measures defined via

d\zeta^{\pm}(\gamma)=d\zeta^{\pm}_{x}(\gamma)d\nu_{t}(x).

We define the gluing map
\operatorname{glu}:Glu\to C([0,1],Y)
on the set
Glu:=\{(\gamma,\sigma)\in\Geo(Y)^{2}:\gamma_{1}=\sigma_{0}\}
. The gluing map is continuous, so for
\nu_{t}
-a.e
x
, the measure

\zeta^{M}_{x}:=\operatorname{glu}_{\#}(\zeta^{-}_{x}\otimes\zeta^{+}_{x}),

is well defined. Therefore we may define the mixed plan
\zeta^{M}\in\mathcal{P}(C([0,1],X))
via

d\zeta^{M}(\gamma)=\zeta^{M}_{x}(\gamma)d\nu_{t}(x).

We claim that
\zeta^{M}
is a
q
-optimal plan between
\nu_{0}
and
\nu_{1}
, and yet cannot be concentrated on a timelike non-branching set. The argument is based on Steps 2 and 3 of the proof of
[
7
, Lem. 2.23; pp 68-70]
, which itself is more or less identical to
[
20
, §4]
. Within those proofs is the assumption that the t-midpoints
\nu_{t}
are
\mathfrak{m}
-absolutely continuous, which we remove. Apart from this, we also struggle to understand the finer details of the proofs in the above references, and so we attempt to provide our own below.

Optimality of
\zeta^{M}
:

We must show that
\zeta^{M}\in\mathcal{P}(\Geo(X))
, and

\ell_{q}^{q}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})=\int\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})d\zeta^{M}(\gamma).

As
\zeta
is a timelike
q
-optimal plan, there is an
\ell^{q}
-cyclically monotone set
\Gamma\subset Y_{\ll}^{2}
such that
\zeta
is concentrated on
\hat{\Gamma}:=(e_{0}\times e_{1})^{-1}(\Gamma)\subset\Geo(Y)
. Let
\gamma,\sigma\in\hat{\Gamma}
be such that
\gamma_{\lambda}=\sigma_{\lambda}
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})+\ell^{q}(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})&\geq\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\sigma_{1})+\ell^{q}(\sigma_{0},\gamma_{1})\geq\left(\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{\lambda})+\ell(\sigma_{\lambda},\sigma_{1})\right)^{q}+\left(\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{\lambda})+\ell(\gamma_{\lambda},\gamma_{1})\right)^{q}\\
&=\left(\lambda\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})+(1-\lambda)\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})\right)^{q}+\left(\lambda\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})+(1-\lambda)\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\right)^{q}\\
&\geq\lambda\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})+(1-\lambda)\ell^{q}(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})+\lambda\ell^{q}(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})+(1-\lambda)\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\\
&=\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})+\ell^{q}(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}),\end{split}

(3)

which gives equality in all above inequalities. This implies that
\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})=\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})=\ell(\gamma_{0},\sigma_{1})=\ell(\sigma_{0},\gamma_{1})
.

Observe that for
\nu_{t}
-a.e.
x\in Y
,
\zeta^{-}_{x}
(resp.
\zeta^{+}_{x}
) is concentrated on
r_{0}^{t}(\hat{\Gamma})\cap e_{1}^{-1}(x)
(resp.
r_{t}^{1}(\hat{\Gamma})\cap e_{0}^{-1}(x)
). Therefore for
\nu_{t}
-a.e.
x
,
\zeta^{M}_{x}
is concentrated on the set
S(x):=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],Y):r_{0}^{t}\gamma\in r_{0}^{t}(\hat{\Gamma}),r_{1}^{t}\gamma\in r_{1}^{t}(\hat{\Gamma}),\gamma_{t}=x\}
. The claim is that each
\gamma\in S(x)
is a geodesic whose age
\age(\gamma)=\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
is a constant
c(x)>0
depending, a priori, on
x
only.

Indeed, let
\gamma\in S(x)
. Then there is
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\hat{\Gamma}
such that
r_{0}^{t}\gamma=r_{0}^{t}\sigma
and
r_{1}^{t}\gamma=r_{1}^{t}\sigma^{\prime}
. Since
\gamma_{t}=x
, we have that
\sigma_{t}=\sigma_{t}^{\prime}=x
. Then from (
3
), we see that
\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})=\ell(\sigma^{\prime}_{0},\sigma^{\prime}_{1})=\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma^{\prime}_{1})=\ell(\sigma^{\prime}_{0},\sigma_{1})
.

Now let
s,s^{\prime}\in[0,1]
,
s<s^{\prime}
. If
s^{\prime}\leq t
, then

\ell(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{s^{\prime}})=\ell(\sigma_{s},\sigma_{s^{\prime}})=(s^{\prime}-s)\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})=(s^{\prime}-s)\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}^{\prime})=(s^{\prime}-s)\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}).

Similarly if
t\leq s
, we obtain
\ell(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{s^{\prime}})=(s^{\prime}-s)\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
. Finally in the case
s\leq t\leq s^{\prime}
, we obtain

\ell(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{s^{\prime}})=\ell(\sigma_{s},\sigma^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})\geq\ell(\sigma_{s},\sigma_{t})+\ell(\sigma^{\prime}_{t},\sigma^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})=(t-s)\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})+(s^{\prime}-t)\ell(\sigma^{\prime}_{0},\sigma^{\prime}_{1})=(s^{\prime}-s)\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}).

This proves that
\gamma
is a geodesic. Now let
\bar{\gamma}\in S
. Then there is
\bar{\sigma},\bar{\sigma}^{\prime}\in\hat{\Gamma}
such that
r_{0}^{t}\bar{\gamma}=\bar{\sigma}
and
r_{t}^{1}\bar{\gamma}=\bar{\sigma}^{\prime}
, and moreover comparing the ages of the four geodesics
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\bar{\sigma},\bar{\sigma}^{\prime}
using (
3
), we see that

\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})=\ell(\sigma_{0},\sigma^{\prime}_{1})=\ell(\sigma_{0},\bar{\sigma}^{\prime}_{1})=\ell(\bar{\sigma}_{0},\bar{\sigma}^{\prime}_{1})=\ell(\bar{\gamma}_{0},\bar{\gamma}_{1}),

hence there is some constant
c(x)>0
depending only on
x
such that
\ell(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})=c(x)
for all
\gamma\in S
. In particular we have that
\zeta^{M}_{x}\in\mathcal{P}(\Geo(Y))
for all
\nu_{t}
-a.e
x\in Y
, and so
\zeta^{M}\in\mathcal{P}(\Geo(X))
.

Disintegrating
d\zeta(\gamma)=\zeta_{x}(\gamma)d\nu_{t}
, one can see, by the same proof as above, that
\zeta_{x}
is also concentrated on the set of geodesic of constant age
c(x)
, for
\nu_{t}
-a.e
x\in Y
.

Now observe

\displaystyle\int\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})d\zeta^{M}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int\int\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})d\zeta^{M}_{x}(\gamma)d\nu_{t}(x)=\int c^{q}(x)d\nu_{t}(x)=\int c^{q}(x)d\zeta_{x}(\gamma)d\nu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\int\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})d\zeta_{x}(\gamma)d\nu_{t}(x)=\int\ell^{q}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})d\zeta(\gamma)=\ell_{q}^{q}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}).

Thus
\zeta^{M}
is
q
-optimal.

Branchingness of
\zeta^{M}
:

Let
C\subset\Geo(Y)
be a set on which
\zeta^{M}
is concentrated. We will show that
C
is timelike branching.

For each
i=1,2
, we define
\eta^{-}_{k_{1}}=(r_{0}^{t})_{\#}\eta_{k_{i}}
. We then disintegrate
d\eta^{-}_{k_{i}}=(\eta^{-}_{k_{i}})_{x}d\mu_{t,k_{i}}
. Since
\mu_{t,k_{1}}\not\perp\mu_{t,k_{2}}
, we can find a Borel set
E\subset Y
of positive
\nu_{t}
-measure such that
(\eta^{-}_{i})_{x}
is concentrated on
e_{1}^{-1}(x)
for all
x\in E
. We may write

\zeta^{-}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}(\eta^{-}_{k_{1}})_{x}+\frac{1}{2}(\eta^{-}_{k_{2}})_{x}.

As
\mu_{0,k_{1}}\perp\mu_{0,k_{2}}
, we have that
\eta^{-}_{k_{1}}\perp\eta^{-}_{k_{2}}
, and therefore
(\eta^{-}_{k_{1}})_{x}\perp(\eta^{-}_{k_{2}})_{x}
. We then have

\zeta^{-}_{x}\otimes\zeta^{+}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}(\eta^{-}_{k_{1}})_{x}\otimes\zeta^{+}_{x}+\frac{1}{2}(\eta^{-}_{k_{2}})_{x}\otimes\zeta^{+}_{x}

is concentrated on

,

is concentrated on
C
, and
\zeta^{-}_{x}\otimes\zeta^{+}_{x}
is concentrated on

. Moreover

implies that

. Therefore there is a disjoint partition

such that
(\eta^{-}_{k_{1}})_{x}\otimes\zeta^{+}_{x}
concentrates on

, and
(\eta^{-}_{k_{2}})_{x}\otimes\zeta^{+}_{x}
concentrates on

. Let

. Note that for both

It follows that

is concentrated on both

and

are disjoint, so therefore there are three geodesics

are two distinct geodesics which restrict to the same geodesic

∎

2.4
Simple Measures and Simple Sequences

Observe that for any uniform measure
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
,
-S_{N}(\mathfrak{m}_{A})=U_{N}(\mathfrak{m}_{A})=\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A)
. One can therefore think of the
\sTBM_{q}(K,N)
condition as a curvature condition at the level of uniform measures. It’s perhaps straightforward to see then that
\TCD_{q}(K,N)
implies
\sTBM_{q}(K,N)
, since uniform measures are in
\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
. To prove the converse amounts to showing that
any
probability measure in
\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
can be approximated in some way by uniform measures. At the level of the densities of the measures in
\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
, this amounts to approximating
L^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
functions by simple, that is, piecewise constant, functions.

Definition 2.20

(Simple measures)
.

We call a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)

simple
if it is a finite convex combination of mutually singular uniform measures. That is, there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
, mutually disjoint Borel sets
\{A_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
of positive finite
\mathfrak{m}
-measure, and positive numbers
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}=1
, and

\displaystyle\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\mathfrak{m}_{A_{j}}.

We want to be able to approximate measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
by a sequence of simple measures
\{\mu^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Later on we will want to choose sequences such that a
q
-timelike pair
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
can be approximated by a sequence of pairs of simple measures
(\mu_{0}^{n},\mu_{1}^{n})
which are ‘‘correlated’’ in some precise way. To do this we require existence and uniqueness of optimal maps, so we delay this lemma for later. For now we prove that any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
can be approximated by a sequence of simple measures with some additional desirable properties. Such a sequence we call a
simple sequence
.

Proposition 2.18

(Existence of simple sequences)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
. Then there exists a sequence
\{\mu^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of simple measures, which we call a ‘‘simple sequence", which satisfies the following properties:

1.

(Narrow Convergence). As
n\to\infty
,
\mu^{n}\rightharpoonup\mu
.

2.

(Controlled Decomposition). For each metrizer
d
of
X
, and each positive sequence
\delta_{n}\to 0
, we may write
\mu^{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\mathfrak{m}_{A_{j}^{n}}
, where for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and each

,

is compact and contained in
\spt\mu_{0}
, and
\diam_{d}(A_{j}^{n})<\delta_{n}
.

3.

(Continuity along Entropy) For each

U_{N}(\mu^{n})\to U_{N}(\mu)
, and
S_{N}(\mu^{n})\to S_{N}(\mu)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
, and let

be such that

. There exists an increasing sequence
\bar{\rho}^{n}
of simple functions which converge pointwise to
\rho
. Observe then that

It’s clear that

where

is a collection of disjoint Borel sets of positive and finite
\mathfrak{m}
-measure. Since
\bar{\rho}^{n}\leq\rho
, we have that
\tilde{A}_{j}^{n}\subset\spt\mu_{0}
for each

and each

. Since

is compact, for any metrizer

\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\rho^{n}\log\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\rho}^{n}}{\|\tilde{\rho}_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}}\log\left(\frac{\tilde{\rho}^{n}}{\|\tilde{\rho}_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}}\right)

:

\displaystyle\ell_{q}^{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{t})

\displaystyle\geq\int\ell^{q}(x,y)d\mu_{0}(x)d\mu_{t}(y)=\int\ell_{+}^{q}(x,y)d\mu_{0}(x)d\mu_{t}(y)\geq\int\left(\ell_{+}^{q}(x^{\prime},y)-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)d\mu_{0}(x)d\mu_{t}(y)

\displaystyle=\int\ell_{+}^{q}(x^{\prime},y)d\mu_{t}(y)+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}=t^{q}\int\ell_{+}^{q}(x^{\prime},z(y))d\mu_{t}(y)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\geq t^{q}\int\ell_{+}^{q}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})d\mu_{t}(y)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle=t^{q}\int\left(\ell_{+}^{q}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)d\mu_{t}(y)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=t^{q}\ell_{+}^{q}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})-\varepsilon.

As
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
are totally timelike, there is a timelike optimal coupling
\pi\in\TOpt_{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
. Integrating the above inequality with respect to
\pi
yields:

\ell_{q}^{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{t})=\int\ell_{q}^{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})d\pi(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})\geq t^{q}\int\ell_{+}^{q}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})d\pi(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})-\varepsilon=t^{q}\int\ell^{q}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})d\pi(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})-\varepsilon=t^{q}\ell_{q}^{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})-\varepsilon.

An exactly symmetric argument gives the existence of a
\delta_{1}>0
such that if
\diam_{d}(\spt\mu_{1})<\min\{\delta^{\prime},\delta_{1}\}
and
\diam_{d}(\spt\mu_{0})<\delta^{\prime}
, then

\ell_{q}^{q}(\mu_{t},\mu_{1})\geq(1-t)^{q}\ell_{q}^{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})-\varepsilon.

Then taking
\delta=\min\{\delta_{0},\delta^{\prime},\delta_{1}\}
gives the result.
∎

3
Main Results

3.1
Curvature-Dimension Spacetimes are Strong Brunn-Minkowski Spacetimes

In this section we prove that timelike curvature dimension spaces admit a strong Brunn-Minkowski inequality.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
N>1
,
K\in\mathbb{R}
, and
q\in(0,1)
. If
X\in\TCD_{q}(K,N)
, then
X\in\sTBM_{q}(K,N^{\prime})
for all
N^{\prime}>N
, and if
X\in\TCD_{q}^{e}(K,N)
, then
X\in\sTBM_{q}^{*}(K,N)
.

Proof.

Suppose
X\in\TCD_{q}(K,N)
. Let
(A,B)
be a pair of compact
q
-timelike sets. Then there exists a geodesic
\mu_{t}
, and a timelike
q
-optimal plan
\pi\in\TOpt_{q}(\mathfrak{m}_{A},\mathfrak{m}_{B})
, such that for all
N^{\prime}>N
,

S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t})\leq-\int\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\ell(x,y))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A)+\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(t)}(\ell(x,y))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(B)d\pi(x,y)

Let
\Lambda=\left(\int\ell^{2}d\pi\right)^{1/2}
. By the convexity of
\theta\mapsto\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(t)}(\sqrt{\theta})
, we have, by Jensen’s inequality, that

\int\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\ell(x,y))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A)d\pi(x,y)\geq\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A)\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}\left(\int\ell^{2}(x,y)d\pi(x,y)\right)^{1/2}=\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Lambda)\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A).

Similarly, we have

\int\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(t)}(\ell(x,y))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(B)d\pi(x,y)\geq\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(t)}(\Lambda)\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(B).

Jensen’s inequality also gives that
-\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(\spt\mu_{t})\leq S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t})
. Combining these inequalities gives

\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(\spt\mu_{t})\geq\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Lambda)\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A)+\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(t)}(\Lambda)\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(B),

Monotonicity of
\theta\mapsto\tau_{K,N}^{(t)}(\theta)
then gives

\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(\spt\mu_{t})\geq\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta)\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A)+\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(t)}(\Theta)\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(B),

as desired.

If
X\in\TCD^{e}(K,N)
, then,

U_{N}(\mu_{t})\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Lambda)\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}U_{N}(\mathfrak{m}_{A})+\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}(\Lambda)\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}U_{N}(\mathfrak{m}_{B})

Using the fact that
U_{N^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{m}_{A})=\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A)
,
U_{N^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{m}_{B})=\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(B)
, and
U_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t})\leq\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(\spt\mu_{t})
, and monotonicity of
\theta\mapsto\sigma_{k}^{(t)}(\theta)
, we get the desired result.
∎

3.2
Brunn-Minkowski Spacetimes are Measure Contracting Spacetimes

In this section we prove that
\TBM^{*}
spaces are
\TMCP^{e}
. This step is needed in order to obtain unique optimal maps, which are necessary to prove all of the following results. Most of the arguments presented here will be recapitulated in the following section.

The following is a well-known lemma which allows us to reduce the
\TMCP
condition to entropic convexity along
t
-midpoints, rather than displacement geodesics. The proof can be found within
[
4
, Thm. 5.7]
. We rewrite it explicitly for the sake of completeness. Later, we will need a similar version of this result for the
\TCD_{q}^{e}(K,N)
condition (
Lemma

3.7
), but the careful reader can see that the proof would be essentially the same, so we omit it. A similar proof of this result for
\TCD_{q}(K,N)
spaces can be found within
[
7
, Thm. 3.33]
.

Lemma 3.2

(
\TMCP
on
t
-midpoints)
.

Let
X
be a measured globally hyperbolic synthetic spacetime. Suppose there exists
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N>1
such that, for every
\mu=\rho\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
, every
x_{0}\in X
such that
\spt\mu_{0}\subset I^{-}(x_{0})
, and every
t\in(0,1)
, there exists a
t
-midpoint
\mu_{t}
between
\mu
and
\delta_{x_{0}}
such that

\displaystyle U_{N}(\mu_{t})\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Lambda)U_{N}(\mu),

(18)

where
\Lambda:=\sqrt{\int\ell^{2}(x,x_{0})d\mu(x)}
. Then
X\in\TMCP^{e}(K,N)
.

Proof.

The aim is to show that we can construct a geodesic joining
\mu
to
\delta_{x_{0}}
which satisfies the entropic convexity.

Fix
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{ac}_{c}(X)
and
x_{0}
such that
\spt\mu\subset I^{-}(x_{0})
. Let
\mathcal{I}_{t}(\mu,\delta_{x_{0}})
denote the set of
t
-midpoints from
\mu
to
\delta_{x_{0}}
which satisfy (
18
). Let
Y=J(\spt\mu,x_{0})
, which is a compact synthetic spacetime by global hyperbolicity and
Lemma

2.15
. Notice that if
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
, then
\mathcal{I}_{t}(\nu,\delta_{x_{0}})\subset\mathcal{P}(Y)
.

For each
t\in(0,1)
, iteratively define the collection of measures
\{\mu^{m}_{t}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
such that
\mu^{1}_{t}\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(\mu,\delta_{x_{0}})
, and
\mu^{m}_{t}\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(\mu_{t}^{m-1},\delta_{x_{0}})
. Notice then that
\mu^{m}_{t}\in\mathcal{I}_{1-(1-t)^{m-m^{\prime}}}(\mu^{m^{\prime}}_{t},\delta_{x_{0}})
, for any
m>m^{\prime}
. Letting
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence decreasing to
0
, we arrive at a countable collection of measures
\{\mu^{m}_{n}:=\mu^{m}_{t_{n}}\}_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Compactness of
Y
implies narrow compactness of
\mathcal{P}(Y)
and thus
\{\mu^{m}_{n}\}_{n,m}
is narrowly precompact. Let
D\subset(0,1)
denote a countable dense subset of
(0,1)
. For each
s\in D
, there is a sequence
\{m_{n,s}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
1-(1-t_{n})^{m_{n,s}}\to s
as
n\to\infty
. Narrow compactness of
\mathcal{P}(Y)
together with a standard diagonalization argument shows that the countable collection
\{\mu^{n}_{s}:=\mu^{m_{n,s}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a subsequential limit
\mu_{s}
for all
s\in D
. Notice that for each
s\in D
,
\mu_{s}
satisfies (
18
) (with
t=s
), by narrow lower semicontinuity of
\Ent
and continuity of the right hand side in
t
.

As
\mathcal{P}(Y)
is compact we may complete
D\ni s\mapsto\mu_{s}
to a causal path on
[0,1]
. It then remains to show that
s\mapsto\mu_{s}
is a geodesic. Indeed, for
s_{1}\leq s_{2}\in D
, we have, by upper-semicontinuity of
\ell_{q}
,

\displaystyle\ell_{q}(\mu_{s_{1}},\mu_{s_{2}})

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\ell_{q}(\mu^{n}_{s_{1}},\mu^{n}_{s_{2}})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}1-(1-\lambda_{n})^{m_{n,s_{2}}-m_{n,s_{1}}}\ell(\mu_{s}^{m},\delta_{x_{0}})

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Big(1-(1-\lambda_{n})^{m_{n,s_{2}}-m_{n,s_{1}}}\Big)(1-\lambda_{n})^{m_{n,s_{1}}}\ell_{q}(\mu,\delta_{x_{0}})

\displaystyle=(s_{2}-s_{1})\ell_{q}(\mu,\delta_{x_{0}}),

which then proves by upper-semicontinuity that
\ell_{q}(\mu_{s_{2}},\mu_{s_{1}})\geq(s_{2}-s_{1})\ell_{q}(\mu,\delta_{x_{0}})
for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,1]
with
s_{1}\leq s_{2}
. Thus
s\mapsto\mu_{s}
is an
\ell_{q}
-path. Since
Y
is regular,
\mathcal{P}(Y)
is regular by
Lemma

2.15
, and so
s\mapsto\mu_{s}
is a geodesic, by
Lemma

2.3
.
∎

Next we obtain a timelike Brunn-Minkowski inequality from sets to points. The proof is a simple approximation argument.

Lemma 3.3

(
\TBM(K,N)
for Dirac targets)
.

Let
(X,\ell,\tau,\mathfrak{m})
be a measured synthetic spacetime, with
\ell_{+}
continuous. Suppose
X\in\TBM^{*}(K,N)
. Let
x_{0}\in X
and let
A\subset X
be a compact set of positive measure such that
A\subset I^{-}(x_{0})
. Then

\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(G_{t}(A,x_{0}))\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta(A,x_{0}))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A).

Proof.

Let
d
be a metrizer of
X
. Then the collection
\{U_{n}:=B^{d}(x_{0},1/n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a countable local base for
x_{0}
. Since
\mathfrak{m}
is Radon with full support,
\mathfrak{m}(U_{n})>0
, and there exists a compact set
K_{n}\subset U_{n}
such that
\mathfrak{m}(K_{n})>0
. The set
B_{n}:=K_{n}\cup\{x_{0}\}
is then compact, contains
x_{0}
, and is contained in
U_{n}
. It follows that
\{B_{n}\}
is a compact local base for
x_{0}
of positive
\mathfrak{m}
-measure, and WLOG we may assume the sequence is nesting.

As
\ell_{+}
is continuous,
A
is compact, and
(A,x_{0})
is
q
-timelike, the map
x\mapsto\inf_{y\in A}\ell_{+}(y,x)
is well-defined, lower-semicontinuous and positive at
x=x_{0}
. Therefore there is a neighbourhood
U
of
x_{0}
where
\ell_{+}(y,x)>0
for all
(y,x)\in A\times U
, so the pair
(A,U)
is
q
-timelike. Therefore for all sufficiently large
n
, the pair
(A,B_{n})
is totally timelike.

Let
A_{t}^{n}:=G_{t}(A,B_{n})
, and
A_{t}:=G_{t}(A,x_{0})
. From the
\TBM^{*}(K,N)
property, we have

\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{t}^{n})\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta(A,B_{n}))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A)+\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}(\Theta(A,B_{n}))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(B_{n})

As the
B_{n}
are nested, the
A_{t}^{n}
are nested, and
A_{t}\subset\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{t}^{n}
. We claim that
A_{t}:=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{t}^{n}
. Indeed, let
z\in\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{t}^{n}
. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
x_{n}\in A
and
y_{n}\in B_{n}
such that
z
is a
t
-midpoint between
x_{n}
and
y_{n}
. By construction it is clear that
y_{n}\to x_{0}
. Compactness of
A
implies that a subsequence of
x_{n}
converges to some point
x\in A
. Passing to subsequences, we see that
\ell_{+}(x_{n},z)\to\ell_{+}(x,z)
, whereas
\ell_{+}(x_{n},y_{n})\to\ell(x,x_{0})
. It follows that
\ell_{+}(x,z)=t\ell_{+}(x,x_{0})
and similarly
\ell(z,x_{0})=(1-t)\ell_{+}(x,x_{0})
. Therefore
z
is a
t
-midpoint between
x\in A
and
x_{0}
, so
z\in A_{t}
. This proves the claim.

Moreover continuity of
\ell_{+}
over the compact sets
A\times B_{1}
implies that for each
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,

d(x_{1},x_{2})<\delta\implies|\ell_{+}(y,x_{1})-\ell+(y,x_{2})|<\varepsilon.

It follows that there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>n_{0}
and all
x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{n}
, we have

|\ell_{+}(y,x_{1})-\ell_{+}(y,x_{2})|<\varepsilon

and so we have for
K\geq 0

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Theta(A,B_{n})\geq\Theta(A,x_{0}),

and for
K<0
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Theta(A,B_{n})\leq\Theta(A,x_{0}).

In either case, continuity of
\theta\mapsto\sigma_{k}^{(t)}(\theta)
uniformly in
t
then gives

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta(A,B_{n}))\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta(A,x_{0})).

Finally the fact that
A_{t}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{t}^{n}
and continuity of
\mathfrak{m}
from above then gives,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{t})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{t}^{n})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Big(\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta(A,B_{n}))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A)+\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}(\Theta(A,B_{n}))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(B_{n})\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta(A,x_{0}))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A)+\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}(\Theta(A,x_{0}))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta(A,x_{0}))\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A),

as desired.
∎

We now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.4

(
\TBM^{*}(K,N)
spaces are
\TMCP^{e}(K,N)
spaces)
.

Fix
0<q<1
. Let
(X,\ell,\tau,\mathfrak{m})
be a timelike
q
-essentially non-branching measured globally hyperbolic synthetic spacetime, with
\ell_{+}
continuous. If
X\in\TBM^{*}(K,N)
, then
X\in\TMCP^{e}(K,N)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
, and
x_{0}\in X
be such that
(\spt\mu,x_{0})
is timelike. In light of
Lemma

3.2
, we aim to show that for each
t\in(0,1)
, there is a
t
-midpoint
\mu_{t}
joining
\mu
to
\delta_{x_{0}}
such that

\displaystyle U_{N}(\mu_{t})\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Lambda)U_{N}(\mu),

(19)

where
\Lambda:=\sqrt{\int\ell^{2}(x,x_{0})d\mu(x)}
. Fix
t\in(0,1)
.

Step 1: Obtaining the
t
-midpoint
\mu_{t}
.

From global hyperbolicity, the set
Y:=J(\spt\mu,x_{0})
is a compact synthetic spacetime. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we apply
Lemma

2.19
with the choice of metrizer
d
of
Y
and
\varepsilon=1/n
to obtain some
\delta_{n}>0
such that for any pair
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}(Y)^{2}
of compactly supported totally timelike measures, and any measure
\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
with
\spt\mu_{t}\subset G_{t}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, if
\diam_{d}(\spt\mu_{i})<\delta_{n}
for
i=0,1
, then

\ell_{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{t})\geq t\ell_{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})-\frac{1}{n};\quad\ell_{q}(\mu_{t},\mu_{1})\geq(1-t)\ell_{q}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})-\frac{1}{n}.

WLOG we may assume
\delta_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. By
2.18
there exists a simple sequence
\{\mu^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges to
\mu
. From the controlled decomposition property, we may write
\mu^{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\mathfrak{m}_{A_{j}^{n}}\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
, where for each
n
, the collection
\{A_{j}^{n}\}_{j=1}^{M_{n}}
are compact, mutually disjoint, have finite positive
\mathfrak{m}
-measure,
A_{j}^{n}\subset\spt\mu\subset Y
, and
\diam_{d}(A_{j}^{n})<\delta_{n}
.

Let
A_{t,j}^{n}:=G_{t}(A_{j}^{n},x_{0})\subset Y
. From
Lemma

3.3
, we know that
A_{t,j}^{n}
has positive
\mathfrak{m}
-measure. Since
A_{t,j}^{n}
is compact and
\mathfrak{m}
is Radon, we also know that it has finite
\mathfrak{m}
-measure. We define

\mu_{t}^{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\mathfrak{m}_{A_{t,j}^{n}}.

Observe that
\mu_{t}^{n}\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
, and so
\mu_{t}^{n}
admits a narrow subsequential limit
\mu_{t}
. Concavity of
\ell_{q}^{q}
and
Lemma

2.19
applied to the triple
(\mu_{0},\mu_{t},\mu_{1}):=(\mathfrak{m}_{A_{j}^{n}},\mathfrak{m}_{A_{t,j}^{n}},\delta_{x_{0}})
then gives that

\displaystyle\ell_{q}^{q}(\mu^{n},\mu_{t}^{n})\geq\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\ell_{q}^{q}(\mathfrak{m}_{A_{j}^{n}},\mathfrak{m}_{A_{t,j}^{n}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}t^{q}\ell_{q}^{q}(\mathfrak{m}_{A_{j}^{n}},\delta_{x_{0}})-\frac{1}{n}=t^{q}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\int\ell^{q}(x,x_{0})d\mathfrak{m}_{A_{j}^{n}}(x)-\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle=t^{q}\int\ell^{q}(x,x_{0})d\mu^{n}(x)-\frac{1}{n}

Narrow convergence of
\mu^{n}\rightharpoonup\mu
, continuity of
\ell_{+}^{q}
, and positivity of
\ell^{q}
on
\spt\mu\times\{x_{0}\}
, and upper semicontinuity of
\ell_{q}
then gives that, after passing to subsequence, that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\ell_{q}^{q}(\mu,\mu_{t})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\ell_{q}^{q}(\mu^{n},\mu_{t}^{n})=t^{q}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int\ell^{q}(x,x_{0})d\mu^{n}(x)-\frac{1}{n}=t^{q}\int\ell^{q}(x,x_{0})d\mu(x).

Hence we may conclude that

\ell_{q}(\mu,\mu_{t})\geq t\ell_{q}(\mu,\delta_{x_{0}}).

A symmetric argument gives also that

\ell_{q}(\mu_{t},\delta_{x_{0}})\geq(1-t)\ell_{q}(\mu,\delta_{x_{0}}),

which proves that
\mu_{t}
is indeed a
t
-midpoint between
\mu
and
\delta_{x_{0}}
.

Step 2: Entropic Convexity along
\mu_{t}
.

We now verify the entropic convexity inequality (
19
) along
\mu_{t}
. The
q
-essential non-branching assumption guarantees that for each
n
, the sets
A_{t,j}^{n}
are mutually disjoint for all
j=1,\dots M_{n}
, since the
A_{j}^{n}
are mutually disjoint. As a result, we have

\displaystyle\Ent(\mu_{t}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\Ent(\lambda_{j}^{n}\mathfrak{m}_{A_{t,j}^{n}})=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\log\frac{\lambda_{j}^{n}}{\mathfrak{m}(A_{t,j}^{n})}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle U_{N}(\mu_{t}^{n})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{N}\Ent(\mu_{t}^{n})\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(A_{t,j}^{n})}{\lambda_{j}^{n}}\right)^{\lambda_{j}^{n}/N}\geq\prod_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\left(\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{0,j}^{n})}{(\lambda_{j}^{n})^{1/N}}\right)^{\lambda_{j}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\left(\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\right)^{\lambda_{j}^{n}}U_{N}(\mu^{n}),

where
\Theta_{j}^{n}=\Theta(A_{j}^{n},x_{0})
. Now observe that log-convexity of
\theta\mapsto\sigma_{k}^{(t)}(\sqrt{\theta})
implies that

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})^{\lambda_{j}^{n}}\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}\left(\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}(\Theta_{j}^{n})^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\right).

(20)

Continuity of
\ell
, compactness of
A_{j}^{n}
, and the fact that
\diam_{d}(A_{j}^{n})<\delta_{n}
gives that for each there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>n_{0}
, and all
x_{j}^{n}\in A_{j}^{n}
,

|\Theta_{j}^{n}-\ell(x_{j}^{n},x_{0})|<1/n.

It follows that, with
\Lambda^{n}:=\sqrt{\int\ell^{2}(x,x_{0})d\mu^{n}}
, we have

\left|\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}(\Theta_{j}^{n})^{2}\Big)^{1/2}-\Lambda^{n}\right|<\frac{1}{n},

And so by monotonicity of
\theta\mapsto\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}(\theta)
we obtain

U_{N}(\mu_{t}^{n})\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}\left(\Lambda^{n}\mp\frac{1}{n}\right)U_{N}(\mu^{n}),

where we choose
-\frac{1}{n}
if
K\geq 0
and
+\frac{1}{n}
if
K<0
. Narrow convergence of
\mu^{n}
to
\mu
guarantees that
\Lambda^{n}\to\Lambda
, and the continuity along entropy guarantees that
U_{N}(\mu^{n})\to U_{n}(\mu)
. Thus from upper semicontinuity we obtain

U_{N}(\mu_{t})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{N}(\mu_{t}^{n})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}\left(\Lambda^{n}\mp\frac{1}{n}\right)U_{N}(\mu^{n})=\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Lambda)U_{N}(\mu).

The last equality holds since
\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Lambda)>0
.

∎

Lemma 3.6

(Existence of good simple sequences)
.

Let

\mu_{i}

d

\delta^{n}\to 0

Recall that by assumption, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

q
-optimal map

\tilde{\mu}^{n}_{0}
to
\tilde{\mu}_{1}^{n}

q

\tilde{\mu}_{1}^{n}=(T_{n})_{\#}\tilde{\mu}_{0}^{n}

1.

Entrywise simplicity;

2.

Correlated controlled decomposition;

3.

\tilde{\mu}_{i}^{n}

\mu_{i}

is a
q
-optimal map joining
\mu_{0}^{n}
to
\mu_{1}^{n}
.
Moreover observe now that for every Borel set

-optimal between

\ell_{q}^{q}(\nu_{0},\nu_{t})\geq t^{q}\ell_{q}^{q}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})-\frac{1}{n};\quad\ell_{q}^{q}(\nu_{t},\nu_{1})\geq(1-t)^{q}\ell_{q}^{q}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})-\frac{1}{n}.

WLOG we may assume that
\delta_{n}\to 0
.
From
Lemma

3.6
, there exists a good simple sequence
(\mu_{0}^{n},\mu_{1}^{n})
which narrowly converges to
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}(Y)^{2}
. From the correlated controlled decomposition, we have
\mu_{i}^{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\mathfrak{m}_{A_{i,j}^{n}}\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
, and
\diam_{d}(A_{i,j}^{n})<\delta_{n}
. Applying
\sTBM_{q}(K,N)
to the totally timelike pair
(A_{0,j}^{n},A_{1,j}^{n})
, we see that there exists a geodesic
\mu_{t,j}^{n}
, such that, with
A_{t,j}^{n}:=\spt\mu_{t,j}
, we have

\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{t,j}^{n})\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{0,j}^{n})+\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A_{1,j}^{n})\quad\forall t\in[0,1],

where
\Theta_{j}^{n}:=\Theta(A_{0,j}^{n},A_{1,j}^{n})
. Define
\mu_{t}^{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\mathfrak{m}_{A_{t,j}^{n}}
. By the same argument as in
Theorem

3.4
using
Lemma

2.19
, we see that
\mu_{t}^{n}
converges subsequentially to a
t
-midpoint of
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
. As the sets
A_{0,j}^{n}
are mutually disjoint, from the stable couplings property together with
Lemma

2.14
, the sets
A_{t,j}^{n}
are also mutually disjoint. Therefore

\displaystyle\Ent(\mu_{t}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\Ent(\lambda_{j}^{n}\mathfrak{m}_{A_{t,j}^{n}})=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\log\frac{\lambda_{j}^{n}}{\mathfrak{m}(A_{t,j}^{n})}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle U_{N}(\mu_{t}^{n})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{\Ent(\mu_{t}^{n})}{N}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}(A_{t,j}^{n})}{\lambda_{j}^{n}}\right)^{\lambda_{j}^{n}/N}

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\left(\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{0,j}^{n})}{(\lambda_{j}^{n})^{1/N}}+\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{1,j}^{n})}{(\lambda_{j}^{n})^{1/N}}\right)^{\lambda_{j}^{n}}

From this point, one can apply the same argument that was used in
Lemma

3.7
to arrive at

\displaystyle U_{N}(\mu_{t}^{n})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}\left(\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}(\Theta_{j}^{n})^{2}}\right)U_{N}(\mu_{0}^{n})+\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}\left(\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}(\Theta_{j}^{n})^{2}}\right)U_{N}(\mu_{1}^{n})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}\left(\Theta^{n}\right)U_{N}(\mu_{0}^{n})+\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}\left(\Theta^{n}\right)U_{N}(\mu_{1}^{n}).

Taking an appropriate subsequential limit, one arrives at

\displaystyle U_{N}(\mu_{t})\geq\sigma_{K/N}^{(t)}(\Theta)U_{N}(\mu_{0})+\sigma_{K/N}^{(1-t)}(\Theta)U_{N}(\mu_{1}),

which implies that
X\in\TCD_{q}^{e}(K,N)
, by

3.4

\sTBM_{q}(K,N^{+})

\TCD_{q}(K,N)

We now reproduce the results of the previous section to prove that
X\in\TCD_{q}(K,N)
iff
X\in\sTBM_{q}(K,N^{\prime})
for all
N^{\prime}>N

\TCD_{q}
condition, in analogy with

Lemma 3.9

(
\TCD_{q}(K,N)
for
t
-midpoints between totally timelike pairs)
.

Fix
0<q<1
and let
(X,\ell,\tau,\mathfrak{m})
be a measured timelike
q

\ell_{+}

(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)^{2}

t

\mu_{t}

Proof.

By recreating the proof of
Lemma

3.2
, we see that the assumption implies that each totally timelike pair
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)
admits a geodesic
\mu_{t}
such that

S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t})\leq\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta)S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{0})+\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta)S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{1})\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

It’s sufficient to prove that this implies that
X\in\TCD_{q}(K,N)
. So let
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}(X)^{2}
be
q
-timelike. By the same argument as in
Lemma

3.7
, we obtain sequences
\mu_{i}^{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{k}^{n}\mu_{ik}^{n}
for each
i=0,1
, such that
\mu_{i}^{n}\rightharpoonup\mu_{i}
, with corresponding optimal couplings
\pi^{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{k}^{n}\pi_{k}^{n}
such that
\pi_{k}^{n}\in\TOpt_{q}(\mu_{0k}^{n},\mu_{1k}^{n})
, and
\pi^{n}\in\TOpt_{q}(\mu_{0}^{n},\mu_{1}^{n})
. Moreover the pairs
(\mu_{0k}^{n},\mu_{1k}^{n})
are totally timelike,
\mu_{ik}^{n}\perp\mu_{ik^{\prime}}^{n}
for all
k\neq k^{\prime}
, and we have
S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{i}^{n})\to S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{i})
for all
N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle|\Theta_{k}^{n}-\ell(x,y)|<\frac{1}{n}\quad\forall(x,y)\in\spt\mu_{0k}^{n}\times\spt\mu_{1k}^{n},

(24)

where

\Theta_{k}^{n}:=\begin{cases}\inf_{\spt\mu_{0k}^{n}\times\spt\mu_{1k}^{n}}\ell(x,y)&K\geq 0;\\
\sup_{\spt\mu_{0k}^{n}\times\spt\mu_{1k}^{n}}\ell(x,y)&K<0.\end{cases}

Since

\mu_{tk}^{n}

S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{tk}^{n})\leq\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{k}^{n})S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{0k}^{n})+\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{k}^{n})S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{1k}^{n}).

We define
\mu_{t}^{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{k}^{n}\mu_{tk}^{n}
, and observe that
\mu_{t}^{n}
converges subsequentially to a geodesic
\mu_{t}
, by global hyperbolicity. Mutual singularity at the endpoints then gives mutual singularity along the entire geodesic
\mu_{t,k}^{n}

we obtain

\displaystyle S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M_{n}}(\lambda_{k}^{n})^{1-1/N^{\prime}}S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t,k}^{n})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M_{n}}(\lambda_{k}^{n})^{1-1/N^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{k}^{n})S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{0k}^{n})+\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{k}^{n})S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{1k}^{n})\right)

Theorem 3.10
.

Fix
0<q<1
. Let
X
be a timelike
q
-essentially non-branching globally hyperbolic synthetic spacetime with
\ell_{+}
continuous. If
X\in\sTBM_{q}(K,N^{\prime})
for all
N^{\prime}>N
, then
X\in\TCD_{q}(K,N)
.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}_{c}^{ac}(X)^{2}
be a totally timelike pair. By the same proof as in
Theorem

3.8
, and keeping the same notation, we obtain a good simple sequence
(\mu_{0}^{n},\mu_{1}^{n})
with

\mu_{i}^{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\mathfrak{m}(A_{i,j}^{n}),

where
\diam_{d}(A_{i,j}^{n})<\delta^{n}
for some metrizer
d
and
\delta^{n}\to 0
, which we are free to specify later. For each
N^{\prime}>N
, we may then obtain a
t
-midpoint
\mu_{t}
between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
as a (subsequential) limit of

\mu_{t}^{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\mathfrak{m}_{A_{t,j}^{n}}.

Each of the
A_{t,j}^{n}
are disjoint, and satisfy

\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A_{t,j}^{n})\geq\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A_{0,j}^{n})+\tau_{K,N}^{(t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A_{1,j}^{n}).

Recall that for each
N^{\prime}>N
,
A_{t,j}^{n}
is the support of a geodesic joining
\mathfrak{m}_{A_{0,j}^{n}}
to
\mathfrak{m}_{A_{1,j}^{n}}
satisfying the above inequality. But by hypothesis, geodesics are unique, so there is one geodesic satisfying the above inequality for all
N^{\prime}>N
. Consequently we obtain

\displaystyle S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t}^{n})

\displaystyle=-\int\left(\sum_{j}^{M_{n}}\lambda_{j}^{n}\frac{\chi_{A_{t,j}^{n}}}{\mathfrak{m}(A_{t,j}^{n})}\right)^{1-1/N^{\prime}}d\mathfrak{m}=-\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}(\lambda_{j}^{n})^{1-1/N^{\prime}}\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A_{t,j}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}(\lambda_{j}^{n})^{1-1/N^{\prime}}\left(\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{0,j}^{n})+\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(t)}(\Theta_{j}^{n})\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A_{1,j}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta^{n})\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}(\lambda_{j}^{n})^{1-1/N^{\prime}}\mathfrak{m}^{1/N}(A_{0,j}^{n})-\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(t)}(\Theta^{n})\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}(\lambda_{j}^{n})^{1-1/N^{\prime}}\mathfrak{m}^{1/N^{\prime}}(A_{1,j}^{n})

\displaystyle=\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta^{n})S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{0}^{n})+\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta^{n})S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{1}^{n}).

Taking an appropriate subsequential limit gives

S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{t})\leq\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta)S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{0})+\tau_{K,N^{\prime}}^{(1-t)}(\Theta)S_{N^{\prime}}(\mu_{1}),

which implies that
X\in\TCD_{q}(K,N)
, by
Lemma

3.9
.
∎
