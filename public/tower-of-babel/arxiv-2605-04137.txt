CP asymmetries in charged meson decay to two pions
Yuval Grossman, Zoltan Ligeti, Yosef Nir

Abstract
We study the CP asymmetries in B + B^{+} , D + D^{+} , and K + K^{+} decays into π + ​ π 0 \pi^{+}\pi^{0} . These asymmetries are commonly known to vanish in the isospin limit. We clarify what is meant by the term “isospin limit” in this context. We provide a unified formalism to discuss these asymmetries, and develop an understanding of how various suppression factors distinguish between them qualitatively. We estimate the size of the asymmetries in the Standard Model: A C ​ P ​ ( B + → π + ​ π 0 ) ∼ 3 × 10 − 3 A_{CP}(B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\sim 3\times 10^{-3} , A C ​ P ​ ( D + → π + ​ π 0 ) ∼ 10 − 5 A_{CP}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\sim 10^{-5} , and A C ​ P ​ ( K + → π + ​ π 0 ) ∼ 10 − 6 A_{CP}(K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\sim 10^{-6} .

The term “isospin limit” needs clarification in the case of
P\to\pi\pi

•

Under the
SU(2)_{I}

I=1/2

I=0

2

This literal interpretation is obviously not how the term “isospin limit” is used in the relevant literature. Given that, within the SM, isospin is broken by the electroweak interactions and quark masses, and is only an approximate symmetry of the strong interactions, one finds in the literature two different definitions of what is meant by the “isospin limit”:

•

Switching off isospin breaking in flavor conserving interactions, and considering only tree and strong penguin operator contributions to flavor changing processes.

•

Switching off isospin breaking in flavor conserving interactions.

In the first definition (and unlike in the literal interpretation), the processes are allowed. However, as is well known and reviewed below, the CP asymmetry in
P^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0}
vanishes. Although we prefer to use the second definition for the term “isospin limit”, in Section
II.3
we do truncate the effective Hamiltonian in accordance with the first definition.
However, it is important to understand that the approximation of omitting the electroweak penguin (EWP) operators has nothing to do with imposing isospin as a symmetry of the Lagrangian.

II
General Formalism

II.1
Isospin decomposition

The rotation from the physical basis to the isospin basis is given by

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{+}\pi^{-}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\langle 2,0|+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\langle 0,0|,

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{0}\pi^{0}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\langle 2,0|-\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\langle 0,0|,

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{+}\pi^{0}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle 2,1|.

(2)

The decomposition of the corresponding decay amplitudes is given by
Gronau:1990ka
;
Botella:2006zi

\displaystyle A_{+-}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\,A_{1/2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{6}}\,A_{3/2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{6}}\,A_{5/2},

(3)

\displaystyle A_{00}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{6}}\,A_{1/2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\,A_{3/2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\,A_{5/2},

(4)

\displaystyle A_{+0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,A_{3/2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\,A_{5/2},

(5)

where the sub-indices on the RHS correspond to the
\Delta I
generated by operators in the effective Hamiltonian
{\cal H}_{\rm eff}
.

Using CKM unitarity, each decay amplitude
A_{X}
(
X=+-,00,+0
for the mass basis,
X=1/2,3/2,5/2
for the isospin basis) can be written as a sum of two contributions proportional to two different CKM factors
Grossman:1997gd
, which we denote by
\lambda_{T}
and
\lambda_{P}
:

A_{X}=A_{X}^{T}\lambda_{T}+A_{X}^{P}\lambda_{P}\,,\qquad\overline{A}_{X}=A_{X}^{T}\lambda_{T}^{*}+A_{X}^{P}\lambda_{P}^{*}\,,

(6)

where
A_{X}^{T}
and
A_{X}^{P}
are complex hadronic amplitudes whose phases correspond to strong phases. It is convenient to choose
\lambda_{T}
such that tree operators contribute only to
\lambda_{T}
, while penguin operators contribute to both
\lambda_{T}
and
\lambda_{P}
. Explicitly, we choose

\lambda_{T}=\begin{cases}V_{ub}^{*}V_{ud}&\text{for }B,\\
V_{cd}^{*}V_{ud}&\text{for }D,\\
V_{us}^{*}V_{ud}&\text{for }K,\end{cases}\qquad\lambda_{P}=\begin{cases}V_{tb}^{*}V_{td}&\text{for }B,\\
V_{cb}^{*}V_{ub}&\text{for }D,\\
V_{ts}^{*}V_{td}&\text{for }K.\end{cases}

(7)

Interference between the two gives the weak phase that is necessary for a direct CP asymmetry,

A_{CP}(P\to\pi\pi)\propto{\cal I}m(\lambda_{P}/\lambda_{T}).

(8)

II.2
The operator basis

We work within the general framework of hadronic decays. The decay amplitude is written as

A=\langle out|{\cal H}_{\text{eff}}|in\rangle.

(9)

The effective Hamiltonian is given by

{\cal H}_{\text{eff}}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\lambda_{X}\left(\sum_{i}c_{i}\,Q_{i}\right),

(10)

where
\lambda_{X}
is a linear combination of the relevant CKM factor discussed above,
c_{i}
are the Wilson coefficients, and
Q_{i}
are local operators.
The operators are defined at a scale of order the mass of the decaying meson when it is heavy, or at some scale that is above
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
.
Degrees of freedom with masses much larger than this scale have been integrated out. When considering the amplitudes, the parameters are separated into three groups: well known (
G_{F}
,
\lambda_{X}
), perturbatively calculable (
c_{i}
), and non-perturbative (
\langle out|Q_{i}|in\rangle
).

In general, there is an infinite number of operators in the sum. When considering the quark transitions
q_{h}\to q_{l}
(
b\to d
,
c\to u
,
s\to d
for
P=B,D,K
, respectively), then, within the SM and to leading order in
G_{F}
, there are ten operators relevant to
P\to\pi\pi
decays:

\displaystyle Q_{1}=(\bar{q}_{h\alpha}q_{\beta})_{V-A}(\bar{q}_{\beta}q_{l\alpha})_{V-A},

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=(\bar{q}_{h}q)_{V-A}(\bar{q}q_{l})_{V-A},

\displaystyle Q_{3}=(\bar{q}_{h}q_{l})_{V-A}\mbox{$\sum_{q}$}(\bar{q}q)_{V-A},

\displaystyle Q_{4}

\displaystyle=(\bar{q}_{h\alpha}q_{l\beta})_{V-A}\mbox{$\sum_{q}$}(\bar{q}_{\beta}q_{\alpha})_{V-A},

\displaystyle Q_{5}=(\bar{q}_{h}q_{l})_{V-A}\mbox{$\sum_{q}$}(\bar{q}q)_{V+A},

\displaystyle Q_{6}

\displaystyle=(\bar{q}_{h\alpha}q_{l\beta})_{V-A}\mbox{$\sum_{q}$}(\bar{q}_{\beta}q_{\alpha})_{V+A},

\displaystyle Q_{7}=(3/2)\mbox{$\sum_{q}$}e_{q}(\bar{q}_{h}q_{l})_{V-A}(\bar{q}q)_{V+A},

\displaystyle Q_{8}

\displaystyle=(3/2)\mbox{$\sum_{q}$}e_{q}(\bar{q}_{h}q)_{V-A}(\bar{q}q_{l})_{V+A},

\displaystyle Q_{9}=(3/2)\mbox{$\sum_{q}$}e_{q}(\bar{q}_{h}q_{l})_{V-A}(\bar{q}q)_{V-A},

\displaystyle Q_{10}

\displaystyle=(3/2)\mbox{$\sum_{q}$}e_{q}(\bar{q}_{h}q)_{V-A}(\bar{q}q_{l})_{V-A},

(11)

The operators are usually denoted as tree-level operators (
Q_{1,2}
), QCD penguin operators (
Q_{3,4,5,6}
), and electroweak penguin (EWP) operators (
Q_{7,8,9,10}
).

II.3
Truncating
{\cal H}_{\rm eff}
with
Q_{1,\ldots,6}

As a first approximation for the
q_{h}\to q\bar{q}q_{l}
transition amplitudes, we truncate
{\cal H}_{\rm eff}
with the six leading operators, that is, the tree operators
Q_{1,2}
and the strong penguin operators
Q_{3,4,5,6}
.
These six operators can be written in terms of
SU(2)_{I}
representations
Bhattacharya:2025rrv
:

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}{\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}{\cal O}^{1/2}_{1/2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\cal O}^{\prime 1/2}_{1/2},\qquad Q_{2}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}{\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}{\cal O}^{1/2}_{1/2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\cal O}^{\prime 1/2}_{1/2},

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}{\cal O}^{1/2}_{1/2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\cal O}^{\prime 1/2}_{1/2}+{\cal O}^{\prime\prime 1/2}_{1/2},\qquad\qquad Q_{4}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}}{\cal O}^{1/2}_{1/2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\cal O}^{\prime 1/2}_{1/2}+{\cal O}^{\prime\prime 1/2}_{1/2},

(12)

where the isospin basis operators correspond to
{\cal O}^{I}_{I_{3}}
.
The
Q_{5}
and
Q_{6}
operators have the same isospin decomposition as
Q_{3}
and
Q_{4}
, respectively. For the
P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
decay, the initial state is

|1/2,+1/2\rangle
under
SU(2)_{I}
, and the final state is
\langle 2,+1|
. Thus, only
{\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2}
can mediate this decay. (In principle, also
{\cal O}^{5/2}_{1/2}
can do so, but it is not generated by the four-quark operators of the SM.) We learn that the QCD penguin operators
Q_{3,4,5,6}
do not
contribute to the charged meson decays.

In the absence of a strong penguin contribution, as is the case for
P^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0}
, there is only a single CKM factor,
\lambda_{T}
, at play, and thus

A_{CP}(P^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})=0.

(13)

We emphasize again that Eq. (
13
) is based on two approximations: First, truncating
{\cal H}_{\rm eff}
with tree and strong penguin operators, and second, neglecting isospin violation in flavor conserving interactions. In the next section we go beyond the first approximation, and in Section
IV
beyond the second one.
Since both effects are small, we treat them separately, that is, we assume isospin symmetry when we discuss the contributions of EWP operators, and we neglect EWP when we discuss other sources of isospin breaking.

III
Electroweak penguins

In this section, we extend the operators we consider above and add to
{\cal H}_{\rm eff}
the four electroweak penguin (EWP) operators relevant to
q_{h}\to q_{l}
transitions,
Q_{7,8,9,10}
.
The expectation is that the inclusion of EWPs in the SM calculation leads to a direct CP asymmetry different from zero, but suppressed by the corresponding CKM factor and by
{\cal O}(\alpha)
. As we discuss below, the actual suppression turns out to be even stronger.

The EWP operators provide the
{\cal O}_{1/2}^{3/2}
terms that contribute to the
P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
decay
Bhattacharya:2025rrv
:

Q_{9}=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}{\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\cal O}^{\prime 1/2}_{1/2}-\frac{1}{2}{\cal O}^{\prime\prime 1/2}_{1/2},\qquad Q_{10}=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}{\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\cal O}^{\prime 1/2}_{1/2}-\frac{1}{2}{\cal O}^{\prime\prime 1/2}_{1/2}.

(14)

The
Q_{7}
and
Q_{8}
operators have the same isospin decomposition as
Q_{9}
and
Q_{10}
, respectively. The EWP terms also provide the required second CKM factor,
\lambda_{P}
, that interferes with the tree CKM factor
\lambda_{T}
.

When discussing
\Delta I=3/2
transitions, Eqs. (
II.3
) and (
14
) imply the following:

•

The combination
(c_{1}-c_{2})(Q_{1}-Q_{2})
does not contribute,

•

The strong penguin operators
Q_{3},Q_{4},Q_{5},Q_{6}
do not contribute,

•

The combinations
(c_{7}-c_{8})(Q_{7}-Q_{8})
and
(c_{9}-c_{10})(Q_{9}-Q_{10})
do not contribute.

Thus,
(c_{1}+c_{2})(Q_{1}^{(3/2)}+Q_{2}^{(3/2)})
,
(c_{7}+c_{8})(Q_{7}^{(3/2)}+Q_{8}^{(3/2)})
and
(c_{9}+c_{10})(Q_{9}^{(3/2)}+Q_{10}^{(3/2)})
are left as the only three contributing combinations.

Then, we can consider only the contributions from the tree,
Q_{1}+Q_{2}

Q_{9}+Q_{10}

But these two combinations fulfill

Q_{1}^{(3/2)}+Q_{2}^{(3/2)}=(3/2)\big(Q_{9}^{(3/2)}+Q_{10}^{(3/2)}\big).

(16)

Considering the matrix element

\langle 2|{\cal H}_{\rm eff}^{3/2}|1/2\rangle=\lambda_{T}A_{3/2,2}^{T}+\lambda_{P}A_{3/2,2}^{P},

(17)

where
{\cal H}_{\rm eff}^{3/2}
is the
\Delta I=3/2

\displaystyle A_{3/2,2}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1/\sqrt{3})(c_{1}+c_{2})\langle 2|{\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2}|1/2\rangle,

(18)

\displaystyle A_{3/2,2}^{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\sqrt{3}/2)(c_{9}+c_{10})\langle 2|{\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2}|1/2\rangle,

(19)

we obtain

A_{3/2,2}^{P}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{(c_{9}+c_{10})}{(c_{1}+c_{2})}A_{3/2,2}^{T}.

(20)

We learn that, neglecting
Q_{7,8}
, there is no strong phase between the EWP and tree contributions to
\Delta I=3/2
transitions
Neubert:1998pt
;
Bhattacharya:2025rrv
, so Eq. (
13
) still follows. (In Eq. (
18
) we neglect the
{\cal O}(\alpha)
contribution of
Q_{9}+Q_{10}
to
A_{3/2,2}^{T}
. Including this contribution would not affect the strong phase alignment argument.)

The different Lorentz structure of
Q_{7}+Q_{8}
from that of
Q_{9}+Q_{10}
implies that, in contrast to
Q_{9}+Q_{10}
, the
\Delta I=3/2
matrix element of
Q_{7}+Q_{8}
is not related to that of
Q_{1}+Q_{2}
, and in general differs by size and strong phase. Including
Q_{7}+Q_{8}
, Eq. (
19
) obtains an extra term:

A_{3/2,2}^{P}=-(\sqrt{3}/2)(c_{9}+c_{10})\,\langle 2|({\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2})_{LL}|1/2\rangle\times(1+r_{78}e^{i\delta_{78}}),

(21)

with

r_{78}=\frac{c_{7}+c_{8}}{c_{9}+c_{10}}\left|\frac{\langle 2|({\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2})_{LR}|1/2\rangle}{\langle 2|({\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2})_{LL}|1/2\rangle}\right|,\qquad\delta_{78}=\arg\left(\frac{\langle 2|({\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2})_{LR}|1/2\rangle}{\langle 2|({\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2})_{LL}|1/2\rangle}\right),

(22)

where
r_{78}
is real. Assuming that the hadronic matrix elements are of similar size, we have
r_{78}\ll 1
. On the other hand, we do not have any information about
\delta_{78}
. The sine of the relative strong phase is then given by

\sin\left[\arg\left(1+r_{78}e^{i\delta_{78}}\right)\right]=r_{78}\sin\delta_{78}+{\cal O}(r_{78}^{2}).

(23)

The CP asymmetry in
P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
to leading order in
r_{78}
is now given by

A_{CP}(P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})=3{\cal I}m\left(\frac{\lambda_{P}}{\lambda_{T}}\right)\frac{c_{9}+c_{10}}{c_{1}+c_{2}}\,r_{78}\sin\delta_{78}.

(24)

Thus, three suppression factors are at play:

•

CKM suppression,
{\cal I}m\left(\lambda_{P}/\lambda_{T}\right)
.

•

{\cal O}(\alpha)
suppression from
(c_{9}+c_{10})/(c_{1}+c_{2})
.

•

Strong phase suppression,
r_{78}
.

We note that the first two factors are reliably calculated. For the last one, the ratio of Wilson coefficients is known to be small. The overall suppression, however, relies on the assumption that the hadronic matrix elements are of comparable size.

IV
Isospin breaking in QCD+QED

In flavor conserving interactions, there are two main isospin breaking parameters: The mass difference,
m_{d}-m_{u}
, and the EM charge difference,
Q_{d}-Q_{u}
.
The size of the corresponding contributions are of order
(m_{d}-m_{u})/\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\sim 0.01
and
\alpha\sim 0.01
, respectively. Both accompany the operator
(u\bar{u}-d\bar{d})
, and thus can be treated collectively as a spurion transforming as
\delta_{\rm IB}{\cal O}^{1}_{0}
under
SU(2)_{I}
, with
\delta_{\rm IB}\sim 0.01
.

There are three leading effects to the introduction of
\delta_{\rm IB}
:

•

The
\pi^{0}
mass eigenstate is no longer a pure
|1,0\rangle
isospin eigenstate, as it acquires a
|0,0\rangle
component (“
\pi
–
\eta
mixing”). Consequently
\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
acquires a
|1,1\rangle
component.

•

The
{\cal O}^{1/2}_{1/2}{\cal O}^{1}_{0}
operators have a
|3/2,1/2\rangle
component, allowing the strong penguin operators to contribute to
P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
decays.

•

The
{\cal O}^{3/2}_{1/2}{\cal O}^{1}_{0}
and
{\cal O}^{1/2}_{1/2}{\cal O}^{1}_{0}{\cal O}^{1}_{0}
operators have a
|5/2,1/2\rangle
component, allowing these operators to contribute to
P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
in this novel way.

Below we discuss these three effects in turn.

IV.1
\pi
–
\eta
mixing

Isospin breaking results in
\pi^{0}
mixing with
\eta
,
\eta^{\prime}
and other states. In the following, we consider only the
\pi
–
\eta
mixing as it is the dominant effect.

The two relevant states of well-defined transformation properties under
SU(2)_{I}
are
\pi_{3}(1,0)
, the EM-neutral member in the
SU(2)_{I}
-triplet, and
\pi_{1}(0,0)
, an
SU(2)_{I}
-singlet. We denote the rotation angle from the isospin basis
(\pi_{3},\pi_{1})
to the mass basis
(\pi^{0},\eta)
by
\theta_{\pi\eta}
. To lowest order in chiral perturbation theory (
\chi
PT), it is given by
Gross:1979ur

\theta_{\pi\eta}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\frac{m_{d}-m_{u}}{m_{s}-\hat{m}}\simeq 0.011,

(25)

where
\hat{m}=(m_{d}+m_{u})/2
. (Including higher order terms in
\chi
PT, there is a substantial uncertainty in this mixing angle; see, e.g., Refs.
Cirigliano:2003gt
;
Kroll:2005sd
;
Osipov:2025znd
;
FlavourLatticeAveragingGroupFLAG:2024oxs
.) Then,

\pi^{0}=c\pi_{3}+s\pi_{0},\qquad\eta=c\pi_{1}-s\pi_{3},

(26)

where
c\equiv\cos\theta_{\pi\eta}
and
s\equiv\sin\theta_{\pi\eta}
.

We define the decay amplitudes
A_{3}
and
A_{1}
as follows:

A_{3}=A(P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi_{3}),\qquad A_{1}=A(P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi_{1}),

(27)

so that

A(P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})=cA_{3}+sA_{1},\qquad A(P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta)=-sA_{3}+cA_{1}.

(28)

The CP-averaged decay rates are given by

\displaystyle\overline{\Gamma}(P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|{\vec{p}}|}{8\pi m_{P}}\frac{1}{2}\left[c^{2}(|A_{3}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{3}|^{2})+2cs{\cal R}e(A_{3}A_{1}^{*}+\bar{A}_{3}\bar{A}_{1}^{*})+s^{2}(|A_{1}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{1}|^{2})\right],

\displaystyle\overline{\Gamma}(P^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|{\vec{p}}|}{8\pi m_{P}}\frac{1}{2}\left[s^{2}(|A_{3}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{3}|^{2})-2cs{\cal R}e(A_{3}A_{1}^{*}+\bar{A}_{3}\bar{A}_{1}^{*})+c^{2}(|A_{1}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{1}|^{2})\right].\qquad

(29)

Given the smallness of
\theta_{\pi\eta}
, Eq. (
25
), we take a zeroth order approximation:

\frac{|A_{3}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{3}|^{2}}{|A_{1}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{1}|^{2}}\simeq\frac{{\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}}{{\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\eta}}.

(30)

This estimate does not apply to
P=K
, where phase space does not allow
K\to\pi\eta
.

Considering the CP asymmetries, we have, to leading order in
s
, and taking into account that in the isospin limit and neglecting EWP operators
|A_{3}|=|\bar{A}_{3}|
,

A_{CP}^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}=\frac{2s{\cal I}m(A_{3}A_{1}^{*}-\bar{A}_{3}\bar{A}_{1}^{*})}{|A_{3}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{3}|^{2}},\qquad A_{CP}^{\pi^{+}\eta}=\frac{|A_{1}|^{2}-|\bar{A}_{1}|^{2}}{|A_{1}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{1}|^{2}},

(31)

and thus

\frac{A_{CP}^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}}{A_{CP}^{\pi^{+}\eta}}=\frac{2s{\cal I}m(A_{3}A_{1}^{*}-\bar{A}_{3}\bar{A}_{1}^{*})}{|A_{1}|^{2}-|\bar{A}_{1}|^{2}}\times\frac{|A_{1}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{1}|^{2}}{|A_{3}|^{2}+|\bar{A}_{3}|^{2}}.

(32)

The CKM factor
{\cal I}m(\lambda_{T}\lambda_{P}^{*})
cancels out in this ratio, and we obtain

\frac{A_{CP}^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}}{A_{CP}^{\pi^{+}\eta}}=2s\frac{{\cal I}m(A_{3}^{T}A_{1}^{P*}-A_{3}^{P}A_{1}^{T*})}{2{\cal I}m(A_{1}^{T}A_{1}^{P*})}\times\frac{{\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\eta}}{{\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}},

(33)

where
A_{i}^{T,P}
are the coefficients of
\lambda_{T,P}
.

Taking as a rough estimate,
|A_{3}^{T,P}/A_{1}^{T,P}|\sim({\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}/{\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\eta})^{1/2}
, we obtain (again, a rough estimate)

\frac{A_{CP}^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}}{A_{CP}^{\pi^{+}\eta}}\sim 2s\,\frac{\sin\delta_{31}^{ut}-\sin\delta_{31}^{tu}}{2\sin\delta_{11}^{ut}}\times\left(\frac{{\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\eta}}{{\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}}\right)^{1/2},

(34)

where
\delta_{ij}^{qp}
are strong phases. Assuming that the ratio involving strong phases is
{\cal O}(1)
, we thus estimate, for
P=B,D
,

\left|A_{CP}(P^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})\right|\sim 0.02\,({\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\eta}/{\cal B}^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}})^{1/2}\left|A_{CP}(P^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\eta)\right|.

(35)

V
The CP asymmetries in charged
B
,
D
and

In this section, we apply the formalism presented above to the concrete cases of
B^{+}
,
D^{+}
, and
K^{+}
decays into
\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
, and provide a quantitative estimate for each.

V.1
A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})

The CP asymmetry in
B\to\pi\pi
is not CKM suppressed:

{\cal I}m\left(\frac{\lambda_{P}}{\lambda_{T}}\right)={\cal I}m\left(\frac{V_{tb}^{*}V_{td}}{V_{ub}^{*}V_{ud}}\right)=\sin\alpha\approx 0.99.

(38)

The contribution of the EWP operators is suppressed not only by the expected
{\cal O}(\alpha)
factor in
(c_{9}+c_{10})/(c_{1}+c_{2})
, but also by the strong phase alignment which introduces an additional suppression factor,
(c_{7}+c_{8})/(c_{9}+c_{10})
. Together, the suppression factor is given by (see Table
2
)

\displaystyle A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{3(c_{7}+c_{8})}{c_{1}+c_{2}}

(39)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 1.3\times 10^{-3}.

The contribution generated by
\pi
–
\eta
mixing
Gronau:2005pq
;
Gardner:2005pq
can be estimated from Eq. (
35
) and the data in Table
3
:

\displaystyle A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 0.02\left[{\cal B}(B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta)/{\cal B}(B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\right]^{1/2}A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\eta)

(40)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle(2.5\pm 0.9)\times 10^{-3},

currently providing a
2.8\sigma
hint of a nonzero contribution.

The contribution generated by isospin breaking in the strong penguin operators can be estimated by the CP asymmetry in neutral
B
decays:

\displaystyle A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm IB}\times A_{CP}(B^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-})

(41)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 3\times 10^{-3},

where we used the experimental value from Table
5
.
To summarize, Eq. (
39
) tells us that EWP operators contribute at a level of
10^{-3}
, Eq. (
40
) tells us that the contribution of
\pi
–
\eta
mixing is
\sim{\rm few}\times 10^{-3}
(depending on the not yet established
A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\eta)
), and Eq. (
41
) tells us that isospin violation in the QCD penguin contributions yields
{\cal O}({\rm few}\times 10^{-3})
CP asymmetry.

We conclude that the CP asymmetry
A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
is generated by three different effects, of order
\alpha\alpha_{s}
,
\theta_{\pi\eta}\alpha_{s}
, and
r_{78}\alpha
, which all happen to be of a similar order of magnitude. It is expected then to be of
{\cal O}(0.003)
, with large uncertainties from hadronic matrix elements and strong phases.

While there are many statements in the literature that the asymmetry is small, only a few works provide explicit numerical estimates. Ref.
Beneke:2003zv
uses QCD factorization to estimate the electroweak penguin (EWP) contribution and quotes a central value of
-2\times 10^{-4}
, with theoretical uncertainties of order
10^{-3}
. Ref.
Bauer:2005kd
employs SCET and finds that the asymmetry is below
0.05
. Ref.
Huber:2021cgk
states that the asymmetry is very small, and their global fit yields
0.05\pm 0.20
.
A recent global fit yields
-0.024\pm 0.016

Fang:2026fhl
, a factor of a few above our estimate.
The difference is due to the value of
r_{78}
. In the notation of our paper, the fit in Ref.
Fang:2026fhl
gives a value of
r_{78}
that is much larger than the one we used. As stated below Eq. (
22
), our estimate is based on the assumption that the two relevant hadronic matrix elements are comparable, while the central value from the fit of Ref.
Fang:2026fhl
implies that the ratio of hadronic matrix elements is much larger than 1.

V.2
A_{CP}(D^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})

The CP asymmetry in
D\to\pi\pi
is strongly CKM suppressed:

{\cal I}m\left(\frac{\lambda_{P}}{\lambda_{T}}\right)={\cal I}m\left(\frac{V_{cb}^{*}V_{ub}}{V_{cd}^{*}V_{ud}}\right)\approx 7.0\times 10^{-4}\sin\gamma\approx 6.4\times 10^{-4}.

(42)

Other suppression factors are challenging to estimate because long distance contributions to
D\to\pi\pi
are significant and can even be dominant (see,
e.g.
, Refs.
Golden:1989qx
;
Brod:2011re
). The short distance contributions of the penguin operators are suppressed not only by
{\cal O}(\alpha)\sim 0.01
, but also by the GIM mechanism, roughly a factor of
{\cal O}(m_{b}^{2}/m_{W}^{2})\sim 0.003
.

The estimation of the EWP contribution to the CP asymmetry is particularly difficult. The naive estimate combines the CKM suppression of Eq. (
42
) with the
\alpha
-suppression of EWP-to-tree ratio, suggesting that this contribution to the CP asymmetry is
\sim{\rm few}\times 10^{-6}
.

The contribution generated by
\pi
–
\eta
mixing can be estimated from Eq. (
35
) and the data in Table
3
:

\displaystyle A_{CP}(D^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 0.02\left[{\cal B}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta)/{\cal B}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\right]^{1/2}A_{CP}(D^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\eta)

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1.0\pm 1.6)\times 10^{-4},

currently providing only an upper bound on this contribution. The two relevant suppression factors – the CKM suppression of Eq. (
42
) and the
\pi
–
\eta
mixing of Eq. (
25
) – suggest that also this contribution is
\sim{\rm few}\times 10^{-6}
.

The contribution generated by isospin breaking in the strong penguin operators can be estimated by the CP asymmetry in neutral
D
decays:

A_{CP}(D^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})\sim\delta_{\rm IB}\times A_{CP}(D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-})\approx 2\times 10^{-5},

(44)

where we used the experimental value from Table
5
. Note that the CP asymmetry in
D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
is a factor of three larger than even just the CKM suppression factor of Eq. (
42
). There has been much discussion in the literature whether this surprisingly large asymmetry is due to new physics or to a QCD effect.
Either way, it implies a strong enhancement,
|A^{P}/A^{T}|\gg 1
(for an explicit estimate, see Ref.
Gavrilova:2023fzy
), which we expect to affect also the asymmetry in the charged
D
decay.

We conclude that the CP asymmetry
A_{CP}(D^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
is likely to be dominated by isospin violation in the strong interactions. It is expected to be of
{\cal O}(10^{-5})
, with large uncertainties from hadronic matrix elements and strong phases.

Previous studies
Grossman:2006jg
;
Brod:2011re
;
Cheng:2012wr
;
Grossman:2012eb
did not go beyond “the isospin limit” prediction of vanishing asymmetry.

V.3
A_{CP}(K^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})

The CP asymmetry in
K\to\pi\pi
is strongly CKM suppressed:

{\cal I}m\left(\frac{\lambda_{P}}{\lambda_{T}}\right)={\cal I}m\left(\frac{V_{ts}^{*}V_{td}}{V_{us}^{*}V_{ud}}\right)=1.6\times 10^{-3}\sin\beta\approx 6.2\times 10^{-4}.

(45)

The contribution of EWP operators is suppressed not only by
{\cal O}(\alpha)
(see the
(c_{9}+c_{10})/(c_{1}+c_{2})
entry in Table
2
), but also by a tiny strong phase. In fact, this suppression is much stronger than in the corresponding
B
-meson decay. It follows from the Watson theorem
Watson:1952ji
: If
\pi-\pi
scattering is elastic, then the strong phase in
K\to\pi\pi
decays depends only on the isospin of the final state. Indeed, the inelasticity in
\pi-\pi
scattering is known to be small at
E\sim m_{K}

Caprini:2005zr
;
Garcia-Martin:2011iqs
;
Hyams:1973zf
;
Pennington:1973xv
,
\lesssim 10^{-4}
for final
I=2
states due to the absence of corresponding resonances. We conclude that the contribution of EWP operators to the CP asymmetry in
K^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0}
is
\lesssim 10^{-9}
.

The contribution from
\pi
–
\eta
mixing cannot be estimated in a way
similar to that for the
B
and
D
decays, because, due to phase space, there is no
K\to\pi\eta
decay. We can only make an estimate on the basis of the known suppression factors: The CKM suppression of Eq. (
45
) and the
\pi
–
\eta
mixing angle of
Eq. (
25
). Their combination implies that the contribution of the
I=1
component in the
\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
state is
\lesssim 6\times 10^{-6}
.

The contribution generated by isospin breaking in the strong penguin operators can be estimated by the CP asymmetry in neutral
K
decays:

A_{CP}(K^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})\sim\delta_{\rm IB}\times A_{CP}(K^{0}\to\pi\pi)\approx 2\times 10^{-7},

(46)

where we used the experimental value from Table
5
.

We conclude that
A_{CP}(K^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
gets a negligible contribution from EWP operators, and is dominated by isospin violation in QCD, particularly by the contribution related to the
I=1
component in
\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
stemming from
\pi
–
\eta
mixing. It is expected to be of
{\cal O}(10^{-5}-10^{-6})
, with large uncertainty coming from the strong phase of the
I=1
final state.

A similar conclusion is stated in Refs.
Dib:1990gr
;
Dib:1990qj
, where an estimate of
{\cal O}(10^{-6})
is quoted with large uncertainties. In particular, they argue that electromagnetic corrections to the weak amplitude give the dominant contribution, leading to an asymmetry at the level of
10^{-6}
, while isospin-breaking effects from quark mass differences do not contribute. While we agree with their numerical estimate, we differ in the identification of the leading effect.
Ref.
Riazuddin:1993pn
studies the effect of intermediate vector meson exchange in generating the asymmetry. Using updated values of the CKM factors, we find that their estimate corresponds to an upper bound of order
10^{-5}
.

VI
Conclusions

There are three possible sources of CP asymmetries in charged meson decays into two pions. Electroweak penguins (EWP) and isospin breaking in QCD penguins are both suppressed, but are roughly of the same order. The
\Delta I=5/2
contribution is suppressed by second-order isospin breaking or by second order in the weak interaction, and is much smaller than the other two.

In our analysis, some of our arguments are based on experimental data and spurion analysis. This is the case in estimating the contribution from
\pi
–
\eta
mixing using the experimental value of
A_{CP}(P^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\eta)
and in estimating the contribution from isospin breaking in strong penguins using the experimental value of
A_{CP}(P^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
. These estimates are then valid also beyond the Standard Model. In cases where we use explicit SM input, such as the estimate of EWP operators using the SM values of the relevant Wilson coefficients, new physics can modify these predictions.

The order of magnitude estimates of the yet-unmeasured CP asymmetries
A_{CP}(P^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
are presented in Table
6
.
We estimate the size of the asymmetries to be

\displaystyle A_{CP}(B^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle\sim 3\times 10^{-3},

\displaystyle A_{CP}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle\sim 10^{-5},

\displaystyle A_{CP}(K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle\sim 10^{-6}.

(47)

For the CP asymmetries in
D
and
K
decays, there are large hadronic uncertainties, and each could be an order of magnitude different (in either direction – enhancement or suppression) from these estimates. For the CP asymmetry in
B
decay there are several contributions of order a few permille which can yield a total asymmetry in the range
0.001-0.01
.

Let us highlight some of the unique features for each of the decaying mesons:

•

A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
: Perturbative calculations are possible. They imply, in particular, that there is an approximate strong phase alignment between tree and EWP operators, which suppresses the EWP contribution to the CP asymmetry well below the naive estimate. Measuring
A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\eta)
would allow a better estimate of the contribution from
\pi
–
\eta
mixing.

•

A_{CP}(D^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
: The CKM structure and the quark mass hierarchy combine to imply that long distance physics plays a significant role, and makes an estimate of the asymmetry particularly challenging. The experimental measurement of
A_{CP}(D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
combined with a spurion analysis of isospin breaking provide the best handle over the expected size of the asymmetry. Measuring
A_{CP}(D^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\eta)
would allow a better estimate of the contribution from
\pi
–
\eta
mixing.

•

A_{CP}(K^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
: The Watson theorem implies that all contributions to the final
I=2
state carry the same strong phase, and thus do not generate an asymmetry. Consequently,
\pi
–
\eta
mixing plays a crucial role, as it adds a final
I=1
component, accompanied by an independent strong phase.

These considerations warrant increased experimental efforts to measure
A_{CP}(P^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
for
P=B,D,K
, as there is much to learn from improved bounds or measurements in the future. In particular,

•

Measuring
A_{CP}(B^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
has direct implications for the extraction of
\alpha
from
B\to\pi\pi
decays
Gronau:1990ka
;
Gardner:2005pq
, and indirect implications for the so-called
K\pi
puzzle that is also related to isospin violation
Gronau:2005kz
.

•

Measuring
A_{CP}(D^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
may shed light on the controversy regarding the surprisingly large value of
\Delta A_{CP}
(see,
e.g.
, Refs.
Grossman:2019xcj
;
Dery:2019ysp
;
Schacht:2022kuj
;
Sinha:2025cuo
;
Friday:2025gpj
;
Fleischer:2025zhl
).

•

Measuring
A_{CP}(K^{\pm}\to\pi^{\pm}\pi^{0})
will provide a unique experimental probe of
\pi
–
\eta
mixing.
