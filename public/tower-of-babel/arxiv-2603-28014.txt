Ancient Ricci flows with nonnegative Ricci curvature
Yuxing Deng, Ganqi Wang, Yongjia Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we study the asymptotic geometry of a noncollapsed ancient Ricci flow with nonnegative Ricci curvature via its tangent flow at infinity — a noncollapsed 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -limit metric soliton [ Bam23 , CMZ24 ] . We first prove some estimates for noncollapsed 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -limit metric solitons with nonnegative Ricci curvature, and then obtain two dichotomy theorems for ancient Ricci flows. In particular, we show that: (1) for a noncollapsed ancient Ricci flow with nonnegative Ricci curvature, either its asymptotic volume ratio is always zero, or every tangent flow at infinity is a Ricci flat cone; (2) for a noncollapsed ancient Ricci flow with positively pinched Ricci curvature ( Ric ≥ ε ​ R ​ g \Ric\geq\varepsilon Rg ), either it is compact, or every tangent flow at infinity is a Ricci flat cone.

Introduction

In
[
Per02
]
, Perelman proved the existence of an asymptotic shrinker for every
\kappa
-solution; by
\kappa
-solution we mean noncollapsed ancient solution with nonnegative curvature operator. This result sheds much light on the asymptotic geometry for
\kappa
-solutions, and is therefore an important part of the proof of the geometrization conjecture
[
Ham93
,
Per02
,
Per03a
,
Per03b
]
, since all three-dimensional singularity models are
\kappa
-solutions. For instance, combining the existence of asymptotic shrinker and the classification of three-dimensional Ricci shrinkers, Perelman showed that there is a universal noncollapsing constant
\kappa_{0}>0
for all
\kappa
-solutions. There are many other examples where asymptotic shrinkers are implemented in the study of ancient solutions; see
[
Nab10
,
CZ11
,
Zhang20
,
CMZ25
]
, to name but a few.

For general noncollapsed ancient solutions, asymptotic shrinkers, unfortunately, do not necessarily exist. Its existence is known to be true either in the case of Type I curvature bound
[
Nab10
]
, or in the case where there holds Hamilton’s trace Harnack estimate, such as the PIC2 case
[
Bre09
]
. In general, because of the lack of the “bounded curvature at bounded distance condition” for the blow-down sequence, the asymptotic limit for an ancient Ricci flow either does not exist, or, even if it existed, may not be smooth.

Bamler’s works
[
Bam20a
,
Bam20b
,
Bam23
]
provide many new ideas and new techniques for sequences of Ricci flows that do not necessarily converge smoothly. Viewing a Ricci flow coupled with a conjugate heat kernel as an evolving metric measure space (called a metric flow pair, see §2), he defined a weak notion of convergence called the
\mathbb{F}
-convergence for a sequence of metric flow pairs, which, roughly speaking, is tantamount to the Gromov-Wasserstein convergence for almost every time-slice. It is proved in
[
Bam23
]
that every sequence of
H
-concentrated metric flow pairs, after passing to a subsequence, converges in the
\mathbb{F}
-sense. More details can be found in §2. In a recent work of Fang-Li
[
FL25
]
, a new notion of weak convergence is introduced.

If a sequence of metric flow pairs (constructed with Ricci flow of the same dimension) is noncollapsed, namely, admits a uniform bound for the Nash entropy, then its
\mathbb{F}
-limit is an almost-everywhere-smooth metric flow pair, with its singular part a set of space-time Minkowski codimension no less than four. Furthermore, the convergence is also smooth on the regular part of the limit flow. In addition, if the Nash entropies of the sequence converge to a constant, then the
\mathbb{F}
-limit is a self-similar metric flow pair called a
noncollpased
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric soliton
; see §2 for more details. This object is exactly the asymptotic limit for general noncollapsed ancient solutions corresponding to Perelman’s asymptotic shrinker.

Noncollpased
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric soliton is understood to be a natural generalization of smooth Ricci shrinker. We briefly summarize some of its properties proved in
[
Bam20b
]
. Sometimes, because of its self-similarity, a noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric solitons is not distinguished from its model, a metric mesure space
(X,d,\nu)
, which is identified with the
t=-1
time-slice of the self-similar metric flow pair. Furthermore, the model is fully determined by its regular part
(\mathcal{R}_{X},\mathfrak{g},f_{0})
, since the metric completion of
(\mathcal{R}_{X},d_{\mathfrak{g}})
is
(X,d)
and the singular part is a null-set, i.e.

\displaystyle\nu(X\setminus\mathcal{R}_{X})=0,

with Minkowski codimension no less that four. Here,
f_{0}
is a smooth function on
\mathcal{R}_{X}
called the potential function, and
\mathfrak{g}
and
f_{0}
satisfy the shrinker equation on
\mathcal{R}_{X}
:

\displaystyle\Ric_{\mathfrak{g}}+\nabla^{2}_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{g}\quad\text{ and }\quad d\nu=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f_{0}}d\mathfrak{g}\quad\quad\text{ on }\qquad\mathcal{R}_{X},

Because of its importance as generalized singularity models,
[
CMZ24
]
studied systematically noncollpased
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric solitons, to which many classical results for Ricci shrinkers are extended. For instance, the potential function
f_{0}
has almost quadratic growth from a point
x_{0}\in\mathcal{R}_{X}
. Such point is called a
center
of the soliton, and henceforth will always be denoted by
x_{0}
; see §2. Furthermore, a global Sobolev inequality, a quadratic lower bound estimate for the scalar curvature, a volume growth lower bound, and a local gap theorem for large balls around a center are proved in
[
CMZ24
]
.

To summarize the points we have made by far, let us recall the following facts: Perelman’s asymptotic shrinker is a powerful tool for the study of the asymptotic geometry of
\kappa
-solutions; general noncollapsed ancient solutions do not have asymptotic shrinkers, but their tangent flows at infinity are noncollpased
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric solitons; the convergence of the blow-down sequence of an ancient solution to its tangent flow at infinity is smooth on the smooth part of the limit flow; the tangent flow at infinity is almost everywhere a smooth shrinker. It is therefore clear that, by studying noncollpased
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric solitons, especially its smooth part, we can obtain some results concerning the asymptotic geometry of noncollapsed ancient solutions. The main results of this paper are in this fashion.

Before introducing our results, a few clarifications need to be made for our terminologies. Our notion of noncollapsedness is somewhat different from Perelman’s, but is identical to Bamler’s, namely, our noncollapsing condition is defined in terms of the boundedness of the Nash entropy. By saying that an ancient Ricci flow
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
is noncollapsed, we mean that for some
(x,t)\in M\times(-\infty,0]
and
Y>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(\tau)\geq-Y\quad\text{ for all }\quad\tau>0.

By saying that a sequence
\{(M^{i},g^{i}_{t},x_{i})_{t\in[-T_{i},0]}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
n
-dimensional Ricci flows is noncollapsed, we mean that for some
\tau>0
and
Y<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x_{i},0}(\tau)\geq-Y\quad\text{ for all }\quad i.

A
noncollapsed

\mathbb{F}
-limit metric soliton is therefore the
\mathbb{F}
-limit of a
noncollapsed
sequence of Ricci flows.

We first introduce our results on noncollpased
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric solitons with nonnegative Ricci curvature, and then we present their applications to ancient Ricci flows. For the definitions that appear in the statements of the theorems, refer to §2. Our first result shows that on the smooth part of a non-Ricci-flat metric soliton with nonnegative Ricci curvature, the scalar curvature must be bounded from below by a positive constant; this result is analogous to
[
Ni05
, Proposition 1.1]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,d,\nu)
be (the model of) a noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric soliton and
(\mathcal{R}_{X},\mathfrak{g},f_{0})
its regular part. Assume that the Ricci curvature is non-negative on
\mathcal{R}_{X}
and does not vanish everywhere. Then there exists a positive number
\delta=\delta(X)\in(0,1]
such that

R(x)\geq\delta,\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathcal{R}_{X}.

In spite of the similarity of the conclusion of the above result to
[
Ni05
, Proposition 1.1]
, our proof is somewhat different, since we cannot estimate the Ricci curvature along a geodesic by the second variational formula; the regular part of a metric soliton is not necessarily convex, and any geodesic may possibly exceed the regular part. Therefore, we estimate the scalar curvature along integral curves of
\nabla f_{0}
instead.

Our next result on metric soliton is a volume estimate of the distance ball
B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},A)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}
, which is not necessarily a geodesic ball since
(\mathcal{R}_{X},\mathfrak{g})
is generally not complete. For complete smooth Ricci shrinkers, Cao and Zhou
[
CZ10
]
showed
|B(x_{0},A)|\leq CA^{n}
, where
C
is a constant depending on the soliton; an improvement was given by Haslhofer-Müller
[
HM11
]
, showing that if
x_{0}
is a minimum point of
f
, then
C
is a dimensional constant; a recent work of Li-Wang
[
LW24
]
further improved Cao-Zhou’s estimate, showing that
C
is a dimensional constant for any
x_{0}
; if the scalar curvature of the shrinker is bounded from below by a positive constant, namely,
R\geq\delta>0
, then Zhang
[
Zhang11
]
gave a sharp volume growth estimate of the form
|B(x_{0},A)|\leq CA^{n-2\delta}
, where
C
is a constant depending on the soliton, and the sharpness of the estimate is seen in the spherical and cylindrical cases. We generalize
[
Zhang11
, Theorem 1.2]
to distant balls on the regular part of a metric soliton.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,d,\nu)
be (the model of) a noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric soliton and
(\mathcal{R}_{X},\mathfrak{g},f_{0})
its regular part. Assume the scalar curvature is bounded from below by some
\delta>0
on
\mathcal{R}_{X}
. Then we have

|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},A)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}|\leq CA^{n-2\delta}\quad\text{ for all }\quad A\geq 1,

where
x_{0}
is a center of the soliton, and
C
is a constant depending on the soliton.

Next, we shall consider the asymptotic volume ratio (
\AVR
) of a metric soliton. For a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, the
\AVR
is defined to be

\displaystyle\AVR:=\lim_{A\to+\infty}\frac{|B_{g}(x,A)|}{\omega_{n}A^{n}},

where
x_{0}
is a fixed point on
M
. Note that the
\AVR
may not exist for a manifold. But for manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature, it always exists and is independent of

[
CLY12
]
shows that the
\AVR
is well-defined for every Ricci shrinker, and we prove the same result for noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric solitons.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X,d,\nu)
be (the model of) a noncollapsed
\mathbb{F}
-limit metric soliton,
(\mathcal{R}_{X},\mathfrak{g},f_{0})
be its regular part, and
x_{0}
be a center of the metric soliton. Then the limit

\lim_{A\to+\infty}\frac{|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},A)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}|}{\omega_{n}A^{n}}\in[0,C(n)e^{W}]

exists, where
\omega_{n}
denotes the volume of the unit Euclidean ball and
W\leq 0
is the soliton entropy. We define the limit as the
\AVR
of a metric soliton.

For a Ricci flow, the
\AVR
is defined time-wise, namely, it is a function of time, should it exist. Perelman proved that the
\AVR
is always zero for a non-flat
\kappa
-solution; this is an important element in his proof of the
\kappa
-compactness theorem
[
Per02
, §10]
. Therefore, it is also interesting to study the
\AVR
in more general cases.
If we replace
\Rm\geq 0
in
[
Per02
, 11.4]
by
\Ric\geq 0
, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be a noncollapsed ancient Ricci flow with bounded curvature on each compact time interval. Suppose
\Ric\geq 0
on
M\times(-\infty,0].
Then either

1.

every tangent flow at infinity is a Ricci-flat cone, or

2.

\AVR_{g_{t}}\equiv 0
for all
t\leq 0
.

At last, we consider noncollapsed ancient Ricci flows with positively pinched Ricci curvature (
\Ric\geq\varepsilon Rg
). This study is inspired by a problem proposed by Hamilton (see also
[
Ni05
]
) — is it true that a complete manifold with positively pinched Ricci curvature always compact? Hamilton asked this question in dimension three, since an affirmative answer will simplify Hamilton’s classification of three-dimensional manifolds with positive Ricci curvature
[
Ham82
]
. However, it was solved in dimension three in
[
LT25
]
by applying Hamilton’s Ricci flow, and it remains open in higher dimensional cases.

On the other hand, Hamilton
[
Ham94
]
solved an extrinsic counterpart of the question above, namely, every complete hypersurface with positively pinched second fundamental form must be closed. Ni
[
Ni25
]
gave an alternative proof for Hamilton’s theorem, and also a relatively comprehensive survey of this problem. It is known that any Ricci shrinker with positively pinched Ricci curvature must be compact. This is nothing but a corollary of
[
Ni05
, Proposition 1.1]
. For expanding and steady cases, if, in addition, the potential function admits a critical point, then every soliton with positively pinched Ricci curvature must be Ricci flat; see
[
DZ15
]
. A noncollapsed ancient solution with a smooth asymptotic soliton is clearly closed if it has positively pinched Ricci curvature. For the general case, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be a noncollapsed ancient Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each compact time interval. Suppose

\Ric\geq\varepsilon Rg\quad\text{ on }\quad M\times(-\infty,0]

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then either

1.

every tangent flow at infinity is a Ricci-flat cone, or

2.

the ancient Ricci flow is compact.

Finally, in view of the dichotomy in Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
, we propose the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
(M,g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be a noncollapsed ancient Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each compact time interval. Suppose
\Ric_{g_{t}}\geq 0
on
M\times(-\infty,0]
and every tangent flow at infinity is a Ricci flat cone. Then
g_{t}
is a static Ricci flat metric with positive
\AVR
.

This paper is organized as follows. In §2, we introduce some basic notions and results in the literature, while omitting some lengthy definitions in
[
Bam23
]
. In §3, we prove Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
, and Theorem
1.3
. In §4, we prove Theorem
1.4

3.1
A scalar curvature lower bound

In this subsection, we prove Theorem

. Since our method is not completely the same as
[

, we summarize the idea as follows. Let us fix a large ball
B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},D)
. By Theorem
2.8
, since the soliton is non-Ricci-flat, there is a lower bound
\delta_{0}>0
of
R
on
B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},D)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}
. It is known that
\nabla f_{0}
is complete, and that along the integral curve of
\nabla f_{0}
, the scalar curvature is always increasing due to the Ricci nonnegative assumption. Now, for any
x\in\mathcal{R}_{X}\setminus B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},D)
, we consider the integral curve of
-\nabla f_{0}
starting from
x
, there are two possibilities:

•

This integral curve intersects
\mathcal{R}_{X}\setminus B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},D)
at some time. This then means that
R(x)
is larger than
\delta_{0}
;

•

This integral curve never intersect
\mathcal{R}_{X}\setminus B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},D)
. This means the integral curve approaches a critical point of
\nabla f_{0}
. But (
2.6
) shows that a critical point in
\mathcal{R}_{X}\setminus B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},D)
is where the scalar curvature is large, so
R(x)
would be even larger.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
x_{0}
be a center of the soliton and let
D>0
be a constant to be determined. By Theorem
2.6
and Theorem
2.8
, since the soliton is non-Ricci-flat, we can find a constant
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(D)>0
depending on the soliton also, such that

\displaystyle R\geq\delta_{0}\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},D)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}.

By (
2.7
), we have

\langle\nabla f_{0},\nabla R\rangle=2\Ric(\nabla f_{0},\nabla f_{0})\geq 0,

hence along the integral curve of
\nabla f_{0},

R
is always increasing. Note that
\nabla f_{0}
is complete by Theorem
2.5
(a). When applying Theorem
2.6
, we shall fix
\varepsilon=1,
so that

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{5}d^{2}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},x)-C(n)\leq f_{0}(x)-W\leq\tfrac{1}{4}(d_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},x)+C(n))^{2}.

(3.1)

Henceforth the estimation constant
C
may vary from line to line, so long as it is a dimensional constant.

Fix an
x\in\mathcal{R}_{X}\backslash B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},D).
If
R(x)>1,
then there is nothing to prove, since we have already found a lower bound
1
for
R(x)
. So we assume
R(x)\leq 1
. Let
\sigma(s)
be an integral curve of
\nabla f_{0}
starting from
x
. Precisely, we define:

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\nabla f_{0}\quad\text{ for all }s\leq 0,

\displaystyle\sigma(0)

\displaystyle=x.

Now it is clear that

R(\sigma(s))\leq R(\sigma(0))=R(x)\leq 1\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\leq 0.

We claim that there exists an
s_{1}<0,
such that

Assume by contradiction that

d_{\mathfrak{g}}(\sigma(s),x_{0})>D\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\leq 0.

Then by (

) and (

), we have

We take

So

\varepsilon>0
we have,

|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},s-C)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}|\leq V(s)\leq\left|B_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(x_{0},\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}(s+C(\varepsilon))\right)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}\right|\quad\text{ for all }\ s>0,

where we omit the dependence on
n
in the constants above. Thus, it is clear that

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to+\infty}\frac{|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},s-C)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}|}{s^{n}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\liminf_{s\to+\infty}\frac{V(s)}{s^{n}}\leq\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{V(s)}{s^{n}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\limsup_{s\to\infty}\frac{\left|B_{\mathfrak{g}}\left(x_{0},\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}(s+C(\varepsilon))\right)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}\right|}{s^{n}}.

This immediately implies that

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to+\infty}\frac{|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},s)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}|}{s^{n}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\liminf_{s\to+\infty}\frac{V(s)}{s^{n}}\leq\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{V(s)}{s^{n}}

\displaystyle\ \leq(1+\varepsilon)^{\frac{n}{2}}\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},s)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}|}{s^{n}}.

In like manner, we also have

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to+\infty}\frac{V(s)}{s^{n}}\leq

\displaystyle\ (1+\varepsilon)^{-n/2}\liminf_{s\to+\infty}\frac{|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},s)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}|}{s^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},s)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}|}{s^{n}}\leq\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{V(s)}{s^{n}}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, taking
\varepsilon\to 0
, the proof is completed.
∎

Theorem
1.3
now follows from the corollary below.

Corollary 3.3
.

For a metric soliton described above, the
\AVR=\lim_{A\to+\infty}\frac{|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},A)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}|}{\omega_{n}A^{n}}
exists and

\lim_{s\to+\infty}P(s)=\omega_{n}\AVR\in[0,C(n)e^{W}],

where
W\leq 0
is the soliton entropy. In particular, the
\AVR
is bounded from above by a constant depending only on
n
.

Proof.

As we have seen in the proof of Theorem
1.2
,
P^{\prime}(s)\leq 0
for all
s\geq s_{0}
large enough, which means
\lim_{s\to+\infty}P(s)
exists. Then by (
3.2
) and Lemma
3.2
,

\lim_{s\to+\infty}P(s)=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\tfrac{V(s)}{s^{n}}=\omega_{n}\AVR.

The upper bound of the
\AVR
is a direct consequence of
[
CMZ24
, Corollary 1.3]
.
∎

Now we show a characterization of when the
\AVR
is positive; see also
[
CLY12
]
.

Corollary 3.4
.

For a metric soliton described above we have that

\AVR>0\;\text{if and only if there exists}\;\;s_{0}>0,\;\text{such that}\int_{s_{0}}^{+\infty}\tfrac{\chi(t)}{tV(t)}dt<+\infty

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, for
s\geq s_{0}
, where
s_{0}
is a dimensional constant, we have

P(s)=P(s_{0})\exp\left(-\int_{s_{0}}^{s}t^{-1}(2t^{2}-4n-8)\tfrac{Q(t)}{1-4Q(t)}dt\right).

(3.5)

By (
3.4
), for all
s\geq s_{0}
,

\tfrac{3}{2}\int_{s_{0}}^{s}tQ(t)dt\leq\int_{s_{0}}^{s}t^{-1}(2t^{2}-4n-8)\tfrac{Q(t)}{1-4Q(t)}dt\leq 2\int_{s_{0}}^{s}tQ(t)dt.

So (
3.5
) becomes

P(s_{0})e^{-2\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\tfrac{\chi(t)}{tV(t)}dt}\leq P(s)\leq P(s_{0})e^{-\frac{3}{2}\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\tfrac{\chi(t)}{tV(t)}dt}.

Then by Corollary
3.3
we have finished the proof.
∎

4
Ancient Ricci flow with nonnegative Ricci curvature

In this section and the next, we shall consider noncollapsed ancient solutions. Since one side of the dichotomies in Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
is that every tangent flow at infinity is a Ricci flat cone, we need only to focus on the case where
one
tangent flow at infinity is non-Ricci-flat in view of Theorem
2.5
(e). Let us recall our setting.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each compact time interval. Furthermore, suppose for some
y_{0}\in M
and
Y<+\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{y_{0},0}(\tau)\geq-Y\quad\text{ for all }\quad\tau>0.

(4.1)

Let
\lambda_{i}\nearrow+\infty
be a sequence of scales, and define

\displaystyle g^{i}_{t}:=\lambda_{i}^{-1}g_{\lambda_{i}t}.

Then, there is a metric soliton
(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)})
, called a
tangent flow at infinity
, and a correspondence
\mathfrak{C}
, such that, after passing to a subsequence,

\displaystyle\left((M,g^{i}_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]},(\nu^{i}_{y_{0},0;t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}\right)\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{\mathbb{F},\mathfrak{C}}\big(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)}\big).

(4.2)

Since the limit flow is a metric soliton, we remark the following important fact.

Lemma 4.1
.

After passing to a further subsequence, the convergence (
4.2
) is time-wise at any
t\in(-\infty,0)
.

Proof.

In view of the self-similarity of the metric soliton, the function

\displaystyle t\mapsto\iint_{\mathcal{X}_{t}\times\mathcal{X}_{t}}d_{t}(x,y)\,d\nu_{t}(x)d\nu_{t}(y)

varies only by scaling, and hence is continuous. Then by
[
Bam23
, Definition 4.7, Theorem 4.9]
, the metric soliton
\big(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)}\big)
is continuous at any
t\in(-\infty,0)
. Since the limit
\big(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)}\big)
is always continuous, it must be the unique representative of the limit in the statement of
[
Bam23
, Theorem 7.6]
, for any other equivalent representative shall not be future continuous. Thus, by
[
Bam23
, Theorem 7.6]
, after passing to a subsequence, (
4.2
) is time-wise at any continuous time of
\big(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)}\big)
, namely, at any
t\in(-\infty,0)
.

∎

Note that the soliton entropy of
(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)})
is equal to

\displaystyle W:=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{N}_{y_{0},0}(\tau).

Theorem
1.4
is reduced to the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient solution as described above, with bounded curvature within each compact time interval, and satisfying the noncollapsing condition (
4.1
). Assume furthermore that
\Ric\geq 0
on
M\times(-\infty,0]
. If a tangent flow at infinity obtained in (
4.2
) has non-Ricci-flat regular part, then

\displaystyle\AVR_{g_{t}}\equiv 0\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\leq 0.

We briefly summarize the ideas of the proof of Proposition
4.2
. First of all, we recall the fact that the
\AVR
is non-increasing for the ancient flow in question, so it remains to show that

\displaystyle\AVR_{g_{-\lambda_{i}}}\to 0\quad\text{ or }\quad\AVR_{g^{i}_{-1}}\to 0.

On the other hand, by Theorem
1.2
, the smooth part of the limit has zero
\AVR
. So it is sufficient to show that the volume of a large ball in
(M,g^{i}_{-1})
converges in some sense to the volume of a large ball on the regular part of the limit.

4.1
Monotonicity of the AVR

The following lemma is a simple consequence of Perelman’s distance distortion estimate.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(M,g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0])}
be the ancient solution in Proposition
4.2
. Then we have

\displaystyle\AVR_{g_{t_{1}}}\geq\AVR_{g_{t_{2}}}\quad\text{ for }\ \ t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq 0.

Proof.

Fix
t_{1}<t_{2}
. There is a positive number
K
such that

\displaystyle|\Ric_{g_{t}}|\leq K\quad\text{ on }\quad M\times[t_{1},t_{2}].

Let
x\in M
be a fixed point. By Perelman’s distance distortion estimate
[
Per02
, Lemma 8.3(b)]
, for any
A\gg 1
, we have

\displaystyle B_{g_{t_{2}}}(x,A)\subset B_{g_{t_{1}}}\Big(x,A+C(K)(t_{2}-t_{1})\Big).

On the other hand, since
R\geq 0
, the volume form is decreasing. Thus

\displaystyle|B_{g_{t_{2}}}(x,A)|_{g_{t_{2}}}\leq\left|B_{g_{t_{1}}}\Big(x,A+C(K)(t_{2}-t_{1})\Big)\right|_{g_{t_{1}}}\quad\text{ for all }\ A\gg 1.

It is clear that

\displaystyle\AVR_{g_{t_{2}}}\leq\AVR_{g_{t_{1}}}.

∎

4.2
The small volume of the singular part

We continue with the proof of Proposition
4.2
. Let
\{(M,g^{i}_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)})
be the sequence and the limit in (
4.2
) as described at the beginning of the section.
(y_{0},0)
is the fixed base point for each
(M,g^{i}_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
. We shall write the regular part of
(\mathcal{X},(\nu_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)})
as
(\mathcal{R},\mathfrak{g}_{t},f)_{t\in(-\infty,0)}
with

\displaystyle d\nu_{t}=(4\pi|t|)^{-n/2}e^{-f}d\mathfrak{g}_{t}.

Let
(X,d,\nu)
be the model of the metric soliton and
(\mathcal{R}_{X},\mathfrak{g},f_{0})
its regular part.

Let
z_{i}\in M
be a sequence of points such that for each
i
,
(z_{i},-1)
is an
H_{n}
-center of
(y_{0},0)
with respect to the scaled flow
g^{i}_{t}
(or, in other words,
(z_{i},-\lambda_{i})
is an
H_{n}
-center of
(y_{0},0)
with respect to the unscaled flow
g_{t}
). Our goal is to estimate

\displaystyle\left|B_{g^{i}_{-1}}(z_{i},A)\right|_{g^{i}_{-1}},\quad A\gg 1,

when
i
is large enough. To this end, we decompose this geodesic ball into two parts:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A):=B_{g^{i}_{-1}}(z_{i},A)\cap\{r_{\Rm}\geq r\},\quad\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A):=B_{g^{i}_{-1}}(z_{i},A)\cap\{r_{\Rm}<r\},\quad r>0,\ \ A<+\infty.

Obviously,

\displaystyle B_{g^{i}_{-1}}(z_{i},A)=\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A)\sqcup\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A).

In this subsection and the next, we shall estimate the volumes of
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A)
and
\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A)
, respectively.

The singular part
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A)
is estimated by Bamler’s theory.

Lemma 4.4
.

If

\displaystyle\quad A\geq 1,\quad r\leq\overline{r}(A,Y),

then there is an
\alpha=\alpha(Y)>0
, such that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A)\right|_{g^{i}_{-1}}\leq C(A,Y)r^{\alpha}\quad\text{ for all }\quad i.

Here
Y
is the entropy bound in (
4.1
).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.10
, if
\varepsilon=\overline{\varepsilon}(Y)\in(0,1)
and
r<\varepsilon
, then for any
r^{\prime}\geq r/\varepsilon
, the points in
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A)
are neither weakly
(n-1,\varepsilon,r^{\prime})
-split, nor
(\varepsilon,r^{\prime})
-static and weakly
(n-3,\varepsilon,r^{\prime})
-split at the same time. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A)\subset\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r/\varepsilon,1}^{\varepsilon,n-2}(g^{i}),

where
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r/\varepsilon,1}^{\varepsilon,n-2}(g^{i})
is the quantitative strata with respect to the flow
g^{i}_{t}
in Definition
2.9
.

By the nonnegative Ricci curvature assumption and the Bishop-Gromov comparison theorem, we can find points
\{y_{j}^{(i)}\}_{j=1}^{N^{(i)}}\subset\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A)
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A)\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N^{(i)}}B_{g^{i}_{-1}}\left(y_{j}^{(i)},1/\varepsilon\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad N^{(i)}\leq\overline{N}(A,\varepsilon).

(4.3)

The monotonicity of the
W_{1}
-Wasserstein distance
[
Bam20a
, Lemma 2.7]
implies that
B_{g^{i}_{-1}}\left(y_{j}^{(i)},1/\varepsilon\right)\subset P^{*}\left(y_{j}^{(i)},-1;1/\varepsilon\right)
, and thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A)\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N^{(i)}}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r/\varepsilon,1}^{\varepsilon,n-2}(g^{i})\cap P^{*}\left(y_{j}^{(i)},-1;1/\varepsilon\right)\right).

(4.4)

Now, applying Proposition
2.11
to
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r/\varepsilon,1}^{\varepsilon,n-2}(g^{i})\cap P^{*}\left(y_{j}^{(i)},-1;1/\varepsilon\right)
with
\varepsilon\to\varepsilon
,
r\to 1/\varepsilon
,
\sigma\to r
, we can find
\{y^{(i)}_{j,k}\}_{k=1}^{N^{(i)}_{j}}
, such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{r/\varepsilon,1}^{\varepsilon,n-2}(g^{i})\cap P^{*}\left(y_{j}^{(i)},-1;1/\varepsilon\right)\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N^{(i)}_{j}}P^{*}\left(y_{j,k}^{(i)},-1;r/\varepsilon\right),\quad N^{(i)}_{j}\leq C(Y,\varepsilon)r^{-n+2-\varepsilon}.

(4.5)

Since, by
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 9.8]
,

\displaystyle\left|P^{*}\left(y_{j,k}^{(i)},-1;r/\varepsilon\right)\bigcap M\times\{-1\}\right|_{g^{i}_{-1}}\leq C(Y)r^{n}\varepsilon^{-n},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,N^{(i)}_{j},\ \ j=1,2,\ldots,N^{(i)},

(4.6)

we have, combining (
4.3
)(
4.4
)(
4.5
)(
4.6
),

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{S}^{(i)}(r,A)\right|_{g^{i}_{-1}}\leq C(Y,A,\varepsilon)r^{2-\varepsilon}.

Since
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(Y)
, we obtain the lemma by letting
\alpha=2-\varepsilon>0
.

∎

4.3
The volume estimate of the regular part

In this subsection, we proceed to estimate the size of
\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A)
via the local smooth convergence on
\mathcal{R}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
A\geq\underline{A}(n)
,
r\in(0,1)
, then passing to a subsequence, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A)\right|_{g^{i}_{-1}}\leq 2\left|B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},2A)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}\right|\quad\text{ for all }\ i\ \text{ large enough}.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\psi_{i}:\mathcal{R}\supset U_{i}\to V_{i}\subset M\times(-\infty,0]

(4.7)

be the sets and diffeomorphism defined in Theorem
2.3
. By the smooth convergence on
\mathcal{R}
, it is obviously sufficient to show that

\displaystyle M\times\{-1\}\supset\psi_{i}\left(U_{i}\cap B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},2A)\right)\supset\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A)

for all
i
large enough, depending on
r
and
A
.

For each
i
, we find a finite sequence of points
\left\{x^{(i)}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{N_{i}}
in
\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A)\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N_{i}}B_{g^{i}_{-1}}\left(x_{k}^{(i)},\tfrac{r}{3}\right),\qquad N_{i}\leq\overline{N}(A,r),

by the Bishop-Gromov comparison theorem, where
\overline{N}(A,r)
is independent of
i
. By passing to a (not relabeled) subsequence, we may assume that
N_{i}\equiv N\leq\overline{N}(A,r)
for all
i
. Next, we show that, for each
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
, the sequence
\left\{x_{k}^{(i)}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges, after passing to a subsequence, to some point in
\mathcal{R}_{-1}
.

Claim.

For each
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
, there is a point
\tilde{x}_{k}\in B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},A+C(n))\subset\mathcal{R}_{X}=\mathcal{R}_{-1}
, such that, after passing to a subsequence,

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{-1}\left(x^{(i)}_{k},-1\right)\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}\tilde{x}_{k}.

(4.8)

Furthermore,
P\left(\tilde{x}_{k};\tfrac{1}{2}r\right)
is unscathed and

\displaystyle\psi_{i}\left(P\left(\tilde{x}_{k};\tfrac{1}{2}r\right)\right)\supset P\left(x^{(i)}_{k},-1;\tfrac{1}{3}r\right)\quad\text{for $i$ large enough}.

(4.9)

Proof of the claim.

For the sake of notational simplicity, we shall denote
x^{(i)}_{k}
by
x^{(i)}
and
\tilde{x}_{k}
by
\tilde{x}
, and suppress the subindex. Let
0<r^{\prime}\ll r
be a small number to be fixed, and set
t^{\prime}=-1-r^{\prime 2}.
Since
x^{(i)}\in B_{g^{i}_{-1}}(z_{i},A)
, we have, by
[
Bam20a
, Lemma 3.2, Definition 3.10]

\displaystyle d^{g^{i}_{-1}}_{W_{1}}\left(\delta_{x^{(i)}},\nu^{i}_{y_{0},0\,;\,-1}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\ d^{g^{i}_{-1}}_{W_{1}}\left(\delta_{x^{(i)}},\delta_{z_{i}}\right)+d^{g^{i}_{-1}}_{W_{1}}\left(\delta_{z_{i}},\nu^{i}_{y_{0},0\,;\,-1}\right)\leq A+\sqrt{2H_{n}}.

Thus, by Theorem
2.2
, we may find a conjugate heat flow
(\tilde{\nu}_{t})_{t<-1}
on the metric soliton
(\mathcal{X},\nu_{t})
, such that

\left(\nu^{i}_{x^{(i)},-1\,;\,t}\right)_{t\leq-1}\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{\mathfrak{C}}(\tilde{\nu}_{t})_{t<-1},\qquad\lim_{t\nearrow-1}\Var(\tilde{\nu}_{t})=0,

where
\mathfrak{C}
is the correspondence in (
4.2
). Since the limit flow pair is also
H_{n}
-concentrated
[
Bam23
, Theorem 7.4]
, by
[
Bam23
, Definition 2.6, Proposition 3.23]
, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{X}_{t}\times\mathcal{X}_{t}}d_{t}^{2}(x,y)\,d\tilde{\nu}_{t}(x)d\tilde{\nu}_{t}(y)=\Var(\tilde{\nu}_{t})\leq H_{n}|t+1|\quad\text{ for all }\quad t<-1

In particular, we can find a
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{t^{\prime}}
, such that

\displaystyle\Var(\delta_{y^{\prime}},\tilde{\nu}_{t^{\prime}})=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t^{\prime}}}d_{t^{\prime}}^{2}(x,y^{\prime})\,d\tilde{\nu}_{t^{\prime}}(x)\leq\iint_{\mathcal{X}_{t^{\prime}}\times\mathcal{X}_{t^{\prime}}}d_{t^{\prime}}^{2}(x,y)\,d\tilde{\nu}_{t^{\prime}}(x)d\tilde{\nu}_{t^{\prime}}(y)\leq H_{n}|t^{\prime}+1|.

Since
\mathcal{R}_{t^{\prime}}
is dense in
\mathcal{X}_{t^{\prime}}
, we may, without loss of generality, assume
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{t^{\prime}}
, this causes at most an additional dimensional factor. We then have, by
[
Bam23
, Lemma 2.8]
and
t^{\prime}=1-r^{\prime 2}
,

d_{W_{1}}^{\mathcal{X}_{t^{\prime}}}(\delta_{y^{\prime}},\tilde{\nu}_{t^{\prime}})\leq\sqrt{\Var(\delta_{y^{\prime}},\tilde{\nu}_{t^{\prime}})}\leq C(n)r^{\prime}.

(4.10)

Since
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{t^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{R}
, for all
i
large enough, we may set

(y^{\prime}_{i},t^{\prime}):=\psi_{i}(y^{\prime})\in M\times\{t^{\prime}\},

according to Theorem
2.3
, where
\psi_{i}
is the diffeomorphism in (
4.7
).

Next, we estimate the space-time distance between
\left(x^{(i)},-1\right)
and
(y^{\prime}_{i},t^{\prime}).
Since by Lemma
4.1
, the convergence (
4.2
) is time-wise at
t^{\prime}
, we have (see
[
Bam23
, Definition 6.7]
)

d_{W_{1}}^{Z_{t^{\prime}}}\left((\varphi_{t^{\prime}}^{i})_{*}\nu_{x^{(i)},-1;t^{\prime}}^{i},(\varphi_{t^{\prime}})_{*}\tilde{\nu}_{t^{\prime}}\right)\to 0.

(4.11)

\mathfrak{C}=(Z_{t},\varphi^{i}_{t},\varphi_{t})
is the correspondence,
\varphi_{t}^{i}:(M,g^{i}_{t})\to(Z_{t},d_{t}^{Z})
and
\varphi_{t}:(\mathcal{X}_{t},d_{t})\to(Z_{t},d^{Z}_{t})
are isometric embeddings.

Putting (
4.10
) into
\mathfrak{C}
yields

d_{W_{1}}^{Z_{t^{\prime}}}\left((\varphi_{t^{\prime}})_{*}\delta_{y^{\prime}},(\varphi_{t^{\prime}})_{*}\tilde{\nu}_{t^{\prime}}\right)\leq C(n)r^{\prime}.

(4.12)

The definition of
(y_{i}^{\prime},t^{\prime})
implies that (see Theorem
2.3
(c))

d_{W_{1}}^{Z_{t^{\prime}}}\left((\varphi_{t^{\prime}}^{i})_{*}\delta_{y_{i}^{\prime}},(\varphi_{t^{\prime}})_{*}\delta_{y^{\prime}}\right)\to 0.

(4.13)

By (
4.11
)-(
4.13
) and the triangle inequality, we have

d_{W_{1}}^{Z_{t^{\prime}}}\left((\varphi_{t^{\prime}}^{i})_{*}\nu_{x^{(i)},-1;t^{\prime}}^{i},(\varphi_{t^{\prime}}^{i})_{*}\delta_{y_{i}^{\prime}}\right)\leq C(n)r^{\prime}
for all
i
large enough. This is then equivalent to

d_{W_{1}}^{g^{i}_{t^{\prime}}}(\nu^{i}_{x^{(i)},-1;t^{\prime}},\delta_{y_{i}^{\prime}})\leq C(n)r^{\prime}\quad\text{ for all }\ i\ \text{ large enough.}

(4.14)

By the assumption
x^{(i)}\in\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A)
, we have
r_{\Rm}(x^{(i)},-1)\geq r
. While
r^{\prime}\ll r
, we have
|\Ric|\leq r^{\prime-2}
on

. Thus, by
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 9.5]
,

d_{W_{1}}^{g^{i}_{t^{\prime}}}(\nu^{i}_{x^{(i)},-1;t^{\prime}},\delta_{x^{(i)}})\leq C(n)r^{\prime}
.
In combination with (
4.14
), we have

d_{g^{i}_{-1}}\left(x^{(i)},y_{i}^{\prime}\right)\leq d_{g^{i}_{t^{\prime}}}\left(x^{(i)},y_{i}^{\prime}\right)=d_{W_{1}}^{g^{i}_{t^{\prime}}}(\delta_{x^{(i)}},\delta_{y_{i}^{\prime}})\leq C(n)r^{\prime},

where the first inequality is due to the nonnegative assumption of the Ricci curvature. Thus, we have
(y_{i}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in P\left(x^{(i)},-1;C(n)r^{\prime}\right).

Now we fix
r^{\prime}=r^{\prime}(r)\ll r
to ensure that, via the standard distance distortion estimate,

\displaystyle r_{\Rm}(y_{i}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\geq\tfrac{99r}{100}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left(x^{(i)},-1\right)\in P\left(y_{i}^{\prime},t^{\prime};\tfrac{1}{100}r\right).

By Theorem
2.3
, the parabolic cube
P\left(y^{\prime};\tfrac{98r}{100}\right)\Subset\mathcal{R}

. By the smooth convergence on

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{-1}\left(x^{(i)},-1\right)\in P\left(y^{\prime};\tfrac{98r}{100}\right)\quad\text{ for all }\ i\ \text{ large enough},

and, after passing to a sequence,

\displaystyle\psi^{-1}_{i}\left(x^{(i)},-1\right)\to\tilde{x}\in P\left(y^{\prime};\tfrac{2r}{100}\right)\cap\mathcal{R}_{-1}.

If we take
r^{\prime}
to be even smaller if necessary (depending only on

and

), then the standard distance distortion estimate implies that

P\left(\tilde{x};\tfrac{1}{2}r\right)\subset P\left(y^{\prime};\tfrac{98r}{100}\right),

and (
4.8
)(
4.9
) follow easily by the smooth convergence on
P\left(\tilde{x};\tfrac{1}{2}r\right)\Subset\mathcal{R}_{-1}
.

To see
\tilde{x}\in B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},A+C(n))
, we compute

\displaystyle d_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},\tilde{x})\leq

\displaystyle\ d^{Z}_{-1}\left(\varphi_{-1}(\tilde{x}),\varphi^{i}_{-1}\left(x^{(i)}\right)\right)+d_{g^{i}_{-1}}\left(x^{(i)},z_{i}\right)+d^{Z}_{-1}\left(\varphi_{-1}(x_{0}),\varphi^{i}_{-1}\left(z_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ d^{Z}_{-1}\left(\varphi_{-1}(\tilde{x}),\varphi^{i}_{-1}\left(x^{(i)}\right)\right)+d_{g^{i}_{-1}}\left(x^{(i)},z_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\ +d^{Z_{-1}}_{W_{1}}\left((\varphi_{-1})_{*}(\nu_{-1}),(\varphi^{i}_{-1})_{*}\left(\nu^{i}_{y_{0},0\,;\,-1}\right)\right)+d^{\mathfrak{g}}_{W_{1}}\left(\delta_{x_{0}},\nu_{-1}\right)+d^{g^{i}_{-1}}_{W_{1}}\left(\delta_{z_{i}},\nu^{i}_{y_{0},0\,;\,-1}\right).

Since the convergence (
4.2
) is time-wise at
t=-1
by Lemma
4.1
, taking a limit at the right-hand-side of the above inequality, we conclude from Theorem
2.3
(c) that

d_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},\tilde{x})\leq C(n)+A.

∎

We continue with the proof of the lemma. Applying the claim successively to
\{x^{(i)}_{k}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,N
, we find
\tilde{x}_{k}\in B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},A+C(n))\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,N
, such that after passing to subsequences for finitely many times, (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) both hold. Thus, if
r<1
and
A\geq\underline{A}(n)
, we have

\displaystyle K:=\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}P\left(\tilde{x}_{k};\tfrac{1}{2}r\right)\right)\cap\mathcal{R}_{-1}\Subset B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},A+1+C(n))\cap\mathcal{R}_{X}\subset B_{\mathfrak{g}}(x_{0},2A)\cap\mathcal{R}_{X},

for all
i
large enough. Since

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(K)\supset\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}P\left(x^{(i)}_{k},-1;\tfrac{1}{3}r\right)\right)\cap M\times\{-1\}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}B_{g^{i}_{-1}}\left(x^{(i)}_{k},\tfrac{1}{3}r\right)\supset\mathcal{R}^{(i)}(r,A),

the conclusion follows from the smooth convergence on
K\Subset\mathcal{R}_{-1}
.
∎

4.4
Completion of the proof

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Since the tangent flow at infinity is non-Ricci-flat, we may assume that

\displaystyle R_{\mathfrak{g}}\geq\delta>0\quad\text{ on }\quad\mathcal{R}_{X}=\mathcal{R}_{-1}.

Then, by Theorem
1.2
, Lemma
4.4
, and Lemma
4.5
, if we fix
A\geq\underline{A}(n)
and
r\leq\overline{r}(A,Y)
and pass to a subsequence, we have

\displaystyle\AVR_{g^{i}_{-1}}\leq\frac{\left|B_{g^{i}_{-1}}(z_{i},A)\right|}{\omega_{n}A^{n}}\leq C(n)\frac{(2A)^{n-2\delta}}{A^{n}}+\frac{C(A,Y)r^{\alpha}}{A^{n}}\leq C(n)A^{-2\delta}+C(A,Y)r^{\alpha},

for all
i
large enough; here we have applied the monotonicity of the volume ratio. Due to Lemma
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}\AVR_{g_{t}}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\AVR_{g^{i}_{-1}}\leq C(n)A^{-2\delta}+C(A,Y)r^{\alpha},

for all
A\geq\underline{A}(n)
and
r\leq\overline{r}(A,Y)
. Taking
r\to 0
first and then
A\to\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}\AVR_{g_{t}}=0,

and hence, by Lemma
4.3
,

\displaystyle\AVR_{g_{t}}\equiv 0\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in(-\infty,0].

∎

5
Ancient Ricci flow with positively pinched Ricci curvature

We now consider an ancient solution described at the beginning of §4. As we have already explained, Theorem
1.5
can be reduced to the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient solution as in the statement of Proposition
4.2
. Assume that there is some
\varepsilon>0
, such that

\displaystyle\Ric_{g_{t}}\geq\varepsilon R_{t}g_{t}\quad\text{ on }\quad M\times(-\infty,0].

If a tangent flow at infinity obtained in (
4.2
) has non-Ricci-flat regular part, then
M
is a closed manifold.

The key idea of this proof is to improve the lower bound of the scalar curvature by convolution of heat kernel. Note that
R
is a super-solution of the heat equation, so if
\int_{M}R\,d\nu_{x,t\,;\,t^{\prime}}\geq\delta
, we would obtain a lower bound of
R
near
(x,t)
. This local lower bound, by a covering argument, can be extended to a global lower bound, which compels the manifold to be compact by the pinching condition of the Ricci curvature.

5.1
Local lower bound for the scalar curvature

We apply Bamler’s gradient estimate for the heat equation to obtain a local lower bound for the scalar curvature. The idea of the following lemma was also applied in
[
CMZ24
, Proposition 5.2]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in[-r^{2},0]}
be a Ricci flow with bounded curvature. Suppose furthermore that
R_{g_{t}}\geq 0
on
M\times[-r^{2},0]
. If for some
x\in M
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}R_{g_{-r^{2}}}\,d\nu_{x,0\,;\,-r^{2}}\geq\delta r^{-2}>0,

then we have

\displaystyle R_{g_{0}}\geq n\sqrt{2}\Phi\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Phi^{-1}(\delta/n)-A\right)r^{-2}\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{g_{0}}(x,Ar).

where

\Phi(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{s}(4\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-r^{2}/4}dr,

is defined in
[
Bam20a
, Section 4]
.

Proof.

By parabolic scaling, we may assume
r=1
. Consider the solution to the heat equation:

\displaystyle u:M\times[-1,0]\to

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{R},

\displaystyle(y,s)\mapsto

\displaystyle\ \int_{M}R_{g_{-1}}\,d\nu_{y,s\,;\,-1}.

Since
u(\cdot,-1)=R_{g_{-1}}
, and the scalar curvature is a super-solution of the heat equation

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)R_{g_{t}}=2\left|\Ric_{g_{t}}\right|^{2}\geq 0,

we have

\displaystyle R_{g_{s}}\geq u(\cdot,s)\quad\text{ on }\quad M\times[-1,0].

By
[
Bam20a
, Proposition 5.13]
,

\displaystyle u(y,s)\in(0,n)\quad\text{ for all }\ (y,s)\in M\times[-1/2,0].

Applying
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 4.1]
to
\frac{1}{n}u(\cdot,0)
, we have that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{n}u(\cdot,0)\right)
is
1
-Lipschitz. The conclusion follows immediately.

∎

5.2
Global lower bound for the scalar curvature

Let us now consider the ancient solution in the statement of Proposition
5.1
. Let
(\mathcal{X},\nu_{t})
be the tangent flow obtained in (
4.2
). Let
(\mathcal{R}_{X},\mathfrak{g},f_{0})
be the regular part of the model. In particular
(\mathcal{R}_{-1},\mathfrak{g}_{-1})=(\mathcal{R}_{X},\mathfrak{g})
. By the assumption of Proposition
5.1
and Theorem
1.1
, we assume

\displaystyle R_{\mathfrak{g}}\geq\delta>0\quad\text{ on }\quad\mathcal{R}_{X}=\mathcal{R}_{-1}.

Pick
z_{i}\in M
, such that
(z_{i},-1/2)
is an
H_{n}
-center of
(y_{0},0)
with respect to the rescaled flow
g^{i}_{t}
(or, in other words,
(z_{i},-\lambda_{i}/2)
is an
H_{n}
-center with respect to the unscaled flow
g_{t}
).

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
A<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle R_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}\geq\delta/4\qquad\text{on}\qquad B_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}(z_{i},A)

for all
i
large enough.

Proof.

First of all, we show that the integral of the scalar curvature is not small if
i
is large enough.

Claim.

For any sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with
x_{i}\in B_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}(z_{i},A)
, we have

\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{M}R_{g{{}^{i}_{-1}}}d\nu_{x_{i},-1/2;-1}^{i}\geq\delta.

Proof of the claim.

For any
x_{i}\in B_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}(z_{i},A)
, we have, by
[
Bam20a
, Definition 3.10]

d_{W_{1}}^{g^{i}_{-1/2}}(\delta_{x_{i}},\nu^{i}_{y_{0},0;-1/2})\leq d_{W_{1}}^{g^{i}_{-1/2}}(\delta_{x_{i}},\delta_{z_{i}})+d_{W_{1}}^{g^{i}_{-1/2}}(\delta_{z_{i}},\nu^{i}_{y_{0},0;-1/2})\leq A+\sqrt{\tfrac{H_{n}}{2}}.

Thus for any subsequence of
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, according to Theorem
2.2
, we may find a conjugate heat flow
(\tilde{\nu}_{t})_{t<-1/2}
on
\mathcal{X}
, such that, after passing to a further subsequence,

\displaystyle\left(\nu^{i}_{x_{i},-1/2\,;\,t}\right)_{t\leq-1/2}\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{\mathfrak{C}}(\tilde{\nu}_{t})_{t<-1/2}.

By Theorem
2.3
(d), we have

\displaystyle\nu^{i}_{x_{i},-1/2;-1}\xrightarrow[i\to\infty]{C_{\loc}^{\infty}}\tilde{\nu}_{-1}\quad\text{ on }\quad\mathcal{R}_{-1},

where the local smooth convergence is also understood in terms of Theorem
2.3
(d). Since
R_{\mathfrak{g}}\geq\delta
on
\mathcal{R}_{-1}
and
\tilde{\nu}_{-1}(\mathcal{S}_{-1})=0
, by Fatou’s lemma we have

\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{M}R_{g{{}^{i}_{-1}}}d\nu_{x_{i},-1/2;-1}^{i}\geq\int_{\mathcal{R}_{-1}}R_{\mathfrak{g}}\,d\tilde{\nu}_{-1}\geq\delta.

The conclusion then follows.
∎

Let us fix
r=r(\delta)>0
, such that

\displaystyle 2n\sqrt{2}\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{\delta}{4n}\right)-\sqrt{2}r\right)>\frac{\delta}{4}.

(5.1)

Then, for each
i
, we pick a finite sequence

\left\{x_{k}^{(i)}\right\}_{k=1}^{N_{i}}
in
B^{i}_{-1/2}(z_{i},A)
, such that

\displaystyle B_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}(z_{i},A)\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N_{i}}B_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}\left(x_{k}^{(i)},r\right),\qquad N_{i}\leq\overline{N}(A,r),

by the Bishop-Gromov volume comparison, where
\overline{N}
is independent of
i
. By passing to a subsequence, we may assume
N_{i}\equiv N
.

Applying the claim to the sequence
\left\{x_{k}^{(i)}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,N
, we obtain that whenever
i
is large enough

\displaystyle\int_{M}R_{g{{}^{i}_{-1}}}d\nu_{x^{(i)}_{k},-1/2;-1}^{i}\geq\delta/2,\quad k=1,2,\ldots,N.

Applying Lemma
5.2
with
r^{2}\to 1/2
,
\delta\to\delta/4
,
A\to\sqrt{2}r
, and in view of the choice of
r
(
5.1
), we have that, when
i
is large enough

\displaystyle R_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}\geq\frac{\delta}{4}\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}\left(x_{k}^{(i)},r\right)\quad\text{ for all }\quad k=1,2,\ldots,N.

The conclusion follows immediately.
∎

5.3
Completion of the proof

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

By Lemma
5.3
, for any
A<\infty
, we have
\Ric_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}\geq\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon\delta
on
B_{g^{i}_{-1/2}}(z_{i},A)
for all

∎
