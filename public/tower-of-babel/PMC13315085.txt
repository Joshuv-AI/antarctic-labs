Multifocal lymphangioma involving the liver, spleen, and retroperitoneum: A rare adult presentation
Authors: Mohsen Bagheri, Pegah Moharrami Yeganeh, Nasir Fakhar, Niloofar Ayoobi Yazdi, Faezeh Salahshour
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hepatic lymphangioma is an extremely rare benign tumor of the lymphatic system, especially in adults. It often presents as a cystic lesion and can mimic other hepatic or abdominal cystic diseases such as hydatid cysts or cystadenomas. Due to its rarity and nonspecific imaging features, diagnosis is often challenging and confirmed only by histopathology. We report a 40-year-old male who presented with several months of abdominal discomfort and mild distension. Physical examination revealed no tenderness, and laboratory results were within normal limits. Abdominal ultrasound showed multiple cystic lesions throughout the liver. Further CT and MRI imaging demonstrated multiple well-defined, hypodense cystic lesions in the liver, spleen, and retroperitoneal space. The largest lesion was located adjacent to the psoas muscle and anterior to the lumbar spine, showing an insinuating appearance characteristic of lymphangioma. Surgical excision and histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of hepatic and retroperitoneal lymphangioma. Intrahepatic lymphangioma is a rare benign lesion that can present with multifocal abdominal involvement. Awareness of this condition is important to avoid misdiagnosis and unnecessary extensive surgery. Complete surgical excision remains the definitive treatment, providing both diagnostic confirmation and excellent prognosis.

Introduction

Lymphangioma is a rare benign tumor arising from congenital malformations of the lymphatic system. Although commonly observed in pediatric populations and frequently located in the neck or axilla, intra-abdominal lymphangiomas are uncommon, and hepatic involvement is exceptionally rare, accounting for less than 5% of all lymphangiomas [1]. Hepatic lymphangiomas may present as solitary or multicystic lesions and are typically discovered incidentally during imaging for unrelated conditions, owing to their often-asymptomatic nature [2].

Histologically, these tumors are characterized by dilated lymphatic channels lined by endothelial cells, embedded within fibrous or adipose stroma. While typically benign, large hepatic lymphangiomas can exert mass effects, leading to abdominal discomfort, palpable mass, or complications such as hemorrhage, rupture, or infection [3]. Due to their radiological resemblance to other cystic hepatic lesions—including cystadenomas, echinococcal cysts, or biliary cysts—the preoperative diagnosis is frequently challenging and often requires histopathological confirmation.

The rarity of hepatic lymphangioma in adults and the absence of pathognomonic clinical features contribute to diagnostic uncertainty. Therefore, reporting such cases plays an essential role in expanding clinical awareness and guiding appropriate surgical and histological management.

In this case report, we present an unusual instance of multiple hepatics, splenic and abdominal lymphangioma masses in an adult patient, detailing the diagnostic process, surgical intervention, and pathological findings, followed by a brief review of the existing literature.

Case presentation

A 40-year-old male, previously healthy, presented with complaints of abdominal discomfort and pain that had persisted for several months. He denied experiencing jaundice or any significant weight loss. On physical examination, the abdomen was soft without tenderness; however, mild distension was noted in the left lower quadrant (LLQ). Laboratory investigations, including liver function tests, complete blood count, and inflammatory markers, were within normal limits.

Initial abdominal ultrasonography revealed multiple well-defined, round, cyst-like lesions distributed throughout the liver (Fig. 1). Due to the atypical imaging findings and the pressuring effect of the mass on multiple organs, the patient underwent surgical biopsy and excision of representative lesions. Histopathological examination of the resected hepatic lesion revealed features consistent with a hepatic lymphangioma—a benign vascular malformation composed of dilated lymphatic vessels.

This diagnosis is particularly rare, as lymphangiomas typically occur in superficial soft tissues of the neck and axilla and rarely originate within solid organs like the liver. Intrahepatic lymphangiomas are exceptional and pose significant diagnostic challenges due to their rarity and nonspecific imaging features.

Discussion

In this report, we review a case of a 40-year-old man with multiple well-defined, round, cyst-like lesions distributed throughout the liver and abdominal cavity, eventually diagnosed with hepatic and intra-abdominal lymphangiomas. Lymphangiomas -also referred to as Lymphatic malformations [4]- are rare, benign growths that come from abnormal development of lymphatic vessels. They are most common in children and usually appear in the neck or armpit [1,5]. Finding a lymphangioma inside the liver is extremely rare, especially in adults. Most cases are incidental imaging findings [1].

Lymphangiomas are classified into 3 types: Simple (capillary)—small, thin-walled vessels. Cavernous—larger lymphatic spaces within connective tissue. Cystic—multiple fluid-filled cavities, also called cystic hygroma [2,4].

The cystic type is the most common form found in the liver. On ultrasound, CT, or MRI, these appear as thin-walled cystic lesions, which can be mistaken for liver cysts, hydatid cysts, or cystadenomas [4].

The exact cause of hepatic lymphangioma is not clear. Most cases are thought to be congenital, meaning they result from a problem during embryonic development when lymphatic channels fail to connect properly. In rare cases, it can also develop later in life due to trauma, infection, or surgery, which can block lymphatic flow [6].

The main treatment is complete surgical removal, which is both diagnostic and curative [7]. If the lesion cannot be fully removed, partial excision or sclerotherapy (injection of a hardening agent like OK-432) may be used to reduce symptoms [8]. After complete removal, recurrence is rare, and patients usually recover well [3].

Conclusions

Hepatic lymphangioma should be considered when multiple cyst-like lesions are found in the liver. Although rare, correct diagnosis is important to avoid unnecessary extensive surgery. Surgical excision remains the best treatment option and offers an excellent prognosis.
