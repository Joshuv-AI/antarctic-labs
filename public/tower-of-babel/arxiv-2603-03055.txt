Hasse-Witt invariants of Calabi-Yau varieties 1 1 1 August 10, 2026


Abstract
We define the Hasse-Witt invariant of Calabi-Yau varieties in two different ways. The first method is through Cartier operator and the second method is through the theory of Calabi-Yau modular forms developed by the third author. We conjecture that these two definitions are equivalent and provide many examples of Calabi-Yau varieties in support of this conjecture.

Introduction

Let
X
be an elliptic curve over a perfect field
{\mathfrak{k}}
of characteristic
p\not=2,3
and let
\alpha

1
-form on

C

1
-forms on

C(\alpha)={\rm HW}(X,\alpha)^{\frac{1}{p}}\alpha.

The algebra of (elliptic) modular forms for
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
gives us another method for computing the Hasse-Witt invariant. It is well-known that this algebra is generated by the Eisenstein series
E_{4}
and
E_{6}
. In particular, this implies that

(1)

E_{p-1}=A_{p}\left(\frac{1}{12}E_{4},\frac{-1}{216}E_{6}\right)

for some polynomial
A_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}[x,y]
of weighted degree

where
\deg(x)=4,\ \deg(y)=6
. It turns out that
p
does not appear in the denominators of the coefficients of
A_{p}
, we can reduce
A_{p}
modulo
p
. If we write the pair
(X,\alpha)
in the Weierstrass coordinates

(2)

{\sf X}_{t_{2},t_{3}}:y^{2}=4x^{3}-t_{2}x-t_{3},\ \alpha=\frac{dx}{y}

then the following equality in characteristic

{\rm HW(X,\alpha)}=A_{p}(t_{2},t_{3})

is a classical result, mainly attributed to P. Deligne, see
[
6

The first part of the above story was developed by N. Katz for arbitrary varieties. In recent years, there has been some interest in similar notions in the case of Calabi-Yau varieties e.g.
[
19

The third author has developed the theory of modular forms for Calabi-Yau varieties (CY modular forms) in many articles with the title ”Gauss-Manin connection in disguise”, see

and the reference therein. Much of this work has been motivated by the study of generating series of Gromov-Witten invariants, even though there is no well-known enumerative algebraic geometry for the CY modular forms of the present article.

The theory of CY modular forms generalizes the theory of quasi-modular forms

Cartier operator and Hasse-Witt invariant

In this section we review Cartier operator and Hasse-Witt invariant from
[
8
]
.
Let
X=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
be an affine scheme over a perfect field
{\mathfrak{k}}
of characteristic
p>0
and
\Omega_{A}^{1}
be the module of Kähler differentials of
A

, which is equipped with the
{\mathfrak{k}}

d:A\to\Omega^{1}_{A},a\to da.

Then the de Rham complex
(\Omega_{A}^{\bullet},d)

\Omega_{A}^{\bullet}
of
\Omega_{A}^{1}
over
A
together with the extended maps
d:\Omega^{\bullet}_{A}\to\Omega^{\bullet}_{A}
.
We let

Z^{i}_{A}=\{\alpha\in\Omega_{A}^{i}\mid d\alpha=0\},\ \ \ B^{i}_{A}=d\Omega_{A}^{i-1},H_{A}^{i}=Z^{i}_{A}/B^{i}_{A}.

Then one may define an
A
-algebra homomorphism:

\displaystyle\gamma:\Omega^{\bullet}_{A}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle H^{\bullet}_{A}

\displaystyle a_{1}da_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge da_{n}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle a_{1}^{p}a_{2}^{p-1}da_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge a_{n}^{p-1}da_{n}(\mathrm{mod}\ B_{A}^{\bullet}),

where the
A
-module structure on

comes from the Frobenius action on

, see
[
8
, Lemma 1.3.3]
. Now we consider
X
is a scheme over
{\mathfrak{k}}
and let
F
be the relative Frobenius
X\to X^{(p)}

\gamma:\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{(p)}}\to\bigoplus^{\infty}_{i=0}\mathcal{H}^{i}F_{*}\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}

which satisfies:

•

\gamma(f)=f^{p}

•

\gamma(df)=f^{p-1}df(\mathrm{mod}\ d\mathcal{O}_{X^{(p)}})

•

For

\gamma(\alpha+\beta)=\gamma(\alpha)+\gamma(\beta)

\gamma(\alpha\wedge\beta)=\gamma(\alpha)\wedge\gamma(\beta)

Calabi-Yau Operators

The function
A_{p}(z)
, as defined in
2
, can be computed directly from the periods. It follows that specific cases of the conjecture can be tested without any reference to an underlying family of varieties. We test this conjecture for a list of 545 Calabi-Yau operators and find that it fails sometimes. This list of Calabi-Yau opearators consists mainly of operators from the AESZ list
[
1
]
and can be found as an attached file, along with a SageMath Jupyter notebook.

A Calabi-Yau operator is a fourth order linear differential operator in a variable
z
, which satisfies a number of properties that make it a candidate Picard-Fuchs equation for a family of Calabi-Yau threefolds
[
1
,
2
,
20

z=0
is a point of maximal unipotent monodromy (MUM), the mirror map
z(q)
defined by this MUM point has integer coefficients when expanded in
q
, the genus
0
instanton numbers are integers up to some overall multiple, etc.

There is no guarantee that all of the Calabi-Yau operators in the AESZ list
[
1
]
are really the Picard-Fuchs equations of a family of Calabi-Yau threefolds with
h^{2,1}=1
, so these examples do not immediately invalidate Conjecture
2

.

For each operator, we compute the holomorphic period

\varpi_{0}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}

From a list of 545 Calabi-Yau operators, we find that 460 operators satisfy

A_{p}(z)=\varpi_{0}(z)^{p-1}~\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}c_{n}z^{n}\equiv_{p}1+O(z^{600})

for the first
100

is an arbitrarily chosen cutoff.

The remaining 85 operators do not satisfy this condition. However, we still find that

A_{p}(z)\equiv_{p}1+O(z^{p})

for all of the operators that we check and the first
100
primes.
