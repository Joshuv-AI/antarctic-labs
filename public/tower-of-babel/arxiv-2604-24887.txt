Symmetry-Protected Topological Phases in the Triangular Majorana–Hubbard Ladder
Will Holdhusen, Alberto Nocera, Jian-Xin Zhu, Armin Rahmani

Abstract
We map the phase diagram of the triangular-lattice Majorana-Hubbard model on a four-leg ladder using DMRG and variational uniform matrix product states, revealing a richer variety of phases than previously known. Analysis of entanglement-spectrum degeneracies and adiabatic connections identifies multiple symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases. These phases could be realized in arrays of vortex-bound Majorana modes on the surface of a topological superconductor.

Introduction.
—Recent years have seen significant progress in detecting Majorana zero modes (MZMs) in condensed matter systems, where they serve both as exotic counterparts to electrons and a potential platform for fault-tolerant topological quantum computation
32
. In a magnetic field, vortex-bound MZMs can form a lattice on the surface of a topological
p
-wave superconductor
29
;
6
;
43
. While evidence for
p
-wave superconductivity and MZMs exists in some iron-based materials
60
;
33
;
13
, the Fu–Kane model provides a particularly promising platform: a topological insulator (TI) proximitized by an
s
-wave superconductor effectively realizes
p
-wave pairing
22
. In this setup, tuning the TI chemical potential controls MZM hybridization, enabling both weakly and strongly interacting regimes
14
;
49
;
50
. Notably, access to the strongly interacting regime has spurred the emergence of a new field focused on strongly interacting Majorana lattice models. Candidate systems include Bi
2
Te
3
/NbSe
2
, SnTe/Pb-based topological insulators, and rhombohedral graphene
61
;
39
;
23
.

The Majorana–Hubbard (MH) model, with nearest-neighbor hopping and local four-site interactions, provides a minimal lattice description of interacting vortex-bound MZMs
49
;
47
. In one dimension, it exhibits gapped and critical phases as well as a supersymmetric region. In two dimensions, the model has been studied on square, honeycomb, and triangular lattices
3
;
48
;
35
;
34
;
55
, providing a framework to explore interaction-driven topological phenomena. Here, we investigate the phase diagram of MH model on the triangular-lattice on a four-leg ladder and uncover symmetry-fractionalized and symmetry-protected topological phases.

Earlier studies of the triangular-lattice Majorana–Hubbard (TLMH) model revealed notable differences between mean-field theory in two dimensions and DMRG for a four-leg ladder
55
. In particular, while mean-field theory predicted a gapped phase in the strongly attractive regime, DMRG on four-leg tori suggested a gapless, critical phase. Here, we present a comprehensive analysis of four-leg ladders with various boundary conditions and show that the apparent gapless phase is an artifact of specific boundary condition and system sizes (odd-length ladders).
Instead, we unveil a richer phase diagram summarized in Fig.
1
. In particular, we find that the phase in the strongly attractive region, which we label G
4
, is gapped and exhibits a ground state degeneracy due to both spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) and zero-energy Majorana edge modes. Likewise, we demonstrate that the G
1
phase, which extends from weakly attractive to strongly-repulsive interactions, possesses fermionic symmetry-protected topological (SPT) order. Between these phases, we can identify one other gapped phase (G
3
) and
a gapless phase (GL
4
) with dynamical critical exponent
z=2
characteristic of quantum Lifshitz models
5
.

The gapped phases we identify (G

, G

;

\mathcal{P}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

2

Examples of degenerate SPTs include one-dimensional fermionic SPTs formed as “stacks” of an odd number of Kitaev wires

Phase diagram.
—With our understanding of the low and strong-coupling limits, we now discuss the full phase diagram of the TLMH illustrated in Fig.
1

The fermionic SPT G
1
phase extends from a first-order transition at
g\rightarrow\infty
to a continuous phase transition at
g_{1}\approx-0.62
. At this point, the gap closes and a singularity appears in energy susceptibility
-\partial_{g}^{2}e_{0}
, as illustrated in Fig.

, indicating a second-order transition.

Below this point, we observe another gapped phase, which we dub G
3

x
-OBC).
Like the G
1

4

3

Another continuous transition at

brings us out of the G
3
phase. Below this point, we find a region characterized by strong entanglement, which makes obtaining high-quality DMRG results difficult. Unlike the phases enumerated in Figs.

3

One hint at the nature of this phase or phases is that unlike in the rest of the phase diagram, most of this region does not possess the degenerate entanglement spectrum characteristic of symmetry-fractionalized phases.
In any case, we leave detailed examination of this region for future work.

Decreasing

4

As in the other named phases, this is accompanied by a fermion parity switch. Correlation functions in this phase are plotted in Sec. V of the supplemental material

Frustration-induced gapless mode.
—Throughout the preceding analysis we assumed
L_{x}
to be even. On a cylinder (
x
-OBC), no appreciable difference is observed between even and odd
L_{x}
. However, on odd-
L_{x}
tori (as studied in Ref.
55
), the G
4
phase is replaced by a critical phase with relativistic dispersion (
\Delta\propto 1/L
)
55
above a twofold degenerate ground state.
Insight from the preceding discussion of the G
1
and G
4
phases helps account for this discrepancy.

The G
1
phase occurs on both even and odd
L_{x}
tori over essentially the same range of couplings
g
. This is unsurprising, since the magnetic order in this phase is commensurate for both even and odd
L_{x}
(the total number of spins
N=2L_{x}
remains even in either case). More precisely, this commensurability holds when the spin Hamiltonian has open or periodic boundary conditions, whereas antiperiodic boundary conditions force the formation of a domain-wall excitation. The APBC case in the G
1
phase can be disregarded by noting how boundary conditions transform under the Jordan–Wigner mapping introduced in Eq. (
2
). Under this transformation, the even-parity ground state of the
x
-APBC fermionic Hamiltonian and the odd-parity ground state of the
x
-PBC fermionic Hamiltonian both correspond to periodic boundary conditions in the spin Hamiltonian.

The G
4
phase has a four-spin unit cell (inherited from the previously-discussed ground states of
H_{4}
previously) that is incommensurate when the number of spins,
2L_{x}
, is not a multiple of four.
As in the case of spin-APBC in the G
1
phase, this incommensurability forces the formation of a domain wall. In the classical spin Hamiltonian
H_{4}
(which, as in the previous case, we can always take to have either OBC or PBC), this induces a degeneracy
d_{0}\propto L_{x}
. The additional interactions present in the full TLMH then delocalize these domain walls through hybridization, leading to a gapless excitation spectrum consistent with results obtained through finite-size scaling of DMRG results
55
.
Similar frustration-induced gapless modes occur in other triangular lattice models when placed on toric and cylindrical geometries

;
26

as well as in one-dimensional spin models with periodic boundary conditions
9
;
11
;
17
;
21
;
24
;
41
.

Consistent with our identification of the gapless mode with a single defect, we find that a torus with odd
L_{x}
has a higher energy than a similarly-sized even
L_{x}
torus by a roughly constant amount (i.e.,
\mathcal{O}(1)
), thus causing a
\mathcal{O}(1/L_{x})
difference in energy density as compared to the even-
L_{x}
case as shown in Sec. VII of the supplemental material

This picture of an isolated defect is complicated by the scaling of entanglement entropy in this phase, which has been shown to be consistent with a CFT possessing central charge 1 for systems up to
L_{x}=25
55
, contrasting with the non-critical scaling found when considering a single deconfined defect.
Following the same procedure
10
, we replicate this result on systems of up to
L_{x}=65
in Sec. VII of the Supplemental Material
1
, making it unlikely this is a finite size effect.

As shown in Sec. VII of the supplemental material, we find a degenerate entanglement spectrum in this gapless phase. Given that this phase also exhibits a fermion parity switch, which is associated with the MZMs of the G
1
and other gapped phases of the TLMH, this phase may represent a gapless SPT
36
.

Conclusions.
—We have performed a comprehensive numerical study of the TLMH on four-leg ladders under various boundary conditions, using large-scale DMRG and VUMPS in the computationally challenging attractive regime. We show that the previously reported gapless phase
55
arises from specific choices of boundary conditions and system size. Our results reveal a rich phase diagram, featuring a sequence of gapped phases, including a conventional fermionic SPT and several phases that combine SPT order with spontaneous symmetry breaking. This work underscores the crucial role of boundaries in stabilizing exotic phases of interacting Majorana fermions in quasi-1D triangular-lattice geometries.
