A quantum multi-sensor data fusion self-supervised learning framework for IIoT anomaly detection
Authors: Yikai Guo, Pengcheng Zhao, Xinchao Wang, Xingjia Wei, Xin Li, Enhui Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractScarce labels, limited edge-node resources, and degraded data quality are common in industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) multivariate time-series anomaly detection. Under these constraints, existing deep anomaly detection models often struggle to maintain high detection accuracy. At the same time, they find it difficult to ensure strong anomaly discrimination under class-imbalanced conditions and stable performance. To address this problem, a Self-Supervised Quantum Convolutional Neural Network (SQ-CNN) is proposed in this paper. The model is developed for IIoT multivariate time-series anomaly detection. A shared quantum convolutional feature extractor is adopted in the model. A self-supervised branch is constructed for representation learning. A supervised classification branch is constructed for anomaly recognition. A weighted joint loss is used for joint optimization. The parameters of the quantum circuit and the weights of the classical network are jointly updated. Consistent training is thus achieved from representation learning to recognition-oriented decision making. In addition, a classical head and a low-bit quantization strategy are designed. This design is intended to reduce the deployment overhead at the edge side and improve inference energy efficiency. Experimental results show that the proposed SQ-CNN achieves an 18.62% improvement in AUROC and AUPRC over baseline methods. A 19.72% reduction in unit energy consumption is also achieved. In addition, the single-window inference latency is reduced by 13.38%. The throughput reaches up to 392.28 windows/s.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The IIoT continuously collects high-frequency, multi-source, and heterogeneous sensing data through distributed sensors and edge-computing infrastructure. These data include time-series data and multimodal data. A data foundation is thus provided for predictive maintenance, anomaly diagnosis, and operational optimization. At the same time, IIoT data are usually characterized by high dimensionality, strong noise, and dynamic distributions. In addition, anomalous samples are scarce. Manual annotation is also costly1. As a result, it is difficult for anomaly detection models to achieve a good balance among detection accuracy, response timeliness, and cross-scenario adaptability2,3. In resource-constrained embedded and edge-computing scenarios, low-latency inference is required. Limited computing resources and energy budgets must also be considered. Cross-scenario generalization capability must still be maintained under these conditions4,5.

Traditional anomaly detection methods include approaches based on statistical feature extraction6, signal processing7, and deep learning architectures8. The first two types of methods show certain efficiency advantages in low-dimensional and regular scenarios. By contrast, stronger capabilities in complex pattern representation and automatic feature learning are shown by deep learning methods. However, large-scale labeled data are usually required by these methods. Complex training procedures are also required. In addition, limited performance is often shown in cross-dataset generalization and inference latency9,10. Quantum machine learning (QML) provides a new way for high-dimensional nonlinear pattern representation. Complex feature mapping in quantum-state space is exploited in QML. Better separability of anomalous patterns in complex data can thus be achieved11. However, several problems still remain in IIoT scenarios

These limitations prevent existing models from achieving high accuracy, low energy consumption, and high stability at the same time. To address this problem, an anomaly detection model based on self-supervised quantum convolution is proposed in this paper. A shared quantum convolutional encoder is integrated into the model. A self-supervised contrastive learning branch is also constructed in the model. A supervised anomaly recognition branch is further constructed in the model. In this way, unlabeled data can be more fully exploited. At the same time, anomaly discrimination capability can be enhanced. As a result, the energy performance, detection accuracy, and robustness of the model can be improved under low-label, complex-perturbation, and edge-deployment conditions. The motivation for introducing quantum convolution is not merely to replace a lightweight classical encoder, but to provide a compact nonlinear feature mapping for strongly coupled multi-sensor time-series patterns. In IIoT anomaly detection, abnormal behaviors are often reflected not by a single sensor channel, but by high-order correlations among temperature, vibration, photoelectric, humidity, and other sensing variables. Classical lightweight convolutional architectures are efficient, but their representation capacity is usually constrained by the depth and width of the network when the label budget is limited. By encoding windowed multi-sensor states into a parameterized quantum circuit, the proposed SQ-CNN maps local sensor patterns into a quantum-state space and extracts expectation-based compact features. This design is intended to enhance nonlinear feature separability while keeping the downstream classical head lightweight. Therefore, quantum convolution is adopted as a compact feature extraction module for label-scarce and resource-constrained IIoT anomaly detection. Related work

Anomaly detection methods for IIoT

IIoT anomaly detection is oriented to high-dimensional, multivariate, and strongly coupled continuous time-series data. Its core objective is to accurately identify behaviors that deviate from normal operating patterns under complex working conditions. USAD14, a dual-autoencoder architecture and an adversarial reconstruction mechanism are jointly introduced. The discrimination ability for anomalous patterns is thereby enhanced, and anomaly scoring is performed on the basis of reconstruction errors. OmniAnomaly15, uncertainty in complex dynamic systems is characterized by stochastic recurrent modeling and probabilistic inference mechanisms. TranAD16, the ability to model long-term dependencies is enhanced through the Transformer architecture and a self-conditioning mechanism. Anomaly Transformer17, the differences between anomalous points and normal points in temporal dependency structures are represented by an association discrepancy mechanism. The effectiveness of anomaly detection at the relational level is thus improved. Deep SVDD18, anomaly detection is formulated as a deep one-class classification problem. A compact normal sample space is learned for anomaly identification. DAGMM19, low-dimensional representation learning and Gaussian mixture density estimation are unified within an end-to-end framework. Joint modeling of the data distribution is therefore achieved. DLAE-PMU20, deep learning-based PMU anomaly detection and authenticated encryption are combined to improve event recognition and secure data transmission in industrial cyber-physical systems. DCdetector21, discriminative representations are constructed through a dual-attention mechanism and a contrastive learning strategy. The overfitting of traditional reconstruction-based methods to anomalous samples is thereby alleviated. UCF-SG22, ensemble learning and hybrid encryption are integrated to detect data integrity attacks and protect smart-grid data transmission. MEMTO23, a memory-guided Transformer and a two-stage training mechanism are designed. The over-generalization phenomenon of reconstruction-based models is thereby suppressed. AIC-ICS24, AI-driven attack detection and cryptographic privacy protection are jointly considered for cyber-resilient industrial control systems. This method improves secure industrial monitoring, but low-label representation learning and edge-side inference efficiency are not the main focus. CATCH25, cross-variable correlations are further modeled through frequency-domain patching and a channel-aware mechanism. The capability of identifying complex anomalous patterns is thus enhanced. IEC-ID26, feature optimization, privacy protection, and honeypot-firewall integration are combined to improve IEC 60870-5-104 smart-grid intrusion detection. This method enhances protocol-level attack identification and secure communication, but multi-sensor representation learning under limited labels is not its main focus. Continuous progress has been achieved by the above methods in anomaly recognition accuracy, security protection, and time-series pattern representation. However, most existing studies are still focused on a single detection metric, secure communication, or a specific modeling mechanism. A unified and effective modeling framework is still lacking for cross-scenario generalization and deployment feasibility under resource-constrained conditions. The coordinated trade-off among engineering objectives such as detection performance, real-time response, and energy consumption also remains insufficiently explored.

Self-supervised learning for unlabeled scenarios

In IIoT scenarios, anomalous samples are difficult to obtain, and manual annotation is costly. Unlabeled or low-label learning has therefore become an important research direction in anomaly detection. In self-supervised learning, transferable representations can be extracted from unlabeled data through auxiliary tasks, contrastive objectives, or consistency constraints. An effective pretraining pathway can thus be provided for anomaly detection. BERT32, anomaly representation in complex time series is modeled within a degradation-based self-supervised framework. The discrimination ability for anomalous patterns is enhanced by degradation-aware tasks. STEN33, normal patterns in multivariate time series are characterized within a spatiotemporal normality learning framework. The anomaly recognition capability under unlabeled conditions is improved by a joint mechanism of sequence order prediction and feature-space distance prediction. AD-CIFC34, the anomaly detection process under incomplete observations is modeled within an integrated framework of missing-value completion and collaborative feature analysis. The adaptability of the model to missing-data scenarios is enhanced by a comprehensive imputation strategy and a collaborative learning mechanism. LATAD35, discriminative features in unlabeled time-series data are characterized within a learnable data augmentation framework. The anomaly recognition performance of the model in multi-industry scenarios is improved by a self-supervised training mechanism. SPT-AD36, vibration time-series anomalies in manufacturing scenarios are modeled within a multi-scale pyramid Transformer framework. The accuracy and computational efficiency in mechanical fault detection are improved by a hierarchical temporal representation mechanism. Overall, positive progress has been achieved by existing methods in unlabeled representation learning, missing-data processing, and complex time-series feature extraction. However, further improvement is still needed in model generalization under low-label conditions. In this paper, we address a core problem in IIoT anomaly detection. Due to the coexistence of edge deployment constraints and complex data perturbations, existing models find it difficult to simultaneously balance multiple objectives. These objectives include detection performance, energy consumption control, real-time inference, and robustness. A self-supervised model is therefore proposed. In this model, quantum convolution and contrastive learning are deeply integrated. The model is intended to maintain high accuracy, low latency, and good cross-scenario generalization performance, even under conditions of scarce labels and limited computational resources.

System architecture

This system is designed for anomaly detection applications in the IIoT. A hybrid model based on a self-supervised quantum convolutional neural network is developed, as shown in Fig. 1. The model consists of a self-supervised training path and a supervised classification path. A front-end quantum convolutional feature extractor is shared by the two paths. The extractor is used for self-supervised representation learning and supervised anomaly recognition, respectively.

System architecture of the proposed SQ-CNN framework.

SQ-CNN takes multivariate sensing data collected from the industrial Internet of Things as input. The raw data are first processed by preprocessing and data augmentation. Training samples are then generated. For time-series data, the raw sequences are segmented into fixed-length input windows by a sliding-window strategy. Additive noise, local temporal perturbations, and frequency-domain perturbations are then applied to these windows. The augmented samples are fed into a shared encoder. A front-end quantum convolutional layer is used by the encoder as the main feature extraction unit. Representation transformation is performed on the input samples by the encoder, and feature vectors for subsequent tasks are produced. During training, the learned feature representations are fed into a self-supervised contrastive learning branch and a supervised anomaly detection branch, respectively. In the former branch, positive sample pairs are constructed from different augmented views generated from the same sample. The representation distribution is then constrained by a contrastive loss. In the latter branch, the encoded features are input into a classical fully connected detection head. Anomaly category discrimination is then completed. During training, a two-stage optimization framework is adopted by the model. In the first stage, the shared encoder is pretrained through a self-supervised objective. In the second stage, supervised fine-tuning is performed on a small number of labeled samples. A freeze–unfreeze strategy is further adopted, so that collaborative optimization of the shared encoder and the classification head can be achieved.

Quantum convolutional network structure

On the basis of the preprocessed input sample x defined above, a front-end quantum convolutional encoder is adopted in this paper as a shared feature extraction module. Here, x is not treated as a static vector in the general sense. Instead, x is defined as a local state segment that is obtained from IIoT multivariate sensing data after normalization, temporal alignment, and sliding-window segmentation. Instantaneous amplitudes of sensing variables within the window are preserved in this segment. Local evolution trends are also preserved in this segment. Cross-variable coupling relationships are further retained in this segment. Industrial anomalies are often not manifested as isolated abrupt changes at a single measurement point. In many cases, such anomalies are reflected by imbalances in collaborative patterns among multiple sensing channels, by perturbations in local dynamic processes, or by the destruction of periodic structures. Therefore, a quantum convolutional encoder is employed in this paper to generate compact representations of windowed samples, so that high-order correlated features for anomaly recognition can be extracted.

Quantum contrastive learning and anomaly recognition fine-tuning

In IIoT scenarios, normal operating data are usually easier to obtain, whereas the number of anomalous labeled samples is limited. A two-stage training strategy is therefore adopted in this paper, including self-supervised pretraining and supervised fine-tuning. In the former stage, stable state representations are learned from many unlabeled industrial operating windows. In the latter stage, the anomaly decision boundary is strengthened by a small number of labeled anomalous samples.

Experimental evaluation

Experimental settings

All experiments are conducted under the same hardware and software environment. The hardware platform consists of an Intel Core i7-13700F CPU, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 Ti Super 16GB GPU, and 32GB RAM. Ubuntu 22.04 LTS is adopted as the operating system. The software environment consists of Python 3.11, PyTorch 2.7.0, CUDA 11.8, and PennyLane 0.44.1.

In our experiments, the quantum convolutional module is implemented as a parameterized quantum circuit and integrated with the classical projection head and supervised anomaly classifier. Each input window is first compressed into a low-dimensional angle vector. The angle vector is then encoded by rotation gates and processed by variational quantum layers. The measured expectation values are concatenated as compact quantum features and fed into the self-supervised branch and the supervised anomaly detection branch for joint optimization.

In the experimental configuration, the quantum convolutional encoder uses four qubits and two variational layers. Each input window is mapped into a four-dimensional rotation-angle vector. Angle encoding is performed by gates. Each variational layer consists of trainable and rotation gates followed by a ring CNOT entanglement block. The last qubit is connected back to the first qubit to form a closed entanglement topology. After circuit evolution, Pauli-Z expectation values are measured and concatenated as the quantum feature representation.

In order to verify the applicability of the proposed method in real IIoT environments, an experimental design close to edge manufacturing scenarios is established in this paper. The Indoor data dataset is used to simulate indoor production units and equipment operating environments. Scenarios such as robotic workstations, conveyor lines, and multi-sensor monitoring nodes are thereby covered. CIFAR-10, as a typical public visual benchmark dataset, is used to further verify the representation learning and anomaly discrimination capability of the proposed model in non-temporal image scenarios. Although Multi-source traffic sensing originates from a traffic sensing scenario, characteristics such as multi-source sensing, continuous monitoring, dynamic variation, and scarce anomalous samples are exhibited by this dataset. It is therefore used as a nn cross-scenario validation dataset, so that the generalization capability of the model in complex multivariate time-series monitoring tasks can be evaluated.

Real industrial deployment scene used in the manuscript.

Industrial anomaly detection is usually characterized by multivariate time-series coupling, scarce anomalous samples, and high real-time requirements at the edge side. The experiments are therefore conducted from six aspects, namely the overall energy–performance behavior, inference latency and throughput, robustness to strong noise and missing-data perturbations, detection capability under limited label budgets, component-level ablation of the quantum encoder and self-supervised branch, and the effect of quantum measurement shots on convergence and inference overhead. In this way, the effectiveness, engineering feasibility, module-level contribution, and finite-shot convergence behavior of the model under practical deployment conditions can be systematically verified.

As the sensor scale increases across the three datasets, the system maintains high AUROC values (from 0.942 to 0.929) with only a slight increase in latency (from 2.58 ms to 2.91 ms per window). This consistent performance under growing sensor dimensionality validates the strong scalability of the proposed method. The input sensing channels include environmental and equipment-related measurements, such as temperature, humidity, vibration, photoelectric signals, CO2 concentration, and other monitoring variables collected from industrial sensing nodes.

Baseline Methods: The following representative baselines are selected for comparison. USAD, TranAD, and Anomaly Transformer are used as representative classical deep anomaly detection baselines. USAD14 is an unsupervised anomaly detection method based on a dual-decoder autoencoder, in which reconstruction errors are used to identify anomalies in multivariate time series.

TranAD16 is a Transformer-based anomaly detection model, in which an attention mechanism is used to capture global temporal information, while both detection accuracy and inference efficiency are taken into account.

Anomaly Transformer17 is a time-series anomaly detection method based on association discrepancy, in which the discrimination capability between normal points and anomalous points is enhanced by a specially designed attention mechanism.

QSVDD27 is a quantum one-class anomaly detection method, in which normal samples are described in a quantum feature space and anomalous samples are identified by their deviation from the learned boundary.

QAE28 is a quantum autoencoder-based anomaly detection method, in which latent time-series structures are compressed and reconstructed through quantum encoding to detect abnormal deviations.

QSVMs14 use quantum-kernel-based feature mapping to construct nonlinear decision boundaries for anomaly detection in industrial control scenarios.

FQKL14 is a federated quantum kernel learning method, in which quantum kernel representations are combined with distributed anomaly detection for multivariate Internet of Things time-series data.

Experiment 5: Ablation study

Although the previous experiments verify the overall effectiveness of SQ-CNN from the perspectives of detection performance, inference efficiency, robustness, and label efficiency, the individual contributions of the quantum encoder and the self-supervised branch still need to be further analyzed. Therefore, an ablation study is conducted in Experiment 5.

The complete SQ-CNN is compared with three variants. The first variant removes the quantum feature extraction module and only retains the lightweight classical discrimination path. The second variant replaces the quantum convolutional encoder with a classical CNN encoder under the same downstream optimization setting. The third variant removes the self-supervised contrastive learning branch and only uses the supervised classification objective. In this way, the contribution of quantum feature extraction and the contribution of self-supervised representation learning can be separately examined.

As shown in Fig. 7, the complete SQ-CNN consistently achieves the best AUROC and AUPRC on the three datasets, with AUROC/AUPRC values of 0.942/0.938, 0.931/0.917, and 0.917/0.904 on Indoor Data, Multi-source Traffic Sensing, and CIFAR-10, respectively. When the quantum encoder is removed, the largest performance degradation is observed, and the AUROC/AUPRC values decrease to 0.889/0.869, 0.881/0.854, and 0.866/0.836, respectively. This indicates that the quantum convolutional feature extractor contributes to compact nonlinear representation learning for strongly coupled multi-sensor patterns.

Ablation study of the proposed SQ-CNN framework.

When the quantum encoder is replaced by a classical CNN encoder, the performance also decreases, with AUROC/AUPRC values of 0.909/0.888, 0.896/0.873, and 0.882/0.856 on the three datasets, respectively. This result suggests that the quantum feature mapping provides additional representation capability beyond the classical convolutional alternative under the same lightweight downstream structure. In addition, removing the self-supervised branch reduces the AUROC/AUPRC values to 0.915/0.899, 0.899/0.878, and 0.891/0.866, respectively. This result confirms that self-supervised contrastive learning improves the stability and discriminability of the learned representations. Overall, the ablation results demonstrate that the performance gain of SQ-CNN is not caused by a single component, but by the joint effect of quantum feature extraction and self-supervised representation learning. The standard-deviation results show that SQ-CNN maintains relatively stable performance across repeated runs. In addition, the paired significance test indicates that the improvements of SQ-CNN over the strongest competing baseline are statistically significant under the adopted evaluation settings, with P < 0.05.

Experiment 6: Effect of quantum shots on convergence

To further connect the theoretical convergence analysis with empirical behavior, the effect of the number of quantum measurement shots is evaluated. According to the convergence analysis, the finite-shot measurement noise term decreases as S increases, where S denotes the number of shots. Therefore, four shot settings, namely S=64, S=128, S=256, and S=512, are compared from the perspectives of convergence epoch and inference latency.

As shown in Fig. 8, increasing the number of shots reduces the number of epochs required for convergence. When S increases from 64 to 256, the convergence epoch decreases from 76.6 to 61.4, indicating that larger shot numbers can reduce finite-shot measurement uncertainty and stabilize the optimization process. When S further increases to 512, the convergence epoch only decreases slightly to 59.6, showing a marginal improvement.

Effect of the number of quantum measurement shots on convergence epoch and inference latency.

However, a larger number of shots also introduces additional inference overhead. The latency increases from 2.42 ms/window at S=64 to 2.89 ms/window at S=256, and further increases to 3.44 ms/window at S=512. These results are consistent with the theoretical analysis that the finite-shot noise term decreases with S, while the measurement cost increases with the number of shots. Considering both convergence stability and edge-side inference efficiency, S=256 provides a practical trade-off in the current implementation.

Conclusion

In IIoT multivariate time-series anomaly detection scenarios, complex coupling among multi-source sensing data, the scarcity of anomalous labeled samples, and the resource constraints of edge nodes jointly reduce the detection stability and deployment efficiency of anomaly recognition models. To address this issue, a self-supervised quantum convolutional neural network, namely SQ-CNN, is proposed for IIoT anomaly detection. A shared quantum convolutional encoder is introduced to support feature extraction, while self-supervised pretraining and supervised fine-tuning are incorporated into the training framework. In addition, a lightweight detection head and a low-bit quantization strategy are adopted for edge-side deployment. Experimental results show that the proposed method exhibits favorable overall performance in detection accuracy, convergence stability, and unit energy consumption control. In future work, the mechanism through which coupling relationships among physical attributes influence anomaly representation and detection performance will be further investigated.
