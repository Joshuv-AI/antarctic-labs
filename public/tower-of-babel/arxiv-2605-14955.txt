On the number of rational power factors in a finite word
S. Li, Y. Song

Abstract
Let w w be a finite word of length n n . In this paper, we study the maximum possible number of distinct rational power factors in a finite word. A rational power is a word of the form u = p k ​ p ′ u=p^{k}p^{\prime} , where p p is a nonempty finite word, k k is an integer larger than 1 1 , p k p^{k} is a concatenation of k k copies of p p and p ′ p^{\prime} is a prefix of p p . The rational powers can be recognized as a generalization of k k -powers, and it is proved in [ 5 ] that, the number C k ​ ( w ) C_{k}(w) of distinct k k -powers in w w satisfies C k ​ ( w ) ≤ n − 1 k − 1 C_{k}(w)\leq\frac{n-1}{k-1} . However, the number of rational powers has not been studied in the literature. In this article, we prove that the number RP ⁡ ( w ) \mathrm{RP}(w) of distinct rational power factors of w w satisfies RP ⁡ ( w ) ≤ 1 8 ​ n 2 + O ⁡ ( n ) . \mathrm{RP}(w)\leq\frac{1}{8}n^{2}+O(n). We also illustrate a novel approach to study pattern-counting problems: using a graph-theoretic representation of words and a few word equations, we transform the traditional pattern-counting problems into a constrained extremal problem.



1
Introduction

The study of repetitions is a long-standing and active topic in combinatorics on words. The first result concerning repetitions can be dated back to Thue
[
9
]
, who proved that, the simplest repetition - the square words, i.e. the words of the form
w=vv
, can be avoided in infinite words of three letters.

Counting the number of repetitions in finite words was initiated by Fraenkel and Simpson, who proved that the number of distinct square factors in a word of length
n
is at most
2n
and conjectured that
C_{2}(w)\leq n
, see
[
4
]
. This conjecture was proved by Brlek and the first author in
[
1
]
.
Similar questions have also been studied for
k
-powers. Crochemore et al. proved in
[
2
]
that for a fixed integer
k\geq 3
, the number of distinct
k
-powers in a word of length
n
is at most
\frac{n}{k-2}
. Pachocki, Radoszewski and the first author proved in
[
5
]
that for a fixed integer
k\geq 2
, the maximum number of distinct nonempty
k
-power factors in a word of length

n
is between

\frac{n}{k-1}-\Theta(\sqrt{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{n-1}{k-1}.

These results show that the counting function of
k
-powers increases linearly in terms of the length of the word. An easy observation shows that the number of rational powers can increase quadratically in terms of length, see for example the words of the form
(ab)^{k}
.

Another related work is about closed-word countings. In
[
7
]
, Olga and Svetlana proved that the number
C_{closed}(w)
of closed words, i.e. the words of the form
aba
, where
a
is a nonempty word, in
w
, satisfies that
C_{closed}(w)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{6}
and that this upper bound is sharp. Since all rational powers are closed words, we have naturally the upper bound
\mathrm{RP}(w)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{6}
. The strategy used in
[
7
]
is to consider how many new closed words can be created by adding one single letter at the end of a string. The same strategy has also been applied by Fraenkel and Simpson in
[
4
]
to prove
C_{2}(w)\leq 2n
. In
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
, to give a tighter upper bound to
C_{2}(w)
as well as
C_{k}(w)
, the first author and his co-authors used a graph-theoretic method and constructed an injection from
k
-powers in
w
to the small circuits in the Rauzy graphs of
w
. In this work, using the same correspondence between
k
-powers and small circuits and a few more word equations, we transform the rational-power-counting problems into a constrained extremal problem and prove that
\mathrm{RP}(w)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{8}
.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
recalls basic terminology on words, primitive words, conjugacy classes, rational powers, and Rauzy graphs.
Section
3
gives some basic facts of rational power factors.
Section
4
proves the local estimates for one primitive conjugacy class, establishes the restrictions on all the classes, and completes the optimization leading to the main theorem.

3
Some basic facts of rational power factors

Let
w
be any word. For any
u\in\mathrm{Prim}(w)
such that
u^{2}\in\hbox{\rm Fac}(w),|u|=\ell
, let

\mathrm{Class}_{w}(u)=\{p^{m}\in\hbox{\rm Fac}(w)|p\in[u],m\in\mathbb{Z},m\geq 2\};

\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w}(u)=\{p^{m}\in\hbox{\rm Fac}(w)|p\in[u],m\in\mathbb{Q},m\geq 2\};

D(w)=\{[u]|u^{2}\in\hbox{\rm Fac}(w)\}.

Let
\mathrm{RP}(w)
be the number of distinct rational power factors of
w
. By Corollary
3
,
\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w}(u)\cap\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w}(v)=\emptyset
if and only if
u\not\in[v]
, hence

\mathrm{RP}(w)=\sum\limits_{[u]\in D(w)}|\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w}(u)|.

Let

\mathrm{RP}(n)=\max_{|w|=n}\mathrm{RP}(w).

Let
M(u)
be the number of small circuits in
\Gamma(w)
of the form
C(u,m)
. In the following of this section, the factor
u
is fixed, and we use
M
instead of
M(u)
for simplicity.

Let
S_{[u]}(w)=\{C(u,m)|C(u,m)\sqsubset\Gamma_{m}(w),\;1\leq m\leq|w|\}
be the set of all small circuits in
\Gamma(w)
of the form
C(u,m)
.
An easy observation is that if

C(u,m)=([u]_{m},[u]_{m+1})\sqsubset\Gamma_{m}(w)
for some
m>\ell
, then
C(u,i)=([u]_{i},[u]_{i+1})\sqsubset\Gamma_{i}(w)
for all
\ell\leq i\leq m
. Thus,

S_{[u]}(w)=\{C(u,\ell+i)|0\leq i\leq M(u)-1,i\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Define a map
f
from
S_{[u]}(w)
to
2^{\hbox{\rm Fac}(u^{\omega})}
as follows:

f(C(u,\ell+i))=[u]_{2\ell+i},\quad 0\leq i\leq M-1,i\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence for
i\neq j,\ f(C(u,\ell+i))\cap f(C(u,\ell+j))=\emptyset
.
Let

T=\bigcup_{i=0}^{M-1}f(C(u,\ell+i)).

Lemma 5

For any integer
i\geq M
,
\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w}(u)\cap[u]_{2\ell+i}=\emptyset
.

Proof

If there exists a word
v\in\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w}(u)\cap[u]_{2\ell+i_{0}}
for some
i_{0}>M
, then its prefix
v_{p}(2\ell+M)\in\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w}(u)\cap[u]_{2\ell+M}
. Therefore, it is enough to prove in the case

. Given a word
p\in[u]_{2\ell+M}
, for any word

, we have

. Hence if

, then

is a small circuit in

, which contradicts the definition of

. ∎

Lemma 6

For any integer
1\leq i\leq\ell
,
|\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w}(u)\cap[u]_{2\ell+M-i}|\leq i
.

Proof

If

,
|\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w}(u)\cap[u]_{2\ell+M-\ell}|\leq|[u]_{\ell+M}|=\ell

Here we prove that for all

be the first occurrence of

Define

Then

\mathrm{RP}(w)\leq\frac{1}{8}n^{2}+O(n).

. In this case, for all
[u_{i}]\in D_{3}(w)
,

\ell_{1}\leq\ell_{i}\leq A_{i}\leq A_{1}.

Thus,

m_{i}=|I_{i}\cap I_{1}|.

Moreover, from the definition of the function
F
, one has

F(\frac{\ell}{N},\frac{m}{N})\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)^{2}.

Define

K:=\sum_{[u_{i}]\in D_{3}}|I_{i}\cap I_{1}|.

One has

\sum_{[u_{i}]\in D_{3}}F(\frac{\ell_{i}}{N},\frac{m_{i}}{N})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{[u_{i}]\in D_{3}}\left(\frac{m_{i}}{N}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{[u_{i}]\in D_{3}}\left(\frac{|I_{i}\cap I_{1}|}{N}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K}{N}\right)^{2}.

Next we compute
\mathrm{RP}_{2}(w)
. We claim

\sum_{[u_{i}]\in D_{2}}F(\frac{\ell_{i}}{N},\frac{m_{i}}{N})\leq\mathcal{P}(\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N},\frac{N-m_{1}-K}{N}).

To verify this, it is enough to check

\frac{\ell_{i}+m_{i}}{N}\leq\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N},\ \forall[u_{i}]\in D_{2},

(5)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{[u_{i}]\in D_{2}}m_{i}\leq\frac{N-m_{1}-K}{N},

(6)

0\leq\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N}\leq\frac{1}{2},

(7)

\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N}\leq\frac{N-m_{1}-K}{N}\leq 2\,\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N}.

(8)

We first verify Inequality
5
. By Equation
3
, we have
K\leq N-2\ell_{1}-m_{1}
.
For any
[u_{i}]\in D_{2}
, one has
A_{i}\leq A_{1}
and
\ell_{i}<\ell_{1}
. If
A_{i}\leq\ell_{1}
, then

A_{i}\leq\ell_{1}\leq N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K
, where the last Inequality follows from Equation
3
also. If
A_{i}>\ell_{1}
, then

I_{i}\cap I_{1}=\{\ell_{1},\ell_{1}+1,\ldots,A_{i}-1\}
. Thus

A_{i}-\ell_{1}=|I_{i}\cap I_{1}|\leq N-2\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K
, and again
A_{i}\leq N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K
. Therefore, in all cases, Inequality
5
holds.

Next, if
[u_{i}]\in D_{3}
, then
\ell_{i}\geq\ell_{1}
and
A_{i}\leq A_{1}
, hence
I_{i}\subseteq I_{1}
and
|I_{i}\cap I_{1}|=m_{i}
. Therefore
K=\sum_{[u_{i}]\in D_{3}}m_{i}
. From Equation
1
, we get

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{[u_{i}]\in D_{2}}m_{i}\leq\frac{N-m_{1}-\sum_{[u_{i}]\in D_{3}}m_{i}}{N}=\frac{N-m_{1}-K}{N},

which proves Inequality
6
.

Third, from Equation
3
, we have
N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K\geq\ell_{1}>0
. Since we are in Case 2,
A_{1}=\ell_{1}+m_{1}>N/2
, and hence
N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K<N/2
. This proves inequality
7
.

Lastly, the right inequality of
8
is equivalent to
2\ell_{1}+m_{1}+K\leq N
, which follows again from Equation
3
.

Combining the three estimates gives

\sum_{i=1}^{t}F(\frac{\ell_{i}}{N},\frac{m_{i}}{N})\leq F(\frac{\ell_{1}}{N},\frac{m_{1}}{N})+\mathcal{P}(\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N},\frac{N-m_{1}-K}{N})+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{K}{N})^{2}.

By Lemma
12
,

N^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{t}F(\frac{\ell_{i}}{N},\frac{m_{i}}{N})\leq\frac{1}{8}N^{2}.

Finally it is enough to check the two conditions of Lemma
12
, that is,

0<\frac{\ell_{1}}{N}\leq\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N}\leq\frac{1}{2},

(9)

0\leq\frac{K}{N}\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N}.

(10)

The inequality
0\leq\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N}\leq\frac{1}{2}
is from Equation
7
;
\frac{\ell_{1}}{N}\leq\frac{N-\ell_{1}-m_{1}-K}{N}
is obtained by Equation
3
in Lemma
9
and Inequality
10
by
A_{1}>\frac{N}{2}
. ∎

We now prove the two elementary optimization lemmas used in the proof of
Theorem
10
.

Lemma 11

Let
0<s\leq 1/2
and
s\leq B\leq 2s
. Then

\mathcal{P}(s,B)\leq s^{2}V(B/s),

where, for
1\leq T\leq 2
,

V(T):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(2-T)(3T-2)}{4},&1\leq T\leq\dfrac{4}{3},\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{3}{2}T^{2}-4T+3,&\dfrac{4}{3}\leq T\leq\dfrac{3}{2},\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}(3-T)(T-1),&\dfrac{3}{2}\leq T\leq 2.\end{cases}

In particular,

\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)\leq\frac{1}{8}.

Proof

Let
T:=\frac{B}{s}
, then
1\leq T\leq 2
.

Fix one pair
(x,y)
with
x+y\leq s
. Write

y=rs,\qquad 0\leq r\leq 1.

For a fixed
y
, the largest possible value of
F(x,y)
, under the condition

0\leq x\leq s-y
, is
s^{2}\varphi(r)
, where

\varphi(r):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{2}r^{2},&0\leq r\leq\dfrac{1}{2},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}(1-r)(3r-1),&\dfrac{1}{2}\leq r\leq 1.\end{cases}

Indeed, if
r\leq 1/2
, then one can choose
x\geq y
, and the maximum is

\frac{1}{2}y^{2}=s^{2}\frac{1}{2}r^{2}.

If
r\geq 1/2
, then
x\leq s-y\leq y
, and

F(x,y)=xy-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}

is increasing in
x
on
0\leq x\leq s-y
. Hence the maximum is attained at

x=s-y
, and equals

(s-y)y-\frac{1}{2}(s-y)^{2}=s^{2}\frac{1}{2}(1-r)(3r-1).

Therefore, for every admissible family
(x_{j},y_{j})
, if
r_{j}:=\frac{y_{j}}{s}
, then

F(x_{j},y_{j})\leq s^{2}\varphi(r_{j}),\quad\sum_{j}r_{j}\leq T.

It remains to maximize
\sum_{j}\varphi(r_{j})
under the constraints

0\leq r_{j}\leq 1,\qquad\sum_{j}r_{j}\leq T.

Let
r=T-\sum_{j}r_{j}
, then
\max\sum_{j}\varphi(r_{j})\leq\max(\sum_{j}\varphi(r_{j})+\varphi(r))
. We can now compute
\max\sum_{j}\varphi(r_{j})
under the restrictions:

0\leq r_{j}\leq 1,\qquad\sum_{j}r_{j}=T.

We remark that for any pair
0\leq r_{i},r_{j}<\frac{1}{2}
with
r_{i}\neq r_{j}
, if
r_{i}+r_{j}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then

\varphi(r_{i})+\varphi(r_{j})\leq\varphi(r_{i}+r_{j});

if
r_{i}+r_{j}>\frac{1}{2}
, then

\varphi(r_{i})+\varphi(r_{j})\leq\varphi(\frac{1}{2})+\varphi(r_{i}+r_{j}-\frac{1}{2}).

Thus, one can reduce the number of variables in objective function
\sum_{j}\varphi(r_{j})
into at most
4
. That is,
\max\sum_{j}\varphi(r_{j})\leq\max\sum_{j=1}^{k}\varphi(r^{\prime}_{j})
under the restrictions:

0\leq r^{\prime}_{1}\leq r^{\prime}_{2}\leq\ldots\leq r^{\prime}_{k}\leq 1,\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{k}r^{\prime}_{j}=T,\qquad r^{\prime}_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Moreover, since
\varphi
is concave in
[\frac{1}{2},1]
,

\sum_{j=2}^{k}\varphi(r^{\prime}_{j})\leq(k-1)\varphi(\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{j=2}^{k}r^{\prime}_{j}).

Hence,

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\varphi(r^{\prime}_{j})\leq\varphi(r^{\prime}_{1})+(k-1)\varphi(\frac{T-r^{\prime}_{1}}{k-1}).

For
k=2,3,4
, a direct one-variable calculation gives the following three possible maxima:

2\varphi(T/2)\quad\text{for }1\leq T\leq\frac{4}{3},

2\varphi(2-T)+\frac{1}{2}(3T-4)^{2}\quad\text{for }\frac{4}{3}\leq T\leq\frac{3}{2},

and

3\varphi(T/3)\quad\text{for }\frac{3}{2}\leq T\leq 2.

These are exactly

V(T)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(2-T)(3T-2)}{4},&1\leq T\leq\dfrac{4}{3},\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{3}{2}T^{2}-4T+3,&\dfrac{4}{3}\leq T\leq\dfrac{3}{2},\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}(3-T)(T-1),&\dfrac{3}{2}\leq T\leq 2.\end{cases}

∎

Lemma 12

Let
0<a\leq s\leq 1/2
,
0\leq y\leq 1/2-s
.
Then

F(a,1-a-s-y)+\mathcal{P}(s,s+a)+\frac{1}{2}y^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8}.

Proof

By Lemma
11
,

\mathcal{P}(s,s+a)\leq s^{2}V\left(1+\frac{a}{s}\right).

For brevity, let

W(a,s):=s^{2}V\left(1+\frac{a}{s}\right).

From the explicit formula for
V
, we get

W(a,s)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{4}s^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}as-\dfrac{3}{4}a^{2},&0\leq\frac{a}{s}\leq\frac{1}{3},\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}s^{2}-as+\dfrac{3}{2}a^{2},&\frac{1}{3}\leq\frac{a}{s}\leq\frac{1}{2},\\[8.0pt]
as-\dfrac{1}{2}a^{2},&\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{a}{s}\leq 1.\end{cases}

It is enough to prove

F(a,1-a-s-y)+W(a,s)+\frac{1}{2}y^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8}.

Let

S=s+y.

Then

0<a\leq s\leq S\leq\frac{1}{2}.

For fixed
a
and
S
, we maximize
W(a,s)+\frac{1}{2}(S-s)^{2}
over
a\leq s\leq S
. Then
one has

\displaystyle W(a,s)+\frac{1}{2}(S-s)^{2}\leq\max\begin{cases}U_{1}(a,S)=\frac{1}{2}a^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(S-a)^{2},\quad&0\leq a\leq S\leq\dfrac{1}{2},\\
U_{2}(a,S)=\frac{3}{2}a^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(S-2a)^{2},\quad&0\leq 2a\leq S\leq\dfrac{1}{2},\\
U_{3}(a,S)=3a^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(S-3a)^{2},\quad&0\leq 3a\leq S\leq\dfrac{1}{2},\\
U_{4}(a,S)=W(a,S),\quad&0\leq a\leq S\leq\dfrac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Thus it remains to prove

F(a,1-a-S)+U_{j}(a,S)\leq\frac{1}{8},\quad j=1,2,3,4.

Recall that

F(a,1-a-S)=\begin{cases}\frac{(1-a-S)^{2}}{2},&\;\text{if $1-a-S\leq a$},\\
a(1-a-S-\frac{a}{2}),&\;\text{if $1-a-S>a$}.\end{cases}

One has

\max_{0<a\leq S\leq\frac{1}{2}}F(a,1-a-S)+U_{j}(a,S)=\begin{cases}\max_{i=1,2,3,4}\{U_{i}+F(a,1-a-S)\},&\text{if $3a\leq S<\frac{1}{2}$},\\
\max_{i=1,2,4}\{U_{i}+F(a,1-a-S)\},&\text{if $2a\leq S<3a$},\\
\max_{i=1,2}\{U_{i}+F(a,1-a-S)\},&\text{if $a\leq S<2a$}.\\
\end{cases}

One can check in all cases

\max_{0<a\leq S\leq\frac{1}{2}}F(a,1-a-S)+U_{j}(a,S)\leq\frac{1}{8}.

Therefore

F(a,1-a-s-y)+W(a,s)+\frac{1}{2}y^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8}.

∎

Extremal profiles allowed by the relaxation.

The preceding proof also indicates where the constant
1/8
comes from.

In Case 1, we achieve the maximum value if the two following conditions hold:

A_{i}=\ell_{i}+m_{i}\approx\frac{N}{2}\;\text{for all $[u_{i}]\in D(w)$}

and

\sum_{i}m_{i}\approx N.

Two typical equality profiles are:

(\ell_{i},m_{i})\approx\left(\frac{N}{6},\frac{N}{3}\right)\quad\text{for three classes,}

or

(\ell_{i},m_{i})\approx\left(\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4}\right)\quad\text{for four classes.}

In Case 2, we achieve the maximum value when:

(\ell_{1},m_{1})\approx\left(\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{2}\right)

and

D_{3}=\emptyset,\quad\text{and for any }[u_{i}]\in D_{2},\ A_{i}+\ell_{i}\approx\ell_{1}.

A typical relaxed profile is

(\ell_{i},m_{i})\approx\left(\frac{N}{12},\frac{N}{6}\right)\quad\text{for three classes,}

or

(\ell_{i},m_{i})\approx\left(\frac{N}{8},\frac{N}{8}\right)\quad\text{for four classes.}

5
A lower bound of
\mathrm{RP}(n)

In this section, we give the first estimation of lower bound of
\mathrm{RP}(n)
by proving that there exist infinitely many words
w
such that
\mathrm{RP}(w)\geq\frac{|w|^{2}}{9}-O(|w|)
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

w_{n}=(a^{n}ba^{n-1}b)^{4}a^{n-1}.

One can check
|w_{n}|=9n+3.
Let us compute
\mathrm{RP}(w)
.

Since
a^{n}\in\hbox{\rm Fac}(w_{n})
,

\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w_{n}}(a)=\{a^{k}|2\leq k\leq n\}.

Since
v_{1}=(a^{n-1}ba)^{2}a^{n-2}\in\hbox{\rm Fac}(w_{n})
,

\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w_{n}}(a^{n-1}ba)=\{v_{1}[i,j]|1\leq i<j\leq 3n,j-i\geq 2n+1\}.

Since
v_{2}=(a^{n-1}b)^{2}a^{n-1}\in\hbox{\rm Fac}(w_{n})
,

\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w_{n}}(a^{n-1}b)=\{v_{2}[i,j]|1\leq i<j\leq 3n-1,j-i\geq 2n-1\}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w_{n}}(a^{n-1}ba^{n}b)

\displaystyle=\{w_{n}[i,j]|1\leq i\leq 2n+1,4n+2\leq j\leq 6n+2,j-i\geq 4n+1\}

\displaystyle\cup\{w_{n}[i,j]|1\leq i\leq 2n+1,6n+3\leq j\leq 9n+3\}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathrm{RP}(w_{n})

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w_{n}}(a)|+|\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w_{n}}(a^{n-1}ba)|+|\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w_{n}}(a^{n-1}b)|

\displaystyle+|\mathbb{Q}\mathrm{Class}_{w_{n}}(a^{n-1}ba^{n}b)|

\displaystyle=(n-1)+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{(2n+1)(2n+2)}{2}+(2n+1)(3n+1)

\displaystyle=9n^{2}+9n+1=\frac{|w|^{2}}{9}-O(|w|).

6
Conclusion

In this work, we prove that
\mathrm{RP}(n)
the maximum number of distinct rational powers in a word of length
n
satisfies
\frac{n^{2}}{9}-O(n)\leq\mathrm{RP}(n)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{8}
. However, a computer simulation suggests that
\mathrm{RP}(n)\sim 0.115n^{2}
. Thus, both upper bound and lower bound of
\mathrm{RP}(n)
may be improved.
