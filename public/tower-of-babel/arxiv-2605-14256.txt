Worst-Case Sample Complexity Bounds for Distributed Inner Product Estimation with Local Randomized Measurements
Zhenyuan Huang, Kun Wang, Ping Xu

Abstract
We study distributed inner product estimation for n n -qubit states using local randomized measurements, for which rigorous worst-case guarantees are less understood. We first reduce the minimax kernel optimization to Hamming-distance kernels. Within this class, unbiasedness fixes a unique kernel. For this kernel under local Clifford sampling, we prove a sharp fourth-moment bound using the single-qubit Clifford commutant. This yields worst-case sample complexity 𝒪 ⁡ ( 4.5 n ) \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{4.5^{n}}) , attained by identical pure product stabilizer states. For the same kernel under local Haar sampling, we prove a local twirling identity that compares its fourth moment with the Clifford fourth moment. This gives the same rigorous upper bound as in the Clifford case, but the comparison is lossy. This motivates the conjectured sharper Haar scaling 𝒪 ⁡ ( 3.6 n ) \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{3.6^{n}}) attained by product states, and verify it for several important classes of states. We also show that independent single-qubit Pauli shadows have worst-case scaling 𝒪 ⁡ ( 7.5 n ) \mathcal{O}(\sqrt{7.5^{n}}) for large n n .

Distributed inner product estimation (DIPE)

\rho

The optimal sample complexity of DIPE was established in

The problem addressed in this work is to establish rigorous worst-case sample

LRM is a practically significant variant of the randomized measurement
toolbox

n

This differs significantly from global randomized measurements,
where one samples a single
n
-qubit unitary,

LRM is therefore the experimentally favorable setting for distributed architectures:

across the full system

11

Despite this practical appeal, the theoretical picture remains incomplete.
Elben

8

et al.

provides a sample complexity analysis.
Zheng

state-dependent sample complexity bounds for structured random circuits and
related ensembles, while Wu
et al.

17

upper bound
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{6^{n}})

These results do not determine the optimal post-processing kernel under
worst-case variance, nor do they identify the tight worst-case sample
complexity of DIPE with LRM.
This gap substantially limits the theoretical understanding of DIPE with LRM
and, in turn, impedes its reliable practical use.

In this work, we address these gaps in three steps.
First, we identify the unique unbiased Hamming-distance kernel
selected by the worst-case symmetry reduction.
Then, we derive worst-case guarantees for local Clifford and Haar sampling
based on this unique kernel.
Third, we compare these shared-randomness protocols with independent
single-qubit Pauli shadows.

II
DIPE with Local Randomized Measurements

We recall the standard kernel estimator formalism for DIPE specialized to the LRM setting.
This subsection reviews standard material;
the new results begin in the following sections.

Let
\rho_{\bm{A}},\sigma_{\bm{B}}\in\mathscr{D}((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n})
.
In one round of local DIPE, Alice and Bob apply the same random product unitary

\displaystyle U=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}U_{\ell},

(1)

where each
U_{\ell}
is sampled independently from a single-qubit ensemble
\mathcal{U}_{1}
, and then measure in the computational basis, obtaining bit strings
\bm{s},\bm{t}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. A kernel estimator is specified by a function

\displaystyle f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{0,1\}^{n}\times\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}.

(2)

Given
N_{U}
independent unitary samples and
N_{M}
repeated shots for each unitary sample, the empirical estimator reads

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{f}(\rho_{\bm{A}},\sigma_{\bm{B}})=\frac{1}{N_{U}N_{M}^{2}}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{U}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N_{M}}f(\bm{s}_{l,j},\bm{t}_{l,k}).

(3)

Since each shot consumes one copy of
\rho
and one copy of
\sigma
, the total number of copies used per party is
N\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=N_{U}N_{M}
; throughout, we refer to this quantity as the sample complexity. All sample complexity statements below refer to sufficient copy guarantees obtained from variance bounds via Chebyshev’s inequality. In particular, we do not prove matching minimax lower bounds for these estimators or protocols.

The averaged estimator can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{f}(\rho_{\bm{A}},\sigma_{\bm{B}})]=\tr\left[(\rho_{\bm{A}}\otimes\sigma_{\bm{B}})\,\bar{\Omega}_{f}\right],

(4)

where

\displaystyle\bar{\Omega}_{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{U}\!\left[(U^{\dagger}\otimes U^{\dagger})\Omega_{f}(U\otimes U)\right],

(5)

with

\displaystyle\Omega_{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\bm{s},\bm{t}}f(\bm{s},\bm{t})|{\bm{s}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{s}}|_{\bm{A}}\otimes|{\bm{t}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{t}}|_{\bm{B}}.

(6)

Thus unbiased estimation of
\tr\left[\rho\sigma\right]
is equivalent to the operator identity

\bar{\Omega}_{f}=\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}

denotes the full swap between the two
n
-qubit copies
\bm{A}
and
\bm{B}
.

To expose the variance-based copy scaling, it is convenient to group the data by unitary sample. Define

\displaystyle X_{M}^{(l)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{N_{M}^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N_{M}}f(\bm{s}_{l,j},\bm{t}_{l,k}),\qquad\widehat{X}_{f}=\frac{1}{N_{U}}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{U}}X_{M}^{(l)}.

(7)

Let
X_{M}
denote a generic copy of the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables
X_{M}^{(1)},\dots,X_{M}^{(N_{U})}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[\widehat{X}_{f}]=\frac{1}{N_{U}}\mathbb{V}[X_{M}].

(8)

Applying the law of total variance and classifying the coincidences among the shot indices yields the decomposition into four terms

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[X_{M}]=\mathbb{V}^{(1)}+\mathbb{V}^{(2)}+\mathbb{V}^{(3)}+\mathbb{V}^{(4)},

(9)

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\tr\left[\rho\sigma\right]^{2},

(10a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{M}^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{U}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s},\bm{t}\mid U}[f(\bm{s},\bm{t})^{2}],

(10b)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{U}\!\left[\Xi_{U}(\rho,\sigma)+\Xi_{U}(\sigma,\rho)\right],

(10c)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}_{U}[(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s},\bm{t}\mid U}[f(\bm{s},\bm{t})])^{2}].

(10d)

In the above, for a fixed unitary sample
U
,
\bm{s},\bm{s}^{\prime}
are independent outcomes from measuring
U\rho U^{\dagger}
in the computational basis,
\bm{t},\bm{t}^{\prime}
are independent outcomes from measuring
U\sigma U^{\dagger}
.

\Xi_{U}(\rho,\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s},\bm{t}\mid U}\![f(\bm{s},\bm{t})\,\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s}^{\prime}\mid U}f(\bm{s}^{\prime},\bm{t})]

and the quantity
\Xi_{U}(\sigma,\rho)
is defined analogously, with the roles of
\rho
and
\sigma
exchanged.
The derivation of the variance decomposition is given in Appx.
A
.

For any unbiased estimator, Chebyshev’s inequality gives the failure probability bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\widehat{X}_{f}-\tr\left[\rho\sigma\right]|\geq\varepsilon)\leq\mathbb{V}[\widehat{X}_{f}]/\varepsilon^{2}.

(11)

Therefore, for a target failure probability
\delta\in(0,1)
, using
\mathbb{V}[\widehat{X}_{f}]=\mathbb{V}[X_{M}]/N_{U}
,
a sufficient condition is

\displaystyle N\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=N_{U}N_{M}\geq N_{M}\mathbb{V}[X_{M}]/(\delta\varepsilon^{2}).

(12)

Thus worst-case sample complexity bounds follow by optimizing the variance term
in Eq. (
12
).

III
Main Results for DIPE with Local Randomized Measurements

We now present the main theoretical results of this work. We first characterize the optimal kernel structure
under worst-case variance optimization,
and then derive the resulting Chebyshev-based sufficient-copy guarantees
for the single-qubit Clifford and single-qubit Haar ensembles.

III.1
Optimal Hamming distance kernels

We first show that the kernel optimization problem admits a sharp symmetry reduction. All proofs in this subsection are collected in Appx.
B
.

Lemma 1
.

Assume that the local sampling protocol is covariant under the action of

\displaystyle\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(S_{2})^{n}\rtimes S_{n},

(13)

where
\rtimes
denotes the semidirect product for the natural action of
S_{n}
on
(S_{2})^{n}
by permuting the
n
factors.
That is,
\Gamma
is generated by local outcome relabelings and qubit permutations.
Then, for every admissible kernel
f
, its symmetrization

\displaystyle f^{\mathrm{sym}}(\bm{s},\bm{t})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi\sim S_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim(S_{2})^{n}}\left[f(\tau(\pi(\bm{s})),\tau(\pi(\bm{t})))\right]

(14)

depends only on the Hamming distance
D(\bm{s},\bm{t})
and satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{f^{\mathrm{sym}}}]\leq\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{f}].

(15)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\inf_{f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{\Omega}_{f}=\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}}\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{f}]=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}g=g(D)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\\
\bar{\Omega}_{g}=\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{g}].

(16)

The covariance requirement above is natural in the local i.i.d. setting. Qubit permutation covariance follows automatically from exchangeability of the independently sampled single-qubit unitaries. Local bit-flip covariance further requires invariance of the one qubit sampling law under left multiplication by Pauli
X
, which holds for both single-qubit Haar and uniform single-qubit Clifford sampling studied in this work.

Lem.
1
reduces the kernel search space from
4^{n}
entries to the
n+1
values
g(0),\dots,g(n)
. We now show that unbiasedness removes even this residual freedom.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f(\bm{s},\bm{t})=g(D(\bm{s},\bm{t}))
be a Hamming distance kernel. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

\widehat{X}_{f}
is unbiased for
\tr\left[\rho\sigma\right]
for all states
\rho_{\bm{A}}

and
\sigma_{\bm{B}}
;

2.

\bar{\Omega}_{f}=\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}
;

3.

\forall d=0,1,\dots,n
,
g(d)=(-1)^{d}2^{n-d}
.

As a consequence, the unique unbiased kernel in the Hamming distance class is

\displaystyle f(\bm{s},\bm{t})=(-1)^{D(\bm{s},\bm{t})}2^{n-D(\bm{s},\bm{t})}=\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\left(3\delta_{s_{\ell},t_{\ell}}-1\right).

(17)

The kernel in Eq. (
17
) is exactly the local estimator
introduced in
8
. Thus Lem.
1

and Thm.
2
show that this estimator is not an ad hoc
choice: within the Hamming distance class singled out by the worst-case
symmetry reduction, it is the unique unbiased kernel.

III.2
DIPE with single-qubit Clifford ensemble

The single-qubit Clifford ensemble is experimentally attractive and forms an
exact unitary
3
-design
16
.
Practically, single-qubit Clifford randomized measurements are implemented
by choosing, independently for each qubit, one of the Pauli
X
,
Y
, and
Z

bases uniformly at random and measuring in that basis.
All discussions and proofs in this subsection
are collected in Appx.
C
.

Since the single-qubit Clifford ensemble form an unitary
3
-design,
for the unique kernel in Eq. (
17
),
the terms defined in Eq. (
10
) can be analytically calculated as

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(1)}_{{\rm Cl}}

\displaystyle=-\tr\left[\rho\sigma\right]^{2},

(18a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(2)}_{{\rm Cl}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{M}^{2}}\tr\left[(2\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F})^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma)\right],

(18b)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{\rm Cl}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}^{2}}\left(\operatorname{Tr}[R_{AA^{\prime}B}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)]\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\;+\left.\operatorname{Tr}[R_{ABB^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\sigma)]\right),

(18c)

where
{\rm Cl}
denotes Clifford sampling and

\displaystyle R_{AA^{\prime}B}=\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AA^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AB}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{A^{\prime}B}-\mathbb{I},

(19)

\displaystyle R_{ABB^{\prime}}=\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{F}_{BB^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AB}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AB^{\prime}}-\mathbb{I}.

(20)

The genuinely Clifford-specific input enters only through the fourth term,
where the failure of the
4
-design property becomes essential.
To evaluate
\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{{\rm Cl}}
, we invoke the recent complete characterization of the Clifford commutant
3
. For one qubit, uniform Clifford conjugation maps
Z
to a uniformly random element of
\{\pm X,\pm Y,\pm Z\}
, so the single-qubit Clifford fourth moment operator can be written exactly as

\displaystyle R_{4,{\rm Cl}}=-\mathbb{I}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{F}_{A_{1}B_{1}}+\mathbb{F}_{A_{2}B_{2}}\right)+\frac{3}{2}\,\Omega_{4}^{(1)},

(21)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{4}^{(1)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{I}^{\otimes 4}+X^{\otimes 4}+Y^{\otimes 4}+Z^{\otimes 4}\right)

(22)

is the one qubit Clifford commutant generator beyond the permutation sector.
Since the local Cliffords are sampled independently, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{{\rm Cl}}=\left(\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\right)^{2}\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right].

(23)

We prove the following state independent upper bound.

Theorem 3
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\max_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{D}((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n})}\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]=\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(24)

Notably, the maximum is attained by a pair of
identical pure product stabilizer states.

In view of Eq. (
23
), Thm.
3
immediately implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{{\rm Cl}}\leq\left(\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(25)

Together with the state-independent upper bounds

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(2)}_{{\rm Cl}}\leq\frac{3^{n}}{N_{M}^{2}},\qquad\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{{\rm Cl}}\leq 2\,\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}^{2}}\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n},

(26)

this yields the following sufficient-copy corollary.

Corollary 4
.

Let
\widehat{X}_{f}
be the estimator in Eq. (
3
) built from the unique kernel in Thm.
2
.
Under single-qubit Clifford sampling,
for arbitrary
n
-qubit states
\rho
and
\sigma
, the guarantee

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\widehat{X}_{f}-\tr\left[\rho\sigma\right]|\geq\varepsilon)\leq\delta

(27)

holds with worst-case sample complexity per party satisfying

\displaystyle N_{\star,{\rm Cl}}\leq\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{4.5^{n}}}{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

(28)

This bound is minimized by choosing
N_{M}=\Theta(2^{n/2})
.

Cor.
4
resolves the open problem raised in
(
19
, Theorem 13)
,
which established an
O(\sqrt{4.5^{n}})
behavior for product stabilizer states under local Clifford sampling.
Our result closes this gap by proving a worst-case guarantee with the same scaling for arbitrary
n
-qubit states.

III.3
DIPE with single-qubit Haar ensemble

The single-qubit Haar ensemble is an exact unitary
t
-design for every positive integer
t

16
.
Accordingly, the first three terms in the variance decomposition coincide with those of the single-qubit Clifford ensemble in Eq. (
18
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(r)}_{\mathrm{H}}=\mathbb{V}^{(r)}_{{\rm Cl}},\qquad r=1,2,3.

(29)

The only nontrivial change occurs in the fourth term, where the full
4
-design property becomes relevant. All proofs in this subsection
are collected in Appx.
D
.

The fourth term for the Haar ensemble is

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{H}}=\left(\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\right)^{2}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right],

(30)

where
R_{4,\mathrm{H}}
is the single-qubit Haar fourth moment operator whose explicit form is derived in Appx.
D
.
We show that the single-qubit Haar fourth moment
is the Haar average of conjugates of the single-qubit Clifford fourth moment.
Taking tensor products yields the desired
n
-qubit identity.

Theorem 5
.

For
n
qubits,

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{W}\sim\nu^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\bm{W}^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\bm{W}^{\otimes 4}\right],

(31)

where
\nu
denotes the single-qubit Haar measure and
\bm{W}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=W_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes W_{n}
with
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
sampled independently from
\nu
.
Consequently, for arbitrary
n
-qubit states
\rho
and
\sigma
,

\displaystyle\max_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{D}((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n})}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(32)

In view of Eq. (
30
), Thm.
5
implies that the Haar fourth term obeys the same worst-case bound as the Clifford fourth term. Since the first three variance terms also coincide, the same Chebyshev-based sufficient-copy upper bound given in Cor.
4
follows as well.

Corollary 6
.

Let
\widehat{X}_{f}
be the estimator in Eq. (
3
) built from the unique kernel in Thm.
2
. Under single-qubit Haar sampling, the same worst-case additive-error guarantee as in Cor.
4
holds. In particular,

\displaystyle N_{\star,\mathrm{H}}\leq\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{4.5^{n}}}{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

(33)

This bound is minimized by choosing
N_{M}=\Theta(2^{n/2})
.

However,
Eq. (
32
) is not saturated in general,
and thus the bound in Cor.
6
should be viewed as a comparison sufficient-copy bound rather than a sharp sample complexity characterization.
Indeed, Thm.
5
expresses every Haar value as a
\nu^{\otimes n}
average of the corresponding Clifford value over the full local unitary orbit. Since each integrand is bounded by
(3/2)^{n}
from Thm.
3
, equality in Eq. (
32
) would force the Clifford value to equal
(3/2)^{n}
for
\nu^{\otimes n}
-almost every local rotation. The twirling step is therefore strictly lossy: it averages the Clifford fourth moment over a continuum of locally rotated states instead of evaluating it on a fixed Clifford extremizer. Notably, this loss is already visible from the exact Haar
value
6/5<3/2
in the single-qubit case.

Conjectured tighter bound.

It is therefore natural to look for sharper benchmarks.
A sharper benchmark may be obtained by
evaluating this optimization on identical pure product states of the form

|{\psi}\rangle=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}|{\psi_{\ell}}\rangle
, with each

|{\psi_{\ell}}\rangle
a single-qubit pure state:

\displaystyle\max_{\psi=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}\psi_{\ell}}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right]=\left(\frac{6}{5}\right)^{n}.

(34)

This product state benchmark, together with the Clifford result that the
fourth-moment upper-bound scale is saturated by pure product stabilizer states,
motivates the following conjecture on the tighter
worst-case Chebyshev-based sufficient-copy upper bound for the Haar case.

Conjecture 7
.

Let
\widehat{X}_{f}
be the estimator in Eq. (
3
) built from the unique kernel in Thm.
2
.
Under single-qubit Haar sampling,
for arbitrary
n
-qubit states
\rho
and
\sigma
, the guarantee

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\widehat{X}_{f}-\tr\left[\rho\sigma\right]|\geq\varepsilon)\leq\delta

(35)

holds with worst-case sample complexity per party satisfying

\displaystyle N_{\star,\mathrm{H}}\leq\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{3.6^{n}}}{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

(36)

This bound is minimized by choosing
N_{M}=\Theta((5/2)^{n/2})
.

Appx.
D
proves the conjecture on several supporting classes:
arbitrary product state pairs, the complete case
n=1
, the identical pure
sector for
n=2
, pure biseparable states for
n=3
, and the GHZ,
W
, and
Bell-dimer families. The general case remains open. For fixed
N_{M}
, the main
obstruction is the tradeoff between
\mathbb{V}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{H}}
and

\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{H}}
. Entanglement can enhance the fourth variance
term, but it can also suppress the second variance term through reduced
subsystem purities. For example, a two-qubit Bell state has a larger Haar
fourth-moment contribution than a two-qubit product state, and tensor products
of Bell pairs amplify this fourth-moment enhancement. However, the accompanying
decrease in
\mathbb{V}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{H}}
prevents this enhancement from
directly improving the optimized sample complexity bound. This tradeoff is why
the fourth-moment contribution alone is not the right optimization target.
The detailed proofs, examples, and arguments are collected in
Appx.
D
.

IV
DIPE with independent single-qubit classical shadow

For comparison with the shared-randomness LRM protocols analyzed above,
we also consider an independent local Pauli shadow estimator,

\displaystyle\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{P}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr\left[\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\rho}_{i}\right)\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{j}\right)\right],

(37)

where
\hat{\rho}_{i}
and
\hat{\sigma}_{j}
are independent Pauli shadow
snapshots of
\rho
and
\sigma

12
, respectively.
This estimator was experimentally investigated in
21
, but without a variance-based copy analysis.
We derive its Chebyshev-based worst-case sufficient copy bound below; the proof is deferred to Appx.
E
.

Proposition 8
.

For arbitrary
n
-qubit states
\rho
and
\sigma
, the estimator

VI
Conclusions

We established worst-case sample complexity bounds for distributed inner product
estimation with local randomized measurements and identified the kernel selected
by the worst-case framework. The kernel optimization can be reduced to Hamming
distance kernels, and unbiasedness in this class fixes the unique kernel.
The Clifford and Haar analyses share the same first three variance terms
because both single-qubit ensembles are unitary
3
-designs, but their
fourth-moment structures are different and must be treated separately. In the
single-qubit Clifford case, the Clifford commutant gives a sharp fourth-moment
bound and hence worst-case scaling
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{4.5^{n}})
, with extremizers given
by identical pure product stabilizer states. In the single-qubit Haar case, a
local twirling relation gives the same rigorous upper bound as the Clifford
case, but this comparison bound is not attainable. Identical pure product
states instead yield the sharper conjectured sample complexity scaling

\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{3.6^{n}})
. We proved the conjecture on several supporting classes:
arbitrary product state pairs, the complete one-qubit case, identical pure
two-qubit pairs, pure biseparable states for
n=3
, and the GHZ,
W
, and
Bell-dimer families.
For comparison, we showed that the independent local Pauli-shadow protocol has worse
worst-case scaling
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{7.5^{n}})
, already attained by identical pure
product states. This separation highlights shared randomness as a dominant
resource for DIPE with LRM.
The main results in this work are summarized in Tab.
1

for reference and comparison.

Two questions remain open. The most important one is to prove or disprove
Conj.
7
beyond the cases established here.
The main difficulty is the tradeoff exposed in this work:
entanglement can increase the Haar fourth-moment contribution
while decreasing the universal second-moment contribution.
The second question is to derive matching lower bounds for DIPE with LRM,
which would determine whether the present local protocols are worst-case optimal.

and

B.1
Proof of Lem.
1

Let
\Gamma=(S_{2})^{n}\rtimes S_{n}
act on pairs
(\bm{s},\bm{t})
by simultaneous relabeling and permutation. For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, write

\displaystyle f^{\gamma}(\bm{s},\bm{t})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f(\gamma(\bm{s}),\gamma(\bm{t})).

(61)

Then
f^{\mathrm{sym}}=\mathbb{E}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f^{\gamma}
. Two pairs lie in the same orbit under
\Gamma
if and only if they have the same Hamming distance. Hence the orbit average in Eq. (
14
) depends only on
D(\bm{s},\bm{t})
.

The covariance property implies that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
there exists a unitary
W_{\gamma}
such that

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{f^{\gamma}}(\rho,\sigma)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\widehat{X}_{f}(W_{\gamma}\rho W_{\gamma}^{\dagger},W_{\gamma}\sigma W_{\gamma}^{\dagger}).

(62)

Assume now that
f
is admissible. Taking expectations in the preceding distributional identity gives, for all states
\rho
and
\sigma
,

\displaystyle\tr\left[(\rho\otimes\sigma)\bar{\Omega}_{f^{\gamma}}\right]

\displaystyle=\tr\left[((W_{\gamma}\rho W_{\gamma}^{\dagger})\otimes(W_{\gamma}\sigma W_{\gamma}^{\dagger}))\bar{\Omega}_{f}\right]

(63)

\displaystyle=\tr\left[(\rho\otimes\sigma)(W_{\gamma}^{\dagger}\otimes W_{\gamma}^{\dagger})\bar{\Omega}_{f}(W_{\gamma}\otimes W_{\gamma})\right]

(64)

\displaystyle=\tr\left[(\rho\otimes\sigma)\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}\right],

(65)

where the last step uses
\bar{\Omega}_{f}=\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}
and the invariance of the full swap under simultaneous conjugation by
W_{\gamma}\otimes W_{\gamma}
. Since this holds for all
\rho
and
\sigma
,
\bar{\Omega}_{f^{\gamma}}=\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}
, and by linearity
\bar{\Omega}_{f^{\mathrm{sym}}}=\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}
. Thus symmetrization maps admissible kernels to admissible Hamming-distance kernels.

Variance is convex under averaging of estimators, so for any fixed
(\rho,\sigma)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{f^{\mathrm{sym}}}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{f^{\gamma}}].

(66)

Taking the supremum and using the bijectivity of
(\rho,\sigma)\mapsto(W_{\gamma}\rho W_{\gamma}^{\dagger},W_{\gamma}\sigma W_{\gamma}^{\dagger})
gives

\displaystyle\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{f^{\mathrm{sym}}}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{f^{\gamma}}]=\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{f}],

(67)

where the last step again uses the covariance identity above. Let

\displaystyle V(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{\rho,\sigma}\mathbb{V}_{\rho,\sigma}[\widehat{X}_{f}].

(68)

For every admissible
f
, we have constructed an admissible Hamming-distance kernel

with

, so

\displaystyle\inf_{g=g(D)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{\Omega}_{g}=\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}}V(g)\leq\inf_{f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{\Omega}_{f}=\mathbb{F}_{\bm{AB}}}V(f).

(69)

The reverse inequality is immediate because admissible Hamming-distance kernels are a subclass of all admissible kernels. This proves Eq. (
16

B.2
Proof of Thm.
2

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(g)=0,\qquad\alpha_{n}(g)=1.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{V}[O_{1}(V)^{2}]=\frac{5}{2}\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{2\mathbb{F}-\mathbb{I}}{3}=2\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F}.

(86)

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(2)}_{{\rm Cl}}=\frac{1}{N_{M}^{2}}\tr\left[(2\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F})^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma)\right],

(87)

which is Eq. (
18b
). Since the eigenvalues of
2\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F}
are
3,3,3,1

\|2\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F}\|_{\infty}=3

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(2)}_{{\rm Cl}}\leq\frac{3^{n}}{N_{M}^{2}}.

(88)

This is the bound stated in Eq. (

),
and is attained by any identical pure product state; for example,

\rho=\sigma=|{0}\rangle\!\langle{0}|^{\otimes n}
gives equality because
(2\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F})|{00}\rangle=3|{00}\rangle
.

The third term
\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{{\rm Cl}}
.

We derive the operator form of
\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{{\rm Cl}}
directly from

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{{\rm Cl}}=\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{U}\!\left[\Xi_{U}(\rho,\sigma)+\Xi_{U}(\sigma,\rho)\right].

(89)

Define

\displaystyle K_{b}(V)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}h(a,b)\,P_{a}(V)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}(-1)^{b}A(V).

(90)

Since
h(a,b)=h(b,a)

\displaystyle K_{a}(V)=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}h(a,b)\,P_{b}(V).

(91)

For a product sample
U=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}U_{\ell}
and a bit string
\bm{b}\in\{0,1\}^{n}

\displaystyle K_{\bm{b}}(U)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}K_{b_{\ell}}(U_{\ell}),\qquad P_{\bm{b}}(U)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=U^{\dagger}|{\bm{b}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{b}}|U.

(92)

Then the first ordered contraction is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{U}[\Xi_{U}(\rho,\sigma)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{U}\sum_{\bm{b}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\tr\left[P_{\bm{b}}(U)\sigma\right]\left(\sum_{\bm{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}f(\bm{a},\bm{b})\,\tr\left[P_{\bm{a}}(U)\rho\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{U}\sum_{\bm{b}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\tr\left[K_{\bm{b}}(U)\rho\right]^{2}\tr\left[P_{\bm{b}}(U)\sigma\right]

\displaystyle=\tr\left[R_{AA^{\prime}B}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right],

(93)

where

\displaystyle R_{AA^{\prime}B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{V}\sum_{b=0}^{1}K_{b}(V)_{A}\otimes K_{b}(V)_{A^{\prime}}\otimes P_{b}(V)_{B}.

(94)

Likewise, the second ordered contraction is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{U}[\Xi_{U}(\sigma,\rho)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{U}\sum_{\bm{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\tr\left[P_{\bm{a}}(U)\rho\right]\left(\sum_{\bm{b}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}f(\bm{a},\bm{b})\,\tr\left[P_{\bm{b}}(U)\sigma\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{U}\sum_{\bm{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\tr\left[P_{\bm{a}}(U)\rho\right]\tr\left[K_{\bm{a}}(U)\sigma\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\tr\left[R_{ABB^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\sigma)\right],

(95)

with

\displaystyle R_{ABB^{\prime}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{V}\sum_{a=0}^{1}P_{a}(V)_{A}\otimes K_{a}(V)_{B}\otimes K_{a}(V)_{B^{\prime}}.

(96)

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{{\rm Cl}}=\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}^{2}}\left(\tr\left[R_{AA^{\prime}B}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]+\tr\left[R_{ABB^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\sigma)\right]\right),

(97)

which is Eq. (
18c
) in MT.

Now we compute state independent upper bound on
\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{{\rm Cl}}
.
We first compute the one qubit operators
R_{AA^{\prime}B}
and
R_{ABB^{\prime}}
explicitly.
Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{b=0}^{1}K_{b}(V)\otimes K_{b}(V)\otimes P_{b}(V)=\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{I}^{\otimes 3}+\frac{9}{4}A(V)\otimes A(V)\otimes\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{4}A(V)\otimes\mathbb{I}\otimes A(V)+\frac{3}{4}\mathbb{I}\otimes A(V)\otimes A(V).

(98)

Averaging and using Eq. (
80
) gives

\displaystyle R_{AA^{\prime}B}=-\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AA^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AB}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{A^{\prime}B},

(99)

and, by the same calculation with the roles of
A
and
B
exchanged,

\displaystyle R_{ABB^{\prime}}=-\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{F}_{BB^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AB}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AB^{\prime}}.

(100)

A direct diagonalization yields the eigenvalues

\displaystyle\{3/2,3/2,3/2,3/2,0,0,-2,-2\}

(101)

for both
R_{AA^{\prime}B}
and
R_{ABB^{\prime}}
.

The operator norms of
R_{AA^{\prime}B}
and
R_{ABB^{\prime}}
are both
2

This yields a loose universal upper bound
2^{n}
on
\tr\left[R_{AA^{\prime}B}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]
.
We can further tighten this bound by exploiting the state structure.

Proposition 9
.

For any
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{D}((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{AA^{\prime}B}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{\bm{A}}\otimes\rho_{\bm{A}^{\prime}}\otimes\sigma_{\bm{B}})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n},

(102)

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{ABB^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{\bm{A}}\otimes\sigma_{\bm{B}}\otimes\sigma_{\bm{B}^{\prime}})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n}.

(103)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{{\rm Cl}}\leq 2\,\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}^{2}}\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n}.

(104)

Proof.

It suffices to prove Eq. (
102
); the proof of
Eq. (
103
) is identical after exchanging the
roles of the two repeated registers.

Let

\displaystyle\Pi_{-}^{AA^{\prime}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\mathbb{I}_{AA^{\prime}}-\mathbb{F}_{AA^{\prime}}}{2}

(105)

be the antisymmetric projector on the one-qubit pair
AA^{\prime}
, and let

\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{AA^{\prime}B}
be the projector onto the fully symmetric subspace
of the three one-qubit registers
AA^{\prime}B
. Since the local Hilbert space is
two-dimensional, the fully antisymmetric three-qubit representation is absent,
and

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{AA^{\prime}B}=\frac{1}{3}\left(\mathbb{F}_{AA^{\prime}}+\mathbb{F}_{AB}+\mathbb{F}_{A^{\prime}B}\right).

(106)

Using

\displaystyle R_{AAB}=-\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AA^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AB}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{A^{\prime}B},

(107)

we obtain the spectral decomposition

\displaystyle R_{AAB}=\frac{3}{2}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{AA^{\prime}B}-2\,\Pi_{-}^{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}.

(108)

The two projectors in Eq. (
108
) have orthogonal
supports, because the fully symmetric subspace is symmetric under

A\leftrightarrow A^{\prime}
, whereas
\Pi_{-}^{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}
is antisymmetric under

A\leftrightarrow A^{\prime}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left|{R_{AAB}}\right|=\frac{3}{2}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{AA^{\prime}B}+2\,\Pi_{-}^{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}.

(109)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathrm{sym}}^{AA^{\prime}B}\leq\mathbb{I}_{AA^{\prime}B}-\Pi_{-}^{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B},

(110)

and hence

\displaystyle\left|{R_{AAB}}\right|\leq\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{I}_{AA^{\prime}B}+\frac{1}{2}\,\Pi_{-}^{AA^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}.

(111)

Now set

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Pi_{-}^{A_{\ell}A^{\prime}_{\ell}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B_{\ell}},\qquad\ell=1,\ldots,n.

(112)

For the positive operator

X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho_{\bm{A}}\otimes\rho_{\bm{A}^{\prime}}\otimes\sigma_{\bm{B}}
,
we have

\displaystyle\left|{\tr\left[R_{AAB}^{\otimes n}X\right]}\right|\leq\tr\left[\left|{R_{AAB}^{\otimes n}}\right|X\right]=\tr\left[\left|{R_{AAB}}\right|^{\otimes n}X\right]\leq\tr\left[\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}\left(\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{I}_{A_{\ell}A^{\prime}_{\ell}B_{\ell}}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{\ell}\right)X\right],

(113)

where the last inequality uses Eq. (
111
) and positivity
under tensor products.

Expanding the product in Eq. (
113
) gives

\displaystyle\left|{\tr\left[R_{AAB}^{\otimes n}X\right]}\right|\leq\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n-|S|}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{|S|}\tr\left[\Pi_{-,S}^{\bm{A}\bm{A}^{\prime}}(\rho_{\bm{A}}\otimes\rho_{\bm{A}^{\prime}})\right],

(114)

where

\displaystyle\Pi_{-,S}^{\bm{A}\bm{A}^{\prime}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigotimes_{\ell\in S}\Pi_{-}^{A_{\ell}A^{\prime}_{\ell}}

(115)

with identity operators on the complementary tensor factors. If

\rho_{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr_{S^{c}}[\rho]
denotes the reduced state of
\rho
on the subsystem

S
, then

\displaystyle\tr\left[\Pi_{-,S}^{\bm{A}\bm{A}^{\prime}}(\rho_{\bm{A}}\otimes\rho_{\bm{A}^{\prime}})\right]=\tr\left[\left(\bigotimes_{\ell\in S}\Pi_{-}^{A_{\ell}A^{\prime}_{\ell}}\right)(\rho_{S}\otimes\rho_{S})\right].

(116)

Let
k\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|S|
. The projector

\bigotimes_{\ell\in S}\Pi_{-}^{A_{\ell}A^{\prime}_{\ell}}
is a rank-one maximally
entangled projector across the bipartition
A_{S}|A^{\prime}_{S}
. Thus there exists a
unitary
W_{S}
on
A^{\prime}_{S}
such that, with

\displaystyle|{\Phi_{S}}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=2^{-k/2}\sum_{\bm{x}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}|{\bm{x}}\rangle_{A_{S}}|{\bm{x}}\rangle_{A^{\prime}_{S}},

(117)

one has

\displaystyle\bigotimes_{\ell\in S}\Pi_{-}^{A_{\ell}A^{\prime}_{\ell}}=(\mathbb{I}_{A_{S}}\otimes W_{S})|{\Phi_{S}}\rangle\!\langle{\Phi_{S}}|(\mathbb{I}_{A_{S}}\otimes W_{S}^{\dagger}).

(118)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\tr\left[\left(\bigotimes_{\ell\in S}\Pi_{-}^{A_{\ell}A^{\prime}_{\ell}}\right)(\rho_{S}\otimes\rho_{S})\right]=2^{-k}\tr\left[\rho_{S}\,W_{S}\rho_{S}^{T}W_{S}^{\dagger}\right],

(119)

where the transpose is taken in the computational basis. Since

W_{S}\rho_{S}^{T}W_{S}^{\dagger}
is again a density operator,

\displaystyle\tr\left[\rho_{S}\,W_{S}\rho_{S}^{T}W_{S}^{\dagger}\right]\leq 1.

(120)

Combining this with Eq. (
116
) gives

\displaystyle\tr\left[\Pi_{-,S}^{\bm{A}\bm{A}^{\prime}}(\rho_{\bm{A}}\otimes\rho_{\bm{A}^{\prime}})\right]\leq 2^{-|S|}.

(121)

Substituting Eq. (
121
) into
Eq. (
114
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|{\tr\left[R_{AAB}^{\otimes n}X\right]}\right|\leq\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n-|S|}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{|S|}2^{-|S|}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n-k}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{k}=\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n}.

(122)

This proves Eq. (
102
). The same argument with

\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\sigma
and the antisymmetric projector on
BB^{\prime}
proves
Eq. (
103
). Finally,
Eq. (
104
) follows immediately from
Eq. (
18c
).
∎

C.2
Calculations of the fourth term

We compute the analytic forms of the single-qubit Clifford fourth-moment operator
R_{4,{\rm Cl}}
in Eq. (

) of MT.

Clifford fourth-moment operator.

For a product Clifford unitary
C=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}C_{\ell}
, define

\displaystyle O(C)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\bm{s},\bm{t}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}f(\bm{s},\bm{t})(C^{\dagger}|{\bm{s}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{s}}|C)\otimes(C^{\dagger}|{\bm{t}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{t}}|C).

(123)

Since
f(\bm{s},\bm{t})=\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}h(s_{\ell},t_{\ell})
and
C
is a product unitary, this factorizes as

\displaystyle O(C)=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}O_{1}(C_{\ell}),

(124)

where
O_{1}(\cdot)
is defined in Eq. (
81
).
Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s},\bm{t}\mid C}[f(\bm{s},\bm{t})]=\sum_{\bm{s},\bm{t}}f(\bm{s},\bm{t})\tr\left[C^{\dagger}|{\bm{s}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{s}}|C\,\rho\right]\tr\left[C^{\dagger}|{\bm{t}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{t}}|C\,\sigma\right]=\tr\left[O(C)(\rho\otimes\sigma)\right],

(125)

and hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{C}\!\left[\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s},\bm{t}\mid C}[f(\bm{s},\bm{t})]\right)^{2}\right]=\tr\left[\mathbb{E}_{C}\!\left[O(C)_{A_{1}B_{1}}\otimes O(C)_{A_{2}B_{2}}\right](\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right].

(126)

By independence of the single-qubit Clifford unitaries, the averaged operator factorizes as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{C}\!\left[O(C)_{A_{1}B_{1}}\otimes O(C)_{A_{2}B_{2}}\right]=R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n},

(127)

where the single-qubit local Clifford fourth-moment operator
R_{4,{\rm Cl}}
is defined as

\displaystyle R_{4,{\rm Cl}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{D}\!\left[O_{1}(D)_{A_{1}B_{1}}\otimes O_{1}(D)_{A_{2}B_{2}}\right]

(128)

with
D
a uniformly random single-qubit Clifford unitary.
For one qubit, uniform Clifford conjugation maps
Z
to a uniformly random Pauli from
\{\pm X,\pm Y,\pm Z\}
. Hence

\displaystyle O_{1}(C)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}P(C)\otimes P(C),

(129)

where
P(C)
is uniformly distributed over
\{\pm X,\pm Y,\pm Z\}
.
Averaging
O_{1}(C)\otimes O_{1}(C)
over the one-qubit Clifford group yields the
one-qubit operator
R_{4,{\rm Cl}}
appearing above; explicitly,

\displaystyle R_{4,{\rm Cl}}=\mathbb{E}_{{\rm Cl}}[O_{1}(C)\otimes O_{1}(C)]=-\mathbb{I}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{F}_{A_{1}B_{1}}+\mathbb{F}_{A_{2}B_{2}}\right)+\frac{3}{2}\,\Omega_{4}^{(1)},

(130)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{4}^{(1)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{I}^{\otimes 4}+X^{\otimes 4}+Y^{\otimes 4}+Z^{\otimes 4}\right).

(131)

Note that the appearance of
\Omega_{4}^{(1)}
reflects the nontrivial
k=4
Clifford commutant described in Ref.
3
.

C.3
Proof of Thm.
3

We now prove Thm.
3
of MT
via the following three steps:

1.

Sec.
C.3.1
(containing Prop.
10
)
shows that the maximum in Eq. (
24
)
is attained by a pair of identical pure states.

2.

Sec.
C.3.2
(containing Prop.
11
)
then shows that, within this pure state optimization, an optimizer may be chosen to be a pure stabilizer state.

3.

Finally, Sec.
C.3.3
(containing Prop.
12
) proves the sharp upper bound
(3/2)^{n}
over the stabilizer family, with equality attained by pure product stabilizer states.

We now establish these three ingredients in turn.

C.3.1
Fourth-moment optimum occurs for identical pure states

Proposition 10
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\max_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{D}((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n})}\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]=\max_{|{\psi}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}}\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right].

(132)

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\mu_{C}(\rho,\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s},\bm{t}\mid C}[f(\bm{s},\bm{t})].

(133)

Then

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{C}[\mu_{C}(\rho,\sigma)^{2}].

(134)

For every fixed product Clifford sample
C
and every fixed state
\sigma
,
the quantity
\mu_{C}(\rho,\sigma)
is affine in
\rho
.
Therefore, for
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathscr{D}((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n})
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\;\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}((\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2})\otimes\sigma\otimes(\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2})\otimes\sigma)\right]

(135)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathbb{E}_{C}\!\left[\left(\lambda\mu_{C}(\rho_{1},\sigma)+(1-\lambda)\mu_{C}(\rho_{2},\sigma)\right)^{2}\right]

(136)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\lambda\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{1}\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho_{1}\otimes\sigma)\right]+(1-\lambda)\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{2}\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\sigma)\right],

(137)

because
x\mapsto x^{2}
is convex. Hence, for fixed
\sigma
, the fourth-moment
objective is convex in
\rho
on the compact convex set of density operators,
so its maximum is attained at a pure state. The same argument applies to the
second argument. Therefore a maximizing pair may be chosen pure.

It remains to show that the two pure states may be chosen identical. For a fixed product Clifford sample
C
and state
\tau
, define

\displaystyle p_{C}^{\tau}(\bm{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr\left[C^{\dagger}|{\bm{s}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{s}}|C\,\tau\right],\qquad\widehat{p}_{C}^{\tau}(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\bm{s}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}(-1)^{\sum_{\ell\in S}s_{\ell}}p_{C}^{\tau}(\bm{s}).

(138)

Here
p_{C}^{\tau}
is the computational basis measurement distribution of
\tau
after applying
C
, and
\widehat{p}_{C}^{\tau}(S)
is its Walsh coefficient on
S\subseteq[n]
. Expanding the product kernel in Eq. (
17
) in the Walsh basis gives

\displaystyle\mu_{C}(\rho,\sigma)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\frac{3^{|S|}}{2^{n}}\,\widehat{p}_{C}^{\rho}(S)\,\widehat{p}_{C}^{\sigma}(S),

(139)

where
p_{C}^{\tau}
and
\widehat{p}_{C}^{\tau}
are defined above. Since all weights
3^{|S|}/2^{n}
are nonnegative, the weighted Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality imply

\displaystyle\mu_{C}(\rho,\sigma)^{2}\leq\mu_{C}(\rho,\rho)\,\mu_{C}(\sigma,\sigma)\leq\frac{\mu_{C}(\rho,\rho)^{2}+\mu_{C}(\sigma,\sigma)^{2}}{2}.

(140)

Averaging over
C
yields

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]\leq\frac{\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\rho^{\otimes 4}\right]+\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\sigma^{\otimes 4}\right]}{2}\leq\max\left\{\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\rho^{\otimes 4}\right],\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\sigma^{\otimes 4}\right]\right\}.

(141)

Applying this to a maximizing pure state pair shows that at least one identical pure state pair also attains the global maximum.
∎

C.3.2
Extremizers of the pure state optimization

By Prop.
10
, it remains to optimize over identical pure inputs.
We now show that, in this pure state optimization, the maximum can be attained within the
pure stabilizer family
.

For one qubit, define

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}(a,b)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}1,&a=b=I,\\
1,&a=I,\ b\in\{X,Y,Z\}\ \text{or}\ b=I,\ a\in\{X,Y,Z\},\\
3,&a=b\in\{X,Y,Z\},\\
0,&a,b\in\{X,Y,Z\},\ a\neq b,\end{cases}

(142)

and for
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
set

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}(P,Q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\kappa_{1}(P_{\ell},Q_{\ell}).

(143)

The exact Pauli basis formula for the Clifford fourth moment can be written as

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum_{P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\kappa_{n}(P,Q)\widehat{\rho}(P)\widehat{\sigma}(P)\widehat{\rho}(Q)\widehat{\sigma}(Q).

(144)

Specialize to identical pure inputs,
\rho=\sigma=|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|
. Define

\displaystyle X_{\psi}(P)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widehat{\rho}(P)^{2},\qquad\Xi_{\psi}(P)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=2^{-n}X_{\psi}(P).

(145)

Here
\Xi_{\psi}
is the Pauli squared characteristic distribution of
|{\psi}\rangle
. By Pauli orthogonality, purity, and the bound
|\widehat{\rho}(P)|\leq 1
, one has

\displaystyle 0\leq\Xi_{\psi}(P)\leq 2^{-n},\qquad\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\Xi_{\psi}(P)=1.

(146)

Hence

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\rho^{\otimes 4})\right]=\sum_{P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\Xi_{\psi}(P)\Xi_{\psi}(Q)\kappa_{n}(P,Q).

(147)

Since the one qubit kernel matrix associated with
\kappa_{1}
is positive semidefinite, so is
\kappa_{n}=\kappa_{1}^{\otimes n}
; therefore Eq. (
147
) is a convex quadratic functional of the probability vector
\Xi_{\psi}
.

Proposition 11
.

In the optimization

\displaystyle\max_{|{\psi}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}}\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|^{\otimes 4})\right],

(148)

there exists an optimal state
|{\psi_{*}}\rangle
that is a pure stabilizer state.

Proof.

Ref.
10
shows that the characteristic distribution of any pure state belongs to the convex hull of the characteristic distributions of pure stabilizer states. Hence, for every pure state
|{\psi}\rangle
, there exist pure stabilizer states
|{\phi_{j}}\rangle
and probabilities
\lambda_{j}
such that

\displaystyle\Xi_{\psi}=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\Xi_{\phi_{j}}.

(149)

Since Eq. (
147
) is convex in
\Xi_{\psi}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|^{\otimes 4})\right]\leq\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(|{\phi_{j}}\rangle\!\langle{\phi_{j}}|^{\otimes 4})\right]\leq\max_{j}\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(|{\phi_{j}}\rangle\!\langle{\phi_{j}}|^{\otimes 4})\right].

(150)

Therefore every pure state value is bounded above by a value realized within the stabilizer family, so an optimal pure state may be chosen to be a pure stabilizer state.
∎

C.3.3
Sharp bound within the stabilizer family

By Prop.
11
, it remains to optimize Eq. (
24
) over states of the form
\psi=|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|
, where
|{\psi}\rangle
is a pure stabilizer state. Specializing the exact Pauli basis formula to identical inputs gives

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\psi^{\otimes 4})\right]=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\\
P\sim Q\end{subarray}}3^{c(P,Q)}\,\widehat{\psi}(P)^{2}\,\widehat{\psi}(Q)^{2},

(151)

where
\widehat{\psi}(P)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr\left[\psi P\right]
is the Pauli coefficient of
\psi
,
P\sim Q
means that on each qubit the local Paulis are either identical or one of them is identity, and
c(P,Q)
counts the positions where the two local Paulis coincide nontrivially.

Proposition 12
.

Let
\psi=|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|
be a pure stabilizer state on
n
qubits. Then

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\psi^{\otimes 4})\right]\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(152)

Equality is attained by product stabilizer states, equivalently by identical local Pauli eigenstates.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\psi=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{P\in S}\varepsilon(P)P,

(153)

where
S\subset\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the phase-free stabilizer support of
\psi
and

\varepsilon(P)\in\{\pm 1\}
. Since
\psi
is a pure stabilizer state,
S
has
cardinality
2^{n}
and may be identified with an
n
-dimensional totally
isotropic linear subspace of
(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2})^{n}
. Thus

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\psi^{\otimes 4})\right]=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum_{P,Q\in S}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\kappa(P_{j},Q_{j}),

(154)

where the local kernel is

\displaystyle\kappa(a,b)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}1,&a=b=0,\\
1,&a=0,\ b\neq 0\ \text{or}\ a\neq 0,\ b=0,\\
3,&a=b\neq 0,\\
0,&a\neq 0,\ b\neq 0,\ a\neq b.\end{cases}

(155)

Here
0
denotes the identity Pauli and the three nonzero elements of

\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
denote the three phase-free nonidentity Paulis.

For
r\geq 0
, write
V_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2})^{r}
and

\displaystyle\kappa_{r}(u,v)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\kappa(u_{j},v_{j}),\qquad F_{r}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{4^{r}}\sum_{u,v\in A}\kappa_{r}(u,v)

(156)

for
A\subseteq V_{r}
. An affine set
A\subseteq V_{r}
will be called affine
isotropic if
A=u+K
, where
K\subseteq V_{r}
is a totally isotropic linear
subspace and
u\in K^{\perp}
, with orthogonality taken with respect to the
standard symplectic form. Equivalently, every two elements of
A
are
symplectically orthogonal.

We prove the following induction statement. If
A\subseteq V_{r}
is
affine isotropic of affine dimension
m
, then

\displaystyle F_{r}(A)\leq B_{r,m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{r-m}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m}.

(157)

The desired stabilizer bound follows by taking
A=S
,
r=m=n
.

The proof is by induction on
r
, uniformly over all possible affine
dimensions
m
. The case
r=0
is immediate. Assume the claim has been proved
for
V_{r-1}
and all affine dimensions. Put
s=r-1
, and let

\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V_{r}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
be the projection onto the last coordinate.
For each
a\in\pi(A)
define the fiber

\displaystyle A_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\in V_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(x,a)\in A\}.

(158)

If
A=u+K
, then all nonempty fibers have common direction

\displaystyle L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\in V_{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(x,0)\in K\}.

(159)

Moreover, each fiber is affine isotropic in
V_{s}
. Indeed, if

x,y\in A_{a}
, then
(x,a),(y,a)\in A
, so

\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathrm{sp}}+\langle a,a\rangle_{\mathrm{sp}}=0
, and

\langle a,a\rangle_{\mathrm{sp}}=0
.

We shall also use the following consequence of the induction hypothesis. Let

B\subseteq V_{s}
be affine isotropic of affine dimension
d
, and suppose

h\notin L_{B}
, where
L_{B}
is the direction of
B
, is such that

B\cup(B+h)
is affine isotropic. Define

\displaystyle C(B,B+h)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{4^{s}}\sum_{x\in B,\,y\in B+h}\kappa_{s}(x,y).

(160)

Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(F_{s}(B)+2C(B,B+h)+3F_{s}(B+h)\right)\leq B_{s,d}.

(161)

Indeed,
B\cup(B+h)
is affine isotropic of dimension
d+1
, so by the
induction hypothesis

\displaystyle F_{s}\bigl(B\cup(B+h)\bigr)=F_{s}(B)+2C(B,B+h)+F_{s}(B+h)\leq B_{s,d+1}=2B_{s,d}.

(162)

Also
F_{s}(B+h)\leq B_{s,d}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(F_{s}(B)+2C(B,B+h)+3F_{s}(B+h)\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left(F_{s}\bigl(B\cup(B+h)\bigr)+2F_{s}(B+h)\right)\leq B_{s,d}.

(163)

This is the estimate that rules out the missing full-projection case.

We now distinguish the possible affine projections
\pi(A)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
.

Case 1:
\pi(A)
is a single point.

Write
\pi(A)=\{a\}
. Then
A=A_{a}\times\{a\}
and
A_{a}
has affine dimension

m
. Hence

\displaystyle F_{r}(A)=\frac{\kappa(a,a)}{4}F_{s}(A_{a}).

(164)

If
a=0
, then
\kappa(a,a)=1
; if
a\neq 0
, then
\kappa(a,a)=3
. In either
case,

\displaystyle F_{r}(A)\leq\frac{3}{4}B_{s,m}=B_{r,m}.

(165)

Case 2:
\pi(A)
has two points.

Let
\pi(A)=\{a,b\}
, where
a\neq b
. Put
A_{b}=A_{a}+h
. The common fiber
direction has dimension
d=m-1
.

If one of
a,b
is zero, relabel so that
a=0
and
b\neq 0
. In this subcase

A_{a}\cup A_{b}
is affine isotropic in
V_{s}
, because the two last-coordinate
labels
0
and
b
are symplectically orthogonal. If
h\in L
, then

A_{b}=A_{a}
and

\displaystyle F_{r}(A)=\frac{1}{4}\left(F_{s}(A_{a})+2F_{s}(A_{a})+3F_{s}(A_{a})\right)=\frac{3}{2}F_{s}(A_{a})\leq\frac{3}{2}B_{s,d}=B_{r,m}.

(166)

If
h\notin L
, Eq. (
161
) gives the stronger
bound

\displaystyle F_{r}(A)=\frac{1}{4}\left(F_{s}(A_{a})+2C(A_{a},A_{b})+3F_{s}(A_{b})\right)\leq B_{s,d}\leq B_{r,m}.

(167)

It remains in this case to consider the possibility that both
a
and
b
are
nonzero. Since
a\neq b
, the local factor
\kappa(a,b)
is zero. Therefore

\displaystyle F_{r}(A)=\frac{1}{4}\left(3F_{s}(A_{a})+3F_{s}(A_{b})\right)\leq\frac{3}{2}B_{s,d}=B_{r,m}.

(168)

Case 3:
\pi(A)=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
.

In this case the projection of the direction
K
has dimension
2
, and hence

L
has dimension
d=m-2
. Write the four fibers as

\displaystyle A_{0},\qquad A_{a}=A_{0}+h_{a}\quad(a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}\setminus\{0\}).

(169)

For every nonzero
a
, the vector
h_{a}
is not in
L
. To see this, suppose

h_{a}\in L
. Since
(h_{a},a)\in K
and
(h_{a},0)\in K
, their difference

(0,a)
would belong to
K
. Since
\pi(K)=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
, there is

b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
with
\langle a,b\rangle_{\mathrm{sp}}=1
and some

(h_{b},b)\in K
. But then

\displaystyle\left\langle(0,a),(h_{b},b)\right\rangle_{\mathrm{sp}}=\langle a,b\rangle_{\mathrm{sp}}=1,

(170)

contradicting the isotropy of
K
.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
, set

\displaystyle C_{ab}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{4^{s}}\sum_{x\in A_{a},\,y\in A_{b}}\kappa_{s}(x,y).

(171)

The local factor
\kappa(a,b)
vanishes whenever
a
and
b
are distinct
nonzero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}
. Therefore

\displaystyle F_{r}(A)=\frac{1}{4}\left(C_{00}+2\sum_{a\neq 0}C_{0a}+3\sum_{a\neq 0}C_{aa}\right).

(172)

For each nonzero
a
, the set
A_{0}\cup A_{a}
is affine isotropic and

h_{a}\notin L
, so Eq. (
161
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(C_{00}+2C_{0a}+3C_{aa}\right)\leq B_{s,d}.

(173)

Summing this inequality over the three nonzero values of
a
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(3C_{00}+2\sum_{a\neq 0}C_{0a}+3\sum_{a\neq 0}C_{aa}\right)\leq 3B_{s,d}.

(174)

Since
C_{00}\geq 0
, Eq. (
172
) implies

\displaystyle F_{r}(A)\leq 3B_{s,d}.

(175)

Finally, because
s=r-1
and
d=m-2
,

\displaystyle 3B_{s,d}=3\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{r-m+1}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m-2}=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{r-m}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{m}=B_{r,m}.

(176)

This completes the induction and proves Eq. (
157
).

Applying the claim to the stabilizer support
S\subseteq V_{n}
, which has

r=m=n
, gives

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\psi^{\otimes 4})\right]=F_{n}(S)\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(177)

Equality is attained by product stabilizer states. Indeed, if

S=\{0,p_{1}\}\times\cdots\times\{0,p_{n}\}
with each
p_{j}\neq 0
, then the sum
factorizes into the one-qubit value

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(\kappa(0,0)+2\kappa(0,p_{j})+\kappa(p_{j},p_{j})\right)=\frac{1}{4}(1+2+3)=\frac{3}{2}

(178)

on every qubit. Therefore
F_{n}(S)=(3/2)^{n}
for every product stabilizer state.
Together with Prop.
10
and
Prop.
11
, this proves
Thm.
3
of MT.
∎

C.4
Proof of Cor.
4

We now combine the variance bounds available for local Clifford sampling to derive the optimized Chebyshev-based sufficient-copy bound summarized in Cor.
4
of MT. By Appx.
C.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(1)}_{{\rm Cl}}\leq 0,\qquad\mathbb{V}^{(2)}_{{\rm Cl}}\leq\frac{3^{n}}{N_{M}^{2}},\qquad\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{{\rm Cl}}\leq 2\,\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}^{2}}\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n}.

(179)

For the fourth term, Thm.
3
of MT gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{{\rm Cl}}\leq\left(\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(180)

Therefore, by Eq. (
9
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[X_{M}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}^{(1)}_{{\rm Cl}}+\mathbb{V}^{(2)}_{{\rm Cl}}+\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{{\rm Cl}}+\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{{\rm Cl}}

(181)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3^{n}}{N_{M}^{2}}+2\,\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}^{2}}\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n}+\left(\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(182)

Substituting Eq. (
182
) into the general Chebyshev sufficient-copy relation Eq. (
12
), we obtain the sufficient condition

\displaystyle N\geq\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}\left[\frac{3^{n}}{N_{M}}+2\,\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n}+\frac{(N_{M}-1)^{2}}{N_{M}}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\right].

(183)

Since

\displaystyle\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\leq 1,\qquad\frac{(N_{M}-1)^{2}}{N_{M}}\leq N_{M},

(184)

it further suffices to require

\displaystyle N\geq\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}\left[\frac{3^{n}}{N_{M}}+2\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n}+N_{M}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}\right].

(185)

The only
N_{M}
-dependent leading terms in Eq. (
185
) are

\displaystyle g(N_{M})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{3^{n}}{N_{M}}+N_{M}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n},

(186)

and differentiating gives

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(N_{M})=-\frac{3^{n}}{N_{M}^{2}}+\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(187)

Thus the minimizing scale is

\displaystyle N_{M}^{2}=\frac{3^{n}}{(3/2)^{n}}=2^{n},\qquad N_{M}=2^{n/2}.

(188)

Equivalently, this is the point where

\displaystyle\frac{3^{n}}{N_{M}}=N_{M}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}=\sqrt{4.5^{n}}.

(189)

Hence choosing, for example,

\displaystyle N_{M}=\lceil 2^{n/2}\rceil

(190)

makes the sufficient bound in Eq. (
185
) scale as

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{4.5^{n}}+2(7/4)^{n}}{\delta\varepsilon^{2}},

(191)

and therefore

\displaystyle N=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{4.5^{n}}}{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}\right),

(192)

which is exactly Eq. (
28
) in MT.
Since the middle term in Eq. (
185
) is independent of
N_{M}
and exponentially smaller than
\sqrt{4.5^{n}}
, while the remaining two terms are balanced at
N_{M}=2^{n/2}
, the optimizing scale is

\displaystyle N_{M}=\Theta(2^{n/2}).

(193)

This proves Cor.
4
.

Appendix D
DIPE with single-qubit Haar ensemble

This section focuses on the single-qubit Haar-specific part of the analysis. The first three variance terms were already derived in Appx.
C.1
, since they depend only on the second and third moments and therefore coincide for the single-qubit Clifford and single-qubit Haar ensembles. We now study the fourth term
\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{H}}
, prove the twirl statement and universal upper bound summarized in Thm.
5
, and then combine these ingredients with the shared first-three-term bounds to derive Cor.
6
.

D.1
Calculations of the fourth term

We first derive the operator form of
\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{H}}
from Eq. (
10d
).

Haar fourth-moment operator.

For a product Haar sample
U=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}U_{\ell}
, define

\displaystyle O(U)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\bm{s},\bm{t}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}f(\bm{s},\bm{t})\left(U^{\dagger}|{\bm{s}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{s}}|U\right)\otimes\left(U^{\dagger}|{\bm{t}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{t}}|U\right).

(194)

Since
f(\bm{s},\bm{t})=\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}h(s_{\ell},t_{\ell})
and
U
is a product unitary, we have

\displaystyle O(U)=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}O_{1}(U_{\ell}),

(195)

where
O_{1}(\cdot)
is defined in Eq. (
81
).
Therefore

\displaystyle\mu_{U}(\rho,\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{s},\bm{t}\mid U}[f(\bm{s},\bm{t})]=\tr\left[O(U)(\rho\otimes\sigma)\right],

(196)

and hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{U}[\mu_{U}(\rho,\sigma)^{2}]=\tr\left[\mathbb{E}_{U}\!\left[O(U)_{A_{1}B_{1}}\otimes O(U)_{A_{2}B_{2}}\right](\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right].

(197)

By independence of the single-qubit Haar samples, the averaged operator factorizes as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{U}\!\left[O(U)_{A_{1}B_{1}}\otimes O(U)_{A_{2}B_{2}}\right]=R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n},

(198)

where the single-qubit local Haar fourth-moment operator
R_{4,\mathrm{H}}

is defined as

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{H}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{V}\!\left[O_{1}(V)_{A_{1}B_{1}}\otimes O_{1}(V)_{A_{2}B_{2}}\right]

(199)

with
V
a single-qubit Haar random unitary. Combining this with Eq. (
10d
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{H}}=\left(\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\right)^{2}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right],

(200)

which is exactly the operator form given in Eq. (
30
) of MT.

Haar commutant expansion.

For later use, we also record the explicit Haar commutant expansion of
R_{4,\mathrm{H}}

by exploiting the unitary
4
-design property of the Haar ensemble.
With the replica ordering
(A_{1},B_{1},A_{2},B_{2})
, let
\Pi_{\pi}
denote the permutation operator for
\pi\in S_{4}
, and write

\mathbb{F}_{ij}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Pi_{(ij)}
.
Then the single-qubit local Haar fourth-moment operator
R_{4,\mathrm{H}}

admits the permutation-operator expansion

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{H}}=\frac{1}{5}\left(\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F}_{12}+\mathbb{F}_{34}\right)+\frac{3}{5}\left(\mathbb{F}_{12}\mathbb{F}_{34}+\mathbb{F}_{13}\mathbb{F}_{24}+\mathbb{F}_{14}\mathbb{F}_{23}\right)-\frac{3}{10}\left(\mathbb{F}_{13}+\mathbb{F}_{24}+\mathbb{F}_{14}+\mathbb{F}_{23}\right).

(201)

Equivalently, in cycle notation,

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{5}\,\Pi_{\operatorname{id}}+\frac{1}{5}\,\Pi_{(12)}+\frac{1}{5}\,\Pi_{(34)}+\frac{3}{5}\,\Pi_{(12)(34)}+\frac{3}{5}\,\Pi_{(13)(24)}+\frac{3}{5}\,\Pi_{(14)(23)}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{3}{10}\,\Pi_{(13)}-\frac{3}{10}\,\Pi_{(24)}-\frac{3}{10}\,\Pi_{(14)}-\frac{3}{10}\,\Pi_{(23)}.

(202)

The optimization problem.

The fourth-moment expression satisfies

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{U}[\mu_{U}(\rho,\sigma)^{2}],

(203)

where
\mu_{U}(\rho,\sigma)
is defined in Eq. (
196
).
We need to solve the following optimization problem in order to obtain the worst-case upper bound
on
\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{H}}
over all possible quantum states:

\displaystyle\max_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{D}((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n})}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right].

(204)

Now we show that the maximum in Eq. (
204
) is attained
by a pair of identical pure states.
The argument closely parallels the proof of Prop.
10

established for the single-qubit Clifford ensemble;
for completeness, we present the Haar version here in a self-contained form.

Proposition 13
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\max_{\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{D}((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n})}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]=\max_{|{\psi}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right].

(205)

In particular, the maximum is attained by a pair of identical pure states.

Proof.

Recall the quantity
\mu_{U}(\rho,\sigma)
defined in Eq. (
196
).
For every fixed
\sigma
and every Haar sample
U
,
\mu_{U}(\rho,\sigma)
is affine in
\rho
.
Therefore, for
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\mathscr{D}((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n})
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\;\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}((\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2})\otimes\sigma\otimes(\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2})\otimes\sigma)\right]

(206)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathbb{E}_{U}\!\left[\left(\lambda\mu_{U}(\rho_{1},\sigma)+(1-\lambda)\mu_{U}(\rho_{2},\sigma)\right)^{2}\right]

(207)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\lambda\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{1}\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho_{1}\otimes\sigma)\right]+(1-\lambda)\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{2}\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\sigma)\right],

(208)

because
x\mapsto x^{2}
is convex. Hence, for fixed
\sigma
, the Haar
fourth-moment objective is convex in
\rho
on the compact convex set of
density operators, so its maximum is attained at an extreme point, namely a
pure state. Similarly, for fixed
\rho
, the objective is convex in
\sigma
.

Now let
(\rho_{\star},\sigma_{\star})
be a maximizing pair. By convexity in the first argument, there exists a pure state
|{\psi}\rangle
such that

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\psi\otimes\sigma_{\star}\otimes\psi\otimes\sigma_{\star})\right]\geq\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{\star}\otimes\sigma_{\star}\otimes\rho_{\star}\otimes\sigma_{\star})\right].

(209)

Since
(\rho_{\star},\sigma_{\star})
is already globally optimal, equality must hold. Applying the same argument to the second argument yields a pure state
|{\phi}\rangle
such that

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\psi\otimes\phi\otimes\psi\otimes\phi)\right]\geq\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\psi\otimes\sigma_{\star}\otimes\psi\otimes\sigma_{\star})\right]=\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{\star}\otimes\sigma_{\star}\otimes\rho_{\star}\otimes\sigma_{\star})\right].

(210)

Thus equality must hold, so the global maximum is attained by the pure state pair
(|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|,|{\phi}\rangle\!\langle{\phi}|)
.

It remains to show that the two pure states may be chosen identical. For a fixed Haar sample
U
and state
\tau
, define

\displaystyle p_{U}^{\tau}(\bm{s})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr\left[U^{\dagger}|{\bm{s}}\rangle\!\langle{\bm{s}}|U\,\tau\right],\qquad\widehat{p}_{U}^{\tau}(S)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\bm{s}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}(-1)^{\sum_{\ell\in S}s_{\ell}}p_{U}^{\tau}(\bm{s}).

(211)

Here
p_{U}^{\tau}
is the computational basis measurement distribution of
\tau
after applying
U
, and
\widehat{p}_{U}^{\tau}(S)
is its Walsh coefficient on the subset
S\subseteq[n]
. With this notation, we write

\displaystyle\mu_{U}(\rho,\sigma)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\frac{3^{|S|}}{2^{n}}\,\widehat{p}_{U}^{\rho}(S)\,\widehat{p}_{U}^{\sigma}(S).

(212)

Since all weights
3^{|S|}/2^{n}
are nonnegative, the weighted Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give

\displaystyle\mu_{U}(\rho,\sigma)^{2}\leq\mu_{U}(\rho,\rho)\,\mu_{U}(\sigma,\sigma)\leq\frac{\mu_{U}(\rho,\rho)^{2}+\mu_{U}(\sigma,\sigma)^{2}}{2}.

(213)

Averaging over
U
yields

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]\leq\frac{\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\rho^{\otimes 4}\right]+\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\sigma^{\otimes 4}\right]}{2}\leq\max\left\{\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\rho^{\otimes 4}\right],\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\sigma^{\otimes 4}\right]\right\}.

(214)

Applying this to the maximizing pure state pair
(|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|,|{\phi}\rangle\!\langle{\phi}|)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\psi\otimes\phi\otimes\psi\otimes\phi)\right]\leq\max\left\{\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right],\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\phi^{\otimes 4}\right]\right\}.

(215)

The left-hand side is the global maximum, while each term on the right-hand side is bounded above by the same quantity. Therefore equality must hold, and at least one of the identical pure state pairs
(|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|,|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|)
or
(|{\phi}\rangle\!\langle{\phi}|,|{\phi}\rangle\!\langle{\phi}|)
also attains the global maximum.
∎

D.2
Proof of Thm.
5

Proof of Eq. (
31
)
in Thm.
5
.

Recall the definitions of the one-qubit fourth-moment operators

R_{4,\mathrm{H}}
and
R_{4,{\rm Cl}}
in
Eqs. (
201
) and (
21
). We first prove the
one-qubit twirling identity

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{H}}=\mathbb{E}_{W\sim\nu}\!\left[(W^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}R_{4,{\rm Cl}}W^{\otimes 4}\right],

(216)

where
\nu
denotes the single-qubit Haar measure.
For a one-qubit Hermitian unitary
M
with eigenvalues
\pm 1
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}\,M\otimes M\right)_{A_{1}B_{1}}\otimes\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}\,M\otimes M\right)_{A_{2}B_{2}}.

(217)

Since

O_{1}(V)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}(V^{\dagger}ZV)\otimes(V^{\dagger}ZV)
, the definition
of
R_{4,\mathrm{H}}
in Eq. (
201
) gives

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{H}}=\mathbb{E}_{V}\!\left[\mathcal{K}(V^{\dagger}ZV)\right],

(218)

where
V
is a single-qubit Haar random unitary.

On the other hand, if
C
is a uniformly random single-qubit Clifford, then
C^{\dagger}ZC
is uniformly distributed over
\{\pm X,\pm Y,\pm Z\}
,
and the sign disappears inside
M\otimes M
.
Hence it follows from the definition of
R_{4,{\rm Cl}}
in Eq. (
21
)
that,

\displaystyle R_{4,{\rm Cl}}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{P\in\{X,Y,Z\}}\mathcal{K}(P).

(219)

For arbitrary single-qubit unitary
W
sampled according to the Haar measure
\nu
, we have

\displaystyle(W^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}R_{4,{\rm Cl}}W^{\otimes 4}

\displaystyle=(W^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}\left(\frac{1}{3}\sum_{P\in\{X,Y,Z\}}\mathcal{K}(P)\right)W^{\otimes 4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{P\in\{X,Y,Z\}}(W^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}\mathcal{K}(P)W^{\otimes 4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{P\in\{X,Y,Z\}}\mathcal{K}(W^{\dagger}PW),

(220)

where the last step follows from the definition of
\mathcal{K}
, since

\displaystyle(W^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}\!\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}\,P\otimes P\right)_{A_{1}B_{1}}\otimes\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}+\frac{3}{2}\,P\otimes P\right)_{A_{2}B_{2}}\right]W^{\otimes 4}=\mathcal{K}(W^{\dagger}PW).

(221)

Averaging over all single-qubit Haar-random unitary
W
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{W\sim\nu}\!\left[(W^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}R_{4,{\rm Cl}}W^{\otimes 4}\right]=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{P\in\{X,Y,Z\}}\mathbb{E}_{W\sim\nu}\!\left[\mathcal{K}(W^{\dagger}PW)\right].

(222)

For each fixed
P\in\{X,Y,Z\}
, the random observable
W^{\dagger}PW
is uniformly distributed over all Bloch-sphere directions
since
W
is Haar distributed, and hence has the same distribution as
V^{\dagger}ZV
for Haar-random
V
.
Therefore each term in the sum equals
R_{4,\mathrm{H}}
, and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{W\sim\nu}\!\left[(W^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}R_{4,{\rm Cl}}W^{\otimes 4}\right]=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{P\in\{X,Y,Z\}}R_{4,\mathrm{H}}=R_{4,\mathrm{H}}.

(223)

This proves Eq. (
216
).

Taking the tensor product over
n
independent qubits and using independence of
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
yields

\displaystyle R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{W_{\ell}\sim\nu}\!\left[(W_{\ell}^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}R_{4,{\rm Cl}}W_{\ell}^{\otimes 4}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{W}\sim\nu^{\otimes n}}\!\left[(\bm{W}^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\bm{W}^{\otimes 4}\right],

(224)

which is the operator identity (
31
)
in Thm.
5
.

Proof of Eq. (
32
)
in Thm.
5
.
For arbitrary

n
-qubit states
\rho
and
\sigma
, the operator identity just proved gives

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{W}\sim\nu^{\otimes n}}\tr\left[(\bm{W}^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\bm{W}^{\otimes 4}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]

(225)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{W}\sim\nu^{\otimes n}}\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\left(\bm{W}\rho\bm{W}^{\dagger}\otimes\bm{W}\sigma\bm{W}^{\dagger}\otimes\bm{W}\rho\bm{W}^{\dagger}\otimes\bm{W}\sigma\bm{W}^{\dagger}\right)\right].

(226)

For each fixed
\bm{W}
, the pair

(\bm{W}\rho\bm{W}^{\dagger},\bm{W}\sigma\bm{W}^{\dagger})
is again a valid pair of

n
-qubit density operators. Therefore Thm.
3

implies the pointwise bound

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\left(\bm{W}\rho\bm{W}^{\dagger}\otimes\bm{W}\sigma\bm{W}^{\dagger}\otimes\bm{W}\rho\bm{W}^{\dagger}\otimes\bm{W}\sigma\bm{W}^{\dagger}\right)\right]\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(227)

Averaging over
\bm{W}
yields

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}.

(228)

Since this holds for every
\rho
and
\sigma
, taking the maximum over all
state pairs proves Eq. (
32
).
This completes the proof of Thm.
5
.

D.3
Proof of Cor.
6

The proof is the same as the proof of Cor.
4
in
Appx.
C.4
, after replacing the Clifford
subscript by the Haar subscript. Indeed, the first three variance terms coincide
with the single-qubit Clifford case by the shared
3
-design calculation in
Appx.
C.1
. For the fourth term,
Thm.
5
, together with
Eq. (
30
), gives the same worst-case upper bound as in the

are

) with the same optimizing scale

.

D.4
The bound in Cor.
6
is not saturated

We now explain that the Haar sufficient-copy upper bound in Cor.
6
should be viewed only as a comparison
bound rather than a sharp sample complexity characterization.
In particular, the underlying fourth-moment comparison is already not saturated for
n=1
and
n=2
.

The key input is the operator identity (
31
)
in Thm.
5
.
Applying this identity to a pure state
\psi=|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|
gives the scalar
relation

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{W}\sim\nu^{\otimes n}}\tr\left[(\bm{W}^{\otimes 4})^{\dagger}R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}\bm{W}^{\otimes 4}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{W}\sim\nu^{\otimes n}}\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\bm{W}\psi\bm{W}^{\dagger})^{\otimes 4}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{W}\sim\nu^{\otimes n}}\!\left[\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\bm{W}\psi\bm{W}^{\dagger})^{\otimes 4}\right]\right],

(229)

where
\bm{W}=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}W_{\ell}
and each
W_{\ell}\sim\nu
.
This consequence of the operator identity yields a necessary condition for equality. Indeed, if a pure state
\psi
were to satisfy

\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right]=(3/2)^{n}
, then the random variable

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\bm{W}\psi\bm{W}^{\dagger})^{\otimes 4}\right]

(230)

would have expectation equal to its pointwise upper bound
(3/2)^{n}
. Therefore it must equal
(3/2)^{n}
for almost every
\bm{W}
. Since the map

\displaystyle\bm{W}\mapsto\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\bm{W}\psi\bm{W}^{\dagger})^{\otimes 4}\right]

(231)

is continuous, it follows that in fact

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,{\rm Cl}}^{\otimes n}(\bm{W}\psi\bm{W}^{\dagger})^{\otimes 4}\right]=\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}

(232)

for all local unitaries
\bm{W}
. Thus, Haar saturation would require that the
entire local unitary orbit
of
\psi
consists of Clifford maximizers.
This is a highly rigid condition, and the two cases below already show that the universal upper bound is not generally saturated.

The single-qubit case.

Let
\psi=|{\psi}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}|
be an arbitrary one qubit pure state.
Since
\psi^{\otimes 4}
is invariant under every replica permutation, then for arbitrary
\pi\in S_{4}
,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\pi}\,\psi^{\otimes 4}\,\Pi_{\pi}^{\dagger}=\psi^{\otimes 4},\qquad\tr\left[\Pi_{\pi}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right]=1.

(233)

Eq. (
D.1
) gives

\displaystyle\max_{|{\psi}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{2}}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right]=\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{5}+3\cdot\frac{3}{5}-4\cdot\frac{3}{10}=\frac{6}{5}<\frac{3}{2}.

(234)

Therefore the upper bound
(3/2)^{n}
is already not saturated for
n=1
.

The two-qubit case.

For two qubits, local unitary invariance allows us to parameterize every bipartite pure state in the Schmidt form

\displaystyle|{\psi_{\lambda}}\rangle=\sqrt{\lambda}|{00}\rangle+\sqrt{1-\lambda}|{11}\rangle,\qquad 0\leq\lambda\leq 1.

(235)

Let
\psi_{\lambda}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|{\psi_{\lambda}}\rangle\!\langle{\psi_{\lambda}}|
. A direct Pauli-basis computation gives

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(II)=\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(ZZ)=1,\quad\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(ZI)=\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(IZ)=2\lambda-1,\quad\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(XX)=-\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(YY)=2\sqrt{\lambda(1-\lambda)},

(236)

with all other Pauli coefficients equal to zero. Substituting these coefficients into the exact Pauli formula for the Haar fourth moment yields

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes 2}\psi_{\lambda}^{\otimes 4}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{36}{25}-\frac{58}{25}\lambda(1-\lambda)+\frac{236}{25}\lambda^{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}.

(237)

Let
t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda(1-\lambda)\in[0,1/4]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes 2}\psi_{\lambda}^{\otimes 4}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{36}{25}-\frac{58}{25}t+\frac{236}{25}t^{2}.

(238)

This is a convex quadratic function of
t
, so its maximum over
[0,1/4]
is attained at an endpoint. Therefore

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes 2}\psi_{\lambda}^{\otimes 4}\right]=\max\left\{\frac{36}{25},\frac{29}{20}\right\}=\frac{29}{20}.

(239)

Hence

\displaystyle\max_{|{\psi}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes 2}}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes 2}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right]=\frac{29}{20}<\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2}.

(240)

The maximum is attained by a Bell state, whereas product states attain only
(6/5)^{2}=36/25
.

D.5
Discussions on Conj.
7

This subsection collects analytical evidence for Conj.
7
: we first record the product-state fourth-moment benchmark, then verify the conjectured sample complexity scaling on several structured state families, and finally explain the competing effects of entanglement on the second- and fourth-moment contributions.

D.5.1
Product state benchmark

We first prove Eq. (
34
) of MT.
Let

\displaystyle|{\psi}\rangle=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}|{\psi_{\ell}}\rangle

(241)

be an arbitrary pure product state. Since the Haar fourth-moment operator is
local across qubits, the expectation factorizes:

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right]=\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}\psi_{\ell}^{\otimes 4}\right].

(242)

For every single-qubit pure state
\psi_{\ell}
, Eq. (
234
)
gives

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}\psi_{\ell}^{\otimes 4}\right]=\frac{6}{5}.

(243)

Therefore every pure product state satisfies

\displaystyle\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{H}}^{\otimes n}\psi^{\otimes 4}\right]=\left(\frac{6}{5}\right)^{n}.

(244)

Taking the maximum over pure product states gives
Eq. (
34
).

D.5.2
Supporting evidence for Conj.
7

We now verify the conjectured Haar sample complexity scaling on several
representative classes. For a state pair
(\rho,\sigma)
and

\mathrm{E}\in\{{\rm Cl},\mathrm{H}\}
, write

\displaystyle A_{n}(\rho,\sigma)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr\left[(2\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F})^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma)\right],

(245)

\displaystyle C_{n}(\rho,\sigma)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr\left[R_{AA^{\prime}B}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right]+\tr\left[R_{ABB^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\sigma)\right],

(246)

\displaystyle B_{n,\mathrm{E}}(\rho,\sigma)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr\left[R_{4,\mathrm{E}}^{\otimes n}(\rho\otimes\sigma\otimes\rho\otimes\sigma)\right].

(247)

For
\mathrm{E}=\mathrm{H}
, by Eqs. (
18b
) and
(
30
), these quantities are the state-dependent
coefficients of the Haar second, third, and fourth variance terms:

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(2)}_{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{n}(\rho,\sigma)}{N_{M}^{2}},

(248)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(3)}_{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}^{2}}C_{n}(\rho,\sigma),

(249)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N_{M}-1}{N_{M}}\right)^{2}B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho,\sigma).

(250)

For Haar sampling, Eqs. (
12
),
(
10
), and (
30
) imply the
state-dependent sufficient condition

\displaystyle N_{\star,\mathrm{H}}(\rho,\sigma;N_{M})\leq\frac{c_{0}}{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}\left[\frac{A_{n}(\rho,\sigma)}{N_{M}}+2\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)^{n}+N_{M}B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho,\sigma)\right]

(251)

for a universal constant
c_{0}>0
. Hence any class satisfying

\displaystyle A_{n}(\rho,\sigma)B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho,\sigma)\leq\left(\frac{18}{5}\right)^{n}

(252)

has the conjectured scaling in that class: choosing

N_{M}=\Theta(\sqrt{A_{n}(\rho,\sigma)/B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho,\sigma)})
gives

\displaystyle N_{\star,\mathrm{H}}(\rho,\sigma)=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{3.6^{n}}}{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}\right),

(253)

because
(7/4)^{n}
is exponentially smaller than
(18/5)^{n/2}
.
Thus the examples below support Conj.
7
directly by
proving Eq. (
252
).

D.5.2.1 Product state pairs

Proposition 14
.

For arbitrary product state pairs

\displaystyle\rho=\rho_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{n},\qquad\sigma=\sigma_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{n},

(254)

one has

\displaystyle A_{n}(\rho,\sigma)B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho,\sigma)\leq\left(\frac{18}{5}\right)^{n}.

(255)

Therefore product state pairs satisfy the sample complexity scaling in
Conj.
7
. Equality is attained by identical pure
product states.

Proof.

Both
A_{n}
and
B_{n,\mathrm{H}}
factorize on product state pairs, so it
suffices to prove the one-qubit bound. Write

\displaystyle\rho_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}+\bm{r}\cdot\bm{\sigma}),\qquad\sigma_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}+\bm{s}\cdot\bm{\sigma}),

(256)

with
\|\bm{r}\|,\|\bm{s}\|\leq 1
, and set

x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bm{r}\cdot\bm{s}
and
q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\|\bm{r}\|\,\|\bm{s}\|
. Then

\displaystyle A_{1}(\rho_{1},\sigma_{1})=2+\tr\left[\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\right]=\frac{5+x}{2}.

(257)

For the Haar fourth-moment coefficient, measuring along a Haar-random Bloch
direction
\bm{n}
gives the conditional kernel expectation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{r})(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{s}).

(258)

Using the spherical moments

\mathbb{E}[n_{i}n_{j}]=\delta_{ij}/3
and

\mathbb{E}[n_{i}n_{j}n_{k}n_{l}]=(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})/15
,
we obtain

\displaystyle B_{1,\mathrm{H}}(\rho_{1},\sigma_{1})=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{x}{2}+\frac{3}{20}q^{2}+\frac{3}{10}x^{2}.

(259)

Since
q\leq 1
and
-1\leq x\leq 1
,

\displaystyle A_{1}(\rho_{1},\sigma_{1})B_{1,\mathrm{H}}(\rho_{1},\sigma_{1})\leq\frac{5+x}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}+\frac{x}{2}+\frac{3x^{2}}{10}\right)\leq\frac{18}{5},

(260)

where the last step is an elementary one-variable maximization over

x\in[-1,1]
, with maximum attained at
x=1
. Equality requires
q=1
and

x=1
, hence
\rho_{1}=\sigma_{1}
is pure. Multiplying the one-qubit bounds over
all qubits gives the claim.
∎

D.5.2.2
n=1

Proposition 15
.

For
n=1
, all state pairs satisfy

\displaystyle A_{1}(\rho,\sigma)B_{1,\mathrm{H}}(\rho,\sigma)\leq\frac{18}{5}.

(261)

Therefore Conj.
7
holds in the complete one-qubit
case. Equality is attained by identical pure one-qubit states.

Proof.

The one-qubit case is included in Prop.
14
,
so the upper bound holds for all one-qubit state pairs. Conversely, for any
pure one-qubit state
\psi
,

\displaystyle A_{1}(\psi,\psi)=\tr\left[(2\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{F})\psi^{\otimes 2}\right]=2+\tr\left[\psi^{2}\right]=3.

(262)

Using Eq. (
D.1
), every replica
permutation has expectation one on
\psi^{\otimes 4}
, and therefore

\displaystyle B_{1,\mathrm{H}}(\psi,\psi)=\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{5}+3\cdot\frac{3}{5}-4\cdot\frac{3}{10}=\frac{6}{5}.

(263)

Thus

\displaystyle A_{1}(\psi,\psi)B_{1,\mathrm{H}}(\psi,\psi)=\frac{18}{5},

(264)

which proves the reverse inequality and the claim.
∎

D.5.2.3
n=2

Proposition 16
.

For identical pure two-qubit state pairs,

\displaystyle\max_{|{\psi}\rangle\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes 2}}A_{2}(\psi,\psi)B_{2,\mathrm{H}}(\psi,\psi)=\left(\frac{18}{5}\right)^{2},

(265)

and the maximum is attained by product states. Therefore the identical pure
two-qubit sector satisfies the sample complexity scaling in
Conj.
7
.

Proof.

By local-unitary invariance, every pure two-qubit state
is equivalent to

\displaystyle|{\psi_{\lambda}}\rangle=\sqrt{\lambda}|{00}\rangle+\sqrt{1-\lambda}|{11}\rangle,\qquad 0\leq\lambda\leq 1.

(266)

Set
t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda(1-\lambda)\in[0,1/4]
. The reduced one-qubit purities are

1-2t
, and hence

\displaystyle A_{2}(\psi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})=9-8t.

(267)

Direct substitution into the local Haar fourth moment gives

\displaystyle B_{2,\mathrm{H}}(\psi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})=\frac{36}{25}-\frac{58}{25}t+\frac{236}{25}t^{2}.

(268)

Therefore

\displaystyle A_{2}(\psi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})B_{2,\mathrm{H}}(\psi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})=\frac{324}{25}-\frac{162}{5}t+\frac{2588}{25}t^{2}-\frac{1888}{25}t^{3}.

(269)

The second derivative of the right-hand side is

\displaystyle\frac{5176}{25}-\frac{11328}{25}t>0,\qquad 0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{4}.

(270)

Hence the cubic in Eq. (
269
) is convex on
[0,1/4]
and
attains its maximum at an endpoint. The endpoint values are

\displaystyle\frac{324}{25}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{203}{20}.

(271)

The larger value is
324/25
, attained at
t=0
, namely at product states.
This is
(18/5)^{2}
, proving the claim.
∎

D.5.2.4 Biseparable states for
n=3

Proposition 17
.

For pure biseparable three-qubit states,

\displaystyle\max_{|{\psi}\rangle\ \mathrm{biseparable}}A_{3}(\psi,\psi)B_{3,\mathrm{H}}(\psi,\psi)=\left(\frac{18}{5}\right)^{3}.

(272)

The maximum is attained by fully product pure states. Therefore this sector
satisfies the sample complexity scaling in Conj.
7
.

Proof.

Any biseparable pure three-qubit state is, up to permutation of the parties,
of the form

\displaystyle|{\psi}\rangle=|{\varphi_{AB}}\rangle\otimes|{\chi_{C}}\rangle,

(273)

where
|{\varphi_{AB}}\rangle
is a pure two-qubit state and
|{\chi_{C}}\rangle
is a
pure one-qubit state. Since both
A_{n}
and
B_{n,\mathrm{H}}
are defined by
local tensor-product operators, they factorize across tensor-product states:

\displaystyle A_{3}(\psi,\psi)B_{3,\mathrm{H}}(\psi,\psi)

\displaystyle=A_{2}(\varphi_{AB},\varphi_{AB})B_{2,\mathrm{H}}(\varphi_{AB},\varphi_{AB})A_{1}(\chi_{C},\chi_{C})B_{1,\mathrm{H}}(\chi_{C},\chi_{C}).

(274)

By Props.
15
and

16
, this is at most

\displaystyle\left(\frac{18}{5}\right)^{2}\frac{18}{5}=\left(\frac{18}{5}\right)^{3}.

(275)

Equality requires the two-qubit factor to be product, and hence the whole
three-qubit state to be fully product.
∎

D.5.2.5 GHZ-family

Proposition 18
.

For the standard GHZ family

\displaystyle|{\mathrm{GHZ}_{n}}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{|{0^{n}}\rangle+|{1^{n}}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|{\mathrm{GHZ}_{n}}\rangle\!\langle{\mathrm{GHZ}_{n}}|,

(276)

one has

\displaystyle A_{n}(\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n},\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n})B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n},\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n})\leq\left(\frac{18}{5}\right)^{n}.

(277)

Therefore the GHZ family satisfies the sample complexity scaling in
Conj.
7
.

Proof.

For the GHZ state, every nontrivial proper subsystem has purity
1/2
, while
the empty subsystem and the full subsystem have purity one. Therefore

\displaystyle A_{n}(\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n},\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n})=\frac{3^{n}+2^{n}+1}{2}.

(278)

For the fourth moment, write

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b\in\{0,1\}}\left(|{a}\rangle\!\langle{b}|\right)^{\otimes n}.

(279)

Then

\displaystyle B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n},\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n})=\frac{1}{16}\sum_{\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\{0,1\}^{4}}m(\bm{a},\bm{b})^{n},\qquad m(\bm{a},\bm{b})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\langle{\bm{b}}|R_{4,\mathrm{H}}|{\bm{a}}\rangle.

(280)

A direct evaluation of this one-qubit matrix gives

\displaystyle B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n},\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n})

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{8}\left(\frac{6}{5}\right)^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{n}+\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{1}{5}\right)^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{5}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{3}{10}\right)^{n}+\left(-\frac{3}{10}\right)^{n}.

(281)

For
n=1
, the GHZ state is a one-qubit pure product state and equality holds.
For
n\geq 2
, divide the product of Eqs. (
278
) and
(
281
) by
(18/5)^{n}
. Since

(2/3)^{n}\leq 4/9
,
3^{-n}\leq 1/9
,
(1/6)^{n}\leq 1/36
, and

(1/4)^{n}\leq 1/16
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{A_{n}(\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n},\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n})B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n},\rho_{\mathrm{GHZ},n})}{(18/5)^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{7}{9}\left(\frac{5}{8}+\frac{2}{9}+\frac{5}{144}+\frac{1}{8}\right)=\frac{1015}{1296}<1.

(282)

This proves the claimed bound.
∎

D.5.2.6 W-family

Proposition 19
.

For the
W
family

\displaystyle|{W_{n}}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}|{0^{\ell-1}1\,0^{n-\ell}}\rangle,\qquad\rho_{W,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|{W_{n}}\rangle\!\langle{W_{n}}|,

(283)

one has

\displaystyle A_{n}(\rho_{W,n},\rho_{W,n})B_{n,\mathrm{H}}(\rho_{W,n},\rho_{W,n})\leq\left(\frac{18}{5}\right)^{n}.

(284)

Therefore the
W
family satisfies the sample complexity scaling in
Conj.
7
.

Proof.

D.5.3
Opposing effects of entanglement

The certificate in Eq. (
252
) should be
understood as a competition between two opposing effects of entanglement.

On the one hand,

\displaystyle A_{n}\left(\psi,\psi\right)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}2^{\,n-\left|S\right|}\tr\left[\psi_{S}^{2}\right]

(300)

is a weighted sum of subsystem purities. Hence
A_{n}\left(\psi,\psi\right)\leq 3^{n}

with equality if and only if
|{\psi}\rangle
is a product state. In this sense,

A_{n}

penalizes entanglement
: once
|{\psi}\rangle
is entangled,
some reduced states become mixed, and the corresponding purities decrease.

On the other hand,
B_{n,\mathrm{H}}\left(\psi,\psi\right)
behaves differently.
It is not simply maximized by product states, and it may even increase for
entangled states. For instance, in the two-qubit Schmidt family

\displaystyle|{\psi_{\lambda}}\rangle=\sqrt{\lambda}|{00}\rangle+\sqrt{1-\lambda}|{11}\rangle,\qquad t=\lambda\left(1-\lambda\right),

(301)

one has

\displaystyle A_{2}\left(\psi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=9-8t,

(302)

\displaystyle B_{2,\mathrm{H}}\left(\psi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{36}{25}-\frac{58}{25}t+\frac{236}{25}t^{2}.

(303)

Thus

\displaystyle A_{2}\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=9,

\displaystyle B_{2,\mathrm{H}}\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{36}{25},

(304)

for product states, whereas for the Bell state
t=1/4
,

\displaystyle A_{2}\big|_{t=1/4}

\displaystyle=7,

\displaystyle B_{2,\mathrm{H}}\big|_{t=1/4}

\displaystyle=\frac{29}{20}.

(305)

Therefore entanglement decreases
A_{2}
but slightly increases
B_{2,\mathrm{H}}
. However,

\displaystyle A_{2}B_{2,\mathrm{H}}\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=9\cdot\frac{36}{25}=\frac{324}{25}>\frac{203}{20}=7\cdot\frac{29}{20}=A_{2}B_{2,\mathrm{H}}\big|_{t=1/4},

(306)

so the product state still gives the larger state-dependent product of the second- and fourth-moment coefficients.

This example illustrates the main difficulty of the general problem:
entanglement can help
B_{n,\mathrm{H}}
, but it simultaneously hurts
A_{n}
.
The direct certificate for Conj.
7
asks whether, in
the product

\displaystyle A_{n}\left(\psi,\psi\right)B_{n,\mathrm{H}}\left(\psi,\psi\right),

(307)

the purity loss in
A_{n}
always outweighs any possible gain in

B_{n,\mathrm{H}}
at the level needed for the conjectured sample complexity
scaling.

Appendix E
DIPE with independent single-qubit classical shadow

This appendix gives the details behind
Prop.
8
. The protocol is
based on independent local classical shadows and should be distinguished from
the local randomized-measurement estimator studied in the main text: here Alice
and Bob do not share their single-qubit Pauli bases. Instead, each party forms
an empirical local Pauli shadow state, and the final estimate is the
Hilbert–Schmidt inner product of the two empirical shadows.

Pauli shadow.

The one-qubit Pauli shadow is obtained by choosing one of the
three Pauli bases
X,Y,Z
uniformly at random, measuring in that basis, and
then applying the inverse of the corresponding classical-shadow measurement
channel. Equivalently, define the six one-qubit Pauli eigenstate projectors

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{|{0}\rangle\!\langle{0}|,|{1}\rangle\!\langle{1}|,|{+}\rangle\!\langle{+}|,|{-}\rangle\!\langle{-}|,|{i+}\rangle\!\langle{i+}|,|{i-}\rangle\!\langle{i-}|\right\},

(308)

where
|{i\pm}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(|{0}\rangle\pm i|{1}\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
. The induced POVM elements
are

\displaystyle E_{\psi}=\frac{1}{3}\psi,\qquad\psi\in\mathcal{P}_{1},

(309)

because each of the three bases is selected with probability
1/3
. For an
observed one-qubit projector
\psi
, the inverse channel assigns the snapshot

\displaystyle s_{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=3\psi-\mathbb{I}_{2}.

(310)

The six states in
\mathcal{P}_{1}
form a one-qubit projective
3
-design, and this
measurement is the standard single-qubit Pauli shadow
12
.
For an
n
-qubit state, the measurement and reconstruction are applied
independently on each qubit. Thus, for an outcome

\bm{\psi}=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n})
, the corresponding product shadow snapshot of

\rho
is

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\bm{\psi}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{n}s_{\psi_{\ell}}.

(311)

If
\bm{\psi}
is sampled from the Pauli measurement distribution of
\rho
,
then the tensor-product inverse channel guarantees

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\hat{\rho}_{\bm{\psi}}]=\rho.

(312)

We use the analogous notation
\hat{\sigma}_{\bm{\varphi}}
for Bob’s local
Pauli-shadow snapshots sampled from
\sigma
.

DIPE with independent Pauli shadows.

Alice and Bob do not share their single-qubit Pauli bases.
Instead, Alice constructs the empirical local Pauli shadow

N^{-1}\sum_{i}\hat{\rho}_{i}
, Bob constructs the independent empirical local
Pauli shadow
N^{-1}\sum_{j}\hat{\sigma}_{j}
, and the final estimate is the Hilbert–Schmidt
inner product of these two empirical shadows. The corresponding estimation protocol is given in

.

Algorithm 2

DIPE with independent Pauli shadows

0:

N
copies of
\rho

N

\sigma

0:

.

Write

\displaystyle h_{ij}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr\left[\hat{\rho}_{i}\hat{\sigma}_{j}\right],\qquad\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{P}}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}h_{ij},\qquad f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tr\left[\rho\sigma\right].

(314)

Introduce the three second-moment quantities

\displaystyle A_{\rho,\sigma}^{\mathrm{P}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\rho},\hat{\sigma}}\tr\left[\hat{\rho}\hat{\sigma}\right]^{2},\qquad B_{\rho,\sigma}^{\mathrm{P}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\rho}}\tr\left[\hat{\rho}\sigma\right]^{2},\qquad B_{\sigma,\rho}^{\mathrm{P}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\sigma}}\tr\left[\rho\hat{\sigma}\right]^{2}.

(315)

Expanding the square gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{g}_{\mathrm{P}}^{2}]=\frac{1}{N^{4}}\sum_{i,j,q,r=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}[h_{ij}h_{qr}].

The summands are classified by the same index coincidences as in the global
