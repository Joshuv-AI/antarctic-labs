Quantitative Global Proteomics of Formalin-Fixed Paraffin-Embedded Tumors Identifies Dimethylated G3BP1 at Arginine 460 as a Robust Pharmacodynamic Biomarker of Methylthioadenosine Phosphorylase-Selective Protein Arginine Methyltransferase 5 Inhibition
Authors: Anqi Tu, Elizabeth J Wiseman, Steve M Sweet, Jelena Urosevic, Joanna Loizou, Alan Barnicle, Christopher Richardson, Sudhanshu Abhishek, David C Chain, Yeoun Jin Kim, Andrew G Chambers
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
MTA-cooperative inhibition of protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5) is synthetic lethal with methylthioadenosine phosphorylase (MTAP)-deficient cancers. PRMT5′s enzymatic activity can be assessed by measuring symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA) modification levels of protein substrates. However, conventional assays that attempt to measure total SDMA levels lack the specificity to measure individual PRMT5 substrates, and thus potentially reduce selectivity and sensitivity. This study aims to identify and characterize specific DMA peptides that can serve as clinical pharmacodynamic (PD) biomarkers for PRMT5 inhibition in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded solid tumors, leveraging data-independent acquisition-mass spectrometry (DIA-MS)-based global proteomics without additional DMA enrichment. We evaluated 145 DMA peptides in xenograft models treated by a novel MTAP-selective PRMT5 inhibitor, AZD3470. Arginine dimethylation of G3BP1 at residue R460 (G3BP1­(R460)) was identified as the primary PD biomarker for PRMT5 inhibition due to its high abundance and significant post-treatment reduction that correlated with dose. The global proteomics assay with a limit of detection/quantification characterized the quantitative performance and allowed for confident detection of G3BP1­(R460). Using this quantitative assay, more than 90% reduction in G3BP1­(R460) modification at 100 mg/kg was reproducibly detected in MTAP-null xenograft models. Our findings suggested that the DIA-MS proteomics assay can provide high specificity and sensitivity in the detection of PRMT5 inhibition.

Introduction

Protein arginine methyltransferases (PRMTs) methylate protein arginine residues and thus play pivotal roles in regulating a variety of critical cellular processes, such as gene expression, signal transduction, RNA splicing, and DNA repair. − PRMTs can be categorized into three major types. Type I PRMTs catalyze the formation of monomethylarginine (MMA) and asymmetric dimethylarginine (ADMA), Type II PRMTs produce MMA and symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA), and Type III PRMT (PRMT7) only generates MMA. PRMT5 is a member of the Type II PRMT group, of which overexpression in a variety of cancer types is often associated with poor prognosis. − This relates to the role of PRMT5 in cell proliferation and survival through histone and nonhistone modifications and epigenetic regulation, which may activate oncogenes and/or suppress immunity.

PRMT5 inhibitors developed as anticancer agents are being actively investigated in preclinical and clinical studies, some of which have achieved promising preliminary clinical efficacy. Second-generation PRMT5 inhibitors specifically target methylthioadenosine phosphorylase (MTAP)-deficient cancer cells and demonstrate an improved therapeutic index. − MTAP is an enzyme involved in the methionine salvage pathway and is ubiquitously expressed in normal tissues. The deletion of MTAP occurs frequently in cancer, most notably in 42.6% of glioblastoma (GBM), 13.4% of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), 22.3% of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas and 24.9% of bladder urothelial carcinomas. This is because MTAP is adjacent to, and hence codeleted with, the tumor suppressor gene, CDKN2A, which results in the accumulation of methylthioadenosine (MTA). MTA partially inhibits PRMT5 and consequently increases the vulnerability of cancer cells to PRMT5 inhibitors. A large-scale short hairpin RNA (shRNA) screen across 390 cancer cell line models showed that the viability of cancer cells lacking the MTAP gene is selectively impaired by PRMT5 depletion.

AZD3470 is a second-generation PRMT5 inhibitor that is MTA-cooperative and thus selectively targets MTAP-deficient cancer cells while sparing normal cells, hence achieving a more efficient and potentially less toxic treatment modality. AZD3470 is currently being evaluated in the first-time-in-human (FTiH) phase I/II clinical study targeting participants with MTAP-deficient advanced solid tumors (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT06130553), and in another phase I/II trial in participants with relapsed/refractory (R/R) hematologic malignancies (NCT06137144). To investigate the inhibitory effect on PRMT5, pharmacodynamic (PD) analysis is required by measuring levels of SDMA modifications on acceptor proteins. However, current clinical approaches, such as immunohistochemistry (IHC), measure total SDMA residues across all proteins beyond PRMT5 substrates, and this lack of specificity may decrease sensitivity and dynamic range. Mass spectrometry-based proteomics offers unparalleled insights into the alterations of the proteome from clinically relevant processes and has advanced the fields of biomarker discovery, mechanism elucidation, and personalized medicine. , Targeted proteomics focusing on absolute quantification of a set of preselected targets using multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) and parallel reaction monitoring (PRM) has wide applications in biomedical settings due to its high specificity, sensitivity, and accuracy. Recently, the emergence of global proteomics based on data-independent acquisition-mass spectrometry (DIA-MS) has opened new avenues in clinical studies that benefit from its discovery nature and quantitative capability, allowing for the profiling of thousands of proteins simultaneously, − and localizing post-translational modifications (PTMs). However, DIA-MS is typically considered a discovery tool and poses challenges in confident quantification due to a lack of analytical metrics. To overcome this hurdle, Pino et al. reported the strategy of matrix-matched calibration by spiking a sample of interest into a background proteome of equivalent complexity to approximate limit of detection (LOD), lower limit of quantification (LLOQ), and linear range for every detectable peptide. This work set a foundation for improved quantitative label-free global proteomics.

Here, we conducted an in-depth profiling of dimethylation at specific arginine sites from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumors using DIA-based global proteomics. We analyzed several cell-line-derived xenograft models of NSCLC, characterized the modification levels of over 100 DMA peptides across treatment groups, and discovered candidate biomarkers to monitor for MTA-cooperative PRMT5 inhibition by AZD3470, including G3BP1­(R460). We further characterized the LODs/LLOQs and dynamic ranges of detected targets using calibration curves generated by spiking a human cancer cell line digest into a yeast matrix, enabling more confident quantification.

Experimental Section

Mouse Xenograft Models

Mouse experiments performed in AstraZeneca UK were conducted in compliance with guidelines specified by the UK Home Office Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act 1986 and AstraZeneca Global Bioethics policy. Experimental procedures are outlined in Project License PP3292652, which has gone through the AstraZeneca Ethical Review Process and has been reviewed by the University of Cambridge’s Animal Welfare and Ethical Review Body. Mouse experiments performed in AstraZeneca US were conducted in accordance with the guidelines established by the internal IACUC (Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee) and AstraZeneca Global Bioethics policy.

For the establishment of LU99 xenograft models, 5 × 106 of cells suspended in 0.1 mL of 50% RPMI-1640 and 50% Matrigel were injected subcutaneously into the right flanks of 7–10 week old Nude male mice. Tumors were allowed to reach ∼300 mm3 before randomization into vehicle and AZD3470-treated groups. Treatment was initiated 1 day after randomization, and AZD3470-treated animals were dosed orally with 100 and 10 mg/kg twice daily for 14 or 21 days. On the last treatment day, animals that received the AZD3470 AM dose were sacrificed 6 h later, and the tumor was collected in 10% neutral buffered formalin (NBF).

NCI-H838 xenografts were established by subcutaneous injection of 5 × 106 cells suspended in 0.1 mL of 50% RPMI-1640 and 50% Matrigel into the right front flank of 5–6 week old female NSG mice. Tumors were allowed to reach ∼250 mm3 before randomization into vehicle and AZD3470-treated groups. Treatment was initiated 1 day after randomization, and AZD3470-treated animals were dosed orally with 100, 10, and 1 mg/kg twice daily for 22 days. On the last treatment day, animals that received the AZD3470 AM dose were sacrificed 6 h later, and the tumor was collected in 10% neutral buffered formalin (NBF).

NSCLC Samples

Human NSCLC tissue samples (n = 41) were obtained with patient consent from certified vendors, including Asterand, Indivumed, Tissue Solutions, GE Healthcare, and Tristar. The use of these samples in research at AstraZeneca is covered under approval from the North West – Haydock Research Ethics Committee (REC reference: 22/NW/0102; IRAS ID: 313152). Samples were not obtained from a clinical trial nor exposed to AZD3470.

Tissue Fixation and Processing

All xenograft tissues were fixed in 10% NBF at a pH of 7. Samples were submerged in 10% NBF for 24 h. After fixation, all tissues were transferred to 70% ethanol and processed into FFPE blocks using an automated program on a Leica ASP300S instrument (Leica Biosystems).

Hematoxylin and Eosin Staining

4 μm sections were taken from each FFPE block and left to dry overnight at 37 °C. Slides were then dewaxed in xylene, rehydrated through graded ethanols, before being stained for 5 min with Gills III Hematoxylin (Leica Biosystems), followed by washing in running water, 1% acid alcohol (Leica Biosystems), and Scott’s tap water, and then staining in Eosin (Leica Biosystems) for 3 min, followed by running water, graded ethanols, and xylene. Slides were coverslipped using ClearVue mountant (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Tissue Microarray

Prior to the IHC analysis, tissue microarrays (TMAs) were constructed. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) slides of each block were visualized under a microscope to select tumor regions to punch cores from and place into the TMAs, which were constructed using the Tissue-Tek Quick-Ray Microarray System (Sakura Finetek) and Quick-Ray molds with 1.5–2 mm-size cores. Two cores from the selected regions (generally the center and edge of the tumor) were taken per sample. The completed TMAs were then sectioned and H&E-stained as described above.

IHC Analysis

IHC was carried out on TMA sections that had been left to dry overnight at 37 °C. The primary antibody used for staining SDMA was Symmetric Di-Methyl Arginine Motif [sdme-RG] MultiMab Rabbit mAb mix (Cell Signaling Technologies). Matched-concentration IgG isotype control in place of the primary antibody step was tested during antibody assay validation. Staining of SDMA was carried out on the Leica Bond RX automated staining platform (Leica Biosystems), using Bond Dewax Solution for deparaffinization; Bond Epitope Retrieval Solution 2 (pH 9) at 95 °C for 20 min; followed by a DAB staining protocol using a Bond Polymer Refine detection kit (Leica Biosystems), with Hematoxylin II and Bluing Reagent for counterstaining nuclei (Leica Biosystems).

All H&E and IHC slides were scanned at ×40 using the Aperio GT450 scanner (Leica Biosystems). The image analysis of SDMA was carried out on HALO (Indica Laboratories) using a tumor classifier to exclude any nontumor cells and mouse stroma, before applying a cytonuclear algorithm to generate mean optical density (OD) results. Then the optical density values from the two cores were averaged for each sample. Tumor classifiers and algorithms were adjusted per biomarker.

FFPE Tumor Enrichment from Xenograft Samples by Biopsy Punch for LC-MS

Annotations were generated on an adjacent H&E-stained section. Sections were digitally scanned with a Leica AT2 scanner (Leica BioSystems) at 20× magnification. Images were viewed and manually annotated with Aperio ImageScope software version 12.4.6, which denoted the area of highest density of tumor epithelial cells.

Then, 10 μm-thick FFPE sections were generated and mounted to UV-treated metal-framed polyethylene naphthalate (PEN) membrane histology slides (Leica BioSystems). Slides were deparaffinized, and a light hematoxylin stain was applied to each sample. Because xenograft samples typically show high homogeneity, biopsy punches of appropriate size were employed to maximize the density of tumor epithelial cells dissected from the tissue section. Miltex biopsy punches (Integra Lifesciences) ranged in diameter from 2 to 5 mm. Biopsy punches were collected and placed directly into a 96-well plate with 30 μL acetonitrile (ACN) and stored at −30 °C. Figure S1 shows representative microscopic images of a tissue section scanned pre- and postdissection using a Leica AT2 scanner at 20× magnification for comparison.

FFPE Tumor Enrichment from NSCLC Clinical Samples by Laser Microdissection (LMD) for LC-MS

The 10 μm FFPE tissue sections mounted on PEN membrane slides were deparaffinized using a xylene-based method, hydrated using ethanol, and stained using hematoxylin Gill 1 and 2 (1:1) solution. The hematoxylin-stained tissue samples were coverslipped and scanned using Leica AT2 scanner for HALO-AI (v3.6)-tumor epithelial cells classifier and annotation build. A tissue mask was generated using the HALO-AI machine learning algorithm, with representative samples used to train the model for differentiation between tumor epithelium (TE), immune cells, and stromal cells. To specifically enrich the tumor epithelium cell population, only the TE annotation layer created by HALO-AI was selected for downstream LMD and LC-MS analysis (Figure S2). The HALO-AI-generated TE annotation layer was transferred to Leica LMD7 (software version 8.3). The annotations were dissected using the laser screw method, and the samples were collected in ACN for downstream LC-MS analysis.

Proteomics Sample Preparation

Dissected tumors in ACN were dried down via SpeedVac at 35 °C for 20 min, and then reconstituted in 30 μL of lysis buffer containing 4% sodium dodecyl sulfate (VWR International), 40 mM chloroacetamide (Sigma-Aldrich), and 10 mM TCEP (Thermo Scientific) in 100 mM tris­(hydroxymethyl)­aminomethane (Tris) hydrochloride buffer (Sigma-Aldrich) at pH 8.0. Lysis, reduction, and alkylation were achieved upon incubation at 95 °C while vortexing at 1000 rpm for 90 min in an Eppendorf Thermomixer.

Denatured proteins were then processed in 96-well plates with an automated SP3 protocol on an Agilent Bravo Liquid Handling Platform, adapted from Muller et al. Briefly, Sera-Mag carboxylate-modified magnetic beads A and B (Cytiva) were combined to immobilize both hydrophilic and hydrophobic proteins. Beads were prewashed twice with LC-MS grade water (Honeywell) and resuspended in water to a final working concentration of 50 μg/μL. Five μL of beads and 65 μL of ACN were sequentially aliquoted into each sample by the liquid handler, followed by 5 min of incubation to allow protein binding. The protein-bound beads were consecutively washed with 200 μL of 80% ethanol (×2) and 100 μL ACN (×1). After removal of supernatant, the beads were resuspended in 35 μL of 100 mM Tris hydrochloride buffer. Each sample was digested with 0.5 μg Sequencing Grade Modified Trypsin (Promega) by overnight digestion at 37 °C and 1000 rpm in an Eppendorf Thermomixer. Peptides were acidified by adding 10 μL of 5% trifluoroacetic acid (Sigma-Aldrich), which was then recovered by transferring the supernatant. Peptide concentrations were quantified using the Micro BCA assay (Thermo Scientific) with slight modifications. Briefly, bovine serum albumin (BSA) standards were prepared in 10% RIPA buffer (Thermo Scientific) at final concentrations of 0, 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10, 20, 30, and 40 μg/mL to generate a standard curve. For each sample, 6 μL was diluted in 69 μL of 10% RIPA buffer. 75 μL of Micro BCA working reagent was added, and then the plates were incubated at 37 °C for 2 h, after which the absorbance at 562 nm was measured using a SpectraMax iD3 plate reader. Finally, samples were diluted with water-0.1% formic acid (JT Baker) to 50 ng/μL in a Protein LoBind 96-well plate (Eppendorf), and stored at −80 °C until LC-MS analysis.

Calibration Curve Construction

T47D cells homogeneously embedded in FFPE blocks were purchased from AMSBIO. The FFPE blocks were sectioned onto PEN membrane slides at 10 μm thickness, stained with hematoxylin to enhance visualization, and then a 5 mm-diameter punch was used to provide approximately 74,000 cells/well. Three replicates were lysed and digested using the above-mentioned SP3 procedure, and the peptide digests were pooled and diluted to 50 ng/μL. 100 μg MS-compatible yeast digest (Promega) was resuspended in 500 μL water-0.1% formic acid with sonication for 3 min, and further diluted to 50 ng/μL with water-0.1% formic acid. The calibration curve consists of 14 dilution points, including one pure T47D digest and one pure yeast digest, across 3 orders of magnitude. The preparation of the calibration curve gradients is detailed in the Supporting Information (Table S1). Briefly, the first five points (T47D level at 1, 0.7, 0.5, 0.3, and 0.1) were prepared directly by mixing T47D and yeast working solution at 50 ng/μL. The subsequent gradients were sequentially diluted × 10 twice from vials B-E to avoid propagation of pipetting errors. The LOD was determined by the lowest level that passed the FDR identification threshold for a target, and the LLOQ was defined as the lowest level that met the following four requirements: (1) does not have missing values, (2) exhibits a coefficient of variation (CV) of <20%, (3) shows an R 2 of linear regression fitting of >0.9, and (4) results in a prediction accuracy of ±20%.

LC-MS Acquisition

Samples were mixed with the Biognosys iRT kit calibration peptides and water-0.1% formic acid to 10 ng/μL, where 20 μL (i.e., 200 ng) was directly loaded into Evotips (Evosep) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Peptides were separated with the Evosep One LC system using the standardized 30SPD method over a 15 cm × 150 μm × 1.9 μm analytical column (EV1106, Bruker) at 30 °C. Mobile phase A was 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in water (JT Baker), and mobile phase B was 0.1% (v/v) formic acid in ACN (JT Baker). The LC system was coupled to a timsTOF Pro2 mass spectrometer (Bruker), and data were acquired using a dia-PASEF method. Parameters, including instrument settings and dia-PASEF method, are detailed in Tables S2 and S3 and Figure S3, with the cycle time estimated to be 2.23 s.

Data Analysis

The xenografts and NSCLC patient DIA data were processed using Spectronaut (version 16.3, Biognosys) with default directDIA search settings against a human UniProt database containing 20,400 entries (UP000005640, downloaded 2019/02/06), while the matched matrix calibration curve data were searched against a combined FASTA file of human and yeast (UP000002311, downloaded 2023/02/23) proteome. The false discovery rate (FDR) was set to 0.01, and the precursor/protein Q-value cutoff was also set to 0.01. Carbamidomethyl cysteine was allowed for fixed modification, while oxidation on methionine and methylation/dimethylation on lysine and arginine were allowed for variable modifications. Generally, quantification was normalized against the global median, while no normalization approach was applied to the calibration curves. All statistical analyses were performed in R Studio (R version 4.2.3). Analysis scripts are available at https://github.com/anqitu-massspec/PRMT5i_Publication.

Results and Discussion

Analysis of Arginine Dimethylation by DIA-MS-Based Global Proteomics

We first conducted global DIA-MS-based proteomic analysis on an MTAP-null LU99 cell line-derived xenograft model consisting of a vehicle-treated control group and AZD3470-treated groups at doses of 10 and 100 mg/kg for 14 and 21 days, resulting in the detection of 145 DMA sites from 108 protein groups. The dose-dependent response of each DMA peptide was examined by calculating the Pearson correlation coefficient based on peptide abundances across different doses. As AZD3470 inhibits PRMT5 enzymatic activity, increasing the inhibitor dose is expected to reduce SDMA modification on PRMT5 substrates. Therefore, peptides showing stronger PRMT5 inhibition exhibit increasingly negative correlations between abundance and AZD3470 dose. The correlation coefficient of each peptide was then plotted against its mean abundance in the control group and depicted in Figure . A total of 12 peptides exhibiting a strong negative dose-dependent response (Pearson correlation coefficient r < −0.7) and statistical significance (two-sided t test p < 0.05) were considered candidate PD biomarkers that could be indicative of PRMT5 inhibition efficacy. Table details these potential biomarkers, including the extent of reduction upon 100 mg/kg for 14 days compared to the control group. The relative abundances of six representative peptides are displayed in Figure . Among the 12 candidate peptides, arginine dimethylation of G3BP1 at R460 exhibited the most pronounced extent of reduction (93%) in response to the dose of 100 mg/kg, inferring a higher dynamic range, and its relatively high baseline abundance enhanced the detectability after PRMT5 inhibition. Furthermore, G3BP1­(R460) is preferable as a PD biomarker from an analytical perspective, considering the peptide sequence does not contain a methionine, which may be readily oxidized. Also, no other dimethylation site for this sequence is reported in the UniProt database, which can potentially reduce experimental variation. In contrast, although other highly correlated peptides (e.g., from SNRPB) were considered, they contain multiple arginine residues that can be dimethylated. This multiplicity can split the mass spectrometry signal across various modified peptide sequences and increase analytical variability, making them less developable as a clinical assay. Site-specific dimethylation of G3BP1 at R460 was confirmed by manual inspection of the MS2 fragmentation spectrum (Figure S4). Therefore, G3BP1­(R460) was selected as the primary biomarker to assess PRMT5 inhibition efficacy.

G3BP1 is known to promote the formation of stress granules in response to stress exposure, − and its overexpression in various cancers has been associated with multifaceted roles in promoting oncogenic signaling pathways, facilitating tumor cell proliferation/metastasis, and modulating stress responses. , G3BP1 is subjected to a variety of post-translational modifications (PTMs), including methylation by PRMTs, where R460 has been identified as a specific methylation site for PRMT5. These findings collectively highlight the sensitivity and specificity of G3BP1­(R460) as a PD biomarker for PRMT5 inhibition.

Characterization of DIA-MS Quantitative Metrics Using the Calibration Curve

G3BP1­(R460) was selected as the primary PD biomarker and was further characterized in multiple NSCLC xenograft models. To facilitate a more robust and quantitative measurement, several key analytical metrics of G3BP1­(R460) by DIA-MS were established using a 14-point calibration curve by spiking a human breast cancer cell line digest, T47D, into a yeast digest and injecting in triplicate (Figure A). With the criteria defined in the Experimental Section, the LOD and LLOQ of G3BP1­(R460) were determined to be approximately 60 and 300 in abundance, respectively, as shown in Figure B. The LOD is approximately 40 times lower than the median G3BP1­(R460) abundance detected in the NSCLC tissue cohorts, which highlights that the DIA-MS assay allows for the quantitative PD assessment of PRMT5 inhibition. It is essential to acknowledge that although the peptide abundance readouts can be mass spectrometer- and sample matrix-dependent, the LOD and LLOQ provide valuable reference points for the quantification of targets in this study.

Reduction of G3BP1­(R460) upon PRMT5 Inhibition in Preclinical Xenograft Models

The PD effect of AZD3470 measured by dimethylated G3BP1­(R460) was analyzed in cell-line-derived xenograft samples (n = 71, Table S4). In the control group, the G3BP1­(R460) levels for all samples were above the established LOD and LLOQ; in the treated groups, all samples were above the LOD, and 70% were above the LLOQ. As shown in Figure A, the levels of G3BP1­(R460) demonstrated a strong dose-dependent reduction. More than 90% loss of DMA signals was observed at the dose of 100 mg/kg, reflecting a substantial target engagement. Notably, the total G3BP1 protein expression remained consistent across all dose groups (Figure B), indicating that AZD3470 has a minimal impact on the expression of G3BP1 protein.

Comparison of the IHC SDMA Assay and DIA-MS Proteomics Assay

In addition to DIA-MS, an IHC assay was developed to quantify overall SDMA from solid tumors, and the staining intensity was measured as the optical density. Representative IHC images demonstrating SDMA expression are shown in Figure , alongside their corresponding average nuclear optical density (OD) values. The G3BP1­(R460) levels by DIA-MS and the SDMA readings by IHC from identical specimens are compared in Figure A, showing that these two assays exhibited strong concordance with R 2 ranging from 0.72 to 0.82. Despite the overall good agreement between the two methods, the percentage decrease of SDMA levels at 100 mg/kg relative to the control group detected by IHC (Figure B) ranged from 62% to 77%. It was not as substantial as that of G3BP1­(R460) by DIA-MS for MTAP-null models (94%), presumably due to cross-reactivity of the antibody and/or SDMAs not attributable to PRMT5, also contributing to the total SDMA level. In addition, the SDMA levels from MTAP-null models by IHC changed insignificantly when increasing the dose from 10 mg/kg to 100 mg/kg, in contrast to G3BP1­(R460) abundances by DIA-MS, where further reduction continued (6%–15%) as the dose escalated, indicating that IHC may be less sensitive compared to DIA-MS. Although the DIA-MS assay does not differentiate SDMA from ADMA, its specificity in unambiguously pinpointing the arginine dimethylation sites on the specific substrates of PRMT5 enables the quantification of PRMT5 activity loss directly with AZD3470. Therefore, DIA-MS of G3BP1­(R460) and IHC of SDMA are complementary, and their integration can enhance clinical trial decision-making.

Expression of G3BP1­(R460) in NSCLC Clinical Samples

The DIA-MS assay was subsequently deployed to measure the expression of G3BP1­(R460) in clinical NSCLC tumor FFPE tissues (n = 41) in the absence of PRMT5 inhibition to assess the baseline expression and biological variability, aiming to translate these findings into a clinical context. The results (Figure ) showed that G3BP1­(R460) was expressed at 2672 ± 1200 in abundance at baseline, suggesting that a reduction in G3BP1­(R460) modification close to 90% could be reliably quantified with the DIA-MS assay.

Conclusions

Our study presents the development and application of a novel DIA-MS proteomics assay to comprehensively profile arginine dimethylation on specific proteins from FFPE tissues without DMA enrichment procedures. The quantitative performance of DIA-MS was characterized by constructing calibration curves to establish the pivotal analytical metrics. We identified dimethylated G3BP1 at R460 as a robust PD biomarker that can be used to investigate PRMT5 enzymatic activity in MTAP-deficient solid tumors following its inhibition. The use of G3BP1­(R460) demonstrates its response to PRMT5 inhibition in a dose-dependent manner, which revealed a reduction exceeding 90% at 100 mg/kg of treatment in xenograft models. In comparison to a traditional IHC assay for SDMA, the proteomics assay described above showed a moderate to strong concordance while also exhibiting better specificity and sensitivity. The applicability of G3BP1­(R460) by DIA-MS in clinical trials may provide a valuable tool for monitoring the efficacy and pharmacodynamics of PRMT5 inhibition using second-generation MTA-cooperative inhibitors and support dose selection.
