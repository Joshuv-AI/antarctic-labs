A Dirac–type problem

How does the Hajnal–Szemereédi Theorem generalise to
H
-factors, for a given graph
H
? Shokoufandeh and Zhao
[
26
]
proved that if
\delta(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{\chi_{cr}(H)}\right)n
, then
G
contains a collection of vertex-disjoint copies of
H
covering all but a constant number of vertices; here,
\chi_{cr}(H)
is the
critical chromatic number
of
H
. This result was previously conjectured by Komlós
[
20
]
. The minimum degree condition is optimal for every graph
H
, and the presence of a constant number of uncovered vertices is unavoidable (see Kühn, Osthus
[
24
]
).

We show the
fractional stability version
of
[
26
]
. To be precise, a
weighted graph
is a pair
(F,w_{F})
, where
F
is a graph and
w_{F}:V(F)\longrightarrow\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
is a weight function.
For a family of weighted graphs
{\mathcal{F}}
, an
{\mathcal{F}}
-packing
in
G
is a collection of graphs
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
F_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}
, with corresponding embeddings
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
into
G
, such that for any

,

\sum_{i\in I:Im(f_{i})\ni x}w_{F_{i}}(f_{i}^{-1}(x))\leq 1.

In other words, for each
x\in V(G)
the total weight in
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
of all vertices mapped to
x
is at most 1. For now, we only consider the case
{\mathcal{F}}=\{H\}
, and thus refer to
H
-packings
†
†

†

.

Proof ideas

Let us mention a few proof ingredients, which also justifies treating the cases
s\geq r/2
and
s<r/2
separately. In short, the present proof requires substantial new results and ideas, including Theorem
1.4
. On the other hand, the present proof does not imply the result of
[
4
]
. Finally, part of the article
[
4
]
was devoted to developing tools for constructing ‘spread embeddings’ in regular partitions and applying the recently proved (fractional) Kahn–Kalai conjecture
[
12
,
25
]
. We now use these tools (Lemma
2.7
) as a black box.

Recall that
r=ms+t
with
t<s
, and that we have to prove the statement for every deterministic graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq 1-\frac{s}{r}
. We distinguish a few cases depending on the structure of
G
. Note that the extremal example is
close to
the graph in Figure
1.1
, where
|A|=\frac{sn}{r}-o(n)
, and
G[A]
induces a sparse random-like graph
[
4
]
.

Most of the work and innovation lies in the
non-extremal case
, where
G
does not contain an almost independent set of size roughly
\frac{sn}{r}
. In this case, we apply Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma
[
28
]
, and wish to find a useful substructure in the
reduced
graph. The useful structure is essentially given by Theorem
1.4
.

Even upon finding this structure, we are not done using standard techniques. Namely, a lot of work is devoted to covering the leftover vertices and ensuring exactly the desired part sizes in the regular partition. This is also a major challenge in
[
11
,
4
]
, and whenever exact minimum degree conditions are needed. The absorption strategy for resolving this issue is different from
[
4
]
, and it relies heavily on the fact that, since
\delta(G)>\frac{1}{2}
, the graph is connected, and any pair of vertices has many common neighbours (see, e.g. Definition
4.18
).

In the extremal case, the host graph
G
contains an almost independent set
A
of size roughly
\frac{sn}{r}
, and, due to the minimum degree condition, most edges between
A
and
V\smallsetminus A
are present. Therefore, in constructing a
K_{r}
-factor, most of the copies of
K_{r}
use exactly
s
vertices from
A
, and the respective sets should span cliques in the random graph
G_{n,p}
. However, a number of copies of
K_{s+1}
in
G[A]
may also be required, which is the bottleneck for our construction and which in turn determines the threshold density
p_{s}
.

An additional difficulty in the present paper are
intermediate cases
. For instance, suppose that
G
consists of an independent set
A
of size roughly
\frac{sn}{r}
, but the remaining graph
G[V\smallsetminus A]
does not have a particular structure. The main idea for resolving this case is an inductive approach: recalling that
G^{p}=G\cup G_{n,p}
, we can find a
K_{r-s}
-factor in
G^{p}[V\smallsetminus A]
(which roughly satisfies the
correct
minimum-degree condition and is
non-extremal
), and a
K_{s}
-factor in
G^{p}[A]
. The aim and the difficulty is then to construct those factors in a way that they can be combined into a
K_{r}
-factor. An additional hurdle is that
|A|
can differ from
\frac{sn}{r}
. Most of these issues are addressed in Section
3
. In particular, a stronger conclusion is needed in the
non-extremal
case (Theorem
3.1
).

Finally, a careful reader may notice that the number of possible cases is roughly
\lfloor r/s\rfloor
, and the case distinction (Lemma
3.3
) is rather refined.

2.2.
Regularity

In this section, we recall some standard terminology and results regarding regularity.
Let
G
be a graph, and let
V_{1},V_{2}\subset V(G)
be disjoint subsets of the vertices of
G
. For non-empty sets
X_{1}\subseteq V_{1}
,
X_{2}\subseteq V_{2}
, we define the
density of
G[X_{1},X_{2}]
as
d_{G}(X_{1},X_{2})\coloneq\tfrac{e_{G}(X_{1},X_{2})}{|X_{1}||X_{2}|}
.
Given
\varepsilon,d>0
, we say that a pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
is
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular
in
G
if for
i=1,2
and for all sets
X_{i}\subseteq V_{i}
with
|X_{i}|\geq\varepsilon|V_{i}|
, we have
|d_{G}(X_{1},X_{2})-d|<\varepsilon
. We say that a pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
is
(\varepsilon,d^{+})
-regular if it is
(\varepsilon,d^{\prime})
-regular for some
d^{\prime}\geq d
. We often omit the subscript
G
if it is clear from the context.

We will use the following standard lemmas, which can be found, for example, in
[
1
,
21
]
. We begin by recalling the Slicing Lemma.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
1
]
, Lemma 2.7)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<\beta
,
d\leq 1
and let
(V_{1},V_{2})
be an
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular pair. Then for any pair
(U_{1},U_{2})
such that for
i=1,2
,
U_{i}\subset V_{i}
with
|U_{i}|\geq\beta|V_{i}|
, the pair
(U_{1},U_{2})
is
(\varepsilon^{\prime},d^{\prime})
-regular with
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\max\{\varepsilon/\beta,2\varepsilon\}
and some
d^{\prime}>0
satisfying
|d^{\prime}-d|\leq\varepsilon
.

The following lemma is similar. Although the proof is standard, we have not found a self-contained statement in the literature, so we prove it here.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
0<\xi<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
d\leq 1
and let
(U_{1},U_{2})
be an
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular pair. Then for any pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
such that for
i=1,2
,
U_{i}\subset V_{i}
with
|V_{i}\smallsetminus U_{i}|\leq\xi|U_{i}|
, the pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
is
(\varepsilon^{\prime},d^{\prime})
-regular with
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\max\{\xi/\varepsilon,6\varepsilon\}
and some
d^{\prime}>0
satisfying
|d^{\prime}-d|<3\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
A_{i}\subseteq V_{i}
with
|A_{i}|\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}|V_{i}|
. It is easy to see that
|A_{i}\cap U_{i}|\geq(\varepsilon^{\prime}-\xi)|U_{i}|\geq\varepsilon|U_{i}|
and
|A_{i}\cap U_{i}|\geq|A_{i}|-|V_{i}\smallsetminus U_{i}|\geq|A_{i}|-\xi|U_{i}|\geq|A_{i}|-\xi|V_{i}|\geq|A_{i}|(1-\xi/\varepsilon^{\prime})\geq|A_{i}|(1-\varepsilon).

We have

\displaystyle e_{G}(A_{1},A_{2})

\displaystyle\geq e_{G}(A_{1}\cap U_{1},A_{2}\cap U_{2})\geq d(1-\varepsilon)|A_{1}\cap U_{1}||A_{2}\cap U_{2}|

\displaystyle\geq d(1-\varepsilon)^{3}|A_{1}||A_{2}|\geq(d-3\varepsilon)|A_{1}||A_{2}|

and, since
|A_{i}\smallsetminus U_{i}|\leq\xi|V_{i}|\leq\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}|A_{i}|
,

\displaystyle e_{G}(A_{1},A_{2})

\displaystyle\leq e_{G}(A_{1}\cap U_{1},A_{2}\cap U_{2})+\frac{2\xi}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}|A_{1}||A_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(d+\varepsilon+\frac{2\xi}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\right)|A_{1}||A_{2}|\leq(d+3\varepsilon)|A_{1}||A_{2}|.

As for the density bound, we have

d^{\prime}=\frac{e(V_{1},V_{2})}{|V_{1}||V_{2}|}\geq\frac{e(U_{1},U_{2})}{(1+\xi)^{2}|U_{1}||U_{2}|}\geq(1-3\xi)d\geq d-3\xi

and

d^{\prime}\leq\frac{e(V_{1},V_{2})}{|V_{1}||V_{2}|}\leq\frac{e(U_{1},U_{2})+2\xi|U_{1}||U_{2}|+\xi^{2}|U_{1}||U_{2}|}{|U_{1}||U_{2}|}\leq d+3\xi.

∎

4.2.

Throughout this subsection, let us assume that

b\in\mathds{N}
such that
G
has a
\left\{b^{-1}\ltimes T\right\}
-packing with remainder at most
C
. Furthermore, each copy of
T
has a
\left\{b_{1}^{-1}\ltimes B(m,s,t)\right\}
-factor, for some integer
b_{1}
, which in turn yields a
\left\{b_{2}^{-1}\ltimes H\right\}
-factor for some integer
b_{2}
, as required.
∎

4.4.
Constructing the absorber

In this subsection, let
r=ms+t
with
1\leq t\leq s
. This is a small change of parametrization compared to the previous subsections, as the case
t=s
will now be parametrised as
r=(m+1)s
instead of
r=ms
.

Definition 4.18
.

A clique
K_{g}
in
G
is called
good
for a vertex
v
if
v
has at least
g-2
neighbours in
K_{g}
. A
(k,m,\delta)
-absorber
in
G
is a collection
{\mathcal{A}}
of vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{m+k}
in
G
covering at most
10(k+m)\delta n
vertices and such that for each pair of vertices
u,v\in V(G)
,
{\mathcal{A}}
contains at least
\delta^{2}n
cliques which are good for both
u
and
v
.

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
0<\delta<(4mr)^{-1}
and suppose that
\delta(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{s}{r}-\delta\right)n
.

(i)

If
t<s
, then
G
contains a
(1,m,\delta_{2})
-absorber for some constant
\delta_{2}=\delta_{2}(r)>0
.

(ii)

Suppose
t=s
, and that every subset
X\subset V(G)
with
|X|\geq\left(\frac{1}{m+1}-m\delta\right)n
satisfies
e_{G}(X)\geq\delta_{1}n^{2}
for some
\delta_{1}>0
. Then
G
contains a
(2,m,\delta_{2})
-absorber for some constant
\delta_{2}=\delta_{2}(r,\delta_{1})>0
.

Proof.

For both cases we will need two auxiliary claims.

Claim 4.20
.

Let
\ell=n^{O(1)}
, and let
{\mathcal{K}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}
, be collections of
K_{g}
-cliques in a graph
G
satisfying
|{\mathcal{K}}_{i}|\geq\xi n^{g}
. Then there is a collection
{\mathcal{A}}
of vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{g}
covering at most
\xi gn
vertices such that

|{\mathcal{A}}\cap{\mathcal{K}}_{i}|\geq\xi^{2}n/50

for
i\in[\ell]
.

Proof.

Suppose without loss of generality that
|{\mathcal{K}}_{i}|=\xi n^{g}
for each
i\in[\ell]
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}=\bigcup_{i}{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
, and note that
|{\mathcal{K}}|\leq n^{g}
since
{\mathcal{K}}
consists only of copies of
K_{g}
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
be a random subfamily of
{\mathcal{K}}
containing each element of
{\mathcal{K}}
independently with probability
\frac{\xi}{10}n^{1-g}
. We have

\mathbb{E}[|{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}|]=\frac{\xi}{10}n^{1-g}|{\mathcal{K}}|\leq\frac{\xi}{10}n,\quad\mathbb{E}[|{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathcal{K}}_{i}|]=\frac{\xi}{10}n^{1-g}\xi n^{g}=\frac{\xi^{2}}{10}n.

By Chernoff bounds we also have
\mathbb{P}\left[|{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}|>\frac{\xi}{5}n\right]\leq e^{-\frac{\xi}{20}n}
and
\mathbb{P}\left[|{\mathcal{A}}\cap{\mathcal{K}}_{i}|<\frac{\xi^{2}}{20}n\right]\leq e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{80}n}
for each
i\in[\ell]
, so by the union bound, the probability of any of these events occurring is at most
(\ell+1)e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{80}n}=o(1)
. Next, we bound the number of intersecting pairs of copies of
K_{g}
in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. We have

\mathbb{E}[|\{(K_{1},K_{2})\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\mid K_{1}\cap K_{2}\neq\varnothing\}|]\leq n^{2g-1}\left(\frac{\xi}{10}n^{1-g}\right)^{2}=\frac{\xi^{2}}{100}n.

By Markov’s inequality, with probability at least
1/2
the number of such pairs is at most
\left(\xi^{2}/50\right)n
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the collection of
K_{g}
copies achieved by removing one of the cliques in each intersecting pair. Notice that
|{\mathcal{A}}|\leq|{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}|<\xi n
, so
{\mathcal{A}}
covers at most
\xi gn
vertices. Moreover, with probability at least
1/2-o(1)
, the collection
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies

|{\mathcal{A}}\cap{\mathcal{K}}_{i}|\geq\frac{\xi^{2}}{20}n-\frac{\xi^{2}}{50}n\geq\frac{\xi^{2}}{50}n

for each
i\in[\ell]
. Hence, there exists a family
{\mathcal{A}}
which satisfies the statement of the Lemma.
∎

Claim 4.21
.

In both case (i) and (ii), if
\delta(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{s}{r}-\delta\right)n
, any set
S
of at most
m
vertices of
G
has at least
\left(\frac{t}{r}-m\delta\right)n
common neighbours.

Proof.

It is enough to upper-bound the number of vertices that are a non-neighbour of at least one vertex in
S
. This number is at most

mn\left(\frac{s}{r}+\delta\right)=\frac{n(ms+mr\delta)}{r}=n\left(1-\frac{t-mr\delta}{r}\right).

∎

Suppose now that we are in case
(i)
. We construct the collections
{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
as follows.
Let
\xi_{1}=\frac{t}{2r}
and
\xi=\frac{\xi_{1}^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}
. Note that
\left(\frac{t}{r}-m\delta\right)\geq\xi_{1}
. Order the pairs of vertices of
G
arbitrarily and suppose
\{u,v\}
is the
i
-th pair of vertices. Using Claim
4.21
repeatedly, we can find vertices
w_{1},\dots,w_{m-1}
such that
w_{k}\in N_{G}(\{u,v,w_{1},\dots,w_{k-1}\})
, for all
k\in[m-1]
. Notice that the set
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{m-1}\}
induces in
G
a copy of
K_{m-1}
. By a simple counting argument, there are at least
\frac{\xi_{1}^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}n^{m-1}
different choices for the set
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{m-1}\}
, i.e. we can find a collection of
\frac{\xi_{1}^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}n^{m-1}
different copies of
K_{m-1}
that are contained in
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)
.
Next, extend those copies of
K_{m-1}
, again using Claim
4.21
twice, to get copies of
K_{m+1}
. Each of these has at least
m-1
vertices from
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)
, and there are at least
\frac{\xi_{1}^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}n^{m+1}
of them, by the same counting argument as before. This gives a collection
{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
of
K_{m+1}
-cliques with
|{\mathcal{K}}_{i}|\geq\xi n^{m+1}
, for each
i\in[\binom{n}{2}]
. Applying Claim
4.20
with
\ell={n\choose 2}
and
g=m+1
, we get a collection
{\mathcal{A}}
which is a
(1,m,\delta_{2})
-absorber for
\delta_{2}\leq\xi/10
.

Next, we prove
(ii)
. Let
\xi_{1}=\min\left\{\delta_{1},\frac{t}{2r}\right\}
and
\xi=\frac{\xi_{1}^{m}}{(m+2)!}
. Notice that by Claim
4.21
and the assumptions of
(ii)
, we have the following property

(
\ast
)

for any set
S
of
m
vertices,
N_{G}(S)
spans at least
\delta_{1}n
edges in
G
.

As before, using Claim
4.21
, we can find
\frac{\xi_{1}^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}n^{m-2}
copies of
K_{m-2}
contained in
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)
. Using (
\ast
), these
K_{m-2}
-copies can be extended to
K_{m}
-copies in
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)
, and, once again using (
\ast
), these
K_{m}
-copies can in turn be extended to
K_{m+2}
-copies which are good for both
u
and
v
. Hence, we get a collection
{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
of
K_{m+2}
-copies with
|{\mathcal{K}}_{i}|\geq\xi n^{m+2}
. Applying Claim
4.20
with
\ell=\binom{n}{2}
and
g=m+2
, we get a collection
{\mathcal{A}}
which is a
(2,m,\delta_{2})
-absorber for
\delta_{2}\leq\xi/10
.
∎

Next, we partition our graph with an absorber.

Lemma 4.22
.

Suppose
r=ms+t
with
1<t\leq s
. Let
g=0
if
t<s
and
g=1
if
t=s
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
\gamma>0
the following holds. There exist constants
\delta,\delta_{2}>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that every graph
G
on
n
vertices,
n\geq n_{0}
, with
\delta(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{s}{r}-\delta\right)n
, satisfies one of the following two conditions.

(i)

There is a collection
\mathfrak{A}
of vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{m+1+g},Q_{1}^{(m+g)},\dots,Q_{m}^{(m+g)}
covering all but at most
C
vertices such that
\mathfrak{A}
includes a
(1+g,m,\delta_{2})
-absorber, or

(ii)

There is a subset
A\subset V(G)
with
|A|=\frac{sn}{r}
such that
e_{G}(A)<\gamma n^{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
t<s
, which implies
g=0
.
Let
C\gg r
and
n_{0}^{-1}\ll\delta_{\ref{l:6.2chr}}\ll\gamma,r^{-1}
, where
\delta_{\ref{l:6.2chr}}
is the constant obtained by applying Lemma
4.3
with
\gamma_{\ref{l:6.2chr}}=\gamma/2
. Assume that
2\delta\leq\delta_{\ref{l:6.2chr}}
. Moreover, let
\delta_{2}
be sufficiently small so that
30m\delta_{2}\leq\delta_{\ref{l:6.2chr}}
and the conclusion of Lemma
4.19

(i)
holds. Due to Lemma
4.19
, there is a
(1,m,\delta_{2})
-absorber
\mathfrak{U}
for
G
. Let
G^{\prime}=G-\mathfrak{U}
be the graph obtained by removing vertices from the copies of
K_{m+1}
in
\mathfrak{U}
and set
n^{\prime}=|V(G^{\prime})|
. Since

we have
\delta(G^{\prime})\geq\delta(G)-10(m+1)\delta_{2}n\geq\left(1-\frac{s}{r}-\delta_{\ref{l:6.2chr}}\right)n^{\prime}
. Now apply Lemma
4.3
to
G^{\prime}
. We either get a collection of vertex disjoint copies of
K_{m+1},Q_{1}^{(m)},\dots,Q_{m}^{(m)}
covering all but at most
C
vertices of
G^{\prime}
, which implies
(i)
, or we get an independent set of size
\left(\frac{s}{r}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)n^{\prime}>\left(\frac{s}{r}-\gamma\right)n
, which implies
(ii)
, since we can extend this subset by adding arbitrary
\gamma n
vertices.

Suppose now that
t=s
, so that
g=1
.
Similarly to the previous case, take
C\gg r
and
n_{0}^{-1}\ll\delta,\delta_{2}\ll\gamma,r^{-1}
. Assume that every set of size
\frac{sn}{r}=\frac{n}{m+1}
contains at least
\gamma n^{2}
edges. Applying Lemma
4.19

(ii)
to
G
we get a
(2,m,\delta_{2})
-absorber
\mathfrak{U}
for
G
. Let
G^{\prime}=G-\mathfrak{A}
be the graph obtained by removing vertices from the copies of
K_{m+2}
in
\mathfrak{U}
and set
n^{\prime}=|V(G^{\prime})|
. Since
n^{\prime}\geq(1-10(m+2)\delta_{2})n
we have
\delta(G^{\prime})\geq\delta(G)-10(m+2)\delta_{2}n\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{m+1}-\delta-10(m+2)\delta_{2}\right)n^{\prime}
. Now apply Corollary
4.4
to
G^{\prime}
. We either get a collection of vertex disjoint copies of
K_{m+2},Q_{1}^{(m+1)},\dots,Q_{m+1}^{(m+1)}
covering all but at most
C
vertices of
G^{\prime}
, which implies
(i)
, or we get an independent set of size
\left(\frac{s}{r}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)n^{\prime}>\left(\frac{s}{r}-\gamma\right)n
, which would imply

, leading to a contradiction.
∎

5.
The fully non-extremal case

In this section, we prove Theorem
3.1
. To avoid the proof getting too long, we break it down into two arguments. The first part of the argument are standard manipulations in the reduced graph after applying Lemma
4.22
. The second part, Section
5.2
, is more delicate and requires new ideas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\gamma,\zeta
and
r\in\mathbf{N}
be given with
0<\zeta\ll\gamma
. Take any constants
\delta=\delta(\gamma)
,
d=d(\gamma)
,
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(d)
satisfying the following hierarchy

(5.1)

\zeta\ll\varepsilon\ll d\ll\delta,\delta_{2}\ll\gamma,r^{-1}.

Let
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon)
be sufficiently large. Let
r=ms+t
with
m\geq 1
,
0<t\leq s
. Let
g=0
if
t<s
and
g=1
if
t=s
.

Assume that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph,
n\geq n_{0}
, with
\delta(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{s}{r}-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)n
, and that every subset of
V(G)
of size
\frac{sn}{r}
contains at least
\gamma n^{2}
edges.
Then
G
has a vertex partition into sets
S_{i,j}
,
U_{k,\ell}
and
X
, where
i\in[L_{1}],j\in[m+1]
,
k\in[L-L_{1}]
and
\ell\in[m+1+g]
,
L_{1}\geq(1-10r\delta_{2})L
, with the following properties. Let
R
be the graph whose vertices are the sets
S_{i,j}
and
U_{k,\ell}
, where two vertices
A
and
B
are connected if
(A,B)
is an
(\varepsilon,d^{+})
-regular pair. Then

(i)

For each
i\in[L_{1}]
and
j<j^{\prime}\in[m+1]
, the edges
S_{i,j}S_{i,j^{\prime}}
are in
R
. Similarly, for each
k\in[L-L_{1}]
and
\ell<\ell^{\prime}\in[m+1+g]
, the edges
U_{k,\ell}U_{k,\ell^{\prime}}
are in
R
.

(ii)

For each
i\in[L_{1}]
,

|S_{i,1}|=|S_{i,2}|=\ldots=|S_{i,m}|=\frac{s}{t}\cdot|S_{i,m+1}|,

|S_{i,1}\cup S_{i,2}\cup\dots\cup S_{i,m+1}|=\frac{n}{L}(1\pm\varepsilon),

and
(S_{i,1},\dots,S_{i,m+1})
is a
(\varepsilon,d^{+},d)
-super-regular tuple.

For each
k\in[L-L_{1}]
,

|U_{k,1}|=|U_{k,2}|=\ldots=|U_{k,m+1+g}|,

|U_{k,1}\cup U_{k,2}\cup\dots\cup U_{k,m+1+g}|=\frac{n}{L}(1\pm\varepsilon),

and
(U_{k,1},\dots,U_{k,m+1+g})
is a
(\varepsilon,d^{+},d)
-super-regular tuple.

(iii)

For
A,B\in V(R)
, there are at least
dL
indices
k\in[L-L_{1}]
such that both
A
and
B
have at least
m-1+g

R
-neighbours in
\{U_{k,1},U_{k,2},\ldots,U_{k,m+1+g}\}
.

(iv)

|X|<\varepsilon n/2
.

Proof.

Let
C=C(r)
be the constant given by Lemma
4.22
.
Let
d_{1}=16d
,
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon^{2}
and
\ell_{0}=\lceil 2C/\varepsilon\rceil
. Let
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon)
be given by Lemma
2.10
.
First, apply the Regularity Lemma (Lemma
2.10
) with
\ell_{0}
,
\varepsilon_{\ref{l:regularity}}=\varepsilon_{1}
,
d_{\ref{l:regularity}}=d_{1}
and
\alpha_{\ref{l:regularity}}=1-\frac{s}{r}-\frac{\delta}{2}
to
G
, to obtain an
\varepsilon_{1}
-regular partition
V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup\dots\cup V_{\ell^{\prime}}
with

(5.2)

\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell_{0}\geq\frac{2C}{\varepsilon_{1}},

and let
R
be the
(\varepsilon_{1},d_{1})
-reduced graph corresponding to that partition. Note that
\delta(R)\geq\left(1-\frac{s}{r}-\delta\right)\ell^{\prime}
. We claim that every set of size at least
\frac{s}{r}\ell^{\prime}
in
R
spans at least
\frac{\gamma}{4}\ell^{\prime 2}
edges in
R
. Indeed, it is enough to consider sets of size exactly
\frac{s}{r}\ell^{\prime}
, so suppose that
I
is such a set, i.e.
|I|=\frac{s}{r}\ell^{\prime}
, and
I
spans in
R
less than
\frac{\gamma}{4}\ell^{\prime 2}
edges. Let
V_{I}=\bigcup_{i\in I}V_{i}
and note that for each
i\in I
we have
(1-\varepsilon_{1})\frac{n}{\ell^{\prime}}\leq|V_{i}|\leq\frac{n}{\ell^{\prime}}
. Then

\displaystyle e(G[V_{I}])

\displaystyle\leq{|I|\choose 2}|V_{1}|^{2}(d_{1}+\varepsilon_{1})+\frac{\gamma}{4}\ell^{\prime 2}|V_{I}|^{2}+|I|{|V_{1}|\choose 2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|I||V_{1}|^{2}\left(|I|(d_{1}+\varepsilon_{1})+1\right)+\frac{\gamma}{4}n^{2}\leq\frac{3}{4}\gamma n^{2}.

Moreover,

|V_{I}|\geq\frac{s}{r}(1-\varepsilon_{1})n\geq\left(\frac{s}{r}-\frac{\gamma}{8}\right)n.

If we now take an arbitrary set of vertices
W\supset V_{I}
of size
\frac{sn}{r}
, then

e(G[W])\leq e(G[V_{I}])+\frac{\gamma}{8}n^{2}<\gamma n^{2},

which contradicts the assumption on
G
.
Note that this also implies that
R
has no independent set of size
\left(\frac{s}{r}-\frac{\gamma}{4}\right)\ell^{\prime}
.

Recall that
g=0
if
t<s
and
g=1
if
t=s
. We can apply Lemma
4.22
to
R
to get a collection
\mathfrak{A}
consisting of vertex disjoint copies of
K_{m+1+g},Q_{1}^{(m+g)},\ldots,Q_{m}^{(m+g)}
and a
(1+g,m,\delta_{2})
-absorber (where
\delta_{2}=\delta_{2}(\gamma)>0
). Let
\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{A}
be the subcollection consisting of copies of
K_{m+1+g}
in the absorber, and let
\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{A}\smallsetminus\mathfrak{U}
be the remaining part. Note that by the definition of the absorber,
|V(\mathfrak{U})|\leq 10(m+1+g)\delta_{2}|V(R)|=10(m+1+g)\delta_{2}\ell^{\prime}
. Moreover, let
Y
be the set of vertices of
R
not covered by
\mathfrak{A}
and note that
|Y|\leq C
. The next step is to subdivide the sets in
V(\mathfrak{S})
. To this end, we use the
\left\{\frac{1}{b}\ltimes T\right\}
-factor given by Corollary
4.16
.

The
\left\{\frac{1}{b}\ltimes T\right\}
-factor of
\mathfrak{S}
is given by a collection of vertex sets

\left(\{\sigma_{1}^{i},\dots,\sigma_{m+1}^{i}\}\right)_{i\in[L_{1}]}

for a specific
L_{1}
to be determined in a moment, and a function

f:\bigcup_{j\in[m+1],\,i\in[L_{1}]}\{\sigma_{j}^{i}\}\longrightarrow V({\mathfrak{S}})

such that
f(\sigma_{j}^{i})f(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{i})
is an edge of
R
for each
i\in[L_{1}]
and
j,j^{\prime}\in[m+1]
,
j\neq j^{\prime}
.
Moreover, since the total weight of
\frac{1}{b}\ltimes T
is
\frac{1}{b}
, and a
\left\{\frac{1}{b}\ltimes T\right\}
-factor has residue
0
, we have
L_{1}=b|V(\mathfrak{S})|
. We now define a family of sets
S^{\prime}_{i,j}\subseteq V(G)
corresponding to vertices
\sigma^{i}_{j}
, for
i\in[L_{1}]
and
j\in[m+1]
. For a set
S\in V(\mathfrak{S})
, each vertex
x\in S
randomly chooses one of the sets
S^{\prime}_{i,j}
if and only if
f(\sigma^{i}_{j})=S
. Moreover, if this is the case,
x
chooses
S^{\prime}_{i,j}
with probability

\frac{1}{b}\cdot w(\sigma^{i}_{j})=\begin{cases}\frac{s}{br},&j\in[m]\\
\frac{t}{br},&j=m+1.\end{cases}

Since
f
is a
\left\{\frac{1}{b}\ltimes T\right\}
-factor, this selection gives us a probability measure for each vertex
x\in\bigcup_{S\in V(\mathfrak{S})}S
. Hence, using Chernoff bounds, we get that w.h.p.

|S^{\prime}_{i,j}|=\begin{cases}\frac{s}{br}|V_{1}|(1\pm\varepsilon_{1}),&j\in[m]\\
\frac{t}{br}|V_{1}|(1\pm\varepsilon),&j=m+1,\end{cases}

and

|S^{\prime}_{i,1}\cup S^{\prime}_{i,2}\cup\dots\cup S^{\prime}_{i,m+1}|=\frac{1}{b}|V_{1}|(1\pm\varepsilon_{1}).

Next, we split the sets in
V(\mathfrak{U})
into sets
U^{\prime}_{k,\ell}
, for
k\in[L_{2}]
and
\ell\in[m+1+g]
, using a similar approach. This time each set
U\in V(\mathfrak{U})
is split randomly into
b(m+1+g)
sets so that we get
L_{2}=b(m+1+g)|\mathfrak{U}|=b|V(\mathfrak{U})|\leq 10b(m+1+g)\delta_{2}\ell^{\prime}
and

|U^{\prime}_{k,\ell}|=\frac{1}{b(m+1+g)}|V_{1}|(1\pm\varepsilon_{1}),

and

|U^{\prime}_{k,1}\cup U^{\prime}_{k,2}\cup\dots\cup U^{\prime}_{k,m+1+g}|=\frac{1}{b}|V_{1}|(1\pm\varepsilon_{1}).

Note that

L:=L_{1}+L_{2}=b|V(\mathfrak{S})|+b|V(\mathfrak{U})|=b(\ell^{\prime}-C),

hence

L_{1}\geq\left(1-10r\delta_{2}\right)L

and

\frac{1}{b}|V_{1}|(1\pm\varepsilon_{1})=\frac{n}{L}(1\pm 3\varepsilon_{1}).

Next, by Lemma
2.8
, all pairs stemming from the edges of
R
are
\left(\varepsilon_{1}/\beta,\left(d_{1}/4\right)^{+}\right)
-regular with
\beta=\min\left\{\frac{t}{2br},\frac{1}{2b(m+1+g)}\right\}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. This includes all pairs
S^{\prime}_{i,j}S^{\prime}_{i,j^{\prime}}
and
U^{\prime}_{k,\ell}U^{\prime}_{k,\ell^{\prime}}
, but also some pairs
S^{\prime}_{i,j}U^{\prime}_{k,\ell}
.

Now to get super-regularity consider first a tuple
(S^{\prime}_{i,1},\dots,S^{\prime}_{i,m+1})
. Using Lemma

5.1.
Size adjustment lemmas

The following lemmas are all elementary and will be used to adjust the sizes of the partition sets by removing a constant or small fraction of vertices to obtain appropriate size ratios.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
S_{1},\dots,S_{m},T
be disjoint sets of vertices and let
M\coloneq|S_{1}\cup\dots\cup S_{m}\cup T|
. Let
0<\epsilon\ll r^{-1}
. Suppose that
M\gg r
,
M\equiv 0\pmod{r}
and
\left(\frac{s}{r}-\epsilon\right)M\leq|S_{i}|\leq\frac{s}{r}M
for all
i\in[m]
.
Consider the following move:

•

P_{k}
move: for an index
k\in[m]
remove
s-1
vertices from
S_{k}
,
s
vertices from each
S_{i}
,
i\neq k
, and
t+1
vertices from
T
.

Using the above type of move, we can get new sets
S_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq S_{1},\dots,S_{m}^{\prime}\subseteq S_{m},T^{\prime}\subseteq T
such that
|S_{1}|=\dots=|S_{m}|=\frac{s}{t}|T|
and
|S_{1}^{\prime}\cup\dots\cup S_{m}^{\prime}\cup T^{\prime}|\geq(1-mr\epsilon)M.

Proof.

Suppose we can obtain the required sets
S_{i}^{\prime}
,
i\in[m]
, and
T^{\prime}
by performing
x_{k}
moves
P_{k}
for
k\in[m]
. This leads to a system of linear equations

\displaystyle|S_{1}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{t}|T^{\prime}|

(5.3)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\vdots

\displaystyle|S_{m}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{t}|T^{\prime}|,

where
|S_{k}^{\prime}|=|S_{k}|-s\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}+x_{k}
for
k\in[m]
, and
|T^{\prime}|=|T|-(t+1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}
. So it is enough to check if (
5.1
) has a solution in non-negative integers. Indeed, there is such a solution given by

x_{k}=\frac{s}{r}M-|S_{k}|,\quad\text{ for }k\in[m].

Since
\frac{s}{r}M-|S_{k}|\leq\epsilon M
, and in a single move we remove exactly
r
vertices, the total number of removed vertices is at most
mr\epsilon M
vertices.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

,

and

s

We will perform the following algorithm using the allowed moves. Suppose that we have reached an

-tuple

. Associate an
(m+1
)-tuple

Step 1. Redistributing
