Distributionally Robust Safety Under Arbitrary Uncertainties: A Safety Filtering Approach
Daniel M. Cherenson*, Haejoon Lee*, Taekyung Kim, Dimitra Panagou

Abstract
In this work, we study how to ensure probabilistic safety for nonlinear systems under distributional ambiguity. Our approach builds on a backup-based safety filtering framework that switches between a high-performance nominal policy and a certified backup policy to ensure safety. To handle arbitrary uncertainties from ambiguous distributions, i.e., where the distribution is not of specific structure and the true distribution is unknown, we adopt a distributionally robust (DR) formulation using Wasserstein ambiguity sets. Rather than solving a high-dimensional DR trajectory optimization problem online, we exploit the structure of backup-based safety filtering to reduce safety certification to a one-dimensional search over the switching time between nominal and backup policies. We then develop a sampling-based certification procedure with finite-sample guarantees, where empirical failure probabilities are compared against a Wasserstein-inflated threshold. We validate our method through simulations across three systems, from a Dubins vehicle to a high-speed racing car and a fighter jet, demonstrating the broad applicability and computational efficiency. I
Introduction

Ensuring the safety of autonomous robotic systems remains a challenge in the presence of complex nonlinear robot dynamics with actuation limits, operation under uncertainty due to model mismatch, perception errors, and exogenous disturbances. These uncertainties might be unknown or poorly characterized, complicating the synthesis of a safe controller that does not compromise mission performance.

Safety filters have enabled modular architectures, allowing the use of high-performance, safety-agnostic nominal policies and intervening only when a safety violation is imminent
[
14
,
18
,
5
,
3
]
. In stochastic settings, robust safety filters
[
11
,
20
]
provide guarantees under worst-case deterministic bounds, while stochastic filters
[
34
,
29
]
assume known disturbance distributions. However, both approaches rely on accurate or restrictive uncertainty models. Hence, their guarantees may fail when the true distribution is unknown, partially observed, or shifted.

Distributional shift or ambiguity is addressed by distributionally robust (DR) safety-critical planning and control
[
35
,
26
]
. These approaches account for worst-case uncertainty by enforcing safety against an ambiguity set, that is, a family of all probability distributions consistent with available data, defined by moments or Wasserstein distances
[
16
,
12
]
. This framework has been successfully applied to handle uncertain disturbances in external environmental perception
[
21
,
17
,
32
]
and system dynamics
[
37
,
4
]
.

Despite their benefits, integrating DR techniques directly into safety filters remains challenging. Incorporating and verifying DR safety constraints for general nonlinear systems is often computationally expensive
[
23
]
, requiring optimization over a worst-case distribution within high-dimensional ambiguity sets. For instance, nonlinear DR-MPC frameworks can require hundreds of seconds to solve per time step
[
15
]
, rendering them unsuitable for real-time safety filtering.

With tractability in mind, we introduce a DR safety filter for general dynamical systems under arbitrary uncertainty structure. Our approach builds on backup-based safety filters

This architecture is well suited to high-dimensional nonlinear systems with input constraints, avoiding manual construction and feasibility issues common in CBFs. In this paper, we propose
Distributionally Robust Stochastic
gatekeeper
(

We reformulate the infinite-dimensional DR safety problem into a one-dimensional search for the switching time between nominal and backup policies, enabling efficient real-time implementation. Safety is certified using samples drawn only from the nominal distribution, while robustness to distributional shift is achieved by a Wasserstein-derived inflated threshold for checking unsafe rollouts.
The main contributions of this paper are:

•

We introduce
DRS-gatekeeper

•

We derive a finite-sample probabilistic safety certificate under a Wasserstein ambiguity set by computing an upper bound on the worst-case failure probability with a user-specified confidence level.

•

We validate our method across three distinct domains of increasing complexity: a Dubins vehicle, a Formula 1 racing car, and an F-16 fighter jet, demonstrating better safety-performance trade-offs compared to baseline methods and broad applicability.

II-B

Safety Constraint

We require the system (
1

\displaystyle\Pr{}_{{\bm{w}}_{t}\sim\mathbb{P}_{t}}\bigg[\bigcap_{\tau=t}^{\infty}h(\varphi_{\tau}^{\pi}(x_{t},{\bm{w}}^{f}),w^{\theta}_{t})\geq 0\bigg]\geq 1-\varepsilon,

II-C

Wasserstein Ambiguity Sets

The distribution
\mathbb{P}_{t}
is meant to capture the aleatoric uncertainty (inherent stochasticity) in the system and environment. However, the true distribution
\mathbb{P}_{t}
is often unknown. Thus, we typically rely on a nominal distribution
\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{t}
constructed from empirical data or simplified models to evaluate the chance constraint (
CC
). This dependence on
\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{t}
introduces epistemic uncertainty; a controller optimized solely against
\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{t}
may fail if the actual distribution deviates from the nominal model. Consequently, satisfying (
CC

To achieve robustness to such epistemic uncertainty, we consider an ambiguity set centered around the nominal distribution
\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{t}
that contains the true distribution

:

Definition 1

(Ambiguity Set)
.

Given a nominal noise distribution

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\beta}(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{t})=\{\mathbb{Q}~|~d(\mathbb{Q},\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{t})\leq\beta\},

(4)

where

is a distance function between two distributions and
\beta\geq 0
is the radius.

One common distance function between distributions is the
\infty
-Wasserstein distance, which measures the smallest worst-case displacement needed to transport mass from one distribution to another:

Definition 2

(
\infty
-Wasserstein distance
[

]
)

\displaystyle W_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Q}^{\prime})=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Q}^{\prime})}\gamma\text{-}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\|\xi-\xi^{\prime}\|,

\displaystyle\bigg(\inf_{\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{B}_{\beta}(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{t})}\mathrm{Pr}_{{\bm{w}}_{t}\sim\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\bigcap_{\tau=t}^{\infty}h(\varphi_{\tau}^{\pi}(x_{t},{\bm{w}}^{f}),w^{\theta}_{t})\geq 0\bigg]\bigg)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{P})
such that
\|{\bm{w}}-{\bm{w}}^{\prime}\|\leq\beta

\gamma

Now suppose

. Since

, we have

\displaystyle d({\bm{w}}^{\prime},\mathcal{A})\leq\|{\bm{w}}^{\prime}-{\bm{w}}\|\leq\beta.

Hence

\displaystyle\{{\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{A}\}\subseteq\{{\bm{w}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\beta}\}\qquad\gamma\text{-almost surely}.

Taking probabilities with respect to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}_{{\bm{w}}\sim\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{A})=\gamma({\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{A})\leq\gamma({\bm{w}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\beta})=\mathrm{Pr}_{{\bm{w}}^{\prime}\sim\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{A}^{\beta}).

Since this holds for every
\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{B}_{\beta}(\mathbb{P})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{B}_{\beta}(\mathbb{P})}\mathrm{Pr}_{{\bm{w}}\sim\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{A})\leq\mathrm{Pr}_{{\bm{w}}\sim\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{A}^{\beta}).

It remains to show that
\mathcal{A}^{\beta}\subset\{{\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{W}~|~H_{T}^{x,s}({\bm{w}})<L_{H}\beta\}
. Let
{\bm{w}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\beta}
. Then, by definition of
\mathcal{A}^{\beta}
, for any
\eta>0
there exists
\bar{\bm{w}}_{\eta}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\|{\bm{w}}^{\prime}-\bar{\bm{w}}_{\eta}\|\leq\beta+\eta
. Since
\bar{\bm{w}}_{\eta}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

. By
3

\displaystyle H_{T}^{x,s}({\bm{w}}^{\prime})\leq H_{T}^{x,s}(\bar{\bm{w}}_{\eta})+L_{H}\|{\bm{w}}^{\prime}-\bar{\bm{w}}_{\eta}\|<L_{H}(\beta+\eta).

Letting
\eta\downarrow 0
gives

. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\beta}\subset\{{\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{W}~|~H_{T}^{x,s}({\bm{w}})<L_{H}\beta\}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{B}_{\beta}(\mathbb{P})}\mathrm{Pr}_{{\bm{w}}\sim\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pr}_{{\bm{w}}\sim\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{A}^{\beta})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Pr}_{{\bm{w}}\sim\mathbb{P}}(H^{x,s}_{T}({\bm{w}})<L_{H}\beta),

which completes the proof.

Remark 3
.

We highlight that our algorithm’s formal probabilistic safety guarantee given by
Theorem

1
is independent of the specific values of time horizon
T
and the number of samples
N
. That is, the statistical correctness of
DRCC
is maintained regardless of the dimension of the trajectories and sample size. However, increasing
N
and
T
may reduce the conservativeness of the filter by allowing the nominal policy
\pi_{N}
to be executed more frequently without compromising the underlying safety certificate.

Remark 4
.

With a backup policy
\pi_{B}
(
2
),
DRS-gatekeeper
ensures
DRCC
at state
x_{t}
and thus infinite-horizon probabilistic safety. However, constructing such

offline is often challenging for complex, high-dimensional systems. In the absence of a known backup policy, our method no longer guarantees infinite-horizon safety. Instead, it can be used to compute a finite-horizon safe trajectory over a planning horizon

\displaystyle\bigg(\inf_{\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{B}_{\beta}(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{t})}\mathrm{Pr}_{{\bm{w}}\sim\mathbb{Q}}\bigg[\bigcap_{\tau=t}^{t+T}h(\varphi_{\tau}^{\pi}(x_{t},{\bm{w}}^{f}),w^{\theta}_{\tau})\geq 0\bigg]\bigg)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

The nominal aerodynamic model is identified from generated simulator data. Each rollout provides state, action, and next-state samples, converted into aerodynamic coefficient targets. A regularized polynomial regression is fit from the selected flight and control features to the six aerodynamic coefficient channels:

\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{nom}}=[C_{X},C_{Y},C_{Z},C_{L},C_{M},C_{N}]^{\top}.

After fitting the nominal polynomial model, residual coefficient errors are computed on the same generated dataset. Structured residual trends are removed using context-dependent calibration features, namely flight condition. Centered residuals are stored as an empirical uncertainty model. During rollout, uncertainty samples are drawn from residuals observed in nearby historical contexts; injected uncertainty remains dependent on the current flight regime rather than being modeled as fixed Gaussian noise.

In the
DRS-gatekeeper
rollout, sampled uncertainty enters directly in coefficient space. Let

\mathbf{w}=[w_{C_{X}},w_{C_{Y}},w_{C_{Z}},w_{C_{L}},w_{C_{M}},w_{C_{N}}]^{\top}

denote the sampled coefficient residual. Perturbed aerodynamic coefficients are
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{nom}}+\mathbf{w}
. Dimensional forces and moments are converted via:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=(C_{X}+w_{C_{X}})\frac{\bar{q}S}{m}+T(\delta_{t}),

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=(C_{Y}+w_{C_{Y}})\frac{\bar{q}S}{m},

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=(C_{Z}+w_{C_{Z}})\frac{\bar{q}S}{m},

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=(C_{L}+w_{C_{L}})\bar{q}Sb,

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=(C_{M}+w_{C_{M}})\bar{q}S\bar{c},

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=(C_{N}+w_{C_{N}})\bar{q}Sb.

Uncertainty is injected before rigid-body propagation by perturbing aerodynamic coefficients, changing body-axis force and moment channels used by the rollout dynamics.

Remark 5
.

The DR-CBF does not apply to the racecar or F-16 models, as their uncertainty depends on the current state and control input, violating the assumption of a fixed noise distribution in the DR-CBF. Also, for the F-16, the high relative degree and constrained input set make it challenging to guarantee feasibility of the DR-CBF constraint. We note that deterministic simplified control-affine dynamics models without input constraints have been used to construct CBFs for F-16

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced
Distributionally Robust Stochastic
gatekeeper
(
DRS-gatekeeper
)
, a safety filter that provides probabilistic safety certificates under distributional ambiguity. Our method handles arbitrary uncertainty structure and nonlinear dynamics through efficient sampling-based rollouts and optimizes a switching time between a nominal controller and a backup controller. We conduct extensive simulations to validate our method.
