Abstract
We present a trust-region-based adaptive finite-element algorithm for numerically solving a class of nonsmooth PDE-constrained optimization problems that includes problems with sparsifying regularizers and convex constraints. In particular, we consider the class of problems whose objective function is the sum of a smooth, possibly nonconvex, function and a nonsmooth extended real-valued convex function. Our method combines the robustness of inexact trust-region algorithms for nonsmooth problems with the efficiency of adaptive finite-element discretizations. Starting from a coarse mesh, the algorithm automatically refines the discretization based on reliable a posteriori error estimators for both the state and adjoint equations, systematically controlling the accuracy of the computed smooth objective function value and gradient. This adaptivity mechanism balances computational cost and solution accuracy, enabling high resolution of localized phenomena and sparsity structures in the state and control variables. We demonstrate the performance of our algorithm through numerical experiments on representative control and topology optimization examples.

.

Suppose

4
Trust-Region Algorithm

At the
k
-th iteration, classical trust-region methods compute a trial iterate

z_{k}^{+}
by approximately minimizing a local model of the objective function
around the current iterate
z_{k}
within the current trust region, i.e., the
ball of radius
\Delta_{k}>0
centered at
z_{k}
. To handle nonsmooth objective
functions with the form of
F
,
[
8
]
employs the model

m_{k}(z)\coloneqq f_{k}(z)+\phi(z)
, where
f_{k}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth
approximation of
f
around
z_{k}
. For computational convenience, we
utilize quadratic models of the form

f_{k}(z)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left\langle B_{k}(z-z_{k}),z-z_{k}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{Z}^{*},\mathcal{Z}}+\left\langle g_{k},z-z_{k}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{Z}^{*},\mathcal{Z}},

where
B_{k}=B_{k}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Z}^{*})
models curvature information (i.e., the
Hessian or a secant approximation thereof) and
g_{k}\approx f^{\prime}(z_{k})
.
As in
[
8
, Algorithm 1]
, we compute trial iterates
z_{k}^{+}
that
approximately solve the subproblem

\displaystyle\min_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\;m_{k}(z)\quad\text{subject to}\quad\left\|z-z_{k}\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}}\leq\Delta_{k},

(14)

using the proximal methods introduced in
[
10
]
.
In particular, we require that
z_{k}^{+}
satisifies the trust-region constraint
(up to a constant)

\displaystyle\left\|z^{+}-z_{k}\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}}\leq\kappa_{\textup{rad}}\Delta_{k}

and the fraction of Cauchy decrease condition

\displaystyle m_{k}(z_{k})-m_{k}(z_{k}^{+})\geq\kappa_{\textup{fcd}}\Psi_{k}\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\frac{\Psi_{k}}{1+\|B_{k}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Z}^{*})}}\right\},

(15)

where
\Psi_{k}
, defined as

\Psi_{k}\coloneqq t_{k}^{-1}\left\|\mathrm{Prox}_{t_{k}\phi}(z_{k}-t_{k}\nabla f_{k}(z_{k}))-z_{k}\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}},

(16)

indicates the stationarity of the iterate
z_{k}
for the model
m_{k}
.
Here,
\kappa_{\textup{rad}}>0
and
\kappa_{\textup{fcd}}>0
are constants independent of
k
. In our
numerical examples, we choose the parameter
t_{k}
to be Cauchy point step
length, as in
[
8
, Algorithm 5]
and
[
10
, Section 5.1]
,
although one could simply take
t_{k}
to be a constant. The fraction of Cauchy
decrease condition (
15
) ensures that
z_{k}^{+}\in{\rm dom}\,{\phi}
since
the left-hand side would be
-\infty
otherwise.

Traditionally, one decides whether to accept or reject the trial iterate

z_{k}^{+}
based on the ratio of actual and predicted reductions

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{k}\coloneqq\frac{\textup{ared}_{k}}{\textup{pred}_{k}}\coloneqq\frac{F(z_{k})-F(z_{k}^{+})}{m_{k}(z_{k})-m_{k}(z_{k}^{+})}.

(17)

Concretely, given user-specified parameters
0<\eta_{1}<\eta_{2}<1
, we
accept the trial iterate
z_{k+1}=z_{k}^{+}
if
\tilde{\rho}_{k}\geq\eta_{1}
and
otherwise we reject the trial iterate
z_{k+1}=z_{k}
. Moreover, if

\tilde{\rho}_{k}<\eta_{1}
, we decrease the trust-region radius, i.e.,

\Delta_{k+1}<\Delta_{k}
, and if
\tilde{\rho}_{k}\geq\eta_{2}
, we increase the
trust-region radius, i.e.,
\Delta_{k+1}>\Delta_{k}
.

For (
1
), exact evaluations of the objective function
F

are not possible since they require the solution of a system of PDEs
to evaluate
S(z)
and hence
f(z)=J(S(z),z)
. For this problem, we replace

F
with an AFEM approximation
F_{k}
and employ the computed
reduction

\displaystyle\textup{cred}_{k}\coloneqq F_{k}(z_{k})-F_{k}(z_{k}^{+}).

For step acceptance and trust-region radius update, we replace
\tilde{\rho}_{k}

with the ratio of computed and predicted reductions

\displaystyle\rho_{k}\coloneqq\frac{\textup{cred}_{k}}{\textup{pred}_{k}}.

(18)

In order to guarantee convergence of the trust-region method, we require that
the AFEM approximation of the objective function is sufficiently accurate as
defined in the following condition.

Condition 1

(Inexact Value)
.

There exists a constant
\kappa_{\textup{val}}>0
, independent of
k
, such that
the AFEM approximation of the objective function value satisfies

\displaystyle|\textup{ared}_{k}-\textup{cred}_{k}|\leq\kappa_{\textup{val}}\zeta_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta_{k}^{j}\leq\gamma\min\{\textup{pred}_{k},\epsilon_{k}\}\quad\forall\,k.

Here,

and

As with the evaluation of

A Posteriori Error Estimation for Elliptic PDEs

Since both applications are governed by elliptic PDEs, we now review a
posteriori error estimation for the general elliptic PDE

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u)+bu

\displaystyle=q\quad\text{in }\Omega

(22a)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{D}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(A\nabla u)\cdot n=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{N},

(22b)

where
\Gamma_{D}
and

are disjoint subsets of the boundary

\partial\Omega

or that there exists
\Omega_{b}\subseteq\Omega
with
postive measure on which

a.e. Under these assumptions, the bilinear form
associated with the weak form of (

continuous and coercive. Consequently, the Lax-Milgram Lemma guarantees
existence and uniqueness of solutions. We discretize (

)
using continuous finite elements defined on a mesh
\mathcal{T}^{h}
. For each mesh
element
T\in\mathcal{T}^{h}

polynomials
on

and the finite-element solution space by

\mathcal{U}^{h}=\{v\in C(\bar{\Omega})\cap\mathcal{U}~|~v|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{s}(T)~\forall~T\in\mathcal{T}^{h}\}.

(23)

Let
u\in\mathcal{U}
denote the solution to (
22
) and
u^{h}\in\mathcal{U}^{h}

finite-element approximation. As in

(25)

and the edge jump residuals

\mathcal{E}^{h}_{\partial\Omega}\coloneqq\{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}\,|\,E\in\Gamma_{N}\}
