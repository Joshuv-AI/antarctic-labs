Parameterized D-torsors in differential Galois theory Thanks: Omar León Sánchez was partially supported by EPSRC grant EP/V03619X/1
Omar León Sánchez, David Meretzky

Abstract
. In the context of differential fields of characteristic zero with several commuting derivations, we discuss the notion of # \# -differential equations on parameterized D-torsors and their associated Galois extensions. Using model-theoretic methods, we observe that any generalized strongly normal extension (in the sense of Pillay [ 14 ] and, more generally, León Sánchez [ 9 ] ) is the Galois extension of a parameterized D-torsor. Furthermore, we prove a parameterized version of a theorem of Kolchin on differential cohomology, itself of independent interest, and use it to provide a necessary and sufficient cohomological condition for when a generalized strongly normal extension is the Galois extension for a log-differential equation on its Galois group (as a parameterized D-group). We also present general model-theoretic versions of some of the main results.

1.
Introduction

In classical Galois theory, a (finite) Galois extension of fields
L/K
is commonly defined as a finite separable extension which is normal. It is then proven, see
[
4
, §4.3]
for instance, that this is equivalent to asking that
L
be the splitting field of a separable polynomial over
K
. This equivalence, together with the Galois correspondence, are fundamental results that play a key role in Galois’ theorem on radical solvability of polynomial equations.

In the context of differential fields and differential equations (in characteristic zero and commuting derivations), several “Galois theories” have been developed; some suitable for studying linear PDEs
[
1
,
3
,
20
]
usually referred as the (parameterized) Picard-Vessiot theory; and some others being far-reaching generalizations of the PV theory to include extensions that enjoy solely
a key property: some form of (strong) normality, see for example
[
6
,
8
,
11
,
14
]
. The generalized strongly normal theory, as it appears in
[
11
, §3.5]
, has the most general form (to the authors’ knowledge); in the sense that the differential extensions with full Galois correspondences considered in all other “differential Galois theories” are examples of generalized strongly normal extensions.

Briefly, working inside an ambient differentially closed field
(\mathcal{U},\Pi)\models\operatorname{DCF}_{0,m}
, a differential field extension
L/K
is a generalized strongly normal extension if
L
is finitely (differentially) generated over
K
and there is a
K
-definable set
X
in the structure
(\mathcal{U},\Pi)
such that (i) for all
\sigma\in\text{Aut}_{\Pi}(\mathcal{U}/K)
we have
\sigma(L)\subset L\langle X\rangle
, this is called
strong normality
of
L
; and (ii)
K\langle X\rangle\cap L=K
, one could call this the
no new constants
condition (aka
weak orthogonality
). Here
L\langle X\rangle
denotes the differential field generated by all the entries of
X=X(\mathcal{U})
over
L
, similarly for
K\langle X\rangle
.

The purpose of this paper is to establish a suitable analogue of the fundamental result in (algebraic) Galois theory mentioned above to the context of generalized strongly normal extensions. In rough terms, the main result of the paper in Section
5
shows that any such extension is the “splitting differential field” of
the
\#
-differential equation on a parameterized D-torsor. We will define these notions rigorously in Section
3
.

Instances of our results were shown by Kolchin in the Picard-Vessiot and strongly normal setting in
[
6
]
and proved in the finite-dimensional (aka
\Pi
-type zero) generalized strongly normal setting by Pillay under additional assumptions in
[
14
,
15
]
. In the infinite-dimensional case, versions of our results appear (under similar additional assumptions) in work of the first author
[
9
]
. The arguments in these papers use Galois-cohomological machinery to prove what Kolchin and Pillay refer to as “G-primitive” style results. In Section
2
, we collect from the literature the model-theoretic generalizations of the cohomological results necessary for both our main theorem and a model-theoretic generalization.

We now describe in more detail our results and their place within the scope of the literature mentioned above.
Recall that an algebraic D-group over
K
is an algebraic group
G
equipped with a regular section

of the prolongation (or twisted tangent bundle)
\tau_{\Pi}G
, both over
K
, such that

is a group homomorphism. This induces a differential
K
-algebra structure on the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{G}
and we further require that these derivations commute. The
\#
-differential equation on
(G,t)
is defined as

\nabla_{\Pi}(x)=t(x)

where
\nabla_{\Pi}(x)=(x,D_{1}x,\dots,D_{m}x)
with
\Pi=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{m}\}
, and the set of
\#
-points of
(G,t)
, denoted
(G,t)^{\#}
, is the set of solutions in
G
of the
\#
-differential equation. Note that
(G,t)^{\#}
is a definable group in
(\mathcal{U},\Pi)
or equivalently a
\Pi
-algebraic group; moreover, it is finite-dimensional in the sense that its
\Pi
-type is zero (in other words, the differential field generated by a generic point has finite transcendence degree over
K
). Let
e
be the identity of
G
and
\tau_{\Pi}G_{e}
the fibre of
\tau_{\Pi}G
over
e
(this can be identified with the
m
-fold product of the Lie algebra of
G
). For
\alpha
a
K
-point in
\tau_{\Pi}G_{e}
, the
\alpha
-log differential equation on
(G,t)
is defined as

(1.1)

\nabla_{\Pi}(x)=\alpha\cdot t(x)

where the product
\alpha
with
t(x)
is computed in the algebraic group
\tau_{\Pi}G
.

By a (differential) Galois extension over
K
for the
\alpha
-log differential equation (
1.1
) we mean a differential field
L
of the form
K\langle a\rangle
for some solution
a
of the equation such that
K\langle(G,t)^{\#}\rangle\cap L=K
, where recall that
K\langle(G,t)^{\#}\rangle
denotes the differential field generated by all the entries of
(G,t)^{\#}(\mathcal{U})
over
K
.

One can check that if
a
is a solution of (
1.1
), then the set of solutions in
\mathcal{U}
is the coset
a\cdot(G,t)^{\#}
. Thus, if
L=K\langle a\rangle
is a Galois extension for the
\alpha
-log equation, one can think of

as a “splitting field” for (
1.1
). Moreover, it follows from the definitions that
L
is in fact a generalized strongly normal extension with
X=(G,t)^{\#}
. It also follows from
[
11
, §3]
that the Galois group

(1.2)

\text{Aut}_{\Pi}(L\langle(G,t)^{\#}\rangle/K\langle(G,t)^{\#}\rangle),

together with its regular action on
tp(a/K)
, is isomorphic via
\sigma\mapsto a^{-1}\cdot\sigma(a)
to
H^{\#}
for some algebraic D-subgroup

of
(G,t)
over
K
, and there is a natural Galois correspondence between intermediate differential fields and algebraic
D
-subgroups of
H
defined over
K
.

Differential Galois extensions of log-differential equations (as above) are examples of
(G,t)^{\#}
-strongly normal extensions where we may assume that
(G,t)^{\#}
is the Galois group (
1.2
) of the extension. One can now ask whether all such normal extensions arise this way. In the current set up, there are a few reasons why this cannot be the case. For instance, the Galois extensions of equations such as (
1.1
) have finite transcendence degree over
K
(since
(G,t)^{\#}
is finite-dimensional); whereas there are generalized strongly normal extensions of infinite transcedence degree as long as
m\geq 2
(i.e., there are at least two derivations), see
[
1
,
8
]
or
[
9
, Example 6.3]
.

Galois extensions of logarithmic differential equations of infinite transcendence appear in
[
1
,
8
]
and more generally in
[
9
]
. Their construction requires a parameterized version of D-groups. One fixes a partition of the set of derivations
\Pi=\mathcal{D}\cup\Delta
, with
\mathcal{D}
nonempty, and works relative to the theory of
\Delta
-fields; that is, one replaces algebraic geometry with
\Delta
-algebraic geometry. A parameterized D-group w.r.t.
\mathcal{D}/\Delta
is then a
\Delta
-algebraic group
G
with a
\Delta
-regular section

of the parameterized prolongation
\tau_{\mathcal{D}/\Delta}G
(further details are given in Section
3
). Much of what we explained above translates to the parameterized setup. However, now the set of sharp points
(G,t)^{\#}
is defined by the equation

\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}(x)=t(x)

where
\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}(x)=(x,D_{1}x,\dots,D_{r}x)
with
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{1},\dots,D_{r}\}
, and hence it is in general an infinite-dimensional
\Pi
-group (in other words, of positive
\Pi
-type). Similarly, for
\alpha
a
K
-point of
\tau_{\mathcal{D}/\Delta}G_{e}
, a differential Galois extension

for the
\alpha
-log differential equation
\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}(x)=\alpha\cdot t(x)
is generally of infinite transcendence degree over
K
. These Galois extensions are examples of
(G,t)^{\#}
-strongly normal extensions (where we may assume that
(G,t)^{\#}
is the Galois group of the extension) and were studied in
[
9
]
. One can now reformulate the question above to: Do all generalized strongly normal extensions arise as differential Galois extensions for log-differential equations on their (parameterized) Galois group
(G,t)
? The close connection between differential-algebraic torsors and differential Galois extensions tells us that the answer is still
no
.

Briefly, a parameterized D-torsor
(V,s)
for
(G,t)
is (right)
\Delta
-torsor
V
for
G
such that

is compatible with the action (see Section
3
for precise definitions). For notation sake, we sometimes say that
(V,s)\circlearrowright(G,t)
is a parameterized
\mathcal{D}/\Delta
-torsor. The
\#
-differential equation of
(V,s)
is simply
\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}(x)=s(x)
. As we suggested above, our main result (Theorem
5.1
) will say that (up to a
GL_{m}(\mathbb{Q})
-transformation of
\Pi
) any generalized strongly normal extension is the Galois extension of the
\#
-differential equation on a parameterized D-torsor
(V,s)
for the Galois group
(G,t)
of the extension. Furthermore, we show that such extension is the Galois extension of a log-differential equation on
(G,t)
if and only if
V
is a trivial torsor (i.e.,
V
has a
K
-point).

2.
Some model-theoretic generalities

In this section we lay out the general conventions and results that will be used in the rest of the paper. To start with, we review and elaborate on the results from
[
11
]
around model-theoretic definable Galois theory. We fix a first-order totally transcendental theory

with elimination of imaginaries. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a monster model of

. Let

be a small (with respect to
\kappa
)

-closed set of parameters over which we will work. Let

be a copy of the prime model over

.

Let

be an

-definable set and

. Following
[
11
]
we say that

is strongly internal to

if for any pair of realizations

and

of

,

. Furthermore, recall that

is said to be weakly orthogonal to

has a unique extension to a type over

.

Fact 2.1
.

[
11
, Lemma 2.2(i)]

The type

is weakly orthogonal to

if and only if for any (equivalently some) realization

of

.

We say that a

-closed set

relative to

and

is strongly internal and weakly orthogonal to

Fact 2.2
.

[

, §2]
Let

(3)

and

We also recall the notion of definable cocycles from

(2)

for all
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(M/A)

Furthermore, two cocycles

and

such that for all
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(M/A)
we have

\text{Aut}(M/A)
to the identity of

. We call a definable cocycle trivial if it is cohomologous to the constant cocycle.

We denote the set of definable cocycles from

to

by

\operatorname{Z}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(A,G)

We denote the quotient of

by the cohomology relation by

Fact 2.4
.

[

There is a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{P}_{\operatorname{def}}(A,G)\cong\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\operatorname{def}}(A,G).

\nabla^{G}_{\mathcal{D}}\circ\mu=\tau_{\mathcal{D}/\Delta}\mu\circ\nabla^{G\times G}_{\mathcal{D}}.

In other words, the map
\nabla^{G}_{\mathcal{D}}:G\to\tau_{\mathcal{D}/\Delta}G
is a group homomorphism. More generally, if

\Delta
-torsor for

\nabla^{V}_{\mathcal{D}}\circ*=\tau_{\mathcal{D}/\Delta}(*)\circ\nabla^{V\times G}_{\mathcal{D}}

and so the map
\nabla^{V}_{\mathcal{D}}:V\to\tau_{\mathcal{D}/\Delta}V
preserves the action; namely,

\nabla^{V}_{\mathcal{D}}(v*g)=\nabla^{V}_{\mathcal{D}}(v)\cdot\nabla^{G}_{\mathcal{D}}(g).

\mathcal{D}/\Delta

.

More generally, given a parameterized

on

Conversely, if a solution of the

-differential equation on a parameterized

-torsor

Proof.

Let

be a differential Galois extension for a parameterized torsor. Then by Condition (i) we have

4.
A parameterized version of Kolchin’s cohomology theorem

We carry forward the setup from the previous section. In particular, we have a partition of the derivation set as

\Pi=\Delta\cup\mathcal{D}.

We work inside a saturated model
(\mathcal{U},\Pi)\models\operatorname{DCF}_{0,m}
and
K
denotes a (small) differential subfield. By
\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Pi}
we mean definable Galois cohomology in
(\mathcal{U},\ \Pi)\models\operatorname{DCF}_{0,|\Pi|}
; while by
\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Delta}
we mean definable cohomology in the reduct
(\mathcal{U},\Delta)\models\operatorname{DCF}_{0,|\Delta|}
.

Kolchin’s cohomology theorem from
[
7
, §VII.3]
states that for an algebraic
G
over
K
we have
\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Pi}(K,G)\cong\operatorname{H}^{1}_{alg}(K,G)
where the latter is (algebraic) Galois cohomology which coincides with the definable cohomology in the reduct to
\mathcal{U}\models ACF_{0}
(namely when taking
\Delta
to be empty). The aim of this section is to prove a parameterized version of this when the group
G
is a
\Delta
-algebraic group. We will use the following facts, see
[
17
, Corollary 4.2]
and
[
18
, Fact 1.5]
:

Fact 4.1
.

Let
G
be a
\Pi
-algebraic group over
K
.

(1)

There exists an algebraic group
H
over
K
and a
K
-definable group embedding
G\hookrightarrow H
. We identify
G
as a subgroup of the algebraic group
H
.

(2)

In addition, if
V\circlearrowright G
is a
\Pi
-torsor over
K
, then there exists an algebraic torsor
W\circlearrowright H
and a
K
-definable embedding
f:V\hookrightarrow W
such that

f(v*g)=f(v)\cdot g\quad\text{ for all }v\in V,g\in G

where
*
is the action in
V\circlearrowright G
while
\cdot
is the action in
W\circlearrowright H
. In other words,
f(V)\circlearrowright G
is a subtorsor of
W\circlearrowright H
; i.e., the action of
G
on
f(V)
is the restriction of the action of
H
on
W
.

We can now prove the parameterized version of Kolchin’s cohomology theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
G
is a
\Delta
-algebraic group over
K
. Then, there is a canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Delta}(K,G)\cong\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Pi}(K,G).

Proof.

For
\alpha\in\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Delta}(K,G)
, we may write
\alpha=[X]_{\Delta}
where
X
is a
\Delta
-torsor over
K
and
[X]_{\Delta}
denotes the class of
\Delta
-torsors over
K
that are
\Delta
-isomorphic to
X
over
K
. Of course, such an
X
is also a
\Pi
-torsor and we may similarly write
[X]_{\Pi}
(for the class of
\Pi
-torsors over
K
that are
\Pi
-isomorphic to
X
over
K
). We define

\phi:\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Delta}(K,G)\to\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Pi}(K,G)

in the most natural (or canonical) way, that is

\alpha=[X]_{\Delta}\mapsto[X]_{\Pi}.

Note that
\phi
is well defined. Indeed, if
Y
is a
\Delta
-torsor over
K
which is
\Delta
-isomorphic to
X
and
V
is a
\Pi
-torsor
\Pi
-isomorphic to
X
, then
Y
and
V
are
\Pi
-isomorphic (as
\Pi
-torsors). It follows that if
[X]_{\Delta}=[Y]_{\Delta}
then
[X]_{\Pi}=[Y]_{\Pi}
.

We now prove
\phi
is injective. Assume
\phi(\alpha)=\phi(\beta)
with
\alpha=[X]_{\Delta}
and
\beta=[Y]_{\Delta}
. In other words, we are assuming
[X]_{\Pi}=[Y]_{\Pi}
; namely, there is an
\Pi
-isomorphism
f:X\to Y
. To show injectivity it suffices to show that there is a
\Delta
-isomorphism over
K
.

Using Fact
4.1
, there are algebraic torsors
V\circlearrowright H
and
W\circlearrowright H
such that we may view
X\circlearrowright G
as a substorsor of
V\circlearrowright H
and
Y\circlearrowright G
as a substorsor of
W\circlearrowright H
.
Let
h:V\times V\to H
be the function taking
(x,y)
to the unique element of
H
mapping
x\mapsto y
(note that this is ACF-definable). Fix
a\in X
and consider the map
\rho:X\to Y
given by

\rho(x)=f(a)\cdot h(a,x)

where
\cdot
is the action of
H
on
W
(so ACF-definable). Note that, since
X
and
Y
are
\Delta
-definable, the map
\rho
is
\Delta
-definable. Also, a straightforward computation shows that it is a torsor isomorphism. All that remains to show is that
\rho
is definable over
K
. To see this, it suffices to show that for all
\sigma\in Aut_{\Pi}(\mathcal{U}/K)
we have
\sigma(\rho(x))=\rho(\sigma(x))
for all
x\in X
. We have

\displaystyle\sigma(\rho(x))

\displaystyle=\sigma(f(a)h(a,x))

\displaystyle=f(\sigma(a))h(\sigma(a),\sigma(x))

\displaystyle=f(\sigma(a)h(\sigma(a),\sigma(x)))

\displaystyle=f(ah(a,\sigma(x)))

\displaystyle=f(a)h(a,\sigma(x))

\displaystyle=\rho(\sigma(x))

as desired.

We now prove surjectivity. Let
\mu\in\operatorname{H}_{\Pi}^{1}(K,G)
then
\mu=[U]_{\Pi}
for
U
a
\Pi
-torsor. We need to produce a
\Delta
-torsor
\Pi
-isomorphic to
U
over
K
. By Fact
4.1
, there is a
K
-definable embedding from
U
to an algebraic torsor
V
for
H
(an algebraic group where
G
is embedded). Let
X
be the image of
U
inside
V
. We will be done once we show that
X
is
\Delta
-definable. Consider the equivalence relation on
V

a\sim b\quad\iff\quad\text{there is $g\in G$ such that $a\cdot g=b$}

since
G
is
\Delta
-definable and the action
a\cdot g
above is ACF-definable, we have that the above is a
\Delta
-definable equivalence relation. Hence, we may identify
V/G
with a
\Delta
-definable set over
K
and the quotient map

p:V\to V/G

is
\Delta
-definable over
K
. Since
X
is an equivalence class, the point
\gamma=p(X)\in V/G
is a
K
-point. Thus
X=p^{-1}(\gamma)
is
\Delta
-definable over
K
, as desired.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Let us note that the map above

Lemma 4.6

Let
(G,t)
be a connected parameterized

\mathcal{D}/\Delta

. We must show that
\Phi([Q]_{\Pi})=[V]_{\Delta}
. Since

\Pi
-subtorsor of

, by part (4) of Remark

\iota^{1}

to
[V]_{\Pi}
. Noting that the isomorphism
\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Pi}(K,G)\to\operatorname{H}_{\Delta}^{1}(K,G)
from Theorem
4.2
maps
[V]_{\Pi}
to
[V]_{\Delta}
, we see that
\Phi([Q]_{\Pi})
is indeed
[V]_{\Delta}
.

∎

5.
The main result

We recall the setting. We are working in
\operatorname{DCF}_{0,m}
where
\Pi
is the set of
m
commuting derivations.
Recall that for
\Delta\subseteq\Pi
, by
\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Delta}
we mean definable Galois cohomology in the reduct
\operatorname{DCF}_{0,|\Delta|}
.

We now prove the main theorem of the paper.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\Pi)
be a monster model of DCF
0,m
and
K
a (small) differential subfield.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{D}\cup\Delta
be a partition of
\Pi
with
\mathcal{D}
nonempty and
(V,s)\circlearrowright(G,t)
a parameterized
\mathcal{D}/\Delta
-torsor. A solution
a
to the
\#
-differential equation of
(V,s)
generates a
(G,t)^{\#}
-strongly normal extension
L=K\langle a\rangle_{\Pi}
if and only if
(G,t)^{\#}(L)=(G,t)^{\#}(K)
.

(2)

Let
L
be a generalized strongly normal extension of
K
with
L/K
regular (so the differential Galois group is connected). Then, after a
GL_{m}(\mathbb{Q})
-transformation of
\Pi
, there exists a partition
\mathcal{D}\cup\Delta
with
\Delta
witnessing the
\Pi
-type of
L/K
and a parameterized
\mathcal{D}/\Delta
-torsor
(V,s)\circlearrowright(G,t)
over
K
such that
(G,t)^{\#}
is the differential Galois group of
L/K
and
L
is a differential Galois extension for
(V,s)
. In fact,
L
is isomorphic the
\Pi
-function field of
(V,s)^{\#}
.

Moreover,
L/K
is the differential Galois extension for a log-differential equation on
(G,t)
if and only if the image of the class associated to the
\Pi
-torsor
(V,s)^{\#}\circlearrowright(G,t)^{\#}

under the map (from Notation
4.4
)

\Phi:\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Pi}(K,(G,t)^{\#})\to\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Delta}(K,G)

is trivial. In particular, this occurs when
(K,\Delta)
is
\Delta
-closed as then
\operatorname{H}^{1}_{\Delta}(K,G)
is trivial.

Proof.

(1)

Part (1) is exactly Lemma
3.7
.

(2)

Let
L=K\langle b\rangle_{\Pi}
and let
Q
be the realizations of
q:=tp_{\Pi}(b/K)
. By the Torsor Theorem (see Fact
2.2
), the Galois group of
L/K
is isomorphic to a connected
\Pi
-algebraic group
H
over
K
and
Q
is a
\Pi
-torsor for
H
over
K
. Moreover, since
q
is isolated (see Fact
2.2
(1)), the
\Pi
-type of
Q
is smaller than
|\Pi|
. Hence, by Fact
3.1
, after a
GL_{m}(\mathbb{Q})
-transformation of
\Pi
, there exists
\Delta
a proper subset of
\Pi
witnessing the
\Pi
-type of
Q
(which is the same as the
\Pi
-type of
L/K
). Let
\mathcal{D}=\Pi\setminus\Delta
. Now, by Theorem
3.3
,
Q
is of the form
(V,s)^{\#}
for some parameterized
\mathcal{D}/\Delta
-torsor
(V,s)\circlearrowright(G,t)
with
(G,t)^{\#}=H
. Since
b\in(V,s)^{\#}
,
L
is clearly a differential Galois extension for
(V,s)
; and, in fact,
L
is the
\Pi
-function field of
(V,s)^{\#}
since
b
is
\Pi
-generic in
(V,s)^{\#}
over
K
.

We now prove the ’moreover’ clause. By Theorem
2.8
, the image of the class of
(V,s)^{\#}
under the map
\Phi
is trivial if and only if
(V,s)^{\#}
is
\Pi
-isomorphic over
K
to a
K
-definable left coset of
(G,t)^{\#}
in
G
. Under this isomorphism, let
a
be the point in
G
corresponding to
b
. Then,
(V,s)^{\#}\cong a\cdot(G,t)^{\#}
and
L=K\langle a\rangle_{\Pi}
.

Since
a\cdot(G,t)^{\#}
is
K
-definable, the image of this coset under the
K
-definable map

\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}^{G}(x)\cdot t(x)^{-1}:G\to\tau_{\Delta}G_{e}

must be a
K
-point, call it
\alpha
. Since
a
is a solution of
\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}^{G}(x)=\alpha\cdot t(x)
, we see that
L
is a Galois extension of this
\alpha
-log differential equation on
(G,t)
.

∎
