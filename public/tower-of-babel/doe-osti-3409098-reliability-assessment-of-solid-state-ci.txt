 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 Growth in power requirements and the complexity of emerging distribution systems are creating
 the need for new solid-state products for many applications. The qualification requirements,
 testing standards and life-cycle management practices of solid-state devices used in protection
 applications is not established. The project will leverage newly developed accelerated testing
 capabilities design specifically for protection applications to generate large data sets that will
 enable advanced digital techniques for data analysis and embedded health monitoring.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Task 1: Development of an accelerated aging procedure for solid-state circuit breakers

 Task 1a – Literature survey (duration: 0.5 -1 months)

 NLR will conduct a literature review of standard testing procedures and protocols for power
 semiconductor devices. Most established accelerated aging procedures are designed to work with
 devices assumed to be used in a switching circuit (e.g., inverters and converters). The procedure
 for aging a solid-state circuit breaker (SSCB) will have to consider that the device will be kept in
 an “on” state and suffer large temperature swings during the SSCB fault condition.

 Deliverables: Literature survey reports (e.g., Word, PowerPoint, Excel) highlighting the
 standard testing procedures and how they will be modified to the solid-state circuit breaker
 application.

 Results:

 NLR conducted a comprehensive literature survey to establish the current state of knowledge
 on accelerated aging, long-term reliability, and degradation mechanisms of power semiconductor
 devices, with particular emphasis on SiC MOSFETs relevant to solid-state circuit breaker
 (SSCB) applications. The primary objective of this survey was not only to summarize existing
 testing standards and methodologies, but also to critically assess their applicability, limitations,
 and required modifications for SSCB operating conditions.

 2

 The survey began by reviewing industry standards and widely adopted qualification
 procedures for power semiconductor reliability, including power cycling tests, temperature
 cycling tests, and electrical stress tests. These procedures are traditionally developed for devices
 operating in power conversion roles, such as inverters and converters, where devices are
 subjected to continuous high-frequency switching, alternating gate bias, and cyclic electro-
 thermal loading. In such applications, accelerated aging protocols are primarily designed to
 provoke packaging-related failures (e.g., wire-bond fatigue, solder joint degradation) or
 switching-induced electrical degradation under repetitive dynamic stress.

 Figure 1: Different test profiles

 A key finding of the literature review is that SSCB operating conditions differ fundamentally
 from inverter and converter applications, making direct application of standard aging procedures
 insufficient or potentially misleading. SSCBs are typically operated in a normally-on state, where
 the power device remains in conduction for extended periods and experiences prolonged positive
 gate bias rather than alternating gate voltage polarity. Switching events are infrequent and
 usually occur only during fault isolation or protection events, yet these events may
 involve extreme current and voltage transients. As a result, the dominant stress mechanisms in
 SSCBs are expected to arise from long-duration conduction-related electro-thermal
 stress combined with sustained gate bias, rather than repetitive switching stress .

 The literature survey therefore placed special emphasis on gate-oxide and interface reliability in
 SiC MOSFETs, as these mechanisms are strongly influenced by prolonged inversion-mode
 operation. Multiple reviewed studies indicate that sustained gate bias at elevated temperature
 accelerates charge trapping at the SiC/SiO₂ interface, leading to threshold-voltage (Vth) shifts,
 hysteresis effects, and changes in gate-related capacitances. These phenomena are often
 described in terms of bias temperature instability (BTI) and are known to occur on timescales
 relevant to SSCB operation, even in the absence of high-frequency switching .

 3

 In contrast, gate switching instability (GSI)—commonly discussed in inverter reliability
 literature—is primarily associated with extremely large numbers of switching cycles and rapid
 gate-voltage transitions. While GSI remains relevant for SSCBs during fault interruption events,
 the literature indicates that it is not the dominant aging driver under normal SSCB operation.
 This distinction is critical, as many existing accelerated aging procedures implicitly assume
 switching-dominated stress, potentially underestimating degradation pathways associated with
 prolonged DC-like gate bias and conduction heating .

 Another important outcome of the literature survey was the recognition that electrical stress and
 thermal stress cannot be treated independently for SSCB aging. Several reviewed works
 highlight that gate-oxide degradation mechanisms are significantly accelerated when elevated
 junction temperature is present simultaneously with sustained gate bias. This finding implies that
 SSCB aging protocols must intentionally combine conduction-induced self-heating with gate
 bias stress, rather than applying these stressors sequentially or in isolation. Consequently, purely
 environmental temperature cycling tests were deemed insufficient to represent SSCB aging
 behavior without additional electrical bias conditions .

 The survey also evaluated SSCB-specific device configurations, including discrete devices
 versus power modules, and considered the implications for aging evaluation and
 characterization. The literature and reference materials indicate that, while full SSCB assemblies
 provide system-level realism, they can obscure device-level degradation signatures when only
 terminal measurements are available. This insight supported the conclusion that device-level
 aging studies, particularly using discrete SiC MOSFETs, are essential for identifying early
 degradation precursors and isolating underlying physical mechanisms before packaging or
 system-level failures dominate .

 In summary, Task 1a established that existing accelerated aging standards and test protocols
 cannot be directly applied to SSCB applications without modification. The literature survey
 demonstrated that SSCB reliability is governed by a distinct combination of prolonged positive
 gate bias, conduction-driven electro-thermal stress, and occasional high-severity transient events.
 These findings directly informed the technical direction of Task 1b, where NLR developed an
 SSCB-specific accelerated aging procedure emphasizing sustained inversion-mode operation,
 controlled thermal loading, and periodic electrical characterization to track early indicators of
 degradation.

 Deliverables: Literature survey reports (e.g., Word, PowerPoint, Excel) highlighting the
 standard testing procedures and how they will be modified to the solid-state circuit breaker
 application.

 Task 1b – Develop an accelerated aging procedure and validate with experiments.
 (duration: 1 – 2 months)

 NLR will take the information learned from the literature review and adapt standard testing
 procedures for the specific application parameters of solid-state circuit breakers. Once the rough-
 draft procedure is developed, it will be implemented for validation on the aging station.

 The aging station will need to be modified to accommodate the specific requirements of the
 SSCB. This will include modifications such as control hardware power supplies and providing
 cooling as appropriate. The equipment will be provided by NLR. Eaton will provide power semi-
 conductor modules, metal oxide varistors and other discrete devices to be tested. If Eaton does
 not have a specific target device, NLR will perform the aging evaluation on commercial SiC
 MOSFET devices.

 The SSCB components will periodically be tested with the Keysight Power Device Analyzer to

 track any changes in device characteristics throughout the aging process.

 Deliverables: Data (e.g., Word, PowerPoint, Excel,). Due to the limited scope of the project, the
 developed procedure will be handled as suggested best practices and not a comprehensive/fully
 developed testing protocol. All data generated from the experimental processes will be available
 for delivery to Eaton.

 Results:

 Building on the findings of Task 1a, NLR developed and experimentally validated an accelerated
 aging procedure specifically tailored to solid-state circuit breaker (SSCB) applications. The
 primary objective of Task 1b was to translate literature-identified degradation mechanisms into
 a practical, repeatable test methodology that realistically captures SSCB operating stress while
 remaining compatible with available laboratory infrastructure and project scope constraints.

 Development of an SSCB-Specific Aging Concept

 The literature survey established that SSCB operation is characterized by prolonged on-state
 conduction, sustained positive gate bias, and limited switching activity except during fault
 events. Accordingly, NLR determined that conventional inverter-oriented aging methods—
 dominated by high-frequency gate switching and alternating bias—would not adequately
 represent SSCB stress conditions. As a result, the aging concept developed in Task 1b
 emphasized extended inversion-mode operation under controlled electro-thermal stress, rather
 than switching-dominated cycling .

 5

 The aging procedure was therefore structured around two complementary operating modes:

 1. Relay-controlled power cycling, used to emulate SSCB operation by keeping the device
 in a normally-on conduction state with sustained gate bias.
 2. Gate-controlled power cycling, used as a reference condition representative of inverter-
 style operation, enabling direct comparison of degradation behavior between SSCB and
 inverter stress profiles.

 This dual-mode approach allowed the project to isolate SSCB-specific degradation
 mechanisms while leveraging established inverter-style behavior as a baseline for interpretation.

 Aging Station Selection and Modifications

 To implement the proposed aging procedure, NLR evaluated both commercial aging
 platforms and an in-house modular aging station. Based on flexibility, accessibility of device
 terminals, and compatibility with frequent electrical characterization, the in-house multi-channel
 aging station was selected as the primary experimental platform . The station was modified as
 needed to accommodate SSCB-relevant operating conditions, including appropriate control
 hardware power supplies and thermal management provisions.

 The in-house system enabled independent control of drain current, gate bias, and cycling mode,
 while allowing rapid removal of devices for characterization without disturbing the overall test
 configuration. This capability was critical for tracking early-stage degradation trends, which was
 a central goal of the project.

 Devices Under Test and Measurement Strategy

 The primary devices under test (DUTs) for Task 1b were TO-247 discrete SiC MOSFETs,
 selected to provide direct access to device-level electrical parameters and to avoid masking
 degradation effects behind system-level behavior. Eaton provided power semiconductor devices
 and supporting components where available; when a specific target device was not available,
 commercially available SiC MOSFETs were used to validate the aging methodology .

 A key element of the experimental design was periodic electrical characterization using
 a Keysight Power Device Analyzer. Measurements focused on parameters identified in Task 1a
 as sensitive indicators of SSCB-relevant aging, including:

 • Threshold voltage (Vth)
 • Drain-to-source leakage current
 • Qualitative trends related to conduction behavior

 Measurements were conducted at defined intervals during the aging process to track parameter
 evolution as a function of accumulated stress cycles.

 Experimental Validation and Observed Aging Behavior

 Initial aging experiments demonstrated a clear distinction between SSCB-representative and
 inverter-representative stress conditions. Under relay-controlled power cycling, which maintains
 prolonged inversion-mode operation, devices exhibited a measurable increase in threshold
 voltage within the first several hundred cycles. In contrast, devices subjected to gate-controlled
 cycling showed relatively stable threshold voltage behavior over the same cycle range.

 7

 Further experiments were conducted to determine whether the observed threshold-voltage shifts
 were permanent or reversible. By switching devices between relay-controlled and gate-controlled
 cycling modes after a defined number of cycles, NLR observed that early-stage Vth shifts were
 largely reversible, indicating that the degradation mechanism at this stage is consistent
 with charge trapping and detrapping at the SiC/SiO₂ interface, rather than irreversible structural
 damage. This finding aligns with bias-temperature-instability behavior reported in the literature
 and supports the relevance of the selected aging approach for SSCB studies .

 To further separate the effects of electrical bias and temperature, additional tests were performed
 at low drain current levels, where junction temperature rise was minimal. Under these conditions,
 no significant Vth shift was observed regardless of cycling mode. This result confirms that both
 sustained gate bias and elevated temperature are required to produce the observed aging
 behavior, reinforcing the importance of combined electro-thermal stress in SSCB aging
 evaluation.

 Changes from Initial Expectations and Rationale

 The initial project concept anticipated adapting existing power-cycling methodologies with
 minimal modification. However, early experimental observations demonstrated that cycling
 control method (relay-controlled versus gate-controlled) plays a critical role in driving SSCB-
 relevant degradation. As a result, the aging procedure was refined to explicitly distinguish
 SSCB-mode operation from inverter-mode operation, rather than treating them as minor
 variations of the same test. This change was necessary to ensure that the aging results accurately
 reflect SSCB-specific stress physics and do not conflate switching-related effects with prolonged
 conduction behavior.

 Outcomes and Deliverables of Task 1b

 Task 1b resulted in the successful development and validation of an SSCB-focused accelerated
 aging procedure that captures key degradation trends associated with prolonged inversion-mode
 operation under elevated temperature. Due to the limited duration and scope of the project, the
 procedure is presented as recommended best practices rather than a fully standardized
 qualification protocol. All experimental data, measurement results, and supporting
 documentation generated during this task are available for delivery to Eaton in standard formats
 (e.g., Word, PowerPoint, Excel) .

 The findings from Task 1b directly support future work addressing longer-term aging behavior,
 impacts on additional device parameters (e.g., on-state resistance and safe operating area), and
 refinement of SSCB operating temperature recommendations.
