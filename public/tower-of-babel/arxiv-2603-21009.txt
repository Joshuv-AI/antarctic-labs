Lie-algebraic incompleteness of symmetry-adapted VQE for non-Abelian molecular point groups
Leon D. da Silva, Marcelo P. Santos

Abstract
Symmetry-adapted variational quantum eigensolvers (VQE) based on the Unitary Coupled-Cluster ansatz (SymUCCSD) effectively reduce the parameter count for Abelian molecular point groups, yet they systematically fail for non-Abelian groups without a fully established theoretical explanation. In this work, we prove that the Abelian-subgroup restriction induces a spurious splitting of multidimensional irreducible representations, prematurely discarding cross-component excitations. At the Lie-algebraic level, this filter confines the Dynamical Lie Algebra (DLA) to the Abelian subalgebra 𝔲 ​ ( 1 ) d λ \mathfrak{u}(1)^{d_{\lambda}} , restricting the reachable state manifold to a measure-zero torus 𝕋 d λ \mathbb{T}^{d_{\lambda}} . However, completing the algebra is insufficient on its own due to a critical numerical trap: when standard molecular orbitals adapted solely to an Abelian subgroup are utilized, cross-component integrals vanish identically, creating an artificial zero-gradient plateau along non-Abelian algebraic directions. Numerical experiments on NH 3 /STO-3G ( C 3 ​ v C_{3v} , 16 qubits) confirm both the predicted DLA confinement and the gradient plateau, with SymUCCSD converging to an error of 21.8 21.8 mHa above the FCI energy despite full optimizer convergence. Our analysis provides a rigorous algebraic and geometric diagnosis for the observed numerical breakdown, establishing that recovering full equivariant dynamics fundamentally necessitates both the inclusion of complete off-diagonal generators and the strategic parametrization of non-Abelian degrees of freedom.

Introduction

The Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) has emerged as a cornerstone for electronic structure simulations in the Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era
[
12
,
8
]
. Although the Unitary Coupled-Cluster Singles and Doubles (UCCSD) ansatz provides a physically motivated and robust framework for capturing electron correlation
[
14
]
, it is inherently limited by a combinatorial growth in the number of excitation amplitudes. This parameter explosion not only taxes limited quantum resources but also complicates the classical optimization landscape due to redundant degrees of freedom
[
9
]
. To mitigate this complexity, recent efforts have focused on exploiting molecular point group symmetries to prune the excitation pool. The Symmetry-adapted UCCSD (SymUCCSD) method
[
1
]
leverages Abelian subgroups to filter operators that do not transform according to the trivial irreducible representation (irrep) of the system. For Abelian point groups, such as
C_{2v}
, the method significantly reduces the parameter count, by upwards of 80%, without sacrificing spectroscopic accuracy.

However, recent numerical benchmarks by He et al.
[
5
]
revealed a catastrophic failure of SymUCCSD in molecules possessing non-Abelian symmetries (e.g., NH
3
or CH
4
). Although the Proposed Hamiltonian-integral-based UCCSD (HiUCCSD)
[
5
]
restores accuracy through an empirical operator selection, the underlying algebraic mechanism responsible for the SymUCCSD breakdown has remained elusive. In this work, we provide a rigorous theoretical diagnosis of this symmetry obstruction through the lens of Lie algebra. We move beyond simple parameter counting to prove that the SymUCCSD failure is a consequence of Lie-algebraic incompleteness: the Abelian-subgroup restriction removes the off‑diagonal generators that are essential for exploring the non‑Abelian Lie group orbit, and the remaining commuting generators confine the reachable set to a torus.

Specifically, we provide an exact algebraic diagnosis proving that the Abelian restriction induces a spurious splitting of multidimensional irreps, leading to the systematic exclusion of valid cross-component excitations. We establish that the SymUCCSD filter forces the Dynamical Lie Algebra (DLA) to collapse into a purely Abelian subalgebra, structurally restricting the reachable state manifold to a measure-zero torus
\mathbb{T}^{d_{\lambda}}
. Crucially, we analytically reveal that a secondary, yet equally fatal numerical obstruction emerges from standard quantum chemistry procedures: the alignment of the molecular orbital basis. If molecular orbitals are adapted strictly to an Abelian subgroup, cross-component Hamiltonian integrals vanish perfectly. This generates zero energy gradients along non-Abelian directions at initialization, trapping the optimizer in a topological plateau even if the ansatz formally spans the full group algebra. True recovery of equivariant dynamics thus requires simultaneously resolving the Lie-algebraic constraints and bypassing the gradient plateau via non-Abelian parametrization choices.

Algebraic setup and definitions

Throughout this work, we label each spatial orbital
\phi_{p}
by a composite index
p=(\lambda,i,\mu)
, where
\lambda
identifies the irreducible representation (irrep)
\rho_{\lambda}
of dimension
d_{\lambda}
,
i
denotes the orbital shell index, and
\mu\in\{1,\dots,d_{\lambda}\}
the component index. We follow the standard convention:
i,j,k,l
for occupied orbitals;
a,b,c,d
for virtual orbitals; and
p,q,r,s
for general indices. The associated creation and annihilation operators are denoted by
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{p}
and
\hat{a}_{p}
, respectively.

Definition 2.1

(Group action on Fock space)
.

The action of the molecular point group
G
on the orbital operators is defined by the adjoint action of the unitary representation
\hat{R}(g)
as:

\displaystyle\hat{R}(g)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mu}\hat{R}(g)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{\lambda}}D_{\nu,\mu}^{(\lambda)}(g)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\nu}

(1)

\displaystyle\hat{R}(g)\hat{a}_{i,\mu}\hat{R}(g)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu=1}^{d_{\lambda}}D_{\nu,\mu}^{(\lambda)}(g)^{*}\hat{a}_{i,\nu}

(2)

where
D^{(\lambda)}(g)
is the representation matrix of the element
g\in G
in the
\rho_{\lambda}
basis.

Definition 2.2

(Orbital partitioning)
.

Let the set of all spatial orbital shells be partitioned into occupied and virtual subsets. For any irrep
\rho_{\lambda}
of
G
, we define
N_{\mathrm{occ}}(\lambda)
and
N_{\mathrm{vir}}(\lambda)
as the number of occupied and virtual spatial orbital shells transforming according to
\rho_{\lambda}
. The total number of occupied shells is
\sum_{\lambda}N_{\mathrm{occ}}(\lambda)
and the total number of virtual shells is
\sum_{\lambda}N_{\mathrm{vir}}(\lambda)
.

Definition 2.3

(Hamiltonian and integrals)
.

The electronic Hamiltonian in second quantization is:

\mathcal{H}=\sum_{pq}h_{q}^{p}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{p}\hat{a}_{q}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{pqrs}h_{rs}^{pq}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{p}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{q}\hat{a}_{s}\hat{a}_{r}

(3)

where
h_{q}^{p}
and
h_{rs}^{pq}
are the one- and two-electron integrals in the symmetry-adapted basis.

Definition 2.4

(UCCSD pool and adjoint action)
.

The complete excitation pool
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{UCCSD}}
consists of all particle-number-preserving one- and two-body excitation operators. The adjoint action of
G
on the operator space is
\Ad(g)(T):=\hat{R}(g)\,T\,\hat{R}(g)^{-1}
. An operator is
A_{1}
-symmetric
if
\Ad(g)(T)=T
for all
g\in G
.

Definition 2.5

(Symmetry filters:
G
-equivariant vs. SymUCCSD)
.

We define two pool-filtering criteria. The
G
-equivariant pool (
\mathcal{P}_{G}
) consists of operators
T
such that
\Ad(g)(T)=T
. For singles
T_{ia}
, this requires
\rho(a)\cong\rho(i)
. The SymUCCSD pool (
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Sym}}
) uses a maximal Abelian subgroup
H\leq G

[
1
]
. Each orbital receives a 1D irrep label
\sigma(p)\in\widehat{H}
. It retains
T_{ia}
if
\sigma(a)\sigma(i)^{-1}=\sigma_{0}(H)
, and
T_{ijab}
if
\sigma(a)\sigma(b)\sigma(i)^{-1}\sigma(j)^{-1}=\sigma_{0}(H)
.

Definition 2.6

(Trotterized ansatz and Dynamical Lie Algebra)
.

The VQE trial state takes the Trotterized form

\ket{\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})}=\prod_{k}e^{\theta_{k}\hat{A}_{k}}\ket{\mathrm{HF}},\qquad\hat{A}_{k}=\hat{T}_{k}-\hat{T}_{k}^{\dagger}.

(4)

The
Dynamical Lie Algebra
(DLA) associated to a pool
\mathcal{P}
is the Lie algebra generated by the pool generators under iterated commutators:

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{P}}=\Lie\!\left(\{\hat{A}_{k}:\hat{T}_{k}\in\mathcal{P}\}\right).

(5)

The DLA determines the reachable manifold of the ansatz
[
2
, Ch. 3]
:
e^{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{P}}}
is the connected Lie group of unitaries accessible by the Trotterized circuit.

3
The Abelian case: correctness of SymUCCSD

Theorem 3.1

(Equivalence for Abelian groups)
.

Let
G
be an Abelian point group. Then
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Sym}}=\mathcal{P}_{G}
, and every anti-Hermitian
G
-equivariant operator in the UCCSD space can be written as a linear combination of operators in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Sym}}
.

Proof.

For
G
Abelian, every irrep is one-dimensional (
d_{\lambda}=1
). Setting
H=G
, the condition
\sigma(a)\sigma(i)^{-1}=\sigma_{0}
is identical to
\rho(a)\otimes\rho(i)^{*}\ni A_{1}
, since each orbital shell carries a unique one-dimensional label. The pools coincide and the ansatz captures exactly the symmetry-allowed excitations.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

This reproduces and extends the result of Ref.
[
5
]
, which proved for Abelian groups that vanishing Hamiltonian integrals coincide exactly with the symmetry filter. Our Lie-algebraic framework in the following sections unifies the Abelian and non-Abelian cases.

4
The non-Abelian case: linear incompleteness

When
G
is non-Abelian, the underlying assumption of SymUCCSD (that filtering by an Abelian subgroup suffices) breaks down.

Lemma 4.1

(Irrep splitting under Abelian restriction)
.

Let
G
be a non-Abelian point group with irrep
\rho_{\lambda}
of dimension
d_{\lambda}>1
, and let
H\leq G
be the maximal Abelian subgroup used for SymUCCSD filtering. Then
\rho_{\lambda}|_{H}
decomposes as

\rho_{\lambda}\big|_{H}=\sigma_{\lambda,1}\oplus\sigma_{\lambda,2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\sigma_{\lambda,d_{\lambda}}\,,

(6)

with at least two distinct irreps among the summands.

Proof.

Since
H
is an Abelian subgroup, the restriction
\rho_{\lambda}|_{H}
decomposes completely into a direct sum of one-dimensional irreducible representations
\sigma_{\lambda,\mu}
. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that all these one-dimensional irreps are identical, i.e.,
\sigma_{\lambda,\mu}=\sigma
for all
\mu\in\{1,\dots,d_{\lambda}\}
. In this case, the representation matrix for any
h\in H
would be a scalar multiple of the identity:
\rho_{\lambda}(h)=\sigma(h)I_{d_{\lambda}}
. Consequently,
\rho_{\lambda}(h)
would commute with
\rho_{\lambda}(g)
for all
g\in G
. Because
H
is a maximal Abelian subgroup of
G
, this would imply that the image
\rho_{\lambda}(G)
is an Abelian group (or equivalently, that the representation over
G
is reducible). However, an Abelian group only admits one-dimensional irreducible representations. Since we assumed
d_{\lambda}>1
, we reach a contradiction. Therefore, at least two of the one-dimensional components

\sigma_{\lambda,\mu}
must be strictly distinct
(cf.
[
15
, Ch. 2, §2.1]
for the general
restriction–decomposition theorem).
∎

The consequence is that the degenerate components
\phi_{i,1},\dots,\phi_{i,d_{\lambda}}
receive
different
labels under
H
, creating a spurious distinction between physically equivalent orbitals.

Theorem 4.2

(Linear incompleteness of SymUCCSD)
.

Let
G
be non-Abelian with irrep
\rho_{\lambda}
of dimension
d_{\lambda}>1
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{Sym}}\subsetneq\mathcal{P}_{G}
. Specifically, SymUCCSD discards exactly
d_{\lambda}(d_{\lambda}-1)
single excitations per pair of occupied–virtual shells in irrep
\rho_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Consider the single-excitation operators
\hat{E}_{\nu\mu}^{(ia)}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{a,\nu}\hat{a}_{i,\mu}
, which transfer an electron from the
\mu
-th component of the occupied shell
i
to the
\nu
-th component of the virtual shell
a
, where both shells transform according to
\rho_{\lambda}
. The
G
-equivariant rule requires
\rho_{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{*}\ni A_{1}
, which holds for all
\mu,\nu
pairs (since
\rho_{\lambda}\cong\rho_{\lambda}
). Thus all
d_{\lambda}^{2}
excitations belong to
\mathcal{P}_{G}
.

The SymUCCSD rule under
H
requires
\sigma_{\lambda,\nu}\sigma_{\lambda,\mu}^{-1}=\sigma_{0}(H)
. By Lemma
4.1
, for
\mu\neq\nu
:
\sigma_{\lambda,\mu}\neq\sigma_{\lambda,\nu}
, so the condition fails. Only the
d_{\lambda}
diagonal excitations (
\mu=\nu
) survive, losing
d_{\lambda}^{2}-d_{\lambda}=d_{\lambda}(d_{\lambda}-1)
operators.
∎

Theorem 4.3

(Incompleteness in double excitations)
.

For double excitations
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{a,\nu}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{b,\sigma}\hat{a}_{j,\tau}\hat{a}_{i,\mu}
with all orbitals in
\rho_{\lambda}
(
d_{\lambda}>1
), the SymUCCSD discards significant fractions of operators with generically non-vanishing two-body repulsion integrals
h_{\nu\sigma\mu\tau}^{abij}
.

Proof.

The exact
G
-equivariant selection criterion requires that the tensor product
\rho_{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{*}
contains the trivial representation
A_{1}
. We compute this multiplicity exactly. Since
\rho_{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{*}\cong\mathrm{End}(\rho_{\lambda})
as a
G
-module, its decomposition takes the form
\rho_{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{*}=A_{1}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\neq A_{1}}c_{\alpha}\,\rho_{\alpha}
, where the
A_{1}
summand (multiplicity one, by Schur’s lemma) corresponds to the scalar endomorphisms and the remaining summands have total dimension
d_{\lambda}^{2}-1>0
(since
d_{\lambda}>1
). The multiplicity of
A_{1}
in the four-fold product
(\rho_{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{*})\otimes(\rho_{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{\lambda}^{*})
equals
\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{2}
, which satisfies

\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{2}\;\geq\;c_{A_{1}}^{2}+c_{\alpha_{0}}^{2}\;\geq\;1+1=2\,,

(7)

where
\rho_{\alpha_{0}}\neq A_{1}
is any irrep appearing in the decomposition (at least one such irrep exists because
d_{\lambda}^{2}-1>0
). Hence the four-fold product contains
A_{1}
with multiplicity at least two, guaranteeing the existence of linearly independent
A_{1}
-equivariant operators with non-vanishing integrals. Conversely, the SymUCCSD filter under
H
retains only the combinations satisfying
\sigma_{\lambda,\nu}\sigma_{\lambda,\sigma}\sigma_{\lambda,\mu}^{*}\sigma_{\lambda,\tau}^{*}=\sigma_{0}(H)
. By Lemma
4.1
, this condition excludes all index tuples in which the components do not pairwise cancel under the Abelian labels, discarding operators whose projections onto the
A_{1}
subspace of
G
are non-vanishing. The incompleteness follows because the Abelian filter enforces a strictly finer selection rule than the
G
-equivariant one.
∎

5
Topological consequence: confinement of the DLA

The linear-algebraic analysis of Section
4
establishes which operators are
omitted
from the pool. However, the fundamental limitation of the ansatz lies in the
topology
of the reachable set determined by the Dynamical Lie Algebra.

Theorem 5.1

(DLA confinement under SymUCCSD)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{Sym}}
be the DLA generated by the SymUCCSD pool, restricted to an irrep sector
\rho_{\lambda}
with
d_{\lambda}>1
and a single pair of shells
(i,a)
. Then:

(i)

The
d_{\lambda}
retained generators
\{\hat{A}_{\mu\mu}^{(ia)}\}_{\mu=1}^{d_{\lambda}}
commute pairwise:

[\hat{A}_{\mu\mu}^{(ia)},\hat{A}_{\nu\nu}^{(ia)}]=0
for all
\mu\neq\nu
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{Sym}}\big|_{\rho_{\lambda}}
is a purely Abelian Lie algebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{u}(1)^{d_{\lambda}}
.

(iii)

The reachable set is confined to a torus

\exp(\lieg_{\mathrm{Sym}}\big|_{\rho_\lambda})\cong\mathbb{T}^{d_{\lambda}}=\mathrm{U}(1)^{d_{\lambda}}
of dimension
d_{\lambda}
,
which is a
measure-zero submanifold
of
\mathrm{U}(d_{\lambda})
(dimension
d_{\lambda}^{2}
).

(iv)

In contrast, the full
G
-equivariant pool (which includes the off‑diagonal generators) generates
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{full}}\big|_{\rho_{\lambda}}\supseteq\mathfrak{u}(d_{\lambda})
,
whose exponential is
\mathrm{U}(d_{\lambda})
. The dimensional deficit is

\dim\mathrm{U}(d_{\lambda})-\dim\mathbb{T}^{d_{\lambda}}=d_{\lambda}^{2}-d_{\lambda}=d_{\lambda}(d_{\lambda}-1)
.

Proof.

The anti-Hermitian generators are
\hat{A}_{\mu\mu}^{(ia)}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{a,\mu}\hat{a}_{i,\mu}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mu}\hat{a}_{a,\mu}
. For
\mu\neq\nu
, these act on disjoint orbital pairs
(i_{\mu},a_{\mu})
and
(i_{\nu},a_{\nu})
. Since the creation and annihilation operators for distinct orbital indices anticommute, every cross-term in the commutator
[\hat{A}_{\mu\mu},\hat{A}_{\nu\nu}]
vanishes identically. Since all generators commute, the Lie algebra they generate (including all iterated commutators) is Abelian and isomorphic to
\mathfrak{u}(1)^{d_{\lambda}}
. For commuting generators,
\prod_{k}e^{\theta_{k}\hat{A}_{k}}=e^{\sum_{k}\theta_{k}\hat{A}_{k}}
, so the reachable set is exactly
\exp(\lieu(1)^{d_\lambda})\cong\mathrm{U}(1)^{d_{\lambda}}=\mathbb{T}^{d_{\lambda}}
. Since
\dim\mathbb{T}^{d_{\lambda}}=d_{\lambda}<d_{\lambda}^{2}=\dim\mathrm{U}(d_{\lambda})
, the torus is a proper submanifold of measure zero in
\mathrm{U}(d_{\lambda})
, and the dimensional deficit is
d_{\lambda}^{2}-d_{\lambda}=d_{\lambda}(d_{\lambda}-1)
. The full set of
d_{\lambda}^{2}
generators
\{\hat{E}_{\nu\mu}^{(ia)}\}
satisfies
[\hat{E}_{\nu\mu},\hat{E}_{\sigma\tau}]=\delta_{\mu\sigma}\hat{E}_{\nu\tau}-\delta_{\nu\tau}\hat{E}_{\sigma\mu}
, the commutation relations of
\mathfrak{gl}(d_{\lambda})
. From these, the
d_{\lambda}^{2}
anti-Hermitian combinations are precisely the standard generators of
\mathfrak{u}(d_{\lambda})
in the elementary matrix basis (cf.
[
3
, Ch. 9]
). Their exponential is
\mathrm{U}(d_{\lambda})
.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Linear vs Lie-Algebraic Completeness)
.

A critical subtlety emerging from our analysis is the fundamental discrepancy between the
linear span
of the operator pool and the
topology
of the resulting Lie group orbit. Numerical verification confirms that for a single pair of degenerate shells, the
A_{1}
-equivariant subspace of the operator pool is fully spanned by the diagonal generators retained under
H
(rank verification:
\rank P_{A_{1}}|_{\mathrm{retained}}=\rank P_{A_{1}}|_{\mathrm{full}}
). This linear accessibility might naively suggest that SymUCCSD is complete. However, such reasoning overlooks the non-commutative geometry of the unitary group. Because the off‑diagonal generators have been removed by the Abelian‑subgroup filter, the remaining generators commute, confining the reachable set to the maximal Abelian torus
\mathbb{T}^{d_{\lambda}}\subsetneq\mathrm{U}(d_{\lambda})
. Consequently, the off‑diagonal orbital rotations required to explore the full equivariant manifold
U(d_{\lambda})^{G}
are omitted not because their generators are missing from the span, but because the filter eliminates them entirely, leaving only a commuting subalgebra. In short, while linear completeness ensures that any
A_{1}
-equivariant operator can be written as a linear combination of the retained generators, Lie-algebraic completeness additionally requires closure under commutators; the retained diagonal generators, being commutative, fail to generate the missing off-diagonal rotations, restricting the system to a fundamentally smaller Lie group.

7
The Abelian Basis Trap and Requirements for a Complete Ansatz

Although our Lie-algebraic proof establishes exactly which operators are mathematically omitted by an Abelian filter (cf. Sections
4
and
5
), this uncovers a secondary numerical hazard intrinsic to standard quantum chemistry computations: the alignment of the molecular orbital (MO) basis itself. Crucially, this hazard is
independent
of the DLA confinement diagnosed in Theorem
5.1
; even if one restores the full set of off-diagonal generators to complete the DLA, the gradient plateau described below can still prevent the optimizer from exploiting the newly accessible directions.

Proposition 7.1

(Vanishing cross-component integrals in Abelian-adapted bases)
.

Let
H\leq G
be an Abelian subgroup and let
\{\phi_{i,\mu}\}
be the set of molecular orbitals obtained by diagonalizing the Fock operator within each
H
-irrep block (i.e., the standard Abelian-adapted MO basis). Then for any pair of orbitals
\phi_{i,\mu}
and
\phi_{j,\nu}
belonging to distinct
H
-irreps
\sigma_{\lambda,\mu}\neq\sigma_{\lambda,\nu}
, the one-electron integrals satisfy

h^{(i,\mu)}_{(j,\nu)}=\int\phi_{i,\mu}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\,\hat{h}\,\phi_{j,\nu}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}=0,

(12)

and the two-electron integrals
h^{pq}_{rs}
vanish whenever the
H
-irrep product
\sigma(p)\sigma(q)\sigma(r)^{-1}\sigma(s)^{-1}\neq\sigma_{0}(H)
.

Proof.

The Fock operator
\hat{F}
commutes with all symmetry operations in
H
. In the Abelian-adapted basis,
\hat{F}
is block-diagonal with respect to
H
-irreps, so its eigenvectors (the MOs) carry definite
H
-irrep labels. Since the one-electron Hamiltonian
\hat{h}
also commutes with elements of
H
, its matrix elements between orbitals of different
H
-irreps vanish by the orthogonality of irreducible representations
[
15
, Ch. 2]
. The same argument extends to two-electron integrals via the invariance of the Coulomb operator under
H
.
∎

Remark 7.2

(Relationship to DLA confinement)
.

Proposition
7.1
reveals a hazard that is logically distinct from Theorem
5.1
. The DLA confinement is a
structural
property of the generator pool: the SymUCCSD pool generates only a torus regardless of the Hamiltonian. The gradient plateau, by contrast, is a
basis-dependent
property of the energy landscape: even with a DLA-complete pool, if the Hamiltonian is expressed in an Abelian-adapted MO basis, the cross-component integrals vanish identically, producing zero energy gradients along the newly added non-Abelian directions. Resolving one obstruction without the other is insufficient for convergence.

Numerical convergence in VQE requires non-zero energy gradients
\nabla E(\boldsymbol{\theta})
to escape the initial Hartree-Fock state. For an underlying generator
\hat{G}_{k}
acting around the initialization
\boldsymbol{\theta}=0
, the gradient is precisely proportional to
\bra{\mathrm{HF}}[\mathcal{H},\hat{G}_{k}]\ket{\mathrm{HF}}
. Standard computational routines diagonalize the Hartree–Fock density matrix respecting only an Abelian subgroup (e.g., matching
C_{s}\leq C_{3v}
). By Proposition
7.1
, the integrals coupling structurally distinct component vectors of a multidimensional irrep (e.g.,
h_{xy}
) evaluate identically to zero in this basis.

Consequently, even if one defines an algebraically complete operator pool encompassing all necessary
A_{1}
-equivariant cross-component generators, the initialization gradient along these non-Abelian directions evaluates to identically zero. The optimizer is rendered blind, and the optimization process remains trapped in an artificial plateau despite the theoretical presence of a complete Lie algebra (see Figure

\mathfrak{u}(1)^{d_{\lambda}}

Constructing an algebraically complete DLA is inadequate unless the parameterization bypasses the initial zero-gradient plateau. A_{1}

Developing computationally efficient algorithmic configurations that meet these stringent mathematical conditions constitutes a promising direction for future research.

9
Discussion

9.1
Relationship to HiUCCSD

The HiUCCSD method
[
5
]
avoids the SymUCCSD failure by retaining excitations whose Hamiltonian coefficients are nonzero. When the molecular orbitals are not adapted to an Abelian subgroup (e.g., when the Fock eigenvectors within a degenerate subspace adopt an arbitrary orientation), the cross-component integrals
h^{(a,\nu)}_{(i,\mu)}
with
\mu\neq\nu
are generically nonzero, and the HiUCCSD integral-based filter recovers all
G
-equivariant excitations. In this sense, HiUCCSD is numerically equivalent to the Wigner–Eckart selection rule of the full group
G
. However, as established by Proposition
7.1
, if the MOs
are
Abelian-adapted, the HiUCCSD filter coincides with the SymUCCSD filter, since the cross-component integrals that would distinguish the two methods vanish identically. Our analysis thus clarifies a previously unrecognized basis-dependence of the HiUCCSD method and provides the underlying algebraic justification for its empirical success.

9.2
Connection to equivariant quantum circuits

The Lie-algebraic perspective connects to the broader framework of symmetry-equivariant quantum circuits
[
11
,
10
]
and to the DLA-based expressibility and trainability analysis
[
7
,
13
]
, which establishes that the DLA dimension and the topology of the Lie group orbit govern the presence of barren plateaus. Our work highlights a complementary regime. Whereas large DLAs are prone to classical barren plateaus (gradient concentration due to expressibility across vast state spaces), the “over-reduction” of the operator pool through Abelian filters leads to DLAs that are too small and topologically confined. Specifically, the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d_{\lambda}}
is a maximal Abelian subgroup of
\mathrm{U}(d_{\lambda})
; restricting the ansatz to this subgroup causes the gradients in all complementary non-Abelian directions to vanish exactly at initialization. This creates a distinct theoretical gradient plateau driven by basis symmetry, resulting in a reachability deficit that persists even with optimal parameters.

9.3
Limitations and outlook

Recent algorithmic advances, such as ADAPT-VQE and its parameter-based variants
[
4
,
6
]
, aim to efficiently construct compact ansätze. However, to succeed on non-Abelian systems like NH
3
, these iterative algorithms must rely on heuristic, integral-based pools (e.g., Hamiltonian-Informed UCCSD
[
5
]
). Our analysis proves mathematically why this is the case: forced reliance on strictly Abelian-adapted foundational pools would trap any such adaptive algorithm in the exact topological confinement demonstrated herein, emphasizing the necessity of structurally complete pools from the outset.

Our results assume exact Trotterization and noiseless simulation. In practice, Trotter errors and hardware noise may obscure the DLA deficit. Nevertheless, the deficit is
structural
(it persists independently of optimization quality, as confirmed by the numerical plateau in Section
8
) and becomes more pronounced at stretched geometries where full orbital relaxation is required. Since the results of Sections
4
–
5
are stated for arbitrary point groups and irreps, they apply without modification to higher-symmetry groups such as
O_{h}
and
I_{h}
; the deficit scales as
\sum_{\lambda}d_{\lambda}(d_{\lambda}-1)
. A remaining task is to provide explicit deficit tabulations for their irreps (e.g.,
T_{1g}
,
T_{2g}
,
E_{g}
in
O_{h}
) and to extend the analysis to triple excitations, where the combinatorics of six-fold tensor products is considerably richer.

The constructive development of a systematic algorithm that restores the full equivariant DLA while maintaining a minimal parameter count, and that simultaneously circumvents the gradient plateau of Proposition
7.1
, is the subject of ongoing work. The present analysis provides the strict theoretical foundation and the numerical baseline for such algorithmic efforts.

10
Conclusion

We have established that the failure of state-of-the-art symmetry-adapted ansätze based on Abelian subgroups is not a mere numerical artifact, but rather a direct geometrical consequence of structural confinement within the Dynamical Lie Algebra. Our analysis proves that the Abelian-subgroup filter prematurely strips away the off‑diagonal generators necessary for non‑Abelian rotations, leaving exclusively commuting generators that rigidly confine the reachable set to a torus
\mathbb{T}^{d_{\lambda}}
, a measure-zero submanifold of the full equivariant unitary group. Numerical validation on NH
3
/STO-3G confirms this prediction: the SymUCCSD ansatz with 75 parameters converges to a plateau
21.8
mHa above the FCI energy, despite full optimizer convergence.

A critical takeaway of this work is the distinction between linear accessibility and full Lie-algebraic completeness. The missing operators force the DLA to collapse algebraically to an Abelian regime. Furthermore, we identified and formalized a secondary obstruction: the vanishing of cross-component Hamiltonian integrals in Abelian-adapted molecular orbital bases (Proposition
7.1
), which creates a zero-gradient plateau that traps the optimizer even when the DLA is formally complete. Our combined topological and gradient diagnosis maps the observed numerical breakdown to two precise mathematical conditions, both of which must be satisfied simultaneously for successful convergence. These findings expose the fundamental hazard embedded in Abelian restrictions and outline the mandatory mathematical conditions necessary for engineering rigorously complete symmetry-adapted VQE algorithms.
