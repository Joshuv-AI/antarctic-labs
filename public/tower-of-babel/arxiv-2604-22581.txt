Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann Iterations: Convergence without Uniformly Bounded Variance
Daniel Cortild \orcidCortild ( daniel.cortild@maths.ox.ac.uk ) Coralia Cartis \orcidCartis ( coralia.cartis@maths.ox.ac.uk ) Mathematical Institute, University of Oxford, Oxford, United Kingdom

Abstract
We investigate the Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations for expected nonexpansive fixed-point problems in a real Hilbert space. We establish convergence guarantees under significantly weaker assumptions on the variance than those typically used in the literature. In particular, instead of a uniform bound on the variance of the stochastic oracle, we only assume finite variance at a single fixed point. Under this assumption, we prove almost sure weak convergence of the iterates, derive convergence rates for the expected residual, and obtain almost sure convergence rates for the running minimum residual. Notably, we recover the best-known stochastic oracle complexity without imposing uniformly bounded variance. We illustrate the applicability of our results to Stochastic Gradient Descent, where we recover known guarantees, and to Stochastic Three-Operator Splitting, for which we obtain the first results that avoid uniform variance bounds. Keywords. Krasnoselskii-Mann Iterations, Variance-at-Solution Assumption, Stochastic Gradient Descent, Stochastic Three-Operator Splitting. Mathematics Subject Classification (2000) 46N10, 47J26, 65K10, 90C25, 90C15.

Introduction

Fixed-point problems are central in optimization and variational analysis, providing a unified and systematic approach to solve nonlinear and operator equations. In this work, we consider the fixed point problem

\text{Find $p\in\Fix(T)$},

where
T\colon\H{\to}
is a nonexpansive mapping on a real Hilbert space , meaning that
\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in
, where has associated inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and induced norm
\|\cdot\|
, and
\Fix(T)=\{x\in\mathcal{H}\colon Tx=x\}
denotes its set of fixed points. A standard algorithm for tackling such problems is the Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations
(
22
;
20
)
, which are given by

x_{k+1}=(1-\lambda_{k})x_{k}+\lambda_{k}Tx_{k}\quad\text{for $k\geq 0$},

(KM)

where
x_{0}\in
and
(\lambda_{k})\subset(0,1)
is a sequence of
relaxation parameters
. This was introduced independently in
22
for
\lambda_{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}
and in
20
for
\lambda_{k}\equiv 1/2
. Weak convergence of the iterates was later established in
28
for constant parameters and for variable parameters satisfying
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}(1-\lambda_{k})=+\infty
in
17
. For a recent overview of the method, we refer to
11
and the references therein.

A modern challenge resides within the computability of the operator
T
. It is in general computationally prohibitive to evaluate the operator at each iteration. As such, we assume access to the operator only through a stochastic oracle
T_{\xi}
. We assume this estimator to be unbiased, namely that
T=\ExpD{\xi}{T_{\xi}}
, such that the problem becomes

\text{Find $p\in\Fix(T)=\Fix(\ExpD{\xi}{T_{\xi}})$}.

To this end, we study a stochastic variant of the standard Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations, given in Algorithm
1
. As in stochastic optimization, the stochasticity arises from sampling one operator at each iteration, rather than evaluating the expected operator.

{algorithm}

[H]

(Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann)

{algorithmic}

\Require
T_{\xi}\colon\H{\to}
nonexpansive with
\ExpD{\xi}{T_{\xi}}=T
, Relaxation parameters
\lambda_{k}\in(0,1)

\For

\State

\xi_{k}\sim\mathcal{D}
randomly and independently

\State

\EndFor

\Return
x_{K}

As is standard for fixed-point methods, we assume the operator

When dealing with stochastic algorithms, one typically needs to make an assumption about the estimator
T_{\xi}
. The usual one in the literature
5
;
6

\sigma^{2}
such that, for all

\Exp{\|(T-T_{\xi})x\|^{2}}\leq\sigma^{2}<+\infty.

In the functional case, this has been shown to be unverifiable or unrealistic in practice
4

3
;
24
;
25
;
15
;
16
;
19
;
8
;
13
.
This naturally raises the question of whether the above assumption can be relaxed in the fixed point setting while still preserving meaningful guarantees. However, weakening the variance assumptions appears to be nontrivial, as existing analyses heavily rely on uniform variance bounds.

Contributions.
We provide a convergence analysis of Algorithm
1
in a real Hilbert space setting.
Our contributions are as follows.

1.

We introduce a variance-at-solution assumption, requiring a bounded variance at a single solution
p\in\Fix(T)
, namely that

\Exp{\|(T-T_{\xi})p\|^{2}}\leq\sigma_{*}^{2}<+\infty.

Our key insight is that, by exploiting nonexpansiveness, this local variance assumption propagates to all fixed points and yields a global variance control.

2.

Under this assumption, we establish almost sure convergence of the residuals and weak almost sure convergence of the iterates to a point in
\Fix(T)
.

3.

We derive convergence rates on the expected residual, and recover the best-known stochastic oracle complexity
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-4})
, previously established under uniformly bounded variance
5
. We moreover obtain the same complexity rate almost surely on the running minimal residual.

4.

We apply our results to Stochastic Gradient Descent, recovering known guarantees, and to Stochastic Three-Operator Splitting, where we obtain the first results that do not make use of uniformly bounded variance assumptions.

These contributions demonstrate that uniformly bounded variance is unnecessarily strong, and can be relaxed to a weaker, local variance assumption without loss of convergence or complexity guarantees.

Comparison with Previous Works.
Our work is most closely related to the following works.

•

The closest prior work is due to Bravo and Cominetti
5
, who study Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations in normed spaces. They consider a framework with additive noise and allow martingale-difference perturbations whose variance may grow in a controlled manner, and obtain convergence of the expected residual and almost sure weak convergence of the iterates. The most directly comparable explicit complexity bounds are obtained under uniformly bounded variance. In that regime, they prove an
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-6})
rate result for the last-iterate residual, and a
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-4})
rate result for a random iterate in the Euclidean setting. We recover their results under weaker assumptions.

•

Bravo and Contreras
6
study Stochastic Halpern iterations in normed spaces, again under the stronger uniformly bounded variance assumption. Their method uses increasing minibatches and yields an
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-5})
oracle complexity on the last-iterate residual.

•

Finally, we were made aware of the work by Iiduka
18
during the writing of this manuscript, and note that our work was developed independently. They study Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations, still under uniformly bounded variance, and obtain convergence in
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-4})
stochastic oracle calls. They show convergence of the expected residuals, but do not establish weak convergence of the iterates, and do not allow for our weaker assumption.

Convergence Results

In this section, we present our main convergences results for Algorithm
1
. We first introduce our standing assumptions, including our variance-at-solution assumption, and establish key lemmas. We then derive convergence guarantees for the iterates and the expected residual.

2.1
Assumptions

We begin by introducing the assumptions used throughout the analysis. The first assumption is on the problem at hand.

{assumption}

[Problem Assumption]
We assume the operators
T_{\xi}
are nonexpansive for
\mathcal{D}
-almost all
\xi
. We further assume that
\xi\mapsto T_{\xi}x
is measurable for each
x\in
and define
T\colon\H{\to}
by
x\mapsto\ExpD{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}{T_{\xi}x}
. Moreover, we assume
\Fix(T)
to be nonempty.

We note that under the Problem Assumption
2.1
, it immediately holds that
T
is nonexpansive.

The second assumption treats the stochasticity in the problem. Our key contribution is that we do not require a uniformly bounded variance, as done in prior works, but merely a bounded variance-at-solution assumption.

{assumption}

[Variance Assumption]
We assume that, for some
p\in\Fix(T)
,

\Exp{\|(T_{\xi}-T)p\|^{2}}\leq\sigma_{*}^{2}<+\infty.

The next lemma shows that if the quantity in the Variance Assumption
2.1
is bounded for some
p\in\Fix(T)
, then the quantity is constant over all
\Fix(T)
, and hence bounded for all
p\in\Fix(T)
. This parallels the corresponding result in the function setting explored in
(
14
, Lemma 4.17)
.

Lemma 2.1

(Bounded Variance at All Fixed Points)
.

Under the Problem Assumption
2.1
and the Variance Assumption
2.1
, it holds that, for all
p\in\Fix(T)
,

\Exp{\|(T_{\xi}-T)p\|^{2}}\leq\sigma_{*}^{2}.

Proof 2.2
.

Consider any two fixed points
p,p^{\prime}\in\Fix(T)
. As such, by convexity of
x\mapsto\|x\|^{2}
, it holds that

\|p-p^{\prime}\|^{2}=\|\Exp{T_{\xi}p}-\Exp{T_{\xi}p^{\prime}}\|^{2}\leq\Exp{\|T_{\xi}p-T_{\xi}p^{\prime}\|^{2}}\leq\|p-p^{\prime}\|^{2}.

Therefore equality holds, namely
\|\Exp{T_{\xi}p-T_{\xi}p^{\prime}}\|^{2}=\Exp{\|T_{\xi}p-T_{\xi}p^{\prime}\|^{2}}
, and thus
T_{\xi}p-T_{\xi}p^{\prime}
has zero variance and is almost surely constant. In particular, it is equal to its expectation, namely
T_{\xi}p-T_{\xi}p^{\prime}=Tp-Tp^{\prime}
almost surely, which implies that
T_{\xi}-T
is almost surely constant over
\Fix(T)
, and
p\mapsto\Exp{\|(T_{\xi}-T)p\|^{2}}
is constant over
\Fix(T)
, as claimed.

Moreover, if the quantity in Assumption
2.1
is bounded for some
p\in\Fix(T)
, then we may bound it for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
, albeit with a non-constant right-hand side. We refer to this as a variance transfer result. The below generalizes the functional setting
(
14
, Lemmas 4.19 and 4.20)
.

Lemma 2.3

(Variance Transfer Lemma)
.

Under the Problem Assumption
2.1
and the Variance Assumption
2.1
, it holds that, for all
x\in
and
p\in\Fix(T)
,

\Exp{\|(T_{\xi}-T)x\|^{2}}\leq 2\sigma_{*}^{2}+8\|x-p\|^{2}.

Proof 2.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\Exp{\|(T_{\xi}-T)x\|^{2}}\leq 2\Exp{\|(T_{\xi}-T)p\|^{2}}+2\Exp{\|(T_{\xi}-T)x-(T_{\xi}-T)p\|^{2}},

and the result follows by the Variance Assumption
2.1
and by
2
-Lipschitzness of
T_{\xi}-T
. We note this result holds for all
p\in\Fix(T)
, and not only the one in the Variance Assumption
2.1
, by Lemma
2.1
.

2.2
Convergence Results

To establish convergence, we impose assumptions on the step-size sequence. We denote by
\ell^{p}(\N)
all sequences
(\lambda_{k})_{k\in\N}\subset\R
such that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}<+\infty
, where
p\geq 1
.

{assumption}
[Parameter Assumption]
The relaxation parameters
(\lambda_{k})\subset(0,1)
satisfy
(\lambda_{k})\in\ell^{2}(\N)\backslash\ell^{1}(\N)
.

We define the following quantity for notational convenience

\Lambda_{k}=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1+8\lambda_{i}^{2})^{-1}.

We note that
\Lambda_{K}\geq\Lambda_{\infty}>0
under the assumption that
(\lambda_{k})\in\ell^{2}(\N)
. In fact, since
\log(1+x)\leq x
for all
x>-1
,

\Lambda_{K}^{-1}=\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}(1+8\lambda_{k}^{2})=\exp\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\log(1+8\lambda_{k}^{2})\right)\leq\exp\left(8\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}\right),

(1)

so we may pick
\Lambda_{\infty}^{-1}=\exp\left(8\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2}\right)<+\infty
.

We also introduce a random index
N_{K}
, independent of the algorithmic randomness, defined by

\lx@paragraphsign(N_{K}=k)=\frac{\Lambda_{k+1}\lambda_{k}(1-\lambda_{k})}{\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Lambda_{k+1}\lambda_{k}(1-\lambda_{k})}\quad\text{for $k=0,\ldots,K-1$}.

(2)

This index allows us to convert weighted averages of the residual into guarantees on a single (random) iterate.

Remark 2.5
.

The Parameter Assumption
2.2
is naturally satisfied for step-sizes
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{0}(k+1)^{-a}
for
a\in(1/2,1]
. In fact, it holds that

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\lambda_{k}^{2}=\lambda_{0}^{2}\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(k+1)^{-2a}\leq\lambda_{0}^{2}\zeta(2a)\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{K}\geq e^{-8\lambda_{0}^{2}\zeta(2a)},

where the second inequality follows from Equation (
1

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{0}\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(k+1)^{-a}\geq\dcases\lambda_{0}\cdot\frac{(K+1)^{1-a}-1}{1-a}=\mathcal{O}(K^{1-a})\quad&\text{if $a<1$},\\
\lambda_{0}\cdot\log(K+1)=\mathcal{O}(\log(K))\text{if $a=1$}.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we established the convergence of the Stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations for expected fixed-point problems under the weak assumption of finite variance at a single fixed point, relaxing the typically-made uniformly bounded variance assumption. Under this weaker assumption, we recover the best-known
\mathcal{O}(K^{-1/4})
stochastic oracle calls rate for the convergence of the expected residual, show the rate holds almost surely for the running minimum residual, and prove almost sure convergence of the iterates to a fixed point. These results show that uniformly bounded variance is not intrinsic in the analysis of stochastic fixed point methods, and can be replaced by a significantly weaker local condition.

Furthermore, we demonstrate the applicability of our results by considering two representative examples. For the case of Stochastic Gradient Descent, we recover known convergence results. However, for Stochastic Three-Operator Splitting, we obtain results which are the first to bypass the uniformly bounded variance assumption.

Outlook.

An interesting direction of future research is to understand how the stochasticity in Algorithm
1
interacts with inertia. Specifically, in order to control the stochasticity, we made the assumption that the relaxation parameters decrease to
0
. However, for inertial results, it is typical that the relaxation sequences need to be uniformly bounded away from
0

9
;
23
.
