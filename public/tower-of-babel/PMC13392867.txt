Peripheral microvascular function and long-term cardiovascular outcomes after percutaneous coronary intervention in patients with obstructive coronary artery disease
Authors: Ya-Wen Lu, Chuan-Tsai Tsai, Chun-Chin Chang, Ruey-Hsing Chou, Chin-Sung Kuo, Po-Hsun Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDespite advances in percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), patients with obstructive coronary artery disease (CAD) continue to experience recurrent ischemic symptoms and adverse cardiovascular events. Skin post-occlusive reactive hyperemia (PORH) is a noninvasive measure of systemic microvascular function that may add prognostic information beyond epicardial coronary anatomy, but its relation to long-term cardiovascular outcomes after PCI has not been well defined.MethodsWe performed a retrospective analysis of a prospectively established CAD registry that included 197 patients with obstructive CAD who underwent PCI and baseline assessment of the skin PORH response. Long-term composite cardiovascular outcomes were evaluated over follow-up of up to 5 years using Kaplan–Meier methods and Cox proportional hazards regression, with propensity score matching used as a sensitivity analysis.ResultsThe median age of the cohort was 66 years, and 84.8% of patients were male. Compared with those in the higher PORH group, patients with a lower PORH index had more cardiometabolic comorbidities and experienced significantly higher rates of long-term composite cardiovascular events. In multivariable Cox analysis, a higher PORH index was independently associated with a lower risk of composite cardiovascular events (adjusted hazard ratio, 0.422; 95% confidence interval, 0.198–0.898), and this inverse association was preserved in the propensity score–matched cohort.ConclusionsIn patients with obstructive CAD undergoing PCI, impaired skin PORH was independently associated with an increased risk of long-term composite cardiovascular events. These findings support a potential role for skin microvascular assessment in cardiovascular risk stratification, and warrant confirmation in larger prospective studies.

Introduction

Despite major advances in coronary revascularization and secondary prevention, coronary artery disease (CAD) remains a leading cause of death worldwide [1], [2]. Many patients with anatomically significant coronary stenosis continue to experience recurrent ischemic symptoms and adverse cardiovascular events despite successful percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) [3], [4].

Vascular endothelial dysfunction plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of in-stent restenosis (ISR) and adverse CV outcomes after PCI [5]. Impaired brachial artery flow-mediated dilation (FMD), a noninvasive assessment of conduit artery endothelial function, has been shown to be independently associated with late ISR [6] as well as subsequent adverse CV events [7]. In addition, FMD correlates with coronary microvascular dysfunction, suggesting a link between peripheral endothelial dysfunction and coronary vascular pathology [8]. However, FMD primarily reflects nitric oxide-dependent vasoreactivity in large conduit arteries and may not fully capture abnormalities within the microvascular circulation.

Post-occlusive reactive hyperemia (PORH) response of the skin is a noninvasive method for assessing peripheral systemic microvascular function [9]. PORH represents an integrated microvascular response involving endothelial-derived hyperpolarizing factors, sensory nerve activation, potassium channel signaling, and partially nitric oxide-dependent pathways [10]. Prior studies have demonstrated that reduced PORH responses are associated with traditional CV risk factors, chronic kidney disease, diabetes mellitus, and increased arterial stiffness. These previous results suggest that impaired skin microvascular reactivity accompanies adverse vascular remodeling and broader involvement of multiple organs [11].

Given that skin PORH reflects systemic microvascular function, it may provide complementary prognostic information beyond conventional cardiovascular risk factors. However, evidence linking skin PORH responses to long-term CV outcomes in patients with established chronic CAD, particularly those with angiographically confirmed disease who have undergone PCI, remains limited. Therefore, in the present study, we aimed to investigate the association between impaired skin PORH response and long-term composite CV outcomes in a chronic CAD population treated with PCI.

Methods

Study design and population

We performed a retrospective cohort study based on a coronary artery disease registry at Taipei Veterans General Hospital, Taiwan. From January 2017 to June 2023, 551 consecutive patients were enrolled in this registry. As part of the institutional vascular function protocol, all participants underwent coronary angiography and a baseline skin post-occlusive reactive hyperemia (PORH) assessment at the time of catheterization. Patients with a prior history of acute myocardial infarction (n = 29) or concomitant peripheral arterial occlusive disease (n = 4) were excluded. Among the remaining cohort, 268 patients had obstructive CAD. Of these, 71 patients who did not undergo subsequent PCI were excluded from the present analysis. The final study population consisted of 197 patients with obstructive CAD who underwent coronary revascularization, including plain old balloon angioplasty for previous in-stent restenosis (n = 6) or stent implantation for stenotic lesions (n = 191). Coronary angiographic findings, including the presence and severity of obstructive CAD, were independently reviewed and confirmed by at least two interventional cardiologists, each with more than 5 years of PCI experience, who were blinded to PORH measurements and clinical outcomes. Written informed consent was obtained at the time of enrollment in the institutional CAD registry, and the present retrospective analysis was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Taipei Veterans General Hospital with a waiver of additional informed consent. Baseline characteristics and blood sample collection

Information on baseline clinical characteristics was obtained from the medical records, encompassing demographic variables, pre-existing comorbid conditions, medication exposure, and biochemical data. Collected variables included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, atrial fibrillation, diabetes mellitus, chronic heart failure, coronary artery disease, and use of CV-related medications. Laboratory data included total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL—C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), triglycerides, uric acid, and renal function indices. Renal function was assessed by estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), derived from the CKD-EPI formula and reported in mL/min/1.73 m2. Microalbuminuria was defined as a urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) ≥ 30 mg/g.

Assessment of Skin microvascular function

Skin microvascular function was assessed by PORH protocol before cardiac catheterization, with moorVMS-PRES and moorVMS-LDF (Moor Instruments) according to previously described methods. Measurements were performed in a temperature-controlled room (20–25 °C) after at least 10 min of rest in the supine position. The laser Doppler probe was placed on the forearm contralateral to the planned catheterization site. After a 3-min baseline recording, arterial inflow was occluded by inflating an upper-arm cuff to 200 mmHg or at least 60 mmHg above systolic pressure for 3 min. The cuff was then deflated, and skin blood-flow signals were recorded continuously for a further 3 min.

Laser Doppler flux data were analyzed using MoorSoft for Windows (version 4.2; Moor Instruments) with an automated algorithm to minimize interobserver variability. The area under the perfusion curve during the first minute after cuff release (post-AUC₁min) was calculated and normalized to the corresponding 1-min baseline AUC before cuff inflation. The resulting ratio was defined as the PORH index, which reflects the magnitude of reactive cutaneous microvascular vasodilation [[9], [12]].

Composite CV endpoint and long-term follow-up

The primary composite cardiovascular (CV) endpoint was defined as the occurrence of cardiac death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, stroke, or ischemia-driven target lesion or target vessel revascularization during follow-up. Detailed definitions of each clinical outcome are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Patients were enrolled between January 2017 and June 2023, with follow-up data available through June 2025; consequently, not all participants completed a full 5-year follow-up period. Time-to-event analyses were conducted using right-censoring methods. Follow-up was administratively censored at 5 years (1825 days) or at the end of data availability, whichever occurred first. Events occurring beyond 5 years were not included in the 5-year analyses.

Statistical analysis

Patients were categorized into higher and lower PORH response groups according to the median PORH index (2.36). Continuous variables are presented as medians with interquartile ranges (25th–75th percentile), whereas categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons were performed using the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables and the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables.

To reduce baseline imbalance between groups, propensity score matching (PSM) was performed using a 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching algorithm without replacement. Propensity scores were estimated using a logistic regression model including age, diabetes mellitus, and microalbuminuria, which were selected a priori because of their established associations with endothelial dysfunction and peripheral microvascular function. Covariate balance before and after matching was assessed using absolute standardized mean differences (SMDs) and graphically summarized using a Love plot. Given the limited sample size, some residual imbalances (SMDs >0.10) were anticipated and subsequently addressed in multivariable Cox regression models for the matched cohort.

Time-to-event outcomes were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method, with the between-group differences assessed by the log-rank test. Participants without an event were censored at the last follow-up, with follow-up administratively censored at 5 years (1825 days).

Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to evaluate the association between the PORH index and long-term cardiovascular outcomes. In the overall cohort, multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were constructed using a prespecified clinically relevant adjustment model including age, sex, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, eGFR, and smoking status. In the PSM cohort, multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression was further performed to account for potential residual confounding. Given the limited number of outcome events and to minimize the risk of model overfitting, only variables with a univariate P value <0.10 were entered into the multivariable model. Accordingly, higher PORH index, smoking, hypertension, and HDL cholesterol were included in the final multivariable model.

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 23.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R software version 4.6.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

During long-term follow-up (up to 5 years), all-cause mortality did not differ significantly between groups. In contrast, the incidence of composite CV events (CV death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, stroke, and ischemia-driven TLR or TVR) was significantly higher among patients in the lower PORH group than in the higher PORH group (24.5% vs. 11.1%, p = 0.016; Supplementary Table S1). Although each individual component of the composite endpoint occurred more frequently in the lower PORH group, none reached statistical significance when analyzed separately.

In univariate Cox regression analysis, a higher PORH index was associated with a significantly lower risk of long-term composite CV events (hazard ratio [HR], 0.387; 95% CI, 0.190–0.791; p = 0.009). After multivariable adjustment for age, sex, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, eGFR, and smoking status, a higher PORH index (adjusted HR, 0.422; 95% CI, 0.198–0.898; p = 0.025) and hypertension (adjusted HR, 4.626; 95% CI, 1.074–19.925; p = 0.040) remained independently associated with long-term composite CV events (Table 2). Kaplan–Meier analysis also demonstrated a lower cumulative incidence of composite CV events in the higher PORH group (log-rank p = 0.007; Fig. 2A). Discussion

Our findings showed that patients with a higher PORH index had fewer long-term composite cardiovascular events after PCI. This association remained significant after multivariable adjustment and in the propensity score–matched cohort. Patients with a lower PORH index exhibited a higher burden of traditional cardiovascular risk factors, including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, prior stroke, and microalbuminuria, indicating worse systemic vascular health.

These findings are consistent with prior reports showing that reduced skin PORH responses are associated with a higher future CV event risk in patients with peripheral artery disease undergoing vascular surgery [13]. Similar to our findings, impaired microvascular function measured by the RHI using digital peripheral arterial tonometry has been reported to be related to major adverse CV events, including CV death, myocardial infarction, and heart failure hospitalization in patients with established CAD and heart failure [14]. Extending these observations, the present study evaluated a composite outcome that included ischemia-driven TLR or TVR, a component that has not been well investigated in PORH- or RHI-based studies before. Our study provided additional information on the potential relation between skin microvascular reactivity and coronary revascularization outcomes.

The PORH response attenuates in the presence of multiple traditional and non-traditional cardiovascular risk factors. Patients with diabetes exhibit substantially lower PORH responses, and reduced PORH correlates with increased brachial–ankle pulse wave velocity [9]. Even after adjustment for diabetes and blood pressure, lower skin PORH remains associated with greater proteinuria, impaired eGFR, and chronic kidney disease [11], [15], [16]. Collectively, these findings suggest that impaired microvascular reactivity is accompanied by arterial stiffness and target organ damage. At the same time, prior studies have found only modest correlations between the PORH index and large-vessel measures such as aortic strain and aortic distensibility [17], [18]. Skin PORH should therefore be regarded as a marker of systemic vascular dysfunction that reflects one important facet of vascular health, rather than a comprehensive measure of overall vascular status.

Unlike FMD, which primarily assesses endothelial function in conduit arteries, the PORH response integrates multiple physiological pathways involved in microvascular regulation [[19], [20]]. Although impaired FMD is linked to endothelial dysfunction and adverse cardiovascular outcomes, including peripheral artery disease and stable CAD cohorts, its prognostic value in contemporary PCI cohorts treated with second-generation drug-eluting stents remains inconsistent [21], [22], [23]. In contrast, coronary microvascular function after PCI is increasingly recognized as a key determinant of adverse outcomes, as reflected by indices such as the index of microcirculatory resistance [24], [25], [26]. However, direct assessment of coronary microvascular function is technically demanding and relies on invasive physiological measurements or advanced imaging, which limits its routine clinical use [27]. Emerging data suggest that peripheral and coronary microvascular beds share important endothelial-dependent physiological characteristics, whereas endothelium-independent vasodilatory responses do not correlate as closely [28]. Peripheral arterial tonometry demonstrates reasonable discriminatory ability for identifying coronary microvascular endothelial dysfunction, and acute mental stress appears to blunt peripheral and coronary microvascular responses in a similar fashion [29].

Our findings showed that a lower PORH index was associated with a higher incidence of composite CV events, driven primarily by a consistent trend toward increased ischemia-driven TLR or TVR. These findings provide clinical evidence consistent with the concept that cutaneous microvascular dysfunction may reflect a broader microvascular phenotype associated with significant coronary ischemia. These observations highlight the need for dedicated mechanistic studies, including direct assessment of coronary microvascular function, to clarify the underlying relationship between skin microvascular reactivity and coronary microvascular function and to determine whether interventions that improve PORH can reduce hard CV events.

Limitation

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective, single-center design is subject to unmeasured and residual confounding, and causality between PORH index and CV outcomes cannot be established. Second, outcome assessment may be influenced by potential selection and ascertainment bias, because not all patients underwent systematic follow-up coronary angiography or imaging, and ischemia-driven revascularization decisions were made at the operators' discretion. Third, PORH responses can be affected by multiple clinical and physiological factors, including comorbidities, medications, and microvascular bed heterogeneity; therefore, residual differences between groups may persist despite multivariable adjustment and propensity score matching, especially given the modest sample size. Fourth, skin microvascular function was assessed using a single PORH protocol and device, which may limit generalizability to other techniques or vascular beds. Finally, the sample size was relatively modest and the number of individual endpoint events was limited, so the study may be underpowered to detect small effect sizes for specific outcome components. The findings should be confirmed in larger, prospective multicenter studies.

Conclusion

In patients with obstructive CAD undergoing PCI, impaired skin PORH was associated with a higher risk of long-term composite CV events, and this association remained significant in multivariable adjustment, including in the propensity score–matched cohort. Further prospective studies are warranted to elucidate the mechanistic links between skin microvascular reactivity and coronary ischemic outcomes and to assess the potential role of PORH-based microvascular assessment in cardiovascular risk stratification.
