Heterogeneous survival impact of immune-related adverse events in US veterans
Authors: Sadiq Rehmani, Luke Michael Higgins, Daria Brinzevich, Virginia Falvello, Garth W Strohbehn, Michael Dykstra, David Elliott, Nithya Ramnath, Alex K Bryant
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmune-related adverse events (irAEs) have been associated with improved overall survival (OS) in patients with cancer treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), as irAEs are thought to signify a potentially robust antitumor immune response. The interplay between irAE subtypes, irAE severity, and oncological outcomes remains understudied.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective study of patients with cancer in the US Veteran Health Administration who received first-line or second-line ICI therapy (programmed cell death protein 1, programmed death-ligand 1, cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4, or lymphocyte activation gene 3 inhibitors)±chemotherapy from 2010 to 2023. Manual chart review was performed on a random sample to ascertain irAEs. The primary outcome was OS, analyzed by irAE status (no irAE vs irAE), severity (steroid-requiring vs non-steroid-requiring), and subtype (thyroid/cutaneous vs other). To account for immortal time bias, OS was analyzed with multivariable time-dependent Cox regression and 180-day landmark analysis.ResultsAmong 1707 patients, 465 (27.2%) experienced at least one irAE (median onset: 108 days; unadjusted 2-year rate: 38.2% (35%–41%)). The occurrence of any-grade irAE was not associated with improved OS (adjusted HR (aHR) 0.87; 95% CI 0.75 to 1.01). Analysis stratified by steroid requirement (n=222/465 events, 47.7%) showed improved survival with non-steroid-requiring irAE (aHR 0.69, 95% CI 0.57 to 0.84), whereas steroid-requiring irAEs were associated with a trend toward worse survival (aHR 1.13, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.38). The non-steroid-requiring irAE subset was enriched for thyroid (45% vs 8%) and cutaneous (27% vs 16%) toxicities compared with the steroid-requiring group. Exploration of the impact of irAE subtype on OS showed significant survival benefit in patients with thyroid/cutaneous irAEs (aHR 0.57, 95% CI 0.46 to 0.71) but not other irAE subtypes.ConclusionsirAEs are not uniformly associated with improved survival. Favorable outcomes are driven by thyroid-related and skin-related, non-steroid-requiring irAEs, suggesting shared biological mechanisms underlying organ-specific autoimmunity and antitumor immune response.Keywords: Immunotherapy, Thyroid toxicity, Treatment related adverse event - trAE

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI) have improved the survival outcomes of patients with many different types of cancers.1 2 Although these therapies are generally well-tolerated, many patients frequently develop immune-related adverse events (irAEs) of varying severity, which can affect multiple organ systems.3 4 While most irAEs are low grade and can be managed with close monitoring, hormone replacements, or topical steroids, severe irAEs requiring systemic steroid administration and treatment interruption and/or discontinuation can occur.3 5

Development of irAEs following treatment with ICI has been proposed as a biomarker for ICI efficacy. Multiple studies have reported a positive association between irAEs in general and improved survival outcomes, but these have failed to handle irAEs as a time-dependent covariate, leading to severe immortal-time bias.6,8 Patients who developed irAEs must, by definition, have survived long enough to develop toxicity. Classifying patients into irAE groups at baseline thus overestimates survival in patients who develop irAEs, contributing to a potentially flawed clinical assumption that the development of any irAE invariably signals a better prognosis.

Studies employing statistical methods to address immortal time bias in the irAE context are limited by small sample sizes, homogenous tumor types (and thus perhaps host immune profiles), or ICI agents, and often do not provide a nuanced analysis adjusting for irAE subtype and severity.9,12 Large retrospective studies using electronic medical records (EMR) to study the irAE-survival relationship have often employed International Classification of Diseases (ICD) codes to identify patients who may have experienced an irAE despite there being no standardized disease definitions and ICD codes, which can lead to under-representation of true-positive irAEs and over-representation of false-positive irAEs.13 14

Skepticism toward the independent prognostic value of irAEs across various tumor types and ICI agents after accounting for immortal time bias, the impact of irAE severity, and the differential effect of specific irAE subtypes is thus warranted. In this study of a large, multicancer national cohort, we examined the relationship between the full spectrum of irAEs and overall survival (OS) using robust time-dependent survival models.

Methods

Study design and data source

This study used EMR data from the Veteran Health Administration (VHA) Corporate Data Warehouse (CDW), which includes data for all Veterans receiving care through VHA facilities nationwide.

Our dataset has been previously described in detail.15 Briefly, CDW was queried for all patients who received an ICI (single or dual agents including pembrolizumab, nivolumab, atezolizumab, avelumab, durvalumab, tremelimumab, dostarlimab, cemiplimab-rwlc, ipilimumab, or nivolumab/relatlimab-rmbw) monotherapy or combination chemoimmunotherapy between January 1, 2010 and December 31, 2023. The date of the first ICI infusion was defined as the index date for each patient. Cancer histology, date of diagnosis, and stage at diagnosis were obtained using a combination of Veterans Affairs (VA) Cancer Registry System data and ICD-9/10 codes. Given a significantly large number of all ICI-treated patients, performing a comprehensive manual chart review was not practically feasible. To balance feasibility with rigorous data validation, we derived a random sample for detailed manual chart review of irAEs. The details of randomization and matching have been previously reported in a prior publication.15 Briefly, a random sample of 1000 black patients (self-reported non-Hispanic or African-American) were matched to 1000 white patients (self-reported non-Hispanic white) using 1:1 exact matching without replacement. Matching variables included demographics and other patient factors that could potentially influence irAE risk. These included age, sex, cancer type, ICI type (programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-[L)1), cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4), combination CTLA-4/PD-[L)1, or programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/lymphocyte activation gene 3), ICI monotherapy versus combination with other therapies, baseline autoimmune disease, baseline hypothyroidism, stage at original diagnosis, number of prior lines of systemic therapy, and presence/severity of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. The sample was race-balanced to ensure adequate representation of black patients, who we previously showed experience a lower overall risk of irAEs.15 Baseline demographics and comorbidity data were ascertained through CDW as previously described.15

Immune-related adverse event and steroid exposures

All irAEs were ascertained by manual chart review of the matched cohort by an experienced radiation oncology physician (AKB). Hematology/Oncology clinical notes and discharge summaries within the first 2 years after ICI treatment initiation were reviewed. A 2-year period was chosen as the vast majority of irAEs occur within this time period.4 irAEs were recorded if they were felt to be possibly, probably, or definitely related to ICI treatment in the judgment of the managing hematologist/oncologist or inpatient physician. Timing, organ system(s) involved, and whether irAEs led to permanent ICI discontinuation, systemic steroid treatments, or hospitalization were recorded. Treatment with systemic steroids is a marker of clinically significant (grade 2 or higher) irAE events16; we considered this a pragmatic measure of irAE severity, given the difficulty and subjectivity of formally grading irAE events by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) criteria from routinely collected health records with variable documentation quality and completeness. Patients with pre-existing hypothyroidism prior to ICI treatment were excluded from the analysis of thyroid events to capture only treatment-emergent thyroiditis.

Statistical analysis

The primary outcome was OS defined as time from index date until death or censored at their last follow-up. Median follow-up time was estimated using the reverse Kaplan-Meier method. The data cut-off date was August 28, 2024. Our primary analysis used a 180-day landmark approach to mitigate immortal time bias. We chose 180 days as the vast majority of irAEs occurred by this time point (online supplemental figure 1). The analysis compared survival across the following groups in separate regressions: (1) no irAE versus any irAE, (2) no irAE versus steroid-requiring and non-steroid-requiring irAEs, and (3) no irAE versus thyroid/cutaneous irAE versus all other irAE types. In sensitivity analyses, we repeated the primary analysis with a landmark time of 90 days. To test the robustness of our results, we used a Cox model with a time-varying irAE covariate, using the same irAE categorizations as above. Patients who developed multiple irAEs were classified based on the first irAE they developed. For multivariate analysis, the models were adjusted for age, gender, Charlson Comorbidity Index, primary tumor site, cancer staging, and treatment approach (monotherapy vs combination chemoimmunotherapy). We repeated the above analyses in subgroups defined by (1) non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) histology and (2) stage IV disease at diagnosis, given the prognostic impact of tumor type and stage on patient survival.

For comparison purposes, we performed a ‘naïve’ analysis that did not account for immortal time bias. This analysis classified patients into exposure groups (eg, irAE vs no irAE) at baseline using irAE events that occurred during follow-up. Because patients with irAEs have (by definition) lived long enough to experience the irAE, this analytic approach produces a strong artificial bias toward improved survival for patients who experience irAEs.17

The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate the median OS and the log-rank test was used to compare unadjusted survival across exposure groups. Cumulative 1-year and 2-year event rates for all irAEs, irAE subtypes, irAEs requiring steroid treatment, irAEs requiring ICI discontinuation, and irAEs resulting in hospitalizations were calculated as the complement of the Kaplan-Meier function. Analysis was performed with R V.4.4.1 (R Core Team, Vienna, Austria).

Funding for database creation has been previously described.15 This study received no direct financial support.

Results

Cohort characteristics

Of the 1710 patients identified, 1707 were included in the final analysis after excluding patients with inadequate follow-up time. Median follow-up time was 41 months (95% CI 37.9 to 45.6). Overall, 465 (27.2%) patients experienced ≥1 irAE (table 1, online supplemental table 1). Compared with patients without irAE, patients with irAE were more frequently aged 70–79 years (46% vs 37%, p=0.009) and white (56% vs 47%, p=0.002). Cancer type distribution was similar between the two groups, with NSCLC as the predominant histological diagnosis (58% vs 55%). Patients with irAE had a lower proportion of patients with stage IV disease at presentation (28% vs 33%, p=0.012). Nearly all (>95%) patients in both groups received PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors. No significant differences were observed in other measured characteristics.

irAE types, incidence, and management

The unadjusted 1-year and 2-year incidence of any-grade irAEs in the full cohort was 30.9% (95% CI 28.3% to 33.4%) and 38.2% (95% CI 35.0% to 41.3%), respectively (online supplemental table 2). Median time to onset of any irAE was 108 days (IQR 48–210) and the majority of events occurred within 6 months of ICI initiation (online supplemental figure 1). Among those who experienced an irAE, the most frequently documented irAEs included hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism (27.5%), dermatitis (21.5%), pneumonitis (21.1%), colitis (14.4%), and hepatitis (13.3%). Multiorgan involvement was observed in 84 patients (18.1%).

The clinical management of these irAEs varied. Overall, among patients with an irAE (n=465), 222 (47.7%) required systemic steroids, 211 (45.4%) permanently discontinued immunotherapy, and 106 (22.8%) required hospitalization due to irAE. The rates of these interventions differed substantially by irAE phenotype (table 2). High rates of steroid use and treatment discontinuation were observed for patients with pneumonitis (79.6% and 77.6%, respectively), colitis (59.7% and 62.7%), and hepatitis (61.3% and 53.2%). In contrast, thyroiditis and dermatitis were associated with much lower rates of both steroid use (14.1% and 35.0%) and treatment discontinuation (15.6% and 27.0%).

Effect of irAEs on overall survival

In the primary analysis using a 180-day landmark, development of any-grade irAE was not significantly associated with OS (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.72 to 1.03, p=0.11 figure 1A, table 2). Similar results were found in the sensitivity analysis using a 90-day landmark (online supplemental figure 2, online supplemental table 3). In the multivariable time-dependent Cox model, there was similarly no significant association between any-grade irAE and OS (aHR 0.87; 95% CI 0.75 to 1.01, p=0.07; table 3).

We next stratified by irAE severity, using systemic steroid requirement as a proxy for more severe irAEs. In the primary 180-day landmark analysis, patients with irAEs that did not require steroids lived longer than those who did not have irAEs at all (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.59 to 0.86 p=0.00; figure 1B, table 3), although this did not reach significance in the 90-day landmark analysis (online supplemental figure 2). In contrast, steroid-requiring irAEs were not associated with OS (HR 1.07, 95% CI 0.83 to 1.37, p=0.60). The time-dependent Cox model similarly showed an association between irAEs that did not require steroids and improved OS (adjusted HR (aHR) 0.69; 95% CI 0.57 to 0.84, p<0.001), but no association for steroid-requiring irAEs (aHR 1.13; 95% CI 0.92 to 1.38, p=0.25; table 3).

Stratifying by irAE type, we observed improved OS for thyroid/cutaneous irAEs in the landmark analysis (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.51 to 0.86, p=0.00) but not for other irAE types (HR 1.11, 95% CI 0.89 to 1.38, p=0.37) (figure 1C). Similar results were observed in the 90-day landmark sensitivity analysis (online supplemental figure 1). The time-dependent Cox model reinforced this finding, showing a significant survival benefit associated with thyroid/cutaneous irAEs (aHR 0.57; 95% CI 0.46 to 0.71, p<0.001), but not for other irAEs (aHR 1.18; 95% CI 0.98 to 1.42, p=0.08) (table 2).

Subgroup analyses

To examine the robustness of our findings, we performed similar analyses in specified subgroups. Among patients with NSCLC, thyroid/cutaneous irAEs were associated with improved OS (aHR 0.69; 95% CI 0.52 to 0.91, p=0.01), while other irAEs were associated with worse OS (aHR 1.30; 95% CI 1.03 to 1.63, p=0.02) (online supplemental figure 3). Among patients with stage IV disease, a similar pattern was observed (thyroid/cutaneous irAEs: aHR 0.66, 95% CI 0.45 to 0.97, p=0.04; non-thyroid/non-cutaneous irAEs: aHR 1.11, 95% CI 0.80 to 1.55, p=0.53) (online supplemental figure 3).

Analysis not adjusted for immortal time bias

Finally, a naïve analysis that did not account for immortal time bias was performed for comparison. This analysis showed a much stronger association between any irAE and improved OS than our primary analyses (HR 0.58; 95% CI 0.50 to 0.66, p<0.0001; figure 2). Contrary to the primary analysis, we observed a survival benefit for irAEs requiring steroids (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.61 to 0.85, p<0.0001) and for irAEs other than thyroid/cutaneous (HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.66 to 0.91, p<0.0001).

Discussion

In this large national cohort of patients with cancer treated with ICI that appropriately accounted for immortal time bias, we demonstrated that the development of an irAE was not, in and of itself, associated with improved OS, contrary to previously published reports. However, a significant survival advantage was observed in patients with less severe irAEs that did not require systemic steroid administration. This benefit was driven exclusively by thyroid-related and skin-related irAEs, suggesting that specific subsets of non-steroid-requiring or low-grade, organ-specific irAEs may serve as a biomarker for an effective antitumor response. In our sensitivity analysis that did not account for immortal time bias, we replicated previously published findings suggesting a large survival benefit from all-cause irAEs. This raises the possibility that much of the hypothesized survival advantage from irAEs likely reflects immortal time bias rather than a biological phenomenon, while the true survival benefit of irAEs is highly type-dependent and grade-dependent.

Our results confirm and extend prior reports that the survival impact of irAEs correlates inversely with clinical severity and depends on the organ system involved.1118,20 In our study, less severe irAEs were associated with improved survival, while more severe irAEs that required systemic steroids conferred no survival benefit and even possible harm. This observation aligns with prior reports of favorable outcomes in patients with lower-grade irAEs.20,23 A large pooled analysis of randomized trials evaluating atezolizumab-containing regimens for stage IV nonsquamous NSCLC provides further support for this observation, demonstrating significantly longer OS in patients with low-grade irAEs with a trend toward worse outcomes in those with high-grade irAEs. However, the lack of an OS benefit with any-grade irAE in our study reflects the clinical heterogeneity in a real-world, pan-tumor setting compared with a homogenous trial population. Although a prior analysis of VA data using the same VHA CDW database suggested superior survival in patients with steroid-requiring irAEs compared with non-steroid-requiring irAEs and patients without irAEs,8 it did not account for the time-dependent nature of irAEs, thus limiting its interpretation. Whether the potential survival detriment observed in patients with more severe irAEs reflects the intrinsic severity of the irAE itself (eg, associated morbidity, immune profile), early discontinuation of immunotherapy, or the immunosuppressive effects of the high-dose steroids on ICI efficacy cannot be fully determined from this analysis but is an important area of future research.

Stratification by irAE subtype confirmed prior observations of superior survival outcomes among patients with cutaneous and thyroid/endocrine irAEs across diverse tumor types, ICI regimens, and patient populations.1118 23,25 These irAEs are usually lower-grade toxicities that often do not require systemic steroid administration or treatment discontinuation, which can potentially explain the associated survival gains. However, the survival benefits persist even after adjusting for steroid use and treatment discontinuation in large-scale analyses.19 The occurrence of cutaneous and endocrine irAEs, therefore, may serve as a surrogate marker for effective immune activation, essential for durable antitumor response. This may be explained by a shared genetic susceptibility for tissue-specific autoimmunity and a robust antitumor immune response. There is evidence to suggest that polygenic risk scores for thyroid and skin autoimmunity can predict corresponding irAE development, as well as improved ICI response and longer OS, implicating shared biological pathways, shaped by host genetics, which mediate both organ-specific autoimmunity and a potent response to immunotherapy.26 27

From a clinical standpoint, the nuanced relationship between patient outcomes and irAE, with regard to its time-varying nature, severity, and subtype, emphasizes the need for exercising caution when interpreting unadjusted observations reported in the literature. While the prognostic significance of irAEs has been broadly explored, analyses that fail to account for immortal time bias have repeatedly shown a robust association between any-grade irAEs and improved survival,6,8 a finding our own data replicated under naïve models. For instance, a recently published report using the same VHA CDW database suggested superior survival in patients with steroid-requiring irAEs.8 However, such analyses are flawed because irAEs are time-dependent events: patients must survive long enough to develop them, leading to misclassification of early deaths as irAE-free. This results in a severe overestimation of the survival benefits of irAEs.6,8 Our study addresses these concerns through rigorous analysis using complementary statistical approaches—specifically landmark analysis and time-dependent Cox modeling—to mitigate this bias. By directly comparing our adjusted models with a naïve unadjusted analysis, we clearly demonstrate that failing to account for immortal time bias leads to erroneous conclusions, including the false attribution of a survival advantage to severe, steroid-requiring irAEs. The survival benefits observed in patients with non-steroid-requiring irAEs, particularly those involving skin and thyroid, suggest that irAEs should not be viewed as a uniform biomarker of efficacy. Clinicians must consider both the irAE severity and phenotype to inform prognostication and toxicity management decisions. Such an understanding will be useful in guiding discussions with patients and setting realistic expectations about toxicity and efficacy. Moreover, the differential impact of organ-specific irAEs highlights the need for further investigation into biomarkers and host immune profiles associated with irAE risk and therapeutic response to improve risk stratification.

Our study has several limitations, although these must be weighed against distinct methodological strengths. Unlike many EMR-based real-world database studies that traditionally rely on administrative ICD codes lacking clinical specificity, a major strength of our analysis is the rigorous manual chart annotation used to ascertain irAEs and steroid utilization across a large patient sample. Furthermore, whereas previous reports have largely been restricted to a single tumor site or immunotherapy agent, our cohort encompasses multiple tumor types and various ICI agents, allowing us to estimate the true underlying prognostic signal of irAEs across the broader clinical spectrum. The rate of irAEs in our cohort was lower than reported frequencies in the clinical trials, which decreases the power to compare individual irAEs and estimate the effects of rare irAEs. Despite adjusting for multiple covariates, we cannot fully mitigate the residual and unmeasured confounding bias given the retrospective nature of our analysis. Although irAEs were extracted by manual chart review, accurate ascertainment of these events is dependent on clinician assessment and documentation and thus may be subject to ascertainment bias. We used systemic steroid administration as a proxy for irAE severity. Although CTCAE grading is more precise, it is often underdocumented, missing, or inconsistently applied by clinicians, while administration of steroids is an objective clinical datapoint and more reliably captured in pharmacy records. Additional information on steroid dose and duration of use could have provided a more granular assessment of the relationship between steroid administration and survival outcomes. Additionally, our cohort is predominantly older male patients with cancer, reflective of the VA demographic, which may limit the generalizability to broader populations.

In conclusion, our study provides a realistic assessment of the relationship between irAE incidence and severity and OS using a large, national real-world cohort of patients with varying tumor types and ICI regimens and employing appropriate statistical methods to address immortal time bias. Our data suggest that skin-related and thyroid-related irAEs may have a prognostic value for patients receiving ICI and add to a growing body of literature characterizing the prognostic value of irAEs.
