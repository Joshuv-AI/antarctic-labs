Toller matrices and the Feynman 𝐢 ​ 𝜺 \mathrm{i}\varepsilon in spinfoams
Eugenio Bianchi, Chaosong Chen, Mauricio Gamonal

Abstract
We study the analytic properties and three equivalent representations of the Toller matrices T ( ± ) T^{(\pm)} which appear in the causal formulation of spinfoam transition amplitudes for 4d Lorentzian quantum gravity. These are polynomially bounded functions on the Lorentz group which satisfy the relation T ( + ) + T ( − ) = D T^{(+)}+T^{(-)}=D , where the Wigner matrix D D provides a unitary irreducible representation of S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) SL(2,\mathbb{C}) . Rühl’s definition of T ( ± ) T^{(\pm)} in terms of analyticity and asymptotic properties is shown to be equivalent to the recently introduced Feynman i ​ ε \mathrm{i}\varepsilon prescription in spinfoams. We show that, equivalently, they can be represented as an integral over eigenvalues of the boost operator, which results in a sum over residues. The latter reproduces the Wick rotation relating Euclidean S ​ p ​ i ​ n ​ ( 4 ) Spin(4) to Lorentzian S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) SL(2,\mathbb{C}) spinfoams studied by Donà, Gozzini and Nicotra. We provide explicit expressions in terms of hypergeometric functions and specialize them to the γ \gamma -simple representations relevant for spinfoams.

II
Wigner and Toller matrices

The spinfoam formulation of
4d
Lorentzian quantum gravity provides a covariant framework for quantum geometry with Lorentz symmetry, described by the group
\mathrm{SO}^{\uparrow}(1,3)
, defining the elementary building blocks. By Wigner’s theorem, continuous symmetries are represented in the quantum theory by projective unitary operators, which can be lifted to ordinary unitary representations of the universal (double) cover
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
. A detailed discussion of the representation theory of this group, which plays a central role in the construction of the EPRL model, can be found in
Martin-Dussaud:2019ypf
.

Here we focus on the unitary, infinite-dimensional irreducible representations of the principal series,
\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g)
, which are labeled by a spin
k\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and a continuous parameter
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
. In the canonical basis of simultaneous eigenstates of
\vec{L}^{2}
and
L_{z}
, denoted by
\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}
, these representations are encoded in the Wigner
D
-matrix, with elements given by

D^{(\rho,k)}_{jp\,ln}(g)=\bra{ (\rho,k);j,p}\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g)\ket{(\rho,k);l,n}\,.

(1)

The Cartan decomposition allows us to express any group element
g\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
as

g=U_{1}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta K_{z}}\,U_{2}\,,

(2)

where
U_{1},U_{2}\in SU(2)
,
\beta\geq 0
is the rapidity of the boost along the axis
z
, and
K_{z}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\sigma_{z}
is the
z
-component of the boost generator, with
\sigma_{z}
a Pauli matrix. Consequently, the Wigner
D
-matrix can be decomposed as

D^{(\rho,k)}_{jp\,ln}(g)=\sum_{m=-\min(j,l)}^{\min(j,l)}D^{(j)}_{pm}(U_{1})\,d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\,D^{(l)}_{mn}(U_{2})\,

(3)

where
D^{(j)}_{mn}(U)
is the Wigner
D
-matrix of
SU(2)
, and
d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\equiv D^{(\rho,k)}_{jmlm}(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta K_{z}})
is the reduced Wigner
d
-matrix, which captures the non-compact part of the representation (see Appendix
A
for additional details).

In Rühl’s monograph
Ruhl:1970lor
, the Wigner
d
-matrices are referred to as “functions of the first kind”, and are denoted by
d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
. These are entire functions in the complex
\rho
-plane, i.e., they have no poles and no branch cuts.
Throughout this paper we adopt Rühl’s phase convention, so
that the analytic structure of
d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
is
carried entirely by the Toller
t
-matrices introduced below.
Other conventions commonly used in the spinfoam literature, such as the
one in
Speziale:2016axj
, differ from Rühl’s by an
overall
\rho
-dependent phase

\displaystyle\Phi(\rho;j,l)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{-\frac{j-l}{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma(j+\mathrm{i}\rho+1)}{|\Gamma(j+\mathrm{i}\rho+1)|}\,\frac{\Gamma(l-\mathrm{i}\rho+1)}{|\Gamma(l-\mathrm{i}\rho+1)|}\,

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{\pi}{2}j}\mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}\Psi_{j}^{\rho}})\;(\mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}\tfrac{\pi}{2}l}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Psi_{l}^{\rho}})

(4)

which has unit modulus on the real axis but, once analytically continued,
introduces additional singularities and branch cuts in the complex

\rho
-plane.

Assuming
\beta>0
, Rühl proves the existence and uniqueness of “functions of the second kind” that satisfy

e^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)=d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)-(-1)^{j-l}\,e^{(-\rho,-k)}_{ljm}(\beta)\,.

(5)

Motivated by the role played by Toller poles, studied by M. Toller in the context of scattering amplitudes
Toller:1968gr
;
Toller:1968pole
;
Sciarrino:1967
as one of their defining features, we will refer to these functions of the second kind as the
reduced Toller
t
-matrices
, which we identify as

\displaystyle t^{(+,\,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)

\displaystyle=e^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\,,

\displaystyle t^{(-,\,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-l}\,e^{(-\rho,-k)}_{ljm}(\beta)\,.

(6)

These objects are uniquely characterized by the following asymptotic and analytic properties
Ruhl:1970lor
:

1.

Asymptotic properties
:

Rapid decay across the upper (lower)
\rho
plane for
t^{(+,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
(
t^{(-,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
):

t^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)=\order{\frac{1}{\abs{\rho}^\alpha}}\,,

(7)

as
\absolutevalue{\rho}\to\infty
, and for all
\alpha
such that

\begin{cases}\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\,&\mathrm{for}\,\Im{\rho}\gtrless 0\\[5.0pt]
0<\alpha<1&\mathrm{for}\,\Im{\rho}=0\end{cases}

(8)

2.

Matching properties
:

Matching with
d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
across the lower (upper)
\rho
plane for
t^{(+,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
(
t^{(-,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
):

t^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)=d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)+\order{\frac{1}{\abs{\rho}^\alpha}}\,,

(9)

as
\absolutevalue{\rho}\to\infty
, and for all
\alpha
such that

\begin{cases}\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\,&\mathrm{for}\,\Im{\rho}\lessgtr 0\\[5.0pt]
0<\alpha<1&\mathrm{for}\,\Im{\rho}=0\end{cases}

(10)

3.

Pole structure
:

The matrices are meromorphic in the
\rho
plane, with a finite number of simple poles (Toller poles) located on the following points:

\mathrm{i}\,\rho=-j\,,(-j+1)\,\,,\ldots\,,(l-1)\,,l\,.

(11)

Consequently, the two reduced Toller
t
-matrices sum up to the reduced Wigner
d
-matrix, as

t^{(+,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)+t^{(-,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)=d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\,.

(12)

In Sec.
III.1
we will show that the Feynman

\mathrm{i}\varepsilon
prescription extracts each Toller branch directly
from
d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
, and that the analytic properties
(
7
)–(
11
)
together with the sum rule (
12
)
determine
t^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
uniquely.

Using the Cartan decomposition (
2
), we can construct analogously the Toller
T
-matrices

T^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jp\,ln}(g)=\sum_{m=-\min(j,l)}^{\min(j,l)}D^{(j)}_{pm}(U_{1})\,t^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\,D^{(l)}_{mn}(U_{2})\,.

(13)

We note that, while the Wigner
D
-matrix has a group theoretical interpretation as a unitary irreducible representation of the Lorentz group, the Toller
T
-matrices can be thought of instead as functions over
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
which are polynomially bounded in
\Tr(g g^\dagger)
, specifically
|t^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)|\sim e^{-(1+|k\pm m|)\beta}
as
\beta\to\infty
, and fully determined by their analytic properties. Crucially, the Toller matrices do not provide a representation of elements of the Lorentz group, i.e., for
g_{1},g_{2}\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, we have

T^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jm\,ln}(g_{1}\,g_{2})\neq\sum_{l^{\prime},n^{\prime}}T^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jm\,l^{\prime}n^{\prime}}(g_{1})\;T^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{l^{\prime}n^{\prime}\,ln}(g_{2})\,.

(14)

Nevertheless, the Toller
T
-matrices satisfy the additive property

T^{(+,\,\rho,k)}_{jm\,ln}(g)\,+\,T^{(-,\,\rho,k)}_{jm\,ln}(g)=D^{(\rho,k)}_{jm\,ln}(g)\,,

(15)

and, as discussed in detail in
Ruhl:1970lor
,
they play a significant role in the harmonic analysis of the Lorentz group, particularly as a basis for the inverse Fourier transform of distributions over
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
.

III
Equivalent representations of the Toller matrices

In this section we present three equivalent representations of
the Toller matrices
t^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
defined in
Sec.
II
. The first
(Sec.
III.1
) is a Feynman
\mathrm{i}\varepsilon

contour integral, which is distinguished in that it extracts each
branch from
d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
directly as an analytic projector, and simultaneously establishes the uniqueness asserted in Sec.
II
. The second (Sec.
III.2
) is a residue
sum over the complex eigenvalues of the boost generator
K_{z}
.
The third (Sec.
III.3
) is obtained by Wick
rotation of the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients of
Spin(4)
, and
yields the closed-form hypergeometric expressions originally found by Rühl
Ruhl:1970lor
. We summarize these representations
in Fig.
1
.

In

\displaystyle P_{jl}(\tilde{\rho};\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(-j-\mathrm{i}\rho)\Gamma(l-\mathrm{i}\tilde{\rho}+1)}{\Gamma(-j-\mathrm{i}\tilde{\rho})\Gamma(l-\mathrm{i}\rho+1)}\,,

which encodes the Toller poles (

acting on

\tilde{\rho}

confined to the Toller poles (

Motivated by the matching property (
9

d
-matrices, namely
I_{\varepsilon}^{(\pm)}[d]=I_{\varepsilon}^{(\pm)}[t^{+}]+I_{\varepsilon}^{(\pm)}[t^{-}]
, where we have omitted other indices and variables for simplicity. We first consider the quantity
I_{\varepsilon}^{(+)}[t^{+}]
. Since the product
P_{jl}(\tilde{\rho};\rho)\,t^{+}(\tilde{\rho})
cancels out exactly all the Toller poles in
\tilde{\rho}
, the only remaining pole of the integrand is at
\tilde{\rho}=\rho+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon
. Using the asymptotic property (
7
), we can close the contour in the upper half-plane, as shown in Fig.
2
. Hence, Cauchy’s residue theorem gives

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{\varepsilon}^{(+)}[t^{+}]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\operatorname*{Res}_{\tilde{\rho}=\rho+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}\left[\frac{P_{jl}(\tilde{\rho};\rho)}{\tilde{\rho}-\rho-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}\,t^{+}(\tilde{\rho})\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}P_{jl}(\rho+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon;\rho)\,t^{+}(\rho+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=t^{+}(\rho)\,.

(18)

Then we consider the action on the other Toller branch,
I_{\varepsilon}^{(+)}[t^{-}]
. Because of (
7
), we close the contour on the lower half-plane. The product
P_{jl}(\tilde{\rho};\rho)\,t^{-}(\tilde{\rho})
cancels all the Toller poles again, but in this case the only remaining pole
\tilde{\rho}=\rho+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon
is not inside the contour and the integral vanishes:
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{\varepsilon}^{(+)}[t^{-}]=0
. Similarly, we find
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{\varepsilon}^{(-)}[t^{-}]=t^{-}
and
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{\varepsilon}^{(-)}[t^{+}]=0
. Consequently, we find that the Toller
t
-matrices can be expressed as a Feynman
\mathrm{i}\varepsilon
prescription applied to the reduced Wigner
d
-matrices, as

t^{\pm}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{\varepsilon}^{(\pm)}[d]\,,

(19)

or more explicitly, we can write:

\boxed{t^{(\pm,\,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\differential{\tilde{\rho}}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\;\frac{\pm 1}{\tilde{\rho}-\rho\mp\mathrm{i}\,\varepsilon}\;\quantity(\prod_{n=0}^{j+l}\frac{\ii\, \tilde{\rho}- \,(n-j)}{\ii\,\rho-(n-j)} )\;d^{(\tilde{\rho},k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\,.}

(20)

This integral representation was first presented in the companion
paper
Bianchi:2026rjd
, where it defines the elementary building block of causal spinfoams; the present derivation is
self-contained and makes the additive property
(
12
) manifest.

Uniqueness.
The same contour argument establishes that the
Toller splitting (
15
) is unique. Let
u_{\pm}(\rho,\beta)
be functions
meromorphic in
\rho
satisfying the asymptotic decay
(
7
), the pole structure
(
11
), and the sum rule

u_{+}+u_{-}=d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
. Applying
I^{(\pm)}_{\varepsilon}
to

gives

. The Feynman
\mathrm{i}\varepsilon

The choice of half-plane in the prescription
(

) is not arbitrary: as we will show in
Sec.

, the reduced Wigner

-matrix admits a
decomposition into two boost-frequency components in

proportional to
\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\beta\rho}
, and the contours in the upper
and lower half-planes project onto these two components. The Toller
matrices
t^{(\pm,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
are therefore the positive-
and negative-frequency parts of
d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)
in the
boost parameter, in direct analogy with the decomposition of a
Feynman propagator in ordinary quantum field theory.

Using (

-matrices follow immediately from the properties of the reduced Wigner

\displaystyle\overline{t^{(\pm,\,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)}\;\,

\displaystyle=\,t^{(\pm,\,-\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)

(21)

\displaystyle\overline{t^{(\pm,\,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)}\;\,

\displaystyle=\,(-1)^{j-l}\,t^{(\mp,\,\rho,k)}_{lj-m}(\beta)

(22)

\displaystyle t^{(\pm,\,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\;\,

\displaystyle=\,(-1)^{j-l}\,t^{(\mp,\,-\rho,k)}_{lj-m}(\beta)

\displaystyle t^{(\pm,\,\rho,-k)}_{jlm}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\,t^{(\pm,\,\rho,k)}_{jl-m}(\beta)

(24)

where the overline indicates complex conjugation. These relations imply that, under the unitary equivalence between the representations
(\rho,k)
and

t^{(\pm,-\rho,-k)}_{jlm}(\beta)=(-1)^{j-l}\,t^{(\mp,\,\rho,k)}_{ljm}(\beta)\,.

III.2
Toller matrices from the boost
K_{z}

Another convenient representation of the reduced Toller
t
-matrices can be found by writing the matrix elements of the reduced Wigner
d
-matrix in a different basis. We consider the basis built from the simultaneous eigenstates of the boost
K_{z}
and the rotation
L_{z}
, denoted by
\ket{\omega,m}
, such that

\displaystyle K_{z}\ket{ \omega\,m}

\displaystyle=\omega\,\ket{\omega\,m}\,,

(26a)

\displaystyle L_{z}\ket{\omega\,m}

\displaystyle=m\,\ket{\omega\,m}\,,

(26b)

where
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. The complete set of states
\ket{\omega\, m}
is orthonormal,
\bra{\omega\,m}\ket{\omega'\, m'}=\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\;\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})
, and satisfy the resolution of the identity,

\mathbbm{1}=\sum_{m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential{\omega}\;\ket{\omega\, m}\bra{\omega\,m}\,.

(27)

The bases
\ket{\omega\,m}
and
\ket{j\,m}
are related by the
overlap coefficients

\bra{\omega\, m}\ket{j\,m}
, which were computed by Huszár in
Huszar:1971nn
(See also
Rashid:1979xv
), and are reported explicitly in (
80
). By inserting the resolution of the identity (
27
) into the definition of the reduced Wigner
d
-matrix, we can write

\displaystyle d_{jlm}^{(\rho,k)}(\beta)

\displaystyle=D^{(\rho,k)}_{jmlm}(\mathrm{e}^{\tfrac{\beta\sigma_{z}}{2}})

\displaystyle=\bra{j\,m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta\hat{K}_{z}}\ket{l\,m}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential{\omega}\bra{j\,m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta K_{z}}\ket{ \omega\, m }\bra{\omega\, m}\ket{l\,m}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential{\omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta\omega}\overline{\bra{ \omega\, m }\ket{j\, m}}\bra{ \omega\, m}\ket{l\, m}\,.

(28)

The overlap coefficients
\bra{\omega\, m }\ket{j\,m}
have four families of simple poles in the complex
\omega
plane. Since the asymptotic behavior of the integrand is
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta\omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\absolutevalue{\omega}}
for
\absolutevalue{\omega}\to\infty
, we can close the integration contour in the lower-half complex
\omega
plane for
\beta>0
. Now, if we deform the integration region of (
28
) into two contours
\mathcal{C}_{+}
and
\mathcal{C}_{-}
, as shown in Fig.
3
, each of these contours will enclose a corresponding family of poles
\omega_{n}^{+}
and
\omega_{n}^{-}
:

\omega_{n}^{\pm}=\mp\rho\,-\,\mathrm{i}\,\big(2n+\absolutevalue{k\pm m}+1\big)\,,

(29)

with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. These poles are found by analysing the explicit expressions for the overlap coefficients, as discussed in Appendix
B
. Using Cauchy’s residue theorem, the integral can then be expressed as a sum over the residues of the relevant poles, and we find that the reduced Toller
t
-matrices can be expressed as a contour integral, or more explicitly, as the infinite sum over the associated residues that lie within each contour:

\boxed{t^{(\pm,\,\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\pm}}\differential{\omega}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta\omega}\overline{\bra{ \omega\, m }\ket{j\, m}}\bra{ \omega\, m}\ket{l\, m}=-2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta\omega_{n}^{\pm}}\,\operatorname*{Res}_{\omega=\omega_{n}^{\pm}}\quantity[ \overline{\bra{\omega\, m} \ket{j\,m} } \bra{\omega\, m} \ket{l\,m}]\,.}

(30)

Figure 3:
Analytic structure of the integrand in (
28
), in the complex

-plane, where the four families of poles are indicated with crosses and dots. For

, we close the lower-half-plane, where only the poles in (

One can check by direct computation that the information regarding the location of the Toller poles (
11
) is captured in the residues of the overlap coefficients, while the asymptotic property (
7

\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta\omega_{n}^{\pm}}
. Since the additive property (

9

K_{z}
representation of the Toller

III.3
Toller matrices from Wick rotation

As we have described above, both the Feynman

) and the boost representation (
30
) provide explicit expressions for the reduced Toller
t
-matrices which use the analytic properties in the complex
\rho
and
\omega
plane, respectively. This fact suggests that the properties of the Toller matrices should become manifest within a fully complexified structure of the Lorentz group. Indeed, if one introduces the self-dual and anti-self-dual combinations of the Lorentz generators,
\vec{A}^{\pm}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\vec{L}\pm\mathrm{i}\vec{K})
, one finds a decomposition that splits the complexified Lorentz algebra into two commuting
\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{C}}(2)
algebras, i.e.,
\mathfrak{so}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,3)\cong\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{C}}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{C}}(2)
. In this decomposition, the unitary irreducible representations
(\rho,k)
correspond to pairs of complex spins
(\ell_{+},\ell_{-})
, defined as

\ell_{+}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(k+\mathrm{i}\rho-1)\,,\qquad\ell_{-}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(k-\mathrm{i}\rho-1)\,.

(31)

With these labels, the new basis
\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega, m}
can be expressed in terms of the simultaneous eigenstates of
A_{z}^{\pm}
,

\displaystyle A_{z}^{\pm}\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega, m}

\displaystyle=m_{\pm}\;\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega, m}\,,

(32)

where
m_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(m\pm\mathrm{i}\,\omega)
, with
m
and
\omega
being the eigenvalues of
L_{z}
and
K_{z}
. In this way, we can define the overlap coefficients as
\bra{\omega\, m}\ket{j\,m}\equiv\bra{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega, m}\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}
.

With this rich analytic structure in place, we can now explore the relation with the Euclidean theory described in terms of representations of the group
SO(4)
and the Wick rotation studied by Donà, Gozzini and Nicotra in
Dona:2021ldn
. We note that the complexified Lorentz algebra admits a compact real form corresponding to the simply-connected cover of the Euclidean rotation group,
Spin(4)\cong SU(2)\times SU(2)
. Denoting the generators of rotations
\vec{L}
and Euclidean boosts
\vec{M}
, we can introduce the
self-dual and anti-self-dual combinations
\vec{J}_{L,R}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{L}\pm\vec{M})
, which generate two commuting
\mathfrak{su}(2)
subalgebras and realize the canonical decomposition

\mathfrak{so}(4)\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)_{L}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R}
. In this decomposition, the
unitary irreducible representations of
Spin(4)
are labeled by a
pair of spins
(j_{L},j_{R})\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}_{0}
, that we can parametrize as

j_{L}=\frac{1}{2}(p+q-1),\qquad j_{R}=\frac{1}{2}(p-q-1)\,,

(33)

with
p,q\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, which are the Euclidean counterparts of the labels
\rho
and
k
of the Lorentzian case. These labels are used to construct a new basis of simultaneous eigenstates of the
z
-component of
\vec{J}_{L,R}
, in analogy to (
32
),

\displaystyle J_{zL}\,|(j_{L},j_{R});\omega^{(E)}\,m\rangle

\displaystyle=m_{L}\,|(j_{L},j_{R});\omega^{(E)}\,m\rangle\,,

(34)

\displaystyle J_{zR}\,|(j_{L},j_{R});\omega^{(E)}\,m\rangle

\displaystyle=m_{R}\,|(j_{L},j_{R});\omega^{(E)}\,m\rangle\,,

with
m
and
\omega^{(E)}
being the eigenvalues of
L_{z}
and
M_{z}
, respectively, such that
m_{L}=\tfrac{1}{2}(m+\omega^{(E)})
and
m_{R}=\tfrac{1}{2}(m-\omega^{(E)})
. The Cartan decomposition, for
h\in Spin(4)
, reads

h=u_{1}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\,t\,M_{z}}\,u_{2}
, where
t\in[0,2\pi)
, and
u_{1},\,u_{2}\in\mathrm{diag}\,SU(2)
. Consequently, the reduced Wigner
d
-matrix for
Spin(4)
, also known as the Biedenharn-Dolginov function, can be explicitly written by inserting a resolution of the identity in the

\{\ket{j_L,m_L}\otimes\ket{j_R,m_R}\}
basis
Biedenharn:1961drs
;
Barut:1976ub
;
Lorente:2003wn
,

\displaystyle d_{jlm}^{(p,q)}(t)=D^{(p,q)}_{jmlm}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\,t\,M_{z}})

(35)

\displaystyle\quad=\bra{(p,q);j,m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\,tM_{z}}\ket{(p,q);l,m}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{\omega^{(E)}=-j_{L}-j_{R}}^{j_{L}+j_{R}}\!\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\,t\,\omega^{(E)}}\;\overline{\langle\omega^{(E)}\,m|j\,m\rangle}\langle\omega^{(E)}\,m|l\,m\rangle\,,

where the Euclidean overlap coefficients can be written in terms of the usual
SU(2)
Clebsch-Gordan coefficients,

\displaystyle\langle\omega^{(E)}\,m|j\,m\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\langle(j_{L},j_{R});\omega^{(E)},m|(p,q);j,m\rangle

(36)

\displaystyle=C_{j_{L}m_{L}\,j_{R}m_{R}}^{jm}\,,

with
m=m_{L}+m_{R}
. The striking similarity between (
35
) and (
28
) suggests an underlying relation between the Euclidean and Lorentzian theories for a specific analytic continuation of the labels, i.e., a
Wick rotation
. The role of a Wick rotation in spinfoams was identified recently by Donà, Gozzini and Nicotra in
Dona:2021ldn
. Here we uncover its relation to the unique decomposition (
15
) in Toller matrices.

To see this, first note that the sum in (
35
) is over
\omega^{(E)}=m_{L}-m_{R}=2m_{L}-m
. Writing
m_{L}=n-j_{L}
, with
n=0,\ldots,2j_{L}
, or equivalently, the same finite sum can be parametrized by
m_{R}
. Writing
m_{R}=j_{R}-r
and
m_{L}=m-j_{R}+r
, with
r=0,\ldots,2j_{R}
, gives the identity

d^{(p,q)}_{jlm}(t)\;=\;d^{(p,q)}_{L\,jlm}(t)\;=\;d^{(p,q)}_{R\,jlm}(t)\,,

(37)

with

\displaystyle d^{(p,q)}_{L\,jlm}(t)\;

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{n=0}^{2j_{L}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\,(2n-2j_{L}-m)}\;C^{jm}_{j_{L}(n-j_{L})\,j_{R}(m-n+j_{L})}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times C^{lm}_{j_{L}(n-j_{L})\,j_{R}(m-n+j_{L})}\,,

(38)

\displaystyle d^{(p,q)}_{R\,jlm}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{2j_{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\,(m-2j_{R}+2r)}\;C^{jm}_{j_{L}(m-j_{R}+r)\,j_{R}(j_{R}-r)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times C^{lm}_{j_{L}(m-j_{R}+r)\,j_{R}(j_{R}-r)}\,.

(39)

IV
Application in Spinfoams

The building blocks of the EPRL spinfoam vertex

,

\displaystyle t^{(+,\,\rho,0)}_{000}(\beta)

\left|\bra{\omega\,\mu}\ket{0\,0}\right|^{2}=\delta_{\mu,0}\,\frac{\sinh(\pi\rho)}{2\rho\big(\cosh(\pi\rho)+\cosh(\pi\omega)\big)}\,.

The poles (
29
) relevant to the two Feynman branches here are

\omega_{n}^{\pm}=-\mathrm{i}(1+2n)\mp\rho,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

(51)

Therefore,

\displaystyle t^{(\pm,\rho,0)}_{000}(\beta)

\displaystyle=-2\pi\mathrm{i}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname*{Res}_{\omega=\omega_{n}^{\pm}}\left[\frac{\sinh(\pi\rho)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta\omega}}{2\rho\big(\cosh(\pi\rho)+\cosh(\pi\omega)\big)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\pm\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\rho}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-(1+2n\mp\mathrm{i}\rho)\beta}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\rho}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(1\mp\mathrm{i}\rho)\beta}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta}}\;=\;\pm\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\rho}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\rho\beta}}{\sinh\beta}\,.

(52)

Finally, the same result follows from the Wick-rotation representation
(
40
). For

k=j=l=m=0
, we have

\ell_{+}=\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho-1}{2},\qquad\ell_{-}=\frac{-\mathrm{i}\rho-1}{2},\qquad n_{+}=n_{-}=0.

(53)

Using (
41
), the analytically
continued Clebsch–Gordan products reduce to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}^{(+,\rho,0)}_{000,s}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{C}\!\left(\ell_{+},\,s-\ell_{+};\,\ell_{+},\,-s+\ell_{+};\,0,0\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\rho},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}^{(-,\rho,0)}_{000,s}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{C}\!\left(\ell_{-},\,s-\ell_{-};\,\ell_{-},\,-s+\ell_{-};\,0,0\right)^{2}=-\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\rho}.

(54)

Therefore,

\displaystyle t^{(\pm,\rho,0)}_{000}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta(1+2s\mp\mathrm{i}\rho)}\left(\pm\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\rho}\right)

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\rho}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(1\mp\mathrm{i}\rho)\beta}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta}}\;=\;\pm\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\rho}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\rho\beta}}{\sinh\beta}\,.

(55)

These expressions show concretely the equivalence of the representations in a simple example. In Table
2
we report other examples relevant for the EPRL spinfoam amplitudes
Engle:2007wy
and
Bianchi:2026rjd
, which can be derived in a similar way.

Appendix A
Unitary irreducible representations of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})

The principal series of unitary irreducible representations of

SL(2,\mathbb{C})
is infinite-dimensional and is generated by the six
self-adjoint Lorentz generators
J^{IJ}=-J^{JI}
, with

I,J=0,1,2,3
. Introducing a unit timelike vector
t^{I}
, we decompose
the generators into rotations that leave
t^{I}
invariant,

L^{I}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{I}{}_{JKL}J^{JK}t^{L},\qquad K^{I}=J^{IJ}t_{J}.

(56)

In coordinates where
t^{I}=(1,0,0,0)
, we write

L^{I}=(0,L^{i})
and
K^{I}=(0,K^{i})
, with
i=1,2,3
. The generators obey

\displaystyle[L^{i},L^{j}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\,\epsilon^{ij}{}_{k}\,L^{k},

\displaystyle[K^{i},K^{j}]

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\,\epsilon^{ij}{}_{k}\,L^{k},

\displaystyle[L^{i},K^{j}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\,\epsilon^{ij}{}_{k}\,K^{k}.

(57)

In the defining
2\times 2
matrix realization of the Lorentz algebra,
one may take

L^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{i},\qquad K^{i}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\sigma^{i},

(58)

which satisfies the same commutation relations. This finite-dimensional
matrix realization should be distinguished from the Hermitian generators
acting on the unitary principal-series Hilbert space. The principal series of the unitary irreducible representations is labeled by two parameters,
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, and is infinite-dimensional. Its associated Hilbert space admits the canonical
SU(2)
-decomposition,

\mathcal{H}^{(\rho,k)}=\bigoplus_{j=\absolutevalue{k}}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{j}\,,

(59)

where
\mathcal{H}_{j}
is the Hilbert space associated to the spin-
j
representation of
SU(2)
with dimension
d_{j}=2j+1
. In the
(\rho,k)
representation, the invariant Casimir operators have eigenvalues

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}J^{IJ}J_{IJ}=\vec{K}^{2}-\vec{L}^{2}=\rho^{2}-k^{2}+1\,,

(60a)

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\epsilon_{IJKL}J^{IJ}J^{KL}=\vec{K}\cdot\vec{L}=\rho\,k\,.

(60b)

A complete basis of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{(\rho,k)}
is given by the orthonormal basis of simultaneous eigenstates of
\vec{L}^{2}=\delta_{ij}L^{i}L^{j}
and
L_{z}=L^{I}z_{I}
, with
z_{I}=(0,0,0,1)
, such that

\displaystyle C_{1}\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}

\displaystyle=(\rho^{2}-k^{2}+1)\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}\,,

(61a)

\displaystyle C_{2}\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}

\displaystyle=\rho\,k\;\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}\,,

(61b)

\displaystyle\vec{L}^{2}\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}

\displaystyle=j(j+1)\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}\,,

(61c)

\displaystyle L_{z}\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}

\displaystyle=m\;\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}\,,

(61d)

with
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
,
k\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
,
\absolutevalue{k}\leq j
, and
\absolutevalue{m}\leq j
. In the principal series, the unitary representation is a map
\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g):SL(2,\mathbb{C})\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}^{(\rho,k)})
, where
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}^{(\rho,k)})
is the group of all unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}^{(\rho,k)}
, such that
\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g^{-1})=\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g)^{\dagger}
, and

\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g_{1}g_{2})=\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g_{1})\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g_{2})\,,

(62)

for all
g,g_{1},g_{2}\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
, and
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}^{(\rho,k)}
, with
\norm{\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g) \ket{\psi}}=\norm{\ket{\psi}}
. The action of the group element
g
on a state
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}^{(\rho,k)}
is given by
\ket{\psi_g}=\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g)\ket{\psi}
. In the canonical basis (
61
), the unitary representations are encoded in the Wigner
D
-matrix, with elements given by

D^{(\rho,k)}_{jm\,ln}(g)=\bra{ (\rho,k);j,m}\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g)\ket{(\rho,k);l,n}\,,

(63)

and satisfying the following properties

\displaystyle D^{(\rho,k)}_{jm\,ln}(g_{1}g_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l^{\prime},n^{\prime}}D^{(\rho,k)}_{jm\,l^{\prime}n^{\prime}}(g_{1})D^{(\rho,k)}_{l^{\prime}n^{\prime}\,ln}(g_{2})\,,

(64)

\displaystyle D^{(\rho,k)}_{jm\,ln}(g^{-1})

\displaystyle=\overline{D^{(\rho,k)}_{ln\,jm}(g)}\,,

(65)

\displaystyle\sum_{j,m}\overline{D^{(\rho,k)}_{jm\,l^{\prime}n^{\prime}}(g)}D^{(\rho,k)}_{jm\,ln}(g)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\,,

(66)

for all
g,g_{1},g_{2}\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
. It can be checked that the
representations
(\rho,k)
and
(-\rho,-k)
are unitarily equivalent. In
the following we will only need the corresponding identity for the
matrix elements of a pure boost along the
z
-axis.

In the Cartan decomposition, any group element
g\in SL(2,\mathbb{C})
can be
expressed as

g=U_{1}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta K_{z}}\,U_{2}\,,

(67)

where
U_{1},U_{2}\in SU(2)
,
\beta\geq 0
is the rapidity of the boost
along the
z
-axis, and
K_{z}=\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\sigma_{z}
is the

z
-component of the boost generator. For the pure boost

g_{\beta}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta K_{z}}
the matrix element is diagonal in the
magnetic index,

D^{(\rho,k)}_{jm\,ln}(g_{\beta})=0\qquad\text{unless }m=n.

(68)

With Rühl’s phase convention, the equivalence between

(\rho,k)
and
(-\rho,-k)
gives

D^{(-\rho,-k)}_{jm\,lm}(g_{\beta})=(-1)^{j-l}\,D^{(\rho,k)}_{lm\,jm}(g_{\beta}),\qquad m=-\min(j,l),\ldots,\min(j,l).

(69)

Inserting (
67
) into
\mathcal{D}^{(\rho,k)}(g)
yields the
Cartan decomposition of the Wigner
D
-matrix,

D^{(\rho,k)}_{jp\,ln}(g)=\sum_{m=-\min(j,l)}^{\min(j,l)}D^{(j)}_{pm}(U_{1})\,d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\,D^{(l)}_{mn}(U_{2}),

(70)

where
D^{(j)}_{mn}(U)
is the Wigner
D
-matrix of
SU(2)
, and

d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\equiv D^{(\rho,k)}_{jmlm}(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta K_{z}})

is the reduced Wigner-
d
function, which encodes the non-compact part of the
representation. An explicit expression as a sum of hypergeometric
functions is available. Following Rühl’s phase
conventions
Ruhl:1970lor
, we have

\displaystyle d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(1+2j)(1+2l)}\,\sqrt{\frac{(j-k)!\,(j+k)!\,(-k+l)!\,(k+l)!}{(j-m)!\,(l-m)!\,(j+m)!\,(l+m)!}}\;\frac{1}{(1+j+l)!}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}(-1)^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\binom{j-m}{k-m+n_{1}}\binom{l-m}{k-m+n_{2}}\binom{j+m}{n_{1}}\binom{l+m}{n_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times(j-k+l+m-n_{1}-n_{2})!\,(k-m+n_{1}+n_{2})!\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\beta(-1-k+m-2n_{1}-\mathrm{i}\rho)}

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(1+k-m+n_{1}+n_{2},\;1+j+\mathrm{i}\rho;\;2+j+l;\;1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta}\Big)\,,

(71)

where the sums over
n_{1},n_{2}
run over
n_{1}=\max(0,m-k),\ldots,\min(j+m,j-k)
and
n_{2}=\max(0,m-k),\ldots,\min(l+m,l-k)
. With this phase convention, the Wigner
d
-matrices are entire in the complex
\rho
-plane. Note that other phase conventions are typically used in the literature. As we adopted Rühl’s phase convention to ensure the analyticity of
the Wigner
d
-matrix in the complex
\rho
-plane, our expressions
differ from the ones commonly used in the spinfoam literature, e.g.,

Speziale:2016axj
, by the overall phase

\Phi(\rho;j,l)
defined in (
4
), which has been indicated explicitly within the paper.

With our choice of the overall phase, the reduced Wigner
d
-matrices satisfy the following properties
Martin-Dussaud:2019ypf
:

\displaystyle\overline{d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)}

\displaystyle=d^{(-\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)

(72a)

\displaystyle d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-l}\,d^{(-\rho,k)}_{lj-m}(\beta)

(72b)

\displaystyle d^{(\rho,-k)}_{jlm}(\beta)

\displaystyle=d^{(\rho,k)}_{jl-m}(\beta)

(72c)

which automatically implies the unitary equivalence between the representations
(\rho,k)
and
(-\rho,-k)
,

(-1)^{j-l}\,d^{(-\rho,-k)}_{ljm}(\beta)=d^{(\rho,k)}_{jlm}(\beta)\,.

(73)

Appendix B
Self-dual representation and eigenvalues of boosts
K_{z}

Unitary irreducible representations of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
can be conveniently analyzed by introducing the self-dual and anti-self-dual combinations of the Lorentz generators, defined as
\vec{A}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\vec{L}\pm\mathrm{i}\vec{K})
. These generators provide a decomposition that splits the complexified Lorentz algebra into two commuting
\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{C}}(2)
algebras, i.e.,
\mathfrak{so}_{\mathbb{C}}(1,3)\cong\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{C}}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{C}}(2)
. The self-dual and anti-self-dual generators obey the commutation relations

[A_{i}^{(\pm)},A_{j}^{(\pm)}]=\mathrm{i}\,\epsilon_{ij}^{~k}\,A_{k}^{(\pm)}\,,\qquad\quad[A_{i}^{(+)},A_{j}^{(-)}]=0\,.

(74)

In this decomposition, the unitary irreducible representations
(\rho,k)
correspond to pairs of complex spins
(\ell_{+},\ell_{-})
, defined as

\ell_{+}=\frac{1}{2}(k+\mathrm{i}\rho-1)\,,\qquad\quad\ell_{-}=\frac{1}{2}(k-\mathrm{i}\rho-1)\,.

(75)

Consequently, the Casimir operators are expressed as

\displaystyle C_{+}

\displaystyle=(\vec{A}^{+}{})^{2}=\ell_{+}(\ell_{+}+1)

(76a)

\displaystyle C_{-}

\displaystyle=(\vec{A}^{-}{})^{2}=\ell_{-}(\ell_{-}+1).

(76b)

The basis states
\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}
can be expressed in terms of the simultaneous eigenstates of
C_{\pm}
,
L_{z}
and
K_{z}
,

\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega, m}\equiv\ket{\ell_+,m_{+}}\otimes\ket{\ell_{-},m_{-}}\,,

(77)

such that

\displaystyle C_{\pm}\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega,m}

\displaystyle=\ell_{\pm}(\ell_{\pm}+1)\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega, m}\,,

(78a)

\displaystyle A_{z}^{\pm}\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega, m}

\displaystyle=m_{\pm}\;\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega, m}\,,

(78b)

where
m_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(m\pm\mathrm{i}\omega)
, with
m
and
\omega
being the eigenvalues of
L_{z}
and
K_{z}
, respectively,

\displaystyle L_{z}\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega,m}

\displaystyle=m\,\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega,m}\,

(79a)

\displaystyle K_{z}\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega,m}

\displaystyle=\omega\,\ket{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega,m}\,.

(79b)

The quantum number
m
takes half-integer values, while the eigenvalues of boosts
\omega
take real values. From now on we will omit the representation labels
(\rho,k)
and
(\ell_{+},\ell_{-})
in the kets
\ket{j\, m}
and
\ket{\omega\, m}
, respectively, to simplify the notation. The two bases are related by the
overlap coefficients
, which were introduced by Huszár
Huszar:1971nn

(see also
Rashid:1979xv
), and take the following form

\displaystyle\bra{\omega\, m}\ket{j\,m}\equiv\bra{(\ell_+,\ell_-); \omega, m}\ket{(\rho,k);j,m}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\pi j}\,\mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}\Psi_{j}^{\rho}}\sqrt{\frac{2j+1}{4\pi}}\;\mathfrak{U}_{jm}^{(\rho,k)}(\omega)\times\begin{cases}1&\text{if }k\leq m\\[5.0pt]
\frac{\sin[\pi(\ell_{+}-m_{+})]}{\sin[\pi(\ell_{-}-m_{-})]}&\text{if }k>m\end{cases}

(80)

where the function
\mathfrak{U}_{jm}^{(\rho,k)}(\omega)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}_{jm}^{(\rho,k)}(\omega)

Appendix C
Wick rotation and Clebsch-Gordan coefficients

The self-dual decomposition of the complexified Lorentz algebra presented in (
74
) has a compact real form, defined by the real algebra
\mathfrak{so}(4)\cong\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{R}}(2)\bigoplus\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{R}}(2)
, which generates the group
Spin(4)\cong SU(2)\times SU(2)
. The algebra is generated by
\vec{J}_{L}
and
\vec{J}_{R}
, such that

\displaystyle[J_{L}^{i},J_{L}^{j}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\,\epsilon^{ij}_{~k}J^{k}_{L}\,,

\displaystyle[J_{R}^{i},J_{R}^{j}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\,\epsilon^{ij}_{~k}J^{k}_{R}\,,

\displaystyle[J_{L}^{i},J_{R}^{j}]

\displaystyle=0\,.

(87)

In this decomposition, the finite dimensional unitary irreducible representations are labeled by the pair of half-integers
(j_{L},j_{R})
, such that the two corresponding Casimir operators are

\displaystyle C_{L}

\displaystyle=\vec{J}_{L}^{~2}=j_{L}(j_{L}+1)\,,

(88)

\displaystyle C_{R}

\displaystyle=\vec{J}_{R}^{~2}=j_{R}(j_{R}+1)\,.

(89)

Consequently, the associated Hilbert space admits the decomposition

\mathcal{H}^{(j_{L},j_{R})}=\mathcal{H}_{j_{L}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{j_{R}}\,,

(90)

with
\dim(\mathcal{H}^{(j_{L},j_{R})})=(2j_{L}+1)(2j_{R}+1)
. A decomposition which is directly analogous to the Lorentzian case is also available, as we can write
\vec{J}_{L}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{L}+\vec{M})
and
\vec{J}_{R}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{L}-\vec{M})
, where
\vec{L}
is the generator of rotations that leave a fixed unit vector
t^{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
invariant, and
\vec{M}
is the generator of rotations in the planes containing
t^{I}
, i.e., Euclidean boosts. With this identification, we can determine a basis in terms of the simultaneous eigenstates of
C_{L,R}
,
(J_{L,R})_{z}
,
L_{z}
and
M_{z}

\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}\equiv\ket{j_L,m_L}\otimes\ket{j_R,m_R}\,,

(91)

such that

\displaystyle C_{L}\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}

\displaystyle=j_{L}(j_{L}+1)\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}\,,

(92a)

\displaystyle C_{R}\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}

\displaystyle=j_{R}(j_{R}+1)\;\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}\,,

(92b)

\displaystyle(J_{L})_{z}\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}

\displaystyle=m_{L}\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}\,,

(92c)

\displaystyle(J_{R})_{z}\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}

\displaystyle=m_{R}\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}\,,

(92d)

where
m
are the eigenvalues of
L_{z}
, and
\omega^{(E)}
are the eigenvalues of the Euclidean boost along the
z
-axis,
M_{z}

\displaystyle L_{z}\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}

\displaystyle=m\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}\,,

(93)

\displaystyle M_{z}\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}

\displaystyle=\omega^{(E)}\ket{(j_L,j_R);\omega^{(E)},m}\,,

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\,\epsilon^{ij}_{~k}L^{k}\,,

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\,\epsilon^{ij}_{~k}M^{k}\,,
