Towards Hodge-Riemann relations for non-Archimedean analogs of valuations on convex sets.
Semyon Alesker

Abstract
In [ 8 ] , a non-Archimedean analogue of the space of translation-invariant even valuations on convex sets was introduced. In [ 7 ] , motivated by a further analogy with the classical theory, this space was equipped with two multiplicative structures, the product and the convolution. Both structures satisfy Poincaré duality and the (non-mixed) hard Lefschetz theorem. In this paper, we formulate a conjecture concerning a more general mixed versions of the hard Lefschetz theorem and the Hodge–Riemann relations. We prove the non-mixed Hodge–Riemann relations in degree 1 1 for the product and, equivalently, in codegree 1 1 for the convolution.

Introduction

1

A Kähler package is an algebra over the real or complex numbers with a finite (bi)grading that satisfies Poincaré duality, the hard Lefschetz theorem, and the Hodge–Riemann relations.

We state below a general definition of a Kähler package in the commutative graded setting. Although this definition does not encompass many examples arising in algebraic geometry, it is sufficient for our purposes.

Kähler packages are typically rare, and proving their existence is often highly non-trivial.

Originally, examples of Kähler packages were provided by the cohomology algebras of compact Kähler (for example, complex projective) manifolds; see, for example,
[
17
]
.

Later, examples of a different nature were discovered in combinatorics and convex geometry, in particular in the theory of valuations on convex sets.

Relevant work in combinatorics includes
[
28
,
25
,
26
,
21
,
1
,
19
]
.
In some of these works, long-standing conjectures in combinatorics were solved by constructing a Kähler package or some of its constituent structures.

The Kähler packages constructed in the theory of valuations on convex sets strongly influenced the present work. Several constructions used in this paper have direct analogues in that theory.
However, from a technical point of view, the present paper is independent of that theory, and no prior knowledge of it is required. Relevant analogies from the theory of valuations on convex sets will be mentioned below as needed.

The new results of this work are Conjecture
1.4
, which formulates the existence of the Kähler package in a new setting, and Theorem
1.5
, which establishes the Hodge–Riemann relations in a special case of this conjecture.
Below, we review the relevant background.

2

Let

A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus A_{n}

be a commutative graded algebra over
\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle A_{i}\cdot A_{j}\subset A_{i+j}.

(1.1)

Assume that
A_{0}=\mathbb{R}
and
\dim A_{n}=1
. We also assume that
A_{n}
is oriented, so that the notion of a positive element of
A_{n}
is well defined.

3

One says that
A
satisfies Poincaré duality if the bilinear pairing

A_{i}\times A_{n-i}\longrightarrow A_{n}

given by multiplication is perfect. That is, for every nonzero element
x\in A_{i}
, there exists
y\in A_{n-i}
such that
x\cdot y\neq 0
.

1.1 Remark
.

If
A
is finite dimensional, Poincaré duality implies that

\dim A_{i}=\dim A_{n-i}.

4

One says that
A
satisfies the mixed hard Lefschetz property if there exists a subset
{\cal K}\subset A_{1}
such that, for every
i<n/2
and every choice of

\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n-2i}\in{\cal K}
, the map
A_{i}\longrightarrow A_{n-i}
given by

x\mapsto x\cdot\omega_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot\omega_{n-2i}

is an isomorphism.

If this property holds only in the special case
\omega_{1}=\dots=\omega_{n-2i}
, then one says that
A
satisfies the non-mixed hard Lefschetz property.

1.2 Remark
.

If
A
is finite-dimensional, then the hard Lefschetz property, whether mixed or non-mixed, implies that

1=\dim A_{0}\leq\dim A_{1}\leq\dots\leq\dim A_{[n/2]}.

Applications of the hard Lefschetz property to combinatorics often proceed along these lines; see, for example,
[
28
]
.

5

One says that
A
satisfies the mixed Hodge–Riemann relations if the following condition holds. Let
i\leq n/2
, and let
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n-2i+1}\in{\cal K}
. Define the subspace
P_{i}\subset A_{i}
of primitive elements by

\displaystyle P_{i}=\{x\in A_{i}|\,x\cdot\omega_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot\omega_{n-2i}\omega_{n-2i+1}=0\}.

(1.2)

Consider the quadratic form on
A_{i}
with values in
A_{n}
:

\displaystyle Q(x)=(-1)^{i}x^{2}\cdot\omega_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot\omega_{n-2i}.

(1.3)

One says that
A
satisfies the mixed Hodge–Riemann relations if the restriction of
Q
to
P_{i}
is positive definite; that is,

\displaystyle Q(x)>0\mbox{ for any }x\in P_{i}\backslash\{0\}.

(1.4)

One says that
A
satisfies the non-mixed Hodge–Riemann relations if the above property holds in the special case

\omega_{1}=\dots=\omega_{n-2i}=\omega_{n-2i+1}.

In this paper, we will work with algebras over
\mathbb{C}
obtained by complexifying a real graded algebra. In this setting, the definition (
1.3
) of the quadratic form
Q
should be replaced by

Q(x)=(-1)^{i}x\cdot\bar{x}\cdot\omega_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot\omega_{n-2i},

where
\bar{x}
denotes the complex conjugate of
x
. This defines a Hermitian form.

1.3 Remark
.

1.

The well-known Khovanskii–Teissier argument derives the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality for mixed volumes of convex bodies from a special case of the non-mixed Hodge–Riemann relations for toric varieties; see, for example,
[
13
]
, §27.

2.

The mixed Hodge–Riemann relations for valuations on convex sets also imply the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality; see
[
22
]
, Corollary 8.1. They further imply new inequalities for mixed volumes of convex bodies; see
[
6
,
24
]
.

3.

For applications of the non-mixed Hodge–Riemann relations to the combinatorics of matroids, see
[
1
]
.

The product and the convolution are related by a Fourier-type transform. More precisely, the author constructed in

For valuations on convex sets, the Fourier transform was constructed by the author in

For any lattice

(Definition

The author proved in

.

We now state the new results of this paper. We begin with a conjecture formulated for the product; the corresponding formulation for the convolution is immediate.

1.4 Conjecture
.

1.

Val^{\infty}(V)
satisfies the mixed hard Lefschetz theorem for the subset
{\cal K}
as above.

2.

Val^{\infty}(V)
satisfies the mixed Hodge-Riemann relations for the subset
{\cal K}
as above.

The non-mixed case of part
1
of the conjecture was proved by the author in
[
7
]
.
The main new result of this paper is a proof of the second part of this conjecture in the special case of the non-mixed Hodge–Riemann relations for
i=1
.

1.5 Theorem
.

For any lattice
\Lambda\subset V
, the quadratic form

Q(\phi)=-\phi\cdot\bar{\phi}\cdot(V_{\Lambda,1})^{n-2}

is positive definite on the subspace

P_{1}={\phi\in Val_{1}^{\infty}(V)\mid\phi\cdot(V_{\Lambda,1})^{n-1}=0}.

[

,

]
and by Bernig and Bröcker

]

More recently, Kotrbatý

conjectured a much more general version of the mixed hard Lefschetz theorem. In the same paper, he also conjectured mixed Hodge–Riemann relations for valuations on convex sets and proved them in a special non-mixed case. Subsequently, Kotrbatý and Wannerer

Finally, Bernig, Kotrbatý, and Wannerer

is motivated by Kotrbatý’s proof

The quadratic form

This reduces the proof of

Reminder on local fields.

1

In this section we collect a few basic well known facts on local fields sufficient for this paper. We refer to
[
29
]
, Ch. 1, for details.

By definition, a local field is a topological locally compact non-discrete field such that the operations of addition, multiplication, and division are continuous in this topology.
There is a classification of such fields: they are precisely
\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{F}_{q}((t))
, and finite extensions of the
fields of
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Here
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denotes the finite field with
q
elements, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}((t))
denotes the field of formal Laurent power series.
The first two examples, namely
\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
, are called Archimedean, while all others are called non-Archimedean local fields.

2

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a non-Archimedean local field. It has a unique maximal compact subring
{\cal O}\subset\mathbb{F}
. For example if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}((t))
then
{\cal O}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[[t]]
is the ring of all Taylor power series.
If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
then
{\cal O}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the ring
p
-adic integers.

The field of fractions of
{\cal O}
equals
\mathbb{F}
.

3

{\cal O}
has a unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset{\cal O}
. For example for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}((t))
the ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is generated by
t
,
while for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
the ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is generated by
p
.

The quotient
k:={\cal O}/\mathfrak{m}
is necessarily a finite field; it is called the residue field of
\mathbb{F}
.

4

There exists a unique multiplicative norm

|\cdot|\colon\mathbb{F}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

such that

\displaystyle|x|=1\,\,\,\forall x\in{\cal O}\backslash\mathfrak{m},

\displaystyle|x|=\frac{1}{|k|^{i}}\,\,\,\forall x\in\mathfrak{m}^{i}\backslash\mathfrak{m}^{i+1},\mbox{where }i\geq 1.

where
|k|
denotes the cardinality of the residue field.
Multiplicativity means that
|x\cdot y|=|x|\cdot|y|
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{F}
.

This norm satisfies the strengthened triangle inequality

|x+y|\leq\max\{|x|,|y|\}.

5

The norm
|\cdot|
has the following property. Let
\mu
be a Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{F}
(
\mu
exists and is unique up to a proportionality). Let
x\in\mathbb{F}
.
Then

\mu(x\cdot A)=|x|\mu(A)

for any compact subset
A\subset\mathbb{F}
.

Grassmannians.

1

The material of this section is a folklore. We describe the Grassmannian over a commutative ring rather than a field.

2

Let

\mathfrak{p}
different from

.
The quotient ring

is necessarily a field, called the residue field, and will be denoted by

3

Let

.

.

1.

If

2.

It is well known that if

For this work, the main examples of the rings of integers

The sine transform.

1

Let

n

\mathbb{F}
. Let
\Lambda\subset V
be a lattice. Let

be the Lebesgue measure on

normalized so that

vol_{\Lambda}(\Lambda)=1.

Let

be two subspaces of complementary dimensions
i
and
n-i

\Lambda_{L}=L\cap\Lambda,\,\Lambda_{M}=M\cap\Lambda.

In

7

L
and
M
by

\displaystyle s(L,M):=vol_{\Lambda}(\Lambda_{L}+\Lambda_{M}).

(5.1)

Clearly,
0\leq s(L,M)\leq 1
.

2

Let us give another description of
s(L,M)
. Let

p\colon\Lambda\longrightarrow\Lambda/\Lambda_{L}\oplus\Lambda/\Lambda_{M}

be the obvious homomorphism. Let
\nu
be any non-zero Lebesgue measure on
V/L\oplus V/M
.

5.1 Proposition
.

One has

s(L,M)=\frac{\nu(p(\Lambda))}{\nu(\Lambda/\Lambda_{L}\oplus\Lambda/\Lambda_{M})}.

Clearly,
s(L,M)
is independent of
\nu
.
This proposition follows from the following more precise result.

5.2 Proposition
.

There is a groups isomorphism

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda_{L}+\Lambda_{M}}\simeq\frac{\Lambda/\Lambda_{L}\oplus\Lambda/\Lambda_{M}}{p(\Lambda)}.

(5.2)

Proof.
We will prove that both groups are isomorphic to

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda/\Lambda_{L}}{Im[\Lambda_{M}\longrightarrow\Lambda/\Lambda_{L}]}.

(5.3)

For the left hand side of (
5.2
) this is obvious. For the right hand side consider the homomorphism

F\colon\Lambda/\Lambda_{L}\longrightarrow\frac{\Lambda/\Lambda_{L}\oplus\Lambda/\Lambda_{M}}{p(\Lambda)}

given by
F(x)\equiv(x,0)\mbox{ mod }p(\Lambda)
.
It is easy to see that
F
is onto. Let us show that

Ker(F)=Im[\Lambda_{M}\longrightarrow\Lambda/\Lambda_{L}].

Let
x\in Ker(F)
. That means that
(x,0)\in p(\Lambda)
. In other words there exists
\tilde{y}\in\Lambda
such that
x\equiv\tilde{y}\mbox{ mod }\Lambda_{L}
and
0\equiv\tilde{y}\mbox{ mod }\Lambda_{M}
.
Hence
\tilde{y}\in\Lambda_{M}
and hence
x\in Im[\Lambda_{M}\longrightarrow\Lambda/\Lambda_{L}]
.
Q.E.D.

The sine transform

2

Sometimes it is also called cosine transform.

-invariant Haar probability measure on

\displaystyle s(gL,gM)=s(L,M)\mbox{ for any }g\in GL_{n}({\cal O}).

4

Let us compute more explicitly

when
\dim L=1,\,\dim M=n-1

,

For

\langle\textbf{x},\textbf{y}\rangle_{0}=x_{1}y_{1}+\dots+x_{n}y_{n}.

1-dimensional subspace

Let

1

\displaystyle Val_{i}^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{n})=C^{\infty}(Gr_{i,n}(\mathbb{F}),{\cal L}_{i})\mbox{ for }i=0,1,n-1,n.

(6.1)

3

-valued functions on the Grassmannian:

.

Note that a function on

[
8
]

7
Explicit description of projective space over a local ring.

1

The material presented in this section is not new and is part of the folklore.

Let
(A,\rho)
be a local ring. In this section, we present a more explicit description of the Grassmannian of lines

Gr_{1,n}(A)
which one denotes by
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(A)
and calls a projective space.

Consider the subset of
A^{n}
which we call a sphere

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(A)=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\,\exists i=1,\dots,n\mbox{ s.t. }x_{i}\in A^{*}.\}.

7.1 Lemma
.

If
\textbf{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(A)
then
A\cdot\textbf{v}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(A)
.

Proof.
The
A
-submodule
A\cdot\textbf{v}\subset A^{n}
is free of rank 1. Indeed, the map
A\longrightarrow A\cdot\textbf{v}
given by

x\mapsto x\cdot\textbf{v}

is an isomorphism.

Let us assume for simplicity of the notation that
v_{1}\in A^{*}
. Set

{\cal Q}=\{(0,x_{2},\dots,x_{n})|,x_{i}\in A\mbox{ for }i=2,\dots,n.\}

Clearly,
{\cal Q}
is isomorphic to
A^{n-1}
and

A^{n}=A\cdot\textbf{v}\oplus{\cal Q}.

Hence
A\cdot\textbf{v}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(A)
. Q.E.D.

7.2 Lemma
.

1.

The map
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(A)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(A)
given by

\displaystyle\textbf{v}\mapsto A\cdot\textbf{v}

(7.1)

is onto.

2.

Two vectors
\textbf{v},\textbf{w}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(A)

7.1
) if and only if there is
\alpha\in A^{*}
such that

\textbf{w}=\alpha\cdot\textbf{v}.

Such
\alpha
is necessarily unique.

Proof.
Part
1
. Assume that

A^{n}={\cal P}\oplus{\cal Q},

where
{\cal P}\simeq A,\,{\cal Q}\simeq A^{n-1}
. Let
\textbf{v}\in{\cal P}
be a basis of
{\cal P}
. Let us prove that
\textbf{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(A)
.
Choose a basis
\bm{\xi}_{1},\dots,\bm{\xi}_{n-1}
of
{\cal Q}
. Then

\textbf{v},\bm{\xi}_{1},\dots,\bm{\xi}_{n-1}

is a basis of
A^{n}
. Let us denote their images in
A^{n}\otimes_{A}A/\mathfrak{p}=\kappa^{n}
by

\tilde{\textbf{v}},\tilde{\bm{\xi}}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\bm{\xi}}_{n-1}.

The latter elements form a basis of
\kappa^{n}
. In particular,
\tilde{\textbf{v}}\neq 0
in
\kappa^{n}
. That means that

\textbf{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(A).

Part
2
. Assume that
A\cdot\textbf{v}=A\cdot\textbf{w}
where
\textbf{v},\textbf{w}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(A)

v_{1}\in A^{*}
. Then necessarily
w_{1}\in A^{*}

v
and

A^{*}

v_{1}=w_{1}=1.

Let us prove that
\textbf{v}=\textbf{w}

A\cdot(1,v_{2},\dots,v_{n})=A\cdot(1,w_{2},\dots,w_{n}).

In particular

(1,v_{2},\dots,v_{n})=\gamma(1,w_{2},\dots,w_{n}),

where
\gamma\in A
. Hence
\gamma=1

\textbf{v}=\textbf{w}

2

(7.2)

\bar{P}_{m}\in C(\bar{P}\backslash G/P)

G
on

and

are the standard ones.

9.2 Proposition
.

Let
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Then
\bar{P}_{0}=1
, and for
m\geq 1
one has

\bar{P}_{m}(\textbf{x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\mbox{ if }&|x_{n}|\leq q^{-m},\\
-\alpha_{n,m}&\mbox{ if }&|x_{n}|=q^{-(m-1)}\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise }&\end{array}\right.,

where

\alpha_{n,m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1-q^{-(n-1)}}{q-1}&\mbox{ if }&m=1,\\
\frac{1}{q-1}&\mbox{ if }&m\geq 2.\end{array}\right..

Proof.

Step 1.
It is clear that
\bar{P}_{m}
are
P
-invariant (for the action
9.1
).

Step 2.
Let us prove that
\bar{P}_{m}
with
m\geq 1
are orthogonal to
P_{0}=1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}P_{m}(\textbf{x})d^{n}x=0.

(9.2)

For
m\geq 2
one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\bar{P}_{m}(\textbf{x})d^{n}x=\int_{|x_{n}|\leq q^{-(m-1)}}\bar{P}_{m}(\textbf{x})d^{n}x=

\displaystyle vol_{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2})vol_{1}(\pi^{m}{\cal O})-\frac{1}{q-1}vol_{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2})vol_{1}(\pi^{m-1}{\cal O}^{*})=

\displaystyle vol_{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2})\left(q^{-m}-q^{-(m-1)}\frac{1-q^{-1}}{q-1}\right)=0.

For
m=1
one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\bar{P}_{1}(\textbf{x})d^{n}x=

\displaystyle vol_{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2})\cdot vol_{1}(\pi{\cal O})-\frac{1-q^{-(n-1)}}{q-1}vol_{n-1}({\cal O}^{n-1})\cdot vol_{1}({\cal O}^{*})=

\displaystyle(1-q^{-(n-1)})q^{-1}-\frac{1-q^{-(n-1)}}{q-1}(1-q^{-1})=0.

Step 3.
Let

\theta_{m-1}\colon\bar{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}\longrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}({\cal O}/\mathfrak{m}^{m-1})

be the natural map (
4.6
). It is
G
-equivariant, the target is a finite set. The pull-back induced by this map is a
G
-equivariant map on function spaces

\displaystyle C(\bar{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}({\cal O}/\mathfrak{m}^{m-1}))\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}).

(9.3)

In order to show that
\bar{P}_{m}\in\bar{\rho}_{m}\subset C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1})

it suffices to show that
\bar{P}_{m}
is orthogonal to the image of the map (
9.3
). For
m=1
this is just (
9.2
).

Let
m\geq 2
. The pull-back of any function

depends only on the class of
(x_{1}:\dots:x_{n})
modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{m-1}
. In particular, it is constant on any subset of the form

\{(a_{1}:\dots:a_{n-1}:x_{n})|\,x_{n}\in\pi^{m-1}{\cal O}\},

where
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}
are fixed.

But
\bar{P}_{m}(\textbf{x})=\bar{P}_{m}(|x_{n}|)
is supported on the set

\{(x_{1}:\dots:x_{n-1}:x_{n})|\,x_{n}\in\pi^{m-1}{\cal O}\}=\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times\pi^{m-1}{\cal O}.

Hence

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f\cdot\bar{P}_{m}d^{n}x=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}dx_{1}\dots dx_{n-1}\int_{\pi^{m-1}{\cal O}}dx_{n}f(\textbf{x})\cdot\bar{P}_{m}(|x_{n}|)\overset{(\ref{E:integ-vanish})}{=}0.

Q.E.D.

1

In this section, we reformulate the Hodge-Riemann relations in terms of analysis on Grassmannians in a way similar to Kotrbatý’s approach
[
kotrbaty-even
]
for even valuations on convex sets.

2

Let us fix a lattice
\Lambda\subset V
. For any subspace
M\subset V
denote by
vol_{M}
the Lebesgue measure on
M
such that
vol_{M}(M\cap\Lambda)=1
.
This induces a trivialization of the vector bundle
{\cal L}_{i}
over
Gr_{i}^{V}
: indeed the fiber
{\cal L}_{i}|_{M}=D(M)=\mathbb{C}\cdot vol_{M}
. Thus we will identify the space
Val_{i}^{\infty}(V)

with a subspace of locally constant functions on
Gr_{i}^{V}
.

Consider valuations
\phi\in Val_{i}^{\infty}(V),\psi\in Val_{n-i}^{\infty}(V)
given by

\displaystyle\phi=\int_{Gr_{n-i}^{V}}\hat{f}(L)p^{*}_{L}(\mu_{L})dL,

(10.1)

\displaystyle\psi=\int_{Gr_{i}^{V}}\hat{g}(M)p^{*}_{M}(\mu_{M})dM,

(10.2)

where the integrations are with respect to the
GL(\Lambda)
-invariant Haar probability measures on the Grassmannians,
p_{L}\colon V\longrightarrow V/L
is the canonical quotient map,
and
\mu_{L}
is the Lebesgue measure on
L
normalized so that
\mu_{L}(p_{L}(\Lambda))=1
, and similarly for
p_{M},\mu_{M}
.
In other words

\displaystyle\phi={\cal D}_{i}\hat{f},

(10.3)

\displaystyle\psi={\cal D}_{n-i}\hat{g}.

(10.4)

10.1 Lemma
.

\frac{\phi\cdot\psi}{vol_{\Lambda}}=\int_{Gr_{n-i}^{V}}\int_{Gr_{i}^{V}}\hat{f}(L)\hat{g}(M)s(L,M)dL\cdot dM=(\bar{\hat{f}},{\cal D}_{n-i}\hat{g})=({\cal D}_{i}\bar{\hat{f}},\hat{g}),

where
vol_{\Lambda}
is the Lebesgue measure on
V
normalized so that
vol_{\Lambda}(\Lambda)=1
, and
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Hermitian product on
L^{2}
functions on corresponding Grassmannians.

Proof.
Denote
p_{L}\times p_{M}\colon V\longrightarrow V/M\times V/L
. By
[
7
]
, formula (14.1), we have

\displaystyle\phi\cdot\psi=\int_{Gr_{n-i}^{V}}dL\int_{Gr_{i}^{V}}dM\hat{f}(L)\hat{g}(M)(p_{L}\times p_{M})^{*}(\mu_{L}\boxtimes\mu_{M})\overset{\mbox{Prop. }\ref{P:sine-another}}{=}

(10.5)

\displaystyle\int_{Gr_{n-i}^{V}}dL\int_{Gr_{i}^{V}}dM\hat{f}(L)\hat{g}(M)s(L,M).

(10.6)

Q.E.D.

3

Assume
\phi\in Val_{i}^{\infty}(V)
is given by (
10.1
).

10.2 Lemma

(Lemma 16.2,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\phi\in Val_{i}^{\infty}(V)
be given by (
10.1
).
Assume
k\leq n-i
. Then, identifying
Val_{i}^{\infty}(V)
with subspace of functions on
Gr_{i}^{V}
using lattice
\Lambda
, one has

V_{1}^{k}\cdot\phi=c_{n,i,k}({\cal D}_{i+k}\circ R_{n-i-k,n-i})\hat{f},

where
c_{n,i,k}>0
is a constant.

In particular, if
k=n-2i
one has

V_{1}^{n-2i}\cdot\phi=c_{n,i}{\cal D}_{n-i}(R_{i,n-i}\hat{f}),

where
c_{n,i}>0
.

4

For
i\leq n/2
define the Hermitian form on
Val_{i}^{\infty}(V)
.

\displaystyle Q(\phi)=(-1)^{i}\frac{\bar{\phi}\cdot\phi\cdot V_{1}^{n-2i}}{vol_{\Lambda}}.

(10.7)

By Lemmas
10.2
and
10.1
immediately imply

10.3 Lemma
.

One has

Q(\phi)=(-1)^{i}c_{n,i}(\hat{f},{\cal D}_{n-i}(R_{i,n-i}\hat{f})).

5

The (non-mixed) Hodge-Riemann relations say that if
i\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
\phi\in P_{i}\subset Val_{i}^{\infty}(V)
is a non-zero valuation then

Q(\phi)>0,

where

P_{i}=\{\phi\in Val_{i}^{\infty}(V)|\,\phi\cdot(V_{1})^{n-2i+1}=0\}

is the subspace of primitive valuations.

Let us rewrite this conjecture in analytic language. Let us denote by
{\cal P}_{i}
the
GL_{n}({\cal O})
-invariant subspace of
C^{\infty}(Gr_{n-i,n}(\mathbb{F}))
consisting of functions
f
satisfying

1.

f
is orthogonal to
Ker({\cal D}_{i})
in
L^{2}(Gr_{n-i,n}(\mathbb{F}))
;

2.

{\cal D}_{i}(f)\in P_{i}
.

Then Lemma
10.3
implies that the Hodge-Riemann relations in degree
i
are equivalent to

10.4 Conjecture
.

For
\hat{f}\in{\cal P}_{i}\backslash\{0\}

(-1)^{i}(\hat{f},({\cal D}_{n-i}\circ R_{i,n-i})\hat{f})>0.

We can reformulate the last conjecture a bit further. The representation of
GL_{n}({\cal O})
in the space
C^{\infty}(Gr_{n-i,n}(\mathbb{F}))
is multiplicity free, and this space is a direct sum
of irreducible finite dimensional representations. The operator
(-1)^{i}{\cal D}_{n-i}\circ R_{i,n-i}
is
GL_{n}({\cal O})
-equivariant, hence it maps each irreducible component
\rho
to itself, and by Schur’s lemma
its restriction to
\rho
is proportional to the identity operator. Let us denote by
\lambda_{\rho}
this constant of proportionality. Then Conjecture
10.4
is equivalent to

10.5 Conjecture
.

Let an
GL_{n}({\cal O})
-component
\rho

Ker({\cal D}_{i})
in
L^{2}(Gr_{n-i,n}(\mathbb{F}))
and
{\cal D}_{i}(\rho)\subset P_{i}
then

\lambda_{\rho}>0.

6

Let us consider the case
i=1
more explicitly. We have

P_{1}=\{\phi\in Val^{\infty}_{1}(\mathbb{F}^{n})|\,\phi\cdot(V_{1})^{n-1}=0\}.

10.6 Lemma
.

Under the isomorphism (
6.2
) (see also the next line after (

))

Val_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{n})\tilde{\longrightarrow}C^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})

the subspace

is identified with the subspace of functions

where

is the
GL_{n}({\cal O})
-invariant Haar probability measure on
\mathbb{P}
.

Proof.
The map
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})\simeq Val_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}^{n})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by

\phi\mapsto\frac{\phi\cdot(V_{1})^{n-1}}{vol},

where

is any non-zero Lebesgue measure, is a

-equivariant linear functional whose kernel is equal to

. This functional does not vanish on the constant function 1 because

Thus Conjecture

10.7 Theorem

The restriction of the operator

-submodule

The Radon transform.

1

Consider the Radon transform

Since this map is
G
-equivariant and maps isomorphically
\bar{\rho}_{m}
to

\alpha_{m}
,

such that

11.1 Proposition
.

For each
m\geq 0
one has

\alpha_{m}>0.

Proof.
For
m=0
the result is obvious. Hence assume that
m\geq 1
. Then

P_{m}(\textbf{e}_{n})=1.

Hence it suffices to show that

\displaystyle(R(\bar{P}_{m}))(\textbf{e}_{n})>0.

(11.1)

We have

12
The sine-transform.

1

Consider the transform

{\cal D}\colon C^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{P}}).

This is a
G
-equivariant isomorphism. Hence there are constants
\beta_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
,
m\geq 0
, such that

{\cal D}(P_{m})=\beta_{m}\bar{P}_{m}.

12.1 Proposition
.

For
m\geq 1
one has

\beta_{m}<0,

while
\beta_{0}>0
.

Proof.
For
m=0
the statement is obvious. Let us assume that
m\geq 1
. One has

\bar{P}_{m}(\textbf{e}_{1})=1.

Hence it suffices to show that

\displaystyle({\cal D}P_{m})(\textbf{e}_{1})<0.

(12.1)

For
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
. Let us denote
X:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
. Thus
\textbf{x}=(X,x_{n})
.

First let us assume that
m\geq 2
. One has

\displaystyle({\cal D}P_{m})(\textbf{e}_{1})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\langle\textbf{e}_{1},\textbf{x}\rangle_{0}|P_{m}(\textbf{x})d^{n}x=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|x_{1}|P_{m}(\textbf{x})d^{n}x=

\displaystyle\int_{x_{n}\in{\cal O}^{*}}dx_{n}\left(\int_{|X|\leq q^{-m}}|x_{1}|d^{n-1}X-\frac{1}{q^{n-1}-1}\int_{|X|=q^{-(m-1)}}|x_{1}|d^{n-1}X\right)=

\displaystyle vol_{1}({\cal O})^{*}\left(q^{-mn}\int_{{\cal O}^{n-1}}|x_{1}|d^{n-1}X-\frac{q^{-(m-1)n}}{q^{n-1}-1}\int_{X\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}|x_{1}|d^{n-1}X\right)=

\displaystyle vol_{1}({\cal O}^{*})q^{-(m-1)n}\left(q^{-n}\int_{x\in{\cal O}}|x|dx-\frac{1}{q^{n-1}-1}\int_{X\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}|x_{1}|d^{n-1}X\right)=

\displaystyle vol_{1}({\cal O}^{*})q^{-(m-1)n}\left(\frac{q^{-n}}{1+q^{-1}}-\frac{1}{q^{n-1}-1}\int_{X\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}|x_{1}|d^{n-1}X\right).

Let us compute the integral in brackets

\displaystyle\int_{X\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}|x_{1}|d^{n-1}X.

Case 1.
Assume
n=2
. Then this integral is equal to
vol_{1}({\cal O})=1-q^{-1}
. Hence in this case

\displaystyle({\cal D}P_{m})(\textbf{e}_{1})=vol_{1}({\cal O}^{*})q^{-2(m-1)}\left(\frac{q^{-2}}{1+q^{-1}}-\frac{1}{q-1}(1-q^{-1})\right)<0,

as required.

Case 2.
Assume
n>2
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{X\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}|x_{1}|d^{n-1}X=\int_{|x_{1}|=1}+\int_{|x_{1}|<1}=

\displaystyle\int_{{\cal O}^{*}\times{\cal O}^{n-2}}d^{n-1}x+\int_{x_{1}\in\pi{\cal O}}|x_{1}|dx_{1}\cdot vol_{n-2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-3})=

\displaystyle vol_{1}({\cal O}^{*})+q^{-2}\left(\int_{{\cal O}}|x|dx\right)(1-q^{-(n-2)})=

\displaystyle 1-q^{-1}+\frac{q^{-2}}{1+q^{-1}}(1-q^{-(n-2)}).

Then we obtain

\displaystyle({\cal D}P_{m})(\textbf{e}_{1})=

\displaystyle vol_{1}({\cal O}^{*})q^{-(m-1)n}\left(\frac{q^{-n}}{1+q^{-1}}-\frac{1}{q^{n-1}-1}\left(1-q^{-1}+\frac{q^{-2}}{1+q^{-1}}(1-q^{-(n-2)})\right)\right)=

\displaystyle-vol_{1}({\cal O}^{*})q^{-(m-1)n}\left(\frac{q-1}{(q+1)(q^{n-1}-1)}\right)<0.

Thus, we have shown so far that for any
n\geq 2
and
m>1
one has

\beta_{m}<0.

It remains to show that for
n\geq 2

\beta_{1}<0.

Since

\bar{P}_{1}(\textbf{e}_{n})<0,

It suffices to show that

({\cal D}P_{1})(\textbf{e}_{n})>0.

One has

\displaystyle({\cal D}P_{1})(\textbf{e}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|x_{n}|P_{1}(\textbf{x})d^{n}x=\int_{|X|<1}+\int_{|X|=1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\pi{\cal O}^{n-1}\times{\cal O}^{*}}d^{n}x-\frac{q-1}{q(q^{n-1}-1)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times{\cal O}}|x_{n}|d^{n}x=

\displaystyle vol_{n-1}(\pi{\cal O}^{n-1})\cdot vol_{1}({\cal O}^{*})-\frac{q-1}{q(q^{n-1}-1)}vol_{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-2})\int_{{\cal O}}|x|dx=

\displaystyle q^{-(n-1)}(1-q^{-1})-\frac{q-1}{q(q^{n-1}-1)}(1-q^{-(n-1)})\frac{1}{1+q^{-1}}=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{-n}(q-1)}{q+1}>0,

as required. Q.E.D.
