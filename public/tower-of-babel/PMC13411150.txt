Long Non-Coding RNA PVT1 Promotes Doxorubicin Resistance by Inhibiting Ferroptosis in Breast Cancer
Authors: Dan Zhao, Zheqiong Tan, Weiqun Chen, Shuiyi Liu, Tangwei Wu, Xiaoyi Li, Yuan Xiang, Hui Wang, Zhongxin Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThere is growing evidence that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play crucial roles in cancer progression and therapy. Our previous study showed that the lncRNA plasmacytoma variant translocation 1 (PVT1) regulates tumor growth and metastasis in breast cancer (BC). As a conventional chemotherapeutic drug, doxorubicin (DOX) resistance continues to be a major challenge in BC treatment. This study aimed to explore the role and underlying mechanism of PVT1 in doxorubicin-resistant BC.MethodsQuantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) and Western blotting (WB) were carried out to detect gene and protein expression levels. The extent of ferroptosis was measured based on the cellular glutathione (GSH) levels and total or lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels. An in-situ tumor implantation model in nude mice was employed to validate the mechanism in vivo. Transcriptome analysis was conducted to identify downstream target genes.ResultsThis study found that PVT1 was highly expressed in the plasma of drug-resistant patients and drug-resistant cell lines. Silencing PVT1 reduced cellular glutathione level, increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) and lipid peroxidation (LPO), while ferroptosis inhibition in rescue experiments partially reversed the oxidative stress. In-vivo study confirmed that silencing PVT1 increased the sensitivity of BC cells to doxorubicin treatment. Transcriptomic sequencing revealed that solute carrier family 3 member 2 (SLC3A2) was the most potential target gene of PVT1, which was confirmed in PVT1-silenced cell models.ConclusionMechanistically, PVT1 increased SLC3A2 expression, thus inhibiting ferroptosis and promoting doxorubicin resistance in BC, indicating that PVT1 could be a promising therapeutic target for doxorubicin-resistant BC patients.Keywords: PVT1, breast cancer, drug resistance, ferroptosis, SLC3A2

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer among women worldwide. According to the Global Cancer Observatory (GLOBOCAN) 2022, published by International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), approximately 2.3 million new cases of female breast cancer were reported globally, accounting for one in four of all cancer cases in women.1 Meanwhile, 670,000 breast cancer-related deaths occurred, ranking first among women.1 Consistent with the statistical results of the appeal, the incidence and mortality of breast cancer still were occupied with the first position, according to data from the American Cancer Society in United States in 2025.2 For women in Human Development Index (HDI) countries, the mortality from breast cancer has been declining since 1990s. In contrast, in middle- and low-HDI countries, high incidence was still accompanied by high mortality, imposing a heavy economic burden on families and society.1 Therefore, focusing on breast cancer research will help to improve the situation of women’s cancers and related diseases globally, especially in low- and middle-HDI countries.3

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) poses significant clinical challenges due to the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2).4 The lack of these molecular targets reduces responsiveness to conventional targeted therapies and contributes to higher recurrence rates.5–7 TNBC is more commonly diagnosed in younger women and is often associated with larger tumor size, higher histological grade, and an increased likelihood of lymph node involvement and systemic metastasis.8 Doxorubicin, a conventional anthracycline-based chemotherapeutic agent, remains a key component of standard chemotherapy regimens.9 In early stages, chemotherapy can improve response and survival in BC patients.10 However, an increasing number of patients develop resistance to chemotherapy, including doxorubicin, in the later stages.11,12 This highlights the urgent need for novel diagnostic and therapeutic biomarkers to enhance BC detection and treatment outcomes.

Recent studies suggested that lncRNAs play an essential role in BC progression and therapy.13–15 PVT1 was the first lncRNA gene identified in human Burkitt’s lymphoma, located on chromosome 8q24.16 Substantial evidence indicates that PVT1 functions as an oncogene, directly regulating ribonucleic acid (RNA), deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), and protein expression across various malignancies. These regulatory functions contribute to tumor progression, recurrence, metastasis, and drug resistance.17–19 Our previous study demonstrated that PVT1 could function as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) for miR-128-3p, regulating forkhead box Q1 (FOXQ1) expression and promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT).20 Additionally, PVT1 enhanced tumor development in TNBC by stabilizing KLF5 protein and increasing β-catenin expression.21 However, the application of PVT1 in the diagnosis of BC and its correlation with therapy remain poorly understood.

Ferroptosis, a recently identified type of regulated cell death, has significantly garnered oncologists’ attention due to its potential therapeutic implications.22–24 In contrast to apoptosis, ferroptosis, a well-characterized form of programmed cell death associated with the accumulation of lipid peroxides and iron-dependent reactive oxygen species, results in oxidative damage and subsequent cell death.25,26 Cancer cells typically experience high levels of oxidative stress and employ mechanisms to reduce ROS levels, thereby maintaining the balance of redox homeostasis in the tumor microenvironment.27,28 Targeting the ferroptosis pathway and its associated signaling molecules presents a promising therapeutic strategy for inducing tumor cell death.29–35

In this study, PVT1 was found to be highly expressed in doxorubicin-resistant BC cell lines and in the plasma of chemotherapy-resistant BC patients. Silencing PVT1 activated ferroptosis by regulating SLC3A2. In vivo studies further confirmed that PVT1 silencing enhanced BC sensitivity to doxorubicin treatment. These findings suggest that PVT1 is a critical regulator in BC and a potential therapeutic target for its treatment.

Materials and Methods

Patients’ Specimens

Peripheral blood samples were collected using ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA)-containing tubes and immediately centrifuged at 1000 revolutions per minute (rpm) for 15 minutes (min) at 4°C. Following centrifugation, the supernatant was delicately transferred into RNase-free microtubes and stored at −80 °C. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of The Central Hospital of Wuhan (Wuhan, China). Written informed permission was signed by each participant and this study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Cell Culture

This study utilized various human breast epithelial and BC cell lines, including normal breast epithelial cells (MCF-10A) and several BC cell lines (MCF7, T47D, MDA-MB-453, MDA-MB-468, MDA-MB-231, BT549, SUM159PT, and HS578T). All cell lines were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC; Manassas, VA, USA). BC cell lines were cultured in a Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM; G4515; Wuhan Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; ST200806, PAN). In contrast, MCF-10A cells were maintained in DMEM/F12 containing 5% horse serum (HS), 20 ng/mL epidermal growth factor (EGF), 0.5 μg/mL hydrocortisone, 10 μg/mL insulin, 1% non-essential amino acids (NEAA), and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S) (CM0525, Pricella). To generate doxorubicin-resistant HS578T cells (HS578T/DOX-R), HS578T cells were cultured in a medium containing 200 nM doxorubicin (S1208, Selleck) for six months. After that, DOX was removed, and the cells were maintained in a DMEM. All cell lines were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. In order to guarantee that the cell was free of mycoplasma contamination, mycoplasma detection was regularly carried out. The annual STR profiling was conducted out to confirm the identity of the cell lines.

RNA Extraction and Quantitative Reverse Transcription Polymerase Chain Reaction (qRT‑PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells and plasma samples using the FastPure® Cell/Tissue Total RNA Isolation kit (RC101; Vazyme, Nanjing, China). Following cell lysis, the lysate was applied to a gDNA-Filter Column and centrifuged to remove genomic DNA. The resulting flow-through was combined with ethanol, transferred to an RNA Column, washed with Buffer RW, and total RNA was subsequently eluted with RNase-free water. The synthesis of first-strand cDNA for qRT-PCR was carried out using the HiScript II Q RT SuperMix (R222, Vazyme), and the relative messenger RNA (mRNA) expression was measured using the ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix reagent (Q311, Vazyme) in the StepOne™ Real-Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA, USA). β-actin functioned as the endogenous control for both PVT1 and SLC3A2. The relative expression levels of PVT1 and SLC3A2 were calculated using the 2–ΔΔCt method for BC cells and the 2–ΔCt method for plasma samples.

Cell Transfection and Generation of Stable shRNA Knockdown Cell Lines

Negative control and siRNAs targeting PVT1 were all purchased from RiboBio Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). When cell confluence reached 40–60%, transfection was performed using the ExFectR Transfection Reagent (T101, Vazyme) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, plasmid DNA and ExFect reagent were separately diluted in serum-free medium, gently mixed, and incubated for 15–20 min at room temperature to allow complex formation. The resulting ExFect–DNA complexes were then added dropwise to cells. To establish stable PVT1-knockdown cell lines and their corresponding negative control (NC) cell lines (SUM159PT shPVT1, SUM159PT shNC, HS578T/DOX-R shPVT1, and HS578T/DOX-R shNC), cells were infected with lentiviruses containing either shNC (TTCTCCGAACGTGT CACGTAA) or shRNA sequences targeting PVT1 (#1, #2, #3) in the presence of 5 μg/mL polybrene. Cells were incubated in T25 flasks for 48 hours (h), after which puromycin was added to eliminate uninfected cells. qRT-PCR was employed to validate the efficiency of PVT1 knockdown.

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK8) Assay and Drug Sensitivity Evaluation

The evaluation of cell sensitivity to doxorubicin was conducted using the CCK8 kit (C0039, Beyotime). Cells were seeded into 96-well plates at the density of 4000 cells per well and co-cultured at various concentrations of doxorubicin (0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6, 3.2, 6.4, 12.8, and 25.6 μM). After co-culturing for 24 h, the supernatant was discarded, and 100 μL of DMEM containing 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well, followed by 2 h incubation. Absorbance was measured at 450 nanometer (nm) by the EnSpire multimode reader (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA).

Colony Formation Assay

After transfection, SUM159PT or HS578T/DOX-R cells were seeded into 12-well plates at a density of 500 cells/well and incubated in a DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS at 37 °C in the presence of 5% CO2. After cell adhesion overnight, doxorubicin was added, and the medium was replaced every three days. After 7–14 days, plates were washed twice with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), followed by fixing with 4% paraformaldehyde (G1101, Servicebio) for 15 min and staining with 0.1% crystal violet (C0121, Beyotime) for 15 min. Colonies containing more than 50 cells were identified.

Western Blot Assay

Total cellular proteins were extracted using RIPA Lysis Buffer (P0013B, Beyotime) supplemented with PMSF (Servicebio, China), and protein concentrations were determined using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) Protein Assay kit (P0012, Beyotime). BCA Reagent A and Reagent B were mixed at a ratio of 50:1 to prepare the working solution. Protein standards/samples were added to a 96-well plate, followed by the BCA working solution. After incubation at 37 °C for 20–30 min, absorbance was measured at 562 nm using a microplate reader. For Western blot analysis, 20–30 μg of protein was loaded onto sodium dodecyl-sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane (Millipore, New York, NY, USA). The membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 h at room temperature, following by overnight incubation with primary antibodies at 4°C under gentle agitation. After thrice washing with PBS containing Tween-20 (PBS-T), the membrane was incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h with agitation. Protein bands were detected using BeyoECL Moon (P0018FS, Beyotime). The primary antibodies used were rabbit polyclonal anti-SLC3A2 (1:5000, Proteintech), rabbit polyclonal anti-ATP-binding cassette subfamily B member 1 (ABCB1, 1:500, Proteintech), and mouse polyclonal anti-β-actin (1:2000, Proteintech) as an internal control. The secondary antibodies included HRP-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG (1:20000, Abways Technology, Shanghai, China) and HRP-labeled anti-mouse IgG (1:20000, Abways Technology).

In vivo Tumorigenesis Studies

Animal experiments were conducted according to protocols approved by Huazhong University of Science and Technology (Wuhan, China). For xenograft experiments, athymic BALB/c female mice (4–6 weeks old, 16–20 g) were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. Mice were housed in negative pressure barrier environment. The feed for nude mice was derived from the maintenance diet for mice and rats produced by Beijing Keao Xieli Feed Co., Ltd., consisting of moisture, crude protein, crude fat, crude fiber, crude ash, calcium, and phosphorus, and treated by irradiation sterilization. A 100 μL cell suspension mixed with Matrigel (356237, Corning, NY, USA) containing 5 × 106 cells was injected into the mammary fat pads of mice. Tumor size was measured every other day using a Vernier caliper, and tumor volume was calculated using the following formula: tumor volume = 0.5 × length × width2. Once tumors reached approximately 1000 mm3, mice were randomly assigned into four groups: shNC + NS, shPVT1 + NS, shNC + DOX, and shPVT1 + DOX. Doxorubicin or control treatments were administered intraperitoneally every three days, and tumor volumes were monitored at the same intervals. At the end of experiments, mice were euthanized by CO2 inhalation. All procedures were performed in accordance with the Health Resource Extension Act of 1985, Public Law 99–158 (1985).

GSH Assays

The relative GSH concentration in cell lysates was assessed using GSH and GSSG assay kits (S0053, Beyotime) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. To summarize, cells were seeded into six-well plates and incubated for 24h. After PBS washing, cells were trypsinized and resuspended in protein removal reagent M solution at three times the volume of the cell pellet. The samples were thereafter vortexed thoroughly and subjected to two freeze-thaw cycles at −80°C and 37°C. After incubation on ice for 5 min, samples were centrifuged at 10,000 g for 10 min at 4°C. The supernatant was either stored at −80°C for long-term use or 4°C for temporary storage. The detection buffer and reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) were added, and absorbance was measured at 405 nm.

Flow Cytometry for Total ROS and Lipid Peroxidation

To calculate the total ROS in the cells, a Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) detection kit (S0033S, Beyotime) was employed. Cells were incubated with 5 μM 2,7-Dichlorodi-hydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) in serum-free media for 30 min at 37°C without exposure to light. The measurement of lipid peroxidation in cells was conducted using the 11-(5,5-Difluoro-7-((1E,3E)-4-phenylbuta-1,3-dien-1-yl)-5H-5L4,6L4-dipyrrolo[1,2-c:2′,1′-f][1,3,2]diazaborinin-3-yl) undecanoic acid (C11-BODIPY 581/591) probe (D3861; Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). After being incubated for 30 min with a concentration of 5 μM at 37°C away from the light, the cells were harvested, washed twice with PBS, and resuspended in 500 μL PBS. Detection was carried out using a flow cytometer equipped with a 488 nm laser. The data analysis was conducted using FlowJo software. The presented data showed the relative total ROS levels and lipid peroxidation, with values normalized to the control samples for accurate comparison.

Statistical Analysis

Data analysis was performed using a two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA, Tukey’s test) in GraphPad Prism 9.0 software (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance was indicated as follows: *P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ***P < 0.001; ****P < 0.0001, with ns indicating no significance.

Results

PVT1 Was Overexpressed in Chemotherapy-Resistant BC Cell Lines and Patients

To investigate the role of PVT1 in BC, available databases were screened, revealing that PVT1 expression level was higher in tumors compared with that in normal tissues (Figure 1a and b). Patients with elevated PVT1 expression level also had shorter overall survival (Figure 1c). Moreover, it was found that the plasma PVT1 mRNA level was higher in chemotherapy-resistant BC patients than chemotherapy-responsive patients (Figure 1d). In addition, compared with the immortalized mammary epithelial cell line MCF-10A, PVT1 mRNA expression was higher in BC cell lines, especially in SUM159PT, a TNBC cell line (Figure 1e).

Doxorubicin is one of the standard chemotherapeutic drugs for BC. Therefore, doxorubicin-resistant cells were established by continuously exposing the doxorubicin-sensitive HS578T cell line to the drug for over three months in vitro (Supplementary Figure S1a). The doxorubicin-resistant ability of HS578T/DOX-R cell line were confirmed by its higher IC50 values for doxorubicin and overexpression of ABCB1 compared with the doxorubicin-sensitive parental cell line (Supplementary Figure S1b–e). PVT1 expression was also higher in the doxorubicin-resistant HS578T/DOX-R cells compared with the parental HS578T cells (Figure 1f). These findings suggest that PVT1 is associated with doxorubicin resistance in BC. Therefore, SUM159PT and HS578T/DOX-R were selected for subsequent experiments and further study.

Silencing PVT1 Inhibited Cell Proliferation and Activated Doxorubicin-Induced Ferroptosis in BC

To explore the biological function of PVT1 in BC, NT and siPVT1-1, siPVT1-2, and siPVT1-3 were transfected into SUM159PT and HS578T/DOX-R, and experiments on functionality were performed. According to the efficiency of siRNAs, siPVT1-1 and siPVT1-2 were finally selected and mixed as a siRNA pool for the subsequent experiments (Figure 2a).

According to our previous study and other researches, PVT1 could function as an oncogene, and its deletion could alleviate tumor proliferation, migration, and invasion.36–39 CCK8 and colony formation assays showed that silencing PVT1 decreased BC cell viability, while increased sensitivity to DOX treatment (Figure 2b–d).

The activation of ferroptosis is characterized by the increase of ROS and lipid peroxidation levels. This study indicated that cellular GSH levels were decreased, while total ROS and lipid peroxidation levels were elevated following the silence of PVT1 (Figure 2e–h). In the presence of doxorubicin, silencing PVT1 further increased cellular oxidative stress and enhanced doxorubicin-induced cell death in BC. The results indicated that silencing PVT1 could elevate the sensitivity of BC cells to doxorubicin treatment.

Inhibition of Ferroptosis Rescued BC Cells Following the Silence of PVT1

To confirm the role of PVT1 in ferroptosis regulation, BC cells were treated with the ferroptosis inhibitor Ferrostain-1 (Fer-1), the autophagy inhibitor 3-Methyladenine (3-MA), or the apoptosis inhibitor benzyloxycarbonyl-Val-Ala-Asp(OMe)-fluoromethylketone (Z-VAD-FMK), in the presence of doxorubicin, and cell viability was assessed. The addition of Fer-1 partially restored cell viability in the ferroptosis inhibitor group, while 3-MA and Z-VAD-FMK did not regenerate the cells’ activity during 24-hour incubation (Figure 3a and b). Notably, PVT1 disruption aggravated ferroptosis-induced cell death, indicating that PVT1 played a critical role in regulating this process.

Under doxorubicin treatment, silencing PVT1 further increased total ROS levels and lipid peroxidation. However, incubation with Fer-1 partially alleviated oxidative stress damage, confirming that ferroptosis occurred in the cells (Figure 3c–f).

Silencing PVT1 Enhanced Doxorubicin-Induced Cell Death in BC in vivo

To further assess the in vivo effects of PVT1, stable cell lines of SUM159PT and HS578T/DOX-R were established, including shNC, shPVT1#1, shPVT1#2, and shPVT1#3 (Supplementary Figure S2a and S2b). ShPVT1#1 and shPVT1#3 were selected for subsequent rescue experiments. Knockdown of PVT1 resulted in a decreased IC50 value to DOX in these cells (Supplementary Figure S2c and S2d).

Next, control and PVT1-silenced BC cell lines were inoculated into the mammary fat pads of female nude mice. Mice were euthanized when the largest tumor in the control group reached an average volume of 1000 millimeter(mm)3 (Figure 4a). Tumor size and weight were significantly reduced in the shPVT1 group treated with doxorubicin compared to the normal saline (NS) injection group, indicating that PVT1 inhibition enhanced the anti-tumor effects of doxorubicin (Figure 4b–d). These results demonstrated that decreased expression of PVT1 substantially suppressed tumor growth in vivo and potentiated the anti-cancer activity of doxorubicin.

PVT1 Regulated the Expression of SLC3A2

To further investigate the molecular mechanisms of PVT1 in the regulation of ferroptosis, transcriptome sequencing was employed to identify its molecular targets. Metascape was used for downstream gene analysis.40 The analysis revealed that silencing PVT1 impacted glutathione and ferroptosis-related metabolic pathways (Figure 5a). Through the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) database (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/), we found a positive correlation between PVT1 and SLC3A2 (Figure 5b).41 As a result, SLC3A2 was studied, a heavy chain component of the transmembrane transporter (System Xc-), forming a disulfide bond with solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11).42–44 SLC3A2 plays a crucial role in maintaining intracellular cysteine/glutamate balance and regulating oxidative stress.43,45,46 Further experiments demonstrated that PVT1 knockdown decreased the mRNA and protein levels of SLC3A2 (Figure 5c and d), therefore increasing the sensitivity to feroptosis and inhibiting the proliferation of tumor cells. Consequently, it can be inferred that PVT1increased the expression of SLC3A2, thereby inhibiting ferroptosis in tumor cells.

(c) Knockdown of PVT1 and SLC3A2 mRNA levels in SUM159PT and HS578T/DOX-R (n=3; mean ± SD). Discussion

Our previous study found that PVT1 acted as a ceRNA regulating EMT, and it also interacted with the oncogene up-frameshift protein 1(UPF1) to promote BC proliferation and metastasis.20 Similarly, a study revealed PVT1 physically connected and stabilized the transcription factor ERα, histone methyltransferase DOT1L, and scaffold protein menin, forming a functional chromatin regulatory complex, which collaboratively acted on specific loci on the genome to jointly regulate a series of key genes driving tumor growth and survival in estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer.47 Also, a scientific exploration revealed that PVT1 formed an epigenetic repression complex by bridging ERα and polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2), inhibiting apoptosis and promoting tumor survival in ERα-positive breast cancer.48 This study further explored the potential mechanism of PVT1 in breast cancer-related drug resistance. It was found that PVT1 mRNA level was elevated in treatment-resistant BC patients, suggesting that PVT1 could provide a theoretical basis and potential new target for targeted therapy of breast cancer targeting lncRNA.

It was revealed that PVT1 was also upregulated in doxorubicin-resistant BC cells and patients, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target. A drug-resistant cell model, HS578T/DOX-R, was established to evaluate growth, drug sensitivity, and the mRNA and protein levels of the drug-resistant gene ABCB1. It was confirmed that inhibition of PVT1 enhanced doxorubicin-induced cell death and oxidative stress. Fer-1, a ferroptosis inhibitor, could partially restore the cell death and oxidative stress caused by the silencing of PVT1. Those vivo and vitro experiments confirmed that knocking down PVT1 could enhance the level of doxorubicin-induced oxidative stress and the sensitivity of cells to ferroptosis, thus enhancing the anti-tumor effect of doxorubicin.

Hence, how does this regulatory mechanism function? Firstly, the downstream target gene SLC3A2 was identified through transcriptome sequencing. SLC3A2, a pro-carcinogenic gene, exhibited abnormally elevated expression in lung adenocarcinoma, colorectal cancer, pancreatic cancer, BC, hepatocellular carcinoma, and other tumors, and it was found to be associated with poor prognosis, drug resistance, and metastasis.28,45,46,49–52 SLC3A2 acts as a key heavy chain molecule in the glutathione transporter protein system, which is crucial for the intra- and extracellular exchange of glutamate and cystine.43,53,54 Glutamate is transferred from intracellular to extracellular space via System Xc-, while cystine is transported in the opposite direction.42,55,56 Cystine is an essential component in cysteine synthesis, a precursor for the synthesis of glutathione.55 Glutathione plays a remarkable role in maintaining tumor cells in a high-reduced state and facilitating their response to oxidative stress.57 Silencing PVT1 led to the decreased expression of SLC3A2, reduced intracellular cystine level, and diminished cysteine and glutathione synthesis, thereby weakening the cell’s ability to combat oxidative stress, increasing sensitivity to ferroptosis, and inhibiting tumor cell proliferation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the results indicated that PVT1 could function as an oncogene in BC and affect drug resistance by regulating SLC3A2. Silencing PVT1 inhibited tumor growth in vivo and triggered ferroptosis in doxorubicin-resistant BC cells (Figure 6). Simultaneously, this experiment also exhibited certain limitations. The findings were only validated in a subset of triple-negative breast cancer cell lines and also failed to reveal the specific interaction mechanism between PVT1 and SLC3A2. However, on the whole, it also offered a new avenue for targeted treatment of breast cancer.
