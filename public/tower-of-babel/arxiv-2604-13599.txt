Xiaoyu Fu, Gengsheng Wang, Huaiqiang Yu, Xiaomin Zhu

Abstract
We establish an observability inequality from space-time measurable sets for a class of strongly coupled parabolic systems consisting of two equations, where the observation acts on a single-component. The model is motivated by parabolic equations with complex coefficients and serves as a prototypical example of strongly coupled systems. The main difficulty lies in the fact that, unlike in the scalar and weakly coupled cases, pointwise-in-time interpolation observability estimates fail, as the observed component may exhibit high-frequency oscillatory cancellations induced by the coupling. To overcome this difficulty, we develop a new integral-type interpolation observability inequality based on a Remez-type inequality. With the aid of this integral-type interpolation observability inequality and the strategy developed in [ 13 , 3 ] for deriving observability from measurable sets, we obtain the desired observability inequality.



Keywords.

Strongly coupled parabolic systems, single-component observation, observability from measurable sets,
bang-bang property of time-optimal control.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz, locally star-shaped domain
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
T>0
, and let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

a>0
and
b\neq 0
. We consider the following strongly coupled parabolic system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y_{1}=a\Delta y_{1}-b\Delta y_{2}&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{t}y_{2}=b\Delta y_{1}+a\Delta y_{2}&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega\times(0,T),\\
y_{1}=y_{2}=0&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega\times(0,T).\end{cases}

(1.1)

System (
1.1
) admits a unique solution in
C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}))
for each initial state
y_{0}:=(y_{1}(0),y_{2}(0))^{\top}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
We denote this solution by
y(\cdot,\cdot;y_{0}):=(y_{1}(\cdot,\cdot;y_{0}),y_{2}(\cdot,\cdot;y_{0}))^{\top}
, viewed as a function from
\Omega\times[0,T]
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, or by
y(\cdot;y_{0}):=(y_{1}(\cdot;y_{0}),y_{2}(\cdot;y_{0}))^{\top}
, regarded as a function from
[0,T]
to
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

The aim of this paper is to establish the following observability inequality:

\|y(T;y_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq N\int_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\chi_{\mathcal{D}}(x,t)|y_{1}(x,t;y_{0})|dxdt\quad\text{for all }y_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{D}\subset\Omega\times(0,T)
is a set of positive measure, and
N:=N(a,b,T,\mathcal{D})>0
is a constant.
Inequality (
1.2
) allows one to quantitatively reconstruct the full system state from a single-component observation over a space-time measurable subset.

One motivation for studying (
1.2
) arises from parabolic equations with complex coefficients.
A typical example is
the linearized Ginzburg-Landau equation around the steady state
0
:

\begin{cases}z_{t}=(a+\mathrm{i}b)\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}z&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega\times(0,T),\\
z=0&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the complex Laplacian
on
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
.
For this equation, observability inequality (
1.2
) takes the following form:

\|z(T;z_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})}\leq N\int_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\chi_{\mathcal{D}}|\textsf{Re}\,z(x,t;z_{0})|dxdt\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;z_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}),

where
z(\cdot;z_{0})
denotes the solution corresponding to the initial state
z_{0}
.
Another important motivation for studying (
1.2
) comes from an open problem raised in
[
5
]
:
can strongly coupled parabolic systems be observed from space-time
measurable sets under partial (single-component) observation?

Observability inequalities on space-time measurable sets have been studied for scalar parabolic equations, largely motivated by time-optimal control problems (see
[
3
,
13
,
14
]
and references therein).

For coupled parabolic systems in which the number of observed components is less than the number of state components, existing observability results fall into two categories. The first concerns strongly coupled systems, but the observation domains are cylindrical sets of the form

\omega\times(0,T)
, where
\omega
is an open subset of
\Omega

(see
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
and references therein). The second category deals with weakly coupled systems, i.e., systems whose principal-part operators are uncoupled (see
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
6
,
15
,
18
]
). To the best of our knowledge,
observability from general measurable sets for strongly coupled parabolic systems under single-component
observation has not been established by any existing method. The present work provides the first positive result in this setting.

The observability of system (
1.1
) under single-component observation is significantly more challenging than its scalar counterpart, due to two main difficulties:

•

For the observability of system (
1.1
) under single-component observation, the observed component may exhibit cancellations of high-frequency oscillations, which may cause the pointwise-in-time observation signal to vanish. Consequently, the single-time-point interpolation observability inequality used in
[
3
,
5
,
13
,
14
]
, which is crucial for scalar parabolic equations with observations on measurable sets, does not hold, as shown in Proposition
3.3
. Even the multi-time-point interpolation observability inequality, established in
[
18
]
for weakly coupled parabolic systems, may fail, as demonstrated in Proposition
3.4
. This difficulty stems from the intrinsically strong coupling structure of system (
1.1
).
To overcome this difficulty, we establish an integral-type interpolation observability inequality with the aid of a Remez-type inequality, rather than relying on pointwise-in-time interpolation observability inequalities.

•

When
\mathcal{D}=\omega\times(0,T)
with
\omega\subset\Omega

an open subset,
inequality (
1.2
) was established in
[
11
]
.
Their approach combines the Lebeau-Robbiano inequality with an observability estimate for linear ODE systems, which requires the observability constant
C(T)

to have a specific dependence on
T
.
However, this approach does not apply to our setting, as
\mathcal{D}\subset\Omega\times(0,T)

is only a measurable set of positive measure.
Instead, we derive inequality (
1.2
) by combining the strategy for observability from measurable sets developed in
[
13
,
3
]
with the integral-type interpolation observability inequality mentioned above.

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subset\Omega\times(0,T)
be a set of positive measure.
There exists a constant

N=N(a,b,T,\mathcal{D})>0
such that the observability inequality (
1.2
) holds.

Remark 1.2
.

Several comments on Theorem
1.1
are in order.

(i)

In Theorem
1.1
, we only consider the case where the observer acts on the first component of system (
1.1
). The same conclusion holds when the observer is imposed on the second component, as shown in Proposition
4.1
of Section
4
.

(ii)

Two extreme cases are noteworthy:

(1)

If
a=0
and
b\neq 0
,
system (
1.1
) reduces to the Schrödinger equation. Our observability problem then reduces to recovering the full state of the system by observing only the real part of the solution on a measurable space-time set.
Unfortunately, this problem remains open even when
\mathcal{D}=\omega\times(0,T)
with
\omega\subset\Omega

an open subset. For related results, we refer the reader to
[
4
,
16
]
and references therein.

(2)

If
a\neq 0
and
b=0
, system (
1.1
) becomes decoupled.
Consequently, the state of the system cannot be recovered by observing only one component of the solution.

(iii)

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we establish the bang-bang property for the time-optimal control problem studied in Section
5
.
While the idea of the proof originates from
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
, it involves certain technical difficulties that were not emphasized in
[
3
]
, where the proof was omitted.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
collects the notation and several preliminary results.
Section
3
establishes refined integral-type interpolation inequalities for strongly coupled parabolic systems.
Section
4
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Section
5
applies Theorem
1.1
to derive the bang-bang property of time-optimal control problems.

Preliminaries

We collect here some notation that will be used repeatedly in the sequel. Let
\mathbb{R}^{+}:=(0,+\infty)
and
\mathbb{N}^{+}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. For
d\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
B_{r}^{d}(x_{0})
denote the closed ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
centered at
x_{0}
with radius
r
. In particular, we write
B_{r}^{d}
when
x_{0}=0
. For any Lebesgue measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
|E|
denotes its Lebesgue measure.
Given a matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}
with
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,
we denote by
M^{\top}
its transpose. For a Banach space
X
,

\|\cdot\|_{X}
denotes its norm. If
X
is a Hilbert space,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}
denotes
its inner product.
For two Banach spaces
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, let
\mathcal{L}(X_{1};X_{2})
denote the space of all bounded linear operators from
X_{1}
to
X_{2}
, and write
\mathcal{L}(X_{1}):=\mathcal{L}(X_{1};X_{1})
.
For
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we define
a_{1}\wedge a_{2}:=\min\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
and
[a_{1}]_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:k\leq a_{1}\}
.

Let
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
, with
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots
and
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
as
k\to+\infty
, be the eigenvalues of
-\Delta
under the homogeneous
Dirichlet boundary condition. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be the corresponding normalized eigenfunctions, which form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
. We further define

k_{\lambda}:=\max\{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}:\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda\}\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;\lambda>\lambda_{1}.

(2.1)

By the classical Weyl law (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
), there exists a constant
M=M(\Omega)>0
such that

k_{\lambda}=M\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}+o(\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}})\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;\lambda\to+\infty.

(2.2)

We now recall the
L^{1}
-type Lebeau-Robbiano inequality for measurable sets (see

[
3
, Theorems 3 and 5]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
R\in(0,1]
be such that
B_{4R}^{d}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. For each measurable set
\omega\subset B_{R}^{d}(x_{0})
with
|\omega|>0
, there exists a constant
C(R,|\omega|/|B_{R}^{d}|)>0
such that,
for all
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
and all
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}|a_{i}|^{2}\leq C(R,|\omega|/|B_{R}^{d}|)e^{C(R,|\omega|/|B_{R}^{d}|)\sqrt{\lambda}}\Big(\Big\|\chi_{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}a_{i}e_{i}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\Big)^{2}.

(2.3)

As a corollary of Lemma
2.1
, we obtain the following result, which will be used in the sequel.

Corollary 2.2
.

Suppose that
B^{d}_{4R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
for some
R\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Let

D\in(0,|B^{d}_{R}|)
.
Then there exists a constant
C(R,D)>0
such that for every measurable set
\omega\subset B^{d}_{R}(x_{0})

satisfying
|\omega|\geq D
,
inequality (
2.3
) holds
with
C(R,|\omega|/|B_{R}^{d}|)
replaced by
C(R,D)
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary subset
\omega\subset B^{d}_{R}(x_{0})
with
|\omega|\geq D
.
Since
|\omega|\geq D
and
D\in(0,|B^{d}_{R}|)
,
the absolute continuity of the Lebesgue integral ensures the existence of a radius

r^{*}\in(0,R]
such that

|B_{r^{*}}^{d}(x_{0})\cap\omega|=\int_{B^{d}_{r^{*}}(x_{0})}\chi_{\omega}(x)dx=D.

Let
\omega_{r^{*}}:=B_{r^{*}}^{d}(x_{0})\cap\omega\subset B^{d}_{R}(x_{0})
.
From the equality above, it follows that
|\omega_{r^{*}}|/|B^{d}_{R}|=D/|B_{R}^{d}|
.
By virtue of this fact, we apply Lemma
2.1
with
\omega
replaced by
\omega_{r^{*}}

to obtain a constant

C(R,|\omega_{r^{*}}|/|B_{R}^{d}|)=C(R,D/|B_{R}^{d}|)>0
such that for all
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
and all
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}|a_{i}|^{2}\leq C(R,D/|B_{R}^{d}|)e^{C(R,D/|B_{R}^{d}|)\sqrt{\lambda}}\Big(\Big\|\chi_{\omega_{r^{*}}}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}a_{i}e_{i}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\Big)^{2}.

Since
\omega_{r^{*}}\subset\omega
, the above inequality yields (
2.3
)
with
C(R,|\omega|/|B_{R}^{d}|)
replaced by
C(R,D/|B_{R}^{d}|)
.

Redefine
C(R,D):=C(R,D/|B_{R}^{d}|)
. This is well defined since, for fixed dimension
d
,
the quantity
D/|B_{R}^{d}|
depends only on
R
and
D
. Consequently, (
2.3
)
holds
with
C(R,|\omega|/|B_{R}^{d}|)
replaced by
C(R,D)
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma deals with the partition of measurable sets and will be used repeatedly in the sequel.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{l}
with
l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and let
Q\subset\mathcal{O}
be a set of positive Lebesgue measure.
Then there exist
q\in Q
and
R>0
such that, for every
r\in(0,R]
,
B_{4r}^{l}(q)\subset\mathcal{O}
and
|B_{r}^{l}(q)\cap Q|\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{r}^{l}|
.

Proof.

Let

q\in Q
be a Lebesgue point of
\chi_{Q}
. Then
\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|B_{r}^{l}(q)\cap Q|}{|B_{r}^{l}|}=1
,
so
there exists
R_{1}>0
such that

|B^{l}_{r}(q)\cap Q|\geq\frac{1}{2}|B^{l}_{r}|\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;r\in(0,R_{1}].

(2.4)

Moreover, since
\mathcal{O}
is open and
q\in Q
, there exists
R_{2}>0
such that

B_{4r}^{l}(q)\subset\mathcal{O}
for each
r\in(0,R_{2}]
.
Combining this with (
2.4
) implies that for
all
r\in(0,R_{1}\wedge R_{2}]
,

B_{4r}^{l}(q)\subset\mathcal{O}
and
|B_{r}^{l}(q)\cap Q|\geq\frac{1}{2}|B_{r}^{l}|
. Setting
R:=R_{1}\wedge R_{2}
completes the proof.
∎

The following is a standard result. We include its proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and let
F_{i}(\cdot)\in C(H;[0,+\infty))
for
i=1,2,3
. Assume that

F_{1}(h)\leq F_{3}(h)
for all
h\in H
. Then for each
\theta\in(0,1)
, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exists
\Pi_{1}\geq 1
such that
F_{1}(h)\leq\Pi_{1}(\varepsilon^{-\gamma}F_{2}(h)+\varepsilon F_{3}(h))

for all
h\in H
and all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, where
\gamma:=\theta(1-\theta)^{-1}
.

(ii)

There exists
\Pi_{2}\geq 1
such that
F_{1}(h)\leq\Pi_{2}(F_{2}(h))^{1-\theta}(F_{3}(h))^{\theta}
for all
h\in H
.

Moreover, if
(i)
holds with constant
\Pi_{1}
, then
(ii)
holds with
\Pi_{2}=2\Pi_{1}
.

Proof.

We arbitrarily fix
\theta\in(0,1)
.
The implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
follows immediately from Young’s inequality.

We now prove
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Assume that
(i)
holds with some constant
\Pi_{1}\geq 1
.
It suffices to prove that

F_{1}(h)\leq 2\Pi_{1}(F_{2}(h))^{1-\theta}(F_{3}(h))^{\theta}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;h\in H.

(2.5)

To this end, we fix an arbitrary
h\in H
.
In the case
F_{3}(h)\leq F_{2}(h)
,
inequality (
2.5
)
follows directly from the assumption
F_{1}(h)\leq F_{3}(h)
. We now consider the case
F_{3}(h)>F_{2}(h)
. If
F_{2}(h)=0
, then from
(i)
,
F_{1}(h)\leq\varepsilon\Pi_{1}F_{3}(h)
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we obtain
F_{1}(h)=0
, and hence (
2.5
) follows. If
F_{2}(h)>0
, define
\varepsilon_{*}:=\big(F_{2}(h)/F_{3}(h)\big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+1}}
, where
\gamma:=\theta(1-\theta)^{-1}
.
Then
\varepsilon_{*}\in(0,1)
and
(\varepsilon_{*})^{-\gamma}F_{2}(h)=\varepsilon_{*}F_{3}(h)
.
Combining these with
(i)
yields (
2.5
). Moreover, from (
2.5
), one can see that if
(i)
is true with the constant
\Pi_{1}
, then the constant
\Pi_{2}
in
(ii)
can be chosen to be
2\Pi_{1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We conclude this section with the following Remez-type inequality, which plays an important role in the

.

Lemma 2.8
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
denote the space of all trigonometric polynomials of degree at most
n
with real coefficients, i.e.,

\mathcal{T}_{n}:=\Big\{f\in C([-\pi,\pi];\mathbb{R}):f(\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(a_{k}\sin(k\theta)+b_{k}\cos(k\theta)\Big),\theta\in(-\pi,\pi),\{(a_{k},b_{k})^{\top}\}_{k=0}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\Big\}.

Let
f\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
and let
E\subset(-\pi,\pi)
be a set of positive measure. Then for every
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(-\pi,\pi;\mathbb{R})}\leq\Big(\frac{64}{\sin\frac{|E|}{4}}\Big)^{2(n+\frac{1}{p})}\|\chi_{E}f\|_{L^{p}(-\pi,\pi;\mathbb{R})}.

(2.9)

Proof.

Fix
f\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
arbitrarily. It follows from
[
8
, Theorem 1]
that
for any set
\hat{E}\subset(-\pi,\pi)
of positive measure,

\|f\|_{C([-\pi,\pi];\mathbb{R})}\leq\Big(\frac{2}{\sin\frac{|\hat{E}|}{4}}\Big)^{2n}\sup_{t\in\hat{E}}|f(t)|.

(2.10)

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Define

E_{f,\varepsilon}:=\Big\{\theta\in(-\pi,\pi):|f(\theta)|\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Big)^{2n}\|f\|_{C([-\pi,\pi];\mathbb{R})}\Big\}.

(2.11)

We claim that

|E_{f,\varepsilon}|\leq\varepsilon.

(2.12)

We prove it by contradiction. Assume that (
2.12
) does not hold, i.e.,
|E_{f,\varepsilon}|>\varepsilon
. Then by (
2.10
), with
\hat{E}=E_{f,\varepsilon}
(defined in (
2.11
)), we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C([-\pi,\pi];\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{|E_{f,\varepsilon}|}{4}\Big)^{-2n}\sup_{\theta\in E_{f,\varepsilon}}|f(\theta)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{|E_{f,\varepsilon}|}{4}\Big)^{-2n}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Big)^{2n}\|f\|_{C([-\pi,\pi];\mathbb{R})}<\|f\|_{C([-\pi,\pi];\mathbb{R})},

which yields a contradiction.

Now, we set
\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}
. By (
2.12
), we have that
\Big|E_{f,\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}}\Big|\leq\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}
, which implies

\Big|E\cap\Big(E_{f,\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}}\Big)^{c}\Big|\geq|E|-\Big|E_{f,\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}}\Big|\geq|E|-\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}\geq\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}.

Since
\sin x-\frac{1}{2}x\geq 0
for all
x\in[0,\frac{1}{16}]
, the above yields

\displaystyle\|\chi_{E}f\|_{L^{p}(-\pi,\pi;\mathbb{R})}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\chi_{E}(\theta)\chi_{\Big(E_{f,\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}}\Big)^{c}}(\theta)|f(\theta)|^{p}d\theta\geq\Big|E\cap\Big(E_{f,\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}}\Big)^{c}\Big|\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sin\Big(\frac{1}{16}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}\Big)\Big)^{2np}\|f\|^{p}_{C([-\pi,\pi];\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}\Big(\frac{1}{64}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}\Big)^{2np}\|f\|^{p}_{C([-\pi,\pi];\mathbb{R})}\geq\Big(\frac{1}{64}\sin\frac{|E|}{4}\Big)^{2np+2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(\theta)|^{p}d\theta.

Thus, inequality (
2.9
) holds. This completes the proof.
∎

3
Integral-type interpolation observability inequality

This section presents a new integral-type interpolation observability inequality, which plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let

A:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&-b\\
b&a\\
\end{array}\right),\;\;\mathcal{A}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a\Delta&-b\Delta\\
b\Delta&a\Delta\\
\end{array}\right)

(3.1)

and

B=(1,0).

(3.2)

It is clear that
\mathcal{A}
, with domain
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, generates an
analytic semigroup
\{e^{\mathcal{A}t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
For each
z\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, let
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}:=\{(v_{1,j},v_{2,j})^{\top}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
denote the Fourier coefficients of
z
with respect to the orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
. Then

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{A}t}z=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}e^{-A\lambda_{j}t}v_{j}e_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}e^{-a\lambda_{j}t}\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}\cos(\lambda_{j}bt)&\sin(\lambda_{j}bt)\\
-\sin(\lambda_{j}bt)&\cos(\lambda_{j}bt)\end{array}\right)v_{j}e_{j}.

The main result of this section is the following integral-type interpolation observability inequality.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E\subset(0,T)
be a set of positive measure, and let

\{\mathcal{D}_{t}\}_{t\in(0,T)}
be a family of subsets of
\Omega
. Suppose that

(a)

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is measurable;

(b)

for any
f\in L^{1}(\Omega\times(0,T))
,
the function
t\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}(x)|f(x,t)|dx
is integrable over
(0,T)
;

(c)

there exists a constant
D>0
, independent of
E
, such that for a.e.
t\in E
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}|a_{i}|^{2}\leq De^{D\sqrt{\lambda}}\Big(\|\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}a_{i}e_{i}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\Big)^{2}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\lambda>\lambda_{1}\;\;\mbox{and all}\;\;\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\subset\mathbb{R}.

Then there exists a constant
M=M(a,b,D)>0
such that for all
\theta\in(0,1)
, all
0<S_{1}<S_{2}\leq T
satisfying
|E\cap[S_{1},S_{2}]|>0
, and all
z\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\|e^{\mathcal{A}S_{2}}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq K(E,\theta,S_{1},S_{2})\Big(\frac{1}{|E\cap[S_{1},S_{2}]|}\int_{S_{1}}^{S_{2}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}Be^{\mathcal{A}t}z\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}dt\Big)^{1-\theta}\|z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\theta},

(3.6)

where

K(E,\theta,S_{1},S_{2}):=\frac{Me^{M\big(\frac{S_{2}}{1-\theta}+\frac{1}{\theta S_{1}}\big)}}{|E\cap[S_{1},S_{2}]|^{3}}.

Proof.

First, we emphasize that, under Assumptions
(a)
and
(b)
, the integral on the right-hand side of inequality (
3.6
) is well defined for any
z\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Our strategy is as follows: We first decompose the semigroup
\{e^{\mathcal{A}t}\}_{t\geq 0}

into high- and low-frequency components. Next, we establish a low-frequency observability inequality for the observation operator
\chi_{E}\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}B

(t\in(0,T))
, with an explicit observability constant depending on the measure of the observation set.
Finally, we combine this inequality with the decay property of the high-frequency component to derive the desired result.
The Remez-type inequality in Lemma
2.8
plays a crucial role in establishing the low-frequency observability inequality.

The proof proceeds in four steps. First,
we arbitrarily fix
S_{1},S_{2}\in(0,T]
with

S_{1}<S_{2}
and
|E\cap[S_{1},S_{2}]|>0
, as well as
\theta\in(0,1)
.
Set

E_{S_{1},S_{2}}:=E\cap[S_{1},S_{2}],\;\;\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}:=E_{S_{1},S_{2}}-S_{1}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;S:=S_{2}-S_{1}.

(3.7)

It is clear that
\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\subset[0,S]
and

|\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|=|E_{S_{1},S_{2}}|>0.

(3.8)

For each
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
, we define the following low-frequency subspace:

H_{\lambda}:=\Big\{f=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}a_{i}e_{i}:\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\Big\},

where
k_{\lambda}
is defined by (
2.1
).

Step 1: We prove that there exists a constant
N_{1}=N_{1}(b)>0
such that for all
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
,

\|e^{\mathcal{A}S}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{N_{1}(1+S)^{4}(1+\lambda)^{\frac{d}{4}}\lambda}{|\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4}}\int_{0}^{S}\chi_{\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(t)\|Be^{\mathcal{A}t}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}dt\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;\varphi\in H_{\lambda}.

(3.9)

To this end,
we arbitrarily fix
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
and
\varphi\in H_{\lambda}
.

We write

\varphi:=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\varphi_{1,j}e_{j},\sum_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\varphi_{2,j}e_{j}\Big)^{\top},

(3.10)

where

\{(\varphi_{1,j},\varphi_{2,j})^{\top}\}_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then, by (
3.2
) and (
3
), we have

Be^{\mathcal{A}t}\varphi=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}v_{j}(t)e_{j},

(3.11)

where

v_{j}(t):=e^{-a\lambda_{j}t}\Big(\varphi_{1,j}\cos(\lambda_{j}bt)+\varphi_{2,j}\sin(\lambda_{j}bt)\Big),\quad t\in[0,T].

(3.12)

Now,
we arbitrarily fix
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,k_{\lambda}\}
.

By (
3.8
) and (
3.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{S}\chi_{\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(t)|v_{j}(t)|dt

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{j}b)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{j}bS}\chi_{\widehat{E}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\sigma)e^{-ab^{-1}\sigma}|\varphi_{1,j}\cos\sigma+\varphi_{2,j}\sin\sigma|d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\lambda_{j}b)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{j}bS}\chi_{\widehat{E}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\sigma)e^{-ab^{-1}\sigma}|\varphi_{1,j}\cos\sigma+\varphi_{2,j}\sin\sigma|d\sigma&\mbox{if}\;\;b>0,\\
(\lambda_{j}|b|)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{j}|b|S}\chi_{\widehat{E}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\sigma)e^{-a|b|^{-1}\sigma}|\varphi_{1,j}\cos\sigma-\varphi_{2,j}\sin\sigma|d\sigma&\mbox{if}\;\;b<0,\end{cases}

where

\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}^{j}:=\Big\{\vartheta=\lambda_{j}|b|t:t\in\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\Big\}.

We use (
3.13
) to prove (
3.9
). First, we note that it suffices to consider
(
3.13
) for the case
b>0
. Indeed, by
(
3
), we have

\|e^{\mathcal{A}t}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})^{\top}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|e^{\mathcal{A}t}(\varphi_{1},-\varphi_{2})^{\top}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})^{\top}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Thus, via the transformation:
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})^{\top}\rightarrow(\varphi_{1},-\varphi_{2})^{\top}
, the case

b<0
can be reduced to the case
b>0
.
Therefore, in what follows, we assume that
b>0
.

From (
3.13
), we have

\int_{0}^{S}\chi_{\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(t)|v_{j}(t)|dt\geq(\lambda_{j}b)^{-1}\big((\varphi_{1,j})^{2}+(\varphi_{2,j})^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-a\lambda_{j}S}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{j}bS}\chi_{\widehat{E}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\sigma)|\sin(\sigma+\delta_{j})|d\sigma,

(3.14)

where
\delta_{j}\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
is defined by

\delta_{j}:=\begin{cases}\mbox{arctan}\big(\varphi_{1,j}(\varphi_{2,j})^{-1}\big)&\mbox{if}\;\;\varphi_{2,j}\neq 0,\\
\frac{\pi}{2}&\mbox{if}\;\;\varphi_{2,j}=0,\;\;\varphi_{1,j}>0,\\
-\frac{\pi}{2}&\mbox{if}\;\;\varphi_{2,j}=0,\;\;\varphi_{1,j}<0,\\
0&\mbox{if}\;\;\varphi_{1,j}=\varphi_{2,j}=0.\end{cases}

We next estimate the integral on the right-hand side of (
3.14
).
Let
\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}:=\widehat{E}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}+\delta_{j}
. Then

\int_{0}^{\lambda_{j}bS}\chi_{\widehat{E}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\sigma)|\sin(\sigma+\delta_{j})|d\sigma=\int_{\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta.

(3.15)

A key step in estimating the integral on the right-hand side of (
3.14
) is to prove that

2^{-50}\Big(\lambda_{j}bS+\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{-4}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4}\leq\int_{\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta.

(3.16)

This is achieved by considering the following two cases:

In the case that

\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}\leq\pi
,

we use the fact that
\delta_{j}\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
to obtain

\int_{\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta.

(3.17)

Let

I_{i}:=\Big[\big(-1+\frac{i}{4}\Big)\pi,\Big(-1+\frac{i+1}{4}\Big)\pi\Big],\;\;i\in\{0,1,\ldots,7\}.

(3.18)

Then,
[-\pi,\pi]=\cup_{i=0}^{7}I_{i}
. Choose

i^{*}\in\{0,1,\ldots,7\}
such that

|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\cap I_{i^{*}}|=\max_{0\leq i\leq 7}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\cap I_{i}|
.
It is clear that

|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\cap I_{i^{*}}|<\frac{\pi}{3}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\cap I_{i^{*}}|\geq\frac{1}{8}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|.

(3.19)

Since
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\sin\eta|d\eta=4
and
\sin(\frac{x}{4})-\frac{x}{16}>0
for each
x\in(0,\frac{\pi}{3})
, it follows from (
3.17
),
Lemma
2.8
and (
3.19
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{7}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\cap I_{i}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta\geq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\cap I_{i^{*}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2^{-24}\Big(\sin\Big(\frac{1}{4}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\cap I_{i^{*}}|\Big)\Big)^{4}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\sin\eta|d\eta\geq 2^{-50}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2^{-50}\Big(\lambda_{j}bS+\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{-4}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4}.

Hence, (
3.16
) holds in this case.

In the case that

\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}>\pi
,
we set

m_{j}:=\Big[\frac{\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}-\pi}{2\pi}\Big]_{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then

m_{j}\geq 0
, and

(2m_{j}+1)\pi\leq\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}<(2m_{j}+3)\pi.

(3.20)

Moreover,

\int_{\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta=\sum_{k=-1}^{m_{j}}\int_{(2k+1)\pi}^{(2k+3)\pi}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j,k}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta,

(3.21)

where

\widehat{F}^{j,k}_{S_{1},S_{2}}:=\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}\cap[(2k+1)\pi,(2k+3)\pi],\;\;k\in\{-1,0,\ldots,m_{j}\}.

Choose
k^{*}\in\{-1,0,\ldots,m_{j}\}
such that

|\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|=\max_{-1\leq k\leq m_{j}}|\widehat{F}^{j,k}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|
. Then

|\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|\geq(m_{j}+2)^{-1}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|.

(3.22)

We now claim that

\int_{(2k^{*}+1)\pi}^{(2k^{*}+3)\pi}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta\geq 2^{-50}|\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4}.

(3.23)

To this end, we define

G^{j,k^{*}}:=\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}-2(k^{*}+1)\pi.

Then
|G^{j,k^{*}}|=|\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|
,

G^{j,k^{*}}\subset[-\pi,\pi]
, and

\int_{(2k^{*}+1)\pi}^{(2k^{*}+3)\pi}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\chi_{G^{j,k^{*}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta.

(3.24)

Let

i^{*}\in\{0,\ldots,7\}
be such that

|G^{j,k^{*}}\cap I_{i^{*}}|=\max_{0\leq i\leq 7}|G^{j,k^{*}}\cap I_{i}|
,
where
I_{i}
for
i\in\{0,\ldots,7\}
is given in (
3.18
). Then

|G^{j,k^{*}}\cap I_{i^{*}}|<\frac{\pi}{3}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;|G^{j,k^{*}}\cap I_{i^{*}}|\geq\frac{1}{8}|G^{j,k^{*}}|.

(3.25)

It follows from (
3.24
),
Lemma
2.8
and (
3.25
) that

\displaystyle\int_{(2k^{*}+1)\pi}^{(2k^{*}+3)\pi}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\chi_{G^{j,k^{*}}\cap I_{i^{*}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta\geq

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2^{-22}\Big(\sin\Big(\frac{1}{4}|G^{j,k^{*}}\cap I_{i^{*}}|\Big)\Big)^{4}\geq 2^{-50}|G^{j,k^{*}}|^{4}=2^{-50}|\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4},

i.e., (
3.23
) holds.

Now, it follows from (
3.20
), (
3.21
), (
3.22
)
and (
3.23
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta\geq\int_{(2k^{*}+1)\pi}^{(2k^{*}+3)\pi}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j,k^{*}}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta\geq 2^{-50}(m_{j}+2)^{-4}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-50}\Big(\lambda_{j}bS+\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{-4}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4},

which yields (
3.16
) in this case. Therefore, combining the two cases, (
3.16
) holds.

Meanwhile, it follows from the definitions of
\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}
and

that

|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|=|\widehat{E}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|=\lambda_{j}b|\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|.

Hence,

\Big(\lambda_{j}bS+\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{-1}|\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|=\Big(S+\frac{\pi}{2\lambda_{j}b}\Big)^{-1}|\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|\geq\Big(S+\frac{\pi}{2\lambda_{1}b}\Big)^{-1}|\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|.

This, together with (

), implies

2^{-50}\Big(S+\frac{\pi}{2\lambda_{1}b}\Big)^{-4}|\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4}\leq\int_{\delta_{j}}^{\lambda_{j}bS+\delta_{j}}\chi_{\widehat{F}^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(\eta)|\sin\eta|d\eta.

(3.26)

Now, by (
3
), (
3.14
), (
3.15
) and (
3.26
), we have

\displaystyle k_{\lambda}\Big(\int_{0}^{S}\chi_{\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(t)\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}|v_{j}(t)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}dt\Big)^{2}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\Big(\int_{0}^{S}\chi_{\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(t)|v_{j}(t)|dt\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2^{-100}(\lambda b)^{-2}\Big(S+\frac{\pi}{2\lambda_{1}b}\Big)^{-8}|\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{8}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}((\varphi_{1,j})^{2}+(\varphi_{2,j})^{2})e^{-2\lambda_{j}aS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-100}(\lambda b)^{-2}\Big(S+\frac{\pi}{2\lambda_{1}b}\Big)^{-8}|\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{8}\|e^{\mathcal{A}S}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

However, it follows from (

) that there exists
a constant
C_{1}>0
(which only depends on

and
\Omega
) such that

.

), yields a constant

\displaystyle\|e^{\mathcal{A}S}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{C_{2}(1+S)^{4}(1+\lambda)^{\frac{d}{4}}\lambda}{|\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4}}\int_{0}^{S}\chi_{\widehat{E}_{S_{1},S_{2}}}(t)\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}|v_{j}(t)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}dt.

The above, combined with (
3.11
), yields (
3.9
) with
N_{1}=C_{2}
.

Step 2: We show that there exists a constant
N_{2}=N_{2}(b,D)>0
such that for all

,

\|e^{\mathcal{A}S_{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{N_{2}(1+S_{2}-S_{1})^{4}\lambda e^{N_{2}\sqrt{\lambda}}}{|E_{S_{1},S_{2}}|^{4}}\int_{S_{1}}^{S_{2}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}Be^{\mathcal{A}t}\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}dt\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\varphi\in H_{\lambda}.

(3.28)

We arbitrarily fix
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
and
\varphi\in H_{\lambda}
, which is given by (
3.10
). Therefore, by (
3.2
) and (
3
), we have (
3.11
).

We first show that

\int_{S_{1}}^{S_{2}}\chi_{E}(t)\|Be^{\mathcal{A}t}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}dt\leq De^{D\sqrt{\lambda}}\int_{S_{1}}^{S_{2}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}Be^{\mathcal{A}t}\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}dt.

(3.29)

Indeed, by Assumption
(c)
and (
3.11
), we have that for a.e.
t\in E
,

\displaystyle\chi_{E}(t)\|Be^{\mathcal{A}t}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}=\chi_{E}(t)\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}|v_{j}(t)|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D^{2}e^{2D\sqrt{\lambda}}\chi_{E}(t)\Big\|\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{\lambda}}v_{j}(t)e_{j}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}=D^{2}e^{2D\sqrt{\lambda}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}Be^{\mathcal{A}t}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})},

which yields (
3.29
).

We now prove (
3.28
).
Replacing
\varphi
with
e^{\mathcal{A}S_{1}}\varphi
in (

Remark 3.2
.

Theorem
3.1
establishes an integral-type interpolation observability inequality for the strongly coupled parabolic system (
1.1
) with single-component observation over measurable sets.
For weakly coupled parabolic systems, inequalities of this type over open subsets have been established in
[
15
, Lemma 2.6]
.
By contrast, to the best of our knowledge, the validity of such inequalities for strongly coupled parabolic systems has not been addressed in the existing literature.
Against this background, the following two issues are of particular interest:

(P1)

Does the following pointwise-in-time interpolation observability inequality hold?

\|e^{\mathcal{A}T}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|\chi_{\omega_{i}}Be^{\mathcal{A}S_{i}}z\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\right)^{1-\theta}\|z\|^{\theta}_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;z\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),

(3.36)

where
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,
\omega_{i}
(
i=1,2,\ldots,m
) are measurable subsets of
\Omega
with positive measure,
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset(0,T)
,
\theta\in(0,1)
, and
C>0
.

When
m=1
, inequality (
3.36
) is referred to as the single-time-point interpolation observability inequality; when
m>1
, it is referred to as the multi-time-point interpolation observability inequality.

When
\omega_{i}=\omega
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, where
\omega
is an open subset of
\Omega
, the
L^{2}
-type multi-time-point interpolation observability inequality (i.e., the observation term is measured in the
L^{2}
-norm) for weakly coupled parabolic systems was established in
[
18
, Proposition 1.3 and Lemma 5.1]
, under the assumptions that the pair formed by the coupling and observation matrices satisfies the Kalman rank condition and that sufficiently many observation time points are available. Therefore, it remains of interest to investigate whether such an inequality holds for strongly coupled systems.

(P2)

Do interpolation observability inequalities, either pointwise-in-time or of integral-type, remain valid for the following observation operators?

\widehat{B}:=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),\;\mbox{with}\;\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\;\mbox{and}\;|\mu_{1}|+|\mu_{2}|\neq 0;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\widetilde{B}:=I:=\mbox{diag}\{1,1\},

(3.37)

where
\widehat{B}
corresponds to observation along the direction
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
while
\widetilde{B}
corresponds to observation along directions
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
These cases are closely related
to the null controllability of the controlled parabolic equation with complex coefficients from measurable set:

\begin{cases}w_{t}=(a+\mathrm{i}b)\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}w+\chi_{\mathcal{D}}u&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega\times(0,T),\\
w=0&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
w(0)=w_{0}&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\end{cases}

(3.38)

where
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}))
,
w_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
,
\mathcal{D}
is
a measurable subset of
\Omega\times(0,T)
with
|\mathcal{D}|>0
and
\chi_{\mathcal{D}}
is the characteristic function of
\mathcal{D}
.
Indeed, the null controllability of system (
3.38
) is equivalent to the observability (with observation set
\Omega
) of system
(
1.3
) (see, for instance,
[
16
, Lemma 5.1]
for the abstract setting).
The choice
B=(1,0)
corresponds to the null controllability of (
3.38
) by a real-valued control acting on its real part. The choice
\widehat{B}
with
\mu_{2}\neq 0
represents a real-valued control acting through a genuinely complex direction. The case
\widetilde{B}
corresponds to a fully complex-valued control acting on both components.

The following propositions address the issues posed in Remark
3.2
. The first proposition shows that the single-time-point interpolation observability inequality (i.e., inequality (
3.36
) with
m=1
) does not hold.

Proposition 3.3
.

For each

We arbitrarily fix

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Two remarks on Proposition
3.4
are given.

(i)

In Proposition
3.4
, we show that, regardless of how many observation time points are used, the pointwise-in-time interpolation observability inequality (

(5.3)

where
\mathcal{A}
is defined in (
3.1
).

5.1
L^{\infty}
-null controllability

We consider the null controllability of system (
5.1
).
The following results are direct consequences of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

It holds that
L>0
, where

L:=\inf_{y_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\chi_{\mathcal{D}}(x,t)|y_{1}(x,t;y_{0})|dxdt}{\|y(T;y_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}}.

(5.4)

(ii)

For each
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, there exists a control
u\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, satisfying

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\leq L^{-1}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})},

such that the solution

v(\cdot;u,v_{0})
to (
5.1
) satisfies

v(T;u,v_{0})=0
.

Proof.

We first prove
(i)
. Let
N>0
be given such that (
1.2
) holds, whose existence is guaranteed by Theorem

1.1
. Then, we have

\frac{\int_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\chi_{\mathcal{D}}(x,t)|y_{1}(x,t;y_{0})|dxdt}{\|y(T;y_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}}\geq N^{-1}\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;y_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\}.

By (
5.4
), it follows that
L\geq N^{-1}>0
, which proves
(i)
.

We now prove
(ii)
. From
(i)
, we have

\|y(T;y_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq L^{-1}\int_{\Omega\times(0,T)}\chi_{\mathcal{D}}(x,t)|y_{1}(x,t;y_{0})|dxdt\quad\text{for all }y_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

By the standard duality argument (see, for instance,
[
16
, Lemma 5.1]
),
(ii)
is well known. Thus, we omit its proof.
∎

5.2
Time-optimal control problem

Let

\mathcal{S}
be a target set, which is assumed to be closed in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
We fix an arbitrary
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\mathcal{S}
and consider the following time-optimal control problem:

\mbox{{(TP)}}\;\;T^{*}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}}\{T>0:v(T;u,v_{0})\in\mathcal{S}\},

where
v(\cdot;u,v_{0})
stands for the solution to (
5.2
). In what follows, we investigate the bang-bang property of optimal controls for problem
(TP)
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
T^{*}
and
u^{*}
be the optimal time and an optimal control for
(TP)
, respectively. Then
u^{*}

possesses the bang-bang property, i.e.,

u^{*}(x,t)\in\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\}
for a.e.
(x,t)\in\omega\times(0,T^{*})
.

Proof.

Since
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\mathcal{S}
, it is clear that
T^{*}>0
.

We now argue by contradiction and suppose that the bang-bang property does not hold.
Then, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a set
\mathcal{D}\subset\omega\times(0,T^{*})
of positive measure such that

\nu_{1}+\varepsilon<u^{*}(x,t)<\nu_{2}-\varepsilon\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;(x,t)\in\mathcal{D}.

(5.5)

We aim to find
\delta^{\sharp}\in(0,T^{*})
and
w^{\sharp}\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}
such that

v(T^{*}-\delta^{\sharp};w^{\sharp},v_{0})=v(T^{*};u^{*},v_{0})(\in\mathcal{S}).

(5.6)

Once this is achieved, we reach a contradiction to the optimality of
T^{*}
,
which completes the proof.

To prove (
5.6
), we first establish some preliminary results.
By Lemma
2.3
, there exist
R>0
and
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathcal{D}
such that

B_{4R}^{d+1}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset\Omega\times(0,T^{*});\;\;|B_{R}^{d+1}(x_{0},t_{0})\cap\mathcal{D}|>0.

(5.7)

We define the following set and its time slices:

\mathcal{D}^{R}:=B_{R}^{d+1}(x_{0},t_{0})\cap\mathcal{D};\;\;\;{\mathcal{D}^{R}}_{t}:=\{x\in\Omega:(x,t)\in\mathcal{D}^{R}\}\;\;\mbox{with}\;\;t\in(0,T^{*}),

and further define the set

E^{R}:=\left\{t\in(0,T^{*}):|{\mathcal{D}^{R}}_{t}|\geq\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{R}|}{2T^{*}}\right\}.

By the above definitions and (
5.7
), we apply Lemma
2.5
to obtain
the following properties:

(c_{1})

{\mathcal{D}^{R}}_{t}
is measurable for a.e.
t\in(0,T^{*})
;

(c_{2})

the function
t\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}^{R}}_{t}}(x)dx
is integrable over
(0,T^{*})
;

(c_{3})

E^{R}
is measurable and
|E^{R}|\geq\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{R}|}{2|B_{R}^{d}|}
.

By
(c_{3})
, there exists
0<\delta_{0}<\min\{1,T^{*}\}
such that

|E^{R}_{\delta}|\geq\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{R}|}{4|B_{R}^{d}|}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}],\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;E^{R}_{\delta}:=(E^{R}-\delta)\cap\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(5.8)

Moreover, for all
0\leq\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}\leq\delta_{0}
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
,

\mathcal{Q}^{R}_{\delta_{2}}\subset\mathcal{Q}^{R}_{\delta_{1}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\mathcal{Q}^{R}_{\delta}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\delta}:=\{(x,t-\delta)\in\Omega\times(0,T^{*}-\delta):(x,t)\in\mathcal{D}\},

(5.9)

where

\mathcal{Q}^{R}_{\delta}:=\{(x,t):t\in E^{R}_{\delta},\;x\in{{\mathcal{D}}^{R}}_{t+\delta}\},\;\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}].

By the definition of
E^{R}_{\delta}
,
(c_{1})
and
(c_{2})
, we have that, for each
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
,
{\mathcal{D}^{R}}_{t+\delta}

is measurable for a.e.
t\in(0,T^{*}-\delta)
, and the function
t\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}^{R}}_{t+\delta}}(x)dx
is integrable over
(0,T^{*}-\delta)
. This implies that
\mathcal{Q}^{R}_{\delta}
is measurable for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
.

We claim that

|\mathcal{Q}^{R}_{\delta}|>0\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}].

(5.10)

Indeed, combining
(c_{1})
,
(c_{2})
with (
5.8
) and the definition of

E^{R}
,
we obtain

|\mathcal{Q}^{R}_{\delta}|=\int_{0}^{T^{*}-\delta}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{E^{R}_{\delta}}(t)\chi_{{\mathcal{D}^{R}}_{t+\delta}}(x)dxdt\geq\frac{|\mathcal{D}^{R}|^{2}}{8T^{*}|B_{R}^{d}|}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}],

which yields (
5.10
).

We now proceed to construct the desired quantity

\delta^{\sharp}
and control
w^{\sharp}
. To this end, we first observe from (
5.3
) that for all

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
and
w\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}
,

\displaystyle v(T^{*};u^{*},v_{0})-v(T^{*}-\delta;w,v_{0})

(5.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{A}^{*}(T^{*}-\delta)}v_{\delta}+\int_{0}^{T^{*}-\delta}e^{\mathcal{A}^{*}(T^{*}-\delta-s)}\chi_{\omega}B^{\top}[u^{*}(s+\delta)-w(s)]ds,

where

v_{\delta}:=(e^{\mathcal{A}^{*}\delta}-I)v_{0}+\int_{0}^{\delta}e^{\mathcal{A}^{*}(\delta-s)}\chi_{\omega}B^{\top}u^{*}(s)ds.

(5.12)

For each
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, let
L_{\delta}
be defined by (
5.4
) where
T
and
\mathcal{D}
are replaced by
T^{*}-\delta

and
Q_{\delta}^{R}
, respectively.
A direct verification shows that

0<L_{\delta_{2}}\leq L_{\delta_{1}},\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}\leq\delta_{0}.

(5.13)

For each
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, using (
5.10
) and (
5.13
), we apply Theorem
5.1
(with
\mathcal{D}
and
T
replaced by
Q^{R}_{\delta}
and
T^{*}-\delta
,
respectively) to obtain
a control

f_{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T^{*}-\delta);\mathbb{R})
such that

\|f_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T^{*}-\delta);\mathbb{R})}\leq(L_{\delta_{0}})^{-1}\|v_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(5.14)

and

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{A}^{*}(T^{*}-\delta)}v_{\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T^{*}-\delta}e^{\mathcal{A}^{*}(T^{*}-\delta-s)}\chi_{\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}^{R}}(\cdot,s)B^{\top}f_{\delta}(s)ds

(5.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T^{*}-\delta}e^{\mathcal{A}^{*}(T^{*}-\delta-s)}\chi_{\omega}B^{\top}\left(\chi_{\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}^{R}}(\cdot,s)f_{\delta}(s)\right)ds.

(Here, we used the inclusion
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}^{R}\subset\omega\times(0,T^{*})
, which follows from (
5.9
) and the assumption
\mathcal{D}\subset\omega\times(0,T^{*})
.)

By (
5.12
), there exists
\delta^{\sharp}\in(0,\delta_{0}]
such that

\|v_{\delta^{\sharp}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon L_{\delta_{0}}.

(5.16)

We then define

w^{\sharp}(x,t):=\begin{cases}u^{*}(x,t+\delta^{\sharp})+\chi_{\mathcal{Q}_{\delta^{\sharp}}^{R}}(x,t)f_{\delta^{\sharp}}(x,t)&\mbox{if}\;\;(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T^{*}-\delta^{\sharp}],\\
\frac{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}}{2}&\mbox{if}\;\;(x,t)\in\Omega\times(T^{*}-\delta^{\sharp},+\infty).\end{cases}

(5.17)

Since
u^{*}
,
\chi_{\mathcal{Q}_{\delta^{\sharp}}^{R}}
and
f_{\delta^{\sharp}}
are measurable, it is clear that

w^{\sharp}
is measurable over
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

We now claim that

\displaystyle\nu_{1}\leq w^{\sharp}(x,t)\leq\nu_{2}\;\;\mbox{for a.e.}\;\;(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(5.18)

To prove (
5.18
), it suffices to show

\displaystyle w^{\sharp}(x,t)\in\left[\nu_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\nu_{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]\;\;\mbox{for a.e.}\;\;(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}_{\delta^{\sharp}}^{R},

(5.19)

since we have

\displaystyle w^{\sharp}(x,t)=u^{*}(x,t+\delta^{\sharp})\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;(x,t)\in(\Omega\times(0,T^{*}-\delta^{\sharp}])\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{\delta^{\sharp}}^{R};

\displaystyle w^{\sharp}(x,t)=\frac{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}}{2}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;(x,t)\in\Omega\times(T^{*}-\delta^{\sharp},+\infty).

To prove (
5.19
), we first use (
5.9
) to obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{Q}_{\delta^{\sharp}}^{R}}(x,t)\leq\chi_{\mathcal{D}_{\delta^{\sharp}}}(x,t)=\chi_{\mathcal{D}}(x,t+\delta^{\sharp})\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T^{*}-\delta^{\sharp}].

Then by the above observation and (
5.5
), we have

\displaystyle\nu_{1}+\varepsilon<u^{*}(x,t+\delta^{\sharp})<\nu_{2}-\varepsilon\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}_{\delta^{\sharp}}^{R}.

(5.20)

Meanwhile, by (
5.14
) and (
5.16
), we see

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq f_{\delta^{\sharp}}(x,t)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\;\;\mbox{for a.e.}\;\;(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}_{\delta^{\sharp}}^{R}.

(5.21)

By (
5.20
), (
5.21
) and (
5.17
), we obtain (
5.19
),
which implies (
5.18
).

Finally, it follows from the measurability of
w^{\sharp}
and (
5.18
) that
w^{\sharp}\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}
.
Moreover, by (
5.11
), (
5.12
), (
5.15
) and (
5.17
), we obtain
(
5.6
).
This completes the proof.
∎
