Perfect matchings, Fano planes, and orthogonal bases of type E 8 E_{8}
R.M. Green and Tianyuan Xu

Abstract
. We use perfect matchings and labelled Fano planes to construct and study the 2025 2025 orthogonal bases of positive roots in the E 8 E_{8} root system. The set of these bases forms a highly structured, Bruhat-like graded poset ( Ω , ≤ Q ) (\Omega,\leq_{Q}) whose rank function can be computed from the cardinalities of so-called generalized Rothe diagrams. We give combinatorial characterizations of these diagrams in terms of matchings and Fano planes, and we explain how to compute the ranks of the elements of Ω \Omega using suitable combinatorial statistics such as the weights of perfect matchings. We establish simple formulas for the rank generating functions of Ω \Omega and of its 50 congruence classes under a natural order congruence relation. Our derivation of the generating functions contains some intermediate results on general perfect matchings and labelled Fano planes that can be stated without mentioning root systems and may be of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to use the combinatorics of perfect matchings and
Fano planes to gain insight into certain combinatorial structures associated
with the
E_{8}
root system
\Phi=\Phi(E_{8})
. Recall that the
E_{8}
lattice gives
the densest possible packing of spheres in
8
-dimensional Euclidean space

[
16
]
, and that the
E_{8}
root system consists of the
240

elements of the lattice with minimal nonzero norm. The roots can be partitioned
into
120
positive and
120
negative roots by choosing a vector space total
order on the ambient vector space
V
. There are
2025
orthogonal bases for
V

that consist entirely of positive roots, and the set
\Omega
of all such bases
appears in the physics literature, where it has been used to give parity proofs
of the Kochen–Specker theorem in quantum mechanics
[
18
]
.

The set
\Omega
is naturally a
W
-set for the Weyl group
W=W(E_{8})
associated to

\Phi
(Section
2.2
). In previous work
[
5
]
, we showed that

\Omega
can be identified with a natural spanning set of a certain Macdonald
representation of
W

[
5
, Section 1]
. We defined a certain function

\lambda:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
that equips
\Omega
with the structure of a quasiparabolic

W
-set, which means that
\Omega
forms a special type of
W
-set defined by
Rains–Vazirani
[
10
]
that carries a graded, Bruhat-like partial order
whose rank function is given by
\lambda
. The function
\lambda
is called the
level function in the context of quasiparabolic sets, and it can be used to
deform the
W
-action on
\Omega
in a suitable way to create a module for the
Iwahori–Hecke algebra associated to
W

[
10
, Theorem 7.1]
. In

[
5
, Section 7.1]
, we showed using a computer that the generating function

PS_{X}(q)=\sum_{R\in\Omega}q^{\lambda(R)}
, known as the Poincaré polynomial of

\Omega
, has a nice factorization

(1.1)

PS_{\Omega}(q)=[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[9]_{q}[15]_{q}

into the quantum integers

[d]_{q}=1+q+q^{2}+\dots+q^{d-1}
for
d\in\{3,5,9,15\}
. In
[
6
]
, we defined
a generalized Rothe diagram,
\mathscr{D}(R)
, for every element

, and we
showed that
\lambda(R)
equals the cardinality of
\mathscr{D}(R)
, thus giving

.

The results mentioned in the last paragraph have all been developed in the more
general setting of so-called

-roots
[

, Section 2.2]

these results have direct analogues in type

the generalized Rothe diagram in the more general setting. The
main contribution of this paper is an extension of this combinatorial framework
to type

To be more precise, we introduce two combinatorial models that make it possible
to understand the orthogonal bases in
\Omega
in the same concrete way that
perfect matchings allow us to understand the orthogonal bases in type

.
These are the “matching model” and “Fano model”, and they provide a neat

. We show that there exists a partition

into two parts, where

can be naturally and
bijectively constructed from the 945 perfect matchings of the size-10 set

(Definition

(Definition

We use these models to give precise descriptions for the generalized Rothe

\Omega

), and we use

arising from a perfect matching

As applications of the matching and Fano models for

The results of this paper have natural analogues for the root system of type

2.1.
The

The primary object of study for this paper is the root system

There are several well-known constructions of the root system

Each element of
\Phi

Remark 2.1

As explained in

2.2.
Orthogonal bases

We call a subset of the root system
\Phi=\Phi(E_{8})

if its elements are
pairwise orthogonal. We recall from
[
5

6

some key definitions and

in this subsection. Our
focus will be on the set

\Omega=\{R\subseteq\Phi_{+}:\lvert R\rvert=8\text{ and $R$ is orthogonal}\}.

The elements of

are called “positive

-roots” in
[

The set

for all

Definition 2.5
.

[
10

Let

be a Coxeter system and

-set
is a pair

-set and

, such that

for all

. A

, or

, is a scaled

, then

;

for any

,

, if
\lambda(rx)>\lambda(x)
and
\lambda(srx)<\lambda(sx)
, then

.

whenever

,

, and
\lambda(x)\leq\lambda(rx)
. The

The level function
\lambda

4.1.

Recall that the Fano plane is the unique projective plane

. The simple reflections

, and

, and

5.2.
Generating functions

Maintain the notation of Section
5.1
. We are now ready to
prove Theorem
2.15
. Recall that the theorem asserts that for any
sequence
\underline{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})\in\mathcal{S}
and the element
\gamma=\Psi(\underline{d})\in\Sigma
, the level generating function for the
\sigma
-class in
\Omega

corresponding to
d
can be expressed as

(5.5)

\sum_{R\in\sigma^{-1}(\gamma)}q^{\rho(R)}=q^{\lvert\underline{d}\rvert}\prod_{i=1}^{4}[d_{i}+1]_{q},

where
\lvert\underline{d}\rvert=d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}+d_{4}
.

Proof of Theorem
2.15
.

Let
\underline{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})\in\mathcal{S}
and
\gamma=\Psi(\underline{d})
.
By Proposition
5.7
, we have

\gamma=4e_{9}+2\sum_{i=1}^{4}e_{2i-d_{i}}
if
\underline{d}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}

and
\gamma=4e_{9}+2e_{8-d_{4}}
if
\underline{d}\in\mathcal{S}_{F}
.
Let

\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}=\{M\in\mathcal{M}:\text{the left endpoints of
$M$ are $0,2-d_{i},4-d_{2},6-d_{3},$ and $8-d_{4}$}\}

and

\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:\min(L^{c}(F))=8-d_{4}\}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:L^{c}(F)=\{8-d_{4},y\},\text{where $8-d_{4}<y\leq 9$}\}.

By Lemma
3.3
, Lemma
4.4
, Corollary
4.6
,
and Proposition
5.7
, we have

\sigma^{-1}(\gamma)=\{R(M):M\in\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}\}\subseteq\Omega_{M}

if
d\in\mathcal{S}_{M}

and

\sigma^{-1}(\gamma)=\{R(F):F\in\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}\}\subseteq\Omega_{F}

if
d\in\mathcal{S}_{F}
.

Suppose that
\underline{d}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
. Let
K=\psi(\underline{d})
and
\underline{d}^{\prime}_{K}=(d^{\prime}_{1},d^{\prime}_{2},d^{\prime}_{3},d^{\prime}_{4})
be
as in Lemma
5.5
, so that
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
consists of the elements of

\mathcal{M}
with right endpoints
r_{1}:=d^{\prime}_{1}+1,r_{2}:=d^{\prime}_{2}+3,r_{3}:=d^{\prime}_{3}+5,r_{4}:=d^{\prime}_{4}+7
, and
r_{5}:=9
by Lemma
5.5
. In
[
19
, Section
3.3]
, Watson defines an equivalence relation denoted
\Delta
on the
set of all perfect matchings of the set
[2n]=\{1,2,\dots,2n\}
for any
positive integer
n
, where each equivalence class for
\Delta
consists of all
perfect matchings of the set
[2n]
with a fixed set of right endpoints. (Watson
denotes such right endpoints as “
d_{i}(\delta)
”, where
\delta
refers to
certain Dyck paths associated with perfect matching and the symbol “
d_{i}
” is
not to be confused with our notation for the entries of the sequences
\underline{d}\in\mathcal{S}
.) It follows from the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 1]
that

\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}}q^{\wt(M)}=q^{\sum_{i=1}^{4}(r_{i}-(2i-1))}\prod_{i=1}^{4}[r_{i}-(2i-1)+1]_{q}=q^{d^{\prime}_{1}+d^{\prime}_{2}+d^{\prime}_{3}+d^{\prime}_{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}[d^{\prime}_{i}+1]_{q},

where
\wt
is the the weight function we defined in Definition

3.5
(iii). We have
\wt(M)=h(M)
by Proposition

3.8
and
h(M)=\rho(R(M))
for any
M\in\mathcal{M}
by Proposition

3.11
, where
h
is the height function from Definition

3.5
(ii), so it follows that

\sum_{R\in\sigma^{-1}(\gamma)}q^{\rho(R)}=q^{d^{\prime}_{1}+d^{\prime}_{2}+d^{\prime}_{3}+d^{\prime}_{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}[d^{\prime}_{i}+1]_{q}.

The fact that
\{\!\{d_{i}:1\leq i\leq 4\}\!\}=\{\!\{d^{\prime}_{i}:1\leq i\leq 4\}\!\}

by Lemma
5.4
(ii) then implies that

\sum_{R\in\sigma^{-1}(\gamma)}q^{\rho(R)}=q^{d^{\prime}_{1}+d^{\prime}_{2}+d^{\prime}_{3}+d^{\prime}_{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}[d^{\prime}_{i}+1]_{q}=q^{d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}+d_{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}[d_{i}+1]_{q}=q^{\lvert\underline{d}\rvert}\prod_{i=1}^{4}[d_{i}+1]_{q},

which proves Equation
(
5.5
) in this case.

Now suppose that
\underline{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4})\in\mathcal{S}_{F}
, so that
\underline{d}=(1,2,4,j)
for some

0\leq j\leq 7
, and
\gamma=4e_{9}+2e_{8-j}
by Proposition
5.7

(ii). By
[
4
, Proposition 3.7, Proposition 4.12]
, if
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the
set of all
30
inequivalent labellings of the Fano plane with labels from a
fixed
7
-element set, then we have

(5.6)

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}q^{h(F)}=[2]_{q}[3]_{q}[5]_{q}.

Define

\mathcal{F}_{y}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}:L^{c}(F)=\{8-j,y\}\}

for each
8-j<y\leq 9
. We have

\rho(R(F))=24-(8-j)-y+h(F)=h(F)+(j+16)-y,

for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{y}

by Proposition
4.10
, and it then follows from Equation (
5.6
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\sigma^{-1}(\gamma)}q^{\rho(R)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y:8-j<y\leq 9}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{y}}q^{h(F)+(j+16)-y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[2]_{q}[3]_{q}[5]_{q}\cdot\left(\sum_{y:-9\leq-y<{j-8}}q^{(j+16)-y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[2]_{q}[3]_{q}[5]_{q}\cdot\left(\sum_{z=j+7}^{2j+7}q^{z}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{j+7}[2]_{q}[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[j+1]_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\lvert\underline{d}\rvert}\prod_{i=1}^{4}[d_{i}+1]_{q}.

This completes the proof. ∎

Theorem 5.8
.

We have

(5.7)

\sum_{R\in\Omega_{M}}q^{\rho(R)}=[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[7]_{q}[9]_{q}

and

(5.8)

\sum_{R\in\Omega_{F}}q^{\rho(R)}=q^{7}[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[8]_{q}[9]_{q}.

The Poincaré series of
\Omega
is given by

PS_{\Omega}(q)=[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[9]_{q}[15]_{q}.

Proof.

As explained in the proof of Theorem
2.15
, we have

\sum_{R\in\Omega_{M}}q^{\rho(R)}=\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}}q^{\wt(M)},

where the generating function on the right hand side is also studied in

[
19
]
. It follows from
[
19
, Theorem 1]
that
\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}}q^{\wt(M)}=[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[7]_{q}[9]_{q}
, and Equation (
5.7
)
follows.

We have
\Omega_{F}=\cup_{\underline{d}\in\mathcal{S}_{F}}\sigma^{-1}(\Psi(\underline{d}))
where

\mathcal{S}_{F}=\{(1,2,4,j):0\leq j\leq 7\}
by Lemma

4.4
, Corollary
4.6
, and Proposition

5.7
(ii). A routine calculation shows that

\sum_{k=1}^{8}q^{k-1}[k]_{q}=\frac{[8]_{q}[9]_{q}}{[2]_{q}},

and it then follows that

\sum_{R\in\Omega_{F}}q^{\rho(R)}=\sum_{j=0}^{7}q^{j+7}[2][3][5][j+1]_{q}=q^{7}[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[8]_{q}[9]_{q}.

This proves the first sentence of the theorem.

It follows from the definition of quantum integers that

[7]_{q}+q^{7}[8]_{q}=[15]_{q}
. Since
\Omega
is the disjoint union of
\Omega_{M}
and

\Omega_{F}
, Equations (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) imply that

PS_{\Omega}(q)=\sum_{R\in\Omega}q^{\rho(R)}=[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[7]_{q}[9]_{q}+q^{7}[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[8]_{q}[9]_{q}=[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[9]_{q}[15]_{q},

which completes the proof.
∎

6.
Concluding remarks

Remark 6.1
.

The results of this paper have direct analogues for the root system

\Phi(E_{7})
of type
E_{7}
and the collection
\Omega^{\prime}
of the positive orthogonal
bases of type
E_{7}
, i.e., the collection of size-7 subsets of
\Phi(E_{7})
that
consist of
mutually orthogonal positive roots. The set
\Omega^{\prime}
is a quasiparabolic

W(E_{7})
-set of size
135
by the results of
[
5
,
6
]
, and we can mimic
the methods of this paper to decompose
\Omega^{\prime}
as the disjoint union of two
subsets
\Omega_{M}^{\prime}
and
\Omega_{F}^{\prime}
, where
\Omega_{M}^{\prime}
contains 105 orthogonal bases modelled by the perfect
matchings of the set
\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}
and
\Omega_{F}^{\prime}
contains 30
orthogonal bases modelled by the 30 inequivalent labellings of the Fano plane using
labels from the set
\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}
. The map
\sigma
has
15
fibres,
with
\Omega_{F}^{\prime}
forming a single fibre and

\Omega_{M}^{\prime}
being the union of 14 fibres that correspond naturally to the 14 Dyck
paths of semilength 4. More generally, we can uniformly index all the 15 fibres
of
\sigma
by 15 suitable length-3 sequences
obtained via a chain decomposition of the heap of a fully commutative
element, namely, the so-called “nonnesting element” of type
E_{7}
from

[
5
, Section 6.2]
. This indexing scheme and the
decomposition
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega_{M}^{\prime}\cup\Omega_{F}^{\prime}
are new, and they can be used to prove that

PS_{\Omega^{\prime}}(q)=[3]_{q}[5]_{q}[9]_{q}
.

Remark 6.2
.

As mentioned in the introduction, there are many interesting examples of
quasiparabolic sets that can be constructed from the orthogonal sets of roots of
type
E_{n}
, including certain configurations of curves in del Pezzo surfaces of
degrees 2 and 3. The poset
(\Omega,\leq)
is the largest of these exceptional
configurations, and the results of this paper can be used to gain insight into
these smaller examples by restriction. For example, instead of mimicking the
methods of this paper to prove the facts about the set
\Omega_{7}
in Remark

6.1
from scratch, one may also deduce them from the results of this
paper by identifying
\Phi(E_{7})
as the set of roots
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{8}c_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\Phi(E_{8})
where
c_{8}=0
, or as the set of roots in
\Phi(E_{8})
that
are orthogonal to the highest root
\theta
of
\Phi(E_{8})
. For another example,
one can use the matching and Fano models for
\Omega
to study the set
T(E_{8},8)

from
[
6
, Section 5.1]
. This is a set of 630 quadruples of pairwise
orthogonal roots that are closely related to the aforementioned curve
configurations and that form another quasiparabolic set for
W(E_{8})
. We can use
the matching and Fano models to obtain the level generating function for
T(E_{8},8)
established in
[
6
, Remark 5.10 (i)]
.

Remark 6.3
.

Corollary
5.6
and its proof can be readily generalized to show that for
any positive integer
k
and
any nonnesting perfect matching of the set
\{1,2,\dots,2k\}

with left endpoints
1<l_{1}<l_{2}<\cdots<l_{k-1}
and right endpoints

r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots<r_{k-1}<2k
, we have the multiset equality

\{\!\{2i-l_{i}:1\leq i\leq{k-1}\}\!\}=\{\!\{r_{i}-(2i-1):1\leq i\leq k-1\}\!\}.

This generalization may be of independent interest in that it is a simple but
non-obvious equality concerning the endpoints of perfect matchings. It is also
interesting that the statement of this equality does not involve root systems or
even any permutation actions, even though our proof of Corollary
5.6

relied heavily on the actions of permutations.
