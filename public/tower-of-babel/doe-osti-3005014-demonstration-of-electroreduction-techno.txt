Nature of Work
Describe the research (summary of Scope of Work and principal objectives of the CRADA):
Global Nuclear Fuel – Americas LLC (GNF) has partnered with Argonne National Laboratory to demonstrate
electroreduction of uranium oxides produced by deconversion of UF 6 to uranium metal through the Gateway for
Accelerated Innovation in Nuclear (GAIN) program under the U.S. Department of Energy to accelerate the domestic
production of metallic advanced reactor fuels.

ANL-1060 (02/13/2023)

Electroreduction of uranium oxide was first demonstrated and patented by Argonne in the early 2000s as a
technology to convert used oxide nuclear fuel from light water reactors to metal for further fuel reprocessing. More
recently, electroreduction has been proposed as a front-end technology for metallization of uranium oxides produced
by deconversion of UF6 and for scrap recovery of oxide materials.

During the electroreduction process, UO2 powder is contained in a stainless-steel mesh basket with a cathode lead
located in the center of the UO2 bed. The basket is immersed in lithium chloride molten salt electrolyte containing
1 wt% lithium oxide along with a platinum anode and a nickel/nickel oxide (Ni/NiO) reference electrode. Current is
applied to the cell between the cathode and anode to reduce the UO2 to metallic uranium via a solid-state reduction
reaction. Oxide ions released from the UO2 during reduction are transported through the salt to the anode where
oxygen gas is evolved. Once reduction is complete, the basket containing the metallicized uranium is removed from
the salt and can be processed to remove the salt and consolidate the uranium into an ingot for use in metallic fuel
fabrication.

This project was performed to provide evidence of the electroreduction technology readiness level for metallization
of UO2 powder, identify and retire technical risks for industrialization of electroreduction, and accelerate the path to
commercialization for metallic fast reactor fuel production. To that end, five electroreduction tests were performed
with UO2 provided by GNF and the resulting product was analyzed for the extent of conversion to metal and for
impurity contents of the metal product to verify that electroreduction does not introduce impurities that would prevent
use of the product in metallic fuel fabrication.

Conclusions drawn from this CRADA; include any major accomplishments:
This document serves as a summary report for the work conducted under GAIN Voucher CRADA A24534 between
Argonne National Laboratory and Global Nuclear Fuel – Americas LLC (GNF) to demonstrate the efficacy of
electroreduction of GNF-sourced UO2 to uranium metal. Five electroreduction tests were performed and the
products were assessed for the extent of reduction and product impurity concentrations. The results of those tests
demonstrated that high extent of reduction and minimal product contamination of the uranium metal product was
achieved by electroreduction of UO2 produced from UF6 deconversion.

Test were performed with up to 0.33 kg UO2 and an applied current maximum of 16 A. The UO2 was obtained from
GNF’s manufacturing line immediately after deconversion of UF6 to UO2. This powder was extremely fine and
electroreduction of fine powders has not been previously demonstrated. The electroreduction tests confirmed that
the particle size did not affect the electroreduction operation nor the extent of reduction achieved. On average, the
extent of reduction from UO2 to uranium metal was 97.6 ± 0.41%. Extent of reduction was determined by two
independent chemical analyses, combustion analysis and uranium metal dissolution with ethyl bromine acetate
followed by inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectroscopy. These tests demonstrated that highly
metallized products are achievable by electroreduction.

The impurity content of each product was analyzed and depended strongly on the efficiency of oxygen gas removal.
Tests 1 and 2 had low off-gas flowrates that resulted in excess oxygen gas head space and some corrosion of the
stainless-steel crucible. As a result, minor Fe contamination was observed in the uranium product from those tests.
Tests 3 and 5 were performed with high off-gas flow rates and resulted in products with Fe content below the ICP-

MS level of detection, demonstrating the viability of uranium metallization using electroreduction technology at an

Overall, these results demonstrate that electroreduction of UO2 powder produced by deconversion of UF6 can be
used to produce metallic uranium that is suitable for further processing to support commercialization of metallic fuel
production.
