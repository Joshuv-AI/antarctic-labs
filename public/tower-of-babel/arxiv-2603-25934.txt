Abstract
In this work, we investigate how to develop sharp concentration inequalities for sub-Weibull random variables, including sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential distributions. Although the random variables may not be sub-Guassian, the tail probability around the origin behaves as if they were sub-Gaussian, and the tail probability decays align with the Orlicz Ψ α \Psi_{\alpha} -tail elsewhere. Specifically, for independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) { X i } i = 1 n \{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n} with finite Orlicz norm ‖ X ‖ Ψ α \|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}} , our theory unveils that there is an interesting phase transition at α = 2 \alpha=2 in that ℙ ⁡ ( | ∑ i = 1 n X i | ≥ t ) {\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right) with t > 0 t>0 is upper bounded by 2 ​ exp ⁡ ( − C ​ max ⁡ { t 2 n ​ ‖ X ‖ Ψ α 2 , t α n α − 1 ​ ‖ X ‖ Ψ α α } ) 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha-1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}}\right\}\right) for α ≥ 2 \alpha\geq 2 , and by 2 ​ exp ⁡ ( − C ​ min ⁡ { t 2 n ​ ‖ X ‖ Ψ α 2 , t α n α − 1 ​ ‖ X ‖ Ψ α α } ) 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha-1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}}\right\}\right) for 1 ≤ α ≤ 2 1\leq\alpha\leq 2 with some positive constant C C . In many scenarios, it is often necessary to distinguish the standard deviation from the Orlicz norm when the latter can exceed the former greatly. To accommodate this, we build a new theoretical analysis framework, and our sharp, flexible concentration inequalities involve the variance and a mixing of Orlicz Ψ α \Psi_{\alpha} -tails through the min and max functions. Our theory yields new, improved concentration inequalities even for the cases of sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential distributions with α = 2 \alpha=2 and 1 1 , respectively. We further demonstrate our theory on martingales, random vectors, random matrices, and covariance matrix estimation. These sharp concentration inequalities can empower more precise non-asymptotic analyses across different statistical and machine learning applications.

Introduction

Concentration inequalities are central to modern statistics and machine learning, especially in problems where tail probabilities determine finite-sample performance, such as matrix analysis
(

X
whose tails decay at an exponential–Weibull rate

{\mathbb{P}}\left(|X|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{\alpha}}{K}\right),

where

are constants, and

\alpha=2
corresponds to sub-Gaussian distributions, while the case of
\alpha=1
corresponds to sub-exponential distributions. Throughout the paper, we work with random variables having finite Orlicz
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm, defined as follows.

Definition 1

(Orlicz
\|\cdot\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
-norm)

For a given random variable

\|\cdot\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}

\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}:=\inf_{u>0}\left\{{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(|X|/u)^{\alpha}\right\}\leq 2\right\}.

A large literature has contributed to developing concentration theory for sub-Weibull random variables; see, e.g.,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)

for random variables
X_{i}
’s and

, the existing results in the literature still leave an important gap. On the one hand, for
\alpha>2
, existing concentration inequalities either require information
stronger
than
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
or
fail
to deliver a sharp
\Psi_{\alpha}

\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}

\sigma_{X}

\Psi_{\alpha}

Specifically, for
\alpha>2

31

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq K_{\alpha}\left(\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\left(\left\|X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{s}}\right)\right\|_{\beta,\infty}\right),

(1)

where
\alpha<s<\infty
. However,
\left\|X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{s}}

\left\|X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{s}}=\infty
while still having
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
. In that case, (
1

\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
. For
\alpha\in[1,2]
, the same line of work established that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq K_{\alpha}\left(\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right\|_{1}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\right),

(2)

which has a particularly clean form, but still does not separate the role of variance from that of the Orlicz norm and as we will demonstrate later, Orlicz norm is not always a sharp characterization of tail probability. From a different perspective, the foundational work of
19
showed that for
\alpha\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}^{2}},\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)}\right\}\right),

(3)

which is sharp for sufficiently small deviations since it preserves the variance term. However, it yields only a sub-exponential tail for
\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
regardless of
\alpha\geq 1
.

At the same time, the crude triangle inequality

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq n\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty

shows that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
still has finite
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm under the assumption of
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
. Although this bound is far from sharp, it strongly suggests that a
sharper
concentration theory should exist. This motivates the following questions:

For the tail probability
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)
, can one obtain a sharp
\Psi_{\alpha}
-tail under only
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
while simultaneously retaining variance-controlled concentration for sufficiently small
t
? More broadly, what is the corresponding concentration theory when
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
are dependent?

In this paper, we aim to answer these questions and identify a sharp phase transition at
\alpha=2
in univariate sub-Weibull concentration. The central novelty of our work is that sums of
\Psi_{\alpha}
random variables display local sub-Gaussian behavior around the origin even when the summands themselves are not sub-Gaussian, while their large-deviation behavior retains the correct
\Psi_{\alpha}
-tail. This yields a sharp, density-free concentration theory in the regime of
\alpha\geq 2
, and Section
3
further develops a variance-sensitive theory that remains statistically optimal when
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
and
\sigma_{X}
are not comparable. The corollary below illustrates the main phenomenon in the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) setting.

Corollary 1

(Concentration for i.i.d. univariate)
.

Assume that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
are i.i.d. mean-zero real-valued random variables, and satisfy
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
for some
\alpha\geq 1

\begin{split}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)&\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha-1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}}\right\}\right)\ \ \text{ for }\alpha\geq 2,\\
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)&\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha-1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}}\right\}\right)\ \ \text{ for }1\leq\alpha\leq 2,\end{split}

(4)

where
C>0
is some constant that does not depend on
\alpha,t,n,X
.

Corollary
1

1

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\frac{t^{2}}{n\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\frac{t^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha-1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}}\right),

which is consistent with the fact that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|^{p}\right\}\leq C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{p}+C_{1}^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}n^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{p}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{p}\cdot\exp(-cn)\ \ \text{ when }\alpha\geq 1,

2
Concentration inequalities when Orlicz norm is proportional to standard deviation

To illustrate our main ideas, we start with concentration inequalities for independent univariate sub-Weibull random variables, when it is
not necessary
to emphasize the difference between the Orlicz norm
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
and the standard deviation
\sigma_{X}:=\sqrt{\operatorname{var}(X)}
. The lemma below upper bounds the moment generating function for general sub-Weibull random variables with
\alpha>1
.

Lemma 1

(Moment generating function)
.

Let
X
be a mean-zero random variable with
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
for some
\alpha>1
, and
\beta
the conjugate of
\alpha
with
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1
. Then there exist some positive constants
C,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4},C_{5}
that do not depend on
\alpha
,
\lambda
, and
X
such that for any
\lambda\geq 0
,

1.

for
\alpha\geq 2
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp\left(C_{1}\min\left\{\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2},\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right\}\right);

2.

for
\alpha\in(1,2]
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp(C_{2}\beta\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})\ \ \text{ when }\lambda\leq 1/(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp(C_{3}^{\beta}\beta\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})\ \ \text{ when }\lambda\geq 1/(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}});

and further it holds for any
\tau\in(0,1)
that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp\left(C_{4}\frac{1}{1-\tau}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)\ \ \text{ when }\lambda\leq\tau/(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp\left(C_{5}^{\beta}\frac{\tau^{-[\beta]-1}}{1-\tau}\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)\ \ \text{ when }\lambda\geq\tau/(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}).

Lemma
1
above shows that for
\alpha>1
, regardless of
\alpha\geq 2
or
\alpha\in(1,2]
, when
\lambda
is sufficiently small, the moment generating function can be upper bounded with
\exp(O(\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}))
; when
\lambda
is sufficiently large, the bound for the moment generating function becomes
\exp(O(\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}))
. Lemma
1

improves and completes
the moment generating function bound in
32
or
(
5
). The following lemma provides the lower bound on the moment generating function for
\lambda
with sufficiently small values.

Lemma 2

(Lower bound on moment generating function)
.

Let
X
be a mean-zero random variable with
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
for some
\alpha\geq 1
, and
\sigma_{X}^{2}:=\operatorname{var}(X)
. Then we have that for all
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\geq\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}/8\right).

Consequently, combining Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
verifies that for sufficiently small
\lambda
, it holds that

\log({\mathbb{E}}(\lambda X))\asymp\lambda^{2},

which is quadratic in
\lambda
. In fact, we will demonstrate in Example
1
and Theorem
3
in Section
3
later that
\log({\mathbb{E}}(\lambda X))\asymp\lambda^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}
holds in this range. It implies that regardless of values of
\alpha
, for small enough
\lambda
, the moment generating function of
X
behaves as if the random variable were sub-Gaussian with the Orlicz norm
proportional to the standard deviation. An application of Lemma
1
yields the following concentration inequalities.

Theorem 1

(Concentration inequalities)
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
be independent mean-zero random variables with
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
,
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}
any
n
scalars, and
\beta
satisfy
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1
. Then there exist some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
that do not depend on
\alpha
,
t
, and
X
such that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C_{1}\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right\}\right),

and for
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C_{2}\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right\}\right),

for all
t\geq 0
.

Theorem
1
above establishes the concentration inequalities for sub-Weibull random variables. It unveils an interesting phase transition at
\alpha=2
. Regardless of
\alpha\in[2,\infty)
or
\alpha\in[1,2]
, for sufficiently small
t
, the tail probability of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}
behaves as if
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
were sub-Gaussian. When
t
is large enough, the tail probability presented in Theorem
1
enjoys a
\Psi_{\alpha}
decaying tail. Indeed, the triangle inequality with respect to the
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm leads to

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|\left\|X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}},

which entails that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}
has a finite
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm. This is consistent with Theorem
1
, while Theorem
1

sharpens
the triangle inequality. In addition, when
\alpha=1
, we have
\beta=\infty
and
(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{1}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}=\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{1}}
.

The following theorem provides the moment inequalities for
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}
.

Theorem 2

(Moment inequalities)
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
be independent mean-zero random variables with
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
for some
\alpha\geq 1
,
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}
any
n
scalars, and
p\geq 1
.
Then we have that for all
\alpha\geq 1
,

{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|^{p}\right\}\leq C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
+C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}\exp\left(-C\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right),

and additionally for all
\alpha\geq 2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|^{p}\right\}\leq C_{2}^{p}\min\left\{p^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}},p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}\right\},

where constants
C,C_{1},C_{2}>0
do not depend on
\alpha
,
p
, and
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

Theorem
2
above provides upper bounds for the
p
th moment of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}
. Specifically, in the context of i.i.d.
X_{i}
’s with equal weights, Theorem
2
implies that for all
\alpha\geq 1
and all
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|^{p}\right\}\leq C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{p}+C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}n^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{p}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{p}\cdot\exp(-cn).

Hence, for each fixed
p
, when
\sigma_{X}\asymp\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
, the right-hand side of the expression above is dominated by
C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{p}\asymp C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\sigma_{X}^{p}
as
n\to\infty
, which is consistent with the central limit theorem. Meanwhile, for
p
varying with
n
and the scenario of
\alpha\leq 2
, when
p\leq n
the upper bound is dominated by
p^{\frac{p}{2}}
, whereas when
p\geq n
the upper bound is dominated by
p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}
. Moreover, for the scenario of
\alpha\geq 2
, Theorem
2
entails the following upper bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|^{p}\right\}\leq C^{p}\min\left\{p^{\frac{p}{2}},p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}n^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{p}{\alpha}}\right\}\cdot\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{p},

where
X_{i}
’s are i.i.d. The above inequality shows that for
\alpha\geq 2
, when
p\leq n
it is bounded by
C_{1}^{\frac{p}{2}}p^{\frac{p}{2}}
, while when
n\leq p
it is bounded by
C_{1}^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}n^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{p}{\alpha}}
.

A combination of Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
further yields the following bound for the Orlicz norm.

Corollary 2

(Bound on Orlicz norm)
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
be independent mean-zero random variables with
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
, and
a=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
any
n
-dimensional vector. Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq C_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\quad\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{2}}\leq C_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and for
\alpha\in[1,2]
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq C_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are constants that do not depend on
\alpha
,
a
, and
X
.

Corollary
2
above gives the upper bound for the
\Psi_{\alpha}
- and
\Psi_{2}
-norms of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}
, where the bound is expressed explicitly by coefficients
a_{i}
and
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
. Similarly, a phase transition at
\alpha=2
is observed. Generally, Corollary
2
is
not
improvable, and when it is necessary to distinguish
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
and
\sigma_{X}
, a
sharper and more delicate
bound is given in Corollary
3
in Section
3
later. Taken together, Theorem
1
, Theorem
2
, and Corollary
2

improve and complete
the univariate concentration inequalities in the works including
23
,
8
, and
20
, among others.

Remark 1

(Characterization of concentration)
.

A natural question is what the appropriate characterization of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}
is. Indeed, we provide the tail probability in Theorem
1
, the bound of moments in Theorem
2
, and its
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm in Corollary

. The tail probability in Theorem

presents a delicate interplay between

3
Concentration inequalities when Orlicz norm can exceed standard deviation greatly

In this section, we further investigate the concentration inequalities of sub-Weibull random variables when the Orlicz norm may
not
be proportional to the standard deviation, in which the tail probability bounds established in Section
2
earlier may
no longer
be tight.
Indeed, it holds that
\sigma_{X}:=\sqrt{\operatorname{var}(X)}\leq\sqrt{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
for any random variable
X
. However, these two quantities (i.e., the Orlicz norm and standard deviation) may
not
have the same scale in general, e.g., for Bernoulli distributions; that is, the Orlicz norm can exceed the standard deviation greatly. This requires us to distinguish the standard deviation
\sigma_{X}
from the Orlicz norm
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
. Specifically, we now focus on random variables that are characterized by moments, where the moments are determined jointly by two positive quantities
\sigma
and
L
as specified in the definition below.

Definition 2
.

There exist two positive constants
\sigma
and
L
such that for all integers
k\geq 2
and some
\alpha\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\leq k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\sigma^{2}L^{k-2}.

Definition
2
above gives a delicate characterization of the distribution and has been prevalent in the literature; see, e.g.,
34
and
5
for the condition under
\alpha=2
. Indeed, Definition
2
is related to the Orlicz
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm; see the remark below.

Remark 2
.

If a random variable
X
satisfies Definition
2
with some
(\sigma,L)
, its Orlicz
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm can be bounded as
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq C\max\{\sigma,L\}
. On the other hand, if a random variable
X
admits
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
, it satisfies Definition
2
with the choice of
(\sigma,L)=(\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}},C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})
.

We emphasize that
\sigma\ll L
can indeed occur in many scenarios. As a concrete example, consider a Bernoulli random variable
X\sim\operatorname{Ber}(p)
. Its centered moments scale with its variance, although its Orlicz norm remains bounded by an absolute constant, i.e.,

{\mathbb{E}}|X-{\mathbb{E}}X|^{k}=p(1-p)\left\{p^{k-1}+(1-p)^{k-1}\right\}.

It shows that
\operatorname{Ber}(p)
satisfies Definition
2
with
\sigma=\sqrt{p(1-p)}
and
L=1
. More generally, for a random variable
X
with a finite
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm, we provide two examples of admissible pairs
(\sigma,L)
defined through
\sigma_{X}:=({\mathbb{E}}X^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
.

Example 1
.

Assume that
X
is a mean-zero random variable with
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
. Denote by
\sigma_{X}:=({\mathbb{E}}X^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then
X
satisfies Definition
2
with

\displaystyle\sigma:=\sigma_{X},\quad L:=\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right).

This characterization is sharp with the selection of
\sigma
in light of Lemma
2
and the fact that
\sigma_{X}^{2}={\mathbb{E}}X^{2}\leq 2^{2/\alpha}\sigma^{2}
. The proof is nontrivial and presented in Section
B
of the Supplementary Material. Essentially, the proof exploits the truncation technique used in
4
,
28
,
12
,
13
, and
19
.

Example 2
.

Assume that
X
is a mean-zero random variable with
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
. Denote by
\sigma_{X}:=({\mathbb{E}}X^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then
X
satisfies Definition
2
with

\displaystyle\sigma:=\sqrt{\sigma_{X}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}},\quad L:=C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}.

This characterization is sharp with the value of
L
. Indeed, the definition of the sub-Weilbull random variable guarantees that
\|X\|_{k}\leq Ck^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
, which entails that
L\geq C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
for some constant
C>0
. The proof is also included in Section
B
.

The lemma below provides the bound on the moment generating function for random variables satisfying Definition
2
, where a
delicate interplay
between
\sigma
and
L
is observed. Due to the bounds given in Examples
1
and
2
above, where generally each random variable
X
with
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
satisfies Definition
2
for nontrivial
\sigma\leq L
, in what follows we assume that
\sigma\leq L
.

Lemma 3

(Moment generating function)
.

Assume that random variable
X
has mean zero and satisfies Definition
2
with some
\alpha\geq 1
and
(\sigma,L)
. Then we have the following bounds for the moment generating function of
X
, where
c,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
are constants that do not depend on
\alpha,X,\sigma,L
.

1.

When
\alpha\geq 2
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp\left(C_{1}\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}\right)\ \ \text{ for all }\lambda\leq\frac{c}{L},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp\left(C_{2}\min\left\{\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta},\lambda^{2}L^{2}\right\}\right)\ \ \text{ for all }\lambda\geq 0.

2.

When
1<\alpha\leq 2
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp\left(C_{1}\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}\right)\ \ \text{ for all }\lambda\leq\frac{c}{L},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp\left(C_{3}^{\beta}\frac{\tau^{-[\beta]-1}}{1-\tau}\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right)\ \ \text{ for all }\lambda\geq\frac{c\tau}{L},

where
\tau>0
is any constant in
(0,1)
.

3.

When
\alpha=1
, it holds that
{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp\left(C_{1}\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}\right)
for all
\lambda\leq\frac{c}{L}
.

Lemma
3
above bounds the moment generating function of
X
under different ranges of
\alpha
. When
\lambda
is sufficiently small, the upper bound depends
only
on
\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}
, which is interestingly
independent of

L
. Specifically, combining Example
1
, Lemma
2
, and Lemma
3
together shows that for
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
,

\log\left({\mathbb{E}}(\lambda X)\right)\asymp\lambda^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}.

When
\lambda
is large enough, the upper bound behaves
differently
, which coincides with the fact that
X
has a finite
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm.

The following concentration inequality follows from Lemma
3
.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
are independent with mean zero, and
X_{i}
satisfies Definition
2
with
(\sigma_{i},L_{i})
. Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}},\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}L_{i}^{2}},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|L_{i}}\right\}\right\}\right),

and for
\alpha\in[1,2]
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|L_{i}}\right\},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

Theorem
3
above establishes the concentration inequality under Definition
2
. Interestingly, the tail probability presents a
mixing
of Orlicz
\Psi_{2}
-,
\Psi_{1}
-, and
\Psi_{\alpha}
-tails. For sufficiently small
t
, the tail probability is the exponential of
-C\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}}
, which depends only on
\{\sigma_{i}\}
. For
t
with intermediate values, it admits a sub-exponential tail. For large enough
t
, the tail presents a
\Psi_{\alpha}
decay. The corollary below proves the
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm and moment bounds under the framework of Definition
2
.

Corollary 3

(Bounds on
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm and moments)
.

Assume that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
are independent with mean zero, and
X_{i}
satisfies Lemma
1
with
(\sigma_{i},L_{i})
. Then we have that for
\alpha\in[1,2]
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},

and for
\alpha\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq C_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\quad\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{2}}\leq C_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}L_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, for
\alpha\geq 1
, it holds for the
p
th moment with
p\geq 1
that

{\mathbb{E}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|^{p}\leq C^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
+C^{p}p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}\exp\left(-C\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right).

Indeed, when
\alpha\in[1,2]
, we have
\beta\geq 2
and
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{2}L_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which show that Corollary
3
above
improves
over Theorem
2
and Corollary
2
in Section
2
earlier when the Orlicz norm exceeds the standard deviation greatly. We remark that
(\sigma_{i},L_{i})
can be expressed as functions of
\sigma_{X}
and
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
, and the forms are generally
not
unique, as illustrated in the following three examples.

Example 3

(Connections between Theorems
1
and
3
)
.

Theorem
3
is consistent with Theorem
1
. Indeed, substituting the choice of
(\sigma_{i},L_{i})=(\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}},\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})
(presented in Remark
2
) into Theorem
3
yields Theorem
1
, where we employ

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\gamma}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\geq\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\ \ \text{ for all }\gamma\geq 2.

Thus, Theorem
3
generalizes
Theorem
1
.

Example 4
.

Let us continue with Example
1
. Substituting the choice of
(\sigma_{i},L_{i})
given by

\sigma_{i}:=\sigma_{X_{i}},\quad L_{i}:=\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X_{i}}}\right)

into Theorem
3
leads to the following concentration inequalities. For ease of presentation, let us assume that
X_{i}
’s are i.i.d. Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha-1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}^{2}},\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)}\right\}\right\}\right),

(7)

and for
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}^{2}},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha-1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)},\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)}\right\}\right\}\right).

(8)

By (
7
) and (
8
), the tail probability is
\exp(-Ct^{2}/\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2})
for
t\leq\frac{n\sigma_{X}^{2}}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)}
. The tail probability bound for this range is not improvable, which arises from the sharp value of
\sigma_{i}
and has also been proved in the foundational work of
19
. On the other hand, an additional
\log(\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}/\sigma_{X})
appears for sufficiently large
t
compared to Theorem
1
.

Example 5
.

Let us continue with Example
2
. Assume that
X_{i}
’s are i.i.d. for simplicity. Then by the choice of
(\sigma_{i},L_{i})
in Example
2
, we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha-1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}},\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}},\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\right\}\right\}\right),

(9)

and for
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}},\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha-1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}},\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

(10)

The tail probability in
(
9
) and (
10
) above is sharp when
t
is large enough, which coincides with Theorem
1
. Indeed, Example
2
gives the sharp value for
L=C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
, which yields the sharp tail probability when
t\geq n\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
; for this range, it improves over Example
4
. However, for sufficiently small
t
, the tail probability is
\exp\left(-\frac{Ct^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{X_{i}}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\right)
, which is weaker than the corresponding one in Example
4
.

In order to obtain the
sharpest
tail probability represented as a function of variance and
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm, one strategy is to consider the minimum tail probability over all admissible pairs of
(\sigma,L)
. To this end, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4
.

Assume that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
are independent with mean zero, and
X_{i}
satisfies
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}_{i}:=\{(\sigma_{i},L_{i}):\,{\mathbb{E}}|X_{i}|^{k}\leq k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\sigma_{i}^{2}L_{i}^{2}\ \text{ for all }k\geq 2\}
. Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\inf_{(\sigma_{i},L_{i})\in\mathcal{D}_{i}}\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}},\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}L_{i}^{2}},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|L_{i}}\right\}\right\}\right),

and for
\alpha\in[1,2]
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\inf_{(\sigma_{i},L_{i})\in\mathcal{D}_{i}}\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\max\left\{\frac{t}{\displaystyle\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|L_{i}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

An application of Corollary
4
above yields the following concentrations of sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential random variables that are
new
to the literature.

Example 6

(sub-exponential random variables)
.

For i.i.d. sub-exponential
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
, combining Corollary
4
and (
7
)–(
10
) gives that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}^{2}}\right)\ \ \text{ for all }t\leq\frac{n\sigma_{X}^{2}}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{1}}\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{1}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{1}}}\right)\ \ \text{ for all }t\geq n\sigma_{X}.

For
t
between the above two end points, the tail probability involves a function with a mixing of
\exp(-t/(\|X\|_{\Psi_{1}}\log(\|X\|_{\Psi_{1}}/\sigma_{X})))
and
\exp(-ct^{2}/(n\sigma_{X}\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}))
. More specifically, we have that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}^{2}},\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{1}}\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)}\right\},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{1}}},\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}\|X\|_{\Psi_{1}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

Notably, the above concentration inequality cannot be obtained by combining the works of
19
and
36
.

Example 7

(sub-Gaussian random variables)
.

For i.i.d. sub-Gaussian
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
, an application of Corollary
4
and (
7
)–(
10
) yields that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}^{2}}\right)\ \ \text{ for all }t\leq\frac{n\sigma_{X}^{2}}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{n\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}^{2}}\right)\ \ \text{ for all }t\geq n\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}.

For
t
between the above two end points, the tail probability involves a function with a mixing of
\exp(-t/\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}})
,
\exp(-t/(\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}\log(2\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}/\sigma_{X})))
, and
\exp(-ct^{2}/(n\sigma_{X}\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}))
. Specifically, we have that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}^{2}},\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}^{2}},\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)}\right\},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{n\sigma_{X}\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}},\frac{t}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{2}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

Again, the above concentration inequality cannot be derived by combining the previous works.

4
Applications

In this section, we present four applications of our new concentration theory. Section
4.1
explores the convergence of martingales, where we
no longer
assume that
X_{i}
’s are independent, and Section
4.2
considers the norm of a random vector. Section
4.3
studies the operator norm of a random matrix, and Section
4.4
focuses on covariance matrix estimation.

4.1
Martingales

Here, we study the concentration behavior for
dependent
data. Let
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing filtration and
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of real-valued random variables adapted to
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Specifically, we will focus on dependent random variables that satisfy the conditional moment condition below.

Assumption 1
.

There exist two sequences of constants
\{\sigma_{n}\}
and
\{L_{n}\}
such that for all integers
k\geq 2
and some
\alpha\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{|X_{n}|^{k}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\right\}\leq k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\sigma_{n}^{2}L_{n}^{k-2}\quad\text{a.s.}

Assumption
1
above generalizes the independent-scenario condition presented in Definition
2
earlier. In particular, when
\alpha=2
, Assumption
1
reduces to the characterization used in
5
. Additionally, Assumption
1
is related to the conditional Orlicz norm
(
30
)
; see the following remark.

Remark 3

(Conditional
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm)
.

We inherit the notation and definition of the conditional Orlicz norm
\|\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{n}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
from Appendix A of
30
. If
\|X_{n}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq K_{n}
for some constant
K_{n}
,
X_{n}
satisfies Assumption
1
with

\sigma_{n}=K_{n},\quad L_{n}=CK_{n}.

On the other hand, if
X_{n}
satisfies Assumption
1
with
(\sigma_{n},L_{n})
, it holds that
\|X_{n}|\mathcal{F}_{n}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq C\max\{\sigma_{n},L_{n}\}
. However, in some scenarios, it is necessary to express
\sigma_{n}
as a function of the conditional variance and
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm, and distinguish the variance from the squared norm. Assume that
X_{n}
satisfies
{\mathbb{E}}\{X_{n}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\}=0
and denote by
\sigma_{X_{n}}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\{X_{n}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\}
. Then in parallel to Examples
1
and
2
, we have two pairs of admissible quantities for
(\sigma_{n},L_{n})
given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{2}:=\sigma_{X_{n}}^{2},\quad L_{n}:=K_{n}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2K_{n}}{\sigma_{X_{n}}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{2}:=\sigma_{X_{n}}K_{n},\quad L_{n}:=CK_{n}.

The above two choices of
(\sigma_{n},L_{n})
remain valid for all martingale differences that admit finite conditional
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norms.

Based on the above remark, without loss of generality, let us assume that
\sigma_{n}\leq L_{n}
. In particular, we are interested in the limit of the following martingale

\displaystyle M_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\bigg\{X_{k}-{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{X_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}\bigg\}\bigg\}.

Specifically, we will study the convergence of
M_{n}
under the conditions

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}<\infty,\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{\beta}L_{k}^{\beta}<\infty,

(11)

which are necessary in general. Indeed, by Rosenthal’s inequality and the martingale convergence theorem,
M_{n}
converges almost surely and in
L^{\beta}
to
M_{\infty}
given by

\displaystyle M_{\infty}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\bigg\{X_{k}-{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{X_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}\bigg\}\bigg\}.

On the other hand,
29
studied the convergence from a
different
perspective. Specifically,
29
made direct assumptions on the moment generating function; in contrast, we assume
only
the finite conditional
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm or conditional moments. We emphasize that for any
\gamma>\beta
, it holds that

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{\gamma}L_{k}^{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{\beta}L_{k}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}<\infty.

Hence, under
(
11
), we have
\max_{k=1,\cdots}|a_{k}|L_{k}<\infty
. However, for
\theta\in(0,\alpha)
, when (
11
) is satisfied,
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{\theta}L_{k}^{\theta}
can go to
\infty
. We are now ready to present the convergence of
M_{n}
.

Theorem 4
.

Assume that
\{X_{k}\}
is a martingale difference adapted to
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and satisfies Assumption
1
. Let
\{a_{k}\}
be any sequence of coefficients satisfying (
11
). Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(|M_{\infty}|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{\beta}L_{k}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}},\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}L_{k}^{2}},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{k=1,\cdots}|a_{k}|L_{k}}\right\}\right\}\right),

and for
\alpha\in[1,2]
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|M_{\infty}\right|\geq t\right)\\
\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}},\max\left\{\frac{t}{\max_{k=1,\cdots}|a_{k}|L_{k}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{\beta}L_{k}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

It is noteworthy that when
\alpha\leq 2
, (
11
) does guarantee that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}L_{k}^{2}<\infty
. Theorem
4
above implies the following bound for the
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm of
M_{\infty}
.

Corollary 5

(
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm)
.

Assume that the same conditions as in Theorem
4
are satisfied. Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\left\|M_{\infty}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq C_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\quad\left\|M_{\infty}\right\|_{\Psi_{2}}\leq C_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{2}L_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and for
\alpha\in[1,2]
,

\displaystyle\left\|M_{\infty}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq C_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are constants that do not depend on
\alpha
and
\{\sigma_{n},L_{n}\}
.

4.2
Random vectors

In this section, we provide an application to the norm of random vectors of independent components. The bound of random vector norm can be traced back to the classical book of
36
, where Theorem 3.1.1 therein gives the norm for random vectors with sub-Gaussian components.
15
later improved it with a sharper tail probability. Here, we aim to improve and generalize the corresponding results in
36
and
15
. Theorem
5
below gives the concentration inequality for the Euclidean norm of a random vector, which is
sharp
when
\sigma_{X}\asymp K
. Then Theorem
6
later distinguishes
\sigma_{X}
from the
\Psi_{\alpha}
-norm, which further generalizes Theorem
5
.

Theorem 5

(Heterogeneous components)
.

Assume that
X=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a mean-zero random vector with independent components, namely
\mathbb{E}X=0
, and
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq K_{i}<\infty
for some
\alpha\geq 2
. Denote by
\sigma_{X_{i}}:=\|X_{i}\|_{2}
. Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 4
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}}\right|\geq s\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{4}},\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{4}}s^{2},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2}}},\frac{s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2}}}\right\}\right),

and for
\alpha\in[2,4]
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}}\right|\geq s\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{4}},\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{4}}s^{2}\right\},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\max\left\{\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2}}},\frac{s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

For sufficiently large
s
, the tail probability established in Theorem
5
above is dominated by the
\Psi_{\alpha}
one under both scenarios. When applying Theorem
5
to the identical distributions, the concentration inequality can be simplified as follows.

Corollary 6

(Isotropic)
.

Assume that
X=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a mean-zero random vector with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) components, namely
\mathbb{E}X=0
, and
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq K<\infty
for some
\alpha\geq 2
. Denote by
\sigma_{X}:=\|X_{i}\|_{2}
with
\sigma_{X}\leq K
. Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 4
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|X\|-\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-Cd\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{K^{4}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}},\frac{s^{\alpha}}{K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right),

and for
\alpha\in[2,4]
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|X\|-\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-Cd\min\left\{\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{K^{4}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}}\right\},\frac{s^{\alpha}}{K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right).

Corollary
6
above bounds the norm of random vector
X
with i.i.d. components. In general, regardless of
\alpha\geq 4
or
2\leq\alpha\leq 4
, for
s\leq\sigma_{X}
, the tail probability is
2\exp\left(-Cd\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}/K^{4}\right)
; for
s\in[\sigma_{X},K]
, the tail probability is
2\exp\left(-Cds^{4}/K^{4}\right)
; and for
s\geq K
, the tail probability is
2\exp\left(-Cds^{\alpha}/K^{\alpha}\right)
. When
\sigma_{X}\asymp K
, the bound is
sharp
and the intermediate phase can be eliminated.

The theorem below provides a delicate bound of random vector norm under the characterization of moments.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that
X=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a mean-zero random vector with independent components, namely
\mathbb{E}X=0
, and
X_{i}^{2}-{\mathbb{E}}X_{i}^{2}
satisfies Definition
2
for some
\alpha\geq 1
and
(\sigma,L)
. Denote by
\sigma_{X}^{2}\geq\operatorname{var}(X_{i})
. Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}}\right|\geq s\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{s^{2\alpha}}{d^{\alpha-1}L^{\alpha}},\frac{s^{4}}{dL^{2}},\frac{s^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}}{L^{2}},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\min\left\{\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{d\sigma^{2}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}s^{2}\right\},\max\left\{\frac{s^{2}}{L},\frac{s\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}}{L}\right\}\right\}\right\}\right),

and for
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{d\sigma^{2}},\frac{s^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\min\left\{\max\left\{\frac{s^{2\alpha}}{d^{\alpha-1}L^{\alpha}},\frac{d^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sigma_{X}^{\alpha}}{L^{\alpha}}s^{\alpha}\right\},\max\left\{\frac{s^{2}}{L},\frac{s\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}}{L}\right\}\right\}\right\}\right).

The proof of Theorem
6

Example 8

Remark 4

(Comparisons to existing works under

Here, we consider the scenario when random vector

4.3
Random matrices

In this section, we investigate the largest and smallest nonzero
singular values
of a random matrix. The study of the operator norm for random matrices can be traced back to the elegant work of
6
, where asymptotic convergence was established for the eigenvalues of sample covariance matrices in the scenario of i.i.d. Gaussian entries. See, e.g., the
works of
22
,
9
,
35
,
11
, and references therein. Specifically,
35
focused on the nonasymptotic behaviors of singular values with independent rows or columns where the rows or columns are isotropic sub-Gaussian. Here, we will extend the results in
35
to general sub-Weibull distributions, and improve the results when standard deviation
\sigma_{X}
needs to be distinguished from the Orlicz norm.

Let
X
be a
d_{1}\times d_{2}
random matrix, with rows
X=\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{d_{1}}\right)^{\top}
. Here,
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d_{1}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
are independently distributed. We emphasize that the components of
X_{i}
may be dependent. The operator norm of
X
is defined as

\|X\|:=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\|Xu\|.

The lemma below upper bounds the difference between
\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X
and its expectation
\Sigma
.

Lemma 4
.

Assume that random matrix
X=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{d_{1}})^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}
contains i.i.d. mean-zero rows, and there exist some
\alpha\geq 1
,
\sigma,L>0
such that for any
u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d_{2}-1}
and all
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left|\left(X_{i}^{\top}u\right)^{2}-{\mathbb{E}}\left(X_{i}^{\top}u\right)^{2}\right|^{k}\leq k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\sigma^{2}L^{k-2}.

(14)

Denote by
\Sigma:={\mathbb{E}}X_{i}X_{i}^{\top}
the population covariance matrix. Then when
\alpha\geq 2
, it holds for all
t>0
that with probability over
1-\exp(-t)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|\leq C\min\left\{L\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\sigma\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+L\cdot\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right\}.

When
\alpha\in[1,2]
, it holds for all
t>0
that with probability over
1-\exp(-t)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|\leq C\sigma\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+CL\min\left\{\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right\}.

Lemma
4
above unveils an interesting phase transition at
\alpha=2
. Let
s_{\max}(X)
be the maximum singular value of
X
, and
s_{\min}(X)
its smallest nonzero singular value. Denote by
M_{\max}:=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)}
and
M_{\min}:=\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)}
.

Theorem 7
.

Assume that the same conditions as in Lemma
4
are satisfied. Then for any
\alpha\geq 2
and
t>0
, we have that with probability over
1-\exp(-t)
,

d_{1}M_{\min}^{2}-C\min\left\{L(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\sigma(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+L(d_{2}+t)\right\}\leq s_{\min}^{2}\\
\leq s_{\max}^{2}\leq d_{1}M_{\max}^{2}+C\min\left\{L(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\sigma(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+L(d_{2}+t)\right\}.

For any
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
and
t>0
, we have that with probability over
1-\exp(-t)
,

d_{1}M_{\min}^{2}-\sigma(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}-CL\min\left\{(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\beta}},d_{2}+t\right\}\leq s_{\min}^{2}\\
\leq s_{\max}^{2}\leq d_{1}M_{\max}^{2}+C\sigma(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+CL\min\left\{(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\beta}},d_{2}+t\right\}.

Theorem
7
above provides high probability bounds for the singular values of
X
, with explicit dependence on
M_{\max},M_{\min},\sigma,L
, and
\alpha,d_{1},d_{2}
. Theorem
7
is consistent with the classical asymptotic results for the case of i.i.d. Gaussian entries
(
6
)
.

Example 9

(Random matrix with i.i.d. sub-Gaussian entries)
.

Here, we provide the example values of
(\sigma,L)
when the
d_{1}\times d_{2}
random matrix
X
consists of i.i.d. mean-zero sub-Gaussian entries. Assume that each entry of
X
has variance
\sigma_{X}^{2}
and Orlicz norm
\|X_{ij}\|_{\Psi_{2}}
. Then we have
M_{\max}=\sigma_{X}
and for any
u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
,

\operatorname{var}(X_{i}^{\top}u)=\sigma_{X}^{2}<\infty,\quad\|X_{i}^{\top}u\|_{\Psi_{2}}\leq C\|X_{ij}\|_{\Psi_{2}}=:K<\infty.

In particular, Example
8
implies that the conditions of Lemma
4
hold with
\alpha=1
and three valid pairs of
(\sigma,L)
:

(K^{2},K^{2})
,
(\sigma_{X}^{1/2}K^{3/2},CK^{2})
, and

(\sigma_{X}K\log^{1/2}\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma_{X}}\right),CK^{2}\log\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma_{X}}\right)).

Hence, an application of Theorem
7
and the above values of
(\sigma,L)
yields that for any
t>0
, we have that with probability over
1-\exp(-t)
,

s_{\max}^{2}\leq d_{1}\sigma_{X}^{2}+C\sqrt{d_{2}+t}\min\left\{K^{3/2}{\sigma_{X}^{1/2}}\sqrt{d_{1}}+K^{2}\sqrt{d_{2}+t},\right.\\
\left.\sigma_{X}K\sqrt{d_{1}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma_{X}}\right)+K^{2}\sqrt{d_{2}+t}\log\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right\}.

Moreover, when
t\geq\frac{\sigma_{X}}{K}d_{1}-d_{2}
, it can be simplified as
{\mathbb{P}}(s_{\max}\leq\sqrt{d_{1}}\sigma_{X}+CK\sqrt{d_{2}+t})\geq 1-\exp(-t)
.

4.4
Covariance matrix estimation

As another application, we examine in this section the problem of mean and covariance matrix estimation based on a sample of
n
observed random vectors. See, e.g., the works of

10
,
27
,
17
, and references therein. We emphasize that our focus here is
different
. In particular,
17
investigated the delicate dependence of estimation error on the effective rank, under the assumption that the standard deviation of
X_{i}^{\top}u
has the
same
scale as the Orlicz norm. In contrast, we aim to
distinguish
these two quantities and examine their effects on covariance matrix estimation.

Assume that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are i.i.d.
d
-dimensional random vectors with
unknown
mean
\mu
and covariance matrix
\Sigma
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}X_{i}=\mu,\quad{\mathbb{E}}(X_{i}-\mu)(X_{i}-\mu)^{\top}=\Sigma.

We estimate
\mu
and
\Sigma
with the sample mean and sample variance matrix

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},\quad\widehat{\Sigma}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\widehat{\mu})(X_{i}-\widehat{\mu})^{\top},

respectively. The lemma below bounds the estimation error of sample mean under the characterization of moments.

Lemma 5

(Mean estimation)
.

Assume that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
are i.i.d.
d
-dimensional random vectors, and for any
u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
,
\left(X-\mu\right)^{\top}u
satisfies Definition
2
with some
\alpha\geq 1
and
(\sigma,L)
.
Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
and all
t>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\|\widehat{\mu}-\mu\|\geq C\min\left\{L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\sigma\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+L\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t),

and for
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
and all
t>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\|\widehat{\mu}-\mu\|\geq C\sigma\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\min\left\{L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},L\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t).

Lemma
5
above again reveals an interesting phase transition at
\alpha=2
. However, in both regimes, for sufficiently small
t
, the concentration presents itself as sub-Gaussian with deviation
\sigma\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, whereas when
t
is large enough, the deviation becomes
L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
.

Theorem 8

(Covariance matrix estimation)
.

Assume that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
are i.i.d.
d
-dimensional random vectors, and for any
u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
,
\left(X-\mu\right)^{\top}u
satisfy Definition
2
with some
\alpha\geq 1
and
(\sigma,L)
.
Then we have that for
\alpha\geq 4
and all
t>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|\geq C\min\left\{L^{2}\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},\sigma L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+L^{2}\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq 2\exp(-t),

and for
2\leq\alpha\leq 4
and all
t>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|\geq C\sigma L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\min\left\{L^{2}\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},L^{2}\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq 2\exp(-t).

Theorem
8
above upper bounds the estimation error of sample covariance matrix. Here, the estimation error also has a sub-Gaussian tail for sufficiently small
t
, whereas it admits a
\frac{\alpha}{2}
decay tail when
t
is large enough. Indeed, the sample covariance matrix is quadratic in
X
so that the power on dimensionality
d
is
\frac{2}{\alpha}
. For a random vector
X_{i}
, let
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}:=\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\|X_{i}^{\top}u\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
. If
\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
,
X_{i}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
8
with
\alpha
and infinite pairs of
(\sigma,L)
. Denote by
\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}\geq\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\operatorname{var}(X_{i}^{\top}u)
. We provide three specific examples of
(\sigma,L)
below

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}:=\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}},\quad L_{1}:=\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}};

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}:=\sigma_{X_{i}},\quad L_{2}:=C\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X_{i}}}\right);

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}:=\sqrt{\sigma_{X_{i}}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}},\quad L_{3}:=\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}},

which follow naturally from Examples
1
and
2
.

Example 10

(Covariance matrix estimation for sub-Gaussian entries)
.

We illustrate Theorem
8
when
X_{i}
consists of i.i.d. components. Assume that each component of the random vector has variance
\sigma_{X}^{2}
and Orlicz norm
\|X_{ij}\|_{\Psi_{2}}
. Then for any
u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
, it holds that

\operatorname{var}(X_{i}^{\top}u)=\sigma_{X}^{2},\quad\|X_{i}^{\top}u\|_{\Psi_{2}}\leq C\|X_{ij}\|_{\Psi_{2}}=:K,

which along with Examples

and

entail that

satisfies the conditions of Theorem

with

(K,K),\;(\sqrt{\sigma_{X}K},K),\;(\sigma_{X},K\log^{1/2}\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma_{X}}\right)).

Consequently, an application of Theorem

and the above three pairs of

gives that for all

1-2\exp(-t)

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|\leq C\min\left\{\sigma_{X}K\log^{1/2}\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right.\\
\left.+K^{2}\log\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\frac{t+d}{n},\sqrt{\sigma_{X}}K^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+K^{2}\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}.

The covariance matrix estimation error above unveils a delicate interplay between the standard deviation
\sigma_{X}
and the Orlicz norm. For sufficiently small

, it is dominated by
\sigma_{X}K\log^{1/2}\left(\frac{2K}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

.

5
Discussions

We have investigated in this paper the problem of how to develop sharp concentration inequalities of sub-Weibull random variables with general rate parameter
\alpha\geq 1
, including the commonly used sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential distributions with
\alpha=2
and
1
, respectively. Such new theoretical results will enable us to conduct more precise non-asymptotic analyses across different statistical and machine learning applications. Our unified concentration bounds involving the Orlicz norm have revealed an interesting phase transition at
\alpha=2
, with the minimum of two quantities switching to the maximum once
\alpha
is above
2
. Further, when the Orlicz norm can exceed the standard deviation greatly, we have established sharp, flexible concentration bounds that involve the variance and a mixing of Orlicz
\Psi_{\alpha}
-tails through the min and max functions. These sharp concentration inequalities are new even for the cases of sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential distributions with
\alpha=2
and
1
. We have showcased the utilities of our new theory with applications to martingales, random vectors, random matrices, and covariance matrix estimation. It would be interesting to extend our theory to more general settings of Banach space-valued random variables, reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHSs), and time series or online adaptive data. These problems are beyond the scope of the current paper and will be interesting topics for future research.

Appendix A

This section presents the proofs of Lemmas
1

A.1
Proof of Lemma
1

To prove this lemma, we will employ the Taylor expansion of function
\exp(\cdot)
that involves a sum of series with
\lambda^{k}/\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!
. Intuitively, for small enough
\lambda
, the higher-order terms are much smaller than the second-order term
\lambda^{2}
, whereas for large enough
\lambda
, the higher-order terms dominate the summation. To figure out the sum of the series, we will consider the power of
\lambda
and the factorial that appears in the denominator
\left(k,\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!\right)
, which can be roughly approximated by
\left(\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right],\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!\right)
, but rigorous and delicate analyses are needed to derive the desired bounds. We also emphasize that the bound of the moment generating function varies across scenarios of
\alpha\geq 2,1<\beta\leq 2
and
1<\alpha<2,\beta>2
. As such, we will analyze the moment generating function over these two scenarios separately.

In view of Lemma
11
in Section
D
, we have that

\|X\|_{k}\leq C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}k^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

for some constant
C>0
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}={\mathbb{E}}\left\{1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\lambda^{k}X^{k}\right\}\leq 1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}}{k!}\lambda^{k}(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})^{k}.

(A.1)

Recall that given
\alpha>1
, its conjugate
\beta>1
satisfies
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1
. An application of Stirling’s formula gives that for all
k=1,2,\cdots
,

\displaystyle\frac{k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}}{k!}\leq C\frac{c^{k}}{k^{\frac{k}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}}},

where
c,C>0
are some universal constants. We can deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}}{k!}\lambda^{k}(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})^{k}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{k^{\frac{k}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}}}\lambda^{k}(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})^{k}\leq C\frac{1}{\left(\frac{k}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{k}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{\beta^{\frac{k}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}}}\lambda^{k}(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})^{k}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]^{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]+\frac{1}{2}}}\lambda^{k}(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})^{k}\leq C\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}\lambda^{k}(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})^{k},

where
[x]
represents the largest integer that is no bigger than
x
. Consequently, (
A.1
) above can be further bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq 1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}\lambda^{k}C^{k}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{k}.

(A.2)

We will consider the two scenarios of
\alpha\geq 2,1<\beta\leq 2
and
1<\alpha<2,\beta>2
seperately.

Case 1:
\alpha\geq 2,1<\beta\leq 2
.
In this case, the sequence
\left\{\left[\frac{2}{\beta}\right],\left[\frac{3}{\beta}\right],\cdots\right\}
contains all positive integers and each integer appears in the sequence at most
2
times, which is due to
\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]<\left[\frac{k+2}{\beta}\right]
. For even integers, it holds that
\left[\frac{2k}{\beta}\right]\geq k
, and for odd integers, it holds that
\left[\frac{2k+1}{\beta}\right]\geq k
. As a result, (
A.2
) above can be further bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq 1+\frac{1}{1!}C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1!}C^{3}\lambda^{3}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{3}+\frac{1}{2!}C^{4}\lambda^{4}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{4}+\cdots.

(A.3)

We will bound (
A.3
) above from two perspectives. First, note that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}(C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})^{2k}(1+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}).

(A.4)

We then extract the common term
1+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
and can deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+(1+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})\left(\exp(C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})\exp(C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}),

where the last step above is due to
1+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\geq 1
. Hence, it follows from
1+x\leq\exp(x)
that

{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp(C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}).

On the other hand, in light of
1+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\geq 1
, (
A.4
) above can be further bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}(C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})^{2k}(1+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}})^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C^{3}\lambda^{3}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{3}).

Combining the above two results yields that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(\min\bigg\{C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C^{3}\lambda^{3}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{3},C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\bigg\}\bigg),

which is in fact equivalent to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp(C\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}).

To see why, when
C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C^{3}\lambda^{3}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{3}\leq C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
, it holds that

C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq 1,

under which we have that

C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C^{3}\lambda^{3}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{3}\leq 2C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}.

Similar arguments apply when
C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C^{3}\lambda^{3}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{3}\geq C^{2}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C\lambda\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
.

We then proceed to provide another bound of the moment generating function. Observe that (
A.2
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq 1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}\left(\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right)^{\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]+\left(k-\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]\right)},

(A.5)

where
k-\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]\in[0,\beta)
. When
\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq 1
, we have that

\left(\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right)^{\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]+\left(k-\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]\right)}\leq\left(\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right)^{\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]},

which shows that (
A.5
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}\left(\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right)^{\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]}\leq 1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{k!}\left(\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right)^{\beta\cdot k}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(C\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}).

When
\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\geq 1
, we have that

\left(\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right)^{\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]+\left(k-\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]\right)}\leq\left(\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right)^{\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]+\beta},

which shows that (
A.5
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}\left(\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right)^{\beta\cdot\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]+\beta}\leq 1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{k!}\left(\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\right)^{\beta\cdot(k+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 1+C\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\left(\exp(C\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})-1\right)\leq C\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\exp(C\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})

\displaystyle\leq\exp(C\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})\cdot\exp(C\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\exp(C_{1}\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}),

where
C_{1}
is another positive constant.
Thus, combining the above results, we can obtain that when
\alpha\geq 2
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp(C\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}),\quad{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp(C\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})

for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

Case 2:
1<\alpha<2,\beta>2
.
In this case, (
A.2
) can be further written as

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq 1+\frac{1}{0!}\lambda^{2}C^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{0!}\lambda^{[\beta]}C^{[\beta]}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{[\beta]}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{1!}\lambda^{[\beta]+1}C^{[\beta]+1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{[\beta]+1}+\cdots+\frac{1}{1!}\lambda^{2[\beta]+1}C^{2[\beta]+1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2[\beta]+1}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2!}\lambda^{2[\beta]+2}C^{2[\beta]+2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2[\beta]+2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2!}\lambda^{3[\beta]+2}C^{3[\beta]+2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{3[\beta]+2}\\
&\quad+\cdots,\end{split}

(A.6)

where
0!=1!=1
by convention. Let us consider different ranges of
\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}
. When
\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\tau
with
\tau>0
some given number, we can incorporate terms of (
A.6
) by the common denominator and deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{1}{0!}\lambda^{2}C^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\cdot\left(1+\cdots+\tau^{[\beta]-2}\right)+\frac{1}{1!}\lambda^{[\beta]+1}C^{[\beta]+1}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{[\beta]+1}\cdot\left(1+\cdots+\tau^{[\beta]}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2!}\lambda^{2[\beta]+2}C^{2[\beta]+2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2[\beta]+2}\cdot\left(1+\cdots+\tau^{[\beta]}\right)+\cdots.

Specifically, if
\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq\tau<1
, (
A.6
) above can be bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{1}{1-\tau}\lambda^{2}C^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\tau}\frac{1}{2!}\lambda^{2[\beta]+2}C^{2[\beta]+2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2[\beta]+2}+\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\exp(C\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}/(1-\tau)).

If more generally
\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq 1
, (
A.6
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+(2[\beta]-1)\lambda^{2}C^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+([\beta]+1)\frac{1}{2!}\lambda^{2[\beta]+2}C^{2[\beta]+2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2[\beta]+2}+\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\exp(C\beta\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})

accordingly.

When
\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\geq\tau
with
\tau\in(0,1)
some given number, (
A.6
) can be upper bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{1}{\tau^{[\beta]+1}}\frac{1}{1-\tau}\frac{1}{0!}\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}+\frac{1}{\tau^{[\beta]+1}}\frac{1}{1-\tau}\frac{1}{1!}\lambda^{2\beta}C^{2\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2\beta}+\cdots

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{1}{\tau^{[\beta]+1}}\frac{1}{1-\tau}\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\cdot\exp(C^{\beta}\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})

\displaystyle\leq 1+\left(\exp\left(\frac{1}{\tau^{[\beta]+1}}\frac{1}{1-\tau}\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)-1\right)\cdot\exp(C^{\beta}\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}).

Then by resorting to
\exp(C^{\beta}\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})\geq 1
and
1+x\leq\exp(x)
, it holds that

{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp\left(C\frac{1}{\tau^{[\beta]+1}}\frac{1}{1-\tau}\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right).

If further
\lambda C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\geq 1
, it follows from (
A.6
) that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\left([\beta]-1\right)\frac{1}{0!}\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}+([\beta]+1)\frac{1}{1!}\lambda^{2\beta}C^{2\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2\beta}+\cdots.

We then extract the common term
\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}
and can show that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+([\beta]+1)\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\cdot\exp\left(\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+([\beta]+1)\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(([\beta]+1)\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\lambda^{\beta}C^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(([\beta]+1)\lambda^{\beta}C_{1}^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right),

where the last two steps above have used the facts that
1+x\leq\exp(x)
and
\exp\left(x\right)\geq 1
for
x\geq 0
.

Therefore, in view of
[\beta]+1\leq 2\beta
, we can obtain that when
\alpha\in(1,2)
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp(C_{1}\beta\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})\ \ \text{ if }\lambda\leq\frac{1}{C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp(C_{2}^{\beta}\beta\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})\ \ \text{ if }\lambda\geq\frac{1}{C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}},

where
C,C_{1},C_{2}>0
are some constants. Moreover, we have that for any
\tau\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp\left(C_{3}\frac{1}{1-\tau}\lambda^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)\ \ \text{ if }\lambda\leq\tau/(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\leq\exp\left(C_{4}^{\beta}\frac{\tau^{-[\beta]-1}}{1-\tau}\lambda^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)\ \ \text{ if }\lambda\geq\tau/(C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}),

where
C,C_{3},C_{4}>0
are some constants. This completes the proof of Lemma
1
.

A.2
Proof of Lemma
2

It is well-known that sub-Weibull random variables satisfy that
{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\leq C^{k}k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{k}
. However, a more delicate bound can be derived. Our technical lemma establishes that
{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{3}\leq C\sigma_{X}^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}})
(see Lemma
9
in Section
D
), and see Examples
1
and
2
for the bound with general
k\geq 2
, which is key to the proof of the current lemma.

Let us first expand the exponential function

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left\{1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\lambda^{k}X^{k}\right\}=1+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\{X^{2}\right\}+\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\lambda^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\left\{X^{k}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq 1+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\{X^{2}\right\}-\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\lambda^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\left|X\right|^{k}.

We then examine the high-order term in the expression above and can write it as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\lambda^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\left|X\right|^{k}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}\frac{\exp(\lambda|X|)-1-\lambda|X|-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}X^{2}}{\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\left\{|X|\leq\tau\right\}

\displaystyle~~~~+{\mathbb{E}}\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}\frac{\exp(\lambda|X|)-1-\lambda|X|-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}X^{2}}{\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\left\{|X|>\tau\right\}.

For
\lambda\leq 1/\tau
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}\frac{\exp(\lambda|X|)-1-\lambda|X|-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}X^{2}}{\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\left\{|X|\leq\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}\frac{e-1}{1}\leq c{\mathbb{E}}\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}.

Hence, it follows from Lemma
9
that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}\frac{\exp(\lambda|X|)-1-\lambda|X|-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}X^{2}}{\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\left\{|X|\leq\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{3}\sigma_{X}^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

On the other hand, the term with
|X|\geq\tau
can be bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}\frac{\exp(\lambda|X|)-1-\lambda|X|-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}X^{2}}{\lambda^{3}|X|^{3}}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\left\{|X|\geq\tau\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{3}{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{3}\frac{\exp(\frac{|X|}{C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}})-1-(\frac{|X|}{C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}})-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{|X|}{C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}})^{2}}{(\frac{|X|}{C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}})^{3}}{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|\geq\tau\}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{3}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{3}{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left(\frac{|X|}{C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\right)\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|\geq\tau\},

where the first step above has utilized the fact that the fraction is monotone increasing with respect to
\lambda
, and the routine is partially borrowed from
19
. Then with the choice of
\tau=C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
as in
19
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left(\frac{|X|}{C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\right)\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|\geq\tau\}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left(\frac{2|X|}{C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\right)}\cdot\sqrt{{\mathbb{P}}\{|X|\geq\tau\}}

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\left(-c\left(\frac{\tau}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)\leq\left(\frac{\sigma_{X}}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\right)^{2}.

Combining the above results leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\lambda^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\left|X\right|^{k}\leq C\lambda^{3}\sigma_{X}^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Consequently, we can obtain the following lower bound for the moment generating function

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\geq 1+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}-C\lambda^{3}\sigma_{X}^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

For
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, we can show that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp(\lambda X)\right\}\geq 1+\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}\geq\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}/8\right),

where the last step above is due to
1+x\geq\exp(x/2)
for
x\in[0,1]
.
This concludes the proof of Lemma
2
.

A.3
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof of Theorem
1
is rooted on Lemma
1
.
By the Markov inequality, for any
\lambda>0
we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\geq t\right)\leq\frac{1}{\exp(\lambda t)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right)\right\}=\frac{1}{\exp(\lambda t)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\lambda a_{i}X_{i}\right\}.

For
\alpha\geq 2
, an application of Lemma
1
gives that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\lambda a_{i}X_{i}\right\}\leq\exp\left(C_{1}\min\left\{\lambda^{2}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2},\,\lambda^{\beta}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right\}\right),

which yields that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(C_{1}\min\left\{\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2},\,\lambda^{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right\}-\lambda t\right).

Inserting
\lambda=\max\left\{\frac{t}{2C_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\left(\frac{t}{C_{1}\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}}\right)^{{}^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}}\right\}
into the above expression, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(-C_{2}\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}}\right\}\right).

For
\alpha\in(1,2)
, by invoking Lemma
1
we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\lambda a_{i}X_{i}\right\}\leq\exp\left(C_{3}\lambda^{2}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C_{3}^{\beta}\lambda^{\beta}2^{\beta}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right).

Then it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(C_{3}\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}+C_{3}^{\beta}2^{\beta}\lambda^{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}-\lambda t\right).

Inserting
\lambda=\min\left\{\frac{t}{4C_{3}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\left(\frac{t}{4C_{3}^{\beta}2^{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\right\}
into the above expression yields that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(-C_{5}\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}}\right\}\right),

(A.7)

where we have used
\beta\geq 2
and
C_{3}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}\leq C_{3}^{2}
.

Finally, for
\alpha=1
, an application of Lemma
1
and setting

\lambda=\min\left\{\frac{t}{C\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{1}}^{2}},\frac{1}{\max_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{1}}}\right\}

give that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(-C_{5}\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{1}}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{1}}}\right\}\right),

which has also been proved by Theorem 2.9.1 of
36
. Due to

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}=\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}},

the concentration inequality for
\alpha=1
is contained by (
A.7
). This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

A.4
Proof of Theorem
2

Due to the phase transition over the regimes of
\alpha\geq 2
and
\alpha\in[1,2]
, the following proof first establishes the bound for the case of
\alpha\geq 1
and then provides a bound that holds only for the case of
\alpha\geq 2
.

Let us first consider the case of
\alpha\geq 1
. For any
p>0
, by the definition of integral it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|^{p}\right\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\cdot{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\;dt.

(A.8)

An application of Theorem
1
leads to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|^{p}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}pt^{p-1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{Ct^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}}\right)\,dt

(A.9)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\tau}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right)\,dt,

where quantity
\tau>0
satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\frac{\tau^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}}=\frac{\tau^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}.

(A.10)

It suffices to bound each term on the right-hand side of (
A.9
) above. Specifically, the first term admits that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}pt^{p-1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{Ct^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{Ct^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{p/2}p^{p/2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{p/2},

where the last step above follows from standard integrals and Gamma function properties.

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
A.9
), it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}\int_{\frac{\tau^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}}^{\infty}\;s^{\frac{p}{\alpha}-1}\exp(-Cs)\,ds.

Then when
p\leq\alpha
, we have
\frac{p}{\alpha}-1\leq 0
and thus it follows from Lemma
8
in Section
D
that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{2}}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right)\,dt\leq\frac{p}{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2(p-\alpha)}{\alpha-2}}\exp\left(-C\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq p\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}\exp\left(-C\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right),

where the last step above has exploited the fact that
\|r\|_{q_{1}}\leq\|r\|_{q_{2}}
if
q_{1}\geq q_{2}
for any vector
r
.

On the other hand, when
p\geq\alpha
, we have
\frac{p}{\alpha}-1\geq 0
and thus it follows from Lemma
8
that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}\exp\left(-C\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right).

Hence, for all
p\geq 1
, we have the following bound for the second term on the right-hand side of (
A.9
)

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{Ct^{\alpha}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}\exp\left(-C\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right).

Combining the above results yields that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|^{p}\right\}\leq C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}^{p}p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}\exp\left(-C\left(\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right).

Further, for the case of
\alpha\geq 2
, by resorting to Theorem
1

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\min\left\{\exp\left(-C\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}}\right),\exp\left(-C\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)}\right)\right\}.

Therefore, by inserting the above expression into (

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|^{p}\right\}\leq C_{1}^{p}\min\left\{p^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}},p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\beta}}\right\},

which concludes the proof of Theorem
2
.

A.5
Proof of Corollary
2

In what follows, we will derive the bound of the Orlicz norm based on Theorems
1
and
2
. Again, we will analyze the cases of
\alpha\geq 2
and
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
separately.

Case 1:
\alpha\geq 2
.
In this case, according to Theorem
2
, it holds that for all
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{p}\leq C_{1}p^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}.

Denote by
K:=C_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
with
C_{2}>0
some sufficiently large constant. It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp\left(\left|\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}}{K}\right|^{\alpha}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p!}\frac{1}{K^{p}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{p\alpha}^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p!}\frac{1}{K^{\alpha p}}C_{1}^{p\alpha}p^{p}\alpha^{p}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p\alpha}{\beta}}.

Then by Stirling’s formula and inserting the value of
K
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp\left(\left|\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}}{K}\right|^{\alpha}\right)\right\}\leq 1+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\frac{C_{3}^{p}}{C_{2}^{\alpha p}}C_{1}^{p\alpha}\alpha^{p}.

Hence, with
C_{2}\geq C_{1}+C_{3}
and
C_{2}\geq\max_{\alpha\geq 2}\alpha^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, it holds that

{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp\left(\left|\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}}{K}\right|^{\alpha}\right)\right\}\leq 2.

This establishes that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\leq C_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}.

Moreover, an application of Theorem
2
gives that for all
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{p}\leq C_{1}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which entails that
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{\Psi_{2}}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
using the standard arguments as in
36
.

Case 2:
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
. In this scenario, we have that
\beta>2
,
\sqrt{p}\leq p^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, and

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By invoking Theorem
2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}p^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{1}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}p^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\cdot\exp(-cn/p).

Thus, by setting
K:=C_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
with
C_{2}>0
some sufficiently large constant, we can obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp\left(\left|\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}}{K}\right|^{\alpha}\right)\right\}=1+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p!}\frac{1}{K^{\alpha p}}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right\|_{p\alpha}^{p\alpha}\leq 1+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{1}^{p\alpha}}{p!}\frac{1}{K^{\alpha p}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left\{p^{p}\alpha^{p}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{p\alpha}{\beta}}\cdot\exp(-n)+\alpha^{\frac{\alpha p}{2}}p^{\frac{\alpha p}{2}}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\|X_{i}\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha p}{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2.

This completes the proof of Corollary
2
.

B.1
Proof of Example

The lemma below proves Example

Lemma 6
.

Assume that
X
is a mean zero random variable and satisfies that
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty

for some
\alpha\geq 1
. Denote by
\sigma_{X}^{2}:=\operatorname{var}(X)
. Then it holds that for some universal constant
C>0
and all
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\leq C^{k}k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\cdot\sigma_{X}^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{k-2}\log^{\frac{k-2}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right).

Proof
.
Note that for any
\tau>0
, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}={\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|>\tau\}+{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|\leq\tau\}.

We will bound each of the terms on the right-hand side of the expression above. The first term admits that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|>\tau\}

\displaystyle\leq\left({\mathbb{E}}|X|^{2k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\mathbb{P}}\left(|X|\geq\tau\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{k}k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{k}\cdot\exp\left(-c^{\alpha}\frac{\tau^{\alpha}}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}}\right),

where the last step above has utilized Lemma
11
and the properties of sub-Weilbull random variables
[
36
]
, and
C>0
is some universal constant. For the second term, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|\leq\tau\}\leq\tau^{k-2}{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}=\tau^{k-2}\sigma_{X}^{2}.

Hence, combining the above results leads to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\leq C^{k}k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{k}\cdot\exp\left(-c^{\alpha}\frac{\tau^{\alpha}}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{\alpha}}\right)+\tau^{k-2}\sigma_{X}^{2}.

Therefore, with the choice of
\tau=C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma}\right)
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\leq C^{k}k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{k-2}\sigma_{X}^{2}\log^{\frac{k-2}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma}\right).

This concludes the proof of Lemma
6
.

Proof of Example

The lemma below proves Example

Lemma 7
.

Assume that
X
is a mean zero random variable and satisfies that
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty

for some
\alpha\geq 1
. Denote by
\sigma_{X}^{2}:=\operatorname{var}(X)
. Then it holds that for some universal constant
C>0
and all
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\leq C^{k}k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\cdot\sigma_{X}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{k-1}.

Proof
.
By Hölder’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}\leq\left({\mathbb{E}}X^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left({\mathbb{E}}|X|^{2k-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sigma_{X}\cdot\|X\|_{2k-2}^{k-1}.

Note that in light of Lemma

, it holds that for all

\|X\|_{k}\leq Ck^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}.

Consequently, inserting this bound into the expression above yields that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{X}\cdot C^{k-1}2^{\frac{k-1}{\alpha}}(k-1)^{\frac{k-1}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{k}\cdot\sigma_{X}k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{k-1},

where
C_{1}>0
is some universal constant. This completes the proof of Lemma
7
.

B.3
Proof of Lemma
3

Essentially, Lemma
3
extends Lemma
1
. We will consider the Taylor expansion of the moment generating function. Here, the term is
\lambda^{k}\sigma^{2}L^{k-2}
, whose
k
th root is
\lambda L
as
k
goes to infinity and this intuitively explains why there is a phase transition at
\lambda=\frac{1}{L}
instead of
1/\sigma
for a fixed
\alpha
. The following proof establishes the bound first for the case of
\alpha\geq 2
and then for the case of
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
.

With the aid of the Taylor expansion and Definition
2
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq 1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{k^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}}{k!}\lambda^{k}\sigma^{2}L^{k-2}.

In view of Stirling’s inequality, the above expression can be further bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq 1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}C^{k}\lambda^{k}\sigma^{2}L^{k-2};

see the proof of Lemma
1
in Section
A
for more details. We will bound the above expression for the cases of
\alpha\geq 2
and
\alpha\in[1,2]
separately.

Case 1:
\alpha\geq 2
. Note that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq 1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{L^{2}}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}C^{k}\lambda^{k}L^{k}=1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}C^{k}\lambda^{k}L^{k}+1\right)-1\right).

Then by the proof of Lemma
1
in Section
A
, we have that when
\alpha\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}C^{k}\lambda^{k}L^{k}+1\leq\exp\left(C\min\left\{\lambda^{2}L^{2},\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right\}\right).

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq 1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(\exp\left(C\min\left\{\lambda^{2}L^{2},\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right\}\right)-1\right).

When
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{L}
, it holds that
\lambda L\leq 1
and thus
\lambda^{2}L^{2}\leq\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}
. Consequently, when
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{L}
, we have that

\exp(\lambda^{2}L^{2})-1\leq C_{1}\lambda^{2}L^{2},

which entails that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq 1+C_{2}\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}\leq\exp(C_{2}\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}).

On the other hand, it holds that for all
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq 1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(\exp\left(C\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right)-1\right).

Then given
\sigma\leq L
, it follows from
\exp\left(C\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right)-1\geq 0
that

{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq 1+1\cdot\left(\exp\left(C\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right)-1\right)=\exp\left(C\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right).

Thus, when
\alpha\geq 2
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp\left(C\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}\right)\ \ \text{ for }\lambda\leq\frac{1}{L},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp\left(C\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right)\ \ \text{ for all }\lambda\geq 0.

Case 2:
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
.
By the proof of Lemma
1
, when
\alpha\in[1,2)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}C^{k}\lambda^{k}L^{k}+1\leq\exp\left(C\frac{1}{1-\tau}\lambda^{2}L^{2}\right)

for
\lambda\leq\frac{C\tau}{L}
, where
\tau
is any constant in
(0,1)
. Setting
\tau=\frac{1}{2}
, we have that when
\lambda\leq\frac{C}{2L}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq 1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(\exp(C\lambda^{2}L^{2})-1\right)\leq 1+C\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}\leq\exp(C\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}).

Further, for
\lambda\geq\frac{C\tau}{L}
and
\alpha\in(1,2]
, it follows from Lemma
1
that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\frac{k}{\beta}\right]!}C^{k}\lambda^{k}L^{k}+1\leq\exp\left(C_{1}^{\beta}\frac{\tau^{-[\beta]-1}}{1-\tau}\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right).

Hence, for
\lambda\geq\frac{C\tau}{L}
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq 1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(\exp\left(C_{1}^{\beta}\frac{\tau^{-[\beta]-1}}{1-\tau}\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right)-1\right).

Therefore, given
\sigma\leq L
, we have that
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{L^{2}}\leq 1
and thus

{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp\left(C_{1}^{\beta}\frac{\tau^{-[\beta]-1}}{1-\tau}\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}\right).

This concludes the proof of Lemma
3
.

B.4
Proof of Theorem
3

The following proof is built upon Lemma
3
.
By the Markov inequality, it holds that for all
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\geq t\right)\leq\exp(-\lambda t)\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\exp\left(a_{i}\lambda X_{i}\right).

Then for
\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|L_{i}}
, an application of Lemma
1
gives that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp(-\lambda t)\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}\right).

Inserting
\lambda=\min\left\{\frac{t}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\frac{1}{\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|L_{i}}\right\}
into the above expression, we can deduce that for all
t\geq 0
and
\alpha\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|L_{i}}\right\}\right).

(A.11)

Let us first investigate the case of
\alpha\geq 2
. In this case, we have that
{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp(C\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta})
, which implies that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp(-\lambda t)\exp\left(C\lambda^{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right).

Inserting
\lambda=\left(\frac{t}{\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}
into the above expression leads to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right).

Moreover, due to
{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp(C\lambda^{2}L^{2})
, it holds that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}L_{i}^{2}}\right).

Hence, we have that for
\alpha\geq 2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}},\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}L_{i}^{2}},\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|L_{i}}\right\}\right\}\right).

It remains to bound the tail probability for the case of
\alpha\in[1,2]
. Recall that Lemma
3
establishes that for all
\lambda\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp(C2^{\beta}\lambda^{\beta}L^{\beta}+C\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}).

Then it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(C\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}+C2^{\beta}\lambda^{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}-\lambda t\right).

By taking
\lambda
to minimize the above tail probability, we can obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right\}\right).

Thus, combining the above expression with (
A.11
) yields that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\right|\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}},\max\left\{\frac{t}{\max_{i=1,\cdots,n}|a_{i}|L_{i}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.

C.1
Proof of Theorem

Theorem

. Indeed, by the Markov inequality, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}X_{k}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\lambda t){\mathbb{E}}\exp\bigg\{\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}X_{k}\bigg\}.

Observe that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\bigg\{\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}X_{k}\bigg\}={\mathbb{E}}\left\{\exp\bigg\{\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}X_{k}\bigg\}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\exp\bigg\{\lambda a_{n}X_{n}\bigg\}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\bigg\}\right\}.

Additionally, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{\exp\bigg\{\lambda a_{n}X_{n}\bigg\}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\bigg\{1+\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p!}\lambda^{p}|a_{n}|^{p}|X_{n}|^{p}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\frac{p^{\frac{p}{\alpha}}}{p!}\lambda^{p}|a_{n}|^{p}\sigma_{n}^{2}L_{n}^{p-2},

where the last step above follows from Assumption
1
. Then by the proof of Lemma
3
in Section
B
, it holds that for
\lambda\leq\frac{c}{a_{n}L_{n}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda a_{n}X_{n})\leq\exp(C\lambda^{2}a_{n}^{2}\sigma_{n}^{2}).

Hence, by induction, we can deduce that for all
\lambda\leq\frac{c}{\max_{k=1,\cdots.n}|a_{k}|L_{k}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\exp\bigg\{\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}X_{k}\bigg\}\leq\exp\left(C\lambda^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{n}^{2}\sigma_{n}^{2}\right).

Further, setting
\lambda:=\min\left\{\frac{t}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}},\frac{1}{\max_{k=1,\cdots,n}|a_{k}|L_{k}}\right\}
yields that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}X_{k}\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{k=1,\cdots,n}|a_{k}|L_{k}}\right\}\right).

The remaining arguments are similar to those in the proof of Theorem
3
in Section
B
and thus omitted.

When
\alpha\geq 2
, it holds that for any fixed
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(|M_{n}|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}},\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{2}L_{i}^{2}},\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{k=1,\cdots}|a_{k}|L_{k}}\right\}\right\}\right).

Under (
11
),
M_{n}
converges to
M_{\infty}
almost surely which satisfies that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(|M_{\infty}|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{i}|^{\beta}L_{i}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}},\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{2}L_{i}^{2}},\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\left.\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{k=1,\cdots}|a_{k}|L_{k}}\right\}\right\}\right).

When
\alpha\in[1,2]
, we have that for any fixed
n
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|M_{n}\right|\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}},\max\left\{\frac{t}{\max_{k=1,\cdots}|a_{k}|L_{k}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{\beta}L_{k}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

Under (
11
),
M_{n}
converges to
M_{\infty}
almost surely and it also holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|M_{\infty}\right|\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\sigma_{k}^{2}},\max\left\{\frac{t}{\max_{k=1,\cdots}|a_{k}|L_{k}},\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{\beta}L_{k}^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}}\right\}\right\}\right).

This concludes the proof of Theorem
4
.

Proof of Theorem

The following proof exploits Theorem

.
By invoking Theorem

, we have that when
\alpha\geq 4
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{4}},\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{i}^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2}}}\right\}\right).

Additionally, an application of Lemma
10
in Section
D
shows that for any
s

\displaystyle\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}}\right|\geq s,

\displaystyle\left|\|X\|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}\right|\geq\max\left\{s^{2},s\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}}\right\}.

Hence, we can deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}}\right|\geq s\right)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}\right|\geq\max\left\{s^{2},s\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{X_{i}}^{2}}\right\}\right)

C.3
Proof of Corollary
6

We emphasize that by definition, it holds that
\sigma_{X}\leq 2K
. Let us consider the squared Euclidean norm of
X

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|^{2}-{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}\right|\geq t\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(X_{i}^{2}-{\mathbb{E}}X_{i}^{2}\right)\right|\geq t\right).

Notice that
{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}=d\sigma_{X}^{2}
. When
\alpha\geq 4
, an application of Theorem
1
gives that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|^{2}-d\sigma_{X}^{2}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{dK^{4}},\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{d^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right).

(A.12)

Moreover, for any
s
satisfying

\displaystyle\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s,

it follows from Lemma
10
that

\displaystyle\left|\|X\|^{2}-d\sigma_{X}^{2}\right|\geq\max\{s^{2},\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}s\}.

Consequently, for any
s\geq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|^{2}-d\sigma_{X}^{2}\right|\geq\max\{s^{2},\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}s\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{dK^{4}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}},\frac{s^{\alpha}}{d^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}K^{\alpha}},\frac{s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sigma_{X}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{d^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-1}K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right).

In other words, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|X\|-\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-Cd\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{K^{4}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}},\frac{s^{\alpha}}{K^{\alpha}},\frac{s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sigma_{X}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right).

Obviously, when
s\leq\sigma_{X}
, the maximum term on the right-hand side of the expression above is
\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}}
. When
s
is between
\sigma_{X}
and
K
, the maximum term is
\frac{s^{4}}{K^{4}}
. When
s\geq K
, the maximum term is
\frac{s^{\alpha}}{K^{\alpha}}
. Thus, we can simplify the above expression as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|X\|-\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-Cd\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{K^{4}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}},\frac{s^{\alpha}}{K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right).

It remains to analyze the norm for the scenario of
\alpha\in[2,4]
. In view of Theorem
1
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|^{2}-d\sigma_{X}^{2}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{dK^{4}},\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{d^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right).

Further, an application of Lemma
10
shows that for any
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s\right)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|^{2}-d\sigma_{X}^{2}\right|\geq\max\left\{s^{2},\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}s\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-C\min\left\{\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{dK^{4}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}}\right\},\max\left\{\frac{s^{\alpha}}{d^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}K^{\alpha}},\frac{s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sigma_{X}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{d^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-1}K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right\}\right),

which can be simplified as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|X\|-\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-Cd\min\left\{\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{K^{4}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}}\right\},\max\left\{\frac{s^{\alpha}}{K^{\alpha}},\frac{s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sigma_{X}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right\}\right).

Indeed, when
s\leq\sigma_{X}
, the tail probability is dominated by term
\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}}
. When
s\in[\sigma_{X},K]
, the tail probability is dominated by term
\exp\left(-Cd\frac{s^{4}}{K^{4}}\right)
. When
s\geq K
, the tail probability is dominated by term
\frac{s^{\alpha}}{K^{\alpha}}
. Therefore, we can further simplify the tail probability as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|X\|-\sigma_{X}\right|\geq s\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-Cd\min\left\{\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{K^{4}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}s^{2}}{K^{4}}\right\},\frac{s^{\alpha}}{K^{\alpha}}\right\}\right).

This concludes the proof of Corollary
6
.

(A.13)

leads to

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\|X\|-\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}}\right|\geq s\right)\\

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-C\max\left\{\frac{s^{2\alpha}}{d^{\alpha-1}L^{\alpha}},\frac{s^{4}}{dL^{2}},\frac{s^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}}{L^{2}},\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\left.\min\left\{\max\left\{\frac{s^{4}}{d\sigma^{2}},\frac{\sigma_{X}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}s^{2}\right\},\max\left\{\frac{s^{2}}{L},\frac{s\sqrt{d}\sigma_{X}}{L}\right\}\right\}\right\}\right).

.

C.5
Proof of Lemma
4

The following proof is based on the concentration inequality presented in Theorem
3
and a standard
\varepsilon
-net argument. We refer interested readers to
33
and
36
for the detailed discussion of
\varepsilon
-net. From the definition of the operator norm, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|=\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d_{2}-1}}\left\{\frac{1}{d_{1}}\left\|Xu\right\|^{2}-u^{\top}\Sigma u\right\}.

In particular, vector
Xu
consists of independent components

\displaystyle Xu=\left(\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{\top}u\\
X_{2}^{\top}u\\
\vdots\\
X_{d_{1}}^{\top}u\end{matrix}\right),

which arises from the independent row vectors of
X
. Additionally, it should be noted that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(X_{i}^{\top}u\right)^{2}=u^{\top}\Sigma u.

Hence, when
\alpha\geq 2
, an application of Theorem
3
yields for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{d_{1}}\left\|Xu\right\|^{2}-u^{\top}\Sigma u\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(-Cd_{1}\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{L^{\alpha}},\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sigma^{2}},\frac{t}{L}\right\}\right\}\right).

(A.15)

We emphasize that on the right-hand side of the above expression, the coefficient of
\exp
is one instead of
2
, which is because the above expression bounds a mean-zero random variable rather than its absolute value. On the other hand, by the standard arguments of
\varepsilon
-net (see details in the classical books
33
and
36
), we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|\leq 2\sup_{u\in\mathcal{N}}\left\{\frac{1}{d_{1}}\left\|Xu\right\|^{2}-u^{\top}\Sigma u\right\},

where
\mathcal{N}
is a
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
-net of
{\mathbb{S}}^{d_{2}-1}
with cardinality
|\mathcal{N}|\leq 5^{n}
. Then after taking the union over
\mathcal{N}
in (
A.15
), we can obtain that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|\geq 2t\right)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{u\in\mathcal{N}}\left|\frac{1}{d_{1}}\left\|Xu\right\|^{2}-u^{\top}\Sigma u\right|\geq t\right)\\
\quad\leq 5^{d_{2}}\cdot\exp\left(-Cd_{1}\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{L^{\alpha}},\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sigma^{2}},\frac{t}{L}\right\}\right\}\right),

which is in fact equivalent to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|\geq C\min\left\{L\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\sigma\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+L\cdot\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t).

It remains to consider the case when
\alpha\in[1,2]
. In light of Theorem
3
, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{d_{1}}\left\|Xu\right\|^{2}-u^{\top}\Sigma u\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(-Cd_{1}\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sigma^{2}},\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{L^{\alpha}},\frac{t}{L}\right\}\right\}\right).

(A.16)

Similar to the arguments for the case of
\alpha\geq 2
, (
A.16
) furthers implies that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|\geq 2t\right)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{u\in\mathcal{N}}\left|\frac{1}{d_{1}}\left\|Xu\right\|^{2}-u^{\top}\Sigma u\right|\geq t\right)\\
\quad\leq 5^{d_{2}}\cdot\exp\left(-Cd_{1}\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sigma^{2}},\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{L^{\alpha}},\frac{t}{L}\right\}\right\}\right),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|\geq C\sigma\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+CL\min\left\{\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t).

This concludes the proof of Lemma
4
.

C.6
Proof of Theorem
7

The proof of Theorem
7
relies on the results in Lemma
4
and the well-known Weyl’s Inequality
[
37
]
. Recall that Lemma
4
proves that when
\alpha\geq 2
, with probability over

,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|\leq C\min\left\{L\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\sigma\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+L\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right\}.

(A.17)

An application of Weyl’s Inequality
[
37
]
gives that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{1}}s_{\max}^{2}(X)\leq M_{\max}^{2}+\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|.

Consequently, the above result together with (
A.17
) entails that for any

\displaystyle s_{\max}^{2}(X)\leq d_{1}M_{\max}^{2}+C\min\left\{L(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\,\sigma(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+L(d_{2}+t)\right\}

holds with probability over
1-\exp(-t)
. Additionally, Weyl’s inequality yields that

\frac{1}{d_{1}}s_{\min}^{2}(X)\geq M_{\min}^{2}-\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|,

and thus, we have the following lower bound on the smallest nonzero singular value under (
A.17
)

\displaystyle s_{\min}^{2}(X)\geq d_{1}M_{\min}^{2}-C\min\left\{L(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\,\sigma(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+L(d_{2}+t)\right\}.

It remains to investigate the case of
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
. In view of Lemma
4
, we have that with probability over
1-\exp(-t)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{d_{1}}X^{\top}X-\Sigma\right\|\leq C\sigma\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\min\left\{L\left(\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},L\frac{t+d_{2}}{d_{1}}\right\}.

(A.18)

Similar to the arguments for the case of
\alpha\geq 2
, it holds with probability over
1-\exp(-t)
that

\displaystyle s_{\max}^{2}(X)\leq d_{1}M_{\max}^{2}+C\sigma(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\min\left\{L(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\,L(d_{2}+t)\right\}

and

\displaystyle s_{\min}^{2}(X)\geq d_{1}M_{\min}^{2}-C\sigma(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}-C\min\left\{L(d_{2}+t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\,L(d_{2}+t)\right\}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
7
.

C.7
Proof of Lemma
5

The proof of Lemma
5
employs Theorem
3
and the standard
\varepsilon
-net argument
[
33
,
36
]
. It follows from the definition of the Euclidean norm that

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\mu}-\mu\right\|=\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\left(\widehat{\mu}-\mu\right)^{\top}u.

For any fixed
u\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
, we see that

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\mu}-\mu\right)^{\top}u=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)^{\top}u

is the sum of
n
i.i.d. random variables. Hence, for
\alpha\geq 2
, an application of Theorem
3
gives that for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)^{\top}u\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(-Cn\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{L^{\alpha}},\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sigma^{2}},\frac{t}{L}\right\}\right\}\right).

A standard
\varepsilon
-net argument (more details can be found in
33
and
36
) leads to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\widehat{\mu}-\mu\right\|\geq 2t\right)\leq 5^{d}\cdot\exp\left(-Cn\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{L^{\alpha}},\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sigma^{2}},\frac{t}{L}\right\}\right\}\right),

which is in fact equivalent to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\widehat{\mu}-\mu\right\|\geq C\min\left\{L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\sigma\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+L\cdot\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t).

On the other hand, when
\alpha\in[1,2]
, by invoking Theorem
3
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)^{\top}u\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(-Cn\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\sigma^{2}},\max\left\{\frac{t^{\alpha}}{L^{\alpha}},\frac{t}{L}\right\}\right\}\right).

It then implies the following bound on the norm of
\widehat{\mu}-\mu

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\widehat{\mu}-\mu\right\|\geq C\sigma\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+CL\min\left\{\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t),

which concludes the proof of Lemma
5
.

C.8
Proof of Theorem
8

The estimation error of the sample covariance matrix can be decomposed into two terms. One term is the square of the mean estimation error, and another term is the quantity given in Lemma
4
. With some calculations,
\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\widehat{\mu})(X_{i}-\widehat{\mu})^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)(X_{i}-\mu)^{\top}-\Sigma-(\widehat{\mu}-\mu)(\widehat{\mu}-\mu)^{\top}\right\|.

Then by resorting to the triangle inequality,
\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|
can be upper bounded as

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|&\leq\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)(X_{i}-\mu)^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|+\left\|(\widehat{\mu}-\mu)(\widehat{\mu}-\mu)^{\top}\right\|\\
&=\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)(X_{i}-\mu)^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|+\big\|\widehat{\mu}-\mu\big\|^{2}.\end{split}

(A.19)

In particular, the first term on the right-hand side of (
A.19
) above can be bounded by Lemma
4
, while the second term above is the squared estimation error of the mean that is given by Lemma
5
. To this end, it remains to verify the conditions of Lemmas
4
and
5
.

An application of Jensen’s inequality gives that

{\mathbb{E}}\left|\left((X-\mu)^{\top}u\right)^{2}-{\mathbb{E}}\left((X-\mu)^{\top}u\right)^{2}\right|^{k}\\
\leq 2^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\left|\left((X-\mu)^{\top}u\right)\right|^{2k}+2^{k}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left((X-\mu)^{\top}u\right)^{2}\right)^{k}.

(A.20)

Additionally, it follows from the conditions of Theorem
8
that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left|\left((X-\mu)^{\top}u\right)\right|^{2k}\leq 2^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}k^{\frac{k}{\alpha/2}}\cdot\sigma^{2}L^{2k-2},\quad{\mathbb{E}}\left((X-\mu)^{\top}u\right)^{2}\leq 2^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\sigma^{2},

which lead to the following upper bound for (
A.20
)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left|\left((X-\mu)^{\top}u\right)^{2}-{\mathbb{E}}\left((X-\mu)^{\top}u\right)^{2}\right|^{k}\leq 2^{1+k/\alpha}k^{\frac{k}{\alpha/2}}\cdot(\sigma L)^{2}\cdot(L^{2})^{k-2}.

Hence, we have verified the condition of Lemma
4
; that is, the condition of Lemma
4
is satisfied with
(\frac{\alpha}{2},\sigma L,4L^{2})
. Consequently, an application of Lemma
4
yields that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)(X_{i}-\mu)^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|\geq C\min\left\{L^{2}\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\sigma L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+L^{2}\cdot\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t)\ \ \text{ when }\alpha\geq 4,

and

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu)(X_{i}-\mu)^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|\geq C\sigma L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right.\\
\left.+C\min\left\{L^{2}\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},L^{2}\cdot\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t)\ \ \text{ when }2\leq\alpha\leq 4.

So far, we have finished bounding the first term on the right-hand side of (
A.19
) above. It remains to analyze the second term on the right-hand side of (
A.19
). Regarding the second term, Lemma
5
proves that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\|\widehat{\mu}-\mu\|\geq C\min\left\{L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\sigma\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+L\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\|\widehat{\mu}-\mu\|^{2}\geq C\min\left\{L^{2}\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},\sigma^{2}\cdot\frac{t+d}{n}+L^{2}\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{2}\right\}\right)\leq\exp(-t).

Therefore, putting the above results together, we can obtain that when
\alpha\geq 4
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|\geq C\min\left\{L^{2}\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},\sigma L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+L^{2}\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq 2\exp(-t),

and when
2\leq\alpha\leq 4
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|\geq C\sigma L\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\min\left\{L^{2}\left(\frac{t+d}{n}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},L^{2}\frac{t+d}{n}\right\}\right)\leq 2\exp(-t).

This completes the proof of Theorem
8
.

Appendix D
Technical Lemmas

We provide in this section some technical lemmas and their proofs.

Lemma 8
.

Let
K,\tau>0
be any given numbers. Then for any
q\leq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}t^{q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt\leq\tau^{q}K\exp\left(-\frac{\tau}{K}\right),

and consequently,
\int_{\tau}^{\infty}t^{q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt\leq K^{q+1}\exp\left(-\frac{\tau}{K}\right)
when
\tau/K\geq 1
. For any
q\geq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}t^{q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt\leq K^{q+1}\sqrt{\Gamma\left(2q+1\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{\tau}{2K}\right).

Proof
.
For the case of
q\leq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}t^{q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt\leq\tau^{q}K\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\frac{1}{K}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt=\tau^{q}K\exp\left(-\frac{\tau}{K}\right),

which entails that

\int_{\tau}^{\infty}t^{q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt\leq K^{q+1}\exp\left(-\frac{\tau}{K}\right)

when
\tau/K\geq 1
. It remains to consider the case when
q\geq 0
. By resorting to Hölder’s inequality, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}t^{q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt\leq\left(\int_{\tau}^{\infty}t^{2q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt=K\exp\left(-\frac{\tau}{K}\right)

and

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}t^{2q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt=K^{2q+1}\Gamma\left(2q+1\right).

Thus, when
q\geq 0
we can obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}t^{q}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{K}\right)\,dt\leq K^{q+1}\sqrt{\Gamma\left(2q+1\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{\tau}{2K}\right).

This concludes the proof of Lemma
8
.

Lemma 9
.

Assume that
X
is a mean zero random variable and satisfies that
\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}<\infty
for some
\alpha\geq 1
. Denote by
\sigma_{X}^{2}:=\operatorname{var}(X)
. Then it holds that for some universal constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{3}\leq C\sigma_{X}^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Proof
. Observe that for any
\tau>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{3}={\mathbb{E}}X^{2}\cdot|X|={\mathbb{E}}X^{2}\cdot|X|\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|\geq\tau\}+{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}\cdot|X|\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|\leq\tau\}.

The second term on the right-hand side of the expression above can be bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}\cdot|X|\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|\leq\tau\}\leq\tau{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}=\tau\sigma_{X}^{2}.

It remains to consider the first term above, which can be bounded as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}\cdot|X|\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\{|X|\geq\tau\}

\displaystyle\leq\left({\mathbb{E}}X^{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\mathbb{P}}\left(|X|\geq\tau\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}^{3}\exp\left(-c\left(\frac{\tau}{\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}\right)^{\alpha}\right).

Hence, by taking
\tau=C\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{3}\leq C\sigma_{X}^{2}\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2\|X\|_{\Psi_{\alpha}}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
9
.

Lemma 10
.

For any nonnegative values
a,b\geq 0
and any
t>0
, if
|a-b|\geq t
, we have that
|a^{2}-b^{2}|\geq\max\{bt,t^{2}\}
.

Proof
.
We will bound
|a^{2}-b^{2}|
for the cases when
a\geq b
and
a\leq b
separately. When
a\geq b
, it follows from
|a-b|\geq t
that
a\geq t+b
, which along with
b\geq 0
and
t>0
entails that

|a^{2}-b^{2}|=a^{2}-b^{2}\geq(t+b)^{2}-b^{2}=t^{2}+2bt\geq\max\{t^{2},bt\}.

When
a\leq b
, it follows from
|a-b|\geq t
that
a\leq b-t
, which together with
a\geq 0
and
t>0
yields that

|a^{2}-b^{2}|=b^{2}-a^{2}\geq 2bt-t^{2}\geq bt=\max\{bt,t^{2}\}.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
10
.
