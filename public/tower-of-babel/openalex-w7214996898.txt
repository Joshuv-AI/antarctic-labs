ABSTRACT: Environmental Education in schools is essential for fostering a critical understanding of
socio-environmental interactions, particularly when combined with playful and regionalized teaching
strategies. This study aimed to develop and evaluate the applicability of the board game “Rio Abaixo,
Rio Arriba” for teaching environmental impacts associated with the Parnaíba River to 6th-grade
elementary school students. The qualitative-applied research involved 30 students from a state public
school in Teresina, Piauí, Brazil. The pedagogical intervention included an expository lecture (50 min),
pre- and post-intervention questionnaires, and two rounds of the board game, which features 57 spaces
integrating geographic features, human activities, and reward/penalty mechanics. In the perception
diagnosis, waste disposal, sewage discharge, and riparian deforestation emerged as the most critical
issues (70% of indications each; n=21), whereas predatory fishing was underestimated by students
(76.7% rated it as minor impact; n=23). Regarding the evaluation of the educational tool, 60% (18/30) of
students reported that the game facilitated learning, and 70% (21/30) rated the methodology as excellent,
reaching 96.7% positive or neutral approval. The findings demonstrate high acceptance of the activity
and suggest that regionalized playful learning can enhance Science education and environmental
awareness in basic education.
Keywords: Environmental education. Didactic game. Science education. Playful learning. Teaching
resources.

1 INTRODUCTION
Environmental issues have assumed an increasingly prominent role in educational
contexts due to the need to understand the relationships between society and the environment
and their consequences for natural resources. In Brazil, the National Curriculum Parameters
incorporated the environment as a cross-cutting theme, encouraging its integration across
different curricular components and its connection to students’ realities (BRASIL, 1998).
Subsequently, the National Environmental Education Policy (PNEA) established
environmental education as an integrated, continuous, and permanent educational practice
across different levels and modalities of formal education (BRASIL, 1999).
Environmental education is not limited to the transmission of information about
environmental problems. Nunes (1998) describes it as an integrated process related to
interactions between human beings and the environment, whereas Guerra and Abílio (2006)
emphasize its interactive, participatory, and critical nature. Moura (2009) highlights the
articulation of different forms of knowledge in the construction of environmental thinking,
while Leff (2006) relates the complexity of environmental issues to the need for
interdisciplinary methods. From this perspective, school-based approaches can integrate

In science education, geographic and territorial contextualization can bring school
content closer to situations experienced by students. The use of local river systems and regional
ecosystems can foster scientific literacy and strengthen affective bonds and a sense of belonging
(DIAS et al., 2021). The Parnaíba River provides favorable conditions for this type of approach
because of its historical, economic, and environmental importance to the state of Piauí and its
direct relationship with different municipalities and local populations. At the same time,
environmental changes observed along its course allow the river to serve as an organizing
element linking scientific knowledge, everyday life, and environmental education.
The incorporation of environmental content into schools, however, requires strategies
that move beyond exclusively content-centered approaches or rote memorization (SANTOS
et al., 2021). Guimarães (2008) draws attention to educational practices that reduce
environmental education to the transmission of information about behaviors considered
appropriate. Barcelos (2008), in turn, emphasizes the importance of creating spaces for
interaction and participation that are consistent with the values discussed throughout the
educational process. Active and playful methodologies, such as educational and board games,
have emerged as alternatives capable of placing students at the center of the learning process
and fostering agency, autonomy, and critical thinking (DIAS et al., 2021; SANTOS et al., 2021;
SMANIOTTO et al., 2022; PINTO et al., 2025).
Play-based activities represent a promising approach for addressing this content. Play is
part of child development and can promote interaction, autonomy, and sociability
(OLIVEIRA, 2002). In educational settings, game-based situations can bring students closer to
school content in an engaging manner (KISHIMOTO, 1994). Caillois (1986) associates the
playful dimension with freedom and spontaneity, whereas Ronca (1989) emphasizes its
contribution to different aspects of knowledge construction. Educational games focused on
biodiversity and regional environmental impacts have shown considerable potential to
stimulate students’ interest, strengthen concept retention, and encourage conservation-oriented
behaviors across different Brazilian biomes and contexts (DIAS et al., 2021; SANTOS et al.,
2021; BUENO et al., 2024; PINTO et al., 2025; ALMEIDA et al., 2026).

2 MATERIALS AND METHODS
2.1 STUDY AREA AND CHARACTERIZATION OF RESEARCH PARTICIPANTS
The Parnaíba River basin was used as the geographic reference for the study. The river
rises in the Chapada das Mangabeiras and extends for approximately 1,485 km, forming an
important river system in northeastern Brazil. According to Baptista (1989), the basin lies
mainly in the state of Piauí, with portions extending into Maranhão and Ceará. Based on its
longitudinal extent, the river is divided into three reaches: Upper Parnaíba, from the
headwaters to the Boa Esperança Dam; Middle Parnaíba, from the dam to the confluence of the
Poti and Parnaíba rivers in Teresina; and Lower Parnaíba, from this confluence to its mouth in
the Atlantic Ocean (DIAS, 2004).
The basin spans a transition between semiarid and humid conditions, accompanied by
variations in vegetation, including steppe formations, seasonal forests, and Cerrado (IBGE,
1996). The river supports multiple uses, including water supply, irrigation, transportation,
power generation, agriculture, and fishing.
The development of the game encompassed 14 municipalities and strategic locations
along the river course: Santa Filomena, Ribeiro Gonçalves, Uruçuí, Porto Alegre do Piauí,
Guadalupe, Floriano, Amarante, Palmeirais, Nazária, Teresina, União, Miguel Alves,
Luzilândia, Porto, and the Parnaíba Delta, preserving the territorial scope used in the original
study.
The practical intervention was conducted in September 2012 at Unidade Escolar
Deputado Tertuliano Milton Brandão, a state public school located at Rua Anchieta, No. 2401,
Dirceu Arcoverde I neighborhood, Teresina, Piauí. The participants comprised a purposive
sample of 30 students regularly enrolled in the sixth grade of Elementary School, a stage in
which water- and ecosystem-related content is included in the Science curriculum. Prior to
implementation, the study was submitted to and approved by the Ethics Committee of the
Federal University in March 2010, before the implementation of the “Plataforma Brasil” system.

2.2 DEVELOPMENT OF THE EDUCATIONAL GAME “RIO ABAIXO, RIO ARRIBA”
The game was developed based on the principles of play-based learning as a resource for
bringing students closer to school content and on the use of regionally contextualized teaching
materials (KISHIMOTO, 1994; OLIVEIRA, 2002; SANTOS et al., 2021). The physical
structure of the game “Trilhas da Liberdade”, developed by environmental analysts from the
Brazilian Institute of the Environment and Renewable Natural Resources in Piauí (IBAMAPI), was used as a reference and adapted to address the hydrological and socio-environmental
context of the Parnaíba River.
The thematic content was selected between March and June 2012 through a literature
review and the collection of information on Environmental Education, the Parnaíba River, and
environmental impacts associated with its course. Photographic collections available on
regional websites and documentaries were consulted, and field photographs were produced by
the research team along the Parnaíba River in Teresina and in its Delta, using a 5-m aluminum
boat equipped with a 15-hp outboard motor. Thirteen environmental themes were selected: solid
waste and other pollutants, recycling, riparian forest removal, wildlife, predatory fishing, fish
spawning season, mangrove destruction, water use and importance, IBAMA environmental
enforcement, erosion, siltation, dredging, and hydropower generation. The selection sought to
represent situations observed and/or discussed in different locations associated with the
Parnaíba River.
The Rio Abaixo, Rio Arriba game was structured as a path-based board game containing
57 numbered spaces. The spaces, which include images and explanatory texts, represent cities
along the basin, ecological characteristics of the river, and problem situations governed by a
color-coded system distinguishing three types of situations: 1) red for penalties associated with
environmentally degrading actions, which could result in missing a turn or moving backward
along the path; 2) purple for explanatory stops; and 3) yellow for rewards associated with
sustainable actions, allowing players to advance along the path. The game uses one die and
individual markers and was designed for two to four players per board (Figure 1).
The environmental problems incorporated into the board path included, among others,
removal of riparian vegetation, erosion, siltation, wildfires, fishing during the fish breeding
season, waste disposal, water pollution, and hydropower generation at the Boa Esperança Dam.

2.4 DATA COLLECTION INSTRUMENTS AND DATA ORGANIZATION
In July 2012, two semi-structured questionnaires consisting of open- and closed-ended
questions were developed. The first instrument assessed aspects related to students’ perceptions
of the environmental impacts addressed during the lecture. Students evaluated floodplain
cultivation, riparian forest removal, washing cars with soap on riverbanks, waste disposal,
dredging, predatory fishing, erosion and siltation, and recycling. They were also asked which
problems associated with the Parnaíba River had most caught their attention.
The second questionnaire addressed the participants’ experience with the game and their
perceptions of using such activities in the classroom. The instrument included questions about
feelings associated with participating in games, perceptions of classes using differentiated
activities, ease of learning, and evaluation of the game. Responses to the closed-ended questions
were summarized descriptively as absolute and percentage frequencies, whereas the open-ended
responses were used to complement the interpretation of participants’ perceptions.
Data obtained from the questionnaires administered to the students were tabulated and
analyzed using descriptive statistics (absolute and percentage frequencies) and qualitative
analysis of the participants’ justifications.
3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1 STUDENTS’ PERCEPTIONS OF ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACTS ON THE
PARNAÍBA RIVER
During the lecture, students were observed to have prior knowledge of recycling and
proper waste disposal, which facilitated dialogue and the contextualization of the board-game
topics during the activity. The content presented was intended to complement the information
incorporated into the board and to contextualize the situations subsequently encountered
during the game.
When asked about the extent to which specific human activities harmed the
environment, waste disposal into the river received a unanimous response, being classified as
highly harmful by 100% (n=30) of the students. Riparian forest removal was considered highly
harmful by 27 students (90%), slightly harmful by one (3.3%), and not harmful by two (6.7%).
Washing vehicles with soap along the riverbanks received 26 responses in the highly harmful
category (86.7%) and four in the slightly harmful category (13.3%). Erosion and siltation were
considered highly harmful by 22 students (73.3%), slightly harmful by seven, and not harmful

The emphasis placed on waste, sewage, and deforestation demonstrates the participants’
attention to situations directly associated with the landscape and with human activities
discussed during the intervention. Using the Parnaíba River as a reference made it possible to
link environmental content to a regional geographic feature, thereby bringing Environmental
Education closer to the students’ context. This approach is consistent with the understanding
that Environmental Education should integrate knowledge and participation rather than being
limited to the transmission of information (GUERRA; ABÍLIO, 2006; BARCELOS, 2008).
These findings indicate that students associate environmental degradation with visible
disturbances in their urban surroundings and the riverine landscape. Their strong awareness of
waste, sewage, and riparian forest removal is consistent with the findings of Santos et al. (2021)
in a study using the game De Olho no Velho Chico in the Lower São Francisco region, and
with those of Silva et al. (2024) involving students in Amapá. In both studies, proximity to
local rivers led students to readily identify sewage and waste as direct threats to water quality
and the health of riverine populations. Territorial contextualization through a regional river,
such as the Parnaíba or São Francisco, can serve as a framework for scientific literacy by
moving beyond decontextualized teaching and connecting science to students’ everyday
realities (SANTOS et al., 2021).
On the other hand, a clear atypical pattern was observed in students’ perceptions of
predatory fishing: 23 students (76.7%) classified it as slightly harmful, whereas only seven

incorporated explanations, penalties, and rewards, allowed concepts to be revisited throughout
the course of play. This organization is consistent with the use of interdisciplinary approaches
in Environmental Education (LEFF, 2006; PHILIPPI JR.; ROMERO; BRUNA, 2004).
The findings, however, refer to an experience conducted with a single class of 30
students and primarily represent the responses and perceptions obtained after the activity.
Accordingly, they allow the receptiveness of the game and the content identified by the
participants to be assessed within the investigated context, but they do not support
generalization to other student populations.

In this sense, the game extends beyond a recreational function by representing

elements associated with the Parnaíba River. The combination of rules, images, and
environmental consequences transforms a regional socioecological system into an object of
learning and promotes the integration of scientific knowledge, local reality, and reflection on
conservation. Its educational potential, however, depends on articulation with clear pedagogical
objectives and sustained Environmental Education actions.
3.4 RELATIONSHIP TO THE UNITED NATIONS SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT
GOALS
The proposal can also be discussed in the context of the 2030 Agenda. Its most direct
relationship is with Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG 4 – Quality Education),
particularly Target 4.7, which focuses on ensuring that learners acquire the knowledge and
skills needed to promote sustainable development. Socio-environmental education associated
with the 2030 Agenda requires methodologies that connect environmental problems, civic
competencies, and ecosocial transformation across local and global scales (MORALES
HERNÁNDEZ; CAURÍN ALONSO; LACRUZ PÉREZ, 2023).
By contextualizing learning through situations associated with the Parnaíba River, the
game also relates to SDG 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation) by addressing sewage, waste, and
water quality; SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production) through its treatment of
waste disposal and recycling; and SDG 15 (Life on Land) by incorporating riparian forests,

4 FINAL CONSIDERATIONS
The development and implementation of the “Rio Abaixo, Rio Arriba” board game
demonstrated the feasibility and favorable receptiveness of playful educational methodologies
for Environmental Education among sixth-grade Elementary School students. By adopting the
Parnaíba River basin as the territorial framework, the intervention achieved its objective of
bringing school-based scientific knowledge closer to students’ socio-environmental
experiences, thereby promoting the contextualization of biological content and scientific
literacy.
Analysis of students’ perceptions revealed marked sensitivity to environmental impacts
that were visually prominent in the urban and riverine landscape, particularly solid-waste
pollution (waste disposal), effluent discharge (sewage), and riparian forest degradation.
Conversely, the atypical classification of predatory fishing as a lower-severity impact revealed
a perceptual gap that underscores the pedagogical relevance of the playful resource: the game
and its dynamics can serve not only to diagnose simplistic perceptions but also to provide
conceptual foundations that require moments of teacher-mediated discussion (debriefing) and
targeted reflective mechanisms.
From a pedagogical perspective, the tool proved capable of combining entertainment,
social interaction, and reinforcement of essential ecological concepts through its dynamics. The
experience indicates that educational games can serve as teaching resources for Environmental
Education when linked to clearly defined objectives and situations that are close to students’
everyday realities. In the present case, using the Parnaíba River as a contextual reference made
