Deployable real-time spinal endoscopic instance segmentation with lightweight multi-scale attention mechanism
Authors: Qi Lai, Qiang Cai, JunYan Li, Chi Man Vong, Lei Jin, Lei Wang, Tao Yan, XiaoKun Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractReal-time instance segmentation in spinal endoscopy is vital for identifying and protecting key anatomy, but is hampered by narrow views, specular highlights, smoke/bleeding artifacts, fuzzy boundaries, and large-scale variation. Deployment further demands accuracy, speed, and stability under small-batch (often batch-size-one) settings. We present EndoSeg-RT, a deployable real-time framework co-designed across backbone, neck, and head with lightweight multi-scale attention. The backbone, C2f-Pro, combines RepViT-style re-parameterized convolutions with efficient multi-scale attention: multi-branch training for rich features that collapses to a single low-latency path at inference. The neck strengthens cross-scale consistency and boundary quality via Scale-Sequence Feature Fusion and Triple Feature Encoding, enhancing high-resolution anatomy. The head uses a Lightweight Multi-task Shared Head with shared convolutions and GroupNorm to cut redundancy and stabilize batch-size-one training/inference. We also release a clinically reviewed PELD dataset of 610 images from 61 patients with instance-level labels for adipose tissue, bone, ligamentum flavum, and nerve. Extensive experiments show EndoSeg-RT delivers competitive or superior accuracy with markedly lower complexity—only 1.8M parameters and 8.8 GFLOPs—outperforming heavier instance segmentation baselines. The model generalizes well to a public dental instance segmentation benchmark, demonstrating robustness beyond spinal endoscopy. Code and dataset are publicly available at: https://github.com/hhwmortal/PELD-Instance-segmentation.Subject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Introduction

Spinal endoscopy has become an essential modality in minimally invasive spine surgery, offering reduced tissue disruption, faster postoperative recovery, and improved access to delicate anatomical regions1–3. During these procedures, surgeons operate within a constrained visual environment and must continuously identify and preserve critical anatomical structures-including the ligamentum flavum, neural elements, osseous landmarks, and surrounding adipose tissue-to avoid iatrogenic injury and ensure procedural safety4. Consequently, deployable real-time and accurate delineation of anatomical structures directly from endoscopic video streams represents a key unmet clinical need for intraoperative guidance and decision support.

Instance segmentation is particularly well suited to this setting, as it provides pixel-level localization while explicitly distinguishing individual anatomical entities, enabling actionable visual overlays for navigation, dissection planning, and risk awareness during surgery5–11. However, spinal endoscopic imaging constitutes an exceptionally challenging visual domain. Images are characterized by a narrow field of view, non-uniform illumination, frequent specular highlights, smoke and bleeding artifacts, ambiguous tissue boundaries, strong inter-class similarity, and pronounced scale variation across structures12. Achieving robust, low-latency instance segmentation under these conditions-while remaining deployable on resource-constrained surgical hardware-remains a major barrier to clinical translation13.

Recent advances in deep learning have driven substantial progress in medical image segmentation14,15, evolving from semantic labeling to instance-level understanding16–18. Two-stage frameworks such as Mask R-CNN5 often provide strong accuracy, but their computational complexity and inference latency limit their suitability for real-time intraoperative use19. Single-stage methods, including YOLACT6, SOLOv27, and recent YOLO-based variants11,20, offer improved speed and deployability but frequently struggle with boundary precision and sensitivity to small, clinically critical structures in visually complex endoscopic scenes. Transformer-based and hybrid architectures enhance global context modeling21,22, yet their memory and computational demands complicate stable deployment on edge devices such as endoscopy workstations. Despite numerous lightweight design strategies, there remains no end-to-end solution that simultaneously delivers fine-grained anatomical accuracy, consistent multi-scale fusion, and reliable real-time performance tailored to spinal endoscopy.

These challenges give rise to three persistent limitations in existing approaches. First, the persistent accuracy–efficiency trade-off under surgical constraints means that models with high accuracy are often too computationally heavy, while lightweight networks tend to degrade on fine boundaries and small structures such as thin ligaments or nerve margins. Second, insufficient multi-scale and fine-grained feature fusion leads to semantic–spatial misalignment, resulting in mask leakage or missed instances under glare, noise, and high tissue similarity. Third, limited robustness under small-batch training and domain variability—including batch-size sensitivity, class imbalance, and variations in anatomy and imaging conditions—reduces generalization and deployability.

To address these challenges, we propose EndoSeg-RT, a deployable real-time spinal endoscopic instance segmentation framework with lightweight multi-scale attention. EndoSeg-RT adopts a deployment-driven, top-down design that co-optimizes the backbone, neck, and head to balance anatomical fidelity with computational efficiency. The backbone alleviates the accuracy-efficiency bottleneck, the neck strengthens multi-scale and boundary-aware fusion, and the head improves stability under small-batch and real-time inference conditions typical of intraoperative deployment.

At the backbone level, EndoSeg-RT introduces C2f-Pro, which integrates Vision-Transformer-inspired re-parameterized blocks and efficient multi-scale attention into a lightweight convolutional framework. Multi-branch pathways enrich representation during training, while structural re-parameterization collapses them into a single low-latency path at inference, preserving complementary local and global context without the overhead of full transformer models. Building on this compact representation, the neck incorporates Scale-Sequence Feature Fusion (SSFF) and Triple Feature Encoding (TFE) to enhance cross-scale coherence and sharpen anatomical boundaries, particularly for small or visually ambiguous structures. A shared, GroupNorm-based segmentation head further ensures stable performance under batch-size-one inference, which is common in real-time surgical settings.

Results

Dataset definition

To evaluate EndoSeg-RT under realistic intraoperative conditions, we built a spinal endoscopic instance-segmentation dataset from percutaneous endoscopic lumbar discectomy (PELD) cases recorded on an IPS710A endoscopic system at Xiangyang Central Hospital. Digital videos from 61 patients were exported and parsed into frames. A standardized sampling-and-filtering pipeline was then applied: every video was sampled at 25 frames per second, followed by removal of frames with motion blur, severe overexposure, dominant instruments, or insufficient anatomical content. After screening, we retained 10 images per patient, yielding a total of 610 images at 1080 × 720 resolution (approximately 10 frames per case). The dataset can be download on https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Ha-UWBhVUrsgJWLx5Eo4igPxtwlViqcy/view.

Annotation protocol

Curated frames were annotated in LabelMe under the guidance of spine surgeons. Four clinically relevant structures were labeled with pixel-wise, instance-level masks: adipose tissue, bone, ligamentum flavum, and nerve (see Fig. 1). To control selection bias and enforce consistency, all data were manually annotated by two trained raters (R1, R2; PELD experience > 10 years) using a standardized protocol and nomenclature reviewed by a senior spine surgeon. An initial calibration phase with feedback ensured consistency. For each case, R1 and R2 produced the primary instance masks and class labels; disagreements were flagged and resolved in weekly adjudication meetings led by the expert (PELD experience > 15 years), with final masks approved by the expert once consensus was reached. We measured inter-rater agreement on a 10% double-annotated subset: mask-level agreement using Dice and boundary F1 score, and instance matching via IoU≥0.5. Mean Dice across classes is reported in the paper; per-class agreement and example adjudication cases are provided in the supplement. Colors are used only for visualization (purple: adipose; red: bone; yellow: ligamentum flavum; green: nerve). The training pipeline consumes masks exclusively without relying on color cues.

Procedural coverage and visual challenges

These labeled images span the typical PELD workflow and its visual challenges (Fig. 2). Early frames commonly show the establishment of the working canal with the ligamentum flavum covering deeper layers; subsequent steps include partial flavectomy that exposes subcutaneous fat and adjacent neural tissue; further clearance reveals bony landmarks and an intact nerve; and the procedure concludes with adequate decompression of the compressed nerve root. Consequently, the dataset naturally contains narrow fields of view, specular highlights, irrigation/blood contamination, and frequent instrument occlusions-conditions that stress-test small-structure segmentation.

Implementation details

Experimental settings

All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 GPU with 12 GB memory. Our software stack consisted of Python 3.10 and PyTorch 2.10, with CUDA 12.1 for GPU acceleration. Input images were resized to 640 × 640 pixels. We trained each model for 300 epochs using a batch size of 8. Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) was adopted as the optimizer with momentum set to 0.9 and an initial learning rate of 1.0 × 10−3. Unless otherwise specified, all other hyperparameters followed the defaults of the corresponding implementations.

Dataset split and statistics

For fair and reproducible evaluation across methods, we applied a stratified split that preserves the relative frequency of all four classes. The 610 images are divided into train/val/test with an 8:1:1 ratio (490/60/60). Because a single frame may not include every category, per-class instance counts are lower than the number of images. The final statistics are summarized in Table 1.

Evaluation metrics

Following14,15, we evaluate models using Dice, IoU, parameter count, FLOPs, and FPS. Dice and IoU quantify segmentation accuracy. The number of parameters reflects model size. FLOPs denote the computational cost for a single forward pass at a specified input resolution. FPS is the inverse of the average per-image inference time and, unless noted otherwise, excludes data loading and post-processing. All FPS measurements are obtained on the same hardware as Section “Implementation details” with a batch size of 1. Parameter counts and FLOPs are reported for the final trained models at an input resolution of 640 × 640 to ensure fair comparison.

Training details

We train with SGD (Nesterov) and a 300-epoch cosine LR schedule. The LR is linearly warmed from 0 to 1.0 × 10−3 in the first 5 epochs, then cosine-decays to 1.0 × 10−6 by epoch 300 (no restarts). We use momentum 0.9, weight decay 5 × 10−5, and exclude BatchNorm and bias from decay. The same setup is used for SE and EMA, with global batch size 16 and gradient clipping at 1.0. Full configs (optimizer, warmup, cosine params, seeds) are provided in configs/train.yaml.

Further analysis

Attention mechanism selection

To disentangle recognition quality from computational cost, we report accuracy and efficiency separately. Discussion

This work presents EndoSeg-RT, a lightweight multi-scale attention framework designed for deployable real-time instance segmentation in spinal endoscopy, where intraoperative scenes are characterized by narrow fields of view, specular glare, fluid interference, ambiguous tissue boundaries, and pronounced scale variation. EndoSeg-RT adopts a holistic, deployment-driven co-design across all network stages. Specifically, it introduces i) a C2f-Pro backbone that integrates RepViT-style re-parameterized convolution blocks with efficient multi-scale attention to achieve strong feature representation at low computational cost; ii) a dual-neck architecture combining Scale-Sequence Feature Fusion (SSFF) and Triple Feature Encoding (TFE) to enhance cross-scale consistency and boundary delineation; and iii) a Lightweight Multi-task Shared Head (LMSH) employing shared convolutions and GroupNorm to reduce parameter redundancy and ensure stable batch-size-one inference during real-time deployment.

In addition, we curate a clinician-reviewed PELD spinal endoscopy dataset comprising 610 images from 61 patients, with pixel-level instance annotations for adipose tissue, bone, ligamentum flavum, and neural structures, enabling standardized benchmarking in this domain. Extensive experiments demonstrate that EndoSeg-RT effectively balances segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency, achieving 74.4 mAP50 and 43.0 mAP50−95 on the PELD dataset with only 1.8 M parameters and 8.8 GFLOPs, while operating at ~189 FPS under batch-size-one inference at 640 × 640 resolution. Ablation studies further confirm that each proposed component contributes positively to segmentation performance and model compactness. Collectively, these results highlight EndoSeg-RT as a practical and robust solution for real-time spinal endoscopic guidance, with strong potential for clinical translation.

While EndoSeg-RT demonstrates competitive accuracy and notable efficiency on spinal endoscopic instance segmentation, several limitations remain:

i) Single-center data and modest scale. The PELD dataset is collected from a single clinical center with 61 patients and 610 images. Although clinically reviewed, its size and source may limit diversity in anatomy, device vendors, illumination conditions, and surgical styles. Multi-center, larger-scale cohorts are needed to improve generalizability.

ii) Image-only, frame-level evaluation. All experiments are performed on static frames at a fixed resolution (640 × 640). We do not exploit temporal coherence or video-based priors, which can stabilize predictions under rapid endoscope motion, occlusions, and smoke. Extending EndoSeg-RT to temporal models and evaluating end-to-end video performance are important future steps.

iii) Challenging artifacts and edge cases. Failure cases persist under severe specular highlights, heavy bleeding/irrigation, bone dust/smoke, and tool-induced occlusions, where thin boundaries (e.g., nerve margins, ligament edges) may be fragmented or leak. Artifact-aware augmentation, boundary-centric supervision, and uncertainty modeling could further mitigate these errors.

These limitations suggest several directions for future work: multi-center video datasets with richer artifact distribution; temporal and uncertainty-aware modeling; artifact-robust training schemes.

Dual-branch neck for fusion

Spinal endoscopy requires a neck that fuses multi-scale features while preserving thin, low-contrast boundaries under strict real-time constraints. Building on the aligned outputs of Backbone, we structure the neck as two tightly coupled stages: first, Sequence-Style Scale Fusion (SSFF) exchanges information across scales in a single pass; then, Target-Focused Enhancement (TFE) leverages the fused semantics to refine the highest-resolution features where small structures reside. This top-down flow-from global fusion to local emphasis-keeps the design coherent and latency-aware.
