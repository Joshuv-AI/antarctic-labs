Gang-Kim-Yoon integrality conjectures on adjoint Reidemeister torsions for torus knots
Yuji Terashima, Yoshikazu Yamaguchi

Abstract
. We study the conjecture that a sum of the ( g − 1 ) (g-1) -st powers of adjoint Reidemeister torsions for a torus knot is an integer. We prove that the conjecture is true for any torus knot and all g ≥ 0 g\geq 0 . To prove the conjecture, we introduce the Verlinde numbers for torus knots from the viewpoint of modular S S -matrix and show the recursion formulas and initial values of them. The recursion formulas of Verlinde numbers prove the integrality of the sum of the ( g − 1 ) (g-1) -st powers of adjoint Reidemeister torsions. Related to a modular S S -matrix, we also provide a birational model of the character variety for a torus knot and show how to recover the adjoint Reidemeister torsion for a torus knot from the Hessian of the polynomial defining the birational model.

1.
Introduction

Recently, Gang, Kim, and Yoon
[
GKY
]
proposed a striking conjecture stating that, for a
3
-manifold and any non-negative integer
g
, a sum of the
(g-1)
-st powers of adjoint Reidemeister torsions for irreducible representations of the fundamental group is an integer. This conjecture is based on the
3
d–
3
d correspondence
[
TY1
,
TY2
,
DG
,
DGG
,
CCV
]
, a physical duality that relates geometry of a
3
-manifold
M
to
3
-dimensional gauge theory labeled by
M
. More concretely, via the
3
d–
3
d correspondence, they identify the index of a 3-dimensional gauge theory on the product of a genus
g
surface and a circle with the sum of adjoint Reidemeister torsions above, and derive the conjecture from the integrality of the index. See also
[
GK
,
GKP
,
BGP
]
.

In this paper, we prove this integrality conjecture for the case of complements of torus knots, for arbitrary non-negative integers
g
. The key idea of the proof is to introduce modular
S
-matrices for torus knots, formulated as generalizations of the modular
S
-matrices of Wess-Zumino-Witten models, and to interpret the sum of adjoint Reidemeister torsions above as a generalization of the Verlinde numbers of Wess-Zumino-Witten models. We then prove the conjecture as follows.

Theorem

(Theorem
5.5
)
.

The following sum of the adjoint Reidemeister torsions for any
(p,\,q)
-torus knot exterior on a level set of the trace function of the meridian
\mu
at a generic
c\in\mathbb{C}

\sum_{[\rho]\in\mathrm{tr}_{\mu}^{-1}(c)}(2\mathop{\mathbb{T}_{\mu}}\nolimits(\rho))^{g-1}

is an integer for all
g\geq 0
.

This theorem is derived by showing that this generalized Verlinde number satisfies a recursion relation with respect to the genus
g
. The integrality of the initial value in the recursion follows from a result of
[
TrY
]
; in fact, we show that for all torus knots, each sequence
\{\sum_{[\rho]\in\mathrm{tr}_{\mu}^{-1}(c)}(2\mathop{\mathbb{T}_{\mu}}\nolimits(\rho))^{g-1}\}
on
g
starts with the common value
1
at
g=0
, that is, it holds that
\sum_{[\rho]\in\mathrm{tr}_{\mu}^{-1}(c)}(2\mathop{\mathbb{T}_{\mu}}\nolimits(\rho))^{-1}=1
for all torus knots.

It is expected that the modular
S
-matrix above for torus knots will play an important role in research that links mathematics of knot theory with physics of gauge theory such as a project to associate modular tensor categories to 3-manifolds
[
CGK
,
CQW
]
, knot-quiver correspondence
[
KRSS1
,
KRSS2
,
EKL
]
and knot-gauge correspondence
[
MTT
]
.

In this paper, we focus on
S
-matrices derived from plane algebraic curves which is called
Chebyshev curves
.
We propose a rational model of the character varieties of torus knot groups as the resolution of Chebyshev curves (see Theorem
2.8
) and recover the adjoint Reidemeister torsions of a torus knot from the Hessians of the defining polynomial of a Chebyshev curve (see Theorem
3.1
) at a critical point as follows.

Set
F(X,Y)=C_{p}(X)-C_{q}(Y)
for coprime integers
p
and
q
where
C_{k}(z)
is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind (see SubSect.
2.2
for the details) and denote by
C_{p,\,q}
the Chebyshev curve
F(X,Y)=0
.

Theorem

(Theorem
2.8
)
.

The resolution
Y_{p,\,q}
of
C_{p,\,q}
by blowing-up consists of a non-singular curve
D_{p,\,q}
and
(p-1)(q-1)/2
exceptional lines in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}
.

The character variety
X(G_{p,\,q})
of a torus knot group
G_{p,\,q}
is birational to
Y_{p,\,q}
.
More precisely, the component
X^{red}(G_{p,\,q})
of reducible characters is birational to
D_{p,\,q}
and the others
X^{irr}(G_{p,\,q})
of irreducible characters are isomorphic to
Y_{p,\,q}\setminus D_{p,\,q}
.

The
Y_{p,\,q}\setminus D_{p,\,q}
above is the union of
L_{a,\,b}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{\hbox{two points}\}
(
0<a<p
,
0<b<q
,
a\equiv b\pmod{2}
). Each component
L_{a,b}
lies on the critical point
(2\cos(\pi a/p),\,2\cos(\pi b/q))
of
F(X,\,Y)
over the Chebyshev curve

. The adjoint Reidemeister torsion gives the following locally constant function on
Y_{p,\,q}\setminus D_{p,\,q}
.

Theorem

(Theorem
3.1
)
.

The adjoint Reidemeister torsion
\mathop{\mathbb{T}_{\mu}}\nolimits(\rho)
for the
(p,\,q)
-torus knot exterior and a meridian
\mu
satisfies

\mathop{\mathbb{T}_{\mu}}\nolimits(\rho)=\frac{-1}{4pq}\det\left.\left(\frac{\partial(F_{X},\,F_{Y})}{\partial(X,\,Y)}\right)\right|_{(X,\,Y)=(2\cos(a\pi/p),\,2\cos(b\pi/q))}

where
(\partial(F_{X},\,F_{Y})/(X,\,Y))
is the Jacobian determinant of partial derivative
F_{X}
and
F_{Y}
of
F(X,Y)
, which are the defining polynomials of exceptional lines, on
L_{a,\,b}
.
In other words, it holds that

\mathop{\mathbb{T}_{\mu}}\nolimits(\rho)=\frac{-1}{4pq}\left.\mathrm{Hess}(F)\right|_{(X,\,Y)=(2\cos(\pi a/p),\,2\cos(\pi b/q))}

on
L_{a,\,b}
.
Here
\mathrm{Hess}(F)
is the Hessian of the defining polynomial
F(X,Y)
of the Chebyshev curve
C_{p,\,q}
.

This paper is organized as follows:
Sect. 2 reviews the character varieties of torus knot groups and provides a rational model for the character varieties of torus knot groups by a resolution of Chebyshev curves.
We will see the Hessian of the defining polynomial for a Chebyshev curve gives the adjoint Reidemeister torsions for a torus knot exterior in Sect. 3.
Sect. 4 introduces the Verlinde numbers of torus knots. Sect. 5 gives the recursion relation and the initial values of our Verlinde numbers and shows the integrality of the power sum of the adjoint Reidemeister torsions. We also touch a relation between the generalized Verlinde numbers and classical ones in Sect. 6.

2.
Character varieties for torus knots

We start with review on some known results on the character varieties of torus knot groups and then we will provide a rational model of our character varieties by resolutions of algebraic plane curves, called
Chebyshev curves
, in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

2.1.
Review on the character variety for a torus knot group

Let
p
and
q
be coprime integers. Let
T_{p,\,q}
be the torus knot of type
(p,\,q)
.
For simplicity, we assume that
p
and
q
are positive and
q
is always odd.
We write
E_{p,\,q}
for the torus knot exterior
S^{3}\setminus N(T_{p,\,q})
in the
3
-sphere.
Here
N(T_{p,\,q})
denotes an open tubular neighbourhood of the knot
T_{p,\,q}
.

It is known that the knot group
G_{p,\,q}=\pi_{1}(E_{p,\,q})
has the following presentation:

(1)

G_{p,\,q}=\langle\alpha,\beta\,|\,\alpha^{p}=\beta^{q}\rangle

A meridian
\mu
and a preferred longitude
\lambda
of
T_{p,\,q}
are expressed as

(2)

\mu=\alpha^{-r}\beta^{s}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda=\alpha^{p}\mu^{-pq}(=\beta^{q}\mu^{-pq})

for integers
r
and
s
such that
ps-qr=1
in the presentation (
1
).

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representations
\rho
of
G_{p,\,q}
mean homomorphisms from
G_{p,\,q}
into
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. The
character
of an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\rho
is defined as a function
\chi_{\rho}:G_{p,\,q}\to\mathbb{C}
given by
\chi_{\rho}(g)=\mathrm{tr}\,\rho(g)
.
The set of characters has a structure of affine algebraic variety, which is called
the character variety
of the torus knot group
G_{p,\,q}
and denoted by
X(G_{p,\,q})
.

An
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\rho
is referred to as being
irreducible
if there is no invariant line in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
under the action of
\rho(G_{p,\,q})
and
\rho
is said to be
reducible
otherwise.
We write
X^{irr}(G_{p,\,q})
and
X^{red}(G_{p,\,q})
for the set of characters of irreducible and reducible
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representations of
G_{p,\,q}
respectively.

It is shown in
[
CS
]
that irreducible
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representations
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are conjugate if and only if
\chi_{\rho}=\chi_{\rho^{\prime}}
.
We can regard
X^{irr}(G_{p,\,q})
as the set of conjugacy classes
[\rho]
of irreducible
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representations
\rho
of
G_{p,\,q}
.
The set
X^{irr}(G_{p,\,q})
is the disjoint union of algebraic varieties which are isomorphic to the complex lines
\mathbb{C}
without two points.
We call
X^{irr}(G_{p,\,q})
the irreducible part of the character variety
X(G_{p,\,q})
. According to
[
Jo
]
, the irreducible part
X^{irr}(G_{p,\,q})
consists of
(p-1)(q-1)/2
components as follows.

•

the traces
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\rho(x)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\rho(y)
have the constant values

2\cos(\pi a/p)
and
2\cos(\pi b/q)
such that
a\equiv b\pmod{2}
and
0<a<p
,
0<b<q
on each component in
X(G_{p,\,q})
.

•

the trace
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\rho(\mu)
on every component gives an isomorphism to
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{2\cos(ar/p\pm bs/q)\pi\}
(i.e., except for two points).

We write
\mathbb{C}_{a,b}
for each component of
X^{irr}(G_{p,\,q})
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\rho(x)=2\cos(\pi a/p)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\rho(y)=2\cos(\pi b/q)
for all conjugacy classes
[\rho]
on the component.

Example 2.1
.

We examine the images of
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\mu
in
G_{p,\,q}
under an irreducible
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\rho
.
We can assume that
\rho(\alpha)
,
\rho(\beta)
are given by

\rho(\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}x&1\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho(\beta)=\begin{pmatrix}y&0\\
u&y^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

up to conjugation.
The relation
\alpha^{p}=\beta^{q}
gives the equality that

\begin{pmatrix}x^{p}&(x^{p}-x^{-p})/(x-x^{-1})\\
0&x^{-p}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}y^{q}&0\\
u(y^{q}-y^{-q})/(y-y^{-1})&y^{-q}\end{pmatrix}.

Thus we have
x=e^{\pm a\pi\sqrt{-1}/p}
and
y=e^{\pm b\pi\sqrt{-1}/q}
where
0<a<p
,
0<b<q
and
a\equiv b\pmod{2}
.
If we set
x=e^{a\pi\sqrt{-1}/p}
and
y=e^{b\pi\sqrt{-1}/q}
, then the trace of
\rho(\mu)=\rho(\alpha^{-r}\beta^{s})
is given by

\displaystyle x^{-r}y^{s}+x^{r}y^{-s}-u\frac{x^{r}-x^{-r}}{x-x^{-1}}\frac{y^{s}-y^{-s}}{y-y^{-s}}

\displaystyle=2\cos(-ar/p+bs/q)\pi-u\frac{\sin(ar\pi/p)}{\sin(a\pi/p)}\frac{\sin(bs\pi/q)}{\sin(b\pi/q)}.

We need to exclude
u=0
and
u=4\sin(a\pi/p)\sin(b\pi/q)
for
\rho
to be irreducible.
In these cases,
\mathrm{tr}\,\rho(\mu)
turns out to be
2\cos(ar/p\mp bs/q)\pi
.

We also set the function
\mathrm{tr}_{\gamma}
for any
\gamma\in G_{p,\,q}
on
X(G_{p,\,q})
as

\mathrm{tr}_{\gamma}(\chi_{\rho})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\rho(\gamma)\,(=\chi_{\rho}(\gamma))

and call it the
trace function
of
\gamma
.

On the reducible part
X^{red}(G_{p,\,q})
, each character
\chi_{\rho}
factors through the abelianization
\mathrm{ab}:G_{p,\,q}\to G_{p,\,q}/[G_{p,\,q},G_{p,\,q}]\simeq H_{1}(E_{p,\,q};\mathbb{Z})=\langle[\mu]\rangle
. We write
\varphi_{\rho}:\langle\mu\rangle\to\mathbb{C}
for the induced function from the character of a reducible
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\rho
such that
\chi_{\rho}=\varphi_{\rho}\circ\mathrm{ab}
. Since
\varphi_{\rho}
is determined by the image of
\mu
, the character
\chi_{\rho}
is also determined by the image of
\mu
. Therefore the trace function of
\mu
also gives an isomorphism between the reducible part
X^{red}(G_{p,\,q})
and
\mathbb{C}
. We will see explicit forms of trace functions on the reducible part in the following subsections and give a birational model of algebraic curve.

2.2.
Chebyshev curves

We denote by
C_{k}(z)
and
S_{k}(z)

(k\in\mathbb{Z})
the Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kinds such that

\displaystyle C_{k+1}(z)=zC_{k}(z)-C_{k-1}(z),\quad C_{1}(z)=z,\,C_{0}(z)=2,

\displaystyle S_{k+1}(z)=zS_{k}(z)-S_{k-1}(z),\quad S_{1}(z)=z,\,S_{0}(z)=1.

Remark 2.2
.

The Chebyshev polynomials
T_{k}(z)
and
U_{k}(z)
of the first and second kinds are usually defined by

\displaystyle T_{k+1}(z)=2zT_{k}(z)-T_{k-1}(z),\quad T_{1}(z)=z,\,T_{0}(z)=1,

\displaystyle U_{k+1}(z)=2zU_{k}(z)-U_{k-1}(z),\quad U_{1}(z)=2z,\,U_{0}(z)=1.

Our Chebyshev polynomials w satisfies that

C_{k}(z)=2T_{k}(z/2)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{k}(z)=U_{k}(z/2).

Moreover if
z=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}
, then
C_{k}(z)
and
S_{k}(z)
are expressed as

C_{k}(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})=\zeta^{k}+\zeta^{-k}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{k}(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})=\frac{\zeta^{k+1}-\zeta^{-(k+1)}}{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}.

Lemma 2.3
.

If the homology class of
\gamma\in G_{p,\,q}
is expressed as
[\gamma]=[\mu^{k}]
, then the trace function
\mathrm{tr}_{\gamma}
satisfies
C_{k}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{\mu}
on the reducible part
X^{red}(G_{p,\,q})
.

In particular, the trace functions
\mathrm{tr}_{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{tr}_{\beta}
of the generators
\alpha
and
\beta
are expressed as
\mathrm{tr}_{\alpha}=C_{q}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{\mu}
and
\mathrm{tr}_{\beta}=C_{p}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{\mu}
.

Proof.

When we set
\chi_{\rho}(\mu)=m+m^{-1}
, the value
\chi_{\rho}(\mu^{k})
is given by
C_{k}(m+m^{-1})
. The character
\chi_{\rho}
of a reducible
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation factors through
H_{1}(E_{p,\,q};\mathbb{Z})=\langle[\mu]\rangle
since we can assume the image of
\rho
is contained in upper triangular matrices in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. The character
\chi_{\rho}
sends
\gamma
to
C_{k}(m+m^{-1})
for any
\gamma\in G_{p,\,q}
in the homology class
[\mu^{k}]
.

It is enough to show that the homology classes
[\alpha]
and
[\beta]
are given by
[\mu^{q}]
and
[\mu^{p}]
for
\mathrm{tr}_{\alpha}=C_{q}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{\mu}
and
\mathrm{tr}_{\beta}=C_{p}\circ\mathrm{tr}_{\mu}
.
The relation
\alpha^{p}=\beta^{q}
implies
[\alpha]=[\mu^{kq}]
and
[\beta]=[\mu^{kp}]
. By
\mu=\alpha^{-r}\beta^{s}
for
ps-qr=1
we have
k=1
.
∎

We review a notion of
Chebyshev curves
, which are plane algebraic curves defined by two Chebyshev polynomials.

Definition 2.4

(Chebyshev curve of type
(p,\,q)
)
.

For integers
p
and
q
, we define the Chebyshev curve
C_{p,\,q}
of type
(p,\,q)
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
as

C_{p,\,q}=\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\,|\,C_{p}(X)-C_{q}(Y)=0\}

Figure 1.
Chebyshev curves of type
(2,3)
and
(3,4)

We can find fundamental properties of Chebyshev curves in
[
Fis
, Sec. 3.9]
.

Remark 2.5
.

If
p
and
q
are coprime, the Chebyshev curve
C_{p,\,q}
is irreducible.

For coprime
p
and
q
, the Chebyshev curve has
(p-1)(q-1)/2
nodes (double points) as singularities. These singular points are given by

\left\{\left.\left(2\cos\frac{a\pi}{p},2\cos\frac{b\pi}{q}\right)\,\right|\,0<a<p,\quad 0<b<q,\quad a\equiv b\pmod{2}\right\}

from the system of
F(X,Y)=0
,
F_{X}(X,Y)=0
and
F_{Y}(X,Y)=0
where
F(X,Y)=C_{p}(X)-C_{q}(Y)
.
For the details, we refer the reader to
[
Fis
, Sec. 3.9]
.
We denote by
\mathrm{Sing}(C_{p,\,q})
the set of singular points above.

The solutions of the system
F_{X}(X,Y)=0
and
F_{Y}(X,Y)=0
as above give
(p-1)(q-1)
points
\{(2\cos a\pi/p,2\cos b\pi/q)\,|\,0<a<p,0<b<q\}
.
We blow up
\mathbb{C}^{2}
on these
(p-1)(q-1)
points and give a resolution of singularity for the Chebyshev curve
C_{p,\,q}
.

We write
B
for the resulting surface by the blowing-up of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
on
(p-1)(q-1)
points
\{(2\cos a\pi/p,2\cos b\pi/q)\,|\,0<a<p,\,0<b<q\}
and
D_{p,\,q}
for the resolution of the Chebyshev curve
C_{p,\,q}
. Summarizing,
we can express the resulting surface
B
and the non-singular curve
D_{p,\,q}
as

B=\{(X,Y,[Z_{0}:Z_{1}])\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\,|\,F_{X}(X,Y)Z_{0}=-F_{Y}(X,Y)Z_{1}\}

where
F(X,Y)=C_{p}(X)-C_{q}(Y)
and

D_{p,\,q}=\overline{\pi^{-1}(C_{p,\,q}\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(C_{p,\,q}))}

which is the closure of
\pi^{-1}(C_{p,\,q}\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(C_{p,\,q}))
by the projection
\pi:B\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
We can realize the closure of
\pi^{-1}(C_{p,\,q}\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(C_{p,\,q}))
by a parametrization of the Chebyshev curve as follows.

Proposition 2.6
.

The algebraic curve
D_{p,\,q}
is parametrized as

D_{p,\,q}=\{(C_{q}(t),C_{p}(t),[C^{\prime}_{q}(t):C^{\prime}_{p}(t)])\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\,|\,t\in\mathbb{C}\}.

In particular,
D_{p,\,q}
intersects with the exceptional line on
(2\cos(a\pi/p),2\cos(b\pi/q))
at
[q\sin(a\pi/p):\pm p\sin(b\pi/q)]\in\mathbb{P}
.

Proof.

The Chebyshev polynomials
C_{k}(z)
satisfies
C_{k}(C_{l}(z))=C_{kl}(z)
.
The defining equation
F(X,Y)=C_{p}(X)-C_{q}(Y)=0
of the Chebyshev curve
C_{p,\,q}
has the solution
X=C_{q}(t)
and
Y=C_{p}(t)
. Moreover we have
[Z_{0}:Z_{1}]=[C^{\prime}_{q}(t):C^{\prime}_{p}(t)]
by

F_{X}(C_{q}(t),C_{p}(t))C^{\prime}_{q}(t)+F_{Y}(C_{q}(t),C_{p}(t))C^{\prime}_{p}(t)=0

on
C_{p,\,q}
.

By next Lemma
(C_{q}(t),C_{p}(t))=(2\cos(a\pi/p),2\cos(b\pi/q))
implies
t=2\cos(ar/p\pm bs/q)\pi
where
a\equiv b\pmod{2}
and
ps-qr=1
. It follows from
C^{\prime}_{k}(z)=kS_{k-1}(z)
that

\displaystyle[C^{\prime}_{q}(t):C^{\prime}_{p}(t)]

\displaystyle=\left[q\frac{\sin(aqr\pi/p\pm bs\pi)}{\sin(ar\pi/p\pm bs\pi/q)}:p\frac{\sin(ar\pi\pm bps\pi/q)}{\sin(ar\pi/p\pm bs\pi/q)}\right]

\displaystyle=[q\sin(a\pi/p):\mp p\sin(b\pi/q)]

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

The system of
C_{q}(t)=2\cos(a\pi/p)
and
C_{p}(t)=2\cos(b\pi/q)
for
a\equiv b\pmod{2}
has the solution
t=2\cos(ar/p\pm bs/q)\pi
with
ps-qr=1
.

Proof.

If we set
t=m+m^{-1}
, then we have
m=e^{\pm a\pi\sqrt{-1}/(pq)+2\pi k\sqrt{-1}/q}
with some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
by

C_{q}(t)=e^{a\pi\sqrt{-1}/p}+e^{-a\pi\sqrt{-1}/p}.

Similarly we have
m=e^{\pm b\pi\sqrt{-1}/(pq)+2\pi\ell\sqrt{-1}/p}
with some
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
by
c_{p}(t)=2\cos(b\pi/q)
.
By
a/(pq)+2k/q=\pm b/(pq)+2\ell/p
it holds that

kp-\ell q=(-a\pm b)/2
.
We can set
k=s(-a\pm b)/2
and
\ell=r(-a\pm b)/2
by
ps-qr=1
,
which implies
t=2\cos(ar/p\pm bs/q)\pi
.
Note that we have the same

if we replace

and
r
with
s+dq
and
r+dp
.
∎

2.3.
Birational model of character varieties for torus knots

We provide a birational model of the character variety
X(G_{p,\,q})
through blowing-up the Chebyshev curve
C_{p,\,q}
at
(p-1)(q-1)/2
points.
We use the same symbol
\pi
for the restriction of
\pi:B(\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
to
B\cap\mathbb{C}^{2}\times(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{\infty=[1:0]\})
.
We set
Y_{p,\,q}=\pi^{-1}(C_{p,\,q})
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times(\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{\infty=[1:0]\})
. Our variety
Y_{p,\,q}
is the union of the non-singular curve
D_{p,\,q}
and
(p-1)(q-1)/2
exceptional lines. We have seen in Proposition
2.6
that each exceptional line intersects with
D_{p,\,q}
at two points. We will show that the decomposition

The character variety

Proof.

We show that both of

Set

in Proposition

Set

3.
Adjoint Reidemeister torsion for torus knot exteriors

We review the adjoint Reidemeister torsion for torus knot exteriors.

We think of the adjoint Reidemeister torsion as a function on the set of conjugacy classes of irreducible

Theorem 3.1

The adjoint Reidemeister torsion

Lemma 5.2

For

(7)

, the Verlinde number
d(0,\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{a,\,b})
is expressed as

\displaystyle d(0,\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{a,\,b})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<i<p\\
0<j<q\end{subarray}}\frac{2\sin\frac{\pi i}{p}2\sin\frac{\pi j}{q}}{pq}\sin\frac{\pi ia}{p}\sin\frac{\pi ja}{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2}{pq}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i}-\zeta^{-i})(\zeta^{ia}-\zeta^{-ia})\sum_{0<j<q}(\eta^{j}-\eta^{-j})(\eta^{jb}-\eta^{-jb})

\displaystyle=\frac{-2}{pq}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i(a+1)}+\zeta^{-i(a+1)}-(\zeta^{i(a-1)}+\zeta^{-i(a-1)}))

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{-2}{pq}}\quad\cdot\sum_{0<j<q}(\eta^{j(b+1)}+\eta^{-j(a+1)}-(\eta^{j(b-1)}+\eta^{-j(b-1)}))

where
\zeta=e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/p}
and
\eta=e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/q}
.
If
a\not=1
(resp.
b\not=1

\zeta
(resp.
\eta
) vanishes by Eq. (
7
)
since the exponents of
\zeta
(resp.

(
ii
)
We can express

\displaystyle d(0,\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{a,\,b}+\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{c,\,d})

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{ps}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<i<p\\
0<j<q\end{subarray}}\sin\frac{\pi ia}{p}\sin\frac{\pi ic}{p}\sin\frac{\pi jb}{q}\sin\frac{\pi jd}{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{ps}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{ia}-\zeta^{-ia})(\zeta^{ic}-\zeta^{-ic})\sum_{0<j<q}(\eta^{jb}-\zeta^{-jb})(\zeta^{jd}-\zeta^{-jd})

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{ps}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i(a+c)}+\zeta^{-i(a+c)}-(\zeta^{i(a-c)}+\zeta^{-i(a-c)}))

\displaystyle\phantom{\frac{4}{pq}}\quad\cdot\sum_{0<j<q}(\eta^{j(b+d)}+\eta^{-j(b+d)}-(\eta^{j(b-d)}+\zeta^{-j(b-d)}))

where
\zeta=e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/p}
and
\eta=e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/q}
.
Then Eq. (
7
) shows that this summation equals
1
if
(a,b)=(c,d)
or
0
otherwise.

(
iii
)
Set
\zeta=e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/p}
and
\eta=e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/q}
.
We can also express
d(0,\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{a,\,b}+\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{c,\,d}+\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{e,\,f})
as

\displaystyle d(0,\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{a,\,b}+\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{c,\,d}+\mbox{\boldmath{$\ell$}}_{e,\,f})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{-1}{2p}\sum_{0<i<p}\frac{(\zeta^{ia}-\zeta^{-ia})(\zeta^{ic}-\zeta^{-ic})(\zeta^{ie}-\zeta^{-ie})}{(\zeta^{i}-\zeta^{-i})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\cdot\frac{-1}{2q}\sum_{0<j<q}\frac{(\eta^{jb}-\eta^{-jb})(\eta^{jd}-\eta^{-jd})(\eta^{jf}-\eta^{-jf})}{(\eta^{j}-\eta^{-j})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{-1}{2p}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i(a+c)}+\zeta^{-i(a+c)}-\zeta^{i(a-c)}-\zeta^{-i(a-c)})(\zeta^{i(e-1)}+\zeta^{i(e-3)}+\cdots+\zeta^{-i(e-3)}+\zeta^{-i(e-1)})

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{-1}{2q}\sum_{0<j<q}(\eta^{j(b+d)}+\eta^{-j(b+d)}-\eta^{j(b-d)}-\eta^{-j(b-d)})(\eta^{j(f-1)}+\eta^{j(f-3)}+\dots+\eta^{-j(f-3)}+\eta^{-j(f-1)})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2p}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{e}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i(a-c)}+\zeta^{-i(a-c)})\zeta^{i(e-(2k-1))}-\sum_{k=1}^{e}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i(a+c)}+\zeta^{-i(a+c)})\zeta^{i(e-(2k-1))}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\frac{1}{2q}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{f}\sum_{0<j<q}(\eta^{j(b-d)}+\eta^{-j(b-d)})\eta^{j(f-(2k-1))}-\sum_{k=1}^{f}\sum_{0<j<q}(\eta^{j(b+d)}+\eta^{-j(b+d)})\eta^{j(f-(2k-1))}\right).

We focus on the sums of
\zeta
.
The each sum of
\zeta
vanishes if
a-c+e\equiv a+c+e\equiv 0\pmod{2}
by Eq. (
7
) respectively. In the case of

,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{e}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i(a-c+e-(2k-1))}+\zeta^{-i(a-c+e-(2k-1))})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-2e+2p&\text{if $\exists k$ such that $a-c+e-(2k-1)=0$ }\\
&\Leftrightarrow|a-c|<e;\\
-2e&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{e}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i(a+c+e-(2k-1))}+\zeta^{-i(a+c+e-(2k-1))})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-2e+2p&\text{if $\exists k$ such that $a+c+e-(2k-1)=0$ or $2p$}\\
&\text{$\Leftrightarrow a+c<e$ or $a+c+e>2p$};\\
-2e&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Note that the case of
|a-c|\geq e
and
a+c<e

Summarizing, we can see

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2p}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{e}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i(a-c)}+\zeta^{-i(a-c)})\zeta^{i(e-(2k-1))}-\sum_{k=1}^{e}\sum_{0<i<p}(\zeta^{i(a+c)}+\zeta^{-i(a+c)})\zeta^{i(e-(2k-1))}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $2\mathrm{max}\{a,c,e\}<a+c+e<2p$, $a+c+e$ odd};\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Similar arguments apply to the sum of
\eta
which proves the initial value (
iii

∎
