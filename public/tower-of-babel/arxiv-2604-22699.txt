Entrywise Low-Rank Approximation and Matrix p → q p\rightarrow q Norms via Global Correlation Rounding
Prashanti Anderson, Ainesh Bakshi, Samuel B. Hopkins

Abstract
Given a matrix A A , the goal of the entrywise low-rank approximation problem is to find arg ​ min ⁡ ‖ A − B ‖ p \argmin\|A-B\|_{p} over all rank- k k matrices B B , where ∥ ⋅ ∥ p \|\cdot\|_{p} is the entrywise ℓ p \ell_{p} norm. When p = 2 p=2 this well-studied problem is solved by the singular value decomposition, but for p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 the problem becomes computationally challenging. For every even p > 2 p>2 and every fixed k k , we give the first polynomial-time approximation scheme for this problem, improving on the ( 3 + ε ) (3+\varepsilon) approximation of Ban, Bhattiprolu, Bringmann, Kolev, Lee, and Woodruff, the bi-criteria approximation of Woodruff and Yasuda, and the additive approximation scheme of Anderson, Bakshi, and Hopkins. Prior algorithmic approaches based on sketching and column selection, which yielded a polynomial-time approximation scheme in the p < 2 p<2 setting, face concrete barriers when p > 2 p>2 . Instead, we use the Sherali-Adams hierarchy of convex programs, and in so doing establish a blueprint for how to use convex hierarchies to design polynomial-time approximation schemes for continuous optimization problems. We use the same algorithmic strategy to give a new family of additive approximation algorithms for matrix p → q p\rightarrow q norms, which are intimately related to small-set expansion and quantum information. In particular, we give the first nontrivial additive approximation algorithms in the regime p < 2 < q p<2<q .

We design new approximation algorithms for two fundamental families of high-dimensional continuous optimization problems:

Our algorithms are obtained via a new analysis of the Sherali-Adams hierarchy of convex relaxations for both problems.

Matrix

Norms.

For

\|A\|_{p\rightarrow q}=\max_{x\in\mathbbm{R}^{d}}\frac{\|Ax\|_{q}}{\|x\|_{p}}\,,

where

The

For most choices of

[
HO10
,

,
BBHKSZ12

,

And yet, the problem of computing

Examples include the

[

,

,
LS08
,

,

]
, the

norm used in high-dimensional statistics
[

]
and the

[
BBHKSZ12
,
BH15
,
DHPT25
]

We focus on the “hypercontractive”
p<q

Even for the special case of the
2\rightarrow q
norms, they are NP-hard to approximate within inverse polynomial multiplicative error, and any constant factor approximation would refute both the exponential time hypothesis and the small set expansion hypothesis
[
BBHKSZ12
]
.
But algorithms with other forms of approximation guarantees – in particular,
additive

[
BH15
]
and low-rank approximation
[

]

have easy-to-compute

Our second main contribution is a new additive approximation algorithm for the
p\rightarrow q
norm.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q\geqslant 2
be an even integer with Hölder dual
q^{*}=\tfrac{q}{q-1}
, and let
p\in[q^{*},2]
have Hölder dual
p^{*}=\tfrac{p}{p-1}
which is an even integer.
For every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
(nd)^{O(\varepsilon^{-4q})}
time algorithm which takes a matrix
A\in\mathbbm{R}^{n\times d}
and with probability
0.99
returns
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{d}
such that

\frac{\|Ax\|_{q}^{q}}{\|x\|_{p}^{q}}\leqslant\|A\|_{p\rightarrow q}^{q}\leqslant\frac{\|Ax\|_{q}^{q}}{\|x\|_{p}^{q}}+\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}

where
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
are the rows of
A
.

An illustrative example is the
4/3\rightarrow 4
norm, chosen because in this case both
p^{*},q>2
, a setting in which prior additive approximations suffer.
Theorem
1.2
says that there is a polynomial time algorithm to approximate
\|A\|_{4/3\rightarrow 4}^{4}
to additive error
\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{ij}A_{ij}^{4}
.
Note that by Hölder’s inequality,
\sum_{ij}A_{ij}^{4}\geqslant\|A\|_{4/3\rightarrow 4}^{4}
.
The only prior additive approximation we are aware of for the
p<2
case, by
[
BH15
]
, instead yields incomparable additive error (see Section
8.1
).

More generally, to interpret the right-hand side of Theorem
1.2
, note that
\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}
is a natural upper bound on
\|A\|_{p\rightarrow q}^{q}
via Hölder’s inequality.
The theorem says that there is an efficient algorithm which improves on this upper bound by any constant factor; alternatively, the
p\rightarrow q
norm is easy to compute up to
(1+\varepsilon)
multiplicative error when
\|A\|_{p\rightarrow q}
is within a constant factor of this upper bound.

All prior polynomial-time additive approximation algorithms for
p\rightarrow q
norm either apply only to the case
p=2
or incur error which can be larger than
\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}
by a polynomial factor in
n
or
d

[
BH15
]
.
Thus, we view Theorem
1.2
as an important step to chart the algorithmic landscape in the thus-far underexplored
p<2

We give more detailed comparison to prior algorithms with additive approximation guarantees in
Section

8.1

A New Perspective on Pinning Lemmas: Taming Pairwise Correlations via Mirror Descent

Our third main contribution is a new perspective on
pinning lemmas
, which we use to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.1
.
Pinning lemmas are
measure decomposition
theorems in high-dimensional probability which show that multivariate probability distributions can be decomposed as mixtures of a small number of simple distributions.
Here “simple” means having small pairwise correlations.
Each of the simple distributions in the mixture is just the original distribution conditioned on the values of a few coordinates, or “pinned”.

Pinning lemmas arose separately in statistical physics
[
Mon08
]
and rounding algorithms for semidefinite programs
[
BRS11
,
RT12
]
, in particular the “global correlation rounding” paradigm, and have since seen extensive use in both areas
[
YZ14
,
JKR18
,
CLN22
,
JST23
,
CKPZ18
,
CP19
,
CP19a
]
, as well as high-dimensional probability
[
CH21
]
, random matrix theory, and error-correcting coding
[
CEGHR20
]
.
More broadly, pinning lemmas are an important example of the power of
localization schemes

[
CE22
]
, which have seen an explosion of applications in high-dimensional probability and sampling
[
EG18
,
EG18a
,
Eld20
,
CE22
]
.
We introduce a new connection between pinning lemmas and the
mirror descent
framework from online convex optimization; this connection also unifies pre-existing pinning lemmas under the umbrella of a single proof technique.
Because of the broad applicability and importance of pinning lemmas, we expect that our pinning lemmas, as well as the new bridge between pinning lemmas and mirror descent, will have further applications in the future.

There are two known pinning lemmas
[
Mon08
,
BRS11
,
RT12
]
, one of which applies to discrete random variables and the other to real-valued random variables with bounded variance.
Because
p\rightarrow q
norm and entrywise LRA are optimization problems over the reals, the version which allows real-valued random variables is our starting point.
(We will expand on the relationship between pinning lemmas and rounding convex relaxations in Section
2
.)

Theorem 1.3

(Pinning Lemma, Bounded Variance Version
[
Mon08
,
RT12
,
BRS11
]
)
.

For all
t\in\mathbbm{N}
there exists
s\leqslant t
such that if
S\subseteq[n]
is a random set of size
s
and
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
are any jointly distributed random variables,

\E_{S}\E_{i,j\sim[n]}|\Cov(X_{i},X_{j}\,|\,X_{S})|\leqslant O\left\lparen\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right\rparen\cdot\E_{i\sim[n]}\Var(X_{i})\,,

where
\Cov(X_{i},X_{j}\,|\,X_{S})
is the covariance between
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
after conditioning on the values
X_{k}
for
k\in S
, and
\Var(\cdot)
denotes variance.

Theorem
1.3
breaks down in the face of heavy-tailed
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
with large or infinite variance.
Many probabilistic phenomena undergo a qualitative change when passing from finite-variance to infinite-variance settings – e.g. central limit theorems no longer apply to the average of independent random variables with infinite variance; thus
a priori
it could be that finite variance is needed for the “conditioning tames pairwise correlations” phenomenon expressed by Theorem
1.3
.
We show the opposite is true by proving a pinning lemma which applies to heavy-tailed random vectors.

Theorem 1.4

(Pinning Lemma, Heavy-Tailed Version)
.

For every
p\in\mathbbm{N}
and
t\in\mathbbm{N}
there is
s\leqslant t
such that if
S\subseteq[n]
is a random set of size
s
and
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
are any jointly distributed random variables,

\E_{S}\E_{i,j\sim[n]}|\Cov(X_{i},X_{j}\,|\,X_{S})|\leqslant\frac{1}{t^{\Omega(1)}}\cdot\left[\E_{X}\left\lparen\E_{i\sim[n]}|X_{i}|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right\rparen^{2}\right]^{\frac{p}{p+1}}

Theorem
1.4
can be much stronger than Theorem
1.3
when the average pairwise product
\E|X_{i}|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}|X_{j}|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}
is much smaller than the average
\Var(X_{i})^{\frac{p+1}{p}}

We will later prove a version of Theorem

which applies even when each
X_{i}
could have infinite variance, roughly speaking by removing the “

We also prove a version which allows for a weighted average of the pairs

Interestingly, the right-hand side of the heavy-tailed bound depends on pairwise moments, while the bounded-variance version depends on an average of the one-wise moments
\E_{i\sim[n]}\Var(X_{i})

A simple example (see

High-dimensional heavy-tailed random vectors are ubiquitous, arising everywhere from quantum gravity

AI Disclosure, Centaur Math, and a Short History of this Paper.

Centaur math
refers to math accomplished by a hybrid of human and artificial intelligence.
Like chess before them, we believe that math and theoretical computer science are entering their centaur phase.
In addition to our algorithmic results, which remain our main contribution, we present this paper as an artifact of interest in this transformation of our field.

We wrote the initial version of this paper in late 2025 and January/February of 2026 with minimal AI assistance.
At the time, we were able to obtain a PTAS for entrywise rank-one approximation, as well as our additive approximation for
p\rightarrow q
norm; we felt this to be already more than sufficiently novel to merit publication.
Then two things happened in March 2026:

1.

In the course of testing generative AI tools for math, one of the authors prompted GPT 5.4 Pro to formulate and prove the heavy-tailed pinning lemma, given only a high-level description of the lemma statement.
With only this prompt, GPT 5.4 Pro provided a correct formulation and proof; even more remarkably, it provided a much simpler proof than the authors had, one that didn’t rely on any connection to mirror descent but instead a simple reduction to the bounded-variance case.
We present this proof in Section
A.4
.

2.

Over several rounds of interaction and given access to the LaTeX files for our complete writeup of the rank-one algorithm, GPT 5.4 Pro formulated and proved the key matrix-norm inequalities we needed to generalize our result to rank

, yielding Theorem

.

We have chosen to preserve the “pre-AI” versions of both the heavy-tailed pinning lemma and our low-rank approximation result, for two reasons.
First, we feel that our unification of pinning lemmas via mirror descent is independently interesting, even though the heavy-tailed pinning lemma can be proved without it.
Second, the main ideas for the rank-

2.1
Sum-of-Squares Relaxation and Global Correlation Rounding for
p\rightarrow q
Norms

As a warm-up, we start with our additive approximation algorithm for
p\rightarrow q
norms.
For concreteness, we focus on
4/3\rightarrow 4
.
Our algorithms for more general
\|A\|_{p\rightarrow q}
follow the exact same template.
The first step is to describe the convex relaxation and rounding scheme we use, both of which are fairly standard.

Setting Up the Convex Relaxation

We can phrase
\|A\|_{4/3\rightarrow 4}
as the following bilinear optimization problem over the
\ell_{4/3}
unit ball:

\|A\|_{4/3\rightarrow 4}=\max_{\|x\|_{4/3}=1,\|y\|_{4/3}=1}x^{\top}Ay

It is not too hard to show that there exists a (not too large) discrete subset
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathbb{R}
such that there are
\ell_{4/3}
unit vectors
x,y
which satisfy
x^{\top}Ay\geqslant\|A\|_{4/3\rightarrow 4}-\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{4}^{4}
and have all their entries in
\mathcal{S}
.
Thus, we can restrict attention to
x,y
with entries in
\mathcal{S}
.
This lets us formulate
\max x^{\top}Ay
as a discrete assignment problem over
\mathcal{S}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}^{d}
.

The first step of our algorithm is to solve the degree
O(\varepsilon^{-4})
Sum-of-Squares (SoS) semidefinite programming relaxation of this discrete assignment problem.
3
3

3

Our algorithms would also work using a variant of the Sherali-Adams linear programming relaxation; we do not need the full power of SoS. But since the SoS notation has become standard, we will follow the SoS conventions.

A solution to the degree-
\ell
SoS relaxation is a
pseudodistribution
.
For our purposes, a pseudodistribution is a collection of
local distributions

\{\mu_{T}\}_{T\subseteq[n+d],|T|=\ell}
on
\mathcal{S}^{\ell}
.
These local distributions are
consistent
in that
\mu_{T}
and
\mu_{T^{\prime}}
must induce the same marginal distribution on
T\cap T^{\prime}
.
Such a collection of local distributions induces a
pseudoexpectation
, which is a linear operator

\pE\,:\,(\text{linear combinations of functions depending on $\leqslant\ell$ variables})\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}\,,

by defining, for each function
f(x,y)
depending on at most
\ell
variables
A\subseteq[n]\cup[d]
,

\pE f(x,y)=\E_{(x_{A},y_{A})\sim\mu_{A}}f(x_{A},y_{A})\,,

and extending
\pE
linearly.
We impose additional (convex) constraints on the local distributions to ensure that
\pE\|x\|_{4/3}^{4/3}=\pE\|y\|_{4/3}^{4/3}=1
.
We can write the objective value of a solution to the relaxation as
\pE\sum_{i\leqslant n,j\leqslant d}A_{ij}x_{i}y_{j}
, which is a linear function of
\{\mu_{T}\}
, and hence we can maximize it over solutions.
Our next goal is to round
\{\mu_{T}\}
to a pair of vectors
\hat{x},\hat{y}
without losing too much in objective value
4
4

4

It suffices to only consider the objective value; in fact, we can slightly modify the rounding procedure to ensure we produce vectors with norm at most
1
.
.

Global Correlation Rounding and Pinning Lemmas.

Two key algorithmic operations comprise the global correlation rounding scheme.
First is
conditioning:
choosing an index
i\in[n]
or
j\in[d]
, sampling
x_{i}
or
y_{j}
from its marginal distribution, and replacing all the local distributions with their conditioned versions, which yields a new degree
\ell-1
pseudodistribution.
Second is independent sampling: we can always draw each
x_{i}
and
y_{j}
from their one-wise marginals, though this will not preserve any correlations across coordinates
5
5

5

Note that independently sampling (in expectation) produces the same objective value as outputting the expectation of each coordinate on bilinear objectives. We utilize expectation rounding in our final algorithm to ensure that we produce vectors with bounded norm.
.

Algorithm 2.1

(Global Correlation Rounding
[
BRS11
,
RT12
]
for
4/3\rightarrow 4
Norm)
.

Input:

Pseudo-distribution
\pE
of degree at least
\ell
over variables
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}\cup\{y_{j}\}_{j\in[d]}
.

Operations:

1.

For every subset
\mathcal{T}\subseteq[n]\cup[d]
,
|\mathcal{T}|\leqslant\ell-2
of variables:

(a)

Sample the values of
(x_{\mathcal{T}},y_{\mathcal{T}})
from their joint marginal local distribution and condition on their values.

(b)

Sample
\hat{x}_{i},\hat{y}_{j}
from the one-wise conditional marginals.

2.

Output the
\hat{x},\hat{y}
achieving the best objective value.

By adding appropriate constraints to the convex relaxation, we can ensure that conditioning preserves objective value.
So the main thing we need to show is that at least one of the conditioned pseudodistributions satisfies the following “small rounding loss” inequality.

\left\lvert\text{SoS value}-\text{expected rounded value}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[d]}A_{ij}\cdot(\pE x_{i}y_{j}-\pE x_{i}\pE y_{j})\right\rvert\leqslant\varepsilon\cdot\left\lparen\sum_{i}\|A_{i}\|_{4}^{4}\right\rparen^{1/4}\,.

Now we can see the use of a pinning lemma:
\sum_{ij}A_{ij}(\pE x_{i}y_{j}-\pE x_{i}\pE y_{j})\leqslant\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|\cdot|\Cov(x_{i},y_{j})|
is exactly a weighted sum of pairwise covariances.
(We abuse notation and use
\Cov(x_{i},y_{j})
to denote the covariance of the variables
x_{i},y_{j}
in their
2
-wise local distribution.)
The underlying (pseudo)random variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}
might not have variances we can control – our relaxation promises us nothing about
\Var(x_{i}),\Var(y_{j})
.
6
6

6

Strictly speaking, these variances are bounded in terms of the largest-magnitude element of our discretization
\mathcal{S}
. But using this bound would cost us a
\poly(n,d)
factor, which we cannot afford.

But we have a tight handle on the
4/3
moments, because
\pE\|x\|_{4/3}^{4/3}\|y\|_{4/3}^{4/3}=\sum_{ij}\pE|x_{i}|^{4/3}|y_{j}|^{4/3}=1
via the constraints on our relaxation.
Thus, we are in the setting of a heavy-tailed pinning lemma!
Our weighted, bipartite

\{x_{i}\},\{y_{j}\}
version of Theorem

, which also applies to pseudodistributions, shows that the small rounding loss inequality above holds for at least one choice of pinning subset
\mathcal{T}

2.2
PTAS for Entrywise Low-Rank Approximation

We turn to Theorem
1.1
.
We focus on
p=4
for simplicity; the ideas easily extend to any even
p
.

Additive Approximation, Rank-One Case.

We start by describing how to get an additive approximation algorithm to
\ell_{4}
LRA with additive error
\varepsilon\cdot\|A\|_{4}^{4}
. Even for this guarantee, the best prior algorithm
[
ABH25
]
runs only in quasipolynomial time.
As with
p\rightarrow q
norm, we solve a degree
\poly(1/\varepsilon)
SoS relaxation obtained by discretizing the allowed set of values assumed by
u
and
v
.
We show that there exists an optimal solution
u,v
obeying
\|u\|_{4}^{4},\|v\|_{4}^{4}\leqslant O(\|A\|_{4}^{2})
, and hence our convex program can enforce that
\pE\|u\|_{4}^{4},\pE\|v\|_{4}^{4}\leqslant O(\|A\|_{4}^{2})
.

We once again deploy global correlation rounding.
To see the “small rounding loss” inequality we need in this setting, it helps to expand the objective function as a polynomial:

\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[d]}(A_{ij}-u_{i}v_{j})^{4}=A_{ij}^{4}-4A_{ij}^{3}u_{i}v_{j}+6A_{ij}^{2}u_{i}^{2}v_{j}^{2}-4A_{ij}u_{i}^{3}v_{j}^{3}+u_{i}^{4}v_{j}^{4}\,.

We can break the loss in objective value incurred by independent rounding on a conditioned pseudodistribution
\pE
into four terms:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{ij}4A_{ij}^{3}(\pE u_{i}v_{j}-\pE u_{i}\pE v_{j})\right\rvert\,,\quad\left\lvert\sum_{ij}6A_{ij}^{2}(\pE u_{i}^{2}v_{j}^{2}-\pE u_{i}^{2}\pE v_{j}^{2})\right\rvert\,,\quad

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{ij}4A_{ij}(\pE u_{i}^{3}v_{j}^{3}-\pE u_{i}^{3}\pE v_{j}^{3})\right\rvert\,,\quad\left\lvert\sum_{ij}\pE u_{i}^{4}v_{j}^{4}-\pE u_{i}^{4}\pE v_{j}^{4}\right\rvert\,.

We can show that one of the conditioned pseudodistributions discovered by global correlation rounding after conditioning on
\poly(1/\varepsilon)
coordinates makes the first three of these terms less than
\varepsilon\cdot\|A\|_{4}^{4}
using pinning lemmas.
(Again, variants of Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.3
which allow for weighted averages of covariances, and two sets of variables
u
and
v
.)
Crucially, the term involving
\pE u_{i}^{3}v_{j}^{3}-\pE u_{i}^{3}\pE v_{j}^{3}=\Cov(u_{i}^{3},v_{j}^{3})
demands a heavy-tailed pinning lemma. We don’t control the variances
\Var(u_{i}^{3}),\Var(v_{j}^{3})
, so a variance-style pinning lemma won’t work, but we do control the
4/3
moments, since
\E|u_{i}^{3}|^{4/3}=\E u_{i}^{4}
.

For the last term, involving
\Cov(u_{i}^{4},v_{j}^{4})
, we are able to include constraints in our relaxation which ensure that no rounding loss is incurred.
This term factors as
\pE\|u\|_{4}^{4}\|v\|_{4}^{4}-\pE\|u\|_{4}^{4}\pE\|v\|_{4}^{4}
, and we can ensure that this difference is zero, following
[
ABH25
]
. Anderson, Bakshi and Hopkins
[
ABH25
]
follow the same template as above to obtain their additive approximation, but, lacking a heavy-tailed pinning lemma, they employ a pinning lemma suited to discrete random variables in an
ad hoc
manner; it is unclear how to improve their approach from quasipolynomial to polynomial time except via the heavy-tailed pinning lemma.

From Additive to Multiplicative Approximation, Rank-One Case

We now describe the main ideas required to extend the additive approximation described above to a PTAS.
The key idea is to “warm start” our additive approximation algorithm with a constant-factor optimal rank-one approximation.
Using e.g.
[
BBBKLW19
]
, in polynomial time we can find
x,y
such that
\|A-xy^{\top}\|_{4}^{4}\leqslant 3.1\cdot\min_{u,v}\|A-uv^{\top}\|_{4}^{4}
.
8
8

8

Here
3.1
stands in for any constant greater than
3
, since
[
BBBKLW19
]
gives a
3+\varepsilon
-approximation algorithm.

Then we search for a rank-one approximation to
A
only in a small
\ell_{4}
ball around
(x,y)
.
To enable this, we prove a structure lemma, which shows that some optimal rank-one approximation to
A
must exist in a small ball around
(x,y)
.

Lemma 2.2

(Nearby Optimal Solutions for Rank-One Approximation)
.

Let
p
be an even integer,
x,u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
y,v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then

\min_{c}\left(\lVert x+cu\rVert_{p}^{p}\lVert y-v/c\rVert_{p}^{p}+\lVert x-cu\rVert_{p}^{p}\lVert y+v/c\rVert_{p}^{p}\right)\leqslant O(1)\cdot\lVert uv^{\top}-xy^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}\,.

To interpret Lemma
2.2
, note that if
(x,y)
has objective value
O(\textsf{OPT})
and
(u,v)
is optimal, then
\|xy^{\top}-uv^{\top}\|_{p}^{p}\leqslant O(\textsf{OPT})
by triangle inequality.
Furthermore, the solution
(cu,v/c)
has the same objective value as
(u,v)
.
So at a high level, Lemma
2.2
tells us there exists an optimal solution
(cu,v/c)
that is close to
(x,y)
in
\ell_{p}
norm.

The quantitative bound on the distance between
(cu,v/c)
and
(x,y)
implied by Lemma
2.2
is stronger than we might expect in a not-obvious way.
A first (but incorrect) interpretation of Lemma
2.2
might be that all of
\|x+cu\|_{p}^{p},\|y-v/c\|_{p}^{p},\|x-cu\|_{p}^{p},\|y+v/c\|_{p}^{p}
are small, perhaps of order
\sqrt{\textsf{OPT}}
.
But this is actually impossible (assuming the optimal error is sufficiently small) –
\|x+cu\|_{p}^{p}
and
\|x-cu\|_{p}^{p}
cannot simultaneously be very small, and the same goes for
\|y-v/c\|_{p}^{p},\|y+v/c\|_{p}^{p}
.
WLOG, imagine that
\|x+cu\|
and
\|y+v/c\|
are the “large” ones, much larger than
\sqrt{\textsf{OPT}}
.
Then
\|y-v/c\|_{p}^{p}
and
\|x-cu\|_{p}^{p}
must be much less than
\sqrt{\textsf{OPT}}
.
This improvement over the naive interpretation of Lemma
2.2
turns out to be crucial to our PTAS.

With Lemma
2.2
in hand, we can add constraints to our convex program ensuring that the norms of the residual vectors,
\pE\|u-x\|_{4}^{4}
and
\pE\|v-y\|_{4}^{4}
are much smaller than
\pE\|u\|_{4}^{4},\pE\|v\|_{4}^{4}
, scaling (roughly speaking) with
OPT
instead of with
\|A\|_{4}^{4}
.
Then, at a high level, we repeat the additive approximation algorithm above, except that when we analyze the objective value loss due to rounding, we expand
\|A-uv^{\top}\|_{4}^{4}
as a polynomial in the residual variables
u_{i}-x_{i},v_{j}-y_{j}
.
We then apply pinning lemmas to these residual variables rather than
u_{i},v_{j}
.

The Rank-
k
Case.

Most of the ideas from the rank-one case extend immediately to rank

We can still set up a convex program whose solutions are
\ell

(\mathbbm{R}^{k})^{\ell}
rather than
\mathbbm{R}^{\ell}
, and condition on the values of individual coordinates

As we alluded to earlier, the two key ingredients (supplied by GPT 5.4 Pro) are the higher-rank version of Lemma

and a

Lemma 2.3

(Nearby Optimal Solutions for Rank-
k
Approximation)
.

Let
X,U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
and
Y,V\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
be full column rank matrices. Then there exists an invertible matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
such that

\lVert(U+XM)(V-Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}+\lVert(U-XM)(V+YM^{-1})^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}\leqslant k^{O_{p}(1)}\lVert UV^{\top}-XY^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}\,.

Furthermore, if
X
,
U
,
\left(X^{\top}X\right)^{-1/2}
, and
\left(U^{\top}U\right)^{-1/2}
have entries bounded in magnitude by
\beta
, then
M
and
M^{-1}
have entries bounded by
\poly(\beta\cdot nmk)
.

9

9

The second part of this lemma, regarding the bounds on the entries of

, was proved by human analysis of the GPT-provided proof of the first part of the lemma on the existence of

.

Note that, unlike in
Lemma

2.2

2.3
only bounds the norm of the product matrix of the residuals (rather than the product of the norms). Thus, it is not immediately clear whether

2.3
is enough to analyze the rounding error on the rank

10

10

We will also need in the analysis that there exist

Lemma 2.4

(Residual Balancing (Informal))
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
be a matrix and let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k},Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
be matrices such that
\lVert A-XY^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}\leqslant\textsf{OPT}
. Let
B
be a rank
k
matrix such that
\lVert A-B\rVert_{p}^{p}\leqslant\textsf{OPT}
. Then there exists a factorization
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
,
V\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
such that
B=UV^{\top}
and invertible matrices
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
such that

\displaystyle\lVert(X+U)N_{1}^{-1}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant O_{p,k}(1)\cdot\sqrt{OPT}

\displaystyle\lVert(Y-V)N_{1}^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant O_{p,k}(1)\cdot\sqrt{OPT}

\displaystyle\lVert(X-U)N_{2}^{-1}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant O_{p,k}(1)\cdot\sqrt{OPT}

\displaystyle\lVert(Y+V)N_{2}^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant O_{p,k}(1)\cdot\sqrt{OPT}\,.

We prove
Lemma

2.4
by combining

2.3
with

, a tool from functional analysis.
Lemma

2.4
allows us to bound the norms of individual residuals, albeit at the cost of introducing matrices

. It turns out that this is sufficient, and we can decompose the objective in terms of

,

,

2.3
Pinning and Mirror Descent

Now we can turn to the heavy-tailed pinning lemma and our new bridge between pinning lemmas and mirror descent.
We start by reviewing the proof of the variance pinning lemma (Theorem
1.3
), and re-interpreting that proof as an instance of (online) gradient descent.
Then by replacing gradient descent with its generalization, online mirror descent, we will be able to prove Theorem
1.4
.
As discussed earlier, after we developed the connection between pinning lemmas and mirror descent, GPT 5.4 Pro found a proof of Theorem
1.4
via a relatively simple reduction to Theorem
1.3
.
Nonetheless, we present the more sophisticated proof because it presents a clear picture of the landscape of pinning lemmas.

The Variance Pinning Lemma.

Theorem
1.3
is proved via a nonnegative potential function:

\Phi(s)=\E_{S\subseteq[n],|S|=s}\E_{i\sim[n]}\Var(X_{i}\,|\,X_{S})=\frac{1}{n}\E_{S\subseteq[n],|S|=s}\E\|X-\E[X\,|\,X_{S}]\|^{2}\,.

\Phi(s)
provides a measure of the “uncertainty about
X
” remaining after conditioning on
s
random coordinates of
X
.
Since
\Phi(0)=\E_{i}\Var(X_{i})
, to prove Theorem
1.3
it is enough to lower bound the decrement in potential function
\Phi(s)-\Phi(s+1)
in terms of
\E_{S}\E_{ij}|\Cov(X_{i},X_{j}\,|\,X_{S})|
; this decrement cannot be large for too many steps and thus eventually
\E_{S}\E_{ij}|\Cov(X_{i},X_{j}\,|\,X_{S})
must be small.
The heavy lifting is done by the following simple fact; we give a particularly suggestive proof below.

Fact 2.5

(Conditioning reduces Variance)
.

Given scalar random variables
u,v
drawn from the joint distribution
\mathcal{D}
, the decrease in variance of
u
when conditioning on
v
can be lower bounded as follows:

\Var(u)-\E_{v}\Var(u|v)\geqslant\Omega(1)\cdot\frac{\Cov^{2}(u,v)}{\Var(v)}\,.

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that
\Cov(u,v)>0
.
Note that since the conditional expectation of
u
given
v
minimizes mean squared error among all functions which attempt to predict the value of
u
given knowledge of
v
, we have that

\Var(u)-\E_{v}\Var(u|v)\geqslant\E\left(u-\E u\right)^{2}-\min_{f\,:\,\mathbbm{R}\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}}\E_{v}\E_{u}\left(u-f(v)\right)^{2}\,,

To prove the fact, we consider a particular predictor
f(v)
, which we can view as a gradient descent update to the predictor
\E u
, interpreting
v
as a “gradient”.
In particular, let
f(v)=\E u+\eta(v-\E v)
for some step size
\eta>0
we will choose later.
Expanding, we have that

\displaystyle\Var(u)-\E_{v}\Var(u|v)

\displaystyle\geqslant\E\left(u-\E u\right)^{2}-\E_{v}\E_{u}\left(u-\E u-\eta(v-\E v)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\E\left(u-\E u\right)^{2}-\left[\E\left(u-\E u\right)^{2}-2\E_{v}\E_{u}\eta\left(v-\E v\right)\left(u-\E u\right)+\E_{v}\eta^{2}(v-\E v)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geqslant 2\eta\Cov(u,v)-\eta^{2}\Var(v)\,.

Setting
\eta=\frac{1}{100}\cdot\frac{\Cov(u,v)}{\Var(v)}
completes the proof.
∎

Given Fact
2.5
, a simple Cauchy-Schwarz argument to lower bound
\Phi(s)-\Phi(s+1)
finishes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Since all the arguments above look only at
t+2
coordinates of
X
at a time, they work unchanged for pseudodistributions of degree at least
t+2
.
So now we can move on to the heavy-tailed pinning lemma.
The main idea to obtain the heavy-tailed pinning lemma can be summed up as:

The above argument proves that conditioning reduces variance via the witness of taking a gradient step with “gradient”
X_{j}
. Gradient descent is a special case of mirror descent with quadratic regularization
\|x\|^{2}
. Replace this with the regularization function
\|x\|_{1+1/p}^{1+1/p}
.

We refer the reader to
[
Bub15
]
for background material on mirror descent.
This pattern fits a long line of work in theoretical computer science using mirror descent with non-Euclidean regularizers as a proof technique – e.g., in spectral sparsification
[
ALO15
]
and discrepancy theory
[
DJR21
]
.
We now carry it out in more detail.

Generalizing the Potential Function via Bregman Divergence.

For this overview, we will focus on the case of Theorem
1.4
where we get a bound on the pairwise correlations which depends on the
4/3
moments of
X
.
We start by introducing a new potential function, to replace
\Phi(s)
.
Following the potential functions used to analyze mirror descent in the context of online convex optimization, we use Bregman divergences:

Definition 2.6

(Bregman Divergence)
.

For a differentiable function
f
, the Bregman divergence associated with
f
is

B_{f}(x,y)=f(x)-f(y)-\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle\,.

We now define the “Bregman uncertainty” of a random variable, which will be our measure of its uncertainty, generalizing variance.

Definition 2.7

(Bregman Uncertainty)
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}^{>0}
and let
B_{p}
be the Bregman divergence associated with
f(x)=\frac{p-1}{p}|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
. Then the
p
-Bregman uncertainty of a random variable
u
is

\min_{c}\E_{u}B_{p}(u,c)\,.

In fact, the minimum is always obtained at
c=\E u
, so an equivalent formulation is

\E_{u}B_{p}(u,\E u)=\frac{p-1}{p}\left\lparen\E|x|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}-|\E x|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right\rparen\,,

which also conveniently shows that
\E_{u}B_{p}(u,\E u)\geqslant 0
.

When
p=2
, this is precisely the variance.
Directly generalizing from the variance case, our potential function will be

\Phi_{p}(s)=\E_{S\subseteq[n],|S|=s}\E_{i\sim[n]}\E B_{p}(X_{i},\E[X_{i}\,|\,X_{S}])\geqslant 0\,.

It turns out that
\E[X_{i}\,|\,X_{S}]
minimizes
\E B_{p}(X_{i},f(X_{S}))
among all functions depending only on
X_{S}
.
We can think of it as the “best prediction” of
X_{i}
given only the random variable
X_{S}
.

Conditioning Reduces Bregman Uncertainty.

Focusing on the
4/3
case again, the heart of the argument is the following analogue of Fact
2.5
, which will be enough to show a lower bound on
\Phi_{4}(s)-\Phi_{4}(s+1)
in terms of
\E_{S\subseteq[n],|S|=s}\E_{ij}|\Cov(X_{i},X_{j}\,|\,X_{S})|
.

Lemma 2.8
.

For all jointly distributed random variables
u,v
,

\E_{u,v}B_{4}(u,\E u)-B_{4}(u,\E[u|v])\geqslant\Omega\left(\frac{\Cov^{6}(u,v)}{\left(\E|v|^{4/3}\right)\left(\E_{u,v}[|u|^{4/3}|v|^{4/3}]+\E_{u}[|u|^{4/3}]\E_{v}[|v|^{4/3}]\right)^{7/2}}\right)\,.

Lemma
2.8
captures a reduction in the Bregman uncertainty associated with
u
when conditioning on
v
for which
|\Cov(u,v)|
is significant and the
4/3
moments of
u
and
v
are not too big, exactly what we need.
To prove Lemma
2.8
, roughly speaking, we construct an explicit function
f(v)
which is designed to “predict”
u
given only
v
, and prove a lower bound on
B_{4}(u,\E u)-B_{4}(u,f(v))
.
The latter then implies a lower bound on
B_{4}(u,\E u)-B_{4}(u,\E[u|v])
because the divergence is minimized at
B_{4}(u,\E[u|v])
.
At a high level, the idea is to take
f(v)
to be a mirror-descent step starting at
\E u
with gradient
v
.
The formula for such
f
is inscrutable, but can be derived from the mirror descent update rules:

f(v)=\operatorname{sign}\!\Big(\operatorname{sign}(\E u)\,|\E u|^{1/3}-\eta\,v\Big)\,\Big|\operatorname{sign}(\E u)\,|\E u|^{1/3}-\eta\,v\Big|^{3}\,,

where
\eta
is an appropriately-chosen step size.
In fact, even this
f
will not quite work – for technical reasons, we need to instead use a mirror descent step starting at
v^{2/3}\E u
with gradient
v^{1/3}
; an explanation why is beyond the scope of this overview.

A major challenge in the proof of Lemma
2.8
is that
x\mapsto|x|^{4/3}
is not strongly convex, so we cannot use the standard analysis of mirror descent for strongly convex regularizers.
Instead we give a “bare hands” proof.
To prove Theorem
1.4
(the
4/3
case) from Lemma
2.8
, we can use Hölder’s inequality, generalizing the argument which proves Theorem
1.3
from Fact
2.5
.

Pseudo-Distributions.

A discrete probability distribution over
\mathbbm{R}^{m}
is defined by its probability mass function,
D\colon\mathbbm{R}^{m}\to\mathbbm{R}
, which must satisfy
\sum_{x\in\mathrm{supp}(D)}D(x)=1
and
D\geqslant 0
.
We extend this definition by relaxing the non-negativity constraint to merely requiring that
D
passes certain low-degree non-negativity tests.
We call the resulting object a pseudo-distribution.

Definition 3.1

(Pseudo-distribution)
.

A
degree-
\ell
pseudo-distribution
is a finitely-supported function
D:\mathbbm{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}
such that
\sum_{x}D(x)=1
and
\sum_{x}D(x)p(x)^{2}\geqslant 0
for every polynomial
p
of degree at most
\ell/2
, where the summation is over all
x
in the support of
D
.

Next, we define the related notion of pseudo-expectation.

Definition 3.2

(Pseudo-expectation)
.

The
pseudo-expectation
of a function
f
on
\mathbbm{R}^{m}
with respect to a pseudo-distribution
\mu
, denoted by
\widetilde{\E}_{#1}\,\left[f(x)\right]
, is defined as

\widetilde{\E}_{#1}\,\left[f(x)\right]=\sum_{x}\mu(x)f(x).

We use the notation
\widetilde{\E}_{#1}\,\left[(1,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})^{\otimes\ell}\right]
to denote the degree-
\ell
moment tensor of the pseudo-distribution
\mu
.
In particular, each entry in the moment tensor corresponds to the pseudo-expectation of a monomial of degree at most
\ell
in
x
.

Definition 3.3

(Constrained pseudo-distributions)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\Set{p_{1}\geqslant 0,p_{2}\geqslant 0,\dots,p_{r}\geqslant 0}
be a system of
r
polynomial inequality constraints of degree at most
d
in
m
variables.
Let
\mu
be a degree-
\ell
pseudo-distribution over
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
We say that
\mu

satisfies

\mathcal{A}
at degree
\ell\geqslant 1
if for every subset
\mathcal{S}\subset[r]
and every sum-of-squares polynomial
q
such that
\deg(q)+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\max\left\lparen\deg(p_{i}),d\right\rparen\leqslant\ell
,
\widetilde{\E}_{#1}\,\left[q\prod_{i\in\mathcal{S}}p_{i}\right]\geqslant 0
.
Further, we say that
\mu

approximately satisfies
the system of constraints
\mathcal{A}
if the above inequalities are satisfied up to additive error
\widetilde{\E}_{#1}\,\left[q\prod_{i\in\mathcal{S}}p_{i}\right]\geqslant-2^{-n^{\ell}}\lVert q\rVert\prod_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\lVert p_{i}\rVert
, where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
denotes the Euclidean norm of the coefficients of the polynomial, represented in the monomial basis.

Crucially, there’s an efficient separation oracle for moment tensors of constrained pseudo-distributions.
Below gives the unconstrained statement; the constraint statement follows analogously.

Fact 3.4

(

For any
m,\ell\in\mathbbm{N}

[
GLS81
]
:

S\subset\mathbbm{R}^{M}
is an algorithm that can decide whether a vector
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{M}
is in the set, and if not, provide a hyperplane between
v
and
S
.
Roughly, a weak separation oracle is a separation oracle that allows for some
\eta

3.2
Potential Aligned Functions

In this subsection, we restate some definitions and basic facts about potential functions used in the analysis of global correlation rounding, taken from Section 4 in
[
ABH25
]
.

Definition 3.7

(Pseudo-distribution potentials)
.

A function
\Phi
from pseudo-distributions over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}
is a pseudo-distribution potential if

1.

For all pseudo-distributions
\mu
we have that
\Phi(\mu)\geqslant 0
.

2.

For a fixed
i\in[n]
, let
\mu^{\prime}
be the pseudo-distribution given by sampling a random value
\hat{x}_{i}
from the marginal of
x_{i}
and conditioning on the event
\Set{x_{i}=\hat{x}_{i}}
. Then,

\E_{x_{i}}\left[\Phi(\mu)-\Phi(\mu^{\prime})\right]\geqslant 0\,.

The typical potential functions used in global correlation rounding by
[
RT12
,
BRS11
]
are
\E_{i\sim[n]}H(\{x_{i}\})
and
\E_{i\sim[n]}\Var(x_{i})
.
Both are pseudo-distribution potentials in the sense of Definition
3.7
.

Definition 3.8

(Potential aligned functions)
.

A function
f:(\mathbb{R}^{k})^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
(\delta,\delta^{\prime})
-potential aligned with a pseudo-distribution potential
\Phi
, initial pseudo-distribution
\mu_{0}
, and family of polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
which have
\pE_{\mu}p_{i}=0
if for all distributions over pseudo-distributions
\mu
such that

1.

\E_{\{\mu\}}\mu=\mu_{0}
,

2.

all
\mu^{\prime}
in the support of
\{\mu\}
satisfy
\pE_{\mu^{\prime}}p_{i}=0
for all
i\leqslant m
, and

3.

\left|\E_{\{\mu\}}\pE_{\mu}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})-\pE_{\mu^{\otimes}}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\right|\geqslant\delta\,,

we have that there exists an index
i\in[n]
such that

\E_{\{\mu\}}\E_{\hat{x_{i}}\sim\{x_{i}\}}\left[\Phi(\mu)-\Phi(\mu^{\prime})\right]\geqslant\delta^{\prime}\,,

where
\mu^{\prime}
is the pseudo-distribution given by sampling a random value
\hat{x}_{i}
from the marginal of
x_{i}
and conditioning on
x_{i}=\hat{x}_{i}
.

In this language, prior works like
[
BRS11
,
RT12
]
can be interpreted as showing that any function
f
of the form
\E_{i,j\sim[n]}f_{ij}(x_{i},x_{j})
where
f_{ij}

[-1,1]
-valued is potential-aligned with respect to
\Phi=\E_{i}H(\{x_{i}\})
.
And, functions of the form
f=\E_{i,j\sim[n]}A_{ij}\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle
are potential-aligned with respect to
\Phi=\E_{i\sim[n]}\Var(x_{i})
; this follows almost directly from prior work on global correlation rounding (see
[
BRS11
]
).

We can also combine multiple potential functions to analyze correlation rounding on a sum of objective functions.

Lemma 3.9

(Handling several potential aligned functions (see Lemma 4.4 in
[
ABH25
]
))
.

Let
\varepsilon\geqslant 0
. Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in[\ell]}
be a collection of functions from
(\mathbb{R}^{k})^{n}
such that

is
(\varepsilon/\ell,\delta_{j})
-potential aligned for some pseudo-distribution potential
\Phi_{j}
and some initial pseudo-distribution
\mu
. Then there exists a set

of size at most

such that after conditioning on the values of
x_{i}\in\mathcal{T}
,

\left|\E_{\{\mu_{\mathcal{T}}\}}\pE_{\mu_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{j\in[\ell]}f_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})-\pE_{\mu^{\otimes}_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{j\in[\ell]}f_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\,.

4
Designing Potential Functions for Global Correlation Rounding

In this section, we will quantify the decrease in the uncertainty of a random variable
u
when conditioning on some other random variable
v
. In prior work, uncertainty has often been measured by either entropy or variance of the associated random variable. We give extensions of these potential decrease inequalities based on a general framework inspired by mirror descent.

In all of our applications, our potential functions will be defined using Bregman divergences.

Definition 4.1

(Bregman Divergence)
.

For a differentiable function
f
, we have that the Bregman divergence associated with
f
is

B_{f}(x,y)=f(x)-f(y)-\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle\,.

At a high level, our proof of both of our new potential decrease lemmas will revolve around using the decrease in Bregman divergence given by mirror descent to bound the potential decrease when there are large correlations among variables.

4.1
Low-moment potential decay based on covariance

One common approach to understanding how uncertainty decreases when conditioning uses the fact that if
u
and
v
have large covariance and
v
has small variance, then the variance of
u
significantly drops when conditioning on
v
. We will generalize this approach to work in settings where the variance of the associated random variable might be very large, but lower moments are still bounded. Thus, it is necessary to understand how the
q
moments (or related functions of them) for
q\in(1,2)
behave when conditioning on other random variables. We give a bound that characterizes this and generalizes the following fact, which has been used to analyze GCR rounding schemes in prior work:

\displaystyle\Var(u)-\E_{v}\Var(u|v)\geqslant\frac{\Cov^{2}(u,v)}{\Var(v)}\,.

(1)

Instead of working with variance as our measure of uncertainty, we will instead work with a more general measure of uncertainty. These will be defined using Bregman divergences associated with polynomial functions
f
. Note that for polynomial functions of degree strictly less than
q
, the associated Bregman divergences only depend on smaller moments of the distribution than the
q
th-moment.

Definition 4.2

(Bregman Uncertainty)
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}^{>0}
and let
B_{p}
be the Bregman divergence associated with the function
f(x)=\frac{p-1}{p}|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
. Then the
p
-Bregman uncertainty
\phi^{(p)}_{u}
is

\phi^{(p)}_{u}=\min_{c}\E_{u}B_{p}(u,c)\,.

Note that the following fact gives us an explicit formula for
\phi^{p}_{u}
:

Fact 4.3
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}^{>0}
and any random variable
x
, the minimizer over all
constants

c
of the function
c\mapsto\E_{x}B_{p}(x,c)
is
c=\E x
, and the minimum value is
\frac{p-1}{p}\left[\E|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}-\left|\E x\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]
.

This also implies the following easy bound on the potential:

Fact 4.4
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}^{>0}
and any random variable
x
we have that

\phi^{(p)}_{x}\leqslant O\left(\E|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\,.

Finally, we will need the following equality in the analysis of potential decreases:

Fact 4.5

(Bregman Divergence Three Point Equality)
.

For any Bregman Divergence
B_{h}
we have that

B_{h}(x,y)+B_{h}(y,z)=B_{h}(x,z)+\langle\nabla h(z)-\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle\,.

4.2
Bregman uncertainty generalizes variance

Note that when
p=2
the associated Bregman divergence is simply
B_{2}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}(x-y)^{2}
and since the minimizing constant
c
is the expectation we have that
\phi^{(2)}_{u}=\frac{1}{2}\Var(u)
. Furthermore, the guarantee of
Eq.

1
(up to constant factors) can be recovered using mirror descent progress lemmas as follows:

Theorem 4.6

(Conditioning reduces Variance)
.

Given scalar random variables
u,v
drawn from the joint distribution
\mathcal{D}
, the decrease in variance of
u
when conditioning on
v
can be lower bounded as follows:

\Var(u)-\E_{v}\Var(u|v)\geqslant\frac{\Cov^{2}(u,v)}{4\cdot\Var(v)}\,.

In the proof of
Theorem

4.6
, we will need the following standard mirror descent progress lemma:

Lemma 4.7

(Mirror Descent Progress Lemma)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable function that is
1
-strongly convex with respect to a norm
\|\cdot\|
and let
B
be the Bregman divergence induced by
f
. Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that

x^{\prime}=\arg\min_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\eta\langle y,z\rangle+B(z,x)\right\},

for some cost vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and step size
\eta>0
. Then for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the following inequality holds:

\eta\langle y,u-x\rangle\leqslant B(u,x)-B(u,x^{\prime})+\eta^{2}\|y\|_{*}^{2}\,,

where
\|\cdot\|_{*}
denotes the dual norm of
\|\cdot\|
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.6
.

Using
Definition

4.2
, we note that for any
c
which is just a function of
v
and the joint distribution of
u,v
we have that

\Var(u)-\E_{v}\Var(u|v)=2\left(\phi^{(2)}_{u}-\E_{v}\phi^{(2)}_{u|v}\right)\geqslant\E_{u}B_{2}(u,\E u)-\E_{v}\E_{u|v}B_{2}(u,c)\,,

using the fact that the divergence is minimized at
c=\E_{u}
. Thus, it suffices to exhibit a
c
which causes a sufficiently large decrease in the Bregman divergence. We can compute such a
c
by performing a mirror descent update step on
x=\E u
using the cost vector
y=v-\E v
. Note that in our setting
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}
, which is
1
-strongly convex with respect to the
\ell_{2}
norm and is self-dual. Thus, we can apply
Lemma

4.7
with
x=\E u
and
y=v-\E v
to get that

\eta\langle v-\E v,u-\E u\rangle\leqslant B_{2}(u,\E u)-B_{2}(u,x^{\prime})+\eta^{2}\lVert v-\E v\rVert_{2}^{2}\,.

When we take an expectation over both
u,v
on both sides, we get that this inequality gives us that

\displaystyle\eta\Cov(u,v)=\E_{u,v}\eta\langle v-\E v,u-\E u\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\E_{u}B_{2}(u,\E u)-\E_{v}\E_{u|v}B_{2}(u,x^{\prime})+\eta^{2}\E_{v}\lVert v-\E v\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\E_{u}B_{2}(u,\E u)-\E_{v}\E_{u|v}B_{2}(u,x^{\prime})+\eta^{2}\Var(v)\,.

Now setting
\eta=\frac{\Cov(u,v)}{2\Var(v)}
and rearranging, we have

\begin{split}\left\lparen\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right\rparen\frac{\Cov^{2}(u,v)}{\Var(v)}&\leqslant\E_{u}B_{2}(u,\E u)-\E_{v}\E_{u|v}B_{2}(u,x^{\prime})\\
&\leqslant\Var(u)-\E_{v}\Var(u|v)\end{split}

which yields the desired variance reduction inequality.
∎

Thus, Bregman uncertainty generalizes variance as a metric of uncertainty. Furthermore, the associated mirror descent progress bounds and analysis can be used to understand how Bregman uncertainty decreases when conditioning on other random variables.

4.3
Fractional Bregman uncertainty decrease

We now extend the proof of the case where
p
is an even integer larger than
2
. Specifically, we prove the following bound on the decrease in Bregman uncertainty:

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
p
be an even integer and let
B_{p}
be the Bregman divergence associated with
\frac{p-1}{p}|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
. Let
y,z
be jointly distributed random variables over
\mathbb{R}
. Then we have that

\E_{y,z}\left[B(y,\E y)-B(y,\E[y|z])\right]\geqslant\Omega\left(\frac{\Cov^{2(p-1)}(y,z)}{\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\left(\E_{y,z}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]+\E_{y}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(2p-1)}{p}}}\right)\,.

Before we proceed to the proof of
Lemma

4.8
, we first prove a few helper lemmas which will be useful to us in the proof.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
c\in(0,1)
and let
X,Y
be non-negative jointly distributed random variables. Then we have that

\E_{X,Y}[XY]\E_{Y}[Y]^{c}\leqslant O\left(\E_{X,Y}[XY^{c+1}]+\E_{X}[X]\E_{Y}[Y^{c+1}]\right)

Proof.

Note that by Hölders with
1+c,(1+c)/c
we have that

\E[XY]\E[Y]^{c}=\E\left[\left(X^{\frac{1}{1+c}}Y\right)\left(X^{\frac{c}{1+c}}\right)\right]\E[Y]^{c}\leqslant\E[XY^{1+c}]^{\frac{1}{1+c}}\E[X]^{\frac{c}{1+c}}\E[Y]^{c}\,.

Using that
\E[XY^{1+c}]^{\frac{1}{1+c}}>0,\E[X]^{\frac{c}{1+c}}\E[Y]^{c}>0
and
3.10
we have that

\E[XY]\E[Y]^{c}\leqslant\E[XY^{1+c}]^{\frac{1}{1+c}}\E[X]^{\frac{c}{1+c}}\E[Y]^{c}\leqslant O\left(\E[XY^{1+c}]+\E[X]\E[Y]^{1+c}\right)\,.

Applying convexity to
\E[Y]^{1+c}
and using that
\E[X]>0
completes the proof.
∎

Note that
Lemma

4.9
also implies the following bound:

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
y,z
be jointly distributed random variables and let
B
be the Bregman divergence associated with
\frac{p-1}{p}|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
. Then we have that

\displaystyle\E_{y,z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^{2}}}(B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant O\left(\left(\E_{y,z}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]+\E_{y}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\left(\E_{y,z}(B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)\,.

Proof.

For every fixed
z
we have

\E_{y|z}B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z])\geqslant 0.

Hence, by Hölders with
\frac{p-1}{p-2},p-1
we have that

\displaystyle\E_{y,z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^{2}}}(B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^{2}}}\E_{y|z}(B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant O\left(\left(\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\cdot(\E_{y|z}B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right]\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\left(\E_{y,z}(B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)\,.

Thus, it remains to bound
\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\cdot(\E_{y|z}B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right]
. We have that

\displaystyle\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\cdot(\E_{y|z}B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right]\leqslant\E_{y,z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}B(y,\E[y])\right]\,,

since
|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
and
\E_{y|z}B(y,\E[y|z])
are both non-negative. Expanding the definition of
B
, we have that

\displaystyle\E_{y,z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}B(y,\E[y])\right]

\displaystyle=\E_{y,z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}-\left|\E[y]\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}-\frac{p}{p-1}\cdot\sgn(\E[y])\cdot\left|\E[y]\right|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(y-\E[y]\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\E_{y,z}[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]+\frac{p}{p-1}\left(\E[|y|]\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\E[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\cdot|y|]\right)+\frac{p}{p-1}\E[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\E[|y|]^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,.

Using
Lemma

4.9
to bound the second term in the sum, we have that

\E_{y,z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}B(y,\E[y])\right]\leqslant O\left(\E_{y,z}[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]+\E[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\E[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]+\E[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\E[|y|]^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\,.

Applying convexity to the last term in the bound completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We will also need a simple bound on the divergence incurred by the mirror descent update.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geqslant 2}
and let
B
be the Bregman divergence associated with
\frac{p-1}{p}|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
. Fix any
x_{1}\in\mathbbm{R}
and
\Delta\in\mathbbm{R}
, and define
x_{2}=\left\lparen\sgn(x_{1})\cdot|x_{1}|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\Delta\right\rparen^{p-1}
.
Then we have that

B(x_{1},x_{2})\leqslant O\left(\Delta^{2}|x_{1}|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}+\Delta^{p}\right)\,.

In particular, setting
\Delta=\eta\cdot\sgn(z)\cdot|z|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
yields

B(x_{1},x_{2})\leqslant O\left(\eta^{2}|z|^{\frac{2}{p-1}}|x_{1}|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}+\eta^{p}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\,.

Proof.

Let
a=\sgn(x_{1})\cdot|x_{1}|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
so that
x_{1}=a^{p-1}
and
x_{2}=(a+\Delta)^{p-1}
.
Expanding the definition of
B
gives

\displaystyle B(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{p}\left(a^{p}-(a+\Delta)^{p}-\frac{p}{p-1}(a+\Delta)\left(a^{p-1}-(a+\Delta)^{p-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{p}\left(-\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\binom{p}{i}a^{i}\Delta^{p-i}+\frac{p}{p-1}\left(a+\Delta\right)\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}\binom{p-1}{i}a^{i}\Delta^{p-1-i}\right)

Note that

-\binom{p}{p-1}a^{p-1}\Delta+\left(\frac{p}{p-1}a\right)\left(\binom{p-1}{p-2}a^{p-2}\Delta\right)

is identically
0
, so we have that the only monomials with non-negative coefficients are of the form
a^{i}\Delta^{p-i}
for
i\in[0,p-2]
and

B(x_{1},x_{2})\leqslant O\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p-2}|a|^{i}\cdot|\Delta|^{p-i}\right)\,.

By
3.10
we have that
|a|^{i}\cdot|\Delta|^{p-i}\leqslant O\left(\Delta^{2}|a|^{p-2}+|\Delta|^{p}\right)
for
i\in[0,p-2]
, and therefore
B(x_{1},x_{2})\leqslant O\left(\Delta^{2}a^{p-2}+\Delta^{p}\right)
.
Substituting
a^{p-2}=|x_{1}|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}
completes the proof.
∎

We now return to the proof of
Lemma

4.8
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.8
.

WLOG let
\Cov(y,z)\geqslant 0
. We consider the following setup: let
x_{0}=\E y
be the predictor for
y
, minimizing
B(y,x)
. We will show that we can construct a good predictor
x(z)
which is a function of only
z
and the joint distribution of
y,z
such that
B(y,x(z))
is sufficiently smaller than
B(y,x_{0})
in expectation. Note that this means that the potential function we are considering decreases since it picks the best such function. We can describe the predictor as follows:

1.

Sample
z
and let
x_{1}
=
|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}x_{0}=|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y
.

2.

Let
x_{2}
be the result of a mirror descent update step on
x_{1}
with cost function
\sgn(z)\cdot|z|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
and some step size
\eta
.

3.

Output
x_{2}/|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}
.

We will analyze this using a mirror descent-inspired analysis on the evolution between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
, which can be thought of as trying to predict
z^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y
.

Note that the update in step 2 has the following explicit form:

\left(\sgn\left(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y\right)\cdot\left||z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y\right|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\eta\sgn(z)\cdot|z|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}

Consider
4.5
applied to
|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y,|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y
, and the mirror-descent update
x_{2}
.
Since
x_{2}
is defined by a mirror-descent update with gradient
\eta\cdot\sgn(z)\cdot|z|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
, we have that

B(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y,|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y)-B(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y,x_{2})=\langle\eta\cdot\sgn(z)\cdot|z|^{\frac{1}{p-1}},|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y-|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y\rangle-B(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y,x_{2})\,.

If we take the expectation over
y,z
on both sides, note that we have that

\E_{y,z}B(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y,|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y)-B(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y,x_{2})\leqslant\E_{y,z}B(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y,|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y)-B(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y,|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E[y|z])\,,

since Bregman divergences are minimized at the expectation by
4.3
. Furthermore, since the Bregman divergence for the
\frac{p}{p-1}
power is a homogeneous function of degree
\frac{p}{p-1}
we can move the terms involving
z
out of the divergence. Using this as well as
Lemma

4.10
we have that

\displaystyle\E_{y,z}\Biggl[

\displaystyle B(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y,|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y)-B(|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y,|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E[y|z])\Biggr]=\E_{y,z}|z|^{\frac{p(p-2)}{(p-1)^{2}}}\left[B(y,\E y)-B(y,\E[y|z])\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant O\left(\left(\E_{y,z}[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]+\E_{y}[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\E_{z}[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\left(\E_{y,z}(B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)\,.

Combining this with our initial expression, we have that

\displaystyle\left(\E_{y,z}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]+\E_{y}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\left(\E_{y,z}(B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

(2)

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\E_{y,z}\langle\eta\sgn(z)\cdot|z|^{\frac{1}{p-1}},|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y-|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y\rangle-\E_{y,z}B(x_{1},x_{2})\,.

(3)

Note that
\E_{y,z}\langle\eta\sgn(z)\cdot|z|^{\frac{1}{p-1}},|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}y-|z|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\E y\rangle
is simply
\eta\Cov(y,z)
so it suffices to bound the last divergence. By
Lemma

4.11
applied with
\Delta=\eta\sgn(z)\cdot|z|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
, we have that

B(x_{1},x_{2})\leqslant O\left(\eta^{2}|z|^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\cdot|x_{1}|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}+\eta^{p}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)=O\left\lparen\eta^{2}|z|^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{(p-1)^{2}}}(\E[|y|])^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}+\eta^{p}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right\rparen\,.

When we take the expectation over
z
on both sides, by Hölders and convexity we have that

\E_{z}B(x_{1},x_{2})\leqslant O\left(\eta^{2}\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p(p-1)}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}+\eta^{p}\left(\E\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)\right)\,.

Now we choose
\eta
so that
\eta\Cov(y,z)
dominates
\E_{v}B(x_{1},x_{2})
.
It suffices to ensure

\eta\ll\frac{\Cov(y,z)}{\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p(p-1)}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}}

and

\eta\ll\left\lparen\frac{\Cov(y,z)}{\left\lparen\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right\rparen}\right\rparen^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\,,

so for a small enough constant
c
we choose

\eta=c\min\left\lparen\frac{\Cov(y,z)}{\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p(p-1)}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}},\left\lparen\frac{\Cov(y,z)}{\left\lparen\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right\rparen}\right\rparen^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right\rparen\,.

Putting this together with (
2
), we get

\E_{y,z}(B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))\geqslant\Omega(1)\cdot\frac{\min\left\lparen\frac{\Cov(y,z)^{2(p-1)}}{\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{p}}},\frac{\Cov^{p}(y,z)}{\left\lparen\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right\rparen}\right\rparen}{\left(\E_{y,z}[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]+\E_{y}[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\E_{z}[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\right)^{p-2}}

We now simplify the minimum term.
Using

\min\{A,B\}\geqslant\frac{AB}{A+B}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{A}+\frac{1}{B}}

with

A=\frac{\Cov(y,z)^{2(p-1)}}{\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{p}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B=\frac{\Cov^{p}(y,z)}{\left\lparen\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right\rparen}\,,

we get

\displaystyle\min\{A,B\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{\frac{\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{p}}}{\Cov(y,z)^{2(p-1)}}+\frac{\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)}{\Cov(y,z)^{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Cov(y,z)^{2(p-1)}}{\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{p}}+\Cov(y,z)^{p-2}\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)}\,.

Plugging this lower bound into the previous display yields

\displaystyle\E_{y,z}(B(y,\E[y])-B(y,\E[y|z]))

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\Omega(1)\cdot\left(\Cov(y,z)^{2(p-1)}\right)\cdot\left(\E_{y,z}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]+\E_{y}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{-(p-2)}

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{p}}+\Cov(y,z)^{p-2}\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\right)^{-1}\,.

We now lower bound the last two factors using only
\frac{p}{p-1}
-th moments.
Using triangle, Holder, and Jensen’s inequality,

\lvert\Cov(y,z)\rvert\leqslant\left\lparen\E[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\right\rparen^{\frac{p-1}{p}}+\left\lparen\E|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right\rparen^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Cov(y,z)^{p-2}

\displaystyle\leqslant O\left(\left\lparen\E[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\right\rparen^{\frac{p-1}{p}}+\left\lparen\E|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right\rparen^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant O\left(\left\lparen\E[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]+\E|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right\rparen^{\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{p}}\right)\,.

We thus have that

\displaystyle\left(\E_{y,z}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]+\E_{y}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{p-2}

\displaystyle\quad\left(\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{p}}+\Cov(y,z)^{p-2}\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leqslant O\Biggl(\left(\E_{y,z}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]+\E_{y}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{p-2}\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p^{2}-2p+2}{p}}\left(\E\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(p-1)}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad+\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\left(\E_{y,z}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]+\E_{y}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(2p-1)}{p}}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leqslant O\left\lparen\left(\E|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)\left(\E_{y,z}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]+\E_{y}\left[|y|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\E_{z}\left[|z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(2p-1)}{p}}\right\rparen

∎

4.4
Rounding error to potential decrease

In this subsection, we will give a general lemma that can be used to analyze how rounding error on typical objectives is related to a potential decrease when the potential function is based on Bregman uncertainty.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
p
be an even integer and let
q
be an integer in
[1,p-1]
. Let
\mu_{y,z}
be a random pseudodistribution with indeterminates
y,z
and let
\mathrm{err}
denote

\E_{\{\mu\}}\left[\pE_{y,z\sim\mu_{y}\otimes\mu_{z}}\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}^{q}y_{i}z_{j}-\pE_{y,z\sim\mu_{y,z}}\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}^{q}y_{i}z_{j}\right]\,,

where
\E_{\{\mu\}}
denotes the expectation over the randomness in the choice of
\mu
. Let

\Phi(y)=\sum_{i}\left(\phi_{y_{i}}^{(p)}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\,,

where
\phi_{y_{i}}^{(p)}
is the
p
-Bregman uncertainty of
y_{i}
. Let
\psi
be a distribution over
[n]
where
\psi(j)\propto\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\pE[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}
. Then we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\left[\sum_{j}\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\right]\right]\right)^{\frac{p-q}{2(p-1)^{2}}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\left[\sum_{ij}\pE\left[|y_{i}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\right]+\pE\left[|y_{i}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\right]\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\right]\right]\right)^{\frac{(p-q)(p-2)(2p-1)}{2p(p-1)^{2}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{q}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\left[\E_{j\sim\psi}\Phi(y)-\E_{z_{j}}\Phi(y|z_{j})\right]\right)^{\frac{p-q}{2(p-1)^{2}}}\,.

Remark 4.13
.

Note that a sum-of-squares pseudodistribution is consistent with a real distribution on subsets of size at most the degree. Thus, we abuse notation and write
\pE
to denote the expectation over these local distributions for functions of one or two indeterminates, even when they are not (integer) polynomials.

Fact 4.14
.

Let
c>1
and let
x_{i}
be real-valued random variables with
p
-Bregman uncertainty
\phi_{x_{i}}^{(p)}
. Then we have that for any random variable
z
that

\left[\sum_{i\in[n]}\left|\phi_{x_{i}}^{(p)}-\E_{z}\phi_{x_{i}|z}^{(p)}\right|^{c}\right]\leqslant\left[\sum_{i\in[n]}\left(\left|\phi_{x_{i}}^{(p)}\right|^{c}-\E_{z}\left|\phi_{x_{i}|z}^{(p)}\right|^{c}\right)\right]

Note that the above fact allows us to utilize bounds from
Lemma

4.8
in bounding the decrease in potential values of the form
\Phi=\sum_{i}\left(\phi_{x_{i}}^{(p)}\right)^{c}
.

Proof.

This follows by noting that for non-negative numbers
a\geqslant b
and
c>1
we have that
|a-b|^{c}\leqslant a^{c}-b^{c}
. Bregman divergences (and thus our potential functions) are non-negative and only decrease in expectation when conditioning on a variable, so applying this to each entry of the sum and using convexity to bound
\left|\E_{z}\phi_{x_{i}|z}^{(p)}\right|^{c}\leqslant\E_{z}\left|\phi_{x_{i}|z}^{(p)}\right|^{c}
completes the proof.
∎

We now return to the proof of
Lemma

4.12
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.12
.

Note that we have that

\mathrm{err}=\E_{\{\mu\}}\left[\pE_{y,z\sim\mu_{y}\otimes\mu_{z}}\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}^{q}y_{i}z_{j}-\pE_{y,z\sim\mu_{y,z}}A_{ij}^{q}y_{i}z_{j}\right]\leqslant\E_{\{\mu\}}\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^{q}\,|\Cov(y_{i},z_{j})|\,.

Let
\Delta(i|j)=\E_{z_{j}}\phi_{y_{i}}^{(p)}-\phi_{y_{i}|z_{j}}^{(p)}
and define

\alpha_{ij}=\pE_{y_{i},z_{j}}\left[|y_{i}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]+\pE\left[|y_{i}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\,.

Then by
Lemma

4.8
we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}

\displaystyle\leqslant\E_{\{\mu\}}\left[\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^{q}\,|\Cov(y_{i},z_{j})|\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant O(1)\cdot\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^{q}\cdot\E_{\{\mu\}}\left[\left(\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}\alpha_{ij}^{\frac{(p-2)(2p-1)}{2p(p-1)}}\Delta(i|j)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}\right]\,.

Note that
\frac{1}{2(p-1)}+\frac{(p-2)(2p-1)}{2p(p-1)}+\frac{1}{2(p-1)}\leqslant 1
for
p\geqslant 2
and thus we have by Hölder and convexity that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}

\displaystyle\leqslant O(1)\cdot\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^{q}\cdot\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\alpha_{ij}\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(2p-1)}{2p(p-1)}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\Delta(i|j)\right)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}\,.

Applying Hölder with
p/q
and
p/(p-q)
we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}

\displaystyle\leqslant O(1)\cdot\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^{q}\cdot\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\alpha_{ij}\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(2p-1)}{2p(p-1)}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\Delta(i|j)\right)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\leqslant O(1)\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{q}\left(\sum_{i,j}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{p}{2(p-1)(p-q)}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\alpha_{ij}\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(2p-1)}{2(p-q)(p-1)}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\Delta(i|j)\right)^{\frac{p}{2(p-q)(p-1)}}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{p}}\,.

Applying Hölders again with
\frac{2(p-1)^{2}}{(p-2)(2p-1)}
and
\frac{2(p-1)^{2}}{p}
gives us that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}

\displaystyle\leqslant O(1)\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{q}\left(\sum_{i,j}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{p}{2(p-1)(p-q)}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\alpha_{ij}\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(2p-1)}{2(p-q)(p-1)}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\Delta(i|j)\right)^{\frac{p}{2(p-q)(p-1)}}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant O(1)\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{q}\left(\sum_{i,j}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\alpha_{ij}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\right)^{\frac{(p-q)(p-2)(2p-1)}{2p(p-1)^{2}}}\left(\sum_{i,j}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\E\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\Delta(i|j)\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{2(p-1)^{2}}}\,.

Let by
\psi(j)\propto\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\left[\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right]\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}
. Then we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}

\displaystyle\leqslant O(1)\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{q}\left(\sum_{i,j}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\alpha_{ij}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\right)^{\frac{(p-q)(p-2)(2p-1)}{2p(p-1)^{2}}}\left(\sum_{i,j}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\Delta(i|j)\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{2(p-1)^{2}}}

\displaystyle=O(1)\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{q}\left(\sum_{i,j}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\alpha_{ij}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\right)^{\frac{(p-q)(p-2)(2p-1)}{2p(p-1)^{2}}}\left(\sum_{j}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\left[\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]\right]\right)^{\frac{p-q}{p-q}}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{2(p-1)^{2}}}

\displaystyle\quad\left(\E_{j\sim\psi}\sum_{i}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\Delta(i|j)\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{2(p-1)^{2}}}\,.

We now note that

\E_{j\sim\psi}\sum_{i}\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\Delta(i|j)\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\leqslant\E_{j\sim\psi}\E_{\{\mu\}}\sum_{i}\Delta(i|j)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}=\E_{j\sim\psi}\E_{\{\mu\}}\sum_{i}\left(\phi_{y_{i}}^{(p)}-\E_{z_{j}}\phi_{y_{i}|z_{j}}^{(p)}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}

where
\frac{p-1}{p-q}>1
so we can apply
4.14
to get that

\E_{j\sim\psi}\E_{\{\mu\}}\sum_{i}\Delta(i|j)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\leqslant\E_{\{\mu\}}\E_{j\sim\psi}\sum_{i}\left(\phi_{y_{i}}^{(p)}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}-\E_{z_{j}}\left(\phi_{y_{i}|z_{j}}^{(p)}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\,.

Plugging this back into the previous inequality and using that by convexity and Almost Triangle Inequality we have that

\left(\E_{\{\mu\}}\pE[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}]\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\leqslant\E_{\{\mu\}}\pE[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}]\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{ij}^{\frac{p-1}{p-q}}\leqslant O\left(\pE\left[|y_{i}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\right]+\pE\left[|y_{i}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\right]\pE\left[|z_{j}|^{\frac{p}{p-q}}\right]\right)

completes the proof.
∎

4.5
Generalizing fractional Bregman uncertainty to asymmetric correlations

In this section, we generalize the potential decrease lemma from the previous sections to cases where we have correlations of the form
\Cov(u^{p},v^{q})
for
p\neq q
. This will be necessary for our application to
p\rightarrow q
norms.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
p\leqslant q
be even integers and let
B
be the Bregman divergence associated with
|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
. Then we have that

\E_{u,v}\left(B(u^{p-1},\E u^{p-1})-B(u^{p-1},\E[u^{p-1}|v])\right)\geqslant\frac{\Cov^{\frac{2q(p-1)}{p}}(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})}{\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{(q-p)(p-2)+pq}{p^{2}}}\left(\E[u^{p}v^{q}]+\E u^{p}\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{2pq(p-2)-(p^{2}-2q)}{p^{2}}}}\,.

Proof.

Consider the problem of predicting
v^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1}
given
v
. Let
x_{1}=|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E u^{p-1}
and let
x_{2}
be the result of a mirror descent update step on
x_{1}
with cost function
\sgn(v)\cdot|v|^{\frac{q}{p}}
. Applying
4.5
to
|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1}
,
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
we have that

\displaystyle\langle\eta\sgn(v)\cdot|v|^{\frac{q}{p}},|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1}-|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E u^{p-1}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=B(|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1},|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E u^{p-1})-B(|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1},x_{2})+B(x_{1},x_{2})\,.

When we take the expectation over
u,v
on both sides, we have that the left-hand side is
\eta\Cov(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})
, while the right-hand side is at most

\E_{u,v}\left[B(|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1},|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E u^{p-1})-B(|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1},|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E[u^{p-1}|v])+B(x_{1},x_{2})\right]\,,

since expectations minimize the expected Bregman divergence to a fixed point (
4.3
). Thus,

\displaystyle\eta\Cov(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})\leqslant\E_{u,v}\Biggl[

\displaystyle B(|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1},|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E u^{p-1})

\displaystyle\quad-B(|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1},|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E[u^{p-1}|v])+B(x_{1},x_{2})\Biggr]\,.

Note that by a similar argument to
Lemma

4.10
we have that

\displaystyle\E_{u,v}\Biggl[B(|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1},|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E u^{p-1})-B(|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}u^{p-1},|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E[u^{p-1}|v])\Biggr]

\displaystyle\quad=\E_{u,v}\Biggl[|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p-1}}\left(B(u^{p-1},\E u^{p-1})-B(u^{p-1},\E[u^{p-1}|v])\right)\Biggr]

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\left(\E_{u,v}v^{q}\left(B(u^{p-1},\E u^{p-1})-B(u^{p-1},\E[u^{p-1}|v])\right)\right)^{\frac{pq-p-q}{q(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left(\E_{u,v}\left(B(u^{p-1},\E u^{p-1})-B(u^{p-1},\E[u^{p-1}|v])\right)\right)^{\frac{p}{q(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\left(\E[u^{p}v^{q}]+\E u^{p}\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{pq-p-q}{q(p-1)}}\left(\E_{u,v}\left(B(u^{p-1},\E u^{p-1})-B(u^{p-1},\E[u^{p-1}|v])\right)\right)^{\frac{p}{q(p-1)}}\,.

We now turn our attention to the
\E_{u,v}B(x_{1},x_{2})
error term. By
Lemma

4.11
we have that

\displaystyle\E_{u,v}B(x_{1},x_{2})\leqslant\E_{u,v}\left[\eta^{2}|v|^{\frac{2q}{p}}\left(|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q}{p}}\E u^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}+\eta^{p}|v|^{q}\right]\leqslant\E_{u,v}\left[\eta^{2}|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q+2}{p-1}}\left(\E u^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}+\eta^{p}|v|^{q}\right]\,.

Thus, combining this we have that

\displaystyle\eta\Cov(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\E[u^{p}v^{q}]+\E u^{p}\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{pq-p-q}{q(p-1)}}\left(\E_{u,v}\left(B(u^{p-1},\E u^{p-1})-B(u^{p-1},\E[u^{p-1}|v])\right)\right)^{\frac{p}{q(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\quad+\E_{u,v}\left[\eta^{2}|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q+2}{p-1}}\left(\E u^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}+\eta^{p}v^{q}\right]\,.

We aim to pick
\eta
such that the error term
\E_{u,v}\left[\eta^{2}|v|^{\frac{pq-p-q+2}{p-1}}\left(\E u^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}+\eta^{p}v^{q}\right]
is smaller than the left hand side. Thus, it suffices to take

\eta\leqslant\min\left(\frac{\Cov(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})}{\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{pq-p-q+2}{q(p-1)}}\left(\E u^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}},\,\left(\frac{\Cov(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})}{\E v^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)\,.

Furthermore, note that since

\min(A,B)\geqslant\frac{AB}{A+B}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{A}+\frac{1}{B}}\,,

we have that

\displaystyle\eta\geqslant\frac{1}{\frac{\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{pq-p-q+2}{q(p-1)}}\left(\E u^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}}{\Cov(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})}+\frac{\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\Cov^{\frac{1}{p}}(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})}}=\frac{\Cov(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})}{\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{pq-p-q+2}{q(p-1)}}\left(\E u^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}+\Cov^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})\cdot\left(Ev^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\,.

Observe that by Triangle Inequality, we have that

\Cov^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})\leqslant O(1)\cdot\left(\E_{u,v}u^{p-1}v^{q-1}+\E u^{p-1}\E v^{q-1}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}

and by Hölders and convexity we have that

\E_{u,v}u^{p-1}v^{q-1}\leqslant\left(\E_{u,v}u^{p}v^{q}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{q-p}{pq}}\,.

Thus we have that

\Cov^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})\leqslant\left(\E_{u,v}u^{p}v^{q}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{(q-p)(p-2)}{pq(p-1)}}+\left(\E u^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{(p-2)(q-1)}{(p-1)q}}\,,

and thus

\eta\geqslant\frac{\Cov(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})}{\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{(q-p)(p-2)+pq}{pq(p-1)}}\left(\left(\E_{u,v}u^{p}v^{q}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}+\left(\E u^{p}\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\right)}\,.

Returning to the original inequality, we have that

\eta\Cov(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})\leqslant\left(\E[u^{p}v^{q}]+\E u^{p}\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{pq-p-q}{q(p-1)}}\left(\E_{u,v}\left(B(u^{p-1},\E u^{p-1})-B(u^{p-1},\E[u^{p-1}|v])\right)\right)^{\frac{p}{q(p-1)}}\,.

Note that

\left(\E_{u,v}u^{p}v^{q}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}+\left(\E u^{p}\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\leqslant O(1)\cdot\left(\E_{u,v}u^{p}v^{q}+\E u^{p}\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}

so plugging in the lower bound on
\eta
and rearranging yields

\E_{u,v}\left(B(u^{p-1},\E u^{p-1})-B(u^{p-1},\E[u^{p-1}|v])\right)\geqslant\frac{\Cov^{\frac{2q(p-1)}{p}}(u^{p-1},v^{q-1})}{\left(\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{(q-p)(p-2)+pq}{p^{2}}}\left(\E[u^{p}v^{q}]+\E u^{p}\E v^{q}\right)^{\frac{2pq(p-2)-(p^{2}-2q)}{p^{2}}}}\,.

∎

\displaystyle\qquad=\tr\left((U+XM)^{\top}(U-XM)(V+Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})^{\top}(V-Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})\right)\,,

and we will show that
(U+XM)^{\top}(U-XM)=0
. We have that

\displaystyle(U+XM)^{\top}(U-XM)

\displaystyle=U^{\top}U-M^{\top}X^{\top}XM+M^{\top}X^{\top}U-U^{\top}XM\,.

Note that

M^{\top}X^{\top}XM=(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}Q^{\top}Q(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}=U^{\top}U\,,

so we have that
(U+XM)^{\top}(U-XM)=M^{\top}X^{\top}U-U^{\top}XM
. Furthermore, for our choice of
M
, the matrix
M^{\top}X^{\top}U
is symmetric, so this is
0
. We have that

\displaystyle U^{\top}XM

\displaystyle=U^{\top}X(X^{\top}X)^{-1/2}Q(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=U^{\top}\widehat{X}Q(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}\widehat{U}^{\top}\widehat{X}Q(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}H(Q^{\top}Q)(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}H(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}\,,

using the definitions of
\widehat{X},\widehat{U}
, and
M
along with the fact that
Q
is unitary. Finally, note that
H
is symmetric, so we have that
U^{\top}XM
is symmetric. Thus, we have that
(U+XM)^{\top}(U-XM)=0
and

\displaystyle\langle(U+XM)(V-Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})^{\top},(U-XM)(V+Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})^{\top}\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle\qquad=\tr\left((U+XM)^{\top}(U-XM)(V+Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})^{\top}(V-Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})\right)=0\,,

Since
(U+XM)(V-Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})^{\top},(U-XM)(V+Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})^{\top}
are orthogonal, we have that

\lVert(U+XM)(V-Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})^{\top}\rVert_{F}^{2}+\lVert(U-XM)(V+Y\left(M^{-1}\right)^{\top})^{\top}\rVert_{F}^{2}\leqslant 4\lVert UV^{\top}-XY^{\top}\rVert_{F}^{2}\,,

which completes the proof of the identifiability inequality.

We now consider the explicit form above for
M
and
M^{-1}
to bound the entries. We have that

M=(X^{\top}X)^{-1/2}Q(U^{\top}U)^{1/2}\,,

which has bounded entries by our assumption on the entries of
U
and
(X^{\top}X)^{-1/2}
, along with the fact that
Q
is a unitary matrix and thus also bounded. We now consider the explicit form for
M^{-1}
. We have that

M^{-1}=(X^{\top}X)^{1/2}Q^{\top}(U^{\top}U)^{-1/2}\,,

which has bounded entries by our assumption on the entries of
X
and
(U^{\top}U)^{-1/2}
, along with the fact that
Q^{\top}
is a unitary matrix and thus also bounded. This completes the second part of the proof.
∎

Balancing Residuals.

We now show that there exist
N_{1}
,
N_{2}
satisfying the SDP constraints. Intuitively, these matrices are attempting to balance the residuals such that the norms
\lVert(U+X)(V-Y)^{\top}\rVert_{p},\lVert(U-X)(V+Y)^{\top}\rVert_{p}
being large is witnessed by the norms of both matrices in the product.

Lemma 7.8

(Pairwise
\ell_{p}
Balancing)
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}^{\geqslant 1}
and let
F\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
and
G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
have full column rank. Then there exists an invertible
N\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
such that

\lVert FN\rVert_{p}^{p}=\left\|G\left(N^{-1}\right)^{\top}\right\|_{p}^{p}\leqslant k\cdot\lVert FG^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p/2}\,.

In the proof of
Lemma

7.8
, we will need the following fact:

Fact 7.9

(Auerbach’s Lemma)
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional normed vector space with norm
\|\cdot\|
.
There exists
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V
which is a basis for
V
and
v_{1}^{*},\ldots,v_{n}^{*}\in V^{*}
such that
v_{i}^{*}(v_{j})=\delta_{ij}
,
\|v_{i}\|=1
, and
\|v_{i}^{*}\|_{*}=1
.

Proof of
Lemma

7.8
.

Note that it suffices to show that there exists an invertible
N_{0}
such that

\lVert FN_{0}\rVert_{p}^{p}\cdot\left\|G\left(N_{0}^{-1}\right)^{\top}\right\|_{p}^{p}\leqslant k^{2}\cdot\lVert FG^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}\,.

Given such an
N_{0}
we can pick
s
such that
\lVert FsN_{0}\rVert_{p}^{p}=\lVert G(sN_{0})^{-1}\rVert_{p}^{p}
and then letting
N=sN_{0}
proves the claim. Define the following norm on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
:

\lVert x\rVert_{F}\vcentcolon=\lVert Fx\rVert_{p}\,.

Note that since
F
has full column rank, this is in fact a norm. Furthermore, by
7.9
we have that there exists a basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
and a dual basis
v_{1}^{*},\ldots,v_{k}^{*}
satisfying the above Auerbach basis properties for
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{F}
. Let
N_{0}
be the invertible matrix whose
t
-th column is
v_{t}
. Then we have that

\lVert FN_{0}\rVert_{p}^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert F_{i}N_{0}\rVert_{p}^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=1}^{k}(\langle F_{i},v_{t}\rangle)^{p}=\sum_{t=1}^{k}\lVert Fv_{t}\rVert_{p}^{p}=k\,.

Now consider
\lVert G\left(N_{0}^{-1}\right)^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}
. We will bound this by showing that
FN_{0}
does not contract the
\ell_{p}
norm of any vector
x
. Note that we have that for any vector
x
and any
t\in[k]
,

\lvert x_{t}\rvert=\left|v_{t}^{*}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}v_{i}\right)\right|

by the Auerbach basis properties since
v_{t}^{*}(v_{t})=1
and
v_{t}^{*}(v_{u})=0
for all
u\neq t
. Furthermore, by generalized Cauchy-Schwartz

\lvert x_{t}\rvert=\left|v_{t}^{*}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}v_{i}\right)\right|\leqslant\lVert v_{t}^{*}\rVert_{*}\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}v_{i}\rVert_{F}=\lVert FN_{0}x\rVert_{p}\,.

Thus, we have concluded that
\lVert x\rVert_{\infty}^{p}\leqslant\lVert FN_{0}x\rVert_{p}^{p}
and
\lVert x\rVert_{p}^{p}\leqslant k\lVert FN_{0}x\rVert_{p}^{p}
. Let
\widetilde{G}=G\left(N_{0}^{-1}\right)^{\top}
, and we now apply the above fact with
\widetilde{g}_{j}
. Note that we have that

\displaystyle\lVert FG^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\lVert FN_{0}\widetilde{G}^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lVert FN_{0}\widetilde{g}_{j}\rVert_{p}^{p}\geqslant\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lVert\widetilde{g}_{j}\rVert_{p}^{p}=\frac{1}{k}\left\|G\left(N_{0}^{-1}\right)^{\top}\right\|_{p}^{p}\,.

Putting this all together, we have that

\lVert FG^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}\geqslant\frac{1}{k}\lVert G\left(N_{0}^{-1}\right)^{\top}\rVert_{p}^{p}\geqslant\frac{1}{k^{2}}\lVert FN_{0}\rVert_{p}^{p}\cdot\left\|G\left(N_{0}^{-1}\right)^{\top}\right\|_{p}^{p}\,,

which completes the proof.
∎

Discretization.

We now analyze the effect of discretization on the SDP objective value. In particular, we prove the following lemma:

Lemma 7.10

Lemma 7.11
.

We now apply Hölders with

and
\frac{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-1\right)}{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)+\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2{p^{*}}+2q\right)}
to the outer sum to get that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}\E v_{i}^{q}\right)^{\frac{(q-{p^{*}})({p^{*}}-2)+{p^{*}}q}{2{p^{*}}q({p^{*}}-1)}}

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}\left(\sum_{j\in[m]}|A_{ij}|\cdot\delta(j|i)^{\frac{{p^{*}}}{2q({p^{*}}-1)}}\cdot\alpha_{ij}^{\frac{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)-\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2q\right)}{2{p^{*}}q({p^{*}}-1)}}\right)^{\frac{2{p^{*}}q({p^{*}}-1)}{2{p^{*}}q({p^{*}}-2)+({p^{*}}^{2}-2{p^{*}}+2q)}}\right)^{\frac{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)+\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2{p^{*}}+2q\right)}{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-1\right)}}\,.

Furthermore, since we enforce that

\sum_{i}\E v_{i}^{q}\leqslant 1

\mathrm{err}\leqslant\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}\left(\sum_{j\in[m]}|A_{ij}|\cdot\delta(j|i)^{\frac{{p^{*}}}{2q({p^{*}}-1)}}\cdot\alpha_{ij}^{\frac{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)-\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2q\right)}{2{p^{*}}q({p^{*}}-1)}}\right)^{\frac{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-1\right)}{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)+\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2{p^{*}}+2q\right)}}\right)^{\frac{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)+\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2{p^{*}}+2q\right)}{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-1\right)}}\,.

\frac{2{p^{*}}q({p^{*}}-1)}{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)-\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2q\right)}
. This gives us that

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}\leqslant\Biggl(\sum_{i\in[n]}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\in[m]}A_{ij}^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{2q\left({p^{*}}-1\right)}{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)+\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2{p^{*}}+2q\right)}}\left(\sum_{j\in[m]}\delta(j|i)\right)^{\frac{{\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}}}{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)+\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2{p^{*}}+2q\right)}}

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{j\in[m]}\alpha_{ij}\right)^{\frac{2p^{*}q(p^{*}-2)-((p^{*})^{2}-2q)}{2p^{*}q(p^{*}-2)+((p^{*})^{2}-2p^{*}+2q)}}\Biggr)^{\frac{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-2\right)+\left(\left(p^{*}\right)^{2}-2{p^{*}}+2q\right)}{2{p^{*}}q\left({p^{*}}-1\right)}}\,.

We now apply Hölders again on the outer sum with
\frac{2p^{*}q(p^{*}-2)+((p^{*})^{2}-2p^{*}+2q)}{2p^{*}(p^{*}-1)}
and
\frac{2p^{*}q(p^{*}-2)+((p^{*})^{2}-2p^{*}+2q)}{2p^{*}q(p^{*}-2)-((p^{*})^{2}-2q)}

\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\alpha_{ij}=\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\E[u_{j}^{p^{*}}v_{i}^{q}]+\E u_{j}^{p^{*}}\E v_{i}^{q}=\E\left[\lVert u\rVert_{p^{*}}^{p^{*}}\lVert v\rVert_{q}^{q}\right]+\E\lVert u\rVert_{p^{*}}^{p^{*}}\E\lVert v\rVert_{q}^{q}\leqslant 2\,.

\displaystyle\mathrm{err}\leqslant O(1)\cdot\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}\left(\sum_{j\in[m]}A_{ij}^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{*}}}\left(\sum_{j\in[m]}\delta(j|i)\right)^{\frac{p^{*}}{2(p^{*}-1)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

Final Approximation Guarantee.

We now argue that the output vector
\hat{x}
at the end of the algorithm in fact witnesses a lower bound on the

norm with additive error
\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}

Proof of
Theorem

8.1
.

Note that by

we have that some iteration of our loop has SDP value at least
\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}-\varepsilon\cdot\left(\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}
. Consider the
\hat{x}
and
\hat{y}=\pE y
produced by this iteration of the loop. Note that since we are rounding a bilinear form, outputting the expectation of

produces the same objective value as the expected objective when rounding each coordinate independently. Note that by
Lemma

8.4
and the fact that the potential function is at most
1
by norm bounds, we have that

\E_{\{\mu\}}\langle\hat{y},A\hat{x}\rangle\geqslant\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}-\varepsilon\cdot\left(\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\,,

where the expectation on the LHS is over the randomness in the conditioning. Furthermore, note that we always have that
\lVert\hat{x}\rVert_{p},\lVert\hat{y}\rVert_{q^{*}}\leqslant 1
by Jensen’s since for

\lVert\hat{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}=\sum_{j}\|\pE x_{j}\|^{p}\leqslant\sum_{j}\pE\|x_{j}\|^{p}=\pE\lVert x\rVert_{p}^{p}\leqslant 1\,,

and similarly for
\hat{y}
we also have that
\lVert\hat{y}\rVert_{q^{*}}\leqslant 1
. Thus,
\langle\hat{y},A\hat{x}\rangle\leqslant\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}
with probability

, and thus by Markov’s we have with probability

\langle\hat{y},A\hat{x}\rangle\geqslant\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}-100\varepsilon\cdot\left(\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\,.

Note that since
\hat{x},\hat{y}
have the relevant norms at most
1

\tilde{x},\tilde{y}
. We thus have that

\langle\tilde{y},A\tilde{x}\rangle\geqslant\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}-100\varepsilon\cdot\left(\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}

for
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}

and

. Therefore,

\lVert A\tilde{x}\rVert_{q}\geqslant\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}-100\varepsilon\cdot\left(\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\,,

and taking

\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}\leqslant\left(\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}
(via Hölders) completes the proof of the approximation guarantee. For the runtime analysis, we note that each iteration of the loop takes time
(nm)^{O\left(\varepsilon^{-4q}\right)}
by

and there are

∎

8.1
Comparison to Prior Work

We compare the additive error
\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}
to the additive error obtained in the algorithms of
[
BBHKSZ12
]
and
[
BH15
]
.

The
p=2
case

When
p=2
,
[
BH15
]
, improving over
[
BBHKSZ12
]
, give a polynomial time algorithm for each fixed
\varepsilon
with additive error
\varepsilon\cdot\|A\|_{2\rightarrow 2}^{2}\cdot\|A\|_{2\rightarrow\infty}^{2}
.
This is strictly incomparable to
\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{i}\|A_{i}\|_{2}^{q}
– smaller if the row-norms of
A
are all similar, and larger if
A
has one very large row making
\|A\|_{2\rightarrow\infty}
very large.

The
p<2
case

[
BH15
]
’s Theorem 15 also provides a nontrivial additive approximation for
\|A\|_{p\rightarrow q}
when
p<2
, the only such algorithm we are aware of.
Specializing Theorem 15 of
[
BH15
]
to the map
A:\ell_{p}^{m}\to\ell_{q}^{n}
, the relevant quantity in their guarantee is the factorization norm of
A
through
\ell_{1}
,

\gamma_{1}(A)\vcentcolon=\lVert A\rVert_{\ell_{p}\rightarrow\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{q}}=\inf_{A=RL}\lVert L\rVert_{\ell_{p}\rightarrow\ell_{1}}\lVert R\rVert_{\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{q}}.

Their theorem, given such a factorization explicitly, gives additive error
\varepsilon\cdot\gamma_{1}(A)
for
\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}
, and hence, after adjusting constants depending only on
q
, additive error
\varepsilon\cdot\gamma_{1}(A)^{q}
for
\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow q}^{q}
.
Thus the comparison is between our error term
\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{i\leqslant n}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}^{q}
and the factorization-based quantity
\varepsilon\cdot\gamma_{1}(A)^{q}
.

These two bounds are not ordered in general. If
A
admits a genuinely good factorization through
\ell_{1}
, namely if

\gamma_{1}(A)^{q}\ll\sum_{i\leqslant n}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}^{q},

then
[
BH15
]
can be substantially better. On the other hand, the trivial row decomposition

Ax=\sum_{i\leqslant n}\langle A_{i},x\rangle e_{i}

immediately gives

\gamma_{1}(A)\leqslant\lVert A\rVert_{p\rightarrow 1}\leqslant\sum_{i\leqslant n}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}.

Hence, whenever this trivial factorization is close to optimal, Theorem 15 yields error at most

\varepsilon\cdot\left(\sum_{i\leqslant n}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}\right)^{q},

whereas our error is
\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{i\leqslant n}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}^{q}
. Since

\sum_{i\leqslant n}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}^{q}\leqslant\left(\sum_{i\leqslant n}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}\right)^{q},

and the gap can be as large as
n^{q-1}
when the row norms are comparable, our guarantee is better on instances whose row
p^{*}
-mass is spread across many rows and for which the trivial row factorization is near-optimal, while
[
BH15
]
is better on instances with a small
\ell_{1}
-factorization norm.

We note that by our bounds on

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(A_{ij}\pm\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}-u_{i}v_{j}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(A_{ij}-\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}\right)^{p}+\sum_{q=1}^{p}\binom{p}{q}\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}-u_{i}v_{j}\right)^{q}\left(A_{ij}-\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}\right)^{p-q}\,.

Thus, plugging this all in, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(A_{ij}-u_{i}v_{j}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(A_{ij}-\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}\right)^{p}+\sum_{q=1}^{p}\binom{p}{q}\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}-u_{i}v_{j}\right)^{q}\left(A_{ij}-\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}\right)^{p-q}

we have that for all

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(A_{ij}-u_{i}v_{j}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(A_{ij}-\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}\right)^{p}+\sum_{q=1}^{p}\binom{p}{q}\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}-u_{i}v_{j}\right)^{q}\left(A_{ij}-\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}\right)^{p-q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i\in[n],j\in[m]}\left(A_{ij}-\tilde{u}_{i}\tilde{v}_{j}\right)^{p}+O_{p}(1)\cdot\sum_{q=1}^{p}\left(nm\cdot\gamma\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{1/2}\right)^{q}\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{p-q}\,.

\left(nm\cdot\gamma\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{1/2}\right)^{q}\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{p-q}\leqslant\left(nm\cdot\gamma\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{1/2}\right)\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{p-1}+\left(nm\cdot\gamma\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{1/2}\right)^{p-1}\lVert A\rVert_{p}\,.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
have bit complexity
B
and let
G\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
satisfy
\rank(G)=k
and

\lVert A-G\rVert_{p}^{p}\leqslant\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{p}\,,

Let
G=U\Sigma V^{\top}
be the SVD factorization of
G
. Then
X=U\Sigma^{1/2}
and
Y=V\Sigma^{1/2}
satisfy
\lVert X\rVert_{\infty},\lVert Y\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\poly_{p}(nmk)\cdot 2^{O_{p}(B)}
.

Proof.

Note that since
\lVert U_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2},\lVert V_{j}\rVert_{2}^{2}\leqslant 1
it suffices to bound the singular values or
\lVert G\rVert_{F}^{2}
. Observe that since
A
has bounded bit complexity, we have that

\lVert A-G\rVert_{p}^{p}\leqslant\lVert A\rVert_{p}^{p}\leqslant nm\cdot 2^{pB}\,.

Furthermore, since
\ell_{p}
and
\ell_{2}
norms are equivalent up to polynomial in dimension factors, we also have that

\lVert A-G\rVert_{F}^{2}\leqslant\poly(nm)\cdot 2^{pB}\,.

Thus, we have that

\lVert G\rVert_{F}^{2}\leqslant O\left(\lVert A-G\rVert_{F}^{2}+\lVert A\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)\leqslant\poly(nm)\cdot 2^{pB}\,.

Therefore, all singular values are most
\poly(nm)\cdot 2^{pB}
and therefore
\lVert X\rVert_{\infty},\lVert Y\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\poly_{p}(nmk)\cdot 2^{O_{p}(B)}
.
∎

p\rightarrow q
Discretization.

We now also show that our discretization is sufficient for
p\rightarrow q
norms.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
p\leqslant 2\leqslant q
and let
p^{*}
be the Hölder dual of
p
. Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
and let
\Sigma
be a
1/(\poly(nm/\varepsilon))
-fineness grid of
[-1,1]
. Then we have that

\max_{\lVert x\rVert_{p}\leqslant 1,\lVert y\rVert_{q^{*}}\leqslant 1}\langle y,Ax\rangle\leqslant\max_{\lVert x\rVert_{p}\leqslant 1,\lVert y\rVert_{q^{*}}\leqslant 1,x_{i}\in\Sigma,y_{j}\in\Sigma}\langle y,Ax\rangle+\varepsilon\cdot\left(\sum_{i\leqslant n}\|A_{i}\|_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\,.

Proof.

Let
x,y
be the vectors maximizing

\max_{\lVert x\rVert_{p}\leqslant 1,\lVert y\rVert_{q^{*}}\leqslant 1}\langle y,Ax\rangle\,.

Note that since
\lVert x\rVert_{p}\leqslant 1,\lVert y\rVert_{q^{*}}\leqslant 1
we have that all
y_{i},x_{j}\in[-1,1]
. Thus, if we let
\bar{x},\bar{y}
be
x,y
with each entry being rounded to the nearest element in
\Sigma
then we have that
\lVert x-\bar{x}\rVert_{\infty},\lVert y-\bar{y}\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant 1/(\poly(nm/\varepsilon))
.

\displaystyle\langle y,Ax\rangle-\langle\bar{y},A\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y-\bar{y},Ax\rangle+\langle\bar{y},A(x-\bar{x})\rangle\,.

Note that for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n},v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we have that

u^{\top}Av\leqslant\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}\lVert v\rVert_{\infty}\sum_{i\in[n]}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{1}\,.

Applying this above we have that

\langle y,Ax\rangle-\langle\bar{y},A\bar{x}\rangle\leqslant\left(\lVert y-\bar{y}\rVert_{\infty}+\lVert x-\bar{x}\rVert_{\infty}\right)\cdot\sum_{i\in[n]}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{1}\,.

We now bound
\sum_{i\in[n]}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{1}
. Note that
\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{1}\leqslant m\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}
so we have that

\sum_{i\in[n]}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{1}\leqslant m\sum_{i\in[n]}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}\,,

and
q\geqslant 2
so by Hölders

m\sum_{i\in[n]}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}\leqslant mn^{1-1/q}\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leqslant mn\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\,.

Finally letting
\lVert y-\bar{y}\rVert_{\infty},\lVert x-\bar{x}\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon/(nm)
yields that

\langle y,Ax\rangle-\langle\bar{y},A\bar{x}\rangle\leqslant\varepsilon\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}\lVert A_{i}\rVert_{p^{*}}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\,.

∎

A.2
Discrete Pinning Lemma from Mirror Descent

We now describe how to obtain discrete pinning lemmas from mirror descent. In particular, we sketch a proof of the following:

Lemma A.4
.

For every
t\in\mathbb{N}
there is
s\leqslant t
such that if
S\subseteq[n]
is a random set of size
s
and
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
are any jointly distributed discrete random variables,

\E_{S}\E_{i,j\sim[n]}\tv(\{X_{i},X_{j}|X_{S}\},\{X_{i}|X_{S}\}\otimes\{X_{j}|X_{S}\})\leqslant\left(\frac{\E_{i}H(X_{i})}{t}\right)^{2}\,.

The above lemma is proved by utilizing the following non-negative potential function:

\Phi(s)=\E_{S\subseteq[n],|S|=s}\E_{i\sim[n]}H(X_{i}|X_{S})\,.

In order to prove
Lemma

A.4
it is enough to lower bound the decrement in potential function
\Phi(s)-\Phi(s+1)
. This is captured by the following lemma:

Lemma A.5
.

Let
x,y
be jointly distributed random variables over
[m]
. Then we have that

\tv^{2}(\{x,y\},\{x\}\otimes\{y\})\leqslant O(1)\cdot\left(H(x)-H(x|y)\right)\,.

Lemma

A.5
is a simple consequence of Pinsker’s inequality and the definition of mutual information. From
Lemma

A.5
we have that
Lemma

A.4
easily follows. Below we give a proof of
Lemma

A.5
using mirror descent.

Proof.

We consider the following setup for our mirror descent game: let
x,y
be sampled and let the player guess the hidden value of
x
. Guesses take the form of distributions over
[m]
or vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
on the simplex, where the correct guess is
\mathbf{1}_{x}
.

We study how a player can update their guess for
x
when given
y
, when measuring error with respect to the Bregman divergence associated with
f(z)=-\sum_{i\in[m]}z_{i}\log z_{i}
. Note that the Bregman divergence between two distributions is the KL divergence between them. When given no other information other than the distribution of
x
, the guess which minimizes the Bregman divergence to the value
\mathbf{1}_{x}
in expectation is
\E\mathbf{1}_{x}=\{x\}
. Furthermore, note that this value is

\E_{x}B(\mathbf{1}_{x},\{x\})=\E_{x}\log\left(\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)=H(x)\,.

We now explicitly construct a function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
which updates the original guess for
x
(which was
\{x\}
) when given the value of
y
. This function
f
is a single step of (projected) mirror descent with the negative entropy regularizer. Note that negative entropy is
1
strongly convex with respect to
\ell_{1}
. Therefore, for any cost function
g_{y}
, we have by
Lemma

4.7

\eta\langle g_{y},\mathbf{1}_{x}-\{x\}\rangle\leqslant B(\mathbf{1}_{x},\{x\})-B(\mathbf{1}_{x},z)+\eta^{2}\|g_{y}\|_{\infty}^{2}\,,

where
z
is the result of taking a mirror descent step with gradient
g
and step size
\eta
from
\{x\}
and then Bregman projecting the result back to the simplex.

We now describe how to pick
g_{y}
based on the value of
g
. Note that by the variational characterization of
\tv
there exists some function
h
such that
\lVert h\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant 1
and

\tv(\{x,y\},\{x\}\otimes\{y\})=\E_{\{x,y\}}h(x,y)-\E_{\{x\}\otimes\{y\}}h(x,y)\,.

Given such a function
h
we let
g_{y}=h(\cdot,y)
. Plugging this into the above inequality and taking an expectation over the joint distribution of
x,y
we have that

\eta\E_{x,y}\langle h(\cdot,y),\mathbf{1}_{x}-\{x\}\rangle\leqslant\E_{x}B(\mathbf{1}_{x},\{x\})-\E_{x,y}B(\mathbf{1}_{x},z)+\eta^{2}\E_{x,y}\|h(\cdot,y)\|_{\infty}^{2}\,.

Note that we have that

\E_{x,y}\langle h(\cdot,y),\mathbf{1}_{x}\rangle=\E_{x,y}h(x,y)\,,

while

\E_{x,y}\langle h(\cdot,y),\{x\}\rangle=\E_{\{x\}\otimes\{y\}}h(x,y)\,,

so the left hand side of this inequality is
\eta\tv(\{x,y\},\{x\}\otimes\{y\})
. Furthermore, since
h
is bounded in
\ell_{\infty}
norm by
1
we have that

\eta\tv(\{x,y\},\{x\}\otimes\{y\})\leqslant\E_{x}B(\mathbf{1}_{x},\{x\})-\E_{x,y}B(\mathbf{1}_{x},z)+\eta^{2}\,.

We now note that the value minimizing the expected Bregman divergence to
\mathbf{1}_{x}
out of all functions of
y
is simply
\{x|y\}
. Combining with the fact that this expected Bregman divergence is entropy (as noted above) we have that

\eta\tv(\{x,y\},\{x\}\otimes\{y\})\leqslant H(x)-H(x|y)+\eta^{2}\,.

Setting
\eta=\frac{1}{2}\tv(\{x,y\},\{x\}\otimes\{y\})
completes the proof.
∎

A.3
Impossibility of Heavy-Tailed Pinning Lemma Without Pairwise Upper Bound

We show that the following “dream statement” is false.

Conjecture A.6

(False)
.

For every
p\in\mathbbm{N}
and
t\in\mathbbm{N}
there is
s\leqslant t
such that if
S\subseteq[n]
is a random set of size
s
and
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
are any jointly distributed random variables,

\E_{S}\E_{i,j\sim[n]}|\Cov(X_{i},X_{j}\,|\,X_{S})|\leqslant\frac{1}{t^{\Omega(1)}}\cdot\left[\E_{X}\left\lparen\E_{i\sim[n]}|X_{i}|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right\rparen\right]^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}

The only difference between the above conjecture and the heavy-tailed pinning lemma is whether the exponent
2
lies inside or outside the final brackets on the right-hand side – this makes a crucial difference!

Consider the following family of counterexamples, for the
4/3
case (they generalize easily to other cases).
We divide the coordinates into
n/2
pairs.
For each pair, say
X_{i}
and
X_{i+1}
, we independently draw a random variable
Y_{i}
with infinite first moment but finite
2/3
moment.
Let
X_{i},X_{i+1}
both be equal to the square root of
|Y_{i}|
.
Then even after pinning
o(n)
coordinates, the left-hand side
\E_{S}\E_{i,j\sim[n]}|\Cov(X_{i},X_{j}\,|\,X_{S})
is infinite, because
\Cov(X_{i},X_{i+1})=\E|Y_{i}|-(\E\sqrt{|Y_{i}|})^{2}
, which is infinite.
But the right-hand side depends only on the
4/3
moments of the
X_{i}
variables, which are
2/3
moments of
Y_{i}
, so the right-hand side is finite.

A.4.2
Proof of Heavy-Tailed Pinning Lemma via Truncation

Proposition A.7

(Heavy-tailed pinning via truncation)
.

Assume
1<q<2
, and let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be real-valued random variables. Define

M_{q}:=\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\,\mathbb{E}|X_{i}X_{j}|^{q}=\mathbb{E}\!\left(\mathbb{E}_{i}|X_{i}|^{q}\right)^{2}.

Assume
M_{q}<\infty
. Let
\pi
be a uniformly random permutation of
[n]
, independent of
X
, and define

\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma\bigl(\pi(1),\dots,\pi(t),\,X_{\pi(1)},\dots,X_{\pi(t)}\bigr),\qquad 0\leqslant t\leqslant n.

Then for every
m\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
, there exists
t\in\{0,\dots,m\}
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(X_{i},X_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|\,\Big]\;\leqslant\;7\,(m+1)^{-(q-1)/2}\,M_{q}^{1/q}.

Consequently, if

m+1\geqslant\left(\frac{7}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2/(q-1)},

then for some
t\leqslant m
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(X_{i},X_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|\,\Big]\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon\,M_{q}^{1/q}.

Proof.

If
M_{q}=0
, then
X_{i}=0
a.s. for every
i
, and there is nothing to prove. So assume
M_{q}>0
.

Set

A:=\mathbb{E}_{i}|X_{i}|^{q}.

Then

M_{q}=\mathbb{E}A^{2}.

Fix a truncation level
L>0
, and write

Y_{i}:=\tau_{L}(X_{i}):=\operatorname{sgn}(X_{i})\min\{|X_{i}|,L\},\qquad R_{i}:=X_{i}-Y_{i}.

Thus
X_{i}=Y_{i}+R_{i}
, with
Y_{i}
the clipped part and
R_{i}
the tail.

Apply the variance pinning lemma to
Y_{i}
. Since
Y_{i}
is a measurable function of
X_{i}
, there exists
t\in\{0,\dots,m\}
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{i},Y_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|\,\Big]\leqslant(m+1)^{-1/2}\,\mathbb{E}_{i}\operatorname{Var}(Y_{i}).

Now

Y_{i}^{2}\leqslant L^{2-q}|X_{i}|^{q},

hence

\mathbb{E}_{i}\operatorname{Var}(Y_{i})\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{i}\mathbb{E}Y_{i}^{2}\leqslant L^{2-q}\,\mathbb{E}A\leqslant L^{2-q}(\mathbb{E}A^{2})^{1/2}=L^{2-q}M_{q}^{1/2}.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{i},Y_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|\,\Big]\leqslant(m+1)^{-1/2}L^{2-q}M_{q}^{1/2}.

Next define

U:=\mathbb{E}_{i}|R_{i}|,\qquad V:=\mathbb{E}_{i}|Y_{i}|.

Since

|R_{i}|=|X_{i}|\,\mathbf{1}_{\{|X_{i}|>L\}}\leqslant L^{1-q}|X_{i}|^{q},

we get

U\leqslant L^{1-q}A,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\mathbb{E}U^{2}\leqslant L^{2-2q}M_{q}.

Also,
|Y_{i}|\leqslant|X_{i}|
, so by the power-mean inequality,

V\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{i}|X_{i}|\leqslant\left(\mathbb{E}_{i}|X_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}=A^{1/q}.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}V^{2}\leqslant\mathbb{E}A^{2/q}\leqslant(\mathbb{E}A^{2})^{1/q}=M_{q}^{1/q},

since
2/q\leqslant 2
.

Now decompose the covariance:

\operatorname{Cov}(X_{i},X_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})=\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{i},Y_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})+\operatorname{Cov}(R_{i},Y_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})+\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{i},R_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})+\operatorname{Cov}(R_{i},R_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}).

For the mixed term,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(R_{i},Y_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\mathbb{E}\big[|R_{i}Y_{j}|\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]+\mathbb{E}_{i}\mathbb{E}\big[|R_{i}|\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]\,\mathbb{E}_{j}\mathbb{E}\big[|Y_{j}|\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[UV\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]+\mathbb{E}[U\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\,\mathbb{E}[V\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}].

Taking expectations and using Cauchy–Schwarz (together with Jensen for the second term),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(R_{i},Y_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|\,\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}[UV]+\mathbb{E}\!\big[\mathbb{E}(U\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\mathbb{E}(V\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2(\mathbb{E}U^{2})^{1/2}(\mathbb{E}V^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2L^{1-q}M_{q}^{(q+1)/(2q)}.

The same bound holds with
R_{i},Y_{j}
replaced by
Y_{i},R_{j}
.

For the tail-tail term,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(R_{i},R_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\mathbb{E}\big[|R_{i}R_{j}|\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]+\mathbb{E}_{i}\mathbb{E}\big[|R_{i}|\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]\,\mathbb{E}_{j}\mathbb{E}\big[|R_{j}|\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[U^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]+\mathbb{E}[U\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]^{2}.

Taking expectations and using Jensen,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(R_{i},R_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|\,\Big]\leqslant 2\mathbb{E}U^{2}\leqslant 2L^{2-2q}M_{q}.

Combining the last three displays,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(X_{i},X_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|\,\Big]\leqslant(m+1)^{-1/2}L^{2-q}M_{q}^{1/2}+4L^{1-q}M_{q}^{(q+1)/(2q)}+2L^{2-2q}M_{q}.

Choose

L:=M_{q}^{1/(2q)}(m+1)^{1/2}.

Then the first two terms become

(m+1)^{-(q-1)/2}M_{q}^{1/q}\quad\text{and}\quad 4(m+1)^{-(q-1)/2}M_{q}^{1/q},

while the last term becomes

2(m+1)^{-(q-1)}M_{q}^{1/q}\leqslant 2(m+1)^{-(q-1)/2}M_{q}^{1/q}.

Hence

\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\mathbb{E}_{i,j}\big|\operatorname{Cov}(X_{i},X_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})\big|\,\Big]\leqslant 7\,(m+1)^{-(q-1)/2}M_{q}^{1/q}.

This proves the proposition.
∎
