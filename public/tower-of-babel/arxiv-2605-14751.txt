Scattering and depletion in a flying focus from conformal transformations
Tim Adamo, Anton Ilderton, Adam Noble

Abstract
We show that flying focus fields can be obtained from complex conformal transformation of plane waves, and that solutions of the massless wave equation in the so-obtained fields are, correspondingly, conformal transformations of the Volkov solutions. This leads to the result that photon emission amplitudes in a totally depleting flying focus beam may be computed directly from the corresponding plane wave amplitudes by taking a simple Gaussian average over certain momentum variables. In effect, this gives a way of introducing focussing effects into strong-field QED calculations ‘for free’. The extension of these results to scattering amplitudes including only partial depletion is discussed and some first results presented in the anti-self-dual limit.



I
Introduction

Modern lasers produce light of such intensity that they access the quantum and relativistic regime of light-matter interactions
Abramowicz et al. 2021
;
Sarri et al. 2025
;
Los et al. 2026
. Furthermore, the effective laser-matter coupling exceeds unity, demanding a non-perturbative treatment. The appropriate theory framework for laser-particle scattering is then ‘strong-field quantum electrodynamics (QED)’, which employs background-field quantum field theory (QFT) methods to account for the strong laser coupling; for reviews see
Gonoskov et al. 2022
;
Fedotov et al. 2023
.

Performing analytic calculations in strong-field QED requires two main ingredients: solutions of Maxwell’s equations which model the strong background field, and explicit electron wavefunctions which are exact in that background. Of course, there is the additional practical requirement that these objects (the background and its associated wavefunctions) are amenable to calculation – that is, that scattering amplitudes and associated observables can actually be computed to reasonable levels of precision within the Furry expansion
Furry 1951
. This is one of the main bottlenecks to making analytic progress in strong-field QED, since there are few backgrounds which allow this.

For these reasons, plane waves provide one of the most ubiquitous examples of strong QED backgrounds, as the required charged particle wavefunctions (the Volkov solutions) are known for arbitrary plane waves as well as particle spin and charge
Wolkow 1935
;
Seipt 2017
. However, intense electromagnetic fields created in the laboratory, or nature, typically feature spatial inhomogeneity, one example being the focussing geometry of ultra-intense lasers. Plane waves, though, are completely un-focussed, and will therefore miss physics resulting from focussing effects, see e.g.
Fedotov 2009
;
Gies et al. 2013
;
Gonoskov et al. 2013
;
Heinzl and Ilderton 2017a
;
Karbstein et al. 2019
;
Di Piazza 2021
;
Degli Esposti and Torgrimsson 2024
;
Valialshchikov et al. 2025
. This has motivated interest in exact solutions of Maxwell’s equations which go beyond the plane wave approximation by including focussing behaviour. Prominent examples are
flying focus
fields, part of a wide class of solutions of Maxwell’s equations with a focal spot that moves with or against the beam, see
Brittingham 1983
;
Sezginer 1985
;
Hillion 1988
;
Hillion 1992
for some of the original investigations of such solutions,
Froula et al. 2018
;
Jolly et al. 2020
;
Kabacinski et al. 2023
;
Fu et al. 2025
for experiments, and
Di Piazza 2021
;
Formanek et al. 2022
;
Formanek et al. 2024
;
Adamo and Ilderton 2025
for recent applications to strong-field QED processes.

However, it is not possible to obtain exact solutions for the wavefunctions of charged fields in general flying focus backgrounds, meaning that most studies have utilized numerics or perturbative approximations of the background. Nevertheless, it was recently shown that for certain
complex-valued
flying focus solutions it is possible to obtain exact, analytic expressions for massless scalar/ultra-relativistic electron wavefunctions
Adamo and Ilderton 2025
. Furthermore, scattering amplitudes in these complex backgrounds have a
real
physical interpretation, encoding the total depletion of an initial flying focus field (viewed as an incoming coherent state) during a scattering process
Endlich and Penco 2017
;
Ilderton and Seipt 2018
;
Aoude and Ochirov 2023
. We will here tie these ideas together, showing first that a flying focus beam can be obtained by applying a complex conformal transformation to a real plane wave. That the resulting field is complex is not an issue at this level – one simply takes the real part to obtain the fields of a real flying focus.
Next, we will show that the same conformal transformation turns Volkov wavefunctions (exact solutions of the wave equation for charged particles in plane wave backgrounds
Wolkow 1935
) into the exact wavefunctions for charged particles in the flying focus background.

There are two caveats here – first, this result holds only for massless charges, due to the transformation being conformal. Second, the operation of taking real parts and solving the Klein-Gordon (or Dirac) equations do not commute, meaning that we can only find wavefunctions in the
complex
flying focus background. This restricts us to considering amplitudes for processes in which the flying focus beam, again viewed as an incoming coherent state of photons, is completely absorbed during scattering. Despite this, we will be able to find
all-multiplicity
results relating scattering in focussed fields to scattering in plane wave backgrounds.

While an interesting theory challenge, the case of complete depletion is not phenomenologically relevant for current experiments
Seipt et al. 2017
. This prompts us to ask how to account for only partial depletion, and we will provide some initial results in the latter part of this paper. Restricting now to
anti-self-dual
flying focus beams, we find a set of exact charged particle wavefunctions which allow for arbitrary levels of depletion (staying within the anti-self-dual sector) and discuss their properties. Although only a first step, these results offer an interesting look into the depth of complexities one might expect to appear in wavefunctions for realistic, and real, focussed backgrounds.

This paper is organised as follows. In Sec.
II
we provide a short review of relevant results on scattering amplitudes involving coherent states, the relation of such amplitudes to scattering on backgrounds (real and complex), and essential properties of the Volkov wavefuntions describing charged particles scattering on plane wave backgrounds. In Sec.
III
we show that a flying focus beam can be constructed by taking a complex special conformal transformation of a plane wave, and that the same transformation turns Volkov solutions into wavefunctions for charged particles in flying focus backgrounds. We proceed to the computation of amplitudes in Sec.
IV
, beginning with the simplest case of nonlinear Compton scattering (with complete absorption). This will set the scene for our main all-multiplicity result: knowing the plane wave
N
-photon emission amplitude gives – almost automatically – the corresponding amplitude in a flying focus background. In Sec.
V
we begin the investigation of partial depletion amplitudes, restricting here to the case of anti-self-dual backgrounds, where we are able to determine an exact set of wavefunctions for which we expect an extra degree of simplicity. We conclude in Sec.
VI
.

II
Review: Coherent states, backgrounds and plane waves

In this section, we briefly review some familiar structures of strong-field QED in plane wave backgrounds, and recall that strong-field scattering amplitudes in complex-valued electromagnetic fields encode real depletion/backreaction effects in scattering between coherent states.

II.1
Background fields and coherent states

Our focus throughout will be on massless scalar QED, viewed as an ultra-relativistic approximation for QED proper. In the presence of a fixed, classical background
A
, the Lagrangian governing the Furry expansion of this theory is
Furry 1951
:

\mathcal{L}=(D^{\mu}\phi)^{*}D_{\mu}\phi\,-i\,e\,\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\left(\phi^{*}\,D^{\mu}\phi-\phi(D^{\mu}\phi)^{*}\right)+e^{2}\,\mathcal{A}^{2}\,|\phi|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\,\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}\,\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}\,,

(2)

in which
\phi
is the complex, charged scalar,
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ieA_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative associated with the background, and
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
is the fluctuating photon field, with field strength
\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
.

We denote the S-matrix of this theory by
\mathcal{S}[A]
, highlighting the dependence on the background. Then scattering amplitudes in the Furry picture correspond to matrix elements
\left\langle\mathrm{out}|S[A]|\mathrm{in}\right\rangle
, where the initial state
|\mathrm{in}\rangle
and final state
|\mathrm{out}\rangle
are composed of scalars and free photons. Now, suppose the background is a vacuum solution of Maxwell’s equations which can be written in terms of on-shell photon modes as

A_{\mu}(x)=\int_{\ell}\,\left[\varepsilon_{\mu}^{(h)}(\ell)\,\alpha_{h}(\ell)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-i\,\ell\cdot x}+\varepsilon^{(-h)}_{\mu}(\ell)\,\alpha^{*}_{h}(\ell)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\,\ell\cdot x}\right]\,,

(3)

where
\varepsilon^{(h)}_{\mu}(\ell)
are helicity state polarization vectors with
h=\pm 1
(repeated helicity labels are implicitly summed over), obeying
\ell\cdot\varepsilon^{(h)}(\ell)=0
and
[\varepsilon^{(h)}_{\mu}(\ell)]^{*}=\varepsilon^{(-h)}_{\mu}(\ell)
; and
\alpha_{h}(\ell)
are (as we will see) occupation numbers for photons of helicity
h
and momentum
\ell
.

It is a long-known result that scattering amplitudes calculated in the presence of a vacuum solution (
3
) are closely and precisely related to scattering of
coherent states

Frantz 1965
;
Kibble 1965
, defined as follows.
Let
a^{\dagger}_{h}(\ell)
denote the creation operator for a photon of helicity
h
and momentum
\ell
, and
a_{h}(\ell)
denote the corresponding annihilation operator. Then a coherent state
|\,\alpha\,\rangle
is given by acting on the vacuum with the displacement operator
\mathbb{D}(\alpha)
, so
|\,\alpha\,\rangle=\mathbb{D}(\alpha)|\,0\,\rangle
where

\mathbb{D}(\alpha)=\exp\!\left[\int_{\ell}\left(a_{h}^{\dagger}(\ell)\,\alpha_{h}(\ell)-a_{h}(\ell)\,\alpha^{*}_{h}(\ell)\right)\right]\,,

(4)

and the
\alpha_{h}(\ell)
are, again, two freely chosen functions of momentum and helicity. The choice of notation here is deliberate, because in terms of the classical field (
3
), it is a remarkable fact that the displacement operator can be used to translate the vacuum S-matrix,
S
(i.e., with no background field) to the strong-field S-matrix
S[A]
via:

S[A]=\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\,S\,\mathbb{D}(\alpha)\,.

(5)

In terms of scattering amplitudes, this has the consequence that

\left\langle\mathrm{out}\,|\,S[A]\,|\,\mathrm{in}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathrm{out}\,|\,\mathbb{D}^{\dagger}(\alpha)\,S\,\mathbb{D}(\alpha)\,|\,\mathrm{in}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathrm{out\,;}\,\alpha\,|\,S\,|\mathrm{in\,;}\,\alpha\right\rangle\,.

(6)

In other words, the background field amplitude
\left\langle\mathrm{out}\,|\,S[A]\,|\,\mathrm{in}\right\rangle
is equal to the vacuum probability amplitude to evolve from an incoming state
|\mathrm{in}\rangle
to an outgoing state
|\mathrm{out}\rangle
in the presence of a given coherent state of photons,
|\alpha\rangle
.

II.2
Complex backgrounds and depletion amplitudes

One expects that scattering off an initial coherent state should result, in realistic settings, in a
different
final coherent state, due to e.g. depletion or backreaction effects. Scattering amplitudes for such processes are given by

\left\langle\mathrm{out\,;}\,\beta\,|\,S\,|\mathrm{in\,;}\,\alpha\right\rangle\,,

(7)

with
|\alpha\rangle
the incoming coherent state and
|\beta\rangle
the outgoing coherent state. (Independent of the physical interpretation, these are in any case sensible amplitudes to consider as, for example, coherent states provide an overcomplete basis of all states.) Using basic properties of the displacement operator (
4
) and restricting attention to incoming/outgoing number states which have zero kinematic overlap with the outgoing/incoming coherent states
1
1

1

See
Ilderton and Seipt 2018
for extensions.
, it follows that

\left\langle\mathrm{out\,;}\,\beta\,|\,S\,|\mathrm{in\,;}\,\alpha\right\rangle=\exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\ell}\left(|\alpha_{h}(\ell)|^{2}+|\beta_{h}(\ell)|^{2}-2\,\alpha_{h}(\ell)\,\beta_{h}^{*}(\ell)\right)\right]\left\langle\mathrm{out}|\,S[A]\,|\mathrm{in}\right\rangle\,,

(8)

where the classical potential
A_{\mu}
defining the background field S-matrix
S[A]
is now
complex
:

A_{\mu}(x)=\int_{\ell}\,\left[\varepsilon_{\mu}^{(h)}(\ell)\,\alpha_{h}(\ell)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-i\,\ell\cdot x}+\varepsilon^{(-h)}_{\mu}(\ell)\,\beta^{*}_{h}(\ell)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\,\ell\cdot x}\right]\,.

(9)

In other words, amplitudes with different coherent pieces in the initial and final states are equivalent (up to an overall exponential factor which is
not
a phase) to amplitudes in a complex-valued background (
9
), whose positive and negative frequency modes are given by the incoming and outgoing coherent state profiles, respectively. This observation provides a theoretical framework to compute depletion amplitudes (i.e. scattering between distinct asymptotic coherent states) in terms of background field amplitudes where the background is complex. For example, a
total depletion
process, whereby the incoming coherent state is totally absorbed during the scattering process, is modelled by a complex background (
9
) with
\alpha_{h}(\ell)\neq 0
,
\beta_{h}(\ell)=0
.

Note that despite the appearance of a complex background in the Furry picture, the initial transition amplitude between coherent states is defined with respect to the ordinary S-matrix of (scalar) QED, so unitarity is preserved.

II.3
Plane waves and Volkov wavefunctions

It is illustrative and useful to consider the example of plane wave backgrounds, and the Volkov solutions of the Klein-Gordon equation in them.
Let the Minkowski line element in lightfront coordinates
(x^{\scriptscriptstyle+},x^{\scriptscriptstyle-},x^{1},x^{2})
be

{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}=2\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}{\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}-|{\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}|^{2}\;,\quad\qquad x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}=\left(x^{1},\,x^{2}\right)\;,

(10)

with
|x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}|^{2}\equiv(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}
. Then plane wave Maxwell fields may be described by gauge potentials

A_{\text{pw}}(x)=-x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}\,{\dot{f}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}\;,\qquad\dot{f}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\equiv\frac{{\mathrm{d}}f(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})}{{\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\,,

(11)

where
\dot{f}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})
are two freely chosen functions of lightfront time
x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
. Minus signs and derivative are for convenience.
It is easy to see that (
11
) solves the vacuum Maxwell equations for any choice of
f_{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})
,
real or complex
, though physical considerations can be used to place some restrictions on their form. For instance, demanding that
\dot{f}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}
be compactly supported in
x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
gives rise to ‘sandwich’ plane waves which have well-defined asymptotic in- and out- regions for charged particle scattering, and for which the potential conveniently vanishes asymptotically
Schwinger 1951
.

Furthermore, it is a fact that
any
electromagnetic field in Minkowski spacetime is well-approximated by a plane wave in the neighbourhood of a null geodesic
Penrose 1976
, and the high degree of symmetry preserved by plane waves – a five-dimensional Heisenberg algebra whose centre is generated by translations in the
x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}
-direction
Bergmann and Flaherty 1978
;
Trautman 1980
;
Heinzl and Ilderton 2017b
;
Heinzl and Ilderton 2017a
;
Adamo et al. 2018
– means that wave equations for charged fields on plane wave backgrounds are
exactly
solvable. For the simple example of a massless charged scalar
\phi(x)
, viewed as an approximation to an ultra-relativistic electron, the appropriate equation is Klein-Gordon:

D^{2}_{\text{pw}}\phi\equiv\left(\partial+i\,e\,A_{\text{pw}}\right)^{2}\phi=0\;.

(12)

The solutions of this equation are the well-known Volkov wavefunctions
Wolkow 1935
given by, writing
f_{\mu}\equiv\delta_{\mu}^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}f_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}
,

\phi(x)={\mathrm{e}}^{-i\,S_{p}(x)}\,,\qquad S_{p}(x)=-ef(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\cdot x+p\cdot x+\frac{1}{2\,p_{+}}\int^{x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}}_{-\infty}\!\!{\mathrm{d}}s\left(2\,ef(s)\cdot p-e^{2}f^{2}(s)\right)\,,

(13)

where
p_{\mu}
is an on-shell 4-momentum obeying
p^{2}=0
. Similar solutions exist for massive fields with arbitrary spin
Seipt 2017
.

The exact Volkov solutions and the symmetry properties of the plane wave background mean that scattering amplitudes computed in the Furry expansion can be obtained fairly explicitly, even for generic wave profiles
f_{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})
, although for every vertex there is an integral over lightfront time
x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
which typically cannot be performed exactly. (See e.g.
Gonoskov et al. 2022
;
Fedotov et al. 2023
for reviews of various approximation schemes.) Notable exceptions are constant fields
Ritus 1985
, and the impulsive limit, for which closed-form, exact expressions for scattering probabilities have been obtained for non-linear Compton and pair creation at tree-level
Fedotov and Mironov 2013
;
Ilderton 2019
, as well as spin/helicity-flip at one loop
Ilderton and Kingham 2024
;
MacLeod 2026
.

III
Focussed fields & wavefunctions from conformal transformations

In this section, we show that the origin of the flying focus field and its associated wavefunctions can be understood as a complex-valued conformal transformation of familiar structures in plane wave backgrounds. Of course, the method of generating new solutions to massless wave and Maxwell equations via conformal transformations goes back to Bateman
Bateman 1909
, and the observation that flying focus fields can be generated from complex conformal transformations has also been made (using somewhat different terminology) previously
Hillion 1992
. However, the fact that these conformal transformations can also be used to generate exact solutions for massless wavefunctions on flying focus backgrounds corresponding to total depletion processes is new. (For applications of conformal transformations to radiation reaction see e.g.
Hobbs 1968
;
Noble et al. 2021
.)

III.1
Flying focus from a conformal transformation

Conformal transformations of Minkowski spacetime rescale the metric (
10
) by an overall positive factor,
{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}\to\Omega^{2}(x)\,{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}
, which preserves angles but not scales. These transformations form the group SO
(2,4)
, which is generated by Poincaré translations, Lorentz transformations, dilatations (or scale transformations) and the special conformal transformations, which act as

x^{\mu}\to\frac{x^{\mu}-b^{\mu}\,x^{2}}{1-2\,b\cdot x+b^{2}\,x^{2}}\,,

(14)

for some 4-vector
b^{\mu}
. When considering physical theories which are conformally invariant, conformal transformations can be used to generate interesting new solutions to equations of motion from known ones. Now, the vacuum Maxwell equations are famously conformally invariant
Cunningham 1910
;
Bateman 1910
, so the transformation (
14
) will send, for example, a plane wave Maxwell field to a
new
solution of the vacuum Maxwell equations, with spatial inhomogeneity.

Consider the specific case of a special conformal transformation (
14
) in which
b^{\mu}
is chosen to be a null vector such that
2b\cdot x=\kappa\,x^{+}
with
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. This special conformal transformation defines a new set of coordinates

x^{\mu}\rightarrow\sigma^{\mu}\,,\qquad\sigma^{{\scriptscriptstyle-}}=x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\,\frac{|x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}|^{2}}{1-\kappa\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\,,\quad\sigma^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}=\frac{x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}{1-\kappa x^{+}}\,,\quad\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+}=\frac{x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{1-\kappa\,x^{+}}\,.

(15)

Suppose we apply this special conformal transformation to a plane wave. Before giving any details, let us first consider some broad features of the new Maxwell field. The solution will have the attractive feature of introducing spatial inhomogeneity: whereas the initial plane wave was totally homogeneous in the transverse plane (being a function of lightfront time
x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
only), the new solution depends on the
x^{\perp}
-coordinates through the combination
\sigma^{-}
given in (
15
). Unfortunately, the resulting field will almost inevitably be singular, blowing up along the lightfront where
x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}=\kappa^{-1}
. This can be interpreted as a sort of focussing caustic induced by the conformal transformation.

This singular behaviour can be avoided if we allow ourselves to consider
complex
conformal transformations. Indeed, replacing
\kappa
in (
15
) with
-ik
for some real number
k\geq 0
leads to

x^{\mu}\rightarrow\sigma^{\mu}\,,\qquad\sigma^{{\scriptscriptstyle-}}=x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}-\frac{i\,k}{2}\,\frac{|x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}|^{2}}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\,,\quad\sigma^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}=\frac{x^{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}{1+i\,kx^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\,,\quad\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+}=\frac{x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\;.

(16)

The apparent downside of such a choice is that the conformal transformation of a real Maxwell field will inevitably become complex. However, we have already seen in Sec.
II.2
how to interpret such fields, and so we will now apply (
16
) to the plane wave (
11
) and explore the newly generated field. The special conformal transformation of (
11
) is

\begin{split}A(x)&=-\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}\,{\dot{f}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\,{\mathrm{d}}\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}\ \\
&=\frac{-x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}{\dot{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-})}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\bigg({\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}-\frac{i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}-\frac{k^{2}}{2}\frac{|x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}|^{2}}{(1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+})^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}\bigg)\;.\end{split}

(17)

This is easily confirmed to be a vacuum solution of Maxwell’s equations, but we need to do some work to clarify its physical behaviour. Let us consider first the functions
\dot{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-})
, which we write in terms of their Fourier modes as

{\dot{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega}{2\pi}(-i\omega)f_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(\omega)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-i\omega\big[x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}-\frac{ik}{2}\frac{|x^{\perp}|^{2}}{1+ikx^{+}}\big]}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega}{2\pi}(+i\omega)f_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(-\omega)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\omega\big[x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}-\frac{ik}{2}\frac{|x^{\perp}|^{2}}{1+ikx^{+}}\big]}\;.

(18)

We have deliberately separated the field into positive energy/incoming and negative energy/outgoing modes to highlight the following: the exponent is not a pure phase, there is a real part equal to

\mp\frac{\omega k}{2}\frac{|x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}|^{2}}{1+k^{2}\,(x^{\scriptscriptstyle+})^{2}}\;,

(19)

representing either an exponential damping or exponential increase. Thus, the Maxwell field (
17
) resulting from the conformal transformation (
16
) is only physically sensible (i.e., the Fourier integrals do not diverge due to exponentially increasing contributions) for purely positive-frequency, i.e. incoming, profiles
f_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}
.

We therefore restrict to this case. With this we have a sensibly defined, albeit still complex, background. From Sec.
II.2
we know, though, that this is equivalent to the presence of an
initial
coherent state, and therefore an initially present (real!) field in our scattering process. The associated field is just the real part of (
17
). To establish the corresponding coherent state profile, we first observe that the transformed gauge potential may be written

A=-{\mathrm{d}}\big(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}f_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\big)+\frac{f_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-})}{1+i\,k\,x^{+}}\bigg({\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}-\frac{i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}{1+i\,k\,x^{+}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}\bigg)\,,

in which
f_{h}:=(f_{1}-i\,h\,f_{2})/\sqrt{2}

III.2
Massless wavefunctions from a conformal transformation

The complex flying focus solutions above present an opportunity to analytically compute scattering amplitudes which include the effects of both depletion
and
spatial inhomogeneity. However, to do so requires solutions of the charged wave equations in this complex background. In generic flying focus Maxwell fields, there are no known exact solutions, however it is possible to write down
exact
wavefunctions in the flying focus (
17
), by again using the complex conformal transformation (
16
), as follows. We again consider a massless, complex charged scalar
\Phi
, viewed as an ultrarelativistic description of electrons/positrons. If we act with (
16
) then the Klein-Gordon equation on a plane wave background becomes, up to a conformal factor, the wave equation on a complex flying focus background, that is

D^{2}(x)=\Omega^{-3}D^{2}_{\text{pw}}(\sigma)\Omega\;,

(28)

in which
D_{\text{pw}}^{\mu}
is the plane-wave covariant derivative and the ‘conformal factor’
\Omega
is defined by
\Omega(x)=1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, equivalently
\Omega(\sigma)=1/(1-i\,k\,\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+})
.

It follows that if
\Phi
is a solution of the Klein-Gordon equation in a plane wave background, then after the conformal transformation (
16
)
\hat{\Phi}\equiv\Omega^{-1}\Phi
is a solution of the wave equation in the complex flying focus gauge field. (In other words the scalar has conformal weight -1.) Even more simply, the latter is solved by simply taking the conformal transformation of the Volkov solutions (
13
);

D^{2}(x)\,\Omega^{-1}\Phi(\sigma(x))=\Omega^{3}D^{2}_{\text{pw}}(\sigma)\Phi(\sigma)=0\;.

(29)

We can thus write down the solutions of the wave equation in the complex flying focus background (
17
) as

\begin{split}\hat{\Phi}_{p}(x)&=\Omega^{-1}(x)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-i\,S_{p}(\sigma(x))}\\
&=\frac{1}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\exp\bigg[ief(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\cdot\sigma-ip\cdot\sigma-\frac{i}{2\,p_{+}}\int^{\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\!\!\!{\mathrm{d}}s\left(2\,ef(s)\cdot p-e^{2}f^{2}(s)\right)\bigg]\bigg|_{\sigma\equiv\sigma(x)}\\
&=\frac{1}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\exp\bigg[i\frac{ef(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}(x))\cdot x}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}-i\frac{p_{\scriptscriptstyle+}x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}+p_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}-ip_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}(x)-\frac{i}{2\,p_{+}}\int^{\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}(x)}\!\!\!{\mathrm{d}}s\left(2\,ef(s)\cdot p-e^{2}f^{2}(s)\right)\bigg]\;,\end{split}

(30)

in which we have not written out
\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}(x)
explicitly for brevity.

These solutions preserve several features from the plane-wave case.
For example, despite clearly depending nontrivially on all spacetime coordinates, they are still semiclassical/WKB exact, in that the exponent is exactly of order
1/\hbar
. (The conformal prefactor essentially takes the place of an
\hbar^{0}
term.) The exponent continues to obey

(\partial S_{p}+e\,A)^{2}=0\,,

(31)

that is, it solves the charged Hamilton-Jacobi equations on the complex flying focus background.

Let us comment on boundary conditions. For pulsed plane wave fields we may always choose
f(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\to 0
as
x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}\to-\infty
, in which case (
13
) has incoming boundary conditions – it describes a free electron of momentum
p_{\mu}
in the asymptotic past. The wavefunction for an outgoing electron, momentum
q
in the future, is obtained by replacing
p\to q
, setting the integral limit to
+\infty
, and replacing
Kibble 1965
;
Dinu et al. 2012

f(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\to f_{\text{out}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}):=f(x^{\scriptscriptstyle-})-f_{\infty}\,,\qquad f_{\infty}:=\lim_{x^{-}\to\infty}f(x^{-})\;.

(32)

Finally, we take the conjugate, as this is how the wavefunction appears in
S
-matrix elements. Performing the conformal transformation one obtains the corresponding outgoing flying focus wavefunction (dropping the hat – from hereon all our wavefunctions are those in flying focus backgrounds)

\begin{split}{\bar{\Phi}}_{q}(x)&=\frac{1}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\exp\bigg[-ief_{\text{out}}(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\cdot\sigma+iq\cdot\sigma+\frac{i}{2\,q_{+}}\int^{\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}}_{\infty}\!\!\!{\mathrm{d}}s\left(2\,ef_{\text{out}}(s)\cdot q-e^{2}f_{\text{out}}^{2}(s)\right)\bigg]\bigg|_{\sigma\equiv\sigma(x)}\;.\end{split}

(33)

One might hope to obtain similar Volkov-like solutions for massive scalars and Dirac spinors. However, the equations of motion obeyed by these fields are not conformally invariant, due to the scale set by their masses. As such, it is not possible to generate exact solutions for their wavefunctions using the same conformal transformation. Nevertheless, we are now armed with the exact, ultrarelativistic wavefunctions (
30
) on an incoming flying focus field, and can thus turn to the computation of total depletion amplitudes in these backgrounds.

IV
Total depletion amplitudes in focussed fields

We have now seen that applying a conformal transformation to a plane wave electromagnetic field turns it into a flying focus, and that the same transformation turns Volkov solutions into solutions of the Klein-Gordon equation in the flying focus background. The next question to address is what happens if we apply the same transformation to scattering amplitudes in plane wave backgrounds. We will do this in Sec.
IV.2
. As a warm-up, we will first consider nonlinear Compton scattering, and calculate its amplitude following the method of
Adamo and Ilderton 2025

IV.1
Warm up: Nonlinear Compton scattering

We consider the tree-level scattering of an electron on the flying focus background. The electron scatters from momentum
p
to momentum
q
, and emits a photon of momentum
\ell
and polarisation
\varepsilon^{(h)}_{\mu}(\ell)
. This is nonlinear Compton (NLC) scattering with total depletion.

To calculate the amplitude, let
\Phi_{p}
and
{\bar{\Phi}}_{q}
be, respectively, incoming and outgoing wavefunctions (
30
) and (
33
) for electrons of momentum
p
and
q
. It is easily checked that the current

j_{\mu}(x)={\bar{\Phi}}_{q}D_{\mu}\Phi_{p}-\Phi_{p}{\bar{D}}_{\mu}{\bar{\Phi}}_{q}\;,

(34)

with
{\bar{D}}_{\mu}:=\partial_{\mu}-i\,eA_{\mu}
, is conserved as expected,
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}(x)=0
. In terms of this current, the
S
-matrix element for NLC scattering is

S_{fi}=e\,{\mathrm{e}}^{W[\alpha]}\int\!{\mathrm{d}}^{4}x\,\mathrm{e}^{i\ell\cdot x}\,\varepsilon_{\mu}^{(-h)}(\ell)\,j^{\mu}(x)\;,

(35)

which takes the same form as in a real background, aside from the factor
{\mathrm{e}}^{W[\alpha]}
, which is shorthand for the exponential in (
8
). We will perform the integrals over
x^{\mu}
in (
35

Adamo and Ilderton 2025
; an alternative method will also be described below. We first displace the
x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
contour from the real axis by the non-trivial part of
\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
,

x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}\to x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\frac{ik}{2}\frac{|x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}|^{2}}{1+ikx^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\;,

(36)

and integrate along a real slice of
x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
. This change of variable causes a shift in the exponent of the photon wavefunction, and as a result the
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}
–integrals become Gaussian. We rotate the
x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}

\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}=x^{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}/(1+ikx^{\scriptscriptstyle+})
. Performing the integrals generates the factor
\Delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(q+\ell+ef_{\infty}-p)
where

\Delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(p):=\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{2k\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\exp\bigg[-\frac{|p_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}|^{2}}{2k\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\bigg]

\lim_{k\to 0}\Delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(p)={\hat{\delta}}^{2}(p_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp})\;.

(38)

This should be contrasted with the plane wave calculation – the exponent appearing in the integrand of the amplitude is there linear in

The next step is to perform the

to
\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+}=x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}/(1+ikx^{\scriptscriptstyle+})

\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+}

q_{\scriptscriptstyle+}=q_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{\star}:=p_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\frac{|p_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}-q_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}|^{2}}{2\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\;.

(39)

Only the integral over
\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
remains, and cannot be evaluated in general, just as for the integral over
x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
in a general plane wave background. We can however simplify by dropping any term which is a total derivative with respect to
\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}

;
Dinu et al. 2012

\begin{split}\pi^{\mu}_{p}(s)&:=p^{\mu}-ef^{\mu}(s)+n^{\mu}\frac{2ef(s)\cdot p-e^{2}f(s)^{2}}{2p_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\;,\\
\pi^{\mu}_{q}(s)&:=q^{\mu}-ef_{\text{out}}^{\mu}(s)+n^{\mu}\frac{2ef_{\text{out}}(s)\cdot q-e^{2}f_{\text{out}}(s)^{2}}{2q_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\;,\end{split}

(40)

which are functionally identical to those appearing in plane wave backgrounds. They inherit the boundary conditions of the corresponding in and out modes, that is
\pi_{p}(-\infty)=p
and
\pi_{q}(+\infty)=q
. We will write, for the transverse components of these momenta,
\sfpi(s)\equiv(\pi_{1}(s)+i\pi_{2}(s))/\sqrt{2}
; these are just the projections onto the transverse photon polarisation vectors, see (
22

Finally then, the amplitude is

\begin{split}S_{fi}=2ie\,\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\,&{\hat{\delta}}(q_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-q_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{\star})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{W[\alpha]}\,{\Delta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}(\ell+q+ef_{\infty}-p)\\
&\int\!{\mathrm{d}}\sigma\,\bigg[\delta_{h}^{+1}\frac{q_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sfpi_{p}(\sigma)-p_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sfpi_{q}(\sigma)}{(\sfpi_{q}(\sigma)-\sfpi_{p}(\sigma))^{2}}+\delta_{h}^{-1}\frac{q_{\scriptscriptstyle+}{\bar{\sfpi}_{p}(\sigma)}-p_{\scriptscriptstyle+}{\bar{\sfpi}}_{q}(\sigma)}{({\bar{\sfpi}}_{q}(\sigma)-{\bar{\sfpi}}_{p}(\sigma))^{2}}\bigg]\exp\bigg(\frac{i}{2}\int^{\sigma}\!\!\!{\mathrm{d}}s\,\frac{(\pi_{q}(s)-\pi_{p}(s))^{2}}{q_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-p_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\bigg)\;.\end{split}

(41)

In the second line, we have written the pre-exponential factor as the sum of two terms, one for each possible emitted photon helicity. Everything in the second line can be mapped directly to what would be obtained in a plane wave background – it may not look familiar when comparing to the literature because in the plane wave case one usually eliminates
q_{\mu}
in favour of
\ell_{\mu}
, whereas here we have lost conservation of transverse momentum. Recall that this is encoded in the Gaussian factor
\triangle_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}
, which also contains the usual ‘memory’

;
Bieri and Garfinkle 2013

f_{\infty}

\triangle_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}
may equivalently be written
(\ell+\sfpi_{q}-\sfpi_{p})_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}

(\ell+q-p)_{\scriptscriptstyle+}=0
.

IV.2
All-multiplicity results

It should be clear that the contour deformations in the previous calculation are effectively
undoing
the conformal transformation, bringing the amplitude into a form which is closely related to that in a plane-wave background but not, crucially, equal to it – there are focussing effects. In this section we therefore use the conformal transformation to reduce amplitudes for an
arbitrary
number of photon emissions to what are essentially plane wave calculations. We first present the amplitude, then consider how its constituent parts behave under conformal transformation, before combining those parts and obtaining the result.

Amplitude:

We again consider the tree-level scattering of an electron, momentum
p
to momentum
q
, on the flying focus background with total depletion, but now with the emission of
N
photons, momenta
\ell_{j}
and polarisations
\varepsilon_{j}\equiv\varepsilon^{(h_{j})}(\ell_{j})
. The amplitude is constructed from the sum over all possible permutations of the three-point and four-point vertices. A general term in this sum has the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{N}=(-ie)^{N}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{W[\alpha]}\,\int\!{\mathrm{d}}^{4}x_{1}&\cdots{\mathrm{d}}^{4}x_{N}\,{\bar{\Phi}}_{q}(x_{n})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\ell_{n}\cdot x_{n}}\cdots{\mathrm{e}}^{i\ell_{1}\cdot x_{1}}\\
&\cdots G(x_{j+1},x_{j})\varepsilon_{j}\cdot\overset{\leftrightarrow}{D}(x_{j})G(x_{j},x_{j-1})\cdots G(x_{r+1},x_{r})\varepsilon_{r}\cdot\varepsilon_{r-1}G(x_{r},x_{r-1})\cdots\Phi_{p}(x_{1})\;,\end{split}

(42)

in which we have chosen some set of labels for the photons,
G(x,y)
is the scalar propagator in the flying focus background, and each three and four-point vertex may be repeated multiple times. (These vertices may also appear next to the incoming and outgoing electron wavefunctions.) We take the amplitude and perform the complex conformal transformation
x\to\sigma
as defined in (
16
). Recalling the definition of the conformal weight
\Omega=1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}=1/(1-i\,k\,\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+})
, the integration measures transform as

{\mathrm{d}}^{4}x_{i}={\mathrm{d}}^{4}\sigma_{i}\ \Omega^{4}(\sigma_{i})\;,

(43)

and we can now consider the transformation of the various other components in (
42
).

Wavefunctions and propagator:

As established in Sec.
III.2
, the scalar wavefunction obeys

\Phi(x)=\Omega^{-1}\phi(\sigma)

(44)

in which
\phi
is the Volkov solution in a plane wave background. The new ingredient we must consider in (
42
) is the (scalar) electron propagator in the flying focus background. A massless Volkov solution can easily be extended off-shell by replacing the massless 4-momentum
p_{\mu}
with a massive 4-momentum (i.e.,
p^{2}\not=0
); a propagator is then easily constructed by stitching together these off-shell solutions. However, this strategy does not immediately extend to the flying focus background: the mass scale introduced in this way breaks conformal invariance and the resulting wavefunctions are not off-shell in a controlled way. Nevertheless, the conformal transformation linking plane wave and flying focus backgrounds can still be exploited to arrive at a propagator as follows.

We know that the propagator obeys, for an arbitrary function
J(x)
,

D^{2}(x)\int\!{\mathrm{d}}^{4}x^{\prime}\,G(x,x^{\prime})J(x^{\prime})=J(x)\;,

(45)

and that under conformal transformation the covariant derivative obeys (
28
), which we repeat here:

D^{2}(x)=\Omega^{-3}D^{2}_{\text{pw}}(\sigma)\Omega\;.

(46)

Using (
46
) in (
45
), and also performing the conformal change of variable in the integral,
x^{\prime}\to\sigma^{\prime}
, we quickly land on

D^{2}_{\text{pw}}(\sigma)\int\!{\mathrm{d}}^{4}\sigma^{\prime}\bigg[\frac{G(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})}{(1-ik\sigma^{{\scriptscriptstyle+}})(1-ik\sigma^{\prime{\scriptscriptstyle+}})}\Bigg]j(\sigma^{\prime})=j(\sigma)\;,

(47)

in which we have written
j(\sigma)\equiv J(\sigma)/(1-i\,k\,\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+})^{3}
, which is still arbitrary. It follows that the object in square brackets above
is
the Green function in a (totally depleting) plane wave, call it
G_{\text{pw}}
, which is, of course, known exactly. Hence, as long as the propagator
G
appears under at least one integral, which it always does in the amplitude (
42
), then we may write it in terms of the plane wave propagator as

G(x_{i},x_{j})=(1-ik\sigma_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle+})G_{\text{pw}}(\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j})(1-ik\sigma_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle+})=\Omega^{-1}(\sigma_{i})G_{\text{pw}}(\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j})\Omega^{-1}(\sigma_{j})\;.

(48)

As a consistency check, observe that the propagator transforms as a product of wavefunctions, as expected.

Vertices:

We begin with the three-point vertex. Spatial derivatives in the vertex always appear coupled to an external photon polarisation; changing variables we have, for an arbitrary scalar function
F(x)
,

\varepsilon\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{1}{1+i\,k\,x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}}F(x)=\varepsilon^{\nu}\frac{\partial\sigma^{\mu}}{\partial x^{\nu}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma^{\mu}}(1-i\,k\,\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+})F(\sigma)\;.

(49)

Now, since we use (anti)-lightfront gauge
\varepsilon^{\scriptscriptstyle+}=0
, see (
22
), there is no
\partial/\partial\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+}
term in the above, hence the final
\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+}
–dependent factor passes through the derivative. From here it is easily checked that, for the full covariant derivative,

\varepsilon\cdot D(x)\,\Omega^{-1}(x)F(x)=\Omega^{-3}(\sigma)\mathcal{E}(\ell;\sigma)\cdot D_{\text{pw}}(\sigma)F(\sigma)\,,

(50)

in which we have
defined

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(\ell;\sigma)&:=\Omega^{2}(\sigma)\varepsilon^{\nu}(\ell)\frac{\partial\sigma^{\mu}}{\partial x^{\nu}}\\
&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,(1-i\,k\,\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+})}\,\bigg(\frac{\ell_{1}+i\,h\,\ell_{2}+i\,k\,\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle-}(\sigma^{1}+i\,h\,\sigma^{2})}{\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\,(1-i\,k\,\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle+})},\,0,\,1,\,i\,h\bigg)\;.\end{split}

(51)

The reason for this definition will become clear in a moment (but compare (
22
)). As for the contribution from four-point vertices, observe that, in terms of (
51
), we may write

\varepsilon_{i}\cdot\varepsilon_{j}=\Omega^{-1}(\sigma)\,\mathcal{E}(\ell_{i};\sigma)\cdot\mathcal{E}(\ell_{j};\sigma)\,\Omega^{-1}(\sigma)\;,

(52)

in which
\sigma
is the appropriate vertex position (labelled
\sigma_{r}
in the example contribution (
42
)). We will use this result immediately below.

Assembly:

Putting everything together, our change of variables turns wavefunctions, propagators and covariant derivatives into their plane wave counterparts. Each wavefunction is accompanied by a factor of
\Omega^{-1}
, each propagator by two such factors (one for each argument).
At each four-point vertex (
52
), we generate two further such factors, for a total of four. These are cancelled exactly by the factor of
\Omega^{4}
in the measure (
43
). At each three-point vertex we obtain three powers of
\Omega^{-1}
from the covariant derivative acting on a propagator/wavefunction, see (
50
), and one power of
\Omega^{-1}
from the propagator/wavefunction which multiplies this. This is again cancelled by the measure at this vertex. Hence all factors of
\Omega
cancel from the amplitude and we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{N}

\displaystyle=(-ie)^{N}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{W[\alpha]}\,\int\!{\mathrm{d}}^{4}\sigma_{1}\cdots{\mathrm{d}}^{4}\sigma_{N}\,{\bar{\phi}}_{q}(\sigma_{n})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\ell_{n}\cdot x(\sigma_{n})}\cdots{\mathrm{e}}^{i\ell_{1}\cdot x(\sigma_{1})}

(53)

\displaystyle\cdots G_{\text{pw}}(\sigma_{j+1},\sigma_{j})\mathcal{E}(\ell_{j};\sigma_{j})\cdot{\overset{\leftrightarrow}{D}}_{\text{pw}}(\sigma_{j})G_{\text{pw}}(\sigma_{j},\sigma_{j-1})\cdots G_{\text{pw}}(\sigma_{r+1},\sigma_{r})\mathcal{E}(\ell_{r};\sigma_{r})\cdot\mathcal{E}(\ell_{r-1};\sigma_{r})G_{\text{pw}}(\sigma_{r},\sigma_{r-1})\cdots\phi_{p}(\sigma_{1})\;,

in which we note the transformed exponents in the emitted photon wavefunctions, and the new functions
\mathcal{E}
replacing the usual photon polarisations.

We have thus reduced the
N

The only difference compared to the plane wave amplitude is that the usual free wavefunctions for emitted photons
\varepsilon^{\mu}(\ell)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\ell\cdot x}
are replaced by new functions
\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(\ell;\sigma)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\ell\cdot x(\sigma)}
– all the complexity of beam focussing has been shifted into these functions. This is interesting in itself, but there is an even simpler representation of this result which also lends itself to explicit evaluation of amplitudes, as we shall now see.

IV.3
Focussing for free

It is straightforward to verify that the new functions appearing in our amplitude obey

\partial\cdot\big(\mathcal{E}(\sigma)e^{i\ell\cdot x(\sigma)}\big)=0\;,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial^{2}\big(\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(\sigma)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\ell\cdot x(\sigma)}\big)=0\;,

(54)

and so are solutions of the vacuum Maxwell equations. In other words, the new functions
are
wavepackets of free photons – performing the conformal transformation on the flying focus amplitude has in fact converted it into the equivalent plane wave amplitude, but with the usual (plane wave) asymptotic photon states replaced by photon wavepacket states. We have genuinely reduced the flying focus calculation to one in a plane wave background.

It will at this stage be unsurprising to learn that the wavepacket in question is nothing but the same Gaussian appearing in the definition of the flying focus beam itself:

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(\ell;\sigma)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\ell\cdot x(\sigma)}&=\int_{q}2q_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\,\hat{\delta}(q_{\scriptscriptstyle-}-\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle-})\bigg[\frac{2\pi}{k\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-|q_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}-\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}|^{2}/(2k\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle-})}\bigg]\,\varepsilon^{\mu}(q)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{iq_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu}}\\
&=:\langle\!\langle\,\varepsilon^{\mu}(\ell)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\ell\cdot\sigma}\,\rangle\!\rangle\,,\end{split}

(55)

which defines the Gaussian average
\langle\!\langle\cdot\rangle\!\rangle
. We therefore have the remarkable final result that beam focussing effects can be added to total depletion plane wave tree-level amplitudes simply by taking a Gaussian average over the emitted photon momenta:

\mathcal{A}_{N}(p\to q+\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{N})={\mathrm{e}}^{W[\alpha]}\,\big\langle\!\big\langle\,\mathcal{A}^{\text{pw}}_{N}(p\to q+\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{N})\,\big\rangle\!\big\rangle\;,

(56)

in which there is an independent averaging for each photon. We stress that this result holds at arbitrary multiplicity.

V.1
Anti-self-dual fields

An electromagnetic field
F_{\mu\nu}
is said to be
ASD
if it obeys

{\tilde{F}}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,F^{\rho\sigma}=-i\,F_{\mu\nu}\,.

(57)

Anti-self-duality is a powerful feature: ASD field strengths automatically solve the vacuum Maxwell equations (by virtue of the Bianchi identity) and the ASD condition gives a set of first-order partial differential equations for the gauge potential which are classically integrable
Ward 1977
;
Belavin and Zakharov 1978
.

Thanks to the factor of
i
appearing in (
57
), all ASD fields are inherently complex-valued. Following the logic of Sec.
II.2
, these will still represent real scattering processes when the ASD fields encode incoming and outgoing coherent states of photons. These correspond to gauge potentials of the form

A_{\mu}(x)=\int_{\ell}\,\varepsilon_{\mu}^{(+)}\left[\alpha_{+}(\ell)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-i\,\ell\cdot x}+\beta^{*}_{-}(\ell)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\,\ell\cdot x}\right]\;.

(58)

In other words, scattering in the ASD background (
58
) encodes transitions from an incoming coherent state of positive helicity photons
\alpha_{+}
to an outgoing coherent state of negative helicity photons
\beta_{-}
.

When dealing with ASD plane waves and flying focus solutions, it is convenient to work in coordinates where the transverse
x^{\perp}
–plane is identified with the complex plane. If
(z,\bar{z})
are complex conjugate coordinates on the complex plane, then the Minkowski metric becomes

z=\frac{x^{1}+i\,x^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\quad\bar{z}=\frac{x^{1}-i\,x^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\qquad{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}=2\left({\mathrm{d}}x^{\scriptscriptstyle-}\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{+}-|{\mathrm{d}}z|^{2}\right)\,.

(59)

In these coordinates, the profile functions
f_{\perp}(x^{-})
can be combined into complex functions

{\mathsf{f}}\equiv\frac{f_{1}-i\,f_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\qquad\bar{\mathsf{f}}\equiv\frac{f_{1}+i\,f_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(60)

so that the plane wave gauge potential (
11
) becomes

A_{\mathrm{pw}}(x)=-\left[z\,\dot{{\mathsf{f}}}(x^{-})+\bar{z}\,\dot{\bar{\mathsf{f}}}(x^{-})\right]{\mathrm{d}}x^{{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\,.

(61)

For complex-valued fields,
{\mathsf{f}}
and
\bar{\mathsf{f}}
are
not
related by complex conjugation, but are instead independent functions – an ASD plane wave corresponds to
\bar{\mathsf{f}}=0
(or
f_{1}=-i\,f_{2}
). In this case, the gauge field has a single component in either of the gauges used in Sec.
III.1
, and we will work with:

A_{\mathrm{asd}}={\mathsf{f}}(x^{-})\,{\mathrm{d}}z\quad\implies\quad F_{\mathrm{asd}}=\dot{{\mathsf{f}}}(x^{-})\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\wedge{\mathrm{d}}z\,,

(62)

which is easily seen to obey (
57
). In the correspondence with coherent state scattering, the positive (negative) frequency modes of the profile function
{\mathsf{f}}
correspond to the incoming (outgoing) coherent state
\alpha_{+}
(
\beta_{-}
).

Just as we obtained totally-depleting flying focus solutions from a (complex) conformal transformation of a totally depleting plane wave, one imagines that something similar occurs in the ASD but partially depleting case. However, this expectation is too naïve: after performing the conformal transformation (
16
), the resulting gauge potential will be proportional to
{\mathsf{f}}(\sigma^{-})
. Since
{\mathsf{f}}
has both positive and negative frequency Fourier modes, the latter will produce exponential growth in the transverse
(z,\bar{z})
-plane, rather than the desired exponential decay, as discussed in Sec.
III.1
.

One resolution to this problem is to use two distinct conformal transformations: one on the incoming modes of
{\mathsf{f}}
and another on the outgoing modes. Specifically, let

\begin{split}{\mathsf{f}}(x^{-})&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega}{2\pi}\,{\mathsf{f}}_{+}(\omega)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-i\,\omega\,x^{-}}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\omega}{2\pi}\,{\mathsf{f}}_{-}(-\omega)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{i\,\omega\,x^{-}}\\
&={\mathsf{f}}_{+}(x^{-})+{\mathsf{f}}_{-}(x^{-})\,,\end{split}

(63)

be the decomposition of
{\mathsf{f}}
into its incoming/positive frequency (
{\mathsf{f}}_{+}
) and outgoing/negative frequency (
{\mathsf{f}}_{-}
) parts. Then we act with the conformal transformation (
16
) on the positive frequency part of the plane wave, while acting on the negative frequency part with

x^{\mu}\to\tilde{\sigma}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\tilde{\sigma}^{-}=x^{-}+i\,\tilde{k}\,\frac{|z|^{2}}{1-i\,\tilde{k}\,x^{+}}\,,\quad\tilde{\sigma}=\frac{z}{1-i\,\tilde{k}\,x^{+}}\,,\quad\tilde{\sigma}^{+}=\frac{x^{+}}{1-i\,\tilde{k}\,x^{+}}\,,

(64)

where
\tilde{k}\geq 0
. The resulting gauge potential is

A=\frac{{\mathsf{f}}_{+}(\sigma^{-})}{1+i\,k\,x^{+}}\left({\mathrm{d}}z-\frac{i\,k\,z}{1+i\,k\,x^{+}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{+}\right)+\frac{{\mathsf{f}}_{-}(\tilde{\sigma}^{-})}{1-i\,\tilde{k}\,x^{+}}\left({\mathrm{d}}z+\frac{i\,\tilde{k}\,z}{1-i\,\tilde{k}\,x^{+}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\right)\;,

(65)

whose incoming Fourier modes decay as before, while the outgoing Fourier modes now also decay exponentially due to the sign change in (
64
). Thus (
65
) is a partially depleting flying focus field, where the focussing of the incoming field is controlled by
k
, while the focussing of the outgoing field is controlled by
\tilde{k}
. It is easy to check that the corresponding field strength is ASD, as expected since the ASD condition (
57
) is conformally invariant.

V.2
Alternative wavefunctions for anti-self-dual total depletion

To build a toolkit for computing amplitudes on the partial depleting ASD flying focus background (
65
), we need explicit wavefunctions for charged particles on the background. Recall that in the totally depleting case, we obtained wavefunctions in the flying focus background by applying the conformal transformation (
16
) to the Volkov wavefunctions of the plane wave background. In the partially depleting case, we have
two
independent conformal transformations – one for the incoming and one for the outgoing field modes – and it is not clear which one to apply to the Volkov solutions.

To resolve this problem, we first return to the setting of total depletion: the incoming ASD flying focus background is given by

A=\frac{{\mathsf{f}}_{+}(\sigma^{-})}{1+i\,k\,x^{+}}\left({\mathrm{d}}z-\frac{i\,k\,z}{1+i\,k\,x^{+}}\,{\mathrm{d}}x^{+}\right)\,,

(66)

with
{\mathsf{f}}_{+}
having only positive frequency Fourier modes. In this case, we can simply adapt our previous solutions for massless charged scalar wavefunctions to the special case of an ASD background:

\Phi_{p}(x)=\frac{1}{1+i\,k\,x^{+}}\,\exp\!\left[-i\,p\cdot\sigma-\frac{i\,e\,\mathsf{p}}{p_{+}}\,\int^{\sigma^{-}}\!\!{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathsf{f}}_{+}(s)\right]\,,

(67)

where, here and below,
\mathsf{p}:=(p_{1}+i\,p_{2})/\sqrt{2}
.

As we have seen, these wavefunctions are well-adapted to performing explicit computations of amplitudes in totally-depleting flying focus fields. Furthermore, when
k\to 0
they reduce to the Volkov solutions (
13
) in an ASD (totally depleting) plane wave. However, their behaviour in the free limit is somewhat non-standard: considering the
e\to 0
limit where the scalar decouples from the background, the wavefunction (
67
) goes to

\begin{split}\lim_{e\to 0}\Phi_{p}(x)&=\frac{1}{1+i\,k\,x^{+}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-i\,p\cdot\sigma}\\
&=\frac{2\pi}{k\,p_{-}}\,\int\hat{{\mathrm{d}}}^{2}q_{\perp}\,\exp\!\left[-\frac{|q_{\perp}-p_{\perp}|^{2}}{2k\,p_{-}}-i\,q_{\perp}\,x^{\perp}-i\,\frac{|q_{\perp}|^{2}}{2\,p_{-}}\,x^{+}-i\,p_{-}\,x^{-}\right]\,.\end{split}

(68)

In particular, we see that
\Phi_{p}
goes to a Gaussian wavepacket of free plane waves as
e\to 0
.

While there is nothing wrong with this (such Gaussian wavepackets are simply an over-complete basis of solutions in the free limit), one might have hoped that there exist wavefunctions which reduce to a (single) free plane wave in the
e\to 0
limit. Remarkably, such solutions can be obtained by simply solving the Klein-Gordon equation order-by-order in
e
starting from
{\mathrm{e}}^{-ip\cdot x}
. The resulting power series can be re-summed to give the alternative wavefunctions

\psi_{p}(x)=\exp\!\left[-i\,p\cdot x-\frac{i\,e\,\mathsf{p}}{p_{+}\,(1+i\,k\,x^{+})+i\,k\,\mathsf{p}\,\bar{z}}\,\int_{\tau^{k}_{p}(x)}^{\sigma^{-}}\!\!{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathsf{f}}_{+}(s)\right]\,,

(69)

where the lower limit of integration appearing in the exponential is

\tau^{k}_{p}(x):=\frac{p\cdot x}{p_{-}}-i\,\frac{p_{+}}{k\,p_{-}}\,.

(70)

Clearly, as
e\to 0
,
\psi_{p}(x)\to{\mathrm{e}}^{-ip\cdot x}
, as desired. The appearance of the quantity
\tau^{k}_{p}(x)
as a lower limit of integration may seem surprising, but it plays two important roles. First, it ensures that
\psi_{p}(x)
is non-singular – when the denominator in (
69
) vanishes, it can be checked that
\tau^{k}_{p}\to\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
, pinching the integral to zero such that the exponent remains finite. Furthermore, the imaginary part of
\tau_{p}(x)
provides a Gaussian damping factor in the
k\to 0
limit, ensuring that we recover the Volkov solution.

V.3
Wavefunctions for partial depletion

The alternative, perturbative basis of wavefunctions
\psi_{p}
can now be generalised to provide exact wavefunctions in the ASD flying focus background (
65
) with incoming
and
outgoing parts. One simply takes

\psi_{p}(x)=\exp\!\left[-i\,p\cdot x-\frac{i\,e\,\mathsf{p}}{p_{+}\,(1+i\,k\,x^{+})+i\,k\,\mathsf{p}\,\bar{z}}\,\int_{\tau^{k}_{p}(x)}^{\sigma^{-}}\!\!{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathsf{f}}_{+}(s)-\frac{i\,e\,\mathsf{p}}{p_{+}\,(1-i\,\tilde{k}\,x^{+})-i\,\tilde{k}\,\mathsf{p}\,\bar{z}}\,\int^{\sigma^{-}}_{\tau^{-\tilde{k}}_{p}(x)}\!\!{\mathrm{d}}s\,{\mathsf{f}}_{-}(s)\right]\,,

(71)

which is essentially the product of solutions in each of the positive and negative frequency fields. It can be verified by direct calculation that this solves the massless Klein-Gordon equation in the ASD flying focus background. Furthermore, these solutions reduce to free plane waves as
e\to 0
, are non-singular, and produce Volkov solutions coupling to the positive/negative frequency modes of the background when focussing is turned off (
k/\tilde{k}\to 0
).

In principle, these wavefunctions provide us with the tools needed to compute processes like non-linear Compton scattering in a partially-depleted, focussed background. Unfortunately, so far we have been unable to find a way to analytically evaluate the integrals which arise in amplitude calculations using the
\psi_{p}
wavefunctions. In essence, our ability to perform calculations in the totally depleting case followed from using the wavefunctions
\Phi_{p}
generated by a conformal transformation: the contour prescriptions or Gaussian averaging used to evaluate the resulting integrals essentially followed from this conformal transformation acting on the usual integrals in plane wave backgrounds. Of course, it may still prove to be possible to perform analytic calculations with the wavefunctions
\psi_{p}
. In any case, the integrals arising in amplitudes evaluated on these wavefunctions should be amenable to numerical integration.

We note, finally, an interesting consequence of the fact that our partially-depleting field model contains both incoming and outgoing parts – the field is no longer null:

F_{\mu\nu}\,F^{\mu\nu}=i\,F_{\mu\nu}{\tilde{F}}^{\mu\nu}=-4\frac{z^{2}\,{\dot{\mathsf{f}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle+}(\sigma^{\scriptscriptstyle-})\,{\dot{\mathsf{f}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle-}({\tilde{\sigma}}^{\scriptscriptstyle-})}{(1+i\,k\,{x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}})^{3}(1-i\,{\tilde{k}}\,{x^{\scriptscriptstyle+}})^{3}}\,(k+{\tilde{k}})^{2}\;.

(72)

In a real field, non-zero invariants can signal e.g. the possibility of spontaneous particle production from the vacuum. The interpretation here, though, is not so clear. Working perturbatively, one can easily check that the leading contribution to the
0\to 2
process, which goes like
e^{2}\alpha^{2}
for any initial coherent state with profile
\alpha
, vanishes because both incoming photons have the same helicity. Similarly, the order
e^{3}\alpha^{2}\beta
contribution vanishes. These are of course examples of ‘all plus’ (helicity) amplitudes which vanish at all multiplicity
Gastmans and Wu 1990
, suggesting that the perturbative contribution to the
0\to 2
amplitude in an ASD flying focus is zero. It would be very interesting to investigate particle creation processes in more detail, potentially using the wavefunctions (
71
).

VI
Conclusions

We have investigated strong-field QED scattering processes in a class of highly inhomogeneous backgrounds which includes flying focus beams. We have shown that these fields can be obtained from conformal transformations of plane waves, and that the corresponding wavefunctions describing massless scalars scattering on the backgrounds are given by applying the same transformation to the Volkov wavefunctions.

While the required conformal transformation, and resulting electromagnetic fields, were complex, the well-known equivalence between backgrounds and coherent states meant that we were still able to calculate sensible amplitudes, namely those in which the flying focus beam was completely absorbed during some scattering process. We considered the amplitudes for
N
-photon emission from an electron scattering on the background and, by exploiting the conformal transformation, showed that this was given by a simple Gaussian average over the emitted photon momenta in the corresponding plane wave amplitude. This is an elegant result which shows how much-sought-after focussing effects can be included into plane-wave calculations with little additional effort, and furthermore adds to the library of results which show how to use plane wave amplitudes to construct amplitudes in other backgrounds
Adamo et al. 2021
;
Copinger et al. 2024
;
Ilderton and Kingham 2024
;
Copinger et al. 2026
.

Finally, we considered the extension of our results to partial depletion amplitudes.
In this case it is unclear how to construct the corresponding massless charge wavefunctions via a conformal transformation. To make progress, we made the simplifying assumption that the flying focus background was anti-self-dual; physically, this means that all incoming coherent state photons are either absorbed or, if not, flip helicity.
In this setting, we were able to find a set of charge wavefunctions with an intriguing structure, seemingly quite different to that encountered earlier in the paper. The continued investigation of these new solutions is a natural target for future work, including their use in calculating amplitudes and, if possible, extending the solutions beyond the anti-self-dual sector.

It would also be interesting to see whether the connection between amplitudes in plane wave and flying focus backgrounds, summarised by the averaging result (
56
), extends to loop corrections, or can be generalised to scattering in Yang-Mills and gravitational backgrounds. We could also seek other, non-conformal, transformations which allow us to ‘lift’ plane wave results to other background fields of interest, as we have done here for the flying focus.
