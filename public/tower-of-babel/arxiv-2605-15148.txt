Noether symmetries and conservation laws of a class of time-dependent multidimensional nonlinear wave equations
F. Güngör, C. Özemir

Abstract
Conservation laws of a class of time-dependent damped nonlinear multidimensional wave equations are derived by Noether’s theorem. For arbitrary nonzero damping coefficient and nonlinear interaction term, its infinitesimal variational symmetries span a Euclidean algebra e ⁡ ( n ) \euclid(n) of space translations and rotations. They produce conservation of linear and angular momentums. For some specific forms of these two terms symmetry algebra is enlarged to a subalgebra of the conformal algebra c ⁡ ( 1 , n ) \conf(1,n) and in this case more interesting conservation laws are found.



Keywords: Time-dependent damped nonlinear wave equations, Lie point symmetry, Variational symmetry, Noether’s theorem, First Integral, Conservation law

1
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to study symmetries and conservation laws of the nonlinear wave equations

\mathcal{E}=\Box u+a(t)u_{t}+f(u)=0,\quad f^{\prime\prime}\neq 0

(1.1)

where
\Box=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta
is the
(n+1)
-dimensional wave operator,
u(t,x)
is the wave function with
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
n\geq 2
and
f(u)
is an arbitrary function.

Equation (
1.1
) is the Euler-Lagrange equation
E_{u}(L)=\mathcal{E}
with the Lagrangian

L=\mu(t)L_{0},\quad L_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\big(u_{t}^{2}+|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}\big)-F(u),\quad F(u)=\int f(u)du

(1.2)

where
\mu>0
satisfies
\dot{\mu}=a(t)\mu
,
\nabla_{x}
and
E_{u}
are the spatial gradient and the Euler-lagrange operators, respectively. Quite recently, the same class in the one-dimensional case has been investigated in
[
1
]
. The authors of this paper use a multiplier approach to find conservation laws. Here, in the multi-dimensional case, we prefer to take the variational symmetry approach with the same goal in mind. Very recently, Noether symmetries and first integrals for the general class of Lagrangians

L=\mu(t)L_{0}
, which models motion with general linear damping in a Riemannian space have been derived in Ref.
[
2
]
.

Obviously,
L_{0}
in (
1.2
) is the Lagrangian of the undamped nonlinear wave equation obtained when
a(t)\equiv 0
,

\Box u+f(u)=0.

(1.3)

In a recent paper
[
3
]
, we have analysed Lie point symmetries of more general classes of equations

\displaystyle\Box u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(x,u,\nabla u),

(1.4a)

\displaystyle\Box u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(x,u),

(1.4b)

where
x=(t,x)=(t,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
\nabla=(\partial_{0},\nabla_{x})=(\partial_{t},\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}})
is the gradient operator in
\mathbb{R}^{1,n}
.
Throughout this paper, unless otherwise stated we will sum over repeated indices. Eq. (
1.1
) belongs to the first class (
1.4a
) with the special choice

G(x,u,\nabla u)=-a(t)u_{t}-f(u),\quad f^{\prime\prime}\neq 0,

which is a nonlinear extension of the linear wave equation

\Box u=0.

(1.5)

A well-known fact about (
1.5
) is that it is invariant under the conformal symmetry group
\mathsf{C}(1,n)
, isomorphic to the pseudo-orthogonal group
\mathsf{O}(n+1,2)
.

For the undamped wave equation (
1.3
),
a(t)=0
, the energy

E(u,\nabla u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\big(u_{t}^{2}+|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}\big)+F(u)\Big]dx

(1.6)

is a constant of the motion (conserved quantity). On the other hand, when
a(t)\neq 0
,
we have the relation

\frac{dE}{dt}=-a(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{t}^{2}dx\leq 0

(1.7)

and the energy is monotonically decreasing for
a>0
. Another conserved quantity is angular momentum (see below formula (
2.26
)).

A number of interesting conservation laws of (
1.5
) were derived in
[
4
]
(see examples 4.15 and 4.36) based on Noether’s theorem. We shall make use of the same theorem to derive conservation laws of the class of (
1.1
) and its subclasses allowing maximal symmetry algebra.

2
Symmetries and Conservation Laws

A general element of a Lie symmetry algebra
\mathfrak{g}
will be a vector field of the form

\mathbf{v}=\sum_{a=0}^{n}\xi_{a}\partial_{x_{a}}+\eta\partial_{u},

(2.1)

where the coefficients
\xi_{a}
and
\eta
are functions of
(t,x,u)
with the identification
x_{0}=t
.

The Lie symmetry algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\conf(1,n)
of (
1.5
) has dimension
\dim\mathfrak{g}=(n+2)(n+3)/2
and is spanned by the vector fields

\begin{split}P_{a}&=\partial_{a},\\
J_{ab}&=x_{a}\partial_{b}-x_{b}\partial_{a},\\
D&=x^{a}\partial_{a}+\frac{(1-n)}{2}u\partial_{u},\\
C_{a}&=2x_{a}D-(x_{\alpha}x^{\alpha})\partial_{a},\end{split}

(2.2)

where
a,b=0,1,\dots,n
.

Preferably,
C_{a}
can be written as

\begin{split}C_{0}&=(t^{2}+r^{2})\partial_{t}+2tx_{l}\partial_{l}+(1-n)tu\partial_{u},\\
C_{k}&=2tx_{k}\partial_{t}+2x_{k}x_{l}\partial_{l}+(t^{2}-r^{2})\partial_{k}+(1-n)x_{k}u\partial_{u},\end{split}

(2.3)

where
r^{2}=|x|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}
,
k,l=1,\dots,n
.

For arbitrary
a(t)
and
f(u)
, the symmetry algebra of (
1.1
) is the Euclidean algebra
\euclid(n)
of translations and rotations of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Its dimension is
n(n+1)/2
and infinitesimal symmetries are

P_{k}=\partial_{k},\quad J_{kl}=x_{k}\partial_{l}-x_{l}\partial_{k},\quad k<l,\quad k,l=1,\dots,n.

(2.4)

We note that the (equivalence) transformation
u\to u+u_{0}
,
u\to\lambda u
takes Eq. (
1.1
) to the same form with
f(u)
changed.
If we require invariance under the dilational symmetry

D=x^{\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}+du\partial_{u}=t\partial_{t}+x_{k}\partial_{k}+du\partial_{u},

(2.5)

then we find that for

a(t)=\frac{m}{t},\quad f(u)=f_{0}u^{p},\quad k\neq 1,\quad d=\frac{2}{1-k},

(2.6)

where
m
and
f_{0}
are integration constants.
Dilatational invariance under

D=t\partial_{t}+x_{k}\partial_{k}-\frac{2}{m}\partial_{u}

(2.7)

leads to
the exponential nonlinearity

f(u)=f_{0}e^{mu}.

(2.8)

Additional invariance under

C_{k}=2tx_{k}\partial_{t}+2x_{k}x_{l}\partial_{l}+(t^{2}-r^{2})\partial_{k}+qx_{k}u\partial_{u}

(2.9)

restricts
p
to be

p=\frac{n+3+m}{n-1+m},\quad q=1-n-m,\quad m\neq 1-n.

(2.10)

We hereby have corrected a misprint concerning the conformal factor
q
in our paper
[
3
]
.

So we will be dealing with the damped PDE (partial differential equation)

\Box u+\frac{m}{t}u_{t}+f_{0}u^{p}=0,\quad p=\frac{n+3+m}{n-1+m},

(2.11)

or

\Box u+\frac{m}{t}u_{t}+f_{0}e^{mu}=0,\quad m\neq 0

(2.12)

admitting a Lie symmetry algebra of dimension
(n+1)(n+2)/2
.
In the case (
2.12
), we replace
C_{k}
by

C_{k}=2tx_{k}\partial_{t}+2x_{k}x_{l}\partial_{l}+(t^{2}-r^{2})\partial_{k}-\frac{4}{m}x_{k}\partial_{u},\quad m\neq 0,\quad k=1,2,\ldots,n.

(2.13)

f_{0}
can be set to
f_{0}=\pm 1
using the above-mentioned equivalence transformation. We comment that when
m=0
, Eq. (
2.11
) reduces to the well-known conformally-invariant nonlinear wave equation with Lie symmetry algebra (factoring out the symmetry representing the linear superposition principle) shared with the homogenous linear wave equation (
1.5
). For an alternative derivation of (
2.11
) in the general case
n\geq 2
, see Refs.
[
3
,
5
]
or
[
6
,
7
]
for
n=2
. In the undamped case
m=0
with power non-linearity
f(u)=\pm u^{p}
where
u=u(t,r)=u(t,|x|)
, a study of conservation laws was given in
[
8
]
. The interested reader can also find basic definitions, ideas and interesting results applied to a lot of PDEs in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

Our primary interest is to derive conservation laws of the damped nonlinear wave equation (
2.11
) utilizing variational structure of the equation. This will be done by means of possible variational symmetries.

A (local) conservation law is an expression of the form

\Div\tilde{I}=D_{t}I_{0}+\Div I=0,\quad\tilde{I}=(I_{0},I)=(I_{0},I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}),

(2.14)

which holds for all solutions of the PDE (
1.1
). Here
I_{0}
is the conserved density,
I=(I_{1},\ldots,I_{n})
the associated (spatial) flux all of which are functions of
(t,x,u)
and the derivatives of
u
with respect to the independent variables
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Here
\Div I
is defined to be the spatial total derivative of the
n
-tuple
I
with respect to
(t,x)

\Div I=\sum_{j=1}^{n}D_{j}I_{j},

where
D_{j}
is the total differential operator defined by

D_{j}=\partial_{j}+u_{j}\partial_{u}+u_{jk}\partial_{u_{k}}+\ldots

For a more detailed discussion on this theme and related issues we refer to
[
4
,
12
,
13
]
.

The following is the infinitesimal criterion of invariance for a first order Lagrangian
L
:

\mathrm{pr}^{(1)}\mathbf{v}(L)+L\Div{\xi}=\Div\tilde{B}=D_{t}B_{0}+\Div B,

(2.15)

where
\xi=(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
and
B
is a
(n+1)
-tuple of differential function in the jet space
J(t,x,u,u_{a})
. The evolutionary form
\mathbf{v}_{Q}=Q\partial_{u}
of the vector field (
2.1
) permits us to express (
2.15
)

\mathrm{pr}^{(1)}\mathbf{v}_{Q}(L)=\Div B,

(2.16)

where
Q(t,x,u,u_{a})
is the characteristic function of the vector field
\mathbf{v}
of (
2.1
) and
B
is another
(n+1)
-tuple differential function.
In terms of
Q(t,x,u,u_{a})
of the variational symmetries we can express the conservation laws in characteristic form

\Div I=Q.E(L)=Q.\mathcal{E}=0,

(2.17)

where
\mathcal{E}=0
is the wave equation defined by (
1.1
). For a first order Lagrangian, the constants of motion or first integrals are given either by integrating by parts (
2.17
) or explicitly by the formula

I_{a}=\xi_{a}L+Q\frac{\partial L}{\partial u_{a}}=\mu(t)\left[\xi_{a}L_{0}+Q\frac{\partial L_{0}}{\partial u_{a}}\right],\quad a=0,1,\ldots,n.

(2.18)

As was also observed in Ref.
[
1
]
, for the Lagrangian
L=\mu L_{0}
given by (
1.2
), the action of
L
on the first prolongation of
\mathbf{v}_{Q}
is

\mathrm{pr}^{(1)}\mathbf{v}_{Q}(L)=-\mu Q\mathcal{E}+D_{t}(\mu u_{t}Q)+\Div(\mu Q\nabla u)

(2.19)

and
the invariance condition (
2.16
) takes the form

\mathrm{pr}^{(1)}\mathbf{v}_{Q}(L)=-\mu Q\mathcal{E}+D_{t}(B_{0}-\mu u_{t}Q)+\Div(B-\mu Q\nabla_{x}u).

(2.20)

This shows that if
\mathbf{v}_{Q}
generates a variational symmetry then
\tilde{Q}=\mu Q
is a multiplier for the corresponding conservation laws, namely,

\mu Q.\mathcal{E}=\Div\tilde{I}=D_{t}I_{0}+\Div I=0,

(2.21)

and
E_{u}(\mu Q.\mathcal{E})
vanishes identically.

Integrating the conservation laws (
2.21
) over a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, using divergence theorem under the assumption that the flux term (an
n
-tuple of functions of
(t,x,u)
and first order derivatives of
u
)
I
tends to zero as
x
approaches the boundary
\partial\Omega
, or if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
|x|\to\infty
we can construct a constant of the motion of (
1.1
) in the integral form

\int_{\Omega}I_{0}\;dx=\text{const.}

(2.22)

2.1
Conservation laws for (
1.1
)

We now turn to the derivation of conservation laws for the original class (
1.1
) for any function
a(t)
, and start with Noether symmetries corresponding to the Euclidean algebra
\euclid(n)
. The characteristic function for
P_{k}
is
Q=u_{k}
(up to sign),
k=1,2,\ldots,n
and integration by parts of the equation

\Div\tilde{I}=\mu u_{k}\mathcal{E}=D_{t}(\mu u_{k}u_{t})+\mu D_{j}\left[u_{k}u_{j}+(\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{t}^{2}-\left|\nabla_{x}u\right|^{2}-2F(u)\right)\delta_{kj}\right].

(2.23)

So we have the conserved density and the current components of the linear momentum conservation

I_{0}^{k}=\mu u_{k}u_{t},\quad I_{j}^{k}=\mu(u_{k}u_{j}+L\delta_{kj}),\quad\mu=\exp{\int a(t)dt},\quad j,k=1,2,\ldots,n.

(2.24)

A similar manipulation gives the angular momentum conservation for the characteristic function
Q=x_{k}u_{l}-x_{l}u_{k}
,
k<l
of the geometrical rotations
J_{kl}

\mu(x_{k}u_{l}-x_{l}u_{k})\mathcal{E}=D_{t}(\mu Qu_{t})+\mu D_{j}[x_{l}\Phi_{jk}-x_{k}\Phi_{jl}+(\delta_{jl}x_{k}-\delta_{kj}x_{l})F],

(2.25)

where the symmetric function
\Phi_{jk}
is defined as

\Phi_{jk}=u_{j}u_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{x}u\right|^{2}\delta_{jk}.

From this we see that the components of the conservation laws in the form

I_{0}^{kl}=\mu(x_{k}u_{l}-x_{l}u_{k})u_{t},\quad I_{j}^{kl}=\mu[x_{l}\Phi_{jk}-x_{k}\Phi_{jl}+(\delta_{jl}x_{k}-\delta_{kj}x_{l})F]=0,\quad j,k,l=1,2,\ldots,n.

(2.26)

Now we wish to determine if the subclasses (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) admit variational symmetries. Our main tool is the infinitesimal criterion (
2.15
) (necessary and sufficient for a connected group of transformations to be a symmetry of a variational problem). Let us start with the dilational generator

D=t\partial_{t}+x_{k}\partial_{k}+du\partial_{u},\quad d\neq 0

(2.27)

and compute the left side of (
2.15
) as

\mathrm{pr}^{(1)}D(L)+(n+1)L=(t\dot{\mu}+(n+1)\mu+2\mu(d-1))L_{0}-\mu(duF^{\prime}-2(d-1)F)

using

\Div\xi=n+1,\quad\mathrm{pr}^{(1)}D=D+(d-1)u_{i}\partial_{u_{i}}.

The coefficients of
L_{0}
and
\mu\neq 0
in this relation imply

t\dot{\mu}+(n+1)\mu+2\mu(d-1)=\mu[ta(t)+(n+1)+2(d-1)]=0,\quad duF^{\prime}-2(d-1)F=0.

The first relation suggests that we must have
ta=m=\rm constant
and
\mu=t^{m}
so we find the dilational factor
d=(1-m-n)/2
. The second one in terms of
f
is

duf^{\prime}-(d-2)f=0,

which has the solution

f=f_{0}u^{p},\quad p=\frac{d-2}{d}=\frac{n+3+m}{n-1+m},

(2.28)

exactly as in the form (
2.11
). Consequently, this result confirms that
D
with
d=(1-m-n)/2
is a variational symmetry for arbitrary
m
and
f(u)
as in (
2.28
). Taking

Q=du-tu_{t}-x_{j}u_{j},\quad L_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{t}^{2}-\left|\nabla_{x}u\right|^{2}\right)-\frac{f_{0}}{p+1}u^{p+1},\quad p=\frac{n+3+m}{n-1+m}

from (
2.18
) it follows the following conserved currents

I_{0}=t^{m}[tL_{0}+Qu_{t}],\quad I_{j}=t^{m}[x_{j}L_{0}+Qu_{j}].

(2.29)

The same calculation for (
2.7
) gives a dimension-dependent constraint on
m
,

m=1-n
. Hence, only for these values of
m
, conservation laws can be obtained from (
2.18
).

We can repeat the same steps for the conformal symmetry generators
C_{k}
with the conformal factor
q
, that is ab initio left free.
We take into account the relations

\Div\xi=2(n+1)x_{k},\quad\mathrm{pr}^{(1)}C_{k}=C_{k}+\tau_{k,0}\partial_{u_{t}}+\tau_{k,l}\partial_{u_{l}},

where the coefficients of the first prolongation are functions of
(t,x,u,u_{a})
defined by

\tau_{k,l}=D_{l}Q_{k}+u_{jl}\xi_{j}=D_{l}\eta_{k}-u_{j}D_{l}\xi_{j},\quad j,k=1,2,\ldots,n.

If we take the coefficients
\xi
and
\eta
of
C_{k}
as

\xi_{k,0}=2tx_{k},\quad\xi_{k,l}=2x_{k}x_{l}+(t^{2}-r^{2})\delta_{kl},\quad\eta_{k}=qx_{k}u

and use the relations

2tx_{k}\dot{\mu}=2m\mu x_{k},\quad u_{t}\tau_{k,0}=(q-2)x_{k}u_{t}^{2}-2tu_{t}u_{k},\quad-u_{l}\tau_{k,l}=-quu_{k}-(q-2)x_{k}\left|\nabla_{x}u\right|^{2}+2tu_{t}u_{k}

\mathrm{pr}^{(1)}C_{k}(L)+2(n+1)x_{k}L=\\
-q\mu uu_{k}+\mu(q+m+n-1)[u_{t}^{2}-\left|\nabla_{x}u\right|^{2}]-\mu[quF^{\prime}+2(m+n+1)F]x_{k}.

(2.30)

The left side of this expression is a total derivative
D_{k}(-qt^{m}u^{2})/2
if we choose

q=1-m-n,\quad quf^{\prime}+[q+2(m+n+1)]f=0,\quad f=F^{\prime}.

The second relation shows that

,
p=(n+3+m)/(n-1+m)
and the conformal transformations (
2.9
) are also variational (more precisely divergence) symmetries. The conserved currents of the corresponding conservation law from formula (

) are

I_{k,0}=t^{m}(2x_{k}L_{0}-Q_{k}u_{k}),\quad I_{k,j}=t^{m}[L_{0}\xi_{k,j}-Q_{k}u_{j}+\frac{q}{2}u^{2}\delta_{jk}],\quad j,k=1,2,\ldots,n.

(2.31)

In (
2.18
),
\xi_{k,j}
denotes the horizontal coefficients of the
k
-th components of
C_{k}
and
Q_{k}
is the characteristic function of
C_{k}
in (
2.9

Finally, we check the formula (
2.15

C_{k}
given in (
2.13

and find

\mathrm{pr}^{(1)}C_{k}(L)+2(n+1)x_{k}L=\frac{4}{m}t^{m}u_{k}+t^{m}(m+n-1)x_{k}[u_{t}^{2}-\left|\nabla_{x}u\right|^{2}].

Again we can express the right side of (

I_{0}^{k}=t^{1-n}(2x_{k}L_{0}-Qu_{k}),\quad I_{j}^{k}=t^{1-n}[L_{0}\xi_{k,j}-Q_{k}u_{j}-\frac{4}{1-n}u\delta_{jk}],\quad j,k=1,2,\ldots,n.

For any global solution to the damped wave equation (
1.1

We recall that in the undamped case (
1.3
), for any
f(u)
, Eq. is invariant under the Poincaré group

which is a semidirect product of the Lorentz group with the group of spacetime translations:
\G=\Poinc(1,n)=\mathbb{R}^{1,n}\rtimes\Ort(1,n)
. This group of transformations are variational symmetries and has dimension
\dim{\G}=(n+1)(n+2)/2
. All conservation laws associated to them can be found by (

f(u)=f_{0}u^{p}
,

is even larger, the conformal group
\Conf(1,n)
. In this case, there are

more conservation laws obtainable from (

). One of them is of course the conservation of energy given by (

) with multiplier

. The remaining symmetries consist of one more conformal and all Lorentz generators given by

C_{0}=(t^{2}+r^{2})\partial_{t}+2tx_{l}\partial_{l}+(1-n)tu\partial_{u},\quad K_{k}=J_{k0}=t\partial_{k}+x_{k}\partial_{t},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,n

(2.34)

are also variational.

The fact that in the one-dimensional case there exists a simple change of dependent variable

u(t,x)=\mu(t)^{-1/2}v(t,x)

(2.35)

respecting the
u
-dependence of the nonlinear interaction
f(u)
and transforming the damped equation (
1.1
) to the undamped form (
1.3
), namely removing the damping term
a(t)
was studied in Ref.
[
1
]
. This happens when
a(t)
satisfies

\frac{\dot{a}}{2}+\frac{a^{2}}{4}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\int a(t)dt=\sigma_{0}=\text{const.},

(2.36)

and
f(u)
, which has no explicit time dependence, is a logarithmic interaction

f(u)=(\sigma+\kappa\ln|u|)u.

(2.37)

f(u)
is transformed to
g(v)=(\sigma-\sigma_{0}+\kappa\ln|v|)v
. This means, by transformation, the constant
\sigma
shifts as
\sigma\to\sigma-\sigma_{0}
.
The same result holds in the multidimensional case. The general solution of (
2.36
) is not elementary. It can be expressed in terms of Lambert function. The elementary solutions exist for instance when
\kappa=0
, but in this case
g(v)
becomes linear. In this case, all known conservation law results as mentioned above paragraph for the undamped PDE.

We conclude with the observation that, for the damping term
a(t)=m/t
(
\mu=t^{m}
) frequently used here, the left side of (
2.36
) is

m\left[\frac{1}{4}t^{-2}(m-2)+\frac{\kappa}{2}\ln|t|\right],\quad m\neq 0

which can never be a constant unless
m=2
,
\kappa=0
for which the logarithmic nonlinearity would not be present.
