Contact activation of coagulation in newly inserted central venous catheters (CAS-2): protocol for a randomised controlled study comparing four commercially available catheters
Authors: Aida Zorlak, Leila Naddi, Ola Borgquist, Thomas Kander
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCentral venous catheters (CVCs) are essential in modern healthcare. Insertion of a CVC promotes a hypercoagulable environment, in part through activation of the intrinsic coagulation pathway. We have previously shown that blood obtained from newly inserted CVCs demonstrates immediate coagulation activation, with differences between samples collected directly after insertion and after flushing of the catheter. The aim of this randomised trial is to compare early coagulation activation between four commonly used CVCs and to assess whether the different CVCs demonstrate different degrees of coagulation activation.Methods and analysisThis is a single-centre, randomised, parallel-group trial where adult patients (≥18 years) with a clinical indication for a two-lumen CVC will be eligible for inclusion. Participants will be randomised in a 1:1:1:1 ratio to receive one of four commercially available CVCs. The calculated sample size is 88 participants. Two blood samples will be obtained from each newly inserted CVC, the first obtained from the initial backflow of blood within seconds after insertion and the second after flush and discard. Samples will be analysed using rotational thromboelastometry with the non-activated thromboelastometry (ROTEM NATEM) and plasma-based coagulation assays. The primary outcome is the between-group comparison of the change in ROTEM NATEM clotting time between the two samples. Secondary outcomes include within-group and overall cohort comparisons of changes in clotting time. Additional secondary outcomes are between-group, within-group and overall cohort comparisons of changes in other ROTEM NATEM parameters, including clot formation time, alpha angle and maximum clot firmness, and changes in plasma-based coagulation markers, including prothrombin time–international normalised ratio, activated partial thromboplastin time, Factor VII, Factor XII and thrombin–antithrombin complex concentration. The results may help identify catheter materials that are less prone to activate coagulation and support in the selection and development of CVCs.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the Swedish Ethical Review Authority (#2022-00265-0). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before inclusion. The results will be published in a peer-reviewed journal and presented at scientific meetings.Trial registration numberNCT07014722.

Introduction

Central venous catheters (CVCs) provide reliable intravascular access for administration of medications, fluids and parenteral nutrition as well as for advanced haemodynamic monitoring. However, their use is associated with complications, among which catheter-related thrombosis may be a concern.16

The insertion of a CVC has several consequences, including endothelial injury, altered blood flow and direct interaction between the catheter surface and the coagulation system, all of which promote a hypercoagulable environment.7 8 Contact between blood and catheter surface activates the intrinsic coagulation pathway, leading to Factor XII–dependent thrombin generation and early formation of thrombin at the blood–material interface.7 9 Preanalytical studies have shown that even short exposure of blood to foreign material, such as that occurring in collection tubes, results in measurable activation of the contact system, demonstrating how sensitive this pathway is to surface exposure.10 The extent of contact activation depends on the material to which blood is exposed, as different polymers and surface properties lead to varying degrees of coagulation activation, and studies of commercially available CVCs have demonstrated material-dependent differences in blood compatibility.11 12

In addition, surface coatings such as silver or chlorhexidine, which are primarily intended to reduce catheter-related infections, also modify surface properties and may influence coagulation and platelet responses.13

Recently, we investigated the immediate coagulation activation in newly inserted CVCs, peripheral venous catheters (PVCs) and arterial catheters by analysing paired blood samples collected immediately after insertion and then after flushing. Blood obtained from CVCs demonstrated an overt coagulation activation whereas samples from PVCs and arterial catheters did not. Although different catheter types were used, the study was not designed to compare different CVCs.14 This study was designed to address that knowledge gap.

Aim and hypothesis

The aim of this study is to compare early coagulation activation between four commonly used two-lumen CVCs, with the hypothesis that early coagulation activation differs between different CVCs. The results may help identify catheters that are less prone to activate coagulation and support in the selection and development of CVCs.

Methods and analyses

Study design

This is an investor-initiated, non-commercial, single-centre, randomised, parallel-group, superiority trial evaluating coagulation activation in newly inserted CVCs. Participants will be randomly allocated to four different CVC groups, and blood from the newly inserted CVCs will be collected and analysed for coagulation parameters.

The study protocol has been developed in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) 2025 statement15 (online supplemental appendix A). A detailed study flow diagram is provided in the online supplemental appendix B.

Study population

Adult (≥18 years) patients at Skåne University Hospital, Lund, Sweden, with a clinical indication for a two-lumen CVC and able to provide written informed consent will be eligible for inclusion. Included patients will have blood samples collected from the newly inserted CVC for rotational thromboelastometry (ROTEM) and plasma-based coagulation analyses. CVC insertions will be performed by specialist anaesthesiologists or residents in anaesthesiology with formal training in CVC placement according to hospital standards. Enrolment started on 10 September 2025 and will continue until the calculated sample size of 88 participants is reached (estimated by December 2026).

Blood sampling and laboratory analyses

Two blood samples will be collected from the distal lumen of each newly inserted CVC, without pre-flushing or adding connectors. The first sample (sample 1) will be drawn from the initial backflow of blood, seconds after catheter insertion. The second blood sample (sample 2) will be collected after flushing the catheter with at least 10 mL of saline and discarding 5 mL of blood. Samples will be collected into Vacuette CTAD (Citrate, theophylline, adenosine och dipyridamole) tubes16 (Greiner Bio-One, Kremsmünster, Austria), containing sodium citrate, theophylline, adenosine and dipyridamole. Tubes will be gently inverted immediately after collection and kept at 37 °C until processing. ROTEM non-activated thromboelastometry (NATEM) analyses will be performed on whole blood within 3 hours of collection. Following ROTEM analysis, the remaining blood will be centrifuged at 1700 g for 15 min. Plasma from each sample will then be transferred into cryotubes, immediately frozen and stored at –80°C before plasma coagulation analysis.

Study participation ends after completion of blood sampling and laboratory analyses. No trial-specific follow-up is required.

Adherence to this protocol is ensured through a small study team trained in all trial procedures, including patient inclusion, informed consent, randomisation, blood sampling, ROTEM analyses and laboratory work.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the between-group comparison of the change in ROTEM NATEM clotting time (ΔCT, seconds), calculated as the difference between sample 1 and sample 2 for each group.

Adverse events

All serious adverse events considered potentially related to the intervention will be promptly reported to the principal investigators. A serious adverse event is defined as a major mechanical complication, as described in a previous publication.17

All participants are protected under the Swedish Patient Safety Act (Patientsäkerhetslagen, SFS 2010:659) and covered by the national patient insurance.

Sample size

Sample size calculation was based on the primary outcome. Prior data indicate a mean ΔCT of 450 s with an SD of 152 s.14 Assuming group means of 450 s, 275 s, 350 s and 400 s, a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) requires 17 participants per group to achieve 80% power at α=0.05. To account for non-normality, potential sample loss and technical issues such as clotting or machine errors, the sample size was increased to 22 per group, giving a total of 88 participants with an equal 1:1:1:1 allocation across the four CVC types. SAS Proc Power, V.9.4, (SAS Institute Inc, Cary, North Carolina, USA) was used for sample size determination.

Recruitment

A dedicated member of the trial team will monitor surgical schedules and CVC referrals to identify potential participants in both the operating department and the department for CVC insertion. Screening will take place when a member of the trial team is available at the department during daytime hours. Randomisation will be performed once eligibility criteria have been confirmed and written informed consent has been obtained.

Randomisation and blinding

The allocation sequence will be computer-generated using a random number generator implemented in REDCap (Research Electronic Data Capture; Vanderbilt University, Nashville, Tennessee, USA). Participants will be randomly assigned to one of four CVC types using simple, unrestricted randomisation in equal proportions. No stratification will be applied, as major factors affecting coagulation, such as anticoagulant therapy or known coagulopathies, are excluded. In addition, the primary outcome is a within-subject comparison, with each patient serving as their own control.

As catheter type is apparent during insertion, anaesthesiologists, trial participants and personnel performing blood sampling cannot be blinded. The same personnel who collect the blood samples will perform the ROTEM analyses and are therefore aware of group allocation. Plasma coagulation analyses will be performed using coded samples, ensuring that laboratory personnel, data analysts and outcome assessors for both primary and secondary outcomes remain blinded to group allocation.

Data management and confidentiality

All data will be entered into REDCap as pseudonymised participant records. The information connecting study IDs to patient identities will be kept securely in a locked location at the hospital and will be accessible only to the study team. Data will be stored for at least 10 years after trial completion.

Statistical methods

Analyses will be conducted according to the intention-to-treat principle. Continuous variables with a symmetrical distribution will be reported as mean (SD), whereas skewed variables will be summarised as median (IQR). Categorical variables will be presented as number (percentage).

Between-group comparisons, including differences in ΔCT and other coagulation markers among the CVC groups, will be analysed using the Kruskal–Wallis test with Dunn’s post hoc test when data are not normally distributed. If normality is confirmed, a one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test will be applied.

Comparisons between sample 1 and sample 2 will be performed using the Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test for non-normally distributed data, and the paired t-test when data meet the assumption of normality.

If appropriate, simple or multiple imputation methods may be applied, and any approach used will be reported in the final analysis.

For the primary outcome, a p value <0.05 will be considered statistically significant, as predefined in the sample size calculation. For secondary outcomes, correction for multiple testing will be performed. An adjusted significance level of p<0.01 will be applied, representing a modified Bonferroni correction that accounts for the fact that the tested variables are not fully independent of each other.

Data and trial monitoring

Study conduct and data integrity will be monitored continuously by the research team. The site investigator will monitor data entry to ensure accuracy.
