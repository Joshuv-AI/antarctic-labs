Toeplitz exactness for strong convergence
David Gao, Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli

Abstract
. We prove a new “ Toeplitz exactness ” theorem for strong convergence. This is a machine to upgrade strong convergence in the general setting of C ∗ C^{\ast} -correspondences, and has several applications.

1.
Introduction

Recent years have witnessed substantial advancements in the programs of strong convergence of unitary representations
[
7
,
19
,
9
]
and the free structure of
C^{\ast}
-algebras
[
15
,
1
,
11
]
. The current paper is particularly inspired by the recent development
[
5
]
that found a new connection. Our main result here reveals a general “
Toeplitz exactness
” phenomenon that upgrades strong convergence to Toeplitz-Pimsner algebras associated to general
C^{\ast}
-correspondences, constructed in Pimsner’s work
[
12
]
. This is
not
to be confused with preservation of “on the nose exactness” for Toeplitz-Pimsner algebras in the sense of
[
3
]
. Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix index sets
I
and
J
. Let
(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i\in I}
be a tuple in a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A^{(k)}
and
(\xi_{j}^{(k)})_{j\in J}
be a tuple in a
C^{\ast}
-correspondence
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
over
A^{(k)}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Assume further that
A^{(\infty)}
is generated by
(X_{i}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
and
\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}
is generated by
(\xi_{j}^{(\infty)})_{j\in J}
as a
C^{\ast}
-correspondence over
A^{(\infty)}
. If
(X_{i}^{(k)};\xi_{j}^{(k)})_{i\in I,j\in J}
converges strongly to
(X_{i}^{(\infty)};\xi_{j}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I,j\in J}
, then
(X_{i}^{(k)},T_{\xi_{j}}^{(k)})_{i\in I,j\in J}
in
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}^{(k)})
converges strongly to
(X_{i}^{(\infty)},T_{\xi_{j}}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I,j\in J}
in
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)})
.

Our approach uses the
gauge invariant-uniqueness
theorem of Fowler–Muhly–Raeburn
[
4
]
for Toeplitz-Pimsner algebras associated to arbitrary
C^{\ast}
-correspondences, which completed a line of research beginning in the visionary work of Pimsner
[
12
]
. We now demonstrate the utility of Theorem
1.1
by quickly listing a few applications. Concerning the next result, recall the free exactness results of Skoufranis
[
17
]
and Pisier
[
13
]
(see also Shlyakhtenko’s appendix in
[
10
]
). Recall that, for instance, Pisier’s
[
13
, Theorem 7.1]
says roughly that taking amalgamated free products over a fixed subalgebra preserves strong convergence. The following corollary recovers and generalizes all these results with a new proof:

Corollary 1.2
.

Fix an index set
J
and an index set
I_{j}
for each
j\in J
. Let
D^{(k)}\subset A_{j}^{(k)}
be an inclusion of
C^{\ast}
-algebras with nondegenerate condition expectation
E_{j}^{(k)}
; and let
(X_{i,j}^{(k)})_{i\in I_{j}}
be a tuple in
A_{j}^{(k)}
, for each
j\in J
and
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Assume further that
A_{j}^{(\infty)}
is generated by
(X_{i,j}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I_{j}}
for every
j\in J
. If
(X_{i,j}^{(k)};E_{j}^{(k)})_{i\in I_{j}}\to(X_{i,j}^{(\infty)};E_{j}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I_{j}}
strongly for all
j\in J
, consistently on
D^{(k)}
, then
(X_{i,j}^{(k)};\ast_{D^{(k)}}^{j\in J}E_{j}^{(k)})_{i\in I_{j},j\in J}
in
\ast_{D^{(k)}}A_{j}^{(k)}
converges strongly to
(X_{i,j}^{(\infty)};\ast_{D^{(\infty)}}^{j\in J}E_{j}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I_{j},j\in J}
in
\ast_{D^{(\infty)}}A_{j}^{(\infty)}
.

We note that Pisier’s proof crucially used the analytic Khintchine type inequality of Ricard-Xu
[
14
]
from noncommutative
L^{p}
–space theory. Our proof avoids this completely, and is based on a conceptually different
algebraic
approach. As a side remark, note that our work provides also a satisfactory answer to question (3) of Section 5 in Skoufranis’ work
[
17
]
. In particular, our work is able to circumvent the technical challenges that Skoufranis faced in his approach
[
17
]
, and extend the result significantly.

Another advantage of the general setup of Corollary
1.2
is that it could be used to aid in proving the existence of strongly converging unitary representations, following the novel approach of
[
5
]
. Particularly, in one of the steps in the proof of Lemma 2.3 in
[
5
]
, one could alternatively use Corollary
1.2
in place of an isomorphism theorem for Toeplitz-Pimnser algebras
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
(in Remark
3.6
the argument is explained for the benefit of the reader). However, recall that the proof of the isomorphism theorem
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
is much easier than the proof of our Corollary
1.2
, rendering the original application of Toeplitz-Pimsner machinery in
[
5
, Lemma 2.3]
much more succinct and transparent. While our results in this paper cannot produce finite-dimensional models for strong convergence on their own, they can assist in the above manner owing to the apparatus developed in
[
5
]
.

Below we document another application of upgrading strong convergence in the setting of HNN extensions (
[
18
,
6
]
).

Corollary 1.3
.

Fix an index set
I
. Let
A^{(k)}
be a
C^{\ast}
-algebra equipped with a faithful state
\rho^{(k)}
,
B_{1}^{(k)},B_{-1}^{(k)}\subset A^{(k)}
be subalgebras admitting state-preserving conditional expectations
E_{1}^{(k)}
and
E_{-1}^{(k)}
, resp., and
\theta^{(k)}:B_{1}^{(k)}\to B_{-1}^{(k)}
be a state-preserving
\ast
-isomorphism; and let
(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i\in I}
be a tuple in
A^{(k)}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Assume further that
A^{(\infty)}
is generated by
(X_{i}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
. If
(X_{i}^{(k)};\rho^{(k)},E_{1}^{(k)},E_{-1}^{(k)},\theta^{(k)})_{i\in I}\to(X_{i}^{(\infty)};\rho^{(\infty)},E_{1}^{(\infty)},E_{-1}^{(\infty)},\theta^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
strongly, then
(X_{i}^{(k)},w^{(k)})_{i\in I}
converges strongly to
(X_{i}^{(\infty)},w^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
, where
w^{(k)}\in\mathrm{HNN}(A^{(k)},\theta^{(k)},B_{1}^{(k)},B_{-1}^{(k)})
is the unitary implementing
\theta^{(k)}
in the HNN extension, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.

As a final application we describe here, one can also upgrade strong convergence to Shlyakhtenko’s
A
-valued semicircular systems
[
16
]
, in a precise sense. This is in particular useful because it can be used to remove one of the hypotheses involving ambient strong convergence in Theorem B of the recent interesting work
[
8
]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Fix index sets
I
and
J
. Let
(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i\in I}
be a tuple in a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A^{(k)}
and
\eta^{(k)}=(\eta_{j_{1}j_{2}}^{(k)})_{j_{1},j_{2}\in J}
be a covariance matrix, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Assume further that
A^{(\infty)}
is generated by
(X_{i}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
. Let
(S_{j}^{(k)})_{j\in J}
be the
A^{(k)}
-valued semicircular family with covariance
\eta^{(k)}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. If
(X_{i}^{(k)};\eta^{(k)})_{i\in I}
converges strongly to
(X_{i}^{(\infty)};\eta^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
, then
(X_{i}^{(k)},S_{j}^{(k)})_{i\in I,j\in J}
converges strongly to
(X_{i}^{(\infty)},S_{j}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I,j\in J}
.

Remarks to the reader

The proof of the main result, Theorem
1.1
, is quite succinct using the gauge-invariant uniqueness theorem of Fowler-Muhly-Raeburn
[
4
]
, once the relevant setup and notations have been constructed precisely. One of the more notationally challenging aspects of these results is to set up the ambient weak and strong convergence hypotheses appropriately in various concrete settings (see Examples
2.5
). Once this is set up with care in amalgamated free products, Corollary
1.2
is proved by fitting in
[
2
, Theorem 4.8.2]
which concretely links general reduced amalgamated free products to Toeplitz-Pimsner algebras.

As far as HNN extensions are concerned, we exploit the recent insight from
[
6
]
expressing the HNN extension as a concrete unital subalgebra of an amalgamated free product. Therefore, Corollary
1.3
can be proved by using Corollary
1.2
appropriately. The proof of Corollary
1.4
on the other hand is quite straightforward from the way the
A
-valued semicirculars are constructed, and is a direct application of Theorem
1.1
in that setting.

2.
Preliminaries and Notations

Unless otherwise mentioned, amalgamated free products of
C^{\ast}
-algebras equipped with nondegenerate conditional expectations will be in the reduced sense
[
20
]
. Additionally, all tensor products will be minimal. All ultraproducts mentioned will be Banach space ultraproducts, which are naturally
C^{\ast}
-algebras (and are in fact the standard
C^{\ast}
-algebraic ultraproducts with respect to the operator norm) should the input Banach spaces all be
C^{\ast}
-algebras. Finally, if
\rho
is a state on a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A
, then
\mathrm{Aut}(A,\rho)
shall denote the group of
\ast
-automorphisms of
A
that preserve
\rho
.

We start with the definition of strong convergence for
C^{\ast}
-correspondences:

Definition 2.1
.

Fix index sets
I
and
J
. Let
(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i\in I}
be a tuple in a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A^{(k)}
and
(\xi_{j}^{(k)})_{j\in J}
be a tuple in a
C^{\ast}
-correspondence
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
over
A^{(k)}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Assume further that
A^{(\infty)}
is generated by
(X_{i}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
and
\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}
is generated by
(\xi_{j}^{(\infty)})_{j\in J}
as a
C^{\ast}
-correspondence over
A^{(\infty)}
. We say that
(X_{i}^{(k)};\xi_{j}^{(k)})_{i\in I,j\in J}

converges strongly
to
(X_{i}^{(\infty)};\xi_{j}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I,j\in J}
if

(1)

(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i\in I}\to(X_{i}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
strongly in the classical sense, i.e., for any
\ast
-polynomial
P
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|P(X_{i}^{(k)})\|=\|P(X_{i}^{(\infty)})\|
; and,

(2)

for any
\ast
-polynomials
P,Q
and
j_{1},j_{2}\in J
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|P(X_{i}^{(k)})-\langle\xi_{j_{1}}^{(k)},Q(X_{i}^{(k)})\xi_{j_{2}}^{(k)}\rangle\|=\|P(X_{i}^{(\infty)})-\langle\xi_{j_{1}}^{(\infty)},Q(X_{i}^{(\infty)})\xi_{j_{2}}^{(\infty)}\rangle\|.

Remark 2.2
.

Recall that, if we fix an index set
I
and let
(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i\in I}
be a tuple in a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A^{(k)}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Assume further that
A^{(\infty)}
is generated by
(X_{i}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
. Then
(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i\in I}
converges strongly to
(X_{i}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
in the classical sense if and only if, for each free ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
, there exists an embedding
\pi:A^{(\infty)}\to\prod_{\mathcal{U}}A^{(k)}
s.t.
\pi(X_{i}^{(\infty)})=(X_{i}^{(k)})_{\mathcal{U}}
for each
i\in I
. There is a corresponding ultraproduct characterization of strong convergence for
C^{\ast}
-correspondences, as follows:
(X_{i}^{(k)};\xi_{j}^{(k)})_{i\in I,j\in J}\to(X_{i}^{(\infty)};\xi_{j}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I,j\in J}
strongly if and only if, for each free ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
, there exist embeddings
\pi:A^{(\infty)}\to\prod_{\mathcal{U}}A^{(k)}
and
\phi:\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}\to\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
such that

(1)

\pi(X_{i}^{(\infty)})=(X_{i}^{(k)})_{\mathcal{U}}
for all
i\in I
and
\phi(\xi_{j}^{(\infty)})=(\xi_{j}^{(k)})_{\mathcal{U}}
for all
j\in J
;

(2)

for
a,b\in A^{(\infty)}
,
\xi\in\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}
, if
\pi(a)=(a^{(k)})_{\mathcal{U}}
,
\pi(b)=(b^{(k)})_{\mathcal{U}}
, and
\phi(\xi)=(\xi^{(k)})_{\mathcal{U}}
, then
\phi(a\xi b)=(a^{(k)}\xi^{(k)}b^{(k)})_{\mathcal{U}}
; and,

(3)

for
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{(\infty)}
, if
\phi(\xi)=(\xi^{(k)})_{\mathcal{U}}
and
\phi(\eta)=(\eta^{(k)})_{\mathcal{U}}
, then
\pi(\langle\xi,\eta\rangle)=(\langle\xi^{(k)},\eta^{(k)}\rangle)_{\mathcal{U}}
.

We leave it to the reader to check the above equivalence.

We will also use various standard notations in
C^{\ast}
-algebras. Please consult
[
2
]
. In particular, recall the following:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
C^{\ast}
-correspondence over a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A
. Then the
Toeplitz-Pimsner algebra

\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
associated to
\mathcal{H}
is defined to be the universal
C^{\ast}
-algebra generated by
A
and
T_{\xi}
(called
creation operators
) for
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, subject to the relations that

T_{\alpha\xi+\eta}=\alpha T_{\xi}+T_{\eta}\text{ for }\alpha\in\mathbb{C},\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H};

T_{a\xi b}=aT_{\xi}b\text{ for }a,b\in A,\xi\in\mathcal{H};

T_{\xi}^{\ast}T_{\eta}=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle\text{ for }\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}.

Note that the above relation implies
\mathcal{H}\ni\xi\mapsto T_{\xi}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
is an isometric
A
-
A
-bimodular linear map. From this it follows that, should
\mathcal{H}
be generated by
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j\in J}
, then
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
can alternatively be described as the universal
C^{\ast}
-algebra generated by
A
and
T_{\xi_{j}}
for
j\in J
, subject to the relations that
T_{\xi_{j_{1}}}^{\ast}aT_{\xi_{j_{2}}}=\langle\xi_{j_{1}},a\xi_{j_{2}}\rangle
.

From the definition, it is easy to check that there is an action
\beta:\mathbb{T}\curvearrowright\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
defined by
\beta_{z}(a)=a
for all
a\in A
and
\beta_{z}(T_{\xi})=zT_{\xi}
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. This is called the
gauge action
.

We also note that the Toeplitz-Pimsner algebra admits a canonical nondegenerate conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})\to A
which satisfies
E(T_{\xi}T_{\eta}^{\ast})=0
for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}

[
2
, Theorem 4.6.6(2)]
. In fact, by applying Cauchy-Schwarz, the reader may check that
E
is uniquely characterized by the properties that it is positive, restricts to the identity map on
A
, and satisfies
E(T_{\xi}T_{\xi}^{\ast})=0
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
.

We recall the gauge-invariant uniqueness theorem for Toeplitz-Pimsner algebras
[
4
]
(see also
[
2
, Theorem 4.6.18]
).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
C^{\ast}
-correspondence over a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A
. Suppose
\mathcal{H}
is generated by
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j\in J}
. Let
B
be a
C^{\ast}
-algebra,
\pi:A\to B
be an embedding. Suppose
S_{j}\in B
for
j\in J
satisfies
S_{j_{1}}^{\ast}\pi(a)S_{j_{2}}=\pi(\langle\xi_{j_{1}},a\xi_{j_{2}}\rangle)
. Suppose further that
B
admits a gauge action, i.e., there is an action
\beta:\mathbb{T}\curvearrowright B
s.t.
\beta_{z}(\pi(a))=\pi(a)
for all
a\in A
and
\beta_{z}(S_{j})=zS_{j}
for all
j\in J
. Suppose also that

(1)

\pi(A)\cap\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{aS_{j_{1}}bS_{j_{2}}^{\ast}c:a,b,c\in A,j_{1},j_{2}\in J\}=\{0\}.

Then
\pi
extends to a (unique) embedding
\tilde{\pi}:\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})\to B
s.t.
\pi(T_{\xi_{j}})=S_{j}
for all
j\in J
.

Examples 2.5
.

(1)

Consider
\mathcal{H}=A
as a
C^{\ast}
-correspondence over
A
, which is generated by
1
. By the gauge-invariant uniqueness theorem,
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})\cong A\otimes\mathcal{T}
, where
\mathcal{T}
is the classical Toeplitz algebra (i.e., the universal
C^{\ast}
-algebra of an isometry), where
T_{1}\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
corresponds to the generating isometry of
\mathcal{T}
.

Suppose for each

we have a
C^{\ast}
-algebra

and
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}=A^{(k)}
. We leave it to the reader to check that
(X_{i}^{(k)};1^{(k)})_{i\in I}\to(X_{i}^{(\infty)},1^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
strongly if and only if
(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i\in I}\to(X_{i}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
strongly in the classical sense.

(2)

For a more general example, consider a subalgebra
B\subset A
admitting a nondegenerate conditional expectation
E:A\to B
. Then we can consider the
C^{\ast}
-correspondence
\mathcal{H}=A\otimes_{B}A
, i.e., the completion of

\hskip 42.67912pt\langle a\otimes b,c\otimes d\rangle=b^{\ast}E(a^{\ast}c)d.

(we shall also denote it by

, where
\mathcal{T}
is understood to be equipped with its vacuum state and where

corresponds to the generating isometry of

.

Suppose for each

C^{\ast}

(a)

(X_{i}^{(k)})_{i\in I}\to(X_{i}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
strongly in the classical sense; and,

(b)

for any
\ast
-polynomials

,

\hskip 51.21504pt\lim_{k\to\infty}\|P(X_{i}^{(k)})-E^{(k)}(Q(X_{i}^{(k)}))\|=\|P(X_{i}^{(\infty)})-E^{(\infty)}(Q(X_{i}^{(\infty)}))\|.

(X_{i}^{(k)};E^{(k)})_{i\in I}
converging to
(X_{i}^{(\infty)};E^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}

-probability spaces

(a)

(b)

(3)

For an even more general example, recall from
[
16
]
that, for an index set
J
, we say a matrix of maps
\eta=(\eta_{j_{1}j_{2}}:A\to A)_{j_{1},j_{2}\in J}

-correspondence
\mathcal{H}(\eta)
defined as the completion of
A\odot c_{00}(J;A)
under the

\hskip 39.83368pt\langle a\otimes b,c\otimes d\rangle=\langle b,\eta(a^{\ast}c)d\rangle_{c_{00}(J;A)}=\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\in J}b_{j_{1}}^{\ast}\eta_{j_{1}j_{2}}(a^{\ast}c)d_{j_{2}}.

Note that
A\otimes_{B}A
in the previous example is a special case of the construction here. Namely, it corresponds to the case where

and

.

For the general case,
\mathcal{H}(\eta)
is generated by

(we shall also denote it by

\hskip 51.21504pt\lim_{k\to\infty}\|P(X_{i}^{(k)})-\eta_{j_{1}j_{2}}^{(k)}(Q(X_{i}^{(k)}))\|=\|P(X_{i}^{(\infty)})-\eta_{j_{1}j_{2}}^{(\infty)}(Q(X_{i}^{(\infty)}))\|.

Remark 3.10
.

We note that, using in addition a combinatorial argument similar to the classical proof of weak convergence, we can obtain the stronger conclusion that
(X_{i}^{(k)},w^{(k)};E_{A^{(k)}}^{(k)})_{i\in I}
converges strongly to
(X_{i}^{(\infty)},w^{(\infty)};E_{A^{(\infty)}}^{(\infty)})_{i\in I}
. Here,
E_{A^{(k)}}^{(k)}
is the conditional expectation from
\mathrm{HNN}(A^{(k)},\theta^{(k)},B_{1}^{(k)},B_{-1}^{(k)})
onto
A^{(k)}
, inherited from
C^{(k)}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. We leave it to the reader to check the details.
