Capturing cash non-additivity and horizon risk via BSDEs and generalized shortfall
Giulia Di Nunno, Emanuela Rosazza Gianin

Abstract
Whenever dealing with horizons of different times scales, risk evaluation of losses may incur in both interest rate uncertainty and horizon risk as introduced in [ 11 ] . With the goal to capture both effects, we work with cash subadditive fully-dynamic risk measures. In this work we consider such measures obtained via the BSDE and the shortfall approaches. We stress that we consider BSDEs both with Lipschitz and quadratic drivers. We then introduce the hq-entropic risk measure on losses as an effective example of fully-dynamic risk measure serving the scope. Shortfall risk measures are extended to capture cash non-additivity. For our newly introduced h-generalized shortfall risk measures we provide a dual representation and we connect them to fully-dynamic certainty equivalent. To conclude, we can see that the hq-entropic risk measures on losses belong to the family h-generalized shortfall, but they are not of certainty equivalent type. We note that the classical entropic risk measure, besides being generated by a BSDE, is also both a shortfall and a certainty equivalent. Keywords: horizon risk, relative entropy, cash non-additivity, interest rate uncertainty, dynamic risk measures, Lipschitz and quadratic BSDE MSC2020: 60H10, 60H20, 91B70, 91G70

Introduction

Horizon risk is defined as the use of a risk measure designed for long term positions when evaluating short term investments, and vice versa.
This risk is particularly critical, in the context of, e.g., pensions and health insurance, where long-term claims are to be expected and hedged.
In this context, for instance, we know that the use of outdated mortality rate, or the choice of an incorrect cohort may lead to wrong premia evaluations with a consequent impact on capital requirements.
In
[
11
]
, horizon risk has been identified using fully-dynamic risk measures and introducing the notion of
horizon longevity
or
h-longevity
, in short, as an index of quantification.
Indeed, a

is a family
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}\triangleq(\rho_{tu})_{0\leq t\leq u\leq T}

To understand horizon risk, we must recall the

Roughly speaking, this means that a risky position

On the contrary, whenever the restriction is lifted, we have an open possibility to quantify horizon risk. For this, the notion of

\gamma(t,u,v,X)\triangleq\rho_{tv}(X)-\rho_{tu}(X)\geq 0,\quad t\leq u\leq v.

The first part of this work is dedicated to formalizing the concepts above. We detail the properties of h-longevity and cash non-additivity in the BSDE approach both with Lipschitz and quadratic drivers, so to accommodate the extensions of entropic risk measures we will introduce.

The classical entropic risk measure can also be regarded as a
shortfall risk measure
, that is

\rho_{tu}(X)=\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\big\{m_{t}\;\mathcal{F}_{t}-\mbox{measurable}:\;E\big[U(X+m_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]\geq B\big\}

Motivated by this connection, we study the extension of shortfall risk measures to capture both cash non-additivity and horizon risk.
This leads to the concept of

\rho^{U,f,B}_{tu}(X)=\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\big\{m_{t}\;\mathcal{F}_{t}-\mbox{measurable}:\;E\big[U_{u}(f_{u}(X,m_{t}))|\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]\geq B_{tu}\big\}

by means of the families of concave utility functions

Even further, we have studied the relationship between the h-generalized shortfall and the so-called
certainty equivalent risk measure
, which we present in the fully-dynamic version:

\tilde{U}_{u}(-\rho^{\tilde{U}_{u}}_{tu}(X))=E[\tilde{U}_{u}(X)|\mathcal{F}_{t}],

(\tilde{U}_{u})_{u}

Indeed our results show that a h-generalized shortfall is a certainty equivalent under specific relationship between parameters
(U_{u},f_{u},B_{tu})_{t,u}
and
(\tilde{U}_{u})_{u}

Winding back to our hq-entropic risk measure (
6

We see that while it is true that (
6

Our work is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we present the BSDE approach to the construction of fully-dynamic risk measures. We consider both the Lipschitz and the quadratic drivers, bearing in mind to work with the entropic risk measures. We focus on cash non-additivity and characterize h-longevity. In the Lipschitz case we achieve an explicit formula for the h-longevity index.
To complement the study we also suggest to construct fully-dynamic risk measure from a family of BSDEs instead of a single one. This approach exacerbates the role of horizon risk.
In Section
3
we introduce and study the hq-entropic risk measure on losses.
Section
4
is proposing the generalized shortfall representation (static and dynamic) to embed both cash non-additivity and h-longevity. These measures are quasi-convex. We study their dual representation.
In Section
5
we consider the relationship between h-generalized shortfall and fully-dynamic certainty equivalent risk measures.
In the last section, we recognize the hq-entropic risk measure on losses as a generalized shortfall. We note, however, that it is not a fully-dynamic certainty equivalent.

A q-entropic risk measure on losses

We consider the generalized entropy studied in
[

31
]
based on the generalization of the logarithmic and exponential functions.
This has found recent application to pricing and risk measures in
[

,
29
]

Here below we study the connection of the generalized entropy with BSDE (
7
) with driver

g_{q}(t,y,z)=\frac{q}{2}\frac{|z|^{2}}{1+(1-q)y},\quad\mbox{for }q>0,

which is of type (
8

Note that when

Observe that, for

is convex in

for

We choose to work with

and we consider the driver (
16

, the solution exists, is unique, and non-negative for the terminal condition

We can also see that the solution has representation

Y_{t}=\ln_{q}E\left[\left.\exp_{q}(X)\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

(17)

In fact, we can consider

\mathcal{Y}_{t}=\exp_{q}(X)-\int_{t}^{u}\mathcal{Z}_{s}dB_{s}.

(18)

We apply Itô formula to

17

This representation is already presented in
[

In this work, we are interested in risk evaluation of losses exceeding a given level of severity
\beta\geq 0

17

For this purpose, we define the

In particular, we see that, for a fixed

4
Generalized shortfall approach

Keeping our intent to provide families of risk measures capturing horizon risk and cash non-additivity and motivated by the fact that the classical entropic risk measure can be regarded as a shortfall risk measure (see
[
17

), we study this approach and provide the necessary generalizations to achieve the goal.

We recall that a
dynamic shortfall risk measure

\rho^{U,B}_{t}

\rho^{U,B}_{t}(X)=\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\{m_{t}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t}):E[\left.U(X+m_{t})\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\geq B_{t}\},\quad X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}),

and a target

\mathcal{F}_{t}

Here we set
\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\,\emptyset=+\infty
and
\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\,L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=-\infty

Observe that whenever
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u})

-\infty\leq E[U(X)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq U(E[X|\mathcal{F}_{t}])<+\infty,

by Jensen inequality.
See
[
17
]
for the static case on
L^{\infty}
and see
[
12
]
for the dynamic case on

(in the context of systemic risk).
Dynamic shortfall risk measures can be equivalently formulated in terms of acceptance sets

\mathcal{A}^{U,B}_{t}\triangleq\{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}):E[\left.U(X)\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\geq B_{t}\}

as

\rho^{U,B}_{t}(X)=\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\{m_{t}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t}):X+m_{t}\in\mathcal{A}^{U,B}_{t}\},\quad X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}).

Note that, in the sequel, we will assume that the target
B_{t}
is
a priori
not related to
U
and it is deterministic.
To tackle horizon risk we introduce the following definition.

Definition 10

A fully-dynamic shortfall risk measure is the family
(\rho^{U,B}_{tu})_{t,u}
:

\rho^{U,B}_{tu}(X)=\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\{m_{t}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t}):E[\left.U_{u}(X+m_{t})\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\geq B_{tu}\},\quad X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}),

generated by the families of real numbers

and of mappings

with concave and non-decreasing

U_{u}:[-\infty,+\infty)\longrightarrow[-\infty,+\infty)

f(y,m)=U^{-1}\left(\tilde{U}(y)-\tilde{U}(-m)+B\right),\quad y,m\in\mathbb{R}.

(41)

Note that, taking
U(x)=x
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, (
41
) becomes

f(y,m)=\tilde{U}(y)-\tilde{U}(-m)+B,\quad\mbox{ for any }y,m\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.
In view of (
28
)-(
29
), the certainty equivalent risk measure coincides with the generalized shortfall if and only if, for all
m
,

\mathcal{A}^{\rho^{\tilde{U}},m}=\mathcal{A}^{U,f,B;m},

(42)

namely,

\{Y\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{T}):\tilde{g}(Y)\geq 0\}=\{Y\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{T}):g(Y)\geq 0\},

where
\tilde{g}(Y)=E[\tilde{U}(Y)-\tilde{U}(-m)]
and
g(Y)=E[U(f(Y,m))-B]
.

If (
40
) holds, it is immediate to see that (
42
) is true.
Conversely,
if (
42
) holds then

\mathcal{A}^{\rho^{\tilde{U}},m}\>=\>\mathcal{A}^{\rho^{\tilde{U}},m}\cap\mathcal{A}^{U,f,B;m}\>=\>\mathcal{A}^{U,f,B;m}.

Also

\mathcal{A}^{\rho^{\tilde{U}},m}\cap\mathcal{A}^{U,f,B;m}=\{Y\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{T}):\min(\tilde{g}(Y),g(Y))\geq 0\}.

Hence, for
Y\in\mathcal{A}^{\rho^{\tilde{U}},m}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\rho^{\tilde{U}},m}\cap\mathcal{A}^{U,f,B;m}
,
we have that

\tilde{g}(Y)\geq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\min(\tilde{g}(Y),g(Y))\geq 0,

thus
\tilde{g}(Y)=\min(\tilde{g}(Y),g(Y))
.
Similarly, we get
g(Y)=\min(\tilde{g}(Y),g(Y))
, for
Y\in\mathcal{A}^{U,f,B;m}
.
Hence
\tilde{g}(Y)=g(Y)
. The relationship holds for all
Y
and
m
, thus we have (
40
).

Due to the assumptions on
U
and on

•

f
is non-decreasing in
y

;

•

•

From a dynamic perspective,
[
18
]
introduced the conditional certainty equivalent risk measure. See also
[
24
]
for a further study on intertemporal preferences.
Here we provide the fully-dynamic version of the conditional certainty equivalent risk measures.

Definition 26

A fully-dynamic certainty equivalent risk measure is the family
(\rho^{\tilde{U}_{u}}_{tu})_{t,u}
with
\rho^{\tilde{U}_{u}}_{tu}
solution (if it exists and is unique
P
-a.s.) of

\tilde{U}_{u}(-\rho^{\tilde{U}_{u}}_{tu}(X))=E[\tilde{U}_{u}(X)|\mathcal{F}_{t}],\quad X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}),0\leq t\leq u,

(43)

for
(\tilde{U}_{u})_{u}
of concave non-decreasing (non-trivial) functions
\tilde{U}_{u}:[-\infty,+\infty)\rightarrow[-\infty,+\infty)
.
Hence,

\rho_{tu}^{\tilde{U}_{u}}(X)\triangleq-\tilde{U}_{u}^{-1}\left(E[\tilde{U}_{u}(X)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\right),\quad\mbox{ for any }X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}),0\leq t\leq u,

(44)

when
\tilde{U}_{u}(\cdot)
is strictly increasing.

As in Proposition
25
, we can study the relation between conditional certainty equivalent and generalized shortfall risk measures.

Proposition 27

The fully-dynamic certainty equivalent risk measure (
43
) with
(\tilde{U}_{u})_{u}
is equal to the h-generalized shortfall (
36
) with
(U_{u},f_{u},B_{tu})_{t,u}
if an only if

B_{tu}=B_{0u},\quad t\in[0,u],

(45)

and

U_{u}(f_{u}(y,m))-B_{0u}=\tilde{U}_{u}(y)-\tilde{U}_{u}(-m),\quad y,m\in\mathbb{R},u\in[0,T].

(46)

Furthermore, when
U_{u}
is invertible, this reduces to

f_{u}(y,m)=U_{u}^{-1}\left(\tilde{U}_{u}(y)-\tilde{U}_{u}(-m)+B_{0u}\right),\quad y,m\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.
The argument is similar to Proposition
25
with the natural adjustments to the dynamic setting.
Additionally, we see that we obtain the equation

U_{u}(f_{u}(y,m))-B_{tu}=\tilde{U}_{u}(y)-\tilde{U}_{u}(-m),

which must hold for any
t\leq u
. Thus (
45
) follows together with (
46
).

Proposition 28
(horizon longevity)

Given a family
(\tilde{U}_{u})_{u}
of concave and strictly increasing functions
\tilde{U}_{u}(\cdot)
, the associated fully-dynamic certainty equivalent risk measure satisfies horizon longevity whenever
(\tilde{U}_{u})_{u}
is non-increasing in
u
.

Proof.
From (
44
), it holds that

\displaystyle\rho_{tu}^{\tilde{U}_{u}}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tilde{U}_{u}^{-1}\left(E[\tilde{U}_{u}(X)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\tilde{U}_{u}^{-1}\left(E[\tilde{U}_{v}(X)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\tilde{U}_{v}^{-1}\left(E[\tilde{U}_{v}(X)|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{tv}^{\tilde{U}_{v}}(X),

for
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u})
and
0\leq t\leq u\leq v
. Horizon longevity is then verified.

6
Hq-entropic on losses as a h-generalized shortfall

As conclusion to this work, it is natural to study if the hq-entropic risk measure on losses has relationship with h-generalized shortfall and fully-dynamic certainty equivalent risk measures. We recall that

\rho_{tu}^{hq}(X)=\ln_{q}E\left[\left.\exp_{q}\left((X+\beta)^{-}+\alpha_{q}+A(t,u)\right)\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right],

with
A(t,u)\geq 0
, for all
t\leq u
, increasing in
u
, see (
20
).

Theorem 29

The hq-entropic risk measure on losses is a h-generalized shortfall with representation via
(U_{u},f_{u},B_{tu})_{t,u}
satisfying:

U_{u}(f_{u}(y,m))-B_{tu}=\exp_{q}(m)-\exp_{q}\left((y+\beta)^{-}+\alpha_{q}+A(t,u)\right),\qquad y,m\in\mathbb{R}.

(47)

In particular, we can choose
U_{u}(x)=x
and

\displaystyle f_{u}(y,m)

\displaystyle=B_{tu}+\exp_{q}(m)-\exp_{q}((y+\beta)^{-}+\alpha_{q}+A(t,u))

(48)

\displaystyle=B_{tu}+\exp_{q}(m)\left[1-\exp_{q}\left(\frac{(y+\beta)^{-}+\alpha_{q}+A(t,u)-m}{1+(1-q)m}\right)\right]\qquad y,m\in\mathbb{R},

for some target
(B_{tu})_{t,u}
.

Proof.
We consider the acceptance sets of the cash non-additive risk measure (
38
). We compare

\mathcal{A}_{tu}^{\rho^{hq},m_{t}}=\big\{Y\>:\rho_{tu}^{hq}(Y)\leq m_{t}\big\}.

and

\mathcal{A}_{tu}^{U,f,B;m_{t}}=\{Y\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}):E[U_{u}(f_{u}(Y,m_{t}))|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\geq B_{tu}\}.

Observe that the relation
\rho_{tu}^{hq}(Y)\leq m_{t}
is actually

\ln_{q}E\left[\left.\exp_{q}\left((Y+\beta)^{-}+\alpha_{q}+A(t,u)\right)\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq m_{t}=\ln_{q}\tilde{m}_{t}\quad(\tilde{m}_{t}\geq 0),

which corresponds to

E\left[\left.\exp_{q}\left((Y+\beta)^{-}+\alpha_{q}+A(t,u)\right)\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq\tilde{m}_{t}.

A sufficient condition to have

\mathcal{A}_{tu}^{\rho^{hq},\tilde{m}_{t}}=\big\{Y\>:E\left[\left.\exp_{q}\left((Y+\beta)^{-}+\alpha_{q}+A(t,u)\right)\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\leq\tilde{m}_{t}\big\}=\mathcal{A}_{tu}^{U,f,B;\tilde{m}_{t}}

is then that for some
(U_{u},f_{u},B_{tu})_{t,u}
, the relationship

U_{u}(f_{u}(y,m))-B_{tu}=\exp_{q}(m)-\exp_{q}\left((y+\beta)^{-}+\alpha_{q}+A(t,u)\right)

holds.

Note that
(U_{u}\circ f_{u}-B_{tu})_{u}
is monotonically non-increasing in
u
, which is coherent with the representation of a shortfall risk measure with h-longevity, see Proposition
15
.

Remark 30

The hq-entropic risk measure on losses is not a fully-dynamic certainty equivalent risk measure.
For this, we refer to Proposition
27
and we can see that, the two relationships
(
47
) and (
46
) cannot be the same for all
y,m\in\mathbb{R}
even in the cases
\beta=0
,
\alpha_{q}=0
, and
A\equiv 0
.

defined (
34
).

Remark 31

Consider now, for simplicity, the static case where
t=0
and
u=T
.
The
(U,f)
with
U(x)=x
and
f

48

18
