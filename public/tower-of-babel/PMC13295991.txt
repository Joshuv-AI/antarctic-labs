Exploring the mechanism of reducing the risk of falls in knee osteoarthritis patients by using Baduanjin based on the functional connection of vestibular network: a study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Han Zhang, Liwei Chen, Jing Chen, Xingchen Zhou, Shiqi Wang, Zesen Zhuang, Yingqi Lu, Yiwen Wan, Shangjie Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionFalls are a major global public health challenge and a leading cause of severe consequences, such as fractures, traumatic brain injuries and even death among older adults. Older adults with knee osteoarthritis (KOA) are at a higher risk of falling and tend to experience more severe injuries due to impaired balance. Baduanjin, a mind-body exercise, has been shown to improve lower limb muscle strength and balance in older adults and to reduce fall risk in those with KOA; however, its specific mechanisms remain unclear. Therefore, this study aims to examine the effectiveness of Baduanjin in reducing fall risk among older adults with KOA and to preliminarily elucidate its underlying mechanisms.Methods and analysisIn this prospective trial, 72 KOA participants will be recruited and randomly assigned (1:1:1) to perform Baduanjin training, brisk walking training and health education. The health education group will conduct health education for the participants in the group once a month, a total of 3 times. Based on the health education group, the Baduanjin group and the brisk walking group were respectively subjected to 12 weeks of Baduanjin exercise intervention (40 min each, 3 times a week) and 12 weeks of brisk walking exercise intervention (60 min each, 3 times a week). The primary outcome will be the fall-risk self-assessment questionnaire and modified falls efficacy scale. The secondary outcome will be balance function test, gait subscale of the Tinetti performance-oriented mobility assessment, lower limb muscle strength and functional MRI (fMRI). In addition to the fMRI scan, the results will be measured before and after the intervention. Other primary and secondary outcomes will be measured at baseline, at week 6 at week 12 (at the end of the trial) and after an additional 12-week follow-up period. The linear mixed model will be used to observe the effect of intervention.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Bao'an District People’s Hospital, Shenzhen (No. BYL20230811). Our research findings will be published in peer-reviewed journal articles and conference presentations.Trial registration numberChiCTR2400081342.

Introduction

According to the latest statistics of the WHO, the average number of falls in the world each year exceeds 35 million, 10% of the elderly fall at least twice a year1 and about 15% of the elderly over the age of 65 in China have fallen at least once in the past year.2 Falling will lead to fracture, dislocation, sprain, concussion and other adverse consequences in the elderly3 4 and even threaten life, which is an important cause of death in the elderly.5 It has been reported that patients with knee osteoarthritis (KOA) are 30% more likely to fall than their healthy peers.6 KOA patients with symptoms such as pain and knee mobility disorders were 39% more likely to fall in the future than asymptomatic KOA patients.7 In addition, pain caused by KOA puts elderly patients at risk for repeated falls.8 It can be seen that patients with KOA have a higher risk of falling and are more harmful, which requires focused research and attention.

The mechanism of fall is relatively complex, in which the vestibular neural network plays a key role, and the vestibular neural network has plasticity.9 By enhancing proprioceptive input function, neuromuscular response and activity ability, balance ability10 can be improved, thus reducing the risk of falling.11 Although the vestibular neural network pathway has been found in the laboratory before, its scope and location have not been accurately defined, and it is not until 2020 that the accurate image map of the vestibular neural network has been obtained,12 which has played an important role in promoting the research on the mechanism of fall occurrence. Studies have shown that exercise intervention is the best way to reduce the risk of falls in the elderly.13 Baduanjin is an outstanding representative of traditional Chinese exercise therapy, which has the characteristics of harmonisation of body and mind and co-nourishing of body and spirit. At the same time, Baduanjin movement is simple, compared with other traditional Chinese exercise therapy, with less knee flexion and extension rotation, more suitable for KOA patients to practice and has a large audience. Existing evidence shows that Baduanjin can effectively improve the lower limb muscle strength and activity ability of the elderly, improve balance function and reduce the risk of falls.14

However, the mechanism by which Baduanjin reduces the risk of falls in patients with KOA is currently unclear. Prior research has revealed that in the brain network of KOA patients receiving acupuncture at Taixi Point, the strongest connection is between the hippocampus and caudate nucleus. This is followed by the connections between the insula and putamen of the brain, the orbital prefrontal cortex and the hippocampus, and the middle temporal gyrus and orbital prefrontal cortex.15 Furthermore, the Baduanjin study on older adults with mild cognitive impairment discovered that this training had a regulating effect on the functional connections of brain regions related to vestibular neural pathways in patients with mild cognitive impairment.1618 These regions included the right insula, amygdala, right fusiform gyrus, right anterior cuneus and right inferior parietal marginal angular gyrus. Although these current studies mainly focus on acupuncture intervention of KOA brain network changes and the functional connectivity of vestibular nerve pathway-related brain regions in the elderly with mild cognitive impairment and do not directly focus on the brain network mechanism of Baduanjin intervention in KOA patients with falls, some studies have found that the functional connectivity of the supratemporal-right anterior motor area in patients with mild cognitive impairment is significantly reduced.19 This can be a major manifestation of impaired vestibular network function in patients, negatively affecting self-perception and motor planning, resulting in reduced balance and an increased risk of falls in patients. In addition, connections between the vestibular cortex and other sensory brain areas were higher in young adults than in older adults during vestibular stimulation, and vestibular connectivity increased from the resting state to the stimulation test.20 This suggests that vestibular neural networks are a potential pathway for improving balance. Therefore, can Baduanjin improve the lower limb muscle strength and balance function of KOA patients, enhance proprioceptive input and then change the connection pattern of vestibular neural network function and ultimately achieve the purpose of reducing the fall risk of elderly KOA patients? At present, there is a lack of research. Therefore, we intend to conduct a prospective randomised controlled trial to evaluate the impact of 3-month Baduanjin training on fall risk in elderly patients with KOA so as to verify the intervention effect of Baduanjin training on fall risk in elderly patients and explore the vestibular neural network mechanism of Baduanjin reducing fall risk in elderly patients with KOA from the perspective of brain functional connectivity.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The design of this study will be a three-arm RCT in a 1:1:1 allocation ratio with allocation concealment and assessor blinding. Random assignment will be used to assign 72 eligible participants to three different groups: the Baduanjin group will receive 40 min of exercise per week for 12 weeks, the Fast Walking group will receive 60 min of exercise per week for 12 weeks, and the Health Education group will receive 12 weeks of health education (once per month, three times total). Apart from the MRI scans that will be examined both before and after the intervention, we will assess other primary and secondary measures at baseline, 6 and 12 weeks (after the intervention ends), and then again for a further 12 weeks following the intervention. Diagnostic criteria

The diagnostic criteria of KOA refer to the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Osteoarthritis recommended by the Chinese Branch of Osteology (2018 edition)21: (1) Recurrent knee pain within the past 1 month. (2) Radiographs (standing or weight-bearing position) show narrowing of the joint space, subchondral sclerosis and/or cystic degeneration, and osteophyte formation at the joint margin. (3) Joint fluid (at least two times) clear, viscous, white cell count <2.0 × 10⁹/L. (4) Middle-aged and elderly patients (≥40 years old). (5) Morning stiffness: ≤3 min. (6) There is bone friction sound (feeling).

Note: a combination of clinical, laboratory and X-ray tests that meet criteria (1) + (2) or (1) + (3) + (5) + (6) or (1) + (4) + (5) + (6) can be diagnosed as KOA.

Inclusion criteria

(1) Meeting the above diagnostic criteria, at least one knee joint is diagnosed as KOA, and other diseases that may cause lower limb joint pain are excluded.22 (2) Age 40–70 years old. (3) Bilateral or unilateral knee pain, pain Visual Analogue Scale 2 points, will not affect knee flexion and extension. (4) X-ray examination within 6 months shows that Kellgren-Lawrence classification is grade I or grade II. (5) Sign informed consent voluntarily.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Have a history of knee surgery or are waiting for knee surgery (knee replacement or arthroscopic knee surgery). (2) Visual impairment, hearing impairment or balance impairment caused by other diseases. (3) Have serious acute and chronic organic or psychoneurotic diseases, such as epilepsy or severe anxiety/depression states. (4) Taking drugs that affect skeletal muscle, proprioceptive or postural balance (such as antidepressants, hypnotics, etc).23 (5) Participating in other clinical investigations within the last 3 months. (6) Patients with MRI contraindications. (7) Do not meet the inclusion criteria and be misled. (8) Incomplete clinical data after inclusion affect efficacy and safety judgement. (9) Not according to the requirements of training and impact assessment. (10) Unable to cooperate with the indicators before and after Baduanjin training evaluation. (11) Subjects who participated in exercises other than the subject (such as Tai Chi, aerobics, square dancing) during the study period.

Withdrawal criteria and management

Participants who experience serious adverse events (AEs) during the study and are deemed unsuitable to continue the intervention by the researchers will be considered withdrawn. This includes but is not limited to serious injuries during training, new severe illnesses and major accidental injuries unrelated to the intervention.2 Participants who fail to complete the treatment or training procedures specified in the trial protocol, lose to follow-up and have unknown efficacy will be considered withdrawn.3 Participants who voluntarily request to withdraw from the study.

Additionally, while falls are a key potential AE of interest in this study, a fall itself does not constitute grounds for withdrawal. All fall events, regardless of whether they lead to withdrawal, will be recorded in detail, including time, circumstances, severity of injury, etc, and included in the analysis. Only in cases where a fall results in serious physical harm, such as fractures or traumatic brain injury, and the participant is considered unfit to continue the intervention, will they be withdrawn for safety reasons. If a fall only causes minor injuries such as abrasions or bruises, participants should be encouraged to continue. If a participant insists on withdrawing due to subjective reasons such as fear, it will be treated as a voluntary withdrawal.

In statistical analysis, the intention-to-treat principle will be strictly followed. All randomised participants’ data, including recorded fall events and the last available functional assessment data from withdrawn participants, will be included in the final analysis to minimise information bias due to differential attrition.

Recruitment

We will recruit participants online and offline at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Shenzhen University (The People’s Hospital of Baoan Shenzhen) and its affiliated Community Health Service centres, including recruitment through the Internet, social media software, distribution of leaflets, posters and on-site recruitment. Eligible participants will be prioritised for completion of screening by a community physician at a community health service centre based on inclusion and exclusion criteria to ensure compliance with trial criteria. At the same time, we will arrange a research assistant with relevant experience to communicate with participants who meet the criteria, inform them about the relevant informed consent and sign the informed consent. In addition, the research assistant also conducted a baseline assessment of the participants after they signed the informed consent form.24

Randomisation, allocation concealment and blinding

After baseline assessment, all eligible participants will be randomly assigned to the Baduanjin group, the brisk walking group or the health education group. Simple random assignment sequences will be generated using the PLAN programme of statistical software SAS V.9.0 and managed by independent research assistants who are not involved in recruitment, evaluation and intervention. Independent research assistants will inform eligible participants of their assignment results over the phone. Since this trial is a study of non-drug intervention, we cannot blind subjects and sports coaches, but we will blind outcome evaluators and data statisticians.25

Sample size

This study will be a three-arm, multi time point randomised controlled trial. The sample size calculation will be based on the primary endpoint, that is, the differences in fall risk and fall efficacy among the three groups at 12 weeks postintervention. While longitudinal data analysis in this study will employ a linear mixed model, a conservative approach will be taken for the sample size estimation by using a cross-sectional comparison framework with one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) at the primary endpoint time point. Studies have shown that 3-month exercise interventions can improve balance ability and motor function in older adults.26 However, existing three-arm RCTs comparing Baduanjin, brisk walking and health education for fall prevention in older adults are scarce, and no published results are currently available. Therefore, based on the meta-analysis by Jones et al,27 Baduanjin exercise significantly reduces fall risk in older adults (SMD=−0.59, 95% CI −0.83 to −0.35). We will use the absolute value of this effect and conservatively estimating SMD as 0.65, and applying Cohen’s conversion formula (f=d/2), Cohen’s f will be approximated as 0.33, which will be conservatively rounded up to f=0.40 considering the three-arm design. In GPower V.3.1.9.7, an F-test (ANOVA, fixed effects, overall comparison of three groups) will be set with an effect size f=0.40, α=0.05 and 80% power, yielding a total sample size of 60 participants (20 per group). Accounting for a 20% attrition rate, the final sample size will be set at 24 participants per group, totalling 72 across all three groups.

Intervention

Health education group

Subjects will only receive health education, including the basic knowledge of KOA management, factors affecting falls, the hazards of falls, common ways to prevent falls, etc.,28 and will be asked to maintain their original activity habits. At the same time, in order to exclude the occurrence of other exercise bias, subjects will be asked to avoid participating in other regular exercise.

Baduanjin group

On the basis of health education, Baduanjin Group will follow the standard of ‘Fitness Qigong Baduanjin’ issued by the General Administration of Sport of China in 200329 and join the Baduanjin exercise training for 12 weeks, 3 days a week, 40 min a day, including 10 min of warm-up and 30 min of Baduanjin exercise.30 31 In addition, a coach with more than 5 years of experience in the teaching of Baduanjin will serve as a training guide for participants to prevent AEs. The training programme is consistent with the previous plan description, consisting of 10 postures, including preparation, two hands supporting the Tianli sanjiao, a left and right bow like shooting eagle, conditioning the spleen and stomach must with single lift, looking back, shaking the head and tail to the heart fire, two hands climbing feet to strengthen the kidney waist, saving fists and angry eyes to increase strength, and behind the seven diseases, winding down. Baduanjin sports training will be conducted in Shenzhen Community Health Center (Dalang Community Center, Xingdong Community Center or Jian 'an Community Center), with 30–40 people in each centre.

Brisk walking group

On the basis of health education, the walking group will increase the walking training. Before the intervention, the participants will do brisk walking exercises under the guidance of the coach and then practice at home by themselves after learning. The project leader will call or use WeChat to urge the participants to train as required before the weekly intervention, and the participants self-reported the training time. The brisk walking group will train for 12 weeks, three times a week, for 60 min each time, including 15 min of warm-up, 40 min of brisk walking and 5 min of tidying. Brisk walking training requires that subjects walk 120–140 steps per minute, maintain a heart rate of 55%–75% of the maximum heart rate (206.9–0.67* age), meet the requirements of moderate exercise intensity, and feel good and relaxed or free from dizziness, nausea and fatigue.16

Outcome assessment

The variables in this experiment include basic information, primary indicators, and secondary indicators. Basic characteristics will be measured at baseline (1–2 weeks before random assignment). In addition to MRI scans, which will be measured before and after the intervention, other primary and secondary measures will be measured at baseline, in the middle of the intervention (6 weeks after randomisation), at the end of the trial (12 weeks after randomisation), and after an additional 12-week follow-up period. Basic information

Participants’ demographic characteristics (such as sex, age, education, marital status, living arrangements, occupation, exercise frequency, socioeconomic status) and disease history (such as past history, family medical history, adverse habits, drug use) will be collected by recruiters using self-designed questionnaires and completed prior to randomisation.

Primary outcomes

Fall risk

The fall-risk self-assessment questionnaire (FRQ) will be used for assessment, which is a 12-item short questionnaire designed specifically for elderly people living in the community to assess their own fall risk.32 The total score is 14 points, and the higher the score, the higher the fall risk. The total score of FRQ will be obtained by adding the questions answered by the participants, in which questions 1 and 5 are 2 points and others are 1 point. Rubenstein et al33 believe that it has a strong consistency with clinical assessment and is an effective and reliable tool to assess the fall risk of the elderly.34

Fall efficacy

The modified falls efficacy scale (MFES) will be used to evaluate the fall efficacy. It comprises a total of 14 items, each item is rated with 1 to 10 points according to the level of grasp, and the higher the score, the better the fall efficiency.35 According to Hasan et al,36 it shows excellent internal consistency, a retest reliability score, and a strong correlation with the International fall efficacy and activity-specific balance confidence scale.

Secondary outcomes

Balance function test

A plantar pressure sensor will be used to accurately plot the plantar centre of gravity deviation trajectory of subjects in four postures: bipedal eyes open, bipedal eyes closed, monopodial eyes open and monopodial eyes closed. By measuring the total track length of shaking (mm), peripheral area (mm2), track length per unit area, X-axis mean centre change (mm), Y-axis mean centre change (mm), Romberg’s coefficient, etc. The visual adjustment coefficient, balance stability coefficient and proprioception coefficient will be analysed and the influence degree of vision, vestibular and proprioception on balance will be evaluated, and the balance index of subjects will be finally obtained. The visual adjustment coefficient reflects the role of vision in postural control. The greater the value, the greater the role of vision in postural control. Conversely, in the absence of visual feedback, the balance function of the body will become worse. The balance stability coefficient reflects the stability of the body; the greater the coefficient, the smaller the degree of shaking, the better the stability of the body. The proprioceptive coefficient reflects the function of proprioception in posture control. The larger the coefficient, the stronger the proprioceptive posture adjustment ability and the easier it is to maintain balance. The test will be performed using a dynamic and static balance assessment analyser (Model: Bismarck Super Balance).

Gait assessment

The gait subscale of the Tinetti performance-oriented mobility assessment will be used for the assessment. Subscale with seven items below: gait initiation, step length, step symmetry, step continuity, path, trunk and walking position. The total score is 12 points, and the higher the score, the higher the score and the better the mobility and balance.37

Lower limb muscle strength assessment

Lower limb muscle strength will be assessed by the 5 Times Chair Stand (5STS) and the 30 s Sit-to-Stand Test (30STS). The chair will be prepared, adjusted to the participant’s corresponding height and fixed on the wall. After the researchers explained the precautions, participants will be asked to put their feet on the ground, cross their arms in front of their chest, and place each hand on the opposite shoulder, then stand up and sit down five times as quickly as possible without using upper body strength, and record the time required for the participants to complete. Participants will then be asked to repeat as many seconds of action from sitting to standing as possible in 30 s and record the maximum number of completions. The minimum value of the three trials of 5STS and the maximum of the two trials of 30STS will be the scores of the participants considered. The trial will be terminated if the participant is unable to execute the motion or request a suspension of the trial.38 39

Functional MRI scan

To further explore the potential mechanism by which Baduanjin reduces the risk of falls in KOA patients. This study will collect resting state functional MRI (fMRI) data of participants before and after the intervention. fMRI scans will be performed at the Guangdong MRI Center and Multimodal Laboratory. The Siemens Prisma 3.0T MRI system and the Siemens 64-channel head and neck coil were used to acquire fMRI data. The scanning parameters of fMRI in resting state are as follows: TR=2000 ms, TE=30 ms, inverse angle=90°, layer thickness=3.6 mm, FOV=230 mm × 230 mm, matrix=64×64, voxel size=3.6×3.6×3.6 mm3, number of layers=37, axial layers=35. Number of AD phases per position=240.

Safety measurements

The incidence and severity of AEs will be taken as safety indicators, and any AE that occurs during the study will be truthfully recorded in the case report form, including symptoms, occurrence time, duration, treatment procedures, etc. The causal relationship with Baduanjin intervention and the severity of AE will be evaluated. Serious AEs will be reported to the Ethics Committee.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS V.26.0 software, with a two-sided significance level of α=0.05. Descriptive statistics will present categorical variables as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables as mean±SD or median and IQR, based on normality assessed by the Shapiro-Wilk test. The primary analysis for longitudinal outcomes will employ a linear mixed model with fixed effects for group, time and their interaction, plus a random intercept per participant. Secondary analyses will use ANOVA for normally distributed data or the Kruskal-Wallis test for non-normally distributed data. As a sensitivity analysis to verify the robustness of the primary findings, a repeated-measures ANOVA will be conducted on the per-protocol set with complete follow-up data. Categorical safety outcomes will be analysed using the χ² test.

Data collection and management

This study will be randomised using the Clinical Trial Management Public Platform (ResMan) from the Chinese Clinical Trials Registry, and participants will be evenly assigned to each group according to a ratio of 1:1:1. After the study is completed, the data will also be made public and disseminated through the website.

Ethics and dissemination

This study was conducted in accordance with the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Bao’an District People’s Hospital, Shenzhen (Approval No.: BYL20230811). The study protocol has publicly registered and accessible via the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (registration identifier: ChiCTR2400081342; URL: www.chictr.org.cn). All participants will receive detailed information regarding the study purpose, procedures, potential benefits and risks, their right to withdraw at any time without penalty, and the confidentiality of personal data prior to enrolment. Written informed consent will be obtained from each participant (online supplemental material 1). The consent process, including a full explanation of the trial, will be conducted by trained investigators at the community health service centres.

The findings will be disseminated through presentations at local, national and international conferences focusing on cognitive rehabilitation, and will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals. Key outcomes will also be shared broadly with researchers, healthcare professionals, service providers and the public via training courses, seminars and online platforms, irrespective of the magnitude or direction of the observed effects.

Patient and public involvement

This study is a research protocol for a randomised controlled trial. Patients or the public were not involved in the design of this initial protocol. However, we plan to involve patients with KOA in the dissemination phase of the research. Specifically, we will collaborate with patient representatives to develop lay summaries of the final research findings, which will be distributed to participants and relevant patient communities to ensure the results are accessible and impactful.

Discussion

Baduanjin belongs to the category of guiding techniques of traditional Chinese medicine, which contains profound traditional cultural connotation and health preservation and rehabilitation value. Baduanjin emphasises the common coordination of mind, qi, body and heart and requires dynamic and static retraction and release freely in the process of movement and the integration and adjustment of body and mind. In addition, we should pay attention to the cooperation of mind and qi to guide the activities of body. Outside, through the movement of the limbs, improve the muscles, bones, and flesh while relaxing the body and mind in order to achieve the effect of adjusting the body and improving consciousness. Baduanjin is completed on the basis of holding the ball pile, and the multiopposition stretching action can enhance the lower limb muscle strength of the practitioner. The movement is coherent and smooth, and the ability of spatial conversion and orientation can be enhanced in the process of movement transformation. The whole set of movements is completed in beautiful and gentle classical music, which can mobilise the participation of multiple senses such as vision, hearing and proprioception. More and more studies have shown that Baduanjin exercise can significantly improve the limb strength of the elderly, improve their posture control ability, joint flexibility and maintain the body’s balance and stability.26 40 At the same time, Baduanjin has the potential effect of regulating the functional connection of the vestibular neural network. It can regulate the brain network, alleviate the clinical symptoms of KOA, improve the quality of life, and is suitable for widespread development in KOA patients. However, most current studies are based on the brain network mechanism of Baduanjin in alleviating KOA pain rather than the brain network mechanism of improving balance and thus reducing the risk of falls in KOA patients.

Therefore, we will verify the effects of Baduanjin training on fall risk and balance function in elderly KOA patients through this randomised controlled trial. If the successful completion of the experiment shows meaningful results, it will help to provide a scientific basis for this study on the effect of Baduanjin training on the fall risk of KOA patients, and it will also be conducive to clinical promotion and application and patients to continue to practice after discharge.

Strengths and limitations

This study has the following strengths: (1) The research is innovative, exploring the mechanism of Baduanjin’s impact on fall risk in older adults from the perspective of vestibular function-related brain network connectivity. (2) A standardised operational manual was developed based on the Chinese Guidelines for Fall Prevention in the Elderly. The intervention is uniformly delivered by nationally certified fitness coaches, ensuring reproducibility. If the results are positive, the protocol can be directly translated into a standard community-based health management programme for older adults. (3) The assessment system is comprehensive and multidimensional, integrating objective functional tests, neuroimaging indicators and patient-reported outcomes, allowing for a more holistic evaluation of the intervention effects.

In addition, this study has the following limitations: (1) Potential bias in self-reported outcomes: The primary outcome measures (FRQ and MFES) are self-report scales. As behavioural interventions cannot implement blinding for participants and instructors, and the control group receives health education (a non-active control), expectation bias and performance bias may occur during implementation, which should be cautiously considered in the interpretation of results. (2) Single-centre recruitment limitation: Recruitment is limited to communities within a single city, and participants are required to be eligible for fMRI scans (eg, no metal implants, no claustrophobia). This may restrict the representativeness of the sample and limit the generalisability of the findings to a broader elderly population, such as those living in rural areas or with severe cognitive impairment or poor physical health.
