Homogeneity of the Lévy collapse from the perspective of Fraïssé theory
Ziemowit Kostana

Abstract
. Given a strongly inaccessible cardinal λ \lambda , we study the Fraïssé class of all Boolean algebras of size < λ <\lambda , together with regular embeddings. We prove that this is indeed a Fraïssé class, and its limit has the same completion as the Lévy collapse. We also give a direct proof that the collapsing algebra of density κ \kappa is not the union of a κ \kappa -chain of regular sub-algebras of density < κ <\kappa .



Keywords:
Fraisse theory, homogeneous structures, regular sub-algebras, collapsing algebra, Levy collapse

Introduction

0.1.
Fraïssé-Jónsson theory

The classical Fraïssé theory studies relations between certain classes of finite structures, called
Fraïssé classes
, and countable structures that are homogeneous with respect to finite substructures. However, since the original paper of Fraïssé
[
5
]
, the theory received a large number of far-reaching generalizations. The category-theoretic framework for Fraïssé theory was introduced by Droste and Göbel
[
3
]

[
4
]
, and was further generalized by Kubiś
[
15
]
. The theory originally developed in the language of models has became an influential technology in general topology
[
8
]
, topological dynamics
[
12
]
, theory of Polish groups
[
20
]

[
12
]
, or functional analysis
[
6
]

[
1
]
. The article
[
17
]
is an excellent survey of what is known about homogeneous structures and their automorphism groups, and
[
13
]
contains an extensive introduction to the study of topological properties of these groups.

The theory of uncountable Fraïssé limits is often called
Fraïssé-Jónsson theory
, as Jónsson was the first to generalize the original work of Fraïssé to the uncountable case. Perhaps the most remarkable instance of Fraïssé-Jónsson theory is Parovičenko Theorem, stating that under Continuum Hypothesis
\mathcal{P}(\omega)/\operatorname{Fin}
is the unique Boolean algebra of size continuum that has Strong Countable Separation Property
[
18
]
.

A streamlined, model-theoretic exposition of the classical Fraïssé theory can be found in
[
7
]
.

0.2.
Collapsing algebras

Given an uncountable cardinal
\kappa
, the
collapsing algebra

\operatorname{Coll}(\omega,\kappa)
is the unique complete Boolean algebra of density
\kappa
that collapses
\kappa
to a countable ordinal. It has many interesting properties. For example, by a theorem of Solovay, it is countably generated as a complete Boolean algebra (
[
14
]
, p.191), showing that countably generated complete Boolean algebras can be arbitrarily large. Also, every Boolean algebra embeds into some collapsing algebra as a regular sub-algebra. Any collapsing algebra can be characterized up to isomorphism by certain non-distributivity condition. This characterization, and some further properties can be found in
[
14
]
.

0.3.
Lévy collapse

The
Lévy collapse
is a classical forcing notion, used for collapsing strongly inaccessible cardinals to "small" cardinals, like
\omega_{1}
or
\omega_{2}
. One of the most spectacular effects of Lévy-collapsing a strongly inaccessible cardinal to
\omega_{1}
is making all projective sets Lebesgue-measurable
[
2
]
. Collapsing a strongly inaccessible cardinal to
\omega_{2}
kills all Kurepa trees (
[
19
]
,
[
9
]
Thm. 27.9). Somewhat less known application of the Lévy collapse is forcing that every compact Hausdorff space of weight
\omega_{1}
has size
\omega_{1}
or
2^{\omega_{1}}
, although in this case the forcing is the product of the Lévy collapse with the generalized Cohen forcing
[
11
]
.

Given a strongly inaccessible cardinal
\lambda
, we study the class of all Boolean algebras of size less than
\lambda
– equipped with regular embeddings – from the perspective of Fraïssé-Jónsson theory. We denote this class by
\Bool_{\lambda}
. We also consider the class
\BoolSeq_{\lambda}
, consisting of all Boolean algebras that are unions of increasing
\lambda
-chains of regular sub-algebras. For precise definitions, see Definition
2.7
. The main results of the paper are:

A.

\Bool_{\lambda}
is a Fraïssé class, and its limit has the same completion as the Lévy collapse of
\lambda
to
\omega_{1}
.

B.

The automorphism group of the Lévy collapse is a universal group in the class of groups
\operatorname{Aut}(B)
, for
B\in\BoolSeq_{\lambda}
.

We also give a new proof of the fact that
\operatorname{Coll}(\omega,\kappa)
is not in the class
\BoolSeq_{\kappa}
. This fact itself is rather standard, but the argument relies on the preservation of
\kappa
-chain condition by finite support iterations. We give a direct proof that still might be of some interest.

Main definitions and notation

The paper is self-contained with respect to Fraïssé theory. Formulations of most of the theorems base only on basic model-theoretic notions, however proofs often rely on forcing arguments. All relevant notions from the theory of Boolean algebras can be found in the following short introduction, or in

1.1.
Partial orders

All partial orders under consideration are separative. For a partial order
P
, we denote by
\overline{P}
the
completion
of
P
– the unique complete Boolean algebra containing
P
as a dense subset. The completion of

can be realized as the Boolean algebra of regular open subsets of
P
, in some specific topology. The former universal property is however more relevant, than what the elements of
\overline{P}
are specifically.

1.2.
Regular Boolean sub-algebras

A subset
R\subseteq A
of a Boolean algebra
A
is
dense
if for every
a\in A\setminus\{0\}
there exists
r\in R\setminus\{0\}
such that
r\leq a
. The least size of a dense subset of
A
is called the
density
of
A
, and denoted
\dens(A)
.

A sub-algebra
A\subseteq B
is
regular
if every maximal antichain in
A
is also a maximal antichain in
B
. We write
A\sqsubseteq B
. An embedding of Boolean algebras is called a
regular embedding
if its image is a regular sub-algebra. Regular embeddings are similar to
complete embeddings
. An embedding

is
complete
if
A
is a complete algebra, and for each subset
S\subseteq A

\bigvee e[S]=e[\bigvee S],\;\bigwedge e[S]=e[\bigwedge S].

The difference is that the expressions
\bigvee S
and
\bigwedge S
might be undefined in non-complete algebras. We define a regular suborder of a partial order the same way, and also write
P\sqsubseteq R
. We will be frequently applying the following simple and well-known consequence of the Sikorski’s Extension Criterion.

Proposition 1.1
.

Any regular embedding
e:A\longrightarrow B
extends uniquely to a complete embedding

\bar{e}:\overline{A}\longrightarrow\overline{B}
.

Forcing arguments

In the proof of Theorem

Proposition 4.1
