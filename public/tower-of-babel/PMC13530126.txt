Schleyer hyperconjugative aromaticity as an efficient strategy to induce more delocalization in penguinone and thiopenguinone derivatives: a DFT investigation
Authors: Erfan Shafipour, Zohreh Mirjafary, Hamid Saeidian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, the impact of Schleyer hyperconjugative aromaticity on penguinone and thiopenguinone derivatives was systematically investigated using DFT at the B3LYP/6-311 + G(d, p) level. Structural, electronic, and magnetic aromaticity indices were computed to evaluate the delocalization character of the designed systems. The results reveal that bulky electron-donating substituents such as –GeMe3 and –SiMe3 significantly enhance delocalization through electron injection and formation of a pseudo-6π electron system. In contrast, the electron-withdrawing –F substituent reduces electron delocalization and aromatic stabilization. Sulfur-containing derivatives exhibit relatively more delocalization in several cases, attributed to the larger atomic size and lower electronegativity of sulfur compared to oxygen. A strong correlation was observed among most aromaticity indices. Overall, Schleyer hyperconjugation emerges as an effective strategy for inducing delocalization in inherently nonaromatic frameworks.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58077-x.

Introduction

Aromatic compounds represent one of the most fundamental and intriguing classes of organic molecules, characterized by their unusual thermodynamic stability arising from cyclic π-electron delocalization within a conjugated and nearly planar ring framework. The concept of aromaticity plays a central role in understanding molecular structure and reactivity and serves as a cornerstone for the rational design of advanced materials, pharmaceuticals, and functional molecular systems1,2. Beyond the classical Hückel definition of aromaticity, modern extensions such as hyperconjugative aromaticity (HA) have emerged. Within this framework, interaction between adjacent σ-bonds and the ring π-system enables electron density transfer into antibonding π* orbitals, thereby generating an effective pseudo-aromatic system3. This concept was first introduced by Schleyer and co-workers, who demonstrated that electron-donating substituents can inject electron density into the ring and generate an effective 6π electron system, thus inducing delocalization in intrinsically nonaromatic structures4,5. The HA phenomena, has been reported in a variety of nonclassical systems in which σ-bonds donate electron density into adjacent π* orbitals, leading to effective cyclic delocalization6–10. Previous studies have demonstrated that Schleyer-type hyperconjugative interactions provide an effective conceptual and practical framework for engineering and amplifying delocalization character in a wide range of molecular architectures11–14,14.

Despite these advances, the potential for hyperconjugation-induced delocalization in penguinone-type systems has not been systematically explored. Penguinone inherently represents a 4π nonaromatic framework due to the presence of a sp3-hybridized carbon center that disrupts classical cyclic conjugation. To date, no comprehensive computational investigation has examined whether strategic substituent engineering can transform penguinone into a pseudo-6π aromatic system via Schleyer hyperconjugation. Furthermore, the influence of heteroatom substitution (O vs. S) on hyperconjugative efficiency and delocalization modulation remains unaddressed. This gap motivates the present multidimensional DFT study. Penguinone (3,4,4,5-tetramethylcyclohexa-2,5-dien-1-one) is a cyclic ketone that is formally less delocalized due to the presence of an sp³-hybridized carbon within the ring and the existence of only 4π electrons, thereby violating Hückel’s rule. Nevertheless, the presence of adjacent C–C σ-bonds capable of hyperconjugative interaction raises the question of whether more delocalization can be induced through rational substituent engineering (Fig. 1).

In the present work, a series of penguinone and thiopenguinone derivatives bearing electron-donating and electron-withdrawing substituents on carbon-4 were designed to investigate the role of Schleyer hyperconjugation in inducing delocalization (Fig. 2). A comprehensive evaluation of delocalization character was performed using structural, electronic, and magnetic indices within the framework of density functional theory (DFT). This study aims to provide a theoretical strategy for enhancing delocalization in nonaromatic systems and to deepen mechanistic insight into hyperconjugative effects.

Computational details

All quantum chemical calculations were performed using the Gaussian 09 program package15 within the framework of DFT using the B3LYP hybrid exchange–correlation functional in conjunction with the 6–311 + G(d, p) basis set. Molecular structures were constructed and visualized using GaussView 6.0.16 software16. Geometry optimizations for all penguinones (1O–7O) and thiopenguinones (1–7 S) were carried out in the gas phase. Frequency calculations were subsequently performed at the same level of theory to confirm that all optimized structures correspond to true minima on the potential energy surface, as verified by the absence of imaginary frequencies. Thermochemical corrections were evaluated at 298 K. Aromaticity was assessed through a multidimensional approach including structural, electronic, and magnetic criteria: Structural indices: bond length alternation (∆BL), Harmonic Oscillator Model of Aromaticity (HOMA)17, and Bird aromaticity index (BI)18. Electronic index: Para-Delocalization Index (PDI)19. Magnetic indices: NICS(1) and NICS(1)ZZ values computed 1 Å above the ring center20. NICS calculations were performed using the gauge-including atomic orbital (GIAO) method21. Aromaticity indices (∆BL, HOMA, BI, PDI and NICS) were computed using the Multiwfn program22. Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) analysis was carried out to quantify donor–acceptor interactions and evaluate second-order perturbation stabilization energies (E(2)), particularly those associated with lone pair σ → π* interactions contributing to hyperconjugative aromaticity23. The Cartesian coordinates of the optimized structures, along with other related data, are provided in the Supporting Information.

Results and discussion

Investigation of MEP, HOMO and LUMO profiles

A series of penguinones (1O–7O) and thiopenguinones (1–7 S) derivatives were designed and investigated at the B3LYP/6–311 + G(d, p) level of theory. The unsubstituted compounds 1O and 1 S were considered as reference structures to enable systematic comparison of substituent effects on aromaticity. In derivatives 2O and 2 S, a weak electron-donating methyl group (–CH3) was introduced. In contrast, the electron-withdrawing –F substituent was incorporated into 3O and 3 S, which is expected to reduce delocalization by decreasing electron density within the ring. To promote Schleyer hyperconjugative aromaticity, stronger electropositive substituents such as –SiH3 and –GeH3 were introduced in derivatives 4O, 5O and 4 S, 5 S, aiming to inject two π-electrons into the ring and generate a pseudo-6π electron system. Furthermore, bulky and strongly electron-donating substituents –SiMe3 and –GeMe3 were employed in derivatives 6O/6S and 7O/7S to maximize electron injection and enhance hyperconjugative delocalization stabilization.

Molecular electrostatic potential (MEP) maps represent a powerful and widely employed tool in computational chemistry for analyzing electron density distribution, molecular polarity, atomic charge characteristics, and predicting sites susceptible to electrophilic or nucleophilic attack24. The MEP maps of the penguinones (1O–7O) and thiopenguinones (1–7 S) are shown in Fig. 3. In all penguinone structures, the highest concentration of negative electrostatic potential is localized predominantly on the oxygen atom, as evidenced by the red regions. In derivative 3O, incorporation of the electron-withdrawing –F substituent leads to a noticeable reduction in electron density at the oxygen center, reflected by a color shift from deep red toward yellow, indicating attenuation of negative electrostatic potential. Conversely, introduction of electron-donating or bulky electropositive substituents such as –SiMe3 and –GeMe3 significantly enhances electron density at the oxygen atom. This effect is clearly observed in derivatives 4O, 6O, and 7O, where intensified red coloration indicates increased negative electrostatic potential. These electron-rich regions can be identified as preferred sites for electrophilic attack.

Fig. 3Electrostatic potential maps of penguinones (1O–7O) and thiopenguinones (1–7 S).

Electrostatic potential maps of penguinones (1O–7O) and thiopenguinones (1–7 S).

The principal difference between MEP maps of thiopenguinones (1–7 S) and those of the oxygen-based penguinone derivatives (1O-7O) lies in the greater accumulation of negative electrostatic potential on the sulfur atom. This behavior can be attributed to the larger atomic size and higher polarizability of sulfur compared to oxygen, which facilitate more effective electron density distribution and charge accumulation. In the MEP map of derivative 3 S, incorporation of the electron-withdrawing substituent results in depletion of negative charge at the sulfur center, reflected by a color transition from red to yellow, indicating a reduction in negative electrostatic potential. In contrast, derivative 6 S, bearing the bulky electropositive –SiMe3 group, exhibits a pronounced intensification of the red region, corresponding to significant enhancement of negative charge accumulation on the sulfur atom.

Frontier molecular orbitals, namely the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO), constitute key descriptors for assessing chemical stability, reactivity, and electronic properties of molecular systems. The HOMO and LUMO profiles of penguinones (1O–7O) and thiopenguinones (1–7 S) are depicted in Fig. 4. In these orbital representations, green regions generally correspond to the negative phase of the molecular orbital wavefunction, whereas red regions denote the positive phase. It should be emphasized that the color contrast reflects the sign of the wavefunction rather than relative electron density magnitude. In general, the energies of HOMO (electron donor), LUMO (electron acceptor), and the LUMO–HOMO energy gap (ΔEL–H) provide valuable insight into chemical reactivity. A smaller energy gap is typically associated with enhanced reactivity, increased polarizability, and facilitated charge-transfer interactions. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the HOMO orbitals of the studied structures (1O–7O) and (1–7 S) are predominantly localized over the penguinone ring framework, with only minor contributions from the substituents. In contrast, the LUMO orbitals exhibit a broader spatial distribution, with significant phase alternation extending over a larger portion of the molecular skeleton, consistent with the delocalized acceptor character of the π-system. The HOMO orbitals of (1–7 S) exhibit pronounced phase alternation lobes with significant localization around the C = S bond region. This marked concentration indicates substantial contribution of the sulfur atom to the donor orbital and highlights its role in modulating the electronic properties of the ring framework. In contrast, the LUMO orbitals display a more extended and nearly uniform distribution over the entire molecular skeleton of the thiopenguinone derivatives (1–7 S), consistent with the delocalized acceptor character of the π-system in these compounds.

Furthermore, the HOMO and LUMO energy levels of the (1O–7O) and (1–7 S) were calculated at the selected level of theory and are summarized in Table 1. Consistent with frontier molecular orbital theory, the HOMO energy level is higher (less negative) than that of the LUMO. Introduction of electron-donating and bulky electropositive substituents in derivatives 6O/6S and 7O/7S leads to an elevation of the HOMO energy (less negative EHOMO), thereby strengthening the electron-donating character of the system. This effect promotes more efficient electron injection into the ring framework and supports the formation of pseudo-6π hyperconjugative systems. Accordingly, derivatives 6O/6S and 7O, 7 S, bearing bulky –SiMe3 and –GeMe3 substituents, are predicted to exhibit the highest degree of delocalization character based on their elevated EHOMO values. In contrast, derivative 3O and 3 S containing the electron-withdrawing –F substituent displays the most negative EHOMO value, reflecting reduced electron-donating capacity and consequently diminished aromatic character among the designed compounds.

The calculated HOMO–LUMO energy gaps (Eg) do not exhibit a direct correlation with the aromaticity trends observed from structural, magnetic, and electronic aromaticity indices. This behavior indicates that, in the present systems, aromatic stabilization is governed predominantly by hyperconjugative σ→π* electron delocalization rather than by simple frontier orbital gap enlargement. Electron-donating Si- and Ge-containing substituents elevate the HOMO energy and facilitate electron injection into the cyclic π-framework, thereby enhancing pseudo-6π aromaticity even when the HOMO–LUMO gap remains nearly unchanged or slightly decreases.

Although aromaticity cannot be quantified experimentally as a single, directly measurable observable, it can be reliably inferred from a combination of structural, magnetic, and energetic parameters. In this context, DFT calculations provide a powerful framework for indirectly evaluating delocalization behavior through well-established theoretical descriptors. Accordingly, a multidimensional approach incorporating structural, magnetic, and electronic aromaticity indices was employed to assess the delocalization character of compounds (1O–7O) and (1–7 S). The individual indices and their implications are discussed in detail below.

Evaluation of structural parameters of aromaticity

The molecular geometry is one of the primary and most direct indicators for assessing aromaticity. A regular structure with delocalized double bonds, such as in benzene, indicates reduce the non-aromatic character. Various structural indices, including ∆BL, BI, and HOMA, were employed to evaluate the aromaticity of compounds (1O–7O) and (1–7 S), and the results are presented in Table 2. Resonance and π-electron delocalization in aromatic rings result in C–C bond lengths that are intermediate between single and double bonds. In benzene, all C–C bond lengths are approximately 1.388 Å. Accordingly, the difference between the longest and shortest bond lengths in a ring, called the ∆BL index, is used to assess delocalization. A smaller ∆BL value indicates more delocalized system.

Penguinone derivative (1O) has a HOMA of −0.904, indicating less delocalized character. For 1 S, HOMA = −0.558, indicating a less delocalized structure. As expected, the highest delocalization based on HOMA is observed for 7 S. These results demonstrate good agreement between the ∆BL and HOMA indices for the thiopenguinones. The structural aromaticity order for the (1–7 S) derivatives is as follows: 7 S > 5 S > 1 S > 6 S > 4 S > 3 S > 2 S.

Another geometric index, Bird index (BI), was used to assess aromaticity. BI, also called the benzene index, evaluates bond uniformity in cyclic systems. Values range from 0 to 100, with benzene set at the maximum. Higher BI values indicate a more uniform π-electron distribution and stronger aromaticity. BI values for the studied structures are listed in Table 2. Compound 1O has BI = 37.383, while 7O exhibits the highest BI = 43.339, reflecting better π-electron delocalization due to bulky electron-donating –Ge(CH3)3 substituents. Compound 5O follows with BI = 41.443. Ge-containing substituents improve penguinone aromaticity via electron donation and hyperconjugation. Compounds 6O and 4O have BI values of 39.356 and 38.358, respectively, indicating relatively significantly increase delocalization. Substituents –Si(CH3)3 and –Si(H3)3 also enhance delocalization via electron donation and formation of a pseudo-6π system, but their effect is less pronounced than Ge-substituents. Additionally, the BI index was calculated for sulfur-containing penguinone derivatives and the results are also presented in Table 2. For 1 S, BI = 43.718. The highest BI values are obtained for 7 S and 5 S, 50.209 and 48.286, respectively, due to the presence of bulky electropositive –Ge substituents. Based on the ΔBL, HOMA, and BI indices (Table 2), thiopenguinone derivatives (1–7 S) exhibit consistently higher delocalization than their oxygen-containing counterparts (1O–7O). This enhancement cannot be attributed merely to the larger atomic size of sulfur; rather, it arises from its greater polarizability and distinct electronic structure. The systematically higher aromaticity observed for thiopenguinone derivatives (1–7 S) can be rationalized at the orbital interaction level. Replacement of oxygen by sulfur introduces a more polarizable atom with accessible 3p orbitals and reduced electronegativity. This substitution lowers the energy of the π*(C = S) acceptor orbital relative to π*(C = O), resulting in improved energy matching with the donor σ(C–Si) and σ(C–Ge) orbitals of the substituents. Consequently, stronger σ(C–Si/C–Ge) → π*(C = C/C = S) hyperconjugative interactions are established, leading to more efficient electron injection into the cyclic π-framework.

Evaluation of electronic parameters of aromaticity

The primary aim of electronic indices is to measure electron delocalization, which is directly related to the aromaticity of a ring. To this end, several electronic indices such as PDI were employed to assess the aromaticity of compounds (1O–7O) and (1–7 S) (Table 3). The PDI is an important electronic index that evaluates electron delocalization between carbon atoms in para positions. It is defined as the average electron delocalization index (DI) of carbons at positions 1 and 4. Higher PDI values indicate greater electron delocalization, which corresponds to higher aromaticity. The PDI values for penguinones (1O–7O) and thiopenguinones (1–7 S) were computed at the appropriate theoretical level and are presented in Table 3. For penguinone (1O), PDI = 0.024. The highest PDI, 0.027, is observed for 7O, reflecting its more delocalization character. The PDI-based aromaticity trend for penguinones (1O–7O) is: 7O > 6O > 5O > 4O > 1O > 2O > 3O.

Evaluation of magnetic parameters of aromaticity

Magnetic aromaticity indices are based on the circulation of π-electrons in a ring induced by external magnetic fields. One of the most important magnetic indices is the nucleus-independent chemical shift (NICS), first introduced by Schleyer et al. in 199626. NICS values are negative for magnetic shielding and are typically calculated at the ring center or at a certain distance above or below the ring plane. More negative NICS values (in ppm) indicate higher delocalization character. It has been established that NICS values measured 1 Å above the ring center, referred to as NICS(1), provide a better measure of aromaticity. In this study, the magnetic indices NICS(1) and NICS(1)ZZ were employed to evaluate the aromaticity of structures (1O–7O) and (1–7 S) derivatives, and the results are presented in Table 4.

While Ge-containing substituents generally produce stronger geometric and electronic delocalization effects, Si-containing substituents occasionally exhibit slightly stronger magnetic ring-current responses as reflected by the NICS descriptors (Table 5). Accordingly, the relative aromatic enhancement depends partially on the specific aromaticity criterion employed.

Natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis

The NBO analysis method provides a detailed assessment of intermolecular interactions, molecular stability, and charge transfer. Additionally, NBO analysis allows the calculation of quantitative orbital interaction energies for σ → π* based on second-order perturbation theory (E²). The E² value represents the amount of electron density transferred from donor NBOs to acceptor orbitals. Higher E(²) values indicate stronger donor–acceptor interactions. In this study, NBO analysis was employed to investigate electron delocalization in structures (1O–7O) and (1–7 S) (Table 5). Here, the E(²) values correspond to an electron transfer from the σC−X to the π*C=C bonds in the penguinone/thiopenguinone ring, which plays a significant role in enhancing reduce the non-aromatic character via hyperconjugation.

A strong linear correlation was observed between the NBO second-order stabilization energies E(2) (σ → π*) and the PDI values, with a correlation coefficient of R² ≈ 0.85 across both penguinone (1O–7O) and thiopenguinone (1–7 S) series. This result confirms that increased σ→π* hyperconjugative stabilization directly enhances π-electron delocalization within the ring. Similarly, HOMA values exhibit an excellent inverse correlation with ΔBL (R2 > 0.98), consistent with the geometric definition of aromaticity. More importantly, a meaningful correlation between PDI and NICS(1) (R2 = 0.92 (Fig. 5a) and 0.91 (Fig. 5b) for O and S series, respectively).

Finally, for comparison purposes, it should be noted that benzene, as a prototypical fully aromatic system, typically exhibits HOMA values close to 1.0, a BI of 100, and NICS(1) values around − 10 ppm. In contrast, the most delocalized compounds investigated in the present work, namely 7O and 7 S, display substantially smaller aromaticity indices, indicating that although Schleyer hyperconjugation significantly enhances delocalization character in penguinone and thiopenguinone frameworks, the resulting pseudo-6π systems remain less delocalized system than classical benzene. Nevertheless, the consistent improvement observed across structural, electronic, magnetic, and NBO descriptors clearly demonstrates the efficiency of hyperconjugative electron donation in promoting aromatic stabilization within these formally nonaromatic frameworks.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive theoretical investigation of Schleyer hyperconjugative aromaticity in penguinone and thiopenguinone derivatives using a multidimensional DFT-based approach. The parent penguinone framework, inherently nonaromatic due to its 4π electron configuration and sp3-hybridized carbon, was successfully transformed into pseudo-6π aromatic systems through rational substituent engineering. Bulky electron-donating substituents, particularly –GeMe3 and –SiMe3, significantly enhance delocalization by promoting σ→π* electron injection and reinforcing cyclic delocalization. Structural indices (∆BL, HOMA, BI), electronic descriptor (PDI), magnetic criteria (NICS(1) and NICS(1)ZZ), and NBO second-order perturbation energies consistently support this hyperconjugation-driven aromatic stabilization mechanism. Germanium-containing substituents generally provide stronger geometric and electronic aromatic enhancement, whereas silyl substituents occasionally exhibit slightly stronger magnetic ring-current responses. In contrast, electron-withdrawing substitution (–F) disrupts electron delocalization, reduces hyperconjugative stabilization energies, and suppresses aromatic character. Thiopenguinone derivatives frequently display enhanced aromaticity relative to oxygen analogues, which can be attributed to the larger atomic radius and greater polarizability of sulfur, enabling improved charge dispersion within the ring system. This work demonstrates that Schleyer hyperconjugation constitutes a robust and tunable strategy for inducing delocalization in formally nonaromatic frameworks. These findings may guide the rational design of nonbenzenoid aromatic scaffolds for organic electronic materials or reactive intermediates.
