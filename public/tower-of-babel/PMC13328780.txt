A literature review of multimodal interventions for improving health behaviors in obese upper elementary school students
Authors: Yun-Jeong Boo, Yeong-Mi Jo, Da-Jung Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Recent studies emphasize that behavioral change is a key strategy in managing childhood obesity, stressing the importance of improving healthy behaviors rather than focusing solely on weight loss. This study aimed to analyze research trends in multimodal interventions targeting the improvement of health behaviors in obese upper elementary school children. A systematic literature review was conducted on studies published in Korean academic journals from 2005 to 2025. The analysis focused on intervention components, delivery methods, and outcome variables. The results showed that multimodal interventions generally integrate nutrition education, physical activity, and behavioral strategies, which leads to improvements in self-efficacy and health behaviors, particularly dietary habits and physical activity. These interventions have been proven effective in improving health behaviors. Future research needs to develop more systematic intervention designs and comprehensive evaluation frameworks to enhance long-term effectiveness.

INTRODUCTION

Childhood obesity is recognized as a significant public health issue worldwide. Obesity during childhood has been identified as a strong predictor of obesity in adolescence and adulthood and is associated with an increased risk of developing chronic diseases, including cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes (Lobstein et al., 2015; Singh et al., 2008). Childhood obesity highlights the importance of effective management and prevention strategies, as well as early intervention. Numerous international studies emphasize that childhood obesity is not merely a matter of weight gain, but a complex issue involving the interplay of environmental factors and health behavioral factors, such as dietary habits, physical activity, and sedentary lifestyles (Sahoo et al., 2015; Weihrauch-Blüher and Wiegand, 2018). The development of these health behaviors is influenced by various environments, including the home, school, and community. Therefore, because these behaviors are changeable, they serve as important targets for intervention.

The upper grades of elementary school represent a transitional period from childhood to adolescence, during which levels of physical activity generally decrease (Dumith et al., 2011). During this time, children gain greater autonomy regarding food choices and physical activity. These behavioral choices can have a lasting impact on the formation of health behaviors in adolescence and adulthood. Therefore, upper elementary school students are considered a key target group for obesity prevention and management interventions focused on health behaviors. However, many intervention studies on childhood obesity have primarily used changes in physical indicators such as weight, body mass index, and body fat percentage as primary evaluation metrics (Simmonds et al., 2016). While these indicators are useful for measuring obesity outcomes, they have limitations in fully explaining changes in underlying factors that contribute to the onset and persistence of obesity, such as dietary habits, physical activity, and self-care behaviors.

Many studies emphasize that strategies focusing on energy balance and behavioral change are key to managing childhood obesity, and that it is important to target behavioral change itself rather than focusing solely on weight loss outcomes (Hill et al., 2012). In particular, behavioral determinants such as self-efficacy and environmental support have been shown to be closely related to changes in children’s physical activity and health behaviors (Craggs et al., 2011).

In this context, nursing interventions hold the potential to play a significant role in the management of childhood obesity. These interventions utilize a systematic approach that includes a comprehensive assessment of an individual’s lifestyle and health behaviors, and induces behavioral change through counseling and support. However, existing studies have various limitations, such as differences in intervention components, durations, and evaluation indicators, making it difficult to comprehensively understand the research results. Furthermore, there is a lack of literature systematically summarizing the characteristics and effects of nursing intervention studies that use health behavior improvement as a primary outcome variable (Cheng et al., 2021). Therefore, this study conducted a literature review on multimodal intervention studies aimed at improving the health behaviors of obese upper elementary school students. This review aimed to provide a systematic overview of the characteristics and effects of these interventions and to suggest directions for the future development and practical application of evidence-based nursing interventions for childhood obesity.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

This study is a literature review aimed at identifying trends in multimodal intervention studies targeting the improvement of health behaviors in obese upper-grade elementary school children. It has the advantage of providing evidence that can be helpful in clinical practice by comprehensively understanding the trends and characteristics of specific research topics through a systematic review and analysis of existing studies.

Research selection and literature search

This study aimed to systematically analyze the characteristics and effects of multimodal intervention studies designed to improve the health behaviors of obese upper-grade elementary school students. The subjects of analysis were studies published in domestic academic journals from 2005 to 2025. A literature search was conducted targeting studies published in the field of rehabilitation nursing in Korea, utilizing resources such as the National Assembly Library, Korean Studies Information Service System, and DataBase Periodical Information Academic. The search keywords were “obese children,” “health behavior education,” and “intervention study.” Unpublished studies, academic conference materials, studies targeting adults or adolescents, qualitative studies, and studies that did not include multimodal interventions were excluded. Ultimately, the full texts of five studies that met the inclusion criteria were used for the analysis.

Data analysis

To ensure consistency in the analysis of research trends, the number of selected studies, general characteristics, research methods, content of health behavior education-based multimodal interventions, outcome variables, results of effect analysis, and limitations were systematically identified based on existing research frameworks. The collected data were classified and organized according to research characteristics. For quantitative analysis, descriptive statistics, including frequencies and percentages, were calculated using IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 21.0 (IBM Co., USA). Additionally, to enhance the objectivity of the literature analysis, a keyword frequency analysis was conducted on the text descriptions of intervention content and health behavior-related outcome variables. This analysis aimed to identify core concepts repeatedly emphasized in multimodal intervention studies and the common characteristics of intervention components, ultimately deriving 10 keywords.

DISCUSSION

The literature review comprehensively examined multimodal intervention studies aimed at improving health behaviors in obese upper elementary school students to analyze the intervention content, application methods, effects, limitations, and areas for improvement. A total of five academic studies were selected for analysis. Through the analysis of core components of the intervention, research tools, results of effect analysis, and keyword analysis, this study aimed to present the overall trends and future directions of nursing intervention research.

First, this study revealed that a multimodal intervention integrating obesity and nutrition education, regular physical activity, and behavior modification strategies is associated with positive changes in the overall dietary, exercise, and lifestyle habits of obese elementary school students. These results are consistent with reports from a systematic review of overweight and obese children aged 6 to 11 years that multidimensional interventions are effective in improving health behaviors (Mead et al., 2017), and also align with previous research findings (Brown et al., 2019) that interventions combining education and physical activity improve dietary habits and physical activity behaviors. These findings suggest that integrated and multimodal interventions are more effective than single-component strategies for interventions targeting obese elementary school students.

Second, future multimodal intervention studies targeting obese upper elementary school students should place greater emphasis on the sustainability of health behavior changes and their applicability to daily life. While existing studies have reported positive changes immediately after intervention, there is a lack of research that adequately evaluates whether these behavioral changes persist beyond the intervention period. In this context, nurses can play a pivotal role in supporting sustained behavioral change by providing repetitive counseling and feedback tailored to each child’s life context. Nurse-led interventions have been shown to improve health behaviors and strengthen self-care abilities in children and adolescents (Cheng et al., 2021). Furthermore, the continuous participation of medical professionals has been identified as a critical factor in enhancing the effectiveness of school and community-based interventions (Waters et al., 2011).

Third, the studies included in this analysis reported improvements in dietary habits, lifestyle, and regular exercise practices, as well as a reduction in obesity-related stress. However, it may be difficult to sustain these lifestyle changes through short-term school-based interventions alone. According to a meta-analysis of physical activity interventions, while structured programs can facilitate behavioral change, their effectiveness may be limited if the intervention environment is not closely integrated with daily life (Metcalf et al., 2012). Furthermore, since children’s health behaviors are influenced by home and community environments (Sallis et al., 2012), future research should design integrated interventions that extend beyond the school to include collaboration with families and communities.

Fourth, while the measurement range of health behavior-related variables (e.g., obesity-related knowledge, health behavior indicators) varied across studies, positive changes were reported in most cases where these variables were evaluated after intervention. Conversely, studies with short intervention periods showed minimal changes in weight or body composition, suggesting that sufficient time is required for behavioral changes to be reflected in physical indicators. These results are consistent with previous research findings (Ho et al., 2012; Whitlock et al., 2010) indicating that changes in health behavior tend to appear in short-term interventions, whereas changes in weight and body composition are more pronounced in medium-to-long-term interventions. Therefore, future research should interpret health behavior indicators and body measurement indicators separately and conduct stepwise evaluations using a sufficiently long intervention period.

Fifth, keyword analysis results showed that physical activity, dietary habits, obesity management, and education appeared consistently across all studies, whereas self-efficacy, health behaviors, and lifestyle habits were repeatedly emphasized in various studies. These results suggest that multimodal intervention studies targeting obese upper elementary school children should prioritize behavioral change and the psychological and cognitive factors mediating such change, rather than focusing solely on weight loss. In particular, the repeated measurement of self-efficacy as a key variable reaffirms the importance of individual cognitive factors emphasized in behavior change theory (Bandura, 2004). This observation is consistent with previous research findings that behavior modification strategies, such as goal setting and self-monitoring, can enhance the effectiveness of interventions (Michie et al., 2009).

Overall, multimodal intervention studies targeting obese upper elementary school children have evolved with a focus on self-efficacy and changes in health behaviors, particularly improvements in physical activity and dietary habits. Therefore, future research should develop an intervention design and evaluation framework that systematically integrates these core concepts.
