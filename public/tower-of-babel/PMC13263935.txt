Predictive value of the hemoglobin–albumin–lymphocyte–platelet score for intravenous immunoglobulin resistance in children with Kawasaki disease
Authors: Kai Li, Fei Li, Na Zeng, Haiyan Sang, Ya Zhao, Mingwu Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) resistance is a major clinical challenge in children with Kawasaki disease (KD) and is associated with an increased risk of coronary artery lesions. Early identification of patients at high risk of IVIG resistance remains clinically important.MethodsMedical data were collected from KD patients at Anhui Provincial Hospital between January 2018 and December 2023. Multivariable logistic regression was performed to identify independent risk factors for IVIG resistance in KD. The study further performed subgroup analyses and restricted cubic spline (RCS) to visualize the dose–response association between hemoglobin–albumin–lymphocyte–platelet score (HALP) and IVIG resistance. The predictive performance of HALP was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and decision curve analysis (DCA), and internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling.ResultsA total of 598 patients were included, of whom 58 (9.7%) were identified as IVIG-resistant. Patients with IVIG-resistant KD had significantly lower HALP scores, and low HALP was identified as an independent risk factor for IVIG resistance. The association was consistent across subgroups defined by age, sex, and incomplete KD (IKD). The area under the curve (AUC) for HALP in predicting IVIG resistance was 0.854, outperforming NLR (AUC = 0.751), PLR (AUC = 0.793), and SII (AUC = 0.759). In addition, DCA showed that HALP provided greater net benefit when the threshold probability ranged from 0.02 to 0.63. Moreover, subgroup ROC indicated that HALP had higher predictive value in patients aged ≤ 6 months (AUC = 0.934) and ≥ 48 months (AUC = 0.947).ConclusionsHALP is independently associated with IVIG resistance in children with KD and demonstrated good performance for early risk stratification, particularly in patients aged ≤ 6 months and ≥ 48 months. As a simple and readily available biomarker, HALP may serve as a useful complementary tool in routine pediatric clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13052-026-02288-5.

Introduction

Kawasaki disease (KD) is an acute systemic vasculitis that predominantly affects children under 5 years of age and has become one of the leading causes of acquired heart disease in this population. Without timely treatment, approximately 25% of patients develop coronary artery lesions (CALs) [1], which may progress to coronary artery aneurysm rupture, myocardial infarction, or even death [2]. Although standard treatment with intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) combined with aspirin significantly reduces the risk of CALs, 10%–20% of patients remain resistant to IVIG [3, 4]. These patients are at higher risk of developing CALs and experiencing adverse clinical outcomes [5, 6]. Therefore, early identification of patients at high risk of IVIG resistance is of substantial clinical importance.

In recent years, numerous predictive models and biomarkers have been developed to facilitate early identification of IVIG resistance; however, these approaches have notable limitations. Traditional clinical scoring systems, such as the Kobayashi and Egami scores, show considerable variability across different populations and regions [7–9]. In addition, several novel biomarkers, including serum amyloid A (SAA), serum ferritin (SF), and pentraxin 3 (PTX3), are not widely used due to their high testing costs [10–12]. To facilitate risk stratification, several composite inflammatory biomarkers derived from routine blood tests have been proposed, including the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and systemic immune–inflammation index (SII) [13–14]. Due to their low cost and wide availability, these biomarkers have attracted considerable attention for predicting IVIG resistance and coronary artery complications. However, although these indices reflect the inflammatory burden of KD to some extent, they do not capture the impact of disease-related nutritional depletion, which may limit their predictive performance.

The hemoglobin–albumin–lymphocyte–platelet (HALP) score is an emerging composite biomarker that integrates both nutritional and inflammatory status. Recent studies have demonstrated that HALP is useful for predicting outcomes in various malignancies, including gastric, bladder, and esophageal cancers [15–17]. In cardiovascular diseases, higher HALP scores have been associated with reduced all-cause mortality in patients with coronary artery disease and lower in-hospital mortality in those with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction [18–19]. Similarly, HALP has been shown to predict 28-day survival in patients with sepsis [20]. These findings suggest that HALP is closely associated with disease progression across a range of clinical conditions. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the association between HALP and IVIG resistance in children with Kawasaki disease and to assess its predictive performance for early risk stratification.

Materials and methods

Patients

Patients diagnosed with KD at Anhui Provincial Hospital from January 2018 to December 2023 were enrolled in this study. Diagnosis of KD patients was based on the American Heart Association (AHA) guidelines [21] and verified by experienced pediatric clinicians. A total of 641 patients were initially screened. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients lacking essential laboratory or echocardiographic data (n = 10); (2) patients with recurrent Kawasaki disease (n = 3); (3) patients with initial IVIG at other hospitals (n = 7); (4) patients who did not receive standard-dose IVIG (n = 10); (5) patients concurrently treated with corticosteroids or other immunosuppressants (n = 8); and (6) patients with other immune or congenital heart diseases (n = 5). After exclusions, 598 patients were included and categorized into IVIG-responsive and IVIG-resistant groups based on treatment response.

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Anhui Provincial Hospital (No. 2025-re378). Informed consent was waived because of its retrospective nature.

Data collection

Demographic characteristics, clinical features, laboratory findings, and echocardiographic data were extracted from the hospital database. Laboratory data were obtained within 24 h of admission from routine peripheral blood examinations, including white blood cell (WBC) count, neutrophil (NE) percentage, lymphocyte (LY) percentage, neutrophil (NE) count, lymphocyte (LY) count, platelet (PLT) count, mean platelet volume (MPV), hemoglobin (HGB), red blood cell (RBC) count, C-reactive protein (CRP), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), procalcitonin (PCT), albumin (ALB), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), serum sodium (Na), and total bilirubin (TB). The HALP, NLR, PLR, and SII biomarkers were calculated from these parameters. All variables were obtained from routine admission examinations. Patients with missing key variables were excluded during data collection; therefore, no missing data were present in the final dataset, and complete-case analysis was performed.

Definitions

All patients were treated according to AHA recommendations. IVIG was administered at 2 g/kg, along with aspirin at 30–50 mg/kg/day. After 48–72 h, the aspirin dose was reduced to 3–5 mg/kg/day and continued for 6–8 weeks. In patients with CALs, aspirin was maintained until resolution. Typical and incomplete KD were diagnosed based on AHA criteria. Body surface area (BSA) was calculated using the Dallaire formula, and coronary artery Z-scores ≥ 2.5 were defined as CALs [22]. IVIG resistance was defined as persistent or recrudescent fever (≥ 38.0 °C) lasting more than 36 h after completion of IVIG infusion. Statistical analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 26.0) and R software (version 4.2.2). The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were compared using independent-samples t-tests. Non-normally distributed variables are expressed as interquartile ranges (IQRs) and were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages (n, %) and were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

The adequacy of the sample size for logistic regression was assessed using the events-per-variable (EPV) rule. With 58 IVIG-resistant events and three predictors in the final model, the EPV was 19.3, exceeding the recommended threshold of 10–15 and indicating adequate statistical power. To explore factors associated with IVIG resistance, univariable logistic regression analysis was performed (Supplementary Table 2). Variables with P < 0.10 were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model for exploratory adjustment. However, this multivariable analysis was conducted for exploratory purposes only, and the primary objective of this study was to evaluate HALP as a single predictive biomarker. All analyses were conducted using the same cohort of 598 patients.

Discriminative ability was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, and comparisons between AUCs were performed using the DeLong test. Calibration of HALP as a single predictor was assessed using calibration slope, observed-to-expected (O/E) ratio, Brier score, and calibration plots. The calibration slope was obtained by regressing the observed outcome on the predicted logit. The O/E ratio was calculated as the ratio of observed events to the sum of predicted probabilities, and the Brier score was defined as the mean squared difference between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes. Calibration plots were generated by grouping predicted risks into deciles. Internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling (1,000 iterations) to correct for potential overfitting. In each iteration, a univariable logistic regression model with HALP as the sole predictor was refitted in the bootstrap sample and evaluated in the original dataset to estimate optimism. Optimism-corrected performance metrics were reported.

For subgroup analyses, patients were stratified into three age groups (≤ 6 months, > 6 to < 48 months, and ≥ 48 months). This classification was defined a priori based on established physiological and hematological developmental patterns. Peripheral neutrophil and lymphocyte proportions undergo two physiological crossovers at approximately 4–6 days and 4–6 years of age [23]. In addition, hemoglobin levels in infants younger than 4–6 months mainly depend on maternal iron stores. Therefore, age stratification using 6 and 48 months as boundaries was applied to reduce the influence of age-related hematologic variation.

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis was performed to explore the potential nonlinear association between HALP and IVIG resistance. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was conducted to evaluate clinical utility across a range of threshold probabilities. Net benefit was calculated at each threshold and interpreted in the context of clinical decision-making, such as initiating intensified immunomodulatory therapy (e.g., corticosteroids or second-line IVIG). A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics of KD patients

A total of 598 patients with KD were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1), comprising 383 males (64.0%) and 215 females (36.0%), with a median age of 21 months. Among these patients, 58 (9.7%) were classified as IVIG-resistant. As shown in Table 1, IVIG-resistant patients had a significantly higher incidence of CALs. However, no significant differences were observed between the IVIG-resistant and IVIG-responsive groups with respect to age, sex, KD type, or duration of fever prior to initial IVIG treatment (all P > 0.05). Compared with IVIG-responsive patients, those with IVIG resistance exhibited a more pronounced inflammatory response, reflected by significantly higher levels of NLR, PLR, SII, neutrophil percentage (NE%), CRP, PCT, and ALT. In contrast, they demonstrated poorer nutritional status, as indicated by lower levels of RBC, HGB, ALB, Na, LY count, and HALP score (all P < 0.05). No significant differences were found in NE count, PLT count, MPV, ESR, AST, or TB between the two groups (all P > 0.05).

Subgroup analyses further demonstrated that the inverse association between HALP and IVIG resistance was consistent across different age groups, sexes, and incomplete KD (IKD) status (Fig. 2). Nonlinear analysis between HALP and IVIG resistance

There was a clear dose–response relationship between HALP and IVIG resistance (Fig. 3). Restricted cubic spline analysis demonstrated a nonlinear association between HALP and IVIG resistance (nonlinear P < 0.05). As HALP increased, the risk of IVIG resistance in KD gradually decreased. The overall model was statistically significant (P overall < 0.005).

Predictive value of HALP, NLR, PLR, and SII for KD complicated with IVIG resistance

ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive performance of HALP as the primary biomarker, along with other inflammatory indices. (Fig. 4; Table 3). HALP demonstrated good discriminative ability, with an AUC of 0.854 (95% CI: 0.800–0.908). At the optimal cutoff value of 25.459, the sensitivity and specificity were 74.1% and 83.9%, respectively. Calibration and internal validation were performed for HALP. The calibration curve showed good agreement between predicted probabilities and observed event rates (Fig. 5). After bootstrap correction using 1000 resamples, the calibration slope was 1.00, the O/E ratio was 1.00, and the Brier score was 0.066, indicating good calibration performance.

Additionally, we conducted the Delong test to compare the predictive performance of HALP with other inflammatory biomarkers and the combined model (Table 3). The AUC of HALP was significantly higher than those of NLR (0.751, 95% CI: 0.680–0.823, P < 0.001), PLR (0.793, 95% CI: 0.723–0.864, P < 0.001), and SII (0.759, 95% CI: 0.690–0.829, P < 0.001). An exploratory multivariable model (HALP + NE% + RBC) showed a slightly higher AUC (0.865); however, this improvement was not statistically significant compared with HALP alone (P = 0.245).

The DCA curves illustrated the clinical utility of different inflammatory biomarkers (Fig. 6). When the threshold probability ranged from 0.02 to 0.67, HALP provided a positive net clinical benefit compared with both treat-all and treat-none strategies. Moreover, HALP showed a higher net benefit than NLR, PLR, and SII within the threshold range of 0.02 to 0.63. From a clinical perspective, this range reflects decision thresholds at which clinicians may consider additional interventions. Accordingly, patients identified as high-risk by HALP may benefit from closer monitoring and early adjunctive therapies, such as corticosteroids.

Discussion

IVIG resistance in KD represents a major clinical challenge and is strongly associated with the development of CALs. A large meta-analysis reported that IVIG-resistant patients had an approximately 3.89-fold increased risk of CALs (95% CI: 3.18–4.75), with consistent findings across regions. In addition to prolonging fever duration and hospital stay, IVIG resistance increases the risk of acute coronary injury and is associated with persistent CALs or coronary artery aneurysms (CAA) [24, 25]. Therefore, early identification of patients at high risk of IVIG resistance is essential for optimizing treatment strategies and improving coronary outcomes. However, existing biomarkers primarily reflect inflammatory status and often overlook nutritional alterations, which may limit their predictive performance.

In the present study, IVIG resistance occurred in 9.7% of patients, consistent with previously reported rates of 10–20% [3–4]. In this retrospective cohort of 598 patients, we identified a significant nonlinear inverse association between HALP and IVIG resistance. This association remained robust after adjustment for potential confounders, including age, sex, and IKD. These findings support the potential role of HALP as a useful biomarker for risk stratification of IVIG resistance in KD.

From a clinical perspective, early risk stratification has direct therapeutic implications. First, intensified first-line therapies may reduce the incidence of CALs. Evidence from the RAISE and KAICA trials has shown that combining corticosteroids or cyclosporine with IVIG improves coronary outcomes in high-risk patients [26, 27]. Second, for patients with established IVIG resistance, additional treatments such as repeat IVIG, corticosteroids, or anti–TNF-α agents (e.g., infliximab) can reduce inflammation and shorten disease duration [28]. According to current American Heart Association guidelines, IVIG-resistant patients are at increased risk of CAA and should receive second-line therapy [21]. In this context, a simple and accessible biomarker for early identification of IVIG resistance is of considerable clinical value.

Previous retrospective studies have identified anemia and hypoproteinemia as important risk factors for early IVIG resistance in patients with KD [29]. Consistent with these findings, our data showed that IVIG-resistant patients had significantly lower HGB and RBC levels compared with IVIG-responsive patients, suggesting that anemia may contribute to an inadequate therapeutic response. Similar observations have been reported by H. Huang and Li et al. [30, 31]. Anemia is common in KD and is closely associated with the intensity and duration of systemic inflammation [32, 33]. Hepcidin plays a central role in linking inflammation to disordered iron metabolism [34]. In KD, excessive inflammatory activation leads to increased interleukin-6 production, which in turn stimulates hepcidin synthesis. Elevated hepcidin levels reduce intestinal iron absorption and inhibit erythropoiesis, ultimately resulting in anemia [35, 36].

Serum albumin, primarily synthesized in the liver, is widely recognized as an indicator of both hepatic function and nutritional status. In the present study, ALB levels were markedly reduced in IVIG-resistant patients, supporting previous reports that hypoalbuminemia is an important predictor of IVIG resistance in KD [37]. Consistent with this, ALB has been incorporated into several predictive models and shown to carry substantial weight [38]. The decline in ALB may be attributed not only to increased metabolic demands during acute inflammation [39] but also to enhanced vascular permeability mediated by inflammatory cytokines, leading to albumin extravasation into the interstitial space [40]. Accordingly, reduced ALB levels reflect both nutritional depletion and heightened inflammatory burden, and are typically associated with more severe disease and prolonged illness [41].

We also observed significantly lower absolute lymphocyte counts and lymphocyte percentages in IVIG-resistant patients, in line with previous studies [42, 43]. During the acute inflammatory phase, lymphocytes migrate from the circulation to sites of inflammation, resulting in decreased peripheral counts [44]. Beyond their immunoregulatory functions, lymphocytes are essential for maintaining vascular endothelial integrity and immune homeostasis. Increasing evidence suggests that lymphopenia is associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes. Reduced lymphocyte counts have been linked to a higher incidence of cardiovascular events, and persistent lymphopenia has been associated with poorer prognosis in cardiovascular diseases [45–47].

In contrast, platelet counts did not differ significantly between IVIG-resistant and IVIG-responsive patients in our cohort. This finding may be related to the timing of blood sampling, as platelet levels typically peak 2–3 weeks after disease onset [48], whereas samples in our study were collected at varying stages of fever. Although no significant difference was observed at the group level, platelets may still have prognostic relevance at the individual level and warrant further investigation. Platelets play a critical role in hemostasis, coagulation, and inflammation [49]. Upon activation, they adhere to the vascular endothelium and release pro-inflammatory mediators, thereby contributing to endothelial injury and increasing the risk of coronary thrombosis and myocardial infarction [50, 51].

Based on these findings, we introduced the HALP score, which integrates nutritional markers (HGB and ALB) and inflammatory markers (LY and PLT), as a predictor of IVIG resistance in children with Kawasaki disease. Compared with previously reported systemic inflammatory markers, HALP demonstrated superior predictive performance, outperforming NLR, PLR, and SII. We also explored an exploratory combined model incorporating HALP and additional variables, which showed a slight increase in AUC. However, this improvement was modest and not statistically significant, and no further validation was performed, indicating that HALP alone provides adequate discriminatory ability for early risk stratification. Moreover, decision curve analysis demonstrated the clinical utility of HALP in identifying children at increased risk of IVIG resistance. Compared with conventional inflammatory indices, HALP may enhance early risk stratification and assist clinicians in identifying patients who may benefit from closer monitoring or early adjunctive therapies, such as corticosteroids. This advantage may be attributed to the fact that traditional biomarkers such as NLR, PLR, and SII primarily reflect inflammation-related hematologic changes, while failing to capture inflammation-induced nutritional impairment. In contrast, HALP integrates both inflammatory and nutritional dimensions, which may underlie its improved predictive performance and enhance its clinical applicability.

In subgroup analyses, we found that the inverse association between HALP and IVIG resistance was consistent across age, sex, and IKD, suggesting stable predictive performance across different patient characteristics. Notably, the association appeared stronger in patients aged ≤ 6 months (OR: 0.83) and ≥ 48 months (OR: 0.80). Age-specific ROC analyses further demonstrated excellent discriminatory ability, with AUC values of 0.934 and 0.947 in these two groups, respectively. These age groups are known to exhibit distinct clinical features, including lower HGB and ALB levels and a higher risk of coronary artery involvement, reflecting more severe inflammation-related nutritional impairment and poorer prognosis [52–56]. By integrating HGB and ALB, HALP may better capture the inflammation–nutrition imbalance in these high-risk populations, thereby enhancing its predictive value for IVIG resistance. However, the relatively wide confidence intervals observed in subgroup analyses may be attributable to the limited number of IVIG-resistant cases within these age groups. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted with caution, and further studies with larger sample sizes are needed to validate the predictive performance of HALP in these populations.

The HALP score is not intended to replace existing risk scoring systems but may serve as a simple and complementary tool for early risk stratification in children with KD. Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center retrospective study, which may introduce selection bias. Second, laboratory data were collected only at admission, and dynamic changes in HALP were not assessed, which may limit a more comprehensive evaluation of its temporal trends and prognostic value. Third, although subgroup analyses suggested stronger associations in certain age groups, the number of IVIG-resistant cases in these subgroups was relatively small, which may have resulted in wide confidence intervals and reduced estimate stability, with a potential risk of overfitting. Therefore, these findings should be considered exploratory and interpreted with caution. Finally, although internal validation using bootstrap resampling demonstrated good model stability, the lack of external validation may limit generalizability. Future prospective, multicenter studies are warranted to validate these findings.

Conclusion

This retrospective study demonstrated that a lower HALP score was independently associated with IVIG resistance in children with Kawasaki disease. Compared with commonly used inflammatory indices, HALP showed good predictive performance, particularly in patients aged ≤ 6 months and ≥ 48 months. As a simple biomarker derived from routine laboratory parameters, HALP may serve as a complementary tool for early identification of children at high risk of IVIG resistance, thereby facilitating individualized treatment strategies. Further prospective multicenter studies are warranted to validate its clinical utility.
