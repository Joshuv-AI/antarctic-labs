1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is a prime power. For positive integers
n
and
k
with
k\leq n
, an
[n,k]_{q}
linear code (or, simply an
[n,k]
-code)
C
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of the vector space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
. The parameters
n
and
k
are called the length and the dimension of the code
C
, respectively. Elements of a code are called codewords. The vector space, with usual coordinate system, can be endowed with a metric, known as the
Hamming metric
, denoted by
d_{H}
, where for any two elements
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
and
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, their
Hamming distance
is given by

d_{H}({\bf a},{\bf b})=|\{i:a_{i}\neq b_{i}\}|.

Furthermore, the Hamming distance naturally gives rise to the notion of
Hamming weight
of a codeword. Given an element
{\bf c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, we define its
Hamming weight
, denoted by

w_{H}({\bf c})=|\{i:c_{i}\neq 0\}|.

We define the
minimum distance of a code
C
, denoted by
d(C)
, as

d(C)=\min\{d({\bf x},{\bf y}):{\bf x},{\bf y}\in C,{\bf x}\neq{\bf y}\}.

Due to the linearity of the code
C
, it follows that the minimum distance of a code
C
is equal to the
minimum weight
of
C
, where the latter is defined as

\min\{w_{H}({\bf c}):{\bf c}\in C,{\bf c}\neq 0\}.

In order to produce good codes, one often takes recourse to algebraic varieties over finite fields and codes that occur from them. One way to construct codes from algebraic varieties is to use the notion of a projective system. We omit a detailed discussion of projective systems and codes here, but refer the reader to
[
15
]
.

The codes from Schubert varieties, also known as Schubert codes, were first introduced in
[
4
]
by Ghorpade and Lachaud. Let
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})\in I(\ell,m)
, as defined in (
2
),
V_{m}
a vector space of dimension
m
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with an ordered basis
\mathcal{B}
and
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
be the Schubert subvariety of the Grassmannian
G(\ell,V_{m})
embedded in a nondegenerate manner in the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
as described in Subsection
2.2
. It turns out the length
n_{\alpha}
, dimension
k_{\alpha}
, and the minimum distance/weight
d_{\alpha}
of the code
C_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
arising from the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
are given by the following formulas:

(1)

n_{\alpha}=|C_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|
. We refer to
[
7
, Eq. (9), (10) and Theorem 1]
for combinatorial formulas describing
n_{\alpha}
. We also discuss this matter at length in Subsection
3.1
later.

(2)

k_{\alpha}
is given in
[
7
, Theorem 7]
.

(3)

d_{\alpha}=n_{\alpha}-\max\{|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|:\Pi_{\alpha}\ \text{is a hyperplane of ${\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}$}\}=q^{\delta({\alpha})}
, where
\delta({\alpha})=({\alpha}_{1}+\dots+{\alpha}_{\ell})-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}
. This formula was first conjectured in
[
4
]
, and subsequently proved in
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
in the case
\ell=2
, and in full generality in
[
16
]
. An independent proof for this formula was also given in
[
6
]
.

We note that when
{\alpha}=(m-\ell+1,\dots,m)
, the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
equals the Grassmannian
G(\ell,V_{m})
. In this particular case, all the above formulas are known:

n_{\alpha}={m\brack\ell}_{q},\ k_{\alpha}={m\choose\ell}\ \text{and}\ d_{\alpha}=q^{\ell(m-\ell)}.

While the first two formulas are classical, the minimum distances of this particular Schubert code, namely the Grassmann code, were determined by Nogin
[
13
]
. Moreover, Nogin also provided a complete classification of the minimum-weight codewords of Grassmann codes. Even though, in general, the minimum weight of the Schubert codes is known, a classification of all the minimum weight codewords is yet to be done. In
[
6
]
, the authors conjectured a classification of all the minimum weight codewords of Schubert codes (cf. Conjecture
1
). Their conjecture states that the minimum-weight codewords of Schubert codes are given by Schubert decomposable hyperplanes (cf. Definition
2.3
). Ghorpade and Singh proved the conjecture in the affirmative in the case when
\ell=2
. In this paper, we show that the conjecture is true for all positive integers
\ell
, when
q
is sufficiently large. In achieving this, we also give an alternative proof of the formula for
d_{\alpha}
as mentioned above.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we gather all the preliminary definitions and results that are needed to prove our main result. This includes a detailed discussion on Grassmann varieties and their Schubert subvarieties. In Section 3, we provide several combinatorial decompositions of Schubert subvarieties and discussions on the enumerations on the points of Schubert subvarieties over finite fields. Finally, in Section 4, we prove our main result which is given in Theorem
4.7
.

,

,

,

]
, and in modern texts such as
[

]
. Moreover, we borrow some definitions and results in coding theory from several sources

,

[

2.1.
Grassmannian, Plücker projective space, and Plücker embedding

Fix integers
\ell,m
satisfying
1\leq\ell\leq m
and an
m
-dimensional vector space
V_{m}
over a field
\mathbb{F}
with an ordered basis
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
of
V_{m}
.
The
Grassmannian of all
\ell
-dimensional subspaces of
V_{m}
, denoted by
G(\ell,V_{m})
is defined as

G(\ell,V_{m}):=\{L\subset V_{m}:L\ \text{is a subspace of}\ V_{m},\ \dim L=\ell\}.

Let us now
impose a geometric structure on
G(\ell,V_{m})
via the so-called
Plücker embedding
that is given by:

(1)

\pi_{\ell,m}:G(\ell,V_{m})\to{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}\right)\ \ \text{given by}\ \ L\mapsto[\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{\ell}],

where
\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\ell}\}
is a basis of
L
. It is well-known that the map is well-defined, that is, it is independent of the choice of basis for the elements of
G(\ell,V_{m})
. The projective space
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}\right)
is known as the
Plücker projective space
. Let

(2)

I(\ell,m):=\{{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}:1\leq{\alpha}_{1}<\cdots<{\alpha}_{\ell}\leq m\}.

Note that
\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}
is a
k={m\choose\ell}
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}
. Moreover, a fixed ordering of elements of
I(\ell,m)
gives an ordered basis
\{v_{\alpha}=v_{{\alpha}_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{{\alpha}_{\ell}}:{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)\}
of
\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}
. This ordered basis, in turn, helps us in introducing the homogeneous coordinates of the Plücker projective space. In particular, if
L\in G(\ell,V_{m})
and
A_{L}=(a_{ij})
is an
\ell\times m
matrix with entries in
{\mathbb{F}}
such that the row-space of
A_{L}
is
L
, then

(3)

\pi_{\ell,m}(L)=[p_{\alpha}(A_{L})]_{{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)}

where
p_{{\alpha}}(A_{L})
is the
{\alpha}
-th minor of
A_{L}
.
As mentioned above, it is known (cf.
[
11
, Section 5.2.1]
) that
\pi_{\ell,m}(L)
is independent of the choice of
A_{L}
and consequently the map
\pi_{\ell,m}
is well-defined. Moreover, the map
\pi_{\ell,m}
is injective
[
11
, Theorem 5.2.1]
. We remark that the rows of
A_{L}
above are written with respect to the fixed basis
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
of
V_{m}
. However, to our advantage, it turns out that changing the basis of
V_{m}
, does not affect the situation drastically. More precisely, a change of basis of
V_{m}
induces a collineation of
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m})
taking
G(\ell,V_{m})
to itself.
When
{\mathbb{F}}
is an algebraically closed field, it can be shown that
G(\ell,V_{m})
is given by the solutions to a system of some homogeneous quadratic equations in
X_{\alpha}
-s, known as the Plücker relations. We refer to
[
11
, Theorem 5.2.3]
for a complete proof of this fact. Consequently, the subset
G(\ell,V_{m})
can be regarded as a projective algebraic variety when working over an algebraically closed field.

We keep working with the fixed ordered basis
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
of
V_{m}
, as mentioned above. For every
L\in G(\ell,V_{m})
, We may choose a basis of
L
in a way so that
A_{L}
is in
right-row-reduced-echelon
form, which is uniquely determined by
L
. That is,
L
can be uniquely represented by an
\ell\times m
matrix
M_{\mathcal{B}}(L)
satisfying

(a)

the rows of
M_{\mathcal{B}}(L)
are elements of
L
written with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}
of
V_{m}
.

(b)

the row-space of
M_{\mathcal{B}}(L)
is
L
,

(c)

for each
i=1,\dots,\ell
the last non-zero entry of the
i
-th row, called the
pivot of
i
-th row
, is equal to
1
,

(d)

the last non-zero entry of
(i+1)
-st row appears to the right of the last nonzero entry of the
i
-th row,

(e)

all the entries above and below of a pivot are
0
.

In particular, we have a natural one-to-one correspondence,

(4)

G(\ell,V_{m})\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{M}(\ell,m)\ \ \text{given by}\ \ L\longleftrightarrow M_{\mathcal{B}}(L),

where
\mathcal{M}(\ell,m)
is the set of all
\ell\times m
matrices in right-row-reduced echelon form with entries in
{\mathbb{F}}
.
Moreover, with respect to a fixed basis
\mathcal{B}
of
V_{m}
, the map
\pi_{\ell,m}
in (
1
) can be rewritten as

(5)

\pi_{\ell,m}:G(\ell,V_{m})\to{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}\right)\ \ \text{as}\ \ \pi_{\ell,m}(L)=[p_{\alpha}(M_{\mathcal{B}}(L))]_{{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)}.

This description will be used later in this article.

2.2.
Schubert subvarieties of
G(\ell,V_{m})

The Grassmannian
G(\ell,V_{m})
contains a special class of subvarieties, known as the
Schubert subvarieties
, which are the objects of central interest in this article. For
{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)
, the
{\alpha}
-th Schubert cell
, with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
, denoted by
C_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, is defined as,

C_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}):=\{L\in G(\ell,V_{m}):\ \text{the pivots of}\ M_{\mathcal{B}}(L)\ \text{are on the columns}\ {\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}\}.

Note that
C_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
is identified with an affine space of dimension
\delta({\alpha})
over
{\mathbb{F}}
, where

\delta({\alpha})=({\alpha}_{1}+\dots+{\alpha}_{\ell})-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}.

We define the partial order, also known as the Bruhat order, on the elements of
I(\ell,m)
as follows: For
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}),\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell})\in I(\ell,m)
, we say that

{\alpha}\leq\beta\iff{\alpha}_{i}\leq\beta_{i}\ \ \ \text{for all}\ i\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}.

For
{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)
, define

\Delta({\alpha})=\{\beta\in I(\ell,m):\beta\not\leq{\alpha}\},\ \nabla({\alpha})=I(\ell,m)\setminus\Delta({\alpha}),\ \text{and}\ k_{\alpha}=|\nabla({\alpha})|.

The
{\alpha}
-th Schubert subvariety in

G(\ell,V_{m})
with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}
, denoted by
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, is defined as

(6)

\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}):=\bigcup_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})}C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}).

Indeed, the union mentioned above is clearly a disjoint union of Schubert cells. Evidently, if
{\alpha}=(m-\ell+1,\dots,m)
, then
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})=G(\ell,V_{m})
.
For
i=1,\dots,m
, let
V_{i}
be the subspace of
V_{m}
spanned by
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{i}\}
. We see that for every
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})\in I(\ell,m)
, there is a partial flag of subspaces of
V_{m}
given by

0\subset V_{{\alpha}_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset V_{{\alpha}_{\ell}}\subset V_{m}\ \ \ \text{with}\ \ \ \dim V_{{\alpha}_{i}}={\alpha}_{i}\ \ \text{for all}\ i=1,\dots,\ell.

Moreover, it follows rather trivially that

L\in C_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\iff\dim L\cap V_{{\alpha}_{j}}=j\ \ \text{for every}\ j=1,\dots,\ell,

while

L\in\Omega_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\iff\dim L\cap V_{{\alpha}_{j}}\geq j\ \ \text{for every}\ j=1,\dots,\ell.

In literature, several authors define a Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
in terms of the above equivalence condition.
The Plücker embedding
\pi_{\ell,m}
restricted to
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
allows us to view the Schubert variety as a subset of the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}\right)
. As mentioned above, with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}
and a fixed ordering of the elements of
I(\ell,m)
, the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{k-1}={\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}\right)
is endowed with the homogeneous coordinates
(X_{{\alpha}})_{{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)}
. We define the projective linear subspace
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
of
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}\right)
by

(7)

{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}:=\left\{P\in{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}\right):X_{\beta}(P)=0\ \text{for all}\ \beta\in\Delta({\alpha})\right\}.

It is well-known that

(8)

\pi_{\ell,m}(\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}))=G(\ell,V_{m})\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1},

where
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
is as defined above. As with the Grassmann varieties, we will denote by
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, the Plücker embedding of
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
.
We see that the
{\alpha}
-th Schubert variety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
can be geometrically understood as a linear section of the Grassmann variety
G(\ell,V_{m})
given by some coordinate hyperplanes of the Plücker space.

2.3.
Hyperplanes, their decomposability, and Schubert decomposability

One of our main goals in this article is to study the maximum number of points lying on a
hyperplane
section of a Schubert variety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
defined over a finite field. That is, we want to determine the quantity

\max\{|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|:\Pi_{\alpha}\ \text{is a hyperplane of}\ {\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}\},

when all the subsets mentioned above are defined over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
To this end, it is imperative to first understand the algebraic and geometric properties of hyperplanes in the Plücker space. We will briefly recall the definitions and the properties of them in this subsection, but refer the reader to
[
3
, Section 2.3]
and the references therein for more leisurely reading.

The hyperplanes of the Plücker space
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m})
can be represented in two ways. On one hand, they are given by elements of the dual vector space
(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m})^{*}
which is known to be isomorphic to
(\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m})
. Thus a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m})
can be defined by an element
F
, given by

F=\sum_{{\alpha}\in I(m-\ell,m)}c_{\alpha}v_{{\alpha}_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{{\alpha}_{m-\ell}}\in\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}.

As usual, a point
L\in G(\ell,V_{m})
lies on the hyperplane given by
F
if and only if
F(L)=0
.
Here

F(L)=\sum_{{\alpha}\in I(m-\ell,m)}c_{\alpha}v_{{\alpha}_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{{\alpha}_{m-\ell}}\wedge(w_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge w_{\ell}),

where
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{\ell}\}
is an ordered basis of
L
. On the other hand, we may also describe a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m})
geometrically in terms of the homogeneous coordinates
X_{\alpha}
as mentioned earlier. Here, we may represent a hyperplane as the vanishing set of a homogeneous linear polynomial

\sum_{{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)}c_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}.

It is understood that, for
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})
the coordinate hyperplane given by
X_{\alpha}
corresponds to the hyperplane given by the element
v_{{\alpha}^{\mathsf{C}}}=v_{{\alpha}^{\prime}_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{{\alpha}^{\prime}_{m-\ell}}\in\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}
, where
\{{\alpha}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}^{\prime}_{m-\ell}\}\cup\{{\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}\}=\{1,\dots,m\}
. This correspondence naturally extends to the following identification

(9)

\sum_{{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)}c_{\alpha}X_{{\alpha}}\longleftrightarrow\sum_{{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)}c_{\alpha}v_{{\alpha}^{\mathsf{C}}},

where for each
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})\in I(\ell,m)
, the element
{\alpha}^{\mathsf{C}}=({\alpha}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}^{\prime}_{m-\ell})\in I(m-\ell,m)
satisfying
\{{\alpha}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}^{\prime}_{m-\ell}\}\cup\{{\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}\}=\{1,\dots,m\}
.
The non-degeneracy of the Grassmannian
G(\ell,V_{m})
in
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m})
implies that if
\sum_{{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)}c_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}(L)=0
for all
L\in G(\ell,V_{m})
, then
c_{\alpha}=0
for all
{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)
.

Let us now consider the
{\alpha}
-th Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. We recall that
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})=G(\ell,V_{m})\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
, where
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}}-1}
is the linear subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k-1}
as given in (
7
). Take a hyperplane
\Pi
of
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m})
given by
F(X_{\alpha}:{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m))=\sum_{\beta\in I(\ell,m)}c_{\beta}X_{\beta}
. We may write

F=F_{\Delta({\alpha})}+F_{\nabla({\alpha})},\ \ \text{where}\ \ F_{\Delta({\alpha})}=\sum_{\beta\in\Delta({\alpha})}c_{\beta}X_{\beta}\ \ \text{and}\ \ F_{\nabla({\alpha})}=\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})}c_{\beta}X_{\beta}.

We see that a hyperplane
\Pi
restricts to a hyperplane
\Pi_{\alpha}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
if
F_{\nabla({\alpha})}\neq 0
. Indeed the restricted hyperplane
\Pi_{\alpha}
is defined as the set of zeroes of
F_{\nabla({\alpha})}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
.
Moreover, it is well-known (cf.
[
11
, Remark 5.3.4]
) that the
{\alpha}
-th Schubert variety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
of
G(\ell,V_{m})
is also a non-degenerate subset of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
. Consequently, the hyperplane
\Pi
described above contains the Schubert variety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
if and only if
F_{\nabla({\alpha})}=0
. While a hyperplane of the Plücker space restricts
uniquely
to a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
, a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
can be
extended
to a hyperplane of the Plücker space in several ways. That is, if
\Pi_{\alpha}
is a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
given by the vanishing set of
F_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})}c_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}
, then one can construct a hyperplane
\Pi
of
{\mathbb{P}}(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m})
given by
F=F_{\alpha}+\sum_{\gamma\in\Delta({\alpha})}c_{\gamma}X_{\gamma}
such that
\Pi\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}=\Pi_{\alpha}
. One readily checks that the choice of
\Pi
mentioned above is not unique. A few remarks are in order and we summarize them below.

Remark 2.1
.

(a)

Our notation
C_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
to define the
{\alpha}
-th Schubert variety, emphasizing its dependence on the basis
\mathcal{B}
, while letting
G(\ell,V_{m})
denote the Grassmannian independent of basis might lead to some confusion. As mentioned earlier, we see that when we change the basis of
V_{m}
, it only induces a projective linear isomorphism of the Plücker space that fixes
G(\ell,V_{m})
as a set. It is understood, of course, that the homogeneous coordinates of the Plücker space get changed. However, for two bases
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
of
V_{m}
, the sets
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
and
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
can be different as subsets.

(b)

As before, let
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
be a fixed basis of
V_{m}
and for each
i=1,\dots,m
, let
V_{i}
denote the subspace spanned by
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{i}\}
. It is easily seen that
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})=\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{{\alpha}_{\ell}},\mathcal{B}_{{\alpha}_{\ell}})
where
\mathcal{B}_{{\alpha}_{\ell}}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{{\alpha}_{\ell}}\}
. In what follows, we shall use this identity. Indeed, this means we can view
\Omega_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
as a subset of
G(\ell,V_{{\alpha}_{\ell}})
. From now on, when we mention the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, we will always assume that
{\alpha}_{\ell}=m
and that
V_{{\alpha}_{\ell}}=V_{m}
.

A nonzero element
z\in\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}
is said to be
decomposable
if there exist
w_{1},\dots,w_{m-\ell}\in V_{m}
such that
z=w_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{m-\ell}
. A hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}\right)
is said to be
decomposable
if it is given by a decomposable element of
\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}
. Thus a hyperplane
\Pi
of
{{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{\ell}V_{m}\right)}
given by the equation
\displaystyle{\sum_{{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)}}c_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}
is decomposable if
\displaystyle{\sum_{{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)}c_{\alpha}v_{{\alpha}^{\mathsf{C}}}}
is a decomposable element of
\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}
.

Remark 2.2
.

For a nonzero element
z\in\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}
, we define
{\mathcal{V}}(z)=\{x\in V_{m}:z\wedge x=0\}
. We have

(a)

[
12
, Section 4.1, Theorem 1.1]

z
is decomposable if and only if
\dim{\mathcal{V}}(z)=m-\ell
.

(b)

[
12
, Section 4.1, Theorem 1.3]
if
\ell=1
, then
z
is decomposable.

We now recall a suitable generalization of the notion of decomposable hyperplanes in the setting of Schubert subvarieties as given in
[
6
, Definition 2.1]
.

Definition 2.3

(Schubert Decomposability,
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose
{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)
and
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
be the
{\alpha}
-th Schubert subvariety of
G(\ell,V_{m})
with respect to the fixed basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
.
We divide
{\alpha}
into
blocks of consecutive components
as follows:

({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{p_{1}},{\alpha}_{p_{1}+1},\dots,{\alpha}_{p_{2}},\dots,{\alpha}_{p_{u}+1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}),

where
u,p_{0},\dots,p_{u+1}
are unique integers determined by
{\alpha}
satisfying

(a)

p_{0}:=0<p_{1}<\dots<p_{u}<p_{u+1}=:\ell
,

(b)

{\alpha}_{p_{i}+1}-{\alpha}_{p_{i}}\geq 2
for
1\leq i\leq u
, and

(c)

{\alpha}_{p_{i}-j}={\alpha}_{p_{i}}-j
for
1\leq i\leq u+1
and
1\leq j<p_{i}-p_{i-1}
.

This allows us to consider the
refined partial flag
of subspaces associated with
{\alpha}
. That is, we now consider the partial flag:

0\subset V_{{\alpha}_{p_{1}}}\subset\cdots\subset V_{{\alpha}_{p_{u}}}\subset V_{m}\ \ \ \text{where}\ \ V_{{\alpha}_{i}}=\text{span}\{v_{1},\dots,v_{{\alpha}_{i}}\}\ \text{for}\ i=p_{1},\dots,p_{u}.

An element
z\in\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}
is said to be
Schubert decomposable with respect to

\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
if
z
is decomposable and
\dim({\mathcal{V}}(z)\cap V_{{\alpha}_{p_{i}}})={\alpha}_{p_{i}}-p_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,u
. A hyperplane
\Pi_{\alpha}
of
\mathbb{P}^{k_{{\alpha}}-1}
given by
F_{\alpha}
is said to be a
Schubert decomposable hyperplane
if there exists a Schubert decomposable element
F\in\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}
such that
F_{\nabla({\alpha})}=F_{\alpha}
and the hyperplane
\Pi
defined by
F
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k-1}
restricts to
\Pi_{\alpha}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
.

From now onwards, we will denote by

Question 3.1
.

Let
\ell,m
be positive integers satisfying

(b)

Classify all the hyperplanes

.

As mentioned in the Introduction, the above theorem was proved in the special case when

3.1.
Enumeration of Schubert varieties over finite fields

The first question one would encounter is the following: How many points are there on a Schubert variety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
defined over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
? Indeed, if
{\alpha}=(m-\ell+1,\dots,m)
, then
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})=G(\ell,V_{m})
and its cardinality is given by the well-known
Gaussian Binomial coefficient

{m\brack\ell}_{q}
defined as follows:

{m\brack\ell}_{q}:=\frac{(q^{m}-1)\cdots(q^{m}-q^{\ell-1})}{(q^{\ell}-1)\cdots(q^{\ell}-q^{\ell-1})}.

The Gaussian binomials are well-studied in mathematics, especially in combinatorics. It is beyond the scope of the article to discuss several nice properties of them, but we refer the readers to
[
1
]
for a more leisurely reading on this topic.
We now look at the general cases of Schubert varieties
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. For this, let us look at the equation (
6
). From this, we already know the following:

(10)

|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})}|C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})}q^{\delta(\beta)}\ \ \text{where}\ \ \delta(\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}.

The last equality above follows from (
6
) and the well-known fact that
C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
is an affine space of dimension
\delta(\beta)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. This fact is easy to verify directly from looking at the matrices in right-row-reduced-echelon form that belong to
C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. It is difficult to trace back in the literature to find the first proof of the same, but we refer to
[
7
, Theorem 1]
and the references therein. To that end,
Now we present a rather weak upper bound for the number of points on
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. We begin with a Lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\ell\geq 2
and
{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)
, we have

\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})}q^{\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{\ell}}<\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{\ell}q^{{\alpha}_{1}+\dots+{\alpha}_{\ell}}.

Proof.

We prove this assertion by induction on
\ell
. We first look at the case when
\ell=2
. Fix
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},{\alpha}_{2})\in I(2,m)
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\nabla({\alpha})}q^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta_{1}=1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}\sum_{\beta_{2}=\beta_{1}+1}^{{\alpha}_{2}}q^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta_{1}=1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}q^{\beta_{1}}\left(\sum_{\beta_{2}=\beta_{1}+1}^{{\alpha}_{2}}q^{\beta_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta_{1}=1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}q^{\beta_{1}}\left(q^{\beta_{1}+1}\frac{q^{{\alpha}_{2}-\beta_{1}}-1}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\beta_{1}=1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}q^{\beta_{1}}q^{\beta_{1}+1}q^{{\alpha}_{2}-\beta_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{{\alpha}_{2}+2}}{q-1}\sum_{\beta_{1}=1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}q^{\beta_{1}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{{\alpha}_{2}+2}}{q-1}\frac{q^{{\alpha}_{1}}-1}{q-1}<q^{{\alpha}_{1}+{\alpha}_{2}}\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{2}.

Now assume that
\ell\geq 3
and the assertion is true for all every
{\alpha}\in I(\ell-1,m)
. Fix
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})\in I(\ell,m)
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell})\in\nabla({\alpha})}q^{\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell-1})\in\nabla({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell-1})}q^{\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{\ell-1}}\left(\sum_{\beta_{\ell}=\beta_{\ell-1}+1}^{{\alpha}_{\ell}}q^{\beta_{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell-1})\in\nabla({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell-1})}q^{\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{\ell-1}}q^{\beta_{\ell-1}+1}\left(\frac{q^{{\alpha}_{\ell}-\beta_{\ell-1}}-1}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle<\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell-1})\in\nabla({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell-1})}q^{\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{\ell-1}}q^{\beta_{\ell-1}+1}\left(\frac{q^{{\alpha}_{\ell}-\beta_{\ell-1}}}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{{\alpha}_{\ell}}\frac{q}{q-1}\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell-1})\in\nabla({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell-1})}q^{\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{\ell-1}}

\displaystyle<q^{{\alpha}_{\ell}}\frac{q}{q-1}q^{{\alpha}_{1}+\dots+{\alpha}_{\ell-1}}\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{\ell-1}=\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{\ell}q^{{\alpha}_{1}+\dots+{\alpha}_{\ell}}.

Here the strict inequality in the penultimate step follows from the induction hypothesis. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

We have
|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|<\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{\ell}q^{\delta({\alpha})}
.

Proof.

Follows directly from Lemma
3.4
, (
10
), and the definition
\delta({\alpha})={\alpha}_{1}+\dots+{\alpha}_{\ell}-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2}
for all
{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

(a)

In order to completely understand the number of points on
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, one may write:

(11)

|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=\sum_{i=0}^{\delta({\alpha})}a_{i}q^{i}.

The integers
a_{i}
equals the number of Schubert cells of dimension
i
in
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. It is in general not difficult to determine these quantities. However, it is conceivable that determination of
a_{i}
-s will give rise to a better bound for
|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|
.

(b)

A bound analogous to Proposition
3.5
for Gaussian binomials can be found in the literature. For example, it follows from
[
14
, Lemma 3.5]
that
{m\brack\ell}_{q}<\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-q-1}q^{\ell(m-\ell)}
. Indeed, in the special case when the concerned Schubert subvariety is a Grassmannian, we see that the above bound is stronger in general, while they coincide with our bound when
q=2
. We believe that the bound we presented above can be improved and leave this for future investigations.

3.2.
A combinatorial bound on Schubert subvarieties

As noted above, it is not always easy to explicitly determine the coefficients
a_{i}
as presented in equation (
11
). Here, our goal is to bypass the determination of these quantities while still obtaining some effective combinatorial identities on the number of points on Schubert varieties, which are of independent interest and facilitate calculations. We stick to our basic definitions of
\ell,m,{\alpha}
and work with a fixed ordered basis
\mathcal{B}
of
V_{m}
.
First, we need to introduce some more notations.

Definition 3.7
.

Suppose
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})\in I(\ell,m)
and
{\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}
are not consecutive integers. We define:

(a)

k:=\max\{j:a_{j+1}-a_{j}\geq 2\}
. Since
{\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}
are not consecutive integers, we have
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
.

(b)

{\alpha}^{\prime}:=({\alpha}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}^{\prime})
where

{\alpha}_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}{\alpha}_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ i\leq k\\
{\alpha}_{i}-1\ \ \text{if}\ \ \ i>k.\end{cases}

(c)

\widecheck{{\alpha}}:=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell-1})
.

(d)

For every
\beta\in I(\ell,m)
and
1\leq k\leq\ell
, define
\beta^{(k)}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})\in I(k,m)
.

(e)

For
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k})\in I(k,m)
with
\gamma\in\nabla({\alpha}^{(k)})
, we define

C_{\gamma}^{(k)}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}):=\left\{L\in\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}):\text{the first $k$ pivots of}\ M_{\mathcal{B}}(L)\ \text{occur on the columns}\ \gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}\right\}

Example 3.8
.

Suppose
{\alpha}=(3,4,6,7)\in I(4,7)
. In this case,
k=2
,
{\alpha}^{\prime}=(3,4,5,6)
and
\widecheck{{\alpha}}=(3,4,6)
. On the other hand, if
{\alpha}=(3,4,6,8)
, then we have
k=3
,
{\alpha}^{\prime}=(3,4,6,7)
and
\widecheck{{\alpha}}=(3,4,6)
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
{\alpha},\ell,m,{\alpha}^{\prime},\widecheck{{\alpha}}
be as in Definition
3.7
and as before, let
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
be a fixed ordered basis of
V_{m}
. We define

(a)

V_{i}=\text{span}\{v_{1},\dots,v_{i}\}
for
i=1,\dots,m
. In particular, we have the
m-1
dimensional subspace
V_{m-1}
of
V_{m}
that is spanned by
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m-1}\}
.

(b)

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m-1}\}
.

In particular, when
\ell\leq m-1
, the Schubert subvarieties
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
and
\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
are well-defined.

(i)

Using the definition of
{\alpha}^{\prime}
, we have

\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}):=\left\{L\in G(\ell,V_{m})\middle|\begin{array}[]{l}\dim L\cap V_{{\alpha}_{i}}\geq i\ \ \text{if}\ \ 1\leq i\leq k\\
\dim L\cap V_{{\alpha}_{i}-1}\geq i\ \ \text{if}\ \ k<i\leq\ell\end{array}\right\}.

(ii)

Similarly, with the definition of
\widecheck{{\alpha}}
, we have

\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}):=\{L\in G(\ell-1,V_{m-1})|\dim L\cap V_{{\alpha}_{i}}\geq i\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq i\leq\ell-1\}.

It is evident that
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\subset\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. Furthermore,
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
comes up with its own cellular decomposition. In fact, we may write

(12)

\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha}^{\prime})}C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}).

Let us note an interesting coincidence below.

Lemma 3.10
.

For each
\beta\in\nabla({\alpha}^{\prime})
, we have
C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
.

Proof.

Let
\beta\in\nabla(\alpha^{\prime})
. By definition,

C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=\left\{L\in G(\ell,V_{m-1}):\dim(L\cap V_{\beta_{i}})=i\text{ for all }i=1,\dots,\ell\right\}.

Since
V_{m-1}\subset V_{m}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m-1}\}\subset\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
, for any
\beta\in\nabla(\alpha^{\prime})
, the flag

0\subset V_{\beta_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset V_{\beta_{\ell}}

is identical whether regarded as a flag in
V_{m-1}
or in
V_{m}
. The defining rank conditions of the Schubert cell
C_{\beta}
in
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
and
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
coincide. Consequently, a subspace
L\subset V_{m-1}
satisfies the defining conditions of

C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
if and only if it satisfies those of

C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. Hence,

C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}),

as required.
∎

Let us now introduce some more definitions.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
{\alpha},\ell,k
be as in Definition
3.7
. Define a map
\phi:\nabla({\alpha}^{\prime})\to\nabla({\alpha})
given by

(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k},\beta_{k+1},\dots,\beta_{\ell})\mapsto(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k},\beta_{k+1}+1,\dots,\beta_{\ell}+1).

Evidently, the map
\phi
is well-defined. The map
\phi
induces a map

\Phi:\{C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|\beta\in\nabla({\alpha}^{\prime})\}\to\{C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})\}

given by

(13)

\Phi(C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}))=C_{\phi(\beta)}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}).

The Schubert cells of
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
that are not images of
\Phi
are called the
missing cells
.

Indeed, the definition of missing cells is not vacuous. One can easily check that the cell corresponding to
\beta=(1,\dots,\ell)
is a missing cell. Moreover, there are some missing cells of very high dimensions. For example, the cell corresponding to
{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{k},{\alpha}_{k}+1,{\alpha}_{k+2},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})
is a missing cell with dimension

\delta({\alpha}^{\prime\prime})=\delta({\alpha})-({\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1)
. In fact, one could easily check that any
\gamma\in I(\ell,m)
with
\gamma_{k+1}-\gamma_{k}=1
corresponds to a missing cell.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
{\alpha},\ell,k
be as in Definition
3.7
. Define
{\alpha}_{0}=(1,\dots,\ell-1,m)
. For
\beta\in\nabla({\alpha}^{\prime})
, we have

(a)

|C_{\phi(\beta)}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=q^{\ell-k}|C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|
.

(b)

q^{{\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1}(|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\ell-k}|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|)\geq\begin{cases}q^{\delta({\alpha})}\ \ &\text{if}\ {\alpha}={\alpha}_{0}\\
q^{\delta({\alpha})}+q^{\delta({{\alpha}^{\prime}})}&\text{if}\ {\alpha}\neq{\alpha}_{0}\end{cases}
.

Proof.

(a)

The first assertion follows since the left hand side equals
q^{\delta(\phi(\beta))}
, while the right hand side equals
q^{\delta(\beta)+\ell-k}
and by definition
\delta(\phi(\beta))=\delta(\beta)+\ell-k
.

(b)

Let us denote by
I_{1}=\{\gamma\in\nabla({\alpha}):\gamma=\phi(\beta)\ \text{for some}\ \beta\in\nabla({\alpha}^{\prime})\}
and
I_{2}=\nabla({\alpha})\setminus I_{1}
. Note that for every
\gamma\in I_{2}
, the cell
C_{\gamma}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
is a missing cell, as defined above. We have

\displaystyle\ \ |\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\ell-k}|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\nabla({\alpha})}|C_{\gamma}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\ell-k}\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha}^{\prime})}|C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha}^{\prime})}(|C_{\phi(\beta)}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}))|-q^{\ell-k}|C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|)+\sum_{\gamma\in I_{2}}|C_{\gamma}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in I_{2}}|C_{\gamma}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|.

It is easy to see that for any
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})
, with
k\leq\ell-1
, the cell corresponding to the tuple
\tilde{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{k},{\alpha}_{k}+1,{\alpha}_{k+2},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})
is a missing cell. Moreover,

q^{{\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1}q^{\delta(\tilde{\alpha})}=q^{\delta({\alpha})}.

In particular, if
k=\ell-1
, then
\tilde{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell-1},{\alpha}_{\ell-1}+1)
and the same equality as above holds. This proves that

q^{{\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1}(|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\ell-k}|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|)\geq q^{\delta({\alpha})}

for every
{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)
.
Moreover, if
{\alpha}\neq{\alpha}_{0}
, then we can produce another missing cell
\bar{{\alpha}}
such that
{\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1+\delta(\bar{\alpha})\geq\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})
as given below. Let

k^{\prime}=\begin{cases}1\ &\text{if}\ {\alpha}_{1}>1\\
\min\{j:{\alpha}_{j}-{\alpha}_{j-1}>1\}\ &\text{if}\ {\alpha}_{1}=1\end{cases}.

Clearly,
k^{\prime}
is well-defined, thanks to the condition that
{\alpha}\neq{\alpha}_{0}
.
Define
\bar{\alpha}=(\bar{\alpha}_{1},\dots,\bar{\alpha}_{\ell})
as follows:

\bar{\alpha}_{i}=\begin{cases}{\alpha}_{i}-1&\text{if}\ i=k^{\prime}\\
{\alpha}_{k}+1\ &\text{if}\ i=k+1\\
{\alpha}_{i}\ &\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

One checks that
{\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1+\delta(\bar{\alpha})=\delta({\alpha})-1\geq\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})
.

This completes the proof.
∎

We state and prove the following bound. Undoubtedly, the bound looks unmotivated at this stage, but it will be central in the proof of our main theorem.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
{\alpha},\ell,k
be as in Definition
3.7
. For
q>q_{0}(\ell):=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}}{2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}-1}

q^{{\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1}(|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\ell-k}|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|)>q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}+q^{m-\ell}|\Omega_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|-q^{\delta({\alpha})}.

Proof.

We distinguish the proof into two cases.

Case 1:

Suppose that
{\alpha}=(1,\dots,\ell-1,m)
.
From Lemma
3.12
, we have

q^{{\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1}(|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\ell-k}|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|)\geq q^{\delta({\alpha})}=q^{m-\ell}.

On the other hand, we have
|\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|=1.

Combining all these, we see that the right-hand side of the desired inequality equals
q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}
. The assertion follows since
q^{\delta({\alpha})}>q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}
.

Case 2:

Suppose that
{\alpha}
is not of the form
(1,\dots,\ell-1,m)
. Again Lemma
3.12
shows that the left-hand side of the desired inequality is bigger than or equal to
q^{\delta({\alpha})}+q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}
. On the other hand Proposition
3.5
shows that,

\displaystyle q^{m-\ell}|\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|+q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}-q^{\delta({\alpha})}

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{\ell-1}q^{\delta(\widecheck{{\alpha}})}q^{m-\ell}+q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}-q^{\delta({\alpha})}.

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{\ell-1}q^{\delta({\alpha})}+q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}-q^{\delta({\alpha})}.

In order to prove the assertion, it is enough to show that

q^{\delta({\alpha})}+q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}>\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{\ell-1}q^{\delta({\alpha})}+q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}-q^{\delta({\alpha})},

which is equivalent to the inequality

\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)^{\ell-1}<2.

The above inequality holds if and only if

\left(\frac{q}{q-1}\right)<2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}\iff\frac{1}{q-1}<2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}-1\iff q>1+\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}-1}=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}}{2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}-1}=q_{0}(\ell).

This completes the proof.
∎

Before concluding the subsection, we remark that the function
q_{0}(\ell)
is increasing in
\ell
.

3.3.
A decomposition of Schubert subvarieties and hyperplane sections

This subsection is motivated by
[
3
, Section 3]
. We will continue with the notations introduced in the previous sections and subsections. We note that the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
is naturally contained in the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. One can prove it directly using the definitions. On the other hand, this is a direct consequence of Lemma
3.10
. Independently, one can also see that
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\cap G(\ell,V_{m-1})\subseteq\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. Lemma
3.10
implies that

(14)

\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})=\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\bigsqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})\cap\nabla({\alpha})}C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\right).

As in
[
3
, Section 3]
, we decompose
\displaystyle\left(\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})\cap\nabla({\alpha})}C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\right)
into
strings
to facilitate computations. First, we note that

(15)

\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})\cap\nabla({\alpha})=\{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha}):\beta_{\ell}=m\}.

We recall that the condition
\beta_{\ell}=m
mentioned above is a consequence of our running assumption that in our Schubert variety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, the index
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})
satisfies
{\alpha}_{\ell}=m
.
Before delving into the technical details, for the sake of clarity, let us revisit our notations related to matrix representations from (
4
). Recall that the bijection in (
4
) associates every element of
G(\ell,V_{m})
to a matrix in
\mathcal{M}(\ell,m)
with respect to the fixed basis
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
. Let us denote by
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}}(\ell,m)
the image of this map restricted to
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
and
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
that of
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
. In particular, we have the following description of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\ell,m)

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\ell,m)=\{M\in\mathcal{M}(\ell,m)|\ \text{the pivot in the $i$-th row occur on $\beta_{i}$th column with}\ \beta_{i}\leq{\alpha}_{i},\ \forall i\}.

The description of
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
can be given similarly. An important remark on
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
is in order. As per our continued assumptions, we are treating the elements of
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
as
\ell\times(m-1)
matrices. However, when we say that we view
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
as a subset of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\ell,m)
, we use the natural inclusion
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
into
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\ell,m)
by juxtaposing a zero column to the right of each element of
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
. Thus, we may regard the elements of
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
as
\ell\times m
matrices with entries in
{\mathbb{F}}
so that they can safely be regarded as elements of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\ell,m)
.
Inspired from
[
3
, Section 3]
, we will provide a decomposition of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\ell,m)
into
\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
and union of
strings
. This decomposition is essentially obtained through (
14
) above.
Let us write

T_{\alpha}(\ell,m):=\{M\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\ell,m)|\text{the pivot of the last row of $\mathcal{M}$ occurs on $m$-th column}\}.

One can see that, under the correspondence in (
4
), the set
T_{\alpha}(\ell,m)
corresponds to

\mathfrak{F}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}):=\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\setminus\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=\bigsqcup_{\beta\in\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})\cap\nabla({\alpha})}C_{\beta}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}).

Indeed, the second equality is a consequence of (
14
). It is understood that a stratification of
T_{\alpha}(\ell,m)
into strings gives rise to a decomposition of
\mathfrak{F}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
into smaller components.

Let
M\in T_{{\alpha}}(\ell,m)
. We may write

(16)

M=\begin{pmatrix}\widecheck{M}&&{\bf 0}\\
c_{M}&&1\end{pmatrix},

where
\widecheck{M}\in\mathcal{M}_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)
, the symbol
{\bf 0}
denotes a column matrix of length
(\ell-1)
with all entries equal to
{0}
, whereas
c_{M}
is a row vector of length
m-1
, and
1
is the multiplicative identity of
{\mathbb{F}}
. Since
M
is in right-row-reduced echelon form, so is
\widecheck{M}
, which justifies the inclusion
\widecheck{M}\in\mathcal{M}_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)
. The pivot columns of
\widecheck{M}
are determined by those of
M
, and the entries in
c_{M}
on the pivot columns of
\widecheck{M}
are zero.
Let us denote
(M_{n_{1}},\dots,M_{n_{m-\ell}})
the entries in the non-pivotal columns of
c_{M}
, where
n_{1}<\cdots<n_{m-\ell}
. This defines a map

s:T_{\alpha}(\ell,m)\to{\mathbb{F}}^{m-\ell}.

The map
s
is clearly surjective. We claim that
s^{-1}(\nu)
can be identified with
\mathcal{M}_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)
for any
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{m-\ell})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m-\ell}
.
Define

(17)

\phi_{\nu}:\mathcal{M}_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)\to s^{-1}(\nu)\ \ \ \text{by}\ \ \ M^{\prime}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}{M^{\prime}}&&0\\
c_{M^{\prime},\nu}&&1\end{pmatrix},

where
c_{M^{\prime},\nu}
is uniquely determined by the non-pivot positions of
M^{\prime}
and
\nu
. Clearly, the map
\phi_{\nu}
is a bijection. Using the bijection
\mathcal{M}_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)\longleftrightarrow\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
, we note that
|s^{-1}(\nu)|=|\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|
for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m-\ell}
. Moreover, it follows trivially that
s^{-1}(\nu)\cap s^{-1}(\nu^{\prime})=\emptyset
whenever
\nu\neq\nu^{\prime}
. Thus,

(18)

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\ell,m)=\mathcal{M}_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)\bigsqcup T_{\alpha}(\ell,m)\ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ T_{\alpha}(\ell,m)=\bigsqcup_{\nu\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m-\ell}}s^{-1}(\nu).

We call
s^{-1}(\nu)
,
the
\nu
-th string of
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
with respect to
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
. We look at a quick enumerative application of the above decomposition.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Then

(a)

|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|+q^{m-\ell}|\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|
.

(b)

with
k
as defined above and
q>q_{0}(\ell)
, we have

\displaystyle q^{{\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1}(|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-

\displaystyle q^{\ell-k}|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|)

\displaystyle>q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}+(|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|)-q^{\delta({\alpha})}.

Proof.

Part (a) follows at once from (
18
) and the discussion above, while part (b) is a consequence of part (a) and Theorem
3.13
.
∎

We would like to understand how certain hyperplanes behave with the above decomposition of
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. Recall the following inclusion of
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\ \text{and}\ \Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
in
G(\ell,V_{m})
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{k_{\alpha^{\prime}}-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{k_{\alpha}-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(\ell,V_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{k-1}}

Let
\Pi_{\alpha}
be a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
given by a homogeneous polynomial
F_{\alpha}(X_{\beta}|\beta\in\nabla({\alpha}))
.

(I)

Note that
\Pi_{\alpha}
does not contain
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
.
This follows from the non-degeneracy of
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
. Thus
F_{\alpha}\neq 0
. We write

F_{\alpha}=F_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(X_{\beta}|\beta\in\nabla({\alpha}^{\prime}))+F^{\prime}(X_{\beta}|\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})).

Then
\Pi_{\alpha}
restricts to a hyperplane
\Pi_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}=\Pi_{\alpha}\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}-1}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}-1}
given by the vanishing of
F_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}-1}
, provided
F_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}\neq 0
.

(II)

Suppose that
\Pi_{\alpha}
contains
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
. The non-degeneracy of
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}-1}
implies that the assumption is equivalent to the statement that
F_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}=0
. Consequently, the polynomial
F_{\alpha}
is a linear combination of
X_{\beta}
as
\beta
varies in the set
\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})
. We note that for each such
\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})
, we have
\beta_{\ell}={\alpha}_{\ell}=m
.
It also follows from (
18
) that

(19)

|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|+\sum_{\nu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m-\ell}}|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap s^{-1}(\nu)|.

Since
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\subseteq\Pi_{\alpha}
, we see that
|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|=|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|
. Consequently, the equality above translates to

(20)

|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|+\sum_{\nu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m-\ell}}|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap s^{-1}(\nu)|.

We will, until the end of this section, assume that
\Pi_{\alpha}
is a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
satisfying (II) above.
In order to compute
|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|
, it is now imperative to better understand the set
\Pi_{\alpha}\cap s^{-1}(\nu)
for each
\nu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m-\ell}
. For a fixed
\nu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m-\ell}
, recall that set
s^{-1}(\nu)
is given by

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\widecheck{M}&&\bf{0}\\
c&&1\end{pmatrix}:\widecheck{M}\in\mathcal{M}_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)\right\},

which as per the above discussion is in one-one correspondence with
M_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)
.
We thus obtain the following bijections given by natural identifications:

(21)

s^{-1}(\nu)\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)\longleftrightarrow\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}).

Take
M\in s^{-1}(\nu)
. For
\beta\in\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})\cap\nabla({\alpha})
, we see that
\beta_{\ell}=m
and consequently,
X_{\beta}(M)=X_{\widecheck{\beta},\nu}(\widecheck{M})
, where the latter is determined by taking the
(\ell-1)\times(\ell-1)
minor of
M
given by the columns numbered
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell-1}
and the first
\ell-1
rows.
For

F_{\alpha}=\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})\cap\nabla({\alpha})}c_{\beta}X_{\beta},\ \ \text{we write}\ \ F_{\widecheck{{\alpha}},\nu}=\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})\cap\nabla({\alpha})}c_{\beta}X_{\widecheck{\beta},\nu}.

For each
M\in\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\setminus\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
, we have

F_{\alpha}(M)=F_{\widecheck{{\alpha}},\nu}(M)
. Before proceeding further, we remark that the evaluation is
independent
of
\nu
. Namely, if
M,M^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\setminus\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
, with
M\in s^{-1}(\nu)
and
M^{\prime}\in s^{-1}(\mu)
for some distinct
\nu,\mu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m-\ell}
, but
\widecheck{M}=\widecheck{M^{\prime}}
, then
F_{{\alpha}}(M)=F_{{\alpha}}(M^{\prime})
. In particular, we have

|\{M\in s^{-1}(\nu):F_{{\alpha}}(M)=0\}|=|\{M\in s^{-1}(\mu):F_{{\alpha}}(M)=0\}|\ \ \text{for}\ \ \nu,\mu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m-\ell}.

Combining this with (
20
), we obtain

(22)

|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|+q^{m-\ell}|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap s^{-1}(\nu)|

Thus we may fix
\nu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m-\ell}
and focus on computing
|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap s^{-1}(\nu)|
.
We have already identified
s^{-1}(\nu)
with the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
. Let us ponder on the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
a little more before proceeding further. Indeed,
\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\subset G(\ell-1,V_{m-1})
. Now
G(\ell-1,V_{m-1})
is embedded inside the projective space

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\displaystyle{\bigwedge^{\ell-1}}V_{m-1}\right)
which is a projective space of dimension
\widecheck{k}-1
, where
\widecheck{k}={m-1\choose\ell-1}
. Moreover, as usual, the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
is obtained by taking an appropriate linear section of
G(\ell-1,V_{m-1})
given by the polynomials
X_{\widecheck{\beta}}
where
\widecheck{\beta}\in I(\ell-1,m-1)
with
\widecheck{\beta}\not\leq\widecheck{{\alpha}}
.
We see that these
\widecheck{\beta}=(\widecheck{\beta}_{1},\dots,\widecheck{\beta}_{\ell-1})
-s get naturally identified with
\beta\in\Delta({\alpha})\subset I(\ell,m)
with
\beta_{i}=\widecheck{\beta}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,\ell-1
and
\beta_{\ell}=m
.
In fact, if we look at the identification
X_{\widecheck{\beta}}
as an element of
\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m-1}\subset\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}
, then it is actually equal to
X_{\beta}
.
At any rate, we may assume that
\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
is embedded in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}-1}}
, where the latter is given by the linear subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}-1}
mentioned above. Thus, under the identification of
s^{-1}(\nu)
with
\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
described in (
21
), we see that the set
s^{-1}(\nu)\cap\Pi_{\alpha}
gets identified with the set
\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\cap\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}
where
\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}
is the hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}-1}
.

To summarize, let
\Pi_{\alpha}
is a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
satisfying (I) and (II) above. Then
\Pi_{\alpha}
is given by a linear homogeneous polynomial

F_{\alpha}=\sum_{{\beta\in\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})\cap\nabla({\alpha})}}c_{\beta}X_{\beta}.

Then
\Pi_{\alpha}
restricts to the hyperplane
\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}
given by

F_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}=\sum_{{\beta\in\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})\cap\nabla({\alpha})}}c_{\beta}X_{\widecheck{\beta}}.

Remark 3.15
.

We readily see that a hyperplane satisfying (I) and (II) above can not contain
s^{-1}(\nu)
. Otherwise, it follows from (
21
), (
22
), and Proposition
3.14
that
|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|
, which implies that
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\subset\Pi_{\alpha}
, contradicting (I). In particular, we have
|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap s^{-1}(\nu)|<|s^{-1}(\nu)|.

Let us now introduce some notations for ease of reference.

Definition 3.16
.

Let
{\alpha},{\alpha}^{\prime},\widecheck{{\alpha}},\ell,m
be as defined above. With
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we define

(a)

e_{\alpha}(\ell,m):=\max\{|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\cap\Pi_{\alpha}|:\Pi_{\alpha}\ \text{is a hyperplane of}\ {\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}}-1}\}
.

(b)

e_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1):=\max\{|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\cap\Pi_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}|:\Pi_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}\ \text{is a hyperplane of}\ {\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}-1}\}
.

(c)

e_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1):=\max\{|\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})\cap\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}|:\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}\ \text{is a hyperplane of}\ {\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}-1}\}
.

We conclude this section with the following Proposition summarizing the discussion in the current section which can be directly used in the proof of our main theorem.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. If
\Pi_{\alpha}
is a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
such that
\Pi_{\alpha}
contains
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
, then

|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|\leq|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|+q^{m-\ell}e_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1).

Proof.

From (
22
), we know that
|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|+q^{m-\ell}|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap s^{-1}(\nu)|
. Moreover, as mentioned in Remark
3.15
, the hyperplane
\Pi_{\alpha}
does not contain
s^{-1}(\nu)
. Thus
|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap s^{-1}(\nu)|=|\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}\cap\Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|\leq e_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1).
The assertion now follows trivially.
∎

4.
Main Theorem

Throughout this section, we will assume that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is a finite field with
q
elements. All the notations
V_{m},{\alpha},{\alpha}^{\prime},\widecheck{{\alpha}},\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}),\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}),\ \text{and}\ \Omega_{\widecheck{\alpha}}(\ell-1,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
are as discussed in the previous sections. Before presenting our first result, which is a Schubert-analog of
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
, we define a special type of containment of Schubert subvarieties.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\ell\leq m-1
.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be as above. Let
W_{m-1}
be an
(m-1)
dimensional subspace of
V_{m}
with an ordered basis
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{m-1}\}
. As usual, we denote by
W_{i}=Span\{w_{1},\dots,w_{i}\}
for
i=1,\dots,m-1
. For
{\alpha}^{\prime}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{k},{\alpha}_{k+1}-1,\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}-1)
as defined above,
we say that
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
is
strictly contained in

\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
if
V_{{\alpha}_{i}}=W_{{\alpha}_{i}}\ \text{for}\ i=1,\dots,k
and
V_{{\alpha}_{k}}\subset W_{m-1}\subset V_{m}
.
In this case, we write
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\leq\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
and define

\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}):=\{\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\leq\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\}.

Remark 4.2
.

Before proceeding, it is worth understanding the above definition and its consequences a bit more carefully. Let us take
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. We write
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{m-1}\}
which serves as the fixed ordered basis of
W_{m-1}
. Let us extend it to an ordered basis
\tilde{B}=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{m-1},w_{m}\}
of
V_{m}
. Let us consider the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\tilde{\mathcal{B}})
. This is given by the flag

V_{{\alpha}_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset V_{{\alpha}_{k}}\subset W_{m-(\ell-k)+1}\subset\cdots\subset W_{m-1}\subset V_{m}.

Note that, for any
L\in\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, we have the Schubert conditions:

\dim(L\cap V_{{\alpha}_{i}})\geq i
for
i=1,\dots,\ell
.
It is clear that
i=m-(\ell-k)+1,\dots,m-1
, we have
\dim(L\cap W_{i})\geq i
. As a consequence, we have
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\subset\Omega_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\tilde{\mathcal{B}})
. A similar argument establishes the reverse inclusion, consequently implying that
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})=\Omega_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\tilde{\mathcal{B}})
. Moreover, as per Definition
3.9
(b), we have
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}_{1}
.

(a)

If a hyperplane
\Pi_{\alpha}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
contains
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
for some
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, then we may assume, after a change of coordinates (induced by the change of bases from
\mathcal{B}
to
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
) that
\Pi_{\alpha}
contains
\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
. By doing this, we can use the tools we have developed above, as in Proposition
3.17
, among others.

(b)

Next, suppose
\Pi_{\alpha}
is a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
such that
\Pi_{\alpha}
does not contain
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
. Again, with respect to the new coordinate system on
{\mathbb{P}}^{k-1}
and in particular
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
, we may assume that
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})={\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}-1}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. We see that
\Pi_{\alpha}\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}-1}
is a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}-1}
and we have

|\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\cap\Pi_{\alpha}|\leq e_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1)
.

We begin with the following proposition concerning the cardinalities of
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
and some of its special subsets.

Proposition 4.3
.

With the notations as above, we have

(23)

|\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=\frac{q^{m-{\alpha}_{k}}-1}{q-1}.

Moreover, for
L\in\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, we have

(24)

|\{\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}):L\in\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1}\}|\geq\frac{q^{(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(\ell-k)}-1}{q-1}.

Proof.

Note that
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\leq\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
if and only if
V_{{\alpha}_{i}}=W_{{\alpha}_{i}}
for all
i=1,\dots,k
and
V_{{\alpha}_{k}}\subset W_{m-1}\subset V_{m}
. Moreover, for every pairwise distinct
W_{m-1}
and
W_{m-1}^{\prime}
satisfying the above condition, we get pairwise distinct Schubert subvarieties
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
and
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime})
, where
\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime}
is obtained by using the partial flag obtained from the partial flag defining
\mathcal{B}_{1}
by replacing
W_{m-1}
with
W_{m-1}^{\prime}
. Thus

|\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=|\{W_{m-1}:V_{{\alpha}_{k}}\subset W_{m-1}\subset V_{m}\}|=\frac{q^{m-{\alpha}_{k}}-1}{q-1}.

This proves (
23
). Using a similar argument, we see that

\displaystyle|\{\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1}):L\in\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\leq\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\}|

\displaystyle=|\{W_{m-1}:L+V_{{\alpha}_{k}}\subset W_{m-1}\subset V_{m}\}|

\displaystyle=|\{W_{m-1}:L+V_{{\alpha}_{k}}\subset W_{m-1}\subset V_{m},\ \dim(L+V_{{\alpha}_{k}})\leq{\alpha}_{k}+\ell-k\}|

\displaystyle\geq|{\mathbb{P}}^{m-({\alpha}_{k}+\ell-k)-1}|=\frac{q^{(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(\ell-k)}-1}{q-1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Pi_{\alpha}
be a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
. Then

|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|\leq\left(\frac{q^{m-{\alpha}_{k}}-1}{q^{(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(\ell-k)}-1}\right)M_{\alpha},

where
M_{\alpha}=\max\{|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},B_{1})|:\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},B_{1})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\}.

Proof.

We define the following incidence set
\mathfrak{I}
and count it in two ways:

\mathfrak{I}:=\left\{\left(L,\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\right)\in\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\times\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B}):\ L\in\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\right\}.

One one hand, we have

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{I}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})}\#\{\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})|L\in\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\leq\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{L\in\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})}\frac{q^{(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(\ell-k)}-1}{q-1}

\displaystyle=|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|\frac{q^{(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(\ell-k)}-1}{q-1}.

The inequality above is a consequence of (
24
). On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{I}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\leq\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})}\#\{L\in\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\leq\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})}M_{\alpha}=|\{\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\leq\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\}|M_{\alpha}=\frac{q^{m-{\alpha}_{k}}-1}{q-1}M_{\alpha}.

The last equality follows from (
23
).
We have thus obtained

|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|\frac{q^{(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(\ell-k)}-1}{q-1}\leq|\mathfrak{J}|\leq\frac{q^{m-{\alpha}_{k}}-1}{q-1}M_{\alpha}.

The assertion now follows trivially.
∎

Let us now include an auxiliary Lemma that will be useful in the computations.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
q>q_{0}(\ell)=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}}{2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}-1}
, we have

\frac{q^{m-{\alpha}_{k}}-1}{q^{(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(\ell-k)}-1}\left(|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|-q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}\right)<|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\delta({\alpha})}.

Proof.

Note that the inequality in the assertion is equivalent to the inequality:

\displaystyle q^{(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(\ell-k)}(|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-

\displaystyle q^{\ell-k}|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|)

\displaystyle>(|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-|\Omega_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|)-q^{\delta({\alpha})}+q^{\delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}.

It follows from the definition of
k
that
m={\alpha}_{\ell}={\alpha}_{k+1}+(\ell-k-1)
. Consequently,
(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(l-k)={\alpha}_{k+1}-{\alpha}_{k}-1
. The assertion now follows from Proposition
3.14
(b).
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Before proving our main theorem, let us warm up by considering a few trivial cases. As before, let
1\leq\ell<m
,
V_{m}
a vector space of dimension
m
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with an ordered basis
\mathcal{B}
and
{\alpha}\in I(\ell,m)
. Our goal will be to prove the following results, when
q>q_{0}(\ell)
:

(a)

e_{\alpha}(\ell,m)=|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\delta({\alpha})}
.

(b)

If
\Pi_{\alpha}
is a hyperplane such that
|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\delta({\alpha})}
, then
\Pi_{\alpha}
is Schubert decomposable.

As we have mentioned in Remark
3.3
(a), it was proved by Ghorpade and Singh
[
6
]
that if
\Pi_{\alpha}
is a hyperplane that satisfies
|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\delta({\alpha})}
and
\Pi_{\alpha}
is decomposable, that is
\Pi_{\alpha}
is given by a decomposable hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k-1}
, then
\Pi_{\alpha}
is Schubert decomposable. Thus, while proving our main theorem, we will only restrict ourselves to proving that a hyperplane attaining the above bound is decomposable. Also, we will settle a few extreme cases completely here, so that we can ignore them in our main proof.

(I)

Suppose
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})
are consecutive. In this case,
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
is the Grassmannian
G(\ell,V_{m})
and the result is known in this case due to Nogin
[
13
]
. Moreover, a method similar to this article was used in
[
3
]
to obtain an alternative proof of this result.

(II)

Let us now consider the case when
\ell=m-1
. In this case
G(\ell,V_{m})
is the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1}
. We write
I(\ell,m)=\{\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{m}\}
, where
\Gamma_{i}=(1,2,\dots,i-1,\widecheck{i},i+1,\dots,m)
for
i=1,\dots,m
. That is, each
\Gamma_{i}
is obtained by writing down the string of consecutive integers
1,\dots,m
and dropping the integer
i
. Also, due to our standing convention, we do not need to consider the Schubert subvariety corresponding to
\Gamma_{m}=(1,\dots,m-1)
. Let us now proceed to prove part (a) above and fix
{\alpha}_{i}
. Note that, with respect to a fixed basis
\mathcal{B}
of
V_{m}
,
\Omega_{\Gamma_{i}}(m-1,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
is a linear subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1}
of codimension
(i-1)
. Consequently,
|\Omega_{\Gamma_{i}}(m-1,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|=1+\dots+q^{m-i}
. Clearly, a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1}
, that does not contain
\Omega_{\Gamma_{i}}(m-1,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
intersect it at most at
1+\dots+q^{m-i-1}
many points. This proves part (a). Moreover, part (b) is trivially true since any hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{m-1}V_{m}\right)
is decomposable.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
q>q_{0}(\ell)=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}}{2^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}-1}
and
\ell,m
be positive integers satisfying
2\leq\ell<m
. For
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})\in I(\ell,m)
and any hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
, we have

|\Pi_{\alpha}\cap\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|\leq e_{\alpha}(\ell,m):=|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\delta({\alpha})}.

Moreover, the bound is attained if and only if
\Pi_{\alpha}
is Schubert Decomposable.

Proof.

We prove the assertions by induction on
m
. Note that the hypothesis mandates
m\geq 3
. When
m=3
, we have
(\ell,m)=(2,3)
, and the assertions follow from the remark above.
We may now assume that
m>3
and that both assertions are true for Schubert subvarieties
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m-1},\mathcal{B})
for any vector space
V_{m-1}
of dimension
m-1
and
\ell\leq m-1
.
Let
V_{m}
be a vector space of dimension
m
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with a fixed ordered basis
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
. For
{\alpha}=({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell})\in I(\ell,m)
, we consider the Schubert subvariety
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. In view of Remark
4.6
, we may assume that
{\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{\ell}
are not completely consecutive.
If
\ell=m-1
, then the assertion follows from Remark
4.6
(II). In fact, the remark shows that the result is true even without the hypothesis that
q>q_{0}(\ell)
. Thus, we may assume that
2\leq\ell\leq m-2
.

Suppose
\Pi_{\alpha}
is a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{k_{\alpha}-1}
. We distinguish the proof in two cases:

Case 1:

Suppose
\Pi_{\alpha}
does not contain
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
for any
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. In view of Remark
4.2
(b) and induction hypothesis, we have, for
q>q_{0}(\ell)

|\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\cap\Pi_{\alpha}|\leq|\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})|-q^{\delta({{\alpha}^{\prime}})}.

Since this is true for all elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
, we may assume that
M_{{\alpha}}\leq|\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})|-q^{\delta({{\alpha}^{\prime}})}
as in Lemma
4.4
and obtain

|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\cap\Pi_{\alpha}|\leq\left(\frac{q^{m-{\alpha}_{k}}-1}{q^{(m-{\alpha}_{k})-(\ell-k)}-1}\right)\left(|\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})|-q^{\delta({{\alpha}^{\prime}})}\right).

Since
q>q_{0}(\ell)
, using Lemma
4.5
, we have

|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\cap\Pi_{\alpha}|<|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})|-q^{\delta({\alpha})},

as desired. We note that, the equality does not occur in this case.

Case 2:

Suppose
\Pi_{\alpha}
contains
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
for some element in
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
. Proposition
3.17
applies, and yields

|\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\cap\Pi_{\alpha}|\leq|\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,m-1,\mathcal{B}_{1})|+q^{m-\ell}e_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1).

Since, by assumption
q>q_{0}(\ell)
and
q_{0}(\ell)>q_{0}(\ell-1)
, the induction hypothesis implies that
e_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)=|\Omega_{\widecheck{a}}(\ell-1,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime})|-q^{\delta(\widecheck{{\alpha}})}
. The desired inequality now follows from Proposition
3.14
(a).

Note that, a possible equality arises if and only if
\Pi_{\alpha}
contains an element
\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})\in\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})
and that for each string
s^{-1}(\nu)
of
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\setminus\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
the equality
|s^{-1}(\nu)\cap\Pi_{\alpha}|=e_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell-1,m-1)
holds. As noted in Remark
4.2
, we have
\Omega_{\alpha}(\ell,V_{m},\mathcal{B})\setminus\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\mathcal{B}_{1})=\Omega_{{\alpha}}(\ell,V_{m},\tilde{\mathcal{B}})\setminus\Omega_{{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\tilde{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime})
. Thus, we may use the bijection

s^{-1}(\nu)\longleftrightarrow\Omega_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}(\ell,W_{m-1},\tilde{B}^{\prime})
as discussed before. By induction hypothesis,
\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}
is Schubert decomposable and hence decomposable.

We claim that, the hyperplanes
\Pi_{\alpha}
and
\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}
are given by the same element of
{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigwedge^{m-\ell}V_{m}\right)
.
Note that if the hyperplane
\Pi_{\alpha}
is given by the polynomial
F_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}c_{\beta}X_{\beta}
, then in terms of
(m-\ell)
-wedges, the polynomial
F_{\alpha}
can be rewritten as

F_{\alpha}=\displaystyle{\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}}c_{\beta}v_{\beta^{\mathsf{c}}},

where for every
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell})\in\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})
, we have
v_{\beta^{\mathsf{c}}}=v_{\beta_{1}}^{\prime}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{\beta_{m-\ell}^{\prime}}
such that
\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell}\}\cup\{\beta_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\beta_{m-\ell}^{\prime}\}=\{1,\dots,m\}
. Since, for all the
\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})
, we have
\beta_{\ell}=m
, we see that
\beta_{j}^{\prime}<m
, for all
j=1,\dots,m-\ell
.
On the other hand, given a hyperplane
\Pi_{\alpha}
defined by the polynomial
F_{\alpha}
, as above, the hyperplane
\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}
is given by
F_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}=\displaystyle{\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}}c_{\beta}X_{\widecheck{\beta}}
, where for each
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell})\in\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})
, we have
\widecheck{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell-1})
. At any rate, in terms of
(m-1)-(\ell-1)
wedges, we may write

F_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}=\displaystyle{\sum_{\beta\in\nabla({\alpha})\cap\Delta({\alpha}^{\prime})}}c_{\beta}v_{\widecheck{\beta^{\mathsf{c}}}},

where, as above, for
\widecheck{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell-1})\in I(\ell-1,m-1)
, we have
v_{\widecheck{\beta^{\mathsf{c}}}}=v_{\beta_{1}^{\prime}}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{\beta_{m-\ell}^{\prime}}
such that
\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell-1}\}\cup\{\beta_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\beta_{m-\ell}^{\prime}\}=\{1,\dots,m-1\}
. Thus our claim is proved.

Thus the decomposability of
\Pi_{\widecheck{{\alpha}}}
guarantees that
\Pi_{\alpha}
is also decomposable. Thus by
[
6
, Theorem 5.5]
, as pointed out in Remark
3.3
(a), that
\Pi_{\alpha}
is Schubert decomposable.
∎

We conclude this article with a remark on the quantity
q_{0}(\ell)
.

Remark 4.8
.

The function
q_{0}(\ell)
, as pointed out before, is an increasing function on
\ell
. One can check with a calculator that
q_{0}(2)=2
,
q_{0}(3)\approx 3.14,...
. In particular, our result establishes the MWCC for
\ell=2
with
q\geq 3
,
\ell=3
with
q\geq 4
etc. We also note, and leave it to the reader to check that,

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\dfrac{q_{0}(\ell)}{\ell}=\frac{1}{\ln 2}\approx 1.4427.

An obvious question arises. How can one improve the situation? In particular, is it possible to use our methodology to prove the MWCC in complete generality? If not, is it at least possible to replace
q_{0}(\ell)
with some
q_{1}(\ell)
in our hypothesis of Theorem
4.7
where
q_{1}(\ell)<q_{0}(\ell)
for all large values of
\ell
? We do not yet have answers to these questions. However, it is conceivable that the possible improvement lies in obtaining better bounds for the number of points on Schubert subvarieties over finite fields in Proposition
3.5
, which might also lead to understanding the quantities
{\alpha}_{i}
in (
11
). We leave these as problems for further research.
