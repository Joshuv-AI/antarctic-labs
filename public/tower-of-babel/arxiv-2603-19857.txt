FoleyDirector: Fine-Grained Temporal Steering for Video-to-Audio Generation via Structured Scripts
You Li, Dewei Zhou, Fan Ma, Dongliang He, Yi Yang

Abstract
Recent Video-to-Audio (V2A) methods have achieved remarkable progress, enabling the synthesis of realistic, high-quality audio. However, they struggle with fine-grained temporal control in multi-event scenarios or when visual cues are insufficient, such as small regions, off-screen sounds, or occluded/partially visible objects. In this paper, we propose FoleyDirector , a framework that, for the first time, enables precise temporal guidance in DiT-based V2A generation while preserving the base model’s audio quality and allowing seamless switching between V2A generation and temporally controlled synthesis. FoleyDirector introduces Structured Temporal Scripts(STS), a set of captions corresponding to short temporal segments, to provide richer temporal information. These features are integrated via the Script-Guided Temporal Fusion Module, which employs Temporal Script Attention to fuse STS features coherently. To handle complex multi-event scenarios, we further propose Bi-Frame Sound Synthesis, enabling parallel in-frame and out-of-frame audio generation and improving controllability. To support training and evaluation, we construct the DirectorSound dataset and introduce VGGSound-Director and DirectorBench. Experiments demonstrate that FoleyDirector substantially enhances temporal controllability while maintaining high audio fidelity, empowering users to act as Foley directors and advancing V2A toward more expressive and controllable.

Video-to-Audio

V2A generation has recently advanced through a variety of architectures that emphasize semantic alignment, temporal synchronization, and controllable sound synthesis. Early works such as FoleyCrafter
46

Current open-source datasets further constrain control, as their captions are short and lack temporal annotations. In contrast, our method introduces fine-grained temporal control, enriching synthesis with detailed semantic cues and enabling more precise and creative audio generation.

Method

3.1
Overview

Task Definition.

Given an input video
\mathcal{V}
and its textual description
\mathcal{T}
, the conventional V2A task aims to generate an audio sequence
\mathcal{A}
that is semantically consistent with
\mathcal{T}
and temporally aligned with the visual events in
\mathcal{V}
.
While the timing of sounds is often guided by visual cues, these cues are often incomplete or ambiguous in real-world videos, such as off-screen sounds, subtle motion in small regions, or occluded objects.
In such cases, providing the model with additional temporal and semantic cues beyond visual information can both
compensate for missing visual features
and offer
fine-grained temporal controllability.

Specifically, as shown in Fig.
, we aim to generate audio
\mathcal{A}
that aligns with the video content where possible, while allowing users to supply temporal guidance for off-screen or visually subtle events.

Method Overview.

As illustrated in Fig.
1
,
FoleyDirector
provides fine-grained temporal control built upon the pre-trained MMAudio generator
4
. First, as described in Section
3.2
, we decompose the video into finer segments and introduce a segment-level pipeline to generate Structured Temporal Scripts (STS) features to provide
richer temporal cues
for each segment. Next, in Section
3.3
, we introduce Script-Guided Temporal Fusion Module (SG-TFM), an adapter that integrates these STS features into the model in a temporally coherent manner. Finally, we utilize
FoleyDirector
’s STS controllability in both V2A and T2A tasks, propose Bi-Frame Sound Synthesis Framework (Bi-Frame) in Section
3.4
, enhance controllability in complex in-frame and out-of-frame scenarios.

3.2
Structured Temporal Scripts

In this section, we discuss how to construct features that
provide additional temporal information beyond visual cues
while remaining easily
interpretable by the model
.

Scripts Control in Finer Segment.

As described in Section
3.1
, we aim to incorporate more fine-grained temporal cues into the V2A process. A straightforward approach would be to use the global caption with complex temporal descriptions and explicit timestamps. However, in practice, users rarely provide precise timestamps, and detailed descriptions of complex sound events can be cumbersome and difficult for the model to interpret.
Inspired by recent advances in MIGC
50
;
49
,
we decompose the complex global caption into multiple fine-grained temporal captions (1s per segment), each corresponding to the semantic control of a specific audio segment. This structured temporal text, akin to a director’s script, allows users to specify the semantic content of each segment; we therefore refer to it as Structured Temporal Scripts. STS effectively treated the entire audio generation process as multiple short segments, providing richer temporal cues for each segment, and providing short-range, segment-level global control.

STS Extraction.

As illustrated in Fig.
1
(a), we construct a fine-grained audio annotation pipeline that extracting Structured Temporal Scripts, consisting of two main components:

1) Perception and Recognition.

We first leverage Qwen-Omni 7B
40
to generate content-aware captions for each audio clip, providing a global semantic description of the sounds. Based on this overall description, Qwen-Omni is then used to identify the types of sounds present in the current audio.

2) Segment-level Classification.

After forming a coarse understanding of the audio content, we then localize all potential audio events within each segment. As described in the previous section, we treat the STS control as a global control at the segment-level. Therefore, we formulate the localization task as a segment-level binary classification problem to annotate the STS. Specifically, we divide the audio into 1-second segments and, based on the recognized audio events, employ a MLLM
40
to determine whether each audio event is present in the current segment. If an event is present, the model further provides intra-segment audio descriptions, loudness, and timbre. Using this pipeline, we collect the DirectorSound data set and the VGGSound-Director test set, which we describe in Section
4
. We train our model only on the V2A DirectorSound dataset without any T2A dataset.

3.3
Script-Guided Temporal Fusion Module

After obtaining the STS, we need to incorporate it into the
FoleyDirector
. Achieving an effective and harmonious integration poses the following challenges:

C1
. How to extract STS features that effectively capture temporal information;

C2
. How to fuse STS features while preserve the model’s original generative capability;

C3
. How to achieve temporal alignment between STS and audio Features.

C1: STS Features.

We use
\mathcal{T}_{tsr}^{i}
to denote the Structured Temporal Scripts within the
i
-th time segment. These textual caption can either be generated automatically by an MLLM or provided directly by the user.
As shown in Fig.
1
(b), following MMAudio’s modality processing strategy, we use a CLIP text encoder
30
to extract textual features from each STS. Since these features contain many tokens, using too many temporal scripts would increase computational overhead and complicate temporal alignment. We thus use the pooling CLIP feature, yielding a compact temporal-semantic representation for the corresponding sound segment, denoted as
\mathbf{F}_{tsr}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times 1\times D}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{tsr}^{i}=\mathrm{Pool}\big(\mathrm{CLIP}(\mathcal{T}_{tsr}^{i})\big),\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,\dots,N,

(1)

and all
\mathbf{F}^{i}_{tsr}
are concatenated along sequence dimmension. Each segment-level STS is replicated
T
times to match the number of video tokens, preserving temporal consistency while providing sufficient semantic information. This yields the full STS representation
\mathbf{F}_{tsr}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times(T\times N)\times D}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{tsr}=[\mathbf{F}^{1}_{tsr},\mathbf{F}^{2}_{tsr},\dots,\mathbf{F}^{N}_{tsr}],

(2)

where
N
denotes the number of segments,
[\cdot]
denotes the concatenation operation.
If a segment lacks an explicit description, we substitute it with an empty embedding.

C2: Script-Guided Temporal Fusion Module.

Since training an audio generator from scratch requires massive computational resources, we aim to integrate Structured Temporal Scripts into the model while preserving the generative capability of the pretrained generator as much as possible. As illustrated in Fig.
1
(b), we design a Script-Guided Temporal Fusion Module (SG-TFM) that injects STS features via an additional Temporal Script Attention (TSA), leaving the rest of MMAudio architecture unchanged. This independent attention layer allows the model to
flexibly switch between traditional V2A and temporal script control simply by dropping SG-TFM.

\mathbf{F}_{a}^{(l)}

\mathbf{F}_{v}^{(l)}

\mathbf{F}_{a^{\prime}}^{(l)}
:

C3: Interleaved RoPE for Temporal Alignment.

Since STS features have temporal characteristics, we draw inspiration from HunyuanVideo-Foley
32
and introduce an interleaved RoPE encoding mechanism to enhance temporal alignment during feature fusion. Specifically, we first upsample STS tokens to match the temporal resolution of audio features, then interleave audio and STS features along the temporal dimension:

\mathbf{F}_{int}=\mathrm{Interleave}(\mathbf{F}_{a}^{(l)},\mathrm{Up}(\mathbf{F}_{tsr}^{(l-1)})),

(5)

where
\mathrm{Up}(\cdot)
is the upsampling operation,
\mathrm{Interleave}(\cdot)
interleaves neighboring tokens, and
\mathbf{F}_{int}
denotes interleaved audio-STS features. We then apply RoPE to
\mathbf{F}_{int}
, separate the features back into audio and STS features, downsample them to their original temporal lengths, and perform TSA:

\mathbf{F}_{rope}=\mathrm{RoPE}(\mathbf{F}_{int}),

(6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}^{(l)}_{a,rope},\mathbf{F}^{(l-1)}_{tsr,rope}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{UnInterleave}(\mathbf{F}_{rope}),

(7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{a^{\prime}}^{(l)},\mathbf{F}_{tsr}^{(l)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{TSA}(\mathbf{F}^{(l)}_{a,rope},\mathbf{F}^{(l-1)}_{tsr,rope}),

Interleaved RoPE assigns correlated positional indices to temporally adjacent features, enabling temporally coherent STS fusion.

3.4
Bi-Frame Sound Synthesis Framework

Challenge: Complex In-frame/Out-of-frame Scenarios.

Introducing STS features provides temporal control over audio generation. However, users may encounter more complex scenarios involving multi-event in-frame and out-of-frame sounds, or even counterfactual combinations. For instance, a user might want to generate a sound where a person suddenly utters a humorous or off-gender voice while speaking, or a dog bark unexpectedly occurs amid a chorus of birds.
In such cases, visual cues can dominate, causing the model to ignore text-specified attributes unrelated to the visuals and lose control.
Therefore, we aim to explore methods to further improve the framework’s controllability to perform flexible in-frame/out-of-frame synthesis.

Bi-Frame Sound Synthesis.

Building upon pretrained MMAudio’s multimodal generative capability, we find that training
FoleyDirector
on the V2A dataset (DirectorSound)
without T2A data
preserves temporal script control in T2A generation. Motivated by this, we propose the Bi-Frame Sound Synthesis Framework (Bi-Frame), combining MMAudio’s audio generation with our module’s temporal control to achieve
attribute disentanglement
for in-frame and out-of-frame sounds, enhancing both
controllability
and
semantic expressiveness
.

As shown in Fig.
1
(c), generation is split into:
1)
In-frame sounds
: events visible in the video;
2)
Out-of-frame sounds
: off-screen or narrative events.
Given audio latent
\mathbf{F}_{a}
, video features
\mathbf{F}_{v}
, text control
\mathbf{F}_{t}
, and STS condition
\mathbf{F}_{tsr}
, we duplicate
\mathbf{F}_{a}
into two streams and process each block
l
in parallel:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{a,in}^{(l)},...

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Block}(\mathbf{F}_{a}^{(l-1)},\mathbf{F}_{v}^{(l-1)},\mathbf{F}_{t}^{(l-1)},\mathbf{F}_{tsr}^{(l-1)}),

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{a,out}^{(l)},...

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Block}(\mathbf{F}_{a}^{(l-1)},\mathbf{F}_{v}^{\varnothing},\mathbf{F}_{t}^{(l-1)},\mathbf{F}_{tsr}^{(l-1)}),

In-frame audio
\mathbf{F}_{a,in}^{(l)}
is conditioned on video, text, and STS; out-of-frame audio
\mathbf{F}_{a,out}^{(l)}
uses a learnable null visual embedding, with text and STS as control, while Synchformer features remain unchanged.
Within each Script-Guided Temporal Fusion Module, the two streams are fused according to temporal segments:

\mathbf{F}_{a}^{(l)}=\mathrm{Fuse}(\mathbf{F}_{a,in}^{(l)},\mathbf{F}_{a,out}^{(l)}),

(9)

where
\mathrm{Fuse}
concatenates in-frame and out-of-frame segments in temporal order. This bi-frame design enables joint rendering of on- and off-screen sounds, preserves temporal coherence, and enhances controllability.

4
DirectorSound and Benchmarks

Following the pipeline described in Section
3.2
, we collected and constructed a set of training data, and built a corresponding benchmark to evaluate the model’s generation quality and control capabilities.

4.1
Data Source

We construct the DirectorSound dataset from multiple sources.
First, we utilize several existing open-source V2A datasets, including VGGSound
2
and AudioCaps
16
.
VGGSound is a large-scale audio-visual correspondence dataset containing over 200,000 video clips collected from YouTube, covering more than 310 sound classes and totaling about 550 hours of data.
AudioCaps, on the other hand, is built upon the AudioSet corpus and provides human-written natural language descriptions for 10-second audio clips.
In addition, we also incorporate a portion of our in-house V2A dataset to further enrich the diversity and coverage of DirectorSound.

4.2
DirectorBench and VGGSound-Director

To evaluate our method, we construct two benchmark: DirectorBench and VGGSound-Director.

DirectorBench
focuses on evaluating the model’s
Controllability
. We collected 100 videos from the VGGSound test set and Pexels, and manually annotated the sound-emitting segments in two types:
1) Temporal Control
. Involves out-of-frame sounds or duration control, such as specifying when a sound should occur or enforcing silence in certain audio segments.
2) Counterfactual Control
. Introduces counterfactual sound attributes, generating audio that contradicts the video content in specific actions, or allowing flexible combinations of conventional and counterfactual sounds. For each video, we annotated approximately 1–4 control templates per sound type, resulting in roughly 400 test samples in total.
During evaluation, we employ the audio grounding tool
43
to locate target vocalization segments and compute the intersection-over-union (IoU) with the annotated time segments. Segments exceeding a predefined threshold are considered correctly triggered, from which we calculate Precision, Recall, and F1 score.
Besides, we also want to evaluate the audio
Quality
we generated, so we design another benchmark named
VGGSound-Director
. VGGSound-Director is a subset of VGGSound containing 2.2K videos, annotated using the temporal labeling procedure described in Section
3.2
. These annotations are based on real audio, allowing us to evaluate the
Quality
of controllable synthesized audio.
Additional information can be found in the supplementary materials.

5
Experiments

5.1
Details

Impletmetation.
All experiments are conducted using MMAudio-medium with full model training. We set the learning rate to 2e-5 and employ a batch size of 16. During training, STS features are randomly dropped with a probability of 0.1 (replaced with empty text features) to enable Classifier-free Guidance(CFG). A cosine learning rate scheduler is used, and the model is trained for 1.2 million iterations. Training is performed on 8 × 40GB A800 GPUs, with a total training time of approximately 3 days.
At inference, we adopt MMAudio’s default configuration, run 25 inference steps, and set the CFG scale to 4.5.

Baselines.
We compare our method with several state-of-the-art V2A models, including MMAudio, HunyuanVideo-Foley, and ThinkSound. For controllability comparisons, in addition to these methods, we also include Video-Foley, which uses video-extracted RMS signals to control the generation process. On VGGSound-Director, all models use the original VGGSound captions, while on DirectorBench, we manually annotate temporal captions, including the timing of vocalizations. All methods are evaluated using their default parameter configurations.

Metrics.

On VGGSound-Director, we evaluate our generated audio using five complementary metrics:
1) Distribution Matching
: We measure how closely the generated audio matches the ground-truth distribution using Fréchet Distance (FD) and KL divergence (KL). FD is computed with embeddings from PaSST
19
, PANNs
17
, and VGGish
11
, while KL uses PaSST and PANNs as classifiers.
2) Audio Quality
: To assess perceptual quality without reference audio, we use the Inception Score (ISC), with PANNs as the classifier.

3) Semantic Alignment
: We measure the semantic consistency between the input video and generated audio using ImageBind
10
, reporting the average cosine similarity as the IB-score.

4) Temporal Alignment
: Audio-visual synchrony is evaluated using DeSync
15
, predicted by Synchformer, which estimates the timing misalignment between audio and video.
On DirectorBench, we divide controllability into temporal control and counterfactual control, and evaluate metrics for each case as well as overall performance. As described in Section
4.2
, we use grounded audio event timestamps to match with the target time segments and compute the IoU. Vocalizations exceeding the threshold are considered correct, from which we calculate Precision, Recall, and F1 score.

We present qualitative comparisons with several baselines in Fig.

2) Effective collaboration with visual features.

In the rightmost example, providing an STS description aligned with the visual content (dog barking, 6–8s) allows our model to generate audio matching the rhythm of HunyuanVideo-Foley. 6
Conclusion

We presented
FoleyDirector
, empowering users to act as foley directors who can control when and how sounds emerge.
We enhance the pretrained MMAudio with additional temporal cues beyond visual features, decomposing videos into finer segments with Structured Temporal Scripts for each. We also propose a Script-Guided Temporal Fusion Moduleto introduce these temporal features, allow user to seamlessly switch between traditional V2A generation and temporally controll by disabling SG-TFM. We also propose Bi-Frame Sound Synthesis Frameworkto enable more flexible in and out of frame audio generation.
We construct a annotation pipeline and DirectorSound dataset. Besides, we propose DirectorBench and VGGSound-Director to evaluate the controllability and quality.
Extensive experiments highlight improvements in precision and F1-score, validating the effectiveness of our method in advancing controllable audio generation.
