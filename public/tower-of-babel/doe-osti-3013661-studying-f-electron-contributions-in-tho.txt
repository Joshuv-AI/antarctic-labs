Executive Summary

This project utilized high-resolution rotational spectroscopy to probe the geometric and
electronic structures of actinide-containing molecules in the gas phase. The primary
objective was to elucidate the role of f-electrons in chemical bonding within heavy element
systems, speciﬁcally those containing Thorium (Th) and Uranium (U). Utilizing a laser
ablation source coupled with Chirped-Pulse Fourier Transform Microwave (CP-FTMW)
spectroscopy, the research team successfully synthesized and characterized a series of
novel actinide sulﬁdes and oxides.

Signiﬁcant achievements include the ﬁrst pure rotational spectroscopic observation of a
uranium-containing polyatomic molecule (SUO2) and the characterization of the mixed
chalcogenides OThS and OCeS. Additionally, the project provided a comprehensive
analysis of highly excited vibrational states in ThS and HfS, allowing for the determination
of highly accurate equilibrium parameters. These results provide critical benchmarks for
relativistic quantum chemical calculations and offer new insights into the "periodic"
behavior of actinides versus their transition metal and lanthanide counterparts.

Comparison of Actual Accomplishments with the Goals and Objectives of the Project

Original Goals:

The original proposal aimed to:

 1. Determine geometric structures and spectroscopic constants for a speciﬁc set of
 target diatomics (e.g., UF, UCl, ThCl) and polyatomics (e.g., ThO2, UO2C2).

 2. Identify gas-phase products of laser ablation with various binding gases (Cl2, O2,
 etc.).

 3. Probe f-electron contributions to bonding.

Actual Accomplishments:

While the speciﬁc molecular targets evolved during the course of research, the project
successfully met and exceeded the core scientiﬁc objectives through the study of more
complex systems:

 • Polyatomic Complexity: Instead of limiting the scope to simple halides (UCl, ThCl),
 the project successfully isolated and characterized complex polyatomic species
 including SUO2, OThS, and OCeS. The characterization of SUO2 represents a
 milestone as the ﬁrst pure rotational study of a polyatomic uranium species.

 • Highly Excited States: The research on ThS and HfS went beyond standard ground-
 state characterizations. We successfully measured rotational transitions in
 vibrational states up to v=17 (ThS) and v=19 (HfS), providing a depth of data on
 potential energy curves rarely achieved in heavy-element spectroscopy.

 • Chemical Synthesis Strategy: The proposal aimed to explore reaction products.
 The team successfully identiﬁed that using Carbonyl Sulﬁde (OCS) as a precursor
 gas in the presence of laser-ablated actinides is a robust pathway for generating
 both actinide sulﬁdes and actinide oxysulﬁdes.

Summary of Project Activities and Technical Results

The research focused on three primary areas of discovery, resulting in three peer-reviewed
publications.

A. Pure Rotational Spectroscopy of Uranium-Containing Polyatomics (SUO2)

Reference: Isert, J. E., Grubbs II, G. S., & Cooke, S. A. (2024). Chemical Physics Letters.

We successfully recorded the ground-state rotational spectra of two isotopologues 32S
and 34S of SUO2.

 • Synthesis: Produced via laser ablation of uranium in the presence of OCS and
 Argon.

 • Structure: Conﬁrmed a C2v structure analogous to the uranyl moiety, but with
 signiﬁcant angular deviation due to the sulfur substitution.

 • Key Findings:

 o Determined rotational constants: A0 5150$ MHz, B0 2950$ MHz, C0 1872$
 MHz.

 o Calculated a non-zero inertial defect (∆= 0.410 amu Å2), which was
 consistent with low-frequency vibrational contributions rather than non-
 planarity.

 o Validates that laser ablation CP-FTMW is a viable technique for studying
 complex uranium polyatomics, opening the door for future uranyl-derivative
 studies.

B. Highly Excited Vibrational States of HfS and ThS

Reference: Isert, J. E., Glenn, J. R., Cooke, S. A., & Grubbs II, G. S. (2024). Journal of
Molecular Spectroscopy.

This study focused on the diatomic monosulﬁdes HfS and ThS, serving as a comparison
between transition metals and actinides.

 • Data Depth: Rotational transitions were recorded in vibrational states up to v=19 for
 HfS and v=17 for ThS.

 • Key Findings:

 o First detection of the34}S isotopologue of ThS.

 o Performed isotopically invariant ﬁts to determine highly accurate equilibrium
               bond lengths: re(HfS) = 2.156140(3) Å and re(ThS) = 2.3418068(3) Å.

 o Comparison of bond lengths with Pyykkö’s triple-bond radii supports the
 view of a triple bond environment (two π, one σ involving Th 6d and S 3p
 electrons.

C. Actinide vs. Lanthanide Bonding: OThS and OCeS

Reference: Isert, J. E., Davies, A. R., Grubbs II, G. S., & Cooke, S. A. (2025). Physical
Chemistry Chemical Physics.

To directly address the project goal of studying f-electron behavior, we performed a
comparative study of OThS (actinide) and OCeS (lanthanide).

 • Synthesis: Laser ablation of Th/Ce metal in the presence of OCS.

 • Structure: Both molecules were determined to be bent, but with signiﬁcant
 structural differences.

 • Key Findings:

 o Bond Angles: OThS exhibits a bond angle of $\approx 115.4^\circ$, while
 OCeS is signiﬁcantly wider at $\approx 130.1^\circ$. This structural
 divergence highlights the differences in polarizability and valence orbital
 participation (5f vs 4f) between actinides and lanthanides.

 o Bond Order: Effective structure analysis suggests metal-oxygen bonds are
 close to triple bond character, while metal-sulfur bonds approach double
 bond character, implying a pentavalent nature for the metal centers.

5. Conclusion

This project has successfully expanded the frontier of gas-phase actinide spectroscopy. By
moving beyond simple diatomics to characterize SUO2, OThS, and OCeS, we have
demonstrated that rotational spectroscopy is a powerful tool for probing the complex
electronic environments of heavy elements. The data generated provides high-precision
benchmarks that will assist the theoretical community in reﬁning models for f-electron
bonding, ultimately contributing to better predictive capabilities for actinide chemistry.
