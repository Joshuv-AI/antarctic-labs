On the pointwise convergence of cubic Schrödinger flow on 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2}
Fanfei Meng, Yilin Song, Chenmin Sun, Ruixiao Zhang, Jiqiang Zheng

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the almost everywhere convergence of the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger flow to the initial data on 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} , i ​ u t + Δ g ​ u = | u | 2 ​ u , ( t , x ) ∈ ℝ × 𝕊 2 . iu_{t}+\Delta_{g}u=|u|^{2}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}. Inspired by the randomization method and the ansatz introduced by Burq, Camps, Sun, and Tzvetkov [Preprint, arXiv:2404.18229], we prove almost sure pointwise convergence almost everywhere for the nonlinear solution at very low regularity. This extends Compaan-Lucà-Staffilani [Int. Math. Res. Not. IMRN, (1) (2021), 596–647] to the spherical setting. We also provide a new necessary condition for the associated L p L^{p} maximal estimate for the linear Schrödinger equation on 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} . More precisely, we show that the L p L^{p} maximal estimate fails for s < 1 2 − 1 2 ​ p s<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p} with p ⩾ 2 p\geqslant 2 . In the special case p = 3 p=3 , our result matches the corresponding range in the ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} case, up to the endpoint, and improves the previous result of Chen-Duong-Lee-Yan [J. Math. Pures Appl. 163 (2022), 433–449].

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

In this work, we consider the pointwise convergence for solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with initial data on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Let
u(t,x)
be the solution to the following cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &iu_{t}+\Delta_{g}u=|u|^{2}u,\\
&u(0,x)=\phi(x),\end{aligned}\right.\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},

(NLS)

where
\Delta_{g}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
The main problem studied in our paper can be formulated as follows.
For
\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, what is the minimal
s
such that
u(t,x)\to\phi(x)
as
t\to 0
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
?

Let us briefly review the progress in the pointwise convergence for the free Schrödinger flow.
For the case of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this problem was formulated by Carleson in
[
13
]
:
Find the minimal
s\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}e^{it\Delta}\phi(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{a.e.}}}{{=}}\phi(x),\qquad\forall~\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Carleson proved the convergence holds for
s\geqslant\frac{1}{4}
in the one-dimensional case. By using harmonic analysis tools, the a.e. convergence of the Schrödinger operator can be reduced to proving an
L^{p}
maximal-in-time estimate.
Later, Dahlberg-Kenig
[
17
]
gave a counterexample which reveals that
s\geqslant\frac{1}{4}
is necessary in dimension one.

For the higher dimensional Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the geometry will play an important role and is more delicate than in the 1D case.
By establishing the smoothing estimate, Sjölin
[
31
]
and Vega
[
37
]
established the pointwise convergence for
s>\frac{1}{2}
with
d\geqslant 2
. Bourgain
[
4
]
showed the pointwise convergence holds for some
s<\frac{1}{2}
. Later, Lee
[
24
]
improved it to
s>\frac{3}{8}
for
d=2
by utilizing the localization argument and the wave-packet decomposition. Meanwhile, Bourgain
[
5
]
proved that the necessary condition of pointwise convergence is
s\geqslant\frac{d}{2(d+1)}
. Inspired by the recent development in harmonic analysis in
[
10
,
21
,
22
]
, Du-Guth-Li
[
19
]
and Du-Zhang
[
20
]
proved the convergence for
s>\frac{d}{2(d+1)}
with
d\geqslant 2
which is sharp up to the endpoint.

Compared to the Euclidean case, the Carleson problem on the torus is more subtle,
due to the erratic behavior of the periodic Schrödinger kernel, which is linked to analytic number theory.
For general initial data, Moyua-Vega
[
27
]
showed the convergence for
s>\frac{1}{3}
in the case of
\mathbb{T}
.
Following Moyua-Vega’s argument together with the Strichartz inequality on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, thanks to the decoupling theory, Compaan-Lucà-Staffilani
[
16
]
proved the convergence for
s>\frac{d}{d+2}
which is the best known result up to now.
For special initial data
\phi
such that

\displaystyle\phi(x):=\sum_{|k|\sim N}e^{ik\cdot x},

the Schrödinger flow can be regarded as a type of Weyl sum.
Barron
[
3
]
investigated the
L^{4}
maximal estimate for the Weyl sum on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
based on the Weyl-sum estimates and the circle method.
For the higher dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
d\geqslant 2
, Miao-Yuan-Zhao
[
26
]
proved the convergence for this special initial data for
s>\frac{d}{2(d+1)}
which is sharp up to the endpoint.
They also gave some new upper bounds on the Hausdörff dimensions of the sets associated with the large values of the Weyl sums.
Baker, Chen, and Shparlinski
[
1
,
2
]
considered generalized maximal estimates for Weyl sums of degree
d
.
Very recently, Chen-Miao-Yuan-Zhao
[
15
]
extended
[
3
]
to maximal estimates for higher-order Weyl sums with
k\geqslant 3
of the form

\displaystyle S(k,t)=\sum_{|n|\leqslant N}e^{i(n\cdot x+n^{k}t)}.

For
k=3
, their result is sharp up to the endpoint.
They also gave counterexamples extending the previous result of
[
29
]
to
k\geqslant 3
.

For a non-flat compact manifold without boundary
M
, Wang-Zhang
[
36
]
showed the convergence for
s>\frac{3}{4}
when
d=2
and
s>\frac{9}{10}
when
d=3
. For
M=\mathbb{S}^{2}
or
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, they improved the convergence for
s>\frac{1}{2}
.
For the zonal spherical harmonics, which are the special eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{g}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
with
d\geqslant 2
, Chen-Duong-Lee-Yan
[
14
]
proved the convergence for
s>\frac{1}{3}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
They also show the necessity of
s>\frac{1}{4}
for all
d\geqslant 1
. Despite a huge gap between
\frac{1}{3}
and
\frac{1}{2}
for the general initial data on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
we construct the new counterexample in Section 3 (
s\geqslant\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}
). If
p=3
, our result can match the same result as
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

For the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation, one can follow the strategy in
[
16
]
to prove almost everywhere convergence for solutions with initial data in
H^{s}
with
s>\max\{s_{\text{lin}},s_{c}\}
on certain compact manifolds, where
s_{\text{lin}}
is the regularity at which the linear pointwise convergence holds and
s_{c}
is the threshold for local well-posedness. For example, when
M=\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
s_{c}=\frac{1}{4}
and
s_{\text{lin}}=\frac{1}{2}
while for
M=\mathbb{T}^{2}
,
s_{c}=0
and
s_{\text{lin}}=\frac{1}{2}
. To further lower the regularity, Compaan-Lucà-Staffilani
[
16
]
considered the following randomized initial data

\displaystyle\phi^{\omega}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{g_{n}^{\omega}}{\langle n\rangle^{\frac{d}{2}+\alpha}}e^{in\cdot x},\,\alpha>0,

where
g_{n}^{\omega}
are independent complex-valued Gaussian variables.
Under this randomization,
e^{it\Delta}\phi^{\omega}(x)\in\bigcap_{s<\alpha}H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
almost surely.
For the Wick-ordered nonlinearity,
F(u)=\big(|u|^{2}-2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\big)u
, they proved almost sure everywhere convergence for this nonlinearity when
\alpha>\frac{d-1}{2}
.

1.2.
Main result

In this paper, we study the probabilistic almost everywhere convergence for the cubic NLS on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Note that if
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
in (
NLS
), by taking the gauge transform (where we normalize the area of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
to be
1
)

u(t,x)\mapsto u(t,x)e^{-it+2it\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}},

(
NLS
) is equivalent to the following Wick-ordered NLS:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &iu_{t}+(\Delta_{g}-1)u=\mathcal{N}(u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}:|u|^{2}u:,\\
&u(0,x)=\phi(x),\end{aligned}\right.\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},

(1.1)

where
:|u|^{2}u:
is defined as

:|u|^{2}u:(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}\big(|u(x)|^{2}-2\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\big)u(x).

Before presenting our main result, we introduce the randomization of initial data.

For a given probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, we denote by
\{g_{n,k}^{\omega}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},|k|\leqslant n}
a sequence of complex standard Gaussian variables which are i.i.d. Let
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
be the spectrum of
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}+1}
, and let
(\mathbf{b}_{n,k})_{|k|\leqslant n}
be an orthonormal basis of the spectral projection
\pi_{n}
for the eigenspace
E_{n}
of
\lambda_{n}^{2}
. In particular, the eigenvalues can be given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=\sqrt{n^{2}+n+1},\qquad\text{for}~n=0,1,2,\cdots.

The eigenspace
E_{n}
consists of the spherical harmonic functions of degree
n
and the multiplicity for
\lambda_{n}^{2}
is
2n+1
, i.e.
\dim(E_{n})=2n+1
.
We randomize the initial data of (
1.1
) as

\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}(x):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}}\sum_{|k|\leqslant n}g_{n,k}^{\omega}\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x).

(1.2)

This randomization induces a Gaussian probability measure
\mu_{\alpha}
with covariance operator
(-\Delta_{g}+1)^{-\alpha}
, which does not depend on the choice of orthonormal basis
(\mathbf{b}_{n,k})
.
It is known that

H^{\alpha-1-}(\mathbb{S}^{2}):=\bigcap_{s<\alpha-1}H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})

is of full
\mu_{\alpha}
-measure, and
\mu_{\alpha}(H^{\alpha-1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))=0
.
Note that the linear Schrödinger flow
e^{it\Delta_{g}}
preserves the law
\mu_{\alpha}
.

For the pointwise convergence problem, our first result concerns the linear Schrödinger flow in both probabilistic and deterministic settings:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
p\geqslant 2
and
I=[0,2\pi]
, the maximal estimate

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|e^{it\Delta_{g}}\phi\big|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

fails for
s<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}
.

For
p=2
, we recover the necessary condition
s>\frac{1}{4}
proved in
[
14
]
for the almost everywhere convergence property of the linear flow.
After introducing randomness, the pointwise convergence property can be substantially improved:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha>1
. Then
\mu_{\alpha}
-almost surely,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}e^{it\Delta_{g}}\phi(x)=\phi(x),\quad\mbox{a.e.}~x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Our second result addresses the same problem for the nonlinear Schrödinger flow.
Note that for
\alpha>\frac{5}{4}
,
\mu_{\alpha}
-almost every initial datum belongs to
H^{\frac{1}{4}+}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
; hence we always have a local flow thanks to
[
8
]
.
Below the regularity threshold
\frac{1}{4}
, it is also known from
[
9
]
that the Cauchy problem (
NLS
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is ill-posed in the sense that the flow map fails to be uniformly continuous in
H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
for any
s<\frac{1}{4}
.
Therefore, for
\alpha\leqslant\frac{5}{4}
, the solutions are constructed by the probability method in
[
6
]
.
More precisely, if
\alpha>1
, then for
\mu_{\alpha}
-almost every initial datum
\phi
, the solution constructed in
[
6
]
exists on a time interval
[-T,T]
with
T=T(\phi)>0
and belongs to
C^{0}([-T,T];H^{(\alpha-1)-}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))
.
For these solutions, we have:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha>1
. For
\mu_{\alpha}
-almost every initial datum
\phi\in H^{(\alpha-1)-}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, the local solution
u(t,x)
to (
NLS
) with initial data
\phi
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}u(t,x)=\phi(x),\quad\text{a.e.}~x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

(1.3)

Remark 1.4
.

From the current literature (see
[
36
]
), the almost everywhere convergence property for (
NLS
) is known for any initial data
\phi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
with
s>\frac{1}{2}
. See Lemma
2.8
and Remark
2.9
.

Remark 1.5
.

For the linear equation, one can promote the convergence to every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
in Theorem
1.2
.
However, since the Duhamel term will not gain any regularity compared to the linear case, by Sobolev embedding, we can only prove convergence everywhere for
\alpha>2
in Theorem
1.3
.

Remark 1.6
.

The framework of this paper may also be useful for studying probabilistic pointwise convergence for nonlinear Schrödinger equations with higher algebraic nonlinearities on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, in connection with the ansatz in
[
18
]
.

Remark 1.7
.

It is also interesting to address a similar problem for the cubic wave equation on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
on a compact manifold. We also refer the resolution of such problem on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
by Lucá-Merino
[
25
]
.

Let us discuss the main difference between our work and
[
16
]
.
In the context of the cubic NLS on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
,
Bourgain
[
4
]
found that, up to a gauge transform, the second Picard iteration exhibits a regularization effect almost surely, thanks to the multi-linear smoothing effect and the statistical properties of the randomized initial data. Such an observation motivates the introduction of the re-centering ansatz of Bourgain by decomposing the solution to NLS as

\displaystyle u(t):=e^{it\Delta}\phi+w(t),

(1.4)

where
w(t)
is the remainder with almost
\frac{1}{2}
order smoother than the initial data
u_{0}
, measured by the Sobolev regularity.
Such a good structure allows the authors in
[
16
]
to prove the almost sure almost everywhere convergence of the nonlinear Schrödinger flow on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
Indeed,
the pointwise convergence of the linear part
e^{it\Delta}\phi
follows from the randomization structure and from the fact that the law of the initial data is invariant under the linear evolution. As the remainder can be controlled in a Fourier restriction space
X^{0,\frac{1}{2}+}\hookrightarrow C_{t}^{0}H_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}+}
, one can essentially apply the deterministic maximal function estimate to prove the pointwise convergence of the nonlinear part.

However, this structure is not valid in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
In
[
7
]
, the authors computed the regularity of the second Picard iteration associated to (
1.1
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(t,\phi):=-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)(\Delta_{g}-1)}\big(:|e^{is(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi|^{2}e^{is(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi:\big)\,{\rm d}s.

Using the spectral decomposition, the Wick-ordered nonlinearity can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(u)=\mathcal{N}^{1}(u)+\mathcal{N}^{2}(u)+\mathcal{N}^{3}(u),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{1}(u)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\\
n_{1}\neq n_{2},n_{2}\neq n_{3}\end{subarray}}u_{n_{1}}\overline{u_{n_{2}}}u_{n_{3}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{2}(u)

\displaystyle:=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\neq n_{2}\end{subarray}}(|u_{n_{2}}|^{2}-\|u_{n_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})u_{n_{1}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{3}(u)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n}(|u_{n}|^{2}-\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})u_{n}.

For
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, the second term vanishes and the algebraic property of the eigenfunctions makes the first term convenient to handle. For
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the additional term
\mathcal{N}^{2}(u)
as well as another resonant high-low-low frequency interaction will prevent the improvement of the regularity as observed in
[
7
]
. More precisely,
the second Picard iteration will not have better regularity than the linear part, in the following sense:

Theorem 1.8

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}
be the randomized initial data as in (
1.2
).
For
t\geqslant 0
and
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
, there exist
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\eta>0
such that for all
N\geqslant N_{0}
, there holds

\displaystyle\eta|t|(\ln N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(t,P_{\leqslant N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega})\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,H^{\alpha-1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))},

where
P_{\leqslant N}
denotes the spectral projector on frequencies lower than
N
.

In this spirit, the structure of the probabilistic solution associated to the general randomized initial data
\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}
cannot be of the same form as (
1.4
).

Based on these observations, we adapt the newly built ansatz from
[
6
]
to prove our theorem for the general randomized initial data. For the special data, i.e. zonal spherical harmonics, the symmetry makes the problem similar to the one-dimensional periodic setting. The Duhamel term still offers the opportunity to gain regularity. We refer to
[
12
,
14
]
. After introducing the random averaging operator that is typically used to remove the high-low-low interaction in
[
6
,
18
]
, one can partially extract the strong resonance in the iteration scheme. The new ansatz has the leading term retaining the main regularity of the initial data, while the remainder term could be controlled in a smoother Fourier-restriction space. The key point is that the leading term in the ansatz still has the same law as the initial data (after the frequency localization) for fixed time, and is also continuous in time. Consequently, it enjoys a nice space-time continuity which allows us to prove its pointwise convergence as
t\rightarrow 0
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some
L^{p}
eigenfunction estimates
in both deterministic and probabilistic settings, together with some useful probabilistic estimates. We also give definitions and properties of Fourier-Lebesgue spaces and Bourgain spaces.

2.1.
Randomization for the initial data

Let
\{g_{n,k}^{\omega}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},|k|\leqslant n}
be a sequence of i.i.d. standard complex-valued Gaussian random variables on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, let
\lambda_{n}
be the spectrum of
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}+1}
, and let
\mathbf{b}_{n,k}
be a real eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{g}+1
associated to
\lambda_{n}^{2}
.
Then we randomize the initial data
u_{0}
of (
NLS
) as

\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}(x):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}}\sum_{|k|\leqslant n}g_{n,k}^{\omega}\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x).

(2.1)

Remark 2.1
.

We denote

z_{n}^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})}:=\lambda_{n}^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})}\bigg(\frac{2n+1}{\lambda_{n}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad e_{n}^{\omega}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n+1}}\sum_{|k|\leqslant n}g_{n,k}^{\omega}\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x).

and rewrite (
2.1
) as

\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z_{n}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}}e_{n}^{\omega}(x).

(2.2)

Since
\lambda_{n}=\sqrt{n^{2}+n+1}
,
\lambda_{n}
and
z_{n}
enjoy the same asymptotic. Thus, we can replace
z_{n}
by
\lambda_{n}
. By the local Weyl’s law
[
7
]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{|k|\leqslant n}|\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x)|^{2}=\sum_{|k|\leqslant n}\|\mathbf{b}_{n,k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}=2n+1,\qquad\forall~x\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

(2.3)

one has
\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\in H^{\alpha-1-}(\mathbb{S}^{2})

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, which coincides with the results in
[
12
,
28
]
for
\alpha=\frac{3}{2}
. Also,
e^{it\Delta_{g}}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\in H^{\alpha-1-}(\mathbb{S}^{2})

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely.

2.2.
L^{p}
eigenfunction estimates on
\mathbb{S}^{2}

Let
\lambda_{n}^{2}
be the
n
-th eigenvalue associated to
-\Delta_{g}
on a compact Riemannian surface
M
and
\pi_{n}
is the spectral projector on the
n
-th eigenspace
E_{n}
. Thus, we define

P_{\leqslant N}:=\sum_{0\leqslant n\leqslant N}\pi_{n},\quad P_{N}:=P_{\leqslant N}-P_{\leqslant N/2}.

Sogge
[
32
]
proved the following
L^{p}
estimate :

Proposition 2.2

(Spectral projector estimate)
.

For a general compact Riemannian surface
M
, there exists
C>0
such that for all
n\geqslant 1
and
f\in L^{2}(M)
, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\|\pi_{n}f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leqslant C\begin{cases}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\big)}\|\pi_{n}f\|_{L^{2}(M)},&2\leqslant p\leqslant 6,\\
\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}\|\pi_{n}f\|_{L^{2}(M)},&6\leqslant p\leqslant\infty.\end{cases}

Specifically, this estimate is sharp in two-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

In our paper, to achieve the threshold regularity in the pointwise convergence, we need the following probabilistic eigenfunction estimate.

Lemma 2.3

(Probabilistic
L^{p}
eigenfunction estimate,
[
11
]
)
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\|e_{n}^{\omega}(x)\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\sqrt{p},\quad p\in[2,\infty].

Furthermore, for every
R\geqslant 1
, there exists
C_{0}>c_{0}>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[\|e_{n}^{\omega}(x)\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}}>R\big]\leqslant C_{0}e^{-c_{0}R^{2}}.

2.3.
Wiener chaos

We also need the following classical probabilistic estimates including Khinchine’s inequality and Wiener Chaos.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
35
]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probabilistic space, for sequence
\{c_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{N})
and i.i.d. Gaussian variables
\{g_{n}^{\omega}\}\in N_{\mathbb{C}}(0,1)
, the following two statements are equivalent and true:

(i)

(Khinchin’s inequality)
: there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}g_{n}^{\omega}\Big\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\sqrt{p}\|c_{n}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{N})},\qquad\forall~p\geqslant 2.

(2.4)

(ii)

(Large deviation)
: there exists two constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big\{\omega\in\Omega~\Big|~\Big|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}g_{n}^{\omega}\Big|>\lambda\Big\}\Big)\leqslant C_{1}e^{-\frac{C_{2}\lambda^{2}}{\|c_{n}\|_{l^{2}(\mathbb{N})}^{2}}},\qquad\forall~\lambda>0.

(2.5)

Proposition 2.5

(Wiener chaos,
[
28
,
30
]
)
.

For
k\geqslant 1
,
n=(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
,
c(n)\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\{g_{n}^{\omega}\}\in N_{\mathbb{C}}(0,1)
i.i.d., we define

S_{k}(\omega):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}\Big[c(n)\prod_{i=1}^{k}g_{n_{i}}^{\omega}\Big].

Then

\|S_{k}\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant(p-1)^{k/2}\|S_{k}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)},\qquad\forall~p\geqslant 2.

(2.6)

Proof.

One can use Nelson’s hypercontractive bounds to prove it, for more details, see Theorem 1.22 in
[
30
]
.

3.
Deterministic counterexample

In this section, we give the alternative counterexample for
L^{r}
maximal estimate for linear Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with some
r\geqslant 2
.

3.1.
An arithmetic result

We recall the following classical result concerning an average order of the Euler indicator function (see Chapter 2,3 of
[
34
]
)

\phi(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant m\leqslant n\\
\mathrm{gcd}(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}1.

Recall the Möbius function :

\mu(n):=\begin{cases}(-1)^{s},\quad&\text{ if }n=p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{s},\;p_{j}\text{ are dinstinct primes},\\
0,\quad&\text{ if }n\text{ is divided by a square of a prime}.\end{cases}

By the well-known Möbius inversion formula

\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)=\delta(n),

\phi(n)=\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)\frac{n}{d}.

Consequently, for any
\lambda\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant\lambda}\frac{\phi(n)}{n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant\lambda}\sum_{d|n}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}=\sum_{d\leqslant\lambda}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda}{d}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\sum_{d\leqslant\lambda}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2}}+O(\log\lambda).

Again, using the Möbius inversion formula, we have

\Big(\sum_{d\geqslant 1}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2}}\Big)\Big(\sum_{d\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\Big)=1.

We deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant\lambda}\frac{\phi(n)}{n}=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\lambda+O(\log\lambda),\quad\lambda\rightarrow+\infty.

(3.1)

Using this and Chebyshev, we have the following consequence :

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
b>a>0
be two constants. Then for
c_{0}\in(0,6/\pi^{2})
and for sufficiently large
\lambda
,

\#\Big\{n\in[a\lambda,b\lambda]:\phi(n)\geqslant\Big(\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}-c_{0}\Big)n\Big\}\geqslant(b-a)\lambda-O_{c_{0}}(\log\lambda),

as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proof.

By (
3.1
),

\sum_{a\lambda\leqslant n\leqslant b\lambda}\frac{\phi(n)}{n}=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}(b-a)\lambda+O(\log\lambda).

By Chebyshev, we have

\displaystyle\#\Big\{n\in[a\lambda,b\lambda]:\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}-\frac{\phi(n)}{n}\geqslant c_{0}\Big\}\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{0}}O(\log\lambda).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Since
\mathbb{S}^{2}
can be embedded into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we can write

\mathbb{S}^{2}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}=1\}.

Thus, the highest weight spherical harmonic is given by
\phi_{n}(x)=(x_{1}+ix_{2})^{n}
. Using polar coordinates, we can further rewrite
\phi_{n}(x)=(\lambda e^{i\theta})^{n}
,
with

\lambda=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}},\quad\theta=\arctan\left(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\right).

Here
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
.
Direct computation shows

\|\phi_{n}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\sim n^{s-\frac{1}{4}}.

Let
\varphi
be a bump function
\varphi
satisfying

\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\varphi\subset\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right);\,\,\varphi(x)\equiv 1,\,\,\mbox{if }x\in\left[\frac{3}{4},\frac{9}{4}\right].

Let
f_{N}:=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\frac{n}{N})\phi_{n}(x)
where
N\geqslant 2
is a dyadic number. Thanks to the orthogonality, we have

\|f_{N}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{3N}\|\phi_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{3N}n^{2s-\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sim N^{s+\frac{1}{4}}.

By the spectral resolution, one can write

\displaystyle e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}f_{N}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\big(\frac{n}{N}\big)e^{itn(n+1)}e^{in\theta}\lambda^{n}.

For convenience, we denote
S_{N}(t,\theta,\lambda):=e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}f_{N}
.
Also, we define the maximal Schrödinger operator by

MS_{N}(\theta,\lambda)=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\big(\frac{n}{N}\big)e^{itn(n+1)}e^{in\theta}\lambda^{n}\right|.

By direct computation, one can verify that

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}f_{N}|\Big\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{r}=2\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}}|MS_{N}(\theta,\lambda)|^{r}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(3.2)

Then, it is sufficient to give a pointwise lower bound for
MS_{N}(\theta,\lambda)
.

We assert that there exists
C>0
independent of
\theta,\lambda,N
and a measurable set
\mathcal{E}
(defined in (
3.9
)) with
\operatorname{Mes}(\mathcal{E})\sim 1
(where
\operatorname{Mes}
denotes Lebesgue measure on
[0,2\pi]
), such that

\left|MS_{N}(\theta,\lambda)\right|\geqslant CN^{\frac{3}{4}}\lambda^{N},\quad\text{if}\,\,(\theta,\lambda)\in\mathcal{E}\times(1-N^{-1-},1).

(3.3)

With this claim, we can prove the lower bound for maximal estimate. Using (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}f_{N}|\Big\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{r}

\displaystyle\gtrsim N^{\frac{3r}{4}}\int_{1-N^{-1-}}^{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\lambda^{Nr+1}}{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}}(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\gtrsim N^{\frac{3r}{4}-\frac{1}{2}-}.

It implies

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|e^{it\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}f_{N}|\Big\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\gtrsim N^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{2r}-}.

Since
\|f_{N}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim N^{s+\frac{1}{4}}
, thus for
s<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2r}

\Big\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|e^{it\Delta}f_{N}\right|\Big\|_{L^{r}_{x}}>C\|f_{N}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Therefore, we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Now, we turn to prove (
3.3
).
We take
C_{0}^{-1}N^{\frac{1}{2}}<p<C_{0}N^{\frac{1}{2}}
for a large integer
C_{0}
to be fixed later. We also postponed the argument on
\mathcal{E}
later.

Next, for an odd integer
p
and an even integer
q
such that
\mathrm{gcd}(p,q)=1
, by taking
t=\frac{2\pi}{p}
and
\theta=\frac{2\pi q}{p}
, one obtains

\displaystyle S_{N}\left(\frac{2\pi}{p},\frac{2\pi q}{p},\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\lambda^{n}e^{2\pi i\frac{n(n+1)}{p}}e^{2\pi i\frac{nq}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\left(\frac{pm+k}{N}\right)\lambda^{pm+k}e^{2\pi i\frac{(pm+k)(pm+k+1)}{p}}e^{2\pi i(pm+k)\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}e^{2\pi i(\frac{k^{2}+k(q+1)}{p})}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\left(\frac{pm+k}{N}\right)\lambda^{pm+k}.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

F(x)=G\left(\frac{px+k}{N}\right)=\varphi(\frac{px+k}{N})\lambda^{px+k}.

Thus, by introducing the change of variable
y=\frac{px+k}{N}
we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{F}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(\frac{px+k}{N})\lambda^{px+k}e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\mathrm{d}x=e^{2\pi i\frac{k}{p}\xi}\frac{N}{p}\widehat{G}\left(\frac{N\xi}{p}\right).

By Poisson’s summation, we have

\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\left(\frac{pm+k}{N}\right)\lambda^{pm+k}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi i\frac{k}{p}\xi}\frac{N}{p}\widehat{G}\left(\frac{N\xi}{p}\right)=\frac{N}{p}\widehat{G}(0)+\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}e^{2\pi i\frac{k}{p}\xi}\frac{N}{p}\widehat{G}\left(\frac{N\xi}{p}\right),

which allows us split

into two parts:

S_{N}\left(\frac{2\pi}{p},\frac{2\pi q}{p},\lambda\right)=\underbrace{\frac{N}{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p}e^{2\pi i(\frac{k^{2}+k(q+1)}{p})}\widehat{G}(0)}_{\mathcal{A}}+\underbrace{\frac{N}{p}\sum_{k=1}^{p}e^{2\pi i(\frac{k^{2}+k(q+2)}{p})}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}\widehat{G}\left(\frac{N\xi}{p}\right)}_{\mathcal{B}}.

Bound on the main part

:

Direct computation yields that

\frac{N}{p}\widehat{G}(0)\geqslant\frac{N}{p}\int_{\frac{3}{4}}^{1}\lambda^{Nx}\mathrm{d}x\geqslant\frac{N}{p}\lambda^{N}.

Next, by the Gauss sum
[

]
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{p-1}e^{2\pi i\frac{n^{2}+(q+1)n}{p}}=\omega_{p}\sqrt{p}e^{2\pi i\frac{m(q+1)^{2}}{p}},

(3.4)

where

and

\displaystyle\omega_{p}=\begin{cases}1,\quad&p\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}4),\\
-i,\quad&p\equiv 3(\operatorname{mod}4).\end{cases}

For
C_{0}^{-1}N^{\frac{1}{2}}<p<C_{0}N^{\frac{1}{2}}
with a positive integer

, we have for sufficiently large
N
,

|\mathcal{A}|\geqslant\sqrt{p}\cdot\lambda^{N}\frac{N}{p}\geqslant C_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{3}{4}}\lambda^{N}.

(3.5)

Bound on the remainder
\mathcal{B}
:

Applying integrating by parts, for all

, we get

\left|\widehat{G}\left(\frac{N\xi}{p}\right)\right|=\left(\frac{p}{N\xi}\right)^{k}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{5}{2}}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}y^{k}}\left(\varphi(y)\lambda^{Ny}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}y.

Since
\varphi(y)
is smooth enough, we only need consider the case that all derivatives fall on

. Recall that

, we have a pointwise bound:

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}y^{k}}(\lambda^{Ny})\right|=\lambda^{Ny}|N\log(\lambda)|^{k}\leqslant\lambda^{Ny}.

(3.6)

Recall that
\lambda\in(1-N^{-1-},1)
, we can find for all

it follows that

. With this in hand, we take

and apply (

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{B}\right|\leqslant\sqrt{p}\cdot\left|\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{N}{p}\widehat{G}\left(\frac{N\xi}{p}\right)\right|\leqslant 4\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}|\xi|^{-2}\frac{p^{\frac{3}{2}}}{N}\ll N^{\frac{3}{4}}\lambda^{N}.

(3.7)

From (
3.6
), the above estimate is independent of

and

.

Combine (
3.5
) with (

\left|S_{N}\left(\frac{2\pi}{p},\frac{2\pi q}{p},\lambda\right)\right|\geqslant|\mathcal{A}|-|\mathcal{B}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}C_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{3}{4}}\lambda^{N}.

(3.8)

Construction of
\mathcal{E}
:

Then, we turn to construct the set

, following the strategy in
[
27
]
. For odd integer

Next, we also define

To complete the proof, for

\displaystyle\left|S_{N}\left(\frac{2\pi}{p},\widetilde{\theta},\lambda\right)-S_{N}\left(\frac{2\pi}{p},\frac{2\pi q}{p},\lambda\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\leqslant

4.
Linear Schrödinger equation

In this section, our main goal is to prove Theorem
1.3
, the almost sure convergence for the linear flow associated with randomized initial data.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}
be as in (
2.1
). Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigg\{\omega\in\Omega\,\bigg|\,\lim_{t\to 0}\big\|e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}-\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=0\bigg\}\bigg)\geqslant 1-\varepsilon.

(4.1)

To handle the linear and nonlinear problems within a common notation, we only prove the convergence for
e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}
. Setting
v=e^{it}u
, where
u=e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}
, immediately yields the convergence for
e^{it\Delta_{g}}
.

Let
\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x)
be an eigenfunction in the
n
-th eigenspace, and let
f(x)
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
be given by

f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|k|\leqslant n}c_{n,k}\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x),

(4.2)

we denote by
P_{N}
the frequency projection onto the annulus of size
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.

P_{N}(f)(x)=\sum_{\frac{N}{2}<|\lambda_{n}|\leqslant N}\sum_{|k|\leqslant n}c_{n,k}\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x).

(4.3)

To prove Proposition
4.1
, we first present a probabilistic
L^{p}
estimate and an improved Strichartz estimate:

Proposition 4.2
.

For
\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}
as in (
2.1
), there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\big\|P_{N}\big(\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big)\big\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\sqrt{p}N^{-\alpha+1},\qquad\text{\rm uniformly in}~x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

(4.4)

Proof.

By the definition of
P_{N}
in (
4.3
), one has

P_{N}\big(\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big)=\sum_{\frac{N}{2}<\lambda_{n}\leqslant N}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n+1}}\sum_{|k|\leqslant n}g_{n,k}(\omega)\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x).

Hence, one can make the use of Khinchin’s inequality for

\displaystyle c_{n,k}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n+1}}\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x)

(4.5)

to obtain

\text{LHS of}~\eqref{large deviation estimate}\leqslant C\sqrt{p}\bigg(\sum_{\frac{N}{2}<\lambda_{n}\leqslant N}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2\alpha-1}}\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{|k|\leqslant n}|\mathbf{b}_{n,k}(x)|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, by the exact Weyl’s law (

\big\|P_{N}\big(\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big)\big\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\sqrt{p}N^{-\alpha+1}.

∎

Remark 4.3

Similar as Proposition

(4.6)

Next, we show that

Corollary 4.4

(Improved

estimate)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigg\{\omega\in\Omega~\bigg|~\big\|P_{N}\big(e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big)\big\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim N^{-\alpha+1}\ln\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\big)\bigg\}\bigg)\geqslant 1-\varepsilon

holds for any
2\leqslant p<\infty
,
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
and

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigg\{\omega\in\Omega~\bigg|~\big\|P_{N}\big(e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big)\big\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim N^{-\alpha+1+}\ln\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\big)\bigg\}\bigg)\geqslant 1-\varepsilon

holds for any
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}

Proof.

Fix
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and

Then, for any

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\big\|P_{N}\big(e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big)\big\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{q}{\rm d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigg\{\omega\in\Omega~\bigg|~\big\|P_{N}\big(e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big)\big\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}>\lambda\bigg\}\bigg)\leqslant

We claim
that there exists

and a constant

such that

Proposition 4.5

(Improved Strichartz estimate)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigg\{\omega\in\Omega~\bigg|~\big\|e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim N^{-\alpha+1}\ln\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\big)\bigg\}\bigg)\geqslant 1-\varepsilon

(4.12)

holds for any
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Corollary
4.4
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Choose
\eta>0
such that
\eta<\alpha-1
. For each dyadic
N\geqslant 2
, define the event

E_{N}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\,\|P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leqslant CN^{-\alpha+1+\eta}\log N\right\}.

By (
4.8
) and (
4.12
), applied with
\varepsilon=N^{-4}
, we may choose
C>0
so that

\mathbb{P}(E_{N}^{c})\lesssim N^{-4}.

Since
\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbb{P}(E_{N}^{c})<\infty
, the Borel-Cantelli lemma yields an event
\Omega_{\text{lin}}\subset\Omega
of full probability such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{lin}}
, there exists
N_{0}(\omega)
with
\omega\in E_{N}
for all dyadic
N\geqslant N_{0}(\omega)
.

Fix
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{lin}}
. Since
\eta<\alpha-1
, the dyadic series

\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}N^{-\alpha+1+\eta}\log N

converges. Hence, for every dyadic
M\geqslant N_{0}(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\|\sum_{N>M}e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{N>M}\|e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N>M}N^{-\alpha+1+\eta}\log N,

and similarly,

\left\|\sum_{N>M}P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\sum_{N>M}N^{-\alpha+1+\eta}\log N.

Therefore, both tails converge to
0
uniformly as
M\to\infty
.

For a fixed dyadic
M
, the low-frequency part
P_{\leqslant M}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}
is smooth. Hence

\lim_{t\to 0}\|e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}P_{\leqslant M}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}-P_{\leqslant M}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=0.

Combining the dyadic decomposition

e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}-\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}=\sum_{N>M}e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}+\big(e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}P_{\leqslant M}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}-P_{\leqslant M}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\big)-\sum_{N>M}P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega},

we conclude that

\lim_{t\to 0}\|e^{it(\Delta_{g}-1)}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}-\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=0

for every
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{lin}}
. Since
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{\text{lin}})=1
, this proves the proposition.
∎

In summary, the proof of Theorem
1.3
is now complete.

5.
Resolution ansatz for the cubic NLS

In this section, we recall from
[
6
]
only the ingredients of the random averaging operator (RAO) ansatz that will be used in Section
6
. In particular, we keep the dyadic decomposition of the truncated flow, the time-localized objects
\psi_{N}^{\dagger}
and
w_{N}^{\dagger}
, the conditional law-equivalence lemma, and the large-probability bounds coming from the
\operatorname{Loc}(N)
scheme.

5.1.
Dyadic decomposition of the truncated flow

For each dyadic number
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, let
u_{N}
be the smooth solution to the truncated Wick-ordered cubic NLS

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u_{N}+(\Delta_{g}-1)u_{N}=\mathcal{N}(u_{N}),\\
u_{N}(0)=P_{\leqslant N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}.\end{cases}

(5.1)

Following
[
6
, Section 1.4]
, one introduces the dyadic increments

v_{N}:=u_{N}-u_{N/2},\qquad N\geqslant 2,

and decomposes

v_{N}=\psi_{N}+w_{N}.

Consequently,

u_{N}=\sum_{L\leqslant N}(\psi_{L}+w_{L}).

The singular part
\psi_{N}
is encoded by the random averaging operators
\mathcal{H}^{N}_{n}(t):E_{n}\to E_{n}
, defined for
N/2<n\leqslant N
, through the identity

\pi_{n}\psi_{N}(t)=\lambda_{n}^{-\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\mathcal{H}^{N}_{n}(t)(e_{n}^{\omega}).

(5.2)

We shall not use the explicit Duhamel formula for
\mathcal{H}^{N}_{n}(t)
.

The main probabilistic well-posedness result from
[
6
]
is the following local convergence theorem.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
\alpha>1
. There exist constants
C_{1}>c_{1}>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that, for every
R\geqslant 1
, there is a measurable set
\Sigma_{R}\subset H^{\alpha-1-}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfying

\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma_{R}^{c})<C_{1}e^{-c_{1}R^{\delta_{0}}},

with the following property. If
\phi\in\Sigma_{R}
, then the sequence
(u_{N})_{N}
converges in

C([-\tau_{R},\tau_{R}];H^{\alpha-1-}(\mathbb{S}^{2})),\qquad\tau_{R}:=R^{-C_{1}},

to a solution
u
of (
1.1
). Moreover,

u=\psi_{\leqslant\infty}+w_{\leqslant\infty},

where

\psi_{\leqslant\infty}\in C([-\tau_{R},\tau_{R}];\mathcal{C}^{(\alpha-1)-}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))\quad\text{and}\quad w_{\leqslant\infty}\in C([-\tau_{R},\tau_{R}];H^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))

for some
s_{0}>\frac{1}{2}
. Here

\mathcal{C}^{(\alpha-1)-}(\mathbb{S}^{2}):=\bigcap_{\beta<\alpha-1}C^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{2}).

5.2.
Time-localized ansatz and the large-probability event

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
\chi\equiv 1
on
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
and
\operatorname{supp}\chi\subset(-1,1)
. For
0<T<\frac{1}{2}
, write
\chi_{T}(t):=\chi(T^{-1}t)
. The time-localized version of the RAO ansatz constructed in
[
6
, Section 5]
provides, for every dyadic
N
, compactly supported functions
u_{N}^{\dagger}
,
\psi_{N}^{\dagger}
,
w_{N}^{\dagger}
and operators
\mathcal{H}^{N,\dagger}_{n}(t):E_{n}\to E_{n}
such that

u_{N}^{\dagger}(t)=u_{N}(t),\qquad\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t)=\psi_{N}(t),\qquad w_{N}^{\dagger}(t)=w_{N}(t)

(5.3)

for all
|t|\leqslant T/2
. In addition, for
N/2<n\leqslant N
,

\pi_{n}\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t)=\chi_{T}(t)\lambda_{n}^{-\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\mathcal{H}^{N,\dagger}_{n}(t)(e_{n}^{\omega}).

(5.4)

Since each truncated solution
u_{N}
is smooth, all these objects are defined pathwise for every
\omega\in\Omega
.

We denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\leqslant N}
the sigma-algebra generated by the Gaussian variables

\big\{g_{m,k}^{\omega}:0\leqslant m\leqslant N,\ |k|\leqslant m\big\}.

For
|t|\leqslant T
, the operator
\mathcal{H}^{N,\dagger}_{n}(t)
is unitary on
E_{n}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\leqslant N/2}
-measurable; see
[
6
, Lemma 7.1]
. The following conditional law-equivalence lemma is the key probabilistic tool in Section
6
.

Lemma 5.2

(Conditional law-equivalence,
[
6
, Lemma 7.1]
)
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and let
n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}\in(N/2,N]
be integers, not necessarily distinct. Let
F
be a bounded Borel measurable functional on
\mathbb{C}\times E_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times E_{n_{\ell}}
, and let
Y
be a
\mathcal{B}_{\leqslant N/2}
-measurable random variable. Then, for every
|t|\leqslant T
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[F\big(Y,\mathcal{H}^{N,\dagger}_{n_{1}}(t)(e_{n_{1}}^{\omega}),\dots,\mathcal{H}^{N,\dagger}_{n_{\ell}}(t)(e_{n_{\ell}}^{\omega})\big)\,\big|\,\mathcal{B}_{\leqslant N/2}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[F\big(Y,e_{n_{1}}^{\omega},\dots,e_{n_{\ell}}^{\omega}\big)\,\big|\,\mathcal{B}_{\leqslant N/2}\Big]

almost surely in
\omega
.

The other ingredient needed in Section
6
is the following large-probability statement of the
\operatorname{Loc}(N)
property. We only record the bounds that will actually be used later.

Proposition 5.3

(
[
6
, Definition 5.2, Corollary 5.7 and Lemma 7.2]
)
.

Assume that
\alpha>1
. There exist parameters

(q,\gamma,\gamma_{1},\delta,s,\theta,b)=(q(\sigma),\gamma(\sigma),\gamma_{1}(\sigma),\delta(\sigma),s(\sigma),\theta(\sigma),b(\sigma))

as in (
2.7
), with
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
b>\frac{1}{2}
, and constants
R_{0}\geqslant 1
,
C_{2}>c_{2}>0
,
\delta_{1}>0
such that the following holds. For every
R\geqslant R_{0}
, set

T:=R^{-10/\theta}.

Then there exists a good data set
\Sigma_{R}^{\mathrm{loc}}\subset H^{(\alpha-1)-}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
satisfying

\mu_{\alpha}\big((\Sigma_{R}^{\mathrm{loc}})^{c}\big)\leqslant C_{2}e^{-c_{2}R^{\delta_{1}}},

with the property that, for every initial data
\phi\in\Sigma_{R}^{\mathrm{loc}}
, every dyadic
N
, and every integer
n
with
N/2<n\leqslant N
, the time-localized colored term satisfies

\|\chi_{T}(t)\pi_{n}\psi_{N}^{{\dagger}}\|_{X_{q,\infty}^{0,\gamma}(E_{n})}\lesssim RT^{-\gamma+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}N^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{2}{q}+\delta},

(5.5)

and the time-localized remainder
w_{N}^{\dagger}
satisfies

\|w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{0,b}}\leqslant R^{-1}N^{-s},

(5.6)

and, for every dyadic
L\geqslant 2N
,

\|(\mathrm{Id}-P_{\leqslant L})w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{0,b}}\leqslant\left(\frac{N}{L}\right)^{10}R^{-1}N^{-s}.

(5.7)

Remark 5.4
.

The estimate (
5.5
) is not written in this form in Definition 5.2 and Corollary 5.7 of
[
6
]
; it is obtained from the Fourier-Lebesgue bounds in

. This is the only additional piece of the RAO analysis that we shall need in Section

.

6.
Almost sure pointwise convergence of the NLS flow

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
. We work with the
\mu_{\alpha}
-almost sure solution provided by Theorem
5.1
. The singular terms
\psi_{N}
will be controlled in

by combining the time-regularity estimate (
5.5
) with the law-equivalence lemma, while the remainders
w_{N}
will be treated by Lemma
2.8
together with (

)–(
5.7
).

Fix
R\geqslant R_{0}
, let
T=R^{-10/\theta}
be as in Proposition

, and define

T_{R}:=\min\Big\{\tau_{R},\frac{T}{2}\Big\}=\min\Big\{R^{-C_{1}},\frac{1}{2}R^{-10/\theta}\Big\}.

(6.1)

On the interval
[-T_{R},T_{R}]
, the sequence
u_{N}(t)
converges to the limit
u(t)
from Theorem
5.1
, and the identities in (

Proposition 6.1
.

Fix
R\geqslant R_{0}
and set

\epsilon_{0}:=\alpha-1-\frac{1}{q}-\delta>0.

Then there exist constants
C,c,\delta_{*}>0
and a measurable set
\Sigma_{R}^{\prime}\subset\Sigma_{R}^{\mathrm{loc}}

\mu_{\alpha}\big((\Sigma_{R}^{\prime})^{c}\big)\leqslant Ce^{-cR^{\delta_{*}}},

(6.2)

and, for every
\phi\in\Sigma_{R}^{\prime}
and every dyadic
M

satisfy

\sum_{N>M}\|\psi_{N}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}([-T_{R},T_{R}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim_{R}M^{-\epsilon_{0}}.

(6.3)

Proof.

Set

\beta:=\gamma-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}>0.

Choose
p_{0}>2
so large that

\eta_{0}:=\frac{1}{q}+\delta-\frac{2}{p_{0}}>0.

Let

T_{R}\leqslant T/2

\chi_{T}(t_{j,N})=1
for all
|j|\leqslant K_{N}

Let

A_{R}:=RT^{-\beta}=R^{1+10\beta/\theta}.

For each dyadic
N

\Sigma_{R,N}^{\prime}:=\Big\{\phi\in\Sigma_{R}^{\mathrm{loc}}:\|\psi^{\dagger}_{N}(t_{j,N})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\leqslant A_{R}N^{-\epsilon_{0}}\ \text{for all }|j|\leqslant K_{N}\Big\},

and set

\Sigma_{R}^{\prime}:=\bigcap_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\Sigma_{R,N}^{\prime}.

We prove first that, for every
\phi\in\Sigma_{R}^{\prime}

\|\psi_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}([-T_{R},T_{R}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim_{R}N^{-\epsilon_{0}}.

(6.4)

Since
\psi_{N}^{\dagger}=\psi_{N}
on
[-T_{R},T_{R}]
, it is enough to estimate
\psi_{N}^{\dagger}
.

For
N/2<n\leqslant N

F_{N,n}(t):=e^{it\lambda_{n}^{2}}\chi_{T}(t)\pi_{n}\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t).

By (
5.5
) and Lemma
2.7
,

\|F_{N,n}\|_{C_{t}^{0,\beta}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{\infty})}\lesssim A_{R}N^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{2}{q}+\delta}.

(6.5)

Now fix

\chi_{T}\equiv 1
on
[-T_{R},T_{R}]

\pi_{n}\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t)=e^{-it\lambda_{n}^{2}}F_{N,n}(t),\qquad\pi_{n}\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(s)=e^{-is\lambda_{n}^{2}}F_{N,n}(s).

Using
|e^{-i\theta}-1|\lesssim|\theta|^{\beta}
for

\displaystyle\|\pi_{n}\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t)-\pi_{n}\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(s)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|F_{N,n}(t)-F_{N,n}(s)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}+|e^{-i(t-s)\lambda_{n}^{2}}-1|\,\|F_{N,n}(s)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

Since
\lambda_{n}\sim N
on the shell

\|\pi_{n}\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t)-\pi_{n}\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(s)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim A_{R}N^{-(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{2}{q}+\delta+2\beta}|t-s|^{\beta}.

Summing over

, we deduce

\|\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t)-\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(s)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim A_{R}N^{\frac{3}{2}-\alpha+\frac{2}{q}+\delta+2\beta}|t-s|^{\beta}.

(6.6)

For any
t\in[-T_{R},T_{R}]

and

\Big(\frac{3}{2}-\alpha+\frac{2}{q}+\delta+2\beta\Big)+\epsilon_{0}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}+2\beta<3+\epsilon_{0},

\|\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t)-\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t_{j,N})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim A_{R}N^{-\epsilon_{0}}.

If
\phi\in\Sigma_{R}^{\prime}

\Sigma_{R,N}^{\prime}

\|\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t_{j,N})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\leqslant A_{R}N^{-\epsilon_{0}}.

Therefore,

\|\psi_{N}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}=\|\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim A_{R}N^{-\epsilon_{0}},\qquad|t|\leqslant T_{R},

which proves (
6.4
). Summing over dyadic
N>M

\sum_{N>M}\|\psi_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}([-T_{R},T_{R}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim_{R}\sum_{N>M}N^{-\epsilon_{0}}\lesssim_{R}M^{-\epsilon_{0}},

that is, (
6.3
).

It remains to prove (
6.2
). Since
\mu_{\alpha}
is the law of
\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}
, it suffices to estimate the pullback of
\Sigma_{R}^{\prime}
to the Gaussian probability space. For fixed
N
and

E_{N,j}:=\Big\{\omega:\ \|\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t_{j,N})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}>A_{R}N^{-\epsilon_{0}}\Big\}.

By Bernstein’s inequality on the frequency shell

\|\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t_{j,N})\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim N^{2/p_{0}}\|\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t_{j,N})\|_{L_{x}^{p_{0}}}.

Hence

E_{N,j}\subset\Big\{\omega:\ \|\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t_{j,N})\|_{L_{x}^{p_{0}}}>cA_{R}N^{-\alpha+1+\eta_{0}}\Big\}

(6.7)

for some absolute constant
c>0
. Since
|t_{j,N}|\leqslant T_{R}\leqslant T/2
, we have
\chi_{T}(t_{j,N})=1
; moreover, by Lemma

, the conditional law of
\psi_{N}^{\dagger}(t_{j,N})
given
\mathcal{B}_{\leqslant N/2}
coincides with the law of
P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}
. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}(E_{N,j})\leqslant\mathbb{P}\Big(\|P_{N}\phi_{\alpha}^{\omega}\|_{L_{x}^{p_{0}}}>cA_{R}N^{-\alpha+1+\eta_{0}}\Big).

Applying Corollary
4.4
with
p=p_{0}
and

\varepsilon_{N,R}:=\exp(-c_{0}A_{R}N^{\eta_{0}})

for a sufficiently small absolute constant

, and enlarging the implicit constant in the definition of

if necessary, we obtain

\mathbb{P}(E_{N,j})\leqslant\varepsilon_{N,R}=e^{-c_{0}A_{R}N^{\eta_{0}}}.

Since
K_{N}\leqslant T_{R}N^{\frac{3+\epsilon_{0}}{\beta}}
, we have

\sum_{|j|\leqslant K_{N}}\mathbb{P}(E_{N,j})\leqslant T_{R}N^{\frac{3+\epsilon_{0}}{\beta}}e^{-c_{0}A_{R}N^{\eta_{0}}}.

Moreover,

(\Sigma_{R}^{\prime})^{c}\subset(\Sigma_{R}^{\mathrm{loc}})^{c}\cup\bigcup_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\bigcup_{|j|\leqslant K_{N}}E_{N,j}.

Therefore,

\mu_{\alpha}\big((\Sigma_{R}^{\prime})^{c}\big)\leqslant\mu_{\alpha}\big((\Sigma_{R}^{\mathrm{loc}})^{c}\big)+\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{|j|\leqslant K_{N}}\mathbb{P}(E_{N,j})\lesssim e^{-c_{2}R^{\delta_{1}}}+\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}T_{R}N^{\frac{3+\epsilon_{0}}{\beta}}e^{-c_{0}A_{R}N^{\eta_{0}}}.

Since
A_{R}=R^{1+10\beta/\theta}
is a positive power of
R
, the dyadic series on the right-hand side is bounded by
Ce^{-cA_{R}}
, and hence

\mu_{\alpha}\big((\Sigma_{R}^{\prime})^{c}\big)\leqslant Ce^{-cR^{\delta_{*}}}

for some constants
C,c,\delta_{*}>0
. This completes the proof of Proposition
6.1
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Fix
R\geqslant R_{0}
and choose
s_{0}\in(\frac{1}{2},s)
. Then, for every initial data
\phi\in\Sigma_{R}^{\mathrm{loc}}
, the nonlinear remainders
w_{N}
for the solution
u(t)
with initial data
\phi
in Theorem
5.1
satisfy the tail estimate

\sum_{N>M}\|w_{N}\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{t}^{\infty}([-T_{R},T_{R}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim_{R}M^{-(s-s_{0})}.

(6.8)

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\chi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\widetilde{\chi}\equiv 1
on
[-T,T]
. Since
w_{N}^{\dagger}
is compactly supported in
[-T,T]
, Lemma
2.8
gives

\|w_{N}\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{t}^{\infty}([-T_{R},T_{R}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leqslant\|w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{t}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\|\widetilde{\chi}w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{t}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{s_{0},b}}.

We now estimate the right-hand side by using (
5.6
) and (
5.7
). Decompose dyadically as

\|w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{s_{0},b}}\lesssim\|P_{\leqslant 2N}w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{s_{0},b}}+\sum_{L\in 2^{\mathbb{N}},\,L\geqslant 4N}\|P_{L}w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{s_{0},b}}.

For the low-frequency part, (
5.6
) yields

\|P_{\leqslant 2N}w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{s_{0},b}}\lesssim N^{s_{0}}\|w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{0,b}}\lesssim_{R}N^{s_{0}-s}.

For
L\geqslant 4N
, we have
P_{L}w_{N}^{\dagger}=P_{L}(\mathrm{Id}-P_{\leqslant L/2})w_{N}^{\dagger}
, and hence

\displaystyle\|P_{L}w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{s_{0},b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{s_{0}}\|P_{L}w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{0,b}}

\displaystyle\leqslant L^{s_{0}}\|(\mathrm{Id}-P_{\leqslant L/2})w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{0,b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{R}L^{s_{0}}\left(\frac{N}{L/2}\right)^{10}N^{-s}\lesssim_{R}N^{10-s}L^{s_{0}-10}.

Since
s_{0}<10
, the dyadic sum over
L\geqslant 4N
is bounded by a constant multiple of
N^{s_{0}-s}
. Therefore,

\|w_{N}^{\dagger}\|_{X^{s_{0},b}}\lesssim_{R}N^{s_{0}-s}.

Because
s_{0}-s<0
, the dyadic series
\sum_{N}N^{s_{0}-s}
converges, and (
6.8
) follows.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

For every
R\geqslant R_{0}
, let

\Sigma_{R}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}:=\Sigma_{R}\cap\Sigma_{R}^{\prime}.

Then

\mu_{\alpha}((\Sigma_{R}^{{}^{\prime\prime}})^{c})\leqslant Ce^{-cR^{\delta_{*}}}

(6.9)

for some constants
C,c,\delta_{*}>0
, and for every
\phi\in\Sigma_{R}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
, the solutions
u_{M}(t)
of (
5.1
) with initial data
P_{\leqslant M}\phi
and their limit
u(t)
satisfy

\lim_{M\to\infty}\left\|\sup_{|t|\leqslant T_{R}}|u(t)-u_{M}(t)|\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=0.

(6.10)

Proof.

The measure estimate (
6.9
) follows immediately from Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
6.1
. Now fix
\phi\in\Sigma_{R}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
. On
[-T_{R},T_{R}]
, the limit solution
u
from Theorem
5.1
satisfies

u(t)-u_{M}(t)=\sum_{N>M}(\psi_{N}(t)+w_{N}(t)).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{|t|\leqslant T_{R}}|u(t)-u_{M}(t)|\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{|t|\leqslant T_{R}}\left|\sum_{N>M}\psi_{N}(t)\right|\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\left\|\sup_{|t|\leqslant T_{R}}\left|\sum_{N>M}w_{N}(t)\right|\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{N>M}\|\psi_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}([-T_{R},T_{R}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{N>M}\|w_{N}\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{t}^{\infty}([-T_{R},T_{R}]\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

The first term tends to
0
by Proposition
6.1
. The second one tends to
0
by Lemma
6.2
. Hence (
6.10
) follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Set

\Sigma_{*}:=\bigcup_{m\geqslant R_{0},\,m\in\mathbb{N}}\Sigma_{m}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}.

By (
6.9
),

\mu_{\alpha}((\Sigma_{*})^{c})=\mu_{\alpha}\Big(\bigcap_{m\geqslant R_{0}}(\Sigma_{m}^{{}^{\prime\prime}})^{c}\Big)\leqslant\inf_{m\geqslant R_{0}}\mu_{\alpha}((\Sigma_{m}^{{}^{\prime\prime}})^{c})=0.

Hence
\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma_{*})=1
.

Fix
\phi\in\Sigma_{*}
. Then there exists
m\geqslant R_{0}
such that
\phi\in\Sigma_{m}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
. Let
u
be the corresponding local solution on
[-T_{m},T_{m}]
, where
T_{m}
denotes the quantity in (
6.1
) with
R=m
. For
\varepsilon>0
, define

E_{\varepsilon}:=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}:\limsup_{t\to 0}|u(t,x)-\phi(x)|>\varepsilon\Big\}.

We remark that
E_{\epsilon}
depends on
\phi
. Below we will prove that
E_{\epsilon}
is a null Lebesgue measurable set.

To see this, fix a dyadic number
M
. Since
u_{M}
is a smooth solution with smooth initial data
P_{\leqslant M}\phi
, we have

\lim_{t\to 0}u_{M}(t,x)=P_{\leqslant M}\phi(x)\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}\subset

\displaystyle~\Big\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}:\sup_{|t|\leqslant T_{m}}|u(t,x)-u_{M}(t,x)|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\cup\Big\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}:|P_{>M}\phi(x)|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big\}.

By Chebyshev’s inequality and Proposition
6.3
,

\displaystyle|E_{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left\|\sup_{|t|\leqslant T_{m}}|u(t)-u_{M}(t)|\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|P_{>M}\phi\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }M\to\infty.

Here the first term tends to
0
by Proposition
6.3
, while the second tends to
0
because
\phi\in\Sigma_{m}\subset H^{(\alpha-1)-}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
P_{>M}\phi\to 0
in
L^{2}
. We conclude that
|E_{\varepsilon}|=0
for every
\varepsilon>0
, that is,

\lim_{t\to 0}u(t,x)=\phi(x)\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

This proves Theorem
1.3
.
∎
