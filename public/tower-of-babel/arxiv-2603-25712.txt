Nonlocal Portal to the Dark Sector
Sergey Kovalenko, Sergey Kuleshov, Valery E. Lyubovitskij, Alexey S. Zhevlakov

Abstract
We propose a nonlocal realization of the Stueckelberg portal between the Standard Model and Dark Sector, which decouple in the local limit. This implies that the mediator, U ​ ( 1 ) D U(1)_{\rm D} Dark Photon A ′ A^{\prime} with a Stueckelberg mass, interacts nonlocally with the Standard Model quarks and leptons. We study phenomenological implications of this scenario for the meson decays into semi-invisible and invisible channels. We discuss the experimental limitations on the model parameters, including the nonlocality scale.



I
Introduction

Light, feebly coupled vector bosons appear ubiquitously in extensions of
the Standard Model (SM), motivated by Dark Sectors (DSs), anomaly-free

U(1)
gauge symmetries, and mediators connecting visible and hidden degrees
of freedom. A particularly well studied benchmark is the “Dark Photon”
scenario, in which a new Abelian gauge boson interacts with the SM through
kinetic mixing with hypercharge or electromagnetism
Okun:1982xi
;
Holdom:1985ag
;
Essig:2013lka
;
Fabbrichesi:2020wbt
.
In this work we focus on a complementary and phenomenologically distinct
setup: a massive Dark Photon
A^{\prime}_{\mu}
as a
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge boson
acquiring its mass via the Stueckelberg mechanism. The latter also leads
to flavor non-diagonal
A^{\prime}-
SM fermion couplings—Stueckelberg portal (StP)
introduced in Ref.
Kachanovich:2021eqa
.
The kinetic mixing, if introduced, can be absorbed by the flavor diagonal
vector couplings by an appropriate field redefinition. Moreover, we assume
that these
A^{\prime}-
SM interactions are nonlocal with a characteristic
nonlocality scale
\Lambda_{\rm NL}
related to some UV, possibly
super-Planckian, physics. As revealed in Ref.
Krasnikov:1987mz

the nonlocality, allows in the case of the kinetic mixing, reconciling
the tension between the observable DM relic density and non-observations
DM signal in direct detection experiments.

Depending on the spectrum of possible hidden-sector states,
A^{\prime}
can decay
visibly into lepton flavor conserving as well as flavor violating channels

A^{\prime}\to\ell_{1}^{\pm}\ell_{2}^{\mp}
with
\ell=e,\mu,\tau
and into hadrons
or dominantly invisibly
A^{\prime}\to\chi\bar{\chi}
into the DS
particles. The hadronic phenomenology is controlled by the embedding of
the quark currents in into low-energy QCD degrees of freedom. This makes
rare meson processes one of the primary probes of the model for
m_{A^{\prime}}

ranging from the sub-MeV up to the multi-GeV scale, and yields a set of
constraints and signatures that can be different from the usual kinetically
mixed Dark Photon scenario.

Our goal is twofold. First, we match the quark-level
A^{\prime}
nonlocal
interactions onto an effective theory of pseudoscalar and vector mesons
including the anomalous Wess–Zumino–Witten (WZW) termz.
Second, using this effective framework we compute a set of benchmark amplitudes
and decay widths relevant to laboratory searches, including radiative
semi-invisible pseudoscalar meson decays and invisible vector meson decays
with missing energy lost in
A^{\prime}
decays into the DS particles.
These observables allow for a consistent recasting of existing limits and
provide a map between fermion-level couplings and accessible meson
phenomenology. We analyze the impact of the nonlocality of
A^{\prime}-
SM
interactions and estimate the corresponding scale of nonlocality.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we formulate the
quark-level nonlocal
A^{\prime}-
model with the assumptions and conventions we adopt.
In Sec.
III
we match the quark currents onto the chiral
and resonance (vector-meson) effective theory, including the WZW-induced
interactions relevant for anomalous transitions.
In Sec.
IV
we derive decay widths and branching ratios
for the key mesonic channels and discuss characteristic
kinematic regimes (on-shell versus off-shell
A^{\prime}
production).
Also we assemble and interpret the corresponding constraints, distinguishing
visible and invisible
A^{\prime}
decay scenarios, and summarize our analysis.
In Sec.
V
, we briefly discuss existing constraints
on Dark Photons from the perspective of our nonlocal model.
Finally, in Sec.
VI
we present the summary of our
results and findings.

II
Theoretical setup

We consider a setup involving two isolated sectors—the SM
sector and the DS mutually singlet/sterile with respect to gauge symmetries

U(1)_{D}

S

\chi

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\equiv\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{C}}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{Y}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{D}}

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}{X}^{\mu\nu}{X}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}B_{\mu\nu}{\epsilon}_{Y}{X}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}_{X}X^{\mu}X_{\mu}

(2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\,(i\not{\partial}-m_{\psi})\,\psi+\bar{\chi}\,(i\not{\partial}-m_{\chi})\chi+e^{\prime}X^{\mu}\,\bar{\chi}\,\gamma_{\mu}\chi

\displaystyle{X}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}{X}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}{X}_{\mu},\ \ \ {B}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}{B}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}{B}_{\mu}

Note that this type of nonlocality cannot be generated by local renormalizable

Krasnikov:1987mz
.
Its fundamental origins should reside among nonlocal objects like strings,

In this case, one expects that
\Lambda_{\rm NL}

\Lambda_{\rm Pl}

do not exclude sub-Planckian values for the nonlocality scale

\Lambda_{\rm NL}

may lie in the TeV ballpark.

s
-channel processes, where a massive Dark Photon is produced on-shell,
corresponding to time-like momentum transfer
k^{2}=m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}
.
In the nonlocal portal models, the constant kinetic mixing parameter

\epsilon_{Y}
in (
2
) or, as in the present case,
the Stueckelberg couplings
g^{v,a}_{ij}
in (
5
)
are replaced by momentum-dependent quantities according
to Eq. (
6
).
Therefore, the bounds existing in the literature on local Dark Photon models,
typically presented in the form
\epsilon<\epsilon_{\rm exp}(m_{A^{\prime}})
,
can be recast into the constraint

\hat{\epsilon}(m_{A^{\prime}}^{2};\Lambda_{\rm NL})g/e<\epsilon_{\rm exp}(m_{A^{\prime}})

for processes involving

s
-channel photon exchange
k^{2}=m^{2}_{A^{\prime}}
, resulting in a lower bound curve
in the
(m_{A^{\prime}},\Lambda_{\rm NL})
plane. We show the corresponding exclusion plot
in Fig.
6
, derived by applying this approach
to the collider and cosmological bounds on the local Dark Photon collected
in Ref.
Caputo:2025avc
.

.

As seen from Fig.
6
, the scale
\Lambda_{\rm NL}
of
nonlocal interaction between Dark Photons of mass
m_{A^{\prime}}
and SM fermions
weakens in the small
m_{A^{\prime}}
limit, although the couplings may remain
significant. This behavior originates from the infrared suppression of
the nonlocal form factor
\hat{\epsilon}(k^{2})\propto(k^{2}/\Lambda_{\rm NL}^{2})^{2}

in (
7
), significantly reducing the on-shell production
rate in the regime
m_{A^{\prime}}\ll\Lambda_{\rm NL}
. Consequently, searches
relying on resonant Dark Photon production lose sensitivity in the presence
of nonlocal interactions.

As observed in Ref.
Krasnikov:2024fdu
, this behaviour also allows
one to reconcile the stringent limits from direct dark-matter searches
with the observed relic abundance. In our setup, with
\chi
as the DM
candidate, direct detection proceeds via

\chi+\mathrm{SM}\to\chi+\mathrm{SM}
scattering mediated by
t
-channel

A^{\prime}
exchange. This rate is suppressed because the relevant momentum
transfers are small, and the nonlocal form factor is correspondingly reduced.
By contrast, the thermal annihilation
\chi\chi\to\mathrm{SM}
dominated
by
s
-channel on-shell
A^{\prime}
contribution can remain large enough to
reproduce the observed relic density.

Invisible and semi-invisible meson decays considered in the previous sections
are tightly constrained by fixed-target
experiments employing missing-energy and missing-momentum techniques.
For fully invisible final states, the strongest bounds come from the BABAR
search
BaBar:2009gco
and from the NA64 program at the CERN SPS using
electron and positron beams
NA64:2023wbi
.
In addition, projected sensitivities based on the expected
\pi^{0}
,
\eta
,
and
\eta^{\prime}
yields in NA64 with a negative-pion beam were estimated
in Ref.
Gninenko:2023rbf
. For the invisible decay

\rho^{0}\to\chi\bar{\chi}
, constraints derived from the
\rho
-meson
yields—computed within a Glauber-model treatment for NA64—were presented
in Ref.
Schuster:2021mlr
.
We used these experimental constraints to analyze the numerical impact of
the nonlocality form factor (
6
) on the meson decay rates
derived in the previous sections and estimated the corresponding limits
on the scale
\Lambda_{\rm NL}
.

We found that the decay rates of both vector

V=\rho,\omega,\phi,J/\psi,\Upsilon
and pseudoscalar
P=\pi^{0},\eta,\eta^{\prime}

mesons probe the effective non-local couplings
at time-like momentum transfer,
\hat{\epsilon}(k^{2}=m_{A^{\prime}}^{2})
, implying that
the corresponding bounds become suppressed in the nonlocal scenario when

\Lambda_{\rm NL}\gg m_{M}
. Consequently, limits derived from invisible
meson decay searches rapidly weaken due to the momentum-dependent suppression

\hat{\epsilon}(k^{2})\propto(k^{2}/\Lambda_{\rm NL}^{2})^{2}
.
As a result, for
\Lambda_{\rm NL}>1
TeV the Dark Photon mass
m_{A^{\prime}}
becomes
unconstrained both from meson decays and from cosmological and collider data
in Fig.
6
. We already noted in Sec.
II

that values of the nonlocality scale
\Lambda_{\rm NL}
below the TeV range
are challenging from the viewpoint of theoretical consistency and
the construction of a sensible UV completion.

VI
Conclusions

We have developed a meson-level effective description for a light vector boson

A^{\prime}_{\mu}
that couples directly to SM quark and lepton currents through generic
vector and axial-vector interactions. In this class of models, the dominant
hadronic production and decay channels of
A^{\prime}
at sub- to few-GeV energies are
governed by how the underlying quark currents are embedded into low-energy QCD,
so rare meson processes provide an essential—and often leading—set
of probes.

Starting from the quark-level interactions in Eq. (
5
), we
constructed an effective theory for pseudoscalar mesons supplemented by
vector-meson resonances, Eq. (
8
). The matching is realized
through resonance saturation: vector quark currents generate
A^{\prime}
couplings
mediated by vector mesons and their mixing, providing a practical description
in the GeV regime. We also incorporated the anomalous WZW
terms that govern radiative and anomalous transitions such as
P\to\gamma A^{\prime}
,

V\to PA^{\prime}
, and related channels, and expressed the corresponding effective
couplings in terms of the underlying quark-level parameters. Within this unified
framework we derived the decay widths and branching ratios relevant for both
visible and invisible scenarios. For visible
A^{\prime}
decays, narrow-resonance
searches in dilepton spectra map directly onto meson transition rates, while for
invisible
A^{\prime}
decays the same meson transitions lead to missing-energy or
missing-mass signatures, enabling stringent constraints on
A^{\prime}
models.

We also explored the phenomenological viability of the nonlocal setup, assuming
that the SM–dark-sector portal is nonlocal and characterized by a nonlocality
scale
\Lambda_{\rm NL}
controlling the effective
A^{\prime}
–SM-fermion interaction.
In particular, for values of the nonlocality scale in the UV-controlled regime,

\Lambda_{\rm NL}\gtrsim 1~\mathrm{TeV}
, the portal suppression renders existing
meson-decay, cosmological, and collider constraints insensitive to the
Dark Photon mass
m_{A}^{\prime}
leaving it unconstrained, at least for the benchmark
assumptions adopted in our analysis.
Moreover, the nonlocal portal can reconcile an observable relic abundance
of the dark fermion
\chi
with stringent direct-detection limits as well
as with bounds from rare meson decays, cosmology, and colliders.
This reinforces the phenomenological motivation for introducing
a nonlocal portal, as first suggested in Ref.
Krasnikov:2024fdu
.

Finally, the effective description presented here provides a systematic and
flexible framework for embedding nonlocality in quark–
A^{\prime}
interactions into
meson-level observables, and can be straightforwardly extended to refined
hadronic inputs and dedicated experimental sensitivity studies.
