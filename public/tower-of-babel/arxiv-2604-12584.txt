Introduction

Computing the distance between two graphs is a notoriously hard
algorithmic problem

[
2
,
16
,
20
,
25
]
. One of the most
natural distance measures is the
graph edit distance
, defined to be
the minimum number of edges that have to be flipped (added or deleted) in
one of the graphs to make it isomorphic to the other graph (assuming
both graphs have the same number of vertices). Grohe, Rattan, and
Woeginger
[
20
]
proved that the problem of computing the
edit distance between two graphs of the same order (denoted by GED in the
following) is NP-hard even if both input graphs are trees, and Arvind,
Köbler, Kuhnert, and Vasudev
[
2
]
proved that there is
no PTAS for GED unless
{\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{NP}}
. However, there is a very interesting and
nontrivial upper bound. Arora, Frieze, and Kaplan
[
1
]
give an
additive approximation algorithm for GED running in
quasipolynomial time: given
\varepsilon>0
and two graphs
G,H
of order

n
, the algorithm approximates the edit distance up to an additive
error of
\varepsilon n^{2}
in time
n^{O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log n)}
. Note
that such an approximation is only interesting in the
dense regime

of graphs with
\Omega(n^{2})
edges.

We parametrise the edit-distance problem by the VC dimension of the
input graphs, where we define the VC dimension of a graph
G
to be
the VC dimension of the neighbourhood set system

\mathcal{N}_{G}\coloneqq\{N_{G}(v)\mid v\in V(G)\}
.
As our first main result, we show that edit distance of graphs
of bounded VC dimension can be approximated up to an additive error
of
\varepsilon n^{2}
in polynomial time.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an algorithm that, given
\varepsilon>0
and
n
-vertex
graphs
G,H
of VC dimension at most
d
, outputs a bijection

\varphi\colon V(G)\to V(H)
with
\mathrm{cost}(\varphi)\leq\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)+\varepsilon n^{2}

in time
n^{O\left(\varepsilon^{-2}d\right)}
.

Here,
\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)
denotes the graph edit distance between
G
and
H
,
and
\mathrm{cost}(\varphi)
denotes the number of edge mismatches between
G

and
H
under the bijection
\varphi
. Note that the algorithm interpolates
smoothly between this case of bounded VC dimension and Arora et al.’s
quasipolynomial time algorithm for arbitrary graphs
[
1
]
,
as the VC dimension of an
n
-vertex graph is at most
\log n
.

In fact, our algorithm even works for edge-weighted graphs (with
rational edge weights bounded in their absolute value). Here, an
isomorphism also needs to preserve edge weights, and changing the
weight of an edge
e
from
w
to
w^{\prime}
has cost
|w-w^{\prime}|
(where
non-edges are assumed to have weight
0
). Also, to generalise the
notion of VC dimension, we employ the natural approach of defining
a neighbourhood set system for each weight
threshold
t\in\mathbb{Q}
. More precisely, for every

t\in\mathbb{Q}
, we require that the set system

\mathcal{N}_{G}^{t}\coloneqq\{N_{G}^{t}(v)\mid v\in V(G)\}
has VC
dimension at most
d
, where
N_{G}^{t}(v)
denotes all neighbours of

v
reachable via an edge of weight
>t
.
1
1

1

One might also be
tempted to consider the set system

\mathcal{N}_{G}^{*}\coloneqq\{N_{G}^{t}(v)\mid v\in V(G),t\in\mathbb{Q}\}
. However, note that this is a more restrictive definition: If

\mathcal{N}_{G}^{*}
has VC dimension
d
, then in particular

\mathcal{N}_{G}^{t}
has VC dimension at most
d
for every

t\in\mathbb{Q}
.

While VC dimension has played an important role in algorithmic
geometry at least since the late 1980s (see, for example,

[
29
]
), graph algorithms exploiting bounded VC dimension
have only started to appear fairly recently

[
7
,
13
,
14
,
26
]
. In this context, it is
worth mentioning that the monadically dependent graph classes,
conjectured to be the limit for the fixed-parameter tractability of
first-order model checking, can be defined in terms of bounded VC
dimension of set systems definable in first-order logic
(see, for example,
[
11
, Conjecture 1]
). However, all
these results and the conjecture are based on
more general set systems than just the neighbourhood set system

\mathcal{N}_{G}
. We are not aware of algorithmic results just
exploiting a bounded VC dimension of
\mathcal{N}_{G}
.
However, many other graph parameters that have received a lot of
attention in the graph theory literature, such as maximum degree,
tree width, clique width
[
8
]
, twin width
[
5
]

or merge width
[
12
]
, can be bounded in terms of VC
dimension, so algorithms for graph classes of bounded VC dimension
also apply to classes where any one of these parameters is bounded.

Actually, Arora et al.’s approximation algorithm
[
1
]

is not just for edit distance, but for the much wider family of quadratic
assignment problems (QAP). In an assignment problem, we generally wish
to minimise a cost function over the set of all permutations on
the set
[n]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,n\}
. Each permutation can be represented by

n^{2}
variables
x(v,v^{\prime})
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{v^{\prime}}x(v,v^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v}x(v,v^{\prime})=1

\displaystyle\forall v,v^{\prime}\in[n],

(1.1)

\displaystyle x(v,v^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq 0

\displaystyle\forall v,v^{\prime}\in[n].

(1.2)

An (integral) solution
x
satisfying these constraints is called
a
perfect matching
. In an instance of QAP, we are given rational
coefficients
c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Q}
for all
v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}
and we wish
to find a perfect matching
x
that minimises

\mathrm{cost}(x)=\sum_{v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}}c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})\cdot x(v,v^{\prime})\cdot x(w,w^{\prime}).

It is well-known that GED is a special case of QAP
[
1
,
2
]
;
see
Example

2.1
.

To generalise our results to QAP, we introduce a notion of VC dimension
for QAP instances. Basically, the idea is to view the coefficients

c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})
as edge weights in a graph with vertex set
[n]\times[n]
,
and define the VC dimension of the QAP instance to be the VC dimension of the
resulting edge-weighted graph. We stress that for graph problems such as GED,
the VC dimension of the corresponding QAP instance coincides with the
VC dimension of the underlying (edge-weighted) graphs (up to a constant factor).
We obtain the following result that extends the results
of Arora et al.
[
1
]
. A QAP instance is
B
-bounded

if
|c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})|\leq B
for all
v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}\in[n]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There is an algorithm that, given
\varepsilon>0
and a
B
-bounded
QAP instance of VC dimension at most
d
, outputs a perfect matching
x

such that
\mathrm{cost}(x)\leq\mathrm{cost}(x^{*})+\varepsilon n^{2}
in time

n^{O\left(B^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}(d+\log B\varepsilon^{-1})\right)}
,
where
x^{*}
denotes an optimum solution.

Going back to graphs, a particularly interesting special case of GED
is the
robust graph isomorphism
problem. For every
\varepsilon>0
,
consider the following promise problem
\varepsilon
-
{\mathsf{GI}}
: given two

n
-vertex graphs
G,H
such that either
G
and
H
are isomorphic
or
G
and
H
have edit distance at least
\varepsilon n^{2}
, decide
if
G
and
H
are isomorphic. Note that this is a version of robust
graph isomorphism for the dense regime. For the sparse regime where
the promise is that the edit distance is either
0
or at least

\varepsilon(n+m)
, where
m
denotes the number of edges of the input
graph, the problem has been studied by O’Donnell, Wright, Wu, and
Zhou
[
31
]
, and they obtained various hardness results.

It is an open problem whether
\varepsilon
-
{\mathsf{GI}}
can be solved in
polynomial time for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
; we remark that O’Donnell
et al.’s lower bounds
[
31
]
do not yield direct insights
for the dense regime. It follows from
[
1
]
that
\varepsilon
-
{\mathsf{GI}}

can be solved in time
n^{O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log n)}
, slightly improving
over Babai’s quasi-polynomial isomorphism test
[
4
]

(which solves the problem without making use of the promise). More generally,

Theorem

1.1
implies that
\varepsilon
-
{\mathsf{GI}}
can be
solved in time
n^{O(\varepsilon^{-2}d)}
on graphs of VC dimension at most
d
.
Observe that this regime is interesting since the restriction of (exact)
graph isomorphism is known to be hard (as hard as the general graph
isomorphism problem) for graph classes of bounded expansion and bounded
twin width, both of which implies bounded VC dimension.

We show that both runtime bounds can be slightly improved, and in fact this can be achieved by a much simpler
algorithm than for
Theorem

1.1
. The

Weisfeiler-Leman (WL) algorithm
, going back to
[
36
]
,
is a simple combinatorial algorithm originally designed as an (incomplete)

(see, e.g.,
[
4
,
6
,
18
,
24
]
).
Additionally, it has recently received a lot of attention due to a number

[

19

28
]
, graph homomorphisms

[

,
9
]
, and even machine learning

[

,

,

]
.
We prove that the
O(\varepsilon^{-1}d\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))
-dimensional

\varepsilon
-
{\mathsf{GI}}
on graphs of VC dimension
d
.
Also, for general
n
-vertex graphs, we show that

\varepsilon
-
{\mathsf{GI}}
.
Since the
k

O(k^{2}n^{k+1}\log n)

[
23
]
, this implies that

\varepsilon
-
{\mathsf{GI}}

,
and in time

Our upper bound on the WL dimension are in stark contrast to well-known results stating that the

[

For coloured graphs, we also give an upper bound of

We complement these results by a lower bound stating that

{\mathsf{GI}}

Preliminaries

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
[a,b]
the set

\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid a\leq x\leq b\}
and by
[a\pm b]
the set
[a-b,a+b]
.
For sets
X,Y
, we denote their symmetric difference by
X\mathbin{\triangle}Y\coloneqq(X\cup Y)\setminus(X\cap Y)
.
We often consider partial injective functions
\alpha\colon X\to Y
between two sets
X,Y
.
Depending on the context, it may be convenient
to view them as sets of pairs
\alpha\subseteq X\times Y
. We use
the notations
\alpha(x)=y
and
(x,y)\in\alpha
interchangeably.

Graphs

We use standard graph notation (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
).
All graphs considered in this paper are simple (i.e., no loops or multiedges)
and undirected. We write
V(G)
and
E(G)
for the vertex and
edge set of a graph
G
, respectively.
We use
vw
as a shorthand for an edge
\{v,w\}
in
G
.
For
v\in V(G)
, we write
N_{G}(v)\coloneqq\{w\in V(G)\mid vw\in E(G)\}

to denote the neighbourhood of
v
. Let
G,H
be two graphs with

|V(G)|=|V(H)|=n
, and let
\pi
be a bijection from
V(G)
to
V(H)
.
For a set of 2-element subsets
P\subseteq\binom{V(G)}{2}
,
we write
P^{\pi}\coloneqq\{\{\pi(v),\pi(w)\}\mid\{v,w\}\in P\}
.
The
edit cost of
\pi
with respect to
G,H
, denoted
by
\mathrm{cost}_{G,H}(\pi)
, is the cardinality of the set

E(G)^{\pi}\mathbin{\triangle}E(H)
, i.e., the sum of the number of
edges of
G
sent by
\pi
to non-edges of
H

and the number of non-edges of
G
sent by
\pi
to edges of
H
.
We usually write
\mathrm{cost}(\pi)
if the graphs
G,H
are clear from context.
The
graph edit distance from
G
to
H
, denoted by
\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)
,
is the minimum edit cost amongst all bijections between
V(G)
and
V(H)
.

An
edge-weighted
graph is a tuple
(G,\omega_{G})
where

\omega_{G}\colon E(G)\to\mathbb{R}
is a weight function.
Let
(H,\omega_{H})
be a second edge-weighted graph.
For a bijection
\pi\colon V(G)\to V(H)
, we define

\mathrm{cost}_{G,H}(\pi)\coloneqq\sum_{v,w\in V(G)}|\omega_{G}(vw)-\omega_{H}(\pi(v)\pi(w))|

(as a convention, we define
\omega(vw)\coloneqq 0
if
vw\notin E(G)
).
Then the graph edit distance is defined as before.

A
coloured
graph is a tuple
(G,\lambda_{G})
with

\lambda_{G}\colon V(G)\to C
, where
C
is a finite set of colours.
The sets
\lambda_{G}^{-1}(c)
,
c\in C
, are the
colour classes

of
\lambda_{G}
. The graph edit distance over coloured graphs is defined
by considering only those bijections that preserve the colour of the vertices.

Recall the definition of the quadratic assignment problem (QAP) from the
introduction. We call the domain size
n
the
order
of an instance.
Also recall that a QAP instance is
B
-bounded if the absolute value of all
coefficients is at most
B
. We write
\mathcal{A}
to denote integral feasible
solutions
x
(to the constraints (
1.1
), (
1.2
)); each such
solution corresponds to a bijection
\varphi_{x}\colon[n]\to[n]
mapping every

v\in[n]
to the unique
v^{\prime}\in[n]
such that
x(v,v^{\prime})=1
. In this case,
we have

\mathrm{cost}(x)=\sum_{v\in[n]}\sum_{w\in[n]}c(v,\varphi_{x}(v),w,\varphi_{x}(w)).

Example 2.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let

be two graphs with

Consider the coefficient function

An optimal integral solution to the QAP defined by

VC Dimension

In this section, we introduce and compare various set systems for (weighted) graphs
and QAP instances, which give rise to corresponding notions of VC dimension.
We start by recalling basic definitions and standard tools in this context.

Basics.

A
set system on V
is a set
\mathcal{H}\subseteq 2^{V}

(where
2^{V}
denotes the power set of
V
).
A subset
X\subseteq V
is
shattered by
\mathcal{H}
if

\mathcal{H}\cap X\coloneqq\{H\cap X\mid H\in\mathcal{H}\}=2^{X}
.
The
VC dimension

{\textup{VC}}(\mathcal{H})
of
\mathcal{H}
is
the maximal cardinality of a subset
X\subseteq V
shattered by
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
. An
\varepsilon
-net
for
\mathcal{H}

is a set
S\subseteq V
such that for all
H\in\mathcal{H}

with
|H|>\varepsilon\cdot|V|
, we have
H\cap S\neq\emptyset
.
Furthermore, we say that
S
is an
\varepsilon
-approximation
for
\mathcal{H}
if for all

H\in\mathcal{H}
,

|H|\in\left[\frac{n}{|S|}|H\cap S|\pm\varepsilon n\right].

Let us recall a few combinatorial lemmas concerning VC dimension.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
29
]
)
.

For every set system
\mathcal{H}
and
\varepsilon>0
,
there is an
\varepsilon
-net
S
for
\mathcal{H}
with

|S|\leq\frac{\ln|\mathcal{H}|}{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
21
]
)
.

For every set system
\mathcal{H}
of VC dimension
d

and every
\varepsilon>0
, there is an
\varepsilon
-net

S
for
\mathcal{H}
of size
|S|\leq O\left(\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right).

Lemma 3.3

(
[
27
]
,
[
17
, Theorem 47.2.2]
)
.

For every set system
\mathcal{H}
of VC dimension
d

and every
\varepsilon,\gamma>0
, a uniform sample of size

O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(d+\log\frac{1}{\gamma}\right)\right)

is an
\varepsilon
-approximation with probability at least
1-\gamma
.

Graphs.

Next, let us define the VC dimension of (weighted) graphs.
Let
G
be a graph.
The
neighbourhood set system
of
G
is defined by

\mathcal{N}_{G}\coloneqq\{N_{G}(v)\mid v\in V(G)\}.

(3.1)

We define the
VC dimension
of
G
to be the VC dimension
of
\mathcal{N}_{G}
. Let us remark at this point that various possible
definitions for the VC dimension of a graph have been considered
in the literature by associating different set systems (see,
e.g.,
[
7
,
13
,
14
,
26
]
).
However, those set systems are usually supersets of
\mathcal{N}_{G}
,
i.e., our definition of VC dimension is the most relaxed (and thus,
our algorithmic results are applicable more widely).

Let
(G,\omega)
be an edge-weighted graph with weight function

\omega\colon E(G)\to\mathbb{R}
(recall that we define

\omega(v,w)\coloneqq 0
if
vw\not\in E(G)
as a convention).
We consider a family of
threshold set systems
over
V(G)
as follows.
For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
N^{t}_{G}(v)\coloneqq\{w\in V(G)\mid\omega(v,w)>t\}
, and let
\mathcal{N}^{t}_{G}\coloneqq\{N^{t}_{G}(v)\mid v\in V(G)\}
.
This is simply the neighbourhood set system for
the
threshold graph

G^{t}
, which is the graph with the same
vertex set as
G
and edge set consisting of all edges of weight
>t
.
We define the VC dimension of
(G,\omega)
to be the maximum VC
dimension of
\mathcal{N}_{G}^{t}
over all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Observe that the maximum exists because the threshold graphs form
an increasing sequence of subgraphs of
G
, which implies that there are
at most
|E(G)|+1
distinct threshold graphs.
Furthermore, if all edge-weights are equal to a fixed constant

a\neq 0
, then the VC dimension of
(G,\omega)
is identical to the VC
dimension of the unweighted graph
G
.

QAPs.

For QAPs, we can define the VC dimension in a similar spirit.
Recall that a QAP instance of order
n
is given by the
coefficients
c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})
for all
v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}\in[n]
.
The basic idea is to view the coefficient function as an
edge-weighted graph over vertex set
[n]\times[n]

(i.e., the weight of an edge between
(v,v^{\prime})
and
(w,w^{\prime})
is

\omega((v,v^{\prime}),(w,w^{\prime}))\coloneqq c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})
).
With this in mind, we again define a threshold set system
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
similar to above as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c}&\coloneqq\{M^{t}_{c}(v,v^{\prime})\mid(v,v^{\prime})\in[n]\times[n]\},\\
\text{where }M^{t}_{c}(v,v^{\prime})&\coloneqq\{(w,w^{\prime})\mid c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})>t\}.\end{split}

(3.2)

In other words, each
(v,v^{\prime})
induces a hypothesis, made of exactly
those associations
(w,w^{\prime})
which, taken together with
(v,v^{\prime})
,
would not incur a cost above our fixed threshold.

Let us point out that there is a second, more relaxed definition
for the VC dimension of a QAP instance that is actually sufficient
for all our algorithmic applications. Indeed, while the definition
above does capture the combinatorial complexity of the subsets
of
[n]\times[n]
definable from the coefficient function

c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})
, it does not account
for one additional feature of the problem at hand: a solution to
a QAP is not an arbitrary subset of
[n]\times[n]
, but a perfect
matching, i.e., the graph of a bijection
\varphi\colon[n]\to[n]
.
Yet, the set systems
\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c}
contain many sets which are not subsets
of such a matching. As such, there may be some large subsets of
[n]\times[n]

which are irrelevant to the complexity of the solution space of the QAP at hand,
while being shattered by
\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c}
. In contrast, for a fixed bijection

\varphi:[n]\to[n]
, let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c,\varphi}&\coloneqq\{M^{t}_{c,\varphi}(v,v^{\prime})\mid(v,v^{\prime})\in[n]\times[n]\},\\
\text{where }M^{t}_{c,\varphi}(v,v^{\prime})&\coloneqq\{(w,\varphi(w))\mid c(v,v^{\prime},w,\varphi(w))>t\}.\end{split}

(3.3)

Note that
\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c,\varphi}
is the restriction of
\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c}
to
\mathrm{graph}(\varphi):=\{(v,\varphi(v))\mid v\in[n]\}
. As
such,

{\textup{VC}}(\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c,\varphi})\leq{\textup{VC}}(\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c}).

(3.4)

(We omit the index
c
in
M^{t}_{c},\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c},M^{t}_{c,\varphi}
and

\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c,\varphi}
if the
coefficient function
c
is clear from context.)

We say that a QAP represented by the coefficient function
c

has a
VC dimension weakly bounded by
d
, if for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

and all bijections
\varphi:[n]\to[n]
it holds that

{\textup{VC}}\left(\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c,\varphi}\right)\leq d
.

Remark 3.4
.

Having VC dimension weakly bounded by
d

is testable in time
n^{O(d)}
. Namely, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
the set system
\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c,\varphi}
has VC dimension at most
d

for any
\varphi
if and only if for all injective partial function

\alpha:[n]\rightharpoonup[n]
with
|\alpha|=d+1
there is a restriction

\alpha^{\prime}\subseteq\alpha
such that there are no
v,v^{\prime}
with

\alpha^{\prime}=\{(w,\alpha(w))\in\alpha\mid t<c(v,v^{\prime},w,\alpha(w))\}
.
Because the function
c
takes at most
n^{4}
different values, we only
need to consider
n^{4}+1
different values of
t
.

Note that by (
3.4
), if a QAP has VC dimension
bounded by
d
, then it has VC dimension weakly bounded by
d
.
However, the other direction is not true in general.

Lemma 3.5
.

For each
n\geq 4
, there is a cost function

c:[n]\times[n]\times[n]\times[n]\to\{0,1\}
,
such that
{\textup{VC}}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{c})=\left\lfloor\log n\right\rfloor
, whereas for each
bijection
\varphi
we have
{\textup{VC}}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{c,\varphi})\leq 1
.

Proof.

Let
k=\left\lfloor\log n\right\rfloor
. We define the cost function
c
as follows.
Let
v_{A}\in[n]
be distinct vertices for all sets
A\subseteq[k]
;
such vertices exist, since there are
2^{k}\leq n
subsets of
[k]
. Define

c(n,v_{A},1,x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in A\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and all other undefined values are
0
.
Then the VC dimension of
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{c}
is
\left\lfloor\log n\right\rfloor
,
since the set
\{(1,x)\mid x\in[k]\}
is shattered.

Furthermore, let
\varphi:[n]\to[n]
be an arbitrary bijection.
Note that each set
M^{0}_{c,\varphi}(v,v^{\prime})
contains at most one element,
namely
(1,\varphi(1))
. This means that any set of size
\geq 2

cannot be shattered.
∎

Comparison.

One of main problems considered in this paper is the GED, which can be naturally
translated into a QAP, as seen in
Example

2.1
.
Towards this end, we show that two weighted graphs of bounded VC
dimension translate to a QAP instance of bounded VC dimension (in fact,
the VC dimension is linearly bounded).

The following lemma can be proved by standard arguments (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G=(V,E,\omega_{G}),H=(V,E,\omega_{H})
be edge-weighted graphs
of VC dimensions
d_{G},d_{H}\geq 1
, respectively,
and let
d=\max\{d_{G},d_{H}\}
.
Let
c
be the coefficient function defined
in (
2.2
).
Then
{\textup{VC}}(\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c})\leq 10d
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Towards the proof, we will need the following standard result about
VC dimension, known as the Sauer-Shelah lemma.

Lemma 3.7

(
[
34
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a set system on
V
of VC dimension
d
. Then

\max_{X\subseteq V,|X|=s}|\{H\cap X\mid H\in\mathcal{H}\}|\leq\left(\frac{es}{d}\right)^{d}

for all
s\geq d
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.6
.

Suppose a set
X\subseteq V(G)\times V(H)
of size
s
shattered by

\mathcal{H}^{t}_{c}
. Note that for each
v_{1},v_{2}\in V(G)
and each

v^{\prime}\in V(H)
such that
M^{t}(v_{1},v^{\prime})=M^{t}(v_{2},v^{\prime})
, then

N^{t}_{G}(v_{1})=N^{t}_{G}(v_{2})
. Therefore,
X\cap M^{t}(v,w)
only depends on

\pi_{1}(X)\cap N^{t}_{G}(v)
and
\pi_{2}(X)\cap N^{t}_{H}(w)
.
(Here,
\pi_{i}(X)
is the projection onto the
i
-th coordinate for
i=1,2
.)

\displaystyle 2^{s}

\displaystyle=\left|\left\{X\cap M^{t}(v,w)\mid v\in V(G),w\in V(H)\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\{\pi_{1}(X)\cap N^{t}(v)\mid v\in V(G)\right\}\right|\cdot\left|\left\{\pi_{2}(X)\cap N^{t}_{H}(w)\mid w\in V(H)\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{es}{d}\right)^{2d}.

Let
c\coloneqq\frac{s}{d}
. Then we have
2^{c}\leq(ec)^{2}
, which implies

c<10
. Therefore, any shattered set has size less than
10d
.
∎

For unweighted graphs, we can prove that all above mentioned notions
of VC dimensions are linearly dependent.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
G,H
be unweighted graphs
of VC dimensions
d_{G},d_{H}\geq 1
, respectively and let

d=\max\{d_{G},d_{H}\}
.
Let
c
be the coefficient function defined in (
2.1
)
and let
\varphi:V(G)\to V(H)
be an arbitrary bijection.
Then
d\leq{\textup{VC}}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{c,\varphi})\leq{\textup{VC}}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{c})\leq 10d
.

Proof.

The second inequality holds by (
3.4
), and the third
inequality comes from
Lemma

3.6
. For the first inequality,
let
\varphi:V\to W
be a bijection.
Without loss of generality, suppose that
d_{G}\geq d_{H}
. (Otherwise, swap
the graphs through the bijection
\varphi
.) Let

\overline{\varphi}:2^{V}\to 2^{V\times W}
defined by

\overline{\varphi}(X):=\{(x,\varphi(x))\mid x\in X\}
. Note that
\overline{\varphi}
is
a bijection between subsets of
V
and subsets of
\overline{\varphi}(V)
.
Let
X\subseteq V
be shattered of size
d
by
\mathcal{N}_{G}
.
We claim that
\overline{X}\coloneqq\overline{\varphi}(X)
is shattered by

\mathcal{M}^{0}_{\varphi}
. Note that

M^{0}_{\varphi}(v,w)=\overline{\varphi}(N_{G}(v))\mathbin{\triangle}\overline{\varphi}(\varphi^{-1}(N_{H}(w)))
.
Let
\overline{X}^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{X}
. Let
w\in W
be arbitrary.

There is
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
such that
\overline{\varphi}(X^{\prime})=\overline{X}^{\prime}
.
Since
X
is shattered by
\mathcal{N}_{G}
, we have a vertex
v\in V

such that
N_{G}(v)\cap X=X^{\prime}\mathbin{\triangle}\left(\varphi^{-1}(N_{H}(w))\cap X\right)
.
Then

\displaystyle M^{0}_{\varphi}(v,w)\cap\overline{X}

\displaystyle=\left(\overline{\varphi}(N_{G}(v))\mathbin{\triangle}\overline{\varphi}(\varphi^{-1}(N_{H}(w)))\right)\cap\overline{X}

\displaystyle=\left(\overline{\varphi}(N_{G}(v))\cap\overline{X}\right)\mathbin{\triangle}\left(\overline{\varphi}(\varphi^{-1}(N_{H}(w)))\cap\overline{X}\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{\varphi}\left(N_{G}(v)\cap X\right)\mathbin{\triangle}\left(\overline{\varphi}(\varphi^{-1}(N_{H}(w)))\cap\overline{X}\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{X}^{\prime}\mathbin{\triangle}\left(\overline{\varphi}(\varphi^{-1}(N_{H}(w)))\cap X\right)\mathbin{\triangle}\left(\overline{\varphi}(\varphi^{-1}(N_{H}(w)))\cap X\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{X}^{\prime}

Hence,
d\leq{\textup{VC}}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{c,\varphi})
.
∎

4
Approximation with bounded VC dimension

As mentioned in the introduction, Arora et al.
[
1
]
show
that optimal solutions to
B
-bounded QAPs can be approximated
in quasipolynomial time. As a result, they obtain
a quasipolynomial time approximation for GED. Building
on this work, we show in this section that under the additional
assumption that the QAP instance under consideration has size
n
and
VC dimension at most
d
, its optimal solution can be approximated
up to an additive error of
\varepsilon n^{2}
in time

n^{O\left(B^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}(d+\log B\varepsilon^{-1})\right)}

(
Theorem

4.5
).
Because the reduction from the GED problem to QAP preserves VC dimension
(see
Lemma

3.6
), this result yields an algorithm
approximating GED up to an additive error of
\varepsilon n^{2}
for graphs
of VC dimension bounded by
d
, which runs in time
n^{O(\varepsilon^{-2}d)}

(proving
Theorem

1.1
).

Let us rephrase the quasipolynomial time approximation algorithm for QAPs
provided in
[
1
]
in our setting. For any injective partial function

\alpha:[n]\to[n]
, define

\displaystyle b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{n}{|\alpha|}\sum_{(w,w^{\prime})\in\alpha}c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}).

Although not stated explicitly,
[
1
, Section 3]
is devoted to
the proof of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

There is an algorithm that, given any
B
-bounded QAP and

\varepsilon>0
, finds a perfect matching
x
such that

\mathrm{cost}(x)\leq\mathrm{cost}(x^{*})+\varepsilon n^{2}
, where
x^{*}
is a fixed optimal
solution to the input QAP. The algorithm runs in time
n^{O(m)}
,
where
m
is the smallest integer such that there is
an
\alpha\subseteq\mathrm{graph}(\varphi_{x^{*}})
of size
m
, such that
for all
v,v^{\prime}\in[n]

b_{\varphi_{x^{*}}}(v,v^{\prime})\in\left[b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\pm\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon n\right].

(4.1)

For completeness, we provide a proof of
Lemma

4.1

in
Appendix

A
. Arora et al. obtain a quasipolynomial time
additive approximation algorithm for QAP by additionally proving the existence
of such
\alpha
of size
m=O\left(B^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}\log n\right)
.
In the following, we show that
m
can be bounded by the VC dimension
of the QAP at hand, yielding a polynomial-time algorithm for bounded
VC dimension QAPs.

Let
x^{*}
be a fixed optimal integral solution to the input QAP,
and let
\varphi^{*}\coloneqq\varphi_{x^{*}}
be the bijection
[n]\to[n]

corresponding to
x^{*}
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{t}_{\varphi^{*}}
be the hypergraph
for
\varphi^{*}
as defined in (
3.3
).

In order to ease exposition, let us first consider the restricted case where
the coefficient function
c
is
\{0,1\}
valued.
Note that for any
\alpha\subseteq\mathrm{graph}(\varphi^{*})
, we have

b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})=\frac{n}{|\alpha|}\sum_{(w,w^{\prime})\in\alpha}c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})=\frac{n}{|\alpha|}|M^{0}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})|.

(4.2)

where
M^{t}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime}):=M^{t}_{\varphi^{*}}(v,v^{\prime})\cap\alpha
.
Now, by
Lemma

3.3
there is an
\alpha

of size
O\left(d/\varepsilon^{2}\right)
such that

b_{\varphi^{*}}(v,v^{\prime})=|M^{0}_{\varphi^{*}}(v,v^{\prime})|\in\left[\frac{n}{|\alpha|}|M^{0}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})|\pm\frac{\varepsilon}{3}n\right]=\left[b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\pm\frac{\varepsilon}{3}n\right]

which concludes the argument.

In order to extend this proof to any
B
-bounded QAP of VC dimension

d
, we discretise
[-B,B]
using a set
T
of thresholds which
enable a suitable approximation of
b_{\varphi^{*}}
. Divide the interval

[-B,B]
into
k\coloneqq\left\lceil\frac{24B}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil

intervals of length
\frac{2B}{k}
. Let

T=\{-B,-B+\frac{2B}{k},\ldots,B-\frac{2B}{k}\}

be the set of (left) boundaries of these intervals.

The following lemma provides a statement similar to (
4.2
).
While in the general case we do not obtain equality, we show that averaging

|M^{t}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})|
over
t\in T
yields a good approximation
of
b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\alpha\subseteq\mathrm{graph}(\varphi^{*})
and any
v,v^{\prime}\in[n]
, we have

b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\in\left[\frac{2B}{k}\sum_{t\in T}\frac{n}{|\alpha|}\left|M^{t}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\right|-Bn\pm\frac{2B}{k}n\right].

Proof.

Let
v,v^{\prime}\in[n]
. For each
t\in T
and
(w,\alpha(w))\in\alpha
, we define
the “bit”

\beta(w,t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{if $t<c(v,v^{\prime},w,\alpha(w))$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By definition, we have

\left|M^{t}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\right|=\sum_{(w,\alpha(w))\in\alpha}\beta(w,t),

and for fixed
w\in[n]
,

\sum_{t\in T}\beta(w,t)=\left\lfloor\frac{k\cdot c(v,v^{\prime},w,\alpha(w))+kB}{2B}\right\rfloor\in\left[\frac{k\cdot c(v,v^{\prime},w,\alpha(w))}{2B}+\frac{k}{2}\pm 1\right].

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{t\in T}\frac{n}{|\alpha|}\left|M^{t}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{k|\alpha|}\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{(w,\alpha(w))\in\alpha}\beta(w,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{k|\alpha|}\sum_{(w,\alpha(w))\in\alpha}\sum_{t\in T}\beta(w,t)

\displaystyle\in\left[\frac{n}{k|\alpha|}\sum_{(w,\alpha(w))\in\alpha}\left(\frac{k\cdot c(v,v^{\prime},w,\alpha(w))}{2B}+\frac{k}{2}\pm 1\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2B}b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})+\frac{n}{2}\pm\frac{1}{k}n\right].

Rearranging concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Let
K\coloneqq\min\{k,|\{c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})\mid v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}\in[n]\}|\}
.
We now provide an
\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{12B}\right)
-approximation of the
set systems
\mathcal{H}^{t}_{\varphi^{*}}
for all
t
. Intuitively, we use a union
bound over all values
t
on the probability that a sample is not

\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{12B}\right)
-approximation and get the following result.

Lemma 4.3
.

There is an
\alpha
of size
O\left(\frac{B^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}(d+\ln K)\right)

such that for all
t\in T
and all
v,v^{\prime}
,

\left|M^{t}_{\varphi^{*}}(v,v^{\prime})\right|\in\left[\frac{n}{|S|}\left|M^{t}_{\varphi^{*}}(v,v^{\prime})\cap\alpha\right|\pm\frac{\varepsilon}{12B}n\right].

Proof.

Observe first that the set system
\mathcal{H}^{t}_{\varphi^{*}}
only changes when

t
passes a value of the form
c(v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime})
for some
v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}\in[n]
.
Hence, the family
\{\mathcal{H}^{t}_{\varphi^{*}}\mid t\in T\}
contains at most

K
distinct set systems.
For fixed
t
inducing a distinct set system, we have by

Lemma

3.3
that a uniform sample
\alpha
of size

O\left(\frac{c^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(d+\log\frac{1}{\gamma}\right)\right)

is an
\frac{\varepsilon}{12B}
-approximation for
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi^{*}}^{t}

with probability at least
1-\gamma
.
By the union bound on the complement event and setting

\gamma\coloneqq\frac{1}{2K}
, we have that the probability
of a random sample
\alpha
of size

O\left(\frac{c^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}(d+\ln K)\right)

being an
\frac{\varepsilon}{12B}
-approximation for

\mathcal{H}_{\varphi^{*}}^{t}
for all
t\in T
is positive, in particular, such
set exists.
∎

Similarly to the 0-1-valued case, the following result follows from

Lemma

4.2
and

Lemma

4.3
.

Lemma 4.4
.

There is an
\alpha
of size

O\left(\frac{B^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}(d+\log K)\right)
such that for all

v,v^{\prime}
,

b_{\varphi^{*}}(v,v^{\prime})\in\left[b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\pm\frac{\varepsilon}{3}n\right]

Proof.

By
Lemma

4.3
we get an
\alpha

of the given size such that

\left|M^{t}_{\varphi^{*}}(v,v^{\prime})\right|\in\left[\frac{n}{|\alpha|}\left|M^{t}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\right|\pm\frac{\varepsilon}{12B}n\right]

(4.3)

for all
t\in T
.
Then

\displaystyle b_{\varphi^{*}}(v,v^{\prime})

\displaystyle\in\left[\frac{2B}{k}\sum_{t\in T}\left|M^{t}_{\varphi^{*}}(v,v^{\prime})\right|-Bn\pm\frac{2B}{k}n\right]

\displaystyle\subseteq\left[\frac{2B}{k}\sum_{t\in T}\left(\frac{n}{|\alpha|}\left|M^{t}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\right|\pm\frac{\varepsilon}{12B}n\right)-Bn\pm\frac{2B}{k}n\right]

(
4.3
)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{2B}{k}\sum_{t\in T}\frac{n}{|\alpha|}\left|M^{t}_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\right|-Bn\pm\left(\frac{2B}{k}n+\frac{\varepsilon}{6}n\right)\right]

\displaystyle\subseteq\left[\left(b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\pm\frac{2B}{k}n\right)\pm\left(\frac{2B}{k}n+\frac{\varepsilon}{6}n\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\left[b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\pm\left(\frac{4B}{k}n+\frac{\varepsilon}{6}n\right)\right]

\displaystyle\subseteq\left[b_{\alpha}(v,v^{\prime})\pm\frac{\varepsilon}{3}n\right],

since
k=\left\lceil\frac{24B}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil
.

∎

Hence,
Lemma

4.4
guarantees the existence of an

\alpha
of size
m=O\left(\frac{B^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}(d+\log K)\right)

satisfying the requirements of
Lemma

4.1
, implying that we
can find a perfect matching
x
with

\mathrm{cost}(x)\leq\mathrm{cost}(x^{*})+\varepsilon n^{2}
in time
n^{O(m)}
.
Since
K\leq k=\left\lceil\frac{12B}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil
, we obtain the following
result.

Theorem 4.5
.

There is an algorithm that, given
\varepsilon>0
and a
B
-bounded
QAP instance of VC dimension weakly bounded by
d
, outputs a perfect
matching
x
such that
\mathrm{cost}(x)\leq\mathrm{cost}(x^{*})+\varepsilon n^{2}

in time

n^{O\left(B^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}(d+\log B\varepsilon^{-1})\right)}
,
where
x^{*}
denotes an optimum solution.

In particular, we obtain

Theorem

1.2
via
(
3.4
). In the special case where the coefficient function
takes a moderate number of different values, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

There is an algorithm that, given
\varepsilon>0
and a
B
-bounded
QAP instance of VC dimension weakly bounded by
d
, outputs a perfect matching

x
such that
\mathrm{cost}(x)\leq\mathrm{cost}(x^{*})+\varepsilon n^{2}
, where
x^{*}

denotes an optimum solution. If the coefficient function of the QAP instance
takes
\kappa
different values, the algorithm runs in time

n^{O(B^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}(d+\log\kappa))}
.

Let us now turn the problem GED of computing the edit distance between two
graphs. The objective is to find a bijection
\varphi
between the vertex
sets of the two input graphs that minimises the cost, defined as the number
of edge mismatches. We have seen in
Example

2.1
how to translate this
into a QAP. Furthermore, by
Lemma

3.6
, if the VC dimension
of the two input graphs is bounded by
d
then the VC dimension of the QAP is
weakly bounded by
O(d)
. Thus, as a corollary to

Theorem

4.5
, we obtain
the following result.

Corollary 4.7
.

There is an algorithm that, given
\varepsilon>0
and weighted

n
-vertex graphs
G,H
of VC dimension at most
d
,
outputs a bijection
\varphi:V(G)\to V(H)

such that
\mathrm{cost}(\varphi)\leq\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}+\varepsilon n^{2}
in time

n^{O\left(B^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}(d+\log B\varepsilon^{-1})\right)}
,
where
B
bounds the edge weights (in absolute value) in
G
and
H
.

For unweighted graphs, GED translates to a 0-1-valued QAP, so we can
apply
Corollary

4.6
and obtain the slightly
better bound stated as
Theorem

1.1
in the introduction.

Remark 4.8
.

It is also possible to generalise our proof for higher-degree assignment
problems. Similar to
[
1
, Section 3]
, we can proceed by induction
on the degree
r
. For
r>2
, we have to find
\alpha
such that

b_{\varphi^{*}}(v_{2},v_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{r},v_{r}^{\prime})\in\left[b^{\prime}_{\alpha}(v_{2},v_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{r},v_{r}^{\prime})\pm\frac{1}{r}\varepsilon n\right]
.
We can define the set systems accordingly, so that the

\varepsilon
-approximations will guarantee the existence of such small sample

\alpha
. Furthermore, the average will again approximate the
b
-values.
An analogous variant of
Lemma

4.4

will then be used for
\varepsilon/r
in place of
\varepsilon/3
. Then we can show
an algorithm running in time

n^{O\left(B^{2}r^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}(d+\log Br\varepsilon^{-1})\right)}

that additively approximates the solution (up to
\varepsilon n^{r}
) for every

B
-bounded degree-
r
assignment problem. Since
d\leq\log n^{2r}
,
this slightly improves the corresponding result
in
[
1
, Section 3]
.

5
Robust Graph Isomorphism and Weisfeiler-Leman Algorithm

A particularly interesting special case of GED is the

robust graph isomorphism
problem. This is a promise problem, where we are
given two graphs
G,H
, which are promised to be either isomorphic or have large
edit distance, and the task is to decide which of the two options holds.
Formally, for a fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the promise problem

\varepsilon
-GI defined as follows:

\varepsilon
-
GI

Input:
Two
n
-vertex graphs
G,H
.

Promise:
Either
G\cong H
or
\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)\geq\varepsilon n^{2}
.

Question:
Is
G\cong H
?

By
Theorem

1.1
the problem
\varepsilon
-
GI
can
be solved in time
n^{O(\varepsilon^{-2}d)}
on graphs of VC dimension at most
d
.
Still, the algorithm is fairly complicated relying on, e.g., advanced rounding
methods in the proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

In this section, we show that, for the special case of
\varepsilon
-
GI
,
we can instead use the Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm. For
k\geq 1
, the

k
-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm (
k
-WL)
is a standard
heuristic in the context of graph isomorphism testing (see, e.g.,

[
4
,
6
,
18
,
24
]
) which tries to distinguish
between two input graphs by propagating information on the local structure
of
k
-tuples
of vertices. In their seminal work, Cai, Fürer and Immerman

[
6
]
show that dimension
\Omega(n)
is required for WL to
distinguish between all non-isomorphic pairs of
n
-vertex graphs
G,H

(in particular,
k
-WL is an incomplete isomorphism test for every
k\geq 1
).
However, the graph pairs constructed by Cai, Fürer and Immerman only have small
edit distance, and thus are not a valid input for
\varepsilon
-
GI
(and
the same holds for other known constructions). In the following, we show that
much better upper bounds on the WL dimension can be achieved if we are
guaranteed that the inputs graphs have large edit distance.

5.1
The Weisfeiler-Leman Algorithm

We start by giving a brief description of the WL algorithm. Fix some
k\geq 1

and let
G
be a graph. For colourings

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\colon(V(G))^{k}\to C
, we say that
\lambda_{1}

refines

\lambda_{2}
, denoted by
\lambda_{1}\preceq\lambda_{2}
,
if
\lambda_{2}(\overline{v})=\lambda_{2}(\overline{w})
for all
\overline{v},\overline{w}\in(V(G))^{k}

for which
\lambda_{1}(\overline{v})=\lambda_{1}(\overline{w})
.
The two colourings are
equivalent
, denoted
\lambda_{1}\equiv\lambda_{2}
,
if
\lambda_{1}\preceq\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{2}\preceq\lambda_{1}
.

The
k
-WL algorithm iteratively computes finer and finer colourings
of
k
-tuples of vertices.
For
i\geq 0
we describe the colouring
\chi_{(i)}^{k,G}\colon(V(G))^{k}\to C

computed in the
i
-th round of the algorithm.
For
i=0
, each tuple is coloured with the isomorphism type of the underlying
ordered subgraph.
More formally, if
H
is a second graph,
\overline{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in(V(G))^{k}

and
\overline{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})\in(V(H))^{k}
, then

\chi_{(i)}^{k,G}(\overline{v})=\chi_{(i)}^{k,H}(\overline{w})
if and only if, for every

i,j\in[k]
, it holds that
v_{i}=v_{j}\iff w_{i}=w_{j}
and

v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\iff w_{i}w_{j}\in E(H)
.

Suppose
k\geq 2
. For
i>0
, we set

\chi_{(i)}^{k,G}(\overline{v})\coloneqq\big(\chi_{(i-1)}^{k,G}(\overline{v});\mathcal{M}_{i}(\overline{v})\big)
where

\mathcal{M}_{i}(\overline{v})\coloneqq\Big\{\!\Big\{\big(\chi_{(i-1)}^{k,G}(\overline{v}[w/1]),\dots,\chi_{(i-1)}^{k,G}(\overline{v}[w/k])\big)~\Big|~w\in V(G)\Big\}\!\Big\}

and
\overline{v}[w/i]
denotes the
k
-tuple

(v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1},w,v_{i+1},\dots,v_{k})

obtained from
\overline{v}
by replacing the
i
-th entry with
w
.
For
k=1
, the definition is analogous, but we set

\mathcal{M}_{i}(v)=\Big\{\!\Big\{\chi_{(i-1)}^{k,G}(w)~\Big|~w\in N_{G}(v)\Big\}\!\Big\}.

By definition, we obtain that
\chi_{(i+1)}^{k,G}\preceq\chi_{(i)}^{k,G}
for
every
i\geq 0
.
Hence, there is a minimal
i_{\infty}
such that

\chi_{(i_{\infty})}^{k,G}\equiv\chi_{(i_{\infty}+1)}^{k,G}
, and we denote

\chi_{(\infty)}^{k,G}\coloneqq\chi_{(i_{\infty})}^{k,G}
the
k
-stable colouring
.
The
k
-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm takes as input a graph
G
and
outputs (a colouring that is equivalent to)
\chi_{(\infty)}^{k,G}
.
This can be done in time
O(k^{2}n^{k+1}\log n)

[
23
]
.

Let
H
be a second graph.
The
k
-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm
distinguishes

G
and
H

if there is a colour
c\in C
such that

\Big|\Big\{\overline{v}\in(V(G))^{k}\mathrel{\Big|}\chi_{(\infty)}^{k,G}(\overline{v})=c\Big\}\Big|\neq\Big|\Big\{\overline{w}\in(V(H))^{k}\mathrel{\Big|}\chi_{(\infty)}^{k,H}(\overline{w})=c\Big\}\Big|.

Bijective
k
-pebble game.

We will need a well-known game characterisation of
k
-WL

[
6
,
22
]
.

Let
k\geq 1
.
For graphs
G
and
H
on the same number of vertices, we define the

bijective
k
-pebble game

{\mathsf{BP}}_{k}(G,H)
as follows:

•

The game has two players called
Spoiler
and
Duplicator
.

•

The game proceeds in rounds, each of which is associated with a pair
of positions
(\overline{v},\overline{w})
with
\overline{v}\in\big(V(G)\big)^{k}

and
\overline{w}\in\big(V(H)\big)^{k}
.

•

To determine the initial position, Duplicator plays a bijection

f\colon\big(V(G)\big)^{k}\rightarrow\big(V(H)\big)^{k}
and Spoiler
chooses some
\overline{v}\in\big(V(G)\big)^{k}
.
The initial position of the game is then set to
(\overline{v},f(\overline{v}))
.

•

Each round consists of the following steps.
Suppose the current position of the game is

(\overline{v},\overline{w})=((v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}),(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}))
.

(S)

Spoiler chooses some
i\in[k]
.

(D)

Duplicator picks a bijection
f\colon V(G)\rightarrow V(H)
.

(S)

Spoiler chooses
v\in V(G)
and sets
w\coloneqq f(v)
.
Then the game moves to position
\big(\overline{v}[i/v],\overline{w}[i/w]\big)

where
\overline{v}[i/v]\coloneqq(v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1},v,v_{i+1},\dots,v_{k})

is the tuple obtained from
\overline{v}
by replacing the
i
-th entry by
v
.

If mapping each
v_{i}
to
w_{i}
does not define an isomorphism of the induced
subgraphs of
G
and
H
, Spoiler wins the play.
More precisely, Spoiler wins if there are
i,j\in[k]
such
that
v_{i}=v_{j}\nLeftrightarrow w_{i}=w_{j}

or
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\nLeftrightarrow w_{i}w_{j}\in E(H)
.
If vertices are coloured, then
v_{i}
and
w_{i}
also need to receive the same
colour for every
i\in[k]
.
If there is no position of the play such that Spoiler wins, then Duplicator
wins.

We say that Spoiler (and Duplicator, respectively)
wins
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k}(G,H)
if
Spoiler (and Duplicator, respectively) has a winning strategy for the game.
Also, for a position
(\overline{v},\overline{w})
with
\overline{v}\in\big(V(G)\big)^{k}
and

\overline{w}\in\big(V(H)\big)^{k}
, we say that Spoiler (and Duplicator,
respectively)
wins
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k}(G,H)
from position
(\overline{v},\overline{w})

if Spoiler (and Duplicator, respectively) has a winning strategy for the game
started at position
(\overline{v},\overline{w})
.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
6
,
22
]
)
.

Suppose
k\geq 2
and let
G
and
H
be two graphs.
Then
k
-WL distinguishes
G
and
H
if and only if Spoiler wins the game

{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G,H)
.

5.2
Upper Bounds on the WL Dimension

Recall that, by the seminal work of Cai, Fürer and Immerman

[
6
]
, WL dimension
\Omega(n)
is required for WL to
distinguish between all non-isomorphic pairs of
n
-vertex graphs
G,H
.
In fact, this even holds true if we restrict to graphs of maximum degree
3
,
and hence to graphs of VC dimension at most
3
.
In the following, we show that much better upper bounds on the WL dimension can
be achieved if we are guaranteed that the edit distance between the two graphs

G,H
is large.

Towards this end, we first require some additional notation.
Let
G
be a graph.
For a tuple
\overline{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell})\in(V(G))^{\ell}
of pairwise distinct
vertices we write
(G,\overline{s})
to denote the coloured graph obtained by
individualising vertices in
\overline{s}
.
More formally, we colour the vertices of
G
using the colouring
\lambda

defined via
\lambda(s_{i})\coloneqq i
for every
i\in[\ell]
, and

\lambda(v)=0
for all
v\in V(G)\setminus\{s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}\}
.
Usually, the ordering of the elements in
\overline{s}
is not relevant for our
purposes.
Hence, slightly abusing notation, for
S\subseteq V(G)
, we also write
(G,S)

for the graph
(G,\overline{s})
where
\overline{s}
is any tuple containing each element
of
S
exactly once.
We also write
\gamma^{G,S}\coloneqq\chi_{(\infty)}^{1,(G,S)}
we denote the colouring
computed by
1
-WL on the graph
(G,S)
.
If the graph
G
is clear from context, we usually omit it and simply write

\gamma^{S}
.

The next definition introduces the key notion for our upper-bound results.
For
v,w\in V(G)
, we define
M_{G}(v,w)\coloneqq N_{G}(v)\mathbin{\triangle}N_{G}(w)
to be
the
mixed neighbourhood
of
v,w
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph and
\varepsilon>0
.
A set
S\subseteq V(G)
is
\varepsilon
-homogenising
if

\gamma^{S}(v)=\gamma^{S}(w)\implies|M_{G}(v,w)|\leq\varepsilon n.

(5.1)

for all
v,w\in V(G)
.

The following lemma is the key tool to obtain our upper bounds.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G,H
be
n
-vertex graphs,
\varepsilon>0
, and
S\subseteq V(G)

a
\left(\varepsilon/2\right)
-homogenising
set in
G
.
If
(|S|+1)
-WL does not distinguish
G
and
H
, then

\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)\leq\varepsilon n^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{\ell}
denote the colour classes of
\gamma^{G,S}
.
Since
(|S|+1)
-WL does not distinguish
G
and
H
, there is a set
S^{\prime}\subseteq V(H)
such that colour refinement does not distinguish
(G,S)
and
(H,S^{\prime})
.
Let
C^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,C^{\prime}_{\ell^{\prime}}\subseteq V(H)
denote the corresponding colour classes of
\gamma^{H,S^{\prime}}
.
Note that
\ell^{\prime}=\ell
and
|C_{i}|=|C^{\prime}_{i}|
for all
i\in[\ell]
.
Furthermore, by the properties of colour refinement,
there are integers
d_{ij}
such that every vertex in
C_{i}

has exactly
d_{ij}
neighbours in
C_{j}
for any
i,j\in[\ell]
.
Let
\pi\colon V(G)\to V(H)
be an arbitrary colour preserving bijection,
that is,
\pi(C_{i})=C_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i\in[\ell]
.
We will show that for every
v\in V(G)
,

|\{w\in V(G)\mid vw\in E(G)\iff\pi(v)\pi(w)\not\in E(H)\}|\leq\varepsilon n,

which immediately implies
\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)\leq\varepsilon n^{2}
.

Therefore, for the rest of the proof, we fix an arbitrary vertex
v\in V(G)

and let
j\in[\ell]
be the unique index such that
v\in C_{j}
.
We define

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{i\in[\ell]\mid 0<d_{ji}<|C_{i}|\}.

\displaystyle I_{+}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{i\in I\mathrel{\Big|}d_{ji}\leq\frac{1}{2}|C_{i}|\right\}

\displaystyle I_{-}

\displaystyle\coloneqq I\setminus I_{+}.

For every
w\in C_{j}
we define

R_{G}(w)\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in I_{+}}\left(C_{i}\cap N_{G}(w)\right)\quad\cup\quad\bigcup_{i\in I_{-}}\left(C_{i}\setminus N_{G}(w)\right).

Intuitively, the set
R_{G}(w)
consists of those vertices
x\in C_{i}
(for
i\in I
)
whose adjacency to
w
disagrees with the majority adjacency between

w
and the colour class
C_{i}
.
Observe that for all
w\in C_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle|R_{G}(w)|

\displaystyle=|R_{G}(v)|,

(5.2)

\displaystyle M_{G}(v,w)

\displaystyle=N_{G}(v)\mathbin{\triangle}N_{G}(w)=R_{G}(v)\mathbin{\triangle}R_{G}(w)

(5.3)

Claim 5.4
.

Let
i\in I
and let
x\in C_{i}
. Then

|\{w\in C_{j}\mid x\in R_{G}(w)\}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|C_{j}|.

Proof of Claim.

Fix
x\in C_{i}
.
If
i\in I_{+}
, then
d_{ji}\leq\frac{1}{2}|C_{i}|
and hence, by definition
of
R_{G}
, we have

|\{w\in C_{j}\mid x\in R_{G}(w)\}|=d_{ij}=d_{ji}\frac{|C_{j}|}{|C_{i}|}\leq\frac{1}{2}|C_{j}|.

Similarly, if
i\in I_{-}
, then

|\{w\in C_{j}\mid x\in R_{G}(w)\}|=|C_{j}|-d_{ij}=|C_{j}|-d_{ji}\frac{|C_{j}|}{|C_{i}|}<|C_{j}|-\frac{1}{2}|C_{j}|=\frac{1}{2}|C_{j}|.

∎

Claim 5.5
.

There is a
w\in C_{j}
with

|R_{G}(w)\cap R_{G}(v)|\leq|R_{G}(w)\setminus R_{G}(v)|
.

Proof of Claim.

Suppose for contradiction, that for all
w\in C_{j}
, we have

|R_{G}(w)\cap R_{G}(v)|>|R_{G}(w)\setminus R_{G}(v)|.

In particular, we have

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{w\in C_{j}}|R_{G}(w)|<\sum_{w\in C_{j}}|R_{G}(w)\cap R_{G}(v)|.

Furthermore, by
5.4
,

\sum_{w\in C_{j}}|R_{G}(w)\cap R_{G}(v)|=\sum_{x\in R_{G}(v)}|\{w\in C_{j}\mid x\in R_{G}(w)\}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|C_{j}||R_{G}(v)|.\\

Together, we have

\sum_{w\in C_{j}}|R_{G}(w)|<2\sum_{w\in C_{j}}|R_{G}(w)\cap R_{G}(v)|\leq|C_{j}||R_{G}(v)|,

which is a contradiction by (
5.2
).
∎

Claim 5.6
.

|R_{G}(v)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon n
.

Proof of Claim.

Let
w\in C_{j}
be as given by
5.5
. Then

\displaystyle|R_{G}(v)|

\displaystyle\leq|R_{G}(v)|+|R_{G}(w)\setminus R_{G}(v)|-|R_{G}(w)\cap R_{G}(v)|

\displaystyle=|R_{G}(v)\triangle R_{G}(w)|

\displaystyle=|M_{G}(v,w)|

(
5.3
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon n,

as
\gamma^{G,S}(v)=\gamma^{G,S}(w)
and
S

is
(\varepsilon/2)
-homogenising.
∎

We now want to bound the quantity

|\{w\in V(G)\mid vw\in E(G)\iff\pi(v)\pi(w)\notin E(H)\}|
.
Observe that outside the sets
R_{G}(v)
and
R_{H}(\pi(v))
,
the adjacency between
v
and
w
is determined by the majority behaviour
within the corresponding colour classes, and hence the adjacency must agree.
Any disagreement can hence only occur if either

w\in R_{G}(v)
or
\pi(w)\in R_{H}(\pi(v))
. Furthermore, since colour
refinement does not distinguish
(G,S)
and
(H,S^{\prime})
, we have

|R_{H}(\pi(v))|=|R_{G}(v)|\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}n
, and hence

|\{w\in V(G)\mid vw\in E(G)\iff\pi(v)\pi(w)\notin E(H)\}|\leq|R_{G}(v)|+|R_{H}(\pi(v))|\leq\varepsilon n.

∎

In order to construct an
\varepsilon
-homogenising set for a graph
G
, we
consider the set system
\mathcal{M}_{G}\coloneqq\{M_{G}(v,w)\mid v,w\in V(G)\}
of mixed neighbourhoods.
Now, the key observation is that every
\varepsilon
-net

S
for

\mathcal{M}_{G}
is
\varepsilon
-homogenising.

Indeed, if
\gamma^{S}(v)=\gamma^{S}(w)
, then, in particular,

S\cap M_{G}(v,w)=\emptyset
, which by the definition
of an
\varepsilon
-net
implies
|M_{G}(v,w)|\leq\varepsilon n
.
Hence, with
Lemma

3.2
in mind, it only remains to bound the
VC dimension of
\mathcal{M}_{G}
. We can get the following bound as a direct
consequence of
Lemma

3.6
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
G
be a graph of VC dimension
d
.
Then
\mathcal{M}_{G}
has VC dimension at most
10d
.

Theorem 5.8
.

The
O\left(\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)
-WL
distinguishes all pairs of non-isomorphic
n
-vertex graphs
G,H
such that

G
has VC dimension at most
d
and
\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)\geq\varepsilon n^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon
.
Let
G,H
be non-isomorphic
n
-vertex graphs such that
G
has VC dimension
at most
d
and
\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)\geq\varepsilon n^{2}
.
By
Lemmas

3.2
and
5.7
, there is an
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-net
S

for the set system
\mathcal{M}_{G}
of size

O(\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\prime}})=O(\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
.
Since
S
is
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-homogenising, we conclude by

Lemma

5.3
that
(|S|+1)
-WL distinguishes
G,H
.
∎

Replacing
Lemma

3.2
by
Lemma

3.1
in the last
proof, we obtain a slightly improved bound for general
n
-vertex graphs.

Theorem 5.9
.

The
O\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log n\right)
-WL algorithm distinguishes
all pairs of non-isomorphic
n
-vertex graphs
G,H
such that

\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)\geq\varepsilon n^{2}
.

Finally, for coloured graphs of colour class size at most
s
, we can also find

\varepsilon
-homogenising sets of small size via a different argument.
In this case, we proceed inductively: if a set
S
is not

\varepsilon
-homogenising because
|M_{G}(v,w)|>\varepsilon n
for some
v,w
,
individualising
S
and
v
splits a linear number of colour classes. Since
such a split may happen only constant number of times, we get the existence
of a small
\varepsilon
-homogenising set.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
(G,\lambda_{G})
be a coloured graph such that all colour classes have size
at most
s
. Then there is an
\varepsilon
-homogenising set
S\subseteq V(G)

of size
|S|\leq\frac{s-1}{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

Let
S_{0}=\emptyset
.
Consider some
i\geq 0
for which
S_{i}
is defined, and suppose that
S_{i}
is not
\varepsilon
-homogenising
.
Then there are
v,w
such that
\gamma^{S_{i}}(v)=\gamma^{S_{i}}(w)
and

|M_{G}(v,w)|>\varepsilon n
.
We set
S_{i+1}\coloneqq S_{i}\cup\{w\}
.

Let
\ell
denote the index at which the iterative process is stopped, i.e., the set
S_{\ell}
is
\varepsilon
-homogenising.
We argue that
\ell\leq\frac{s-1}{\varepsilon}
.
Since only one element is added in each iteration, this implies the desired bound on the size of
S
.

Claim 5.11
.

Let
0\leq i<\ell
, and let
v,w
be a pair such that
\gamma^{S_{i}}(v)=\gamma^{S_{i}}(w)
.
Let
C
be a colour class of
\gamma^{S_{i}}
such that

M_{G}(v,w)\cap C\neq\emptyset
.
Then there are
x,y\in C
such that

\gamma^{S_{i+1}}(x)\neq\gamma^{S_{i+1}}(y)
.

Proof of Claim.

Let
v,w
be such a pair and
C
such a colour class.
Let
x\in M_{G}(v,w)\cap C
.
Without loss of generality, suppose that

x\in N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{G}(w)
. By the properties of colour
refinement, there is a
y\in N_{G}(w)\cap C
and hence
y\neq x
.
But this means that by individualising
w
, the vertices
x
and
y

get different colours, that is,

\gamma^{S_{i+1}}(x)\neq\gamma^{S_{i+1}}(y)
.
∎

Claim 5.12
.

For every
i\in[\ell]
, there are at least
\frac{1+i\varepsilon}{s}n
colour classes in
\gamma^{S_{i}}
.

Proof of Claim.

We prove the statement by induction on
i
. For
i=0
, since each colour class has size
at most
s
, their number is at least
n/s
.

Suppose that the statement holds for
i<\ell
.
Consider the set
S_{i}
and let
v,w
be a pair
such that
\gamma^{S_{i}}(v)=\gamma^{S_{i}}(w)
,
|M_{G}(v,w)|>\varepsilon n

and
w\in S_{i+1}\setminus S_{i}
.

Clearly, there are at least
\frac{1}{s}|M_{G}(v,w)|
colour classes
C

(of the colouring
\gamma^{S_{i}}
) such that
C\cap M_{G}(v,w)\neq\emptyset
.
Hence, by
5.11
,
individualising
w
splits
\frac{1}{s}|M_{G}(v,w)|
colour classes.
This means that
\gamma^{S_{i+1}}
has at least

\frac{1+i\varepsilon}{s}n+\frac{1}{s}|M_{G}(v,w)|\geq\frac{1+(i+1)\varepsilon}{s}n
colour classes.
∎

Since there can be at most
n
colour classes in
\gamma^{S_{\ell}}
, we conclude that
\ell\leq\frac{s-1}{\varepsilon}
as desired.
∎

Using the same arguments as before, we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.13
.

The
O(\frac{s}{\varepsilon})
-WL algorithm distinguishes all
pairs of non-isomorphic
n
-vertex graphs
G,H
of colour class size at most

s
such that
\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)\geq\varepsilon n^{2}
.

5.3
Lower bounds on the WL dimension

We complement our upper bound results for the WL algorithm with some lower
bounds, which in particular show that the dependence on
\varepsilon
on the above
results cannot be improved by much.
Our lower bounds build on existing constructions from

[
6
,
31
]
.
While the pairs of graphs constructed in
[
6
,
31
]

only have small edit distance, we can use blowups to increase their edit
distance.
Let
G=(V,E,\lambda)
be a coloured graph and
\ell\geq 1
.
We define the
\ell
-blowup
of
G
to be the graph
G^{(\ell)}
obtained
by replacing each edge of
G
by a complete bipartite graph
K_{\ell,\ell}
.
More formally,
G^{(\ell)}
has vertices

V(G^{(\ell)})=\{(v,i)\mid v\in V,i\in[\ell]\}
and edges

E(G^{(\ell)})=\{(v,i)(w,j)\mid vw\in E,i,j\in[\ell]\}
.
We equip
G^{(\ell)}
with the colouring
\lambda^{(\ell)}
defined via

\lambda^{(\ell)}(v,i)=(\lambda(v),i)
.
The following lemma states that large blowups have quadratic edit distance.

Lemma 5.14

(
[
32
]
)
.

Let
G,H
be coloured graphs and
\ell\geq 1
. Then

\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G^{(\ell)},H^{(\ell)})\geq\frac{1}{3}\cdot\ell^{2}\cdot\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)
.

Also, taking the blowup of a graph does not affect the power of the WL algorithm.

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
G,H
be coloured graphs and
\ell\geq 1
,
k\geq 2
.
Then
k
-WL distinguishes
G
and
H
if and only if
k
-WL distinguishes

G^{(\ell)}
and
H^{(\ell)}
.

Proof.

First suppose that
k
-WL does not distinguish
G
and
H
, i.e., Duplicator
has a winning strategy for
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G,H)
.
We give a winning strategy for Duplicator in
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G^{(\ell)},H^{(\ell)})
.

Let
f\colon(V(G))^{k+1}\to(V(H))^{k+1}
be the bijection chosen by
Duplicator in the initial round of
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G,H)
.
We define the bijection

f^{(\ell)}\colon(V(G^{(\ell)}))^{k+1}\to(V(H^{(\ell)}))^{k+1}
via

f^{(\ell)}((v_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(v_{k+1},j_{k+1}))=((w_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(w_{k+1},j_{k+1}))

where
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k+1})=f(v_{1},\dots,v_{k+1})
.
Duplicator initially plays the bijection
f^{(\ell)}
in the game

{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G^{(\ell)},H^{(\ell)})
.
Throughout the game, Duplicator maintains the invariant that only positions
of the form

\Big(\big((v_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(v_{k+1},j_{k+1})\big),\big((w_{1},j_{1}),\dots,(w_{k+1},j_{k+1})\big)\Big)

are reached, where
((v_{1},\dots,v_{k+1}),(w_{1},\dots,w_{k+1}))
is a winning
position in the game
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G,H)
.
Clearly, this is the case after the initial round.

We need to argue that the invariant can be maintained.
So suppose the game is in such a position, and Spoiler chooses
i\in[k+1]

in the game
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G^{(\ell)},H^{(\ell)})
.
Let
f\colon V(G)\to V(H)
be Duplicator’s response in the game

{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G,H)
if Spoiler chooses the same index
i
in the position

((v_{1},\dots,v_{k+1}),(w_{1},\dots,w_{k+1}))
.
Then, in the game
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G^{(\ell)},H^{(\ell)})
, Duplicator responds
with the bijection
f^{(\ell)}\colon V(G^{(\ell)})\to V(H^{(\ell)})
defined
via

f^{(\ell)}(v,j)=(f(v),j)

for all
v\in V(G)
and
j\in[\ell]
.
It is easy to see that Duplicator maintains the invariant no matter which
vertex is chosen by Spoiler to complete the round.

Finally, note that Duplicator can never loose if the invariant is maintained,
which completes the first direction.

Now, suppose that
k
-WL distinguishes
G
and
H
, i.e., Spoiler has a winning strategy for
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G,H)
.
Let
X_{G}\coloneqq\{(v,1)\mid v\in V(G)\}
and
X_{H}\coloneqq\{(w,1)\mid w\in V(H)\}
.
Observe that
X_{G}
and
X_{H}
are unions of corresponding color classes in
G
and
H
, respectively.
Moreover,
G^{(\ell)}[X_{G}]\cong G
and
H^{(\ell)}[X_{H}]\cong H
.
Hence, a winning strategy for Spoiler in
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G,H)
immediately translates to a winning strategy in
{\mathsf{BP}}_{k+1}(G^{(\ell)},H^{(\ell)})
, by identifying a vertex
v
in
G
or
H
with the vertex
(v,1)
in
G^{(\ell)}
or
H^{(\ell)}
, respectively.
Observe that, due the coloring of vertices, Spoiler immediately wins whenever Duplicator chooses a bijection that does not bijectively map vertices from
W_{G}
to
W_{H}
.
∎

Now, we obtain the following using the standard CFI construction from

[
6
]
.

Theorem 5.16
.

For every
k\geq 1
,
n\geq k
there are non-isomorphic (coloured) graphs

G,H
such that

•

|V(G)|=|V(H)|=\Theta(n)
,

•

k
-WL does not distinguish
G,H
,

•

\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{k^{2}}
,

•

G
and
H
have VC dimension at most
3
, and

•

each colour class in
G
and
H
has size at most
4
.

Proof.

Let
k\geq 1
.
By
[
6
]
, there are non-isomorphic 3-regular coloured graphs

G^{\prime},H^{\prime}
of size
\Theta(k)
, with colour class size at most
4
, that are
not distinguished by
k
-WL.
In particular,
\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})\geq 1
.

Let
n\geq k
and set
\ell\coloneqq\left\lceil 3n/k\right\rceil
.
Consider the graphs
G\coloneqq(G^{\prime})^{(\ell)}
and

H\coloneqq(H^{\prime})^{(\ell)}
.
Then
|V(G)|=|V(H)|=\Theta(n)
, and
k
-WL does not distinguish

G
and
H
by
Lemma

5.15
.
Also, by
Lemma

5.14
, we have that

\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G,H)\geq\frac{1}{3}\cdot\ell^{2}\cdot\delta_{\mathsf{edit}}(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})\geq\frac{n^{2}}{k^{2}}.

Let
S\subseteq V(G)
be a set shattered by
\mathcal{N}_{G}
. Clearly, we
cannot have
(v,i),(v,j)\in S
for some
v\in V(G)

and
i\neq j\in[n]
, since the two vertices have exactly the same
neighbours.
Hence the set

S^{\prime}=\{v\in V(G^{\prime})\mid(v,i)\in V(G)\text{ for some }i\in[n]\}
has the
same cardinality as

.
Furthermore, since
S
is shattered by
\mathcal{N}_{G}
, the set
S^{\prime}
is
shattered in
\mathcal{N}_{G^{\prime}}
.
However, since every vertex in

, we conclude that

|S|=|S^{\prime}|\leq 3
.
The same reasoning holds true for
H
.

Lastly, the colour class size of
G^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
is at most

, and the

n
-th blowup does not increase the colour class sizes.
∎

In particular, the theorem implies that

-WL cannot
distinguish between graphs of edit distance

, even for graphs
of constant VC dimension and constant colour class size.

by instead using a construction

For every

6
Concluding Remarks

We identified VC dimension as a key parameter governing the runtime
of additive approximation algorithms for the graph edit distance problem
and the quadratic assigment problem: for a fixed approximation error

\varepsilon
the runtime is
n^{O_{\varepsilon}(d)}
, where
d
is
an appropriately defined VC dimension of the input instance.
Though we did not discuss them here except briefly in
Remark
4.8
, similar results can be obtained
for related assignement problems (see
[
1
]
).

To solve the robust graph isomorphism problem, which may be viewed as
a special case of graph edit distance, we can even use the

O_{\varepsilon}(d)
-dimensional WL algorithm, which implies that for all
graphs the
O_{\varepsilon}(\log n)
-dimensional WL algorithm suffices to
solve
\varepsilon
-
\mathsf{GI}
, whereas exact graph isomorphism
requires WL dimension
\Omega(n)
.

The question whether for every
\varepsilon
there is a polynomial time
algorithm solving
\varepsilon
-
{\mathsf{GI}}
remains open. We believe this
a natural and interesting question that has received surprisingly
little attention. Related to this is the question whether for all

\varepsilon
there is a
k
(not depending on the order
n
of the input
graphs or their VC dimension
d
) such that
k
-WL solves

\varepsilon
-
\mathsf{GI}
. Note that our lower bound says that for every
k
there
is an
\varepsilon
such that
k
-WL does not solve
\varepsilon
-
\mathsf{GI}
.

Let us consider the following generalisation of the assignment problem,
called

\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{A}~

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0ptk\in[K],

,

.

(

]
)
.
