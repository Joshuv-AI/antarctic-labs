Review of the existing maximum residue levels for clofentezine according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Sofia Batista Leite, Marco Binaglia, Luis Carrasco Cabrera, Anna Federica Castoldi, Arianna Chiusolo, Mathilde Colas, Federica Crivellente, Luna Greco, Frederique Istace, Anna Lanzoni, Jochem Louisse, Iris Mangas, Stefanie Nave, Martina Panzarea, Juan Manuel Parra Morte, Hermine Reich, Silvia Ruocco, Alessia Pia Scarlato, Manuela Tiramani, Elena Zioga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA has reviewed the maximum residue levels (MRLs) currently established at European level for the pesticide active substance clofentezine. Although this active substance is no longer authorised within the European Union, codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) were established by the Codex Alimentarius Commission and import tolerances were reported by Member States and the UK (including the supporting residues data). Based on the available data, EFSA assessed the CXLs and the import tolerances reported, and a consumer risk assessment was carried out. Although no risk to consumers was identified, all CXLs and import tolerances were found to be supported by inadequate data. Hence, the consumer risk assessment is considered indicative only and further consideration by risk managers is needed.Keywords: acaricide, clofentezine, consumer risk assessment, MRL review, Regulation (EC) No 396/2005

SUMMARY

Clofentezine was approved for use as pesticide and included in Annex I to Council Directive 91/414/EEC on 1 January 2009 by Commission Directive 2008/69/EC. Subsequently, it was deemed to be approved under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, in accordance with Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 540/2011, as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 541/2011.

In 2021, EFSA conducted a peer review of clofentezine, as required by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012. Based on the EFSA conclusion on the peer review, the approval of clofentezine was not renewed, by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/2456.

Since the active substance was originally approved after Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 entered into force on 2 September 2008, the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) must provide a reasoned opinion on the review of the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for the active substance as required by Article 12(1) of the Regulation.

As the basis for the MRL review, on 16 September 2024 EFSA initiated the collection of data for clofentezine. In a first step, Member States and the United Kingdom (UK) were invited to submit, by 14 October 2024, the Good Agricultural Practices (GAPs) in non‐EU countries for which import tolerances are authorised. The data were submitted in the format of specific GAP forms, allowing the designated rapporteur Member State Spain to identify the critical GAPs in the format of a GAP overview file. Subsequently, Member States and the UK were requested to provide residue data supporting the critical GAPs, within a period of 1 month, by 27 February 2025.

Under the same timeframe, the EU Reference Laboratories for Pesticides Residues (EURLs) were asked to provide information on the analytical methods for enforcement used in routine analysis. Based on all the data submitted by Member States, the UK and the EURLs, EFSA asked the RMS to complete the Pesticide Residue Overview File (PROFile) and to prepare a supporting evaluation report. The Evaluation Report and the PROFile were provided by the RMS to EFSA respectively on 30 April 2025 and 7 July 2025. Subsequently, EFSA performed the completeness check of these documents with the RMS. The outcome of this exercise, including the clarifications provided by the RMS, was compiled in the completeness check report.

Furthermore, for the purpose of this MRL review, an Evaluation Report, including the toxicological assessment of metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN), was submitted by the co‐rapporteur Member State (Co‐RMS) Netherlands (NL) in August 2025 and discussed in a pesticide peer review experts' meeting (TC 190) held in November 2025.

Based on the information provided by the RMS, the Co‐RMS, Member States, the UK and the EURLs, and taking into account the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 as well as the MRLs established by the Codex Alimentarius Commission (CAC), EFSA prepared a draft reasoned opinion in January 2026. The draft was circulated to Member States and the EURLs for consultation via written procedure. Comments received by 13 February 2026 were discussed during a pesticide peer review experts' meeting (TC 208), which took place in April 2026, and the outcome of that meeting was considered during the finalisation of this reasoned opinion. The following conclusions are derived.

The metabolism of clofentezine in plants was investigated in primary crops. According to the results of the metabolism studies, the residue definition for enforcement can be proposed as clofentezine (applicable to fruit and leafy crops following foliar application). As regards to risk assessment, based on the data available, two residue definitions were set separately, namely, RD‐RA1: clofentezine and RD‐RA2: 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN). A specific residue definition for rotational crops is not deemed necessary considering that clofentezine is no longer authorised within the European Union. The residue definition for processed commodities remains open.

Analytical methods are available for high‐water and high‐acid content, and for dry and high‐oil matrices, but the extraction efficiency in high‐water and high‐acid content commodities needs to be demonstrated (data gap). According to the EURLs, clofentezine can be monitored in high‐water content, high‐acid content, dry and high‐fat content commodities and in black tea with an limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg. For hops, no specific analytical method was submitted under the present review and is in principle still required. Nevertheless, it is highlighted for further consideration by risk managers that a liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) method was considered sufficiently validated by the JMPR for the enforcement of clofentezine at the LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg.

Available residue trials data were considered sufficient to derive tentative MRL proposals as well as risk assessment values (i.e. highest residue (HR) and supervised trials median residue (STMR)) for all commodities under evaluation.

Clofentezine is authorised for use on apples that might be fed to livestock. Since the calculated dietary burdens for all groups of livestock were found to be below the trigger value of 0.1 mg/kg dry matter (DM), further investigation of residues as well as the setting of MRLs in commodities of animal origin is unnecessary.

Although not required, the metabolism of clofentezine residues in livestock was investigated in lactating ruminants (cows and goats) and laying hens. According to the results of these studies, the residue definition for enforcement in livestock commodities was provisionally proposed as sum of clofentezine and 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine, expressed as clofentezine, applicable for all livestock animals or alternatively, as 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine applicable for ruminants and as clofentezine applicable for poultry. The residue definition for risk assessment was defined as the sum of clofentezine and 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine, expressed as clofentezine. It noted that, as all MRLs for livestock are proposed at the LOQ of the livestock feeding studies assessed by the JMPR and analysing for the sum of clofentezine, and all metabolites containing the 2‐chlorobenzoyl moiety, expressed as clofentezine, the MRL proposals are expected to be valid for both residue definitions.

An analytical method for the enforcement of clofentezine and its metabolite 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine, at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for each compound (corresponding to a combined LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg) is available for all animal commodities. This LOQ is achievable by the EURLs during routine analysis.

Pending the submission of studies addressing the general toxicity and the magnitude of metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) in the crops under assessment, only an indicative risk assessment limited to parent clofentezine (for plants) and parent and metabolite 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine (for livestock) could be performed.

Chronic consumer exposure resulting from the authorised uses reported in the framework of this review was calculated using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo. The highest chronic exposure represented 4% of the acceptable daily intake (ADI) (NL toddler). Acute exposure calculations were not carried out because an acute reference dose (ARfD) was not deemed necessary for this active substance.

Apart from the MRLs evaluated in the framework of this review, internationally recommended CXLs have also been established for clofentezine. Additional calculations of the consumer exposure, considering these CXLs, were therefore carried out and the highest chronic exposure represented 30% of the ADI (NL toddler).

BACKGROUND

Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 1 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Regulation’) establishes the rules governing the setting and the review of pesticide maximum residue levels (MRLs) at European level. Article 12(1) of that Regulation stipulates that the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) shall provide within 12 months from the date of the inclusion or non‐inclusion of an active substance in Annex I to Council Directive 91/414/EEC 2 a reasoned opinion on the review of the existing MRLs for that active substance.

Clofentezine was approved for use as pesticide and included in Annex I to Council Directive 91/414/EEC on 1 January 2009 by means of Commission Directive 2008/69/EC. 3 Subsequently it was deemed to be approved under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, 4 in accordance with Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 540/2011, 5 as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 541/2011. 6

In 2021, EFSA initiated the peer review process for the renewal of the approval of clofentezine in accordance with Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012. 7 The EFSA conclusion on the pesticide risk assessment of clofentezine was approved on 28 July 2021 (EFSA, 2021).

EFSA concluded that clofentezine meets the cut‐off criteria for non‐approval laid down in Annex II, point 3.6.5, of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) No 2018/605, as it has been identified as an endocrine disruptor that may cause adverse effects in humans.

Therefore, the approval of clofentezine was not renewed by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/2456. 8

Subsequently, as required by Article 12(1) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA initiated the review of all existing MRLs for that active substance.

According to the legal provisions, EFSA shall base its reasoned opinion on the relevant assessment report prepared under Council Directive 91/414/EEC (repealed by Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009). However, it should be noted that in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, only a few representative uses are evaluated, whereas MRLs set out in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 should accommodate all uses authorised within the European Union (EU) and uses authorised in third countries that have a significant impact on international trade. The information included in the assessment report prepared under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 is therefore insufficient for the assessment of all existing MRLs for a given active substance.

To gain an overview of the pesticide residue data that have been considered for the setting of the existing MRLs, EFSA developed the Pesticide Residue Overview File (PROFile). The PROFile is an inventory of all pesticide residue data relevant to the risk assessment and MRL setting for a given active substance. This includes data on:

the nature and magnitude of residues in primary crops;

the nature and magnitude of residues in primary crops;

the nature and magnitude of residues in processed commodities;

the nature and magnitude of residues in processed commodities;

the nature and magnitude of residues in rotational crops;

the nature and magnitude of residues in rotational crops;

the nature and magnitude of residues in livestock commodities;

the nature and magnitude of residues in livestock commodities;

the analytical methods for enforcement of the proposed MRLs.

the analytical methods for enforcement of the proposed MRLs.

As the basis for the MRL review, on 16 September 2024 EFSA initiated the collection of data for clofentezine. As a first step, Member States and the UK 9 were invited to submit by 14 October 2024 Good Agricultural Practices (GAPs) in non‐EU countries for which import tolerances (IT) are authorised in the format of specific GAP forms. In the framework of this consultation 11 Member States provided feedback. Moreover, the UK submitted GAPs on national uses that are expected to remain authorised in the UK until 2030 and therefore will require import tolerances. Based on the GAP data submitted, the designated RMS Spain was asked to identify the critical GAPs to be further considered in the assessment, in the format of a specific GAP overview file. Subsequently, in a second step, Member States and the UK were requested to provide residue data supporting the critical GAPs by 27 February 2025. Under the same timeframe, the EU Reference Laboratories for Pesticides Residues (EURLs) were asked to provide information on the analytical methods for enforcement used in routine analysis.

Based on all the data submitted by Member States, the UK and the EURLs, EFSA asked Spain to complete the PROFile and to prepare a supporting evaluation report. The Evaluation Report and the PROFile were provided by the RMS to EFSA respectively on 30 April 2025 and 7 July 2025. Subsequently, EFSA performed the completeness check of these documents with the RMS. The outcome of this exercise including the clarifications provided by the RMS, was compiled in the completeness check report.

Furthermore, for the purpose of this MRL review, an additional Evaluation Report including the toxicological assessment of the metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) was submitted by the co‐rapporteur Member State (Co‐RMS) Netherlands (NL) on 4 August 2025 and revised in October 2025. The results of this assessment were discussed in a pesticide peer review experts' meeting held on 28 November 2025 (TC 190).

Considering all available information and considering the MRLs established by the Codex Alimentarius Commission (CAC) (i.e., codex maximum residue limit; CXLs), EFSA prepared in January 2026 a draft reasoned opinion, which was circulated to Member States and the EURLs for commenting via a written procedure. All comments received by 13 February 2026 were evaluated by EFSA. As further discussion was required to confirm that the Health Based Guidance Values (HBGVs) derived by EFSA during the peer review in 2021 were sufficiently protective against the endocrine mediated changes observed in the dataset, a meeting with Member State experts took place in April 2026. The outcome of the consultation (TC 208) was considered by EFSA during the finalisation of the reasoned opinion (EFSA, 2026b).

The evaluation reports submitted by the RMS (Spain, 2025) and the co‐RMS (Netherlands, 2025) together with the information provided by Member States and the UK during the collection of data, as well as the EURLs report on analytical methods (EURLs, 2025) are considered as main supporting documents to this reasoned opinion and, are therefore made publicly available.

In addition, the completeness check report (EFSA, 2025a), the Member States consultation report (EFSA, 2026a) and the Pesticides Peer Review experts' teleconference Reports (TC 190 and TC 208) (EFSA, 2025b, 2026b) also serve as supporting documents to this reasoned opinion. These reports are developed to address all issues raised during the review, from the initial completeness check to the reasoned opinion. Furthermore, the exposure calculations for all crops reported in the framework of this review performed using the EFSA Pesticide Residue Intake Model (PRIMo) and the PROFile as well as the GAP overview file listing all authorised uses and import tolerances are key supporting documents and made publicly available as background documents to this reasoned opinion. 10 A screenshot of the report sheet of the PRIMo is presented in Appendix C.

MAMMALIAN TOXICOLOGY

During the peer review on clofentezine (EFSA, 2021), EFSA identified a data gap for the assessment of aneugenicity and the general toxicity of metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN). Under the current MRL assessment a new in vivo micronucleus (MN) test was made available. The in vivo MN test, addressing aneugenicity and clastogenicity, gave negative results (see Appendix B.1). The outcome of the assessment was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Teleconference Experts' Meeting 190 (EFSA, 2025b). The experts agreed that, based on the weight of evidence, the metabolite is considered unlikely to be genotoxic and the data gap for aneugenicity identified during the peer review was addressed. Nevertheless, data addressing the general toxicity of 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) are still missing and therefore it was not possible to derive toxicological reference values for this metabolite.

Furthermore, during the Member States' consultation on the draft reasoned opinion, two Member States questioned whether the HBGVs derived by EFSA during the 2021 peer review were sufficiently protective regarding endocrine disruption. Since this was not specifically discussed in the afore‐mentioned peer review, further discussion was considered necessary and an expert meeting (Pesticides Peer Review Teleconference Experts' Meeting 208; EFSA, 2026b) was held in April 2026.

The experts agreed that, based on a detailed evaluation of the dataset, the current HBGVs (EFSA, 2021) are confirmed to be sufficiently protective against endocrine disruption. Namely, the critical no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) used to set the current ADI (EFSA, 2021) is protective versus T‐mediated adverse effects (including histopathological thyroid effects and changes in thyroid hormone levels), as supported by benchmark dose (BMD) analysis. The uncertainty related to the lack of sufficient information to conclude on EAS modalities (EFSA, 2021) was considered not to impact on the current ADI, namely no adverse effects indicating a pattern of EAS adversity were observed in the available dataset.

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

The metabolism of clofentezine was investigated after application of drops directly over the fruits (apple, peach and grape) (Spain, 2019) and assessed in the framework of the peer review (EFSA, 2021). In these studies, clofentezine was radiolabelled in the N‐heterocyclic ring of the molecule.

The metabolic pattern was shown to be similar in all fruit crops since the major component identified was clofentezine, representing up to 90% of the total radioactive residues (TRR) while the metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) was present below 10% TRR in all crops and all characterised fractions accounted for low levels of total residues (EFSA, 2021). The bound residue in fruit crops was shown to arise from the binding of clofentezine or its metabolites to insoluble plant components and largely restricted to the peel fraction (EFSA, 2021).

A new leafy crop metabolism study using [tetrazine‐3‐14C] clofentezine was submitted in the framework of the present MRL review (Spain, 2025). After foliar application of 0.28 kg a.s./ha on lettuce at BBCH 39 and pre‐harvest interval (PHI) 21, the only component of the residue in leafy crop (lettuce) was clofentezine (90.8% TRR in mature lettuce). The metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) was not detected. It should be noted that this study was not evaluated during the peer review for the renewal. The metabolic pathway of clofentezine was similar in fruit and leafy crops.

Nature of residues in rotational crops

Rotational crop studies are not triggered since clofentezine is no longer authorised for use within Europe. However, a guideline‐compliant metabolism study for rotational crops has previously been evaluated during the peer review (Spain, 2019). The confined rotational crop study demonstrated a metabolic pattern like that observed in primary crops, with clofentezine being the predominant compound and 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) metabolite identified in the edible parts.

Nature of residues in processed commodities

A study investigating the nature of residues in processed commodities was assessed during the peer review (EFSA, 2021; Spain, 2019). This experiment simulating representative hydrolytic conditions for pasteurisation (20 min at 90°C, pH 4), boiling/brewing/baking (60 min at 100°C, pH 5) and sterilisation (20 min at 120°C, pH 6), showed clofentezine being stable under pasteurisation conditions but slightly degrading to hydrazide‐hydrazone (HH; 12.4% TRR) under baking, brewing, boiling conditions and completely decomposing to form HH (77.6% TRR), 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN; 4.9% TRR) and 2‐chlorobenzamide (2‐CBZ; 17.0% TRR) under sterilisation conditions (EFSA, 2021).

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

During the peer review for the renewal, LC–MS/MS and gas chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (GC–MS/MS) methods were assessed (EFSA, 2021; Spain, 2019) and found to be adequately validated to enforce clofentezine in high‐water, high‐acid, high‐oil content and dry matrices at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg (EFSA, 2021; Spain, 2019). Independent laboratory validations are available for both methods.

It should be highlighted that extraction conditions in enforcement methods for high‐water and high‐acid content commodities were not the same as those for high‐oil content and dry commodities and under the peer review a data gap was set for the extraction efficiency of the procedures used in the monitoring methods for plant commodities (EFSA, 2021). For this reason, a new study was provided under this review and summarised in the evaluation report (Spain, 2025). According to the technical guideline SANTE/2017/10632 Rev. 5 (European Commission, 2023b), demonstration of the extraction efficiency can only be assessed with samples bearing incurred residues (from metabolism studies or residue trials). Hence, EFSA considers the new study performed spiking fortified pepper fruit samples not acceptable to demonstrate extraction efficiency. Therefore, it is concluded that the data gap for the extraction efficiency is still open.

Based on data within the EURLs (2025), showing successful validation in various commodities of plant origin, clofentezine can be monitored in high‐water content, high‐acid content, dry and high‐fat content commodities and in black tea (specific matrix) with an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. In high‐water content, high‐acid content and dry commodities even lower levels, down to 0.002 mg/kg, were successfully validated (high‐fat content commodities down to 0.005 mg/kg). For hops, no specific analytical method was submitted under the present review (data gap). Nevertheless, a LC–MS/MS method was considered sufficiently validated by the JMPR for the enforcement of clofentezine and 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) at the LOQs of 0.02 and 0.05 mg/kg, respectively (FAO, 2021).

Stability of residues in plants

The storage stability of clofentezine and its metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) in high‐water, high‐oil and high‐acid content matrices was investigated in the new study submitted under this review (Spain, 2025). This study demonstrated storage stability for clofentezine and its metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) for 3–24 months in three main plant matrix groups, when stored at ≤ −18°C.

Specifically, in high‐water content matrices, the available study demonstrated storage stability of clofentezine for a period of 12 (apples), 14 (tomatoes) and 6 (cucumber) months when stored at ≤ −18°C. For the same matrices and storage conditions, the storage stability of the metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) was reported for up to 24 months. In high‐oil content matrices, the available studies demonstrated storage stability of clofentezine for a period of 3 (hazelnut and soya beans) months and of the metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) for a period of 9 months when stored at ≤ −18°C. In high‐acid content matrices, the available studies demonstrated storage stability of clofentezine for a period of 9 (orange) and 24 (grapes) months, and of the metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) for a period of 24 months when stored at ≤ −18°C.

Proposed residue definitions

As the parent compound clofentezine was found to be a sufficient marker in fruits and fruiting vegetables and in leafy crops following foliar application, the residue definition for enforcement is proposed as clofentezine only.

An analytical method for the enforcement of the proposed residue definition at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg in all relevant matrices is available (EFSA, 2021; Spain, 2025), but extraction efficiency needs to be demonstrated (data gap). According to the EURLs, this LOQ can be enforced during routine analysis (EURLs, 2025).

Based on the results of the available metabolism studies and residue trials where metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) was found at levels above the LOQ in raspberries (see Appendix B.2.2.1), and considering the available toxicological data for metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN), showing that it is more acutely toxic than parent (EFSA, 2021), in the present review the following two residue definitions are tentatively proposed for risk assessment:

RD‐RA1: clofentezine.

RD‐RA1: clofentezine.

RD‐RA2: 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN).

RD‐RA2: 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN).

The above proposed residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment are limited to fruit and leafy crops, following foliar applications. It is underlined that, based on the data available, metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) is not expected to be present in leafy crops.

The assessment for aneugenicity of the metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) performed under the present review (EFSA, 2025b) gave negative results, but the data gap on the general toxicity of this metabolite is still open. Therefore, additional data is still needed to support its risk assessment.

Under hydrolysis conditions simulating baking, brewing, boiling and sterilisation conditions, clofentezine is degraded to 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN), hydrazide‐hydrazone (AE C593600 or HH) and 2‐chlorobenzamide (2‐CBZ) (EFSA, 2021). Although, processing studies on apples suggest that hydrazide‐hydrazone (AE C593600 or HH) and 2‐chlorobenzamide (2‐CBZ) are not formed under real processing conditions, additional processing studies analysing for the degradation products identified in the hydrolysis studies are still missing for the other commodities under assessment to confirm this conclusion. Therefore, the residue definition for risk assessment for processed commodities is different to that of raw commodities and remains open, since there are data gaps regarding the magnitude and the toxicological profiles (aneugenicity and/or general toxicity assessments) of these three compounds.

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

To assess the magnitude of clofentezine residues resulting from the reported GAPs, EFSA considered all residue trials reported by the RMS in its evaluation report (Spain, 2025) as well as the residue trials evaluated in the framework of the peer review (Spain, 2019). All residue trial samples considered in this framework were stored in compliance with the conditions for which storage stability of residues was demonstrated. Decline of residues during storage of the trial samples is therefore not expected.

The number of residue trials and extrapolations were evaluated in accordance with the European guidelines on comparability, extrapolation, group tolerances and data requirements for setting MRLs (European Commission, 2023b).

Magnitude of residues in rotational crops

Rotational crops studies are not triggered since clofentezine is no longer authorised within Europe (Appendix B.2.2.2).

Magnitude of residues in processed commodities

The effect of industrial processing and/or household preparation was assessed during the peer review for renewal in studies conducted on apples and processing factors could be derived for apple (juice, dry and wet pomace, and sauce) (EFSA, 2021; Spain, 2019). An overview of all available processing studies is available in Appendix B.2.2.3. In the processing studies on apples, hydrazide‐hydrazone (AE C593600 or HH) and 2‐chlorobenzamide (2‐CBZ) were analysed and not detected, suggesting that they are not formed under real processing conditions. Nevertheless, the derived processing factors are tentative only since the residue definition for risk assessment of processed commodities is still open (see Section 2.1.6).

Proposed MRLs

The available data is considered sufficient to derive MRL proposals as well as risk assessment values (i.e. HR and STMR) for all commodities under evaluation. It should be noted however that due to the data gaps identified in the previous sections (additional trials, extraction efficiency, additional data addressing the magnitude and the toxicological profiles of 2‐CNB, H‐H and 2‐CBZ) all proposed MRLs should be considered tentative only.

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK

Clofentezine is authorised for use on apples that might be fed to livestock. Livestock dietary burden calculations were therefore performed for different groups of livestock according to OECD guidance (OECD, 2013), which has now also been agreed upon at European level. The input values for all relevant commodities are summarised in Appendix D. Since the calculated dietary burdens for all groups of livestock were found to be below the trigger value of 0.004 mg/kg dry matter (DM), further investigation of residues as well as the setting of MRLs in commodities of animal origin is unnecessary.

Although not required, the metabolism of clofentezine residues in livestock was investigated in lactating ruminants (cows and goats) and laying hens (Spain, 2019). These studies were assessed in the framework of the peer review (EFSA, 2021) and are considered for completeness. In all studies radiolabelled [tetrazine‐14C] clofentezine was used. The metabolic profile in the tested livestock species was similar with 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine (4‐OH clofentezine) as major residue in ruminant (no parent recovered in any matrix) and in poultry clofentezine and to a lower extent 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine.

Based on these studies, according to the peer review, the residue definition for monitoring in food of animal origin was defined as sum of clofentezine and 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine, expressed as clofentezine, applicable for all livestock or, alternatively, as 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine applicable for ruminants and as clofentezine applicable for poultry (EFSA, 2021). However, since several shortcomings were identified in the studies (EFSA, 2021), the residue definition for animal commodities was considered tentative only.

According to the peer review, sufficiently validated analytical methods are available for the determination of clofentezine and its metabolite 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine in milk, meat, egg, fat, liver and kidney at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for each compound and an independent laboratory validation (ILV) is also available (EFSA, 2021). It is noted that the analytical method for liver (applicable for liver and kidney), which was assessed in the peer review, is not acceptable anymore according to SANTE/2020/12830 rev. 2 (European Commission, 2021) due to the use of dichloromethane. Nevertheless, since no significant residues are expected in livestock based on the uses assessed under the present review and considering that alternative analytical methods are available to the EURLs for the enforcement of both clofentezine and its metabolite 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine in all animal commodities, at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for each compound (corresponding to a combined LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg) (EURLs, 2025), an additional method is not required.

For risk assessment, parent and 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine are toxicologically relevant and thus should be considered in the consumer exposure. The metabolite 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine is encountered in the rat metabolism and was considered covered by the toxicological profile of the parent compound. Therefore, the residue definition for risk assessment was defined as the sum of clofentezine and 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine, expressed as clofentezine (for ruminants and provisionally for poultry) (EFSA, 2021).

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

In the framework of this review, only the uses of clofentezine reported by the RMS in Appendix A were considered; however, the use of clofentezine was previously also assessed by the JMPR (FAO, 2007). The CXLs, resulting from the assessment by JMPR and adopted by the CAC, are now international recommendations that need to be considered by European risk managers when establishing MRLs. To facilitate consideration of these CXLs by risk managers, the consumer exposure was calculated both with and without consideration of the existing CXLs. Pending the submission of studies addressing the general toxicity and the magnitude of metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) in the crops under assessment, only an indicative risk assessment limited to parent clofentezine (for plants) and parent and metabolite 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine (for livestock) could be performed.

Consumer risk assessment without consideration of the existing CXLs

Chronic exposure calculations for all crops reported in the framework of this review were performed using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018, 2019). Input values for the exposure calculations were derived in compliance with the decision tree reported in Appendix E. Hence, for those commodities where a tentative MRL could be derived by EFSA in the framework of this review, input values were derived according to the internationally agreed methodologies (FAO, 2016, 2021). All input values included in the exposure calculations are summarised in Appendix D. Acute exposure calculations were not carried out because an acute reference dose (ARfD) was not deemed necessary for this active substance, and general toxicity data was not available for metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN).

The exposures calculated were compared with the ADI for clofentezine, derived by EFSA (EFSA, 2021). The highest chronic exposure was calculated for NL toddler, representing 4% of the ADI. Although major uncertainties remain due to the data gaps identified in the previous sections, particularly the general toxicity of the metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN), these calculations indicate that the uses assessed under this review result in a consumer exposure lower than the ADI for clofentezine.

Consumer risk assessment with consideration of the existing CXLs

To include the CXLs in the calculations of the consumer exposure, CXLs were compared with the EU MRL proposals in compliance with Appendix E and all data relevant to the consumer exposure assessment were collected from JMPR evaluations. An overview of the input values used for this exposure calculation is also provided in Appendix D. Currently, the residue definition derived for livestock during the peer review (i.e. sum of clofentezine and 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine, expressed as clofentezine) (EFSA, 2021), is in principle not compatible with the residue definition of the JMPR (i.e. sum of clofentezine, and all metabolites containing the 2‐chlorobenzoyl moiety, expressed as clofentezine) (FAO, 2021). Nevertheless, since the CXLs for livestock are all set at the LOQ of the livestock feeding studies, the residue definitions can be considered comparable. Hence, the CXLs established for livestock were considered in the current assessment.

In the 53rd session of the Codex Committee on Pesticide Residues (CCPR, 2022) the EU expressed a reservation on the CXL for hops, since the peer review for the renewal was on‐going. Considering that the peer review has been meanwhile finalised, the reservation is not valid any longer and a CXL for hops was included in the assessment.

No information on the levels of the metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) is available in the trials assessed by JMPR. However, this data gap is not relevant for hops, as the available metabolism studies on leafy crops indicate that this metabolite is not expected to occur in this commodity. Likewise, no information on the levels of H‐H and 2‐CBZ is reported by JMPR. While these processing metabolites might be relevant for the risk assessment of the CXLs for commodities liable to undergo baking, brewing/boiling and sterilisation in line with the current guidance (OECD, 2007h, 2008b), this data gap is not relevant for apples, since the available processing studies demonstrated that these processing metabolites did not form.

Chronic exposure calculations were also performed using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo and the exposures calculated were compared with the ADI derived for clofentezine. The highest chronic exposure was calculated for NL toddler, representing 30% of the ADI. Based on these indicative calculations, although major uncertainties remain due to the data gaps identified in the previous sections, in particular the general toxicity of the metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN), EFSA concludes that the CXLs do not lead to an exceedance of the ADI for clofentezine.

CONCLUSIONS

The metabolism of clofentezine in plants was investigated in primary crops. According to the results of the metabolism studies, the residue definition for enforcement can be proposed as clofentezine (applicable to fruit and leafy crops following foliar application). As regards to risk assessment, based on the data available, two residue definitions were set separately, namely, RD‐RA1: clofentezine and RD‐RA2: 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN). A specific residue definition for rotational crops is not deemed necessary considering that clofentezine is no longer authorised within the European Union. The residue definition for processed commodities remains open.

Analytical methods are available for high‐water and high‐acid content, and for dry and high‐oil matrices, but the extraction efficiency in high‐water and high‐acid content commodities needs to be demonstrated (data gap). According to the EURLs, clofentezine can be monitored in high water content, high‐acid content, dry and high‐fat content commodities and in black tea with an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. For hops, no specific analytical method was submitted under the present review and is in principle still required. Nevertheless, it is highlighted for further consideration by risk managers that a LC–MS/MS method was considered sufficiently validated by the JMPR for the enforcement of clofentezine at the LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg.

Available residue trials data were considered sufficient to derive tentative MRL proposals as well as risk assessment values (i.e. HR and STMR) for all commodities under evaluation.

Clofentezine is authorised for use on apples that might be fed to livestock. Since the calculated dietary burdens for all groups of livestock were found to be below the trigger value of 0.1 mg/kg DM, further investigation of residues as well as the setting of MRLs in commodities of animal origin is unnecessary.

Although not required, the metabolism of clofentezine residues in livestock was investigated in lactating ruminants (cows and goats) and laying hens. According to the results of these studies, the residue definition for enforcement in livestock commodities was provisionally proposed as sum of clofentezine and 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine, expressed as clofentezine, applicable for all livestock animals or alternatively, as 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine applicable for ruminants and as clofentezine applicable for poultry. The residue definition for risk assessment was defined as the sum of clofentezine and 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine, expressed as clofentezine. It noted that, as all MRLs for livestock are proposed at the LOQ of the livestock feeding studies assessed by the JMPR and analysing for the sum of clofentezine, and all metabolites containing the 2‐chlorobenzoyl moiety, expressed as clofentezine, the MRL proposals are expected to be valid for both residue definitions.

An analytical method for the enforcement of clofentezine and its metabolite 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine, at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for each compound (corresponding to a combined LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg) is available for all animal commodities. This LOQ is achievable by the EURLs during routine analysis.

Pending the submission of studies addressing the general toxicity and the magnitude of metabolite 2‐chlorobenzonitrile (2‐CBN) in the crops under assessment, only an indicative risk assessment limited to parent clofentezine (for plants) and parent and metabolite 4‐hydroxy‐clofentezine (for livestock) could be performed.

Chronic consumer exposure resulting from the authorised uses reported in the framework of this review was calculated using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo. The highest chronic exposure represented 4% of the ADI (NL toddler). Acute exposure calculations were not carried out because an ARfD was not deemed necessary for this active substance.

Apart from the MRLs evaluated in the framework of this review, internationally recommended CXLs have also been established for clofentezine. Additional calculations of the consumer exposure, considering these CXLs, were therefore carried out and the highest chronic exposure represented 30% of the ADI (NL toddler).
