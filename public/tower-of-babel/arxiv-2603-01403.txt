Koopman-based Estimation of Lyapunov Functions: Theory on a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space
Wentao Tang \aref ncsu, Xiuzhen Ye \aref ncsu

Abstract
Koopman operator provides a general linear description of nonlinear systems, whose estimation from data (via extended dynamic mode decomposition) has been extensively studied. However, the elusiveness between the Koopman spectrum and the stability of equilibrium point poses a challenge to utilizing the Koopman operator for stability analysis , which further hinders the construction of a universal theory of Koopman-based control . In our prior work, we defined the Koopman operator on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) using a linear–radial product kernel, and proved that the Koopman spectrum is confined in the unit disk of the complex plane when the origin is an asymptotically stable equilibrium point. Building on this fundamental spectrum–stability relation, here we consider the problem of Koopman operator-based Lyapunov function estimation with a given decay rate function. The decay rate function and the Lyapunov function are both specified by positive operators on the RKHS and are related by an operator algebraic Lyapunov equation (ALE), whose solution exists uniquely. The error bound of such a Lyapunov function estimate, obtained via kernel extended dynamic mode decomposition (kEDMD), are established based on statistical learning theory and verified by a numerical study.

Introduction

Nonlinear systems are ubiquitous in engineering applications, which, however, often lack accurate models from first principles. The concept of
Koopman operator
, originated in the studies of statistical physics, has become a standard tool of nonlinear systems theory
[
1
]
.
Considering the evolution of state-dependent functions, the Koopman operator maps any such function to its composition with the flow of the dynamics. Whenever the function space in consideration remains invariant upon this composition, the Koopman operator is linear, and thus conceptually provides a “global linearization” of the nonlinear system.
As a linear operator, the Koopman operator can be
learned from data
in a computationally efficient convex optimization formulation
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. This brings hope for a generic “data-driven” control strategy for nonlinear systems
[
5
]
.

To the end of data-driven Koopman modeling, the
proper choice of a function space
, which must be
invariant
under the action of Koopman operator, becomes an issue not only for theoretical rigor but also for its computation and application in control.
For example, in
[
6
]
, based on the idea that Koopman operator converts a controlled nonlinear system into a high-dimensional bilinear system, a linear robust controller synthesis was proposed. To guarantee control performance, it is assumed that the Koopman operator is invariant on a finite-dimensional space spanned by some basis functions. Similar thoughts are followed in our recent works on Koopman-based state observers
[
7
,
8
]
.
In fact, if the Koopman operator maps outside of its domain, the resulting error analysis must be restricted to instantaneous (one-sampling-time) prediction, thus invalidating its prediction over long horizons
[
9
]
and hence any stability guarantee.

Unfortunately but expectedly, no function space is universally suitable for the definition of Koopman operators; instead, the choice depends on the qualitative behaviors of nonlinear systems.
For a measure-preserving dynamics, namely the ergodic motion on an attractor set, one defines the Koopman operator as an isometry on
L^{2}(\mu)
(where
\mu
denotes the invariant measure)
[
10
,
11
]
. For an asymptotically stable dynamics towards an equilibrium point, the Koopman operator can be defined on a Segal–Bargmann space (which is a subspace of the collection of analytical functions)
[
12
]
.
For the convenience of data-based estimation, however, one should rather define the Koopman operator on a
reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)

[
13
]
and thus use kernel-based learning methods without choosing basis functions
[
14
,
15
]
.
The RKHS theory of Koopman operators is only recently noted in the literature — as long as the dynamics has sufficient smoothness, the Koopman operator can be accommodated in an RKHS, if choosing a kernel that makes the RKHS equivalent to a Sobolev-type Hilbert space
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
— see Section
2
for an elementary introduction to this theory.

While Koopman operator gives a linear representation of nonlinear systems, the use of Koopman operator for dynamical analysis and control largely remains an art of practicality rather than a theoretically rigorous framework. Intrinsically, the property of an operator on an (infinite-dimensional) function space differs from that of a matrix on a (finite-dimensional) Euclidean space.
As a symptomatic fact, if the spectral radius of
A
is below
1
, then the origin is asymptotically stable
[
19
]
; but when
A
stands for the Koopman operator
A
, the spectrum of
A
depends on the choice of the kernel function in RKHS, and the
fundamental relation between the spectrum and stability can be elusive
.
As commonly pointed out, despite many exploratory efforts, a general theory for Koopman-based control does not yet exist
[
20
,
21
]
. Clearly, to this end, the spectrum–stability relation on RKHS is of pivotal importance.
This issue was addressed in the authors’ very recent work (Tang and Ye
[
22
]
). By defining a linear–radial product kernel, we proved that the spectral radius of
A
is below
1
when the origin is asymptotically stable, while upon local bifurcation that alters the stability, the spectrum of
A
escapes from the unit disk — see Section
3
for more detailed theoretical discussions.

The aim of this paper is to utilize the fundamental relation between the Koopman operator’s spectrum, previously established in
[
22
]
, for Lyapunov stability analysis.

•

Considering the problem of finding a Lyapunov function with a fixed decay rate, we represent the decay rate function and the Lyapunov function as “
kernel quadratic forms
” represented by positive operators on the RKHS, thus formulating a (discrete-time)
operator algebraic Lyapunov equation
(operator ALE).

•

We show that when the spectral radius of the Koopman operator is below
1
, the operator ALE has a unique solution expressed by a convergent infinite series of Hilbert–Schmidt operators. (See Section
4
.)

•

When the Koopman operator must be estimated from data by a kernel extended dynamic mode decomposition (kEDMD) routine, we prove that the resulting Lyapunov function estimate can have an error at most in proportion to the learning error of the Koopman operator, thus justifying a data-based routine to calculate the Lyapunov function. (See Section
5
.)

The proposed approach is demonstrated with a numerical study on a non-polynomial nonlinear system (Section
6
), where the Lyapunov function is shown to be approximately correctly found.

In machine learning, a standard technique to formulate the nonlinear function learning as (computationally tractable) convex optimization problems (as if the function is “linear”) is the

2.2
Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS)

We say that function

Suppose that

4
4

4

According to

For any

3
Linear–Radial Product Kernel

While a radial kernel suffices to guarantee the well-definedness of Koopman operator under regularity conditions, it is “indifferent to” or “unaware of” the existence of an equilibrium point, and hence the Koopman operator, in particular its spectrum, will be noninformative of stability.
To see this point, in view of the relation (
3

(A^{*})^{t}\kappa^{=}(x,\cdot)=\kappa^{=}(f^{t}(x),\cdot)

\|\kappa(x,\cdot)\|=\rho(0)^{1/2}=\|\kappa(f^{t}(x),\cdot)\|

\|A^{t}\|\geq 1

To address this theoretical gap, our prior work

\kappa=\kappa^{-}\kappa^{=}

\kappa^{-}(x,x^{\prime})=x\cdot x^{\prime}

\kappa^{=}(x,x^{\prime})=\rho(|x-x^{\prime}|)
as specified by Lemma
1
.
5
5

5

To explain the rationale of the symbols, the superscript “

The two RKHSs are, respectively,

As such, the following lemma gives a general depiction of the members of such a novel RKHS.
Simply speaking, the RKHS specified by a

Lemma 2

(Tang & Ye

If

3.2
Spectrum–Stability Relation

For a finite-dimensional linear systems represented by a matrix
A\in\mathds{R}^{d\times d}
, its spectrum (i.e., set of eigenvalues) is fundamentally related to the stability. Now for the Koopman operator
A

In particular, we hope that the Koopman spectrum
\sigma(A)\subset\mathds{D}=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:|\lambda|<1\}
when the origin the asymptotically stable.
6
6

6

By the spectrum of an operator,

The characterization of

12

We are particularly interested in the homeomorphism that carries

, where

.

The key condition that enables the analysis is the existence of a

Lemma 4

(
[

]

Let the conditions in Lemma

.
Then
\textstyle r(A)\leq r(F)

is a product of Jacobian eigenvalues or a limit of such products.

For the Brusselator model in continuous time:

For the discrete-time dynamics with a sampling interval

. This dynamics satisfy all the conditions in Lemma

4
Lyapunov Function as a Kernel Quadratic Form

As the main objective of this paper, we discuss in this section how a Lyapunov function can be obtained from the Koopman operator, when the latter has a spectrum contained in
\mathds{D}
provided that the origin is an asymptotically stable equilibrium point on
\mathds{X}
.
It is well known that for a finite-dimensional linear system, the Lyapunov function can be written as a quadratic form
v(x)=x^{\top}Px
where

is a positive semidefinite matrix.
As an extension to the RKHS

, where the Lyapunov function can be more general, we postulate a “
kernel quadratic form
”
v(x)=\langle\kappa(x,\cdot),\,P\kappa(x,\cdot)\rangle
specified by some
positive operator

P:H_{\kappa}\rightarrow H_{\kappa}
.

7
7

7

By a positive operator
P
we refer to the property that
\langle g,\,Pg\rangle\geq 0
(
\forall g\in H_{\kappa}
).
We write
Q\geq 0
to indicate that

is a positive operator and

to indicate that
Q-R\geq 0
.

For subsequent discussions, we review the concepts of
Hilbert–Schmidt (HS) operators

[
23
]
.

Definition 1
.

A symmetric operator
Q=Q^{\ast}
on a Hilbert space

is said to be Hilbert–Schmidt, denoted by

, if under any orthonormal basis
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in\mathds{N}}
of

\textstyle Q=\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}}q_{i}u_{i}\times u_{i}

(7)

for some
\{q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathds{N}}\subset\mathds{R}
such that
\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}}q_{i}^{2}<\infty
.
8
8

8

Here

refers to a rank-
1
operator defined by
(g\times h)k=\langle h,\,k\rangle g
for any

. Clearly,

since for any

,
\langle g,\,(u_{i}\times u_{i})g\rangle=|\langle u_{i},\,g\rangle|^{2}\geq 0
. The choice of orthonormal basis is non-influential.

Proposition 1
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space. Then

1.

\mathrm{HS}(H)
is a Hilbert space of operators. Specifically, for any
Q,R\in\mathrm{HS}(H)
,
\langle Q,\,R\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}}q_{i}r_{i}
and
\|Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}=\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}}q_{i}^{2}
.

2.

Q\in\mathrm{HS}(H)
is a positive operator if and only if
q_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in\mathds{N}
in its above representation.

3.

Finite-rank operators are dense in the set of HS operators. That is, for any
\epsilon>0
and any
Q\in\mathrm{HS}(H)
, there exists a
N\in\mathds{N}
and

of rank not exceeding

, such that
\|Q-Q_{N}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}<\epsilon
.

4.1
Operator Algebraic Lyapunov Equation

For finite-dimensional linear systems, we know that the

For nonlinear system (

We focus on the case where

q_{i}\downarrow 0
(without loss of generality), we have
w(x)=\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}}q_{i}u_{i}(x)^{2}
.
Here
\{u_{i}\}
forms a basis of
H_{\kappa}
; hence, according to Lemma
2
,
u_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}e_{j}h_{ij}
for some
h_{ij}\in W^{s,2}(\mathds{X})
. In this sense, we say that
w
is an “approximate sum of squares” of “locally linear” and “globally regular” functions.
For example, if
w(\cdot)=|\cdot|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e_{i}^{2}
, then
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e_{i}\times e_{i}
is simply a rank-
d
operator.

Clearly,
\kappa(0,\cdot)=0
,
w(0)=0
, and thus
v(0)=0
for any solution of (
8
). Then, by doing a telescopic sum of (
8
) and bringing to the infinite-time limit, as
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}f^{t}(x)\rightarrow 0
for all
x\in X
, we obtain

v(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}w\left(f^{t}(x)\right)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\langle A^{*t}\kappa(x,\cdot),\,QA^{\ast t}\kappa(x,\cdot)\rangle.

That is,

\textstyle v(x)=\langle\kappa(x,\cdot),\,P\kappa(x,\cdot)\rangle,\kern 5.0ptP=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}A^{t}QA^{*t}.

(9)

The operator
P
here satisfies the following
operator algebraic Lyapunov equation (operator ALE)
:

APA^{\ast}-P=-Q.

(10)

4.2
Existence and Uniqueness of the Solution

Now we present the main theoretical contribution of this paper, which serves as a
converse Lyapunov theorem
in the language of a Koopman operator on a RKHS.

Theorem 1
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
Q\in\mathrm{HS}(H)
a positive operator, and
A:H\rightarrow H
a bounded linear operator with
r(A)<1
, i.e.,
\sigma(A)\subset\mathds{D}
. Then the the series given in (
9
) determines a
P\in\mathrm{HS}(H)
as a solution to the operator ALE (
10
) and in fact the unique solution.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in\mathds{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of
H
and
Q
be represented as (
7
). Then for
t\in\mathds{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\|A^{t}QA^{\ast t}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}=\|\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}}q_{i}A^{t}u_{i}\times A^{t}u_{i}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathds{N}}\sum_{j\in\mathds{N}}q_{i}q_{j}|\langle A^{t}u_{i},\,A^{t}u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq\|Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\|A^{t}\|^{4}.

and hence
\|A^{t}QA^{\ast t}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq\|Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\|A^{t}\|^{2}
. Given any
\epsilon\in(0,(1-r(A))/2)
, there exists a
\overline{t}\in\mathds{N}
such that
\|A^{t}\|\leq(r(A)+\epsilon)^{t}
for all
t\geq\overline{t}
, and hence
\sum_{t=\overline{t}}^{\infty}A^{t}QA^{\ast t}
is an HS operator with HS norm bounded by
\|Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\sum_{t=\overline{t}}^{\infty}(r(A)+\epsilon)^{2t}\leq(r(A)+\epsilon)^{-2\overline{t}}
.
Clearly,
\sum_{t=0}^{\overline{t}-1}A^{t}QA^{\ast t}
is HS. Thus the series
P
is HS. To prove uniqueness, suppose that
P
and
P^{\prime}
are two solutions to (
10
), then
\tilde{P}=P-P^{\prime}
satisfies
A\tilde{P}A^{\ast}=\tilde{P}
. It follows that
\tilde{P}=A^{t}\tilde{P}A^{\ast t}
for all
t\in\mathds{N}
. Hence we have,

\|\tilde{P}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|A^{t}\tilde{P}A^{\ast t}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq\|\tilde{P}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|A^{t}\|=0.
This implies
\tilde{P}=0
. Therefore the solution to (
10
) must be unique.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Suppose that the conditions of Lemma
4
hold. Then for any
w:\mathds{X}\rightarrow\mathds{R}_{+}
with
w(x)=\langle\kappa(x,\cdot),\,Q\kappa(x,\cdot)\rangle
for some
Q\geq 0
,
Q\in\mathrm{HS}(H_{\kappa})
, there exists a unique
v
specified by
v(x)=\langle\kappa(x,\cdot),\,P\kappa(x,\cdot)\rangle
for some
P\geq 0
,
P\in\mathrm{HS}(H_{\kappa})
such that (
8
) hold.

5
Data-based Estimation and Its Error

The Koopman operator can be learned from data by fitting a parametric representation to the evolutionary relation (
3
) of the kernel functions. This learning procedure is known as kEDMD
[
14
]
.
Suppose we have an i.i.d. sample
\{(x_{j},y_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}
. The kEDMD finds an approximation

\textstyle\hat{A}^{\ast}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\theta_{ij}\kappa(y_{i},\cdot)\times\kappa(x_{j},\cdot)

(11)

where the coefficient matrix
\Theta=[\theta_{ij}]
solves a least-squares problem on RKHS:

\textstyle\min\kern 5.0pt\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\hat{A}^{\ast}\kappa\left(x_{j},\cdot\right)-\kappa\left(y_{j},\cdot\right)\|_{H}^{2}.

(12)

It admits an explicit solution of
\Theta=G_{xx}^{-1}
, where the matrix
G_{xx}
has its entry at the
(i,j)
-th position specified by
\kappa(x_{i},x_{j})
.
For later analysis, we provide following conclusion on the error of kEDMD.

Lemma 5

(Köhne et al.
[
16
]
, Theorem 5.2)
.

Suppose that the conditions in Lemma
3
hold, and let
\hat{A}
be as in (
12
), where the sample on
\mathds{X}
has a fill distance of
h_{\mathrm{fill}}=\sup_{x\in\mathds{X}}\min_{j=1,\dots,n}|x-x_{j}|
. Then for some constant
c_{0}
,

\|\hat{A}-A\|\leq c_{0}h_{\mathrm{fill}}^{k+1/2},

where
k
is the index in the radial kernel (see Footnote 3).

5.1
Kernel Ridge Regression for Decay Rate Function

In addition to approximating the Koopman operator
A
using data, the operator
Q
that specifies the decay rate function
w
should also be estimated from data. To this end, suppose that
w
is a sum of squares of functions on
H_{\kappa}
:
w(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\omega_{i}(x)|^{2}
, and hence
w(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\langle\kappa(x,\cdot),\,\omega_{i}\rangle|^{2}
gives
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\omega_{i}\times\omega_{i}
.
Denote
\epsilon^{Q}_{N}=\|\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}\omega_{i}\times\omega_{i}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
the truncation error (which equals
0
if
w
is an exact sum of squares of
N
terms), and consider any approximation of
Q_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}\times\omega_{i}
by
\hat{Q}_{N}=\hat{\omega}_{i}\times\hat{\omega}_{i}
. We have

\textstyle\|Q-\hat{Q}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(2\|\omega_{i}\|\cdot\|\hat{\omega}_{i}-\omega_{i}\|+\|\hat{\omega}_{i}-\omega_{i}\|^{2}\right)+\epsilon^{Q}_{N}.

Approximating
\omega_{i}
by
\hat{\omega}_{i}
based on data is realized via a well-known routine called
kernel ridge regression
(KRR), where we let
\hat{\omega}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}\kappa(x_{j},\cdot)
and optimize the coefficients by a regularized least-squares problem:

\textstyle\min_{\{\alpha_{ij}\}_{j}}\kern 5.0pt\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\hat{\omega}_{i}(x_{k})-\omega_{i}(x_{k})\right)^{2}+\lambda_{i}\|\hat{\omega}_{i}\|_{H}^{2}.

(13)

Through this routine, we have the approximation of
Q
as

\textstyle\hat{Q}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\eta_{ij}\kappa(x_{i},\cdot)\times\kappa(x_{j},\cdot)\text{ with }\eta_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\alpha_{ki}\alpha_{kj}.

(14)

Since practically the regularization constants
\lambda_{i}
can be fine-tuned through validation, we implicitly assume that they can be tuned to the near-optimal order of magnitude.
The error of KRR was established in the literature and presented as the following lemma.

Lemma 6

(Smale & Zhou
[
27
]
, Corollary 2)
.

Suppose that
\mathds{X}\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
is compact and
\omega_{i}\in H=H_{\kappa}
. Then given any
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that
\|\hat{\omega}_{i}-\omega_{i}\|_{H}\leq c\|\omega_{i}\|_{H}n^{-1/6}\log(2/\delta)
holds with probability at least
1-\delta
.

Corollary 3
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
6
, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have

\textstyle\|Q-\hat{Q}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq c^{Q}_{n,\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\omega_{i}\|_{H}^{2}+\epsilon^{Q}_{N},

where
c^{Q}_{n,\delta}=2cn^{-1/6}\log(2/\delta)+c^{2}n^{-1/3}\log^{2}(2/\delta)
.

5.2
Estimation of the Lyapunov Function

With kEDMD estimate
\hat{A}
as in (
11
) and KRR estimate
\hat{Q}
as in (
14
), we end up with a data-based approximation of Lyapunov operator
\hat{P}
, which we require satisfy the operator ALE:
\hat{A}\hat{P}\hat{A}^{\ast}-\hat{P}=-\hat{Q}
. Since
\mathrm{range}~\hat{A}=\mathrm{span}\{\kappa(x_{j},\cdot)\}_{j=1}^{n}
, it becomes obvious that
\hat{P}
can be represented by a matrix of coefficients
\Pi=[\pi_{ij}]
:

\textstyle\hat{P}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\pi_{ij}\kappa(x_{i},\cdot)\times\kappa(x_{j},\cdot).

(15)

The coefficients in (
15
) turn out to satisfy the following matrix algebraic Lyapunov equation, which can be deduced by elementary linear algebra manipulations:

\textstyle\Theta^{\top}\Gamma^{\top}\Pi\Gamma\Theta-\Pi=-\mathrm{H},

(16)

where
\Gamma\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
has its
(i,j)
-th entry as
\kappa(x_{i},y_{j})
and
\mathrm{H}\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
has its
(i,j)
-th entry equal to
\eta_{ij}
as calculated by (
14
).

5.3
Error Bound on the Lyapunov Function Estimate

Finally, we combine the operator norm error on
A
and the HS norm error on
Q
to provide the guaranteed HS norm error on
P
. In view of
P=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}A^{t}QA^{\ast t}
and
\hat{P}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\hat{A}^{t}Q\hat{A}^{\ast t}
, the error bound is derived from the errors in each term of the infinite series.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that the conditions of Lemmas
5
and
6
hold, and in addition,
r(\hat{A})<1
. Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that with probability
1-\delta
, we have

\|\hat{P}-P\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq c_{1}\epsilon_{n,N,\delta}+c_{2}\epsilon_{n,N,\delta}^{2}.

where
\epsilon_{n,N,\delta}=\max\left\{c_{0}h_{\mathrm{fill}}^{k+1/2},c^{Q}_{n,\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\omega_{i}\|_{H}^{2}+\epsilon^{Q}_{N}\right\}
.

Proof.

For any bounded operator
A
and HS operator
B
, we have
\|AB\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq\|A\|\cdot\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
. Hence we verify that

\displaystyle\|\hat{P}-P\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\|A^{t}\|^{2}\|\hat{Q}-Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}+\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}2\|\hat{A}^{t}-A^{t}\|

\displaystyle\cdot(\|\hat{A}^{t}\|\vee\|A^{t}\|)(\|\hat{Q}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\vee\|Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}})

Since
r(A)<1
, for any small enough
\epsilon>0
,
\|A^{t}\|\leq(r(A)+\epsilon)^{t}
after some
\overline{t}
and hence the first series is bounded by a constant multiple of
\epsilon_{n,N,\delta}
. In the second series,
\|\hat{Q}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\vee\|Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq\mathrm{const.}+\epsilon_{n,N,\delta}
, and for large enough
t
,
\|\hat{A}^{t}\|\vee\|A^{t}\|\leq(r(A)\vee r(\hat{A})+\epsilon)^{t}
. Moreover, for all
t\geq 1
,

\textstyle\|\hat{A}^{t}-A^{t}\|\leq\|\hat{A}-A\|\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\|\hat{A}^{s}\|\cdot\|A^{t-1-s}\|.

When
t
is large enough, the sum is always bounded by
t(r(A)\vee r(\hat{A})+\epsilon)^{t}
, which is summable over
t
. As
\|\hat{A}-A\|\leq\epsilon_{n,N,\delta}
, the proof is completed.
∎

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a data-driven approach for estimating the Lyapunov function corresponding to a given decay rate function based on the concept of Koopman operator on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space.
Specifically, with a linear–radial kernel, when the origin is an asymptotically stable equilibrium point, the Koopman spectral radius is below
1
, which allowing the Lyapunov function to be the unique solution to an operator algebraic Lyapunov equation.
Under data-based approximation of the Koopman operator, the resulting estimation of the Lyapunov function has an error that vanishes in the large data limit.

In the circumstances when the origin is not globally asymptotically stable over all
\mathds{X}
, the estimation of its domain attraction, or the estimation of all domains of attractions of multiple attractors, naturally become the next step of our research.
More importantly, the fundamental spectrum–stability relation in a “Koopman operator on a Hilbert space” formalism lays a foundation for promising advances in nonlinear control and observation theory, which we consider as a noteworthy direction.
