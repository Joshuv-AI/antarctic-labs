Zero-cycles on moduli spaces of twisted sheaves and applications to double EPW quartics
Carl Mazzanti

Abstract
. Chen, Li, Zhang, and Zhang extended the results of Shen, Yin, and Zhao on zero-cycles on moduli spaces of stable objects on K ​ 3 K3 surfaces to the twisted setting. In this work, we complement this by extending results by Vial and Martin–Vial to moduli spaces on twisted K ​ 3 K3 surfaces. Exploiting the fact that double EPW quartics can be realised as moduli spaces of twisted sheaves, we show that effective zero-cycles agree if and only if they agree in the associated Verra fourfold and show that the twisted Beauville–Voisin class of Chen, Li, Zhang, and Zhang agrees with the Beauville–Voisin class in that case.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Zero-cycles on moduli spaces of twisted sheaves on
K3
surfaces

Let
S
be a
K3
surface and
o_{S}\in\chow_{0}(S)
its Beauville–Voisin class in the Chow group of zero-cycles with rational coefficients, defined as the class of any point lying on a rational curve in
S

[
BV04
]
.
O’Grady considered a filtration on the zero-cycles of
S
by defining
S_{d}^{\text{OG}}\chow_{0}(S)\subset\chow_{0}(S)
as the subset of all zero-cycles of the form
[p_{1}]+\ldots+[p_{d}]+ao_{S}
for
a\in\mathbb{Q}
and points
p_{i}\in S
.
O’Grady conjectured that

c_{2}(\mathcal{E})\in S_{d(\mathcal{E})}^{\mathrm{OG}}\chow_{0}(S)

for any
\mathcal{E}\in D^{b}(S)
, where
d(\mathcal{E})=\frac{1}{2}\dim\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
.

This was verified by Shen, Yin, and Zhao, who defined a filtration on zero-cycles of moduli spaces of stable objects
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v)
in
[
SYZ20
]
as

S_{i}^{\text{SYZ}}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v))\coloneq\left\langle[\mathcal{E}]\penalty\ |\penalty\ \mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(v)\text{ and }c_{2}(\mathcal{E})\in S_{i}^{\text{OG}}\chow_{0}(S)\right\rangle.

This was shown in
loc. cit.
to be independent of the modular interpretation and by Li and Zhang
[
LZ23
]
to agree with a filtration defined by Voisin
[
Voi16
]
in the more general context of hyperkähler varieties.
Following Shen, Yin, and Zhao’s work, Barros, Flapan, Marian, and Silversmith introduced another such filtration in
[
BFMS22
]
.
This was subsequently generalised to settings much more general than moduli spaces of stable objects on
K3
surfaces and called the co-radical filtration by Vial
[
Via22
]
.
All of these filtrations are known to agree
[
Via22
,
LZ23
]
.
The results of
[
SYZ20
]
have recently been extended to the setting of twisted
K3
surfaces by Chen, Li, Zhang, and Zhang
[
CLZZ25
]
.

We extend the fact that Vial’s co-radical filtration agrees with the Shen–Yin–Zhao and Voisin’s filtration to the setting of twisted
K3
surfaces.

Theorem 1.1

(
Proposition
3.13
)
.

Let
\mathscr{X}=(S,\beta)
be a twisted

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
a moduli space of stable objects in the derived category of twisted sheaves

Then Voisin’s, the Shen–Yin–Zhao, and Vial’s co-radical filtrations agree,

S_{i}^{\text{V}}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))=S_{i}^{\text{SYZ}}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))=R_{i}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)).

Work by Marian and Zhao
[
MZ20
]
established that

[\mathcal{E}]=[\mathcal{F}]\text{ in }\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v))\iff c_{2}(\mathcal{E})=c_{2}(\mathcal{F})\text{ in }\chow_{0}(S)

and Martin and Vial
[

]
generalised this to higher-degree effective zero-cycles.
We extend this result to the twisted setting.

Proposition 1.2

(
Proposition
3.14
)
.

Consider a twisted
K3
surface
\mathscr{X}=(S,\beta)
and let
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
be a moduli space of stable objects in

of dimension

Let
\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i}

1.2.
The twisted Beauville–Voisin class

Chen, Li, Zhang, and Zhang introduced a
twisted Beauville–Voisin class
in the setting of twisted
K3
surfaces
\mathscr{X}=(S,\beta)

[
CLZZ25
]
.
It is defined as

o_{\mathscr{X}}=\frac{c_{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}+\left(1-\frac{\deg c_{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}\right)o_{S},

where
\mathcal{F}
is a locally free sheaf lying on a constant cycle subvariety of some
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
.
It is shown to depend only on
\mathscr{X}
in
[
CLZZ25
]
and is a main technical tool in
loc. cit.
, required to adapt the results of
[
SYZ20
]
to the twisted setting.
It is conjectured that
o_{\mathscr{X}}=o_{S}
.
We provide evidence for this via the Franchetta conjecture and prove it in a special case.

Theorem 1.3

(
Theorem
4.6
)
.

Let
S
be a
K3
surface of genus
2
and
\beta\in\mathrm{Br}(S)
the Brauer class of order
2
discussed in
[
Gee05
, Sec. 9.8]
.
Let
\mathscr{X}=(S,\beta)
be the associated twisted
K3
surface.
Then the twisted and untwisted Beauville–Voisin classes agree, i.e.

o_{\mathscr{X}}=o_{S}.

The proof of this theorem goes through a more geometric description of the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{(0,H,0)}(\mathscr{X})
as double EPW quartics
[
IKKR17
]
.
This allows for more control over the constant cycle subvarieties of the moduli space and hence of the second Chern class of points lying on them.

1.3.
Double EPW quartics as moduli spaces of twisted sheaves

Double EPW quartics
Y
are hyperkähler fourfolds of
K3^{[2]}
-type first introduced by Iliev, Kapustka, Kapustka, and Ranestad in
[
IKKR17
]
.
They admit an anti-symplectic involution
\iota
and the general
Y
is a moduli space of stable objects on a twisted
K3
surface of genus
2
.
This isomorphism was first obtained via lattice-theoretic methods in
[
IKKR17
]
and later made more explicit in
[
CKKM19
]
.
Double EPW quartics also admit a more geometric description, similar to double EPW sextics, via conics in Verra fourfolds.
We exploit this description to study the group of zero-cycles on
Y
and relate it to the underlying
K3
surface.
The involution induces an eigenspace decomposition

\chow_{0}(Y)=\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}\oplus\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\oplus\chow_{0}(Y)^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}},

where
o_{Y}
is the class of any point fixed by
\iota
.

Theorem 1.4

(
Theorem
4.7
)
.

Let
Y
be a general double EPW quartic and let
S
be either of the two K3 surfaces associated to
Y
.
Then the map

\chow_{0}(Y)\cong\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{(0,H,0)}(S,\beta))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ch_{2}}}\chow_{0}(S)

is
0
on
\chow_{0}(Y)^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}
, maps
o_{Y}
to
o_{S}
, and induces an isomorphism

\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\cong\chow_{0}(S)_{\mathrm{hom}}.

Its inverse is given up to scalars by the correspondence
\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{t}
composed with multiplication by the square of the polarisation
h^{2}
.

We introduce another, more geometric filtration on
\chow_{0}(Y)
, similar to the one introduced by Shen and Yin
[
SY20
]
in the setting of Fano varieties of lines and show that it agrees with the filtrations discussed above and with the one induced by
\iota
.
This result provides another proof of
[
Maz26
, Prop. 5.14]
.
Similar results are known for Fano varieties of lines
[
SV16
,
Voi16
]
and double EPW sextics
[

]
.

Theorem 1.5

(
Proposition
4.9
)
.

Let
Y
be a general double EPW quartic.
Then, all four filtrations, the Shen–Yin, Voisin’s, Shen–Yin–Zhao, and Vial’s co-radical filtration, agree,

S_{i}^{\text{SY}}\chow_{0}(Y)=S_{i}^{\text{V}}\chow_{0}(Y)=S_{i}^{\text{SYZ}}\chow_{0}(Y)=R_{i}\chow_{0}(Y).

Furthermore, they all agree with the filtration obtained from the decomposition of
\chow_{0}(Y)
,

\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}\subset\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}\oplus\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\subset\chow_{0}(Y).

We also prove a criterion analogous to the one from
Proposition
1.2
in the setting of double EPW quartics that takes the geometric construction via conics in Verra fourfolds into account.
There is an analogous result in the case of the Fano variety of lines on a cubic fourfold
[
MV25
, Thm. 2.8]
.

(

)
.

Let

1.5.
Conventions

We work over the complex numbers.
A variety refers to a separated, integral scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
and a subvariety to a closed, reduced, possibly reducible, subscheme of a variety, unless stated otherwise.
A hyperkähler variety refers to a smooth, projective variety that is simply connected in the analytic topology and carries a holomorphic symplectic form that is unique up to scalars, i.e.
H^{0}(T,\Omega_{T}^{2})=\mathbb{C}\sigma
.
Points in varieties refer to closed points, unless otherwise specified.
Chow rings will be assumed to have rational coefficients and
\chow^{\ast}(T)_{\mathrm{hom}}
always refers to homologically trivial cycles with respect to singular cohomology.
A general point in a variety refers to a point contained in a non-empty open subset, while a very general point in a variety refers to a point contained in the non-empty complement of a countable union of subvarieties.

2.
Birational motives

We begin by providing some motivation.
The category of birational motives
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\mathbb{C})_{\mathbb{Q}}
has the property that
\Hom_{\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(\mathbb{C})_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(Y),\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X))=\chow_{0}(X_{\mathbb{C}(Y)})
for two smooth projective varieties
X
and
Y
.
Hence, by resolution of singularities,
\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)
encodes information about
\chow_{0}(X_{L})
for all finitely generated field extensions
L/\mathbb{C}
.
Furthermore,
\chow_{0}
is not a tensor functor, as the natural map
\chow_{0}(X)\otimes\chow_{0}(Y)\to\chow_{0}(X\times Y)
is not an isomorphism in general.
Birational motives, on the other hand, do form a tensor functor and are easier to handle than Chow motives, making them the ideal setting in which to discuss zero-cycles.
Finally, they provide the natural framework and language to prove the results that follow, as avoiding them would be significantly more technical.

In this section we work over an arbitrary field
k
for the sake of generality, as there is no advantage in working over
\mathbb{C}
.
We define birational motives and recall results that will be needed.
We refer to
[
KS16
]
and
[
Via22
]
for more details, the latter in particular with respect to the co-multiplicative structure.

Definition 2.1
.

The (covariant) category
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)_{R}
of
effective Chow motives
over
k
with coefficients in
R
is defined as the pseudo-effective envelope of the category
\mathrm{Corr}(k)_{R}
, the objects of which are smooth projective varieties over
k
.
Define the morphisms by
\Hom_{\mathrm{Corr}(k)_{R}}(X,Y)\coloneq\chow^{\dim Y}(X\times_{k}Y)\otimes R
.
The composition is given by the composition of correspondences, i.e.

\Gamma\circ\Gamma^{\prime}\coloneq(\pr_{X,Z})_{\ast}(\pr_{X,Y}^{\ast}\Gamma^{\prime}\cdot\pr_{Y,Z}^{\ast}\Gamma).

Explicitly, objects of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)_{R}
are given by pairs
(X,p)
, where
X
is a smooth projective variety over
k
and
p\in\chow^{\dim X}(X\times_{k}X)
is an idempotent correspondence.
A morphism
f\colon(X,p)\to(Y,q)
is given by a correspondence
\Gamma\in\chow^{\dim X}(X\times_{k}Y)
that satisfies
q\circ\Gamma=\Gamma\circ p
.
Define the Chow motive of
X
as
\mathfrak{h}(X)\coloneq(X,\delta_{X})
, where
\delta_{X}
is the class of the graph of the diagonal.
Define a tensor product by
(X,p)\otimes(Y,q)\coloneq(X\times_{k}Y,p\times q)
with unit
\mathds{1}=\mathfrak{h}(\spec k)
.
This endows the category of effective Chow motives with the structure of an
R
-linear tensor category.

The motive of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
decomposes as
\mathfrak{h}(\mathbb{P}^{1})=(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\{0\})\oplus(\mathbb{P}^{1},\{0\}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})=\mathds{1}\oplus\mathbb{L}
, the latter summand of which is called the
Lefschetz motive
.

Definition 2.2
.

The category
\mathcal{M}(k)_{R}
of
Chow motives
over
k
with coefficients in
R
is constructed from
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)_{R}
by inverting the functor
-\otimes\mathbb{L}
.
It is rigid and the natural functor
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)_{R}\to\mathcal{M}(k)_{R}
fully functor.
We define
\mathfrak{h}(X)(n)
as
\mathfrak{h}(X)\otimes(\mathbb{L}^{-1})^{\otimes n}
.

Instead of inverting
-\otimes\mathbb{L}
one can take the quotient by it to obtain the category of
birational Chow motives
over
k
with coefficients in
R
, denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(k)_{R}
, as introduced by Kahn and Sujatha in
[
KS16
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the
\otimes
-ideal in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)_{R}
consisting of morphisms which factor through an object of the form
P\otimes\mathbb{L}
.
Define the category
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(k)_{R}
of birational motives as the pseudo-abelian envelope of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)_{R}/\mathcal{L}
.
Denote the birational motive of
X
by
\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)
.

Morphisms of birational motives also admit an explicit description.
For irreducible
X
, we have

\Hom(\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X),\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(Y))\coloneq\chow_{0}(Y_{k(X)})\otimes R=\varinjlim_{U\subset X}\chow_{\dim X}(U\times_{k}Y)\otimes R,

where the limit runs over all non-empty open subsets of
X
.
A morphism in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{eff}}(k)_{R}
induces a morphism in
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}(k)_{R}
by restricting the correspondence to the generic point.
Furthermore, a rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
induces a morphism
f_{\ast}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)\to\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(Y)
by restricting the graph of
f|_{U}
to the generic point, where
U
is a dense open on which
f
is defined.
Additionally, a generically finite rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
induces a well-defined morphism
f^{\ast}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(Y)\to\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)
by
[
She16
, Cor. 2.5]
.

A key feature of morphisms of birational motives with values in
\mathbb{Q}
is that they are defined by their action on zero-cycles after base-changing to a sufficiently large field.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
Via22
, Lem. 2.1]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two smooth projective varieties over a field
k
and
\Omega
a universal domain containing
k
.
Let
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
be two morphisms
\mathfrak{h}(X)\to\penalty\ \mathfrak{h}(Y)
of Chow motives.
Then

\gamma_{\ast}=\gamma^{\prime}_{\ast}\colon\chow_{0}(X_{\Omega})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\to\chow_{0}(Y_{\Omega})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\iff\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}.

The pullback along the diagonal morphism
\delta\colon X\to X\times_{k}X
endows the contravariant motive
\mathfrak{h}(X)
with a commutative algebra structure with unit induced by the structure morphism
X\to\spec k
.
Working covariantly instead, the push-forward along the diagonal endows
\mathfrak{h}(X)
with the structure of a co-commutative co-algebra.
Passing to birational motives, we obtain a co-commutative co-algebra structure on
\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)
with co-unit
\epsilon\colon\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)\to\mathds{1}
induced by pushing forward along the structure morphism.
It is straightforward to check that the identities necessary to ensure that this indeed defines a co-commutative co-algebra hold at the level of morphisms of varieties already.
Finally, just as a morphism of varieties
f\colon X\to Y
induces a morphism
f^{\ast}\colon\mathfrak{h}(Y)\to\mathfrak{h}(X)
of algebra objects, a rational morphism
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
induces a morphism
f_{\ast}\colon\mathfrak{h}(X)\to\mathfrak{h}(Y)
of co-algebra objects.
We recollect the following lemma about morphisms of birational motives as co-algebra objects, as it will be needed later.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
Via22
, Prop. 2.3]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth varieties over
k
of dimension
d
and let
\Omega
be a universal domain containing
k
.
Assume there exists a projective variety
\Gamma

and generically finite morphisms
\phi\colon\Gamma\to X
and

, such that one of the following equivalent conditions hold.

(1)

\textstyle{\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\delta_{X}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta_{Y}}

\psi_{\ast}[p]=\psi_{\ast}[q]
for any two general points

, then

We also define (co-multiplicative) birational Chow–Künneth decompositions, as they will be needed later.
They are the counterpart to (multiplicative) Chow–Künneth decompositions in the setting of birational motives.
We refer to
[
Via22
, Sec. 4]
for more details.

Definition 2.6
.

Fix a Weil cohomology theory
H^{\ast}
for smooth projective varieties defined over
k
.
Define the transcendental cohomology of a smooth projective variety as

H_{\mathrm{tr}}^{k}(X)\coloneq H^{k}(X)/N^{1}H^{k}(X),

where
N^{\bullet}
denotes the coniveau filtration

N^{r}H^{k}(X)\coloneq\sum_{Z\subset X}\ker(H^{k}(X)\to H^{k}(X\setminus Z)),

and the sum runs over all codimension
r
subvarieties of
X
.

A morphism

\gamma\in\Hom(\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X),\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(Y))=\chow_{0}(Y_{\mathbb{C}(X)})

induces a homomorphism

\gamma^{\ast}\colon H^{k}_{\mathrm{tr}}(Y)\to H^{k}_{\mathrm{tr}}(X),

by choosing a lift of
\gamma
to
\chow^{\dim X}(X\times Y)
that acts on
H^{k}(Y)
.
This is well-defined, as the difference between any two lifts will be supported on
D\times Y
for some divisor
D\subset X
and hence maps
H^{k}(Y)
to
N^{1}H^{k}(X)
.

Definition 2.7

(
[
She16
]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
.
A
birational Chow–Künneth decomposition
is a collection
\{\varpi_{0},\ldots,\varpi_{d}\}\subset\End(\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X))
such that

(1)

\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)}=\varpi_{0}+\ldots+\varpi_{d}

(2)

\varpi_{k}\circ\varpi_{k}=\varpi_{k}

(3)

\varpi_{i}\circ\varpi_{j}=0
for
i\neq j

(4)

\varpi_{k}^{\ast}\colon H^{l}_{\mathrm{tr}}\to H^{l}_{\mathrm{tr}}
is the identity if
l=k
and
0
otherwise.

Following Vial
[
Via22
]
, we say that a birational Chow–Künneth decomposition is
co-multiplicative
if the induced decomposition defines a unital grading of the co-algebra object
\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(X)
, or equivalently if

(1)

\varpi_{0}=o_{k(X)}
for some
o\in\chow_{0}(X)
, and

(2)

(\varpi_{i}\otimes\varpi_{j})\circ\delta_{X}\circ\varpi_{k}=0
if
k\neq i+j
.

3.
Zero-cycles on moduli spaces of twisted sheaves on
K3

We begin by reviewing what is known in the case of moduli spaces of stable objects on
K3
surfaces before discussing what changes have to be made in the twisted case.

Let
S
be a
K3
surface and denote its bounded derived category of coherent sheaves by
D^{b}(S)
.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v)
be a moduli space of stable objects in
D^{b}(S)
with respect to
\sigma
and of fixed primitive Mukai vector
v
.
It is a hyperkähler variety of dimension
\frac{v^{2}+2}{2}
.

Following the notation in
[
CLZZ25
]
, we denote a twisted
K3
surface by
\mathscr{X}=(S,\beta)
, where
\beta
is a Brauer class on
S
.
Denote the category of
d
-fold
\beta
-twisted sheaves on
S
by
D^{(d)}(\mathscr{X})
.
We denote a moduli space of stable objects on
\mathscr{X}
by
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
.
See
[
CLZZ25
, Sec. 2]
for more details.

We may abbreviate the notation
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v)
and

3.1.
The untwisted case

Beauville and Voisin proved in
[
BV04
]
the existence of a special zero-cycle of
o_{S}\in\chow_{0}(S)
, which is defined as the class of any point lying on a rational curve in

.
It has the property that the second Chern class and all products of divisors are a multiple of
o_{S}
.
O’Grady defined the following increasing filtration by subsets of
\chow_{0}(S)
in
[
OGr13
]
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
OGr13
]
)
.

Define
O’Grady’s filtration
on
\chow_{0}(S)
as

S_{i}^{\text{OG}}\chow_{0}(S)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Q},z_{j}\in S}\{ko_{S}+[z_{1}]+\ldots[z_{i}]\}

After having first been conjectured to be the case by O’Grady in
[
OGr13
]
, the following was proven by Shen, Yin, and Zhao.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
SYZ20
]
)
.

For any object
\mathcal{E}\in D^{b}(S)

c_{2}(\mathcal{E})\in S^{\text{OG}}_{d(\mathcal{E})}\chow_{0}(S),

where
d(\mathcal{E})=\frac{1}{2}\dim\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
.

This result is necessary to ensure that the following filtration, induced by O’Grady’s filtration, is exhaustive.

Definition 3.3

(
[
SYZ20
]
)
.

Let
S
be a
K3
surface and
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v)
a moduli space of stable objects in

.
Define the
Shen–Yin–Zhao filtration

S_{i}^{\text{SYZ}}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v))\coloneq\left\langle[\mathcal{E}]\penalty\ |\penalty\ \mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v)\text{ and }c_{2}(\mathcal{E})\in S_{i}^{\text{OG}}\chow_{0}(S)\right\rangle.

In a quest to find a splitting of the conjectural Bloch–Beilinson filtration on the Chow ring
[

, Voisin defined a filtration on zero-cycles of hyperkähler varieties that is expected to split the conjectured filtration.
Barros, Flapan, Marian, and Silversmith considered a different filtration in the setting of moduli spaces of stable objects on

[

These filtrations have all been shown to agree in the case of moduli spaces of sheaves.

Definition 3.4

(
[
Voi16
]
)
.

Let
T
be a hyperkähler variety of dimension

and let

be the set of points rationally equivalent to

.
This is a countable union of closed algebraic subvarieties of

Define

on
\chow_{0}(T)
as

S_{i}^{\text{V}}\chow_{0}(T)\coloneq\langle[x]\penalty\ |\penalty\ x\in T\text{ and }\dim O_{x}\geq n-i\rangle

Definition 3.5

(
[
Via22
]
)
.

Let
T
be a smooth projective variety and fix a
point-like
zero-cycle
o\in\chow_{0}(T)
, meaning that
(\Delta_{T})_{\ast}o=o\times o
.
Define
\overline{\delta}^{0}=\mathrm{id}-o\cdot\deg
,
\overline{\delta}=(\Delta_{T}-o\times\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{id}\times o)\circ\overline{\delta}^{0}
, and
\overline{\delta}^{k}=(\overline{\delta}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\overline{\delta}^{k-1}

Vial’s co-radical filtration
is defined as

R_{k}(T)\coloneq\ker(\overline{\delta}^{k}_{\ast}\colon\chow_{0}(T)\to\chow_{0}(T^{k+1}))

Theorem 3.6

(
[
LZ23
]
,
[
Via22
]
)
.

Let
S
be a
K3

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v)

Then Voisin’s, the Shen–Yin–Zhao, Vial’s co-radical, and the BFMS filtration agree, i.e.

[\mathcal{E}]=[\mathcal{F}]\text{ in }\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(v))\iff c_{2}(\mathcal{E})=c_{2}(\mathcal{F})\text{ in }\chow_{0}(S),

This led Voisin to conjecture that two points

in

This was extended to the following conjecture in

Let

\sum_{i=1}^{m}[x_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}[y_{i}]\text{ in }\chow_{0}(T)\text{ if and only if }\sum_{i=1}^{m}[x_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}[y_{i}]\text{ in }\chow_{0}(T)/F^{md+1}\chow_{0}(T),

and any

This has been verified in

(
[

]
)
.

Let

be a
K3

\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v)

of dimension

.

\sum_{i=m}^{m}[\mathcal{E}_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}[\mathcal{F}_{i}]\text{ in }\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(S,v))\\

3.2.
The twisted case

A
twisted Beauville–Voisin class

o_{\mathscr{X}}\in\chow_{0}(S)
was introduced by Chen, Li, Zhang, and Zhang
[
CLZZ25
]
for a twisted
K3
surface
\mathscr{X}=(S,\beta)
.
It is defined as

o_{\mathscr{X}}=\frac{c_{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}+\left(1-\frac{\deg c_{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}\right)o_{S}

where
\mathcal{F}
is any locally free sheaf of positive rank that lies on some constant cycle subvariety of some moduli space of stable objects in
D^{b}(\mathscr{X})
.
It was shown to be independent of the choice of constant cycle subvariety and moduli space.
The authors of
loc. cit.
conjecture that
o_{\mathscr{X}}=o_{S}
.
The definition of the Shen–Yin–Zhao filtration needs to be changed slightly to take the twist into account.

Remark 3.9
.

The conjecture that
o_{\mathscr{X}}=o_{S}
is supported in
[
CLZZ25
]
by descent arguments and the Bloch–Beilinson conjecture, but it is also implied by the Franchetta conjecture, see
Section
4.2
below.

Definition 3.10

(
[
CLZZ25
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a twisted
K3
surface and
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
a moduli space of stable objects in
D^{(1)}(\mathscr{X})
.
Define the Shen–Yin–Zhao filtration as

S_{i}^{\text{SYZ}}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))\coloneq\left\langle[\mathcal{E}]\penalty\ |\penalty\ \mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)\text{ and }c_{2}(\mathcal{E})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})o_{\mathscr{X}}\in S_{i}^{\text{OG}}\chow_{0}(S)\right\rangle

Note that it agrees with
Definition
3.3
in the untwisted case.

It was shown that

c_{2}(\mathcal{E})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})o_{\mathscr{X}}\in S_{d(\mathcal{E})}^{\text{OG}}\chow_{0}(S)
for all
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)

[
CLZZ25
]
, implying that it is exhaustive.
It was also shown to be independent of the modular interpretation.
Marian and Zhao’s result also holds in the twisted case and it was proven in
loc. cit.
that Voisin’s filtration and the Shen–Yin–Zhao filtration agree.
We introduce the twisted incidence correspondence and extend their results to include Vial’s co-radical filtration.

Definition 3.11

(
[
CLZZ25
]
)
.

Consider the set

\Gamma^{\prime}\coloneq\left\langle(\xi,\mathcal{E})\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ c_{2}(\mathcal{E})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})o_{\mathscr{X}}=[\xi]+ko_{S}\text{ in }\chow_{0}(S)\right\rangle\subset S^{[n]}\times\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v),

where
k
is determined by the Mukai vector.
There is a subvariety
\Gamma\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
such that both projections from it are dominant and generically finite, which we call the
twisted incidence correspondence
.

Indeed,
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a countable union of subvarieties and the second projection is surjective, since
c_{2}(\mathcal{E})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})\in S_{d(\mathcal{E})}^{\text{OG}}\chow_{0}(S)
.
By a Baire category argument, there is a component
\Gamma_{0}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
on which the second projection is dominant.
Then, it follows from
[
Voi16
, Lem. 1.1]
that the symplectic forms on
S^{[n]}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
agree after pullback to
\Gamma_{0}
.
In particular, since the second projection is dominant, the pullbacks must be of rank
2d(v)
, implying that the first projection must also be dominant.
After possibly taking hyperplane sections of
\Gamma_{0}
we obtain a subvariety
\Gamma
such that both projections from it are dominant and generically finite.

The twisted incidence correspondence is used in
[
CLZZ25
]
to show that the birational motives of
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
and
S^{[n]}
are isomorphic as co-algebra objects.
As we will need the explicit isomorphism later, we give the proof here again.

Proposition 3.12

(
[
CLZZ25
, Prop. 6.2]
)
.

The twisted incidence correspondence
\Gamma
induces an isomorphism

\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))\cong\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(S^{[n]})

as co-algebra objects.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
admits a co-multiplicative birational Chow–Künneth decomposition in the sense of
Definition
2.7
.

Proof.

Since we are working over
\mathbb{C}
, we may use
Lemma
2.5
without having to base change to a universal domain.
Marian and Zhao’s result
[
MZ20
]
, which states that
c_{2}(\mathcal{E})=c_{2}(\mathcal{F})
if and only if
[\mathcal{E}]=[\mathcal{F}]
, ensure that the statement can be applied to show that
\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))\cong\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(S^{[n]})
as co-algebra objects.
It is proven in
[
Via22
, Thm. 4.5]
that there is a co-multiplicative birational Chow–Künneth decomposition
\{\varpi_{2i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
on
S^{[n]}
.
This induces a birational Chow–Künneth decomposition on
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
via the isomorphism
\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))\cong\penalty\ \mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(S^{[n]})
.
∎

We can now show that Vial’s co-radical filtration agrees with Voisin’s and the Shen–Yin–Zhao filtration also in the case of
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
and that

also holds in the twisted case.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
\mathscr{X}=(S,\beta)
be a twisted
K3
surface and
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
a moduli space of stable objects in
D^{(1)}(\mathscr{X})

Then Voisin’s, the Shen–Yin–Zhao, and Vial’s co-radical filtration agree,

S_{i}^{\text{V}}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))=S_{i}^{\text{SYZ}}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))=R_{i}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)).

Proof.

The first equality is already known by
[
CLZZ25
, Thm. 1.4]
.
Furthermore, it is known from
[
Via22
, Thm. 3]
that
R_{i}
is of motivic nature.
More precisely,

R_{k}\chow_{0}(T)=\left(\bigoplus_{i\leq k}\varpi_{2i}^{T}\right)\!{}_{\ast}\chow_{0}(T),

where
\{\varpi^{T}_{2i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
denotes the co-multiplicative birational Chow–Künneth decomposition on
T=S^{[n]}
or
T=\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
from
Proposition
3.12

Since they are compatible, the isomorphism
\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))\cong\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(S^{[n]})
sends

to
R_{k}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))
.
The twisted incidence correspondence also respects

by construction and

on

by
[
Via22

Hence,
S_{i}^{\text{SYZ}}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))=R_{i}\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))
.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\mathscr{X}=(S,\beta)
be a twisted
K3
surface and
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
a moduli space of stable objects in
D^{(1)}(\mathscr{X})
of dimension
2n
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{m},\mathcal{F}_{i},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{m}
be points in
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
.
Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}[\mathcal{E}_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}[\mathcal{F}_{i}]\text{ in }\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))\\
\iff\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i})^{\times k}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i})^{\times k}\text{ in }\chow_{0}(S^{k})\text{ for all }k\leq\min(m,n).

Proof.

Recall that we have an isomorphism
\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))\cong\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(S^{[n]})
as co-algebra objects by
Proposition
3.12
.
In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
satisfies the conclusion of
[
Via22
, Conj. 5.4]
.
We know by
[
Via22
, Thm. 5.5(i)]
that

\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))\cong\sym^{\leq n}\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}_{\varpi}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)),

for a birational idempotent correspondence
\varpi
that factors as

\varpi\colon\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}_{2}(S)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{\circ}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)).

The left arrow is given by
[\mathcal{E}]\mapsto c_{2}(\mathcal{E})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})o_{\mathscr{X}}-co_{S}
, where
c=\deg c_{2}(\mathcal{E})-\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E})
depends only on the Mukai vector.

Finally,
[

, Prop. 2.1]
states that for a smooth projective variety

with a direct summand
\mathfrak{h}_{\varpi}^{\circ}(X)
that co-generates

\sum_{i=1}^{m}[x_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}[y_{i}]\text{ in }\chow_{0}(X)\iff\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\varpi_{\ast}[x_{i}])^{\times k}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\varpi_{\ast}[y_{i}])^{\times k}\text{ in }\chow_{0}(X^{k})

is co-generated in degree

.

, we obtain that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}[\mathcal{E}_{i}]=\penalty\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}[\mathcal{F}_{i}]
in
\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))
if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{m}(c_{2}(\mathcal{E})-ro_{\mathscr{X}}-co_{S})^{\times k}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(c_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i})-ro_{\mathscr{X}}-co_{S})^{\times k}

∎

4.
Double EPW quartics as moduli spaces of twisted sheaves

Introduced by Iliev, Kapustka, Kapustka, and Ranestad in
[
IKKR17
]
, double EPW quartics
Y
are hyperkähler fourfolds of
K3^{[2]}
-type that form a
19
-dimensional family.
They carry a polarisation
h
of degree
4
with respect to the Beauville–Bogomolov form
q
, i.e.
q(h,h)=4
, and an anti-symplectic involution with invariant lattice isometric to
U(2)
.
The general double EPW quartic is isomorphic to a moduli space of stable objects on a twisted
K3
surface of genus
2
.
Furthermore, they admit two Lagrangian fibrations.

There are three different constructions of the general double EPW quartic
Y
, which we summarise in the following theorem.
We then discuss the construction as moduli spaces in more detail.
We refer to
[
Maz26
, Sec. 2]
for more details on the other constructions.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
IKKR17
]
)
.

There is a
19
-dimensional family
\mathcal{U}
of hyperkähler varieties
Y
of
K3^{[2]}
-type, called double EPW quartics, that admit a polarisation of Beauville degree
4
and an anti-symplectic involution
\iota\colon Y\to Y
with invariant lattice
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})^{\iota}
isometric to
U(2)
.
Each double EPW quartic admits two Lagrangian fibrations
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
The general double EPW quartic
Y
admits three distinct realisations:

(i)

as the moduli space of stable sheaves twisted by a
2
-torsion Brauer class on a polarised
K3
surface of genus
2
. The general double EPW quartic admits exactly two such associated
K3
surfaces.

(ii)

as the base of a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\alpha\colon F(X)\to Y
, where
F(X)
is the Hilbert scheme of
(1,1)
-conics in a Verra fourfold
X=Q\cap C(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2})
, where
Q
is a general quadric hypersurface.

(iii)

as a double cover
Y\to D_{1}^{\overline{A}}

We briefly summarise the consequences of the second construction.
It is shown in
[
IKKR17
, Sec. 3]
that there is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration
\alpha\colon F(X)\to Y
, where
F(X)
is the Hilbert scheme of
(1,1)
-conics in
X

(1,1)
-curves under the double cover

Theorem 4.2

(
[
Maz26

)
.

Let
Y
be a general double EPW quartic,
X
its associated Verra fourfold.

\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\xrightarrow{\cong}\chow_{1}(X)_{\mathrm{hom}}.

The composition

is also an isomorphism with inverse given up to scalars by multiplication by the square of the polarisation

.

4.1.
Construction of double EPW quartics as moduli spaces

Double EPW quartics were first realised as moduli spaces of twisted sheaves on

[

via lattice-theoretic methods.
Instead of studying this construction, we follow the more explicit approach in
[
CKKM19

Consider the Segre embedding
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{8}
and the projective cone
C(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2})\subset\mathbb{P}^{9}

A
Verra fourfold

X=Q\cap C(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2})
is the intersection of the cone with a general quadric
Q

The two projections
\pi_{i}\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}

The double covers

\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibrations on

We consider only

Denote
\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(a,b)

It is known by

[
Kuz08
]

Furthermore, it is proven in

Let
F(X)
be the Hilbert scheme of conics that project to
(1,1)
-curves under the double cover
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
We need the existence of the

\alpha\colon F(X)\to Y

. Define a map to the moduli space of stable objects with Mukai vector

4.2.
The Franchetta property

In this section we briefly discuss the Francehtta property and show that it holds for cubes of Verra fourfolds, as we will need it later.
We refer to
[
Maz26
, Sec. 3]
for a more detailed discussion.

Let
\mathcal{X}\to B
be a family of smooth varieties over a smooth base
B
.
We say that a cycle
\gamma\in\chow^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}_{b})
is
generically defined
if it lies in the image of the pullback map from
\mathcal{X}
.
We denote the subring of generically defined cycle over
B
by
\gdch^{\ast}_{B}(\mathcal{X}_{b})
.
Note that generically defined cycles depend on the family
\mathcal{X}\to B
.
Which family is meant will usually be clear from the context, so we often omit the subscript
B
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\to B
be a family of smooth varieties over a smooth base
B
.
We say that the family
\mathcal{X}\to B
satisfies the
Franchetta property
if for any closed point, or equivalently for the very general point,
b\in B
we have that the cycle class map restricted to the subring of generically defined cycles,

\mathrm{cl}\colon\gdch_{B}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}_{b})\to H^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}_{b},\mathbb{Q}),

is injective.

O’Grady noticed in
[
OGr13
]
that the Beauville–Voisin class is generically defined and asked whether all generically defined zero-cycles on a
K3
surface are multiples of it.
It was conjectured in
[
FLV19
]
that locally complete families of hyperkähler varieties satisfy the Franchetta property, but it is also known to hold in the case of some Fano varieties
[
FLV21
]
.

Theorem 4.4

(
[
Maz26
, Thm. 3.5]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}^{n/\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{U}
be the family of self-products of Verra fourfolds.
It satisfies the Franchetta property for
n\leq 3
, meaning that for any point
b\in\mathcal{U}
, the cycle class map restricted to generically defined cycles,

\mathrm{cl}\colon\gdch^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}^{n}_{b})\to H^{\ast}(\mathcal{X}_{b}^{n},\mathbb{Q})

is injective for
n\leq 3
.
Furthermore, the family of double EPW quartics
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{U}
satisfies the Franchetta property.
In particular, there exists a generically defined zero-cycle
o_{Y}
on
Y
.

Proof.

It is known from
[
Lat20
]
that there is an isomorphism of Chow motives

\mathfrak{h}(X)\cong\mathfrak{h}(S)(1)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{4}\mathds{1}(i),

where
S
is a
K3
surface associated to the Verra fourfold
X
, as in
Section
4.1
.
This construction can be done in families and by
[
FLV19
, Rmk. 2.6]
it suffices to know that the Franchetta property holds for the cube of
S
, which is known from
[
FLV19
, Thm. 1.5]
.

Since
Y\cong\mathcal{M}_{(0,H,0)}(S,\beta)
, the Chow motive
\mathfrak{h}(Y)
is a direct summand of
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{h}(S^{2})(l_{i})
, where
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
l_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
, by
[
FLV21
, Thm. 1.5]
.
Then, applying the Franchetta property for squares of genus-
2

K3
surfaces
[
FLV19
, Thm. 1.5]
yields the Franchetta property for
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{U}
.
∎

4.3.
The twisted and untwisted Beauville–Voisin classes

The aim of this subsection is to discuss how the Franchetta conjecture would imply that the twisted and untwisted Beauville–Voisin classes agree.
We will use this idea to prove that
o_{\mathscr{X}}=o_{S}
for the family of twisted
K3
surfaces associated to double EPW quartics.

We begin by discussing the situation in general and explain what is missing.
Then, we discuss that this is known in the case of
K3
surfaces associated to double EPW quartics.
Unfortunately, there remains a problem, for which we discuss a solution before proving the main theorem of this section.

Recall that

o_{\mathscr{X}}=\frac{c_{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}+\left(1-\frac{\deg c_{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}\right)o_{S},

where
\mathcal{F}
is any locally free sheaf of positive rank lying on some constant cycle subvariety of some moduli space of stable objects on
\mathscr{X}
.

Since Chow motives of moduli spaces on twisted
K3
surfaces are direct summands of the Chow motives of powers of the underlying
K3
surface
[
FLV21
, Thm. 1.5]
, we expect them to satisfy the Franchetta property
[
LV26
, Conj. 2]
.
Furthermore, we expect the classes of points on constant cycle subvarieties to be generically defined.
Hence, it would follow that for any
\mathcal{F}
on a constant cycle subvariety,
c_{2}(\mathcal{F})
is also generically defined, i.e. a multiple of
o_{S}
.
This would imply
o_{\mathscr{X}}=o_{S}
.

Remark 4.5
.

Unfortunately, this approach does not yield a proof of
o_{\mathscr{X}}=o_{S}
, even in the cases where the Franchetta property is known for powers of
K3
surfaces, since it is not clear whether the classes of points lying on constant cycle subvarieties in some
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
are generically defined.

In the case of double EPW quartics, we can try to make this approach work.
We know that the fixed locus
Z\subset Y
of the involution
\iota
is a generically defined constant cycle subvariety
[
Maz26
, Thm. 5.3]
.
Furthermore, points on other constant cycle subvariety define the same class
o_{Y}
as those on
Z

[
Maz26
, Prop. 5.4]
.
This removes most of the obstacles – the only remaining problem now is that
Y
is a moduli space of rank
0
sheaves on
(S,\beta)
, and hence provides no information on
o_{\mathscr{X}}
.

The solution to this is to construct an isomorphism between
Y
and a moduli space of twisted sheaves of positive rank.
A similar argument was also used in
[
AS18
, Sec. 3]
.
We adapt it to the twisted case.

Construction
.

We begin by reviewing the argument in the untwisted case.
Let
S
be a
K3
surface and
\mathcal{F}
a sheaf of pure dimension
1
on
S
.
After possibly twisting by
\mathcal{O}_{S}(n)
, we may assume that
H^{1}(S,\mathcal{F})=0
and
\mathcal{F}
is globally generated.
Define
M_{\mathcal{F}}
as the kernel of the evaluation map.

0\to M_{\mathcal{F}}\to H^{0}(S,\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0.

Then, one can compute the cohomology of
M_{\mathcal{F}}
and
M_{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}
, the latter of which is a locally free sheaf.
In particular, one can check that there are isomorphisms of differential graded Lie algebras

\Ext^{\ast}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\cong\Ext^{\ast}(M_{\mathcal{F}},M_{\mathcal{F}})\cong\Ext^{\ast}(M_{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee},M_{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}).

In particular, one obtains isomorphisms between the deformation spaces of
\mathcal{F}
and
M_{\mathcal{F}}
.
Furthermore, it is proven that if
\mathcal{F}
is stable, then so is
M_{\mathcal{F}}

[
AS18
, Thm. 3.8]
, yielding an isomorphism of moduli spaces.
The key point of this construction for the following theorem is that
M_{\mathcal{F}}
is of positive rank.
This construction relies crucially on the fact that
\mathcal{O}_{S}
is a spherical object in
D^{b}(S)
.

Consider now a twisted
K3
surface
\mathscr{X}=(S,\alpha)
.
One can define a replacement for
\mathcal{O}_{S}
on
\mathscr{X}
, which we denote by
\mathcal{L}
, in the following manner.
Considering
\mathscr{X}\to S
as a
\mu_{n}
-gerbe, choose an étale cover
U\to S
over which
\mathscr{X}
becomes trivial, meaning that
\mathscr{X}\times_{S}U\cong\penalty\ B\mu_{n}\times U
.
Then, the tautological character
\mu_{n}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
defines a line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{taut}}
on
B\mu_{n}
.
After pulling it back to
B\mu_{n}\times U
, this descends to a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathscr{X}
.
If one prefers to work with Čech cocycles, one can glue
\mathcal{O}_{U_{ij}}
along transition maps
g_{ij}
such that
g_{ij}g_{jk}g_{ki}=\alpha_{ijk}
, where
\{\alpha_{ijk}\}
is the Čech
2
-cocycle representing
\alpha
.
This yields an
\alpha
-twisted line bundle
\mathcal{L}
.
Since this is a line bundle, we have
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{O}_{S}
, and
\mathcal{L}
is a spherical object, i.e.
\Ext^{\ast}(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L})=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}[2]
.
We refer to
[
Căl00
,
Lie07
]
for more details.

Now, let
\mathcal{F}
be an object in
D^{b}(S,\alpha)
of rank
0
.
After twisting by
\mathcal{O}_{S}(n)
for sufficiently large
n
, the untwisted sheaf
\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\vee}
is globally generated and
H^{1}(S,\mathcal{F})=0
.
We define the spherical twist
T_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
via the triangle

R\Hom(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{F}\to T_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{F}).

Since
\mathcal{L}
is spherical, one obtains an isomorphism
\Ext^{\ast}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\cong\Ext^{\ast}(T_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{F}),T_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{F}))
as differential graded algebras and of their deformation spaces.
Furthermore, one obtains isomorphisms
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)\cong\mathcal{M}_{(T_{\mathcal{L}})_{\ast}\sigma}(\mathscr{X},(T_{\mathcal{L}})_{\ast}v)
, where
(T_{\mathcal{L}})_{\ast}v
has positive rank.
Notice that this relies only on
\mathcal{L}
being spherical, meaning it could be replaced by another spherical object.

Since the rank of
(T_{\mathcal{L}})_{\ast}(\mathcal{F})
is of positive rank, we can apply this construction in the case of double EPW quartics to prove that the twisted and untwisted Beauville–Voisin classes agree.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
S
be a general
K3
surface of genus
2
and
\beta\in\mathrm{Br}(S)
the Brauer class of order
2
discussed in
[
Gee05
, Sec. 9.8]
.
Denote the associated twisted by
K3
surface by
\mathscr{X}
.
It is associated to a general double EPW quartic.
Then the twisted and untwisted Beauville–Voisin classes agree, i.e.

o_{S}=o_{\mathscr{X}}.

Proof.

Using the
Construction
described above, we obtain an isomorphism

Y\cong\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)

for a Mukai vector
v
of positive rank.
Furthermore, by twisting with
\mathcal{O}_{S}(n)
before applying
T_{\mathcal{L}}
, we can ensure that the rank of the Mukai vector is large enough to ensure that
r>\frac{v^{2}+2}{2}
.
In that case, any
\mu_{H}
-stable sheaf is locally free.
Since the moduli space of
\mu_{H}
-stable sheaves
\mathcal{M}_{H}(\mathscr{X},v)
is birational to
Y\cong\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v)
, we know that

\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{H}(\mathscr{X},v))\cong\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}(\mathscr{X},v))

and
\mathcal{M}_{H}(\mathscr{X},v)
admits a birational Lagrangian fibration.
Hence, it follows from
[
CLZZ25
, Prop. 3.1]
that there is a dense collection of constant cycle subvarieties of
\mathcal{M}_{H}(\mathscr{X},v)
and that points lying on them define the same class.
Hence, there exists a constant cycle subvariety
C
in
\mathcal{M}_{H}(\mathscr{X},v)
whose image in
Y
is a constant cycle subvariety.
Since points on constant cycle subvarieties in
Y
define the same generically defined class
o_{Y}\in\chow_{0}(Y)
by
[
Maz26
, Prop. 5.4]
, we have
[\mathcal{F}]=o_{Y}
for any
\mathcal{F}\in C
.
Finally, recall the definition of the twisted Beauville–Voisin class as

o_{\mathscr{X}}=\frac{c_{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}+\left(1-\frac{\deg c_{2}(\mathcal{F})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}\right)o_{S},

Since
[\mathcal{F}]=o_{Y}
is generically defined, so is
c_{2}(\mathcal{F})
and the only generically defined cycle on
S
is
o_{S}
, implying that
o_{\mathscr{X}}=o_{S}
.
∎

4.4.
Applications

Let
Y
be a double EPW quartic.
It admits an involution
\iota
and a generically defined Beauville–Voisin class
o_{Y}
, which is defined as the class of any point fixed by
\iota
.
This is well-defined by
[
Maz26
, Thm. 5.3]
.
The involution
\iota
induces an eigenspace decomposition

\chow_{0}(Y)=\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}\oplus\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\oplus\chow_{0}(Y)_{\mathrm{hom}}^{+}.

This induces a filtration

\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}\subset\mathbb{Q}o_{Y}\oplus\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\subset\chow_{0}(Y),

which is known to agree with Voisin’s filtration by
[
Maz26
, Prop. 5.12]
, and hence with Vial’s co-radical and the Shen–Yin–Zhao filtrations.
We provide another proof of this via the construction as moduli spaces.

The Chern character induces a map on
\chow_{0}(Y)
via the construction as a moduli space,

\chow_{0}(Y)\cong\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{(0,H,0)}(S,\beta))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ch}_{2}}\chow_{0}(S)

This map is induced by a correspondence, namely the second Chern character of a twisted universal family, which always exists.
The Bloch–Beilinson conjecture predicts that
\mathrm{ch}_{2}(F^{4}\chow_{0}(Y))\subset F^{4}\chow_{0}(S)=0
, meaning that
\mathrm{ch}_{2}
should be zero on
\chow_{0}(Y)^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}
.
This follows from the explicit description of the isomorphism
Y\cong\mathcal{M}_{(0,H,0)}(S,\beta)
and a key geometric property.
Two conics
c
and
c^{\prime}
with
\iota(\alpha(c))=\alpha(c^{\prime})
have the property that
c\cup c^{\prime}
is a hyperplane section in
D_{(L,M)}=\penalty\ \pi^{-1}(L\times\penalty\ M)
, as in the proof of
[
Maz26
, Thm. 5.3]
.
As
D_{(L,M)}
is the zero-section of a vector bundle on
X
, we can control
\mathcal{I}_{c/X}(1)
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
Y
be a general double EPW quartic and let
S
be either of the two K3 surfaces associated to
Y
.
Then the map

\chow_{0}(Y)\cong\chow_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{(0,H,0)}(S,\beta))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ch_{2}}}\chow_{0}(S)

is
0
on
\chow_{0}(Y)^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}
, maps
o_{Y}
to
o_{S}
, and induces an isomorphism

\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\cong\chow_{0}(S)_{\mathrm{hom}}.

Its inverse is given by the correspondence
\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{t}
composed with multiplication by
h^{2}
.

Proof.

First, notice that we do not need to worry about the Brauer class, since we are working with Chow groups with rational coefficients.
Using
[
CLZZ25
, Thm. 1.1]
, the surjectivity of the map on all of
\chow_{0}(Y)
follows from the argument following
Definition
3.11
or from the argument used in the proof of
[
OGr13
, Prop. 1.3]

We prove the injectivity on
\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}
before proving that
\mathrm{ch}_{2}
vanishes on
\chow_{0}(Y)^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}
.
Recall from
Theorem
4.1
that there is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration
\alpha\colon F(X)\to Y
from the Hilbert scheme of
(1,1)
conics on the Verra fourfold
X
.
Then the universal conic
P
induces a morphism

\psi\colon\chow_{1}(X)_{\mathrm{hom}}\xrightarrow{P_{\ast}^{t}}\chow_{2}(Y)^{-}_{\mathrm{hom}}\xrightarrow{\cdot h^{2}}\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ch}_{2}}\chow_{0}(S)_{\mathrm{hom}}.

Since the first two maps are isomorphisms by
Theorem
4.2
, it suffices to prove that the composition is injective.
We show that there is a morphism
\chow_{0}(S)\to\chow_{1}(X)_{\mathrm{hom}}
induced by a generically defined correspondence such that the composition is the identity.
To prove this, we show that this is the case in cohomology and then use the Franchetta property for
X\times X
from
Theorem
4.4
.
Consider the composition

\phi\colon\chow_{0}(S)_{\mathrm{hom}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{t}}\chow^{2}(Y)^{-}\xrightarrow{\cdot h^{2}}\chow_{0}(Y)^{-}\xrightarrow{P_{\ast}}\chow_{1}(X)_{\mathrm{hom}},

and define the primitive motive
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{pr}}^{4}(X)
of
X
as the motive cut out by the idempotent correspondence

\pi_{4}^{X}=\Delta_{X}-X\times x-x\times X-g_{1}\times g_{1}g_{2}^{2}-g_{2}\times g_{2}g_{1}^{2}-\frac{g_{1}^{2}\times g_{1}^{2}}{\deg g_{1}^{2}g_{2}^{2}}-\frac{g_{2}^{2}\times g_{2}^{2}}{\deg g_{1}^{2}g_{2}^{2}},

where the
g_{i}
are the two pullbacks of the hyperplane classes along the two projections
\pi_{i}\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
x\in X
is a point.
Let
\theta
be the endomorphism of
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{pr}}^{4}(X)(-1)
defined by
\theta=\penalty\ \pi_{4}^{X}\circ\phi\circ\psi\circ\pi_{4}^{X}
.
Since
H_{\mathrm{pr}}^{k}(X)=0
for
k\neq 4
, the cohomological realisation becomes

H^{4}_{\mathrm{pr}}(X)\xrightarrow{P^{t}_{\ast}}H_{\mathrm{pr}}^{2}(Y)\xrightarrow{\cdot h^{2}}H_{\mathrm{pr}}^{6}(Y)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ch}_{2}}H_{\mathrm{pr}}^{2}(S)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{t}}H_{\mathrm{pr}}^{2}(Y)^{-}\xrightarrow{\cdot h^{2}}H_{\mathrm{pr}}^{6}(Y)\xrightarrow{P_{\ast}}H_{\mathrm{pr}}^{4}(X).

Notice that for the very general
X
,
H_{\mathrm{pr}}^{4}(X)=H_{\mathrm{tr}}^{4}(X)
, which is a simple Hodge structure.
Hence, this endomorphism is a multiple of the identity for the very general
X
.
This holds for all
X
after spreading out.
The second Chern character of a universal family induces an isomorphism between
H^{2}(Y)
and the orthogonal complement of a class of square
2
in the Mukai lattice
\tilde{H}^{2}(S)
.
Furthermore, both
\mathrm{ch}_{2}
and
\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{t}
are isomorphisms on transcendental cohomology
[
Yos06
, Sec. 3.3]
.
Multiplication by
h^{2}
is an isomorphism by the Hard Lefschetz Theorem and
P_{\ast}
and
P_{\ast}^{t}
are isomorphisms by
[
Maz26
, Prop. 5.9]
.
Hence, the composition is a non-zero multiple of the identity and we may apply the Franchetta property for
X\times X
to deduce that this is also the case up to rational equivalence.

It remains to prove that the map vanishes on
\chow_{0}(Y)^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}
.
First, consider
o_{Y}
.
As the isomorphism
Y\cong\mathcal{M}
and the map
\mathrm{ch}_{2}
are generically defined, it must be sent to a multiple of
o_{S}
.
We know that the Mukai vector of any object in
\mathcal{M}_{(0,H,0)}(S,\beta)
is
(0,H,0)
, so
\mathrm{ch}_{2}(o_{Y})=\frac{1}{2}H^{2}=o_{S}
.
Next, consider a cycle of the form
y+\iota(y)
for
y\in Y
.
Let
c
and
c^{\prime}
be conics such that
\alpha(c)=y
and
\alpha(c^{\prime})=\iota(y)
.
Denote the lines that
c
and
c^{\prime}
project to under the two projections
\pi_{i}\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
by
L
and
M
.
The key geometric property that we will use is the fact that
c\cup c^{\prime}
is a hyperplane section in
D_{(L,M)}=\pi^{-1}(L\times M)
, as in the proof of
[
Maz26
, Thm. 5.3]
.
For the sake of clarity we denote
D_{(L,M)}
by
D
for the remainder of the proof.
From the short exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{I}_{D/X}(1,1)\to\mathcal{I}_{c/X}(1,1)\to\mathcal{I}_{c/D}(1,1)\to 0

and

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(1,0)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(0,1)\to\mathcal{I}_{D/X}(1,1)\to 0,

we see that
\pr(\mathcal{I}_{c/X}(1,1))=\pr(\mathcal{I}_{c/D}(1,1))
.
Let
H_{2}
be the pullback of a hyperplane along the second projection. From the exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{O}_{H_{2}}(0,1)\to\mathcal{O}_{H_{2}}(1,1)\to\mathcal{O}_{D}(1,1)\to 0

and

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(0,-1)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{H_{2}}\to 0

we obtain that
\pr(\mathcal{O}_{D}(1,1))=0
.
Finally, we using the fact that
c\cup c^{\prime}
is a hyperplane in
D
, we obtain

\mathcal{I}_{c/D}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{c^{\prime}/D}=\mathcal{I}_{c\cup c^{\prime}/D}=\mathcal{O}_{D}(-1,-1).

Twisting with
\mathcal{O}_{X}(2,2)
and applying the Chern character to the above, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{D}(1,1))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ch}_{2}(\mathcal{I}_{c/D}(1,1)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{c^{\prime}/D}(1,1))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}(\mathcal{I}_{c/D}(1,1)+c_{1}(\mathcal{I}_{c^{\prime}/D}(1,1))))^{2}-c_{2}(\mathcal{I}_{c/D}(1,1))-c_{2}(\mathcal{I}_{c^{\prime}/D}(1,1))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D}(1,1))^{2}-c_{2}(\mathcal{I}_{c/D}(1,1))-c_{2}(\mathcal{I}_{c^{\prime}/D}(1,1)).

Since
\pr(\mathcal{O}_{D}(1,1))=0
, we get that

c_{2}(\pr(\mathcal{I}_{c/X}(1,1)))+c_{2}(\pr(\mathcal{I}_{c^{\prime}/X}(1,1)))=0,

meaning that
\mathrm{ch}_{2}
composed with the isomorphism
Y\cong\mathcal{M}_{(0,H,0)}(S,\beta)
vanishes on cycles of the form
y+\iota(y)-2o_{Y}
, completing the proof.
∎

Let us now consider the filtrations from
Section
3.1
for double EPW quartics.
Recall that
o_{(S,\beta)}=o_{S}
by
Theorem
4.6
, so we may safely ignore the twisted Beauville–Voisin class.

Let
X
be the Verra fourfold associated to
Y
.
We define a filtration on
\chow_{1}(X)
analogous to the filtration defined by Shen–Yin in the setting of Fano varieties of lines on cubic fourfolds
[
SY20
]
.
Recall that a
uniruled divisor
in a
2n
-dimensional hyperkähler variety
T
is a divisor
D\subset T
that admits a rational map to a
(2n-2)
-dimensional variety
B
such that the fibres of
D\dashrightarrow B
are rational curves.
It is known that uniruled divisors exist in hyperkähler varieties of
K3^{[2]}
-type
[
CMP24
]
.

Definition 4.8
.

Define a filtration by subsets
S^{\text{SY}}_{i}\chow_{1}(X)
as the union of all classes of the form

k\xi+[c_{1}]+\ldots+[c_{i}],

where
k\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\xi
is the class of a conic such that
\alpha(\xi)
lies on a constant cycle surface, and
c_{j}
are conics such that
\alpha(c_{j})
lies on a fixed uniruled divisor in
Y
.
Define the
Shen–Yin filtration
as

S^{\text{SY}}_{i}\chow_{0}(Y)\coloneq\left\langle\left\{\alpha_{\ast}[c]\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ c\in F(X)\text{ and }[c]\in S^{\mathrm{SY}}_{i}\chow_{1}(X)\right\}\right\rangle.

This is well-defined, as all points on constant cycle surfaces have the same class by
[
Maz26
, Prop. 5.4]
.
Furthermore, it is independent of the choice of uniruled divisor by
[
SY20
, Lem. 1.1]
and exhaustive by
[
Lat20
, Cor. 4.4]
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
Y
be a general double EPW quartic.
Then, all four filtrations, the Shen–Yin, Voisin’s, Shen–Yin–Zhao, and Vial’s co-radical filtration, agree,

Proof.

\chow_{1}(X)\xrightarrow{P^{t}_{\ast}}\chow_{2}(Y)\xrightarrow{\cdot h^{2}}\chow_{0}(Y)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ch}_{2}}\chow_{0}(S),

It is known that

Remark 4.11
.

While the case
m=1
follows from
Theorem
4.2
, it also admits a more elementary proof that is independent of
Theorem
4.2
.
This alternative proof requires only
[
Maz26
, Lem. 5.1]
, which is itself independent of the rest of
loc. cit.

However, this method does not generalise to
m>1
.

Given two conics
c,c^{\prime}
with
[c]=[c^{\prime}]
, applying
P_{\ast}^{t}
means that
P_{\ast}^{t}(c)=P_{\ast}^{t}(c^{\prime})
.
Squaring and using
[
Maz26
, Lem. 5.1]
we obtain
(
P_{\ast}^{t}(c)=D_{c}
in the notation of
loc. cit.
)

\alpha_{\ast}(h_{F}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{8}\ell_{j})+3m[\iota(\alpha(c))]-m[\alpha(c)]=\alpha_{\ast}(h_{F}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{8}\ell_{j}^{\prime})+3m[\iota(\alpha(c^{\prime}))]-m[\alpha(c^{\prime})],

where
\ell_{j}
and
\ell_{j}^{\prime}
are the pencils of conics contained in the del Pezzo surfaces
D_{(L,M)}
and
D_{(L^{\prime},M^{\prime})}
associated to
c
and
c^{\prime}
, respectively.
We claim that there is an equality
\alpha_{\ast}(h_{F}\cdot\penalty\ \sum_{j=1}^{8}\ell_{j})=\alpha_{\ast}(h_{F}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{8}\ell_{j}^{\prime})
.
Indeed, the
8
points in
Y
associated to the
\ell_{j}
are contracted to
4
points in
D_{1}^{\overline{A}}
, which is a degree
4
cover of
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
and these points are contracted to a point in
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
, meaning that the two classes are pulled back from
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
and hence agree.
Rearranging proves that
\alpha_{\ast}[c]=\alpha_{\ast}[c^{\prime}]
.
