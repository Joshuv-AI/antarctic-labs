Resolving the Edge of a Quantum Pyramid
Alvan Arulandu

Abstract
Standing on the shoulders of giants, we resolve the quantum pyramids conjecture, confirming the globally information-optimal measurement for an ensemble of equiangular equiprobable pure states ( 6 ) . We do so by proving the remaining entropy inequalities from ( 10 ) which certify optimality for obtuse and flat pyramids. For obtuse pyramids, our key contribution is a rigorous proof that local minimizers of the corresponding entropy inequality can not have three distinct coordinate values. We show that eliminating this family can be reduced to a neat algebraic reciprocal inequality relating branches of the Lambert W W function, which may be of independent interest. For flat pyramids, we specifically prove a tight ℓ p \ell^{p} inequality for zero-sum vectors which was recently conjectured, analytically proved for dimension d = 3 d=3 , and computationally verified for d ≤ 200 d\leq 200 by ( 9 ) . We prove this bound for all d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 via a technique in symmetric inequalities known as the equal variables method.

Introduction

The story of the quantum pyramid begins with a fundamental question in quantum information:

What is the maximal information that a measurement can extract

from an ensemble of quantum states?

This basic quantity, called the
accessible information

(1)

If

Lemma 2.4

Let

2.2

At this point, we seek to construct a

\displaystyle y^{\top}\nabla_{x}^{2}\mathcal{L}_{{\tilde{\nu}_{0}}}(x;\lambda)y

\displaystyle=M_{11}\alpha^{2}+2M_{12}\alpha\beta+M_{22}\beta^{2}

\displaystyle M_{11}

\displaystyle\equiv kh_{0}+\frac{k^{2}}{s^{2}}h_{2}+2{\tilde{\nu}_{0}}k^{2}\left(1+1/s\right)^{2}

\displaystyle M_{12}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{kr}{s^{2}}h_{2}+2{\tilde{\nu}_{0}}k\left(1+1/s\right)\left(1+r/s\right)

\displaystyle P_{k}(r,s,c)

, and

\displaystyle P_{1}(r,s,c)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{\sigma^{4}}K(\sigma,t)

\displaystyle\equiv\kappa^{2}(\sigma^{2}+t^{2}+1)+\kappa(\sigma^{2}(t-1)-\sigma(t^{2}+1)-t(t-1))+\sigma t(t-\sigma-1)

Thus, it suffices to show that

on the curve (

We now use yet another re-parameterization to reduce this to a simple algebraic inequality. Let

and
\beta\equiv\kappa/\sigma
. We first claim that

and

Similarly,

Noting that the entire pre-factor is positive, it suffices to show that:

\frac{1}{\kappa(1-\kappa)}<\frac{1}{\alpha(1+\alpha)}+\frac{1}{\beta(\beta-1)}

We prove in Lemma

Lemma 2.8

Suppose

Proof.

From Lemma

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}^{2}\ln x_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=-p^{\prime}\ln p^{\prime}-(m-1)q^{\prime}\ln q^{\prime}=\ln(r'^2+m-1)-\frac{2r^{\prime 2}\ln r^{\prime}}{r^{\prime 2}+m-1}\equiv H_{m}(r^{\prime})

\displaystyle S(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{p^{\prime}}-(m-1)\sqrt{q^{\prime}})^{2}=\frac{(r^{\prime}-m+1)^{2}}{r^{\prime 2}+m-1}\equiv R_{m}(r^{\prime})

We now argue that

as a function of

\displaystyle=-\frac{2r^{\prime}\ln r^{\prime}}{(r^{\prime}-m+1)(r^{\prime}+1)}

\displaystyle=2\ln r^{\prime}+1-\frac{m-1}{r^{\prime 2}}\geq 0\quad

for

, and

\displaystyle f_{{\tilde{\nu}_{0}}(p)}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}^{2}\ln x_{j}^{2}+{\tilde{\nu}_{0}}(p)S(x)^{2}=H_{m}(r^{\prime})+{\tilde{\nu}_{0}}(p)R_{m}(r^{\prime})

Lemma 3.1
.

For

\displaystyle\|x\|_{2\alpha}^{2\alpha}=\sum_{i\in[n]}u_{i}^{2\alpha}+\sum_{i\in[m]}v_{i}^{2\alpha}\geq S(u)^{2\alpha}+S(v)^{2\alpha}=2s^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i\in[n]}u_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i\in[m]}v_{i}^{2}\leq S(u)^{2}+S(v)^{2}=2s^{2}

∎

3.2
Three-Value Reduction via the Equal Variables Method

As before, we begin by handling various families of minimizers.

Lemma 3.3
.

Define the following Lagrange function along with its respective gradient:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{2\alpha}^{2\alpha}-\lambda_{1}S(x)-\lambda_{2}(\|x\|_{2}^{2}-1)

(67)

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\mathcal{L}(x;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=2\alpha x_{j}|x_{j}|^{2(\alpha-1)}-2\lambda_{2}x_{j}-\lambda_{1}

, then there exists a choice of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(x;\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=0
.

Proof.

This follows by direct computation and standard calculus.
∎

We first analyze minimizers with a zero coordinate via a similar argument to the proof of Lemma
2.3
for the obtuse case.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\alpha\in(1/2,1)\cup(1,\infty)

Let

such that

. Then, from Lemma
3.3
,

\lambda_{1}=0

(68)

meaning

for some

. Since
\|x\|_{2}^{2}=1
and

, there are the same number of positive and negative coordinates, say

of each. Then,

Then,

We now derive the following minimizer family.

Lemma 3.5

(Equation (22) of
(

)
)

For any

Since the form in Lemma

with no zero coordinate.
From Lemma
3.3

We now claim that

Lemma 3.7

x
with no zero coordinate. From Lemma

\displaystyle\|x\|_{2\alpha}^{2\alpha}\geq\inf\{k_{0}s_{0}^{2\alpha}+(d-k_{0}-1)s_{1}^{2\alpha}+s_{2}^{2\alpha}:k_{0}s_{0}=(d-k_{0}-1)s_{1}+s_{2},\,k_{0}s_{0}^{2}+(d-k_{0}-1)s_{1}^{2}+s_{2}^{2}=1\}

\alpha

\beta

(0,1)

(0,1)

). Applying the upper bounds in (

) and (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha(1+\alpha)}+\frac{1}{\beta(\beta-1)}-\frac{1}{x(1-x)}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{A(x)(1+A(x))}+\frac{1}{B(x)(B(x)-1)}-\frac{1}{x(1-x)}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}(Q(x)-x^{3/2}\sqrt{x^{3}+8}\,P(x))}{x(x-1)(x^{3/2}-\sqrt{x^{3}+8})(x^{3/2}+2\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{x^{3}+8})(x^{3}+6x+2)}

(84)
