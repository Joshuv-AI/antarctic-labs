S-packing chromatic critical paths and cycles
Gülnaz Boruzanlı Ekinci, Csilla Bujtás, Didem Gözüpek, Aslıhan Gür

Abstract
Let S = ( s 1 , s 2 , … ) S=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots) be a non-decreasing sequence of positive integers. For a graph G G with vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) , a labeling ϕ : V ⁡ ( G ) → { 1 , … , k } \phi\colon V(G)\to\{1,\ldots,k\} is an S S -packing k k -coloring if, whenever two distinct vertices u , v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) u,v\in V(G) are assigned the same color i i , their distance in G G is greater than s i s_{i} . The minimum k k for which G G admits such a coloring is the S S -packing chromatic number of G G . A graph G G is χ S \chi_{S} -vertex-critical if χ S ​ ( G − v ) < χ S ​ ( G ) \chi_{S}(G-v)<\chi_{S}(G) for every v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , and it is χ S \chi_{S} -critical if χ S ​ ( H ) < χ S ​ ( G ) \chi_{S}(H)<\chi_{S}(G) holds for every proper subgraph H H of G G . In this paper, the exact value of χ S ​ ( P n ) \chi_{S}(P_{n}) is determined for every path of order n n and for every packing sequence S S where s i < 2 i s_{i}<2^{i} holds for each entry s i s_{i} . As a consequence, χ S \chi_{S} -critical and χ S \chi_{S} -vertex-critical paths are identified for each such sequence S S . Introduction

A
packing sequence

S=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots)
is a non-decreasing sequence of positive integers. Consider a graph
G
with vertex set
V(G)
. A labeling

\phi\colon V(G)\to[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}
is an
S
-packing
k
-coloring

if, whenever two distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
are assigned the same color
i
, their distance in
G
satisfies
d_{G}(u,v)>s_{i}
.
The minimum
k
for which
G
admits such a coloring is the

S
-packing chromatic number
of
G
, denoted by
\chi_{S}(G)

[
9
]
.

The notion of
S
-packing coloring is very general. In particular, when

S=(k,k,\ldots)
, one obtains the class of
k
-distance colorings

[
15
]
, where
k=1
coincides with the classical graph coloring.
Furthermore, taking
S=(1,2,3,\ldots)
yields the standard packing coloring
[
8
,
4
]
. In recent years, the concept of
S
-packing coloring has drawn considerable attention
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
10
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
18
,
21
]
.

Several works in the literature have investigated criticality in packing colorings
[
13
,
3
,
7
]
. As packing colorings have been generalized to
S
-packing colorings, the notion of criticality has also been extended to
S
-packing colorings. In particular, vertex criticality in
S
-packing coloring was introduced by Holub et al.
[
11
]
, where a graph
G
is
\chi_{S}
-vertex-critical
if
\chi_{S}(G-v)<\chi_{S}(G)
for every
v\in V(G)
. Later, a characterization of
\chi_{S}
-vertex-critical graphs with
\chi_{S}(G)=4
for a family of packing sequences was provided by Klavžar et al.
[
12
]
. Recently, criticality in
S
-packing coloring was introduced in
[
1
]
, where a graph
G
is called
\chi_{S}
-critical
if
\chi_{S}(H)<\chi_{S}(G)

for every proper subgraph
H
of
G
. In the same paper,
\chi_{S}
-critical cycles were characterized under different conditions,
and the remaining cases were left as an open problem. In this paper, among other results, we address this problem and obtain characterizations of
\chi_{S}
-critical cycles for several cases.

1.1
Terminology

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph, where
V(G)
and
E(G)
correspond to the vertex and edge set of
G
, respectively. The distance between two vertices
u
and
v
in
G
is denoted by
d_{G}(u,v)
.
Let
P_{n}
and
C_{n}
stand for the path and the cycle of order
n
, respectively.

The set of all packing sequences is denoted by
\mathcal{S}=\{(s_{1},s_{2},...):s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq...\}
. Throughout this paper, we assume that all packing sequences are infinite. The notation
{\cal S}_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\,\overline{a_{i}},\,a_{i+1},\ldots}

stands for the set of packing sequences
(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots)
satisfying
s_{j}=a_{j}
for
j\neq i

and
a_{i}\leq s_{i}\leq a_{i+1}
. For example,
{\cal S}_{1,2,\overline{4},7}
is the set of packing sequences with
s_{1}=1
,
s_{2}=2
,
4\leq s_{3}\leq 7
, and
s_{4}=7
. Note that
{\cal S}_{1}
is the set of all packing sequences
(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots)
with
s_{1}=1
.

Next, we introduce the following family of packing sequences. For an integer
k\geq 2
, let us define

{\cal S}(k)=\{(s_{1},s_{2},\dots)\colon 2^{i-1}\leq s_{i}<2^{i}\mbox{ for all }i\leq k-1\mbox{ and }s_{k}<2^{k-1}\}.

(1)

Then
{\cal S}(1)
is not defined, and
{\cal S}(2)={\cal S}_{1,1}
. In general
{\cal S}(k)\subseteq{\cal S}_{1}
.

The following observation follows directly from the definition and will be used in the sequel.

Observation 1.1
.

If
S=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots)
and
S^{\prime}=(s_{1}^{\prime},s_{2}^{\prime},\dots)
are two packing sequences such that
s_{i}\leq s_{i}^{\prime}
for every positive integer
i
, then
\chi_{S}(G)\leq\chi_{S^{\prime}}(G)
holds for every graph
G
.

1.2
Summary of results

The earlier studies left open the problem of determining the
S
-packing chromatic number of paths except when
S
belongs to a very specific subfamily of
{\cal S}
. In
[
1
]
, this question was posed directly. In this paper, we determine
\chi_{S}(P_{n})
for all packing sequences
S=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots)
where the entries are not too large. More precisely, we consider all sequences where
s_{i}<2^{i}
holds for each
i\leq\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
. Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
(ii) can be summarized in the following way.
If
S=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots)
and
2^{i-1}\leq s_{i}<2^{i}
holds for each
i\leq\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
, then

\chi_{S}(P_{n})=\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1.

Otherwise,
S\in{\cal S}(k)
for an integer
k\leq\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
, and

\chi_{S}(P_{n})=k
.

Based on these results, we identify
\chi_{S}
-critical and
\chi_{S}
-vertex-critical paths for the considered packing sequences. In particular, we show that if
S\in{\cal S}(k)
, then a path
P_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical (and
\chi_{S}
-vertex-critical) if and only if
n\in\{2^{0},2^{1},\dots,2^{k-1}\}
.

In
[
1
]
,
\chi_{S}
-critical cycles were identified for the following families of packing sequences.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
1
]

If
n\geq 3
, then the following hold.

(i)

If
S\in\mathcal{S}_{1,1}
, then
C_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical if and only if

is odd.

(ii)

If
S\in\mathcal{S}_{1,2,2}
, then
C_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical if and only if
n\in\{3,5\}
.

(iii)

If
S\in\mathcal{S}_{1,\overline{2},3}
, then
C_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical if and only if
n\not\equiv 0\pmod{4}
.

Observe that Theorem
1.2
characterizes
\chi_{S}
-critical cycles for
S\in{\cal S}(2)\cup{\cal S}(3)
. This paper concentrates on the next case and solves the problem for every
S\in{\cal S}(4)
.
The seven cases in Theorems
4.1
,
4.2
, and
4.3
together provide a complete characterization of
\chi_{S}
-critical cycles for
S\in{\cal S}(4)
. For example, it is proved that for every

,
C_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical if and only if

is
\chi_{S}
-critical if and only if

and

. In addition, Theorem

identifies
\chi_{S}
-vertex-critical cycles for every

.

Preliminaries

In this section, several auxiliary results are established to provide insight into the problem and to be used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following statements are true for every positive integer
n
and packing sequence
S\in{\cal S}_{1}
.

(i)

There exists an
S
-packing coloring of the path
P_{n}\colon v_{1}\dots v_{n}
with
\chi_{S}(P_{n})
colors such that color
1
is assigned to every vertex
v_{i}
with an odd index
i
.

(ii)

If
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
and
S^{\prime}=(s_{1}^{\prime},s_{2}^{\prime},\dots)
are two packing sequences such that
s_{i}^{\prime}=\lfloor\frac{s_{i+1}}{2}\rfloor
for every positive integer
i
, then it holds for every
n\geq 2
that

\chi_{S}(P_{n})=\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor})+1.

(2)

Proof.

(i) Set
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
and let
\phi
be an
S
-packing coloring of
G=P_{n}
. Construct the path
G^{\prime}
by removing all vertices with color
1
from
G
and forming a path
G^{\prime}:v_{i_{1}}\dots v_{i_{p}}
with the remaining vertices such that
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}
. Then, to get a path
G^{\prime\prime}
of order
n
and a coloring
\phi^{\prime\prime}
, we insert new vertices with color
1
into
G^{\prime}
. Formally, we
define
V(G^{\prime\prime})=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
such that
u_{j}=v_{i_{j/2}}
holds for every even index
j\leq n
and
u_{1},u_{3},\dots
are new vertices. The remaining vertices of
G^{\prime}
(if they exist) are omitted from
G^{\prime\prime}
. The edges of
G^{\prime\prime}
are chosen so that
G^{\prime\prime}\colon u_{1}\dots u_{n}
is a path. Finally, a vertex coloring
\phi^{\prime\prime}
of
G^{\prime\prime}
is defined such that

\phi^{\prime\prime}(u_{j})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $j$ is odd},\\
\phi(v_{i_{j/2}}),&\text{if $j$ is even}.\\
\end{cases}

We state that
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is an
S
-packing coloring of
G^{\prime\prime}
. The
S
-packing coloring condition clearly holds for the vertices assigned color
1
. If
\phi^{\prime\prime}(u_{x})=\phi^{\prime\prime}(u_{y})=\ell\neq 1
, then
x
and
y
are even integers, and
u_{x}=v_{i_{x/2}}
,
u_{y}=v_{i_{y/2}}
hold. Further, the distance of
v_{i_{x/2}}
and
v_{i_{y/2}}
is
d^{\prime}=|\frac{x}{2}-\frac{y}{2}|
in
G^{\prime}
. It follows by the construction of
G^{\prime}
that
G
contains at most
d^{\prime}
vertices with color
1
between these two vertices and consequently

d_{G}(v_{i_{x/2}},v_{i_{y/2}})\leq 2d^{\prime}=|x-y|.

The definition of
\phi^{\prime\prime}
gives
\phi(v_{i_{x/2}})=\phi(v_{i_{y/2}})=\ell
, so the distance of these two vertices in
G
is at least
s_{\ell}+1
. These facts together imply

d_{G^{\prime\prime}}(u_{x},u_{y})=|x-y|\geq d_{G}(v_{i_{x/2}},v_{i_{y/2}})\geq s_{\ell}+1.

This proves that
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is an
S
-packing coloring of the path
P_{n}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
does not use more colors than
\phi
. Therefore, if
\phi
uses
\chi_{S}(P_{n})
colors, so does
\phi^{\prime\prime}
. This completes the proof of (i).

(ii) According to statement (i), we may start with an
S
-packing coloring
\phi
of
G=P_{n}
where every vertex with an odd index receives the color
1
and
\phi
uses
k=\chi_{S}(P_{n})
colors. When we construct the path
G^{\prime}
by removing the vertices with color
1
from
G
, only the vertices of even indices remain. Therefore,
G^{\prime}:v_{i_{1}}\dots v_{i_{p}}
is a path on
p=n^{\prime}=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
vertices and
i_{j}=2j
for every
j\in[n^{\prime}]
.

Define the coloring
\phi^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
such that
\phi^{\prime}(v_{i_{j}})=\phi(v_{2j})-1
for every
j\in[n^{\prime}]
. Then
\phi^{\prime}
uses
k-1
colors. We prove that
\phi^{\prime}
is an
S^{\prime}
-packing coloring of
G^{\prime}
. Let
\phi^{\prime}(v_{i_{a}})=\phi^{\prime}(v_{i_{b}})=\ell
and assume that
a<b
. Hence
\phi(v_{2a})=\phi(v_{2b})=\ell+1
also holds. These facts together with
s_{\ell+1}\geq 2s_{\ell}^{\prime}
imply

d_{G^{\prime}}(v_{i_{a}},v_{i_{b}})=\frac{d_{G}(v_{2a},v_{2b})}{2}=b-a\geq\left\lceil\frac{s_{\ell+1}+1}{2}\right\rceil\geq\left\lceil\frac{2s_{\ell}^{\prime}+1}{2}\right\rceil=s_{\ell}^{\prime}+1,

and we may infer that
\phi^{\prime}
is an
S^{\prime}
-packing coloring of
G^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\chi_{S}(P_{n})\geq\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor})+1
.

To prove the other direction, we start with an
S^{\prime}
-packing coloring
\varphi^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}=P_{n^{\prime}}\colon u_{1}\dots u_{n^{\prime}}
where
n^{\prime}=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. We assume that
\varphi^{\prime}
uses
k^{\prime}=\chi_{S^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
colors. Let
G=P_{n}\colon v_{1}\dots v_{n}
and let
\varphi
be a vertex coloring of
G
such that
\varphi(v_{i})=1
if
i
is odd, and
\varphi(v_{i})=\varphi^{\prime}(u_{i/2})+1
if
i
is even. Then
\varphi
uses
k^{\prime}+1
colors. Now, we show that
\varphi
is an
S
-packing coloring of
G
. If
\varphi(v_{i})=\varphi(v_{j})=1
, then
d_{G}(v_{i},v_{j})\geq 2
by definition. If
\varphi(v_{i})=\varphi(v_{j})=\ell
for an integer
\ell\geq 2
, then
i
and
j
are even, and
\varphi^{\prime}(u_{i/2})=\varphi^{\prime}(u_{j/2})=\ell-1
. For this case,
d_{G}(v_{i},v_{j})=2d_{G^{\prime}}(u_{i/2},u_{j/2})
also holds, and we may infer

d_{G}(v_{i},v_{j})=2d_{G^{\prime}}(u_{i/2},u_{j/2})\geq 2(s^{\prime}_{\ell-1}+1)=2\left(\left\lfloor\frac{s_{\ell}}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)\geq s_{\ell}+1.

Since this inequality holds for every two vertices sharing a color
\ell
different from
1
in
G
, we conclude that
\varphi
is an
S
-packing coloring of
G
and therefore
\chi_{S}(P_{n})\leq\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor})+1
. This completes the proof for (ii).

Lemma 2.2
.

The following statements hold for every positive integer
n
and packing sequence
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
.

(i)

If
n
is even, then
\chi_{S}(P_{n+1})=\chi_{S}(P_{n})
.

(ii)

If
S^{\prime}=(1,s_{2}^{\prime},\dots)
such that
s_{i}^{\prime}\in\{2\lceil\frac{s_{i}+1}{2}\rceil-1,s_{i}\}
for every
i\geq 2
, then
\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{n})=\chi_{S}(P_{n})
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
S\in{\cal S}_{1}
,
P_{n}\colon v_{1}\dots v_{n}
, and
\chi_{S}(P_{n})=k
. By Lemma
2.1
(i), there is an
S
-packing
k
-coloring
\phi
of
P_{n}
that assigns color
1
to every vertex
v_{i}
with an odd index
i
. If
n
is even, then
\phi(v_{n})\neq 1
, and
\phi
can easily be extended to an
S
-packing
k
-coloring of
P_{n+1}
. Then
\chi_{S}(P_{n+1})\leq\chi_{S}(P_{n})
. As the reverse inequality holds by definition,
\chi_{S}(P_{n+1})=\chi_{S}(P_{n})
follows.

(ii)

If
s_{i}
is odd,
2\lceil\frac{s_{i}+1}{2}\rceil-1=s_{i}
, and
s_{i}^{\prime}=s_{i}
. If
s_{i}
is even, then
2\lceil\frac{s_{i}+1}{2}\rceil-1=s_{i}+1
and
s_{i}^{\prime}
equals either
s_{i}
or
s_{i}+1
.
Let
\phi
be an
S
-packing coloring of
P_{n}\colon v_{1}\dots v_{n}
such that every vertex with an odd index receives color
1
. Such a coloring with
\chi_{S}(P_{n})
colors exists by Lemma
2.1
(i). Then the distance between any two vertices
v_{x}
and
v_{y}
sharing a color
\ell\neq 1
is even. Therefore, if
s_{\ell}
is even, then

d(v_{x},v_{y})\geq s_{\ell}+2\geq s_{\ell}^{\prime}+1;

and if
s_{\ell}
is odd, then
s_{\ell}^{\prime}=s_{\ell}
directly implies
d(v_{x},v_{y})\geq s_{\ell}^{\prime}+1
. We conclude that
\phi
is an
S^{\prime}
-packing coloring and
\chi_{S}(P_{n})\geq\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{n})
. The equality then follows by Observation
1.1
.

∎

.

Let
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
and

be packing sequences such that
s_{i}^{\prime}=\lfloor\frac{s_{i+1}}{2}\rfloor
for every positive integer
i
. If

is an integer, the following statements hold.

(i)

\chi_{S}(C_{2n})\leq\chi_{S^{\prime}}(C_{n})+1
.

(ii)

If the cycle
C_{2n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical, then
C_{n}
is
\chi_{S^{\prime}}
-critical.

Proof.

(i)

Consider an
S^{\prime}
-packing coloring
\phi^{\prime}
of
C_{n}
with
k^{\prime}=\chi_{S^{\prime}}(C_{n})
colors. We construct a vertex coloring
\phi
for
C_{2n}
as in the proof of Lemma

(ii). That is, let
G^{\prime}=C_{n}\colon u_{1}\dots u_{n}u_{1}
and

. If
i
is odd, let
\phi(v_{i})=1
. If
i
is even, set
\phi(v_{i})=\phi^{\prime}(u_{i/2})+1
. We state that
\phi

-packing coloring of

i
and

are even integers and
\phi(v_{i})=\phi(v_{j})=\ell

\phi^{\prime}(u_{i/2})=\phi^{\prime}(u_{j/2})=\ell-1

verifies that

is an

-packing coloring of

that uses

colors. Therefore,
\chi_{S}(C_{2n})\leq\chi_{S^{\prime}}(C_{n})+1
.

(ii)

By Lemma
2.1
(ii),
\chi_{S}(P_{2n})=\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{n})+1
. If

is

-critical, then

\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{n})=\chi_{S}(P_{2n})-1<\chi_{S}(C_{2n})-1\leq\chi_{S^{\prime}}(C_{n}).

Therefore,

is

-critical as stated.

3
S
-packing chromatic number and criticality of paths

In this section we study the
S
-packing chromatic number and
\chi_{S}
-criticality of paths. In particular, we give exact formulas for
\chi_{S}(P_{n})
for every
n
and
S\in\bigcup_{k=2}^{\infty}{\cal S}(k)
, and identify all
\chi_{S}
-critical paths when
S
belongs to this class. The section is concluded with the characterization of
\chi_{S}
-vertex-critical paths for each sequence
S\in\bigcup_{k=2}^{\infty}{\cal S}(k)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer and
P_{n}
be a path. If
S\in{\cal S}(k)
, then

\chi_{S}(P_{n})=\min\{k,\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1\}.

In particular, if
n\geq 2^{k-1}
also holds, then
\chi_{S}(P_{n})=k
.

Proof.

Let
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
be a packing sequence from
{\cal S}(k)
.
The proof proceeds by induction on
k
. If
k=2
, then
S\in{\cal S}_{1,1}
and
\chi_{S}(P_{n})=2
if
n\geq 2
and
\chi_{S}(P_{1})=1
. We may now assume that
k\geq 3
.

We set

S^{\prime}=\left(\left\lfloor\frac{s_{2}}{2}\right\rfloor,\left\lfloor\frac{s_{3}}{2}\right\rfloor,\dots\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad n^{\prime}=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor.

The condition on
s_{2}
gives
2\leq s_{2}<4
and hence
\lfloor\frac{s_{2}}{2}\rfloor=1
. We infer that
S^{\prime}\in{\cal S}_{1}
. Similarly, every entry
s_{i}^{\prime}=\lfloor\frac{s_{i+1}}{2}\rfloor
with
2\leq i\leq k-2
in
S^{\prime}
satisfies
2^{i-1}\leq s_{i}^{\prime}<2^{i}
, and
s_{k-1}^{\prime}<2^{k-2}
. Then
S^{\prime}\in{\cal S}(k-1)
. Let
k^{\prime}=k-1
. By the hypothesis,
\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{n^{\prime}})=\min\{k^{\prime},\lfloor\log_{2}(n^{\prime})\rfloor+1\}
. By Lemma
2.1
(ii),
\chi_{S}(P_{n})=\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{n^{\prime}})+1
. Using also the definitions of
n^{\prime}
and
k^{\prime}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\chi_{S}(P_{n})=\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{n^{\prime}})+1

\displaystyle=\min\{k^{\prime},\lfloor\log_{2}(n^{\prime})\rfloor+1\}+1

\displaystyle=\min\{k-1,\lfloor\log_{2}(n)-1\rfloor+1\}+1

\displaystyle=\min\{k,\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1\}.

This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If
S\in{\cal S}(k)
, then for every path
P_{n}\colon v_{1}\dots v_{n}
, an
S
-packing coloring with
\chi_{S}(P_{n})
colors can be obtained in the following way.

\phi(v_{i})=\begin{cases}j,&\text{if $i\equiv 2^{j-1}\pmod{2^{j}}$ and $1\leq j\leq k-1$},\\
k,&\text{if $i\equiv 0\pmod{2^{k-1}}$.}\\
\end{cases}

(3)

Note that the color of a vertex
v_{i}
can be easily identified if we consider the last non-zero digit in the binary form of
i
. It also shows that every vertex of
P_{n}
is colored. Further, for any two vertices sharing a color
j\leq k-1
, their distance is at least
2^{j}\geq s_{j}+1
; and if both vertices are colored with
k
, the distance is at least
2^{k-1}\geq s_{k}+1
. If
n\geq 2^{k-1}
, then
\phi
uses
k=\chi_{S}(P_{n})
colors; otherwise, the number of colors is
\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1=\chi_{S}(P_{n})
.

For packing sequences not contained in
\bigcup_{k=2}^{\infty}{\cal S}(k)
, we present the following results. Note that Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
(ii) together determine
\chi_{S}(P_{n})
for every
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
satisfying
s_{i}<2^{i}
for all
i\geq 2
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
be a packing sequence and
P_{n}
be a path.

(i)

If there exists an index
i
such that
s_{i}<2^{i-1}
and
k=\min\{i\colon s_{i}<2^{i-1}\}
, then

\chi_{S}(P_{n})\geq\min\{k,\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1\}.

(ii)

If
2^{i-1}\leq s_{i}<2^{i}
holds for every
2\leq i\leq\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
, then

\chi_{S}(P_{n})=\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1.

(iii)

If
2^{i-1}\leq s_{i}
holds for every index
2\leq i\leq\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
, then

\chi_{S}(P_{n})\geq\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1.

Proof.

(i)

Let
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
be a packing sequence that satisfies the conditions in (i), and define
S^{\prime}=(1,s_{2}^{\prime},\dots)
such that
s_{i}^{\prime}=2^{i-1}
holds for every
1\leq i\leq k-1
and
s_{j}^{\prime}=s_{j}
for all
j\geq k
. In particular,
s_{p}\geq s_{p}^{\prime}
holds for every positive integer
p
. Observation
1.1
therefore implies
\chi_{S}(P_{n})\geq\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{n})
. Since
S^{\prime}\in{\cal S}(k)
, Theorem
3.1
gives
\chi_{S^{\prime}}(P_{n})=\min\{k,\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1\}
and statement (i) follows.

(ii)

Set
k=\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
. Then
2^{k-1}\leq n<2^{k}
. First observe that the coloring (
3
) applied for the path
P_{n}\colon v_{1}\dots v_{n}
is an
S
-packing coloring of
P_{n}
which uses
k
colors. Consequently,
\chi_{S}(P_{n})\leq k
. On the other hand, define the packing sequence
S^{\prime\prime}=(1,s_{2}^{\prime\prime},\dots)
such that
s_{i}^{\prime\prime}=s_{i}
for all indices except that
s_{k}^{\prime\prime}=s_{k-1}
. Hence
s_{i}^{\prime\prime}\leq s_{i}
holds for every
i
and Observation
1.1
implies
\chi_{S^{\prime\prime}}(P_{n})\leq\chi_{S}(P_{n})
. By the conditions in (ii), Theorem
3.1
can be applied to
S^{\prime\prime}
,
P_{n}
, and
k
. This yields

\chi_{S^{\prime\prime}}(P_{n})=\min\{k,\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1\}.

Since
k=\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
, we may derive

\chi_{S}(P_{n})\geq\chi_{S^{\prime\prime}}(P_{n})=\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1=k,

which completes the proof for the equality in (ii).

(iii)

Under the conditions of (iii), we may define a packing sequence
S^{*}=(1,s_{2}^{*},\dots)
, where
s_{i}^{*}=2^{i-1}
for every index
2\leq i\leq\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
, and
s_{i}^{*}=s_{i}
for
i>\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
. By the equality stated in (ii),
\chi_{S^{*}}(P_{n})=\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
. As
s_{i}^{*}\leq s_{i}
for all indices, we may deduce statement (iii) from Observation
1.1
.

∎

Having Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
(ii) in hand, we are now able to identify the
\chi_{S}
-critical paths for every packing sequence
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
satisfying (i)
S\in\bigcup_{k=2}^{\infty}{\cal S}(k)
or (ii)

for each

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
be a packing sequence and
k\geq 2
be an integer.

(i)

If
S\in{\cal S}(k)
, then a path
P_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical if and only if
n\in\{2^{0},2^{1},\dots,2^{k-1}\}
.

(ii)

If
2^{i-1}\leq s_{i}<2^{i}
holds for every entry
s_{i}
, then a path
P_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical if and only if
n\in\{2^{j}\colon j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
.

Proof.

By definition,
P_{1}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical for every
S\in{\cal S}
. From now on, we consider only paths of order at least two.

(i)

Let
S\in{\cal S}(k)
and
n\geq 2
. Assume first that the path
G=P_{n}\colon v_{1}\dots v_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical. By removing the edge
v_{1}v_{2}
from
G
we obtain
G-v_{1}v_{2}
, which is the disjoint union of an isolated vertex and a path of order
n-1
. From the
\chi_{S}
-criticality of
P_{n}
we may infer

\chi_{S}(P_{n})>\chi_{S}(G-v_{1}v_{2})=\max\{\chi_{S}(P_{1}),\chi_{S}(P_{n-1})\}=\chi_{S}(P_{n-1}).

By Theorem
3.1
, the strict inequality holds if and only if
n\in\{2^{1},2^{2},\dots,2^{k-1}\}
. We conclude that it is a necessary condition for the
\chi_{S}
-criticality of
P_{n}
if
n\geq 2
.

Now assume that
n=2^{i}
, for an integer
1\leq i\leq k-1
. By Theorem
3.1
,
\chi_{S}(P_{n})=i+1
. The removal of an edge
e
from
G=P_{n}
yields
G-e
, which consists of two paths
P_{r}
and
P_{q}
such that
r+q=n
. We may suppose that
r\leq q
and hence,
\chi_{S}(G-e)=\chi_{S}(P_{q})
. Observe that
q\leq 2^{i}-1
.
Using the formula in Theorem
3.1
again, we obtain

\chi(G-e)=\chi_{S}(P_{q})<i+1=\chi_{S}(P_{n})

that shows the
\chi_{S}
-criticality of
P_{n}
, and the proof is complete for (i).

(ii)

If
S=(1,s_{2},\dots)
is a packing sequence satisfying
2^{i-1}\leq s_{i}<2^{i}
for all
s_{i}
, we may use the formula
\chi_{S}(P_{n})=\lfloor\log_{2}(n)\rfloor+1
from Theorem
3.3
(ii). Then
\chi_{S}(P_{n-1})<\chi_{S}(P_{n})
holds if and only if
n\in\{2^{1},2^{2},\dots\}
as stated.

This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Every
\chi_{S}
-critical graph
G
is
\chi_{S}
-vertex-critical by definition. The following proposition shows that the reverse implication also holds if
G
is a path.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
S
be a packing sequence and
P_{n}
be a path of order
n
. Then
P_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-vertex-critical if and only if it is
\chi_{S}
-critical.

Proof.

By definition, it suffices to show that, for every
S\in{\cal S}
and
n\geq 2
, if
P_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-vertex-critical, then it is
\chi_{S}
-critical.
The deletion of a leaf from
P_{n}
gives
P_{n-1}
and the
\chi_{S}
-vertex-criticality of
P_{n}
implies
\chi_{S}(P_{n-1})<\chi_{S}(P_{n})
. On the other hand, every proper subgraph
H
of
P_{n}
consists of path components
H_{1},\dots,H_{\ell}
, each of which is of order at most
n-1
. Since
\chi_{S}(H)=\max\{\chi_{S}(H_{i})\colon 1\leq i\leq\ell\}
, the inequality
\chi_{S}(H)\leq\chi_{S}(P_{n-1})
holds for every proper subgraph
H
. Therefore,
\chi_{S}(P_{n-1})<\chi_{S}(P_{n})
implies
\chi_{S}(H)<\chi_{S}(P_{n})
for every
H\subset P_{n}
, and we may conclude that
P_{n}
is
\chi_{S}
-critical.
∎

The following theorem characterizes the

-critical cycles for all packing sequences

Theorem 4.3
.

If

Proof.

Let

\chi_{S}(P_{n})=4

If

\chi_{S}
-critical if

-packing coloring of

with
4

\phi

Claim 1.
There are two consecutive vertices in the cycle such that neither of them is assigned color
1
.

Proof.

Suppose that the statement is not true. Then
n

receives color
1
. Let

(i), consider the cycle

\phi^{\prime}

and
v_{j}
