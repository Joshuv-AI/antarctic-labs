An Updated Characterization of Luminous Ly α \alpha emitters at the End of Reionization Facilities: Magellan: Clay (M2FS), JWST (NIRCam) BAGPIPES ( Carnall et al. 2018 ) , SEP ( Barbary 2016 )
Yuanhang Ning, Zheng Cai, Linhua Jiang, Yucheng Guo, Qiong Li, Si-Yue Yu, Xiaodi Yu, Zhen-Ya Zheng

Abstract
We present a multi-wavelength physical characterization of 14 luminous Ly α \alpha emitters (LAEs) at z ≈ 6 z\approx 6 , integrating deep ground-based Magellan/M2FS spectroscopy with heterogeneous JWST/NIRCam broad- and medium-band imaging. Identified via strong Ly α \alpha lines with extreme Ly α \alpha luminosities of > 10 42.6 {>}10^{42.6} erg s -1 , the sample exhibits very large rest-frame equivalent widths ( ≳ 100 {\gtrsim}100 Å) and steeply blue UV continua ( β \beta median ≃ − 2.2 \simeq-2.2 , − 18.2 > -18.2> M 1500 M_{\rm 1500} > − 20.2 >-20.2 mag). Crucially, the integration of NIRCam medium-band photometry (F410M) breaks the degeneracy between strong rest-optical nebular emission and Balmer breaks, resolving prior mass overestimations. The tightly constrained spectral energy distribution modeling demonstrates that these luminous LAEs tend to be unequivocally low-mass, ultra-young dwarf starbursts; half the sample is characterized by stellar masses of M ∗ < 10 8 ​ M ⊙ M_{*}<10^{8}M_{\odot} , ages ≲ 10 \lesssim 10 Myr, and negligible dust attenuation. We also map the production efficiency of ionizing photons and Ly α \alpha escape fractions ( f esc Ly ​ α f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha} ). The f esc Ly ​ α f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha} values are exceptionally high, with a median of ≳ 40 {\gtrsim}40 %, increasing for the bluer UV continua. Finally, analyzing spatial offsets between the Ly α \alpha centroid and the stellar counterpart, we demonstrate empirically that internal dust content, rather than neutral hydrogen gas, dominate the suppression of Ly α \alpha radiative transfer. Our study reveals that strong Ly α \alpha emission of the luminous LAEs are generally attributed to both the vigorous starburst activities and the high f esc Ly ​ α f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha} . Resembling Lyman continuum leakers, these extreme dwarf systems function as highly efficient ionizing engines at the conclusion of the Epoch of Reionization.



I
Introduction

Ly
\alpha
emitting galaxies are powerful probes to explore galaxy formation and evolution in the early universe.
Theoretically expected as a “spectral beacon”
(
Partridge & Peebles 1967
)
, Ly
\alpha
helps to gradually find galaxies at higher redshift
(
Hu & McMahon 1996
;
Rhoads et al. 2000
)
.
After the first galaxy to be spectroscopically confirmed by Ly
\alpha
at a redshift greater than 6
(
Hu et al. 2002
)
, Ly
\alpha
selected galaxies, the Ly
\alpha
emitters (LAEs) found by narrowband (NB) technique, keep spectroscopic redshift record as the most distant galaxies for a decade
(
Rhoads et al. 2004
;
Taniguchi et al. 2005
;
Iye et al. 2006
;
Martin et al. 2008
;
Tilvi et al. 2010
;
Hu et al. 2010
, e.g.,)
.

As a complementary method, the drop-out technique help select Lyman Break Galaxies (LBGs) or high-redshift (high-
z
) candidates
(
Steidel et al. 1996
;
Giavalisco 2002
;
Douglas et al. 2009
;
Bouwens et al. 2015
;
Pentericci et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
. Especially, Hubble Space Telescope (HST) pushed the redshift frontier to
z\sim 9
with the installation of the Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3/IR)
(
Ellis et al. 2013
;
McLeod et al. 2016
;
Oesch et al. 2018
;
Bouwens et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
. After the launch in 2022, James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) substantially enlarges the sample at
z>9
through deep infrared imaging
(
Castellano et al. 2022
;
Finkelstein et al. 2022
;
Finkelstein et al. 2024
;
Donnan et al. 2023
;
Franco et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
and revolutionize the number of confirmed galaxies by the spectroscopic identification of Ly
\alpha
line and/or break
(
Arrabal Haro et al. 2023
;
Bunker et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2023
;
Castellano et al. 2024
;
Carniani et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.

In the JWST era, galaxies at
z>7
are being analyzed with Ly
\alpha
emission by spectroscopy in details
(
Chen et al. 2024
;
Napolitano et al. 2024
;
Jones et al. 2024
;
Tang et al. 2024a
;
Heintz et al. 2025
;
Runnholm et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2026
, e.g.,)
. The process of cosmic reionization becomes mostly complete at
z\lesssim 6

(
Ďurovčíková
et al. 2024
;
Spina et al. 2024
;
Zhu et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, which is a key stage to study how galaxies contribute to the ionizing budget. As potentially emitting Lyman Continuum (LyC) photons
(
Gazagnes et al. 2020
;
Choustikov et al. 2024
;
Izotov et al. 2024
;
Navarro-Carrera et al. 2025
;
Marques-Chaves et al. 2026
, e.g.,)
, LAEs serve as an ideal laboratory at this redshift slice, especially those luminous ones which typically represent the extreme cases.

Although recent JWST works have specifically studied high-
z
LAEs
(
Goovaerts et al. 2024
;
Iani et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, most of the LAEs are not luminous and not representative of those already identified by the large ground-based surveys based on the NB technique
(
Kashikawa et al. 2011
;
Ning et al. 2020
;
Kikuta et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
.
On the other hand, some JWST works study the LAEs from ground-based surveys but most of them have not been spectroscopically confirmed, lacking the key redshift information
(
Shimizu et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.
More luminous (confirmed) LAEs thus need to be further analyzed, which could also in turn indirectly help preparing the candidates for the future ground-based surveys taken by, for example, the MUltiplexed Survey Telescope (MUST;
Cai et al. 2025
;
Zhao et al. 2024
).

We carried out a ground-based, multi-object Magellan/M2FS spectroscopic survey spectroscopic survey to establish a large sample of high-
z
galaxies
(
Jiang et al. 2017
)
, including LAEs at
z\approx 5.7

(
Ning et al. 2020
)
and
z\approx 6.6

(
Ning et al. 2022
)
, and LBGs at
z\sim 6

(
Fu et al. 2024
)
.
These galaxies are spectroscopically confirmed via Ly
\alpha
lines from the high-
z
candidates which are designed to be selected from famous fields, waiting for JWST covering, including A370, CDFS, COSMOS, SXDS, and SSA22.
As JWST data is gradually accumulated by more surveys, more than 10 luminous LAEs from our M2FS survey have been covered by a series (
{>}4
) of infrared (IR) bands, especially including the medium band (F410M). In this work, we make use of the imaging data to further explore these luminous LAEs, focusing on comparing their Ly
\alpha
properties and physical properties.

We organize this paper as follows. In Section 2, we briefly state the LAE sample from the M2FS spectroscopic observations, NIRCam imaging dataset from several JWST surveys, and photometric measurements. In Section 3, we describe the methods and present the results including the physical properties from spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting, UV and H
\alpha
-related quantities, Ly
\alpha
properties (line width, misalignment to stellar counterpart, escape fraction). We give further discussions in Section 4. We summarize our paper in Section 5. Throughout this work, we adopt a standard flat cosmology with
H_{0}=\rm{70\ km\ s^{-1}\ Mpc^{-1}}
,
\Omega_{m}=0.3
and
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
. All magnitudes refer to the AB system.

II
Sample and Data

In this section, we present the sample of the LAEs at
z\sim 6
with the JWST/NIRCam imaging data. Then we describe how to detect the sources and take photometry from the images for the LAE sample.

II.1
Sample & Imaging Data

Our parent sample is drawn from the Magellan/M2FS spectroscopic survey of high-
z
galaxies presented in
Ning et al. 2020
;
Ning et al. 2022
.
With the high-throughput spectrograph M2FS on the 6.5m Magellan Clay telescope, the effective integration time per pointing is averagely
{\sim}5
hr (equivalently 35.8 hr in total for the sample in this work).
The LAEs are selected as candidates based on the Subaru/Suprime broadband and narrowband images and then identified with (strong) Ly
\alpha
lines by our Magellan/M2FS spectroscopic survey (in a Ly
\alpha
luminosity range from
10^{42.3}
to
10^{43.7}
erg s
-1
). The survey spectroscopically confirmed 260 LAEs at
z\approx 5.7
and 36 LAEs at
z\approx 6.6
in total over nearly 2 deg
2
in the sky.
So far as JWST has collected more extensive IR data across various fields, a fraction of our confirmed LAEs have been covered in more NIRCam bands.
In this work, we retrieve and adopt the JWST imaging data from DAWN JWST Archive (DJA
1
1

1

https://dawn-cph.github.io/dja/index.html
), a repository of public JWST galaxy data from a number of JWST surveys.
The JWST imaging data are reduced based on
grizli
2
2

2

https://zenodo.org/records/8370018

(
Brammer 2023
)
.
Valentino et al. 2023
provides the NIRCam data reduction in detail. The version of the imaging dataset is as of December 2025.

Among the JWST/NIRCam bands, the medium ones are especially sensitive to the presence/absence of emission line.
Including photometric data only covering optical continua can break the degeneracy between strong nebular lines of younger galaxies and prominent Balmer jumps of old galaxies
(
Jiang et al. 2016
, e.g.,)
.
Thereinto, F410M is crucial to constrain continuum level for galaxies at
z\approx 6
while F410M
\--
F444W color is sensitive to H
\alpha
emission line of star-forming galaxies.
To construct the sample, we thus select those covered by the NIRCam/F410M observations from the
260+36
LAEs. Then we select those also detected in the F444W band and at least two bands among the F115W, F150W, and F200W bands.
In this work, we focus on those detected only with a single component (also see the end of next subsection).
Among those satisfying the criteria, there is one in the A370 field and another one in the CDFS field, three in the SXDS field, and the rest nine locate in the COSMOS field.

The final sample includes 13 LAEs at
z\approx 5.7
and a LAE at
z\approx 6.6
.
All of them are luminous in terms of Ly
\alpha
, the lowest of which is
{\approx}10^{42.6}
erg s
-1
.
For comparison, three out of
{\lesssim}200
LAEs in
Iani et al. 2024
span a similar range of Ly
\alpha
luminosity as our sample, but their redshift values are all lower than 5.
In Figure
1
, the thumbnail images in the JWST/NIRCam bands are shown for the LAE sample in this work.
Most of them (12/14) are covered by the JWST Cycle-1 program (GO 1837), Public Release IMaging for Extragalactic Research (PRIMER;
Dunlop et al. 2021
)
which has the NIRCam imaging observations in eight bands, including F090W, F115W, F150W, and F200W in the short wavelength (SW), and F277W, F356W, F444W, and F410M in the long wavelength (LW). They are also covered by the JWST Cycle 1 program (GO 1727), COSMOS-Web
(
Kartaltepe et al. 2021
;
Casey et al. 2023
)
.
CDFS-z6lae-021 is covered by the JWST Cycle-1 program (GO 1210), JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey (JADES/South;
Rieke et al. 2023
;
Eisenstein et al. 2026
). A370a-z6lae-017 is covered by the JWST Cycle-1 program (GO 1208), The CAnadian NIRISS Unbiased Cluster Survey (CANUCS;
Willott et al. 2022
;
Sarrouh et al. 2026
).
Note that COSMOS-z6lae-101 is excluded because its JWST colors and morphology suggest that it is likely an “Little Red Dot” (LRD) source, as discussed in our previous work
Ning et al. 2024
.
A370a-z6lae-024 is also excluded because it is not as luminous as the sample in terms of Ly
\alpha
luminosity, which is magnified (
\mu\gtrsim 10
) by the foreground cluster A370
(
Hernán-Caballero et al. 2017
)
.

II.2
Photometry

We use
SEP

(
Barbary 2016
)
, a python implementation of
SExtractor

(
Bertin & Arnouts 1996
)
, to detect sources and measure the photometry of the objects in the bands.
We create the empirical point-spread function (PSF) by selecting and stacking at least 100 bright (not saturated) stars for each band. The images are PSF-homogenized to F444W and stacked to feed the detection image.
We then take the source detection by adopting a minimum area of five pixels, a threshold of 2
\sigma
, 64 deblending levels, and a contrast of 0.001 (no gaussian kernel in pixels).
Then we match the output catalogs to the original Ly
\alpha
centroid (from the NB imaging data). In the candidate selection of our Magellan/M2FS program, the photometric apertures have an angular radius of
1\farcs 0
. The spectroscopic fibers have an angular radius of
0\farcs 6
. We set the distance tolerance by a radius of
0\farcs 8
, averaging the above two values.

Next we also use
SEP
to perform photometry in all bands for each detected source. This procedure is same as the dual-image mode in
SExtractor
.
We measure the total
MAG_AUTO
flux with elliptical apertures.
Kron factor and the minimum aperture size are set to be 2.0 and 2.5, respectively.
The aperture corrections are also measured by increasing both the parameters,
which is equivalent to the standard
PHOT_AUTOPARAMS
values of 2.5 and 3.5.
Our sample only includes the sources with a single component detected above
3\sigma
in the F444W band. Such single component dominates H
\alpha
emission and contributes Ly
\alpha
emission so that it is proper to obtain the corresponding Ly
\alpha
escape fraction when comparing other properties.

III.1
Physical Properties

To constrain and obtain the physical properties, we perform SED fitting using
BAGPIPES
, a Python framework
for self-consistently modeling spectra and photometry with galactic properties
(
Carnall et al. 2018
)
. We run the latest version with the pure Python
Nautilus
nested sampling algorithm
(
Lange 2023
)
. In the fitting, we do not include the F090W data due to the Ly
\alpha
line strongly boosting this band. We do not also consider the Ly
\alpha
line in the posterior models of spectra. Flux of this line is mainly up to magnitude of the corresponding NB data as stated as follows.

We employ a constant star-forming history (SFH) model to parametrize that of each source in the sample. We start to perform the SED fitting within a broad parameter space. The age varies logarithmically from 0.1 Myr to the cosmic age at the redshift of each source (
{\sim}1
Gyr). The formed mass is set in the logarithmic range of
6-11
. The metallicity also varies logarithmically within the range of 0.001 and 10 times the solar metallicity. The nebular emission has a Cloudy model with an ionization parameter of
-4<{\rm log}(U)<0
.
Assuming a Calzetti law
(
Calzetti et al. 2000
)
for dust attenuation,
A_{V}
(absolute attenuation in the V band) is assigned to vary between 0 and 4 magnitudes.

The SED fitting results are shown with
1\sigma
uncertainty region in Figure
2
(a). Derived physical properties (Age,
A_{V}
, and
M_{*}
) are listed in the Column 5, 6, and 7 of Table
1
(the upper part), respectively.
LAEs generally tend to be young galaxies
(
Jiang et al. 2016
;
Iani et al. 2024
;
Firestone et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, which is confirmed by the output properties of the LAE sample in this work.
Strikingly, half of them are very young with ages smaller than 10 Myr.
Only one has an age exceeding 100 Myr.
For most of the LAEs in the sample, their small
A_{V}
show the low content of dust (
A_{V}<1
) while only one has
A_{V}>0.5
. The median value of
A_{V}
is 0.12. The output
M_{*}
has a logarithmic range of
{\sim}7.5-8.5
and a median of
{<}8.0
.
Previous works show the luminous LAEs, identified by the ground-based observations, have a typical stellar mass range of log
{}_{10}M_{*}/M_{\odot}\gtrsim 8-9

(
Jiang et al. 2016
;
Ouchi 2019
;
Ning et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.
Now with a series of JWST/NIRCam bands, especially the medium band F410M covering the optical continuum,
M_{*}
is better constrained and apparently reduced by a factor of
{\sim}0.5
dex.
The results reveal that these luminous LAEs have a similar
M_{*}
range to the fainter LAEs (for example, from
Iani et al. 2024
).

III.2
UV Continua and H
\alpha
Lines

We use the output SED-fitting model spectra to estimate UV continua of the sample, from which we have the absolute UV magnitude
M_{\rm 1500}
at the rest-frame wavelength of 1500 Å.
We constrain the UV-continuum slope with the three SW photometric datapoints (F115W, F150W, and F200W; only F115W and F150W for COSMOS-z6lae-174). We do not make use of the F090W data which is largely contributed by Ly
\alpha
emission. We keep using a power-law formula as did by previous works
(
Jiang et al. 2020
;
Ning et al. 2023
;
Ning et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. We fit the datapoints by a linear relation (in AB magnitude units) of
m_{\rm AB}\propto(\beta+2)\times{\rm log}(\lambda)
to obtain the UV slope
\beta
.

We also estimate the flux of H
\alpha
line based on the output SED-fitting model spectra. For each LAE, we interpolate the continuum around H
\alpha
line and subtract it to obtain the H
\alpha
flux. The corresponding flux error is estimated from the posterior model spectra in the
1\sigma
uncertainty region.
Note that for the galaxies at
z\sim 6
, without constraining the optical continua, the model spectra usually degenerate between strong nebular lines (of young galaxies) and prominent Balmer breaks (of old galaxies). The F410M photometric data breaks such degeneracy and significantly improves the accuracy of estimating the H
\alpha
line flux.

After constraining UV continua and H
\alpha
line, we compute the Hydrogen ionizing photon production efficiency
\xi_{\rm ion}
by

\displaystyle\xi_{\rm ion}=\frac{\dot{N}_{\rm ion}}{L_{\nu}^{\rm UV}}.

(1)

In this formula,
L_{\nu}^{\rm UV}
(erg s
-1
Hz
-1
) is the mono-chromatic UV-continuum luminosity per photon frequency. It is usually derived from the measured
M_{\rm 1500}
.
\dot{N}_{\rm ion}
(s
-1
) is the intrinsic production rate of Hydrogen ionizing photons from stellar populations, which can be calculated from H
\alpha
emission by

\displaystyle\dot{N}_{\rm ion}=\frac{L({\rm H\alpha})}{1-f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}}\times 7.35\times 10^{11}\ {\rm erg^{-1}}

(2)

in the (
T_{e}=10^{4}K
) case-B recombination
(
Kennicutt et al. 1994
;
Leitherer & Heckman 1995
;
Madau et al. 1998
)
.
We then correct
L_{\nu}^{\rm UV}
and
L({\rm H\alpha})
with the canonical stellar/nebular extinction ratio of 0.44 and the dust reddening law of
Calzetti et al. 2000
.
We adopt the production efficiency of ionizing photons
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
by assuming
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm LyC}
= 0.

In Table

, Column 8 lists the
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
results.

\alpha

of
{\simeq}-19.5
mag in the range of [–20.2, –18.2] mag, which are slightly fainter than those of

The UV slopes of our sample have a range roughly from –3.3 to –1.3. The median value is
\beta

\simeq-2.2

(
Jiang et al. 2013

. Comparing with the brighter or fainter sample
(

suggests no clear dependence between UV slopes and luminosities for the LAEs at the redshift.

The
\xi_{\rm ion,0}
results distribute in a range of log

III.3.1
Ly
\alpha
Line Width

For the LAE sample in this work, we have the Ly
\alpha

With the effective integration time of
{\sim}5
hr averagely per pointing, the total integration time is equivalent to about 36 hr (six pointings) for the sample in this work.
More details are provided in
Jiang et al. 2017
and
Ning et al. 2020

To measure the width of Ly
\alpha
lines, we implement a Markov-Chain-Monte-Carlo method in the line fitting. We keep using our above mentioned template of the composite Ly
\alpha
line. For each LAE, we have the spectroscopic redshift. We vary scaling factors of line flux from 0.3 to 3.0 times the peak value. We shrink and expand the template line to adjust its width with scaling factors in a range of [0.2, 5].

In Figure

(b), we plot the fitting results for all sources in the sample.
We can see that fitting is suitable for most of the sources but bad for cs-z6lae-104 and cs-z6lae-107. The reason is that there are too many “spikes” around Ly
\alpha
wavelength. If we mask these abnormal values, the number of data points is not enough for executable fitting especially at the redder side of Ly
\alpha
peak. We thus abandon these two sources when comparing Ly
\alpha
line width with other properties.
For COSMOS-z7lae-006, its Ly
\alpha
profile is considerably cut by the skyline subtraction at the red side of the peak so we also ignore its FWHM(Ly
\alpha
) result. As the instrument resolution keeps basically consistent around 816 nm for the remaining LAEs at
z\approx 5.7
, we adopt the directly measured FWHM(Ly
\alpha
) in this work.
In Table

, Column 13 lists the results of FWHM(Ly

). We plot the FWHM(Ly

) as functions of other properties in the 3rd row of Figure

. We do not found any clear relation between FWHM(Ly

) and these properties for our LAE sample.

III.3.2
Ly
\alpha
Offset from Stellar Counterpart

Recent JWST observations covering Ly
\alpha
show the non-negligible misalignment between Ly
\alpha
emission and stellar counterparts
(
Jiang et al. 2024
;
Napolitano et al. 2024
;
Ning et al. 2024
)
.
As shown by Figure
1
, the stellar counterparts of the LAEs revealed by JWST/NIRCam do not locate at the corresponding Ly
\alpha
centroid, the center of the dashed circular aperture. We have reported such phenomenon of the misalignment between Ly
\alpha
emission and the stellar counterpart of luminous LAEs in our previous work
Ning et al. 2024
. In this work, we explore how such misalignment relates to several properties. The Ly
\alpha
and stellar centroids are detected still based on the Subaru/Suprime NB images and the JWST/NIRCam images, respectively. The positional distances are computed to obtain the Ly
\alpha
-stellar projected offsets
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
.
We follow the same method and procedure as
Ning et al. 2024
to measure and evaluate the corresponding errors.

The astrometric accuracy is first addressed. We use the detected sources within a circle of 2
\farcm
0 diameter and compare their positions in the JWST/NIRCam and Suprime NB images. The systematic offsets are obtained by computing the median and eliminated from
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
. The astrometric precision is also addressed. We perform a Monte Carlo simulation by inserting the mock LAEs randomly in the NB images. We obtain the positional error of source detections as a function of LAE brightness. Fainter LAEs have larger measurement errors. For each source in the sample, the error of
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
is generated by interpolating its NB photometric data to the above magnitude-dependent relations. More details are provided in the Section 3.4 of
Ning et al. 2024
.

In Table
1
, Column 11 lists the results of
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
. We plot the
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
as functions of other properties in the bottom row of Figure
3
. Part of them spread consistently along the best-fit linear function previously obtained by
Ning et al. 2024
but others locate below this relation. The projection effect is supposed to be a main cause. If Ly
\alpha
emission goes along the line of sight when escaping from a galaxy, such observed offset is still very small even though the true offset is large.
We also discuss how
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
is affected by other properties in next section.

III.3.3
Ly
\alpha
Escape fraction

Before computing
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
, we update the Ly
\alpha
luminosities of our spectroscopically confirmed LAEs in this work. The flux of Ly
\alpha
line is re-computed based on the NB photometric data and the best-fit SED model for each LAE. Except Ly
\alpha
the continuum around Lyman break also contributes the NB magnitude although the Ly
\alpha
line usually dominates. We subtract the continuum integral flux (weighted by the NB filter curve) from the total NB flux.
Then by matching the above difference, we obtain the Ly
\alpha
flux with the dimensionless line profile of our composite Ly
\alpha
template (from our previous work
Ning et al. 2020
;
Ning et al. 2022
) which is redshifted to the observed frame for each one. We then estimate the Ly
\alpha
equivalent width in the rest frame, EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
).
Note that the spectroscopic redshift is crucial when measuring Ly
\alpha
line flux with NB data. NB filter curve is largely different from the top-hat shape. A strong line may exhibits faint if it is (red)shifted into the edge of the NB filter curve.

With the above obtained flux of H
\alpha
line, we compute the escape fraction of Ly
\alpha
photons (
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
) for each LAE. We adopt the canonical ratio
L({\rm Ly\alpha})/L({\rm H\alpha})=8.7
under the assumption of case-B recombination in
T_{e}=10^{4}K

(
Osterbrock & Ferland 2006
)
to calculate the intrinsic Ly
\alpha
flux and obtain:

\displaystyle f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}=\frac{L_{\rm obs}({\rm Ly\alpha})}{L({\rm H\alpha})\times 8.7}

(3)

which is also corrected by the dust reddening law of
Calzetti et al. 2000
. Although lacking information on the Balmer decrement (H
\alpha
/H
\beta
),
BAGPIPES
provides proper
A_{V}
values in the SED-fitting output. In Table
1
, Column 12 lists the results of
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
. We plot the computed
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
as functions of other properties in the 2rd row of Figure
3
.

For our LAE sample,
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
is all larger than
20\%
which could be a lower limit of
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
for the luminous LAEs at
z\approx 6
. Half of the sample have
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}

\gtrsim 40\%
.
For these luminous LAEs, we do not see any indication of the relation between EW
0
(Ly
\alpha

\alpha

<100

On the whole, the

Another reason could be possible. Part of literature works carry out JWST/NIRSpec prism observations for Ly

IV
Discussion

IV.1
Bursty Star Formation

We compare the star-formation rates (SFRs) with stellar mass (
M_{*}
) for our sample of luminous LAEs at
z\sim 6
.
These LAEs with EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
)
\gtrsim 50
Å (12/14
>100
Å) are associated to very low-mass galaxies. We use the method of
Kennicutt 1998
adapted for
Chabrier 2003
IMF to compute the SFR based on the H
\alpha
luminosity. Most of the LAEs are very young with the SED-derived ages of
\lesssim 30
Myr, we compare the H
\alpha
-based SFRs with the stellar mass by referring to the results from literature in Figure
4
. “Recent” star-formation activities power nebula to emit (optical) lines, H
\alpha
emission can infer SFRs averaged over a timescale of 10 Myr revealed by recent works using state-of-the-art hydrodynamical simulations
(
Kramarenko et al. 2025
;
Ismail et al. 2026
, e.g.,)
.

In the SFR-
M_{*}
diagram, the luminous LAEs tend to own starburst activity.
Most of galaxies at
z>5

(
Curti et al. 2024
)
follow the SFMS relation
(
Clarke et al. 2024
)
while our sample has a relatively diverse distribution.
Only one LAE in our sample locates closed to the MS line and half of them lie around the relation derived for starburst galaxies for
z\sim 5-6.5
by
Rinaldi et al. 2022
.
It is worth noting that our LAEs have (overall) lower mass than high-
z
extreme emission line galaxies from
Llerena et al. 2025
.
With the seven NIRCam (6 broad- and 1 medium-) bands in the SED fitting, we constrain their physical properties more accurately compared to our previous work
Ning et al. 2024
. One parameter of the crucial properties, stellar mass, is overall reduced with a median of
{\rm log}_{10}M^{*}<8.0
, which exhibit the importance to utilize the medium band covering the (rest-frame) optical continuum.

Strikingly, another half of the LAEs exhibit apparently more vigorous bursts of star formation, beyond the starburst regime.
These LAEs even have higher sSFR reaching
{\approx}10^{6.5}\rm{yr}^{-1}
, larger than the two UV-bright LAEs at
z\sim 8.5

(
Witstok et al. 2025
)
and
the starburst branch corresponding to the young LAEs at
z\simeq 3-7
reported by
Iani et al. 2024
.
As shown by Figure
4
, they also resemble LCEz4-M1, GN-z8-LAE and U37126 in the aspects of age, SFR, and
M_{*}
.
LCEz4-M1 is a LyC emitter candidate at
z=4.444
which is determined by the Ly
\alpha
line
(
Zhu et al. 2026
)
.
GN-z8-LAE is a prominent LAE at
z\approx 8.3
and a robust LyC leaker candidate with a strong C
iv
] line
(
Navarro-Carrera et al. 2025
)
.
U37126 is an strong LyC leaker at
z\approx 10.3
but without Ly
\alpha
line due to a phase of ISM-naked starburst
(
Marques-Chaves et al. 2026
)
.
Considering strong Ly
\alpha
lines as one of the reliable indicators of LyC emission
(
Gazagnes et al. 2020
;
Izotov et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, such similarity may suggest our luminous LAEs efficiently leak LyC emission supporting ionized bubbles in the Epoch of Reionization (EoR), which is also our conclusion from our previous work
Ning et al. 2022
by comparing Ly
\alpha
luminosity functions at redshift 5.7 and 6.6.

IV.2
Ly
\alpha
Transfer and Escape

Our LAE sample exhibits non-negligible misalignment between Ly
\alpha
emission and stellar components. We also reveal such phenomenon in our previous work
Ning et al. 2024
. We discussed a tentative relation between
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
and EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
). For luminous LAEs, Ly
\alpha
emission would displace farther with larger EW
0
(Ly
\alpha
). In this work, we constrain physical properties of the LAEs better with photometric data from more JWST/NIRCam bands. We can explore whether other properties determine
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
more effectively.

Unlike other emission lines such as H
\alpha
, Ly
\alpha
suffer resonant scattering by neutral Hydrogen (H
i
) atoms and passes longer routines before absorbed by dust
(
Dijkstra et al. 2011
;
Dijkstra 2014
, e.g.,)
. Ly
\alpha
emission thus distributes more complex in/around galaxies
(
Hayes 2015
, e.g.,)
. Besides gas turbulence or outflow altering the pathways of Ly
\alpha
emission
(
Rivera-Thorsen et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
, possible low-opacity channels in the ISM/CGM, driven by Supernova (SN) feedback
(
Kong et al. 2026
, e.g.,)
and/or AGN
(
Wang et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, may also help Ly
\alpha
and ionizing photons leaking
(
Markov et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. Ly
\alpha
photons thus propagate inhomogeneously and anisotropically so that its overall distribution could misalign with the corresponding stellar population.

Figure 5:
Ly
\alpha
escape fraction (
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
) and offset to stellar components (
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
) as functions of dust attenuation (
A_{V}
) and Ly
\alpha
line width FWHM(Ly
\alpha
). In the lower left panel, the dashed line qualitatively gives a limit trend of suppressing
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
as
A_{V}
increasing.

In Figure
5
, we compare
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
and
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
with
A_{V}
and FWHM(Ly
\alpha
), respectively. The plots shows that both
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
and
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
are obviously suppressed by increasing
A_{V}
but much less influenced when changing FWHM(Ly
\alpha
). The trends can be explained by the mechanism of Ly
\alpha
transfer process.
A_{V}
quantitatively measures the attenuation of dust content. The Ly
\alpha
line width due to Doppler or (multiple) scattering broadening, expressed by FWHM(Ly
\alpha
) in this work, is directly influenced by the density, temperature, and kinematic state of the H
i
atoms
(
Dijkstra 2014
)
.

In general, more H
i
would increase times of resonant scattering and distances of propagation for Ly
\alpha
photons, which boosts velocity offset with respect to the systemic redshift
\Delta\nu_{\rm Ly\alpha}
and broadens the Ly
\alpha
line width
(
Dijkstra et al. 2016
;
Prieto-Lyon et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. However, H
i
gas content can not determine how much Ly
\alpha
photons survive out of galaxies and how far they reach when scattered to velocities where the gas is transparent.
Although literature works report broad H
i
(absorption) velocity width results in lower
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
, this anti-correlation is demonstrated by a higher probability for Ly
\alpha
photons to be destroyed by dust grains when more likely residing in the optically thick H
i
gas
(
Gazagnes et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
.

If a galaxy contains more dust content, Ly
\alpha
emission would be less possible to escape and propagate far away due to being absorbed by dust grains
(
Dijkstra 2014
)
. This trend is qualitatively represented by the dashed line in the lower left panel. The dust content thus plays a relatively dominant role in regulating Ly
\alpha
transfer and escape.
When a galaxy has a smaller
A_{V}
, its
f_{\rm esc}^{\rm Ly\alpha}
could be small due to high opacity in the anisotropic ISM distribution. Its
\Delta d_{\rm Ly\alpha}
could also be small if Ly
\alpha
is emitted homogeneously and/or the (spatial) centroid locates closed to the line of sight.
The above demonstration can be verified further with the
\Delta\nu_{\rm Ly\alpha}
data measured from the difference of observed wavelength between Ly
\alpha
line and any other optical lines such as [O
iii
], H
\alpha
and so on.

V
Summary

In this work, we study a spectroscopically confirmed sample of 14 luminous LAEs in terms of Ly
\alpha
luminosity (
>10^{42.6}
erg s
