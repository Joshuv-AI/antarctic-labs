Contrasting Spin Excitations in Octahedral and Square-Planar n =8 Ruddlesden–Popper Nickelates Preprint: APS/123-QED
K. Scott, H. LaBollita, G. A. Pan, X. Yang, A. Kar, C. Lim, A. Thorshov, D. Ferenc Segedin, C. M. Brooks, F. Yakhou-Harris, K. Kummer, N. B. Brookes, F. Boschini, A. Frano, J. A. Mundy, E. H. da Silva Neto, A. S Botana, S. Blanco-Canosa

Abstract
The discovery of superconductivity in reduced square-planar nickelates marked a major advance in identifying structural and electronic analogs to the high- T c T_{c} cuprates. The more recent observation of superconductivity in parent Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) octahedral nickelates—with a clear difference in electron count with respect to cuprates—raises new questions about the nature of superconductivity across these related but distinct nickelate families. Here, we use Ni L 3 L_{3} -edge resonant inelastic x-ray scattering (RIXS) to probe the low-energy excitations in a representative compound of both families: the parent octahedral n = 8 n=8 RP phase Nd 9 Ni 8 O 25 (p-RP), which is non-superconducting, and its reduced square-planar counterpart Nd 9 Ni 8 O 18 (r-RP), which exhibits superconducting correlations with a T c ≈ 5 T_{c}\approx 5 K. The n = 8 n=8 p-RP develops a spin-density-wave (SDW) ground state with ordering wave vector q SDW = ( 1 / 4 , 1 / 4 ) q_{\mathrm{SDW}}=(1/4,\,1/4) , analogous to the bilayer RP, while the n = 8 n=8 r-RP shows an elastic peak at q ⋆ = ( 1 / 3 , 0 ) q^{\star}=(1/3,\,0) . Polarimetric RIXS shows that the p-RP exhibits low-energy spectra dominated by weakly dispersive paramagnons along the 0 → π \rightarrow\pi and π → π \pi\!\rightarrow\!\pi directions, whereas the r-RP with superconducting correlations displays dispersionless magnetic excitations. Our results comprehensively map out the spin excitations and reveal fundamental differences in the ground state between these two distinct structural families.
