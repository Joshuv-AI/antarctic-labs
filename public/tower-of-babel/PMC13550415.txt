SecureTrust-FL: trust-aware privacy-preserving federated learning for network intrusion detection
Authors: Naif S Alshammari, Shailendra Mishra, Megha Rathi, Nidhi Goel, Shams Tahzib
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe proliferation of distributed network environments and the Internet of Things (IoT) has increased the need for privacy-preserving intrusion detection systems capable of operating effectively under heterogeneous and non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) data conditions. This paper proposes SecureTrust-FL, a trust-aware federated learning framework for privacy-preserving intrusion detection. The framework integrates Federated Learning, Blockchain-based Trust Management, Differential Privacy, FGSM-based Adversarial Learning, and Zero-Trust Security principles to support secure collaborative learning without requiring raw data sharing among participating entities. The framework is evaluated using three benchmark intrusion detection datasets, namely CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT, which are treated as independent federated clients. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves an overall Accuracy of 92.91% ± 0.45%, Balanced Accuracy of 93.25% ± 0.43%, Macro F1-Score of 92.89% ± 0.45%, and AUC-ROC of 95.50% ± 0.40% across heterogeneous datasets. The results indicate that the federated model can effectively learn from distributed and heterogeneous data while preserving data privacy. Further analysis reveals the impact of class imbalance on intrusion detection performance, particularly in datasets containing skewed attack distributions, highlighting the importance of Balanced Accuracy and F1-Score in addition to overall Accuracy. Differential privacy experiments demonstrate the privacy–utility trade-off, where stronger privacy protection leads to a reduction in model performance. Adversarial robustness evaluation using FGSM perturbations also shows a noticeable decline in detection performance, indicating the need for stronger defense mechanisms against adversarial attacks. In addition, the trust ledger enhances transparency and accountability by monitoring client participation and recording the trust scores used during trust-weighted aggregation and maintaining trust records throughout the collaborative learning process. The results demonstrate that SecureTrust-FL provides an effective framework for privacy-preserving collaborative intrusion detection while integrating trust management, privacy protection, and secure federated learning within a unified architecture.Keywords: Intrusion Detection, Federated Learning, Zero-Trust Security, Differential Privacy, Adversarial RobustnessSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The attack surface of the network has grown significantly with the increased adoption of cloud computing, Internet of Things (IoT), and other devices. Consequently, intrusion detection systems (IDS) have become essential in the protection of contemporary computing infrastructure and data against sophisticated cyber threats such as ransomware and denial-of-service attacks. Conventional IDS systems are normally implemented using a centralized architecture that involves the aggregation and processing of data from various sources at a central point. Although useful in isolated or standalone settings, the architecture has significant drawbacks, especially with respect to data privacy, scalability, and applicability in cross-domain settings1,2.

Recently, federated learning (FL) has emerged as a new learning paradigm with great potential to support the design of IDS systems. FL involves training a global model by multiple clients without sharing their raw data, thus ensuring data privacy while allowing learning across different environments3,4. However, FL-based IDS systems face several challenges in practice, including adversarial attacks on model updates, the impact of non-IID data distribution among clients, and the effect of privacy-preserving mechanisms on detection performance5,6.

To enhance privacy in FL, differential privacy (DP) mechanisms are used to safeguard gradients and model updates transmitted during training.

7,8. Adversarial training methods such as the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) have also been employed to evaluate robustness against evasion attacks9. However, these methods are normally evaluated for individual data sets, which limits their extension to heterogeneous IoT environments10,11. In addition, the aggregation methods such as Krum do not take into consideration the trust relationships between clients; therefore, poor-quality information may influence the global model6. Even though some recent research has considered the evaluation of privacy preservation and robustness to adversaries, these have been considered separately without taking into account the transparent monitoring of clients’ participation in heterogeneous federated environments11.

Considering the research gaps mentioned above, this study aims to develop a SecureTrust-FL framework for federated intrusion detection that incorporates a differential privacy evaluation, robustness analysis to adversaries, and a trust ledger monitoring mechanism in a federated learning environment.

The framework is evaluated using three IDS datasets, namely CICIDS201710, UNSW-NB1512, and BoT-IoT13, which are treated as independent federated clients to emulate non-IID data distributions.

The research questions addressed in this study are as follows:

RQ1: Can the proposed federated intrusion detection framework achieve high detection performance across heterogeneous IDS datasets under non-IID data conditions?

RQ2: How does the federated model perform across individual datasets with different traffic distributions and class imbalance characteristics?

RQ3: What is the impact of differential privacy mechanisms on the detection performance of the federated IDS model?

RQ4: How robust is the trained federated model against adversarial perturbations generated using FGSM?

RQ5: Can trust-ledger monitoring and trust-score-based aggregation improve transparency, auditability, and reliable client participation during federated training?

While significant progress has been achieved in the field of federated intrusion detection systems, there are still a number of challenges that need to be addressed. For instance, most aggregation methods that are effective against malicious client behaviors are not designed to monitor individual client behaviors. In addition, differential privacy, although useful for ensuring client privacy, often results in compromised performance. Finally, while the individual aspects of privacy preservation, adversarial robustness, and client monitoring have been explored, they are not often considered collectively within a common framework.

The proposed SecureTrust-FL framework is particularly beneficial for distributed cyber security systems such as IoT environments, telecommunication systems, cloud-edge computing environments, smart city implementations, industrial control systems, and enterprise security collaboration systems where intrusion detection across domains is required while maintaining privacy.To address some of the limitations of existing federated learning-based intrusion detection systems, this paper presents a federated learning-based framework, referred to as SecureTrust-FL, that utilizes a monitoring-only trust ledger.

The proposed framework is validated using three diverse intrusion detection datasets, namely CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT, that are considered as individual clients to mimic non-IID scenarios. In addition, the proposed framework utilizes a comprehensive evaluation of differential privacy, adversarial robustness, and cross-dataset generalization, allowing for joint analysis of performance, privacy, and robustness for collaborative intrusion detection.

Literature review

Federated learning (FL) has been recognized as one of the possible techniques for developing efficient and effective privacy-preserving IDS by leveraging the federated learning approach. The federated learning technique helps to address the critical challenge of privacy and data management in the context of distributed networks. Furthermore, a variety of research has been conducted to improve the performance of federated learning-based IDSs in terms of accuracy and robustness. In the below sections, some of the significant results of federated learning-based IDS, robustness, security, privacy-preserving federated learning, and federated learning-based IoT networks are discussed.

Federated learning in IDS

The study of Khraisat et al. (2025)1 provides a detailed review of federated learning architectures used in IDS with respect to the importance of privacy-preserving distributed IDS using benchmark datasets like CICIDS2017 and NSL-KDD. Another significant result is the work done by Alsamiri et al. (2023)3 where the authors demonstrate the effectiveness of federated learning IDS by showing its impressive accuracy of almost 98%. However, the evaluation of FL-IDS models using single datasets has been the most common approach in recent studies, limiting the generality of the IDS models for various network environments.

The study of Bhavsar et al. (2024)14 focused on the federated IDS for transportation IoT networks and proved that FL-based IDS models can achieve better performance compared to centralized IDS while preserving the privacy of the network data. However, the cross-domain generality of federated IDS models has been the major drawback in the implementation of FL-based IDS systems. The cross-domain federated learning-based IDS was addressed in the study of Vyas A et al. (2024)2.FL-based IDS models have been based on the Federated Averaging (FedAvg) algorithm proposed in the study of McMahan et al. (2017)4.

Robustness and security in federated learning

Another significant challenge in federated learning systems is to guarantee robustness against harmful client activities. In this context, Blanchard et al. (2017)5 proposed Byzantine-resilient optimization techniques such as Krum and Multi-Krum to filter out harmful client activities in federated learning systems. Later, Ni et al. (2024)6 extended the work on Byzantine-resilient optimization techniques and proposed rFedFW to incorporate client trustworthiness in federated optimization.In addition, there are many studies on adversarial robustness in federated learning systems. Shaukat K et al. (2022)15 proposed adversarial learning techniques to enhance the robustness of deep learning models against adversarial attacks. Later, many researchers studied and proposed various techniques on adversarial learning in federated learning systems to enhance robustness while preserving privacy (Xie C, Wang X et al., 202416; Askhatuly A et al., 202517. Zero-trust mechanisms were studied and proposed in federated IDS systems. In this context, Javeed et al. (2024)18 proposed a zero-trust federated IDS system for IoT networks using CNN-BiLSTM feature extraction techniques while preserving data locality. In addition, mist-assisted federated learning was studied to enhance robustness in IoT IDS systems (Izadi S et al., 202519.

Privacy-preserving federated IDS

The main driving factor behind the application of federated learning to intrusion detection systems is the need to preserve privacy. There are various research works that have been conducted to explore the application of differential privacy and secure aggregation to protect client data during training. Li et al.20 proposed a personalized federated learning framework based on knowledge distillation, which utilizes unlabeled pseudo data to enhance model performance in IoT environments. The proposed model achieved an accuracy rate of around 94%. El Ouadrhiri and Abdelhadi7 proposed a federated learning-based IDS that leverages differential privacy to protect data during training. The proposed model achieved a decrease of around 2–5% in accuracy.

Du et al. (2025)8 proposed an adaptive DP-enabled federated learning framework named DP-Fed6G, which is suitable for large-scale distributed networks. The experiments showed the effectiveness of the privacy protection with competitive performance. Other collaborative IDS frameworks, like JCP-FedPrIDS and FedEff, have also been studied to achieve efficient multi-dataset federated optimization (Li et al., 202520; Narmadha & Varalakshmi, 202521. Knowledge distillation-based federated learning has also been proposed to address the problem of non-IID data distribution for the IoT intrusion detection system (Peng et al., 202522.

Federated learning techniques currently being explored by researchers aim to address privacy leakage and optimization challenges in decentralized environments. Identity-preserving covert communication through generative perturbation has been proposed to enhance privacy and security in decentralized networks operating under adversarial conditions23. Fed-EHP introduced an efficient heterogeneous personalized federated learning framework that improves privacy-preserving collaborative learning in non-IID environments24. Generative shadow-mapping techniques have been investigated to mitigate gradient inversion attacks by protecting local model updates from adversarial reconstruction attempts25. Furthermore, Fed-GAN architectures have demonstrated the potential of generative adversarial mechanisms for privacy-preserving collaborative learning in cross-device federated learning environments26.

Federated learning for IoT and edge networks

Applying federated learning to IoT and edge environments introduces additional challenges due to heterogeneous devices, resource constraints, and non-IID data distributions. Fang C et al. (2021)27 proposed a secure and efficient FL framework for IoT networks that achieves a balance between detection performance and computational overhead. Sarhan et al. (2022)28 proposed a blockchain-enabled hierarchical FL architecture to improve the security and auditability of distributed IDS deployment. More recent work has investigated advanced deep learning architectures to improve intrusion detection performance in heterogeneous environments.

Transformer-based FL models have demonstrated improved detection accuracy in cyber-physical systems (Hamad NA et al., 202529. Two-stage transformer architectures have also been applied to intrusion detection in vehicular and controller-area-network environments (Zhang et al., 202530. In addition, attention-based and tab-transformer IDS models have shown promising performance in IoT security applications (Abd Elaziz et al., 202531. Personalized federated learning approaches have also been proposed to improve robustness and privacy preservation in heterogeneous IoT deployments (Sun et al., 202532.

Research gaps and positioning of SecureTrust-FL

Recent studies highlight several important trends in FL-based intrusion detection research. Firstly, the integration of zero trust security principles with federated learning has garnered significant research interest, which has enabled the development of safe collaborative learning environments. This has been demonstrated by various research studies, including Chandu et al. (202511, Javeed et al. (202418, and Yaseen et al. (2026)33. Secondly, aggregation techniques have been a significant research area to address issues related to malicious or unreliable clients during federated learning. This has been demonstrated by Blanchard et al. (20175 and Ni (20246.Thirdly, differential privacy has been a widely researched area to ensure the privacy of clients during collaborative learning. This is because obtaining an optimal privacy-utility trade-off is a challenging task. This has been demonstrated by Vyas A et al. (20242, El Ouadrhiri & Abdelhadi (20227, and Du et al. (20258. Finally, efficient federated learning frameworks for resource-constrained IoT environments are a significant area of research to ensure efficient federated learning. This has been demonstrated by various research studies, including Fang C et al. (202127, Hamad NA et al. (202529, and Sun et al. (202532. Despite various research studies on federated learning, several challenges need to be addressed to develop federated IDS frameworks.

Existing federated intrusion detection systems typically only consider the performance of the model or the preservation of privacy, without considering the performance of the privacy mechanism and the robustness of the model to adversaries. There are a few studies on the performance of the FL-IDS model on a single dataset, which does not provide a clear understanding of the performance of the model on diverse network scenarios. There is a lack of empirical research on the performance of the federated intrusion detection system on cross-dataset scenarios, specifically on heterogeneous datasets such as CIC-IDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT. There is a lack of research on the joint effect of privacy mechanisms and adversarial perturbations on the performance of federated intrusion detection systems.

Recent studies have proposed privacy-enhanced federated frameworks1, achieving high detection accuracy (~ 95%) in IoT environments while preserving data locality. Likewise, various studies21,29,31 have been carried out on the optimal federated learning-based IDS for IoT and graph-based methods30, which indicates the necessity of comprehensive evaluation studies that take into consideration various aspects such as privacy-utility trade-offs, adversarial robustness, class imbalance issues, and transparent client monitoring. The proposed SecureTrust-FL addresses these issues with the integration of various aspects such as differential privacy, robustness evaluation via FGSM, cross-dataset generalization with various datasets such as CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT, and trust ledger.In an effort to bridge the existing gaps, this research introduces SecureTrust-FL: a federated intrusion detection system for collaborative learning over heterogeneous datasets while assessing the influence of privacy mechanisms, adversarial perturbations, and decentralized learning.

The privacy preservation and robustness are analyzed with experiments on differential privacy noise injection and adversarial perturbation.

SecureTrust-FL system architecture and federated training workflow

The SecureTrust-FL approach uses a client-server federated learning approach that includes three main modules. These three modules work together to ensure the effective operation of intrusion detection with trust-based aggregation.

Client layer

The organization becomes an independent federated client, which means that each organization stores its network traffic data locally. The client performs local operations such as preprocessing, harmonization, feature extraction, and the training of an intrusion detection model. At this stage, clients use class-weighted losses, Mixup augmentation, FedProx regularization, gradient clipping, and differential privacy approaches. No data leaves the client environment except the model parameters.

Federated aggregation server

The aggregation server manages the federated learning process through dissemination of the existing global model to clients, receiving model parameters that have been locally updated, and carrying out the aggregation operation based on weights. Client contributions are evaluated based on accuracy, reliability, and cosine similarities to calculate normalized trust values. These values dictate how much contribution from the client will be made to the aggregation process of the model. Finally, the new global model obtained from the aggregate is again disseminated to the clients.

Monitoring layer for trust ledger

The trust ledger records all training events that occur during federated learning. It holds records related to training events such as participation by clients, their trust score, model aggregation weight, and other data about model update and round-based training statistics. In doing so, the method ensures traceability and audibility in training while retaining historical information about trust.

Based on the client performance metrics, the trust score computation is performed at the server level and is used to compute the weight associated with each update. The trust scores are normalized and used as a part of the trust-weighted aggregation algorithm to obtain a new global model. This model is then distributed back to all clients and this procedure is iteratively executed for several rounds of communication.

The information associated with trust calculation, aggregation, and client performance metrics is logged into the trust ledger during the entire federated learning process. At the end of federated training, the final obtained global model is tested using intrusion detection criteria, confusion matrix computation, differential privacy evaluation, adversarial robustness tests based on FGSM method, and dataset generalization.

Through this design, SecureTrust-FL framework is able to achieve collaborative intrusion detection in heterogenous environment without compromising the privacy of data and trust-aware aggregation.

Implementation, deployment and operational framework

The prototype of SecureTrust-FL adopts the core federated learning protocol that was presented in the architectural design framework. Model training is carried out locally at distributed clients whereas model aggregation takes place at a central aggregation server. The client uses its local data to train a neural intrusion detection model while sending only the model parameters to the server without sharing the actual data points.

In addition, SecureTrust-FL employs the following features: trust-aware aggregation, differential privacy testing, adversary resistance analysis, and ledger-based monitoring of trust levels. As such, clients perform gradient clipping and differential privacy noise addition in the process of training before sending their local updates to the aggregation server. The server uses validation accuracy, reliability, and cosine similarity measures to evaluate local updates from different clients and calculate trust scores. Trust scores are normalized and aggregated along with other parameters into a global model and distributed back to the clients.

To ensure transparency and auditability, the framework utilizes a non-intervention trust ledger for logging the metadata associated with training processes, along with trust score values, aggregation weight values, model update metadata, and client participation data. The ledger logs all training activities in history but does not impact the client selection process except the trust-weighted aggregation process.

The framework architecture adheres to zero-trust assumptions and divides into three operational zones, namely:

The SecureTrust-FL approach comprises different operational modules. Data pre-processing and training of the local model happen within the Local Intrusion Detection Module. The Federated Aggregation Server is responsible for facilitating federated learning and updating the global model based on trust weighting. The module that checks the degree of privacy is called the Differential Privacy Evaluation Module, where Gaussian noise is injected to analyze privacy preservation. The module that uses adversarial examples to evaluate the robustness of the model is the Adversarial Robustness Evaluation Module.

The operational workflow assumes a federated environment in which multiple network domains collaboratively train a shared intrusion detection model without exchanging raw data. Local models are trained independently, transmitted to the aggregation server, aggregated into a global model, and redistributed to clients until completion of the training process.

The threat model considers adversaries that may attempt to infer private information from model updates, inject malicious or poisoned gradients, perform adversarial input attacks, or intercept communications between participating entities. Differential privacy mechanisms and adversarial robustness evaluation are incorporated to assess the potential impact of these threats within the federated learning environment.

After federated training is completed, the final global intrusion detection model is evaluated using accuracy, balanced accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, confusion matrix analysis, adversarial robustness testing, and cross-dataset evaluation. These experiments provide an assessment of model performance, privacy–utility trade-offs, trust-aware aggregation behavior, and robustness under heterogeneous federated learning conditions.

Experimental evaluation

The performance of the SecureTrust-FL framework was tested in a heterogeneous federated learning environment. The performance of the proposed SecureTrust-FL framework was tested in terms of accuracy, adversarial attacks, and sensitivity to privacy-preserving techniques. The experiments are designed and implemented in Python 3.10, and the PyTorch library and the flower federated learning library (v1.x) are used. The simulation of all experiments was carried out on a workstation with NVIDIA RTX 3090 and 64 GB of RAM.

To simulate a realistic scenario of a network intrusion detection system based on federated learning, the CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT benchmarking datasets are used as separate federated clients. The CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT benchmarking datasets are diverse network behaviors that include traditional network intrusion attacks and IoT-based network attacks. All the datasets were passed through a unified preprocessing step to ensure their compatibility. Time stamps, flow ids, and non-informative columns were removed from all the datasets. Missing and infinite values were handled by median imputation. All numerical columns were normalized using standard scaling, whereas attack types were binarized to malicious or benign. Finally, 13 features were selected from all the datasets using mutual information-based feature selection for computational efficiency. The complete list of selected features, along with the preprocessing script, is provided in the supplementary materials for complete reproducibility. All the experimental results in this study were conducted using the trust-weighted aggregation strategy implemented in SecureTrust-FL. Advanced security components such as zero trust enforcement, as well as Byzantine robust aggregation, are part of the conceptual framework for future work. Federated training setup

SecureTrust-FL was implemented with Flower federated learning framework with PyTorch as backend. In the experiments, three federated clients for CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT datasets, respectively, were utilized as the source of data for federated learning. The three clients served as independent datasets to demonstrate heterogeneity and non-IID distributions of training data. Global learning was carried out by 20 communication rounds for 8 epochs at each client. A learning rate of 0.001 and a batch size of 128 were adopted throughout the experiment. Mutual information-based feature selection was conducted to keep the feature dimension of the datasets to 13. Trust-weighted aggregation based on normalized trust scores was utilized for model aggregation.

Intrusion Detection was implemented based on multi-layer perceptrons (MLP). It contained one input layer with inputs being the selected features, two fully connected hidden layers with ReLU activation function, and one output layer with the purpose of binary classification.

To evaluate privacy preservation, differential privacy was tested through introducing Gaussian noise to the models with noise scale = 0.005. Robustness against adversarial attacks was investigated through employing FGSM with ε = 0.01. Architecture alignment with the implementation

The implemented SecureTrust-FL architecture aligns with the proposed architecture, where a client-server federated learning workflow was implemented.

Client Layer

The proposed SecureTrust-FL architecture consists of a local workflow implemented at the client side, where the local IDS model training is performed, and the model update is sent to the aggregation server. The raw network traffic data is kept at the client side, ensuring the confidentiality of the data. The raw network traffic data is kept at the client side, ensuring the confidentiality of the data.

Federated Aggregation Server

The server handles the federated learning rounds by sending the global model to the clients, receiving the model parameters, and performing trust-weighted aggregation. The aggregated global model is sent to the clients again for the next training rounds.

Training Ledger Monitoring

The training ledger monitors the training process by recording the training events, including the round-level metrics, client participation, and aggregated model statistics.The training ledger records the trust scores and aggregation decisions used during trust-weighted aggregation and provides transparency throughout the federated training process.

Global model performance

The global model was tested using the aggregated test dataset, based on the results obtained, the global model attained a total accuracy of 92.91% ± 0.45%. The precision (97.32% ± 0.39%),), recall (89.46% ± 0.73%), and F1 score (92.89% ± 0.45%)) indicate that the model maintains a high rate of detection for the attack instances, while the false negative rate is relatively low. The ROC-AUC score of 95.50% ± 0.40% shows the model’s capacity to distinguish between the benign and malicious instances of the data. The balanced accuracy of 93.25% ± 0.43% and macro F1 score of 0.9847 show the consistency of the model in classifying the instances of the two classes, considering the class balance (Table 2).

The results presented in Figs. 3–8 provide a comprehensive assessment of the proposed SecureTrust-FL framework. The figure confusion matrix 5, shows that 1405 benign instances and 1692 attack instances were correctly classified. The number of misclassifications is relatively low, with 116 instances of benign data being incorrectly identified as attacks (false positives), while 132 instances of attack data were incorrectly identified as benign (false negatives), it shows that most of the samples are classified correctly with only a small number of errors. The relatively low number of false negatives is particularly important in intrusion detection because it indicates that only a limited number of attack instances are missed. From this analysis, it is clear that the proposed federated learning framework has strong detection capability while maintaining data privacy.

Class imbalance analysis

Class imbalance is a common problem in intrusion detection datasets since there is usually a significant difference in the proportion of benign and attack traffic in each of the datasets. This may affect federated training for models since the heterogeneity of client data and its non-IID nature would affect the model’s performance. Balanced accuracy and macro-F1 scores would give additional insight into the model’s performance since it might perform well on the overall accuracy measure despite poor performance with attack class due to its minority within training dataset. Results from Table 5 indicate variability in federated intrusion detection model performance which is affected by different distribution of client data.

The observed differences among clients confirm the continued influence of heterogeneity of client data on the performance of federated models. It may be concluded from the research findings that using several criteria in federated intrusion detection evaluation is beneficial. Future studies could examine methods such as federated focal loss and resampling at client level to improve model’s performance in the context of class imbalance in heterogeneous environments.

The robustness of the trained intrusion detection model was evaluated using the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) adversarial attack, which generates adversarial samples by perturbing inputs in the direction of the loss gradient. Limitations of the study

Despite the promising results of the introduced SecureTrust-FL framework in relation to privacy-preserving federated intrusion detection, there are certain limitations that should be mentioned. Firstly, the experiments were conducted on three benchmark intrusion detection datasets, CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT in a simulation federated learning environment. The used datasets are heterogeneous and non-IID, yet it can be argued that such datasets cannot fully cover the complexity of large-scale real-life application cases.

Secondly, the federated environment included three clients, and it does not represent the behavior of the federated ecosystem consisting of many different organizations. Thirdly, the analysis of adversarial robustness of the proposed model was conducted only on the basis of the FGSM method. Iterative adversarial attacks, poisoning attacks, and backdoor attacks have not been considered in the current research.

Fourthly, while SecureTrust-FL includes trust-weighted aggregation and trust-ledger monitoring among its components, other advanced tools, such as fully decentralized trust management, Byzantine-resilient aggregation, and zero-trust were not employed.

Also, SecureTrust-FL includes trust-weighted aggregation and trust-ledger monitoring as the framework’s operational parts, while some advanced techniques like fully decentralized trust management, Byzantine-tolerant aggregation approaches, and zero-trust approach have not been included and still pose research questions to address in future work.

Consequently, the results presented in this paper should be viewed under the context of the data sets used, experimentation setup and threat models addressed in this study. Further research will consider federated environments that are larger, more complex adversarial threat models, improved aggregation techniques, and practical implementation of the framework.

Discussion

The experimental results show that the SecureTrust-FL framework supports collaborative intrusion detection in decentralized and heterogeneous environments. The results include an Accuracy of 92.91% ± 0.45%, Balanced Accuracy of 93.25% ± 0.43%, Macro F1-score of 92.89% ± 0.45%, and AUC-ROC of 95.50% ± 0.40% on the federated test dataset of SecureTrust-FL. Furthermore, the achieved value of AP was 95.17%. The proposed model managed to detect attacks using federated learning, which made it possible to address data heterogeneity without the need to share the traffic data of the participating clients.Nevertheless, the detailed analysis of the results received reveals some limitations associated with the approach under consideration. Although the overall performance level is rather high, the comparison of different data sets shows that the influence of data heterogeneity and class imbalance is evident. It is important to note that such a finding is characteristic for most intrusion detection tasks.

The obtained performance of the three datasets indicates the efficiency of the model in heterogeneity and non-IID settings. Even though the performance differs among the datasets, the global model preserves constant detection capabilities in participating clients. This implies that federated learning can be used in decentralized cybersecurity applications. Also, differential privacy shows that there is a trade-off between privacy and utility. Although high levels of privacy provide increased data security, noise added through Gaussian reduces model performance and convergence.

Performance evaluation on the security of the model was carried out using adversarial attacks known as FGSM. There was considerable performance degradation as the strength of the adversarial attacks increased. In spite of the effectiveness of the intrusion detection capability of the proposed model, there is need for more improvements to counter any possible adversarial attacks.

The trust ledger ensures trust and accountability through constant evaluation of the clients and their assigned trust score throughout the training process. From the small difference in the trust score obtained from all the participating clients, we can conclude that stability was achieved during training. However, further research is required to test the effect of malicious participants on the trust mechanism.

Though the SecureTrust-FL framework has yielded positive results under federated learning, other factors will determine its practical deployment. Some of these include communication latency, connection, participation issues, heterogeneous resources, and traffic variability. For these reasons, the experimental result should only be considered in relation to the federated learning setup.

In conclusion, the SecureTrust-FL framework represents a promising approach for protecting IoT/IIoT systems from intrusion attacks. The framework incorporates federated learning and other advanced techniques such as differential privacy, trust awareness, and adversarial evaluation. Future research will address issues relating to robustness against adversarial attacks and trust-based aggregation methods for a greater number of clients.

Conclusion

This study presented SecureTrust-FL, a federated intrusion detection framework that combines federated learning, trust-aware aggregation, differential privacy evaluation, and adversarial robustness assessment. The framework was evaluated using three benchmark intrusion detection datasets, namely CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and BoT-IoT, to represent a heterogeneous and non-IID federated learning environment. The experimental results showed that SecureTrust-FL achieved an overall accuracy of 92.91% ± 0.45%, a balanced accuracy of 93.25% ± 0.43%, a macro F1-score of 92.89% ± 0.45%, and an AUC-ROC of 95.50% ± 0.40%. The findings reveal that it is possible to perform collaborative intrusion detection within federated learning and maintain data locality. It was found from the client-level experiments that model performance differed based on the dataset since there were differences in traffic attributes, imbalances in classes, and degree of data heterogeneity. Other issues identified with federated intrusion detection include those related to privacy preservation and adversarial robustness. For instance, from the differential privacy tests, it was observed that the increase in model accuracy correlated with loss of privacy, as expected, whereas FGSM-based experiments showed that the accuracy of the models decreased as the adversarial perturbation level rose. From these findings, it is clear that the issues related to privacy preservation and adversarial robustness need to be addressed for federated intrusion detection. The trust-ledger mechanism offered an organized method for keeping track of participants’ trust scores and aggregation activities during training. However, the presented framework was tested under simulation involving three clients and three datasets. Furthermore, robustness analysis was conducted based on only the FGSM attacks, while techniques such as Byzantine resilient aggregation and complete zero-trust enforcement still belong to the proposed conceptual design and have yet to be incorporated into the proposed prototype design. Future work will concentrate on analyzing the framework in more extensive federated systems, studying other attack models, optimizing performance for severe imbalanced classes, and developing trust aware aggregation to improve robustness.
