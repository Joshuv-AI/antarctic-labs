Regularization by noise for Gevrey well-posedeness of a weakly hyperbolic operator
Enrico Bernardi, Alberto Lanconelli

Abstract
We present an example of a linear partial differential equation whose Cauchy problem becomes well-posed when perturbed by noise. Specifically, we make clear how a suitable multiplicative Stratonovich perturbation of Brownian type renders a weakly hyperbolic operator with double involutive characteristics well-posed in the C ∞ C^{\infty} -category, while its deterministic counterpart is only well-posed in the Gevrey s s classes with 1 ≤ s < 2 1\leq s<2 .



Key words and phrases: regularization by noise, weakly hyperbolic operator, Itô-Stratonovich integration.

AMS 2020 classification: 60H15, 35L10, 60H50.

1
Introduction and statement of the main result

It is well-known that the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathrm{P}(t,x,\partial_{t},\partial_{x})u(t,x)=0,&(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\\
\partial_{t}^{j}u(0,x)=u_{j}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},j=0,\ldots,m-1,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\mathrm{P}
is a linear hyperbolic differential operator of order
m\in\mathbb{N}
with analytic coefficients, may fail to be
well-posed in the
C^{\infty}
-category if
\mathrm{P}
is weakly
hyperbolic, that is if some of its characteristic roots coincide. When
this happens, and
r\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the largest multiplicity of these roots,
then a classical result in
[
5
]
shows that for arbitrary
lower order terms of the operator
\mathrm{P}
, one can in general only
expect well-posedness in the Gevrey classes
\gamma^{(s)}
, with
1\leq s<\frac{r}{r-1}
. Moreover, the threshold
\frac{r}{r-1}
is usually optimal.
We recall that
f\in\gamma^{(s)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, the Gevrey class of order
s\geq 1
, if for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exist constants
C>0
and
h>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(x)\right|\leq Ch^{-|\alpha|}(\alpha!)^{s}\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in K\mbox{ and }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}.

We also denote
\gamma^{(s)}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\gamma^{(s)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In this paper we study an explicit example of a weakly hyperbolic operator with a manifold of involutive double characteristics
\Sigma_{2}=\{t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}:(t,x,\tau=0;\xi\neq 0)\}
and
m=r=2
, i.e.
T_{\rho}\Sigma_{2}^{\sigma}\subset T_{\rho}\Sigma_{2}
the
symplectic dual of the tangent space at
\rho\in\Sigma_{2}
is
contained in
T_{\rho}\Sigma_{2}
. The operator reads as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}+i\partial_{x})u(t,x)=0,&t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R};\\
u(0,x)=\varphi_{0}(x),\partial_{t}u(0,x)=\varphi_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Albeit (
1.2
) is admittedly very simple, its principal part is nonetheless one of the only three possible cases in which a hyperbolic quadratic form can be invariantly
represented in suitable symplectic coordinates. Thus, (
1.2
) is actually the microlocal model for
any
hyperbolic operator with double involutive characteristics. Many physical phenomena are driven by equations of the type (
1.2
): conical refraction of light rays through prisms, see e.g
[
18
]
and
[
22
]
, dynamics of wet-dry regimes in linearized Saint-Venant shallow
waters systems see e.g.
[
21
]
and
[
17
]
, to name just a few.

To be more specific, localizing the principal symbol at a double
point, we end up with a symplectic quadratic form of one of these three
invariant cases, as proved in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
:

\displaystyle\bullet\!\!\!\!\quad p(t,\tau,x;\xi)=2\lambda t\tau,\penalty\ \lambda>0;

\displaystyle\bullet\!\!\!\!\quad p(t,\tau,x;\xi)=-\tau^{2};

(1.3)

\displaystyle\bullet\!\!\!\!\quad p(t,\tau,x;\xi)=-\tau^{2}+2\tau\xi_{1}+x_{1}^{2}.

The first case in (
1
) corresponds to the so called
effectively hyperbolic case, while the third one is the only invariant
case where its Hamiltonian matrix has a Jordan block of size
4
. Stochastic generalizations of the first item in (
1
) were
recently studied in
[
4
]
, while some results for associated stochastic partial differential equations in this last case where also obtained in
[
3
]
. For the analysis of other weakly hyperbolic partial differential equations perturbed by noise we refer the reader to
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.

It is immediate to check that the lower order term in (
1.2
) does not satisfy
the necessary Ivrii-Petkov-Hörmander condition, which for this model
states that the sub-principal symbol of
\mathrm{P}
must vanish on
\Sigma_{2}
, see
[
9
]
and
[
12
]
. This implies that the Gevrey threshold
s=2
is
the natural barrier for equation (
1.2
).

Aim of the present manuscript is to understand the effect of noise on this particular setup. Is it possible to gain in-mean well-posedness for a suitable stochastic perturbation of (
1.2
)? The answer presented and proved here is affirmative,
positioning this outcome among the already vast array of results capped under the general
title of regularization by noise.

Dating back to results of Khasminskii
[
13
]
, where examples of unstable systems of
ordinary differential equations become asymptotically stable when their parameters are
perturbed by white noise, passing through the works of Zvonkin
[
24
]
and Veretennikov
[
23
]
, that prove existence of a unique strong solution for stochastic differential equation with merely bounded and measurable drift, and reaching for instance to the theory of transport-type stochastic perturbations and the foundational work of Flandoli–Gubinelli–Priola
[
6
]
,
where we can see that, in those setting, noise restores uniqueness for
transport equations below deterministic thresholds, we do know that there are many
instances of noise–regularized behaviors.
See also the survey
[
8
]
and lecture notes
[
7
]
for a broader overview of the phenomenon.

The general philosophy of regularization by noise is that stochastic
perturbations may introduce effective coercivity or averaging mechanisms that are absent in the deterministic problem.
In many examples, this becomes visible after converting the equation
to Itô form and exploiting quadratic variation

[
6
]
. Something along these lines is happening here as well.

On a slightly different angle but in a similar vein too, we may also cite some recent results
[
16
]
on
the unique continuation property for stochastic wave equations, which seem to
present a similar scheme: stochastic Carleman estimates restore
unicity properties near characteristic surfaces, which are known to
usually fail in the deterministic case.

We may now state our main result whose proof is postponed to Section
3
below. We first rewrite (
1.2
) as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=v(t,x),&t\in(0,T],x\in\mathbb{R};\\
\partial_{t}v(t,x)=-i\partial_{x}u(t,x),&t\in(0,T],x\in\mathbb{R};\\
u(0,x)=\varphi_{0}(x),v(0,x)=\varphi_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R};\end{cases}

(1.4)

then, we consider the stochastic partial differential equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dU(t,x)=V(t,x)dt+\sigma\partial_{x}U(t,x)\circ dB(t),&t\in(0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}\\
dV(t,x)=-i\partial_{x}U(t,x)dt&t\in(0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}\\
U(0,x)=\varphi_{0}(x),V(0,x)=\varphi_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.5)

where
\sigma
is a positive real number and

is a standard one dimensional Brownian motion. As usual by

we denote

[

. Equation (

1.4
). The next theorem states the better well-posedness of (
1.5
) over (
1.4
); in the sequel we set
H_{x}^{\infty}:=\bigcap_{k\geq 0}H_{x}^{k}
and

denoting the usual Sobolev space

Theorem 1.1
.

The Cauchy problem for the stochastic Stratonovich perturbation (
1.5
) is well-posed in
H_{x}^{\infty}
in the continuous
sense, i.e. it has a unique solution process in

\displaystyle U\in\bigcap_{k\geq 0}C\big([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{k})\big),\qquad V\in\bigcap_{k\geq 0}C\big([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{k-\frac{1}{2}})\big)\penalty\ .

Remark 1.2
.

This result can be compared with the known result, a proof of which is sketched in the next section, which states that for

the Cauchy problem for (
1.4

The paper is organized as follows: in Section

).
In Section

2
A brief review of the deterministic case

The next two subsections present simple self-contained arguments showing how
the threshold Gevrey
s=2
appears naturally for this
model. Needless to say every result given here is well-known and
belongs to a historically well-established theoretical framework. An exhaustive and
complete collection of results can be found for instance in

[
20
]
, source of some of the theorems adapted and used below.

2.1
Sufficiency

We present a very short and sketchy review of how to get Gevrey energy estimates
for the model (
1.2
). We consider here, with Fourier
derivatives
D_{y}:=\frac{1}{i}\partial_{y}
, the slightly more
general symbol

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(x,D)=-D_{t}^{2}+AD_{x}

with
A\in\mathbb{C}
. Taking the Fourier transform in
x
and setting

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}u(x)dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

we get

\displaystyle 2i\Im\langle\mathrm{P}\widehat{u},-D_{t}\widehat{u}\rangle=D_{t}|D_{t}\widehat{u}|^{2}+2i\Im\xi\langle A\widehat{u},D_{t}\widehat{u}\rangle

and

\displaystyle 2i\Im\langle D_{t}\widehat{u},\widehat{u}\rangle=D_{t}|\widehat{u}|^{2}.

(2.1)

With the proviso, not affecting the gist of the argument, that
\xi
is positive and large and with
\lambda>0
to be suitably chosen, define the Gevrey norm

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{s}^{2}(\xi):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\lambda t\xi^{1/s}}|v(t;\xi)|^{2}dt,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

and inner product

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{s}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\lambda t\xi^{1/s}}f(t;\xi)\bar{g}(t;\xi)dt,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, multiplying the two previous identities by
\displaystyle e^{2\lambda t\xi^{1/s}}
and integrating by parts we get

\displaystyle 2\Im\langle P\widehat{u},-D_{t}\widehat{u}\rangle(\xi)\geq 2\lambda\xi^{1/s}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|D_{t}\widehat{u}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{s}^{2}(\xi)+2\Im\xi\langle A\widehat{u},D_{t}\widehat{u}\rangle(\xi).

(2.2)

Since we also have from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|D_{t}\widehat{u}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{s}^{2}(\xi)\geq\lambda^{2}\xi^{2/s}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{u}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{s}^{2}(\xi),

using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2-\frac{1}{s}<\frac{3}{s}

we see that we can estimate the last
summand in (
2.2
) with a choice of
\lambda
and therefore, after an inverse Fourier
transform, we have reached a Gevrey
s

with
s<2
energy estimate. Standard arguments allow us then to
conclude.

Remark 2.1
.

For the sake of completeness we can also remark that the
Cauchy problem for (
1.2
) fails when
s=2
, albeit in a
slightly weaker sense. Since the main focus here is however on the lack of
solvability for
s>2
, we point the interested reader to some
classical literature for these cases:
[
20
]
.

We say that the Cauchy problem for the second order differential

3
The stochastic energy estimate

In this section we prove the main theorem of our work, i.e. Theorem (
1.1
). We start taking the Fourier transform with respect to
x
in (
1.5
) to get

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\widehat{U}(t;\xi)=\widehat{V}(t;\xi)dt+i\sigma\xi\widehat{U}(t;\xi)\circ dB(t),&t\in(0,T],\xi\in\mathbb{R};\\
d\widehat{V}(t;\xi)=\xi\widehat{U}(t;\xi)dt,&t\in(0,T],\xi\in\mathbb{R};\\
\widehat{U}(0;\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(\xi),\widehat{V}(0;\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(\xi),&\xi\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(3.1)

which in Itô’s form reads

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\widehat{U}(t;\xi)=\left[\widehat{V}(t;\xi)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi^{2}\widehat{U}(t;\xi)\right]dt+i\sigma\xi\widehat{U}(t;\xi)dB(t),&t\in(0,T],\xi\in\mathbb{R};\\
d\widehat{V}(t;\xi)=\xi\widehat{U}(t;\xi)dt,&t\in(0,T],\xi\in\mathbb{R};\\
\widehat{U}(0;\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(\xi),\widehat{V}(0;\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(\xi),&\xi\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(3.2)

For
t\in[0,T]
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
we define

\displaystyle m_{1}(t;\xi):=\mathbb{E}|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)|^{2},\qquad m_{2}(t;\xi):=\mathbb{E}\,\Re\left(\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}\widehat{V}(t;\xi)\right),\qquad m_{3}(t;\xi):=\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}(t;\xi)|^{2}.

(3.3)

The idea is to do an energy estimate, of the type of Section
(
2.1
), at the expectation level. It is here that the role
of the perturbation
\sigma
can be seen to play its role.

Lemma 3.1

(Second moments system)
.

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
the function

\displaystyle t\mapsto m(t;\xi)=(m_{1}(t;\xi),m_{2}(t;\xi),m_{3}(t;\xi))^{\top}

defined in (
3.3
) solves the linear system of ordinary differential equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{m_{1}}(t;\xi)=2m_{2}(t;\xi),\\
\dot{m_{2}}(t;\xi)=\xi m_{1}(t;\xi)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi^{2}m_{2}(t;\xi)+m_{3}(t;\xi),\\
\dot{m_{3}}(t;\xi)=2\xi m_{2}(t;\xi);\end{cases}

(3.4)

here,
\dot{m_{i}}(t;\xi):=\frac{d}{dt}m_{i}(t;\xi)
for
i=1,2,3
.

Proof.

Applying Itô’s formula to
|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)|^{2}=\widehat{U}(t;\xi)\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}
gives

\displaystyle d|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)|^{2}=\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}d\widehat{U}(t;\xi)+\widehat{U}(t;\xi)d\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}+d\widehat{U}(t;\xi)\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}.

Now, if we substitute
d\widehat{U}(t;\xi)
and
d\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}
with the corresponding expressions from (
3.2
), we find

\displaystyle d|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)|^{2}=2\Re(\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}\widehat{V}(t;\xi))dt.

Taking expectations yields
\dot{m_{1}}(t;\xi)=2m_{2}(t;\xi)
, i.e. the first equation in (
3.4
).

Moreover, using Itô’s product rule we get

\displaystyle d(\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}\widehat{V}(t;\xi))=d\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}\widehat{V}(t;\xi)+\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}d\widehat{V}(t;\xi)+d\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}d\widehat{V}(t;\xi).

Since
d\widehat{V}(t;\xi)
has no Brownian part, the last term vanishes and, upon taking real parts and expectations, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{m_{2}}(t;\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}(t;\xi)|^{2}+\xi\mathbb{E}|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)|^{2}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi^{2}\mathbb{E}\Re(\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}\widehat{V}(t;\xi))

\displaystyle=\xi m_{1}(t;\xi)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi^{2}m_{2}(t;\xi)+m_{3}(t;\xi).

This corresponds to the second line in (
3.4
).

Lastly, applying Itô’s formula to
|\widehat{V}(t;\xi)|^{2}
and recalling that
dV(t;\xi)
has no stochastic part, we see that

\displaystyle d|\widehat{V}(t;\xi)|^{2}=2\xi\,\Re(\overline{\widehat{U}(t;\xi)}\widehat{V}(t;\xi))dt.

Taking expectations yields
\dot{m_{3}}(t;\xi)=2\xi m_{2}(t;\xi)
thus completing the proof.
∎

Eigenvector decomposition

The eigenvalues of the matrix

\displaystyle A(\xi):=\begin{bmatrix}0&2&0\\
\xi&-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi^{2}&1\\

Lemma 3.2

\displaystyle\xi\leq 0\quad\mbox{ implies }\quad\Re\lambda_{+}(\xi)=-\frac{\sigma^{2}\xi^{2}}{4}\leq 0.

\xi\end{pmatrix}.

(3.7)

thus implying that the second component of the vector
m(t)=(m_{1}(t;\xi),m_{2}(t;\xi),m_{3}(t;\xi))^{\top}
can be controlled by the other two.

We now define the weighted energy

F(t;\xi):=m_{1}(t;\xi)+\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}m_{3}(t;\xi)\quad\mbox{ with }\quad\langle\xi\rangle:=\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}.

(3.8)

To ease the notation we also write

\displaystyle\gamma:=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi^{2}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\Delta:=\sqrt{\gamma^{2}+16\xi}

so that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}(\xi)=\frac{-\gamma\pm\Delta}{2}.

Writing the solution to (
3.4
) in eigenvector coordinates, i.e.

\displaystyle m(t;\xi)=q_{0}v_{0}(\xi)+q_{+}(t;\xi)v_{+}(\xi)+q_{-}(t;\xi)v_{-}(\xi),

we have

\displaystyle q_{0}(t)=q_{0}(0),\qquad q_{+}(t;\xi)=e^{\lambda_{+}(\xi)t}q_{+}(0),\qquad q_{-}(t;\xi)=e^{\lambda_{-}(\xi)t}q_{-}(0).

(3.9)

Moreover,

\displaystyle m_{1}(t;\xi)=q_{0}+q_{+}(t;\xi)+q_{-}(t;\xi),\qquad m_{3}(t;\xi)=-\xi q_{0}+\xi\bigl(q_{+}(t;\xi)+q_{-}(t;\xi)\bigr),

and therefore using (
3.8
) we get

\displaystyle F(t;\xi)=\Bigl(1-\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}\Bigr)q_{0}+\Bigl(1+\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}\Bigr)\bigl(q_{+}(t;\xi)+q_{-}(t;\xi)\bigr).

On the other hand, since
0\leq 1\pm\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}\leq 2
, we obtain the basic estimate

F(t;\xi)\leq 2|q_{0}|+2|q_{+}(t;\xi)|+2|q_{-}(t;\xi)|.

(3.10)

Notation
.

In the sequel to ease the notation we will omit to write in the functions
m
,
q_{+}
,
q_{-}
,
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}
the explicit dependence on
\xi
.

Let
m(0):=(m_{10},m_{20},m_{30})^{\top}
. A direct computation yields

\displaystyle q_{0}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}m_{10}-\frac{1}{2\eta}m_{30},

(3.11)

\displaystyle q_{+}(0)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda_{-}}{2\Delta}m_{10}+\frac{2}{\Delta}m_{20}-\frac{\lambda_{-}}{2\eta\Delta}m_{30},

(3.12)

\displaystyle q_{-}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{+}}{2\Delta}m_{10}-\frac{2}{\Delta}m_{20}+\frac{\lambda_{+}}{2\eta\Delta}m_{30}.

(3.13)

Note that, from (
3.8
) at
t=0
,

m_{10}\leq F(0;\xi),\qquad m_{30}\leq\langle\xi\rangle\,F(0;\xi).

(3.14)

Also, by (
3.7
) at
t=0
,

|m_{20}|\leq\frac{m_{10}+m_{30}}{2}.

(3.15)

Lemma 3.3

(Large-
|\xi|
coefficient bounds)
.

There exist
\xi_{0}\geq 1
and
C>0
(depending only on
\sigma
) such that for all
|\xi|\geq\xi_{0}
,

\frac{1}{|\Delta|}\leq\frac{C}{\xi^{2}},\qquad\Bigl|\frac{\lambda_{-}}{\Delta}\Bigr|\leq C,\qquad\Bigl|\frac{\lambda_{-}}{\xi\Delta}\Bigr|\leq\frac{C}{|\xi|},\qquad\Bigl|\frac{\lambda_{+}}{\Delta}\Bigr|\leq\frac{C}{|\xi|^{3}},\qquad\Bigl|\frac{\lambda_{+}}{\xi\Delta}\Bigr|\leq\frac{C}{|\xi|^{4}}.

(3.16)

Proof.

Since
\gamma=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\xi^{2}
and
\Delta=\sqrt{\gamma^{2}+16\xi}
, we have
\Delta\sim\gamma\sim\xi^{2}
as
|\xi|\to\infty
,
hence
1/|\Delta|\lesssim 1/\xi^{2}
.

Moreover,
\lambda_{-}=\frac{-\gamma-\Delta}{2}
satisfies
|\lambda_{-}|\sim\gamma\sim\xi^{2}
, so
|\lambda_{-}|/|\Delta|\lesssim 1
and

|\lambda_{-}|/(|\xi||\Delta|)\lesssim 1/|\xi|
.

Finally,
\lambda_{+}=\frac{-\gamma+\Delta}{2}=O(1/\xi)
as
|\xi|\to\infty
, because

\Delta=\gamma\sqrt{1+16\xi/\gamma^{2}}=\gamma+O(1/\xi)
, hence

\lambda_{+}=(-\gamma+\Delta)/2=O(1/\xi)
. Therefore

|\lambda_{+}|/|\Delta|\lesssim(1/|\xi|)/\xi^{2}=1/|\xi|^{3}
and similarly

|\lambda_{+}|/(|\xi||\Delta|)\lesssim 1/|\xi|^{4}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Control by
F(0;\xi)
of eigenvector coefficients)
.

There exist
\xi_{0}\geq 1
and
C>0
such that for all
|\xi|\geq\xi_{0}
,

|q_{0}(0)|+|q_{+}(0)|+|q_{-}(0)|\leq C\,F(0;\xi).

(3.17)

Proof.

Step 1: bound
q_{0}(0)
.

By (
3.11
) and (
3.14
),

|q_{0}(0)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}m_{10}+\tfrac{1}{2|\xi|}m_{30}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}F(0;\xi)+\tfrac{\langle\xi\rangle}{2|\xi|}\frac{m_{30}}{\langle\xi\rangle}\leq CF(0;\xi),

since
\langle\xi\rangle/|\xi|\leq\sqrt{2}
for
|\xi|\geq 1
.

Step 2: bound
q_{+}(0)
.

Using (
3.12
) and Lemma
3.3
,

|q_{+}(0)|\leq Cm_{10}+\frac{C}{\xi^{2}}|m_{20}|+\frac{C}{|\xi|}m_{30}.

We bound each term by
F(0;\xi)
. First,
m_{10}\leq F(0;\xi)
.
Next, by (
3.15
) and (
3.14
),

\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}|m_{20}|\leq\frac{1}{2\xi^{2}}(m_{10}+m_{30})\leq\frac{C}{\xi^{2}}F(0;\xi)+\frac{C}{\xi^{2}}\langle\xi\rangle F(0;\xi)\leq\frac{C}{|\xi|}F(0;\xi)\leq CF(0;\xi),

for
|\xi|\geq 1
.
Finally,

\frac{1}{|\xi|}m_{30}\leq\frac{\langle\xi\rangle}{|\xi|}\frac{m_{30}}{\langle\xi\rangle}\leq CF(0;\xi).

Hence
|q_{+}(0)|\leq CF(0;\xi)
.

Step 3: bound
q_{-}(0)
.

Using (
3.13
) and Lemma
3.3
,

|q_{-}(0)|\leq\frac{C}{|\xi|^{3}}m_{10}+\frac{C}{\xi^{2}}|m_{20}|+\frac{C}{|\xi|^{4}}m_{30}\leq CF(0;\xi),

using again (
3.15
) and (
3.14
) and
|\xi|\geq 1
. Summing the three bounds yields (
3.17
).
∎

Theorem 3.5

(Uniform weighted-energy bound for large
|\xi|
)
.

Fix
T>0
. There exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and
\xi_{0}\geq 1
such that for all
|\xi|\geq\xi_{0}

and all
t\in[0,T]
,

F(t;\xi)\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}t}F(0;\xi).

(3.18)

Proof.

From (
3.10
) and (
3.9
),

F(t;\xi)\leq 2|q_{0}(0)|+2e^{\Re\lambda_{+}t}|q_{+}(0)|+2e^{\Re\lambda_{-}t}|q_{-}(0)|.

For
|\xi|
large,
\Re\lambda_{+}=O(1/|\xi|)
and
\Re\lambda_{-}\sim-\gamma\leq 0
,
so there exist
\xi_{0}
and
C_{2}>0
such that for all
|\xi|\geq\xi_{0}
,

e^{\Re\lambda_{+}t}\leq e^{C_{2}t},\qquad e^{\Re\lambda_{-}t}\leq 1,\qquad t\in[0,T].

Therefore,

F(t;\xi)\leq 2e^{C_{2}t}\bigl(|q_{0}(0)|+|q_{+}(0)|+|q_{-}(0)|\bigr).

Applying Lemma
3.4
gives (
3.18
).
∎

The large-
|\xi|
estimate in Theorem
3.5
leaves open the compact region

|\xi|\leq\xi_{0}
. On this region we can bound the weighted energy by a
uniform constant using compactness.

Lemma 3.6

(Bounded-
\xi
patch)
.

Fix
T>0
and
\xi_{0}\geq 1
. There exists a constant
M=M(T;\xi_{0},\sigma)>0
such that
for all
|\xi|\leq\xi_{0}
and all
t\in[0,T]
,

F(t;\xi)\leq M\,F(0;\xi).

Consequently, for any
C_{2}\geq 0
,

F(t;\xi)\leq M\,e^{C_{2}t}\,F(0;\xi),\qquad t\in[0,T].

Proof.

Recalling the definition of
A(\xi)
in (
3.5
), define the diagonal weight

\displaystyle W(\xi):=\mathrm{diag}\left[1,\,1,\,\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\right];

then for each
\xi
,

\displaystyle W(\xi)m(t)=W(\xi)e^{tA(\xi)}W(\xi)^{-1}\,W(\xi)m(0).

The map
(t;\xi)\mapsto B(t;\xi):=W(\xi)e^{tA(\xi)}W(\xi)^{-1}
is continuous on the compact set

[0,T]\times[-\xi_{0};\xi_{0}]
(matrix exponential is continuous and
W(\xi)
is smooth); hence

\displaystyle M:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{|\xi|\leq\xi_{0}}\|B(t;\xi)\|_{1\to 1}<\infty.

Moreover, using that
m_{1},m_{3}\geq 0
, the definition (
3.8
) implies

F(t;\xi)=m_{1}(t)+\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}m_{3}(t)=\|W(\xi)m(t)\|_{\ell^{1}}-|m_{2}(t)|\leq\|W(\xi)m(t)\|_{\ell^{1}},

and similarly
F(0;\xi)\geq 0
and
\|W(\xi)m(0)\|_{\ell^{1}}=F(0)+|m_{2}(0)|\leq 2F(0;\xi)
by (
3.7
).
Therefore,

F(t;\xi)\leq\|W(\xi)m(t)\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq\|B(t;\xi)\|_{1\to 1}\,\|W(\xi)m(0)\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq 2M\,F(0;\xi),

uniformly for
t\in[0,T]
and
|\xi|\leq\xi_{0}
.
The last statement follows from
e^{C_{2}t}\geq 1
.
∎

Combining Lemma
3.6
with Theorem
3.5
yields constants

C_{1}^{\ast},C_{2}^{\ast}>0
such that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and all
t\in[0,T]
,

F(t;\xi)\leq C_{1}^{\ast}e^{C_{2}^{\ast}t}F(0;\xi).

Thus we have proved:

Theorem 3.7
.

Fix
T>0
. There exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}

and all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle F(t;\xi)\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}t}F(0;\xi).

(3.19)

We now apply the standard Plancherel technique that upgrades the frequency estimate for
F(t;\xi)

to Sobolev moment bounds in the
x
variable.

Lemma 3.8

(Sobolev moment bounds from the weighted energy)
.

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
T>0
. Assume that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle F(t;\xi)\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}t}F(0;\xi),

(3.20)

where

\displaystyle F(t;\xi)=m_{1}(t;\xi)+\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}m_{3}(t;\xi),\qquad m_{1}(t;\xi)=\mathbb{E}\,|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)|^{2},\quad m_{3}(t;\xi)=\mathbb{E}\,|\widehat{V}(t;\xi)|^{2}.

Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left[\mathbb{E}\|U(t)\|_{H_{x}^{s}}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|V(t)\|_{H_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\right]\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}T}\left[\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}^{2}+\|\varphi_{1}\|_{H_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\right].

Proof.

Multiply the frequency estimate (
3.20
), which was proved in
Theorem
3.19
, by
\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}
and integrate over
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}F(t;\xi)\,d\xi\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}F(0;\xi)\,d\xi.

By definition of
F
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}F(t;\xi)\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)|^{2}d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s-1}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}(t;\xi)|^{2}d\xi,

and using Fubini’s theorem and Plancherel’s identity in the
x
-variable gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)|^{2}d\xi=\mathbb{E}\|U(t)\|_{H_{x}^{s}}^{2},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s-1}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{V}(t;\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\mathbb{E}\|V(t)\|_{H_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2},

since
\langle\xi\rangle^{2s-1}=\langle\xi\rangle^{2(s-\frac{1}{2})}
. The same identities hold at
t=0
with

U(0)=\varphi_{0}
and
V(0)=\varphi_{1}
. Taking the supremum over
t\in[0,T]
yields the claim.
∎

An immediate consequence of the Lemma
3.8
and Theorem
3.7
is:

Theorem 3.9

(Mean-square
H_{x}^{s}\times H_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}
well-posedness)
.

Assume that the estimate of Lemma
3.8
holds for a given
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then the homogeneous Cauchy problem (
1.5
) is well-posed in

H_{x}^{s}\times H_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}
in the
mean-square sense
, namely:

•

for every
(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1})\in H_{x}^{s}\times H_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}
there exists a unique
adapted solution
(U,V)
;

•

the solution satisfies

\displaystyle U\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{s})),\qquad V\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}));

•

the solution map
(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1})\mapsto(U,V)
is continuous with respect to
these norms.

Corollary 3.10

(Mean-square
C^{\infty}
well-posedness)
.

Assume that the estimate of Lemma
3.8
holds for all
s\geq 0
.
Then the Cauchy problem (
1.5
) is well-posed in
H_{x}^{\infty}
in the mean-square
sense:

\displaystyle U\in\bigcap_{k\geq 0}L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{k})),\qquad V\in\bigcap_{k\geq 0}L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{k-\frac{1}{2}})).

Due to the fact that (
3.1
) is a linear system we can also
improve the regularity of the solution process from
L^{\infty}[0,T]
to

C[0,T]
utilizing the following proposition:

Proposition 3.11

(Time continuity in
L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{s})
from mode-wise SDE continuity)
.

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
T>0
. Let
(U,V)
be a (homogeneous) solution whose
x
-Fourier
transform
\widehat{U}(t;\xi)
and
\widehat{V}(t;\xi)
satisfy,
for each fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, a linear finite-dimensional SDE system with continuous
sample paths in
t
(e.g. the mode system obtained by Fourier transforming a
linear stochastic partial differential equation in
x
). Assume moreover the uniform Sobolev moment bounds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\|U(t)\|_{H_{x}^{s}}^{2}<\infty,\qquad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\|V(t)\|_{H_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}<\infty.

(3.21)

Then,

\displaystyle U\in C\big([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{s})\big),\qquad V\in C\big([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}})\big).

Proof.

We prove the statement for
U
; the proof for
V
is identical. Fix
t_{0}\in[0,T]
and let
t
tend to
t_{0}
. By Plancherel’s identity in
x
,

\mathbb{E}\|U(t)-U(t_{0})\|_{H_{x}^{s}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\mathbb{E}\big|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)-\widehat{U}(t_{0};\xi)\big|^{2}d\xi.

(3.22)

For each fixed
\xi
, the assumed continuity of the mode process yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)-\widehat{U}(t_{0};\xi)\big|^{2}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\mbox{ as }t\to t_{0}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)-\widehat{U}(t_{0};\xi)\big|^{2}\leq 2|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)|^{2}+2|\widehat{U}(t_{0};\xi)|^{2},

hence

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\mathbb{E}\big|\widehat{U}(t;\xi)-\widehat{U}(t_{0};\xi)\big|^{2}\leq 4\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{U}(\tau;\xi)|^{2}.

The right-hand side is integrable in
\xi
because (
3.21
) is
equivalent by Plancherel to

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\mathbb{E}|\widehat{U}(\tau;\xi)|^{2}d\xi<\infty.

Therefore dominated convergence applies in (
3.22
) and yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|U(t)-U(t_{0})\|_{H_{x}^{s}}^{2}\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{ as }t\to t_{0},

which is exactly
U\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{s}))
.
∎

Therefore finally we have:

Corollary 3.12

(Continuity
C^{\infty}
well-posedness)
.

Assume that the estimate of Lemma
3.8
holds for all
s\geq 0
.
Then the Cauchy problem (
1.5
) is well-posed in
H_{x}^{\infty}
in the continuous
sense:

\displaystyle U\in\bigcap_{k\geq 0}C\big([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{k})\big),\qquad V\in\bigcap_{k\geq 0}C\big([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H_{x}^{k-\frac{1}{2}})\big)
