Final Technical Report

Executive Summary:

During the last several Heavy Elements Chemistry grant cycles under award(s): DE-FG02-
13ER16414, we have published nearly 90 research articles (see bibliography) in 25 peer-reviewed
journals. Additionally, this funding has resulted in several review articles. Together, these
publications have already reached well over 4500 citations. These research articles have introduced
several hundreds of new crystallographically authenticated f-element compounds and materials
and have greatly affected the field of Heavy Elements Chemistry. Concentrating on the heaviest
elements that can be studied on the milligram scale, the transplutonium elements are the focus of
nearly 30 of these manuscripts containing results such as the first single crystal structure of a
berkelium compound and californium
presenting itself as an additional
transition point in the actinide series.

 Second, the identity of the ligand makes a big impact on the effect of shifting as a function
of pressure. This is exemplified by the curium compounds NH4[Cm(pydtc)4]·nCH3OH and
Cm2[C6(COO)6](H2O)8·2H2O.12 In NH4[Cm(pydtc)4]·nCH3OH, the ligand is a bidentate sulfur
donor ligand where the 1− charge is delocalized among the two sulfur atoms, a carbon atom, and
a nitrogen atom acting as a soft Lewis-base. On the other hand, [C6(COO)6]6− is a hard donor
ligand. Comparing the shifting of the photoluminescence of the two compounds, Figure 6, the
shifting of NH4[Cm(pydtc)4]·nCH3OH is much greater than Cm2[C6(COO)6](H2O)8·2H2O. This
observation is due to the nephelauxetic effect where the hybridization of ligand orbitals causes a
decrease in electron-electron repulsion thus decreasing the energy of excited states.
