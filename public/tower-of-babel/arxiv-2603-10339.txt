Insensitivity of the Coulomb breakup of halo nuclei to spectroscopic factors Journal: Physics Letters B
L.-P. Kubushishi, P. Capel

Abstract
Exotic nuclear structures such as halos are mostly studied using reactions. In Coulomb breakup, the radioactive projectile dissociates through its interaction with a heavy target. Often, a spectroscopic factor for the core-halo structure is inferred from experimental data. In this work, we present a new calculation of the Coulomb breakup of the one-neutron halo nucleus 11 Be performed with a coupled-channel effective particle-rotor model of that nucleus, which accounts for the excitation of the 10 Be core. Changes in the spectroscopic factor have no effect on the cross sections when the asymptotic normalisation coefficient is fixed, hence confirming the insensitivity of Coulomb-breakup cross sections to spectroscopic factors.



1
Introduction

In the 1980s, the development of radioactive-ion beams led to the discovery and study of halo nuclei
[
37
,
38
]
.
These exotic nuclear structures exhibit a large matter radius compared to their isobars
[
39
]
.
This exceptional size results from the weak binding for one or two nucleons, which enables the valence nucleons to tunnel far from the core and form a diffuse halo around a compact core
[
22
]
.
Typical examples are
11
Be and
15
C for one-neutron halo nuclei, and
6
He and
11
Li for two-neutron halos.

Being located far from stability, halo nuclei are short lived, and their structure is mostly studied through reactions, such as breakup
[
2
,
4
]
.
In breakup reactions, the halo dissociates from the core through the interaction with the target, hence unveiling the loosely-bound structure of the projectile.
From the analysis of data, a spectroscopic factor (SF) for the core-halo structure is usually inferred
[
34
,
20
]
.
The SF corresponds to the square of the norm of the dominant core-halo configuration in the reaction.
Although several articles have shown the non-observability of SFs in nuclear structure
[
21
,
30
,
29
]
, this practice is widely used within the nuclear-reaction community.
Moreover, breakup reactions have been shown to be peripheral in the sense that they probe only the tail of the projectile wave function
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
Being insensitive to the internal part of the wave function, they cannot be used to infer its norm.
Instead, they are much better suited to deduce the asymptotic normalisation coefficient (ANC) of the core-halo wave function.
Since this conclusion has been drawn assuming overlap wave functions in a single core-halo channel with a unit norm, it has been the subject of criticisms
[
24
]
.
So far, no analysis of this issue has been performed within a model of reaction including a coupled-channel description of the projectile structure, although such models exist
[
36
,
31
]
.

In the present work, we include the effective coupled-channel model of
11
Be developed in Ref.
[
27
]
in a first-order description of Coulomb breakup
[
3
,
6
]
.
This model accounts for the possible excitation of the
10
Be towards its first excited
2^{+}
state within a particle-rotor description of the nucleus
[
19
,
33
,
40
]
.
By changing the coupling between the different core-neutron configurations, we vary the SF in the main channel,
enabling us to assess its impact on the Coulomb-breakup cross section.
As an application, we consider the breakup of
11
Be on Pb at 69 MeV/nucleon, which has been measured at RIKEN
[
20
]
.

After defining the effective particle-rotor model of
11
Be, we briefly present how the Coulomb breakup is described at the first order of the perturbation, see Sec.
2
.
In Sec.
3
we present our results before drawing our conclusions in Sec.
4
.

2
Formalism

To compute the collision between
11
Be and
208
Pb, we consider the following three-body model.
The projectile
P
is described as a neutron n loosely bound to a
10
Be core
c
; it impinges on a Pb target
T
, of which the internal structure is neglected.

Following our previous work
[
27
]
, we improve the usual single-particle description of the projectile
[
4
]
by including core excitation through a collective model
[
7
]
.
The core is assumed to suffer a permanent quadrupole deformation and to be a rigid rotor
[
19
,
33
,
40
]
.
The
c
-n Hamiltonian then reads

H(\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\xi)=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\Delta+V_{c\rm n}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}},\xi)+h_{c}({\xi}),

(1)

where
r
is the
c
-n relative coordinate,
\xi
are the internal coordinates of the core, i.e., the Euler angles,
\mu=m_{c}m_{\rm n}/(m_{c}+m_{\rm n})
is the
c
-n reduced mass, with
m_{c}
and
m_{\rm n}
the masses of the core and the neutron, respectively.

The effective core-neutron potential
V_{c\rm n}
is non-central, as it explicitly depends on the core degrees of freedom
\xi
.
As explained in Ref.
[
27
]
, it is obtained by axially deforming the central
c
-n potential obtained within halo-effective field theory (Halo-EFT) at next-to-leading order
[
15
]

V(r;\sigma)=V_{J^{\pi}}^{(0)}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}+V_{J^{\pi}}^{(2)}r^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\quad,

(2)

where the range of the interaction
\sigma
is an unfitted parameter corresponding to the short-distance physics neglected in this approach.
The low-energy constants (LECs)
V_{J^{\pi}}^{(0)}
and
V_{J^{\pi}}^{(2)}
are adjusted for each value of the total angular momentum
J
and of the parity
\pi
to known structure observables such as the binding energy and the ANC.

The non-central part of
V_{c\rm n}
, induced by the deformation of the core, translates into an angle dependence, which reads at first order

V_{c\rm n}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\xi)=V(r;\sigma)+\beta\,\sigma\,Y_{2}^{0}(\hat{r}^{\prime})\frac{d}{d\sigma}V(r;\sigma),

(3)

where
\hat{r}^{\prime}
is the solid angle of the
c
-n relative coordinate
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}^{\prime}
expressed in the core’s intrinsic reference frame,
Y_{\lambda}^{\mu}
is a spherical harmonic and
\beta
is the quadrupole deformation of the core, which acts here as a coupling strength between the different
c
-n configurations.
In the derivation over
\sigma
, we neglect the second term of
V
, see Eq. (
2
).

In Eq. (
1
), the intrinsic Hamiltonian of the core
h_{c}
accounts for the core structure in a collective model.
It satisfies the eigenvalue equation

h_{c}({\xi})\phi_{M_{c}}^{I_{c}^{\pi_{c}}}(\xi)=\epsilon_{c}^{I_{c}^{\pi_{c}}}\phi_{M_{c}}^{I_{c}^{\pi_{c}}}(\xi)\quad,

(4)

where
\epsilon_{c}
are the energies of the core states identified by its spin
I_{c}
, spin projection
M_{c}
and parity
\pi_{c}
.
Since we describe the core as an axially symmetric rigid rotor, its eigenstates
\phi_{M_{c}}^{I_{c}\pi_{c}}
are given by the Wigner matrices
{\cal D}^{I_{c}}_{M_{c}K_{c}}

[
7
]
, where
K_{c}
is the bandhead of the rotational band.

The eigenstates
\Psi^{J^{\pi}M}
of the
c
-n Hamiltonian (
1
) are expanded in a basis coupling the
c
-n relative motion to the internal structure of the core

\Psi^{J^{\pi}M}(\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\xi)=\sum_{\alpha}i^{\ell}\frac{u_{\alpha}(r)}{r}[\mathcal{Y}_{\ell sj}(\hat{r})\otimes\phi^{I_{c}\pi_{c}}(\xi)]^{JM},

(5)

where
\alpha
=
\left\{n_{r},\ell,s,j,I_{c},\pi_{c}\right\}
defines a channel, i.e., a set of quantum numbers describing each allowed
c
-n configuration.
In details,
n_{r}
is the number of nodes in the radial wave function
u_{\alpha}
,
\ell
is the orbital angular momentum of the
c
-n relative motion,
s=\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}
is the neutron spin and
j
is the angular momentum resulting from the coupling of
\ell
and
s
:
\mbox{\boldmath$j$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\ell$}+\mbox{\boldmath$s$}
.
This angular momentum
j
is then coupled to the spin of the core
I_{c}
to obtain the total angular momentum
J
(
\mbox{\boldmath$J$}=\mbox{\boldmath$j$}+\mbox{\boldmath$I_{c}$}
).
The spin-angular part of the wave function
\mathcal{Y}_{lsjm}=[Y_{\ell}\otimes\chi_{s}]^{jm}
couples the spherical harmonic
Y_{\ell}^{m_{\ell}}
to the spinor
\chi_{s}^{m_{s}}
.

Expanding the
c
-n Hamiltonian (
1
) in the set of basis functions (
5
) leads to coupled-channel equations for the reduced radial wave functions
u_{\alpha}
.
We solve these coupled equations for bound and scattering states using the R-matrix method on a Lagrange mesh
[
17
,
5
]
.
For bound states, the spectroscopic factor of a given channel
\alpha
is defined as

{\cal S}_{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|u{{}_{\alpha}}(r)\right|^{2}dr\quad.

(6)

In this exploratory step, we describe the Coulomb breakup of the projectile at the first order in perturbation theory
[
3
,
6
]
.
We assume the reaction to be dominated by a pure E1 transition, and we simulate the
P
-
T
nuclear interaction by an impact-parameter cutoff
b_{\rm min}
.
The breakup cross section then reads

\frac{d\sigma_{\rm bu}}{dE}=\frac{16\pi^{3}}{9\hbar c}N_{{\rm E1}}(E,b_{\rm min})\frac{dB({\rm E1})}{dE}\quad,

(7)

where
N_{{\rm E}1}
is the number of equivalent photons exchanged between projectile and the target
[
3
,
6
]
and
dB({\rm E1})/dE
is the reduced E1 transition probability computed between the projectile ground state and its continuum states.

Admittedly, higher-order effects are present
[
18
,
11
]
and the nuclear interaction affects the reaction process
[
41
,
9
]
.
Nevertheless, the one-step E1 transition grasps most of the breakup strength, especially if the data are selected at forward angles
[
12
]
, as will be done in the next section.

Accordingly, we confirm the conclusion of previous analyses performed within single-particle descriptions of halo nuclei

that spectroscopic factors cannot be inferred from Coulomb breakup measurements.

4
Conclusion

Breakup reactions are used to probe the structure of halo nuclei
[
34
,
20
]
.
Often a SF for the core-halo structure is inferred from measurements.
Previous theoretical studies have shown that these reactions are peripheral in the sense that they probe only the tail of the projectile wave function, viz. the ANC and not the SF
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
Since these studies were made with single-particle descriptions of the projectile, their conclusion has been criticised
[
24
]
.

In this Letter, we have presented a new calculation of the Coulomb breakup of
11
Be using an effective particle-rotor description of the one-neutron halo nucleus
[
27
]
.
We have investigated the influence of deformation on the wave functions of the
\frac{1}{2}^{+}
ground state of
11
Be and computed the Coulomb breakup of
11
Be on
208
Pb at 69 MeV/nucleon at first order in perturbation theory.
The corresponding cross section is in good agreement with existing data
[
20
]
and shows no dependence on the SF of the ground state wave function in the main
1s_{1/2}\otimes 0^{+}
channel.
This answers the criticisms to the previous studies and confirms that SFs cannot be inferred from breakup experiments.
In a near future, we plan to include the effective particle-rotor description of Ref.
[
27
]
within a Coulomb-corrected eikonal reaction model
[
10
]
to account for both higher-order effects and projectile-target nuclear interactions, which are missing in the present study.
