Sharp Bounds on the Eigenvalues of Kikuchi Graphs and Applications to Quantum Max Cut
Ainesh Bakshi Arpon Basu ainesh@nyu.edu arpon.basu@princeton.edu NYU Princeton Pravesh Kothari Anqi Li kothari@cs.princeton.edu aqli@stanford.edu Princeton Stanford

Abstract
We prove that the maximum eigenvalue of the (both signed and unsigned) Laplacian of level k k Kikuchi graph of any graph G G with m m edges is at most m + k m+k . This confirms four recent conjectures of Apte, Parekh, and Sud. As applications, we obtain that tensor products of one and two qubit product states achieve an approximation ratio of 5 / 8 5/8 for Quantum Max Cut and 5 / 7 5/7 for the XY Hamiltonian. Moreover, combining our bounds with the algorithms analyzed by Apte, Parekh, and Sud, yields efficient algorithms achieving an approximation ratio of 0.614 0.614 for Quantum Max Cut and 0.674 0.674 for the XY Hamiltonian. Finally, we also make modest progress on Brouwer’s conjecture and improve Lew’s bound on the sum of the top- k k eigenvalues of a Graph Laplacian.

Introduction

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
with
m
edges.
For
0\leqslant k\leqslant n

Kikuchi graph
of

, has vertex set

\binom{[n]}{k}
. Two
k

are adjacent precisely when
their symmetric difference is an edge of

S\oplus T=\{i,j\}
for some edge

\{i,j\}
of

which can be interpreted as the graph of configurations of
k
indistinguishable tokens placed on
distinct vertices of
G

token
across
an edge of
G
to an unoccupied vertex.
The Kikuchi perspective is useful because it turns questions about high-dimensional incidence
structure into questions about walks, eigenvalues, and cycles in an ordinary graph. This
viewpoint has led to the proof of the Hypergraph Moore
Bound conjectured by Feige
[
7
]
and refutation algorithms for random constraint satisfaction

[
11

13
]

correctable codes
[
19
]

Yet another connection emerges from the quantum information literature, where Apte, Parekh, and
Sud
[
5
]
observed that the maximum energies of several well-studied
2
-local Hamiltonians
can be expressed in terms of extremal eigenvalues of Kikuchi graphs. In particular, after decomposing
the
n

weight

k
Kikuchi graph. Thus, sharp spectral bounds for Kikuchi graphs translate directly into
sharp upper bounds on the maximum energies of these local Hamiltonians. This observation led Apte,
Parekh, and Sud to propose a set of sharp spectral conjectures for Kikuchi graphs:

Conjecture 1.1

(Apte–Parekh–Sud Conjectures
[
5
]
)
.

Given a graph
G=([n],E)

be the associated level

be

be the signed and unsigned
Laplacian respectively. Then, for every graph

with
m=\lvert E\rvert

\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{\hspace{1.2em}}l@{\qquad\qquad}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{\hspace{1.2em}}l}\lambda_{\max}(L^{(k)}_{G})&\leqslant&m+k\hfil\hskip 11.99998pt&\textup{(C1)}&\lambda_{\max}(Q^{(k)}_{G})&\leqslant&m+k\hfil\hskip 11.99998pt&\textup{(C2)}\\[5.0pt]

In this paper, we resolve the four APS conjectures (C1)–(C4).

Theorem 1.2
.

For any graph
G=([n],E)
with
|E|=m
edges, and any

-(m+k)\leqslant-\lambda_{\max}(L^{(k)}_{G})\leqslant 2\lambda_{\min}(A^{(k)}_{G})\leqslant 2\lambda_{\max}(A^{(k)}_{G})\leqslant\lambda_{\max}(Q^{(k)}_{G})\leqslant m+k.

Prior to this work, the best known general bound in this direction was Lew’s estimate
for the unsigned Laplacian:

[
22
]
. Further,

k

k

Applications.

Local Hamiltonians are the basic mathematical model for quantum many-body
systems. A Hamiltonian is a Hermitian operator whose eigenvalues are the possible energies
of the system. In physically relevant models, it is typically a sum of simple terms, each
involving only a few particles. The central computational task is to estimate an extremal
energy: the ground-state energy, or, after negating the Hamiltonian, the maximum energy.
From the perspective of theoretical computer science, this is the quantum analogue of
constraint satisfaction, containing classical problems such as Max Cut and
3
-SAT as
special cases. We focus on
2
-local Hamiltonians of the form

H=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{i,j}h_{i,j},\qquad w_{i,j}>0,

where
h_{i,j}
acts non-trivially only on particles
i
and
j
. The maximum energy is

\lambda_{\max}(H)=\max_{\|\psi\|=1}\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle
.
We consider the three families of
2
-local Hamiltonians that have been extensively studied,
the Quantum Max Cut (QMC) Hamiltonian, the EPR Hamiltonian, and the XY Hamiltonian:

\begin{gathered}H_{\mathrm{QMC}}=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lparen I_{i}I_{j}-X_{i}X_{j}-Y_{i}Y_{j}-Z_{i}Z_{j}\right\rparen,\\
\begin{aligned} H_{\mathrm{EPR}}&=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lparen I_{i}I_{j}+X_{i}X_{j}+Y_{i}Y_{j}-Z_{i}Z_{j}\right\rparen,\qquad H_{\mathrm{XY}}&=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}\left\lparen I_{i}I_{j}-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lparen X_{i}X_{j}+Y_{i}Y_{j}\right\rparen\right\rparen.\end{aligned}\end{gathered}

Here
X_{i}
denotes the operator acting as
X
on qubit
i
and as the identity
on all other qubits, and similarly for
Y_{i},Z_{i},I_{i}
and together they form the set of
Pauli matrices:

I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

As a direct consequence of
[

approximation guarantees for the QMC, EPR, and XY problems.

Brouwer’s Conjecture.

Brouwer’s conjecture is a central open problem on partial sums of Laplacian
eigenvalues. For a graph
G=([n],E)
, let
\varepsilon_{r}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}(L(G))-\lvert E\rvert
.
Brouwer
[
6

12
]
conjectured that for every graph
G
and every
1\leqslant r\leqslant n
,

\varepsilon_{r}(G)\leqslant\binom{r+1}{2}
.

Theorem 1.4

(Improved estimates for Brouwer’s conjecture)
.

Let
G=([n],E)
be a graph with
m
edges, and order the Laplacian eigenvalues as

\lambda_{1}(L(G))\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n}(L(G))
.
For every
1\leqslant r\leqslant n
, we have

\varepsilon_{r}(G)\leqslant\binom{r+1}{2}+\frac{4}{3}r^{3/2}-r+\sqrt{r}.

Theorem

1.4
improves a recent estimate of Lew
[
22
]
, who showed
that
\varepsilon_{r}(G)\leqslant\binom{r+1}{2}+4r^{3/2}-r-2\sqrt{r}
. The asymptotic improvement is
a factor of
1/3
on the leading term.

Notation and Preliminaries

For any two sets
X,Y
, define their symmetric difference
X\oplus Y
to be

(X\setminus Y)\cup(Y\setminus X)
. It is easy to see that
X\oplus Y=Y\oplus X
,
and if
X\oplus Y=Z
, then
X\oplus Z=Y
.

Definition 2.1

(Kikuchi Graphs)
.

Let
G=([n],E)
be a graph with
m=\lvert E\rvert
edges. For any
k\leqslant n
, the

level
k
Kikuchi graph

F_{k}(G)
of
G
is a graph on
\binom{[n]}{k}

with edges
S\sim T
if and only if
S\oplus T\in E
.

We denote the Laplacian of
F_{k}(G)
by
L_{G}^{(k)}
. Also denote by
A_{G}^{(k)}
the
adjacency matrix of
F_{k}(G)
, and let

D_{G}^{(k)}\in\mathbbm{R}^{\binom{[n]}{k}\times\binom{[n]}{k}}
be the
degree matrix
of

F_{k}(G)
, i.e.
D_{G}^{(k)}
is a diagonal matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by
elements of
\binom{[n]}{k}
, with
D_{G}^{(k)}(S,S)
being defined to be the degree of
the vertex
S\in\binom{[n]}{k}
in the graph
F_{k}(G)
.

Note that
L_{G}^{(k)}=D_{G}^{(k)}-A_{G}^{(k)}
. We also define the
unsigned

Laplacian
Q_{G}^{(k)}
to be
Q_{G}^{(k)}:=D_{G}^{(k)}+A_{G}^{(k)}
. It is a standard fact
that
L_{G}^{(k)},Q_{G}^{(k)}
are positive semidefinite matrices, i.e.

L_{G}^{(k)}\succcurlyeq 0,Q_{G}^{(k)}\succcurlyeq 0
. Here
\succcurlyeq
refers to the
Loewner order on Hermitian matrices, with
A\succcurlyeq B
denoting that
A-B
is
positive semidefinite. One consequence of positive semidefiniteness is the following:

Proposition 2.2
.

-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\lambda_{\max}(L_{G}^{(k)})\leqslant\lambda_{\min}(A_{G}^{(k)})\leqslant\lambda_{\max}(A_{G}^{(k)})\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\cdot\lambda_{\max}(Q_{G}^{(k)})
.

Proof.

Note that
Q_{G}^{(k)}-L_{G}^{(k)}=2A_{G}^{(k)}
, and thus

A_{G}^{(k)}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lparen Q_{G}^{(k)}-L_{G}^{(k)}\right\rparen\overset{(\ast)}{\preccurlyeq}\frac{1}{2}\cdot Q_{G}^{(k)}\implies\lambda_{\max}(A_{G}^{(k)})\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\cdot\lambda_{\max}(Q_{G}^{(k)}),

where
(\ast)
uses the fact that
L_{G}^{(k)}\succcurlyeq 0
. Similarly, we also have

A_{G}^{(k)}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lparen Q_{G}^{(k)}-L_{G}^{(k)}\right\rparen\overset{(\ast)}{\succcurlyeq}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot L_{G}^{(k)}\implies\lambda_{\min}(A_{G}^{(k)})\geqslant-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\lambda_{\max}(L_{G}^{(k)}).\qed

Thus to establish
Theorem

1.2
it suffices to show that

\lambda_{\max}(L_{G}^{(k)}),\lambda_{\max}(Q_{G}^{(k)})\leqslant m+k
.

Eigenvalue Bounds for Kikuchi Graphs

In this section, we prove the Laplacian and signless Laplacian bounds in the
Apte–Parekh–Sud conjectures.
It suffices to prove these two bounds: once the signless Laplacian bound is known,
the adjacency bound in
1.1
follows from

Proposition

2.2
and

\rho(Q_{G}^{(k)})=\lambda_{\max}(Q_{G}^{(k)})
.
We treat the two cases simultaneously.
For
b\in\{-1,1\}
, define

L_{b,G}^{(k)}=D_{G}^{(k)}+bA_{G}^{(k)}.

Thus
L_{-1,G}^{(k)}=L_{G}^{(k)}
and
L_{1,G}^{(k)}=Q_{G}^{(k)}
.
The main theorem of this section is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any graph
G=([n],E)
with
m=\lvert E\rvert
, any
0\leqslant k\leqslant n
, and any

\lambda_{\max}(L_{b,G}^{(k)})\leqslant m+k.

Setting up definitions.

For an edge

\mathcal{E}_{f}^{(k)}=\left\{\{S,S\oplus f\}:S\in\binom{[n]}{k},\,\lvert S\cap f\rvert=1\right\}

(1)

be the set of token-graph edges obtained by moving a token across the fixed edge

The edge set of

E(F_{k}(G))=\bigcup_{f\in E(G)}\mathcal{E}_{f}^{(k)}.

(2)

For

(3)

The proof proceeds by induction on
k

The key idea is to differentiate an eigenvector across an edge of the original graph.
For
b=-1

b=1

the corresponding signless edge derivative. Edges disjoint from the differentiated edge
produce a
(k-1)

coordinate permutations of neighboring edge derivatives.

The case

Next, consider the edge gradient vector

e=\{i,j\}
gives

\lambda\sum_{e\in E(G)}\left\lVert g_{e}\right\rVert_{2}\leqslant\sum_{e=\{i,j\}\in E(G)}\left\lparen 2+m-d_{i}-d_{j}+1+(k-1)\right\rparen\left\lVert g_{e}\right\rVert_{2}+\sum_{e\in E(G)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in E(G)\\
\lvert f\cap e\rvert=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert g_{f}\right\rVert_{2}.

(12)

We reindex the double sum. Fix an edge
f=\{u,v\}
.
The number of edges
e
sharing exactly one endpoint with
f
is

(d_{u}-1)+(d_{v}-1)=d_{u}+d_{v}-2.

Thus the total coefficient of
\left\lVert g_{f}\right\rVert_{2}
on the right-hand side of
(
12
) is

2+m-d_{u}-d_{v}+1+(k-1)+(d_{u}+d_{v}-2)=m+k.

Therefore,

\lambda\sum_{e\in E(G)}\left\lVert g_{e}\right\rVert_{2}\leqslant(m+k)\sum_{e\in E(G)}\left\lVert g_{e}\right\rVert_{2}.

If
\sum_{e}\left\lVert g_{e}\right\rVert_{2}>0
, we divide by this quantity and obtain
\lambda\leqslant m+k
.
If
\sum_{e}\left\lVert g_{e}\right\rVert_{2}=0
, then
x_{S}+bx_{T}=0
for every token-graph edge

\{S,T\}
. Hence every local summand in
L_{b,G}^{(k)}x
vanishes, so

L_{b,G}^{(k)}x=0
and
\lambda=0
.
In either case,
\lambda\leqslant m+k
.
Since
\lambda
was an arbitrary eigenvalue of
L_{b,G}^{(k)}
, this proves the theorem.
∎

4
Improved Bounds for Brouwer’s Conjecture

In this section, we prove
Theorem

1.4
.

Background and notation.

Brouwer’s conjecture predicts that the error term
\varepsilon_{r}(G)
is at most

\binom{r+1}{2}
for every graph
G
and every
1\leqslant r\leqslant n
.
We recast the partial sum
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}(L(G))
as the largest eigenvalue
of a natural operator on an exterior power.
For
U\subseteq[n]
, let
H_{s}(U)=\bigwedge\nolimits^{s}\mathbbm{R}^{U}
, with the conventions

H_{0}(U)=\mathbbm{R}
and
H_{s}(U)=\{0\}
if
s<0
or
s>\lvert U\rvert
.
For an
s
-set
S=\{v_{1}<\cdots<v_{s}\}\subseteq U
, write

e_{S}=e_{v_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{v_{s}}
; the vectors
(e_{S})_{\lvert S\rvert=s}

form an orthonormal basis of
H_{s}(U)
.

Let
M:\mathbbm{R}^{U}\to\mathbbm{R}^{U}
be a linear operator. Its
s
-th additive compound is the operator

M^{[s]}:H_{s}(U)\to H_{s}(U)
defined on decomposable vectors by

M^{[s]}(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{s})=\sum_{q=1}^{s}v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge Mv_{q}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{s}.

For
s=0
, set
M^{[0]}=0
on
H_{0}(U)=\mathbbm{R}
.
We write
A_{s}(G)=L(G)^{[s]}
for the
s
-th additive compound of the Laplacian.
Thus, if
L(G)z_{i}=\lambda_{i}(L(G))z_{i}
, then

z_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge z_{i_{s}}
is an eigenvector of
A_{s}(G)
with eigenvalue

\lambda_{i_{1}}(L(G))+\cdots+\lambda_{i_{s}}(L(G))
.
With the eigenvalues ordered as above, this gives

\lambda_{\max}(A_{s}(G))=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}(L(G)).

(13)

See for instance
[
22
,
8
,
23
]
and the references therein for further exposition and properties of the additive compound.

Proof.

The proof follows the same edge-gradient template as the proof of

Theorem

3.1
.
Differentiating across an edge
e=\{i,j\}
turns edges disjoint from
e
into an
operator on
G-\{i,j\}
, while adjacent edges are handled by a twist term.
The exterior-power gradient
G_{e}
has two components:
u_{e}
is the signed analogue
of the token-graph edge derivative, and
w_{e}
records the coordinates in which both
endpoints of
e
are present.
We introduce an auxiliary operator
B_{r}
so that the quadratic form of one edge is

\left\lVert u_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert w_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
.

The edge operators.

For an edge
e=\{i,j\}
, let
L_{e}=(e_{i}-e_{j})(e_{i}-e_{j})^{\top}
and define

A_{s,e}=L_{e}^{[s]}
.
This is the one-edge Laplacian operator, as in the previous section.
Let
W_{s,e}
be the projection

W_{s,e}e_{S}=\begin{cases}e_{S},&e\subseteq S,\\
0,&e\not\subseteq S.\end{cases}

Define
B_{s,e}=A_{s,e}-W_{s,e}
,
W_{s}(G)=\sum_{e\in E(G)}W_{s,e}
, and

B_{s}(G)=\sum_{e\in E(G)}B_{s,e}
.
In particular,
A_{s}(G)=B_{s}(G)+W_{s}(G)
.
The operator
W_{s}(G)
is diagonal in the exterior basis:

W_{s}(G)e_{S}=e_{G}(S)e_{S}
, where
e_{G}(S)
is the number of edges induced by
S
.
Hence,

0\preccurlyeq W_{s}(G)\preccurlyeq\binom{s}{2}I.

(14)

Remark 4.1
.

The point of introducing
B_{r}(G)
is that a direct application of the token-graph
Laplacian bound from the previous section gives only the weaker estimate

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}(L(G))\leqslant\lambda_{\max}(A_{r}(G))\leqslant m+r^{2}
.
Indeed,
A_{r}(G)=L(G)^{[r]}
contains the signed
r
-token Laplacian together with
an extra diagonal contribution from edges with both endpoints in the
r
-set:

A_{r}(G)=L_{G}^{(r)}+2W_{r}(G)
.
Combining
Theorem

1.2
with (
14
) gives

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}(L(G))\leqslant m+r+2\binom{r}{2}=m+r^{2}
.
The improvement in
Theorem

1.4
comes from applying the edge-gradient
argument to the grouped term
B_{r}(G)=L_{G}^{(r)}+W_{r}(G)
.

We first record a useful identity relating
B_{s,e}
and
W_{s,e}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every edge
e
and every
s\geqslant 0
, the operator
B_{s,e}
is positive semidefinite and
satisfies
B_{s,e}^{2}=2B_{s,e}-W_{s,e}
.

Proof.

Since the single-edge operator only acts on the two coordinates
i
and
j
, the space

H_{s}([n])
decomposes according to whether the indexing set
S

contains neither endpoint of
e
, both endpoints of
e
, or exactly one endpoint of
e
.
We check the claim on each of these subspaces.

First, if
S
contains neither endpoint of
e
, then
A_{s,e}e_{S}=0
,
W_{s,e}e_{S}=0
, and

B_{s,e}e_{S}=0
.
Thus both sides of the identity vanish on this subspace.
Second, if
S
contains both endpoints of
e
, then the one-edge Laplacian contributes
eigenvalue
2
to the additive compound, so
A_{s,e}e_{S}=2e_{S}
.
Also
W_{s,e}e_{S}=e_{S}
, and hence

B_{s,e}e_{S}=e_{S}
.
Here
B_{s,e}=W_{s,e}=I
, so
B_{s,e}^{2}=I=2B_{s,e}-W_{s,e}
.
It remains to consider the subspace spanned by

e_{R\cup\{i\}}
and
e_{R\cup\{j\}}
, where

R\subseteq[n]\setminus\{i,j\}
and
\lvert R\rvert=s-1
.
Up to a possible change in signs from a suitable change of basis,
A_{s,e}=B_{s,e}

has matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{pmatrix},

whose eigenvalues are
0
and
2
.
On this subspace
W_{s,e}=0
.
Moreover, this matrix squares to twice itself, so again

B_{s,e}^{2}=2B_{s,e}-W_{s,e}
.
Over all cases, the only eigenvalues of
B_{s,e}
are
0,1,2
, so

B_{s,e}\succcurlyeq 0
.
∎

Fix an orientation of every edge.
For an oriented edge
e=(i,j)
, set
V_{e}=[n]\setminus\{i,j\}
.
Let
\iota_{i}
denote contraction by
e_{i}
, so that

\iota_{i}(e_{v_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{v_{s}})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{p-1}e_{v_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{e_{v_{p}}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{v_{s}},&\text{if }i=v_{p},\\
0,&\text{if }i\notin\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\}.\end{cases}

We also let
\pi_{e}^{(t)}:H_{t}([n])\to H_{t}(V_{e})
be the projection defined by

\pi_{e}^{(t)}e_{S}=\begin{cases}e_{S},&S\subseteq V_{e},\\
0,&S\not\subseteq V_{e}.\end{cases}

That is,
\pi_{e}^{(t)}
keeps only the coordinates indexed by
t
-sets disjoint from the endpoints of
e
.
For
x\in H_{r}([n])
, define

u_{e}(x)=\pi_{e}^{(r-1)}(\iota_{i}-\iota_{j})x\in H_{r-1}(V_{e}),\qquad w_{e}(x)=\pi_{e}^{(r-2)}\iota_{j}\iota_{i}x\in H_{r-2}(V_{e}).

We define the “edge gradient” to be

G_{e}x=\left\lparen u_{e}(x),w_{e}(x)\right\rparen\in H_{r-1}(V_{e})\oplus H_{r-2}(V_{e}).

Thus
u_{e}
is the signed version of the difference between the two coefficients indexed by

R\cup\{i\}
and
R\cup\{j\}
, while
w_{e}
records the signed coefficient indexed by

P\cup\{i,j\}
.
The adjoint of
G_{e}:H_{r}([n])\to H_{r-1}(V_{e})\oplus H_{r-2}(V_{e})
is

G_{e}^{*}:H_{r-1}(V_{e})\oplus H_{r-2}(V_{e})\to H_{r}([n])
, defined by

\langle G_{e}x,(u,w)\rangle=\langle x,G_{e}^{*}(u,w)\rangle
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For every edge
e
, we have

G_{e}^{*}G_{e}=B_{r,e}.

(15)

Consequently,

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\left\lVert G_{e}x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\langle x,B_{r}(G)x\rangle.

(16)

Proof.

As before, we split into the three cases determined by
S\cap e
.
First, if
S
contains neither endpoint of
e
, then both components of
G_{e}
vanish,
which agrees with
B_{r,e}=0
.
Second, if
S
contains both endpoints, then
u_{e}
vanishes and
w_{e}
records that
coefficient with a sign, agreeing with
B_{r,e}=I
.

Finally, on the span of
e_{R\cup\{i\}}
and
e_{R\cup\{j\}}
, the map
G_{e}

becomes
\alpha-\varepsilon\beta
, with
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
determined by the ordering
in the exterior product.
The associated quadratic form is
(\alpha-\varepsilon\beta)^{2}
, agreeing with the
quadratic form of
B_{r,e}
.
Thus
G_{e}^{*}G_{e}=B_{r,e}
, and summing over
e\in E(G)
gives
(
16
).
∎

For an edge
e=\{i,j\}
, write
G-\{i,j\}=G[[n]\setminus\{i,j\}]
, and let

a_{e}=d_{i}+d_{j}-2
be the number of edges adjacent to
e
.
For
x\in H_{r}([n])
, define

T_{r}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e,f\in E(G)\\
e\neq f,\ e\cap f\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\langle x,B_{r,e}B_{r,f}x\rangle.

As we will see, this term is the analogue of the twist term in the previous section.

Lemma 4.4

(Derivative identity)
.

Let
x\in H_{r}([n])
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(G)}\langle G_{e}x,G_{e}(B_{r}(G)x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{e=\{i,j\}\in E(G)}\left\langle u_{e}(x),\left\lparen 2I+B_{r-1}(G-\{i,j\})\right\rparen u_{e}(x)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{e=\{i,j\}\in E(G)}\left\langle w_{e}(x),\left\lparen I+B_{r-2}(G-\{i,j\})\right\rparen w_{e}(x)\right\rangle+T_{r}(x).

Proof.

We fix
e=(i,j)
and expand
B_{r}(G)=\sum_{f\in E(G)}B_{r,f}
.
First, if
f
is disjoint from
e
, then
B_{r,f}
acts only on coordinates in
V_{e}
, while the
contractions defining
u_{e}
and
w_{e}
remove
i
and
j
. Hence, we have

u_{e}(B_{r,f}x)=B_{r-1,f}u_{e}(x),\qquad w_{e}(B_{r,f}x)=B_{r-2,f}w_{e}(x).

Summing over such
f
gives the operators
B_{r-1}(G-\{i,j\})
and

B_{r-2}(G-\{i,j\})
respectively.

Second, if
f=e
, then

G_{e}(B_{r,e}x)=\left\lparen 2u_{e}(x),w_{e}(x)\right\rparen.

Finally, if
f
is adjacent to
e
and
f\neq e
, then by
(
15
),

\langle G_{e}x,G_{e}(B_{r,f}x)\rangle=\langle x,B_{r,e}B_{r,f}x\rangle.

Summing over all
e
and all adjacent
f\neq e
gives
T_{r}(x)
.
∎

Bounding
T_{r}
with stars.

For a vertex
v
, write
E_{v}=\left\{e\in E(G):v\in e\right\}
and

S_{v}=\sum_{e\in E_{v}}B_{r,e}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
v
has degree
d
, then
\lambda_{\max}(S_{v})\leqslant d+\sqrt{r}
.

We note that a variant of this lemma was established in
[
22
, Lemma 5.1]
, though we include its short proof here for completeness.

Proof.

Let
X
be the set of the
d
neighbors of
v
. It is clear that it suffices to analyze the exterior space generated by
\{v\}\cup X
.
Fix
q
and consider the corresponding space

\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathbbm{R}^{X}\oplus\left\lparen e_{v}\wedge\bigwedge\nolimits^{q-1}\mathbbm{R}^{X}\right\rparen.

The first summand consists of configurations not containing the center
v
, and the second consists of configurations containing
v
. On
\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathbbm{R}^{X}
, each chosen leaf contributes one edge, so the
diagonal part is
qI
. On
e_{v}\wedge\bigwedge\nolimits^{q-1}\mathbbm{R}^{X}
, the diagonal part is
dI
.
Up to harmless sign changes of exterior basis vectors, the off-diagonal part corresponds to contraction by
\mathbf{1}_{X}=\sum_{x\in X}e_{x}
. Therefore,
S_{v}
has block matrix

M_{q}=\begin{pmatrix}qI&C_{q}^{*}\\
C_{q}&dI\end{pmatrix},

where
C_{q}:\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathbbm{R}^{X}\to\bigwedge\nolimits^{q-1}\mathbbm{R}^{X}
is contraction by
\mathbf{1}_{X}
, again up to changes of signed basis.
Since
\left\lVert\mathbf{1}_{X}\right\rVert_{2}=\sqrt{d}
and contraction by a unit vector has operator norm at most
1
on exterior space, we have
\left\lVert C_{q}\right\rVert\leqslant\sqrt{d}
. Thus, the Rayleigh quotient of
M_{q}
is at most that of
\begin{pmatrix}q&\sqrt{d}\\
\sqrt{d}&d\end{pmatrix}
. That is, for
q\in[d]
, we have

\lambda_{\max}(M_{q})\leqslant\frac{d+q+\sqrt{(d-q)^{2}+4d}}{2}\leqslant d+\sqrt{q}\leqslant d+\sqrt{r}.

Finally, the edge cases: If
q=0
, the only eigenvalue is
0
; if
q=d+1
, then the eigenvalue is
d
, proving the desired.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For every
x\in H_{r}([n])
,

T_{r}(x)\leqslant\sum_{e\in E(G)}(a_{e}+2\sqrt{r}-2)\left\lVert u_{e}(x)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{e\in E(G)}(a_{e}+2\sqrt{r})\left\lVert w_{e}(x)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

We rewrite
T_{r}
by grouping terms according to their common vertex

T_{r}(x)=\sum_{v\in[n]}\left\langle x,\left\lparen S_{v}^{2}-\sum_{e\in E_{v}}B_{r,e}^{2}\right\rparen x\right\rangle.

By
Lemma

4.5
,
0\leqslant S_{v}\leqslant(d_{v}+\sqrt{r})I
, so that
S_{v}^{2}\leqslant(d_{v}+\sqrt{r})S_{v}.

Combining this with
Lemma

4.2
, we have

S_{v}^{2}-\sum_{e\in E_{v}}B_{r,e}^{2}=S_{v}^{2}-2S_{v}+\sum_{e\in E_{v}}W_{r,e}\leqslant(d_{v}+\sqrt{r}-2)S_{v}+\sum_{e\in E_{v}}W_{r,e}.

Taking the quadratic form at
x
and summing over
v
gives

T_{r}(x)\leqslant\sum_{v\in[n]}(d_{v}+\sqrt{r}-2)\sum_{e\in E_{v}}\langle x,B_{r,e}x\rangle+\sum_{v\in[n]}\sum_{e\in E_{v}}\langle x,W_{r,e}x\rangle.

By
Lemma

4.3
,
\langle x,B_{r,e}x\rangle=\left\lVert u_{e}(x)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert w_{e}(x)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
.
We also have
\langle x,W_{r,e}x\rangle=\left\lVert w_{e}(x)\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
.
Combining all these gives the desired conclusion.
∎

Induction.

Define
\theta_{0}=0
and

\theta_{r}=\theta_{r-1}+2\sqrt{r}-1
for
r\geqslant 1
.
Equivalently,

\theta_{r}=2\sum_{s=1}^{r}\sqrt{s}-r.

(17)

Proposition 4.7
.

For every simple graph
G
with
m
edges and every
r\geqslant 1
, we have

\lambda_{\max}(B_{r}(G))\leqslant m+\theta_{r}.

Proof.

The base case is
r=1
, where

\lambda_{\max}(B_{1}(G))=\lambda_{\max}(L(G))\leqslant m+1=m+\theta_{1}
.
This follows for instance from
[
1
]
. It suffices to prove the inductive step for
r\geqslant 2
.
Let
\nu=\lambda_{\max}(B_{r}(G))
and let
x\in H_{r}([n])
be a corresponding unit
eigenvector
B_{r}(G)x=\nu x
.
Since
B_{r}(G)\succcurlyeq 0
, we have
\nu\geqslant 0
and we may assume
\nu>0
.
By (
16
), since
G_{e}(B_{r}(G)x)=\nu G_{e}x
, we have

\nu\sum_{e\in E(G)}\left\lVert G_{e}x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{e\in E(G)}\langle G_{e}x,G_{e}(B_{r}(G)x)\rangle.

(18)

For
e=\{i,j\}
, by the induction hypothesis applied to
G-\{i,j\}
, we have

\lambda_{\max}(B_{r-1}(G-\{i,j\}))\leqslant m-a_{e}-1+\theta_{r-1},\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{\max}(B_{r-2}(G-\{i,j\}))\leqslant m-a_{e}-1+\theta_{r-2}.

Therefore, we have

\left\langle u_{e},\left\lparen 2I+B_{r-1}(G-\{i,j\})\right\rparen u_{e}\right\rangle\leqslant(m-a_{e}+1+\theta_{r-1})\left\lVert u_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

and

\left\langle w_{e},\left\lparen I+B_{r-2}(G-\{i,j\})\right\rparen w_{e}\right\rangle\leqslant(m-a_{e}+\theta_{r-2})\left\lVert w_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Combining these estimates with
Lemma

4.4
and
Lemma

4.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nu\sum_{e}\left\lVert G_{e}x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{e}\left\lparen m-a_{e}+1+\theta_{r-1}+a_{e}+2\sqrt{r}-2\right\rparen\left\lVert u_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{e}\left\lparen m-a_{e}+\theta_{r-2}+a_{e}+2\sqrt{r}\right\rparen\left\lVert w_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

The coefficient of
\left\lVert u_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
is
m+\theta_{r-1}+2\sqrt{r}-1=m+\theta_{r}
,
while the coefficient of
\left\lVert w_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
, since
r\geqslant 2
, is

m+\theta_{r-2}+2\sqrt{r}\leqslant m+\theta_{r}
.
Plugging into (
18
), we get

\nu\sum_{e}\left\lVert G_{e}x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leqslant(m+\theta_{r})\sum_{e}\left\lparen\left\lVert u_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert w_{e}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right\rparen=(m+\theta_{r})\sum_{e}\left\lVert G_{e}x\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

which implies that
\nu\leqslant m+\theta_{r}
. This completes the induction.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

Recall that
A_{r}(G)=B_{r}(G)+W_{r}(G)
.
By (
14
) and
Proposition

4.7
, we have

\lambda_{\max}(A_{r}(G))\leqslant\lambda_{\max}(B_{r}(G))+\binom{r}{2}\leqslant m+\theta_{r}+\binom{r}{2}=m+\binom{r+1}{2}+2\sum_{s=1}^{r}(\sqrt{s}-1),

where the last equality is due to (
17
). The desired conclusion follows from (
13
).
The final numerical bound in the theorem follows from

\sum_{s=1}^{r}\sqrt{s}\leqslant\int_{0}^{r}\sqrt{x}\,dx+\sqrt{r}=\frac{2}{3}r^{3/2}+\sqrt{r}
and
\sqrt{r}\leqslant r
.
∎
