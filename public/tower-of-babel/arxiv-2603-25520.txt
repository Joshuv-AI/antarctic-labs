Regularity of solutions to Monge–Ampère equations on compact Hermitian manifolds
Quang-Tuan Dang

Abstract
. We study the stability and Hölder continuity of solutions to degenerate complex Monge–Ampère equations associated with a (non-closed) big form on compact Hermitian manifolds. We also show that the solution is globally continuous when the reference form is the pullback of a Hermitian metric. As a consequence, we establish a uniform diameter bound for the twisted Chern–Ricci flow.

The regularity of solutions to complex degenerate complex Monge-Ampère equations has deep implications in both complex dynamics and complex geometry; see, for instance,

,

Let
X
be a compact complex
n
-dimensional manifold equipped with a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}
. Let
\theta
be a smooth real (1,1) form on
X
.
We let
\PSH(X,\theta)
denote the set of
\theta
-plurisubharmonic (
\theta
-psh for short) functions which are defined as being locally the sum of a plurisubharmonic function and a smooth one and any such function
\varphi
satisfies
\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
in the weak sense of currents. Here, we put
d^{c}=\frac{i}{2}(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
so that

.

We say that
\theta
is
big
if there exists a
\theta
-psh function
\psi_{0}
with analytic singularities (see Definition
2.1
) such that
\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{0}
dominates a Hermitian form. We let
\Omega
denote the Zariski open set where
\psi_{0}
is locally bounded and
\psi_{0}=-\infty
on
\partial\Omega

Following Bedford-Taylor’s theory
[
1
,
2
]
, it was shown (see e.g.
[

,

]
) that for any
\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)

the complex Monge-Ampère operator

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}

is a well-defined positive Borel measure in
\Omega
.
It is therefore meaningful to study the complex Monge-Ampère equation

(1.1)

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=c\mu\quad\text{in}\;\Omega,

for a given positive measure
\mu
and
c
a normalization constant.

When
\mu=fdV_{X}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
dV_{X}
, with density
f\in L^{p}(X)
for some
p>1
, extending the result of
[
3
]
, it has been known in
[
4
, Theorem D]
that there exists a solution
(\varphi,c)\in\PSH(X,\theta)\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
to (
1.1
). Moreover, such a solution is continuous on the Zariski open set

(see
[
33
, Theorem 3.7]
). In this setting, Hölder continuity is typically the strongest regularity we can expect.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mu=fdV_{X}
be a measure absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure with density
0\leq f\in L^{p}(X,dV_{X})
,
p>1
.
Let
(\varphi,c)\in\PSH(X,\theta)\times(0,+\infty)
be such that
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
,

V_{\theta}-C_{0}\leq\varphi\leq V_{\theta},\qquad(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=c\mu\;\text{in}\,\Omega,

with a uniform constant
C_{0}>0
. Then
\varphi
is Hölder continuous in
\Omega
.

Here
V_{\theta}=\sup\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\leq 0\}
is a
\theta
-psh function with minimal singularities.
We note that the constant
c>0
is uniformly bounded in terms of
n
,
p
,
\omega_{X}
, and a lower bound for
\int_{X}f^{1/n}dV_{X}
; see
[
4
, Theorem 4.7]
. The proof of Theorem
A
relies on the stability result (cf. Theorem
3.3
) together with the arguments of
[
12
, Theorem D]
, which are based on Demailly’s regularization technique. Our proof of the stability result is based on the use of auxiliary equations, an approach that can also be applied in the local setting.
We refer interested readers to
[

,

,

,

,

The higher regularity of solutions on the Zariski open set

Next, we extend the results of Dinew–Zhang

Theorem B
.

Let

be compact complex variety of dimension

with log terminal singularities, equipped a Hermitian form
\omega_{V}
. Let
\pi:X\to V
be a log resolution of singularities. Set

Assume that

\sup_{X}\varphi=0,\qquad(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=cfdV_{X}.

The existence of a globally bounded solution was established in
[

]

Finally, we apply our continuity results to study diameter bounds along the Chern–Ricci flow, in analogy with
[
17
]
for the Kähler–Ricci flow. Generalizing the work of Guedj–Zeriahi
[

with initial condition

, that is, a smooth family of Hermitian metrics
(\omega_{t})_{t\in[0,T_{\max})}
satisfies

(1.2)

\frac{\partial\omega_{t}}{\partial t}=-\textrm{Ric}(\omega_{t})+\eta,\quad\omega_{t}\xrightarrow{t\to 0}T_{0}\;\text{weakly}.

Here

denotes the Chern–Ricci form of

,

is a smooth

-form, and

is a positive
(1,1)
-current with
\varphi_{0}\in L^{\infty}(X)

T_{\max}=\sup\{t>0:\exists\,\hat{\psi}_{t}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X),\omega_{X}+t(\eta-\textrm{Ric}(\omega_{X}))+dd^{c}\hat{\psi}_{t}>0\}.

Note that the convergence at

holds in the weak sense of currents. We further investigate diameter bounds for solutions to the twisted Chern–Ricci flow (
1.2
) under geometric assumptions on the initial data

.

Theorem C
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
.
Assume that
\varphi_{0}
is continuous and

are quasi-psh function on

Then for any

\textrm{diam}(X,\omega_{t})\leq C\qquad d_{\omega_{t}}(x,y)\leq Cd_{\omega_{X}}(x,y)^{\alpha},\;\forall\,t\in[0,T_{\max}/2],

for some constants

that only depend on

,
\omega_{X}
,

and an upper bound for
\|e^{-\psi^{-}}\|_{p}
, where

denotes the Riemannian distance associated to

.

The diameter bound of the families of Kähler metrics has been studied in
[

,

,

,

,

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper,
X
denotes a compact Hermitian manifold of complex dimension
n
, equipped with a Hermitian form
\omega_{X}
.
Let
dV_{X}:={\omega_{X}^{n}}/n!

denote the volume form associated with
\omega_{X}
. For any
p\geq 1
, we simply write
\|f\|_{p}
for the
L^{p}(X,dV_{X})
-norm of
f
.

2.1.
Quasi-psh functions and Monge-Ampère measures

Recall that an upper semi-continuous function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}

is called
quasi-plurisubharmonic
(
quasi-psh
for short) if it is locally the sum of a smooth and a plurisubharmonic (psh for short) function. In particular,
\varphi
is usc and integrable. Quasi-psh functions are actually in
L^{p}(X,dV_{X})

for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, and the induced topologies are also equivalent; see, e.g.,
[
16
,
36
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a quasi-psh function
\varphi
has
analytic singularities
if
\varphi
can be locally written as

\varphi=c\log\sum_{j=1}^{N}|f_{j}|^{2}+g

where
c>0
, the
f_{j}
’s are smooth functions and
g
is a locally bounded function.

Let
\theta
be a real smooth (1,1) form on
X
. We say that
\varphi
is
\theta
-plurisubharmonic
(
\theta
-psh
for short) if it is quasi-psh, and

\theta_{\varphi}:=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0

in the sense of currents, where
{\rm d}=\partial+\bar{\partial}
and
{\rm d}^{c}=\frac{i}{2\pi}(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
so that
dd^{c}=\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}
. Let
\PSH(X,\theta)
denote the set of all
\theta
-psh functions which are not identically
-\infty
.

We say that
\theta
is
big
if there exists a
\psi_{0}\in\PSH(X,\theta)
such that
\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{0}\geq\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{X}
for some
0<\varepsilon_{0}<<1
. As a consequence of Demailly’s regularization
[
13
]
, we can choose
\psi
to have analytic singularities.

We denote by

V_{\theta}=\sup\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\leq 0\}

a
\theta
-psh function with minimal singularities. We observe that
V_{\theta}\geq\psi_{0}
, in particular,
V_{\theta}
is locally bounded in
\Omega
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
X
. Let
\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)\cap L^{\infty}(U)
.
Following the construction of Bedford-Taylor
[
1
,
2
]
, it has been shown in
[
19
,
47
]
that the complex Monge-Ampère measure

\theta^{n}_{\varphi}=(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}

is well-defined on
U
.

We recall the following domination principle, which will be useful in the sequel.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
\varphi,\psi
be
\theta
-psh functions on
X
such that
\min\{\varphi,\psi\}\geq\varphi
and
\psi\leq\varphi+C
for some
C>0
. If

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq c(\theta+dd^{c}\psi)^{n}\;\text{on}\;\{\varphi<\psi\}\cap\{\rho>-\infty\},

for some
c\in[0,1)
, then
\varphi\geq\psi
.

2.2.
Demailly’s regularization

Following
[
15
]
, we consider
\rho_{\delta}\varphi
-regularization of the function
\varphi
defined by

\rho_{\delta}\varphi(z)=\frac{1}{\delta^{2n}}\int_{\zeta\in T_{z}X}\varphi(\exph_{z}(\zeta))\rho\left(\frac{|\zeta|^{2}_{\omega_{X}}}{\delta^{2}}\right)dV_{\omega_{X}}(\zeta),\quad\delta>0,

where
\zeta\mapsto\exph_{z}(\zeta)

is the (formal) holomorphic part of the Taylor expansion
of the Riemann exponential map of the Chern connection on the tangent bundle of
X

associated to
\omega
and the smoothing kernel
\rho:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is given by

\rho(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{\eta}{(1-t)^{2}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{t-1}\right),&0\leq t\leq 1\\
0,&t>1\end{cases}

with a normalizing constant
\eta
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(t)dt=1
. we define the Kiselman-Legendre transform:

(2.1)

\Phi_{c,\delta}:=\inf_{t\in[0,\delta]}\left(\rho_{t}\varphi+Kt-K\delta-c\log\frac{t}{\delta}\right),

where
c>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
and
K
is a positive (curvature) constant (as in
[
15
]
) to be chosen
K
so that
t\to\rho_{t}\varphi+Kt^{2}

increases in
t
.
Following
[
50
, Lemma 4.1]
we obtain the following

(2.2)

\theta+dd^{c}\Phi_{c,\delta}\geq-(Ac+2K\delta)\omega_{X}

where
A
is a lower bound of the negative part of the Chern curvature of
\omega_{X}
.

By
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\omega_{X}
and
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
such that

(2.3)

\int_{X}\frac{|\rho_{\delta}\varphi-\varphi|}{\delta^{2}}dV_{X}\leq C.

We remark that although the lemma is stated for Kähler manifolds,
the same proof works for Hermitian ones after replacing the Riemann curvature tensor
by the Chern curvature tensor used in
[
15
]
.

3.
Local Hölder continuity of Monge-Ampère potentials

Assume
\theta
is a real smooth (1,1) form such that there exists a
\theta
-psh function
\psi_{0}
with
analytic singularities
satisfying

\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{0}\geq\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{X}

in the sense of currents, for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
We observe that
\psi
is locally bounded on an open Zariski subset
\Omega:=X\setminus\{\psi_{0}=-\infty\}
. By subtracting a positive constant, we may assume that
\psi_{0}\leq V_{\theta}
.

Let
0\leq f\in L^{p}(X,dV_{X})
where
p>1
. It follows from
[
4
, Theorem D]
that there exist a unique constant
c=c(\theta,f)>0
and a
\theta
-psh function such that
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
,

(3.1)

V_{\theta}-C\leq\varphi\leq V_{\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=cfdV_{X}\quad\text{in}\;\Omega,

for
C>0
depending on
\omega_{X}
,
\theta
,
p
, and
\|f\|_{p}
.

In this context, we prove the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mu=fdV_{X}
be a measure absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure with density
0\leq f\in L^{p}(X,dV_{X})
,
p>1
.
Let
(\varphi,c)\in\PSH(X,\theta)\times(0,+\infty)
be such that
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
,

V_{\theta}-C_{0}\leq\varphi\leq V_{\theta},\qquad(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=c\mu\;\text{in}\,\Omega,

with a positive constant
C_{0}>0
depending only on
\omega_{X}
,
X
,
\theta
,
p
and
\|f\|_{p}
. Then
\varphi
is Hölder continuous in
\Omega
.

3.1.
Stability

We recall the following lemma, which is used in the proof of the stability result.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
4
, Lemma 4.5]
)
.

Let
g
be a measurable function on
X
such that
g\in L^{q}(dV_{X})
for some exponent
q>1
. Then there exists

v\in\PSH(X,\theta)
with
V_{\theta}-1\leq v\leq V_{\theta}
such that

(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\geq cgdV_{X}\;\;\text{on}\,\Omega

where
c
is a positive constant only depending on
X
,
\omega_{X}
,
q
,
\theta
, and a lower bound for
\|g\|_{q}^{-1}
.

We establish the following stability result, which is analog to the one
[
35
, Theorem C]
for the Kähler case.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu=fdV_{X}
be a probability measure absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure with density
f\in L^{p}(X,dV_{X})
,
p>1
.
Let
\varphi
be a
\theta
-psh function such that

\sup_{X}\varphi=0,\quad(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=c\mu\;\text{on}\,\Omega,

for some
c>0
and
V_{\theta}-C_{0}\leq\varphi\leq V_{\theta}
, with
C_{0}>0
.
Assume
\psi\in\PSH(X,\theta)
. Then there exists
\gamma_{0}
depending on
n
,
p
such that for any
0<\gamma<\gamma_{0}=\frac{p-1}{np+p-1}
,

\sup_{X}(\psi-\varphi)_{+}\leq C\|(\psi-\varphi)_{+}\|_{1}^{\gamma}

where
C
depends only on
X
,
c
,
C_{0}
and an upper bound
\|f\|_{L^{p}}
.

The proof is based on the use of auxiliary subsolutions and the comparison principle, an approach that arose from discussions with Chinh H. Lu at AIMS, Senegal. We also provide an alternative proof in the Kähler case that avoids the use of the relative Monge–Ampère capacity.

Proof.

We observe that
(\psi-\varphi)_{+}=\max(\psi,\varphi)-\varphi
. We may assume that
\psi\geq\varphi
.

Fixing any
q>1
, we can find
r>1
such that
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}\big(1-\frac{1}{q}\big)+\frac{1}{q}
.
We will prove the following stability estimate.

(3.2)

\sup_{X}(\psi-\varphi)\leq\delta+C\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)^{\frac{1}{nq}}

for
C>0
only depending on
X
,
\omega_{X}
,
q
,
\theta
, and a lower bound for
\|f\|_{q}^{-1}
.
Since the case
\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)=0
is trivial, we assume that
\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)>0
.
If we set
F=\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<\psi-\delta\}}f}{\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)^{\frac{1}{q}}}
then
the Hölder inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{X}F^{r}dV_{X}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}f^{r-\frac{r}{q}}\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<\psi-\delta\}}f}{\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{q}}dV_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|^{r-\frac{r}{q}}_{p}\bigg(\int_{X}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<\psi-\delta\}}f}{\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)}dV_{X}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{q}}=\|f\|_{p}^{r-\frac{r}{q}}.

We apply Lemma
3.2
to obtain a constant
c>0
and
v\in\PSH(X,\theta)
such that
V_{\theta}-1\leq v\leq V_{\theta}
and

(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\geq c^{n}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<\delta-\delta\}}fdV_{X}}{\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)^{\frac{1}{q}}},

for
c
only depending on
\omega_{X}
,
p
,
q
,
\theta
and
\|f\|_{p}^{-1}
.

Set
u:=(1-\lambda)\psi+\lambda v
for
\lambda\in(0,1)
to be determined later. Since
V_{\theta}\leq\psi+C_{0}
, we infer that

\{\varphi<u-\delta-C_{0}\lambda\}\subset\{\varphi<\psi-\delta\}.

We have

\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<u-\delta-C_{0}\lambda\}}\omega_{u}^{n}\geq\lambda^{n}\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<u-\delta-C_{0}\lambda\}}\omega_{v}^{n}\geq\lambda^{n}c^{n}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<u-\delta-C_{0}\lambda\}}fdV_{X}}{\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)^{\frac{1}{q}}}.

If
\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq c^{n}/2
, then the stability property (
3.2
) follows trivially.
Otherwise,
\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)^{\frac{1}{q}}<c^{n}/2
, hence we can choose

\lambda=2^{1/n}c^{-1}\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)^{\frac{1}{qn}}\in(0,1)

to obtain that

\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<u-\delta-C_{0}\lambda\}}\omega^{n}_{u}\geq 2\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<u-\delta-C_{0}\lambda\}}fdV_{X}=\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<u-\delta-C_{0}\lambda\}}2\omega_{\varphi}^{n}.

We observe that
\varphi+C_{0}\geq V_{\theta}\geq u-\delta-C_{0}\lambda
.
Thus, it follows from the domination principle (Proposition
2.2
) that
\varphi\geq u-\delta-C_{0}\lambda
on
X
, hence

\psi-\varphi\leq\delta+(C_{0}+1)\lambda

This implies the desire estimate (
3.2
). By the Chebyshev inequality and the Hölder inequality, we have

(3.3)

\mu(\varphi<\psi-\delta)\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}\int_{X}(\psi-\varphi)_{+}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}fdV_{X}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{p}\cdot\|(\psi-\varphi)\|_{1}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}{\delta^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}.

Choosing
\delta:=\|(\psi-\varphi)_{+}\|^{\alpha}
for
\alpha=\frac{1}{q}\frac{p-1}{np+p-1}
, it follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) that

\sup_{X}(\psi-\varphi)\leq(C+1)\|(\psi-\varphi)_{+}\|_{1}^{\alpha}.

Since
q>1
was arbitrarily chosen, we get the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

We can consider an alternative auxiliary subsolution to obtain the stability result, which is based on the idea of Fang

We set
g_{\delta}=\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi<\psi-\delta\}}f

It follows from Lemma

(\theta+dd^{c}v)^{n}\geq c^{n}\frac{g_{\delta}dV_{X}}{\|g_{\delta}\|_{q}},

for

only depending on
\omega_{X}
,

, and
\theta

We proceed in the same way as above to obtain

\sup_{X}(\psi-\varphi)\leq\delta+C\|g_{\delta}\|_{q}^{1/n},

for

depending on

,
\omega_{X}
,

Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\|g_{\delta}\|_{q}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{p}\|(\psi-\varphi)_{+}\|_{1}^{\frac{p-q}{pq}}}{\delta^{\frac{p-q}{pq}}}

Choosing
\delta:=\|(\psi-\varphi)_{+}\|_{1}^{\alpha}
with
\alpha=\frac{p-q}{npq+p-q}
we obtain

\sup_{X}(\psi-\varphi)\leq C\|(\psi-\varphi)_{+}\|^{\alpha}_{1}

for

depending on
C_{0}
,
p
,
q
and
\|f\|_{p}
. Since
q\in(1,p)
was taken arbitrarily
the desired estimate holds for any

.

3.2.
Hölder continuity

In this section, we study the Hölder continuity for solutions on the Zariski open set
\{\psi_{0}>-\infty\}
. In the Kähler case, the result is due to Demailly, Dinew, Guedj, Kolodziej, Pham and Zeriahi
[
12
, Theorem D]
. The crucial ingredient in the latter proof was the application of Demailly’s regularization approximations for quasi-psh functions which makes use of the holomophic part of the Riemannian exponential mapping
[
15
]
. This method can be also applied to the non-Kähler case. We are going to follow the scheme of their proof with tiny refinements.

Recall that a
\theta
-psh function
\psi_{0}
with analytic singularities such that
\theta+dd^{c}\psi_{0}\geq\varepsilon_{0}\omega
and
\sup_{X}\psi_{0}\leq 0
.
Set

\varphi_{c,\delta}:=\frac{Ac+2K\delta}{\varepsilon_{0}}\psi_{0}+\left(1-\frac{Ac+2K\delta}{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)\Phi_{c,\delta}.

In the following arguments, we choose
c=O(\delta^{\gamma})
so that
Ac+2K\delta=\delta^{2\gamma}
and we write
\varphi_{\delta}
instead of
\varphi_{c,\delta}
. We observe that
\Phi_{c,\delta}\leq K\delta^{2}+K\delta
so
\varphi_{\delta}-B\delta^{2\gamma}\leq 0
for some
B>0
and
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
sufficiently small. By the stability result (Theorem
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{X}(\varphi_{\delta}-\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|(\varphi_{\delta}-\varphi-B\delta^{2\gamma})_{+}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{1}}+B\delta^{2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|(\rho_{\delta}\varphi+K\delta-\varphi)\|^{\gamma}_{L^{1}}+B\delta^{2\gamma}.

By
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
we have

\sup_{X}(\varphi_{\delta}-\varphi)\leq C_{1}\delta^{2\gamma},

where
C_{1}
depends on
B
,
C_{0}
,
K
,
\|\varphi-V_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and the curvature of
\omega
. From this point we can conclude the Hölder continuity of
\varphi
as in the proof of
[
12
]
. For the sake of completeness, we give all the details for the reader convenience.

Fix the point
z\in\Omega
.
Then the minimum in the definition of
\Phi_{c,\delta}
is realized at
t_{0}=t_{0}(z)
.
Therefore, the last inequality yields at the point
z
,

\delta^{2\gamma}(\psi_{0}-\varphi)+(1-\delta^{2\gamma})(\rho_{\delta}\varphi+K\delta-\varphi-c\log(t/\delta))\leq C_{1}\delta^{2\gamma}.

Since
\rho_{\delta}\varphi+K\delta-\varphi\geq 0
we infer that

c(1-\delta^{2\gamma})\log\frac{t_{0}}{\delta}\geq\delta^{2\gamma}(\psi_{0}(z)-M_{0}-C_{1}).

Combining with
c=\varepsilon_{0}A^{-1}\delta^{2\gamma}-2KA^{-1}\delta\geq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{0}A^{-1}\delta^{2\gamma}
if
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
sufficiently small, one gets that

t_{0}\geq\delta\kappa\;\;\text{with}\;\kappa(z)=\exp\left(\frac{2A(\psi_{0}(z)+V_{\theta}(z)-C_{1})}{\varepsilon_{0}(1-\delta_{0}^{2\gamma})}\right).

Since
t\mapsto\rho_{t}\varphi+Kt^{2}
is increasing and
t_{0}(z)\delta\kappa(z)
it follows that

\displaystyle\rho_{\delta\kappa}\varphi(z)+K\delta\kappa

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{t_{0}}\varphi(z)+Kt_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{c,\delta}(z)=\frac{1}{1-\delta^{2\gamma}}(\varphi_{\delta}(z)-\delta^{2\gamma}\psi_{0})

B the stability result again, we obtain

\varphi_{\delta}-\delta^{2\gamma}\psi_{0}\leq\varphi+\delta^{2\gamma}(C_{1}-\psi_{0}),

using that
\varphi\leq 0
, hence

\rho_{\delta\kappa(z)}\varphi(z)-\varphi(z)\leq\frac{\delta^{2\gamma}}{1-\delta^{2\gamma}}(C_{1}-\psi_{0}(z))\leq(1-\delta_{0}^{2\gamma})^{-1}\delta^{2\gamma}(C_{1}-\psi_{0}(z)).

Replacing
\delta
by
\delta\kappa(z)^{-1}
, one gets

\rho_{\delta}\varphi(z)-\varphi(z)\leq(1-\delta_{0}^{2\gamma})^{-1}(C_{1}-\psi_{0}(z))\exp\left(\frac{4A\gamma(\psi_{0}(z)+V_{\theta}(z)-C_{1})}{\varepsilon_{0}(1-\delta_{0}^{2\gamma})}\right)\delta^{2\gamma}.

It follows that for any compact set
K\subset\subset\Omega
there exists a constant
C
depending on
K
such that for any
z\in K
,

\rho_{\delta}\varphi(z)-\varphi(z)\leq C\delta^{2\gamma}

which implies the Hölder continuity of
\varphi
on
K
; see e.g.,
[
30
, Lemma 4.2]
or
[
66
]
. Since
K
was taken arbitrarily, one can conclude that
\varphi
is Hölder continuous on
\Omega
.

4.1.
Continuity of potentials of Ricci flat currents

To state our result, we fix some notation and terminology. Let
(Y,\omega_{Y})

Let

(the discrepancies) satisfy

Let

Let

be a smooth Hermitian form on

.
We denote by

with respect to the

since the singularities of

are log terminal; see e.g.,
[
23
]
.
Set

. It follows from
[
33
]
that there exists a unique constant

and a bounded

-psh function

such that

(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=cfdV_{X},\qquad\sup_{X}\varphi=0.

Theorem 4.1
.

If

is a solution to (

is continuous on

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

There exists a constant

.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

, Proposition 3.6]
that
\varphi
is continuous on
(0,T)\times\Omega
for some open Zariski set
\Omega\subset X
. The following result improves our later one.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\varphi_{0}
be a bounded
\omega_{0}
-psh function.
The potential
\varphi:[0,T)\times X\to\mathbb{R}
of the unique solution to the flow (
CMAF
) on
X
starting from
\omega_{0}+dd^{c}\varphi_{0}
is continuous on
(0,T)\times X
. In particular, if
\varphi
is continuous on
X
, then
\varphi
is continuous on
[0,T)\times X
.

Proof.

Fix
J\Subset(0,T)
.
Since
\varphi
is locally uniformly Lipschitz in
t
and
F
is bounded from above, there exists a constant
M=M(J)>0
for almost every
t\in J
. We thus obtain

(\omega_{t}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}\leq e^{M}fdV_{X}

for almost every
t\in J
. This inequality holds for all
t\in J
due the continuity of the left-hand side. By Theorem
4.1
,
\varphi_{t}
is continuous on
X
for each
t\in J
.
Let
\kappa

be the uniform Lipschitz constant of
\varphi
on
J
. Then for any
s,t\in J
and

x,y\in X
we have

\displaystyle|\varphi(s,x)-\varphi(t,y)|

\displaystyle\leq|\varphi(s,x)-\varphi(t,x)|+|\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(t,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa|s-t|+|\varphi(t,x)-\varphi(t,y)|.

It follows that
\varphi
is continuous on
J\times X
. Thus,
\varphi
is continuous on
(0,T)\times X
.
∎

5.
Geometric applications

5.1.
Modulus of continuity and diameter bound

Let
\mu=fdV_{X}
be a positive Radon measure with
f\in L^{p}(dV_{X})
for some
p>1
. It follows from
[
19
,
47
]
that there exists a continuous
\omega_{X}
-psh function
\varphi
and
c>0
such that

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=cfdV_{X},\quad\sup_{X}\varphi=0

with a uniform a priori bound on
c
and
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure with
f\in L^{p}(dV_{X})
for some
p>1
.
Let
\psi
be a bounded
\omega
-psh function. Assume
\varphi\in\PSH(X,\omega_{X})\cap{L}^{\infty}(X)
and
c>0
solve the complex Monge-Ampère equation

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=cfdV_{X},\quad\sup_{X}\varphi=0.

Fix
0<\alpha<\frac{p-1}{np+p-1}.

Then

(5.1)

\sup_{X}(\psi-\varphi)\leq C(\|(\psi-\varphi)_{+}\|_{1})^{\alpha}

where
C>0
depends on
n
,
\omega_{X}
,
\alpha
,
p
and
\|f\|_{p}
.

Moreover, the modulus of continuity of
\varphi
satisfies
m_{\varphi}(r)=O(r^{2\alpha})
; in particular
\varphi
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
on
X
.

The stability estimates were proved by Kołodziej–Nguyen
[
49
]
, where they developed the Monge–Ampère capacity in the Hermitian setting.
Kołodziej–Nguyen
[
50
]
demonstrated the Hölder continuous of the solution to the complex Monge–Ampère equation with strictly positive right hand side
f\geq c_{0}>0
. Lu-Phung-Tô
[
54
]
later removed this technical assumption.

Proof.

The stability estimate (
5.1
) is a consequence of Theorem
3.3
where
\theta=\omega_{X}
and
V_{\omega_{X}}=0
.

We now prove the modulus of continuity. Recall that
\Phi_{c,\delta}
is Kiselman-Legendre transform of
\varphi
.
We consider

\psi_{\delta}=(1-[Ac+K\delta])\Phi_{c(\delta),\delta}

which is
\omega_{X}
-psh. We observe that

\psi_{\delta}\leq\Phi_{c,\delta}\leq\rho_{\delta}\varphi.

From
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
,
\|\rho_{\delta}\varphi-\varphi\|_{1}=O(\delta^{2})
, hence

\|(\psi_{\delta}-\varphi)_{+}\|_{1}\leq\|\rho_{\delta}\varphi-\varphi\|_{1}=O(\delta^{2}).

We apply the stability theorem (Theorem
5.1
) with
\psi=\psi_{\delta}
to obtain

\psi_{\delta}\leq\varphi+C\delta^{2\alpha}

with
C>0
under control. At
z\in X
, we can find
t=t(z)
achieving the infimum of
\Phi_{c,\delta}
.
Since
\rho_{t}\varphi+Kt^{2}\geq\varphi
we have

\displaystyle\varphi(z)+C\delta^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\rho_{t}\varphi(z)+Kt-K\delta-c\log\frac{t}{\delta}

\displaystyle\geq\varphi(z)-K\delta-c(\delta)\log\frac{t}{\delta}.

If we choose
c(\delta)=K\delta+C\delta^{2\alpha}=O(\delta^{2\alpha})
, then
\log\frac{t}{\delta}\geq-C
. Hence
t\geq\kappa\delta
for some
\kappa\in(0,1)
. This yields at any
z\in X
and
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0})
we have

\rho_{\kappa\delta}\varphi+K\kappa\delta-K\delta-\varphi\leq\rho_{t}\varphi+Kt-K\delta-\varphi=\Phi_{c,\delta}-\varphi.

We apply the stability estimate again to have that

\rho_{\kappa\delta}\varphi-\varphi\leq K\kappa\delta+C\delta^{2\alpha}\leq C^{\prime}\delta^{2\alpha}.

Replacing
\delta
by
\kappa^{-1}\delta
we have

\rho_{\delta}\varphi-\varphi\leq C^{\prime\prime}\delta^{2\alpha}.

This yields the Hölder continuity of
\varphi
, cf.
[
66
, Theorem 3.4]
or
[
54
, Lemma 4.4]
.∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a Hermitian manifold of complex dimension
n
. Assume
\varphi\in\PSH(X,\omega_{X})
is continuous in an open set
U\subset X
, with modulus of continuity
m_{\varphi}
which satisfies the Dini condition
m_{1}(r):=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{m_{\varphi}(t)}{t}dt<+\infty
. If
\omega=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi
is a Hermitian form in
U
, then for each
compact set
K\subset U
there exists
C_{K}>0
such that for all
p,q\in K
,

d_{\omega}(p,q)\leq C_{K}m_{1}\circ d_{\omega_{X}}(p,q).

Proof.

The idea goes back to Y. Li
[
53
]
, but requires slight modifications in the Hermitian context.
We give a proof here for the reader’s convenience.
Let
d_{p}(x):={d}_{\omega}(p,x)
denote the distance function with respect to the Hermitian form
\omega
.
Set
r_{p}=\inf_{x\in\Omega\setminus K}d_{p}(x)
and
B:=B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r_{p}/2)
.
The function
d_{p}(x)
defines a distance function on
B
; hence it is
1
-Lipschitz with respect to
d_{\omega}
.
This implies that
\nabla d_{p}
is well defined almost everywhere with
|\nabla d_{p}|_{\omega}\leq 1
.
For any
r\leq r_{p}/2
, we choose a smooth function
\chi
such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r/2)
and
{\rm supp}(\chi)\subset B_{\omega_{X}}(p,3r/2)
.
Moreover,
\chi
can be chosen to satisfy

(5.2)

|\nabla^{2}\chi|_{\omega_{X}}\leq Cr^{-2},\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla\chi|_{\omega_{X}}^{2}\leq Cr^{-2}.

where
C>0
is a uniform constant.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r)}|\nabla d_{p}|_{\omega_{X}}^{2}\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r)}|\nabla d_{p}|_{\omega}^{2}(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,3r/2)}\chi|\nabla d_{p}|_{\omega}^{2}(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,3r/2)}\chi(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,3r/2)}n\chi\omega_{X}^{n}+\underbrace{\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,3r/2)}\chi(\tr_{\omega_{X}}dd^{c}\varphi)\omega_{X}^{n}}_{=:I}.

Note that there is a constant
C>0
such that

(5.3)

-C\omega_{X}^{2}\leq dd^{c}\omega_{X}\leq C\omega_{X}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad-C\omega_{X}^{3}\leq d\omega_{X}\wedge d^{c}\omega_{X}\leq C\omega_{X}^{3}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that

(5.4)

\left|\frac{df\wedge d^{c}\omega_{X}^{n-1}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\right|\leq C\left(\frac{df\wedge d^{c}f\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-1}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}+1\right)

for any smooth function
f
.
By (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), one can derive

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=n\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,3r/2)}\chi dd^{c}\varphi\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=n\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,3r/2)}(\varphi-\varphi(p))dd^{c}(\chi\omega_{X}^{n-1})

\displaystyle=n\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,3r/2)}(\varphi-\varphi(p))(dd^{c}\chi\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-1}+2d\chi\wedge d^{c}\omega_{X}^{n-1}+\chi dd^{c}\omega_{X}^{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,3r/2)}\left|\varphi-\varphi(p)\right|\left(|\Delta_{\omega_{X}}\chi|+|\nabla\chi|_{\omega_{X}}^{2}+1\right)\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq Cm_{\varphi}(r)r^{2n-2}.

This yields

\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r)}|\nabla d_{p}|_{\omega_{X}}^{2}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq Cm_{\varphi}(r)r^{2n-2}.

By the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r)}\left|d_{p}-\underline{d_{p}}\right|^{2}\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r)}|\nabla_{\omega_{X}}d_{p}|^{2}\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}m_{\varphi}(r)r^{2n-2},

where
\underline{d_{p}}:=\frac{1}{\Vol(B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r))}\int_{B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r)}d_{p}\omega_{X}^{n}
.
It follows that
d_{p}
belongs to a generalized Morrey-Campanato space. Applying Campanato–Morey’s estimate with
\sqrt{m_{\varphi}}
(see
[
46
]
), we find that
d_{p}
is continuous and
m_{d_{p}}(r)\leq Cm_{1}(r)
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Estimates along Chern-Ricci flows

In this section, we study a generalization of the Chern–Ricci flow, namely the
twisted Chern–Ricci flow
: a smooth family of Hermitian metrics
(\omega_{t})_{t>0}
satisfies

(5.5)

\frac{\partial\omega_{t}}{\partial t}=-\textrm{Ric}(\omega_{t})+\eta,\quad\omega_{t}\xrightarrow{t\to 0}T_{0}\;\text{weakly}

where
\textrm{Ric}(\omega_{t})
is the Chern–Ricci form of
\omega_{t}
,
\eta
is a smooth (1, 1) form, and

T_{0}=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{0}
is a positive (1,1) current. Solving the twisted Chern-Ricci flow is equivalent to solving the parabolic Monge-Ampère equation

(5.6)

(\widehat{\omega}_{t}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\partial_{t}\varphi}\omega_{X}^{n},\quad\varphi_{t}\xrightarrow{t\to 0}\varphi_{0},\;\text{in}\,L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n}),

where
\widehat{\omega}_{t}:=\omega_{X}+t(\eta-\textrm{Ric}(\omega_{X}))
.
In this case,
\omega_{t}=\widehat{\omega}_{t}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
.

Generalizing the work of Tosatti–Weinkove
[
61
]
, Tô
[
58
]
showed that if
\varphi_{0}
is a bounded
\omega_{X}
-psh function
there exists a unique solution of the twisted Chern-Ricci flow (
5.5
) on
[0,T_{\max})
, where

T_{\max}=\sup\{t>0:\exists\,\hat{\psi}_{t}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X),\widehat{\omega}_{t}+dd^{c}\hat{\psi}_{t}>0\}.

More precisely, we have

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\varphi_{0}
be a bounded
\omega_{X}
-psh function. Then there exists a unique solution
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}((0,T_{\max})\times X)
to (
5.6
) such that
\|\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\to 0
as
t\to 0^{+}
.

As in
[
17
]
, we consider the case where
\eta=\textrm{Ric}(\omega_{X})
, then
T_{\max}=+\infty
and the equation (
5.6
) becomes

(5.7)

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\partial_{t}\varphi}\omega_{X}^{n},\quad\varphi_{t}\xrightarrow{t\to 0}\varphi_{0},\;\text{in}\,L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n}).

Remark 5.4
.

In general, since we are interested in the behavior of the flow near
0
, we can assume that for
0<S<T_{\max}
, there is a constant
C_{S}>0
such that

C_{S}^{-1}\omega_{X}\leq\widehat{\omega}_{t}\leq C_{S}\omega_{X},\;\,\forall\,t\in[0,S].

The uniform bounds in the sequel are independent of
t
.

We further assume that
\varphi_{0}
is continuous and
T_{0}^{n}=e^{\psi^{+}-\psi^{-}}\omega_{X}^{n}
where
\psi^{\pm}
are quasi-psh function on
X
with
e^{-\psi^{-}}\in L^{p}(\omega_{X}^{n})
, for
p>1
.

Proposition 5.5
.

There exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that for all
0\leq t\leq 1
and
x\in X
,

(1)

-C\leq\varphi\leq C
;

(2)

-C+\psi^{+}\leq\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}\leq C-\psi^{-}
;

(3)

\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}\leq Ce^{-\delta\psi^{-}}\omega_{X}
for some
\delta>0
.

Proof.

We choose
A>1
so that
\psi^{\pm}\in\PSH(X,A\omega_{X})
. We normalize
\sup_{X}\psi^{\pm}\leq 0
. We let
\Delta_{t}
denote the Laplacian with respect to
\omega_{t}=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
.

Case 1:

\psi^{\pm}
are smooth.
The (1) follows immediately from the maximum principle.

We prove (2).
Consider for
(t,x)\in[0,1]\times X
,

H(t,x)=\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}(x)+\psi^{-}(x)-A\varphi_{t}(x).

If
H
attains its maximum at
t=0
we obtain

H\leq AC.

Otherwise,
H
attains its maximum at
(t_{0},x_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
. At
(t_{0},x_{0})
, we have

0\leq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)H=A(n-\partial_{t}\varphi_{t})-\tr_{\omega_{t}}(A\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi^{-})\leq A(n-\partial_{t}\varphi_{t})

hence
\partial_{t}\varphi_{t_{0}}(x_{0})\leq n
. This implies that

H\leq n+AC.

Since
|\varphi_{t}(x)|\leq C
, we get the upper bound for
\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}
.

For the lower bound of
\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}
, we consider

G(t,x)=\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}(x)-\psi^{+}+(A+1)\varphi_{t}(x).

If
G
attains its minimum at
t=0
then

G\geq\partial_{t}\varphi_{0}-\psi^{+}-(A+1)C\geq-\sup_{X}\psi^{-}-(A+1)C\geq-C^{\prime}.

Otherwise,
G
attains its minimum at
(t_{0},x_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
.
Recall that the elementary inequality
\tr_{\alpha}\beta\geq n(\frac{\beta^{n}}{\alpha^{n}})^{1/n}
for (1,1) forms
\alpha,\beta
. We have at
(t_{0},x_{0})
,

0\geq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)G=(A+1)[\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}-n]-\tr_{\omega_{t}}(\omega_{X})\geq(A+1)[\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}-n]+ne^{-\frac{1}{n}\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}}.

Thus
\partial_{t}\varphi_{t_{0}}(x_{0})\geq-C(A,n)
. We infer that

G\geq-C(A,n)-\sup_{X}\psi^{+}-(A+1)C,

which implies the desired estimate.

The proof of (3) is almost to that of
[
11
, Lemma 5.5]
, we include here for readers’ convenience. We set
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}:=\varphi_{t}-\frac{1}{2A}\psi^{-}+C\geq 0
. We consider

Q(t,x)=\log\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega_{t}(x)-M\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(x)+e^{-\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(x)}.

If
Q
attains its maximum at
t=0
, we are done since
{\omega_{X}}+dd^{c}\varphi_{0}\leq Ce^{-(2A)^{-1}\psi^{-}}\omega_{X}
by
[
33
, Theorem 4.2]
. Otherwise,
Q
attains its maximum at
(t_{0},x_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
. In what follows, we compute at this point. For simplicity, we suppress the subscript
t
.
From
[
61
, Proposition 3.1]
(also
[
61
, (4.2)]
) we have

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)\log\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega\leq\frac{2}{(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)^{2}}\textrm{Re}(g^{\bar{q}k}(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}\partial_{\bar{q}}\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)+C\tr_{\omega}\omega_{X},

where
(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}
denote the torsion terms corresponding to
\omega_{X}
. At the maximum point
(t_{0},x_{0})
of
Q
, we have
\partial_{i}Q=0
, hence

\frac{1}{\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega_{t}}\partial_{i}\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega-M\partial_{i}\tilde{\varphi}-e^{-\tilde{\varphi}}\partial_{i}\tilde{\varphi}=0.

Thus, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\begin{split}\left|\frac{2}{(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)^{2}}\textrm{Re}(g^{\bar{q}k}(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}\partial_{\bar{q}}\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)\right|&\leq\left|\frac{2}{(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)^{2}}\textrm{Re}((M+e^{-\tilde{\varphi}})g^{\bar{q}k}(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}\partial_{\bar{q}}\tilde{\varphi}\right|\\
&\leq e^{-\tilde{\varphi}}|\partial\tilde{\varphi}|^{2}_{\omega}+C(M+1)^{2}e^{\tilde{\varphi}}\frac{\tr_{\omega}\omega_{X}}{(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)^{2}}\end{split}

for uniform
C>0
only depending on the torsion term.
It thus follows that, at the point
(t_{0},x_{0})
,

(5.8)

\begin{split}0\leq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)Q&\leq C(M+1)^{2}e^{\tilde{\varphi}}\frac{\tr_{\omega}\omega_{X}}{(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)^{2}}+C\tr_{\omega}\omega_{X}\\
&\quad-(M+e^{-\tilde{\varphi}})\partial_{t}{\varphi}+(M+e^{-\tilde{\varphi}})\tr_{\omega}(\omega-(\omega_{X}+(2A)^{-1}dd^{c}\psi^{-}))\\
&\leq C(M+1)^{2}e^{\tilde{\varphi}}\frac{\tr_{\omega}\omega_{X}}{(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)^{2}}+(C-M/2)\tr_{\omega}\omega_{X}\\
&\quad-(M+e^{-\tilde{\varphi}})\partial_{t}\varphi+(M+1)n\end{split}

If at
(t_{0},x_{0})
, we have
(\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega)^{2}\leq e^{\tilde{\varphi}}C(M+1)^{2}
then at the same point

Q\leq C+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varphi}-M\tilde{\varphi}+e^{-\tilde{\varphi}}\leq C+1

since
\tilde{\varphi}\geq 0
, we are done. Otherwise, we choose
M=2(C+A)
. Hence, from (
5.8
) one gets

(5.9)

\tr_{\omega}\omega_{X}\leq-(2C+2A+e^{-\tilde{\varphi}})\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}+n(2C+2A+1).

Recall that

\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega\leq n\left(\frac{\omega^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\right)(\tr_{\omega_{t}}\omega_{X})^{n-1}=e^{\partial_{t}\varphi}(\tr_{\omega_{t}}\omega_{X})^{n-1}.

We divide into two cases.

•

If
\partial_{t}\varphi_{t_{0}}(x_{0})\geq 0
then (
5.9
) becomes
tr_{\omega}\omega_{X}(t_{0},x_{0})\leq n(2C+2A+1)
. This implies that at
(t_{0},x_{0})

{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}\omega\leq C^{\prime}e^{C-\psi^{-}}

because by (2)
\partial_{t}\varphi\leq C-\psi^{-}
. It follows that

Q\leq Q(t_{0},x_{0})\leq C^{\prime}-\psi^{-}-2(C+A)\varphi+(C/A+1)\psi^{-}+1\leq C^{\prime\prime}.

•

If
\partial_{t}\varphi_{t_{0}}(x_{0})\leq 0
then at
(t_{0},x_{0})

\tr_{\omega_{X}}\omega\leq e^{\partial_{t}\varphi}(-C^{\prime}\partial_{t}\varphi+C^{\prime})^{n-1}\leq C^{\prime\prime}.

Thus
Q
is uniformly bounded from above.

Since
\varphi_{t}
is uniformly bounded, we obtain the desired estimate.

Case 2:

\psi^{\pm}
are merely
A\omega_{X}
-psh function. Thanks to the regularization theorem
[
14
,
5
]
, we can find sequences
(\psi^{\pm}_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of smooth
A\omega_{X}
-psh functions decreasing pointwise to
\psi^{\pm}
. It follows from Tosatti-Weinkove’s main theorem
[
60
]
that there exist
\varphi_{0,j}\in\PSH(X,\omega_{X})\cap\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
and
c_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
which solve

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{0,j})^{n}=e^{\psi^{+}_{j}-\psi^{-}_{j}+c_{j}}\omega_{X}^{n},\quad\sup_{X}\varphi_{0,j}=0.

We see that for
p>1
,
\|e^{-\psi^{-}_{j}}\|_{p}
are uniformly bounded. It thus follows from
[
47
]
that
\|\varphi_{0,j}\|_{\infty}
are uniformly (independent of
j
).
From
[
61
,
58
]
, there exists a smooth solution to the corresponding complex Monge-Ampère flow

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t,j})^{n}=e^{\partial_{t}\varphi_{t,j}}\omega_{X}^{n},\quad\varphi_{t,j}|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0,j}.

We observe that
\varphi_{t,j}
converges to
\varphi_{t}
in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}((0,T)\times X)
; see, for example,
[
58
, Theorem B]
.
Since
\psi_{j}^{\pm}
are decreasing, we obtain the desired estimates for
\varphi_{t,j}
, and hence also for
\varphi_{t}
.
∎

From our previous estimates, we apply the complex parabolic Evans-Krylov theory together with Schauder’s estimates (see also
[

]
) to obtain the following higher order a priori estimates.

Proposition 5.6
.

Assume
\psi^{\pm}
is smooth in some open Zariski set
\Omega\subset X
. For any compact subset
K\subset\Omega
and
0<S<+\infty
, there exists constants
C(k,S)
such that

\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k}([0,S]\times K)}\leq C(k,S).

Moreover,
\varphi_{t}
converges to
\varphi_{0}
in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
as

.

Theorem 5.7
.

For
0<S<+\infty
and any

, we have

\textrm{diam}(X,\omega_{t})\leq C\qquad d_{\omega_{t}}(x,y)\leq Cd_{\omega_{X}}(x,y)^{\alpha},\;\forall\,t\in[0,S],

for some constants

that only depend on

,
\omega_{X}
,

and an upper bound for

Proof.

By Proposition

(2),

. The

and Proposition

.
∎
