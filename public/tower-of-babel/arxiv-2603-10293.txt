As

For our convenience, we will define the following whether

A matroid can alternatively be defined by its collection of

{\mathcal{H}}
of subsets of

H_{1}\not\subset H_{2}

Then we say

is the collection of hyperplanes for a matroid. We say this matroid is

if every hyperplane has cardinality

(k-1)
-subset of

Let

denote the number of matroids, paving matroids, and sparse paving matroids (respectively) of rank

Theorem 2
.

Let
k=k(n)\geq 3
and
k=o(\log n)
. Then

\log s(n,k)=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\Big(\log n+1-k+o(1)\Big).

Theorem 3
.

Let
k=k(n)\geq 4
and
k=o\left(\frac{\log^{1/2}n}{\log\log n}\right)
. Then
\log p(n,k)
and
\log m(n,k)
are both

\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\Big(\log n+1-k+o(1)\Big).

Theorem
3
also has a flavor that feels relevant to Conjecture

\sum_{k}p(n,k)=(1+o(1))\sum_{k}m(n,k)
. Note that Theorem

requires

[

Theorem

[
12
]
. We prove Theorem

by adapting the tools and proof techniques used in
[
12
]
. In particular, we use methods for matchings in hypergraphs. Suppose
{\mathcal{H}}
is the collection of hyperplanes for a sparse paving matroid, and let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
be the hyperplanes of size
k
. Then from the definition of sparse paving matroids, no two hyperplanes in
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
intersect in

are just all the
(k-1)
-sets which are not contained in a hyperplane of size
k
. (A

-set must be contained in at least one hyperplane. Thus

is a partial Steiner system

i.e. a set of
k
-subsets, such that each
(k-1)
-set is contained in at most one such

Thus, there is a one-to-one correspondence between partial Steiner systems

and sparse paving matroids of rank
k
. Meanwhile, there is also a one-to-one correspondence between partial Steiner systems
S_{p}(n,k,k-1)
and matchings in an appropriately defined hypergraph. In particular, for
\ell\leq k\leq n
we let
\mathcal{G}_{n,k,\ell}
be the hypergraph having vertex set
\binom{[n]}{\ell}
and a hyperedge
\binom{K}{\ell}
for every
k

. Then a partial Steiner system
S_{p}(n,k,\ell)
is just a matching in
\mathcal{G}_{n,k,\ell}
.

Thus, we can prove Theorem
2
by estimating the number of matchings in a hypergraph. For the case of constant
k
, the lower bound in
[
12
]
is proved by applying a result of the first author and Bohman
[

who analyzed a random process for matchings in

-uniform hypergraphs. To prove the lower bound in Theorem

we will need to extend the result of
[

]
to allow for growing

Our next theorem may be of independent interest since matchings in hypergraphs are widely applicable. Here we will only directly use the second part which estimates the number of matchings in a hypergraph. However to prove the second part we will use the first part, which gives sufficient conditions for an almost-perfect matching. This theorem also extends results of Chakravarty and Spanier

X_{v_{2}},\ldots,X_{v_{k}}
appear after
v
in the ordering
\lambda
, which happens with probability
\lambda(v)^{k(k-1)}
.

Now taking the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda(v)}
of the above we get the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda(v)}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}[\log(N_{v})|\lambda(v)]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\lambda(v)}\left[\lambda(v)^{k-1}\log\left(1+d_{v}\lambda(v)^{k(k-1)}+|B_{v}|\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\log\left(1+d_{v}x^{k(k-1)}+|B_{v}|\right)\;dx.

Summing over
v
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda(v)}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}[\log(N_{v})|\lambda(v)]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V}\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\log\left(1+d_{v}x^{k(k-1)}+|B_{v}|\right)\;dx

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\log\left(1+Dx^{k(k-1)}+kD\phi\right)\;dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{k}\int_{0}^{1}\log D+\log\left(u^{k-1}+2k\phi\right)\;du

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{k}\int_{0}^{1}\log D+(k-1)\log\left(u+\left(2k\phi\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\right)\;du

(33)

Explanation:
The second line follows by convexity of
\log(x)
. On the third line we have used that
1\leq kD\phi
and substituted
u=x^{k}
. On the fourth line we have used

u^{k-1}+2k\phi\leq\left(u+\left(2k\phi\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\right)^{k-1}.

Now to evaluate the integral in (
33
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\log\left(u+\left(2k\phi\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\right)\;du

\displaystyle=\int_{\left(2k\phi\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}^{1+\left(2k\phi\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}\log w\;dw

\displaystyle=\Big[w\log w-w\Big]_{\left(2k\phi\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}^{1+\left(2k\phi\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}

\displaystyle=-1+O\left(\phi^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\right).

Returning to (
31
) and using (
33
) (and evaluating the integral as above), we have

\log(|{\mathcal{X}}|)\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}\frac{N}{k}\left(\log D-k+1+O\left(k\phi^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\right)\right)=\frac{N}{k}\left(\log D-k+1+O\left(k\phi^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\right)\right)

(34)

where the last equality is just because there is no dependence on
X
anymore.
∎
