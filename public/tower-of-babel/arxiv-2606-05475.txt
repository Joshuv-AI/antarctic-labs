Introduction and statement of the results

1.1.
Introduction

We consider a metric measure space
(X,d,m)
endowed with a non-negative self-adjoint operator
\Delta
acting on
L^{2}(X,m)
. Associated with the corresponding Dirichlet form

\mathcal{E}(f,g)=-\int_{X}f\,\Delta g\,dm,

one can introduce a notion of gradient operator
\nabla
, which behaves in
L^{2}(X)
like
\Delta^{1/2}
. A natural and longstanding question is how these two “differential operators”
\nabla
and
\Delta^{1/2}
compare in
L^{p}
spaces.

In the Euclidean setting, the Riesz transform
\mathcal{R}=\nabla\Delta^{-1/2}
, which can be defined via the Fourier transform or as the singular integral operator

\mathcal{R}f(x)=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+1}}f(y)\,dy,

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. One standard proof relies on Calderón–Zygmund theory, where the Riesz transform serves as a model example; details can be found in
[
Ste70a
, Chapter 2]
.

The
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Riesz transform yields the estimate

(R
p
)

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\,\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}},\qquad\text{for }f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}.

From this, one may also consider the reverse estimate

(RR
p
)

\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}.

Moreover, a simple duality argument shows that (
Rp
) implies
(\mathrm{RR}_{p^{\prime}})
, where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p\in(1,\infty)
; see Lemma
2.1
for the precise statement in our setting. As such, in Euclidean spaces, we have the equivalence
\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{L^{p}}\approx\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p}}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.

In 1983, Strichartz asked in
[
Str83
]
for which Riemannian manifolds and which values of
p\in(1,\infty)
the inequalities (
Rp
) and (
RRp
) hold. This initiated an extensive line of research aimed at identifying necessary and sufficient conditions for these inequalities.

We briefly highlight a few representative results:

•

Under doubling volume growth and Gaussian heat kernel estimates, (
Rp
) holds for all
1<p\leq 2
on manifolds
[
CD99
]
and on graphs
[
Rus00
]
.

•

Even under the same assumptions as in
[
CD99
]
, there exist Riemannian manifolds for which (
Rp
) fails for all
p>2
; see
[
CD99
, Section 5]
.

•

If one additionally assumes an
L^{2}
-Poincaré inequality on balls, then (
Rp
) holds for a small range
p\in(2,2+\varepsilon)
, see
[
AC05
]
.

•

Assuming doubling volume growth and sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimates, (
Rp
) holds for all
1<p\leq 2
on manifolds and graphs
[
CCFR17
]
.

Further results in the context of Riemannian manifolds or graphs can be found in
[
CD03
,
ACDH04
,
BR09
,
BF16
,
DR22
,
Ouh24
]
, as well as results using Hardy spaces in the endpoint case

see
[
DY05
,

,

,

]
.

The behavior of the Riesz transform in fractal-type structures appears to be strikingly different from the Euclidean case. In
[
CCFR17
]
, we established a complete picture of the validity of (
Rp
) and (
RRp
) in the case of Vicsek graphs (see Figure
1
for an illustration, and Subsection
2.3
for the definition). Namely, (
Rp
) holds if and only if
p\in(1,2]
, while (
RRp
) holds if and only if
p\in[2,\infty)
. Vicsek graphs are particularly well-suited to analysis: their fractal-like structure leads to behavior very different from the Euclidean setting, while their additional structure (in particular their tree-like nature) makes them more tractable than most fractal spaces; see for instance
[
BC23
,
BC24
]
.

This naturally led Devyver and Russ to ask whether, in the case of Vicsek graphs, it is meaningful to compare
\|\nabla f\|_{p}
directly with
\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{p}
, or whether instead one should compare
\|\nabla f\|_{p}
with
\|\Delta^{\gamma^{*}(p)}f\|_{p}
, for some exponent
\gamma^{*}(p)
to be determined.

We therefore introduce a parameter into (
Rp
) and (
RRp
), and study for which
\gamma\in[0,1]
and
p\in(1,\infty)
the following inequalities hold on Vicsek graphs, or more generally on discrete fractal-type structures:

(R
p,γ
)

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}}\quad\text{for any compactly supported function }f,

and

(RR
p,γ
)

\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}}\quad\text{for any compactly supported function }f.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that the two inequalities automatically holds when the graph is finite. Indeed, for a graph with
n
vertices, functions on the graph are vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, while the Laplacian is a non-negative symmetric matrix. Therefore, on
finite graphs
, a simple application of the spectral theorem for matrices gives that

\|\Delta^{\gamma_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Delta^{\gamma_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{ whenever }0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}.

By definition of
\nabla
and
\Delta
, we also have
\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{2}}=\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}
. Together with the fact that norms in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are all equivalent, we obtain that

(C_{p,q,n,\gamma})^{-1}\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{p,q,n,\gamma}\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p}}\quad\text{whenever $p,q\in[1,\infty]$ and $\gamma>0$,}

meaning that (
Rp,γ
) and (
RRp,γ
) always holds. Computing the optimal constant remains an interesting question, see for instance
[
ELP08
]
, but will not be the focus of the current article. And in the rest of the presentation, we will always assume that the considered metric space is non-compact.

In the continuous setting (Riemannian manifolds or cable systems), we instead consider the regularized versions of (
Rp,γ
) and (
RRp,γ
):

(R
p,γ
)

\|\nabla e^{-\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}},

and

(RR
p,γ
)

\|\Delta^{\gamma}e^{-\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}},

for
f\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}}(\Delta)
, the domain of
\Delta
in
L^{2}
.
The distinction between discrete and continuous settings is essential. On graphs, both the Laplacian and the gradient are bounded on
L^{p}
, and it is therefore meaningful to compare
\nabla
with arbitrary powers of
\Delta
. In contrast, on Riemannian manifolds or cable systems, the local structure is Euclidean, and a heat semigroup regularization is needed to isolate the genuinely large-scale, fractal behavior.

The inequalities (
Rp,γ
) and (
RRp,γ
), referred to as quasi-Riesz inequalities and reverse quasi-Riesz inequalities, were first introduced in
[
Che14
]
. Positive and negative results in fractal-type settings were subsequently obtained in
[
DRY23
]
,
[
DR26
]
and
[
Fen26
]
. A first indication that the exponent

is not the correct scaling for the gradient in the sub-Gaussian regime was obtained by Devyver and Russ in
[

]
. For Vicsek cable systems of dimension
D
, they showed that the reverse inequality is governed by the critical exponent

\gamma^{*}(p):=\frac{1}{D+1}+\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{2}{D+1}\right),

rather than
1/2
. More precisely, they proved the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(
[

)
.

Let

Then:

(i)

) holds for

whenever
\gamma\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
, and for

\gamma>\gamma^{*}(p):=\frac{1}{D+1}+\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{2}{D+1}\right);

(ii)

the estimate (

) fails whenever

By duality, this further implies the failure of (

) for all

We also study the validity of (

2.
The discrete setting

2.1.
Definitions of gradient and Laplacian on graphs

Let
G
be an infinite set and let
\mu_{xy}=\mu_{yx}\geq 0
be a symmetric weight on
G\times G
.
The pair
(G,\mu)
induces a (weighted, unoriented) graph structure by defining the set of edges

E_{G}:=\{(x,y)\in G\times G\,:\,\mu_{xy}>0\}.

We say that
x
and
y
are neighbors, and write
x\sim y
, if and only if
(x,y)\in E_{G}
.
The
distance

\delta(x,y)
between two vertices
x,y\in G
is the length of the shortest path connecting them, i.e.

\delta(x,y):=\min\Big\{N\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,\exists(x_{0},\dots,x_{N})\in G^{N+1}\text{ such that }\\
x_{0}=x,\;x_{N}=y,\;\text{and }(x_{i-1},x_{i})\in E_{G}\text{ for all }i=1,\dots,N\Big\}.

We always assume that
G
is
connected
, meaning that
\delta(x,y)<\infty
for all
x,y\in G
.

We define the
weight

m(x)
of a vertex
x\in G
by

m(x):=\sum_{y\sim x}\mu_{xy}.

More generally, the measure of a subset
E\subset G
is

m(E):=\sum_{x\in E}m(x).

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we say that a function
f
on
G
belongs to
L^{p}(G,m)
(or simply
L^{p}(G)
) if

\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}:=\left(\sum_{x\in G}|f(x)|^{p}m(x)\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

while
L^{\infty}(G)
consists of functions such that

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}:=\sup_{x\in G}|f(x)|<\infty.

For
x\in G
and
r>0
, we define the
ball

B(x,r):=\{y\in G\,:\,\delta(x,y)<r\}.

We say that
B\subset G
is a ball if there exist
x_{B}\in G
and
r_{B}>0
such that
B=B(x_{B},r_{B})
.
Note that the center and radius are not necessarily unique, but this will play no role in what follows.
As usual, for a ball
B
or
(x,r)\in G\times(0,\infty)
, we set

V(B):=m(B),\qquad V(x,r):=V(B(x,r)).

We also equip
E_{G}
with the measure
\mu(e):=\mu_{xy}
for
e=(x,y)\in E_{G}
.
We say that
\omega
is a
1-form
on
G
if
\omega
is a function on
E_{G}
such that

\omega(x,y)=-\omega(y,x)\qquad\text{for all }(x,y)\in E_{G},

in particular
\omega(x,x)=0
.
We define

|\omega|(x):=\left(\frac{1}{2m(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}\mu_{xy}|\omega(x,y)|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\qquad x\in G,

which allows us to view 1-forms as functions when estimating them.
For instance, we sometimes write
|\omega|\in L^{p}(G)
instead of
\omega\in L^{p}(E_{G},\mu)
.
Note that when
\mu_{xy}\simeq 1
(as in Vicsek graphs introduced later), we have

|\omega|\in L^{p}(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\omega\in L^{p}(E_{G},\mu).

The
differential operators

\nabla
and
\nabla^{*}
are defined by

\nabla f(x,y):=f(x)-f(y),\qquad(x,y)\in E_{G},

for functions
f
on
G
, and

\nabla^{*}\omega(x):=\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}\mu_{xy}\,\omega(x,y),\qquad x\in G,

for 1-forms
\omega
.
One can check the duality identity

(2.1)

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in E_{G}}\nabla f(e)\,\omega(e)\,\mu(e)=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\,\nabla^{*}\omega(x)\,m(x),

whenever all quantities are well-defined.

The (nonnegative) Laplacian is defined by

(2.2)

\Delta:=\nabla^{*}\nabla.

For
x,y\in G
, we define the reversible Markov kernel

p(x,y):=\frac{\mu_{xy}}{m(x)m(y)}.

For functions
f
on
G
, we define

(2.3)

Pf(x)=\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}\mu_{xy}f(y).

One verifies that

(2.4)

\Delta=I-P.

The discrete kernel
p_{k}(x,y)
is defined by

p_{0}(x,y)=\frac{\delta(x,y)}{m(y)},\qquad p_{k+1}(x,y)=\sum_{z\in G}p(x,z)p_{k}(z,y)m(z).

Moreover,

(2.5)

\sum_{y\in G}p_{k}(x,y)m(y)=1,\qquad p_{k}(x,y)=p_{k}(y,x).

Finally, whenever
|\nabla f|\in L^{2}(G)
,

(2.6)

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}=\sum_{x\in G}\frac{1}{2m(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}\mu_{xy}|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}\,m(x)\\
=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in E_{G}}\nabla f(e)^{2}\,\mu(e)=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\Delta f(x)\,m(x)=\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}.

We finish the paragraph with the following duality result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(G,\mu)
be a graphs. Then, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
and any
\gamma\in[0,1]
, we heve

\text{\eqref{Rpggbis} $\implies$ {(RR${}_{p^{\prime},1-\gamma}$)}},

where

is the Hölder conjugate of

.

Proof:
This result is classical, and analogue versions have already been proven in other settings, like for instance in cable systems (see
[
DR26
, Lemma 1.4]
). We prove it for completeness.

(a)
\Delta^{\gamma}
has dense range in
L^{p}
.
Since
\gamma\in[0,1]
and
\Delta
is bounded,
\Delta^{\gamma}
has dense range if
\Delta
has also dense range. Let us prove the later.

If by contradiction,
\Delta
does not have a dense range in
L^{p}
, then by the Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(G)\setminus\{0\}
such that

\left<g,\Delta f\right>=\sum_{z\in G}g(z)\Delta f(z)m(z)=0\qquad\text{ for all }f\in L^{p}.

So by the symmetry of (
P
and)
\Delta

\left<\Delta g,f\right>=\left<g,\Delta f\right>=0\qquad\text{ for all }f\in L^{p},

meaning that
\Delta g=0
. Since

, it has a global maximum, and if

is such that
g(x)=\max_{G}g
, we have

0=\Delta g(x)=\sum_{y\sim x}\underbrace{[g(x)-g(y)]}_{\geq 0}p(x,y)m(y),

meaning that
g(y)=g(x)=\max_{G}g
for all the neighbors

of

. Since

is connected, it means that

is constant equal to

, and since

, it means that

. A contradiction with our choice of

.

(b) Duality.
Since

has dense range,

\|\Delta^{1-\gamma}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}=\sup_{g\in L^{p}(G)\atop\|\Delta^{\gamma}g\|_{p}\leq 1}\sum_{z\in G}\Delta^{1-\gamma}f(z)\,\Delta^{\gamma}g(z)m(z)=\sup_{g\in L^{p}(G)\atop\|\Delta^{\gamma}g\|_{p}\leq 1}\sum_{z\in G}[\nabla^{*}\nabla f](z)\,g(z)m(z)\\
=\sup_{g\in L^{p}(G)\atop\|\Delta^{\gamma}g\|_{p}\leq 1}\sum_{e\in E_{G}}\nabla f(e)\,\nabla g(e)\mu(e)

by (

). We then continue as

\|\Delta^{1-\gamma}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}\leq\sup_{g\in L^{p}(G)\atop\|\Delta^{\gamma}g\|_{p}\leq 1}\sum_{z\in G}|\nabla f|(z)\,|\nabla g|(z)m(z)\leq\sup_{g\in L^{p}(G)\atop\|\Delta^{\gamma}g\|_{p}\leq 1}\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}\||\nabla g|\|_{L^{p}(G)}\\
\lesssim\sup_{g\in L^{p}(G)\atop\|\Delta^{\gamma}g\|_{p}\leq 1}\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}\|\Delta^{\gamma}g\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}

by (
Rp,γ
). The constant in the inequality above being independent of

, the lemma follows.

\square

2.2.
Conditions on graphs

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a graph
(G,\mu)
is
doubling
- or satisfies (
D
) - if there exists
C>0
such that for any
x\in G
and any

,

(D)

V(x,2r)\leq CV(x,r).

There are few consequences of the doubling property that is worthy of comments. First, a doubling graph is uniformly locally finite, meaning that the numbers of neighbors of a vertex is (finite and) uniformly bounded. Second, doubling graphs have an upper bound in the dimension, in the sense that if
(G,\mu)
is doubling, then there exists
C>0
and
D_{G}>0
such that

(2.7)

V(x,\lambda r)\leq C\lambda^{D_{G}}V(x,r)\qquad\text{ for }x\in G,\,\lambda\geq 1,\,r>0.

A stronger variant of the doubling property is the Ahlfors regularity. We say that a graph is
D
-Ahlfors regular - or satisfies
(AR)D
- if there exists
C>0
such that

(AR)
D

C^{-1}(1+r)^{D}\leq V(x,r)\leq C(1+r)^{D}\qquad\text{ for }x\in G,\,r>0.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
(G,\mu)
satisfies (
LB

(x,x)\in E_{G}
for all
x\in G
and there exists

\mu_{xy}\geq\epsilon m(x)\qquad\text{ for }(x,y)\in E_{G}.

The condition (

), it implies that the graph is uniformly locally finite. The fact that

The operator

;

and the discrete semigroup

is analytic, i.e. for any

Details for those implications, as well as characterizations of analyticity on graphs, can be found in
[
Fen18

CSC90
]

Definition 3.1
.

The cone with vertex at

\Gamma(x):=\{(y,k)\in G\times\mathbb{N}^{*}|\,\delta^{\beta}(x,y)<k\}.

The Carleson region over

We then define the functionals

\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{f})(x):=\left(\sum_{(y,k)\in G(x)}\frac{m(y)}{kV(x,k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(y)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{f})(x)=\sup_{x\in B}\left(\frac{1}{V(B)}\sum_{(y,k)\in\hat{B}}\frac{m(y)}{k}|f_{k}(y)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For any

denotes the space of collections

such that

\|.\|_{T^{p}(G)}:=\|\mathcal{A}(.)\|_{L^{p}(G)}
. The tent space

is the space of collections
\mathfrak{f}
such that

, and is equipped with the norm
\|.\|_{T^{\infty}(G)}:=\|\mathcal{C}(.)\|_{L^{p}(G)}
.

The first result that we need is the fact that changing the aperture of the cones

will not change the tents spaces

Lemma 3.2
.

For

, define

\Gamma_{[i]}(x):=\{(y,k)\in G\times\mathbb{N}^{*}|\,\delta^{\beta}(x,y)<2^{i}k\}

when

\mathcal{A}_{[i]}(\mathfrak{f})(x):=\left(\sum_{(y,k)\in\Gamma_{[i]}(x)}\frac{m(y)}{kV(x,k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(y)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

when
\mathfrak{f}
is a collection of functions on
G
and
x\in G
.

For all

, there exists

such that for any collection

,

\|\mathcal{A}_{[i]}(\mathfrak{f})(x)\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C^{i}\|\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{f})(x)\|_{L^{p}(G)}.

Proof:
The complicated part is to prove that

\|\mathcal{A}_{[2]}(\mathfrak{f})(x)\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C_{1}\|\mathcal{A}_{[0]}(\mathfrak{f})(x)\|_{L^{p}(G)}.

The argument relies on a good lambda argument which is now classical. The proof in the Euclidean case is found as Proposition 4 in
[

]

Then, given

The lemma follows.

\square

\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(1+4/q)}\sum_{y\in B(x,2^{j+2}k^{1/\beta})}\frac{m(y)}{kV(x,k^{1/\beta})}\big|\bar{f}_{k}(y)\big|^{2}

where

\bar{f}_{k}(y):=\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})^{1/q}}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{q}(B(y,k^{1/\beta}))}.

Now, we sum over
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
to get that

\mathcal{A}_{(j)}^{q}\big(\mathfrak{f}\big)(x)\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(1/2+2/q)}\mathcal{A}_{[j]}\big(\bar{\mathfrak{f}}\big)(x).

By Lemma
3.2
, we can find
D_{p}
such that

\big\|\mathcal{A}_{(j)}^{q}\big(\mathfrak{f}\big)\big\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(1/2+2/q)}\big\|\mathcal{A}_{[j]}(\bar{\mathfrak{f}})\big\|_{L^{p}(G)}\\
\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(1/2+2/q)+jD_{p}}\big\|\mathcal{A}(\bar{\mathfrak{f}})\big\|_{L^{p}(G)}=2^{jD}\big\|\mathcal{A}^{q}(\mathfrak{f})\big\|_{L^{p}(G)}

for a
D
independent of
\mathfrak{f}
. The lemma follows.

\square

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G
be a doubling graph,
q\in[1,\infty]
and
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then there exists
C>0
and
D>0
such that, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, any collection of functions
\mathfrak{f}=\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
, and any
x\in G
, we have

C^{-1}2^{-jD}\mathcal{C}^{p}(\mathfrak{f})(x)\leq\mathcal{C}^{q}_{(j)}(\mathfrak{f})(x)\leq C2^{jD}\mathcal{C}^{q}(\mathfrak{f})(x).

Proof:
We do not need to be particularly subtle for the proof of the lemma. Moreover, we write the proof for the case
q<+\infty
, as the case
q=\infty
is a simpler variant.

We write
D_{G}
for an upper bound on the “dimension” given in (
2.7
). The first inequality of the lemma is clear, as the doubling property, or more exactly (
2.7
), gives that
V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}}V(y,k^{1/\beta})
and hence

\mathcal{C}^{p}\mathfrak{f}(x)\leq C2^{jD_{G}/q}\mathcal{C}^{p}_{(j)}\mathfrak{f}(x).

Let us turn to the second inequality, which is a bit less clear. Let
\mathfrak{f}=\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a collections of functions on
G
. Pick a ball
B=B(x,r)\subset G
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
k\leq r^{\beta}
. By the Vitali lemma, we can find a covering
\{B_{i}^{k}\}_{1\leq i\leq N_{k}}
of
B
by
N_{k}\leq Cr/k^{1/\beta}
balls of radius
k^{1/\beta}
. We can also ensure that
\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}{\mathds{1}}_{2^{j+2}B_{i}^{k}}
is bounded by
C2^{jD_{G}}
, where
C
is independent of
B
and
k
. It ensures that

I_{k}:=\sum_{y\in B}\left|\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{m(y)}{k}\\
\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\sum_{y\in B^{k}_{i}}\left|\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{m(y)}{k}

But since
B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})\subset 2^{j+1}B^{k}_{i}
whenever
y\in B^{k}_{i}
, we have

I_{k}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}V(B^{k}_{i})\left|\frac{1}{V(2^{j+1}B^{k}_{i})}\sum_{z\in 2^{j+1}B_{i}^{k}}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{m(y)}{k}.

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,N_{k}\}
, take
\bar{B}_{i}^{k}\subset 2^{j+2}B_{i}^{k}
a ball of radius
k^{1/\beta}/2
such that

\sum_{z\in\bar{B}_{i}^{k}}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\gtrsim\frac{1}{2^{jD_{G}}}\sum_{z\in 2^{j+1}B_{i}^{k}}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z),

and since
V(2^{j+1}B_{i}^{k})\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}}V(\bar{B}_{i}^{k})
, we get that

I_{k}\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(4/q+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}V(\bar{B}^{k}_{i})\left|\frac{1}{V(\bar{B}^{k}_{i})}\sum_{z\in\bar{B}_{i}^{k}}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{m(y)}{k}\\
\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(1+4/q)}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\sum_{y\in\bar{B}_{i}^{k}}\left|\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{m(y)}{k}\\
\leq 2^{jD_{G}(1+4/q)}\sum_{y\in 2^{j+2}B}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}{\mathds{1}}_{2^{j+2}B^{k}_{i}}(y)\left|\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{m(y)}{k}\\
\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(2+4/q)}\sum_{y\in 2^{j+2}B}\left|\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{m(y)}{k},

where we use the fact that
\bar{B}_{i}^{k}\subset 2^{j+2}B_{i}^{k}
for the third line, and the fact that
\sum_{1\leq i\leq N}{\mathds{1}}_{2^{j+2}B_{i}^{k}}\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}}
for the last one.

Now, we sum over the
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and we get

\left(\frac{1}{V(x,r)}\sum_{k=1}^{r^{\beta}}\sum_{y\in B(x,r)}\frac{m(y)}{k}\left|\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(1+2/q)}\left(\frac{1}{V(x,r)}\sum_{k=1}^{r^{\beta}}\sum_{y\in 2^{j+2}B}\left|\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{m(y)}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(3/2+2/q)}\left(\frac{1}{V(x,2^{j+2}r)}\sum_{(y,k)\in\widehat{2^{j+2}B}}\left|\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}m(z)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}}\frac{m(y)}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq 2^{jD_{G}(3/2+2/q)}\mathcal{C}^{q}\mathfrak{f}(x),

Using again the doubling property to control the ratio
V(x,2^{j+2}r)/V(x,r)
. Taking the supremum on
r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
gives

\mathcal{C}^{q}_{(j)}\mathfrak{f}(x)\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}(3/2+2/q)}\mathcal{C}^{q}\mathfrak{f}(x)

as desired for the lemma.

\square

3.3.
Horizontal Lusin functional

Definition 3.7
.

Define, for
\alpha>0
, the quadratic functionals
g_{\alpha}
and
L_{\alpha}
on
L^{2}(G)
by

g_{\alpha}f(x):=\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\alpha-1}|\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f(y)|^{2}m(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

L_{\alpha}f(x):=\left(\sum_{(y,k)\in\Gamma(x)}\frac{k^{2\alpha-1}}{V(x,k)}|\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f(y)|^{2}m(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{A}(\{k\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}})(x),

where
\Gamma(x)=\left\{(y,k)\in G\times\mathbb{N}^{*},\,\delta(x,y)^{\beta}<k\right\}
, as before.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(G,\mu)
be a graph as defined in Subsection
2.1
. If
G
satisfies (
LB
) and
\alpha>0
, then the Littlewood-Paley functional
g_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. By duality, we also have the reverse bound. It means that there exists
C_{\alpha,p}
such that

C_{\alpha,p}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq\|g_{\alpha}(f)\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C_{\alpha,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}

for any
f\in L^{p}(G)
.

Proof:
For instance, the proof can be found in
[
Ste70b
, Chapter IV]
or
[
LMX12
, Theorem 3.3]
for the case
\alpha=1
, and
[
ALM14
, Theorem 3.3]
for the extension to other values of
\alpha>0
.

\square

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(G,\mu)
be a graph as defined in Subsection
2.1
.
If
\alpha>0
and
G
satisfies (
LB
), (
D
) and
(UE)β
for some
\beta\geq 2
, then the Lusin functional
L_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. By duality, we also have the reverse bound. It means that there exists
C_{\alpha,p}
such that

C_{\alpha,p}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq\|L_{\alpha}(f)\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C_{\alpha,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}

for any
f\in L^{p}(G)
.

Proof:
Assume that
x\in G
,
y\in G
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
are such that
\delta(x,y)^{\beta}<9k
. We claim that for any
g\in L^{2}(G)
, we have

(3.3)

|P^{k-1}g(x)|\leq\mathcal{M}(g)(y),

where
\mathcal{M}
is the uncentered maximal function

\mathcal{M}(g)(x):=\sup_{B\ni x}\sum_{z\in B}|g(z)|\,m(z).

Indeed,

|P^{k-1}g(x)|=\Big|\sum_{z\in G}p_{k-1}(x,z)g(z)m(z)\Big|\\
\lesssim\frac{1}{V(x,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in G}|g(z)|\exp\left(-c\Big[\frac{\delta(x,y)^{\beta}}{k}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\right)m(z)

We partition the graph
G
into annulus. If
C_{1}(x,k^{1/\beta})=B(x,4k^{1/\beta})
and
C_{j}(x,k^{1/\beta})=B(x,2^{j+1}k^{1/\beta})\setminus B(x,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})
, we obtain then that

|P^{k-1}g(x)|\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{V(x,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{C_{j}(x,k^{1/\beta})}|g(z)|\exp\left(-c\Big[\frac{\delta(x,y)^{\beta}}{k}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\right)m(z)\\
\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{V(x,2^{j+1}k^{1/\beta})}{V(x,k^{1/\beta})}\exp\left(-c2^{\frac{j\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)\frac{1}{V(x,2^{j+3}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{B(x,2^{j+3}k^{1/\beta})}|g(z)|m(z)\\
\lesssim\mathcal{M}(g)(y)\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{V(x,2^{j+3}k^{1/\beta})}{V(x,k^{1/\beta})}\exp\left(-c2^{\frac{j\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)\lesssim\mathcal{M}(g)(y),

since
y\in B(x,2^{j+3}k^{1/\beta})
for all
j\geq 1
, and

\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{V(x,2^{j+1}k^{1/\beta})}{V(x,k^{1/\beta})}\exp\left(-c2^{\frac{j\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{jD_{G}}\exp\left(-c2^{\frac{j\beta}{\beta-1}}\right)\lesssim 1

by (
2.7
). The claim (
3.3
) follows.

From there, we have then that, if
l=(k-1)/2
or
k/2-1
(depending whether
k
is odd or even),

\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f(z)|^{2}m(z)=\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|P^{l}[\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1-l}f(z)]|^{2}m(z)\\
\lesssim\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1-l}f)(x)|^{2}m(z)=|\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1-l}f)(x)|^{2}

Summing over
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
yields

L_{\alpha}f(y):=\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\alpha-1}\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{z\in B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f(z)|^{2}m(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\alpha-1}|\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{\alpha}P^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}f)(y)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\approx\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\alpha-1}|\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f)(y)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the last equivalence is obtained by reindexing the sum. After taking the
L^{p}
norm, the Fefferman-Stein inequality (whose proof in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be found in
[
Stein93
, Chapter 2, Theorem 1]
and which extends easily to any doubling spaces as it relies on the Calderón-Zygmund decompostion) gives, for
p\in(1,\infty)
, that

\|L_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{y\in G}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\alpha-1}|\mathcal{M}(\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1-l}f)(y)|^{2}m(y)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\lesssim\left(\sum_{y\in G}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\alpha-1}|\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1-l}f(y)|^{2}m(y)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\|g_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}}.

Thanks to Theorem
3.8
, we deduce the
L^{p}
-boundeness of the Lusin functional
L_{\alpha}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.

The lower bound can be deduced by duality. Let us define the variant of the Lusin functional

\tilde{L}_{\alpha}f(x):=\left(\sum_{(y,k)\in\Gamma(x)}\frac{c_{k}^{(2\alpha)}}{V(y,k)}|(I-P^{2})^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f(y)|^{2}m(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
c_{k}^{(2\alpha)}
are the coefficients of the power series

(1-z)^{-2\alpha}=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{(2\alpha)}z^{k-1}.

By the doubling property (
D
), we have
V(x,k^{1/\beta})\approx V(y,k^{1/\beta})
whenever
y\in B(x,k^{1/\beta})
. Moreover, Lemma B.1 in
[
Fen15b
]
shows that
c_{k}^{(2\alpha)}\approx k^{2\alpha-1}
. All this proves that

\tilde{L}_{\alpha}f(x)\approx L_{\alpha}(I+P)^{\alpha}f(x)

for all function
f
on
G
and all
x\in G
. However,
Observe that
\tilde{L}_{\alpha}f
is a better substitute of
L_{\alpha}
because it is an isometry on
L^{2}
. Indeed, Fubini’s theorem yields that
\|\tilde{L}_{\alpha}f\|_{2}^{2}=\|\tilde{g}_{\alpha}f\|_{2}^{2}
, where

\tilde{g}_{\alpha}f(x):=\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{(2\alpha)}|(I-P^{2})^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f(y)|^{2}m(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and Lemma 3.1 in
[
Fen15b
]
shows that
\|\tilde{g}_{\alpha}f\|_{2}^{2}=\|f\|_{2}^{2}
, meaning that
\tilde{L}_{\alpha}
is an isometry, and thus, for
f,h\in L^{2}(G)

\sum_{x\in G}f(x)h(x)m(x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{(2\alpha)}\sum_{x\in G}\sum_{y\in B(x,k^{1/\beta})}\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}[\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f(y)][\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}h(y)]m(y)

So if
f\in L^{2}(G)\cap L^{p}(G)
with
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}}=\sup_{h\in L^{2}\cap L^{p^{\prime}}\atop\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\sum_{x\in G}f(x)h(x)m(x)\\
=\sup_{h\in L^{2}\cap L^{p^{\prime}}\atop\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{(2\alpha)}\sum_{x\in G}\sum_{y\in B(x,k^{1/\beta})}\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}[\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}f(y)][\Delta^{\alpha}P^{k-1}h(y)]\\
\leq\|\tilde{L}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}}\sup_{h\in L^{2}\cap L^{p^{\prime}}\atop\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\|\tilde{L}_{\alpha}h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|L_{\alpha}(I+P)^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}}\sup_{h\in L^{2}\cap L^{p^{\prime}}\atop\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\|L_{\alpha}(I+P)^{\alpha}h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\\
\lesssim\|L_{\alpha}(I+P)^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}}

by the
L^{p^{\prime}}
-boundedness of
L_{\alpha}
and
(I+P)^{\alpha}
. Finally, since
G
satisfies (
LB
), the operator
(I+P)
is invertible in
L^{p}(G)
, as discussed previously in (
2.8
). It means that, for any
f\in L^{2}(G)\cap L^{p}(G)
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|(I+P)^{-\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|L_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}}.

The theorem follows by density of
L^{2}(G)\cap L^{p}(G)
in
L^{p}(G)
.

\square

4.
Consequences of Poincaré inequalities

4.1.
Estimates on
P^{k-1}\nabla^{*}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(G,\mu)
be a graph satisfying (
LB
), (
D
),
(UE)β
for a
\beta\geq 2
, and
(Pq)s
for some
q\in[1,\infty]
and
s\geq 1
. Then there exists
C,c>0
such that, for any function
f\in L^{q}_{loc}(G)
, any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and any
y\in G
, we have that

[P^{k-1}\nabla^{*}\nabla f](y)\leq Ck^{\frac{s}{\beta}-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}e^{-c\lambda^{j}}\left(\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{x\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|\nabla f|^{q}(x)m(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
\lambda=2^{\beta/(\beta-1)}
.

Proof:
We use the fact that
\nabla^{*}\nabla=\Delta
, and we have

[P^{k-1}\nabla^{*}\nabla f](y)=[\Delta P^{k-1}f](y)\\
=\sum_{x\in G}\Delta[p_{k-1}(.,y)](x)\,f(x)\,m(x)=\sum_{x\in G}[\partial_{k}p_{k-1}(.,y)](x)\,f(x)\,m(x)\\
=\sum_{x\in G}[\partial_{k}p_{k-1}(.,y)](x)\,[f(x)-f_{B(y,k^{1/\beta})}]\,m(x)

where
f_{B(y,k^{1/\beta})}:=\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1\beta})}\sum_{B(y,k^{1/\beta})}f(z)m(z)
, and where the last equality holds because
\sum_{x\in G}[\partial_{k}p_{k-1}(.,y)](x)m(x)=0
- a simple consequence of (
2.5
). Let now, as usually,
C_{1}(y,k):=B(y,2k^{1/\beta})
and
C_{j}(y,k):=B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})\setminus B(y,2^{j-1}k^{1/\beta})
for
j\geq 2
, so that we can write

\left|[P^{k-1}\nabla^{*}\nabla f](y)\right|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\sum_{x\in C_{j}(y,k)}\big|[\partial_{k}p_{k-1}(.,y)](x)\big|\,\big|f(x)-f_{B(y,k^{1/\beta})}\big|\,m(x)

But thanks to the pointwise bounds (
2.10
) on the discrete time derivative, we have for
x\in C_{j}(y,k)
,

|\partial_{k}p_{k-1}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{kV(y,k^{1/\beta})}\exp(-c\lambda^{j-1})\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{j(s+D_{G})}kV(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\exp(-c^{\prime}\lambda^{j}),

for
0<c^{\prime}<c/\lambda
, because the doubling property (
2.7
) of
G
gives

\frac{2^{j(s+D_{G})}kV(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}{kV(y,k^{1/\beta})}\lesssim 2^{j(s+2D_{G})}\lesssim\exp\big([c/\lambda-c^{\prime}]\lambda^{j}\big).

Consequently

(4.1)

\left|[P^{k-1}\nabla^{*}\nabla f](y)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\exp(-c^{\prime}\lambda^{j})2^{-j(\gamma+D_{G})}\Big(\underbrace{\displaystyle\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{x\in C_{j}(y,k)}|f(x)-f_{B(y,k^{1/\beta})}|m(x)}_{T_{j}}\Big).

We bound the terms
T_{j}
with the help of the Poincaré inequality. Indeed,

T_{j}\leq\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{x\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|f(x)-f_{B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|m(x)+|f_{B(y,k^{1/\beta})}-f_{B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|\\
\leq\Big(\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}+\frac{1}{V(y,k^{1/\beta})}\Big)\sum_{x\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|f(x)-f_{B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|m(x)\\
\lesssim\frac{1+2^{jD_{G}}}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{x\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|f(x)-f_{B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|m(x)\\
\lesssim 2^{jD_{G}}\left(\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{x\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|f(x)-f_{B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|^{q}m(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
\lesssim 2^{j(D_{G}+s)}k^{s/\beta}\left(\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{x\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|\nabla f|^{q}(x)m(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

where we used the doubling property (
2.7
) in the third inequality, and the Poincaré inequality
(Pq)s
in the last one. After reinjecting the estimates on
T_{j}
in (
4.1
), we conclude that

\left|[P^{k-1}\nabla^{*}\nabla f](y)\right|\lesssim k^{\frac{s}{\beta}-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\exp(-c^{\prime}\lambda^{j})\left(\frac{1}{V(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}\sum_{x\in B(y,2^{j}k^{1/\beta})}|\nabla f|^{q}(x)m(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

The lemma follows.
\square

4.2.
Vertical Lusin functionals

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(G,\mu)
be a graph satisfying (
LB
), (
D
),
(UE)β
for a
\beta\geq 2
, and
(Pq)s
for some
q\in[1,\infty]
and
s\geq 1
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for any for any collection
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
of functions in
L^{p}_{loc}(G)
and any

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(G,\mu)
be a
D
-dimensional Vicsek graph, and let
S\subset G
be a connected component. For
p\in[1,\infty]
, define the Sobolev space

\dot{W}^{1,p}(S):=\{f\in L^{p}_{loc}(G,m),\,\nabla f\in L^{p}(E_{S},\mu)\},

where
E_{S}:=\{(x,y)\in E_{G},\,(x,y)\in S^{2}\}
, equipped with the semi-norm

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}(S)}:=\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(E_{S},\mu)}.

Then the Sobolev spaces interpolate with the complex method. That is, if
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are two compatible Banach spaces for complex interpolation, if
\theta\in[0,1]
and
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
verify
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{2}}
, and if
M:\,\dot{W}^{1,p_{1}}(S)\cup\dot{W}^{1,p_{1}}(S)\to X\cup Y
is a maps verifying, for
i\in\{1,2\}
,

\|Mf\|_{X_{i}}\leq C_{i}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p_{i}}(S)}\qquad\text{ for }f\in\dot{W}^{1,p_{i}}(S).

Then

(5.1)

\|Mf\|_{[X_{1},X_{2}]_{\theta}}\leq 2(C_{1})^{\theta}(C_{2})^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}(S)}\qquad\text{ for }f\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(S).

Proof:
Let
x\in S
fixed randomly. Since
G
is a tree, we can define a partial order on
G
as follows: for
y,z\in G
, we write
y\prec z
if
y\neq z
and the (shortest) path from
x
to
z
goes through
y
. From there, we can see 1-forms on
S
(as defined in Subsection
2.1
) as functions on

\bar{E}_{S}:=\{(y,z)\in S\times S,\,y\sim z,\,y\prec z\}=\{(y,z)\in E_{S},\ y\prec z\},

and construct a primitive of the 1-form
\omega
as a function on
S
defined by

\Omega(z):=\sum_{i=1}^{\delta(x,z)}\omega(e_{i}),

where
e_{i}=(x_{i-1},x_{i})\in\bar{E}_{G}
satisfies
x=x_{0}
,
z=x_{\delta(x,z)}
, and
x_{i-1}\sim x_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,\delta(x,z)\}
. Indeed, the primitive is well defined since the shortest path from
x\in S
to
z\in S
necessary stays in
S
, because
S
is connected inside a tree.

We define then
T
be the operator

T:\omega\to\Omega,

and since
T\nabla f=f
and
\nabla T\omega=\omega
, we have that

T:\omega\in\bigcup_{q\in[1,\infty]}L^{q}(\bar{E}_{S})\to\Omega\in\bigcup_{q\in[1,\infty]}W^{1,q}(S)

with, for all
q\in[1,\infty]

\|T\omega\|_{\dot{W}^{1,q}(S)}=\|\omega\|_{L^{q}(\bar{E}_{S},\mu)}.

Let
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
,
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
\theta
,
p
, and
M
like in the theorem. We have then, for
i\in\{1,2\}

\|MT\omega\|_{X_{i}}\leq C_{1}\|T\omega\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p_{i}}(S)}=C_{i}\|\omega\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\bar{E}_{S},\mu)}\qquad\text{ for }\omega\in L^{p_{i}}(\bar{E}_{S},\mu),

so by complex interpolation, we get that

\|MT\omega\|_{[X_{1},X_{2}]_{\theta}}\leq 2(C_{1})^{\theta}(C_{2})^{1-\theta}\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(\bar{E}_{S},\mu)}\qquad\text{ for }\omega\in L^{p}(\bar{E}_{S},\mu).

Applying the above inequality for
\omega=\nabla f
and

, we deduce (
5.1
). The theorem follows.

\square

Corollary 5.2
.

Let

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
(G,\mu)
be a

-dimensional Vicsek graph. Then, for any

, the Poincaré inequality
(P
q
)
{}_{1+\frac{D-1}{q}}
holds. More precisely, there exists

such that, for any

, any

(5.3)

\left(\sum_{z\in B(x,r)}|u(z)-u(x)|^{q}m(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq Cr^{1+\frac{D-1}{q}}\left(\sum_{z,z^{\prime}\in B(x,r)\atop z\sim z^{\prime}}|\nabla u(z,z^{\prime})|^{q}\mu_{zz^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Proof:
Let

With the notation of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
We need to prove the following 4 facts:

(a)

(
RRp,γ
) holds when
p\in(1,\infty
) and
\gamma^{*}(p)<\gamma<1
;

(b)

(
RRp,γ
) fails when
p\in(1,\infty
) and
0<\gamma<\gamma^{*}(p)
;

(c)

(
Rp,γ
) fails when
p\in(1,\infty
) and
\gamma^{*}(p)<\gamma<1
;

(d)

(
Rp,γ
) holds when
p\in(1,\infty
) and
0<\gamma<\gamma^{*}(p)
;

Let us show the 4 assertions, in order.

Assertion (a).
By Corollary
5.3
, for any
q\in[1,\infty]
, the Poincaré inequality (P
q
)
{}_{1+\frac{D-1}{q}}
holds. Together with Theorem
1.4
, it means that (
RRp,γ
) whenever there exists
q\in[1,\infty]
satisfying
q<p
and

\frac{1+\frac{D-1}{q}}{D+1}<\gamma<1,

i.e. whenever

\gamma>\frac{1+\frac{D-1}{p}}{D+1}=\frac{1}{D+1}+\frac{1}{p}\Big(\frac{D-1}{D+1}\Big)=\gamma^{*}(p).

Assertion (b).
Assume by contradiction that there exists
p_{0}\in(1,\infty)
and
0<\gamma_{0}<\gamma^{*}(p_{0})
such that (RR
{}_{p_{0},\gamma_{0}}
) holds. Assertion (a) gives us also that (
RRp,γ
) holds whenever
p\in(1,\infty
) and
\gamma^{*}(p)<\gamma<1
. By interpoling those two situations - using Corollary
5.2
- we deduce that (RR
2,γ
) holds for a
\gamma<2
. However, by
[
Fen26
, Lemma 4.4]
, the later is impossible, hence the contradiction.

Assertion (c).
This comes directly from Assertion (b) and the duality given in Lemma
2.1
.

Assertion (d).

First, observe that
\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p}(G)}\approx\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(E_{G})}
. Fix
x\in G
and define the set
E_{G}
and the map
T
as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
. We have
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(E_{G})}^{p}=2\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\bar{E}_{G})}^{p}
, and thus

\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim\sup_{\omega\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\bar{E}_{G})\atop\|\omega\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\sum_{e\in\bar{E}_{G}}\nabla f(e)\,\omega(e)\,\mu(e)=\sup_{\omega\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\bar{E}_{G})\atop\|\omega\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\sum_{e\in\bar{E}_{G}}\nabla f(e)\,\nabla T\omega(e)\,\mu(e)

We use the fact that
E_{G}=\{(y,z)\in G^{2},\,(y,z)\text{ or }(z,y)\in\bar{E}_{G}\}
, and that
\nabla u(y,z)=-\nabla u(z,y)
for
(y,z)\in E_{G}
to say that

(5.4)

\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim\sup_{\omega\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\bar{E}_{G})\atop\|\omega\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\sum_{e\in E_{G}}\nabla f(e)\,\nabla T\omega(e)\,\mu(e)\\
=\sup_{\omega\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\bar{E}_{G})\atop\|\omega\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\sum_{z\in G}\Delta f(z)\,T\omega(z)\,m(z)=\sup_{\omega\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\bar{E}_{G})\atop\|\omega\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\sum_{z\in G}\Delta^{\gamma}f(z)\,[\Delta^{1-\gamma}T\omega](z)\,m(z)\\
\leq\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\sup_{\omega\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\bar{E}_{G})\atop\|\omega\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1}\,\|\Delta^{1-\gamma}T\omega\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}

where the first equality of the second line is due to (
2.1
). But with our assumptions on
\gamma
and
p
, we have
\gamma^{*}(p^{\prime})<1-\gamma<1
, meaning that we can invoke Assertion (a) to obtain

\|\Delta^{1-\gamma}T\omega\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}\leq\||\nabla T\omega|\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}\approx\|\nabla T\omega\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\bar{E}_{G})}

by (

). But since

is the identity on

, we deduce that

\|\Delta^{1-\gamma}T\omega\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\bar{E}_{G})}

and then, by reinjecting this last bound in (

),

\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim\|\Delta^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}.

Assertion (d) and then the Theorem follows.

\square
