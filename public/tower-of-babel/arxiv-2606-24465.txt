History estimation in random recursive trees: Pointwise approach via iterated Jordan centralities
Johannes Bäumler, Simon Briend, Joost Jorritsma

Abstract
We study the problem of estimating the arrival times of vertices in a uniform random recursive tree from its unlabeled structure. We adopt a pointwise perspective and analyze the distribution of the relative estimation error, and derive tail bounds that are uniform in both the vertex and the tree size. For the ranking induced by Jordan centrality, the probability that the estimate exceeds the true arrival time by a factor S S decays on the order of 1 / S 1/S , while the probability of underestimating the arrival time by a factor 1 / S 1/S decays exponentially in S S . We introduce a refined centrality measure whose overestimation tail decays on the order of ( log ⁡ S ) / S 2 (\log S)/S^{2} , at the cost of a heavier lower tail of order 1 / S 2 1/S^{2} . These results reveal a tradeoff between upper- and lower-tail performance in arrival-time estimation that is invisible to the previously studied risk functional. Nevertheless, the refined centrality measure attains the optimal order of the risk for all its parameter values.



Keywords:
Network archaeology, centrality measures, history estimation, uniform attachment trees, random recursive tree.

Introduction and main results

Network archaeology asks what can be inferred about the growth history of a network from its final structure. A central instance is root-finding: given a graph generated by a growth process but observed without arrival-time labels, identify the vertex that arrived first. This problem models, for example, the detection of patient zero in infection networks
[
18
,
20
]
, and has been studied extensively for random tree models, including uniform random recursive trees
[
10
,
1
,
2
,
5
,
19
]
, preferential attachment trees
[
11
,
14
,
10
]
, nearest neighbor trees
[
7
]
, and Bienaymé–Galton–Watson trees
[
6
]
. Related questions have also been explored for random graphs beyond trees, see
[
8
,
3
,
13
]
.
Root-finding algorithms commonly proceed either via Bayesian approaches
[
12
,
24
]
or by ranking vertices according to a centrality statistic. The latter builds upon the idea that the root is among the most ‘central’ nodes in the graph, and a variety of centrality measures have been considered, including degree, rumor centrality, and Jordan centrality.

Motivated by these results, recent work has asked whether centrality-based rankings can be used to recover more detailed temporal information. Rather than identifying only the earliest vertex, the goal is to estimate the arrival time of an arbitrary vertex
v
, given only the unlabeled tree. This problem was first investigated for preferential attachment trees in
[
21
,
24
]
, and more recently for uniform random recursive trees in
[
9
]
. In these works, the arrival time of
v
is estimated by
\widehat{\sigma}(v)
, defined as the rank of
v
induced by a given centrality measure. In
[
9
]
, the performance of
\widehat{\sigma}
is evaluated through the risk functional

R_{\alpha}(\widehat{\sigma}):=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{v\in V_{n}}\frac{|\widehat{\sigma}(v)-v|}{v^{\alpha}}\bigg],\qquad\alpha>0.

(1.1)

Our contribution.

In this paper, we take an alternative, pointwise perspective. For a vertex
v\in[n]
in a uniform random recursive tree, we study the distribution of the relative error
\widehat{\sigma}(v)/v
and derive tail bounds that are uniform in both
v
and
n
.
Proposition
1.2
shows that every label-invariant estimator incurs errors of order
v
with constant probability, making relative error the natural scale for arrival-time estimation.
Moreover, this perspective distinguishes between overestimation and underestimation. While this distinction is absent in root finding and only weakly reflected by the risk functional (
1.1
), it reveals qualitative differences between estimators that are invisible from the risk alone. At the same time, pointwise bounds still determine the asymptotic order of the global risk.
We summarize our main findings informally.

-

Jordan-2 estimator
. We introduce a new centrality measure
\phi^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}}
and study the ranking
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}
based on this centrality measure. Theorem
1.3
shows that the upper tail decays faster than the ranking based on Jordan centrality, as

\mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\big)\asymp\frac{1+\log S}{S^{2}},\qquad S\to\infty.

(1.2)

This improvement comes at the cost of a heavier lower tail, as

\mathbb{P}(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\leq v/S)\asymp S^{-2}
for this estimator. Furthermore, the tail bounds (
1.2
) allow us to determine the asymptotic growth of the risk for any value of
\alpha>0
(Corollary
1.7
), which turns out to be optimal up to constant factors (Corollary
1.6
).

-

Jordan estimator
. For the estimator
\widehat{\sigma}_{J}
induced by Jordan centrality, we obtain matching upper and lower bounds, up to constants, for the upper tail uniformly in
v
and
n
; see Theorem
1.5
. That is,

\mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\big)\asymp\frac{1}{S},\qquad S\to\infty.

We also show that for the estimator induced by Jordan centrality, the lower tail decays much faster than the upper tail, as
\log\mathbb{P}(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq v/S)\asymp-S
.
This upper-tail estimate allows us to identify the order of the parametrized risk (
1.1
) for Jordan centrality for all values of
\alpha>0
, determining regimes in which this order is suboptimal relative to that of
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}
(Corollary
1.7
).

Our analysis of these two estimators suggests a qualitative tradeoff that we expect to hold more broadly (for instance, for rankings based on rumor centrality): improvements in the upper-tail behavior of the estimation error come at the expense of worse lower-tail behavior, and vice versa. From this perspective, the Jordan-2 estimator may be viewed as comparatively balanced, in that both its upper and lower tails exhibit quadratic decay, up to logarithmic factors.
Next, we formalize our model, the estimators, and our results.

The model: uniform random recursive trees

We study uniform random recursive trees (RRT). We denote by
T_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n})
a RRT of size
n
, where the vertex set is
V_{n}=[n]
, and the random edge set
E_{n}
is sampled recursively as follows. Start with
T_{1}
, consisting of a single vertex labeled
1
, the root, and no edges. Given
T_{n}
, the tree
T_{n+1}
is constructed by first choosing
u\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
uniformly at random, independent of
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
, and then adding a new vertex labeled
n+1
and the undirected edge
\{u,n+1\}
to the tree. We also write
\mathrm{pa}(n+1)=u
and say that
u
is the
parent
of
n+1

The tree
T_{n}
is
recursive
: its vertex labels are increasing on any simple (non-backtracking) path starting at the root. We sometimes relabel trees by applying a permutation to their vertex labels. When the vertex labels of a labeled tree
\mathrm{t}
are permuted by
\tau

\mathrm{t}^{\tau}
. In general,
\mathrm{t}^{\tau}
is not a recursive tree.

History estimation.
Our goal is to estimate the history of the tree, that is, to estimate the arrival time of each vertex using only the unlabeled tree structure. Formally, given a vertex-labeled tree
\mathrm{t}_{n}
on
n
vertices, an estimator
\widehat{\sigma}
is a (possibly random) bijection
\widehat{\sigma}:V_{n}\to[n]
. Throughout, we only consider
label-invariant estimators
, which satisfy, for any fixed labeled tree
\mathrm{t}
, any permutation of its vertices
\tau
, and any vertex
u
,

\widehat{\sigma}\left(u,\mathrm{t}\right)\quad\overset{\mathcal{L}}{=}\quad\widehat{\sigma}\left(\tau(u),\mathrm{t}^{\tau}\right),

(1.3)

where the equality would be deterministic if
\widehat{\sigma}
itself were deterministic given
\mathrm{t}
.

Main results

We start with a lower bound on the performance of arbitrary label-invariant estimators, which we prove in Section
4
. This lower bound implies that
|\widehat{\sigma}(v)-v|
is of the same order as
v
(with positive probability), for all label-invariant estimators
\widehat{\sigma}
.

Proposition 1.2

(Pointwise estimation for arbitrary estimators)
.

Let
\widehat{\sigma}
be a label-invariant estimator of the arrival times in
T_{n}
. Then, for any
n\geq 3
, and any
v\in[n]
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(|\widehat{\sigma}(v)-v|\geq\frac{v}{56}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{224}.

This proposition implies that one should focus on the relative error of
\widehat{\sigma}(v)/v
. Unlike root-finding, arrival-time estimation naturally distinguishes between two directions of error: an arrival time may be estimated substantially later or earlier than it truly is. This distinction is only weakly reflected by the risk functional (
1.1
): estimators with markedly different pointwise behavior may nevertheless have the same leading-order risk; see Section
1.4
.
We therefore distinguish between
overestimation
and
underestimation
, and control the upper tail (the event
\left\{\widehat{\sigma}(v)\geq Sv\right\}
) and lower tail (the event
\left\{\widehat{\sigma}(v)\leq v/S\right\}
) separately, for large values of
S
, and uniformly over
v\in[n]
. The next theorem describes our results for the Jordan-2 ordering.

Theorem 1.3

(Pointwise estimation of the Jordan-
2
ordering)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist constants
c,C>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
, any
v\in[n]
and any
S\in\left[1,c(n/v)^{1-\varepsilon}\right]
, the upper tail satisfies

c\frac{1+\log S}{S^{2}}\ \leq\ \mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\big)\ \leq\ C\frac{1+\log S}{S^{2}}.

(1.10)

The upper bound holds for all
S\geq 1
.

Moreover, there exist constants
c^{\prime},C^{\prime},\delta>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
, any
v\in[n/4]
, and any
S\in[1,\delta\sqrt{v}]
, the lower tail satisfies

\frac{c^{\prime}}{S^{2}}\ \leq\ \mathbb{P}\Big(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\leq\frac{1}{S}v\Big)\ \leq\ \frac{C^{\prime}}{S^{2}}.

(1.11)

The upper bound holds for all
S\geq 1
.

For
v=1
, the upper bound on the probability of overestimating the label of
v
, (
1.10
), directly implies the following corollary for root-finding with the Jordan-2 ordering.

Corollary 1.4

(Root detection using Jordan-2 centrality)
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the set
\mathcal{S}_{2}
containing the
C\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)/\varepsilon}
vertices with largest Jordan-2 centrality contains vertex
1
with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
.

For small values of
\varepsilon>0
, the required set
\mathcal{S}_{2}
defined above is much larger compared to the optimal confidence set at confidence level
1-\varepsilon
, whose size is of order
\exp\big(\Theta\big(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\big)\big)
, see
[
2
]
. On the other hand, when using the Jordan ordering, the required set has size that scales as
1/\varepsilon
, see
[
10
, Theorem 3]
, which also follows from the next theorem.

Theorem 1.5

(Pointwise estimation of the Jordan ordering)
.

For the Jordan-based estimator
\widehat{\sigma}_{J}
, there exist constants
c,C,\delta>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
, any
v\in[n]
and any
S\in[1,\delta n/v]
, the upper tail satisfies

\frac{c}{S}\ \leq\ \mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\big)\ \leq\ \frac{C}{S}.

(1.12)

The upper bound holds for all
S\geq 1
.

Moreover, there exist constants
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime},C^{\prime},C^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime}>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
, any vertex
v\in[n/2]
, and any
S\in[1,\min(\sqrt{v},\delta^{\prime}v)]
, the lower tail satisfies

c^{\prime\prime}\exp(-C^{\prime\prime}S)\ \leq\ \mathbb{P}\Big(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq\frac{1}{S}v\Big)\ \leq\ C^{\prime}\exp(-c^{\prime}S).

(1.13)

The upper bound holds for all
v\in[n]
and
S\in[1,\sqrt{v}]
; the lower bound holds for all
v\in[n/2]
and
S\in[1,\delta^{\prime}v]
.

Heuristics and comparison.

The different tail behaviors of the two orderings can be understood from the growth of the fringe trees. For the Jordan ordering, the upper tail is governed by the event that vertex
v
remains a leaf until time of order
Sv
. This event has probability of order
1/S
; on it, the fringe tree of
v
looks like the fringe tree of a vertex that arrived around time
Sv
, leading to an overestimation by a factor of order
S
. By contrast, underestimation requires the fringe tree of
v
to be atypically large, of order
Sn/v
, an event exponentially unlikely in
S
.

For the Jordan-2 ordering, overestimation requires not only the fringe tree of
v
, but also the fringe tree of its parent, to be small. Heuristically,

\Big\{\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\Big\}\approx\bigg\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\cdot\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\lesssim\left(\frac{n}{Sv}\right)^{2}\bigg\}=\bigg\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\frac{v}{n}\cdot\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\frac{v}{n}\lesssim\frac{1}{S^{2}}\bigg\},

where
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)=|(T_{n},1)_{u\downarrow}|
denotes the size of the fringe tree of
u
.
The probability of this event is of order
(\log S)/S^{2}
. The lower tail has a different mechanism:
v
can be estimated as much older than it is when its parent arrived much earlier than
v
. If the parent arrived before time
v/S^{2}
, an event of probability of order
1/S^{2}
, then the product of the two fringe tree sizes is typically larger by a factor
S^{2}
. This causes the Jordan-2 ordering to rank
v
as if it had arrived around time
v/S
. We see that, compared to the ordering
\widehat{\sigma}_{J}
based on the Jordan centrality, the ordering
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}
based on the Jordan-2 centrality improves the upper tail from order
1/S
to essentially
1/S^{2}
. However, this gain comes at the cost of a thicker lower tail, which is exponentially small for the Jordan ordering, but polynomially small for the ordering based on the Jordan-2 centrality.

Risk

The pointwise tail bounds established in Proposition
1.2
and Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
readily yield bounds on the risk
R_{\alpha}
defined in (

.
In particular, the Jordan-2 ordering attains the optimal order of the risk for every
\alpha>0

(Lower bounds for arbitrary estimators)
.

For each
\alpha>0
, there exists a constant

such that for any label-invariant estimator
\widehat{\sigma}
, and any

,

R_{\alpha}(\widehat{\sigma})\geq\begin{cases}c_{\alpha}n^{2-\alpha},&\text{if }\alpha\in(0,2),\\
c_{\alpha}\log n,&\text{if }\alpha=2,\\
c_{\alpha},&\text{if }\alpha>2.\end{cases}

Corollary

improves the lower bounds obtained in

f_{\alpha}(n)/g(n)\in[\tfrac{1}{C_{\alpha}},C_{\alpha}]

(Jordan-

Proofs.

We give the proof of the upper bound on
R_{\alpha}(\widehat{\sigma}_{2})
in Corollary
1.7
; the other bounds follow analogously.
The risk defined in (
1.1
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle R_{\alpha}(\widehat{\sigma}_{2})=\sum_{u=1}^{n}u^{-\alpha}\mathbb{E}\big[|\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(u)-u|\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{u=1}^{n}u^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{n}\mathbb{P}\left(|\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(u)-u|\geq x\right)\ \mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{u=1}^{n}u^{1-\alpha}\int_{0}^{n/u}\mathbb{P}\left(|\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(u)-u|\geq uy\right)\ \mathrm{d}y.

(1.14)

When
y>1
,
|\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(u)-u|\geq uy
implies that
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(u)\geq uy
, since
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(u)\geq 0
. Thus, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{n/u}\mathbb{P}\left(|\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(u)-u|\geq uy\right)\ \mathrm{d}y\leq 1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(u)\geq uy\right)\ \mathrm{d}y\overset{\eqref{jordan2:overest_combined}}{\leq}1+\int_{1}^{\infty}C\frac{1+\log(y)}{y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\eqqcolon C^{\star},

where
C^{\star}>0
is a constant not depending on

. Inserting this bound into (
1.14
) finishes the proof of the upper bound. The other bounds in Corollaries

and
1.7
can be obtained analogously.
∎

For
\alpha\geq 2
, Corollary

improves the risk upper bound on the Jordan ordering of

from
[

, Theorem 4]
. It was already noted below
[

, Theorem 4]
that the risk of the Jordan ordering should be at least of order

\alpha\geq 2
. This bound is sharp for

; for

the risk is of order

instead. When

]
.

The proof of Corollary
1.7
highlights a limitation of the risk
R_{\alpha}
. Indeed, controlling
R_{\alpha}
only requires bounds on the
upper tail
of the pointwise estimation error: if an estimator
\widehat{\sigma}
satisfies
\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}(v)\geq Sv\right)\leq f(S)
uniformly in
v
and
n
for an integrable function
f
, the risk is always of the optimal order.
Thus, once the upper tail is integrable, further improvements do not affect the leading-order asymptotics of the risk, while the
lower tail
is invisible altogether. In particular, estimators with qualitatively different lower-tail behavior may nevertheless have the same asymptotic risk. This motivates the pointwise perspective developed throughout the paper.

Further directions

The tradeoff between upper and lower tails described above raises the question whether there are estimators that can strictly improve upon the Jordan-2 ordering in one tail without sacrificing performance in the other, or whether the observed phenomenon is inevitable.

Open problem 1.8
.

Find a label-invariant estimator
\widehat{\sigma}_{\ast}
such that there exist constants
a,b>2
, such that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
,
v\in[n]
, and
S\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{\ast}(v)\leq v/S\right)\leq CS^{-a+\varepsilon},\qquad\text{and }\qquad\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{\ast}(v)\geq vS\right)\leq CS^{-b+\varepsilon}.

We have no evidence that such estimators exist; proving non-existence would be equally interesting.
Such an estimator would be genuinely better at the pointwise level, even though it would not improve the risk
R_{\alpha}
(defined in (
1.1
)) by more than a constant factor, since
R_{\alpha}
is insensitive, to first order, to the lower tail. Capturing this improvement would require a risk functional that distinguishes upper- and lower-tail errors in a more refined way.

The Jordan ordering fails the desired upper-tail bound by Theorem
1.5
. More generally, orderings that incorporate more information about the ancestral line of a vertex may improve the upper tail, but we expect them to do so at the expense of the lower tail. This motivates the following extension of the Jordan-
2
construction.
Adopting the convention
v^{\scriptscriptstyle{({0}})}=v
, define recursively

v^{\scriptscriptstyle{({j}})}:=\argmin_{u\in\mathrm{t}:u\sim v^{\scriptscriptstyle{({j-1}})}}\big|(\mathrm{t},\,u)_{v^{\scriptscriptstyle{({j-1}})}\downarrow}\big|,\qquad j\geq 1.

Thus,
v^{\scriptscriptstyle{({j}})}
estimates the
j
-th ancestor of
v
, with ties resolved analogously to the case
j=1
in Definition
1.1
. For
k\geq 3
, we define the Jordan-
k
centrality by

\phi^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({k}})}}_{\mathrm{t}}(v):=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\big|(\mathrm{t},v^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({j+1}})}})_{v^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({j}})}}\downarrow}\big|\right)\vee\left(\phi^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({k-1}})}}_{\mathrm{t}}(v)\right)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}.

The Jordan-
k
ordering is obtained by ordering vertices decreasingly with respect to
\phi^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({k}})}}
. Note that
k=1
recovers the standard Jordan ordering introduced by
\phi
in (
1.4
).

We expect that increasing
k
makes the estimator less likely to overestimate the arrival time, with upper tails of order
S^{-k\pm o(1)}
, but more likely to underestimate it, with lower tails of order
S^{-k/(k-1)\pm o(1)}
. The mechanism behind the lower tail is that young vertices attached close to the root may be estimated as much older than they actually are, an effect that becomes stronger as more ancestral information is incorporated. In this sense, Jordan-2 appears to be the most balanced member of the family, as both tails exhibit quadratic decay up to logarithmic factors.

A separate question concerns the far lower tail. The lower-tail estimates in Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
describe a polynomial regime and an exponential regime, respectively, valid for small and intermediate values of
S
. For the Jordan-
2
ordering, this polynomial decay cannot persist for
S\gg\sqrt{v}
. Indeed, Remark
2.18
shows that
\mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\leq v/S\big)
is already stretched-exponentially small when
S\gg\sqrt{v}
.
Figure
2
provides empirical evidence for this transition for large values of
S
. We conjecture that the lower tail of the Jordan-2 ordering undergoes a transition from polynomial decay to stretched-exponential decay, and that a similar phenomenon occurs for the Jordan ordering.

Performance of the Jordan-
2
ordering

In this section, we study the Jordan-
2
centrality measure
\phi^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}}
and its associated ordering
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}
, proving the four inequalities of Theorem
1.3
in Lemmas
2.14
,

2.15
,

2.17
, and
2.19
, respectively.
We start with an outline.

Outline: upper tail – upper bound.

Since
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(\cdot)
ranks vertices in decreasing order of Jordan-2 centrality, the event
\{\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\}
implies that at least
Sv
vertices have Jordan-2 centrality at least
\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)
.
The proof splits the event
\{\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\}
into two possibilities: the Jordan-2 centrality of the fixed vertex
v
is unusually small, or there are unusually many vertices whose Jordan-2 centrality exceeds an explicit threshold. We use that, for some constant
C>0
,

\big\{\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\big\}\subseteq\Big\{\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\leq\left(C\tfrac{n}{Sv}\right)^{2}\Big\}\,\cup\,\Big\{\big|\big\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(u)\geq\left(C\tfrac{n}{Sv}\right)^{2}\big\}\big|\geq Sv\Big\}.

(2.1)

Our cutoff value for
\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)
is motivated by its typical scale: recall from (
1.6
) that

\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)=\phi_{n}(v)\left(\phi_{n}(v)\vee\phi_{n}(v^{\scriptscriptstyle{({1}})})\right),

(2.2)

where
\phi_{n}(v)
and
\phi_{n}(v^{\scriptscriptstyle{({1}})})
estimate the sizes of the related subtrees
(T_{n},1)_{v\downarrow}
and
(T_{n},1)_{\mathrm{pa}(v)\downarrow}
. These subtree sizes follow a beta-binomial distribution and are typically of order
n/v
each.

The probability of the first event in the union in (
2.1
) gives the dominant contribution to the upper bound on
\mathbb{P}(\widehat{\sigma}(v)\geq Sv)
. This event depends on the local structure around
v
, i.e., the fringe trees of
v
and
\mathrm{pa}(v)
.
We derive a deterministic lower bound on
\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)
as the product of two subtree sizes of vertices that arrive before time
v
, but where we only count descendants arriving after time
v
. These restricted subtree sizes follow a beta-binomial distribution, and because the subtrees involved are disjoint, their sizes are negatively associated. A tail bound for the product of two negatively associated beta-binomial variables then yields the decay of order
(\log S)/S^{2}
.

The second event in the union in (
2.1
)—that many vertices have Jordan-2 centrality at least
\big(\tfrac{n}{Sv}\big)^{2}
—requires information about subtree sizes throughout the tree, and is substantially more difficult.
To control it, we use a construction of the Ulam–Harris embedding of the tree. In this construction—called fringe-tree splitting—the root begins with mass
n
, and its children receive random integer masses that sum up to
n-1
; the procedure continues recursively for every vertex with mass at least
2
. This construction provides enough independence to count vertices with large Jordan-2 centrality and forms the other main technical ingredient of the proof.

Outline: upper tail – lower bound.

For
\{\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\}
to hold, it suffices that at least
Sv
vertices have Jordan-2 centrality exceeding
\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)
. Using a similar cutoff for
\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)
to (
2.1
),

\big\{\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\big\}\,\supseteq\,\Big\{\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\leq\left(\tfrac{1}{64S}\tfrac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\Big\}\,\setminus\,\Big\{\big|\big\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(u)>\left(\tfrac{1}{64S}\tfrac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\big\}\big|<Sv\Big\}.

We establish a lower bound on the first probability on the right-hand side analogously to the reasoning below (
2.2
). This reduces lower bounding
\mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\big)
to establishing an upper bound on the probability of the second event on the right-hand side.

To have few vertices with large Jordan-2 centrality, it is required that few vertices in the tree have a fringe tree of size at least
n/(64Sv)
.
Since the tree
T_{n}
typically contains order
Sv
vertices whose fringe trees are at least
n/(Sv)
,
having substantially fewer such vertices is unlikely.

Outline: lower tail – upper and lower bounds.

For both the upper and lower bound on the probability that the Jordan-2 estimator is unlikely small, we use similar decomposition as for the upper tail. The main difference is that we instead need to control
\mathbb{P}\big(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\geq(Sn/v)^{2}\big)
, which is dominated by the probability that the parent of
v
arrived before time
v/S^{2}
.

Organisation of the section.

Thus, to prove the four inequalities of Theorem
1.3
, we always decompose the relevant event into a local event on the Jordan-2 centrality of
v
, and a global event ensuring that the Jordan-2 centrality throughout the tree behaves regularly.
The probability of the local event provides the dominant contribution for all of these tail bounds, whereas the probability of the global event is negligible.
In Section
2.2
, we introduce the fringe-splitting construction and develop the counting lemmas used to control the number of vertices with Jordan-
2
centrality at least

. The remaining subsections complete the proofs of the four inequalities in Theorem
1.3
. That is, in Section
2.3
, we finish the proof of the upper bound on the upper tail by combining these results. In Section
2.4
, we prove a lower bound on the upper tail. In Section
2.5
, we analyze the lower tail relying on the auxiliary lemmas developed before.

By Lemma
2.4
, the upper bound on the lower tail has been reduced to controlling products of witnessed fringe-tree sizes. Since these variables are negatively associated, the following elementary claim allows us to study the lower tail of their product.

Claim 2.5
.

Let
X,Y
be two non-negative random variables that are negatively associated and for which there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
s>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq s\right),\ \mathbb{P}\left(Y\leq s\right)\leq Cs.

Then, for any
s\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(XY\leq s\right)\leq 2C^{2}(\log_{2}(1/s)+1)s+2Cs.

Proof.

We bound
\mathbb{P}(XY\leq s)\leq\mathbb{P}(X\leq s)+\mathbb{P}(Y\leq s)+\mathbb{P}\left(XY\leq s,X>s,Y>s\right).

The first two terms are each at most
Cs
. For the third term we use a dyadic decomposition for
X
: for
j\geq 0
, define
I_{j}\coloneqq(s2^{j},s2^{j+1}]
. If
X\in I_{j}
and
XY\leq s
, then
Y\leq 2^{-j}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(XY\leq s,\ X>s,\ Y>s\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}(1/s)\rfloor}\mathbb{P}\left(X\in I_{j},Y\leq 2^{-j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}(1/s)\rfloor}\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq s2^{j+1},Y\leq 2^{-j}\right).

By negative association,

\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq s2^{j+1},Y\leq 2^{-j}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}(X\leq s2^{j+1})\cdot\mathbb{P}(Y\leq 2^{-j})\leq Cs2^{j+1}\cdot C2^{-j}=2C^{2}s.

Summing over
j=0,\dots,\lfloor\log_{2}(1/s)\rfloor
yields the claimed bound.
∎

We next turn to the analysis of the upper tail of the Jordan-2 centrality
\phi^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}}
, which will be used to study the lower tail of the Jordan-2 ordering
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)
. We state the lower and upper bounds in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 2.7

(Upper tail of the Jordan-2 centrality: lower bound)
.

C>0
such that for any

,

Lemma 2.8

There exists a constant

such that for any

,

and

\mathbb{P}\Big(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\Big)\leq\frac{C}{S}.

The proofs of these lemmas rely on the following auxiliary lemma on fringe tree sizes, complementing Lemma
2.3
. The lemma assumes

, as

deterministically.

Lemma 2.9

(Fringe tree: upper tail)
.

For any

,

, and any

,

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k\right)\leq 2e^{2}\cdot\frac{v}{n}\cdot\exp\left(-k\frac{v}{n}\right).

(2.14)

Moreover, for any
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq t\frac{n}{v}\right)\leq 2e^{2}\exp(-t),

(2.15)

and for all

,

, and

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)>t\frac{n}{v}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-8t\right).

(2.16)

Furthermore, there exists a constant

such that for all

,

(2.18)

. We finish this section with a proof of the lower bound in Lemma

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

The statement of the lemma is clear for
S>\frac{v}{16}
or for
v=1
, by taking the constant
C
large enough. Thus, we can assume that
S\leq\frac{v}{16}
for the rest of the proof.
Fix some
n\geq 1
and
S\geq 1
. We first relate the Jordan-2 centrality to the product of the fringe-tree size of
v
and its parent, that is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\leq\frac{n}{2}\right\}\right).

If
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\leq n/2
, then
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq n/2
and hence
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}_{n}(v)=\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\Big)-\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))>\frac{n}{2}\Big).

By (
2.18
), there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
v\geq 2
, and
n\geq 3
, the probability of the event
\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))>n/2\}
is at most
C/v
. So,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\Big)-C/v.

(2.19)

In the remainder, we establish a lower bound on the probability on the right-hand side of this inequality. We intersect with the likely events that both
v
and its parent have a fringe-tree size of the right order. That is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{n}{4v}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq\frac{n}{4\mathrm{pa}(v)}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\frac{n^{2}}{16v\mathrm{pa}(v)}\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{n}{4v}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq\frac{n}{4\mathrm{pa}(v)}\right\}\right).

The event
\left\{\tfrac{n^{2}}{16v\mathrm{pa}(v)}\geq S\left(n/v\right)^{2}\right\}
is satisfied for
\mathrm{pa}(v)\leq v/(16S)
.
Using that
\mathrm{pa}(v)
is uniformly distributed on
[v-1]
and is independent from
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
, the law of total probability yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{v-1}\sum_{u\leq v/(16S)}\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{n}{4v}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\frac{n}{4u}\right\}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=u\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\tfrac{n}{4v}\right)}{v-1}\sum_{u\leq v/(16S)}\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\frac{n}{4u}\right\}\Big|\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{n}{4v}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathrm{pa}(v)=u\right\}\right),

where the last inequality holds since
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
and
\mathrm{pa}(v)
are independent.
Furthermore, the random variable

\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\mid\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\tfrac{n}{4v}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathrm{pa}(v)=u\right\}
stochastically dominates
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)
, so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\tfrac{n}{4v}\right)}{v-1}\sum_{u\leq v/(16S)}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\frac{n}{4u}\right).

Using Lemma

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{n}{4v}\right)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
and
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\tfrac{n}{4u}\right)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
, so

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq S\left(\tfrac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)\geq\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}}{v-1}\lfloor v/(16S)\rfloor\frac{1}{2}\geq\frac{1}{128S},

where we used the assumption
S\leq\frac{v}{16}
for the last inequality. Inserting this into (
2.19
) concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Many vertices with large Jordan-2 centrality is unlikely

We now proceed to controlling the
global
events to bridge the Jordan-2 centrality to the Jordan-2 ordering. We start with the upper bound on the upper tail, bounding the second event in (
2.1
) in the outline: the event that many vertices have unusually large Jordan-2 centrality.

By the upper bound
\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(u)\leq\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\cdot\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(u))
, see (
1.9
), it is enough to bound the number of vertices for which this product exceeds a threshold of order
(n/t)^{2}
. The following lemma is the main result of this subsection.

Lemma 2.10
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\big|\big\{v\in[n]\setminus{1}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}\left(\mathrm{pa}(v)\right)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)>(n/t)^{2}\big\}\big|>Ct\right)\leq\exp\left(-t^{1/4}\right).

(2.20)

Unlike the analysis of the lower tail of the Jordan-2 centrality of a fixed vertex, Lemma
2.10
requires understanding of global counts of vertices whose subtree sizes lie in prescribed ranges, together with information about the fringe-tree sizes of their children. Counting these is difficult in the arrival-order description of
T_{n}
given in Section
1.1
.

To overcome this, we use an equivalent sampling of the random recursive tree on the Ulam–Harris embedding. To motivate this construction, we first state a construction close to the arrival-order model and record its properties. We then proceed to the fringe-splitting construction, which provides the conditional independence needed for our counting arguments. Both constructions are well-known, see for example
[
1
, Section 2.1]
or
[
17
,
4
,
23
]
and references therein. Afterwards, we prove two auxiliary lemmas that also rely on the fringe-splitting construction, and eventually prove Lemma
2.10
.

To start the constructions, we first define the
Ulam–Harris
(UH) description of a tree. Consider the set of vertices

\mathcal{U}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{U}_{j}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{N}^{j},\qquad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{U}_{0}=\{\emptyset\},

(2.21)

whose elements we call Ulam–Harris nodes. The root is the empty sequence
\emptyset
, and each node
u\in\mathcal{U}
has ordered child slots
\{(u,i)\}_{i\geq 1}
. A node is said to be occupied once a vertex of the growing tree has been placed at that location. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we also say that
u
is the parent of
(u,k)
and that
\left((u,i)\right)_{i\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}}
are the left siblings of
(u,k)
.
Both constructions produce a UH tree with additional node labels for the arrival times. In the first construction, labels are assigned dynamically as the tree grows; in the second, they are drawn only after the underlying tree on
n
vertices has been generated. In both cases, the labeled unordered tree obtained by forgetting the Ulam–Harris ordering has the same law as the random recursive tree.

Construction 1: Ulam–Harris embedding.

In the first construction, we embed the recursive tree into the Ulam–Harris tree respecting the Ulam–Harris left-to-right order.
The process starts with the root
\emptyset
, labeled
1
.
At each step
t\geq 2
, a new vertex arrives and chooses one of the currently occupied nodes uniformly at random as its parent.
If the chosen parent is the Ulam–Harris node
u
, the new vertex is placed at its first available child slot
(u,k)
, where
k
is the smallest index such that
(u,k)
is not yet occupied.
The node is then labeled with the arrival time
t
and marked as occupied.

This construction yields a labeled ordered tree
A_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
with
|A_{n}|=n
, where labels increase along ancestral lines, and among siblings the label order coincides with their lexicographic order in the Ulam–Harris tree. For each occupied node
u\in A_{n}
, we write

\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u):=\big|\{v\in A_{n}:v\text{ has $u$ as an ancestor in $\mathcal{U}$}\}\big|

for the fringe-tree size of
u\in\mathcal{U}
(so we abuse notation slightly, as
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}
can act both on
\mathcal{U}
and on
V_{n}=[n]
).
We record three properties:

1.

Forgetting the Ulam–Harris labels recovers the random recursive tree defined above, in which each vertex
t
attaches uniformly to a vertex in
[t-1]
.

2.

Conditional on the occupied set
A_{n}=a_{n}
(without arrival time labels), each admissible labeling – labels increase along simple root-to-leaf paths, and among siblings, the label order is consistent with the left-to-right order in the Ulam–Harris tree – is equally likely. Indeed, the UH-embedding is uniquely determined by the sequence of parent choices
(\mathrm{pa}(v))_{v\in[2,n]}
, and each such sequence has probability
\prod_{v=2}^{n}1/(v-1)
. Thus all sequences resulting in the occupied set
A_{n}
are equally likely.

3.

Conditional on any realization of the tree such that the fringe tree sizes of
u
and
(u,1),\ldots,(u,k)
equal
m
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
, respectively, the size of the fringe tree of
(u,k+1)
is uniformly distributed on
\{1,\ldots,m-(1+m_{1}+\ldots+m_{k})\}
if
1+m_{1}+\ldots+m_{k}<m
, and equals
0
otherwise. This follows from a standard Pólya-urn argument that tracks the evolution of the subtree sizes.

Observations 2 and 3 motivate the next construction.

Construction 2: Fringe-size splitting.

An equivalent way to generate the tree is to work directly with fringe-tree sizes, rather than attaching vertices one by one. We start with the root
\emptyset
, whose fringe tree contains all
n
vertices. The remaining
n-1
vertices are allocated recursively along the Ulam–Harris tree by successive uniform splits.

Given a node
u
with
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)=m>1
, we reveal the fringe tree sizes of its children from left to right. Set
R_{1}=m-1
; for its leftmost child
(u,1)
, let
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}\left((u,1)\right)=X_{1}\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\ldots,R_{1}\}
. For
k\geq 1
, work conditionally on
R_{k}
and
X_{k}
for
k\geq 1
, and continue while
R_{k+1}\geq 1
:

R_{k+1}=R_{k}-X_{k},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{Fr}_{n}\left((u,k+1)\right)=X_{k+1}\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\ldots,R_{k+1}\}.

(2.22)

This construction provides the occupied set
A_{n}
in the Ulam–Harris tree, but does not assign arrival-time labels. Conditional on the occupied set, we let the arrival-time labeling be uniform among all admissible labelings, matching Construction 1. By Observations 2 and 3 above, the resulting tree has the same distribution as the uniform random recursive tree (omitting the Ulam–Harris ordering).

The subtree sizes can be sampled in any admissible order—that is, the fringe-tree size of each vertex
(u,k)
in the Ulam–Harris tree is sampled after its parent
u
and left siblings,
\left((u,i)\right)_{i\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}}
, have been assigned fringe-tree sizes. The recursive allocation may therefore be carried out in depth-first, breadth-first, or any other admissible order without changing the distribution.

Auxiliary lemmas.
We use the independence in this second construction to prove two auxiliary lemmas on the fringe-tree-size distribution, which then leads to the proof of Lemma
2.10
. We introduce notation for the number of vertices with fringe-tree size in an interval

(2.23)

Informally, the size of the fringe-tree of vertex

; thus the number of vertices with fringe-tree size in

should be of order

We also write

for the set of occupied nodes that are not the root.

Lemma 2.11

(Fringe-tree size counts)
.

Let
\ell,n\in\mathbb{N}
, and assume

. Then,

\mathbb{P}\left(N_{n}^{\mathrm{Fr}}(\ell,2\ell]\geq r\right)\leq 27e^{-0.04r}.

Proof.

The statement is clear for
\ell\geq n

N_{n}^{\mathrm{Fr}}(\ell,2\ell]=0
for
\ell\geq n

\ell<n
for the rest of the proof.
We work under the fringe-tree splitting construction. Recall that
A_{n}^{\prime}
denotes the set of occupied Ulam–Harris nodes in the tree of size
n
that are not the root.
Let

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle:=\big\{(u,i)\in A_{n}^{\prime}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}\left((u,i)\right)\in(\ell,2\ell]\big\},

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle:=\big\{(u,i)\in A_{n}^{\prime}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\ell+1,\ \mathrm{Fr}_{n}\left((u,i)\right)\in[\ell/2,\ell]\big\}.

The lemma aims to establish an upper bound on the size of the set
B_{1}
. The set
B_{2}
will assist with this. Note that
B_{1}\cup B_{2}
is the set of nodes that are not the root, whose fringe size lies in
(\ell/2,2\ell]

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(N_{n}^{\mathrm{Fr}}(\ell,2\ell]\geq r\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=r-1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|=t,|B_{1}|\geq r-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=r-1}^{\infty}\big(\mathbb{P}\left(|B_{1}|\geq 4t/5,|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|=t\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(|B_{2}|>t/5,|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|=t\right)\big).

The fringe trees attached to vertices in

are disjoint, as a node with fringe-tree size at most
\ell

\ell

|B_{2}|\cdot\ell/2\leq n
, which is equivalent to
|B_{2}|\leq 2n/\ell
. As

. Thus,

\mathbb{P}\left(N_{n}^{\mathrm{Fr}}(\ell,2\ell]\geq r\right)\leq\sum_{t=r-1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(|B_{1}|\geq 4t/5,|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|=t\right).

For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\{u^{i}_{1},\ldots,u^{i}_{n^{i}}\}=[n]^{i}
in lexicographic ordering and let
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{K}\}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}[n]^{i}
with
K=\sum_{i=1}^{n}n^{i}
, so that the level of
v_{j}
, i.e., the unique value
i\in[n]
s.t.
v_{j}\in[n]^{i}
, is non-decreasing in
j
, and so that each block
[n]^{i}
is in the lexicographic ordering.
The sets
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
can be constructed iteratively via the fringe-tree splitting construction, by sampling fringe-tree sizes of the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{K}
. Let
B_{1}^{j}
and
B_{2}^{j}
be the sets

\displaystyle B_{1}(j)

\displaystyle:=\big\{(u,i)\in\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j}\}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}\left((u,i)\right)\in(\ell,2\ell]\big\},

\displaystyle B_{2}(j)

\displaystyle:=\big\{(u,i)\in\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j}\}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\ell+1,\ \mathrm{Fr}_{n}\left((u,i)\right)\in[\ell/2,\ell]\big\}

Let

and suppose that we reveal the uniform random variable
X_{i}=\mathrm{Fr}_{n}((u,i))
in (
2.22
) determining the fringe-tree size of
(u,i)
in two stages: first we reveal whether

with probability at least

with a sequence of i.i.d. Bernoulli(1/3)-distributed random variables

so that

|B_{2}(j)|\geq\sum_{\ell=1}^{|B_{1}(j)|+|B_{2}(j)|}Y_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=r-1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(|B_{2}|\leq\tfrac{1}{5}t,|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|=t\right)

where

(2.24)

is the large-deviation rate function of a Bernoulli-
p

∎

The next lemma bounds the number of children with a large fringe tree relative to their parent.

Lemma 2.12

(Children with large fringe trees)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N},u\in\mathcal{U}
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then, for all
k\geq 3\log_{2}(1/\alpha)
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

\mathbb{P}\left(\big|\{i\in\mathbb{N}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}\left((u,i)\right)\geq\alpha\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\}\big|\,>\,k\,\mid\,\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)=\ell\right)\ \leq\ 0.95^{k}.

Proof.

We analyze the splitting as in (
2.22
).
We track for each child of
u
if its fringe-tree size exceeds slightly less than its expectation: let

Y_{j}=\mathbbm{1}{\{X_{j}\geq R_{j}/2\}},\qquad Y^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}Y_{j}.

Observe that
\mathbb{P}(Y_{j}=1\mid Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{j-1})\geq 1/2
as
X_{j}
is uniform on
\{1,\ldots,R_{j}\}
. Moreover, each time
Y_{j}=1
, the remaining mass halves, i.e.,
R_{j+1}\leq R_{j}/2
. Hence, after
m
halvings the remaining mass is at most
R_{1}2^{-m}
. In particular, if the number of halvings among the first
k
children exceeds
\log_{2}(1/\alpha)
, then the remaining mass before child
k+1
is less than
\alpha\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)
, and it is impossible to have a
(k+1)
-st child of size at least
\alpha\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)
, i.e.,
R_{k+1}<\alpha\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)
.
So, if there are at least
k+1
children of
u
with fringe-tree size at least
\alpha\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)
, we must have that
Y^{(k)}\leq\log_{2}(1/\alpha)
. Since
Y^{(k)}
stochastically dominates a binomial random variable with
p=1/2
, we find for
k\geq 3\log_{2}(1/\alpha)
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\big|\{i\in\mathbb{N}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}\left((u,i)\right)\geq\alpha\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\}\big|>k\,\mid\,\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)=\ell\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\big(Y^{(k)}\leq\log_{2}(1/\alpha)\big)\\
\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Bin}(k,1/2)\leq k/3\right)\leq\exp\left(-k\Lambda_{1/2}(\tfrac{1}{3})\right)\leq 0.95^{k},

where
\Lambda_{1/2}
is the large deviations rate function of a Bernoulli(1/2)-distribution, see (
2.24
). ∎

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\big|\big\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(u)\geq\phi_{n}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}}(v)\big\}\big|\geq Sv\right).

is unlikely small, or there are many vertices with large Jordan-2 centrality. Let

be the constant
C
from Lemma
2.10
. We bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\leq\left(\frac{2C_{1}n}{Sv}\right)^{2}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\Big\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(u)>\left(\frac{2C_{1}n}{Sv}\right)^{2}\Big\}\Big|>Sv\right).

We use that
\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\leq\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))
by (

) for
v\geq 2
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\leq\left(\frac{2C_{1}n}{Sv}\right)^{2}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\Big\{u\in[n]\setminus\{1\}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(u))>\left(\frac{2C_{1}n}{Sv}\right)^{2}\Big\}\Big|>Sv-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\leq\left(\frac{2C_{1}n}{Sv}\right)^{2}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\Big\{u\in[n]\setminus\{1\}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(u))>\left(\frac{2C_{1}n}{Sv}\right)^{2}\Big\}\Big|>C_{1}\frac{Sv}{2C_{1}}\right)

where the last inequality holds for

.
Applying inequality (

) from Lemma
2.1
with

to the first term and Lemma
2.10
with

to the second term, we get that for

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\right)\leq C_{2}\cdot 4C_{1}^{2}\frac{1+\log\left(S/(2C_{1})\right)}{S^{2}}+\exp\left(-(2C_{1})^{-1/4}S^{1/4}\right),

is the constant

from Lemma

.
As a result, there exists a constant

such that for all

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\geq Sv\right)\leq C\frac{1+\log S}{S^{2}}.

Increasing the constant

also implies that the same inequality holds for all

.
∎

We proceed with the analysis of the lower bound of the upper tail, i.e., the first inequality in (

For every

Lemma 2.16

\displaystyle:\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(u)>\Big(\frac{n}{64t}\Big)^{2}\Big\}\Big|\leq t\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\left\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)>\frac{n}{64t}\right\}\Big|\leq t\right)\leq C\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{8}\Big).

As

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\hskip 34.14322pt+\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\left\{u\in\left\{8t,\ldots,16t\right\}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)>\frac{n}{64t}\right\}\Big|\leq t\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\hskip 34.14322pt+\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\left\{u\in\left\{8t,\ldots,16t\right\}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{>16t}(u)>\frac{n}{64t}\right\}\Big|\leq t\right),

We turn our attention to the lower tail, which analyses the probability that the Jordan-2 ordering underestimates the arrival time of a vertex. As for the upper tail, we split the event

Lemma 2.17

(Upper bound)
.

For the Jordan-
2
ordering
\widehat{\sigma}_{2}

C
such that for any

We give two (slightly) different proofs, depending whether

We start the proof for the case where

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\widehat{\sigma}_{2}(v)\leq\tfrac{1}{S}v\Big)

2.16
for

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq\frac{n}{64Sv}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)>\frac{n}{64Sv}\right\}\right|>Sv\right\}.

On the event
\mathcal{A}
, the fringe-tree size of
v
is smaller than
n/2
, so
\phi_{n}(v)=\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq\frac{n}{64Sv}
. Moreover, on the event
\mathcal{B}
, there are at least
Sv
vertices
u
with
\phi_{n}(u)>\frac{n}{64Sv}
. Thus, on the event
\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}
one has that

\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)>\phi_{n}(v)\right\}\right|\,>\,Sv,

which implies that

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\right)\ \geq\ \mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}\right)\ \geq\ \mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{B}^{c}\right).

To bound
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})
, we apply the lower bound on the lower tail of the fringe-tree size in (
2.8
) in Lemma
2.3
(3), which requires
S\leq n/(64v)
, which holds by our assumptions on
S
. We obtain

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq\frac{n}{64Sv}\right)\geq\frac{1}{8\cdot 64S}=\frac{1}{512S}.

In order to bound
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}^{c})
from above, we use Lemma
2.16
and get that for
S\leq\frac{n}{16v}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{B}^{c}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)>\frac{n}{64Sv}\right\}\right|\leq Sv\right)\leq C\exp(-Sv/8)\leq C\exp(-S/8),

where
C
is the constant from inequality (
2.32
).
Thus, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{B}^{c}\right)\geq\frac{1}{512S}-Ce^{-S/8}\geq\frac{c_{\star}}{S},

for some constant
c_{\star}>0
, and all
S
large enough, say
S\geq S_{0}>1
. It remains to prove the result for
1\leq S\leq S_{0}
. Let
\delta=\tfrac{1}{(S_{0}+1)64}
. For
v>\delta n
, there is nothing to show since
\delta\tfrac{n}{v}<1
and we only consider
1\leq S\leq\delta\tfrac{n}{v}
. If
\delta\tfrac{n}{v}\geq 1
(and thus
S_{0}\leq\tfrac{n}{64v})
, then for
v\in\{2,\ldots,\lfloor\delta n\rfloor\}
, and
1\leq S\leq S_{0}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq S_{0}v\right)\geq\frac{c_{\star}}{S_{0}}\geq\frac{c_{\star}/S_{0}}{S},

showing inequality (
3.1
) with
c=c_{\star}/S_{0}
and
\delta=\tfrac{1}{(S_{0}+1)64}
, for all
v\in\{2,\ldots,\lfloor\delta n\rfloor\}
. For
v=1
, observe that the vertices
1
and
2
are indistinguishable for any label-invariant estimator, so that for
1\leq S\leq\delta\frac{n}{v}
we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(1)\geq S\cdot 1\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(2)\geq S/2\cdot 2\right)\geq\frac{c}{S/2}\geq\frac{c}{S},

where we applied inequality (
3.1
), which was previously established for
v=2
.
∎

We proceed to an upper bound on the upper tail for the Jordan ordering, for which we employ a similar strategy as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, which gave an upper bound on the upper tail for the Jordan-2 ordering.

Lemma 3.2

(Jordan ordering: upper bound for the upper tail)
.

For the Jordan ordering
\widehat{\sigma}_{J}
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
n\geq 1
, any vertex
v\in[n]
, and any
S\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\right)\leq\frac{C}{S}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Fix some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
v\in[n]
and
S\geq 1
. Since
\widehat{\sigma}_{J}
is a permutation of
[n]
, if
S>n/v
then the probability in (
3.2
) equals 0. Further, it suffices to consider
S
large enough and
v\leq\frac{n}{2}
, by taking the constant
C
in (
3.2
) large enough.

We first assume
v\geq 2
. Note that if
\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv
then at least
Sv
vertices have Jordan centrality at least
\phi_{n}(v)
. Thus, for some
M=M(S,v)>0
, we decompose

\displaystyle\{\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\Big\{\big|\big\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)\geq\phi_{n}(v)\big\}\big|\geq Sv\Big\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\phi_{n}(v)\leq\frac{n}{Mv}\right\}\cup\left\{\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)\geq\frac{n}{Mv}\right\}\right|\geq Sv\right\}.

By Lemma
2.4
,
\phi_{n}(v)\geq\min(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{>v}(v),\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{>v}(1))
, which we use for the first event in the union. Further, by the exchangeability in Lemma
2.3
(1),
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{>v}(v)
and
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{>v}(1)
have the same distribution as
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
.
For the second event we use the upper bound
\phi_{n}(u)\leq\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)
, see (
1.8
).
Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\phi_{n}(v)\leq\frac{n}{Mv}\Big)+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)\geq\frac{n}{Mv}\right\}\right|\geq Sv\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ 2\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq\frac{n}{Mv}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\frac{n}{Mv}\right\}\right|\geq Sv\right).

Inequality (
2.7
) from Lemma
2.3
(3) (which actually holds for all
M>0
) provides an upper bound for the first term, implying that

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\right)\leq\frac{4}{M}+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\frac{n}{Mv}\right\}\right|\geq Sv\right).

We bound the remaining term using Corollary
2.13
. Let
C
be the constant from Corollary
2.13
, let
\widetilde{C}>C
, and let
M\coloneqq\frac{S}{C}
. Then Corollary
2.13
implies that

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\geq Sv\right)\leq\frac{4}{S/\widetilde{C}}+\mathbb{P}\bigg(\Big|\Big\{u\in[n]:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\frac{n}{Sv/\widetilde{C}}\Big\}\Big|\geq\widetilde{C}\frac{Sv}{\widetilde{C}}\bigg)\\
\leq\frac{4\widetilde{C}}{S}+\exp\left(-\widetilde{C}^{-1/4}\left(Sv\right)^{1/4}\right)\leq\frac{8\widetilde{C}}{S},

where the last inequality holds for
S
large enough. To prove (
3.2
) for
v=1
, note that vertices
1
and
2
are indistinguishable for any label-invariant estimator. Thus for
S
large enough,

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(1)\geq S\cdot 1\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(2)\geq(S/2)\cdot 2\right)\leq\frac{8\widetilde{C}}{S/2}=\frac{16\widetilde{C}}{S},

so that the result (
3.2
) follows by taking the constant
C
in (
3.2
) large enough.
∎

Lower tail

In this section, we provide upper and lower bounds on
\mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq v/S\big)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For the Jordan ordering
\widehat{\sigma}_{J}
, there exist positive constants
C
and
c
such that for any
n\geq 1
, any vertex
v\in[n]
and any
S\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq\frac{v}{S}\right)\leq C\left(e^{-cv/S}+e^{-S/64}\right).

(3.3)

Proof.

The statement is clear for
4v\leq S
, since the relevant probability equals
0
. Further, it suffices to consider the case when
v/S\in\mathbb{N}
, which we will assume for the rest of the proof. Lastly, the statement is clear for
S\in[1,32]
by taking
C
large enough.
So let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
v\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
4v>S\geq 32
. First, note that if
\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq v/S
then no more than
v/S
vertices have Jordan centrality exceeding
\phi_{n}(v)
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq\frac{1}{S}v\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\big|\big\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)>\phi_{n}(v)\big\}\big|\leq\frac{v}{S}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\{u:\phi_{n}(u)>S\frac{n}{64v}\right\}\right|\leq\frac{v}{S}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}(v)\geq S\frac{n}{64v}\right).

The first summand can be bounded using Lemma
2.16
for
t=v/S
, which applies as
v/S\leq n/16
because
S\geq 32
. For the second summand we use that
\phi_{n}(v)\leq\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
, so that

\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}(v)\geq S\frac{n}{64v}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{S}{64}\frac{n}{v}\right)\leq C^{\prime}\exp\left(-\frac{S}{64}\right)

with
C^{\prime}=2e^{2}
, by inequality (
2.15
). Thus, we get that

\mathbb{P}\Big(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq\frac{1}{S}v\Big)\leq C\exp\Big(-\frac{v}{8S}\Big)+C^{\prime}\exp\Big(-\frac{S}{64}\Big).\qed

Lemma 3.4
.

For the Jordan ordering
\widehat{\sigma}_{J}
, there exist positive constants
C,c^{\prime}
and
c
such that for any
n\geq 1
, any vertex
v\in[n/2]
and any
S\in[1,c^{\prime}v]

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq\frac{v}{S}\right)\geq ce^{-CS}.

(3.4)

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case where
v/S\in\mathbb{N}
, which we will assume for the rest of the proof. Moreover, we assume that
v\geq 3
, as the range for
S
can be made empty for
v\in\{1,2\}
by choosing
c^{\prime}
small.
We start with the case where
S\leq\sqrt[10]{v}
.
Let
M\geq 1
be a constant such that for all
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\big|\big\{v\in[n]:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)>n/t\big\}\big|>Mt\right)\leq\exp\left(1-t^{1/4}\right).

(3.5)

Such a constant exists by Corollary
2.13
.
The most likely way to underestimate the arrival time of
v
is when its fringe tree is large. Similar to the proof of Lemma
2.19
, we bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq\frac{v}{S}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\big|\big\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)\geq\phi_{n}(v)\big\}\big|\leq\frac{v}{S}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\Big\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\in\left[MS\frac{n}{v},\frac{n}{2}\right]\Big\}\cap\Big\{\big|\big\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)\geq MS\frac{n}{v}\big\}\big|\leq v/S\Big\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq MS\frac{n}{v}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq n/2\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\mathbb{P}\left(\big|\big\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)\geq MS\frac{n}{v}\big\}\big|>v/S\right).

(3.7)

To bound the probabilities in (
3.6
), we employ (
2.16
) and (
2.15
) from Lemma
2.9
, which imply that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq MS\frac{n}{v}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-8MS\right),\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq n/2\right)\leq 2e^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{v}{2}\right).

Further, the remaining probability on (
3.7
) is bounded from above by (
3.5
) (see also Corollary
2.13
), using additionally that
\phi_{n}(v)\leq\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
:

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)\geq MS\frac{n}{v}\right\}\right|>\frac{v}{S}\right)\\
\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\frac{n}{v/(MS)}\right\}\right|>M\frac{v}{MS}\right)\leq\exp\left(1-\left(\frac{v}{MS}\right)^{1/4}\right).

Combining the above inequalities, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq\frac{v}{S}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-8MS\right)-2e^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{v}{2}\right)-\exp\left(1-\left(\frac{v}{MS}\right)^{1/4}\right).

Since we assumed that
S\leq\sqrt[10]{v}
, we see that there exist positive constants

so that

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq\frac{v}{S}\right)\geq ce^{-CS}.

for any
1\leq S\leq c^{\prime}v\wedge\sqrt[10]{v}
.

Next, we consider the case where
\tfrac{v}{2}\geq S>\sqrt[10]{v}
. We intersect with the event that
\mathrm{pa}(v)=1
and also condition on the size of the fringe tree of
v
. We first prove for each
k\in[2RSn/v,n/2]
, where
R
is a large constant, the inclusion

\Big\{\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq\frac{v}{S}\Big\}\,\supseteq\,\left\{\mathrm{pa}(v)=1,\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k\right\}\cap\left\{\left|\left\{u\notin(T_{n},1)_{v\downarrow}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq k\right\}\right|\leq\frac{Rn}{k}\right\}.

(3.8)

We start from the right-hand side.
If the fringe tree of
v
has size equal to
k
, then all its children have fringe tree size strictly smaller than
k
, allowing to change the counted set in the last event to include all

without changing the event. Since for

\displaystyle\left\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k,\left|\left\{u\notin(T_{n},1)_{v\downarrow}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq k\right\}\right|\leq\frac{Rn}{k}\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\subseteq\left\{\phi_{n}(v)=k,\left|\left\{u\in[n],\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq\phi_{n}(v)\right\}\right|\leq\frac{Rn}{k}+1\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\subseteq\left\{\phi_{n}(v)=k,\left|\left\{u\in[n]:\phi_{n}(u)\geq\phi_{n}(v)\right\}\right|\leq\frac{Rn}{k}+1\right\},

for

. Since the events on the right-hand side in (

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq v/S\big)\geq\sum_{k=\lceil 2RSn/v\rceil}^{n/2}\bigg(

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\left|\left\{u\notin(T_{n},1)_{v\downarrow}:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq k\right\}\right|\leq\frac{Rn}{k}\,\Big|\,\mathrm{pa}(v)=1,\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\cdot\mathbb{P}\big(\mathrm{pa}(v)=1\big)\cdot\mathbb{P}\big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k\big)\bigg),

(3.9)

using that the size of the fringe tree of
v
and the parent of
v
are independent. Conditioned on the event
\left\{\mathrm{pa}(v)=1,\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k\right\}
, the tree

is distributed like a uniform attachment tree of size

. By Markov’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\Big\{u\notin(T_{n},1)_{v\downarrow}

\displaystyle:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq k\Big\}\Big|\leq\frac{Rn}{k}\,\Big|\,\mathrm{pa}(v)=1,\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k\Big)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\Big(\left|\left\{u\in[n-k]:\mathrm{Fr}_{n-k}(u)\geq k\right\}\right|>\frac{Rn}{k}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{k}{Rn}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\{u\in[n-k]:\mathrm{Fr}_{n-k}(u)\geq k\right\}\right|\right]

\displaystyle=1-\frac{k}{Rn}\sum_{u\in[n-k]}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n-k}(u)\geq k\Big)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{k}{Rn}\sum_{u\in[n]}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)\geq k\Big).

By (

there exists an absolute constant

such that the last sum in the above display is at most

. Fixing

\mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{J}(v)\leq v/S\big)\geq\sum_{k=\lceil 2RSn/v\rceil}^{n/2}\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{v-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k\right)=\frac{1}{2(v-1)}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\in\big[2RSn/v,n/2\big]\Big).

) in Lemma

Lower bounds on the performance of arbitrary estimators

This section proves Proposition
1.2
, providing lower bounds on

\mathbb{P}\left(|\widehat{\sigma}(v,T_{n})-v|\geq Sv\right)

for arbitrary label-invariant arrival-time estimators
\widehat{\sigma}
. We include
T_{n}
as an argument of
\widehat{\sigma}
to stress that
\widehat{\sigma}(\,\cdot\,,T_{n})
is a random permutation whose distribution depends on the realization of the random tree
T_{n}
.

The proof rests on isolating, for each vertex
v
, a subset
I_{v}(T_{n})
of vertices that are indistinguishable from
v
given the information contained in the tree. Every label-invariant estimator has to assign to half of these vertices an estimated arrival time that is at a distance of at least
|I_{v}(T_{n})|/4
from
v
, and with probability
1/4
,
v
is among those vertices. Our lower bound will follow by bounding the size of the set
I_{v}(T_{n})
from below. Next, we formalize this reasoning, starting with a definition of
I_{v}
.

Let
(\mathrm{t}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a deterministic sequence of recursive trees (i.e., with labels increasing along every non-backtracking path started at the root) such that for all
n\geq 1
,
\mathrm{t}_{n}
is the subtree of
\mathrm{t}_{n+1}
induced on the vertices with labels in
[n]
.
Recall that
\mathrm{pa}(i)
is the parent of vertex
i
, fix some
v\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
and define the set

I_{v}(\mathrm{t}_{n}):=\big\{i\in[v/2,v]\ :\ i\text{ is a leaf in }\mathrm{t}_{v},\ \mathrm{pa}(i)<v/2\big\}.

(4.1)

In words,
I_{v}(\mathrm{t}_{n})
is the set of all vertices in
\mathrm{t}_{n}
which arrived between time
v/2
and
v
, that are still leaves at time
v
, and which are children of vertices that arrived before time
v/2
. The set
I_{v}(\mathrm{t}_{n})
satisfies
I_{v}(\mathrm{t}_{n})=I_{v}(\mathrm{t}_{v})
for all
n\geq v
, and contains
v
if and only if
\mathrm{pa}(v)<v/2
. The key observation is that if
I_{v}(\mathrm{t}_{n})=\mathcal{I}
, then any permutation of labels in
\mathcal{I}
yields a relabeled tree
\mathrm{t}_{n}^{\tau}
that is also recursive (here we slightly abuse notation and extend
\tau
to act as the identity on
\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\mathcal{I}
).
An illustration is given in Figure
6
and the proof of this fact is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any recursive tree
\mathrm{t}
of size at least
v
and any permutation
\tau
of
I_{v}(\mathrm{t})
, the tree
\mathrm{t}^{\tau}
is recursive. Moreover,
I_{v}(\mathrm{t})=I_{v}(\mathrm{t}^{\tau})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{t}
be a recursive tree of size
n\geq v
. The proof of the lemma is trivial if
I_{v}(\mathrm{t})
has only one element. Suppose
|I_{v}(\mathrm{t})|\geq 2
and let
i,j
be two distinct vertices in
I_{v}(\mathrm{t})
. Since every permutation on
I_{v}(\mathrm{t})
can be written as a product of transpositions on
I_{v}(\mathrm{t})
, it suffices to prove the claim for transpositions. Let
\tau=(i,j)
. Every simple path from the root to a leaf that does not pass through
i
or
j
remains increasing in
\mathrm{t}^{\tau}
. Consider the two simple paths
\pi^{\scriptscriptstyle{({i}})}
and
\pi^{\scriptscriptstyle{({j}})}
from vertex
1
passing through
i
and
j
, respectively, and ending at a leaf of
\mathrm{t}
. We have to show that the labels along these paths remain increasing if we swap
i
and
j
and that
i
and
j
belong to
I_{v}(\mathrm{t}^{\tau})
.

Because
\mathrm{t}
is recursive, these paths are of the form
\pi^{\scriptscriptstyle{({i}})}=(1,i_{1},\dots,i_{k},i,i_{k+1},\dots,i_{K})
and
\pi^{\scriptscriptstyle{({j}})}=(1,j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell},j,j_{\ell+1},\dots,j_{L})
with increasing labels along both paths.
By definition of
I_{v}(\mathrm{t})
, we have that
i_{k}<v/2\leq i\leq v<i_{k+1}
and
j_{\ell}<v/2\leq j\leq v<j_{\ell+1}
.
As a consequence, the two simple paths in
\mathrm{t}^{\tau}
starting at
1
, going through
i
and
j
, respectively, and finishing at the same leaves become
(1,i_{1},\dots,i_{k},j,i_{k+1},\dots,i_{K})
and
(1,j_{1},\dots,j_{\ell},i,j_{\ell+1},\dots,j_{L})
. Hence these paths remain increasing and both
i
and
j
belong to
I_{v}(\mathrm{t}^{\tau})
. Thus
\mathrm{t}^{\tau}
is recursive and
I_{v}(\mathrm{t})=I_{v}(\mathrm{t}^{\tau})
.
∎

See

Proof.

For the first item, we observe that the fringe trees witnessed after time
m
are all disjoint.
Their evolution after time
m
is modeled by a Pólya urn
[
16
]
with
m
colors, starting at time
m
with one ball of each color.
At each time
n>m
, a ball is picked uniformly at random from the urn, and the picked ball is replaced by two balls of the same color as the picked ball.
The number of balls of color
i\in[m]
at time
n
describes the witnessed fringe tree size of a vertex: indeed, each arriving vertex joins a fringe tree with probability proportional to the size of the tree.

The number of balls of each color at time
n\geq m
is an exchangeable family of random variables, yielding point 1.
The family follows a Dirichlet-multinomial distribution, implying that the witnessed fringe-tree sizes are negatively associated, proving the second item.

For the third item of Lemma
2.3
, we use the exchangeability, and observe that
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{>m}(m)=\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)
. The size of a single fringe tree evolves according to a beta-binomial random variable plus one. That is,

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=k\right)=(m-1)\frac{(n-m)!}{(n-1)!}\frac{(n-k-1)!}{(n-m-k+1)!},\quad k\in\{1,\ldots,n-m\}.

(A.1)

We start with the upper bound on the lower tail, i.e., inequality (
2.7
). When
m=1
,
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(1)=n
and the bound is trivial. So we can assume that
n\geq m\geq 2
for the rest of the proof of (
2.7
).
Observe that for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-m+1\}
,

\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=k+1\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=k\right)}=\frac{(n-k-2)!}{(n-m-k)!}\frac{(n-m-k+1)!}{(n-k-1)!}=\frac{n-m-k+1}{n-k-1}=1-\frac{m-2}{n-k-1}\leq 1.

So the function
k\mapsto\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k)
is non-increasing for
m\geq 2
. Hence,

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)\leq\frac{n}{Mm}\right)\leq\frac{n}{Mm}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=1\right)=\frac{n}{Mm}(m-1)\frac{(n-m)!}{(n-1)!}\frac{(n-2)!}{(n-m)!}=\frac{n}{Mm}\frac{m-1}{n-1}.

The right-hand side is at most
2/M
, since
n\geq 2
.
We proceed to the lower bound on the lower tail, inequality (
2.8
), assuming
n\geq m\geq 2
,
m\leq n/M
, and
M\geq 4
. Set

k=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{Mm}\right\rfloor,\qquad\text{and observe}\qquad m-2\leq\frac{n}{4}\leq\frac{n}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{Mm}\right)\leq\frac{n-k}{2}.

(A.2)

Since the function
j\mapsto\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=j)
is non-increasing for
m\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)\leq k\big)\ \geq\ k\cdot\mathbb{P}\big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=k\big)

\displaystyle=\ k\cdot\mathbb{P}\big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=1\big)\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=j+1\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=j\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{Mm}\right\rfloor\frac{m-1}{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{m-2}{n-j-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4M}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{m-2}{n-j-1}\right),

where we used the formula for
\tfrac{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=j+1\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(m)=j\right)}
proven above.
By (

), each term

in the above product product is at most
1/2
. We use that
1-x\geq\exp(-2x)
for all

. This yields

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq k\right)\geq\frac{1}{4M}\exp\bigg(-2(m-2)\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{n-j-1}\bigg)=\frac{1}{4M}\exp\bigg(-2(m-2)\sum_{j=2}^{k}\frac{1}{n-j}\bigg).

Recall
k=\lfloor n/(Mm)\rfloor
. We bound the sum in the exponential from above by

, which is at most
1/(Mm-1)
. We obtain for

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}_{\left(\frac{v}{n}\right)^{2-\varepsilon}\leq s\leq\frac{v}{n}}\log\left(s\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)+\mathbbm{1}_{s>\frac{v}{n}}\log\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbbm{1}_{\left(\frac{v}{n}\right)^{2-\varepsilon}\leq s\leq\frac{v}{n}}\log\left(\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{\varepsilon}\right)+\mathbbm{1}_{s>\frac{v}{n}}\log\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbbm{1}_{\left(\frac{v}{n}\right)^{2-\varepsilon}\leq s\leq\frac{v}{n}}\frac{\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}\log\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)+\mathbbm{1}_{s>\frac{v}{n}}\log\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\log\left(\frac{1}{s}\right),

and inserting this into (
A.5
) finishes the proof of (
2.12
).

To prove (
2.13
), it suffices to consider
s
with
3(v/n)^{2}\leq s\leq v/n
. From (
A.5

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\leq s\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)\geq\frac{sc_{\mathrm{B}}}{4}\sum_{k=3}^{\lfloor s\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\wedge\frac{n}{v}\rfloor}\frac{k-2}{k^{2}}\geq\frac{sc_{\mathrm{B}}}{12}\sum_{k=3}^{\lfloor s\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\rfloor}\frac{1}{k}\geq\frac{c_{\mathrm{B}}}{36}s.\qed

Proof of Claim
A.1
.

Writing
B(x,y)=\frac{(x-1)!(y-1)!}{(x+y-1)!}
for the Beta function, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{BetaBin}(n-v,2,v-2)=k\right)

\displaystyle=\binom{n-v}{k}\frac{B(k+2,n-(k+2))}{B(2,v-2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-v)!}{k!(n-v-k)!}\frac{(k+1)!(n-k-3)!}{(n-1)!}\frac{(v-1)!}{(v-3)!1!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k+1)(v-1)(v-2)}{(n-1)(n-2)}\frac{(n-v)!}{(n-v-k)!}\frac{(n-3-k)!}{(n-3)!}.

For the first term in the above product, we have, since
v\geq 3
, that

\frac{(k+1)(v-1)(v-2)}{(n-1)(n-2)}\geq\frac{k(v-1)(v-2)}{n^{2}}\geq\frac{kv^{2}}{6n^{2}}.

To bound the remaining terms, assume first that
v\geq k
.
Using that
\frac{2v}{n}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and the elementary inequality
1-x\geq\exp(-2x)
for
x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
, we get that

\frac{(n-v)!}{(n-v-k)!}\frac{(n-3-k)!}{(n-3)!}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{n-v-j}{n-3-j}\geq\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{n-v-k}{n}\geq\left(\frac{n-2v}{n}\right)^{k}\\
=\left(1-\frac{2v}{n}\right)^{k}\geq\exp\left(-\frac{4kv}{n}\right)\geq\exp\left(-4\right),

where we used that
k\leq\frac{n}{v}
for the last inequality.
Similarly, for
v<k
we get that

\frac{(n-v)!}{(n-v-k)!}\frac{(n-3-k)!}{(n-3)!}=\frac{(n-v)!}{(n-3)!}\frac{(n-3-k)!}{(n-v-k)!}=\prod_{j=0}^{v-4}\frac{n-3-k-j}{n-3-j}\\
\geq\prod_{j=0}^{v-4}\frac{n-v-k}{n}\geq\left(\frac{n-2k}{n}\right)^{v}=\left(1-\frac{2k}{n}\right)^{v}\geq\exp\left(-\frac{4kv}{n}\right)\geq\exp\left(-4\right).

Combining the three previous inequalities, we get that

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{BetaBin}(n-v,2,v-2)=k\right)\geq\frac{e^{-4}}{6}k\frac{v^{2}}{n^{2}},

proving the claim.
∎

Proof.

Fix some
n\geq 3
,
v\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n-v+1
. Then, we use that, for
v\geq 2
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k\right)=(v-1)\frac{(n-v)!}{(n-1)!}\frac{(n-k-1)!}{(n-v-k+1)!}=\frac{v-1}{n-1}\prod_{j=0}^{k-2}\frac{n-v-j}{n-2-j}\leq\frac{v-1}{n-1}\left(\frac{n-v}{n-2}\right)^{k-1},

as the factors are decreasing in
j
.
Using that
\tfrac{v-1}{n-1}\leq 2v/n
, that
(\tfrac{n}{n-2})^{k-1}\leq(\tfrac{n}{n-2})^{n-2}\leq e^{2}
, that
\log(1-v/n)\leq-v/n
, and that
\exp(-(k-1)v/n)\leq\exp(1-kv/n)
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{v-1}{n-1}\leq 2v/n,\qquad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n-v}{n-2}\right)^{k-1}=\left(1-\frac{v-2}{n-2}\right)^{k-1}\leq\exp\left(-(k-1)\frac{v-2}{n-2}\right)\leq\exp\bigg(2-k\frac{v}{n}\bigg),

(A.6)

where the last inequality holds since
1\leq k\leq n-v+1
and
v\geq 2
: indeed, since the two functions
k\mapsto\tfrac{-(k-1)(v-1)}{n-1}
and
k\mapsto 2-\tfrac{kv}{n}
are linear in
k
, it suffices to prove the inequality for
k=1
and for
k=n-v+1
. The statement for
k=1
is clear, since
v\leq n
. For
k=n-v+1
, the statement is equivalent to

(n-v+1)\frac{v}{n}-(n-v)\frac{v-2}{n-2}-2=\frac{-3nv+2v^{2}-2v+4n}{n\left(n-2\right)}\leq 0.

(A.7)

The function
h(v)\coloneqq-3nv+2v^{2}-2v+4n
is convex, so that the maximum in the domain
[2,n]
is attained at one of the boundary values. One readily checks that
h(2),h(n)\leq 0
, so that
h(v)\leq 0
for all
v\in[2,n]
, implying (
A.7
) and thus also (
A.6
).
Because (
2.14
) is also trivially true for
k>n-v+1
, these bounds yield (
2.14
).

For the proof of (
2.15
), it suffices to consider
t>2
, and thus also for
v\geq 3
, since the probability of the event on the left-hand side of (
2.15
) equals zero for
v=2,t>2
. Further, the statement is clear when
t\tfrac{n}{v}>n-v+1
. Using the previously established bound on
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k)
, we obtain for
v\geq 3
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq t\frac{n}{v}\right)\leq\frac{v-1}{n-1}\sum_{k\geq t\tfrac{n}{v}}\bigg(\frac{n-v}{n-2}\bigg)^{k-1}\leq\frac{v-1}{n-1}\frac{n-2}{v-2}\bigg(\frac{n-v}{n-2}\bigg)^{t(n/v)-1}\\
\leq\frac{v-1}{v-2}\bigg(\frac{n-v}{n-2}\bigg)^{t(n/v)-1}\leq 2\bigg(\frac{n-v}{n-2}\bigg)^{t(n/v)-1}\leq 2e^{2}\exp\left(-t\right).

(A.8)

In the last inequality, we used that
(\tfrac{n-v}{n-2})^{t(n/v)-1}\leq\exp(2-t\tfrac{n}{v}\tfrac{v}{n})
for
t\tfrac{n}{v}\leq n-v+1
, as proven in inequality (
A.6
).
This proves (
2.15
) for
v\geq 3
.

We next prove (
2.16
). We use the de-Finetti representation of the Beta-Binomial distribution and see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)>t\frac{n}{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Bin}(n-v,p)+1>t\frac{n}{v}\right)(v-1)(1-p)^{v-2}\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\frac{4t}{v}}^{1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Bin}\left(\Big\lceil\frac{n}{2}\Big\rceil,p\right)\geq t\frac{n}{v}\right)(v-1)(1-p)^{v-2}\mathrm{d}p.

(A.9)

First, observe that for a Binomial random variable
X_{p}
with parameters
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
and
p\geq\frac{4t}{v}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X_{p})\leq\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]=\Big\lceil\frac{n}{2}\Big\rceil p\geq\frac{n}{2}\frac{4t}{v}=2t\frac{n}{v}\geq 4,

where the last inequality holds since
v\leq\tfrac{n}{2},t\geq 1
.
Combining these facts with Cantelli’s inequality, we get that

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Bin}\left(\Big\lceil\frac{n}{2}\Big\rceil,p\right)\geq t\frac{n}{v}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(X_{p}\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]}{2}\right)\geq 1-\frac{\mathrm{Var}(X_{p})}{\mathrm{Var}(X_{p})+\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]^{2}/4}\\
=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]^{2}/4}{\mathrm{Var}(X_{p})+\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]^{2}/4}\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]^{2}/4}{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]^{2}/4}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]/4}{1+\mathbb{E}\left[X_{p}\right]/4}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Inserting this bound into (
A.9
) yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)>t\frac{n}{v}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\frac{4t}{v}}^{1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Bin}\left(\Big\lceil\frac{n}{2}\Big\rceil,p\right)\geq t\frac{n}{v}\right)(v-1)(1-p)^{v-2}\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{4t}{v}}^{1}(v-1)(1-p)^{v-2}\mathrm{d}p

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{4t}{v}\right)^{v-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-\frac{8t}{v}\right)^{v-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-8t\right),

where we used the elementary inequality
1-x\geq e^{-2x}
for all
x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
in the last line. This finishes the proof of (
2.16
).

For the proof of inequality (
2.17
), we start by decomposing on the event
\{\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq tn/(2v)\}
and its complement, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq t\frac{n}{v}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{t}{2}\frac{n}{v}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq t\frac{n}{v},\ \mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq t\frac{n}{2v}\right).

For the first summand in the above sum, we use inequality (
2.15
) with
t/2
. Using further that
\mathrm{pa}(v)
is uniformly distributed on
[v-1]
on the second summand, we obtain

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq t\frac{n}{v}\right)\leq 2e^{-t/2+2}+\frac{1}{v-1}\sum_{i=1}^{v-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(i)\geq t\frac{n}{v},\ \mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq t\frac{n}{2v}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=i\right).

(A.10)

If
\mathrm{pa}(v)=i
and
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq t\frac{n}{2v}
, then the subtree
(T,1)_{i\downarrow}\setminus(T,1)_{v\downarrow}
must have at least
t\frac{n}{2v}
elements for
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(i)
to exceed
t\frac{n}{v}
. So,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(i)\geq t\frac{n}{v},\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq t\frac{n}{2v}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=i\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\big|(T,1)_{i\downarrow}\setminus(T,1)_{v\downarrow}|\geq t\frac{n}{2v}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=i\right).

One can couple a RRT and a RRT conditioned on
\{\mathrm{pa}(v)=i\}
by grafting
v
and its subtree at
i
. Doing so, it is direct that
|(T,1)_{i\downarrow}\setminus(T,1)_{v\downarrow}|
conditioned on
\mathrm{pa}(v)=i
is stochastically dominated by
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(i)
. So,

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(i)\geq t\frac{n}{v},\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\leq t\frac{n}{2v}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=i\right)\\
\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|(T,1)_{i\downarrow}\setminus(T,1)_{v\downarrow}|\geq t\frac{n}{2v}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=i\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(i)\geq t\frac{n}{2v}\right).

Inserting this inequality into (
A.10
) and using (
2.15
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq t\frac{n}{v}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-t/2+2}+\frac{1}{v-1}\sum_{i=1}^{v-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(i)\geq t\frac{n}{2v}\right)

\displaystyle=2e^{-t/2+2}+\frac{1}{v-1}\sum_{i=1}^{v-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(i)\geq i\frac{t}{2v}\frac{n}{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-t/2+2}+\frac{1}{v-1}\sum_{i=1}^{v-1}2\exp\left(-i\frac{t}{2v}+2\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-t/2+2}+\frac{2e^{2}}{v-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-i\frac{t}{2v}\right)

\displaystyle=2e^{-t/2+2}+\frac{2e^{2}}{v-1}\frac{1}{\exp(t/(2v))-1}\leq 2e^{-t/2+2}+\frac{2e^{2}}{v-1}\frac{2v}{t},

where we used the elementary inequality
\exp(t/(2v))-1\geq t/(2v)
for the last inequality.
The bound (
2.17
) follows. Inequality (
2.18
) follows from an application of (
2.17
) with
t=\frac{v}{2}
.

Proof.

We start by treating the cases

\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\leq\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
at
v=2

\mathbb{P}\Big(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(1)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(2)\geq S\frac{n^{2}}{4}\Big)\overset{\eqref{eq:proba_fringe-large}}{\leq}2e^{2}e^{-S/2}\leq\frac{C}{S}

for some positive constant
C

v=1

\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(1)
and
\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(2)

\mathbb{P}\Big(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(1)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{1}\right)^{2}\Big)=\mathbb{P}\Big(\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(2)\geq 4S\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}\Big)\leq\frac{C}{4S},

concluding the proof of the lemma for
v=1,2
. For
v\in[3,n]

\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\coloneqq\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(u)-\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\mathbbm{1}_{v\in(T_{n},1)_{u\downarrow}}

for the number of vertices in the fringe tree of
u
that are not contained in the fringe tree of
v
. In particular, for
\mathrm{pa}(v)

\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))=\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(\mathrm{pa}(v))+\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v).

\phi_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}(v)\leq\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)+\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)^{2}.

Thus we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)^{2}\geq\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right).

(A.11)

By (
2.15
), there exists a constant
C^{\prime}<\infty
so that
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)^{2}\right]\leq C^{\prime}(n/v)^{2}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N},v\in[n]
. Markov’s inequality thus implies that

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)^{2}\geq\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)^{2}\right]}{\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{2C^{\prime}}{S}.

(A.12)

For the first term in (
A.11
), we use the law of total probability to get that.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{v-1}\sum_{u=1}^{v-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=u\right).

(A.13)

For fixed
u\in[v-1]
, the random variable
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
is independent of
\mathrm{pa}(v)
. Indeed, both
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
and
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)
are determined by
\left(\mathrm{pa}(w);w\in[n]\setminus\{v\}\right)
. Further, the random variables
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)
and
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)
are negatively associated. Indeed, conditioned on
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)=k
, the tree
T_{n}\setminus(T_{n},1)_{v\downarrow}
is just distributed like a uniform attachment tree of size
n-k
. Thus, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=u\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{2}\frac{n}{v}\right)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq 2^{\ell}\frac{n}{v},\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{4}2^{-\ell}\frac{n}{v}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{2}\frac{n}{v}\right)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq 2^{\ell}\frac{n}{v}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{4}2^{-\ell}\frac{n}{v}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:proba_fringe-large}}{\leq}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{2}\frac{n}{v}\right)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2e^{2}\exp\left(-2^{\ell}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{4}2^{-\ell}\frac{n}{v}\right).

Inserting this into (
A.13
) and using again that
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)
and
\mathrm{pa}(v)
are independent, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{v-1}\sum_{u=1}^{v-1}\left(\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{2}\frac{n}{v}\right)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2e^{2}\exp\left(-2^{\ell}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{4}2^{-\ell}\frac{n}{v}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{v-1}\sum_{u=1}^{v-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{2}\frac{n}{v}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=u\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2e^{2}\exp\left(-2^{\ell}\right)\frac{1}{v-1}\sum_{u=1}^{v-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(u)\geq\frac{S}{4}2^{-\ell}\frac{n}{v}\ \Big|\ \mathrm{pa}(v)=u\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq\frac{S}{2}\frac{n}{v}\right)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2e^{2}\exp\left(-2^{\ell}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq\frac{S}{4}2^{-\ell}\frac{n}{v}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq\frac{S}{2}\frac{n}{v}\right)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2e^{2}\exp\left(-2^{\ell}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq\frac{S}{4}2^{-\ell}\frac{n}{v}\right).

By Lemma
2.9
, there exists a constant
C<\infty
so that
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\geq tn/v\right)\leq C/t
for all
t>0
. Further, let
C^{\star}>0
be a constant so that
\exp\left(-x\right)\leq C^{\star}x^{-2}
for all
x\geq 1
. Then we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\neq v}(\mathrm{pa}(v))\mathrm{Fr}_{n}(v)\geq\frac{S}{2}\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{S/2}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2e^{2}\exp\left(-2^{\ell}\right)\frac{C}{\frac{S}{4}2^{-\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C}{S}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2e^{2}C^{\star}2^{-2\ell}\frac{4C}{S2^{-\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2C}{S}+\frac{8e^{2}C^{\star}C}{S}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2^{-\ell}=\frac{2C}{S}+\frac{16e^{2}C^{\star}C}{S}.

Inserting this inequality and inequality (
A.12

A.11

\mathbb{P}\left(\phi_{n}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{({2}})}}(v)\geq S\left(\frac{n}{v}\right)^{2}\right)\leq\frac{2C^{\prime}}{S}+\frac{2C}{S}+\frac{16e^{2}C^{\star}C}{S},

finishing the proof.
