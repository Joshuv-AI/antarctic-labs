Prognostic markers, quality of life (QoL) and value of health (V-He) in advanced biliary cancers (ABC) treated with second-line active symptom control (ASC) alone or ASC with oxaliplatin–5-FU chemotherapy (ASC + FOLFOX) in the randomised phase III, multicentre, open-label ABC-06 clinical trial

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe ABC-06 trial established FOLFOX as the reference second-line regimen for patients diagnosed with advanced biliary tract cancer (ABC). Here we analysed the prognostic role of tumour markers carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), and cancer antigen 125 (CA125) and the impact of FOLFOX on quality of life (QoL).Patients and methodsPatients diagnosed with ABC were randomly assigned (1:1) to second-line active symptom control (ASC) + modified FOLFOX or ASC alone. Tumour markers (CA19-9, CEA, and CA125) were measured and QoL (EORTC QLQ-C30, QLQ-BIL21) and value of health (EuroQol EQ-5D) questionnaires were completed at baseline and during follow-up. The prognostic value of baseline tumour markers, CA19-9 dynamics (week 4 change), time to deterioration of QoL (TTD; ≥10-point decline in global health status), and QoL changes over time (month 4 status) were assessed.ResultsPaired baseline and week 4 CA19-9 data were available for 37 patients in the ASC + FOLFOX arm. Stable/decreasing CA19-9 was associated with a numerically longer median radiological progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS; P values >0.05). Baseline CA19-9 data were available for 135 of 162 patients: 92 (68.1%) high, 43 (31.9%) low. High baseline CA19-9 was associated with shorter unadjusted OS [4.4 versus 6.4 months; hazard ratio (HR) 1.97, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.33-2.93, P < 0.001] and unadjusted PFS (3.2 versus 5.0 months; HR 1.53, 95% CI 1.05-2.23, P = 0.027) in the FOLFOX arm. In 120 patients with baseline data for all markers available (CA19-9, CEA, and CA125), each marker independently predicted OS in the multivariable analysis: adjusted HR (aHR) 1.56 (95% CI 1.03-2.35, P = 0.003), aHR 1.60 (95% CI 1.06-2.43, P = 0.026), and aHR 1.70 (95% CI 1.13-2.56, P = 0.011) for CA19-9, CEA, and CA125, respectively. In addition, median OS decreased from 8.9 months (no markers elevated) to 3.2 months (all three elevated). QoL analysis (n = 76) showed no significant difference in TTD between arms (aHR 0.97, 95% CI 0.48-1.97, P = 0.94). FOLFOX did not worsen global, physical, social, or symptom QoL scores, whereas patients receiving ASC recorded declines across several domains.ConclusionsIn the setting of second-line FOLFOX for ABC, elevated tumour markers impacted on prognosis and could be utilised as stratification factors in future clinical trials. The addition of FOLFOX did not imply QoL deterioration, and did, in fact, preserve some of the status in several domains.

Introduction

Biliary tract cancer (BTC), including cholangiocarcinoma, gallbladder cancer, and (to a lesser degree) ampullary tumours, are aggressive malignancies.1 Unfortunately, most BTCs are diagnosed at advanced stages, when prognosis remains poor despite active treatment. For over a decade, the combination of cisplatin and gemcitabine served as the standard first-line chemotherapy, as established by the pivotal ABC-02 trial.2 More recently, the addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors [specifically durvalumab (TOPAZ-1 trial3) and pembrolizumab (KEYNOTE-966 trial4)] to cisplatin–gemcitabine has demonstrated significant improvements in overall survival (OS). These combinations are now incorporated into the standard of care for patients with advanced BTC (ABC).1

For patients harbouring targetable alterations, targeted therapies have changed the treatment paradigm by incorporating tailored strategies for alterations5 such as IDH1 mutations and FGFR2 fusions with treatments such as ivosidenib,6 pemigatinib,7 or futibatinib.8 Zanidatamab has been recently approved for patients with HER2 amplification by the European Medicines Agency,9 while trastuzumab deruxtecan has gained a tumour-agnostic approval by the US Food and Drug Administration.10 In addition to these, many other targeted therapies are now under evaluation.1

However, for ∼60% of patients diagnosed with ABC, there is currently no targetable alteration identified; thus second-line therapy options will be limited to further cytotoxic chemotherapy.11 In this setting, second-line treatment options remain limited and primarily include FOLFOX (as per ABC-0611) and irinotecan-based regimens such as liposomal irinotecan plus 5-fluorouracil–leucovorin,12 now also supported by guidelines.1

The ABC-06 clinical trial established FOLFOX combined with active symptom control (ASC) as the new standard of care treatment at progression to cisplatin–gemcitabine for ABC.13 This study was a phase III, open-label clinical trial carried out across 20 UK centers and enrolled 162 patients with ABC (including cholangiocarcinoma, gallbladder, or ampullary carcinoma) who experienced disease progression after first-line cisplatin–gemcitabine therapy and had Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (PS) 0-1. Participants were randomly assigned 1:1 to ASC (including 4-weekly clinical review for early identification and treatment of worsening symptoms and cancer-related complications) alone or ASC in combination with FOLFOX (oxaliplatin, fluorouracil, and folinic acid), administered biweekly for up to 12 cycles. The primary endpoint of OS favoured the FOLFOX arm, with a median OS of 6.2 versus 5.3 months [adjusted hazard ratio (HR) 0.69, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.50-0.97, P = 0.031], along with clinically meaningful improvements in 6- and 12-month survival rates: 50.6% and 25.9% for the ASC + FOLFOX and 35.5% and 11.4% for the ASC arm, respectively. Grade 3-5 adverse events occurred more frequently in the FOLFOX cohort (69% versus 52%), including three treatment-related fatalities, with the most common grade 3-5 FOLFOX-related toxicities being neutropenia (10 patients, 12%), fatigue/lethargy (9 patients, 11%), and infection (8 patients, 10%).

Based on these data, FOLFOX was established as the new standard of care regimen in this setting and adopted by international guidelines.1,14,15 However, the benefit derived from FOLFOX remains modest, and patient selection to identify patients who may benefit the most16 is critical. Moreover, understanding the impact of chemotherapy on QoL in this fragile population helps with treatment individualisation. In this article, we present data with respect to biomarkers associated with prognosis (with special focus on tumour markers) and QoL.

Methods

Study design and patient population

Within the ABC-06 study, patients diagnosed with ABC (cholangiocarcinoma, gallbladder, or ampullary cancer) with progression after cisplatin–gemcitabine were randomly assigned (1:1) to ASC + FOLFOX or ASC. Full design of the ABC-06 clinical trial has previously been reported.13

The primary publication from ABC-06 clinical trial explored clinical prognostic factors by performing subgroup analyses.13 At the time of study design, randomisation was stratified to three well-described prognostic factors, including albumin [<3.5 (low) versus ≥3.5 mg/L (high)], platinum sensitivity [sensitive, defined as ‘progression after three months (90 days) of day 1 of the last cycle of first-line cisplatin and gemcitabine’, versus refractory/resistant], and stage (locally advanced versus metastatic).17, 18, 19, 20 PS was not included, as only patients with PS 0-1 were eligible. In the multivariable analysis carried out, the impact of FOLFOX was adjusted by these prognostic factors (Supplementary Material S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107777).

Study procedures linked to tumour markers and QoL assessment

All patients randomly assigned to the chemotherapy arm had a 3-monthly computed tomography scan to evaluate progression; progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as per RECIST 1.1. In contrast, following advice from the ethics committee, the patients randomly assigned to the control arm (no chemotherapy) did not have such radiological follow-up. Thus, PFS data available in the control arm are based on clinical progression or progression identified on radiological evaluations carried out for clinical reasons. OS was defined as the time from random assignment to death.

Tumour markers [carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), and cancer antigen 125 (CA125)] were measured at baseline and at every follow-up visit.

As per clinical trial protocol, QoL and health status questionnaires [European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire (EORTC QLQ)-C30, EORTC QLQ-BIL21, and EuroQol EQ-5 Dimension (EQ-5D)] were completed at baseline and every 12 weeks thereafter. In addition, patients were provided with a predesigned health resource use diary; these diaries were completed daily by patients at home and collected by the clinical team at baseline (the diary included data retrospectively annotated by patients for the 2-week period before study entry) and every 12 weeks thereafter. Results from the QoL and health status questionnaires only are included in the present manuscript.

Analysis of tumour markers and their role as prognostic factors

Elevation of a tumour marker at baseline was defined as a value that was >1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) as per local laboratory testing. This cut-off was selected because elevations <1.5 ULN in laboratory tests are usually not considered clinically significant. This post hoc analysis explored the clinical relevance of the dynamics of CA19-9 and other tumour markers by evaluating if changes (stable/decreasing versus increasing) in CA19-9 at week 4 from initiation of FOLFOX were associated with radiological PFS in the setting of the ABC-06 clinical trial. This was analysed for all patients in the experimental arm, as well as in patients with increased CA19-9 at baseline who were treated with FOLFOX. Patients with week 4 CA19-9 at or below baseline were considered stable/decreasing; if week 4 CA19-9 was higher than baseline, they were classified as increased, with no specific cut-off for such definition.

Secondary endpoints included assessment of the prognostic impact of baseline tumour markers on OS. In this regard, we assessed the impact of increased baseline CA19-9 on OS. CEA and CA125 were also analysed in a similar manner.

Survival analysis was carried out using Kaplan–Meier and Cox regression. Multivariate Cox regression was adjusted to treatment arm and stratification factors (previously defined for ABC-06 clinical trial) as appropriate.

QoL analysis objectives and endpoints

Analysis of QoL was a prespecified secondary endpoint in the main ABC-06 clinical trial study design. Patients who completed all or part of the EORTC QLQ-C30 or EQ-5D measure at baseline were eligible for the QoL analysis. Follow-up data reported closest to month 4 from the time of random assignment (selected for being the median PFS in the FOLFOX arm of the ABC-06 clinical trial) were also analysed.

The primary aim of the QoL analysis was to compare the time to QoL deterioration (TTD) between treatment arms, defined as the time from randomisation to the first QoL deterioration (reduction) ≥10 points for each patient in the global health status/QoL (derived from Q29-30 of QLQ-C30). This analysis was carried out with Cox regression and with Kaplan–Meier curves, with death as a competing risk and adjusted for stratification factors. In addition, descriptive analyses of the QoL measures by study arm were analysed; t-test was used for comparison between time points. A two-tailed P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Trial conduct

The ABC-06 clinical trial was a phase III, randomised, open-label study conducted under the auspices of the UK National Cancer Research Institute’s Upper Gastrointestinal Cancer Studies Group. Patients were enrolled at 20 UK centres specialising in BTC management, with trial oversight, data collection, monitoring, quality control, and analysis carried out by the Manchester Clinical Trials Unit. The study was sponsored by The Christie NHS Foundation Trust and executed in accordance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was granted by a research ethics committee, and all participants provided written informed consent before any study-related procedures (registered as NCT01926236 and EudraCT 2013-001812-30).

Dynamic changes in CA19-9

Paired baseline and week 4 CA19-9 data were available for 37 patients in the ASC + FOLFOX arm. CA19-9 was stable/decreasing in 17 patients (45.9%) and increasing in 20 patients (54.1%). Stable/decreasing CA19-9 was associated with a numerically longer median radiological PFS (4.3 months versus 3.3 months), but differences did not reach statistical significance (HR 1.08, 95% CI 0.55-2.14; P = 0.81). When the analysis was restricted to patients with elevated baseline CA19-9 (23 patients), the impact on PFS was more marked (5.7 months versus 3.2 months), but remained nonsignificant (HR 1.68, 95% CI 0.70-4.01, P = 0.23). Stable/decreasing CA19-9 at week 4 (37 patients) did not significantly impact OS (P = 0.56); this was also not significant in patients with high baseline CA19-9 (23 patients; P = 0.84).

Prognostic role of tumour markers at baseline

Of 162 patients randomly assigned, baseline CA19-9 data were available for 135 patients: 92 patients were classified as high (68.1%) and 43 patients as low (31.9%).

Elevated baseline CA19-9 was associated with both shorter unadjusted median clinical/radiological PFS (3.2 months versus 5.0 months; HR 1.53, 95% CI 1.05-2.23, P = 0.027) when patients in both arms were analysed together (Supplementary Material S2A, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107777). When the analysis was limited to patients in the FOLFOX arm, the differences did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.072; Supplementary Material S2B, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107777).

High baseline CA19-9 also impacted unadjusted OS when all patients were analysed (4.4 months versus 6.4 months; HR 1.97, 95% CI 1.33-2.93, P < 0.001; Supplementary Material S3A, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107777). When the analysis was limited to patients on the chemotherapy arm, statistical significance was maintained (P = 0.004; Supplementary Material S3B, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107777), whereas statistical significance did not hold in the control arm (P = 0.12; Supplementary Material S3C, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.107777).

Discussion

ABC remains a highly aggressive malignancy with limited treatment options beyond first-line therapy. Although recent advances in molecularly targeted therapy and immunotherapy have transformed the management of biomarker-defined subgroups, around 60% of patients lack actionable alterations. This leaves cytotoxic chemotherapy as the only available second-line option. In this context, the ABC-06 trial represented a landmark achievement by establishing FOLFOX as the new standard of care after progression to first-line cisplatin–gemcitabine. However, the magnitude of benefit is modest, underlying the relevance of more effective therapeutic strategies and a better understanding of patient selection and impact of these treatments on QoL. In this regard, we present data on prognostic markers (with special focus on tumour markers) and on QoL within the ABC-06 clinical trial.

In our analysis of the ABC-06 clinical trial data, the utility of CA19-9 measured at week 4 after chemotherapy initiation was limited and did not impact patient outcomes. This is contrary to what had been previously suggested by Grunnet et al.,21 who assessed CA19-9 response (defined as 20% decrease from baseline) at 4 or 10-12 weeks from initiation of first-line chemotherapy for ABC. The study reported that CA19-9 responders had a better OS with an aHR of 0.60 (95% CI 0.44-0.80, P = 0.0005). The predictive value of CA19-9 response was confirmed in all three validation cohorts in this study. We were unable to replicate these findings. Limited sample size could be a potential explanation, along with the fact that in the ABC-06 trial, we solely focused on the second-line setting, which could represent a different patient population with a poorer prognosis and quicker deterioration.

In contrast, baseline CA19-9, CEA, and CA125 all demonstrated independent prognostic value for OS when assessed at baseline. Interestingly, baseline increase of any of these markers did impact OS, with OS shortening with a higher number of markers increased at baseline. Other prior studies in ABC did suggest, but were unable to confirm, the prognostic impact of elevated tumour markers on OS.18,22 The only prior study that identified the impact of CA19-9, CEA, and CA125 on prognosis in ABC was the ABC-03 clinical trial.23 This study explored the role of the vascular endothelial growth factor inhibitor, cediranib, in the first-line setting,23 where all three high baseline CA19-9, CEA, and CA125 levels impacted both OS (aHRs ranging between 1.03 and 1.08) and PFS (aHRs ranging between 1.03 and 1.24), with all P values <0.03.

With these findings in mind, we can make the following conclusions. First, our results suggest that we should probably test not only for CA19-9, which will be negative in around 10% of patients due to their Lewis phenotype,24 but for all three markers (including CEA and CA125) at baseline assessment of ABC patients. Second, the number of elevated markers at baseline appeared to carry additive prognostic weight: patients with none of the three markers elevated had the longest survival, whereas those with all three elevated fared the worst. This cumulative effect underscores the potential of integrating these routinely available and inexpensive laboratory parameters into clinical decision making. If our results are validated in another series, we can propose including a combination of the three elevated markers as a stratification factor for future clinical trials (i.e. 0-1 raised versus 2-3 raised).

In advanced cancers with limited survival prospects, preserving QoL is a therapeutic imperative. Our data demonstrate that, despite the attendant toxicities of chemotherapy, adding FOLFOX to ASC did not lead to QoL deterioration, as measured by QoL questionnaires. The lack of improvement is unsurprising in a population with progressive metastatic disease, but the absence of a negative impact is reassuring and supports the continued use of FOLFOX, a relatively well-tolerated regimen, in appropriate patients. Interestingly, patients in the ASC-alone arm reported significant declines in several QoL domains, suggesting that disease control may help preserve functional status and symptom control.

Despite the strengths of these analyses, and the use of standardised QoL questionnaires, these tools may fail to capture the day-to-day fluctuations in well-being experienced by patients with ABC. Digital oncology platform tools provide an opportunity to address this gap by enabling real-time, remote monitoring of patient-reported outcomes, symptoms, and PS. Mobile applications and wearable technologies could complement standard QoL questionnaires, providing more granular data and facilitating earlier supportive care interventions. Recent data support their good correlation with PS and QoL assessment.25 In the future, digital tools might also serve as trial endpoints, enabling continuous QoL assessment and enhancing the relevance of study findings to patient-centred care.

Several limitations of the analysis presented here warrant discussion. First, tumour marker analyses were post hoc and exploratory, with potential selection bias due to incomplete marker data. Although statistical adjustments were carried out, residual confounding cannot be excluded. Second, CA19-9 levels can be influenced by biliary obstruction or infection, which may limit specificity26; similar nonspecific elevations may occur with CEA and CA125. Third, although our QoL analysis used validated instruments and was a prespecified secondary endpoint with prospectively collected data, sample size was modest, and missing data could have affected precision. Additionally, radiological PFS was not available for all patients in the control arm, limiting some comparative analyses. Moreover, QoL assessments were carried out at baseline and every 12 weeks thereafter; considering that this first reassessment was taken close to the median PFS in the FOLFOX arm, and that some patients may have completed their first follow-up assessment at or after disease progression, we cannot exclude the possibility of the current QoL data being an underestimation of the potential treatment benefit derived within those first 3 months of therapy. Earlier assessments during the treatment period may be a more appropriate approach in scenarios with such poor prognosis in future clinical trials.

The ABC-06 trial firmly established FOLFOX plus ASC as the current reference regimen for patients with ABC delivering survival benefit that, even though modest, does not negatively compromise QoL. Our analyses reinforce the prognostic value of CA19-9, CEA, and CA125, individually and cumulatively, and support their consideration as stratification factors in future clinical trials. The absence of QoL deterioration with chemotherapy in this frail population highlights the importance of disease control, even in the absence of symptomatic improvement. Looking ahead, there is an urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies, biomarker-driven trial designs, and digital health innovations that can improve both survival and the lived experience of patients with ABC.
