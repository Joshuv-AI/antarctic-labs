Introduction

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be a closed set, and denote by
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
the set of polynomials in
n
variables with real coefficients.
Denote by
\mathrm{Pos}_{\mathds{R}}(K):=\{p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\mid p(x)\geq 0\text{ for }x\in K\}
the set of positive polynomials on
K
.
A real sequence
(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is called a
K
-moment sequence, if there exists a measure
\mu:\mathrm{Bor}(K)\to\mathds{R}
supported on
K
, such that

s_{\alpha}=\int_{K}t^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(t)

for all
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Every linear map
T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
has the
canonical representation

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\cdot q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}

with polynomials
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Such a linear map
T
is called a
K
-positivity preserver, if
T\mathrm{Pos}_{\mathds{R}}(K)\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}_{\mathds{R}}(K)
.
Borcea’s Theorem
[
3
, Thm. 3.1]
asserts that
T
is a
\mathds{R}^{n}
-positivity preserver, if and only if,
(q_{\alpha}(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is a
\mathds{R}^{n}
-moment sequence for all
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
.
In
[
8
, Thm. 3.5]
, di Dio and Schmüdgen generalized this to any closed
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
T
is a
K
-positivity preserver if and only if
(q_{\alpha}(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is a
(K-y)
-moment sequence for all
y\in K
.
In
[
7
]
this connection between
K
-positivity preserving maps and general
K
-moment sequences was extended to matrix polynomials
p\in\mathrm{Herm}_{m}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for
m\in\mathds{N}
.
In the present paper we extend these results further to operator polynomials
p\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for an arbitrary Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
We use operator-valued measures
\mu:\mathrm{Bor}(\mathds{R}^{n})\to B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
which are used in quantum physics for describing states of observables, see e.g.
[
11
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
.
In quantum physics, the operators
\mu(S)\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
for Borel sets
S\in\mathrm{Bor}(\mathds{R}^{n})
encode probability distributions of measurement outcomes.
Therefore, characterizing operator moment sequences is of interest and has been done in
[
5
]
for the univariate moment problem to a large degree.
A Borcea type theorem for characterizing operator moment sequences and positivity preserving maps, however, has not yet been given and will be investigated in the current work.
Additionally, contrary to
[
5
]
, we will work in the general multivariate case.

This paper is structured in the following way.
After the preliminaries, in
Section

3
, we give our main result (
3.10
) which characterizes positivity preservers on closed sets
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Afterwards we discuss the shortcomings of this theorem which stem from the difference of local and general operator moment problems.
In
Section

4
we remedy these problems by restricting ourself to compact sets
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Operator Polynomials

Let
\mathcal{K}\in\{\mathds{R},\mathds{C}\}
and let
\mathcal{H}
be a
\mathds{K}
-Hilbert space with inner product
\left\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
and induced norm
||\,\cdot\,||_{\mathcal{H}}
.
Let
B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
be the Banach space of all linear, bounded, and self-adjoint operators on
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
B(\mathcal{H})_{h,+}
be the set of all positive operators in
B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
. Then
B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
is a real vector space and
B(\mathcal{H})_{h,+}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
is a full-dimensional convex cone with non-empty interior.
Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and let
B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the set of all
operator polynomials
, i.e., linear maps
p:\mathds{R}^{n}\to B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
of the form

p=\sum_{\alpha\preceq\beta}p_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}

for some
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
,
p_{\alpha}\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
, see e.g.
[
12
]
.
An operator polynomial
p\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is said to be positive on
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
p\succeq 0
on
K
, if
p(x)\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h,+}
for all
x\in K
.
We define

\mathrm{Pos}(K):=\big\{p\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\;\big|\;p(x)\succeq 0\ \text{ for all }x\in K\big\}.

2.2
Operator-Valued Measures

Definition 2.1

(see
[
1
, Def. 1 and Thm. 1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
be a measurable space.
We say
\mu:\mathcal{A}\to B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
is a
positive operator-valued measure
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
if

\mu_{x}:=\left\langle\mu(\,\cdot\,)x,x\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathds{R}

is a finite positive measure on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
.

The definition of positive operator-valued measures coincides with the definitions in
[
6
, Rem. 2]
.
Note that only
positive
operator-valued measures are defined which are always countably additive in the strong operator topology, see e.g.
[
1
, Prop. 1]
.
If one wishes to work with signed operator-valued measures, then one needs to pay attention to the convergence of countable additivity.

For integration with respect to operator-valued measures, see
[
1
, Sec. 5]
or
[
6
, Rem. 4]
.
For every operator-valued measure
\mu
on a measurable space
\mathcal{X}
and every integrable function
f
, one finds a linear bounded self-adjoint operator denoted by

\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(t)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(t)\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}

such that the usual integral properties are satisfied, i.e., linearity in
f
and positive functions leading to positive integrals, see
[
1
, Thm. 10]
.

In the matrix case, integration was defined using an inner product on
B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
with the trace of matrices, see
[
7
]
.
For infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces,
B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
is not a Hilbert space anymore and a trace does not exist for every operator.

2.3
Operator Moment Sequences

There are two different types of moment sequences for operators, general moment sequences and local moment sequences.
For an overview about these two types in the univariate case see
[
5
]
.
We will work with the multivariate case instead.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and let
\mathcal{T}=(T_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
be a sequence of operators.

\mathcal{T}
is called an
operator
K
-moment sequence
, if there exists a positive operator-valued measure
\mu:\mathrm{Bor}(K)\to B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
such that

T_{k}=\int_{K}t^{k}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu(t)

for all
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed, and let
\mathcal{T}=(T_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
be a sequence of operators.

\mathcal{T}
is called a
local operator
K
-moment sequence
, if for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
,
(\left\langle T_{k}x,x\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}})_{k\in\mathds{N}}\subseteq\mathds{R}
is a
K
-moment sequence.
That is,
\mathcal{T}
is a local operator
K
-moment sequence, if for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a measure
\mu_{x}:\mathrm{Bor}(K)\to\mathds{R}
such that

\left\langle T_{k}x,x\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{K}t^{k}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{x}(t)

for all
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

On compact sets
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
, both definitions coincide, i.e., every local operator
K
-moment sequence is also an operator
K
-moment sequence, see
[
2
, Thm. 2]
or
[
5
, Thm. 3.3]
and the discussion thereafter.

2.4
Map-Valued Measures

We will also use another type of measure called map-valued measures.

Definition 2.4

(see e.g.
[
6
, Sec. 2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}
be Hilbert spaces and
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
be a measurable space.
We say
\mu:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},B(\mathcal{K})_{h})
is a
map-valued measure
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
, if

\mu[A]:=\mu(\,\cdot\,)(A):\mathcal{A}\to B(\mathcal{K})_{h}

is an operator-valued measure for all
A\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
.
A map-valued measure is called
positive
if
\mu[A]
is a positive operator-valued measure for all
A\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h,+}
.

Both operator- and map-valued measures were defined and used in
[
6
]
but with different terminology.
Both types of measures were denoted as operator-valued measures in
[
6
]
and a positive map-valued measure was called a non-negative operator-valued measure.
For the purpose of clarity, we will distinguish between these two types of measures as
operator
-valued and
map
-valued measures.
Integration with respect to map-valued measures is defined the same as in
[
6
, Sec. 2]
.
Let
p\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Then there exist
m\in\mathds{N}
,
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
, and
p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with

p=A_{1}p_{1}+\dots+A_{m}p_{m}.

For a map-valued measure
\mu:\mathrm{Bor}(K)\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},B(\mathcal{K})_{h})
and
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
closed,

\int_{K}p\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu:=\int_{K}p_{1}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu[A_{1}]+\dots+\int_{K}p_{m}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu[A_{m}].

This integral does not depend on the representation
A_{1}p_{1}+\dots+A_{m}p_{m}
.
Additionally for a positive map-valued measure
\mu:\mathrm{Bor}(K)\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},B(\mathcal{K})_{h})
with
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
closed, and a positive operator polynomial
p\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,

\int_{K}p\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu\succeq 0.

Haviland Theorems

Theorem 2.5

(see
[
6
, Thm. 3]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,

Let

(i)

(ii)

.

Theorem 2.6

(see
[
6
, Thm. 4 and 5]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be compact, and
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space.
Let
L:B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
be linear.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

L
preserves positivity on

, i.e.,

for all

.

(ii)

There exists a unique positive Borel map-valued measure

on

for all

.

In
[
6
]
,

was given with additional restrictions on the functional

.

Lemma 2.7

(see
[

)
.

Let

(1)

.

-Positivity Preservers for closed

Theorem 3.10

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,

,

(i)

T\mathrm{Pos}(K)\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)

(ii)

For every

(iii)

For every

\nu_{y,a}:\mathrm{Bor}(K-y)\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},\mathds{R})

\left\langle Q_{\alpha}(A)(y)a,a\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{K-y}t^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y,a}[A](t)

\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}

is the set of Hermitian matrices
\mathrm{Herm}_{2}\in\mathds{C}^{2\times 2}
which is a real Hilbert space with inner product
\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle_{B(\mathcal{H})_{h}}:=\mathrm{tr}(XY)\in\mathds{R}
and orthonormal basis

\displaystyle H_{1,1}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle H_{1,2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle H_{2,1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle H_{2,2}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

For
k\in\mathds{N}_{0}
, define
Q_{k}:\mathrm{Herm}_{2}\to\mathrm{Herm}_{2}\otimes\mathds{R}[x]
,

\displaystyle Q_{k}(H_{1,1})=Q_{k}(H_{1,2})=Q_{k}(H_{2,1})=Q_{k}(H_{2,2}),

\displaystyle Q_{0}(H_{1,1}):=\begin{pmatrix}4&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_{1}(H_{1,1}):=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\
2&0\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_{2}(H_{1,1}):=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&4\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle Q_{2k-1}(H_{1,1}):=0\text{ for }k\geq 2,\quad Q_{2k}(H_{1,1}):=\begin{pmatrix}a_{k}&0\\
0&a_{k}\end{pmatrix}\text{ for }k\geq 2

with
a_{n}=2^{(n+2)!}
for
n\geq 0
.
Then for all
M\in\mathrm{Herm}_{2}
and
y\in\mathds{R}
,
(Q_{k}(M)(y))_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
is a local operator
\mathds{R}
-moment measure, see
[
2
, Thm. 1]
.
Hence, by
3.10
,
T:\mathrm{Herm}_{2}\otimes\mathds{R}[x]\to\mathrm{Herm}_{2}\otimes\mathds{R}[x]
with
T=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}Q_{k}\times\partial^{k}
preservers positivity.
However, by
[
13
, Prop. 2.1]
, for
H_{1,1}\in\mathrm{Herm}_{2,+}
and
y\in\mathds{R}
,
(Q_{k}(H_{1,1})(y))_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
is not an operator
\mathds{R}
-moment sequence, since

\begin{pmatrix}Q_{0}(H_{1,1})&Q_{1}(H_{1,1})\\
Q_{1}(H_{1,1})&Q_{2}(H_{1,1})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}4&0&0&2\\
0&1&2&0\\
0&2&1&0\\
2&0&0&4\end{pmatrix}

is not positive semi-definite.

\circ

The proof of
3.10
cannot yield an operator moment sequence for
(Q_{\alpha}(A)(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
.
The problem stems from the step (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) where the Haviland theorem for operator polynomials (
2.5
) is used.
In order to construct a measure
\nu_{y}:\mathrm{Bor}(K)\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},B(\mathcal{H})_{h})
with moments
(Q_{\alpha}(A)(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
, the measures from the Haviland theorem must fulfill certain conditions.
These conditions are, for example, outlined in
[
1
, Thm. 2]
and ensure that one can construct a bounded sesquilinear form from which the operator
\nu_{y}(A)
follows.

However, on compact sets
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
, the moment problem is determinate and every local operator moment sequence is a general operator moment sequence.

4
Operator
K
-Positivity Preserver for Compact
K

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
,
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
be compact, and
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space.
Let

T:B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],\ T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\cdot Q_{\alpha}\times\partial^{\alpha}

with linear operators
Q_{\alpha}:B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\to B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
preserves positivity on
K
, i.e.,
T\mathrm{Pos}(K)\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)
.

(ii)

For each
y\in K
, there exists a positive map-valued measure
\nu_{y}:{\mathrm{Bor}(K-y)}\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},B(\mathcal{H})_{h})
such that

Q_{\beta}(A)(y)=\int_{K-y}t^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[A](t)

for all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.

The proof is an adapted version of the proof of
[
8
, Thm. 3.5]
using
[
6
, Thm. 4]
on compact sets and a generalization of
[
7
, Thm. 5.4]
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
Let
y\in K
and define

L_{y}:B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to B(\mathcal{H})_{h},\quad p\mapsto L_{y}(p):=Tp(y).

Then
L_{y}(p)\succeq 0
for all
p\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)
.

2.6
asserts the existence of a positive Borel map-valued measure
\mu_{y}:\mathrm{Bor}(K)\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},B(\mathcal{H})_{h})
such that

L_{y}(p)=\int_{K}p(t)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(t)

for all
p\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
By
2.8
,

Q_{\beta}(A)(y)=\sum_{\alpha\preceq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot(-1)^{\beta-\alpha}\cdot T(A\otimes t^{\alpha})(y)\cdot y^{\beta-\alpha}

for all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle Q_{\beta}(A)(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\preceq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot(-1)^{\beta-\alpha}\cdot y^{\beta-\alpha}\cdot T(A\otimes t^{\alpha})(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\preceq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot(-1)^{\beta-\alpha}\cdot y^{\beta-\alpha}\cdot\int_{K}A\otimes t^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{K}A\otimes(t-y)^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(t)=\int_{K}(t-y)^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}[A](t)=\int_{K-y}t^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[A](t)

for
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
,
A\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
where
\nu_{y}:=\mu_{y}(\,\cdot\,+y):\mathrm{Bor}(K-y)\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},B(\mathcal{H})_{h})
is a positive map-valued measure.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Let
y\in K
and let
\nu_{y}:\mathrm{Bor}(K-y)\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},B(\mathcal{H})_{h})
be a positive map-valued measure such that

Q_{\beta}(A)(y)=\int_{K-y}t^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[A](t)

for all
A\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
and all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Define
\mu_{y}:=\nu_{y}(\,\cdot\,-y):\mathrm{Bor}(K)\to\mathcal{L}(B(\mathcal{H})_{h},B(\mathcal{H})_{h})
as the pushforward measure.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{K}A\otimes t^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{K-y}A\otimes(t+y)^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\preceq\beta}\binom{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot y^{\beta-\alpha}\cdot\int_{K-y}t^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\nu_{y}[A](t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\cdot Q_{\alpha}(A)(y)\cdot\partial^{\alpha}y^{\beta}=T(A\otimes x^{\beta})(y)

for all
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
,
A\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}
, and
y\in K
.
By linearity,

\int_{K}p(t)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(t)=Tp(y)

for all
p\in B(\mathcal{H})_{h}\otimes\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
y\in K
.
Hence, by
2.6
,
T
is a
K
-positivity preserver.
∎

(i) from

.
Additionally, (ii) states that for every

(K-y)
-moment sequence.
