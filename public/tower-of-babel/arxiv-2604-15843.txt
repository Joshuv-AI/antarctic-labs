Polish spaces for countable and separable structures through quotient encodings Thanks: IM CAS (RVO 67985840).
Tomasz Kania

Abstract
. We develop a unified framework for locating natural properties of algebraic and analytic structures within the Borel hierarchy. Objects are presented as quotients of a universal generator and definability is read directly from the quotient data. For separable Banach-type structures (Banach algebras, C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, Banach lattices, TROs) the kernel space is Polish under the Wijsman topology, and the quotient-norm functional K ↦ ‖ x + K ‖ K\mapsto\|x+K\| is continuous, yielding a uniform definability scheme whose Borel ranks are bounded by quantifier alternation depth. For countable algebraic structures (groups, rings, lattices) we work on compact Polish spaces of congruences where atomic predicates are clopen. We obtain explicit Borel upper bounds: in the unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebra coding based on C max ∗ ​ ( F ∞ ) C^{*}_{\max}(F_{\infty}) , stable finiteness is closed, nuclearity is Borel, simplicity is G δ G_{\delta} , AF-ness lies in Π 3 0 \Pi^{0}_{3} , nuclear dimension ⩽ n \leqslant n lies in Π 3 0 \Pi^{0}_{3} , and for fixed exact D D , D D -absorption is analytic. For countable groups, soficity is G δ G_{\delta} ; for abelian groups, slenderness is Π 3 0 \Pi^{0}_{3} . We give an internal Borel coding of the K 0 K_{0} -assignment in the quotient/Wijsman framework; for each fixed coordinate the corresponding section is F σ F_{\sigma} , and suspension together with Bott periodicity yields Borel codings of all higher K K -groups. We also show that several bounds are optimal ( Σ 2 0 \Sigma^{0}_{2} - and Π 2 0 \Pi^{0}_{2} -complete). To calibrate the method’s reach, we exhibit a Π 1 1 \Pi^{1}_{1} -complete property (separable dual in the commutative C ∗ C^{*} -setting), provably outside the Borel hierarchy.

1.
Introduction

A central goal in modern mathematics is the classification of abstract structures up
to isomorphism. Descriptive set theory provides a powerful framework for quantifying
the complexity of such classification problems, primarily through the lens of
Borel reducibility. This theory has been successfully applied to operator
algebras. For example, Sabok proved that the isomorphism relation for
separable
C^{*}
-algebras is complete among orbit equivalence relations induced
by continuous actions of Polish groups
[
43
]
. Earlier work
of Farah–Toms–Törnquist established major non-classifiability results by
turbulence and analysed standard Borel codings and invariants for separable

C^{*}
-algebras
[
18
,
19
]
.

Very recently, Pi, Szachniewicz, and Tartarotti showed that
KK
-equivalence for
unital
C^{*}
-algebras is analytic and deduced that the class of separable

C^{*}
-algebras satisfying the UCT is analytic
[
40
]
.

A parallel line of work on the Banach-space side was recently developed by
Cúth, Doležal, Doucha, and Kurka. In
[
9
]

they introduced natural Polish spaces of separable Banach spaces
(as spaces of norms and pseudonorms on a countable rational vector space),
compared them with the admissible topologies of Godefroy–Saint-Raymond,
and analysed their generic structure. In the companion paper

[
10
]
they computed the descriptive complexity of
many natural isometry and isomorphism classes in those Polish spaces.
Our approach is complementary: rather than parameterising bare Banach spaces by
norms, we work with quotient presentations adapted to Banach-type categories
carrying algebraic or analytic operations.

However, this focus on the comparative difficulty of isomorphism problems leaves a
different, equally fundamental question unanswered: what is the
intrinsic
logical complexity
of the properties that define these structures? While knowing
that classifying all
C^{*}
-algebras is intractable is crucial, it does not tell us
how difficult it is to determine whether a single, given
C^{*}
-algebra is simple,
finite-dimensional, or admits a tracial state. Many such properties are known to be
Borel measurable in standard codings, but their precise location within the Borel
hierarchy (
\Sigma^{0}_{\alpha},\Pi^{0}_{\alpha}
) — a measure of their descriptive
complexity — has remained largely unexplored.

The present paper develops a unified framework to address precisely this. We construct
explicit Polish parameter spaces for two broad regimes of mathematical structures
— countable algebraic systems and separable Banach-type structures — and use their
topological properties to compute the Borel complexity of key algebraic and analytic
properties. Our approach is grounded in a single methodological principle: parameterise
a class of structures as quotients of a single, universal “quotient generator”.

Why quotients rather than embeddings?

A natural first attempt at a unified coding of separable Banach algebras would be to
fix a single separable Banach algebra
U
and parameterise all separable Banach algebras
as (isomorphic copies of) subalgebras of
U
. However, this is impossible:
there is
no
universal separable Banach algebra for isomorphic embeddings;
indeed, no separable Banach algebra contains an isomorphic copy of every separable
Banach algebra
[
29
]
.
This makes quotient encodings conceptually natural: in many Banach-type categories
there are explicit separable quotient generators (
e.g.

\ell_{1}(S)
for Banach algebras),
and the corresponding kernel/ideal hyperspaces carry canonical Polish topologies.
Our parameter spaces are therefore built from admissible kernels and quotient norms.

•

For separable Banach-type structures
(such as Banach algebras,

C^{*}
-algebras, and Banach lattices), we take as our parameter space the hyperspace of
all admissible ideals in a separable quotient generator
(e.g.
C^{*}_{\max}(\mathsf{F}_{\infty})
for
C^{*}
-algebras). We prove this space is
Polish when endowed with any admissible topology. Crucially, under the Wijsman topology,
the quotient norm functional
K\mapsto\|x+K\|
becomes continuous. This continuity
is the key that unlocks direct complexity calculations.

•

For countable algebraic structures
(such as groups, rings, and lattices),
we use the space of all congruences (or ideals/normal subgroups) on a free object on
countably many generators. This space is a closed subset of a Cantor space, making it
a compact, zero-dimensional Polish space. This quotient-by-congruence viewpoint is
standard-Borel equivalent to the usual Polish space of countable
L
-structures on

\mathbb{N}
used throughout classical descriptive set theory; see

[
33
,
6
,
21
,
27
,
24
]
.

In both settings, the topological structure allows us to formulate a

definability scheme
: any property expressible in a logical language built from the
continuous quotient norm (in the Banach case) or congruence membership (in the countable
case) defines a Borel subset of the parameter space whose rank is determined by its
quantifier complexity. This provides a powerful engine for generating a comprehensive
catalogue of complexity bounds.

Our philosophy is conservative: we aim primarily for clean
upper bounds
which can
be read off from definability. In several cases one can sharpen these bounds, and we do
so where the argument is straightforward.

2.
Preliminaries and notation

Throughout,
X
denotes a separable Banach space. For a non-empty closed set

F\subseteq X
and
x\in X
we write

d(x,F)=\inf\{\|x-y\|:y\in F\}.

If
F
is a closed linear subspace then
d(x,F)=\|x+F\|_{X/F}
.
For each separable space
X
under discussion we fix a countable dense set

D^{X}=\{u_{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

We shall use standard descriptive set theory; see
[
33
,
6
,
24
]
.
We recall the key notions for the reader’s convenience.

2.1.
The Borel and projective hierarchies

Let
X
be a Polish space (separable, completely metrisable).
The
Borel hierarchy
stratifies the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
X
into
countable levels. At the base,

\Sigma^{0}_{1}
denotes the open sets and
\Pi^{0}_{1}
the closed sets.
Inductively, for
n\geqslant 1
:

\Sigma^{0}_{n+1}
consists of countable unions of
\Pi^{0}_{n}
sets, and

\Pi^{0}_{n+1}
consists of countable intersections of
\Sigma^{0}_{n}
sets.
Equivalently,
\Pi^{0}_{n}
is the class of complements of
\Sigma^{0}_{n}
sets.
In low levels:

\Sigma^{0}_{2}=F_{\sigma}
(countable unions of closed sets),

\Pi^{0}_{2}=G_{\delta}
(countable intersections of open sets),

\Sigma^{0}_{3}=G_{\delta\sigma}
, and
\Pi^{0}_{3}=F_{\sigma\delta}
.

Beyond the Borel hierarchy lie the
projective
(or
analytic
) classes.
A set is
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
(
analytic
) if it is the continuous image of a Borel set
in another Polish space, or equivalently the projection of a closed subset of

X\times\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
. A set is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
(
coanalytic
) if its complement is analytic.
Suslin’s theorem asserts that a set is Borel iff it is both analytic and coanalytic.

Hardness and completeness.

Let
\Gamma
be a pointclass (
\Sigma^{0}_{n}
,
\Pi^{0}_{n}
,
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
,
\Pi^{1}_{1}
, etc.).
A set
A\subseteq X
is
\Gamma
-hard
if for every set
B\in\Gamma(Y)
(where
Y

is any Polish space) there exists a continuous map
f:Y\to X
with
f^{-1}(A)=B
;
informally,
A
is “at least as complex as any
\Gamma
set”.
The set
A
is
\Gamma
-complete
if it belongs to
\Gamma
and is
\Gamma
-hard.
Completeness for
\Gamma
implies that
A
does not belong to the dual class

\check{\Gamma}
(e.g. a
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-complete set is not
\Pi^{0}_{2}
, and a

\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete set is not Borel).
Proving a property
\Gamma
-complete therefore establishes that its upper bound
within the hierarchy is
optimal
and cannot be improved.

Connection to definability.

In this paper, upper bounds on Borel complexity arise from
expressing properties via countable quantifier alternations over
continuous predicates. Each universal quantifier (over a countable domain) preserves

\Pi^{0}_{n}
; each existential quantifier preserves
\Sigma^{0}_{n}
; alternation
increments the level by one. Lower bounds (completeness results) are proved
by explicit continuous reductions from canonical complete sets, such as the set of
eventually-zero sequences in
2^{\mathbb{N}}
(
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-complete) or the set of
well-founded trees (
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete).

2.2.
Hyperspaces and admissible topologies

Let
X
be a separable metric space and let
\mathscr{F}(X)
denote the hyperspace of
non-empty closed subsets of
X
.

Definition 2.1

(Wijsman topology)
.

The
Wijsman topology
on
\mathscr{F}(X)
is the coarsest topology making each map

F\mapsto d(x,F)
continuous for every fixed
x\in X
.
Equivalently, a net
(F_{\alpha})
converges to
F
in the Wijsman topology iff

d(x,F_{\alpha})\to d(x,F)
for every
x\in X
; in other words, the distance functions
converge pointwise.

If
X
is separable, the Wijsman topology is metrisable
(indeed, fixing a countable dense set
\{u_{m}\}
one can take

d_{W}(F,G)=\sum_{m}2^{-m}\min\bigl(1,|d(u_{m},F)-d(u_{m},G)|\bigr)

as a compatible metric).
If
X
is Polish, then
(\mathscr{F}(X),\mathrm{Wijsman})
is Polish;
see Beer
[
2
]
.

The Wijsman topology is the natural topology on our parameter spaces throughout
this paper: for Banach-type structures, it makes the quotient-norm functional

K\mapsto\|x+K\|
continuous by definition, and for countable structures
the Wijsman topology on closed subsets of a discrete space coincides with the
Vietoris topology.

Lemma 2.2

(Wijsman approximation)
.

Let
F_{n}\to F
in the Wijsman topology.

(i)

If
x\in F
, there exist
x_{n}\in F_{n}
with
x_{n}\to x
.

(ii)

If
F_{n}\subseteq G_{n}
for all
n
and
G_{n}\to G
(Wijsman), then
F\subseteq G
.

Proof.

(i) Since
d(x,F)=0
and
d(x,F_{n})\to d(x,F)=0
, choose
x_{n}\in F_{n}
with

\|x-x_{n}\|\leqslant d(x,F_{n})+1/n
. Then
\|x-x_{n}\|\to 0
.

(ii) Let
x\in F
. Wijsman convergence
F_{n}\to F
gives
d(x,F_{n})\to d(x,F)=0
.
Since
F_{n}\subseteq G_{n}
for all
n
, we have
d(x,G_{n})\leqslant d(x,F_{n})
for all
n
,
hence
d(x,G_{n})\to 0
. As
G_{n}\to G
in the Wijsman topology,

d(x,G_{n})\to d(x,G)
, so
d(x,G)=0
and therefore
x\in G
.
∎

The Wijsman topology is one instance of a broader class of
hyperspace topologies introduced by Godefroy and
Saint-Raymond
[
26
]
.

Definition 2.3

(Admissible topology
[
26
]
)
.

A Polish topology
\tau
on
\mathscr{F}(X)
is
admissible
if:

(i)

for each open
U\subseteq X
,

E^{+}(U)=\{F\in\mathscr{F}(X):F\cap U\neq\varnothing\}
is
\tau
-open;

(ii)

\tau
has a subbase consisting of countable unions of sets of the form

E^{+}(U)\setminus E^{+}(V)
with
U,V
open in
X
;

(iii)

the membership relation
\{(x,F):x\in F\}\subseteq X\times\mathscr{F}(X)

is
\tau
-closed.

Condition (i) says that the topology detects hits:
if a closed set meets an open region, nearby sets (in the
\tau
-sense) also
do. Condition (ii) is a regularity constraint ensuring
\tau

is generated by set-theoretically definable data.
Condition (iii) is the key geometric requirement: it says precisely that the
singleton embedding
x\mapsto\{x\}
is a homeomorphism from
X
onto a
closed subset of
(\mathscr{F}(X),\tau)
— in other words, points are
“robustly detected” by the topology, and any sequence of closed sets converging
in
\tau
to a point mass
\{x\}
must eventually carry elements near
x
.

The Wijsman topology is admissible.
The first two conditions are standard (see
[
2
]
). For the third:
if
(x_{n},F_{n})\to(x,F)
in
X\times\mathscr{F}(X)
and
x_{n}\in F_{n}
for all
n
,
then
d(x,F_{n})\leqslant d(x,x_{n})+d(x_{n},F_{n})=d(x,x_{n})\to 0
,
so
d(x,F)=\lim_{n}d(x,F_{n})=0
, hence
x\in F
and the membership relation is closed.

Let
\mathrm{SB}(X)
denote the family of closed linear subspaces of
X
. For admissible
\tau
,

\mathrm{SB}(X)
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
(\mathscr{F}(X),\tau)
and hence Polish

[
26
]
.

Proposition 2.4

(
\mathrm{SB}(X)
is Wijsman-closed)
.

Let
F_{n}\in\mathrm{SB}(X)
and
F_{n}\to F
in the Wijsman topology on
\mathscr{F}(X)
.
Then
F\in\mathrm{SB}(X)
; hence
\mathrm{SB}(X)
is closed in
(\mathscr{F}(X),\mathrm{Wijsman})
.

Proof.

By Wijsman convergence,
d(0,F_{n})=0
for all
n
implies
d(0,F)=\lim_{n}d(0,F_{n})=0
,
so
0\in F
.

Let
x,y\in F
and
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
. By the Wijsman approximation lemma, there exist

x_{n},y_{n}\in F_{n}
with
x_{n}\to x
and
y_{n}\to y
in
X
.
Since each
F_{n}
is a linear subspace, we have
z_{n}:=ax_{n}+by_{n}\in F_{n}
.
By continuity of addition and scalar multiplication,
z_{n}\to z:=ax+by
.
Therefore
d(z,F_{n})\leqslant\|z-z_{n}\|\to 0
,
and Wijsman convergence yields

d(z,F)=\lim_{n}d(z,F_{n})=0
, so
z\in F
.
Hence
F
is closed under rational linear combinations.
Finally, for arbitrary
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in F
, choose rationals
t_{k}\to t
;
then
t_{k}x\in F
for all
k
, and since
F
is closed,
tx=\lim_{k}t_{k}x\in F
.
Thus
F
is a closed linear subspace.
∎

Substructure variant.

The same argument does not use linearity per se. If
X
is a separable metric
space equipped with countably many continuous operations

\omega_{i}:X^{n_{i}}\to X
, then the family of all non-empty closed subsets

F\subseteq X
that are closed under each
\omega_{i}
is Wijsman-closed in

\mathscr{F}(X)
. Indeed, if
F_{n}\to F
in the Wijsman topology and each
F_{n}

is a closed substructure, then for any
x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{i}}\in F
one may choose

x_{j}^{(n)}\in F_{n}
with
x_{j}^{(n)}\to x_{j}
by
Lemma
2.2
. Continuity gives

\omega_{i}(x_{1}^{(n)},\dots,x_{n_{i}}^{(n)})\to\omega_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{i}})
, and since

\omega_{i}(x_{1}^{(n)},\dots,x_{n_{i}}^{(n)})\in F_{n}
for all
n
, Wijsman convergence
implies
\omega_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{i}})\in F
. (For nullary operations, the same
argument applies to the distinguished constants.) In particular, for a separable

C^{*}
-algebra
A
, the family of closed
*
-subalgebras of
A
is Wijsman-closed.

Remark 2.5
.

One should
not
expect
\mathrm{SB}(X)
to be
\tau
-closed for every admissible
\tau

in full generality. The defining condition
“
\forall x,y\in F
and
\forall a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
, we have
ax+by\in F
” is universal
and thus naturally produces countable intersections of open conditions, but universal
formulae do not automatically yield closed sets for arbitrary admissible hyperspace
topologies. For Wijsman, however, the above proposition gives closedness.

The key tool for working with admissible topologies is the following selection theorem,
which provides continuous dense selections and is the workhorse that allows us to
pass from the abstract hyperspace to concrete dense-set data.

Theorem 2.6

(Godefroy–Saint-Raymond
[
26
]
)
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space and
\tau
an admissible topology on
\mathrm{SB}(X)
.
There exist continuous maps
f_{k}:(\mathrm{SB}(X),\tau)\to X
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) such that

f_{k}(F)\in F\ \ \text{for all }F\in\mathrm{SB}(X)\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{N},\quad\overline{\{f_{k}(F):k\in\mathbb{N}\}}=F\quad(F\in\mathrm{SB}(X)).

Proof.

See
[
26
]
.
∎

2.3.
Matrix-level quotient norms

In the
C^{*}
-algebra parts of this paper, several arguments require norms
in
matrix amplifications

M_{n}(A/K)
rather than merely in
A/K
itself
(see
[
5
, II.6.6]
or
[
8
, Chapter 1]
for background on
matrix norms and operator-space structure).
We introduce a systematic notation.

Definition 2.7

(
\Phi^{(n)}
: matrix-level quotient norm)
.

Let
A
be a separable
C^{*}
-algebra and
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
.
Because
M_{n}
is nuclear, the canonical identification
M_{n}(A)\cong A\otimes M_{n}

carries
M_{n}(K)=K\otimes M_{n}
isometrically. For
X\in M_{n}(A)
define

\Phi^{(n)}(K,X):=\mathrm{dist}\bigl(X,\,M_{n}(K)\bigr)=\|X+M_{n}(K)\|_{M_{n}(A/K)}.

Lemma 2.8
.

For each fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
X\in M_{n}(A)
, the map

K\mapsto\Phi^{(n)}(K,X)
is continuous in the Wijsman topology on
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
(K_{m})
be a sequence in
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
converging to
K
in the Wijsman topology.
Write
L_{m}:=M_{n}(K_{m})\subseteq M_{n}(A)
and
L:=M_{n}(K)
.

We first observe that for any
a\in A
and standard matrix units

(e_{ij})_{i,j\leqslant n}
in
M_{n}
,

(2.1)

\mathrm{dist}(a\otimes e_{ij},\,L)=\mathrm{dist}(a,K).

Indeed, the coordinate extraction
E_{ij}:M_{n}(A)\to A
,

E_{ij}(Z)=e_{1i}Ze_{j1}
, is contractive, so for any
Y\in L
we have

\|a-E_{ij}(Y)\|\leqslant\|a\otimes e_{ij}-Y\|
.
Since
E_{ij}(Y)\in K
, this gives
\mathrm{dist}(a,K)\leqslant\mathrm{dist}(a\otimes e_{ij},L)
.
Conversely, for any
k\in K
we have
k\otimes e_{ij}\in L
and

\|a\otimes e_{ij}-k\otimes e_{ij}\|=\|a-k\|
, hence

\mathrm{dist}(a\otimes e_{ij},L)\leqslant\mathrm{dist}(a,K)
.

Now fix a countable dense set
D^{A}=\{u_{s}:s\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq A
.
The set

\mathcal{D}:=\{u_{s}\otimes e_{ij}:s\in\mathbb{N},\ 1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n\}

is countable and dense in
M_{n}(A)
.
By (
2.1
) and Wijsman convergence in
A
,

\mathrm{dist}(u_{s}\otimes e_{ij},L_{m})=\mathrm{dist}(u_{s},K_{m})\to\mathrm{dist}(u_{s},K)=\mathrm{dist}(u_{s}\otimes e_{ij},L)

for every element of
\mathcal{D}
.
Since
Z\mapsto\mathrm{dist}(Z,F)
is
1
-Lipschitz for any closed
F\subseteq M_{n}(A)
,
convergence of distance functions on a dense subset implies convergence on all
of
M_{n}(A)
, so
L_{m}\to L
in the Wijsman topology on
\mathscr{F}(M_{n}(A))
.
Evaluating at
X
gives

\Phi^{(n)}(K_{m},X)=\mathrm{dist}(X,L_{m})\to\mathrm{dist}(X,L)=\Phi^{(n)}(K,X)
.
∎

Remark 2.9

2.4.

The following elementary observation is used several times (most explicitly in Proposition

.

3.
Main theorem for separable continuous structures

This section develops the analytic half of our general framework:
we construct Polish parameter spaces for separable Banach-type structures
(such as Banach algebras,
C^{*}
-algebras, Banach lattices, and ternary rings of
operators) and establish a general definability theorem linking algebraic formulae
to Borel complexity. The key idea is to view such structures not abstractly, but
concretely as
quotients
of a single universal object endowed with
finitely or countably many continuous multilinear operations.

3.1.
Categories with continuous operations

Definition 3.1
.

A
concrete category
\mathcal{C}
with continuous operations
consists of:

•

Objects
: Separable Banach spaces
X
equipped with countably many
continuous operations

\omega_{i}:X^{n_{i}}\to X
,
i\in I
(countable), where
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
is the arity
of
\omega_{i}
.

•

Morphisms
: Bounded linear maps
T:X\to Y
preserving all operations:

T(\omega_{i}^{X}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n_{i}}))=\omega_{i}^{Y}(T(x_{1}),\ldots,T(x_{n_{i}})).

•

Quotients
: If
T:X\to Y
is surjective with kernel
K=\ker(T)
,
then
Y\cong X/K
as objects in
\mathcal{C}
, where operations descend to quotients.

Definition 3.2

(Admissible kernels /
\mathcal{C}
-ideals)
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{C}
. A closed linear subspace
K\subseteq X
is an

admissible kernel
(or
\mathcal{C}
-ideal
) if for every
i\in I
and all

x_{1},\ldots,x_{n_{i}},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n_{i}}^{\prime}\in X
with
x_{j}-x_{j}^{\prime}\in K
for

1\leqslant j\leqslant n_{i}
, one has

\omega_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{i}})-\omega_{i}(x_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{n_{i}}^{\prime})\in K.

Equivalently,
\omega_{i}
descends to a well-defined operation on
X/K
;
in the Banach setting, this means that the equivalence relation

E_{K}
on
X
given by
x\,E_{K}\,y
iff
x-y\in K
is a congruence for all the
operations
\omega_{i}
. We write
\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
for the collection of
admissible kernels.

Congruence viewpoint.

The Banach-space formulation is chosen because later arguments exploit the quotient
norm. Abstractly, the same quotient-coding setup can be stated for separable
Polish structures with continuous operations by replacing closed linear subspaces

K\subseteq X
with closed congruences
E\subseteq X^{2}
. In the Banach setting,

K
and
E_{K}
are interchangeable, and Section
4
is the
discrete counterpart of this congruence-based viewpoint.

Remark 3.3

(Why the general framework is written in Banach form)
.

Up to this point, the definitions and closure arguments admit a parallel
formulation for arbitrary separable Polish structures with continuous operations,
using closed congruences
E\subseteq X^{2}
rather than kernels
K\subseteq X
.
We do not develop that notation systematically in the sequel only because the
later applications repeatedly use quotient norms, distance-to-kernel functions,
and linear perturbation arguments. Thus the paper is written in Banach form for
economy of notation, but whenever a later argument is purely formal it may be read
equally in the congruence language.

Example 3.4
.

We list canonical examples fitting the framework of
Definition
3.1
.

(i)

Banach algebras.

\omega_{1}(x,y)=xy
(multiplication).
Admissible kernels are closed two-sided ideals.

(ii)

C^{*}
-algebras.

\omega_{1}(x,y)=xy
,
\omega_{2}(x)=x^{*}
.
Admissible kernels are closed
*
-ideals.

(iii)

Banach lattices.

\omega_{1}(x,y)=x\vee y
,
\omega_{2}(x,y)=x\wedge y
,
\omega_{3}(x)=|x|
.
Admissible kernels are closed lattice ideals.

(iv)

Ternary rings of operators (TROs).

\omega_{1}(x,y,z)=[x,y,z]=xy^{*}z
.
Admissible kernels are closed ternary ideals.

(v)

Operator systems (not treated here).

Operator systems do not fit Definition
3.1
as stated.
Their natural quotient theory is by completely order ideals (kernels of unital
completely positive maps), and an Archimedeanisation step is generally needed to
form the quotient operator system
[
34
]
. We therefore exclude operator
systems from the present framework.

(vi)

Banach
A
-modules (encoding caveat).

Fix a separable Banach algebra
A
and a countable dense subset

(a_{m})
of its unit ball.
A left Banach
A
-module may be encoded by the countable family of
unary operations
\omega_{m}(x)=a_{m}x
. We do not use this variant below.

(vii)

Jordan–Banach algebras (JB or JB
∗
).

The fundamental operation is the Jordan product

\omega_{1}(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{2}(xy+yx)
; in the JB
∗
case one adds

\omega_{2}(x)=x^{*}
.
Admissible kernels are closed Jordan ideals.

(viii)

Banach–Lie algebras.

\omega_{1}(x,y)=[x,y]
(the Lie bracket).
Admissible kernels are closed Lie ideals.

(ix)

Operator spaces (encoding caveat).

Operator spaces are naturally described by a countable family of matrix-norm
predicates, rather than only by operations
X^{n_{i}}\to X
. They therefore fall
outside the literal scope of Definition
3.1
; a multi-sorted
or predicate-enriched version of the framework should cover them.

Remark 3.5

(Banach lattices)
.

While Banach lattices fit the general quotient-kernel framework at the level of
closed lattice ideals, the descriptive set-theoretic analysis of the natural Polish
parameter spaces of separable Banach lattices involves additional lattice-specific
topological subtleties. These will be treated separately in forthcoming work of
M. Niwiński
[
37
]
.

3.2.
Closedness of the admissible-kernel space

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{C}
be separable and let
\tau
be an admissible topology on
\mathrm{SB}(X)
.
Then
\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is
\tau
-closed in
\mathrm{SB}(X)
, hence Polish.

Proof.

Fix a countable dense set
D^{X}=\{u_{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
. By Theorem
2.6
, there
exist continuous selections
f_{k}:(\mathrm{SB}(X),\tau)\to X
with
f_{k}(F)\in F
and

\overline{\{f_{k}(F):k\in\mathbb{N}\}}=F
for all
F\in\mathrm{SB}(X)
.

For
i\in I
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant n_{i}
, and
\bar{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n_{i}})\in\mathbb{N}^{n_{i}}
define
a continuous map
\Delta_{i,j,\bar{m}}:X\to X
by

\Delta_{i,j,\bar{m}}(x):=\omega_{i}(u_{m_{1}},\dots,u_{m_{j-1}},u_{m_{j}}+x,u_{m_{j+1}},\dots,u_{m_{n_{i}}})-\omega_{i}(u_{m_{1}},\dots,u_{m_{n_{i}}}).

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
set

\mathcal{K}_{i,j,\bar{m},k}:=\bigl\{F\in\mathrm{SB}(X):\ \Delta_{i,j,\bar{m}}\bigl(f_{k}(F)\bigr)\in F\bigr\}.

The map
F\mapsto\Delta_{i,j,\bar{m}}(f_{k}(F))
is continuous since

f_{k}
and
\Delta_{i,j,\bar{m}}
are continuous.
By admissibility, the membership relation
\{(x,F):x\in F\}\subseteq X\times\mathrm{SB}(X)

is
\tau
-closed, hence each
\mathcal{K}_{i,j,\bar{m},k}
is
\tau
-closed.

Set
\mathcal{K}=\bigcap_{i,j,\bar{m},k}\mathcal{K}_{i,j,\bar{m},k}
,
a countable intersection of
\tau
-closed sets, hence
\tau
-closed.
We show
\mathcal{K}=\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
.

Suppose that
F\in\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
. Then
f_{k}(F)\in F
for all
k

(by Theorem
2.6
), so each defining condition of
\mathcal{K}_{i,j,\bar{m},k}
holds.
Hence
F\in\mathcal{K}
.

Conversely, let
F\in\mathcal{K}
. Fix
i
,
j
, elements

x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{i}}\in X
, and
h\in F
.
Choose
u_{m_{\ell}(t)}\to x_{\ell}
with
u_{m_{\ell}(t)}\in D^{X}
,
and choose
f_{k(t)}(F)\to h
(this is possible because
f_{k}(F)\in F
for all
k
and
the selections
\{f_{k}(F):k\in\mathbb{N}\}
are dense in
F
by Theorem
2.6
).
Since
F\in\mathcal{K}
, for each
t
,

\Delta_{i,j,\bar{m}(t)}\bigl(f_{k(t)}(F)\bigr)\in F,

where
\bar{m}(t)=(m_{1}(t),\dots,m_{n_{i}}(t))
.
By continuity of
\omega_{i}
and closedness of
F
, the limit

\omega_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j}+h,\dots,x_{n_{i}})-\omega_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{i}})

lies in
F
. This establishes invariance under perturbation of one coordinate by an element of
F
.
To deduce the full admissible-kernel condition
(
x_{j}-x_{j}^{\prime}\in F
for all
j
implies that indeed

\omega_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{i}})-\omega_{i}(x_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{n_{i}}^{\prime})\in F
),
telescope over coordinates: replace
x_{1}
by
x_{1}^{\prime}
while fixing all other entries
(applying the single-coordinate invariance), then replace
x_{2}
by
x_{2}^{\prime}
, and so on.
After
n_{i}
applications,
F
absorbs the full difference.
Hence
F\in\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
.

∎

3.3.
Quotient norms and definability

Definition 3.7

(Quotient-norm functional)
.

For
x\in X
and
F\in\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
, define

\Phi(F,x)=d(x,F)=\inf_{z\in F}\|x-z\|.

If
F
is a subspace,
\Phi(F,x)=\|x+F\|_{X/F}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Under the Wijsman topology, for each fixed
x\in X
, the map

F\mapsto\Phi(F,x)
is continuous on
\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
.

Proof.

Immediate from Definition
2.1
: Wijsman convergence

F_{n}\to F
means
d(x,F_{n})\to d(x,F)
for all
x
.
∎

Lemma 3.9

(Subbasic Wijsman sets)
.

Let
X
be separable and let
\tau_{W}
be the Wijsman topology on
\mathscr{F}(X)
.
For
x\in X
and
r\in\mathbb{Q}
the sets

U(x,r):=\{F:\ d(x,F)<r\},\qquad V(x,r):=\{F:\ d(x,F)\leqslant r\}

are
\tau_{W}
-open and
\tau_{W}
-closed respectively.
If
F\subseteq X
is a closed subspace, then
d(x,F)=\Phi(F,x)
.

Proof.

For each fixed
x
, the map
F\mapsto d(x,F)
is continuous (Definition
2.1
).
Thus
U(x,r)
is the preimage of the open ray
(-\infty,r)
and
V(x,r)
is the
preimage of the closed ray
(-\infty,r]
.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{C}
be separable with countably many
operations
\omega_{i}
of finite arity. There exists a countable dense subset

D\subseteq X
closed under all operations
\omega_{i}
and under rational
linear combinations.

Proof.

Start with any countable dense
D_{0}
. Define inductively
D_{n+1}
to be the
set obtained from
D_{n}
by closing under all operations
\omega_{i}
applied to tuples
from

.
∎

Definition 3.11
.

An object
P\in\mathcal{C}
is a
separable quotient generator
if

P
is separable and every separable object
Y\in\mathcal{C}
is isomorphic to

P/K
for some
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)
.

Remark 3.12
.

In the categorical literature, a
projective generator
additionally satisfies
a lifting property with respect to epimorphisms. We do not require such a lifting
property; our arguments use only the quotient-universality encoded in
Definition

. We therefore use the more descriptive term
‘separable quotient generator’ throughout.

3.4.
Main theorem and definability scheme

Main Theorem 3.13

(Polish parameter space for continuous structures)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a concrete category with continuous operations admitting a
separable quotient generator
P
. Fix:

•

a countable dense subset
D^{P}=\{u_{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq P
closed under all
operations
\omega_{i}
and possible rational (or complex-rational, depending on the ground field) linear combinations
1
1

1

Formally, there may be no rational/complex-rational vector space structure in which case this condition becomes vacuous.
;

•

an admissible topology
\tau
on
\mathrm{SB}(P)
(e.g. Wijsman).

Then:

(i)

(Polishness.)

\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)
is a Polish space with the topology
\tau
.

(ii)

(Borel encoding.)

Define a code
c(K)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
for
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)
by
concatenating all values

\Phi\Bigl(K,\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}q_{j}u_{m_{j}}\Bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi\bigl(K,\omega_{i}(u_{m_{1}},\dots,u_{m_{n_{i}}})-u_{r}\bigr),

enumerated over all rational data. For any admissible topology
\tau
,
each coordinate
K\mapsto\Phi(K,x)=d(x,K)
is upper semicontinuous
(being the infimum of continuous functions via the
Godefroy–Saint-Raymond selections), hence Borel, and

c:\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
is Borel.
Under the
Wijsman
topology, each coordinate is continuous.

(iii)

(Faithfulness.)

If
c(K)=c(L)
, then
K=L
. In particular,
P/K\cong P/L
as objects in
\mathcal{C}

via the unique isometric isomorphism sending
u_{m}+K
to
u_{m}+L
.
(
Proof:

c(K)=c(L)
implies
\Phi(K,u_{m})=\Phi(L,u_{m})
for all
m
, hence

d(\cdot,K)=d(\cdot,L)
on a dense set, hence everywhere, so
K=L
.)

(iv)

(Surjectivity.)

Every separable object
Y\in\mathcal{C}
is isomorphic to
P/K
for some

K\in\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)
. In particular,
c
gives a Borel coding of
quotient presentations of separable objects in
\mathcal{C}
.
We do
not
claim injectivity on isomorphism classes: distinct kernels may
still yield isomorphic quotients.

(v)

(Definability scheme.)

Any property
\mathcal{P}
of
P/K
expressible by a formula built from:

•

atomic predicates of the form

F(\Phi(K,x_{1}),\ldots,\Phi(K,x_{m}))\bowtie r
,
where
x_{j}\in D^{P}
,
F:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous,

\bowtie\in\{<,\leqslant,=,\geqslant,>\}
, and
r\in\mathbb{Q}
;

•

Boolean connectives;

•

countable quantification over variables in
D^{P}
or
\mathbb{N}
;

defines a Borel subset of
\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)
for
any
admissible
topology
\tau
. Under the
Wijsman
topology specifically,
the Borel rank is bounded by the quantifier alternation depth:

\forall
alone gives
\Pi^{0}_{2}
,

\forall\exists
gives
\Pi^{0}_{3}
,

\forall\exists\forall
gives
\Pi^{0}_{4}
, and so on.

Proof.

Part (1) is Proposition
3.6
.
For (2), each coordinate map
K\mapsto d(x,K)
is upper semicontinuous for any
admissible topology (as the infimum of continuous Godefroy–Saint-Raymond selections),
hence Borel; under the Wijsman topology it is continuous by
Lemma
3.8
.
Part (3): as argued in the statement,
c(K)=c(L)
forces
K=L
.
Part (4) holds because
P
is a quotient generator.
Part (5): for any admissible
\tau
, each atomic predicate is Borel
(since
K\mapsto\Phi(K,x)
is u.s.c. by (2)),
Boolean combinations and countable quantification preserve Borelness.
Under the Wijsman topology, each
\Phi
-coordinate is continuous,
so each atomic predicate is open (for
<,>
)
or closed (for
\leqslant,\geqslant,=
), and countable
quantification over a countable set raises the Borel rank by at most one per alternation.
∎

Definition 3.14

(Stable relations on quotients)
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a finite family of
\mathcal{C}
-terms

t_{1}(\bar{x}),\dots,t_{r}(\bar{x})
in variables
\bar{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
.
For an object
Y\in\mathcal{C}
and
\bar{y}\in Y^{m}
set the
defect

\mathrm{def}_{\bar{t}}^{Y}(\bar{y}):=\max_{\ell\leqslant r}\|t_{\ell}^{Y}(\bar{y})\|.

Given
M\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that the relation

\Sigma_{\bar{t},M}(\bar{x}):\ \ \bigl(\|x_{j}\|\leqslant M\ (1\leqslant j\leqslant m)\bigr)\ \wedge\ \bigl(t_{1}(\bar{x})=\cdots=t_{r}(\bar{x})=0\bigr)

is
stable on quotients of
P
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

\delta>0
such that for every
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)
and every

\bar{y}\in(P/K)^{m}
with
\|y_{j}\|\leqslant M
,

\mathrm{def}_{\bar{t}}^{P/K}(\bar{y})<\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists\bar{z}\in(P/K)^{m}:\ \Sigma_{\bar{t},M}(\bar{z})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \max_{j}\|y_{j}-z_{j}\|<\varepsilon.

Lemma 3.15

(Stable existence is witnessed on the dense set)
.

Assume
\Sigma_{\bar{t},M}
is stable on quotients of
P
.
Fix
\varepsilon=1
and let
\delta>0
be the corresponding stability constant.
Then for every
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)
the following are equivalent:

(i)

there exists
\bar{x}\in(P/K)^{m}
with
\Sigma_{\bar{t},M}(\bar{x})
;

(ii)

there exists
\bar{u}\in(D^{P})^{m}
such that

\max_{j}\Phi(K,u_{j})<M
and
\mathrm{def}_{\bar{t}}^{P/K}(\bar{u}+K)<\delta
.

In particular, the set of
K
satisfying
\exists\bar{x}\,\Sigma_{\bar{t},M}(\bar{x})

is
open
in the Wijsman topology on
\mathrm{Ideal}_{\mathcal{C}}(P)
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2).
Let
\bar{x}\in(P/K)^{m}
be an exact solution with
\|x_{j}\|\leqslant M
.
By continuity of the finitely many term maps
t_{\ell}^{P/K}
at
\bar{x}
,
choose
\eta>0
such that

\max_{j}\|y_{j}-x_{j}\|<\eta\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{def}_{\bar{t}}^{P/K}(\bar{y})<\delta.

Pick
\bar{v}\in(D^{P})^{m}
with
\max_{j}\|v_{j}+K-x_{j}\|<\eta/4
.
Then for each
j
,

\Phi(K,v_{j})=\|v_{j}+K\|\leqslant\|x_{j}\|+\eta/4\leqslant M+\eta/4.

For each
j
, if
\Phi(K,v_{j})<M
set
u_{j}=v_{j}
.
Otherwise
M\leqslant\Phi(K,v_{j})\leqslant M+\eta/4
.
Consider the open interval

\Bigl(1-\frac{\eta/2}{\Phi(K,v_{j})},\,\frac{M}{\Phi(K,v_{j})}\Bigr).

Its length equals

\frac{M-\Phi(K,v_{j})+\eta/2}{\Phi(K,v_{j})}\ \geqslant\ \frac{M-(M+\eta/4)+\eta/2}{\Phi(K,v_{j})}\ =\ \frac{\eta/4}{\Phi(K,v_{j})}\ >\ 0,

so the interval is non-empty. Choose a rational
\lambda_{j}
in this interval and set

u_{j}=\lambda_{j}v_{j}\in D^{P}
(using closure of
D^{P}
under rational scalars).
Then
\Phi(K,u_{j})=\lambda_{j}\Phi(K,v_{j})<M
and

\|u_{j}+K-(v_{j}+K)\|=|1-\lambda_{j}|\Phi(K,v_{j})<\eta/2.

Hence

\|u_{j}+K-x_{j}\|\leqslant\|u_{j}+K-(v_{j}+K)\|+\|v_{j}+K-x_{j}\|<\eta/2+\eta/4<\eta,

and therefore
\mathrm{def}_{\bar{t}}^{P/K}(\bar{u}+K)<\delta
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1).
Let
\bar{u}\in(D^{P})^{m}
satisfy the inequalities in (2), and set
\bar{y}=\bar{u}+K
.
Then
\|y_{j}\|=\Phi(K,u_{j})<M
and
\mathrm{def}_{\bar{t}}^{P/K}(\bar{y})<\delta
.
By stability there exists
\bar{z}
with
\Sigma_{\bar{t},M}(\bar{z})
.

Finally, for fixed
\bar{u}
the conditions

\max_{j}\Phi(K,u_{j})<M
and
\mathrm{def}_{\bar{t}}^{P/K}(\bar{u}+K)<\delta

are strict inequalities of continuous functions of
K
, hence open.
Taking the union over countably many
\bar{u}\in(D^{P})^{m}
yields openness.
∎

Theorem 3.16

(Borel evaluation with stable relations; rank bounds)
.

Work in the setting of Theorem
3.13
with the Wijsman topology.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be the language generated by:

(a)

quotient-norm atoms:

predicates of the form

(b)

.

Build formulae from these atoms using Boolean connectives and countable quantifiers
over

Then for every such formula

Proof.

By Lemma

Quotient-norm atoms of type (a) are therefore either open (for

∎

Remark 3.17
.

Remark 3.18
.

4.
Main theorem for countable algebraic structures

4.1.
Congruences on free algebras

Let
L=\{f_{i}:i\in I\}
be a countable finitary signature and let
F_{\infty}
be the
free
L
-algebra on generators
\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

Theorem 4.1

(Closedness of free-object parameter spaces)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a finitary algebraic variety over a countable signature

L=\{f_{i}:i\in I\}
. Let
F_{\infty}
be the free
L
-algebra on the countable set of
generators
\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Consider the following Polish spaces, all with the
product (Cantor) topology:

(i)

Congruences (general case).

\mathrm{Con}_{L}(F_{\infty})\subseteq 2^{F_{\infty}\times F_{\infty}}
, the family of

L
-congruences on
F_{\infty}
.

(ii)

Groups.

\mathrm{NSub}(F_{\infty})\subseteq 2^{F_{\infty}}
, the family of normal subgroups of the
free group on countably many generators,
F_{\infty}=\langle x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\rangle
.

(iii)

Rings.

\mathrm{Id}(R_{\infty})\subseteq 2^{R_{\infty}}
, the family of two-sided ideals in the
free unital ring generated by countably many free variables,
R_{\infty}=\mathbb{Z}\langle X_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\rangle
.

(iv)

Abelian groups.

\mathrm{Sub}(\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})})\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}}
, the family of subgroups of

\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}e_{n}
.

(v)

Lattices.

\mathrm{Con}_{\wedge,\vee}(L_{\infty})\subseteq 2^{L_{\infty}\times L_{\infty}}
, the family of
lattice congruences on the free lattice
L_{\infty}
.

Then each of the above parameter spaces is a
closed
subset of the ambient
Cantor cube, hence compact, zero-dimensional, and Polish.

Proof.

We treat (1) in full generality; the remaining cases are specialisations.

A congruence
R\subseteq F_{\infty}\times F_{\infty}
must be an equivalence relation
compatible with each operation. Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity are
universal Horn conditions: for instance, transitivity states that for all
a,b,c
,
if
(a,b)\in R
and
(b,c)\in R
then
(a,c)\in R
; the violation for a fixed
triple
(a,b,c)
forms the clopen cylinder

\{R:(a,b)\in R,\ (b,c)\in R,\ (a,c)\notin R\}
.
Taking complements and intersecting over all triples yields a closed set.

For
L
-compatibility, fix an operation
f_{i}
of arity
n_{i}
and tuples

\bar{a},\bar{b}\in F_{\infty}^{n_{i}}
. The violating set

\{R:\bigwedge_{j}(a_{j},b_{j})\in R\text{ but }(f_{i}(\bar{a}),f_{i}(\bar{b}))\notin R\}

depends on finitely many coordinates and is clopen. Taking complements and
intersecting gives a closed set.

Thus
\mathrm{Con}_{L}(F_{\infty})
is a countable intersection of closed sets, hence closed
in
2^{F_{\infty}\times F_{\infty}}
. By compactness of the Cantor cube,

\mathrm{Con}_{L}(F_{\infty})
is compact Polish.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In the group case the subclass of
abelian groups
is the closed subspace

\{N\in\mathrm{NSub}(F_{\infty}):[F_{\infty},F_{\infty}]\subseteq N\}

=\bigcap_{g,h\in F_{\infty}}\{N:[g,h]\in N\}
,
and in the ring case
commutative rings
form the closed subspace

\{I\in\mathrm{Id}(R_{\infty}):\forall a,b,\ ab-ba\in I\}
.

4.2.
Canonical encoding of quotients on a fixed domain

Definition 4.3
.

For a subobject
S
of the appropriate kind (congruence, normal subgroup, ideal, or
subgroup) such that
F_{\infty}/S
is
countably infinite
,
we encode the quotient
canonically on the fixed domain
\mathbb{N}
as follows. For each
a\in F_{\infty}
, let

[a]_{S}
be its
S
-coset or
S
-class. Define

\mathrm{Min}_{S}=\{\min([a]_{S}):a\in F_{\infty}\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
,
the set of minimal representatives.
Since
F_{\infty}/S
is infinite,
\mathrm{Min}_{S}
is infinite;
let
\rho_{S}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathrm{Min}_{S}
enumerate
\mathrm{Min}_{S}
increasingly and let
q_{S}:F_{\infty}\to\mathbb{N}

map each element to the index of its class representative. For each basic operation

f_{i}
of arity
n_{i}
, define

f_{i}^{S}(k_{1},\dots,k_{n_{i}})=q_{S}\bigl(f_{i}^{F_{\infty}}(\rho_{S}(k_{1}),\dots,\rho_{S}(k_{n_{i}}))\bigr).

Then
\Theta(S)=(f_{i}^{S})_{i\in I}
is an
L
-structure on
\mathbb{N}
isomorphic
to
F_{\infty}/S
.

Finite quotients.
If
F_{\infty}/S
is finite (with
n
elements, say),
\mathrm{Min}_{S}
has exactly

n
elements and
\rho_{S}
is not defined as a map
\mathbb{N}\to\mathrm{Min}_{S}
.
The canonical encoding
\Theta
is therefore restricted to
infinite quotients; finite quotients are coded directly
by
S
(which determines the quotient structure up to isomorphism).

Lemma 4.4
.

On the
G_{\delta}
subspace

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}:=\{S\in\mathcal{S}:F_{\infty}/S\text{ is infinite}\}
,
the maps
S\mapsto\rho_{S}
and
S\mapsto q_{S}
are Borel, and hence
\Theta
is Borel.

Proof.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The value
\rho_{S}(k)
is the least
a\in\mathbb{N}
such that the family of

S
-classes met by
\{0,1,\ldots,a\}
has size
k{+}1
.

For
b,b^{\prime}\in F_{\infty}
, the predicate “
b
and
b^{\prime}
represent the same element of
F_{\infty}/S
”
is clopen in the Cantor topology on
\mathcal{S}
, since it is one of:

•

(b,b^{\prime})\in S
if
S
is a congruence on
F_{\infty}
;

•

bb^{\prime-1}\in S
if
S
is a normal subgroup of
F_{\infty}
;

•

b-b^{\prime}\in S
if
S
is an additive subgroup/ideal (abelian groups or rings).

Therefore the relation “among
\{0,\ldots,a\}
there are at least
k{+}1
distinct
S
-classes”
is Borel in
S
for each fixed
(a,k)
, and so
S\mapsto\rho_{S}(k)
is Borel.

Similarly, for fixed
a\in F_{\infty}
, the value
q_{S}(a)
is determined by finitely many
tests of whether
a
is equivalent (mod
S
) to
\rho_{S}(j)
for
j\leqslant a
, so
S\mapsto q_{S}(a)
is Borel.
∎

Main Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
and
F_{\infty}
be as above, and let
\mathcal{S}
denote any of the
closed coding spaces from Theorem
4.1
.
Set

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}:=\{S\in\mathcal{S}:\ F_{\infty}/S\text{ is infinite}\}.

Then:

(i)

\mathcal{S}
is a compact zero-dimensional Polish space, and
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}

is a
G_{\delta}
subspace of
\mathcal{S}
(hence Polish).

(ii)

The map

\Theta:\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Str}_{L}(\mathbb{N})=\prod_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}^{n_{i}}},\qquad S\mapsto(f_{i}^{S})_{i\in I},

is Borel.

(iii)

The induced map from
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
to the set of isomorphism classes
of countably infinite
L
-algebras in
\mathcal{V}
is surjective. Equivalently,
for every countably infinite
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{V}
there exists

S\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
with
\Theta(S)\cong\mathbf{A}
.

(iv)

For each first-order sentence
\varphi
in the language
L
, the set

\{S\in\mathcal{S}:\ F_{\infty}/S\models\varphi\}

is Borel in
\mathcal{S}
.
Quantifier-free sentences yield clopen sets; one leading existential quantifier
yields open; one leading universal quantifier yields closed; alternations give the
expected Borel ranks.

Proof.

Part (1):
\mathcal{S}
is compact Polish by Theorem
4.1
.
Moreover,

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{S:\ F_{\infty}/S\text{ has at least }n\text{ classes}\}

is a countable intersection of open sets, hence
G_{\delta}
.

Part (2) is Lemma
4.4
.
Part (3) holds by the universal property of the free object.
Part (4): atomic formulae are decided by finitely many coordinates of
S
,
hence clopen. Existential quantification adds a countable union; universal
quantification adds a countable intersection; further alternations raise the Borel rank accordingly.
∎

Remark 4.6

(Relation to the standard Polish space of countable structures)
.

For a countable finitary signature
L=\{f_{i}:i\in I\}
, a classical parameter space for
countable

\mathrm{Str}_{L}(\mathbb{N}):=\prod_{i\in I}\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}^{n_{i}}},

equipped with the product of the discrete topologies, where a point

\mathbf{A}=(f_{i}^{\mathbf{A}})_{i\in I}
represents the
L

\mathbb{N}
with
basic operations
(f_{i}^{\mathbf{A}})
.
The descriptive set theory of countable structures is typically developed in this
coding (together with the natural logic action of
S_{\infty}
and its orbit equivalence
relation of isomorphism); see, for instance,
Kechris
[

]
, Becker–Kechris
[
6
]
,

[

]

[

]
, and Gao
[

]
.

Our quotient coding via congruences on the free algebra

(Recall that
\mathrm{Str}_{L}(\mathbb{N})

\mathrm{Str}_{L}(\mathbb{N})

Concretely, the map

Remark 4.7

(Novikov’s implicit coding viewpoint)
.

In algorithmic group theory, Novikov’s proof of the existence of finitely presented

From a modern descriptive-set-theoretic perspective, a (finite or countable) group

In this sense, Novikov’s presentation-based arguments may be viewed (in hindsight) as

(

as in

At the same time, Novikov’s paper is not phrased in the language of Polish spaces or

definability and classification problems belongs to the later descriptive set theory
literature (

.
[

,
6
,
27
,
24
]
).

Remark 4.8

(Discrete versus continuous commutativity)

For countable rings coded by congruences, commutativity is

\forall a,b,\ (ab,ba)\in\theta

For Banach algebras the corresponding condition

6.
Banach algebras: complexity bounds

Let
A
be a fixed separable Banach-algebra quotient generator and work on

\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
with the Wijsman topology.
Fix a countable dense multiplicatively closed
D^{A}
.

Remark 6.1

(Admissible versus Wijsman complexity)
.

All admissible topologies on a given ideal space generate the same standard Borel

\sigma
-algebra
[
26
]
. In particular, the statement
“property
\mathcal{P}
is Borel” is
topology-independent
: it holds for
one admissible topology iff it holds for all.
However, the
Borel rank
— the precise level
\Sigma^{0}_{\alpha}
or

\Pi^{0}_{\alpha}
at which
\mathcal{P}
sits — depends on the choice of topology,
because it involves the interplay between open and closed sets, which can differ
from one admissible topology to another.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, all references to specific Borel classes

\Sigma^{0}_{\alpha}
,
\Pi^{0}_{\alpha}
throughout
Sections
6
–
7
are with respect to
the Wijsman topology on the relevant ideal space
\mathrm{Ideal}(P)
. The Wijsman topology
is the natural choice for rank computations because it makes the quotient-norm
functional
\Phi
continuous (not merely Borel), so that strict inequalities in

\Phi
define open sets and non-strict inequalities define closed sets.

Convention 6.2

(Proper quotients for unital properties)
.

Whenever a property below explicitly involves the unit
(topological stable rank, direct finiteness, stable finiteness, simplicity, tracial
states, and so on), we tacitly restrict to proper ideals
K\subsetneq A
.
Equivalently, we ignore the singleton corresponding to the zero quotient.
Since that singleton is closed, none of the stated upper bounds is affected.

6.1.
Commutativity and uniformity

Proposition 6.3
.

The set of
K
such that
A/K
is commutative is closed (so in particular
\Pi^{0}_{2}
) in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

A/K
is commutative if and only if
\Phi(K,uv-vu)=0
for all
u,v\in D^{A}
.
Each condition is closed and we take a countable intersection.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

The set of
K
such that
A/K
is a uniform algebra is
\Pi^{0}_{2}
in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Uniformity is
\|z^{2}\|=\|z\|^{2}
for all
z
. It suffices to test
z\in D^{A}
.
Each condition
\Phi(K,u^{2})=\Phi(K,u)^{2}
defines a closed subset, and the intersection
over
u\in D^{A}
is a countable intersection of closed sets.
∎

6.2.
Right topological stable rank

The
topological stable rank
of a unital Banach algebra
B
, denoted
\mathrm{tsr}(B)
,
is the smallest integer
n\geqslant 1
such that the set of right-unimodular
n
-tuples

U_{n}(B)
is dense in
B^{n}
(see Rieffel
[
42
]
). For
C^{*}
-algebras, the
topological stable rank coincides with the Bass stable
rank
[
28
]
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The set of
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
such that
A/K
is unital and

\mathrm{tsr}(A/K)\leqslant n

is
\Pi^{0}_{3}
in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Write
B=A/K
. The condition
\mathrm{tsr}(B)\leqslant n
means
U_{n}(B)
is dense in
B^{n}
.
Since
D^{A}
is dense in
A
, it suffices to approximate elements of
(D^{A})^{n}
.
Thus
\mathrm{tsr}(B)\leqslant n
holds iff

\forall\bar{a}\in(D^{A})^{n}\;\forall m\in\mathbb{N}\;\exists\bar{b}\in(D^{A})^{n}:\ \max_{j}\Phi(K,a_{j}-b_{j})<\frac{1}{m}\ \wedge\ \bar{b}+K\in U_{n}(B).

The unimodularity condition
\bar{b}+K\in U_{n}(B)
is equivalent to the existence
of an approximate right inverse:

\exists\bar{v}\in(D^{A})^{n}:\ \Phi\Bigl(K,\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}v_{j}-1\Bigr)<1,

which is an open condition in
K
(a countable union of strict
\Phi
-inequalities).
The combined closeness and unimodularity conditions are therefore open
(a finite intersection of open sets). The overall formula has the quantifier pattern

\forall\bar{a}\;\forall m\;\exists\bar{b}\;\exists\bar{v}\;(\text{open})
, which defines a
\Pi^{0}_{3}
set.
∎

6.3.
Uniformly open multiplication

Uniformly open multiplication in Banach algebras has been studied
in connection with topological stable rank and differential
embeddings; see
[
14
,
30
]
.

Definition 6.6
.

Let

\mathcal{D}:=\{\delta:(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\to(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}:\delta\text{ is non-decreasing}\}.

We equip the range
(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
with the discrete topology and endow

\mathcal{D}
with the subspace topology inherited from the product space

\prod_{r\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}}\bigl((0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\bigr)_{\mathrm{disc}}.

Remark 6.7
.

The space
\mathcal{D}
is Polish. Indeed, since
(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
is countably infinite,
the ambient product

\prod_{r\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}}\bigl((0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\bigr)_{\mathrm{disc}}

is homeomorphic to the Baire space
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Moreover,

\mathcal{D}=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}\\
r\leqslant s\end{subarray}}\{\delta:\delta(r)\leqslant\delta(s)\},

and for each fixed pair
(r,s)
the set on the right is closed in the product topology
(since the coordinate spaces are discrete). Hence
\mathcal{D}
is a closed subspace of
a Polish space, and therefore Polish.

Definition 6.8

(Uniformly almost open and uniformly open)
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces and let
f:X\to Y
be continuous.
Fix
\delta\in\mathcal{D}
.

(i)

We say that
f
is
\delta
-uniformly almost open
if for every

r\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
and every
x\in X
,

B_{Y}\bigl(f(x),r\bigr)\subseteq\overline{f\bigl(B_{X}(x,\delta(r))\bigr)}.

(ii)

We say that
f
is
\delta
-uniformly open
if for every

r\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
and every
x\in X
,

B_{Y}\bigl(f(x),r\bigr)\subseteq f\bigl(B_{X}(x,\delta(r))\bigr).

(iii)

We say that
f
is
uniformly almost open
(resp.
uniformly open
)
if it is
\delta
-uniformly almost open (resp.
\delta
-uniformly open) for some

\delta\in\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma 6.9

(Schauder)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces and let
f:X\to Y
be continuous.
If
X
is complete and
f
is uniformly almost open, then
f
is uniformly open.

Proof.

This is Schauder’s lemma; see the author and Draga
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

Lemma 6.10

(Continuity of products of balls)
.

Let
B
be a Banach algebra and let
m:B\times B\to B
be multiplication.
Equip
B\times B
with the max metric

d_{\infty}\bigl((a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\bigr):=\max\{\|a-a^{\prime}\|,\|b-b^{\prime}\|\}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and define a set-valued map

F_{\varepsilon}:B\times B\to\mathscr{F}(B),\qquad F_{\varepsilon}(a,b):=\overline{m\bigl(B_{\varepsilon}(a)\times B_{\varepsilon}(b)\bigr)}.

Then
F_{\varepsilon}
is Wijsman-continuous: for each fixed
y\in B
the map

(a,b)\mapsto\mathrm{dist}\bigl(y,\,F_{\varepsilon}(a,b)\bigr)

is continuous on
B\times B
.

Proof.

Let
(a_{n},b_{n})\to(a,b)
in
d_{\infty}
and set
u_{n}:=a_{n}-a
,
v_{n}:=b_{n}-b
.
Fix
x\in B_{\varepsilon}(a)
and
y\in B_{\varepsilon}(b)
. Then

x+u_{n}\in B_{\varepsilon}(a_{n})
,
y+v_{n}\in B_{\varepsilon}(b_{n})
, and

(x+u_{n})(y+v_{n})-xy=xv_{n}+u_{n}y+u_{n}v_{n}.

Since
x
and
y
range over the bounded sets
B_{\varepsilon}(a)
and
B_{\varepsilon}(b)
,
there is a constant
C=C(a,b,\varepsilon)
such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in B_{\varepsilon}(a)\\
y\in B_{\varepsilon}(b)\end{subarray}}\|(x+u_{n})(y+v_{n})-xy\|\leqslant C\bigl(\|u_{n}\|+\|v_{n}\|\bigr)+\|u_{n}\|\,\|v_{n}\|\to 0.

The same estimate holds in the reverse direction (shifting from
(a_{n},b_{n})
to
(a,b)
).
Hence the Hausdorff distance between the bounded sets

m(B_{\varepsilon}(a_{n})\times B_{\varepsilon}(b_{n}))
and

m(B_{\varepsilon}(a)\times B_{\varepsilon}(b))

tends to
0
, and therefore
F_{\varepsilon}(a_{n},b_{n})\to F_{\varepsilon}(a,b)

in the Wijsman topology.
∎

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
A
be a separable Banach-algebra quotient generator and work on
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)

with the Wijsman topology. Fix a countable dense multiplicatively closed
D^{A}
and fix

\delta\in\mathcal{D}
.

(i)

The set of
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
such that multiplication in
B=A/K
is

\delta
-uniformly almost open is
\Pi^{0}_{3}
.

(ii)

The set of
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
such that multiplication in
A/K
is
uniformly open (
i.e.
admits some modulus) is analytic.

Proof.

Write
B=A/K
and let
m_{B}:B\times B\to B
be multiplication, equipped with the max metric
on
B\times B
.

We begin by constructing a countable
\Phi
-scheme that captures

\delta
-uniform almost openness.
Fix
r\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
,
a,b,y\in D^{A}
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consider the condition on
K
:

(6.1)

\Phi(K,y-ab)\geqslant r\quad\lor\\
\exists a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in D^{A}:\ \Phi(K,a^{\prime}-a)<\delta(r)\ \wedge\ \Phi(K,b^{\prime}-b)<\delta(r)\ \wedge\ \Phi(K,a^{\prime}b^{\prime}-y)<\tfrac{1}{n}.

For fixed
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
, the conjunction on the right is a finite intersection of strict
inequalities in Wijsman-continuous functions of
K
, hence is open; therefore the right
disjunct is open (countable union over
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in D^{A}
).
The left disjunct is closed. Hence (
6.1
) defines a
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
subset of

\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
be the intersection of (
6.1
) over all

(r,a,b,y,n)\in((0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q})\times(D^{A})^{3}\times\mathbb{N}
.
Then
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
is a countable intersection of
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
sets, hence

\mathcal{U}_{\delta}\in\Pi^{0}_{3}
.

We now verify that
\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
coincides with

\delta
-uniform almost openness of
m_{B}
.
Fix
r\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
and set
\varepsilon=\delta(r)
.
For
a,b\in B
, define the closed set

F_{\varepsilon}(a,b):=\overline{m_{B}\bigl(B_{\varepsilon}(a)\times B_{\varepsilon}(b)\bigr)}\subseteq B.

By Lemma
6.10
, for fixed
\varepsilon
the map

(a,b)\mapsto F_{\varepsilon}(a,b)

is Wijsman-continuous, and hence the function

(a,b,z)\mapsto\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,F_{\varepsilon}(a,b)\bigr)

is continuous on
B\times B\times B
.

Assume
K\in\mathcal{U}_{\delta}
.
Take
a,b\in D^{A}
(viewed in
B
) and
z\in B
with
\|z-ab\|<r
.
Since
D^{A}+K
is dense in
B
and
F_{\varepsilon}(a,b)
is closed, it suffices to show

z\in F_{\varepsilon}(a,b)
for
z
ranging over
D^{A}+K
.
Fix
y\in D^{A}
with
\Phi(K,y-ab)<r
.
Then for each
n
the left disjunct in (
6.1
) fails, and we obtain

a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}\in D^{A}
with

\Phi(K,a^{\prime}_{n}-a)<\varepsilon,\qquad\Phi(K,b^{\prime}_{n}-b)<\varepsilon,\qquad\Phi(K,a^{\prime}_{n}b^{\prime}_{n}-y)<\tfrac{1}{n}.

In
B
this means
(a^{\prime}_{n}+K)\in B_{\varepsilon}(a+K)
,
(b^{\prime}_{n}+K)\in B_{\varepsilon}(b+K)
and

\|(a^{\prime}_{n}+K)(b^{\prime}_{n}+K)-(y+K)\|<1/n
, hence
y+K\in F_{\varepsilon}(a+K,b+K)
.
Density of
D^{A}+K
now gives
B_{r}(ab)\subseteq F_{\varepsilon}(a,b)
for all

a,b\in D^{A}+K
.

If
\delta
-uniform almost openness failed somewhere in
B\times B
, there would exist

a,b\in B
and
z\in B
with
\|z-ab\|<r
but
\mathrm{dist}(z,F_{\varepsilon}(a,b))>0
.
By continuity of

and density of

, a contradiction.
Therefore multiplication in
B
is
\delta
-uniformly almost open.

Conversely, if multiplication in
B
is
\delta
-uniformly almost open, then for any

with
\Phi(K,y-ab)<r
we have

a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in B
within

of

with

within

of

.
Approximating

by elements of

inside the open balls and using continuity of

). Hence

.
This proves (1).

For genuine uniform openness, let

Unwinding (
6.1
) shows

is Borel in

.
Hence its projection

If multiplication in

is uniformly open, it is in particular uniformly almost open, so

Conversely, if

then multiplication in

∎

6.4.
Pure infiniteness and norm control

Let
B
be a unital Banach algebra. Following Daws–Horváth
[
11
]
,
define for
a\in B

C_{\mathrm{pi}}^{B}(a):=\inf\{\|b\|\,\|c\|:\ b,c\in B,\ bac=1\}\in(0,\infty].

In particular,
C_{\mathrm{pi}}^{B}(a)<\infty
iff
1\in BaB
.

Definition 6.12

(Purely infinite vs. uniformly purely infinite)
.

A unital Banach algebra
B
is
purely infinite
(in the sense of

[
11
]
) if
B\not\cong\mathbb{C}
and
C_{\mathrm{pi}}^{B}(a)<\infty
for every
non-zero
a\in B
.

We say that
B
is
uniformly purely infinite
if
B\not\cong\mathbb{C}
and there exists

M\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup\{C_{\mathrm{pi}}^{B}(a):\ \|a\|=1\}\leqslant M.

By
[
11
, Prop. 2.2]
, uniform pure infiniteness is equivalent to pure
infiniteness of every ultrapower
B^{\mathcal{U}}
for countably incomplete ultrafilters.

Proposition 6.13

(Uniform pure infiniteness is Borel)
.

Let
A
be a unital Banach-algebra quotient generator and work on
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
with
the Wijsman topology. The set of
K
such that
A/K
is uniformly purely infinite
is
\Sigma^{0}_{3}
.

Proof.

Write
B=A/K
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
set

r_{N}:=\frac{1}{8N^{2}}\in(0,1)

and let
\mathcal{U}_{N}\subseteq\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
be the set of all
K
such that

(6.2)

\forall a\in D^{A}:\ \bigl|\Phi(K,a)-1\bigr|>r_{N}\quad\lor\\
\exists b,c\in D^{A}:\ \Phi(K,b)<N,\ \Phi(K,c)<N,\ \Phi(K,bac-1)<\tfrac{1}{8}.

For fixed
a\in D^{A}
, both disjuncts in (
6.2
) define open subsets of

\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
, so
\mathcal{U}_{N}
is
G_{\delta}
,
i.e.

\Pi^{0}_{2}
.

Let
\mathrm{NonScal}
denote the set of
K
such that
A/K\not\cong\mathbb{C}
.
This set is
F_{\sigma}
: indeed,

K\in\mathrm{NonScal}\iff\exists a\in D^{A}\ \exists m\in\mathbb{N}\ \forall\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}(i):\ \Phi(K,a-\lambda 1)\geqslant\tfrac{1}{m}.

For fixed
(a,m)
this is the superlevel condition

\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}(i)}\Phi(K,a-\lambda 1)\geqslant\tfrac{1}{m}.

Since each map
K\mapsto\Phi(K,a-\lambda 1)
is continuous in the Wijsman topology,
the infimum over the countable set
\mathbb{Q}(i)
is upper semicontinuous. Hence its
superlevel set is closed. Taking the countable union over
(a,m)\in D^{A}\times\mathbb{N}

shows that
\mathrm{NonScal}
is
F_{\sigma}
.

We
claim
that

\mathrm{UPI}=\mathrm{NonScal}\cap\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{U}_{N}.

(
\Rightarrow
)
Assume that
B
is uniformly purely infinite.
Choose
M\in\mathbb{N}
with

\sup\{C_{\mathrm{pi}}^{B}(x):\ \|x\|=1\}\leqslant M
,
and then choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
N^{2}>M
.
Fix
a\in D^{A}
with
|\|a+K\|-1|\leqslant r_{N}
and put

u:=(a+K)/\|a+K\|
.
Then
\|u\|=1
and
\|u-(a+K)\|\leqslant r_{N}
.
Since
C_{\mathrm{pi}}^{B}(u)\leqslant M
, there exist
b_{0},c_{0}\in B
with
b_{0}uc_{0}=1
and

\|b_{0}\|\,\|c_{0}\|\leqslant M
.
Rescale by

\lambda:=\Bigl(\frac{\|c_{0}\|}{\|b_{0}\|}\Bigr)^{1/2},\qquad b:=\lambda b_{0},\qquad c:=\lambda^{-1}c_{0}.

Then
buc=1
and

\|b\|=\|c\|=\sqrt{\|b_{0}\|\,\|c_{0}\|}\leqslant\sqrt{M}<N
.
Hence

\|b(a+K)c-1\|=\|b(a+K-u)c\|\leqslant\|b\|\,\|a+K-u\|\,\|c\|<N^{2}r_{N}=\tfrac{1}{8}.

Approximating
b,c
by elements of
D^{A}
preserves the strict bounds

\Phi(K,b)<N
,
\Phi(K,c)<N
, and
\Phi(K,bac-1)<1/8
.
Thus
K\in\mathcal{U}_{N}
.
Since
B\not\cong\mathbb{C}
by definition,
K\in\mathrm{NonScal}
as well.

(
\Leftarrow
)
Assume
K\in\mathrm{NonScal}\cap\mathcal{U}_{N}
.
Let
x\in B
with
\|x\|=1
and choose
a\in D^{A}
with

\|a+K-x\|<r_{N}/2
.
Then
|\|a+K\|-1|\leqslant r_{N}/2
, so the left disjunct in (
6.2
) fails.
Hence there exist
b,c\in D^{A}
with

\|b+K\|<N
,
\|c+K\|<N
, and
\|b(a+K)c-1\|<1/8
.
Therefore

\|bxc-1\|\leqslant\|b(x-a-K)c\|+\|b(a+K)c-1\|<N^{2}\cdot\frac{r_{N}}{2}+\frac{1}{8}=\frac{3}{16}<\frac{1}{2}.

So
bxc
is invertible and
\|(bxc)^{-1}\|<2
.
Putting
c^{\prime}:=c(bxc)^{-1}
gives
bxc^{\prime}=1
and

\|b\|\,\|c^{\prime}\|\leqslant\|b\|\,\|c\|\,\|(bxc)^{-1}\|<2N^{2}
.
Thus
C_{\mathrm{pi}}^{B}(x)\leqslant 2N^{2}
for every
\|x\|=1
.
Since
K\in\mathrm{NonScal}
, we also have
B\not\cong\mathbb{C}
.
Therefore
B
is uniformly purely infinite.

Finally,
\mathrm{NonScal}
is
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
and
\bigcup_{N}\mathcal{U}_{N}
is

\Sigma^{0}_{3}
, so

\mathrm{UPI}=\mathrm{NonScal}\cap\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{U}_{N}

is
\Sigma^{0}_{3}
.
∎

Remark 6.14

(Non-uniform pure infiniteness)
.

The bare condition “
C_{\mathrm{pi}}^{A/K}(a)<\infty
for all non-zero
a
” involves
existential witnesses with
no uniform norm control
. As in the directly finite
case, such unbounded-witness phenomena can
obstruct reductions to a single bounded fragment. We therefore isolate the uniform
variant (Definition
6.12
), which is the version naturally compatible with
the dense-set quantification method and with ultrapower permanence
[
11
]
.
At present we do not know whether the bare non-uniform class of purely infinite quotients
is Borel, analytic, or genuinely more complicated in this coding.

6.5.
Dedekind (in)finiteness and unbounded witnesses

A unital ring (or Banach algebra)
B
is
Dedekind finite
(or
directly
finite
) if
ab=1
implies
ba=1
for all
a,b\in B
; otherwise
B
is

Dedekind infinite
.

In Banach algebras, a central subtlety is that direct finiteness is not controlled by
uniformly bounded fragments: Daws–Horváth show that ring-theoretic (in)finiteness
behaves delicately under reduced products of Banach algebras, and in particular that
the norms of witnesses to Dedekind infiniteness cannot in general be bounded uniformly
along families
[
12
]
.

For complexity purposes it is therefore natural to isolate the bounded fragment:
for
M\in\mathbb{N}
we say that
B
is
M
-Dedekind finite
if

\forall a,b\in B\ \bigl(\|a\|\leqslant M\ \wedge\ \|b\|\leqslant M\ \wedge\ ab=1\bigr)\ \Rightarrow\ ba=1.

Then
B
is Dedekind finite if and only if it is
M
-Dedekind finite for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 6.15
.

For each fixed
M\in\mathbb{N}
, the class of
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
such that
A/K
is

M
-Dedekind finite is
\Pi^{0}_{2}
in the Wijsman topology.
Consequently, the class of Dedekind finite quotients is also
\Pi^{0}_{2}
.

Proof.

Fix
M\in\mathbb{N}
and write
B=A/K
.

Step 1: a uniform correction estimate.

Let
u_{0},v_{0}\in B
with
\|u_{0}\|\leqslant M
and
\|v_{0}\|\leqslant M
.
If
\|u_{0}v_{0}-1\|<\delta<1
, then
u_{0}v_{0}
is invertible with

\|(u_{0}v_{0})^{-1}\|\leqslant(1-\delta)^{-1}
, and hence

u_{0}\bigl(v_{0}(u_{0}v_{0})^{-1}\bigr)=1
.
Moreover,

\|v_{0}(u_{0}v_{0})^{-1}-v_{0}\|\leqslant\|v_{0}\|\,\|(u_{0}v_{0})^{-1}-1\|\leqslant M\cdot\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}.

Step 2: a
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
description of
M
-Dedekind infiniteness.

For
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geqslant 1
set
M_{k}:=M-\tfrac{1}{k}
and fix a rational

\delta_{M,k,n}:=\min\Bigl\{\tfrac{1}{2},\ \tfrac{1}{2kM},\ \tfrac{1}{4nM^{2}+1}\Bigr\}\in(0,1).

Consider the condition

(\star)_{k,n}:\ \exists u,v\in D^{A}:\ \Phi(K,u)\leqslant M_{k},\ \Phi(K,v)\leqslant M_{k},\\
\Phi(K,uv-1)<\delta_{M,k,n},\ \Phi(K,vu-1)>\tfrac{1}{n}.

We claim that
B
is
not

M
-Dedekind finite iff
(\star)_{k,n}
holds for some

k,n
.

(
\Rightarrow
) If
B
is not
M
-Dedekind finite, pick
a,b\in B
with

\|a\|,\|b\|\leqslant M
,
ab=1
, and
\|ba-1\|>0
.
Choose
n
with
\|ba-1\|>\tfrac{3}{n}
.

Strictification.
Since it may happen that
\max(\|a\|,\|b\|)=M
, we first
rescale: choose a rational
\lambda\in(0,1)
close enough to
1
that

\lambda^{2}\|ba-1\|>\tfrac{3}{n}
and
\|\lambda^{2}-1\|<\tfrac{1}{n}
.
Set
a_{0}=\lambda a
and
b_{0}=\lambda b
.
Then
\|a_{0}\|,\|b_{0}\|<M
(strictly),
a_{0}b_{0}=\lambda^{2}\cdot 1
with

\|a_{0}b_{0}-1\|=|1-\lambda^{2}|<\tfrac{1}{n}
, and

\|b_{0}a_{0}-1\|=\|\lambda^{2}ba-1\|\geqslant\lambda^{2}\|ba-1\|-|1-\lambda^{2}|>\tfrac{3}{n}-\tfrac{1}{n}=\tfrac{2}{n}
.

Now choose
k
large enough that
M_{k}>\max(\|a_{0}\|,\|b_{0}\|)

(possible because
\|a_{0}\|,\|b_{0}\|<M
), then pick

u,v\in D^{A}
so that
u+K
is close to
a_{0}
and
v+K
is close to
b_{0}
, with

\Phi(K,u)\leqslant M_{k}
and
\Phi(K,v)\leqslant M_{k}
, and so that

\Phi(K,uv-1)<\delta_{M,k,n}
and
\Phi(K,vu-1)>\tfrac{1}{n}
.
This is possible by density of
D^{A}
in
B
and continuity of multiplication.

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume
(\star)_{k,n}
holds, and set
u_{0}=u+K
,
v_{0}=v+K
in
B
.
Let
w=(u_{0}v_{0})^{-1}
(exists since
\|u_{0}v_{0}-1\|<\delta_{M,k,n}<1
)
and put
\widetilde{b}=v_{0}w
and
\widetilde{a}=u_{0}
.
Then
\widetilde{a}\,\widetilde{b}=1
.
Also
\|\widetilde{b}\|\leqslant\|v_{0}\|\,\|w\|\leqslant M_{k}(1-\delta_{M,k,n})^{-1}\leqslant M
by the choice of

\delta_{M,k,n}
(which ensures
M_{k}/(1-\delta_{M,k,n})\leqslant M
).
Finally,

\|\widetilde{b}\,\widetilde{a}-1\|\geqslant\|v_{0}u_{0}-1\|-\|v_{0}(w-1)u_{0}\|>\frac{1}{n}-M^{2}\frac{\delta_{M,k,n}}{1-\delta_{M,k,n}}\geqslant\frac{1}{2n}>0,

so
\widetilde{b}\,\widetilde{a}\neq 1
. Hence
B
is not
M
-Dedekind finite.

Step 3: Borel complexity.

For fixed
k,n,u,v
, the set of
K
satisfying the four inequalities in
(\star)_{k,n}

is the intersection of two closed conditions (
\leqslant M_{k}
) and two open conditions
(
<\delta_{M,k,n}
and
>\tfrac{1}{n}
), hence an
F_{\sigma}
set.
Thus the set of
K
for which
B
is not
M
-Dedekind finite is a countable union of

F_{\sigma}
sets,
i.e.

\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.
Therefore the set of
K
such that
B
is
M
-Dedekind finite is
\Pi^{0}_{2}
.

Finally,
B
is Dedekind finite iff it is
M
-Dedekind finite for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
,
and a countable intersection of
G_{\delta}
sets is
G_{\delta}
.
∎

Remark 6.16
.

The Daws–Horváth analysis
[
12
]
shows that the norms of witnesses
to Dedekind infiniteness cannot in general be bounded uniformly along families.
This means no single
M
captures the full property, which provides
motivation

for the parameterised approach, but it does not by itself raise the Borel rank.
As shown above, the full Dedekind finiteness class remains
\Pi^{0}_{2}
.

Remark 7.1

Remark 7.2

(1) The concrete coding
\Gamma(H)
.

Let
\Gamma(H):=B(H)^{\mathbb{N}}
equipped with the product of the strong operator topology.
Each
\gamma=(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\Gamma(H)
codes the separable
C^{*}
-algebra

C^{*}(\gamma)\subseteq B(H)
generated by
\{\gamma_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

(2) The seminorm coding
\Xi
.

Fix an enumeration
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of all formal

\mathbb{Q}(i)
-
*
-polynomials in countably
many non-commuting variables
X_{0},X_{1},\dots
.
The space
\Xi
consists of all functions

\xi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
that arise as

\xi(n)=\|p_{n}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots)\|

for some separable
C^{*}
-algebra
B
and some sequence
(a_{k})
in
B
.
Equivalently,
\xi
encodes a
C^{*}
-seminorm on the free
*
-algebra;
the completion of the corresponding quotient is the coded algebra
B_{\xi}
.

Farah–Toms–Törnquist show that
\Gamma(H)
and
\Xi
(as well as several related
codings, including abstract countable-structure codings
\widehat{\Gamma}
and

\widehat{\Xi}
) are equivalent parameterisations: there are Borel maps between the
coding spaces sending a code to a code for an isomorphic algebra; see

[
19
, Section 2]
and
[
18
]
.
These are also the codings used in

[
17
]
.

Borel bijections via Schröder–Bernstein.

For standard Borel spaces, the existence of
injective
Borel maps in both
directions implies the existence of a Borel isomorphism (a Borel bijection with
Borel inverse) by the Borel Schröder–Bernstein theorem; see, for instance,
Kechris
[
33
]
or Gao
[
24
]
.
In particular, once one has explicit Borel injections between two concrete coding
spaces (as in Remark
7.4
for
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
and
\Xi
), one can obtain
a Borel bijection between the coding spaces themselves.
We do not attempt to control the Borel complexity of such a bijection: the
Schröder–Bernstein construction can increase Borel rank substantially.
For our purposes, the key consequence is that
Borelness
of a class of codes
is invariant under passage between these standard parameterisations.

Lemma 7.3

(Pullback of ideals under surjective
*
-homomorphisms)
.

Let
\sigma:C\twoheadrightarrow D
be a surjective
*
-homomorphism between separable

C^{*}
-algebras. Then the pullback map

\sigma^{*}:\mathrm{Ideal}(D)\to\mathrm{Ideal}(C),\qquad J\mapsto\sigma^{-1}(J),

is a Wijsman-continuous injection.

Proof.

Injectivity: if
J\neq L
then
\sigma^{-1}(J)\neq\sigma^{-1}(L)
because
\sigma
is
surjective. For Wijsman continuity, fix
c\in C
. Because
\sigma
is a surjective

*
-homomorphism, the quotient
C/\sigma^{-1}(J)\cong D/J
isometrically (via the
canonical isomorphism), and therefore

\mathrm{dist}\bigl(c,\,\sigma^{-1}(J)\bigr)=\|\sigma(c)+J\|_{D/J}=\mathrm{dist}\bigl(\sigma(c),\,J\bigr).

Since
J\mapsto\mathrm{dist}(\sigma(c),J)
is continuous in the Wijsman topology on
\mathrm{Ideal}(D)
,
it follows that
J\mapsto\mathrm{dist}(c,\sigma^{-1}(J))
is continuous on
\mathrm{Ideal}(C)
.
As
c
was arbitrary,
\sigma^{*}
is Wijsman-continuous.
∎

Remark 7.4

(The
\Xi
-coding as an ideal space, and explicit Borel injections)
.

The seminorm coding
\Xi
is naturally identified with an ideal space. Let

U:=C^{*}\bigl(x_{n}\ (n\in\mathbb{N})\ :\ \|x_{n}\|\leqslant 1\bigr)

be the universal unital
C^{*}
-algebra generated by countably many contractions. Then
U

is separable, and there is a canonical Borel bijection
\Xi\cong\mathrm{Ideal}(U)
: a seminorm

\xi\in\Xi
corresponds to the kernel
I_{\xi}
of the canonical surjection
U\to B_{\xi}
,
and conversely
I\in\mathrm{Ideal}(U)
yields the seminorm
\xi_{I}(p)=\|p+I\|_{U/I}
, which is
continuous coordinatewise in
I
.

(3) A continuous injection
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)\hookrightarrow\Xi
.

Let
A=C^{*}_{\max}(F_{\infty})
and let
D^{A}=\{u_{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the fixed countable dense

*
-subalgebra from Theorem
3.13
.
Define

\Theta:\mathrm{Ideal}(A)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\qquad\Theta(K)(n):=\|p_{n}(\bar{u})+K\|_{A/K},

where
p_{n}(\bar{u})
denotes the evaluation of
p_{n}
at the chosen dense generating
sequence
\bar{u}=(u_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Each coordinate
K\mapsto\|p_{n}(\bar{u})+K\|
is continuous in the Wijsman topology,
hence
\Theta
is continuous.
Moreover,
\Theta
is injective: if
\Theta(K)=\Theta(L)
then
\mathrm{dist}(x,K)=\mathrm{dist}(x,L)

for every
x\in D^{A}
, and by continuity of the distance functions we get
K=L
.
Finally,
\Theta(K)
satisfies the axioms defining
\Xi
(it is exactly the

C^{*}
-seminorm induced by the quotient map
A\to A/K
), and the
C^{*}
-algebra coded by

\Theta(K)
is canonically
*
-isomorphic to
A/K
.

Alternatively, by Lemma
7.3
: since the generators
u_{m}
of
A

are unitaries and hence contractions, there is a surjective
*
-homomorphism

\sigma:U\twoheadrightarrow A
sending
x_{m}\mapsto u_{m}
.
The pullback
K\mapsto\sigma^{-1}(K)
is a Wijsman-continuous injection

\mathrm{Ideal}(A)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Ideal}(U)\cong\Xi
.

(4) A continuous injection
\Xi\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
.

Because
A=C^{*}_{\max}(F_{\infty})
is a quotient generator,
U
(being separable) is a
quotient of
A
. Fix once and for all a surjective
*
-homomorphism

\pi:A\twoheadrightarrow U
.
By Lemma
7.3
, the pullback
I\mapsto\pi^{-1}(I)
is a
Wijsman-continuous injection
\mathrm{Ideal}(U)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
.
Under the identification
\Xi\cong\mathrm{Ideal}(U)
, this gives a continuous injection

\Xi\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
. Combining (3) and (4) with the Borel
Schröder–Bernstein theorem, we conclude that
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
and
\Xi
are Borel
isomorphic as standard Borel spaces.

(5) Borel compatibility with
\Gamma(H)
.

Composing the continuous injections of (3) and (4) with the Borel equivalences
between
\Xi
and
\Gamma(H)
from

[
19
]
, we obtain Borel maps
\Gamma(H)\to\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
and

\mathrm{Ideal}(A)\to\Gamma(H)
which send a code to a code for an isomorphic
C^{*}
-algebra.
In particular, any Borel-complexity statement for a
C^{*}
-algebraic property in our
ideal-quotient coding transfers to the equivalent statement in the FTT codings, and
vice versa.

Nuclearity.

Because nuclearity is invariant under
*
-isomorphism and
Remark
7.4
identifies our coding with the standard codings up to
Borel isomorphism, the class

\{K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A):A/K\text{ is nuclear}\}

is Borel in the Wijsman topology. Indeed, one route is via the CPAP
reformulation of nuclearity: Effros proved Borelness of the nuclear class in the
standard parameterisations; see Section 5 of
[
19
]
.
This gives a quick external route to Borelness in the present coding.

It seems plausible that one can sharpen this to a low finite Borel rank by combining the
CPAP with an internal coding lemma for completely positive contractive maps from
finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras into quotients
A/K
. Since that lemma is not
developed in the present paper, we record only the Borel upper bound here.

Continuous logic.

It is also natural to compare the present quotient/Wijsman formalism with the
continuous-logic treatment of axiomatizable and definable classes of
C^{*}
-algebras;
see
[
20
]
. After transferring between
our ideal coding and the standard FTT codings, this gives an alternative abstract
route to Borelness for many axiomatizable classes. We nevertheless retain the
direct arguments here because they keep track of concrete Borel ranks inside the
present parameter space.

7.1.
Coding projections, unitaries, and matrix units

Lemma 7.5
.

For
x\in D^{A}
and
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
:

(i)

x+K
is a projection in
B
iff
\Phi(K,x^{2}-x)=0
and
\Phi(K,x^{*}-x)=0
.

(ii)

u+K
is unitary in
B
iff
\Phi(K,u^{*}u-1)=0
and
\Phi(K,uu^{*}-1)=0
.

For fixed finite families, the predicate “forms a system of matrix units” is closed.

Proof.

All assertions follow directly from identities in
B
expressed by
\Phi
and
the continuity of
\Phi
in
K
.
∎

Lemma 7.6

(Borel coding of positivity and order)
.

Let
B=A/K
.

(i)

An element
a+K\in B
is positive iff for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists

y\in D^{A}
with
\Phi(K,a-y^{*}y)<1/m
. In particular, the set of
K
for which a
fixed
a\in D^{A}
has positive image is
\Pi^{0}_{2}
.

(ii)

Every positive element of
B
is approximated by elements of the form
b^{*}b+K

with
b\in D^{A}
. Hence in quantifications
“
\forall
positive
a\in B_{+}
”, it suffices to range
a
over
\{b^{*}b:b\in D^{A}\}
,
which is countable.

(iii)

For
a\in D^{A}
and a projection
p\in B
(coded as in
Lemma
7.5
), set
x=p(a^{*}a+K)p\in pBp
.
Then

x\geqslant\tfrac{1}{n}p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\text{ is invertible in }pBp\text{ and }\|x^{-1}\|\leqslant n.

Moreover, if there exists
y\in pBp
with
\|y\|\leqslant n
and

\|xy-p\|<\tfrac{1}{2},\qquad\|yx-p\|<\tfrac{1}{2},

then
x
is invertible in
pBp
and
\|x^{-1}\|\leqslant 2n
; hence

x\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2n}p
.
Thus, for the descriptive-set-theoretic arguments below, the relation

x\geqslant\tfrac{1}{n}p
may be replaced by this bounded stable witness scheme,
at the expense of renumbering
n
by a fixed multiplicative constant.

Proof.

(i) If
a+K\geqslant 0
in
B
, let
c
be a positive square root, and approximate
c
by

y_{m}\in D^{A}
to get
\|a+K-y_{m}^{*}y_{m}+K\|<1/m
. Conversely, if
a+K=\lim(y_{m}^{*}y_{m}+K)
,
then
a+K\geqslant 0
since the positive cone is closed.

(ii) follows from (i) and the fact that
D^{A}
is closed under
*
and multiplication.

(iii) In the unital
C^{*}
-algebra
pBp
(with unit
p
), a positive element
x\geqslant 0

satisfies
x\geqslant\tfrac{1}{n}p
iff
x
is invertible with
\|x^{-1}\|\leqslant n

(this is a standard spectral fact:
x\geqslant\tfrac{1}{n}p
iff

\mathrm{sp}(x)\subseteq[1/n,\infty)
, iff
x^{-1}
exists with

\|x^{-1}\|=1/\min(\mathrm{sp}(x))\leqslant n
).
If there exists
y\in pBp
with
\|y\|\leqslant n
and
\|xy-p\|<1/2
, then the Neumann
series gives
\|x^{-1}\|\leqslant\|y\|\,\|(xy)^{-1}\|\leqslant n\cdot 2=2n
, so
x\geqslant\frac{1}{2n}p
.
The constant
2
is harmless for our purposes: replacing
n
by
2n
does not change
the quantifier structure.
Approximating
p
and
y
by elements of
D^{A}
and using stability of
projections/invertibles (Lemma
7.7
and
Remark
7.8
), this is detected by strict
\Phi
-inequalities
on bounded balls.
∎

Lemma 7.7

(Stability of finite-dimensional relations)
.

For each
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that the following
holds in every
C^{*}
-algebra
B
.

If
(x_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d}\subseteq B
satisfies

\displaystyle\max_{i,j}\|x_{ij}^{*}-x_{ji}\|

\displaystyle<\delta,

\displaystyle\max_{i,j,k,\ell}\|x_{ij}x_{k\ell}-\delta_{jk}x_{i\ell}\|

\displaystyle<\delta,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{ii}-1\Bigr\|

\displaystyle<\delta,

then there exists a system of matrix units
(e_{ij})_{i,j\leqslant d}\subseteq B
such that

\max_{i,j}\|e_{ij}-x_{ij}\|<\varepsilon
.
In particular, the
C^{*}
-subalgebra generated by
(e_{ij})
is (unitally) isomorphic
to
M_{d}
.

Proof.

This is a standard perturbation/semiprojectivity fact for finite-dimensional

C^{*}
-algebras; see
[
35
]
.
∎

Remark 7.8

(Stable relations and the dense-set paradigm)
.

Several arguments below (for AF, QD, MF, and related approximation properties, as well
as for the
K_{0}
coding in Section
8
) involve quantification over the
countable dense
*
-subalgebra
D^{A}
using exact equalities such as
\Phi(K,\cdot)=0

to assert that certain quotient elements are projections, matrix units, or unitaries.
Such exact witnesses need not belong to
D^{A}
.

The resolution is standard: one replaces exact relations by
approximate
ones
(expressed by strict inequalities
\Phi(K,\cdot)<\delta
on elements of
D^{A}
) and
appeals to perturbation stability (Lemma
7.7
and its generalisations)
to promote approximate witnesses to genuine ones in the quotient.
Since strict inequalities are open predicates, the quantifier-complexity analysis is
unchanged.

Lemma 7.9

(Borel coding of ucp maps and finite-dimensional
*
-subalgebras)
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

(UCP maps with prescribed values.)

Let
\bar{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\subseteq D^{A}
and let
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{r}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{Q}(i))
.
The condition that there exists a unital completely positive (ucp) map

\varphi:A/K\to M_{m}
satisfying
\varphi(x_{j}+K)=Y_{j}
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant r

is a
closed
(and in particular Borel) predicate in
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
.

(ii)

(Approximate embeddings of f.d.
C^{*}
-algebras.)

A family
(e_{ij})_{i,j\leqslant d}\subseteq D^{A}
whose images in
B

approximately
satisfy matrix-unit relations (within tolerance
\delta
) yields,
by Lemma
7.7
, a genuine unital copy of
M_{d}
in
B
.
More precisely, the predicate “
\exists
a unital
*
-homomorphism

\pi:F_{0}\to B
with
F_{0}\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}M_{d_{j}}
and

\max_{s}\mathrm{dist}(x_{s}+K,\pi(F_{0}))<\varepsilon
” is Borel in
K
:
quantify over all choices of approximate matrix-unit families for each direct summand

M_{d_{j}}
with entries in
D^{A}
(a countable search), require approximate matrix-unit
relations to hold within tolerance
\delta
via strict
\Phi
-inequalities, and require

\varepsilon
-closeness of the target elements. This contributes one existential
quantifier block.

Proof.

Part (i): The existence of a ucp map
\varphi:A/K\to M_{m}
with

\varphi(x_{j}+K)=Y_{j}
is equivalent to the existence of a ucp map

\psi:A\to M_{m}
with
\psi(x_{j})=Y_{j}
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
and
\psi
vanishing on
K
.
Since any ucp map is contractive, vanishing on
K
is equivalent to

\|\psi(a)\|\leqslant\|a+K\|_{A/K}=\Phi(K,a)
for all
a\in A
; by density
and continuity it suffices to check this inequality for
a\in D^{A}
.

Let
\mathrm{UCP}(A,M_{m})
denote the space of all ucp maps
A\to M_{m}
, equipped
with the point-norm topology.
Since
M_{m}
is finite-dimensional, the point-norm topology on
\mathrm{UCP}(A,M_{m})

coincides with pointwise convergence of the
m^{2}
matrix-entry functionals in the
weak
∗
topology of
A^{*}
; hence
\mathrm{UCP}(A,M_{m})
is compact metrisable by
Banach–Alaoglu and separability of
A
.
Consider the constraint on pairs

(\psi,K)\in\mathrm{UCP}(A,M_{m})\times\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
:

\forall j\leqslant r\ \bigl(\psi(x_{j})=Y_{j}\bigr)\ \wedge\ \forall a\in D^{A}\ \bigl(\|\psi(a)\|\leqslant\Phi(K,a)\bigr).

For each fixed
a\in D^{A}
, the evaluation
\psi\mapsto\|\psi(a)\|
is continuous,
and
K\mapsto\Phi(K,a)
is continuous (Lemma
3.8
), so

\|\psi(a)\|\leqslant\Phi(K,a)
defines a closed subset of the product.
Similarly, each condition
\psi(x_{j})=Y_{j}
is closed.
A countable intersection of closed sets is closed, so the full constraint set
is closed in
\mathrm{UCP}(A,M_{m})\times\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
.
Because
\mathrm{UCP}(A,M_{m})
is compact, the projection of this closed set onto

\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
is closed.

Part (ii): approximate
matrix-unit families on a fixed finite set are quantified over

(x_{ij})\subseteq D^{A}
, with the matrix-unit relations required to hold up to a
tolerance
\delta
in
B
(written using strict inequalities in
\Phi
).
Lemma
7.7
then yields genuine matrix units in
B

nearby, hence an actual unital copy of the prescribed finite-dimensional algebra.
Closeness to a finite set is measured by
\Phi
.
∎

Lemma 7.10

(C.p.c. maps with prescribed values)
.

Let
F_{0}
be a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra, let

\bar{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\subseteq D^{A}
, and let
\bar{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{r})\subseteq F_{0}
.
Then the set of
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
for which there exists a completely positive
contractive map

\psi:A/K\to F_{0}

satisfying
\psi(x_{j}+K)=y_{j}
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant r
is closed.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{CPC}(A,F_{0})
denote the space of all completely positive contractive
maps
A\to F_{0}
, equipped with the point-norm topology.
Since
F_{0}
is finite-dimensional,
\mathrm{CPC}(A,F_{0})
is compact metrisable:
using the dense sequence
D^{A}=\{u_{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
, each

\psi\in\mathrm{CPC}(A,F_{0})
is determined by the tuple

(\psi(u_{m}))_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\prod_{m}B_{\|u_{m}\|}^{F_{0}}
, a compact metrisable product,
and the conditions of linearity,
*
-preservation, complete positivity, and
contractivity define a closed subset.

A c.p.c. map
\psi:A\to F_{0}
factors through
A/K
iff

\|\psi(a)\|\leqslant\|a+K\|_{A/K}=\Phi(K,a)\qquad(a\in A),

and by density it suffices to check this on
a\in D^{A}
.
Hence the desired relation on pairs
(\psi,K)\in\mathrm{CPC}(A,F_{0})\times\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
is

\forall j\leqslant r\ \bigl(\psi(x_{j})=y_{j}\bigr)\quad\wedge\quad\forall a\in D^{A}\ \bigl(\|\psi(a)\|\leqslant\Phi(K,a)\bigr).

Each condition
\psi(x_{j})=y_{j}
is closed, and for fixed
a\in D^{A}
the inequality

\|\psi(a)\|\leqslant\Phi(K,a)
is closed because
(\psi,K)\mapsto\|\psi(a)\|
and

(\psi,K)\mapsto\Phi(K,a)
are continuous.
Thus the full relation is closed in

\mathrm{CPC}(A,F_{0})\times\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
.
Projecting this closed set along the compact factor
\mathrm{CPC}(A,F_{0})
yields a
closed subset of
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
.
∎

7.2.
AF and related approximation classes

A
C^{*}
-algebra
B
is
approximately finite-dimensional
(
AF
) if it is the
closure of an increasing union of finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-subalgebras, or
equivalently, if for every finite
F\subseteq B
and every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-subalgebra
F_{0}\subseteq B
approximating
F

within
\varepsilon
(see
[
5
, II.8.2.2]
).

Proposition 7.11
.

The class
\mathrm{AF}=\{K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A):A/K\text{ is AF}\}

is
\Pi^{0}_{3}
in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Recall that
B
is AF iff for every finite set
F\subseteq B
and every

\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-subalgebra

F_{0}\subseteq B
such that every element of
F
is within
\varepsilon
of
F_{0}
.

It suffices to test finite sets drawn from
D^{A}+K
and tolerances
\varepsilon=1/n
.
Thus

B\text{ is AF}\iff\forall\bar{x}\in(D^{A})^{<\mathbb{N}}\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\ \exists F_{0}\ \exists\pi:F_{0}\to B

such that
F_{0}
is finite-dimensional,
\pi
is a unital
*
-homomorphism, and

\max_{x\in\bar{x}}\mathrm{dist}(x+K,\pi(F_{0}))<\frac{1}{n}.

Every finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra is isomorphic to a finite direct sum

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}M_{d_{j}}
, so the search over
F_{0}
reduces to a countable
search over finite lists
(d_{1},\dots,d_{\ell})
.
For each fixed algebra type, Lemma
7.9
(ii) shows that the predicate

\exists\ \text{a unital }*\text{-homomorphism }\pi:F_{0}\to B\text{ with }\max_{x\in\bar{x}}\mathrm{dist}(x+K,\pi(F_{0}))<\frac{1}{n}

is Borel, and is obtained by a countable existential search over approximate
matrix-unit families from
D^{A}
together with strict
\Phi
-inequalities.
Hence, for fixed
(\bar{x},n)
, the set of
K
admitting such an AF witness is

\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.

Intersecting over all finite tuples
\bar{x}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
yields a

\Pi^{0}_{3}
set.
∎

Remark 7.12
.

Proof.

Remark 7.14

Proposition 7.15
.

The classes of quasidiagonal and MF quotients are

in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Write

7.3.
Stable finiteness

A unital
C^{*}
-algebra
B
is
finite
if every isometry in
B
is unitary, and

stably finite
if
M_{n}(B)
is finite for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

(see
[
5
, V.2.1.1]
).

Proposition 7.17
.

The class of stably finite quotients is closed (and in particular
\Pi^{0}_{2}
)
in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Write
B=A/K
.
By definition,
B
is stably finite iff
M_{n}(B)
is finite for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
i.e.
iff there is no
n
for which
M_{n}(B)
contains a non-unitary isometry.

We use the matrix-level quotient norms
\Phi^{(n)}
of
Definition
2.7
and the countable dense set
M_{n}(D^{A})

(Remark
2.9
).
Fix the concrete constants

\alpha:=\tfrac{1}{4},\qquad\beta:=\tfrac{1}{2}.

Claim.

B
is
not
stably finite iff there exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

v\in M_{n}(D^{A})
such that

(7.2)

\Phi^{(n)}(K,v^{*}v-1)<\alpha\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Phi^{(n)}(K,vv^{*}-1)>\beta.

(
\Rightarrow
) If
B
is not stably finite, then for some
n
there exists an
isometry
u\in M_{n}(B)
which is not unitary. Then
p:=uu^{*}
is a proper projection,
so
\|p-1\|=1
,
i.e.

\|uu^{*}-1\|=1
.
Choose
v\in M_{n}(D^{A})
with
\|v+M_{n}(K)-u\|<\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon>0
is so
small that

\displaystyle\|(v+M_{n}(K))^{*}(v+M_{n}(K))-u^{*}u\|

\displaystyle<\alpha,

\displaystyle\|(v+M_{n}(K))(v+M_{n}(K))^{*}-uu^{*}\|

\displaystyle<1-\beta.

Since
u^{*}u=1
and
\|uu^{*}-1\|=1
, this yields (
7.2
).

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose (
7.2
) holds for some
n
and
v\in M_{n}(D^{A})
.
Let
x:=v+M_{n}(K)\in M_{n}(B)
and set
s:=x^{*}x
.
Then
\|s-1\|<\alpha<1
, so
s
is invertible with

\|s^{-1}-1\|\leqslant\alpha/(1-\alpha)
.
Define the polar correction
w:=x\,s^{-1/2}\in M_{n}(B)
.
Then
w^{*}w=s^{-1/2}s\,s^{-1/2}=1
, so
w
is an isometry.

We show
w
is not unitary. Since
ww^{*}=xs^{-1}x^{*}
, we have

ww^{*}-xx^{*}=x(s^{-1}-1)x^{*}
, so

\|ww^{*}-xx^{*}\|\leqslant\|x\|^{2}\,\|s^{-1}-1\|.

Moreover
\|x\|^{2}=\|s\|\leqslant 1+\alpha
, so with
\alpha=\tfrac{1}{4}
,

\|ww^{*}-xx^{*}\|\leqslant(1+\tfrac{1}{4})\cdot\frac{1/4}{1-1/4}=\tfrac{5}{4}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}=\tfrac{5}{12}<\tfrac{1}{2}.

Since
\|xx^{*}-1\|=\Phi^{(n)}(K,vv^{*}-1)>\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}
, we conclude

\|ww^{*}-1\|\geqslant\|xx^{*}-1\|-\|ww^{*}-xx^{*}\|>\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{5}{12}=\tfrac{1}{12}>0.

Since
ww^{*}
is a projection (as
w
is an isometry),
\|ww^{*}-1\|>0
forces

ww^{*}\neq 1
, so
w
is a non-unitary isometry and
B
is not stably finite.

Borel complexity.

For fixed
n
and
v
, each inequality in (
7.2
) is a strict
inequality in a Wijsman-continuous function of
K
(Lemma
2.8
),
hence defines an open set in
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
. Taking the union over the countable set of
pairs
(n,v)
shows the non-stably-finite codes form an open set. Therefore the class
of stably finite quotients is closed (and in particular
\Pi^{0}_{2}
).
∎

Remark 7.18
.

Proposition
7.17
is stronger than the generic rank bound supplied by
Theorem
3.16
: although stable finiteness is
a priori
a universal
property, the complement is witnessed by the
strict
inequalities
(
7.2
), so the non-stably-finite class is open and the stably finite class is
actually closed. In particular, this is a genuine
\Pi^{0}_{1}
phenomenon, not merely a

\Pi^{0}_{2}
one.

7.4.
Real rank

The
real rank
of a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
B
, denoted
\mathrm{RR}(B)
, is the
smallest non-negative integer
n
such that for every
(n\!+\!1)
-tuple of self-adjoint
elements
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
in
B
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
self-adjoint tuple
(y_{0},\dots,y_{n})
with
\max_{j}\|x_{j}-y_{j}\|<\varepsilon
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{n}y_{j}^{2}
is invertible (see
[
5
, V.3.2.3]
).

Proposition 7.19
.

For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the class

\{K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A):\mathrm{RR}(A/K)\leqslant n\}
is
\Pi^{0}_{3}
in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Write
B=A/K
. By the definition above,
\mathrm{RR}(B)\leqslant n
iff for every

(n\!+\!1)
-tuple

\bar{x}=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
of self-adjoint elements and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
a self-adjoint tuple
\bar{y}
with
\max_{j}\|x_{j}-y_{j}\|<1/m
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{n}y_{j}^{2}
is invertible. On

\forall\bar{x}\in(D^{A}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}})^{n+1}\;\forall m\;\exists\bar{y}\in(D^{A}\cap A_{\mathrm{sa}})^{n+1}\;\exists z\in D^{A}:

This is
\forall\forall\exists\exists
with continuous

hence
\Pi^{0}_{3}
.
∎

7.5.
Simplicity

A
C^{*}
-algebra
B
is
simple
if it has no non-trivial closed two-sided ideals
(see
[
5
, II.1.1]
).

Proposition 7.20
.

The class
\mathrm{Simple}=\{K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A):A/K\text{ is simple}\}
is
\Pi^{0}_{2}

in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Write
B=A/K
. A unital
C^{*}
-algebra
B
is simple iff for every non-zero
positive
a\in B_{+}
the closed two-sided ideal
\overline{BaB}
equals
B
,
equivalently iff
1\in\overline{BaB}
. Thus
B
is simple iff

\forall a\in B_{+}\setminus\{0\}\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\ \exists m\in\mathbb{N}\ \exists x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\in B:\ \Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}ay_{j}-1\Bigr\|<\frac{1}{n}.

By Lemma
7.6
(ii) it suffices to range
a
over elements of the
form
c^{*}c
with
c\in D^{A}
.
Indeed, the set

\mathcal{F}:=\{a\in B_{+}:1\in\overline{BaB}\}

is open in
B_{+}
: if

\bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}ay_{j}-1\bigr\|<1
,
then the same strict inequality persists for all
a^{\prime}
sufficiently close to

a
. Since
\{c^{*}c+K:c\in D^{A}\}
is dense in
B_{+}
, checking fullness on this
countable dense subset is enough.
Non-zeroness of
c^{*}c+K
is encoded by a rational lower
bound on
\Phi(K,c^{*}c)
.

Fix
t,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
c\in D^{A}
. Consider the set of
K
satisfying

\Phi(K,c^{*}c)<\frac{1}{t}\quad\lor\quad\exists m\ \exists x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\in D^{A}:\ \Phi\Bigl(K,\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}c^{*}c\,y_{j}-1\Bigr)<\frac{1}{n}.

The first disjunct is open (strict inequality in the continuous map

K\mapsto\Phi(K,c^{*}c)
), and for fixed
m,x_{1},\dots,y_{m}
the second disjunct
is open; hence the right-hand side is open as a countable union.
Therefore each displayed condition is open,
and intersecting over countably many
(t,n,c)
yields a
G_{\delta}
set.
∎

Remark 7.21
.

The key point is that the non-zeroness condition can be absorbed into the disjunction
by the quantifier
\forall t
, avoiding the need for an existential witness within
each “fixed-parameter” set. This reduces the complexity from
\Pi^{0}_{3}
(which one
might naively expect from a
\forall\forall\exists
scheme) to
\Pi^{0}_{2}
.

7.6.
Simplicity for unital Banach algebras

The
\Pi^{0}_{2}
complexity of simplicity established in
Proposition
7.20
extends to
unital
separable Banach
algebras, showing that the
C^{*}
-involution is not needed.

Theorem 7.22
.

Let
P
be a
unital
separable Banach-algebra quotient generator.
The class

\mathrm{Simple}=\{K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(P):P/K\text{ is simple}\}
is
\Pi^{0}_{2}

in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

A unital Banach algebra
B=P/K
is simple iff its only proper closed two-sided
ideal is
\{0\}
. This is equivalent to
K
being a maximal proper closed
two-sided ideal in
P
: for every
x\in P\setminus K
, the closed ideal
generated by
K
and
x
is all of
P
,
i.e.

1\in\overline{K+PxP}
.
Since
D^{P}
is dense, it suffices to check this for
x\in D^{P}
.
Indeed, the set
\{b\in B:1\in\overline{BbB}\}
is
open
in
B
:
if
1\in\overline{BbB}
then there exist
y_{j},z_{j}
with

\|\sum y_{j}bz_{j}-1\|<1
, and the same inequality persists for
b^{\prime}

sufficiently close to
b
(by continuity of multiplication).
Hence its complement

S=\{b\in B:1\notin\overline{BbB}\}
is closed.
If
B
is not simple, there exists a non-zero element
a\in B
with
a\in S
.
Since
D^{P}+K
is dense in
B
, choose
x_{m}\in D^{P}
with
x_{m}+K\to a
.
By closedness of
S
, for large
m
we have
x_{m}+K\in S
and
x_{m}\notin K
,
so the condition fails for
x=x_{m}
.
Thus
B
is simple iff for every
x\in D^{P}
,
either
x\in K
or
1\in\overline{K+PxP}
,
i.e.

1\in\overline{BxB}
for
x+K\neq 0
.
Equivalently,

\forall x\in D^{P}\ \forall t,n\in\mathbb{N}:\ \Bigl(\Phi(K,x)<\tfrac{1}{t}\Bigr)\ \lor\\
\Bigl(\exists m\in\mathbb{N}\ \exists y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\in D^{P}:\ \Phi\Bigl(K,1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}y_{j}xz_{j}\Bigr)<\tfrac{1}{n}\Bigr).

Indeed, if
\Phi(K,x)=0
then
\Phi(K,x)<1/t
for every
t
, so the displayed condition holds.
If
\Phi(K,x)>0
, choose
t
with
\Phi(K,x)\geqslant 1/t
; then for that
t
the disjunction forces
the right-hand existential clause for every
n
,
i.e.

1\in\overline{BxB}
.

For fixed
(x,t,n)
, the set of
K
satisfying the displayed disjunction is open,
since it is a union of strict inequalities in Wijsman-continuous functions.
Intersecting over the countable parameter set
(x,t,n)
yields a
G_{\delta}
set.
Hence
\mathrm{Simple}
is
\Pi^{0}_{2}
.
∎

7.7.
Nuclear dimension

The
nuclear dimension
of a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
, denoted

\dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}(B)
, is the smallest non-negative integer
n
such that for
every finite
F\subseteq B
and every
\varepsilon>0
there exist a
finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra
F_{0}
, a c.p.c. map
\psi:B\to F_{0}
, and c.p.c. maps
\varphi_{0},\dots,\varphi_{n}:F_{0}\to B
of order zero such that

\|\sum_{i=0}^{n}\varphi_{i}\psi(x)-x\|<\varepsilon
for all
x\in F

(see
[
47
]
).

Lemma 7.23

(Stable coding of c.p.c. order-zero maps from finite-dimensional algebras)
.

Let

F_{0}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}M_{d_{j}}

be a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra, and for each
j
fix matrix units

\{e_{ab}^{(j)}:1\leqslant a,b\leqslant d_{j}\}
for the summand
M_{d_{j}}
.

Then there exist finitely many noncommutative
*
-polynomials

r_{1}(\bar{z}),\dots,r_{M}(\bar{z})

in variables

\bar{z}=\bigl(z_{ab}^{(j)}:1\leqslant j\leqslant\ell,\ 1\leqslant a,b\leqslant d_{j}\bigr),

and, for every
\varepsilon>0
, a tolerance
\delta_{F_{0}}(\varepsilon)>0
such that for
every
C^{*}
-algebra
B
the following hold.

(i)

If
\varphi:F_{0}\to B
is completely positive, contractive, and order zero, then the
tuple

z_{ab}^{(j)}:=\varphi(e_{ab}^{(j)})

satisfies

r_{s}(\bar{z})=0\qquad(1\leqslant s\leqslant M).

(ii)

If a tuple

\bar{y}=\bigl(y_{ab}^{(j)}\bigr)\subseteq B

satisfies

\|r_{s}(\bar{y})\|<\delta_{F_{0}}(\varepsilon)\qquad(1\leqslant s\leqslant M),

then there exists a completely positive contractive order-zero map

\varphi:F_{0}\to B

such that

\|\varphi(e_{ab}^{(j)})-y_{ab}^{(j)}\|<\varepsilon

for all
j,a,b
.

Proof.

This is the standard weak stability of the finite-dimensional order-zero relations.
By
[
47
, Theorem 2.3]
, c.p.c. order-zero maps

F_{0}\to B
are in bijection with
*
-homomorphisms

C_{0}((0,1])\otimes F_{0}\longrightarrow B.

For finite-dimensional
F_{0}
, the cone
C_{0}((0,1])\otimes F_{0}
admits a stable finite
presentation; equivalently, the relations defining the canonical generators

t\otimes e_{ab}^{(j)}
are weakly stable, see

[
47
, Proposition 1.4]
. Choosing such a finite stable presentation
gives the required polynomials
r_{s}
and tolerances
\delta_{F_{0}}(\varepsilon)
.
∎

Proposition 7.24
.

For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the class

\{K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A):\dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}(A/K)\leqslant n\}
is
\Pi^{0}_{3}

in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Fix a finite tuple

\bar{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\subseteq D^{A}

and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
E_{\bar{x},m}
denote the set of all
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
such that there exist
a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra
F_{0}
, a c.p.c. map

\psi:A/K\to F_{0},

and c.p.c. order-zero maps

\varphi_{0},\dots,\varphi_{n}:F_{0}\to A/K

with

\Bigl\|\sum_{i=0}^{n}\varphi_{i}\psi(x_{t}+K)-(x_{t}+K)\Bigr\|<\frac{1}{m}\qquad(1\leqslant t\leqslant r).

It suffices to prove that each

is
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.
Indeed, then

\{K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A):\dim_{\mathrm{nuc}}(A/K)\leqslant n\}=\bigcap_{\bar{x}\in(D^{A})^{<\mathbb{N}}}\ \bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{N}}E_{\bar{x},m},

hence the desired class is
\Pi^{0}_{3}
.

Fix a finite-dimensional algebra type

and write

N(F_{0}):=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}d_{j}^{2}.

Fix matrix units
e_{ab}^{(j)}
for each block.
Set

M_{\bar{x}}:=1+\max_{1\leqslant t\leqslant r}\|x_{t}\|,\qquad\varepsilon_{F_{0},\bar{x},m}:=\frac{1}{4m(n+1)N(F_{0})M_{\bar{x}}},

and let

\delta_{F_{0},\bar{x},m}:=\delta_{F_{0}}(\varepsilon_{F_{0},\bar{x},m})

be the stability tolerance from Lemma
7.23
.

For each
i=0,\dots,n
, a candidate order-zero map
F_{0}\to A/K
will be coded by a tuple

\bar{d}^{(i)}=\bigl(d_{ab}^{(i,j)}\bigr)_{j,a,b}\in(D^{A})^{N(F_{0})}.

Write

\bar{d}=\bigl(\bar{d}^{(0)},\dots,\bar{d}^{(n)}\bigr)\in(D^{A})^{(n+1)N(F_{0})}.

Step 1: approximate order-zero data.

Let

r_{1}(\bar{z}),\dots,r_{M}(\bar{z})

be the polynomials from Lemma
7.23
.
For fixed
F_{0}
and
\bar{d}
, let

be the set of all

and every

Since each map

is continuous in the Wijsman topology,

is open.

Step 2: the incoming map.

Let
\mathrm{CPC}(A,F_{0})
be the compact metrisable space of c.p.c. maps

from Lemma
7.10
.
For fixed
F_{0}
and
\bar{d}
, define

C_{F_{0},\bar{d}}\subseteq\mathrm{CPC}(A,F_{0})\times\mathrm{Ideal}(A)

to be the set of all pairs

such that:

(i)

\psi
descends to the quotient

i.e.

(ii)

for every

Here

denotes the
(a,b)
-matrix entry of the

Condition (1) is closed by Lemma
7.10
.
Condition (2) is open: the coefficients
\psi(x_{t})^{(j)}_{ab}
depend continuously on

\psi
, the displayed algebraic expression depends continuously on these coefficients,
and
\Phi
is continuous in
K

Hence

is locally closed in the compact-by-Polish space

By Lemma

.

is open and

is

,
the set

is

.

We claim that

where the outer union runs over all finite-dimensional algebra types

F_{0}\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}M_{d_{j}}
.
Since there are only countably many such types and
D^{A}
is countable,
this will show that

is
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.

We prove the two inclusions.

(
\subseteq
).

Assume
K\in E_{\bar{x},m}
.
Then there exist a finite-dimensional algebra
F_{0}
, a c.p.c. map

\psi_{0}:A/K\to F_{0},

and c.p.c. order-zero maps

\varphi_{i}:F_{0}\to A/K\qquad(0\leqslant i\leqslant n)

such that

\Bigl\|\sum_{i=0}^{n}\varphi_{i}\psi_{0}(x_{t}+K)-(x_{t}+K)\Bigr\|<\frac{1}{4m}\qquad(1\leqslant t\leqslant r).

Compose
\psi_{0}
with the quotient map

to obtain

For each
i,j,a,b
, choose

so close to a lift of
\varphi_{i}(e_{ab}^{(j)})
that:

(i)

all relations

hold modulo

within tolerance

;

(ii)

after replacing each exact image

by

is still within

.

This is possible because the tuples

satisfy the relations

exactly,
the set

is dense in

Thus

(
\supseteq

Assume

and
\bar{d}

Since

7.8.
Property (SP)

Proposition 7.25
.

A
C^{*}
-algebra
B
has
property (SP)
if every non-zero hereditary subalgebra
of
B
contains a non-zero projection (see
[
5
, V.2.2.14]
).
The class of
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
such that
A/K
has property (SP)
is
\Pi^{0}_{3}
in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Write
B=A/K
.

B
has property (SP) iff for every non-zero positive
a\in B_{+}
there exists a

non-zero
projection
p\in\overline{aBa}
.

By Lemma
7.6
(ii), it suffices to range over
a=b^{*}b+K

with
b\in D^{A}
, and to express non-zeroness by
\Phi(K,b^{*}b)>0
.
Indeed, the set of non-zero positive
a\in B_{+}
such that
\overline{aBa}

contains a non-zero projection is open in
B_{+}\setminus\{0\}
: if

q\neq 0
is a projection in
\overline{aBa}
, choose

x\in B
with

\|q-axa\|<1/4
.
(This is possible because
aBa
is dense in the hereditary
C^{*}
-subalgebra
\overline{aBa}
.)
Then the same inequality persists for all
a^{\prime}
sufficiently close to
a
,
so
\overline{a^{\prime}Ba^{\prime}}
also contains a non-zero projection.

Fix a tolerance
\eta>0
small enough that if
y\in B
satisfies

\|y^{2}-y\|<\eta
and
\|y^{*}-y\|<\eta
, then functional calculus produces a projection

p(y)
with
\|p(y)-y\|<1/4
(any standard
\eta
from
[
35
]
suffices).
In particular, if additionally
\|y\|>1/2
, then
p(y)\neq 0
.

For
b\in D^{A}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
consider the condition

\Phi(K,b^{*}b)=0\ \lor\ \exists p,x\in D^{A}:\\
\Phi(K,p^{2}-p)<\eta\ \wedge\ \Phi(K,p^{*}-p)<\eta\ \wedge\ \Phi(K,p)>\tfrac{1}{2}\\
\wedge\ \Phi\bigl(K,p-b^{*}b\,x\,b^{*}b\bigr)<\tfrac{1}{n}.

If
\Phi(K,b^{*}b)>0
, the second disjunct asserts the existence of a non-zero
approximate projection
p+K
lying within
1/n
of the hereditary subalgebra

H:=\overline{(b^{*}b+K)B(b^{*}b+K)}.

Choose
h\in H
with
\|h-(p+K)\|<1/n
; for large
n
, the element
h
is also
an approximate (self-adjoint) idempotent.
Since
H
is a closed
C^{*}
-subalgebra, functional calculus
inside
H

produces a genuine projection
q\in H
close to
h
, and
\|q\|\geqslant\|p+K\|-1/4>0

ensures

.
Thus
H
contains a non-zero projection.

For fixed parameters, the first disjunct is closed and the second is a countable union
of open sets (strict
\Phi
-inequalities), hence the whole condition is
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.
Intersecting over
b\in D^{A}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
gives a
\Pi^{0}_{3}
set.
∎

7.9.
Tracial states

Proposition 7.26
.

The class of
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
such that
A/K
admits a tracial state is closed
(in particular
\Pi^{0}_{1}
) in the Wijsman topology.

Proof.

Let
T(A)
be the compact (metrisable) simplex of tracial states on
A

with its weak
∗
topology.
A tracial state on
B=A/K
is the same as a tracial state
\tau\in T(A)

vanishing on
K
(compose with the quotient map
A\to A/K
, and conversely factor
through
A/K
).

Fix the dense
*
-subalgebra
D^{A}\subseteq A
.
For
\tau\in T(A)
and
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
, the condition
K\subseteq\ker(\tau)
is
equivalent to

\forall a\in D^{A}:\ |\tau(a)|\leqslant\|a+K\|_{A/K}=\Phi(K,a),

since
\tau
is contractive and
\Phi(K,k)=0
for
k\in K
.

For each fixed
a\in D^{A}
, the map
(\tau,K)\mapsto|\tau(a)|-\Phi(K,a)
is continuous
on
T(A)\times\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
(weak
∗

\tau
and Wijsman continuity in

hence the inequality defines a closed subset.

Since

is compact, the projection of

onto
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
is closed, and this

for which
A/K
carries a tracial state.
∎

Remark 7.27

(Crossed products and Rokhlin actions)
.

A full treatment of crossed-product codes and action codes would require an
additional parameter-space construction for compact metrisable spaces, group
actions, and quotient actions in the present framework.
Such a treatment should lead to analytic/Borel upper bounds for the following classes:
for a fixed countable discrete group
G
, the class of
K
such that
A/K
is
isomorphic to a crossed product
C(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}G
should be analytic;
and for coded actions
\alpha:G\curvearrowright A/K
, the Rokhlin property should
be
\Pi^{0}_{3}
in the pair
(K,\alpha)
.
These facts are not used elsewhere in the paper, so we omit the details.

Remark 7.28

(Approximately inner automorphisms)
.

Let
B=A/K
.

Coding automorphisms.

In our quotient coding, it is convenient to regard an automorphism
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(B)

as being specified by its values on the named dense
*
-subalgebra
D^{A}+K\subseteq B
.
Concretely, a
code
for
\alpha
may be taken to be a sequence

\bar{y}=(y_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
in
D^{A}
intended to represent
\alpha(u_{m}+K)=y_{m}+K
,
together with the requirement that the assignment
u_{m}+K\mapsto y_{m}+K
extends to a

*
-automorphism of
B
.
This extension condition can be written as a countable family of constraints
expressing
*
-homomorphism identities on
D^{A}
(e.g. preservation of products,

*
, and rational linear combinations) and the existence of a coded inverse; these
are Borel conditions in the pair
(K,\bar{y})
by the general definability scheme
(Theorem
3.16
).

Approximate innerness.

An automorphism
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(B)
is
approximately inner
if it belongs to the
point-norm closure of the inner automorphism group
\mathrm{Inn}(B)
, equivalently:
for every finite
F\subseteq B
and every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a unitary

u\in B
such that

\max_{x\in F}\|\alpha(x)-uxu^{*}\|<\varepsilon.

Since
D^{A}+K
is dense, it suffices to test finite
F\subseteq D^{A}+K
and

\varepsilon=1/n
.
Moreover, the unitary quantifier can be reduced to the fixed countable dense set

D^{A}
by stability of the unitary relation:
if
v\in B
is sufficiently close to a unitary (
i.e.

\|v^{*}v-1\|
and
\|vv^{*}-1\|

are small), then functional calculus produces a genuine unitary
u(v)
close to
v
.
Thus, for a fixed tolerance
\eta>0
(small enough for unitary stability),
approximate innerness of
\alpha
is equivalent to the following countable scheme:

\forall m\in\mathbb{N}\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\ \exists v\in D^{A}:\\
\Phi(K,v^{*}v-1)<\eta\ \wedge\ \Phi(K,vv^{*}-1)<\eta\\
\wedge\ \max_{j\leqslant m}\Phi\!\bigl(K,\alpha(u_{j}+K)-v(u_{j}+K)v^{*}\bigr)<\tfrac{1}{n}.

Here
\alpha(u_{j}+K)
is read from the chosen automorphism code (e.g. via the
representatives
y_{j}+K
).
The displayed predicate is a finite conjunction of strict
\Phi
-inequalities, hence
open; the quantifier pattern is
\forall\forall\exists(\text{open})
.
Therefore, the set of coded pairs
(K,\alpha)
with
\alpha
approximately inner is
a
\Pi^{0}_{3}
subset of the ambient coding space.

Context.

For each fixed
B
(separable),
\mathrm{Aut}(B)
equipped with the point-norm topology is a
Polish group and
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{ai}}(B)
is a closed normal subgroup (being the
closure of
\mathrm{Inn}(B)
). Globally, in the quotient coding, the previous paragraph
identifies approximate innerness as a uniform Borel condition on
pairs

(K,\alpha)
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Lemma 8.6

(Presenting the Grothendieck group from countable semigroup data)
.

Let
M
be a commutative monoid and let
\nu:\mathbb{N}\to M
be surjective.
Assume given sets

Z\subseteq\mathbb{N},\qquad E\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{2},\qquad A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{3}

such that

n\in Z\iff\nu(n)=0,\qquad(m,n)\in E\iff\nu(m)=\nu(n),\qquad(m,n,k)\in A\iff\nu(m)+\nu(n)=\nu(k).

Let
H\leqslant\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
be the subgroup generated by

\{\mathbf{e}_{n}:n\in Z\}\cup\{\mathbf{e}_{m}-\mathbf{e}_{n}:(m,n)\in E\}\cup\{\mathbf{e}_{m}+\mathbf{e}_{n}-\mathbf{e}_{k}:(m,n,k)\in A\}.

Then

\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}/H\cong G(M),

where
G(M)
denotes the Grothendieck group of
M
.

Proof.

Let
\iota:M\to G(M)
be the canonical map and define

\pi:\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}\to G(M),\qquad\pi(\mathbf{e}_{n})=\iota(\nu(n)).

By the defining properties of
Z,E,A
, every generator of
H
lies in
\ker(\pi)
,
so
\pi
factors through a surjective homomorphism

\overline{\pi}:\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}/H\to G(M).

It remains to prove injectivity.
Take
x\in\ker(\pi)
and write

x=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{e}_{m_{i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathbf{e}_{n_{j}}.

Since
\pi(x)=0
in the Grothendieck group, there exists
c\in M
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\nu(m_{i})+c=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\nu(n_{j})+c

in
M
. Choose
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
\nu(\ell)=c
.

We claim that every finite sum of
\nu
-values can be reduced, modulo
H
, to a
single generator. More precisely, for any indices
q_{1},\dots,q_{t}
there exists

u\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathbf{e}_{q_{i}}-\mathbf{e}_{u}\in H\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nu(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\nu(q_{i}).

This is proved by induction on
t
.
For
t=1
there is nothing to show.
If
t\geqslant 2
, choose
u^{\prime}
with

\nu(u^{\prime})=\nu(q_{1})+\nu(q_{2}),

possible by surjectivity of
\nu
.
Then
(q_{1},q_{2},u^{\prime})\in A
, so

\mathbf{e}_{q_{1}}+\mathbf{e}_{q_{2}}-\mathbf{e}_{u^{\prime}}\in H,

and the induction hypothesis applies to
u^{\prime},q_{3},\dots,q_{t}
.

Applying this reduction to the two sums

m_{1},\dots,m_{r},\ell\qquad\text{and}\qquad n_{1},\dots,n_{s},\ell

yields indices
u,v\in\mathbb{N}
with

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{e}_{m_{i}}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}-\mathbf{e}_{u}\in H,\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathbf{e}_{n_{j}}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}-\mathbf{e}_{v}\in H,

and

\nu(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\nu(m_{i})+c=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\nu(n_{j})+c=\nu(v).

Hence
(u,v)\in E
, so
\mathbf{e}_{u}-\mathbf{e}_{v}\in H
.
Subtracting the two displayed relations and cancelling
\mathbf{e}_{\ell}
, we obtain

x\in H
.
Thus
\ker(\overline{\pi})=0
, and
\overline{\pi}
is an isomorphism.
∎

.

Equip
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)

9.

Let
A
and
B
be separable
C^{*}
-algebras. For

\bullet\in\{\max,\min\}
write

for the corresponding
tensor product, and equip each ideal space with the Wijsman topology.
For
I\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
and
J\in\mathrm{Ideal}(B)
define

\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}(I,J)=\ker(q_{I}\otimes_{\bullet}q_{J})\subseteq A\otimes_{\bullet}B.

When
\bullet=\max
one has

\mathcal{J}_{\max}(I,J)=\overline{I\odot B+A\odot J}
.
For
\bullet=\min
, this description holds whenever one factor is exact;
see
[

.

Proposition 9.1

(Upper semicontinuity)
.

Assume either
\bullet=\max
, or
\bullet=\min
and one factor is exact.
Then for each
z\in A\otimes_{\bullet}B
the function

(I,J)\longmapsto\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}(I,J)\bigr)

is upper semicontinuous on
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)\times\mathrm{Ideal}(B)
.

Proof.

Write
d(I,J)=\mathrm{dist}(z,\overline{I\odot B+A\odot J})
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose

w\in I\odot B+A\odot J
such that
\|z-w\|_{\bullet}<d(I,J)+\varepsilon
.
Write

w=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(i_{k}\otimes b_{k}+a_{k}\otimes j_{k})

with
i_{k}\in I
,
j_{k}\in J
.
If
(I_{n},J_{n})\to(I,J)
Wijsman, then
d(i_{k},I_{n})\to 0
and
d(j_{k},J_{n})\to 0
.
Choose
i_{k}^{(n)}\in I_{n}
and
j_{k}^{(n)}\in J_{n}
with
\|i_{k}-i_{k}^{(n)}\|\to 0

and
\|j_{k}-j_{k}^{(n)}\|\to 0
. Set

w_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(i_{k}^{(n)}\otimes b_{k}+a_{k}\otimes j_{k}^{(n)})\in I_{n}\odot B+A\odot J_{n}.

By continuity of the tensor product bilinear map,
\|w_{n}-w\|_{\bullet}\to 0
.
Hence for large

,

\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}(I_{n},J_{n}))\leqslant\|z-w_{n}\|_{\bullet}\leqslant\|z-w\|_{\bullet}+\|w-w_{n}\|_{\bullet}<d(I,J)+2\varepsilon.

Taking
\limsup
and letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
gives upper semicontinuity.
∎

While upper semicontinuity falls short of continuity, it already
pins down the Borel complexity of the tensor-ideal assignment.

Wijsman
topology one can say more:
\mathfrak{T}_{\bullet}

is of Borel class
2
(
i.e.

\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-measurable).
More precisely, for each
z\in A\otimes_{\bullet}B
and

r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, the preimage

\{(I,J):\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}(I,J))<r\}

is
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
(equivalently,
F_{\sigma}
) in the Wijsman topology.
This is the regularity that the complexity arguments in
Section
10
require.

Proof.

The Wijsman topology on
\mathrm{Ideal}(A\otimes_{\bullet}B)
is the initial topology
for the family
K\mapsto d(z,K)
, so its subbasis consists of both the sublevel sets

\{K:\mathrm{dist}(z,K)<r\}
and the superlevel sets
\{K:\mathrm{dist}(z,K)>r\}
.
By Proposition
9.1
, for each fixed
z
the map

(I,J)\mapsto\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}(I,J)\bigr)

is upper semicontinuous, hence the strict sublevel sets

\{(I,J):\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}(I,J))<r\}
are open (and therefore

F_{\sigma}=\Sigma^{0}_{2}
since
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)\times\mathrm{Ideal}(B)
is metrisable).
For the superlevel sets, upper semicontinuity gives

\{(I,J):\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}(I,J))\geqslant q\}
closed for each
q
, and

\{\mathrm{dist}>r\}=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb{Q},\,q>r}\{\mathrm{dist}\geqslant q\}

is therefore
F_{\sigma}
(
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
).
Thus preimages of both families of Wijsman subbasic opens are
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
.
∎

Theorem 9.3

(Wijsman continuity of the tensor product under exactness)
.

Let
A
and
B
be separable
C^{*}
-algebras.

(i)

For the maximal tensor product, the map
\mathfrak{T}_{\max}
is continuous
for the Wijsman topologies on all three ideal spaces.

(ii)

If one factor, say
A
, is exact, then
\mathfrak{T}_{\min}
is also
Wijsman-continuous.

Proof.

Because the Wijsman topology is metrisable, it suffices to prove sequential continuity.

Let

(I_{m},J_{m})\longrightarrow(I,J)

in
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)\times\mathrm{Ideal}(B)
.
Set

T:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\},

where
\mathbb{N}
is discrete and
\infty
is the point at infinity.
Define

I_{t}=\begin{cases}I_{m},&t=m\in\mathbb{N},\\
I,&t=\infty,\end{cases}\qquad J_{t}=\begin{cases}J_{m},&t=m\in\mathbb{N},\\
J,&t=\infty,\end{cases}

and write

A_{t}:=A/I_{t},\qquad B_{t}:=B/J_{t}.

For
a\in A
define a section

\widehat{a}(t):=a+I_{t}\in A_{t},

and similarly for
b\in B
.

The quotient families are continuous fields.

For every fixed
a\in A
, the function

t\longmapsto\|\widehat{a}(t)\|=\|a+I_{t}\|=\mathrm{dist}(a,I_{t})

is continuous on
T
: continuity at points of
\mathbb{N}
is automatic because they are isolated,
and continuity at
\infty
is exactly the Wijsman convergence
I_{m}\to I
.
By Dixmier’s criterion for continuous fields of quotient algebras

[
13
, Prop. 10.3.2]
, the family
\{A_{t}:t\in T\}
, together with the
sections generated by the
\widehat{a}
’s, forms a continuous field of
C^{*}
-algebras
over
T
.
The same argument applies to the family
\{B_{t}:t\in T\}
.

Maximal tensor products.

By the standard continuity theorem for fibrewise maximal tensor products of continuous
fields; see, for example, Kirchberg–Wassermann
[
32
]
, the
fibrewise maximal tensor product is again a continuous field over
T
, whose fibre at
t
is

A_{t}\otimes_{\max}B_{t}.

Therefore, for every algebraic tensor

w=\sum_{r=1}^{N}a_{r}\odot b_{r}\in A\odot B,

the section

t\longmapsto w_{t}:=\sum_{r=1}^{N}(a_{r}+I_{t})\otimes(b_{r}+J_{t})\in A_{t}\otimes_{\max}B_{t}

has continuous norm:

t\longmapsto\|w_{t}\|_{A_{t}\otimes_{\max}B_{t}}\quad\text{is continuous.}

Now let
z\in A\otimes_{\max}B
be arbitrary.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
w\in A\odot B
with
\|z-w\|<\varepsilon
.
Then for every
t\in T
,

\bigl|\|z_{t}\|-\|w_{t}\|\bigr|\leqslant\|z_{t}-w_{t}\|\leqslant\|z-w\|<\varepsilon,

so continuity of
t\mapsto\|w_{t}\|
implies continuity of
t\mapsto\|z_{t}\|
.

Finally, for every
t\in T
one has the canonical quotient identification

(A\otimes_{\max}B)/\mathcal{J}_{\max}(I_{t},J_{t})\cong A_{t}\otimes_{\max}B_{t}.

Hence

\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\max}(I_{t},J_{t})\bigr)=\|z_{t}\|_{A_{t}\otimes_{\max}B_{t}}.

Therefore

\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\max}(I_{m},J_{m})\bigr)\longrightarrow\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\max}(I,J)\bigr)\qquad(m\to\infty)

for every
z\in A\otimes_{\max}B
, which is exactly Wijsman continuity of

\mathfrak{T}_{\max}
.

Minimal tensor products under exactness.

Assume that
A
is exact.
Then each quotient
A_{t}=A/I_{t}
is exact.
By Kirchberg–Wassermann’s exactness theorem for fibrewise minimal tensor products
(see
[
32
, Thm. 4.5]
), the fibrewise minimal tensor product
of the above two continuous fields is a continuous field over
T
, whose fibre at
t
is

A_{t}\otimes_{\min}B_{t}.

Thus, exactly as above, for every

w=\sum_{r=1}^{N}a_{r}\odot b_{r}\in A\odot B

the function

t\longmapsto\|w_{t}\|_{A_{t}\otimes_{\min}B_{t}}

is continuous, and by density of
A\odot B
in
A\otimes_{\min}B
the same holds for every

z\in A\otimes_{\min}B
.

Because
A
is exact, one has for every
t\in T
the canonical quotient identification

(A\otimes_{\min}B)/\mathcal{J}_{\min}(I_{t},J_{t})\cong A_{t}\otimes_{\min}B_{t},

so

\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\min}(I_{t},J_{t})\bigr)=\|z_{t}\|_{A_{t}\otimes_{\min}B_{t}}.

Hence

\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\min}(I_{m},J_{m})\bigr)\longrightarrow\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\min}(I,J)\bigr)\qquad(m\to\infty)

for every
z\in A\otimes_{\min}B
,
i.e.

\mathfrak{T}_{\min}
is Wijsman-continuous.
∎

Remark 9.4
.

The Borelness result (Proposition

9.1.
Projective and injective tensor products of Banach algebras

Let
A
and
B
be separable Banach algebras. For
\alpha\in\{\pi,\varepsilon\}
write

A\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha}B
for the projective (
\pi
) or injective (
\varepsilon
)
completed tensor product of the underlying Banach spaces.
The projective tensor product
A\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}B
is always a Banach algebra.
The injective tensor product
A\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}B
is a Banach algebra
provided that the multiplication on
A\odot B
extends continuously (which is the case,
for instance, when
A
and
B
are uniform algebras).

If
I\triangleleft A
and
J\triangleleft B
are closed
(two-sided) ideals, set

\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(I,J):=\overline{I\otimes B+A\otimes J}^{\ \|\cdot\|_{\alpha}}\subseteq A\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha}B.

Then
\mathcal{J}_{\pi}(I,J)
is always a closed ideal of
A\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}B
, and

\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(I,J)
is a closed ideal of
A\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}B

whenever the latter is a Banach algebra.
The following topological regularity results hold strictly at the level of Banach
spaces and their closed subspaces, and hence apply to both norms without requiring
additional algebraic assumptions.

Proposition 9.5

(Upper semicontinuity)
.

Fix
\alpha\in\{\pi,\varepsilon\}
and
z\in A\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha}B
.
With the Wijsman topologies on
\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
and
\mathrm{Ideal}(B)
, the function

(I,J)\longmapsto\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(I,J)\bigr)

is upper semicontinuous, hence Borel.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose
w\in I\otimes B+A\otimes J
with

\|z-w\|_{\alpha}<\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(I,J))+\varepsilon
.
Write
w=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(i_{k}\otimes b_{k}+a_{k}\otimes j_{k})
.

If
(I_{n},J_{n})\to(I,J)
Wijsman, pick
i_{k}^{(n)}\in I_{n}
and
j_{k}^{(n)}\in J_{n}
with

i_{k}^{(n)}\to i_{k}
and
j_{k}^{(n)}\to j_{k}
in norm.
Set

w_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(i_{k}^{(n)}\otimes b_{k}+a_{k}\otimes j_{k}^{(n)})\in I_{n}\otimes B+A\otimes J_{n}.

Since
(x,y)\mapsto x\otimes y
is jointly continuous into both

\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}
and
\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\|w_{n}-w\|_{\alpha}\to 0
.
Hence, for large
n
,

\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(I_{n},J_{n}))\leqslant\|z-w_{n}\|_{\alpha}<\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(I,J))+2\varepsilon.

Taking
\limsup
and letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
gives upper semicontinuity.
∎

Proposition 9.6

(Borelness of the tensor-ideal assignment)
.

For
\alpha\in\{\pi,\varepsilon\}
the map

\mathfrak{T}_{\alpha}:\mathrm{Ideal}(A)\times\mathrm{Ideal}(B)\to\mathrm{Ideal}(A\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha}B),\qquad(I,J)\mapsto\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(I,J),

is Borel for any admissible topology on the three ideal spaces.
For the Wijsman topology it is of Baire class
1
:
for each
z\in A\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha}B
and
r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, the preimage

\{(I,J):\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(I,J))<r\}
is open.

Proof.

The Wijsman subbasis consists of both
\{K:\mathrm{dist}(z,K)<r\}
and

\{K:\mathrm{dist}(z,K)>r\}
(Lemma
3.9
).
By Proposition
9.5
, the function

(I,J)\mapsto\mathrm{dist}\bigl(z,\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(I,J)\bigr)
is upper semicontinuous, so

\{(I,J):\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}(I,J))<r\}
is open.
For the superlevel direction,
\{\mathrm{dist}>r\}=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb{Q},\,q>r}\{\mathrm{dist}\geqslant q\}

is
F_{\sigma}
(since
\{\mathrm{dist}\geqslant q\}
is closed for u.s.c. functions).
Thus preimages of both families of Wijsman subbasic opens are
F_{\sigma}
,
giving Borelness (indeed Baire class
1
) of
\mathfrak{T}_{\alpha}
.
∎

D

Fix a separable unital
C^{*}

D
. We say that a separable unital

C^{*}
-algebra
B
is
D
-absorbing
if

B\cong B\otimes_{\min}D
. When
D

usual notion of
D
-stability
; see
[
5
, II.8.5.1]

and
[
45

10.1.
Elliott intertwining

A central theme in the classification programme for
C^{*}
-algebras is the passage from
approximate morphisms to genuine isomorphisms. The fundamental device for this is the

Elliott intertwining argument
, which first appeared in
Elliott’s classification of AF-algebras
[
15
]
and has since become one of
the most widely used tools in the field. The argument says, roughly, that if two
separable
C^{*}
-algebras
B
and
C
can be connected by a back-and-forth sequence of
maps that
approximately
compose to the identity on ever-larger finite subsets,
then
B\cong C

genuinely
.

This theorem matters for us for two reasons. First, the

reduction from an isomorphism to finite-level witnesses
is the mechanism
that makes
D
-absorption (
B\cong B\otimes_{\min}D
) expressible as an analytic condition
(Theorem
10.3
). Without the intertwining theorem, one
would need to quantify over an entire isomorphism—an object living in a
non-separable function space—and the resulting condition would a priori be

\Sigma^{1}_{1}
rather than Borel.
Second, it provides the
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
upper bound for isomorphism of
separable
C^{*}
-algebras (Remark
10.2
): the approximate
intertwining witnesses form a countable Borel family, so the existence of a
complete intertwining is an analytic condition.

The proof of the intertwining theorem is entirely constructive (a standard
diagonal argument), and is by now well-established; see
Rørdam–Larsen–Laustsen
[
41
, Theorem 2.3.3]

and Elliott
[
15
]
.
We do not reproduce it here, but state it for reference and proceed
directly to its descriptive set-theoretic consequences.

Theorem 10.1

(Elliott intertwining
[
41
, Thm. 2.3.3]
)
.

Let
B
and
C
be separable unital
C^{*}
-algebras. Suppose there exist sequences of
unital
*
-homomorphisms
\phi_{n}:B\to C
and
\psi_{n}:C\to B
such that for every

b\in B
and
c\in C
,

\|\psi_{n}\phi_{n}(b)-b\|\to 0,\qquad\|\phi_{n+1}\psi_{n}(c)-c\|\to 0.

Then there exists a unital
*
-isomorphism
\Phi:B\to C
. ∎

10.2.
Finite-level intertwining and Borel complexity

Remark 10.2
.

In any standard coding of separable
C^{*}
-algebras with a named dense sequence,
the isomorphism relation is analytic. One way to see this is to code a candidate
Elliott intertwining by a pair of sequences of finite partial maps between the
named dense sets. The coherence conditions asserting that these finite maps are
approximately
*
-homomorphic and approximately inverse on larger and larger
initial segments form a Borel relation on the Polish space of such sequences.
By Theorem
10.1
, existence of such a coded intertwining
is equivalent to genuine isomorphism. Thus
\cong
is analytic.
Moreover, this analytic upper bound is sharp: the isomorphism relation for separable

C^{*}
-algebras is complete analytic; see

[
18
,
43
]
.

Theorem 10.3
.

Let
D
be a separable unital exact
C^{*}
-algebra.
Then the class of
K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A)
such that
A/K
is
D
-absorbing is analytic.

Proof.

Write
B_{K}=A/K
and

C_{K}:=(A/K)\otimes_{\min}D.

Since
D
is exact, Proposition
9.2
applies to the minimal tensor
product, so the assignment

K\longmapsto\mathcal{J}_{\min}(K,\{0\})\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A\otimes_{\min}D)

is Borel. Therefore the quotient assignment
K\mapsto C_{K}
is Borel in the standard
coding of separable
C^{*}
-algebras.

By Remark
10.2
, the isomorphism relation for separable
C^{*}
-algebras
is analytic in the quotient coding. Pulling this analytic relation back along the Borel
map

K\mapsto(B_{K},C_{K})

shows that

\{K\in\mathrm{Ideal}(A):B_{K}\cong C_{K}\}

is analytic.
∎

Remark 10.4
.

For strongly self-absorbing

\Pi^{0}_{3}

However, obtaining that bound in the present coding requires a separate

11.
TROs and ternary ideals

A ternary ring of operators (TRO) is a closed subspace

T\subseteq\mathcal{B}(H,K)
closed under
[x,y,z]=xy^{*}z
.
A closed subspace
J\subseteq T
is a ternary ideal if

[T,T,J]+[T,J,T]+[J,T,T]\subseteq J.

Lemma 11.1
.

There exists a separable TRO
T_{\mathrm{free}}
generated by a sequence

(u_{n})
of contractions such that for every separable TRO
T
and every
sequence
(t_{n})\subseteq\operatorname{Ball}(T)
there exists a unique
contractive ternary homomorphism

\pi:T_{\mathrm{free}}\to T

with
\pi(u_{n})=t_{n}
for all
n
.
In particular every separable TRO is a quotient of
T_{\mathrm{free}}
by a
closed ternary ideal.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{alg}}
be the algebraic free ternary system on symbols

u_{n}
. Define

\|x\|_{\max}:=\sup\bigl\{\|\rho(x)\|:\ \rho:\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{alg}}\to S\text{ is a ternary homomorphism}\\
\text{into a TRO }S,\ \|\rho(u_{n})\|\leqslant 1\ \forall n\bigr\}.

An induction on ternary word length shows that
\|x\|_{\max}<\infty
for every

x\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{alg}}
: if
x=[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
, then

\|\rho(x)\|\leqslant\|\rho(x_{1})\|\,\|\rho(x_{2})\|\,\|\rho(x_{3})\|
,
and the right-hand side is uniformly bounded over all admissible
\rho
by the
inductive hypothesis.

Let
N=\{x:\|x\|_{\max}=0\}
, set
T_{0}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{alg}}/N
, and let

T_{\mathrm{free}}
be the completion of
T_{0}
. By construction the images of
the
u_{n}
are contractions.

Given
(t_{n})\subseteq\operatorname{Ball}(T)
, the algebraic universal property
gives a unique ternary homomorphism

\rho:\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{alg}}\to T
with
\rho(u_{n})=t_{n}
.
By definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\max}
,
\rho
is contractive, so it factors
through
T_{0}
and extends uniquely to a contractive ternary homomorphism

\pi:T_{\mathrm{free}}\to T
.

Finally, if
T
is separable, choose a dense sequence
(t_{n})
in

\operatorname{Ball}(T)
. The resulting map
\pi
has dense range, and the
range of a ternary homomorphism is closed because such a homomorphism extends
to a
*
-homomorphism between linking
C^{*}
-algebras. Hence
\pi
is
surjective, so
T\cong T_{\mathrm{free}}/\ker\pi
.
∎

Proposition 11.2
.

The space of closed ternary ideals in
T_{\mathrm{free}}
is closed in

\mathrm{SB}(T_{\mathrm{free}})
for any admissible topology, hence Polish.
The quotient map
J\mapsto T_{\mathrm{free}}/J
is a Borel surjection onto separable
TROs (up to isomorphism).

Proof.

Apply Proposition
3.6
to the ternary operation

[x,y,z]=xy^{*}z
.
∎

Write
V=T_{\mathrm{free}}/J
and let
D^{T}\subseteq T_{\mathrm{free}}
be a fixed
countable dense subset; set
\Phi_{T}(J,x)=\|x+J\|_{V}
.

Proposition 11.3

(Borel complexity of TRO properties)
.

In the Wijsman topology on the ternary ideal space of
T_{\mathrm{free}}
:

(i)

Ternary commutativity.

The class of
J
such that
[x,y,z]=[z,y,x]
in
V
for all
x,y,z

is closed (
\Pi^{0}_{1}
).

(ii)

Simplicity.

The class of
J
such that
V
is simple (has no non-trivial closed
ternary ideals) is
\Pi^{0}_{2}
.

(iii)

Rectangular AF.

The class of
J
such that
V
is the closure of an increasing union of
finite-dimensional sub-TROs (equivalently, finite direct sums of rectangular
matrix spaces
M_{p,q}(\mathbb{C})
) is
\Pi^{0}_{3}
.

Proof.

(1) The condition
[x,y,z]=[z,y,x]
in
V
for all
x,y,z\in V
is equivalent to

\Phi_{T}(J,[x,y,z]-[z,y,x])=0
for all
x,y,z\in D^{T}
.
Each such predicate is closed in
J
, and a countable intersection of closed sets is
closed.

(2) A TRO
V
is simple iff for every non-zero
v\in V
, the closed ternary ideal
generated by
v
equals
V
.
This ideal is the closure of all finite sums of iterated ternary products involving
v

(of the form
[a_{1},b_{1},[a_{2},b_{2},\ldots,v,\ldots]]
with
a_{i},b_{i}\in V
), and these
form a countable family when the arguments are drawn from
D^{T}
.
We use the same disjunction device as in Theorem
7.22
:

\forall v\in D^{T}\ \forall w\in D^{T}\ \forall t,n\in\mathbb{N}:\\
\Bigl(\Phi_{T}(J,v)<\tfrac{1}{t}\Bigr)\ \lor\ \Bigl(\exists\tau\in\mathcal{W}(v,D^{T}):\ \Phi_{T}(J,w-\tau)<\tfrac{1}{n}\Bigr),

where
\mathcal{W}(v,D^{T})
denotes the countable set of all finite rational linear
combinations of formal ternary words in
v
and elements of
D^{T}
.
If
\Phi_{T}(J,v)=0
then
v+J=0
and the left disjunct holds for every
t
.
If
\Phi_{T}(J,v)>0
, pick
t
with
\Phi_{T}(J,v)\geqslant 1/t
; the disjunction forces
the right clause for every
n
, giving
w\in\overline{\langle v\rangle_{\mathrm{ter}}}
.

For fixed
(v,w,t,n)
the displayed disjunction is a union of strict continuous
inequalities, hence open.
Intersecting over the countable parameter set yields a
G_{\delta}
set,
i.e.

\Pi^{0}_{2}
.

(3) Every finite-dimensional TRO is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}M_{p_{j},q_{j}}(\mathbb{C})
,
so an approximating sub-TRO is coded by approximate
rectangular matrix unit

systems: families
(e_{ij})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant p,\,1\leqslant j\leqslant q}
in
D^{T}
satisfying the
relations
[e_{ij},e_{kl},e_{rs}]=\delta_{jk}\delta_{lr}e_{is}
to within
tolerance
\delta
(measured via
\Phi_{T}
).
By the TRO version of the finite-dimensional perturbation lemma
(Lemma
7.7
applied componentwise to the linking
C^{*}
-algebra),
approximate rectangular matrix units can be corrected to genuine ones.
The condition “
V
is rectangular AF” becomes

\forall\bar{x}\in(D^{T})^{<\mathbb{N}}\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\ \exists(p_{j},q_{j})_{j\leqslant\ell}\ \exists\text{ approximate rectangular units from }D^{T}:\\
\max_{s}\Phi_{T}\!\bigl(J,x_{s}-\textstyle\sum_{j}\sum_{i,k}\lambda_{s,j,i,k}\,e^{(j)}_{ik}\bigr)<\tfrac{1}{n}

with the rectangular-unit relations holding within tolerance
\delta=\delta(n)
.
This is a
\forall\forall\exists
scheme where the inner existential is a countable
union of open conditions, giving
\Pi^{0}_{3}
.
∎

12.
Countable structures: a complexity catalogue

All spaces in this section are compact zero-dimensional Polish spaces (closed subspaces
of Cantor cubes), and atomic predicates are clopen. For a fixed word
w
in the free
object, the predicate “
[w]=1
in the quotient” is clopen. Consequently:
one universal quantifier over a countable domain yields a closed
(
\Pi^{0}_{1}
) condition;
one existential quantifier yields an open (
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
) condition;
alternations proceed in the expected way.

12.1.
Countable groups

We code
G=F_{\infty}/N
with
N\in\mathrm{NSub}(F_{\infty})\subseteq 2^{F_{\infty}}
.

Order and structure.

•

Finite:

\Sigma^{0}_{2}
(
\exists
finite transversal

T\subseteq F_{\infty}
s.t.
\forall g\in F_{\infty}\;\bigvee_{t\in T}gt^{-1}\in N
;
each membership test is clopen, so the inner condition is closed, and the outer
existential over finite
T
gives
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
).

•

Finitely generated:

\Sigma^{0}_{3}
(
\exists
finite
S\subseteq F_{\infty}

s.t.
\forall g\in F_{\infty}
,

12.1.1.
Universality and embeddability

A fundamental asymmetry between the countable-group and
C^{*}
-algebraic settings
is the failure of universality for embeddings.
There is no
universal
countable group with respect to embeddings: no single
countable group contains an isomorphic copy of every countable group.
Indeed, there are
2^{\aleph_{0}}
pairwise non-isomorphic finitely generated groups
(already among
2
-generated quotients of the free group
F_{2}
), whereas any
fixed countable group has only countably many finitely generated subgroups
(since each is generated by a finite subset).
Thus no countable group can contain copies of all finitely generated groups,
and
a fortiori
none can contain copies of all countable groups;
see, e.g.,
[
36
, Chapter IV]
for related standard facts.
In the quotient coding this means that the parameter space
\mathrm{NSub}(F_{\infty})
cannot
be replaced by the ideal space of a single countable group, in contrast with the

C^{*}
-algebraic setting where
C^{*}_{\max}(F_{\infty})
is universal for separable

C^{*}
-algebras.
Nevertheless,
F_{\infty}
is universal for
presentations
: every countable group
is a quotient of
F_{\infty}
, and the normal subgroup lattice
\mathrm{NSub}(F_{\infty})

serves as the standard Borel space for the classification problem.

Gromov
[
25
]
initiated a systematic study of the
space of
finitely generated groups
(quotients of
F_{k}
for fixed
k
, equipped with the
Chabauty topology), viewing group-theoretic properties as subsets of this space
and asking for their topological and measure-theoretic complexity.
The present catalogue may be seen as an extension of this viewpoint to all
countable groups (using
F_{\infty}
in place of
F_{k}
) within the framework
of the Borel hierarchy.

12.1.2.
Sofic groups

Sofic groups were introduced by Gromov
[
25
]
as a common generalisation of amenable
and residually finite groups. They form a robust class of groups that are
well-approximated by finite symmetric groups. The motivation for studying sofic groups
stems from the fact that they are known to satisfy several major conjectures that remain
open for arbitrary groups, such as Gottschalk’s surjunctivity conjecture and
Kaplansky’s direct finiteness conjecture for group rings. Gromov
[
25
]

asked whether
every
group is sofic; this remains one of the most prominent open
problems in group theory.

Definition 12.1

(Sofic groups)
.

A countable group
G
is
sofic
if for every finite subset
F\subseteq G
and
every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a natural number
d
and a map

\sigma:F\to\mathrm{Sym}(d)
(not necessarily a homomorphism) such that:

(i)

\sigma(1)=\mathrm{id}
(if
1\in F
);

(ii)

for all
u,v,w\in F
with
uv=w
in
G
, the normalised Hamming distance
satisfies
d_{H}(\sigma(u)\sigma(v),\sigma(w))<\varepsilon
;

(iii)

for all distinct
u,v\in F
,

d_{H}(\sigma(u),\sigma(v))>1-\varepsilon
.

Proposition 12.2
.

The class of
N\in\mathrm{NSub}(F_{\infty})
such that

is sofic is
G_{\delta}

(
i.e.

\Pi^{0}_{2}
).

Proof.

Let
G_{N}=F_{\infty}/N
. Fix a finite set
S\subseteq F_{\infty}
containing
1
and closed
under inverses. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and write
\varepsilon=1/k
. For
d\in\mathbb{N}
and a map

\sigma:S\to\mathrm{Sym}(d)
, we say that
\sigma
is an
(S,\varepsilon)
-sofic
approximation for
G_{N}
if the following hold:

(a)

\sigma(1)=\mathrm{id}
;

(b)

(
multiplicativity on the partial multiplication table
)
for all
u,v,w\in S
with
uvw^{-1}\in N
(
i.e.

uv=w
in
G_{N}
), we have

d_{H}(\sigma(u)\sigma(v),\sigma(w))<\varepsilon
;

(c)

(
almost injectivity
)
for all
u,v\in S
with
uv^{-1}\notin N
(
i.e.

u\neq v
in
G_{N}
), we have

d_{H}(\sigma(u),\sigma(v))>1-\varepsilon
.

(Here

denotes the normalised Hamming metric on

For fixed

for which

is an

(S,1/k)
-sofic approximation depends only on membership of the finitely many words

in

permutations
\sigma(u)

topology on

is clopen.

Let

be the union of these clopen sets over all

and all

is open.

Finally,

is sofic iff for every finite

and every

-approximation,
i.e.
if and only if

set.
∎

(Dedekind finiteness for countable rings)

12.5.
Optimality of Borel bounds

The bounds established above are not merely upper estimates: several fundamental
properties are
complete
for their respective Borel classes, demonstrating
that the definability method assigns optimal ranks.

Theorem 12.7
.

In the parameter space
\mathrm{NSub}(F_{\infty})
, the property of being
a finite group is
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-complete. The same holds for rings, abelian groups,
and lattices in their respective parameter spaces.

Proof.

Membership in
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
was noted in Section
12.1
. For completeness,
we reduce from the canonical
\Sigma^{0}_{2}
-complete set

\mathrm{Fin}=\{\alpha\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}:\exists N\;\forall n\geqslant N\;(\alpha(n)=0)\}
.

Define
V:=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
with canonical basis
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
For
\alpha\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
set

k_{\alpha}(n):=\sum_{i<n}\alpha(i)\in\mathbb{N},

and define a homomorphism
\pi_{\alpha}:F_{\infty}\to V
by
\pi_{\alpha}(x_{n})=e_{k_{\alpha}(n)}
.
Let

N_{\alpha}:=\ker(\pi_{\alpha})\in\mathrm{NSub}(F_{\infty}).

For the abelian-group coding, define the surjective homomorphism

\widetilde{\pi}_{\alpha}:\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}\to V,\qquad\widetilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(e_{n})=e_{k_{\alpha}(n)},

and let

H_{\alpha}:=\ker(\widetilde{\pi}_{\alpha})\in\mathrm{Sub}(\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}).

Then

\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}/H_{\alpha}\cong\bigoplus_{j\in\operatorname{im}(k_{\alpha})}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},

which is finite iff
\alpha\in\mathrm{Fin}
and countably infinite otherwise.
Exactly as in the free-group coding, membership of a fixed element of
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
in

H_{\alpha}
depends only on finitely many values of
\alpha
, so the map

\alpha\mapsto H_{\alpha}
is continuous.

Then
G_{\alpha}:=F_{\infty}/N_{\alpha}
is abelian of exponent dividing
2
, and in
G_{\alpha}

we have
x_{n}=x_{n+1}
whenever
\alpha(n)=0
(since then
k_{\alpha}(n)=k_{\alpha}(n+1)
).
Thus
G_{\alpha}
has one
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-generator for each
\sim_{\alpha}
-equivalence class
of
\mathbb{N}
generated by the edges
n\sim_{\alpha}n{+}1
when
\alpha(n)=0
.

If
\alpha\in\mathrm{Fin}
, then
\alpha(n)=0
for all
n\geqslant N
, so
k_{\alpha}(n)
is bounded
and
G_{\alpha}
is generated by finitely many elements of order
2
, hence is finite.
If
\alpha\notin\mathrm{Fin}
, then
k_{\alpha}(n)\to\infty
, so
G_{\alpha}\cong\bigoplus_{\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

and is infinite.

Finally, the map
\alpha\mapsto N_{\alpha}
is continuous.
Indeed, for a fixed reduced word
w\in F_{\infty}
let
M
be the largest index of a generator
appearing in
w
. Then
\pi_{\alpha}(w)
depends only on the values
\alpha(0),\dots,\alpha(M{-}1)
,
hence the set
\{\alpha:w\in N_{\alpha}\}=\{\alpha:\pi_{\alpha}(w)=0\}
is clopen in
2^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Therefore
\alpha\mapsto N_{\alpha}
is continuous as a map into
2^{F_{\infty}}
.

For rings, let

V_{\alpha}:=\bigoplus_{j\in\operatorname{im}(k_{\alpha})}\mathbb{F}_{2}e_{j},\qquad R_{\alpha}:=\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus V_{\alpha},

with multiplication

(a,v)(b,w):=(ab,aw+bv),

so that
V_{\alpha}^{2}=0
.
Define the unique unital ring homomorphism

\rho_{\alpha}:R_{\infty}\to R_{\alpha}

by

\rho_{\alpha}(1)=(1,0),\qquad\rho_{\alpha}(X_{n})=(0,e_{k_{\alpha}(n)})\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

This map is surjective, since
(1,0)
is the unit of
R_{\alpha}
and the elements

(0,e_{k_{\alpha}(n)})
generate the square-zero ideal
V_{\alpha}
.
Then
R_{\alpha}
is finite iff
\operatorname{im}(k_{\alpha})
is finite,
i.e.
if and only if

\alpha\in\mathrm{Fin}
; otherwise
R_{\alpha}
is countably infinite.

For lattices, let
I_{\alpha}:=\operatorname{im}(k_{\alpha})
and let
L_{\alpha}
be
the lattice consisting of all finite subsets of
I_{\alpha}
together with the top
element
I_{\alpha}
, ordered by inclusion. This lattice is finite iff

\alpha\in\mathrm{Fin}
and countably infinite otherwise. It is generated by the
top element together with the singletons
\{k_{\alpha}(n)\}
, so there is a
surjective lattice homomorphism

\lambda_{\alpha}:L_{\infty}\to L_{\alpha}

and the remaining generators to

\{k_{\alpha}(n)\}

.

In each case, membership of a fixed word / polynomial / lattice term in the

\alpha

\alpha\mapsto N_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\mapsto H_{\alpha}
,

\alpha\mapsto\ker(\rho_{\alpha})
,
\alpha\mapsto\theta_{\alpha}

are continuous, completing the proof.
∎

Theorem 12.8
.

In the parameter space
\mathrm{Sub}(\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})})
, the properties
of being a torsion group and of being a divisible group are
both
\Pi^{0}_{2}
-complete.

Proof.

Membership in
\Pi^{0}_{2}
is recorded in Section
12.2
.
We reduce from the canonical
\Pi^{0}_{2}
-complete set

P_{\infty}=\{\alpha\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}:\exists^{\infty}n\;(\alpha(n)=1)\}
.

Torsion.

Map
\alpha
to the subgroup
H_{\alpha}\leqslant\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
generated by

\{2e_{n}-e_{n+1}:\alpha(n)=0\}\cup\{e_{n}:\alpha(n)=1\}
,
and set
A_{\alpha}=\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}/H_{\alpha}
.

If
\alpha\in P_{\infty}
, for every
n
there exists
m>n
with
\alpha(m)=1
,
so
e_{m}=0
in
A_{\alpha}
. The intermediate relations
2e_{k}=e_{k+1}

(for
k
with
\alpha(k)=0
) then force
2^{m-n}e_{n}=0
.
Thus every generator is annihilated by a power of
2
and
A_{\alpha}
is torsion.

If
\alpha\notin P_{\infty}
, there exists
N
with
\alpha(n)=0
for all

n\geqslant N
. The generators
\{e_{n}\}_{n\geqslant N}
satisfy

2e_{n}=e_{n+1}
with no torsion relations, generating a copy of
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
.
Hence
A_{\alpha}
is not torsion.

Divisible.

Map
\alpha
to the subgroup
J_{\alpha}\leqslant\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
generated by

\{e_{n}-e_{n+1}:\alpha(n)=0\}\cup\{e_{n}-(n{+}2)!\,e_{n+1}:\alpha(n)=1\}
,
and set
B_{\alpha}=\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}/J_{\alpha}
.
These relations identify
e_{n}=d_{n}e_{n+1}
where
d_{n}=1
if
\alpha(n)=0
and

d_{n}=(n{+}2)!
if
\alpha(n)=1
, so
B_{\alpha}
is the direct limit of copies of
\mathbb{Z}

with bonding maps
\times d_{n}
.

If
\alpha\in P_{\infty}
, then
\alpha(n)=1
for infinitely many (hence unbounded)
n
.
Fix
m\geqslant 1
and choose
n
with
\alpha(n)=1
and
n{+}2\geqslant m
; then
m\mid(n{+}2)!
.
In the direct limit this makes the image of
e_{0}
divisible by
m
. As
m
was
arbitrary,
B_{\alpha}
is divisible. Moreover,
B_{\alpha}
is torsion-free (all bonding
maps are injective) and generated by the image of
e_{0}
, hence it is a divisible
torsion-free rank-
1
group, so
B_{\alpha}\cong\mathbb{Q}
.

If
\alpha\notin P_{\infty}
, then
\alpha(n)=0
for all sufficiently large
n
, so
all but finitely many bonding maps are
1
. Hence
B_{\alpha}\cong\mathbb{Z}[1/M]
for some

M\in\mathbb{N}
, which is not divisible.

Both maps
\alpha\mapsto H_{\alpha}
and
\alpha\mapsto J_{\alpha}
are continuous
(each relation depends on one coordinate of
\alpha
), establishing

\Pi^{0}_{2}
-hardness.
∎

13.
A
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete property in a quotient coding

We give an explicit non-Borel example within the quotient paradigm, showing that some
natural Banach-space properties of quotients cannot be located within the Borel
hierarchy. This calibrates the reach of the definability method developed in earlier
sections.

13.1.
Closed subsets of Cantor space and commutative quotients

Let
K_{0}=2^{\mathbb{N}}
be the Cantor space and let
\mathscr{F}(K_{0})
be the hyperspace of
non-empty closed subsets of
K_{0}
with its Effros Borel structure (equivalently, the
standard Borel structure induced by the Vietoris topology).

For
F\in\mathscr{F}(K_{0})
define the closed ideal

I_{F}=\{f\in C(K_{0}):f|_{F}=0\}\triangleleft C(K_{0}).

Then the restriction map
C(K_{0})\to C(F)
is a surjective
*
-homomorphism with kernel

I_{F}
, hence
C(K_{0})/I_{F}\cong C(F)
as
C^{*}
-algebras.

Lemma 13.1
.

The map
F\mapsto I_{F}
from
\mathscr{F}(K_{0})
onto
\mathrm{Ideal}(C(K_{0}))
is a
Borel isomorphism.

Proof.

For
f\in C(K_{0})
,

\mathrm{dist}(f,I_{F})=\|f|_{F}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in F}|f(x)|.

Hence
F\mapsto I_{F}
is Borel. The map is bijective by the ideal theory of
commutative
C^{*}
-algebras.

To see that the inverse is Borel, fix a countable clopen basis

\mathcal{U}
of
K_{0}
. For each
U\in\mathcal{U}
, the indicator
1_{U}
is a
continuous function on
K_{0}
, and

F\cap U\neq\varnothing\iff\sup_{x\in F}1_{U}(x)=1\iff\mathrm{dist}(1_{U},I_{F})=1.

Since the hit sets
\{F:F\cap U\neq\varnothing\}
generate the Vietoris
Borel structure on
\mathscr{F}(K_{0})
, the inverse map is Borel.
∎

13.2.
Countability of closed sets is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete

Let
\mathrm{CTbl}\subseteq\mathscr{F}(K_{0})
be the set of countable closed subsets
of
K_{0}
.

Theorem 13.2
.

\mathrm{CTbl}
is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete.

Proof.

It is classical that
\mathrm{CTbl}
is coanalytic: a closed set
F\subseteq K_{0}
is
uncountable iff it contains a perfect subset.

\Pi^{1}_{1}
-hardness goes back to Hurewicz; see

[
33
, Theorem 27.5 and Section 33.B]
. In particular, for every
uncountable Polish space
X
, the classes of countable compact subsets

K_{\mathrm{No}}(X)
and countable closed subsets
F_{\mathrm{No}}(X)
are

\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete. Since
K_{0}=2^{\mathbb{N}}
is compact, every closed subset of
K_{0}

is compact, and the Wijsman/Effros/Vietoris Borel structures coincide on

\mathscr{F}(K_{0})
. Hence
\mathrm{CTbl}
is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete.
∎

13.3.
Separable dual of
C(F)

Lemma 13.3
.

For
F\in\mathscr{F}(K_{0})
, the dual Banach space
C(F)^{*}
is separable iff
F
is
countable.

Proof.

If
F
is countable, then every regular Borel measure on
F
is atomic and

C(F)^{*}\cong\ell_{1}(F)
, which is separable.

If
F
is uncountable, then the family of point masses
(\delta_{x})_{x\in F}\subseteq C(F)^{*}

is uncountable and satisfies
\|\delta_{x}-\delta_{y}\|=2
for
x\neq y
.
Hence
C(F)^{*}
contains an uncountable
2
-separated subset and is non-separable.
∎

Theorem 13.4
.

In the quotient coding
F\mapsto C(K_{0})/I_{F}\cong C(F)
, the property

\{F\in\mathscr{F}(K_{0}):\ C(F)^{*}\ \text{is separable}\}

is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete. Consequently, the corresponding class of ideals

\{I\in\mathrm{Ideal}(C(K_{0})):\ C(K_{0})/I\ \text{has separable dual}\}

is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete.

Proof.

By Lemma
13.3
, the set in question is exactly
\mathrm{CTbl}
,
which is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete by Theorem
13.2
.
Since
F\mapsto I_{F}
is a Borel isomorphism (Lemma
13.1
),

\Pi^{1}_{1}
-completeness transfers to the ideal coding.
∎

Remark 13.5
.

Theorem
13.4
shows that certain natural properties of
quotient algebras are
provably not Borel
, even for commutative
C^{*}
-algebras.
This stands in contrast to the properties in our catalogue
(Sections
6
–
7
), all of which are
Borel at finite levels. The distinction reflects a fundamental boundary: properties
whose definitions involve quantification over uncountable sets (here, over all
regular Borel measures, or equivalently over all closed subsets of
F
) tend to escape
the Borel hierarchy, whereas those expressible by countable quantification over
a dense set of witnesses remain Borel.

13.4.
Superatomic Boolean algebras

The parameterisation of countable Boolean algebras provides another application
of the
\mathrm{CTbl}
space. Let
B_{\infty}
be the free Boolean algebra on countably many
generators. The Stone space of
B_{\infty}
is canonically homeomorphic to the Cantor
space
K_{0}=2^{\mathbb{N}}
.

By Stone duality, there is a canonical bijection between ideals

I\in\mathrm{Id}(B_{\infty})
and closed subsets
F\in\mathscr{F}(K_{0})
: the quotient Boolean
algebra
B_{\infty}/I
is isomorphic to the algebra of clopen subsets of
F
.

A Boolean algebra is called
superatomic
if every homomorphic image contains
an atom. A classical result in topology and logic establishes that a countable
Boolean algebra is superatomic if and only if its Stone space is countable.

Theorem 13.6

(Superatomicity is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete)
.

In the parameter space of countable Boolean algebras coded by ideals in the free
Boolean algebra
\mathrm{Id}(B_{\infty})
, the property

\{I\in\mathrm{Id}(B_{\infty}):\ B_{\infty}/I\ \text{is superatomic}\}

is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete.

Proof.

Under the Stone duality correspondence
I\leftrightarrow F
, the map

F\mapsto I_{F}
from
\mathscr{F}(K_{0})
to
\mathrm{Id}(B_{\infty})
is a Borel isomorphism.
By the characterisation above,
B_{\infty}/I_{F}
is superatomic if and only if

F\in\mathrm{CTbl}
.

Since
\mathrm{CTbl}
is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete
(Theorem
13.2
), the preimage of
\mathrm{CTbl}
under this
Borel isomorphism is exactly the set of ideals generating superatomic quotients.
Therefore superatomicity is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete.
∎

Remark 13.7
.

Proposition
12.3
shows that, for countable abelian groups,
slenderness is already Borel: indeed

\mathrm{Slender}=\mathrm{TorsionFree}\cap\{G:G^{1}=0\}\in\Pi^{0}_{3}.

Thus the only remaining issue is the optimality of this upper bound.

14.
Extensions and open questions

(i)

Completeness.

Determine completeness (optimality) of some of the
\Pi^{0}_{3}
classes in the
C^{*}

setting (AF, real rank
\leqslant n
). Are any of these
\Pi^{0}_{3}
-complete?
The
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-completeness results in Section
13
—separable
dual (Theorem
13.4
) and superatomicity
(Theorem
13.6
)—show that non-Borel phenomena span both
continuous and discrete quotient codings. It would be interesting to identify further
such examples among less elementary properties.

(ii)

Other invariants.

The Borel coding of
K_{0}
(Theorem
8.7
) and
K_{1}
(Theorem
8.9
)
extends to all higher
K
-groups via Bott periodicity (Remark
8.10
).
It remains to code traces and broader Elliott-type invariants as countable Borel data.

(iii)

Tensor-product regularity.

Refine tensor-product kernel regularity: when does the tensor-ideal
assignment become continuous for natural choices of topology beyond
the cases treated in Theorem
9.3
?

(iv)

Slender abelian groups.

The class of slender groups is
\Pi^{0}_{3}
in the present
coding. Determine whether this upper bound is optimal; in particular, is
slenderness
\Pi^{0}_{3}
-complete?

(v)

Dedekind finiteness for Banach algebras.

Proposition
6.15
gives a
\Pi^{0}_{2}
(
i.e.

G_{\delta}
) upper bound for
Dedekind finiteness.
Is this sharp? The unbounded-witness phenomenon
of Daws–Horváth
[
12
]
is suggestive but does not by itself resolve
the question of optimality.

(vi)

Simplicity for non-unital Banach algebras.

Theorem
7.22
establishes that simplicity of unital Banach
algebras is
\Pi^{0}_{2}
. Does the same bound hold in the non-unital setting,
where the appropriate criterion is that every element generates a dense two-sided
ideal?

(vii)

Isomorphism complexity in the non-
C^{*}
settings.

For
P=C^{*}_{\max}(F_{\infty})
, the relation

K\sim L\iff P/K\cong P/L

is precisely isomorphism of separable
C^{*}
-algebras, whose complexity is
already known by
[
18
,
43
]
.
It would be interesting to determine the analogous complexity for the
Banach-algebra, TRO, and other quotient codings developed here.

(viii)

Other categories.

Apply the framework to Banach lattices with additional structure
(Banach function spaces, KB-spaces), operator systems, Jordan algebras, and other
operator-algebraic structures.
