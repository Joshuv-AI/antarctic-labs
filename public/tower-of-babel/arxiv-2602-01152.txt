An Efficient Memory Gradient Method for Extreme M-Eigenvalues of Elastic type Tensors Thanks: This work was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of P.R. China (Grant No.12171064).
Zhuolin Du, Yisheng Song

Abstract
M-eigenvalues of fourth order hierarchically symmetric tensors play a significant role in nonlinear elastic material analysis and quantum entanglement problems. This paper focuses on computing extreme M-eigenvalues for such tensors. To achieve this, we first reformulate the M-eigenvalue problem as a sequence of unconstrained optimization problems by introducing a shift parameter. Subsequently, we develop a memory gradient method specifically designed to approximate these extreme M-eigenvalues. Under this framework, we establish the global convergence of the proposed method. Finally, comprehensive numerical experiments demonstrate the efficacy and stability of our approach. Keywords: Hierarchically symmetric tensors; M-eigenvalues; Memory gradient method; Global convergence AMS Subject Classification(2010): 90C23, 65H17, 15A18, 90C30.

2
Unconstrained framework for M-eigenvalues of elastic type tensors

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
n
dimensional real Euclidean space. Generally, scalars are denoted by lowercase letters such as
a,b
and vectors are denoted by bold lowercase letters such as
{\bf x},{\bf y}
. Furthermore, matrices are denoted by capital letters such as
{\bf A}
,
{\bf B}
and tensors are denoted by calligraphic capital letters such as
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
. The superscript
⊤
stands for the transpose of vectors and matrices. Let
\mathbb{H}^{m\times n\times m\times n}
denote the set of all four order hierarchically symmetric tensors. For any
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijkl})\in\mathbb{H}^{m\times n\times m\times n}
, the corresponding gradient is as follows:

\nabla_{\bf x}(\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y})=2\mathcal{A}\cdot{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y},~~~~\nabla_{\bf y}(\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y})=2\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}\cdot.

For the sake of simple,
\mathcal{A}\cdot{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}
and
\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}\cdot
are also denoted by
\mathcal{A}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}
and
\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}
respectively.

Motivated by the work of Auchmuty
[
1
]
, we consider the following unconstrained optimization problem, which has close relationship with the M-eigenvalue problem (see Theorem
2.1
):

\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\\
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{subarray}}f({\bf x},{\bf y})=\frac{1}{4}({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}.

(2.1)

Then, the gradient of
f({\bf x},{\bf y})
with respect to
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are given by

g_{1}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\nabla_{\bf x}f({\bf x},{\bf y})=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x}){\bf x}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}-\mathcal{A}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y},

(2.2)

g_{2}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\nabla_{\bf y}f({\bf x},{\bf y})=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y}){\bf y}-\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}.

(2.3)

Moreover, we have the following results.

Theorem 2.1

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{H}^{m\times n\times m\times n}
be a nonzero tensor. Assume that
\lambda^{\ast}
is the largest M-eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}
. Then we have the following results

(1) The problem (
2.1
) has a global minimum.

(2) Let
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{\bf 0\}
and
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\bf 0\}
be critical points of (
2.1
). Then
\lambda=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})
is a positive M-eigenvalue of
{\cal A}
, with the associated M-eigenvectors
{\bf u}={\bf x}/\|{\bf x}\|
and
{\bf v}={\bf y}/\|{\bf y}\|
.

(3) If
\lambda^{*}>0
, then
f({\bf x},{\bf y})
attains its global minimum
f_{\min}=-\frac{1}{4}(\lambda^{\ast})^{2}
at nonzero critical points
({\bf x},{\bf y})
.

Proof.
(1) Since the unit sphere is compact and the function
({\bf u},{\bf v})\mapsto\left|\mathcal{A}{\bf u}{\bf v}{\bf u}{\bf v}\right|
is continuous, denote
C=\max_{\|{\bf u}\|=1,\|{\bf v}\|=1}\left|\mathcal{A}{\bf u}{\bf v}{\bf u}{\bf v}\right|
, and the optimal value
C
is attainable, i.e., there exist unit vectors
{\bf u}_{0},{\bf v}_{0}
such that
C=\left|\mathcal{A}{\bf u}_{0}{\bf v}_{0}{\bf u}_{0}{\bf v}_{0}\right|
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is a nonzero tensor, we have
C>0
. Now, we consider two cases based on the sign of
\mathcal{A}{\bf u}{\bf v}{\bf u}{\bf v}
.
Let
t=\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\|{\bf y}\|^{2}
, if
t=0
, then
f({\bf x},{\bf y})=0
. Otherwise, we obtain that

f({\bf x},{\bf y})=\frac{1}{4}({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}\geq\frac{1}{4}\|{\bf x}\|^{4}\|{\bf y}\|^{4}-\frac{C}{2}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\|{\bf y}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}t^{2}-\frac{C}{2}t.

By direct computation, we know that
f({\bf x},{\bf y})\geq-\frac{1}{4}C^{2}
, and the equality holds when
t=C
.

(2) By (
2.2
)-(
2.3
), the critical points of
f({\bf x},{\bf y})
satisfy

\mathcal{A}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}{\bf x}~\mbox{and}~\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y}){\bf y}.

(2.4)

Denote
{\bf u}={\bf x}/\|{\bf x}\|,{\bf v}={\bf y}/\|{\bf y}\|
. Then, combining this with (
2.4
), it holds that

\mathcal{A}{\bf v}{\bf u}{\bf v}=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})\frac{({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}}{\|{\bf y}\|^{2}}{\bf u}~\mbox{and}~\mathcal{A}{\bf u}{\bf v}{\bf u}=\frac{({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}}{\|{\bf x}\|^{2}}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y}){\bf v}.

By Definition
1.2
, we have that
\lambda=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})>0
satisfies
\mathcal{A}{\bf v}{\bf u}{\bf v}=\lambda{\bf u}
,
\mathcal{A}{\bf u}{\bf v}{\bf u}=\lambda{\bf v}
. Thus,
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{\bf 0\}
and
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\bf 0\}
are M-eigenvectors corresponding to the positive M-eigenvalue
\lambda
.

(3) Let
\lambda
be an arbitrary positive M-eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}
. By Definition
1.2
, there exist unit vectors
{\bf u},{\bf v}
such that
\mathcal{A}{\bf v}{\bf u}{\bf v}=\lambda{\bf u}~\mbox{and}~\mathcal{A}{\bf u}{\bf v}{\bf u}=\lambda{\bf v}
. Denote
{\bf x}=p{\bf u}
and
{\bf y}=q{\bf v}
for some scalars
p,q\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
The gradients of
f({\bf x},{\bf y})
are derived as follows

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bf x}}f({\bf x},{\bf y})

\displaystyle=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}{\bf x}-\mathcal{A}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}

\displaystyle=p^{2}q^{4}p{\bf u}-\mathcal{A}(q{\bf v})(p{\bf u})(q{\bf v})

\displaystyle=p^{3}q^{4}{\bf u}-pq^{2}\mathcal{A}{\bf v}{\bf u}{\bf v}=p^{3}q^{4}{\bf u}-pq^{2}\lambda{\bf u}

\displaystyle=pq^{2}(p^{2}q^{2}-\lambda){\bf u}

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bf y}}f({\bf x},{\bf y})

\displaystyle=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y}){\bf y}-\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}

\displaystyle=p^{4}q^{2}q{\bf v}-\mathcal{A}(p{\bf u})(q{\bf v})(p{\bf u})

\displaystyle=p^{4}q^{3}{\bf v}-p^{2}q\mathcal{A}{\bf u}{\bf v}{\bf u}=p^{4}q^{3}{\bf v}-p^{2}q\lambda{\bf v}

\displaystyle=p^{2}q(p^{2}q^{2}-\lambda){\bf v}.

Note that
{\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x}=(p{\bf u})^{\top}(p{\bf u})=p^{2}
, and
{\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y}=(q{\bf v})^{\top}(q{\bf v})=q^{2}
. Choosing
p,q
such that
p^{2}q^{2}=\lambda
, we have
\nabla_{{\bf x}}f({\bf x},{\bf y})=0
and
\nabla_{{\bf y}}f({\bf x},{\bf y})=0
. Consequently,
({\bf x},{\bf y})
is a nonzero critical point of
f({\bf x},{\bf y})
, and satisfying
({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})=p^{2}q^{2}=\lambda
.
At this critical point, we also have
\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}=\lambda^{2}
.
Therefore,

f({\bf x},{\bf y})=-\frac{1}{4}({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}=-\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{2}\geq-\frac{1}{4}(\lambda^{\ast})^{2}.

This implies that the global minimum is

f_{\min}=-\frac{1}{4}(\lambda^{\ast})^{2},

attained at a critical point corresponding to
\lambda^{\ast}=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})
.

\Box

Remark 2.1

If all M-eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}
are non-positive, then
f({\bf x},{\bf y})
has no nonzero critical points. Indeed, the existence of a nonzero critical point
({\bf x},{\bf y})
would imply that
\lambda=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})>0
is an M-eigenvalue. Thus, in this case, the set of critical points is restricted to trivial solutions of the form
(\mathbf{0},{\bf y})
or
({\bf x},\mathbf{0})
, which do not correspond to any M-eigenvalue.

To address the case where
\mathcal{A}
has only non-positive M-eigenvalues, we introduce the following shifted problem with a parameter
t>0
as follows

\mathop{\min}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\\
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{subarray}}f_{t}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\frac{1}{4}({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}-\frac{t}{2}({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y}).

(2.5)

For a sufficiently large
t
, it holds that
f_{t}({\bf x},{\bf y})<0
for any nonzero vectors
({\bf x},{\bf y})
.
On the other hand,
f_{t}({\bf 0},{\bf y})=0
and
f_{t}({\bf x},{\bf 0})=0
for all
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore, points of the form
(\bf 0,{\bf y})
or
({\bf x},\bf 0)
are maximizers of
f_{t}({\bf x},{\bf y})
, while the global minimum is strictly negative and attained at a nonzero point. The gradients of
f_{t}({\bf x},{\bf y})
are given by

g_{t_{1}}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\nabla_{\bf x}f_{t}({\bf x},{\bf y})=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y})^{2}{\bf x}-\mathcal{A}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}-t({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y}){\bf x},

(2.6)

g_{t_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\nabla_{\bf y}f_{t}({\bf x},{\bf y})=({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x})^{2}({\bf y}^{\top}{\bf y}){\bf y}-\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}-t({\bf x}^{\top}{\bf x}){\bf y}.

(2.7)

It is clear that for all
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the points
(\bf 0,{\bf y})
and
({\bf x},\bf 0)
are critical points of
f_{t}({\bf x},{\bf y})
. More importantly, any nonzero critical point
({\bf x},{\bf y})
of (
2.5
) corresponds to an M-eigenvalue
\lambda=({{\bf x}}^{\top}{\bf x})({{\bf y}}^{\top}{\bf y})-t
. Since
f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
attains negative values for nonzero
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
, a suitable descent algorithm for (
2.5
) is expected to converge to a nonzero critical point.

Therefore, by solving either the original problem (
2.1
) or the shifted problem (
2.5
), the M-eigenvalues and their associated M-eigenvectors can be effectively computed. The following algorithm outlines this adaptive procedure.

Algorithm 1

Step 0. Input
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{P}^{m\times n\times m\times n},~t\geq 1,~\rho>1
and
0<\epsilon\ll 1
,
k=0
.

Step 1. Solving problem (
2.1
) to obtain
({\bf x}_{k},{\bf y}_{k})
. Compute
\lambda_{k}=({{\bf x}_{k}}^{\top}{\bf x}_{k})({{\bf y}_{k}}^{\top}{\bf y}_{k})
.

Step 2. If
\min\{\|{\bf x}_{k}\|,\|{\bf y}_{k}\|\}>\epsilon
, output
({\bf x}_{k},{\bf y}_{k})
and
\lambda_{k}
, Stop. Otherwise go to

Step 3.

Step 3. Solve problem (
2.5
) to obtain
({\bf x}_{k},{\bf y}_{k})
. Compute
\lambda_{k}=({{\bf x}_{k}}^{\top}{\bf x}_{k})({{\bf y}_{k}}^{\top}{\bf y}_{k})-t
.

Step 4. If
\min\{||\mathbf{x}_{k}||,||\mathbf{y}_{k}||\}>\epsilon
, output
(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{y}_{k})
and
\lambda_{k}
, Stop. Otherwise, update

t=\rho t
, and repeat Step 3.

Remark 2.2

The iterative process between Step 2 and Step 3 constitutes an inner loop. Since any suitable descent algorithm applied to (
2.5
) is guaranteed to converge to a nonzero critical point provided that
t
is sufficiently large, this inner loop ensures finite termination. Consequently, Algorithm 1 is well-defined.

Remark 2.3

In practice, the same unconstrained optimization method should be applied to solve both the original problem (
2.1
) and the shifted problem (
2.5
). In the subsequent section, we introduce a specialized memory gradient method tailored for this class of problems.

3
A memory gradient method and its convergence

In this section, we present the MGM designed to solve the unconstrained optimization problems formulated in Section 2. Specifically, MGM is employed as the primary solver in Steps 1 and 3 within the adaptive framework of Algorithm 1.

For simplicity, let
\mathbf{z}=(\mathbf{x}^{\top},\mathbf{y}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
and
\Phi(\mathbf{z}):\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the objective function (representing either
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
or
f_{t}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
). Correspondingly, let
g(\mathbf{z})=\nabla\Phi(\mathbf{z})
denote the gradient. The iterative update scheme of the proposed method is given by

{\bf z}_{k+1}={\bf z}_{k}+\alpha_{k}{\bf d}_{k},

where
\alpha_{k}>0
is a step size determined by a line search procedure. The search direction
{\bf d}_{k}
is computed as follows for
k\geq 1

{\bf d}_{k}=-\gamma_{k}g({\bf z}_{k})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}{\bf d}_{k-i},

(3.1)

where
\beta_{k_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
and
\gamma_{k}>0
. For the case
k<N
, (
3.1
) is interpreted as
{\bf d}_{k}=-\gamma_{k}g({\bf z}_{k})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k}{\bf d}_{k-i}
. The initial search direction is set as the steepest descent direction with a scaling parameter
\gamma_{0}>0
, specifically,
{\bf d}_{0}=-\gamma_{0}g({\bf z}_{0})
. Note that the parameters are different from those proposed by Miele et al.
[
36
]
. To ensure the search direction satisfies descent property, we define the parameters as follows

\beta_{k_{i}}=\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}\varphi_{k_{i}}^{\dagger},\quad\text{where }a^{\dagger}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }a=0,\\
\dfrac{1}{a}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Here, the scalars
\varphi_{k_{i}}
are chosen to satisfy

\begin{cases}\varphi_{k_{1}}>\max\left\{\dfrac{{g({\bf z}_{k})}^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-1}}{\gamma_{k}},\ 0\right\},&i=1,\\
\varphi_{k_{i}}\geq\max\left\{\dfrac{{g({\bf z}_{k})}^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}}{\gamma_{k}},\ 0\right\},&i=2,\ldots,N.\end{cases}

(3.3)

It is important to note that
a^{\dagger}a\leq 1
, and from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), it follows that
\beta_{k_{1}}>0
and
\beta_{k_{i}}\geq 0
for
i=2,\ldots,N
.

Building on these properties, the following theorem establishes the descent property of the proposed search direction.

Theorem 3.1

Let the search direction
{\bf d}_{k}
be defined by (
3.1
). Suppose that the parameters
\beta_{k_{i}}
and
\varphi_{k_{i}}
are chosen to satisfy conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) for all
k
. Then, the descent condition holds for all
k
, i.e.,
g(\mathbf{z}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{d}_{k}<0
.

Proof.
For
k=0
, we have
g({\bf z}_{0})^{\top}{\bf d}_{0}=-\gamma_{0}\|g({\bf z}_{0})\|^{2}<0
, which establishes the descent property at the initial step.

For
k\geq 1
, we consider the expression

-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\beta_{k_{i}}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i},~i=1,\ldots,N.

Case 1:
i=1
.
From (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), and considering that
\beta_{k_{1}},\gamma_{k}>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\beta_{k_{1}}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\beta_{k_{1}}\max\left\{g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-1},0\right\}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}\varphi_{k_{1}}^{\dagger}\max\left\{g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-1},0\right\}

\displaystyle<-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}\varphi_{k_{1}}^{\dagger}\varphi_{k_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

The strict inequality follows from the condition in (
3.3
) for
i=1
, and the last inequality uses the property
a^{\dagger}a\leq 1
.

Case 2:
i=2,\ldots,N
. Similarly,

\displaystyle-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\beta_{k_{i}}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\beta_{k_{i}}\max\left\{g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i},0\right\}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}\varphi_{k_{i}}^{\dagger}\max\left\{g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i},0\right\}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}\varphi_{k_{i}}^{\dagger}\varphi_{k_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Combining this with (
3.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\beta_{k_{i}}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right)<0.

Therefore, the descent condition is satisfied and the desired results hold.

\Box

It is worth noting that the index satisfying the strict inequality (
3.3
) is not necessarily restricted to
i=1
. Specifically, the conclusion of the theorem remains valid provided that there exists at least one index
i
such that

\varphi_{k_{i}}>\max\left\{\frac{g({\bf z}_{k}){\bf d}_{k-i}}{\gamma_{k}},\,0\right\}.

However, utilizing the most recent iterative information is a natural choice.

Next, we focus on establishing the sufficient descent condition, i.e.,

g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\leq-c\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2},\quad\forall~k\geq 1

(3.5)

for some positive constant
c
. The following theorem shows that condition (
3.5
) is satisfied by the proposed method under stronger assumptions on
\gamma_{k},\varphi_{k_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
.

Theorem 3.2

Let the search direction
{\bf d}_{k}
be defined by (
3.1

\bar{\gamma}>0
such that
\gamma_{k}\geq\bar{\gamma}
for all
k

\varphi_{k_{i}}
(

\begin{cases}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-1}+\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|\|{\bf d}_{k-1}\|<\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{1}},&(i=1),\\[6.0pt]
g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}+\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|\|{\bf d}_{k-i}\|\leq\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}},&(i=2,\ldots,N).\end{cases}

(3.6)

Then, the proposed method satisfies the sufficient descent condition (
3.5

Proof.
Define the index set
\mathcal{I}=\{i\in\{1,\dots,N\}\mid g(\mathbf{z}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{d}_{k-i}>0\}

t=|\mathcal{I}|
. It is clear that
0\leq t\leq N
.

Note that for

, we have
g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\leq 0

3.1
),(
3.2
), and (

), it holds that

\displaystyle g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}\varphi_{k_{i}}^{\dagger}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}}{\varphi_{k_{i}}}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{\gamma_{k}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}}{g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}+\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|\|{\bf d}_{k-i}\|}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{\gamma_{k}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}}{2g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}\left(1-\frac{t}{2N}\right).

Since
0\leq t\leq N

1-\frac{t}{2N}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Combining this with

, it follows that

g(\mathbf{z}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{d}_{k}\leq-\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2}\|g(\mathbf{z}_{k})\|^{2}\leq-\frac{\bar{\gamma}}{2}\|g(\mathbf{z}_{k})\|^{2},

which implies that the sufficient descent condition (

.

\Box

Note that condition (

) is stronger than (
3.4
).

Remark 3.1

It is crucial to observe that the objective function
\Phi(\mathbf{z})
is invariant under the transformation
\mathbf{x}\to\xi\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}\to\xi^{-1}\mathbf{y}
for any
\xi\neq 0
. Consequently, the generated sequence
\{\mathbf{z}_{k}\}
could theoretically be unbounded where
\|\mathbf{x}\|\to\infty
and
\|\mathbf{y}\|\to 0
. In Algorithm 2, the rescaling step restricts the iterates to satisfy
\|\mathbf{x}\|=\|\mathbf{y}\|
. This operation guarantees the compactness required for the convergence analysis (see Lemma
3.1
) without altering the objective function value or the directions of the M-eigenvectors.

To prove the global convergence of the proposed method, we first establish several key lemmas.

Lemma 3.1

Let
\{\mathbf{z}_{k}\}
be the sequence generated by MGM. Then, the sequence
\{\mathbf{z}_{k}\}
is bounded. Specifically, there exist constants
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}>0
such that
\|\mathbf{x}_{k}\|\leq\eta_{1}
and
\|\mathbf{y}_{k}\|\leq\eta_{2}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Proof.
Since
\{\mathbf{z}_{k}\}
is generated by Algorithm 2, the descent property ensures
\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{k})\leq\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{0})
for all
k
. Moreover, the algorithm enforces
\|\mathbf{x}_{k}\|=\|\mathbf{y}_{k}\|
. Let
s_{k}=\|\mathbf{x}_{k}\|\|\mathbf{y}_{k}\|
. It follows that
\|\mathbf{x}_{k}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{y}_{k}\|^{2}=s_{k}
, and consequently
\|\mathbf{z}_{k}\|^{2}=2s_{k}
.

Recall the objective function
\Phi(\mathbf{z})
defined in (
2.5
) (which covers the original problem when
t=0
).

\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{k})=\frac{1}{4}\|\mathbf{x}_{k}\|^{4}\|\mathbf{y}_{k}\|^{4}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\mathbf{y}_{k}-\frac{t}{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{k}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{y}_{k}\|^{2}.

By Theorem 2.1(1),
|\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}_{k}\mathbf{y}_{k}\mathbf{x}_{k}\mathbf{y}_{k}|\leq C\|\mathbf{x}_{k}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{y}_{k}\|^{2}=Cs_{k}^{2}
for some constant
C>0
. Consequently,

\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{k})\geq\frac{1}{4}s_{k}^{4}-\frac{C}{2}s_{k}^{2}-\frac{t}{2}s_{k}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}s_{k}^{4}-\frac{C+t}{2}s_{k}^{2}.

Suppose for contradiction that the sequence
\{\mathbf{z}_{k}\}
is unbounded. Then there exists a subsequence
\{\mathbf{z}_{k_{j}}\}
such that
\|\mathbf{z}_{k_{j}}\|\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
, which is equivalent to
s_{k_{j}}\to\infty
.
Thus, we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{k_{j}})\geq\lim_{s_{k_{j}}\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{4}s_{k_{j}}^{4}-\frac{C+t}{2}s_{k_{j}}^{2}\right)=+\infty.

This implies that for sufficiently large
j
,
\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{k_{j}})>\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{0})
, which leads to a contradiction. Therefore, the sequence
\{\mathbf{z}_{k}\}
is bounded. Consequently, there exist constants
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}>0
such that
\|\mathbf{x}_{k}\|\leq\eta_{1}
and
\|\mathbf{y}_{k}\|\leq\eta_{2}
for all
k\geq 0
.

\Box

For the subsequent convergence analysis, define

\mathcal{D}=\left\{\mathbf{z}=(\mathbf{x}^{\top},\mathbf{y}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\mid\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq\eta_{1},\|\mathbf{y}\|\leq\eta_{2}\right\},

(3.9)

where
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are constants from Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2

If
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{H}^{m\times n\times m\times n}
, then
\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}
and
\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}
are Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.
Let
M=\max_{i,j,k,l}|a_{ijkl}|
. First, we establish the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{A}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}
. For any
({\bf x},{\bf y}),({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y})\in\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}-\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\mathbf{y}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}a_{ijkl}\,y_{j}\,(x_{k}-x_{k}^{\prime})\,y_{l}\right)_{i=1}^{m}\right\|

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{m}M\sum_{j=1}^{n}|y_{j}|\sum_{l=1}^{n}|y_{l}|\sum_{k=1}^{m}|x_{k}-x_{k}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{m}M\left(\sqrt{n}\|\mathbf{y}\|\right)\left(\sqrt{n}\|\mathbf{y}\|\right)(\sqrt{m}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|)

\displaystyle\leq mnM\eta_{2}^{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|=P_{1}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|,

where
P_{1}=mnM\eta_{2}^{2}
is a positive constant.

Next, for
(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}),(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{D}
, utilizing the inequality
|ab-a^{\prime}b^{\prime}|\leq|a||b-b^{\prime}|+|b^{\prime}||a-a^{\prime}|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\mathbf{y}-\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}a_{ijkl}\,x^{\prime}_{k}\,(y_{j}y_{l}-y^{\prime}_{j}y^{\prime}_{l})\,\right)_{i=1}^{m}\right\|

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{m}M\sum_{k=1}^{m}|x_{k}^{\prime}|\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}\left(|y_{j}||y_{l}-y_{l}^{\prime}|+|y_{l}^{\prime}||y_{j}-y_{j}^{\prime}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{m}M\left(\sqrt{m}\eta_{1}\right)\left(2\sqrt{n}\eta_{2}\sqrt{n}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|\right)

\displaystyle=2mnM\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|=P_{2}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|,

where
P_{2}=2mnM\eta_{1}\eta_{2}
is a positive constant. Thus,
\mathcal{A}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to both
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
.

Similarly, we analyze
\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}
. For
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y})\in\mathcal{D}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}-\mathcal{A}{\bf x}^{\prime}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sum_{i,k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ijkl}\,y_{j}\,(x_{i}x_{k}-x^{\prime}_{i}x_{k}^{\prime})\right)_{l=1}^{n}\right\|

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n}M\sum_{j=1}^{n}|y_{j}|\sum_{i,k=1}^{m}\left(|x_{k}||x_{i}-x_{i}^{\prime}|+|x_{i}^{\prime}||x_{k}-x_{k}^{\prime}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n}M\left(\sqrt{n}\eta_{2}\right)\left(2\sqrt{m}\eta_{1}\sqrt{m}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|\right)

\displaystyle=P_{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|.

For
({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}),({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{D}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}{\bf x}^{\prime}{\bf y}{\bf x}^{\prime}-\mathcal{A}{\bf x}^{\prime}{\bf y}^{\prime}{\bf x}^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sum_{i,k=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ijkl}\,x^{\prime}_{i}\,(y_{j}-y_{j}^{\prime})x^{\prime}_{k}\right)_{l=1}^{n}\right\|

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n}M\sum_{i,k=1}^{m}|x_{i}^{\prime}x_{k}^{\prime}|\sum_{j=1}^{n}|y_{j}-y_{j}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n}M\left(m\eta_{1}^{2}\right)\left(\sqrt{n}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|\right)

\displaystyle=mnM\eta_{1}^{2}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|=P_{3}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|,

where
P_{3}=mnM\eta_{1}^{2}
is a positive constant.
By (
3.10
)-(
3.13
), we conclude that both
\mathcal{A}{\bf y}{\bf x}{\bf y}
and
\mathcal{A}{\bf x}{\bf y}{\bf x}
are Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{D}
.

\Box

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\|g_{t_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})-g_{t_{2}}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle=\|g_{t_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})-g_{t_{2}}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y})+g_{t_{2}}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y})-g_{t_{2}}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})\|

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{t_{2}}({\bf x},{\bf y})-g_{t_{2}}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})\|+\|g_{t_{2}}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y})-g_{t_{2}}({\bf x}^{\prime},{\bf y}^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{21}\|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}\|+L_{22}\|{\bf y}-{\bf y}^{\prime}\|.

Combining the above, let
M_{1}=\sqrt{2L_{11}^{2}+2L_{21}^{2}}
and
M_{2}=\sqrt{2L_{12}^{2}+2L_{22}^{2}}
. Using the inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\|g_{t}(\mathbf{z})-g_{t}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|g_{t_{1}}(\mathbf{z})-g_{t_{1}}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\|^{2}+\|g_{t_{2}}(\mathbf{z})-g_{t_{2}}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(L_{11}^{2}+L_{21}^{2}\right)\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|^{2}+2\left(L_{12}^{2}+L_{22}^{2}\right)\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2}\right)\left(\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=L_{t}^{2}\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\|^{2}.

Taking the square root completes the proof with
L_{t}=\max\left(M_{1},M_{2}\right)
.
\Box

Based on the lemmas established above, we now state the following well-known lemma which was proved by Zoutendijk
[
53
]
, which applies to general iterative methods.

Lemma 3.4

Let the sequences
\{{\bf z}_{k}\}
and
\{{\bf d}_{k}\}
be generated by MGM. Then,

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\right)^{2}}{\|{\bf d}_{k}\|^{2}}<\infty.

Proof.
From the Wolfe line search condition (
3.8
) and Lemma
3.3
, it follows that

(\sigma-1)g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\leq\left(g({\bf z}_{k+1})-g({\bf z}_{k})\right)^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\leq L_{t}\alpha_{k}\|{\bf d}_{k}\|^{2},

which implies that

\alpha_{k}\geq\frac{(\sigma-1)g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}}{L\|{\bf d}_{k}\|^{2}}.

Given that
g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}<0
, it follows from (
3.7
) that

\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{k+1})-\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{k})\leq\rho\alpha_{k}g(\mathbf{z}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{d}_{k}\leq\frac{\rho(\sigma-1)}{L_{t}}\frac{\left(g(\mathbf{z}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{d}_{k}\right)^{2}}{\|\mathbf{d}_{k}\|^{2}}.

(3.17)

It follows from (
3.17
) that the sequence
\{\Phi({\bf z}_{k})\}
is non-increasing. Moreover, by Theorem
2.1
,
\Phi({\bf z}_{k})
is bounded below. Thus,
\{\Phi({\bf z}_{k})\}
converges.
By taking a series for both sides of (
3.17
), it holds that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\right)^{2}}{\|{\bf d}_{k}\|^{2}}<\infty.

\Box

By Lemma
3.4
, we establish the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3

Let the sequence
\{{\bf z}_{k}\}
be generated by MGM. Then, the method either terminates at a stationary point or converges in the sense that

\liminf\limits_{k\to\infty}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|=0

(3.18)

Proof.
From (
3.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}-g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right)\beta_{k_{i}}

\displaystyle>(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{1}}-\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{1}})\beta_{k_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}\left(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}-g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right)\beta_{k_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=2}^{N}(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}-\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}})\beta_{k_{i}}=0.

Since all the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied, search directions are descent, which implies that

g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}<0\quad\text{for all }k.

Therefore, it follows from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\left|g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\right|

\displaystyle=-g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\gamma_{k}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}-\beta_{k_{i}}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}-g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right)\beta_{k_{i}}>0.

Rewrite (
3.2
) as

{\bf d}_{k}+\gamma_{k}g({\bf z}_{k})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}{\bf d}_{k-i}.

Squaring both sides and simplifying

\|{\bf d}_{k}\|^{2}=\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right\|^{2}-2\gamma_{k}g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}-\gamma_{k}^{2}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}.

Dividing both sides by
\left(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\right)^{2}
and applying (
3.19
), it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\|{\bf d}_{k}\|^{2}}{\left(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right\|^{2}}{\left(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\right)^{2}}-2\gamma_{k}\frac{g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}}{\left(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\right)^{2}}-\gamma_{k}^{2}\frac{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}{(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k})^{2}}

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right\|^{2}}{(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k})^{2}}-\frac{2\gamma_{k}}{g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}}-\gamma_{k}^{2}\frac{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}{(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right\|^{2}}{(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k})^{2}}-\left(\frac{1}{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|}+\gamma_{k}\frac{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|}{g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right\|^{2}}{(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k})^{2}}+\frac{1}{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}\|{\bf d}_{k-i}\|}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}-g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i})}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}.

Noting that
\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}\geq g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}+\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|\|{\bf d}_{k-i}\|
and multiplying this by
\beta_{k_{i}}\geq 0
, we have

\beta_{k_{i}}\left(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}-g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right)\geq\beta_{k_{i}}\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|\|{\bf d}_{k-i}\|.

Summing the above inequality over
i
, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}\left(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}-g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}\right)\geq\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}\|{\bf d}_{k-i}\|.

It follows from (
3.19
) that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}-g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i})>0
, and we obtain

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}\|{\bf d}_{k-i}\|}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\beta_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{k}\varphi_{k_{i}}-g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i})}\leq\frac{1}{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|}.

(3.21)

By (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), we have

\frac{\|{\bf d}_{k}\|^{2}}{\left(g_{1}({\bf x}_{k},{\bf y}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}+\frac{1}{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}=\frac{2}{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}\quad\text{for all }k.

(3.22)

If (
3.18
) is not true, there exists a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|\geq c_{1}\quad\text{for all }k.

(3.23)

Therefore, it follows from (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) that

\frac{(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k})^{2}}{\|{\bf d}_{k}\|^{2}}\geq\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2}.

Thus, we obtain

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k}\right)^{2}}{\|{\bf d}_{k}\|^{2}}=\infty.

This contradicts Lemma
3.4
, confirming that (
3.18
) holds.

\Box

This theorem implies that for any choices of
\gamma_{k},\varphi_{k_{i}}
(
i=1,\ldots,N
) satisfy condition (
3.6
), global convergence of our method is achieved.

4
Numerical results

In this section, we conduct some computational experiments to evaluate the efficacy and stability of the proposed method for solving problem (
2.1
) or (
2.5
). We compare the MGM method with the SS-HOPM in
[
47
]
and the SIPM in
[
46
]
, which have been reported to be efficient for unconstrained optimization. All numerical experiments were implemented in MATLAB 9.0 on a personal computer with AMD Ryzen 7 4800H CPU 2.90GHz and 16 GB random-access memory (RAM).

As established in Theorem
3.2
, the global convergence of the method is guaranteed for any parameters
\gamma_{k},\varphi_{k_{i}}
satisfying conditions (
3.6
). In our experiments, we first selected
\gamma_{k}
, and then determined corresponding values of
\varphi_{k_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
such that (
3.6
) holds. For all methods, we set
\bar{\gamma}=10^{-15}
. In particular, we adopted the following two choices for
\gamma_{k}
.

(i)

\gamma_{k}=1
for all
k
;

(ii)

\gamma_{0}=1
, and for
k\geq 1

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }~\dfrac{{\bf w}_{k-1}^{\top}{\bf s}_{k-1}}{{\bf w}_{k-1}^{\top}{\bf w}_{k-1}}<\bar{\gamma},\\
\dfrac{{\bf w}_{k-1}^{\top}{\bf s}_{k-1}}{{\bf w}_{k-1}^{\top}{\bf w}_{k-1}},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

To clarify the adaptive rules, we define

{\bf s}_{k-1}={\bf z}_{k}-{\bf z}_{k-1},{\bf t}_{k-1}=g({\bf z}_{k})-g({\bf z}_{k-1}).

The modified vectors are defined as

{\bf w}_{k-1}={\bf t}_{k-1}+\frac{\theta_{k-1}}{{\bf s}_{k-1}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k-1}},

where
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k-1}
is any vectors satisfying
{\bf s}_{k-1}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{k-1}\neq 0
, ensuring that the division is well-defined. The history-dependent correction coefficient is given by

\theta_{k-1}=6(f({\bf z}_{k})-f({\bf z}_{k-1}))+3(g({\bf z}_{k})-g({\bf z}_{k-1}))^{\top}{\bf s}_{k-1}.

The choice of
\gamma_{k}
in (ii) is motivated by the sizing technique of the modified secant condition introduced by Zhang et al.
[
51
,
52
]
. For a given

\gamma_{k}
, we define
\varphi_{k_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
as follows

\varphi_{k_{i}}=\dfrac{\|g({\bf z}_{k})\|\|{\bf d}_{k-i}\|+g({\bf z}_{k})^{\top}{\bf d}_{k-i}+m+n}{\gamma_{k}}.

This choice of
\varphi_{k_{i}}
satisfies condition (
3.6
).
We denote the MGM method with
\gamma_{k}
from (i) and (ii) as MGM-1 and MGM-2, respectively.

In the implementation of the MGM algorithm, we set parameters
\epsilon=10^{-6},\rho=0.1,\sigma=0.5
. The maximum number of allowed outer iterations was set to 2000.

Conclusions

In this paper, we focused on computing extremal M-eigenvalue of fourth order hierarchically symmetric tensors by transforming the M-eigenvalue problem into an unconstrained optimization framework.
Based on a new reformulaton, we proposed a memory gradient method to find an approximate solution of the problem. Furthermore, global convergence of the proposed method is established and several numerical examples verify the performance of the algorithm.
