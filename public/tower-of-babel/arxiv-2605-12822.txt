Unimodality of q q -Fibonomial coefficients for small cases
Brendan B. Connelly, Ezekiel Ito, Thomas C. Martinez, Olha Shevchenko, Kacey Yang

Abstract
. Bergeron–Ceballos–Küstner introduced the q q -Fibonomial coefficients [ m + n n ] ℱ \left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\ n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}} , gave a combinatorial interpretation of the q q -Fibonomial coefficients via a weighted path-domino tiling model, and conjectured that these polynomials are unimodal. We prove the conjecture for n ≤ 3 n\leq 3 . For the n = 2 n=2 case, we give a combinatorial proof of both unimodality and symmetry by defining a nearly symmetric saturated chain decomposition on the set of tilings. For all three cases, we give an algebraic proof. Finally, for the n = 3 n=3 case, we establish a more general unimodality result for certain products of q q -analogs and propose several related conjectures.

11B39, 05A30, 05A10

1.
Introduction

The
q
-Fibonomial coefficients, introduced by Bergeron–Ceballos–Küstner
4
, are the Fibonacci analogs of the Gaussian polynomials. For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the
q
-Fibonomial as follows:

(1.1)

\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}:=\frac{[F_{m+n}]^{!}_{q}}{[F_{m}]^{!}_{q}\cdot[F_{n}]^{!}_{q}},

where
[F_{n}]^{!}_{q}:=\prod_{k=1}^{n}[F_{k}]_{q}
, and
[n]_{q}:=1+q+\dots+q^{n-1}
.
4
shows
q
-Fibonomials are polynomials with non-negative integer coefficients by attaching
q
-weights to a variant of the path-domino tiling model of Sagan–Savage
10
described in
Section

2
.

The polynomials
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k}q^{k}
are symmetric, i.e.,
c_{k}=c_{N-k}
, and Bergeron–Ceballos–Küstner further conjectured
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
are unimodal, i.e.,
c_{0}\leq c_{1}\leq\dots\leq c_{M-1}\leq c_{M}\geq c_{M+1}\geq\dots\geq c_{N-1}\geq c_{N}
for some
M
, mirroring the classical results for the Gaussian polynomials
12
;
9
.

Conjecture 1.1

(
(
4
, Conjecture 2.5)
)
.

The polynomials
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
are unimodal.

Our main result resolves the conjecture for
n\leq 3
:

Theorem 1.2
.

The polynomials
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+1\\
1\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
,
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+2\\
2\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
, and
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+3\\
3\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
are unimodal.

Unimodality is often proved using log-concavity
(
11
, p. 500)
. However,
q
-Fibonomial coefficients generally fail to be log-concave—
\left[\!\begin{matrix}3+3\\
3\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
is not log-concave for example—so the recent machinery developed for such results
2
;
1
;
6
;
7
is not applicable.

We instead prove
Theorem

1.2
algebraically in
Section

4
. The cases
n=1,2
are immediate, while
n=3
reduces to the following characterization of unimodality for a three-term product of
q
-analogs, proved in
Section

4
as
Proposition

4.5
.

Proposition 1.3
.

For
a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}
, the polynomial
T(q)=[a]_{q}[b]_{q}[c]_{q^{2}}
is symmetric and unimodal if and only if
2c\leq a+b
, or
a
or
b
is even.

After establishing relevant background in
Section

2
, we also give an independent combinatorial proof for
n=2
using the weighted path-domino tiling model of
4
in
Section

3
. We accomplish this by constructing equivalence classes of tilings which may be grouped to have symmetric and unimodal
q
-weights

Section

5
records further conjectures supported by computational evidence and develops the connection to the
q
-Fibonacci analog of the rational Catalan numbers, or the (rational)
q
-FiboCatalan numbers. In particular, we observe that
1.1
would imply non-negativity of these polynomials, a fact stated but, to our knowledge, not previously proved.

2.1.
Fibonacci Facts

The Fibonacci numbers
F_{n}

F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
for
n\geq 2

F_{0}=0

F_{1}=1

We collect a few well-known Fibonacci identities (see, for example,
3

Proposition 2.1
.

For all
m,n\geq 0
, we have

(1)

F_{1}+F_{2}+F_{3}+\dots+F_{n}=F_{n+2}-1
.

(2)

F_{1}+F_{3}+\dots+F_{2n-1}=F_{2n}
.

(3)

F_{2}+F_{4}+\dots+F_{2n}=F_{2n+1}-1
.

(4)

If
m
divides
n
, then
F_{m}
divides
F_{n}
.

We will also be using Zeckendorf’s theorem
13

Theorem 2.2

(
13
)
.

Every positive integer has a unique representation as a sum of non-consecutive Fibonacci numbers.

2.2.
q
-Fibonomial Coefficients

The Fibonomial coefficients were originally defined by Lucas
8
: for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left(\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right)_{\mathcal{F}}:=\frac{F_{m+n}^{!}}{F_{m}^{!}F_{n}^{!}},

where
F_{n}^{!}=F_{n}\cdot F_{n-1}\cdots F_{2}\cdot F_{1}
. Sagan–Savage
10
gave a combinatorial interpretation of these Fibonomial coefficients as enumerating certain path-domino tilings of an
m\times n
rectangle, and
4
introduced a
q
-weighting on this model that recovers the
q
-Fibonomial coefficients
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
as in (
1.1
). We recall the construction.

Fix
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Choose a lattice path from
(0,0)
to
(m,n)
, and tile the region above the path with squares and horizontal dominoes (
2\times 1
rectangles) and the region below with squares and vertical dominoes (
1\times 2
rectangles). We further require each tile immediately below a horizontal segment of the path to be a vertical domino. Assign weights to the tiles as follows:

w(
)=1
,
w(

,
w(
)=q^{F_{i}F_{j}}
,
w(

(i,j)
denotes the coordinates of the top right most corner of the tile, and the blue vertical domino represents the domino forced to lie immediately beneath the lattice path. Squares contribute weight 1 and are recoverable from the placement of dominoes, so we omit them from diagrams.

q

Theorem 2.3

(

For

\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\

Symmetry and Unimodality of
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+2\\
2\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}

3.1.
Partitioning
\mathcal{T}_{m,2}

Define a map
\pi:\mathcal{T}_{m,2}\to\mathcal{T}_{m,2}
as follows.

(1)

If there is a horizontal domino at
(2,1)
, remove it.

\overset{\pi}{\longmapsto}

(2)

Otherwise, if there are any horizontal dominoes in the bottom row, shift the leftmost domino left by one tile and add as many dominoes directly to the left of it as possible.

\overset{\pi}{\longmapsto}

(3)

Otherwise, if there are vertical dominoes, rotate the leftmost domino occupying
(k,2)
to occupy
(k,1)
and add as many dominoes directly to the left of it as possible.

\overset{\pi}{\longmapsto}

(4)

Otherwise, if the bottom row is completely empty,
\pi
fixes the tiling.

\overset{\pi}{\longmapsto}

We also define a partial inverse
\pi^{*}:\mathcal{T}_{m,2}\to\mathcal{T}_{m,2}
.

(1)

If
(2,1)
does not contain a domino, add a horizontal domino there.

(2)

Otherwise, if there exists a horizontal domino in the bottom row that can be shifted to the right, shift the leftmost domino that can be shifted to the right, and remove all the dominoes to the left of it.

(3)

Otherwise, if there exists a domino in the bottom row that can be rotated (i.e. there exists a horizontal domino occupying
(k,1)
, and
(k,2)
is empty, and there are no more horizontal dominoes to the right of that domino in the top row), rotate the rightmost domino and remove all the dominoes to the left of it on the bottom row.

(4)

If none of the above are possible,
\pi^{*}
fixes the tiling.

.

Note that
\pi(\pi^{*}(T))=T

\pi^{*}(T)\neq T

\pi^{*}(\pi(T))=T

\pi(T)\neq T

Remark 3.2
.

The top horizontal tiling remains unchanged under

\pi^{*}

4.1.
Key Properties of

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
a\leq b
be two positive integers. Then,

[a]_{q}[b]_{q}=\sum_{k=0}^{a+b-2}c_{k}q^{k},\text{ where }c_{k}=\begin{cases}k+1,&0\leq k\leq a-1,\\
a,&a\leq k\leq b-1,\\
a+b-1-k,&b\leq k\leq a+b-2.\end{cases}

In particular,
[a]_{q}[b]_{q}
is symmetric and unimodal.

Proof.

We express each polynomial as a finite geometric series:

[a]_{q}[b]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{a}}{1-q}\cdot\frac{1-q^{b}}{1-q}=(1-q^{a}-q^{b}+q^{a+b})(1-q)^{-2}.

Using the power series expansion
(1-q)^{-2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)q^{j}
, we distribute the numerator:

[a]_{q}[b]_{q}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)q^{j}-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)q^{j+a}-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)q^{j+b}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)q^{j+a+b}.

Let
[a]_{q}[b]_{q}=\sum_{k}c_{k}q^{k}
. The coefficient
c_{k}
of
q^{k}
is found by shifting the indices and applying the indicator function
\mathbb{I}
:

c_{k}=(k+1)-\mathbb{I}_{k\geq a}(k-a+1)-\mathbb{I}_{k\geq b}(k-b+1)+\mathbb{I}_{k\geq a+b}(k-a-b+1).

This simplifies to the statement above.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
a,b,{r}
are three positive integers such that
{r}\,|\,a
. Then
[a]_{q}[b]_{q^{{r}}}
is symmetric and unimodal.

Proof.

Let
a=k{r}
.
First, notice that

[a]_{q}=\dfrac{1-q^{k{r}}}{1-q}=\dfrac{1-q^{r}}{1-q}\cdot\dfrac{1-(q^{r})^{k}}{1-q^{r}}=(1+q+\ldots+q^{{r}-1})[k]_{q^{r}}.

Therefore

[a]_{q}[b]_{q^{r}}=(1+q+\ldots+q^{{r}-1})[k]_{q^{r}}[b]_{q^{r}}.

Denote
[k]_{q}[b]_{q}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}c_{i}q^{i}
. Then

\displaystyle[a]_{q}[b]_{q}

\displaystyle=(1+q+\ldots+q^{{r}-1})[k]_{q^{r}}[b]_{q^{r}}=(1+q+\ldots+q^{{r}-1})\sum_{i=0}^{N}c_{i}q^{i{r}}=\sum_{j=0}^{{r}-1}\sum_{i=0}^{N}c_{i}q^{i{r}+j}

\displaystyle=c_{0}+c_{0}q+\ldots+c_{0}q^{{r}-1}+c_{1}q^{r}+c_{1}q^{{r}+1}+\ldots+c_{N}q^{N{r}+{r}-1}.

By
Lemma

4.1
, the coefficient sequence
\{c_{k}\}
is symmetric and unimodal.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose

(1)

(2)

Assume

\displaystyle B(k)

\displaystyle=\#\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\Bigm|0\leq\ell\leq\frac{k-b+1}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&k<b-1,\\
\left\lfloor\dfrac{k-b+1}{2}\right\rfloor+1,&b-1\leq k\leq c+\dfrac{a+b}{2}-3.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dfrac{a-1}{2},&b-1\leq k\leq 2c-3,\ k\text{ even},\\[5.0pt]

b,a+b-1
are odd and
\frac{a+b-1}{2}<c-1
. We have
A(a+b-2)<B(a+b-2)
.

∎

Proposition 4.5
.

For any
a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}
,
T(q)=[a]_{q}[b]_{q}[c]_{q^{2}}
is symmetric and unimodal if and only if
2c\leq a+b
or
a
or
b
is even.

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality
a\leq b
. If
a
or
b
is even, this follows from
Corollary

4.3
.

Assume
a
and
b
are odd. Since
T(q)
is a product of symmetric polynomials, it is symmetric, and it is unimodal if and only if
[q^{k+1}]T(q)-[q^{k}]T(q)\geq 0
for every
0\leq k\leq\frac{\deg T}{2}-1=c+\frac{a+b}{2}-3
.

Write

T(q)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{c-1}T_{\ell}(q),\qquad T_{\ell}(q):=q^{2\ell}[a]_{q}[b]_{q},

so that
[q^{k+1}]T(q)-[q^{k}]T(q)=\sum_{\ell}\bigl([q^{k+1}]T_{\ell}(q)-[q^{k}]T_{\ell}(q)\bigr)
. Applying
Lemma

4.1
to the coefficients of
T_{\ell}
, we get

[q^{k+1}]T_{\ell}(q)-[q^{k}]T_{\ell}(q)=\begin{cases}1,&2\ell-1\leq k\leq 2\ell+a-2,\\
-1,&2\ell+b-1\leq k\leq 2\ell+a+b-2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Define

\displaystyle A(k)

\displaystyle:=\#\{\ell\in[0,c-1]:2\ell-1\leq k\leq 2\ell+a-2\}

\displaystyle=\#\left\{\ell\in[0,c-1]:\tfrac{k-a+2}{2}\leq\ell\leq\tfrac{k+1}{2}\right\},

\displaystyle B(k)

\displaystyle:=\#\{\ell\in[0,c-1]:2\ell+b-1\leq k\leq 2\ell+a+b-2\}

\displaystyle=\#\left\{\ell\in[0,c-1]:\tfrac{k-a-b+2}{2}\leq\ell\leq\tfrac{k-b+1}{2}\right\},

so that
[q^{k+1}]T(q)-[q^{k}]T(q)=A(k)-B(k)
. Both directions of the lemma now follow from
Lemma

4.4
.
∎

4.2.
Proof of
Theorem

1.2

We now prove unimodality of
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+1\\
1\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}},\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+2\\
2\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}},\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+3\\
3\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

The case
n=1
is immediate, since
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+1\\
1\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}=[F_{m+1}]_{q}
. For
n=2
,
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+2\\
2\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}=[F_{m+1}]_{q}[F_{m+2}]_{q}
, and the result follows from
Lemma

4.1
.

It remains to treat
n=3
. By definition of factorials and since
[F_{3}]_{q}^{!}=1+q
,

\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+3\\
3\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}=\frac{[F_{m+3}]_{q}^{!}}{[F_{3}]_{q}^{!}\,[F_{m}]_{q}^{!}}=\frac{[F_{m+3}]_{q}[F_{m+2}]_{q}[F_{m+1}]_{q}}{1+q}.

Among the three consecutive Fibonacci numbers
F_{m+1},F_{m+2},F_{m+3}
, exactly one is even by
Proposition

2.1
(4); call this term
E
and the remaining (odd) terms
A\leq B
. Then

\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+3\\
3\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}=[A]_{q}[B]_{q}\cdot\frac{[E]_{q}}{1+q}=[A]_{q}[B]_{q}\cdot\left[\tfrac{E}{2}\right]_{q^{2}}.

Since
2\cdot\frac{E}{2}\leq A+B
,
Proposition

4.5
gives that
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+3\\
3\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
is symmetric and unimodal.
∎

5.2.
More on
q
-analogs

In proving the symmetry and unimodality of
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+3\\
3\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
, we encounter several lemmas regarding
q
-analogs, for example
Proposition

4.5
. We attempt to generalize some of these results below. In
Lemma

4.2
, we proved the simplest case needed to prove
Proposition

4.5
. We prove an analogous proposition below.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
a,b,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Then
[a]_{q}[b]_{q^{r}}
is unimodal if and only if
a\geq r(b-1)
or
r\,|\,a
.

Proof.

If
r\,|\,a
, then
[a]_{q}[b]_{q^{r}}
is unimodal by
Lemma

4.2
. Assume
r\nmid a
; we show
[a]_{q}[b]_{q^{r}}
is unimodal if and only if
a\geq r(b-1)
.

Write

P(q):=[a]_{q}[b]_{q^{r}}=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}q^{i+{r}j}=\sum_{t=0}^{D}c_{t}q^{t},\qquad D:=(a-1)+{r}(b-1).

Since
[a]_{q}
and
[b]_{q^{r}}
are symmetric of degrees
a-1
and
r(b-1)
,
P
is symmetric of degree
D
.

Suppose first that
a\geq{r}(b-1)
, so
\lfloor\frac{D}{2}\rfloor\leq a-1
. For
0\leq t\leq a-1
, the constraint
i+{r}j=t
with
0\leq i\leq a-1
forces only
{r}j\leq t
, so

c_{t}=\#\{j\in\{0,\dots,b-1\}:{r}j\leq t\}=\min\!\left(b-1,\lfloor\frac{t}{{r}}\rfloor\right)+1,

which is weakly increasing in
t
. Symmetry then gives unimodality.

Conversely, suppose
a<r(b-1)
, so
a\leq\frac{D}{2}
; we show
c_{a}<c_{a-1}
. For
c_{a-1}
: any
j\geq 0
with
rj\leq a-1
satisfies
j<b
and
i:=a-1-rj\in[0,a-1]
, so
c_{a-1}=\lfloor\frac{a-1}{r}\rfloor+1
. For
c_{a}
: we need
i=a-rj\leq a-1
, forcing
j\geq 1
, so
c_{a}=\#\{j\geq 1:rj\leq a\}=\lfloor\frac{a}{r}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{a-1}{r}\rfloor
, using
r\nmid a
. Hence
c_{a}<c_{a-1}
.
∎

We also conjecture the following as a generalization of
Corollary

4.3
,
Proposition

4.5
, and
Proposition

5.3
.

Conjecture 5.4
.

Let
r\geq 2,k\geq 1
and let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},b
be positive integers. Then if
r\mid a_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq k
or

b\leq 1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}}{r}\right\rfloor,

the polynomial
[a_{1}]_{q}\dots[a_{k}]_{q}[b]_{q^{r}}
is unimodal. Moreover, if
k\leq 3
or
r\leq 3
, this condition is also necessary.

We have computationally verified
5.4
for
k\leq 5,r\leq 6
and
\max\{a_{i},b\}\leq 15
. In general, the condition is not necessary:
([3]_{q})^{4}[2]_{q^{4}}
is unimodal despite violating the inequality
b\leq 1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lfloor\frac{a_{i}}{r}\rfloor
.

We also note that
5.4
does not directly imply unimodality of
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
, because the factorization of
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
for
n\geq 4
as a product of
q
-analogs does not match the form of
[a_{1}]_{q}\dots[a_{k}]_{q}[b]_{q^{r}}
in the conjecture.

5.3.
q
-FiboCatalan Numbers

For relatively prime integers
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the rational Catalan number

C_{m,n}=\frac{1}{m+n}\binom{m+n}{n}

counts the lattice paths from
(0,0)
to
(m,n)
that stay weakly above the main diagonal of the
m\times n
rectangle
5
. The Fibonacci analog, or the FiboCatalan number, is defined as

\operatorname{FCat}_{m,n}:=\frac{1}{F_{m+n}}\left(\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right)_{\mathcal{F}}=\frac{F_{m+n-1}^{!}}{F_{m}^{!}\,F_{n}^{!}}.

We then define the
q
-FiboCatalan number

[\operatorname{FCat}_{m,n}]_{q}:=\frac{1}{[F_{m+n}]_{q}}\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}=\frac{[F_{m+n-1}]^{!}_{q}}{[F_{m}]^{!}_{q}\,[F_{n}]^{!}_{q}}.

Bergeron–Ceballos–Küstner proved that this rational expression is in fact a polynomial with integral coefficients when
\gcd(m,n)\in\{1,2\}
, and conjectured non-negativity based on computational evidence.

Proposition 5.5

(
(
4
, Proposition 4.2)
)
.

If
\gcd(m,n)\in\{1,2\}
, then
[\operatorname{FCat}_{m,n}]_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

We show that
1.1
implies this observed non-negativity.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
m,n\geq 1
with
\gcd(m,n)\in\{1,2\}
. If
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
is unimodal, then
[\operatorname{FCat}_{m,n}]_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q]
.

Proof.

Write
[\operatorname{FCat}_{m,n}]_{q}=\sum_{i=0}^{D}c_{i}q^{i}
and
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}q^{i}
, where
d=\deg\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
. Symmetry of
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
and
[F_{m+n}]_{q}
together with
Proposition

5.5
implies symmetry of
[\operatorname{FCat}_{m,n}]_{q}
, so it suffices to show
c_{i}\geq 0
for
0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{D}{2}\rfloor
.

From

[\operatorname{FCat}_{m,n}]_{q}=\frac{1-q}{1-q^{F_{m+n}}}\,\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(1-q)\,q^{kF_{m+n}}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}q^{i},

we read off (with the convention
a_{-r}=0
for
r\geq 1
)

c_{i}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\bigl(a_{i-kF_{m+n}}-a_{i-kF_{m+n}-1}\bigr).

For
i\leq\lfloor\frac{D}{2}\rfloor\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
and any
k\geq 0
, we have
i-kF_{m+n}\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
, so unimodality of
\left[\!\begin{matrix}m+n\\
n\end{matrix}\!\right]_{\mathcal{F}}
gives
a_{i-kF_{m+n}}-a_{i-kF_{m+n}-1}\geq 0
. Hence
c_{i}\geq 0
.
∎
