A bibliometric analysis of the application trends of information technology in antimicrobial stewardship within hospitals
Authors: Yannan Ding, Ming Lei, Gang Yuan, Jinzhong Yu, Lili Wu, Jianwen Yang, Hong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study uses bibliometric analysis to systematically map research trends, knowledge structure and evolution of information technology (IT) in hospital antimicrobial stewardship (AMS) over the last 20 years.MethodsA Web of Science Core Collection search (2000–2025) yielded 258 English-language publications on IT applications in hospital AMS. A bibliometric analysis, utilising CiteSpace and Bibliometrix, quantitatively evaluated domain evolution through temporal analysis, network mapping, keyword clustering, burst detection and examination of highly cited publications.ResultsThe bibliometric analysis reveals a fluctuating yet overall increasing trend in annual publications, peaking at 49 articles in 2024. The US (131 publications) and European nations demonstrate significant research output (centrality >0.2), with major collaborative networks coalescing around institutions including Harvard University and Imperial College London. Keyword analysis identifies ‘AMS’ as a core theme, closely associated with technological keywords such as ‘machine learning (ML)’, ‘clinical decision support systems (CDSS)’, ‘electronic health records (EHR)’ and ‘artificial intelligence (AI)’. Emerging trends suggest a shift in research focus from foundational strategies to data-driven prediction of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) and precision interventions. Highly cited literature emphasises the integration of EHR and ML technologies for optimising prescriptions and predicting resistance patterns.ConclusionsIT-driven AMS research has shifted from empirical management to data science. Despite EHR integration, and ML and CDSS support, challenges remain in data standardisation, technical deployment and ethics. Future work must emphasise global collaboration, standardisation, design refinement and ethical guidelines, and provide clear algorithm explanations to enhance AMR mitigation.Keywords: Information Technology; Antimicrobial Stewardship; HOSPITALS; Infection Control; MEDICATION SYSTEMS, HOSPITAL

Introduction

The escalating prevalence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR), driven by the irrational utilisation of antimicrobial agents, constitutes a significant global health challenge.1 Effective antimicrobial stewardship (AMS) is paramount in mitigating this crisis. However, traditional AMS models are hampered by manual processes and data integration limitations, thereby impeding dynamic monitoring and precise intervention.2 In this context, the integration of information technology (IT) to establish intelligent AMS systems is essential for optimising antimicrobial utilisation and curbing the proliferation of drug-resistant bacteria. Recent technological advancements, including big data and artificial intelligence (AI), have spurred interdisciplinary collaboration. Despite these developments, the developmental trajectories within this domain remain inadequately elucidated, necessitating a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of the extant literature.

Bibliometrics is a discipline that analyses literature information through quantitative methods to study academic communication patterns and knowledge structures. It can describe and analyse the dynamics and progress of specific research domains.3 With CiteSpace and Bibliometrix assistance, bibliometric analysis results can be visualised through clear and intuitive mapping.4 5

This study employs bibliometric methods, analysing publications concerning IT applications in hospital AMS, sourced from the Web of Science (WOS) Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-E). Employing visual mapping of overall output, research themes, collaborative networks, citation analysis and keyword co-occurrence, we aim to elucidate the developmental trajectory, research hotspots and emerging trends within this domain over the preceding two decades. The outcomes are anticipated to inform future research endeavours and provide practical insights for hospital AMS initiatives.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and cleaning

The data samples were retrieved from the WOS Core Collection using a topic-based search strategy tailored to keywords relevant to this study. As depicted in figure 1, the search query for literature was designed as follows: TS＝[(‘antimicrobial stewardship’ OR ‘antibiotic stewardship’ OR ‘rational antimicrobial use’ OR ‘antimicrobial optimization’) AND (‘information technology’ OR ‘digital health’ OR ‘artificial intelligence’ OR ‘AI’ OR ‘machine learning’ OR ‘clinical decision support system’ OR ‘CDSS’ OR ‘big data’ OR ‘electronic health records’ OR ‘EHR’ OR ‘natural language processing’ OR ‘blockchain’ OR ‘internet of things’ OR ‘predictive model*’ OR ‘intelligent monitoring’) AND (hospital* OR ‘healthcare facility’ OR inpatient* OR outpatient* OR ‘intensive care unit’ OR ‘ICU’ OR ‘operating room’ OR infect* OR ‘pharmacy’) NOT veterinary]; with a time span from January 2000 to February 2025, document types limited to articles and review articles, and language restricted to English. The initial search yielded 321 papers (including 262 articles and 59 review articles). Irrelevant literature was independently reviewed by two authors (YD and ML), and any contentious publications were subject to final judgement by the corresponding author. After reviewing abstracts or full texts, 63 irrelevant papers were excluded, resulting in 258 valid papers (including 202 articles and 56 review articles).6

Research methods

This study employed bibliometric methods to assess quantitatively the existing research landscape concerning the role of IT in hospital AMS. Employing CiteSpace and Bibliometrix, we performed a comprehensive analysis, including collaboration network analysis (examining countries/regions and key authors), keyword co-occurrence analysis, and an evaluation of research hotspots and emerging trends.4 5

Result determination methods

In the visualised knowledge graph, node size reflects collaboration and co-occurrence frequency, and nodes with a purple ring have centrality ≥0.1, marking them as key ‘bridges’ in research collaboration. Link thickness corresponds to connection strength, and nodes in the same cluster share colour. The colour gradient from cool to warm indicates temporal evolution of topics. Burst detection highlights research hotspots, with red segments indicating activity surges and potential emerging trends. CiteSpace parameter settings appear in the figure’s upper left corner.4 5 7

Results

Analysis of temporal distribution characteristics and journal distribution characteristics in the literature

The distribution and fluctuation of annual publications reflect a field’s research scope and developmental stage. Figure 2A demonstrates a fluctuating growth pattern. No literature in this field was published before 2014. The lowest publication output in 2014 increased to approximately 10 and 15 articles in 2015 and 2016, respectively, indicating the initial development of research and growing academic interest. From 2017 onward, publication numbers showed significant growth, reaching 19 articles in 2018. A decline in 2019 was followed by a substantial increase to 38 articles in 2020. A temporary dip to about 17 publications occurred in 2021. Publication output then increased to 31 articles in 2022, indicating a return to this area. The peak was reached in 2024, with 49 publications, demonstrating significant research growth. Projections suggest that publication volumes will stay high through the end of 2025. Overall, AMS research has experienced substantial growth over the past decade. This trajectory reflects both heightened global recognition of IT’s role in antimicrobial management and deepening scholarly investigations.

Keyword hotspot clustering analysis and keyword burst detection analysis

Using CiteSpace, we performed a visual clustering analysis of keywords, integrating synonymous terms. The keyword co-occurrence network for IT applications in AMS research, sourced from WOS, encompasses 302 nodes and 662 links (figure 5A). Betweenness centrality, a metric of node significance, reflects a keyword’s importance within the research domain; elevated values denote greater significance.

(A) Keyword co-occurrence network. (B) Keyword burst map.

The keyword co-occurrence analysis and frequency-centrality metrics delineate research hotspots. ‘Antimicrobial stewardship’ emerges as the core keyword, with a frequency of 155, indicating its central position. Associated technological keywords such as ‘machine learning’ (frequency: 37, centrality: 0.08), ‘electronic health records’ (frequency: 23, centrality: 0.03), ‘artificial intelligence’ (frequency: 20, centrality: 0.21) and ‘clinical decision support’ (frequency: 16, centrality: 0.28), further define the AMS research landscape.

Keyword burst analysis reveals evolving research trends in IT applications for AMS (figure 5B). Early-stage (2014–2016) research, characterised by terms like ‘Healthcare Epidemiology’ and ‘Hospitals’, focused on foundational AMS implementation. The mid-stage (2017–2020) shifted toward patient-specific management and therapeutic evaluations, and emerging terms like ‘EHR’ and ‘Big Data’ signal growing integration of IT in research. Recent research (2021–2025) emphasises infection control and AMR, with ‘Clinical Decision Support’ as a sustained burst. Keyword burst analysis demonstrates a progression from basic research to patient-focused studies, ultimately leading to advanced infection control and resistance strategies. These trends reflect the field’s development and underscore emerging priorities, thus guiding future research directions.

Discussion

AMS is a critical strategy in addressing the global AMR crisis. IT advancements have introduced new methods for AMS, enhancing management effectiveness. Hospital pharmacists want to learn from the advanced experiences of interdisciplinary cooperation between IT and hospital AMS and apply them to guide clinical practice. Preliminary individual literature analyses reveal fragmentation and regional biases, which hinder in-depth interpretation of research findings. Therefore, it is essential to acquire a comprehensive and holistic understanding of this field. Traditional systematic reviews effectively synthesise evidence related to specific interventions but are limited in depicting developmental trajectories. Bibliometric analysis, utilising quantitative methods and algorithms, offers an objective macro perspective on the evolution of scientific fields. Based on the above reasons, this study utilises bibliometric analysis to examine research trends, international collaboration, key contributors and future research directions in this field, providing insights into the evolution of informatics technology in AMS research.

Current status of IT applied to AMS in hospitals

The integration of IT in hospital antimicrobial drug management is advancing to optimise antibiotic use, combat AMR and improve treatment safety. This involves resistance prediction, dosage optimisation, prescribing decision support, adverse effect monitoring and patient education. Key tools include EHR, big data analytics, ML, AI, CDSS and predictive models.

EHRs are crucial for AMS, facilitating data integration, antibiotic use monitoring and safety assessments.8 Studies by Howard et al and Eickelberg et al highlight the efficacy of EHRs in restricting antibiotic use and developing risk models.9 10 Institutions like Johns Hopkins Hospital have developed AMS modules within EHRs, leading to reduced broad-spectrum antibiotic use.11 EHR-based default settings and e-prescribing systems have also improved prescribing practices.12 13 Big data analyses by Budgell et al show that EHR-based benchmarking can reduce antibiotic use without increasing mortality.14

CDSS are pivotal in AMS interventions, optimising antibiotic selection and curtailing broad-spectrum antibiotic utilisation. Bibliometric analyses underscore ‘clinical decision support’ as a high-centrality keyword. Gulliford et al demonstrated a 12% reduction in respiratory tract infection antibiotic prescriptions via real-time decision support.15 Khadem et al reported that CDSS implementation in community hospitals reduced antimicrobial use and enhanced pharmacist efficiency.16 The COMPASS study revealed that CDSS facilitated intravenous-to-oral therapy transitions.17 Vu et al documented a significant increase in guideline adherence in appendicitis treatment following CDSS integration.18 Stenehjem et al reported a 23% decrease in antibiotic prescribing for respiratory infections with CDSS implementation.19 Amor-García et al found that CDSS implementation reduced antimicrobial use duration and hospitalisation length.20

The application of ML and AI in AMS has significantly improved predictive accuracy and decision-making. These technologies are instrumental in forecasting AMR, refining individualised dosing strategies and stratifying infection risk. For instance, the InSight system employs ML to predict sepsis onset, supporting early, targeted treatment and reducing broad-spectrum antibiotic use.21 Combining MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry with ML has accelerated resistance detection, optimising treatment.22 The XGBoost model predicts antibiotic resistance in bloodstream infections, enhancing treatment effectiveness even without microbial identification.23 AI-driven analytics leverage extensive datasets to identify resistance patterns, predict outbreaks and guide personalised antibiotic therapy. ML algorithms enable rapid pathogen identification and real-time surveillance, improving decision precision. These advancements also facilitate the development of advanced diagnostics, reducing reliance on broad-spectrum antibiotics. AI-enabled surveillance systems enhance the detection of antimicrobial drug resistance trends and global surveillance capabilities.24

Concurrently, these technologies are frequently integrated to enhance AMS practices. Forrest et al integrated EHRs with CDSS for centralised analysis of pharmacy, microbiology and clinical data to support AMS strategies.8 Trubiano et al developed clinical decision rules (PEN-FAST) to mitigate unnecessary β-lactam antibiotic substitutions by rapidly assessing penicillin allergy risk via electronic tools, thereby optimising antimicrobial selection within the electronic medical record.25 Peiffer-Smadja et al demonstrated the application of ML-CDSS in infection diagnosis, drug resistance prediction and therapeutic regimen selection.26 Tran-The et al developed interpretable machine learning models, such as XGBoost and LGBM, to identify hospitalised patients requiring prioritised interventions, resulting in a 17–41% increase in antibiotic stewardship efficiency.27 Flores et al implemented an ML model in the emergency department for urinary tract infection diagnosis, reducing unnecessary urinary cultures and antibiotic prescriptions by 30% through risk stratification.28

Research trends and rising global interest

Over the past decade, the annual number of publications in the field of IT applied to AMS has shown fluctuating growth, reaching a high of 49 in 2024, especially after 2020. This trend reflects the growing importance of AMS, driven by the WHO’s 2015 plan to combat AMR and the potential for relaxed antibiotic controls during the pandemic.29 The steady increase in the number of publications over the past 3 years signifies that the application of IT to AMS research has received increasing attention.

Differential patterns of country-institutional collaboration

The USA exhibits pre-eminence in national publications, with 131 publications and a centrality of 0.25, indicating substantial academic influence. European nations demonstrate significant output, whereas Asian countries lag despite publication growth. US and European institutions lead in IT application within AMS research, potentially reflecting superior AMS policies and funding. International collaborations highlight the US’s close ties with European institutions, facilitating technology standardisation, exemplified by the EHR-CDSS integration solution.8 Asian and South American involvement remains limited, possibly due to resource constraints and technical barriers.

Technology convergence and the evolution of research hotspots

Keyword analysis shows that ‘AMS’ is the core node, closely related to ‘ML’ and ‘AMR’. Antimicrobial drug management is one of the core strategies to cope with the global antibiotic resistance crisis. With the development of science and technology, IT has been applied to the management of antimicrobial drugs, which has greatly promoted the improvement of antimicrobial drug management. Keyword emergence analysis also further validates that technological innovations such as ML-driven precision management mark a paradigm shift from empirically-oriented to data-driven.30

Challenges and future directions

Despite the progress of AMS research, there are still key challenges. Data privacy and ethical issues are critical, especially with EHRs and ML.31 Big data integration is hampered by standardisation issues and reliance on biased single-centre data.32 33 CDSS face limitations due to rule-based designs and poor usability.34 ML applications in drug resistance prediction require rigorous validation,35 and case-based reasoning algorithms lack real-world validation.36 Limited AMR surveillance data in low-income countries restrict global strategy implementation.26 Nowadays, all countries around the world are actively seeking solutions. For instance, ethical principles of AI are emerging,37 and adaptive research on CDSS in the grassroots medical environment in West Africa is also underway.38

Methodological rigour and limitations

Our analysis adhered to the general quality standards of bibliometrics, ensuring the rigour of this research from the perspectives of accuracy, validity, objectivity and reproducibility. This study selected the WOS as the main bibliometric database to ensure the validity of the data. This choice is based on the fact that the WOS has a strict journal selection mechanism, a complete citation data chain and cross-disciplinary coverage capabilities, which meet the core requirements of bibliometric analysis for data quality and historical traceability.39 Meanwhile, the time span of the literature search (from January 2000 to February 2025) covers the appearance time of the first paper in this field (2014), thus avoiding any omissions. The method of conducting mutual verification through the data collection and cleaning carried out by two independent authors, and submitting disputed issues to a third party for adjudication, ensures the accuracy and reproducibility of the data. At the same time, the analysis was conducted using automated tools (CiteSpace and Bibliometrix), avoiding subjective assumptions and reducing human bias through standardised methods, ensuring objectivity. Of course, this study also has inevitable limitations, such as data source bias (for instance, WOS has insufficient coverage of non-English literature; among the current literature, the non-English related documents included in WOS accounted for only 1.2%) and retrieval omissions caused by the evolution of terms. These are the areas where our research group will focus on making improvements in future studies.

Conclusions

AMS research has realised a leapfrog development from basic strategy to technological innovation, and IT has become an indispensable tool for AMS. But its full-scale application still needs to overcome technical, ethical and policy barriers. International collaboration, optimised technology, interdisciplinary integration and scientific algorithms for decisions are vital for AMS to combat AMR effectively and establish a transparent global prevention system.
